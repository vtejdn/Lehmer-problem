# Sharp coefficient cost for two prescribed reciprocal seeds at every pair of comparable primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## The weighted sum of the two measures

Let \(P_1,P_2\) be monic reciprocal minimal polynomials of nontorsion algebraic units \(a_1,a_2\), with logarithmic Mahler measures \(h_1,h_2>0\) and degrees \(e_1,e_2\).
Suppose that for each polynomial separately, each circle of radius greater than one contains at most one of its roots.
The two polynomials may be equal.
Fix \(C>1\) and \(\epsilon>0\).
There is an integer \(w\) such that for every sufficiently large \(L\) and every pair of distinct primes \(p,q\in[L,CL]\), there exist nonzero coprime integral polynomials \(U,V\) of degrees at most \(w\) satisfying
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P_1(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad
 P_2(X)^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q),\\
 D(Y)=U(Y)U(Y^{-1})-V(Y)V(Y^{-1})\ne0,\\
 p h_1+q h_2\ \le\ \log H\ <\ p h_1+q h_2+\epsilon(p+q),
 \qquad H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1).
 \end{gathered}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\|\cdot\|_1\) is the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients.
For any width bound \(b(L)\to\infty\), the minimum log cost with these prescribed contacts equals \(p h_1+q h_2+o(L)\) uniformly over the indicated prime pairs.
In particular (1) applies to any two Salem numbers and to a Salem number and a fixed positive power of it.

<a id="two-nodes"></a>
## The determinant at two exterior nodes

Fix a splitting field \(F\) containing all roots.
For sufficiently large distinct primes the powered conjugates in the two slots are all distinct.
Within a slot equality would require a quotient of distinct roots to have order equal to the powering prime.
Across slots, equality would imply \(p h_{\rm abs}(a_1)=q h_{\rm abs}(a_2)\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The two fixed positive heights determine at most one reduced ratio of distinct primes.
Consequently the two powered minima have degrees \(e_1,e_2\), are distinct and have logarithmic Mahler measures \(p h_1,q h_2\).

For an exterior label \(c\) in the first slot and \(d\) in the second, put \(z=c^{-p}\) and \(v=d^{-q}\).
Consider a pair of polynomials \(A,B\) of degrees at most one subject to the equations
\[
 A(z)=cB(z),\qquad pz(A'-cB')(z)+cB(z)=0
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
and the corresponding equations at \((d,v,q)\).
For all sufficiently large distinct primes the only solution is zero.
Indeed, if \(B(z)=0\), both polynomials vanish at \(z\) and are scalar multiples of \(Y-z\).
The value equation at \(v\ne z\) forces \(A=dB\), and the derivative equation there gives \(d B(v)=0\), hence \(A=B=0\).
The same reasoning applies when \(B(v)=0\).
If \(c=d\), the value equations alone imply \(A=cB\), so both derivative equations force \(B=0\).

In any remaining nonzero solution \(T=A/B\) would be a nonconstant fractional linear function with \(T(z)=c,T(v)=d\).
Its derivatives are \(-c/(pz)\) and \(-d/(qv)\) by (2).
For a fractional linear function, subtraction of the two values and differentiation give
\[
 T'(z)T'(v)=\left(\frac{T(z)-T(v)}{z-v}\right)^2 .
\]
Thus for odd \(p,q\) a nonzero solution would imply
\[
 pq=\left(\frac{c^{(p+1)/2}d^{(q+1)/2}(z-v)}{c-d}\right)^2.             \tag{3}
\]
The right side is a square in \(F\).
A number field has only finitely many quadratic subfields, and the squarefree integers \(pq\) distinguish the fields \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{pq})\) for different unordered pairs of distinct primes.
This excludes (3) beyond finitely many pairs.
At a single exterior node the equations on constant polynomials already force both constants to vanish.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## Separation between integral solutions at fixed width

At any fixed width \(w\) every nonzero integral pair solving the two double-contact conditions satisfies
\[
                          \log H\ge p h_1+q h_2-o(L) .                   \tag{4}
\]
This estimate includes pairs with common factors or with \(D=0\).
To see uniformity, take any sequence with bounded \(\log H/L\) and pass to a subsequence with \(p/L\to s_1,\ q/L\to s_2\).
Reverse the two coefficient lists at their common maximum degree and change the sign of the second row, as in the [fixed-width exterior argument](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#exterior).
After passing to fixed support, let \(\gamma_j\) be the limit of \(L^{-1}\log\) of the largest absolute coefficient at the occupied index \(j\).
The maximum actual degree used in the reversal ensures that \(\gamma_0\) exists and is nonnegative.
For each positive value \(t=s_i\log|c|\) contributed by an exterior root of slot \(i\), let \(m\) count all the contributing labels, with their slots.
Our circle hypothesis makes \(m\le2\), and at \(m=2\) the two slots are different.

For these \(m\) labels take the value and derivative matrix (2) on two lists of \(m\) coefficients.
Its determinant is nonzero by the preceding argument.
Use the common scale \(D_0=|c_0|^{n_0}\) of any contributing label with exponent \(n_0\), replacing each node \(c^{-n}\) by \(D_0c^{-n}\).
This scales both degree-\(j\) columns by \(D_0^j\) and preserves invertibility.
The normalized inverse norm is \(\exp(o(L))\) by the [shared-radius determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#lower).
Indeed all normalized nodes have logarithmic modulus \(o(L)\), and each term of the determinant expansion after expanding integer polynomial factors has logarithmic modulus \(o(L)\), height \(O(L)\) and total absolute logarithmic modulus \(O(\log L)\) outside fixed places.
The normalized nodes involve only fixed algebraic bases to powers \(O(L)\), while the other varying factors are bounded-degree monomials in \(p,q\).
These are precisely the hypotheses of the fixed-field estimate for the nonzero sum used there.

If the first and last maximizers of \(\max_j(\gamma_j-jt)\) differed by less than \(m\), they would fit in \(m\) consecutive positions.
Keeping this block, shifting its positions to start at zero, rescaling its coefficients by the corresponding \(D_0^{-j}\) and dividing by their maximum modulus gives lists of norm one.
The omitted terms in the normalized equations are exponentially small by the strict limiting losses of those positions and the bounded width.
The shift in the derivative equation contributes only a multiple of the value equation.
The inverse estimate contradicts the normalization.
The index jump at \(t\) is therefore at least \(m\).
Summing the positive breakpoints times their index jumps gives \(\max_j\gamma_j-\gamma_0\), hence at least \(s_1h_1+s_2h_2\).
This proves (4) along every such subsequence.

<a id="cost"></a>
## Producing a coprime pair at the sharp cost

Write \(S=p h_1+q h_2\) and choose \(0<\delta<\min(\epsilon,h_1/3,h_2/3)\).
Set \(B=\lfloor\exp(S+\delta L)\rfloor\) and choose fixed \(w\) so large that
\[
           (w+1)\delta>2(e_1+e_2)\big(C(h_1+h_2)+\delta\big).
\]
For both rows take all coefficients in \([0,B]\cap\mathbb Z\) through degree \(w\).
Record the value and first Euler derivative of each graph at its own seed.
By the [integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#many) applied to the two fields separately, the logarithm of the number of images is at most
\[
                    2(e_1+e_2)\log B+2wS+o(L).
\]
Indeed the two coordinates at a conjugate \(c\) of the seed in slot \(n\in\{p,q\}\) are bounded by \(O_{w,P_1,P_2}((1+n) B\max(1,|c|)^{wn})\).
Differences from one member of a largest fiber give distinct homogeneous solutions in the coefficient sup ball of radius \(B\).
Their log count is at least \(2(w+1)\log B-2(e_1+e_2)\log B-2wS-o(L)\).
By (4) the distance between any two is at least \(\exp(S-o(L))\) in the sup norm, since the width is fixed.
Packing disjoint balls of half this radius within any subspace of dimension at most \(w+1\) bounds the log count there by \((w+1)\delta L+o(L)\).
The choice of \(w\) makes the fiber differences exceed this bound, so their span has dimension at least \(w+2\).
Projection onto the first polynomial has a nonzero kernel in this span, and any nonzero pair \((0,V)\) has nonzero reflected determinant.
By polarization, some fiber difference or sum of two differences therefore has \(D\ne0\) and \(\log H\le S+\delta L+O_w(1)\).

Each powered minimum contributes its square to \(D\) by [reciprocal elimination](Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity).
If one powered minimum divides both rows, it contributes at least its cube by the [common-factor argument](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#genuine).
Because the powered minima are distinct, this would give
\[
              2\log H\ge m(D)\ge 2S+\min(p h_1,q h_2),
\]
contradicting the chosen \(\delta\) for large \(L\).
In particular both rows are nonzero, since if one vanished then each powered minimum would divide the other by the value equations.
Divide out the primitive polynomial gcd and joint scalar content.
The graph contacts and nonvanishing of \(D\) persist, and each resulting row has one-norm at most \(2^wH\) by the same common-factor argument.
This proves the strict upper estimate in (1).
The two powered squares in \(D\), together with \(|D|\le H^2\) on the unit circle, give the exact lower estimate for every qualifying pair.
For growing widths the fixed width at each \(\epsilon\) is eventually admissible, which gives the uniform minimum assertion.
