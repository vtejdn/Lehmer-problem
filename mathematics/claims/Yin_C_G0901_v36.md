# The least height of a rational parameter on the integral circle

2026-11-24T21:21 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## A linear cost in the degree of the trace field

In the [quadratic norm-one family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#statement) let \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)=F(i)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(t=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), and \(y=(\beta-\beta^{-1})/i\).
Thus \(t,y\in\mathcal O_F\), \(t^2+y^2=4\), \([L:F]=2\), and \(\beta\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit.
The stereographic parameter and its inverse are
\[
             u=\frac{y}{2+t}=-i\frac{\beta-1}{\beta+1},
             \qquad \beta=\frac{1+iu}{1-iu}.
\]
Every rational birational parameter on this conic is \(M(u)\) for some \(M\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)\), since two coordinates generating the same rational function field in one variable have transition maps of degree one.
As \(u\in F\) and \(L=\mathbb Q(u,i)\), we have \(\mathbb Q(u)=F\).
Hence every such parameter generates \(F\); it is finite and nonzero as soon as \([F:\mathbb Q]>1\).

Put \(d=[L:\mathbb Q]\), let \(h=h(\beta)\) be the absolute logarithmic Weil height, and let \(G=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2\) be Catalan's constant.
Uniformly for \(h\to0,\ d\to\infty\),
\[
 \min_{M\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)} h(M(u))
     =\frac{2G}{\pi}
       +O\!\left(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)}
                   +\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}{d}}\right).                  \tag{1}
\]
The original parameter \(u\) attains the same asymptotic value.
The implied constant in (1) is independent of the conic point and of all rational coefficients in the change of parameter.
In particular the least total logarithmic Mahler measure among all these parameters is \((2G/\pi+o(1))[F:\mathbb Q]\).
This applies to any proposed shrinking sequence of integral circle units, since their degrees diverge by bounded-degree finiteness.
It also applies when \(m(\beta)=d h\) stays bounded and the degree diverges.
A rational parametrization using parameters of bounded total measure cannot produce either kind of sequence.
The argument allows nonintegral parameters; it concerns rational maps of degree one between parameters.

<a id="arch"></a>
## The height error at the two Gaussian embeddings

Write an invertible rational change as \(M(u)=(au+b)/(cu+d_0)\), with a primitive integral matrix.
In the \(\beta\)-coordinate its two linear forms have coefficients \(b-ai,b+ai\) and \(d_0-ci,d_0+ci\).
Divide these four numbers by a greatest common Gaussian divisor \(g\).
Their ideal is stable under conjugation, so \(\epsilon=\bar g/g\) is a Gaussian unit.
For some \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[i]\) the primitive forms are
\[
                  U(Z)=AZ+\epsilon\bar A,\qquad
                  V(Z)=BZ+\epsilon\bar B .
\]
The determinant is nonzero, so \(A,B\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{Im}(A\bar B)\ne0\).
Define
\[
 J(A,B)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\,d\eta(z),
\]
where \(\eta\) is normalized arc length.
A rotation absorbs \(\epsilon\), and complex conjugation of both forms leaves this integral unchanged.

Fix the forms for now and consider the \(n=d/2\) conjugates \(z_j\) of \(\beta\) above one of the two embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(i)\).
They are distinct roots of a monic polynomial integral over \(\mathbb Z[i]\) with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(i)\), so those coefficients are Gaussian integers.
At either embedding the absolute value of its nonzero discriminant is at least one.
For \(n\ge2\), put \(\rho=1-1/n\), and set \(a_j=z_j\) inside the closed unit disk and \(a_j=1/\bar z_j\) outside.
For \(j\ne l\),
\[
 |z_j-z_l|\le\max(1,|z_j|)\max(1,|z_l|)\,|1-a_j\bar a_l|.
\]
For points on the same side use \(|a-b|\le|1-a\bar b|\) in the disk; for points on opposite sides there is equality.
Also \(|1-v|\le 2|1-\rho v|/(1+\rho)\) on the closed disk, by the maximum modulus principle or the circle inequality.
Take the product over ordered \(j\ne l\) and use the discriminant lower bound.
Adding the diagonal terms \(-\log(1-\rho|a_j|^2)\le\log n\), then expanding the absolutely convergent logarithm series, gives
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k}{k}
            \left|\frac1n\sum_j a_j^k\right|^2
 \le\frac{2(n-1)}{n^2}\sum_j\log^+|z_j|
       +\log\frac{2}{1+\rho}+\frac{\log n}{n}
 \ll h+\frac{\log(2n)}n.                                               \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\sum_j\log^+|z_j|\le d h\) and \(\sum_j|\log|z_j||\le2d h\), since \(\beta\) is a unit and the sums over the full field have these values.

On the circle let \(f(z)=\log\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\).
The forms have no common zero.
Thus \(f\) is continuous and piecewise smooth, with bounded one-sided derivatives and finitely many pieces: changes of the larger form occur at the zeros of a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most one.
If its two squared moduli agree identically, either expression suffices where it is positive.
Integration by parts on the pieces gives Fourier coefficients \(O_M((1+|k|)^{-2})\).
For \(K=\min(n,\lceil h^{-1/2}\rceil)\) discard the Fourier tail at cost \(O_M(1/K)\).
For each retained frequency the difference between the mean of the phases \((z_j/|z_j|)^k\) and that of \(a_j^k\) is at most \(k n^{-1}\sum_j|\log|z_j||\).
After Fourier weighting this costs \(O_M(h\log(2K))\) for the positive frequencies and their conjugates.
For the remaining means use Cauchy–Schwarz in (2) and \(\sum_{1\le k\le K}k\rho^{-k}|\widehat f(k)|^2\ll_M1\), since \(K\le n\).
The resulting phase-average error is \(O_M(\sqrt{h+\log(2n)/n})\) for \(0<h\le1/2\).

The function \(\log\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)-\log\max(1,|z|)\) is bounded on the sphere, by invertibility of the matrix of the forms.
On the fixed annulus \(\mathrm e^{-1}\le|z|\le\mathrm e\) it is Lipschitz, since the maximum of the two moduli has a positive minimum there.
Comparison to the phase therefore costs \(O_M(n^{-1}\sum_j|\log|z_j||)=O_M(h)\), using boundedness outside the annulus.
Restoring the subtracted logarithm adds at most \(2h\) to each mean.
At the conjugate Gaussian embedding use the conjugate forms and the same argument.
Consequently the archimedean part of the normalized projective height of \([U(\beta):V(\beta)]\) equals \(J(A,B)+O_M(\sqrt{h+\log(2d)/d})\).

## Bounding the common ideal without a ramification hypothesis

Put \(I=(U(\beta),V(\beta))\mathcal O_L\) and let \(\Delta\in\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}\) be the determinant of the two primitive forms.
The finite-place contribution to their projective height is \(-d^{-1}\log N_{L/\mathbb Q}I\).
The leading-coefficient linear combination of the forms shows that \(\Delta\in I\), so only primes of \(\mathbb Z[i]\) dividing \(\Delta\) can contribute.
Take an integer \(k\ge1\) divisible by the multiplicative group orders of all their residue fields, with \(k=1\) if there are no such primes.

For a Gaussian prime \(\pi\mid\Delta\) and prime \(\mathfrak P\mid\pi\) in \(L\) with \(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)>0\), at least one of the two leading coefficients is a \(\pi\)-unit.
Otherwise at least one constant coefficient is a \(\pi\)-unit by primitivity, and that form cannot vanish modulo \(\mathfrak P\).
The zero \(s\in\mathbb Q(i)\) of a form with unit leading coefficient is a local unit at \(\pi\), since its residue equals that of the unit \(\beta\).
Thus \(s^k\equiv1\bmod\pi\), and
\[
 v_{\mathfrak P}(\beta^k-1)
 \ge\min(v_{\mathfrak P}(\beta-s),v_{\mathfrak P}(\pi))
 \ge\min(v_{\mathfrak P}(I),v_{\mathfrak P}(\pi)).
\]
Since \(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)\le v_{\mathfrak P}(\Delta)
 =v_\pi(\Delta)v_{\mathfrak P}(\pi)\) with \(v_\pi(\pi)=1\), it follows that
\(v_{\mathfrak P}(I)\le v_\pi(\Delta)v_{\mathfrak P}(\beta^k-1)\).
Here the local valuations in \(L\) are integral normalized; they may have any ramification over \(\mathbb Q(i)\).

Multiplying these bounds gives \(\log N_{L/\mathbb Q} I\ll_M\log|N_{L/\mathbb Q}(1-\beta^k)|\).
The [degree-explicit bound for integer norms](Yin_C_G0901_v26.md#integral) bounds the last quantity divided by \(d\) by
\(\log(N+1)/N+(N+2)kh/3\) for every \(N\ge1\).
Take \(N=\lceil\sqrt{\log(2/h)/h}\rceil\) to bound the finite-place error by \(O_M(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)})\).
Together the two height estimates give (1)'s error for each fixed matrix, with main term \(J(A,B)\).

<a id="minimum"></a>
## The exact minimum of the archimedean integral

Applying the circle mean formula for the logarithm of a linear polynomial twice gives
\[
 J(A,B)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max\bigl(|A+zB|,\ |A+\bar zB|\bigr)\,d\eta(z).
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Indeed one first writes \(\log\max(|U|,|V|)=\int\log|U+zV|\,d\eta(z)\), then averages the resulting linear form in the original circle variable.
Interchange of integrals follows by applying Tonelli to a constant upper bound minus the logarithm; the maximum of the two coefficient moduli in the reversed integration is positive everywhere by invertibility.
In particular \(J(A,B)\ge\log\max(|A|,|B|)\), by keeping one entry of (3) and applying the same mean formula.

The forms are primitive Gaussian forms.
If either row length \(|A|,|B|\) is at least two, \(J(A,B)\ge\log2\).
For lengths below two, their squared lengths can only be one or two, since three is not a sum of two integer squares.
Both cannot be two, as \(1+i\) would divide all four coefficients.
If both are one, the two Gaussian units \(A,B\) are at right angles since their ratio is nonreal.
Rotation of the original circle parameter gives
\[
 J(A,B)=\frac4\pi\int_0^{\pi/4}\log(2\cos v)\,dv
             =\frac{2G}{\pi}<\log2 .
\]
The equality follows by integrating the Abel-regularized cosine series for \(\log(2\cos v)\), or its convergent series on this interval.
The identity parameter has these two row lengths.

If the lengths are one and \(\sqrt2\), the angle between \(A,B\) has sine and cosine of absolute value \(1/\sqrt2\).
For \(z=e^{it}\), the squared maximum in (3) is \(3\pm2\cos t+2|\sin t|\).
In the unit-length case the corresponding value is \(2+2|\sin t|\).
Pair \(t\) with \(\pi-t\) modulo \(2\pi\) and put \(s=|\sin t|\).
The product of the two mixed squared maxima is \(5+12s+8s^2\), strictly greater than \((2+2s)^2\).
Thus the mixed case has strictly larger integral.
This proves the minimum \(2G/\pi\) for all fixed primitive matrices.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## Allowing the rational coefficients to vary

It remains to justify minimization over the unbounded set of rational matrices.
For \(d/2\ge3\) choose three distinct conjugates \(z_j\) of \(\beta\) over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), and write \(v_j=U(z_j)/V(z_j)\).
When \([F:\mathbb Q]>1\) all these images are finite, and invertibility gives three distinct \(v_j\).
The three rows \((z_j,1,-v_j z_j,-v_j)\) have rank three.
Indeed any pair of linear polynomials satisfying their three evaluations has zero cross-product with \(U,V\), since that polynomial of degree at most two vanishes at the three distinct \(z_j\).
Coprimality and invertibility of \(U,V\) then make the pair a scalar multiple of \(U,V\).
The signed three-by-three minors consequently give the projective coefficient vector of \(U,V\).
At the archimedean places the maximum of these minors is at most \(3!\) times the product of the three maximum row norms, and at finite places the factorial can be dropped.
The height of row \(j\) is \(h(z_j)+h(v_j)\), by its four coordinates.
Thus the coefficient vector in \(\mathbb P^3(\mathbb Q(i))\) has height at most \(\log6+3(h+h(M(u)))\).
Every matrix with \(h\le1/2\) and \(h(M(u))\le1\) therefore belongs, projectively in the \(\beta\)-coordinate, to one fixed finite set by bounded-degree height finiteness.
The change from the \(u\)-coordinate is invertible, so the same holds for the original rational transformations.
The constants in the previous estimates can now be maximized over this finite set.
The identity gives height less than one for small \(h\) and large \(d\), so the finite minimum gives (1) for all matrices.
Multiplication by \([F:\mathbb Q]\) proves the total-measure assertion.

<a id="example"></a>
## An integral sequence attaining the parameter cost

For a nontorsion example of bounded total measure, start with a unit having one conjugate on the circle.
One such number is a circle root of \(X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1\): its trace satisfies \(T^2-T-3=0\), with one root in \((-2,2)\) and the other greater than two.
The quadratic defining the unit over its trace is irreducible because its discriminant is negative at the first real place, so the exterior root really is a conjugate.
The [circle-embedding construction](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) therefore gives a nontorsion \(\gamma\) of modulus one with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\subseteq\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
For each \(j\ge1\) choose any \(j\)-th root \(\beta_j\) of \(\gamma\).
It is an algebraic unit of modulus one, and its field contains \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Ordinary complex conjugation in this field sends \(\beta_j\) to its inverse, fixes precisely its trace field and acts by inversion on \(\zeta_{12}\).
The [integral basis calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) puts these points in the stated conic family.
They have \(h(\beta_j)=h(\gamma)/j\to0\) and \(m(\beta_j)\le m(\gamma)\), since their degrees grow by a factor of at most \(j\).
Their degrees diverge by bounded-degree height finiteness, so they attain the parameter asymptotic (1).

Their positive total measures stay bounded away from zero.
Indeed for \(b=[\mathbb Q(\beta_j):\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\) the unit \(v=N_{\mathbb Q(\beta_j)/\mathbb Q(\gamma)}\beta_j\) satisfies \(v^j=\gamma^b\), and \(h(v)=b\,h(\beta_j)>0\).
Consequently \(m(\beta_j)=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\), and the positive measures of units in the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) have a positive minimum by bounded-degree finiteness.
The example and (1) quantify the cost in the parameter without deciding whether the unrestricted positive Mahler measures have a uniform lower bound.
