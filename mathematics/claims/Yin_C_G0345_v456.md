# Exact primitive height for double contacts at a Salem number and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum-width height

Let \(\delta\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2s\ge4\), and let \(P_1,P_2\) be the monic minimal polynomials of \(\delta,\delta^2\).
Put \(m=2d\), \(h=\log\delta\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
At all sufficiently large integers \(n\), the primitive integral row \(A_n\) of degree at most \(m\) satisfying
\[
      (P_1P_2)^2\mid A_n(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nm+1}A_n(X^{-n})
\]
is unique up to sign.
It uses all \(m+1\) positions and its numerator is nonzero at every prescribed powered root.
This is the minimum formal width, and
\[
                    \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_n\|_1}{n}
                         =(6d-4)h .
\]
The constant concerns each fixed Salem seed before any coefficient cutoff.

In the splitting field \(K\), choose one representative \(x_i\) from each inverse pair of conjugates of \(\delta\).
Use independent \(z_i\) for \(x_i^n\) and \(N\) for \(n\).
At each site \(y=z_i^k\), \(k=1,2\), with label \(c=\varepsilon x_i^k\), the two matrix rows in column \(l=0,\ldots,m\) have entries
\[
            y^l+cy^{m-l},\qquad Nly^l+c(1+N(m-l))y^{m-l}.
\]
The gcd in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\) of all signed maximal minors \(C_l\) is, up to a constant in \(K^\times\),
\[
                              \prod_{i=1}^s z_i^2(z_i-1)^4.        \tag{1}
\]
The proof determines this gcd after the square substitution.

<a id="ends"></a>
## Extremal exponents and the specialization at zero

The \(x_i\) are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, since an embedding carrying any one representative to \(\delta\) puts all the others on the unit circle.
Thus the \(2d\) elements \(x_i^{\pm1},x_i^{\pm2}\) are distinct with pairwise nontorsion quotients, and give precisely the roots of \(P_1P_2\).
The [minimum-support theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement) yields the claimed minimum width, uniqueness, support and genuineness.
Its [height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) has one residue class because the \(x_i\) freely generate the group of these roots.

For weights \(u_i\) on the variables \(z_i\), the formal site weights before the square substitution are \(u_i,2u_i\).
In any determinant term column \(l\) assigned to a site of weight \(v\) has weight at most \((m/2)v+|l-m/2|\,|v|\).
The distances from \(m/2\) in the columns consist of two copies of each integer from \(m/2\) down to one, and one zero.
The upper rearrangement bound is attained by deleting the central column, assigning the two columns with greatest distance to the site of greatest absolute weight, and proceeding in decreasing order.
Indeed when the \(d\) site weights have distinct nonzero absolute values this gives unique complementary column pairs and unique choices of maximal exponents.
The two row orderings within each pair have determinant \(\pm c\) in their leading coefficients, since the larger or smaller common exponent has derivative multipliers differing by one after the value multipliers are extracted.
Thus the center minor has a unique maximal monomial before substitution, with coefficient the signed product of the labels, independent of \(N\).
No other monomial maps to its image under substitution because such a monomial would have equal weight.
This is the [extremal column calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) also at \(N=0\).
By continuity of these finite support maxima, for the axis weights \(u=\pm e_i\) the rearrangement bounds remain attained both formally and at \(N=0\).
They are respectively \(3m+(3m-2)=6m-2\) and \(-3m+(3m-2)=-2\), so the raw exponents in \(z_i\) range from \(2\) to \(6m-2\).

Fix \(i\), write \(z=z_i\), and choose the other \(z_j\) algebraically independent over \(K\).
The two endpoint coefficients of the center minor in \(z\) at \(N=0\) survive on this slice.
At \(N=0\) the two rows at each site separate into evaluations at \(y,y^{-1}\) through an invertible row transformation for \(y\ne0\).
Thus at finite nonzero \(z\) only coincidences among the \(m\) evaluation nodes can give simultaneous vanishing.
The internal coincidences among \(z,z^{-1},z^2,z^{-2}\) are at \(1,-1,\pm\mathrm i\) and the primitive cube roots.
The external coincidences with the two sites of \(j\ne i\) and their inverses occur at
\[
           z^2=z_j^{\pm1},\qquad z=z_j^{\pm2},\qquad
           z=\pm z_j^{\pm1}.
\]
Algebraic independence gives no coincidences between these listed groups or different \(j\), with the positive and negative choices in the last group considered separately.

<a id="ordinary"></a>
## Two sites meeting away from the fixed points of inversion

We work along a putative sequence of rank loss with \(N\ne0\), \(N\to0\) and \(z\) tending to a listed coincidence.
At two meeting sites \(w,y=w e^\theta\), write \(b,c\) for their respective labels, orienting the first site by inversion if necessary.
Inversion exchanges the two evaluations, replaces \(b\) by \(b^{-1}\), and replaces the centered derivative equation by the new value equation minus the old derivative equation divided by \(b\).
The base \(w\) may depend on the sequence, tending to \(w_0\ne0,\pm1\).
On polynomials \(A\) of degree at most \(m\), define the row functionals
\[
 U(\xi)=(we^\xi)^{-m/2}A(we^\xi),\qquad
 V(\xi)=(we^\xi)^{m/2}A((we^\xi)^{-1}).
\]
Primes indicate the independent Taylor variable \(\xi\), holding all sites and the polynomial fixed.
After removing the centering monomial and \(Nm/2\) times the value row, the moving pair is
\[
 f=U(\theta)+cV(\theta),\qquad
 g=N\big(U'(\theta)+cV'(\theta)\big)+cV(\theta).
\]
Modulo the two rows of label \(b\) at \(\xi=0\) one has exactly
\[
     U_0=N(U_1+bV_1),\qquad V_0=-N(U_1+bV_1)/b,
\]
where subscripts denote derivatives at zero.
Set \(a=1-c/b\).
Eliminating \(U_0,V_0\) gives representatives of \(f,g\) whose first-order coefficients on \(U_1,V_1\) are the rows
\[
          (Na+\theta,Nba+c\theta),\qquad(Na,c\theta),              \tag{2}
\]
with errors \(O(|N\theta|+|\theta|^2)\) in the full finite-dimensional space of row functionals.
Their leading determinant is \(c\theta^2-ba^2N^2\).
Write \(r=\max(|N|,|\theta|)\) and pass to a subsequence on which \(N/r,\theta/r\) converge.
If the leading determinant divided by \(r^2\) has nonzero limit, the four rows can be transformed to four convergent rows spanning in the limit the values and first derivatives at \(w_0,w_0^{-1}\).
Indeed the two original \(b\)-rows give the values in the limit, and the two eliminated rows divided by \(r\) give independent combinations of \(U_1,V_1\).
The four limiting conditions are independent by interpolation at the two distinct points.
The exceptional directions in \(\theta/N\) are the two numbers \(\pm(q-q^{-1})\), with \(q^2=b/c\), when \(b\ne c\).

We also need the test when this is the last pair to impose.
Suppose the other \(m-2\) rows, including the two \(b\)-rows, have after row transformations independent limits whose kernel is spanned by \(H,ZH,Z^2H\), for a monic reciprocal polynomial \(H\) of degree \(m-2\) with simple zeros \(w_0,w_0^{-1}\).
Solving at an invertible limiting minor gives kernel bases converging to this chosen triple.
Every Taylor derivative used below is bounded on a fixed small disk, since the polynomial degrees are bounded and their coefficients converge.
The exact identities for \(U_0,V_0\) hold on each kernel before taking limits.

Now evaluate \(U,V\) on these three basis polynomials, so their derivatives are row vectors.
At the limit \(U,V\) are the same scalar \(Y^{1-m/2}H(Y)\), \(Y=w_0e^\xi\), times \((Y^{-1},1,Y)\) and \((Y,1,Y^{-1})\).
Thus \(U_1,V_1\) are independent in the limit and can be used as the first two vectors of a convergent basis of the row space, with a fixed third vector.
Projection to its third coordinate kills \(U_1,V_1\) exactly.
Its limit is proportional to \((1,-(w_0+w_0^{-1}),1)\), under which the derivatives of both triples have the same nonzero image, proportional to \(w_0-w_0^{-1}\).
Since the scalar has a simple zero, the projected \(U_2,V_2\) tend to the same nonzero \(\alpha\).

If \(f,g\) are dependent on this kernel, (2) gives
\(c\theta^2-ba^2N^2=O(r^3)\), whence \(\theta/N\to t=q-q^{-1}=aq\) on a subsequence for a choice \(q^2=b/c\).
Here \(b\ne c\) implies \(q\ne\pm1\).
The first coordinate of \(f/N\) tends to \(a(1+q)\ne0\), so \(g=\lambda f\) forces \(\lambda\to1/(1+q)\).
In the third coordinate \(f=\alpha(1+c)\theta^2/2+o(|N|^2)\) and \(g=\alpha(1+c)N\theta+c\alpha\theta^2/2+o(|N|^2)\).
Consequently the third coordinate of \((g-\lambda f)/(\alpha N^2t)\) tends to
\[
                  (1+c)+\frac{t}{2}\left(c-\frac{1+c}{1+q}\right)
                      =\frac{(1+q)(c+1/q)}{2}.
\]
It is nonzero when \(bc\ne1\).
This proves full rank for the last pair in every direction under these hypotheses.
The argument uses convergence of the kernel bases alone and allows the base \(w\) and the other sites to move.

<a id="inversion"></a>
## A site meeting its own inverse

At a site \(y=\xi e^\theta\) with \(\xi=\pm1\) let \(v(\theta)=y^{-m/2}A(y)\).
The rows at label \(c\) are \(f=v(\theta)+c v(-\theta)\) and \(g=N(v'(\theta)-c v'(-\theta))+c v(-\theta)\).
With \(r=\max(|N|,|\theta|)\), subtracting \(c f/(1+c)\) from \(g\) gives
\[
       \left((1-c)N-\frac{2c}{1+c}\theta\right)v'(0)+O(r^2).
\]
Here \(c\ne\pm1\).
Except in the direction \(\theta/N\to(c^{-1}-c)/2\), the original value row and the difference row divided by \(r\) therefore tend along a subsequence to independent conditions of value and first derivative at \(\xi\).

If this is the last pair and the other \(m-2\) rows have independent limits with kernel \(H,ZH,Z^2H\), where \(H(\xi)\ne0\), take a converging kernel basis as above.
The moving row vectors \(v(0),v'(0),v''(0)\) are a uniformly invertible basis, by the three independent jets of \(1,Z,Z^2\) at \(\xi\).
Dependence gives \(g=\lambda f\), where the first coordinate in that basis is
\(\lambda=c/(1+c)+O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3)\).
The second coordinate requires
\[
 (1-c)N-\frac{2c}{1+c}\theta=O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3),
\]
so \(\theta/N\to(c^{-1}-c)/2\ne0\).
In the third coordinate the pure quadratic terms cancel, leaving
\((1+c)N\theta=O(|N||\theta|^2+|\theta|^3)\), a contradiction after division by \(N^2\).
This proves full rank for the last inversion pair.

<a id="simultaneous"></a>
## Resolving all collisions except the common fixed point

At the three external groups \(z^2=z_j^{\pm1}\), \(z=z_j^{\pm2}\), \(z=-z_j^{\pm1}\), precisely one pair of distinct sites meets up to inversion.
All remaining evaluation conditions after omitting the moving site are independent in the limit, and their kernel is \(H\langle1,Z,Z^2\rangle\) where \(H\) is the product of the corresponding linear factors.
It is reciprocal and has simple zeros at the two points of the fixed site.
Here \(b\ne c\) and \(bc\ne1\) by independence of the different representatives, so the last-pair test in the [ordinary collision argument](#ordinary) applies.
At a primitive cube root the two sites \(z,z^2\) meet after inversion, and the same argument uses the varying base \(w=z^{-2}\) and the moving site \(y=z\).
The labels are \(b=\varepsilon x_i^{-2}, c=\varepsilon x_i\), which satisfy both inequalities by nontorsion.
At \(z=\pm\mathrm i\) only the square meets its own inverse, and the [inversion test](#inversion) applies with \(H(-1)\ne0\).

At \(z=z_j^{\pm1}\), orient the other representative as \(x_j'=x_j^{\pm1}\) and write \(w=z_j^{\pm1}\).
There are two simultaneous ordinary collisions, one at \(w,w^{-1}\) and one at \(w^2,w^{-2}\), all four points distinct.
Their logarithmic separations are \(\theta=\log(z/w)\) and \(2\theta\).
With \(q^2=x_j'/x_i\ne1\), the exceptional directions for the two blocks in their respective separations are \(\pm(q-q^{-1})\) and \(\pm(q^2-q^{-2})\).
They cannot both occur, since \(2(q-q^{-1})=\pm(q^2-q^{-2})\) would force \(q+q^{-1}=\pm2\) and \(q^2=1\).
After taking a convergent projective direction, choose a nonexceptional block.
The first part of the [ordinary collision argument](#ordinary) transforms its rows into a basis with limiting value and first derivative at each of its two inverse points.
Keep those four rows, the two fixed rows at the remaining collision, and all uncollided rows.
The \(m-2\) limiting conditions are independent by polynomial interpolation with the indicated multiplicities.
Their common kernel has the required form with reciprocal \(H\), double factors at the two resolved points and simple factors at the two points of the remaining collision.
The last-pair test rules out every rank-loss sequence in this case.

At \(z=-1\) the ordinary site meets its inverse at \(-1\) and the square meets its inverse at \(1\).
Their separations from the respective fixed points are \(\theta=\log(-z)\) and \(2\theta\).
The exceptional directions for labels \(\varepsilon x_i,\varepsilon x_i^2\) cannot both occur, since this would imply
\(2(x_i^{-1}-x_i)=x_i^{-2}-x_i^2\), hence \(x_i+x_i^{-1}=2\).
Choose a nonexceptional block and transform its rows by the first part of the [inversion test](#inversion).
The limit imposes a double zero at its fixed point and ordinary zeros at every other external evaluation node.
The corresponding \(H\) has degree \(m-2\) and is nonzero at the unresolved fixed point, so the last inversion pair has full rank there.

<a id="center"></a>
## The four rows at one

At \(z=1\) omit both moving sites.
The other \(m-4\) independent limiting evaluations give the kernel \(H\langle 1,Z,Z^2,Z^3,Z^4\rangle\), with \(H(1)\ne0\).
Near \(N=0\) it has an analytic basis dual to the five jets at zero of \(v(t)=e^{-mt/2}A(e^t)\).
The basis functions are \(t^j/j!+O(t^5)\), \(0\le j\le4\), with derivative errors \(O(t^4)\) uniformly.
An invertible external minor reduces the ideal of maximal minors to that of the four rows restricted to this kernel, by invertible analytic row and column operations.

Write \(\theta=\log z,\ c_k=\varepsilon x_i^k,\ a_k=(1-c_k)/(1+c_k),\ r_k=(1-a_k^2)/2\) for \(k=1,2\).
At \(k\theta\) take the centered inversion rows above, replace \(g\) by \(g-c_k f/(1+c_k)\), and divide both rows by \(1+c_k\).
When \(\theta N\ne0\), scale column \(j\) by \(j!/\theta^j\) and each derivative row by \(\theta/N\).
For bounded \(t=\theta/N\), the resulting value and derivative entries differ by \(O((1+|t|)|\theta|^{5-j})\) from
\[
  (k^j,jk^{j-1})\quad (j\ \mathrm{even}),\qquad
  (a_k k^j,ja_k k^{j-1}-t r_k k^j)\quad (j\ \mathrm{odd}).
\]
Put \(a=a_1,\ b=a_2,\ r=r_1,\ R=r_2\).
At \(t=0\) the minor for columns \(0,1,2,3\) is up to sign \(32b^2-41ab+8a^2\), by subtracting the two value rows and expanding on column zero.
For \(T=x_i+x_i^{-1}\) this determinant divided by \(a^2\) is \((-T^2+46T+128)/T^2\) at the positive sign and \((-T^2-50T+32)/(T+2)^2\) at the negative sign.
The former numerator is positive at every unit-circle conjugate trace in \([-2,2]\), and the latter is negative at the exterior trace above two.
The denominators are nonzero by nontorsion.
For each fixed small nonzero \(N\), undoing the scalings contributes a factor proportional to \(N^2\theta^{\sum j-2}\) to each four-column minor.
The smallest possible exponent is four, attained with the nonvanishing determinant just calculated.
This gives common multiplicity exactly four at \(z=1\), both on these slices and polynomially by generic specialization.

For every finite limiting \(t\), the even columns \(0,2,4\) have a unique annihilating row combination up to scale, \((1,3/4,-1,3/8)\) in value and derivative order.
Vanishing on both odd columns would give \(14a-13b=6t(r+R)\) and \(13a-14b=3t(r+4R)\), forcing
\((5b-4a)r+(10a-8b)R=0\).
At the positive sign its left side is \(2a^3(1-a^2)(6-3a^2-a^4)/(1+a^2)^3\), nonzero at the embedding \(x_i=\delta\).
At the negative sign it is \((1-a^2)/(2a^3)\ne0\).
For \(|t|\to\infty\), retain the column scalings and omit the derivative-row scaling.
The limiting derivative rows are zero on even columns and \(-r_k k^j\) on odd columns.
Together with the value rows they span the evaluations at \(1,-1,2,-2\), which are independent on columns \(0,1,2,3\).
Thus there is no off-divisor sequence of common zeros tending to \((N,z)=(0,1)\).

## Removing the common divisor and averaging over the embeddings

Divide the raw tuple by \(\prod_j z_j^2(z_j-1)^4\), using the [endpoint exponents](#ends) and the [order at one](#center).
On the slice above, the divided tuple has surviving constant and top coefficients in \(z\) at \(N=0\), since the dividing polynomial has nonzero endpoint coefficients after its power of \(z\) is extracted.
The common maximal \(z\)-degree after division is \(6m-8\).
Homogenize to this degree on the projective \(z\)-line.
If there are common zeros on this line for arbitrarily small nonzero \(N\), a sequence has a limiting point at \(N=0\).
The nonzero endpoints exclude zero and infinity.
At every finite nonzero point apart from the listed collisions the separated evaluation rows have full rank.
The preceding local tests exclude all listed limits, including \(z=1\), where exact order four at each sufficiently small nonzero \(N\) also excludes zeros on the divisor itself.
Hence the divided tuple has no common zero on this slice for all sufficiently small nonzero \(N\).

If a common polynomial factor of the divided tuple had positive \(z_i\)-degree, algebraic independence of the other specialized variables would preserve its leading coefficient as a nonzero polynomial in \(N\).
Choosing a sufficiently small nonzero \(N\) outside the finite exceptional set would give a nonconstant common divisor of the univariate tuple and hence a common zero.
This proves the absence of factors of positive degree in any \(z_i\).
A common factor in \(K[N]\) of the divided tuple also divides the raw center minor, which has a coefficient in \(K^\times\) by the generic extremal term in the [support calculation](#ends).
This proves (1).

The fully reduced tuple therefore has coordinate width \(6m-8\) for every \(i\).
At an embedding sending \(x_i\) to \(\delta\), the maximum monomial weight is \(h\) times the largest \(z_i\)-exponent, since every other representative is on the unit circle.
At one sending \(x_i\) to \(\delta^{-1}\), it is \(-h\) times the smallest \(z_i\)-exponent.
These two classes for each \(i\) have relative size \(1/d\) each by transitivity, and exhaust the embeddings as \(i\) varies.
The [projective height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) now gives \(s(6m-8)h/d=(6d-4)h\).
In particular the fixed-seed rates are greater than \(2(6d-4)/(\log(3d))^3\) by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), and this degree bound tends to infinity.
