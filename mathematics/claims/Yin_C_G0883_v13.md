# A finite period search for the sparsest admissible boundary multiple

<a id="search"></a>
## Bounding the periods of a minimizing profile

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal integer polynomial of even degree \(d\ge2\) with \(P(1)\ne0\).
Minimize the number of nonzero coefficients of
\[
       F_i=P\,\frac{\prod_{j=1}^i (X^{h_j}-1)}{X-1},\qquad
                   1\le h_1\le\cdots\le h_i,
\]
over all nonempty [boundary period lists](Yin_C_G0883_v8.md#cycles).
Given an integer budget \(2\le k<|\operatorname{supp}P|\), the following finite search detects whether the minimum is at most \(k\) and finds a minimizing list if it is.
Let \(r_+\) count the positive real roots of \(P\) with multiplicity.
Use at most \(k-r_+\) periods, with
\[
 h_1\le\lfloor(d+k)/2\rfloor,\qquad
 h_{i+1}\le\deg F_i=d-1+\sum_{j\le i}h_j .
\]
At each new period \(h\) it is necessary that at most \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) of the first \(h\) coefficients of \(F_i\) are nonzero, counting from its constant term.
At the first period use the coefficients of \(P/(1-X)\) for this test.
Test \(F_i\) itself before testing extensions.
In particular the maximum degree of a minimizing multiple within the budget is at most \(2^{k-r_+-1}(d-1+\lfloor(d+k)/2\rfloor)\) whenever such a multiple exists.
For fixed \(k\) every period and the searched degree are \(O_k(d)\).
The choice \(h_1=1\) gives the comparison polynomial \(P\) when no saving is possible.

<a id="bounds"></a>
## The first period and a gap between successive periods

Consider any minimizing list producing at most \(k\) terms.
The multiplicity at one from its boundary factor is the list length minus one.
Descartes' rule therefore gives \(r_++i-1\le k-1\) for the completed list of length \(i\).

Write \(S_b=\sum_{a=0}^b[X^a]P\).
Before the first period the coefficients of the completed multiple are, up to one common sign, \(S_b\).
Reciprocity gives \(S_b+S_{d-1-b}=P(1)\ne0\) for \(0\le b<d\).
If \(h_1>d\), its first \(d\) coefficients contain at least \(d/2\) nonzero entries, and by signed reciprocity its last \(d\) contain as many.
These ranges are disjoint.
The coefficient at position \(d\) is \(\pm P(1)\) and belongs to neither range because the degree is at least \(d+h_1-1\ge2d\).
This would give at least \(d+1>k\) entries.
Thus \(h_1\le d\); its initial and final blocks of length \(h_1\) are disjoint.
When \(2h_1>d\), the interval \([d-h_1,h_1-1]\) of length \(2h_1-d\) lies in the initial block and is stable under \(b\mapsto d-1-b\).
At least half its partial sums are nonzero.
Counting their reflected copies at the far end gives \(k\ge2h_1-d\), proving the first bound.

If the next period \(h\) exceeded \(\deg F_i\), multiplying by all the remaining binomials would leave an intact copy of \(F_i\) at each end, up to sign.
Indeed the first nonempty subset sum is at least \(h\), and every proper subset sum is separated from the sum of all remaining periods by at least \(h\).
The two copies would be disjoint.
Truncating the period list at \(i\) would strictly improve a minimizer, which rules this out.
For \(h\le\deg F_i\) the low \(h\) coefficients are likewise preserved up to sign, and the high \(h\) coefficients of the final multiple are their reflection up to sign.
The two intervals are disjoint because the final degree is at least \(\deg F_i+h\).
This proves the pruning rule and at most doubles the degree at each extension.
These are arithmetic bounds on admissible multiples and do not control the volume cost of realizing a candidate.

## Exact minima at degrees eight and ten

Put \(P_8=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\), and let \(L\) denote [Lehmer's polynomial](Yin_C_G0904_v5.md#record).
The minimum admissible support is five for \(P_8(X),P_8(-X)\) and \(L(-X)\).
The first two already have five terms, and the [length-three cycle](Yin_C_G0883_v8.md#partial) achieves five for \(L(-X)\).
For comparison the known minimum for \(L(X)\) is [six](Yin_C_G0883_v8.md#lehmer).

Here is a finite integer calculation excluding support at most four.
For reproducibility, the entries below give the number of profiles inspected at each length and the smallest support found at that length, using the following recurrence.
At the first step use all \(1\le h\le (d+4)/2\) without the optional prefix test and form \(F_1=P(1+X+\cdots+X^{h-1})\).
At every nonempty node with coefficient list \(a_0,\ldots,a_e\) and last period \(v\), count the nonzero entries.
For each \(v\le h\le e\) with at most two nonzero entries among \(a_0,\ldots,a_{h-1}\), take the child whose \(i\)-th coefficient is \(a_{i-h}-a_i\) for \(0\le i\le e+h\), using zero outside \([0,e]\).
Stop at length four, which suffices without using any positive-root count.

| Polynomial | One period | Two periods | Three periods | Four periods |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(P_8(X)\) | \(6,5\) | \(6,6\) | \(10,8\) | \(13,8\) |
| \(P_8(-X)\) | \(6,5\) | \(6,6\) | \(12,8\) | \(19,10\) |
| \(L(-X)\) | \(7,5\) | \(13,6\) | \(27,8\) | \(43,12\) |

Thus changing the sign of the variable can change the optimal boundary support of a single strict record value.
For records of growing degree the bounds make the search finite for each prescribed budget; existence of a saving across such a sequence is open.
