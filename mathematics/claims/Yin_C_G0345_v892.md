# Deciding trace realization over all separating grids

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bounded coefficient search independent of the nodes

Fix integers \(q,n\ge2\), and set \(p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ h=1/M,\ K=4M+1,\ N=n-1\).
Seek a monic integral \(H\) of degree \(n\) with \(M^nH(h)=K\), simple roots, one root in \((0,h)\) and \(N\) roots in \((-4,0)\).
By the [simplex characterization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), this is equivalent to existence on at least one separating grid.
Set \(E_j=(p/q)^j+(q/p)^j\) and \(r=\lfloor4N/M\rfloor\).

For \(j=1,\ldots,n\), extend the low coefficients \(1,s_1,\ldots,s_{j-1}\) of the reciprocal polynomial \(S(z)=z^n H(z+z^{-1}-2)\).
Write \(\Theta_j\) for its \(j\)-th Newton sum.
From the current prefix the pole determines \(s_j\bmod M\), so Newton's identity determines \(\Theta_j\bmod jM\).
Keep all integers in this residue class lying simultaneously in the two open intervals
\[
 (E_j-2N-1,E_j+2N),\qquad
 (E_j-N-j/q-3,E_j+N+j/q+2).
\]
Each retained integer determines the next coefficient uniquely.
At depth \(n\) recover \(H\) and check its exact pole, squarefreeness and the two root counts with the endpoints excluded.
An integer point exists on some grid if and only if at least one candidate passes.
On a prescribed grid add its strict alternating sign tests.

At most \(1+\lfloor4N/(jM)\rfloor\) extensions are possible per prefix at step \(j\).
In particular only the first
\[
 r_*=\min\!\left(r,\left\lfloor\frac{2N+5}{M-2/q}\right\rfloor\right)
\]
coefficients can require branching.
The number of candidates at any depth, hence the number of polynomials realized over the union of all grids, is at most \(\binom{2r}{r}\).
These are bounds on the search and actual polynomials; the final pole and signature tests are part of the equivalence.

<a id="windows"></a>
## Archimedean bounds for the integral moments

Let \(w\in(0,h)\) be the positive root and \(v_1,\ldots,v_N\) the negative roots of a solution.
The constant term gives \(w\prod_i|v_i|=|H(0)|\ge1\).
The pole identity therefore gives
\[
 0<h-w=\frac{K}{M^n\prod_i(h-v_i)}<\frac{Kw}{M^n}<\frac K{M^{n+1}} .
\]
Put \(\phi_j(x)=2T_j((x+2)/2)\), where \(T_j\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.
For \(x=z+z^{-1}-2,\ 1\le z\le p/q\), one has \(\phi_j(x)=z^j+z^{-j}\).
Its derivative is \(j(z^{j-1}+z^{j-3}+\cdots+z^{1-j})\), including the limiting value at \(z=1\).
Thus \(0<E_j-\phi_j(w)<(K/M^2)n^2/q^{2(n-1)}<1\) for \(1\le j\le n\), since \(n\le2^{n-1}\) and \(K<M^2\).
Each \(x_i=\phi_j(v_i)\) belongs to \([-2,2]\).
The identity \(\Theta_j=\phi_j(w)+\sum_i x_i\) proves the first open interval.

The [irreducibility and Salem lift](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) exclude roots of unity among the zeros of \(S\).
Taking absolute resultants with \(z^j-1\) and \(z^j+1\) yields respectively the positive integers
\[
 (\phi_j(w)-2)\prod_i(2-x_i),\qquad
 (\phi_j(w)+2)\prod_i(2+x_i).
\]
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and \(1-e^{-t}\le t\) give
\[
 -N-\log(E_j+2)<\sum_i x_i<N+\log(E_j-2).
\]
For example the first resultant gives \(\sum_i x_i\le N(2-(\phi_j(w)-2)^{-1/N})<N+\log(E_j-2)\).
The second gives the lower bound by the same argument with \(-x_i\).
Both logarithms are less than \(\log(4(p/q)^j)<2+j/q\).
Combining these estimates with \(E_j-1<\phi_j(w)<E_j\) proves the second interval.

<a id="digits"></a>
## From a simultaneous system to complete candidates

Write \(s_0=1\) and \(S(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}s_i z^i\) with \(s_i=s_{2n-i}\).
For \(b=q\) and \(b=-p\), the reciprocal pole evaluations satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{2n} s_i b^i(1+b)^{2n-i}=K.
\]
Indeed \(b/(1+b)\) equals either \(q/p\) or \(p/q\), both giving the same trace \(h\), and \(b(1+b)=M\).
Reduce modulo \(|b|^{j+1}\).
Terms with \(i>j\) vanish and division by \(|b|^j\) in the remaining linear equation gives \(s_j\) modulo \(|b|\), since \(1+b\) is a unit there.
At each extension the preceding residue guarantees this division is exact; the initial congruence uses \(K\equiv1\bmod M\).
The two coprime moduli give one residue modulo \(M\).
Now
\[
 \Theta_j=-js_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}s_i\Theta_{j-i}.
\]
In the first open interval of length \(4N+1\) the extreme possible integers differ by at most \(4N\).
This gives the stated branching bound.
The second interval has length \(2N+5+2j/q\), at most \(jM\) whenever \(j>(2N+5)/(M-2/q)\).
For \(r\ge1\) the product of the branching bounds is at most \(\prod_{j=1}^r(1+r/j)=\binom{2r}{r}\), and the empty product for \(r=0\) is one.

Given the low half, recover the coefficients \(c_i\) of \(H(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_ix^{n-i}\) successively from \(S(z)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_i z^i(1-z)^{2(n-i)}\).
The triangular transformation has diagonal one and preserves integrality.
The rational pole check and exact Sturm root counts now test precisely the original conditions.
Every solution survived the necessary constraints, and every passing candidate has all defining properties.
The [finite census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/free_trace_moments.md#census) applies the test before choosing any grid.
