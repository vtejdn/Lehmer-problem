# Almost every tuple from arbitrary sets of comparable primes has the sharp common-row cost

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive proportion of exclusions suffices for a gap

Use the [common-prime minimum](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) \(M(S,b)\) with one coprime integral pair of width at most \(b\) serving all primes of \(S\).
Let \(r\ge2\) and \(C>1\) be fixed.
For each \(L\to\infty\) let \(\mathcal A_1,\ldots,\mathcal A_r\) be any nonempty finite sets of primes in \([L,CL]\), of cardinalities \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\).
Allow overlapping sets.
Write \(\Sigma=\sum_i q_i\) for an ordered choice \(q_i\in\mathcal A_i\).
For any fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) with all positive powers preserving degree and any \(\delta>0\), there is a fixed integer width \(w\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(L\), all but a fraction at most
\[
                           K_{\alpha,r,C,\delta}\sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{m_i}      \tag{1}
\]
of the choices have distinct exponents and admit a common pair with nonzero reflected determinant, the minimum of **this same unit** repeated at every exponent, and \(\log H/\Sigma<m(\alpha)+\delta\).
The estimate is uniform over the prime sets and also holds for sets of positive integers if only these algebraic conditions and cost are imposed.

In particular fix \(1<Q\le6/5\), let the supplied width \(b(L)\to\infty\), and call a choice passing if its primes are distinct and there is a qualifying common pair with \(H<Q^\Sigma\).
The qualifying seeds in this test may vary with the prime.
If \(\lambda<\log Q\) the passing fraction is \(1-O_{r,C,Q,\alpha}(\sum_i1/m_i)\), using any one all-power seed of measure below the threshold.
If \(\lambda\ge\log Q\) no choice passes.
Consequently a positive lower bound for the rejected fraction on any subsequence with \(\min_i m_i\to\infty\) suffices to prove \(\lambda\ge\log Q\).
For such growing sets the common normalized minima converge in probability and in mean to \(\lambda\), assigning the fallback normalized value \(\log2\) to choices with repeated primes.
This applies even when all the sets lie in the same interval of length \(o(L)\), or consist only of primes in prescribed residue classes.

For two primes the necessary exclusion is weaker still.
If \(\lambda<\log Q\), every sufficiently large prime \(p\) has at most a bounded number of primes \(q\in[p,Cp]\) for which the pair fails at the supplied width \(b(p)\to\infty\).
Here the bound is uniform in \(p\), and the same fixed seed below the threshold works at all retained choices.
Thus \(\lambda\ge\log Q\) is equivalent to the existence of arbitrarily large primes \(p\) with an unbounded number of rejected partners in \([p,Cp]\).
No lower bound on the fraction of partners rejected is needed.

For a fixed seed with at most one exterior conjugate on each of its exterior circles, every tuple of distinct nearby exponents \(q_i/L\to1\) admits the sharp fixed-seed upper bound, without any exceptions.
This includes every Salem seed.
Here the limit in the upper bound is taken through arbitrarily small fixed excesses and sufficiently large fixed widths as above.

<a id="matrices"></a>
## Nonvanishing outside a sparse set on arbitrary integer products

Fix the seed and a width \(w\).
Consider any nonempty subset of the pairs \((i,c)\) of slots and exterior conjugates of \(\alpha\), without repetitions.
Let \(m\) be its size.
Put \(z_{ic}=c^{-q_i}\).
Build a square matrix with two polynomial blocks of columns of degrees \(0,\ldots,m-1\), acting by the two rows per pair
\[
           U(z_{ic})-cV(z_{ic}),\qquad
           q_i z_{ic}(U'-cV')(z_{ic})+cV(z_{ic}).
\]
Its determinant \(F(q_1,\ldots,q_r)\) is a finite sum of polynomials in the \(q_i\) times products \(\prod_i t_i^{q_i}\) with fixed nonzero algebraic bases.
After replacing the \(q_i\) in the derivative multipliers by independent indeterminates \(Z_i\), setting all \(Z_i=0\) gives two Vandermonde blocks up to nonzero constants.
For any distinct positive exponents the nodes \(c^{-q_i}\) in this matrix are distinct.
Across different exponents this follows from the positive height of \(c\), the same for every conjugate.
Within a single exponent it follows from degree preservation.

Choose a finite completion of the common number field at which all bases are units.
Choose an integer \(B\) so that for every base \(t\) its powers on a residue class modulo \(B\) interpolate by a restricted power series in that coordinate on the valuation ring, as in the [finite-dimensional zero count](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#exceptions).
Enlarge \(B\) to kill the torsion quotients of the finitely many bases in each coordinate.
On **every** product of these residue classes the function \(F\) is not identically zero.
Indeed group terms whose base tuples become equal after coordinatewise \(B\)-th powering, absorbing their residue factors into the coefficient polynomials.
If every resulting polynomial vanished identically, the grouped expression with the independent derivative variables would also vanish at \(Z=0\): the coefficient polynomials before replacing \(Z_i\) by \(q_i\) have no other dependence on \(q\) beyond the exponential bases.
This would contradict the Vandermonde test at any distinct positive sample exponents in the chosen classes.
The grouped exponential sequences times polynomials are independent as functions on the full nonnegative integer grid for distinct base tuples.
In one variable, successive shift-minus-base operators annihilate a polynomial times its own exponential after sufficiently many repetitions and are invertible on each fixed polynomial-degree space for a different base.
Grouping by the first base and repeating this one-variable argument in the other variables proves independence on the grid.
It follows that \(F\) takes a nonzero value at an integer tuple on each class product, hence is nonzero there as a restricted analytic function.

On any such product the zeros of \(F\) on arbitrary finite subsets of the integers in those classes number at most
\[
                      K\sum_i\prod_{j\ne i}m_j,                              \tag{2}
\]
using the cardinalities in those classes.
Here is the induction proving this assertion.
All slices in the last coordinate belong to a fixed finite-dimensional space spanned by the corresponding powers and polynomial multiples.
Each nonzero slice has at most a fixed number of zeros on its residue disk by the cited zero bound.
Since the whole function is nonzero on the class product, fix one integer in the last residue class giving a nonzero function on the other disks (an integer witness above suffices).
Every identically zero slice must vanish at this fixed sample.
Induction applied to that nonzero function bounds the number of exceptional prefixes, each costing at most \(m_r\) choices of the last coordinate.
This proves (2), starting from the single-variable disk bound.
Summing over the finite class products yields the same form using the original cardinalities.
When \(r=2\) the last-coordinate slice is identically zero for only finitely many first integers, globally rather than just in a given interval.
Indeed on each pair of residue disks evaluate at the fixed integer in the last disk as above.
The resulting nonzero function of the first coordinate has only finitely many zeros on its disk.
Taking the finite union removes finitely many first integers, and at every other first integer the number of last-coordinate zeros is uniformly bounded on all disks.
This count does not assume comparable sizes, positive density in an interval, or independence of congruence classes within each set.
The finitely many relevant determinants together satisfy (2).
Tuples with coincident coordinates contribute another term of that same size by fixing all but one of an equal pair.

<a id="lower"></a>
## Controlling shared exterior radii across different exponent slots

Omit the exceptional zeros in (2).
Uniformly for the remaining distinct tuples in \([L,CL]^r\), every nonzero integral pair of degree at most \(w\) meeting the double conditions has
\[
                        \log H\ \ge\ h\Sigma-o(L),\qquad h=m(\alpha).        \tag{3}
\]
For a sequence testing (3), pass to limits \(q_i/L\to t_i\in[1,C]\) and to coefficient slopes as in the [exterior estimate for separated scales](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#exterior).
At a fixed candidate breakpoint \(s>0\) group **all** pairs with \(t_i\log|c|=s\).
Let \(m\) be their total count.
Choose one member \((i_0,c_0)\) and put \(D_0=|c_0|^{q_{i_0}}\), so \(\log D_0=sL+o(L)\).
In the matrix of the preceding section replace every node by \(D_0c^{-q_i}\).
This multiplies each column of degree \(j\) by \(D_0^j\) and preserves nonvanishing.
The modulus of every node is \(\exp(o(L))\) and so is that of its inverse.

The normalized matrix has inverse norm at most \(\exp(o(L))\) along this sequence.
To verify the lower bound for its nonzero determinant, expand the determinant and expand each polynomial coefficient into monomials before comparison.
Every nonzero term has absolute logarithmic modulus \(o(L)\) at the selected embedding, height \(O(L)\), and total absolute logarithmic modulus outside a fixed finite set of places \(O(\log L)\).
Indeed the terms are fixed constants times monomials in the \(q_i\) and fixed algebraic numbers to integer powers \(O(L)\).
The only factors outside the fixed places come from the bounded-degree monomials in the positive integers \(q_i\le CL\).
The archimedean estimate follows term by term from the normalized node moduli.
Apply the [fixed-form Subspace Theorem argument](Yin_C_G0345_v490.md#determinants): if a nonzero sum were smaller than \(\exp(-\epsilon L)\), replacing one local coordinate form by the sum gives a proximity excess proportional to \(L\), since both the retained single term and the outside-place errors cost \(o(L)\).
The projective height is \(O(L)\).
A fixed exceptional hyperplane on a subsequence would then eliminate one term, preserving all three estimates under fixed nonzero rescalings.
Induction ends at a single term, which has local logarithmic modulus \(o(L)\).
Thus the nonzero determinant is at least \(\exp(-o(L))\), and the cofactors are at most \(\exp(o(L))\).

If the jump in the maximizing coefficient position at \(s\) were smaller than \(m\), the active indices would fit a common block \(k,\ldots,k+m-1\).
Scale the coefficients by \(D_0^{-(j-k)}\) on this block and normalize the largest scaled coefficient to modulus one.
At these nodes all terms outside the maximizing interval that are omitted have exponential loss relative to this normalization.
Replacing the actual moduli by \(D_0\) costs \(\exp(o(L))\) at fixed width.
Reversal and shifting by \(k\) change the Euler derivative equation only by a multiple of the value row, as in the separated-scale proof.
Thus the two normalized truncated lists have matrix image \(O(\exp(-\eta L))\) for some \(\eta>0\), or zero if no term was omitted.
The inverse estimate contradicts the normalization.
The jump is at least the full number of pairs at the breakpoint, including coincidences among limiting radii of different prime slots.
Integrating the jumps gives \(\sum_i t_i h\) and proves (3) by the subsequence argument.

If all \(t_i=1\) and each exterior circle has a single conjugate \(c\), the matrix needed there has this same label \(c\) at all its nodes.
For every distinct tuple the value rows force \(U-cV\) to vanish identically because it has degree at most \(m-1\) and \(m\) distinct zeros.
The derivative rows then force \(V=0\) by the same argument, and consequently \(U=0\).
The matrix is always invertible, and the size estimate and (3) hold without discarding any tuple.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## Common short pairs and the rejection criterion

The [fixed-width jet count and packing argument](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#many) now applies using (3).
For uniformity fix a positive slack \(\Delta\) in units of \(L\), with \(\Delta<\min(h/3,\delta/2)\).
Use coefficient bound \(\lfloor\exp(h\Sigma+\Delta L)\rfloor\).
Choose \(w\) so that \((w+1)\Delta>2r\deg(\alpha)(rCh+\Delta)\).
The logarithm of the largest fiber exceeds the dimension-\((w+1)\) packing bound by a fixed positive multiple of \(L\), uniformly over retained tuples.
The selected nonzero reflected determinant then has \(\log H\le h\Sigma+\Delta L+O_w(1)\).
A nongenuine node would force an additional copy of its powered minimum into that determinant, costing at least \(h L\) beyond \(2h\Sigma\) in the determinant measure, contradicting \(2\Delta<h\).
The [division argument](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#genuine) gives a coprime pair, still of fixed width, with the claimed strict cost for large \(L\).
This proof also applies without exceptional tuples in the stated single-conjugate case.

For \(\lambda<\log Q\), choose a reciprocal all-power representative of a positive irreducible measure below \(\log Q\) and choose \(\delta\) below the strict difference.
The common seed then works outside (1) for all sufficiently large \(L\) because the supplied widths eventually include its fixed width.
Conversely, the [common tangency inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(\lambda\Sigma\le\log H\) even if each prime uses a different seed.
The same inequalities, taking seeds arbitrarily close to \(\lambda\), prove convergence in probability to \(\lambda\) when \(\min_i m_i\to\infty\).
The fallback bounds every normalized minimum above by \(\log 2\), which gives convergence of the means as well.
For pairs set \(L=p\) and take \(p\) beyond the finite set of exceptional first integers for the fixed width matrices.
The remaining root count in the second coordinate and the one coincident choice together give a uniform bound on rejected partners.
If \(\lambda\ge\log Q\), all distinct partners are rejected and their number tends to infinity by the [prime count at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz).
It remains open whether one can prove a positive proportion of exclusions for any strict positive threshold on sufficiently large prime sets.
