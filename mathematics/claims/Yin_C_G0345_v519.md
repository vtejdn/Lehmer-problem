# Many large invariant factors in the normalized Laurent row quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## An additive quotient at many levels of the invariant factors

Let \(S\) consist of the primes in \([L,2L]\), set \(r=|S|\), and put
\[
 m=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil,\qquad
 g=1+\lfloor\log_2(1+\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor)\rfloor,\qquad
 D_L=\left\lfloor{\sqrt L\over32gm^2}\right\rfloor .
\]
Take the [computable width](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#statement) \(b=rj(L)\) for this \(g\).
Suppose nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) pass the [common test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width, with one reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) dividing every graph \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) to multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\).
Divide the two rows by the gcd of all their coefficients together, obtaining \(u,v\), and let \(A=\mathbb Z[Y,Y^{-1}]/(u,v)\).
By the [normalized Laurent quotient result](Yin_C_G0345_v503.md#statement) its additive group is finite.

For all sufficiently large \(L\) there is a squarefree \(Q\) made up of at least \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) distinct primes in \([1+5jL/2,1+7jL/2]\) such that \(A\) has an additive quotient isomorphic to
\[
               \prod_{i=1}^{k}(\mathbb Z/Q^{\,2i-1}\mathbb Z)^{D_L}.     \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(D_L\to\infty\), \(\log Q\gg L/\log L\), and \(Q^{2i-1}\) divides the \((k-i+1)D_L\)-th largest additive invariant factor of \(A\) for \(1\le i\le k\).
Here invariant factors are listed in decreasing divisibility order.
For example, at double contacts the same \(D_L\) top factors are all divisible by \(Q^3\), and the top \(2D_L\) are all divisible by \(Q\).

At each prime \(\ell\mid Q\) there are at least \(D_L\) distinct nonzero elements \(y\in\mathbb F_\ell\), all of one order \(t>\sqrt\ell/g\), at which both \(\bar u,\bar v\) vanish to multiplicity at least \(k\).
The local calculation below retains the higher valuations of the actual lifted collisions as well.
As in the [full-interval equivalence](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#selection), existence of same-seed rows with multiplicity at least two and normalized coefficient cost tending to zero along these widths is equivalent to vanishing of the unrestricted Lehmer infimum.

<a id="collision"></a>
## Two distinct labels at a common residue

Use the split primes of the [prime-field root theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#statement), set \(n=(\ell-1)/j\), and fix one reduced root \(z\) of \(P\) of order \(t\mid n\).
The prime-field result gives at least \(L/(16m^2(1+L/t))\) distinct \(y=z^p\) as \(p+q=n\) ranges over ordered representations by distinct primes in \(S\).
This number is at least \(D_L\) because \(L/(1+L/t)\ge\min(L,t)/2\) and \(\sqrt L/g\le\min(L,t)\) for large \(L\).
Fix one such representation for each selected \(y\).
Since \(t>\sqrt\ell/g>2\) for large \(L\), the original labels \(z,z^{-1}\) differ.

Let \(O=\mathbb Z_\ell\).
The distinct linear roots of \(P\bmod\ell\) lift uniquely to roots of \(P\) in \(O\).
Take the lift \(\alpha\) of \(z\) and its reciprocal \(\alpha^{-1}\), which is the lift of \(z^{-1}\).
At this representation put \(u_1=\alpha^p,\ u_2=\alpha^{-q}\), \(\delta=u_1-u_2\), and \(w=v_\ell(\delta)=v_\ell(\alpha^n-1)\ge1\).
The valuation is finite because the seed has no root of unity.
Both nodes reduce to \(y\).

Uniformly at these characteristics \(\ell>2k\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
Indeed the two exponent blocks of the graph have disjoint supports for \(p>1\), with at most \(2(b+1)\) terms in total.
A characteristic-zero polynomial with \(s\) nonzero terms cannot have a nonzero root of multiplicity \(s\): applying the first \(s\) Euler derivatives, of orders \(0,\ldots,s-1\), gives a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct exponents with nonzero determinant.
Thus \(k\le2b+1\), whereas \(r\ll L/\log L\) by the [dyadic prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#fourth-moment), \(b=rj\) and \(\ell> 5jL/2\).
Also \(\ell>p,q\).
Both contacts persist on dividing the common coefficient content because \(P^k\) is monic.

Consider the ring
\[
          T_y=O[Y]/((Y-u_1)^k,(Y-u_2)^k).
\]
Modulo \((Y-u_1)^k\), take the polynomial
\[
          f_1(Y)=\alpha\sum_{d=0}^{k-1}\binom{1/p}{d}
                                     ((Y-u_1)/u_1)^d .
\]
The coefficients lie in \(O\), because \(u_1,p\) and all positive factorials up to \((k-1)!\) are units there.
The truncated binomial identity gives \(f_1^p=Y\) in this quotient, and \(P(f_1)^k=0\) since \(f_1\equiv\alpha\bmod(Y-u_1)\).
The graph divisibility yields \(u+f_1v=0\) in \(T_y\).
Using \(\alpha^{-1},q,u_2\) in the second power quotient gives \(u+f_2v=0\) there as well after passing to \(T_y\).
The ring \(T_y\) has nilpotent \(Y-u_1\), and \(\ell\) is nilpotent by the nonzero determinant computed below.
Its reduction modulo \(\ell\) is \(\mathbb F_\ell[Y]/(Y-y)^k\), so it is local.
The difference \(f_1-f_2\) has nonzero residue \(z-z^{-1}\) in its residue field and is a unit.
It follows that both rows vanish in \(T_y\).
Moreover \(Y\) is a unit there, since \(y\ne0\).
For distinct \(y\) the defining ideals are comaximal, already by the linear factors with centers distinct modulo \(\ell\) and their powers.
The maps from \(O[Y,Y^{-1}]\) therefore give a surjection onto the full product of the selected \(T_y\) which kills both rows.
Reduction modulo \(\ell\) also gives the asserted multiplicities.

<a id="smith"></a>
## The exact additive group of a collision of multiplicity \(k\)

On the free \(O\)-module \(O[T]/T^k\), multiplication by \((T+\delta)^k\) presents the additive group of \(T_y\).
Its matrix in degrees \(0,\ldots,k-1\), with rows and columns numbered from zero, has entries
\[
          \binom{k}{i-j}\delta^{\,k-i+j}\quad(i\ge j),
\]
and zero entries for \(i<j\).
Its determinant is \(\delta^{k^2}\).
In any nonzero \(h\)-rowed minor, for \(1\le h\le k\), every permutation term has the same exponent of \(\delta\), namely \(hk-\sum i+\sum j\).
This is at least \(h^2\), by maximizing the sum of the row indices and minimizing the sum of the column indices.
Choose the bottom \(h\) rows and first \(h\) columns for equality.

The residual binomial determinant in this minor is an \(\ell\)-adic unit.
To see this, for \(x=k-h,\ldots,k-1\) express its row at \(x\) as a scalar \(\rho(x)=k!/(x!(k-x+h-1)!)\) times the values of the polynomials
\[
       f_j(x)=\prod_{s=0}^{j-1}(x-s)
                       \prod_{s=j+1}^{h-1}(k-x+s),\qquad 0\le j<h.
\]
This gives the entries \(\binom{k}{x-j}\), including zero when \(x<j\).
The determinant of the evaluation matrix of polynomials of degree at most \(h-1\) equals the Vandermonde determinant times the determinant of their coefficient matrix.
It is unchanged by translating all \(h\) consecutive arguments to \(0,\ldots,h-1\).
At those arguments the binomial matrix is triangular with diagonal one.
The ratio of the two binomial determinants is consequently the ratio of the products of the \(\rho\)'s, an \(\ell\)-adic unit since every factorial argument is at most \(2k-1<\ell\).
The minimal valuation of an \(h\)-rowed minor is exactly \(h^2w\).
Taking consecutive differences gives the Smith exponents
\[
              w,\,3w,\,5w,\,\ldots,\,(2k-1)w .
\]
In particular at each selected root the additive quotient has a further quotient with the odd exponents \(1,3,\ldots,2k-1\), all measured in the rational prime \(\ell\).

<a id="global"></a>
## The same invariant factors at distinct rational primes

Choose \(D_L\) distinct nodes at each of any \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) certified primes, and let \(Q\) be their product.
The local maps descend to their finite targets using integer Laurent polynomials: the target at \(\ell\) is killed by some \(\ell^v\), so coefficients in \(O\) can be replaced by integers modulo \(\ell^v\).
The Chinese remainder theorem on these rational moduli gives simultaneous surjectivity from the single normalized ring \(A\).
Combining the local cyclic factors of the same odd exponent gives (1).
The divisibilities of the ordered invariant factors follow at each rational prime from surjectivity, for example by counting independent generators in each successive power quotient of the \(\ell\)-primary groups.
Finally \(Q\) contains \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) primes greater than \(L\), giving the bound for \(\log Q\).
The local calculation uses the full row ideal before projection to a semisimple period ring.
The [period quotient at the lifted points](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#local) separately controls evaluation at the roots of unity themselves.

The coordinating research agent for Sheng Yin derived the integral two-node jet quotient and its Smith invariants at the common prime-field roots.
