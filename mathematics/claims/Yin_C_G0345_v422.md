# A local contact bound excludes two distinct measures through prime 211

<a id="statement"></a>
## The finite exclusion and an even-multiplicity degree bound

In the [fixed-budget single-row test](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement), for either prescribed sign,
\[
                              N_p\le1\qquad(p\le211).
\]
This rules out both degree pairs \((56,56),(56,58)\) left by the [degree screen at 101](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion).
The argument also gives a width bound at arbitrary large primes, independent of coefficient size.
Let \(A\ne0\) be integral of formal width \(b\ge1\), \(V=\varepsilon Y^b A(1/Y)\), and \(G=A(X^p)+XV(X^p)\).
For distinct monic irreducible unit minima \(P_i\) of nontorsion roots \(\alpha_i\), assume that \(P_i^{m_i}\mid G,\ m_i\ge2\), and \(A(\alpha_i^p)\ne0\).
If \(p>4b+1\), then
\[
                           \sum_i \lfloor m_i/2\rfloor\deg P_i\le b.       \tag{1}
\]
In fact, with bars denoting reduction modulo \(p\) and after dividing \(A\) by its scalar content, \(\prod_i \bar P_i^{\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor}\mid\bar A\).
For double factors this retains all root multiplicities on reduction, including coincidences between distinct minima.

<a id="disk"></a>
## A bound for contacts in one \(p\)-adic residue disk

Use \(v(p)=1\), and work over a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) whose residue field contains all roots of the selected reduced unit minima.
Every nonzero residue \(\xi\) has a unique root-of-unity lift \(\omega\) of order prime to \(p\) in this field, by lifting the simple roots of \(X^{q-1}-1\) for the residue field size \(q\).
For an arbitrary pair \(U,V\) of integral polynomials, at least one of them nonzero on reduction, expand
\[
 U(\omega^p(1+pZ))=\sum_{j\ge0}u_j Z^j,\qquad
 V(\omega^p(1+pZ))=\sum_{j\ge0}v_j Z^j,\qquad
 s=\min_j\{v(u_j),v(v_j)\},
\]
with valuation \(+\infty\) at zero.
The coefficients are divisible by \(p^j\), so \(s\) is a finite nonnegative integer.
If \(\bar U\ne0\) and it has multiplicity \(k\) at \(\xi^p\), the coefficient \(u_k/p^k\) is a unit by translation and scaling over the residue field, so \(s\le k\).

Suppose selected distinct nonzero roots \(x\) of the graph \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) in this disk satisfy \(v(x/\omega-1)\ge1/e\) for an integer \(1\le e<p-1\).
Write \(n_x\) for a required multiplicity at \(x\).
If \(2s+1<p-e\), then
\[
                               \sum_x n_x\le2s+1 .                       \tag{2}
\]
The reduction and degree arguments below use this implication only when the displayed strict condition holds.

To prove it, change coordinates to \(Z=((X/\omega)^p-1)/p\).
On \(v(X/\omega-1)\ge1/e>1/(p-1)\) the binomial expansion gives \(v(Z)=v(X/\omega-1)\), including the center.
The inverse on this disk is \(X=\omega E_p(Z)\), where
\[
 E_p(Z)=(1+pZ)^{1/p}=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}(1-kp)}{j!}\,Z^j .
\]
The series converges there since \(v_p(j!)\le j/(p-1)\), and nonconstant terms beyond the linear one have strictly larger valuation than \(Z\) by \(v_p(j!)\le(j-1)/(p-1)\).
Its \(p\)-th power is \(1+pZ\) by the formal binomial identity.
Uniqueness on the disk follows because for a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), \(v(\zeta-1)=1/(p-1)\) by the Eisenstein polynomial \(((1+T)^p-1)/T\).
The derivative of the coordinate change is nonzero at the units under consideration, so contact multiplicities are preserved.

Write the graph in the new variable and divide by \(p^s\).
It becomes \(L(Z)+\omega E_p(Z)M(Z)=\sum h_jZ^j\), where \(L,M\) are integral polynomials whose reductions have degrees at most \(s\), not both zero.
For \(j<p\) all \(h_j\) are integral and their reductions are the coefficients through degree \(p-1\) of \(\bar L+\xi\bar M\sum_{l=0}^{p-1}Z^l/l!\).
At least one \(h_{j_0}\), \(0\le j_0\le2s+1\), is a unit.
Indeed, if all vanished on reduction, the coefficients in degrees \(k=s+1,\ldots,2s+1\), multiplied by \(k!\), would say that the polynomial \(\sum_{j=0}^s\bar M_j k(k-1)\cdots(k-j+1)\) vanishes at \(s+1\) distinct residues.
The empty product is one, and the falling factorials are a basis in degrees at most \(s\).
This forces \(\bar M=0\), then \(\bar L=0\), a contradiction.

For every \(j\) one has \(v(h_j)\ge-v_p(j!)\) by the series and integrality of \(L,M\).
If \(j\ge p\), the digit-sum bound on the factorial gives
\[
          j-e v_p(j!)\ge j-e(j-1)/(p-1)\ge p-e>j_0 .
\]
For \(j_0<j<p\) use integrality.
After replacing \(Z\) by \(\pi Z\) with \(\pi^e=p\), every coefficient after \(j_0\) therefore has strictly smaller absolute value than \(\pi^{j_0}h_{j_0}\), and the coefficients tend to zero.
For such a convergent series the last index \(l\) attaining its maximum coefficient modulus bounds the number of zeros on the closed unit disk with multiplicity.
Indeed division by \(Z-\beta\) at a zero there gives quotient coefficients \(\sum_{k>j}h'_k\beta^{k-j-1}\) for the rescaled coefficients \(h'_k\).
At \(j=l-1\) the leading term \(h'_l\) dominates the tail, and all subsequent coefficients have smaller modulus.
If \(l=0\), the constant already dominates on the whole disk.
Iteration after extending the field to contain the specified roots proves the bound.
Here \(l\le j_0\), giving (2).

<a id="degree"></a>
## From local contacts to divisibility on reduction

For a selected monic unit minimum \(P_i\), let \(E_i(\xi)\) be the multiplicity of \(\xi\) in \(\bar P_i\).
Hensel factorization over the unramified field separates that residue into a monic factor of degree \(E_i(\xi)\), reducing to \((X-\xi)^{E_i(\xi)}\).
After shifting by \(\omega\) all coefficients below its leading one are divisible by \(p\).
Every lifted root in this disk thus satisfies \(v(x/\omega-1)\ge1/E_i(\xi)\), by its monic equation.
When \(P_i\) is reciprocal, \(E_i(\xi)=E_i(\xi^{-1})\), so at a nonendpoint \(\xi\ne\pm1\) the bound \(E_i(\xi)\le\deg(P_i)/2\) holds.
All multiplicities here count original roots in characteristic zero in their respective residue disks.

For (1), the [powered-factor product](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) with the [exact order identity](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) gives \(\sum_i(m_i-1)\deg P_i\le2b\).
Indeed the nonzero values recover the original roots from their powers, so distinct primitive minima give distinct powered factors of the same degrees.
In a disk containing any selected root take \(e=\max_i E_i(\xi)\le2b\).
Scalar normalization ensures \(\bar A\ne0\), and \(s\le b\).
Then \(p>4b+1\) implies \(2s+1<p-e\).
By (2), \(2s+1\ge\sum_i m_iE_i(\xi)\), hence \(s\ge\sum_i\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor E_i(\xi)\) by integrality.
Since \(s\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^p}\bar A\) and Frobenius permutes the reduced root multiset of each \(P_i\), this proves the divisibility assertion and (1).

<a id="finite"></a>
## The degree and coefficient bounds for primes from 101 to 211

For \(p\le97\) use the [previous exclusion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion).
Suppose \(101\le p\le211\) and a row has two distinct qualifying measures, represented by \(P_1,P_2\).
Here \(a=2,\ b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\le109\).
Write \(d_i=\deg P_i,\ D=d_1+d_2\).
The [budget and reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) make the two polynomials monic reciprocal noncyclotomic unit minima of even degree.
The [improved derivative one-norm](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) gives
\[
 D\le2b,\qquad
 m(P_1)+m(P_2)<\frac{\log2}{4}-\frac1p\log\frac{2pb+1}{pb+1}.          \tag{3}
\]
Divide the row by its scalar content if needed, so that it still passes and its reduction is nonzero.

Both measures are strictly below the logarithm of Lehmer's number \(\rho\).
Otherwise the other measure would be smaller than
\[
      \frac{\log2}{4}-\log(1.17628)-\frac{\log(1.999)}{211}<0.00765 .
\]
Indeed \(pb\ge101\cdot57\), and the [degree-ten polynomial with unique exterior root](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) is negative at \(1.17628=29407/25000\), by direct substitution.
The other degree is at most \(2b-2\le216\).
For \(16\le d\le216\), [Voutier's theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives the lower bound
\[
 m(P)>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3
       \ \ge\ \tfrac14(\log\log216/\log216)^3
       \ >\ \tfrac14(1.6815/5.3755)^3>0.00765 .
\]
Here \((\log t)/t\) decreases for \(t>e\), and \(\log16>e\).
At degrees 2 through 15 the same source's Corollary 2 gives \(m(P)>2/\log^3(45)>1/32\).
For a rational check of the close comparisons, \(\log2<0.69315,\ \log(1.17628)>0.162356,\ \log(1.999)>0.6926,\ 5.375<3\log6<5.3755\), and \(\log(5.375)>1.6815\).
These follow by summing \(2\sum_{j=0}^{31}t^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\) for \(t=(x-1)/(x+1)\), whose remainder for \(x>1\) is between zero and \(2t^{65}/(65(1-t^2))\).
Use \(\log6=\log2+\log3\) and \(\log(5.375)=2\log2+\log(43/32)\).
This contradiction proves the assertion.

The [strict sub-Lehmer degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) now gives \(d_1,d_2\ge56\), so \(D\ge112>b\) and \(d_i\le e_0:=2b-56\).
Let \(H_*\) be the largest nonnegative integer with \(((2pb+1)H_*^2)^4<2^p\).
The following integer inequalities hold uniformly in this range.
\[
   2\lfloor b/2\rfloor+1<p-\lfloor e_0/2\rfloor,\qquad
   2S+1<p-e_0,\quad
   S=\left\lfloor\log_p\!\left(H_*\binom b{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}\right)\right\rfloor .       \tag{4}
\]
Over the 22 primes from 101 to 211, the minimum of the difference of right and left sides in the first check is 14, the minimum of \(p-e_0-2S-1\) is 16, and the maximum \(S\) is 16.
The following standard-library Python reproduces these extrema with integers.

~~~python
from math import comb, isqrt

checks = []
for p in range(101, 212):
    if any(p % k == 0 for k in range(2, isqrt(p) + 1)):
        continue
    b = next(k for k in range(1, p + 1) if k**8 > p**7)
    e0 = 2*b - 56
    fourth_root = isqrt(isqrt(2**p - 1))
    height = isqrt(fourth_root // (2*p*b + 1))
    bound = height * comb(b, b//2)
    s = 0
    while p**(s + 1) <= bound:
        s += 1
    checks.append((p - e0//2 - 2*(b//2) - 1, p - e0 - 2*s - 1, s))
assert len(checks) == 22
assert [min(t[0] for t in checks), min(t[1] for t in checks),
        max(t[2] for t in checks)] == [14, 16, 16]
~~~

## The full product would divide a row of smaller degree

Put \(F=P_1P_2\), \(E(\xi)=E_1(\xi)+E_2(\xi)\), so that the two required double factors give \(2E(\xi)\) contacts counted with multiplicity in the disk above \(\xi\).
For an occupied nonendpoint disk choose \(e=\max_i E_i(\xi)\le\lfloor e_0/2\rfloor\).
If \(s\le\lfloor b/2\rfloor\), the first inequality of (4) permits (2) and yields \(E(\xi)\le s\).
In particular \(E(\xi)>\lfloor b/2\rfloor\) would force \(s>\lfloor b/2\rfloor\) in this disk and also in its distinct inverse disk, since \(E(\xi^{-1})=E(\xi)\).
Then \(\bar A\) would have roots of multiplicity at least \(\lfloor b/2\rfloor+1\) at each of \(\xi^p,\xi^{-p}\), exceeding its degree \(b\).
Therefore \(E(\xi)\le\lfloor b/2\rfloor\) at every nonendpoint, and in all cases \(E(\xi)\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^p}\bar A\).

For an occupied endpoint \(\xi=\omega=\pm1\), remove its whole characteristic-zero factor \((Y-\xi)^k\) from \(A\), using \(k=0\) if \(A(\xi)\ne0\).
It is also a factor of \(V\) to the same order by formal reversal.
Since the selected roots are nontorsion, division of the graph by \((X^p-\xi)^k\) leaves the same \(2E(\xi)\) contacts there.
Write \(U_1=A/(Y-\xi)^k\) and \(V_1=V/(Y-\xi)^k\).
Then \(\bar U_1\ne0\) and the nonzero integer \(U_1(\xi)\) is the \(k\)-th Taylor coefficient of \(A\) at \(\xi\).
Its absolute value is at most \(H_*\binom bk\), since each \(\binom jk\) for \(k\le j\le b\) is at most \(\binom bk\).
The local weight for the quotient rows is at most \(v_p(U_1(\xi))\le S\) by its constant coefficient and \(\xi^p=\xi\).
Use \(e=\max_i E_i(\xi)\le e_0\) and the second inequality of (4).
Now (2) gives \(E(\xi)\le s\le\operatorname{ord}_\xi\bar U_1\le\operatorname{ord}_\xi\bar A\).
This argument requires only the indicated Taylor coefficient of the quotient, even when division increases the one-norm of its rows.

Thus \(\bar F\mid\bar A\), since Frobenius permutes with multiplicity the reduction of the integral polynomial \(F\).
Its degree \(D\ge112\) exceeds \(b\le109\), proving the exclusion.
At \(p=223\), \(b=114\) allows two degrees 56 in a row of that degree, so the stated contradiction stops at 211 and gives no two-value witness at 223.
