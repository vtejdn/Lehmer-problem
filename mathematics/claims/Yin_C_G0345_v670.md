# Optimal decay of the two quotient tails with and without a total energy bound

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two exact exponents on a fixed hyperbolic unit

Fix a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\) with minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), put \(m=\log M(P)\), and define
\[
  \delta_-=\min_{|\rho|<1}-\log|\rho|,\qquad
  \delta_+=\min_{|\rho|>1}\log|\rho|,\qquad
  \kappa=\frac{2}{1/\delta_-+1/\delta_+},\qquad \beta=\frac{2m}{d},
\]
where \(\rho\) runs over the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For an integral Laurent numerator \(A\) indivisible by \(P\) write \(A/P=\sum_j h_jX^j\) on the unit circle, \(E(A)=\sum_j|h_j|^2\), and
\[
                      T_W(A)=\sum_{j\notin[0,W-1]}|h_j|^2
\]
for integers \(W\ge1\).
The window is fixed in these coordinates and the numerator may be translated.
Let \(E_*\) be the [attained minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v633.md#statement) of \(E(A)\).
For every fixed \(H_0\ge E_*\), as \(W\to\infty\),
\[
  \inf_{P\nmid A}T_W(A)\asymp_P e^{-\beta W},\qquad
  \inf_{P\nmid A,\,E(A)\le H_0} T_W(A)\asymp_{P,H_0}e^{-\kappa W}.   \tag{1}
\]
A shortest-vector construction attains the first upper estimate with \(E(A)\le W/4+O_P(e^{-\beta W})\).
For every \(\eta>0\), any numerators with \(T_W(A)\le e^{-(\kappa+\eta)W}\) must satisfy \(E(A)\gg_{P,\eta}\log W\) for all sufficiently large \(W\).
One has \(\kappa\le\beta\), with equality precisely when the moduli on each side of the circle are constant.
Thus different radii on either side produce a strict difference between the two optimal exponents.
These are fixed-polynomial estimates and their implicit constants need not be uniform as \(m\) shrinks.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Two weighted parts of the conjugate lattice

Put \(J=\{0,\ldots,W-1\}\) and \(D=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\).
If \(T_W(A)<\|P\|_2^{-2}\), then the support of \(A\) lies in \([0,W+d-1]\).
Indeed at every exponent outside \(J+[0,d]\), all coefficients of \(A/P\) contributing to the convolution by \(P\) are outside \(J\).
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the absolute value of that integer coefficient of \(A\) strictly below one.
Monic division \(A=PQ+R\) now has \(\deg R<d\) and the polynomial \(Q\) is supported in \(J\).
For every \(x=A(\alpha)\), write \(x_\rho\) for its value under the embedding \(\alpha\mapsto\rho\).
The [partial fractions](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails) of the proper quotient give
\[
 T_W(A)\asymp_P
 \sum_{|\rho|<1}|x_\rho|^2+
 \sum_{|\rho|>1}|\rho|^{-2W}|x_\rho|^2.                              \tag{2}
\]
In the left tail the geometric sequences have nodes \(\rho\) and coefficients \(x_\rho/P'(\rho)\).
In the right tail beginning at \(W\) their nodes are \(1/\rho\) and their coefficients are \(-x_\rho\rho^{-W-1}/P'(\rho)\).
Each set of nodes is distinct and strictly inside the disk.
Its map from coefficients to a square-summable sequence has a bounded norm and a positive least singular value, by the first square Vandermonde block.
This proves (2) with comparison constants independent of \(W\) and the numerator.
The same calculation gives (2) for every proper \(R/P\) without a small-tail assumption.

The product of the \(d\) weighted absolute conjugates in (2), counting complex conjugates separately, is \(|N(x)|e^{-mW}\ge e^{-mW}\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives the lower bound of the first comparison in (1).
If the tail exceeds or equals the support cutoff, its fixed positive size gives the same lower estimate after reducing the constant.

For the upper bound take the lattice of the order \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) in its real and complex embedding space of real dimension \(d\).
The power basis has nonzero determinant by separability.
Scale the exterior coordinates by \(|\rho|^{-W}\), multiplying the covolume by \(e^{-mW}\), including two real dimensions for each complex place.
The Euclidean shortest-vector bound gives a nonzero \(x\) of weighted squared norm at most \(C_P e^{-2mW/d}\).
Equivalently apply Minkowski's convex body theorem to a ball of that squared radius, taking \(C_P\) large enough for its volume to exceed \(2^d\) times the scaled covolume.
Take its integral representative \(R\) of degree \(<d\).
Formula (2) gives the claimed tail, and subtracting nearest integers at all \(W\) observed coefficients leaves it unchanged while giving the stated total energy.
Thus this construction genuinely varies the residue class on the same input.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## The cost of small values at the two slowest poles

Again assume the tail is below the support cutoff and set \(H=\sum_j|[X^j]A|\ge1\).
Since the coefficients are integral, \(H\le\|A\|_2^2\le D^2 E(A)\).
Remove nonempty collections of terms whose sum at \(\alpha\) is zero until the remaining polynomial \(B\) has no such collection.
It is nonempty, has the same value as \(A\) at all roots of \(P\), and its first and last exponents \(a\le b\) still lie in \([0,W+d-1]\).

We record a finite form of the [prefix estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v636.md#prefix).
For an expanding conjugate \(\tau\) with \(\lambda=\log|\tau|\) there are constants \(C_P>0\) and \(B_P>1\), independent of \(H,W,A\), such that
\[
                |B(\tau)|\ \ge
          \exp\big(\lambda b-C_P(1+\log H)B_P^H\big).                 \tag{3}
\]
To see this, take leading terms in decreasing exponent order, starting with \(X^b\) times its nonzero coefficient.
Translate each prefix of span \(l\) by its least exponent to an integral polynomial of degree \(l\).
Its nonzero algebraic integer value has absolute norm at least one.
At embeddings other than \(\tau\), the product of moduli is at most \(H^{d-1}e^{(m-\lambda)l}\).
Hence at \(\tau\) the untranslated prefix has magnitude at least \(H^{-(d-1)}e^{\lambda b-ml}\).
Stop if all terms have been used or the next gap \(g\) satisfies \(\lambda g\ge(m-\lambda)l+d\log H+\log 2\).
The total omitted magnitude is then at most half the prefix lower bound, with zero omitted at the last term.
Otherwise the next span is at most \((m/\lambda)l+(d\log H+\log2)/\lambda\).
Starting from zero and iterating at most \(H\) times bounds the final span by \(C_P(1+\log H)B_P^H\) with any sufficiently large fixed \(B_P>1+m/\lambda\).
This proves (3), including the case \(m=\lambda\).

Choose \(|\tau|=e^{\delta_+}\) and \(|\sigma|=e^{-\delta_-}\) among the exterior and interior roots respectively.
Apply the same argument to \(\alpha^{-1}\) with exponents negated, whose largest remaining exponent is \(-a\), to lower bound \(|B(\sigma)|\).
By increasing the fixed constants to cover both embeddings, (2) and (3) give
\[
    T_W(A)\ \ge\ c_P e^{-C_P(1+\log H)B_P^H}
        \big(e^{-2\delta_- a}+e^{-2\delta_+(W-b)}\big)
    \ \ge\ c_P e^{-\kappa W-C_P(1+\log H)B_P^H}.                     \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(a+(W-b)\le W\), so at least one of \(\delta_- a,\delta_+(W-b)\) is at most \(W/(1/\delta_-+1/\delta_+)\).
For bounded \(E(A)\) this proves the second lower bound of (1), also when the tail exceeds the fixed cutoff.
If \(T_W(A)\le e^{-(\kappa+\eta)W}\), (4) requires \((1+\log H)B_P^H\gg_{P,\eta}W\).
Since its logarithm is \(O_P(H)\) for \(H\ge1\), the required growth of \(E(A)\) follows.

<a id="fixed"></a>
## Positioning a bounded-energy numerator

Choose an integral minimizer \(A_*\) with \(E(A_*)=E_*\).
Far enough from its fixed finite support, its left and right Laurent coefficients decay in magnitude at most as fixed multiples of \(e^{-\delta_- n}\) and \(e^{-\delta_+ n}\), respectively, at distance \(n\).
This follows by the same expansion after monic division and an overall translation.
Multiply \(A_*\) by \(X^j\) with \(j\) within one of \(\delta_+ W/(\delta_-+\delta_+)\).
The two sums outside \(J\) are then each \(O_P(e^{-\kappa W})\), and translation preserves total energy.
Finally, with \(n_-\) interior and \(n_+\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity, \(n_-\delta_-\le m\) and \(n_+\delta_+\le m\).
Their reciprocals give \(1/\delta_-+1/\delta_+\ge d/m\), with equality exactly under the stated constancy of radii on each side.
This proves the comparison of exponents.
