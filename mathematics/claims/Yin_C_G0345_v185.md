# Bounded-length relations among powers in a prescribed measure interval

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound on exceptional exponents

Let \(\beta\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit such that every positive power has its degree, write \(m=m(\beta)>0\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure, and let \(\lambda(z)\) be the minimum sum of absolute coefficients of a nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(z\).
For any \(x>0\) and integer \(L\ge2\),
\[
  \#\{k\ge1: k m\ge x,\ \lambda(\beta^k)\le L\}
        \ \le\ E_L(x):=\left\lfloor B_L\frac{\log L}{x}\right\rfloor ,          \tag{1}
\]
where \(B_L\) is the absolute function in the [count of normalized relations](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#relations).
This controls all integer exponents above the threshold at once and is independent of \(m\), the unit, and its degree.

If the unrestricted Lehmer gap fails, fix **any** interval \(0<x<y\), any integer \(L\ge2\), and any positive integer \(J\).
Then there exist a power-stable unit \(\beta\) of arbitrarily small positive measure and \(J\) consecutive powers of it whose logarithmic measures lie in \([x,y]\), none of which is a root of any nonzero integer polynomial of coefficient length at most \(L\).
All positive powers of each of these \(J\) units also avoid such polynomials.
If the Salem gap fails, the units throughout can be taken to be Salem numbers.

Consequently a uniform bound on \(\lambda\) for all power-stable units in even one positive-length interval of positive logarithmic measures would imply the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
The analogous sufficient criterion holds with the class restricted to Salem numbers.
No interval satisfying such a covering condition is supplied here.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting divisors in the specified range

The [exact union of divisor sets](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#relations) writes the exponents with \(\lambda(\beta^k)\le L\) as the divisors of at most \(B_L\) positive integers \(g_i\).
Each satisfies \(g_i m\le\log L\), using [power stability and the total measure of a short relation](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#prime).
If \(k\mid g_i\) and \(km\ge x\), the complementary divisor \(j=g_i/k\) satisfies \(1\le j\le(\log L)/x\).
Each permitted \(j\) determines at most one \(k\) for that \(i\).
Summing over the list proves (1), including when \(x>\log L\).

If the gap fails, the [degree-lowering norm argument](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#sequence) gives power-stable inputs of arbitrarily small positive measure.
In the Salem case the original inputs are already power-stable and their powers remain Salem.
The integers \(k\) in \([x/m,y/m]\) are consecutive, and their number tends to infinity as \(m\) tends to zero.
Removing at most \(E_L(x)\) of them leaves at most \(E_L(x)+1\) runs and eventually leaves a run of length at least \(J\).
For the surviving exponents \(m(\beta^k)=km\), while \(\lambda((\beta^k)^h)\ge\lambda(\beta^k)>L\) for all \(h\ge1\) by substitution.
This proves the interval assertion and its covering consequence.

<a id="prescribed"></a>
## Prescribed accuracy and growth of the minimum length

Under the same failure hypothesis, for any sequences \(x_j>0,\ \delta_j>0\) and finite integers \(L_j\ge2\) one can simultaneously arrange a sequence of power-stable units \(\gamma_j\) with
\[
              x_j\le m(\gamma_j)\le x_j+\delta_j,\qquad
              \inf_{h\ge1}\lambda(\gamma_j^h)> L_j.
\]
Indeed the interval assertion applies at each \(j\) after fixing \(x_j,\delta_j,L_j\).
The degree and the initial measure of the unit from which \(\gamma_j\) is selected may depend on all three.
More explicitly, among the \(E_{L_j}(x_j)+1\) integers starting at \(\lceil x_j/m\rceil\) there is a permitted \(k\), of measure at most \(x_j+(E_{L_j}(x_j)+1)m\).
This also shows density of logarithmic measures of units with \(\lambda>L\) in the positive half-line for each fixed \(L\), under failure of the gap.

In particular, for every prescribed nondecreasing finite-valued function \(\Psi:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), failure is equivalent to existence of a sequence with \(m(\gamma_j)>0\) tending to zero and
\[
                         \lambda(\gamma_j)>\Psi(1/m(\gamma_j)).
\]
Take \(x_j=1/j\), \(\delta_j=1/j^2\) for \(j\ge2\), and \(L_j\ge\max(2,\Psi(j))\).
The reverse implication follows from the positive measures tending to zero.
The same choices preserve the Salem class in its version.
Here the starting unit is chosen after the target interval and \(L_j\) are specified, whereas the [bound for a prime at a given starting unit](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#prime) also controls the change of measure from that particular input.
