# The integral kernel and Gaussian entropy bound for joint coordinate-line sums

<a id="statement"></a>
## Covolume of the full-grid kernel

Let \(\alpha\) be an algebraic unit of degree \(d\) with monic minimal polynomial \(P\).
Take \(L>d\), \(J\ge1\), \(K=L-d\), \(n=L^J\) and \(r=n-K^J\).
Write \(A:\mathbb Z^L\to\mathbb Z^d\) for the matrix with columns \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{L-1}\) in the integral power basis of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and set \(W=\ker A\).
On the full grid \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^J\), let the joint integer map record \(A\) applied along every coordinate line.
Choose any integral basis of its actual image lattice, and let \(B:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^r\) denote the joint map in that basis.
All kernel covolumes use the Euclidean volume in their real spans induced by the standard input coordinates.
Then
\[
 \ker B=W^{\otimes J},\qquad
 \sqrt{\det(BB^{\mathsf T})}
     =\operatorname{covol}(W^{\otimes J})
     =\operatorname{covol}(W)^{J K^{J-1}}
     =\det(AA^{\mathsf T})^{J K^{J-1}/2}.                             \tag{1}
\]
The one-line determinant satisfies
\[
                     \det(AA^{\mathsf T})^{1/d}\ge\lfloor L/d\rfloor . \tag{2}
\]

For independent fair bits \(X\) on the \(n\) cells put \(Y=BX\), with covariance \(\Sigma=BB^{\mathsf T}/4\).
The entropy of a real Gaussian with covariance \(\Sigma\), computed in image-lattice coordinates of covolume one, is
\[
 G=\frac12\log\det(2\pi\mathrm e\,\Sigma)
   =\frac r2\log(\pi\mathrm e/2)
        +\frac{J K^{J-1}}2\log\det(AA^{\mathsf T}).                   \tag{3}
\]
It is invariant under changing the integral basis of the image.
If \(J\to\infty\) with \(0<a\le L/(Jd)\le b<\infty\), then \(G/n\gg_{a,b}\log J\), whereas \(H(Y)/n\le\log2\).
This concerns one Gaussian using the entire joint covariance, extending the [separate-line entropy obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v786.md#statement).

More precisely, let \(Z\) be independent absolutely continuous noise in the chosen image coordinates, with finite differential entropy \(h(Z)\), finite second moment, and support disjoint up to null sets from all its nonzero \(\mathbb Z^r\)-translates.
This includes uniform noise on a fundamental parallelepiped.
The corresponding Gaussian upper bound on the discrete entropy is
\[
 H(Y)\ \le\ \tfrac12\log\det\!\bigl(2\pi\mathrm e(\Sigma+
                  \operatorname{Cov}Z)\bigr)-h(Z).
\]
The right side is at least \(G\) for every such smoothing.
Thus even the full covariance in this bound cannot establish a positive deficit from \(n\log2\) in the stated range.
The size of the largest common fiber at that scale remains open.

<a id="kernel"></a>
## A splitting over the integers and the Gram determinant

The first \(d\) columns of \(A\) are the identity, giving an integral direct sum \(\mathbb Z^L=W\oplus V\), where \(V\) maps isomorphically to \(\mathbb Z^d\).
For one direction, the line map acts as \(A\) in that tensor slot and as the identity in every other slot.
In a tensor basis formed from bases of \(W\) and \(V\), its kernel consists of exactly the span of tensors with a \(W\) basis vector in that slot.
Intersecting these subgroups for all directions gives the whole integer kernel \(W^{\otimes J}\), of rank \(K^J\).
This establishes the lattice assertion behind the [rational dimension count](Yin_C_G0345_v782.md#dimension).

For any surjective integer matrix \(D:\mathbb Z^u\to\mathbb Z^v\), let \(C\) be a basis matrix of its integer kernel and \(E\) a matrix of integer lifts of the standard output basis.
The combined columns of \(C,E\) form an integral basis of \(\mathbb Z^u\).
Project the columns of \(E\) orthogonally to \((\ker D)^\perp\).
The projected matrix is \(D^{\mathsf T}(DD^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\), since \(DE=I_v\) and the projection preserves the image under \(D\).
The projected columns have volume \(\det(DD^{\mathsf T})^{-1/2}\).
Multiplying this volume by \(\sqrt{\det(C^{\mathsf T}C)}\) gives the original full volume one.
Consequently the kernel has covolume \(\sqrt{\det(DD^{\mathsf T})}\).

Apply this calculation to \(A\) and \(B\), both surjective in the chosen integral output bases.
For a basis matrix \(C\) of \(W\), the columns of \(C^{\otimes J}\) form an integral basis of the joint kernel.
Their Gram matrix is \((C^{\mathsf T}C)^{\otimes J}\).
Its eigenvalues are all \(J\)-fold products of the \(K\) eigenvalues of \(C^{\mathsf T}C\) with repetitions, so its determinant is \(\det(C^{\mathsf T}C)^{J K^{J-1}}\).
This proves (1).
The one-dimensional determinant can also be computed with the coefficient vectors of \(P,XP,\ldots,X^{K-1}P\), which form the integer kernel basis obtained by monic division. [One-dimensional finite Gram identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0004_v1.md#2-orbit-matrix-and-its-exact-integer-relation-lattice)

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Consecutive bases and the Gaussian estimate

Separate \(q=\lfloor L/d\rfloor\) disjoint blocks of \(d\) consecutive columns of \(A\).
In block \(i\), multiplication by \(\alpha^{id}\) takes the original power basis to those columns.
This map preserves the integral power-basis lattice in both directions since \(P\) has unit constant coefficient.
Thus each block matrix \(M_i\), indexed from zero, has determinant of absolute value one.
Put \(T=AA^{\mathsf T}\) and \(S_i=T^{-1/2}M_iM_i^{\mathsf T}T^{-1/2}\).
The matrices \(S_i\) are positive definite with \(\det S_i=(\det T)^{-1}\), and their sum is bounded above by the identity since the remaining columns contribute a positive semidefinite matrix.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on the eigenvalues gives
\[
 d\ \ge\ \sum_i\operatorname{tr}S_i
       \ \ge\ qd\,(\det T)^{-1/d}.
\]
This proves (2).

The bit covariance is \(I_n/4\), giving (3) by (1).
Changing the integral output basis conjugates the covariance by a unimodular matrix and its transpose, preserving the determinant.
In the stated range, \(Jd/L\) is bounded above and below by positive constants.
For large \(J\), \((1-d/L)^{J-1}\) is bounded below in terms of \(a\), for example by using \(\log(1-x)\ge-2x\) at \(x=d/L\le1/2\).
It follows from (2) that
\[
 \frac Gn\ \ge\
       \frac{Jd}{2L}(1-d/L)^{J-1}\log\lfloor L/d\rfloor
       \ \gg_{a,b}\ \log J .
\]
The input has just \(2^n\) possible values, which bounds the actual discrete entropy.

<a id="smoothing"></a>
## Entropy after independent lattice smoothing

Let \(E_Z\) be a measurable support of \(Z\) with pairwise disjoint lattice translates up to null sets.
Translating the intersections \(E_Z\cap(v+[0,1)^r)\), \(v\in\mathbb Z^r\), back into the unit cube gives disjoint pieces up to null sets.
Thus \(\operatorname{vol}(E_Z)\le1\), and \(h(Z)\le\log\operatorname{vol}(E_Z)\le0\) by comparison with the uniform density on \(E_Z\).
The translates carrying the conditional laws of \(Y+Z\) given \(Y\) are also disjoint, so \(h(Y+Z)=H(Y)+h(Z)\).
Its covariance is \(\Sigma+\operatorname{Cov}Z\).
Comparison with a Gaussian of the same covariance gives the displayed upper bound on \(H(Y)\). [Gaussian entropy comparison](Yin_C_G0321_v1.md#argument)
Since \(\Sigma\) is positive definite and \(\operatorname{Cov}Z\) is positive semidefinite, the determinant of the sum is at least \(\det\Sigma\).
Together with \(h(Z)\le0\) this proves the lower bound on the Gaussian expression.
It applies to the stated lattice-disjoint smoothing procedure; equation (3) by itself is a differential entropy and supplies no lower bound for \(H(Y)\).
