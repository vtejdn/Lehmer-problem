# A Salem projection for arbitrary units and an exact alternative for the Lehmer infimum

<a id="statement"></a>
## Detection by a primitive Salem unit

Let \(w\) be any nontorsion algebraic unit, and let \(\alpha>1\) be a primitive Salem number of degree \(2n\ge4\); primitivity is in the units of its field modulo torsion.
In a common finite Galois extension \(N/\mathbb Q\) containing all conjugates of both inputs, put
\[
          f_v(g)=\log|g(v)|,\qquad
          \langle f,h\rangle=|\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)|^{-1}
                                      \sum_{g} f(g)h(g).
\]
The inner products do not change on enlarging \(N\).
If \(\langle f_w,f_\gamma\rangle\ne0\) for at least one conjugate \(\gamma\) of \(\alpha\), then
\[
     m(w)\ge\tfrac12\log\alpha,\qquad
     \deg\alpha\le\max(6,\deg w).
\]
If \(m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}=\tfrac12\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\), the first inequality improves to \(m(w)\ge\log\alpha\).
In particular small-measure units can be tested for orthogonality to every Salem conjugate by checking only finitely many primitive Salem polynomials with the stated degree and measure bounds.

Let \(\lambda_S\) be the infimum of the logarithms of Salem numbers of degree at least four, and \(\lambda_\perp\) the infimum of the positive total measures of units orthogonal to every conjugate of every such primitive Salem unit in the sense above.
For the original unrestricted integer-polynomial infimum one has
\[
                               \mathfrak L=\min(\lambda_S,\lambda_\perp).
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]
For a strict degree record \(w\) with \(\deg w\ge6\) and \(0<m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}\), detection implies its measure is exactly the logarithm of a Salem number of that same degree.
Here a strict degree record first attains its measure at that degree and minimizes all positive integer-polynomial measures up to that degree.

<a id="coefficient"></a>
## Quantization by a norm to one Salem field

Choose one orientation \(\gamma_j\) from each inverse pair of conjugates of \(\alpha\), and put \(\ell=\log\alpha\), \(q_j=\langle f_w,f_{\gamma_j}\rangle/\langle f_{\gamma_j},f_{\gamma_j}\rangle\).
Each \(f_{\gamma_j}\) has values \(\ell,-\ell,0\), taking each nonzero value on a proportion \(1/(2n)\) of the automorphisms.
These nonzero supports are disjoint among the \(n\) labels: each automorphism sends just one inverse pair to the real pair.
Consequently
\[
        2h(w)=\mathbb E|f_w|\ \ge\
           \sum_j |\mathbb E(f_w f_{\gamma_j}/\ell)|
                         =\frac{\ell}{n}\sum_j|q_j| .                     \tag{2}
\]
Reorient to make the nonzero coefficients positive.
For a selected \(\gamma=\gamma_j\) with \(q_j>0\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), \(L=\mathbb Q(w,\gamma)\), \(e=[L:K]\), \(s=[L:\mathbb Q(w)]\), and \(\rho=N_{L/K}w\).
Let \(\tau\) invert \(\gamma\), and use the embedding \(\iota:K\to\mathbb R\) taking \(\gamma\) to \(\alpha\).
The averages of \(f_w\) conditional on \(g\gamma=\alpha\) and \(g\gamma=\alpha^{-1}\) are \(\log|\iota(\rho)|/e\) and \(\log|\iota(\tau\rho)|/e\), respectively, by extension counting.
The unit \(\rho/\tau\rho\) has relative norm one.
The [rank-one Salem kernel](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units), whose unit-rank proof works for \(n\ge2\), gives \(\rho/\tau\rho=\pm\gamma^a\) with \(a\in\mathbb Z\).
Dividing the conditional difference by \(2\ell\) yields \(q_j=a/(2e)\); here \(a>0\).

The stabilizer of \(w\) sends \(\gamma\) to \(s\) distinct conjugates with the same coefficient: the inner product is invariant under simultaneous Galois action and the squared norm does not depend on the label.
None is an inverse of another since inversion negates the coefficient.
Thus \(s\le n\) and \(\sum|q_j|\ge s a/(2e)\).
As \(s\deg w=2ne\), (2) gives \(m(w)\ge a\ell/2\).
If \(m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}\), then
\[
 h(\rho\tau\rho)\le2h(\rho)\le2e h(w)
                          =(s/n)m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}.
\]
This norm is in the totally real trace field of \(\gamma\).
The [totally real height bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) implies it is torsion.
It is positive at a real trace place that becomes complex, so equals one.
Now \(\rho=\pm\gamma^b\) in the same norm kernel, hence \(a=2b\) and \(m(w)\ge\ell\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Orbit size of a nonzero signed coefficient vector

Under the Galois action the vector of \(q_j\) transforms by the signed permutations of the inverse pairs.
Because the stabilizer of \(w\) preserves this signed vector, its orbit has size at most \(\deg w\).
Let it have \(k\) nonzero entries.
The action on pairs is transitive, so the orbit of the support has at least \(n/k\) elements by counting incidences.
The [even inversion subgroup](../ledger/Yin_L_G0162_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) can also be seen directly: complex conjugation flips every pair except the real one, and multiplying two conjugates of this element flips just the two chosen labels.
When \(k<n\), any choice of signs on the support is available by using one zero label to adjust parity, so the orbit size is at least \(2^k n/k\ge2n\).
When \(k=n\), there are at least \(2^{n-1}\) sign choices, at least \(2n\) for \(n\ge4\).
For \(n=2\) complex conjugation and its conjugate give both individual flips.
For \(n=3\) the bound claimed in the statement already allows degree six.
This proves the degree assertion for every detected input.
At bounded degree and measure there are finitely many Salem polynomials by the coefficient bounds for a monic polynomial with bounded Mahler measure.
The conditional norm formula tests zero exactly by checking whether \(\rho/\tau\rho=\pm1\).

<a id="infimum"></a>
## The unrestricted alternative and degree records

Both sets in (1) consist of unrestricted inputs, so \(\mathfrak L\) is at most the right side.
Lehmer's fixed degree-ten Salem example satisfies \(m(\alpha)<(\log2)/4<c_{\mathbb R}\) by the [rational root bound](Yin_C_G0901_v31.md#twist) and \(((1+\sqrt5)/2)^2>2\).
Thus any positive input with measure at least \(c_{\mathbb R}\) already costs at least the right side of (1).
For an integer polynomial of smaller positive measure take a positive-measure irreducible factor.
It is a nontorsion unit minimum, since a nonunit nonzero extreme coefficient costs at least \(\log2\); discard monomials and use additivity of measure.
If its root is orthogonal to all tested conjugates it costs at least \(\lambda_\perp\).
Otherwise the comparison just proved supplies a Salem number of no greater measure.
This proves the reverse inequality in (1).
The zero condition is invariant under conjugating the unit, by simultaneous invariance of the inner product and the use of all Salem conjugates.
Replacing a Salem number by its [positive primitive generator](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) scales its logarithm functions and leaves all zero tests unchanged.
Finally, at a strict degree record with degree at least six, a detected Salem number has no greater degree or measure.
The two record conditions force equality of the measures and of the degrees, as asserted.
