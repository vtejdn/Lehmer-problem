# Rigidity of the number of exterior conjugates throughout a divisible group

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior counts for rational products of conjugates

Let \(w\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(2n\), \(n\ge6\).
Suppose that the Galois group acts as \(S_n\) or \(A_n\) on its \(n\) inverse pairs and contains all even sign changes in the kernel of this action.
Write \(a\) for the number of real exterior conjugates of \(w\) and \(s\) for the number of complex conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior conjugates.
Put \(l=a+2s\) and \(t=a+s\).
Take any reciprocal nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), allowing arbitrary roots of products and torsion factors, and write \(l(x)\) for its number of exterior conjugates.
Then the following hold.

* If \(x\) is a rational power of one conjugate of \(w\) up to torsion, its degree is \(2nh\) and \(l(x)=hl\) for the same positive integer \(h\).
* Every other \(x\) satisfies
  \[
              l(x)\ \ge\ \frac{t}{2n}
                             \min\{2n(n-1),\,2^{n-1}\}.
  \]
  For \(n\ge8\) this gives \(l(x)\ge(n-1)t\).

The least exterior count among all such \(x\) is exactly \(l\).
Every minimizer has degree \(2n\) and is a torsion twist of a rational power of a single conjugate of \(w\).
The statements include reciprocal units without any circle conjugate.
They concern the number of exterior conjugates independently of the sizes of their moduli.

In particular the [degree-eighteen unit of measure about \(1.219447\)](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#group) has \(l=3,\ t=2\).
Its reciprocal nontorsion divisible products that use more than one inverse label have at least sixteen exterior conjugates.
Through degree 55, the least measure among nontorsion units \(w\) such that \(D(w)\) contains no reciprocal nontorsion unit with a neutral conjugate and at most two exterior conjugates is exactly the measure of this degree-eighteen polynomial.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Large orbits outside the coordinate lines

Choose an orientation of each inverse pair.
By the [irreducibility argument for distinct sign characters](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs), the rational signed permutation module on the pairs is irreducible.
Indeed the even flips separate all \(n\) coordinate lines and the action on labels is transitive.
Sending each basis vector to the multiplicative class of the corresponding root is a surjection onto \(V_w\) and has nonzero image, so is an isomorphism.
Thus the class of \(x\) has a unique rational coefficient vector \(q\) in this basis, with \(j\ge1\) nonzero entries.
The space spanned by the orbit of \(q\) is all of \(V_w\).
Complex conjugation fixes the coordinate on each real inverse pair, negates the coordinate on each neutral pair, and exchanges two signed coordinates for each nonreal exterior conjugate pair, contributing one fixed direction per such exchange.
Hence the [fixed-space rank](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules) gives \(r(x)=t\) and \(\dim V_x=n\).

For \(2\le j<n\) the orbit of \(q\) contains at least \(2^j\binom nj\) vectors.
Indeed both permutation groups in the hypotheses are transitive on \(j\)-subsets: an odd permutation taking one such subset onto another can be corrected by a transposition preserving the latter subset since at least one side has size at least two.
At every support arbitrary changes of signs are realized by correcting parity on an unused coordinate.
The smallest of \(2^j\binom nj\) for \(2\le j\le n-1\) is attained at an endpoint because the successive ratios \(2(n-j)/(j+1)\) decrease.
The endpoint values are \(2n(n-1)\) and \(n2^{n-1}\), of which the former is smaller at \(n\ge6\).
For full support, even flips already give \(2^{n-1}\) distinct vectors.
The orbit size is a lower bound on the degree of \(x\): every distinct class occurs as an actual conjugate, even if different conjugates sometimes have the same class.
Combining this degree bound with the [degree and exterior-count inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v64.md#degree) gives the lower bound in the statement.
For \(n=8\) one has \(2^{n-1}\ge2n(n-1)\), and the inequality persists by induction since \(2(n-1)\ge n+1\).
For all \(n\ge6\) both entries in the displayed minimum strictly exceed \(4n\); the mixed-support lower bound is therefore strictly greater than \(2t\ge l\).

<a id="coordinate"></a>
## Single conjugates and the finite minimum

At \(j=1\), one has \(x^N=\zeta b^u\) for a conjugate \(b\) of \(w\), integers \(N>0\) and \(u\ne0\), and a root of unity \(\zeta\).
The orbit of \(q\) consists of \(2n\) distinct vectors because a coordinate can be sent to either orientation at any label.
The projection from the transitive orbit of actual conjugates of \(x\) onto the orbit of their classes is equivariant and all fibers have the same cardinality \(h\).
At the fixed embedding the logarithmic modulus for each class is \(u/N\) times the log modulus of its corresponding conjugate of \(w\), with a consistent choice of the initial orientation.
Either sign of \(u\) gives exactly \(l\) positive values counted over these \(2n\) classes because \(w\) is reciprocal.
This proves the first alternative.
Since \(x=w\) attains \(l\), the minimum and its rigidity follow.

For the indicated degree-eighteen unit, the [two Frobenius factorizations and the real signature](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#group) provide \(S_9\), the even flips, one real exterior root and one pair of nonreal exterior roots.
The theorem excludes every reciprocal nontorsion output of at most two exterior conjugates, with or without a neutral conjugate.
Conversely each of the [thirteen strictly smaller primitive cores through the certified degree range](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#catalogue) has a neutral conjugate and either one or two exterior conjugates, and so do the cores contained up to sign in any of their irreducible power substitutions' divisible groups.
The finite minimum for the stated class follows from the same exhaustive range and strict rational comparisons.
