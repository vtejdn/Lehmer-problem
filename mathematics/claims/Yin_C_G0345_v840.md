# Rank growth among the first dyadic denominators of a reversed block

<a id="statement"></a>
## The first evaluations and their signed products

Use the [reversed block of a least-degree witness](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#statement), write \(m<\log 2\) for its positive log measure, and put \(d=2ub\), \(R=e^{m/2}\) and \(w=f/(\tau f)\).
Let \(r_s\) be the rank of the subgroup of fractional ideals generated by \((w(2^j))\), \(1\le j\le s\).
Equivalently this is the rank of the ideals of the [signed products](Yin_C_G0345_v835.md#statement) \(W_1(2),\ldots,W_s(2)\), since the coefficient of the newest evaluation in each product is \((-1)^{j-1}\) for the product of length \(j\).
These changes of generators are triangular with diagonal entries in \(\{1,-1\}\).
For integers \(h\ge0\) and \(k=s-h-4\ge1\), one has
\[
 r_s\ \ge\ \frac{k(h+1)}{\log_2(1+2^{h+1}k\,2^{h(h+3)/2}d(s+1))}.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(r_s\gg s/\sqrt{\log_2(2ds)}\) whenever \(s\ge 10+4\sqrt{\log_2(2ds)}\), with an absolute implied constant.
At least \(r_s\) distinct primes of the block field split in the orientation field and occur in the support of the reduced denominators of the first \(s\) signed products.
Indeed the valuation at either prime above a split prime determines the valuation at its conjugate by inversion, and the valuations at fixed primes vanish.
The bound concerns prefixes beginning at two, with both the witness and the prefix length allowed to vary.

<a id="digits"></a>
## Canceling powers at infinity enlarges the digit set

Put \(P_h(T)=\prod_{i=1}^h(1-2^iT)=\sum_{i=0}^h p_iT^i\) and \(V_h(q)=\prod_{i=0}^h w(2^iq)^{p_i}\), using the empty product polynomial one for \(h=0\).
Write \(\log w(q)=\sum_{a\ge1}A_a q^{-a}\) at the chosen real place.
The [coefficient proof](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#expansion) gives \(0<A_a\le 2mR^a\).
Thus \(L_h(q)=\log V_h(q)\) has vanishing coefficients through order \(h\) and strictly positive coefficients \(A_aP_h(2^{-a})\le A_a\) thereafter.
Put \(B=2^{h+1}\).
Summing each surviving power along the later dyadic arguments gives
\[
 (B-1)\sum_{j\ge1}L_h(2^jq)<L_h(q),
\]
since the multiplier for order \(a\) is \((B-1)/(2^a-1)\), strictly below one for \(a>h+1\).
Moreover \(L_h(2^jq)\le B^{-j}L_h(q)\).
For \(q=32\) the full absolute difference budget for digits from zero through \(B-1\) is therefore at most
\[
 (B-1)\sum_{j\ge0}L_h(2^jq)\le B L_h(q)
 \le \frac{2m(2R/q)^{h+1}}{1-R/q}<m,
\]
using \(R<\sqrt 2\).
The \(B^k\) digit products of the \(V_h(32\cdot2^j)\), \(0\le j<k\), have distinct fractional ideals.
Indeed the first different digit gives a nonzero real logarithm for their quotient by the strict tail inequality.
Equal ideals would make the quotient a unit, with reciprocal real values and unit modulus at every complex place.
Its positive log measure would then be at most its absolute real logarithm, strictly below \(m\), in a field of degree \(2u<d\), contradicting [least degree](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#ideals).
The last evaluation used is at \(2^{5+k-1+h}=2^s\).

<a id="coordinates"></a>
## Counting the possible valuation vectors

Take one coordinate above each split prime in the finite union of supports of the original evaluations.
Select \(r_s\) coordinate rows which are independent on the rational span of their valuation vectors, so projection onto these coordinates is injective on that span.
For every selected coordinate \(\mathfrak P\) and \(1\le j\le s\),
\[
 |v_{\mathfrak P}(w(2^j))|\le d(s+1).
\]
Indeed each of the two integral evaluations has absolute norm at most \((2^j+R)^d\), since all conjugate roots have modulus at most \(R\) and the norm polynomial has degree \(d\) by the [oriented factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement).
The prime norm is at least two, and the absolute difference of two nonnegative valuations is at most their maximum.
Since \(\sum_i|p_i|=\prod_{i=1}^h(1+2^i)\le2^{h(h+3)/2}\), the projected digit products range over an integer interval of length at most \((B-1)k\,2^{h(h+3)/2}d(s+1)\) in each coordinate.
Counting available integer points and comparing with \(B^k\) proves (1).
For the stated asymptotic take \(h=\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2(2ds)}\rfloor\).
Then \(k\ge s/2\), the numerator is at least \((s/2)\sqrt{\log_2(2ds)}\), and the denominator is \(O(\log_2(2ds))\).
Increasing the rank forces new split primes as the prefix grows, while the argument gives no uniform positive lower bound for \(m\).
