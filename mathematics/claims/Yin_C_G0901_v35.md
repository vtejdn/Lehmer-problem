# The real unit determined by the single nonreal pair of trace embeddings

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conjugates of the positive unit and the size of its measure

Let \(\beta\) belong to the [quadratic norm-one family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#statement), with \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})\), \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)=F(i)\), and \(\sqrt3\in F\).
Suppose \(F\) has exactly one conjugate pair of nonreal embeddings.
Put \(d=[F:\mathbb Q]\), let \(F_0\) be the largest totally real subfield of \(F\), and set \(\ell=[F:F_0]\).
Write \(m(\cdot)\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure.
For the [positive algebraic integer \(D\) formed from the nonreal embeddings](Yin_C_G0901_v34.md#statement), the positive real number \(U=\exp(m(\beta))\) is a reciprocal algebraic unit satisfying
\[
 \begin{split}
 D&=U+U^{-1}-2,\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(U):\mathbb Q]=d(\ell-1),\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(D):\mathbb Q]=d(\ell-1)/2,\\
 m(U)&=(\ell-1)m(\beta).
 \end{split}                                                              \tag{1}
\]
Here \(2\le\ell\le d/2\).
Exactly one conjugate has modulus \(U\), namely \(U\), and exactly one has modulus \(U^{-1}\).
There are \(2(\ell-2)\) conjugates of each modulus \(U^{1/2}\) and \(U^{-1/2}\), and all remaining conjugates lie on the circle.
In particular \(\ell=2\) gives a Salem number with \(\log U=m(\beta)\).
Its trace field contains \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
Let \(\lambda_{S,3}\) be the infimum of \(\log\alpha\) over Salem numbers of degree at least four whose trace field contains \(\sqrt3\), and let \(\lambda_{1,2}\) be the positive measure infimum in this norm-one family with \(r_2(F)=1,\ \ell=2\).
Then \(\lambda_{S,3}\le\lambda_{1,2}\le2\lambda_{S,3}\), using \(+\infty\) for the infimum of an empty family.

When \(\ell\ge3\), there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
           m(\beta)\ge c\,
                  \frac{\sqrt{d/(\ell-1)}}{(\log(6d(\ell-1)))^{106}}.      \tag{2}
\]
Thus every bounded range \(3\le\ell\le\ell_0\) has a uniform positive gap.
Over all \(\ell\ge3\), \(m(U)\to\infty\) as \(d\to\infty\), even if the original \(m(\beta)\) is small.
If \(m(\beta)\to0\) with \(\ell\ge3\), then \(\ell\to\infty\), and the degree \(k=[\mathbb Q(D):\mathbb Q]\) satisfies \(k(\log(6d^2))^{212}/d^2\to\infty\).

For the twelfth-root twist of a positive Salem input \(\alpha\) of degree \(2n\), with minimum \(P\), both possible signatures \(r=r_2(F)\in\{1,2\}\) satisfy
\[
      D=r(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})^2,\qquad
      \mathbb Q(D)=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}),\qquad
      |N_{\mathbb Q(D)/\mathbb Q}D|=r^n|P(1)P(-1)|.                       \tag{3}
\]

<a id="blocks"></a>
## A transposition on the trace embeddings and the common inversion sign

Let \(M\) be the normal closure of \(L\) in \(\mathbb C\), with group \(G\), and let \(\Omega\) be the \(d\) embeddings \(s:F\to M\).
For each label \(s\) let \(u_s\) be the image of \(\beta\) under the unique extension fixing the chosen image of \(i\).
The [archimedean coordinates](Yin_C_G0901_v34.md#embeddings) give \(u_{\bar s}=1/\overline{u_s}\).
For \(g\in G\), put \(\epsilon(g)=1\) when \(g(i)=i\) and \(-1\) when \(g(i)=-i\).
The two extensions of \(s\) send \(\beta\) to mutual inverses, so
\[
                         g(u_s)=u_{gs}^{\epsilon(g)}.                     \tag{4}
\]
In the single nonreal pair \(a,b=\bar a\), choose \(a\) so that \(|u_a|>1\).
Such a choice exists because all real labels have unit modulus and \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
Then \(U=u_a/u_b=|u_a|^2>1\), and the archimedean formulas give \(\log U=m(\beta)\) and \(D=U+U^{-1}-2\).

Complex conjugation \(c\) induces the single transposition \((a\,b)\) on \(\Omega\).
Form a graph on \(\Omega\) with edges all the images of \(\{a,b\}\) under \(G\).
As in the [transposition argument on inverse-pair labels](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action), each edge is the transposed pair of a conjugate of \(c\), fixing every other label.
The edge transpositions of a connected component generate every transposition within it, by conjugating along paths.
Hence the graph consists of complete components, all of equal size by transitivity.
In particular every transposition of two distinct labels in one component has a lift conjugate to \(c\).
Every such lift has \(\epsilon=-1\), since \(\epsilon\) is a character and complex conjugation negates \(i\).

Let \(N\) be the normal subgroup of \(G\) generated by these conjugates of \(c\).
Its orbits on \(\Omega\) are the graph components.
An element \(v\in M\) is totally real exactly when \(c\) fixes \(g(v)\) for every \(g\in G\), equivalently when \(N\) fixes \(v\).
Consequently \(F_0=F\cap M^N\).
For the inclusion label with stabilizer \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(M/F)\), its orbit size under \(N\) is \([NH:H]=[F:F_0]=\ell\).
Each component has at least two labels.
The element \(\sqrt3\) lies in \(F_0\), so there are at least two components.

Every oriented ratio \(u_j/u_k\) for distinct labels of one component is a conjugate of \(U\).
Indeed some \(g\) first carries \(\{a,b\}\) to \(\{j,k\}\), so (4) gives one of the two orientations.
Next choose a transposition in a different component and a lift conjugate to \(c\).
It fixes both \(j\) and \(k\) as labels, but its sign in (4) is \(-1\), so it replaces this ratio by its inverse.
Conversely (4) shows that every conjugate is one of these ratios.
This proves that every within-component ratio of distinct labels is nontorsion, before asserting any distinctness between the ratios.

## Multiplicative relations, exact degrees and the height product

Suppose \(\prod_j u_j^{h_j}\) is a root of unity, for integers \(h_j\).
For a transposition of any \(j,k\) in the same component, choose the lift just described.
By (4), its image of the product, raised to its common sign, is still torsion and has exactly the exponents \(h_j,h_k\) interchanged.
Dividing gives \((u_j/u_k)^{h_j-h_k}\) torsion.
Thus \(h_j=h_k\) throughout each component.
Conversely the product over each whole component is torsion.
Every image of that product by (4) is a component product or its inverse, and every component product has modulus one, since the unique labels of nonzero log modulus are in the same component and their logs sum to zero.
Kronecker's theorem applies because the product is an algebraic unit.
This is the constant-on-components case of the [two-exterior relation calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations), here with the orientations fixed by \(i\).

If two within-component oriented ratios agree even up to torsion, the difference of their exponent vectors is constant on each component.
It also sums to zero on each component, because each oriented ratio contributes \(+1,-1\) within one component.
The vector is therefore zero, forcing the two ordered pairs to coincide.
There are consequently \(d(\ell-1)\) distinct conjugates of \(U\).
Its inverse is among them, and the two roots of \(X^2-(D+2)X+1\) are distinct.
The automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(U)\) taking \(U\) to \(U^{-1}\) fixes \(D\), so \([\mathbb Q(U):\mathbb Q(D)]=2\).

In fact the stabilizer of \(D\) in \(G\) is the stabilizer of the unordered edge \(\{a,b\}\).
Indeed an element fixes \(D\) exactly when it sends \(U\) to \(U\) or \(U^{-1}\), and the distinctness of the ordered ratios and (4) give exactly these two possible endpoints.
It therefore preserves the component of \(a\).
The stabilizer of that component is \(NG_a\), where \(G_a\) fixes the single label \(a\), since the component is the \(N\)-orbit of \(a\).
The field fixed by \(NG_a\) is \(a(F_0)\), by the characterization of \(F_0\) above.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(D)\) contains \(a(F_0)\), in particular \(\sqrt3\).
When \(\ell=2\) the unordered edge is the entire component, so \(\mathbb Q(D)=a(F_0)\).

Only the component of \(a,b\) has nonneutral labels.
Its \(u_a,u_b\) have moduli \(U^{1/2},U^{-1/2}\), respectively, and all its other \(\ell-2\) labels have modulus one.
The two orders of \(a,b\) give the largest and smallest moduli.
An ordered ratio from \(a\) to a neutral label in that component, or from such a label to \(b\), gives modulus \(U^{1/2}\).
Their inverses give modulus \(U^{-1/2}\).
All remaining ratios have modulus one, including those from at least one other component.
This proves the stated counts and the measure identity in (1).
For \(\ell=2\), \(U>1\) is a real algebraic unit with reciprocal conjugate and all other conjugates on the circle, of degree \(d\ge4\).

If \(\ell\ge3\), choose two distinct labels \(j,k\) in the same component.
The constant-exponent criterion proves that \(u_j,u_k\) are multiplicatively independent, since a third label of their component carries exponent zero in any relation involving only those two.
The orbit of this ordered pair has at most \(2d(\ell-1)\) elements by (4), allowing either common sign, so its joint field degree \(D_2\) satisfies that bound.
Both absolute logarithmic heights are \(m(\beta)/(2d)\), since the units are conjugates of the generator of \(L\).
The [rank-two height product of Amoroso and David, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
           \left(\frac{m(\beta)}{2d}\right)^2
                    \ge \frac{c_2}{D_2(\log(3D_2))^{212}} .
\]
This proves (2), reusing the [joint-degree height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) after verifying independence for these two actual labels without any compression hypothesis on \(\beta\).
If \(\ell\) is bounded between \(3\) and \(\ell_0\), the lower bound tends to infinity as \(d\to\infty\), and bounded degrees have a positive gap by the finiteness of unit minima of bounded degree and bounded measure.
In general (1) and (2) give \(m(U)\gg\sqrt{d(\ell-1)}/(\log(6d(\ell-1)))^{106}\), tending to infinity with \(d\).
For \(m(\beta)\to0\), degree finiteness gives \(d\to\infty\), while (2) gives \(d/((\ell-1)(\log(6d^2))^{212})\to0\).
Substitution of the degree in (1) gives the asserted restriction on \(k\).

## The invariant on transfers from Salem inputs

Use the [Salem specialization of the twelfth-root twist](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis), with \(\delta=\alpha\) and \(\beta=w\alpha\).
Each off-circle conjugate of \(\beta\) has modulus \(\alpha\) or \(\alpha^{-1}\), since every image of \(w\) has modulus one and all other conjugates of \(\alpha\) have modulus one.
Every nonreal trace pair therefore contributes \(\alpha^2+\alpha^{-2}-2\) to \(D\), proving its formula in (3).
In particular \(r=1\) gives \(U=\alpha^2\), a Salem number since every power of the original positive Salem generator has the full degree.
The modulus counts in (1) consequently force \(\ell=2\) in that case.

Write \(t_0=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}>2\), \(R=\min_{\mathbb Q}(t_0)\), and \(P(X)=X^nR(X+X^{-1})\).
The trace degree is \(n\), and every conjugate of \(t_0\) other than \(t_0\) belongs to \((-2,2)\), by the Salem conjugate list.
In particular \(-t_0\) is not a conjugate, and an embedding of \(\mathbb Q(t_0)\) fixing \(t_0^2\) must fix \(t_0\).
Since \(D=r(t_0^2-4)\), this proves \(\mathbb Q(D)=\mathbb Q(t_0)\).
Taking the norm yields
\[
   N_{\mathbb Q(t_0)/\mathbb Q}(D)
      =r^n R(2)R(-2)=r^n(-1)^n P(1)P(-1).
\]
The other \(n-1\) conjugates of \(D\) lie in \([-4r,0)\).
Thus on both Salem signatures the integer norm is determined exactly by the two endpoint evaluations of the original minimum.
If the Salem trace field contains \(\sqrt3\), then the real field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) meets \(\mathbb Q(w)\) in exactly \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
The twist has degree ratio two and \(r=1\) by the [embedding count](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis), and the preceding argument gives \(\ell=2\) and \(m(\beta)=2\log\alpha\).
Conversely every input with \(r=1,\ell=2\) supplies the Salem number \(U\), and its trace field \(\mathbb Q(D)\) contains \(\sqrt3\).
Taking infima gives the two inequalities for \(\lambda_{1,2}\).
The unrestricted gap and a uniform lower bound on the branch \(\ell\to\infty\) remain open.
