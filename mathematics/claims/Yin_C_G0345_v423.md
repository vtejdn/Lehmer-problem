# Testing the Lehmer gap with double factors at a fixed width

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quantitative test with the multiplicity held at two

Write \(B=(\log 2)/4\), let \(\lambda\) be the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures, and fix either sign \(\varepsilon\).
At integers \(n\ge2,\ b\ge1\), let \(D(n,b)\) be the maximum number of **distinct positive measures** on a single signed row
\[
  G=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y),
  \qquad ((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n .
\]
Count primitive irreducible factors of multiplicity at least two with a nonzero nontorsion root \(\alpha\) satisfying \(A(\alpha^n)\ne0\).
Include zero as a default maximum.
This uses the same effective factor and algebraic-measure comparisons as the [all-integer count](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#statement).

For \(r\ge1\) set \(T_r=r(r+1)/2\) and
\[
  C_r=\min\left\{2T_r+\sum_{i=1}^r i\lfloor T_r/i\rfloor,\quad 3T_{4r}\right\}.
\]
If \(\lambda<B/C_r\), there is a single finite width \(w\) at which every sufficiently large integer \(n\) has a passing row with at least \(r\) different values.
The same assertion holds on **every** sequence \(b(n)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\), even when the width grows arbitrarily slowly.
In particular \(C_1=3,\ C_2=11,\ C_r=O(r^2)\).
A bound \(D(n,b(n))<r\) on any infinite subsequence certifies the explicit unrestricted gap \(\lambda\ge B/C_r\).
For every such width sequence the zero-gap alternative is equivalent to \(D(n,b(n))\to\infty\), and a positive gap is equivalent to boundedness on some infinite subsequence.
The finite width \(w\) may depend on the input measure and on \(r\).
It applies before choosing any sufficiently large exponent.

There is a separate quantitative improvement for the [original count with growing multiplicity](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement), also valid for the [all-integer extension](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#statement).
If that count stays below \(r\) infinitely often, then \(\lambda\ge B/T_r\).
In particular an infinite exclusion of two values on the original test gives \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/12\).

<a id="multiples"></a>
## Distinct measures generated by one input

For any positive irreducible measure \(h\), the [norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) gives a number \(\delta\) of that exact measure whose every positive power generates \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
The total measure of \(\delta^i\) is therefore \(ih\), by the absolute-height power identity and the unchanged degree.
Consequently the spectrum contains all the positive integral multiples of each of its elements.
Positive measures of integer polynomials factor into contributions of primitive irreducibles and integral contents.
A sufficiently small positive measure, below \(\log 2\), thus supplies a positive irreducible measure no larger than itself.
If \(T_r\lambda<B\), choose such a measure \(h<B/T_r\).
Then \(h,2h,\ldots,rh\) have sum \(T_rh<B\).
The [limit for distinct values](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#statement) proves the claim for the original count.

For the double-factor construction below take a finite list of the powers \(\alpha_i=\delta^i,\ 1\le i\le N\), with \(S=\sum_i ih=T_Nh<B\).
They are reciprocal algebraic units by the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper).
Their minima \(P_i\) and the minima \(Q_i\) of \(\alpha_i^n\) are all monic.
Within each of the two lists the polynomials are pairwise distinct, since their measures are respectively \(ih\) and \(nih\).
The degrees of \(Q_i\) equal those of \(P_i\) for **every** exponent.

<a id="construction"></a>
## Prescribing unequal orders at one finite width

Fix integers \(t_i\ge2\), put \(H_0=\sum_i t_i ih\), and assume \(H_0<B\).
Let \(w_0\) be a constant to be chosen, \(l=2(w_0+1),\ L=n(w_0+1)\), and \(d_i=\deg P_i\).
In the [divided-derivative box](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#seeds) take exactly \(t_i\) jets in the field of the \(i\)-th seed.
There are \(J=\sum_i d_it_i\) coordinates over \(\mathbb Q\) and the logarithmic derivative term has multiplier \(J'=\sum_i d_i t_i(t_i-1)/2\).
For \(l>J\) multiplication of the individual embedding bounds and the same difference of two integral coefficient vectors give a nonzero
\(F_0=U_0(X^n)+XV_0(X^n)\) with rows of degrees at most \(w_0\), divisible by every \(P_i^{t_i}\), and
\[
 \log\|F_0\|_1\le
 \frac{L H_0+J\log(6l)+J'\log(1+L)}{l-J}+\log(2l).
\]
Given \(\eta>0\) with \(\eta<\min(h,B-H_0,H_0)\), first take \(w_0\) so large that \(H_0 l/(l-J)<H_0+\eta/2\), then let \(n\) tend to infinity with this width fixed.
It follows that \(2\log\|F_0\|_1/n\le H_0+\eta/2+o(1)\).
A single nonzero row would contain \(\prod_iQ_i^{t_i}\) before substitution and cost at least \(nH_0\) in log norm.
Both rows therefore occur for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Let \(J_0\) be their primitive polynomial gcd and let \(c_i=\operatorname{ord}_{Q_i}J_0\).
The [derivative divisibility after removing a gcd](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) gives
\[
 \prod_i Q_i^{t_i+c_i-1}\mid
       nY(U'_0V_0-U_0V'_0)-U_0V_0 .
\]
The proof at each node uses only that node's prescribed order and applies even if \(c_i\ge t_i\).
The right side is nonzero because the multiplier of its least product term is \(n(\operatorname{ord}_0 U_0-\operatorname{ord}_0 V_0)-1\).
Its one-norm is at most \((2nw_0+1)\|F_0\|_1^2\).
The lower bound for its measure, divided by \(n\), is \(H_0-S+\sum_i c_i ih\).
Thus \(\sum_i i c_i\le T_N\) eventually, using \(\eta<h\) and integrality.

Divide both rows by \(J_0\).
Every index with \(c_i\le t_i-2\) keeps a double factor, and coprimality and the graph value equation give two nonzero row values there.
Both quotients have one-norm at most \(2^{w_0}\|F_0\|_1\) since \(M(J_0)\ge1\).
The [two signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) and an integer slope from \(0\) to \(N\) preserve all these double factors and all their nonzero numerator tests at the prescribed sign and width \(w=w_0+1\).
This costs a factor at most \(4(1+N)2^{w_0}\) in row norm relative to \(F_0\).
All of these constants are fixed before \(n\) increases, so the strict budget follows from \(H_0+\eta<B\).

For any \(b\ge w\), write \(b-w=2k+e\) with \(e\in\{0,1\}\).
Multiply the signed row by \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\).
This multiplier is its own reversal at formal width \(2k+e\), and is nonzero at every selected powered node.
The new graph is the old one times the multiplier evaluated at \(X^n\), and the row one-norm increases by at most a factor two.
Thus along the stated \(b(n)\) the term \(\log(2nb(n)+1)/n=o(1)\) stays inside the same strict slack.

<a id="cost"></a>
## Two choices of multiplicities and the gap dichotomy

For the first cost take \(N=r\) and \(t_i=2+\lfloor T_r/i\rfloor\).
Every \(c_i\le\lfloor T_r/i\rfloor\), so all \(r\) survive, and \(H_0/h=2T_r+\sum_i i\lfloor T_r/i\rfloor\).
For the second cost take \(N=4r\) and \(t_i=3\) for all \(i\).
If at most \(r-1\) indices survived, at least \(3r+1\) would have \(c_i\ge2\).
Their contribution to \(\sum i c_i\) would be at least \(2T_{3r+1}>T_{4r}\).
Thus at least \(r\) survive at cost \(H_0/h=3T_{4r}\).
Choosing the smaller cost and then an actual positive measure \(h<B/C_r\) proves the construction.

Conversely the [derivative product](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product), valid at [every integer exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#realization), gives
\(n\sum_i m(P_i)\le 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)<nB\) for any one representative per qualifying value.
A positive gap bounds all counts above by \(B/\lambda\), for every \(n,b\).
At zero gap the construction supplies every \(r\) eventually on every stated width sequence.
This yields both equivalences.
For example, one may take primes \(p\) and \(b(p)=\max(1,\lfloor\log p\rfloor)\).
The [local divisibility at large primes](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#degree) then forces the full reduced product of the selected double factors to divide \(\bar A\) after primitive scalar normalization, since \(p>4b(p)+1\) eventually.
The theorem leaves open whether two distinct values can be excluded on an infinite subsequence of this fixed-multiplicity test.
