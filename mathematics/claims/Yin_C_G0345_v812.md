# An octic equation for critical inverse pairs on a two-pole section

<a id="statement"></a>
## A polynomial exceptional set for the local resultant test

Use the [critical points](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#statement) of the two-pole curve at coprime positive exponents \(k,l\).
In the indeterminate \(Z\) set
\[
 h=Z^2-4,\quad J=(h-9)(h-16),\quad \Delta=1-4J,\quad
 A=lJ-7kZ(12-h),\quad G=ZhJ(2h-25)\Delta.
\]
The polynomial
\[
 E(Z)=\frac{\Delta A^2-k^2 Z^2 h(2h-25)^2}{J}
\]
lies in \(\mathbb Z[k,l,Z]\), is quadratic homogeneous in the exponents and has degree eight in \(Z\), with leading coefficient \(-4l^2\).
An explicit choice of the exceptional polynomial in the [good-prime theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#statement) is
\[
 B(k,l)=2kl\operatorname{disc}_Z(E)\operatorname{Res}_Z(E,G).
\]
It is a nonzero binary form of degree 56, so the sum of the logarithms of its distinct prime divisors is at most \(56\log(k+l)+O(1)\) whenever its value is nonzero.
This controls the support of exceptional primes; their powers in the section discriminant require separate estimates.

Over characteristic zero for \(B(k,l)\ne0\), or over any residue field of characteristic prime to this integer, the eight roots of \(E\) correspond bijectively to the inverse pairs of critical points.
The coordinate \(Z\) is \(U+U^{-1}\).
For each root take both square roots \(t\) of \(h\), and set
\[
 U=(Z+t)/2,\qquad y=kZt(2h-25)/A,\qquad V=(y-1)/(2(t-3)(t-4)).
\]
These give the sixteen points, exchanged within each pair by \(t\mapsto-t\).

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Recovering the quadratic branches

On the torus put \(t=U-U^{-1}\), \(a=(t-3)(t-4)\), \(b=(t+3)(t+4)\) and \(y=2aV+1\).
The curve equation says \(aV^2+V+b=0\), so \(y^2=\Delta\) and \(ab=J\).
On the open set where the denominators are invertible, logarithmic differentiation of \(V=(y-1)/(2a)\) gives
\[
 \frac{1}{V}\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{ab'-a'b}{2J}+\frac{J'}{2Jy}
 =\frac{7(12-h)}J+\frac{t(2h-25)}{Jy}.
\]
Since \(U\,dt/dU=Z\), tangency to the direction \((k,l)\) is equivalent there to \(Ay=kZt(2h-25)\).
Squaring and imposing \(y^2=\Delta\) gives \(JE=0\).
To check divisibility before division, at both \(h=9\) and \(h=16\) one has \(49(12-h)^2=h(2h-25)^2\); the remaining terms of the numerator vanish modulo \(J\).
Division by the monic \(J\) is integral, and the leading term follows from the displayed formula.
The discriminant is homogeneous of degree 14 in the coefficients of the octic, and the resultant with the fixed degree-13 polynomial \(G\) has degree 13 in those coefficients.
For a verification that neither vanishes identically, at \(k=l=1\) reduce modulo 19.
The Euclidean algorithm gives \(\gcd(E,E')=\gcd(E,G)=1\); both computations can be made by forming \(h,J,\Delta,A,G\) above in \(\mathbb F_{19}[Z]\) and dividing the numerator exactly by \(J\).

Away from \(B\), the octic has eight simple roots disjoint from \(G=0\).
At each, \(A\ne0\) follows from \(\Delta A^2=k^2 Z^2 h(2h-25)^2\), and both square roots of \(h\) are distinct.
The reconstructed \(U,V\) are units because \(Z^2-t^2=4\) and \(J\ne0\).
The differentiation identity now verifies tangency in both directions and reconstructs the critical equations scheme-theoretically on this open set by eliminating \(y\) and adjoining \(t^2=h\).
This gives sixteen reduced points there.
To see that they exhaust the projective intersection in each permitted characteristic, first exclude a common component.
The curve has no boundary component by its four corner coefficients, and a common torus component would either enter the reconstruction open set or have constant \(Z\) or \(A=0\).
Every excluded coordinate condition restricts \(Z\) to finitely many values, including \(A=0\) since \(l\) is invertible.
A fixed \(Z\) allows at most two \(U\) and at most two \(V\) over each, since the middle coefficient of the quadratic is one.
Thus there is no common component, and the intersection length of two bidegree \((4,2)\) equations is sixteen, as in the [intersection calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#critical).
This excludes any additional point and proves transversality.
Under simultaneous inversion \(Z\) is fixed, \(t\) changes sign and \(V\) goes to \(V^{-1}\), since the reconstructed point with the other \(U\) is unique for that fixed \(Z\).
For a good prime the squarefree octic and quadratic extensions in this presentation lift their roots uniquely in unramified extensions by Hensel's lemma.

<a id="value"></a>
## The critical value in the degree-eight algebra

At a good prime \(p\), put \(R=\mathbb Z_p[Z]/(E)\), where the leading coefficient is a unit, and \(R'=R[t]/(t^2-h)\).
Both are finite étale, of ranks eight and sixteen over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
The reconstruction is valid over \(R'\) since all its denominators are units modulo \(p\).
Compute \(W=V^k/U^l\) and \(T=W+W^{-1}-2\).
The involution \(t\mapsto-t\) exchanges \(W\) with \(W^{-1}\), so \(T\in R\) by the unique decomposition in the basis \(1,t\).
For each residue orbit of octic roots of size \(f\), let \(v_p(T)\) denote the valuation in the corresponding unramified completion, normalized by \(v_p(p)=1\).
Assume the section \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) is squarefree over the rationals.
For each monic integral factorization \(P=SM\), one has
\[
 v_p\operatorname{Res}(S,M)\le
 \sum_{\substack{\text{octic orbits}\\v_p(T)>0,\;4\mid v_p(T)}} f\,v_p(T)/2.
\]
Indeed \(T=(W-1)^2/W\), so both geometric branches have identical \(v_p(W-1)=v_p(T)/2\), finite by squarefreeness.
The total residue degree above the octic orbit is \(2f\) whether its quadratic algebra over that completion splits or stays inert.
Only positive even valuations of \(W-1\) can contribute, and the [local square test](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local) bounds the contribution by half this valuation times the total residue degree.
In particular it is enough to evaluate \(T\) modulo \(p^4\) on the octic residue factors to rule out any contribution when all those reductions are nonzero.
