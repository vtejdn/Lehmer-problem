# Comparing small units across fields and counting Salem traces over one base

<a id="statement"></a>
## Distinct fields of units whose powers preserve degree

Let \(x_1,x_2\) be nontorsion algebraic units, with \(K_i=\mathbb Q(x_i^a)\) independent of the positive integer \(a\), \(d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\), and \(D=[K_1K_2:\mathbb Q]\).
When the fields are distinct as subfields of a fixed algebraic closure, there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
 m(x_1)m(x_2)\ \ge\ c\frac{d_1d_2}{D(\log(3D))^{212}}
 \ \ge\ c\frac{[K_1\cap K_2:\mathbb Q]}{(\log(3D))^{212}}.              \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) is total logarithmic Mahler measure.
This applies to the representatives of any proposed unrestricted shrinking sequence supplied by the [measure-preserving norm construction](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
For distinct Salem fields in their positive real embeddings, the intersection of the fields equals the intersection of their totally real trace fields.

Let \(B\) be a totally real number field of degree \(n\ge2\).
For \(H>0\) consider the \(t\in\mathcal O_B\) such that exactly one real embedding has \(|\sigma(t)|>2\), the others have \(|\sigma(t)|\le2\), and
\[
                  h_t=\operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(|\sigma(t)|/2\bigr)\le H
\]
at that exceptional embedding.
There is an absolute \(c'>0\) such that any two such traces whose quadratic extensions \(B(\sqrt{t^2-4})\) are different satisfy
\[
                         h_{t_1}h_{t_2}\ge
                                c'\frac{n}{(\log(12n))^{212}} .          \tag{2}
\]
In particular for each fixed \(H\), at all sufficiently large \(n\) the entire set of these traces is either empty or exactly
\[
          \bigl\{\,\pm T_k(t_0):1\le k\le \lfloor H/h_0\rfloor\,\bigr\}.
                                                                            \tag{3}
\]
Here \(T_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\), \(t_0\in\mathcal O_B\) is the trace of the primitive norm-one unit of a single quadratic extension, it is positive at the unique exceptional place, and \(h_0=h_{t_0}\).
Every element in (3) has the same exceptional place.
A base admitting such a trace has trivial rational automorphism group for these \(n,H\).
The estimate (2) also bounds the measure required of a second extension when its cutoff is allowed to grow.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The joint field and multiplicative independence

If \(x_1,x_2\) were multiplicatively dependent, two nonzero signed integer powers would agree, and they would generate the same field by the power hypothesis.
Thus they are independent, and the [rank-two height-product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives (1) after multiplying the two absolute heights by \(d_1d_2\).
For the second inequality use \([K_1K_2:K_1]\le[K_2:K_1\cap K_2]\), which does not assume linear disjointness.
In a Salem field every proper subfield is contained in its totally real trace field, by the [real-embedding count](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#subfields), valid from degree four.
Consequently no two different Salem fields can contain one another: a Salem field is not totally real.
Their intersection is proper in each and lies in both trace fields, proving the equality.

<a id="base"></a>
## Recovering every trace from the unique quadratic extension

Each admissible \(t\) generates \(B\), because exactly one embedding has value of absolute value exceeding two, whereas each embedding of any proper subfield would have more than one extension to \(B\).
No other embedding gives exactly \(2\) or \(-2\), since either value as a conjugate would force \(t\) to be rational.
The quadratic \(X^2-tX+1\) therefore has distinct circle roots at all but one place and two real roots at the remaining place.
In particular it is irreducible over \(B\), as its discriminant is negative at a real place.
Either root \(x\) is a unit, generates \(K=B(\sqrt{t^2-4})=\mathbb Q(x)\), and has total measure \(h_t\).
At the unique pair outside the circle one root has strictly largest modulus.
The same argument with that embedding as for a Salem number shows \(\mathbb Q(x^a)=K\) for every \(a\).
This holds also if the leading real value chosen as a conjugate is negative, or the chosen copy of \(x\) lies on the circle.
Two distinct extensions of \(B\) here have degree two, intersection \(B\), and compositum of absolute degree \(4n\).
Applying (1) gives (2).

When \(H^2<c'n/(\log(12n))^{212}\), at most one extension occurs.
It has just one split real place, the same for every admissible trace that yields the extension.
The [unit-rank and norm argument](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) gives one primitive norm-one unit \(\epsilon\), oriented to be positive and greater than one at a fixed lift of that split place.
The argument uses only the signatures \((2,n-1)\) and \((n,0)\) and works also for \(n=2\).
Both roots of every admitted trace have relative norm one, so each is \(\pm\epsilon^k\) for some nonzero integer \(k\).
Conversely, for \(t_0=\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1}\), every \(\pm T_k(t_0)\) with \(k\ge1\) has the prescribed unique exceptional place and measure \(kh_0\).
At a place where the extension is complex its two images are inverse and have modulus one.
This proves (3), including that \(t_0\) itself belongs to the set when the set is nonempty.

Finally a rational automorphism of \(B\) permutes the traces under consideration, hence preserves their unique common exceptional embedding \(\sigma\).
Thus \(\sigma\circ g=\sigma\), which by injectivity forces \(g=1\).
This identifies a restriction on the trace field itself, including automorphisms that were not assumed to lift to the initial Salem field.
