# Global restrictions and finite local tests at numerator eight

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed degree on nearby split primes

Fix a positive odd multiple \(h\) of \(39\) with \(5\nmid h\).
For each choice of positive precisions \(a_2,a_3\) there are arbitrarily large prime pairs \(r<q\), \(g=q-r\) a multiple of \(2h\), with the following properties.
Put \(d=8(q-1)/h\), \(b=4g/h\), and for either sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) put
\[
       Q=\frac{X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g)}
                   {X+\varepsilon}.
\]
A monic rational divisor \(S\) of degree \(d\) would have no real root.
It would satisfy \(S(\varepsilon)=1\) and \(N_2=N_3=5^b\), where \(N_j=\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-j)\).
These [signed resultants](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#statement) impose explicit conditions on which primitive \(q\)-th roots occur modulo two and three.

For \(\ell=2,3\) the primitive grid has \(J_\ell=(q-1)/\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(\ell))\) orbits under Frobenius and inversion; select \(m_\ell=8J_\ell/h\) of these orbits.
At least a fraction
\[
                  p_\ell=\frac{m_\ell(J_\ell-m_\ell)}{J_\ell(J_\ell-1)}
\]
of the selections at \(\ell\) violate the signed resultant condition modulo \(\ell^2\).
For independent selections at two and three the fraction violating one or both is at least \(1-(1-p_2)(1-p_3)\).
Yet on the same pairs some choices at both primes lift to monic reciprocal divisors \(F_\ell\in\mathbb Z_\ell[X]\) of \(Q\) of degree \(d\) satisfying
\[
 (F_\ell(\varepsilon),\operatorname{Res}(F_\ell,X^g-2),
                  \operatorname{Res}(F_\ell,X^g-3))
                  \equiv(1,5^b,5^b)\pmod{\ell^{a_\ell}} .                 \tag{1}
\]
The degree is also realized by a monic reciprocal divisor over \(\mathbb Z_5\).
The local selections can depend on the prescribed precisions, which remain fixed while the prime pairs grow.

For a rational \(S\) there would also be totally positive units obtained by evaluating the binomial resultants in real radical fields.
In particular
\[
        5^{-b/2}\operatorname{Res}(S,X^{g/2}-\sqrt3)
                \bmod5\ \in\{1,\,2+\sqrt3,\,2-\sqrt3\}
                       \subset\mathbb F_{25}^{\times}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Moreover \(S(\xi)\) would be an algebraic integer unit for each \(\xi^{2g}=1\) except \(\xi=-\varepsilon\).
The trace polynomial \(T\) defined by \(S(X)=X^{d/2}T(X+X^{-1})\) would have \(T(-1),T(0),T(1)\in\{1,-1\}\) and \(T(2\varepsilon)=T(-\varepsilon)=1\).
These conditions leave rational divisibility in degree \(d\) open.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Prime pairs and the rational share

Take a fixed auxiliary integer \(M\) divisible by six, to be enlarged below using only the prescribed precisions.
Use the splitting field
\[
         L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2Mh},2^{1/(Mh)},3^{1/(Mh)},5^{1/(Mh)}).
\]
It is finite Galois because the roots of unity needed for the three binomials are present.
The [fixed-field density argument](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#prime-pairs) gives \(\gg_{M,h} X/\log X\) completely split primes in \([X,2X]\).
As in the [large-order refinement](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#prime-pairs), discard primes at which an order of \(2,3,5\) is at most \((\log X)^2\).
There are \(O((\log X)^4)\) discarded primes by taking the product of \(j\)-th powers of each base minus one for \(1\le j\le(\log X)^2\).
Two consecutive retained primes then have gap \(g=O_{M,h}(\log X)\).
Taking \(X\) sufficiently large gives \(r>4g\), \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) for any fixed \(C\), and all three orders at both primes greater than \(2g+2\).
Both primes are \(1\bmod 2Mh\) and each of \(2,3,5\) is an \(Mh\)-th power modulo both.
Thus \(2Mh\mid g\), \(Mh\mid J_2,J_3\), and \(Mh\) divides the analogous orbit count for the primitive \(r\)-grid at five.

The [large-order degree argument](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#prime-pairs) applies with \(k=8\).
A rational \(S\) of the given degree has only primitive grid roots at two and three and exactly \(8g/h=2b\) exceptional roots counted with multiplicity at five.
Consequently the [signed norm calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#sign) and its [sign conclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#quartic) give \(S(\varepsilon)=1\) and \(N_2=N_3=5^b\).
The rational divisor is integral, reciprocal and has constant term one; \(Q\) has no roots \(1,-1\) by the [cyclotomic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion).

By the [integer exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers), \(S\) contains the same number \(s\) of exterior roots near the roots of \(X^g-2\) as near those of \(X^g-3\).
Each of its at most \(g\) irreducible factors has its degree divided by \(r\) within \(1/(3(2g+1)^2)\) of its share divided by \(g\).
As in the [sum for a reducible divisor](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#global), this gives
\[
        \left|\frac{s}{g}-\frac8h\right|
          \le \frac{g}{3(2g+1)^2}+\frac{8(g-1)}{hr}<\frac1g
\]
on increasing \(X\).
Since \(8g/h\) is integral, \(s=8g/h\) is even.

<a id="real"></a>
## Why the divisor would have no real roots

For each \(j=2,3\) the [exterior root disks](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#disks) consist of one disk at each root of \(X^g-j\), each containing one simple root of \(Q\).
The positive and negative real centers therefore give one real exterior root of each sign, while the disks with nonreal centers are disjoint from their conjugates.
There are no other exterior roots.
In each set of disks \(S\) selects the even number \(s\) of roots and its nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs.
It therefore selects either both real exterior roots in the set or neither.
A selected real exterior root \(x\) and its inverse contribute a negative sign to \(S(\varepsilon)\) exactly when \(x\) has sign \(\varepsilon\); conjugate nonreal roots contribute positively.
Since \(S(\varepsilon)=1\), the number of sets in which both real roots are selected is even.
It is thus zero or two.

If it were two, \(S\) would contain all the real roots of \(Q\), by reciprocity and the absence of \(1,-1\).
The complementary factor with only nonreal roots would have positive resultant against \(X^g-2\), by conjugation.
But the numerator of \(Q\) is \(15\varepsilon\) at every root of \(X^g-2\), and removal of \(X+\varepsilon\) divides its resultant by \((-\varepsilon)^g-2=-1\).
Since \(g\) is even, \(\operatorname{Res}(Q,X^g-2)=-15^g<0\), contrary to \(N_2>0\).
Hence \(S\) has no real roots.

<a id="traces"></a>
## The two trace restrictions and the fraction excluded

For either prime \(\ell=2,3\), let \(\operatorname{tr}_\ell(X^g)\) denote the sum in \(\mathbb F_\ell\) of \(\bar x^g\) over a selection of primitive roots \(\bar x=-\varepsilon z\) with \(z\) of order \(q\).
Any corresponding monic reciprocal lift \(F_\ell\) with constant term one has
\[
    \operatorname{Res}(F_\ell,X^g-\ell)
         =\prod_x(1-\ell x^{-g})
         \equiv 1-\ell\sum_x x^{-g} \pmod{\ell^2},
\]
since \(\prod x^g=1\).
The sum reduces to \(\operatorname{tr}_\ell(X^g)\) by inversion.
Because \(b\) is even, \(5^b\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(5^b=25^{b/2}\equiv1+3b\bmod9\).
Thus a rational \(S\) requires
\[
           \operatorname{tr}_2(X^g)=0,\qquad
           \operatorname{tr}_3(X^g)=-b\pmod3.                           \tag{3}
\]

Each orbit contributes a trace in the base field.
Over the full grid their sum is \(-1\), since \(g\) is even, \(q\nmid g\), and \(z\mapsto z^g\) permutes the nontrivial \(q\)-th roots.
As \(\ell\mid J_\ell\), the orbit traces cannot all be equal.
Fix two distinct orbits with unequal traces and transpose them.
For every selection of \(m_\ell-1\) of the other \(J_\ell-2\) orbits the two extensions by precisely one of the fixed orbits have different traces.
At least one extension violates (3).
Dividing the resulting lower bound \(\binom{J_\ell-2}{m_\ell-1}\) by \(\binom{J_\ell}{m_\ell}\) gives \(p_\ell\).
Taking products of the separate upper bounds on the passing fractions gives the simultaneous excluded fraction in the statement.

<a id="precision"></a>
## Matching both binomial resultants at prescribed precision

Let \(E\) be a common exponent for the finite unit groups of \(\mathbb Z/\ell^{a_\ell}\), \(\ell=2,3\), taking exponent one for a trivial group.
Write \(G_\ell=((\mathbb Z/\ell^{a_\ell})^\times)^3\).
Choose \(M\) divisible by \(6E\) and large enough that \(M(h-8)\ge E\max(|G_2|,|G_3|)\).
For each individual primitive orbit \(O\) at \(\ell\), the [coprime lifting and reversal argument](Yin_C_G0345_v216.md#lifts) gives a monic reciprocal divisor \(F_O\) of \(Q\) with constant term one over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
Distinct orbit factors lift simultaneously and their products divide \(Q\), by unique lifting of the pairwise coprime primitive factors against the complement.
All three entries of
\[
             (F_O(\varepsilon),\operatorname{Res}(F_O,X^g-2),
                                     \operatorname{Res}(F_O,X^g-3))
\]
are units at \(\ell\), since the grid avoids \(\varepsilon\) and \(\bar x^g\) has order \(q\) there.
Group the orbits by their tuple in \(G_\ell\), and in each class take disjoint groups of \(E\) orbits, leaving fewer than \(E\) in that class.
The product of the tuple in every such group is the identity in \(G_\ell\).
We have \(J_\ell-m_\ell\ge M(h-8)\) and \(E\mid m_\ell\).
Enough groups are thus available to select precisely \(m_\ell/E\) of them.
The union selects \(m_\ell\) orbits, giving the required degree \(d\) and all three entries one modulo \(\ell^{a_\ell}\).
Since \(E\mid b\), this proves (1).
The two selections are independent and may be combined with the [degree-\(d\) construction at five](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#local).
In particular the three local divisors may be reduced and combined coefficientwise at any desired joint precision; (1) holds to its stated precisions at two and three.

<a id="units"></a>
## Totally positive units from the split binomials

Let \(m\) be any power of two dividing \(g\), \(u=g/m\), \(j=2,3\), and \(\theta=j^{1/m}\) its positive real radical.
The binomial \(Z^m-j\) is Eisenstein at \(j\).
Over \(\mathbb F_5\), every root has order \(4m\), because its \(m\)-th power has order four and \(m\) is a power of two.
For \(m=2^w\) the order of five modulo \(4m\) is \(m\), by \(v_2(5^{2^i}-1)=i+2\).
Hence \(Z^m-j\) is irreducible modulo five and \(\mathbb Z[\theta]/5\) is a field.
For
\[
                A_j=\operatorname{Res}(S,X^u-\theta)\in\mathbb Z[\theta]
\]
the field norm down to \(\mathbb Q\) is \(N_j=5^b\), by multiplying over the conjugates of \(\theta\).
If five divides the norm of an element in \(\mathbb Z[\theta]\), its multiplication matrix reduces to a singular matrix over \(\mathbb F_5\).
In the residue field this requires the element to be zero, so the element is divisible by five in the order.
Removing such factors of five from \(A_j\) divides its norm by \(5^m\) at each step.
It follows that \(m\mid b\) and \(A_j/5^{b/m}\) is a unit of norm one in \(\mathbb Z[\theta]\), by the invertible integral multiplication matrix.
At every real embedding each conjugate pair of roots of \(S\) contributes a positive square modulus to \(A_j\).
The unit is therefore totally positive.

For \(m=2,j=3\) the totally positive norm-one units in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\) are \((2+\sqrt3)^n\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\).
Indeed one can scale any such unit by a power of \(2+\sqrt3\) into \([1,2+\sqrt3)\) in the positive real embedding.
For the resulting unit \(w\), the coefficient \((w-w^{-1})/(2\sqrt3)\) of \(\sqrt3\) is an integer in \([0,1)\), so \(w=1\).
Since \((2+\sqrt3)^3=26+15\sqrt3\equiv1\bmod5\) and \(2+\sqrt3\not\equiv1\), its reduction has order three.
Its square reduces to its inverse \(2-\sqrt3\), proving (2).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Evaluating the divisor at roots of unity

For \(\xi^{2g}=1\) with \(\xi\ne-\varepsilon\), direct evaluation gives
\[
        Q(\xi)=c\,\frac{\xi^r+\varepsilon}{\xi+\varepsilon},
           \qquad c=2\ \text{if }\xi^g=1,\quad c=12\ \text{if }\xi^g=-1 .
\]
Set \(\rho=-\varepsilon\xi\), whose order divides \(2g\) and exceeds one.
The displayed ratio equals \((1-\rho^r)/(1-\rho)\), integral by the geometric sum.
It has integral inverse by the same formula applied to \(\rho^r\) and the inverse of \(r\) modulo its order.
Any prime ideal dividing \(S(\xi)\) must therefore lie over two or three.
But modulo such an ideal the order of \(\xi\) divides \(2g\), whereas all roots of \(\bar S\) have order divisible by \(q\).
It follows that \(S(\xi)\) is a unit in the integers of \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\).

A monic reciprocal polynomial with integer coefficients, even degree and constant one has the form \(X^{d/2}T(X+X^{-1})\) with \(T\in\mathbb Z[Z]\), by the integer recurrence for \(X^k+X^{-k}\).
For the present \(g\), orders \(3,4,6\) all divide \(2g\).
Primitive roots of these orders give \(\xi+\xi^{-1}=-1,0,1\), respectively, so \(T(-1),T(0),T(1)\) are rational integer units.
Also \(T(2\varepsilon)=1\) since \(4\mid d\) and \(S(\varepsilon)=1\).
As \(T(-\varepsilon)\equiv T(2\varepsilon)\bmod3\), its sign is positive.
In particular \((Z-2\varepsilon)(Z+\varepsilon)\mid T(Z)-1\) over \(\mathbb Z\).
