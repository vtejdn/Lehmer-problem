# Proper subfields and small elements at degree minima of Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## The field restrictions

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with irreducible reciprocal minimal polynomial of degree \(d\), and suppose distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient.
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(B=\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\), and \(L=\log M(x)\).
Every proper subfield \(F\) of \(K\) other than \(B\) satisfies
\[
                      [F:\mathbb Q]\le C L^2(\log(3d))^{212}
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\).
On any such growing-degree sequence with bounded \(L\), the automorphism group of \(K/\mathbb Q\) eventually consists precisely of the identity and inversion on the generator.
Every abelian subfield of \(K\), including \(B\) if applicable, also satisfies \([F:\mathbb Q]\le C' L(\log(3d))^{48}\) for an absolute \(C'\).

Suppose further that \(M(x)\) is the smallest Mahler measure greater than one among integer polynomials of degree at most \(d\), and \(d\) is the least degree attaining that value.
Then every \(y\in K^\times\) satisfying
\[
                 h(y)<\frac{c}{L(\log(3d))^{212}}
\]
belongs to \(\mu(K)\langle x\rangle\), where \(\mu(K)\) denotes the roots of unity in \(K\) and \(c>0\) is absolute.
In particular, if \(L_y=\log M(y)>0\) and \(\deg y>C'' L L_y(\log(3d))^{212}\), then \(y=\zeta x^j\) with \(\zeta\in\mu(K)\), \(j\ne0\), and \(\mathbb Q(y)=K,\ L_y=|j|L\).
No integrality hypothesis on \(y\) is needed.

<a id="subfields"></a>
## Counting other conjugates over a subfield

Let \(n=[K:F]\ge2\) and consider the \(n\) images of \(x\) under embeddings of \(K\) fixing \(F\).
If they are just \(x,x^{-1}\), then \(n=2\) and \(F=B\), since the involution \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\) fixes \(F\) and has \(B\) as its full fixed field.
In any other case take an image \(z\notin\{x,x^{-1}\}\).
The [independence of distinct inverse labels](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) applies by torsion compression.
As \(\mathbb Q(x,z)\) contains \(F\), and \(z\ne x\) is a root of the degree \(n\) minimal polynomial of \(x\) over \(F\), its degree over \(K\) is at most \(n-1\) after removing the root \(x\).
Thus the joint degree is at most \(d(n-1)\).
Both heights equal \(L/d\), so the [two-height product inequality](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives \(L^2/d^2\ge c_0/[d(n-1)(\log(3d^2))^{212}]\).
Rearranging gives the bound on \(d/n=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
If a field automorphism sends \(x\) to a root other than \(x,x^{-1}\), the same two independent roots already lie in \(K\).
The stronger joint degree \(d\) would give \(L^2\ge c_0 d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), impossible asymptotically at bounded \(L\).
The map taking \(x\) to its inverse always defines a nonidentity automorphism by reciprocity.

If \(F/\mathbb Q\) is abelian, the degree of \(x\) over the maximal abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\) is at most \([K:F]\).
The [rank-one relative height bound](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) therefore gives \([K:F]h(x)\ge c_1/(\log(3d))^{48}\), proving the second subfield estimate.
Only this last estimate uses abelianity of \(F\).

<a id="minimum"></a>
## Saturation of the small cyclic subgroup at a degree record

For \(y\in K^\times\) multiplicatively independent of \(x\), the [two-height inequality](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) with joint field \(K\) gives \(h(y)\ge c_0/[L(\log(3d))^{212}]\).
Otherwise, unless \(y\) is already torsion, lift a generator \(\rho\in K\) of the free quotient of the group generated by \(x,y\).
Then \(x=\zeta\rho^u,\ y=\eta\rho^v\) for roots of unity in \(K\), with \(u>0\) and \(v\ne0\).
Since \(x\) is an algebraic unit, so is \(\rho\), by applying integrality to the \(u\)-th powers of \(\rho\) and \(\rho^{-1}\).
Its degree is at most \(d\), and \(0<\log M(\rho)\le dh(\rho)=L/u\).
At the stated minimum this forces \(u=1\).
The claimed subgroup and degree criterion now follow using \(h(y)=L_y/\deg y\).
For \(y=\eta' x^j\) with \(j\ne0\), two embeddings of \(K\) giving the same \(y\) would send \(x\) to conjugates with torsion quotient.
Thus \(y\) has degree \(d\), proving also the asserted measure identity.

If the unrestricted positive Mahler gap fails, record inputs of the stated kind exist with measures tending to one and degrees tending to infinity.
Indeed bounded measure and bounded degree leave only finitely many primitive integer polynomials, and any polynomial with measure sufficiently near one has leading coefficient of absolute value one.
Choose the first degree attaining each new minimum, and take a positive-measure irreducible factor at that degree.
Minimality forces its measure to equal the minimum and its degree to be that first degree.
By the [nonreciprocal gap](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) it is eventually reciprocal.
A nontrivial torsion class would produce a [smaller-degree polynomial of exactly the same measure](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers), contrary to the choice of degree.
Hence these restrictions apply to the original unrestricted alternative.
