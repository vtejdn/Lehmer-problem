# Mahler gaps for divisors of monomial perturbations of cyclotomic polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## The position of a reciprocal divisor and the cost of a cofactor

Let \(n\ge1\), \(d=\varphi(n)\), \(k\ge0\) an integer, \(a\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), and set
\[
                         G(X)=\Phi_n(X)+aX^k,\qquad m=\log M.
\]
If \(2k\ne d\), every nonconstant reciprocal irreducible divisor over \(\mathbb Q\) is cyclotomic up to a scalar.
Every divisor \(F\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) with positive logarithmic Mahler measure then satisfies \(m(F)\ge\log\theta_0\), where \(\theta_0>1\) is the real root of \(x^3-x-1\).
These conclusions include \(k>d\) and both degree-one conductors \(n=1,2\).

For \(2k=d\) one has \(n\ge3\).
Factor \(G=FQ\) into monic integer polynomials, allowing either factor to be constant, and put \(e=\deg Q,\ r=\omega(n)\).
Then
\[
           0\le m(G)-m(F)=m(Q)
                    \le\frac ed\bigl(\log|a|+2^r\log(4d)\bigr).            \tag{1}
\]
Along any such sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and
\[
                             \frac{e\,2^r\log(4d)}d\longrightarrow0,      \tag{2}
\]
the ratio \(m(F)/m(G)\) tends to one on the indices with \(m(G)>0\), if there are infinitely many.
Consequently \(m(F)\to\infty\) under (2) and \(d\to\infty\) whenever \(\max\{r,|a|\}\to\infty\).
For every fixed \(K>0,\ \delta>0\) there is a uniform positive gap for \(m(F)>0\) whenever \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\), over all \(n,a\) in the central case.
Every noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor of a central input has degree at least
\[
                  \frac{d\log 2}{8(\log|a|+2^r\log(4d))}.                \tag{3}
\]
In particular, for bounded \(|a|\) under (2), \(Q\) is eventually a product of cyclotomic polynomials and \(m(F)=m(G)\) exactly.
This applies to \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\) with bounded \(|a|\) as \(d\to\infty\).
If \(r\le R\) is bounded, every central monic noncyclotomic irreducible divisor \(J\) satisfies \(m(J)\ge c_R\deg(J)/d\) with \(c_R>0\) independent of \(a\).
Thus a fixed positive degree fraction already supplies a gap for such an individual factor when \(r\) is bounded.

<a id="reciprocity"></a>
## An inverse pair of roots forces the central exponent

The coordinator supplied the inverse-root argument, the transfer by bounding the radial displacement of every root and the use of the house theorem to exclude small noncyclotomic factors.
For \(n\ge2\), \(\Phi_n(1/X)=X^{-d}\Phi_n(X)\).
Suppose a nonzero root \(\alpha\) of \(G\) and its inverse are both roots.
Substitute the equation at \(\alpha\) into the one at \(\alpha^{-1}\) to obtain
\[
                  0=-a\alpha^{k-d}+a\alpha^{-k},
                   \qquad \alpha^{d-2k}=1 .
\]
This calculation uses only the two evaluations, even if the degree of \(G\) changes by cancellation or \(k>d\).
For \(n=1\) the identity \(\Phi_1(1/X)=-X^{-1}\Phi_1(X)\) instead gives \(\alpha^{1-2k}=-1\).
Hence every inverse pair off center consists of roots of unity.
A nonconstant irreducible reciprocal polynomial has nonzero constant coefficient and its nonzero roots occur in inverse pairs, including the self-inverse linear cases.
Its primitive associate must therefore be cyclotomic.

The possible zero root gives the factor \(X\) of measure one.
For a primitive irreducible integer factor of positive logarithmic measure, choose its sign to give positive leading coefficient.
A leading coefficient at least two already gives \(M\ge2\).
Otherwise the factor is monic and its nonzero roots are nonreciprocal algebraic integers, so the [nonreciprocal theorem of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number) yields \(M\ge\theta_0\).
The content of an integer divisor is a positive integer after an overall sign, and content greater than one gives \(M\ge2\) as well.
Multiplicativity and nonnegativity of the logarithmic measures of integer factors give the claimed bound for every positive-measure divisor.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Root moduli in the central case

For \(n\ge3\), \(d\) is even and \(G=\Phi_n+aX^{d/2}\) is monic and reciprocal of degree \(d\) with constant coefficient one.
In particular any monic integer divisor has constant coefficient \(1\) or \(-1\).
At a root \(z\) of modulus \(e^{-t}<1\), the Möbius product for \(\Phi_n\) has at most \(2^r\) binomials in the numerator and denominator together.
Each numerator binomial has modulus at least \(1-e^{-t}\) and each inverse denominator binomial has modulus at least \(1/2\).
Consequently
\[
           |a|e^{-dt/2}=|\Phi_n(z)|
                       \ge\left(\frac{1-e^{-t}}2\right)^{2^r}.
\]
For \(t\ge1/d\) one has \(1-e^{-t}\ge1-e^{-1/d}\ge1/(2d)\).
Taking logarithms gives \(t\le(2/d)(\log|a|+2^r\log(4d))\).
For \(0<t<1/d\) the same upper bound holds because \(d\ge2\) and \(|a|\ge1\).
Reciprocity gives that bound for \(\log|z|\) at every exterior root as well.
If \(Q\) is a monic cofactor, the sum over its roots of \(\log|\alpha|\) is \(\log|Q(0)|=0\).
Thus twice \(m(Q)\) equals \(\sum_{Q(\alpha)=0}|\log|\alpha||\), counted with multiplicity.
Applying the uniform root bound to this sum proves (1), including cofactors that are not reciprocal.

<a id="factor-degree"></a>
## Minimum degree of a noncyclotomic factor

A monic linear factor of a central input has constant coefficient \(1\) or \(-1\), so it is cyclotomic.
For any noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor of degree \(f>1\), the [house bound of Dimitrov recorded in Smy08-U001](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#house-and-its-relation-to-the-gap) gives a root with logarithmic modulus at least \((\log2)/(4f)\).
Comparing this with the upper bound for every exterior root proves (3).
In particular, if \(e\) is strictly below its right side, every irreducible factor of \(Q\) is cyclotomic, with the empty product allowed.
This holds eventually on any sequence for which \(e(\log|a|+2^r\log(4d))/d\to0\), including bounded \(|a|\) under (2).
Thus the exact equality of measures follows from factorization in this range.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Keeping the total measure after removing a cofactor

The [integral bound for a shift of a circle-root polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives \(m(G)\ge\log|a|-C\), where \(C=\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\), since the integrated maximum is at least \(\log|a|\).
The [whole-polynomial gap and growth](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#statement) give an absolute \(\eta>0\) when \(m(G)>0\), and \(m(G)\to\infty\) as \(r\to\infty\) uniformly in \(a\).
In particular \(m(G)\to\infty\) when \(\max\{r,|a|\}\to\infty\), since a bounded-measure subsequence would bound both quantities.
By (1),
\[
            0\le m(G)-m(F)
               \le \frac ed\,m(G)+\frac{Ce}{d}
                            +\frac{e\,2^r\log(4d)}d .
\]
Under (2), \(e/d\to0\).
On the positive branch divide by \(m(G)\ge\eta\) to obtain the asserted ratio and therefore the divergence in the stated range.
When \(m(G)=0\), both integer factors also have zero logarithmic measure.

In increasing order the distinct prime divisors \(p_j\) of \(n\) satisfy \(p_j\ge j+1\).
It follows that \(d\ge\prod_j(p_j-1)\ge r!\).
Thus \(r=o(\log d)\) when \(d\to\infty\), using \(\log(r!)\ge\lfloor r/2\rfloor\log(r/2)\) when \(r\to\infty\) and the immediate bound on subsequences with bounded \(r\).
In particular \(2^r\log(4d)=d^{o(1)}\).
Therefore \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\) implies (2) whenever \(d\to\infty\).
If positive \(m(F)\) tended to zero in this range, a subsequence with \(d\to\infty\) would contradict the ratio and the whole-polynomial gap, since \(m(G)\ge m(F)>0\).
For bounded \(d\), integer factors \(F\) of bounded measure and degree have bounded coefficients by expansion in their roots, so only finitely many occur.
This excludes the remaining possibility and proves a uniform gap depending only on \(K,\delta\).

<a id="bounded-primes"></a>
## Individual factors at bounded prime count

Suppose \(r\le R\) and let \(D(X)\) be the product of the denominator binomials in the Möbius formula for \(\Phi_n\).
There are at most \(2^R\) numerator and denominator factors combined, each of degree at most \(n\).
The integer multiple \(DG=D\Phi_n+aX^{d/2}D\) has a term count bounded in terms of \(R\).
Its exponent span is at most \(\deg(DG)\le d+2^R n\le(1+4^R)d\), since \(n/d=\prod_{p\mid n}(1-1/p)^{-1}\le2^r\).
For a monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(J\) of degree \(f\), take a root of \(J\).
It is a nontorsion algebraic integer unit, and \(DG\) is its nonzero rational annihilator.
The [effective-support inequality for short annihilators](Yin_C_G0092_v1.md#angular-short-annihilator-effective-support) gives a positive lower bound depending only on the term count for \(\operatorname{span}(DG)\,m(J)/f\).
Indeed the effective support is at most the term count, whereas the lower bound for effective support in that inequality tends to infinity when this parameter tends to zero.
This yields \(m(J)\ge c_R f/d\), measured on the original irreducible factor.
