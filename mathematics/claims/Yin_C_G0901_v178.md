# Recovering the trace pole height from the signs of the coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sign count for dense integral traces and the law of their partial evaluations

Let \(q,n\ge2\) be integers and write \(p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ L=2q+1,\ h=1/M,\ K=L^2\), and \(N=n-1\).
Let \(H(x)=\sum_{j=0}^n c_jx^{n-j}\), \(c_0=1\), be a [genuine integral trace realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) at the pole \(M^nH(h)=K\).
Write \(b(H)\) for the number of strictly negative coefficients of \(H\), including its constant term.
There are absolute effective constants in
\[
                  b(H)=\frac{n}{L}+O\big(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}\big).             \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in the height, \(2q+1\) is the nearest integer to \(n/b(H)\).
If \(b(H)\le B\), \(B\ge1\), then
\[
                   q\ll(\log(3B))^3,\qquad n\ll B(\log(3B))^3 .          \tag{2}
\]
All pairs \((q,H)\) with this bound on the negative coefficients alone are therefore effectively finite, allowing dense support and arbitrary coefficient sizes.

The coefficients counted in (1) belong to the trace \(H\), rather than the degree-\(2n\) reciprocal polynomial \(F(Z)=Z^nH(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\).
For general small Salem numbers the [derivative count at the endpoint](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#statement) already gives the negative coefficient count after shifting the trace variable, and the [Bernoulli formula for endpoint Taylor coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v115.md#roots) supplies its probabilistic interpretation.
The pole below fixes the integer height from these signs and gives exact tails for the partial evaluations on its integral lattice.
The partial evaluations of \(H\) are determined by a single sum of Bernoulli variables as in (3).
Their normalized values obey the local Gaussian estimate (6) over the entire prefix.
The one-prime models in the last section meet the exact pole, many zero power sums of \(F\) and all powered capacity tests, while violating (1) by order \(n/L\).

<a id="prefixes"></a>
## An exact rounding identity for all coefficients of the pole quotient

Write \(w\in(0,h)\) and \(-a_1,\ldots,-a_N\in(-4,0)\) for the roots of \(H\), and \(e_j\) for the elementary symmetric functions of the \(a_i\), with \(e_0=1\), \(e_n=e_{-1}=0\).
Put \(Z=\prod_i(1+Ma_i)\) and \(\epsilon=1-Mw=K/Z\).
Let \(X\) be the sum of independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities \(Ma_i/(1+Ma_i)\).
Its generating polynomial gives \(\Pr(X=j)=M^je_j/Z\).
Since \(c_j=e_j-we_{j-1}\), for every \(0\le j\le n\),
\[
 \frac1Z\sum_{i=0}^j c_iM^i=\Pr(X=j)+\epsilon\Pr(X<j).                 \tag{3}
\]
This includes \(j=0\), with the empty lower tail, and \(j=n\), where the sum is \(K\).

By primitive division the integral pole lattice gives \(H=x^{n-1}(x+4)+(Mx-1)G(x)\).
Write \(g_{j-1}=[x^{n-1-j}]G\) for \(1\le j\le N\).
Telescoping those same prefixes gives \(\sum_{i=0}^j c_iM^i=K+M^{j+1}g_{j-1}\), hence
\[
      e_j-Mg_{j-1}=\frac{K}{M^j}\Pr(X\ge j),\qquad
      g_{j-1}=\left\lfloor e_j/M\right\rfloor .                         \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(0<K/M^j<M\) since \(M\ge6\), and the tail is positive.
In particular the errors of all these integer roundings are coupled.

<a id="energy"></a>
## Evaluating a Poisson kernel against the neutral conjugates

Write \(a_i=2-2\cos\theta_i\) and \(d=2n\).
The lift \(F\) has one real exterior root \(T\), its inverse, and the pairs \(e^{\pm i\theta_i}\).
Let \(r=q/p,\ \rho=1-1/d\) and \(P_j=2T^{-j}+2\sum_i\cos(j\theta_i)\).
The [reflected discriminant energy](Yin_C_G0345_v896.md#energy) gives the full bound \(\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^j|P_j|^2/j\ll d\log(2d)\).
The [degree estimate for the genuine Salem lift](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) gives \(q<(\log(6n))^3/2\), since \(\log T<1/q\).
In particular \(d\ge 2p\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in \(q\).

Set \(A(\theta)=(1+M(2-2\cos\theta))^{-1}\).
Its Fourier coefficients at \(j\in\mathbb Z\) are \(r^{|j|}/L\), by the geometric series and \(p^2-q^2=L\).
For \(A^2\) the coefficient at \(j\ge0\) is \(r^j(j+1+2r^2/(1-r^2))/L^2\), by convolution.
In particular for each \(f=A,A^2\)
\[
  |\widehat f(j)|\ll L^{-1}(1+j/L)r^j,\quad
  \sum_{j\ge1}|\widehat f(j)|\ll1,\quad
  \sum_{j\ge1}j\rho^{-j}|\widehat f(j)|^2\ll1 .
\]
For the last estimate \(r^2/\rho\le r\) when \(d\ge2p\), and the geometric moment sums of \(j,j^2,j^3\) are \(O(p^2),O(p^3),O(p^4)\), respectively.
Consequently \(\sum_i f(\theta_i)=N\widehat f(0)+O(\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\), by Cauchy–Schwarz on \(\sum_{j\ge1}\widehat f(j)(P_j-2T^{-j})\).
The constant terms for \(A\) and \(A^2\) are \(1/L\) and \((1+2M)/L^3\).
Thus the mean \(\mu\) and variance \(V\) of \(X\) satisfy
\[
       N-\mu=N/L+O(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}),\qquad
       V=2MN/L^3+O(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}).                                  \tag{5}
\]
The main terms are both of order \(n/L\).
The small-degree exceptions to intermediate estimates can be absorbed effectively in the constants using the stated bound on \(q\).

<a id="signs"></a>
## The sign index and the local Gaussian profile

The polynomial with coefficients \(e_jw^{-j}\), ordered by \(j\) and normalized at one, generates a second Bernoulli sum with success probabilities \(a_i/(a_i+w)\).
Its mass sequence is strictly log-concave by Newton's inequalities, with positive entries from 0 through \(N\); ties at the maximum can only be adjacent.
Denote its rightmost mode by \(m\).
The sign of \(c_j\) is the sign of the change from index \(j-1\) to \(j\) in this second mass sequence, setting entries outside the support to zero.
Hence \(b(H)=n-m\) exactly.
Replacing \(h\) by \(w\) in \(a_i/(a_i+h)\) changes its value by \(O(\epsilon)\) for small \(\epsilon\), uniformly in \(a_i\), by the explicit quotient.
Indeed \(w\prod_i a_i=|H(0)|\ge1\) gives \(Z>M^N/w>M^n\), so \(n\epsilon\le nK/M^n\).
The mean of this second sum is therefore \(\mu+O(1)\) and its standard deviation is at most \(\sqrt n\).
For any such unimodal integer law with standard deviation \(\sigma>0\), the interval of radius \(2\sigma\) around the mean contains mass at least \(3/4\).
Some integer in it has mass at least \(3/(4(4\sigma+2))\).
Every intervening index up to the mode has at least this mass by unimodality; hence the mode differs from the mean by \(O(1+\sigma)\).
Now (5) proves (1).
Since \(L\ll(\log(6n))^3\), (1) implies \(|n/b(H)-L|\ll L^2\sqrt{\log(2n)/n}<1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
It also gives \(n\ll Bq\) if \(b(H)\le B\), for all sufficiently large \(n\); inverting \(n/(\log(6n))^3\ll B\) proves (2).
With bounded \(n,q\) the elementary symmetric functions of the bounded roots give a finite search with exact pole and root-signature tests.

For completeness the first sum obeys
\[
 \sup_{0\le j\le n}\left|
    \frac{\sqrt V}{Z}\sum_{i=0}^j c_iM^i-
    \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(j-\mu)^2}{2V}\right)
                     \right|\ \longrightarrow 0 .                       \tag{6}
\]
Here \(V\to\infty\) by (5), and \(\sqrt V\,\epsilon\to0\).
For a sum of independent Bernoulli variables the centered characteristic function satisfies
\(\log\phi(t)=-Vt^2/2+O(V|t|^3)\) for small \(|t|\), uniformly in the probabilities: Taylor's formula uses the bound \(\mathbb E|B-\mathbb EB|^3\le\operatorname{Var}B\) for one Bernoulli variable, and expanding the logarithm preserves the error bound.
Also \(|\phi(t)|\le\exp(-2V\sin^2(t/2))\), by multiplying the one-variable bounds.
After scaling \(t=y/\sqrt V\) in Fourier inversion on \([-\pi,\pi]\), the integrand is bounded by an integrable Gaussian on \(|y|\le\pi\sqrt V\) and converges, apart from its unit phase, to \(\exp(-y^2/2)\).
The \(L^1\) error goes to zero independently of the phase, giving the local approximation uniformly in \(j\).
Equation (3) gives (6).

## One-prime models with a different sign count

Fix a sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, a denominator prime \(\ell\), \(J=q/2+1\), and \(m=J+1\).
Let \(n\to\infty\) through the [allowable degrees of the integral prefix and pole lattice](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences).
Starting with \(A_0=2n-2J-1\), take \(h_0\) a multiple of the product of primes up to \(q\) such that \(m h_0/n\to1/2\), and put \(A_1=A_0-2m h_0\).
Then \(A_1\to\infty\) is odd and coprime to \(2k\) for \(1\le k<q\).
In the [real model at the exact pole](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#real) replace the factor \(B_{A_0}=1+\cdots+Z^{A_0-1}\) by
\[
       B_{A_1}(Z)\prod_{i=1}^{h_0}\big(Z^{2m}-2b_i Z^m+1\big) .
\]
Initially take all \(b_i=0\).
The degree is unchanged, and the added factors have zero initial coefficients at orders one through \(J\) after their leading ones.
Choose the parameter \(t\) of the same \(Q_t=(Z-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-t Z^{J+1}\) by dividing its original pole correction \(K/q^{2n}\) by the displayed factor evaluated at \(s=p/q\), exactly as in v172.
This produces a positive noninteger rational \(t<1\) tending at fixed \(q\) to \(4\sinh((\log s)/2)\sinh((J+1/2)\log s)\).
It has the same strict circle-root and exterior-root tests.
The added layers have roots of even order, disjoint from the odd star roots, and \(Q_t\) has no torsion roots.
Choose arbitrarily small distinct rational \(b_i\) near zero and adjust \(t\) by the pole equation.
This splits the repeated neutral roots into distinct circle roots and retains separation from the star and \(Q_t\).
It preserves the long real arm and the \(A_1\)-arm capacity bound for \(k<q\); for \(k\ge q\) that real arm alone suffices as in v172.

For \(b_i=0\) the average of \(A(\theta)\) at the roots of \(Z^{2m}+1\) is \(L^{-1}(1-r^{2m})/(1+r^{2m})\).
Indeed the average of \(e^{ij\theta}\) is zero unless \(2m\mid j\), and is then \((-1)^{j/(2m)}\).
The nonidentity \(A_1\)-th roots have mean tending to \(1/L\) as \(A_1\to\infty\), and the remaining \(Q_t\) roots account for boundedly many angles at fixed \(q\).
Each neutral pair contributes the same two equal kernel values, and the added layers contribute \(mh_0\) of the \(n-1\) pairs.
The marked root tends to \(s\), so its trace tends to \(h\).
By choosing the perturbations as small as needed for each \(n\), the mean complement for the second Bernoulli sum divided by \(n/L\) consequently tends to
\[
               c(q)=\frac12\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2m}}{1+r^{2m}}\right)<1 .
\]
The [one-prime approximation and irreducibility argument](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) applies in this same open part of the zero-prefix pole slice, as its compatible integral representative depends only on \(q,n,J\).
Keep the kernel averages and exterior root as close to the model as necessary before selecting the rational polynomial.
The unimodal mode bound above applies over the reals and shows that these irreducible traces over \(\mathbb Z[1/\ell]\) have \(b(H)/(n/L)\to c(q)\) at fixed \(q\).
As \(q\) increases, \(c(q)\to(1+\tanh(1/2))/2\).
Choosing sufficiently large \(n\) for each \(q\) gives models missing the genuine sign law by a fixed proportion.
They satisfy all powered capacity tests, the prescribed pole, the long zero prefix of the reciprocal lift, and can meet any prescribed compatible congruence away from \(\ell\).
Their primitive total Mahler measure pays the denominator cost from v172.
No estimate on the largest coefficientwise distance from an integer polynomial is required for these modified models.
