# Large rational primes in the resultant of each factor at both poles

<a id="statement"></a>
## Prime divisors of individual resultants

Use the [quadratic sections and pole moduli](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#statement) with positive coprime exponents \(k,l\).
At each pole \(j\in\{3,4\}\) use its \(K,\beta,c,\gamma,N\), and let \(b_i=|\operatorname{Res}(S_i,q_j(X^k))|\) for each rational irreducible factor of degree \(d_i\).
For \(M\ge1\) write \(M_{>Y}=\prod_{p>Y}p^{v_p(M)}\), and set \(L=\log(3+k+l)\).
Uniformly for real \(Y\ge2\),
\[
              \log(N/N_{>Y})\ll Y^2 L.                             \tag{1}
\]
On every steep primitive sequence \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\), uniformly in the factor and at both poles,
\[
 \log (b_i)_{>Y}=(\tfrac12\log\beta+o(1))d_i
 \quad\text{if }Y^2 L=o(d_i).
\]
The largest rational prime dividing each \(b_i\) is at least a fixed positive multiple of \(\sqrt{d_i/L}\), hence at least \(d_i^{1/2-o(1)}\).
A positive proportion of \(\log b_i\) comes from primes above a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(\sqrt{d_i/L}\).
If also \(k=o(d_i)\), the same conclusions hold for the split modulus \(m_i\mid b_i\) of [v811](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#statement).
At each pole the qualifying factors then have mutually distinct split prime witnesses, since those moduli are simultaneously pairwise coprime.

<a id="local"></a>
## Bounds in each splitting type

The pole unit \(\beta\) and the integer \(-c\) are units at every prime ideal dividing \(\gamma\), since a prime above a divisor of \(c\) cannot divide \(\gamma\).
In particular the prime two contributes nothing at either pole.
The two terms in \(\gamma\) differ since an equality would give \(c^{2k}=1\) by taking absolute norms.
For an odd split prime \(p\mid N\), write \(t=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\gamma\) for the integral valuation on a chosen branch.
It equals the valuation of \((-c)^k\beta^{-l}-1\).
Use [Yu's two-logarithm bound](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-input-logarithmic-bound) with these fixed bases and exponents \(k,-l\).
If \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), the field hypothesis holds already with residue degree one.
Otherwise the residue field contains a simple root of \(X^2+1\), so one can extend this branch to \(K(i)\) with the same completion by Hensel's lemma.
The global degree is at most four in either case, the residue degree and ramification index are one, and the product of the two truncated heights divided by \((\log p)^2\) is bounded absolutely for the two fixed poles.
Thus \(t\ll p L\) on either branch.
At the finitely many ramified odd primes the same bound \(t\ll L\) holds with constants depending only on the poles, by adjoining \(i\) and using the inequality in that bounded field, then dividing by the relative ramification index.

At an inert prime \(p\) the unique prime ideal is fixed by conjugation, with integral valuation \(v(p)=1\).
Writing \(\beta'=-1/\beta\), both \(\beta^l\) and \((\beta')^l\) are congruent to \((-c)^k\) modulo \(p^t\) at this ideal.
Hence \(t\le v(\delta^l-1)\) for \(\delta=\beta/\beta'\), a fixed nontorsion unit of norm one.
If \(t>0\), its residue order \(r\) divides both \(l\) and \(p+1\), the latter since Frobenius acts by conjugation.
Binomial expansion for odd unramified \(p\) gives \(v(\delta^l-1)=v(\delta^r-1)+v_p(l/r)\): a power prime to \(p\) leaves the positive valuation unchanged, and raising to \(p\) increases it by one.
The nonzero rational integer \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\delta^r-1)|\) has logarithm \(O(r)\) by bounding the two archimedean factors, and is divisible by \(p^{2v(\delta^r-1)}\).
Consequently the entire inert contribution at \(p\) is \(2t\log p\ll p+\log(2l)\).

<a id="sum"></a>
## Summing the rational prime contributions

For every \(p\), \(v_p(N)=\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p} f_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(\gamma)\) with residue degrees \(f_{\mathfrak p}\).
Summing the split bounds uses \(\sum_{p\le Y}p\log p\ll Y^2\) by Chebyshev's elementary bound \(\sum_{p\le Y}\log p\ll Y\).
The inert bounds sum to \(O(Y^2+YL)\) even on replacing the primes by all integers, and the ramified primes contribute \(O(L)\).
This proves (1).
By the [pole size estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#size) and the [integer resultant identity](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#statement), \(\prod_i b_i=N\) and \(\log b_i=(\frac12\log\beta+o(1))d_i\) uniformly.
Subtracting the upper bound (1) gives the full logarithmic mass under the stated little-oh condition.
Taking \(Y=a\sqrt{d_i/L}\) with a sufficiently small absolute \(a>0\) leaves at least \((\log\beta)d_i/4\) above this cutoff for large indices.
The [degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives \(\log d_i\sim\log l\), so this cutoff tends to infinity with logarithmic exponent one half in \(d_i\).
The same subtraction applies to \(m_i\) when \(k=o(d_i)\), since then \(\log m_i\sim(d_i/2)\log\beta\) and \(m_i\mid N\).
