# Reciprocal resultants along Pisot inverse fibers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Resultant bounds and the choice of an inverse

Let \(f\) be a monic Pisot minimal polynomial of even degree \(d\ge4\), positive constant \(q\ge2\), and exterior root \(\theta\).
Use the [integral Schur correspondence](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#correspondence) to put
\[
 N=q^2-1,\qquad A=f^*-qf,\qquad \operatorname{lc}(A)=N+j,\qquad j\ge1.
\]
Reversals of \(f,A\) have degrees \(d,d-1\), respectively.
Write \(\mathcal R(f)=|\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)|\).
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Res}(A,A^*)=-N^{d-2}\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)>0,\qquad
 1\le\mathcal R(f)\le N\left(2j+\frac{j^2}{N}\right)^{d-1}.           \tag{1}
\]
Thus the factor \(N^{d-2}\) forced by the coefficient congruences leaves exactly the integer reciprocal resultant of \(f\).
This comparison alone gives no fixed positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\).

For a supplied Salem polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\) with exterior root \(1<\sigma\le2\), the [uniform inverse theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v292.md#statement) supplies a Pisot inverse at every \(q\ge\lceil B_h\rceil\), where
\[
 h=d/2-1,\qquad
 \kappa_h=288\sqrt{\log(8h)/\pi},\qquad B_h=h(2\kappa_h+1).
\]
One can choose this inverse so that
\[
 \mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}
 \le (50q^3)^{1/(d-1)}(288B_h)^{(d-2)/(d-1)}.                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular at \(q=d^2\) and sufficiently large \(d\), these actual inverses satisfy uniformly in \(P\)
\[
                  \mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}=O(d\sqrt{\log d})=o(q).     \tag{3}
\]
The [sub-plastic Salem family](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#statement) makes (3) a sequence of actual inputs with \(d\to\infty\) and \(\theta-q<1\).
Consequently the proposed bound \(\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}\ge c q\), with an absolute \(c>0\), fails even at the prescribed constants \(q=d^2\) with excess below one.

There is finer variation while the Salem output stays fixed at each degree.
Let \(P_n\) be that family, \(d=60n\), let \(\rho>1\) be the plastic number, and put
\[
 E_n=\sigma_n+\sigma_n^{-1}-2,\qquad q=d^2.
\]
For every positive real sequence \(t_d\) with
\[
             t_d/\kappa_h\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad t_d\le d/4,
\]
there are same-degree integral Pisot inverses returning exactly \(P_n\) and satisfying
\[
 \frac{\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}}{d\,t_d}\longrightarrow\rho,\qquad
 \frac{\theta-q-E_n}{t_d/d}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}}{q(\theta-q-E_n)}
                                              \longrightarrow\rho. \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(t_d=o(d)\) gives \(\theta-q-E_n\to0\) while realizing all the indicated varying scales of the resultant.

<a id="schur-resultant"></a>
## The full coefficient congruence and its scalar resultant

The polynomial \(A\) has degree \(d-1\), leading coefficient \(L=N+j\), constant \(-N\), and all roots strictly inside the unit disk.
At a root \(\beta\) of \(A\) one has \(f^*(\beta)=qf(\beta)\), hence
\[
                         A^*(\beta)=-Nf(\beta)/\beta.
\]
Since \(d-1\) is odd, the product of these roots is \(N/L\).
Taking products and using the leading coefficients gives
\[
 \operatorname{Res}(A,A^*)
 =-N^{d-2}\operatorname{Res}(A,f)
 =-N^{d-2}\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*).
\]
The last equality uses \(d(d-1)\) even and \(A=f^*-qf\), evaluated on the roots of the monic \(f\).
The [stable resultant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v294.md#stable-resultant) gives
\[
 0<\operatorname{Res}(A,A^*)\le(L^2-N^2)^{d-1}
                                      =[j(2N+j)]^{d-1},
\]
proving (1).

The modular rank calculation has the same exact normalization.
Write \(A=zC-N\), where \(C\) has degree \(d-2\), and reverse \(C\) in that degree.
The full inverse congruences are equivalent to \(qC+C^*\equiv0\pmod N\).
Thus \(A,A^*\) reduce to \(zC,-qC\) modulo \(N\).
An integral lift of this Sylvester matrix has rational rank at most \(d\), since each of its columns is a multiple of \(C\) of degree at most \(2d-3\).
Its size is \(2d-2\), so column expansion makes its determinant divisible by \(N^{d-2}\).
The exact identity above identifies the quotient and shows why this divisibility together with stability yields only (1).
The stronger obstruction for the [quadratic descendant](Yin_C_G0345_v294.md#divisibility) uses additional divisibility after its parity reduction and content extraction.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## The additional lower bound that would imply a Salem gap

At \(q=d^2\), a hypothetical bound
\[
             \mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}\ge c q,\qquad c>0,              \tag{5}
\]
on a relevant collection of inputs would imply
\[
     c\le N^{1/(d-1)}
           \left(\frac{2j}{q}+\frac{j^2}{Nq}\right).
\]
The [branch identity](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#branch) identifies \(j/q=(\theta-q)(1+O(1/q))\) when the excess is bounded.
Since \(N^{1/(d-1)}\to1\), (5) would give a positive lower bound on the excess within that collection.

For a Salem gap it would suffice to show that, from every hypothetical sequence of Salem numbers tending to one, one can select same-degree inverses at \(q=d^2\) with \(\theta-q\to0\) and satisfying (5) with a common \(c>0\).
The uniform inverse theorem supplies small-excess inverses, but does not supply the additional lower bound.
A further sufficient form would prove (5) on a sufficiently small fixed-excess range containing all such selected inverses.
A lower bound merely on some inverses whose excesses stay bounded away from zero would not give this implication.
Equations (3) and (4) disprove the universal lower bound on all inputs of excess below one, including a degree-growing family at exactly the prescribed constants.
They also show that the choice of inverse is substantive when using a property of its resultant to constrain its Salem output.

<a id="fiber"></a>
## Exact formulas in an arbitrary same-output fiber

Write
\[
 P(z)=z^mQ(z+z^{-1}),\qquad m=d/2,\qquad
 T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1},\qquad E=T-2,\qquad H(X)=Q(X)/(X-T).
\]
Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_h\) be the neutral trace roots, with \(h=m-1\).
Every integral inverse of constant \(q\) in the [inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) has the form
\[
 f=P+(z-1)z^{m-1}R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
 R(X)=(1-q)H(X)-\sum_{i=1}^h b_i\frac{H(X)}{X-x_i}.                 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\), \(b_i>0\), and \(c_i=b_i/(2+x_i)>0\) satisfy \(\sum_i c_i<q+T/2\).
Put
\[
 \mathfrak a=|H(-2)|=\prod_i(2+x_i),\qquad Z=H(T)=Q'(T)>0,\qquad
 D_Q=\operatorname{disc}Q>0.
\]
Evaluation of (6) gives the exact identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |R(T)|&=Z\left(q-1+\sum_i\frac{b_i}{T-x_i}\right),\\
 |\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)|
       &=\frac{D_Q\,\mathfrak a}{Z}
               \left(q-1+\sum_i\frac{b_i}{T-x_i}\right)\prod_i c_i,\\
 f(-1)&=\mathfrak a\left(2q+T-2\sum_i c_i\right)>0,\\
 P(1)&=-E H(2)<0,\\
 \operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)&=
             f(-1)P(1)\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)^2<0.                  \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the second line use \(R(x_i)=-b_iH'(x_i)\),
\(\left|\prod_iH'(x_i)\right|=\operatorname{disc}H\), and \(D_Q=Z^2\operatorname{disc}H\).
For the third line evaluate (6) at \(-2\), use \(H(-2)=(-1)^h\mathfrak a\), and substitute in \(f(-1)\).
Finally \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\), so evaluating on the roots of the monic \(f\) gives
\(\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)=f(-1)\operatorname{Res}(f,P)\).
Both \(f,P\) have even degree, and evaluation of \(f\) at the roots of \(P\) in (6) yields
\[
                \operatorname{Res}(P,f)=P(1)\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)^2.
\]
The product of the factors \(z-1\) is \(P(1)\), the product of the powers \(z^{m-1}\) is one, and every trace root occurs twice.
This proves the final identity including its sign.

<a id="small-residues"></a>
## A uniform upper bound from bounded residues

Choose the inverse given by [covering the positive box](Yin_C_G0345_v292.md#covering), so \(1\le c_i\le C_h=2\kappa_h+1\).
Then \(b_i\le4C_h\) and \(\sum_i b_i\le4B_h\).
For \(1<\sigma\le2\) one has \(2<T\le5/2\), and consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |R(T)|&\le(q+4B_h)(9/2)^h,\\
 |f(-1)|&\le(2q+5/2)4^h,\\
 |P(1)|&\le\tfrac12\,4^h.                                         \tag{8}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first bound uses (6) directly, with
\(H(T)/(T-x_i)=\prod_{j\ne i}(T-x_j)\le(9/2)^{h-1}\).
It uses no lower bound on a root separation.
The other bounds follow from (7).

Replace the monomial rows of the Vandermonde matrix at the \(x_i\) by the monic polynomials \(1,2T_k(X/2)\) for \(1\le k<h\).
Here \(T_k(\cos t)=\cos(kt)\) is the Chebyshev polynomial.
This replacement preserves the determinant, and every entry of a nonconstant row has modulus at most two on \([-2,2]\).
Hadamard's inequality gives
\[
                         \operatorname{disc}H\le(4h)^h.             \tag{9}
\]
The root formula for \(\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)\), followed by (8), (9) and \(\prod_i b_i\le(4C_h)^h\), yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal R(f)
  &\le(q+5/4)(q+4B_h)^2
       \left(16(9/2)^2(4h)^2(4C_h)^2\right)^h\\
  &=(q+5/4)(q+4B_h)^2(288B_h)^{2h}.
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{10}
\]
Since \(q\ge B_h\) and \(q\ge2\), the first two factors are at most \(50q^3\).
This proves (2).
At \(q=d^2\) the prefactor \((50q^3)^{1/(d-1)}\) tends to one, giving (3).

For a fixed Salem polynomial of degree \(d\ge6\), the same bounded-residue construction as \(q\to\infty\) gives \(\mathcal R(f)=O_P(q^3)\).
Thus even without varying the degree, \(\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}/q\to0\).
The moving-degree bound (3) also rules out rescuing (5) by imposing \(q=d^2\).

<a id="variable-residues"></a>
## Choosing a residue scale on the sub-plastic family

Take
\[
 P_n(z)=\frac{z^{d+2}g(z)-g^*(z)}{z^5-1},\qquad
 g(z)=z^3-z-1,\qquad d=60n,
\]
with exterior root \(\sigma_n\to\rho\), as in [C286](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#statement).
The degree and irreducibility here are exact.
The same source gives \(P_n(-1)=1\) and \(P_n(1)=1-d/5\).
In particular
\[
                        \mathfrak a=(T+2)^{-1}.                    \tag{11}
\]
The [trace discriminant asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v288.md#statement) is
\[
             \log D_Q=(m-4)\log d+2m\log\rho+O(1).                 \tag{12}
\]
Also
\[
                            \log Z=m\log\rho+O(1).                 \tag{13}
\]
Indeed differentiation at the exterior root gives
\[
 P_n'(\sigma_n)=\sigma_n^mQ'(T)(1-\sigma_n^{-2}).
\]
The completion equation and its exponential convergence, proved in the [discriminant calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v288.md#resultant), show
\[
 P_n'(\sigma_n)=
    \frac{\sigma_n^{d+2}g'(\sigma_n)}{\sigma_n^5-1}
                                  (1+O(d\,\sigma_n^{-d})).
\]
Here \(g'(\rho)>0\), and all other displayed factors have nonzero finite limits after removing the power of \(\sigma_n\).
Taking logarithms proves (13).

For the translate of the residue lattice associated with \(q=d^2\), the [covering-radius bound](Yin_C_G0345_v292.md#covering) supplies a point within maximum distance \(\kappa_h\) of \(t_d\mathbf1\).
Thus \(c_i=t_d(1+o(1))\) uniformly in \(i\).
For all sufficiently large \(d\) these entries are positive, and
\[
       \sum_i c_i\le h(t_d+\kappa_h)
                         \le(1+o(1))d^2/8<q+T/2.
\]
They therefore give a strict integral Pisot inverse of degree \(d\) and constant \(q\), returning exactly \(P_n\).
This uses the same translated lattice with a different center.

The numbers \(T-2\) are bounded below by a positive constant in this family.
Consequently
\[
 q-1\le q-1+\sum_i\frac{b_i}{T-x_i}
                                  \le q+O(d t_d)=O(q).
\]
Moreover \(2q+T-2\sum_i c_i\) is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of \(q\).
These two factors and \(|P_n(1)|\) contribute only \(O(\log d)\) to their logarithms.
Finally \(\log\prod_i c_i=h\log t_d+o(d)\).
Substitution of (11)--(13) into (7) gives
\[
 \log\mathcal R(f)
   =2(m-4)\log d+2m\log\rho+2h\log t_d+o(d).
\]
Dividing by \(d-1=2m-1\) proves the first limit in (4).
The differences between the resulting coefficients of \(\log d,\log t_d\) and one contribute \(O(\log d/d)\).

The [exact excess equation](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) is
\[
       \theta-q-E_n
       =\frac{E_n}{\theta-1}
          +\sum_i\frac{b_i}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_i}.                 \tag{14}
\]
The coefficient height one of \(P_n\) gives a bounded sum of its trace roots, since their sum is the negative coefficient of \(z^{d-1}\) in \(P_n\).
Hence \(\sum_i(2+x_i)=2h+O(1)\), and the uniform approximation to the \(c_i\) yields
\[
                        \sum_i b_i=d\,t_d(1+o(1)).                 \tag{15}
\]
The upper bound from (14), using \(\theta>q\), gives \(\theta-q=O(1)\) because \(t_d\le d/4\).
Every denominator in its sum is therefore \(q+O(1)\), uniformly in \(i\).
Equations (14), (15) give \(\theta-q-E_n=(t_d/d)(1+o(1))\), since \(E_n/(\theta-1)=O(1/q)=o(t_d/d)\).
This proves the remaining limits in (4).

For example \(t_d=d^a\), with fixed \(0<a<1\), produces \(\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}\sim\rho d^{1+a}\) while \(\theta-q\to\rho+\rho^{-1}-2\).
Taking \(t_d=\eta d\), with fixed \(0<\eta\le1/4\), instead gives \(\mathcal R(f)^{1/(d-1)}/q\to\rho\eta\) and \(\theta-q-E_n\to\eta\).
Thus within the same output at each degree, raising the resultant to a fixed positive fraction of \(q\) in this construction has a corresponding fixed positive excess cost.
These examples are exact Pisot inverses of actual irreducible Salem polynomials.
Their Salem measures converge to the positive value \(\log\rho\).
For the bounded-residue choices of (2), the uniform inverse estimate gives \(\theta-q\to\rho+\rho^{-1}-2<1\), completing the actual counterexample to the universal form of (5).
