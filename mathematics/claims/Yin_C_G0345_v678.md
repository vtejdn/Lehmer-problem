# Periodic exponent sets avoiding all short signed relations

<a id="statement"></a>
## The density obtained by excluding relations of bounded length

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with minimum \(P\), degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m>0\).
For each integer \(g\ge3\), let \(D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)\) be the supremum of the densities of periodic sets \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z\) with the following property.
No distinct \(t_1,\ldots,t_j\in A\), with \(1\le j\le g\), admit signs \(\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) such that \(\sum_i\epsilon_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\).
Write \(D_1(P)\) for the [limiting maximal density of fully dissociated sets](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement), in which relations of every length are excluded.
Then an absolute constant \(C>0\) gives
\[
                 D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)\ge \exp[-C g^4\log(2g)] .       \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, along every sequence of such units with \(m\to0\), put \(L=\log(1/m)\).
For each fixed \(0<\theta<1\) there is a constant \(a_\theta>0\) depending only on \(\theta\) such that
\[
       3\le g\le a_\theta \bigl(L/\log(2+L)\bigr)^{1/4}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)\ge m^\theta
                                                                        \tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently late inputs.
In this range \(D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)/D_1(P)\to\infty\), by \(0<D_1(P)\le m/\log 2\).

Suppose further that some conjugate \(\gamma\) satisfies \(|\log|\gamma||\ge\eta m\), with fixed \(\eta>0\).
For each fixed \(K>0\), there is \(a_{K,\eta}>0\) such that for sufficiently small \(m\) there is a periodic set of density at least \(Km-O_K(m^2)\) avoiding every relation of length at most \(\lfloor a_{K,\eta}L/m\rfloor\).
Thus for any integers \(g\ge3\) with \(g=o(L/m)\),
\[
                      D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)/m\longrightarrow\infty .   \tag{3}
\]
This includes Salem numbers and requires only the indicated off-circle conjugate.
Both constructions below allow the explicit period \(1+\lceil gd\log(g)/m\rceil\) for the cutoff \(g\) being enforced.
The unrestricted density bound (2) shows that testing dissociation only through a growing length can substantially overestimate the fully dissociated density even when the latter is computed using arbitrary sets.

<a id="period"></a>
## Removing short supports in one period

Let \(A_j\) count the translation classes of inclusion-minimal supports of signed relations with \(j\) terms, normalized by their least exponent.
Here minimality excludes a relation with any sign choice on any proper subset.
Every forbidden support contains such a minimal support, and at weights one and two none exists because \(\alpha\) is nonzero and nontorsion.
All weights in this argument count distinct positions.

For a minimal support of size \(j\), every adjacent exponent gap \(b\) satisfies \(mb\le d\log j\) by the [partial-sum norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v672.md#locations).
Indeed, shift the largest exponent to the left of the gap to zero.
The sum on the left is a nonzero algebraic integer since \(\alpha\) is a unit and the relation is minimal.
Its conjugates at roots of modulus at least one are bounded by \(j\), and the opposite partial sum bounds its conjugates at roots \(\beta\) of modulus less than one by \(j|\beta|^b\).
The product over the latter root moduli, with embedding multiplicities, is \(e^{-m}\).
The integer norm yields the gap bound, so each support with \(j\le g\) has span at most \((g-1)d\log(g)/m\).

Take \(q=1+\lceil gd\log(g)/m\rceil\).
The residues of every such support modulo \(q\) are distinct.
Retain each residue independently with probability \(p\in[0,1]\).
For each normalized support and each translation modulo \(q\), delete a residue from its translate if all its residues were initially retained.
A specified translate of a \(j\)-term support is present with probability \(p^j\).
The union of deletions therefore leaves some choice of residues with density at least
\[
                            p-\sum_{j=3}^{g} A_jp^j .                   \tag{4}
\]
Lifting the surviving residues periodically to all of \(\mathbb Z\) avoids every forbidden relation, including those that cross a period boundary, because a minimal support it contained would have had one of its residues deleted.

<a id="counts"></a>
## Counts uniform in the degree and in the weight

Fix a sequence of signs and an increasing minimal support, and translate the first position to zero.
Dividing the relation by its first signed term gives a nondegenerate equation with \(j-1\) variables in the tuple group \(\langle\alpha\rangle^{j-1}\) of rank \(j-1\).
The powers determine the exponent differences injectively.
The [Amoroso–Viada count at this rank](Yin_C_G0345_v668.md#forest) therefore gives
\[
          A_j\le 2^j(8(j-1))^{4(j-1)^4(2j-1)} .
\]
In particular \(A_j\le \exp((j-1)B)\) for \(3\le j\le g\), where \(B=C_0 g^4\log(2g)\) with absolute \(C_0\).
Set \(p=e^{-B}/4\).
Then the sum subtracted in (4) is at most \(p\sum_{j\ge3}4^{1-j}=p/12\).
Increasing the absolute constant gives (1), and choosing \(a_\theta\) small enough in \(Cg^4\log(2g)\le\theta L\) gives (2).

For the off-circle improvement, fix \(c>0\).
For some \(a=a(c,\eta)>0\), all sufficiently small inputs in that class satisfy
\[
       A_j\le 2^j(c/m)^{j-2},\qquad 3\le j\le \lfloor aL/m\rfloor .    \tag{5}
\]
Otherwise one could choose a sequence with \(m\to0\), \(j=o(L/m)\) violating the count.
Every bounded \(j\) is eventually excluded by the preceding uniform bound at that weight, so \(j\to\infty\).
Pigeonholing the at most \(2^j\) full sign orders would give one fixed signed tuple at each input with more than \((c/m)^{j-2}\) full-order nondegenerate lists up to translation.
For this tuple the product \(H_2\) of one plus the coefficient magnitudes outside the two largest is \(2^{j-2}\).
The [arity lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v672.md#statement) with base \(c/4\) would force \(j\gg_{c,\eta} L/m\), a contradiction.

For a specified fixed \(K>0\), use \(c=1/(8K)\) and \(p=Km\), taking \(m\) small enough that \(p<1\).
The sum in (4) is at most \(4K^2m^2\sum_{j\ge3}(2Kc)^{j-2}\), proving the asserted density for \(g=\lfloor aL/m\rfloor\).
Every smaller cutoff is also avoided by that set.
Alternatively applying (4) and (5) at the smaller cutoff gives the period bound with that value of \(g\).
For \(g=o(L/m)\), each fixed \(K\) eventually gives \(D_{\le g}^{\rm per}(P)/m\ge K-O_K(m)\), which proves (3).

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Exact collisions on the resulting sets

Let \(A\) be any of the periodic sets above, of density \(\delta>0\), avoiding all signed relations of length at most \(g\).
For positive integer \(N\) write \(n_N=|A\cap[0,N)|\).
Among the binary words on these \(n_N\) positions there is a collection with the same exact sum of powers of \(\alpha\), of size at least
\[
                   2^{n_N}e^{-mN}(8N)^{-d},\qquad N\ge1.               \tag{6}
\]
Every two distinct words in the collection have Hamming distance greater than \(g\), since their difference gives a signed relation on precisely the differing positions.
For (6) every binary subset sum has modulus at most \(N\max(1,|\beta|)^N\) at each conjugate \(\beta\).
The [embedding box count](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#count) gives at most \((8N)^de^{mN}\) possible sums, since two sums in one box have difference of modulus less than one at every conjugate and thus coincide by integrality of the norm.
Pigeonholing proves (6).
For the fixed input and mask, \(n_N/N\to\delta\), so the lower limit of the log size of the largest such collection divided by \(n_N\) is at least \(\log2-m/\delta\).
In the regimes (2) and (3), choose at each input a periodic mask with density at least half the corresponding supremum.
These masks force every collision to involve more than \(g\) positions, and their lower rate approaches \(\log 2\) since \(\delta/m\to\infty\), taking \(N\to\infty\) separately at each input.
