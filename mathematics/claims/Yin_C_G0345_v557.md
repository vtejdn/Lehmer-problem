# Multiplicative relations determine a flat reciprocal six-term polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Equality after the indicated substitutions

Let \(F_i=A_i+\epsilon_i X^{N_i}A_i(1/X)\), with \(A_i=c_{0i}+c_{1i}X^{a_i}+c_{2i}X^{t_i}\), signs in \(\{1,-1\}\) and \(0<a_i<t_i<N_i/2\), for \(i=1,2\).
Along any sequence suppose that \(t_i/N_i\to0\) and \(q_i=t_i/\gcd(a_i,t_i)\to\infty\) for each parent.
Choose nontorsion roots \(x_i\) of the respective parents and suppose \(x_i=\omega_i w^{u_i}\), where \(w\) is a nontorsion algebraic number, the \(\omega_i\) are roots of unity and the \(u_i\) are arbitrary positive integers, all allowed to vary.
Eventually there is a polynomial identity
\[
 \frac{F_1(\omega_1 X^{u_1})}{F_1(0)}=\frac{F_2(\omega_2 X^{u_2})}{F_2(0)}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Conversely this identity makes the two specialized root equations equivalent at every common parameter.
If two such parents share a noncyclotomic rational factor, they therefore agree up to their overall sign eventually.
For primitive supports \(\gcd(a_i,t_i,N_i)=1\), (1) forces \(u_1=u_2\), the two exponent triples coincide and \(\omega_2/\omega_1\in\{1,-1\}\), with the corresponding coefficient twist.
For the same primitive parent this twist is one.
Along each such primitive family, eventually every nontorsion root \(x\) satisfies \(\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(x^n)\) simultaneously for all nonzero integers \(n\).
These assertions concern torsion twists and common powers of roots and allow arbitrary degrees of the original factors.

<a id="span"></a>
## Small height at the scale of a sparse relation

Let \(w\) range over nontorsion algebraic units and \(L\) over Laurent polynomials with a bounded number of terms, counting repetitions, and coefficients roots of unity.
Write \(W\) for the span of their exponents before collecting equal powers.
If \(W h(w)\to0\) and \(L(w)=0\), then \(L\) is eventually identically zero.
To prove this, multiply by a monomial so that the exponents are in \([0,W]\), and average over all embeddings of a number field containing \(w\) and the coefficients.
For \(W=0\) the assertion follows by evaluating the constant.
On a subsequence with \(W>0\), put \(\delta=(W h(w))^{1/2}\) and \(z=w/|w|\) at each embedding, writing the embedded coefficients in each sum.
Since \(\mathbb E|\log|w||=2h(w)\), Markov's inequality gives \(W|\log|w||\le\delta\) outside an exceptional set of probability at most \(2\delta\).
On the remaining embeddings each power of \(w\) differs from the corresponding power of \(z\) by at most \(e^\delta-1\).
Since the actual sum vanishes and the phase sum is bounded on all embeddings, the phase sum has mean square \(o(1)\).

For terms with distinct exponents the cross correlation is the average phase of \(\eta w^j\) for a root of unity \(\eta\) and \(0<|j|\le W\).
These nontorsion units have heights tending to zero and leave every finite set, by positivity of height at each nontorsion point.
By [Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) their average phase tends to zero, since the phase map is bounded and continuous near the limiting unit circle and the empirical measures concentrate there.
Passing to subsequences handles the finite number of possible patterns of equal exponents.
After grouping terms at one exponent, each nonzero summed coefficient is an algebraic integer and its mean squared modulus is at least one by its nonzero integral norm.
Expansion of the mean square now rules out every such coefficient.
This extends the [critical-section phase argument](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#orthogonality) to units with arbitrary proportions of conjugates off the circle.

<a id="identity"></a>
## Eliminating and recovering the two short blocks

Write \(k_i=u_i a_i,\ l_i=u_i(t_i-a_i),\ d_i=k_i+l_i,\ n_i=u_i(N_i-t_i)\), and suppress \(i\) in the following coefficient formulas.
After the substitution and normalization the polynomial is precisely \(U+CX^n V\), where
\[
 U=1+PX^k+PRX^d,\quad V=1+RX^l+PRX^d,\quad
 P=(c_1/c_0)\omega^a,\quad R=(c_1/c_2)\omega^{t-a},\quad C=\epsilon(c_2/c_0)\omega^{N-t}.
\]
The equal leading coefficients of \(U,V\) use \(c_0,c_1,c_2\in\{1,-1\}\).
Both \(V_i(w)\) are nonzero by the [middle-cut identity](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#cuts).
The number \(w\) is a unit, since \(w^{u_i}\) is a unit and every nonarchimedean valuation of \(w\) therefore vanishes.
The [height limit for every factor](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#statement) gives \(u_iN_i h(w)=N_ih(x_i)\to\mu>0\).
Thus \(d_ih(w)\to0\) and \((n_1-n_2)h(w)\to0\).
Eliminating the two powers gives at \(X=w\) the equality
\[
 U_1V_2=(C_1/C_2)X^{n_1-n_2}U_2V_1.
\]
Its difference is a Laurent polynomial of at most eighteen terms before collection, of span at most \(d_1+d_2+|n_1-n_2|\).
The preceding lemma proves the identity formally.
Comparison of orders and leading constants at zero gives \(n_1=n_2\) and \(C_1=C_2\), and the two quotients \(U_i/V_i\) agree.

For either quotient put \(j=\min(k,l)\), \(h=\max(k,l)\).
The condition \(q>3\) eventually excludes \(h=j\) and \(h=2j\), since the reduced degree of the pair \((a,t)\) would be two or three in those cases.
In the Taylor expansion of the quotient at zero, the first nonconstant term is at degree \(j\).
If \(k=j\), the coefficient at \(2j\) vanishes since the denominator has its first nonconstant term at \(h>j\), \(h\ne2j\), and the higher shared term has degree \(h+j\).
If \(l=j\), that coefficient is \(R^2\ne0\) by expansion of the denominator, again using \(h\ne2j\).
Thus the quotient determines which short block contains the smaller exponent.
Invert it when that block is the denominator, so the smaller exponent is now in the numerator; this exchanges \((P,k)\) with \((R,l)\) and leaves their product unchanged.
In this orientation the first two nonconstant terms are \(PX^j\) and \(-RX^h\), with the exchanged names if inversion was required.
They recover both exponents and both coefficients and hence the original ordered pair \((U,V)\), even when numerator and denominator have a common cyclotomic divisor.
This proves (1).

<a id="fields"></a>
## Primitive supports and power fields

A shared nontorsion root allows \(u_1=u_2=1,\ \omega_1=\omega_2=1\) and gives the asserted parent equality.
The gcd of the support exponents in (1) is \(u_i\gcd(a_i,t_i,N_i)\).
Thus for two primitive parents the powers coincide, and the ordered supports recover \(a,t,N\) because the first three indices are \(0,a,t\).
Comparing signs at every exponent gives \((\omega_2/\omega_1)^{2e}=1\) for every support exponent \(e\), so primitivity forces \((\omega_2/\omega_1)^2=1\).
When the parent is the same at both indices, coefficient comparison gives \((\omega_2/\omega_1)^e=1\) for every such \(e\), hence the twist is one.
For two roots related by arbitrary nonzero powers modulo torsion, inversion of either root preserves its parent by reciprocal or antireciprocal symmetry.
After choosing signs, write \(x_2^j=\eta x_1^k\) with \(j,k>0\) and torsion \(\eta\); taking \(w^j=x_1\) gives \(x_2=\omega w^k\) for some root of unity \(\omega\).
Finally a proper inclusion \(\mathbb Q(x^n)\subsetneq\mathbb Q(x)\) would give a distinct conjugate of \(x\) with the same \(n\)-th power by separability.
It belongs to the same primitive parent and differs by a nontrivial torsion twist.
Any sequence of failures, with arbitrary choices of roots and powers on its terms, would contradict the identity just established, proving simultaneous eventuality.
