## Statement

Let q be an odd rational prime and M a nonzero finite cyclic Z[t]-module generated by 1, with A=t, Phi_q(A)=0 and gcd(q,|M|)=1.
Every nonzero element has exact period q and |M|=1 modulo q.
For b:{0,1}^q -> M, b(epsilon)=sum_(j<q)epsilon_j A^j1, its support T and fiber sizes nu obey

    AT=T=-T, |T|=1 modulo q,
    nu(Ax)=nu(-x)=nu(x), nu(0)=2 modulo 2q,
    q+1<=|T|<=min(|M|,2^q-1),
    |T|>=2q+1 if 2*1!=0.

If 2*1=0 and r=dim_F2 M, then r<=q-1 and for every n>=r the fair binary law is uniform on M with every fiber of size2^(n-r) and every Renyi entropy log|M|, including orders0,1,infinity.
Equality |T|=q+1 holds precisely for M=F_(2^r), q=2^r-1 prime, with A1 of order q; no infinitude of Mersenne primes is asserted.
For arbitrary M the fair q-word law satisfies max p(x)>=2^(1-q) and sum p(x)^2>=(2^q+2)/4^q.
For q=2 the nonzero cyclic additive group has odd order and T={0,1,-1} has size3.

For every integer k>=2, infinitely many odd primes q have f=ord_q(2) dividing(q-1)/k.
On F_(2^f) with A1 of order q, every complete positive number j of periods has full support2^f, equal fibers2^(jq-f), all Renyi entropies f log2 and maximum atom2^(-f).
An increasing selection q_k therefore has log|M|/q_k<=log2/k tending to zero.
This controls the full chosen field, and supplies no upper bound on a larger prescribed arithmetic primitive quotient or a small-Mahler lift.

For every integer s>=3 with s!=2 modulo5, the irreducible reciprocal unit P_s=t^2-st+1 has every positive power of its outside root alpha of degree2.
With N=s^2+s-1,

    Phi_5(alpha)=alpha^2 N,
    M_5=Z[alpha]/Phi_5(alpha)Z[alpha]=(Z/NZ)^2,
    |M_5|=N^2, gcd(5,N)=1, |T_5|=31.

Only the two constant words collide at zero.
Writing j T_5=T_(5j), full surjectivity requires j>=ceil((N-1)/(2(s+1))), while j=N-1 suffices.
The measures grow without bound, so this is not a counterfamily in the small-Mahler core.

For an arbitrary nontorsion algebraic unit alpha with monic irreducible P of degree d and m=log M(P)>0, set O=Z[alpha],
J_q=(Phi_q(alpha)O:q^infinity), and M_q^prim=O/J_q.
Its order Prim_q is the part of |Norm Phi_q(alpha)| coprime to q.
Every nonzero R(alpha) in J_q has Prim_q dividing |Norm R(alpha)|.
For a length-n binary difference, log|Norm R(alpha)|<=d log n+(n-1)m.
Consequently

    d log n+(n-1)m<log Prim_q

makes projection preserve the entire original binary word partition and all pushforward laws, including every fair-digit Renyi entropy, without asserting original collision-freedom.
The complete primitive-norm witness with eta<=m, q<=B, q>=2+4d log(2B)/eta and log Prim_q>=qm/2 guarantees this for every1<=n<=floor(q/4).
Faithful finite modeling supplies neither a new lower bound on the original binary support nor a positive asymptotic entropy gap.

<a id="prime-binary-orbit-fibers"></a>
### Exact orbit and fiber restrictions

Let q be an odd rational prime.
Let M be a nonzero finite cyclic Z[t]-module with generator v, let A be multiplication by t, and assume

    Phi_q(A)=0,    gcd(q,|M|)=1.

The identity t^q-1=(t-1)Phi_q shows A^q=I, hence A is an automorphism.
Every nonzero element has exact A-period q.
Indeed a fixed element x obeys Phi_q(A)x=qx=0, so x=0; the only divisors of q are 1 and q.
In particular |M|=1 modulo q.

Use v as the multiplicative identity in the cyclic quotient ring Z[t]/ann(v); this is legitimate because ann(v) is an ideal.
We write v=1 and a=A1.
Define the binary map and its support by

    b:{0,1}^q -> M,    b(epsilon)=sum_(j=0)^(q-1) epsilon_j A^j 1,
    T=T_q(A,{0,1})=image(b),
    nu(x)=|b^(-1)(x)|.

Rotation of the q binary coordinates corresponds to A on M.
Complementing all coordinates corresponds to x -> -x because sum_j A^j1=0.
Consequently

    AT=T,    -T=T,    |T|=1 modulo q,
    nu(Ax)=nu(x),    nu(-x)=nu(x),
    nu(0)=2 modulo 2q.                                    (1)

For the last congruence, the only rotation-fixed words are the two constant words, both mapping to zero; all other word orbits have length q.
Thus nu(0)=2 modulo q.
Complementation is a fixed-point-free involution of the zero fiber, so its cardinality is even, giving the displayed congruence because q is odd.

Both constant words collide at zero, hence

    q+1<=|T|<=min(|M|,2^q-1).                              (2)

There is a sharper alternative.
If 2*1 is nonzero, then the A-orbits of 1 and -1 are disjoint.
For otherwise A^j1=-1 would imply A^(2j)1=1; since 1 has period q, j=0 modulo q, contradicting 1!=-1.
Both orbits belong to T, and therefore

    2*1!=0 implies |T|>=2q+1.                              (3)

The case 2*1=0 means that all of M has exponent two, since the iterates of 1 generate it additively.
It is treated exactly below.
These bounds are sharp in the class as stated: q=3 on F_4 gives |T|=4, and q=3 with a=2 on F_7 gives |T|=7.
No infinitude claim about either special prime pattern is used.

For completeness, if q=2, then A=-I and cyclicity forces M to be a cyclic additive group of odd order.
The support is {0,1,-1}, of size three for every nontrivial M.
The remaining sections use odd q, which is the relevant large-prime setting.

<a id="characteristic-two-binary-surjection"></a>
### Characteristic two: full coverage and every fiber exactly

Suppose 2*1=0, and let r=dim_F2 M.
The module is a quotient F_2[t]/(g), with g a divisor of Phi_q modulo 2.
This polynomial is separable because q is odd and t^q-1 has nonzero derivative at its roots.
In particular r<=q-1.
The vectors 1,a,...,a^(r-1) form an F_2-basis.
For every n>=r, the binary trajectory map F_2^n -> M is therefore surjective of rank r, and every fiber has exactly 2^(n-r) elements.
Thus

    T_n(A,{0,1})=M,
    H_beta(sum_(j<n) epsilon_j a^j)=log|M|=r log2
       for every Renyi order 0<=beta<=infinity, n>=r,       (4)

where the digits are independent fair binary variables and all logarithms are natural.
Shannon entropy is the beta=1 case.
This is uniformity relative to the finite quotient, not a positive asymptotic entropy rate in time on that finite group.

In particular equality |T|=q+1 in (2) forces |M|=q+1=2^r.
The q nonzero elements form one A-orbit, so there is no proper nonzero invariant F_2-subspace.
The cyclic algebra F_2[A] is therefore the field F_(2^r), and q=2^r-1 is a Mersenne prime.
Conversely such a field with a generator of its multiplicative group gives equality.
This equality classification does not provide an infinite family of such primes.

For an arbitrary M, (1) also restricts collision statistics.
For fair words, p(x)=nu(x)/2^q.
Since nu(0)>=2,

    max_x p(x)>=2^(1-q),
    sum_x p(x)^2 >= (2^q+2)/4^q.                           (5)

For the second inequality, sum nu(x)^2 is at least sum nu(x) plus the extra two from a fiber of size at least two.
These are upper limits on possible entropy, not lower entropy certificates.
Apart from (4), orbit invariance alone does not make the fibers equal or prove a reverse comparison with support entropy.

<a id="prime-order-small-binary-quotients"></a>
### Prime order and uniformity still allow a vanishing per-period rate

For every integer k>=2 there are infinitely many odd rational primes q such that

    f=ord_q(2) divides (q-1)/k.                             (6)

Here is the contributed elementary existence proof.
If a nonconstant F in Z[t] has F(0)=c!=0, infinitely many primes divide its nonzero integer values.
Otherwise let Q be the product of all such primes.
The polynomial F(cQt)/c has integer coefficients and is 1 modulo Q.
Taking an integer t at which its absolute value exceeds one supplies a prime divisor belonging to Q but also coprime to Q, a contradiction.
Removing any prescribed finite set of primes preserves infinitude.

Apply this to the monic minimal polynomial F of a nonzero integral primitive element theta of

    L=Q(zeta_k,2^(1/k)).

All specified elements of L are rational polynomials in theta.
Choose a nonzero integer D clearing denominators for zeta_k, gamma=2^(1/k), and (zeta_k^j-1)^(-1) for 1<=j<k.
For a prime q not dividing 2D and an integer u with F(u)=0 modulo q, evaluation theta -> u defines a map

    Z[theta,1/D] -> F_q.

The image of zeta_k has exact order k: its kth power is one, and all the listed inverses remain inverses.
Hence k divides q-1.
The image of gamma is nonzero and its kth power is 2, so 2^((q-1)/k)=1 modulo q.
This proves (6).
The preceding polynomial argument supplies infinitely many allowable q.
Equivalently these can be viewed, after discarding the usual finite discriminant/index exceptions, as completely split primes of the splitting field L.
That interpretation is not needed by the direct evaluation proof; no quantitative or density prime-splitting theorem is being used.

For each such q, the field F_(2^f) contains an element a of order q.
Its degree over F_2 is exactly f, because Frobenius fixes it after h steps precisely when 2^h=1 modulo q.
Its first f powers therefore form a basis.
By (4), at every positive integer number j of complete periods,

    |T_(jq)|=2^f,
    every binary fiber has size 2^(jq-f),
    max_x p(x)=2^(-f),
    H_beta=f log2 for every 0<=beta<=infinity.               (7)

Choose an increasing prime q_k satisfying (6) for each k.
Then

    (log|F_(2^f_k)|)/q_k <= log2/k -> 0.                    (8)

This rules out a uniform lower bound exp(cq) for support, a bound H_beta>=cq, or an exponential-in-q maximum-atom bound with a fixed positive c, from these finite primitive field hypotheses alone.
Uniformity is already perfect; even complete coverage does not force enough quotient mass per period.
A bounded number of complete blocks does not repair these claims.

This is a statement about the whole finite field in this construction.
It does not exhibit the full primitive quotient of an actual prescribed small-Mahler irreducible unit.
If a field is only a quotient of a larger module M, projecting T_q(M) onto that field can decrease its cardinality and its entropy.
A small field-image rate is not an upper bound for the support or entropy in the full primitive quotient.
A lift controlling that full quotient and the original unit's Mahler measure remains a separate obligation.

<a id="full-primitive-quotient-saturation-boundary"></a>
### Large full norm does not give binary surjectivity at bounded cost

There is a complementary actual integral-unit family.
Let

    P_s(t)=t^2-s t+1,    s>=3,    s!=2 modulo5,
    N=s^2+s-1,    q=5.

The discriminant s^2-4 lies strictly between (s-1)^2 and s^2, so P_s is irreducible.
Its roots are alpha>1 and alpha^(-1).
It is reciprocal and a unit polynomial; all positive powers of alpha still have degree two, since a rational power would be a rational integral unit and hence +1 or -1.
Direct use of alpha^2=s alpha-1 gives

    Phi_5(alpha)=alpha^2 N.

Thus its FULL cyclotomic quotient is

    M_5=Z[alpha]/Phi_5(alpha)Z[alpha]
       =Z[alpha]/N Z[alpha]=(Z/NZ)^2                       (9)

as an additive group, and D_5=N^2.
The condition on s is exactly 5 not dividing N: modulo5, s^2+s-1=(s-2)^2.
Consequently this entire quotient is primitive in the prime-index sense used above.
Every nonzero element has period5, and 5>d+1 for its degree d=2.

In the basis 1,alpha, the first five orbit vectors, modulo N, can be written as

    (1,0), (0,1), (-1,s), (-s,-s), (s,-1).                  (10)

Their sum is zero.
Suppose two binary words have the same sum, and let delta_i in {-1,0,1} be their coordinatewise difference.
The two resulting integer coordinates are

    delta_0-delta_2+s(-delta_3+delta_4),
    delta_1-delta_4+s(delta_2-delta_3).                     (11)

Their absolute values are at most 2s+2<N for s>=3.
If they vanish modulo N they therefore vanish as integers.
Since s>=3 and each constant term has absolute value at most two, the coefficients of s and the constant terms in both lines must vanish separately.
This forces all five delta_i to be equal.
The only nonidentical collision is therefore between the two constant words.
It follows that

    |T_5|=31,    nu(0)=2,    nu(x)=1 for x!=0 in T_5.       (12)

Meanwhile |M_5|=N^2 tends to infinity.
This already forbids a uniform positive comparison log|T_5|>=c log|M_5| over the stated class, for any fixed c>0.

There is also a quantitative obstruction to surjectivity after several periods.
Since A^5=I on M_5,

    T_(5j)=j T_5.

Using (10), every first coordinate of this sum is represented by an integer in [-j(s+1),j(s+1)].
Covering all of M_5 requires covering all N first coordinates modulo N, hence

    T_(5j)=M_5 implies
    j>=ceil((N-1)/(2(s+1))).                               (13)

This necessary number of blocks grows like s/2.
Surjectivity does eventually hold: every element of M_5 is an integer combination of 1 and alpha with coefficients in {0,...,N-1}, so N-1 complete blocks suffice.
No exact minimum beyond the necessary bound (13) is claimed.

This family preserves full primitive quotients, reciprocity, irreducibility, the unit condition, all-power degree stability and a prime index exceeding d+1.
It lies outside the hard near-one window: alpha>s-1>=2, so its Mahler measure grows without bound.
Therefore it does not refute a binary-support theorem restricted to the target small-Mahler core.
It does show that primitive quotient mass by itself cannot supply uniform binary surjectivity or proportional binary entropy on the broader integral-unit domain.

### A positive transfer: the large primitive norm prevents new short collisions

Return to an actual nontorsion algebraic unit alpha with monic irreducible polynomial P of degree d, O=Z[alpha], and m=log M(P).
For a rational prime index q, put

    J_q=(Phi_q(alpha)O : q^infinity)
       ={x in O: q^a x belongs to Phi_q(alpha)O for some a>=0},
    M_q^prim=O/J_q.

The subgroup J_q/Phi_q(alpha)O is exactly the q-primary part of M_q.
Thus M_q^prim is its full prime-to-q quotient, and

    |M_q^prim|=Prim_q,

where Prim_q is the part of |Norm Phi_q(alpha)| coprime to q, as in the primitive-layer theorem.
This is a statement in the companion order itself; no maximal-order or conductor equality is required.
It is a cyclic Z[alpha]-module generated by 1, annihilated by Phi_q(alpha), with order coprime to q.

If a nonzero R(alpha) in O belongs to J_q, then for some a there is y in O with q^a R(alpha)=Phi_q(alpha)y.
Taking norms gives

    |Norm Phi_q(alpha)| divides q^(ad)|Norm R(alpha)|.

Since Prim_q is coprime to q, it follows that

    Prim_q divides |Norm R(alpha)|.                        (14)

For the difference of two length-n binary words, the polynomial R has coefficients in {-1,0,1} and degree at most n-1.
If R(alpha)!=0, estimating each conjugate separately gives

    log|Norm R(alpha)|<=d log n+(n-1)m.                    (15)

Consequently the strict inequality

    d log n+(n-1)m<log Prim_q                              (16)

implies that projection to M_q^prim introduces NO NEW collisions between length-n binary words.
Words already equal in O remain equal, while a nonzero difference cannot satisfy both (14) and (15).
Equivalently the projection induces a bijection between the original support T_n(alpha,{0,1}) in O and the finite quotient's binary support.
The complete partition of words into fibers is identical.
Hence their pushforward distributions agree under this bijection for any specified law on the words; in particular all fair-digit Renyi entropies agree.
This does not say that the original binary words are collision-free.

The controlled primitive-norm witness supplies a concrete range where (16) holds.
Use its parameters eta<=m, B and q, for which

    q<=B,    q>=2+4d log(2B)/eta,
    log Prim_q>=q m/2.

For every integer 1<=n<=floor(q/4),

    d log n+(n-1)m
      < d log(2B)+q m/4
      <= (q-2)m/4+q m/4
      < q m/2 <=log Prim_q.                               (17)

Here log n<log(2B), n-1<q/4, and the middle estimate uses eta<=m.
Thus one complete primitive quotient gives an exact finite model of every original binary trajectory up to a quarter of that prime period, including all its actual collisions and probability fibers.

This positive interface uses both the quantitative norm certificate and its time cutoff.
It is not implied by prime order alone or by retaining only one field component.
It transfers the unknown original binary support into a faithful finite model; it does not yet lower-bound that support or turn a finite-time slope into an asymptotic entropy bound.

<a id="binary-primitive-entropy-boundary"></a>
### What remains to transfer to algebraic entropy

The finite-prime avoidance theorem can supply a large primitive norm outside a prescribed set of rational primes.
To turn that into a binary trajectory certificate, one still needs a comparison for the FULL finite quotient, keeping its cardinality relative to q and the original unit's Mahler measure in the hypotheses.
Equations (4), (8) and (13) separate uniform mixing, mass per period, and time to surjectivity; these are different requirements.

Even a lower bound for one finite binary trajectory remains finite-time evidence.
As in L G0077, normalized finite-set trajectory cardinalities are upper bounds for the limiting algebraic entropy of that alphabet, by subadditivity.
In a finite quotient they eventually saturate.
A proof of a uniform positive asymptotic rate for the original integral flow must retain information across growing times rather than infer independent block concatenation from one return.
