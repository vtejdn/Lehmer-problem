# A cubic support bound from height over prime-conductor cyclotomic fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Height over one cyclotomic field and arbitrary integral supports

There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that for every prime \(\ell\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\), and nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\) which is not torsion, its relative degree \(D=[K(\alpha):K]\) satisfies
\[
                    D h(\alpha)\ \ge\
                 c\left(\frac{\log\log(5D)}{\log(2D)}\right)^3 .          \tag{1}
\]
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height, including all finite places.
The constant is independent of the conductor \(\ell\).
Consequently for every nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(P\) in any finite number of variables, with \(k\) nonzero monomials,
\[
                     m(P)=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad m(P)\ge c'/k^3          \tag{2}
\]
for an absolute \(c'>0\).
The proof of (1) exploits the single ramified prime of the fields actually used in the [two-variable torsion grid](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#fiber).
It uses a confluent Vandermonde with Frobenius twists, as in the unramified determinant method of [Amoroso and Delsinne](../audits/literature/AD07/readme.md#edition).
We give the determinant, the treatment of bad powers, and the parameter choices for this restricted family of base fields.

<a id="primes"></a>
## How many rational primes can identify powers of two conjugates?

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_D\) be the distinct \(K\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For a rational prime \(p\) the power map identifies two of these conjugates precisely when \(m_p=[K(\alpha):K(\alpha^p)]>1\), by the transitivity and uniform multiplicities of embeddings.
In that case \(m_p\le p\), as \(\alpha\) solves \(X^p-\alpha^p\).
If \(m_p=p\), then \(p\mid D\).
If \(1<m_p<p\), the product of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) over \(K(\alpha^p)\) equals \(\alpha^{m_p}\zeta_p^a\) for an integer \(a\), and belongs to \(K(\alpha^p)\).
Combining this with \(\alpha^p\) using Bézout's identity for \(m_p,p\) yields \(\alpha\zeta_p^b\in K(\alpha^p)\) for some integer \(b\).
Here \(b\not\equiv0\bmod p\), as otherwise \(m_p=1\).
Hence \(\zeta_p\in K(\alpha)\).

The number of distinct primes dividing \(D\) is at most \(\log D/\log 2\).
For any finite set of odd primes \(p\ne\ell\) with \(\zeta_p\in K(\alpha)\), the composite of their cyclotomic fields with \(K\) has relative degree \(\prod(p-1)\le D\), by the totient formula at coprime conductors.
Thus apart from \(2,\ell\) there are at most \(2\log D/\log2\) bad primes of both kinds together.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Frobenius congruences in a confluent Vandermonde

Choose a finite set \(\mathcal P\) of good odd primes \(p\ne\ell\), write \(t=|\mathcal P|\), and fix a positive integer \(T\).
Let \(\sigma_p\) be the automorphism of \(K\) sending \(\zeta_\ell\) to \(\zeta_\ell^p\).
It fixes every prime ideal over \(p\) and acts there by the \(p\)-power map on the residue field, since \(K/\mathbb Q\) is abelian and unramified at \(p\).
Extend \(\sigma_p^{-1}\) to an automorphism of the algebraic closure, and add the packet of \(D\) points \(b_{pi}=(\sigma_p^{-1}a_i)^p\).
Each packet is internally distinct by the choice of good primes.
The original packet and the packets for different primes are mutually disjoint because their points have absolute heights \(h(\alpha)\) and \(p h(\alpha)\), respectively, with \(h(\alpha)>0\).

Take multiplicity \(T\) at each \(a_i\) and multiplicity one at every other point, and write \(S=D(T+t)\) for the sum of multiplicities.
Let \(V\) be the nonzero confluent Vandermonde determinant with columns indexed by exponents \(0,\ldots,S-1\) and with derivative rows divided by the derivative order factorial.
The ordinary Vandermonde formula by confluence gives
\[
        V=\pm\prod_{\{u,v\}}(u-v)^{m_um_v}
\]
over unordered distinct nodal pairs, with an arbitrary fixed orientation and multiplicities \(m_u\).
This follows from successive divided differences within each group coalescing at a node, precisely canceling the within-group Vandermonde factors.

At an archimedean embedding a derivative row of order \(j<m_u\) has Euclidean norm at most \(S^{j+1/2}\max(1,|u|)^{S-1}\), by the binomial formula for the entries.
Hadamard's inequality gives an archimedean factor \(S^{D(T^2+t)/2}\) times \(\prod_u\max(1,|u|)^{(S-1)m_u}\).
At every nonarchimedean place the pair product is at most the same product of local maxima without the power of \(S\), since the degree at a point \(u\) is \(m_u(S-m_u)\le(S-1)m_u\).

There is an additional saving \(p^{-DT}\) at every place over \(p\in\mathcal P\), using absolute values with \(|p|_v=p^{-1}\).
To verify it, fix such a place and let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(K\).
Scale \(f\) to \(g=c f\), \(c\in K^\times\), of Gauss norm one at the underlying prime of \(K\), for instance by dividing by a coefficient of maximal absolute value.
The polynomials \(g\) and \(\sigma_p^{-1}g\) have integral local coefficients and Gauss norm one at this prime.
Coefficientwise Frobenius and unramifiedness give
\[
           g(X^p)-(\sigma_p^{-1}g(X))^p\equiv 0\pmod p
\]
in the local coefficient ring.
At \(z_i=\sigma_p^{-1}a_i\), where \((\sigma_p^{-1}g)(z_i)=0\), this implies
\[
                 |g(z_i^p)|_v\le p^{-1}\max(1,|z_i^p|_v)^D .
\]
Gauss norm multiplicativity gives \(|c|_v\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)=1\).
Consequently
\[
 \prod_{i,j}|b_{pi}-a_j|_v
    \le p^{-D}\prod_i\max(1,|b_{pi}|_v)^D
                \prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)^D .
\]
These cross differences occur to power \(T\), yielding the claimed saving relative to the local pair bounds, also when the original element is not integral.

Use the product formula in one number field containing every node, weighting places by local degree divided by field degree.
All archimedean places together have weight one, as do all places above each rational prime.
Each \(a_i\) contributes height \(h(\alpha)\) and each \(b_{pi}\) contributes \(p h(\alpha)\).
Combining the inequalities and dividing by \(D\) gives
\[
 T\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\log p
    \le \tfrac12(T^2+t)\log S
           +D(T+t)\left(T+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p\right) h(\alpha).    \tag{3}
\]

<a id="choice"></a>
## The unramified prime range

Take \(T=\lceil A\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\rceil\) with an absolute \(A\) chosen sufficiently large below, and \(X=T^2\log(2T)\).
From the prime number theorem the interval \([X,2X]\) contains between \(c_3T^2\) and \(C_3T^2\) primes for sufficiently large \(T\), with absolute constants.
Deleting the primes from the preceding bad-prime count and \(2,\ell\) leaves \(c_4T^2\le t\le C_4T^2\).
Indeed \(1+\log D=O(T^2/A)\) uniformly for large \(A\), and \(T\gg A\).
In (3) the left side is at least \(c_5 T^3\log(2T)\), and \(\tfrac12(T^2+t)\log S\le C_5 T^2(\log(2D)+\log(2T))\).
The ratio \(T\log(2T)/\log(2D)\) is bounded below by a constant times \(A\) uniformly: \(\log(2T)\gg\log\log(5D)\) at this choice.
Thus the logarithmic term on the right is at most half the left for sufficiently large \(A\).
Also \((T+t)(T+\sum p)\ll T^6\log(2T)\).
It follows that \(D h(\alpha)\gg T^{-3}\), giving (1) uniformly including bounded \(D\).

<a id="support"></a>
## Returning to the original support size

For an integral two-variable \(F\) with \(Y\)-width \(R\) and a horizontal nontorsion component, use the prime-conductor grids and the complete orbit of a specialized root from the [relative-height comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#fiber).
The base field of each grid is exactly \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\).
The orbit supplies at least \(D h(\alpha)\) in the grid mean, where \(D\le R\).
By (1) this proves
\[
            m_2(F)\gg \left(\frac{\log\log(5R)}{\log(2R)}\right)^3 .
\]
The ratio on the right before cubing is positive and eventually decreasing, so taking the minimum through \(R\) changes only an absolute factor.

Use the [prime compression](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#compression) with \(B=\lceil(A_0 k)^8\rceil\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(A_0\).
The prime \(H\) there satisfies \(\log(2H)\asymp k\log(A_0 k)\) and the lifted width satisfies \(R<2H/B\).
The specialization error is at most \(Ck\log(2H)/B\).
It is uniformly at most half the new horizontal lower bound, by the same [constant comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#induction), since this lower bound dominates a constant times \([\log(2H)]^{-4}\).
Keeping the numerator gives \(\log\log(5H)/\log(2H)\gg_{A_0}1/k\), proving (2) whenever the lift has a horizontal nontorsion component.
Otherwise the exact top-row reduction in that same argument preserves the measure and decreases \(k\).
Induction and the [multivariable limit](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#variables) prove (2) for every stated input, after including positive monomial measures.
