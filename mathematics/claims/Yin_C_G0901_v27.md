# Integrable exceptional cyclotomic logarithms and exact prescribed multiplicities

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite-integral criterion for the uniform circle measure

Let \(B\) be a finite set of positive integers, let \(\mu\) be a probability measure on \(\mathbb T\), and assume
\(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for every \(n\notin B\).
If \(\int\log|\Phi_b|\,d\mu>-\infty\) for each \(b\in B\), the conjugation symmetrization of \(\mu\) is normalized arc length \(\eta\).
In particular any even feasible measure different from \(\eta\) has an infinite negative logarithmic integral at some forbidden order.
Only the finitely many forbidden logarithms require an integrability hypothesis.

For any nonzero real polynomial \(P\), prescribe a positive rational number \(t_b\) for each \(b\in B\).
As \(a\) increases through positive integers making every \(a t_b\) integral, one has
\[
    \lim_{a\to\infty}\ \frac1a\log\inf_C\|P^a C\|_1=m(P),
     \qquad \operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_b} C=a t_b\quad (b\in B),             \tag{1}
\]
where \(C\) ranges over monic cyclotomic products with the stated exact multiplicities and \(\|\cdot\|_1\) sums absolute coefficients.
One may impose any additional degree budget \(\deg C\le W(a)\) with \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
The polynomial, conductors and positive proportions are fixed in this limit.

There is also a criterion allowing atoms on the forbidden orbits.
If the residual after removing those atoms has density \(f\) relative to \(\eta\) with \(\int f\log^+f\,d\eta<\infty\), its conjugation symmetrization is its mass times \(\eta\).
The unrestricted diffuse classification when a forbidden logarithm is nonintegrable remains open here.

<a id="lcm"></a>
## A least common multiple bound for near returns of rotations

Use the coordinate \(z=e^{2\pi i x}\), \(x\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), and write \(\|x\|\) for distance to the nearest integer.
Put \(H(x)=\log(1/\|x\|)\), with value \(+\infty\) at integers.
We first prove that any probability measure \(\rho\) satisfying
\[
          \sup_{D\ge1}\int H(Dx)\,d\rho(x)=K<\infty
\]
obeys
\[
        \lim_{T\to\infty}
            \int \sup_{q\ge T}\frac{H(qx)}q\,d\rho(x)=0.                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(\rho\) has no mass on rational points of the circle.

For the interval of integers \(m+1,\ldots,m+k\), its least common multiple \(D\) divides
\(\binom{m+k}{k}\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)\).
Indeed at any prime \(p\), each \(p^i\) dividing a member of the interval and exceeding \(k\) contributes one to the valuation of the binomial coefficient by counting multiples of \(p^i\) in the interval.
For the lower powers the least common multiple of \(1,\ldots,k\) supplies every exponent up to \(\lfloor\log_p k\rfloor\).
Also \(\log\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)\ll k\).
For a direct proof at \(k=2^j\), the same divisibility on the upper half implies \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2^j)\mid\binom{2^j}{2^{j-1}}\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2^{j-1})\).
Taking logarithms and summing the bounds \(2^j\log2\) gives the assertion for powers of two, and monotonicity gives it for all \(k\).

Fix \(0<\delta<1/4\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\), partition the integers in \([N,2N)\) into \(O(1/\delta)\) intervals of length at most \(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\).
For every corresponding \(D\) the binomial bound \(\binom{m+k}{k}\le(\mathrm e(m+k)/k)^k\) gives \(\log D\le C\delta\log(2/\delta)N\) for an absolute \(C\).
For \(q\) in that interval, \(\|Dx\|\le(D/q)\|qx\|\), so \(H(qx)\le\log D+H(Dx)\).
Put \(u=C\delta\log(2/\delta)\).
The integral of the positive part of \(\sup_{N\le q<2N} H(qx)/q-u\) is bounded by the sum over the intervals of \(N^{-1}\int H(Dx)\,d\rho\), hence by \(O(K/(\delta N))\).
Summing these positive parts over dyadic blocks starting at an integer \(T\) gives
\[
        \int\sup_{q\ge T} H(qx)/q\,d\rho(x)
                        \le u+O(K/(\delta T)).
\]
First take \(T\to\infty\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\), to obtain (2).
The full supremum for \(q\ge1\) is integrable by (2) and integrability of each of the finitely many remaining terms.

<a id="rotation"></a>
## Cesàro control of the logarithm at the identity

Suppose \(\rho\) is atomless and satisfies the uniform integral bound just used.
For an irrational \(x\) and a positive integer \(N\), choose \(1\le q=q_N(x)\le N\) minimizing \(\|qx\|\), taking the smallest minimizer.
Then \(q_N(x)\to\infty\) because the minimum tends to zero by the pigeonhole principle.
Let \(p\) be the integer nearest \(qx\).
It is coprime to \(q\), since dividing by a common divisor greater than one would reduce the error at a smaller positive denominator.
For \(1\le m<q\), with \(b\) nearest to \(mx\), the nonzero integer \(qb-mp\) gives
\[
            1\le q\|mx\|+m\|qx\|\le(q+m)\|mx\|.
\]
Thus the points \(nx\) in any block of at most \(q\) consecutive indices are mutually separated by at least \(1/(2q)\).
In one such block discard the point nearest zero.
The number of remaining points with \(\|nx\|<y\) is at most \(4qy\) for \(0<y<1/2\), by arc length and separation.
Every discarded point among \(1\le n\le N\) contributes at most \(H(qx)\).
Integrating the counting bound at \(y=e^{-t}\) from \(t=J\) to infinity and using \(\lceil N/q\rceil/N\le2/q\) gives
\[
        \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N(H(nx)-J)_+
             \le 8e^{-J}+\frac{2H(q_N(x)x)}{q_N(x)}
                         \qquad (J\ge\log2).                              \tag{3}
\]
By (2) the decreasing suprema \(\sup_{q\ge T}H(qx)/q\) tend to zero almost everywhere, and the supremum for \(q\ge1\) is integrable.
Since the map \(q_N\) is measurable by its finite definition, dominated convergence shows that the last term of (3) tends to zero in \(L^1(\rho)\).

For \(g(x)=\log|1-e^{2\pi i x}|\), put \(g_J=\max(g,-J)\), which is continuous.
Because \(|1-e^{2\pi i x}|\ge4\|x\|\), the truncation error \(0\le g_J-g\le(H-J)_+\) satisfies the integrated Cesàro bound (3).
For every continuous \(v\),
\[
     \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\left|\int v(nx)\,d\rho(x)-\int v\,d\eta\right|
                              \longrightarrow0 .
\]
Indeed for each nonzero integer \(k\) the mean of \(|\int e^{2\pi i knx}\,d\rho(x)|^2\) tends to zero by integrating the geometric Cesàro sum at \(k(x-y)\) against \(\rho\otimes\rho\).
The exceptional pairs with \(k(x-y)\in\mathbb Z\) have measure zero by atomlessness.
Cauchy–Schwarz, linearity on trigonometric polynomials and uniform approximation prove the assertion for \(v\).
Apply it to \(g_J\) and let \(J\to\infty\) in (3), using \(\int g_J\,d\eta\to\int g\,d\eta=0\).
This proves
\[
                    \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\left|\int g(nx)\,d\rho(x)\right|
                             \longrightarrow0 .                           \tag{4}
\]

## Periodicity of the power logarithms and the coefficient rate

For \(\mu\) in the finite-integral statement, the allowed tests are integrable since their logarithms are bounded above.
All forbidden ones are integrable by assumption, hence no primitive root of any order is an atom.
The [atom exclusion argument](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#regularity) also excludes every nontorsion atom.
Write \(a_s=\int\log|\Phi_s|\,d\mu\).
The [upper bound from averaging powers](Yin_C_G0901_v18.md#measures) gives \(a_s=0\) for every \(s\notin B\), since there are no forbidden-orbit masses.
The factorization of \(X^n-1\) now gives the following periodic sequence of finite integrals.
\[
              A(n):=\int g(nx)\,d\mu(x)=\sum_{b\in B,\ b\mid n}a_b .
\]
They are uniformly bounded below.
The inequalities \(4\|x\|\le|1-e^{2\pi i x}|\le2\pi\|x\|\) give \(H(x)\le\log(2\pi)-g(x)\), so \(\mu\) satisfies the hypotheses of (4).
The periodic sequence \(A(n)\), of period \(\operatorname{lcm}(B)\) with period one for empty \(B\), must consequently vanish identically.
Finite divisor inversion gives \(a_s=0\) for every \(s\ge1\).
The [unrestricted moment theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement) now gives the asserted conjugation symmetrization.

For (1), take a common denominator \(u\) of the \(t_b\), choosing the least such positive integer, and write \(a=uj\).
Apply the [forbidden-order dual formula](Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#dual) at multiplicity \(j\) to
\[
                         G=P^u\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{u t_b},
\]
with the additional multiplier avoiding \(B\).
In that formula every even feasible probability with finite objective \(\int\log|G|\,d\mu\) integrates each forbidden logarithm finitely, because all its coefficients \(ut_b\) are positive and all factor logarithms are bounded above.
It therefore equals \(\eta\).
Arc length has finite objective \(m(G)=u m(P)\), so dividing the dual formula by \(u\) gives (1).
The compulsory factors consume \(a\sum_b t_b\varphi(b)\) degrees, leaving an additional budget divided by \(j\) still tending to infinity when a total cofactor budget is imposed.
For empty \(B\) this argument uses \(u=1\) and the empty product.

## A finite-entropy residual in the presence of atoms

Let \(\nu=f\eta\) be the residual in the last assertion of the statement and fix \(s\ge1\).
Put \(v_s=\log|\Phi_s|\).
For some \(c_s>0\), \(\int e^{c_s|v_s|}\,d\eta<\infty\), since the circle roots are simple and the singularities are logarithmic.
The same bound holds for \(v_s(z^j)\) uniformly in positive \(j\) by invariance of \(\eta\).
Then \(\int_{\{f>M\}} f|v_s(z^j)|\,d\eta\to0\) uniformly in \(j\).
Indeed, for any \(\epsilon>0\), use \(A B\le A\log(1+A)+e^B\) with \(A=f/\epsilon\) and \(B=c_s|v_s(z^j)|\).
After multiplication by \(\epsilon/c_s\) and integration over \(\{f>M\}\), the first term tends to zero for fixed \(\epsilon\) by finite entropy, and the second is bounded by a constant times \(\epsilon\).
For the bounded density \(f\mathbf1_{\{f\le M\}}\), approximate it in \(L^2(\eta)\) by trigonometric polynomials.
The \(L^2\) norm of \(v_s(z^j)\) is fixed and its nonzero Fourier modes are multiples of \(j\), while its Haar mean is zero.
It follows that \(\int f(z)v_s(z^j)\,d\eta(z)\to0\) as \(j\to\infty\).

For each \(s\), these finite integrals therefore obey the simultaneous smallness at nonempty subproducts required in the [two- and three-difference argument](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#differences).
One may take successive integers congruent to one modulo the prescribed modulus and all previous choices, making every newly occurring subproduct sufficiently large.
The difference polynomials integrate nonnegatively against the full measure and have logarithm zero at every forbidden atom.
The opposite bounds from two and three differences thus force \(\int v_s\,d\nu=0\).
Normalize \(\nu\) when its mass is positive and apply the unrestricted moment theorem.
The zero measure satisfies the conclusion directly.
