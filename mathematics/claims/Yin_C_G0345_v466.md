# Lower densities of degrees with simultaneous witnesses at both signs

<a id="statement"></a>
## Counting degrees in independent dyadic intervals

For \(j\ge1\) put \(D_j=2^j\) and choose a computable prime \(p_j\) tending to infinity.
Use the same prime for every even degree \(d\) in \([D_j,2D_j)\).
At such a degree use the [finite paired-factor test](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#statement) at the two signs, with \(H=\|A\|_1\le 2^{p_j}\) and an optional computable support cap \(s_d\to\infty\).
A joint witness consists of two integer rows at a common formal width \(b\), one for each sign, exhibiting the same reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum of degree \(d\) and the distinct degree-\(d\) minimum of the square.
Both factors must occur at least twice on each graph, with nonzero numerators at both powered nodes.
Write \(W_{\rm pr}\) for the primitive part of \(p_jY(A'V-AV')-AV\), where \(V=\varepsilon Y^bA(1/Y)\) on the corresponding row.

For \(\tau>0\), let \(N_j(\tau)\) count the degrees in this interval with a joint witness at some \(2d\le b<3d\) whose two scores are both strictly smaller than \(\tau\).
Use the primitive score
\[
       \frac{(b-2d+1)\log2}{d}+
        \frac{\log H}{p_j}+\frac{\log\|W_{\rm pr}\|_1}{3p_j}.             \tag{1}
\]
If \(p_j/j\to\infty\), one may instead use the raw score
\[
       \frac{(b-2d+1)\log2}{d}+
         \frac{2\log H+\log(2p_j b+1)}{3p_j}.                             \tag{2}
\]
For either applicable score, writing \(\lambda\) for the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures,
\[
 \lambda=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      \text{for every }\tau>0,\quad
              \liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{N_j(\tau)}{D_j}>0 .               \tag{3}
\]
It suffices to test cutoffs \((\log2)/n\) for positive integers \(n\).
Each such block count is computable using only that block, with no data from earlier exponents or a supplied seed.
Any counted degree actually supplies a positive measure below its cutoff.
Multiple degrees may represent the same measure.

For a fixed computable width rule \(w(d)\), let \(J_j(w)\) count the even degrees in \([D_j,2D_j)\) with a joint witness at \(b=w(d)\) for which both rows satisfy
\[
                    ((2p_j b+1)H^2)^4<2^{p_j}.                           \tag{4}
\]
These counts are likewise computable.
The following two conditions imply unrestricted positive lower bounds.

* For \(p_j/j\to\infty\), allow all supports at \(w(d)=3d-1\).
  If \(\liminf_j J_j(w)/D_j=0\), then \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/144\).
* For \(p_j/j\to\infty\) and \(p_j=o(D_j)\), put \(w(d)=5d/2-1\) and optionally impose any cap \(s_d\to\infty\).
  If \(\liminf_j p_j J_j(w)/D_j=0\), then \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/120\).

Both width tests permit the first prime above \(j^2\), since [Bertrand's postulate](../audits/literature/Erd32/Yin_U001.md#erd32-u001) bounds it by \(2j^2\) for \(j\ge2\).
The needed small densities at those widths remain open.

## Which substitutions retain the two minima

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit of even degree \(e\), of positive logarithmic measure \(h\), with every positive power generating \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Write \(P_{0,1},P_{0,2}\) for the distinct degree-\(e\) minima of \(\alpha,\alpha^2\) and put \(G_0=P_{0,1}P_{0,2}\).
Set \(B=2\prod_\ell\ell\), where the product is over the odd primes \(\ell\) with \(\alpha\in K^\ell\).
There are finitely many by the [bounded-degree root argument](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers).
For every integer \(k\ge1\) coprime to \(B\), \(X^k-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\).
Indeed, if \(X^m-\alpha\) is irreducible with \(m\) an odd divisor of \(k\), the norm of a root is \(\alpha\).
An \(\ell\)-th power root in that extension for \(\ell\mid k\) would give \(\alpha\in K^\ell\) by taking norms.
The [prime binomial criterion and induction](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions) give irreducibility through each prime of \(k\) with repetition.

For \(\gamma^k=\alpha\), the degree is \(d=ek\).
Since \(k\) is odd and \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^2)=K\), the field of \(\gamma^2\) contains \(\alpha\) and then \(\gamma\) by Bézout's identity.
The two minima are \(P_{0,1}(X^k),P_{0,2}(X^k)\), of measures \(h,2h\).
If an odd \(p>3\) avoids the fixed \(\operatorname{disc}(G_0)G_0(1)G_0(-1)\) and does not divide \(k\), then \(G_0(X^k)\) has simple disjoint residues away from zero and the two endpoints by the [substitution derivative](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#tower).

For all sufficiently large \(j\) these fixed excluded primes and the prime divisors of \(B\) differ from \(p=p_j\).
Writing \(D=D_j\), elementary interval counting gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \#\{D/e\le k<2D/e:\ (k,Bp)=1\}
   &=\frac{D}{e}\frac{\varphi(B)}B(1-1/p)+O_B(1),\\
 \#\{D/e\le k<2D/e:\ (k,B)=1,\ k\equiv1\bmod p\}
   &=\frac{D}{ep}\frac{\varphi(B)}B+O_B(1).                              \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
For the first formula apply inclusion-exclusion to the prime divisors of \(Bp\).
For the second, sum over squarefree divisors of \(B\) and count one residue class modulo the divisor times \(p\) by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Each interval rounding error is at most one and the number of terms is bounded in terms of \(B\).
Every counted \(k\) gives a different even degree in the block.

<a id="density"></a>
## One small base measure gives positive density at a fixed cutoff

Suppose \(\lambda=0\).
As in the [single-prime reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#limit), we may choose base units as above with \(h\to0\) and \(e\to\infty\).
For such a base take \(r=\max(2,\lceil\sqrt e\rceil,\lceil e\sqrt h\rceil)\), \(q=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), and \(c=2e+r-1\).
Eventually \(r\le e/2,\ r/e\to0\), and \(L=(3c+4)h/q\to0\).

Fix the cutoff \(\tau\) first and choose one base so small that \((r/e)\log2+2L<\tau\) and \(L<(\log2)/2\).
For each \(k\) in the first line of (5), take \(1\le u<p\) with \(ku\equiv1\bmod p\).
The [short-row estimate for the two classes](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#shift) and the [exact substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#lift) produce, for each sign, a genuine row at the common width \(b=kc+(ku-1)/p\) for the same degree-\(ek\) pair.
Both rows have at most \(c+1\) coefficients, \((b-2d+1)/d\le r/e\), and
\[
 \frac{\log H}{p}\le L+o(1),\qquad
 \log\|W_{\rm pr}\|_1\le2\log H+\log(2pc+u).
\]
The first error tends to zero uniformly over the block and both signs, since the base is fixed and the estimate is uniform in \(u\).
Every imposed cap eventually exceeds \(c+1\) throughout the block and both rows eventually lie below \(2^p\).
Their primitive scores are at most \((r/e)\log2+5L/3+o(1)<\tau\).
For (2), the extra term \(\log(2pb+1)/p=o(1)\) is uniform over \(b<6D\), since \(p_j/j\to\infty\).
The raw scores then obey the same cutoff.
The first line of (5) proves the lower limit in (3), with lower bound \(\varphi(B)/(eB)>0\) for this cutoff.

Conversely every counted pair gives \(0<h'<\tau\) for its seed by the [pointwise derivative bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#limit), at both signs.
The existence of any counted degree for each cutoff \((\log2)/n\) forces \(\lambda=0\).
This proves (3).
For a rational multiple of \(\log2\) all comparisons are exact after clearing the denominators of the logarithms, and checking equality of the common monic minima is an exact finite operation.

<a id="wide"></a>
## Positive proportional counts at width \(3d-1\)

If \(0<h<(\log2)/144\), keep this single base and take every \(k\) in the first line of (5).
At \(r=d,\ b=3d-1\) the [finite height bound at a good prime](Yin_C_G0345_v463.md#height) applies to the two inflated minima as in the [single-width argument](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#budget).
For both signs its error after taking normalized logarithms is \(O(\log(2p_j D_j)/p_j)\), uniformly for \(D_j\le d<2D_j\), because the determinant error before division is \(O(d\log(2p_jd))\) and the codimension bound is \(d/2\).
Thus rows at both signs have \((\log H+\tfrac12\log(2p_jb+1))/p_j\le18h+o(1)<(\log2)/8\), giving (4).
It follows that \(\liminf_j J_j(w)/D_j\ge\varphi(B)/(eB)>0\) for this base.
A smaller positive measure of any integer polynomial would give such a base by the [small-measure factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) and [power-field norm reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
This proves the first fixed-width implication.

<a id="narrow"></a>
## A common residue class and bounded support at width \(5d/2-1\)

Now fix a base with \(0<h\le(\log2)/120\) and put \(c=5e/2-1\).
The [fixed-seed shortest-row bound](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#statement) gives genuine rows \(A_0\) for both signs at every sufficiently large \(p=p_j\), at this common width, with
\[
        \frac{\log\|A_0\|_1}{p}\le (15-10/e)h+o(1)<(\log2)/8 .
\]
The strict margin exists for this fixed positive \(h\), including the endpoint.
Take every \(k\) from the second line of (5).
Since \(k\equiv1\bmod p\), the row \(A(Y)=A_0(Y^k)\) at formal width \(b_0=kc+(k-1)/p\) has graph equal to the original signed graph at \(X^k\).
Each inflated minimum divides the new graph at least twice, and both numerator values remain nonzero by substitution.

The required width \(b=5ek/2-1\) exceeds or equals \(b_0\), with difference \(a=(k-1)-(k-1)/p\).
Multiply \(A\) by \(Q(Y)=Y^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}(1+Y)^{a\bmod2}\).
Since \(Q(Y)=Y^aQ(1/Y)\), the graph at the new width is the old graph times \(Q(X^p)\), with sign preserved.
The new numerators stay nonzero, since their powered nodes are nonzero and nontorsion.
There are at most \(2(c+1)\) occupied positions and the one-norm grows by at most two.
The original norm bound and the \(o(1)\) normalization cost of \(\tfrac12\log(2p b+1)+\log 2\) after division by \(p\) apply uniformly across this block.
Thus every counted \(k\) supplies a simultaneous pair of passing rows, within any diverging cap eventually.
The second line of (5) and \(p_j/D_j\to0\) imply
\[
             \liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{p_j J_j(w)}{D_j}
                         \ge\frac{\varphi(B)}{eB}>0 .
\]
This proves the second fixed-width implication by the same reduction to a small positive base measure.
The degrees supplied in either width proof can all have the same two measures \(h,2h\).

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed counting degrees in common-prime dyadic blocks and using the congruence \(k\equiv1\bmod p_j\) at the narrower width.
A collaborating agent developed the interval estimates, support-preserving width correction and the joint-score formulation.
