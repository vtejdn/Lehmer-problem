# The resultant cost of doubling a Salem root depends on the row difference

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform bounds at the prime three

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, \(6p\mid d\), \(m=d/p\), and set
\[
   P(x)=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1,\qquad X=x^p.
\]
This is the [degree-\(d\) Salem minimal polynomial](Yin_C_G0022_v1.md#salem-first-window-precise-statement).
Fix \(1\le r<p\) and consider nonzero integer Laurent multiples of \(P^2\) on two residue classes whose difference modulo \(p\) is \(r\).
After a monomial shift, write such a multiple as \(F=U(X)+x^rV(X)\), with nonzero integral Laurent rows coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Remove the individual lowest powers of \(X\) to obtain polynomials \(U_0,V_0\) with nonzero constant terms.
Let \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|\), and put
\[
     q=3^{v_3(m)},\qquad
     k_r=\max\{p-r-v_3(r),\,r-v_3(p-r)\}.
\]
For the minimum over these witnesses,
\[
 m k_r^2-2k_r+\frac1q
      \ \le\ \min_F v_3(H)
      \ \le\ m\{p^2+r v_3(p-r)+(p-r)v_3(r)\}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The lower bound allows arbitrary coefficients, degree and scalar content.
The upper bound is attained as a witness valuation by a primitive polynomial on the classes \(0,r\) of degree \(p(d+1)\).

In particular, as \(p\to\infty\), the ratio \(\min_F v_3(H)/(dp)\) tends to one for adjacent classes, uniformly over all \(6p\mid d\).
More generally its lower limit is at least \(1/4\) uniformly over all \(r\), and its upper limit is at most one.
The corresponding single-root witness on adjacent classes has \(v_3(H)=d/p\).
Thus doubling this root forces a cost of order \(dp\) in the actual resultant at three, even though the [two-group collision estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#collision) supplies only a contribution of order at most \(d/p\) there.

<a id="congruences"></a>
## Two congruences from a repeated zero

Put \(B=X^m+2\), \(A=2X^m+1=2B-3\), and
\[
         E=xB-A=(x-1)P(x),\qquad K=XB^p-A^p .
\]
If \(\alpha\) is a root of \(P\), let \(\beta=\alpha^p\) and \(Q\) be its monic minimal polynomial.
The [power-index calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) gives distinct \(p\)-th powers on the \(d+1\) roots of \(E\), including one.
Indeed a nontrivial \(p\)-th rotation of a root changes \(E\) by \((\zeta-1)xB\), and \(E\) cannot vanish when \(B=0\), since \(A=-3\) there.
As \(K\) is monic of degree \(d+1\) and vanishes at these powered nodes, \(K=(X-1)Q\).
Also \(A(\beta)=\alpha B(\beta)\ne0\).

Multiplication by a common power of \(X\) makes the original Laurent rows ordinary polynomials and preserves divisibility by \(P^2\).
Use these polynomial rows for the congruences.
Since \(F(\alpha)=0\),
\[
                B^rU+A^rV=Q L,\qquad L\in\mathbb Z[X],
\]
by monic division.
The geometric sum \(T_j(x)=((xB)^j-A^j)/(xB-A)\) satisfies \(T_j(\alpha)=j A(\beta)^{j-1}\).
In particular \(K(x^p)=E(x)T_p(x)\) and
\[
          B^r F=Q L+E T_r V .
\]
Divide by \(E\) and evaluate at \(\alpha\).
The left side has a zero there because \(E\) has a simple zero and \(P^2\mid F\), while \(Q/E=T_p/(X-1)\) is regular there.
This gives
\[
 \begin{split}
       r(\beta-1)V(\beta)&=-p A(\beta)^{p-r}L(\beta),\\
       r(\beta-1)U(\beta)&= p\beta B(\beta)^{p-r}L(\beta).              \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
For the second equality use \(B^r U=-A^r V\) at \(\beta\) and \(A^p=\beta B^p\) there.
By monicity, both identities hold as polynomial congruences modulo \(Q\) over \(\mathbb Z\).
The second orientation is available because \(x^{p-r}F=XV+x^{p-r}U\), giving (2) at row difference \(p-r\) with rows \(XV,U\).
This shift multiplies just one row by an \(X\)-monomial after exchanging their order.

<a id="disks"></a>
## Row valuations on disks above the roots of \(X^m-1\)

Put \(m_0=m/q\).
In a splitting extension of \(\mathbb Q_3\) containing the \(m_0\)-th roots of unity and an element \(\tau\) of valuation \(\sigma=1/q\), normalize \(v(3)=1\).
For each \(\lambda^{m_0}=1\) let \(g_\lambda(W)\) be the minimum coefficient valuation of \(W(\lambda+\tau Y)\).
These Gauss valuations are multiplicative in the sense that \(g_\lambda(WZ)=g_\lambda(W)+g_\lambda(Z)\), by reduction after normalizing the coefficient minima.
The \(\lambda\) have pairwise distinct residues because \(3\nmid m_0\).
As \(B\equiv(X^{m_0}-1)^q\bmod3\) and \(g_\lambda(X^{m_0}-1)\ge \sigma\), we have \(g_\lambda(B)\ge1\).
Hence \(g_\lambda(A)\ge1\) and \(g_\lambda(K)\ge p\).
Set \(\delta_\lambda=g_\lambda(X-1)\).
It equals zero except at \(\lambda=1\), where it is \(\sigma\).
Thus \(g_\lambda(Q)\ge p-\delta_\lambda\ge p-1\).

All coefficient polynomials in the integral congruences (2) have nonnegative Gauss valuations.
In particular \(r(X-1)V\) differs from \(-pA^{p-r}L\) by an integral multiple of \(Q\), and the analogous assertion holds for \(U\).
Both left sides have Gauss valuation at least \(p-r\).
Applying the same argument in the other orientation, and using \(g_\lambda(X)=0\) for every center, gives
\[
       \min\{g_\lambda(U_0),g_\lambda(V_0)\}
                  \ \ge\ k_r-\delta_\lambda .                            \tag{3}
\]
Every monomial introduced or removed in the two row normalizations has valuation zero on these disks.

First apply (3) after dividing \(F\) by its common integer content, which preserves \(P^2\)-divisibility by Gauss's lemma.
At least one of its stripped rows is then primitive at three.
For these disks the [linear-factor estimate for the resultant](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#resultant) gives
\[
          v_3(H)\ \ge\
          \sum_{\lambda^{m_0}=1}\frac{g_\lambda(U_0)g_\lambda(V_0)}{\sigma}.
\]
Only the factor estimate in that argument is used here.
It requires distinct residue centers, positive depth in the value group, and one row primitive at three.
Indeed a normalized linear factor has a positive contribution at at most one center, and its contribution there is at most \(\sigma\).
The determinant of two normalized linear factors covers the product of their contributions divided by \(\sigma\).
The content valuation of the other row is covered by that scalar valuation times the degree of the primitive row.
No separation of specified polynomial roots within these disks is required for this factor estimate.
The omitted common integer content restores a nonnegative scalar valuation times \(\deg U_0+\deg V_0\).

Since \(k_r\ge p/2-\log_3 p>1/q\) for \(p\ge5\) and \(q\ge3\), all lower bounds in (3) are positive.
The resulting sum is at least
\[
       q\bigl((m_0-1)k_r^2+(k_r-1/q)^2\bigr)
          =m k_r^2-2k_r+1/q .
\]
This proves the lower bound in (1) for unbounded witness degree.

<a id="witness"></a>
## An explicit double-root witness for each row difference

Take
\[
           F_r=r(xB)^p+(p-r)A^p-p(xB)^r A^{p-r}.
\]
For independent variables \(t,a\) the corresponding homogeneous expression
\(r t^p+(p-r)a^p-p t^r a^{p-r}\) and its \(t\)-derivative both vanish at \(t=a\).
Thus \((t-a)^2\) divides the expression, so \(E^2\mid F_r\).
Its two rows are
\[
            U=r X B^p+(p-r)A^p,\qquad V=-p B^r A^{p-r}.
\]
The leading coefficient of \(U\) is \(r\), its constant term is \(p-r\), and \(V(0)=-p2^r\ne0\).
The entire polynomial is primitive since \(\gcd(r,p-r)=1\).
At every zero of \(B\), \(U=(p-r)(-3)^p\), and at every zero of \(A\), \(U=rX(3/2)^p\), a nonzero value because \(A(0)=1\).
Consequently \(U,V\) are coprime and no row monomial is removed.
The degree is \(p(d+1)\), and \(F_1\) recovers the [adjacent-row doubling formula](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#doubling).

The leading coefficient of \(V\) is \(-p2^{p-r}\), a unit at three.
The \(m\) roots of \(A\) satisfy \(X^m=-1/2\), so all have valuation zero there.
The root-product formula, counting each root of \(B\) with multiplicity \(r\) and each root of \(A\) with multiplicity \(p-r\), gives
\[
            v_3(H)=m\{p^2+r v_3(p-r)+(p-r)v_3(r)\}.
\]
This proves existence and the stated upper bound.
Dividing (1) by \(dp=mp^2\) and using \(v_3(j)\le\log_3 p\) for \(1\le j<p\) gives the uniform limiting inequalities.
For \(r=1,p-1\), \(k_r=p-1\), proving convergence to one.
For a prescribed translate of the classes \(0,r\), a monomial shift of the upper witness preserves the stripped rows and their resultant.
For comparison the single-root witness \(E\) has \(H=3^m\), since \(|A|=3\) at every root of the monic \(B\).
