# A Mahler gap from the scales of a cyclotomic product's binomial exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## Boundedly many intervals on a multiplicative scale

Let \(b_d\in\mathbb Z\), \(d\ge1\), have finite support and put \(L(\theta)=\sum_d b_d\log|1-e^{id\theta}|\).
Use normalized angular measure on the circle.
Fix \(h\ge1,\ C\ge1\), and suppose the support is contained in a union of at most \(h\) intervals \([x,Cx]\), with unrestricted real \(x>0\).
Then a bound on \(\|L\|_1\) bounds \(\sum_d|b_d|\) in terms of that bound, \(h\) and \(C\).
In particular the norm tends to infinity as the latter sum tends to infinity with \(h,C\) fixed.
The exponents may have either sign and intervals may be arbitrarily far apart.

For a nonconstant integer polynomial \(A=\pm\prod_d(1-X^d)^{b_d}\) under the same support condition and every \(a\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), \(k\ge0\), the positive values of \(m(A+aX^k)=\log M(A+aX^k)\) have a uniform gap depending only on \(h,C\).
Their full measures tend to infinity as \(\sum_d|b_d|\to\infty\), uniformly also over complex \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\).
In particular this covers products of arbitrary numbers of \(1-X^d\) with \(D\le d\le CD\), and their monomial perturbations, uniformly in \(D\).
Every product of cyclotomic polynomials has such a finite binomial expansion by the Möbius formula, with a possible overall sign.
The exponents \(b_d\) are uniquely determined by \(A\), since the coefficient of the first differing positive degree in the ratio of two normalized products is nonzero.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Integer Fourier coefficients on a large prime grid

For any trigonometric polynomial \(f(\theta)=\sum_t c_t e^{it\theta}\) with integer coefficients, a bound \(M\) on \(\|f\|_1\) bounds \(\sum_t |c_t|\) in terms of \(M\) alone.
Indeed extend its coefficient function by zero on \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) for a prime \(p\) large enough to separate the distinct support indices.
Its finite Fourier algebra norm is the mean of \(|f|\) on the \(p\)-th roots of unity and tends to \(\|f\|_1\) as \(p\to\infty\).
For every sufficiently large prime the [integer-valued finite-group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) therefore expresses the coefficient function as a sum of at most \(S(M+1)\) signed coset indicators for a bound depending only on \(M\).
The cosets on this prime grid are singletons or the entire group.
Choose \(p>|\operatorname{supp}f|+S(M+1)\), where the support here is the set of nonzero coefficients.
Some zero coefficient is outside all the singleton terms, so the total contribution of the full-group indicators is zero.
The singleton terms bound \(\sum_t|c_t|\) by \(S(M+1)\).

<a id="filter"></a>
## Recovering the lowest group of exponents

Suppose the least occupied index is \(D\) and the first group ends at an integer \(E\ge D\), with no further occupied index below or at \(2E\).
Here the exclusion refers to indices beyond \(E\).
Set \(K=\lfloor 2E/D\rfloor\), \(F(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^K\mu(j)L(j\theta)/j\), and \(f(\theta)=\sum_{D\le d\le E} b_d\cos(d\theta)\).
The [Fourier series of the circle logarithm](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#fourier) has cosine coefficient \(-1/l\) at each \(l\ge1\).
Thus at frequency \(1\le t\le2E\) the cosine coefficient of \(F\) is
\[
  -\sum_{d\mid t}\frac{d b_d}{t}
       \sum_{\substack{j\mid t/d\\j\le K}}\mu(j)=-b_t .
\]
Indeed \(t/d\le K\) whenever \(b_d\ne0\).
The constant coefficient is zero.
Take the convolution kernel \(2\mathcal F_{2E}-\mathcal F_E\), where the Fejér kernel \(\mathcal F_s=s^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}e^{ij\theta}|^2\) has integral one and multiplier \((1-|t|/s)_+\).
This convolution has multiplier one on \(|t|\le E\) and zero on \(|t|\ge2E\), with operator norm at most three on \(L^1\).
The intervening coefficients vanish by the separation assumption, so applying it to \(F\) gives exactly \(-f\).
Consequently
\[
               \|2f\|_1 \le 6\left(\sum_{j=1}^{K}1/j\right)\|L\|_1 .
\]
All Fourier coefficients of \(2f\) are integral and their absolute sum is \(2\sum_{D\le d\le E}|b_d|\).
The [prime-grid bound](#coefficients) controls the latter sum whenever \(E/D\) and \(\|L\|_1\) are bounded.

<a id="iteration"></a>
## Removing one group at a time

Discard empty intervals and replace each interval by the interval between its least and greatest occupied indices, preserving the bound \(C\) for its endpoint ratio.
Order the intervals by the left endpoint.
Merge consecutive intervals whenever the next left endpoint is at most twice the largest right endpoint reached so far in the current group.
In a merge the right endpoint increases by a factor of at most \(2C\).
Each resulting group has endpoint ratio at most \((2C)^h\), and the lowest occupied index in the next group is strictly greater than twice the last index in the preceding group.

Apply the [finite Fourier inversion](#filter) to the first group.
Its contribution to \(L\) has \(L^1\) norm at most \(\|\log|1-e^{i\theta}|\|_1\sum_{d\ \mathrm{in\ group}}|b_d|\), by invariance of circle probability under integer dilation.
Subtract it and repeat for each of the at most \(h\) groups.
Both the successive norms and the sums of the absolute exponents are bounded by iterating bounds depending only on the initial norm, \(h,C\).
This proves the assertion for signed exponents.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Returning to the full Mahler measure

If the product defining \(A\) is a polynomial then all its roots are on the circle, its leading coefficient has modulus one and \(L=\log|A|\) has mean zero.
The [circle-root integral bound](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives \(m(A+aX^k)\ge\|L\|_1/2-\sqrt{\pi}\,\zeta(3/2)\) when \(|a|\ge1\).
Thus the norm divergence forces the claimed measure divergence.
If the measure stays bounded, the signed exponent sum in absolute value is bounded in the stronger sense \(\sum_d|b_d|=O(1)\) by the same estimate and the iteration.
Multiplying the shifted polynomial by the denominator \(\prod_{b_d<0}(1-X^d)^{-b_d}\) now gives the sum of two monomial multiples of products of boundedly many binomials.
The result is nonzero because \(A\) has a nonzero root, and its measure equals the original one.
For integral \(a\) the [bounded-term Mahler theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives a uniform positive gap below any fixed upper measure bound.
Taking that upper bound to be one for \(m\) suffices.
