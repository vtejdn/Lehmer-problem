# Deciding sparse contact tests over all widths in fields preserved by powering

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact spectra with no bound on the common width or the gaps between terms

Fix \(s\ge2\), a positive integer \(H\), and an integer \(n>n_0(s,1)\) from the [uniform bound for genuine sparse rows](Yin_C_G0345_v482.md#statement).
Consider nontorsion algebraic units \(\gamma\) such that \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^k)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) for every positive integer \(k\).
Use any fixed finite list of signed rows
\[
          F_i(X)=A_i(X^n)+\varepsilon_i X^{nb+1}A_i(X^{-n}),
          \qquad 0\le\deg A_i\le b,\quad b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
Each \(A_i\) has at most \(s\) terms and integer coefficients of total absolute value at most \(H\).
At least one row must vanish at \(\gamma,\gamma^2\) with \(A_i(\gamma^n)\ne0\).
Within those requirements one can impose fixed orders of contact at any finite list of positive powers of \(\gamma\), numerator vanishing or nonvanishing there, and the nonzero determinant condition on a pair of opposite signs from the [transverse test](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement).
Orders may be lower bounds or exact.
The full list of qualifying units, their distinct Mahler measures and the set of **all** admissible widths for each unit are effectively computable.
Each width set is a finite union of arithmetic progressions and a finite set.
The claim allows completely unrelated placements in the different rows.
The computation need not be practical.

For a gap test choose an algebraic \(1<Q\le6/5\) and a computable, sufficiently slow sequence of integer term caps \(s_p\to\infty\) for which \(p>n_0(s_p,1)\) whenever a prime \(p\) is tested.
At each such prime admit all nonnegative integer widths, and use two genuine transverse rows with common double contacts at a reciprocal unit and its square as in the [threshold test](Yin_C_G0345_v480.md#statement).
Require the unit to preserve its degree under all positive powers.
Use the strict budget
\[
                  2H_+H_-<Q^{6p}.                                          \tag{1}
\]
Let \(V_p(Q)\) consist of the distinct positive logarithmic measures represented.
Then each \(V_p(Q)\) and its possible widths for each represented unit are computable, and
\[
 \lambda\ge\log Q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_p(Q)=\varnothing
             \text{ for all tested primes}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
             V_p(Q)=\varnothing\text{ for infinitely many tested primes}.   \tag{2}
\]
Every positive irreducible measure below the threshold occurs for all sufficiently large primes.
One may also multiply the left of (1) by \(2pb+1\).
The uncharged assertion permits an unbounded search in width and degree in its definition.
Decidability follows without a span cap, a prescribed width-to-degree ratio or a common placement pattern.

<a id="targets"></a>
## An effective finite list of units

The genuine row has a nonzero graph since its two layers occupy disjoint residues modulo \(n\).
Its coefficient one-norm is at most \(2H\).
The monic minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) divides that graph over \(\mathbb Z\), so \(M(\gamma)\le2H\) by multiplicativity and the coefficient norm bound.
The smallest power degree in the [sparse row estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v482.md#descent) is exactly \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
Hence \(d\le C(s,1)(1+\log H)^s\), independently of \(b\).
One may take a computable integer upper bound for the right side.
At each degree in this finite range, the coefficient of \(X^{d-i}\) of the monic minimum has modulus at most \(2H\binom di\) by the elementary symmetric functions of the conjugates.
Enumerate this integral box and its irreducible unit minima, testing torsion exactly.
The [power-field calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) tests the equality of all power fields by one finite powering from the degree.
The list of roots after these filters contains every possible input.
It remains necessary to decide the requested graph and numerator conditions at each particular root.

<a id="positions"></a>
## Enumerating simultaneous vanishing patterns of any fixed orders

Fix a retained nontorsion unit and choose one embedding under which its image \(z\) has \(R=|z|>1\).
Such an embedding exists by the unit condition and Kronecker's theorem, and it can be found by exact comparisons.
All positive powers used for contacts have modulus greater than one at this same embedding.
Fix a finite list of nonzero integer coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\) and consider the exponent vectors \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_m)\in\mathbb Z^m\) of the Laurent sum \(\sum c_i X^{u_i}\).
For specified positive integers \(a\) and multiplicities \(l_a\), its vanishing to each prescribed order at \(z^a\) means
\[
           \sum_i c_i u_i^j z^{a u_i}=0 \qquad (0\le j< l_a)
\]
by the Euler derivatives, with the zeroth power convention also at exponent zero.
All these equations together are invariant under a common translation of the exponents, by the binomial expansion of each derivative order and invertibility of the translation.
An empty system imposes no conditions.

For nonempty systems the exponent vectors yielding vanishing form an effective finite union of sets defined by equalities \(u_i-u_k=t_{ik}\) with fixed integers \(t_{ik}\).
To see this, partition any vanishing list into inclusion-minimal nonempty subsets vanishing to all prescribed orders.
For each candidate subset sort its exponents in decreasing order, trying all finitely many orders, and translate its largest exponent to zero.
The remaining exponents are \(-v_i\) with \(0=v_1\le\cdots\le v_m\) on that subset.
Every nonempty proper prefix is nonzero in at least one of the displayed coordinates.
Once its offsets are fixed, choose such a coordinate \((a,j)\) by exact algebraic evaluation and a positive rational lower bound for the modulus of this prefix sum.
If the next offset is at least \(T\), the remaining contribution has absolute value at most
\[
       \left(\sum_i |c_i|\right)\sup_{v\ge T,\ v\in\mathbb Z}v^j R^{-a v}.
\]
A computable upper bound tends to zero with \(T\).
For example choose rational \(1<r<R\) and take \(T\) so large that \(v^j r^{-av}\) decreases for \(v\ge T\), using the ratio of successive terms.
The displayed estimate is then bounded by \((\sum_i|c_i|)T^j r^{-aT}\).
Once this is below the chosen prefix lower bound, all possible next offsets are less than \(T\).
A prefix vanishing in all coordinates cannot extend to a minimal block and is discarded for that enumeration.
The finite recursion finds every possible list of offsets of a minimal block and verifies it exactly, including ties.
Each block may be translated independently because all lower derivative orders were included.
Taking the finite union over partitions and passing offset lists gives the claimed description in differences.
The converse holds even if the exponents of different blocks coincide, since their separate sums and derivatives vanish.
All operations at the chosen embedding test equations of algebraic numbers, so they also decide vanishing at the original powers of \(\gamma\).

<a id="integer"></a>
## Recovering integer widths and joint placements

List the signed coefficients of each row in increasing position order.
The integer norm and term cap give finitely many lists.
The positions \(j_{ih}\) and common width \(b\) are integer variables subject to \(0\le j_{i1}<\cdots<j_{im_i}\le b\).
For each row the exponents in the graph are \(nj_{ih}\) and \(nb+1-nj_{ih}\).
Substitute these affine forms into the finite systems for simultaneous zeros.
A prescribed numerator value of zero uses just the first layer, and nonvanishing is its Boolean complement.
Exact orders are differences between two vanishing conditions.
For a transverse pair the determinant is the sum of two ordinary products of rows with their opposite reversed rows.
Each of its exponents is affine in the positions and width, and all coefficients of its uncollected terms are fixed.
Partition those terms according to equal exponents using affine equalities and disequalities.
The determinant is nonzero precisely when at least one class has nonzero total coefficient.

Project the resulting finite Boolean combination of affine integer conditions onto \(b\).
For completeness, congruences can be allowed throughout this projection.
Use disjunctive normal form, treat a strict integral inequality as a weak one after a unit shift, and expand the complement of a congruence into its other residue classes.
To eliminate a variable \(x\) in one conjunction, first split its residue modulo a common multiple \(M\) of all congruence moduli.
Their truth now only constrains the other variables.
Take \(L\) divisible by all absolute nonzero coefficients of \(x\) in the inequalities, using one for an empty list.
Writing \(y=Lx\) turns these inequalities into lower and upper affine integral bounds on \(y\) by multiplication with the corresponding positive integer.
The class \(x\equiv t\bmod M\) becomes \(y\equiv Lt\bmod LM\).
If one side of the bounds is absent there is always such a \(y\) subject to the retained constraints on the other variables.
Otherwise choose the largest lower bound by a finite disjunction and split its residue modulo \(LM\).
The offset to the first allowed \(y\) above or equal to this bound is then fixed, so it suffices to test that value against all upper bounds.
Iteration leaves a finite Boolean combination of affine inequalities and congruences in \(b\) alone.
Above an effective finite cutoff its inequalities are constant and its congruences are periodic.
Testing the initial interval and a full period gives all nonnegative integer widths, including an effective witness whenever they exist.

## Eventual occurrence of every subcritical measure

An admissible term cap can be chosen by taking the largest \(2\le s\le p\) such that \(p>\max_{2\le t\le s}n_0(t,1)\), ignoring the finitely many primes with no eligible \(s\).
This computable cap tends to infinity.
Any slower computable divergent choice satisfying the stated inequality works.
For a fixed prime in (1), an effective individual norm bound follows from \(H_\pm\ge1\) and (1).
The charged budget adds an affine upper bound on \(b\) for each coefficient pair, using exact algebraic comparisons.
The preceding algorithm therefore computes the spectrum with or without this charge.

The [transverse determinant inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement) gives \(6p\,m(\gamma)\le\log(2H_+H_-)\) for each passing pair, so every value is strictly below \(\log Q\).
For each positive irreducible measure below it, the [fixed-seed threshold construction](Yin_C_G0345_v480.md#spectrum) gives a reciprocal representative with all powers of the same degree.
Before padding, it gives one fixed width and pairs at every sufficiently large prime with \(\log(2H_+H_-)/(6p)\) strictly below \(\log Q\) by a fixed margin.
The chosen cap eventually exceeds that fixed width plus one, and the optional width charge costs \(o(p)\) in the logarithm.
This proves eventual occurrence and (2) by factorization into primitive irreducibles.
Whether infinitely many prime spectra are empty at any positive threshold remains open.
