# A residual period test for a unit factor at either reciprocal endpoint

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on the sum or difference of the exponents

Take the [reciprocal section](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement) \(P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+bX^u(1+\epsilon X^{v-u})\) with its coprime nonzero coefficients and \(0<u<v\).
Let \(z\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with \(P(z^g)=0\), put \(\beta=z^g\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), and \(m(\beta)=D h(\beta)\).
For any prime \(\ell\mid a\) take \(N=v-u\), and for any prime \(\ell\mid b\) take \(N=v+u\).
Use \(M=N\) if \(\ell=2\) or \(\epsilon=-1\), and \(M=2N\) otherwise.
Let \(L\) be the order of \(X\) in the unit group of \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]/\overline S\), where \(S\) is the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Then \(L\mid M\) and
\[
 m(\beta)\ge \lambda_\ell D/L,\qquad
 \lambda_2=(\log2)/2,\qquad \lambda_\ell=\log(\ell/2)\quad(\ell\ge3).
\]
In particular when \(B=|b/a|>1\) and \(\max(|a|,|b|)\ge8\), a unit factor requires \(L/D\ge\lambda_\ell/(3(B-1))\) for every such prime.

For \(|a|=q,\ |b|=q+1\), let \(N=v-u\) if \(q\) is even and \(N=v+u\) if \(q\) is odd.
For every fixed prime \(p\) and fixed positive integer \(c\), the condition \(N=cp^j\), \(j\ge0\), excludes nontorsion unit roots at every dilation for all sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly in \(j,u,v,g\).
For \(N=2^j\) the exclusion holds already at \(q\ge18\), and for \(N=3^j\) it holds at \(q\ge13\).
In each excluded case every positive-measure integral factor has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log2\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Lifting a period back to an integer norm

Monic division gives \(S\mid P\) over \(\mathbb Z\), and \(S(0)=\pm1\).
Modulo \(\ell\mid a\), division by the invertible monomial in the quotient by \(\overline S\) gives \(\overline S\mid 1+\epsilon X^{v-u}\).
Modulo \(\ell\mid b\) one obtains \(\overline S\mid 1+\epsilon X^{v+u}\).
Thus \(X^M=1\) in this ring, with all multiplicities included.
Lifting \(\overline S\mid X^L-1\) gives \(\beta^L-1\in\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Its nonzero norm has magnitude at least \(\ell^D\) and at most \(2^D\exp(Lm(\beta))\).
If \(\ell=2\), squaring \(\beta^L=1+2s\) gives \(\beta^{2L}-1\in4\mathbb Z[\beta]\), so \(4^D\le2^D\exp(2Lm(\beta))\).
These prove the asserted period bound, and the [simultaneous unit measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#count) gives \(m(\beta)\le3(B-1)\).
This uses the full reduction of the primitive unit factor, including repeated factors, as in the [residue-period calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v755.md#period).

<a id="binary"></a>
## Periods on a fixed prime-power progression in characteristic two

In the consecutive-coefficient case there is always an even coefficient, and its index \(M=N\) gives \(L\mid N\).
For \(N=c2^j\) the odd part of \(L\) divides \(c\).
The two-part of \(L\) is at most \(2^{\lceil\log_2 D\rceil}\le2D\), since raising a squarefree annihilator to this power covers the multiplicity of every irreducible factor of \(\overline S\).
This gives \(L\le2cD\).

For \(N=cp^j\) with \(p\) odd, put \(d=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\) and \(e=v_p(2^d-1)\).
Write \(p^r\) for the full \(p\)-part of \(L\).
If \(r>0\), some irreducible factor of \(\overline S\) has a root with order divisible by \(p^r\), since the extra order needed for multiplicities is a power of two.
Its degree over \(\mathbb F_2\) is at least \(\operatorname{ord}_{p^r}(2)\).
For \(r>0\), this order is \(d\,p^{\max(0,r-e)}\): by the binomial expansion each raising to the \(p\)-th power increases the valuation of the difference from one by one, and raising to a power prime to \(p\) preserves it.
Hence \(p^r\le\max(1,p^e/d)D\), also for \(r=0\).
The part of \(L\) prime to \(p\) divides \(c\), and \(L\le c\max(1,p^e/d)D\).
In both cases the resulting positive lower bound for \(m(\beta)\) contradicts \(m(\beta)\le3/q\) for large \(q\).
When \(c=1\) and \(p=2\) the lower bound is \((\log2)/4\), and for \(p=3\) the values \(d=2,e=1\) give \((\log2)/3\).
These give the two explicit thresholds.
The exclusion at the primitive power excludes units at every dilation, and the integral factor gap follows from [the endpoint coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree).
