### 3. Complete positive-integer classification of the needed equation

**Lemma 1.** For positive integers g,X,Y,Z, equation(3) is solvable exactly when g=4.
Every positive solution for g=4 is obtained from(1,1,1) by permuting coordinates and repeatedly replacing one coordinate, for example

    Z -> 4XY-Z.                                    (4)

All these solutions are odd and primitive.

*Proof.* First reduce(3) modulo four.
If none or exactly one of X,Y,Z is odd, the left side is respectively1 or2 modulo four and the right side is divisible by four.
If exactly two are odd, the left side is3 and the right side is even.
Both cases are impossible.
Thus all three are odd, their squared sum plus one is0 modulo four, and XYZ is odd.
Consequently g is divisible by four, in particular g>=4.

For any positive solution, the other root of the monic quadratic in Z is

    Z'=gXY-Z=(X^2+Y^2+1)/Z.                         (5)

It is a positive integer and gives another solution.
If Z is the largest coordinate and Z>gXY/2, replace it by Z'; the sum of the three coordinates strictly decreases.
Reorder and repeat.
The positive integer sum cannot decrease indefinitely, so one reaches a solution with X<=Y<=Z<=gXY/2.
For

    f(t)=t^2-gXYt+(X^2+Y^2+1),

the attained Z is a root and f is decreasing up to gXY/2.

    0<=f(Y)=X^2+1-(gX-2)Y^2.                        (6)

The remaining equation is Z^2-4Z+3=0; the terminal condition Z<=2 selects Z=1, rather than its other root3.
Therefore the terminal solution is exactly g=4,(1,1,1).

Every descent step is reversible by the same involution and a permutation.
Starting from(1,1,1), formula(5) ensures that each coordinate replacement stays positive; it is integral by(4), preserves the equation, and is its own inverse.

### 2. A complete integer descent at discriminant five

For an integral binary quadratic polynomial

    f(x,y)=a x^2+b xy+c y^2,  b^2-4ac=5,             (1)

Neither a nor c can vanish, since that would give the impossible integer equation b^2=5.
Also b is odd, because b^2=5 modulo4.

**Lemma 1.** Every f in(1) is equivalent under SL_2(Z) to

    f_*(x,y)=x^2+xy-y^2.                            (2)

*Proof.* For h in Z let S_h=[[1,h],[0,1]].
Substituting (x+h y,y) replaces the coefficients by

    (a,b,c) -> (a,b+2ah,ah^2+bh+c).                (3)

Choose h so that the new middle coefficient b' satisfies |b'|<=|a|.
This is possible by taking a nearest representative modulo2a, for either sign of a.
Discriminant is preserved, so the new final coefficient is c'=(b'^2-5)/(4a), a nonzero integer.
If |a|>=2, then

    |c'| <= max{5,a^2-5}/(4|a|) < |a|.             (4)

Indeed 0<=b'^2<=a^2, and both 5 and a^2-5 are strictly smaller than4a^2 when |a|>=2.

This is an unbounded integer descent, with no finite scan or class-number assumption.

At termination apply a shear again to have |b|<=1.

If a=-1, apply R once to obtain a=1,c=-1, with b still +/-1.
If the resulting b=-1, apply S_1; its new middle coefficient is1 and its last coefficient remains-1.
