# Which divisible conjugate groups contain Mahler values with small second iterate?

<a id="statement"></a>
## A terminal-term criterion and the least first measure

Write \(m=\log M\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure.
In \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}/\mu_\infty\), viewed as a rational vector space, let \(V_x\) be the span of all conjugates of \([x]\).
Fix any family of nontorsion algebraic units \(w_i\) with \(0<m(w_i)<\varepsilon\), for a sufficiently small absolute \(\varepsilon>0\).
The family may be infinite.
Let \(U\) be any rational Galois-stable subspace of \(\sum_i V_{w_i}\), and put \(\Gamma_U=\{x:[x]\in U\}\).
For each input take the last term \(z\) of its [compressed block-product chain](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent).
Make the following list.

* Require \(V_z\subseteq U\).
* Require that, up to conjugation, a rational sign and inversion, \(z\) is a positive real Salem number or a positive reciprocal Perron unit with the [square pattern](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#statement): one marked half with its unique exterior root and all other roots interior, neutral pairs outside the marked block, torsion half products and \(M(z)=|z|^2\) in the positive exterior orientation.
* Record the positive primitive unit \(\rho>1\) on that oriented line in its actual real root field, with repetitions discarded.

Consider all nonzero integer polynomials \(Q\) for which
\[
     0<m(Q)<\varepsilon,\qquad M(Q)\in\Gamma_U,\qquad
                       m(M(Q))\le 2m(Q).                                 \tag{1}
\]
Only the **value** \(M(Q)\) is required to lie in the group; the roots of \(Q\) are unrestricted.
This class is nonempty exactly when the list is nonempty.
Its infimum of \(m(Q)\) equals \(\inf_\rho m(\rho)\), using \(+\infty\) for the empty set.
Each listed measure is attained by the minimum polynomial of \(\rho\), with all its roots in \(\Gamma_U\), and is at most \(m(w_i)\) for any source whose terminal term supplies this entry.
In particular a finite nonempty list gives an attained minimum.

When the list is empty, for each fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) every integer polynomial \(Q\) with \(M(Q)\in\Gamma_U\) and sufficiently small \(m(Q)>0\) in terms of \(\eta\) satisfies
\[
        m(M(Q))\ \ge\
          \exp\!\bigl(\eta\,m(Q)^{-1/3}\log(1/m(Q))\bigr).                \tag{2}
\]
Thus for an arbitrary integer polynomial \(P\) of sufficiently small positive measure one can test both the group generated by all its nonzero roots and the possibly smaller group generated by the conjugates of its actual value \(A=M(P)\).
Use its positive-measure irreducible factors as the inputs and respectively \(U=\sum_iV_{w_i}\) and \(U=V_A\).
In either group the test decides whether (1) can be reached with first log measure at most \(m(P)\), even if the given \(A\) lies on the rapidly growing branch.

Two further exclusions concern all rational powers on the value's own line and arbitrary mixing of escaping strict degree records, respectively.

<a id="terminal"></a>
## Extracting the endpoint inside the group of the value

Let \(B=M(Q)>1\) satisfy (1).
There is a unit \(y\in\Gamma_{V_B}\) from one of the two structural classes on the list with \(m(y)\le m(Q)\).
Indeed at the lower endpoint of the [interval theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) one can use the Salem number \(B\) itself.
In the strict interior, the [half-product descent](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interior) starts with a unit whose positive power equals \(B\).
The descending values are signed products of conjugates and their powers in its divisible conjugate group, ending in a smaller Salem number.
At the upper endpoint the same proof writes \(B=\lambda^k\), with \(M(\lambda)=\lambda^2\), \(m(\lambda)\le m(Q)\), and all positive powers of \(\lambda\) of full degree.
Thus \(m(M(\lambda))=2m(\lambda)\); the [single-input reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal) yields the asserted \(y\) in the group of \(\lambda\), equal to the group of \(B\).
These arguments apply uniformly by decreasing the absolute cutoff.

In either case \(V_y\) is irreducible.
For a Salem number, ordinary complex conjugation inverts all pairs except the real pair and fixes every pair label.
Its conjugates therefore give sign changes missing each of the labels in turn; products give all double inversions.
At large degree these distinguish the coordinate characters, so averaging over the even inversions projects onto individual coordinates, and transitivity gives irreducibility of the signed coordinate space.
Its nonzero map onto \(V_y\) is an isomorphism.
In the square case the oriented blockwise sum-zero space is irreducible and the only remaining summand comes from the half products, which are torsion here. [Chain decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent)

For a finite set of sources supporting \([y]\), the [combined decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules) is a direct sum of actual pairwise inequivalent simple spaces \(W\).
The identification of repeated spaces uses only the individual small-measure and large-degree cutoffs and therefore also holds without any bound on their sum. [Individually small sources](Yin_C_G0901_v119.md#counts)
As \(V_y\subseteq U\) is irreducible, it equals one such \(W\).
Let \(z\) be an associated chain term from one of these sources.
The kernel field of its leading space \(W\) is the splitting field of \(z\); the same kernel field is the splitting field of \(y\), since a strictly Perron unit is compressed.
Both \(m(y)\) and \(m(z)\) are at most the chosen absolute cutoff, while their compressed degrees tend uniformly to infinity with decreasing cutoff by bounded-degree finiteness.
The [kernel-field comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields) thus forces matching.
It follows that \(V_z=V_y=W\), so \(z\) has no further chain term.

In the real embedding on the matched line the compressed representatives have the same point-stabilizer field, whose roots of unity are the rational signs.
Both \(y\) and a suitable signed conjugate of \(z\) are nonzero integral powers of a single positive primitive \(\rho>1\) there.
All powers have full degree, by the [line saturation and degree identity](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields).
The strict maximum for \(y\) gives a strict maximum for \(\rho\); orienting by inversion if necessary also covers a negative exponent for \(z\).
Taking nonzero powers identifies all reciprocal pairs equivariantly, and positive powers preserve interior, exterior and neutral places, as well as torsion of each half product in both directions.
Thus \(\rho\) and the oriented \(z\) retain the indicated pattern, including \(M(\rho)=\rho\) or \(\rho^2\), and \(m(\rho)\le\min(m(y),m(z))\).
Conversely each listed \(\rho\) belongs to \(\Gamma_U\), its minimum polynomial has first measure \(M(\rho)\) and second log measure at most \(2m(\rho)\), and \(m(\rho)\le m(z)\le m(w_i)<\varepsilon\).
This proves the criterion and the infimum.
With an empty list the interval alternative is excluded, so the other part of the interval theorem gives (2).
For the application to \(P\), below \(\log2\) all nonzero noncyclotomic roots are units, their factor measures are at most \(m(P)\), and \(A\) is a product of their exterior products.

<a id="line"></a>
## The fast lower bound survives extraction of every radical on the value line

For arbitrary \(P\), put \(m=m(P)>0\), \(A=M(P)\), and suppose \(m(A)>2m\).
For each fixed \(\eta\) in (2) and sufficiently small \(m\) in terms of \(\eta\), **every** \(x\) with \(x^j=\xi A^k\), nonzero integers \(j,k\) and a root of unity \(\xi\), satisfies
\[
                   m(x)\ \ge\
                    \exp\!\bigl(\eta\,m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\bigr).            \tag{3}
\]
There are no restrictions on the radical denominator, on the location of \(x\) or on its being a Mahler value.

Indeed \(A\) is a positive Perron unit, so the minimum of \(m(x)\) on this line is \(m(\kappa)\) for the primitive positive unit \(\kappa>1\) on the line in \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
Compression preserves \(m(x)\), and the [saturation identity](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields) gives every possible value there as a positive integral multiple of \(m(\kappa)\).
The primitive unit itself is compressed since \(A\) is a positive power of it of full degree.
Choose \(z\) of maximal degree \(d\) among the active representatives for \([A]\) from the original factors.
If \(A\) matched \(z\), the [factor and coordinate argument](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval) would give \(m(A)\le2m\).
The proportional class \([\kappa]\) is therefore unmatched to this same \(z\); its active spaces and splitting field are identical to those of \(A\).
The [quantitative unmatched estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#quantitative) applies to this compressed target: its nonzero leading projection has orbit size at most \(\deg\kappa\), and the remaining active fields are unmatched of degrees at most \(d\).
In the small-orbit case a marked conjugate of \(z\) is independent of \(\kappa\); in the large-orbit case the supported three-cycle acts nontrivially on its leading projection.
These are the two inputs of that comparison, giving \(m(\kappa)m(z)\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) without scaling the bound down by the exponent of \(A\).
The [degree inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#iterate) uses \(m(z)\le m\) and gives (3).

<a id="records"></a>
## Polynomials assembled from fast strict records at any fixed first-measure ceiling

Fix \(T>0\).
Take any family of reciprocal strict degree records \(w_i\) of distinct degrees with sufficiently small positive individual measures in terms of \(T\), all satisfying \(m(M(w_i))>2m(w_i)\).
Consider an integer polynomial \(Q\) with \(m(Q)\le T\), at least one nontorsion root, and all of its nonzero roots in the divisible group of their conjugates.
Neither \(Q\) nor its factors need be records.
For each nontorsion irreducible factor, mark the record matched by its root; it is unique.
For the marked record \(w_*\) of largest degree \(d_*\), one has with an absolute \(c>0\)
\[
                  m(M(Q))\,m(w_*)\ \ge\
                              \frac{c\,d_*}{(\log(3d_*))^{212}}.          \tag{4}
\]
In particular if the largest allowed individual record measure tends to zero, the second log measures of all these polynomials tend uniformly to infinity, even allowing their first log measures anywhere up to the fixed \(T\).

Every nonzero root is a unit and the polynomial is, up to powers of \(X\), its leading coefficient times a product of monic unit polynomials.
At ceiling \(T\), the [joint bounded spectrum](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#statement) makes each nontorsion root match one record and makes its total log measure a positive integral multiple of that record's measure.
Indeed each root's individual measure is at most \(T\), and the degree cutoff for this theorem holds uniformly by bounded-degree finiteness.
Put \(A_i=M(w_i)\).
Multiplying factor measures with multiplicity gives \(M(Q)=|\operatorname{lc}Q|\,B\), where \(B=\prod_i A_i^{e_i}\) with positive integer exponents on precisely the marked finite set.
Each nonzero \([A_i]\) lies in its own irreducible record module, and the direct sum across distinct records prevents cancellation.
Thus the largest active record for \([B]\) is \(w_*\).
If several records occur, \(B\) cannot match a coordinate of \(w_*\).
If only \(w_*\) occurs, the [matched single-input calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#iterate) and its one-term record chain would imply \(m(A_*)\le2m(w_*)\) in the matching case.
This excludes matching of its power \(B\) also.
The [quantitative record comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#statement) now gives (4) for \(m(B)\).
Multiplication by the positive integer \(|\operatorname{lc}Q|\) preserves the degree of the unit \(B\) and cannot decrease any conjugate's \(\log^+\), proving (4) as stated.

These tests require no assumption that a sequence of fast shrinking records exists.
They characterize when the indicated multiplicative operations can reach bounded second iterate without increasing a small first measure; they do not establish the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
