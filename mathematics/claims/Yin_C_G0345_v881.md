# All radicals and torsion multiples of the sextic Salem example at consecutive coefficient heights

<a id="statement"></a>
## The only occurrences from the sextic seed

Let \(\lambda>1\) be the real exterior root of \(S(X)=X^6-X^5-X^3-X+1\).
Write
\[
 P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v),
 \qquad |a|=q,\quad |b|=q+1,\quad \epsilon=\pm1,
\]
where \(q\ge2\) and \(0<u<v\) are integers with \(\gcd(u,v)=1\).
Suppose \(P(z^g)=0\) for an integer \(g\ge1\), and that for some conjugate \(\lambda'\) of \(\lambda\) one has \(z^n=(\lambda')^k\) with integers \(n\ge1,\ k\ne0\).
Allowing a root of unity on either side gives the same condition after taking a further power.
Then \(q=2,\ u=1,\ v=13,\ \epsilon=1\), and \(-\operatorname{sgn}(b/a)z^g\) is a root of \(S\).
Conversely all such roots and all their lifts occur.
In particular changing the exponents in the parent while taking arbitrary rational powers, conjugates and root-of-unity multiples of this sextic unit produces no new coefficient heights.

More generally suppose \(\beta=z^g\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit in any primitive section above and that \(\beta^n\) lies in a number field \(K\) for some \(n\ge1\).
Then \([K(\beta):K]\le2\).
Thus constructing examples at unbounded \(q\) requires the degree of every field containing a positive power of \(\beta\) to grow at least as \(\tfrac12\exp(cq^{1/3}\log q)\) for an absolute \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(q\), by the [primitive degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement).
This also excludes unrestricted radicals of products of units from any fixed finite list of number fields, by taking their compositum.

<a id="radii"></a>
## Roots of the same modulus in a primitive section

Let \(B=b/a\), \(U=x^u\), and \(V=x^v\) for a root \(x\) of \(P\).
Then \(V=-\epsilon(1+BU)/(U+B)\).
The numerator and denominator cannot vanish together since \(B^2\ne1\), so \(U\) determines \(V\), and together they determine \(x\) by coprimality.
Fix \(|x|=R\ne1\) and write \(r=R^u,\ t=\operatorname{Re} U\).
The squared graph modulus is
\[
 R^{2v}=\frac{1+B^2r^2+2Bt}{r^2+B^2+2Bt}.
\]
As a function of \(t\) it has nonzero derivative with numerator \(2B(1-B^2)(r^2-1)\) wherever the denominator is nonzero.
Cross multiplication at any two admissible \(t\) therefore forces those values to coincide.
The fixed real part and modulus leave only \(U\) and \(\overline U\), so the only possible roots on this circle are \(x\) and \(\overline x\).

For a nontorsion unit \(\beta\), choose an embedding under which \(|\beta|>1\) by Kronecker's theorem and extend it to \(K(\beta)\).
Every conjugate over the image of \(K\) has the same \(n\)-th power and therefore the same modulus.
All of them satisfy the rational equation \(P=0\), proving \([K(\beta):K]\le2\).

<a id="seed"></a>
## Descent to positive half-integral powers of the seed

By conjugating the equation one can take the distinguished \(\lambda\) at the chosen embedding.
Reciprocity up to sign permits replacing \(\beta=z^g\) by \(\beta^{-1}\) to make its modulus exceed one.
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\subset\mathbb R\) in this embedding.
Since \(\beta^n=\lambda^{kg}\), its \(K\)-conjugates lie on the same circle and are among \(\beta,\overline\beta\).
In degree one \(\beta\in K\) is real.
In degree two the product of its two conjugates over \(K\) is \(|\beta|^2\).
In both cases \(|\beta|^2\in K\).

Write \(r_0=\sqrt\lambda\).
Then \(1.2272<r_0<1.2273\), by opposite signs of \(S((12272/10000)^2)\) and \(S((12273/10000)^2)\) and [the uniqueness of the positive exterior root](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/witnesses.md#witness).
There is no positive \(\eta\in K\) with \(\eta^m=\lambda\) for \(m\ge2\).
Indeed such an \(\eta\) generates \(K\) because it contains \(\lambda\) in its generated field, so its six conjugates map bijectively under the \(m\)-th power to the six conjugates of \(\lambda\).
It is an algebraic unit and four of its conjugates lie on the unit circle.
They are nonreal since a conjugate \(\pm1\) would force \(\eta=\pm1\).
Complex conjugation on such a conjugate agrees with inversion, hence the minimal polynomial of \(\eta\) is reciprocal and its sixth conjugate is \(\eta^{-1}\).
Its nonzero integral norm at one would satisfy
\[
 0<|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-\eta)|
 \le16(\eta+\eta^{-1}-2)
 \le16(r_0+r_0^{-1}-2)<1,
\]
a contradiction.
Writing \(|\beta|=\lambda^{A/B_1}\) in lowest terms with \(A,B_1>0\), the membership \(\lambda^{2A/B_1}\in K\) and Bézout's identity imply \(\lambda^{\gcd(2,B_1)/B_1}\in K\).
The preceding indivisibility forces \(B_1\mid2\), hence \(|\beta|=r_0^j\) for some integer \(j\ge1\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## Uniform bounds for the remaining exponents

Put \(T=|\beta|^u=r_0^{ju}>1\) and \(p=q+1\).
The [radial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#radius) gives \(q<2T/(T-1)^2\) and \(T<4\).
If \(u\ge2\), then \(T\ge\lambda>1.506\), and monotonicity of \(2T/(T-1)^2\) gives \(q<12\).
If \(u=1\) and \(q\ge5\), the [short-exponent-one sign theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#statement) gives a unique exterior root up to the sign normalization, of modulus less than \(p/q\le6/5\), contradicting \(|\beta|\ge r_0\).
It remains to treat \(q\le11\).
The inequalities \(T<4\) and \(r_0^7>4\) give \(u\le ju\le6\).

For every integer \(m\ge1\), the bound \(|q r_0^m-(q+1)|>1/100\) follows from the rational bracket for \(r_0\).
At \(m=1\), the nearest indices on opposite sides of \(q(r_0-1)=1\) are \(q=4,5\).
At \(m=2\) and \(m=3\), they are \(q=1,2\), and at \(m\ge4\) one has \(r_0^m>2.25\).
These tests use all integers \(q\ge1\).
The graph formula and the reverse triangle inequality yield
\[
 |\beta|^v\le\frac{pT+q}{|qT-p|}<5900<(6/5)^{50}.
\]
Since \(|\beta|\ge r_0>6/5\), every remaining solution has \(v<50\).
The [exact factorization search](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/witnesses.md#search) for \(2\le q\le12,\ u\le6,\ v\le70\) and all coefficient signs therefore covers every remaining candidate, independently of the Salem catalogue.
Its only nontorsion unit factors are \(S(X)\) and \(S(-X)\) at the parameters stated above.
Conversely each of their roots is a signed conjugate of \(\lambda\), so every lift at every common dilation satisfies the multiplicative dependence condition after clearing its sign.
