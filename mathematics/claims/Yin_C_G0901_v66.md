# A simultaneous real-embedding bound for several independent small units

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finite bounds with the actual heights and the joint real fraction

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) be multiplicatively independent algebraic units, \(s\ge1\), and let \(\delta\) be the fraction of embeddings of their compositum at which all \(x_i\) are real.
The same fraction is obtained in any containing number field.
Write \(h_i=h(x_i)\) for absolute logarithmic height.
For arbitrary integers \(N_i\ge2\), put
\[
     D=\prod_i N_i,\qquad H=\sum_i(N_i-1)h_i.
\]
Then
\[
 \delta\log^+\!\frac{\delta}{4H}
       \ \le\ (1-\delta)\frac{\log D}{D-1}+2H,\qquad
 \delta\ \le\ 8H+3\,\frac{\log D}{D-1}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Take the left side as zero at \(\delta=0\).
In particular, if \(h_i\le h\to0\) and \(s\) is fixed, then
\[
        \delta\ \ll_s\ h^{\,s/(s+1)}
                          \bigl(\log(1/h)\bigr)^{1/(s+1)} .                \tag{2}
\]
For varying \(s\), one has the explicit alternative bound
\[
                    \delta\le 8\sum_i h_i+
                                  \frac{3s\log2}{2^s-1}.                  \tag{3}
\]
The exponents in (2) concern simultaneous reality of the specified independent units.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## The Vandermonde determinant of a box of distinct units

The one-variable determinant uses powers of one squared unit.
Here take all \(D\) numbers \(z_j=\prod_i x_i^{2j_i}\) with \(0\le j_i<N_i\).
Multiplicative independence makes them distinct, so \(V=\prod_{j<k}(z_j-z_k)\) for any ordering is a nonzero algebraic integer in the compositum.
Its absolute norm is at least one.
At an embedding \(\sigma\), divide \(|\sigma V|\) by
\(\prod_j\max(1,|\sigma z_j|)^{D-1}\).
This is the absolute determinant with its Vandermonde rows normalized by the indicated powers.
The \(D\) entries in every row now have absolute value at most one, and Hadamard's inequality bounds the quotient by \(D^{D/2}\).

At a jointly real embedding all \(\sigma z_j\) are positive.
For two positive numbers \(u,v\),
\[
       \frac{|u-v|}{\max(1,u)\max(1,v)}
          \le 1-\exp(-|\log u-\log v|)
          \le \min(1,|\log u-\log v|).
\]
The first inequality follows by taking \(u\ge v\) and using \(\max(1,u)\max(1,v)\ge u\); interchange \(u,v\) otherwise.
Each normalized difference at this embedding is consequently at most
\(\min(1,A_\sigma)\), where \(A_\sigma=2\sum_i(N_i-1)|\log|\sigma x_i||\).
Their product is exactly the normalized determinant.
The average of \(A_\sigma\) over all embeddings is \(4H\), since for each unit the mean absolute logarithm equals twice its height.
If \(\delta>0\), Jensen's inequality for the concave function \(\log\min(1,t)\) bounds its average over the jointly real embeddings by \(-\log^+(\delta/(4H))\).
None of the \(A_\sigma\) there is zero, since a real unit of modulus one equals \(1\) or \(-1\).

In the average logarithm of the denominator, the cost is \((D-1)\sum_j h(z_j)\).
The height triangle inequality gives
\[
       \sum_j h(z_j)\le \sum_j\sum_i 2j_i h_i=DH .
\]
Combine the two estimates for the normalized determinant with the norm lower bound, and divide by \(D(D-1)/2\).
This gives the first inequality of (1).
If \(\delta\ge8H\), then \(\delta\log2\le\log D/(D-1)+\delta/4\).
Since \((\log2-1/4)^{-1}<3\), this proves the second inequality, which also holds if \(\delta<8H\).
For fixed \(s\) let every \(N_i\) be the ceiling of \((\log(1/h)/h)^{1/(s+1)}\) once \(h\) is small.
Substitution of \(D=N_i^s\) and \(H\le sN_i h\) proves (2).
Taking all \(N_i=2\) gives (3) at every rank.

## A gap test for the original total measure with increasing radial rank

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion unit of degree \(d\) and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), and let \(c\) denote ordinary complex conjugation in its splitting field with group \(G\).
For any conjugate \(a\) of \(w\) the positive real unit \(a\,c(a)\) has height at most \(2m/d\).
The [radial rank formula](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules) shows that \(r(w)\) is the maximum number of such units that are multiplicatively independent.
For any selected independent \(s\)-tuple let \(p\) be the fraction of \(g\in G\) taking all its entries to real numbers.
This fraction equals the \(\delta\) of (1), and (3) gives
\[
                p\le \frac{16s\,m}{d}+\frac{3s\log2}{2^s-1}.             \tag{4}
\]
The centralizer of \(c\) contributes to this fraction, since commuting with \(c\) preserves reality.
The actual fraction can also count automorphisms outside the centralizer.

For example, set \(s=2\lceil\log_2 d\rceil\).
On any growing-degree family with \(r(w)\ge s\), if at each input one can select such a tuple with \(p\ge\eta(\log d)/d\) for a fixed \(\eta>0\), (4) gives a positive lower bound on \(m\) for all sufficiently large degrees.
Indeed \(2^s\ge d^2\) and \(s\le 2\log_2 d+2\), so multiplying (4) by \(d/\log d\) bounds its right side by a constant times \(m+1/d\).
Bounded degrees have a positive gap by monic coefficient finiteness.
Hence every positive shrinking-measure sequence in this rank range satisfies \(p=o((\log d)/d)\) uniformly over all independent tuples of that size.
In particular it has \([G:C_G(c)]\,(\log d)/d\to\infty\).
For fixed \(s\), (2) instead bounds the joint real fraction in terms of \(2m/d\).
The results require the indicated independent units and impose no lower bound on their joint real fraction for an unrestricted input.
