# Two separated exponent progressions on the neutral conjugates of a Salem number

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single irreducible polynomial determines the poles

Fix an integer \(T\ge1\), a width bound \(C>0\) and a density \(0<\eta\le1\).
Let \(\alpha\) run along a sequence of Salem numbers with \(m=\log\alpha\to0\), degree \(d\), root field \(K\) and trace field \(F\).
Let \(a,b\) be positive integers with \(b/a\to\infty\).
Take any nonzero complex polynomial supported on a translate of a subset of
\[
          \{0,a,\ldots,Ta\}\ \cup\ \{b,b+a,\ldots,b+Ta\}.
\]
Shift its true minimum exponent to zero, normalize the coefficients to Euclidean norm one and suppose its actual width satisfies \(Wm/d\le C\).
There is \(\epsilon_0>0\), depending only on \(T,C,\eta\), such that if at least \(\eta(d-2)\) neutral conjugates have value of modulus at most \(\epsilon_0\), the following conclusions hold eventually.

There is a choice from a finite list of a monic polynomial \(J\) of positive degree, an integer \(s\) with \(|s|\le T\), a positive integer \(\ell\le T\) and a sign \(\epsilon\), all depending as a list only on \(T,C,\eta\), such that
\[
   \alpha^b=R(\alpha^a),\qquad
   R(Z)=\epsilon Z^s\left(\frac{J^\#(Z)}{J(Z)}\right)^\ell,\qquad
   J^\#(Z)=Z^{\deg J}J(1/Z).                                          \tag{1}
\]
The coefficients of \(J\) generate a totally real field \(E\subseteq F\), \(\ell\deg J\le T\), and \(J\) is irreducible over \(K\).
Every conjugate polynomial of \(J\) has all its roots off the unit circle.
There is a real root \(\lambda>1\) of \(J\) for which
\[
 |\alpha^a-\lambda|\ll_{T,C,\eta} e^{-bm/\ell},\qquad
 am-\log\lambda\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 bm/d-\ell(\deg J)h(\lambda)\longrightarrow0,                          \tag{2}
\]
where \(h\) denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.
For each fixed positive integer \(k\), eventually \(J(Z^k)\) is irreducible over \(K\) at every index with \(k\mid a\).
Conversely a relation (1) with its given coefficients in \(E\subseteq K\) holds at every conjugate above the distinguished embedding of \(E\), including at least \(d/[E:\mathbb Q]-2\) neutral conjugates.
The statement permits both the polynomial and the pole to vary over their finite lists.
It concerns necessary conditions on these sparse tests of hypothetical shrinking inputs.

<a id="graphs"></a>
## From two progressions to a rational function over the trace field

The degrees \(d\) tend to infinity by finiteness at bounded degree and bounded total measure, and every positive power of a Salem number retains degree \(d\) by uniqueness of its exterior conjugate.
For the actual term counts from two to \(2T+2\), choose a common positive threshold from the [critical exact relation](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#statement) with density \(\eta/2\), also taking it smaller than one to exclude a monomial.
On neutral conjugates the evaluation row norm is at least one.
After undoing the common monomial shift, the resulting exact relation has the form
\[
             A(X)+Y D(X)=0,\qquad X=\alpha^a,\quad Y=\alpha^b,
\]
with polynomials of degrees at most \(T\) in a finite list over bounded-degree subfields of \(K\).
The blocks are disjoint eventually.
Both polynomials are nonzero, and \(D(X)\ne0\), for \(X\) has unbounded degree while the possible polynomials form a finite list.
After cancellation, \(Y=R(X)\) with \(R=-A/D\) in a finite list of reduced rational functions.

Take \(E_0\) to be the field generated by the coefficients in the presentation of \(R\) with monic denominator.
It lies in the distinguished real embedding of \(K\).
Above its distinguished embedding at least \(d/[E_0:\mathbb Q]-2\) distinct neutral values of \(X\) have \(|R(X)|=1\).
Real coefficients give \(R(Z)R(1/Z)=1\) identically since the cleared polynomial has degree at most \(2T\).
The involution \(\iota\) of \(K/F\) sends \(X,Y\) to \(1/X,1/Y\).
It follows that \(R^\iota(1/X)=R(1/X)\).
Clearing denominators gives an equation of bounded degree at \(1/X\) with coefficients in a fixed finite set over all indices, so it is an identity eventually.
Uniqueness of the reduced presentation gives \(E_0\subseteq F\).

Separate the power of \(Z\) in \(R\) at zero.
The inverse identity pairs each nonzero finite pole with its inverse zero with the same multiplicity.
Consequently \(R(Z)=\epsilon Z^s H^\#(Z)/H(Z)\), where \(H\) is the monic polynomial of its nonzero finite poles, \(H(0)\ne0\), \(\deg H\le T\), and \(|s|\le T\).
The residual constant squares to one.
Coprimality gives \(\gcd(H,H^\#)=1\).
All coefficients are in the totally real field \(E_0\), and coprimality persists at every conjugate embedding.
Any root on the unit circle at such an embedding would have its inverse as its complex conjugate root, violating coprimality.
If \(H\) is constant, the equation on the positive real axis forces \(b=sa\), which is eventually impossible.

At every real \(x>1\) close enough to one, \(|\log|R(x)||\le C_R\log x\), by differentiability and \(|R(1)|=1\).
For sufficiently large \(x\) away from the poles, polynomial growth also gives \(\log|R(x)|\le C'_R\log x\).
Since \(\log Y/\log X=b/a\to\infty\), the values \(X\) stay in a fixed compact interval inside \((1,\infty)\).
Thus \(am\) is bounded, \(bm\to\infty\) and \(X\) approaches the finite set of real poles \(\lambda>1\).
At a pole of order \(\ell\), boundedness of \((x-\lambda)^\ell R(x)\) gives the first bound in (2).
These estimates are uniform over the finite list after choosing a nearest pole.
It follows that \(am-\log\lambda\to0\).

<a id="height"></a>
## Contribution of all poles to the height

Pass to any subsequence on which \(R\) and the selected pole are fixed.
Write \(H=\prod_{j=1}^q(Z-\lambda_j)\), counting multiplicity.
We show
\[
                       h(Y)\ \longrightarrow\ \sum_{j=1}^q h(\lambda_j).       \tag{3}
\]
The signed monomial in \(R\) changes the height of the quotient by at most \(|s|h(X)=o(1)\).
For \(H^\#/H\) the product formula gives the sum, with the usual normalized local weights, of \(\log\max(|H^\#(X)|_w,|H(X)|_w)\).
At each archimedean embedding of \(E_0\), the conjugates of \(X\) above it form a complete relative orbit and stay in a compact disk because \(am\) is bounded.
Their angular limit is normalized circle measure by [relative small-point equidistribution](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution) applied to \(z\mapsto z^2\).
Here \(h(X)\to0\), and the increasing degrees allow passage to pairwise distinct points on a further subsequence of every subsequence.
The local test function is continuous on this disk by coprimality.
On the circle the two moduli coincide because the coefficients at that embedding are real.
The linear-factor circle mean therefore gives the sum of \(\log^+\) of the moduli of the local roots of \(H\).

At a finite place \(v\) of \(E_0\) put \(G_v=\max_j |[Z^j]H|_v\ge1\), also the coefficient maximum for \(H^\#\).
Since \(X\) is a unit, the two evaluations are at most \(G_v\).
At all but finitely many places \(G_v=1\) and the resultant is a unit, so the maximum is one.
At a remaining place divide both polynomials by a coefficient of absolute value \(G_v\) to obtain integral polynomials \(P,Q\) of coefficient maximum one.
Bézout's identity over the local field bounds \(\max(|P(X)|_w,|Q(X)|_w)\) below by a positive constant independent of the algebraic unit \(X\) and of the extension place \(w\).
Indeed the two Bézout polynomial coefficients have a uniform bound on the unit disk.

For a uniformizer \(\pi\), the reduction of \(P\) is nonzero.
After a power of \(Z\) is removed, this reduction divides \(Z^N-1\) over the finite residue field for some fixed \(N\), allowing a power of the characteristic for repeated roots.
Lifting the divisibility and using \(|X|_w=1\) gives
\[
               |X^N-1|_w\le\max(|\pi|_v,|P(X)|_w).
\]
Thus \(-\log\max(|P(X)|_w,|Q(X)|_w)\) is at most a fixed multiple of \(-\log|X^N-1|_w\), using the Bézout lower bound when the former exceeds \(-\log|\pi|_v\).
By the [finite-place cyclic norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) the normalized sum of this latter cost tends to zero since \(h(X^N)\to0\).
The finite-place contribution therefore tends to the weighted sum of \(\log G_v\).
Multiplicativity of the coefficient maximum at a nonarchimedean place gives \(G_v=\prod_j\max(1,|\lambda_j|_v)\) upon extending the place to a splitting field.
Averaging the archimedean and finite formulas over that field proves (3).

<a id="saturation"></a>
## The distinguished pole exhausts the height

Let \(J\) be the monic minimal polynomial of the selected pole \(\lambda\) over \(E_0\), write \(j=\deg J\), and let \(\ell\) be its multiplicity in \(H\).
For any fixed \(k\mid a\) put \(Z=\alpha^{a/k}\), \(c=\lambda^{1/k}>1\) and \(D=K(c)=\mathbb Q(c,Z)\).
Write \(s_k=[D:K]\le kj\).
The first estimate in (2) and factorization of \(Z^k-c^k\) give \(0<|Z-c|\ll e^{-bm/\ell}\) eventually, by the degree of \(Z\).
Apply the product formula to \(Z-c\) in \(D\), keeping the distinguished logarithm with its full weight \(1/(s_k d)\).
At finite places the other costs from above are bounded by the finite part of \(h(c)\) because \(Z\) is a unit.
At the archimedean places over each embedding of the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(c)\), the \(Z\)-values form its full relative orbit.
They have height tending to zero and stay in a bounded disk.
For fixed \(U>0\) replace the remaining logarithms by \(\max(\log|z-\sigma(c)|,-U)\).
This continuous test increases the remaining sum, and removing the one distinguished truncated term changes its normalized sum by \(O_{U,c}(1/d)\).
Relative equidistribution gives the circle mean in the limit.
All conjugates of \(c\) have modulus different from one by the conclusion for \(H\), so for sufficiently large \(U\) the means equal \(\log^+|\sigma(c)|\).
This proves
\[
          \limsup \frac{bm}{\ell s_k d}\ \le\ h(c)=h(\lambda)/k.
\]
In this calculation one may fix \(s_k\) and all finite-list data on a subsequence.

Take \(k=1\).
Equation (3) gives \(bm/d\to\sum_j h(\lambda_j)\), where every term is strictly positive because no pole is zero or on the unit circle at any embedding.
The \(j\) roots of \(J\) already contribute \(\ell j h(\lambda)\), by invariance under conjugation.
The last inequality bounds the entire sum by \(\ell s_1h(\lambda)\le\ell jh(\lambda)\).
Thus \(s_1=j\), all poles are accounted for, and \(H=J^\ell\).
In particular \(J\) is irreducible over \(K\).
The monic \(J\) has coefficients in the trace field, and the map is recovered from it and the sign and integer data, so its coefficient field equals \(E_0\).
Applying the inequality for any fixed \(k\) now gives \(s_k\ge kj\).
This is exactly irreducibility of \(J(Z^k)\) over \(K\), since it has \(c\) as a root and degree \(kj\).
The subsequence argument for the finite list proves (2) and the eventual assertions, with the indices restricted to \(k\mid a\) for the last assertion.
The converse follows by applying embeddings fixing the coefficients to (1).
