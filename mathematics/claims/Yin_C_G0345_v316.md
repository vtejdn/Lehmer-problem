# Imaginary quadratic twists and the exact exterior minimum in prime trace degree

The ambient-unit researcher developed the global and stabilizer character calculations, the quadratic-twist criterion and the distinctions between the three minima.
The coordinating researcher and the ambient-unit researcher independently obtained the exact five-term peak count and its parity classes.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior minima with and without a measure constraint

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2p\), where \(p>23\) is prime.
Assume its nontorsion roots have exactly one real exterior root, \(c\) conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots with \(0<c<(p-1)/4\), and \(N=p-1-2c\) neutral inverse pairs.
Write \(F\subset\mathbb C\) for its splitting field, \(G\) for its Galois group, \(\tau\) for complex conjugation and \(K=\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\) for the degree-\(p\) field of one chosen trace.
An ambient output here is a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit \(y\) with \(y^j\in F\) for some \(j\ge1\).
Write \(b(y)\) for the number of exterior roots of its minimal polynomial and put \(\kappa=(3+\sqrt5)/2\).

1. Among ambient outputs with a neutral conjugate, without any bound on their measure, the exact minimum is
   \[
                 b_*=\begin{cases}2c,&c\ {\rm odd},\\ 2c+1,&c\ {\rm even}.\end{cases}       \tag{1}
   \]
   The same minimum holds under the disjunction that \(y\) has a neutral conjugate or \(M(y)<\kappa\).
   An actual unit in \(F\) attains (1) in both cases.
2. With no restriction on signature or measure, the ambient minimum is one if \(F\) contains a real quadratic field and is \(b_*\) otherwise.
3. If \(M(P)<\kappa\), the minimum subject to \(M(y)\le M(P)\) is \(2c+1\) for even \(c\).
   For odd \(c\), the minimum is \(2c\) exactly when at least one imaginary quadratic subfield \(D\subset F\) supplies a nontorsion relative norm-one unit in \(KD/K\) of measure at most \(M(P)\).
   In all other cases it is \(2c+1\), attained by the input root.
   Such \(D\) exist precisely for odd \(c\); their existence alone places no upper bound on the measure of a suitable unit.
   If the cutoff \(M(P)\) is strictly below the plastic number, [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes every nontorsion algebraic unit satisfying this cutoff reciprocal.

For the [five-term inputs](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement), \(c=\lfloor p/6\rfloor\).
Both parity branches occur for infinitely many primes.
In the odd branch the only possible \(D\) is the discriminant field of the trace polynomial.
The [five-term consequences](#five-term) specify separately what this gives with an input-measure budget.

<a id="power"></a>
## Multiplicity when passing to a power

For \(w=y^j\), each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w)\) into \(\mathbb C\) has exactly \(d=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q(w)]\) extensions to \(\mathbb Q(y)\).
A value is exterior exactly when its \(j\)-th power is exterior, so
\[
                                b(y)=d\,b(w).                             \tag{2}
\]
The two fields coincide whenever their positive exterior counts are equal.
In that case each embedding with \(w\) real has real image on the full field, so reality of corresponding conjugates is also preserved.

The [stabilizer theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#stabilizers) bounds the nonreal exterior count of every output under the disjunction in part 1 by at least \(2c\).
For \(b(y)<B=\min(4c,(p+1)/2)\), the unordered inverse pairs of the conjugates of \(w\) form the original transitive \(p\)-label \(G\)-set and \(w\) has exactly \(2c\) nonreal exterior conjugates.
Our hypotheses imply \(2c+1<B\): \(4c>2c+1\), and \(p-1-4c\) is a positive even integer.
At \(b(y)=2c\), (2) therefore forces \(d=1\), \(y\in F\), and \(y\) itself has this natural action on its inverse pairs and has no real exterior conjugate.
The input root has \(2c+1\) exterior conjugates and has a neutral conjugate since \(N>0\).

<a id="characters"></a>
## The global and point-stabilizer sign characters

Let \(H\) be the image of \(G\) on the input inverse pairs and \(V\le\mathbb F_2^p\) the diagonal sign kernel.
The [prime-degree classification](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#classification) gives \(H=A_p\) or \(S_p\), and the [mixed-signature calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#signs) gives \(E\subseteq V\), where \(E\) is the subspace of even weight.
Write \(\epsilon(g)\) for the product of the signs on the original labels, and \(\sigma(g)\) for the sign of the underlying permutation.
Both are homomorphisms on \(G\).

The subgroup \(E\) lies in the commutator subgroup of \(G\).
Indeed a lift of a permutation fixing \(i\) and sending \(j\) to \(k\) conjugates the diagonal vector \(e_i+e_j\) to \(e_i+e_k\); the commutator gives \(e_j+e_k\).
The even vectors of weight two generate \(E\).
If \(V=E\), projection identifies \(G/E\) with \(H\).
If \(V=\mathbb F_2^p\), projection together with \(\epsilon\) identifies \(G/E\) with \(H\times\{1,-1\}\), since the image contains the kernel of projection to \(H\).
The abelianizations of \(A_p,S_p\) are respectively trivial and of order two, the latter via the sign.
Thus \(G^{\rm ab}\) is elementary abelian of rank at most two, and every sign character is generated by \(\epsilon,\sigma\).
On complex conjugation one has
\[
                       \epsilon(\tau)=(-1)^N=1,\qquad
                       \sigma(\tau)=(-1)^c.                         \tag{3}
\]
The product of signs on the two entries of a transposed pair is one since \(\tau^2=1\).
An imaginary quadratic subfield corresponds exactly to a sign character whose value at \(\tau\) is \(-1\).
This proves the parity criterion.

Let \(J\) fix one label \(i\), so \(K=F^J\), and let \(\lambda_0\) be its character on the two input roots over \(i\).
Every sign character of \(J\) has the form
\[
                             \lambda_0^u\,\chi|_J,\qquad
                   u\in\{0,1\},\quad \chi:G\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}.    \tag{4}
\]
To see this, for \(j,k\ne i\) all weight-two vectors \(e_j+e_k\) are conjugate under \(J\), since its unsigned image contains \(A_{p-1}\).
A character takes value \(+1\) on these vectors, using \((e_j+e_k)+(e_k+e_l)=e_j+e_l\) for three distinct indices away from \(i\).
They span \(\{v\in E:v_i=0\}\).
On \(E\), the only remaining choice is the \(i\)-th coordinate character, the restriction of \(\lambda_0\).
After removing that character if necessary, the character factors through \(J/E\).
According to the two cases for \(V\), this quotient is \(H_i\) or \(H_i\times\{1,-1\}\), with the latter factor detected by \(\epsilon\).
The unsigned sign detects every nontrivial character of \(H_i=S_{p-1}\); \(H_i=A_{p-1}\) has none.
This proves (4).

<a id="twists"></a>
## All fields attaining \(2c\)

If \(b(y)=2c\) under the disjunction of part 1, identify its inverse-pair action with the natural labels as above.
Choose a conjugate \(z\) over label \(i\) and denote its stabilizer-of-pair sign by \(\lambda\) on \(J\), so \(jz=z^{\lambda(j)}\).
In particular \(\mathbb Q(z)=F^{\ker\lambda}\) and \(z\) is a relative norm-one unit over \(K\).
For a fixed label \(g i\) of \(\tau\) the sign on the two roots is \(\lambda(g^{-1}\tau g)\).
All \(p-2c\) fixed labels must be negative, since a positive fixed inverse pair consists of real reciprocals and contributes one real exterior root.
The character \(\lambda_0\) has both signs on these labels, from the one real and the \(N>0\) neutral input pairs.
Multiplying by \(\chi|_J\) changes every such sign by the same factor \(\chi(\tau)\).
Hence (4) forces \(u=0\), \(\lambda=\chi|_J\) and \(\chi(\tau)=-1\).
Writing \(D=F^{\ker\chi}\), the intersection \(J\cap\ker\chi\) has fixed field \(KD\).
Thus every such \(z\) generates one of these twists of the original trace field.

Conversely, suppose \(D\subset F\) is imaginary quadratic and put \(L=KD\).
The degree-\(p\) field \(K\) has signature \((p-2c,c)\); its real embeddings correspond to the labels fixed by \(\tau\).
The compositum \(L\) has degree \(2p\) and signature \((0,p)\).
The norm from \(\mathcal O_L^\times\) to \(\mathcal O_K^\times\) contains all squared units of \(K\).
By [Dirichlet's unit theorem in arbitrary signature](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002), its kernel has rank \((p-1)-(p-c-1)=c>0\).
Choose any nontorsion member \(\beta\).
The nonidentity automorphism of \(L/K\) sends \(\beta\) to its inverse.
Above every real embedding of \(K\) this automorphism acts as complex conjugation, so both lifts give neutral values of \(\beta\).
The element \(t=\beta+\beta^{-1}\in K\) cannot be rational: a neutral conjugate of \(\beta\) would give \(t\in[-2,2]\), and then the unit would have all its conjugates on the circle and be torsion.
Since \([K:\mathbb Q]=p\), \(t\) generates \(K\).
As \(\beta\ne\beta^{-1}\), it generates the quadratic extension \(L\) over \(K\), hence also generates \(L\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Above each nonreal embedding of \(K\), the trace \(t\) is nonreal, so neither root is real or neutral.
The two lifts give one exterior and one interior value.
There are \(2c\) such embeddings, giving exactly \(2c\) exterior conjugates.
The relative unit has \(p-2c\) neutral inverse pairs.
Equations (2)–(4) and this construction prove parts 1 and 3.

<a id="unrestricted"></a>
## Removing both the measure and neutral-place conditions

A real quadratic subfield supplies a nontorsion norm-one unit with one exterior conjugate by the unit theorem.
Every nontorsion unit has at least one exterior conjugate.
This proves the unrestricted minimum in this case.

If \(F\) has no real quadratic subfield, its maximal abelian subextension is \(\mathbb Q\) or an imaginary quadratic field, since \(G^{\rm ab}\) has exponent two and the product of two different imaginary quadratic characters is real.
The units of either field are torsion.
No positive power of any ambient output can then belong to an abelian extension: combining such a power with the power in \(F\) would give a nontorsion unit of the maximal abelian subextension of \(F\).
The argument detecting order \(p\) in the [ambient output action](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#output) uses the numerical or neutral-place condition only to exclude an abelian power.
Indeed an order-\(p\) element fixing every inverse pair fixes every conjugate of \(w\), and its normal kernel has abelian quotient by the [prime-cycle normalizer argument](Yin_C_G0345_v309.md#normalizer).
Thus the same detection and [stabilizer bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#stabilizers) apply to every ambient output in this case.
The proof of (1) now gives the unrestricted minimum as claimed.

<a id="five-term"></a>
## The exact five-term signature and the two infinite residue classes

For prime \(p\ge5\), let \(P_p(z)=z^{2p}-z^{p+1}-z^p-z^{p-1}+1\) and \(Q_p(T)=C_p(T)-T-1\), where \(C_p(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(p\theta)\).
The [five-term irreducibility and real-place computation](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) gives \(P_p(z)=z^p Q_p(z+z^{-1})\), precisely one real exterior root and no roots at the endpoints of the trace interval.
Neutral trace roots correspond to zeros in \((0,\pi)\) of
\[
                         D_p(\theta)=\cos(p\theta)-\tfrac12-\cos\theta.
\]
Such zeros require \(\theta\ge\pi/3\).
An interior peak \(\theta_k=2\pi k/p\) of \(\cos(p\theta)\) is positive for \(D_p\) exactly when \(\theta_k>\pi/3\); all valleys of \(\cos(p\theta)\) give \(D_p\le-1/2\).
Each eligible peak has one crossing on its rising half-wave, since \(D_p\) increases strictly there.

It has exactly one crossing on the descending half-wave as well.
At any such crossing with \(\theta\ge\pi/2\), the equation gives \(|\cos(p\theta)|\le1/2\).
If \(\theta<\pi/2\), then the peak exceeds \(\pi/3\) by at least \(\pi/(3p)\), and
\[
 \sin^2(p\theta)\ \ge\ 1-\cos(p\theta)=\tfrac12-\cos\theta
 \ \ge\ (\sqrt3/2)\pi/(3p)>1/p^2.
\]
Thus at every descending crossing \(p\sin(p\theta)>\sin\theta\) and \(D_p'(\theta)<0\).
The endpoint signs ensure existence, and strict downward crossing at every zero ensures uniqueness.

There are no other crossings.
A rising half-wave before its ineligible peak stays below the threshold.
If a descending half-wave from an ineligible peak reaches \(\pi/3\), the separation of that peak from the threshold is at least \(\pi/(3p)\), so \(\cos(p\theta)\le1/2\) throughout its portion at or above the threshold.
This can occur only for \(p\equiv1\bmod6\), hence \(p\ge7\): for \(p=6l-1\) the preceding peak is \(5\pi/(3p)\) below the threshold, beyond one descending half-wave.
The portion in question has \(\theta\le\pi/3+\pi/p<\pi/2\), giving \(D_p<0\).
The first peak at zero gives no further root, and the last valley is at \(\pi\).

The number of eligible peaks is \((p-1)/2-\lfloor p/6\rfloor\).
Each yields two distinct neutral trace roots, hence
\[
                N=p-1-2\lfloor p/6\rfloor,\qquad
                c=\lfloor p/6\rfloor.                                  \tag{5}
\]
Thus for primes \(p>3\), \(c\) is even at \(p\equiv1\bmod4\) and odd at \(p\equiv3\bmod4\).
Both classes are infinite: a product \(A\) of all purported primes \(3\bmod4\) gives a new such prime divisor of \(4A-1\), and a product \(B\) of all purported primes \(1\bmod4\) gives a new such divisor of \((2B)^2+1\), since the order of \(2B\) modulo any prime divisor of the latter is four.

On the five-term family \(Q_p(2)=Q_p(-2)=-1\).
For its traces \(t_i\) and representatives \(x_i\), the element \(\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\) has square \(\prod_i(t_i^2-4)=Q_p(2)Q_p(-2)=1\).
It is therefore rational and is transformed by the total coordinate sign \(\epsilon\), so that character is trivial.
The maximal abelian subextension is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}Q_p})\), interpreted as \(\mathbb Q\) for a square discriminant: the sign action on the Vandermonde product gives exactly this field.

For every sufficiently large prime, [the five-term limit](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit) gives \(M(P_p)<\kappa\), in fact below the plastic number.
The neutral-output minimum is \(2\lfloor p/6\rfloor+1\) at \(p\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(2\lfloor p/6\rfloor\) at \(p\equiv3\bmod4\).
At the former primes the same value holds under the input-measure budget.
The completely unrestricted minimum there is one for nonsquare discriminant and is the neutral-output minimum for square discriminant.
At the latter primes the discriminant field is imaginary, giving the unique candidate \(D\), and the completely unrestricted minimum is the neutral-output minimum.
With the input-measure budget it is still necessary and sufficient for a nontorsion relative norm-one unit of \(KD/K\) to have measure at most \(M(P_p)\) in order to attain \(2\lfloor p/6\rfloor\).
The unit-rank calculation establishes existence without that budget; the budgeted existence in this branch remains open.
