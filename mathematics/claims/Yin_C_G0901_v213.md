# Two exact residue periods on one-denominator trace models

<a id="periods"></a>
## Simultaneous order and the real period constraint

Fix the parameters of the [strengthened trace models](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#statement), with \(p=3^\nu\), \(q=p-1\) sufficiently large, \(\nu\) positive even, \(u=2^h\), \(\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)\), and an odd denominator prime \(\ell\nmid pq\).
Put \(J=q/2+1,\ L=J+1,\ C=\operatorname{lcm}(2^h,q)\).
There are monic irreducible reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][X]\) of unbounded even degree \(d\), with constant one, zero coefficients at offsets \(1,\ldots,J\) and exact pole \(q^dF(p/q)=(2q+1)^2\), such that
\[
  \operatorname{ord}_{2,F}(X)=\operatorname{ord}_{q,F}(X)=A,\qquad
  \operatorname{ord}_{p,F}(X)=N=A+2u,\qquad |A-\theta d|=O_{q,h}(1).
\]
Here order at a modulus is the least positive exponent for which \(F\) divides the corresponding binomial.
Both endpoint words are separable after reduction at two and three respectively.
The models retain the [real pin and bounded period error](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) with \(v=A+u\), and the finite-precision graph congruence on the even side.
They use different even periods from the [original construction with large local degrees](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#binary).
In fact, the degree of one binary factor stays bounded in this construction.
Thus the simultaneous orders and pole can be imposed after dropping the uniform lower bound on all dyadic local degrees.
The original prescribed rows and the exact characteristic-zero graph equation remain separate questions.

<a id="packet"></a>
## Multiplying selected roots by primitive tag roots

Use either ordinary roots of unity over \(\mathbb Q\), or roots of order prime to three in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_3\).
Let \(m,R,y\) be positive odd integers with \(R>1\) and \((m,R)=1\), all prime to the characteristic.
Suppose the roots of a monic reciprocal divisor of \(X^m-1\) with constant one and positive degree \(2b\) together have order \(m\), meaning the least common multiple of their orders is \(m\).
Multiply each of these roots by every primitive \(R\)-th root.
The products are distinct and stable under Galois and inversion, have cardinality \(2b\varphi(R)\) and together have order \(mR\), by coprimality.
Include all their preimages under the \(y\)-th power map.
Their polynomial has degree \(2b\varphi(R)y\) and exact order \(mRy\): this follows by the [free basis for power substitution](Yin_C_G0901_v211.md#criterion).
No such preimage lies in \(\mu_m\), since its \(y\)-th power has a nontrivial \(R\)-component.
Any reciprocal divisor on \(\mu_m\) of even degree \(2z\) and constant one can therefore be appended without a common root, preserving the exact order.
The product divides \(X^{mRy}-1\).
In characteristic zero the factors are integral by Galois invariance and integrality.
At three coprime monic factor lifting preserves their divisibility, reciprocity and order at precision \(3^\nu\). [Prime-to-three lifting](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#boundary)

For a target of half-degree \(D'\), suppose the fine factor can supply all half-degrees from \(c\) to \(e\).
Take \(b=\lfloor(D'-c)/(\varphi(R)y)\rfloor\).
If this \(b\) occurs with core order \(m\) and \(e\ge c+\varphi(R)y\), then the remainder \(z=D'-b\varphi(R)y\) is supplied by the fine factor.

## Two periods which differ by the prescribed power of two

Use the fixed auxiliary prime \(t>\max(p,J+2+\max(h,v_2(q)))\) of the [even word](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical) and the [strengthened degree class](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#degrees) \(D_0\bmod P_0\), with \(t\) prime to \(P_0\).
Fix \(B=35\cdot11^e>1000\theta\) with \(e\ge2\) and set \(k=2u+13tB\).
For odd exponents \(s\) to be specified, write \(Z=3^s\), \(V=(Z-1)/2\).
The periods are
\[
             A=Z(kV-2u),\qquad N=V(kZ-4u).
\]
Their difference is \(2u\) since \(Z-2V=1\).
Choose an even integer \(M_0\) such that \(s\equiv1\bmod M_0\) ensures \(V\equiv1\bmod 13tB\), by taking the order of three at \(2\cdot13tB\).
Thus
\[
            A=t\cdot13\cdot m_2 x,\qquad m_2=BZ,\qquad x=(kV-2u)/(13tB).
\]
All these integers are odd, and \(A\) has 3-adic valuation \(s\), since \(V\equiv1\bmod3\) and \(k-2u=13tB\) is prime to three.
Here \(x/m_2\to k/(26tB^2)<1/B\) for \(q\) sufficiently large since \(tB>2u/13\) by the choice of \(B\).

Choose an odd prime \(f\) larger than \(M_0\) and \(p\), and a prime \(\rho\) dividing \((3^f-1)/2\).
The latter integer is odd, and the order of three modulo \(\rho\) is \(f\), since \(f\) is prime and \(\rho\nmid2\).
Put \(R=\rho^{v_\rho(3^f-1)}\).
Impose \(s\equiv f\bmod f\rho\) as well as \(s\equiv1\bmod M_0\); the moduli are coprime since \(\rho>f>M_0\).
Then \(R\) is the exact \(\rho\)-part of \(V\), by the binomial expansion at the odd prime \(\rho\), since \(\rho\nmid s/f\).

There is a fixed prime \(H>\max(100kR^2,L,t,P_0)\) dividing \(k3^{s_*}-4u\) at a positive odd member \(s_*\) of this class.
Indeed these positive odd numbers grow without bound.
Starting at a sufficiently large member, restrict the exponent to a further progression preserving the exact valuations of that member's value at all odd primes up to the indicated bound other than three, using the orders of three modulo the corresponding prime powers one higher.
Three never divides the value, so the product of its small prime powers stays fixed and some later value has a larger prime factor.
Keep \(s\equiv s_*\) modulo \(\operatorname{ord}_H(3)\) and the preceding moduli, writing the resulting class as \(s_*\bmod M\).
We obtain
\[
                N=H Rm_3 y,\qquad m_3=V/R,\qquad y=(kZ-4u)/H .
\]
Here \(\gcd(m_3,R)=1\), \(\varphi(R)y/m_3\to 2kR\varphi(R)/H<1/50\), and \(3\nmid N\).
All parameters chosen before \(s\) is allowed to grow depend only on \(q,h\).

Take \(s=ab\), with \(a,b\) positive odd coprime integers, \(a\to\infty,\ b/a\to2\), in that same class.
To do so choose a fixed positive odd \(c\equiv2-s_*\bmod M\), take \(a\equiv1\bmod Mc\), and put \(b=2a-c\).
Choose \(D\equiv D_0\bmod P_0\), divisible by \(Ht\), nearest to \(A/(2\theta)\) among these integers.
The class is nonempty and fixed since \(Ht\) is prime to \(P_0\).
Consequently \(d=2D\) satisfies the required real period estimate and can be used in both packet constructions.

## The even-coefficient word

For \(j\ge1\) and \(h'\ge0\), \(X^{35\cdot3^j11^{h'}}-1\) has a monic integral divisor of every degree.
For \(j=1,h'=0\), adjoin seven to the practical core fifteen using the [prime-factor interval argument](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical).
In passing from \(35\cdot3^{j-1}\) to \(35\cdot3^j\), \(j\ge2\), the new degrees are \(\varphi(3^j)\) times \(1,4,6,24\), the four cyclotomic degrees at 35.
The subset sums of \(1,4,6\) range from zero to eleven with largest gap three, and their translates by 24 give overall largest gap thirteen.
The old interval has length \(35\cdot3^{j-1}\ge13\varphi(3^j)\), so the consecutive intervals overlap and reach the full degree.
Finally adjoin \(11^{h'}\) using the same prime-factor interval argument.
On these odd levels the only odd-degree cyclotomic factor is \(X-1\), so every even degree is available with reciprocal polynomial and constant one.

Apply the packet with \(m=m_2,\ R=13,\ y=x\) for target half-degree \(D'=D/t\).
Every even degree through \(m_2-1\) is available for the fine factor, so \(c=0,\ e'=(m_2-1)/2\) suffice.
For the coarse factor reserve \(\Phi_{3^s}\Phi_B\), of degree \(h_0=2Z/3+\varphi(B)\), which carries order \(m_2\).
The group of roots of order dividing \(m_2/33=35\cdot11^{e-1}3^{s-1}\) contains neither type of reserved root, and by the preceding paragraph it supplies all even degrees up to \(m_2/33-1\).
Thus every half-degree between \(h_0/2\) and \((h_0+m_2/33-1)/2\) occurs with order \(m_2\).
The prescribed \(b=\lfloor D/(12tx)\rfloor\) satisfies \(b/m_2\to13/(24\theta)\), strictly between \(1/(3B)\) and \(1/(3B)+1/66\) for the chosen \(B\) and sufficiently large \(q\).
Also \(12x<e'\) eventually by the bound for \(x/m_2\).
This gives an integral word of degree \(d/t\) and order \(A/t\).
Substituting \(X^t\) gives an integral reciprocal word \(U\) of degree \(d\) and order \(A\), with zero offsets through \(t-1\).
All its roots remain distinct modulo two since \(A\) is odd, and reduction preserves the orders of odd roots of unity.
It divides \(X^A-1\) integrally and achieves the full order modulo two, hence also modulo \(q\).
A root from the reserved \(\Phi_B\) multiplied by a primitive thirteenth root has order \(13B\).
We can keep \(x\equiv1\bmod13B\) throughout by taking \(M_0\) large enough initially that \(V\equiv1\bmod(13tB)^2\).
That tagged root is then its own \(x\)-th preimage.
Since \(t\nmid13B\), a further \(t\)-th preimage has order \(13B\) as well.
This gives a binary factor of bounded degree on every such row.

## The word at the odd prime power

On \(\mu_{m_3}\) the nonidentity orbits under cubing and inversion have half-sizes at most \(s=ab\).
The cubing order divides the odd \(s\), so these half-sizes equal the residue degrees.
There is a primitive orbit of residue degree \(s\), since \(m_3=(3^s-1)/(2R)>3^{s/3}-1\) eventually and a proper divisor of \(s\) is at most \(s/3\).
For each \(v=a,b\), the roots of order dividing \((3^v-1)/2\) that survive in \(\mu_{m_3}\) still form a subgroup of size at least \((3^v-1)/(2R)\), since only the fixed \(\rho\)-power was removed.
At most \(v3^{v/3}\) of its elements lie in proper subfields, leaving exponentially many classes of half-size \(v\).
The [two-orbit-size filling argument](Yin_C_G0901_v211.md#criterion), applied to these core orbits before any substitution, therefore supplies every integer half-degree in
\[
                         [2s,\ (m_3-1)/2-O(s)]
\]
with exact order \(m_3\).
Indeed with sizes \(a,b\), upper bound \(s\) and \(b/a\to2\), the total reserved half-size in that argument is \(O(s)\).

Apply the packet with \(m=m_3\), tag \(R\) and power \(y\) for half-degree \(D'=D/H\).
Take \(c=2s\) for the fine factor and use the same interval for the coarse one.
The requested coarse half-degree divided by \(m_3\) tends to \(R/(2\theta\varphi(R))\), strictly between zero and one half, while \(c+\varphi(R)y<m_3/3\) eventually.
Thus a reciprocal word of degree \(d/H\) and order \(N/H\) exists modulo \(3^\nu\).
A last substitution by \(X^H\) gives the full degree \(d\) with order \(N\), zero offsets through \(L\) and squarefree reduction since \(3\nmid N\).

The integral even word gives the [even-side pole congruence](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#combine) on the strengthened endpoint degree class.
On the odd side the prescribed [coefficient at offset \(L\)](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) is zero on this class, as required.
The coefficientwise gluing, exact pole correction and [irreducible approximation with one allowed denominator](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) now give a single \(F\) realizing both words in the required archimedean neighborhood.
They use the two words, the degree class, and the zero prefix through \(J\).
The [bounded real-period calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) uses \(A=O(d)\) and the established rounding error.
The [even-side graph congruence](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#combine) uses the same order and dyadic precision.
The height obstruction for these one-denominator models [still excludes the exact graph equation](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement).
