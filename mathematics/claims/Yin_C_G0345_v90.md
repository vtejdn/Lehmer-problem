# A uniform integral bound for shifts of polynomials with all roots on the circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## Mahler measure after adding one monomial

Let \(A\in\mathbb C[z]\) be a nonconstant polynomial all of whose roots lie on the unit circle.
Put \(L(\theta)=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\), let \(d\nu=d\theta/(2\pi)\), and fix \(b>0\).
For every measurable \(B\) on the circle with \(|B|=b\) almost everywhere, \(\log|A+B|\) is integrable and
\[
 \int\max\{L,\log b\}\,d\nu-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)
 \ \le\ \int\log|A+B|\,d\nu
 \ \le\ \int\max\{L,\log b\}\,d\nu+\log2 .                     \tag{1}
\]
Here the zeta factor denotes the convergent series \(\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-3/2}\).

In particular, if \(A\) is monic, \(j\ge0\) is any integer and \(|v|=1\), its shifted logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
    \tfrac12\|L\|_{L^1(\nu)}-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)
       \le m(A+vz^j)\le \tfrac12\|L\|_{L^1(\nu)}+\log2.       \tag{2}
\]
This applies to either central perturbation in the [critical-degree conductor reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v89.md#statement).
Along any sequence of these inputs the shifted measures tend to infinity if and only if their unshifted boundary logarithms have \(L^1\) norm tending to infinity.

<a id="curvature"></a>
## A level interval between consecutive root angles

On each open arc between consecutive distinct root angles, lift the angular variable to an interval \(I=(a,a+\ell)\).
With only one distinct root use the interval of length \(2\pi\) between two consecutive lifts of its angle.
Each root angle \(\beta\), counted with its multiplicity, contributes \(\log|2\sin((\theta-\beta)/2)|\) to \(L\), and the leading coefficient contributes a constant.
Hence
\[
        -L''(\theta)=\frac14\sum_\beta
                     \frac1{\sin^2((\theta-\beta)/2)}
                       \ge \frac4{\ell^2}\qquad(\theta\in I).
\]
Indeed the nearer endpoint has angular distance at most \(\ell/2\) and its single contribution is at least the inverse squared distance.
The function tends to \(-\infty\) at both ends and has one interior maximum, dividing \(I\) into an increasing and a decreasing branch.

For any real \(u\) and \(t>0\), the intersection of \(\{|L-u|\le t\}\) with each branch is an interval, possibly empty.
If \(\theta_1<\theta_2\) belong to that interval on the increasing branch, then
\[
 L(\theta_2)-L(\theta_1)\ge
       \int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}4(\theta_2-\theta)/\ell^2\,d\theta
       = 2(\theta_2-\theta_1)^2/\ell^2 ,
\]
because \(L'(\theta_2)\ge0\).
The corresponding lower bound on the decrease follows by reversing the interval on the decreasing branch.
The variation within the given range is at most \(2t\), so each branch contributes length at most \(\ell\sqrt t\).
Summing over both branches and all arcs gives the uniform estimate
\[
                   \nu\{|L-u|\le t\}\le \min(1,2\sqrt t).      \tag{3}
\]
In particular every finite level has measure zero.

<a id="integral"></a>
## Bounding the logarithm at a possible cancellation

Write \(Y=|L-\log b|\).
By (3), Tonelli's theorem and the Gaussian integral give, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \int e^{-kY}\,d\nu
     = k\int_0^\infty e^{-kt}\nu\{Y\le t\}\,dt
     \le 2k\int_0^\infty e^{-kt}\sqrt t\,dt
     =\sqrt{\pi/k}.
\]
Expanding the logarithm in a nonnegative series now gives
\[
      \int-\log(1-e^{-Y})\,d\nu
            =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1k\int e^{-kY}d\nu
            \le \sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2).                          \tag{4}
\]
The triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give almost everywhere
\[
  \max\{L,\log b\}+\log(1-e^{-Y})
      \le\log|A+B|\le \max\{L,\log b\}+\log2 .
\]
The logarithmic singularities of \(L\) at the finite root set are integrable, and (4) controls the lower bound at the matching level.
Integration proves (1).
For monic \(A\), the circle mean of \(L\) is zero by Jensen's formula, so \(\int\max\{L,0\}\,d\nu=\|L\|_1/2\).
Taking \(B(e^{i\theta})=v e^{ij\theta}\) and \(b=1\) proves (2).
