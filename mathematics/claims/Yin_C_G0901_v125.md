# Residue signs exclude long indices adjacent to arbitrarily high powers of two

<a id="statement"></a>
## New exclusions on the odd positive short-exponent-one branch

Let \(q\ge3\), \(p=q+1\), \(v>1\) odd, and \(F(X)=X^v(qX-p)+pX-q\).
Use either \((N,c)=(v-1,q)\) or \((N,c)=(v+1,p)\), and put \(a=v_2(N)\) and \(h=N/2^a\).
If \(h=1\) and at least one of \(3\) and \(5\) divides \(c\), no \(F(X^g)\), \(g\ge1\), has a nontorsion algebraic-unit root.
In particular \(q=3,\ v=2^a+1\) is excluded for every \(a\), including exponents for which the odd coefficient prime does not ramify in the corresponding binomial index.
More generally, on any positive unit sequence with \(q\to\infty\) and \(a\ge3\), the necessary cofactor bounds are
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 h\ge(\tfrac12-o(1))q\log q & \text{if }3\mid c\text{ or }5\mid c,\\
 h\ge(1-o(1))q\log q & \text{if }8\mid c .
 \end{array}                                                               \tag{1}
\]
The estimates apply to the primitive root after any common dilation.
They leave arbitrary odd cofactors above the bound and other long indices open.

<a id="odd"></a>
## Half of the residue roots must have maximal allowed two-part

Suppose such a unit exists, and let \(\alpha\) be the unique real exterior root, of degree \(d=2n\).
It satisfies \(1<\alpha<p/q\) and
\[
        d< v/K_q,\qquad K_q=\frac{\log(q(q+1))}{2\log(1+1/q)} .
\]
These facts come from the [positive pole and trace estimates](Yin_C_G0901_v110.md#trace).
By the [short-one classification through degree 44](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/short_one.md#classification), \(n\ge23\) for \(q\ge3\).
Take the square root \(\gamma=(p-q\alpha)\alpha^{(v-1)/2}\) of \(1-q(q+1)(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2)\) in the trace field.
It is the actual real square root specified by the graph, and at each prime \(\ell\mid c\),
\[
                         \gamma\equiv\alpha^{N/2}\pmod{\ell^{v_\ell(c)}} .
\]
For \(c=q\) the first factor is one modulo \(q\); for \(c=p\) that factor is \(\alpha\) modulo \(p\).

For odd \(\ell\mid c\), write \(d_-(\ell)\) for the number of conjugate roots of the unit minimum whose residue has \(\alpha^{N/2}=-1\) at that prime.
Each trace embedding contributes two root embeddings of the same sign.
The [weighted sign budget](Yin_C_G0901_v124.md#statement) yields
\[
     \left|1-\frac{2d_-(\ell)}d\right|
      \le \left(\frac{\log(q(q+1))+2\log(36n)+2\log\alpha}
                         {n\,v_\ell(c)\log\ell}\right)^{1/2}.             \tag{2}
\]
In particular the negative class is nonempty for every such \(\ell\): \(q(q+1)<4n\), \(\alpha<4/3\) at \(q\ge3\), and \(\log(4n)+2\log(36n)+2\log(4/3)<n\log3\) for \(n\ge23\) by evaluation at 23 and differentiation.
The error in (2) tends to zero uniformly as \(q\to\infty\).

Each residue in the negative class has order dividing \(N\) with two-part exactly \(2^a\).
Its residue field degree is therefore divisible by \(f=\operatorname{ord}_{2^a}(\ell)\) (use \(f=1\) at \(a=1\)).
The class is a union of complete local conjugacy orbits, each of size its ramification index times residue degree.
Thus \(d_-(\ell)\) is a positive multiple of \(f\).
There is a simultaneous strengthening when \(a\ge2\) and \(s=v_\ell(N)>r=v_\ell(c)\): since the residue is then not an endpoint \(1,-1\), the [ordinary residue disk polygons](Yin_C_G0901_v123.md#polygon) show that the ramification index of each local factor is divisible by \(\ell^{s-r+1}\).
Hence \(f\ell^{s-r+1}\) divides \(d_-(\ell)\) under those extra hypotheses.
These divisibilities concern the negative portion, for which (2) compares its size to \(d/2\).

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## The negative dyadic class fixes a ramification denominator

If \(8\mid c\), use the dyadic sign at valuation \(v_2(c)-1\) for \(\gamma\).
The same estimate (2) holds with denominator \(n(v_2(c)-2)\log2\). [Dyadic sign count](Yin_C_G0901_v124.md#finite)
It again tends uniformly to zero as \(q\to\infty\).
When a conjugate is in the negative class the congruence above gives \(v_2(\alpha^{N/2}+1)\ge2\), normalized by \(v_2(2)=1\); consequently \(v_2(\alpha^{N/2}-1)=1\).
For \(z=\alpha^h\) this says \(v_2(z^{2^{a-1}}-1)=1\).
Reduction in characteristic two gives \(z\equiv1\) in the residue field.
In doubling an exponent, a strictly positive valuation below one doubles, by factoring a difference of squares; a valuation at least one at a previous step would give a valuation at least two after the next doubling.
It follows that \(v_2(z-1)=2^{1-a}\).
Every completion in the negative class thus has ramification index divisible by \(2^{a-1}\), so that integer divides \(d_-(2)\).

<a id="cofactor"></a>
## Pure powers and the cofactor estimates

For \(\ell=3,5\) one has \(f=2^{a-2}\) when \(a\ge3\).
For either base the valuation of \(\ell^2-1\) at two is three, and each subsequent squaring increases it by one, proving the order formula (including the direct modulo-eight case).
For \(h=1\), \(a\ge4\), (2) gives \(d\ge f\), whereas the degree upper bound gives
\[
       d<\frac{2\log(4/3)}{\log12}(2^a+1)<\frac4{17}(2^a+1)\le2^{a-2}.
\]
The strict constant comparison is equivalent to \((4/3)^{17}<144\).
For \(a\le3\), \(v\le9\) and the same elementary degree bound already contradicts \(d\ge46\).
This proves the asserted complete pure-power exclusions.

Finally, under the first condition in (1), \(f\le d_-(\ell)\le(1+o(1))d/2\), while under the second condition \(2^{a-1}\le d_-(2)\le(1+o(1))d/2\) once \(q\) is large.
Use \(d<v/K_q\), \(v=N\pm1\), and \(K_q\sim q\log q\).
Division by \(2^a\) gives the two lower bounds in (1).
