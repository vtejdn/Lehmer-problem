# A sharp cubic-half minimum at rational coefficient trace and a smaller degree-eighteen square endpoint

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact minimum over all totally real bases in a cubic subclass

Consider the irreducible half polynomials in the [square case of the relative norm theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#statement).
Thus \(K/E\) is quadratic over a totally real field of degree \(u\ge2\), with involution \(\tau\), exactly one split real place and complex embeddings over every other place.
At the chosen split embedding the monic integral \(F\) has one simple real exterior root of modulus \(R>1\) and all other roots strictly inside the unit circle.
Its constant is a rational sign, \(F^\tau\) is its monic reciprocal, and all nonsplit copies have only neutral roots.
In degree three, replace \(F(X)\) by \(-F(-X)\) when its constant is \(+1\), and write
\[
             F(X)=X^3-aX^2+a'X-1,\qquad a'=\tau a,\quad
             t=a+a',\quad p=aa'.
\]

If \(t\) is rational, the smallest possible \(R^2\), over **all** such \(E,K\), is attained at
\[
           E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),\qquad t=-1,\qquad p=1-\sqrt2,\qquad
           a=\frac{-1+\sqrt{4\sqrt2-3}}2 .
\]
Up to the choice of conjugate embedding and the original rational sign, this is the only irreducible cubic half with \(t\) rational and \(R^2\le\varphi^2\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
The rational minimum of its positive marked exterior root is
\[
       (X^6+X^5-X^3+X+1)^2-2X^4(X+1)^4 .
\]
It has degree twelve, and \(1.564<R<1.565\).

There is an irreducible cubic half with irrational \(t\) and \(1.537<R<1.540\).
Its positive marked root has degree eighteen and total multiplicative measure \(R^2<2.372\), below the preceding minimum.
Both examples are compressed reciprocal Perron units, and their measures have second iterate \(M(R^2)=R^4\).
The degree-twelve classification imposes no degree bound on the base and no bound on its discriminant.
The degree-eighteen example gives an upper bound in the larger class; the unrestricted infimum for square endpoints remains open.

<a id="circle"></a>
## Removing the reciprocal part at the marked place

For any half degree \(b\ge3\) the polynomial \(F-F^\tau\) has degree exactly \(b-1\), a simple zero at zero and precisely \(b-2\) other zeros, all simple and on the marked unit circle.
Dividing \((F-F^\tau)/X\) by its leading coefficient gives a monic polynomial over \(E\).
Indeed \(F/F^\tau\) maps the circle to itself since the coefficients at the marked embedding are real and \(F^\tau=\pm F^*\).
Its winding number equals the difference between \(b-1\) marked interior roots of \(F\) and the one marked interior root of \(F^\tau\), by the argument principle.
A circle map of positive degree \(b-2\) takes the value one at at least \(b-2\) distinct points: lifting its argument over one full turn gives a total increase of \(2\pi(b-2)\) and hence that many different crossings modulo the starting point.
The monic leading terms and equal constants cancel in \(F-F^\tau\), so the difference has at most \(b-2\) nonzero zeros with multiplicity.
It is nonzero because the marked root counts differ.
The bound is therefore saturated, including simplicity, and \(\tau\) negates every coefficient of the difference, proving descent after division.
The location asserted here concerns only the marked embedding.

<a id="cubic"></a>
## The cubic discriminant at a nonsplit place

At any nonsplit place the two coefficients are \(\gamma,\bar\gamma\).
The corresponding cubic is self-inversive and has product of roots one.
Its discriminant equals the corresponding conjugate of
\[
                         \Delta=p^2+(18+12t)p-4t^3-27 .                   \tag{1}
\]
It has three distinct circle roots exactly when \(\Delta<0\) at this place.
Indeed if the three roots are distinct \(e^{i\theta_j}\), their squared Vandermonde product is \((-4)^3\prod_{j<k}\sin^2((\theta_j-\theta_k)/2)<0\), since their product is one.
In the alternative with an off-circle pair the roots have the form \(\lambda e^{iv},\lambda^{-1}e^{iv},e^{-2iv}\) with \(\lambda>1\).
The discriminant is then \((\lambda-\lambda^{-1})^2(2\cos(3v)-\lambda-\lambda^{-1})^2>0\).
A repeated root gives zero.

Irreducibility ensures separability in every copy, while \(a=a'\) would give \(F(1)=0\).
Thus \(p<t^2/4\) at the mark and \(p_i>t_i^2/4\) at every other embedding, writing subscripts for embedding values.
The sum of the roots of \(F^\tau\) is the inverse-root sum of \(F\).
Pairing complex conjugates at the mark and using the product-one constraint yields
\[
                         |a-a'|<2(R-R^{-1}) .                             \tag{2}
\]
In detail the exterior contribution has absolute value \(R-R^{-1}\); writing the two interior radii as \(e^{-s_1},e^{-s_2}\), their contribution has absolute value at most \(2\sinh s_1+2\sinh s_2<2\sinh(s_1+s_2)=R-R^{-1}\).
This estimate also applies to a negative exterior root after the sign normalization.
In particular if \(R\le\varphi\), then \(p>t^2/4-1\) at the mark.

<a id="rational"></a>
## Restriction to small cyclotomic conductors

Suppose \(t\) is rational, hence an integer.
Viewed as a quadratic in \(p_i\), (1) must take a strictly negative value and has discriminant \(16(t+3)^3\).
Thus \(t>-3\); also \(t<6\) since \(t=2\operatorname{Re}\gamma\) and three distinct circle roots cannot all have real part one.
The integer \(-2\) gives \(F(-1)=0\), leaving \(t=-1,0,1,2,3,4,5\).
At these values \(F(-1)=-2-t<0\), which excludes a negative exterior root: in that case the two interior factors at \(-1\) have positive product and the exterior factor is positive.
Hence the marked exterior root is \(R>1\) and \(a-a'>0\) by \(F(1)<0\).
Since \(p<t^2/4\) only at the mark, \(p\) generates \(E\).
At each nonsplit place (1) implies
\[
                 t^2/4<p_i<-9-6t+2(t+3)^{3/2}.                          \tag{3}
\]

An algebraic integer \(s\) with all conjugates in \([-2,2]\) is \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) for a root of unity \(\zeta\).
Indeed a root of \(X^2-sX+1\) is an algebraic integer all of whose conjugates have modulus one; all its powers have bounded degree and bounded integral coefficients of their minima, so two powers coincide.
For \(\zeta\) of exact order \(N\), the largest trace conjugate is \(2\cos(2\pi/N)\) for \(N\ge2\).

If \(0\le t\le5\), put \(h=\lfloor t^2/4\rfloor\) and \(s=p-h\).
At \(R\le\varphi\), (2) gives \(-1<s\le1/4\) at the mark.
At the other places (3) gives \(0<s_i<7/5\): the six respective upper endpoints after subtracting \(h\) are \(6\sqrt3-9,\ 1,\ 10\sqrt5-22,\ 12\sqrt6-29,\ 14\sqrt7-37,\ 32\sqrt2-45\).
As \(7/5<\sqrt2\), an order \(N\ge8\) is impossible by the largest conjugate.
Among smaller orders only 5 and 7 give irrational traces, and both have a conjugate below \(-1\).
This contradicts \(\deg p=u\ge2\).

At \(t=-1\), the marked cubic equation gives
\[
      a-a'=\frac{R-1}{R+1}\big(2(R+R^{-1}+1)+1\big)
              \le\frac{4\varphi+1}{2\varphi+1}<9/5 .
\]
Both factors of the expression in \(R\) are increasing for \(R>1\).
Thus \(s=p-1\) lies in \((-39/25,-3/4)\) at the mark and in \((-3/4,-4+4\sqrt2)\) elsewhere by (3).
It is again a cyclotomic trace.
As \(-4+4\sqrt2<5/3<2\cos(2\pi/11)\), its order is at most ten; the latter comparison follows from \(\cos y\ge1-y^2/2\) and \(\pi<22/7\).
The nonrational orders are \(5,7,8,9,10\).
At order 5 the sole negative value is \(-\varphi<-39/25\).
At orders 7 and 9 the most negative values are \(-2\cos(\pi/7),-2\cos(\pi/9)<-\varphi\); the only other negative value among them is \(-2\sin(\pi/14)>-3/4\).
At order 10 the negative value is \(1-\varphi>-3/4\).
Hence the marked value is \(-\sqrt2\) at order 8, giving exactly the claimed \(p\) and the indicated positive choice of \(a-a'\).

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Realization of the minimum and a smaller example outside the rational-trace class

For \(t=-1,\ p=1-\sqrt2\), the other real place has \(p_i=1+\sqrt2\), \(4p_i>1\), and \(\Delta_i=p_i^2+6p_i-23=8\sqrt2-14<0\).
It gives the required complex coefficient embeddings and three distinct circle roots.
At the mark \(F(-1)<0\) and \(F(1)<0\).
These signs and root product one show there is just one exterior root, real and positive: the number of roots above one is odd, the number below minus one is even, and three exterior roots are impossible.
A complex unit pair would force the remaining root to be one.
Substitution in \(F\), bounding \(\sqrt2\) between 1.41421 and 1.41422 and \(\sqrt{4\sqrt2-3}\) between 1.6299 and 1.6301, gives \(F(1.564)<0<F(1.565)\).
Over \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) every Salem trace \(s=l+k\sqrt2\) with \(s>2\), \(|l-k\sqrt2|<2\), satisfies \(s\ge1+\sqrt2\).
Indeed \(k\ge1\); at \(k=1\) one needs \(l\ge1\), and \(k\ge2\) gives \(s>4\sqrt2-2\).
The primitive relative norm-one unit \(\sigma>1\) in \(K=E(\sqrt{4\sqrt2-3})\) is Salem and has this trace field.
Since \(R+R^{-1}<8/5+5/8<1+\sqrt2\), one has \(R<\sigma\).
The [relative factor bound](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#measures) now forces irreducibility.
Multiplying \(F F^\tau\) and then taking the norm from \(E\) gives the displayed degree-twelve minimum and measure \(R^2\).

For the degree-eighteen example take the middle root \(x\) of \(h(T)=T^3-T^2-4T+2\) and put
\[
 E=\mathbb Q(x),\quad t=3+x-x^2,\quad p=3-x,\quad
 \delta=t^2-4p=-1+4x-2x^2,\quad K=E(\sqrt\delta),\quad
 a=(t+\sqrt\delta)/2 .
\]
The cubic \(h\) is irreducible by the rational root test.
Sign changes bracket its three roots respectively in \((-1.814,-1.813)\), \((0.47068,0.47069)\), and \((2.342,2.344)\).
These intervals give \(\delta>0\) only at the middle root, and \(\Delta=x^2-4x-12<0\) at all three places by (1) reduced modulo \(h\).
Thus \(F\) has three distinct neutral roots at the two nonsplit places, and at the marked place it has one real root above one because \(F(1)=-\sqrt\delta<0\) and its discriminant is negative.
The nonreal conjugate pair there has common modulus \(R^{-1/2}<1\).
The middle interval gives \(3.2491<t<3.2492,\ 0.6630<\sqrt\delta<0.6632\), and substitution gives \(F(1.537)<0<F(1.540)\).

Modulo 2 the polynomial \(h\) has a simple root at 1.
At its [lift in \(\mathbb Q_2\)](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001), \(t,p\) reduce to \(1,0\), respectively, so \(Y^2-tY+p\) likewise has a simple root lifting 0.
This chooses an embedding \(K\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q_2\) carrying \(a\) to that lift.
The monic \(F\) reduces there to \(X^3+X+1\), which has no root in \(\mathbb F_2\).
It is therefore irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_2\) and over \(K\).
Its rational norm polynomial, explicitly
\[
 N_{E/\mathbb Q}\!\left[
 X^6-tX^5+(p+t)X^4-(t^2-2p+2)X^3+(p+t)X^2-tX+1
 \right],
\]
is the degree-eighteen minimum of the marked root by the [irreducible norm identity](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#factors).
In both examples the marked exterior root strictly exceeds the moduli of all other conjugates, because the product of the two marked interior moduli is \(R^{-1}\) and both are less than one.
Powers consequently have full degree, and the root counts give \(M(R)=R^2\) and \(M(R^2)=R^4\).
