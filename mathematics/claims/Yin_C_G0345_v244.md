# A trace congruence excluding rational lifts at square-root offsets

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational values of the trace polynomial

Let \(n>1\) be odd and prime to three, let \(0<l<n\) be even with \(\gcd(n,l)=1\), and put
\[
       P_l(X)=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1 .
\]
For any nonempty set \(\mathcal T\) of divisors \(t>1\) of \(n\), set \(B=\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}\Phi_{3t}\), \(d=\deg B\), and \(D=d/2\).
If a monic rational divisor \(S\) of \(P_l\) reduces to \(\bar B\) at two, then \(S=X^DT(X+X^{-1})\), with \(T\) monic integral, and
\[
            T(-2)=T(-1)=T(1)=T(2)=1 .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus \((Y^2-1)(Y^2-4)\) divides \(T(Y)-1\), and \(S\) omits the unique positive real exterior root of \(P_l\).
The [squarefree binary factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) always gives a unique monic reciprocal divisor over \(\mathbb Z_2\) with reduction \(\bar B\).
Write its trace polynomial as \(T_l\), and write \(B=X^D T_B(X+X^{-1})\).
The [first digit formula](#digit) below computes \(T_l(-1)\bmod4\) for every offset.
The residue 3 excludes a rational lift by (1).

For the [two-prime antichain](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#statement), take \(A\) a sufficiently large positive integer, \(b_a=\lfloor(A-a)\log_7 5\rfloor\), \(n=5^A7^{b_0}\), and \(\mathcal T=\{t_a=5^a7^{b_a}:0\le a\le A,\ 2\mid a\}\).
For every even \(a\ge2\) with \(b_a\ge2\) let
\[
                  k_a=3\cdot5^{a-1}7^{b_a-1}+2 .
\]
Exactly one of the two offsets \(k_a,k_a-6\) gives \(T_{n-k}(-1)\equiv3\bmod4\).
At that offset the local divisor cannot be rational.
The pairs are disjoint, so this excludes at least \(\gg A\asymp\log n\) distinct offsets \(k\asymp\sqrt n\) for this specified divisor.
At the even index nearest \(A/2\), one side of the pair in its residue class modulo six contains an interval of length \(\gg n^{1/4}\) such that every offset in that class coprime to \(n\) fails the test.
In particular \(\gg n^{1/4}\) admissible offsets comparable to \(\sqrt n\) have no rational lift of \(\bar B\).
Passing the congruence gives no rationality assertion for the other member.

## The two cyclotomic evaluations at every conductor

Let \(t\mid n\), \(s=3t\), and \(\zeta\) be primitive of order \(s\).
Write \(w=\zeta^l\) and \(c=w+w^{-1}\).
Since \(\zeta^n\) has order three, substitution gives
\[
          P_l(\zeta)/\zeta^n=-2c,\qquad
          P_l(-\zeta)/(-\zeta)^n=2(1-c).
\]
For \(t>1\), \(w\) has odd order \(h=t\) or \(3t\), prime to two and different from one and three, and \(w^3\ne1\).
Both \(1+w^2\) and \(1+w^3\) are units, as is \(1+w\).
Indeed for an odd-order root \(u\ne1\), \(u\) and \(u^2\) are conjugate, so \(1+u=(1-u^2)/(1-u)\) is integral of absolute norm one.
It follows that \(c=w^{-1}(1+w^2)\) and \(1-c=-w^{-1}(1+w^3)/(1+w)\) are units.
At \(t=1\), one has \(c=2\) for \(3\mid l\) and \(c=-1\) otherwise.
This accounts for the extra factor of two at exactly one of the two evaluations.

For any monic rational divisor \(S\) of \(P_l\), let \(r_t=\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar\Phi_{3t})\).
It is integral by monic factorization.
At any prime above two of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), reduction of \(-\zeta\) equals reduction of \(\zeta\).
The squarefree reduction of \(P_l\) implies that exactly one of \(S,P_l/S\) vanishes at that residue whenever \(P_l\) does.
Hence the same primes support \(S(\zeta)\) and \(S(-\zeta)\), and at each supporting prime the valuation equals the corresponding valuation for \(P_l\).
There are no other supporting primes by the unit computations.
The odd conductor is unramified at two, and the residue degrees of the supporting primes sum to \(r_t\), since the separable factors of \(\bar\Phi_{3t}\) give its Frobenius orbits.
Thus if \(t>1\), \(S(\zeta)/S(-\zeta)\) is a unit and
\[
       |\operatorname{Res}(S,\Phi_{3t})|
        =|\operatorname{Res}(S,\Phi_{6t})|=2^{r_t}.
\]
For \(t=1\) the corresponding pair is \((2^{2r_1},2^{r_1})\) for \(3\mid l\), and \((2^{r_1},2^{2r_1})\) otherwise.
Here \(r_1\in\{0,2\}\).
This differs from the [two-prime resultant at a single conductor](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#statement) by comparing opposite arguments in the same cyclotomic field.

In particular, when \(r_1=0\), multiplying these resultants over \(t\mid n\) gives \(2^{\deg S}\) at both signs, using \(\sum r_t=\deg S\).
The corresponding products of the cyclotomic polynomials are \(X^{2n}+X^n+1\) and \(X^{2n}-X^n+1\).
At any root \(x\) of \(S\) they equal \(2x^{n-l}(x^{2l}+1)\) and \(2x^{n-l}\Phi_6(x^l)\), respectively.
Since \(x\) is a unit, both \(x^l+x^{-l}\) and \(1-x^l-x^{-l}\) are algebraic units by taking the product over the roots of the monic \(S\).

## Four exact integral evaluations

For \(S\) with the stated full conductors, uniqueness of the reciprocal monic lift and the identity \(P_l(1)=-1\) give \(S^*=S\), as in the [reciprocal lifting argument](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts).
Every monic reciprocal polynomial of even degree \(2D\) has the indicated trace representation by pairing inverse powers.
These representations commute with reduction and \(\bar T=\bar T_B\), since \(Y\mapsto X+X^{-1}\) embeds the polynomial ring in the Laurent ring over either field.
Here \(D=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\varphi(t)\) is even.
For \(t>1\), \(\Phi_{3t}(1)=\Phi_{3t}(-1)=1\), since their absolute values are \(\Phi_{3t}(1),\Phi_{6t}(1)\), both at indices with at least two distinct prime divisors.
The values at one follow also by induction on divisors from \(m=\prod_{h\mid m,\ h>1}\Phi_h(1)\).
Thus \(T_B(2)=T_B(-2)=1\).
For every integral polynomial \(H\), \(H(2)-H(-2)\equiv4H'(0)\pmod8\).
It follows that \(T_B'(0)\) and \(T'(0)\) are even, hence \(T(2)\equiv T(-2)\pmod8\).

At \(x=-e^v\) for real \(v\) the value \(P_l(x)/x^n=-2\cosh(nv)-4\cosh(lv)+1\) is strictly negative.
Thus \(S(-1)>0\) because \(S\) is monic of even degree and has no negative real zero.
Now \(T(2)=S(1)\) divides \(-1\) and \(T(-2)=S(-1)>0\) divides \(P_l(-1)=5\).
Their congruence modulo eight forces both to equal one.
At primitive roots of orders three and six, \(r_1=0\) gives units, so \(T(-1)\) and \(T(1)\) are rational integers of absolute value one.
Reducing their arguments modulo three against \(2\) and \(-2\), respectively, forces both to equal one.
This proves (1).
There is exactly one positive real exterior root of \(P_l\), and it is simple.
Indeed \(P_l(e^v)e^{-nv}=2\cosh(nv)-4\cosh(lv)+1\) starts negative and tends to infinity, while its derivative changes sign at most once, from negative to positive if it changes at all.
For \(v>0\) this follows from strict increase of \(\sinh(nv)/\sinh(lv)\), since \(u\coth u\) increases for \(u>0\) by \(\sinh(2u)>2u\).
The monic polynomial \(S\) with \(S(1)=1\) cannot have this single simple exterior real root, by its sign at infinity.

## The first binary correction at trace minus one

For odd \(s\) put \(c_s(j)=\sum_{\zeta\ {\rm primitive\ of\ order}\ s}\zeta^j\), the integral Ramanujan sum.
Then
\[
\begin{split}
   T_l(-1)&\equiv T_B(-1)(1+2\epsilon_l)\pmod4,\\
   \epsilon_l&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\sum_{j=0}^{3}
           \sum_{i=0}^{(t-3)/2}c_{3t}(l+3+6i+j)\pmod2 .                \tag{2}
\end{split}
\]
In particular the parity mask is periodic in \(l\) modulo the least common multiple of the selected conductors.
For \(\mathcal T\) supported on five and seven each inner progression can be counted by at most eight congruences.
Indeed \(c_s(v)=\sum_{h\mid(s,v)}h\mu(s/h)\) follows by Möbius inversion on all \(s\)-th roots.
Only squarefree \(s/h\) contribute, and \(h\) is odd.

To prove (2), work in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\) containing all the \(3n\)-th roots of unity.
Let \(C_j(Y)\) be the integral polynomial with \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
For \(\zeta\) of a selected order, \(\tau=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) satisfies \(C_n(\tau)+1=0\), and
\[
             C_n'(\tau)=n\,\frac{\zeta^n-\zeta^{-n}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}
                         \equiv\bar\tau^{-1}\pmod2 .
\]
Here \(n\) is odd, \(\bar\zeta^n\) has order three, and \(\bar\zeta\) has order \(3t>3\).
In particular \(\bar\tau\notin\{0,1\}\).
The parent trace polynomial \(C_n+1-2C_l\) lifts this simple root to a unique \(\tau_l\equiv\tau+2\bar\tau C_l(\bar\tau)\bmod4\), where doubling a residue denotes twice any integral representative.
Distinct inverse pairs give distinct residue traces and account for all the roots of \(T_l\).
Multiplying \((-1-\tau_l)/(-1-\tau)\) over the pairs gives the first line of (2) with
\[
          \epsilon_l=\sum_{\eta}\eta^l g(\eta),\qquad
          g(z)=\frac{z^2+1}{z^2+z+1},
\]
where \(\eta\) runs over all selected primitive roots in characteristic two.
Indeed the summand per inverse pair is \(C_l(\bar\tau)\bar\tau/(1+\bar\tau)\).
In characteristic two \((1+z^3)g(z)=1+z+z^2+z^3\).
For \(z\) of order \(3t\), \(1/(1+z^3)=\sum_{i=0}^{(t-3)/2}z^{3(2i+1)}\), by the geometric sum of the nontrivial \(t\)-th root \(z^3\).
Substitution gives (2).
Multiplication by \(1+z^6\) gives a shorter difference formula,
\[
   \epsilon_l+\epsilon_{l+6}
       =\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\sum_{j\in\{0,1,2,4,5,6\}}c_{3t}(l+j)
             \pmod2.                                                   \tag{3}
\]
The local calculations (2) and (3) also hold at intermediate even exponents \(0<l<n\) sharing a divisor with \(n\), because the reduction and its simple derivative are independent of \(l\).

## Distinguishing disjoint pairs at the square-root scale

For odd \(s\) the parity of \(c_s(v)\) is one precisely when \(v_p(v)=e-1\) at every prime power \(p^e\Vert s\).
This follows from the divisor formula in (2) and multiplicativity in \(s\).
For an interior index of the statement put \(k=k_a\) and \(l=n-k\).
Both \(k,k-6\) are odd, congruent respectively to \(2,-4\) modulo 35, and therefore coprime to \(n\).
They are positive and less than \(n\) for all sufficiently large \(A\).
For \(j=2\), \(n-k+2=n-3\cdot5^{a-1}7^{b_a-1}\) is prime to three and has exact valuations \(a-1,b_a-1\) at five and seven.
This selects \(t_a\) alone among the orders divisible by both primes.
The pure seven-power endpoint has exponent \(b_0>b_a\) since \(a\ge2\).
Any pure five-power endpoint has exponent greater than \(a\) since \(b_a\ge2\).
Thus neither endpoint contributes.
For every other \(j\) in (3), neither five nor seven divides \(n-k+j\).
For large \(A\), every selected \(t\) has a squared prime divisor, since \(5^A/7<t\le5^A\) and only five and seven occur.
Hence no such \(j\) contributes.
This proves \(\epsilon_l+\epsilon_{l+6}=1\).
The baseline \(T_B(-1)\) is odd because none of the selected traces reduces to one, so exactly one residue in (2) is three.
The offsets \(k_a\) are distinct by their prime factorizations after subtracting two, and their residue modulo 35 differs from that of all \(k_{a'}-6\).
There are \(A/2+O(1)\) eligible indices, \(k_a\asymp5^A\) and \(5^{2A}/7<n\le5^{2A}\), proving the asserted count and scale.

Choose an even \(a\) nearest \(A/2\), put \(h_a=5^{a-1}7^{b_a-1}\), and let \(H=\min(5^{a-1},7^{b_a-1})\gg5^{A/2}\).
For any selected \(t_{a'}\) write \(K_{a'}=t_{a'}/\operatorname{rad}(t_{a'})\), where the radical is the product of its distinct prime divisors.
The gcd of \(K_{a'}\) and \(h_a\) is at least \(H\), because both are divisible by \(7^{b_a-1}\) when \(a'\le a\) and by \(5^{a-1}\) when \(a'\ge a\).
A change of the digit from \(k\) to \(k-6\) requires \(K_{a'}\mid k-j\) for some \(a'\) and some \(j\in\{0,1,2,4,5,6\}\), by (3) and the parity condition.
Thus \(k-j-3h_a\) is a multiple of that gcd.
Whenever \(|k-3h_a|<H/2\) and \(H>12\) this requires \(k=3h_a+j\).
On the progression \(k\equiv3h_a+2\bmod6\) the only possibility is \(j=2\).
The change at \(k=k_a\) was proved above, so the local residues are constant on the two sides of that step in this progression and differ by two.
The side with residue three gives an excluded interval in that progression.
It has length \(\gg H\) in the offset coordinate, and every 35 successive terms contain 24 coprime to \(n\) since six is prime to 35.
This gives \(\gg n^{1/4}\) admissible exclusions, all at offsets comparable to \(\sqrt n\).
The coordinating agent supplied the common-divisor argument for this interval.

For example \(A=5\) gives \(n=7\,503\,125,\ \mathcal T=\{2401,1225,625\}\) and the index \(a=2\) gives offsets 107 and 101.
The reference trace values at minus one of the three separate cyclotomic factors are \(7,1,-5\), so the baseline product is \(-35\equiv1\bmod4\).
For each \(t\), the sign is \((-1)^{\#\{0<j<t:\gcd(j,3t)=1\}}\) by counting real traces greater than minus one.
Their counts are \(1372,560,333\).
The magnitude is \(\Phi_t(1)\), since \(\Phi_{3t}(X)=\Phi_t(X^3)/\Phi_t(X)\) for \(3\nmid t\), and \(\Phi_t(\omega)\) at a primitive cube root \(\omega\) has absolute value one for these three \(t\).
For the last assertion use \(\Phi_t(\omega)=\prod_{h\mid t}(1-\omega^h)^{\mu(t/h)}\), in which all factors have modulus \(\sqrt3\).
The entries below count the odd summands over \(i\) in (2), in order \(j=0,1,2,3\).

| \(t\) | \(k=107\) | \(k=101\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2401 | \(0,3,3,0\) | \(0,3,3,0\) |
| 1225 | \(0,11,11,0\) | \(0,11,12,0\) |
| 625 | \(0,3,2,0\) | \(0,3,2,0\) |

To reproduce each entry count \(0\le i<(t-1)/2\) for which \(v_p(n-k+3+6i+j)=e-1\) at every \(p^e\Vert3t\).
Thus offset 107 forces residue three and excludes the rational factor, while offset 101 has residue one.
