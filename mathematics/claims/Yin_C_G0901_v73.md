# Height transfer to subfields and exclusion of bounded argument rank throughout the divisible conjugate group

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed subspace dimension and the total measure of the original unit

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), \(L\) its splitting field, \(\Omega\) its conjugates and \(V\) their rational multiplicative span modulo torsion.
For an arbitrary algebraic subfield \(F\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}\), let \(H\) be the image on \(L\) of the absolute Galois group fixing \(F\).
Let \(r=\dim V^H\).
This is the rank of the units of \(D(w)\cap F\) modulo torsion, with \(D(w)\) the divisible group of all conjugates of \(w\).
For \(r>0\),
\[
 m(w)\ \ge\ (r+1)\inf\{h(z):z\in\mathcal O_F^\times,\ z
                                     \text{ nontorsion}\}.                \tag{1}
\]
The same inequality holds with the smaller set of nontorsion units in \(D(w)\cap F\) in the infimum.
Here a unit of an infinite algebraic field means an algebraic unit lying in it.
No disjointness or normality condition on \(F\) is required.

In particular the exact least positive total measure among \(w\) whose \(D(w)\) contains a nontorsion totally real unit is \(\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\).
The same least measure holds for intersection with the units of \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) or of \(\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\).
If \(m(w)\to0\), the least possible degree over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) of any nontorsion element of \(D(w)\) tends to infinity.
Also the *least* multiplicative rank of the arguments of such an element tends to infinity, even allowing its degree and its own measure to grow arbitrarily.

<a id="products"></a>
## Relative orbit products with a total height budget

For each \(H\)-orbit \(O\subset\Omega\) form \(z_O=\prod_{a\in O}a\).
The absolute Galois group fixing \(F\) fixes \(z_O\), so \(z_O\in F\).
It is an algebraic unit in the ordinary group of conjugates.
Averaging over \(H\) sends a generating vector \([a]\) of \(V\) to the orbit sum divided by its orbit size, counting actual roots even when classes agree.
Thus the classes \([z_O]\) span \(V^H\).
Conversely the class of any element of \(D(w)\cap F\) is fixed by \(H\), so these products prove the rank identity.
They satisfy
\[
           \prod_O z_O=N_{\mathbb Q(w)/\mathbb Q} w=\pm1,\qquad
                  \sum_O h(z_O)\le\sum_O |O| h(w)=m(w).
\]
A collection of nonzero classes spanning an \(r\)-dimensional space and summing to zero has at least \(r+1\) members when \(r>0\).
This proves (1) and its stronger version.

For \(F=T=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) the [totally real height bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) gives \(h(z)\ge\tfrac12\log\phi\), with \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
More generally complex conjugation is central in \(T(i)\), and this Galois field contains \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), as shown in the [real and imaginary part decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v55.md#ambient).
For every algebraic unit \(z\in T(i)\), \(z/c(z)\) is a unit with all conjugate moduli one, hence torsion.
Then \(zc(z)\) is totally positive and its positive square root belongs to \(T\).
It has the same height as \(z\): at every embedding its modulus is the modulus of the corresponding conjugate of \(z\), since conjugation is central, and both are units.
A nontorsion \(z\) therefore satisfies the same height floor.
The unit \(\phi\) belongs to all three fields, has total measure \(\log\phi\), and supplies attainment for each of the three classes.

<a id="relative"></a>
## Bounded relative degrees inside the group

Suppose \(x\in D(w)\) is nontorsion and \(e=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(x):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).
Apply (1) with \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(x)\).
Every nontorsion unit of \(F\) has relative degree at most \(e\); the [rank-one relative height estimate](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) gives for each such unit
\[
            h(z)>\big(c_2(1)e(\log(3e))^{48}\big)^{-1}.
\]
At least two actual nontorsion orbit products occur.
Using them in the height budget gives \(m(w)>2/(c_2(1)e(\log(3e))^{48})\).
This proves the uniform assertion along shrinking sequences.

<a id="arguments"></a>
## Applying the argument-rank bound to every rational constituent

For nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) its nonzero conjugate span \(V_x\subseteq V\) is a rational \(G\)-submodule on which complex conjugation has negative eigenspace dimension equal to the multiplicative rank \(k(x)\) of its arguments, as in the [argument rank formula](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection).
Here \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) acts on the classes even when the elements themselves need a larger field.
More precisely, for any \(K\ge0\), if such an \(x\) has \(k(x)\le K\), the original measure \(m(w)\) has an effective positive lower bound depending only on \(K\).

Choose a rational irreducible constituent \(W\subset V_x\), put \(t=\dim W\) and \(j=\dim W^{-c}\le K\).
If \(j=0\), or \(t\ge8j^2\) with \(j>0\), the [projection and selection argument](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#overlap) already gives \(m(w)\ge c_0=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)\), since it selected the product from the original \(\Omega\) for any such \(W\subset V\).
In the remaining case \(1\le t<8K^2\).
Let \(H_0\) be the kernel of \(G\) acting on \(W\), and take \(F=L^{H_0}\).
Its degree is at most \(3^{t^2}\) by the finite integral-matrix bound used in the [degree argument](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree); an invariant lattice in \(W\) is obtained by intersecting it with the lattice of conjugate classes.
There is at least one fixed direction in \(V\) since \(W\subset V^{H_0}\).
Formula (1) now bounds \(m(w)\) below by twice the least height of a nontorsion unit of degree at most \(3^{64K^4}\).
That height minimum is effective and positive by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness: e.g. enumerate the unit minima of degree at most this bound with absolute height at most one, which already includes \(\phi\) when the degree bound is at least two.
This proves the asserted uniformity over \(D(w)\), beyond the [degree bound for compressed inputs](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#statement).
The question when every available argument rank and relative degree diverge remains open.
