# Exterior roots of factors of a five-term polynomial at arbitrary gaps

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small factors outside the reciprocal cases

Let \(P=A(X)+cX^n+X^D B(X^{-1})\) have five nonzero integer coefficients with \(A=A_0+A_1 X^a,\ B=B_0+B_1 X^b,\ 0<a<n<D-b<D\).
After removing content, put \(H=\max_i|[X^i]P|,\ L=\log(5H)\).
Let \(S\) be a primitive irreducible integral factor of degree \(d\) with positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
For \(m\) below a sufficiently small absolute constant, write \(t=\max_{S(x)=0}\log|x|>0\), and let \(e\) be the number of exterior roots of \(S\).
It is a reciprocal unit factor up to sign.
There are absolute implied constants, with no restriction on the position of \(n\), for which the following holds.

Except for the centered reciprocal input \(D=2n,\ A=B\) and the explicit products described below,
\[
                         t\ll m^2 L^2,\qquad e\,m L^2\gg1.                    \tag{1}
\]
If \(A,B\) have a common root which is not a root of unity, then outside the same exceptions one has the stronger bound
\[
                                    m\gg 1/L.                                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular for any fixed coefficient height and any fixed upper bound on the number of exterior conjugates there is an individual-factor gap outside the stated families.
For Salem factors the bound given by (1) is \(m\gg1/(\log(5H))^2\).
The constants for these gap assertions can be decreased to include measures above the absolute smallness threshold.

In each exceptional product a nontorsion unit factor divides an explicit reciprocal four-term factor up to sign under reversal.
The products are the [middle-power products](Yin_C_G0345_v230.md#statement) in either orientation and the following products in either orientation:
\[
 \begin{split}
              n=b=2a,\quad z=X^a,\qquad
       P=B_0(z+\sigma k)
           \big[\sigma(1-\sigma k z)+X^{D-2a}(z-\sigma k)\big],
       \quad k=A_0/B_0\in\mathbb Q^\times,\quad \sigma=\pm1,\quad k^2\ne1 .
 \end{split}                                                               \tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(H\) the four-term factors in both types can be normalized to bounded coefficient height.
The reciprocal five-term and four-term cases, and arbitrary factors with many exterior conjugates, still allow separate questions about small positive measure.

<a id="splits"></a>
## Lower bounds on both middle gaps

Choose the absolute threshold so that [reciprocity and the radial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer) give a monic unit factor up to sign,
\[
                  dtm\ge1/20,\qquad w t\le L,\qquad w=\max(a,b).
\]
Write \(h_S=m/d\), and \(T_U=U(X)U(X^{-1})\).
Splitting off the last two terms of \(P\) shows \(T_{A+cX^n}-T_B\) vanishes at a root of \(S\), since its inverse is also a root.
This Laurent polynomial has a bounded absolute number of terms and width at most \(2\max(n,b)\).
If nonzero, the [width estimate for a sparse relation](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(\max(n,b)h_S\gg1\).
Reverse \(P\) to obtain \(\max(D-n,a)h_S\gg1\) if the other trace difference is nonzero.

Suppose the first difference is identically zero.
Its positive extreme gives \(n=b,\ A_0c=B_0B_1\).
The interior positive exponents of \(T_{A+cX^n}\) are \(a,n-a\), with nonzero terms before collection.
They must coincide and cancel, giving \(n=2a\) and \(c=-A_0\).
The constant term then yields \(A_1^2=(B_0+B_1)^2\).
Thus \(B_1=-A_0^2/B_0,\ A_1=\sigma(B_0+B_1)\), and direct multiplication gives (3).
The five distinct exponents imply \(D>4a\), so the bracket really has four terms.
On reversal it is multiplied by \(\sigma\); the binomial \(z+\sigma k\) cannot contain a nontorsion unit root because a rational power value of an algebraic unit is a rational unit and hence is \(1\) or \(-1\).
The other trace identity gives the reversal of (3).
In (3) the only nontrivial coefficient ratio in the bracket is \(k=A_0/B_0\), whose reduced height is at most \(H\).
For the middle-power product its expansion up to reversal has constant and first end coefficients \(Cz,-C\) up to signs, giving the same bound on the rational height of its parameter.

We can now assume both trace differences nonzero.
If \(w h_S\) exceeds a sufficiently small absolute constant, then \(w h_S\le20Lm^2\) already gives \(m\gg1/\sqrt L\).
This implies (2), and (1) follows from \(t\le m\) and \(m\le et\).
Otherwise the two estimates from the nonzero differences imply
\[
             \Delta h_S\gg1,\qquad \Delta=\min(n,D-n).
                                                                            \tag{4}
\]

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Comparing the end binomials at the same point

Choose a conjugate \(\alpha\) of modulus \(\exp t\) and put \(\beta=1/\alpha\).
Both orientations of the equation for \(P\) give
\[
 A(\beta)=-c\alpha^{-n}-\alpha^{-D}B(\alpha),\qquad
 B(\beta)=-c\alpha^{-(D-n)}-\alpha^{-D}A(\alpha).                         \tag{5}
\]
Since \(\exp(wt)\le 5H\), both \(|A(\alpha)|,|B(\alpha)|\le 10H^2\).
It follows that \(|A(\beta)|,|B(\beta)|\le 11H^2\exp(-\Delta t)\).
Let \(f=\gcd(a,b)\), \(u=a/f,\ v=b/f,\ \lambda=-A_0/A_1,\ \mu=-B_0/B_1\).
Both rational numbers have reduced numerators and denominators of absolute value at most \(H\).

If \(\lambda^v\ne\mu^u\), their difference has absolute value at least \(H^{-(u+v)}\).
Put \(Z=\beta^f\).
The inequalities just obtained bound each of \(|Z^u-\lambda|,|Z^v-\mu|\) by \(11H^2\exp(-\Delta t)\).
Compare the two powers to \(Z^{uv}\) by the difference-of-powers formula, using \(|Z|<1\).
This bounds \(|\lambda^v-\mu^u|\) above by \((u+v)H^{u+v}\,11H^2\exp(-\Delta t)\).
Thus \(\Delta t\ll wL\).
Combining \(wt\le L\) with (4) gives \(d t^2/m\ll L^2\), and \(dtm\ge1/20\) proves (1).

If \(\lambda^v=\mu^u\), take integers \(i,j\) with \(iu+jv=1\).
The rational \(\gamma=\lambda^i\mu^j\) satisfies \(\gamma^u=\lambda,\ \gamma^v=\mu\).
It is the unique common root of \(A_0+A_1 Z^u,\ B_0+B_1 Z^v\), since \(u,v\) are coprime.
For \(\gamma=\pm1\) we have \(|Z^u-\lambda|\ge1-\exp(-at)\ge1-\exp(-t)\gg t\), using \(0<t\le m<1\).
Comparison in (5) gives \(\Delta t\ll L+\log(1/t)\).
Multiplying by \(t\), with \(t\log(1/t)\le1\), gives \(\Delta t^2\ll L\).
Equation (4) therefore again implies (1).

<a id="common"></a>
## End binomials with a common nontorsion root

It remains to treat \(\gamma=p/s\ne\pm1\) in lowest terms with \(s>0\).
Then \(\max(|p|,s)\ge2\); its \(u\)-th and \(v\)-th powers are at most \(H\), giving \(\max(u,v)\le\log H/\log2\).
Over \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) factor the two end polynomials as
\[
 A_0+A_1 Z^u=C_0 C_A,\qquad B_0+B_1 Z^v=C_0 C_B,\qquad C_0=sZ-p .
\]
Indeed \(A_1\) is an integer multiple of \(s^u\), and division of \((sZ)^u-p^u\) by \(sZ-p\) is a geometric sum; the other polynomial has the same description.
The three polynomials \(C_0,C_A,C_B\) have degree \(O(L)\) and coefficient height at most \(H\).
They are pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb Q\) because the common root just found is unique and each binomial is squarefree.

For any two of these polynomials and \(|Z|\le1\), the larger absolute value is at least \(\delta=\exp(-C L^2)\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
For a nonzero constant polynomial this follows from integrality.
For two positive degrees the Sylvester matrix has dimension \(O(L)\), entries of absolute value at most \(H\), and nonzero integer determinant.
Multiplying by its adjugate gives a polynomial Bezout combination equal to the determinant, with each coefficient a minor of absolute value at most \((C' L H)^{C'L}\).
On the closed disk both multipliers are bounded by \(\exp(C''L^2)\).
The determinant has absolute value at least one, proving the lower bound after enlargement of \(C\).

If \(\Delta t\ll L^2\), (4) and \(dtm\ge1/20\) directly give \(m\gg1/L\).
Otherwise take \(\Delta t>C_1 L^2\) with a fixed sufficiently large \(C_1\).
At \(Z=\beta^f\), (5) gives both \(|C_0 C_A|,|C_0 C_B|<\delta^2/2\).
Since \(\max(|C_A|,|C_B|)\ge\delta\), it follows that \(|C_0|<\delta/2\), hence \(|C_A|,|C_B|\ge\delta\).
Their upper bounds on the disk are \(O(LH)\).
The main terms in (5) have relative errors at most \(10H^2\exp(-\Delta t)/|c|<1/2\), so \(A(\beta),B(\beta)\) do not vanish.
The ratio \(A(\beta)/B(\beta)=C_A/C_B\) has logarithmic absolute value of magnitude \(O(L^2)\), whereas (5) gives that same logarithm as \((D-2n)t+O(1)\).
Thus \(|D-2n|t\ll L^2\).

Outside the centered reciprocal case and the [classified middle-power products](Yin_C_G0345_v230.md#statement), the degree comparison there now gives
\[
       1\ll h_S\big(\log(5H)/t+|D-2n|\big)
         \ll (m/(dt)) L^2\ll m^2 L^2.
\]
This proves (2), and implies (1) by \(t\le m\), \(m\le et\) and \(L>1\).
