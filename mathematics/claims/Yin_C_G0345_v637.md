# An improved energy floor from the successive occupied Taylor coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound for an integral numerator of any width

Let \(P\) be monic and irreducible over \(\mathbb Z\), with \(|P(0)|=1\), no unit-circle roots and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) be indivisible by \(P\) in the Laurent ring.
Put \(t=e^{-m}\), \(\gamma_i=((1+t)t^{i-1}-1)_+\) for \(i=1,2,\ldots\), and use normalized circle measure.
For \(1<p\le2\) and \(q=p/(p-1)\),
\[
             \|A/P\|_p^p\ge
             \left(\sum_{i\ge1}\gamma_i^q\right)^{p-1}.                  \tag{1}
\]
The lower bound in (1) is asymptotic as \(m\downarrow0\) to
\[
       \left(\frac1m\int_0^1\frac{y^q}{1+y}\,dy\right)^{p-1}.
\]
In particular
\[
 \liminf_{m\to0}m\|A/P\|_2^2\ge\log2-\tfrac12
      >\frac1{2\mathrm e},                                             \tag{2}
\]
uniformly over the polynomial and numerator along any such sequence.
The constant improves the [occupied-prefix compression bound](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) for squared energy and the [corresponding moment bound](Yin_C_G0345_v631.md#statement) at each fixed \(1<p\le2\).
The endpoint \(p=1\) has a separate [logarithmic lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v632.md#statement).

<a id="compression"></a>
## One new row in the compression of the inner multiplier

Use the [pole-cleared analytic function](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) \(F=IH\), with \(H\) the monomial normalization of \(A/P\) and \(I(0)=t\).
The norms of \(F\) and \(A/P\) agree on the circle.
Let \(h_i\) denote the successive nonzero Taylor coefficients of \(H\) and \(f_i\) the coefficients of \(F\) at those same exponents.
Each \(h_i\) is an integer of magnitude at least one and the list is infinite.
At every prefix the matrix sending the selected \(h_j\) to the \(f_j\) is lower triangular, with diagonal \(t\), and is a contraction, as an orthogonal compression of multiplication by \(I\) in the monomial basis.
Consequently \(u_i^2=\sum_{j\le i}(|h_j|^2-|f_j|^2)\) defines a nonnegative square with \(u_i\ge0\) and \(u_0=0\).

Put \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\).
The last row at step \(i\) has output \(v_i+t h_i\), where \(v_i\) depends only on the earlier coefficients.
Substitute an arbitrary complex \(z\) at the new position in the contraction inequality, keeping the earlier inputs fixed.
This gives \(|v_i+t z|^2-|z|^2\le u_{i-1}^2\) for every \(z\).
Maximizing the left side over \(z\) yields \(|v_i|\le s u_{i-1}\).
Hence if \(a=|h_i|\), \(u=u_{i-1}\), \(y=|f_i|\), then
\[
       y\ge t a-s u,\qquad y^2+u_i^2=a^2+u^2.                         \tag{3}
\]

<a id="weights"></a>
## A weighted inequality that telescopes over the prefix

For any \(c,e\ge0\) with \(tc\ge se\), (3) implies
\[
            c y-e u_i\ \ge\ (tc-se)a-(sc+te)u .                        \tag{4}
\]
To see this, put \(R=\sqrt{a^2+u^2}\) and \(v=ta-su\).
Then \(v\ge-sR\), \(\sqrt{R^2-v^2}=sa+tu\), and \(y\ge v\).
The function \(x\mapsto cx-e\sqrt{R^2-x^2}\) is nondecreasing on \([-sR,R]\), since its derivative on the interior is at least \(c-es/t\).
Evaluation at \(v\) and \(y\) gives (4).

Let \(N\) be the last index with \((1+t)t^{N-1}>1\).
For any nonincreasing weights \(c_1,\ldots,c_N\ge0\), put \(e_N=0\) and \(e_{i-1}=sc_i+te_i\) going backwards.
Then
\[
     se_i=s^2\sum_{j=i+1}^N t^{j-i-1}c_j
          \le (1+t)(1-t^{N-i})c_i\le tc_i .
\]
The last inequality uses \(t^{N-i}\ge t^{N-1}>1/(1+t)\).
Apply (4) with these weights and sum over \(i\).
The terms involving \(u_i\) telescope since \(e_N=u_0=0\), and every coefficient of \(|h_i|\) on the right is nonnegative.
Using \(|h_i|\ge1\) and summing the geometric series gives
\[
       \sum_{i=1}^N c_i |f_i|
       \ge \sum_{i=1}^N(tc_i-se_i)
       =\sum_{i=1}^N c_i\big((1+t)t^{i-1}-1\big).                     \tag{5}
\]
Thus the integrality cost can be recovered across all successive rows up to this index.

<a id="limit"></a>
## The moment and its leading constant

Take \(c_i=\gamma_i^{q-1}\) in (5).
Hölder's inequality gives \(\|(f_i)_{i=1}^N\|_{\ell^q}\ge(\sum_i\gamma_i^q)^{1/q}\).
The normalized Hausdorff–Young inequality bounds this coefficient norm above by \(\|F\|_p\), by interpolation between the coefficient supremum estimate on \(L^1\) and Parseval on \(L^2\).
This proves (1).

The quantity \(m\sum_i\gamma_i^q\) is a Riemann sum with mesh \(m\), supported up to \(m(i-1)\le\log(1+t)\).
The summands converge uniformly on this bounded interval to \(((2e^{-x}-1)_+)^q\).
Integration and the substitution \(y=2e^{-x}-1\) give \(\int_0^1 y^q/(1+y)\,dy\).
At \(q=2\), polynomial division gives \(\log2-1/2\).
For all \(q\ge2\), multiplying the integral by \(q\) and setting \(v=y^q\) gives \(\int_0^1 v^{1/q}/(1+v^{1/q})\,dv\), increasing with \(q\).
Its value at two is \(2\log2-1>1/\mathrm e\).
This proves the strict asymptotic improvement over the earlier constant \((1/(q\mathrm e))^{p-1}\).
