# Discriminant bounds and neutral transfer from quadratic subfields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two quadratic subfields that exclude vanishing measure

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2h>0\), with no unit-circle root.
For a root \(\alpha\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(t_0=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and \(B=\mathbb Q(t_0)\).
Inversion defines the nonidentity automorphism \(\iota\) of \(K/B\), since \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) are distinct rational conjugates satisfying the same quadratic over \(B\).
On the unit circle let \(u=|P|=\pm X^{-h}P\), and let \(m\) denote logarithmic Mahler measure with normalized Haar measure.

* If \(K\) contains a real quadratic field \(F\) of discriminant \(D>0\) on which \(\iota\) acts nontrivially, then \(e^{m(P)}\ge\min\{1+D/4,(\sqrt D+\sqrt{D+4})/2\}\ge9/4\) and there exists a nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(|A|^2\le(2/\sqrt D)u\) throughout the circle.
* If \(B\) contains an imaginary quadratic field \(F\) of discriminant \(D<0\), then \(m(P)\ge\log(1+|D|/4)\) and there exists a nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(|A|^2\le(4/|D|)u\) throughout the circle.

Both pointwise inequalities imply the [lossless neutral transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v605.md#bound) \(\gamma\le m(P)\), since [automatic coprimality](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#coprimality) applies below the factor four.
Here \(\gamma\) is the infimum of the logarithmic Mahler measures of nontorsion units with a unit-circle conjugate.
Since \(D\ge5\) for real quadratic fields and \(|D|\ge3\) for imaginary quadratic fields, every sequence of these hyperbolic inputs with \(m(P)\to0\) eventually avoids both configurations, even for varying fields.

<a id="real"></a>
## A trace from the real quadratic factor

Let \(\sigma\) denote the nonidentity automorphism of \(F\), and let \(q\in\mathcal O_F[X]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(F\).
It has degree \(h\), and \(P=qq^\sigma\) by the norm identity in the tower \(K/F/\mathbb Q\).
These two factors are distinct by separability of \(P\).
Writing \(c=q(0)\), the monic polynomial \(X^hq(X^{-1})/c\) is irreducible over \(F\) and has root \(\alpha^{-1}\), whose minimal polynomial is \(q^\sigma\) by the action of \(\iota\).
Thus on the circle, since \(q\) has real coefficients in our embedding,
\[
           |q|=\sqrt{|c|u},\qquad |q^\sigma|=\sqrt{u/|c|}.
\]
For each element \(v\) of the trace dual \(\mathcal O_F^\vee=\{v\in F:\operatorname{Tr}(v\mathcal O_F)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}\), the coefficientwise trace \(A=vq+v^\sigma q^\sigma\) has integer coefficients.
If \(v\ne0\), it is nonzero because the two monic factors are distinct and hence linearly independent over \(F\).

The embedded trace dual \(\{(v,v^\sigma):v\in\mathcal O_F^\vee\}\) is the Euclidean dual of the embedded ring of integers, by the trace pairing.
The determinant of the embedding matrix of an integral basis has absolute value \(\sqrt D\) by the discriminant identity, so the dual has covolume \(1/\sqrt D\).
Scaling the two coordinates by \(\sqrt{|c|}\) and \(1/\sqrt{|c|}\) preserves this covolume.
Apply [Minkowski's successive-minima bound](../audits/literature/Hen02/Yin_U001.md#minima) to this scaled lattice and the unit \(\ell^1\) diamond, of area two.
Its first minimum satisfies \(\lambda_1^2\le\lambda_1\lambda_2\le2/\sqrt D\).
Taking a nonzero \(v\) realizing the first minimum and applying the triangle inequality gives \(|A|\le\lambda_1\sqrt u\).
By Jensen's formula, a nonzero integer polynomial has \(m(A)\ge0\).
Integrating the logarithm of the squared inequality yields \(m(P)\ge2m(A)+\log(\sqrt D/2)\).

A stronger measure bound follows from the two integral polynomials \(S=q+q^\sigma\) and \(C=(q-q^\sigma)/\sqrt D\).
Indeed an integral basis \(1,\omega\) of \(F\) may be chosen with \(\omega-\omega^\sigma=\sqrt D\), so every coefficient difference belongs to \(\sqrt D\,\mathbb Z\).
The polynomial \(S\) has leading coefficient two and \(C\ne0\).
The parallelogram identity on the circle gives
\[
           |S|^2+D|C|^2=2\bigl(|q|^2+|q^\sigma|^2\bigr)=2(|c|+|c|^{-1})u.
\]
The [geometric-mean inequality](#imaginary) below and Jensen's bound for the two leading coefficients give \(4+D\le2(|c|+|c|^{-1})e^{m(P)}\).
If \(c=\pm1\), this proves \(e^{m(P)}\ge1+D/4\).
Otherwise \(cc^\sigma=P(0)=1\) gives \(0\ne c-c^{-1}\in\sqrt D\,\mathbb Z\).
Both \(|c|\) and \(|c|^{-1}\) are absolute values of products of roots of \(P\), taken from the factors \(q\) and \(q^\sigma\) respectively, and hence are at most \(e^{m(P)}\).
Putting \(r=\max\{|c|,|c|^{-1}\}\) gives \(r-r^{-1}=|c-c^{-1}|\ge\sqrt D\), so \(e^{m(P)}\ge(\sqrt D+\sqrt{D+4})/2\).
Both alternatives are increasing in \(D\ge5\) and give the stated uniform bound.

## Real and imaginary parts of the trace polynomial

Write \(P(X)=X^h Q(X+X^{-1})\), where \(Q\in\mathbb Z[T]\) is the monic minimal polynomial of \(t_0\) of degree \(h\).
Let \(f\in\mathcal O_F[T]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(t_0\) over \(F\).
Taking the norm in the tower \(B/F/\mathbb Q\) gives \(Q=f\bar f\), with distinct factors by separability.
For every real \(t=X+X^{-1}\) on the circle it follows that \(u=|f(t)|^2\).
Set \(s=f+\bar f\) and \(b=(f-\bar f)/\sqrt D\).
Both belong to \(\mathbb Z[T]\), since an integral basis \(1,\omega\) of the quadratic field can be chosen with \(\omega-\bar\omega=\sqrt D\).
The polynomial \(s\) has leading coefficient two and \(b\ne0\) since the factors are distinct.
The identity
\[
                   4u=s(t)^2+|D|b(t)^2
\]
gives the certificate \(A=X^{\deg b}b(X+X^{-1})\).

For a positive function \(H\) with integrable logarithm write \(G(H)=\exp\int\log H\), allowing isolated zeros of polynomials.
For two such functions \(H,J\) here, weighted arithmetic-geometric mean applied to \(H/G(H)\) and \(J/G(J)\), with weights proportional to \(G(H),G(J)\), gives \(G(H+J)\ge G(H)+G(J)\) after logarithmic integration.
For a nonzero polynomial \(g\in\mathbb Z[T]\), the polynomial \(X^{\deg g}g(X+X^{-1})\) has the same leading coefficient as \(g\), so its multiplicative Mahler measure is at least the magnitude of that coefficient by Jensen's formula.
Taking \(H=s(t)^2\) and \(J=|D|b(t)^2\) therefore yields \(4e^{m(P)}\ge4+|D|\), as asserted.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Scope relative to existing bounds

The [general quadratic subfield cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v317.md#discriminant) applies to any quadratic subfield of a unit field of degree \(2n\) and gives \(|D|\le4n^2\exp((4-2/n)m(P))\).
Here the two specified actions of inversion and complex conjugation yield degree-independent bounds and pointwise integer numerators.
[Garza's real-conjugate bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) uses the proportion of real conjugates of the unit, while the [abelian-base relative height estimate](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002) depends on the degree over the base.
Neither proof above requires either height estimate.
