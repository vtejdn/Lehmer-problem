# A sparse set of degrees for traces interlacing the Chebyshev extrema at a fixed pole norm

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exact mesh and a neighborhood of it

Let \(q\ge5\) be an integer, put \(p=q+1,\ M=qp,\ L=2q+1\), and assume \(4\mid M\).
Let \(H\) be a monic integer polynomial of degree \(n\ge4\) with \(M^nH(1/M)=L^2\).
Suppose \(H\) has alternating nonzero signs at the \(n\) nodes \(t_j=2\cos\theta_j-2\), where \(0=\theta_0<\cdots<\theta_{n-1}=\pi\), and \(H(0)<0\).
Put \(\theta_j^0=j\pi/(n-1)\).

* For the exact angles \(\theta_j=\theta_j^0\), the number of possible degrees \(n\le X\) is \(O_q(X^{\log 15/\log M})\).
  At each such degree there is at most one \(H\).
* At \(q=40\), every degree \(23\le n\le10^{12}\) is excluded even when \(\sum_j|\theta_j-\theta_j^0|\le1/512\).
  In particular this covers rational trace nodes satisfying that displacement bound.
  The finite exclusion uses the [integer prefix calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/lobatto_digits.md#results).

These sign tests concern the [trace trial](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v324.md#pole) on the specified norm branch.
Other interlacing meshes can have different coefficient bounds.

<a id="fourier"></a>
## The coefficients near the end of the reciprocal polynomial

Write \(R(x)=\prod_j(x-t_j)\).
Interlacing gives \(H/R=1-\sum_j r_j/(x-t_j)\) with \(r_j=-H(t_j)/R'(t_j)>0\).
There is one root in every gap and the final root \(w_0\) belongs to \((0,1/M)\), since \(H(1/M)>0\).
Comparing coefficients at infinity gives \(s=\sum_jr_j=\sum_{H(w)=0}w-\sum_jt_j\).
Summing the gap endpoints gives \(0<s<4+1/M\).

Set \(D=2n,\ m=n-1\), \(S(z)=z^nH(z+z^{-1}-2)=\sum_{j=0}^D c_jz^j\) and \(U(z)=z^nR(z+z^{-1}-2)\).
Both polynomials are reciprocal with constant coefficient one.
For the exact mesh one has \(U_0=(1-z^2)(1-z^{2m})\), by its roots with multiplicities.
As a formal series at zero,
\[
 S(z)=\frac{U(z)}{U_0(z)}(1-z^{2m})
 \left(1-z^2-sz-\sum_{h\ge1}\mu_h z^{h+1}\right),
 \qquad \mu_h=2\sum_j r_j\cos(h\theta_j).
\]
This follows by substituting into the partial fractions and expanding
\((1-z^2)/(1-2z\cos\theta+z^2)=1+2\sum_{h\ge1}\cos(h\theta)z^h\).
On the exact mesh \(c_1=-s\) is integral, so \(1\le s\le4\).
For \(1\le h\le n-1\) one has \(|\mu_h|<2s\), since some value of \(\cos(h\theta_j)\) differs from one and the value at \(j=0\) is one.
Hence \(1-2s\le c_2+1\le2s-1\), and \(1-2s\le c_j\le2s-1\) for \(3\le j\le n\).
There are at most fifteen choices at each position.

For \(\eta=\sum_j|\theta_j-\theta_j^0|\), the coefficients of \(\log(U/U_0)\) at positive index \(h\) have absolute value at most \(2\eta\).
Indeed they are differences of sums of \(-2\cos(h\theta_j)/h\).
Through any degree \(k\), the sum of absolute nonconstant coefficients of \(U/U_0\) is at most \(\exp(2k\eta)-1\), by the exponential series in the truncated polynomial ring.
If \(k\le8,\ k<n,\ \eta\le1/512\), this is less than \(1/30\).
The coefficients of the parenthesized series have absolute value \(<10\), since \(|\mu_h|\le2s\).
Thus disruption of each of its first \(k\) coefficients in \(S\) is less than \(1/3\).
Their integrality and \(s<4+1/M\) give
\[
 -4\le c_1\le0,\qquad -9\le c_2\le7,\qquad -8\le c_j\le8\quad(3\le j\le k).
\]

<a id="digits"></a>
## One degree class per prefix

The pole identity says \(q^D S(p/q)=L^2\), since \(p/q+q/p-2=1/M\).
By reciprocity and even degree the following integer is \(L^2\) for either \(b=q\) or \(b=-p\):
\[
 (1+b)^D S\big(b/(1+b)\big).
\]
Given \(c_0,\ldots,c_k\), truncate \(S\) at position \(k\) and reduce the expression modulo \(|b|^{k+1}\).
All higher terms vanish and the truncated value of \(S\) is invertible there.
This determines \((1+b)^D\) and hence at most one class of \(D\bmod |b|^k\).
In fact \(1+b\) has order \(|b|^k\) modulo \(|b|^{k+1}\).
For each prime \(l\mid b\), raising \(1+b\) to an exponent \(h>0\) gives \(v_l((1+b)^h-1)=v_l(b)+v_l(h)\).
This follows by the binomial expansion at one: taking an \(l\)-th power adds one to the valuation, and taking a power coprime to \(l\) preserves it.
For \(l=2\) the needed \(4\mid b\) holds by hypothesis.
The two moduli are coprime, so each prefix selects at most one class of \(D\bmod M^k\).

Conversely, for fixed \(D\), two different polynomials with the same pole norm must differ by a multiple of \(M\) in their first differing coefficient.
Divide the difference of the two homogeneous evaluations by \(b^j\) at that index \(j\) and reduce modulo \(|b|\) on each side.
At every index the exact-mesh coefficient range for a common preceding prefix has width less than \(M\); reciprocity then proves uniqueness from the first half.
For \(k=\lfloor\log_M X\rfloor\), the degrees above \(k+1\) have at most \(4\cdot15^{k-1}\) prefixes if \(k\ge1\).
Each class contains at most \(1+2X/M^k\) candidate degrees \(D\le2X\).
Adding the \(O(k+1)\) smaller degrees proves the counting assertion.
