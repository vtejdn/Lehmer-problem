# Which conductor multiples must occur in a near-optimal cyclotomic cofactor

<a id="statement"></a>
## Every prescribed divisor requires unbounded normalized degree

Let \(P\) be a fixed nonzero real polynomial with at least one nonzero root off the unit circle.
Let \(a\to\infty\), let \(C_a=\prod_n\Phi_n^{c_{n,a}}\) be monic cyclotomic products, and suppose that
\[
                     a^{-1}\log\|P^a C_a\|_{\mathbb T}\to m(P).
\]
The norm here is the circle supremum; attainment of this rate for the coefficient one-norm also suffices.
For every fixed positive integer \(N\),
\[
          \liminf_a\frac1a\sum_{N\mid n}c_{n,a}>0,\qquad
          \frac1a\sum_{N\mid n}\varphi(n)c_{n,a}\longrightarrow\infty .  \tag{1}
\]
In particular a fixed upper bound on the number of distinct prime divisors in each cofactor order is incompatible with this rate.
These statements apply to every nontorsion algebraic unit minimum by Kronecker's theorem.
The first constant may depend on \(P,N\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Exact constraints on the multiplicities at multiples of each conductor

Replace every root \(z_i\) of \(P\) outside the unit disk by \(w_i=1/\bar z_i\), leaving all other roots as \(w_i=z_i\) and keeping their multiplicities.
Set \(S_j=\sum_i w_i^j\) for \(j\ge1\).
For any \(F=P^a\prod_n\Phi_n^{c_n}\), let \(\Delta=a^{-1}\log\|F\|_{\mathbb T}-m(P)\).
One has for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \left|\frac1k\sum_{d\mid k}\mu_{\rm ar}(k/d)S_d
                 +\frac1a\sum_{m\ge1}\mu_{\rm ar}(m)c_{km}\right|
             \le4\Delta\prod_{p\mid k}(1+1/p),                         \tag{2}
\]
where \(\mu_{\rm ar}\) denotes the arithmetic Möbius function.
Indeed \(a^{-1}\log|F|-m(P)\) has Haar mean zero and positive part at most \(\Delta\), hence \(L^1\) norm at most \(2\Delta\).
By the [root logarithm Fourier formula](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#necessity) its Fourier moment at \(j\ge1\), multiplied by \(-2j\), is \(S_j+a^{-1}\sum_n c_n r_n(j)\), where \(r_n(j)\) is the sum of the \(j\)-th powers of primitive \(n\)-th roots.
Applying the divisor sum in (2) uses \(r_n(j)=\sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,j)}d\mu_{\rm ar}(n/d)\).
It gives \(\sum_{d\mid k}\mu_{\rm ar}(k/d)r_n(d)=k\mu_{\rm ar}(n/k)\) if \(k\mid n\), and zero otherwise.
Finally \(\sum_{d\mid k}d|\mu_{\rm ar}(k/d)|=k\prod_{p\mid k}(1+1/p)\).
This proves (2) with no restriction on the number or size of the cofactor orders.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Separating the contribution of an entire set of conductor multiples

For \(h\ge1\) write \(U_h\) for pushforward of a finite circle measure by \(z\mapsto z^h\).
On its \(j\)-th moment this replaces \(j\) by \(hj\).
Write \(\sigma_n\) for the counting measure on the primitive \(n\)-th roots and form the commuting operator
\[
               D_N=\prod_{p^e\parallel N}(U_p-I)U_{p^{e-1}} .
\]
The empty product is the identity.
If \(p^e\nmid n\), powering the \(n\)-th primitive roots by \(p^{e-1}\) gives a common multiple of the uniform orbit measure at an order coprime to \(p\), on which \(U_p\) is the identity.
Thus \(D_N\sigma_n=0\) whenever \(N\nmid n\).
This uses the same cancellation as the [conductor extraction on binomial logarithms](Yin_C_G0345_v109.md#extraction).
For each fixed \(j\ge1\), \(\left|\int z^j\,dD_N\sigma_n\right|\) has a bound \(B_{j,N}\ge1\) independent of \(n\).
Indeed the operator is a finite signed sum of \(U_h\) with \(h\mid N\), and \(|r_n(hj)|\le\sum_{d\mid hj}d\).

Let \(\nu_P\) be the sum of Poisson probabilities of the reflected points \(w_i\), using a point mass at each boundary root.
Its \(j\)-th moment is \(S_j\).
The measure \(D_N\nu_P\) has a nonzero nonconstant moment in its absolutely continuous part.
To see this, the image of the Poisson probability at \(w\) under \(U_h\) is the Poisson probability at \(w^h\), by all its moments.
In the expansion of \(D_N\) the smallest power is \(h_0=\prod_{p^e\parallel N}p^{e-1}\) with nonzero coefficient \((-1)^{\omega(N)}\).
Among the nonzero reflected points strictly inside the disk let \(r\) be the largest modulus, positive and below one by hypothesis.
In the signed list \(w_i^h\) of interior points, those with modulus \(r^{h_0}\) occur only at \(h=h_0\) and all with the same sign.
They cannot be cancelled.
A finite signed list with nonzero weight at a nonzero point cannot have every positive power sum zero, by the Vandermonde matrix on its distinct nonzero points.
This proves the assertion for the absolutely continuous part.
The remaining part of \(D_N\nu_P\) is a finite signed atomic measure.
Thus \(D_N\nu_P\) as a whole cannot be a multiple of Haar measure, by uniqueness of the decomposition into an absolutely continuous and a singular part.
In particular some positive moment \(b_j=\int z^j\,dD_N\nu_P\) is nonzero.

For this \(j\) use the Fourier estimate preceding (2) on each of the finitely many indices \(hj\) in \(D_N\).
As \(\Delta\to0\), we get
\[
       \frac1a\sum_{N\mid n}c_{n,a}\int z^j\,dD_N\sigma_n\longrightarrow-b_j .
\]
The uniform bound \(B_{j,N}\) gives the first conclusion in (1).
It also supplies an input-dependent strict increase in the coefficient rate if all multiples of \(N\) are forbidden: otherwise a sequence of costs approaching \(m(P)\) would contradict this estimate.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree escape within each selected set

Suppose along a subsequence that \(\nu_a=a^{-1}\sum_{N\mid n}c_{n,a}\sigma_n\) has bounded mass.
Take a further subsequence with weak limit \(\nu\).
The [primitive-orbit compactness argument](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#necessity) writes \(\nu\) as a nonnegative multiple of Haar measure plus a countable sum of positive atomic orbit measures.
Pushforward by a fixed power is continuous on measures in the weak topology.
Thus \(D_N\nu\) has absolutely continuous part a multiple of Haar measure and its remaining part is atomic.
By the same Fourier limit, all the nonconstant moments of \(D_N(\nu+\nu_P)\) vanish.
It is a multiple of Haar measure, since it is real and finite and trigonometric polynomials are dense.
Its absolutely continuous part would then have no nonconstant moment, contrary to the computation for \(D_N\nu_P\).
The contradiction proves the second conclusion in (1).
These necessary conditions concern the entire set of multiples of each fixed \(N\), rather than prescribing any one cofactor multiplicity.
