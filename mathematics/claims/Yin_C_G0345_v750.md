# Primitive sections of the four-term pole equation and Schinzel's rational conjecture

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single conjectural instance and an unconditional splitting test

Fix a real algebraic number \(B>1\) and \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\).
Let \(D\) range over the distinct conjugates of \(B\), let \(l\) be the positive leading coefficient of its primitive integer minimal polynomial, and put
\[
 F_D(X,Y)=Y(X-D)+\epsilon(DX-1),\qquad
 H_{B,\epsilon}(X,Y)=l\prod_D F_D(X,Y).
\]
Every pullback of \(H_{B,\epsilon}\) by a nonsingular integral monomial substitution is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) in the Laurent ring.
Consequently **Schinzel's rational specialization conjecture for this one polynomial** implies that, outside finitely many primitive directions \((u,v)\), \(H_{B,\epsilon}(Z^u,Z^v)\) has at most one noncyclotomic irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q\), counted with multiplicity and up to rational Laurent units.

Suppose further that \(B\) is totally real.
Put
\[
 h_B=\#\{D:|D|>1\},\qquad
 s_B=\max_{|D|>1}\frac{|D|+1}{|D|-1}.
\]
For positive \(u,v\) with \(v/u>s_B\), the integer polynomial \(H_{B,\epsilon}(Z^u,Z^v)\) has exactly \(u h_B\) roots of modulus greater than one, counted with multiplicity.
The component \(F_B(Z^u,Z^v)\) has a unique positive root greater than one, and it is simple in the entire norm polynomial.
If that root is Salem and \(u h_B\ge2\), the norm polynomial has at least two **distinct** noncyclotomic rational irreducible factors.
This conclusion does not require Schinzel's conjecture.

In particular, fix a critical width bound and a positive density for four-term tests as in [the Salem classification](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement).
If such small-value tests persist along a shrinking Salem sequence, there are a single allowed totally real \(B>1\) and a single sign \(\epsilon\) for which the rational conjecture fails for \(H_{B,\epsilon}\).
Conversely, the conjecture for the finitely many pole and sign choices of that classification excludes the tests at its fixed positive threshold along every shrinking Salem sequence, for all sufficiently large indices.
Primitive direction alone permits a split even at the integer pole two, as the [explicit factorization below](#example) shows.

<a id="pullback"></a>
## Pullbacks of the individual graphs and their rational product

The integrality of \(H_{B,\epsilon}\) follows by evaluating the homogeneous primitive minimal polynomial of \(B\) on \(XY-\epsilon,\ Y-\epsilon X\).
For each positive integer \(n\), consider \(F_D(X^n,Y^n)\) as a polynomial in \(Y\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[X]\).
Its constant coefficient has a simple zero at any solution \(\xi\) of \(\xi^n=1/D\), since \(D\ne0\) and \(\xi\ne0\).
Its leading coefficient is nonzero there because \(D^2\ne1\).
The coefficients are relatively prime, so Eisenstein at \(X-\xi\) and Gauss's lemma give irreducibility.
Neither coordinate divides this polynomial, so it stays irreducible in the Laurent ring.

For a nonsingular integer matrix \(M\), composing its monomial substitution with the signed adjugate gives diagonal powering by \(|\det M|\), as in the [pullback argument for the cubic graph](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#schinzel).
A nonsingular substitution preserves distinct exponent vectors, hence takes every Laurent nonunit to a nonunit.
It follows that each \(F_D(\mathbf X^M)\) is absolutely irreducible.
The original \(F_D\)'s for different \(D\) have the same four-point support and coefficient one at \(XY\), so they are pairwise nonassociate.
Their common transformed support is still finite with no nonzero translation fixing it, and distinct coefficient vectors stay distinct, so their transforms are also pairwise nonassociate.
The absolute Galois group acts transitively on these factors.
Any rational factor of their product selects a Galois-stable subcollection up to association, which must be empty or full.
This proves the rational irreducibility assertion, including when \(B\) is nonrational.
The support has rank two because its convex hull before substitution is the sum of the four-corner squares of the factors, with each extreme coefficient a nonzero product.
In particular the rational parent is noncyclotomic.

<a id="conjecture"></a>
## The exact arithmetic specialization required

[Conjecture 2.1 of Amoroso and Sombra's published formulation](../audits/literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) concerns a rational irreducible noncyclotomic Laurent polynomial.
It permits finitely many exceptional nonzero vectors \(\mathbf c\) and finitely many nonsingular integer matrices \(M\).
For each direction orthogonal to none of the \(\mathbf c\)'s it supplies a representation \((u,v)^{\mathsf T}=M\mathbf b\).
The irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\) of the pullback by \(M\), specialized along \(\mathbf b\) and stripped of cyclotomic factors, are to give the irreducible factorization of the cyclotomic-free specialization, with the original multiplicities and up to Laurent units.
In dimension two each exceptional line contains only two primitive integer vectors.
Every permitted pullback of our parent is itself irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), giving the asserted bound of one factor.
No primitive specialization of this parent is identically zero: that would force one individual graph to contain the image of a one-dimensional subtorus, whose defining ideal is generated by a Laurent binomial, whereas every graph is irreducible of support rank two.

Here \(XYF_D(1/X,1/Y)=-\epsilon F_D(X,Y)\), so this parent is reciprocal up to a Laurent unit.
The established non-self-inversive case does not apply.
The authors' Theorem 2.4 over \(\mathbb C(z)\) concerns nonconstant input and removes the part constant in \(z\).
The present parent is constant in that sense.
The [general conditional reduction for sparse factors](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v193.md#shortness) permits a finite descent through several factors and subtori; the graph pullback above specifies the single rational instance for the present pole.

<a id="winding"></a>
## Unit-circle roots and the simple positive exterior root

Assume \(D\) is real.
No conjugate of \(B\) equals \(1\) or \(-1\).
For a root on the unit circle the equation is
\[
             Z^v=\epsilon\,\frac{D Z^u-1}{D-Z^u}.
\]
The quotient on the right has unit modulus there.
At \(e^{it}\) the angular derivative of \((De^{it}-1)/(D-e^{it})\) is \((D^2-1)/|D-e^{it}|^2\), by logarithmic differentiation.
For \(|D|>1\) the map has winding number one, since its zero \(1/D\) is inside the disk and its pole \(D\) outside.
Its angular derivative is at most \((|D|+1)/(|D|-1)\).
For \(|D|<1\) the winding number is minus one and the angular derivative is negative.
Thus for \(v/u>s_B\) the difference of the left and right arguments increases strictly, with positive derivative everywhere.
It counts exactly \(v-u\) simple unit-circle solutions for \(|D|>1\) and \(v+u\) for \(|D|<1\).
The section of each component has degree \(v+u\), nonzero constant term and is reciprocal up to sign.
Its off-circle roots pair in equal multiplicities across the circle.
Adding the individual counts proves the claimed \(u h_B\) exterior roots for the product.

For the positive root of the \(B\)-component set \(t=Z^u>1\), \(s=v/u\).
At sign minus, \(t\) must exceed \(B\), and \((Bt-1)/(t-B)\) decreases from \(+\infty\) to \(B\) while \(t^s\) strictly increases to infinity.
Their intersection is simple.
At sign plus, \(1<t<B\), and one must solve \(s\log t=\log((Bt-1)/(B-t))\).
As a function of \(\log t\) the right side starts at zero with derivative \((B+1)/(B-1)\), and its derivative
\[
                \frac{B^2-1}{B^2+1-B(t+t^{-1})}
\]
is strictly increasing on \(1<t<B\).
The right side tends to \(+\infty\) as \(t\) tends to \(B\).
Strict convexity gives precisely one positive intersection in \(\log t>0\) with distinct derivatives, hence a simple root in \(Z\).
At any such \(Z>1\), \(Y=Z^v>X=Z^u>1\), so \(Y-\epsilon X\ne0\).
The value \((XY-\epsilon)/(Y-\epsilon X)\) specifies its unique pole component, proving simplicity in the product.
If the root is Salem, its rational minimal polynomial has only one exterior root, with multiplicity one.
The remaining \(u h_B-1\) exterior roots, when positive in number, therefore require a different rational noncyclotomic factor.

<a id="salem"></a>
## Consequence for critical concentration

Given infinitely many tests in a shrinking Salem sequence, use the exact equation of v743 and take a subsequence with fixed pole \(B\) and sign.
Put \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), \(u=a/g,\ v=b/g,\ \beta=\alpha^g\); this \(\beta\) is again Salem because all positive powers preserve the degree and the unique exterior conjugate.
The exponents are primitive, \(v/u\to\infty\), and \(u\to\infty\) by the [uniform bound on common dilation](Yin_C_G0345_v747.md#statement).
Thus eventually \(s_B<v/u\) and \(u h_B\ge2\).
The [exterior count](#winding) gives at least two distinct noncyclotomic factors on these infinitely many primitive directions.
For every proposed finite exceptional list the directions eventually avoid its lines, and none of the nonsingular pullbacks can account for the two factors.
This proves the stated obstruction and its conditional concentration consequence.

<a id="example"></a>
## Two signed Salem factors in a primitive section

At \(B=2,\ \epsilon=1,\ u=2,\ v=13\), direct multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Z^{15}-2Z^{13}+2Z^2-1
 ={}&(Z-1)(Z^6-Z^4-Z^3-Z^2+1)\\
    &{}\cdot(Z^8+Z^7+Z^5+Z^4+Z^3+Z+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(T=Z+Z^{-1}\), the degree-six and degree-eight factors divided by \(Z^3\) and \(Z^4\) are respectively \(T^3-4T-1\) and \(T^4+T^3-4T^2-2T+3\).
The cubic has no rational root, and sign changes give one real root in each of \((-2,-1),(-1,0),(2,3)\).
Modulo two the quartic is \(T^4+T^3+1\), which has neither a linear factor nor the irreducible quadratic \(T^2+T+1\), hence is irreducible.
Sign changes give one quartic root in each of \((-3,-2),(-2,-1),(0,1),(1,2)\).
For both trace fields the discriminant \(T^2-4\) is negative at an embedding, so the quadratic \(Z^2-TZ+1\) over that field is irreducible.
The two original reciprocal factors are therefore irreducible, the first with a positive Salem root and the second with the negative of a Salem root.
Their different exterior roots account for the whole winding count of two.
The conjecture allows this primitive direction among its finitely many exceptions.
