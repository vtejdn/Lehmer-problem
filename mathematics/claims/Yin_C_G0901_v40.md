# Sharp height floors for rational maps of bounded degree at small units

2026-11-24T21:37 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The best constants at each degree

Let \(s\ge1\) be fixed and let \(\mathcal A_s\) consist of the rational functions \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\) of degrees \(1,\ldots,s\), excluding the functions \(\pm X^j\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z\).
Consider algebraic units \(\alpha\) of degrees \(d\to\infty\) with absolute logarithmic Weil heights \(h=h(\alpha)\to0\).
They may include roots of unity.
Write \(m\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive integral minimum, or for logarithmic Mahler measure of a polynomial on its full torus as indicated.
Then
\[
 \min_{R\in\mathcal A_s}h(R(\alpha))
       =\kappa_s+O_s\!\left(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)}+
                                  \sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}d}\right),
 \qquad
 \kappa_s=
 \begin{cases}
       m(1+X+Y),&s=1,2,\\
       \log\theta_0,&s\ge3 ,
 \end{cases}                                                          \tag{1}
\]
where \(\theta_0>1\) solves \(\theta_0^3-\theta_0-1=0\), and \(h\log(2/h)=0\) at \(h=0\).
The minimizing constant for \(s=1,2\) is attained in the limit by \(R=1+X\).
For every \(s\ge3\) a fixed cubic rational function attains the second constant.
The coefficients of all competing maps may vary with \(\alpha\).
For \(d>s\) their values are finite and nonzero.
Since \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(R(\alpha))]\le\deg R\), every allowed map satisfies the uniform total-measure bound
\[
                          m(R(\alpha))\ge(\kappa_s-o(1))\,d/s .          \tag{2}
\]
The degree inequality follows by inserting \(R(\alpha)\) in the reduced numerator-minus-value-times-denominator equation.
The [fixed-map height floor](Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#fixed-rational-function-height-gap) and the [moving rational-map inequalities](Yin_C_G0345_v114.md#statement) give positive-height or degree-dependent obstructions.
Here (1) identifies the exact constants, including their change between degrees two and three.

<a id="evaluation"></a>
## The limiting height of a fixed rational function

Write \(R=A/B\) with coprime nonzero polynomials in \(\mathbb Q[X]\) represented by integer coefficients primitive jointly across the two rows.
Let \(t=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\ge1\).
For \(\eta\) normalized arc length define
\[
             I(A,B)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A(z)|,|B(z)|)\,d\eta(z)
                       =m(A(X)-YB(X)).                                 \tag{3}
\]
The identity follows by integrating in \(Y\) first, using the circle mean of the logarithm of a linear polynomial.
The two rows have no common complex zero.

For fixed \(A,B\) the archimedean part of the projective height of \([A(\alpha):B(\alpha)]\) is \(I(A,B)+O_R(\sqrt{h+\log(2d)/d})\).
To see the angular estimate, apply the [discriminant phase estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v36.md#arch) to all \(d\) conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Its required discriminant bound is \(|\operatorname{disc}P_\alpha|\ge1\) for the monic integral minimum, and its input sums over this orbit are \(\sum\log^+|\alpha_i|=dh\) and \(\sum|\log|\alpha_i||=2dh\).
The circle test \(f=\log\max(|A|,|B|)\) is continuous and piecewise smooth with bounded one-sided derivatives, because \(|A|^2-|B|^2\) is a trigonometric polynomial and the maximum never vanishes.
In case the squared moduli agree identically, either row has positive modulus everywhere on the circle.
Its Fourier coefficients are \(O_R((1+|k|)^{-2})\) by integration by parts on the finitely many pieces.
Explicitly, the squared phase-moment sum of the disk points in the cited estimate is \(O(h+\log(2d)/d)\) with weights \((1-1/d)^k/k\).
Truncate the Fourier series at \(\min(d,\lceil h^{-1/2}\rceil)\), using \(d\) at \(h=0\).
The tail costs \(O_R(1/d+\sqrt h)\), passing from the phases to the disk points up to the cutoff costs \(O_R(h\log(2/h))\) for \(h>0\) and zero at \(h=0\), and the disk-moment contribution has the asserted bound by Cauchy–Schwarz.

On \(\mathbb C\) subtract \(t\log\max(1,|z|)\) from the same log maximum.
The difference extends to a bounded function on the sphere, since at least one row has degree \(t\), and is Lipschitz on \(\mathrm e^{-1}\le|z|\le\mathrm e\), where the maximum stays positive.
Its average difference from its value at the phases is \(O_R(d^{-1}\sum|\log|\alpha_i||)=O_R(h)\), using boundedness outside that annulus.
Restoring the subtracted term adds \(t h\).
This establishes the archimedean assertion.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Finite primes, including ramified and repeated reductions

Choose a nonzero integer \(\Delta\in(A,B)\mathbb Z[X]\) by clearing denominators in a Bézout identity.
Let \(J=(A(\alpha),B(\alpha))\) in the ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Its finite contribution to the projective height is \(-d^{-1}\log N J\), and \(\Delta\in J\).
For each prime \(p\mid\Delta\), the reductions of \(A,B\) are not both zero by joint primitivity.
Their monic gcd has degree at most \(t\).
Choose \(k\ge1\) divisible by \(p^e-1\) for every such \(p\) and \(1\le e\le t\).
Every nonzero root of the gcd has order dividing \(k\), since its degree over \(\mathbb F_p\) is at most \(t\).
The gcd consequently divides \(X^t(X^k-1)^t\), including its zero root and all multiplicities.
Lifting the resulting ideal identity from \(\mathbb F_p[X]\) gives
\[
                X^t(X^k-1)^t \in (A,B,p)\,\mathbb Z[X].
\]
At every \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) in the field of \(\alpha\), integrality and \(\alpha\) being a unit yield
\[
    t\,v_{\mathfrak p}(1-\alpha^k)\ge
               \min(v_{\mathfrak p}(J),v_{\mathfrak p}(p)),\qquad
    v_{\mathfrak p}(J)\le v_p(\Delta)v_{\mathfrak p}(p).
\]
Therefore \(v_{\mathfrak p}(J)\le t\,v_p(\Delta)v_{\mathfrak p}(1-\alpha^k)\).
This argument permits either row to vanish identically modulo an individual prime.
If \(|\Delta|=1\), the ideal \(J\) is already the unit ideal.

For fixed \(k\) and large \(d\), \(\alpha^k\ne1\), and \(d^{-1}\log NJ\ll_R d^{-1}\log|N(1-\alpha^k)|\).
The [Vandermonde bound for cyclic norms](Yin_C_G0901_v26.md#integral) bounds the latter by \(\log(N+1)/N+(N+2)kh/3\) whenever the multiples \(1-\alpha^{kj}\) are nonzero for all \(1\le j\le N\).
This last condition is the only use of nontorsion in that proof.
For a root of unity its order is at least \(d\), so \(N\le d/(2k)\) suffices for torsion as well.
Take \(N\) to be the smaller of \(\lfloor d/(2k)\rfloor\) and \(\lceil\sqrt{\log(2/h)/h}\rceil\), omitting the second argument at \(h=0\).
This gives \(d^{-1}\log NJ\ll_R \sqrt{h\log(2/h)}+\log(2d)/d\): when the first cutoff is smaller, the second term of the cyclic-norm bound still has size \(O_R(\sqrt{h\log(2/h)})\).
Thus \(h(R(\alpha))=I(A,B)\) up to (1)'s error with a constant depending on this fixed map.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## Finitely many matrices suffice at bounded output height

For a reduced map of actual degree \(t\le s\), take \(2t+1\) distinct conjugates \(z_j\) of \(\alpha\), available for \(d>2s\), and their values \(v_j=R(z_j)\).
The evaluation matrix for the two rows of coefficients has rows
\[
               (1,z_j,\ldots,z_j^t,-v_j,-v_jz_j,\ldots,-v_jz_j^t).
\]
Its kernel is one-dimensional: another two rows \(C,D\) passing these tests satisfy \(CB-DA=0\), since this polynomial has degree at most \(2t\) and vanishes at the \(2t+1\) different inputs.
As \(A,B\) are coprime and one has degree \(t\), this forces \((C,D)\) proportional to \((A,B)\).
The coefficient vector in projective space is therefore represented by the signed maximal minors.
The projective height of each matrix row equals \(t h+h(R(\alpha))\), because at every place the largest coordinate modulus is \(\max(1,|z_j|)^t\max(1,|v_j|)\).
Bounding each determinant by the product of the row maxima times \((2t+1)!\) at the archimedean places and by the product alone elsewhere gives coefficient height at most
\[
                     \log((2t+1)!)+(2t+1)(t h+h(R(\alpha))).
\]
For \(h\le1/2,\ h(R(\alpha))\le1\) and \(t\le s\), only a fixed finite set of rational coefficient vectors is possible, by their primitive integral normalization.
Thus we may maximize the fixed-map error constants over that set.
The map \(1+X\) has limiting height below one by the calculation below, so this finite comparison suffices to minimize over every \(R\in\mathcal A_s\).
It remains to determine the minimum possible value of (3).

<a id="plastic"></a>
## The plastic-number bound and its cubic equality case

Write \(A=X^u A_0,\ B=X^v B_0\) with both constant coefficients of \(A_0,B_0\) nonzero.
For \(n>3t+1\), the support of \(A-X^nB\) consists of two nonempty blocks, each of span at most \(t\), with a gap longer than \(t\).
Reflection of the occupied exponents can carry this polynomial to itself up to sign only by interchanging the complete blocks, because the smallest and largest exponents interchange and the two blocks are separated by that gap.
Comparing their lengths and coefficients shows that reciprocity after removal of any initial monomial is equivalent to
\[
       \deg A_0=\deg B_0=a,\qquad B_0(X)=\pm X^a A_0(1/X).              \tag{4}
\]
Conversely (4) does reflect the two blocks with a fixed choice of overall sign.

If (4) fails, each such specialization after removal of its initial monomial is nonreciprocal.
Its measure is at least \(\log\theta_0\).
Indeed an integral scalar content or an irreducible factor having a nonunit extreme coefficient costs at least \(\log2\) if its magnitude exceeds one.
Otherwise a nonreciprocal irreducible unit factor must occur, since a product of reciprocal factors is reciprocal, and [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) supplies the stated floor.
The [Boyd–Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) now yields \(I(A,B)\ge\log\theta_0\), since the shortest nonzero integral relation on \((1,n)\) has sup norm \(n\).

If (4) holds, the two row moduli agree on the unit circle and \(I(A,B)=m(A_0)\).
If \(A_0\) is nonconstant it cannot itself be reciprocal up to sign, because it would divide both rows.
The same factor argument therefore gives \(m(A_0)\ge\log\theta_0\).
If \(A_0\) is constant, (4) and joint primitivity force the excluded maps \(\pm X^j\).
In particular constant multiples of monomials with other rational scalars do not escape the floor: for reduced scalar \(a/b\), (3) equals \(\log\max(|a|,|b|)\ge\log2\).

Choose \(A=X^3-X-1\) and \(B=X^3 A(1/X)\).
The cubic is irreducible by the rational root test, has only one real root, and its two other roots have modulus \(\theta_0^{-1/2}\).
The two rows are coprime since \(A\) is not reciprocal.
Consequently this allowed degree-three map satisfies \(I(A,B)=\log\theta_0\), giving equality for every \(s\ge3\).

## The larger constant through degree two

Set
\[
 C=m(1+X+Y)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log^+|1+z|\,d\eta(z)
           =\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\cos t)\,dt .
\]
It satisfies \(C<1/3\).
In fact \(\tan t\ge t+t^3/3\) for \(0\le t<\pi/2\) by \(\tan t\ge t\) and integration of \(1+\tan^2t\ge1+t^2\).
Hence \(\log\cos t\le-t^2/2-t^4/12\), giving \(C\le (2/3)\log2-\pi^2/81-\pi^4/7290<1/3\) by \(\log2<0.7,\ \pi>3.14\).
The map \(1+X\) attains \(I=C\).

Suppose that \(\deg R\le2\) and \(I(A,B)\le C\).
Both individual row measures are at most \(C\).
After removing signs and powers of \(X\), each is the constant one or a monic integral polynomial of degree at most two with constant term \(\pm1\); larger extreme coefficients would cost at least \(\log2\).
Any noncyclotomic quadratic of this form has measure at least \(\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)>1/3\): for constant term \(-1\) check the roots of \(X^2+aX-1\), and for constant term \(1\) a root off the circle requires \(|a|\ge3\).
The possible shapes with their first two power sums \(S_P(k)=\sum_{P(\xi)=0}\xi^k\), counted with multiplicity after removing monomials, are therefore
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
 P&1&1+X&1-X&(1+X)^2&(1-X)^2&1-X^2&\Phi_3&\Phi_4&\Phi_6\\ \hline
 S_P(1)&0&-1&1&-2&2&0&-1&0&1\\
 S_P(2)&0&1&1&2&2&2&-1&-2&-1 .
 \end{array}                                                           \tag{5}
\]
The shapes are coprime.
Two constant shapes would give excluded monomials, since both constants have modulus one.
The constant shape paired with \(1+X,1-X,1-X^2,\Phi_4=1+X^2\) gives exactly \(C\) by rotation and Haar invariance under powering.

For the other pairs let \(f=\log|A|-\log|B|\) on the circle.
Since both row means are zero, \(I(A,B)=\tfrac12\int|f|\,d\eta\).
The \(k\)-th cosine coefficient, using \(\int f(e^{it})\cos(kt)\,dt/(2\pi)\), equals \(-(S_A(k)-S_B(k))/(2k)\) by the circle logarithm series for individual roots.
Thus \(I(A,B)\ge |S_A(k)-S_B(k)|/(4k)\).
A difference of at least two at \(k=1\), or at least three at \(k=2\), exceeds \(C\).
In (5) the remaining coprime pairs, beyond those with equality already listed, reduce under interchange and \(X\mapsto-X\) to
\[
                    (1,\Phi_3),\qquad(1+X,\Phi_3),\qquad(\Phi_3,\Phi_4).
\]
To handle them set \(\chi_4(l)=(-1)^{(l-1)/2}\) at odd \(l\) and \(G=\sum_{l\text{ odd}}\chi_4(l)/l^2\).
Testing instead with \(\operatorname{sgn}(\cos kt)\) gives
\[
 I(A,B)\ge\frac1{\pi k}
      \left|\sum_{l\text{ odd}}\frac{\chi_4(l)(S_A(kl)-S_B(kl))}{l^2}\right|.
\]
Indeed the square wave has cosine coefficients \(4\chi_4(l)/(\pi l)\) at odd multiples of \(k\).
The identity follows by Abel smoothing of the bounded square wave and dominated convergence against the integrable \(f\).

For odd \(l\), \(S_{\Phi_3}(l)=S_{\Phi_3}(2l)=-1+3\mathbf1_{3\mid l}\), \(S_{\Phi_4}(l)=0\) and \(S_{1+X}(2l)=1\).
The sum of \(\chi_4(l)/l^2\) over odd multiples of three is \(-G/9\).
Taking \(k=1,2,1\) for the three respective pairs gives the lower bounds \(4G/(3\pi),7G/(6\pi),4G/(3\pi)\).
All exceed \(1/3\), since \(G>1-1/9+1/25-1/49>44/49\) and \(\pi<22/7\).
This exhausts all degree-two possibilities and, by inclusion, degree one as well.
It proves \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=C\) and completes (1) and (2).
