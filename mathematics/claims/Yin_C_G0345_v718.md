# Positive-density delays preserving every dilation at each integer Salem seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single progression for both signs at every seed

Fix any integer \(a\ge3\).
For each delay \(n\ge7\) and sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r>1\) be the exterior root of \(G_a(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\), and \(P\) its monic Salem minimal polynomial.
Set
\[
 H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}\frac{a+j}{\operatorname{rad}(a+j)},
 \qquad e_q=\mathbf1_{q\mid a}\quad(q=2,3).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{rad}\) is the product of the distinct prime divisors.
For distinct primes \(p,q\), with \(p\) odd, write \(w_{p,q}=v_p(q^{\operatorname{ord}_p(q)}-1)\ge1\), and define the odd integer
\[
             D=\prod_{\substack{p\mid H(a)\\ p\ {\rm odd}}}p^{w_{p,2}} .
\]
An empty product is one.
For all sufficiently large \(n\) in the single progression
\[
                           n\equiv-e_2\pmod {2D},                       \tag{1}
\]
both signs simultaneously give
\[
       P(X^k)\ \text{irreducible over }\mathbb Q,\qquad
       m(P(X^k))=\log r\quad\text{for every }k\ge1,                    \tag{2}
\]
where \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
The progression has natural density \(1/(2D)\).
Its modulus depends only on the odd prime support of \(H(a)\), and its residue depends only on the parity of \(a\).
The conclusions already hold at every \(n\ge7\) in (1) meeting \(n>\tfrac12\log(a+1)\log^3(3(n+1))\), or meeting the [effective large-delay bound](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement).

There is also an explicit union of progressions, with density \(\prod_{p\mid H(a)}c_p\), all of whose sufficiently large members satisfy (2) for both signs.
For odd \(p\mid H(a)\), define
\[
 c_3=3^{-w_{3,2}}=\tfrac13,\qquad
 c_p=
 \begin{cases}
 p^{-\min(w_{p,2},w_{p,3})},& e_2=e_3,\\
 p^{-w_{p,2}}+p^{-w_{p,3}},& e_2\ne e_3
 \end{cases}\qquad(p>3).
\]
When \(2\mid H(a)\), take \(c_2=1/2+\mathbf1_{e_2\ne e_3}/8\).
One may instead take \(c_2=1\) if \(a\) is even with \(a+1\) nonsquare, or if \(a\equiv1\bmod4\) with \(2(a+1)\) nonsquare.
For \(a\equiv3\bmod4\) the lower natural density of delays giving (2) is at least \(\prod_{p\mid H(a),\,p\ {\rm odd}}c_p\); here the potentially exceptional delays for the prime two number \(O_a(\sqrt X)\) up to \(X\).
None of the conclusions assumes that \(H(a)\) is squarefree or bounds its prime-power exponents.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Auxiliary characteristics for the odd primes

Let \(R\) denote the largest exponent of a positive field radical of \(r\) in \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\).
If \(p\mid R\) then \(r^{1/p}\) generates \(K\), and its minimal polynomial \(S\) has the property that the \(p\)-th powers of its roots give the full root multiset of \(P\), each once.
Reduction in characteristic \(q\ne p\) preserves this powered multiset identity, including repetitions, by the resultant formula for the powered polynomial.
Suppose a root of \(\overline P\) has order \(m\) divisible by \(p\).
Every \(p\)-th root of that point has order \(mp\).
A Frobenius-\(q\) orbit of preimages covers the target orbit uniformly, with \(\operatorname{ord}_{mp}(q)/\operatorname{ord}_m(q)\) points over each point.
If this quotient is \(p\), every source orbit, with its constant multiplicity, contributes a multiple of \(p\).
Thus a target multiplicity prime to \(p\) excludes \(p\mid R\).
This is the prime case of the [orbit degree criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v717.md#statement).

The [seed cofactor](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) \(U=G_a/P\) has only the roots \(1,-1\).
For \(q=2,3\), the reduction of \(G_a\) contains the binomial shown below, multiplied by the displayed linear factor when present:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 a\bmod q&\overline G_a\\ \hline
 0& X^{n+1}-\epsilon\\
 1&(X-1)(X^n+\epsilon)\\
 -1&(X+1)(X^n-\epsilon).
\end{array}
\]
At \(q=2\) the last two rows agree.
Write \(N_q=n+e_q\), and express the binomial as \(X^{N_q}-\eta\).
Away from \(1,-1\), its roots have multiplicity exactly \(q^{v_q(N_q)}\) in \(\overline P\), because the prime-to-\(q\) binomial remaining after Frobenius is separable.

For any odd \(p\ne q\), require \(p^{w_{p,q}}\mid N_q\).
In characteristic two, or when \(\eta=1\), take a root of order \(m=p^{w_{p,q}}\).
In characteristic three with \(\eta=-1\), take a root of order \(m=2^{v_2(N_q)+1}p^{w_{p,q}}\); its \(N_q\)-th power is \(-1\).
In all cases the order exceeds two, and the multiplicity \(q^{v_q(N_q)}\) is prime to \(p\).
Write \(t=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\).
Its definition and \(v_p(q^t-1)=w_{p,q}\) give \(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{w_{p,q}}}(q)=t\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_{p^{w_{p,q}+1}}(q)=pt\), by binomial expansion at \(q^t=1\bmod p^{w_{p,q}}\).
In the signed characteristic-three case the order of three modulo the additional power of two is a power of two, including the value one.
Taking its least common multiple with \(t\) and with \(pt\) therefore preserves the ratio \(p\).
The orbit criterion now excludes \(p\mid R\) for both signs, for either allowed auxiliary characteristic.

<a id="two"></a>
## Excluding square field radicals

For every seed, taking \(n\equiv-e_2\bmod2\) excludes a square radical for both signs.
For even \(a\) this follows from the [even-seed square test](Yin_C_G0345_v716.md#squares), which also excludes all delays if \(a+1\) is nonsquare.

For odd \(a\), suppose a square radical exists.
The [two-adic trace test](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#radicals) requires that \(X+1\) be absent from \(U\) for \(a\equiv1\bmod4\) and present for \(a\equiv3\bmod4\).
Also \(P(-1)\) must be a square integer: putting \(\beta=r^{1/2}\in K\), one has \(1+r=\beta(\beta+\beta^{-1})\), \(\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)=1\) and \([K:\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})]=2\), as in the [reciprocal norm calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v716.md#squares).
For even \(n\), the only sign allowed by the trace test is respectively \(\epsilon=1\), with \(U=X-1\), or \(\epsilon=-1\), with \(U=X+1\).
Evaluation gives \(P(-1)=a+1\) in the first case, and differentiation at \(-1\) gives \(P(-1)=(a+1)n+1-a\) in the second.
Both values are two modulo four under their respective seed conditions.
Thus all even delays work at the prime two for odd seeds.

For odd \(n\) the only allowed sign is \(\epsilon=-1\), \(U=1\), if \(a\equiv1\bmod4\), and \(\epsilon=1\), \(U=X^2-1\), if \(a\equiv3\bmod4\).
Evaluation and differentiation respectively give
\[
       P(-1)=2(a+1),\qquad
       P(-1)=\frac{(a+1)n+1-a}{2}.
\]
The first gives the claimed all-delay exclusion when that integer is nonsquare.
The second takes distinct positive values \(O_a(X)\) at the odd delays \(n\le X\), so a square occurs at most \(O_a(\sqrt X)\) times.

Independently, requiring \(8\mid N_3\) excludes a square field radical for either sign.
Use the characteristic-three binomial above.
For \(\eta=1\) take a primitive eighth root, and for \(\eta=-1\) take a root of order \(2^{v_2(N_3)+1}\).
These orders are at least eight and avoid the linear cofactors.
The target multiplicity is a power of three, while \(\operatorname{ord}_{2^{b+1}}(3)=2\operatorname{ord}_{2^b}(3)\) for every \(b\ge3\), by successive squaring starting at \(v_2(3^2-1)=3\).
The orbit argument with \(p=2,q=3\) gives the exclusion.

## The congruence classes and the delay cutoff

The [explicit trace and degree criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#statement) gives \(R\mid H(a)\) at its stated strict delay inequality, which holds eventually for each \(a\).
The stronger effective criterion of [v701](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) suffices as well.
On (1) the parity criterion excludes two, while the characteristic-two criterion excludes every odd divisor prime of \(H(a)\).
Thus \(R=1\) for both signs.
The [positive field radical theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives irreducibility for each \(k\).
The original Salem polynomial has logarithmic measure \(\log r\), and substitution preserves it by taking all \(k\)-th roots of every root.

For the larger union, retain only the primes dividing \(H(a)\).
At \(p=3\) use \(3\mid n+e_2\).
At each \(p>3\) admit the union of \(n\equiv-e_2\bmod p^{w_{p,2}}\) and \(n\equiv-e_3\bmod p^{w_{p,3}}\).
With equal offsets the smaller modulus contains the other class; with unequal offsets the classes are already disjoint modulo \(p\).
At two use the union of \(n\equiv-e_2\bmod2\) and \(n\equiv-e_3\bmod8\), omitting the two-condition entirely in either all-delay exclusion case.
These classes have exactly the local densities \(c_p\) above.
Their moduli at distinct primes are coprime, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives the product density for the intersection and the finite union of explicit progressions.
The effective cutoff removes only finitely many terms.
For \(a\equiv3\bmod4\), allowing every parity in this intersection leaves only \(O_a(\sqrt X)\) potential square exceptions up to \(X\), giving the stronger lower density.
The [earlier progression](Yin_C_G0345_v717.md#sieve) selected a varying sequence of seeds with the prime support of \(H(a)\) contained in \(\{2,3\}\).
Here each prescribed seed has its own progressions, including when that support contains arbitrary odd primes.
