# Fourier regularity for the coefficient tests with forbidden cyclotomic factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## The part of a dual probability away from roots of unity

Fix a finite set \(B\) of forbidden conductors and a probability measure \(\mu\) on the unit circle with \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for every \(n\notin B\).
Subtract the masses on the primitive roots at the orders in \(B\), leaving a positive measure \(\nu\).
Then \(\nu\) has no atoms.
Suppose in addition that
\[
                    \sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{\tau(\ell)}{\ell}
                                 |\widehat\nu(\ell)|<\infty,             \tag{1}
\]
where \(\widehat\nu(\ell)=\int z^\ell\,d\nu(z)\) and \(\tau\) counts positive divisors.
The conjugation symmetrization of \(\nu\) is then \(\nu(\mathbb T)\) times normalized arc length \(\eta\).
Condition (1) holds in particular when \(\nu\) has a square-integrable density, or when there are \(c,C>0\) with \(\nu(B(\theta,x))\le Cx^c\) for every circle arc of radius \(0<x\le1/2\).

For \(B\subseteq\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), an even dual probability satisfying (1) is exactly a mixture of \(\eta\) and the uniform measures on the forbidden primitive-root orbits.
Consequently, for a nonzero real polynomial \(P\) avoiding those orbits, any strict excess of its restricted coefficient rate above
\[
     \max\left(\{m(P)\}\cup
       \left\{\frac1{\varphi(b)}\sum_{\zeta\ {\rm primitive\ of\ order}\ b}
                                  \log|P(\zeta)|:b\in B\right\}\right)   \tag{2}
\]
requires a maximizing probability whose part away from the exceptional orbits fails (1).
For integral \(P\) the finite-orbit averages are normalized log resultants.
The statement applies to the limiting infima in the [forbidden-order dual formula](Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#dual).

<a id="regularity"></a>
## No atoms off the forbidden orbits and summable logarithmic tests

An allowed primitive root cannot have positive mass because its logarithmic test is bounded above and has value \(-\infty\) there.
Choose a prime \(p\) not dividing any member of \(B\).
For every integer \(j\ge1\) the roots of \(\Phi_p(X^j)\) all have order divisible by \(p\), so its integral logarithm against \(\mu\) is nonnegative by factorization into allowed cyclotomics.
Its logarithm is at most \(\log p\) everywhere on the circle.
If \(z_0\) were a nontorsion atom, its powers could approach a primitive \(p\)-th root arbitrarily closely.
The contribution of this atom would make the integral negative along those powers, a contradiction.
This proves the asserted absence of further atoms.

For the rest of the proof assume (1).
Write \(A(j)=\int\log|1-z^j|\,d\nu(z)\).
For every fixed \(k\), (1) also holds with \(\widehat\nu(k\ell)\) in place of \(\widehat\nu(\ell)\), since \(\tau(\ell)\le\tau(k\ell)\).
The radial logarithm series therefore gives finite \(A(j)\) and
\[
        A(j)=-\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(j\ell)}{\ell},
        \qquad \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|A(kn)|}{n}<\infty .                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the radial integrals converge to the displayed finite series by absolute convergence.
The radial functions are bounded above by \(\log2\), so reverse Fatou makes the boundary integral greater than \(-\infty\).
The lower bound \(\log|1-rz^j|\ge\log|1-z^j|+\frac12\log r\) now gives convergence of the integrals by domination.
The second assertion follows by counting divisors in the absolute double sum.
For \(g_s(z)=\log|\Phi_s(z)|\) define \(I_s(j)=\int g_s(z^j)\,d\nu\).
By the factorization of \(X^s-1\) and finite Möbius inversion, \(I_s(j)\) is a finite integer combination of \(A(dj)\) for \(d\mid s\).
It follows that \(\sum_n |I_s(kn)|/n<\infty\) for all fixed \(k,s\).

<a id="differences"></a>
## Two and three differences remove the exceptional atoms

Fix \(s\) and choose a multiple \(L\) of \(s\) and of all members of \(B\).
Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose pairwise coprime integers \(j_1,j_2,j_3>1\), all congruent to \(1\bmod L\), such that
\[
              |I_s(\prod_{i\in U}j_i)|<\epsilon
                     \quad(\varnothing\ne U\subseteq\{1,2,3\}).
\]
At each of the three choices restrict to integers congruent to one modulo \(L\) times the product of the previously chosen integers.
Their harmonic series diverges.
For the finitely many previous subproducts \(k\), the terms with \(|I_s(kn)|\ge\epsilon\) have finite harmonic sum by (3), which proves the choice is possible.

For \(h=2,3\) consider the multiplicative difference polynomial
\[
       G_h(X)=\prod_{U\subseteq\{1,\ldots,h\}}
        \Phi_s\bigl(X^{\prod_{i\in U}j_i}\bigr)^{(-1)^{h-|U|}} .
\]
For \(J\) coprime to \(s\), the cyclotomic factors of \(\Phi_s(X^J)\) are precisely \(\Phi_{sd}\) for \(d\mid J\), with multiplicity one.
Since the \(j_i\) are pairwise coprime, cancellation therefore leaves exactly the factors \(\Phi_{s d_1\cdots d_h}\) with \(d_i\mid j_i,\ d_i>1\) for every \(i\).
Each such factor is allowed because its order has a prime divisor outside \(L\).
At every forbidden primitive root \(\zeta\) the logarithm of \(|G_h(\zeta)|\) is zero.
If \(s\ne\operatorname{ord}\zeta\), all evaluations in the product are the same nonzero number and cancel.
If \(s=\operatorname{ord}\zeta\), each simple zero gives linear coefficient \(J\Phi_s'(\zeta)\), since \(J\equiv1\bmod L\) for the subproduct substitutions \(X^J\).
The factors \(\Phi_s'(\zeta)\) cancel, as do all factors \(j_i\) because \(h\ge2\).
Thus the dual inequalities give \(\int\log|G_h|\,d\nu\ge0\), with no contribution from the removed atoms.
All integrals in the expanded difference against \(\nu\) are finite.
Using \(h=2\) bounds \(I_s(1)\) below by \(-3\epsilon\), and using \(h=3\) bounds it above by \(7\epsilon\).
Hence \(I_s(1)=0\) for every \(s\ge1\).
If \(\nu\ne0\), the [cyclotomic moment characterization](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement) applied to its normalization proves the conclusion about symmetrization.
The case \(\nu=0\) is immediate.

<a id="objective"></a>
## Conditions that exclude any excess over the orbit tests

For a square-integrable density, Parseval gives a finite sum of squared Fourier coefficients.
Cauchy–Schwarz proves (1) since \(\sum \tau(n)^2/n^2<\infty\) by the [divisor square-sum estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#fourier).
For the power bound on arc masses, decrease \(c\) if necessary to have \(0<c<1\).
The Fejér argument at the same locator gives \(\sum_{n\le N}|\widehat\nu(n)|^2\ll_\nu N^{1-c}\).
The divisor square-sum and Cauchy–Schwarz on a dyadic block bound its contribution to (1) by \(O_\nu(N^{-c/2}(1+\log(2N))^{3/2})\), a summable bound.

If every forbidden order is in \(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), its primitive roots form one conjugation orbit of size one or two.
Evenness therefore makes the removed mass on each such orbit uniform.
Conversely Haar measure and each of the uniform forbidden-orbit measures satisfy all allowed tests, the latter by the nonzero integral resultant.
The set of even feasible probabilities is compact by upper semicontinuity of each moment, and the upper semicontinuous objective \(\int\log|P|\) attains its supremum there since Haar has finite value.
If that supremum exceeds (2), no maximizing probability can have a residual part satisfying (1).
No unconditional exclusion of such a residual probability is obtained here.
