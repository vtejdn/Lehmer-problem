    E=[E_2 E_3], F=VE=[p(A^2) p(A^3)], T=E^T E,
    J=det(FF^T), C=e_d(T), K=product of the d largest eigenvalues ofT,
    B_d=binom(2d,d), H=det(F T^(-1) F^T).

Every original powered conjugate is retained.
Both F blocks are nonsingular integral prime multiples, E has a unit maximal2d minor, and

    J>=13^d, K<=C<=B_d K, K>=sqrt(detT)>=1,
    C>=B_d sqrt(detT)>=B_d, J<=GK<=GC.

    m(p)>=[logDelta+logJ-logC-dlog(4d)]/[2(4d-1)].

## PartI: exact arithmetic floor, full mixed-minor cost and bare-floor loss

<a id="joint-frobenius-minor-test"></a>
### One next discriminating task: the actual two-prime mixed-minor surplus

Use the original companion lattice, so the domain no longer requires Perron dominance, bounded shortness, bounded repair, or bounded rank.
Let p be monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic in Z[t], with p(0)=1, degree d>=2, and companion matrix A in GL_d(Z).
In the target test additionally impose the fixed window 1<M(p)<13/10 and deg(alpha^ell)=d for every prime ell, exactly the endpoint condition proved in L G0014.
No stronger assertion for every composite exponent is needed here.
The formulas in this section do not need that power-degree condition; it is needed below when using the retained discriminant-escape implication.

Write Delta=|disc p| and call the discriminant parameter

    D_disc=d/Delta^(1/d).

This D_disc is unrelated to the canonical repair degree D(p).
For ell=2,3 let

    F_ell=p(A^ell).

The complete local proof in [C217v2](Yin_C_G0217_v2.md#argument) gives F_ell=ell B_ell with B_ell integral and nonsingular.
Frobenius proves integrality.
A zero determinant would give alpha_i^ell=alpha_j for conjugates of p; iteration of a finite-order Galois automorphism would then make alpha_i a root of unity.
Thus

    |det F_2|>=2^d,            |det F_3|>=3^d.                 (1)

These statements retain the actual d powered conjugates even when their degree drops; no degree-loss multiplicity is removed.

The joint determinant has the exact lower floor

    J(p):=det(F_2 F_2^T+F_3 F_3^T) >=13^d.                  (2)

Here is a direct proof, avoiding an additional source input.
If X,Y are positive definite d by d matrices, let lambda_i>0 be the eigenvalues of X^(-1/2)Y X^(-1/2).
Convexity of log(1+exp x) gives

    det(X+Y)^(1/d)
      >=det(X)^(1/d)+det(Y)^(1/d).

Indeed det(X+Y)=det(X) product_i(1+lambda_i), and the geometric mean of the last factors is at least 1+(product_i lambda_i)^(1/d).
Apply this to X=F_2 F_2^T and Y=F_3 F_3^T, and then use (1), giving 4+9=13.

The exact coefficient cost must be retained.
Put N=4d and let V_N have columns t^j mod p for 0<=j<N, as in L G0004.
Let E_ell be the N by d matrix whose j-th column is the coefficient vector of t^j p(t^ell), with 0<=j<d and zero padding.
Both ell=2 and ell=3 fit this window.
Set E=[E_2 E_3], an N by 2d matrix.
Then

    V_N E=[F_2 F_3],           G_N=det(V_N V_N^T).

One entirely integral, explicitly computable upper-comparison cost is

    C(p)=sum_(|I|=|K|=d) det(E[I,K])^2,                     (3)

where I ranges over row subsets of size d and K over column subsets of size d.
Equivalently C(p) is the coefficient of z^d in det(I_(2d)+z E^T E).
Cauchy--Binet followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, first for each K and then summing over K, gives exactly

    J(p)<=G_N C(p).                                         (4)

For clarity, the summand is det((V_N E)[:,K])=sum_I det(V_N[:,I]) det(E[I,K]), and sum_I det(V_N[:,I])^2=G_N.
No independence of the two prime blocks or omission of their mixed minors is assumed.
Also C(p)>=1: E_2 has a unit determinant minor on rows 2d,...,3d-1, since these leading coefficient rows are triangular with diagonal one.
A sharper valid cost is the product of the d largest squared singular values of E; using it would require its full comparison and evaluation, rather than silently dropping (3).

Combine (4) with the retained Vandermonde entropy bound Delta G_N<=N^d exp(2(N-1)m(p)).
This gives the actual sufficient certificate

    m(p) >= [log Delta+log J(p)-log C(p)-d log(4d)]
                 /[2(4d-1)].                              (5)

The precise new test is whether the full arithmetic J(p), after paying the entire coefficient cost C(p), can provide a uniform positive normalized surplus on the stated window.
For example an explicit c>0 and a proof

    Delta J(p)/[(4d)^d C(p)] >= exp(c d)                     (6)

throughout that domain would give m(p)>c/8.
Together with the already proved degree-reducing power descent and the nonreciprocal/unit reductions, this would address the original all-degree gap.
Equation (6) is an unproved, stronger finite certificate target; it is not asserted to follow from Lehmer or from positivity of a finite minor.
It may fail on an actual positive-entropy input without refuting Lehmer.
In particular a failed retained control would reject this specific two-prime cost, not the true entropy inequality (5).

<a id="bare-joint-prime-loss"></a>
### A proved gate: the bare 13^d floor does not remove the degree loss

Inserting only (2) into (5) supplies

    [d log(13/(4 D_disc))-log C(p)]/[2(4d-1)].               (7)

Because C(p)>=1, its numerator is at most d log(13/(4 D_disc)).
Once D_disc>13/4, this particular sufficient bound is nonpositive.
The retained [L G0015](Yin_C_G0219_v1.md#discriminant-escape) proves that any hypothetical sequence preserving degree under every map alpha -> alpha^ell, ell prime, with 0<m(p)->0 must have D_disc->infinity.
Therefore the bare two-prime floor supplies no positive certificate on precisely that hypothetical remaining branch.
This is a failure of the lower estimate obtained from divisibility alone; it is not a counterexample to (6), nor an assertion that a vanishing-entropy sequence exists.

This makes the next investigation concrete: determine whether the actual mixed-minor excess in J(p), using original integer recurrence data, offsets both D_disc and C(p), or prove a structural obstruction to this proposed cost.
Begin with the retained exact controls as checks of the formula, then require an all-degree argument rather than another parameter scan.
A proof of (6), or a properly quantified weaker certificate with the same uniform entropy consequence, is a success.
Recovery only of (7), a per-degree constant, or an assumed bound on D_disc, shortness or expanding-root count is a failure of this mechanism and should close that bounded attempt.

The overlap with earlier work is explicit.
L G0012 supplied the two individual Frobenius maps and identified their coefficient-Gram cost; L G0015 exposed discriminant escape; L G0016 permits a finite choice of a good prime under bounded discriminant and expanding count.
The new proposed test assumes neither of those bounds, keeps mixed minors of two actual prime maps, and must improve their normalized arithmetic gain.
Merely multiplying separate resultant inequalities would repeat the old degree-dependent mechanism.
The elementary floor (2) and the loss calculation (7) already show why a formal stacking argument is insufficient.

## PartII: complete exterior and fixed coefficient-space optimization

<a id="joint-prime-exterior-cost"></a>
### Coefficient rank and the optimal exterior cost

Use exactly the setup of L G0054.
Let p be monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic in Z[t], of degree d>=2 and constant coefficient one.
Let A be its original companion matrix, N=4d, and V=V_N the d by N matrix with columns t^j mod p for 0<=j<N in the power basis.
Set

    F_ell=p(A^ell),       F=[F_2 F_3],
    E=[E_2 E_3],         VE=F,
    W=VV^T,              G_N=det W,
    J=det(FF^T),         T=E^T E.

The j-th column of E_ell is the coefficient vector of t^j p(t^ell), 0<=j<d, padded to length N.
The retained Frobenius argument makes F_2 and F_3 nonsingular.
The first d columns of V are the identity, so W is positive definite.
None of the arguments below requires the additional small-measure or power-degree hypotheses of the proposed uniform test.

The 2d by 2d submatrix of E on rows 2d,...,4d-1 is block upper triangular.
Its E_2 block on rows 2d,...,3d-1 and its E_3 block on rows 3d,...,4d-1 are triangular with diagonal one, and E_2 vanishes on the last d rows.
Thus this maximal minor has determinant one.
In particular E has rank 2d and det T is a positive integer at least one.

Write the eigenvalues of T as lambda_1>=...>=lambda_(2d)>0, and put

    C=C_d(E)=e_d(T)
      =sum_(|I|=|K|=d) det(E[I,K])^2,
    K=K_d(E)=product_(i=1)^d lambda_i,
    B_d=binom(2d,d).

The notation K_d denotes a scalar exterior operator cost, not the polynomial relation matrix of L G0004.
The singular values of the d-th exterior power of E are precisely the square roots of the d-fold eigenvalue products.
Consequently

    K=||exterior^d E||_op^2,
    K<=C<=B_d K,
    K>=sqrt(det T)>=1,
    C>=B_d sqrt(det T)>=B_d.                            (1)

For the third inequality compare the product of the largest d eigenvalues to that of the smallest d.
For the fourth, each eigenvalue occurs in binom(2d-1,d-1) of the B_d products, so their geometric mean is sqrt(det T); apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
These estimates keep all mixed minors.

The d-th exterior power of a d-row full-rank matrix V has squared operator norm det(VV^T).
Applying operator submultiplicativity to VE gives the sharper comparison

    J<=G_N K<=G_N C.                                   (2)

K is the least constant depending only on E for which the first inequality holds for every real full-rank d by N matrix V: choose orthonormal rows of V along the d leading left singular vectors of E.
This characterization does not assert that the original companion V has that row space.

In the entropy certificate with N=4d, replacing C by K improves the lower estimate by exactly log(C/K)/[2(4d-1)].
Its possible size obeys

    0<=log(C/K)/[2(4d-1)]
       <=log B_d/[2(4d-1)] -> (log 2)/4.                (3)

This is a bound on the difference between two sufficient estimates, not a new lower bound on entropy.

<a id="coefficient-subspace-optimum"></a>
### Exact optimization over every column choice in the fixed space

Let S=col(E), a 2d-dimensional subspace of R^N.
Its orthogonal projection and the compressed orbit Gram matrix are

    P_S=E T^(-1) E^T,
    T_S=V P_S V^T=F T^(-1) F^T,
    H=det T_S>0.                                       (4)

Positivity follows because F has row rank d.
We claim that H is the exact maximum of both normalized certificates when all auxiliary columns are required to lie in S, with their number and images otherwise unrestricted.

Precisely, let L be any real N by m matrix, m>=d, all of whose columns lie in S, such that VL has row rank d.
Define

    J_L=det(VL L^T V^T),
    C_d(L)=sum_(|I|=|K|=d) det(L[I,K])^2,
    K_d(L)=product of the d largest squared singular values of L.

Then

    J_L<=H K_d(L)<=H C_d(L).                            (5)

To prove this, choose an N by 2d matrix U whose columns are an orthonormal basis of S, and write L=U R and B=VU.
We have BB^T=T_S.
Isometric multiplication by U leaves singular values and the minor-square sum unchanged.
Therefore

    ||exterior^d(VL)||_op^2
       <=||exterior^d B||_op^2 ||exterior^d R||_op^2
       =H K_d(L),

which is (5).
This proof allows any finite number m of columns, arbitrary dependencies among them and arbitrary real weights.

Both bounds are attained by the same d-column matrix

    L_*=P_S V^T.

Indeed VL_*=T_S and L_*^T L_*=T_S.
Thus

    J_(L_*)=H^2,       C_d(L_*)=K_d(L_*)=H.

Consequently

    max_(cols(L) in S, rank(VL)=d) J_L/C_d(L)
      =max_(cols(L) in S, rank(VL)=d) J_L/K_d(L)=H.      (6)

The maximum is unchanged if one restricts to rational L, since E and V are integral and L_*=E T^(-1) F^T is rational.
It is also unchanged if one restricts to integral linear combinations of the original columns of E, without fixing the image VL.
A common positive integer multiple of T^(-1) F^T is integral.
Multiplying L_* by this integer multiplies J_L, C_d(L) and K_d(L) by the same factor to the power 2d, so their ratios remain H.
Thus integrality alone does not improve the unrestricted-image ceiling in (6).

For comparison, if the original image F is fixed, its rational lift of minimum cost inside S is

    L_0=P_S V^T T_S^(-1) F,
    VL_0=F,
    C_d(L_0)=K_d(L_0)=J/H.                              (7)

To verify the cost, L_0 has rank d and L_0^T L_0=F^T T_S^(-1) F.
The product of its nonzero eigenvalues is det(T_S^(-1/2)FF^T T_S^(-1/2))=J/H.
Inequality (5) shows that no smaller cost is possible.
This rational minimizer need not be integral at the fixed image F; clearing denominators changes that image.
No claim of uniqueness for the operator-cost minimizer is needed here.
The general fixed-image minimum over the entire ambient coefficient space, and its integral equality condition, are the distinct results of the [projection calculation](#unique-minimum-lift).

Applying (5) to the original E, and using (1), gives

    J/C<=J/K<=H<=G_N.                                   (8)

The last inequality also has an exact geometric explanation.
Put Q=V^T W^(-1/2), so Q^T Q=I_d.
Then

    H/G_N=det(Q^T P_S Q),       0<H/G_N<=1.              (9)

The symmetric matrix Q^T P_S Q is positive definite and at most I_d.
Its eigenvalues measure how much of each orthonormal row direction of V survives projection into S; their product is the displayed loss.
No dimension-independent lower bound for this product is proved.

<a id="original-gram-certificate-boundary"></a>
### What the optimum means for the original entropy bound

The retained inequality in [L G0004](Yin_C_G0209_v1.md#gram-lower-certificate) is

    m(p)>=[log(Delta G_N)-d log N]/[2(N-1)],
    Delta=|disc p|.

Substituting any normalized column certificate J_L/C_d(L) or J_L/K_d(L), with its columns in S, gives a lower estimate no larger than

    [log(Delta H)-d log N]/[2(N-1)]
      =[log(Delta G_N)-d log N]/[2(N-1)]
          +log(H/G_N)/[2(N-1)].                         (10)

Thus optimization inside the entire two-prime coefficient space still retains the nonpositive projection term (9).
The value H is invariant under any invertible change of the 2d displayed coefficient columns, with the corresponding change of F.
In particular scaling the separate prime blocks cannot create an additional gain after this exact optimization.

Optimizing rational lifts in the full ambient space instead yields the original G_N, as proved in the separate projection calculation.
This is recovery of the known Gram certificate, not a new entropy mechanism.
A new arithmetic argument may still prove that this original Gram determinant has sufficient uniform surplus.
Nothing here rules that out, and no assertion is made about all collections of primes, all time windows, or all arithmetic entropy methods.

By (6), every choice of auxiliary columns confined to this full two-prime coefficient space fails even positivity of the normalized finite certificate, for either the full minor-square cost or the exterior operator cost.
This includes arbitrary mixing, scaling, choosing different numbers of columns, and arbitrary integral combinations; it is not just a failure of the original generating matrix or of an estimate on its singular values.

On exactly the same input the original Gram certificate satisfies

    Delta G_40/40^10

The projected two-prime span therefore omits directions that matter to this finite certificate.
Equations (11)--(13) refute the proposed universal positive target of L G0054, even with the specified within-space optimizations.
They do not refute the true inequalities (2), (5) or (10), and do not supply an upper bound contradicting positive algebraic entropy.

## PartIII: complete ambient projection, unique lift and integral boundary

<a id="projection-cost-identity"></a>
### The exact normalized-row identity

Let V be a real d by N matrix of full row rank, with N>=d.
Let E be N by m, where m>=d, and suppose F=VE has row rank d.
Put

    W=VV^T,       G=det W,
    J=det(FF^T),
    C(E)=sum_(|I|=|H|=d) det(E[I,H])^2=e_d(E^T E).

Here e_d is the d-th elementary symmetric function of the eigenvalues; I and H are row and column subsets.
Define the normalized row matrix and the orthogonal projection

    U=W^(-1/2)V,       UU^T=I_d,
    Pi=U^T U=V^T W^(-1)V.

The rows of U form an orthonormal basis of the row space of V.
Extend them to an orthonormal basis of R^N.
In these coordinates E takes the block form

    E -> [ B ; Z ],       B=UE=W^(-1/2)F.

Projection replaces Z by zero, while preserving B.
Cauchy--Binet in the d by m matrix B gives

    C(Pi E)=det(BB^T)=J/G.                              (1)

All the terms counted by C(Pi E) are also present among the nonnegative minor-square terms of C(E) in these orthonormal coordinates.
The cost is unchanged by an orthogonal change of row coordinates.
Therefore

    J/C(E)=G eta(E),
    eta(E):=C(Pi E)/C(E),       0<eta(E)<=1.             (2)

This is an exact factorization of the normalized certificate into the actual orbit Gram determinant and a nonpositive logarithmic projection loss.
It uses the full mixed-minor sum, without independence assumptions on the column blocks.

<a id="unique-minimum-lift"></a>
### The projected representative is the unique minimum-cost lift

Fix V and F with the preceding ranks.
Every real lift E' satisfying VE'=F has the same first block B=W^(-1/2)F and an arbitrary second block Z.
Equation (1) shows that

    E_0=V^T W^(-1)F

attains cost J/G.
It is the unique minimizing lift.

To check strictness, suppose Z has a nonzero row z.
The d rows b_1,...,b_d of B are linearly independent because F has row rank d.
There is a choice of d-1 of these rows whose wedge with z is nonzero.
If z is outside their span, any d-1 suffice.
If z lies in their span, omit a row having nonzero coefficient in its expansion.
This also covers d=1 by the empty wedge convention.
At least one d by d column minor of these d selected rows is nonzero.
Its square is an additional positive term in C(E'), distinct from the terms using only B.
Hence

    E'!=E_0  =>  C(E')>J/G.                             (3)

In particular,

    min_(VE'=F, E' real) C(E')=J/G,
    max_(VE'=F, E' real) J/C(E')=G.                     (4)

If V and F are rational, E_0 is rational, so the same minima and maxima hold over rational lifts.
Thus relaxing the coefficient representatives to rational ones cancels the entire arithmetic numerator exactly.
It does not create a new entropy estimate.

<a id="polynomial-relation-lifts"></a>
### The companion relation lattice and integral boundary

For a monic polynomial p of degree d and N>=d, take V=V_N with columns t^j mod p, 0<=j<N, in the power basis.
Its first d columns are the identity.
The relation matrix K consists of the coefficient columns

    p,tp,...,t^(N-d-1)p.

The exact L G0004 interface is

    ker_Z(V)=K Z^(N-d),       det(K^T K)=G_N.

The same columns span the real and rational kernels.
Thus every real, rational or integral lift of the original F=VE is respectively

    E+K H

with H over the same scalar domain.
In polynomial language, these are precisely the modifications of the coefficient columns by multiples of p within the fixed degree window.
Moreover

    E_0=Pi E=E-K(K^T K)^(-1)K^T E.                      (5)

The cost-minimizing operation is therefore projection along the actual polynomial relation space, rather than an unrelated matrix relaxation.

If V,F are integral, W and its adjugate are integral and

    E_0=(1/G_N)V^T adj(W)F.

Projection denominators divide G_N.
They need not disappear: a rational representative of minimal cost is not automatically an admissible integral representative.
Integral lifts exist because the original E is one.
Their costs are positive integers, so the minimum integral cost exists.
By the strictness proof,

    min_(VE'=F, E' integral) C(E')=J/G_N
       if and only if E_0 is integral.                  (6)

If E_0 is not integral, every integral lift has strictly larger cost and the minimum is at least floor(J/G_N)+1.
This is the remaining discrete optimization boundary, not a proof of a positive entropy gap.

There are two useful exact endpoints.
Reducing each column to its degree-less-than-d remainder gives the integral lift

    E_rem=[F;0],       C(E_rem)=J,

so its ratio J/C is exactly one: the joint arithmetic determinant cancels completely in this particular short representative.
Alternatively, multiplying F by G_N clears the denominators of E_0.
For the scaled numerator G_N F, the integral lift G_N E_0 then has ratio exactly G_N.
The numerator and cost both scale by G_N^(2d), so clearing denominators still recovers only the old Gram quantity.

<a id="joint-prime-gram-ceiling"></a>
### Consequence for the proposed two-prime certificate

In L G0054 take N=4d and

    E=[E_2 E_3],       F=[p(A^2) p(A^3)].

Both arithmetic maps are nonsingular by the retained Frobenius argument, so the hypotheses above hold.
The actual E has column rank 2d.
Indeed a relation among its columns is a polynomial identity

    p(t^2)u(t)+p(t^3)v(t)=0,       deg u,deg v<d.

If v is nonzero, its summand has degree at least 3d, whereas the first summand has degree at most 3d-1, which is impossible.
Thus v=0 and then u=0.
Since Pi E has rank d, E differs from Pi E.
The strict minimum result therefore gives

    J/C(E)<G_(4d).                                      (7)

No degree-uniform size of this strict loss follows merely from the rank argument.

Let Delta=|disc p|.
The proposed finite entropy lower bound is exactly

    [log Delta+log J-log C(E)-d log N]/[2(N-1)]
      = [log(Delta G_N)-d log N]/[2(N-1)]
           + log eta(E)/[2(N-1)].                       (8)

The first term is the already proved direct Gram certificate of L G0004.
The second is nonpositive, and is strictly negative for the displayed two-prime coefficient matrix.
Thus evaluating the complete arithmetic maps and their full coefficient cost cannot improve that direct certificate pointwise at the same N.
Optimizing arbitrary rational coefficient lifts attains equality with the old certificate; using integral lifts can leave an additional loss.

This projection identity alone does not refute the arithmetic target proposed in L G0054.
The separate [exact control in L G0055](#lehmer-joint-prime-control) does refute that universal two-prime target on its stated domain, even after optimizing the entire two-prime coefficient space.
Another arithmetic estimate on the original G_N, or a justified change of maps or domain, could still establish a uniform positive Gram surplus.
The result identifies the obligation: arithmetic has to prove that surplus while controlling the projection loss.
It cannot obtain an additional independent prime surplus simply by normalizing the row space or by treating the projected cost as independent of the numerator.
The exact identities hold for any finite collection of full-rank coefficient maps at the fixed window, not only primes two and three; this generality does not supply the missing arithmetic bound or reopen the refuted fixed-space target.
