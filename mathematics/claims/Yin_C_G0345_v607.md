# Deciding whether an integer numerator removes the neutral specialization loss

<a id="statement"></a>
## The decision problem

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2h>0\), with no root on the unit circle.
Choose the sign of \(u=\pm X^{-h}P\) so that \(u>0\) on the circle, and write \(u_k=[X^k]u\).
There is a terminating procedure to decide whether a nonzero integer polynomial \(A\) satisfies \(|A|^2\le4u\) throughout the circle.
An affirmative answer supplies such a polynomial, necessarily coprime to \(P\), and hence the [lossless neutral bound](Yin_C_G0345_v605.md#bound) \(\gamma\le m(P)\).
A negative answer supplies a finite depth at which an explicitly bounded list of integer coefficient prefixes is empty after the tests below.
The bounds control the squared coefficient sum and the number of nonzero coefficients independently of degree.
The search also computes an input-dependent degree bound for every certificate having no shorter passing prefix after the initial monomial is removed.

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Finite tests on coefficient prefixes

Remove any initial power of \(X\) from \(A\), which preserves its modulus on the circle, and write \(A=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kX^k\) with \(a_0\ne0\) and \(a_k=0\) for \(k<0\).
Integration against normalized Haar measure gives \(\sum_k a_k^2\le4u_0\), so there are at most \(4u_0\) nonzero integer coefficients and each has magnitude at most \(\lfloor\sqrt{4u_0}\rfloor\).
At depth \(n\ge1\) enumerate all integer prefixes \((a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})\) with \(a_0\ne0\) and \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_k^2\le4u_0\).
Keep a prefix precisely when
\[
  4T_n(u)-L_n L_n^*\succeq0,\qquad
  T_n(u)_{ij}=u_{i-j},\qquad (L_n)_{ij}=a_{i-j}\quad(0\le i,j<n).
\]
Every entry uses only the listed prefix, and positive semidefiniteness is decidable by all principal minors of this integer matrix.
For a full polynomial write \(B=A(X)A(X^{-1})\) and define \(T_n(B)\) by the same coefficient convention.
Its \((i,j)\) entry is \(\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}a_{i-r}a_{j-r}\), a Gram sum of outer products over columns \(r\).
Columns with \(r\ge n\) vanish and columns \(0\le r<n\) give exactly \(L_nL_n^*\).
Thus \(T_n(B)\succeq L_nL_n^*\), and the integral of \((4u-B)|p|^2\) for polynomials \(p\) of degree below \(n\) gives \(4T_n(u)\succeq T_n(B)\) whenever the pointwise bound holds.
An empty list at any depth therefore excludes every numerator.

<a id="complete"></a>
## Why finite exclusion is complete

Deleting the last coefficient preserves both the energy bound and the matrix test because the leading principal block is the shorter tested matrix.
If every depth has a surviving prefix, these prefixes and their common empty root form an infinite finitely branching tree, hence have an infinite branch.
Every nonzero entry costs at least one in the bounded sum of squares, so this branch is the coefficient sequence of a polynomial of some degree \(d\), with \(a_0\ne0\).
Fix \(m\ge1\) and use the branch test at depth \(n=d+m\).
In the block with row and column indices \(d,\ldots,d+m-1\), the omitted Gram columns \(r<0\) vanish because \(i-r>d\).
Toeplitz translation therefore identifies that principal block with \(T_m(4u-B)\), which is positive semidefinite for every \(m\).
For any \(z\) with \(|z|=1\), its quadratic form on the vector with entries \(z^{-j}/\sqrt m\) is the Fejér sum \(\sum_{|k|<m}(1-|k|/m)[X^k](4u-B)z^k\).
These finite Laurent sums tend to \((4u-B)(z)\) since the symbol is a fixed Laurent polynomial.
The pointwise bound follows on the whole circle.

<a id="coprimality"></a>
## Extremality excludes a common factor

If \(A\) and \(P\) shared a factor, irreducibility and monicity would give \(A=PQ\) with \(0\ne Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Since \(|P|=u\) on the circle, the assumed bound gives \(0\le w\le4\) there for the symmetric integral Laurent polynomial \(w=uQ(X)Q(X^{-1})\).
If \(Q\) has smallest and largest nonzero exponents \(l,s\), then \(w\) has top exponent \(n=h+s-l>0\) with nonzero integer coefficient.
Consequently \(V=2-w\) has the same top exponent, has equal outer coefficients \(v_n=v_{-n}\ne0\), and takes real values in \([-2,2]\) on the circle.
At the nodes \(z_j=e^{\pi i j/n}\), discrete orthogonality gives
\[
  \frac{1}{2n}\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^j V(z_j)=v_n+v_{-n}=2v_n.
\]
Every summand before averaging lies in \([-2,2]\), so \(|v_n|=1\) and equality at the endpoint forces \(V(z_j)=2v_n(-1)^j\) for every \(j\).
The Laurent difference \(V-v_n(X^n+X^{-n})\) has bandwidth at most \(n-1\) and vanishes at these \(2n\) distinct nodes, hence is identically zero after multiplying by \(X^{n-1}\) and comparing degrees.
Thus \(X^nw=-v_n(X^n-v_n)^2\) has only roots of unity, whereas every nonzero root of \(P\) is a root of \(w\) and lies off the circle.
This contradiction proves coprimality.

<a id="termination"></a>
## Bounding the next nonzero coefficient

Write \(E=4u_0\) and start one branch at each nonzero integer constant \(a_0\) with \(a_0^2\le E\).
Each node is a candidate polynomial \(H\) of degree \(d\) with nonzero last coefficient, together with the requirement that all its coefficients through \(d\) remain fixed in descendants.
Test \(4u-H(X)H(X^{-1})\ge0\) on the circle by expressing the symmetric integral Laurent polynomial as an integer polynomial in \(t=X+X^{-1}\).
Evaluate at both endpoints and all real critical points of \([-2,2]\) by exact real algebraic arithmetic, treating a constant polynomial directly.
A passing node supplies a certificate and ends its branch.
A failing node whose coefficients exhaust the energy budget has no descendants.

At any other failed node pad the coefficients of \(H\) by zeros and find the first \(n\ge d+2\) at which this padded prefix fails the matrix test.
This search terminates because the symbol \(4u-H(X)H(X^{-1})\) is strictly negative somewhere.
Its Fejér sums at such a point are eventually negative, and the translated block at depth \(d+m\) realizes the corresponding order-\(m\) Toeplitz matrix exactly, as in the [completeness proof](#complete).
Any certificate extending the failed node must therefore have its next nonzero coefficient at an exponent \(j\) with \(d<j<n\), since otherwise its depth-\(n\) test uses the identical failed prefix.
Create children \(H+bX^j\) for every such \(j\) and every nonzero integer \(b\) whose square fits the remaining energy, and apply the same procedure to each child.

Each node has a computable finite list of children and each step adds at least one to the integral energy, so every branch has at most \(E\) nonzero coefficients and the whole tree is finite.
For every normalized certificate, follow its successive nonzero terms until the first passing prefix.
The branching rule includes each required exponent and coefficient, hence visits that first passing prefix.
Thus exhausting the tree decides existence, and the maximum degree of any visited node bounds the degree of every certificate with no shorter passing prefix.
Take the bound to be zero when there are no initial nodes.
In particular this gives a computable per-input bound for a shortest certificate whenever one exists, without an asymptotic estimate for the bound.
If a single empty prefix level is desired after a negative answer, the [completeness proof](#complete) guarantees that successive level enumeration finds one in finite time.
