# Heights of rational functions of the trace of an algebraic number

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree bound for outputs below the totally real height bound

Let \(x\ne0\) be algebraic of degree \(d\ge2\), with primitive integer minimal polynomial \(P\), conjugates \(x_j=e^{t_j+i\theta_j}\), and \(m=\log M(P)=d h(x)\).
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Define
\[
 \Delta=1+\frac{2(d-1)}{d}m+\log d-\frac1d\log|\operatorname{disc}P|,
 \qquad T=\frac12\sum_{j:\,x_j\notin\mathbb R}|t_j|.
\]
Then \(\Delta\ge1\) and \(0\le T\le m\).
Put \(\tau=x+x^{-1}\), let \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\) be a nonconstant rational function of degree \(k\), and suppose \(y=R(\tau)\) is defined and different from \(0,1,-1\).
With \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2,\ a=2/\varphi,\ c=\varphi^{-(a+1)}\), there is an absolute constant \(C\) such that
\[
     e^{-(2a+2)h(y)}
        \le c+C\sqrt{\frac{k(\Delta+T)}{d}} .                       \tag{1}
\]
The proof applies to rational functions varying with the input, including arbitrary rational coefficients and poles arbitrarily close to the trace interval.
The output may have any signature and need not be an algebraic integer.

Consequently if \(k(\Delta+T)=o(d)\), then \(\liminf h(y)\ge\tfrac12\log\varphi\).
For bounded \(m\) this applies whenever \(k=o(d/\log(2d))\), since \(|\operatorname{disc}P|\ge1\).
For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), an output with \(h(y)\le\tfrac12\log\varphi-\epsilon\) requires \(k\gg_\epsilon d/(\Delta+T)\).
At bounded \(m\) and bounded \(\log d-d^{-1}\log|\operatorname{disc}P|\) the required degree is therefore linear.
Moreover \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\ge d/(2k)\), since \(R(\tau)=y\) gives a nonzero equation of degree at most \(k\) for \(\tau\) over \(\mathbb Q(y)\), and \(x^2-\tau x+1=0\).
In the range \(k(\Delta+T)=o(d)\) this gives \(\log M(y)\gg d/k\) eventually, with the measure of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(y\).
The exceptional values \(0,1,-1\), or a pole at \(\tau\), already require \(k\ge d/2\) by the rational numerator or denominator equation.
For reciprocal units the result applies even when the trace field has no real embedding, as in the [example below](#example).
The [general rational-function degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v77.md#statement) allows functions outside the trace field and assumes bounded total output measure in its uniform degree consequence.

<a id="product"></a>
## Product formula with a quantitative error for a nonreal conjugate

For \(z\in\mathbb C\) let \(\chi(z)=|z-\bar z|/(1+|z|^2)\), with \(\chi(\infty)=0\).
This is the chordal distance from \(z\) to its conjugate, between zero and one.
At a place \(v\) define
\[
       G_v(z)=\frac{|z|_v^a|z^2-1|_v}{\max(1,|z|_v)^{2a+2}} .
\]
At every nonarchimedean place \(G_v(z)\le1\), by treating \(|z|_v\le1\) and \(|z|_v>1\) separately.
At an archimedean place put \(r=\min(|z|,|z|^{-1})\) and \(\psi=\arg z\).
The squared modulus gives
\[
 G_v(z)^2=r^{2a}(1-r^2)^2+4r^{2a+2}\sin^2\psi .
\]
The maximum of \(r^a(1-r^2)\) on \([0,1]\) occurs at \(r^2=a/(a+2)=\varphi^{-2}\) and equals \(c\).
Also \(r^{a+1}|\sin\psi|\le 2r|\sin\psi|/(1+r^2)=\chi(z)\).
The product formula for \(y,y^2-1\) gives \(\prod_v G_v(y)^{n_v}=e^{-(2a+2)h(y)}\) with normalized local degrees \(n_v\).
Dropping the finite factors and taking the arithmetic mean over the \(d\) embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) proves
\[
       e^{-(2a+2)h(y)}
          \le c+\frac2d\sum_j\chi\bigl(R(x_j+x_j^{-1})\bigr).         \tag{2}
\]
Outputs in a proper subfield occur with their common extension multiplicity in this average.

<a id="strip"></a>
## A radial estimate for rational functions real on the circle

Write \(W(z)=R(z+z^{-1})\).
It has degree \(2k\) as a rational map and \(W(1/\bar z)=\overline{W(z)}\), with values taken on the sphere.
The Cayley transform \(V=(W-i)/(W+i)\) has modulus one everywhere on the unit circle, including at a pole of \(W\) by continuation.
It has no circle zeros or poles.
After taking out one disk factor \(B_b(z)=(z-b)/(1-\bar b z)\) for each zero \(b\) in the disk and one inverse factor for each disk pole, the remaining function and its inverse are holomorphic in the closed disk and have unit boundary modulus.
They are therefore unimodular constants by the maximum principle.
The resulting list of factors counted with multiplicity has length at most \(4k\), since both the number of zeros and the number of poles are at most \(2k\).

One has \(\chi(W)=|\tanh(\log|V|)|\), by squaring the moduli of \(W-i,W+i\).
In the disk every \(B_b\) has modulus at most one.
Subadditivity of \(\tanh\) on \([0,\infty)\) and \(\tanh(u)\le1-e^{-2u}\) there give
\[
                  \chi(W(z))\le\sum_b(1-|B_b(z)|^2).
\]
The bound extends to disk zeros and poles of \(V\) by continuity.
By reflection it suffices to estimate \(z=e^{-t+i\theta}\) for \(0\le t\le\delta\), where \(0<\delta\le1\).
Use \(P_s(\omega)=(1-s^2)/|1-s e^{i\omega}|^2\) for the Poisson kernel, normalized to integral one.
Write \(b=e^{-v+i\gamma}\), with \(v=\infty\) and an arbitrary angle for \(b=0\), and \(s_b=\exp(-\max(v,\delta))\).
Uniformly in the strip,
\[
            1-|B_b(z)|^2\le C_0\delta P_{s_b}(\theta-\gamma).        \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the exact defect has numerator \((1-e^{-2t})(1-e^{-2v})\) and denominator \(|1-e^{-(t+v)+i(\theta-\gamma)}|^2\).
For \(v\ge\delta\), the denominator is at least \(\tfrac14|1-e^{-v+i(\theta-\gamma)}|^2\), since \(1-e^{-t}\le1-e^{-v}\le |1-e^{-(t+v)+i(\theta-\gamma)}|\).
This proves (3) in that range, including \(b=0\).
For \(v<\delta\) and circular distance \(\omega=|\theta-\gamma|_{\mathbb T}\le\delta\), the left side is at most one and \(\delta P_{e^{-\delta}}(\omega)\gg1\).
For \(\delta<\omega\le\pi\), the numerator is \(O(\delta^2)\), the denominator is \(\gg\omega^2\) since \(t+v\le2\), and \(\delta P_{e^{-\delta}}(\omega)\asymp\delta^2/\omega^2\).

Set \(w(\theta)=\min(1,C_0\delta\sum_b P_{s_b}(\theta-\gamma_b))\).
Thus \(\chi(W(e^{-t+i\theta}))\le w(\theta)\) throughout the strip.
Writing \(\nu\) for circle probability, one has
\[
                         \int w^2\,d\nu\le\int w\,d\nu\ll k\delta .
\]
For \(|\theta-\theta'|_{\mathbb T}\le\delta\), the values \(w(\theta),w(\theta')\) are comparable with absolute constants.
In fact each Poisson denominator before squaring changes by at most \(\delta\), whereas its modulus is at least \(1-e^{-\delta}\gg\delta\).
Summation and truncation preserve this comparison.

<a id="energy"></a>
## Integration against the conjugate radii and angles

Write \(P(X)=a_d\prod_j(X-x_j)\), \(z_j=e^{-|t_j|+i\theta_j}\), and \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\).
The absolute Vandermonde determinant and Hadamard's row bound give
\[
              |\operatorname{disc}P|\le d^d e^{2(d-1)m},
\]
including the multiplier \(|a_d|^{2d-2}\), so \(\Delta\ge1\).
The sums \(\sum_j\max(t_j,0)\) and \(\sum_j\max(-t_j,0)\) equal \(m-\log|a_d|\) and \(m-\log|P(0)|\), respectively, by the product of the roots.
Both endpoint coefficients are nonzero integers, which gives \(T\le m\).

Retain the actual discriminant in the [reflected energy computation](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases).
For each distinct pair the disk distance inequality there bounds \(|x_i-x_j|\) by \(e^{t_i^++t_j^+}\rho^{-1}|1-\rho^2\bar z_i z_j|\).
Here \(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|x_i-x_j|=\log|\operatorname{disc}P|-2(d-1)\log|a_d|\).
Summing the pair inequalities and bounding diagonal terms separately gives
\[
\begin{split}
 E&=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{e^{-2\ell/d}}{\ell}
                   \left|\sum_j z_j^\ell\right|^2\\
  &\le 2(d-1)m-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|
          +(d-1)+d\log\frac1{1-e^{-2/d}}
   \le d\Delta .
\end{split}                                                         \tag{4}
\]
The series equals \(-\sum_{i,j}\log|1-\rho^2\bar z_i z_j|\) by absolute convergence, and \(1-e^{-2/d}\ge1/d\) for \(d\ge2\).
This deduction permits a primitive nonmonic polynomial, with its leading coefficient included in \(m\) above.

Take \(2/d\le\delta\le1\) and \(H(\theta)=\sum_j P_{\exp(-\delta-|t_j|)}(\theta-\theta_j)\).
It has mean \(d\), and Parseval gives
\[
     \|H-d\|_2^2
       =2\sum_{\ell\ge1}e^{-2\delta\ell}\Big|\sum_jz_j^\ell\Big|^2
       \ll E/\delta\le d\Delta/\delta ,
\]
since \(\sup_{\ell\ge1}\ell e^{-2(\delta-1/d)\ell}\ll1/\delta\).
Each root with \(|t_j|\le\delta\) contributes a Poisson kernel of integral at least an absolute positive constant on \(|\theta-\theta_j|_{\mathbb T}\le\delta\).
Indeed its radial parameter has logarithmic distance in \([\delta,2\delta]\), so its density there is \(\gg1/\delta\).
The local comparison for \(w\), positivity and Cauchy–Schwarz now give
\[
 \frac1d\sum_{j:\,|t_j|\le\delta} w(\theta_j)
     \ll\frac1d\int wH\,d\nu
     \ll k\delta+\sqrt{k\Delta/d}.
\]
At real \(x_j\) the output is real, and the number of nonreal \(x_j\) with \(|t_j|>\delta\) is at most \(2T/\delta\).
Using the strip bound after reflection when \(t_j>0\) therefore yields
\[
 \frac1d\sum_j\chi\bigl(W(x_j)\bigr)
       \ll \frac{T}{d\delta}+k\delta+\sqrt{k\Delta/d}.              \tag{5}
\]
If \(k(\Delta+T)/d\ge1/16\), the trivial bound \(\chi\le1\) suffices.
Otherwise \(d>16k(\Delta+T)\) and \(\delta=2/d+\sqrt{T/(kd)}\) belongs to \([2/d,1]\).
Inserting it in (5), including \(T=0\), bounds the right side by \(O(\sqrt{k(\Delta+T)/d})\).
This proves (1) by (2).

<a id="example"></a>
## An output of height zero at linear degree in a nonreal trace field

Let \(\lambda=i\varphi\), so \(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=i\), \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt5)\), and its minimal polynomial is \(P_0(X)=X^4+3X^2+1\).
Its conjugates have moduli \(\varphi,\varphi,\varphi^{-1},\varphi^{-1}\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), \(\lambda\) has no \(p\)-th root in \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).
Such a root would be a nontorsion algebraic integer of degree at most four and height \(h(\lambda)/p\).
The elementary symmetric coefficient bounds give only finitely many algebraic integers of degree at most four and bounded height, and none of these of positive height can have height tending to zero.
For \(x^p=\lambda\), let \(e=[\mathbb Q(\lambda,x):\mathbb Q(\lambda)]\).
If \(e<p\), the norm of \(x\) satisfies \(N(x)^p=\lambda^e\), and Bézout for \(e,p\) would give a \(p\)-th root of \(\lambda\) in \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).
Thus \(x\) has degree \(d=4p\), its minimal polynomial is \(P_0(X^p)\), and \(m=2\log\varphi\).
The derivative formula \(pX^{p-1}P_0'(X^p)\) and the unit constant term give
\[
          |\operatorname{disc}(P_0(X^p))|
                      =p^{4p}|\operatorname{disc}P_0|^p.
\]
In particular \(\Delta+T=O(1)\).
The integral degree-\(p\) polynomial \(D_p\) defined by \(D_p(z+z^{-1})=z^p+z^{-p}\) sends \(x+x^{-1}\) to \(i\).
Its output has height zero and the trace field contains \(i\), hence has no real embedding.
Together with (1) this realizes the linear degree order for such a height drop at bounded discriminant deficit.
