# The archimedean contribution of an individual boundary point on a curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## An allocation formula for algebraic units

Let \(C\) be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve over a number field \(K\), and \(U,V:C\to\mathbb P^1\) nonconstant rational functions defined over \(K\).
Write \(D=\deg U\).
Fix \(Q\in C(K)\) with \(e=|\operatorname{ord}_Q V|>0\) and \(\lambda=U(Q)\in K^\times\).
Assume \(|\tau\lambda|\ne1\) for every embedding \(\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\).
For each \(\tau\), take a sufficiently small open neighborhood \(B_\tau\) of \(Q^\tau\) on \(C^\tau(\mathbb C)\), with conjugate choices at conjugate embeddings.
Its closure should miss \(|U^\tau|=1\) and every other zero and pole of \(V^\tau\).

Suppose \(P_i\in C(\overline K)\) eventually avoids every finite set, \(h(U(P_i))\to0\), and \(V(P_i)\) is an algebraic unit.
For any number fields \(E_i\) containing \(K\) and the coordinates of \(P_i\),
\[
 \lim_i\frac1{[E_i:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\sigma:E_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
 \mathbf1_{B_{\sigma|K}}(P_i^\sigma)\,
             \big|\log|\sigma V(P_i)|\big|
                         = \frac{e}{D}\,h(\lambda).                         \tag{1}
\]
Separate boundary points contribute even when their \(U\)-values agree.
One can enlarge \(K\) to define a chosen geometric branch.
The formula matches each \(\sigma\) to the corresponding embedding of \(K\) and takes the total over those embeddings.

In particular suppose \(U(P_i)=\xi_i z_i^{k_i}\) and \(V(P_i)=\eta_i z_i^{l_i}\), with roots of unity \(\xi_i,\eta_i\) and \(l_i\ne0\).
At a pole \(Q\), if every conjugate of \(\lambda\) is strictly inside the unit circle, (1) excludes \(k_i/l_i\ge0\) along an infinite subsequence.
If every conjugate is strictly outside, it excludes \(k_i/l_i\le0\).
At a zero the two inequalities reverse.
Two poles with \(U\)-values respectively totally inside and totally outside thus exclude every such sequence, including \(k_i=0\).

<a id="defect"></a>
## The global height and the marked point

Give \(L=U^*\mathcal O(1)\) the pullback of the canonical sup metric and write \(\bar L\) for the resulting adelic divisor.
It is semipositive of degree \(D>0\), and \(h_{\bar L}(P)=h(U(P))\).
Both its absolute and its essential minimum are zero because the fibers over roots of unity contain infinitely many distinct algebraic points of height zero.
Take an adelic divisor \(\bar Q\) over \([Q]\) with Green function \(g_v=-\log|w|_v\) plus a continuous function near \(Q\) in a local parameter \(w\).
Use the model of the horizontal closure of \(Q\) at all but finitely many finite places.
Put \(n_v=[K_v:\mathbb Q_{v_0}]/[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(\mu_v=c_1(\bar L_v)/D\).
The [global limit theorem](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height) and the [intersection formula on a curve](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#point-defect) give
\[
 \lim_i h_{\bar Q}(P_i)
     =\frac{(\bar L\cdot\bar Q)}D
     =\frac{h(\lambda)}D+\sum_v n_v\int g_v\,d\mu_v.                     \tag{2}
\]
The cycle in the intersection term is the single \(K\)-rational point \([Q]\).
Applying the same global limit to a zero divisor with a bounded continuous test at one place gives weak convergence of the local \(K\)-orbit means to \(\mu_v\) by equation (4.4) of that source.
Tests at real places may be symmetrized by conjugation since both measures are invariant.

At every finite place the orbits are supported on the compact locus \(|V|_v=1\), hence so is \(\mu_v\).
At each archimedean place \(\mu_v\) is supported on \(|U|_v=1\).
Indeed the sum over all places of the absolute logarithmic means of \(U(P_i)\) with weights \(n_v\) equals \(2h(U(P_i))\) by the product formula, after dropping the finitely many points where \(U\) is zero or infinite.
Weak convergence for the bounded continuous truncation of the absolute logarithm gives the asserted support.
In particular the closures of the chosen neighborhoods at infinity have limiting orbit frequency zero.

<a id="places"></a>
## Removing the bounded contributions

Outside finitely many finite places choose a smooth proper model of \(C\), with \(Q\) extending to a section, \(V\) extending to a morphism to the projective line and \(g_v\) the model Green function of the closure of \(Q\).
The curve, morphism and section spread out after excluding finitely many places.
A point reducing to the section has \(V\)-image reducing to zero or infinity according to \(V(Q)\).
On the unit locus the defining section of the horizontal divisor therefore has unit model norm, giving \(g_v=0\).
This removes every good finite place from both the orbit means and the integrals in (2).

At the remaining finite places \(g_v\) is bounded on the compact unit locus, which misses \(Q\).
Take a constant \(T\) above all these bounds and above the maxima of \(g_v\) outside the chosen neighborhoods at infinity.
At each of this finite list of places, including all archimedean places, \(\min(g_v,T)\) extends by \(T\) at \(Q\) to a bounded continuous test.
Its orbit means converge to its integral.
It agrees with \(g_v\) on the support of every corresponding \(\mu_v\), and on the sampled unit points at finite places.
Subtracting these bounded-test limits from (2) gives
\[
 \lim_i \sum_{v\mid\infty}n_v\operatorname{avg}_{P_i,v}(g_v-T)^+
                        =h(\lambda)/D.                                    \tag{3}
\]
The sum over the archimedean places with their local orbit averages is the normalized sum over all embeddings of \(E_i\) into \(\mathbb C\).
Any further finite extension repeats each term with the same multiplicity.

<a id="branch"></a>
## Local order and the sign of the short power

The integrand in (3) vanishes outside the chosen neighborhood.
Inside it, \(V=w^{\operatorname{ord}_Q V}\) times a holomorphic unit near \(Q\).
Thus \(\big|\log|V|\big|-e(g_v-T)^+\) is bounded there, taking the neighborhood small enough.
Its contribution to the difference of means tends to zero by the vanishing neighborhood frequency.
Multiplying (3) by \(e\) proves (1).

For a pole, shrink the neighborhoods so that \(|V|>1\).
At every sampled embedding the powered values satisfy \(\log|U|=(k_i/l_i)\log|V|\).
If \(\lambda\) is totally interior or totally exterior, all the chosen neighborhoods lie on its prescribed side of the circle.
The stated incompatible signs would make every summand vanish although \(h(\lambda)>0\) by the product formula.
Replacing \(V\) by \(V^{-1}\) treats zeros.
