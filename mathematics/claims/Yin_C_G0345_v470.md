# The exponents and Mahler measures arising from one signed rational function under shifts and dilations

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite list of exponents and measures

Fix \(f_0=B/C\in\mathbb Q(T)^\times\), with \(B,C\in\mathbb Z[T]\) coprime over \(\mathbb Q\) and their combined coefficient list primitive.
Allow arbitrary integers \(k\ge1,\ v\in\mathbb Z,\ n\ge2\), and write \(f(Y)=Y^v f_0(Y^k)\).
Consider nontorsion algebraic units \(c\) for which \(f(X^n)-X\) has a zero of order at least two at both \(c\) and \(c^2\), with the rational function regular there.
If \(f_0\) is a constant times a monomial, then the logarithmic derivative of \(f\) is an integer, whereas either contact requires it to equal \(1/n\).
For every other fixed \(f_0\), all such exponents divide the nonzero integer \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,C)|\).
All such \(c\) have logarithmic Mahler measures in an effectively bounded finite set of positive numbers depending only on \(f_0\).
The [description by radicals](#radicals) gives every \(c\) for each \(k,v,n\), including the cases in which different signed rows give the same rational function.

For an integral signed row \(A(X^n)+\sigma X A^{*b}(X^n)\), where \(A^{*b}(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\), the function at genuine contacts is \(f=-\sigma A/A^{*b}\).
Here genuine means that \(A^{*b}\) is nonzero at the powered nodes.
If \(A(Y)=Y^s D(Y^k)\), where \(D\) has degree \(e\) and both endpoints nonzero, take \(v=2s+ke-b\) and \(f_0=-\sigma D/D^{*e}\) in reduced form.
The conclusions therefore hold simultaneously for all such placements of the support, all widths and both signs for each fixed \(D\).
In particular a sequence of such genuine contacts with logarithmic Mahler measure tending to zero leaves every joint bound on the degree and the absolute coefficients of the primitive \(D\), after removing the largest common divisor of the support differences.

<a id="radicals"></a>
## Recovering the radicals from the square relation

For nonmonomial \(f_0\) define
\[
 K(T)=B(T^2)C(T)^2-C(T^2)B(T)^2,\qquad r(T)=T f_0'(T)/f_0(T).
\]
The polynomial \(K\) is nonzero.
Indeed, if \(f_0(T^2)=f_0(T)^2\), the finite set of its nonzero finite zeros and poles is its own inverse image under squaring, and that inverse image has twice the cardinality.
The set would be empty and \(f_0\) a constant times a monomial.
Also \(r\) is nonconstant, since at each nonzero finite zero or pole of \(f_0\) the logarithmic derivative has a pole.

At any required pair of contacts put \(z=c^{nk}\).
The value conditions give \(f_0(z)=c^{1-nv}\) and \(f_0(z^2)=f_0(z)^2\).
Differentiating the two graphs logarithmically gives
\[
                      r(z)=r(z^2)=\frac{1-nv}{nk}.
\]
In particular \(z\) is a multiple root of \(K\).
Conversely, at any nonzero \(z\) where \(B,C\) are nonzero at both \(z,z^2\), the vanishing of \(K,K'\) is equivalent to the square value relation and \(r(z)=r(z^2)\).
This follows by logarithmically differentiating the ratio \(f_0(T^2)/f_0(T)^2\) at a point where it equals one.

List the multiple roots \(z\) of \(K\) that are nontorsion units, meet these nonvanishing conditions, and have rational \(r(z)=a/q\) in lowest terms with \(q>0\).
Keep only those with \(z^a=f_0(z)^q\).
Choose \(i,j\in\mathbb Z\) with \(iq+ja=1\) and put \(t=z^i f_0(z)^j\).
Then \(t^q=z,\ t^a=f_0(z)\), and these two equations characterize \(t\) uniquely.
For each \(k\ge1\) put \(\delta=\gcd(q,k)\).
Use this \(k\) exactly when
\[
              n=q/\delta\ge2,\qquad v=(\delta-ka)/q\in\mathbb Z .
\]
For these \(k,v,n\) take every root of \(c^{k/\delta}=t\).
These are precisely all the contacts in the statement.
Indeed \(1/n=v+ka/q\) has reduced denominator \(q/\delta\).
With these \(v,n\), the identities \(nk=(k/\delta)q\) and \(1-nv=(k/\delta)a\) give the necessary value conditions and recover \(c^{k/\delta}=t\) by Bézout.
Conversely these identities and the two equations at \(z,z^2\) give both double contacts for each indicated radical.
All these radicals are nontorsion units because \(z\) is one.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Finite Mahler spectrum along all the radical families

Use \(h_{\rm W}\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(m(\alpha)=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]h_{\rm W}(\alpha)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of the minimal polynomial of a unit.
For one of the finitely many \(t\) above, write \(g=k/\delta,\ F=\mathbb Q(t),\ e=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
If \(c^g=t\), then \(F\subset\mathbb Q(c)\) and \(\ell=[\mathbb Q(c):F]\le g\).
The relative norm \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(c)/F}(c)\) is a unit of \(F\), and \(u^g=t^\ell\).
Consequently
\[
            0<h_{\rm W}(u)=\ell h_{\rm W}(t)/g\le h_{\rm W}(t),
            \qquad m(c)= e h_{\rm W}(u).
\]
The height equalities follow from the power rule, and positivity follows from the nontorsion condition by Kronecker's theorem.
The minimal polynomial of \(u\) is monic integral of degree at most \(e\) and has Mahler measure at most \(\exp(e h_{\rm W}(t))\).
Its coefficient sum in absolute value is at most \(2^e\exp(e h_{\rm W}(t))\), by multiplying the bounds \(1+|\alpha|\le2\max(1,|\alpha|)\) for its conjugates.
Enumerating the monic polynomials in this finite box and retaining the positive \(e h_{\rm W}(u)\) from their unit roots bounds the spectrum effectively.
The finitely many \(t\) were obtained by factoring one polynomial and applying exact algebraic tests.
For bounded degree and coefficients there are finitely many primitive \(D\), so the last assertion of the statement follows.

<a id="bezout"></a>
## The denominator divides the ideal of Bézout integers

Already at the single genuine double contact \(c\), set \(z=c^{nk}\) and write \(r(z)=a/q\) as above.
Both \(z\) and \(f_0(z)\) are units in the ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(z)\), since \(f_0(z)=c^{1-nv}\) belongs to that field and it and its inverse are integral.
The identities
\[
 r(z)B(z)=zB'(z)-zf_0(z)C'(z),\qquad
 r(z)C(z)=zB'(z)/f_0(z)-zC'(z)
\]
show that \(q\) divides \(B(z),C(z)\) in this ring.
Evaluation therefore sends every integer of the ideal \((B,C)\subset\mathbb Z[T]\) into \(q\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(z)}\cap\mathbb Z=q\mathbb Z\).
The resultant \(R=\operatorname{Res}(B,C)\) belongs to this ideal by the adjugate of the Sylvester matrix and is nonzero by coprimality.
Since \(n\mid q\), we have \(n\mid q\mid |R|\).
Hadamard's determinant bound also gives \(|R|\le\|B\|_2^{\deg C}\|C\|_2^{\deg B}\) for the reduced pair.

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## Resultant divisibility from both orbits and their reductions

Let \(H\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be the product of the distinct monic minima of \(z,z^2\) and write \(s=\deg H,\ N=\deg B+\deg C\).
Both points solve \(qT(B'C-BC')-aBC=0\), a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(N\) since \(r\) is nonconstant.
Thus \(s\le N\).
Let \(\mathcal A=\mathbb Z[T]/H\) inside the product \(\mathcal O\) of the rings of integers of the fields given by its distinct factors, writing \(I=[\mathcal O:\mathcal A]\).
Evaluation of polynomials of degree less than \(N\) surjects onto \(\mathcal A\) by monic division.
Every polynomial in the image of the integral Sylvester map \((U,V)\mapsto UB+VC\) of rank \(N\) takes values in \(q\mathcal O\), by the preceding argument at both targets.
Its index is \(|R|\), so
\[
 \frac{q^s}{[\mathcal O:\mathcal A+q\mathcal O]}
       =[\mathcal A:\mathcal A\cap q\mathcal O]\ \mid\ |R|.
\]
The displayed denominator divides both \(I\) and \(q^s\).
In particular \(q^s/\gcd(I,q^s)\mid |R|\), and the loss is supported on primes dividing \(\operatorname{disc}(H)\), since the trace determinant gives \(I^2\mid|\operatorname{disc}(H)|\).

There is a separate bound using only the number \(u_p\) of distinct residues of the conjugates of \(z,z^2\) in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_p\), for each \(p\mid q\).
Choose \(u_p\) conjugates with distinct reductions in the valuation ring of a \(p\)-adic splitting field.
Evaluation at these points maps the polynomials of degree less than \(N\) onto the product of \(u_p\) copies of this valuation ring, by the unit Vandermonde determinant and \(u_p\le s\le N\).
The Sylvester image lies in the product of the ideals generated by \(q\), so comparing lengths over the valuation ring and dividing by its ramification index gives
\[
                              u_p v_p(q)\le v_p(R).
\]
In particular, at a prime exponent \(n=p\), if the minima of \(c,c^2\) both have degree \(d\) and together have \(2d\) distinct reductions, then \(u_p\ge\lceil 2d/k_{p'}\rceil\), with \(k_{p'}=k/p^{v_p(k)}\).
Indeed all the reduced nodes are nonzero and the fibers of the \(pk\)-th power map on the algebraic closure have size \(k_{p'}\).

<a id="pair"></a>
## Uniqueness for a fixed pair of transverse signed rows

Fix two opposite-sign rows \(A_+,A_-\) of the same width \(b\) and suppose their determinant \(\Delta=A_+A_-^{*b}+A_-A_+^{*b}\) is nonzero.
Fix \(d\) with \(b<3d\) and restrict attention to genuine contacts at a nontorsion unit and its square whose minima both have degree \(d\).
There is at most one Galois orbit of seeds and one exponent \(n\ge2\) for this fixed pair.
Indeed, write \(y=c^n\) and use either rational function \(f=-\sigma A_\sigma/A_\sigma^{*b}\).
At \(y\), \(c=f(y)\) gives \(\mathbb Q(c)=\mathbb Q(y)\), and \(n=f(y)/(yf'(y))\); both statements apply also to \(y^2\) and \(c^2\).
The minima of \(y,y^2\) both have degree \(d\), and are distinct because their positive absolute heights differ by a factor of two.
They both divide \(\Delta\) with multiplicity at least two, by subtraction of the two rational graphs and the unramified power substitution at each node, as in the [two-row determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v464.md#integral).
Each such pair gives a directed edge from the orbit of \(y\) to the orbit of \(y^2\).
Distinct edges have at least three vertices, since height strictly increases along each edge and squaring determines its endpoint.
They would require \(\deg\Delta\ge6d>2b\), contrary to the determinant degree bound.
A single edge determines its starting orbit and thereby the seed orbit and exponent via the displayed recovery formulas.
When the determinant is identically zero, the graphs agree as rational functions and the preceding fixed-function results apply.
