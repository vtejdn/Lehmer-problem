# Elliptic point orders and factor degrees on the smooth characteristic-two fibers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The factor attached to a smooth fiber

Let \(q=2^a\) with \(a\ge1\), and take the [quadratic diagonal section](Yin_C_G0345_v842.md#pencil) with \(k=q\) and \(l=q^2+1\).
Write \(G=(1+X^{2q})X^{q^2+1}+X^{2q}+X^q+1\).
For each \(t\in\mathbb F_q\), let \(G_t\) be the monic factor whose roots satisfy \(D_t(x,x^q)=0\), where
\[
 D_t(x,y)=x^2y^2+x^2+y^2+x+y+1+txy.
\]
The projective closure \(E_t\) of \(D_t=0\) in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) is smooth precisely when \(t\ne1\) and \(t^4+t^3+1\ne0\).
For each such \(t\), use the origin \(O=(\infty,1)\) and put
\[
 A_t=(1,(1+t)^{-1}),\qquad h_t=\operatorname{ord}_{E_t(\mathbb F_q)}(A_t),\qquad N_t=\#E_t(\mathbb F_q).
\]
Over \(\mathbb F_q\), \(G_t\) is the product of exactly \(N_t/h_t\) distinct irreducible polynomials of degree \(h_t\), and \(|N_t-(q+1)|\le2\sqrt q\).
The same count and degrees hold for the corresponding trace roots \(x+x^{-1}\).
If \(t\) has degree \(a\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), its full orbit of labels contributes exactly \(N_t/h_t\) binary trace factors, all of degree \(a h_t\).

<a id="curve"></a>
## The curve and its two degree-two maps

The two affine partial derivatives are \(1+ty\) and \(1+tx\).
When \(t\ne0\) their common zero is \((1/t,1/t)\), which belongs to the curve exactly when \(t^4+t^3+1=0\).
The affine curve has no singular point for \(t=0\).
The only boundary points are \(O\) and \(S=(1,\infty)\).
Near \(O\), set \(u=1/x\) and multiply by \(u^2\) to obtain \((y+1)^2+u(1+ty)+u^2(y^2+y+1)\).
At \(O\) the derivative in \(u\) is \(1+t\), and the derivative in \(y\) vanishes.
Symmetry gives the same criterion at \(S\).
For the smooth parameters, consider the divisor sequence over the algebraic closure.
The sequence on \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) has kernel \(\mathcal O(-2,-2)\), whose only nonzero cohomology is one-dimensional in degree two by the product formula for the cohomology of \(\mathbb P^1\).
Thus \(H^0(\mathcal O_{E_t})\) and \(H^1(\mathcal O_{E_t})\) each have dimension one over the algebraic closure, and smoothness gives geometric irreducibility.

Write \(s=x+y,\ v=xy,\ w=s+v\).
On the curve \((1+t)v=w^2+w+1\), and \(s=w+v\).
The swap \(\sigma(x,y)=(y,x)\) therefore exchanges the two roots of \(Z^2+sZ+v\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_q(w)\).
It acts nontrivially since it exchanges \(O\) and \(S\), so \(w\) is a separable degree-two map to \(\mathbb P^1\).
The swap has geometric fixed points on the diagonal \(x^4+t x^2+1=0\).
The projection to \(x\) also has degree two, separable since its affine equation in \(y\) has linear coefficient \(1+tx\).
Denote its fiber involution by \(\iota\).
The pole fiber of \(x\) is \(2[O]\), since setting \(u=0\) in the boundary equation gives \((y+1)^2=0\).
For either degree-two map all fibers are linearly equivalent, counting a ramified point twice.
By the [elliptic Abel–Jacobi isomorphism](../audits/literature/Sut23/Yin_U001.md#group-law) \(P\mapsto[P]-[O]\), this gives \(\iota(P)=-P\) and \(\sigma(P)=S-P\), the latter by evaluating the fiber sum at \(O\).
Consequently \(T=\iota\circ\sigma\) translates by \(-S\).
The fiber over \(x=1\) consists of \(S\) and the simple affine solution \(y=1/(1+t)\), giving \(-S=\iota(S)=A_t\).
On the open set \(x(y^2+1)\ne0\), the quadratic root product gives
\[
 T(x,y)=\left(y,\frac{y^2+y+1}{(y^2+1)x}\right).
\]

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## Frobenius orbits on the graph

Let \(F\) be the \(q\)-power Frobenius of \(E_t\).
If \(F(P)=T(P)\), equality of the first coordinates requires \(y=x^q\) as projective coordinates.
This excludes both boundary points, and \(D_t(0,0)=1\) excludes \(x=0\).
The point \((1,1)\) occurs only on the fiber \(t=0\), where \(T(1,1)=S\) excludes it as well.
In particular the displayed expression for \(T\) applies, and equality of the second coordinates is exactly \(G(x)=0\).
Conversely a root of \(G_t\) has \(x\ne0,1\), and its pair \((x,x^q)\) satisfies both coordinate identities.
Projection to \(x\) thus bijects \((F-1)^{-1}(A_t)\) with the roots of \(G_t\).
The endomorphism \(F-1\) has differential \(-1\) at the origin, hence is nonconstant, separable and surjective on geometric points.
Its kernel consists exactly of the \(N_t\) rational points, so the fiber is a coset of that group and contains \(N_t\) points.
[Hasse's bound](../audits/literature/Sut23/Yin_U001.md#frobenius) applies to this elliptic curve over \(\mathbb F_q\).
Since \(A_t\) is rational, iteration yields \(F^j(P)=P+jA_t\), and the least positive return time on the whole coset is \(h_t\).
The equivariant root projection gives the asserted factorization because \(G\) is squarefree.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Descent of the trace degrees

The [two reciprocal graphs](Yin_C_G0345_v842.md#pencil) give a bijection \(x\mapsto x+x^{-1}\) from roots of \(G\) to roots of the binary trace polynomial, commuting with squaring.
It therefore preserves orbit lengths over every finite extension of \(\mathbb F_2\).
If \(d\) is the binary degree of a root on the smooth fiber \(t\), squaring moves its label to \(t^2\), so \([\mathbb F_2(t):\mathbb F_2]\mid d\).
The \(q\)-power orbit length is \(d/\gcd(d,a)=h_t\).
For a full-degree label this forces \(d=a h_t\), and its \(a\) conjugate fibers each have \(N_t\) roots, proving the binary factor count.
For a proper-subfield label, the same identity gives \(d\le a h_t\) and permits shorter binary orbits.
