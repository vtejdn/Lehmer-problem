# A Frobenius-order criterion for factor gaps in reciprocal tetranomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## The order of one fixed prime at a varying modulus

Fix coprime nonzero integers \(A,B\) with \(|A|\ne|B|\), a prime \(p\mid AB(A^2-B^2)\), and \(\rho>0\).
Consider
\[
 F=A(X^D+\sigma)+B(X^{D-k}+\sigma X^k),\qquad D>2k>0,\quad \sigma=\pm1.
\]
Define \(T=D\) if \(p\mid B\), \(T=D-2k\) if \(p\mid A\), and \(T=D-k\) if \(p\mid A^2-B^2\).
Write \(T=L H\) with \(L=p^{v_p(T)}\) and \(p\nmid H\), and set \(\operatorname{ord}_1(p)=1\).
If \(\operatorname{ord}_H(p)\ge\rho H\), every positive-measure integral divisor of \(F\) has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\eta(A,B,p,\rho)>0\).
This criterion uses the actual exponents, including when they have a common divisor.
In particular a shrinking sequence for fixed coefficients must satisfy \(\operatorname{ord}_H(p)/H\to0\) at every indicated prime with its corresponding \(T\).

More precisely, suppose \(k/D\to0\) along a sequence of nontorsion irreducible unit factors of degree \(d\).
For any divisors \(V\mid H\) having a bounded number of distinct prime factors, eventually
\[
                      d\ \ge\ c(A,B,p)L\,\operatorname{ord}_V(p).       \tag{1}
\]
The primes dividing \(V\) may vary without any bound on their sizes or multiplicities.
The index beyond which (1) holds may depend on the sequence and the bound for the number of primes.

<a id="fixed"></a>
## A common local branch for the fixed primes

For a root \(x\) of the factor let \(y=x^k\) and \(f(Y)=-\sigma(A+BY)/(AY+B)\).
The equation gives \(x^{D-k}=f(y)\).
Thus \(x^T=G(y)\), where \(G(Y)=Y f(Y),f(Y)/Y,f(Y)\) in the three cases above, respectively.
At the \(p\)-adic Gauss point \(\xi\), \(G\) is a unit whose residue is a constant of square one.
The first two cases give the residue \(-\sigma\).
In the last case \(A\equiv\epsilon B\bmod p\) for \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and \((\epsilon+Y)/(\epsilon Y+1)=\epsilon\) in the residue rational function field.
The simple zero at \(-A/B\) ensures \(G^2-1\) is nonzero as a rational function.
Consequently its Gauss valuation \(s=v_\xi(G^2-1)\), normalized by \(v(p)=1\), is a finite positive integer depending only on \(A,B,p\).

If (1) fails infinitely often for a proposed constant, pass to a subsequence with a fixed number of primes in \(V\).
Order these primes and pass again so each is either constant or tends to infinity.
Let \(b\) be the product of the constant primes, taking \(b=1\) when there are none, and keep the sign fixed.
The [cut bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \(D h(x)\le3\log(2|A|+2|B|)\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\).
Hence \(y\) is a nontorsion unit with \(h(y)\to0\).

Consider the normalization of the projective curve \(U^b=G(Y)\) with projection to \(Y\).
The simple zero at \(-A/B\) gives geometric irreducibility by Eisenstein and the projection has degree \(b\).
The points \((y,x^{T/b})\) have nonzero finite coordinates away from the zeros and poles of \(G\), and are generic because \(h(y)>0\) tends to zero.
With the pullback of the metrized standard \(O(1)\) their heights tend to the normalized height zero of the curve by the projection formula.
The metric is semipositive and the bundle ample.
By [Yuan's geometric equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md#yuan-equidistribution) and the [calculation over the Gauss point](Yin_C_G0345_v562.md#branches), the conjugates converge locally to the uniform distribution on the \(b\) branches above \(\xi\).
Indeed the reduction of \(U^b-G(Y)\) there has \(b\) simple constant roots since \(p\nmid b\).
Each lifts in the completed Gauss field, giving local degree one.

Choose such a residue root \(\gamma\) of order divisible by every \(q\mid b\), using a primitive \(b\)-th root for residue \(+1\) and a primitive \(2b\)-th root for residue \(-1\ne1\).
An open neighborhood of its branch is specified by \(|U-\tilde\gamma|_p<1\) and \(s/2<v(G(Y)^2-1)<s+1\), where \(\tilde\gamma\) is a unit lift.
This neighborhood contains at least \(1/(2b)\) of the conjugates eventually.
Use all embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) into \(\mathbf C_p\) with equal weight, which repeats the orbit of the point uniformly.
Call these embeddings in the neighborhood selected, and denote the residue order of \(x\) at such an embedding by \(\ell\).
Then \(\ell\mid 2T\), and \(\bar x^{T/b}=\gamma\) forces \(q^{v_q(T)}\mid\ell\) for every \(q\mid b\).

<a id="varying"></a>
## Increasing primes and a norm tending to one on the logarithmic scale

Take any of the remaining prime divisors \(q\mid V\) tending to infinity.
It is eventually odd, and \(w=x^{2T/q}\) is a nontorsion unit with \(h(w)\ll_{A,B}1/q\to0\).
The [cyclotomic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#norm) applied to \(w-1\) gives
\[
          0\ \le\ \frac{\log|N_{\mathbb Q(x)/\mathbb Q}(w-1)|}{d}
          \ \longrightarrow\ 0 .
\]
In detail, Bilu equidistribution and truncation from below bound the limit superior by the circle integral of \(\log|z-1|\), which is zero.
The contribution at large modulus is controlled by \(h(w)\to0\), and integrality gives the lower bound.
If a selected embedding has \(q^{v_q(T)}\nmid\ell\), then \(\ell\mid 2T/q\).
There \(w\equiv1\), and \(q\ne p\) gives \(v(w-1)=v(w^q-1)=v(x^{2T}-1)>s/2\).
The log norm divided by \(d\) is at least \((s\log p)/2\) times the fraction of all embeddings with this defect, by integrality at every finite place.
Thus this fraction tends to zero.
There are only boundedly many increasing primes under consideration, so eventually some selected embedding has \(V\mid\ell\).

<a id="conclusion"></a>
## Local degree and the size of the prime support

At this embedding the residue degree is at least \(\operatorname{ord}_V(p)\).
For the ramification index \(e\), put \(L'=p^{v_p(2T)}\ge L\).
Since \(\ell\) is prime to \(p\), the integer \(2T/(L'\ell)\) is prime to \(p\).
Consequently \(v(x^{\ell L'}-1)=v(x^{2T}-1)<s+1\).
Starting from \(v(x^\ell-1)\ge1/e\), taking a \(p\)-th power raises principal-unit depth \(t>0\) to at least \(\min(pt,t+1)\).
In at most \(\lceil\log_p e\rceil\) steps the depth reaches \(1/(p-1)\), and every further step raises it by at least one.
Therefore \(L\le L'\le p^{s+2}e\).
The product of the residue degree and \(e\) is at most \(d\), proving (1) with \(c=p^{-s-2}\).
This contradicts any subsequence violating (1) for that constant.

If \(H\) has \(r\ge1\) distinct odd prime divisors, their individual Euler totients are all even.
The least common multiple of the totients of the prime powers dividing \(H\) is therefore at most \(\varphi(H)/2^{r-1}\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_H(p)\) divides that least common multiple.
Hence \(\operatorname{ord}_H(p)\ge\rho H\) bounds the number of distinct primes of \(H\) solely in terms of \(\rho\), including its possible factor two.
If positive log measures tended to zero under the order hypothesis, take irreducible factors of log measure \(m\to0\), eventually unit factors.
The [four-term degree and height bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) give \(d/D\ll m\to0\) and \(d\gg_{A,B}\sqrt{Dk}\), hence \(k/D\to0\) and \(T/D\to1\).
Applying (1) to \(V=H\) gives \(d\gg_{A,B,p}\rho T\), a contradiction.
