# The signed first discrepancy determines the second limit at Salem construction delay two

<a id="statement"></a>
## Concentration after the moving geometric prefix

Consider a sequence of monic Pisot minimal polynomials \(f_i\) with \(M(z^2 f_i\pm f_i^*)>1\) tending to one, where \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure and \(f^*(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z)\).
By [dyadic concentration](Yin_C_G0345_v28.md#statement) the eventual seed is a unit with root \(\theta_i\to2\), and the sign of the polynomial is \(z^2 f_i+e_i f_i^*\), \(e_i=f_i(0)\).
Put \(F_i=e_i f_i/f_i^*\), \(F_*=1/(1-2z)\) and let \(n_i\) be the first Taylor coefficient index at which \(F_i\) differs from \(F_*\).
Write \(\delta_i=[z^{n_i}](F_i-F_*)\) and set
\[
 V_i(z)=\frac{(1-2z)F_i(z)-1}{z^{n_i}\big((z-2)-zF_i(z)\big)}.
\]
Then \(|\delta_i|=1\) eventually and
\[
     V_i+\delta_i/2\longrightarrow0,\qquad
     2^{n_i}(\theta_i-2)-\delta_i/2\longrightarrow0 .
\]
The function convergence is uniform on compact subsets of the open unit disk, where the \(V_i\) are eventually holomorphic.
In particular the sign of the first discrepancy gives the side from which the corresponding seed approaches two, and its absolute distance is asymptotic to \(2^{-n_i-1}\).
Both signs and every binomial second profile in the [first-departure list](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#departure) do occur for actual Pisot units when the matched output has measure one.

<a id="argument"></a>
## The finite recurrence distinguishes the positive excess

The [centered Schur bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#schur) extracts from every subsequence a limit \(R/2\), where \(R\) is a nonzero signed integer constant of magnitude at most two or a binomial \(-a-bz^k\) with \(a,b=\pm1,\ k\ge1\).
In either case \(R(0)=-\delta_i\) on the extracted tail.
The [extreme departures](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#extreme) force the two explicit trinomials when \(|\delta_i|=2\) at large indices, and their [actual matched outputs](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v119.md#delay) have measure one.

On a fixed binomial subsequence write \(p=n_i+1\) and \(K_i^0=(1-z)(1+zF_i)/(1+z^2F_i)\).
Its integer coefficients through any fixed multiple of \(n_i\) approximate the doubled Fourier moments of a positive measure uniformly at positive indices, by the [window estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window).
The [torus limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#slice) has first conditional moment \((a+bx^k)/2\), where \(x=\bar v\) and \(y=v^p\).
The [second conditional moment](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#two) forces the limiting support into either \((y-a)(y-bx^k)=0\) or \(y^2=abx^k\).
Thus the absolute square of \(D(\bar v)\) has integral tending to zero for \(D=(1-a z^p)(1-bz^{p+k})\) or \(D=1-abz^{2p+k}\), respectively.
The [bilateral recurrence](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#recurrence) uses this vanishing and integrality to give an exact recurrence in the comparison coefficients throughout any fixed multiple of \(n_i\).
In each case a [unit Pisot seed of degree at most \(2n_i+2+k\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v119.md#seeds) has the prescribed moments and a neutral matched output.
The [recurrence and the initial block](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#neutral) identify the Taylor jets of its signed quotient and \(F_i\) through degree \(4n_i-1\).
Multiplication by the two pole-canceling Blaschke factors preserves these jets, so [finite Schur uniqueness](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#unique) identifies both seeds once \(n_i\) is sufficiently large for this fixed \(k\).
This contradicts strict positive excess.

Every convergent centered subsequence therefore has constant limit \(-\delta_i/2\) once the discrepancy sign is fixed.
Compactness on each sign gives the asserted convergence, and the [exact identity at the seed pole](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v118.md#side) gives the signed asymptotic.
The explicit neutral seeds realize every binomial and both unit constants as centered limits, including a constant obtained by letting \(k\to\infty\).
The necessary conditions above leave open the existence of a positive-excess sequence with a constant centered limit.
