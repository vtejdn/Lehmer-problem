## 1. Actual moment and complete weighted fibers

Let alpha be a nontorsion algebraic unit of actual degree d, P its monic irreducible polynomial, O=Z[alpha], and m=log M(P).
All logarithms are natural.
Fix 1<=n<=d and write v_j=alpha^j,0<=j<n.
These n actual elements are linearly independent over Q.
For an ideal I of finite index Q, let G=O/I and let bar v_j be their actual residue images.
Haar averages below use the full compact dual of O or the full finite additive dual of G, both with total mass1.
No selected field component or independently changed digit law is substituted silently.

For arbitrary complex c_0,...,c_(n-1), not all zero, and integer h>=1, put

    Z_c(y)=sum_(j<n)c_j y(v_j),
    M_O(c,h)=integral_(dual O)|Z_c(y)|^(2h) dy,
    M_G(c,h)=(1/Q)sum_(chi in dual G)|sum_j c_j chi(bar v_j)|^(2h).

These are high moments of an orbit character sum, not the signed neutral midpoint covariance of L335 and not the original binary endpoint law.
The distinction is essential when considering a transfer to either of those other targets.

Let A_(n,h) be the nonnegative integer vectors a=(a_0,...,a_(n-1)) with sum a_j=h, and define

    u_a=sum_j a_j v_j,
    C_a(c)=h!/(product_j a_j!) * product_j c_j^(a_j).

The convention0^0=1 is used in this polynomial expansion.
Independence of the v_j makes all actual u_a distinct.
Multinomial expansion and character orthogonality therefore prove exactly

    M_O(c,h)=sum_a |C_a(c)|^2,
    M_G(c,h)=sum_(g in G)|sum_(a:bar u_a=g)C_a(c)|^2.       (1)

Every sum is finite.
Formula(1) is the full weighted-fiber correction; it preserves multiplicity and complex phase before the absolute square.
Dropping signs inside a quotient fiber changes the observable and is not a valid identity for signed coefficients.

The initial bounded reuse screen also read [L86](../ledger/Yin_L_G0086_v1.md#linear-moment-necessary-cost).
Its fixed-input all-long-time lower theorem already shows that uniform linear-order original-moment upper bounds contain a Mahler gap.
That theorem is not reproved or strengthened here.
The present question is the separate multiplicative distortion induced by a finite quotient even in the first linearly independent window n<=d.

## 2. Approximate moment fidelity pays a smaller but explicit cost

Suppose first that c_j=w_j>=0.
Let

    S=sum_j w_j>0,    E=sum_j w_j^2>0,    R_w=S^2/E.

Thus1<=R_w<=n is the actual effective support of the positive weights.
Formula(1) has no cancellation, so M_G>=M_O.
It also gives

    M_G>=S^(2h)/Q,    M_O<=h! E^h,
    M_G/M_O>=R_w^h/(Q h!).                               (2)

For the first bound, the nonnegative fiber sums total sum_a C_a=S^h, so Cauchy--Schwarz over at most Q fibers applies.
For the second, the multinomial coefficient b_a=h!/product a_j! is at most h!, whence b_a^2<=h! b_a and the multinomial theorem yields sum_a b_a^2 product w_j^(2a_j)<=h!(sum_j w_j^2)^h.
The denominator M_O is positive because some c_j is nonzero and the corresponding pure multi-index contributes positively.

Thus any proposed approximate fidelity M_G<=A M_O, A>=1, necessarily satisfies

    log A>=h log R_w-log(h!)-log Q.                      (3)

No full2^n binary partition or n log2 correction was used.
At fixed h and flat weights the required quotient size is only of order n^h/h!, rather than2^n.
This is a necessary cost, not an existence theorem for such a quotient or a bound on the number of spurious fibers.

For flat weights w_j=1, exact moment equality holds if and only if reduction is injective on all h-term multisets u_a.
Indeed every C_a is strictly positive, so a quotient fiber containing two distinct a,b adds the strictly positive cross-term2C_a C_b.
Consequently

    M_G=M_O  implies  Q>=binom(n+h-1,h),
    log Q>=h log((n+h-1)/h).                             (4)

The second bound follows from the product formula for the binomial coefficient: each factor in product_(i=0)^(h-1)(n+i)/(i+1) is at least(n+h-1)/h.
For n=1 this yields the harmless zero logarithm; no growing-d conclusion is drawn from that degenerate case.

## 4. Which signed phases preserve the observable

For arbitrary complex c, (1) remains exact but M_G>=M_O need not hold: cross-terms within quotient fibers can have either sign.
Cauchy--Schwarz still gives

    M_G>=|sum_j c_j|^(2h)/Q,
    M_O<=h!(sum_j|c_j|^2)^h,                             (10)

but the first bound may vanish when the signed coefficient sum is zero.
It cannot be replaced by(sum|c_j|)^(2h)/Q without an additional phase condition.

There is a precise class of phase changes that does not evade(2)--(9).
Let rho be an additive character of G and c_j=w_j rho(bar v_j),w_j>=0.
Multiplication chi -> chi rho permutes the FULL finite dual, so

    M_G(c,h)=M_G(w,h).

Also |C_a(c)|=C_a(w), and therefore M_O(c,h)=M_O(w,h) by independence in the actual first-n window.
Every quotient-compatible character phase twist, including those taking only signs+/-1, has exactly the same moment inflation and arithmetic cost as the positive weights.
Calling such a twist a signed improvement would merely reindex the characters.

Incompatible signs can genuinely reduce a quotient moment but can also erase actual signal.
A nontrivial exact control uses

    P=t^2-4t+1,    O=Z[alpha],    I=(alpha-1)O.

P is irreducible over Q because its discriminant12 is not square.
Its two positive reciprocal roots are2+sqrt3 and2-sqrt3, so it is a nontorsion algebraic unit with fixed positive measure M=2+sqrt3.
The quotient is O/I=F_2, because evaluating P at1 gives-2, and alpha maps to1.
Thus alpha has quotient period1.
For n=d=2 and c=(1,-1), every finite character has chi(bar1)=chi(bar alpha), hence Z_c=0 and

    M_G(c,h)=0,
    M_O(c,h)=sum_(a=0)^h binom(h,a)^2=binom(2h,h)>0.     (11)

The final identity follows by taking the coefficient of t^h in(1+t)^h(1+t)^h.
This is an exact signed cancellation in a genuine nonzero actual-order quotient, not a trivial zero ring or a numerical failure.
It supplies no near-one counterexample, since the original measure is fixed away from one.
The sign pattern cannot be a quotient-compatible character twist of positive weights:1 and alpha are equal in G but were assigned opposite phases.

There is also an exact averaging guard.
For arbitrary positive magnitudes w_j and independent phases theta_j uniform on the(h+1)-st roots of unity, put c_j=w_j theta_j.
Since each exponent a_j-b_j lies between-h and h, its phase average vanishes unless a_j=b_j.
Expanding(1) and averaging therefore yields

    average_theta M_G(c,h)=M_O(w,h).                     (12)

The actual original moment at n<=d is itself independent of these phases.
Some incompatible phase choices can consequently cancel the quotient inflation without paying d log2, even if many multiset collisions exist.
But this averages a NEW coefficient-phase experiment; it neither controls the original positive orbit sum nor the binary Fourier product used in L84.
A minimum over such phase choices is at most the original moment, and a maximum is at least it; selecting a favorable finite sample is not an upper bound for the original positive-law moment.
The compatible twists above cannot perform this cancellation because their moment is unchanged exactly.
