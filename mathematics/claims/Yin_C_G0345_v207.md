# Prime divisors of the radical index and irreducibility for the blocks 2 and 3

<a id="statement"></a>
## Restrictions from the block primes

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) with \(k\ge2\), write \(M=\prod_i p_i\), and let \(b_i\ge1\) have greatest common divisor one.
Put \(D=\sum_i b_i\), \(A(X)=\prod_i(X^{b_i}-p_i)\), \(A^*(X)=X^D A(1/X)\), and \(P_N=X^N A+\varepsilon A^*\) for either sign \(\varepsilon\).
For \(N\ge C(D+1)^9\), with \(C\) depending only on the fixed primes, let \(S\) be the [unique irreducible noncyclotomic factor](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) of \(P_N\).
Fix a positive real root \(\beta\) of \(S\), and let \(r\) be the largest integer such that \(\beta^{1/r}\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), with the positive radical understood.

Every prime divisor \(q\) of \(r\) satisfies \(q\nmid M\).
Every odd prime divisor also satisfies \(M^{q-1}\equiv1\pmod{q^2}\).
For any \(q\mid r\) with \(q>k\), the nonempty index set \(I=\{i:q\nmid b_i\}\) satisfies
\[
 \prod_{i\in I}(X^{b_i}-p_i)
       = c\prod_{i\in I}(1-p_iX^{b_i})\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q[X]
 \quad\text{for some }c\in\mathbb F_q^\times,\qquad
 \left(\prod_{i\in I}p_i\right)^2\equiv1\pmod q.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the possible primes are restricted to \(q\le k\) and the prime divisors of \((\prod_{i\in J}p_i)^2-1\) for the nonempty subsets \(J\), subject in both cases to the preceding exclusions.

For the blocks \(p_1=2,p_2=3\) one has \(r=1\) for **all** coprime positive exponents \(b_1,b_2\), both signs and every \(N\) in the indicated range.
For every integer \(h\ge1\), the polynomial \(S(X^h)\) is irreducible.
It is the unique noncyclotomic factor of \(P_N(X^h)\), has degree \(h\deg S\) and has logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(S)\).

<a id="bands"></a>
## The congruence for a power of a unit

The [uniform bound for the radical index](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#statement) uses the norm of a polynomial value.
Here the separated terms of a polynomial congruence distinguish the individual prime divisors.
For a monic polynomial \(F\) of degree \(d\), denote by \(\Phi_q(F)=\prod_\alpha(X-\alpha^q)\) the polynomial obtained by raising all its roots to their \(q\)-th powers, with multiplicities.
Its coefficients are integer polynomials in the coefficients of \(F\) by symmetry.
Over the polynomial ring in these coefficients, reduction modulo \(q\) sends each output coefficient to the \(q\)-th power of the corresponding input coefficient.
Indeed in characteristic \(q\) one has \(\Phi_q(F)(Y^q)=F(Y)^q\).
Thus the partial derivatives of every coefficient polynomial vanish modulo \(q\).
For two monic integer polynomials \(F,G\) of the same degree with \(F\equiv G\pmod q\), expansion in their coefficient differences gives
\[
             \Phi_q(F)\equiv\Phi_q(G)\pmod{q^2}.
\]
If \(q\mid r\), let \(T\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta^{1/q}\).
This root is an algebraic integer in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) whose \(q\)-th power generates that field, so \(T\) has degree \(\deg S\) and \(\Phi_q(T)=S\).
The following congruence is also the companion-matrix case at exponent two of [Mazur and Petrenko's characteristic-polynomial theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence).
The reduction formula gives \(T\equiv S\pmod q\), hence \(\Phi_q(S)\equiv S\pmod{q^2}\).
With \(U=P_N/S\), multiplicativity now shows
\[
                    U\Phi_q(P_N)\equiv P_N\Phi_q(U)\pmod{q^2}.      \tag{2}
\]
The [degree and measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v202.md#tower) bounds any such \(q\) by \(O(1+\log^3(2N))\), since the block primes bound \(m(S)\) and \(\deg S\le N+D\).
Enlarge \(C\) once to ensure \(N>12qD\) for every possible \(q\), using \(D+1\le (N/C)^{1/9}\).

Put \(L=\varepsilon A^*\), choose a primitive complex \(q\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), and set \(X=Y^q\).
Writing \(d_P=N+D\), we have
\[
 \Phi_q(P_N)(X)=(-1)^{(q-1)d_P}
 \prod_{v=0}^{q-1}\big(L(\zeta^vY)+(\zeta^vY)^N A(\zeta^vY)\big).
\]
For \(0\le j\le q\) group the terms that select the second summand at exactly \(j\) positions.
Each sum is invariant under \(Y\mapsto\zeta Y\) and under Galois conjugation of \(\zeta\), so it is an integer polynomial in \(X\).
Its exponents lie in \([jN/q,jN/q+D]\).
The last group is exactly \(X^N\Phi_q(A)(X)\), by the same product formula applied to the monic \(Y^N A(Y)\).
Since \(\deg U=\deg\Phi_q(U)\le10D\), after multiplication by \(U\) these intervals remain disjoint, and the right side of (2) occupies just the two endpoint intervals.
The top interval gives
\[
                         U\Phi_q(A)\equiv A\Phi_q(U)\pmod{q^2}.       \tag{3}
\]
At zero the constants of \(U\) and \(\Phi_q(U)\) are signs, whereas \(A(0)=(-1)^kM\) has valuation one at each \(p_i\).
The constant of \(\Phi_q(A)\) is \((-1)^{(q-1)D}A(0)^q\).
Consequently (3) rules out every \(q\mid M\).
For odd \(q\), \(\Phi_q(U)(0)=U(0)\) and (3) gives \(M^{q-1}\equiv1\bmod q^2\).

<a id="mixed"></a>
## The mixed terms detect multiplicities modulo the dilation prime

Each of the groups with \(1\le j<q\) vanishes modulo \(q^2\) by (2), since its product by the monic \(U\) vanishes in its own interval and a monic polynomial is a nonzero divisor over \(\mathbb Z/q^2\mathbb Z\).
Put \(R(Y)=Y^N A(Y)/L(Y)\), and write \(e_j\) for the \(j\)-th elementary symmetric function of the \(q\) series \(R(\zeta^vY)\).
Apart from the common sign, the \(j\)-th group equals \(e_j\prod_v L(\zeta^vY)\).
The latter product has constant coefficient \(\pm1\), hence \(e_j\equiv0\bmod q^2\) as a formal series at zero for each \(1\le j<q\).
All these symmetric functions have rational integer coefficients by invariance and \(L(0)=\pm1\).
Newton's identities for the \(q\) series then imply that \(\sum_v R(\zeta^vY)^j\equiv0\bmod q^2\) for \(1\le j<q\).
This sum is \(q\) times the series formed by keeping just the powers of \(Y\) divisible by \(q\) in \(R(Y)^j\).
All coefficients at those powers vanish modulo \(q\).

Work now over \(k_0=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\) and denote reduced rational functions by a bar.
Every \(f\in k_0(Y)\) has a unique decomposition \(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}Y^i f_i(Y^q)\) with \(f_i\in k_0(Y)\).
For existence, multiply numerator and denominator by the \((q-1)\)-st power of the denominator and group numerator exponents modulo \(q\).
Uniqueness follows by clearing the denominators in \(Y^q\) and comparing exponents.
If \(f=\bar R^j\), the series calculation gives \(f_0=0\), since only \(f_0(Y^q)\) contributes exponents divisible by \(q\) in the Laurent series at zero.
Then \(f/Y\) is the derivative of the rational function \(\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}Y^i f_i(Y^q)/i\).

Suppose \(a\in k_0^\times\) is a zero or pole of \(\bar R\) of signed order \(s\) not divisible by \(q\).
Choose \(1\le j<q\) with \(js\equiv-1\bmod q\).
The Laurent expansion of \(\bar R^j/Y\) at \(a\) has nonzero leading term of degree \(js\).
The derivative of a rational function has zero coefficient at every degree congruent to \(-1\bmod q\), by differentiating its Laurent series in \(Y-a\).
This contradiction shows that \(q\) divides every such signed order.

For \(q\nmid M\), all zeros of every block in \(A,A^*\) are nonzero.
When \(q\mid b_i\), the corresponding numerator and denominator blocks are \(q\)-th powers modulo \(q\).
When \(q\nmid b_i\), each has simple zeros by differentiation.
After discarding the \(q\)-th powers, the difference of the remaining numerator and denominator multiplicities at any nonzero point is still divisible by \(q\) and has absolute value at most \(k\).
For \(q>k\) it is zero everywhere, so the two products in (1) differ only by a nonzero scalar.
Their constant and leading coefficients give the congruence there, and \(\gcd_i b_i=1\) ensures \(I\ne\varnothing\).

<a id="two-three"></a>
## The pair of primes 2 and 3

The constant coefficient in (3) excludes both 2 and 3.
A prime \(q>3\) dividing \(r\) must divide at least one of \(2^2-1=3\), \(3^2-1=8\), \(6^2-1=35\).
This leaves just 5 and 7, but \(6^4\equiv21\bmod25\) and \(6^6\equiv8\bmod49\).
Neither passes (3), so \(r=1\).
The [exact radical and factor-degree formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives irreducibility of \(S(X^h)\), its degree and its measure for every \(h\).
All zeros of \(U(X^h)\) are roots of unity.
