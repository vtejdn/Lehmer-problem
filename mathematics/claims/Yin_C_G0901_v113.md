# A Pisot exclusion and the Lehmer gap for bounded Mahler orbits

<a id="statement"></a>
## The only possible bounded orbits in the group of a small unit

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of sufficiently small positive total logarithmic measure \(m(w)=\log M(w)\).
In its divisible conjugate group \(D(w)\), allow all root extractions and roots of unity.
For every nontorsion \(x\) therein, let \(d_x\) be its degree after taking the product over its torsion class of conjugates.
Then the rank of the multiplicative group of its conjugates modulo torsion is at most \(d_x/2\).
In particular \(D(w)\) contains **no Pisot number**, however large that number might be.

There is at most one primitive real Salem number \(\sigma>1\) in \(D(w)\).
Here primitivity is on its rational multiplicative line in the units of \(\mathbb Q(\sigma)\) modulo torsion.
It is present exactly when the last compressed block product \(z_s\) in the [constituent chain](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent) is a signed conjugate of a nonzero power of a Salem number.
If present, \(\log\sigma\le m(w)\), \(\deg\sigma=\deg z_s\le\deg w\), and the fixed values \(M(x)=x>1\) in \(D(w)\) are exactly \(\sigma^j,\ j\ge1\).
Every bounded Mahler orbit of a nontorsion member eventually reaches one of these values.
If the group has no such Salem number, the iterates of **every** nontorsion member tend to infinity.

Consequently the existence of a uniform positive Lehmer gap for units whose own Mahler iterates are bounded is equivalent to the real Salem gap.
There is no common bound imposed on any of these individual orbits, their lengths before stabilization, their terminal values or their second iterates.
For a sufficiently small strict degree record either every nontorsion group member reaches a fixed value at its first iteration, or every such member has iterates tending to infinity.
The former case occurs exactly at signed primitive Salem records up to conjugation and inversion.
An unrestricted proof still has to treat potentially shrinking inputs with diverging orbits.

<a id="rank"></a>
## A lower bound on the size of every nonzero vector orbit

Use the [actual multiplicity-free decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#realization) \(V_w=\bigoplus_i W_i\), writing \(V_y\) for the rational class span of all conjugates.
For \(x\) nontorsion let \(i\) be its first nonzero component.
Then \(W_i\subseteq V_x\subseteq V_{z_i}\), and the orbit of \([x]\) maps surjectively onto the orbit of its nonzero projection \(v\in W_i\).
The orbit size of \([x]\) is \(d_x\) by [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree).
Write \(d_i=2u b\) for the degree of \(z_i\), with \(u\) blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs at that stage.
The space \(V_{z_i}\) is a quotient of the formal space on these \(ub\) pairs, so \(\dim V_x\le d_i/2\).
We show \(d_x\ge d_i\).

In the independent signed model the image of a coordinate vector runs through both signs of every coordinate, so every nonzero coordinate multiple has orbit size \(2ub\).
The same holds in the oriented sum-zero model for the image of one coordinate vector obtained by subtracting its block mean.
Indeed the action is transitive on the actual roots, and the two negatives of one another on a label are distinct; for \(b\ge3\) neither sign coincides with either image of a different label.
The orbit of a noncoordinate vector has size at least \(u b(b-1)/2\) in either model by the [block orbit count](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#orbit).
That count uses only the actions on these two model spaces: independent supported even inversions and alternating label actions, or independent ordinary alternating permutations on the sum-zero blocks.
They act on \(W_i\) also when \(V_{z_i}\) has a further summand.
The uniform large-degree choice in the chain gives \(b\ge9\), hence this orbit has at least \(2ub\) elements as well.
This proves the rank bound and also shows that the least possible \(d_x\) tends to infinity as \(m(w)\to0\), since every \(d_i\) does.

Let \(p>1\) be a Pisot unit of degree \(e\), so all its other conjugates are strictly inside the circle.
The multiplicative classes of its conjugates have rank \(e-1\).
To see this, suppose an integer combination of their classes vanishes.
Choose an index with maximal coefficient and send the corresponding conjugate to \(p\).
Taking log moduli of the relation and subtracting that maximal coefficient times the logarithmic norm relation gives zero.
All remaining coefficients are nonpositive and all remaining log moduli strictly negative, forcing all the coefficients to equal the maximum.
Conversely the all-ones vector is a relation since \(p\) is a unit.
If \(p\in D(w)\), the previous paragraph gives \(e-1\le d_p/2\le e/2\), so \(e\le2\).
These degrees are excluded by \(d_p\ge d_i\) at every sufficiently small \(w\).
All members of \(D(w)\) are algebraic units, which completes the Pisot exclusion.

<a id="salem"></a>
## Identification of the terminal Salem line

Suppose \(p\in D(w)\) is a real Salem number.
Taking the positive primitive unit \(\sigma\) on its line in its embedded real field gives \(p=\sigma^h\) for \(h\ge1\).
The equality implies \(\mathbb Q(p)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), and no conjugates coalesce in this power.
Their moduli then show that \(\sigma\) is also a Salem number and belongs to \(D(w)\). [Primitive Salem units](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units)
In a common normal field consider the log-modulus functions of all conjugates of \(w\) and of \(\sigma\) on its Galois group.
The latter function is nonzero and is a rational linear combination of those for \(w\) because \(\sigma\in D(w)\).
It has nonzero inner product with at least one member of that family.
Conjugating both functions transfers this to detection of some conjugate of \(\sigma\) by \(w\).
The [Salem projection estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement) gives \(\log\sigma\le m(w)\) for small \(m(w)\).
The [bounded measure comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#chain) now puts \(\sigma\) on the orbit of a coordinate line of one \(z_i\), with \(V_\sigma=V_{z_i}\).

The unit \(\sigma\) is compressed: distinct conjugates in the same torsion class would give another conjugate in the class of its unique exterior root, contradicting uniqueness.
Its degree must be large by the orbit estimate above.
The oriented alternative for this Salem number is impossible.
In the half containing its real exterior root, complex conjugation fixes that root, and must therefore preserve the entire half.
Any other label in its block is neutral; complex conjugation sends its representative to its inverse in the opposite half.
This contradicts preservation of the half.
Thus the [independent signed alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) applies and \(V_\sigma\) is absolutely irreducible by separation of its coordinate characters and transitivity.
The chain description forces \(i=s\).

At matched embeddings \(\mathbb Q(z_s)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\) after choosing the corresponding conjugates, by equality of the point stabilizers of proportional compressed classes. [Root fields on a line](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)
In the real embedding its roots of unity are just the signs.
Primitivity therefore makes \(z_s\) a signed conjugate of a nonzero power of \(\sigma\), and the converse follows by saturation in \(D(w)\).
Two primitive positive Salem values on that orbit of lines must agree.
Indeed a power equality up to torsion between conjugates transports to the positive exterior embedding of the first; the other value there must be an exterior or interior real conjugate of the second, hence that second Salem number or its inverse.
The two primitive classes in the common real field differ only by orientation, and the positive representatives greater than one coincide.
This proves uniqueness and all the degree and size bounds in the statement.

<a id="iteration"></a>
## From a bounded orbit to its terminal value

For any nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), all its iterates are in the group since \(M(x)\) is a signed exterior product.
After one step the iterates are real numbers greater than one and nondecreasing, because \(M(y)\ge y\) for any real unit \(y>1\).
Only finitely many measure values in the group lie below any fixed ceiling. [Local finiteness in the divisible group](Yin_C_G0901_v96.md#count)
A bounded orbit must therefore reach a fixed value.
If \(y=M(y)>1\), only \(y\) itself is an exterior conjugate of this real unit.
If all others are interior it is Pisot.
If a conjugate lies on the circle, its inverse is its complex conjugate, so the minimum polynomial is reciprocal and the only interior conjugate is \(1/y\); the remaining conjugates are neutral, and \(y\) is Salem. [Fixed-point argument](Yin_C_G0257_v1.md#prescribed-perron-entropy)
The Pisot case has just been excluded, and the only positive Salem values present are the powers specified above.
All such powers are fixed: the unique exterior conjugate prevents torsion-class collisions, also after taking any positive power.
Unbounded monotone orbits tend to infinity.

In particular every shrinking sequence with each orbit bounded supplies primitive Salem numbers of no greater positive log measure, and conversely Salem numbers are fixed.
At a sufficiently small strict degree record the chain has only its first, uncompressed-in-degree term, by strict first attainment.
If the Salem line occurs, the record itself is a signed conjugate of \(\sigma\) or \(\sigma^{-1}\), since a proper power would have greater measure at the very same degree.
In that case \(D(w)=D(\sigma)\).
Its radial rank is one and \(M(\sigma)=\sigma\) with \(\sigma\) primitive, so [all its measure values](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#statement) are powers of \(\sigma\) and are fixed after that first step.
In the other case no nontorsion member can have a bounded orbit.
