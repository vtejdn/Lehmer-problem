# A sharp denominator bound for low-degree representatives of Salem trace residues

<a id="statement"></a>
## The denominator and the branch at a genuine delay-one inverse

Let \(P_i\) be the monic minimal polynomials of Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\), with degree \(d_i\).
For each \(i\), let \(f_i\) be a monic Pisot minimal polynomial of the same degree, with positive constant \(q_i\ge2\) and \(zf_i+f_i^*=(z+1)P_i\).
Set \(j_i=[z](f_i/f_i^*)-q_i^2+1\), and write \(w_{i,\ell}>0\) for the [partial-fraction residues](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) at the trace conjugates \(x_{i,\ell}\), including \(T_i=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\).
Take any integers \(b_i\ge1\) and polynomials \(W_i\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(W_i(x_{i,\ell})=b_i w_{i,\ell}\) at all these traces.
If \(K_i=\deg W_i=o(\sqrt{d_i/\log(2d_i)})\), then
\[
                         \liminf_i \frac{b_i j_i}{d_i}\ge2 .
\]
If these ratios are bounded, then eventually \(W_i(2)=0,\ W_i\ge0\) on \([-2,2]\), and \(W_i(z+z^{-1})\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum and support size.
Its constant coefficient \(B_i\) is an even integer at least four, and
\[
                 \frac{b_i j_i}{d_i}
                       =\frac{B_i}{2}+\frac{W_i(T_i)}{d_i}+o(1).
\]
In particular convergence to two forces \(B_i=4\) eventually and \(W_i(T_i)/d_i\to0\).
The threshold two can be attained with denominator one on every hypothetical sequence of Salem numbers tending to one.
Consequently, a sequence achieving \(\limsup b_i j_i/d_i<2\) has \(\liminf K_i\sqrt{\log(2d_i)/d_i}>0\).

<a id="argument"></a>
## Discrete positivity, Fourier support and the trace endpoint

Restrict first to a subsequence with \(b_i j_i/d_i=O(1)\), dropping indices for individual inputs.
Let \(U(z)=W(z+z^{-1})\).
On the \(d-2\) unit-circle conjugates, its empirical absolute mean is \(2(bj-W(T))/(d-2)\).
The [Fourier energy of the conjugates](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#energy) and the [sampling inequality for \(|U|\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#sampling) compare this with the circle \(L^1\) norm, with relative error \(O(K\sqrt{\log(2d)/d})\) uniformly over the coefficients.
Thus the norms are bounded.
The [integer Fourier support bound](../audits/literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) gives bounded term counts, and the integral bounds each integer Fourier coefficient.
By the [local arc count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#arcs) the largest gap between conjugates is \(O(\sqrt{\log(2d)/d})\).
The derivative bound from the absolute coefficient sum therefore gives \(\min U\ge-o(1)\).
The [stabilized integer relations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v134.md#sampling) among its finitely many exponents put every sufficiently late parametrizing circle, on extraction, inside the same limiting subtorus.
The fixed coefficient polynomial is nonnegative everywhere on that subtorus, proving eventual global positivity.

At trace two the [exact endpoint identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v134.md#weights) expresses \(W(T)/(T-2)\) as \(b(q-1)+\sum_{\ell<m}W(x_\ell)/(2-x_\ell)\), where \(m=d/2\).
If \(W(2)>0\) then it is at least one.
The [endpoint estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v134.md#endpoint) chooses an arc with radius much larger than the maximal gap and much smaller than both \(T-2\) and \(1/(K+1)\), using the degree bound of Voutier.
That arc has enough conjugates with \(W\ge1/2\) to force \(W(T)/d\to\infty\), contradicting bounded scaled branch.
Thus \(W(2)=0\).
The [integer circle-mean bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v130.md#mean) applies, and sampling the signed \(U\) itself gives the branch formula.
Restricting to any bounded subsequence proves the unrestricted lower limit.
The [controlled inverse weights](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) at zero scaled exterior mass attain two, with their degree in the stated window by Voutier's bound.
For any initial fixed Salem input above two, take \(g=(x+2)(D_k-2)\) and \(p=\operatorname{rem}_S(gS')\) as in the tail construction.
As \(k\to\infty\), \(g(T)\to\infty\) and all \(g(x_\ell)\) with \(\ell<m\) stay strictly negative and bounded.
The constant \(q=1+\sum_\ell g(x_\ell)\) thus tends to infinity, whereas the strictly positive simplex residues \(b_\ell=(T-x_\ell)(-g(x_\ell))\) have bounded endpoint sum \(\sum_{\ell<m}b_\ell/(2+x_\ell)\) at this fixed input.
For large \(k\) this sum is less than \(q+T/2\), so the [integral inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) gives a Pisot minimal polynomial and \(W=(x-2)g\) gives its actual residues with denominator one.
These finitely many choices preserve all asymptotic assertions.
