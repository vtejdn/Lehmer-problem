# Exact coefficient cost when every prescribed residue class must occur

<a id="statement"></a>
## All rows present or all row values nonzero

Use the fixed unit \(\alpha\), power \(q\), nonempty residue set \(S\), fields \(E,K\), power \(\beta\), width \(B=B(a)\) and rank \(r(T)\) of the [fixed-exponent coefficient problem](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#statement).
Let \(r(\varnothing)=0\), \(k=|S|\), \(m_0=m(\beta)>0\), and set
\[
        \rho_*=\max_{T\subsetneq S}
                      \frac{r(S)-r(T)}{k-|T|}.
\]
For a graph of order at least \(a\) at \(\alpha\), require that all \(k\) prescribed integral row polynomials be nonzero.
Then
\[
                    \lim_{a\to\infty}a^{-1}\log\min\|F\|_1
                          =m_0\rho_* .                                   \tag{1}
\]
If \(r(S\setminus\{i\})=r(S)\) for every slot \(i\), the same limit holds with every row value \(U_i(\beta)\ne0\).
If deleting slot \(i\) reduces the rank, every graph vanishing to order at least \(a\) has \(\operatorname{ord}_\beta U_i\ge a\), allowing the zero polynomial as having infinite order.
Thus in this case the all-values condition is impossible.
The option to omit entire residue classes can change the rate: its value is \(m_0\min_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq S}r(T)/|T|\).

<a id="projection"></a>
## Projecting the vanishing equations onto the visible rows

Write \(G_s=X^sU_s(X^q)\) and let \(L\) be the row space over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) spanned by the coefficient vectors \((\zeta_j^s)_{s\in S}\) of the conjugate sums \(F(\zeta_j X)\), where the embeddings over \(K\) send \(\alpha\) to \(\zeta_j\alpha\).
The rank of its restriction to the coordinates \(T\) is \(r(T)\), by the [embedding calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#wronskian).
For a graph of contact order at least \(t\), every combination of the \(G_s\) prescribed by \(L\) has that order at \(\alpha\).
If deleting \(i\) lowers the rank, \(L\) contains the coordinate functional of \(i\), so \(G_i\) has that order by itself.

We will also count graphs for which one fixed row \(i\) has order at least \(u\) at \(\beta\), with \(0\le u\le t\).
Use \(L'=L+\langle\mathbf e_i\rangle\) and \(r'(T)=\dim L'|_T\).
All combinations from \(L'\) then have order at least \(u\).
With no extra condition take \(L'=L,u=0\).
Define the set function
\[
                  v(T)=(1-u/t)r(T)+(u/t)r'(T).
\]
Let \(C,J\) be disjoint, \(J\ne\varnothing\), and consider a graph satisfying these conditions, with all its nonzero rows in \(C\cup J\) and every row of \(J\) present.
The combinations from \(L\) which vanish identically on the coefficient slots in \(C\) restrict on \(J\) to a space of dimension \(r(C\cup J)-r(C)\).
The corresponding space from \(L'\) contains it and has dimension \(r'(C\cup J)-r'(C)\).
This follows by subtracting the rank on \(C\) from the rank on \(C\cup J\).
Take a basis of the smaller space, extend to the larger, and then to all \(|J|\) coefficient slots.
In the [Euler Wronskian](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#wronskian) of the rows in \(J\) this change of columns gives the first combinations order at least \(t\) and the additional ones order at least \(u\).
Differentiation through order \(|J|-1\) costs at most \(k-1\) per column.
The determinant itself is an integral polynomial in \(X^q\) times a monomial, so its order at \(\beta\) in the powered variable is at least \(t[v(C\cup J)-v(C)]-k(k-1)\).
Consequently, whenever all row degrees are at most \(b\ge1\),
\[
 \log\max_{s\in J}\|U_s\|_\infty
     \ \ge\ t m_0\,\frac{v(C\cup J)-v(C)}{|J|}-O(\log(2+b)).            \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\|U_s\|_\infty\) is maximum coefficient modulus.
The Wronskian norm estimate introduces only powers of \(b+1\), since \(q,k,\alpha\) are fixed.
All constants are uniform in \(t,u,C,J\) and the coefficients.
For the lower bound of (1) apply (2) with \(C=T,\ J=S\setminus T,\ u=0\) at every proper subset \(T\), using \(\log(B+2)=o(a)\).

<a id="packing"></a>
## Counting a fiber with one extra restriction

Fix an integer box of side \(H\ge1\) for all \(k(b+1)\) coefficients.
Consider a subset of this box whose pairwise differences satisfy the two order conditions just used.
If a specified row is constant across the subset, let \(D=S\setminus\{i\}\) for that slot.
Otherwise use \(D=S\).
Fixed coordinates outside \(D\) disappear in all differences.
For \(h=(\log H)/(tm_0)\) the size of the subset is at most
\[
 \exp\!\left((b+1)t m_0\max_{T\subseteq D}
                 \{\,|T|h-v(T)\,\}+O((b+1)\log(2+b))\right).             \tag{3}
\]
To prove this choose a chain \(\varnothing=D_0\subsetneq D_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq D_j=D\).
At each step add a nonempty set attaining the smallest incremental density
\[
       \lambda_j=\frac{v(D_j)-v(D_{j-1})}{|D_j\setminus D_{j-1}|}
\]
among additions from the remaining slots, taking any minimizer.
Here the same definition applies with the current step index in place of \(j\).
The slopes are nondecreasing: a smaller next slope would make the union of the two additions cheaper per slot than the first minimum.
Expose the blocks in reverse order.
With all later blocks fixed, different values of the current projected block give a difference supported on earlier slots and some nonempty subset \(J\) of the current block.
Apply (2) with \(C=D_{j-1}\) at that step.
In the maximum coefficient norm these projections are separated by at least \(\exp(t m_0\lambda_j-O(\log(2+b)))\).
In a cube of side \(H\) the number of such projections is at most \((1+2H/\mathrm{separation})^{(b+1)|D_j\setminus D_{j-1}|}\) by packing cubes.
Multiplying over the blocks, the leading sum in the logarithm is \((b+1)t m_0\) times \(\sum_j|D_j\setminus D_{j-1}|(h-\lambda_j)_+\).
By monotonicity that sum equals \(|D_j|h-v(D_j)\) at the end of the initial run with \(\lambda_j<h\), with the empty set allowed.
It is therefore at most the maximum in (3).

<a id="active"></a>
## Producing rows on every slot

Fix \(h_0>\rho_*\), take \(H=\lceil\exp(tm_0h_0)\rceil\), and write \(h=\log H/(tm_0)\).
Use \(b=\min(B(a)-1,t^2)\) at \(t=a\) or \(t=\lceil(1+\epsilon)a\rceil\), so \(f=b/t\to\infty\).
In the [integral jet box](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#boxes) take all \(k\) rows and \(r(S)\) independent \(K\)-coordinates for each jet.
At least one fiber of the first \(t\) divided derivatives has logarithmic size, divided by \((b+1)t m_0\), at least
\[
        kh-r(S)-O(\eta),\qquad
        \eta=\frac{\log(2+f)}{f}+\frac{\log(2t)}{t}.                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the logarithm of the box size is at least \(k(b+1)\log H\).
The subtraction for the count of distinct jet outputs uses \(r(S)bt m_0\), the term \(r(S)[K:\mathbb Q]t\log(Ck(b+1)H)\), and \(O(t^2\log(2+f))\) from the binomials.
For every proper \(T\subsetneq S\) one has
\[
                    kh-r(S)>(|T|h-r(T))+(h_0-\rho_*)/2
\]
for all sufficiently large \(t\), directly from the definition of \(\rho_*\).
If slot \(i\) were constant on that fiber, (3) with \(D=S\setminus\{i\}\) and no extra condition would contradict (4).
Hence at each prescribed \(i\) there is a difference in the fiber having a nonzero row on that slot.
Combine at most \(k\) such differences using coefficients \(1,n,\ldots,n^{k-1}\).
For each slot at least one coefficient of the combined row is a nonzero polynomial in \(n\) of degree at most \(k-1\).
One integer \(1\le n\le k^2+1\) avoids all these zeros simultaneously.
At \(t=a\) the combined graph has the required multiplicity and \(\log\|F\|_1\le t m_0 h+O(\log(2t))\).
Let \(h_0\downarrow\rho_*\) to prove the upper bound in (1).

<a id="nonzero"></a>
## Making every value nonzero

Suppose no deletion lowers rank.
For a fixed slot \(i\), adding \(\mathbf e_i\) to \(L\) raises its rank on \(S\) by one, so \(v(S)=r(S)+u/t\) and \(v(T)\ge r(T)\) on every subset.
Set \(u=\lceil t\sqrt{\eta}\rceil\), which is at most \(t\) eventually.
If all differences on the fiber of (4) had \(\operatorname{ord}_\beta U_i\ge u\), (3) with \(D=S\) would contradict (4).
Indeed the term at \(S\) loses \(u/t\ge\sqrt{\eta}\), all proper subsets lose at least the preceding fixed margin, and the error in (3) after normalization is \(O(\log(2+b)/t)=O(\eta)\).
For each \(i\) there is therefore a difference with a finite row order \(c_i<u\).
Apply \(X^{c_i}(d/dX)^{c_i}/c_i!\) to that difference.
The resulting graph has a nonzero value on slot \(i\) at \(\beta\), and order at least \(t-c_i\) at \(\alpha\).
The coefficient norm costs at most the factor \(\binom{q(b+1)}{c_i}\).
Its logarithm is \(o(t)\) by the [divided derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#values), since \(\sqrt{\eta}\log(2+f)\to0\) and \(f\le t\).
Take \(t=\lceil(1+\epsilon)a\rceil\) so all the graphs retain order \(a\) eventually.
Combine them using the same bounded integer choices as above, now avoiding zeros of the row values at \(\beta\).
Letting \(h_0\downarrow\rho_*\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves the asserted nonzero-value limit.

<a id="example"></a>
## A cost increase for three specified residues

Take \(\alpha=\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}\), \(q=4\), and \(S=\{0,1,2\}\).
The polynomial \(X^4-4X^2+1\) makes \(\alpha\) an algebraic unit, and \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^4)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
One has \(\alpha\notin K\), since the root is \((\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2\) and \(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}=\sqrt2\notin K\).
Thus slots \(0,2\) span one dimension and slot \(1\) adds a second.
The rate with omitted rows allowed is \(m(\beta)/2\), whereas (1) gives \(m(\beta)\) for all three rows present.
Every such row in slot \(1\) must vanish to the full order at \(\beta\).
