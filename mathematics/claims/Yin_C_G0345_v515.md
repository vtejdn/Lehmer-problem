# Many common roots in prime fields for rows on a full interval

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree-one roots of large order at superlinear characteristics

Fix a computable positive integer function \(g(L)\to\infty\) on integers \(L\ge2\).
Let \(S\) be all primes in \([L,2L]\), put \(r=|S|\), \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\) and \(m=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\).
There is a computable integer \(j=j(L)\to\infty\), no larger than the [width multiplier for arbitrary deletions](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width), with the following property at width \(b=rj\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
Suppose \(U,V\) pass the [common row test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) on \(S\), and one reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) occurs with multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\) in every graph.
Write \(e=\deg P\) and let \(B>0\) generate \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\).

There are at least \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) distinct rational primes
\[
            1+\tfrac52 jL\le\ell\le 1+\tfrac72 jL
\]
at which all of the following hold on this single pair of rows.
The integer \(n=(\ell-1)/j\) has at least \(L/(16m^2)\) ordered representations \(p+q=n\) by distinct primes of \(S\).
The reduction of \(P\) splits into distinct linear factors over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
Each root \(z\) lies in \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\) and has order \(t\mid n\) satisfying \(t>\sqrt{\ell}/g(L)\).
At each such representation the \(p\)-powered and \(q\)-powered minima of \(P\) agree modulo \(\ell\), split completely with distinct roots and divide both \(\bar U,\bar V\).

For each one of these \(z\), the common roots \(z^p\) as the representation varies all have exact order \(t\), and occur at
\[
           M_z\ \ge\ \frac{L}{16m^2(1+L/t)}
                    \ \gg\ \frac{\sqrt{\ell}}{g(L)m^2}                  \tag{1}
\]
distinct elements of the prime field.
At their unique \(n\)-th root of unity lifts in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) both rows vanish modulo \(\ell^k\).
In particular \(\ell^k\mid B\), and \((\mathbb Z/\ell^k)^{M_z}\) is an additive quotient of \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(Y^n-1,U,V)\).
One may choose \(g(L)=1+\lfloor\log_2(1+\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor)\rfloor\).
The local statement controls each root's actual multiplicative order in \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\).

Vanishing of the unrestricted Lehmer infimum is equivalent to existence of same-seed rows with multiplicity at least two and \(\log H/\Sigma\to0\) along these full intervals, where \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
The seed may vary with \(L\).

<a id="goldbach"></a>
## A localized estimate for sums of two distinct primes

All but \(O(L/(\log L)^3)\) even integers \(n\in[5L/2,7L/2]\) have at least \(L/(16m^2)\) ordered representations as in the statement.
Write \(\mathrm e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)\), \(F(\theta)=\sum_{p\in S}(\log p)\mathrm e(p\theta)\), and \(D=(\log L)^{30}\).
Use the major arcs \(\theta=a/v+\beta\), \(v\le D\), \(\gcd(a,v)=1\), \(|\beta|\le2D/(vL)\), on the circle.
For large \(L\) these are disjoint by the spacing \(1/(vv')\) of distinct reduced fractions.
The bounds used in the [dyadic prime sum calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#fourth-moment) apply with the following logarithmic margins.
Outside the arcs, Dirichlet approximation with denominator cutoff \(\lfloor L/D\rfloor\) gives \(D<v\le L/D\) and \(|\theta-a/v|\le v^{-2}\) up to an integer shift.
[Vaughan's one-frequency estimate](../audits/literature/Vau77/Yin_U001.md#prime-exponential-sum) at both interval endpoints and the \(O(\sqrt L(\log L)^2)\) higher prime-power weight give \(|F|\ll L/(\log L)^8\) there.
As \(\int |F|^2\ll L\log L\), Bessel's inequality bounds the sum over integer \(n\) of the squared absolute minor-arc Fourier coefficients of \(F^2\) by \(O(L^3/(\log L)^{15})\).
At most \(O(L/(\log L)^{15})\) integers thus have a minor-arc error exceeding \(L/8\).

Put \(I(\beta)=\int_L^{2L}\mathrm e(\beta x)\,dx\).
On a major arc the [Siegel–Walfisz estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) with logarithmic exponent \(150\) gives
\[
             F(a/v+\beta)=\frac{\mu(v)}{\varphi(v)}I(\beta)
                                   +O(L/(\log L)^{110}).
\]
Indeed summing the prime-weight errors in the reduced classes after partial summation costs at most \(v(1+L|\beta|)\le3D\) times their initial error, as in the dyadic calculation, and the higher prime-power errors are absorbed.
All primes in \(S\) are coprime to these denominators.
The total arc measure is \(O(D^2/L)\).
Since \(|F|+|I|\ll L\), replacing \(F^2\) by the squared main term therefore costs \(o(L)\) uniformly in each Fourier coefficient.

Write \(c_v(n)=\sum_{(a,v)=1}\mathrm e(-na/v)\), summing over a reduced residue system.
The integral of \(I(\beta)^2\mathrm e(-n\beta)\) over the real line is the triangle \(J(n)=|[L,2L]\cap[n-2L,n-L]|\), by Fourier inversion for the convolution of two interval indicators.
Here bars denote interval length.
The tail outside \(|\beta|\le2D/(vL)\) is \(O(vL/D)\) since \(|I(\beta)|\ll\min(L,|\beta|^{-1})\).
After summing the reduced numerators, the error from extending every arc integral and then completing the sum to all \(v\) is at most
\[
      O(LD^{-1/3}A(n)),\qquad
      A(n)=\sum_{v\ge1}\frac{\mu(v)^2 v^{1/3}|c_v(n)|}{\varphi(v)^2}.
\]
For \(v\le D\) use \(v/D\le (v/D)^{1/3}\), and for \(v>D\) use \(1\le(v/D)^{1/3}\).
The Ramanujan sums are multiplicative in their modulus by the Chinese remainder theorem, and \(c_u(n)=u-1\) if a prime \(u\) divides \(n\), otherwise \(c_u(n)=-1\).
Thus
\[
               A(n)\ll\prod_{u\mid n,\ u\ {\rm prime}}(1+C u^{-2/3})
\]
for an absolute \(C\), since the factors at other primes have product bounded by \(\prod_u(1+O(u^{-5/3}))\).
Expansion over squarefree divisors and \(\#\{1\le n\le4L:d\mid n\}\le4L/d\) show that \(\sum_{n\le4L}A(n)\ll L\), by convergence of \(\prod_u(1+C u^{-5/3})\).
Outside \(O(LD^{-1/3})\) exceptions, the tail error is less than \(L/16\).

The completed main term is \(J(n)\prod_u(1+c_u(n)/(u-1)^2)\).
For even \(n\) the product is at least \(2\prod_{u>2}(1-1/(u-1)^2)\ge1\), using the product of \(1-1/i^2\) over all integers \(i\ge2\).
On the specified interval \(J(n)\ge L/2\).
For large \(L\) the weighted count, after the possible repeated-prime term of at most \((\log(2L))^2\) is removed, is at least \(L/16\) outside the two exceptional sets.
Each ordered pair contributes at most \(m^2\), proving the claimed unweighted count.

<a id="splitting"></a>
## Selecting rational characteristics for each fixed seed and multiplier

Fix \(P\) and a positive integer \(j\).
Take a finite normal splitting field \(K\) containing the \(2j\)-th roots of unity and all \(j\)-th roots of the roots of \(P\).
These radicals are algebraic integer units.
By the [fixed-field Chebotarev counting theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) for the identity class, the primes splitting completely in \(K\) in the interval in the statement number \(\asymp_{P,j} L/\log L\).
This follows by subtracting the counting asymptotics at endpoints with fixed distinct positive leading constants.
Omit the finitely many primes dividing \(2j\operatorname{disc}P\).
All remaining splitting primes are \(1\bmod 2j\), their reduced seed roots are distinct \(j\)-th powers in the prime field, and each such root has order dividing the even integer \(n=(\ell-1)/j\).
The localized estimate discards \(O(L/(\log L)^3)\) splitting primes since \(n\) determines \(\ell\).

If some reduced root of \(P\) has order at most \(T=\lfloor\sqrt{1+7jL/2}/g(L)\rfloor\), the corresponding prime divides
\[
         \prod_{t=1}^{T}|\operatorname{Res}(P,X^t-1)|.
\]
This is a positive integer, since \(P\) has no root of unity.
The logarithm of the product is at most \(\sum_{t\le T}(t\log M(P)+e\log2)\), by bounding each \(|\alpha^t-1|\) at the complex roots.
Each prime counted in the specified interval contributes \(\gg\log L\), so at fixed \(P,j\) the number discarded is \(O_P((T^2+T)/\log L)=o_{P,j}(L/\log L)\).
This argument allows coincidence among the orders of different roots.
It supplies the required lower bound on every order at every surviving prime.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Computability and the original row test

For each \(2\le j\le j_{513}(L)\), where \(j_{513}\) is the [computable width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width), test all monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P\) of degree at most \(j\) and \(M(P)\le2\) using the [finite enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width) with coefficient bound \(2\binom ei\).
Require for each \(P\) at least \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) primes in the stated interval that split \(P\) into distinct linear factors modulo \(\ell\), have \(\ell\equiv1\bmod 2j\), and give \(z^n=1\) and \(g(L)^2\operatorname{ord}(z)^2>\ell\) at every root.
Also require at each counted prime the stated finite number of representations of \(n\).
These are exact integer, finite-field and real algebraic comparisons.
No bound on the discriminant of a varying splitting field is used.
Take the largest passing \(j\), with fallback one.
For each fixed trial \(j\) the finite seed list and the preceding positive proportion among the split primes ensure eventual passage, while \(j_{513}(L)\to\infty\).
Consequently \(j(L)\to\infty\).

Every qualifying common seed satisfies \(re\le b\), \(\Sigma\log M(P)\le\log H\le\Sigma\log2\), by the [tangency inequalities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies), so it is included in these tests.
If the unrestricted infimum is zero, the same-seed [interpolation and its cost](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) give, for each fixed reciprocal unit seed of small positive measure and all sufficiently large \(L\), actual double contacts of width at most \(2er+2\) with normalized cost at most \(2\log M(P)+o_P(1)\).
The [reciprocal reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) supplies arbitrarily small choices and the widths fit for large \(L\).
Selecting successively smaller fixed seeds on successive tails gives the claimed sequence.
Conversely the tangency inequalities show that vanishing normalized cost forces positive seed measures tending to zero.
The existence of these low-cost rows remains open.

## The powered minima and repeated integral congruences

Fix a certified \(\ell\) and any representation \(n=p+q\) with distinct \(p,q\in S\).
One has \(\gcd(p,n)=1\), so powering by \(p\) permutes the group of \(n\)-th roots of unity and preserves orders.
As \(P\) is reciprocal, the set of \(q\)-th powers of its reduced roots is the same set as that of \(p\)-th powers.
The original tangencies preserve the powered degrees, so these are the roots of the respective reduced powered minima, all distinct.

Let \(z\) be any reduced root and \(\xi\in\mathbb Z_\ell\) its lift with \(\xi^n=1\), unique by Hensel's lemma since \(\ell\nmid n\).
As \(P(\xi)\in\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\), the [integral sum-period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement) gives
\[
       U(\xi^p),V(\xi^p)\in P(\xi)^k\mathbb Z_\ell
                                      \subseteq\ell^k\mathbb Z_\ell .
\]
This holds for every reduced root, yielding divisibility of both rows by the common split powered minimum modulo \(\ell\).
For fixed \(z\) of order \(t\), any two representations with the same \(z^p\) have their first primes in one class modulo \(t\).
Among the integers in \([L,2L]\) a class occurs at most \(1+L/t\) times.
The lower bound on distinct partners therefore gives the first inequality of (1).
The second follows from \(L/(1+L/t)\ge\min(L,t)/2\), since \(j\le j_{513}(L)\le(\log_2 L)^{1/4}\) for large \(L\) and therefore \(\sqrt{\ell}/g(L)\le L\).
Evaluating an integral identity for \(B\) at any such lift proves \(\ell^k\mid B\).
For the set of all \(M_z\) distinct lifts, each difference between two lifts is a unit.
Evaluation onto the corresponding product of copies of \(\mathbb Z_\ell/\ell^k\) is surjective by the Chinese remainder theorem over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
Reducing coefficients modulo \(\ell^k\) gives integer polynomial representatives as well.
Each lift kills \(Y^n-1\) and both rows modulo \(\ell^k\), giving the claimed quotient.

A collaborating research agent for Sheng Yin derived the exceptional-set bound and the multiplicity of distinct degree-one roots on each qualifying row.
