# The possible length-six multiples of a Salem polynomial near one

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reduction to one reciprocal family

Let \(\lambda=e^t>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\), and let its minimal polynomial divide a nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) whose coefficient length \(\|P\|_1\), the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients, is at most six.
There is an absolute \(t_0>0\) such that for \(0<t<t_0\), removal of a monomial and an overall sign gives
\[
 P(X)=X^N Q(X)+\sigma Q^*(X),\qquad
 Q(X)=X^a-X^b-1,\qquad 0\le b<a<N,\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},       \tag{1}
\]
where \(Q^*(X)=X^aQ(1/X)\).
The case \(b=0\) gives \(Q=X^a-2\).
In particular, the two blocks both have length three and the full multiple has length exactly six.

Let \(D=N+a\) be its degree after monomial removal and \(g=N-a\) the gap between the two blocks.
With absolute implied constants,
\[
 d\ge\frac1{20t^2},\qquad gt\gg d,\qquad
 \frac1t\ll a\ll\frac{\log(2/t)}{t},\qquad
            |\lambda^a-\lambda^b-1|\le 3e^{-gt}.                    \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(D\gg t^{-3}\), and \(g/D\) tends to one along any sequence with \(t\to0\).
Existence of such a sequence among length-six multiples is equivalent to existence among (1), since each polynomial in (1) has length six.
Whether the Salem factors in this remaining family can approach one remains open.

<a id="scales"></a>
## Both sides of a largest gap

Normalize \(P\) by removing its common monomial, so \(P(0)\ne0\), and write \(D=\deg P\).
It has \(s\) distinct nonzero monomials with \(2\le s\le6\), since a monomial cannot vanish at \(\lambda\).
There is a gap \(g\ge D/(s-1)\ge D/5\) between two consecutive support exponents.
At any largest such gap write \(P=X^N B+H\), with \(B(0)H(0)\ne0\), \(a=\deg B\), \(c=\deg H\), and \(g=N-c\).
For a polynomial \(J\) with nonzero constant coefficient write \(J^*(X)=X^{\deg J}J(1/X)\).

The logarithmic Mahler measure of the Salem minimal polynomial is \(t\).
Only the reciprocal real pair has nonzero logarithmic modulus, so the [quadratic logarithmic bound for units](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(2dt^2\ge1/10\).
[Dobrowolski's divisor estimate](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) applied to this monic noncyclotomic divisor gives \(e^t-1\ge 0.31d/(s!D)\).
Since \(e^t-1\le2t\) for small \(t\), we have \(gt\gg d\gg t^{-2}\).

The minimal polynomial is reciprocal, so both \(P(\lambda)\) and \(P^*(\lambda)\) vanish.
The reversed decomposition is \(P^*=X^{D-c}H^*+B^*\) and its gap is also \(g\).
Each lower block has length at most six.
Evaluating at \(\lambda\) in the two orientations yields
\[
                     |B(\lambda)|,\ |H^*(\lambda)|\le6e^{-gt}.       \tag{3}
\]
If either block had length at most two, its outward-facing polynomial \(B\) or \(H^*\) would be a nonzero integer or a signed binomial \(X^v\pm1\) up to overall sign, \(v\ge1\).
Its absolute value at \(\lambda\) would be at least \(\min(1,e^t-1)\).
This exceeds (3) for all sufficiently small \(t\), by \(gt\gg t^{-2}\).
Since both lengths sum to at most six, both are exactly three, and the common upper bound in (3) improves to \(3e^{-gt}\).

<a id="widths"></a>
## The signs and degrees of the outward-facing blocks

Suppose \(J(0)\ne0\), \(\|J\|_1=3\), and \(|J(\lambda)|<1/2\).
Choose the leading coefficient positive by an overall sign.
A monomial is then the constant \(3\).
For a two-term polynomial the absolute endpoint coefficients are \(1,2\) in some order.
Equal signs give a value at least three, and \(2X^a-1\) is greater than one at \(\lambda\), so only \(X^a-2\) remains.
For a three-term polynomial all coefficients have absolute value one.
Writing the polynomial as \(X^a\pm X^b\pm1\) with \(0<b<a\), the only choice with value of absolute size below \(1/2\) at \(\lambda>1\) is \(X^a-X^b-1\).
Indeed \(X^a+X^b-1\) and \(X^a-X^b+1\) already exceed one there.
Thus both \(B\) and \(H^*\) in (3) have the shape \(Q_{a,b}=X^a-X^b-1\) up to their separate signs, allowing \(b=0\).

For any such normalized block (using \(a,b\) temporarily for its own exponents) one has
\[
             \tfrac12<e^{bt}(e^{(a-b)t}-1)<\tfrac32 .
\]
Hence \(e^{at}>3/2\), \(e^{(a-b)t}<5/2\), and \(e^{bt}<3/(2t)\) because \(a-b\ge1\).
These inequalities give \(1/t\ll a\ll \log(2/t)/t\) for each outward-facing degree, including the binomial case.
In particular \(W=\max(\deg B,\deg H)\ll\log(2/t)/t\), so \(2W<d\) for all sufficiently small \(t\).

<a id="autocorrelation"></a>
## Equality of the two autocorrelations

For an integral polynomial \(J\) put \(T_J(X)=J(X)J(X^{-1})\).
Its Laurent coefficients are the aperiodic autocorrelations of the coefficient sequence, and \(T_{J^*}=T_J\).
At \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^{-1}\), the equations \(X^NB(X)=-H(X)\) hold.
Their product gives \(T_B(\lambda)=T_H(\lambda)\).
Consequently the integral polynomial \(X^W(T_B-T_H)\) of degree at most \(2W<d\) vanishes at \(\lambda\), so it is identically zero.
This proves \(T_B=T_{H^*}\) for the actual blocks.

For \(C_j(X)=X^j+X^{-j}\), direct multiplication gives
\[
                T_{Q_{a,b}}=3-C_a-C_{a-b}+C_b                       \tag{4}
\]
also at \(b=0\), where \(C_0=2\).
The largest positive exponent determines \(a\), and its coefficient is \(-2\) at \(b=0\) and \(-1\) otherwise.
For \(0<b<a\) with \(2b\ne a\), the sole positive coefficient at an exponent strictly between zero and \(a\) occurs at \(b\).
For \(b>0\), all those intermediate coefficients vanish precisely when \(2b=a\).
Thus (4) determines both exponents, including the equally spaced case.
The outward-facing polynomials \(B,H^*\) must be the same \(Q_{a,b}\) up to their signs.
Returning to the original orientation gives (1), with \(a=c\), \(g=N-a>0\), and the final bound in (2) from (3).
Finally \(D=g+2a\) and \(a/D\ll t^2\log(2/t)\), by the preceding degree and gap bounds.
