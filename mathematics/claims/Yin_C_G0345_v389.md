# Prime powers in fields of unit factors of primitive plane sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite set of primes for a fixed plane polynomial

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[U,V]\) be primitive and absolutely irreducible, divisible by neither coordinate, with support differences of rank two.
Write \(D=\deg F\) and \(L=\sum |F_{ij}|\), where \(F_{ij}\) is the coefficient of \(U^iV^j\).
Call a prime \(p\) good here if \(\bar F\) is absolutely irreducible and the differences between exponents with coefficients nonzero modulo \(p\) span \(\mathbb F_p^2\).
All but finitely many primes are good by [Ruppert's reduction theorem](../audits/literature/Rup99/Yin_U001.md#rup99-u001) and a nonzero determinant of two support differences.

Take any primitive \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\), any nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) satisfying \(F(\alpha^a,\alpha^b)=0\), and set \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
If \(\epsilon\alpha\in K^p\) for a good prime \(p\) and \(\epsilon=1\) or \(-1\), then
\[
       d\le (p+2)D^2,\qquad
       p^2\le20d(\log L)^2,\qquad
       p\le40D^2(\log L)^2.                                          \tag{1}
\]
In particular for
\[
                 d>D^2\bigl(40D^2(\log L)^2+2\bigr)                  \tag{2}
\]
and every positive \(q\) with only good prime divisors, the minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(\alpha\) has \(P(X^q)\) irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), of degree \(qd\) and the same logarithmic Mahler measure.
Condition (2) holds for all such unit factors in every sufficiently large primitive direction, by the [uniform degree growth of the individual factors](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).
For fixed \(F\) only finitely many primes, including the nongood ones, can ever give a prime power in the root field of any such factor.

<a id="tangencies"></a>
## Total excess multiplicity at a good prime

Replacing \(\alpha\) by \(-\alpha\) replaces \(F\) by \(F((-1)^aU,(-1)^bV)\).
This preserves \(D,L\) and the good primes, so consider \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) in \(K\).
Then \(\gamma\) and its inverse are integral and \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K\).
Write \(f=\bar F\), \(E_1=U\partial_U,\ E_2=V\partial_V\).
For every nonzero vector \((c_1,c_2)\in\mathbb F_p^2\), the polynomial \(f\) does not divide \(c_1E_1 f+c_2E_2 f\).
Indeed divisibility would give a scalar multiple of \(f\) by the degree bound, including the zero multiple.
Every support exponent \((i,j)\) of \(f\) would have the same value of \(c_1i+c_2j\), contrary to the spanning hypothesis.

The monomial section \(H\) parametrized by \(X\mapsto(X^a,X^b)\) is a smooth one-dimensional subtorus over the residue field, with parameter recovered by Bézout in \(a,b\).
The restriction of \(f\) to \(H\) is nonzero, since an irreducible curve containing \(H\) would have the binomial of \(H\) as a Laurent factor, contrary to the support condition.
Write \(m_x\) for the multiplicity of a nonzero root \(x\) of this restriction in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
On \(H\) the restriction of \(aE_1f+bE_2f\) is \(X\) times the derivative of the restriction of \(f\), so its order at \(x\) is at least \(m_x-1\).
The local intersection length of the two plane equations at \((x^a,x^b)\) is at least \(m_x-1\): quotienting their local ring further by the equation of \(H\) gives the quotient of a discrete valuation ring by two elements of orders at least \(m_x-1\).
The vector \((a,b)\bmod p\) is nonzero by primitivity, so the two plane polynomials have no common component.
Distinct \(x\)'s give distinct points.
Plane Bézout yields
\[
                   \sum_x(m_x-1)\le D^2 .                              \tag{3}
\]
This covers inseparable zero multiplicities and negative exponents in the section.

<a id="correction"></a>
## Residues of the conjugates and the Frobenius correction

At a fixed place over \(p\) in the splitting field of \(P\), all conjugates of \(\alpha\) are units.
The monic \(P\) divides a monomial normalization of \(F(X^a,X^b)\) over the integers.
Each nonzero residue therefore contains at most \(m_x\) of its conjugates.
The nonsimple residues together account for at most \(2D^2\) conjugates by (3).
Each remaining residue contains just one conjugate, which is fixed by inertia, so this conjugate and the corresponding conjugate of \(\gamma\in K\) lie in an unramified local extension.

For \(u=\gamma^a,v=\gamma^b\) at any such embedding, \(F(u^p,v^p)=0\) and \(F(u,v)\) has valuation at least one by the unramified value group and Frobenius.
The residue pair belongs to the two curves with equations \(f=0\) and \(J=0\), where \(J\) is the reduction of
\[
                 (F(U^p,V^p)-F(U,V)^p)/p .
\]
The quotient is integral and its degree is at most \(pD\).
We use the [differential argument of Voloch](../audits/literature/Vol99/Yin_U001.md#vol99-u001) in the function field of \(f=0\), checking its support condition also at the small good primes.
There \(E_i J=(E_i f)^p\) for \(i=1,2\), by differentiation before reduction.
If \(J=fh\), differentiation on the curve would give \((E_i f)^p=h E_i f\) for both \(i\).
Both \(E_i f\) are nonzero in its function field, by the coefficient comparison above.
Their ratio would satisfy \(z^{p-1}=1\), hence belong to \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), again contradicting that comparison.
Thus \(f\) and \(J\) have at most \(pD^2\) distinct intersection points by Bézout.

Different simple residues of \(\alpha\) give distinct residues of \(\gamma\), whose \(p\)-th powers already differ.
The pair of powers \((\bar u,\bar v)\) recovers \(\bar\gamma\) by primitivity.
Counting the intersection points and then restoring the at most \(2D^2\) previously omitted conjugates proves the first bound in (1).

<a id="height"></a>
## The archimedean bound and all admissible dilation exponents

The specialized Laurent polynomial is nonzero, and its coefficient one-norm is at most \(L\) after coincident terms are collected.
Consequently \(m(P)\le\log L\) by Jensen's inequality and factorization over the integers.
The positive conjugate log moduli of \(\alpha\) sum to \(m(P)\), and the negative ones to \(-m(P)\).
Their squares therefore sum to at most \(2m(P)^2\).
Part A of the [all-root square bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) for the unit \(\gamma\) of degree \(d\) gives \(p^2\le20d\,m(P)^2\), since each conjugate log modulus is divided by \(p\) on passing to \(\gamma\).
As \(p+2\le2p\), the first inequality of (1) gives the last.
Under (2) neither sign of \(\alpha\) is a \(p\)-th power for any good \(p\).
The [prime binomial and norm induction](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions) then applies to every \(q\) with prime divisors in that set, including repeated primes.
The root lifts give the stated measure equality.

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## Reciprocal examples of every seed degree

For \(s\ge3\), set \(g_s=U^s-U-1\), \(B_s=1-U^{s-1}-U^s\) and \(F_s=g_s+VB_s\).
Absolute irreducibility in any characteristic follows from coprimality of the two coefficients of this linear polynomial in \(V\), whenever they are coprime there.
A common root \(t\) must satisfy \(t^s=t+1,\ t^{s-2}=-1\); in particular \(t^2+t+1=0\), \(t^3=1\).
In odd characteristic the order dividing three excludes \(t^{s-2}=-1\).
In characteristic two, \(t\) then has order three and the two coefficient polynomials have a common root exactly when \(s\equiv2\bmod3\).
These arguments also give coprimality in characteristic zero.
The support at \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\) ensures the required rank at every prime.
For \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\), all primes are good and every sufficiently large primitive section has all its nontorsion unit factors irreducible under every common dilation.
For \(s\equiv2\bmod3\) the same conclusion holds for every odd dilation, with the status of even dilations left open by this argument.
The reciprocal specialization in positive directions has unit endpoints and all of its factors are unit polynomials.
For the seed \(s=3\) the [exact cubic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v388.md#statement) also includes every small positive direction.

<a id="failure"></a>
## A chosen prime can produce splittings in unbounded degree

Fix any prime \(\ell\) and let
\[
     F_\ell(U,V)=\operatorname{Res}_Z(Z^\ell-U,\ V-(1-Z)^\ell).
\]
Over \(\mathbb C(z^\ell)\) the element \((1-z)^\ell\) has all \(\ell\) distinct conjugates \((1-\zeta z)^\ell\) as \(\zeta\) ranges over the \(\ell\)-th roots of unity, as seen from their linear coefficients in \(z\).
Thus the resultant is monic and irreducible in \(V\) over \(\mathbb C(U)\), and primitive over \(\mathbb C[U]\).
It has rank-two support: its restrictions to \(U=0\) and \(V=0\) are \((V-1)^\ell\) and \(\pm(1-U)^\ell\), respectively.
In characteristic \(\ell\) it reduces to \((V+U-1)^\ell\).

For every \(n=1+\ell j,\ j\ge1\), take the unique real \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) with \(\gamma^n+\gamma=1\).
It is a nontorsion algebraic unit, and \(\alpha=\gamma^\ell\) satisfies \(F_\ell(\alpha,\alpha^n)=0\) and \(\gamma=1/(1+\alpha^j)\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If \(P\) is its minimal polynomial, \(P(X^\ell)\) has a root of degree \(\deg P\), so is reducible.
The roots \(\gamma\) are distinct as \(n\) varies and have \(m(\gamma)\le\log3\) by the trinomial equation.
Their degrees tend to infinity by finiteness in bounded degree and height, and these are the degrees of the corresponding \(\alpha\).
Thus one cannot extend the all-good-prime assertion to arbitrary primes for all fixed curves by deleting a finite set of primitive directions.
