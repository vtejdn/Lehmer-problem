# Periods of the residual factors of reciprocal tetranomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## The squarefree degree and the orders actually represented

Let \(P=A(X^D+\sigma)+B(X^{D-k}+\sigma X^k)\) with \(D>2k>0,\ \gcd(D,k)=\gcd(A,B)=1,\ AB\ne0\), and \(\sigma=\pm1\).
Fix a prime \(p\mid AB\).
Use \(T=D,\ w=v_p(B)\) when \(p\mid B\) and \(T=D-2k,\ w=v_p(A)\) when \(p\mid A\).
Put \(N=p^{v_p(T)},\ h=T/N\).
For a monic irreducible nontorsion unit factor \(S\), let \(d=\deg S,\ m=m(S)\), and let \(a_p\) be the degree of the product of the distinct monic irreducible factors of its reduction \(\bar S\) at \(p\).
Write \(\mathcal S\) for the set of multiplicative orders of the nonzero roots of \(\bar S\).
For \(d>2\) one has
\[
                       d\gg \frac{N a_p}{w p^w}.                        \tag{1}
\]
For any nonempty subset of represented orders let \(V\) be their least common multiple, and let \(\delta\) be the proportion of the \(d\) roots of \(\bar S\) having these orders, counted with multiplicity.
For every integer \(i\ge0\) one has
\[
           m\ \ge\ \frac{[(i+1)\delta\log p-\log2]\,d}{p^iNV}.
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for \(d>2\),
\[
           m\gg \frac{\delta\,a_p}{p^{\lceil1/\delta\rceil} w p^w V}.
\]
For the full set of orders the stronger \(m\gg a_p/(w p^w V)\) holds uniformly in \(p\).
For \(d\le2\) the measure has an absolute positive lower bound.

Here are consequences for the two coefficient orientations, including \(p\mid A\), with the same bounds for either reciprocal sign unless a plus sign is specified.
For a prime \(q\ne p\) put
\[
 c_{p,q}=
 \begin{cases}
 v_q(p^{\operatorname{ord}_q(p)}-1),&q\text{ odd},\\
 v_2(p^2-1)-1,&q=2 .
 \end{cases}
\]

* If \(h=H q^j,\ q\nmid H\), every positive-measure integral divisor has \(m\gg1/(w p^w H q^{c_{p,q}})\).
  Thus one additional prime can occur to arbitrary exponent at either coefficient and for either sign.
* For odd \(p,\ \sigma=1\), if \(h=2^v H q^j\) with \(q\ne2,p\) and \(\gcd(H,2q)=1\), the bound is \(m\gg1/(w p^w H\,2^{c_{p,2}}q^{c_{p,q}})\).
  Both \(v\) and \(j\) can grow arbitrarily.
* More generally suppose all orders represented by a particular \(S\) divide \(H\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}q^{j_q}\) for a finite set \(\mathcal Q\) of primes different from \(p\) and an integer \(H\ge1\).
  If the set of represented orders has at most \(r\) maximal elements under divisibility, where \(r\ge1\), then
\[
           m(S)\gg \frac{1}{r p^r w p^w H\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}q^{c_{p,q}}}.
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
  For a single maximal element the factor \(r p^r\) may be omitted.
  This includes every divisibility chain, and the assertion only requires the bound on the maximal elements of the actual factor.

The implied constants are absolute.
The first two bounds include arbitrary positive-measure integral divisors because they depend only on the input.
For common dilation \(X\mapsto X^g\), each nontorsion root has measure at least that of its reduced root divided by \(R_L\), with \(L=2|A|+2|B|\), by the [radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement).
Thus the three conclusions also apply to nontorsion factors after dilation with that additional denominator, using for (3) the represented orders of the reduced minimal polynomial when it is a unit polynomial.
For example take \(\sigma=-1,\ A=1,\ B=-3,\ D=5^{2s},\ k=5^s+1,\ s\ge1\).
Then \(\gcd(D,k)=1,\ 0<k<D/2\), and the first bound at \(p=3,\ q=5,\ H=1\) gives an absolute gap.
The polynomial vanishes at one, has derivative \(-2D+6k<0\) there, and tends to \(+\infty\) on the positive real axis, so it has a real root greater than one.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Counting each represented Frobenius orbit

In the [two local orientations](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#centers), the unit roots reduce to the roots of \(X^T+\sigma\), with multiplicity at most \(N\) per residue class.
For \(p\mid A\) an extra monomial in the reduction has no effect on unit roots.
To see the same multiplicity bound for \(\bar S\), use monic division \(P=S B_0\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), then reduce the identity modulo \(p\).
The reduced \(P\) is nonzero and \(\bar S\) is monic with unit constant term.
For \(u=v_p(T)\le w\), or \(p=2,\ u=w+1\), use \(d\ge a_p\) and \(N\le 2p^w\).
Otherwise the [Newton polygon bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#ramification) give the following for all classes together.
Over the unramified field splitting the reductions, every root apart from at most two, counted with multiplicities, has local degree at least \(cN/(w p^w)\), with an absolute \(c>0\).
In the binary plus case with \(w\ge2\) all roots in their classes even have local ramification at least \(N\).

In each Frobenius orbit of residue classes represented by \(S\), either there is such a root of \(S\) or all its roots there are among the two possible exceptions.
In the first case, if the orbit size is \(f\), conjugacy and disjointness of the classes give at least \(cfN/(wp^w)\) roots of \(S\) in that orbit.
Different orbits involve disjoint roots even when they correspond to the same order.
The sum of the orbit sizes in the second case is at most two.
If \(d>2\) at least one orbit is in the first case, so its total together with the other first-case orbit sizes is at least \(a_p/3\).
Summing proves (1).
In particular \(a_p\ge\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\), using \(\operatorname{ord}_1(p)=1\).
For \(d\le2\) the [quadratic radial lower bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(4d m^2\ge1/10\).

<a id="period"></a>
## A partial period at any coefficient prime

All represented orders are prime to \(p\).
By coprime Hensel factorization over \(\mathbb Z_p\), the residual irreducible factors of the selected orders, with their complete multiplicities, lift to a monic factor \(S_0\) of \(S\) of degree \(\delta d\).
If all orders are taken, set \(S_0=S\).
The residual multiplicities are at most \(N\), so \(\bar S_0\mid X^{NV}-1=(X^V-1)^N\) in characteristic \(p\).
Monic division gives \(\alpha^{NV}-1\in p\mathbb Z_p[\alpha]\) at every root of \(S_0\).
Raising successively to the power \(p\) shows \(\alpha^{p^i NV}-1\in p^{i+1}\mathbb Z_p[\alpha]\), since \((1+p^a z)^p-1\) is divisible by \(p^{a+1}\) for \(a\ge1\) and integral \(z\).
All other roots are integral and give nonnegative valuations.
The nonzero integer resultant of \(S\) and \(X^{p^iNV}-1\) is therefore divisible by \(p^{(i+1)\delta d}\).
At the complex embeddings its modulus is at most \(2^d\exp(p^i NV m)\), giving (2).

The binary case also follows directly from the [partial binary period inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement), using the gcd degree at least \(\delta d\).
Taking \(i=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\) makes the numerator coefficient in brackets at least \(\delta\log2\).
For the full set use \(i=0\) at \(p\ge3\), giving at least \(\log(3/2)\), and \(i=1\) at \(p=2\), giving \(\log2\) before the factor two in the denominator.
Combining with (1) gives the stated lower bounds.
The [binary-period argument for five terms](Yin_C_G0345_v239.md#period) uses the special residue orders \(3^{v+1}t\) at \(p=2\).
The four-term conclusion uses both coefficient choices, either sign, arbitrary \(p\), and the sum over all residual orbits in (1).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Divisibility maxima and prime-power towers

For every order \(s\) prime to \(p\) and prime \(q\mid s\) one has
\[
                 q^{\max(0,v_q(s)-c_{p,q})}\mid\operatorname{ord}_s(p).
\]
For odd \(q\), put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\).
The binomial expansion gives \(v_q(p^{f b}-1)=c_{p,q}+v_q(b)\), first for \(q\nmid b\), and then by successive raising to the power \(q\).
Since \(f\mid\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\) and \(q\nmid f\), this proves the assertion.
At \(q=2\), successive squaring gives \(v_2(p^{2^a}-1)=c_{p,2}+a\) for \(a\ge1\).
An odd exponent of \(p\) gives \(v_2(p^b-1)=v_2(p-1)\le c_{p,2}\), so the same divisibility follows for every order, including the small moduli.
The prime powers on the left are coprime for distinct \(q\), and therefore multiply in the divisor of the single order on the right.

Let \(V\) now denote the least common multiple of all represented orders.
For either sign they divide \(2h\), because each prime-to-\(p\) lift has its \(T\)-th power equal to a sign.
At \(p=2\) and for odd \(p\) with \(\sigma=-1\) all orders even divide \(h\).
For \(h=Hq^j\) choose a represented \(s\) with maximal \(i=v_q(s)\).
Then \(V\le2Hq^i\) and \(a_p\ge\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\ge q^{i-c_{p,q}}\), which suffices in the full-set estimate.
For odd \(p,\ \sigma=1\), every represented order has exactly the two-part \(2^{v_2(T)+1}\), since its \(T\)-th power is \(-1\).
With \(h=2^v H q^j\) the same chosen \(s\) therefore gives \(\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\ge2^{v+1-c_{p,2}}q^{i-c_{p,q}}\), whereas \(V\le 2^{v+1}Hq^i\).
This proves the second estimate.
The anti-reciprocal minus sign does not force a common two-part, and the first estimate and (3) use only the actual orders.

Finally the divisor sets of the \(r\) or fewer maximal elements cover all represented orders.
One maximal element \(s\) therefore has a divisor set carrying mass \(\delta\ge1/r\).
Select all represented orders dividing this \(s\), so that their least common multiple is \(s\) itself and \(a_p\ge\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\).
The product of the prime-power divisors of \(\operatorname{ord}_s(p)\) just proved gives
\[
              \frac{\operatorname{ord}_s(p)}{s}
                  \ge \frac{1}{H\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}q^{c_{p,q}}}.
\]
Indeed the part of \(s\) outside \(\mathcal Q\) divides \(H\), and each \(q\) in \(\mathcal Q\) loses at most \(q^{c_{p,q}}\).
Substituting into the partial-set estimate, or the full-set estimate for one maximum, proves (3).
Nonunit primitive irreducible factors and integer content of absolute value at least two give logarithmic measure at least \(\log2\), and the irreducible factor measures are nonnegative and additive.
These observations extend the exponent-only bounds to the stated integral divisors.
