# The reciprocal exterior minimum in the radical saturation of arbitrary unit roots

The coordinating agent supplied the complex-conjugation criterion, the subgroup formula and the bounded-coefficient construction.
The agent-s global strategy contributor checked their arithmetic and representation interfaces, the measure cost and the earlier results, and wrote this account.
An auxiliary author-side checker verified the signed averaging and coset multiplicities.

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite exact minimum with a uniform measure cost

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with nontorsion unit roots.
Choose one root \(x_i\) from each inverse pair, \(1\le i\le m\).
Let \(F\subset\mathbb C\) be the splitting field, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), and \(\tau\in G\) the complex conjugation of this embedding.
No restriction is imposed on the real or nonreal exterior roots or on their multiplicative relations.

Let \(\Gamma=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\rangle\), and define
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma=
 \{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:y^N\in\mu_\infty\Gamma
                                  \text{ for some }N\ge1\}.
\]
Write \(\mu=\log M(P)>0\).
Every element of \(\widetilde\Gamma\) is an algebraic unit.
An algebraic number is called reciprocal here when its minimal polynomial has its roots closed under inversion.
For a reciprocal nontorsion element \(y\), let \(b(y)\) be the number of its exterior conjugates, counted as distinct roots of its minimal polynomial.

Let \(U=\mathbb Q^m\) carry the signed permutation action of \(G\) on the \(x_i\).
Put
\[
 \mathcal L=\{a\in\mathbb Z^m:\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\in\mu_\infty\},
        \qquad V=U/(\mathcal L\otimes\mathbb Q).                    \tag{1}
\]
For \(J\le G\) and a nontrivial homomorphism \(\lambda:J\to\{1,-1\}\), define
\[
 V^\lambda(J)=\{v\in V:jv=\lambda(j)v\text{ for every }j\in J\},
                  \qquad J^-=\lambda^{-1}(-1).
\]
Call \((J,\lambda)\) admissible when \(V^\lambda(J)\ne0\).
With \(\mathcal C_\tau\) denoting the conjugacy class of \(\tau\), set
\[
 c(J,\lambda)=[G:J]\left(
                 1-\frac{|\mathcal C_\tau\cap J^-|}
                          {|\mathcal C_\tau|}\right),
 \qquad
                         c_*=\min_{(J,\lambda)\text{ admissible}}
                                             c(J,\lambda).       \tag{2}
\]
The admissible set is finite and nonempty, \(c(J,\lambda)\) is a positive integer for each admissible pair, and
\[
 \min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma\text{ nontorsion}\\
                         y\text{ reciprocal}}} b(y)=c_*.         \tag{3}
\]

Moreover, the minimum is attained by an ordinary signed subset product
\[
                    \beta=\prod_{i=1}^m x_i^{a_i},
                        \qquad a_i\in\{0,1,-1\},                  \tag{4}
\]
whose actual minimal polynomial is reciprocal, and
\[
                         \log M(\beta)\le c_*\mu.                 \tag{5}
\]
For any minimizing admissible pair it can be chosen by averaging a single coordinate over \(J\); its support is one \(J\)-orbit of inverse-pair labels.
Its degree is at most \(2[G:J]\).
Thus if any reciprocal nontorsion radical output has at most \(B\) exterior conjugates, one of the products (4) has at most \(B\) exterior conjugates and measure at most \(M(P)^B\).
No bound on the exponents, radical denominators or torsion multipliers of the original output is required.

Equivalently, one can compute the minimum in (3) by inspecting the at most \(3^m\) products (4), retaining only the reciprocal nontorsion ones.
This is a finite exact reduction, with no efficiency or performed-enumeration claim.

<a id="salem"></a>
## The cost-one Salem criterion

There is a reciprocal nontorsion element of \(\widetilde\Gamma\) with one exterior conjugate if and only if some admissible \((J,\lambda)\) has \(c(J,\lambda)=1\).
In that case (4) gives such an element with
\[
                                M(\beta)\le M(P).                 \tag{6}
\]
Its unique exterior root is real, because nonreal roots of a rational polynomial occur in conjugate pairs.
Choose that conjugate and multiply all its conjugates by a rational sign if needed to make it positive.
The resulting minimal polynomial remains irreducible, integral and reciprocal, and its measure is unchanged.

The result is a Salem number unless its degree is two.
In the latter case its polynomial is \(X^2-aX+1\), with \(a\in\mathbb Z\), \(a\ge3\), so its measure is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Consequently
\[
                          M(P)<(3+\sqrt5)/2                       \tag{7}
\]
makes the output a genuine Salem number.
The sign normalization stays in \(\mu_\infty\Gamma\subset\widetilde\Gamma\); the unnormalized output itself is the signed subset product (4).

There is an exact coset description of \(c(J,\lambda)=1\).
On \(G/J\), all but one coset \(gJ\) satisfy
\[
                              g^{-1}\tau g\in J^-;               \tag{8}
\]
the exceptional coset satisfies \(g^{-1}\tau g\in\ker\lambda\).
In particular \(\tau\in\operatorname{core}_G(J)\), and the signs at the fixed cosets are positive at exactly one coset and negative at every other coset.
The nonzero eigenspace condition remains part of this criterion.
A subgroup/sign pattern without \(V^\lambda(J)\ne0\) does not supply an arithmetic output.

<a id="circle"></a>
## Complex conjugation detects the unit circle in the quotient

The lattice \(\mathcal L\) is saturated: if a positive power of a monomial is torsion, the monomial itself is torsion.
It is \(G\)-stable.
Thus \(V\) is the rational space of monomial classes modulo torsion.
An integral exponent vector maps to zero exactly when its monomial is torsion.

For a monomial \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), let \(v=[\gamma]\in V\).
Then
\[
                    |\gamma|=1
                \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                          \tau v=-v.                             \tag{9}
\]
Indeed \(\tau v=-v\) says that \(\gamma\,\tau(\gamma)\) is torsion.
In the chosen embedding this value is the positive real number \(|\gamma|^2\), and the only positive real root of unity is one.
The converse follows from the same equality.
The criterion remains valid for rational classes after clearing a positive denominator.
Unlike the real-circuit argument, it does not require the logarithm map on \(V\) to be injective.

For later use, an element \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\) and its inverse are algebraic integers: a positive power of each is a unit, and integrality is transitive through the monic power equation.
Hence \(y\) is a unit.
A nontorsion unit has an exterior conjugate.
Here is the elementary finiteness proof.
If every conjugate had modulus at most one, the norm would force every modulus to be one.
All positive powers would then have minimal polynomials of bounded degree with integer coefficients bounded by the corresponding binomial coefficients.
There are finitely many such polynomials and hence finitely many possible power values, forcing a repeated power and torsion.
This also proves \(\mu>0\).

<a id="lower"></a>
## A reciprocal radical output gives an admissible pair

Take any reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\).
Clear its radical denominator and root-of-unity multiplier by a positive power:
\[
                                  w=y^N\in\Gamma.
\]
Then \(v=[w]\ne0\).
The conjugates of \(w\) are inverse-closed, because those of \(y\) are.
Since \(w\in F\), some element of \(G\) takes \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\).
Thus the subgroup and character
\[
            J=\{g\in G:gv\in\{v,-v\}\},
                       \qquad gv=\lambda(g)v                     \tag{10}
\]
are well defined, with \(\lambda\) nontrivial and \(v\in V^\lambda(J)\).
This also shows that admissible pairs exist, by applying the construction to any root of \(P\).

The cosets \(gJ\) index the distinct antipodal pairs \(\{gv,-gv\}\) in the class orbit.
By (9), such a pair is on the unit circle exactly when
\[
               \tau(gv)=-gv
           \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                           g^{-1}\tau g\in J^-.                  \tag{11}
\]
Every pair failing (11) supplies at least one exterior conjugate of \(w\).
To see this without any distinct-modulus assumption, the two global classes have opposite nonzero logarithms and both occur among the actual conjugates of \(w\).
Distinct class pairs cannot refer to the same actual root.
A conjugate of \(w\) lifts to a conjugate of \(y\), and a positive logarithm remains positive under the power map.
Therefore
\[
              b(y)\ge
              \#\{gJ:g^{-1}\tau g\notin J^-\}.                    \tag{12}
\]
Distinct classes may have equal moduli, or one class may contain several actual conjugates differing by torsion.
Neither phenomenon decreases the lower bound in (12).

<a id="cosets"></a>
## The exact coset count and its one-exception case

The condition \(g^{-1}\tau g\in J^-\) is independent of the representative of \(gJ\), because \(J^-\) is invariant under \(J\)-conjugation.
Each element of \(\mathcal C_\tau\) has \(|C_G(\tau)|\) preimages under \(g\mapsto g^{-1}\tau g\).
Consequently
\[
 \#\{gJ:g^{-1}\tau g\in J^-\}
       =\frac{|C_G(\tau)|\,|\mathcal C_\tau\cap J^-|}{|J|}
       =[G:J]\frac{|\mathcal C_\tau\cap J^-|}
                       {|\mathcal C_\tau|}.                       \tag{13}
\]
Thus the right side of (12) is exactly \(c(J,\lambda)\), and (13) proves its integrality.
In particular every reciprocal radical output has \(b(y)\ge c_*\).

If \(c(J,\lambda)=1\), all but one coset satisfy (8).
Every such coset is fixed by the involution \(\tau\), since \(g^{-1}\tau g\in J\).
An involution's moved points occur in pairs, so the remaining coset is fixed too.
At that coset \(g^{-1}\tau g\) belongs to \(J\) but not to \(J^-\), hence belongs to \(\ker\lambda\).
All cosets are fixed, which is exactly \(\tau\in\bigcap_{g\in G}gJg^{-1}\).
Conversely the stated fixed-coset sign pattern has exactly one coset failing (8), so (13) gives \(c(J,\lambda)=1\).
This proves the group-theoretic form of the Salem criterion.

<a id="average"></a>
## Signed averaging produces a bounded-coefficient monomial

Fix any admissible \((J,\lambda)\).
On \(U\) and \(V\), use the rational averaging operator
\[
                    E_\lambda=\frac1{|J|}
                            \sum_{j\in J}\lambda(j)j.             \tag{14}
\]
It projects onto the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace and commutes with the quotient map \(U\to V\).
Since \(V^\lambda(J)\ne0\) and the coordinate vectors span \(U\), some \(E_\lambda e_i\) has nonzero image in \(V\).

Let \(J_i\) be the stabilizer of the inverse-pair label \(i\).
For \(j\in J_i\), write \(j e_i=\varepsilon_i(j)e_i\), where \(\varepsilon_i:J_i\to\{1,-1\}\) is a character.
The coefficient of \(e_i\) in \(E_\lambda e_i\) is
\[
                           \frac1{|J|}
                    \sum_{j\in J_i}\lambda(j)\varepsilon_i(j).
\]
A nontrivial sign character has zero sum, by pairing each element with multiplication by an element of negative sign.
Thus the entire average vanishes unless \(\lambda|_{J_i}=\varepsilon_i\): coefficients at other coordinates in the orbit are the same sum with a sign.
In the nonzero case, every coefficient on \(O=J\cdot i\) has absolute value
\[
                              |J_i|/|J|=1/|O|,
\]
and every coefficient off \(O\) is zero.
Hence
\[
                           a=|O|E_\lambda e_i                    \tag{15}
\]
has entries in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), is supported on the single orbit \(O\), has nonzero image in \(V\), and satisfies
\[
                                ja=\lambda(j)a\quad(j\in J)       \tag{16}
\]
in the raw exponent space, not merely in its quotient.

Set \(\beta=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\).
The nonzero quotient class makes it nontorsion.
Equation (16) gives the exact identities
\[
                                j(\beta)=\beta^{\lambda(j)}.      \tag{17}
\]
There is no root-of-unity correction.
Since \(\lambda\) is nontrivial, \(\beta^{-1}\) is a conjugate of \(\beta\).
Its actual minimal polynomial is consequently reciprocal.
It is monic integral because \(\beta\) is an algebraic unit.

The multiset of inverse pairs
\[
                              \{g\beta,(g\beta)^{-1}\},
                                  \qquad gJ\in G/J,              \tag{18}
\]
covers every actual inverse pair of conjugates.
Condition (8) forces such a pair to be neutral by (17).
It follows that
\[
                                b(\beta)\le c(J,\lambda),
                         \qquad \deg(\beta)\le2[G:J].             \tag{19}
\]
For a multiplicity check, let \(T\) stabilize the actual unordered pair \(\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\}\).
Then \(J\le T\), and every actual inverse pair occurs in (18) exactly \([T:J]\) times.
Every exterior pair has all these cosets outside the forced-neutral set, so in fact
\[
                               [T:J]\,b(\beta)\le c(J,\lambda).
                                                                    \tag{20}
\]
Thus coincident pairs improve rather than invalidate (19).

For each admissible pair, (19) bounds \(c(J,\lambda)\) below by the positive exterior count of a nontorsion unit.
For a pair minimizing (2), the lower bound already proved gives
\[
                          c_*\le b(\beta)\le c_*.
\]
This establishes both exact attainment and (3).
For that minimizing pair, (20) also forces \(T=J\), so this particular construction actually has degree \(2[G:J]\); the weaker bound in the statement suffices for its applications.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Total measure and finite signed-subset search

At the chosen embedding,
\[
                          \sum_{i=1}^m|\log|x_i||=\mu,            \tag{21}
\]
because every reciprocal pair contributes its positive logarithm once, and neutral pairs contribute zero.
At any conjugate of \(\beta\), its exponent vector is again a signed subset vector, since \(G\) acts by signed permutations.
Therefore
\[
                              |\log|g\beta||\le\mu
                                  \quad(g\in G).                 \tag{22}
\]
Summing only over the actual exterior roots of its minimal polynomial gives
\[
                              \log M(\beta)\le b(\beta)\mu.       \tag{23}
\]
For the minimizing construction \(b(\beta)=c_*\), proving (5).
There is no factor from the splitting-field degree or from the size of the original radical exponent in this estimate.

The products (4) include every root \(x_i\), so their reciprocal nontorsion subcollection is nonempty.
Every one belongs to \(\widetilde\Gamma\), and the averaging construction produces a minimum-attaining member.
Thus minimizing their actual exterior counts gives exactly (3).
Their orientations do not affect the collection: replacing \(x_i\) by its inverse just reverses \(a_i\).
The finite search may discard torsion products and nonreciprocal products after exact algebraic evaluation.
It does not assume the relation lattice or a Galois-group presentation has already been computationally determined.

<a id="small-measure"></a>
## Below Smyth's threshold and the unrestricted objective

Let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
The [primary Smyth theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) states that a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer of measure less than \(\theta\) is reciprocal.
Consequently every nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\) with
\[
                                  M(y)<\theta                    \tag{24}
\]
is covered by (3), whether or not reciprocity was initially specified.
The cutoff is strict.
No reciprocity assertion is made at equality, where the plastic-number cubic is an example.

Consider any hypothetical sequence of inputs \(P_\nu\) of the kind in the statement with \(\mu_\nu\to0\).
Suppose there are nontorsion \(y_\nu\in\widetilde\Gamma_\nu\) with a fixed bound \(b(y_\nu)\le B\), and suppose either that the outputs are reciprocal or that \(M(y_\nu)\to1\).
After discarding a finite prefix in the latter case, (3)–(5) replace them by ordinary signed subset products \(\beta_\nu\) satisfying
\[
             b(\beta_\nu)\le B,\qquad
                   0<\log M(\beta_\nu)\le B\mu_\nu\longrightarrow0.
                                                                    \tag{25}
\]
For \(B=1\), (7) eventually holds and the absolute value of the unique real exterior conjugate of \(\beta_\nu\) is a Salem number with measure \(M(\beta_\nu)\le M(P_\nu)\).
Thus arbitrary radical exponents and torsion multipliers provide no additional bounded-exterior compression beyond the finite signed-subset class.

This applies at the actual unrestricted G0001 boundary.
Any hypothetical sequence of integer polynomials with Mahler measures strictly greater than one and tending to one supplies irreducible nontorsion factors with the same limiting property, by multiplicativity of Mahler measure.
Eventually those factors have leading coefficient of absolute value one and nonzero constant coefficient of absolute value one: a larger leading or constant coefficient alone gives measure at least two.
After choosing the positive leading sign, Smyth's theorem makes them reciprocal.
Hence every such shrinking witness eventually has the signature-unrestricted form used here.

What remains unproved is the existence of a uniformly bounded-exterior reciprocal radical output for every sufficiently small input.
In particular no argument here forces \(c_*=1\), or even bounds \(c_*\) independently of \(P\).
The theorem gives an exact, finite group-and-relation criterion for that supply question, and replaces any supplied compression with the uniform-cost construction (25).
It does not by itself construct a shrinking input sequence or prove a universal Mahler gap.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Faithful sparse additive equations

The [small-height relation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#sparse-output) applies to
\[
             \sum_{i=1}^s q_i a_i y^{k_i}=0,\qquad
      2\le s\le B,\quad q_i\in\mathbb Q^*,\quad a_i\in\Gamma,
                         \quad k_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad y\ne0,       \tag{26}
\]
provided its term-relative heights are sufficiently small and
\[
           k_i=k_j,\quad i\ne j
                        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                               a_i/a_j\notin\mu_\infty.           \tag{27}
\]
It gives \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\).
Therefore (3) applies whenever \(y\) is nontorsion and reciprocal, or whenever it is nontorsion and satisfies (24).
The signed subset replacement and its measure bound then apply to this actual additive output.

For Laurent monomials of exponent \(\ell^1\)-norm at most \(L\), with \(|k_i|\le K\), the sufficient sequence condition is
\[
                         L\mu/(2m)+K h(y)\longrightarrow0,       \tag{28}
\]
for one fixed term cap \(B\), by the [exact height budget](Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#height-budget).
As before, the evaluated polynomial in the output variable must be a faithful nonzero constraint, and any performed rational collection must leave (27) valid.

There is a necessary distinction from the real-exterior applications.
For arbitrary complex signatures, torsion monomial quotients in \(\Gamma\) need not be signs.
One cannot absorb general roots of unity into rational coefficients and silently keep the hypotheses of (26).
Condition (27), or an actual rational collection that establishes it, is therefore retained here.
For the real-exterior classes where all torsion monomial relations have sign values, C345v300's same-power sign collection supplies the stated interface.
No general dense transformation or unsupported torsion-coefficient extension is asserted.

## Comparison and checking scope

The [positive-radical minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) minimizes total Mahler measure among factors of all dilations of one positive real unit.
It uses a positive norm to retain the seed field.
Here the invariant is exterior count, the starting group is generated by all roots, signatures are unrestricted, and the attained replacement uses coefficients \(0,1,-1\) with a measure cost independent of radical exponents.
These are different optimization problems; the present theorem does not replace the exact measure formula of C345v205.

The [circuit cut minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v302.md#statement) supplies an explicit cut interpretation and a primitive-action lower bound for real circuits.
The [real-exterior atom theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v303.md#statement) gives a more explicit atomwise count at its real-exterior scope.
Their formulas remain useful refinements.
The proof above is independent of their circuit and atom decompositions: its unit-circle test is the single operator equation (9), valid for arbitrary complex signatures.

The [Salem inverse-Mahler signature theorem](Yin_C_G0866_v8.md#statement) concerns all realizations of one fixed Salem Mahler value, its atom endpoint and raw-norm degree costs.
It expressly supplies a rational-function field description rather than an assumed radical-monomial representation.
Its signed signature counts and finite attainment do not provide (3) for arbitrary reciprocal inputs.
No inverse-Mahler endpoint is assumed to lie in the present radical saturation.

Author-side checking covered actual versus torsion classes, reciprocal powering, possible equal moduli, the exact conjugacy-class count, the nontriviality of the sign character, signed-coordinate cancellation, actual reciprocity without torsion errors, and uniform inverse-pair multiplicities.
It also covered the strict Smyth cutoff, the quadratic exception to Salem descent, and the rational-coefficient boundary in the sparse extension.
The signed averaging and finite group counts are proved in full above.
A bounded search of the retained neighboring statements supplied the comparisons just listed; no exhaustive literature novelty claim or uninspected classification theorem is used.
