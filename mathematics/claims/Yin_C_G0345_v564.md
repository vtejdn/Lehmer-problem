# A factor gap for reciprocal six-term parents with equally spaced trinomial blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite set of primes in the long exponent

Let \(A(Y)=1+aY+cY^2\), where \(a,c\in\{1,-1\}\), and put \(A^*(Y)=Y^2A(1/Y)\).
For \(n>2k>0\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\), form
\[
                 F(X)=A(X^k)+\epsilon X^n A^*(X^k).
\]
Fix a finite set \(Q\) of odd primes and an integer \(H\ge1\).
If the largest divisor of \(n\) prime to every member of \(Q\cup\{2\}\) is at most \(H\), every integral divisor of \(F\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure has log measure at least \(\eta_{Q,H}>0\).
There is no coprimality hypothesis on \(n,k\).

More precisely, along any sequence of positive-measure irreducible factors with log measure tending to zero, their degrees \(d\) eventually satisfy
\[
                   d\gg_Q 2^{v_2(n)}\prod_{q\in Q}q^{v_q(n)}.       \tag{1}
\]
The [earlier dyadic estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v180.md#equal) treats the characteristic part under restrictions on the degree or the valuations of the short exponent.
Here both local contributions come from one embedding.

<a id="cover"></a>
## A conjugate with specified residue and valuation

If \(c=1\), then \(A=A^*\) and both \(A(X^k)\) and \(1+\epsilon X^n\) have only roots of unity.
Take \(c=-1\) and suppose \(m\to0\) for the irreducible factors under consideration.
The parent has leading and constant coefficients of modulus one, so their roots \(x\) are algebraic units with absolute Weil height \(h(x)=m/d>0\).
Put \(D=n+2k\).
The [six-term cut bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) give \(1\ll Dm/d\ll1\) eventually, and in particular \(d/D\to0\).
The gap from the constant term to the first term of positive degree is \(k\), so the [separated monomial estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) gives \(k/D\to0\).
Consequently \(D/n\to1\) and \(y=x^k\) has positive height tending to zero.

Pass to any subsequence with constant signs and constant \(b=\prod_{q\in Q,\ q\mid n}q\), allowing \(b=1\).
Put \(G(Y)=-\epsilon A(Y)/A^*(Y)\).
The polynomials \(A,A^*\) have no common zero because their sum is \(2aY\) and neither vanishes at zero.
The discriminant of \(A\) is five, so a simple numerator zero gives geometric irreducibility of \(U^b=G(Y)\) by Eisenstein at that zero.
Its smooth projective normalization has a finite projection \(\pi\) of degree \(b\) to the \(Y\)-line.
The pairs \((y,u)=(x^k,x^{n/b})\) define points of this curve, since the denominator and \(u\) are nonzero.
Their positive base heights tending to zero give genericity on the curve.

Equip \(\pi^*\mathcal O(1)\) with the pullback of the standard adelic metric.
The resulting height of the curve is zero by the projection formula, and the point heights equal \(h(y)\).
As in the [fixed-cover argument](Yin_C_G0345_v562.md#branches), [Yuan's equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md#yuan-equidistribution) gives the limiting dyadic conjugate measure \(b^{-1}\pi^*\delta_\xi\), where \(\xi\) is the Gauss point and preimages are weighted by local degrees.
At the Gauss valuation the reduction of \(G\) is one, and \(G-1\) has valuation exactly one in the normalization \(v(2)=1\).
Indeed its numerator is \(-2aY\) for \(\epsilon=1\), or \(2(1-Y^2)\) for \(\epsilon=-1\), and the denominator has Gauss norm one.
The equation for \(U\) reduces to \(U^b=1\) with simple roots, all of which lift in the completed Gauss field over \(\mathbb C_2\).
Choose a primitive residue root of order \(b\) and its Teichmüller lift \(r\).
The corresponding point over \(\xi\) has mass \(1/b\) and belongs to the open set \(|U-r|_2<1,\ 2^{-3/2}<|G-1|_2<2^{-1/2}\).
Weak convergence gives a conjugate there at every sufficiently late index.
Extend its embedding to \(\mathbb Q(x)\) and use the resulting place for the rest of the argument.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Ramification and residue degree at that place

Let \(e,f\) be the ramification index and residue degree of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) at this place, and let \(s\) be the multiplicative order of the residue of \(x\).
The residue of \(x^{n/b}\) has order \(b\), so \(v_q(s)=v_q(n)\) for every \(q\mid b\).
Since \(s\mid 2^f-1\), the [prime-power order divisibilities](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#orders) give \(f\gg_Q\prod_{q\mid b}q^{v_q(n)}\).

Write \(n=2^j t\) with \(t\) odd.
Since the residue of \(x^n\) is one, so is the residue of \(x^t\).
Put \(w_i=v(x^{2^i t}-1)\) for \(0\le i\le j\).
These valuations are finite, with \(w_0\ge1/e\) and \(w_j<3/2\) by the chosen open set.
Squaring gives \(w_{i+1}=2w_i\) if \(w_i<1\), and \(w_{i+1}\ge2\) if \(w_i\ge1\).
Thus every \(i<j\) has \(w_i<1\), whence \(w_j=2^j w_0\) including when \(j=0\).
It follows that \(e>(2/3)2^j\), and \(d\ge ef\) proves (1).
Only finitely many choices of signs and \(b\) occur, so the bound holds along the original sequence.
Under the bounded complementary divisor hypothesis (1) implies \(d\gg_Q n/H\), contradicting \(d/D\to0\).
Any integral divisor of positive log measure contains a positive-measure irreducible factor, since the parent is primitive with unit endpoints.
This proves the claimed gap.
