# An endpoint norm obstruction for Salem numbers over even-degree trace fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound from the residue degrees of the trace field

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2n\), with \(n\) even, \(P\) its monic minimum, \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(B=\mathbb Q(t)\), and \(m=\log\alpha\) its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
For a finite prime \(v\) of \(B\), let \(\mathrm Nv\) denote its residue cardinality and \(f_v\) its residue degree over \(\mathbb Q\).
Define
\[
   C(B)=\min\left\{\,\min_{v\mid2}4^{f_v},\
                  \min_{v:\ \mathrm Nv\equiv3\pmod4}\mathrm Nv\,\right\},
\]
where a minimum over an empty set is \(+\infty\).
Then, for every integer \(T\ge2\),
\[
       C(B)\ \le\ |P(1)P(-1)|,\qquad
       \log |P(1)P(-1)|
          \le 2n\left(\frac{\log T}{T-1}
                         +\frac{T+1}{3}\frac mn\right).                   \tag{1}
\]
If the two endpoint values are odd, some odd prime \(v\) of norm \(3\bmod4\) and of odd valuation in \(t^2-4\) ramifies in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)/B\).
The general bound concerns the norm of \(t^2-4\), allowing an index between the quadratic order and the maximal order.
In particular, along a sequence of Salem numbers with positive measures tending to zero and even trace degrees,
\[
                         \log C(B)=o(\sqrt{n\log n}).                      \tag{2}
\]

For every even \(n\ge4\), there are infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic totally real fields \(B\) of degree \(n\), with normal closure group \(S_n\), over which **every** Salem number having trace field \(B\) satisfies
\[
               m\ \ge\ \frac{\log2-\frac14\log5}{2}\,n>0.145\,n.          \tag{3}
\]
Every rational prime \(p\le4^n\) can be required to be unramified and inert in these fields.
For each such \(B\) and each choice of its distinguished real place, infinitely many distinct quadratic Salem extensions of \(B\) realize that place as the unique split real place.
The lower bound thus concerns the full set of Salem extensions of each specified field.

<a id="parity"></a>
## The opposite signs of two congruent norms

The trace \(t\) has degree \(n\), with one conjugate \(>2\) and all others in \((-2,2)\).
Indeed the \(2n\) distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) form inverse pairs giving exactly \(n\) distinct traces.
The positive integers
\[
                 A=-N_{B/\mathbb Q}(t-2),\qquad H=N_{B/\mathbb Q}(t+2)
\]
have the indicated signs because \(n-1\) is odd.
The elements \(t-2\) and \(t+2\) are congruent modulo \(4\mathcal O_B\), and their norms are congruent modulo four by taking the determinants of the multiplication matrices in an integral basis.
Thus \(A+H\equiv0\bmod4\).

If \(A,H\) are odd, their product is \(3\bmod4\).
Some rational prime \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) therefore occurs to odd exponent in \(AH=|N_{B/\mathbb Q}(t^2-4)|\).
The exponent is \(\sum_{v\mid p} f_v v(t^2-4)\), with local valuations normalized to have value group \(\mathbb Z\).
At least one such \(v\) has both \(f_v\) and \(v(t^2-4)\) odd, giving \(\mathrm Nv=p^{f_v}\equiv3\bmod4\) and \(AH\ge\mathrm Nv\).
The quadratic extension \(B(\sqrt{t^2-4})=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) ramifies there because the square root has half-integral valuation in the normalization on \(B_v\).

If \(A,H\) are even, some \(v\mid2\) divides \(t-2\).
It divides \(t+2=(t-2)+4\) as well, so \(AH\ge(\mathrm Nv)^2=4^{f_v}\).
These two cases exhaust the parity possibilities.
Writing \(P(X)=X^nR(X+X^{-1})\) for the monic trace minimum \(R\) gives \(AH=|P(1)P(-1)|\).
This proves the first part of (1).

The norm of \(\alpha^2-1=\alpha(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\) from \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) to \(B\) is \(-(t^2-4)\).
Apply the [cyclic norm bound for consecutive powers](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) at the unit \(z=\alpha^2\), of absolute logarithmic height \(m/n\), using the containing field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) of degree \(2n\).
Its first cyclotomic term bounds \((2n)^{-1}\log |N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^2-1)|\) by \(\log T/(T-1)+(T+1)m/(3n)\), giving all of (1).
If \(m\to0\), then \(n\to\infty\) by finiteness of algebraic integers of bounded degree and bounded measure.
The small-height estimate at the same locator, or optimization over \(T\), gives \(\log C(B)\ll\sqrt{nm\log(n/m)}\) in this range.
Since \(m\log(n/m)/\log n\to0\), this proves (2).

<a id="bases"></a>
## Totally real symmetric-group fields with the required small-prime behavior

Fix an even \(n\ge4\).
Prescribe a monic irreducible degree-\(n\) polynomial over \(\mathbb F_p\) at each rational prime \(p\le4^n\).
At two distinct primes greater than \(4^n\) prescribe monic squarefree polynomials with factor degrees \((n-1,1)\) and \((2,1,\ldots,1)\), respectively.
Such reductions exist because finite fields have irreducibles of every positive degree and the latter two primes exceed \(n\).
One can also choose an additional prime \(s\), different from all the preceding primes and unramified in any specified finite list of previously constructed fields, and prescribe the coefficient residues of \(X^n+s\bmod s^2\).

The Chinese remainder theorem fixes a simultaneous residue class for each nonleading coefficient modulo an integer \(J\).
For a positive integer \(R_0\), start with \(\prod_{j=1}^n(X-jR_0)\) and add to each nonleading coefficient an integer of absolute value at most \(J\) to attain the chosen class.
After substituting \(X=R_0Y\) and dividing by \(R_0^n\), the resulting monic polynomial tends coefficientwise to \(\prod_{j=1}^n(Y-j)\) as \(R_0\to\infty\).
Taking disjoint small intervals around the simple real zeros \(1,\ldots,n\), the limiting polynomial changes sign across each interval and so do the perturbed polynomials for large \(R_0\).
All \(n\) roots are then real.
The resulting integral polynomial is irreducible by its irreducible reduction at two.
Let \(B\) be the field generated by a root.

At every prescribed squarefree reduction the polynomial discriminant is a unit at that prime, so the associated order is maximal locally and the residue factors give the splitting in \(B\).
In particular all primes \(p\le4^n\) are unramified and inert.
In the normal closure, the factor degrees at a squarefree reduction give the cycle lengths of a Frobenius element on the roots, since the residue roots are distinct and residue Frobenius cycles each irreducible factor's roots.
The transitive Galois group contains an \((n-1)\)-cycle and a transposition from the two larger primes.
The stabilizer of the point fixed by the \((n-1)\)-cycle is transitive on the other points, so the group is doubly transitive.
Conjugating the transposition then gives every transposition and hence the whole group \(S_n\).
At \(s\) the polynomial is Eisenstein, so \(s\) is totally ramified in \(B\), distinguishing this field from the prescribed finite list.
This proves infinitude at each degree.

In these fields the unique dyadic place has \(f_v=n\).
At every odd rational prime up to \(4^n\), the only residue degree is \(n\), so the residue cardinality is \(1\bmod4\).
Every odd prime ideal of norm \(3\bmod4\) therefore has norm greater than \(4^n\).
Consequently \(C(B)=4^n\), and (1) at \(T=5\) gives
\[
                     \log2\le\frac14\log5+2m/n .
\]
This proves (3).

## Realizing infinitely many Salem extensions at each real place

More generally, let \(B\) be any totally real field of degree \(n\ge2\) and choose one real embedding \(\sigma\).
Choose an odd prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) unramified in any finite list of quadratic extensions of \(B\) already considered, and choose \(b\in\mathfrak p\setminus\mathfrak p^2\).
The coset \(b+\mathfrak p^2\) in the \(n\) real embeddings contains an element \(b'\) positive at \(\sigma\) and negative at every other embedding.
Indeed the embedded ideal is a full lattice, and its translated bounded fundamental regions meet a ray deep inside this open orthant at points lying within bounded distance of coset elements.
Then \(K=B(\sqrt{b'})\) is quadratic, is split at \(\sigma\), becomes complex at the other real embeddings, and ramifies at \(\mathfrak p\) by the odd valuation.

By the [unit theorem in every signature](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002), the unit ranks of \(K\) and \(B\) are \(n\) and \(n-1\), respectively.
The norm image contains the squares of all units of \(B\), so the relative norm-one group has rank one.
Choose a non-torsion unit in it and use its sign and inverse to give a value \(\alpha>1\) at one of the two real embeddings over \(\sigma\).
This choice is possible because all other places give values of modulus one, the two real values are reciprocal, and an algebraic unit with all conjugates on the circle is torsion.
The value \(\alpha>1\) occurs at exactly one embedding of \(K\), which forces \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) by the number of extensions of an embedding from a subfield.
The reciprocal trace lies in \(B\), and its field has degree \(n\) because the \(2n\) distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) give exactly \(n\) distinct inverse-pair traces.
Hence \(\alpha\) is a Salem number with trace field \(B\).
Repeating the choice of a new ramified prime ideal gives infinitely many distinct extensions.
The bound (3) applies to the constructed bases with the stated local conditions, leaving general Salem trace fields and the unrestricted Lehmer infimum open.
