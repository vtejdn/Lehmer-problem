# Coefficient mass outside short exponent intervals of a multiple

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower bounds for the omitted coefficients

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with degree \(d\), absolute logarithmic height \(h\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=dh\).
Write \(d_0\) for the number of classes of its conjugates under quotient by a root of unity, and \(\eta\) for the proportion of its conjugates off the unit circle.
Let \(F\) be a nonzero complex Laurent polynomial vanishing at every rational conjugate of \(x\), with like exponents collected.
Choose a nonempty subset of its terms lying in an interval of length \(V\), and let \(G\) be their sum, \(R=F-G\) and \(L_j=\|G\|_j\) the coefficient norms for \(j=1,2\).
Put
\[
 E=\min\!\left(1,C\sqrt{Vh+\log(2d_0)/d_0}\right)
\]
for a suitable absolute constant \(C\).
Even when the other exponents are arbitrarily far from the interval, one has
\[
 (1-\eta)\|R\|_1^2\ \ge\ L_2^2-E(L_1^2-L_2^2)-\eta L_1^2.                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(x\) is reciprocal and the interval has either the least or the greatest exponent of \(F\) as its outer endpoint, one also has
\[
 \|R\|_1^2\ \ge\ L_2^2-E(L_1^2-L_2^2)-4Vh L_1^2.                          \tag{2}
\]
The endpoint estimate applies even if all roots are off the circle.
Neither estimate involves the number, total span or individual positions of the remaining terms.

For the [critical six-term factors](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement), \(d_0=d\), \(h\ll 1/b\) and \(\eta\ll s^{-2}\), with constants depending only on the critical bounds.
Thus for any interval of length \(V=o(b)\) and any group of terms in it of bounded effective support \(L_1^2/L_2^2\), the coefficient norm of the remainder satisfies \(\|R\|_1\ge(1-o(1))L_2\).
For a bounded number of coefficients of modulus one, an interval of length \(o(b)\) containing \(p\) terms must leave at least \(\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\) terms outside for all sufficiently large seed degrees, by integrality of the outside count and finiteness of the possible counts.
In particular two or more terms cannot occupy a single such interval with just one remaining term at arbitrary distance.
A single coefficient has at least its absolute value in the remainder whenever there is a circle conjugate, directly by the triangle inequality.
For a reciprocal seed (2) gives the corresponding inequality exactly at either endpoint even without a circle conjugate.

<a id="phase"></a>
## The phase correlations of the selected terms

Write the conjugates as \(r_j z_j\) with \(|z_j|=1\), and let \(\mathbb E\) denote the uniform mean over \(j\).
For each nonzero frequency \(n\) with \(|n|\le V\), every torsion-quotient class gives at least one distinct conjugate of \(x^n\), and conjugates from different classes have distinct powers.
Thus \(D=[\mathbb Q(x^n):\mathbb Q]\ge d_0\), with uniform multiplicities in the original orbit.
Apply the [compact Lipschitz discrepancy estimate](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) with regularization radius \(1/D\) to the minimal polynomial of \(x^n\).
Test the two components of phase multiplied by a fixed piecewise linear radial cutoff supported on \([1/2,2]\) and equal to one on \([3/4,4/3]\).
The tests have zero circle mean, bounded Lipschitz constants and bounded Dirichlet integrals, and the nonzero integral discriminant has modulus at least one.
Removing the cutoff costs \(O(|n|h)\) since the mean absolute logarithmic radius of the powered conjugates is \(2|n|h\).
When \(Vh\le1\) this bounds \(|\mathbb E z_j^n|\) by \(C\sqrt{Vh+\log(2d_0)/d_0}\), using \(\log(2D)/D\ll\log(2d_0)/d_0\).
The trivial bound is one for every \(V\).
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E |G(z_j)|^2\ge L_2^2-E(L_1^2-L_2^2).
\]
At a circle conjugate \(G(z_j)=-R(z_j)\); its modulus is at most \(\|R\|_1\).
At every other phase \(|G(z_j)|\le L_1\), giving (1).
The [critical height](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#statement), [separation of torsion quotients](Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#statement) and the critical scales yield the asserted specialization.

<a id="reflected"></a>
## Evaluating at actual conjugates within the closed unit disk

Shift all exponents so the smallest is zero.
For a right endpoint apply the same argument to the reversed polynomial, which vanishes at all conjugates by reciprocity.
Now the exponents of \(G\) lie in \([0,V]\).
For each \(j\) put \(w_j=e^{-|\log r_j|}z_j\).
This is the original conjugate if \(r_j\le1\) and its reciprocal complex conjugate otherwise, hence \(F(w_j)=0\) in both cases.
Therefore \(|G(w_j)|=|R(w_j)|\le\|R\|_1\) regardless of the exponents of \(R\).
A product of a monomial of \(G\) and the complex conjugate of another acquires a radial multiplier \(e^{-t|\log r_j|}\) with \(0\le t\le2V\).
Its mean absolute difference from one is at most \(2V\mathbb E|\log r_j|=4Vh\).
Summing the errors with the absolute coefficient products proves (2).

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The existing quadratic divisor estimate

Taking the whole support as a single interval in the previously reviewed [quadratic sparse-divisor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) already gives \(Wm/d\gg 1/\rho^2\) for a rational multiple of total span \(W\) and effective support \(\rho=\|F\|_1^2/\|F\|_2^2\), including powers that lose degree.
That theorem includes the irreducible and summed degree-share conclusions and compares the bound with the factorial factor in [Dobrowolski's sparse divisor lemma](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible).
The collaborating research agents also obtained the removal of the power-degree error from the [class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels), of degree \(d_0\) and measure \(m\).
[Voutier's corollary](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(\log(2d_0)/d_0\ll m\), since \(\log(2t)\log^3(3t)/t\) is bounded on \([2,\infty)\) and the nontorsion class-product unit has degree greater than one.
For a full annihilating interval \(W\ge d\), the degree term is therefore absorbed by \(Wh\).
For a proper subset the interval may have \(V<d\), and (1) and (2) keep its own phase error and measure the omitted coefficients even when their exponents increase without bound.
