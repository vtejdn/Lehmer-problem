# When a reciprocal algebraic unit has a two-class multiple

<a id="statement"></a>
## Existence in the original degree

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with reciprocal minimal polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(D\).
Fix an integer \(p\ge2\) and put \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
The following are equivalent.

1. \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
2. There exists a monic \(J\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(J(0)=\pm1,\ J^*=\pm J,\ P\mid J\), of the form
   \[
                 J(x)=U(x^p)+xV(x^p),\qquad U,V\ne0,\quad\gcd(U,V)=1.
   \]

Here \(J^*(x)=x^{\deg J}J(1/x)\), and the gcd is taken in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
When these conditions hold one can take \(\deg J\le p(D+2)+1\) with \(\deg J\equiv1\bmod p\).
In particular every [least-degree record](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#minimum) with no torsion quotients between distinct conjugates has such a multiple for every \(p\), since the \(D\) distinct conjugates have distinct \(p\)-th powers.
The multiple can have additional noncyclotomic factors.
Their total measure satisfies the bound below.

<a id="construction"></a>
## Interpolation and removal of the common row factor

If \(P\mid J\), then \(U(\beta)+\alpha V(\beta)=0\).
The two rows cannot both vanish at \(\beta\), so \(\alpha=-U(\beta)/V(\beta)\).

Conversely assume field equality and let \(Q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
It is monic of degree \(D\), and inversion permutes its roots without fixed points since \(P\) is reciprocal and \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
Thus \(Q(0)=1\) and \(Q^*=Q\).
Choose \(a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree at most \(D-1\) with \(F(\beta)=a\alpha\), by using the power basis over \(\mathbb Q\).
Put \(T=(D+2)p+1\) and set
\[
  H(x)=x^p\{F(x^p)-ax\},\qquad
  J_0(x)=Q(x^p)(1+x^{2p+1})+H(x)+x^T H(1/x).
\]
Both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) are zeros of \(H\), since they are conjugate.
The first summand is reciprocal of degree \(T\) with both extreme coefficients one.
The exponents of \(H\) lie strictly between \(0\) and \(T\), and reversal about \(T\) exchanges residue classes zero and one.
It follows that \(J_0\) is monic and reciprocal, \(J_0(0)=1\), and \(P\mid J_0\).

Write \(J_0=U_0(x^p)+xV_0(x^p)\).
The evaluation of its row of residue zero is
\[
       U_0(\beta)=\beta F(\beta)-a\beta^{D+1}
                 = a\beta(\alpha-\beta^D)\ne0,
\]
since \(\alpha^{pD-1}\ne1\).
Let \(A\) be the monic common gcd of the rows.
It is integral because \(V_0\) is monic, and \(A(0)=\pm1\) since \(U_0(0)=1\).
Reversal exchanges \(U_0,V_0\) by \(W(X)\mapsto X^{D+2}W(1/X)\).
Consequently the nonzero common roots occur with equal multiplicity at \(z\) and \(z^{-1}\), giving \(A^*=\pm A\).
Now \(J=J_0/A(x^p)\) is integral, monic, reciprocal up to sign and has coprime nonzero rows.
It retains \(P\) since \(A(\beta)\ne0\).
Its degree is \(T-p\deg A\), as asserted.

<a id="cost"></a>
## The measure paid by the other factors

Write \(m=\log M\) and let \(\theta>1\) solve \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
For *every* witness in item 2 with degree at most \(p(D+2)+1\) and degree congruent to one modulo \(p\), and every \(p>4\), one has
\[
 m(J/P)\ \ge\
 \left(1-\frac4p\right)\log\theta-m(P)
 -\frac{2}{p}\log(1+p(D+2))
 +\frac{\log2-2\log C_{1/2}}p ,
 \qquad
 C_{1/2}=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(z),
\]
where \(\mu\) is normalized Haar measure.
Indeed write \(\deg J=bp+1\).
Since reversal exchanges the two rows, \(V(X)=\sigma X^b U(1/X)\) with \(\sigma=\pm1\).
For \(f=U,\ n=\deg U,\ S=(b-n)p+1\), this gives \(J=f(x^p)+\sigma x^S f^*(x^p)\), with \(1\le S,\ (p,S)=1,\ n\le b\le D+2\).
We have \(f(0)=\pm1\) and \(\gcd(f,f^*)=1\).
A constant \(f\) would make \(J\) a signed binomial with only torsion roots, contradicting \(P\mid J\).
Apply the [two-row angular derivative inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two) with exponent \(1/2\), use \(|\operatorname{lc}f|\ge1\), and subtract \(m(P)\).
In particular, if \(m(P)\to0,\ p\to\infty\) and \(\log(2D)=o(p)\), then
\[
                         \liminf m(J/P)\ \ge\ \log\theta .
\]
Thus field equality supplies a two-class multiple even at large moduli, where every witness of the displayed degree must pay a positive total measure in its cofactor.
The bound concerns the product of the additional factors.
