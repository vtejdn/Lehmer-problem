# Small reciprocal measures force large orbits in the commutator group of the trace polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## The trace quotient at arbitrary exterior signature

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit of degree \(d\), with total logarithmic Mahler measure \(L=d\,h(x)>0\).
Let \(T\) be the splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\) of the minimal polynomial of \(x+x^{-1}\), let \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\), and let \(H'\) be its commutator subgroup.
The multiplicative rank \(s\) is the rank of the group generated by all conjugates of \(x\) modulo roots of unity.
A list of distinct inverse pairs is called independent when one representative from each pair gives multiplicatively independent classes.
Write \(D=|H'|\).

For each fixed \(r\le s\) and every ordered list of \(r\) independent pairs, the orbit of its ordered traces under \(H'\) has cardinality
\[
       v\ \ge\ c_r\,\frac{(d/L)^r}{(\log(3d))^{r\kappa_1(r)}},
       \qquad \kappa_1(r)=3\{2(r+1)^2(r+1)!\}^{r}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(D\ge v\).
If distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient, every two distinct inverse pairs are independent.
In particular the number of \(H'\)-orbits on ordered distinct trace pairs is \(O(L^2(\log(3d))^{2\kappa_1(2)})\).
These bounds do not count the inverse-pair flips acting trivially on the trace polynomial.

For **every** sequence of reciprocal units with \(L\to0\), the original trace commutator order grows faster than every fixed power of the original degree \(d\).
In particular, for each fixed \(A\ge0\), the condition \(D\le d^A\) gives a uniform positive lower bound on \(L\), allowing arbitrary torsion quotients.
If the inputs already have no torsion quotient between distinct conjugates, \(D\le d^A\) gives the stronger \(L\ge d^{1-\epsilon}\) for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and sufficiently large \(d\) depending on \(A,\epsilon\).
For such inputs, at every fixed \(L\le M\) and sufficiently large \(d\) depending on \(M\), an absolute \(c>0\) gives
\[
         D\ \ge\ d^{\,c\sqrt{\log\log d/\log\log\log d}} .           \tag{2}
\]
A first occurrence by degree of a new positive Mahler minimum has no torsion collision, by [the equal-measure degree reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).
Thus (2) applies in particular to any sequence of reciprocal degree records of bounded measure.

The same argument bounds a correspondence through an auxiliary algebraic number \(y\) in any field.
Put \(e=[\mathbb Q(x,y):\mathbb Q(y)]\), and let \(D_y\) be the order of the commutator subgroup of the splitting group of the minimal polynomial of \(y\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
For fixed \(A,\epsilon>0\), at sufficiently large \(d\) without torsion collisions,
\[
                D_y\le d^A \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                     eL\ge d^{1-\epsilon}.                          \tag{3}
\]
No bound on \(\deg y\), on its measure, or on the order of the abelian quotient of its splitting group is needed here.

<a id="relative"></a>
## Relative degree of an ordered trace tuple

For a finite rational Galois field \(E\), the intersection \(E\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is its maximal abelian subextension.
Hence restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(E\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}/\mathbb Q^{\rm ab})\) with the commutator subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\).
In particular, for each ordered tuple \(t_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\) of conjugate traces, its orbit under \(H'\) has size exactly \([\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(t_1,\ldots,t_r):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).
Adjoining \(x_i\) above these traces costs degree at most \(2^r\), by the \(r\) quadratics \(X^2-t_iX+1\).
The resulting relative degree \(\delta=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(x_1,\ldots,x_r):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\) is also at most \(d^r\) by adjoining the original coordinates directly.

The [explicit relative height product of Delsinne](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) applies to the \(r\) independent roots, all of absolute logarithmic height \(L/d\).
Its independence hypothesis holds since a monomial which is torsion would give an ordinary multiplicative relation after taking a positive power.
With \(a_r=2(r+1)^2(r+1)!\), the source gives
\[
 \left(\frac dL\right)^r
      < c_2(r)\,\delta\,\{\log(3\delta)\}^{r\kappa_1(r)},\qquad
        c_2(r)=(2r^2)^r\exp(64r^2r!\,a_r^{2r}).
\]
Substitute the two degree bounds separately to obtain the explicit estimate
\[
 r\log(d/L)
   <\log c_2(r)+r\log 2+\log v
                 +r\kappa_1(r)\log\!\bigl(r\log(3d)\bigr).          \tag{4}
\]
This gives (1), including \(r=1\).
For two roots \(u,w\) from distinct inverse pairs a dependence \(u^a w^b\in\mu\) would require nonzero \(a,b\) and \(|a|=|b|\) by equality of their positive heights.
It would make the quotient of \(u\) by one of \(w,w^{-1}\) torsion.
Under the stated collision exclusion this is impossible, and applying (1) to all the ordered pairs partitions at most \(d^2/4\) pairs into orbits of the indicated minimum size.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Growing rank at small total measure

First suppose \(x\) has no torsion quotient between distinct conjugates.
The action of the full splitting group on the rank-\(s\) lattice generated by the conjugates modulo torsion is faithful, since an element acting trivially fixes every root by the collision exclusion.
The [finite subgroup reduction modulo three](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#field) gives \(d\le|\operatorname{Gal}(P_x)|\le3^{s^2}\), where \(\operatorname{Gal}(P_x)\) denotes the full splitting group.
Thus \(s\to\infty\) at growing degree.
For any fixed \(r>A/\epsilon\), (4) and \(v\le D\le d^A\) yield \(L\gg_r d^{1-A/r}(\log(3d))^{-\kappa_1(r)}\).
This implies the stated eventual \(d^{1-\epsilon}\) lower bound.

For arbitrary reciprocal inputs, partition their conjugates by torsion quotient.
The [class products](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) form the entire orbit of a reciprocal unit of the same measure, with no torsion collision.
Modulo torsion each product equals its class representative raised to the common class size.
The compressed rank is therefore \(s\).
A sequence with \(s\) bounded has compressed degree at most \(3^{s^2}\).
At bounded degree and measure the elementary coefficient bounds leave only finitely many integral monic polynomials, so the original positive measures on such a sequence cannot tend to one.
Hence for \(L\to0\), both the original degrees and ranks tend to infinity.
Apply (1) to the **original** traces with any fixed \(r>A\).
It gives \(D/d^A\to\infty\); there is no comparison of the two trace fields before and after compression here.
This also proves the asserted uniform gap at \(D\le d^A\).

For (2) write \(Y=\log\log d,\ Z=\log Y\) for large \(d\).
From the displayed constants, both \(\log c_2(r)\) and \(r\kappa_1(r)\) are bounded above by \(\exp(C r^2\log(2r))\) for an absolute \(C\).
This follows from \(r!\le r^r\) and \(\log a_r=O(r\log(2r))\).
Take \(r=\lfloor c_0\sqrt{Y/Z}\rfloor\) with \(c_0>0\) sufficiently small that these quantities are at most \(\exp(Y/3)\) eventually.
The collision-free rank bound permits this choice.
Here \(\log(r\log(3d))=O(Y)\), so the two source error terms in (4) total \(o(\log d)\).
Since \(L\le M\), (4) now gives \(\log D\ge(r/2)\log d\) eventually, which proves (2).

<a id="correspondence"></a>
## Auxiliary fields and their trace special case

For independent conjugates \(x_i\) choose embeddings of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) carrying \(x\) to \(x_i\), and denote their images of \(y\) by \(y_i\).
The field of their \(y_i\)'s over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) has degree bounded by \(D_y\), even when some \(y_i\) coincide.
Each \(x_i\) has degree \(e\) over \(\mathbb Q(y_i)\), so the selected roots have joint relative degree \(\delta\le D_y e^r\).
Their direct bound \(\delta\le d^r\) still holds.
Repeating (4) with \(r\log e+\log D_y\) in place of \(r\log2+\log v\) proves (3), by taking fixed \(r>A/\epsilon\) and absorbing the logarithmic factor as above.
More precisely the factor \(D_y\) can be replaced by the relative orbit size of the ordered \(y_i\)'s under that commutator group.
For \(y=x+x^{-1}\) the local degree is two and the orbit size is exactly the \(v\) of (1).

The comparison with height estimates for the normal closure of \(x\) concerns the field being charged.
Amoroso and David apply Delsinne to independent conjugates in the normal closure over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) of \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(x)\), under their condition on the torsion exponent, and discuss a growing choice of rank. [Proposition 2.4 and Remark 2.5, pp. 23–25](https://www.rivmat.unipr.it/wwwprotetto/2022-13-1/Riv_Parma_13-1-2022_02.pdf#page=5)
Here the relative degree concerns an ordered tuple of **traces of rational conjugates**, and the recovery of just the selected roots costs at most \(2^r\).
The earlier [normal Salem trace-field argument](Yin_C_G0163_v1.md#statement) uses the conjugacy class of a residue Frobenius, and the [proper-subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#statement) concerns subfields inside the original root field.
The [bounded-rank descent obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v116.md#statement) controls an auxiliary extension over a field generated by chosen conjugates, whereas (3) bounds the degree of \(x\) over the proposed auxiliary field.

<a id="examples"></a>
## The cost of the full splitting field and the need for the torsion qualification

There are actual Salem units with \(D=1\) whose full splitting field has degree \(n2^n\) for unbounded even \(n\).
Take \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^a}+\zeta_{2^a}^{-1})\) for \(a\ge3\), of even degree \(n=2^{a-2}\).
It is totally real and abelian.
Choose an integral generator and use its first \(n\) powers starting with one as a rational basis.
The \((K+1)^n\) sums with coefficients in \(\{0,\ldots,K\}\) have their last \(n-1\) real embeddings in a box of side \(O_E(K)\).
Partition this box into \(O_E(K^{n-1})\) boxes of side \(1/3\), so for large \(K\) some two sums occupy the same box.
Their nonzero integral difference \(b\) has \(|b_j|<1/2\) under every embedding except the first.
Its nonzero integer norm forces \(|b_1|>2^{n-1}\), and change sign to make \(b_1>2\).
In particular \(b\) generates \(E\), since its distinguished value occurs just once among all embeddings of \(E\).
The positive root \(x>1\) of \(X^2-bX+1\) is Salem of degree \(2n\), by the negative lifting discriminant at every other embedding.
The trace splitting field is \(E\).
The \(n\) conjugates \(b_i^2-4\) have sign vectors with positive sign at their own distinct position and negative sign elsewhere, since \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\) acts regularly on the conjugates.
Coding a negative sign by one gives the all-ones matrix minus the identity, invertible modulo two for even \(n\).
Thus their squareclasses in \(E^\times/E^{\times2}\) are independent.
Their square roots generate the full splitting field over \(E\) of degree \(2^n\), by the elementary multiquadratic degree formula.

In the other direction, bounded positive \(L\) alone does not imply (2) before torsion compression.
Set \(u=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), take any odd prime \(p\), and let \(w=u^{1/p}>1\).
The polynomial \(X^p-u\) is irreducible over \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
Indeed a monic proper factor of degree \(1\le j<p\) would have, up to sign, constant term \(w^j\zeta\in K\) for a \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\).
Raising to \(p\) and using \(\gcd(j,p)=1\) would make \(u\) a \(p\)-th power in \(K\).
The real \(p\)-th root would then be an algebraic integer unit of norm one, with integral trace \(w+w^{-1}\) strictly between \(2\) and \(u+u^{-1}=3\), an impossibility.
Thus \(w\) has reciprocal minimal polynomial \(X^{2p}-3X^p+1\), of measure \(u\), and its conjugates have multiplicative rank one.
Its real root field has nonreal conjugates and cannot be a subfield of \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
The abelian base contains \(u\) and all \(p\)-th roots of unity, so adjoining \(w\) gives a nontrivial Galois extension with group embedded in \(\mu_p\) via \(g\mapsto g(w)/w\).
It therefore has degree \(p\).
For a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\) the two conjugate traces \(w+w^{-1}\) and \(\zeta w+\zeta^{-1}w^{-1}\) recover \(w\) by linear elimination over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
All other traces already belong to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(w)\), so \(D=p\) exactly.
