# The sharp individual-factor gap for arbitrary integer trinomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Three nonzero coefficients

Let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(X^3-X-1\), and let \(M\) denote multiplicative Mahler measure.
For every nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) with at most three nonzero monomials, every integral divisor \(S\) with \(M(S)>1\) satisfies
\[
                              M(S)\ge\theta .
\]
The constant is attained.
There is no bound on the sizes of the three coefficients, their exponents, or the measure of the cofactor.

For a monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic unit divisor \(S\), the stronger sharp bound is
\[
                         M(S)\ge (3+\sqrt5)/2 .
\]
In fact \(M(S)\) is a real quadratic algebraic unit of norm one.
Every conjugate of a root of \(S\) has one of two reciprocal moduli, both different from one.
Consequently the minimal polynomial of a Salem number of degree at least four cannot divide a polynomial with at most three terms, even with arbitrary integral coefficients.

<a id="powers"></a>
## The two powers generate only a quadratic field

Remove a common monomial from \(P\), since \(X\) has measure one.
A nonzero constant cannot have a nonconstant divisor.
For a binomial \(AX^n+C\) with both coefficients nonzero, if \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) are roots then \(\alpha^n=-C/A=\alpha^{-n}\), which makes \(\alpha\) torsion.
Consider a genuine trinomial
\[
               P(X)=AX^n+BX^m+C,\qquad ABC\ne0,\quad n>m>0 .
\]
Let \(\alpha\) be a root of the reciprocal unit divisor in the statement.
Both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) are roots of \(P\).
Multiplying \(A\alpha^{\pm n}=-B\alpha^{\pm m}-C\) in the two orientations gives
\[
            \alpha^m+\alpha^{-m}=(A^2-B^2-C^2)/(BC)\in\mathbb Q .
\]
Since \(\alpha\) is a unit, this rational trace is an algebraic integer and hence an integer \(k\).
If \(|k|\le2\), the roots of \(Y^2-kY+1\) for those five integer values are all roots of unity, contradicting nontorsion.
Thus \(|k|\ge3\) and the two real roots of this quadratic have reciprocal moduli on opposite sides of one.
The quadratic is irreducible, since a rational root that is an algebraic unit must be \(1\) or \(-1\).
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^m)\) for this real quadratic field.
Also \(\alpha^n=-(B\alpha^m+C)/A\) belongs to \(K\).
In particular Bézout's identity gives \(\alpha^{\gcd(n,m)}\in K\), so if the two exponents are coprime then \(S\) itself has degree two.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The exact measure from a relative norm

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(e=[F:K]\).
Above each of the two real embeddings of \(K\) all \(e\) images of \(\alpha\) have the same modulus, since their \(m\)th powers are the same number.
If the larger modulus among the two conjugates of \(\alpha^m\) is \(s>1\), these two moduli of \(\alpha\) are \(s^{1/m}\) and \(s^{-1/m}\), each attained \(e\) times counting embeddings.
Hence \(M(S)=s^{e/m}\).

The relative norm \(u=N_{F/K}(\alpha)\) is a unit whose value at the exterior embedding of \(K\) has absolute value \(s^{e/m}\).
The reciprocal roots of \(S\) form inverse pairs because \(S\) is irreducible and has no root \(1\) or \(-1\).
Their product is one, so \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(u)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)=1\).
The real quadratic field therefore contains the positive unit \(M(S)\), obtained as the appropriate sign of the exterior conjugate of \(u\).
Its norm is one and it exceeds one, so it has degree two and its integer trace \(M(S)+M(S)^{-1}\) is at least three.
This gives the stronger inequality, with equality for \(S=P=X^2-3X+1\), irreducible by its discriminant five.
The two moduli just found exclude the circle conjugates of any Salem number of degree at least four.

<a id="other"></a>
## The remaining factors and equality at the plastic number

For a primitive irreducible factor of measure greater than one whose leading or nonzero constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, the root-product formula in the two orientations gives \(M\ge2\).
The remaining primitive factors of measure greater than one can be taken monic with unit constant term after choosing their sign.
The [nonreciprocal theorem of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives \(M\ge\theta\) for the nonreciprocal ones.
The reciprocal case proved above exceeds this bound, since \(1<\theta<2\).
Integer content of absolute value at least two also suffices, and measures of nonzero integer polynomials are at least one and multiply under products.
This proves the statement for arbitrary integral divisors.

The trinomial \(X^3-X-1\) is irreducible by the rational root test and has one real root \(\theta>1\) and a conjugate nonreal pair, since its discriminant is \(-23\).
The squared modulus of either nonreal root is \(1/\theta\) by the product of the three roots.
Thus its measure equals \(\theta\).
