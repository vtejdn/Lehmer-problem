# Odd prime cyclotomic intersections and logarithmic Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound over the prime

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer whose positive powers all generate \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Write \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(m=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
For an odd prime \(p\), put \(F=K\cap\bigcup_{a\ge1}\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^a})\), \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(A\ge1\) be the least level for which \(F\subseteq E=\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^A})\).
Such a level exists since the successive intersections form a nested chain of bounded degree.
Put \(w=p^A,\ h=[E:F]\) and
\[
 k=\begin{cases}w,&h=1,\\ (w-1)/h,&h>1.\end{cases}
\]
Then \(h\mid p-1\), \(s\le k\), and for every integer \(J\ge1\),
\[
 (kJ-1)\log p
 \ \le\ \log\!\left(\frac{kh}{w}\,p^{1/(p-1)}Jd\right)
      +\left(2-\frac{2}{kJhd}\right)kh\frac{p^J-1}{p-1}\,m .        \tag{1}
\]
Here \(s=\varphi(w)/h\) counts the primitive-root orbits.
When \(A>1\), the use of lower-order roots makes \(k>s\).

Along any sequence of these inputs with \(hm\to0\), allowing the odd prime \(p\) to vary arbitrarily,
\[
                \limsup\frac{k\log(1/(hm))}{\log d}\le1 .             \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if \(m\to0\) and \(\log h=o(\log(1/m))\), then
\[
            \limsup\frac{k\log(1/m)}{\log d}\le1,\qquad
                          s=o(\log d).                                \tag{3}
\]
This includes fixed \(p\) and the range \(\log p=o(\log(1/m))\).
For each \(\eta>0\) there is an effective \(c(\eta)>0\) such that
\[
                 s\ge\eta\log d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                         hm\ge c(\eta) .                              \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(m\ge c(\eta)/(p-1)\) at fixed \(p\), and the bound \(m\ge c(\eta)/H\) holds simultaneously at all odd primes with \(h\le H\).
All statements allow any number of exterior conjugates.
For an arbitrary nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer they apply to its [torsion-quotient class products](Yin_C_G0345_v714.md#scope), which preserve the original total logarithmic measure, with \(K,d,F,s,h,k\) taken for the product.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## All nonidentity twists at the minimal level

Put \(L=KE\) and \(H_0=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\).
As in the [dyadic twist argument](Yin_C_G0345_v714.md#twists), restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) with \(H_0\), so \([L:\mathbb Q]=hd\).
Reduction modulo \(p\) embeds \(H_0\) in \((\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\).
For \(A=1\) this is immediate.
For \(A>1\) the kernel on the full unit group is cyclic of order \(p^{A-1}\), generated by \(1+p\), since \(v_p((1+p)^{p^j}-1)=j+1\) by the binomial theorem and induction.
Its unique subgroup of order \(p\) is the kernel of restriction from \(E\) to \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^{A-1}})\).
An intersection of \(H_0\) with the first kernel larger than the identity would contain that order-\(p\) subgroup and force \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^{A-1}})\), contrary to minimality.
This proves the injection and \(h\mid p-1\).

Every nonidentity element of \(H_0\) now moves every nonidentity root in \(\mu_w\), since its exponent differs from one modulo \(p\).
For \(h>1\) select representatives from all these free orbits, and for \(h=1\) select all of \(\mu_w\), including one.
There are exactly \(k\) representatives.
For \(0\le j<J\) and any representative \(\zeta\), the element \(\zeta\alpha^{p^j}\) generates \(K(\zeta)\) because raising it to the order of \(\zeta\) gives a generator of \(K\).
Its stabilizer over \(K\) is trivial in \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\), so this field is \(L\).
An embedding identifying two twists at the same \(j\) fixes \(\alpha^{wp^j}\) and thus fixes \(K\), forcing the representatives to come from the same orbit.
The minimal polynomials at that level are therefore distinct, with common logarithmic measure \(hp^jm\) by extension of embeddings.
Different levels have different measures.
The \(kJ\) resulting irreducibles are pairwise coprime and all have degree \(hd\).

<a id="packing"></a>
## The finite inequality

At a prime over \(p\) all \(w\)-th roots of unity reduce to one.
Each embedding of \(K\) occurs \(h\) times among those of \(L\), and raising the reduced roots of the minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(\alpha\) to \(p^j\) permutes their multiset, including repeated roots.
Consequently all \(kJ\) polynomials reduce to \(\overline P^{\,h}\).
Their individual absolute discriminants are at least \(|D_L|\) by the integral power-basis index, and \(|D_L|^{1/(hd)}\ge |D_E|^{1/\varphi(w)}\) by the field discriminant tower formula.
The [cyclotomic field discriminant](../audits/literature/Bel17/Yin_U002.md#bel17-u002) gives
\[
                           |D_E|^{1/\varphi(w)}=w/p^{1/(p-1)} .
\]
Applying the [resultant packing inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v707.md#statement) at modulus \(p\), then taking logarithms and dividing by \(kJhd\), gives the left-hand contribution \((kJ-1)\log p+\log |D_E|^{1/\varphi(w)}\) as a lower bound.
On the right the logarithms of the measures sum to \(kh(p^J-1)m/(p-1)\).
Subtracting the field discriminant contribution gives (1).

<a id="uniform"></a>
## Varying primes and an effective gap

Suppose \(hm\le1\), write \(Y=\log(1/(hm))\), and choose \(J=1+\lfloor Y/\log p\rfloor\).
Then \(p^{J-1}hm\le1\), \(J\log p>Y\), and \(J\le 1+Y/\log3\).
The measure term in (1) is at most \(2k\,p/(p-1)\le3k\).
Also \(kh\le w\) and \(p^{1/(p-1)}\le2\), since \(p\le2^{p-1}\).
It follows that
\[
            (k-1)Y\le(kJ-1)\log p\le \log d+\log(2J)+3k .            \tag{5}
\]
If \(Y\to\infty\) and \(k\to\infty\), then \(\log(2J)+3k=o(kY)\) and \((k-1)/k\to1\), giving (2).
On subsequences with bounded \(k\), \(m\to0\) forces \(d\to\infty\) by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), since a nontorsion nonzero integer of degree one has \(m\ge\log2\).
The same corollary gives \(\log(1/m)\le3\log\log(3d)-\log 2\) for \(d\ge2\).
Thus \(kY/\log d\to0\) on these subsequences.
Every subsequence has a further subsequence of one of the two types, proving (2) without any assumption on the growth of \(p\).
Under the extra hypothesis of (3), \(Y/\log(1/m)\to1\), and \(s\le k\) gives the two conclusions.

For (4) one may take
\[
 c(\eta)=\min\!\left\{\log2,\ \frac{2}{(\log3+\eta^{-1})^3},\
                              \frac{\exp(-12-4/\eta)}{16}\right\}.
\]
When \(hm>1\) the bound is immediate.
When \(k=1\) one has \(s=1\) and \(d\le e^{1/\eta}\), so the first two displayed constants suffice by the degree-one observation and Voutier's corollary.
When \(k\ge2\) and \(hm\le1\), use \(\log d\le k/\eta\) and \(2J\le2(1+Y)\le4e^{Y/2}\) in (5).
This gives \((k-3/2)Y\le k(3+\eta^{-1})+\log4\), hence \(Y\le 12+4/\eta+2\log4\).
This proves the final bound uniformly in \(p\).
