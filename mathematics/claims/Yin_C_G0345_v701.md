# Powers linking integer seeds at the same delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## Effective finiteness and a uniform bound on primes in field radicals

For integers \(a\ge2,\ n\ge7\) and a sign \(\eta\), denote the unique exterior Salem root of \(X^n(X-a)+\eta(aX-1)\) by \(r_{a,n,\eta}\).
There are only effectively finitely many tuples \((n,a,b,\eta,\epsilon,k)\) with \(a,b\ge2,\ k\ge2\) and
\[
                       r_{a,n,\eta}=r_{b,n,\epsilon}^{\,k}.             \tag{1}
\]
Both signs may be chosen independently.
Consequently, if \(r=r_{a,n,\eta}\), \(R\) is its largest positive field radical index, and \(p^e\mid R\) with \(p\) prime, then
\[
                         p^e\log(2p)\le C\log r                         \tag{2}
\]
for an effective absolute constant \(C\).
In particular, for another effective absolute constant \(C_1\),
\[
       n\ge C_1\frac{\log(a+1)}{\log\log(a+3)}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       R\mid H(a),\qquad
       H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}
                    \frac{a+j}{\operatorname{rad}(a+j)} .              \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{rad}(1)=1\).

<a id="size"></a>
## Uniform estimates at the two real roots

Write \(r=r_{a,n,\eta}\), \(z=r_{b,n,\epsilon}\), and \(L=n-1\).
The [classification for seeds at least three](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) and the [seed-two classification](Yin_C_G0345_v682.md#roots) give \(z>3/2,\ b<z+1\), and the same estimates at \(a\).
The root equation gives \(|z-b|\le (z^2+z+1)z^{-n}\ll(3/2)^{2-n}\).
Consequently both roots are within \(1/10\) of their respective seeds at all sufficiently large delays, uniformly in the seeds.
If (1) holds there, \(a>(b-1/10)^2-1/10>b+1\) for every integer \(b\ge2\), so \(a\ge b+2\).

A Salem number and each of its positive integral powers generate the same number field.
Indeed the exterior real power has precisely one preimage among the conjugates of the original number, while each embedding of the subfield generated by that power has equally many extensions.
Thus (1) puts \(z\) in \(\mathbb Q(r)\).
The [irreducibility theorem at two](Yin_C_G0345_v682.md#statement) excludes \(a=2\).
For \(a\ge3\) the [uniform radical index estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) gives
\[
                            k\ll n^2\log^5(3n)                          \tag{4}
\]
with an effective absolute constant.

<a id="phase"></a>
## Two nearby conjugate angles

Use the smooth periodic odd function
\[
        h_b(t)=-\arg(1-e^{it}/b)
               =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\sin(jt)}{jb^j}.
\]
The argument denotes its continuous branch equal to zero at \(t=0\).
By the [circle equation](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#angles), which follows directly from the polynomial for every \(b\ge2\), the neutral roots have angles satisfying \(Lt-2h_b(t)\equiv\arg\epsilon\bmod 2\pi\).
The map \(t\mapsto t-2h_b(t)/L\) on the real line has derivative at least \(1-2/(L(b-1))\ge2/3\), since \(|h_b'|\le1/(b-1)\).
Write \(t_b\) for its inverse and set \(F(x)=h_b(t_b(x))\).
For \(\epsilon=1\), take \(x=2\pi/L,\ s=2\).
For \(\epsilon=-1\), take \(x=\pi/L,\ s=3\).
For sufficiently large \(L\), both \(t_1=t_b(x)\) and \(t_s=t_b(sx)\) belong to \((0,\pi/3)\), by \(0<t_b(y)\le3y/2\) for \(y>0\).
The corresponding roots are conjugates of \(z\), since the only cyclotomic roots possible at these seeds have angles \(0,\pi\), or \(\pm\pi/3\).
Their \(k\)-th powers are roots of the polynomial at seed \(a\).
Subtracting their angle equations from \(k\) times the original angle equations gives
\[
              E_j=kF(jx)-h_a(kt_j)\in(\pi/2)\mathbb Z,\qquad j=1,s.     \tag{5}
\]

For \(0<t_1<\pi/3\), one has \(F(x)>\sin t_1/(b+1)\) by \(\sin h_b(t_1)=\sin t_1/|b-e^{it_1}|\).
Also
\[
            |h_a(kt_1)|\le |\sin(kt_1)|/(a-1)
                         \le k\sin t_1/(a-1)
\]
by the arctangent formula and the elementary multiple-angle inequality.
Since \(a\ge b+2\), \(E_1>0\) and hence \(E_1\ge\pi/2\).
It follows that \(k F(x)\ge\pi/2-\arcsin(1/a)\).
On the other hand \(F(x)\le t_1/(b-1)\ll1/(bL)\), so \(k\gg bL\).
In particular, as \(n\) increases along putative coincidences,
\[
                  \arcsin(1/a)=o\!\left(\frac{k}{bL^3}\right) .        \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(a\ge (3/2)^k-1\) at large delays, and \(k/(bL^3)\gg L^{-2}\).

<a id="curvature"></a>
## A cubic term smaller than the spacing of the possible phases

At the origin put \(v=1-2/(L(b-1))\).
Differentiating \(F(x)=h_b(x+2F(x)/L)\) at zero and using oddness gives
\[
            F'''(0)=h_b'''(0)/v^4=-\frac{b(b+1)}{(b-1)^3v^4}.
\]
Uniformly in \(L\ge6,b\ge2\), its absolute value lies between \(1/b\) and \(C_2/b\).
The series for \(h_b\) bounds \(\|h_b^{(j)}\|_\infty\) by \(b^{-1}\sum_{i\ge1}i^{j-1}2^{1-i}\) for \(1\le j\le5\).
Differentiating the same composition successively to order five gives \(\|F^{(5)}\|_\infty\le C_3/b\).
Indeed at each order the highest derivative of \(F\) has coefficient \(1-2h_b'(t_b(x))/L\ge2/3\), and every term on the other side contains a derivative of \(h_b\) and only bounded lower-order derivatives of \(t_b\).
The constants in these finite estimates are computable.
Taylor's theorem and oddness now give
\[
           sF(x)-F(sx)=\frac{(s^3-s)|F'''(0)|}{6}\,x^3
                            +O(x^5/b)\asymp\frac{1}{bL^3}              \tag{7}
\]
with effective positive bounds for both choices of \(s,x\) at all sufficiently large delays.

By (5), \(sE_1-E_s\in(\pi/2)\mathbb Z\).
It differs from \(k(sF(x)-F(sx))\) by at most \((s+1)\arcsin(1/a)\).
By (6) and (7) it is strictly positive at large delays, while by (4) and (7) it tends uniformly to zero, since \(k/(bL^3)\ll \log^5(3n)/n\).
This contradicts its membership in the discrete set.
Every step uses explicit power series, finite derivative bounds or the effective bound (4), so the excluded tail of delays starts at a computable integer.
For each remaining delay the [effective fixed-delay seed cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v694.md#cutoff) bounds \(a\).
Then \(z>3/2,\ b<z+1,\ z^k=r<a+1\) bound \(b,k\) effectively, and real algebraic root isolation decides (1).
This proves the first assertion.

<a id="divisors"></a>
## Eliminating the equal-root alternative for large primes

For \(a\ge3,\ p\ge11\), apply the [small congruence and norm dichotomy](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#linear) to \(\beta=r^{1/p^e}\).
Its exact-zero alternative yields (1) with \(k=p^e\).
The finite effective list just proved supplies an absolute effective upper bound for \(\log(2p)/\log\beta\) on this alternative.
In the other case,
\[
             \log\beta\ \ge\ \frac{d}{n+1}\log\frac{p}{4j_p},
       \qquad d\ge n-1,\quad j_p\le\lceil\sqrt p\rceil ,
\]
which is at least a positive constant times \(\log(2p)\) for all sufficiently large \(p\).
The [uniform prime-power gap](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) covers all remaining bounded primes.
Multiplication by \(p^e\) proves (2) for \(a\ge3\), while \(R=1\) for \(a=2\).
If a prime \(p>n\) divides \(R\), then \(n\log(2n)<C\log r\le C\log(a+1)\).
Elementary inversion of this increasing function gives the threshold in (3), enlarging \(C_1\) on bounded inputs.
The [short-prime valuation bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#radicals) give \(v_p(R)\le v_p(H(a))\) for every \(p\le n\), proving (3).
