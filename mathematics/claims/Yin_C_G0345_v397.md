# Alternating trace groups for reciprocal sections and primes certifying a quartic factorization

<a id="statement"></a>
## A family of trace fields with a full alternating action

Fix \(s\ge3\) with \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\), and put \(g(T)=T^s-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\).
For coprime positive \(a,b\) set \(F(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\).
Let \(x\) be a nontorsion root of \(F\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\), and put \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
For **every** such direction any intermediate field \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subseteq K\) satisfies
\[
                [K:E]\le2s .
\]
For \(d>4s\) the only subfield of \(K\) normal over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(\mathbb Q\).
For all sufficiently large \(\max(a,b)\), uniformly for the individual factors at fixed \(s\), the minimal polynomial of \(x\) is reciprocal and the group on the \(n=d/2\) conjugates of \(x+x^{-1}\) is \(A_n\) or \(S_n\).
If an odd number of complex conjugate pairs of those traces are nonreal, the group is \(S_n\).

For the quartic \(s=4,\ a=3,\ 3\nmid b\), let \(P_b\) denote the monic polynomial obtained after removing all cyclotomic factors with multiplicities and write \(P_b(X)=X^n H_b(X+X^{-1})\).
For all sufficiently large \(b\), the negative exterior root and the nonreal exterior pair belong to one rational factor if and only if the entire trace \(H_b\) has irreducible reduction at some prime.
In the one-factor case the natural density of those primes is \(1/n\).
For **every** modulus \(m\ge1\) there is such a certificate with \(p\equiv(-1)^{n-1}\bmod m\).
If the core splits, its factor with the nonreal exterior pair still has the full symmetric group on its own traces.
A split for \(b\ge26\) yields the [Salem number below \(9/8\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic).

<a id="inertia"></a>
## Inertia moves at most \(4s\) roots

In every finite characteristic \(g,B\) are coprime for the stated \(s\).
Indeed a common zero \(t\) would be nonzero and satisfy \(t^{s-2}=-1\) by \(g+B=-T(T^{s-2}+1)\).
Then \(g(t)=0\) gives \(t^2+t+1=0\), so \(t^3=1\).
Cubing \(t^{s-2}=-1\) forces characteristic two.
There \(t\) has exact order three, contradicting \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\).

For a root \(z\) of the section over an algebraic closure of a finite residue field put \(t=z^a\).
Here \(g(t),B(t)\ne0\) and \(z^b=-g(t)/B(t)\).
Since \(a,b\) are coprime, the powers \(z^a,z^b\) determine \(z\), so distinct roots have distinct \(t\)-coordinates.
If the characteristic divides \(a\), the derivative \(b z^{b-1} B(t)\) is nonzero at every root.
Otherwise, with
\[
 R(T)=aT(g'B-gB')-b gB ,
\qquad XB(t)F'(X)=(bB(t)+atB'(t))F(X)+R(t),\quad t=X^a ,
\]
\(R\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2s\).
Its constant term is \(b\), and in a characteristic dividing \(b\) the constant term of \(g'B-gB'\) is \(-1\), with \(a\ne0\).
The local change \(t=X^a\) has nonzero derivative.
A root of the reduction of \(F\) of multiplicity \(e_z\) therefore accounts for at least \(e_z-1\) zeros of \(R\), counting multiplicities.
Summing gives \(\sum_z(e_z-1)\le2s\).
All roots with nonsingleton residue clusters occupy at most \(4s\) positions, since \(e\le2(e-1)\) for \(e\ge2\).
Thus each finite inertia element of the rational splitting field moves at most \(4s\) actual roots, also at wildly ramified primes.

As in the [finite inertia argument for the cubic](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields), conjugates of the finite inertia groups generate the rational Galois group, since their fixed quotient field would be unramified at every finite prime and Minkowski's theorem excludes a nontrivial number field of absolute discriminant one.
For \(E\ne\mathbb Q\) in \(K\), the fibers of restriction from the \(K\)-embeddings to the \(E\)-embeddings have size \([K:E]\).
Some inertia element moves an \(E\)-embedding, hence moves at least two fibers and at least \(2[K:E]\) roots of the minimal polynomial of \(x\).
This gives the index bound.
If \(E\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\), every nonidentity automorphism of \(E\) acts without a fixed embedding, so such an inertia element moves all \(d\) roots.

<a id="trace"></a>
## From bounded subfield index to one alternating block

The [primitive-direction degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) makes \(d\) tend to infinity uniformly over nontorsion factors.
Indeed \(g(U)+VB(U)\) is absolutely irreducible by coprimality and linearity in \(V\), and its support differences contain both coordinate unit vectors.
For the same reason the [torsion-quotient classification](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) excludes a torsion quotient between distinct nontorsion roots eventually.

Along any sequence of distinct primitive directions the upper limit of \(m(F)\) is at most \(m(g)\).
For a nonconstant torus character of exponents \((j,k)\) the mean on \(z\mapsto(z^a,z^b)\) is eventually zero, since \(ja+kb=0\) prescribes at most one positive primitive direction.
Approximation by trigonometric polynomials therefore gives convergence of the integrals of each continuous truncated logarithm \(\log\max(\delta,|g(U)+VB(U)|)\).
Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero and integrating first in \(V\) gives the mean of \(\log\max(|g(U)|,|B(U)|)=\log|g(U)|\) on the circle.
This is the [upper-limit argument for reciprocal graph sections](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#pairs).
Also \(M(g)\le\|g\|_2=\sqrt3<\theta^2\), where \(\theta^3=\theta+1,\ \theta>1\).
For the strict comparison, \(\theta>33/25\) by evaluating its cubic, and \((33/25)^4>3\).
A nonreciprocal nontorsion factor and its distinct reversal in the reciprocal parent cost at least \(2\log\theta\) together by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001).
This proves eventual reciprocity at fixed \(s\).

For a reciprocal factor the \(n=d/2\) inverse pairs are now exactly its multiplicative labels, with no torsion compression.
The [alternating block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) provides blocks of size \(h\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) at bounded \(m(x)\), with a full separate \(A_h\) on each.
Here \(m(x)\le\log6\) from the parent coefficient length.
If such a block were proper, its preimage on the roots would be a proper block of size \(2h\).
The fixed field of its setwise stabilizer would be a nontrivial subfield of \(K\) of index \(2h\), since one may choose the block containing \(x\).
The [index bound](#inertia) excludes this for large \(d\).
Thus there is only one block and \(A_n\) acts on all the traces.
Complex conjugation contributes an odd permutation when the number of nonreal trace pairs is odd.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Opposite residue classes for the two parities of the quartic trace degree

For \(b>18\) the [quartic root count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic) gives one negative real exterior root, one nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots and no other exterior roots.
For large \(b\) all nontorsion factors are reciprocal.
Exactly one of the individual trace polynomials has a nonreal conjugate pair, and that pair is its only one: both inverse pairs over the two nonreal exterior roots have nonreal traces, whereas every real or unit-circle root has real trace.
Every nontorsion factor receives an exterior root, so the factor containing the nonreal pair is the entire core if and only if there is no second rational factor.
There are no repeated nontorsion factors since the three exterior roots are simple.
By the preceding result that one factor always has trace group \(S_{n_i}\) on its \(n_i\) inverse pairs.
Thus in the one-factor case the entire trace group is \(S_n\); in the two-factor case the trace has a proper monic factor over the integers, excluding irreducible reduction at every prime.

In the one-factor case let \(L\) be the trace splitting field and \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}H_b})\) its sign field.
Complex conjugation is a single transposition, so \(E\) is imaginary quadratic.
For \(n\) sufficiently large \(E\) is the maximal abelian subfield of \(L\), since \([S_n,S_n]=A_n\).
For \(K_m=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\), the fiber of \({\rm Gal}(LK_m/\mathbb Q)\) over a residue automorphism of \(K_m\) therefore projects either onto all of \(S_n\) when \(E\not\subseteq K_m\), or onto the coset of the specified sign when \(E\subseteq K_m\).
For odd \(n\), the identity residue class permits the even \(n\)-cycles.
For even \(n\), the class of \(-1\) acts as complex conjugation on \(K_m\); it permits the odd \(n\)-cycles since \(E\) is imaginary.
The \(n\)-cycles have proportion \(1/n\) in \(S_n\) and \(2/n\) in their sign coset.
By [Chebotarev's density theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) these give respective relative natural densities \(1/n\) and \(2/n\) inside the indicated congruence class, after removing the finite set of primes of bad reduction.
In particular the unrestricted density is \(1/n\) and every stated progression supplies infinitely many certificates.
The result leaves the least certificate prime and the existence of an actual split unresolved.
