# Four dyadic matches are the limit for distinct integral trace-pole solutions

<a id="statement"></a>
## Separation across different heights and degrees

Take any two genuine [integral trace-pole realizations](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), allowing different heights \(q_i\) and different degrees \(d_i\) of the monic reciprocal lifts \(F_i\).
Fix \(X\ge\max(6,d_1,d_2)\).
There is an absolute effective \(b=O(\log(2X))\) with the following property.
If \(F_1\ne F_2\), any common monic factor of their reductions modulo \(2^b\) has degree at most four.
More precisely, over any finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\) its reduction over the residue field divides one of
\[
 (z+1)^2,\qquad (z+1)^4,\qquad (z+1)^2(z^2+z+1).                      \tag{1}
\]
Here the degree-two bound in the first case uses the unit constant of a common factor.
Conversely a common monic factor of degree five at that precision forces the same height, same exterior root and same rational minimum.

In particular place all roots in a common dyadic splitting field with \(v(2)=1\).
At most four disjoint pairs of conjugates can have \(v(\alpha_i-\alpha_j')\ge b\) for distinct solutions, with each root on either side used at most once.
Every such match reduces to a root of unity of order one or three in the residue field.
Even one match outside these residues determines the solution uniquely among all those under the degree bound.
This criterion compares lifted factors or roots, and places no bound on the ordinary gcd of the two reductions over \(\mathbb F_2\).
It does not require the height or the exact degree as input when making a uniqueness inference from matching data.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Eliminating the large exponent before testing a factor

In each solution write \(T_i=\beta_i^{u_i}\) for the unique exterior root and \(Y_i=(p_iT_i-q_i)/(p_i-q_iT_i)=\beta_i^{v_i}\), with \(p_i=q_i+1\) and \(1\le u_i<v_i\) coprime.
The [two-power criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) and [exponent bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#census) apply at every admissible height.
Put \(\epsilon_i=1,\ x_i=q_i/p_i\) for even \(q_i\), and \(\epsilon_i=-1,\ x_i=p_i/q_i\) for odd \(q_i\).
In either case \(x_i\in2\mathbb Z_2\), and with
\[
 V(x,z)=\frac{1-xz^{-1}}{1-xz},\qquad N_i=v_i-\epsilon_i u_i>0,
\]
the equation in the \(i\)-th field is \(T_i^{N_i}=V(x_i,T_i)^{\epsilon_i u_i}\).
It follows by writing \(Y_i=T_i^{\epsilon_i}V(x_i,T_i)^{\epsilon_i}\).

Suppose \(B\) is a common monic factor modulo \(2^b\) in the integer ring of any finite dyadic extension.
Choose a monic lift and let \(R\) be the quotient of the polynomial ring over that integer ring by the lift.
It is a complete finite free module, and \(z\), \(1-x_i z\), \(1-x_i z^{-1}\) are units there because the residual factor has nonzero constant term.
Clearing denominators in each original power equation gives an integral polynomial identity divisible by the monic \(F_i\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\); reduction gives both equations in \(R/2^bR\).
Eliminating \(z^{N_1N_2}\) yields
\[
   V(x_1,z)^m=V(x_2,z)^n\bmod 2^bR,\qquad
   m=\epsilon_1 u_1 N_2,\quad n=\epsilon_2 u_2 N_1 .                  \tag{2}
\]
The positive degree of each solution is the original degree of the reciprocal minimum.
This step does not identify the multiplicative order of \(z\) in the smaller factor ring with its order in either original field.

<a id="logs"></a>
## Only the first two harmonics can survive at the lowest valuation

For \(x\in2\mathbb Z_2\) the logarithmic series converges in \(R\) and gives
\[
          \log V(x,z)=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{x^j}{j}(z^j-z^{-j}).
\]
The logarithm is additive on products of commuting elements of \(1+2R\) by the convergent formal power series identity over \(R\otimes\mathbb Q_2\).
For \(b\ge2\) it sends \(1+2^bR\) into \(2^bR\).
Thus (2) gives a sum in \(2^bR\) with scalar numerators \(a_j=m x_1^j-n x_2^j\).

If \(a_1,a_2\) are not both zero put \(h=\min(v_2(a_1),v_2(a_2)-1)\), with \(v_2(0)=+\infty\).
All \(a_j\) satisfy the recurrence \(a_{j+2}=(x_1+x_2)a_{j+1}-x_1x_2a_j\).
Since the roots of this recurrence are even, induction gives \(v_2(a_j)\ge h+j-1\).
For \(j\ge3\) the integer \(j-1-v_2(j)\) is positive and tends to infinity.
Therefore, if \(b>h\), division by \(2^h\) and reduction modulo the maximal ideal leave only the first and second harmonics.
At least one of their coefficients is one in \(\mathbb F_2\) by the definition of \(h\).
After multiplication by \(z^2\) the residual relation is consequently one of
\[
                 z^3+z,\qquad z^4+1,\qquad z^4+z^3+z+1 .
\]
The residual monic factor divides that polynomial because the quotient was taken by a monic lift in a free module.
Factoring in characteristic two and removing the impossible factor \(z\) gives (1).
This argument allows nilpotents and arbitrarily ramified finite extensions.

<a id="precision"></a>
## Effective precision, coincidence and matching individual roots

For an explicit common precision set
\[
 H=\lceil 2+(\log(3X))^3\rceil,\quad W=\lceil XH(2+\log(2H+1))\rceil,\quad
 b=2+\left\lceil\log_2\big(2HW(H+1)^4\big)\right\rceil .
\]
Indeed \(u_i,q_i\le H\) and \(N_i\le W\) by the exponent estimates: the primitive Salem generator has \(\log\beta_i>2/(\log(3d_i))^3\) and the pole identity gives \(Y_i<(2q_i+1)^{d_i}\).
Each nonzero \(a_j\) for \(j=1,2\) has odd denominator and numerator of absolute value at most \(2HW(H+1)^{2j}\), by writing \(x_i\) as the even member divided by the odd member of two consecutive integers.
Consequently \(h<b\) unless \(a_1=a_2=0\).
In that exceptional case the two exact rational equations give \(x_1=x_2\) and \(m=n\).
The value of \(x_i\), lying on opposite sides of one in the two parity cases, recovers \(q_i\) uniquely.
Equality of \(m,n\) then gives \(v_1/u_1=v_2/u_2\).
For fixed \(q\) the [strictly increasing real ratio](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#real) \(\log Y/\log T\) on \(1<T<(q+1)/q\) identifies \(T\) and hence the entire minimum.

Finally five disjoint close pairs would supply the common degree-five monic factor obtained by multiplying their five linear factors on each side in a common splitting field and reducing modulo \(2^b\).
A single close pair similarly supplies a common linear factor.
These deductions need neither a rational lift of the selected factor nor squarefreeness at two.
