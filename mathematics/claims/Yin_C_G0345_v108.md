# Reciprocal factors excluded from every monomial shift of a cyclotomic product

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction independent of the parent and cofactor degrees

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(d\) whose roots are not roots of unity.
Write its roots as \(\alpha_j=\exp(t_j+i\theta_j)\), its logarithmic Mahler measure as \(m=\sum_{t_j>0}t_j\), its largest absolute logarithmic modulus as \(T=\max_j|t_j|\), and its number of strictly exterior roots as \(r=\#\{j:t_j>0\}\).
Suppose that \(P\) divides \(A+aX^k\), where \(A\) is any nonconstant monic product of cyclotomic polynomials, \(a\) is any nonzero integer, and \(k\ge0\) is an integer.
Then every pair of conjugates satisfies
\[
       |\cosh t_i-\cosh t_j|
                     \le |\cos\theta_i-\cos\theta_j|.                  \tag{1}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(d\), every such factor satisfies, with an absolute implied constant,
\[
              r\gg \frac{d^{1/4}(\log\log d)^3}{(\log d)^{13/4}}.    \tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(B\), if \(m\le B\), then \(T\ll_B(\log(2d)/d)^{1/4}\).
For each fixed \(R\), there are only finitely many such \(P\) having both \(r\le R\) and at least one root on the unit circle.
In particular, **only finitely many Salem numbers** have a minimal polynomial dividing any of these shifts, even with unrestricted exponents, coefficients and cofactors.
The Salem convention here includes a conjugate on the unit circle.

<a id="geometry"></a>
## Distances of the trace conjugates to the endpoints

Write \(D=\deg A\) and \(\epsilon=A(0)\in\{-1,1\}\).
The identity \(X^DA(1/X)=\epsilon A(X)\) applied to the equations at \(\alpha_j\) and \(\alpha_j^{-1}\) gives \(\alpha_j^{D-2k}=\epsilon\), as in the [inverse-pair test](Yin_C_G0345_v101.md#inverse).
Since \(\alpha_j\) is nontorsion, \(D=2k\) and \(\epsilon=1\).
The symmetric Laurent polynomial \(X^{-k}A(X)\) is a monic polynomial \(C(X+X^{-1})\) of degree \(k\) in \(X+X^{-1}\), by the recurrence for \(X^j+X^{-j}\).
Each zero \(c\) of \(C\) lies in \([-2,2]\), since any solution of \(X+X^{-1}=c\) is a root of \(A\) and has modulus one.
The traces \(z_j=\alpha_j+\alpha_j^{-1}\) therefore satisfy \(C(z_j)=-a\).

Set \(y_j=\cosh t_j,\ x_j=\cos\theta_j\).
The identity \(z_j\pm2=(\alpha_j\pm1)^2/\alpha_j\) gives \(|z_j\pm2|=2(y_j\pm x_j)\).
If \(y_i-y_j>|x_i-x_j|\), both endpoint distances for \(z_i\) strictly exceed the corresponding distances for \(z_j\).
For real \(c\), the difference \(|z_i-c|^2-|z_j-c|^2\) is affine in \(c\), so it is strictly positive throughout \([-2,2]\).
Taking products over the \(k\ge1\) roots of \(C\) yields \(|C(z_i)|>|C(z_j)|\), contradicting their common value.
Interchanging \(i,j\) gives (1).
This argument permits any scalar sign for the parent after normalization and uses only that all parent roots lie on the unit circle and the polynomial is real.
In particular a single neutral conjugate forces \(\cosh T-1\le2\), hence \(T\le\operatorname{arcosh}3\).

<a id="phases"></a>
## A lower count on short arcs

We prove the angular estimate for any monic irreducible integer polynomial with constant coefficient of absolute value one.
Let \(\beta_j=\exp(-|t_j|+i\theta_j)\), and put \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\).
For \(i\ne j\),
\[
      |\alpha_i-\alpha_j|
         \le e^{t_i^++t_j^+}|1-\overline{\beta_i}\beta_j|,
             \qquad t^+=\max(t,0).
\]
For two interior or two exterior points this follows from
\(|v-w|\le|1-\bar v w|\) on the closed disk, with inversion and conjugation in the exterior case.
For one interior and one exterior point the displayed inequality is an equality.
Also \(|1-z|\le\rho^{-1}|1-\rho^2 z|\) for \(|z|\le1\), by squaring.
The nonzero integer discriminant gives \(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|\alpha_i-\alpha_j|\ge0\).
Expanding the logarithm and including the diagonal terms yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2\ell}}{\ell}
                \left|\sum_j\beta_j^\ell\right|^2
 &= -\sum_{i,j}\log|1-\rho^2\overline{\beta_i}\beta_j|\\
 &\le 2(d-1)m+d(d-1)\log(1/\rho)+d\log\frac1{1-\rho^2}
 \ll d\bigl(m+\log(2d)\bigr).
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{3}
\]
Since \(\sum_j|t_j|=2m\), replacing \(\sum_j\beta_j^\ell\) by \(\sum_j e^{i\ell\theta_j}\) has error at most \(2\ell m\).

For any \(0<h\le1\) use the periodic function \(\psi_h(\theta)=(1-|\theta|/h)_+\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\).
Its mean is \(h/(2\pi)\), and direct integration gives \(\widehat\psi_h(\ell)=(1-\cos(\ell h))/(\pi h\ell^2)\) for \(\ell\ne0\).
Thus \(|\widehat\psi_h(\ell)|\ll\min(h,1/(h\ell^2))\) and \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell|\widehat\psi_h(\ell)|^2\ll1\), by splitting at \(1/h\).
For \(\ell\le d\), Cauchy–Schwarz and (3), with \(\rho^{2\ell}\ge e^{-2}\), control the sum of \(|\widehat\psi_h(\ell)|\,|\sum_j\beta_j^\ell|/d\) by \(O(\sqrt{(m+\log(2d))/d})\).
The cost to replace the reflected powers by the phases is \(O(m\log(2d)/(dh))\) because \(\ell|\widehat\psi_h(\ell)|\ll1/(h\ell)\).
The Fourier tail past \(d\), using the trivial bound for the normalized phase sum, costs \(O(1/(dh))\).
For every center \(\theta_0\) these estimates give
\[
 \left|\frac1d\sum_j\psi_h(\theta_j-\theta_0)-\frac{h}{2\pi}\right|
 \ll \sqrt{\frac{m+\log(2d)}d}+\frac{1+m\log(2d)}{dh}.              \tag{4}
\]
Consequently every arc of half-width \(h\le1\) with \(h\ge C\sqrt{(1+m)\log(2d)/d}\) contains at least \(c d h\) conjugate phases, for absolute constants \(C,c>0\).
The constants and the center are independent of the polynomial and its signature.

<a id="count"></a>
## Exterior conjugates near a root of maximal modulus

Take a conjugate with \(|t_i|=T\), and put \(h=\min(1,T^2/4)\).
For every phase within angular distance \(h\) of \(\theta_i\), (1) and the Lipschitz bound for cosine imply \(|t_j|\ge T/2\), since \(\cosh T-\cosh(T/2)\ge3T^2/8\).
Let \(q=((1+m)\log(2d)/d)^{1/4}\).
If \(h\ge Cq^2\), (4) and \(\sum_j|t_j|=2m\) give \(m\gg d h T\).

First suppose there is a neutral root.
Here \(T\le\operatorname{arcosh}3<2\), so \(h=T^2/4\), and \(m/d\) is absolutely bounded.
If \(h<Cq^2\), then \(T\ll q\) directly.
In the other case \(T\ll(m/d)^{1/3}\ll q\), where the last comparison uses bounded \(m/d\) and \(\log(2d)\ge1\).
Thus \(m\le rT\) implies
\[
      r\gg \frac{m}{(1+m)^{1/4}}
                   \left(\frac d{\log(2d)}\right)^{1/4}.             \tag{5}
\]
Dobrowolski's [lower bound for the logarithmic Mahler measure](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\).
Since \(x/(1+x)^{1/4}\) increases on \(x>0\), (5) gives (2).
If there is no neutral root, reciprocity gives \(r=d/2\), so (2) holds in this case as well.

For the bounded-measure house estimate when no neutral root exists, use \(T\le m\le B\) by reciprocity.
For large \(d\), \(h=1\) would imply \(h\ge Cq^2\), and hence \(m\gg dT\) with \(T\ge2\), contrary to \(m\le B\).
Thus \(h=T^2/4\) for large \(d\) and the same two cases again give \(T\ll_B(\log(2d)/d)^{1/4}\).
For the finitely many smaller degrees the estimate follows by enlarging the constant using \(T\le B\).

<a id="salem"></a>
## Finiteness for bounded exterior count and a Salem family outside the construction

Fix \(R\).
Estimate (2) bounds the degree of every represented polynomial with \(r\le R\).
If at least one root is neutral, every root has modulus at most \(\exp(\operatorname{arcosh}3)\), so the elementary symmetric functions bound all integer coefficients in those degrees.
This proves finiteness, and it applies to \(r=1\) for every Salem number.
The [Salem family from the cubic Pisot seed](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#argument) gives distinct Salem numbers below \(4/3\) with degrees tending to infinity.
For every sufficiently large index, the corresponding minimal polynomial divides no monomial shift of a cyclotomic product, although it has a six-term multiple of the same Mahler measure.
A hypothetical sequence of Salem numbers decreasing to one must likewise eventually lie outside this parent family.
The obstruction concerns representation of the original irreducible factor and imposes no lower bound on the Mahler measures of the excluded inputs.

For a finite-degree example that does admit a parent, \(\Phi_{10}(X)-2X^2=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1\) is a Salem minimal polynomial.
Its trace polynomial \(z^2-z-3\) has one root above 2 and the other in \((-2,2)\).
The element \(z^2-4\) is negative at the latter real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})\), hence it is not a square in the trace field.
The quadratic \(X^2-zX+1\) therefore doubles the trace degree, proving irreducibility and the required signature.
