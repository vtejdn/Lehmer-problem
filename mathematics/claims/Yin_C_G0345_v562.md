# Finite prime support of an exponent in a reciprocal tetranomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## A gap for every factor

Fix coprime nonzero integers \(A,B\), a prime \(p\mid AB\), a finite set of primes \(Q\), and an integer \(H\ge1\).
For \(D>2k>0\), \(\gcd(D,k)=1\), and \(\sigma=\pm1\), put
\[
 F=A(X^D+\sigma)+B(X^{D-k}+\sigma X^k),\qquad
 T=\begin{cases}D & p\mid B,\\ D-2k & p\mid A.\end{cases}
\]
If the largest divisor of \(T\) prime to all primes in \(Q\cup\{p\}\) is at most \(H\), every integral divisor of \(F\) with positive logarithmic Mahler measure has measure at least \(c(A,B,p,Q,H)>0\) on that logarithmic scale.
This includes arbitrary multiplicities of all the indicated primes, both reciprocal signs and arbitrarily many maximal residue orders under divisibility.
The same bound up to a constant depending only on \(A,B\) holds for divisors of \(F(X^g)\) for every integer \(g\ge1\).

<a id="curve"></a>
## Small points on a fixed cover

Suppose a sequence of such parents has positive-measure integral divisors with log measure tending to zero.
Pass to irreducible positive-measure factors \(S\) with \(m=m(S)\to0\), which eventually have leading and constant coefficients of absolute value one by the root formula.
Normalize \(S\) to be monic, write \(d=\deg S\), and take a root \(x\).
Then \(h(x)=m/d\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height, and \(x\) is a nontorsion unit.
The [four-term width estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) give \(1\ll Dm/d\ll_{A,B}1\), whence \(d/D\to0\).
The [separated monomial estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) applied at the leading term gives \(k/D\to0\), since its gap to the rest is \(k\).
Thus \(T/D\to1\), and \(y=x^k\) has positive height \(k m/d\to0\).

Pass to a subsequence with fixed sign and fixed squarefree integer \(b=\prod_{q\in Q,\ q\ne p,\ q\mid T}q\), allowing \(b=1\).
Set \(z=x^{D-k}\).
The parent equation gives \(z=f(y)=-\sigma(A+By)/(Ay+B)\).
The numerator and denominator have distinct nonzero roots since \(p\mid AB\) and \(\gcd(A,B)=1\) imply \(|A|\ne|B|\).
The denominator cannot vanish at a root of the parent relation.
Define
\[
 G(Y)=\begin{cases}Y f(Y)&p\mid B,\\ f(Y)/Y&p\mid A.\end{cases}
\]
Then \(G(y)=x^T\), so \((y,u)=(x^k,x^{T/b})\) lies on \(U^b=G(Y)\).
Take its smooth projective normalization \(C\) with projection \(\pi\) to the \(Y\)-line.
The numerator zero \(-A/B\) gives a simple zero of \(G\) even over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
Eisenstein at this zero gives geometric irreducibility and \(\deg\pi=b\).
The indicated points lift uniquely to \(C\) because their coordinates are finite, the denominator is nonzero and \(u\ne0\).
Any fixed finite set of points occurs only finitely often since the positive heights of their \(y\)-coordinates tend to zero along the sequence.
The points are therefore generic on the curve.

<a id="branches"></a>
## A residue order realizing all selected primes

Equip \(\pi^*\mathcal O(1)\) with the pullback of the standard Weil-height metric.
This is ample and semipositive, and the normalized height of the curve is zero by the arithmetic projection formula and the zero normalized height of the standard projective line.
The point heights are \(h(y)\to0\).
By [Yuan, Theorem 3.1](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md), their conjugate measures at \(p\) converge to \(b^{-1}\pi^*\delta_\xi\), where \(\xi\) is the Gauss point and pullback counts local degrees.

At the Gauss valuation of \(\mathbb C_p(Y)\), the function \(G\) has norm one and residue \(-\sigma\).
Indeed for \(p\mid B\) the residue of \(f\) is \(-\sigma/Y\), and for \(p\mid A\) it is \(-\sigma Y\).
Since \(p\nmid b\), the reduced polynomial \(U^b+\sigma\) has \(b\) simple constant roots in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
Each lifts by Hensel's lemma in the completed Gauss field, giving \(b\) points above \(\xi\), each of local degree one.
Among the residue roots choose \(c\) whose multiplicative order is divisible by every prime dividing \(b\).
For \(-\sigma=1\) in the residue field take a primitive \(b\)-th root, and for \(-\sigma=-1\ne1\) take a primitive \(2b\)-th root.
The open set \(|U-\tilde c|_p<1\), for any integral lift \(\tilde c\) of \(c\), contains its branch point and has limiting measure \(1/b\).
Weak convergence gives at least one conjugate in this set at each sufficiently late index.
The embedding giving this conjugate extends to \(\mathbb Q(x)\), so \(S\) has a residue root of order \(s\) satisfying \(\bar x^{T/b}=c\) at that embedding.
For every \(q\mid b\) this implies \(v_q(s)=v_q(T/b)+v_q(\operatorname{ord}(c))\ge v_q(T)\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree of the factor realizing that order

Put \(N=p^{v_p(T)}\) and \(w=v_p(AB)\).
The degree \(d\) tends to infinity by Northcott's theorem, since a bounded-degree subsequence with bounded heights would have only finitely many possible roots.
For \(d>2\), the [residue degree and ramification estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#degree) gives \(d\gg N\operatorname{ord}_s(p)/(wp^w)\), with \(\operatorname{ord}_1(p)=1\).
The [prime-power order estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#orders) give
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_s(p)\gg_{p,Q}\prod_{q\mid b}q^{v_q(s)}
 \ \ge\ \prod_{q\mid b}q^{v_q(T)}.
\]
The left bound uses the product of prime powers at distinct primes dividing the single Frobenius degree, including the binary estimate when \(q=2\).
The hypothesis now gives \(d\gg_{A,B,p,Q} T/H\), contradicting \(d/D\to0\) and \(T/D\to1\).
Finally, the [bounded radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) compares the log measure of each nontorsion root after dilation with that of its \(g\)-th power at cost \(R_{2|A|+2|B|}\).
Integer contents of modulus at least two already have log measure at least \(\log 2\).
