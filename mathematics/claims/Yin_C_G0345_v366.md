# The resultant of two rows is bounded by the integral of their maximum

<a id="statement"></a>
## A resultant bound without a coefficient-height loss

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb C[X]\) be nonzero and coprime, and put \(N=\deg U+\deg V>0\).
For normalized Haar measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb T\), one has
\[
       \log|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|
          \ \le\ N\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max\{|U(z)|,|V(z)|\}\,d\mu(z).     \tag{1}
\]
The coefficient \(N\) is exact under simultaneous scaling of \(U,V\).
The bound also admits equality with nonconstant boundary data in one row, as in the example below.

For the arithmetic consequence, let \(p>4\) be prime and let the primitive integer polynomial
\[
             F(x)=x^rU(x^p)+x^sV(x^p),\qquad 0\le r<s<p,
\]
have two nonzero integral rows coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Use \(U_0,V_0\) for the rows after removing their respective powers of \(X\), and now take \(N=\deg U_0+\deg V_0\), \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|\).
Suppose the monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) divides \(F\) with multiplicity at least \(e\ge1\), and that \(\alpha^p\) has the same degree \(d\) as \(\alpha\).
Let \(h=m(P)\), where \(m=\log M\), and let \(R\) be the product with multiplicities of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P^e\) other than \(x\).
Write \(D\) for the difference between the largest and smallest exponents of \(F\), and \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(z)\).
For every lower bound \(B\le\log H\) one has
\[
           m(R)\ \ge\ (1-4/p)\frac B N-e h-\frac2p\log(CD).              \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(B\) may include all of the prime-to-\(p\) collision weights and, when \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\), an additional contribution at \(p\) as specified below.
There is no additive term proportional to the number of coefficients in the two rows.

<a id="factor"></a>
## A polynomial factor with prescribed boundary modulus

Fix a positive integer \(k\).
The boundary factor used here is the classical [scalar Fejér–Riesz factor](../audits/literature/DR10/Yin_U001.md#dr10-u001), also used in the [positive Gram construction](Yin_C_G0113_v1.md#changing-integer-input-support-discriminator).
The root-pair argument below gives the factor in the strictly positive case needed for the Sylvester estimate.
For \(A\in\mathbb C[X]\) write \(A^\#(z)=\overline{A(1/\bar z)}\), a Laurent polynomial.
Set \(S=(UU^\#)^k+(VV^\#)^k\).
On the circle \(S=|U|^{2k}+|V|^{2k}>0\), since the two polynomials have no common zero.
If \(S\) is constant take \(W=\sqrt S\).
Otherwise \(S^\#=S\), so its extreme Laurent exponents are \(-q,q\) for some \(q>0\).
The polynomial \(z^q S(z)\) has degree \(2q\), has nonzero constant term and has no unit-circle root.
Its zeros occur in pairs \(\rho,1/\bar\rho\) with the same multiplicity.
Let \(W_0\) be the monic polynomial whose zeros are the \(q\) exterior zeros counted with multiplicities.
The quotient \(S/(W_0W_0^\#)\) is constant because numerator and denominator have the same zeros and the same pole order at zero.
Its value on the circle is positive.
Multiplying \(W_0\) by the positive square root of this constant produces a polynomial \(W\) with
\[
                |W|^2=|U|^{2k}+|V|^{2k}\quad\text{on }\mathbb T .
\]
In either case \(W\) has no zero on a disk of radius slightly larger than one, so it has an analytic \(k\)-th root \(w\) there.
On the unit circle both \(|U/w|\) and \(|V/w|\) are at most one.

<a id="sylvester"></a>
## Truncating the coefficients after division by the common factor

Write \(m_U=\deg U,\ m_V=\deg V\).
The \(N\)-by-\(N\) Sylvester matrix has as columns the coefficient vectors in ascending order of \(X^jU\) for \(0\le j<m_V\) and \(X^j V\) for \(0\le j<m_U\).
All have degree below \(N\), and the determinant has modulus \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\).
On the space of Taylor polynomials modulo \(X^N\), multiplication by \(1/w\) is lower triangular with constant diagonal \(1/w(0)\).
It sends each column to the first \(N\) Taylor coefficients of the corresponding shifted \(U/w\) or \(V/w\).
By Parseval each resulting column has Euclidean norm at most one, since the entire squared coefficient norm is its squared boundary \(L^2(\mu)\) norm.
Hadamard's inequality therefore gives
\[
                     |\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\le |w(0)|^N.
\]
The harmonic mean value property for \(\log|w|\) gives
\[
 \log|w(0)|=\frac1{2k}\int_{\mathbb T}\log(|U|^{2k}+|V|^{2k})\,d\mu
    \le \int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\,d\mu+\frac{\log 2}{2k}.
\]
Letting \(k\to\infty\) proves (1), including when one row is constant.

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Prime contributions to the cofactor bound

The integral \(I=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\,d\mu\) is unchanged by the removal of a monomial from either row.
One has \(N>0\), since otherwise a two-term \(F\) vanishing at \(\alpha\) would put a positive power of this unit in \(\mathbb Q\), forcing it to be \(\pm1\).
Equation (1) gives \(I\ge\log H/N\).
Removing \(x^r\) from \(F\) gives offset \(s-r\) with the same exponent span and measure.
The [comparison with the row integral](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) then yields \(m(F)\ge(1-4/p)I-(2/p)\log(CD)\).
Primitivity gives \(m(F)=e h+m(R)\), proving (2).
If \(|U|\) and \(|V|\) are not identically equal on the unit circle, the same comparison gives \(m(R)\ge(1-2/p)B/N-e h\), with no degree error.
For the nonnegative arithmetic choices of \(B\) below this improves (2).

For each \(\ell\ne p\), fix a splitting extension at \(\ell\), use \(v(\ell)=1\), and write \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) for the powered conjugates.
Over each residue \(b\) of the \(\beta_i\), group the indices by the residue of \(\alpha_i\).
Let \(w_{\ell,b}\) be the maximum of \(\sum_{i\in A,j\in A'}v(\beta_i-\beta_j)\) over distinct such groups \(A,A'\), with value zero when fewer than two groups occur.
The [collision divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#branches) gives the permissible choice
\[
                   B_0=e^2\sum_{\ell\ne p}(\log\ell)\sum_b w_{\ell,b}.
\]
This sum is finite because \(\log H\) bounds it and each contributing prime divides \(H\).
Put \(u=\min\{p,\lfloor e/2\rfloor\}\).
If \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\) and \(u\ge1\), the [two-row resultant at the support prime](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v152.md#resultant) gives \(v_p(H)\ge d u^2\), since \(2u\le e\) and \(u\le p\).
The places are disjoint, so one may take \(B=B_0+d u^2\log p\) in that case and \(B=B_0\) otherwise.
A witness having common scalar content is first divided by that content for this application, which preserves \(R\) and the \(P^e\) divisor.

For a sharp single-root test, take the [irreducible example](Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#index) \(P=x^p-ax-1\), \(a=\ell^t\ge2,\ \ell\ne p,\ a\equiv1\bmod p\), with \(F=P\).
Then \(U=X-1,\ V=-a,\ N=1,\ H=a\).
Everywhere on \(\mathbb T\) the maximum of their moduli is \(a\), hence \(N I=\log H\) exactly.
The extra factor is \(R=1\).
This example attains (1) while the resultant cost is carried by the input polynomial itself.

<a id="gap"></a>
## The remaining arithmetic condition for a gap

For a large degree \(d\), take one of the degree-preserving primes \(p\) in the [two-class comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), with \(p\asymp(\log(3d))^4\).
For any two prescribed classes that result supplies a primitive witness with \(P^2\mid F\), \(D\le p(16d+1)\) and \(m(R)\le2p h\).
Suppose there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that for each sufficiently large degree input of sufficiently small positive \(h\), one can choose such a prime and two classes for which every admissible primitive witness satisfies \(B/N\ge cp\), using the arithmetic bound above.
On the witness of small extra measure, (2) then gives
\[
          (2p+2)h\ge (1-4/p)cp-(2/p)\log(CD).
\]
Since \(\log(CD)=O(\log(3d))\), the right side divided by \(2p+2\) tends from below to \(c/2\) uniformly as \(d\to\infty\).
Thus the condition would imply a uniform positive lower bound on \(h\).
Bounded degrees have a positive gap by coefficient finiteness.
For a general integer polynomial remove monomials and factor the rest into content and primitive irreducibles.
Content of absolute value at least two, or a primitive irreducible factor with nonzero constant or leading coefficient of absolute value at least two, gives multiplicative measure at least two.

Here \(B\) depends only on the input, prime and multiplicity.
For two nonzero prescribed classes the degree cap bounds each original row degree by \(16d\), so the simpler arithmetic condition \(B\ge32c p d\) would suffice.
If the class zero is prescribed, the cap allows its row degree to reach \(16d+1\), although the [low-cost box construction](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#finite) itself uses only row degrees at most \(16d\).
The resultant inequality gives no uniform supply of this arithmetic size on small inputs.
