# Squarefree two-pole sections at primes dividing the horizontal exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniformity in the exponents

For positive coprime integers \(k,l\), write \(P_{k,l}(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) for the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement).
There is a set of rational primes of natural density \(5/32\) such that, at each of its primes \(p\), every \(P_{p^a,l}\) with \(a\ge1\) and \(p\nmid l\) is squarefree modulo \(p\).
At \(p=19\), a multiple root for \(19\mid k\) requires both \(90\mid k\) and \(181\mid l\), so all pure powers of 19 have the same squarefreeness property.
For arbitrary odd primes, let the signed weight of \(l\) be the least number of summands in a representation \(l=\sum_j\epsilon_j p^{e_j}\), with \(\epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}\) and \(e_j\ge0\).
For each integer \(s\ge1\), there is a finite exceptional set \(S_s\) with \(\sum_{p\in S_s}\log p=O((s+1)^3)\) such that at every odd \(p\notin S_s\) the reductions of all \(P_{p^a,l}\) with \(a\ge1\), \(p\nmid l\) and signed weight at most \(s\) are squarefree.
In particular if \(s=o(Y^{1/3})\), the proportion of primes in \([Y,2Y]\) satisfying this last simultaneous squarefreeness property tends to one.

<a id="collision"></a>
## The eight possible horizontal coordinates

Write \(y=x^k\), \(v=x^l\), \(t=y-y^{-1}\), \(b_-(t)=(t-3)(t-4)\) and \(b_+(t)=(t+3)(t+4)\).
No root is zero because \(P_{k,l}(0)=1\).
The section equation divided by \(y^2\) is \(b_-v^2+v+b_+=0\).
When \(p\mid k\) and \(p\nmid l\), its logarithmic derivative with respect to \(x\) is \(lv(2b_-v+1)\).
Thus in odd characteristic a multiple root forces
\[
  4(t^2-9)(t^2-16)=1,\qquad v=-1/(2b_-(t)),\qquad v^k=y^l.            \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently the first equation is \(D(y)=0\), where \(D(Y)=Y^4-4A(Y)A^*(Y)\) in the notation of the two-pole section.
Over the reals put
\[
 a_1=(25+5\sqrt2)/2,\quad a_2=(25-5\sqrt2)/2,\quad d_i=a_i+4,
 \quad \alpha_i=(\sqrt{a_i}+\sqrt{d_i})/2.
\]
The first equation gives \(t=\pm\sqrt{a_i}\), and \(y^2-ty-1=0\) gives the eight distinct values \(\pm\alpha_i^{\pm1}\), where both \(\alpha_i>1\).
For each associated real \(v\), one has \(v^4\ne1\).
Indeed \(v=\pm1\) would give \(b_-^2=1/4=b_-b_+\), hence \(14t=b_+-b_-=0\), which contradicts (1).

<a id="density"></a>
## Frobenius orders in the splitting field

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) and \(L=K(\sqrt{a_1},\sqrt{a_2},\sqrt{d_1},\sqrt{d_2})\).
The products of the conjugate radicands are \(575/4=5^2\cdot23/4\) and \(1039/4\), respectively, with 1039 prime by trial division through 31.
Both 23 and 1039 are 7 modulo 8 and hence split in \(K\).
At 23 the valuations of \(a_1\) above 23 are nonnegative and sum to one, and conjugation interchanges them for \(a_2\).
Both \(d_i\) are units at these primes by their norm.
The same argument at 1039 interchanges the roles of the two pairs.
These four valuations separate the four square classes in \(K^\times/K^{\times2}\), giving four independent sign changes and \([L:K]=16\) by the multiquadratic degree formula.
Conjugation on \(K\) lifts to an involution exchanging both pairs of square roots with positive signs, since the four quadratic relations present the degree-16 algebra over \(K\).
Thus \(L/\mathbb Q\) is Galois with group \(G=(C_2)^4\rtimes C_2\) of order 32.
It is the splitting field of \(D\), since the roots recover \(t=y-y^{-1}\), \(y+y^{-1}\) and \(\sqrt2=(2a_1-25)/5\).

Let \(\tau\) fix both \(\sqrt{a_i}\) and negate both \(\sqrt{d_i}\).
It fixes every \(t,v\), sends each \(y\) to \(-1/y\), and is central.
In the sign subgroup only \(\tau\) generates a subgroup containing \(\tau\).
Outside it, the square of an element changes both signs of each pair by the product of that pair's signs.
The element has order at most four and its cyclic subgroup contains \(\tau\) exactly when its square is \(\tau\), requiring equal signs on the first pair and opposite signs on the second.
This gives four further elements, so Chebotarev's density theorem for this conjugacy-stable set gives natural density \(5/32\).
Discard the finite primes of ramification, bad denominators or coincidences among the eight roots, including 2 and the divisors of the leading coefficient of \(D\).
For a remaining prime in the set, choose a prime of \(L\) above it and let \(g\) be its arithmetic Frobenius.
Every pair in (1) is the reduction of a characteristic-zero pair there, and \(g^m=\tau\) with \(m=1\) or 2.
Hence \(y^{p^m+1}=-1\) and \(v^{p^m-1}=1\) in the residue field.
Put \(h=\operatorname{ord}(v)\) and \(j=\operatorname{ord}(y)\).
Their common divisor divides \(\gcd(p^m-1,2(p^m+1))\mid4\).
Equation (1) gives \(h/\gcd(h,k)=\operatorname{ord}(v^k)\mid\gcd(h,j)\), so in particular \(h\le4\gcd(k,p^m-1)\).
For \(k=p^a\) it forces \(h\mid4\).
The nonzero numbers \(v^4-1\) have only finitely many prime divisors after denominators are cleared, so deleting these primes proves the first assertion with the stated density.

<a id="nineteen"></a>
## The order obstruction at 19

Modulo 19, \(D\) factors as \(-4 f_4 f_{-4}\), where \(f_c=Y^4+cY^3+3Y^2-cY+1\).
Indeed \(f_c/Y^2=t^2+ct+5\).
Both quadratics in \(t\) are irreducible since their discriminant is \(-4\), and the norm of \(t^2+4=-ct-1\) from the quadratic field is \(65\equiv8\), a nonsquare modulo 19.
Thus \(t^2+4\) is nonsquare there and each \(f_c\) is irreducible.
The following residues are in \(\mathbb F_{19}[Y]/(f_c)\) with \(y=Y\).
They can be obtained by substituting \(t=Y-Y^{-1}\), using \(v=-2b_+(t)\) from (1), and repeatedly squaring with \(Y^4=-cY^3-3Y^2+cY-1\).

| Power | \(c=4\) | \(c=-4\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(y^{724},y^{362}\) | \(1,-1\) | \(1,-1\) |
| \(y^4\) | \(-4Y^3-3Y^2+4Y-1\) | \(4Y^3-3Y^2-4Y-1\) |
| \(v^{360},v^{180},v^{120}\) | \(1,-1,7\) | \(1,-1,-8\) |
| \(v^{72}\) | \(3Y^3-7Y^2-7Y+1\) | \(3Y^3+7Y^2-7Y-6\) |

Since \(724=4\cdot181\) and \(360=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\), the table checks all prime divisors of both proposed orders, giving \(j=724\) and \(h=360\).
The common power in (1) has order dividing \(\gcd(724,360)=4\).
Raising to the fourth power yields \(360\mid4k\) and \(724\mid4l\), as claimed.

<a id="weight"></a>
## Signed digits and the exceptional norm product

Outside the fixed finite bad-reduction set above, fix a prime of \(L\) over \(p\) and write \(g\) for its Frobenius, now arbitrary in \(G\).
On its residue field the unique \(p^a\)-th root of \(y\) is the reduction of \(g^{-a}(y)\), lifting \(y\) to one of the eight roots.
If \(l=\sum_j\epsilon_jp^{e_j}\) has at most \(s\) summands, then \(x^l\) is the reduction of \(\prod_j g^{e_j-a}(y)^{\epsilon_j}\).
Every factor belongs to \(\{\pm\alpha_1^{\pm1},\pm\alpha_2^{\pm1}\}\).
Thus a collision makes some difference \(v-\delta\alpha_1^A\alpha_2^B\) vanish at the chosen prime, with \(\delta=\pm1\) and \(|A|+|B|\le s\).
Every such difference is nonzero in \(L\), since equality and its image under \(\tau\) would give \(v^2=(-1)^{A+B}\), contrary to \(v^4\ne1\).

Choose a fixed positive integer \(H\) clearing the denominators of all four \(v\)'s and of \(\alpha_i^{\pm1}\), and include its prime divisors in the fixed exceptional set.
Multiplication by \(H^{s+1}\) turns each difference into a nonzero algebraic integer.
At every embedding its absolute value is at most \(C^{s+1}\) for a fixed \(C>1\), by the finite list of possible factors and embeddings.
The logarithm of the absolute norm is therefore \(O(s+1)\).
There are \(O((s+1)^2)\) choices of \((A,B,\delta,v)\), so the product of the absolute norms has logarithm \(O((s+1)^3)\).
Including its prime divisors gives the required \(S_s\), independent of the exponent positions and of \(a\).
At most \(O((s+1)^3/\log Y)\) exceptional primes lie in \([Y,2Y]\), whereas the prime number theorem gives \((1+o(1))Y/\log Y\) primes there.
