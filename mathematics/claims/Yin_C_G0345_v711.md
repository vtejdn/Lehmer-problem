# Height bounds for mixed radical indices from reciprocal orders in finite fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which parts of a mixed exponent can reduce the Mahler measure

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers, let \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\) and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
Suppose the positive real radical \(\gamma=r^{1/q}\) belongs to \(K\) and let \(\ell^e\parallel q\) with \(\ell\) prime and \(e\ge1\).
Assume \(\ell\le n\), or more generally that \(a\bmod\ell\) has a representative \(c\in\{0,1,-1\}\).
Define
\[
 t=q/\ell^e,\qquad
 D=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le d/2}\{\ell^j-1,\ell^j+1\},
 \qquad h=\gcd(t,D).
\]
Write \(c_0=0.427800\), \(c_3=\log(\sqrt{10}/2)\), and \(\delta=c_3/3\).
Then \(\gamma\) is Salem, and
\[
 h\log M(\gamma)=h\log\gamma\ \ge\
 \begin{cases}
       c_0/2, &\ell=2,\\
       c_3/3, &\ell=3,\\
       \displaystyle\frac{d}{n+1}
               \max\!\left\{c_0,\log\frac{\ell}{2(1+|c|)}\right\},
            &\ell\ge5 .
 \end{cases}                                                           \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(h\log\gamma\ge\delta\) and \(\ell^e(t/h)\le\log(r)/\delta\).
The constant \(c_0\) uses the accepted published numerical evaluation in the [scalar height estimates](Yin_C_G0246_v1.md#gim%2B10-arbitrary-prime-scalar-transfer).

Along any sequence with \(\log\gamma\to0\), the integers \(\gcd(t,D)\) tend to infinity uniformly over the indicated prime divisors \(\ell\) on terms where they exist.
For any prime \(p\ne\ell\), put \(k=\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\), with \(k=1\) at \(p=2\).
The primes in \(t\) that can contribute to \(h\) are precisely its primes for which either \(k\) is even and \(k\le d\), or \(k\) is odd and \(k\le d/2\).
The contribution of such a prime is capped at \(p^{v_p(D)}\), regardless of \(v_p(t)\).
In particular, for \(q=\ell^e p^f\) with \(\ell\le n<p\) and \(p\) outside those two order ranges, (1) holds with \(h=1\) for every \(e,f\ge1\).
The [prime-power theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives the earlier lower bound with multiplier \(t\) for a general complementary index.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## Cyclotomic specialization at a prime divisor of the index

Let \(U\) be the product of \(X-v\) over those \(v=\pm1\) with \(v^{n-1}=\epsilon\).
The [root classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(P_a=G_a/U\) as the minimal polynomial of \(r\), with \(n-1\le d\le n+1\).
For each positive divisor \(b\) of \(q\), the power \(x=\gamma^b\) satisfies \(x^{q/b}=r\), so \(\mathbb Q(r)\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\subseteq K\).
Consequently \(z\mapsto z^{q/b}\) takes the conjugates of \(x\) bijectively to the conjugates of \(r\).
Each \(x\) is an algebraic unit with one exterior conjugate, and the inverse of a neutral conjugate is its complex conjugate, forcing reciprocity of its minimal polynomial as in the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Thus each is Salem of degree \(d\), which is even.

For \(\ell=2,3\) the three residues \(0,\pm1\) suffice automatically.
For odd \(\ell<n\) the [delayed trace congruences](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#traces) for \(r^{1/\ell^e}\in K\) imply \(\ell^{e+1}\mid(a-1)a(a+1)\).
For prime \(\ell=n\) the [diagonal congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#diagonal) gives \(\ell\mid a\).
Consequently a representative \(c\) as in the statement exists for every \(\ell\le n\).

Write \(S_x\) for the minimal polynomial of \(x\), and put \(\beta=\gamma^t=r^{1/\ell^e}\).
The [full prime-power reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius) gives \(S_\beta\equiv P_a\equiv P_c\bmod\ell\), where \(P_c=G_c/U\) is integral.
The last congruence also follows directly by division by the fixed monic \(U\).
The specialization \(P_c\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials with multiplicities, since \(G_0=X^{n+1}-\epsilon\) and \(G_c=(X-c)(X^n+\epsilon c)\) for \(c=\pm1\).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Removing the invertible part of the complementary exponent

Every root \(z\) of \(\overline S_\gamma\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\) is nonzero because \(\gamma\) is a unit.
Its Frobenius orbit and the orbit of \(z^{-1}\) have the same multiplicity by reciprocity.
If the two orbits of length \(j\) are distinct, \(2j\le d\) and \(z^{\ell^j-1}=1\).
If they coincide and \(z\ne z^{-1}\), inversion is a nontrivial element of order two on the orbit and equals the half-turn of its cyclic Frobenius action.
The orbit thus has even length \(2j\le d\), and \(z^{\ell^j+1}=1\).
Finally \(z=z^{-1}\) has order dividing two, which divides \(D\) at odd characteristic, while such \(z\) has order one at characteristic two.
All roots therefore lie in \(\mu_D\), even for a reduction with repeated roots.
This argument uses the same [reciprocal-orbit distinction](Yin_C_G0345_v709.md#reciprocity) for each distinct root.

Put \(\xi=\gamma^h,\ u=t/h,\ N=D/h\).
Then \(\gcd(u,N)=1\), and all roots of \(\overline S_\xi\) are \(h\)-th powers of roots of \(\overline S_\gamma\), hence lie in \(\mu_N\).
Raising that multiset to the \(u\)-th power gives the multiset of \(\overline S_\beta=\overline P_c\), with every multiplicity retained.
In particular all roots of \(\overline P_c\) also have orders dividing \(N\).

For any \(m=\ell^v s\) with \(\ell\nmid s\), the reduction of \(\Phi_m\) contains all primitive \(s\)-th roots with common multiplicity \(\varphi(\ell^v)\).
For \(v=0\) this is ordinary separability, and for \(v\ge1\) it follows by reducing the identity \(\Phi_{s\ell^v}(X)\Phi_s(X^{\ell^{v-1}})=\Phi_s(X^{\ell^v})\).
Since \(P_c\) is a cyclotomic product, its reduced multiset has a common multiplicity on all primitive roots of each occurring order.
The \(u\)-th power map permutes \(\mu_N\) and preserves each primitive order, so it fixes that entire target multiset.
Its inverse on \(\mu_N\) now gives \(\overline S_\xi=\overline P_c\).
This is why the full power of a prime in \(t\) can be truncated at its exponent in \(D\).

For odd \(p\ne\ell\), divisibility by some \(\ell^j-1\) means \(k\mid j\), and divisibility by \(\ell^j+1\) means \(k\) is even and \(j\equiv k/2\bmod k\).
For \(p=2\ne\ell\), already \(2\mid\ell-1\).
These tests give exactly the two order ranges in the statement.

<a id="height"></a>
## Height after the reduced polynomial is recovered

The identity \(\overline S_\xi=\overline P_c\) gives \(P_c(\xi)\in\ell\mathcal O_K\).
For \(\ell=2,3\), apply the [scalar power argument](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#small) to this congruence.
Explicitly, for \(c=0\) take \(J=n+1\), while for \(c=\pm1\) with \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\) take \(J=n\), since \(U\) cancels the factor \(X-c\).
In both cases \(\xi^J\equiv\pm1\bmod\ell\mathcal O_K\).
For the remaining choice \(c^{n-1}=-\epsilon\), \(X-c\) divides \(X^n+\epsilon c\), so \(P_c\) divides \((X^n+\epsilon c)^2\).
Its reduction divides the \(\ell\)-th power of that binomial, yielding the same scalar congruence with \(J=n\ell\).
Here \(\deg U\le1\) because \(X-c\) is absent from \(U\), so \(d/J\ge1/\ell\).
For \(J=n+1\) or \(J=n\), one has \(d/J\ge(n-1)/(n+1)\ge3/4\).
The dyadic and ternary scalar estimates thus give the first two lines of (1), since the absolute logarithmic height of \(\xi^J\) is \(J\log\xi/d\).

For \(\ell\ge5\), \(G_c(\xi)\in\ell\mathcal O_K\) and this value is nonzero, because \(\xi>1\) and \(G_c\) has only roots of unity.
Put \(C=2(1+|c|)\le4\).
This bounds the coefficient sum of \(G_c\) in absolute values and hence its unit-circle maximum, strictly below \(\ell\).
The [reciprocal-polynomial height transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v697.md#statement) applies at the Salem unit \(\xi\), giving \((n+1)\log\xi/d\ge c_0\).
The absolute norm of \(G_c(\xi)\) is at least \(\ell^d\), while its conjugate evaluations give the upper bound \(C^d\xi^{n+1}\).
Combining these bounds with \(\log\xi=h\log\gamma\) gives the last line of (1).
Since \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\), all three lines are at least \(\delta\), as in the prime-power theorem.

<a id="local"></a>
## A smaller multiplier from the actual cyclotomic orders

For every order \(s\) occurring in \(\overline P_c\), let \(\mu_s\) be the common multiplicity of its primitive roots.
For \(m\) prime to \(\ell\), write \(L_m=|\langle\ell,-1\rangle|\) in \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\), with \(L_1=1\).
Form the least common multiple \(D_*\) of the following divisors \(m\) of \(D\).
Retain \(m\) when \(s=m/\gcd(m,t)\) occurs in \(\overline P_c\) and \(L_m/L_s\le\mu_s\).
Then (1) remains valid with the smaller multiplier \(h_*=\gcd(t,D_*)\).
This test involves finite integer arithmetic on the known parameters \(n,\epsilon,\ell,c,t\).

Indeed a primitive root of order \(m\) occurring in \(\overline S_\gamma\) belongs to a joint Frobenius and inversion orbit of size \(L_m\).
Raising the orbit to the \(t\)-th power maps it onto a target orbit of order-\(s\) roots with \(L_s\) elements.
Each image has exactly \(L_m/L_s\) preimages in this one orbit, by transitivity, so its multiplicity in the full powered multiset is at least that ratio.
This proves that every actual source order divides \(D_*\).
The preceding inversion argument now applies with \(D_*\) in place of \(D\), followed by the same height bound.
The criterion extends the [reciprocal orbit test for the first prime slice](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0182/exceptional-orders.md#orbits) to arbitrary complementary exponents and all the indicated delays.
