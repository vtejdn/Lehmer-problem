# Finite computation of the least energy of an integral Laurent quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum for a fixed hyperbolic polynomial

Let \(P(X)=\sum_{j=0}^d p_jX^j\) be a monic integer polynomial of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|p_0|=1\) and no zero on the unit circle.
Use normalized Haar measure on the circle, and for \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) put
\[
 e(A)=\|A/P\|_2^2,\qquad
 \mathcal E(P)=\inf_{P\nmid A} e(A),
\]
where divisibility is in the integral Laurent polynomial ring.
There is an explicit finite search, given below, which computes the real algebraic number \(\mathcal E(P)\) exactly and lists all minimizers up to multiplication by \(X^j\).
The minimum is isolated among the energies of admissible numerators.
This applies in particular to the minimal polynomial of any hyperbolic algebraic unit.

For every integer \(a\ge3\), the search value for \(Q_a(X)=X^2-aX+1\) is
\[
              \mathcal E(Q_a)
              =\frac{\min\{a,2a-4\}}{(a^2-4)^{3/2}}.
\]
Up to sign and translation, the unique minimizer is \(1-X\) for \(a=3\) and \(1\) for \(a\ge5\).
Both, and only these two, minimize at \(a=4\).
This extends the [monomial computation at \(a=8\)](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#substitution).
The [periodic Fourier limit](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#hyperbolic) maximizes a product of absolute cosines over homoclinic orbits.
Here the objective is the quadratic circle norm, and the search bounds depend on the individual \(P\).

<a id="finite-search"></a>
## A bound on the gaps between occupied exponents

Write \(M=\sum_j|p_j|\), \(D=\sum_j j|p_j|\), and let \(\rho\) run over the complex roots of \(P\) with multiplicity.
Define
\[
 \mu=\prod_\rho\bigl||\rho|-1\bigr|,\qquad V=2D/\mu^3,\qquad
 \delta=\frac1{2M^2},\qquad
 H=\left\lfloor M^2e(1)+\tfrac12\right\rfloor,\qquad
 G=\max\{1,\lceil M^2H^2V\rceil\}.
\]
Then \(\mu\le |P(e^{it})|\le M\) by the factorization and coefficient bounds, with \(\mu>0\).
In particular \(H\ge1\).
Take as the search set all \(A=\sum_{j=0}^{(H-1)G}a_jX^j\) with \(a_j\in\mathbb Z\), \(a_0\ne0\), \(\sum_j a_j^2\le H\), and \(P\nmid A\).
It is finite and contains \(1\).

For every numerator with \(e(A)\le e(1)+\delta\), Parseval and integrality give
\[
       \sum_j|a_j|\le \sum_j a_j^2
             \le M^2 e(A)\le M^2 e(1)+\tfrac12.
\]
Both sums are integers, hence at most \(H\).
For \(w(t)=|P(e^{it})|^{-2}\) we have \(|w'(t)|\le2D/\mu^3=V\), by differentiating and using \(|(d/dt)P(e^{it})|\le D\).
Integration by parts on one period gives \(|\widehat w(n)|\le V/|n|\) for \(n\ne0\), with the Fourier coefficients taken against normalized measure.

Suppose \(P\nmid A\) and consecutive occupied exponents differ by \(g>G\).
Write \(A=B+C\) by splitting its support at this gap, interchanging the two parts if necessary so that \(P\nmid B\).
Such a choice exists because the multiples of \(P\) are closed under addition.
For \(e(A)\le e(1)+\delta\), every pair of exponents from different parts differs by at least \(g\), so
\[
 \left|2\operatorname{Re}\int B\overline C\,w\right|
 \le \frac{2V}{g}\left(\sum_j|b_j|\right)\left(\sum_j|c_j|\right)
 \le \frac{VH^2}{2g}<\delta.
\]
The second inequality uses the disjoint coefficient supports and the bound on their combined absolute sum.
Since \(C\ne0\) is integral, \(e(C)\ge M^{-2}\sum_j c_j^2\ge 2\delta\).
Consequently \(e(A)>e(B)+\delta\).

Starting with any admissible \(A\) of energy at most \(e(1)\), replace it by the admissible part \(B\) whenever a gap exceeds \(G\).
Each replacement strictly decreases both the energy and the number of occupied exponents, so the process terminates.
The result has at most \(H\) occupied exponents, with consecutive differences at most \(G\), hence has width at most \((H-1)G\).
Multiplication by a power of \(X\) places it in the search set and preserves energy and admissibility.
Since the search set contains \(1\), numerators of energy greater than \(e(1)\) cannot improve its minimum.
This proves attainment and the finite search formula.

In fact, every admissible \(A\) of energy at most \(\mathcal E(P)+\delta\) already satisfies the coefficient bound since \(\mathcal E(P)\le e(1)\).
A gap exceeding \(G\) would give \(e(A)>e(B)+\delta\ge\mathcal E(P)+\delta\).
Thus this whole interval of energies is attained by translates of members of the finite search set, proving isolation and giving all minimizers.

<a id="algebraic"></a>
## Evaluation of the finite list

For a polynomial \(F\) of degree \(h\) write \(F^*(z)=z^h F(1/z)\).
For each normalized candidate of actual degree \(k\), and also for \(A=1\) when forming \(H\), the circle integral is
\[
 e(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=1}
              z^{d-k-1}\frac{A(z)A^*(z)}{P(z)P^*(z)}\,dz .
\]
This follows by substituting \(z=e^{it}\) and using the real coefficients.
The integrand is rational with no pole on the contour.
Cancel common factors and sum the residues at all its poles in the open disk, including zero if it is a pole.
For an integrand \(R(z)\) and a pole \(\beta\) of order \(h\), its residue is the derivative of order \(h-1\) of \((z-\beta)^h R(z)\) at \(\beta\), divided by \((h-1)!\).
This treats shared zeros of \(P\) and \(P^*\) and all other repeated poles.
The roots, their moduli, residues and real energy sums are algebraic, and isolation and exact arithmetic on algebraic numbers decide the disk tests, floors, ceilings, and comparisons between candidate energies, including ties.
Ordinary monic division decides divisibility of the normalized candidate, since \(X\) is invertible modulo \(P\) by \(|p_0|=1\).
These operations determine the finite list and its minimum using \(P\) alone.

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## The quadratic family

Fix \(a\ge3\), set \(s=\sqrt{a^2-4}\), \(r=(a-s)/2\), and use \(w_n\) for the \(n\)-th Fourier coefficient of \(|Q_a|^{-2}=(a-2\cos t)^{-2}\).
Here \(0<r<1\), \(a=r+r^{-1}\) and \(s=r^{-1}-r\).
The Fourier coefficient of \((a-2\cos t)^{-1}=r/|1-re^{it}|^2\) at \(n\ge0\) is \(r^n/s\), by the two geometric series.
Differentiating its negative with respect to \(a\), using \(r'=-r/s\) and \(s'=a/s\), gives
\[
                         w_{-n}=w_n=(a+ns)r^n/s^3 .
\]
The differentiation under the integral is valid locally for real \(a>2\) since the denominator is uniformly bounded away from zero.
For \(n\ge0\) we have
\[
 w_n-2w_{n+1}+w_{n+2}
   =\frac{r^n(1-r)}{s^3}\bigl[(a+ns)(1-r)-2sr\bigr]>0.
\]
The bracket increases with \(n\) and at zero equals \(a+r^2-3>0\).

Put \(v_\ell=w_{\ell-1}-2w_\ell+w_{\ell+1}\) for \(\ell\ge1\), and \(T_\ell(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{\ell-1}\).
Two telescoping sums and exponential decay give \(w_n=\sum_{\ell>n}(\ell-n)v_\ell\) for \(n\ge0\) and \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}v_\ell=w_0-w_1\).
Since the Fourier coefficients of \(|T_\ell|^2\) are \(\max(\ell-|n|,0)\), the energy of every integral Laurent numerator satisfies
\[
                         e(A)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}v_\ell\|AT_\ell\|_2^2 .
\]
Indeed expansion against the finitely many Fourier coefficients of \(|A|^2\) gives this identity directly.

If \(A\ne0\) has positive width, the two endpoint coefficients of \(AT_\ell\) are nonzero integers, so \(\|AT_\ell\|_2^2\ge2\) for all \(\ell\).
A monomial with coefficient of modulus at least two also satisfies this lower bound.
Thus every numerator other than a signed monomial or zero has energy at least \(2(w_0-w_1)\).
The numerator \(1-X\) attains this value since \((1-X)T_\ell=1-X^\ell\).
For equality, \(v_1,v_2>0\) require \(\|A\|_2^2=\|(1+X)A\|_2^2=2\).
The first condition forces two terms each with coefficient of modulus one.
If their exponents differ by more than one, multiplication by \(1+X\) gives four distinct terms of modulus one.
If their exponents are consecutive, the middle coefficient must vanish for the squared norm to remain two, forcing opposite signs.

Every signed monomial has energy \(w_0\), and both \(1\) and \(1-X\) are indivisible by \(Q_a\) by degrees and monic division.
Finally \(w_0=a/s^3\) and \(w_1=(a+s)r/s^3=2/s^3\).
Comparing \(a\) with \(2a-4\) proves the formula and the classification in all three ranges.
