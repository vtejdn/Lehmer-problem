# Integer congruences for rational factors at a quadratic pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous coprime moduli

Use the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#statement) \(P=F(X^k,X^l)=\prod_i S_i\), with positive coprime \(k,l\) and its monic rational irreducible factors counted with multiplicity.
At either \(j=3,4\) write \(q_j(U)=U^2-jU-1\), \(\beta=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(w=\sqrt{j^2+4}\), \(c=(j+3)(j+4)\), and
\[
 \gamma=c^k-(-1)^k\beta^l,\qquad
 D=kw\big((2j-7)c^2+2j+7\big)-lc,\qquad N=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\gamma|.
\]
Set \(b_i=|\operatorname{Res}(S_i,q_j(X^k))|\).
There are pairwise coprime positive integers \(m_i\mid b_i\), supported only on rational primes splitting in \(K\), such that
\[
 0\le\sum_i\log(b_i/m_i)
 =\log\!\left(N/\prod_i m_i\right)
 \le 2\log|c^{2k}-(-1)^l|+\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}D|.                    \tag{1}
\]
For each \(m_i>1\) there is a unit \(x_i\bmod m_i\) satisfying simultaneously
\[
 S_i(x_i)=0,\qquad q_j(x_i^k)=0,\qquad x_i^l=-c\pmod {m_i}.          \tag{2}
\]
All three congruences use one residue class at the full modulus.
They apply separately at each pole.
In particular, on steep primitive sequences \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\), for any factors of degrees \(d_i\) with \(k=o(d_i)\) one has \(\log m_i\sim (d_i/2)\log\beta\).

Write \(B_l=l(\log\log l/\log l)^3\) for large \(l\).
If at one pole the integer \(N\) has a rational prime-power divisor \(p^a\) with
\[
                  k+\log(N/p^a)=o(B_l),                              \tag{3}
\]
then the sections on that steep sequence are eventually irreducible.
This is a sufficient condition in terms of ordinary integers and permits a growing cofactor.
The existence of infinitely many directions satisfying it is open here.

<a id="fiber"></a>
## The integral fiber and the derivative

In the quotient by \(P\) the coordinate \(X\) is a unit since \(P\) is monic with constant term one.
Modulo \(q_j(X^k)\), the coordinates \(U=X^k,V=X^l\) satisfy \(U-U^{-1}=j\) and \(F=U^2(V+c)\).
Consequently the integral fiber has the presentation
\[
 \mathbb Z[X]/(P,q_j(X^k))\simeq\mathcal O_K/(\gamma).
\]
Indeed \(U\) maps to \(\beta\) and \(V\) to \(-c\), which is a unit modulo \(\gamma\).
If \(ak+bl=1\), the inverse construction of \(X\) from these two coordinates is \(X=U^aV^b\), since \(U^l=V^k\).
This first gives the presentation with \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
At \(j=3\) it is the full order of discriminant 13, while at \(j=4\) it has index two in the full order of discriminant five.
In the latter quotient two is invertible since \(c\) is even and invertible, so passing to the full order gives the same finite ring.

Let \(\theta\) denote the image of \(X\) in this ring.
The image of \(XP'\) is \(\beta^2 D\).
Indeed with \(t=U-U^{-1}\) the normalized equation is \(F/U^2=(t-3)(t-4)V^2+V+(t+3)(t+4)\).
At \(t=j,V=-c\), logarithmic differentiation along the section gives the asserted expression since \(U\,dt/dU=w\).
In particular the common quotient of \(P,P'\) and \(q_j(X^k)\) has order at most \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}D|\).
The same bound holds upon imposing \(S_i=P/S_i=0\) at this pole because those two equations imply \(P=P'=0\).

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Selecting split branches and individual factors

For each prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\mid\gamma\), put \(e=v_{\mathfrak p}(\gamma)\).
In the corresponding local factor of the finite fiber define \(e_i\) as the valuation of \(S_i(\theta)\) truncated at \(e\), assigning \(e\) to zero.
One has \(\sum_i e_i\ge e\) because \(P(\theta)=0\).
The ring obtained from the fiber by imposing \(S_i=0\) is \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S_i,q_j(X^k))\), so its order is \(b_i\) by the determinant of multiplication by \(q_j(X^k)\) in the monic order of \(S_i\).
Multiplicativity of the resultant and the fiber presentation give \(\prod_i b_i=N\).
Taking weighted sums of the local inequalities therefore gives equality at every prime ideal.
The divided product rule for \(P'\), that is, \(P'=\sum_i S_i'\prod_{h\ne i} S_h\), now gives
\[
                    e-\max_i e_i\le v_{\mathfrak p}(D)
\]
by evaluating in the local fiber and using the formula for \(XP'\).

Write \(\gamma'\) for the field conjugate of \(\gamma\).
The ideal gcd of \((\gamma)\) and \((\gamma')\) divides the ideal of the rational integer \(c^{2k}-(-1)^l\), since reducing their defining equalities modulo that gcd gives \(c^{2k}=(\beta\beta')^l=(-1)^l\).
Discard all inert and ramified prime powers in the fiber and at each split rational prime retain only the branch of larger valuation, breaking a tie arbitrarily.
Conjugation fixes each inert or ramified prime and exchanges the two split primes.
The product of the norms discarded is thus bounded by the norm of that ideal gcd, at most \(|c^{2k}-(-1)^l|^2\).
A retained split branch of exponent \(e\) is a ring \(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), by completion at a split prime.
On it choose an index with largest \(e_i\), reduce to the quotient of exponent \(e_i\), and assign the rational prime power \(p^{e_i}\) to that index.
The logarithmic loss in this last step summed over the retained branches is at most \(\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}D|\).
Multiplying assigned powers defines \(m_i\); the images of \(\theta\) give (2) by the Chinese remainder theorem.
This proves (1) and the divisibilities by \(b_i\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## An integer cofactor test

The [archimedean pole norm](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#size) gives \(\log b_i/d_i\to(\log\beta)/2\) uniformly, because the resultant is the absolute norm of the projected factor at \(\beta\).
The [degree scale](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives \(\log l=o(d_i)\).
The loss in (1) is \(O(k+\log(2+l+k))\), which proves the asymptotic for \(k=o(d_i)\).
Moreover the [exterior lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#statement) and the [individual pole counts](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth) give \(d_i\gg B_l\) uniformly at large indices.
Under (3) the logarithm of every \(m_i\) is consequently bounded below by a positive constant times \(B_l\).
At most one of the pairwise coprime \(m_i\) can be divisible by \(p\).
Each remaining one divides the prime-to-\(p\) part of \(N\) and hence is at most \(N/p^a\), contradicting (3).
This proves the criterion.
