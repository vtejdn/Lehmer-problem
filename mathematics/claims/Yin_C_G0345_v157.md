# Cancelling the power sums of a Salem polynomial with roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree, primitive orders and the size of the surviving power sum

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(e^a\), of degree \(d\), with \(0<a\le A\).
For a monic cyclotomic product \(Q\), including the empty product, let \(N=d+\deg Q\) and write \(t_j\) for the power sums of \(PQ\).
The genuine exterior excess at power \(j\) is \(\Delta_j=e^{aj}-e^{-aj}\), obtained by reflecting the unique exterior root into the unit disk.
For every integer \(q\ge1\), with prefix norms taken over \(1\le j<q\),
\[
 q+\frac{\sinh(aq)}{\sinh a}
 \ \le\ N+2\min\left(\|t\|_1,\sqrt{2\coth(a/2)}\,\|t\|_2\right).                 \tag{1}
\]
This holds for an arbitrary multiset of added unit-circle roots as well.
Under \(\|t\|_1\le CN\) one has \(\Delta_q/N\ll_{A,C}a\) and \(\sum_{j\le q}(\Delta_j/N)^2\ll_{A,C}a\).
Under \(\|t\|_2\le CN\) one has \(\Delta_q/N\ll_{A,C}\sqrt a\) and the same energy is \(O_{A,C}(1)\).
In both cases \(q/N\to0\) uniformly along every sequence of Salem inputs with \(a\to0\), for all choices of the added roots.
Thus even approximate cancellation with the squared-prefix budget cannot give a macroscopic prefix or a macroscopic genuine exterior signal at the next power.

For an integer \(k\ge1\) define \(D_-(P,k)\) to be the least \(\deg Q\) among cyclotomic products with \(t_1=\cdots=t_k=0\), allowing repetitions.
For fixed \(\delta>0,\ B>1/2+\delta\), uniformly at \((1/2+\delta)\log d\le ak\le B\log d\) as \(d\to\infty\),
\[
                       D_-(P,k)=(1+o(1))V,\qquad V=\frac{e^{ak}}{1-e^{-a}}.      \tag{2}
\]
Put \(H=\lfloor k^{7/8}\rfloor\), \(J=\{k-H+1,\ldots,k\}\), and
\[
                     U(X)=\prod_{j\in J}(X^j+1)^{\lfloor e^{aj}/j\rfloor}.
\]
Every cancelling product \(Q\) of degree \(D\sim V\) satisfies
\[
             \sum_n\varphi(n)\left|\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_n} Q-
                                        \operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_n} U\right|=o(D). \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(\deg\gcd(Q,U)=(1-o(1))D\).
On a shrinking sequence almost all the roots of every such \(Q\) have even order.
For \(2^h u\) with \(u\) odd and \(h\ge0\) fixed, the limiting proportion whose order is divisible by \(2^h u\) is
\[
              2^{-\max(0,h-1)}\frac1u\prod_{p\mid u}\frac{p}{p+1}.               \tag{4}
\]
The degree cost equals the leading cost of [matching a Salem prefix](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement), but cancellation forces factors of \(X^j+1\) at the terminal indices.
The distinction also determines the powers immediately after the cancelled prefix.
The coordinator proposed studying cancellation and the size of its surviving exterior signal.

<a id="all-orders"></a>
## Positivity after removing the real reciprocal pair

Index a Hermitian matrix \(E\) by \(0\le i,l<q\), with diagonal \(N\), lower entries \(t_{i-l}\) and conjugate upper entries.
Every root on the unit circle contributes a positive rank-one matrix.
The remaining roots are \(e^a,e^{-a}\), with joint contribution \(xy^*+yx^*\), where \(x_i=e^{a(i-(q-1)/2)}\) and \(y_i=x_i^{-1}\).
Hence \(E\ge xy^*+yx^*\).
The positive vector \(h=(x+y)/\|x+y\|_2\) has eigenvalue \(q+\sinh(aq)/\sinh a\) for the latter matrix, since \(\langle x,y\rangle=q\) and \(\|x\|_2^2=\|y\|_2^2=\sinh(aq)/\sinh a\).
For \(w_j=\sum_i h_i h_{i+j}\), extending \(h\) by zero off the index interval, one has \(w_j\le1\) and \(\|w\|_2\le\|h\|_1\) by the triangle inequality for a sum of translates of \(h\).
The equal norms and positivity give \(\|h\|_1\le\sqrt2\|x\|_1/\|x\|_2\le\sqrt{2\coth(a/2)}\) by summing the finite geometric progressions.
Evaluating \(h^*Eh\) now gives (1), including \(q=1\) with the empty prefix.

For \(j\le q\), \(\sinh(aj)\le e^{-a(q-j)}\sinh(aq)\).
Summing the resulting squared geometric progression proves the energy bounds from (1).
For small \(a\) the weaker prefix hypothesis gives \(e^{aq}\ll_C1+N\sqrt a\), and hence \(q/N\ll_C\log(2N)/(aN)\).
The [positive-measure degree bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(a\gg_A(\log(2d))^{-3}\) and \(d\to\infty\) along a shrinking sequence.
As \(N\ge d\), the last ratio tends to zero uniformly.
For an exact prefix (1) gives \(N\ge q+\sinh(aq)/\sinh a\) at every degree.
At fixed \(q\), multiplying an exact prefix product by \((X^q-1)^m\) keeps the earlier moments zero and adds \(mq\) both to the degree and to \(t_q\).
Thus the new total normalized \(q\)-th moment tends to one as \(m\to\infty\).
The exterior excess is invariant under this addition.

<a id="negative-degree"></a>
## The cost of the inverse initial coefficients

Write \(p_j\) for the power sums of \(P\) and \(lc_l=\sum_{e\mid l}\mu(l/e)p_e\) for its integral initial Euler exponents.
The cancellation conditions prescribe the exponents \(-c_l\) through \(k\), since the reversal of \(Q\) must equal the inverse of the reversal of \(P\) modulo \(w^{k+1}\).
The [packet construction](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets) applies to these exponents with all signs reversed.
Its degree bound (5) involves only the absolute power sums.
As in the [Salem degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#degree), the high indices give at most \(V+O_A(k^{3/2}\sqrt{d\log(2d)}+k)\), and the remaining cost is \(O_A(k^3(\sqrt{kd\log(2d)}+e^{ak/2}+1))=o(V)\).
Here \(k\ll_{A,B}(\log(2d))^4\).
In the same range that estimate yields
\[
             \sum_{l\le k}|lc_l-e^{al}|=o(e^{ak}),\qquad aH\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
These estimates hold uniformly for the original \(P\).

Use the [divisor tests](Yin_C_G0345_v144.md#weighted) with \(R=k^{1/40}\).
They take arbitrary real \(g_j(u)\in[-1,1]\) for \(j\in J,\ u\mid j\).
For each \(l\mid j\) with \(j/l<R\) set \(w_l=f(j/l)\sum_{u\mid l}\varphi(u)g_j(u)\), and set the other weights to zero, where \(f(s)=\max(0,\min(1,1-\log s/\log R))\).
Such \(j\) is unique.
For \(Q=\prod_n\Phi_n^{b_n}\) put \(T_n(g)=\sum_{l\mid n}\mu(n/l)w_l\), extending the weights by zero past \(k\).
The prescribed exponents give, uniformly in the tests,
\[
 \sum_n b_n T_n(g)=-\sum_{j\in J}\frac{e^{aj}}j
                                  \sum_{u\mid j}\varphi(u)g_j(u)+o(V).          \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(|w_l|\le l\), and the discarded exponentials below \(J\) sum to \(O(Ve^{-aH})\).
In particular \(g=1\) gives \(-(1+o(1))V\), while \(|T_n(1)|\le(1+o(1))\varphi(n)\) by the cited tests.
This proves the matching lower bound in (2).

<a id="negative-orders"></a>
## Which primitive roots attain the negative extremum

For \(n\le kR\) having a divisor with assigned weight, all assigned divisors correspond to a common \(j\in J\).
At \(g=1\) the divisor estimate gives \(T_n(1)/\varphi(n)=f(j/n)\mu(m)/\varphi(m)+o(1)\), \(m=n/(n,j)\).
Inactive orders give zero, and for \(n>kR\) the bound is \(o(1)\) after the same normalization, uniformly in \(g\).
Only \(m=2\) gives \(-1\), and every other ratio is at least \(-1/2\).
Call an order good if it has an assigned divisor, \(n\le kR\), and \(m=2\) at its corresponding \(j\).
For \(D\sim V\), (5) at \(g=1\) shows that the total degree at all other orders and the sum of \((1-f(j/n))\varphi(n)b_n\) over good orders are \(o(D)\).

Write \(v=v_2(j)\).
The good order is \(n=2^{v+1}w\) for some odd \(w\mid j\).
The [prime-power formula (4)–(5)](Yin_C_G0345_v144.md#weighted) for \(T_n(g)\) has a single negative local sign at two.
After the factor \(f(j/n)\), its only masses are \(-\varphi(w)\varphi(2^t)\) at \(u=2^t w\), \(0\le t\le v\), up to an additive \(o(\varphi(n))\) in the whole test.
Indeed every odd local exponent of \(u\) must equal the corresponding exponent of \(n\), and at two the local Möbius factor is \(-1\) at each of the displayed levels.
For arbitrary tests \(h_j(n)\in[-1,1]\) on \(n\mid2j,\ n\nmid j\), choose \(g_j(u)=h_j(2^{v+1}\operatorname{odd}(u))\), where \(\operatorname{odd}(u)\) is the odd part.
All the nonzero masses at a good \(n\) now have the same test value and sum to \(-\varphi(n)h_j(n)\) before the scalar.
On the right of (5), grouping by the odd part of \(u\) also gives the weight \(\varphi(n)h_j(n)\), since \(\sum_{t=0}^v\varphi(2^t)=2^v\).
The uniform absolute bound for the other orders and the scalar deficit estimate show that, in the coordinates \((j,n)\), the good degree measure differs in total absolute mass by \(o(D)\) from the measure giving mass \(e^{aj}\varphi(n)/j\) to each \(n\mid2j,\ n\nmid j\).
This follows by taking the sign of the coordinatewise difference as \(h\).
These \(n\)'s are exactly the primitive orders in \(X^j+1\).
Rounding down the exponents costs at most \(\sum_{j\in J}j\le k^2=o(D)\), proving (3).

The limiting order law follows by choosing \(j\) with weight proportional to \(e^{aj}\) on \(J\), then a uniform root of \(X^j+1\).
As \(a\to0\) the first choice is equidistributed in every fixed residue system by the [geometric-weight calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v144.md#consequences).
At two the order has valuation exactly \(v_2(j)+1\), so divisibility by \(2^h\) has limit \(2^{-\max(0,h-1)}\).
The odd part is uniform on the group of roots of unity of order dividing the odd part of \(j\).
For any odd prime \(p\) the conditional probability of divisibility by \(p^t\) is zero if \(v_p(j)<t\) and otherwise \(1-p^{t-1-v_p(j)}\).
The same geometric averaging gives \(p^{1-t}/(p+1)\) at this prime.
The Chinese remainder theorem gives joint independence at fixed primes, first for truncated valuations and then by the geometric tails, proving (4).

<a id="terminal"></a>
## The return of a large power sum after exact cancellation

For a near-minimum cancelling \(Q\), write \(\tau_m(Q)\) for its own power sums.
Each \(X^j+1\) has zero \(m\)-th sum unless \(j\mid m\), so (3) gives \(\tau_m(Q)=o(D)\) uniformly throughout
\[
                              k<m<2(k-H+1).
\]
On this interval the [original reflected estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#degree) gives \(p_m=e^{am}+O_A(1+\sqrt{md\log(2d)})=e^{am}+o(D)\).
Consequently \(t_{k+1}/N=(e^a-1)D/N+o(1)\), which tends to zero on a shrinking sequence.

On that sequence let \(m_0=\lceil\log N/a\rceil\).
Then \(e^{am_0}/N\to1\), and \(a(m_0-k)\to\infty\) since \(N\ge D\sim e^{ak}/(1-e^{-a})\).
Also \(2ak-\log N\gg_{\delta,A}\log d\), since \(N=d+D\), \(2ak-\log d\ge2\delta\log d\), and \(\log D=ak+O_A(\log\log(3d))\).
As \(aH=o(\log d)\), all the indices from \(m_0-\lfloor1/a\rfloor\) through \(m_0\) lie in the displayed interval eventually.
Thus \(t_{m_0}/N\to1\), but each of the preceding \(\lfloor1/a\rfloor\) normalized sums is bounded below by a positive constant.
By the time the genuine exterior contribution regains macroscopic size both preceding norm budgets have already failed.
