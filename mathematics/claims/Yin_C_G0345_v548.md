# Degree share and Mahler measure for every factor at long slopes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform factor law

Let \(s\ge3\), let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers with \(a\ge2\), and put \(r=b/a\), \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\).
For every monic nontorsion irreducible factor \(P\) over \(\mathbb Q\) of \(g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\), write \(d=\deg P\), \(m=\log M(P)\).
For all \(r>s\) one has \((b-as)m\le d\log 3\).
There are absolute implied constants such that for \(s\) sufficiently large and \(r\ge C s^2\log(2s)\), with a sufficiently large absolute \(C\),
\[
 \frac{bm}{d}=\mu+O\!\left(s^{-1/16}+\frac{s^2\log(2s)}r\right),\qquad
 \mu=\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|1+e^{it}|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}>0.
\]
Every such factor is reciprocal and the fraction of its roots off the circle is \(O(s^2/r)\).
In particular at cubic slopes \(r\asymp s^3\) the error is \(O(s^{-1/16})\), without a restriction on its exterior-root count.
For any sequence at \(r/(s^2\log(2s))\to\infty\) with \(s\to\infty\), the measure tends to zero exactly when \(d=o(b)\).
This characterization leaves open the existence of factors with that degree share.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Two upper bounds from the integral norm

All roots are units, \(g\) and \(B\) have no common zero, and \(x^a\) has the degree \(d\) of \(x\), by the [projection argument](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#discriminant).
Let \(R=|\operatorname{Res}(P,g(X^a))|\).
The [parent resultant computation](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#norm) gives \(1\le R\le4^a\).
For \(|x|<1\), the section equation gives \(\log|g(x^a)|\le b\log|x|+\log3\).
For \(|x|>1\), reversal gives \(\log|g(x^a)|\le as\log|x|+\log3\), while the circle contributes at most \(\log3\) per root.
Since the interior and exterior sums of \(\log|x|\) are \(-m\) and \(m\), summation gives \(0\le\log R\le d\log3-(b-as)m\).
In the long-slope range this proves \(h(x^a)=am/d\ll1/r\), where \(h\) is absolute logarithmic height.
The [exterior-root measure lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#statement) gives \(m\gg\sqrt{s/r}\), so \(d\gg a\sqrt{sr}\) and \(a/d\ll1/\sqrt{sr}\).
Also \(d\le b+as\le2b\); the [explicit Voutier lower bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(d\gg b/(\log(6b))^3\), in particular \(d\ge b^{0.99}\ge s^{1.98}\) for sufficiently large \(s\).
Here a degree-one unit is torsion, so the lower bound applies to all the factors in question.

For an exterior root of the parent the [full localization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles) gives \(a\log|x|\gg s^{-2}\), since its projection differs by \(O(s^{-4})\) from a pole with logarithmic radius at least a constant times \(s^{-2}\).
The parent is reciprocal, so the same absolute radial lower bound holds for every interior root.
Consequently the combined off-circle count is \(O(s^2 am)=O(ds^2/r)\).
For large \(s\) at least one root lies on the unit circle, where complex conjugation takes it to its inverse, proving reciprocity of \(P\) by irreducibility.
For each interior root write \(z=x^a\), \(L=-\log|z|\).
The [localized bound](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#norm) gives \(c/s\le |B(z)|\le3\): its proof uses only the uniform disk localization and the seed identities, both valid here.
The inverse pair contributes \(-(r-s)L+2\log|B(z)|\) to \(\log R\).
Therefore
\[
 \frac{(b-as)m}{d}=\frac1d\sum_{|x|=1}\log|g(x^a)|
       +O\!\left(\frac{s^2\log(2s)}r+\frac{a}d\right).
\]

<a id="phases"></a>
## Quantitative control of the two angular coordinates

Put \(y=x^a\), let \(u=y/|y|\) at each embedding, and average over all \(d\) embeddings.
Let \(H=\lfloor s^{1/4}\rfloor\).
For \(|i|,|j|\le H\) with \((i,j)\ne(0,0)\), one has \(0<|n|=|i+js|\le 2sH\).
The unit \(y^n\) has degree \(D\ge d/|n|\gg s^{0.73}\) and height \(h(y^n)\ll sH/r\ll s^{-3/4}\).
For its minimum polynomial apply the [compact Lipschitz discrepancy estimate](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) with smoothing radius \(D^{-1}\) to the real and imaginary parts of the phase multiplied by a fixed Lipschitz radial cutoff equal to one on \([1/2,2]\) and zero off \([1/4,4]\).
The tests have bounded energy and zero Haar mean.
Removing the cutoff costs \(O(h(y^n))\), since the unit has mean absolute logarithmic radius \(2h(y^n)\), and outside \([1/2,2]\) that radius is at least \(\log2\).
The conjugates are counted with equal multiplicity by the original embeddings.
Since \((\log D)/D\) decreases for \(D>e\), the resulting bound is
\[
 \big|\mathbb E\,u^{i+js}\big|\ll s^{-0.365}\sqrt{\log(2s)}+s^{-3/8}.
\]

Set \(\delta=s^{-1/8}\) and consider \(f(z,w)=\log\max(\delta,|w-z-1|)\) on the unit torus.
This function has Lipschitz constant \(O(\delta^{-1})\) in angular coordinates and supremum norm \(O(\log(2s))\).
Convolve in each angular coordinate with the normalized Fejér kernel of order \(H\).
Its first absolute angular moment is \(O(\log(2H)/H)\) by integrating the kernel bound \(O(\min(H,(Ht^2)^{-1}))\) for \(0<|t|\le\pi\), as follows from the squared sine quotient.
Thus the uniform approximation error is \(O(\delta^{-1}\log(2H)/H)\).
The resulting trigonometric polynomial has at most \((2H+1)^2\) coefficients; by orthogonality, the contraction of convolution in supremum norm and Cauchy–Schwarz, their absolute sum is \(O(H\log(2s))\).
Its constant coefficient is the Haar mean of \(f\).
The character bound now yields \(\mathbb E f(u,u^s)=\int f+O(s^{-1/16})\), where the integral is normalized torus Haar measure.
Deleting all off-circle embeddings in the average incurs an additional \(O(s^2\log(2s)/r)\).

<a id="tails"></a>
## Removing the logarithmic truncation

On the unit circle \(|g(z)|\ge c/s\) and \(|g(z)|\le v\) implies that \(z\) is within \(O(v)\) of the two primitive cube roots, for \(0<v\le v_0\), by the [circle tail estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#tails).
Majorize these neighborhoods by a compact planar test that drops linearly from one to zero over width \(s^{-1/4}\); its Lipschitz constant is \(O(s^{1/4})\), its Dirichlet integral is \(O(s^{1/2})\), and its circle mean is \(O(v+s^{-1/4})\).
Applying the same discrepancy estimate to \(y\) with degree \(d\ge s^{1.98}\), height \(O(1/r)\) and smoothing radius \(1/d\) bounds the fraction of all embeddings both on the circle and in the sublevel by \(O(v+s^{-1/4})\), uniformly in \(v\).
Integrating against \(dv/v\) between \(c/s\) and \(\delta\) shows the truncation costs \(O(\delta+s^{-1/4}\log(2s))\) in the circle sum divided by \(d\).
For full torus Haar measure the same angular restriction gives sublevel mass \(O(v)\) for \(|w-z-1|\le v\); integration from zero to \(\delta\) costs \(O(\delta)\).
The untruncated Haar mean equals \(\mu\) by the one-variable Jensen integral in \(w\), as in the [individual-factor circle calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#tails).
Inserting the two estimates into the norm formula proves the assertion since \(as m/d\ll s/r\).
