# Exact counting of zero-prefix trace factors and the cost of their missing degrees

<a id="statement"></a>
## Questions about the whole second period

Use the [zero-boundary rows](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#statement) with \(p=3^\nu,\ q=p-1\) sufficiently large, \(\nu\) positive and even, and fixed binary precision \(h\ge1\).
Write \(L=(p+3)/2\), \(u=2^h\), \(\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)\), \(d=\deg F\) and \(N=A+2u\), where \(A\) is the even-side order.
Then \(d\) is even, \(N\) is odd and prime to three, and \(N/d\to\theta\).
The [count below](#count) gives the exact number of admissible second-period factors on each prescribed zero-boundary row by a calculation over \(\mathbb F_3\) and integer addition.
For fixed \(q,h\) its running time is polynomial in \(d\).
There is also a [necessary Fourier condition](#fourier) expressed by the multiplicative orbits of the first \(L\) frequencies modulo \(N\).

For every function \(\omega(d)\to\infty\), there exist strengthened zero-boundary rows of unbounded degree, with chosen one-denominator polynomials \(F\) on them, on which
\[
 \frac1d\deg\gcd(\bar F,X^N-1)\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{(\mathbb Z/3^\nu)[X]/(F)} X=N3^b,\qquad
 d/\omega(d)\le 3^b=o(d).
\]
Here \(\bar F\) is the reduction modulo three, and the order refers to the image of \(X\) in the unit group.
All roots of \(\bar F\) satisfy \(X^N=1\).
All repeated roots belong to the same fixed finite subgroup of \(\overline{\mathbb F}_3^\times\) throughout the sequence.
The polynomials retain the [exact pole, even-side period, dyadic degree and archimedean conditions](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#statement) on the one-denominator models.
The estimate concerns selected words on actual rows.
Existence of a different full-degree word of exact order \(N\) on infinitely many of the strengthened rows remains unresolved.

## Counting the factors of the specified degree, order and prefix

For each integer \(m\le L\) with \(m\ge1\) and \(3\nmid m\), put \(s_m=\nu+\lfloor\log_3(L/m)\rfloor\) and define
\[
 G=\prod_{\substack{1\le m\le L\\3\nmid m}}\mathbb Z/3^{s_m}\mathbb Z .
\]
For a polynomial \(B\) of constant term one over \(\mathbb F_3\), take its Euler digits \(a_i\in\{0,1,2\}\) from \(B(X)=\prod_{i\ge1}(1-X^i)^{a_i}\) in the formal power series ring.
Write \(g(B)_m=\sum_{0\le k<s_m}3^k a_{m3^k}\bmod 3^{s_m}\).
Then \(g(B_1 B_2)=g(B_1)+g(B_2)\), since cubing \(1-X^i\) gives \(1-X^{3i}\), and the carries in base three stay in their own \(m\) coordinate.
For a reciprocal factor of the separable \(X^N-1\), the [lifted zero-prefix criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#digits) is exactly \(g(B)=0\).
The number of states is \(|G|=3^{L+(\nu-1)(L-\lfloor L/3\rfloor)}\), since each index up to \(L\) occurs once as \(m3^k\).

For \(n\mid N\) let \(\mathcal O_n\) be the nonidentity orbits in \(\mu_n(\overline{\mathbb F}_3)\) under cubing and inversion.
The monic polynomial \(B_O\) of an orbit pairs distinct inverse roots, so has even degree and constant term one.
The identity cannot occur in a reciprocal divisor of even degree because \(N\) is odd and all the other orbits have even size.
In the integer group ring of \(G\) use \([g]\) for the basis element of \(g\).
For \(n\mid N\) define
\[
 C_n(d)=[z^d[0]]\prod_{O\in\mathcal O_n}\bigl(1+z^{|O|}[g(B_O)]\bigr).
\]
This denotes the coefficient of \(z^d\) at the basis element of the group identity.
The number of full words with exact order \(N\) is
\[
                     \sum_{n\mid N}\mu(N/n)\,C_n(d).
\]
Indeed each subset of orbits gives a different monic factor and its unique lift, whose order is the least common multiple of the selected root orders.
The integer \(C_n(d)\) counts those of order dividing \(n\) with the prescribed degree and prefix.
Möbius inversion gives exactly the required order, and each counted word is eligible for the [pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace).

For each divisor \(n\) update the degree and \(G\) coordinates once per orbit, truncating above degree \(d\).
This takes \(O((d+1)N\tau(N)|G|)\) integer additions over all divisors, where \(\tau(N)\) counts divisors.
Each entry counts subsets of at most \(N\) orbits and takes \(O(N+1)\) bits.
The orbits can be computed by factoring the squarefree \(X^n-1\) over the fixed field \(\mathbb F_3\) and pairing reciprocal factors.
For example, compute the cubing-fixed subalgebra of \(\mathbb F_3[X]/(X^n-1)\) by linear equations.
In the product of its residue fields this subalgebra is one copy of \(\mathbb F_3\) per irreducible factor.
Splitting by the gcds with each basis vector minus each of \(0,1,2\) separates every pair of these components, and hence recovers the factors with polynomially many field operations.
The Euler digits use at most \(3^{\nu-1}L\) coefficients of each orbit factor.
Together with \(N=O_{q,h}(d)\), this proves polynomial time in the degree for fixed modulus, with the displayed state count as a modulus-dependent cost.

## Frequencies that force a factor to contain entire cosets

Fix a primitive \(N\)-th root \(\zeta\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_3\).
Let \(f(a)\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether the root \(\zeta^a\) belongs to a proposed full factor.
Its moments are \(\widehat f(j)=\sum_a f(a)\zeta^{aj}\).
The zero prefix modulo three gives \(\widehat f(j)=0\) for \(1\le j\le L\) by Newton's identities on the reciprocal factor.
Inversion and cubing preserve its roots, so these moments vanish at all residues in
\[
 \Omega=\{\ \pm3^ki\bmod N: k\ge0,\ 1\le i\le L\ \}.
\]
If \(D\mid N\) and \(\Omega\) contains all frequencies outside \(D\mathbb Z/N\), finite Fourier inversion makes \(f\) invariant under translation by \(N/D\).
Consequently its roots are unions of full \(\mu_D\)-cosets, \(D\mid d\), and its lifted polynomial belongs to \((\mathbb Z/3^\nu)[X^D]\).
For the last assertion the unique prime-to-three lifts preserve the complete cosets and their product factors \(X^D-\gamma\).

If \(N=BC\) with \(B,C>1\) coprime and \(\Omega\) contains all residues divisible by neither \(B\) nor \(C\), the remaining Fourier frequencies lie on two axes in the Chinese remainder coordinates.
Inversion then has the form \(f(x,y)=a(x)+b(y)\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_3\).
If both summands varied, their differences would each include a nonzero step, and four values \(v,v+s,v+t,v+s+t\) could not all lie in \(\{0,1\}\).
Indeed the only nonzero step taking \(v\) to the other value is \(1\) or \(-1\), so \(s=t\) and \(v+2s\) is a third value.
It follows that the selected root set is a union of complete cosets in one coordinate, forcing \(B\mid d\) or \(C\mid d\).

For a local infinite example take \(N=13^a\), \(a\ge1\).
The subgroup generated by \(3,-1\) has index two modulo each \(13^j,\ 1\le j\le a\), represented by \(1,2\).
Indeed \(3\) has order three modulo 13 and \(v_{13}(3^3-1)=1\).
The binomial expansion increases this valuation by exactly one at each thirteenth power.
The order of three is therefore \(3\cdot13^{j-1}\), and the residue of \(2\) is outside \(\{\pm1,\pm3,\pm9\}\bmod13\).
If \(1\le e\le a\) and \(2\cdot13^{e-1}\le L\), applying these representatives at each valuation below \(e\) covers every frequency not divisible by \(13^e\).
Hence \(13^e\mid d\) and the lifted factor is a polynomial in \(X^{13^e}\).
The occurrence of these particular periods in the synchronized degree progressions is a separate question.

## Concentrating the obstruction in one fixed subgroup

Use the [synchronized prime powers and orbit budget](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion).
Fix its first chosen prime \(H>L\) once and retain its exact valuation when selecting further primes.
This is possible because each extension in that construction keeps all previously selected valuations fixed on its exponent progression.
For a tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), take sufficiently many further primes to obtain \(N=Rw\) with \(R\) fixed on this stage, \(H\mid R,\ \gcd(R,w)=1\), and the orbit bound \(g/R<\varepsilon/(4\theta)\).
Here \(g\) bounds a cubing-and-inversion orbit in \(\mu_R\).
The original zero-boundary degree progression and its real error constant do not depend on \(R\).
Fix a power \(Q=3^v\) with \(v\ge\nu+\lfloor\log_3 L\rfloor\) and let \(K=2HQ\).

Choose the [orbit product](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion) \(V\) to exclude every root in \(\mu_H\).
There are still \(R-H\) available labels, which exceed the budget \((d-K)/w\) on sufficiently large rows, since \(d/w\to R/\theta\), \(\theta>1\), and \(R\) may be chosen sufficiently large.
The primitive \(R\)-orbit remains available and fits the budget by the bound on \(g\).
Include it, then include further available orbits until the next would exceed the budget.
The lifted \(U=V(X^w)\) has exact order \(N\), and its even deficit \(\Delta=d-\deg U\) satisfies \(K\le\Delta<K+wg<\varepsilon d\) on sufficiently large rows.
It has no root in \(\mu_H\) modulo three.
Along each such fixed-\(R\) sequence there is also a constant \(\delta_R>0\) such that \(\Delta\ge\delta_R d\).
Indeed \(\Delta/w=d/w-\deg V\) is bounded below by the distance of \(d/w\) to the integers, and \(R/\theta\) is irrational by the logarithmic independence of \(p\) and \(p/q\).

Write \(\Delta/2=Hx+Qy\), where \(0\le y<H\) is determined modulo \(H\), and \(x\ge0\) since \(\Delta\ge K\).
Take the [zero-prefix filler](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion)
\[
                G_0=(X^H-1)^{2x}(X-1)^{2Qy}.
\]
The previous coefficient gluing lifts \(UG_0\) to \(F\) with all the asserted row conditions.
Taking sufficiently large rows at each stage makes \(x\ge1\).
Write \(e=2x+2Qy\), so \(\Delta/H\le e\le\Delta\).
The root 1 of \(\bar G_0\) has multiplicity \(e\), and the other \(H-1\) roots have multiplicity \(2x\).
All roots of \(\bar U\) are simple and disjoint from them, hence for each \(k\ge0\)
\[
 \deg\gcd(\bar F,X^{N3^k}-1)=d-\Delta
             +(H-1)\min(3^k,2x)+\min(3^k,e).
\]

## The full order in the finite ring

In \((\mathbb Z/3^\nu)[Y]/(Y^e)\) the unit \(1+Y\), for \(e\ge2\), has exact order \(3^{\nu-1+\lceil\log_3 e\rceil}\).
For \(s\ge\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\) and \(1\le i<e\), the coefficient of \(Y^i\) in \((1+Y)^{3^s}\) has valuation \(s-v_3(i)\), by the binomial formula and \(\binom{3^s-1}{i-1}\not\equiv0\bmod3\).
Reduction modulo three already requires \(s\ge\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\) for a power of three to kill the unit.
Taking the largest power of three strictly below \(e\) as \(i\) gives the stated exact exponent.

Put \(b=\nu-1+\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\).
Over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\) the divisibilities \((X-1)^e\mid G_0\mid (X^H-1)^e\) imply that the order modulo \(G_0\) is a multiple of \(3^b\) and divides \(H3^b\).
The upper divisibility uses the same binomial expansion with \(Y=X^H-1\).
The factors \(U,G_0\) are comaximal because their reductions are coprime and the ideal generated by three is nilpotent.
As \(H\mid N\) and \(3\nmid N\), the order modulo \(UG_0\) is exactly \(N3^b\).
In particular \(\Delta/H\le 3^b\le 3^\nu\Delta\).
Let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and at each stage take a sufficiently large degree with \(\omega(d)\ge H/\delta_R\).
Then \(d/\omega(d)\le3^b=o(d)\), whereas the displayed gcd at \(k=0\) equals \(d-\Delta+H=d-o(d)\).
This separates a return in nearly all degrees at the linear time \(N\) from the exact full period of the specified finite quotient.
