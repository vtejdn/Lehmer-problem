# Polynomial degree suffices for cyclotomic padding on a fixed number of residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bounded witness and its implication for the prime

Fix \(k\ge2\).
Let \(P\) be monic and irreducible in \(\mathbb Z[x]\), of degree \(D\), with unit constant term and support differences of gcd one.
Assume \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\), where \(m=\log M\) and \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
Suppose a product \(PC\), with \(C\) a product of cyclotomics, occupies exactly \(k\) residue classes modulo an odd prime \(p\).
Let \(h\) be the fast width in its [centered lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift) after removing the common row factor.
If \(20h\le p\), then some such product has degree at most
\[
                                (2+p+D)^{A_k}                          \tag{1}
\]
for a constant \(A_k\) depending only on \(k\).
One can retain the original centered fast indices up to a common translation, the slope of specialization, and the same bivariate irreducible factor carrying \(P\) up to a Laurent unit.
In particular (1) bounds the degree of a multiplier witnessing an affirmative answer, even when the specified multiplier has arbitrarily high degree.

Along any such sequence with \(k\) fixed, \(p\to\infty\) and \(m(P)\to0\), there is a constant \(c_k>0\) such that eventually
\[
             \log(2D)\ge c_k\,p/s,\qquad
             p\ll_k \bigl(\log(2D)\bigr)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor},        \tag{2}
\]
where \(s\le h\) is the fast width of the carrier.
The second bound applies to each of the growing primes supplying a representation.
It places no restriction on the initially exhibited padding degree.

<a id="directions"></a>
## How many directions can enter the multiplier

Write \(F\) for the primitive \(k\)-row lift with specialization \(X=x^p,Y=x^b\), \(0<b<p\).
The [support-lattice factor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v350.md#strip) and the [carrier theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#factor) give
\[
       F=B\prod_{\nu=1}^r Q_\nu(X^{a_\nu}Y^{v_\nu})
\]
up to a signed Laurent monomial.
Here \(B\) is the single irreducible carrying \(P\), with diagonal span \(d\le D/(1-10s/p)\le2D\).
Each \(Q_\nu\) is a product of ordinary cyclotomic polynomials, and the pairs \((a_\nu,v_\nu)\) are distinct primitive directions up to sign, oriented so that \(v_\nu>0\).
Factors depending only on \(X\) were removed with the row gcd.
Let \(n_\nu=\deg Q_\nu\), so \(\sum_\nu n_\nu v_\nu=h-s\).

In fact \(r\le k\).
The Newton polygon of a product is the Minkowski sum of its Newton polygons, since the product of two nonzero initial forms is nonzero in the Laurent polynomial ring.
Every segment from a direction \((a_\nu,v_\nu)\) therefore contributes two parallel edges to a two-dimensional Newton polygon of \(F\).
The two exposed faces for opposite normals contain the respective translates of that segment.
All vertices lie among the left and right endpoints of the \(k\) nonempty horizontal rows, yielding at most \(2k\) edges.
If the polygon is a segment, all segment summands are parallel, so \(r\le1\) in this case.
This bounds the number of unknown base exponents after grouping, independently of the number of cyclotomic factors or their multiplicities.

<a id="compression"></a>
## A small integral solution preserving all coefficient cancellations

Translate \(B\) so its fast indices lie in \([0,s]\).
Its base exponents have diameter at most \(d/p+s\): two diagonal exponents differ by at most \(d\), while the fast part of their difference is at most \(bs\).
It has at most \(d+1\) monomials since they remain distinct on the diagonal.
In the product expression replace each \(a_\nu\) by an integer unknown \(z_\nu\).
A term of the formal expansion has a fixed coefficient and fast index, and a base exponent
\[
                         i+\sum_{\nu=1}^r e_\nu z_\nu,
                \qquad 0\le e_\nu\le n_\nu\le h ,
\]
where \(i\) is a base exponent of \(B\).
There are at most \(T=(d+1)(h+1)^r\) such terms.
For pairs within any one fast row, impose equality of these affine forms when they agreed for the original \(a_\nu\), and inequality when they differed.
Any integral solution preserves the same partition into groups with equal base exponent and hence exactly the same nonzero fast rows, even with signed coefficients.

Here is an explicit polynomial height estimate for some solution.
Write the equalities as \(Mz=c\), with entries of absolute value at most \(h\) in \(M\) and at most \(d/p+s\) in \(c\).
Let \(q\le r\) be the rational rank.
Choose independent rows and \(q\) pivot columns with determinant of absolute value \(\delta>0\), taking \(\delta=1\) if \(q=0\).
All other equations follow from these rows because the given system has an actual solution.
For each of the \(r-q\) free coordinates keep its residue modulo \(\delta\) from the original solution, represented between zero and \(\delta-1\), and vary it by \(\delta t_j\).
Solving the pivot system then gives integers for every integral choice of \(t_j\), by the adjugate formula and consistency at the original solution.
Each imposed inequality excludes a proper affine hyperplane in the \(t_j\), or excludes no point, since the original solution belongs to this parametrization.
There are at most \(T^2\) such inequalities.
For \(r>q\), on the grid \(0\le t_j\le T^2\) a single proper hyperplane contains at most \((T^2+1)^{r-q-1}\) points, by solving for one coordinate.
Some point of the grid avoids all forbidden equalities.
For \(r=q\) the unique solution already satisfies the inequalities, including the empty system when \(r=0\).

For fixed \(k\), \(\delta\le r!(1+h)^r\).
Every free coordinate chosen this way has size at most \(\delta(1+T^2)\).
For \(q>0\) the entries of the pivot adjugate are bounded by \((q-1)!(1+h)^{q-1}\).
The right side after fixing free coordinates has size at most \(d/p+s+r h\delta(1+T^2)\).
Together with \(r\le k,\ d\le2D,\ h<p\), these estimates bound all \(|z_\nu|\) by \((2+p+D)^{O_k(1)}\).

The new factors may have nonprimitive directions, which still give products of cyclotomics in Laurent monomials.
On the diagonal \(p z_\nu+bv_\nu\ne0\) because \(0<v_\nu\le h<p\).
Thus their normalized diagonal factors are ordinary cyclotomic products, including for negative powers.
The carrier diagonal is already \(P\) times a cyclotomic product up to a signed monomial.
The full new diagonal has span at most
\[
                    d+\sum_\nu n_\nu |p z_\nu+bv_\nu|
                    \le d+p h\max_{\nu}|z_\nu|+b h ,
\]
where an empty product has no added span.
Distinct fast rows still map to distinct residues, so monomial normalization yields the required witness in (1).
Each new factor is primitive as a polynomial in \(Y\) over the Laurent base, and \(B\) occurs once because its diagonal contains \(P\).
The product is reciprocal up to a signed Laurent monomial and its independent logarithmic measure equals \(m(B)\).

<a id="prime"></a>
## Bounding the prime by the original degree

For fixed \(k\), the centered width satisfies \(h=O_k(p^{1-1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor})=o(p)\).
Thus \(20h\le p\) eventually.
Write \(G\) for the new polynomial in the bounded representation and \(d_G\) for its diagonal span.
All \(k\) rows remain occupied, \(d_G\le(2+p+D)^{A_k}\), and its simple carrier \(B\) has reciprocal fibers away from the exceptional set.
We have \(m(G)=m(B)\ge\eta_k>0\) by the [projected gap on the primitive lift](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#factor).
The [angular derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#statement) applied on the roots of \(B\) bounds the neutral loss by
\[
 L(B)\le 2(h+s)m(B)+C s\{k+\log(2p d_G)\}
\]
with an absolute \(C\).
The [cyclic comparison for that factor](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#loss) bounds \(m(P)\) below by \((1-2(s-1)/p)m(B)-(2s\log s+L(B))/p\).
Combining, and using \(s\le h<p\) and (1), gives
\[
              m(P)\ge(1-6h/p)m(B)
                         -O_k\!\left(\frac{s}{p}\log(2+p+D)\right).
\]
For sufficiently large \(p\) the coefficient of \(m(B)\) is positive and at least \(3/4\).
Once \(m(P)<\eta_k/4\), the remaining term must have magnitude at least \(\eta_k/2\).
Hence \(\log(2+p+D)\gg_k p/s\).
Since \(p/s\ge p/h\gg_k p^{1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\), the quantity \(\log(2+p)\) is \(o(p/s)\).
The inequality \(\log(2+p+D)\le\log(2+p)+\log(2D)\) now proves (2).
Consequently coverage of arbitrary shrinking sequences still requires a growing prime, now in a range bounded by a power of the logarithm of the original degree.
