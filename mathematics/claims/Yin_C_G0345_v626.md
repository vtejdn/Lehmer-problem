# Large Fourier coefficients of full conductor at Salem periods

<a id="statement"></a>
## A power saving on an infinite sequence of periods

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number with monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d=2s+2\), let \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and write \(G_n=R/(\alpha^n-1)R\).
Give \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\alpha^j\) its law \(\mu_n\) on \(G_n\), with independent fair binary digits \(\xi_j\).
Put
\[
             \delta_d=\frac{1}{s(2s(d-1)-1)}.
\]
There is a constant \(C_P\) such that at infinitely many \(n\) a character \(\chi_n\) of \(G_n\) satisfies
\[
 \left|\widehat\mu_n(\chi_n)\right|\ge
        \exp\!\left(-C_P n^{1-2\delta_d}\right),
 \qquad
 \{z\in R:\chi_n(zy)=1\ \text{for every }y\in R\}=(\alpha^n-1)R.       \tag{1}
\]
Here a character on \(G_n\) is evaluated on elements of \(R\) through reduction.
The equality gives full conductor on the digit order, and \(\chi_n\) is nontrivial for all sufficiently large selected periods.
For degree four the exponent of \(n\) in (1) is \(3/5\).
The exponent depends only on the degree, while the constant and the periods may depend on \(P\).
The [periodic Fourier limit](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#slow) gives qualitative subexponential decay along a sequence with asymptotically full logarithmic conductor.
The estimate here concerns the same finite digit laws; it leaves the positive Mahler gap unresolved.

<a id="units"></a>
## Small neutral conjugates for units of the digit order

Let \(r=\alpha^{-1}\), and let \(Z\) be the set of the \(2s\) unit-circle conjugates.
For \(v\in K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and a root \(z\) write \(v_z\) for the image of \(v\) under the embedding taking \(\alpha\) to \(z\).
For every \(0<\varepsilon\le1\) we can choose \(u\in R^\times\), positive at both real embeddings, such that
\[
  \max_{z\in Z}|u_z|\ll_P\varepsilon,\qquad
  \max(|u_\alpha|,|u_r|)\ll_P\varepsilon^{-s}.                     \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the [integral approximation in the power basis](Yin_C_G0328_v1.md#polynomial-height-central-approximation) with \(2s\) neutral and two real coordinates supplies \(c\ne0\) in \(R\) at tolerance \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\).
Its neutral conjugates have modulus at most \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\), and its real conjugates have modulus at most \(C_P\varepsilon^{-s/2}\).
Thus \(1\le |N(c)|\le C_P^2\).
The additive index of \(cR\) in \(R\) equals this norm in absolute value by the determinant of multiplication.
There are only finitely many subgroups of bounded index in \(R\simeq\mathbb Z^d\), since each subgroup of index \(k\) contains \(kR\).
For each occurring principal ideal choose one nonzero generator \(v\).
The equality \(cR=vR\) makes \(c/v\) a unit of \(R\), also for this possibly nonmaximal order.
Taking \(u=(c/v)^2\) gives (2) with constants independent of \(\varepsilon\), as the chosen generators form a fixed finite set.

Choose one \(\zeta\) from each of the \(s\) conjugate pairs in \(Z\).
For arbitrarily large integers \(n\),
\[
                       \max_{z\in Z}|z^{n-1}-1|\ll_s n^{-1/s}.       \tag{3}
\]
To see this, partition the \(s\)-dimensional torus of angles into \(H^s\) cubes of side \(1/H\).
Two of the first \(H^s+1\) nonnegative multiples of the angle tuple lie in the same cube.
Their positive index difference \(q\le H^s\) satisfies \(\max_{z\in Z}|z^q-1|\le2\pi/H\).
These differences are unbounded as \(H\) grows, since no conjugate is a root of unity.
Taking \(n=q+1\) gives (3).
On this sequence all \(|z^n-1|\) are bounded away from zero eventually because \(z\ne1\) and \(z^n\to z\).

<a id="conductor"></a>
## A Fourier character of full conductor

Choose \(u\) as in (2) and put \(b=u/P'(\alpha)\), \(f=b/(\alpha-1)\).
The trace dual \(R^\vee=\{x\in K:\operatorname{Tr}(xR)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}\) is \(R/P'(\alpha)\), where the latter denotes a fractional ideal.
Indeed, Lagrange interpolation gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha^k/P'(\alpha))=0\) for \(0\le k<d-1\) and \(1\) for \(k=d-1\), by reading the leading coefficient of the interpolating polynomial.
For all larger \(k\) these traces are integers by the monic recurrence.
Pairing \(1,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}\) against \(1/P'(\alpha),\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}/P'(\alpha)\) therefore gives an integer matrix with ones on the antidiagonal and zeros before it in each row, hence determinant \(\pm1\).
This proves the trace-dual formula.

For \(x\in R\) use the character
\[
 \chi_n(x)=\exp\!\left(2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}\frac{b x}{\alpha^n-1}\right).
\]
Since \(b\in R^\vee\), this descends to \(G_n\).
Its conductor consists of the \(z\in R\) for which \(bz/(\alpha^n-1)\in R^\vee\).
As \(u\in R^\times\), this is precisely \((\alpha^n-1)R\).
Along (3), the absolute norm of \(\alpha^n-1\) grows at least as a positive constant times \(\alpha^n\), by its two real factors and the neutral lower bounds.
Thus the quotient has order greater than one eventually.
By multiplying the expectations for the independent digits, the absolute Fourier coefficient is \(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}|\cos(\pi t_j)|\) with \(t_j=\operatorname{Tr}(b\alpha^j/(\alpha^n-1))\).

<a id="ends"></a>
## Separation from half-integers at the ends of the product

Set \(m=\log\alpha\) and \(a=2s(d-1)\).
For the selected \(n\) take \(\varepsilon=A n^{-\delta_d}\) with a fixed \(A\ge1\) depending on \(P\), to be chosen below.
Take
\[
 J=\left\lceil s\log(1/\varepsilon)/m\right\rceil+J_0
\]
with fixed \(J_0\) sufficiently large depending on \(P\).
For sufficiently large \(n\) one has \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(2J<n\).
Since \(b\) and \(f\) are fixed multiples of \(u\), each obeys the modulus bounds (2) with constants depending only on \(P\).

For \(0\le j<J\) approximate \(t_j\) by \(L_j\), and for \(1\le l\le J\) approximate \(t_{n-l}\) by \(U_l\), where
\[
 L_j=-b_r r^j+\sum_{z\in Z} f_z z^j,\qquad
 U_l=b_\alpha\alpha^{-l}+\sum_{z\in Z}f_z z^{1-l}.
\]
Both errors are bounded by \(O_P(\varepsilon n^{-1/s}+\varepsilon^{-2s}\alpha^{-n})\).
For the initial neutral terms replace \(1/(z^n-1)\) by \(1/(z-1)\) using (3) and the lower bounds on the denominators.
For the terminal neutral terms the same argument replaces \(z^n/(z^n-1)\) by \(z/(z-1)\).
At the initial end the contracting denominator gives \(1/(r^n-1)=-1+O_P(\alpha^{-n})\), and the expanding term is \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s}\alpha^{J-n})\).
At the terminal end the expanding denominator gives \(\alpha^n/(\alpha^n-1)=1+O_P(\alpha^{-n})\), and the contracting term is \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s}\alpha^{J-n})\).
Here \(\alpha^J\ll_P\varepsilon^{-s}\).

The substitution \(\iota(\alpha)=r\) defines a nontrivial involution of \(K\), since \(\alpha\) and \(r\) generate the same field and are conjugate.
For every integer \(p\) define \(W_p=f_\alpha\alpha^p+f_r r^{p+1}\), an element of \(K\) under its real embedding at \(\alpha\).
The factorization of \(P\) into its two real factors and its conjugate pairs gives \(P'(\alpha)>0\) and \(P'(r)<0\).
Since \(\alpha-1>0>r-1\) and \(u\) is positive at both real embeddings, \(f_\alpha\) and \(f_r\) are positive.
Consequently \(W_p>0\), while \(\iota(W_p)=\alpha W_p\), so \(W_p\) is irrational.
Expanding the two traces gives
\[
 L_j=\operatorname{Tr}(f\alpha^j)-W_j,\qquad
 U_l=\operatorname{Tr}(f\alpha^{1-l})-W_{-l}.
\]
Thus each endpoint approximant \(V=L_j\) or \(U_l\) is irrational and belongs to the field of degree \(d\).

Choose a positive integer \(D_P\) with \(D_P/((\alpha-1)P'(\alpha))\in R\).
Then \(D_P f\in R\), so \(D_P V\) is an algebraic integer, using also that \(\alpha\) is a unit.
All conjugates of \(V\) are \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-2s})\).
Indeed the two trace expressions and the conjugates of \(W_p\) contain only products of a conjugate of \(f\) with a conjugate of \(\alpha\) to an exponent of absolute value at most \(J+1\).
For a nearest half-integer \(k+1/2\) to \(V\), the algebraic integer \(D_P(2V-2k-1)\) is nonzero.
Its other \(d-1\) conjugates are \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-2s})\), including the contribution of the rational integer \(2k+1\).
Taking its nonzero integer norm gives \(\operatorname{dist}(V,\mathbb Z+1/2)\gg_P\varepsilon^a\).

Because \(\delta_d(a-1)=1/s\), the ratio \(\varepsilon n^{-1/s}/\varepsilon^a=A^{1-a}\).
Choose \(A\) large enough that the first approximation error is at most one quarter of the separation bound.
The exponentially small error eventually has the same property.
Every endpoint \(t_j\) then has distance \(\gg_P\varepsilon^a\) from the half-integers.
The inequality \(|\cos(\pi t)|\ge2\operatorname{dist}(t,\mathbb Z+1/2)\) shows that the total negative log of the endpoint factors is \(O_P((\log n)^2)\).

<a id="middle"></a>
## The cost of the remaining factors

At \(J\le j<n-J\) the real denominators and (2) give
\[
 |t_j|\ll_P \varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-s}(\alpha^{-j}+\alpha^{j-n}).
\]
For the neutral terms use (3) and the bounded inverses of \(z^n-1\).
The choice of a sufficiently large \(J_0\) ensures that \(|t_j|\le1/4\) throughout the middle for large \(n\).
Here \(-\log|\cos(\pi t_j)|\ll t_j^2\).
Summing the squares of the two geometric tails starting at distance \(J\) from the ends costs \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-2s}\alpha^{-2J})=O_P(1)\).
The neutral contribution costs \(O_P(n\varepsilon^2)\).
Together with the endpoint bound this gives \(O_P(n^{1-2\delta_d}+(\log n)^2)\) for the whole negative logarithm.
Since \(0<2\delta_d\le2/5<1\), this proves (1).
