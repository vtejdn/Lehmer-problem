# Root-of-unity quotients in primitive specializations of a plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which roots can become equal after taking a power

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}]\) be absolutely irreducible in the Laurent ring and suppose the differences of its support span a rank-two lattice in \(\mathbb Z^2\).
For a primitive pair of integers \(v=(p,q)\) put \(P_v(X)=F(X^p,X^q)\) and
\[
             g_v=\gcd_{e,e'\in\operatorname{supp}F}|\langle e-e',v\rangle| .
\]
There is a constant \(N_F\) depending only on \(F\) such that the following statements hold when \(\max(|p|,|q|)>N_F\).
For every nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(P_v\), the roots of the same specialization whose quotient by \(\alpha\) is a root of unity are exactly
\[
                         \{\zeta\alpha:\zeta^{g_v}=1\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(g_v=1\), then \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for every integer \(j\ge1\).
For arbitrary \(g_v\) and any field \(K\subseteq\overline{\mathbb Q}\) one has
\[
             \bigcap_{j\ge1} K(\alpha^j)=K(\alpha^{g_v}).             \tag{2}
\]
This criterion includes quotients between roots belonging to different rational factors.
It extends the [power-field comparison for prime blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v203.md#twists) to every fixed absolutely irreducible plane Laurent polynomial of full support rank.

In the reciprocal five-term family put \(n>1\) odd and prime to three, \(0<k<n\) odd and prime to \(n\), and \(l=n-k\).
The three displayed power maps are injective on the set of distinct roots of
\[
               P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1
               \qquad\text{for }j=k,\ l,\ n,\quad x\longmapsto x^j . \tag{3}
\]
They preserve each root field for every admissible \(n,k\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\), every positive power has these properties simultaneously at every admissible offset.
These conclusions restrict changes of field under powering and leave the Mahler measures of these factors open.

<a id="curve"></a>
## Intersections with a translate

Multiply \(F\) by a Laurent monomial so that it is an ordinary polynomial divisible by neither coordinate, and write \(D\) for its total degree.
It is absolutely irreducible as an ordinary polynomial under this normalization.
For a root of unity \(\zeta\) set \(a=\zeta^p,\ b=\zeta^q,\ L=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
The polynomials \(F(U,V)\) and \(F(aU,bV)\) are proportional exactly when \(a^{e_1-e'_1}b^{e_2-e'_2}=1\) for every pair of support points \(e,e'\), or equivalently \(\zeta^{g_v}=1\).
In that case multiplication by \(\zeta\) carries every root to a root.

Otherwise the two curves have no common component.
Suppose that \(\alpha\) and \(\zeta\alpha\) are both roots and write \(u=\alpha^p,\ w=\alpha^q\).
The \(L\)-conjugates of \((u,w)\) are distinct intersection points, of which there are at most \(D^2\) by Bézout's theorem.
Since \(p,q\) are coprime, integer powers of \(u,w\) give \(\alpha\), and hence
\[
                      [L(\alpha):L]\le D^2 .                         \tag{4}
\]
The [relative lower bound for height over an abelian base](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) applied over \(L\) gives \(h(\alpha)\ge c_D>0\) for every such nontorsion \(\alpha\).
Here \(h\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(c_D\) is uniform in the cyclotomic field.

There is also a constant \(B_F\) for which \(h(u),h(w)\le B_F\) at all these intersections.
Indeed eliminate \(V\) by the resultant of the two normalized polynomials.
Over \(L[U]\) its value is a nonzero polynomial \(R(U)\) vanishing at \(u\).
The degrees in \(V\) are unchanged by the twist, and the resultant over \(L(U)\) is nonzero by coprimality.
Its coefficients are algebraic integers obtained by fixed integral polynomial expressions in \(a,b\).
Their sum of absolute values at each complex embedding is therefore at most a constant \(B\ge2\) depending only on \(F\).
Write \(c\ne0\) for its actual leading coefficient after specialization.
At each archimedean place the equation at \(u\) gives \(|c|\max(1,|u|)\le B\), using the lower coefficients when \(|u|>1\).
At each finite place the analogous left side is at most one by integrality and the ultrametric inequality.
Summing the logarithms with the normalized place weights and using the product formula for \(c\) gives \(h(u)\le\log B\).
Eliminating \(U\) gives the same bound for \(w\) after enlarging \(B\).
Since \(h(u)=|p|h(\alpha)\) and \(h(w)=|q|h(\alpha)\), an intersection of this kind requires \(\max(|p|,|q|)\le B_F/c_D\).
This proves (1), with no assumption on the size of either coordinate relative to the other.
The specialization cannot vanish identically since a monomially parametrized one-dimensional subtorus would then be contained in this irreducible rank-two curve.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Fields of the powers

For any \(j\ge1\) let \(\beta=\alpha^{g_v}\).
Every pair of conjugates of \(\beta\) comes from two conjugates of \(\alpha\).
If their \(j\)-th powers agree, the original roots have torsion quotient and (1) makes their \(g_v\)-th powers equal.
Separability therefore gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\beta^j)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\).
Adjoining \(K\) preserves the inclusion, and \(j=g_v\) attains equality in (2).

<a id="five"></a>
## The three power maps at every offset

All roots of \(P_l\) are units and [none is torsion](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts).
For \(u=x^k,\ w=x^n\) the equation becomes
\[
              F(u,w)=(u-2)w^2+uw+u(1-2u)=0.                          \tag{5}
\]
As a polynomial in \(w\) it is primitive over \(\mathbb C[u]\), and its discriminant \(u(8u^2-19u+8)\) has a simple zero at zero.
Thus it is absolutely irreducible.
The support points \((0,2),(1,2),(1,1)\) have differences generating \(\mathbb Z^2\), whence \(g_{(k,n)}=1\).
The all-powers conclusion for large \(n\) follows from (1).

First suppose distinct roots \(x,x'\) satisfy \(x^k=x'^k=u\).
Then \(x'/x\) and \(\xi=x'^n/x^n\) have the same odd order greater than one by coprimality.
The two distinct values \(w,w'\) solve (5), where \(u\ne2\) since \(u\) is a unit.
Their sum and product give
\[
           (2+\xi+\xi^{-1})^{-1}
             =\frac{ww'}{(w+w')^2}=5-2(u+u^{-1}).
\]
At any prime above two the right hand side reduces to one, so \(\bar\xi^2+\bar\xi+1=0\).
Reduction is injective on roots of unity of odd order.
Hence \(\xi\) has order three, giving \(2+\xi+\xi^{-1}=1\) and \(u+u^{-1}=2\).
This forces \(u=1\), contrary to nontorsion.

Next suppose \(x^n=x'^n=w\).
The two \(k\)-th powers are distinct by coprimality and their product is \(w^2\), by (5) regarded as a quadratic in \(u\).
Writing \(x'=\zeta x\) therefore gives \(x^{2(n-k)}=\zeta^k\), contrary to nontorsion.
Finally suppose \(x^l=x'^l\).
The identity \(x^n+x^{-n}=2(x^l+x^{-l})-1\) shows that \(x'^n\) is \(x^n\) or \(x^{-n}\).
In the first case the two roots agree by the preceding injectivity.
In the second, \(x^{2n}=(x'/x)^{-n}\) is torsion, again impossible.
This proves (3), and counting conjugates proves the field assertion.
