# Chamber norms, congruences and optimal scales in Salem inverse fibers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Norm spectra and favorable inverse constants

Fix a Salem minimal polynomial \(P(z)=z^mQ(z+z^{-1})\) of degree \(d=2m\ge4\), with exterior trace \(T>2\), neutral traces \(x_1<\cdots<x_h\), and \(h=m-1\).
Throughout, a Pisot inverse has the same degree as \(P\) and integer constant \(q=f(0)\ge2\).
Put
\[
 E=T-2,\quad H=Q/(X-T),\quad Z=H(T),\quad
 \mathfrak a=|H(-2)|,\quad D_Q=\operatorname{disc}Q.
\]
In the full order \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[T]\), let \(\mathcal C\) consist of the elements whose representatives \(R\) of degree at most \(h\) satisfy
\[
                     R(T)<0,\qquad R(x_i)H'(x_i)<0\quad(1\le i\le h).
\]
For an arbitrary integer \(M\ge1\) and coefficient residue polynomial \(W\in(\mathbb Z/M)[X]\) of formal degree at most \(h\), define
\[
 \mathcal C_{M,W}=\{R(T)\in\mathcal C:R\equiv W\pmod M\},\qquad
 \mu_{M,W}=\min_{\nu\in\mathcal C_{M,W}}|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\nu)|,
 \quad F=\mathbb Q(T).
\]
These sets are nonempty and their positive integer minima exist.
The congruence is coefficientwise in the unique power-basis representative.
When \(M=1\), write \(\mu(P)\) for the unconstrained minimum; it satisfies
\[
                         1\le\mu(P)\le |P(1)|D_Q.
\]

Every absolute norm occurring in \(\mathcal C_{M,W}\) is realized by infinitely many monic positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomials
\[
 f=P+(z-1)z^{m-1}R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad zf+f^*=(z+1)P,
\]
of the same degree \(d\), with \(R\equiv W\pmod M\) and constants \(q=f(0)\to\infty\).
Their unique exterior roots satisfy \(\delta=\theta-q\to E\), and their positive residues
\[
                           b_i=-R(x_i)/H'(x_i)
\]
can all be chosen comparable to \(q^{-1/h}\), with positive comparison constants depending on \(P,M,W\) and the chosen chamber element.
Conversely every actual Pisot inverse with this congruence supplies a norm in \(\mathcal C_{M,W}\).

Let \(\mathcal S_{P;M,W}\) be the sequences of these congruent inverses with \(q\to\infty\) and \(\delta\to E\), and put
\[
 \mathcal R(f)=|\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)|,\qquad
 \eta=\delta-\frac{E\theta}{\theta-1}.
\]
Then
\[
 \inf_{(f_k)\in\mathcal S_{P;M,W}}
       \liminf_k\frac{\mathcal R(f_k)}{q_k}
       =2\mathfrak a|P(1)|\mu_{M,W}^2,                           \tag{A}
\]
and the minimum on the right is attained as a limit along one such sequence.
Every sequence in \(\mathcal S_{P;M,W}\) satisfies
\[
 \eta>0,\qquad
 \liminf q^{1+1/h}\eta
                  \ge h\left(\frac{\mu_{M,W}Z}{D_Q}\right)^{1/h}>0. \tag{B}
\]
There also exist sequences in this class for which \(q^{1+1/h}\eta\) is bounded above.
Thus the exponent \(1+1/h\) is optimal, while exact attainment of the lower constant in (B) is asserted only when \(h=1\).
No higher-dimensional density of normalized residue shapes is assumed.

In the zero residue class, \(\mu_{M,0}=M^m\mu(P)\) and \(q\equiv1\pmod M\).
Choosing \(W=Q'\pmod M\) instead gives \(q\equiv1-m\pmod M\), without requiring this residue to be invertible modulo \(M\).
The degree-ten Lehmer polynomial has \(\mu(P)=1\), and its unconstrained value in (A) is \(2/(T+2)\).

Finally, for arbitrary prescribed positive functions \(F(d),\epsilon(d)\), and arbitrary congruence data \(M_P,W_P\) assigned to each Salem polynomial before choosing its inverse constant, the following are equivalent:

1. Salem numbers tend to one along a sequence.
2. There are same-degree Pisot inverses of Salem polynomials of strictly increasing even degrees \(d\), satisfying the assigned congruences and

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 q\ge F(d),\qquad \theta-q\longrightarrow0,\\
 q^{1-\epsilon(d)}<\mathcal R(f)<q^{1+\epsilon(d)},\\
 q^{-(1+\epsilon(d))/h}<b_i<q^{-(1-\epsilon(d))/h}
                         \quad(1\le i\le h).
 \end{gathered}                                                     \tag{C}
\]
The forward construction selects a sufficiently large constant separately for each supplied output.
It provides no upper bound for that constant and does not prescribe \(q=d^2\).

<a id="setup"></a>
## The fixed trace order and the inverse chamber

Fix an arbitrary Salem minimal polynomial
\[
 P(z)=z^mQ(z+z^{-1}),\qquad \deg P=2m\ge4.
\]
Write its distinct trace roots as
\[
 T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}>2,\qquad -2<x_1<\cdots<x_h<2,
 \qquad h=m-1,
\]
and put
\[
 E=T-2,\qquad H(X)=\frac{Q(X)}{X-T},\qquad
 Z=H(T)=Q'(T)>0,
 \qquad D_H=\operatorname{disc}H>0,
 \qquad D_Q=\operatorname{disc}Q=Z^2D_H.
\]
For \(h=1\), the discriminant of the monic linear polynomial \(H\) is one.
Also set
\[
                     \mathfrak a=|H(-2)|=\prod_i(2+x_i)>0.
\]

The trace field \(F=\mathbb Q(T)\) is totally real of degree \(m\).
Indeed the monic integral \(Q\) has degree \(m\), while the quadratic relation for \(\sigma\) over \(F\) and \([\mathbb Q(\sigma):\mathbb Q]=2m\) force \([F:\mathbb Q]=m\).
In particular
\[
                          \mathcal O=\mathbb Z[T]
\]
is a full order in \(F\), with \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(1,T,\ldots,T^h\).
Each element \(\nu\in\mathcal O\) has a unique representative \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree at most \(h\).
Its embeddings are \(R(T),R(x_1),\ldots,R(x_h)\).

Define the strict sign chamber
\[
 \mathcal C=\{R(T)\in\mathcal O:
       R(T)<0,\quad R(x_i)H'(x_i)<0\text{ for every }i\}.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
It is nonempty.
For example reduce \((2-X)Q'(X)\) modulo the monic \(Q\), and call the resulting integer polynomial \(R_0\).
At \(T\) its value is \((2-T)Q'(T)<0\), while
\[
 R_0(x_i)=(2-x_i)(x_i-T)H'(x_i)
\]
has the required opposite sign to \(H'(x_i)\).

Consequently the positive integer
\[
              \mu(P)=\min_{\nu\in\mathcal C}
                        |N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\nu)|                     \tag{2}
\]
exists.
The displayed element also gives
\[
                  1\le\mu(P)\le |P(1)|D_Q.                         \tag{3}
\]
For the upper bound use the product of \((2-y)Q'(y)\) over all roots \(y\) of \(Q\), together with \(Q(2)=P(1)\) and the discriminant formula.
The equality \(\mu(P)=1\) holds precisely when this chamber contains an order unit.

<a id="simplex"></a>
## Exact quantities for an actual inverse

The [full inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) writes every Pisot inverse of constant \(q\ge2\) as
\[
 f=P+(z-1)z^{m-1}R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
 R=(1-q)H-\sum_{i=1}^h b_i\frac{H}{X-x_i},                         \tag{4}
\]
where \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\) and
\[
 b_i=-\frac{R(x_i)}{H'(x_i)}>0,\qquad
 c_i=\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}>0,\qquad
                         \sum_i c_i<q+T/2.                         \tag{5}
\]
These inequalities are necessary and sufficient for a Pisot minimal polynomial of exactly degree \(2m\) returning \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).
Equation (4) gives
\[
 |R(T)|=Z\left(q-1+\sum_i\frac{b_i}{T-x_i}\right).                  \tag{6}
\]
Thus every actual inverse supplies an element of \(\mathcal C\).

The [reciprocal-resultant calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v296.md#fiber) gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 n:=|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(R(T))|
      =|R(T)|D_H\prod_i b_i,\\
 f(-1)=\mathfrak a\left(2q+T-2\sum_i c_i\right)>0,\\
 \mathcal R(f):=|\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)|
                =f(-1)|P(1)|n^2.                                 \tag{7}
 \end{gathered}
\]
For the first identity, \(R(x_i)=-b_iH'(x_i)\) and \(\prod_i|H'(x_i)|=D_H\).
For the second, substitute \(-2\) into (4) and use \(H(-2)=(-1)^h\mathfrak a\).
For the third, evaluation of \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\) on the roots of the monic \(f\) gives
\(\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)=f(-1)\operatorname{Res}(f,P)\).
Evaluation of (4) on the roots of \(P\), whose trace roots each occur twice, gives
\(\operatorname{Res}(P,f)=P(1)\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)^2\).
Both polynomials have even degree, so the two orders of the last resultant agree, and \(\operatorname{Res}(Q,R)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(R(T))\).
Here \(P(1)=(2-T)H(2)<0\), which explains the absolute value in (7).

Let \(\theta>q\) be the Pisot root, \(\delta=\theta-q\), and \(X_\theta=\theta+\theta^{-1}\).
The [exact excess identity](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) is
\[
 \delta=\frac{E\theta}{\theta-1}+\eta,
 \qquad
 \eta=\sum_i\frac{b_i}{X_\theta-x_i}>0.                             \tag{8}
\]
The two-point bound at the same source gives \(\delta<T+2\), uniformly over this fixed fiber, so \(\theta=q+O_P(1)\).
Along a sequence with \(\delta\to E\), necessarily \(q\to\infty\): otherwise the strict lower term \(E/(\theta-1)\) in (8) stays bounded away from zero.
Every denominator in (8) is then \(q+O_P(1)\).
It follows that
\[
 \delta\to E
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_i b_i=o(q),
 \qquad
 \frac{|R(T)|}{q}\to Z,
 \qquad
 \frac{f(-1)}q\to2\mathfrak a.                                    \tag{9}
\]
The equivalence is understood along \(q\to\infty\); all root gaps in this paragraph are fixed positive numbers depending on \(P\).

<a id="unit-orbit"></a>
## Every chamber norm is realized infinitely often

Use the accepted [order-level logarithmic unit theorem](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U001.md#con26-u001).
For the full order \(\mathcal O\), the vectors
\[
 \lambda(\varepsilon)=
       (\log|\varepsilon(T)|,\log|\varepsilon(x_1)|,
                         \ldots,\log|\varepsilon(x_h)|)
\]
of its units form a full lattice in \(\sum_{i=0}^h\lambda_i=0\).
The logarithms of the totally positive units also form a full lattice: they contain the logarithms of all squared units, which give twice the original lattice, and they are a subgroup of the original lattice.
Totally positive units have norm one.

Fix any \(\nu\in\mathcal C\), with absolute norm \(n\).
A bounded fundamental parallelepiped of this positive-unit lattice supplies, for each sufficiently large real \(u\), a totally positive unit \(\varepsilon_u\) whose logarithmic vector differs by a bounded amount from
\[
                              (hu,-u,\ldots,-u).                    \tag{10}
\]
Represent \(\nu\varepsilon_u\) by its unique integer polynomial \(R_u\) of degree at most \(h\).
It remains in the same strict chamber, its absolute norm is exactly \(n\), and
\[
 |R_u(T)|\asymp_{P,\nu}e^{hu},\qquad
 |R_u(x_i)|\asymp_{P,\nu}e^{-u},\qquad
 b_{u,i}\asymp_{P,\nu}e^{-u}.                                      \tag{11}
\]
The constants also incorporate a fixed covering radius for the chosen order-unit lattice.

The leading coefficient in Lagrange interpolation at all the roots of \(Q\) is
\[
 \operatorname{lc}_h R_u
  =\frac{R_u(T)}{Z}
       +\sum_i\frac{R_u(x_i)}{Q'(x_i)}
  =-\frac{|R_u(T)|}{Z}
       +\sum_i\frac{b_{u,i}}{T-x_i}.                               \tag{12}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{lc}_h\) means the coefficient of \(X^h\), including when a polynomial has smaller degree.
Set
\[
                         q_u=1-\operatorname{lc}_h R_u.
\]
This is an integer, and (11)--(12) give
\[
                   q_u\sim |R_u(T)|/Z\asymp e^{hu}\to\infty.
\]
In particular \(R_u\) eventually has degree exactly \(h\) and \(q_u\ge2\).
Moreover \(\sum_i c_{u,i}=O(e^{-u})\), so the strict simplex inequality (5) holds eventually.
The inverse (4) is therefore an actual Pisot minimal polynomial of degree \(2m\), returns exactly the prescribed \(P\), and satisfies
\[
 \delta_u\to E,\qquad
 |N(R_u(T))|=n,
 \qquad
 b_{u,i}\asymp q_u^{-1/h}.                                         \tag{13}
\]
Since \(q_u\) is unbounded, this realizes the norm \(n\) at infinitely many distinct constants, after taking a subsequence if necessary.
Together with the converse chamber inclusion following (6), this proves that the absolute norms occurring in the full Pisot inverse fiber are exactly the absolute norms in \(\mathcal C\).

The external input used here is the complete real logarithmic lattice for an arbitrary order, as recorded from Conrad's Theorem 1.1 and Section 3, especially the compactness and full-lattice conclusion.
The passage from that lattice to (10), the sign preservation, the interpolation, and the strict inverse test are local deductions above.
The accepted source supplies no degree-uniform covering radius, effective regulator bound, or prescribed values of \(q_u\).

<a id="congruences"></a>
## Prescribing any coefficient residue class

Fix \(M\ge1\) and a residue polynomial \(W\) of formal degree at most \(h\), before choosing the constants of the inverses.
Lift \(W\) to an integer polynomial \(\widetilde W\) of degree at most \(h\).
The chamber polynomial \(R_0\) constructed above has strictly correct signs at every embedding.
Thus
\[
                         \widetilde W+kMR_0
\]
lies in the strict chamber for every sufficiently large positive integer \(k\).
It represents an element of \(\mathcal C_{M,W}\), proving nonemptiness and the existence of its integer norm minimum \(\mu_{M,W}\).

For \(M>1\), reduce the totally positive unit group into the finite group \((\mathcal O/M\mathcal O)^\times\).
The kernel
\[
 \Gamma_M=\{\varepsilon\in\mathcal O^\times:
       \varepsilon\text{ totally positive},\quad
       \varepsilon\equiv1\pmod{M\mathcal O}\}
\]
has finite index.
Its logarithms therefore still form a full lattice in the same hyperplane.
For \(M=1\), take \(\Gamma_M\) to be the whole totally positive unit group.
Apply (10)--(13) using this lattice and any \(\nu\in\mathcal C_{M,W}\).
Every product \(\nu\varepsilon_u\) remains congruent to \(\nu\) modulo \(M\mathcal O\).
Since \(1,T,\ldots,T^h\) is the \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of the order, its unique representative satisfies
\[
                 R_u\equiv W\pmod M,
 \qquad q_u\equiv1-[X^h]W\pmod M.                                  \tag{14}
\]
The norm, sign, residue, and strict Pisot conclusions of the unit construction are unchanged.
This proves every asserted realization within a prescribed congruence class, including realization of its minimum norm.
The covering radius and the selected norm may depend on \(M,W\); they need not equal those of an unconstrained minimizing orbit.

Two choices illustrate the exact quantifiers.
If \(W=0\), multiplication by the positive integer \(M\) is a bijection from \(\mathcal C\) to \(\mathcal C\cap M\mathcal O\), with norms multiplied by \(M^m\).
Hence
\[
                       \mu_{M,0}=M^m\mu(P),\qquad q_u\equiv1\pmod M.
                                                                    \tag{15}
\]
If \(W=Q'\pmod M\), then \([X^h]Q'=m\), giving \(q_u\equiv1-m\pmod M\).
Although \(Q'(T)>0\) prevents the exact element \(Q'(T)\) from lying in this chamber, its residue class has the chamber lift just constructed.
No coprimality condition on the resulting constant residue is needed.
Both choices, or any other residue polynomial, may be assigned separately to each supplied polynomial before choosing \(q_u\).

<a id="resultant-minimum"></a>
## The exact normalized resultant minimum

Let \(\mathcal S_{P;M,W}\) be the collection of sequences of actual inverses of this fixed \(P\), satisfying \(R\equiv W\pmod M\), with \(q\to\infty\) and \(\delta\to E\).
The congruence-restricted unit construction shows that this collection is nonempty.
The chamber minimum, (7), and (9) imply
\[
 \boxed{
 \inf_{(f_k)\in\mathcal S_{P;M,W}}
       \liminf_k\frac{\mathcal R(f_k)}{q_k}
          =2\mathfrak a\,|P(1)|\,\mu_{M,W}^2.}
                                                                    \tag{16}
\]
For the lower bound, \(n_k\ge\mu_{M,W}\) and \(f_k(-1)/q_k\to2\mathfrak a\).
For equality, choose a chamber element attaining \(\mu_{M,W}\), and use its balanced orbit under \(\Gamma_M\).
On this sequence the normalized resultants converge to the displayed value.
More generally every norm \(n\) in this congruence chamber is realized with the exact limit \(2\mathfrak a|P(1)|n^2\).

One may also write the constant as
\[
          \frac{2P(-1)|P(1)|}{T+2}\,\mu_{M,W}^2,                  \tag{17}
\]
because \(P(-1)=(T+2)\mathfrak a>0\).
This minimization concerns the near-optimal portion \(\delta\to E\) of the full fixed-output fiber; it makes no assertion at a prescribed integer constant.

## The optimal power for the remaining excess

The exact subtraction in (8) is useful: set
\[
                    \eta=\delta-\frac{E\theta}{\theta-1}.
\]
For any actual inverse, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality applied to the positive summands in (8), together with (7), gives
\[
 \eta\ge h\left(
       \frac{n}{|R(T)|D_H H(X_\theta)}\right)^{1/h}.                 \tag{18}
\]
Along any sequence in \(\mathcal S_{P;M,W}\), equation (9) gives \(|R(T)|/q\to Z\), and \(H(X_\theta)/q^h\to1\).
Thus
\[
 \liminf q^{1+1/h}\eta
      \ge h\left(\frac{\mu_{M,W}}{Z D_H}\right)^{1/h}
       =h\left(\frac{\mu_{M,W}Z}{D_Q}\right)^{1/h}>0.                \tag{19}
\]
On a balanced unit orbit under \(\Gamma_M\) with any fixed norm in \(\mathcal C_{M,W}\), (13) and the denominators \(q+O_P(1)\) in (8) give
\[
                 0<\liminf q^{1+1/h}\eta
                    \le\limsup q^{1+1/h}\eta<\infty.               \tag{20}
\]
Consequently the power \(q^{-1-1/h}\) is attained up to fixed multiplicative constants and cannot be replaced by a smaller asymptotic order over all favorable sequences in the fixed fiber.
This is a refinement after removing the exact positive term \(E\theta/(\theta-1)\), not merely after subtracting \(E+E/q\).

The lower constant in (19) has not in general been proved attainable.
To describe the missing condition, fix an element \(\nu\) of norm \(n\), write \(\nu_0,\nu_i\) for its embeddings, and let \(\lambda\) be a positive-unit logarithmic vector.
Along orbits with the required growth and bounded normalized residue shape,
\[
 \log(q^{1/h}b_i)
   =\log\frac{|\nu_i|}{|H'(x_i)|}
       +\frac1h\log\frac{|\nu_0|}{Z}
       +\lambda_i+\frac{\lambda_0}{h}+o(1).                        \tag{21}
\]
The sum of these coordinates tends to \(\log(n/(ZD_H))\).
Equality in the asymptotic arithmetic-geometric mean bound would require all coordinates in (21) to approach the same value.
Bounded covering distance to (10) ensures bounded coordinates, but does not itself prove that specific approximation in the projected unit lattice.
For \(h=1\) there is only one coordinate, so every fixed-norm balanced orbit does attain the constant \(n/Z\) in (19).
No stronger shape-density theorem is assumed for \(h>1\).

## An exact minimum-norm example for Lehmer's polynomial

For the accepted [degree-ten Lehmer benchmark](Yin_C_G0345_v291.md#benchmark-proof),
\[
 Q(X)=X^5+X^4-5X^3-5X^2+4X+3.
\]
Let
\[
 \alpha=(-1-\sqrt5)/2,\qquad \gamma=(-1+\sqrt5)/2,
 \qquad V(X)=-(X+1)(X^2+X-1).
\]
The exact intervals in that benchmark place \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) respectively in
\[
 (-2,\alpha),\quad(\alpha,-1),\quad(-1,\gamma),\quad(\gamma,2).
\]
Thus \(V(x_i)\) has signs \(+,-,+,-\), opposite to the signs of \(H'(x_i)\), and \(V(T)<0\).
So \(V(T)\in\mathcal C\).
Its absolute norm is one, without a numerical root or unit search:
\[
 |N(T+1)|=|Q(-1)|=1,
\]
and the remainder \(Q(X)\equiv X+2\pmod{X^2+X-1}\) gives
\[
 |N(T^2+T-1)|
   =|Q(\alpha)Q(\gamma)|
   =(\alpha+2)(\gamma+2)=1.
\]
Hence \(\mu(P)=1\).
The representative \(V\) itself has coefficient of \(X^4\) zero and so does not supply an admissible constant \(q\ge2\); the balanced unit orbit is what supplies the actual inverses.

For this polynomial \(|P(1)|=P(-1)=1\), so the unconstrained case \(M=1\) of (16) has the exact value
\[
                            \frac{2}{T+2}.                         \tag{22}
\]
Here \(h=4\), and the third-order lower bound is \(4(Z/D_Q)^{1/4}\), with its power attained up to fixed multiplicative constants as above.
The scalar value (22) is attained as a limit along same-output, same-degree inverses whose constants tend to infinity.

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## A restricted input class sufficient for the Salem gap question

Let \(F(d)>0\) and \(\epsilon(d)>0\) be arbitrary prescribed functions of the even degree.
For each Salem polynomial \(P\), independently assign a positive integer modulus \(M_P\) and a polynomial \(W_P\) over \(\mathbb Z/M_P\) of formal degree at most \(\deg(P)/2-1\).
These assignments precede the choice of inverse constants and may depend on the whole supplied polynomial.
If Salem numbers tend to one, there are monic positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) of strictly increasing even degrees \(d\), with constants \(q\ge F(d)\), returning the corresponding same-degree Salem polynomials and satisfying the assigned congruences, such that
\[
 \theta-q\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 q^{1-\epsilon(d)}<|\operatorname{Res}(f,f^*)|<q^{1+\epsilon(d)} .
\]
In addition one may require \(q^{-(1+\epsilon(d))/h}<b_i<q^{-(1-\epsilon(d))/h}\) for every neutral residue of the corresponding inverse, with \(h=d/2-1\).

Indeed choose a sequence of supplied Salem polynomials approaching one in strictly increasing degrees.
This extraction is possible because bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound every coefficient of a monic integer polynomial, leaving only finitely many possibilities at bounded degree.
At each fixed polynomial first choose a chamber element in \(\mathcal C_{M_P,W_P}\) and one of its congruence-restricted orbits.
As \(q\to\infty\) on that orbit, \(\mathcal R(f)/q\) tends to the positive finite number \(2\mathfrak a|P(1)|n^2\), and each \(q^{1/h}b_i\) stays between two strictly positive constants.
The constants may depend arbitrarily on that polynomial, its assigned congruence data, and the chosen orbit.
Since \(\epsilon(d)\) is already fixed at this degree, choose one sufficiently large orbit member satisfying both power windows, the stated lower bound on \(q\), and \(\delta-E<1/d\).
This diagonal choice proves the assertion.
Conversely, (8) implies \(0<T-2<\delta\) for every member of this restricted class.
Thus excess tending to zero forces the corresponding Salem numbers to tend to one.
Excluding such sequences would therefore suffice for a uniform Salem gap.
The argument permits an arbitrarily large lower threshold for \(q\) and selects its value separately for each output.
It gives no upper threshold for \(q\), and a Salem gap would still leave reciprocal units with several exterior pairs to treat for G0001.

The [congruence construction](#congruences) permits either \(R\equiv Q'\) or \(R\equiv0\) modulo an arbitrarily chosen modulus at each degree, as well as every other assigned residue polynomial.
It can use the minimum norm within that residue class, but does not equate that minimum with the unconstrained \(\mu(P)\).
There is no uniform bound on the modulus, the chamber representative, or the size of the selected inverse constant in this diagonal statement.

## What this changes and what remains supplied

The [fixed-output inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#optimum) already gives the limiting excess \(E\), and the [full simplex result](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) gives the sharp second-order lower limit by making positive residues tend to zero along favorable constants.
The [least-excess envelope](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v33.md#least-excess) already connects these fixed-output inverses to the unrestricted near-one question by a diagonal choice of constants.
The additional information here is exact preservation and realization of every chamber norm, including every assigned coefficient residue class, the minimum (16), the power bound (19)--(20), and the diagonal equivalence with these additional constraints.
The [low-degree residue-representative bound](Yin_C_G0345_v33.md#statement) concerns a different normalization and degree window; no low degree or bounded height of the representatives \(R_u\) beyond reduction modulo \(Q\) is asserted here.

The construction begins with the entire Salem polynomial and retains its field and output throughout.
The chamber is always nonempty, so its mere existence provides no new obstruction to a small Salem measure.
The sign class of its units, the value \(\mu(P)\), and the projected logarithmic shapes in (21) are concrete additional quantities to investigate as the supplied polynomial varies.
The Lehmer example shows that the required sign class can already contain a unit.
None of the current arguments controls these quantities, the unit-lattice covering radius, or the required constants uniformly as the degree varies.
In particular they do not put these near-minimal resultant orbits at \(q=d^2\), and they neither construct Salem numbers tending to one nor rule them out.

The chamber-unit direction, the diagonal selection and the exact Lehmer sign witness were developed with the coordinating agent.
The norm minimum, the residual-excess estimates and the congruence-class extensions are collaborating author deductions.
