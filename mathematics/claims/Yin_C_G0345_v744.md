# Repeated Frobenius powers at one prime and the height of a set of conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A determinant for several powers of the same prime

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero algebraic number of infinite order, of absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\).
In its set of conjugates choose a nonempty subset \(A\) of size \(r\).
Fix any rational prime \(p\) and an integer \(J\ge2\), and suppose the \(p^{J-1}\)-th powers of the elements of \(A\) are distinct.
This condition holds for every \(J,p,A\) in the orbit of a Salem number.
Choose a uniform prime \(P\) of the splitting field \(L\) over \(p\), and, for each \(k\ge1\), a uniform lift \(y_k\) at \(P\) of \(z\mapsto z^{p^k}\) on its residue field.
Write \(\xi_k=r^{-1}\mathbb E|A\cap y_k A|\).
Then
\[
 (\log p)\sum_{k=1}^{J-1}\binom{J-k+1}{2}\xi_k
 \ \le\ \frac J2\log(rJ)+(rJ-1)\frac{p^J-1}{p-1}\,h .              \tag{1}
\]
All probabilities are taken from the full inertia cosets, including at ramified primes.
Both finite and infinite places enter \(h\).

The weights in (1) sum to \(\binom{J+1}{3}\).
If the corresponding weighted mean of the \(\xi_k\) is at least \(\eta>0\), then
\[
 rh\ \ge\ \frac{p-1}{p^J-1}
 \left[\frac{\eta\log p}{6}(J^2-1)-\frac12\log(rJ)\right]_+ .       \tag{2}
\]
Here \([x]_+=\max(x,0)\).
For fixed \(p,\eta\) and \(\delta>0\), take
\[
 J=\left\lceil(1+\delta)\sqrt{\frac{3\log r}{\eta\log p}}\right\rceil .
                                                                        \tag{3}
\]
When the hypotheses hold for this length and \(r\) is sufficiently large, (2) gives \(rh\gg_{p,\eta,\delta} J^2p^{-J}\).
In particular if these hypotheses hold along a sequence \(r\to\infty\) for every fixed \(\delta>0\) and all sufficiently large \(r\) depending on \(\delta\), then
\[
        \liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log(rh)}{\sqrt{\log r}}
                          \ \ge\ -\sqrt{\frac{3\log p}{\eta}}.         \tag{4}
\]
Thus a single prime can constrain a small set of conjugates at a loss of order \(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log r})\) in \(rh\).
The [prime interval estimate for subset overlap](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#interval) gives a stronger logarithmic bound when its simultaneous hypotheses on distinct primes hold.

<a id="valuation"></a>
## Valuation gained at successive powers

Fix \(0\le i<j<J\), two elements \(a,b\in A\), and a prime \(P\mid p\).
Put \(k=j-i\), normalize \(v_P(p)=1\), and denote the ramification index at \(P\cap\mathbb Q(b)\) by \(e\).
The [pair calculation for one Frobenius power](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#edges) gives the following two facts.
If any lift sends \(b\) to \(a\), the conditional probability of that event at \(P\) is \(1/e\), and when \(|b|_P\le1\) one has \(v_P(a-b^{p^k})\ge1/e\).
The probability follows by uniformity on the inertia orbit, and the valuation follows by lifting the residue of \(b\) to its unramified representative.
These two arguments work also at \(p=2\).

For integral \(x,z\) with \(t=v_P(x-z)\ge1/e\), the binomial theorem gives
\[
             v_P(x^p-z^p)\ \ge\ \min(1+t,pt)\ \ge\ t+1/e .
\]
Indeed all the intermediate binomial coefficients are divisible by \(p\), and \((p-1)t\ge1/e\).
Starting with \(x=a,\ z=b^{p^k}\), each of \(i\) successive applications therefore adds at least \(1/e\).
At a hit both elements have the same integrality behavior since a lift preserves the valuation.
For \(|b|_P>1\), apply the same proof with \(a^{-1},b^{-1}\), which have the same hit and ramification index.
In that case the absolute difference of the powered reciprocals equals the normalized difference
\[
 D_{ij}(a,b)=
 \frac{|a^{p^i}-b^{p^j}|_P}
      {\max(1,|a^{p^i}|_P)\max(1,|b^{p^j}|_P)}.
\]
In the integral case this is the ordinary absolute difference.
It is at most one at every finite place by the ultrametric inequality, whether a lift sends \(b\) to \(a\) or not.
It follows that at fixed \(P\)
\[
 -\log D_{ij}(a,b)\ \ge\ (i+1)(\log p)\,\mathbb P(y_kb=a\mid P).
\]
Here the local valuation is used once per ordered pair at these levels, even if several lifts realize its hit or other conjugates have the same residue.
Summing over \(a,b\) and averaging over \(P\) gives at least \(r(i+1)(\log p)\xi_{j-i}\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Global height charge and choice of the length

Use one ordinary Vandermonde row at each \(a^{p^i}\), with \(a\in A\) and \(0\le i<J\).
Injectivity of the last power gives it at every earlier level.
The distinct positive heights \(p^ih\) separate different levels, so the determinant on the \(S=rJ\) nodes is nonzero.
For a Salem number, if two conjugates had equal positive powers, moving the first to the unique conjugate of modulus greater than one would give a second such conjugate.
Thus the injectivity requirement is automatic there.

At every finite place, dividing each difference by its two local maxima uses exactly \(S-1\) factors of the maximum per node.
In the resulting product each unordered pair of nodes on different levels has a unique lower level \(i\), and its preceding valuation contribution can be counted once.
No independence between Frobenius lifts for different \(k\) is needed.
The normalized place weights over \(p\) are uniform since \(L/\mathbb Q\) is Galois.
At infinity a row for \(z\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt S\max(1,|z|)^{S-1}\).
Hadamard and the product formula give an archimedean charge of \((S/2)\log S\) and a total height charge of \((S-1)r\sum_{i=0}^{J-1}p^ih\), as in the [global Vandermonde estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#local).
For a fixed difference \(k\), the sum of the factors \(i+1\) over \(0\le i<J-k\) equals \(\binom{J-k+1}{2}\).
Division by \(r\) yields (1).
Summing these weights, dividing by \(J\) and using \((rJ-1)/J\le r\) gives (2).
With (3), the bracket in (2) is at least a positive multiple of \(J^2\) for all sufficiently large \(r\), because \(\log J=o(\log r)\) and \((1+\delta)^2>1\).
Taking logarithms and then letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves (4).

<a id="salem"></a>
## Primitive Salem trace fields specified at a fixed prime

Let \(p=29\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(n\) there is a totally real field \(F_n\) of degree \(n^3\) without a proper nontrivial subfield, realized as the trace field of a Salem number, such that every Salem number \(\alpha\) with this trace field satisfies
\[
        \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log((\log\alpha)/n)}{\sqrt{\log n}}
                                  \ \ge\ -6\sqrt{\log 29}.             \tag{5}
\]
The limit is along any such sequence of prime indices and choices of Salem generators.
Use the [primitive product field construction](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#product) with the single test prime \(29\equiv1\bmod7\).
Its three additional split primes serve only to force the full Galois group and may vary with \(n\).
That construction permits any finite test set of split primes by the same simultaneous approximation.
It gives the group \(S_n^3\rtimes C_3\) acting on triples and unramified trace Frobenius conjugate to \((c,c',1)\) at \(29\), where \(c,c'\) are \(n\)-cycles.
The [realization by a Salem trace generator](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#salem) then applies to the field.

Use the triangular subset \(\mathcal A=\{(i,j,1):1\le i\le j\le n\}\) of trace embeddings, of size \(u=n(n+1)/2\).
For any Salem generator take \(A\) to contain both inverse conjugates over every member of \(\mathcal A\), giving \(r=2u\).
Full lift cosets upstairs project to the fixed Frobenius power at each restricted trace prime, even if the full splitting field of \(\alpha\) ramifies.
The trace primes are uniform under restriction, and an automorphism takes each whole inverse pair onto its image pair, whatever its orientation on the two members.
The \(\xi_k\) are therefore exactly the normalized overlaps in the trace action.
If \(n\mid k\), every trace embedding is fixed and \(\xi_k=1\).
Otherwise primality of \(n\) preserves the two independent uniform \(n\)-cycles conditional on the choice of the fixed coordinate.
Unless the third coordinate is fixed, the image of \(\mathcal A\) is disjoint from itself.
When it is fixed, each image avoids its original row and column and is uniform over the remaining \((n-1)^2\) possibilities.
Over all \(u\) sources the number of allowed destinations inside the triangle is \(u^2-2\sum_{j=1}^n j^2+u\), by the row and column sizes and inclusion-exclusion.
Thus in this case of \(n\nmid k\) one has
\[
       \xi_k=\frac{u^2-2\sum_{j=1}^n j^2+u}{3u(n-1)^2}
                    =\frac{3n-2}{18(n-1)}\ \ge\ \frac16 .
\]
The triangle has no transitive setwise stabilizer, and only partial overlaps contribute for \(n\nmid k\).
Every length therefore meets the mean test with \(\eta=1/6\).
Now \(h=(\log\alpha)/(2n^3)\) and \(\log r=2\log n+o(1)\).
Substitution in (4) proves (5).
This gives growth at a single specified prime, without a simultaneous density condition in a moving interval.
The proof does not force the displayed overlap pattern in general Salem trace fields.
