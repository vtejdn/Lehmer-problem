# Heights of rational expressions in several distinct conjugate traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform height floor for moving rational maps

Fix a positive integer \(t\) and a real \(M>0\).
Consider compressed reciprocal nontorsion units of degrees \(d\to\infty\) with logarithmic Mahler measures at most \(M\), and the [alternating blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) of their inverse pairs, with \(b\) labels per block and \(u=d/(2b)\) blocks.
Set \(E=((2M+u(\log(2b)+4))/b)^{1/2}\) and \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Choose any \(t\) distinct labels with traces \(v_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\).
Let \(R=A/B\) be a reduced nonconstant rational function over \(\mathbb Q\) depending on every one of its \(t\) variables, with numerator and denominator of degree at most \(D\ge1\) in each variable.
Uniformly over the coefficients, the labels and their block assignments, whenever \(DE\to0\), the value \(y=R(v_1,\ldots,v_t)\) is eventually defined and satisfies
\[
 [\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\gg_t b^t,\qquad
 h(y)\ge \tfrac12\log\varphi-O_t(DE),\qquad m(y)\gg_t b^t.
\]
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(m(y)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive integral minimal polynomial, including its leading coefficient.
For example the cross-ratio of four distinct traces has measure \(\gg b^4\), regardless of which traces are chosen.
The [single-trace bound](Yin_C_G0345_v113.md#statement) permits larger degrees for one trace, while the [polynomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v546.md#statement) also detects growth of the coefficients through the supremum of the polynomial.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The degree of the image

The [interpolation argument on alternating blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v546.md#degree) shows that a rational polynomial in at most \(2t\) distinct named traces with individual degrees at most \(2D\) cannot vanish at those traces unless it is identically zero, provided \(b\ge2t+2\) and \(2D\le b-2t\).
These inequalities hold eventually, since \(u\) is at most polylogarithmic in \(d\) and \(DE\to0\).
Applying interpolation to \(B\) and to \(A-cB\) for \(c=0,1,-1\) ensures that the value is finite and different from all three constants.
For every label permutation in the Galois image, apply interpolation to the difference after clearing the two denominators.
It follows that the formal rational function and its evaluated value have exactly the same stabilizer.
If \(t_J\) of the variables lie in block \(J\), independent even permutations furnish at least \(\prod_J\binom b{t_J}\gg_t b^t\) formal supports, all giving distinct functions because each chosen variable is essential.
This proves the degree bound.

<a id="slices"></a>
## Comparing actual roots and their phases in one coordinate

Choose a uniform element of the label Galois group and apply it to the ordered tuple of labels.
Independently choose one of the two roots over each image label with equal probabilities, writing the chosen root as \(z_i\) and its phase as \(z_i^*=z_i/|z_i|\).
The resulting traces are the images of the original traces under uniform embeddings, since both orientations have the same trace.
Condition first on the coset of the product of the alternating groups, and then on the labels and orientations of all but one coordinate.
The remaining root is uniform among the roots of its assigned block except for the two roots over each already occupied label.
Indeed the assignments of distinct labels in each block extend to even permutations with the same number of extensions, as at least two unused labels remain.
The conditioning increases the expectation of any nonnegative single-root test over the full block by at most a factor of two at sufficiently large degree.

For any disk of radius \(3E\) about any complex center, the conditional probability that \(z_i\) lies in that disk is \(O(E)\), uniformly in the center.
Use a compact cutoff equal to one on that disk, decreasing linearly to zero at distance \(4E\) from the center.
Its Dirichlet integral is bounded, its Lipschitz constant is \(1/E\), and its Haar mean is \(O(E)\) since any such disk meets the unit circle in an arc of length \(O(E)\).
The [block energy bound](Yin_C_G0345_v539.md#energy) at smoothing radius \(1/(2b)\) gives discrepancy \(O(E+1/(bE))=O(E)\).
The conditional expectation of \(\lvert\log|z_i|\rvert\) is at most \(2M/b\), since the sum over all roots is at most \(2M\).
In particular \(\Pr(\lvert\log|z_i|\rvert>E/2)\ll M/(bE)\ll E\).

Fix the other coordinates, taking any prescribed mix of their actual values and phases, and apply \(z\mapsto z+1/z\) in every coordinate before evaluating \(R\).
In the moving coordinate the resulting rational map \(W\) of the sphere has degree at most \(2D\) after multiplying numerator and denominator by \(z^D\) and cancelling common factors.
To ensure all mixed tuples give well-defined values throughout this comparison, replace \(B\) temporarily by \(B+\epsilon\) with arbitrarily small real \(\epsilon\) avoiding the finitely many forbidden values for every tuple of actual roots and phases of this seed.
This also ensures no sliced denominator is the zero polynomial.
The analytic estimates below are independent of \(\epsilon\), and the genuine denominators at all the original conjugate tuples are nonzero by interpolation.

Use chordal distance \(\rho\) on the sphere, with maximum one.
Constant slices give displacement zero.
For any nonconstant slice there are at most \(4D\) finite zeros and poles counted with multiplicity.
Discard roots with \(\lvert\log|z_i|\rvert>E/2\), and roots in the disks of radius \(3E\) about those zeros and poles.
The conditional probability discarded is \(O(DE)\).
On the remaining radial segment from \(z_i\) to \(z_i^*\), its length is at most \(2\lvert\log|z_i|\rvert\le E\) for large degree, and each zero and pole stays at distance at least \(2E\).
The logarithmic derivative expressed as a sum of reciprocals thus has modulus \(O(D/E)\) along this segment.
Since the spherical length element for the image is \(|W'|\,|dz|/(1+|W|^2)\le |W'/W|\,|dz|/2\), the conditional mean displacement, including discarded roots, is
\[
 \mathbb E\,\rho(W(z_i),W(z_i^*))\ll DE+(D/E)M/b\ll DE.
\]
All bounds apply to each mix of the other coordinates and every choice of the slice coefficients.

<a id="height"></a>
## Transfer to the totally real height bound

Replace the \(t\) coordinates successively by their phases.
The final traces are real and the perturbed rational function has real coefficients.
Its final value is real, hence fixed by complex conjugation, an isometry of the chordal metric.
The triangle inequality and the slice bound show that the mean chordal distance from the initial output to its complex conjugate is \(O_t(DE)\).
Passing to \(\epsilon\to0\) at the finitely many initial tuples gives this same bound for the conjugates of the genuine output \(y\).
The [product-formula inequality for nonreal embeddings](Yin_C_G0345_v113.md#product) gives
\[
 e^{-(2q+2)h(y)}\le\varphi^{-(q+1)}+2\,\mathbb E\rho(y,\overline y),\qquad q=2/\varphi,
\]
where the average is over the embedded values of \(y\).
Taking logarithms gives the claimed height bound, and multiplying by the degree proves the assertion on total measure.
