# A finite set of period primes gives a gap for sparse factors with repeated residual roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## From a residual period to a degree bound

Fix a prime \(p\), a positive integer \(H\), a set \(\mathcal Q\) of \(r\ge1\) distinct primes and a constant \(A\ge1\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have nonzero constant term, degree \(D\), \(s\ge2\) nonzero coefficients and coefficient length \(L=\sum_j|[X^j]P|\).
Suppose \(S\) is the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), divides \(P\), and satisfies
\[
    \bar S\mid X^B-1 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[X],\qquad
    B=H\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}q^{a_q}\le AD,\qquad a_q\ge0.
\]
The divisibility is with multiplicities and permits \(p\mid B\).
Put \(d=\deg S\), \(m=\log M(S)\) and \(\gamma=1-1/r\).
Then
\[
            m\ \ge\ c\exp\!\left(-C(1+\log(B/d))^\gamma\right).          \tag{1}
\]
Here \(d\le B\) by monicity, and \(c,C>0\) in (1) depend only on \(p,H,\mathcal Q\).
If no nonempty proper initial sum of the terms of \(P\), ordered by increasing exponent, vanishes at \(\alpha\), then
\[
 \begin{split}
       m&\ge c_1\exp\!\left(-C_1(1+\log(1+\log L))^\gamma\right),\\
       d&\ge \frac{Dm}{(s-1)\log L},
 \end{split}                                                            \tag{2}
\]
where the constants in the first line may also depend on \(A,s\).
In particular at bounded coefficient length such a factor has a positive uniform gap and degree proportional to \(D\).
The initial-sum hypothesis holds for every nontorsion unit factor at \(s\le5\), and for all those with \(m<\log\theta\) at \(s\le7\), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(X^3-X-1=0\).
Thus through seven terms the uniform gap at bounded length holds for every nontorsion unit factor satisfying the period hypothesis.

<a id="multiplicities"></a>
## A period for each residual orbit with its multiplicity

For an irreducible factor of \(\bar S\), let \(t\) be the multiplicative order of one of its roots and \(\mu\) its multiplicity in \(\bar S\).
This factor has degree \(f_t=\operatorname{ord}_t(p)\), with \(f_1=1\).
Since \(t\) is prime to \(p\), its prime divisors belong to a fixed finite set and
\[
                    f_t\ge c_{p,H,\mathcal Q}\,t .
\]
Indeed the [order divisibilities at powers of each prime](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#orders) give \(q^{\max(0,v_q(t)-c_{p,q})}\mid f_t\) for every \(q\mid t\), including \(q=2\) at odd \(p\).
Multiplying these coprime powers gives the bound with loss at most \(\prod_{q\mid H\prod_{u\in\mathcal Q}u,\ q\ne p}q^{c_{p,q}}\).

Attach to this orbit the least period capturing all of its multiplicity,
\[
                         b=t p^{\lceil\log_p\mu\rceil}.
\]
Indeed \(X^J-1\) in characteristic \(p\) contains it with multiplicity \(p^{v_p(J)}\) when \(t\mid J\), and omits it otherwise.
Thus \(b\mid B\), and \(b\le p\mu t\ll\mu f_t\le d\).
For each \(b\) let \(w_b\) be the sum of the degrees with multiplicity of all orbits having that value.
Their prime-to-\(p\) orders are all the same \(t\), and their distinct roots are among the \(\varphi(t)\) primitive \(t\)-th roots.
It follows that \(w_b\le p^{v_p(b)}\varphi(t)\le b\), even when different orbits of the same order have different multiplicities.
All weights together sum to \(d\).

In the representation of \(B\), absorb the powers of primes in \(\mathcal Q\) dividing \(H\) into the exponents \(a_q\), leaving a fixed cofactor coprime to every \(q\in\mathcal Q\).
The [divisor estimate in logarithmic coordinates](Yin_C_G0345_v256.md#box) now gives a subset of these \(b\)'s of total weight \(\delta d\), where \(\delta\ge c/W\), whose least common multiple \(J\) is at most \(C d\exp(CW)\), with \(W=(1+\log(B/d))^\gamma\).
In particular \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,X^J-1)\ge\delta d\).
For any \(i\ge0\), the \(i+1\) integer factors \(X^J-1\) and \((X^{p^{j+1}J}-1)/(X^{p^jJ}-1)\), \(0\le j<i\), each reduce to a multiple of \(X^J-1\) modulo \(p\).
Multiplication by each factor on \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\) has nullity at least \(\delta d\) modulo \(p\), by the gcd degree, so its nonzero determinant is divisible by \(p^{\delta d}\).
This is the [resultant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#resultants) applied at \(p\), and remains valid for repeated residual factors.
Multiplying determinants and bounding \(|\alpha_j^{p^iJ}-1|\le2\max(1,|\alpha_j|)^{p^iJ}\) over the conjugates yields
\[
             p^i Jm\ \ge\ \big((i+1)\delta\log p-\log2\big)d .
\]
Taking \(i=\lceil 1/\delta\rceil\) makes the right side at least \(\delta d\log2\).
The bounds on \(J,\delta\) give (1), absorbing the factor \(W^{-1}\) into the exponential.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## Comparison with the height at surviving cuts

Write \(X=\log(B/d)\).
The [norm estimate at consecutive cuts](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) gives \(m\le(s-1)d\log L/D\le A(s-1)\log L\,e^{-X}\).
Comparison with (1) yields \(X\le\log(A(s-1)\log L/c)+C(1+X)^\gamma\).
Since \(\gamma<1\), the last term is at most \(X/2\) for all sufficiently large \(X\) depending only on \(C,\gamma\).
Hence \(X\ll1+\log(1+\log L)\), giving (2).
A vanishing cut has both sides zero, impossible for a nontorsion unit if one side has at most two terms.
At seven or fewer terms one side has at most three terms and the [individual trinomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement) forces \(m\ge\log\theta\).

<a id="families"></a>
## Both tetranomial orientations and the reciprocal five-term family

* For \(P=a(X^{2n}+1)+b(X^{n+l}+X^{n-l})+cX^n\), take \(a,c\) odd, \(b\ne0\) even, and \(0<l<n\).
  Modulo two, \(\bar P=X^{2n}+X^n+1\) divides \(X^{3n}-1\) with all its multiplicities for every \(n\).
  Thus one can take \(B=3n\), including unrestricted powers of two and three in \(n\).
  If \(n\) is supported on a fixed finite set of primes, (2) applies to every nontorsion unit factor with \(D=2n\).
  This extends the [one additional prime tower](Yin_C_G0345_v239.md#statement).
* For \(P=a(X^D+\sigma)+b(X^{D-k}+\sigma X^k)\), take \(D>2k>0,\ ab\ne0,\ \gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(\sigma=\pm1\).
  At a fixed prime \(p\mid b\) put \(T=D\), and at a fixed prime \(p\mid a\) put \(T=D-2k\).
  In the first case \(\bar P\) is a nonzero scalar times \(X^T+\sigma\), and in the second it is a nonzero scalar times \(X^k(X^T+\sigma)\).
  Since the monic \(S\) has unit constant term, reduction of the integral division \(S\mid P\) gives \(\bar S\mid X^T+\sigma\) in either orientation.
  Thus \(B=2T\le2D\) works for both signs, with all multiplicities.
  If \(T\) is supported on a fixed finite set of primes, (2) applies to every nontorsion unit factor for both [coefficient choices](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#statement).

Neither argument requires coprime exponents.
At bounded coefficient length the gap for the nontorsion unit factors gives a uniform lower bound for every positive-measure integral divisor.
Indeed a primitive irreducible nonunit with nonzero constant term has a leading or constant coefficient of absolute value at least two, so its logarithmic measure is at least \(\log2\), as is that of any nontrivial integer content.
For the tetranomials a coefficient prime must exist for the chosen orientation, and at bounded length there are only finitely many possible such primes.

The same fixed-length conclusions hold when only \(n/\gcd(n,l)\), respectively \(T/\gcd(D,k)\), has the fixed prime support.
Apply the result first to the polynomial obtained by dividing all exponents by their common divisor \(g\).
If \(\alpha\) is a nontorsion unit root of the original polynomial, \(\alpha^g\) is a nontorsion unit root of that reduced polynomial of the same coefficient length.
The [individual radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) gives \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\alpha^g)/R_L\).
The cut inequality at the original width then gives the asserted degree bound for the original unit factor.
