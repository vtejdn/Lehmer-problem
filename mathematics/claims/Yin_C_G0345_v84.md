# Optimal torsion bins and the density of partially interlacing Salem powers

<a id="statement"></a>
## One embedding, with any number of unused traces

Let \(\sigma\) be a Salem number of degree \(2(k+1)\), and fix integers \(D\ge1\) and \(1\le g\le k\).
Allow any monic degree-\(g\) polynomial \(A\) with distinct roots of the form \(\xi+\xi^{-1}\) for roots of unity \(\xi\), whose coefficients generate a field of degree at most \(D\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
At the chosen embedding, ask whether each of the \(g+1\) open intervals cut out by its ordered nodes contains a conjugate of the trace \(\sigma^n+\sigma^{-n}\).
The polynomial may depend on the positive exponent \(n\), and the condition is imposed only at that embedding.
There are \(k-g\) unused trace conjugates when one per interval is selected.

Write \(\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}k\\g\end{smallmatrix}\right\}\) for the number of partitions of \(k\) labeled elements into \(g\) nonempty unlabeled sets, and put
\[
               p(k,g)=\frac{g!}{g^k}\left\{\begin{matrix}k\\g\end{matrix}\right\}.
\]
This is the largest possible natural density of successful powers for any one such \(A\), attained by a rational polynomial of degree \(g\).
The union over all allowed \(A\) has natural density \(\Delta_D(k,g)\).
For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(C_{D,\epsilon}\) independent of \(k,g,\sigma\) such that
\[
        p(k,g)\ \le\ \Delta_D(k,g)\
          \le\ \min\bigl(1,\ p(k,g)\exp(C_{D,\epsilon}g^{1/2+\epsilon})\bigr).
\]
In particular \(\log\Delta_D(k,g)=-k+o(k)\) when \(D\) is fixed and \(g=k-o(k)\).
For \(g\to\infty\) and \(1\le k/g\le C\) with \(C\) fixed, \(\log\Delta_D(k,g)=-\Theta_C(g)\) at fixed \(D\).
If \(g\to\infty\) and \(k/g-\log g\to+\infty\), already the rational maximizing polynomial succeeds on a set of density tending to one.
These are statements about natural densities for the successive individual inputs.

<a id="bins"></a>
## Maximizing the occupancy probability

As in the [power equidistribution theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers), the \(k\) neutral inverse pairs remain distinct under powering and their folded angles equidistribute jointly as independent uniform variables on \([0,\pi]\).
The exterior trace \(\sigma^n+\sigma^{-n}>2\) always supplies the top interval.
Thus the criterion on \(A\) is precisely occupancy of its \(g\) lower bins by \(k\) neutral traces, each distributed as \(2\cos\theta\).
The boundary of any such event has probability zero.

Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_g\) be the lower-bin probabilities, whose sum can be less than one.
Any leftover probability can be redistributed among the required bins without decreasing the chance of occupying all of them, by reassigning only samples in the spare bin.
Among vectors of sum one the chance is maximized at \(p_j=1/g\).
For \(g\ge2\), average two bin probabilities while holding their sum \(s\) fixed.
Condition on the sample positions falling in these two bins and on all the other samples.
Unless the other bins are occupied and the two chosen bins receive at least two samples, success is impossible.
Otherwise, given \(a\ge2\) samples there and \(s>0\), the conditional probability is \(1-(p_i/s)^a-(p_j/s)^a\).
Convexity shows it can only increase when \(p_i,p_j\) are averaged.
If \(s=0\) it stays zero.
Among all maximizers on the compact simplex choose one minimizing \(\sum_j p_j^2\).
A pair of unequal entries could be averaged while keeping the maximum and decreasing this sum, so the entries are equal.
The case \(g=1\) follows by putting all probability into its single required bin.
At equal probabilities the successful assignments are precisely the surjections onto the \(g\) bins, giving \(p(k,g)\).

The coordinating agent identified the rational maximizing grid
\[
                         A_g(x)=(x-2)U_{g-1}(x/2),
\]
where \(U_{g-1}(\cos\theta)=\sin(g\theta)/\sin\theta\).
The recurrence \(U_{j+1}(x/2)=xU_j(x/2)-U_{j-1}(x/2)\) with initial \(1,x\) gives a monic integer polynomial.
Its interior nodes have angles \(j\pi/g\) for \(1\le j<g\), and the remaining node \(2\) has angle zero.
The \(g\) lower bins have equal angular length \(\pi/g\), and no angular mass is lost above the top node.
For \(g=1\) this gives \(x-2\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting node sets without fixing their fields

Agent-S proposed counting the possible intersections of a node set with each primitive cyclotomic orbit, even when the other embeddings of the coefficient field are unrestricted.
For order \(q\ge3\) the primitive traces form a regular orbit of \(G_q=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) of size \(\varphi(q)/2\), by the [cyclotomic trace calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#count).
For \(q=1,2\) use the singleton orbits \(2,-2\).
The coefficient field \(K\) is contained in a cyclotomic field containing the lifts of all the nodes.
For every conductor that occurs, restriction from the Galois group of this cyclotomic field onto \(G_q\) is surjective.
The image of the subgroup fixing \(K\) therefore has index at most \([K:\mathbb Q]\le D\).
The selected nodes of order \(q\) form a union of cosets of this image, since \(A\) is fixed by that subgroup.
Each nonempty choice has cardinality at least \(\varphi(q)/(2D)\) for \(q\ge3\).

Write \(\omega(q)\) for the number of distinct primes dividing \(q\).
By the Chinese remainder theorem, \(G_q\) has a generating set of size at most \(\omega(q)+1\).
Indeed the [unit group at each odd prime power is cyclic](../audits/literature/Con26c/Yin_U001.md#con26c-u001), and at \(2^a\) at most two generators suffice.
For \(a\ge3\) these may be taken as \(-1,5\), since \(v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2\) by repeated squaring and \(5\) generates the units congruent to one modulo four.
The lower powers of two satisfy the same generator bound directly.
Every subgroup of index \(j\le D\) occurs as a point stabilizer in a homomorphism to \(S_j\) via its action on cosets.
Counting the images of the generators, the possible point stabilizers and the at most \(2^j\) unions of their cosets gives at most \(C_D^{\,\omega(q)+2}\) choices for the primitive portion, for a constant \(C_D\ge2\).

For every \(\delta>0\), \(C_D^{\omega(q)}\ll_{D,\delta}q^\delta\) and \(q/\varphi(q)\ll_\delta q^\delta\).
For the first inequality use \(C_D\le p^\delta\) at sufficiently large primes \(p\mid q\), absorbing the finitely many smaller primes in the constant.
For the second apply the same argument to \(\prod_{p\mid q}p/(p-1)\).
It follows that, for every \(\nu>0\), the number \(B(x)\) of all possible nonempty primitive portions of cardinality at most \(x\), across all conductors, is \(O_{D,\nu}(x^{1+\nu})\) for \(x\ge1\).
Indeed \(\varphi(q)\le2Dx\) implies \(q\ll_{D,\delta} x^{1/(1-\delta)}\) for \(0<\delta<1\).
Summing \(O_{D,\delta}(q^\delta)\) over these conductors gives the bound by choosing \((1+\delta)/(1-\delta)\le1+\nu\).

Let \(\mathcal N_D(g)\) count possible full node sets of size at most \(g\).
Ignoring the requirement that all choices use the same coefficient field, and even allowing different portions of a conductor as separate items, gives an upper bound.
For an item \(E\) write \(|E|\) for its cardinality.
For \(0<t\le1\) we obtain
\[
 \log\mathcal N_D(g)
   \le tg+\sum_E\log(1+e^{-t|E|})
   \le tg+\int_0^\infty te^{-tx}B(x)\,dx
   \le tg+O_{D,\nu}(t^{-1-\nu}).
\]
At \(t=g^{-1/(2+\nu)}\), the exponent is \((1+\nu)/(2+\nu)\), which can be taken at most \(1/2+\epsilon\).
There are therefore finitely many candidates, with logarithmic count \(O_{D,\epsilon}(g^{1/2+\epsilon})\).
Their union is another boundary-null occupancy event for the same joint folded-angle distribution, so its natural density exists and depends only on \(k,g,D\).
The union bound and the rational maximizing grid prove the stated inequalities.

<a id="regimes"></a>
## Near-full and proportional occupancy

At equal probabilities, using only the first \(g\) samples and requiring them all distinct shows \(p(k,g)\ge g!/g^g\).
Conversely, choosing one labeled witness for each bin gives \(p(k,g)\le k!/((k-g)!\,g^g)\).
Put \(h=k-g=o(k)\).
By Stirling's estimate the logarithm of this last bound is \(-k+o(k)\), using \(h\log(k/h)=o(k)\) when \(h>0\), and the same holds for the lower bound.
Taking any \(\epsilon<1/2\) in the counting estimate proves the near-full rate.

For \(1\le k/g\le C\), let \(g\) independent Poisson variables have common mean \(k/g\).
Conditional on their sum being \(k\), their joint law is the equal-bin multinomial distribution, by the Poisson mass formula.
The unconditioned chance that all are positive is \((1-e^{-k/g})^g\), while the chance of the conditioning event is \(e^{-k}k^k/k!\gg k^{-1/2}\).
Thus \(p(k,g)\ll\sqrt k(1-e^{-C})^g\).
Together with the same factorial lower bound and the subexponential candidate count, this proves exponential decay in \(g\).
Finally the probability that some equal bin is empty is at most \(g(1-1/g)^k\le g e^{-k/g}\), proving the last density regime.
Positive density gives a finite first successful exponent at each fixed input, but none of these counting estimates bounds that exponent uniformly in the degree or its Mahler cost.
