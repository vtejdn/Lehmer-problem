# Five-term double contacts at a positive real conjugate

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds independent of the coefficient positions and the target

Let \(\gamma\) be a reciprocal algebraic unit having a real conjugate \(a>1\), and write \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
Fix \(p\ge2,\ b\ge0,\ \varepsilon=\pm1\) and a nonzero integer row \(A(Y)=\sum_{j\in J}a_jY^j\) of degree at most \(b\), using at most five positions.
Suppose that
\[
 F(X)=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X^{pb+1} A(X^{-p})
\]
has zeros of order at least two at \(\gamma,\gamma^2\).
Write \(N=pb+1,\ H=\|A\|_1,\ h=\log a\), and put \(s(j)=\max(pj,N-pj)\).
Then \(|J|=5\), and the five distinct integers \(s_1<\cdots<s_5\) obtained from \(J\) satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
 (s_{i+1}-s_i)h&\le L=\log(48H)+4\log(N+1)+12h+
                    4\log^+\!\bigl(1/(a-1)\bigr),\\
                  d&\le2(s_5-s_1)\le8L/h .                              \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
Here \(\log^+v=\max(0,\log v)\).
No prescribed support spacing, nonzero numerator, prime exponent or reduction hypothesis is needed for (1).

For a Salem number \(\gamma>1\), take \(a=\gamma\), so \(h\) is the whole positive logarithmic Mahler measure.
Let \(D\to\infty,\ D\le d<2D,\ b<3d\) and \(p=o(D/\log^3(3D))\).
For any fixed \(K>0\), all sufficiently large such instances with \(H\le\exp(Kp)\) have no double-contact row using at most five terms, uniformly in the Salem number, sign and support.
This includes both coefficient budgets in the [narrow-width degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v476.md#statement) at the first prime above \((\log_2 D)^2\).
The five positions can be arbitrarily spaced; the [normalized-span cuts](Yin_C_G0345_v469.md#counts) concern a different restriction.

<a id="matrix"></a>
## A uniform lower bound for the interpolation minors

For \(1\le r\le4\) take the first \(r\) of the four rows that evaluate a polynomial, then its Euler derivative \(X\,d/dX\), at \(a\), then evaluate the polynomial and its Euler derivative at \(a^2\).
Write \((z_i)_{1\le i\le r}\) for the corresponding initial segment of \((a,a,a^2,a^2)\).
For columns with strictly increasing nonnegative integer exponents \(l_1,\ldots,l_r\), the absolute determinant of this matrix is at least
\[
       a^{-12}\min(1,a-1)^4\prod_{i=1}^r z_i^{l_i}.                      \tag{2}
\]
To see the bound first use distinct positive evaluation points \(z_1,\dots,z_r\).
Dividing \(\det(z_i^{l_j})\) by their Vandermonde product gives a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients containing the monomial \(\prod_i z_i^{l_i-i+1}\).
Indeed for \(z_r=t\), subtract \(t^{l_{j+1}-l_j}\) times column \(j\) from column \(j+1\) in decreasing order of \(j\).
After expansion along the last row, each resulting column in the smaller determinant is a sum of monomials with exponents \(l_j\le m_j<l_{j+1}\), times its row factor \(z_i-t\).
Dividing out the Vandermonde gives the sum of the smaller quotients with weights \(t^{\sum_j l_j-\sum_j m_j-(r-1)}\).
This proves nonnegativity by induction, starting with \(r=1\), and choosing \(m_j=l_j\) at each step gives the indicated monomial.

As adjacent points coalesce in pairs at \(a\) or \(a^2\), a difference quotient gives the ordinary derivative row.
Multiplying by its node gives the Euler derivative row.
The Vandermonde factors at unequal limiting points contribute at least \(\min(1,a-1)^4\), since there are at most four such pairs and \(a^2-a\ge a-1\).
The extra node multipliers are at least one.
Finally \(\prod_i z_i^{-(i-1)}\ge a^{-12}\), giving (2).

<a id="gaps"></a>
## The four conditions bound each gap in the upper exponents

The \(2|J|\) exponents \(pj,N-pj\) are distinct because they occupy different residue classes modulo \(p\).
There is no tie at \(N/2\).
The four positive real points \(a,a^{-1},a^2,a^{-2}\) are distinct and are all zeros of order at least two.
A real polynomial with \(t\) nonzero terms has at most \(t-1\) positive zeros counted with multiplicities: divide out its smallest monomial and apply Rolle's theorem to its derivative, inductively.
Consequently eight positive zeros require at least five positions in the row.

Choose any gap \(s_i<s_{i+1}\), and put \(r=5-i\).
The \(r\) upper terms at \(s_{i+1},\dots,s_5\) have nonzero integer coefficients.
Every remaining term of \(F\), including all five lower terms, has exponent at most \(s_i\).
Move those terms to the right of the first \(r\) value and derivative equations above.
The right side in row \(j\) has absolute value at most \(2H(N+1)z_j^{s_i}\).
Solve for the coefficient at the smallest upper exponent \(s_{i+1}\) by Cramer's rule.
In the new determinant, every product is bounded by \(2H(N+1)^r\) times the sorted assignment of the exponents \(s_i,s_{i+2},\ldots,s_5\) to the increasing \(z_j\), by the rearrangement inequality applied to the logarithms.
There are at most \(4!\) such terms.
The denominator satisfies (2) at exponents \(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_5\).
The absolute value of the recovered nonzero integer is therefore at most \(48H(N+1)^4 a^{12}\min(1,a-1)^{-4}a^{s_i-s_{i+1}}\).
This proves the first inequality of (1).

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Squaring removes the separation of the two exponent intervals

Put \(\Delta=s_5-s_1\).
The upper exponents lie in an interval of length \(\Delta\); the lower exponents occupy its reflection about \(N/2\).
After division by the lowest monomial the graph is
\[
                   X^T U(X)+V(X),\qquad
       T=s_1+s_5-N>0,\quad\deg U,\deg V\le\Delta .
\]
Both polynomials are nonzero.
If \(U(a)=0\) or \(U(a^2)=0\), then \(d\le2\Delta\), since \([\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q(a^2)]\le2\).
Otherwise \(f=-V/U\) agrees with \(X^T\) to order at least two at \(a,a^2\).
In particular the polynomial
\[
                  V(X^2)U(X)^2+U(X^2)V(X)^2
\]
has a zero of order at least two at \(a\), by applying the two local expansions at \(X\) and \(X^2\).
It has degree at most \(4\Delta\).
If it vanished identically, \(f(X^2)=f(X)^2\) would force \(f=X^v\) for an integer \(v\).
Indeed the leading constant in a Laurent expansion at zero must be one, and the first positive index after the leading monomial cannot survive squaring and doubling of the variable at the same degree.
Then \(a^v=a^T\) forces \(v=T\), which would make the whole graph zero despite its distinct exponents.
Thus the square of the degree-\(d\) minimum divides a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(4\Delta\), proving (1).

For the asserted uniform Salem exclusion use [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), giving \(h>2/\log^3(3d)\).
Since \(a-1\ge h\), the last term of \(L\) is \(O(1+\log\log(3d))\).
Move \(96h\) to the left in \(dh\le8L\).
For \(b<3d,\ p=o(D/\log^3(3D))\) and the given norm budget, the remaining right side is \(O_K(p+\log(3D)+\log\log(3D))=o(D/\log^3(3D))\), a contradiction.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## At a squarefree prime the row must reduce to two opposite translated lists

Now let \(p\) be an odd prime and divide the coefficients of \(A\) by their positive content before reducing modulo \(p\).
Let \(s_p\) denote the number of distinct roots of the reduced monic minimum of \(a\).
Group the positions of \(A\) according as \(pj>N/2\) or \(pj<N/2\).
In each group the positions lie in an interval of length at most \(l=\lfloor\Delta/p\rfloor\).
If \(s_p>4l\), both group polynomials must have nonzero reductions, and the two reduced lists, after independent translations, must be negatives of one another.
In particular a content-normalized row must have a coefficient divisible by \(p\), since five is odd.

Indeed the derivative of the graph at any nonzero residue \(\xi\) of a conjugate of \(a\) reduces to \(\varepsilon\xi^{pb}\bar A(\xi^{-p})\).
Both the derivative and the graph vanish there, which gives \(\bar A(\xi^p)=0\) and therefore \(\bar A(\xi)=0\) by Frobenius.
The same holds at \(\xi^2\) by the double contact at the square.
If only one group survives reduction, its polynomial after translation has degree at most \(l\), bounding \(s_p\le l\).
Otherwise write the translated reduction as \(L(Y)+Y^v R(Y)\) with \(v>0\), where \(L,R\) are the reduced group polynomials of degrees at most \(l\).
All the \(s_p\) distinct residues annihilate \(L(Y^2)R(Y)^2+R(Y^2)L(Y)^2\), by substituting the two zeros of the row even when a denominator vanishes.
Its degree is at most \(4l\).
If \(s_p>4l\), the rational function \(-L/R\) therefore commutes with squaring.
The Laurent argument above works also in odd characteristic because two is nonzero, so \(-L/R\) is a monomial of coefficient one.
This proves the restriction on both reduced support and coefficients.

At every odd prime with squarefree original minimum one has \(s_p=d\).
For Salem seeds of sufficiently large degree the restriction then holds at **every** such prime under \(b<3d,\ H\le\exp(Kp)\), uniformly in the prime and the seed for each fixed \(K\).
Indeed (1) bounds \(\Delta/p\) by \(4L/(ph)\).
Here \(\log(pb+2)/p=O(1+\log(3d))\) uniformly for \(p\ge3\); the same lower bound for \(h\) shows \(\Delta/p=O_K(1+\log^4(3d))=o(d)\).
Thus no passing five-term row with all five normalized coefficients prime to \(p\) occurs in this regime.
The argument at a positive real exterior conjugate does not assert the same conclusion for a reciprocal seed whose only real exterior conjugates are negative.
Rows with six or more terms and general moving complex conjugates remain open.
