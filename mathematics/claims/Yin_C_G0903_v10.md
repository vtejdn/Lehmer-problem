# Uniformly few primes support sparse two-row relations at a varying unit

2026-11-24T19:07 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exceptional primes throughout a tail

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), with absolute logarithmic height \(h(\alpha)\le1\).
Put \(e=\min_{u\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^u):\mathbb Q]\).
Fix integers \(2\le k\le e\) and \(B\ge1\), and a real \(X\ge2\).
Count all primes \(p\ge X\) for which some nonzero rational graph
\[
                 A(T^p)+T C(T^p),\qquad \deg A,\deg C\le B,
\]
has at most \(k\) occupied terms and vanishes at \(\alpha\).
Either row may be zero.
There is a bound
\[
              N_k\left(1+\frac{\log(k(B+1)I_k(d))}{\log X}\right),          \tag{1}
\]
where one may take
\[
 \begin{split}
 t&=k!,\qquad C_t=(8t)^{4t^4(t+k+1)},\\
 N_k&=k\,2^k\,t^t C_t^t,\qquad
 I_k(d)=\bigl(1+8k d^{2k}/\log2\bigr)^k .
 \end{split}
\]
The row positions and their rational coefficients may all vary with the prime, and the coefficients have no height cutoff.
In particular the number is \(O_{k,K,a,\delta}(1)\) for \(B\le Kd^a\) and \(X=d^\delta\ge2\) with fixed \(K\ge1,\ a,\delta>0\).
An ordinary graph within these width and support bounds with the given unit as a repeated root satisfies the necessary value condition.

The [fixed-seed determinant argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v325.md#minor) makes each individual support's exceptional exponents finite, even for higher multiplicities.
To count prime exceptions uniformly across all changing positions, we use a single zero set in several integer variables for each list of residue labels.
We use the [inspected statement of Amoroso and Viada, Theorem 6.2](../audits/literature/AV09/Yin_U001.md#retained-primary-statement) on nondegenerate unit equations.

<a id="cosets"></a>
## Vanishing determinants on translates of subgroups

Choose \(s\) with \(2\le s\le k\), since a single monomial cannot vanish.
For \(s\) columns choose labels \(\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\), \(1\le i\le s\).
Choose conjugates \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) of \(\alpha\) from distinct classes modulo roots of unity.
There are \(e\) such classes since taking a common multiple of all orders of torsion quotients identifies exactly the conjugates in each class.
In the common field \(K_0=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_s)\), of degree at most \(d^s\), consider
\[
           D(v)=\det(x_j^{v_i+\epsilon_i})_{1\le j,i\le s},
                          \qquad v\in\mathbb Z^s .
\]
If a graph on distinct occupied columns \((j_i,\epsilon_i)\), \(0\le j_i\le B\), vanishes at \(\alpha\), its coefficient vector gives a nonzero kernel vector at \(v=pj\), where \(j=(j_i)_i\).

In the determinant expansion write \(D(v)=\sum_\pi a_\pi\chi_\pi(v)\), with nonzero fixed coefficients \(a_\pi=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\prod_i x_{\pi(i)}^{\epsilon_i}\) and characters \(\chi_\pi(v)=\prod_i x_{\pi(i)}^{v_i}\).
At each zero partition the at most \(t\) nonzero evaluated summands into inclusion-minimal vanishing blocks.
In a fixed block divide every term by the negative of one designated term, move that term to the other side, and absorb the fixed coefficient ratios into the equation coefficients.
This gives a nondegenerate equation with right side one, in at most \(t-1\) variables.
The tuple of character ratios belongs to the image of \(\mathbb Z^s\), a subgroup of rank at most \(s\).
For an equation in \(m\) variables on a group of rank \(R\), the cited theorem bounds the number of nondegenerate solution tuples by \((8m)^{4m^4(m+R+1)}\).
A one-variable equation has at most one solution.
Thus \(C_t\) bounds the number of choices per block, independently of \(K_0\) and of the coefficients.
There are at most \(t^t\) partitions and at most \(t\) blocks in each.
For any collection of chosen tuples with nonempty simultaneous inverse image, that inverse image is a coset \(v_0+L\) in \(\mathbb Z^s\).
Its subgroup \(L\) is the common kernel of all designated character ratios in all blocks.
Every point of the coset annihilates the determinant.
After all \(s\) and their label lists are included, at most \(N_k\) cosets suffice.

Fix one such coset meeting an actual support \(v=pj\), with the pairs \((j_i,\epsilon_i)\) distinct.
Then \(j\notin L_{\mathbb Q}:=L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).
Otherwise some positive multiple of \(j\) belongs to \(L\), and \(D(nj)\) vanishes on an infinite arithmetic progression through \(p\).
For completeness, order the \(x_j\) by a homomorphism to \(\mathbb Z\) on the free group generated by them modulo torsion, chosen to give them distinct weights.
In \(D(nj)\) the largest weight of a recurrence base requires assigning the sorted \(j_i\) to the increasing node weights.
Each difference between maximizing assignments only exchanges two columns with identical level \(j_i\) and distinct offsets 0 and 1.
The two assignments then have the same actual base, and the corresponding scalar factors combine to \(x_l-x_{l'}\) up to sign for their two distinct nodes.
The different ties are disjoint, and all columns with unique levels contribute nonzero scalars.
The maximizing base therefore has nonzero coefficient and its ratio to every other base is nontorsion.
This term survives on every arithmetic progression.
The Vandermonde determinant in the remaining distinct recurrence bases excludes identically zero values on that progression.
In particular \(v_0+L_{\mathbb Q}\) does not contain zero because it contains \(pj\) outside \(L_{\mathbb Q}\).

<a id="equations"></a>
## Bounded integer equations for the rational span

For one of the designated character ratios, write its coordinate bases as \(\xi_i=x_{\pi(i)}/x_{\pi_0(i)}\).
Its kernel consists of the \(v\) with \(\prod_i\xi_i^{v_i}=1\).
To describe its kernel modulo torsion, send units in \(K_0\) to their vectors of logarithmic moduli at all complex embeddings and give their real span the norm \(\|z\|=(2[K_0:\mathbb Q])^{-1}\sum_\sigma |z_\sigma|\).
For units this norm is their absolute logarithmic height and the kernel consists of roots of unity.
Each generator \(\xi_i\) has norm at most \(2h(\alpha)\le2\).
Every nonzero element in the group of logarithm vectors generated by them has norm at least
\[
                       \eta=\frac{\log2}{4d^{2s}}.
\]
Indeed it represents a nontorsion unit of degree at most \(d^s\) and the [house bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives height at least \(\log2\) divided by four times the square of its degree.

If the generator vectors span dimension \(l>0\), choose \(l\) of them giving maximal absolute determinant in any fixed coordinates on their real span.
The coordinates of every generator in the chosen basis have absolute values at most one by Cramer's rule.
Let \(\Gamma\) be the group generated by all the vectors and \(\Gamma_0\) the subgroup generated by the chosen basis.
A representative of each coset of \(\Gamma_0\) may be put in the centered fundamental parallelepiped by subtracting integer combinations of basis elements.
Its norm is at most \(l\), and distinct representatives have distance at least \(\eta\).
The disjoint norm balls of radius \(\eta/2\) about these points are contained in the ball of radius \(l+\eta/2\), allowing open balls at equality.
Comparison of volumes in dimension \(l\) bounds the index by
\[
                  [\Gamma:\Gamma_0]\le (1+2l/\eta)^l\le I_k(d).
\]
Multiplying coordinates by the index makes the coordinates of all generators integral without changing their common rational kernel.
Each row \(a\) of the resulting integer coordinate matrix has \(\|a\|_\infty\le I_k(d)\).
When \(l=0\) there are no rows for that ratio.

Collect these rows for all character ratios of the coset.
They cut out exactly \(L_{\mathbb Q}\).
Indeed, an integer vector satisfying the zero equations maps to roots of unity in all of those characters, and a common positive multiple of it therefore lies in \(L\).
Conversely every vector of \(L\) satisfies the equations, giving equality after taking rational spans.
Because the affine span of an actual coset misses zero, at least one of these bounded rows has a nonzero integer constant \(c=a\cdot v_0\) throughout the coset.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Counting prime divisors and allowing the term bound to grow

All exceptional primes represented by this coset divide \(c\), since \(c=p\,a\cdot j\) at every corresponding support.
If the coset supplies any such prime at least \(X\), let \(p_0\) be its smallest one in that range, using the width bound \(B\).
Then \(0<|c|\le sI_k(d)p_0 B\), and the product of any \(h\) distinct represented primes at least \(X\) is at least \(p_0^h\) and divides \(|c|\).
Consequently the coset represents at most \(1+\log(k(B+1)I_k(d))/\log X\) such primes, proving (1).

For fixed \(K,a,\delta>0\) as above, let \(k=k(d)\ge2\) grow with \(d\) subject to \(k\le e\) and \(k\log(2k)=o(\log\log d)\).
The total number of exceptional primes in the whole tail \(p\ge d^\delta\) is then \(d^{o(1)}\), uniformly in the seed and the allowed width.
Indeed \(\log N_k\le\exp(O(k\log(2k)))= (\log d)^{o(1)}\), and \(\log I_k(d)=O(k\log(2k)+k^2\log d)\).
In any dyadic prime interval \([X,2X]\) with \(X\ge d^\delta\) the exceptional fraction tends to zero by the prime number theorem.

Along a sequence with \(m(\alpha)\to0\), the [power-field norm bound](Yin_C_G0903_v2.md#interpolation) gives \(e\ge(\log2)/(4m(\alpha))\), so such growing term bounds are available.
In particular, on a shrinking sequence of reciprocal nontorsion unit seeds, at almost every prime \(p\in[d,2d]\) the [ordinary near-degree witnesses](Yin_C_G0901_v5.md#short) must use a number of terms tending to infinity.
The count bounds how many exceptional primes can occur for each seed without deciding whether any one prescribed prime is exceptional or bounding their largest location.
