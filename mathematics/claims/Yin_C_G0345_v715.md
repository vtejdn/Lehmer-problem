# Prime-power divisibility from the three integers adjacent to a Salem seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous lower bounds from auxiliary primes

Let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), with integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Let \(\gamma=r^{1/q}\in K=\mathbb Q(r)\) be a positive field radical, \(q\ge1\), and write \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
Fix \(c\in\{0,-1,1\}\) and a positive divisor \(b\mid q\), and put \(k=q/b\).
Use \(J=2(n+1), B=1\) at \(c=0\), and \(J=2n,B=2\) at \(c=\pm1\).
In the latter case \(B=1\) is also allowed when \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\).
For each prime \(\ell\mid a-c\), put
\[
 D_\ell=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le d/2}\{\ell^j-1,\ell^j+1\},
 \qquad h_\ell=\gcd(q,D_\ell).
\]
Take any subset \(E\) of the primes \(\ell\mid a-c\) with \(h_\ell\mid b\), and put
\[
 A_E=\prod_{\ell\in E}\ell^{v_\ell(a-c)}.
\]
These primes may or may not divide \(q\).
With \(L=12\) one has
\[
 A_E\ \mid\ k^B\big(\gamma^{bJL}-1\big)^B
                    \quad\hbox{in }\mathcal O_K,\qquad
 A_E\le (2k)^B r^{LJB/(dk)}.                                          \tag{1}
\]
One may use \(L=1\) if each \(\ell\in E\) dividing \(k\) satisfies \(\ell-1>B\), including when none divides \(k\).
In particular the full power of every selected prime enters the same bound.

The test may use a smaller \(h_\ell\) obtained from the actual cyclotomic orders and their multiplicities as specified below.
With absolute effective constants, uniformly in all these parameters as \(a\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{\log A_E}{\log a}\ll
                   \frac{1}{\log\log a}+\frac{1}{k}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Thus when \(q/b\to\infty\) the selected primes account for a vanishing fraction of the logarithm of each integer \(a-c\), even if their powers in that integer grow.
For any \(\ell^e\mid q\) and \(\ell\mid a-c\), one also has
\[
             v_\ell(a-c)\log\ell\le
                   B\log(2\ell^e)+\frac{12JB}{d\ell^e}\log r .        \tag{3}
\]
If \(\ell^{v_\ell(a-c)}\ge(a+1)^\theta\) for fixed \(\theta>0\), every such \(\ell^e\) is bounded by an effective constant depending only on \(\theta\).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Cyclotomic reduction at any prime of the seed or its neighbors

Write \(S_x\) for the monic minimal polynomial of a divisor power \(x=\gamma^u\), \(u\mid q\).
Each such \(x\) generates \(K\) since \(x^{q/u}=r\), and is a degree-\(d\) Salem unit as in the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Let \(U\) be the product of \(X-v\) over \(v=\pm1,\ v^{n-1}=\epsilon\), and \(P_c=G_c/U\).
The [seed classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(P_a=S_r\), \(d\ge n-1\), and \(r<a+1\).
The binomials \(X^{n+1}-\epsilon\) and \(X^n-\epsilon X\) are divisible by \(U\), so \(P_c\) is integral and
\[
                      P_c(r)\in(a-c)\mathcal O_K.
\]
At zero \(G_0=X^{n+1}-\epsilon\), and at \(c=\pm1\) one has \(G_c=(X-c)(X^n+\epsilon c)\).
Consequently \(P_c\) is cyclotomic up to products and repetitions, and divides \(F(X)=(X^J-1)^B\) over \(\mathbb Z\) with the stipulated \(J,B\).
When \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\), \(U\) removes \(X-c\) and the single binomial suffices.
Hence \(F(r)\in(a-c)\mathcal O_K\).

For any \(\ell\mid a-c\) the \(q\)-th powers of the reduced root multiset of \(S_\gamma\) give that of \(\overline P_c\).
Indeed the corresponding equality holds over characteristic zero with \(P_a\), by equality of fields, and \(\overline P_a=\overline P_c\).
This does not require a Frobenius power or \(\ell\mid q\).
Every root of \(\overline S_\gamma\) has order dividing \(D_\ell\), by the [reciprocal orbit argument](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#orders).
Set \(N=D_\ell/h_\ell,\ u=q/h_\ell\).
The roots after raising to \(h_\ell\) belong to \(\mu_N\), and \(u\) is coprime to \(N\).
Raising to \(u\) permutes \(\mu_N\).
It fixes the target multiset of \(\overline P_c\) as a whole because that multiset has equal multiplicity on primitive roots of each occurring order.
This last property follows by reducing its integral cyclotomic factors, including factors whose conductor is divisible by \(\ell\).
Indeed if \((s,\ell)=1\), the reduction of \(\Phi_{s\ell^i}\) for \(i\ge1\) is \(\overline\Phi_s^{\varphi(\ell^i)}\), by the cyclotomic quotient identity and Frobenius.
It follows by unique inversion on \(\mu_N\) that \(\overline S_{\gamma^{h_\ell}}=\overline P_c\).
Thus \(F(\gamma^{h_\ell})\in\ell\mathcal O_K\), and since \(F(X)\mid F(X^j)\) for all \(j\ge1\), every \(\ell\in E\) satisfies
\[
                         (\gamma^{bJ}-1)^B\in\ell\mathcal O_K .         \tag{4}
\]

The [local refinement](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#local) works also at these auxiliary primes with the unshortened exponent \(q\).
For each divisor \(m\mid D_\ell\) retain it only when \(s=m/\gcd(m,q)\) occurs in \(\overline P_c\) and the joint Frobenius and inversion orbit sizes satisfy \(L_m/L_s\le\mu_s\), the target multiplicity.
Here \(L_m=|\langle\ell,-1\rangle\bmod m|\) with \(L_1=1\).
Every actual order survives because a single orbit maps with constant fiber size onto its target orbit.
Replacing \(D_\ell\) by the least common multiple of the retained \(m\)'s in \(h_\ell\) gives the same deduction of (4), using this smaller common multiple throughout the inversion.

<a id="valuation"></a>
## Retaining the valuation at each selected prime

Fix \(\ell\in E\) and a prime of \(K\) over \(\ell\), with additive valuation \(v\) normalized by \(v(\ell)=1\).
Write \(x=\gamma^{bJ}\).
By (4), \(v(x-1)\ge1/B\).
If \(\ell\nmid k\), the geometric sum for \(y^k-1\) at \(v(y-1)>0\) gives \(v(y^k-1)=v(y-1)\).
If \(v(y-1)>1/(\ell-1)\), the binomial theorem gives
\[
                         v(y^k-1)=v(y-1)+v_\ell(k)
\]
for arbitrary \(k\): in raising to \(\ell\) the linear term has uniquely least valuation, and one separates the \(\ell\)-part of \(k\).
For \(L=12\), \(y=x^L\) is in this strict range at every \(\ell\).
Indeed a single \(\ell\)-th power takes an initial positive valuation \(v(x-1)=z\) to at least \(\min(1+z,\ell z)\).
Starting at \(z\ge1/2\), cubing suffices at three and raising to the fourth suffices at two.
At larger primes the original bound already suffices, and raising to any positive power cannot reduce it.
This also proves the condition permitting \(L=1\).

Now \(y^k=r^{JL}\), and \(F(r)\mid F(r^L)\) in \(\mathcal O_K\).
Thus \(Bv(y^k-1)\ge v_\ell(a-c)\), whence
\[
               Bv(\gamma^{bJL}-1)+Bv_\ell(k)\ge v_\ell(a-c).
\]
This holds at every prime over \(\ell\), and all the elements on the right of the divisibility in (1) are integral.
Combining the inequalities for all selected primes proves that divisibility.
The right side is nonzero since \(\gamma>1\).
Over all conjugates of \(\gamma^b\), the norm of \((\gamma^{bJL}-1)^B\) is at most \(2^{Bd}\gamma^{bJLB}\) in absolute value, because precisely one conjugate lies outside the unit circle.
Taking norms and \(d\)-th roots proves the upper bound in (1).

<a id="consequences"></a>
## The portion of each adjacent integer that can pass the local tests

Every field radical index divides the greatest one, by the [field exponent theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).
The [uniform index estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) therefore gives \(\log q\ll\log a/\log\log a\) at large \(a\), with an effective absolute constant.
Since \(J/d\le2(n+1)/(n-1)\le8/3,\ B\le2\), taking logarithms in (1) gives (2).
For (3) apply the same theorem (1) to the field radical \(r^{1/\ell^e}\), with \(b=1\) and the single auxiliary prime \(\ell\).
Its \(D_\ell\) is prime to \(\ell\), so the multiplier is one.
Finally the [prime-power gap](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives \(\ell^e\le\log r/\delta\), \(\delta=\log(5/2)/6\).
Hence the first term on the right of (3) is \(O(\log\log(a+1))\).
For large \(a\) depending effectively on \(\theta\), it is at most \(\theta\log(a+1)/2\).
The remaining inequality gives \(\ell^e\le128/\theta\).
The prime-power gap bounds all cases at the remaining bounded seeds, giving the uniform effective assertion.
