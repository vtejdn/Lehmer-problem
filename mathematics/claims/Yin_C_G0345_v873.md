# A single irreducible unit factor attaining the one-third circle proportion

<a id="statement"></a>
## An explicit family with a distinguished rational factor

For \(j\ge1\) put \(W=101^j\), \(u=(W-1)/2\), and
\[
             P_W(X)=1+2X^u+2X^{u+1}+X^W .
\]
There is a unique monic irreducible rational factor \(S_W\) whose reduction modulo two contains \(\Phi_W\bmod2\).
It occurs once, is reciprocal and noncyclotomic, and its degree \(d_W\) satisfies
\[
              \frac{100}{101} W\le d_W\le W-1.
\]
In fact it is the only factor of degree greater than \(W/2\).
If \(N_0,N_-,N_+\) count its complex roots on, inside and outside the unit circle respectively, then
\[
          N_-=N_+,\qquad N_0/d_W,\ N_-/d_W,\ N_+/d_W\longrightarrow 1/3.
\]
For any root \(\alpha_W\) of this factor, \(Wh(\alpha_W)\to\log 2\) and
\[
   (100/101)\log 2\le \liminf_j m(\alpha_W)
       \le \limsup_j m(\alpha_W)\le\log 2,
\]
where \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
Thus an individual irreducible family attains the boundary value in the [short-middle-gap estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v871.md#statement) with a positive proportion of the total degree.

<a id="factor"></a>
## Identifying and lifting one binary Frobenius orbit

More generally take an odd prime \(p\ge5\) such that \(2\) has order \(p-1\) modulo \(p\) and \(2^{p-1}\not\equiv1\pmod{p^2}\), and put \(W=p^j\).
Then \(2\) has order \((p-1)p^{j-1}\) modulo \(p^j\): if \(v_p(t-1)=a\ge1\) the binomial expansion gives \(v_p(t^p-1)=a+1\), and raising to any positive exponent prime to \(p\) preserves \(a\).
Apply this to \(t=2^{p-1}\) after observing that every candidate order is divisible by \(p-1\).
For \(p=101\) the certificates are \(2^{50}\equiv-1\) and \(2^{20}\equiv95\pmod{101}\), and \(2^{100}\equiv9293=1+92\cdot101\pmod{101^2}\).
The two proper-divisor tests suffice since the prime divisors of 100 are 2 and 5.

In characteristic two \(P_W=X^W-1\) is squarefree since \(W\) is odd.
For a primitive \(W\)-th root its distinct Frobenius conjugates have exponents \(1,2,4,\ldots\pmod W\), and their number is the order just computed, equal to \(\varphi(W)\).
Thus all primitive roots form one orbit and \(\Phi_W\bmod2\) is irreducible of that degree.
Factoring the monic \(P_W\) over the rationals gives monic integral factors with unchanged degree modulo two.
The orbit must occur in exactly one such factor and squarefreeness ensures multiplicity one.
That factor has degree at least \((1-1/p)W>W/2\); its monic reciprocal is also a factor of the same degree since the whole polynomial is reciprocal and has unit constant term.
Uniqueness forces it to be self-reciprocal.
The root \(-1\) occurs in \(P_W\), and the distinguished degree exceeds one, giving the upper bound \(W-1\).

If the distinguished factor were cyclotomic, write its root order as \(2^a b\) with \(b\) odd.
On reduction at a prime over two, roots of 2-power order become one, while reduction preserves the order of every root of odd order: any nonidentity odd-order root reducing to one would have a nontrivial odd-order geometric sum reducing both to zero and to one.
All conjugates of the hypothesized cyclotomic factor reduce to roots of order \(b\), and at least one reduces to a primitive \(W\)-th root by the defining binary orbit.
Hence \(b=W\).
For \(a\ge2\) the rational degree is at least \(2\varphi(W)>W\), impossible.
For \(a=1\) a primitive \(2W\)-th root \(z\) satisfies \(z^W=-1\); its equation forces \(1+z=0\), contradicting its order.
For \(a=0\), the equation at a primitive \(W\)-th root yields \(|1+z|=1\), forcing \(z+z^{-1}=-1\) and hence order three, excluded by \(p\ge5\).
This proves noncyclotomicity for every member of the family.

<a id="proportions"></a>
## Passing to one orbit in characteristic zero

All roots of \(P_W\) are algebraic units since it is monic with constant one.
Jensen's inequality and orthogonality of monomials on the unit circle give \(m(P_W)\le\frac12\log(1+4+4+1)=\frac12\log10\).
Each monic integral factor has nonnegative logarithmic Mahler measure, so \(h(\alpha_W)\le(\log10)/(2d_W)\to0\).
The hypotheses of C0345v871 now hold on this individual factor family with \(g=t=1\), \(\epsilon=1\) and \(|b/a|=2\).
Its neutral fraction tends to \(2\arcsin(1/2)/\pi=1/3\), and \(Wh(\alpha_W)\to\log2\).
Inversion pairs every exterior root with an interior root of the same irreducible polynomial, so each of those fractions tends to one third as well.
The measure bounds follow by multiplying \(Wh(\alpha_W)\) by the two degree-ratio bounds.
For any fixed prime satisfying the conditions above the same conclusions hold with \(100/101\) replaced by \(1-1/p\).
