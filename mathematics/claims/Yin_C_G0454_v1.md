## 1. The actual-order relative-unit group

Define E_O={w in O^times: w sigma(w)=1}.
There exists eta>1 in E_O such that

    E_O={epsilon eta^j: epsilon in{1,-1}, j in Z},
    alpha=eta^a for an integer a>=1,
    Q(eta)=K, Z[eta,eta^-1]=O,
    deg eta=d, m(eta)=m(alpha)/a.                       (1)

Here eta is the smallest positive greater-than-one element of E_O, not an uncomputed uniform fundamental unit.

Proof.
Any w in E_O has modulus1 at every complex embedding.
If its distinguished real value is+/-1, injectivity of that embedding makes w=+/-1.
Otherwise a sign and, if needed, inversion turn w into a positive algebraic integer gamma>1 whose original embeddings have exactly one value outside the circle, its reciprocal below1, and all others on the circle.
If [K:Q(gamma)]=e, its expanding value is repeated e times among the K embeddings.
There is only one such embedding, so e=1.
Thus gamma is a genuine Salem number of full degree d, not a smaller-field replacement.

By the accepted fixed-field theorem, all positive Salem numbers in K are powers of a smallest tau_0>1.
Include zero and negative exponents: the set of j in Z with tau_0^j in E_O is a subgroup of Z containing the positive exponent representing alpha.
It equals bZ for some b>=1.
Set eta=tau_0^b.
This proves the first two assertions and eta in O, with its inverse in O.
Since alpha=eta^a and eta in O, the two generated Laurent orders contain each other and are equal.
Also alpha in Q(eta) gives Q(eta)=K.
The accepted power-degree statement, or the same unique-expanding-embedding argument, keeps degree d.
The only expanding root gives the total measure identity.
No maximal-order assumption, regulator estimate or Dirichlet-unit algorithm is used.

## 2. An affine two-unit profile forces a scalar relative unit

Suppose actual units u,v in O^times satisfy

    u sigma(u)+2v sigma(v)=1,
    u-v=alpha^k, k in Z.                              (2)

Set z=alpha^k and w=u+2v=z+3v.
Expanding(2) with u=z+v and z sigma(z)=1 gives

    z sigma(v)+v sigma(z)+3v sigma(v)=0,
    w sigma(w)=1.                                    (3)

The element w lies in O; equation(3) makes sigma(w) its inverse in O.
Hence w in E_O.
By(1) w=epsilon eta^l for some l in Z and epsilon in{1,-1}, while z=eta^(ak).
Therefore

    u=(epsilon eta^l+2eta^(ak))/3,
    v=(epsilon eta^l-eta^(ak))/3.                      (4)

This is an exact field identity on every original conjugate.
Actual integrality and unit status of the two quotients are HYPOTHESES inherited from(2), not consequences of the displayed denominator alone.

Conversely, if the two expressions in(4) are actual units of O, their difference is alpha^k and their relative norm combination is1 by the same direct expansion.
Thus (2) is equivalent to the existence of such exponents/sign with BOTH binomial quotients integral units.
No assertion of existence for an arbitrary input is made.
