# A gap or a smaller polynomial for full interlacing inverse images of Salem traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior poles detect a strict decrease in Mahler measure

Let \(S\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Salem trace, of degree \(m\ge2\).
Take any [interlacing rational map](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#statement) different from \(g(X)=X\), written as
\[
  g(X)=\frac{A(X)}{B(X)}=X/k+c-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\frac{w_i}{X-b_i}.
\]
Here \(A,B\) are coprime integer polynomials, \(A\) is monic of degree \(s\ge1\), \(B\) has degree \(s-1\) with leading coefficient \(k\ge1\), the \(b_i\) are distinct real poles, and \(w_i>0\).
Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(B^\sharp(z)=z^{s-1}B(x)\), and let \(P_g(z)=z^{sm}B(x)^m S(g(x))\) be the full monic reciprocal inverse polynomial.
Let \(L=\log M\) be logarithmic Mahler measure and let \(r(F)\) count the roots of \(F\) strictly outside the unit circle with multiplicity.

There is an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that every \(0<L(P_g)<\eta\) has \(s\ge2\), \(k=1\), \(\sum_i w_i=1\), and at least one pole outside \([-2,2]\).
Its explicitly given monic denominator lift satisfies
\[
      0<L(B^\sharp)<\frac{L(P_g)}m,\qquad
      m\,r(B^\sharp)\le r(P_g),\qquad \deg B^\sharp=2(s-1)<2sm.      \tag{1}
\]
In particular all positive full measures of maps with poles in \([-2,2]\) have a uniform gap over all Salem sources.
The same conclusion holds with arbitrary poles when \(r(P_g)<m\), including all full outputs consisting of a single Salem minimal polynomial and cyclotomic factors.
Every full output below the gap fails to minimize positive measure among polynomials of its degree or less.

More generally, write \(P_g=FQ\) in monic integer polynomials and suppose \(0<L(P_g)<\eta\), \(L(F)>0\), and \(L(Q)\le(m-1)L(F)\).
Then \(B^\sharp\) has strictly smaller positive logarithmic measure than \(F\).
If \(\deg F\ge2(s-1)\), its degree is no greater than that of \(F\).
All the assertions use the full measure bound on \(P_g\).
The criterion for an individual Salem factor with an unrestricted cofactor remains the [trace field inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#fields).

<a id="span"></a>
## The conjugates of an irrational totally real algebraic integer have span greater than two

The coordinator supplied the span argument, the common sparse denominator across all input conjugates, and the outward comparison at exterior poles.
Suppose all conjugates of an irrational algebraic integer lie in a real interval of length at most two.
Enlarge the interval to length two, translate by an integer and reflect if necessary to give \([c-1,c+1]\) with \(0\le c\le1/2\).
These operations preserve integrality and irrationality of the number, now written \(\gamma\).
If \(c=0\), Kronecker's theorem forces the nonzero \(\gamma\) to be a real root of unity, a contradiction.
For \(c>0\) we exhibit a positive integer \(j\) with \(|x^j(x-1)|<1\) on the whole interval.
On \([c-1,0]\) the maximum is \((1-c)^j(2-c)\), on \([0,1]\) it is at most \(1/4\), and on \([1,1+c]\) it is \(c(1+c)^j\).
For \(0<c\le1/3\), take \(j=\lceil(\log2)/c\rceil\).
The left maximum is at most \((2-c)e^{-jc}<1\) and the right maximum is at most \(2ce^c\le 2e^{1/3}/3<1\).
For \(1/3\le c\le2/5\) take \(j=2\), which gives outer maxima at most \(20/27\) and \(98/125\).
For \(2/5\le c\le1/2\) take \(j=1\), which gives at most \(24/25\) and \(3/4\).
The nonzero algebraic integer \(\gamma^j(\gamma-1)\) would consequently have a nonzero integer norm of absolute value less than one.
This proves the span assertion, in particular for each Salem trace polynomial \(S\).

<a id="mass"></a>
## A small full measure forces monicity and residue mass one

Apply the [finite-input theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#finite) at \(L(P_g)\le1\).
Only finitely many original Salem numbers occur, so their trace spans \(D_S\) exceed \(2\) by a uniform positive amount whenever this set is nonempty.
The same theorem bounds \(|c|\) there.
All inverse traces are real and have modulus at most \(2\cosh L(P_g)\), and consecutive inverse roots of each real fiber straddle the poles.
Take \(t=2\cosh L(P_g)+L(P_g)\) for positive \(L(P_g)\), so all roots and poles are strictly inside \((-t,t)\).
Every source root lies strictly between \(g(-t)\) and \(g(t)\), by the outer real branches.
Writing \(W=\sum_i w_i\), including zero for the empty sum, gives
\[
        D_S<g(t)-g(-t)
         =\frac{2t}{k}-\sum_i\frac{2t w_i}{t^2-b_i^2}
         \le\frac{2t}{k}-\frac{2W}{t}.                             \tag{2}
\]
As \(L(P_g)\to0\), one has \(t\to2\).
If \(k\ge2\), the right side is at most \(t\), contradicting the uniform excess of the span over two.
Hence all sufficiently small positive full measures require \(k=1\).
For \(s=1\) this leaves the integral translations \(X+c\) with \(c\ne0\).
There are finitely many since \(c\) is bounded, and the source also belongs to a finite set, so their positive full measures are separated from zero.
For \(k=1,\ s\ge2\), the coefficient of \(X^{-1}\) in the expansion of \(A/B\) at infinity is \(-W\), an integer because \(B\) is monic.
Thus \(W\ge1\).
If \(W\ge2\), the upper bound in (2) is at most \(2t-4/t\to2\), again impossible.
Consequently \(W=1\) whenever the positive full measure is sufficiently small.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## A common sparse rational expression when all poles are neutral

Suppose now \(k=1,\ W=1\) and every \(b_i\in[-2,2]\).
Then \(B^\sharp\) is a monic integer polynomial with all roots on the unit circle, hence cyclotomic up to products.
Choose roots of unity \(\xi_i\) with \(\xi_i+\xi_i^{-1}=b_i\).
This includes the endpoints.
The Laurent expansion at infinity of \(X+c-g(X)=\sum_i w_i/(X-b_i)\) has integer coefficients because both \(A,B\) are monic and \(c\) is an integer.
Thus all sums \(\sum_i w_i b_i^j\), \(j\ge0\), are integers.
The integral recurrence for \(D_j(\xi+\xi^{-1})=\xi^j+\xi^{-j}\), starting with \(D_0=2,\ D_1=X\), gives an integer periodic sequence
\[
                 F_j=\sum_i w_i(\xi_i^j+\xi_i^{-j}).
\]
On the finite cyclic group with period a common multiple of the orders, its normalized Fourier coefficients are the positive masses \(w_i\) at \(\xi_i,\xi_i^{-1}\), combined at coincidences.
Their absolute sum is \(2W=2\).
By [Sanders's bounded Fourier norm theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001), \(F\) is a signed sum of at most an absolute number \(J_0\) of coset indicators.
As in the [reconstruction at torsion poles](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#sparse), write its positive-index generating series as
\[
             \sum_{j\ge1}F_jz^j=\sum_{i=1}^J\epsilon_i
                   \frac{z^{r_i}}{1-z^{d_i}},\qquad J\le J_0,
                   \quad 1\le r_i\le d_i,\quad \epsilon_i=\pm1 .
\]
The zero residue class uses \(r_i=d_i\).
Put \(V=\prod_i(1-z^{d_i})\), \(V_i=V/(1-z^{d_i})\), and \(D=(1-z^2)V\).
The geometric-series identity
\[
   \sum_i\frac{w_i}{x-b_i}=\frac z{1-z^2}
                          \left(1+\sum_{j\ge1}F_jz^j\right)
\]
gives the same numerator for every source conjugate,
\[
          U=D\,z(x+c)-z^2\left(V+\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{r_i}V_i\right),
                  \qquad g(x)=\frac{U}{zD}.                         \tag{3}
\]
Both \(U\) and \(zD\) are integer polynomials with at most \(K=(J_0+8)2^{J_0}\) terms, since \(z(x+c)=1+cz+z^2\).
The polynomial \(Q_0=(zD)^m S(U/(zD))\) is integral, has at most \((m+1)K^m\) terms, and satisfies
\[
                         (B^\sharp)^m Q_0=D^m P_g .                  \tag{4}
\]
This identity follows by substituting (3) and \(B^\sharp=z^{s-1}B(x)\) into the definition of \(P_g\).
All clearing factors have multiplicative measure one, so \(Q_0\ne0\) and \(L(Q_0)=L(P_g)\).
For \(L(P_g)\le1\) the finite input list bounds \(m\).
The [fixed number of terms theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives a uniform gap for positive \(L(Q_0)\) in (4).
Combining with the monicity and residue-mass restrictions proves that some pole lies outside \([-2,2]\) for every sufficiently small positive full measure.

<a id="outward"></a>
## Matching exterior poles to farther roots in each fiber

For any real \(v\), define \(h(v)=\operatorname{arcosh}(|v|/2)\) if \(|v|>2\) and \(h(v)=0\) otherwise.
The two roots of \(z+z^{-1}=v\) contribute exactly \(h(v)\) to the logarithmic measure of their monic quadratic.
In each real fiber \(g(X)=y\), write the ordered inverse traces as \(t_1<b_1<t_2<\cdots<b_{s-1}<t_s\).
Match each pole \(b_i<-2\) with \(t_i\), and each \(b_i>2\) with \(t_{i+1}\).
The matches are distinct even when exterior poles are adjacent, and the matched \(h(t)\) is strictly larger than \(h(b_i)\) on both rays.
Thus if at least one pole is exterior, each of the \(m\) source fibers contributes strictly more than \(\sum_i h(b_i)\), and at least one exterior root for each exterior pole.
For \(k=1\), \(\sum_i h(b_i)=L(B^\sharp)>0\).
Counting the disjoint fibers proves (1).
Any positive-measure irreducible factor of \(B^\sharp\) therefore also has positive measure strictly below \(L(P_g)/m\).
The cofactor assertion follows from \(L(P_g)=L(F)+L(Q)\le mL(F)\).
In particular no sequence of positive full outputs with measures tending to zero can consist of degree minima for the unrestricted problem.

If \(r(P_g)<2m\) and \(0<L(P_g)<\eta\), then (1) forces \(r(B^\sharp)=1\).
Its single exterior root is real, its inverse is the only interior root, and both occur in the same irreducible factor since all factors are monic units.
Replacing the exterior root by its negative if necessary gives a positive Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit.
The latter has positive logarithmic measure at least \(\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)\), because its signed trace is an integer of absolute value at least three.
Decreasing \(\eta\) to at most this constant therefore gives an actual smaller Salem number in this case.
For general exterior counts (1) supplies a smaller integer polynomial with all traces real.
It makes no assumption that this denominator polynomial is itself an inverse image of the same kind.
