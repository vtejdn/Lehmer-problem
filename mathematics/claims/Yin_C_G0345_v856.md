# Independent torsion fibers and Mahler measures of their roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Torsion specialization over the cyclotomic closure

Let \(F(U,V)=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\) define the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement), and let \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}=\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)\).
Fix a primitive signed pair \((k,l)\) with \(kl\ne0\), put \(T=V^k/U^l\), \(n=4|k|+2|l|\), and \(N=\max(|k|,|l|)\).
For all but finitely many roots of unity \(s\), every point over \(T=s\) has degree \(n\) over \(H\).
Equivalently, for every pair of roots of unity \((\xi,\eta)\) with \(\eta^k/\xi^l=s\), clearing the lowest monomial from \(F(\xi X^k,\eta X^l)\) gives an irreducible degree-\(n\) polynomial over \(H\).
The exceptional set here depends only on the direction.

Outside the [finite set of exceptional directions for geometric monodromy](Yin_C_G0345_v855.md#statement), fix any finite list of distinct roots of unity \(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_b\).
For all but finitely many roots of unity \(t\), the compositum of the splitting fields over \(H\) of the fibers \(T=\zeta_i t\) has group \(S_n^b\) in its action on these fibers.
In particular the fields are linearly disjoint over \(H\).
The exceptional parameters may depend on both the direction and the list.
For a singleton with \(\zeta_1=1\), the full group fails at both \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Rational degree and logarithmic Mahler measure

For every root \(\alpha\) of a section in a primitive nonaxis direction, let \(w=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}\xi,\operatorname{ord}\eta)\).
Then
\[
           [\mathbb Q(\alpha,\xi,\eta):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\le16.
\]
Whenever the section is irreducible over \(H\), its roots satisfy
\[
 [\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\ge \frac{n\varphi(w)}{16},\qquad
 m(\alpha)\ge \frac{\varphi(w)}{16}\bigl(m(F)-\epsilon_F(N)\bigr),
 \qquad \epsilon_F(N)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here \(m(\alpha)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of its primitive minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\), and the error is uniform in both twists and the root.
There is a constant \(c_F>0\) such that \(m(\alpha)\ge c_F\varphi(w)\) for every such irreducible section, including bounded \(N\).
These conclusions apply to the cofinite set of torsion invariants for each direction above.
They leave open the possibility of a sequence of small measures on exceptional fibers of increasing primitive norm, including the problem for untwisted sections.

<a id="single"></a>
## The original cover in every nonaxis direction

On the smooth projective normalization \(C\), the [boundary orders](Yin_C_G0345_v854.md#critical) give degree \(n\) for \(T\), and its divisor has coefficients of absolute values \(|k|\) and \(|l|\).
All boundary points map to zero or infinity.
The pullback by \(z\mapsto z^m\) has geometric generic equation \(z^m=T\).
Over \(\mathbb C(C)\) adjoining one root gives a Galois extension whose automorphisms multiply it by a subgroup of \(\mu_m\) of order \(d\mid m\).
Its \(d\)-th power is in \(\mathbb C(C)\), so \(m/d\) divides every coefficient of the divisor of \(T\).
Primitivity forces \(d=m\), proving geometric irreducibility for every multiplication pullback.
The [torsion fiber theorem of Zannier](../audits/literature/Zan10/Yin_U001.md#zan10-u001) now gives the first full-degree assertion.

For any torsion \(s\), choose integers \(i,j\) with \(ik+jl=1\).
One possible twist is \((s^{-j},s^i)\).
For any twist with invariant \(s\), the parametrization \(U=\xi X^k,V=\eta X^l\) has inverse \(X=(U/\xi)^i(V/\eta)^j\) on the coset.
The fiber points and their section roots thus have identical fields over \(H\).

<a id="product"></a>
## Specializing the Galois closure

The compositum of the generic splitting fields over \(H(t)\) acts faithfully by separate permutations of the \(b\) generic fibers.
Its arithmetic group lies in \(S_n^b\) and contains the [full geometric product](Yin_C_G0345_v855.md#group), so the groups agree and the Galois cover is geometrically connected.
After every multiplication pullback the geometric group still has order \((n!)^b\) by the same result, equal to the original degree.
Thus the normal cover satisfies Zannier's pullback condition, and his theorem gives residue degree \((n!)^b\) over \(H\) outside finitely many torsion parameters.
The fibers in question are unramified by [squarefreeness](Yin_C_G0345_v852.md#statement).
At an unramified parameter the residue Galois group is the decomposition subgroup, acting on the specialized sheets through its generic permutation representation, as follows by lifting the geometric fiber to a geometric point of the normal cover.
The action on the union of the sheets is faithful because the generic permutation representation is faithful.
Hence the residue field is generated by the coordinates of all the specialized points, which gives the claimed splitting compositum and group.

Coordinate inversion \((U,V)\mapsto(U^{-1},V^{-1})\) preserves \(C\) by reciprocity and takes \(T\) to \(T^{-1}\).
At \(T=\pm1\) it therefore commutes with the specialized Galois action and fixes no sheet, since a fixed point would have both coordinates in \(\{1,-1\}\) and the curve [contains no torsion point](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
For \(n\ge3\) a nonidentity permutation has proper centralizer in \(S_n\), so the specialized group is proper.
This restriction still permits the fiber to be irreducible.

<a id="intersection"></a>
## Intersections with a conjugate reciprocal curve

The four corner coefficients of \(F\) are one, and the signed weight \(ki+lj\) has unique extreme corners.
Thus the constant and leading coefficients of the cleared section are roots of unity, and every root is an algebraic unit.
A torsion root would give a torsion point of \(C\).
Kronecker's theorem therefore supplies a complex embedding with \(|\alpha|\ne1\), which we fix and extend to the twists.
For every conjugate pair \((x,y)\) of \((\xi,\eta)\) over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the section equation and its complex conjugate after coordinate inversion give
\[
 F(\alpha^k x,\alpha^l y)=0,\qquad
 F(\bar\alpha^{-k}x,\bar\alpha^{-l}y)=0.
\]
The two geometrically irreducible curves are distinct because their scaling ratio \((|\alpha|^{2k},|\alpha|^{2l})\) differs from the identity and the [translation stabilizer is trivial](Yin_C_G0345_v855.md#translations).
Their closures in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) have bidegree \((4,2)\) and intersection number \(4\cdot2+2\cdot4=16\).
Distinct embeddings over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) give distinct conjugate pairs, proving the degree bound.

The twist field \(K=\mathbb Q(\xi,\eta)\) is the full cyclotomic field of order \(w\).
Irreducibility over \(H\) gives \([K(\alpha):K]=n\), so the tower law gives the rational degree estimate.
The [individual height theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(nh(\alpha)=m(F)+o_F(1)\) uniformly because \(n\) is the projected width, and gives \(m(F)>0\).
Multiplying the degree estimate by \(h(\alpha)\) proves the asserted asymptotic lower bound.
For large \(N\) one may take \(c_F=m(F)/32\), and for the remaining bounded norms the [torsion-order bound](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) gives a positive minimum for the corresponding lower-bound constants.
