# Factor measures at integer Salem seeds and the density of the radical bound

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower bounds uniform in the choice of a factor

Let \(a\ge3\) and \(n\ge7\) be integers, let \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and put \(G(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Write \(r\) for its unique exterior root, \(P\) for the Salem minimal polynomial of \(r\), and \(m(F)=\log M(F)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Let \(R\) be the largest positive integer \(l\) for which \(r^{1/l}\in\mathbb Q(r)\), taking positive real radicals.
Use \(\operatorname{rad}(u)=\prod_{p\mid u}p\) and set
\[
 H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}
          \frac{a+j}{\operatorname{rad}(a+j)},\qquad
 B(a)=\operatorname{lcm}\{1,\ldots,\lfloor2(a+1)^{5/3}\rfloor\}.
\]
Then \(R\mid B(a)\) at every delay \(n\ge7\).
If
\[
                   n>\tfrac12\log(a+1)\log^3(3(n+1)),                 \tag{1}
\]
then \(R\mid H(a)\).
For every integer \(h\ge1\), each monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(F\) of \(G(X^h)\) satisfies
\[
         m(F)\ge \frac{\log r}{B(a)}>\frac{\log(a-1)}{B(a)},\qquad
         m(F)\ge \frac{\log r}{H(a)}\quad\text{under (1)}.            \tag{2}
\]
Thus a fixed seed has a positive lower bound simultaneous in the delay, sign and substitution.
Whenever \(H(a)=1\), condition (1) gives irreducibility of \(P(X^h)\) for every \(h\).
The set of seeds with \(H(a)=1\) has natural density \(\prod_p(1-3/p^2)>0\).

For the variation with \(a\), let \(D(a)\) be the infimum of \(m(F)\) over both signs, delays \(n\ge7\) satisfying (1), all \(h\ge1\) and all such factors.
Every \(a\) has admissible delays since \(n/\log^3(3(n+1))\to\infty\).
For each fixed \(0<s<1\) and \(0<t<1\),
\[
                  \overline{\operatorname{dens}}\{a:D(a)<t\log a\}
                          \ \ll_s\ t^s .                               \tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(D(a)\) tends to infinity in natural density.
More generally \(D(a)/f(a)\) tends to infinity in natural density for every positive \(f(a)=o(\log a)\).

<a id="radicals"></a>
## Prime powers for all seeds

The [root count for integer seeds](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(a-1<r<a+1\), \(d=\deg P\ge n-1\), and \(G=PU\) with \(U\) a product of distinct factors from \(X-1,X+1\).
By the [positive radical exponent theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement), the exponents of field radicals are precisely the divisors of \(R\).
If \(p^e\mid R\) for a prime \(p\), apply the [sparse norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) to \(G\) at the first radical \(r^{1/p}\).
This real value lies strictly between \(1\) and \(r\), so \(G(r^{1/p})\ne0\).
The sum of the absolute coefficients of \(G\) is \(2(a+1)\), and \(m(P)=\log r\), giving
\[
        p^e\le2(a+1)r^{(n+1)/(pd)}
              \le2(a+1)^{5/3}.
\]
Here \((n+1)/(pd)\le(n+1)/(2(n-1))\le2/3\).
Taking the least common multiple bound over the prime powers proves \(R\mid B(a)\).

Under (1) all prime divisors of \(R\) are less than \(n\) by the [degree and measure bound](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#dilation).
For odd \(p\), the [delayed trace calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#traces) gives \(p^{e+1}\mid(a-1)a(a+1)\).
An odd prime can divide at most one of the three consecutive integers, so \(p^e\mid H(a)\).

For \(2^e\mid R\) with \(e\ge1\), let \(b\in\{0,1\}\) be the multiplicity of \(-1\) in \(U\).
Use the same trace congruences for \(P\) at \(j=1,3\), keeping the modulus \(2^{e+1}\).
The indices \(2j\le6<n\) are before the first delayed term, and the contribution of \(U\) to the difference of the two sums is \(2b\).
It follows that
\[
              a^2-a\equiv a^6-a^3\equiv2b\pmod{2^{e+1}} .
\]
When \(a\) is even, the second difference is zero modulo four, forcing \(b=0\).
The first congruence then gives \(2^{e+1}\mid a\).
When \(a\) is odd and \(b=0\), it gives \(2^{e+1}\mid a-1\).
When \(a\) is odd and \(b=1\), the factorization \(a^2-a-2=(a-2)(a+1)\) instead gives \(2^{e+1}\mid a+1\).
Thus \(2^e\mid H(a)\) in every case, proving \(R\mid H(a)\) under (1).

Substitution in \(U\) contributes only roots of unity.
For the noncyclotomic factors the [exact minimum at dilation \(h\)](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) is \(m(P)/\gcd(h,R)\), and \(P(X^h)\) is irreducible if \(\gcd(h,R)=1\).
These statements give (2) and the asserted irreducibility.

<a id="distribution"></a>
## The limiting distribution of the integer bound

Let \(V_p(a)=v_p(H(a))\).
For each prime \(p\) and \(j\ge1\), the event \(V_p(a)\ge j\) consists of the residue classes \(0,1,-1\bmod p^{j+1}\).
These three are distinct, including at \(p=2\) because \(j+1\ge2\).
The Chinese remainder theorem gives independent limiting valuations \(V_p\) with
\[
                       \Pr(V_p\ge j)=3p^{-j-1}\quad(j\ge1).
\]
Indeed finite intersections of threshold events have the product densities, and subtraction gives the corresponding exact valuations.

For \(3\le a\le X\), any prime \(p>Y\) contributing to \(H(a)\) satisfies \(p\le\sqrt{X+1}\) and \(p^2\mid a+i\) for some \(i\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
The count for each \(p\) is at most \(3(X/p^2+1)\).
After division by \(X\), summation and then passage to the limsup, the bound is at most \(3\sum_{p>Y}p^{-2}\), which tends to zero.
Among primes \(p\le Y\), the upper density of any valuation exceeding a cutoff \(J\) is at most \(\sum_{p\le Y}3p^{-J-2}\), which tends to zero for fixed \(Y\).
The same two bounds hold for the independent variables in the limit.
In particular \(H_\infty=\prod_p p^{V_p}\) is almost surely a finite integer.
After both truncations only finitely many values occur.
It follows that \(\{a:H(a)\in S\}\) has natural density \(\Pr(H_\infty\in S)\) for every subset \(S\) of the positive integers.
Taking \(S=\{1\}\) gives the stated convergent positive product.

For \(0<s<1\), summing the valuation tails at one prime gives
\[
 \mathbb E H_\infty^s
     =\prod_p\left(1+\frac{3(p^s-1)}{p^2(1-p^{s-1})}\right)
     =:C_s<\infty .
\]
The factors follow from independence and monotone convergence over finite sets of primes.
The excess over one at a prime is \(O_s(p^{s-2})\), which proves convergence.

<a id="density"></a>
## Consequences for all qualifying delays and substitutions

From (2) we have \(D(a)\ge\log(a-1)/H(a)\).
Given any \(0<\eta<1\), for all sufficiently large \(a\) the inequality \(D(a)<t\log a\) forces \(H(a)>(1-\eta)/t\).
The limiting distribution and Markov's inequality bound the upper density by \(C_s(t/(1-\eta))^s\).
Taking \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves (3).
For a positive \(f(a)=o(\log a)\), any fixed \(M>0\) and any \(t>0\), eventually \(Mf(a)<t\log a\).
Applying (3) and then taking \(t\downarrow0\) shows that \(\{a:D(a)\le Mf(a)\}\) has density zero.
The case \(f=1\) gives divergence in density of the factor measures themselves.
