# Counting coefficient lists that admit two genuine double contacts

<a id="statement"></a>
## The saving from disjoint groups of four evaluation points

Let \(B\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have degree \(c\) and nonzero constant term, let \(B^*(Y)=Y^cB(1/Y)\), and let \(L\ge1\) bound the absolute value of each coefficient.
A placement is a rational function \(f(Y)=sY^v B(Y^k)/B^*(Y^k)\), \(s\in\{1,-1\},\ v\in\mathbb Z,\ k\ge1\).
Require a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit \(x\) such that \(f(X^n)-X\) vanishes to order at least two at \(x,x^2\) for some integer \(n\ge2\).
Require the two unreduced denominators to be nonzero there.
Fix \(j\ge1\) and \(c\ge8j-1\).
Among the integer coefficient lists in this box, the number admitting any such placement with
\[
                    [\mathbb Q(x^{2nk}):\mathbb Q]\ge2j
\]
is at most \(O_{c,j}((2L+1)^{c+1-j})\).
The constant is effective and independent of \(x,v,k,n\) and \(L\).
The same list is counted once regardless of how many widths, exponents or degrees it represents.

In the [strict-budget degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v469.md#statement) the all-power field of the target has degree at least \(86\), as in its [counting restrictions](Yin_C_G0345_v469.md#counts).
It lies inside \(\mathbb Q(x^{2nk})\), so one may take \(j=\min(43,\lfloor(c+1)/8\rfloor)\) for every passing list.
In particular at each fixed eligible span a vanishing proportion of coefficient lists admit any of the placements as \(L\to\infty\).
The estimate counts coefficient lists, whereas the [fixed-width gap implication](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#narrow) requires an upper bound for the number of degrees.

<a id="parametrization"></a>
## Solving for two values in each group of interpolation data

Set \(f_0=sB/B^*\) and \(z=x^{nk}\).
The value and derivative equations at the two contacts give
\[
 f_0(z^2)=f_0(z)^2,\qquad
 \frac{z^2f_0'(z^2)}{f_0(z^2)}=\frac{zf_0'(z)}{f_0(z)} .
\]
Here the monomial in the placement cancels from the first equality, and its logarithmic derivative contributes the same constant \(v\) at both nodes in the second.
The numerator values are nonzero by the value equations.
The relations persist at every conjugate of \(z\).

The conjugates of \(z^2\) occur in distinct inverse pairs because \(x\) is reciprocal and nontorsion.
Choose \(j\) distinct pairs, one representative of each, and conjugates \(z_i\) of \(z\) squaring to those representatives.
All \(4j\) points \(z_i,z_i^{-1},z_i^2,z_i^{-2}\) are distinct.
Indeed coincidences between two points from the first powers, or between two from the second powers, would identify the same chosen inverse pair or force torsion.
A first power and a second power have different positive absolute logarithmic Weil heights.

For each \(i\) record the values and first derivatives of \(B\) at its four points.
Write \(b_u=B(u),\,a_u=B'(u)\).
The value equation determines precisely one of the four values by
\[
 b_{z_i^2}=s\, b_{z_i}^2 b_{z_i^{-2}}/b_{z_i^{-1}}^2 .
\]
The logarithmic derivative at \(u\) is \(u a_u/b_u+u^{-1}a_{u^{-1}}/b_{u^{-1}}-c\).
Once the four values are known the other equation determines \(a_{z_i^2}\) linearly from the three remaining derivatives with nonzero coefficient \(z_i^2/b_{z_i^2}\).
Thus \(z_i\) and six freely specified data suffice for each group.

Let \(P(Y)=\prod_u(Y-u)^2\) over the \(4j\) points.
Write \(B=PQ+R,\ \deg R<8j\), with \(Q=0\) when \(c=8j-1\).
The values and first derivatives determine \(R\) uniquely because a polynomial of degree below \(8j\) vanishing twice at every point must be zero.
The square interpolation matrix is therefore invertible, and its inverse expresses the coefficients of \(R\) rationally in the points and data wherever the points are distinct.
Together with the \(c+1-8j\) coefficients of \(Q\), this gives a rational parametrization covering every retained list using
\[
                         j+6j+c+1-8j=c+1-j
\]
scalar variables.
There is one such parametrization for each sign, and all denominators used in it are nonzero at the inputs just constructed.

<a id="grid"></a>
## Counting integral points in the image

For a fixed sign let \(a=c+1-j\), and write the \(c+1\) coefficient functions from the preceding construction over a common polynomial denominator in these \(a\) variables.
Their numerators and denominator have some common total degree bound \(l\ge1\), computable from the interpolation matrix.
Take the integer grid of side \(S=2\lfloor L\rfloor+1\).
For each covered point the product of its \(c+1\) univariate Lagrange interpolation polynomials is one there and zero at every other grid point.
Each product has total degree at most \(t=(c+1)(S-1)\).
After substitution of the rational parametrization and multiplication by the common denominator to the \(t\)th power, all these products give polynomials of degree at most \(lt\) in \(a\) variables.
They are linearly independent because evaluation at one preimage of each covered point yields a diagonal matrix with nonzero diagonal.
Thus there are at most \(\binom{a+lt}{a}\) points per sign.
This proves the claimed effective bound without estimating the number of conjugates or parameters lying in an integer box.

## Excluding every placement of two explicit quartic lists

For each of the two quartic coefficient lists in the [degree-131262 construction](Yin_C_G0345_v467.md#example), no placement at either sign admits even one genuine pair as above.
This assertion permits arbitrarily large widths, exponents and coefficient budgets.
Define
\[
         K_s(T)= B(T^2)B^*(T)^2-s B^*(T^2)B(T)^2.
\]
The two displayed contact relations force the nontorsion \(z=x^{nk}\) to be a multiple root of \(K_s\), by differentiating the ratio \(f_0(T^2)/f_0(T)^2\) where it equals one.
At \(s=1\) one has \((T-1)^3\mid K_s\) for these lists.
Indeed \(B(1)\ne0\), \(F=B/B^*\) satisfies \(F(1)=1,\ F(1/T)=1/F(T)\), and hence the formal power series \(\log F(\exp u)\) is odd.
Its linear term cancels in \(\log F(\exp(2u))-2\log F(\exp u)\), giving order at least three.

For the following Euclidean computations in \(\mathbb F_{17}[T]\), the coefficient lists modulo 17 are \((6,12,1,5,1)\) and \((11,15,14,12,1)\) in ascending order.
The table gives the leading coefficient of its degree-16 reduction, the degrees of its derivative and successive nonzero Euclidean remainders, and the monic last remainder.

| Constant coefficient of \(B\) | \(s\) | Leading coefficient | Degrees | Monic gcd |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| \(4103\) | \(1\) | \(13\) | \(15,14,\ldots,2\) | \((T-1)^2\) |
| \(4103\) | \(-1\) | \(8\) | \(15,13,12,11,8,7,6,4,3,2,1,0\) | \(1\) |
| \(-1757\) | \(1\) | \(8\) | \(15,14,\ldots,2\) | \((T-1)^2\) |
| \(-1757\) | \(-1\) | \(13\) | \(15,14,\ldots,0\) | \(1\) |

For both signs the leading coefficients survive reduction, so reduction of a characteristic-zero common divisor can preserve its degree by taking its primitive integral representative.
At sign \(1\) the forced square exhausts the gcd over \(\mathbb Q\), and at sign \(-1\) the gcd is constant.
Thus neither list has a nontorsion multiple root at either sign.
