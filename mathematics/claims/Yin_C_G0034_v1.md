Put n=L+1 and z(t)=(1,t,...,t^L)^T.

There exists a RATIONAL symmetric POSITIVE DEFINITE n-by-n matrix Q such that the exact Laurent identity

    z(t^-1)^T Q z(t)=h(t)                           (1)

holds, equivalently

    sum_(i=0)^(L-ell) Q_(i,i+ell)=h_ell,
    0<=ell<=L.                                    (2)

Consequently there are EXACTLY n strictly positive rational weights d_a and nonzero rational ordinary polynomials p_a of degree at most L with

    h(t)=sum_(a=0)^L d_a p_a(t)p_a(t^-1).           (3)

The n polynomials from the construction are linearly independent over Q.
This is a weighted rational square sum, not a rational single spectral factor and not an unweighted rational square-sum assertion.
The word dimension refers to the consecutive monomial vector z; no minimum rank or minimum size over arbitrary sparse monomial bases is claimed.
If L>0, the degree bound L cannot be decreased for every summand in an ordinary-polynomial representation of this actual h, since lower degrees cannot produce lag L.

## 3. Complete real factorization after subtracting a rational constant

Continuity of the finite Laurent polynomial on the compact unit circle gives a positive minimum mu.
Choose a RATIONAL gamma with 0<gamma<mu; rational density here follows, for example, by choosing an integer b>2/mu and taking gamma=1/b.
Thus g=h-gamma is strictly positive on the entire circle.
For L=0, g is a positive real constant and q=sqrt(g) is a real constant with g=q q*.
The remaining argument treats L>0.
Subtracting a constant retains the actual radius L and h_L!=0.

The ordinary polynomial R(t)=t^L g(t) has degree EXACTLY 2L, nonzero constant and leading coefficient h_L, real coefficients and

    R(t)=t^(2L) R(t^-1).                          (4)

The real coefficients imply conjugation symmetry of its roots with multiplicities.
Equation(4), and the fact that zero is not a root, imply reciprocal symmetry with multiplicities: if rho!=0 is a root of multiplicity a, then 1/rho has the same multiplicity.
This follows directly by substituting a local factor (t-rho)^a into(4); the factor t^(2L) is a nonvanishing analytic factor near 1/rho.
There are no unit-circle roots, since R(t)=t^L g(t) and g(t)>0 there.
Reciprocal symmetry therefore pairs inside roots with outside roots, so precisely L roots lie outside and L inside, counted with multiplicities.

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, take the monic polynomial

    q_0(t)=product_(rho outside, with multiplicity)(t-rho).

The outside multiset is stable under conjugation, so every coefficient of q_0 equals its conjugate and is REAL.
It has degree L and nonzero constant.
Define q_0#(t)=t^L q_0(t^-1), also a real ordinary polynomial of degree L.
Its root multiset is exactly the reciprocal inside multiset.
Thus R and q_0 q_0# have the SAME complete degree-2L root multiset and differ by a nonzero scalar c.
Their real coefficients and nonzero real leading coefficients give c in R minus{0}.
Dividing that polynomial identity by t^L gives

    g(t)=c q_0(t)q_0(t^-1).                       (5)

On any circle point, q_0(t^-1)=overline(q_0(t)) and q_0(t)!=0.
Since g(t)>0, equation(5) forces c>0.
Put q=sqrt(c)q_0 in R[t].
Then g=q q* as an EXACT Laurent identity and deg q=L.
This proves the strict real scalar factorization locally, including multiplicities, conjugation, reciprocal pairing and the scaling sign.
The coefficients of q are not claimed rational.

Pad the real coefficient column a of q to length n (padding is unnecessary at actual L>0).
Put

    Q_* = a a^T + (gamma/n) I_n.

Then Q_* is REAL symmetric, Q_*>=(gamma/n)I_n, and

    z(t^-1)^T Q_* z(t)=q(t)q(t^-1)+gamma=h(t).     (6)

The scalar shift contributes gamma because sum_(i=0)^L t^-i t^i=n.

## 4. Exact rational affine correction with a complete positivity bound

For real symmetric n-by-n B define its diagonal-sum map Phi by

    Phi(B)_ell=sum_(i=0)^(L-ell) B_(i,i+ell),
    ell=0,...,L.

This is a rational linear map.
The associated negative-lag coefficients agree by symmetry, so Phi(B)=(h_0,...,h_L) is equivalent to(1).
It is not enough to approximate the Laurent coefficients: they must be corrected EXACTLY.

Here is an explicit rational right inverse.
For a real column c=(c_0,...,c_L), let J(c) have entry c_0 at(0,0), entries c_ell at BOTH(0,ell) and(ell,0) for ell>=1, and every other entry zero.
Then J(c) is symmetric and

    Phi(J(c))=c.                                 (7)

Each pair with ell>0 changes only its own positive-lag sum once; the reflected entry supplies the matching negative lag, not a second positive-lag contribution.
The diagonal correction changes only lag zero.
In particular J maps rational columns to rational matrices, and J(h_0,...,h_L) is a rational point of the affine fiber, without any positivity claim for that base point.

Choose a positive RATIONAL eta with

    eta < gamma / [2 n^2(n+1)].                   (8)

Choose a RATIONAL symmetric B with |B_ij-(Q_*)_ij|<eta for all i,j: select its upper-triangular entries independently by rational density, and reflect them.
Explicitly, one common integer denominator b>1/eta and integer floor approximations to b(Q_*)_ij provide errors below1/b<eta.
This is an existence construction on real coefficients, not a performed numerical rounding.

Let c=(h_0,...,h_L)-Phi(B), a RATIONAL column, and set

    Q=B+J(c).                                    (9)

Equation(7) gives Phi(Q)=h exactly.
Since Phi(Q_*)=h and a positive-lag sum has n-ell terms,

    |c_ell| <= (n-ell) eta <= n eta.

Consequently every entry of Q-Q_* has absolute value at most(n+1)eta.
For any real column x, the elementary inequalities give

    |x^T(Q-Q_*)x|
       <=(n+1)eta (sum_i|x_i|)^2
       <=n(n+1)eta ||x||^2
       <gamma/(2n) ||x||^2.                       (10)

Together with(6), this proves Q>(gamma/(2n))I_n, in particular Q is RATIONAL, symmetric and POSITIVE DEFINITE.
Equations(7)--(10) also prove rational points are dense in this rational affine fiber at every real point: replace Q_* by ANY real symmetric point C with Phi(C)=h, approximate C by rational symmetric B and use(9).
