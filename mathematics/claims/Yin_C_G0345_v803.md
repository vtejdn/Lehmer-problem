# Simultaneous cyclotomic zeros in large families of progression relations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Zeros of every progression section

Use the [progression families](Yin_C_G0345_v797.md#statement) with degree bound \(N\), algebraic degree \(d\), step set \(A\) and multiplicities \(r_j\).
Write \(Q_{j,t}\) for the section of the difference of two binary words in residue class \(t\bmod j\).
Under any of the three controlled-span menus there, including the simultaneous prime and dyadic choice, fix positive integers \(K,T\) with
\[
              KT\le b\sqrt{d/\log(ed)}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(b>0\) depending only on the density and root discriminant constants, there is a subfamily of at least \(\exp(\delta N\log(2)/4)\) words in which every difference satisfies, simultaneously,
\[
 P^{r_j}\mid Q_{j,t},\qquad (X^q-1)^{Tr_j}\mid Q_{j,t}
       \quad(j\in A,\ 0\le t<j,\ 1\le q\le K).
\]
Thus even on the shortest dyadic sections, whose length is of order \(d\), all derivatives below order \(Tr_j\) can vanish at every root of unity of order at most \(K\).
After decreasing the fixed step cutoff constant, the subfamily can retain the original guaranteed size \(\exp(\delta N\log(2)/2)\).
Thus the original size guarantee and progression divisibility alone cannot ensure a nonzero value among the prescribed jets.
A choice of a particular collision with an additional nonvanishing condition remains possible.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the integer moments on each residue class

For any family of binary words and a positive step \(s\), put \(L=\lceil N/s\rceil\).
To impose \((X^s-1)^u\) on differences, record for each residue \(w\bmod s\) the integers \(\sum_{v\ge0}a_{sv+w}\binom va\) for \(0\le a<u\), padding the word with zeros up to length \(sL\).
They are precisely the Taylor coefficients at one of the section in residue \(w\).
The direct sum of the residue classes shows that agreement of the record is equivalent to the desired divisibility.
Each coordinate is between zero and \(\binom L{a+1}\) inclusive by summation of binomial coefficients.
If \(u\le N/s\), the logarithm of the number of records is at most
\[
 s\sum_{k=1}^u\log(1+\tbinom Lk)\le C s u^2\log(eN/(su)).
\]
Indeed use \(\binom Lk\le(eL/k)^k\), \(k\log(u/k)\le u/e\), and \(L\le 2N/s\).
On any starting family, keeping the largest fiber of all records costs at most the sum of their log counts in log cardinality.
For each \(j,q\) take \(s=jq\) and \(u=Tr_j\).
Divisibility of the entire difference by \((X^{jq}-1)^u\) is equivalent to divisibility of every \(j\)-section by \((X^q-1)^u\), by expanding in powers of \(X^j\).
If \(KT\le d\), the needed size condition follows from \(jdr_j\le N\).
Summing over \(q\le K\) gives an upper bound for the loss
\[
 C(KT)^2\sum_{j\in A} j r_j^2\log(eN/(jr_j)).
\]
This finite bound applies to arbitrary masks and to any initial family, with no independence requirement.

<a id="menus"></a>
## Cost at the controlled span

For each [controlled menu](Yin_C_G0345_v797.md#menus) one has
\[
 \sum_{j\in A} j r_j^2\log(eN/(jr_jd))\ll N/d.
\]
Indeed the menu estimates there bound the logarithmic cost with \(c_0d\) substituted for \(\rho\), and its absolute numerator constant can be increased to dominate the logarithm displayed here.
Equivalently the consecutive menu contributes \(O((rJ)^2(1+\log(1/m)))\), the prime menu contributes \(O((c/\sqrt m)^2(1+\log(1/m))/\log(c/\sqrt m))\), and the dyadic menu contributes \(O_c(1/m)\) by summing \(2^{-i}(1+i)\) from its largest step downward.
Each is \(O(N/d)\) and the overlapping menus cost at most the sum.
Since \(jdr_j\le N\), the same bound holds for \(\sum j r_j^2\).
Adding \(\log d\) inside each weighted logarithm shows that the loss is at most \(C'(KT)^2N\log(ed)/d\).
Choose \(b\le1\) to make this at most \(\delta N\log(2)/4\) and apply the initial family bound \(\log |\mathcal F|\ge\delta N\log(2)/2\).
For the stronger size assertion, decrease the menu cutoff constant so that the two costs in the original progression estimate together are at most \(\delta N\log(2)/4\), as allowed by the same menu estimates.
This starts with at least \(3\delta N\log(2)/4\) in log cardinality before paying the moment cost.
