# Local periods bound the dilations of terminal exponent lists

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed list of exponents and coefficients

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(B\ge1\) an integer.
Fix nonzero coefficients \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\in K\) and integers \(w_1\le\cdots\le w_s\), not all zero.
Count the positive integers \(q\) for which \(c_q=\sum_i a_i\alpha^{qw_i}\) has absolute degree at most \(B\) and the summands have no nonempty vanishing subsum, including the entire sum.
Choose a prime \(p\), a positive integer \(J\), and \(R\) distinct embeddings \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) satisfying
\[
       v_p(\sigma(\alpha)^J-1)\ge 2/(p-1),\qquad v_p(p)=1.
\]
Let \(v\) be the greatest number of these embeddings in one multiplicative dependence class of images of \(\alpha\).
If \(R>vB\), the count is at most
\[
                 \frac{4sJB}{1-vB/R}.
\]
For \(R\ge2vB\) the bound is \(8sJB\).
For \(B=1\) any two independent images give the bound \(4sJ\).
The coefficients may have arbitrary heights and degrees in \(K\).

<a id="zeros"></a>
## Zeros on one residue class

Work in a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) with the valuation above and write \(\delta=2/(p-1)\).
For distinct \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_t\) of valuation at least \(\delta\), allowing zero, and nonzero constants \(b_i\), the function \(F(z)=\sum_i b_i\exp(\lambda_i z)\) has at most \(2(t-1)\) distinct zeros on the closed unit disk.
This specialization also follows from [van der Poorten and Schlickewei, Lemma 2](../audits/literature/PS91/Yin_U001.md#ps91-u001) by letting the positive parameter \(\epsilon\) increase to \(1/(p-1)\) from below.

For a direct proof put \(U_n=\sum_i b_i\lambda_i^n\), interpreting the zeroth powers as one.
The Vandermonde determinant gives \(c=\min_{0\le j<t}v_p(U_j)<\infty\).
The characteristic polynomial \(\prod_i(X-\lambda_i)\) gives a recurrence for \(U_n\) whose coefficient multiplying \(U_{n-k}\) has valuation at least \(k\delta\).
Induction therefore gives \(v_p(U_n)\ge c+(n-t+1)\delta\) for every \(n\ge0\), the initial inequalities following from the definition of \(c\).
Since \(v_p(n!)\le n/(p-1)\), the coefficients \(D_n=U_n/n!\) of \(F\) satisfy
\[
            v_p(D_n)\ge c+(n-2t+2)/(p-1).
\]
Some \(D_j\) for \(j<t\) has valuation at most \(c\), so the last index \(L\) of maximal absolute value is at most \(2t-2\).
If \(L=0\) the constant dominates throughout the disk.
Division at any disk zero \(a\) gives quotient coefficients \(\sum_{n>j}D_n a^{n-1-j}\), whose last index of maximal absolute value is \(L-1\) because at \(j=L-1\) the leading summand strictly dominates its tail.
Iterating proves the asserted zero count.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Equal images of the output

If any \(q\) is counted, combining equal exponents \(w\) gives a nonzero coefficient \(a(w)\) at every distinct exponent.
Choose independent images \(x=\sigma(\alpha)\) and \(y=\tau(\alpha)\) and work in a finite local extension containing both embedded fields, hence also the coefficients from each embedding.
For each \(0\le h<J\), write \(q=h+Jz\).
The equality \(\sigma(c_q)=\tau(c_q)\) is the vanishing at this integer \(z\) of
\[
 \sum_w \sigma(a(w))x^{hw}\exp\bigl(zw\log(x^J)\bigr)
 -\sum_w \tau(a(w))y^{hw}\exp\bigl(zw\log(y^J)\bigr).
\]
The logarithm preserves valuation of the increment and is inverted by the exponential at depth strictly greater than \(1/(p-1)\), as follows from their convergent power series there.
All frequencies thus have valuation at least \(\delta\), even for negative \(w\).
On each side the frequencies are distinct since the base is nontorsion.
An equality across sides would give \(x^{Jw}=y^{Jw'}\), forcing \(w=w'=0\) by independence.
Combining the constant terms and discarding a zero constant leaves at most \(2s\) terms, with at least one nonzero frequency and a nonzero coefficient there.
The disk estimate bounds the number of integers \(q\) giving equality for this pair by \(4sJ\).

For a counted \(q\), partition the embeddings by their image of \(c_q\).
The sum of squares of the fiber sizes is at least \(R^2/B\), since there are at most \(B\) fibers.
At most \(vR\) ordered pairs belong to the same dependence class, counting diagonals.
Each \(q\) therefore uses at least \(R^2/B-vR\) independent ordered pairs whose images agree.
Summing the pair bound over at most \(R^2\) pairs proves the claim, first for any finite set of counted integers and hence for their full set.
When \(B=1\) both images of every counted output agree for a single fixed independent pair.

<a id="periods"></a>
## Periods from reduction of the minimal polynomial

Put \(d_*=\min_{k\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\).
The [conjugate class bound](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings) gives \(v\le2d/d_*\) also for local embeddings, since all comparisons of algebraic conjugates take place in an embedded global splitting field.
At a strict degree record for the least positive logarithmic Mahler measure of a unit, [power descent](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent) gives \(d_*=d\), hence \(v\le2\).

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
For an odd prime \(p\) and \(f\ge1\), count the roots of the multiplicity-one irreducible factors of \(P\bmod p\) whose degrees divide \(f\), giving each such factor its degree in the count \(R\).
They are distinct nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_{p^f}\) and Hensel's lemma lifts them to distinct roots of \(P\) in the unramified degree-\(f\) extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\).
Each lifted root \(x\) satisfies \(v_p(x^{p^f-1}-1)\ge1\ge2/(p-1)\).
Thus at a strict record, \(R\ge4B\) gives a dilation bound of \(8sB(p^f-1)\), in particular \(8sB(p-1)\) for at least \(4B\) simple linear roots.

At \(p=2\) one period works for all \(d\) embeddings.
Let \(J_0\) be the least common multiple of \(2^k-1\) over the distinct degrees \(k\) of the irreducible factors of \(P\bmod 2\).
Their sum is at most \(d\), so \(J_0\le2^d\).
For each root \(x\) the reduction of \(x\) has order dividing \(J_0\), since it satisfies one such factor and is nonzero.
In \(\mathbb Q_2(x)\) the ramification index is at most \(d\), hence \(v_2(x^{J_0}-1)\ge1/d\).
Squaring increases a positive depth \(b\) to at least \(\min(2b,b+1)\) by expansion about one.
After \(\lceil\log_2 d\rceil\) squarings the depth is at least one and after one further squaring it is at least two.
Consequently \(J=2^{1+\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}J_0\le4d\,2^d\) suffices simultaneously.

<a id="shapes"></a>
## Primitive lists forced on a shrinking sequence

On a sequence with \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\), choose integers \(1\le B\le d_*/4\) with \(Bm\to0\).
By the [terminal count](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement), on each sufficiently late input there is an arity \(r\to\infty\) and one fixed ordered list of \(s=r-1\) outgoing coefficients for which at least \((c_0/m)^{r-2}\) sorted marked exponent lists occur, for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Divide each nonzero list by the positive gcd of its entries and let \(V_r\) count the resulting distinct primitive lists for these coefficients, counting an all-zero list separately if present.
Terminality ensures that every outgoing list has no nonempty vanishing subsum.
For every choice of local data with \(R\ge2vB\) one obtains
\[
       V_r\ \ge\ \frac{(c_0/m)^{r-2}}{8(r-1)JB}.
\]
Indeed each primitive list contributes at most the displayed dilation bound and the all-zero list contributes at most one.
The dyadic choice always meets the incidence threshold since \(R=d\) and \(2vB\le4dB/d_*\le d\).
If \(\log(JB)=o(r\log(1/m))\), this forces \(\log V_r\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m)\).
In particular a period with \(JB\le(1/m)^{O(1)}\) suffices, as does the universal dyadic period when \(d=o(r\log(1/m))\).

Conversely, suppose along such a sequence of strict records that for the indicated witnessing arities and coefficients one has \(V_r\le A^r\) for a fixed \(A\ge1\).
Every qualifying choice with \(R\ge2vB\) must then have
\[
            JB\ \ge\ \frac{(c_0/m)^{r-2}}{8(r-1)A^r}.
\]
In particular every odd prime and residue degree parameter producing at least \(4B\) simple roots as above obeys this inequality with \(J=p^f-1\).
The estimate requires neither real embeddings nor conjugates on the unit circle.
Whether qualifying odd primes with small enough \(p^f\) must exist on the relevant sequences remains open.
