# The signed Galois group of a single block with one nonreal exterior pair

<a id="statement"></a>
## Endpoint tests and the degree of the squared radius

Let \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial with a pair of nonreal complex-conjugate exterior roots \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\).
Assume all other exterior roots are real, and write \(s\) for their number and \(q=m-2-s\) for the number of neutral inverse pairs.
The trace polynomial \(S\) has exactly one nonreal conjugate pair and discriminant \(\Delta<0\).
Use either of the following hypotheses.

* If \(s=0\), assume \(m\ge3\) and the [signed orbit graph](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#statement) of the exterior pair is connected and unbalanced.
* If \(s,q\ge1\), assume \(m\ge5\) and the Galois orbit of the edge joining the two nonreal exterior labels is connected on the \(m\) inverse-pair labels.

Set \(E=S(2)S(-2)\ne0\).
Write a signed permutation of the labels as a permutation \(\pi\) and choices of inversion, and denote by \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) the product of these signs.
The full Galois group on the \(2m\) actual roots is given by the following exhaustive alternatives.

| Arithmetic condition | Group of signed permutations |
| --- | --- |
| \(E\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}\) | All with \(\epsilon=1\). |
| \(E/\Delta\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}\) | All with \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\). |
| Neither condition | All signed permutations. |

The sign of \(E\) is \((-1)^q\), so only the first square test is needed for even \(q\), and only the second for odd \(q\).
Representatives of all \(m\) inverse pairs are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, and the rank of the exterior log moduli over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(s+1\).
The squared radius \(\rho=\lambda\bar\lambda\) has degree \(4\binom m2\), with precisely \(2+4\binom s2\) real conjugates.
Moreover \([\mathbb Q(\lambda,\bar\lambda):\mathbb Q]=4m(m-1)\), and the real elements of this field form exactly \(\mathbb Q(\rho)\).
In the case \(s=0\), one has \(\rho=M(P)\) and \(M(\rho)=M(P)^{2m-3}\).

<a id="flips"></a>
## Signed transpositions and even sign changes

Choose representatives \(v_i\) from the inverse pairs, and use the formal signed basis \(\pm e_i\) to label \(v_i^{\pm1}\).
The splitting-field group \(G\) acts faithfully by signed permutations and ordinary conjugation \(c\) induces a single transposition on inverse labels.
On the remaining labels it fixes the representatives of real pairs and inverts those of neutral pairs.
The transpositions in its projected orbit generate the full symmetric group \(S_m\), since transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate it, as in the [block action](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action).
Let \(J\) denote simultaneous reversal of all labels and \(H=\langle G,J\rangle\) as a permutation group.
It is enough to put every double sign change in \(H'\), since centrality of \(J\) gives \(H'=G'\subset G\).

First take \(s=0\).
For each \(g\) write \(g(\lambda)=v_i^{u}\), \(g(\bar\lambda)=v_j^{v}\), \(u,v\in\{\pm1\}\).
The permutation \(Jgcg^{-1}\) fixes all other signed labels and interchanges \(e_i,e_j\) with multiplier \(-uv\) in both directions.
This is precisely the edge sign in the signed orbit graph.
Orient the representatives along a spanning tree so that all its edge signs become positive.
Its transpositions generate the ordinary permutation subgroup on this oriented basis.
Unbalance gives a remaining negative edge, allowing both signed transpositions on its two labels.
Their product flips just those signs, and conjugating puts every double flip in \(H\).
Commuting the flip on \(i,j\) with a permutation sending \(j\) to \(k\ne i,j\) and fixing \(i\) gives the double flip on \(j,k\).
This puts every double flip in \(H'\).

For \(s,q\ge1\), take a lift \(g\in G\) of a transposition exchanging one real and one neutral label and fixing all others.
The two involutions \(c\) and \(gcg^{-1}\) induce the same transposition on the two moving labels.
On fixed labels their sign patterns differ just at the selected real and neutral labels, since a change of orientation has no effect on a sign there.
On the moving pair each involution has the same multiplier at both endpoints, since its square is the identity.
Their product is a sign change supported on two or four labels, nonempty and proper for \(m\ge5\).
Whenever the sign kernel contains a vector with nonempty proper support, conjugating by a lift of a transposition exchanging one index inside and one outside that support, and multiplying by the original vector, gives a double flip.
The full permutation action then gives every double flip in \(G\).

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## The trace splitting field and the two parity characters

Let \(t_i=v_i+v_i^{-1}\), \(d_i=t_i^2-4\), and \(K=\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\).
The field over \(K\) obtained by adjoining all \(\sqrt{d_i}=v_i-v_i^{-1}\) is the splitting field of \(P\).
Such a choice of sign gives
\[
                w=\prod_i(v_i-v_i^{-1}),\qquad w^2=\prod_i d_i=E .
\]
Each \(g\in G\) acts on \(w\) by \(\epsilon\), independently of the initial orientations.
Since the sign kernel contains all even vectors, its only possibilities are the even sign changes and the full sign group.
The former occurs exactly when \(w\in K\).
The group of \(K/\mathbb Q\) is \(S_m\), whose unique index-two subgroup is the alternating group since transpositions are conjugate and generate \(S_m\).
Thus its only quadratic subfield is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\Delta})\), where the Vandermonde product transforms by \(\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\).
This gives the two parity characters in the table when \(w\in K\), and the full signed action otherwise, by counting the kernel and the surjective permutation quotient.
For the sign of \(E\), each of the \(q\) neutral labels contributes a negative \(d_i\), each real exterior label a positive one, and the two complex traces give a positive product.

There is also a sufficient prime test requiring only that the trace splitting group is \(S_m\), without a prior assumption on the signs.
If \(v_\ell(E)\) is odd for a rational prime \(\ell\nmid\Delta\), then the group is the full signed group.
Indeed at any prime of \(K\) over \(\ell\) the \(t_i\) are pairwise distinct in the residue field, and \(\ell\) is unramified in \(K\) since \(S\) splits over an unramified local extension by lifting its distinct residue roots.
At most two of the \(d_i\) have positive valuation, because their traces reduce to \(2\) or \(-2\).
The valuations sum to the odd integer \(v_\ell(E)\), hence precisely one is odd.
Conjugating this prime isolates each label in turn.
No nonempty subproduct of the \(d_i\) can then be square in \(K\).
Adjoining their square roots consequently has degree \(2^m\).
Indeed, by induction for independent square classes, the products of the first \(j\) square roots give a basis of distinct characters for the \(j\) independent sign changes.
An element of that field with square in \(K^\times\) lies in a single character line, so it cannot supply the square root of the next independent class.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Multiplicative independence and the conjugates of the radius

In \(\mathbb Q^m\) the relation space of the \(v_i\) modulo torsion is invariant under the even sign group.
The \(m\) coordinate characters on that group are distinct for \(m\ge3\), since a flip at \(i\) and a third label separates coordinates \(i,j\).
Character averaging therefore projects the relation space onto its separate coordinate lines.
It contains no nonzero coordinate relation, since a torsion conjugate would make all roots torsion.
Thus it vanishes.
A rational relation between the exterior logs, after clearing denominators and doubling, would give a multiplicative relation among the real exterior roots squared and \(\lambda\bar\lambda\).
Their disjoint nonempty supports give the asserted rank \(s+1\).

All \(4\binom m2\) products \(v_i^{\pm1}v_j^{\pm1}\), \(i<j\), are distinct by independence and form one orbit.
Indeed permutations carry any support to any other, and one can adjust either sign using a double flip with a third index.
The same action on ordered pairs has \(4m(m-1)\) images.
These are the two field degrees in the statement.
Ordinary conjugation exchanges the entries of \((\lambda,\bar\lambda)\), so its fixed field in \(\mathbb Q(\lambda,\bar\lambda)\) is the real subfield and has index two.
It contains \(\mathbb Q(\rho)\), which already has this index.
In counting real images of \(\rho\), the signs on the moving pair must agree when its representatives are chosen as \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\).
This gives \(\rho,\rho^{-1}\).
Among the fixed labels only two real labels give products fixed by conjugation, contributing four per unordered pair of real labels.
In the pure case the moving pair gives the single exterior product \(\rho\), and the products meeting it on just one label contribute \(4(m-2)\) exterior values of modulus \(\sqrt\rho\).
This gives \(M(\rho)=\rho^{2m-3}\).

<a id="fields"></a>
## The field of one root and the smallest norm-one generator

For any individual root \(x\), the only proper nontrivial subfield of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) is \(k=\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\).
Indeed the stabilizer of \(x\) fixes \(x^{-1}\) and is transitive on the \(2(m-1)\) other roots.
It lifts every permutation fixing the label of \(x\) by correcting the sign at \(x\) with a double flip if necessary, and it can adjust the sign on any other label by flipping it and a third label.
Any block containing \(x\) and a root outside its inverse pair must therefore have size at least \(2m-1\), hence equals the whole orbit since the block size divides \(2m\).
The subgroup–block correspondence gives the assertion, and the degree of \(k\) is \(m\).
Neither field is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\), since a point stabilizer moves other conjugates in each transitive action.
Thus the only roots of unity in \(\mathbb Q(x)\) are \(\pm1\), as a cyclotomic subfield is Galois.

In the pure case take \(x=\lambda\).
The signatures of \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) and \(k\) are \((0,m)\) and \((m-2,1)\).
Every norm-one integral unit \(b\) for this quadratic extension has modulus one above each real place of \(k\), where the field involution is ordinary conjugation.
Above its complex place the absolute values are \(|b|\) and \(|b|^{-1}\) and their complex conjugate values, using the chosen embedding.
Consequently a bounded interval for \(\log|b|\) gives only finitely many such units, by the uniform bounds on their degrees and all conjugate moduli and hence on their integer minimal-polynomial coefficients.
This logarithm maps the norm-one group onto a discrete subgroup of \(\mathbb R\) containing \(\log|\lambda|>0\), with finite kernel \(\{\pm1\}\).
If \(\beta\) lifts the positive generator, then \(\lambda=\pm\beta^n\) with \(n\ge1\), and \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).
Its nonreal exterior pair has measure \(M(\beta)=M(P)^{1/n}\).
It is reciprocal by the norm-one identity, and distinct conjugates of \(\beta\) have nontorsion quotient because their signed \(n\)-th powers are distinct conjugates of \(\lambda\), separated modulo torsion by the proved independence and nontorsion.
Thus every positive power retains the degree, and a record minimizing the measure among degrees at most \(2m\) must have \(n=1\).

## Full signed action in Boyd's degree-eighteen example

[Neumann and Reid, §4.5, p. 285](../audits/literature/NR92/Yin_U001.md#nr92-u001) give Boyd's polynomial \(P=z^{18}+z^{17}+z^{16}-z^{13}-z^{11}-z^9-z^7-z^5+z^2+z+1\) and report its measure as approximately \(1.2013961862\).
Its trace polynomial and elementary exact certificates are as follows.
\[
        S=x^9+x^8-8x^7-8x^6+20x^5+19x^4-16x^3-13x^2+2x+1 .
\]
The signs of \(S\) at \(-2,-1,-1/2,0,1/2,5/4,7/4,2\) alternate, starting negative.
Its discriminant by the resultant with its derivative is \(-5537520119\), so seven real roots in \((-2,2)\) leave exactly one nonreal pair.
Modulo two \(f=S\bmod 2=x^9+x^8+x^4+x^2+1\) is irreducible, since \(x^{2^9}-x\equiv0\bmod f\) and \(\gcd(f,x^{2^3}-x)=\gcd(f,x^8+x)=1\).
Modulo 29 one has \(S=(x+12)g\), where
\[
                g=x^8-11x^7+8x^6+12x^5-8x^4-x^3-4x^2+6x-12 .
\]
Here \(x^{29^8}-x\equiv0\bmod g\) and \(\gcd(g,x^{29^4}-x)=1\), by repeated powering and Euclidean division, so \(g\) is irreducible.
Thus the trace group is transitive and contains an eight-cycle fixing one label, as the Frobenius on the distinct residue roots lifts to the unramified local splitting field.
The group is doubly transitive, and conjugates of the transposition from complex conjugation give all transpositions, so it is \(S_9\).
One has \(S(2)=1,\ S(-2)=-7\) and \(7\nmid\Delta\).
The prime test proves that the splitting group of \(P\) is the full signed group on nine labels.
In particular \(P\) is irreducible, its inverse labels are independent, and its signed graph is connected and unbalanced, as both signs occur on every edge.
