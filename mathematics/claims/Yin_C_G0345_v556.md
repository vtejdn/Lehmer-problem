# A single rational factor carries the entire index of the power suborder

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two marked factors at primitive offsets

Use the [two sections at offsets two apart](Yin_C_G0345_v553.md#statement) with \(s\equiv2\pmod3,\ a=3^k,\ k\ge1,\ as+b\) odd and \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,b+2)=1\).
For either \(j=b,b+2\), put \(G=-F_j\), let \(P\) be the unique marked factor from that result, and put \(Q=G/P\), retaining all multiplicities and cyclotomic factors in \(Q\).
Then \(P\) is noncyclotomic for every such \(a,s,j\).
No comparison of \(a\) and \(s\) is needed.
For any monic \(S\mid G\) write \(O_S=\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\), \(x_S=X\bmod S\), and \(D_S=O_S/\mathbb Z[x_S^a]\) as an additive group.
Reduction gives
\[
 D_G\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }D_P\simeq
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^{a-1}(\mathbb Z/2^i\mathbb Z)^2,\qquad D_Q=0.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular every monic divisor of \(Q\) has equal orders before and after taking the \(a\)-th power; (1) also accounts for possible intersections between its different factors.
For a root \(x\) of \(P\), the fields \(\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(x^a)\) coincide and
\[
  |\operatorname{disc}\mathbb Z[x^a]|
    =2^{2a(a-1)}|\operatorname{disc}\mathbb Z[x]|.
\]
These assertions apply, in particular, at both offsets \(as^3\pm1\) for every \(s\equiv2\pmod3,\ s\ge5\) and every \(a=3^k,\ k\ge1\).
They locate the index in a specified noncyclotomic factor without determining its degree share.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Why the marked factor cannot be cyclotomic

Write \(C(T)=T^2+T+1\).
Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the polynomial \(C(X^a)=\Phi_{3a}(X)\) is irreducible of degree \(2a\), and it divides \(\bar P\) but not \(\bar Q\) by the [dyadic intersection](Yin_C_G0345_v553.md#statement); the removed \(X+1\) has no such divisor.
If \(P=\Phi_n\), the odd part of \(n\) must be \(3a\).
Indeed for \(n=2^h m\) with \(m\) odd, at \(h=0\) the roots of \(\bar\Phi_m\) have exact order \(m\) by separability of \(X^m-1\).
For \(h\ge1\), the identity \(\Phi_{2^h m}(X)=\Phi_m(X^{2^h})/\Phi_m(X^{2^{h-1}})\) gives \(\bar\Phi_{2^h m}=\bar\Phi_m^{2^{h-1}}\).

At a section root \(x\), the section equation expresses \(x^j\) as \(-g(x^a)/B(x^a)\).
Here \(g(T)=T^s-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\) have no common complex zero by the [seed identity](Yin_C_G0345_v549.md#change) and \(g(T)=2T^2\bmod C(T)\) in this residue class.
Bézout's identity for \(a,j\) therefore gives \(\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(x^a)\).
For a primitive \(2^h\,3a\)-th root in characteristic zero, the degree over the field of its \(a\)-th power, a primitive \(2^h 3\)-rd root, is exactly \(a>1\) by the totient formula.
This excludes every cyclotomic possibility.

<a id="index"></a>
## Separating the defect from the complementary order

All \(D_S\) are \(\mathbb Z[T]\)-modules with \(T\) acting by multiplication by \(x_S^a\), and reduction from \(D_G\) to \(D_S\) is surjective.
The [elementary-divisor presentation](Yin_C_G0345_v549.md#invariants) gives (1) for \(D_G\) and shows that both \(2^{a-1}\) and \(C(T)^{a-1}\) annihilate it.
Put \(H(T)=\operatorname{Res}_X(Q(X),T-X^a)\), the monic polynomial whose roots, with multiplicity, are the \(a\)-th powers of the roots of \(Q\).
The polynomial \(H(X^a)\) is divisible by \(Q(X)\) over \(\mathbb Z\), including multiplicities, by taking the product of \(X^a-z^a\) over all roots \(z\) of \(Q\).
In characteristic two a common root of \(\bar H(T)\) and \(C(T)\) would supply a root of \(\bar Q\) at which \(C(X^a)=0\).
Irreducibility of \(C(X^a)\) and \(\Phi_{3a}\nmid\bar Q\) exclude this.
Hence there exist integer polynomials \(u,v,w\) such that
\[
            uH+v C^{a-1}=1+2w.
\]
On \(D_G\), the right side is invertible by the finite geometric series for the nilpotent operator \(2w\), and \(C^{a-1}\) vanishes.
Thus \(H(T)\) acts invertibly on \(D_G\).
It annihilates \(D_Q\) since \(Q(X)\mid H(X^a)\), proving \(D_Q=0\).

The kernel of \(D_G\to D_P\) is the image in \(D_G\) of \(P(x_G)O_G\): a representative whose reduction lies in \(\mathbb Z[x_P^a]\) can be adjusted by the very same polynomial in \(x_G^a\).
Since \(G=PQ\), \(H(T)\) annihilates that ideal and therefore this kernel.
Invertibility on \(D_G\) proves injectivity.
The usual index-squared change of basis for the trace pairing now gives the asserted discriminant equality.
