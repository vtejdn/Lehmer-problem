# Filling the last degrees of an exact period using two sets of Frobenius orbits

<a id="periods"></a>
## Exact degree at the odd modulus

Fix the parameters of the [strengthened zero-boundary rows](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#statement), including the odd auxiliary prime \(t>p\), \(p=3^\nu,\ q=p-1,\ u=2^h,\ L=(p+3)/2\), and \(\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)>2\).
Write \(D_0\bmod P_0\) for the [endpoint degree class](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#degrees) giving zero boundary coefficient.
There are unbounded odd periods \(N=A+2u\) and even degrees \(d\) with the following properties.
One has \(d=2tS\), \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P_0\), \(|A-\theta d|=O(1)\), \(15t\mid A\), \(v_3(A)=1\), and \(\min(v_7(A),v_{11}(A))\to\infty\).
Over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\mathbb Z\) there is a monic reciprocal degree-\(d\) word \(W\) of constant term one and exact order \(N\), with zero coefficients at offsets \(1,\ldots,L\).
Here the order is the least positive \(j\) with \(W\mid X^j-1\).
Every residue root has degree \(\gg\sqrt{\log d}\) over \(\mathbb F_3\).
Outside \(O(\log d)\) degrees, its multiplicative order is at least \(d^{1-o(1)}\) and its residue degree is \(\gg\log d\).

The [orbit criterion](#criterion) also applies directly to a prescribed original row once its period and orbit counts meet the stated hypotheses.
The explicit periods below impose several original congruences and the bounded archimedean period error.
Constructing the even-side coefficient word with order \(A\) on these same degrees, or proving the criterion applies to unbounded original rows with their prescribed \(A\), remains open.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## A sufficient orbit count for an original row

Suppose \(H>L\) is odd and divides both \(N\) and \(d\), put \(K=N/H>1\), \(M=d/(2H)\), and assume \(N\) is odd and prime to three.
The nonidentity orbits in \(\mu_K(\overline{\mathbb F}_3)\) under cubing and inversion have even size, since inversion has no fixed point on them.
Write their half-sizes as positive integers, bounded above by \(B\).
Set aside one primitive orbit.
Let \(x,y\ge2\) be coprime integers.
Outside the primitive orbit assume there are at least \(y-1\) distinct orbits of half-size \(x\) and at least \(x+\lceil B/y\rceil\) distinct orbits of half-size \(y\).
Set \(R=x(y-1)+y(x+\lceil B/y\rceil)\).
Then a full-degree word of exact order \(N\) with zero prefix through \(L\) exists whenever
\[
             xy+B\le M\le (K-1)/2-R+xy .
\]

Indeed, reserve the indicated numbers of filling orbits of half-sizes \(x,y\).
Start with the separate primitive orbit and add others outside the reserve until the next would pass \(M-xy\), or the budget is attained.
The lower bound lets the first orbit fit, and the available half-size \((K-1)/2-R\) suffices for this stopping rule.
The remainder \(r\) satisfies \(xy\le r<xy+B\).
Choose \(0\le i<y\) with \(xi\equiv r\bmod y\).
Then \(j=(r-xi)/y\) satisfies \(0\le j\le x+\lceil B/y\rceil\), so \(i,j\) distinct orbits from their respective reserves fill the degree exactly.

The selected inverse-stable root set gives a reciprocal factor on \(\mu_K\) with constant one, and the primitive root forces order \(K\).
Its unique monic factor lift \(V\) divides \(X^K-1\) modulo \(3^\nu\) by the [prime-to-three lift](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#boundary).
The word \(V(X^H)\) has degree \(d\) and zero prefix through \(L\).
It has exact order \(HK\) even if \(H,K\) share primes, since \(1,X,\ldots,X^{H-1}\) form a free basis over the quotient in \(X^H\).
A power of \(X\) equal to one thus has exponent divisible by \(H\), and the resulting power in \(X^H\) has exponent divisible by \(K\).
On an original row the [pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) retains the even-side word.
The test requires a sufficient supply of two disjoint sets of distinct roots, so it introduces no repeated-root factor in the last degrees.

<a id="subfields"></a>
## Divisors from two proper subfields

For odd coprime \(b>a>1\) with \(b/a\to2\), let \(s=ab\), \(K=(3^s-1)/2\), and for the moment take any fixed odd \(H>L\) prime to three.
The number \(K\) is odd and the cubing order on all its roots divides the odd number \(s\).
No nonidentity root is conjugate to its inverse under cubing, since a nontrivial involution cannot belong to a cyclic group of odd order.
Every half-size is therefore the residue degree and is at most \(s\).
For \(v=a,b\) the subgroup of order \((3^v-1)/2\) lies in \(\mu_K\).
At most \(v3^{v/3}\) of its elements lie in proper subfields of \(\mathbb F_{3^v}\), since a proper divisor of the odd \(v\) is at most \(v/3\).
It supplies at least \(((3^v-1)/2-v3^{v/3})/(2v)\) orbits of half-size \(v\).
These two supplies have different residue degrees and exclude a primitive \(K\)-th root, whose degree is \(s\) since \(3^{s-1}-1<K\).
Taking \(x=a,\ y=b,\ B=s\) in the criterion gives every \(M\) between \(2s\) and \((K-1)/2-O(s)\) for all sufficiently large \(a\).

If \(M\sim K/(2\theta)\), the orbits chosen before the final filling can all have half-size \(s\) and root order at least \(K/(8\tau(K))\), where \(\tau\) counts divisors.
Indeed the roots of smaller order number at most \(K/8\), by summing the orders of the eligible divisors of \(K\).
At most \(s3^{s/3}=o(K)\) roots have degree less than \(s\).
The remaining half-size exceeds \(M\) and includes the primitive orbit.
Stop at the same budget \(M-s\), leaving less than \(2s\) half-size to fill.
Under the substitution \(X^H\), a preimage of a root has at least its order and at least its residue degree.
Thus at most \(4Hs=O(\log d)\) degrees come from the filling orbits, all of residue degree at least \(a\asymp\sqrt{\log d}\).
On the others the order is at least \(d^{1-o(1)}\), by the [elementary divisor estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v205.md#large-roots), and the residue degree is at least \(s\).

<a id="congruences"></a>
## Simultaneous period and degree congruences

Let \(T=\operatorname{lcm}(2,6,5,\operatorname{ord}_{5t}(3))\) and let \(r_\ell=v_\ell(3^T-1)\) for \(\ell=7,11\).
Both valuations are positive by the orders six and five at these primes.
Choose a fixed positive odd \(H>L\), prime to \(tP_0\), with
\[
 H\equiv2u\bmod 5t,\qquad 4H-2u\equiv3\bmod9,\qquad
 H\equiv2u\bmod 7^{r_7}11^{r_{11}} .
\]
These conditions specify units at all indicated odd primes, including three.
They are compatible with avoiding the other primes of \(P_0\) by the Chinese remainder theorem.

For each integer \(g>\max(r_7,r_{11})\), choose \(s\equiv1\bmod T\) with \(A=H(3^s-1)/2-2u\) divisible by \(7^g11^g\).
Indeed, writing \(s=1+Tz\), at \(\ell=7,11\) the requirement is
\[
           (3^T)^z\equiv (H+4u)/(3H)\pmod{\ell^g}.
\]
The right side is one modulo \(\ell^{r_\ell}\).
The powers of \(3^T\) give this entire subgroup of units modulo \(\ell^g\), since raising a unit \(1+\ell^r w\) with \(\ell\nmid w,\ r\ge1\) to the odd prime power \(\ell\) increases that valuation by exactly one by the binomial expansion.
Thus \(z\) satisfies one class modulo each of the coprime powers \(7^{g-r_7},11^{g-r_{11}}\).
This gives a class \(s_0\bmod J\) with \(J=T7^{g-r_7}11^{g-r_{11}}\).

Take a positive odd \(c\equiv2-s_0\bmod J\) and choose \(a\equiv1\bmod Jc\) arbitrarily large compared with \(c\).
Put \(b=2a-c\).
Then \(a,b\) are odd and coprime, \(s=ab\equiv s_0\bmod J\), and taking \(a>gc\) gives \(b/a\to2\) along growing stages.
Since \(s\equiv1\bmod T\), one has \(K\equiv1\bmod5t\).
Also \(K\equiv4\bmod9\) for \(s\ge2\), giving \(v_3(A)=1\).
The claimed divisibilities of \(A\) follow.

Finally select \(D\equiv D_0\bmod P_0\), divisible by \(Ht\), nearest to \(A/(2\theta)\) among such integers.
The class exists since \(Ht\) is prime to \(P_0\), and the rounding error is bounded by a constant independent of the stage.
With \(d=2D\), \(N=HK\) one has \(M=d/(2H)\sim K/(2\theta)\), so the subfield construction gives the asserted full-degree word.
This choice of \(A\) does not enforce the original [exact period factorization and binary blocks](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical).
