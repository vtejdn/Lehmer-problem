# Measures of every radical of a unit on a fixed rational graph

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform comparison across dilations when the root field has bounded torsion

Fix a nonzero rational function \(R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(Z)\) and an integer \(W\ge1\).
There are a slope threshold \(s_{R,W}\) and a positive integer \(H_{R,W}\) with the following property.
Let \(\beta\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit satisfying \(\beta^v=R(\beta^u)\) with positive integers \(u,v\) and \(v/u>s_{R,W}\).
For any integer \(g\ge1\) and **any** choice \(\alpha^g=\beta\), put \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(t=[F(\alpha):F]\) and \(d_0=\gcd(g,t)\).
If the order of the group of \(d_0\)-th roots of unity in \(F\) is at most \(W\), then
\[
                g/d_0\mid H_{R,W},\qquad
                \frac{m(\alpha)}{m(\beta)}=\frac{t}{g}
                                          \ \ge\ \frac1{H_{R,W}},       \tag{1}
\]
where \(m\) is total logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular the result holds uniformly in every dilation and every branch if \(|\mu(F)|\le W\).
When \(F\) admits a real embedding one may always take \(W=2\), including for negative real \(\beta\) or a nonreal chosen embedding or radical.
Equivalently every irreducible factor of \(P_\beta(X^g)\), with \(P_\beta\) the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), has logarithmic measure at least \(m(\beta)/H_{R,W}\) in the bounded-torsion family.
The lower bound is relative to the original solution's measure.

For the comparison (1) itself the [earlier bounded-length theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) already works with arbitrary torsion in \(F\) and every nonzero graph section.
Indeed the integral coefficient-conjugate product constructed [for the graph](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#radicals) has coefficient length bounded solely in terms of \(R\); its only identically zero sections arise for \(R(Z)=Z^k\) and \(v=ku\).
At every other section the earlier denominator and all-branch measure bound therefore apply without a slope threshold.
The argument here instead identifies the twist from the relative norm and gives a direct characteristic-polynomial estimate in a bounded relative extension.
The [dyadic example](#counterexample) determines the limitation of assuming merely that untwisted radicals in the root field have bounded orders.
There are totally real fields \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and nontorsion units \(\beta\) for which no \(r\)-th root lies in \(F\) for any \(r>1\), yet a \(2^k\)-th root lies in an extension of \(F\) of degree exactly two for arbitrarily large \(k\).
Thus bounded orders of actual roots in their original field, even with \(\mu(F)=\{\pm1\}\), do not alone give a linear lower bound on the relative degree of every radical.
The [positive real unit formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) addresses positive roots in a specified real embedding by a different norm argument.

<a id="norm"></a>
## A power in the original field after a root-of-unity twist

For arbitrary \(F\), put \(b=\operatorname{N}_{F(\alpha)/F}\alpha\).
Then \(b^g=\beta^t\).
Writing \(r=g/d_0,\ j=t/d_0\) gives coprime positive \(r,j\) and \(b^r=\omega\beta^j\) where \(\omega^{d_0}=1\) in \(F\).
Choose integers \(A,B\) with \(Aj+Br=1\).
Then the element \(\theta=b^A\beta^B\) in \(F\) satisfies
\[
               \theta^r=\xi\beta,\qquad \xi=\omega^A,\qquad
               \xi^{d_0}=1.                                            \tag{2}
\]
This permits a nontrivial twist even when \(\beta\) itself has no roots in \(F\).
If \(\alpha,\beta\) are as in the statement then \(\theta\) is a unit, either from its displayed power or directly from the relative norm.

<a id="extension"></a>
## The prime-power test for roots in a bounded relative extension

Fix a rational map \(S\ne0\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and a positive integer \(E\).
For a nontorsion unit \(z\) on \(z^v=S(z^u)\) at sufficiently large \(v/u\), there is a single \(H_{S,E}'\) divisible by every \(r\) for which a root \(\theta^r=z\) satisfies \([\mathbb Q(\theta):\mathbb Q(z)]\le E\).
Indeed use the integer polynomial \(P_{u,v}\) of [the graph separation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#radicals) for \(S\).
It vanishes at \(z\), has coefficient length at most \(L_S\), and has degree at most \(e_S(v+D_Su)\), for constants depending only on \(S\).
Once the slope is sufficiently large, \(P_{u,v}(\gamma)\ne0\) for every prime \(p\) and **every** \(\gamma\) satisfying \(\gamma^p=z\), with no field restriction on \(\gamma\).
Also \((v+D_Su)h(z)\) is bounded solely in terms of \(S\).

For any \(p^a\mid r\) take \(\theta_0=\theta^{r/p^a}\), \(\gamma=\theta_0^{p^{a-1}}\), and \(e=[\mathbb Q(\theta_0):\mathbb Q(z)]\le E\).
Let \(M\) be the integer companion matrix of the minimal polynomial of \(\theta_0\), which is an algebraic unit.
The characteristic polynomial of \(M^{p^a}\) is \(S_z^e\), with \(S_z\) the monic minimal polynomial of \(z\), since each conjugate of \(z\) has precisely \(e\) preimages among the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\theta_0)\).
The characteristic polynomial of \(M^{p^{a-1}}\) vanishes at \(\gamma\).
The [coefficient congruence for matrix powers](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence) therefore gives \(S_z(\gamma)^e\in p^a\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\theta_0)}\).
Monic division gives \(S_z\mid P_{u,v}\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), so \(P_{u,v}(\gamma)^e\) satisfies the same divisibility and is nonzero.
Taking its norm, and using \(h(\gamma)=h(z)/p\) and \(|P_{u,v}(x)|\le L_S\max(1,|x|)^{\deg P_{u,v}}\) at all complex embeddings, gives
\[
                 p^a\le \left(L_S\exp(\deg(P_{u,v})\,h(z)/p)\right)^e.
\]
The right side is bounded in terms of \(S,E\) alone.
The least common multiple of all positive integers up to such a bound gives \(H_{S,E}'\), including all choices of \(p\) and all \(r\) in the assertion.
The multiplicity \(e\) in the characteristic polynomial permits genuine degree loss on taking powers.

<a id="twists"></a>
## Applying finitely many graph equations

For the unit \(\delta=\xi\beta\) in (2), write
\[
           S(Z)=\xi^v R(\xi^{-u}Z),\qquad \delta^v=S(\delta^u).
\]
The order of \(\xi\) is at most \(W\), so only finitely many maps occur as \(\xi,u,v\) vary.
Since \(F=\mathbb Q(\delta,\xi)\), one has \([F:\mathbb Q(\delta)]\le[\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q]\le W\).
The root \(\theta\in F\) from (2) thus generates an extension of \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\) of degree at most \(W\).
Apply the [bounded-extension estimate](#extension) to these finitely many maps with \(E=W\), using the maximum slope threshold and the least common multiple of their bounds.
It gives \(r=g/d_0\mid H_{R,W}\).
Since \(t/d_0\ge1\), one has \(t/g\ge1/H_{R,W}\).
Finally \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(h(\alpha)=h(\beta)/g\), giving the measure identity in (1).
Any irreducible factor of \(P_\beta(X^g)\) has a root whose \(g\)-th power is \(\beta\), by extending the embedding of its powered field taking the corresponding conjugate back to \(\beta\).
For a field admitting a real embedding the roots of unity inject into \(\{\pm1\}\), giving the asserted condition for all \(g\) and choices of root.

<a id="counterexample"></a>
## An explicit negative unit with no roots in its field and an arbitrarily short radical extension

For \(k\ge3\) put \(n=2^k\), \(\zeta_k=\exp(2\pi i/2^{k+1})\), \(x_k=\zeta_k+\zeta_k^{-1}\), \(F_k=\mathbb Q(x_k)\) and \(a_k=1+x_k\).
The real cyclotomic field \(F_k\) has degree \(2^{k-1}\), since \(\zeta_k\) has degree \(2^k\) and its quadratic over \(F_k\) has a nonreal root while \(F_k\) is real.
The conjugates of \(x_k\) are \(2\cos(2\pi j/2^{k+1})\) for odd \(j\), with \(j\) and \(-j\) identified.
They lie in \([-x_k,x_k]\), and \(x_k>0\) is attained at exactly the distinguished embedding.
Consequently \(a_k=1+x_k\) has strictly larger modulus there than at every other embedding, so every positive power of \(a_k\) generates \(F_k\).
Otherwise the distinguished embedding of its powered field would have more than one extension with the same powered value.

At the bottom \(x_2=\sqrt2\).
Since \(x_j^2=2+x_{j-1}\), \(F_j/F_{j-1}\) is quadratic, with the involution sending \(x_j\) to \(-x_j\).
Thus \(\operatorname{N}_{F_j/F_{j-1}}(a_j)=1-x_j^2=-a_{j-1}\).
Successive application gives \(\operatorname{N}_{F_k/F_2}(a_k)=-a_2=-(1+\sqrt2)\).
The absolute norm of \(a_k\) has modulus one, so this algebraic integer is a unit.
Put \(\beta=-a_k^n\); it is a nontorsion unit generating \(F_k\).
An even root in this real field is impossible because \(\beta<0\).
If an odd \(r>1\) admitted a root, taking its norm to \(F_2\) would give an \(r\)-th power equal to \((1+\sqrt2)^n\), since \([F_k:F_2]\) and \(n\) are even.
As \(\gcd(n,r)=1\), Bézout's identity would make \(1+\sqrt2\) itself an \(r\)-th power in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).
Its real \(r\)-th root would be a unit \(y\) strictly between \(1\) and \(1+\sqrt2\).
Write it as \(a+b\sqrt2\) with integers \(a,b\), using the integer ring \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\).
If its norm were one, then \(a=(y+1/y)/2\) lies strictly between \(1\) and \(\sqrt2\).
If its norm were minus one, then \(a=(y-1/y)/2\) lies strictly between zero and one.
Both alternatives contradict integrality, excluding all odd \(r>1\).

On the other hand \(\alpha=a_k\zeta_k\) satisfies \(\alpha^n=\beta\), and \(F_k(\alpha)=F_k(\zeta_k)\) has degree two over \(F_k\).
In particular \(m(\alpha)/m(\beta)=2/n\to0\) even though only the first radical occurs inside \(F_k\).
These explicit units exhibit the field-theoretic obstruction independently of any fixed graph.
