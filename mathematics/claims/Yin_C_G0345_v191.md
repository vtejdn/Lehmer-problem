# Radial logarithms of irreducible trinomials near the unit circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bounded-measure family with unbounded exterior count

For \(n\ge3\) let \(\beta_n>1\) satisfy \(f_n(\beta_n)=0\), where \(f_n=X^n-X-1\).
Each \(f_n\) is irreducible, the degree of every positive power of \(\beta_n\) is \(n\), and the minimum coefficient length of a nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(\beta_n\) is exactly three.
For the complete conjugate set \(z\) of \(\beta_n\), put \(u_n(z)=n\log|z|\).
The empirical distribution of \((\arg z,u_n(z))\), with mass \(1/n\) per conjugate, converges weakly on \((\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb R\) to the distribution of
\[
                 (\theta,\log|1+e^{i\theta}|)
\]
for a uniformly distributed angle \(\theta\).
The value at \(\theta=\pi\) can be set arbitrarily on this set of measure zero.
In particular,
\[
\begin{split}
  n^{-1}\#\{f_n(z)=0:\ |z|>1\}&\longrightarrow 2/3,\\
  m(\beta_n)&\longrightarrow
      c_*=\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|1+e^{i\theta}|\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}>0 .
\end{split}                                                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
All the exterior logarithmic mass in (1) is carried by roots with \(\log|z|\le (\log 3)/n\).
This realizes a positive residual measure in the [short-factor limit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v190.md#statement), for irreducible units with every power degree preserved and an actual shortest relation of three terms and length three.
The limiting multiplicative measure is the classical \(M(1+X+Y)\), reported as approximately \(1.38135644\). [Boyd and Mossinghoff comparison](../audits/literature/BM05/Yin_U001.md#six-term-limit-comparison)

<a id="algebraic"></a>
## Irreducibility and power degrees

For \(|z|=1\) the equation \(z^n=1+z\) would give \(|1+z|=1\), so \(z\) would be a primitive cube root of unity.
Then \(1+z\) has order six, while \(z^n\) has order dividing three.
Thus no unit-circle zero exists.
The [noncyclotomic trinomial theorem](../audits/literature/Lju60/Yin_U001.md#lju60-u001) gives irreducibility.
The constant term makes every root a unit.
The real polynomial is strictly increasing on \([1,\infty)\) and changes sign between 1 and 2, so its positive root there is unique.
The equation \(|z|^n\le1+|z|\) gives \(|z|\le\beta_n\), with equality only at \(\beta_n\) by equality in the triangle inequality.
Any conjugate mapping to \(\beta_n^j\) under the \(j\)-th power map would therefore have to be \(\beta_n\) itself.
The embedding count in characteristic zero gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta_n^j)=\mathbb Q(\beta_n)\) for all \(j\ge1\).
After removing common monomials a relation of length at most two for a nonzero number is either constant or a signed binomial \(X^j\pm1\).
Hence the exhibited length three is minimal.
A two-term relation with unrestricted coefficients would give \(\beta_n^j\in\mathbb Q\), contrary to degree preservation.

<a id="law"></a>
## Passing through the singular angle

All roots have modulus at most two by the positive-root bound.
The equation gives the uniform exterior bound in the statement.
If \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(|z|\le1-\epsilon\), then \(|1+z|=|z|^n\le(1-\epsilon)^n\), whereas \(|1+z|\ge1-|z|\ge\epsilon\).
Thus all root moduli converge to one uniformly.
Also \(m(\beta_n)\le\log3\) by the circle norm bound for \(f_n\).
It follows that the absolute heights are at most \((\log3)/n\), and the inputs are distinct and nontorsion by their degrees.
The [one-dimensional equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives uniform limiting angles.
Equivalently, this follows by applying the reflected-power discriminant estimate at each fixed Fourier frequency.

The exact equation gives \(u_n(z)=\log|1+z|\).
On every closed set of angles omitting a neighborhood of \(\pi\), radial uniform convergence gives uniform convergence to \(\log|1+e^{i\arg z}|\).
For a bounded continuous test function on the product, its restriction there to the relevant compact range is uniformly continuous.
The remaining angles in an arc about \(\pi\) have limiting mass equal to its normalized length, which may be taken arbitrarily small.
Applying angle equidistribution on the complement proves the asserted weak convergence.

The boundary \(u=0\) has limiting mass zero since \(|1+e^{i\theta}|=1\) occurs at only two angles.
The positive set, \(\cos\theta>-1/2\), has normalized length \(2/3\).
The positive part \(u_n^+\) is bounded by \(\log3\) uniformly and can thus be tested by a bounded continuous function equal to \(u^+\) on \(u\le\log3\).
Its empirical average is exactly \(m(\beta_n)\), since the scale of \(u_n\) is \(n\).
This proves (1), and the integral is positive on an arc about zero.
Integrating \(\log|1+X+Y|\) in \(Y\) first identifies the same value by Jensen's formula.
