# The trace pole, integral initial moments and every powered capacity test on one conjugate set

<a id="statement"></a>
## A construction with one prescribed denominator prime

Let \(\ell\) be any rational prime.
For every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four let \(p=q+1\), \(M=pq\), \(s=p/q\), \(K=(2q+1)^2\) and \(J=q/2+1\).
There are arbitrarily large \(D\) and monic reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) of degree \(2D\) with all the following properties simultaneously.

* \(F\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) with simple roots. Exactly two roots lie off the unit circle, a real pair \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) with \((1-1/q)\log s<\log\lambda<\log s\).
* The first \(J\) coefficients below the leading one and the first \(J\) Newton sums vanish. The coefficientwise distance in supremum norm from a monic reciprocal integer polynomial is \(O(1/q)\), with absolute constant.
* The trace polynomial \(H\), defined by \(F(Z)=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\), satisfies the exact pole equation \(M^D H(1/M)=K\) of the [integral trace construction](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return). It has one root in \((0,1/M)\) and \(D-1\) distinct roots in \((-4,0)\).
* For every positive integer \(k\), the compact union of segments \([0,\alpha^{2k}]\) and \([0,\alpha^{4k}]\) over the roots \(\alpha\) of this same \(F\) has logarithmic capacity greater than one.
* At every finite prime other than \(\ell\) the roots are units. With \(e=v_\ell(M)\), for every sufficiently large \(a\) satisfying \(\gcd(a-e,D-1)=1\), the least common denominator of the coefficients can be prescribed to be \(\ell^a\). The primitive integer minimum has total Mahler measure **exactly** \(\ell^a\lambda\).

For each of these degrees there is an integral monic reciprocal polynomial with the specified prefix and exact pole, without the root conditions.
One can further prescribe the residue of the constructed \(F\), modulo any fixed positive integer \(B\) coprime to \(\ell\), to agree with **any** such integral polynomial.
The degree need not be chosen again when \(B\) or \(\ell\) is specified.
The [powered capacity construction](Yin_C_G0901_v169.md#statement) and the [one-prime pole approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#statement) separately provide models with other parameters.
Here the exact pole, the zero prefix and the archimedean capacity inequalities are imposed on a single irreducible rational polynomial.
The prefix grows with \(q\); no lower bound proportional to \(\sqrt D\) for its length is asserted here.
The finite contribution prevents this construction from producing a counterexample to Lehmer's problem.

<a id="congruences"></a>
## Choosing a common degree for the integral constraints and the rotations

Put \(L=2D-2J-1\) and \(F_0(Z)=(Z^L-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)\), taking \(L>2J+1\).
This integral reciprocal polynomial has zero initial prefix of length \(J\).
Among all monic reciprocal polynomials of the same degree with this prefix, the differences have integral basis given by \(Z^i+Z^{2D-i}\) for \(J<i<D\) and \(Z^D\).
For the functional \(P\mapsto q^{2D}P(s)\) the resulting value increments have gcd \(M^{J+1}\).
Indeed the center has value \(M^D\) and the first free pair has value \(M^{J+1}(p^{2(D-J-1)}+q^{2(D-J-1)})\) with the parenthesized integer prime to \(M\).
All free pairs have values divisible by \(M^{J+1}\).
Consequently an integral starting polynomial with the pole exists exactly when \(q^{2D}F_0(s)\equiv K\bmod M^{J+1}\).

To realize that congruence, impose
\[
  p^D\equiv 1+2q\bmod q^{J+1},\qquad
  (1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^{J+1}.                                   \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(q^{2D}F_0(s)=(p^L-q^L)(p^{2J+1}-q^{2J+1})\), which reduces to \(p^{2D}\) and \(q^{2D}\) modulo the indicated coprime moduli.
Both right sides of (1) square to \(K\).
For each prime \(r\mid M\) put \(x=q\) if \(r\mid q\) and \(x=-p\) otherwise.
Write \(b=v_r(x)\).
The class \(1+x\) generates the group of principal units \(1\bmod r^b\) modulo \(r^{(J+1)b}\).
Indeed for odd \(r\), or for \(r=2\) with \(b\ge2\), the binomial expansion inductively gives \(v_r((1+x)^{r^j}-1)=b+j\), so its order is the full group size \(r^{Jb}\).
Since \(4\mid q\) and \(p\) is odd, these cases cover every \(r\).
The target \(1+2x\) lies in this group, giving a residue for \(D\bmod r^{Jb}\).
Reduction modulo \(r^{b+1}\) shows \(D\equiv2\bmod r\).
Thus \(L=2D-q-3\) is nonzero modulo each \(r\mid M\): it is \(1\bmod r\) for \(r\mid q\) and \(2\bmod r\) for \(r\mid p\).
For each remaining prime \(r\le q\), necessarily odd, impose \(D\equiv J+1\bmod r\), so \(L\equiv1\bmod r\).
Chinese remaindering gives arbitrarily large \(D\) with (1) and \(\gcd(L,2k)=1\) for every \(1\le k\le q\).
For instance at \(q=4,\ J=3\), \(D=6982\) gives \(L=13957\), and (1) has residues \(9\bmod256\) and \(616\bmod625\).
This example checks the degree selection only.

<a id="real"></a>
## An open part of the pole slice passes every capacity test

Write \(B_L=1+Z+\cdots+Z^{L-1}\) and \(Q_t=(Z-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-tZ^{J+1}\).
Choose the positive parameter for large \(D\) by
\[
 t=s^{-J-1}\left((s-1)(s^{2J+1}-1)-\frac{K}{q^{2D}B_L(s)}\right).
\]
Then \(F_t=B_LQ_t=F_0-tZ^{J+1}B_L\) has the exact pole and zero prefix.
At fixed \(q\) and increasing admissible \(D\), \(t\) tends from below to \(4\sinh(U/2)\sinh((J+1/2)U)\), \(U=\log s\).
This limit is asymptotic to \(2\sinh(1/2)/q\) as \(q\to\infty\).
It is positive and strictly less than both one and \(4\sin(\pi/(4J+2))\) at sufficiently large \(q\).

The [alternating sign test](Yin_C_G0901_v169.md#prefix) therefore gives \(J\) pairs of simple neutral roots of \(Q_t\).
Its two positive roots are \(e^{\pm u}\), where \(4\sinh(u/2)\sinh((J+1/2)u)=t\); hence \(u\) tends to \(U\) from below for fixed \(q\).
Since \(t\) is rational and strictly between zero and one, \(Q_t\) has no root of unity: substituting one would make \(t\) an algebraic integer.
Thus all roots of \(F_t\) are simple, none is \(1\) or \(-1\), and its trace polynomial has exactly the stated root configuration.
The coefficient discrepancy from \(F_0\) equals \(t\).

For \(1\le k<q\) the \(2k\)-th powers of the roots of \(B_L\) give every nonidentity \(L\)-th root of unity.
Together with the real segment up to \(R=e^{4ku}\) these contain the extended \(L\)-arm star in the [exact capacity formula](Yin_C_G0901_v169.md#star).
For \(k=1\) that formula gives
\[
 \log {\rm cap}\ \ge -\frac{\log4}{L}+2\log\cosh(2u),
\]
because \((2/L)\operatorname{arcosh}(R^{L/2})\ge\log R\).
This lower bound tends at fixed \(q\) to the strictly positive \(2\log\cosh(2U)\); the longer real segments only increase capacity.
For \(k\ge q\) the real segment alone has capacity \(e^{4ku}/4>1\) as soon as \(u>1/(2q)\), which holds for all sufficiently large \(q\) and then \(D\).
In the real space of trace polynomials with this prefix and pole, a sufficiently small neighborhood of the model keeps every root in its required open interval and retains the strict bound on \(u\).
The finitely many star inequalities persist under perturbation of their simple angles by the [distance comparison for perturbed stars](Yin_C_G0901_v169.md#place).
For all larger \(k\) the single real segment suffices as above.
Shrink this neighborhood to retain a coefficient discrepancy at most \(2t\).

<a id="place"></a>
## Irreducibility and one denominator prime with the prefix fixed

The map \(H\mapsto Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\) is an integral triangular bijection of the monic real trace coefficients and the independent reciprocal coefficients, with integral inverse on the integer lattices.
Fixing the first \(J\) coefficients fixes the first \(J\) trace coefficients.
Two such trace polynomials with the same pole differ by \((MX-1)G\), \(\deg G\le D-J-2\).
Their difference is integral exactly when \(G\) is integral by primitive polynomial division.
Choose an integral representative \(H_1\) as in the first part with the requested residue modulo \(B\).

Choose an auxiliary prime \(r\nmid \ell B M K\).
In \(\mathbb F_r\) the value of \(H_1\) at \(M^{-1}\) is \(KM^{-D}\ne0\).
For each other element \(x\in\mathbb F_r\), at most one constant \(c\) would make \(H_1(x)+(Mx-1)c=0\).
There are only \(r-1\) such \(x\).
Choose a constant avoiding all those values and an integer \(c_0\) with that residue modulo \(r\) and zero residue modulo \(B\).
The integral family \(H_1+(MX-1)(c_0+BrG)\) therefore has no residue root at \(r\).
Allow the coefficients of \(G\) to lie in \(\mathbb Z[1/\ell]\).
This family is dense in the real affine slice since \(\mathbb Z[1/\ell]\) is dense in \(\mathbb R\).
Fix one member \(H_b\) in the open neighborhood above; its coefficients have valuations at \(\ell\) at least \(-b\) for some integer \(b\ge0\).
Now put \(H=H_b+Br\ell^{-a}(MX-1)\), with \(a\) arbitrarily large, preserving the neighborhood and the residues.
Its constant and linear coefficient valuations are exactly \(-a\) and \(-a+e\) when \(a\) is large enough, with \(e=v_\ell(M)\).
The other interior valuations are at least \(-b\).
For \(a>De,\ a-e>(D-1)b\), the lower coefficient polygon consists of the two segments from \((0,-a)\) to \((1,-a+e)\) to \((D,0)\).
The second segment has slope with denominator \(D-1\) when \(\gcd(a-e,D-1)=1\).
By the Newton polygon theorem there are \(D-1\) roots of that slope's negative valuation, and any local irreducible factor containing one has degree at least \(D-1\) by ramification.
Thus a rational factorization could only have degrees \(1,D-1\).
The missing linear factor modulo \(r\) excludes a rational root, since \(H\) is monic and integral at \(r\).
This proves irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\).
Over a negative trace embedding the quadratic discriminant \(x(x+4)\) is negative; the totally real trace field therefore gives a degree-two lift.
The lifted \(F\) is irreducible, and its single largest modulus also precludes torsion quotients between distinct conjugates by Galois transitivity.

The trace coefficient denominator is exactly \(\ell^a\); triangular integrality in both directions gives the same denominator for \(F\).
Its unit constant and monicity make every root a local unit away from \(\ell\).
Clearing denominators gives a primitive integer polynomial since some scaled coefficient is nonzero modulo \(\ell\).
Its only archimedean exterior root is \(\lambda\), so its total measure is \(\ell^a\lambda\).
Newton's identities applied to the preserved zero prefix give the required exact power sums.
