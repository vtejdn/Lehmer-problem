# A bound for every factor of an endpoint Taylor quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## The orders at which a strict decrease of Mahler measure is possible

Let \(\sigma\to1\) along any sequence of Salem numbers of degree \(2m\), with trace minimal polynomial \(S\).
Set \(a=\log\sigma\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), \(P(Z)=Z^m S(Z+Z^{-1})\), and \(H_m=\log\max_{|Z|=1}|P(Z)|\).
Write \(h_j=S^{(j)}(2)/j!\), and let \(K\) be the last order before the first nonnegative \(h_j\), with \(h_0<0\).
For \(1\le k\le K\) put
\[
 Q_k(X)=\frac{S(X)-\sum_{j<k}h_j(X-2)^j}{(X-2)^k},
 \qquad U_k(Z)=Z^{m-k}Q_k(Z+Z^{-1}),
\]
where each Taylor sum starts at zero.
The [endpoint quotient construction](Yin_C_G0345_v115.md#roots) gives \(K\sim ma/2\) and puts the largest real root of each \(Q_k\) strictly between \(2\) and \(T\).

There is an absolute \(C>1\) such that, for every sufficiently late input and every \(1\le k\le K\), define
\[
 b_k=\frac{1}{m}\left((k-1)\log\frac1{Cu}-H_m-\log2\right)_+ .
\]
Then every monic irreducible factor \(F\) of \(U_k\), of any signature and degree \(n\), has total logarithmic Mahler measure satisfying
\[
             \frac{\log M(F)}{n}
                  \ge\frac{b_k\log2}{2(\log8+b_k)}.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(b_k>0\) excludes every cyclotomic factor.
For each fixed \(\delta>0\), uniformly over \(\delta K\le k\le K\), the full logarithmic measure is \(\gg_\delta ma\log(1/a)\), and the absolute height of each factor is \(\gg_\delta a\log(1/a)\).

More generally, a factor with \(0<\log M(F)\le a\), even at a single selected order on each input, requires
\[
 k\ll 1+
 \frac{\sqrt m\,\log(2m)+ma\exp(-c a^{-1/3})}{\log(1/a)}            \tag{2}
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\).
It follows that \(k/K=o(a^A)\) for every fixed \(A>0\).
The bound applies to the factor containing the smaller exterior trace and to all its possible complementary factors, without a degree-minimum assumption.

<a id="ratios"></a>
## Controlling the coefficient just before the mode

At the \(m-1\) neutral traces \(x_i\) set \(p_i=u/(T-x_i)\).
Use the [Taylor and probability identities](Yin_C_G0345_v115.md#roots) with
\[
 B(z)=\prod_i(1-p_i+p_i z)=\sum_j P_jz^j,\qquad
 D_j=P_j-P_{j-1},\qquad u^j h_j=-u S'(T)D_j .
\]
Here \(P_{-1}=0\), \(D_j>0\) through \(K\), and \(K\) is the smallest mode of this list.
Write \(E=\sum_i p_i\) and \(V=\sum_i p_i(1-p_i)\).
The [derivative count](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#statement) gives \(|E-K|\le1\) and \(K\sim ma/2\).
In particular \(V\le E\).
For traces in \([2-2u,2-u]\) one has \(1/3\le p_i\le1/2\).
The corresponding positive angular arc has length comparable to \(\sqrt u\asymp a\).
The [short-arc count](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) places \(\gg ma\) conjugates in that arc, since the [Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) implies \(a\gg(\log(2m))^{-3}\) at large degree.
All these phases are neutral, each with a distinct trace on the positive side of the circle.
Thus \(V\asymp K\to\infty\).

For \(|t|\le\pi\), the squared modulus of each Bernoulli factor on the circle is \(1-4p_i(1-p_i)\sin^2(t/2)\).
It follows that \(|B(e^{it})|\le\exp(-c_0V t^2)\) for some absolute \(c_0>0\).
Inverting its Fourier coefficients, with one or two factors of \(1-e^{it}\), proves
\[
       |P_j-P_{j-1}|\ll V^{-1},\qquad |D_j-D_{j-1}|\ll V^{-3/2}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), by integration of \(|t|^s e^{-c_0Vt^2}\) for \(s=1,2\).
Chebyshev's variance inequality gives at least \(3/4\) of the mass to an interval of length \(4\sqrt V\) centered at \(E\).
The modal coefficient therefore satisfies \(P_K\gg K^{-1/2}\), and the first difference bound gives \(P_{K-1}\gg K^{-1/2}\).

The normalized coefficients \(c_j/\binom Nj\) of any degree-\(N\) real-rooted polynomial satisfy Newton's inequality at three consecutive positive coefficients.
Indeed differentiating \(j-1\) times, reversing, and differentiating \(N-j-1\) times gives a real-rooted quadratic whose discriminant gives that inequality.
This applies to \(B\) and to the positive initial coefficients \(D_j\) of \((1-z)B(z)\).
Using it first at \(j=K-1\) for \(B\) gives
\[
   \frac{P_{K-2}}{P_{K-1}}\le\frac{K-1}{K}\frac{P_{K-1}}{P_K}
                         \le 1-\frac1K .
\]
Consequently \(D_{K-1}\gg K^{-3/2}\).
The second difference bound now yields \(D_{K-2}/D_{K-1}\ll1\).
For \((1-z)B\), Newton's inequality implies that the ratios \(D_{j-1}/D_j\) are nondecreasing while these consecutive coefficients are positive.
Thus for an absolute \(C_1\ge1\),
\[
                 |h_{j-1}|/|h_j|\le C_1 u
                              \quad(1\le j\le K-1).
\]
This reaches the last coefficient used in the Taylor section at \(k=K\), even when \(h_K\) itself is close to a change of sign.

<a id="sector"></a>
## A lower bound for the radial displacement of the conjugates

Since \(h_0=S(2)\) is a nonzero integer, \(|h_{k-1}|\ge (C_1u)^{1-k}\).
On any complex \(w\) with \(|w|\ge1/2\), earlier terms in the Taylor section are bounded by a geometric series relative to its last term, with ratio at most \(2C_1u\).
For sufficiently small \(u\), their total is at most half that last modulus.
Hence for \(C=2C_1\),
\[
   \left|\sum_{j<k}h_j w^j\right|\ge \tfrac12(Cu)^{1-k}
                                      \qquad(1\le k\le K).
\]
If \(\beta\) is any root of \(U_k\), put \(x=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), \(w=x-2\), and \(v=\log|\beta|\).
The identity defining \(Q_k\) yields \(S(x)=\sum_{j<k} h_jw^j\).
On the other hand, evaluating \(P\) at the member of \(\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\}\) in the closed disk gives \(|S(x)|\le\exp(H_m+m|v|)\) by the maximum modulus principle.
It follows that \(|v|\ge b_k\) whenever \(|w|\ge1/2\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## The norm at the endpoint for each individual factor

Every \(U_k\) is monic with constant term one, so its roots are algebraic units.
None equals one, since \(Q_k(2)=h_k<0\) through \(K\).
For each root \(\beta\) of the selected irreducible factor \(F\), the element \(w=(\beta-1)^2/\beta\) is a nonzero algebraic integer.
Its nonzero integer norm gives \(\sum_{\beta:F(\beta)=0}\log|w|\ge0\), including extension multiplicities when \(w\) generates a proper subfield.
At each embedding \(|w|\le4e^{|v|}\), and for the unit \(\sum_\beta |v|=2L\) with \(L=\log M(F)\).
If \(s\) of these \(n\) conjugates have \(|w|\ge1/2\), then
\[
       0\le -(n-s)\log2+s\log4+2L,\qquad 2L\ge s b_k.
\]
Eliminating \(s\) gives (1), including factors of bounded degree.

<a id="orders"></a>
## The remaining orders for a strict descent

The [confluent determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) with parameter \(\lfloor\sqrt{2m}\rfloor\) gives \(H_m\ll\sqrt m\log(2m)\).
Since \(u\sim a^2,\ K\sim ma/2\) and \(a\gg(\log(2m))^{-3}\), the normalized cost \(b_k\) tends to zero uniformly for all these orders.
For \(\delta K\le k\le K\), it is \(\gg_\delta a\log(1/a)\).
Inequality (1) gives the height bound there, and summing over all irreducible factors with multiplicity gives the full measure bound, since \(\deg U_k=2(m-k)\sim2m\).

If \(0<L\le a\), the degrees \(n\) tend to infinity by the elementary coefficient bounds in bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure.
Dobrowolski's lower bound for the nontorsion unit root of \(F\) therefore implies \(\log n\gg a^{-1/3}\).
The denominator in (1) is bounded uniformly, so \(b_k\ll a/n\).
Substitution of the upper bound for \(H_m\) proves (2).
Finally \(m^{-1/2}\log(2m)\) tends to zero faster than every fixed power of \(a\), by the lower bound for \(a\) at degree \(2m\).
The exponential in (2) has the same property, and division by \(K\sim ma/2\) proves the last assertion.
No bound here decides the existence of a descent at the first few Taylor orders.
