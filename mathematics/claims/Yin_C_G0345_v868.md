# Ordinary power degree and the square-root span for four-term multiples

<a id="statement"></a>
## The coefficient and root heights at the square-root scale

Let \(P\) be a primitive integer polynomial with four nonzero terms at exponents \(0,gk_1,gk_2,gW\), where \(0<k_1<k_2<W\), \(g\ge1\) and \(\gcd(k_1,k_2,W)=1\).
Assume its reciprocal polynomial differs from both \(P\) and \(-P\), and write \(\mathcal H\) for its maximum absolute coefficient.
For a nonzero nontorsion root \(z\), set \(d=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\), \(m=d h(z)\), \(\beta=z^g\), and \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
There are absolute constants \(b,c>0\) such that for \(m<b\),
\[
 \frac d g\le D\ll\sqrt W,
 \qquad 1+\log\mathcal H\asymp 1+Wh(\beta),
 \qquad \log\mathcal H\ge c\sqrt W\,G_3(W),
 \qquad G_3(t)=\left(\frac{\log\log(5t)}{\log(2t)}\right)^3.
\]
Along such roots with \(h(\beta)\to0\) one necessarily has
\[
                  W\gg h(\beta)^{-2}G_3(1/h(\beta))^2.
\]
All these necessary inequalities hold in the range \(m<b\), without a smallness hypothesis on the measure \(D h(\beta)\) of the primitive power.
Normalize each rational irreducible factor to a primitive integer polynomial.
For each fixed parent the sum of the degrees, counted with their multiplicities in \(P\), of **all** rational irreducible factors \(S\) with \(0<m(S)<b\) is \(O(g\sqrt W)\).

For \(\log\mathcal H\le(c/2)\sqrt W\,G_3(W)\), all positive-measure integral factors have a uniform positive measure gap.
The same holds, with a gap depending on \(C,\delta\), under \(\log\mathcal H\le C\sqrt W(\log\log W/\log W)^{3+\delta}\) for fixed \(C,\delta>0\).
These estimates sharpen the [bounds at powers strictly below one half](Yin_C_G0345_v867.md#statement).
At \(\log\mathcal H\asymp\sqrt W\) they leave the existence of a uniform gap open.

<a id="isolation"></a>
## A bounded-degree curve contained in a character kernel

Take \(u=(k_1,k_2,W)\), \(x=(\beta^{k_1},\beta^{k_2},\beta^W)\), and the [rational hyperplane](Yin_C_G0345_v867.md#intersection) \(L=a_0+a_1X_1+a_2X_2+a_3X_3\).
For \(m\) smaller than an absolute constant, \(z\) is a reciprocal algebraic unit, so \(x\) belongs to the intersection \(Z\) of \(L=0\) and \(L(X^{-1})=0\).
The two equations have ordinary degrees one and three after clearing denominators and are geometrically coprime in the torus.
Their projective intersections have total curve degree at most three by Bézout.
The plane is irreducible and its intersection with the torus is dense, so it cannot be a component of the cubic surface.
In particular every geometric curve component \(C\) of \(Z\) has degree at most three in the compactification \((1:X_1:X_2:X_3)\).

There is a primitive nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Z^3\) with \(v\cdot u=0\) and \(\|v\|_\infty\le B=\lceil2\sqrt W\rceil\).
Indeed the \((B+1)^3\) vectors in \(\{0,\ldots,B\}^3\) have at most \(3BW+1\) distinct dot products with \(u\), so two agree.
Take their difference and divide by the gcd.
The subtorus \(T_2:X^v=1\) contains \(x\) and the one-parameter subgroup of direction \(u\), and its defining binomial after clearing monomials has degree at most \(3B\).

Suppose a geometric curve component \(C\) through \(x\) lies in \(T_2\).
On its smooth projective normalization each \(X_i\) is the ratio of the \(i\)-th homogeneous coordinate to the zeroth coordinate.
Both are nonzero sections of the pullback of \(\mathcal O(1)\), of degree \(\deg C\), so every entry of every row \((\operatorname{ord}_P X_1,\operatorname{ord}_P X_2,\operatorname{ord}_P X_3)\) has absolute value at most three.
The rank of these rows equals the multiplicative rank of the three coordinate functions modulo constants, since a function of divisor zero on a projective curve is constant.
It is at most two because \(v\) annihilates every row.
Rank zero would make all three coordinates constant on a curve.
In rank one, two independent character functions are constant on \(C\), so \(C\) is contained in a translate of a connected one-dimensional subtorus.
The full relation lattice modulo constants is saturated because the constant field is algebraically closed.
The dimensions force equality inside the torus.
The [character partition on the hyperplane](Yin_C_G0345_v867.md#intersection) then gives two cancelling pairs, so a nonzero power of \(z\) is rational.
In rank two, choose two independent rows.
Their cross product has entries bounded by eighteen, and its primitive normalization equals \(\pm v\) because it spans their common kernel.
Thus \((-\sum_i v_i,v_1,v_2,v_3)\) is a primitive affine relation of bounded length on the four exponents.
The [four-term power-degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v866.md#statement), including its [unit-square case](Yin_C_G0345_v866.md#parents) for the excluded parallelogram relation, gives an absolute bound on the abelian degree of a power of \(z\).
Both ranks therefore give an absolute positive lower bound on \(m\) by [cyclic compression](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#compression).
Choose \(b\) below this bound.
It follows that \(x\) is isolated in \(Z\cap T_2\), and the same holds for each of its rational conjugates because the equations are rational.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Counting the ordinary conjugates and bounding their height

Drop the projective curve components of \(Z\) lying entirely in the closure of \(T_2\).
Each other component contributes at most its degree times \(3B\) points to the finite intersection with the binomial hypersurface, by ordinary curve-hypersurface Bézout.
The preceding isolation ensures that every conjugate counted here belongs to a remaining component, and the sum of their degrees is at most three.
Thus there are at most \(9B\) ordinary conjugates of \(x\).
Since \(u\) is primitive, a Laurent monomial in the three coordinates recovers \(\beta\), so this number is exactly \(D\).
The polynomial \(X^g-\beta\) gives \(d/g\le D\).
In fact fix the auxiliary vector \(v\) once from the exponents of the parent.
For every root belonging to any rational irreducible factor of logarithmic measure in \((0,b)\), the same intersection supplies at most \(9B\) possible points in total, hence at most \(9B\) possible primitive powers \(\beta\).
Each lifts to at most \(g\) distinct values of \(z\).
A nonzero root has multiplicity at most three.
Vanishing of the first four iterates \((X\,d/dX)^jP\), \(0\le j\le3\), would annihilate the vector of four nonzero term values by the invertible Vandermonde matrix of the exponents.
Thus the total root count with multiplicity is at most \(27gB\), giving the asserted aggregate degree bound.

The point \(x\) lies outside every positive-dimensional coset contained in a component of \(Z\), since such a coset would again split the four hyperplane terms into cancelling pairs.
The primitive direction gives an untwisted one-parameter subgroup and \(x\) is nontorsion.
The [point-height estimate of Amoroso, Sombra and Zannier, (3.4)](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U002.md#asz17-u002) applies with ambient dimension three, rational defining equations of degrees at most three and logarithmic heights at most \(\log\mathcal H+O(1)\).
In its projective embedding the local maximum of the coordinate absolute values of \((1:x)\) is \(\max(1,|\beta|_v)^W\) at every place, since zero and \(W\) are the extreme exponents.
Consequently \(Wh(\beta)=h(1:x)\ll1+\log\mathcal H\).
For the opposite inequality the [three-row minor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v867.md#measure) gives \(\log\mathcal H\le3Wh(\beta)+\log6\) in the same small-measure range, after reducing \(b\) if necessary.

<a id="span"></a>
## Ordinary height at degree at most the square root of the span

[Voutier's ordinary degree estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) at the nontorsion \(\beta\) gives \(D h(\beta)\gg G_3(D)\), including small degrees by its corollary and the elementary rational bound.
This step applies for every value of \(D h(\beta)\).
The [weight comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v864.md#width) and \(D\ll\sqrt W\) imply
\[
 h(\beta)\gg W^{-1/2}G_3(W),
\]
since \(G_3(C\sqrt W)\asymp G_3(W)\) for each fixed positive \(C\) as \(W\to\infty\).
For small \(m\), \(W\) exceeds any chosen fixed constant after reducing \(b\), by the [primitive degree and compression estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v867.md#measure).
The comparison of heights now gives the claimed lower bound for \(\log\mathcal H\), as \(\sqrt W G_3(W)\to\infty\).
The two coefficient regimes follow directly in the large-span range, and bounded spans have a uniform gap by the same compression estimate.
A positive-measure integral divisor contains a positive-measure irreducible factor or has nontrivial content.

For \(t=1/h(\beta)\to\infty\), the ordinary height inequality gives \(\sqrt W\gg tG_3(W)\).
If \(W\ge t^2\) the claimed span estimate holds since \(G_3\) is bounded above.
Otherwise \(G_3(W)\gg G_3(t^2)\asymp G_3(t)\) by the same comparison, proving the bound after squaring.
