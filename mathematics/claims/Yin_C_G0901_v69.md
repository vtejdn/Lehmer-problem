# Two independent reciprocal pairs with a nonzero integral remainder

<a id="statement"></a>
## The sharp lower bound

Let \(m\) denote logarithmic Mahler measure with probability Haar normalization.
Let \(H(Y)\) be any nonzero Laurent polynomial with integer coefficients in any finite number of variables, including a nonzero constant.
Let \(Z_1,Z_2\) be two further variables and \(u_1(Y),u_2(Y)\) monomials whose coefficients have absolute value one.
Put
\[
 F=H(Y)+\sum_{j=1}^2\bigl(u_j(Y)Z_j+u_j(Y)^{-1}Z_j^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
 m(F)\ \ge\ C+p\,m(H)\ \ge\ C,\qquad
 C=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
       \frac{\binom{2j}{j}^2}{256^j(2j+1)},\qquad
 p=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2j}{j}^2}{256^j}.             \tag{1}
\]
Here \(0.2513304337<C<0.2513304338\).
The minimum \(C\) is attained exactly when \(H=1\) or \(H=-1\).
In particular there is a single lower bound for all choices of the other coefficients, exponents and numbers of variables.
For constant integer multipliers \(b_1,b_2\) on the two displayed pairs, Jensen's formula in a variable of largest absolute multiplier gives \(m(F)\ge\log\max(|b_1|,|b_2|)\) whenever this maximum is positive.
Thus the lower bound \(C\) holds for arbitrary nonzero integer pair multipliers as long as \(H\ne0\).
If \(H=0\), the two unit-coefficient pairs alone have measure zero by the factorization in the [three-pair bound](Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#statement).
One unit-coefficient pair with the nonzero constant remainder one also has measure zero, since \(Z+1+Z^{-1}=Z^{-1}\Phi_3(Z)\).

<a id="potential"></a>
## The logarithmic potential of two reciprocal pairs

Let \(S=2\cos\theta+2\cos\varphi\) for independent uniform angles and put \(U(z)=\mathbb E\log|z+S|\) for complex \(z\).
Write \(L(t)=U(t)\) for real \(t\ge0\).
For fixed \(z\) the logarithm is integrable on the two-torus, by logarithmic integrability of a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
The law of \(S\) is symmetric, so
\[
 2U(z)=\mathbb E\log|z^2-S^2|\ \ge\ \mathbb E\log\bigl||z|^2-S^2\bigr|
       =2L(|z|).
\]
Indeed the difference of the squared moduli inside this pointwise comparison is \(4S^2(\Im z)^2\).
The inequality is strict for nonreal \(z\) since \(S\ne0\) almost surely, as follows by conditioning on one angle.

Jensen's formula in one angle gives \(L(t)=\mathbb E\,g(t+2\cos\theta)\), where \(g(v)=0\) for \(|v|\le2\) and \(g(v)=\operatorname{arcosh}(|v|/2)\) otherwise.
In particular \(L(0)=0\) and \(L\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)\), including at four, by dominated convergence on compact \(t\)-intervals.
The variable \(s=2-2\cos\theta\) has density \(1/(\pi\sqrt{s(4-s)})\) on \((0,4)\).
For \(0<t<4\), \(g(t+2-s)\) is positive only for \(s<t\), where it equals \(\int_s^t[(v-s)(4+v-s)]^{-1/2}\,dv\).
Tonelli's theorem gives \(L(t)\) as the integral from zero to \(t\) of the continuous function
\[
 L'(v)=\frac1\pi\int_0^v
     \frac{ds}{\sqrt{s(4-s)(v-s)(4+v-s)}}
 =\frac1\pi\int_0^1
     \frac{dy}{\sqrt{y(1-y)(4-vy)(4+v(1-y))}} .
\]
The second expression, with \(s=vy\), is continuous for \(0<v<4\) by domination on compact subintervals.
Putting \(u=y/(1-y)\) and then \(u=(4+v)\tan^2\psi/4\) gives
\[
 L'(v)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
       \frac{du}{\sqrt{u(4+(4-v)u)(4+v+4u)}}
      =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}
       \frac{d\psi}{\sqrt{1-(v^2/16)\sin^2\psi}}.                       \tag{2}
\]
This derivative is positive and strictly increasing on \((0,4)\).
With \(p=L'(1)\), (2) gives \(1/4<p\le1/\sqrt{15}<1\).
At \(0\le v\le1\) the binomial series in (2) converges uniformly.
Integrating its terms with \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{2j}\psi\,d\psi=(\pi/2)\binom{2j}{j}/4^j\) gives the two series in (1), with \(C=L(1)\).
The first nine terms of \(C\) and an upper bound \(1/257698037760\) on the omitted tail give the stated decimal enclosure by rational comparison.
This tail bound follows from \(\binom{2j}{j}\le4^j\) and the geometric series starting at \(j=9\).

## A global supporting line and the role of integrality

On \(0<t<4\) the logarithmic derivative \(tL'(t)\) increases strictly.
Thus \(u\mapsto L(e^u)\) is strictly convex up to \(u=\log4\) and lies above its tangent at \(u=0\).
For \(t>4\), \(t+S\) is strictly positive and \(L'(t)=\mathbb E(1/(t+S))\).
Since \(\mathbb E S=0\), the convexity of \(x\mapsto1/x\) gives \(tL'(t)\ge1>p\).
Continuity at four now extends the same tangent bound to every \(t>0\), with strict inequality except at \(t=1\), giving
\[
                           L(t)\ge C+p\log t .                            \tag{3}
\]
The derivative lower bound on \(t>4\) suffices for this extension.

Conditional on \(Y\), the factors \(u_j(Y)\) rotate two independent Haar variables, so \(m(F)=\int U(H(Y))\,dY\).
The zero set of \(H\) has Haar measure zero and \(\log|H|\) is integrable.
Applying the complex comparison and (3) gives the first inequality in (1).
For a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial, successive use of Jensen's lower bound by the log modulus of the leading coefficient, after clearing a monomial in the integration variable, gives \(m(H)\ge0\).
The last remaining coefficient in this process is a nonzero integer.
Thus equality \(m(F)=C\) requires \(|H|=1\) almost everywhere by strictness in (3), and \(H\) real almost everywhere by strictness in the complex comparison.
Continuity and connectedness of the torus then give \(H=1\) or \(H=-1\), and symmetry of \(S\) yields equality for both constants.
For a reciprocal pair multiplied by \(b\ne0\), multiply the Laurent polynomial by its variable and use Jensen on the resulting quadratic.
Its leading coefficient has absolute value \(|b|\) on the other torus variables, giving the separate bound \(m(F)\ge\log|b|\).

## Necessary relations involving two chosen frequencies

Let a one-variable integer Laurent polynomial contain two pairs of terms \(b_j(X^{a_j}+X^{-a_j})\) with distinct positive integer frequencies \(a_1,a_2\) and nonzero integer coefficients \(b_1,b_2\).
Write \(H(X)\) for the remainder after removing those four terms, assume \(H\ne0\), and let \(k\) be the number of nonzero terms of the original polynomial.
The remainder may have arbitrary integer coefficients.
Replace just the two selected pairs by \(b_j(Z_j+Z_j^{-1})\) in independent variables.
The support of the new polynomial is
\[
 S=\{(h,0):h\in\operatorname{supp}H\}\cup
       \{(0,\pm e_1),(0,\pm e_2)\}\subset\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^2 .
\]
Both the original and the unfolded polynomial have \(k\) terms.
For \(0<\delta<1\), take the regularization parameter \(\eta\) and integer \(N\) given by
\[
 \eta=\left(\frac{\delta}{48k^2}\right)^{4(k-1)},\qquad
 N=\left\lceil(1+k^2/\eta)/\delta\right\rceil
\]
in the [finite constant-coefficient comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#transfer).
In this notation \(N(S-S)\) means all sums of \(N\) support differences, with zero differences allowed.
For \(A(h,v)=h+v_1a_1+v_2a_2\), the cited comparison gives \(m(F_A)\ge m(F)-\delta\) when \(\ker A\cap N(S-S)=\{0\}\).
For \(|b_1|=|b_2|=1\), bound (1) applies after rotating by the signs.
For a larger absolute coefficient the single-variable Jensen bound gives at least \(\log 2>C\).
Thus in both cases \(m(F_A)\ge C-\delta\) under the indicated relation hypothesis, with no coefficient-height factor.

In particular, at multiplicative measure at most \(3049/2500=1.2196\), every such pair of frequencies must satisfy
\[
          (h,v)\in N(S-S),\qquad v\ne0,\qquad h+v_1a_1+v_2a_2=0,          \tag{4}
\]
where \(\delta=1/24\).
Otherwise \(M(F_A)\ge e^{C-1/24}>e^{5/24}>3049/2500\), the last inequality following already from \(1+5/24+(5/24)^2/2\).
A kernel element with \(v=0\) has \(h=0\), so a genuine relation has the required nonzero selected component.
If \(\operatorname{supp}H\subset[-D,D]\), (4) has \(|h|\le2ND\) and \(\|v\|_1\le2N\).
When \(|b_j|\ge2\) for a single selected pair, unfolding only that pair gives the same necessity with one selected coordinate and with all other terms retained in the remainder, which may now be zero.
This follows by applying the same transfer to the measure lower bound \(\log|b_j|\).
The bounds are uniform in degree and coefficients for a fixed number of terms, and use the displayed \(N\) for each individual value of \(k\) when \(k\) varies.
They concern the whole polynomial, and pass to one occurrence of an irreducible factor if the remaining cofactor has multiplicative measure one.
