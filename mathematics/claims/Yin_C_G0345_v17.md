# Every branch-two six-term trace denominator starts by a constant multiple of the second offset

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniformity over both integer offsets

Let a positive-constant Pisot input on branch \(j=2\) have constant \(q\), six-term trace lift of degree \(N=2h+2\) with lower half \(A_i(z)=2+a z^d+b z^e\), \(0<d<e<h+1\), and reciprocal monic integer denominator \(B^\sharp(z)=\sum_{n=0}^N b_n z^n\).
There are absolute constants \(C,Q\) such that for \(q\ge Q\) at least one \(b_n\ne0\) with \(1\le n\le Ce\).
The first coefficient bound includes arbitrary allowed signs and sizes of \(a,b\) and has no restriction on the frequency ratio.
It gives a necessary condition on the inverse denominator with no assertion that any such denominator exists.

<a id="fixed"></a>
## Projection at the common divisor

If the assertion fails, choose \(q_i\to\infty\) with the first positive nonzero index \(t_i\) satisfying \(t_i/e_i\to\infty\).
The [large reduced frequency theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v16.md#statement) permits us to take \(d_i=g_i m,\ e_i=g_i n\) for a fixed coprime pair \(1\le m<n\).
By the [six-term restrictions](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six), \(e_i=o(N_i)\) and we may take fixed \(a,b\) with \(|b|=1,\ |a|\le3\).
Let \(F(w)=1+(a w^m+b w^n)/2\).
As in the [interior cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v16.md#middle), \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
Indeed that proof uses only \(e_i=o(N_i)\), bounded \(\|F\|_\infty\), the [signed representation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) and the [delay-one fixed-support contradiction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).

Push the positive part of the signed measure \(\omega_i\) for \(J_i=2/K_i\) forward by \(v\mapsto(w,u)=(\bar v^{g_i},v^{t_i})\) and take a weak limit \(\Omega\).
Its mass is one and its \(w\)-marginal is symmetric with Herglotz series \(1/F\).
Indeed \(B^\sharp=F(z^{g_i})J_i\) through \(N_i-e_i-1\) and equals one through \(t_i-1\), while \(t_i/g_i\to\infty\).
The negative mass tends to zero and, for every fixed \(l>0\), the \(lg_i\)-th coefficient of \(J_i\) is eventually the \(l\)-th Taylor coefficient of \(1/F\).
In particular \(\Re(1/F)>0\) in the open disk.
The rational Herglotz representation has bounded continuous density off its circle poles and possible atoms only at zeros of \(F\).
This follows by removing the simple boundary-pole kernels first, as in the [rational measure decomposition](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#departure).
The doubled continuous density is \(\sigma=2\Re(1/F)\) on regular points.
It is positive except at finitely many points, since the trigonometric polynomial \(\Re F\) has positive mean and is nonnegative.

<a id="shapes"></a>
## A bounded family of integer polynomials on the circle

For fixed \(k\ge1\), weight \(2\Omega\) by \(u^k\), project to \(w\), and multiply by \(F(w)\).
This kills all the marginal atoms.
The remaining density \(H_k=F\sigma c_k\), where \(c_k\) are conditional probability moments, has integer Fourier coefficients.
At every integer \(l\) its coefficient is the limit of \(b_{kt_i+lg_i}\) by the same exact interior moment identity, since \(e_i=o(t_i)\) and \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
In particular \(H_1\ne0\).
We have \(|H_k|\le D=2\Re F/|F|\le2\) off the zeros, and \(\int D^2<4\) since the nonconstant analytic polynomial \(F\) has nonzero imaginary part on an open arc.
Hence \(H_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial with squared coefficient sum at most three.
The modulus bound holds at all regular points by continuity and its uniform bound two extends over the whole circle.

No signed trinomial in one variable can obey this bound.
After division by a signed monomial write its three coefficients as signs at indices \(0,p,q\), \(0<p<q\).
If \(q\ne2p\), the three nonzero positive differences are distinct, so Parseval on the squared modulus gives fourth moment \(9+6=15\), whereas a modulus at most two gives fourth moment at most \(4\cdot3=12\).
If \(q=2p\), rotate \(w^p\) and change the overall sign to reduce to \(1+x\pm x^2\).
The plus sign attains three at one and the minus sign attains \(\sqrt5\) at \(i\).

Thus each nonzero \(H_k\) is a signed monomial or a signed monomial times \(1\pm w^r\), \(r\ge1\).
Where the binomial has modulus two one must have \(\Im F=0\), except possibly at a zero of \(F\), which already has zero imaginary part.
The nonzero trigonometric polynomial \(\Im F\) has at most \(2n\) zeros on the circle.
It follows that \(r\le 2n\), and hence there are finitely many shapes up to monomial shift for this fixed \(F\).
Moreover \(M(D)<2\), since \(D\le2\) and the inequality is strict on an open set.
All expressions involved are rational or their moduli, with only finite-order zeros or removable exclusions on the circle.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## Prime specialization for the trinomials

For each possible nonzero shape \(P\) define
\[
                   R_P(w)=\frac{P(w)F(1/w)}{P(1/w)F(w)}.
\]
The quotient \(P(w)/P(1/w)\) is a signed monomial in every case just found.
The rational function \(F(1/w)/F(w)\) has a pole off the unit circle and away from zero.
Indeed \(F\) has no zeros in the open disk by its reciprocal Herglotz representation, and the product of its root moduli is two since \(|b|=1\).
Any root of modulus greater than one is therefore a pole with nonvanishing reversed numerator.
No \(R_P\) can satisfy \(R_P(w^2)=R_P(w)^2\) identically, since the squarings of an exterior pole would give infinitely many poles.

For clarity, apply the [prime specialization of conditional moments](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) with the present \(F\).
Choose a sufficiently large odd prime \(p\) so that each primitive \(p\)-th root and its conjugates avoid all poles, zeros of the shapes and exceptional zeros of \(\sigma,D\), and avoid the finitely many zeros of the nonidentities just established.
For such a root \(\xi\), \(R_P(\xi)\) is not a \(p\)-th root of unity, since the automorphism \(\xi\mapsto\xi^2\) would then give the forbidden squaring equality at \(\xi\).
Thus every nonzero \(c_k(\xi)\) is nonreal, its quotient by its conjugate being \(\xi^{2s}R_P(\xi)\) for an integer shift \(s\).

At regular circle points the continuous representatives \(H_k/(F\sigma)\) satisfy all positive semidefinite moment inequalities by continuity from the conditional probabilities.
At \(\xi\) and every conjugate the full two-sided sequence is therefore the Fourier transform of a probability.
A common integer clears the single denominator \(F(\xi)\sigma(\xi)\) of all these moments in the cyclotomic integer lattice, since \(H_k(\xi)\) are algebraic integers and negative indices are complex conjugates.
Inverting the matrix of the embeddings makes each integral coordinate sequence a Fourier–Stieltjes transform.
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) makes every coordinate periodic outside a finite set.
With common period \(T\), the positive-tail value at multiples of \(T\) is the mass of the \(T\)-th roots of unity under the probability, by Cesàro averaging.
It must be zero since every nonzero value is nonreal.
The same averages on any residue class then vanish, forcing all sufficiently late moments at \(\xi\) to be zero.
As \(\xi\) avoids the roots of every shape, the corresponding \(H_k\) vanish identically for all sufficiently large \(k\).

If \(r\) is the greatest index of a nonzero row, raising the circle variable of the conditional probability to the \(r\)-th power leaves only the first conjugate pair of nonconstant moments.
The resulting density \(1+2\Re(c_r\bar u)\ge0\) implies \(|c_r|\le1/2\).
Thus \(1\le M(H_r)\le M(D)/2<1\), a contradiction to \(H_1\ne0\).
This rules out the remaining fixed primitive offsets and proves uniformity.
