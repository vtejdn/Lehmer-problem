# The degree of a small Salem factor relative to its trinomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## A necessary asymptotic equality

Let \(e^t>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\) whose minimal polynomial \(S\) divides a nonzero integer polynomial of coefficient length at most six.
For all sufficiently small \(t\), the [two-block reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v174.md#statement) gives exponents \(0\le b<a<N\), a sign \(\sigma\), and \(Q=X^a-X^b-1\) such that \(S\mid X^NQ+\sigma Q^*\), where the star reverses the coefficients.
Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
With an absolute error constant the degree must satisfy
\[
             \frac{(N-a)t}{d}=m(Q)+O(d^{-1/16}).                     \tag{1}
\]
The right-hand main term is at most \(\log 3\) and is bounded below by a positive absolute constant.
This includes \(b=0\), when it equals \(\log 2\).
The equality is necessary for a sequence tending to one and leaves its existence open.

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Removing the common roots

Set \(C=\gcd(Q,Q^*)\) monic and \(q=Q/C\).
For \(b=0\) the two polynomials have no common root.
For \(b>0\) a common root \(\beta\) satisfies \((1+\beta^b)(1+\beta^{-b})=1\).
It follows that \(\beta^b+\beta^{-b}=-1\), so \(\beta^{3b}=1\).
The polynomial \(Q\) is squarefree.
Indeed a multiple root for \(b>0\) would satisfy \(\beta^{a-b}=b/a\) and \(\beta^b=-a/(a-b)\), which give opposite strict bounds on \(|\beta|\), and \(X^a-2\) is squarefree.
Consequently \(C\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials, \(C^*=\pm C\), \(m(q)=m(Q)\), and \(q,q^*\) are coprime.
Every unit-circle zero of \(Q\) is a common zero since \(Q\) has real coefficients.
Dividing the original multiple by \(C\) shows \(S\mid X^Nq\pm q^*\).
Taking the resultant with the monic polynomial \(q\) and using multiplicativity gives
\[
             1\le |\operatorname{Res}(q,S)|
                 \le |\operatorname{Res}(q,q^*)|\le 2^a6^{a^2}.     \tag{2}
\]
For the last inequality the roots of both \(Q\) and \(Q^*\) have modulus in \([1/3,3]\) by the elementary coefficient root bound and inversion.
The leading coefficient of \(q^*\) has absolute value at most two.
In the pair-product formula for the last resultant every root difference has modulus at most six.
Since that resultant is a nonzero integer, each such difference has modulus at least \(2^{-a}6^{-a^2}\).
For each root \(\beta\) of \(q\), the root \(1/\bar\beta\) of \(q^*\) gives in particular
\[
                  \big||\beta|-1\big|\ \ge\ \tfrac14\,2^{-a}6^{-a^2}.
\]

<a id="circle"></a>
## Averaging the logarithm over the neutral conjugates

Let \(u(\theta)=\log|q(e^{i\theta})|\).
The root distances just obtained and the monic product give \(\|u\|_\infty=O(a^3)\).
The derivative of \(|q(e^{i\theta})|^2\) is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(a\), so it is either identically zero or has at most \(2a\) zeros in a period.
Therefore the total variation of \(u\) is \(O(a^4)\).

The empirical measure of the arguments of all \(d\) conjugates of \(e^t\) differs from normalized angular length by \(O(d^{-1/8})\) on every arc, uniformly for \(t\le1\).
To see this, use the [translated triangular weights](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) in equation (4) there with \(h=d^{-1/8}\) and \(m=t\).
Normalize the triangle to have integral one for normalized angular length.
The discrepancy of every translate of this kernel is then \(O(h^{-1}\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}+\log(2d)/(dh^2))\).
Convolving the indicators of the arc expanded and contracted by \(h\) at each end with this kernel gives upper and lower bounds for its indicator, using the empty set or full circle when necessary.
Their means differ from its length by \(O(h)\), which proves the assertion by integration.
Integration by parts against \(u\), followed by deletion of the two real conjugates from the phase sum, now gives
\[
        \sum_{\substack{S(z)=0\\|z|=1}}\log|q(z)|
           =d\,m(Q)+O(a^4d^{7/8}+a^3).                              \tag{3}
\]
Here Jensen's formula identifies the circle mean with \(m(q)\).

<a id="real"></a>
## The reciprocal pair and the error

Write \(\tau=e^t\) and \(r=\deg q\).
The reduced equation gives \(|q(\tau)|=e^{-(N-r)t}|q(\tau^{-1})|\).
One has \(1\le |Q(\tau^{-1})|\le2\) and \(|C(\tau^{-1})|\le2^a\), hence \(|q(\tau^{-1})|\ge2^{-a}\).
The root product also gives \(|q(\tau^{-1})|\le4^a\).
Taking the logarithm of the resultant over the roots of \(S\), inserting the two real values and using (2) and (3), gives
\[
             (N-r)t=d\,m(Q)+O(a^4d^{7/8}+a^3+a^2).
\]
The earlier reduction gives \(d\ge1/(20t^2)\) and \(a\ll\log(2/t)/t\).
The [Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) at degree \(d\) gives \(1/t\ll(\log(2d))^3\) for large \(d\), so \(a\ll(\log(2d))^4\).
Since \(0\le a-r\le a\), replacing \(r\) by \(a\), dividing by \(d\), and absorbing logarithms proves (1).
The positive real root of \(Q\) greater than one belongs to a positive-measure factor, so the [length-five bound](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) gives the asserted absolute lower bound.
The estimate \(m(Q)\le\log\|Q\|_1=\log 3\) proves the upper bound.
