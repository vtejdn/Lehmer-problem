# Mahler orbits of products of small real Salem numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least marked trace field decides the entire orbit

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_s>1\) be real Salem numbers in pairwise distinct embedded root fields, with \(s\ge1\).
Write \(F_i=\mathbb Q(x_i+x_i^{-1})\) in the specified positive real embedding, \(n_i=[F_i:\mathbb Q]\), \(m_i=\log x_i\), and \(S=\sum_i m_i\).
There is an absolute \(\delta>0\) such that whenever \(S<\delta\), the following conclusions hold for every \(A=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\) with arbitrary positive integer exponents.

* If one \(F_i\) is contained in every other \(F_j\), some finite iterate of the multiplicative Mahler measure \(M\) is a positive power of \(x_i\), fixed by all further iterations.
* Otherwise the forward orbit tends to infinity.
  Two original factors persist as positive powers in every iterate, and the exponent of each at least doubles at every step.

There is no restriction on the number of factors or the ratios of their degrees.
More generally, for every \(H>0\) the same conclusions hold if \(S\le H\) and \(\min_i n_i\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(H\).
Sources in the same root field can first be written as powers of its one positive [primitive norm-one generator](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) and combined.
For the single remaining factor the initial value is already fixed.
For two sources this recovers the [near-one orbit criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v157.md#orbits).
With three or more sources, two minimal fields suffice for divergence even when other pairs are comparable.
The criterion concerns products of genuine Salem values and does not assert their accumulation at one or that every strict record has Salem measure.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## Conditional probabilities of marking a second field

Put \(F=F_1\cdots F_s\), and sample one of its real embeddings uniformly.
Write \(E_i\) for the event that this embedding restricts to the identity on \(F_i\).
It has probability \(1/n_i\), and
\[
       \Pr(E_j\mid E_i)=\frac1{[F_iF_j:F_i]}.
\]
Both equalities count extensions of an embedding through a tower of separable fields.
Let \(D=[\mathbb Q(x_i,x_j):\mathbb Q]\) for a pair of distinct indices.
Each input has degree \(2n_i\) or \(2n_j\), and \(D\le4[F_iF_j:\mathbb Q]\le4n_in_j\).
All positive powers of a Salem number generate its root field, since taking such a power preserves the unique strictly largest conjugate.
The [two-unit comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#comparison) at these distinct root fields gives, with an absolute \(C\),
\[
       \Pr(E_j\mid E_i)\le
           C m_i m_j\frac{(\log(12n_in_j))^{212}}{n_j}.
\]
For \(L(n)=\sup_{u\ge n}(\log(12u^2))^{212}/u\), this yields
\[
       \sum_{j\ne i:\,n_j\ge n_i}\Pr(E_j\mid E_i)
                          \le C m_i S L(n_i).                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(L(2)<\infty\) and \(L(n)\to0\).
Under either hypothesis of the statement, we may ensure that (1) is at most \(1/8\) for every \(i\).
In particular all the embedded \(F_i\) are distinct, since an equality would contribute one.
The same bound holds for subfamilies, and their conditional probabilities are unchanged when their trace compositum replaces \(F\).

<a id="signs"></a>
## Independent quadratic lifts and nonnegative exponents

Put \(d_i=(x_i+x_i^{-1})^2-4\in F_i\).
Its image at an embedding of \(F\) is positive precisely on \(E_i\), and negative elsewhere.
For any nonempty set \(J\) of indices choose \(i\in J\) of smallest trace degree.
By (1) some embedding marks just \(i\) among \(J\).
Some other embedding marks none of \(J\), since \([F_iF_j:F_i]\le n_j\) gives
\[
       \sum_{j\in J}\Pr(E_j)\le 1/n_i+
                     \sum_{j\in J\setminus\{i\}}\Pr(E_j\mid E_i)
                      \le 1/2+1/8<1 .
\]
The product of the \(d_j\) for \(j\in J\) has opposite signs at these two embeddings and hence is not a square in the totally real field \(F\).
Thus the squareclasses are independent, and the root compositum \(F(\sqrt{d_1},\ldots,\sqrt{d_s})\) has degree \(2^s\) over \(F\).
Here the elementary quadratic-extension criterion follows by induction: in a multiquadratic extension with all independent sign flips, a nonzero element whose square is in the base is an eigenvector for every flip, hence a base multiple of a square-root monomial in the preceding radicands.
Each real embedding of \(F\) has all \(2^s\) root choices as complex lifts.
The compositum is exactly \(\mathbb Q(A)\), since an automorphism fixing the product must preserve each factor's unique strictly largest conjugate. [Perron product field](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#fields)

Write \(U_i=a_i m_i\).
Over a base embedding \(\sigma\), the \(2^s\) logarithms of the conjugate moduli are \(\sum_{i:\,\sigma\in E_i}\epsilon_i U_i\), with all sign vectors \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}^s\) occurring.
Summing their positive parts therefore gives
\[
     M(A)=\prod_i x_i^{a_i c_i},\qquad
     c_i=\sum_{\sigma\in E_i}\sum_{\epsilon}
         \epsilon_i\,\mathbf1_{\{\sum_{j:\,\sigma\in E_j}\epsilon_jU_j>0\}} .
\]
On fixing the other signs, the two contributions together are the indicator at \(T+U_i\) minus that at \(T-U_i\), where \(T\) is the signed sum from the other marked indices and the indicator tests strict positivity.
Their difference is zero or one, including at ties.
Put \(h_i=|E_i|=[F:F_i]\) and let \(t_i\) count the embeddings marking only \(i\).
On these last embeddings the difference is always one, so
\[
                     2^{s-1}t_i\le c_i\le 2^{s-1}h_i.                 \tag{2}
\]
After a zero exponent is dropped, the same computation applies with the trace and root composita of the surviving sources.
Thus (2) controls arbitrarily late iterates even if they have large measures.

<a id="least"></a>
## A least field absorbs every other factor

Suppose \(F_0\) is contained in every other marked trace field, using \(0\) to denote its index.
At every stage when it is present, (1) gives \(t_0\ge7h_0/8\).
It therefore always survives.
For each other remaining source, \(E_j\subset E_0\) and \(h_j/h_0=\Pr(E_j\mid E_0)\le1/8\).
By (2), \(c_j/c_0\le1/7\), uniformly after any deletions.
Hence the sum of the other log contributions divided by \(U_0\) decreases by a factor of at least seven at each step until it is less than one.
At that stage an embedding marking any index also marks \(0\), and the sign of its sum is the sign chosen at \(0\).
Every other choice then cancels in opposite pairs in the formula for \(c_j\).
The next iterate is a power of \(x_0\), which has just one exterior conjugate and is fixed.

<a id="minimal"></a>
## Two minimal fields persist

Suppose there is no least field.
In the finite inclusion order there are at least two minimal fields.
Let \(F_u,F_v\) be the first two minimal fields in order of nondecreasing degree, breaking ties arbitrarily.
Every field contains a minimal one, so \(n_u\) is the smallest degree of any source.
By (1), \(E_u\) has a positive proportion of embeddings marking only \(u\).
Every source of degree strictly below \(n_v\) contains \(F_u\), since it must contain a minimal field of degree strictly below \(n_v\), and only \(F_u\) can have that property.
All its marks consequently lie in \(E_u\).
The incomparable fields \(F_u,F_v\) give \(\Pr(E_u\mid E_v)\le1/2\), since \(F_uF_v\) properly contains \(F_v\).
The other events of degree at least \(n_v\), apart from \(E_v\), cover at most another \(1/8\) of \(E_v\) by (1).
Thus an embedding marking only \(v\) also exists.
These two exclusive markings persist upon restriction to the compositum of any subfamily containing \(u,v\).
At each such stage there are at least two sources, so (2) multiplies each of the two exponents by at least two.
Induction gives the divergence asserted in the statement.
