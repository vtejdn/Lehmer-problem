# Small branches in positive-constant Pisot inverses and positive traces in the codifferent

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree-preserving criterion for sublinear and bounded branches

Let \(P=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma\) of degree \(d=2m\).
Put \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) and let \(t(S)\) be the least leading coefficient among integer polynomials of degree \(m-1\) that strictly interlace \(S\) and have positive leading coefficient.
This is the least trace of a totally positive element of the codifferent of the order \(\mathbb Z[T]\).
In particular \(1\le t(S)\le m\).

Every positive-constant same-degree Pisot inverse
\[
     q=f(0)\ge2,\qquad zf+f^*=(z+1)P,\qquad f^*(z)=z^d f(1/z)
\]
has \(j\ge t(S)\), where its integer branch is \(j=[z](f/f^*)-q^2+1\).
Along every sequence of Salem numbers \(\sigma\to1\) one can select such monic Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) with exterior roots \(\theta\) and
\[
        q\sim\frac{t(S)}{\log\sigma},\qquad \theta-q\longrightarrow0,
        \qquad t(S)\le j\le(4+o(1))t(S).                              \tag{1}
\]
Thus along the given Salem sequence there exist these near-integer Pisot inverses with \(j=o(d)\) if and only if \(t(S)=o(d)\).
There exist such inverses with bounded \(j\) if and only if \(t(S)\) is bounded.
Monic strict interlacers suffice for \(j\le4\) eventually.

There is also a criterion that gives the exact limiting branch ratio \(2/\ell\) for a fixed prime \(\ell\).
Factor \(\bar S=\prod h^{a_h}\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
For each \(h\ne x+2\) with \(\ell\nmid a_h\), choose a root \(\xi\) in a finite extension and let \(w+w^{-1}=\xi\).
Let \(r_\ell\) be the least common multiple of the multiplicative orders of these \(w\), with value one for an empty list.
It does not depend on the root choices.
If
\[
                  (T-2)\sigma^{r_\ell}\longrightarrow0,              \tag{2}
\]
the given Salem sequence admits same-degree Pisot inverses with \(\theta-q\to0\) and \(j/d\to2/\ell\).
In particular this holds if the trace polynomials are \(\ell\)-th powers modulo \(\ell\).
All sequence assertions here concern any supplied sequence of Salem numbers tending to one.

<a id="interlacer"></a>
## The lower and upper bounds from a trace interlacer

Write \(x_l\) for the roots of \(S\), with \(x_m=T\).
For a strict interlacer \(C\) of positive leading coefficient \(t\), the residues \(c_l=C(x_l)/S'(x_l)\) are positive and sum to \(t\).
Conversely positive residues give such an interlacer.
The choice \(C=S'\) has \(t=m\), and the [trace pairing calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v135.md#minimum) identifies the codifferent with \(S'(T)^{-1}\mathbb Z[T]\).
The [rational trace of an actual inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) gives an integral interlacing numerator of leading coefficient \(j\), proving the lower bound.

Take a minimizing \(C\), write \(a=\log\sigma\), and define \(D_v(z+z^{-1})=z^v+z^{-v}\) for positive integers \(v\).
The [two frequency choices](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v135.md#frequencies) give even \(s=2h\le2\lfloor a^{-1/8}\rfloor\) and an integer \(k\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
   g(x)&=(D_s(x)-2)(D_k(x)-2)/(x-2)\ \in\mathbb Z[x],\\
   c_m g(T)&\sim t/a,\qquad
   \sum_{l<m} c_l(x_l-2)g(x_l)\le(4+o(1))t .
 \end{split}
\]
The first averaging uses the nonnegative Fejér kernel in the even frequencies to make \(\sum_{l<m}c_l(2-D_s(x_l))\le (2+o(1))t\).
The second uses consecutive \(k\)'s over a block of length \(\lceil a^{-1/2}\rceil\).
Across the block \(D_k(T)-2\) varies by a relative \(o(1)\), and the factor \(2-D_s\) bounds the error in replacing \(2-D_k\) by its circle average two.

Take the remainder \(p\equiv gC\bmod S\), \(\deg p<m\), and the monic integer polynomial \(f=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1})\).
Its exact delay-one return follows by reversal, and partial fractions give \(q=1+\sum_l c_lg(x_l)\).
For \(x=2\cos v\), \((2-D_s(x))/(4-x^2)=\sin^2(hv)/\sin^2 v\le h^2\).
Therefore the interior terms are negative with \(-g(x_l)\le4h^2(2+x_l)\), and \(q\sim t/a\).
The [inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) has positive residues \(b_l=(T-x_l)c_l(-g(x_l))\) for \(l<m\), with endpoint sum \(O(h^2t)=o(q)\).
Consequently the [constructed seed](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v135.md#seed) is a Pisot minimal polynomial.
The [root equation](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) bounds \(\theta-q-(T-2)\) between zero and \(O(((T-2)+h^2 t)/(q-2))=o(1)\).
Its branch equals \(\sum_l c_l(x_l-2)g(x_l)\), whose exterior summand is \((T-2)c_mg(T)=o(t)\).
This proves (1) and its consequences.

<a id="prime"></a>
## When division by a prime preserves an integral inverse

Take \(G_k=(x+2)(D_k-2)\).
The remainder of \(G_k S'\) modulo \(S\) is divisible coefficientwise by \(\ell\) exactly when \(E_\ell=\bar S/\gcd(\bar S,\bar S')\) divides \(G_k\bmod\ell\).
Here \(E_\ell=\prod_{\ell\nmid a_h}h\).
For a root of each relevant \(h\ne x+2\) one has \(D_k(\xi)-2=w^{-k}(w^k-1)^2\).
Thus the [exact prime criterion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v131.md#prime) is \(r_\ell\mid k\), since the orders are invariant under Frobenius and inversion.
The factor \(x+2\) divides \(G_k\) already.

With \(u=T-2\), condition (2) lets us choose a multiple \(k\) with \(d=o(X)\) and \(uX=o(d)\), \(X=\sigma^k\).
Specifically take the least multiple with \(X\ge d(u\sigma^{r_\ell})^{-1/2}\).
The overshoot is a factor of at most \(\sigma^{r_\ell}\).
Use \(p=\operatorname{rem}_S(G_k S')/\ell\) in the same inverse formula.
The [divided residue estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v131.md#range) gives \(q\sim4X/\ell\), a strict Pisot seed, \(\theta-q\to0\) and exterior branch mass \(o(d)\).
The [circle residue limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) divides by \(\ell\), giving \(2/\ell\) per degree.
Its degree condition \(\log X=o(d)\) follows from \(X=o(d/u)\) and the [explicit height bound](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) for the Salem input.
