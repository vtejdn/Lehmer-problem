# Two residue classes with a bounded seed measure and a bounded number of exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform convergence for a growing cyclic quotient

Write \(m=\log M\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(z^3-z-1\).
Fix an integer \(K\ge1\) and real numbers \(\Theta>1,\ T>0\).
Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be nonconstant, with \(f(0)=\pm1\), and put
\[
 g(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z),\qquad \gcd(f,g)=1.
\]
Assume that \(M(f)\le\Theta\) and that \(f\) has at most \(K\) roots strictly outside the unit disk, counted with multiplicities.
The leading coefficient of \(f\) need not be a unit.
For positive integers \(d,s\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), define
\[
 P_{d,s,\sigma}(x)=f(x^d)+\sigma x^s g(x^d),\qquad
 D=\frac d{\gcd(d,s)},\qquad S=\frac s{\gcd(d,s)}.
\]
If \(s/d\le T\), then
\[
             m(P_{d,s,\sigma})-m(f)\longrightarrow0
                         \quad(D\longrightarrow\infty) .                 \tag{1}
\]
The convergence is uniform in \(f,d,s,\sigma\) subject to these hypotheses.
There is no bound on \(\deg f\), its individual coefficients, or its number of terms.
More precisely, the difference in (1) is always at most \((\log2)/D\), and for each \(\epsilon>0\) it is at least \(-\epsilon\) once \(D\) exceeds a threshold depending only on \(K,\Theta,T,\epsilon\).
No explicit rate for this threshold is asserted.

Every such nonconstant \(f\) is nonreciprocal, since reciprocity would contradict \(\gcd(f,g)=1\).
The [whole-polynomial form of Smyth's bound](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) gives \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\).
Consequently
\[
          \liminf_{D\to\infty}m(P_{d,s,\sigma})\ge\log\theta               \tag{2}
\]
uniformly under the stated budgets.
For example, all sufficiently large \(D\) in this class satisfy \(m(P_{d,s,\sigma})\ge\frac12\log\theta\).
The bounds concern the whole polynomial; they do not give this lower bound for each of its irreducible factors separately.

<a id="rows"></a>
## The exact reduction from two occupied residue classes

Suppose
\[
 P(x)=U(x^d)+x^rV(x^d),\qquad
 d\ge3,\quad 0<r<d,\quad \gcd(r,d)=1,\quad \gcd(U,V)=1,
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
where both rows are nonzero integral polynomials and \(P(0)\ne0\).
If \(m(P)<\log\theta\), the [reciprocity threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) implies \(P^*=\sigma P\) for a sign \(\sigma\), and its leading and constant coefficients are units.
Let \(N=\deg P\).
Reversal must preserve the set of occupied residues \(\{0,r\}\).
If \(N\equiv0\pmod d\), it would require \(2r\equiv0\pmod d\), contrary to \(d\ge3\) and \(\gcd(r,d)=1\).
Thus \(N=nd+r\), reversal exchanges the two rows, and
\[
       \deg U=a\le n,\qquad V(y)=\sigma y^nU(1/y).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Taking \(f=U,\ g=f^*\), and \(s=d(n-a)+r\), we obtain exactly \(P=P_{d,s,\sigma}\).
Here \(f(0)=\pm1,\ s>0,\ \gcd(s,d)=1\), and \(\gcd(f,g)=1\), since multiplication of \(g\) by \(y^{n-a}\) introduces no common factor with \(f\).
The effective ratio is \(s/d=n-a+r/d\), not just the canonical residue ratio \(r/d\).

If \(f\) is constant, it is a unit and \(P\) is a signed binomial of measure one.
Otherwise (1) applies whenever the three budgets \(M(f)\), the number of exterior roots of \(f\), and \(s/d\) are bounded.
In particular a sequence of positive-measure inputs (3) with \(d\to\infty\) and \(m(P)\to0\) cannot keep all three budgets bounded.
This is a necessary escape condition for a shrinking sequence, not a gap for arbitrary rows in (3).

<a id="norm"></a>
## The norm and its disk normalization

Substitution by a positive power preserves logarithmic Mahler measure.
We may therefore replace \(x^{\gcd(d,s)}\) by \(x\) and work with the relatively prime integers \(D,S\).
For \(y=x^D\), the exact product over \(D\)-th roots of unity is
\[
 \prod_{\zeta^D=1}
       \bigl(f((\zeta x)^D)+\sigma(\zeta x)^Sg((\zeta x)^D)\bigr)
       =f(y)^D-(-\sigma)^D y^Sg(y)^D .
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
This uses that \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^S\) permutes those roots of unity.
All factors on the left have the same Mahler measure.
Writing \(\varepsilon_D=(-\sigma)^D\), we obtain
\[
       D\,m(P)=m\bigl(f^D-\varepsilon_D z^Sg^D\bigr).                  \tag{6}
\]
The polynomial on the right is nonzero, since its value at zero is \(f(0)^D\).

Let \(A\ne0\) be the leading coefficient of \(f\).
The polynomial \(f\) has no unit-circle root: for a real-coefficient polynomial such a root is also a root of its reversal, contradicting the gcd assumption.
Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) be all roots of \(g\) in the open unit disk, including multiplicities.
They are nonzero, \(k\le K\), and
\[
 \prod_{\nu=1}^k|a_\nu|=\frac{|A|}{M(f)} .
\]
Define the finite Blaschke product, with each factor positive at zero, by
\[
 C(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^k
       \frac{|a_\nu|}{a_\nu}\frac{a_\nu-z}{1-\overline{a_\nu}z},
       \qquad B(z)=C(z)\frac{f(z)}{g(z)} .
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
All poles of the quotient in the disk are canceled with their multiplicities.
Both \(B\) and \(C\) are rational functions analytic across the closed disk, with boundary modulus one, so \(|B|,|C|\le1\) in the disk.
In particular,
\[
       C(0)=\frac{|A|}{M(f)}\ge\Theta^{-1},
       \qquad |B(0)|=M(f)^{-1}\ge\Theta^{-1}.                         \tag{8}
\]
Every individual \(a_\nu\) has modulus at least \(\Theta^{-1}\), since its modulus is at least their product.

Put
\[
             F_D(z)=B(z)^D-\varepsilon_D z^S C(z)^D .
\]
On the unit circle \(|F_D|=|f^D-\varepsilon_D z^Sg^D|/|g|^D\), and hence
\[
 m(P)-m(f)=\frac1D\int_{\mathbb T}\log|F_D|\,dm_{\mathbb T},
 \qquad |F_D|\le2.                                                   \tag{9}
\]
Here \(dm_{\mathbb T}=dt/(2\pi)\).
Possible boundary zeros have integrable logarithms, because \(F_D\) is a nonzero rational function analytic across the circle.
The asserted upper bound follows immediately.

<a id="jets"></a>
## A bounded number of poles prevents a constant limiting quotient

Consider any sequence of admissible data with \(D_i\to\infty\).
Passing to a subsequence, take \(k_i=k\le K\), let each \(a_{\nu,i}\) converge in the closed disk, and use normality to arrange
\[
                   C_i\longrightarrow C,\qquad B_i\longrightarrow B
                           \quad\hbox{locally in the disk}.           \tag{10}
\]
A normalized factor in (7) whose zero tends to a boundary point tends locally uniformly to the constant one.
The remaining factors are genuine Blaschke factors.
Thus \(C\) is a finite Blaschke product, possibly constant, and \(C(0)\ge\Theta^{-1}\).
The function \(B\) is a disk map with \(|B(0)|\ge\Theta^{-1}\), so it is not identically zero.
On a fixed neighborhood of zero, \(G_i=f_i/g_i=B_i/C_i\) converges holomorphically to \(G=B/C\).

The integer leading coefficients \(A_i\), bounded in absolute value by \(\Theta\), may be taken constant, as may \(f_i(0)\).
If \(|A_i|>1\), then \(|G(0)|=1/|A_i|<1\).
This already rules out a limiting quotient of constant modulus one.
No integer-Taylor assertion is used in this nonmonic case.

It remains to handle \(A_i=\pm1\).
Only in this case does division by \(g_i(0)=A_i\) give integral Taylor coefficients.
Explicitly, writing \(G_i=\sum_{j\ge0}c_{i,j}z^j\), coefficient comparison gives
\[
 A_i c_{i,j}
       =[z^j]f_i-\sum_{\ell=1}^j [z^\ell]g_i\,c_{i,j-\ell},
                                                                    \tag{11}
\]
so all \(c_{i,j}\) are integers and \(c_{i,0}=c_i=f_i(0)/A_i=\pm1\).
For \(0\le\lambda<1\), Rouché's theorem on the unit circle gives exactly \(k_i\) disk zeros of \(g_i-\lambda c_i f_i\).
Its limit \(g_i-c_i f_i\) is not identically zero, because \(f_i,g_i\) are coprime and \(f_i\) is nonconstant.
The order \(j_i\) of its zero at zero is therefore at most \(k_i\le K\), by persistence of zeros on a sufficiently small circle as \(\lambda\uparrow1\).
Since
\[
               g_i-c_i f_i=-c_i g_i(G_i-c_i),
\]
the first nonconstant Taylor coefficient of \(G_i\) has index \(j_i\le K\).

If the holomorphic limit \(G\) were the constant \(c_i\), its first \(K\) nonconstant Taylor coefficients would all be zero.
Convergence of these coefficients and their integrality would force all the corresponding coefficients of \(G_i\) to be zero for large \(i\), a contradiction.
Thus \(G\) cannot be a constant of modulus one in this case either.
The gcd assumption has two distinct uses here: it excludes unit-circle poles in (7), and it excludes the identity \(g_i=c_i f_i\).

<a id="boundary"></a>
## Recovering the boundary logarithm from compactness in the disk

Pass to a further subsequence with \(S_i/D_i\to t\in[0,T]\).
The functions \(|B(z)|\) and \(|z|^t|C(z)|\) cannot agree on an open subset of the punctured disk.
Indeed their squared moduli are real analytic there, so such equality would extend throughout the punctured disk.
Letting \(z\to0\) and using the nonzero values in (8) forces \(t=0\).
The equality would then make \(B/C\) a constant of modulus one, by the open mapping theorem and removable singularities, contrary to the preceding section.
Consequently the set of strict inequality between these moduli is dense.

Suppose, contrary to the lower bound in (1), that along some such sequence
\[
                     m(P_i)-m(f_i)\le-\epsilon
\]
for a fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
Define finite positive measures
\[
 d\nu_i=\frac{\log2-\log|F_{D_i}(e^{it})|}{D_i}\,dm_{\mathbb T}.
                                                                    \tag{12}
\]
Their masses equal
\[
 \nu_i(\mathbb T)
      =\frac{\log2}{D_i}-m(P_i)+m(f_i)
      \in\left[\epsilon,\ \log\Theta+\frac{\log2}{D_i}\right].
                                                                    \tag{13}
\]
The upper bound uses nonnegativity of integral logarithmic Mahler measure; it also follows from Jensen's formula and \(F_{D_i}(0)=B_i(0)^{D_i}\).
Take a weakly convergent subsequence with limit \(\nu\), whose mass is still at least \(\epsilon\).

For \(0<|z|<1\) with unequal limiting moduli, one term of \(F_{D_i}(z)\) dominates the other by an exponential factor.
The sign \(\varepsilon_{D_i}\) is immaterial, and
\[
 \frac1{D_i}\log|F_{D_i}(z)|
       \longrightarrow
       \log\max\{|B(z)|,\ |z|^t|C(z)|\}.                              \tag{14}
\]
Subharmonicity and the Poisson upper bound give
\[
 \frac1{D_i}\log|F_{D_i}(z)|
       \le\frac{\log2}{D_i}-\int_{\mathbb T}P_z\,d\nu_i .
                                                                    \tag{15}
\]
For instance this follows by applying the Poisson inequality to
\(\log(|F_{D_i}|+\delta)\) and decreasing \(\delta\) to zero.
Passing to the limit at the dense set in (14), then using continuity, yields
\[
           |z|^t|C(z)|
                 \le \exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb T}P_z\,d\nu\right)
                              \qquad(0<|z|<1).                       \tag{16}
\]
For circles \(|z|=\rho\) sufficiently close to one, \(C\) has no zero on the circle.
Since \(C\) is a finite Blaschke product,
\[
             t\log\rho+
             \int_{\mathbb T}\log|C(\rho e^{it})|\,dm_{\mathbb T}
                              \longrightarrow0\quad(\rho\uparrow1).
                                                                    \tag{17}
\]
Taking logarithms in (16) and averaging on that circle makes its right side \(-\nu(\mathbb T)\), because the angular mean of a Poisson kernel is one.
Equations (16)--(17) contradict \(\nu(\mathbb T)\ge\epsilon\).
This proves the uniform lower bound, and hence (1).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Essential boundaries of the argument

The reduced denominator \(D\), rather than the unreduced \(d\), is essential.
For \(f=z^3-z-1\) and \(g=1-z^2-z^3\), one has
\[
                       f+zg=-(z^4+1).
\]
Taking \(s=d\) therefore makes \(P_{d,d,+}=-(x^{4d}+1)\) have measure one for every \(d\), although \(M(f)=\theta\).
The original two-residue hypothesis \(\gcd(r,d)=1\) avoids precisely this fixed-denominator resonance.

The proof does not cover \(s/d\to\infty\).
In that case the second term of (14) tends to zero in the disk, and the conclusion of (15) is only
\[
                      \log|B(z)|\le-\int P_z\,d\nu .
                                                                    \tag{18}
\]
The fact that \(B\) is nonzero does not make its boundary logarithmic mean zero.
This is an actual limitation of the available compactness data, even for integer Pisot unit seeds with one exterior root.
For the [multinacci seeds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v29.md#multinacci), with \(n\ge2\),
\[
 f_n=z^n-z^{n-1}-\cdots-z-1,\qquad
 (1-z)f_n=(2-z)z^n-1,\qquad
 (1-z)g_n=1-2z+z^{n+1},
\]
their Pisot roots tend to two.
With the positive-at-zero normalization (7), their local limits are
\[
                  C(z)=\frac{1-2z}{2-z},\qquad
                  B(z)=-\frac1{2-z}.
                                                                    \tag{19}
\]
The positive measure
\[
            d\nu(e^{it})=\log|2-e^{it}|\,dm_{\mathbb T}
\]
has mass \(\log2\) and satisfies equality in (18), since \(\log|2-z|\) is harmonic in the disk.
Thus (18) is compatible with losing the entire limiting seed measure.
This does not assert that the actual specialized polynomials realize that loss.
The [sparse cyclotomic multiple argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v29.md#uniform-multinacci) supplies additional information for the phase family recorded there; its input is absent from the disk-compactness step alone.

A large modulus \(d\) need not force many exterior roots of the output, and coprimality need not give a seed resultant of absolute value greater than one.
For example, \(x^{d+1}-2x^d-2x+1\) has the coprime two-row representation \(U=1-2y,\ V=y-2,\ r=1\).
On the unit circle \(|x-2|=|1-2x|\), so Rouché applied to \(x^d(x-2)+\lambda(1-2x)\), \(0\le\lambda<1\), and passage to \(\lambda=1\) show that the output has at most one exterior root.
It has a real root \(\beta_d>2\), which is therefore its sole exterior root, and
\(\beta_d^d(\beta_d-2)=2\beta_d-1\) implies \(\beta_d\to2\).
Also, for the plastic seed \(f=z^3-z-1,\ g=f^*\), reduction modulo \(f\) gives \(g=-z(z+1)\).
Since \(\operatorname{Res}(f,z)=\operatorname{Res}(f,z+1)=1\), it follows that \(\operatorname{Res}(f,g)=-1\).
Coprimality of the two rows cannot by itself supply a resultant of absolute value greater than one.

The [free-lattice two-row result](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#two) has unrestricted rows and independent torus variables.
The present theorem instead treats the dependent cyclic specialization, at the explicit seed and offset budgets in (1).
Its proof extends the [single-pole phase argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#uniform-proof) by using a bounded number of poles, a bounded nonzero integral jet, and the continuous limit \(t=S/D\).
It does not resolve the finite-quotient two-row problem with arbitrary seed measure, arbitrary exterior-root count and arbitrary effective offset.

The strategy-discovery contributor derived the cyclic norm, the bounded-pole extension and the seed-budget obstruction.
An ordinary author-side collaborator checked the disk argument and identified the necessary reduced-denominator condition and the one-exterior-root example.
These contributions are part of the author argument, not an independent review.
