# The excess measure of a two-class multiple with unequal row moduli

<a id="statement"></a>
## An integral factor and a positive circle integral

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(h=m(P)\) be its logarithmic Mahler measure, and suppose \(p>2\) is an integer for which powering a root by \(p\) preserves its degree.
Let \(Q\) be the powered minimal polynomial, so \(m(Q)=ph\).
Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y^{\pm1}]\) be nonzero, \(r\in\mathbb Z,\ \gcd(p,r)=1\), and suppose
\[
             G(x)=U(x^p)+x^r V(x^p), \qquad P^t\mid G,\qquad t\ge1 .
\]
Put \(U^\iota(Y)=U(1/Y)\), and assume that \(H=UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) is nonzero.
Multiply \(H\) by the unique Laurent monomial of coefficient one giving a polynomial \(\widetilde H\) with nonzero constant term.
Then \(\widetilde H=Q^t K\) for a nonzero integral polynomial \(K\).
On the unit circle let \(\rho=\min(|U|,|V|)/\max(|U|,|V|)\) away from the finitely many common zeros, and set
\[
                 L=\int_{\mathbb T}-\log(1-\rho^2)\,d\mu ,
\]
with Haar probability \(\mu\).
The integral is finite and strictly positive.
The complete excess satisfies
\[
   \left(\tfrac12-\tfrac1p\right)L
       \ \le\ m(G)-\tfrac12\{t p h+m(K)\}
       \ \le\ \left(\tfrac12+\tfrac1p\right)L .                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular one has \(m(G)>t p h/2+m(K)/2\) at each individual \(p>2\), with no degree error or coprimality assumption on the rows.
Under primitivity the noncyclotomic part of \(G/P^t\) has logarithmic measure \(m(G)-th\).

<a id="identity"></a>
## Reflection retains the multiplicity and controls the error

Since \(P\) is reciprocal, \(P^t\) divides \(x^rG(1/x)=V^\iota(x^p)+x^r U^\iota(x^p)\) in the Laurent ring as well.
Eliminating the second class from these two multiples gives \(H(x^p)\), using the [two-graph determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#determinant) with the reflected graph.
All powered conjugates are distinct, and \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified at the nonzero roots of \(P\).
Thus \(Q^t\mid\widetilde H\), with integral quotient by monic division.
In particular \(m(H)=tph+m(K)\).

Write \(I=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\,d\mu\).
Since \(H\ne0\), equality of the two moduli holds only at finitely many points.
The identity \(|H|=\max(|U|,|V|)^2(1-\rho^2)\) almost everywhere gives \(L=2I-m(H)\).
All polynomial logarithms here are integrable, as is \(\log\max(|U|,|V|)\) by comparison with one of the two rows below and a constant above.
Both rows are nonzero almost everywhere, so \(0<\rho<1\) almost everywhere and \(L>0\).

The [circle product over \(x^p=Y\)](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) has modulus \(|U(Y)^p-(-1)^pY^r V(Y)^p|\), since \(\gcd(p,r)=1\).
After division by \(\max(|U|,|V|)^p\), the modulus lies between \(1-\rho^p\) and \(1+\rho^p\).
The absolute value of its logarithm is therefore at most \(-\log(1-\rho^p)\le-\log(1-\rho^2)\).
Averaging logarithms over the fibers gives \(|p(m(G)-I)|\le L\).
Substitution of \(I=(tph+m(K)+L)/2\) proves (1).

In particular, along any sequence with \(p\to\infty\), at fixed multiplicity \(t\), the convergence \(m(G)/(ph)\to t/2\) is equivalent to **both** \(m(K)/(ph)\to0\) and \(L/(ph)\to0\).
There is no cancellation between these two conditions because \(m(K)\ge0\).

<a id="four"></a>
## The degree-six-times-the-seed residual in the four-class construction

Take either of the [four-class witnesses](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows) for a representative \(P_0(x^n)\), where \(e=\deg P_0,\ d=ne,\ h=m(P_0)\).
Here \(u\) is the inverse of \(p\) modulo \(n\) in \([1,n-1]\), \(pu=1+vn\) and \(1\le v<p\).
The lifted row and the seed diagonal are
\[
 A(Y)=a(Y^n)+Y^u b(Y^n),\qquad
 G(z)=a(z^p)+\sigma z^{pB-v} b(z^{-p}),\qquad B=4e ,
\]
where \(\deg a=B,\ a(0)\ne0,\ \deg b<B\).
The lifted diagonal is \(F(x)=G(x^n)+\sigma x^{npB+1}G(x^{-n})\).
Use \(U=a\), \(V=\sigma Y^B b(1/Y)\), \(r=-v\) in (1), now with input \(P_0\) and powered minimal polynomial \(Q_0\).
The real moduli on the circle are \(|a|\) and \(|b|\) since coefficients are real.
The extreme terms of \(aa^\iota-bb^\iota\) come from \(a\), so (1) applies and
\[
   T^B\{a(T)a(1/T)-b(T)b(1/T)\}=Q_0(T)^2 K(T),\qquad \deg K=6e .     \tag{2}
\]
The constant term of \(K\) has absolute value \(|a(0)\operatorname{lc}(a)|\).
Both sides of (2) are reciprocal after including \(K\), which is itself reciprocal by monic division and the reciprocity of \(Q_0\).

There is also a direct interpretation of (2) in the [normalized determinant of all rotations](Yin_C_G0345_v404.md#determinant).
Use the lifted rows \(A,\sigma A^*\) and offset one.
For rotation \(x\mapsto\zeta^j x\) their row vector over \(Y=x^p\) is the sum of the two vectors
\[
 (a(T),\,\sigma Y^{nB-u} b(1/T))
       +\zeta^j(Y^u b(T),\,\sigma T^B a(1/T)),\qquad T=Y^n .
\]
This uses \(\zeta^{pu}=\zeta\).
Every pair determinant divided by \(\zeta^j-\zeta^i\) therefore equals \(\sigma Q_0(T)^2K(T)\).
For \(N=n(n-1)/2\) the full determinant quotient after removing the powered minimum to multiplicity \(2N\) is \(\sigma^N K^N\) in the seed variable.
Thus its measure divided by \(N\) is precisely \(m(K)\).

For the noncyclotomic cofactors \(R_G\) of \(G/P_0^2\) and \(R_F\) of \(F/P_0(x^n)^2\), put \(L=\int-\log(1-\rho^2)\,d\mu\) using \(a,b\).
Both witnesses are primitive.
Formula (1) and the [two-sided seed measure estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v232.md#measure) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 m(R_G)&=(p-2)h+\tfrac12\{m(K)+L\}+\epsilon,\qquad |\epsilon|\le L/p,\\
 m(R_F)&=m(R_G)+O(ph/n).
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
The second error is uniform over the short witnesses of the cited construction, using their \(m(G)=O(ph)\) and \(\log(2npB)=o(ph)\).
In particular each true seed cofactor has measure strictly greater than \((p-2)h\), and its lifted counterpart is at least \((p-2)h-O(ph/n)\).
All dependence on the number of rotations in their difference is paid explicitly.
To obtain a lower bound independent of \(h\) on the scale \(m(R_F)/p\) along a hypothetical shrinking sequence, one would need a lower bound of order \(p\) from \(m(K)+L\).
This decomposition identifies those two quantities and leaves such an absolute lower bound open.
