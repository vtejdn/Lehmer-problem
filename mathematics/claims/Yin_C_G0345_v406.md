# One prescribed prime per seed degree suffices for a finite integer test of the Lehmer gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## Integral rows at a prime fixed by the degree

For each even \(e\ge4\) take the least prime \(p=p_e>16e^4\) and put \(B=4e\).
For a monic reciprocal irreducible polynomial \(P_0\) of degree \(e\) with nontorsion unit root \(\alpha\), set \(K^+=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) and \(z=\alpha^p\).
Consider a pair of integral coefficient lists \((a_i,b_i)\), \(i=1,2\), subject to the following conditions.

* Each \(a_i\) has degree \(B\), with leading and constant coefficients one, and \(0\le\deg b_i<B\).
  The polynomials \(a_i(Y)\) and \(Y^{B-1}b_i(1/Y)\) are coprime.
* For \(f_i=Y^{-2e}a_i,\ g_i=Y^{-2e}b_i,\ \mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\), write \(u_i=f_i(z),\ \ell_i=(\mathcal E f_i)(z)\).
  Both belong to \(K^+\), with \(u_i\ne0,\ \ell_i/u_i\notin\mathbb Q\), and
  \[
        (g_i(z),(\mathcal E g_i)(z))
                 =\alpha^{-1}(-u_i,\ell_i+u_i/p).
  \]
  The two columns satisfy \(u_1\ell_2-u_2\ell_1\ne0\).
* For \(i=1,2\), the integer polynomial
  \[
            G_i(x)=a_i(x^p)+x^{pB-1}b_i(x^{-p})
  \]
  is divisible by \(P_0^2\).

Let \(H(P_0)\) be the least possible maximum of the absolute coefficients of the four polynomials in such a pair, and set \(H(P_0)=+\infty\) for an empty set.
Writing \(h=m(P_0)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, one always has
\[
            \log H(P_0)\ \ge\ p_e h-\log(4e+1).                        \tag{1}
\]
There is an absolute \(C\) such that for all sufficiently large \(e\) and **all** such \(P_0\) with \(h\le1\),
\[
            \log H(P_0)\ \le\ C p_e h.                                 \tag{2}
\]
The two lists supplying (2) also lift to short signed four-class witnesses with coprime rows at a substitution prime \(n_e\) determined by \(e\) alone.
The sign is fixed to plus in the test; the construction also permits a prescribed minus sign with the corresponding signed equation.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## The same substitution prime works for the entire degree

Set \(X=\exp(0.9p^{1/4})/(3e)\).
For all sufficiently large \(e\) take \(n_e\) to be the least prime in \((X,2X]\) congruent to \(-1\bmod p\).
In the [Siegel–Walfisz estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz), the difference at \(X,2X\) has main term at least \(X/((p-1)\log(2X))\) and error \(O(X/(\log X)^8)\).
Since \(p>16e^4\), one has \(\log X=0.9p^{1/4}-\log(3e)\asymp p^{1/4}\) with absolute constants as \(e\to\infty\).
The main term dominates, giving existence independently of the seed.
For \(d=en_e\) one has \(e\le\log(3d)\), \(n_e>p\), and \((\log(3d))^4/p\to0.9^4\in(1/2,1)\).
Thus this particular \(p\) lies in the fourth-logarithmic window of \(d\), with offset \(v=1\).

If \(\alpha=\beta^{n_e}\) for some \(\beta\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), then \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(\beta\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(e\).
Absolute logarithmic heights scale by \(n_e\), so \(h=n_e m(\beta)>2n_e/(\log(3e))^3\) by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
This contradicts \(h\le1\) for all sufficiently large \(e\).
The [prime substitution argument](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers) therefore makes \(P_0(x^{n_e})\) irreducible uniformly over these seeds.
The same argument preserves the powered degrees since \(p>e(e-1)+1\), \(p<n_e\) and the two primes are distinct.
For (1) at any \(e\ge4\) only seed degree preservation is needed, and that already follows from \(p>e(e-1)+1\).

The [uniform four-class construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows) applies to every sufficiently large such representative and gives \(\log\max_i\|A_i\|_1=O(ph)\) with an absolute constant.
The [two unit endpoint corrections](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#endpoints) preserve the original jets exactly, and their norm estimate uses \(\log\|Q_0\|_1\le ph+e\log2\) and \(O(d)\) excluded scalar choices.
Here \(Q_0\) is the minimum of \(z\).
Both \(e\) and \(\log(2dp)\) are \(o(ph)\) uniformly, either by the [representative bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) or by Voutier at degree \(e\).
The correction hence keeps the same absolute \(O(ph)\) coefficient bound even as the seed varies.
The [seed equations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets) at \(v=1\) give exactly the specified value, derivative and independence conditions, and [grouping the two lifted residue classes](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed) gives \(P_0^2\mid G_i\).
This proves (2) with the claimed simultaneous lift.

<a id="integer"></a>
## The unrestricted gap as finite exclusions in each degree

For an individual admissible row put \(H_*=\max_j(|[Y^j]a|,|[Y^j]b|)\), taking missing coefficients to be zero.
The [reflected elimination](Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) gives \(Q_0^2\mid Y^B(aa^\iota-bb^\iota)\), where \(\iota\) inverts \(Y\).
The reflected polynomial is nonzero because \(a\) has two unit endpoints and \(b\) has smaller degree.
It has integral quotient, and \(m(Q_0)=ph\).
Hence \(2ph\le 2\int\log\max(|a|,|b|)\le2\log((B+1)H_*)\), using the pointwise circle bound \(\big||a|^2-|b|^2\big|\le\max(|a|,|b|)^2\).
This proves (1) on each row before minimizing.

The unrestricted Lehmer gap is equivalent to the following assertion entirely in integer polynomial arithmetic.
There exist integers \(k\ge1\) and \(e_0\) such that, for every even \(e\ge e_0\), **no** pair in the box of coefficient magnitudes at most \(2^{\lfloor p_e/k\rfloor}\) passes the stated test for any \(P_0\).
For fixed \(e,k\) this is a finite decision with no list of candidate seeds required.
Enumerate the four coefficient lists in the box, form the two nonzero \(G_i\), factor \(\gcd(G_1,G_1')\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and check the monic reciprocal nontorsion factors of degree \(e\) against every condition, including divisibility of both \(G_i\) by their squares.
Field computations use \(\mathbb Q[x]/(P_0)\), where membership in \(K^+\) is invariance under \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\).
All these operations are exact.

A uniform positive gap implies the exclusions by (1), since \(\log(4e+1)/p_e\to0\) and \(k\) may be taken sufficiently large.
If the gap fails, the [reciprocal unit reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) gives seeds \(P_0\) of unbounded even degrees with \(0<h\to0\).
For every fixed \(k\), (2) then puts witnesses inside the box in arbitrarily large degrees.
This proves the equivalence.
The construction of \(n_e\) is used only in its proof and never occurs in the finite test.
No single numerical cutoff in \(e\) proves the required exclusion for all subsequent degrees.
