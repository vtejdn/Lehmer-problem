# Quotient frequencies and the location of repeated roots in the trace construction

<a id="frequencies"></a>
## Fourier tests for the full degree and period

Use the [strengthened zero-boundary rows](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#statement) at fixed \(p=3^\nu,\ q=p-1,\ h\), and retain their notation \(L=(p+3)/2,\ u=2^h,\ N=A+2u,\ N/d\to\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)\).
Write \(d=2D\).
Their [endpoint congruence](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#degrees) implies \((1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^2\), so \(d\equiv4\bmod p\).
In particular \(3\nmid d\).
Let \(\Omega=\{\pm 3^k i\bmod N:k\ge0,\ 1\le i\le L\}\).

If a full degree-\(d\) reciprocal word divides \(X^N-1\) with the specified zero prefix modulo \(p\), every set \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\) whose distinct pairwise differences lie in \(\Omega\) has size at most \(d\).
More precisely, let \(R\mid N,\ w=N/R>1\).
If \(\Omega\) contains every nonzero residue in \(R\mathbb Z/N\), each fiber of \(x\mapsto x^R\) on \(\mu_N\) contains the same number \(d/w\) of selected roots.
In particular \(w\mid d\).
On all sufficiently large strengthened rows, coverage of this subgroup already rules out the full word.

To prove the assertions, lift the selected roots to the prime-to-three torsion in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_3\).
For their set \(S\), put \(s_i=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha^i\).
Newton's identities and the zero prefix give \(s_i\equiv0\bmod 3^\nu\) for \(1\le i\le L\).
Inversion and Frobenius permute \(S\), so this congruence holds throughout \(\Omega\). [Lifted power sums](Yin_C_G0901_v201.md#boundary)
Modulo three, the two matrices with entries \(\alpha^\lambda\) and \(\alpha^{-\lambda}\), indexed by \(S\times\Lambda\), have transposed product \((s_{\lambda-\lambda'})_{\lambda,\lambda'\in\Lambda}=dI\) up to ordering of the indices.
Its rank is \(|\Lambda|\), giving the first bound.

For \(b\in\mu_w\) let \(c_b\) count the chosen points above \(b\).
The moments \(\sum_b c_b b^j=s_{Rj}\) vanish modulo \(3^\nu\) for every \(1\le j<w\) under the subgroup hypothesis.
Fourier inversion, with \(w\) a unit, gives \(wc_b\equiv d\bmod 3^\nu\) for every \(b\).
The frequency \(R\) itself must come from some \(i\le L\) divisible by \(R\), since \(3\) is prime to \(N\).
Hence \(0\le c_b\le R\le L<p\).
All these integer counts are equal, proving the assertion about fibers.
Finally \(\theta>L\), by \(\log(p/q)\le 1/q\) and the assumed large \(p\).
Thus \(w=N/R\ge N/L>d\) on sufficiently large rows.
Equivalently, a row with a full word must leave an undetected frequency in every such nontrivial subgroup.
These are necessary tests on the specified row and make no assumption that such coverage occurs infinitely often.

## A nearly quadratic delay with bounded multiplicities in each fixed finite field

For every function \(\omega(d)\to\infty\) there are actual strengthened rows and full degree one-denominator words on them with the following properties.
A zero-prefix divisor \(U\) of degree \(d-\Delta\) has exact order \(N\), where \(\Delta=o(d)\).
Every root of the word modulo three has order dividing \(N\), and the full finite-ring order is \(N3^b\) with
\[
                  d/\omega(d)\le 3^b=o(\Delta).
\]
In every fixed finite extension of \(\mathbb F_3\) the total multiplicity of the residue roots is bounded independently of the row.
After taking a subsequence every individual root of finite degree has an eventually constant multiplicity.
The largest multiplicity is attained on roots of a single prime order tending to infinity.
In particular the excess \(3\)-power can exceed \(d/\omega(d)\) even while each fixed finite field has bounded multiplicities and the fraction returning at \(N\) tends to one.

Fix \(Q=3^v\) with \(v\ge\nu+\lfloor\log_3 L\rfloor\).
Use the [successive synchronization of prime divisors](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion) to keep a fixed prime \(H_0>L\) and successively include primes \(H_1<H_2<\cdots\) larger than \(H_0\) in \(N\).
Put \(D_j=Q(H_j-1)\) and \(B=2H_0Q\).
At stage \(i\), choose \(0<\epsilon_i<1/8\) tending to zero and take sufficiently many further prime divisors on the progression that the [large-order, zero-prefix packets](Yin_C_G0901_v205.md#large-roots) each have degree less than \(\epsilon_i d\) on arbitrarily large rows.
Previously required divisors stay present when the progression is refined.
Use the original degree rounding at every stage.
Start with the required zero-prefix primitive packet, and add packets in their given order up to the budget \((1-2\epsilon_i)d\).
There are more than enough available degrees by the packet construction and \(N/d\to\theta>2\).
Consequently its squarefree product \(U\) has exact order \(N\) and
\[
                      2\epsilon_i d\le\Delta=d-\deg U<3\epsilon_i d .
\]
Every residue root in \(U\) has order at least \(N/(8\tau(N))\), where \(\tau\) counts divisors.
Choose the row large enough at each stage that these orders exceed \(H_i\).

Beginning with \(\Delta-B\), divide successively by \(D_i,D_{i-1},\ldots,D_1\), using the preceding remainder as the new dividend and denoting the quotients by \(a_i,a_{i-1},\ldots,a_1\).
The remaining degree \(r=\Delta-\sum_{j=1}^i a_jD_j\) is even and belongs to \([B,B+D_1)\).
Write \(r/2=H_0x+Qy\) with \(x\ge0,\ 0\le y<H_0\), by choosing \(y\) modulo \(H_0\).
Multiply \(U\) by the integral polynomial
\[
       (X^{H_0}-1)^{2x}(X-1)^{2Qy}
                       \prod_{j=1}^i\Phi_{H_j}(X)^{Qa_j}.
\]
The [prime-power prefix calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v205.md#filler) shows that this reciprocal filler has constant term one and zero coefficients through \(L\) modulo \(p\).
The resulting word has degree \(d\) and lifts by the [original pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) with the required even-side and real conditions.

The total multiplicity at orders dividing \(H_0\) is less than \(B+D_1\).
For fixed \(j<i\) one has \(a_j<D_{j+1}/D_j\), since the dividend at that step is less than \(D_{j+1}\).
The only other filler roots have exact orders \(H_1,\ldots,H_i\).
Thus the contribution from each fixed finite field is uniformly bounded, whereas eventually the roots of \(U\) all lie outside it.
The residue algebraic closure is countable, giving the stabilization assertion by diagonal subsequence selection.

At fixed stage take \(d\) arbitrarily large.
The largest multiplicity is then \(e_i=Qa_i\) at every primitive \(H_i\)-th root, since the multiplicities from earlier levels are bounded by the displayed stage constants.
We may ensure \(\Delta/(2H_i)\le e_i\le 2\Delta/H_i\) by the formula for the first quotient.
The whole residue word divides \((X^N-1)^{e_i}\), and order in the full coefficient ring is a multiple of \(N\) because \(U\) has exact order \(N\).
Each cyclotomic power divides \((X^N-1)^{e_i}\) also over the full ring.
For the factor at orders dividing \(H_0\) use \(X-1\mid X^{H_0}-1\) and \(2x+2Qy\le e_i\).
All these factors and \(U\), grouped by their disjoint residue root sets, are pairwise comaximal there, so their product divides \((X^N-1)^{e_i}\) over the full ring.
The [truncated binomial estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#order) gives full order \(N3^b\) with \(e_i\le3^b\le3^\nu e_i\).
The lower bound follows already from the multiplicity \(e_i\) in characteristic three.
As \(H_i\to\infty\), this proves \(3^b=o(\Delta)\).
Taking \(d\) sufficiently large that \(\omega(d)>2H_i/\epsilon_i\) gives the asserted lower bound as well.
These selected words still leave open whether infinitely many of the original rows admit a different full-degree word of exact order \(N\).
