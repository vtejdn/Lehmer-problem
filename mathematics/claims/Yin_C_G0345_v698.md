# A uniform Mahler measure gap for all prime-power radicals of the integer Salem families

<a id="statement"></a>
## The prime-power gap and its consequence for arbitrary factors

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Let \(r\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\), write \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), and put \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
For a prime \(p\) and integer \(e\ge1\), suppose \(\beta=r^{1/p^e}\in K\), taking the positive real root.
With \(c_0=0.427800\) and \(c_3=\log(\sqrt{10}/2)\), one has
\[
             \log\beta\ \ge\
             \begin{cases}
                 c_0/2,&p=2,\\
                 c_3/3,&p=3,\\
                 c_0\,d/(n+1),&p\ge5 .
             \end{cases}                                                \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\log M(\beta)=\log\beta\ge\delta=c_3/3>0\), uniformly in the seed, delay, sign, prime and exponent.
For \(p\ge11\) let \(j_p\) be the least nonnegative integer with \(1+2j_p(j_p+1)>p\).
Either \(\beta\) is the exterior root of the same polynomial family at an integer seed \(b\ge2\) with \(b\equiv a\bmod p\), or
\[
                    \frac{(n+1)\log\beta}{d}\ \ge\ \log\frac{p}{4j_p}.
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]

Let \(R\) be the largest positive integer \(q\) with \(r^{1/q}\in K\) and \(B=\lfloor\log r/\delta\rfloor\).
Then
\[
                 R\mid\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B).
\]
In particular each prime power dividing \(R\) is at most \(\log r/\delta\).
For every integer \(h\ge1\), each noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(F\) of \(G_a(X^h)\) satisfies
\[
           \log M(F)\ \ge\ \frac{\log r}{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)}
                        \ \ge\ (\log r)\,r^{-\log(4)/\delta}.            \tag{3}
\]
More strongly, writing \(X=\log r\), one has the uniform estimates as \(a\to\infty\)
\[
       \log R\ll \frac{X}{\log X},\qquad
       \log M(F)\ge X\exp\!\left(-C\frac{X}{\log X}\right)              \tag{4}
\]
with an effective absolute constant \(C\), without any restriction on the growth of \(n\) or \(h\).
For varying fields and arbitrary radicals \(r^{1/q}\in K\), convergence of \(\log(r^{1/q})\) to zero requires \(q/p^{v_p(q)}\to\infty\) uniformly over primes \(p\mid q\) on the terms with \(q>1\).
The absolute gap in (1) concerns prime powers.

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## The same reduction at a prime across its full tower

Write \(L=n-1\), let \(U\) be the product of \(X-c\) over \(c=\pm1\) with \(c^L=\epsilon\), and let \(P=G_a/U\).
By the [root and cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots), \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of \(r\), \(a-1<r<a+1\), and \(d=n+1-\deg U\ge n-1\).
The binomials \(X^{n+1}-\epsilon\) and \(X^n-\epsilon X\) are both divisible by \(U\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
Thus every integral specialization \(G_c/U\) is itself a monic integral polynomial of degree \(d\).

Since \(\beta\in K\) and \(\beta^{p^e}=r\), \(\beta\) generates \(K\) and the \(p^e\)-th powers of its distinct conjugates give those of \(r\) bijectively.
It is a Salem unit by the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Let \(S\) be its monic integral minimal polynomial.
Raising all roots of a monic integral polynomial to their \(p^e\)-th powers preserves its coefficients modulo \(p\), since over the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_p\), Frobenius preserves their multiset, including multiplicities.
Equivalently, reduce the elementary symmetric functions in the powered roots at a prime above \(p\) and use that each original coefficient is in \(\mathbb F_p\).
It follows that \(S\equiv P\pmod p\).
In particular \(P(\beta)\in p\mathcal O_K\), and, for \(c\equiv a\bmod p\), also \((G_c/U)(\beta)\in p\mathcal O_K\).

<a id="linear"></a>
## A small integral combination for the primes at least five

Put
\[
     D_{u,v}(X)=u(X^{n+1}-\epsilon)-v(X^n-\epsilon X).
\]
Whenever \(v\equiv au\bmod p\), \(D_{u,v}(\beta)\in p\mathcal O_K\) since \(D_{u,v}\equiv uG_a\bmod p\).
Its maximum modulus on the unit circle is at most \(2(|u|+|v|)\).
For every \(p\ge5\) under consideration there are integers \(u\ne0,v\) satisfying
\[
                          v\equiv au\bmod p,\qquad 2(|u|+|v|)<p .
\]

For \(p\ge11\), take \(j=\lfloor(p-1)/4\rfloor\ge2\).
The \(1+2j(j+1)>4j+3\ge p\) integer pairs \((x,y)\) with \(|x|+|y|\le j\) give two with the same residue \(y-ax\bmod p\).
The same pigeonhole argument therefore works with \(j_p\le j\).
The nonzero difference \((u,v)\) in this smaller diamond has \(|u|+|v|\le2j_p\).
If \(u=0\), then \(p\mid v\) and \(|v|<p\), contradicting nonzero difference.
For \(p=7\), use \((1,0),(1,\pm1),(1,\pm2)\) for residues \(0,\pm1,\pm2\), and \((2,\mp1)\) for \(a\equiv\pm3\).
For \(p=5<n\), the [delayed trace congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#traces) gives \(5\mid a(a-1)(a+1)\) for a field radical.
Thus \(a\bmod5\) has a representative \(v\in\{0,\pm1\}\), and \(u=1\) works.

The polynomial \(D_{u,v}\) has degree \(n+1\) and nonzero constant term.
If \(D_{u,v}(\beta)\ne0\), the [reciprocal-word height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v697.md#statement) at this Salem input, with modulus \(p\) strictly larger than the circle maximum, gives
\[
                            (n+1)\log\beta/d\ \ge c_0.
\]
Only the congruence in the ring of integers is needed.
In this nonvanishing case the absolute norm is at least \(p^d\).
At \(p\ge11\) the coefficient sum of \(D_{u,v}\) is at most \(4j_p\).
Multiplying the elementary bounds \(4j_p\max(1,|z|)^{n+1}\) over the conjugates of \(\beta\) therefore gives \(p^d\le(4j_p)^d\beta^{n+1}\), proving (2).

If \(D_{u,v}(\beta)=0\), put \(b=v/u\).
Then \(D_{u,v}/(uU)=G_b/U\) is monic of degree \(d\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and vanishes at \(\beta\), because \(U(\beta)\ne0\).
It must equal \(S\).
Consequently \(G_b=US\) has integral coefficients and comparison at \(X^n\) gives \(b\in\mathbb Z\).
The root equation gives
\[
       b=\frac{\beta^{n+1}-\epsilon}{\beta^n-\epsilon\beta}>1,
\]
since the denominator is positive and the numerator minus the denominator is \((\beta-1)(\beta^n+\epsilon)>0\).
Thus \(b\ge2\), and \(p\nmid u\) in all the choices above, whence \(b\equiv a\bmod p\).
At \(x=5/3\), \(G_b(x)\le x^n(x-b)+bx-1\le(7-x^n)/3<0\), where the middle expression is decreasing with \(b\).
There is a real root above \(5/3\), and it must be \(\beta\) since \(G_b=US\) and \(\beta\) is the unique exterior root of \(S\).
Hence \(\log\beta>\log(5/3)>c_0\ge c_0d/(n+1)\), proving (1) also in the exact-zero case.

<a id="small"></a>
## Scalar powers at two and three

Now let \(p=2\) or \(3\), with \(c\in\{0,\pm1\}\) a representative of \(a\) modulo \(p\).
There is a positive integer \(M\) with \(\beta^M\equiv\pm1\bmod p\mathcal O_K\) as follows.
If \(c=0\), use \(G_0=X^{n+1}-\epsilon\) and \(M=n+1\).
If \(c=\pm1\), then
\[
                          G_c=(X-c)(X^n+\epsilon c).
\]
When \(c^L=\epsilon\), the factor \(X-c\) occurs in \(U\), so \(G_c/U\) divides \(X^n+\epsilon c\) integrally.
This gives \(M=n\).
When \(c^L=-\epsilon\), \(X-c\) divides \(X^n+\epsilon c\), so \(G_c/U\) divides \((X^n+\epsilon c)^2\).
Modulo \(p\) it therefore divides \((X^n+\epsilon c)^p=X^{np}+(\epsilon c)^p\).
This gives \(M=np\).
In this last case \(d\ge n\), because \(\deg U=2\) requires \(L\) even and \(\epsilon=1\), when \(c^L=-\epsilon\) is impossible.

The [integral scalar height estimates](Yin_C_G0246_v1.md#gim%2B10-arbitrary-prime-scalar-transfer) give \(h(\beta^M)\ge c_0\) at \(p=2\) and \(h(\beta^M)\ge c_3\) at \(p=3\).
These estimates use absolute logarithmic heights and apply because \(\beta^M\) is a nontorsion unit with nonzero residue.
Now \(h(\beta^M)=M\log\beta/d\).
For \(M=n+1\) or \(n\) we have \(d/M\ge(n-1)/(n+1)\ge3/4\).
For \(M=np\) we have \(d/M\ge1/p\).
These estimates prove the first two cases of (1).
The least of the three uniform constants in (1) is \(c_3/3\), since \(c_3=\tfrac12\log(5/2)<1/2\) and \(c_0>1/3\).

<a id="all"></a>
## A logarithmic cutoff on the prime powers in the radical index

Every prime power \(p^e\mid R\) gives an element \(r^{1/p^e}=(r^{1/R})^{R/p^e}\in K\).
By (1) this forces \(p^e\le\log r/\delta\), hence the asserted divisibility of \(R\).
Likewise for any \(q>1\) with \(r^{1/q}\in K\) one has
\[
                    \log(r^{1/q})\ge\delta\,p^{v_p(q)}/q
                    \qquad(p\mid q).
\]
This proves the necessary growth of the complementary factors.
By the [factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement), each logarithmic Mahler measure of an irreducible factor of \(P(X^h)\) is at least \(\log r/R\).
Substitution in \(U\) contributes only cyclotomic factors.

Finally \(\ell_j=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,j)\le4^j\) for \(j\ge1\).
Indeed for \(j\ge2\) put \(u=\lceil j/2\rceil,\ v=\lfloor j/2\rfloor\).
Each prime occurs at most once in the quotient \(\ell_j/\ell_u\), and for each such prime there is a power in \((u,j]\).
The factorial valuation formula then gives a positive valuation in \(\binom jv\) at that prime, using that power and the nonnegative floor differences at every other power.
Thus \(\ell_j\mid\ell_u\binom jv\).
The binomial is at most \(2^j\) for even \(j\) and at most \(2^{j-1}\) for odd \(j\), since the two central binomials are then equal.
Induction gives \(\ell_j\le4^j\), proving (3).

<a id="growing"></a>
## Counting the large prime divisors without counting delays

For \(X=\log r\) sufficiently large, put \(T=16X/\log X\).
The prime powers \(p^{v_p(R)}\) with \(v_p(R)\ge2\) have \(p\le\sqrt{X/\delta}\), and together contribute at most \(\sqrt{X/\delta}\log(X/\delta)\) to \(\log R\).
The primes dividing \(R\) to the first power and not exceeding \(T\) have product dividing \(\ell_{\lfloor T\rfloor}\), so their log contribution is at most \(T\log4\).

For each remaining \(p>T\), \(\beta_p=r^{1/p}\) satisfies \(\log\beta_p<(\log X)/16\).
Here \(p>\sqrt X\) for large \(X\), and \(j_p\le\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\).
Consequently \(\log(p/(4j_p))\ge\tfrac12\log p-\log8\ge\tfrac14\log X-\log8\).
As \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\), (2) is incompatible with the upper bound for \(\log\beta_p\) once \(X\) exceeds an absolute effective constant.
Each such \(\beta_p\) is therefore the exterior Salem root at an integer seed \(b_p\ge2\) for the same \(n,\epsilon\).
For \(b_p\ge3\) the original root interval gives \(b_p<\beta_p+1\), and this inequality also holds for \(b_p=2\) since \(\beta_p>1\).
In particular \(b_p\le X^{1/16}+1\).
The map \(p\mapsto b_p\) is injective because the positive numbers \(r^{1/p}\) for different \(p\) are distinct, while in every exact case the factorization \(G_{b_p}=US_{\beta_p}\) has a unique exterior root.
There are thus at most \(X^{1/16}+1\) remaining primes.
Each contributes at most \(\log(X/\delta)\), since \(p\le X/\delta\).
Both \(\sqrt{X/\delta}\log(X/\delta)\) and \((X^{1/16}+1)\log(X/\delta)\) are \(O(X/\log X)\).
Summing the three contributions and applying the factor formula proves (4), uniformly in all the asserted parameters.
