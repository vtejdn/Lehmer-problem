# A uniform excess gap for quadratic Schur descendants

<a id="statement"></a>
## The complete quadratic model and its degree restriction

Let \(f\) be a monic Pisot minimal polynomial of even degree \(d\ge4\), positive constant \(q\ge2\), and exterior root \(\theta\).
Use the [integer Schur correspondence](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#correspondence) to put
\[
 N=q^2-1,\qquad A=f^*-qf,\qquad
 L=\operatorname{lc}(A)=N+j,\qquad
 B=\frac{LA+NA^*}{j(2N+j)z},\qquad \ell=\frac{d-2}{2}.
\]
The reversals of \(f,A\) use degrees \(d,d-1\), respectively.
Suppose that the monic strictly stable descendant \(B\) is even, so
\[
                         B(z)=D(z^2)
\]
for a monic rational polynomial \(D\) of degree \(\ell\), all of whose roots are strictly inside the unit disk.
If \(0<j<q-1\), then
\[
                   \left(\frac{2q}{q+2}\right)^\ell<j<q.              \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(q=d^2\) and \(d\ge20\), this model cannot have \(j<q-1\).
Writing \(\delta=\theta-q\), every such quadratic-descendant input at \(q=d^2\) satisfies
\[
 \delta>\frac1{327},
 \qquad
 \delta>\frac{q-1}{q+2+q^{-1}}\quad(d\ge20).                           \tag{2}
\]
Thus its excess has lower limit at least one along any such sequence with \(d\to\infty\).

The exact coefficient problem behind (1) is also retained below.
For fixed \(q,d,j\) with \(0<j<q-1\), the model is equivalent to an integer strictly stable polynomial \(V(w)\) of degree \(\ell\), leading coefficient \(S\in\{q,q+1\}\), constant \(qS-L\), satisfying
\[
               (q^2-w)V^*+q(w-1)V\equiv0\pmod L                       \tag{3}
\]
coefficientwise.
The reversal of \(V\) uses degree \(\ell\).
The proof uses the full congruence (3), not just its scalar consequences.

Together with the [power-substitution exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#substitution), the uniform bound \(\delta>1/327\) and the limiting bound \(\liminf_{d\to\infty}\delta\ge1\) also hold at \(q=d^2\) for every descendant \(z^tD(z^k)\) with \(k\ge2\), \(D\) monic of positive degree and \(D(0)\ne0\), and for the pure monomial descendant.
Thus a vanishing-excess sequence at this prescribed constant must eventually have at least two support exponents in \(B\), with greatest common divisor of their differences equal to one.
It does not establish a uniform Mahler gap for the Salem outputs of arbitrary inputs in this model.

<a id="coefficient-model"></a>
## Two half-degree polynomials with opposite root conditions

The inverse Schur identity gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 f(z)&=U(z^2)-zV(z^2),\\
 U(w)&=wD(w)+qD^*(w),\\
 V(w)&=\frac LN\bigl(qD(w)+D^*(w)\bigr),                              \tag{4}
 \end{split}
\]
where the reversal of \(D\) uses degree \(\ell\).
The parity classes are disjoint, so \(f\) is integral exactly when \(U,V\) are integral.
Rouché's theorem makes \(V\) strictly stable.
It also shows that \(U\) has no roots in the closed unit disk: on the circle \(q|D^*|>|wD|\), and \(D^*\) has no interior zeros.
The polynomial \(U\) is monic of degree \(\ell+1\) and constant \(q\).

For an integral instance, let \(S=\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
Since \(|D(0)|<1\),
\[
 \frac LN(q-1)<S=\frac LN(q+D(0))<\frac LN(q+1).
\]
The restriction \(0<j<q-1\) places this open interval inside \((q-1,q+2)\).
Therefore
\[
 S\in\{q,q+1\},\qquad V(0)=qS-L,\qquad
 D(0)=
 \begin{cases}
 -qj/L,&S=q,\\
 (N-qj)/L,&S=q+1.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{5}
\]
Both displayed constants of \(D\) are nonzero in this range.

Conversely, take an integer strictly stable \(V\) with the degree, leading coefficient and constant specified in the statement, and set
\[
 D=\frac{qV-V^*}{L},\qquad
 U=\frac{(q^2-w)V^*+q(w-1)V}{L}.                                    \tag{6}
\]
The leading coefficient of \(qV-V^*\) is \(qS-(qS-L)=L\).
Rouché therefore makes \(D\) monic and strictly stable.
Reversal verifies (4), and (3) is exactly the remaining integrality condition.
The polynomial \(A=LzD(z^2)-ND^*(z^2)\) is strictly stable by \(L>N\), and \(A(1)=jD(1)>0\).
The strict Schur correspondence consequently gives the required integral Pisot minimal polynomial, of exactly degree \(d\) and constant \(q\).

For completeness, if \(c_D\) is the least common denominator of the coefficients of \(D\), the disjoint parity classes of \(A\) show
\[
                         c_D\mid\gcd(N,j).
\]
Using the integrality of \(ND(0)\) in (5) gives the necessary scalar conditions
\[
 L\mid qj^2\quad(S=q),\qquad
 L\mid(q+1)j^2\quad(S=q+1).                                        \tag{7}
\]
They do not suffice for (3) and stability.

<a id="content"></a>
## Forced content and one integral Schur step

Put \(F=(q^2-w)V^*+q(w-1)V\), and reverse \(F\) in formal degree \(\ell+1\).
A direct calculation gives
\[
 q(1-w)F-(q^2-w)F^*=-(q^2-1)^2wV=-N^2wV.
\]
Thus (3) forces every coefficient of \(V\) to be divisible by
\[
 h=\frac{L}{\gcd(L,N^2)},\qquad
 M=\frac Lh=\gcd(L,N^2).
\]
Write \(V=hW\), and define
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 a=\operatorname{lc}(W)=S/h,\qquad b=W(0)=(qS-L)/h,\\
 g=\gcd(M,N)=\gcd(L,N)=\gcd(N,j),\qquad
 m=M/g,\qquad n=N/g.
 \end{gathered}                                                     \tag{8}
\]
The polynomial \(W\) is integral and strictly stable, with \(a>0\) and \(|b|<a\); it need not be primitive.
The full remaining congruence is
\[
                     (q^2-w)W^*+q(w-1)W\equiv0\pmod M.              \tag{9}
\]
In particular \(h\mid S\), equivalently \(L\mid S N^2\); since \(N^2\equiv j^2\pmod L\), this recovers (7).

One has \(\gcd(n,m)=1\).
Also \(M\mid N^2\) gives \(m\mid g\), by cancelling the coprime factor \(n^2\) from \(gm\mid g^2n^2\).
Since \(g\mid j\),
\[
              m\le g\le j,\qquad M=gm\le j^2,\qquad h\ge2.           \tag{10}
\]
For the last assertion, \(L=q^2-1+j\ge q^2>j^2\), while \(h=L/M\).

Reducing (9) modulo \(g\), where \(q^2\equiv1\), gives
\[
                   (w-1)(qW-W^*)\equiv0\pmod g.
\]
Coefficient comparison cancels the monic linear factor over \(\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\), giving
\[
                         W^*\equiv qW\pmod g.                       \tag{11}
\]
Consequently
\[
 c=\frac{a^2-b^2}{g}\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
 Y=\frac{aW-bW^*}{gw}\in\mathbb Z[w].                               \tag{12}
\]
Indeed \(b\equiv qa\pmod g\), and the numerator of \(Y\) has zero constant and coefficients divisible by \(g\).
Rouché shows that \(Y\) is strictly stable of degree \(\ell-1\), with leading coefficient \(c\).
For \(\ell=1\), it is the positive constant \(c\).

<a id="stable-resultant"></a>
## An upper bound for the stable resultant

For a real polynomial \(P\) of degree \(r\) and leading coefficient \(a>0\), define
\[
 \mathcal R_r(P)=\operatorname{Res}_{r,r}(P,P^*),
 \qquad P^*(w)=w^rP(1/w).
\]
Both formal degrees in this Sylvester resultant are \(r\), even if \(P(0)=0\) makes the actual degree of \(P^*\) smaller.
Equivalently, if \(\beta_i\) are the roots of \(P\) with multiplicity,
\[
                    \mathcal R_r(P)=a^r\prod_iP^*(\beta_i).
\]
If \(P\) is strictly stable and \(b=P(0)\), then
\[
                  0<\mathcal R_r(P)\le(a^2-b^2)^r.                  \tag{13}
\]

Here is an elementary proof that also handles zero constant coefficients during Schur reduction.
First normalize \(P\) to be monic, put \(\tau=P(0)\), and define
\[
 Z=\frac{P-\tau P^*}{(1-\tau^2)w}.
\]
Then \(Z\) is monic and strictly stable of degree \(r-1\), and
\(P=wZ+\tau Z^*\).
For \(\tau\ne0\), evaluation at the roots of \(Z\) gives
\(\operatorname{Res}(P,Z)=\tau^{r-1}\mathcal R_{r-1}(Z)\).
Evaluation of \(P-\tau P^*=(1-\tau^2)wZ\) at the roots of \(P\) gives
\[
 \tau^{r-1}\mathcal R_r(P)
                  =(1-\tau^2)^r\operatorname{Res}(P,Z).
\]
For \(\tau=0\), the identities \(P=wZ\), \(P^*=Z^*\) give the same resulting recurrence directly:
\[
                  \mathcal R_r(P)
                      =(1-\tau^2)^r\mathcal R_{r-1}(Z).
\]
Starting from \(\mathcal R_0(1)=1\), induction proves positivity, \(\mathcal R_{r-1}(Z)\le1\), and the required upper bound for monic \(P\).
Scaling by \(a\) multiplies the formal resultant by \(a^{2r}\), proving (13).

<a id="divisibility"></a>
## The full congruence supplies almost a full additional power

Define the integral polynomial
\[
                         R=\frac{W^*-qW}{g}.
\]
Its degree is \(\ell\) and its leading coefficient is
\((b-qa)/g=-M/g=-m\).
Substitution into (9) yields
\[
                       (q^2-w)R+qnW\equiv0\pmod m.                  \tag{14}
\]
Here \(\gcd(qn,m)=1\), since \(m\mid N\), \(\gcd(q,N)=1\), and \(\gcd(n,m)=1\).
Evaluate at \(w=q^2\), and put
\[
                  Q(w)=\frac{W(w)-W(q^2)}{w-q^2}\in\mathbb Z[w].
\]
Then \(W(q^2)\equiv0\pmod m\), and cancellation of the monic factor \(w-q^2\) in (14) gives
\[
                W\equiv(w-q^2)Q,\qquad R\equiv qnQ\pmod m.           \tag{15}
\]
The polynomial \(Q\) has degree \(\ell-1\).

Take the \(2\ell\)-by-\(2\ell\) Sylvester matrix of the degree-\(\ell\) pair \((W,R)\).
By (15), it is \(S_0+mS_1\) with integral matrices, where \(S_0\) is the formal-degree Sylvester matrix of \(((w-q^2)Q,qnQ)\).
Every column of \(S_0\), interpreted as a coefficient vector, represents a multiple of \(Q\) of degree at most \(2\ell-1\).
That rational vector space has dimension \(\ell+1\), so \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}S_0\le\ell+1\).
In the column expansion of \(\det(S_0+mS_1)\), a nonzero term must therefore use at least \(\ell-1\) columns from \(mS_1\).
This proves
\[
                  m^{\ell-1}\mid\operatorname{Res}_{\ell,\ell}(W,R).
\]
No coefficient of \(W\) needs to be a unit modulo \(m\).
The formal resultant is unchanged on replacing \(W^*\) by \(W^*-qW=gR\); thus
\[
                   g^\ell m^{\ell-1}\mid\mathcal R_\ell(W).          \tag{16}
\]
Strict stability makes this integer positive and nonzero by (13).
Combining (13), (16) and (12) gives the decisive inequality
\[
                         m^{\ell-1}\le c^\ell.                      \tag{17}
\]

<a id="degree-exclusion"></a>
## Both leading-coefficient branches have the same degree obstruction

In the two cases in (5), respectively,
\[
 \frac cm=\frac{a^2-b^2}{M}
       =\frac{S^2-(qS-L)^2}{hL}
       =
 \begin{cases}
 \bigl(q^2-(j-1)^2\bigr)/(hL),&S=q,\\
 j\bigl(2(q+1)-j\bigr)/(hL),&S=q+1.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{18}
\]
In the first case the numerator is at most \(L\), since their difference is \(j(1-j)\).
Hence \(c/m\le1/h\le1/2\).
In the second case \(j\le q-2\) gives numerator at most \(q^2+2q-8<q^2+2q\), while \(h\ge2\) and \(L\ge q^2\).
Both cases consequently satisfy
\[
                  \frac cm<\rho_q:=\frac12+\frac1q<1.                \tag{19}
\]
The range of \(j\) is nonempty only for \(q\ge3\), as used in the last inequality.
From (17), (19) and \(m\le j\) one obtains
\[
                \rho_q^{-\ell}<m\le j<q,
\]
which proves (1).

Now take \(q=d^2\) and even \(d\ge20\).
Then \(\rho_q<0.51\), whereas (1) would require \(q\rho_q^\ell>1\).
But
\[
                400(0.51)^9<1,
\]
and the upper comparison \(d^2(0.51)^{(d-2)/2}\) decreases at each even step \(d\mapsto d+2\):
its ratio is at most \((22/20)^2(0.51)<1\).
This is a contradiction.
Thus every quadratic-descendant input in these degrees has \(j\ge q-1\).

The [exact branch bound](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#branch) gives
\(\delta>j/(q+2+q^{-1})\), proving the second inequality in (2).
For the remaining even degrees \(4\le d\le18\), one has \(q=d^2\le324\) and \(j\ge1\), so
\[
                 \delta>\frac1{q+2+q^{-1}}>\frac1{327}.
\]
The large-degree bound is also greater than \(1/327\), completing (2).

For the power-substitution consequence, first suppose \(j<N\).
The [earlier restriction](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#substitution) forces \(t=0,k=2\), so the quadratic bound applies.
The pure monomial \(B=z^{d-2}\) is excluded in this range by the [zero-multiplicity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#descendant).
If \(j\ge N\) instead, the branch inequality gives
\[
                      \delta>\frac{N}{q+2+q^{-1}},
\]
which is greater than \(1/327\) at \(q=d^2\ge16\) and tends to infinity with \(d\).
This proves both asserted bounds for all these descendants.
Any polynomial with at least two support exponents whose differences have greatest common divisor \(k\ge2\) can be written \(z^tD(z^k)\), with \(t\) the least support exponent and \(D(0)\ne0\).
This gives the support consequence.

<a id="scalar-example"></a>
## Why scalar feasibility did not suffice

The developing coefficient problem had the scalar instance
\[
 d=34,\quad q=1156,\quad N=1336335,\quad j=715,\quad
 L=1337050,\quad S=q.
\]
It satisfies \(\gcd(N,j)=715\), \(D(0)=-34/55\), and \(qj^2=442L\).
Here \(h=34\), so an actual model would require a strictly stable integer \(W\) of degree \(16\), leading coefficient \(34\), constant \(-21\), satisfying (9) modulo \(M=39325\).
However \(g=715\), \(m=55\), and \(c=(34^2-21^2)/715=1\), contradicting (17).
This was a scalar admissible candidate, not a Pisot or Salem example.
The full coefficient congruence together with stability excludes it exactly.

<a id="scope"></a>
## What the obstruction settles

The deterministic constant \(q=d^2\) is available for supplied small Salem inputs by the [uniform inverse theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v292.md#prescribed).
The present theorem excludes using an even Schur descendant to obtain vanishing Pisot excess at that scale.
It also closes the quadratic case left by the higher-power restriction, as stated above.
It leaves general descendants with no such power substitution, including irreducible stable coefficient models, unresolved.

The inequality from the positive return is \(E=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2<\delta\).
A lower bound for \(\delta\) therefore does not itself give a lower bound for \(E\) for every possible output of this model.
No uniform Mahler gap for all Salem numbers or all outputs of this restricted construction is asserted.
The coordinator developed the half-degree coefficient correspondence, Agent-S developed the resultant comparison, and the coordinator checked its two-branch divisibility and quantitative exclusion.
These are collaborating author calculations, separate from the independent finding adopted in the selected assessment below.
