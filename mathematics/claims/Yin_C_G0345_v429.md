# Degree at least 68 at measure 1.091 and signed tests through 241

<a id="statement"></a>
## The degree threshold and the first remaining primes

Every nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) satisfying \(1<M(P)\le1091/1000\) has degree at least 68.
For an algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of measure in this interval, \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\ge68\) for every integer \(k\ge1\).
In the [original fixed-budget count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) at either sign, every prime \(p\le241\) has at most one distinct positive qualifying value.
At \(p=251\) the only possible ordered degree pairs for two such values, with smaller measure first, are \((68,56),(68,58),(70,56)\).
At every prime in this count the smaller-measure factor of any two-value row must have degree at least 68.

For the particular pair of minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\), where \(\delta\) is a reciprocal nontorsion unit whose positive powers all generate its field, simultaneous genuine double contact is impossible on a passing row of either sign at any prime through 271 with width \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\).
At the next prime 277 such a pair requires \([\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]=68\).
These finite restrictions do not establish the infinite exclusion in the [square-pair criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests).

<a id="degree"></a>
## The six-degree census and descent into each power field

Set \(T=1091/1000\).
By multiplicativity, an integer polynomial with measure in \((1,T]\) has an irreducible factor in that interval.
Its leading and constant coefficients have absolute value one by the root product, with the factor \(X\) having measure one.
After normalization it is reciprocal by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
An antireciprocal polynomial has the root one, and an odd-degree reciprocal polynomial has the root minus one.
The [published degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives degree at least 56, since \(T\) lies strictly below Lehmer's measure.
A power substitution in any remaining degree below 68 would preserve the measure in degree at most 33, contradicting that theorem.
The substitutions and sign symmetry can therefore be removed before enumerating.

The [rational ellipse certificates](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v7/readme.md#weights) and their [lower-degree specialization](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v10/readme.md#boxes) bound the first half of every integral Newton sequence in these six degrees.
The certified maxima remain valid for the smaller degrees after multiplying the unit-radius maximum by the actual half-degree minus one.
The signed auxiliary factors cannot vanish since the relative norm argument below already gives power-field degree at least 56 using the published theorem.
The [enumeration in degrees 56 to 62](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v10/readme.md#census) has respectively 256,394,919, 642,515,687, 1,619,542,805 and 10,687,611,707 representatives.
After reflected traces, nonzero endpoints, three cyclotomic divisors and modular Graeffe tests, exactly 2,869, 3,895, 5,343 and 10,761 remain.
Every one has an exact cyclotomic divisor.
The [degree-64 enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v8/readme.md#census) has 20,557,031,095 representatives and all 12,184 final candidates have a cyclotomic divisor.
The [degree-66 enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v9/readme.md#census) has 51,979,784,484 representatives.
Of its 19,799 final candidates, exact cyclotomic division excludes 19,794, and the coefficient of \(X^7\) in the eighth exact Graeffe iterate excludes the other five.
These searches exhaust 85,742,880,697 representatives and rule out every remaining degree below 68.

For \(L=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(e=[K:L]\), put \(u=N_{K/L}(\alpha)\).
Then \(u^k=\alpha^{ke}\), so its absolute logarithmic height is \(e h(\alpha)>0\).
Consequently \(1<M(u)\le M(\alpha)\) by \([\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\le[L:\mathbb Q]\).
Applying the proved threshold to the minimum of \(u\) gives \([L:\mathbb Q]\ge68\).

## Degree sums at the support prime

The [earlier finite theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v426.md#primes) excludes the two-value rows through 233.
At 239 and 241 the existing [residue and archimedean estimates](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#primes) put both factors strictly below Lehmer's measure and bound the total degree by 121 and 122, respectively.
The strict derivative budget at every prime of the original count gives \(m(P_1)+m(P_2)<B=(\log2)/4\), where \(m=\log M\).
Thus the smaller multiplicative measure is below \(2^{1/8}<T\) and its degree at least 68.
The companion has degree at least 56 at both specified primes, so the least total degree 124 excludes them.

For \(p=251,257,263,269,271,277\), the widths are \(126,129,132,134,135,138\), respectively, by \((b-1)^8<p^7<b^8\).
At 251 consider any two distinct values in the original count.
At the six indicated primes one may also take the prescribed pair from the statement in the fixed-double test, with the same width and budget.
In each case it suffices to require exactly two orders at each root when deriving necessary conditions.
After removing scalar content the row stays primitive and still passes.
The [derivative estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) gives the total degree \(D=d_1+d_2\le2b\) and the strict inequality
\[
        m(P_1)+m(P_2)<B-\frac1p\log\frac{2pb+1}{pb+1}.
\]
For the original count each constituent already has degree at least eight by the [small-budget reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#frontier).
For the prescribed pair both degrees agree, so each degree is at most \(b\).
The maximum individual residue multiplicity \(e_\xi\) is therefore less than \(p-1\), using \(2b-8<p-1\) at 251.
Let \(E(\xi)\) be the multiplicity of the product of the two distinct minima in each residue disk.
Since \(D\le276,\ b\le138,\ H<2^{35}\) and \(p>2^7\), the [local resultant estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#energy) gives
\[
                      \sum_\xi E(\xi)^2
             <(2\cdot276\cdot35+277\cdot138/2)/7<75^2.
\]
Thus \(E(\xi)\le74\) and \(2E(\xi)-1<p-e_\xi\), since \(e_\xi\le E(\xi)\) and \(p\ge251\).
The [local Taylor divisibility](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#digits) forces the reduced product to divide the nonzero primitive row, proving \(D\le b\).

At 251 both factors have measure below Lehmer's.
Otherwise the other has log measure less than \(0.69315/4-0.162356-0.6926/251<1/108\), by \((2pb+1)/(pb+1)>1.999\) and the [verified logarithm bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite).
This contradicts [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) because each degree is at most 126 and \(3\cdot126<e^6\).
The smaller-measure degree is at least 68, the companion degree at least 56, and their even degrees sum to at most 126, giving exactly the stated alternatives.

For the prescribed pair write \(h=m(\delta)>0\).
Since squaring preserves the degree, both degrees equal \(d\) and the measures have logarithms \(h,2h\).
The budget gives \(3h<B\), so \(M(\delta)<2^{1/12}<T\) and \(d\ge68\).
The inequality \(2d\le b\) excludes this pair up to 271 among the six new primes, and at 277 forces \(d=68\) by parity.
At primes through 241 the prescribed pair gives two different qualifying measures in the original count because the required order there is two throughout this range.
This proves the finite square-pair exclusion.
