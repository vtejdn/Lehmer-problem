## 1. Exact degree-one and degree-two classification

A chronological state has exactly one legal successor if and only if |q_+|>b0.
Outside the central strip this is [L319](Yin_C_G0601_v1.md#actual-forced-boundary-segments).
Inside it, the zero digit and a digit opposite to the sign of q_+ are legal; all original digits preserve the stable strip.
Equality |q_+|=b0 is absent from D, since it would identify q with +/-B/beta and give stable magnitude beta S>S.
A forced digit has sign opposite to q_+, and its successor lies in(B-1,B) or(-B,1-B), respectively.
Thus the sign persists while the motion is forced.

Suppose a state has exactly two successors.
Their digits are adjacent, since if both-1 and+1 are legal then0 is legal too, so the successor states differ by1.
They cannot both belong to the one-successor region: either same-sign forced interval has length B-b0=beta^-1<1, whereas opposite-sign forced states have separation greater than2b0>1.
Here b0>1/2 follows from beta<18/10 and beta(beta-1)<2.
At least one successor therefore has at least two successors of its own.
The two one-step successor sets are disjoint, since a common next state would make beta an integer digit difference.
A two-way branching followed by one additional step thus produces at least three distinct two-step words and endpoints.

If a four-step path branches within its first three steps, that argument or an immediate three-way branch gives at least three prefix words.
Each prefix extends inside D to four steps, since every core state has a successor.
The completed words have distinct endpoints by the degree-four basis in(2).
A vertex c can therefore have four-step degree at most2 only if its first three steps are forced.
Conversely, three positive forced steps give q_(3,+)=B-beta^3(B-c_+)>B-1.
A fourth digit+1 is impossible because beta q_(3,+)+1>beta(B-1)+1>B.
Only-1 and possibly0 remain, and the negative case is symmetric.
Hence the exact classification is

    deg(c)<=2  iff  |c_+|>B-beta^-3,
    deg(c)=1   iff  |c_+|>B-beta^-4.                    (3)

The threshold equalities are absent from D: q=+/-(B-beta^-j) has stable magnitude S+beta^j>S.
When deg(c)=2 its two terminal neighbors differ by1.
Its chronological four-word choices are exactly(-1,-1,-1,-1),(-1,-1,-1,0), or their global sign reversals.
When deg(c)=1 only the all-equal word occurs.

Applying J and time reversal gives

    deg(y)<=2  iff  |y_-|>S-beta^-2,
    deg(y)=1   iff  |y_-|>S-beta^-3.                    (4)

When deg(y)=2 its two predecessor vertices differ by beta^-1, up to sign: their J-images differ by1, and J(1)=beta^-1.
Their chronological words have their LAST three digits forced and equal, with the remaining digit equal to them or zero.
These statements retain every legal middle word and every original tail realization.

## 2. Low-degree paths and short leaf connections are impossible

Suppose a degree2 vertex y had both predecessor vertices c,c' of degree at most2.
Their expanding values differ by beta^-1, while(3) puts both in

    (-B,-B+beta^-3) union(B-beta^-3,B).                 (5)

Two points in the same fringe differ by less than beta^-3<beta^-1.
Opposite fringes have separation greater than2(B-beta^-3)>beta^-1: B>1 and beta>5/3 give a left side exceeding2[1-(3/5)^3]>1, whereas beta^-1<1.
This is impossible.
Reciprocal reversal proves the assertion with the two sides exchanged.
Consequently

    the graph induced by vertices of degree<=2
    is a disjoint union of single vertices and edges.  (6)

Every non-isolated component contains a vertex of degree at least3.
There is no other component that is a finite path or a cycle, nor an infinite component all of whose degrees are at most2.
Every low-degree vertex in a non-isolated component is at distance at most2 from a degree-at-least3 vertex.
This excludes indefinite concatenation of only deterministic and binary endpoint pieces, but not finite components with genuine branching.

There is also a restriction on leaves even when intervening vertices have larger degrees.
No vertex has two distinct leaf neighbors.
For outgoing leaves c, an all-minus word puts y_+ in(B-1,B), while an all-plus word puts it in(-B,1-B); these intervals are disjoint.
For a fixed word and y there is only one c.
The other side follows through J.

Two leaves cannot be joined by a three-edge path either.
Write such a path c0--y0--c1--y1, with c0 and y1 leaves, and use sign reversal to make c0's word all-minus.
Put T=1+beta+beta^2+beta^3.
Then y0=beta^4 c0-T and y0_+>B-1.
The distinct middle edge c1--y0 has word-T+e, with e!=0 having coefficients in{0,1,2}, and

    c1=c0-beta^-4 e.                                  (7)

Since y1 is a leaf, its unique incoming word is all-minus or all-plus.
In the first case y1=y0-e, so the actual two strips force

    0<e_+<2B, 0<beta^4 e_-<2S.                        (8)

But beta^2>2B because beta^2(beta-1)>(17/10)^2(7/10)>2, and hence beta^3>2S.
A nonzero coefficient of e in degree2 or3 violates the first bound in(8); one in degree0 or1 violates the second.
Thus(8) is impossible.
In the all-plus case y1=y0+(2T-e).
This polynomial has nonnegative coefficients and is nonzero because y1!=y0, so its expanding value is at least1.
Then y1_+>B, also impossible.

The only adjacent leaves form an isolated edge, and every other leaf-to-leaf path has length at least4.
This is a local exclusion, not a finite-component classification.
Both real strips are essential: smallness of a nonnegative polynomial at just one real place would not give(8).
