# Finiteness near measure one for full interlacing inverse images of irrational traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## An absolute gap with arbitrary source conjugates

Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be monic and irreducible of degree \(m\ge2\).
For any [integral interlacing rational map](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#statement) write
\[
 g(x)=A(x)/B(x)=x/k+c-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}w_i/(x-b_i),
 \qquad b_1<\cdots<b_{s-1},\quad w_i>0,
\]
where \(A,B\) are coprime, \(A\) is monic of degree \(s\ge1\), and \(B\) has degree \(s-1\) with positive integer leading coefficient \(k\).
Exclude the integral translations \(g(x)=x+c\).
Use all fibers and their multiplicities in the monic integer polynomial
\[
 P_g(z)=z^{sm}B(z+z^{-1})^m S(g(z+z^{-1})).
\]
There are absolute constants \(\epsilon>0\) and \(d_0<\infty\) such that \(\log M(P_g)\le\epsilon\) implies \(2sm\le d_0\).
Consequently only finitely many such polynomials have measure at most \(e^\epsilon\), all positive logarithmic measures have an absolute lower bound, and only finitely many full outputs are products of cyclotomic polynomials.
There is no restriction on the size, signature or degree of the original algebraic integer or on the locations of the poles.
The statement covers reciprocal inputs by taking the original trace minimal polynomial for \(S\).

The proof uses the [positive integral moments for a fixed real source](Yin_C_G0345_v121.md#statement) developed by agent-S for Salem inputs.
The main researcher supplies control of nonreal fibers and finiteness of the possible source polynomials up to integer translation.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Returning a complex fiber to the real axis

Write \(L=\log M(P_g)\).
For every root \(y\) of \(S\), write \(u_j\) for the roots with multiplicity of the monic \(A-yB\).
For each \(u_j\) choose one root \(z_j\) of \(z+z^{-1}=u_j\) of modulus at least one and put \(\ell_j=\log|z_j|\).
The complete lift gives \(\sum_{y,j}\ell_j=L\).
In particular every \(|\Re u_j|\le2\cosh L\), and in each fiber \(\sum_j|\Im u_j|\le2\sum_j\sinh\ell_j\le2\sinh L\).
The coefficient of \(x^{s-1}\) in \(A-yB\) gives \(k|\Im y|=|\sum_j\Im u_j|\le2\sinh L\).

Take \(t=2\cosh L+2\sinh L+\delta\) with any \(\delta>0\).
For real \(x\ge t\), each factor \(x-u_j\) has positive real part, and the sum of the absolute values of its arguments over the fiber is at most
\[
 \frac{\sum_j|\Im u_j|}{t-2\cosh L}<1 .
\]
Consequently \(A(x)-(\Re y)B(x)=\Re\prod_j(x-u_j)>0\).
For \(x\le-t\), use \(u_j-x\) to get the strict sign \((-1)^s\).
The entire real fiber of \(\Re y\) thus lies in \((-t,t)\).
It has one simple root in each pole interval and on each outer branch, since \(g'>0\) and the one-sided limits run through the real axis.
All poles belong to the same interval and \(g(-t)<\Re y<g(t)\).

Putting \(D=\max\Re y-\min\Re y\), \(W=\sum_i w_i\), gives
\[
 D<g(t)-g(-t)
   =2t/k-\sum_i\frac{2t w_i}{t^2-b_i^2}\le 2t/k-2W/t.       \tag{1}
\]
For \(k=1\) the denominator is monic and the Laurent coefficients of \(A/B\) at infinity are integers.
In particular \(c,W\) are integers, and \(W\ge1\) when \(s\ge2\).

<a id="sources"></a>
## A thin rectangle bounds the degree of the original trace

Consider any sequence with \(L\to0\), allowing \(L=0\), and take \(\delta\downarrow0\), so \(t\to2\).
If \(k\ge2\), (1) gives \(D<t\).
If \(k=1\) the exclusion of translations forces \(s\ge2\), and \(D<2t-2/t\).
In either case \(D\le13/4\) eventually, while every \(|\Im y|\le2\sinh L\to0\).
Let \(c_0\) be the midpoint of the real parts of the conjugates, put \(v=y-c_0\), and take \(q=13/16\).
On \([-2q,2q]\) the monic polynomial \(C(v)=v^4-4q^2v^2+2q^4\) has absolute value at most \(2q^4<1\), by evaluating at \(v=2q\cos\theta\).
By uniform continuity there is a fixed \(\rho<1\) with \(|C(v)|\le\rho\) at every translated conjugate for every sufficiently late input.
We can also take \(|v|\le2\).

In the Vandermonde determinant at the \(m\) conjugates use the monic column polynomials \(C(y-c_0)^j(y-c_0)^h\) for degrees \(4j+h\), \(0\le h<4\).
The triangular change of basis preserves the determinant, including for nonintegral \(c_0\).
The square of its absolute value is the absolute discriminant of \(S\), a positive integer.
Hadamard's bound gives
\[
 1\le|\det|\le (8\sqrt m)^m\,\rho^{\sum_{e=0}^{m-1}\lfloor e/4\rfloor}.
\]
The exponent is \(m^2/8+O(m)\), so \(m\) is absolutely bounded.
After shifting \(y\) by an integer nearest to \(c_0\), all conjugates are uniformly bounded, so the monic translated polynomials form a finite set by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
The identity \(S(g) = S_N(g-N)\), where \(S_N(Y)=S(Y+N)\), preserves the full inverse and the interlacing hypotheses for every integer \(N\).

Thus on a subsequence one translated source is fixed.
All its roots are real since their imaginary parts tend to zero.
Its degree is at least two, so its span \(D\) strictly exceeds two by the [integer norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v120.md#span).
Inequality (1) then forces \(k=1\) eventually.
It also forces \(W=1\), since \(W\ge2\) would give \(D<2t-4/t\to2\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Positive integer moments bound the number of poles

Suppose that the degrees \(2sm\) were unbounded along a sequence with \(L\to0\).
Pass to a subsequence with \(s\to\infty\) and the fixed real source above.
The pole measures \(\mu_n=\sum_i w_i\delta_{b_i}\) have mass one and support in \((-t_n,t_n)\) with \(t_n\to2\).
After passage to a subsequence they converge weakly to a probability measure \(\mu\) on \([-2,2]\).
For fixed \(T>2\) and large \(n\) the kernel \(2T/(T^2-x^2)\) is smaller than the corresponding kernel at \(t_n\) on the support.
Inequality (1), weak convergence on an interval strictly inside \((-T,T)\), and then monotone convergence as \(T\downarrow2\) give
\[
 \int\frac{4}{4-x^2}\,d\mu(x)\le4-D<2.                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular the endpoints have measure zero.

The Laurent expansion of \(x+c-g(x)\) shows that every polynomial moment of \(\mu_n\) against an integer polynomial is an integer.
Write \(V_j\) for the monic integer polynomial defined by \(V_j(2\cos\theta)=\sin((j+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\), obtained from \(V_0=1,V_1=x,V_{j+1}=xV_j-V_{j-1}\).
For fixed \(j\) there are eventually more than \(j\) distinct poles, so \(\int V_j^2\,d\mu_n\) is a positive integer.
Its limit \(\int V_j^2\,d\mu\) is at least one.
For \(0<\theta<\pi\), the Cesàro mean of \(\sin^2((j+1)\theta)\) tends to \(1/2\), by summing a geometric series.
By (2) the integrable majorant \(1/\sin^2\theta=4/(4-x^2)\) permits passage to the integral, giving
\[
 1\le\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int V_j^2\,d\mu
   =\tfrac12\int\frac4{4-x^2}\,d\mu<1 .
\]
This contradiction excludes unbounded degrees even if the full measures are identically one.
If the stated uniform degree cap failed, one could choose \(L\le1/n\) and \(2sm>n\) for every \(n\).
The degree cap and the elementary coefficient bounds for monic polynomials of bounded measure now give finiteness and the gap.

<a id="exceptions"></a>
## The affine and rational-source exceptions

At \(g=x+c\) every monic irreducible integer trace polynomial \(H\) occurs as a full inverse trace, by taking \(S(Y)=H(Y-c)\).
For a rational source, every monic squarefree integer polynomial \(H\) of degree \(s\ge2\) with all roots real already occurs at \(S(Y)=Y\).
Indeed let \(A=H,\ B=H'\), with \(k=s\).
The poles of \(H/H'\) are the simple critical points between the roots of \(H\).
At each critical point \(b\), differentiating \(H'/H=\sum_\alpha1/(x-\alpha)\) shows that \(H''(b)/H(b)=-\sum_\alpha(b-\alpha)^{-2}<0\).
Thus all residues are negative, the map has the required form, and the full inverse trace is \(H\).
In particular the rational-source cases already include the original Salem question.
