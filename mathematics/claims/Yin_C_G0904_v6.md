# Cyclotomic cofactors and boundary orbits needed for a signed modular return

<a id="deficit"></a>
## A lower bound for the degree the cofactor must supply

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit polynomial of degree \(d\) with \(M(P)\le 19/16\), which includes [strict degree minima from degree ten on](Yin_C_G0904_v5.md#record).
Let \(N\ge1\) with \(d\le2N\), \(s=\pm1\), and \(C\) any monic integral polynomial.
If \(X^N-s\) divides \(\bar P\bar C\) over a prime field, then
\[
                      \deg\gcd(\bar C,X^N-s)>N/40 .
\]
This counts only the part of \(C\) sharing the specified residue roots, with the multiplicities that can actually fit in the binomial.

Put \(k=\deg\gcd(\bar P,X^N-s)\), \(m=\log M(P)\).
Using this actual nullity in the [three determinant estimates](Yin_C_G0904_v5.md#binomial) gives
\[
 5k\log2\le 6Nm+d\log(16/(3\sqrt3)),\qquad
 2k\log3\le 3Nm+d\log2,\qquad
 k\log p\le Nm+d\log2
\]
in characteristics two, three and \(p\ge5\), respectively.
Their proof uses only the nullity for \(P\), with no assumption on the integer polynomial \(PC\).
Inserting \(d/N\le2\), \(M(P)\le19/16\) gives \(k/N<39/40\) in all cases.
The exact comparisons after raising to powers are
\[
 2^{195}>(19/16)^{240}(256/27)^{40},\qquad
 3^{78}>2^{80}(19/16)^{120},\qquad
 5^{39}>(19/4)^{40}.
\]
The last comparison works for all \(p\ge5\).
The quotient of \(X^N-s\) by its gcd with \(\bar P\) divides both \(\bar C\) and the binomial, proving the bound.

<a id="orders"></a>
## Computing the overlap by cyclotomic orders

For \(C=\prod_n\Phi_n^{c_n}\), \(N=p^e M\), \(p\nmid M\), define
\[
 h_m=\sum_{a\ge0}c_{mp^a}\varphi(p^a)\qquad(p\nmid m).
\]
In characteristic two or for \(s=1\), take \(\mathcal I=\{m:m\mid M\}\).
For odd \(p\) and \(s=-1\) take \(\mathcal I=\{m:m\mid 2M,\ m\nmid M\}\).
Then the preceding gcd degree is exactly
\[
                  \sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\varphi(m)\min\{h_m,p^e\}.
\]
Indeed in characteristic \(p\) each \(\Phi_{mp^a}\) reduces to \(\bar\Phi_m^{\varphi(p^a)}\).
For \(a\ge1\) this follows from \(\Phi_{mp^a}(X)=\Phi_m(X^{p^a})/\Phi_m(X^{p^{a-1}})\), and \(a=0\) is immediate.
The distinct prime-to-\(p\) orders have disjoint simple root sets.
Every eligible root of the binomial has multiplicity \(p^e\), which proves the formula.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## Periods of boundary circles in a capped realization

Suppose \(C\) divides \(\prod_{i=1}^t(X^{h_i}-1)\), where \(t\ge1\) and \(h_i\) are positive integers.
This applies in particular when capping the connected orientable fiber of a bundle with orientation-preserving monodromy whose boundary circles form \(t\) permutation cycles of lengths \(h_i\).
The kernel on first homology is generated by the oriented boundary circles modulo their sum, so its characteristic polynomial is \(\prod_i(X^{h_i}-1)/(X-1)\). [Boundary module for closed fibers](Yin_C_G0883_v5.md#hironaka)
If the closed fiber has polynomial \(P\) and the open fiber polynomial acquires the above binomial modulo \(p\), then
\[
           \sum_{i=1}^t\gcd(h_i,N)>N/40 .
\]
Indeed the contribution to the gcd degree of a product is at most the sum of the individual gcd degrees.
For \(X^{h_i}-1\) the intersection with \(X^N-s\) has degree at most \(\gcd(h_i,N)\).
Over the algebraic closure, any common root translates the set of distinct common roots to the group of order \(\gcd(h_i,N)/p^{\min(v_p(h_i),v_p(N))}\), with multiplicity \(p^{\min(v_p(h_i),v_p(N))}\) per root.
If there is no common root its degree is zero.
Thus some boundary period \(h_i\) must be divisible by \(N/q\) for an integer \(q\mid N\), \(q<40t\).
This is a necessary arithmetic condition on a proposed realization using a signed binomial after capping; it neither constructs that realization nor requires every open-fiber polynomial to have such a binomial.
