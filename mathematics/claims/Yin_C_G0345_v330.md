# Two limits on constructing bounded-energy coefficient dips by division

This finding addresses two specific ways of seeking an integral band polynomial with bounded independent Mahler measure and unbounded negative coefficient logarithm.
It does not settle that general existence question.
The strategy researcher developed the division obstruction with an author-side collaborator, who supplied the torsion-coset argument; the conditional-independence calculation below supplies the second obstruction.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statements and the unresolved bounded-energy question

Use normalized circle measure \(d\nu=dt/(2\pi)\), write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\), and let \(\|f\|_1=\int|f|\,d\nu\).
Here \(\log^+x=\max(0,\log x)\) and \(\log^-x=\max(0,-\log x)\).
For a nonzero rational Laurent polynomial \(P\), its complete cyclotomic part \(C_P\) is the monic product of all its cyclotomic factors, with their full multiplicities; powers of \(X\) are omitted.

For every integer \(s\ge1\), there is a finite constant \(K_s\), depending only on \(s\), such that every rational Laurent polynomial with at most \(s\) nonzero terms satisfies
\[
                  \|\log|C_P(e^{it})|\|_1\le K_s.                 \tag{1}
\]
The exponents, coefficient heights and cyclotomic orders are unrestricted.
For an integer Laurent polynomial \(P\) with at most \(s\) terms, the quotient \(Q=P/C_P\) consequently satisfies
\[
       \int\log^-|Q(e^{it})|\,d\nu
                      \le 24s^2+\log s+K_s/2.                  \tag{2}
\]
No upper bound on \(m(P)\) is required in (2).
Thus if an integral band row contains one such nonzero coefficient \(Q\), its coefficient maximum has a uniformly bounded negative logarithmic mean.

For the second statement, choose \(2k\) distinct primes, partition them into sets \(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\) of size \(k\), and put
\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q}p,\\
 U&=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(X^{N/p}-1),\qquad
 V=\prod_{p\in\mathcal Q}(X^{N/p}-1),\\
 G&=\gcd(U,V),\qquad A=U/G,\qquad B=V/G,
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{3}
\]
where \(G\) is monic.
For this coprime cyclotomic quotient family there are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
                 c\sqrt{k}\le I:=m_2(AY+B)\le C\sqrt{k}          \tag{4}
\]
for every choice of the primes and the partition.
The upper bound and exact logarithmic identities already occur in [the original coefficient-dip construction](Yin_C_G0345_v328.md#example).
The lower bound here shows that choosing the primes differently cannot keep its independent measure bounded as \(k\to\infty\).

In particular neither construction route supplies a row
\[
 F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^M A_j(X)Y^j,\qquad
 \gcd_j A_j=1,
\]
with fixed \(M\), bounded \(m_2(F)\), and
\[
                   L_-:=\int\log^-\max_j|A_j(e^{it})|\,d\nu
                           \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The existence or impossibility of such general rows remains unresolved here.

<a id="cosets"></a>
## A sparse polynomial has finitely many torsion-coset patterns

For \(s\ge2\), let \(p_s\) be the product of the primes at most \(s\), let \(\mathcal B_s\) be the number of set partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,s\}\), and put
\[
                           T_s=\mathcal B_s p_s^s.
\]
We show that the set of roots of unity at which a rational polynomial with at most \(s\) terms vanishes is a union of at most \(T_s\) finite sets of the form
\[
                  \{z:z^g=\xi\},\qquad g\ge1,
                  \qquad \xi\text{ a root of unity}.           \tag{5}
\]
Multiplication by a Laurent monomial does not affect this assertion.

Write the polynomial as \(P=\sum_{i=1}^u a_iX^{n_i}\), where \(u\le s\), the exponents are distinct, and all \(a_i\in\mathbb Q\) are nonzero.
A monomial has no nonzero zero, so assume \(u\ge2\).
At a torsion zero, partition the terms into minimal vanishing subrelations.
Each block has at least two terms.
The [rational minimal-relation bound](Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order) says that every root ratio within a block belongs to \(\mu_{p_s}\).
Choose a base index in each block and write these ratios as \(\xi_i\), with the base ratio equal to one.
There are at most \(\mathcal B_s p_s^s=T_s\) partition-and-ratio choices, including choices that will be discarded.

Retain a choice only when its block coefficient relations
\(\sum_{i\text{ in block}}a_i\xi_i=0\) hold and its equations
\[
                         z^{n_i-n_{\rm base}}=\xi_i             \tag{6}
\]
have a common solution.
Every solution of (6) is a zero of \(P\), because each block sum then vanishes.
Conversely each torsion zero supplies at least one retained choice by the preceding paragraph.
The resulting union is therefore exact, rather than merely a cover by sets containing extra zeros.

For a consistent system (6), let \(g\) be the positive gcd of its nonzero exponents.
There is a nonzero exponent since the support is distinct and the blocks have at least two elements.
If \(z_0\) is one solution, all solutions form \(z_0\mu_g\): the ratios of two solutions satisfy every homogeneous power equation, whose common kernel is \(\mu_g\), by Bezout's identity.
Also \(z_0\) is torsion, since a nonzero power of it is a root of unity.
Thus the solution set has the form (5).
An intersection of finitely many sets (5) is either empty or another set of that form, by the same argument.

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## Inclusion–exclusion and the full multiplicity layers

Set
\[
                   c_h=\int\left|\log|1-e^{it}|\right|\,d\nu<\infty.
\]
For each binomial in (5), rotation and the measure-preserving map \(t\mapsto gt\) give
\[
                      \|\log|e^{igt}-\xi|\|_1=c_h.
\]
If a finite set \(S\) is a union of at most \(T\) sets of the form (5), apply inclusion–exclusion to its root-counting measure.
Every nonempty intersection is again a binomial root set.
For the monic squarefree polynomial \(C_S=\prod_{\alpha\in S}(X-\alpha)\), the same inclusion–exclusion identity holds for \(\log|C_S(e^{it})|\) almost everywhere, by addition of the individual root logarithms.
The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
                      \|\log|C_S|\|_1\le(2^T-1)c_h.             \tag{7}
\]
Coincident sets cause no problem, and empty intersections contribute nothing.

The monomial case has \(C_P=1\), so assume for the multiplicity argument that \(P\) has at least two terms.
Let \(\mathcal D=X\,d/dX\).
For a nonzero \(\alpha\), multiplicity at least \(r\) in \(P\) is equivalent to
\(\mathcal D^jP(\alpha)=0\) for \(0\le j<r\).
This follows from the triangular change between ordinary derivatives and powers of \(\mathcal D\), whose diagonal entries are nonzero at \(\alpha\).
Each \(\mathcal D^jP\) has at most \(s\) terms.
Its torsion zero set is thus a union of at most \(T_s\) binomial root sets, or is empty.
The layer of torsion roots of multiplicity at least \(r\) is an intersection of \(r\) such unions, hence a union of at most \(T_s^r\) binomial root sets.

Every nonzero root of a polynomial with \(u\) terms has multiplicity at most \(u-1\).
Indeed multiplicity at least \(u\) would make
\(\sum_i n_i^j a_i\alpha^{n_i}=0\) for \(0\le j<u\); the Vandermonde matrix on the distinct \(n_i\) is invertible, contradicting \(a_i\alpha^{n_i}\ne0\).
The product of the monic squarefree polynomials for layers \(r=1,\ldots,s-1\) is exactly \(C_P\).
Applying (7) to each layer proves (1), for example with
\[
             K_s=c_h\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}(2^{T_s^r}-1),\qquad K_1=0. \tag{8}
\]
These constants are deliberately coarse; their finiteness and independence from degree are the point.

<a id="division"></a>
## The integer quotient and the negative coefficient mean

Let \(P\) now have integer coefficients, with coefficient maximum \(H\ge1\), and at most \(s\) terms.
For \(s\ge2\), the [sparse regularization estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#regularity), equation (2) there with regularization parameter \(H\), gives
\[
                              m(P)\ge\log H-24s^2.
\]
Indeed its regularized mean is at least \(\log H\).
Also \(|P(e^{it})|\le sH\), and therefore
\[
 \int\log^-|P|\,d\nu
       =\int\log^+|P|\,d\nu-m(P)
                         \le\log s+24s^2.                      \tag{9}
\]
For \(s=1\), the negative integral is zero and the displayed bound remains valid.
Laurent shifts preserve every modulus used here.

The monic cyclotomic product \(C_P\) has logarithmic mean zero, so its positive and negative logarithmic integrals both equal \(\|\log|C_P|\|_1/2\).
The elementary pointwise inequality
\[
                   \log^-|P/C_P|
                         \le\log^-|P|+\log^+|C_P|
\]
holds away from their finitely many circle zeros, which do not affect the integrals.
Equations (1) and (9) prove (2).
The quotient is integral by division by monic cyclotomic factors.

For any row with a coefficient \(Q=P/C_P\), one has
\(\max_j|A_j|\ge|Q|\), so (2) bounds its \(L_-\) directly.
This conclusion does not need coprimality of the row or an energy bound.
It concerns division by the complete cyclotomic part: arbitrary additional removal of reciprocal noncyclotomic factors is not covered.

<a id="prime-energy"></a>
## Exact energy of the prime-partition quotient family

For (3), set \(u(t)=\log|A(e^{it})|\) and \(v(t)=\log|B(e^{it})|\).
Both \(A\) and \(B\) are monic cyclotomic products, so these logarithms have mean zero.
Jensen's formula in \(Y\) and \(\max(u,v)=(u+v+|u-v|)/2\) give the existing identity
\[
 I=\tfrac12\|u-v\|_1,\qquad
 u-v=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\log|1-e^{i(N/p)t}|
       -\sum_{p\in\mathcal Q}\log|1-e^{i(N/p)t}|.               \tag{10}
\]
The common factor has canceled from the difference.
For \(h_a(t)=\log|1-e^{iat}|\), the Fourier formula retained with that construction is
\[
              \langle h_a,h_b\rangle
                   =\frac{\pi^2}{12}\frac{\gcd(a,b)^2}{ab}.     \tag{11}
\]
Its diagonal terms are constant, and for distinct prime indices \(p,q\) the ratio on the right is \(1/(pq)\).
Thus the squared \(L^2\) norm in (10) is \(O(k)\): use
\((\sum_{p\mid N}1/p)^2\le2k\sum_{r\ge2}r^{-2}\).
This recovers the upper bound in (4).

For the lower bound, write a uniformly distributed circle point as
\[
                 t=\frac{2\pi(J+x)}N,
 \quad J\text{ uniform on }\{0,\ldots,N-1\},
 \quad x\text{ uniform on }[0,1],
\]
with \(J,x\) independent.
Conditional on \(x\), the residues \(R_p=J\bmod p\) are independent and uniform, by the Chinese remainder theorem.
The summands in (10) are consequently the independent random variables
\[
              H_p(x)=\log\left|2\sin\frac{\pi(R_p+x)}p\right|.
                                                                    \tag{12}
\]
Fix \(x\in[1/4,3/4]\) for the next estimates.

Their fourth moments have an absolute upper bound, uniformly in \(p\) and \(x\).
The positive part is at most \(\log2\).
For the negative part, \(\sin(\pi y)\ge2\operatorname{dist}(y,\mathbb Z)\) for \(0\le y\le1\), and the distances of \((r+x)/p\) from the endpoints are bounded below by
\((\min(r,p-1-r)+1/4)/p\).
The resulting fourth-power sum is bounded by a constant times
\[
 \frac1p\left((\log p)^4+
                    \sum_{r=1}^{p}\{\log(p/r)\}^4\right)=O(1), \tag{13}
\]
using monotonicity and \(\int_0^1\log^4(1/y)\,dy=24\).

For \(p\ge3\), put \(C_p=\cos(2\pi(R_p+x)/p)\).
The first two trigonometric sums on this grid give
\(\mathbb E C_p=0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(C_p)=1/2\).
Since \(H_p=h(C_p)\), where \(h(c)=\tfrac12\log(2-2c)\) and \(|h'(c)|\ge1/4\) for \(-1\le c<1\),
\[
                       \operatorname{Var}(H_p)\ge1/32.         \tag{14}
\]
For example apply \(|h(c)-h(c')|\ge|c-c'|/4\) to two independent copies and use the variance identity.
At most one of the \(2k\) primes is two, so (14) applies to at least \(2k-1\) summands.

Let \(Z=\sum_p\epsilon_pH_p\), with the signs in (10), and take an independent copy \(Z'\) conditional on the same \(x\).
Then \(S=Z-Z'\) is a sum of independent centered variables.
Equations (13)–(14) and the fourth-moment expansion yield
\[
                       \mathbb E S^2\ge c_1k,
                 \qquad \mathbb E S^4\le C_1k^2               \tag{15}
\]
with absolute positive constants.
Hölder interpolation and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E|Z|\ge\tfrac12\mathbb E|S|
       \ge\frac{(\mathbb E S^2)^{3/2}}
                    {2(\mathbb E S^4)^{1/2}}
       \ge c_2\sqrt{k}.                                       \tag{16}
\]
This avoids any assumption about the conditional mean of \(Z\).
Integrating (16) over the interval \([1/4,3/4]\) and using (10) proves the lower bound in (4).

For completeness, bounded \(k\) also keeps the negative coefficient mean of this family bounded uniformly in the primes.
Every cyclotomic index dividing \(U\) or \(V\) divides the squarefree integer \(N\), which has \(2k\) prime factors.
For such an index \(t\), the identity
\(\Phi_t(X)=\prod_{a\mid t}(X^a-1)^{\mu(t/a)}\)
gives \(\|\log|\Phi_t|\|_1\le2^{2k}c_h\).
There are at most \(2^{2k}\) divisor indices and each has multiplicity at most \(k\) in \(A\).
Hence \(\|\log|A|\|_1\le k2^{4k}c_h\), which also bounds
\(\int\log^-\max(|A|,|B|)\,d\nu\).
Together with (4), this excludes a bounded-\(I\), divergent-\(L_-\) sequence anywhere in the family, not only along an explicitly prescribed \(k\to\infty\).

<a id="scope"></a>
## What these obstructions leave open

The first obstruction allows growing degree, unrestricted coefficients and multiplicities, but requires a uniformly bounded number of terms in a parent polynomial and removal of its complete cyclotomic part.
The second gives the actual energy order of a specific family whose number of binomial factors grows.
Neither proves a bound on \(L_-\) for all coprime rows with bounded independent measure.

The reversal mechanism remains a concrete unresolved alternative.
For an integer polynomial \(A\) with nonzero constant, write \(A^*(X)=X^{\deg A}A(1/X)\).
On the circle \(|A|=|A^*|\), so \(m_2(AY+A^*)=m(A)\).
To make this a coprime-row example one must first remove the common factor of \(A,A^*\).
If that removal consists only of the complete cyclotomic part of a uniformly sparse parent, (2) prevents divergent \(L_-\).
The argument supplies no corresponding bound when reciprocal noncyclotomic factors are also divided out, or when the nonreciprocal polynomial is supplied without such a sparse parent.
No counterexample to the general bounded-energy assertion or to the Mahler gap is claimed.
