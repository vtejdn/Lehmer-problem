# A boundary coefficient obstructs the second trace period at a power of three

<a id="statement"></a>
## Candidate statement

Take \(p=3^\nu\) with \(\nu\) positive and even, and \(q=p-1\) sufficiently large for the [even-modulus construction](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#statement).
Fix its binary precision \(h\ge1\), an odd denominator prime \(\ell\nmid pq\), and its auxiliary prime \(t>p\).
Write \(u=2^h,\ s=p/q,\ \theta=u\log p/\log s,\ J=q/2+1,\ L=J+1=(p+3)/2\).
A row has degree \(d=2D\), exact pole \(q^d F(s)=(2q+1)^2=K_0\), even-side order \(A=tn\) and proposed second order \(N=A+2u\).
Here \(15\mid n\), so \(3\nmid N\).
The model \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][X]\) is monic and irreducible with a unique root \(T\) outside the closed unit disk.
The first \(L-1\) coefficients after the constant term of the reciprocal polynomial \(F\) vanish, and the pole prescribes the next coefficient modulo \(p\) as
\[
       c(D)=q^{-(d-L)}\frac{K_0-q^d}{p^L}\pmod p .
\]
This is the [rowwise lifting criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace).

Every class \(c\bmod p\) occurs on arbitrarily large [admissible rows](#degrees).
A monic reciprocal polynomial with that row's prescribed coefficients can divide \(X^N-1\) over \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) only if \(3^{\nu-1}\mid c\).
This excludes \(p-3\) of the \(p\) possible boundary classes.
For \(\nu\ge4\) and every \(c\) with \(0<v_3(c)<\nu-1\), there are [rows of unbounded degree](#strict-excess) on which all roots of \(F\bmod3\) satisfy \(X^N=1\), a degree-\(d-o(d)\) divisor of \(F\bmod p\) has exact order \(N\), and the full order modulo \(p\) is \(N3^b\) with \(1<3^b=o(d)\).
Every choice of full word satisfying that row's prescribed coefficients fails exact order \(N\).

For strengthened rows with \(c=0\), the [finite-field test](#digits) determines whether a chosen reciprocal factor of \(X^N-1\bmod3\) lifts to the required zero prefix modulo \(3^\nu\).
It uses only coefficients through \(3^{\nu-1}L\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) and applies more generally at any odd prime power \(r^\nu\) with \(r\nmid N\).
Whether suitable factors of the full prescribed degree and order occur on unbounded strengthened rows remains open.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## The first available coefficient of a prime-to-\(r\) torsion word

Work at an odd prime \(r\nmid N\), and let \(W\) be a monic divisor of \(X^N-1\bmod r^\nu\).
Its reduction \(V\bmod r\) is squarefree.
In an unramified splitting field of \(\mathbb Q_r\), take the unique \(N\)-th roots of unity lifting the roots of \(V\).
Their Frobenius-stable product polynomial gives the unique monic factor lifting \(V\) at every precision, by coprime Hensel factorization. [Teichmüller roots and Frobenius](Yin_C_G0901_v167.md#contraction)
Let \(B(X)=\prod_\alpha(1-\alpha X)\) for those lifts and \(S_j=\sum_\alpha\alpha^j\).
Thus \(B\in\mathbb Z_r[X]\), \(S_{rj}=S_j\) exactly since Frobenius permutes the roots, and
\[
             \log B(X)=-\sum_{j\ge1}S_j X^j/j .
\]
The polynomial \(B\bmod r^\nu\) is the reversed monic factor, which is \(W\) itself when \(W\) is reciprocal with constant one.

Suppose \(B(X)\equiv1+cX^L\bmod (r^\nu,X^{L+1})\).
In degrees below \(L\) the logarithmic coefficients belong to \(r^\nu\mathbb Z_r\), and its coefficient in degree \(L\) is \(c\bmod r^\nu\).
Indeed the logarithm of a truncated series in \(1+r^\nu X\mathbb Z_r[[X]]\) has coefficients divisible by \(r^\nu\), by \(k\nu-v_r(k)\ge\nu\) for \(k\ge1\).
Apply this to the ratio of \(B\) and \(1+cX^L\) in the truncated series ring.
When \(r\mid L\), write \(e=v_r(L)\).
The coefficient in degree \(L/r\) gives \(S_{L/r}\equiv0\bmod r^{\nu+e-1}\).
Since \(S_{L/r}=S_L\), comparison in degree \(L\) gives
\[
                            c\equiv0\pmod {r^{\nu-1}}.
\]
Here \(r=3\) divides \(L=(3^\nu+3)/2\) exactly once for \(\nu\ge2\).
Consequently failure of this congruence excludes every full word dividing \(X^N-1\) at the required precision.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Realizing the excluded boundary classes on the original rows

Use the [degree progression](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical) before strengthening its \(p\)-side condition.
Its condition at \(3\) is \((1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^L\).
The admissible \(D\) form one class modulo \(3^{\nu(L-1)}\) at this prime, since \(1-p\) has that exact order modulo \(3^{\nu L}\).
This follows from \(v_3((1-p)^{3^a}-1)=\nu+a\).
The [remaining endpoint and rotation conditions](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) are congruences of prime-power period at primes distinct from three, including the strengthened \(q\)-side condition in the even-modulus construction.
Thus one can take the full admissible class \(D_0\bmod P\) with \(v_3(P)=\nu(L-1)\).
The prime \(t\) is coprime to \(pP\).

For \(D=D_0+Pj\), the unit \(q^{-(2D-L)}\bmod p\) is constant, since \(q\equiv-1\bmod p\).
Also \(v_3(q^{2P}-1)=\nu+v_3(2P)=\nu L\).
Write \(q^{2P}=1+p^L z\) with \(3\nmid z\).
Then
\[
        \frac{K_0-q^{2D}}{p^L}
           \equiv \frac{K_0-q^{2D_0}}{p^L}-q^{2D_0}zj\pmod p.
\]
The numerator at \(D_0\) is divisible by \(p^L\) by squaring the endpoint congruence.
The coefficient of \(j\) is a unit, so each desired \(c\) prescribes exactly one refined class of \(D\bmod pP\).
Choose \(S\) nearest \(n/(2\theta)\) in the class giving \(D=tS\) there.
Then \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta t pP\) independently of \(n\), and the [binary filling](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#binary) and [archimedean period bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) continue to apply.
Reciprocal words with this coefficient and the zero prefix exist at sufficiently large degrees by reflecting the free low coefficients.
The [coefficient gluing and pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) lift each such word to the one-denominator model.
This realizes the boundary classes on actual rows.

<a id="strict-excess"></a>
## Near-complete divisors on rows where exact completion is impossible

Fix \(\nu\ge4\) even and \(0<v_3(c)<\nu-1\).
Use the synchronized prime divisors and Frobenius orbits of the [degree-\(d-o(d)\) construction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion).
For each tolerance \(\eta>0\) it produces \(R\mid N,\ w=N/R,\ \gcd(R,w)=1\) on arbitrarily large rows and a reciprocal divisor \(U=V_0(X^w)\mid X^N-1\bmod p\) of exact order \(N\).
Choose \(D\) in the refined class for \(c\) above in place of the strengthened class for zero.
This changes the rounding bound to \(\theta t pP\) independently of \(R\) and \(\eta\).
The limits \(N/d\to\theta,\ d/w\to R/\theta\) used to select the orbits therefore still hold at fixed \(R\).
Consequently their same degree budget gives an even deficit \(\Delta=d-\deg U<\eta d\), with \(\Delta>2L\) by the lower reserve in that construction.
The first \(L\) coefficients after the constant term of \(U\) vanish since \(w>L\).

The cited construction supplies a monic reciprocal filler \(G\) of degree \(\Delta\), constant one, zero prefix through \(L\), and \(G\mid(X^N-1)^\Delta\bmod p\).
Replace it by
\[
                 G'=G+c(X^L+X^{\Delta-L})\pmod p .
\]
The two modified coefficients are distinct and \(G'\equiv G\bmod3\).
The word \(UG'\) now has the prescribed coefficient \(c\) and lifts to \(F\) by the same pole correction.
All its geometric roots modulo three satisfy \(X^N=1\), and the divisor \(U\) retains exact order \(N\) modulo \(p\).
Modulo three, \(UG'\) divides \((X^N-1)^{\Delta+1}\).
Hence the order there divides \(N3^a\) for \(a=\lceil\log_3(\Delta+1)\rceil\).
In the full quotient by \(UG'\) over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\mathbb Z\), \(X^{N3^a}\) accordingly belongs to \(1+3\mathcal A\), where \(\mathcal A\) is that quotient.
Units in \(1+3\mathcal A\) have exponent dividing \(3^{\nu-1}\) by binomial expansion.
The full order therefore has the form \(N3^b\) with \(3^b\le3^\nu(\Delta+1)\).
The [boundary obstruction](#boundary) gives \(b\ge1\) for every full word of this form on these rows.
Taking increasing degrees along \(\eta\downarrow0\) yields \(1<3^b=o(d)\).
The construction has the same allowed denominator \(\ell\), and \(T\) and \((pT-q)/(p-qT)\) remain [multiplicatively independent in characteristic zero](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement).

<a id="digits"></a>
## Testing a zero prefix using only the factor over the residue field

Let \(V\mid X^N-1\bmod r\) be monic and reciprocal with constant one, \(r\) odd and \(r\nmid N\).
Compute its unique expansion in \(\mathbb F_r[[X]]\)
\[
              V(X)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-X^j)^{a_j},\qquad 0\le a_j<r.
\]
After dividing out earlier factors, the negative of the current coefficient at \(X^j\) gives \(a_j\bmod r\).
The unique lift of \(V\) as a divisor of \(X^N-1\bmod r^\nu\) has zero coefficients at offsets \(1,\ldots,L\) exactly when
\[
   a_{m r^k}=0\qquad
   (1\le m\le L,\ r\nmid m,\ 0\le k<\nu+\lfloor\log_r(L/m)\rfloor).       \tag{1}
\]
The indices are at most \(r^{\nu-1}L\).
Thus (1) is a finite test over the residue field.

To prove this, use \(B,S_j\) from [the lifted factor](#boundary).
Zero prefix modulo \(r^\nu\) is equivalent to \(S_j/j\in r^\nu\mathbb Z_r\) for \(1\le j\le L\).
For the converse, exponentiating a truncated series with all positive-degree coefficients in \(r^\nu\mathbb Z_r\) preserves this divisibility, since \(k\nu-v_r(k!)\ge\nu\).
By \(S_{rj}=S_j\), the conditions are
\[
             S_m\equiv0\bmod r^{\nu+\lfloor\log_r(L/m)\rfloor}
                    \qquad(m\le L,\ r\nmid m).
\]
For \(r\nmid m\) let \(b_m=m^{-1}\sum_{e\mid m}\mu(m/e)S_e\in\mathbb Z_r\), where \(\mu\) denotes the Möbius function.
The same inversion formula gives zero for every index divisible by \(r\) because the consecutive \(r\)-power terms in the numerator cancel.
The formal Euler product \(B=\prod_{r\nmid m}(1-X^m)^{b_m}\) follows by comparing logarithms, using \(\sum_{e\mid j,\ r\nmid e}e b_e=S_j\).
Divisibility of \(S_m\) to the displayed precision is equivalent to the same divisibility of \(b_m\) for all the indicated \(m\).
Indeed \(m\) is a unit, and the required precision for each divisor \(e\mid m\) is at least the one for \(m\), in both directions of Möbius inversion.
Expand \(b_m=\sum_{k\ge0}b_{m,k}r^k\) in base \(r\).
Reducing \((1-X^m)^{b_m}\) modulo \(r\) gives \(\prod_{k\ge0}(1-X^{m r^k})^{b_{m,k}}\) by approximating \(b_m\) by integers and applying Frobenius.
As \(B\bmod r=V\), uniqueness of the digit expansion identifies \(b_{m,k}=a_{m r^k}\), proving (1).

For the zero-boundary \(p=3^\nu\) rows, group the \(N\)-th roots over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_3\) into orbits under Frobenius and inversion.
Select a union of orbits of size \(d\), constant term one in its monic polynomial, and least common multiple of the selected root orders \(N\).
The unramified lift preserves each root order, and the monic factor is uniquely determined by its reduction.
Consequently such a selection passes the full rowwise order and coefficient test precisely when its polynomial passes (1).

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## How many coefficients of the residue factor can be needed?

The cutoff \(r^{\nu-1}L\) in (1) can be attained even by a reciprocal integer divisor of a prime-to-\(r\) torsion polynomial.
Write \(L=m r^e\) with \(r\nmid m\), take \(\nu\ge2\), and put \(H=r^{\nu+e-1}\).
Choose \(H\) distinct odd primes \(a_i>r^{\nu-1}L\) different from \(r\) and coprime to \(m\).
With \(E_a(T)=1+\cdots+T^{a-1}\), put \(B_0(X)=\prod_i E_{a_i}(X^m)\).
The factors have disjoint simple roots, since \(X^m\) has a different prime order in each.
Thus \(B_0\) divides \(X^{m\prod_i a_i}-1\), whose exponent is prime to \(r\).

Through degree \(mH\) the power series for \(B_0\) agrees with \((1-X^m)^{-H}\) by the geometric-sum formula.
Its first nonconstant coefficient modulo \(r\) is therefore at \(mH=r^{\nu-1}L\).
At degree \(m j\) for \(1\le j\le r^e\), the integer coefficient is
\[
       \binom{H+j-1}{j}=\frac Hj\binom{H+j-1}{j-1}.
\]
For \(j<r^e\) its valuation is at least \(\nu\).
For \(j=r^e\) the second binomial factor is a unit, since each \((H+i)/i\) for \(1\le i<r^e\) is a unit congruent to one modulo \(r\).
Thus the first nonzero coefficient modulo \(r^\nu\) occurs at \(L\) and has valuation exactly \(\nu-1\).
Together with the constant factor \(1\), this gives two local torsion factors agreeing in every smaller residue-field position and passing different zero-prefix tests at precision \(r^\nu\).
For \(r\mid L\) it also realizes the minimum allowed valuation of a nonzero boundary coefficient in the local divisor problem.
