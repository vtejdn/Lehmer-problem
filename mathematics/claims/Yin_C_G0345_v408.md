# The Lehmer infimum is a limit of finite integer coefficient minima

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite minimum at each prime

Let \(\lambda\) be the infimum of the strictly positive logarithmic Mahler measures of nonzero integer polynomials.
For odd primes \(p\), choose any positive integer bounds \(b(p)\to\infty\).
At each prime take the following finite minimum \(M_p\).
Include the integer \(2^p\) as an initial candidate.
Consider nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b(p)\) with \(\gcd(U,V)=1\) over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), and put \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), using sums of absolute coefficients.
Include \(H\le 2^p\) if \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(\iota\) sends \(Y\) to \(Y^{-1}\), and
\[
                 U(x^p)+xV(x^p)
\]
has a repeated monic reciprocal irreducible factor which is the minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit.
There is no seed list in the definition.
Then
\[
          \frac{\log M_p}{p}\ \ge\ \lambda,\qquad
          \lim_{\substack{p\to\infty\\p\ {\rm prime}}}
                      \frac{\log M_p}{p}=\lambda.                      \tag{1}
\]
The integer row degrees \(b(p)\) may grow arbitrarily slowly.
If \(b\) is computable, the integer \(M_p\) is computable with polynomial factorizations of degree at most \(2b(p)\), regardless of the much larger degree of the displayed graph.
In particular a positive uniform gap is equivalent to the existence of an integer \(k\ge2\) and **infinitely many** primes \(p\) at which no qualifying pair has \(H\le2^{\lfloor p/k\rfloor}\).
If the gap vanishes, for each fixed \(k\) such pairs occur at every sufficiently large prime.

<a id="lower"></a>
## The repeated factor gives an individual lower bound

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of a qualifying repeated factor \(P\), and put \(z=\alpha^p\).
Since \(U,V\) are coprime and \(U(z)+\alpha V(z)=0\), both values are nonzero and \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\).
Thus \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and the monic reciprocal minimum \(Q\) of \(z\) has \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\).
The [elimination with the reciprocal graph](Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) gives \(Q^2\mid UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) in the Laurent ring.
On the circle this nonzero determinant has absolute value at most \(\max(|U|,|V|)^2\).
Monic division and integration therefore give \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\).
The initial candidate also gives \(\log 2\ge\lambda\), for example using the constant integer two.

It suffices in computing \(\lambda\) to take monic reciprocal minimal polynomials of nontorsion units.
After factoring over \(\mathbb Z\), scalar content or a nonunit extreme coefficient already costs at least \(\log2\).
Nonreciprocal monic units have measure at least the logarithm of the real root of \(x^3-x-1\), by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
The [degree-ten Salem member below \(6/5\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks) has strictly smaller positive log measure than either bound.
Products add their nonnegative logarithmic measures, which proves the reduction.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Recovering each fixed reciprocal measure with shrinking relative width

Fix such a \(P\), of degree \(e\) and log measure \(h>0\).
At every sufficiently large prime let \(b_*=\min(b(p)-1,\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor)\), so \(b_*\to\infty\) and \(b_*=o(p)\).
Use the [two-class coefficient box and separation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v231.md#separation) in the **seed variable** with offset \(r=1\), before the substitution used there for the lifted graph.
For fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/4\), that argument gives raw rows with degrees at most \(b_*+1\), a nonzero reflected determinant, and
\[
          P^2\mid U_0(x^p)+xV_0(x^p),\qquad
          \log\max(\|U_0\|_1,\|V_0\|_1)
                     \le (1+\epsilon)ph+O(\log(2b_*)).
\]
In detail the largest fiber of the two-jet box has logarithmic size at least \((2\epsilon+o(1))b_*ph\).
Multiples of a single primitive row ratio in that fiber have logarithmic count at most \((\epsilon+o(1))b_*ph\), by the [fixed-seed contraction bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v231.md#jets).
Thus two distinct ratios occur, and the two original differences and their two sums with multipliers \(1,Y\) supply a pair with unequal row moduli.
The proof of these seed statements uses every sufficiently large odd prime for the fixed seed, independently of any subsequent lift or prime window.

The primitive common polynomial divisor of these two raw rows cannot contain \(Q\), the minimum of \(\alpha^p\).
Otherwise the same contraction estimate applied after removing \(Q(x^p)\) would require measure at least \((3/2-o(1))ph\) for the original graph, contradicting its coefficient bound.
Here the seed is fixed and the graph degree is \(O(pb_*)=o(p^2)\), as required.
Consequently division by the common polynomial and joint scalar content keeps both copies of \(P\), nonzero unequal-modulus rows and ordinary polynomial degrees at most \(b_*+1\).
For an integral row \(A\) and an integral divisor \(D\) with \(A/D\) integral one has
\[
             \log\|A/D\|_1\le (b_*+1)\log2+m(A/D)
                         \le (b_*+1)\log2+\log\|A\|_1.
\]
This controls the coefficients after division even when some have grown.
The resulting qualifying cost therefore has \(\limsup p^{-1}\log H\le(1+\epsilon)h\).
For any fixed seed with \(h<\log2\), first taking sufficiently small \(\epsilon\) puts it within the finite height bound for all large primes.
Letting \(\epsilon\) tend to zero gives \(\limsup p^{-1}\log M_p\le h\), and then taking the infimum over such seeds proves (1).
For the integer exclusion choose \((\log2)/k<\lambda\) if \(\lambda>0\).
Conversely, if \(\lambda=0\), (1) eventually puts the genuine minimum below \(2^{\lfloor p/k\rfloor}<2^p\) for every fixed \(k\).
These quantifiers permit a subsequence of primes in a proof of the positive gap.

<a id="factor"></a>
## Detecting a candidate by low-degree factorizations

For a proposed pair let \(b=b(p)\), \(H_0=Y^b(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\) and
\[
             W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV.
\]
Both polynomials have degree at most \(2b\).
The lowest nonzero product term in \(W\) has coefficient equal to that of \(UV\) at its lowest exponent times \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0U-\operatorname{ord}_0V)-1\), so \(W\ne0\).
If \(H_0=0\) the pair is excluded at the outset.
Factor \(\gcd(H_0,H_0',W)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and keep only monic reciprocal integral factors \(Q\) which are irreducible, have unit constant term and are noncyclotomic.
For each retained factor put \(z=Y\bmod Q\).
The necessary and sufficient remaining test is
\[
                V(z)\ne0,\qquad (-U(z))^p=z\,V(z)^p.                  \tag{2}
\]
Powers in (2) need only \(O(\log p)\) multiplications modulo \(Q\) by repeated squaring.
For necessity the [individual lower-bound argument](#lower) puts \(Q^2\) in \(H_0\), and eliminating \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\) from the graph's Euler derivative gives \(W(z)=0\).
For sufficiency set \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\).
It has \(\alpha^p=z\) and generates \(\mathbb Q(z)\); it is a nontorsion algebraic unit because \(z\) is.
The involution sending \(z\) to \(z^{-1}\) takes \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) because \(H_0(z)=0\), so its minimum is reciprocal.
The value of the graph at \(\alpha\) vanishes and its Euler derivative there is \(W(z)/V(z)\).
This proves double divisibility and completes the finite test.
Equivalently \(z\) is a fixed point of the rational map \(Y\mapsto(-U(Y)/V(Y))^p\) at which its derivative is one.
The convergence in (1) gives no uniform lower bound for the minima as \(p\) varies.
