# A Dirichlet identity for two polynomial rows and a finite cofactor test

<a id="identity"></a>
## An exact identity for the maximum of two polynomials

Let \(U,V\) be coprime nonzero complex polynomials with \(k=\max(\deg U,\deg V)>0\).
Let \(B\) be the absolute resultant of the two binary forms obtained by homogenizing both polynomials to degree \(k\).
Write \(\sigma\) for Haar probability on the circle and set
\[
 f(z)=\log\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|),\qquad I=\int f\,d\sigma,\qquad
 g(z)=f(z)-k\log^+|z|.
\]
Then
\[
    2kI=\log B+\mathcal D(g),\qquad
    \mathcal D(g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}|\nabla g|^2\,dA .        \tag{1}
\]
For integral rows \(B\ge1\), so both terms on the right are nonnegative.
When \(\deg U=k\) and \(\deg V=l\), \(B=|\operatorname{lc}U|^{k-l}|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\).
This follows by evaluating the homogenized second row at infinity or by the determinant formula, and also holds if \(V\) is constant.

Let \(\nu\) be the probability obtained by averaging the \(k\) roots of \(W_t=U-tV\), counted with multiplicity and weight \(1/k\), over \(|t|=1\).
At most one \(t\) lowers its degree, which does not affect this measure.
Write \(L_t\) for the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(W_t\) and \(a=\int\log|L_t|\,d\sigma(t)\), finite by Jensen's formula.
The same formula pointwise in \(z\) gives \(f(z)=\int\log|W_t(z)|\,d\sigma(t)\).
Applying the distributional Laplacian gives \((2\pi)^{-1}\Delta f=k\nu\), by the logarithmic potential of a root.
The integration against compactly supported tests is valid by local integrability: on each compact set the positive logarithms are bounded and the averaged logarithm \(f\) is continuous because the rows have no common zero.
In the coordinate \(w=1/z\), \(g\) near infinity is the logarithmic maximum of the reversed rows of degree \(k\), at least one nonzero at zero.
Consequently \(g\) is locally Lipschitz on both charts of the sphere and its Dirichlet integral is finite.
Integration by parts on the sphere, or smoothing on its two charts, gives
\[
 \mathcal D(g)=k\int g\,d(\sigma-\nu)
\]
as in the [energy estimate for polynomial maxima](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test).

At every finite zero of \(W_t\) on a circle fiber, \(f=\log|V|\).
For almost every \(t\), the absolute homogeneous resultant of \(W_t,V\) is \(B\) by subtracting a scalar multiple of the second row from the first.
Evaluating at all roots of \(W_t\) gives
\[
 \sum_{W_t(z)=0}\log |V(z)|=\log B-k\log|L_t| .
\]
Also \(\sum_{W_t(z)=0}\log^+|z|=m(W_t)-\log|L_t|\).
On averaging, \(\int m(W_t)\,d\sigma(t)=I\) by the circle identity for \(f\) and integrability on the two circles.
The latter follows again by boundedness of the positive part and continuity of \(f\) on the circle.
Thus \(k\int f\,d\nu=\log B-ka\) and \(k\int\log^+|z|\,d\nu=I-a\).
The negative part of \(f\) is bounded globally by coprimality and growth, so these equalities also justify the individual integrals if a leading coefficient approaches zero.
Substitution yields \(k\int g\,d\nu=\log B-kI\), which proves (1).

<a id="radial"></a>
## Counting pencil roots outside an annulus

For any \(\tau>0\), let \(n_-\) and \(n_+\) be the mean numbers of zeros of \(U-tV\) in \(|z|\le e^{-\tau}\) and \(|z|\ge e^\tau\), respectively, with the same counting convention.
Then
\[
                       \mathcal D(g)\ \ge\ \tau(n_-^2+n_+^2).             \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the logarithmic radial derivative of the angular mean of \(g(e^{s+i\theta})\) is
\(k(\nu\{|z|\le e^s\}-\mathbf 1_{s>0})\) for almost every \(s\), by circular averaging of the logarithm of each root factor.
Its square integrates to at most \(\mathcal D(g)\) by Cauchy–Schwarz on each circle in logarithmic polar coordinates.
On \(-\tau<s<0\) the derivative is at least \(n_-\), and on \(0<s<\tau\) it is at most \(-n_+\).
Equality is possible in (2): \(U=z^k,\ V=e^{-k\tau}\) has the pencil on the single circle \(|z|=e^{-\tau}\) and \(\mathcal D(g)=k^2\tau\).

## A finite cofactor test and its required scale

For the [double-root comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), let \(P\) have degree \(d\) and log measure \(h\), take an admissible prime \(p\), and write \(F\) for a primitive two-row witness and \(R\) for its noncyclotomic cofactor after dividing by \(P^2\).
Use the two individually stripped signed rows for \(U,V\), and let \(D\) be the ordinary exponent span of \(F\).
They are coprime and cannot both be constant because a binomial has only simple nonzero roots.
Stripping row monomials preserves both magnitudes on the circle.
The [row integral comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) and the pointwise triangle inequality give
\[
 (1-4/p)I-(2/p)\log(CD)\ \le\ m(F)=2h+m(R)\ \le\ I+\log2 ,
 \qquad C=\int(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\sigma(z).
\]
By (1) and (2), in particular
\[
 \frac{m(R)}p\ \ge\
 \frac{1-4/p}{2kp}\{\log B+\tau(n_-^2+n_+^2)\}
      -\frac{2h}p-\frac{2\log(CD)}{p^2}.                            \tag{3}
\]
Throughout the cap \(D\le p(16d+1)\) and the fourth-logarithmic window, the final error tends to zero.
If \(d\to\infty,\ h\to0\), each admissible prime and pair of residues has a witness with \(m(R)\le2ph\).
For every such witness, \(\log B/(kp)\to0\) and \(\mathcal D(g)/(kp)\to0\) by (1) and the lower comparison.
Its mean pencil counts are \(o(\sqrt{kp/\tau})\) on both sides of the annulus, also for varying \(\tau>0\).
Thus a lower bound of order \(kp\) on the energy, or on the annular count in (3), valid on all witnesses at one selected prime per input would suffice for a gap.
