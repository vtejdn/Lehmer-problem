# All exceptional-unit measures in the divisible group of a fixed unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite test inside a cyclotomic extension

Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(L=K(\mu_\infty)\), and consider
\[
    \mathcal H=\{\beta:\beta^j\in\mu_\infty\langle\alpha\rangle
                                      \text{ for some }j\ge1\}.
\]
An exceptional unit here is a unit \(u\) for which \(1-u\) is a unit.
There is a largest integer \(t\ge1\) such that some \(\delta\in L\) satisfies \(\delta^t=\alpha\).
Choose any such \(\delta\) and put
\[
                T_\delta=\{\eta\in\mu_\infty:\delta-\eta
                                       \text{ is an algebraic unit}\}.
\]
This is a finite set.
The group \(\mathcal H\) contains a nontorsion exceptional unit if and only if \(T_\delta\ne\varnothing\).
When it is nonempty, the least positive total logarithmic Mahler measure of such units is exactly
\[
                              \min_{\eta\in T_\delta} m(\delta/\eta).      \tag{1}
\]
In fact the set of all their measures is finite, even though roots of arbitrarily large degrees are allowed.
For any prescribed finite set of roots of unity containing \(1\), (1) is still the exact minimum when \(u-\zeta\) must be a unit at every prescribed \(\zeta\).
Every such finite set can be met at this same measure by units of arbitrarily large degree.
This statement concerns the divisible group of the fixed input, and does not assert that every shrinking sequence of inputs has nonempty \(T_\delta\).

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Choosing an element with full radical degrees

Every finite subextension of \(L/K\) is abelian, since \(L\) is obtained from the abelian cyclotomic extension by base change.
The [abelian height bound](../audits/literature/AZ10/Yin_U001.md#az10-u001) gives a positive lower bound depending only on \([K:\mathbb Q]\) for the absolute logarithmic height of its nontorsion elements.
The height of a \(t\)-th root of \(\alpha\) is \(h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)/t\), so only bounded \(t\) can occur in \(L\).
By maximality \(\delta\) is not a \(p\)-th power in \(L\) for any prime \(p\).
All choices of its \(t\)-th root branch differ by roots of unity in \(L\), and changing the branch merely rotates \(T_\delta\) and preserves the list (1).

Any nontorsion \(\beta\in\mathcal H\) satisfies
\[
                     \beta^s=w=\xi\delta^r,\qquad
                \xi\in\mu_\infty,\quad s\ge1,\quad (r,s)=1,\quad r\ne0 .
\]
Indeed reduce the rational exponent of \(\delta\) coming from a defining power equality, absorbing the remaining finite-order factor into \(\xi\).
The roots of \(X^s-w\) are the \(s\) multiples of \(\beta\) by \(\mu_s\).
As \(L\) contains them as multipliers, \(L(\beta)/L\) is finite Galois with group a subgroup of \(\mu_s\) acting on \(\beta\) by multiplication.
If it has order \(D<s\), then \(\beta^D\in L\) and \(s/D\) divides \(s\).
The element \(w\) would be an \((s/D)\)-th power in \(L\).
Absorb its factor \(\xi\) using roots of unity and use \((r,s/D)=1\) and Bézout to make \(\delta\) an \((s/D)\)-th power in \(L\), a contradiction.
The full \(s\) conjugates therefore occur.
Since \(\mathbb Q(w)\subset L\), the degree over \(\mathbb Q(w)\) is also \(s\).
The [height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) now gives \(m(\beta)=m(w)\), exactly.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Finiteness of the primitive torsion differences

Use \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) and put \(D_\eta=[K_0(\eta):K_0]\).
As the order of \(\eta\) tends to infinity one has
\[
        D_\eta^{-1}\log\big|N_{K_0(\eta)/\mathbb Q}(\delta-\eta)\big|
                              \ \longrightarrow\ m(\delta)>0 .
\]
For every embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K_0\) with \(|\sigma\delta|\ne1\) the logarithm of the distance is continuous on the circle.
The conjugates of \(\eta\) over the fixed embedded field become uniformly distributed, so their average gives \(\log^+|\sigma\delta|\) by Jensen's formula.
At embeddings on the circle, \(\sigma\delta\) is nontorsion and the [Baker–Ih–Rumely logarithmic orbit estimate](../audits/literature/BIR08/Yin_U001.md#torsion-logarithm) gives the same limit, zero.
The angular equidistribution used for the continuous case also follows from the orbit estimate for arcs in that source.
Summing over the fixed finite set of embeddings proves the displayed norm limit, since \(\delta\) is a unit.
Only finitely many orders can therefore yield norm of absolute value one.
This proves the finiteness of \(T_\delta\) without supplying a uniform bound for varying inputs.

<a id="anchors"></a>
## Recovering the measures from the primitive torsion differences

Every element of \(\mathcal H\) is a unit because some positive power and its inverse are integral.
In the full radical extension just constructed, the conjugate product of \(1-\beta\) over \(L\) is \(1-w\).
Thus \(1-\beta\) is a unit if and only if \(1-w\) is a unit.
One direction uses the product of conjugate units.
For the other, each \(1-\omega\beta\) is integral and their product is a unit, hence every factor is a unit.
This argument uses only finitely many algebraic integers even though \(L\) is infinite.

For \(r>0\), the factorization of \(1-\xi\delta^r\) gives unit factors precisely when every solution of \(\eta^r=\xi^{-1}\) belongs to \(T_\delta\).
For \(r=-u<0\), multiplying by the unit \(\delta^u\) reduces this to the solutions of \(\eta^u=\xi\).
There are \(|r|\) distinct solutions in either case.
It follows that \(|r|\le |T_\delta|\), and that for each possible \(r\) only finitely many \(\xi\) occur, determined by the powers of members of \(T_\delta\).
Hence the measures \(m(w)=m(\beta)\) form a finite set.
Conversely every successful pair \(r,\xi\) gives the exceptional unit \(w\in\mathcal H\), so the finite description introduces no spurious measures.

For any solution \(\eta\) of the indicated torsion equation, \(\delta/\eta\) is exceptional, and its \(|r|\)-th power equals \(w\) or \(w^{-1}\).
The degree of a root of order \(|r|\) over its power field is at most \(|r|\).
Its total measure is therefore at most \(m(w)\).
Taking all \(\eta\in T_\delta\) proves (1) and the emptiness criterion.
For any minimizer \(v=\delta/\eta\) in (1), \(X^N-v\) has full degree \(N\) over \(\mathbb Q(v)\) for all \(N\), by the radical argument with \(r=1\).
Every \(N\)-th root has exactly the same measure, and the [torsion-difference factorization](Yin_C_G0901_v17.md#lifting) gives unit differences at every \(N\)-th root of unity since \(1-v\) is a unit.
Choose arbitrarily large \(N\) divisible by all the prescribed orders.
The inclusion of \(1\) among the tests gives the matching lower bound (1).
