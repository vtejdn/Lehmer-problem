# Simultaneous ramified degree congruences for the positive short-exponent-one family

<a id="statement"></a>
## Restrictions at all three consecutive exponents

Let \(q\ge3\), \(p=q+1\), \(L=2q+1\), and \(F(X)=X^v(qX-p)+pX-q\), with integer \(v>1\).
Suppose that \(F(z^g)=0\) for a nontorsion algebraic unit \(z\) and an integer \(g\ge1\).
Let \(D\) be the degree of the primitive polynomial's unique exterior root \(\alpha\).
The [signed pole theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#sign) gives \(v>L\), a reciprocal unit \(1<\alpha<p/q\), and a unit \(p-q\alpha\).
The even degree \(D\) satisfies
\[
     D<\frac{2v\log(1+1/q)}{\log(q(q+1))}<\frac{2v}{L}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The first inequality is the [trace degree bound](Yin_C_G0901_v110.md#trace); the second follows from \(L\log(1+1/q)<2+1/q\le7/3<\log12\).

For each \((N,c)=(v,L),(v-1,q),(v+1,p)\) and each prime \(\ell\mid c\), put \(a=v_\ell(N)\), \(b=v_\ell(c)\).
Use an odd prime if \(a>b\), or the prime two if \(a\ge b+2\), and put \(A=\ell^{a-b+1}\).
The following restrictions hold simultaneously.

| Prime and index | Permitted values of \(e\) |
| --- | --- |
| Odd \(\ell\), \(N=v\) | \(0,1\) |
| Odd \(\ell\), \(N=v-1\) or \(v+1\), with \(N\) odd | \(0,1\) |
| Odd \(\ell\), \(N=v-1\) or \(v+1\), with \(N\) even | \(0,1,2\) |
| \(\ell=2\), \(N=v-1\) or \(v+1\) | \(0,2\) |

For each eligible prime the degree obeys \(D\equiv-e\pmod A\) for a permitted value of \(e\), and \(D\ge A-1\) at odd primes or \(D\ge A-2\) at two.
In particular in **any** of the three rows \((N,c)\), if \(N\) is a power of a prime dividing \(c\), there is no nontorsion unit root at any dilation.
This includes all long prime powers supported at a prime of the odd coefficient sum, complementary to the [unramified prime-power condition](Yin_C_G0901_v110.md#cycles).
The restrictions also exclude mixed exponents by intersecting their degree conditions.

<a id="equations"></a>
## The three equations in a residue disk

Write \(E(x)=p-qx\).
At every conjugate \(x\) of \(\alpha\), the graph gives
\[
       x^v-1=L\frac{x-1}{E(x)},\qquad
       x^{v-1}-1=q\frac{x^2-1}{x E(x)},\qquad
       x^{v+1}-1=p\frac{x^2-1}{E(x)}.                                    \tag{2}
\]
All denominators here are units in every completion.
Thus the residue of each conjugate satisfies \(\bar x^m=1\) for \(m=N/\ell^a\).
Pass to a finite unramified local extension containing the \(m\)-th roots of unity, where each such residue has a unique lift \(\xi\) of order dividing \(m\).
Normalize the valuation by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
With \(x=\xi(1+y)\) each of these conjugates has \(v_\ell(y)>0\), and \(x^N=(1+y)^N\).
In the first row \(\ell\mid L\), the residue \(-1\) is excluded because \(E(-1)=L\) whereas \(E(x)\) is a unit.
At the other two rows the denominator is a unit at every permitted residue.

At odd primes, the numerator on the right of (2), after removing its integer factor \(c\), has a simple zero at \(\xi=1\), and also at \(\xi=-1\) in the last two rows when \(m\) is even.
These are the only such disks.
At two, only the last two rows occur; there the disk of one contains both exact roots \(x=1,-1\), and every other disk has nonzero numerator at its center modulo two.
In these statements zeros at \(-1\) occur only for even \(N\), when the last two associated parent equations indeed have that torsion root.
Below we remove the indicated exact common factors; the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) contains none of those roots.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Ramification degrees and the exceptional endpoint factors

First take an odd prime, or a disk away from the endpoints at two.
Let \(s=1\) at a simple endpoint and \(s=0\) in an ordinary disk.
After division by \(y^s\) the equation in that disk has polynomial form
\[
       U(y)\frac{(1+y)^N-1}{y^s}-\ell^b V(y)=0,                        \tag{3}
\]
where \(U,V\) have integral coefficients and unit constant terms over the unramified base.
For \(1\le k\le\ell^a\),
\[
                  v_\ell\binom Nk=a-v_\ell(k).
\]
Indeed \(\binom Nk=(N/k)\binom{N-1}{k-1}\), and the low coefficients through degree \(\ell^a-1\) of \((1+y)^{N-1}\bmod\ell\) agree with those of \((1+y)^{-1}\).
The constant in (3) has valuation \(b\) since \(a>b\).
The lower polygon with negative slopes starts at \((0,b)\) and passes through
\[
            (A\ell^j-s,b-1-j),\qquad 0\le j\le b-1.                     \tag{4}
\]
Before each listed binomial coefficient all earlier coefficients have strictly larger valuation, so multiplication by \(U\) preserves its valuation and the intermediate lower bounds.
The other polynomial in (3) has all coefficients divisible by \(\ell^b\) and gives the initial height.
After reaching height zero, all remaining coefficients are integral.
The successive horizontal lengths are \(A-s\) and \((\ell-1)A\ell^j\) for \(0\le j<b-1\), all with height drop one.
At \(\ell=2,s=0\) the first two slopes can coincide; this leaves their common reduced denominator \(A\) unchanged.

The [Newton polygon rule](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#ramification) counts the positive-valuation roots of (3) at each slope, with multiplicity.
It follows also by factoring over the local algebraic closure: the minimum weighted coefficient valuation at weight \(t>0\) is additive on products, so its breakpoints and changes of slope count root valuations and their multiplicities.
For a root of valuation \(1/h\) in lowest terms, its ramification index and hence its degree over the unramified base are multiples of \(h\).
In an ordinary disk all degrees of local factors containing such roots are consequently multiples of \(A\).
In a simple endpoint disk the first segment has precisely \(A-1\) roots of valuation \(1/(A-1)\).
They form one irreducible factor there, since each factor degree is a multiple of \(A-1\) and its roots all share the same valuation.
Every other contributing factor degree in that disk is divisible by \(A\).
Each exceptional factor is selected at most once by the separable minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
There are at most the one or two indicated disks, and summing the selected local degrees gives the odd-prime congruences and the lower bound.

It remains to compute the exceptional disk at two with \(a\ge b+2\).
Write \(x=1+y\), \(h=x^2-1=y(y+2)\) and \(N'=N/2\).
Both linear endpoint factors can be divided out, giving
\[
           U(y)\frac{(1+h)^{N'}-1}{h}-2^b V=0,                           \tag{5}
\]
with \(U(y)\) the unit denominator in (2) and \(V=c/2^b\) odd.
The constant in (5) has valuation \(b\), since \(v_2(N')=a-1>b\).
Consider \(0<t<1\) and assign \(y\) weight \(t\), taking the minimum of coefficient valuation plus monomial weight.
The initial term of \(h\) is uniquely \(y^2\), of weight \(2t\); the initial term of \(U\) is its unit constant.
Distinct powers of \(h\) have distinct leading monomial degrees, so even at ties their initial terms do not cancel.
The constant term of (5) coming from \(2^b V\) cannot cancel a positive-degree initial monomial.
The weighted minimum and its slopes therefore come from the polygon (4) with exponent valuation \(a-1\), \(s=1\), and doubled horizontal lengths.
The resulting first length is \(2(2^{a-b}-1)=A-2\), and the subsequent lengths are multiples of \(A\), each with height drop one.
All indicated root valuations are strictly below one.
For \(v_2(y)\ge1\) one has \(v_2(h)\ge2\), and the sum \(((1+h)^{N'}-1)/h\) has valuation \(a-1\): its first coefficient is \(N'\), with each higher term strictly more divisible, by the same binomial bound or repeated squaring.
Thus there are no roots there after dividing out the endpoints.
The first segment contributes exactly one irreducible local factor of degree \(A-2\); this yields the two dyadic alternatives and the lower bound.

<a id="exclusions"></a>
## Prime-power exclusions and simultaneous incompatible degrees

When \(N=\ell^a\) and \(\ell\mid c\), the ramified test always applies for \(v>L\).
For \(c=L\) one has \(N>L\ge\ell^b\) with \(\ell\) odd.
For \(c=q,p\) one has \(N\ge2c\) and indeed \(N>2c\) from \(v>L\) in both endpoint rows.
Thus at two \(a\ge b+2\), and at odd primes \(a>b\).
In the first row \(D\ge A-1\ge3v/L-1>2v/L\), contradicting (1).
In the endpoint rows one always has \(D\ge 2N/c-2\).
At \(v=L\) this last expression equals \(2v/L=2\) for each corresponding \(N,c\), and its slope in \(v\) is \(2/c>2/L\).
It too contradicts (1) at every \(v>L\).

For \(q=3,v=784\), (1) in its logarithmic form gives \(D<196\), since \((4/3)^8<12\).
The prime seven at \(N=784=16\cdot49\) requires \(D\equiv0,-1\pmod{49}\); the only positive even possibilities below 196 are \(48,98,146\).
At \(N=783=27\cdot29\) the prime three requires \(D\equiv0,-1\pmod{27}\), excluding all three.
Neither of these indices is a prime power.

For arbitrary \(q\), the explicit family \(v=q(q+1)(1+(2q+1)j)\), \(j\ge1\), has \(\gcd(v,L)=\gcd(v-1,q)=\gcd(v+1,p)=1\).
It meets no prime in the tests (3)–(5).
The [inertia criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v97.md#statement) gives a necessary symmetric label action on this family.
Neither the congruences nor that action decides whether it supplies a unit, and the positive branch at unbounded coefficients remains open.
