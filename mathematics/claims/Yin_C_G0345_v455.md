# Three measure cutoffs excluding a third qualifying value through prime 503

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree thresholds and the next possible prime

For a nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(1<M(P)\le T\), the degree of \(P\) satisfies the following bounds.
For an algebraic unit \(c\) of measure in the same interval, each bound also holds for \([\mathbb Q(c^k):\mathbb Q]\) for every positive integer \(k\).

| \(T\) | 1.04902 | 1.04116 | 1.02769 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Minimum required degree | 88 | 90 | 94 |

For \(r\) primitive irreducible integer factors of positive logarithmic measures with sum less than \((\log2)/4\), their total degree is at least 442 for \(r=5\) and at least \(112r-122\) for \(r\ge6\).
In the [signed prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) and the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), every passing row at either sign has at most two distinct positive qualifying values at each prime through 503.
At the next prime 509 the sixteen ordered degree triples in the [table below](#primes) are necessary possibilities for three distinct qualifying values.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Individual degrees and power fields

For each cutoff in the table the [small-measure theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree) reduces the problem to a monic reciprocal irreducible noncyclotomic factor of even degree at least 86.
The [new censuses](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v15/readme.md#census) give strict lower cutoffs \(1.04902,1.04116,1.02769\) in the individual degrees \(86,88,92\), respectively.
They cover 70,296,444 sign and substitution representatives.
Every one of the 2,198 polynomials reaching final division has a [verified cyclotomic factor](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v15/readme.md#scope).
At \(T=1.04902\) this excludes 86, and at \(T=1.04116\) it excludes 86 and 88.
At \(T=1.02769\) it excludes 86, 88 and 92, while the [strict cutoff 1.036 in degree 90](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#degree) excludes the only intervening even degree.
The resulting bounds on an irreducible factor imply the polynomial bounds by multiplicativity.
The [relative norm](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree) from a unit's field to its positive power field has measure in \((1,M(c)]\), giving the asserted degree bound for the latter field.

<a id="sums"></a>
## Degree under a fixed total measure budget

Use the [degree cutoff table and deficit argument](Yin_C_G0345_v454.md#sums), replacing \(f(86),f(88),f(92)\) by the new three cutoffs.
When the total degree is at most \(112r\), all factors in that argument have even degree at least 56.
At most eight have degree below 112 because each such factor has multiplicative measure above 1.02 and \(1.02^{36}>2\).
For \(s\) such degrees the largest sum \(\sum(112-d)\) compatible with the necessary cutoff product \(\prod f(d)<2^{1/4}\) is given below.

| \(s\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Largest deficit | 0 | 56 | 98 | 110 | 116 | 118 | 122 | 114 | 98 |

To obtain the table, start with the empty multiset and append each even \(d\) from the last degree (initially 56) to 110, discarding the extension if the fourth power of its cutoff product reaches two.
Each sorted multiset is reached once, and each extension increases the product.
The [exact integer comparisons](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v15/check_results.py) give the displayed maxima.
Taking the greatest deficit over \(s\le r\) proves the total-degree bounds, which also hold when the degree already exceeds \(112r\).

<a id="primes"></a>
## The strict budget at primes 503 and 509

The [preceding prime bound](Yin_C_G0345_v454.md#primes) covers the primes through 499 and lists four possible triples at 503.
At \((p,b)=(503,232),(509,234)\), the inequalities \((b-1)^8<p^7<b^8\) give the widths.
The orders are two and \(2b\le p+1\) in both cases.
Hence the [prime-width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement) gives \(i+j+k\le b\) for a triple in increasing order of measure.
By the [sorted degree estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#sums), \(i\ge86,\ j\ge68,\ k\ge56\), because \(b<3\cdot112\).
All these degrees are even, and the greatest possible single degree is at most 110.
Use the cutoff table \(f\) in the [deficit calculation](#sums).
Put \(z=f(i)\max(f(i),f(j))\max(f(i),f(j),f(k))\).
The [strict derivative budget](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) requires
\[
 z^{4p}(2pb+1)^4<2^p(pb+1)^4 .
\]
Testing every even triple satisfying the degree restrictions by [integer powers](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v15/check_results.py) leaves [none at 503](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/inputs/versions/v15/prime503.json) and the following [sixteen at 509](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/inputs/versions/v15/prime509.json).

| First degree \(i\) | Possible ordered pairs \((j,k)\) |
| ---: | --- |
| 86 | \((74,72),(74,74),(76,72)\) |
| 88 | \((72,72),(72,74),(74,72)\) |
| 90 | \((72,72),(74,70),(88,56)\) |
| 92 | \((72,68),(72,70),(74,68),(86,56)\) |
| 94 | \((72,68)\) |
| 100 | \((74,60)\) |
| 102 | \((74,58)\) |

Three triples, \((86,74,72),(88,72,72),(92,72,68)\), have total degree 232.
The other thirteen have total degree 234, so the same width criterion forces the primitive row modulo 509 to be a nonzero scalar multiple of the product of the three reduced minima.
For the three triples of total degree 232 the quotient has degree at most two.
The existence of a passing row with three distinct values at 509 remains open.

The computation contributor for Sheng Yin obtained the censuses and the degree and prime consequences for the [unrestricted Lehmer investigation](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
