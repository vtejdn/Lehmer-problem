# Cyclotomic boundary logarithms controlled by prime valuations and dyadic intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## A norm comparison for exponents separated by valuations

Let \(D\) be a nonempty finite set of positive integers and \(\mathcal P\) a set of \(R\ge0\) primes.
Define \(j(d)=\lfloor\log_2 d\rfloor\) and \(\mathbf v(d)=(v_p(d))_{p\in\mathcal P}\).
Let \(W\) be the maximum number of elements of \(D\) with a prescribed pair \((j(d),\mathbf v(d))\).
For real \(b_d\), normalized angular measure \(\nu=d\theta/(2\pi)\) and
\[
          L(\theta)=\sum_{d\in D}b_d\log|1-e^{id\theta}|,
          \qquad \|b\|_2^2=\sum_{d\in D}b_d^2 ,
\]
there is an absolute \(C>1\) such that
\[
  C^{-(R+1)}W^{-5/2}\|b\|_2
        \ \le\ \|L\|_1\ \le\ \|L\|_4
        \ \le\ C^{R+1}W^{1/2}\|b\|_2 .                         \tag{1}
\]
The selected primes, valuation vectors, exponents and dyadic intervals may all vary.
In particular, separating the elements of \(D\) by \(R\) prime valuations alone gives \(W=1\).
This applies to every \(D\) lying in one multiplicative coset of a subgroup of \(\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\) of rank at most \(R\), uniformly over the subgroup and coset.
Taking no primes recovers the [dyadic occupancy bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#statement).
For example the exponents \(2^j q_j\), for distinct \(j\ge1\) and distinct odd primes \(q_j\), are separated by \(v_2\), although their ratios may generate groups of unbounded rank.

Suppose that \(b_d\) are nonzero integers and \(A=\pm\prod_{d\in D}(1-X^d)^{b_d}\) is a nonconstant polynomial.
For fixed \(R,W\), the positive logarithmic Mahler measures \(m(A+aX^k)\) have a uniform gap for nonzero integer \(a\) and integer \(k\ge0\).
They tend to infinity with \(S=\sum_{d\in D}|b_d|\), also for complex \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\).
The same conclusions hold under a fixed bound on the product doubling \(|DD|/|D|\) of the exponent support, where \(DD=\{de:d,e\in D\}\).
Thus bounded \(\|L\|_1\) and \(S\to\infty\) force \(|DD|/|D|\to\infty\), and for every fixed \(R\) even the minimum possible \(W\) over choices of \(R\) primes tends to infinity.

<a id="sixth"></a>
## The sixth moment first on valuation fibers and then on dyadic intervals

Let \(B(\theta)=\sum_{d\in D} b_d e^{id\theta}\).
In the expansion of \(\|B\|_6^6\), replacing every coefficient by its modulus bounds the sum over \(d_1+d_2+d_3=d_4+d_5+d_6\) from above.
Work with these nonnegative coefficients in this paragraph.
For one prime \(p\), split \(B=\sum_j B_j\) according to \(v_p(d)=j\).
Every contributing equality has two positions tied for its least valuation, by reduction modulo \(p\) after dividing by the common least power.
Counting over all \(15\) unordered pairs and dropping the minimum requirement gives an upper bound, since every tuple has nonnegative weight.
For a fixed pair and shared valuation \(j\), the corresponding integral in absolute value is at most \(\int |B_j|^2|B|^4\,d\nu\), including pairs on the same side of the equality.
Hölder and division by \(\|B\|_6^4\) when positive give \(\|B\|_6^2\le15\sum_j\|B_j\|_6^2\).
Iterating over the selected primes gives \(15^R\) times the sum of the squared sixth norms on the joint valuation fibers.
This is Chang's [prime-valuation inequality](../audits/literature/Cha03/Yin_U001.md#valuation-estimate), with the [minimum-tie count](../audits/literature/Cha03/Yin_U001.md#minimum-ties) giving its needed inequality directly.

On each terminal fiber \(E\), at most \(W\) frequencies lie in any dyadic interval.
In a contributing six-tuple at least one of the other five frequencies is at least one fifth of the largest, since their sum bounds the largest.
Choose ordered positions for these two entries, with at most \(30\) choices, and enlarge their possible values to pairs \(u,v\in E\) with \(1/5\le u/v\le5\).
For fixed positions and values the sum of weights of all completing quadruples is at most \(|b_u b_v|\int |B_E|^4\,d\nu\) by Fourier coefficient extraction, where \(B_E=\sum_{d\in E}|b_d|e^{id\theta}\).
For each \(u\), at most \(O(W)\) values \(v\) are comparable in this range, by covering \([u/5,5u]\) by boundedly many standard dyadic intervals.
The pair condition is symmetric, so \(2|b_u b_v|\le |b_u|^2+|b_v|^2\) bounds the sum of these pair weights by \(O(W)\sum_{d\in E}|b_d|^2\).
Using \(\|B_E\|_4\le\|B_E\|_6\) and dividing the resulting sixth-moment bound proves \(\|B_E\|_6^2\ll W\sum_{d\in E}|b_d|^2\).
Summing on the valuation fibers yields, for the original coefficients of either sign,
\[
                   \|B\|_6\ll 15^{R/2}\sqrt W\,\|b\|_2 .      \tag{2}
\]

<a id="gram"></a>
## A summable arithmetic bound for each row of the Gram matrix

For \(d,e\in D\), write \(e/d=u/v\) in lowest terms and \(Z(d,e)=uv=de/\gcd(d,e)^2\).
This number is at least \(\max(d/e,e/d)\ge 2^{|j(d)-j(e)|-1}\).
It is also at least \(\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{|v_p(d)-v_p(e)|}\).
Dividing the decay between these two bounds gives
\[
 Z(d,e)^{-1/2}\le 2^{1/4}2^{-|j(d)-j(e)|/4}
                      \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}2^{-|v_p(d)-v_p(e)|/4}.
\]
For fixed \(d\), each joint cell contains at most \(W\) values of \(e\).
Summing all \(R+1\) differences over \(\mathbb Z\) consequently gives an absolute \(C_1>1\) such that
\[
                \sup_{d\in D}\sum_{e\in D}Z(d,e)^{-1/2}
                           \le C_1^{R+1}W .                       \tag{3}
\]
The [Möbius inversion matrix](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#inversion) expresses \(b_d\) in the positive Fourier coefficients of \(L\) with entries \(-2\mu(d/q)q/d\) at \(q\mid d\).
The absolute values in its row Gram matrix are bounded by \(4\zeta(2)Z(d,e)^{-1}\), since \(\sum_{q\mid\gcd(d,e)}q^2\le\zeta(2)\gcd(d,e)^2\).
Use \(Z^{-1}\le Z^{-1/2}\), (3), symmetry and the Schur norm bound, then Parseval, to obtain
\[
                  \|b\|_2 \le C_2^{R+1}\sqrt W\,\|L\|_2 .      \tag{4}
\]

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Summing the logarithmic harmonics

For \(H=2^s\), \(s\ge0\), put
\[
             T_H(\theta)=\sum_{H\le k<2H}k^{-1} B(k\theta).
\]
Dilation preserves the circle norm, and the sum of the weights in this band is at most one, so (2) bounds \(\|T_H\|_6\) uniformly in \(H\).
For its squared \(L^2\) norm, a matrix entry counts equalities \(kd=le\) with \(H\le k,l<2H\), weighting each by \(1/(kl)\).
With \(e/d=u/v\) in lowest terms, these have \(k=ut,l=vt\), so there are at most \(2H/\max(u,v)\) possibilities for the positive integer \(t\).
Each weight is at most \(H^{-2}\), giving an entry at most \(2/(H\sqrt{Z(d,e)})\).
Using (3) once more yields \(\|T_H\|_2\le C_3^{R+1}W^{1/2}H^{-1/2}\|b\|_2\).
Interpolation with the sixth norm gives
\[
       \|T_H\|_4\le \|T_H\|_2^{1/4}\|T_H\|_6^{3/4}
                \le C_4^{R+1}W^{1/2}H^{-1/8}\|b\|_2 .
\]
This is summable over dyadic \(H\).
The negative real part of \(\sum_H T_H\) equals \(L\), by the [Fourier coefficients of the circle logarithm](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#fourier) and uniqueness in \(L^2\).
Thus \(\|L\|_4\le C_5^{R+1}W^{1/2}\|b\|_2\).
Finally \(\|L\|_2^3\le\|L\|_1\|L\|_4^2\), which combines with (4) to give (1) after enlarging the absolute constant.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Multiplicative dimension, product doubling and the shifted gap

For \(d_0\in D\), the subgroup generated by \(d/d_0\) for \(d\in D\) has rank equal to the [multiplicative dimension of \(D\)](../audits/literature/Cha03/Yin_U001.md#dimension).
Indeed the prime valuation vectors of those ratios span the translate of the affine span through the origin and have integer coordinates, so their real span dimension equals their rational rank.
If the rank is \(m\), a nonzero \(m\)-row minor of a basis matrix selects \(m\) prime coordinates inducing an injection on that span, hence on \(D\).
This proves the coset specialization with \(W=1\), including the singleton case of rank zero.
If \(|DD|\le K|D|\), take \(\alpha=K+1\).
For \(|D|>\alpha^2\), Chang's [dimension bound from product doubling](../audits/literature/Cha03/Yin_U001.md#product-doubling) gives multiplicative dimension at most \(\alpha\).
For the smaller sets it is at most \(|D|-1\le\alpha^2\).
Thus the comparison is uniform in a fixed doubling bound.

For the polynomial products in the statement, \(L=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\) has mean zero and \(S\le\|b\|_2^2\) by integrality.
The [circle-root estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives
\[
          m(A+aX^k)\ge \tfrac12 C^{-(R+1)}W^{-5/2}\sqrt S
                                    -\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2).
\]
This proves the divergence assertion.
At bounded \(R,W\), if the full logarithmic measure is at most one then \(S\) is uniformly bounded.
As in the [binomial denominator argument](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#mahler), multiplying by \(\prod_{b_d<0}(1-X^d)^{-b_d}\) gives a bounded-term polynomial and preserves the measure.
The shifted polynomial is nonzero because \(A\) has a nonzero circle root.
For integer \(a\), the [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives the gap for every positive logarithmic measure in this range, and measures with logarithm greater than one already satisfy a gap.
By (1), bounded \(\|L\|_1\) and \(S\to\infty\) rule out bounded joint occupancy on any subsequence with at most \(R\) selected primes, even if they change with \(D\).
The rank and doubling implications above then exclude a subsequence of bounded product doubling as well.
