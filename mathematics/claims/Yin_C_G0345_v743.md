# Four-term tests on Salem conjugates determine a reciprocal equation and a limiting height

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exponent pattern and its critical scale

Fix \(C>0\) and \(0<\eta\le1\).
There are \(\epsilon_0>0\) and a finite set \(\mathcal B\) of totally real algebraic numbers greater than one, depending only on \(C,\eta\), with the following property.
Let \(\alpha_i\) be Salem numbers with \(m_i=\log\alpha_i\to0\) and degrees \(d_i\).
Take any nonzero complex Laurent polynomial with at most four nonzero terms at each \(i\).
Remove the lowest monomial power, normalize the coefficient Euclidean norm to one, and write \(Q_i\) for the result and \(W_i\) for its exponent span.
Suppose \(W_i m_i/d_i\le C\) and \(|Q_i(z)|\le\epsilon_0\) on at least \(\eta(d_i-2)\) neutral conjugates \(z\) of \(\alpha_i\).
Then eventually the actual support of \(Q_i\) has precisely the form
\[
                      \{0,a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i\},\qquad 0<a_i<b_i .
\]
There are \(B_i\in\mathcal B\cap\mathbb Q(\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1})\) and signs \(\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) such that
\[
  \alpha_i^{b_i}(\alpha_i^{a_i}-B_i)
                         +\epsilon_i(B_i\alpha_i^{a_i}-1)=0.           \tag{1}
\]
The given coefficients of \(Q_i\) are unrestricted.
Equation (1) is an exact relation on its original exponents.
Furthermore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
    |\alpha_i^{a_i}-B_i|&\ll_{C,\eta} e^{-b_i m_i},\\
                  a_i m_i-\log B_i&\longrightarrow0,\\
                  b_i m_i/d_i-h(B_i)&\longrightarrow0,\qquad
         \frac{b_i}{a_i d_i}-\frac{h(B_i)}{\log B_i}\longrightarrow0 .
 \end{aligned}                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Here \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height, including the finite-place part at a nonintegral seed.
In particular the limit points of \(W_i m_i/d_i\) belong to the finite set of heights of the seeds.
Conversely, if (1) holds at a Salem number \(\alpha\) and \([\mathbb Q(B):\mathbb Q]=e\) with \(B\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the polynomial in (1) vanishes at at least \(d/e-2\) neutral conjugates.
The criteria do not establish the existence of a shrinking sequence of such factors.

<a id="edges"></a>
## Two short end gaps in an exact relation

Suppress the index \(i\).
Bounded degree at bounded total Mahler measure allows only finitely many integer minimal polynomials, so \(d\to\infty\).
The monomial case is excluded by any \(\epsilon_0<1\).
At the neutral roots the unnormalized monomial evaluation row has Euclidean norm at least one.
For two to four terms apply the [critical exact relation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#statement) at density \(\eta/2\) of the full orbit, taking the minimum of its three positive thresholds and \(1/2\).
Indeed \((d-2)/d\ge1/2\) and \(Wh(\alpha)=Wm/d\le C\).
It gives a nonzero exact form on a subset of the stated exponents with coefficients in \(E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \([E:\mathbb Q]\le M_0\), from a finite list.
The coefficients and all of their Galois conjugates have uniform upper and positive lower bounds in modulus when nonzero.
All \(d/[E:\mathbb Q]\) conjugates fixing \(E\) satisfy that same form.
At least \(d/[E:\mathbb Q]-2\) of them are neutral.

With \(e_1<\cdots<e_s\) the surviving exponents, \(s\ge2\).
At the positive exterior root the largest term is bounded by the sum of the preceding absolute values, giving \((e_s-e_{s-1})m\ll_{C,\eta}1\).
The embedding sending \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) carries the coefficients to members of the same bounded collection of conjugates.
Apply the same comparison there to the two lowest exponents to get \((e_2-e_1)m\ll_{C,\eta}1\).
If \(s\le3\), the entire surviving span times \(m/d\) tends to zero.
The [small-value bound at subcritical span](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement), after shifting the lowest exponent to zero and normalizing the coefficient vector, excludes its exact vanishing on a fixed positive fraction of the original neutral roots.
This contradiction applies on any subsequence with \(s\le3\).
The same test rules out \(Wm/d\to0\) on any subsequence, so \(\liminf Wm/d>0\).

Consequently all four original exponents survive for large indices and their form can be written
\[
                   u+vX^a+X^b(w+tX^{a'}),\qquad
       a<a_3=b,\quad a'=W-b,\quad (a+a')m\ll_{C,\eta}1 ,
\]
with all four coefficients nonzero and from a finite set.
Here \(a_3\) denotes the third exponent counting zero as the first.
In particular \(bm\to\infty\).

<a id="reflection"></a>
## An identity for the two end binomials

At every neutral root \(z\) in the set fixing \(E\),
\[
                      |u+vz^a|^2-|w+tz^{a'}|^2=0 .
\]
Writing conjugates of powers of \(z\) as inverse powers gives a Laurent polynomial with at most five terms and span at most \(2\max(a,a')\).
This span times \(m/d\) tends to zero.
If the polynomial were nonzero on an infinite subsequence, division by its lowest monomial and its coefficient norm would preserve all these exact zeros and again contradict [the subcritical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement).
Thus there is an identity of Laurent polynomials for all sufficiently large indices.
Comparing nonconstant coefficients gives \(a=a'\) and \(\bar u v=\bar w t\); the constant coefficient gives \(|u|^2+|v|^2=|w|^2+|t|^2\).
The unordered pairs of moduli coincide because their products and squared sums agree.

If \(|w|=|u|\), then \((w,t)=\lambda(u,v)\), \(|\lambda|=1\).
The exact relation factors as \((u+vX^a)(1+\lambda X^b)=0\) at \(\alpha\).
The second factor cannot vanish there because \(\alpha^b>1\), leaving \(\alpha^a=-u/v\) in a finite set.
Every power of a Salem number retains its degree: two distinct conjugates with equal positive powers could be sent by one Galois automorphism to \(\alpha\) and to a different conjugate of the same modulus, contrary to its unique exterior root.
Thus the finite set contradicts \(d\to\infty\).

In the other case \(|w|=|v|\ne|u|\) and \((w,t)=\lambda(\bar v,\bar u)\), \(|\lambda|=1\), by the equality of complex cross coefficients.
Put \(\xi=-\bar v/\bar u\) and \(\gamma=\lambda\bar u/u\).
At \(\alpha\) the relation divided by \(u\) gives
\[
            1-\bar\xi\alpha^a+\gamma\alpha^b(\alpha^a-\xi)=0 .
\]
The number \(\alpha^a\) is bounded by the end-gap estimate, hence \(|\alpha^a-\xi|\ll_{C,\eta}e^{-bm}\to0\).
Since the choices of \(\xi\) are finite and \(\alpha^a>1\), eventually \(\xi\) is real and at least one.
The inequality of the two moduli excludes one.
Write \(B=\xi>1\).
The quantity \(\alpha^a-B\) is nonzero by the last displayed equation and \(B^2\ne1\).
That same equation now gives \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), so \(\gamma=\pm1\), yielding (1) with \(\epsilon=-\gamma\).
It also gives the first two limits or estimates of (2).
Moreover
\[
                         B=\frac{\alpha^{a+b}-\epsilon}
                                    {\alpha^b-\epsilon\alpha^a}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero since \(b>a\).
This expression is unchanged on replacing \(\alpha\) by \(\alpha^{-1}\), so \(B\) belongs to the trace field, the fixed field of that involution in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
All conjugates of the trace generator \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) are real.
Thus the seeds are totally real and belong to the claimed finite list.
The converse follows by applying the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) fixing \(B\).

<a id="height"></a>
## The height of the power at the longer exponent

To obtain a convergent difference for the finite set of possible seeds, it suffices to work on each subsequence with a fixed \(B\).
Put \(F=\mathbb Q(B)\), \(X=\alpha^a\), \(Y=\alpha^b\).
Then \(X\) is an algebraic unit of absolute height \(am/d\to0\) and degree \(d\), and \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(X)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Equation (1) gives \(Y=\epsilon(1-BX)/(X-B)\).
By the product formula for \(X-B\),
\[
 h(Y)=\sum_w d_w\log\max(|1-BX|_w,|X-B|_w),
 \qquad d_w=[\mathbb Q(\alpha)_w:\mathbb Q_v]/d ,
\]
with \(v\) the place of \(\mathbb Q\) below \(w\) and \(|p|_w=p^{-1}\).

At finite places with \(|B|_w\ne1\), the maximum in the sum equals \(\max(1,|B|_w)\) since \(|X|_w=1\).
Where \(|B|_w=1\), the maximum equals \(\max(|X-B|_w,|1-B^2|_w)\) because \(1-BX=1-B^2-B(X-B)\).
It lies between \(|X^2-1|_w\) and one, using \(X^2-1=(X-B)(X+B)+B^2-1\).
The magnitude of the total negative contribution is therefore at most the [normalized finite-place cyclic norm](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) \(L_1(X^2)\).
This tends to zero since \(X^2\) is a nontorsion unit of height tending to zero.
The finite-place sum tends to the finite-place part of \(h(B)\).

For each real embedding \(\tau:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\), the conjugates of \(X\) above \(\tau\) have the full orbit law over \(\tau F\) after extending \(\tau\) to an automorphism of the algebraic closure.
Their normalized complex counting measures tend to Haar measure on the unit circle by [small-point equidistribution over a fixed number field](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution), applied to the map \(z\mapsto z^2\).
Its canonical height is \(h\), and normalized angular measure satisfies the canonical pullback identity for this map.
Degrees tending to infinity suffice to apply the distinct-point theorem here: every subsequence has a further subsequence of pairwise distinct points.
All conjugates of \(X\) stay in a fixed compact disk by \(am\ll_{C,\eta}1\).
The function \(\log\max(|1-\tau(B)z|,|z-\tau(B)|)\) is continuous there since \(\tau(B)^2\ne1\), and extends from that disk to a bounded continuous test function on the sphere.
On the unit circle both moduli are \(|z-\tau(B)|\), since \(\tau(B)\) is real.
The circle mean of its logarithm is \(\log^+|\tau(B)|\) by the elementary linear-factor mean formula.
Each \(\tau\) occurs with the same number of embeddings in the full orbit, so the archimedean sum tends to the archimedean part of \(h(B)\).

Combining the two sums yields \(bm/d=h(Y)=h(B)+o(1)\).
Division by \(am=\log B+o(1)\) is legitimate since the finite set contains only numbers strictly greater than one.
This proves (2), and \(am/d\to0\) gives the assertion for the full span.
