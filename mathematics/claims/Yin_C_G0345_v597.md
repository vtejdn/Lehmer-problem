# Factorially many primitive terminal lists with one rational coefficient tuple

<a id="statement"></a>
## Permuting representations of the same field element

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) such that each positive power has degree \(d\).
Suppose that it has a conjugate on the unit circle or has two exterior conjugates of distinct absolute values.
For every \(n\ge2\), put \(p=d+2\) and \(r=pn+1\).
There is one fixed ordered tuple of \(pn\) nonzero rational integers and one fixed nonzero integer output at exponent zero admitting at least \(n!\) distinct primitive marked shapes of sorted positive exponents, each realized by a fully [rational-terminal relation](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
In particular the unrestricted coefficient and weight count at each such fixed unit exceeds \(A^r\) for every fixed \(A\) at sufficiently large weights in this progression.
Every Salem number satisfies the hypotheses by [power-degree preservation](Yin_C_G0345_v591.md#trace).
All sufficiently late members of any hypothetical sequence of reciprocal strict degree records of shrinking measure also satisfy them by [finite power degrees](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm) and the [two-circle exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v587.md#statement).
This gives an obstruction even when the upper bound under consideration imposes full rational terminality, fixes the entire coefficient tuple including the output and counts only primitive shapes.
The coefficients and exponents below have no prescribed height or span, and the construction allows weights arbitrarily large relative to the input degree.
Bounds restricted to the actual [height, exponent and weight ranges of the witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement) remain open.

<a id="jobs"></a>
## A sum of widely spaced powers with no rational-power partial ratio

Write \(N=n-1\), \(J=\{0,\ldots,N\}\), \(S_I=\sum_{j\in I}\alpha^{qj}\) and \(S=S_J\).
We choose a positive even integer \(q\) such that \(S\ne0\) and, for every proper nonempty \(I\subset J\), the ratio \(R_I=S_I/S\) is nonzero and belongs to none of the lines \(\mathbb Q\alpha^h\), \(h\in\mathbb Z\).
At any fixed exterior conjugate of modulus \(a>1\), evaluation gives \(|R_I|=a^{q(\max I-N)}(1+o(1))\) as \(q\to\infty\), by unique dominance of the highest power in each sum.
At the inverse conjugate it gives \(|R_I|=a^{-q\min I}(1+o(1))\) by dominance of the lowest power.
These estimates in particular exclude zero numerator and denominator at all sufficiently large \(q\).

If two distinct exterior moduli \(a,b\) occur and \(R_I=c\alpha^h\) for rational \(c\ne0\), division of the two exterior absolute values gives \((h-q(\max I-N))\log(a/b)=o(1)\).
The difference is an integer and hence zero for all sufficiently large such \(q\).
Using the two inverse conjugates similarly gives \(h=q\min I\).
Thus \(h=0\), \(\max I=N\) and \(\min I=0\).
But the nonempty complement would also have rational ratio \(1-c\), which by the same reasoning requires both endpoints.

If a neutral conjugate \(z\) occurs, take an unbounded sequence of even \(q\) with \(z^q\to1\), available by compactness of the unit circle (take differences of widely separated convergent powers of \(z^2\)).
At this embedding \(|R_I|\to |I|/n\).
If \(R_I=c\alpha^h\), the product of the absolute values at an exterior conjugate and its inverse equals \(|c|^2\).
The displayed asymptotics give \(|c|^2=a^{q(\max I-\min I-N)}(1+o(1))\), so it approaches zero if the span is smaller than \(N\), and one otherwise.
At the neutral embedding it must instead approach \((|I|/n)^2\), strictly between zero and one.
Finiteness of the subsets makes one \(q\) sufficient in either case.

<a id="clusters"></a>
## Representations with two independent rational parameters

Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
By the [simultaneous independence lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v309.md#dilation) there is a positive integer \(D\) such that any \(d\) of the \(p\) powers \(1,\alpha^D,\ldots,\alpha^{(p-1)D}\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Choose positive integers \(L_1<\cdots<L_n\) separated by more than \(qN+(p-1)D\).
For each \(i\) independently choose rational coefficients \(b_{ik}\), \(0\le k<p\), solving
\[
                 \sum_{k=0}^{p-1} b_{ik}\alpha^{L_i+kD}=1/S.
\]
This is a nonempty rational affine plane because the columns span \(K\).
For any bijection \(\pi:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to J\) replace the exponents in group \(i\) by \(L_i+kD+q\pi(i)\).
Every permuted sum is exactly one, while all terms retain their sorted order and fixed coefficients.
The sums of all these exponents are identical and positive, so different permutations give different shapes after common gcd division.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## Keeping all the permuted sums terminal

Work in the product of the \(n\) affine planes and fix one permutation.
For any subset of outgoing terms take its partial sum as an affine map on this product.
In a group with \(s\) terms chosen, the rank of its linear part is \(\min(s,d)+\min(p-s,d)-d\), by the intersection-of-spans argument for the [partial sum map](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v309.md#avoidance).
The rank is zero at either empty or full selection, one for a singleton or its complement, and two otherwise.
In the rank-one case its direction is precisely the rational line of the singled-out power, after the permutation shift.
Powers at distinct positions determine distinct lines, since a nonzero rational power of a nontorsion unit would be a rational unit of infinite order.
A cut with any rank-two group, or at least two rank-one groups, consequently has image of dimension at least two.

If all groups are whole or empty the partial sum is \(R_I\); the choice of \(q\) excludes every forbidden line and zero for nonempty proper \(I\).
If precisely one group is rank one and it contributes just one term, the image is \(R_I+\mathbb Q\alpha^t\), where \(I\) consists of the whole jobs and \(t>0\) is the singled-out exponent.
For a cut with at least two outgoing terms, \(I\) is nonempty and proper, so the image is not contained in any forbidden line.
If instead the group contributes all but one term, take \(I\) to consist of its own job and all the whole jobs.
The image is again \(R_I+\mathbb Q\alpha^t\).
For proper \(I\) the same exclusion holds, while \(I=J\) has \(R_I=1\notin\mathbb Q\alpha^t\), since \(t>0\).
Thus for every outgoing subset of size between two and \(pn-1\) the image avoids containment in every forbidden line, and is not identically zero.
Complementation in the zero relation including the marked term \(-1\) reduces every prospective split to one of these outgoing cuts.
A singleton coefficient zero lies in a proper affine hyperplane because its coordinate varies in its group.
With singleton zeros removed, the same cuts and complementation exclude all proper zero subsums.

Choose rational base points and rational bases for the plane directions, letting each of the \(2n\) parameters run through integers from \(-H\) to \(H\).
One common positive integer clears the denominators of every coefficient in all these parameter boxes.
After this common scaling all partial sums are algebraic integers with absolute logarithmic height \(O(\log(2H))\) since each conjugate is an affine function of the parameters with fixed coefficients.
If such a nonzero sum is \(c\alpha^h\) with rational \(c\), its norm is \(\pm c^d\), giving \(h_{\rm Weil}(c)\le h_{\rm Weil}(c\alpha^h)\) and hence \(|h|h_{\rm Weil}(\alpha)\le2h_{\rm Weil}(c\alpha^h)\).
There are only \(O(\log(2H))\) possible carrier exponents across the fixed cuts and permutations.
Each preimage of a forbidden line, including zero, is empty or lies in a proper rational affine hyperplane by the image analysis; scaling by a rational number does not affect this conclusion.
Each hyperplane removes at most \((2H+1)^{2n-1}\) box points, so the union of all exceptions for all permutations misses some point when \(H\) is sufficiently large.
Its scaled coefficients and its common scaled integer output establish the assertion.
