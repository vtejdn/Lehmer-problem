# Sublinear local factors at prime periods and simultaneous trace congruences

<a id="statement"></a>
## What trace evaluations can distinguish at a moving prime

Let \(q>3\) be prime, \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)\), and \(h=(q-1)/f\).
For odd \(0<k<q\) put \(l=q-k\) and \(P_l=X^{2q}-2X^{q+l}+X^q-2X^{q-l}+1\).
At binary order \(3q\) there are \(h\) distinct irreducible trace factors of degree \(f\).
Each gives a reciprocal divisor of degree \(2f\) in the original variable over \(\mathbb Z_2\).

Consider any sequence of primes along which \(h\to\infty\), and put \(j=\lfloor(\log_2 h/8)^{1/4}\rfloor\).
For every sufficiently large member, choose any list of at most \(j\) admissible offsets.
In any prescribed pool of \(\lfloor h/j\rfloor\) of the \(h\) trace factors one can select all but at most \(h^{1/4}\) of them so that the following holds at **every offset in the list with the same binary selection**.
Writing \(S_k=X^{d/2}T_k(X+X^{-1})\) for its unique monic dyadic lift at that offset, one has
\[
 \begin{split}
     d&=(2+o(1))q/j=o(q),\\
     T_k(y+Z)&\equiv 1\pmod{(2^j,Z^j)}
                                     \qquad\text{for every }y\in\mathbb Z_2 .
 \end{split}                                                            \tag{1}
\]
In particular all integer evaluations have value one modulo \(2^j\), and their Taylor coefficients of orders one through \(j-1\) vanish at that precision.
The best numerical lower bound given by **all** periods and doublings in the binary resultant inequality for this reduction is exactly \(d\log2/(6q)\).
These candidates exceed the [necessary near-linear degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#orders) and reach the maximal possible order \(3q\).

There are infinitely many eligible primes with \(h\to\infty\).
One may include two offsets \(k_1\sim q/j^3\) and \(k_2\sim q/j\) in (1).
At \(k_2\) this entire local selection cannot be rational, whereas at \(k_1\) it satisfies both the trace congruences in (1) and the [two-scale rational degree restriction](Yin_C_G0345_v247.md#five).
Rationality at \(k_1\) is open here.
Thus these finite-precision comparisons can hold in the sublinear degree range relevant to a shrinking sequence.

<a id="labels"></a>
## Local factors and primes with many labels

Let \(\eta\) be a primitive root of order \(3q\) in characteristic two.
Its trace \(\eta+\eta^{-1}\) has degree equal to the least \(f>0\) such that \(2^f\equiv\pm1\bmod 3q\).
Indeed two roots have the same trace precisely when they are equal or inverse.
Modulo three the sign of \(2^f\) is forced to be \((-1)^f\), so this condition is \((-2)^f\equiv1\bmod q\).
There are \(\varphi(3q)/2=q-1\) such traces, whence the stated number \(h\).
In particular \(q\le2^f+1\), so \(f\ge\log_2(q-1)\).
The \(h\) Frobenius orbits on traces are equivalently the cosets of \(\langle-2\rangle\) in \(\mathbb F_q^*\).
The number of Frobenius orbits on the original primitive roots is \(h\) or \(2h\), according as individual orbits are or are not stable under inversion.
Thus \(h\to\infty\) is exactly the [necessary growth of the top orbit count](Yin_C_G0345_v258.md#five) for this prime-index family.
The binary parent is squarefree and divisible by all these factors on the original roots.
Their [reciprocal Hensel lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) have constant term one and degree \(2f\), and hence monic trace polynomials in \(\mathbb Z_2[Y]\).
Their trace reductions are the distinct irreducibles of degree \(f\) just described.

To see the prime supply directly, fix an integer \(H\ge2\).
The splitting field of \(U^H+2\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is a finite Galois extension generated by an integral primitive element \(\theta\), with monic integral minimal polynomial \(A\).
There are infinitely many primes dividing some nonzero \(A(m)\) for \(m\in\mathbb Z\).
After translating its argument assume \(A(0)=a\ne0\).
For any product \(M\) of finitely many primes, \(A(a Mz)/a\) is an integer congruent to one modulo \(M\) and has unbounded absolute value as \(z\) varies over the integers.
It has a prime divisor outside the list.
All roots of \(U^H+2\) are polynomials in \(\theta\) with rational coefficients.
Outside finitely many primes dividing the common denominators or the discriminant of \(U^H+2\), a root of \(A\bmod q\) therefore specializes this factorization to a full set of distinct roots in \(\mathbb F_q\).
Their ratios give all \(H\)-th roots of unity, and \(-2\) is an \(H\)-th power there.
It follows that \(H\mid q-1\) and \(h\ge H\).
Arbitrarily large \(H\) give the required sequence.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Finite quotient rings at several offsets

More generally prescribe, at each of finitely many offsets \(k\), a precision \(b_k\ge1\) and a monic polynomial \(G_k(Y)\in\mathbb Z_2[Y]\) of positive degree \(s_k<f\).
Define
\[
              A_k=(\mathbb Z/2^{b_k}\mathbb Z)[Y]/(G_k).
\]
For each binary label record its lifted trace polynomial in \(A_k\) at the corresponding offset.
Its reduction modulo two is irreducible of degree \(f>s_k\), so it is coprime to the reduction of \(G_k\).
It is a unit in \(A_k\), since an inverse modulo two lifts across the nilpotent ideal generated by two.
The list of its values therefore belongs to the abelian group \(\prod_k A_k^*\), of order at most \(J=2^{\sum_k b_ks_k}\).
One can also attach a prescribed element of an additional finite abelian group to each label and multiply \(J\) by that group's order.

In any pool of labels one can discard fewer than \(J\) and make the product of the remaining labels equal the identity in this group.
Indeed among any \(J\) elements there is a nonempty subsequence with product one, by comparing the \(J+1\) initial products including the identity.
Remove such products from the pool and keep them as the chosen selection until fewer than \(J\) labels remain.
The union of the chosen subsequences has product one since the group is abelian.
At each offset the product of the corresponding lifted traces is exactly the trace of the lifted reciprocal selection, by monicity, multiplicativity of trace form and uniqueness of the Hensel divisor.
Thus \(T_k\equiv1\bmod(2^{b_k},G_k)\) for each prescribed pair.

For (1) use \(b_k=j\), let \(A(Y)=\prod_{i=0}^{2j-1}(Y-i)\), and take \(G_k(Y)=A(Y)^j\).
The degree is \(2j^2<f\) for all sufficiently large \(h\), using \(h\le q-1\) and the lower bound on \(f\).
There are at most \(j\) offsets, giving \(J\le2^{2j^4}\le h^{1/4}\).
Each chosen label contributes \(2f\) to the degree.
The discarded count is \(o(h/j)\), and hence \(d\sim2fh/j\sim2q/j\).
For every integer \(y\), at least \(j\) of the \(2j\) consecutive integers \(y-i\) are even, so \(A(y)\in2^j\mathbb Z\).
The same holds on \(\mathbb Z_2\) by reduction.
Every coefficient below degree \(j\) in \(A(y+Z)^j\) contains the constant term \(A(y)\) as a factor.
Consequently \(G_k(y+Z)\) vanishes modulo \((2^j,Z^j)\), which proves (1).

## Period comparisons and failure at the larger offset

The selected binary divisor consists entirely of distinct primitive roots of order \(3q\).
Its gcd degree with \(X^D-1\) equals \(d\) when \(3q\mid D\) and zero otherwise.
The [binary resultant expression](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement) has nonnegative supremum \(d\log 2/(6q)\), attained at \(D=3q\) and one doubling, since \(\max_{i\ge0} i/2^i=1/2\).
This computes the expression on the local reduction and does not assert the existence of an integral divisor with this measure.
Also \(j\ll(\log q)^{1/4}\), so \(d/[q(\log\log q/\log q)^3]\to\infty\).

Choose odd integers nearest \(q/j^3\) and \(q/j\); these are distinct admissible offsets for all sufficiently large \(h\).
At the smaller offset \(d/\sqrt{qk_1}\asymp\sqrt j\to\infty\).
At the larger \(d/\sqrt{qk_2}\asymp1/\sqrt j\to0\).
The degree lower bound for every genuine rational divisor therefore rules out a rational lift there.
Both local divisors still satisfy the same simultaneous finite tests.
