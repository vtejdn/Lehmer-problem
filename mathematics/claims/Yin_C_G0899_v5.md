# One common divisor of two integer values recovers the Mahler rate at prime orders

<a id="statement"></a>
## The largest modulus with a common integer root

Let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), with logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(Q)\).
For each odd prime \(p\) choose the following pair of monic integer polynomials \(T,S_p\).
If \(Q\) is nonreciprocal take \(T=Q,\ S_p=\Phi_p\), and put \(b=1\).
If \(Q\) is reciprocal put \(b=2\), take \(T\) minimal for \(\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and define \(S_p\) by \(z^{(p-1)/2}S_p(z+z^{-1})=\Phi_p(z)\).
The symmetric Laurent polynomial \(z^{-(p-1)/2}\Phi_p(z)\) is integral in \(z+z^{-1}\) by the recurrence for \(z^h+z^{-h}\).
Define
\[
        G_p(Q)=\max_{a\in\mathbb Z}\gcd\bigl(T(a),S_p(a)\bigr),
        \qquad B_p=|\operatorname{Res}(T,S_p)|.
\]
The gcd is positive and divides the nonzero \(B_p\) by the integral resultant identity.
For every fixed \(Q\), along **all** primes \(p\to\infty\),
\[
 \log(B_p/G_p(Q))=o_Q(p),\qquad
 \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log G_p(Q)}{p-1}=\frac{m(Q)}{b}.            \tag{1}
\]
A single integer \(0\le a<G_p(Q)\) attains the maximum, and every achievable gcd divides it.
All primes dividing it for large \(p\) are \(1\bmod p\) in the nonreciprocal case and \(\pm1\bmod p\) in the reciprocal case.
The estimate allows one to discard every higher residue degree and all but one congruence class of roots at each rational prime while retaining the full logarithmic rate.

The unrestricted Lehmer gap is equivalent to an absolute \(c>0\) such that each fixed nontorsion unit admits \(G_p(Q)\ge\exp(cp)\) for infinitely many prime orders.
Requiring this for every sufficiently large prime order for each base gives the same criterion.
One may require in the existence formulation that every prime factor of the common divisor exceed \(c_0 p\log p\), for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
The onset of (1) may depend on the base polynomial.

<a id="local"></a>
## Combining one local root at each characteristic

The absolute norm of \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\) is \(B_p^b\).
For \(b=2\), inversion gives a nontrivial automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and \(\alpha^2-\beta\alpha+1=0\) gives relative degree two.
The prefactor in the trace identity is a unit.
The fixed-base [signed resultant estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution), divided by the fixed nonzero \(|Q(1)|\) in the identity for \(\Phi_p\), gives
\[
                   \log B_p=(p-1)m(Q)/b+O_Q(\log(2p)).
\]

For every prime \(\ell\mid B_p\), let \(e_\ell\) be the largest \(e\ge0\) for which \(T,S_p\) have a common integer root modulo \(\ell^e\).
This exponent exists since any common divisor divides \(B_p\).
Choosing one maximizing class for each \(\ell\) and combining them by the Chinese remainder theorem gives \(G_p=\prod_\ell\ell^{e_\ell}\), with a representative in the stated interval.
Every prime divisor of \(G_p\) gives a common root over the prime field.
For \(p\) large one has \(p\nmid Q(1)\), so \(p\nmid B_p\).
In the nonreciprocal case this root itself has order \(p\).
In the reciprocal case the two roots with the given trace have order \(p\), and Frobenius fixes or interchanges them.
These alternatives give the asserted congruence classes.

Fix a Galois splitting field \(L\) of \(Q\) of degree \(d_L\).
For all large \(p\), every \(\ell\mid B_p\) avoids the discriminants of \(L,Q,T\).
Indeed for each of the finitely many exceptional characteristics the reduction of each unit conjugate has a fixed finite order; only that order can equal \(p\) away from characteristic \(p\).
The characteristic-\(p\) exception was already excluded.
Write \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n\) for the roots of \(T\).
For \(\mathfrak P\mid\ell\) in \(L\), put \(w_i=v_{\mathfrak P}(S_p(\tau_i))\) with integral valuation.
At these unramified primes \(v_\ell(B_p)=\sum_i w_i\).
Frobenius permutes the indices preserving their weights.
Each index fixed by Frobenius has \(\tau_i\in\mathbb Z_\ell\), as the local decomposition group is generated by Frobenius.
Approximating it modulo \(\ell^{w_i}\) by an integer gives \(e_\ell\ge w_i\).
If a maximal positive weight is unique it is fixed; the remaining weights are bounded in sum by the pairwise minima involving this maximum.
If the maximum occurs at least twice, twice the sum of all pairwise minima already bounds the full sum of the weights.
Consequently
\[
 \log(B_p/G_p)\le
  \frac{2}{d_L}\sum_{i<j}\sum_{\mathfrak P}
    \min(v_{\mathfrak P}(S_p(\tau_i)),v_{\mathfrak P}(S_p(\tau_j)))
                      \log N\mathfrak P .                            \tag{2}
\]
Here summing over primes above \(\ell\) with weight \(\log N\mathfrak P/d_L\) recovers \(\log\ell\) at the unramified supports.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## The cost of two different roots at the same prime

For \(b=1\) set \(x_i=\tau_i\), and for \(b=2\) choose one conjugate \(x_i\) of \(\alpha\) from each inverse pair, with \(\tau_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\).
In both cases \((S_p(\tau_i))=(\Phi_p(x_i))\) as ideals in \(L\), and \(\Phi_p(x_i)\mid x_i^p-1\).
If \(x_i,x_j\) are multiplicatively independent, so are \(x_i^p,x_j^p\).
They are ordinary units in the fixed field, with equal absolute logarithmic heights \(p\,m(Q)/\deg Q\).
By [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2, inequality (2.8)](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), the normalized log norm of \((x_i^p-1,x_j^p-1)\) is \(o_Q(p)\).
Indeed any fixed positive multiple of their maximum height bounds this log norm apart from finitely many independent pairs, and the sequence of pairs is injective.
The finite-place contribution is bounded by the full place sum in the proposition at its stated normalization.
This bounds the corresponding term of (2).

For a dependent pair, equality of the positive heights of the conjugates gives \(x_j=\zeta x_i^\sigma\) with \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\) and a root of unity \(\zeta\), by taking heights in a dependence relation.
One has \(\zeta\ne1\): the conjugates are distinct, the reciprocal case selected only one element of each inverse pair, and a nonreciprocal irreducible unit polynomial cannot have two inverse roots.
For all primes sufficiently large for the fixed base, \(\zeta^p-1\ne0\).
The ideal generated by \(x_i^p-1,x_j^p-1\) contains \(\zeta^p-1\), using \(x_j^p=\zeta^p x_i^{\sigma p}\) and the fact that \(x_i\) is a unit.
The finitely many possible nonzero values of \(\zeta^p-1\) give a bounded log norm in this case.
There are only finitely many pairs for the fixed base.
Together with (2) and the full norm estimate this proves (1).

<a id="criterion"></a>
## A modulus criterion and the associated finite-order matrices

If the gap holds, (1) gives the uniform lower rate in the statement since \(b\le2\); the fixed-base error may require different starting primes for different inputs.
Conversely a lower bound on an infinite subsequence gives \(m(Q)\ge bc\) by (1).
It suffices to test \(0<m(Q)\le1\), since larger measures, nonunits and nonunit leading coefficients have absolute lower bounds and logarithmic measure adds across factors.
For the extra restriction on prime sizes, use the [screened prime orders](Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#statement) for each fixed bounded-measure base and arbitrarily large \(y\).
For a fixed base of any larger measure the same [order construction](Yin_C_G0899_v4.md#orders) applies directly: the integer determining the discarded primes, built from \(Q(1)\) and \(\operatorname{disc}Q\), is fixed while the smallest candidate prime tends to infinity.
Every prime factor of \(B_p\), hence also of \(G_p\), then meets the restriction.
This proves both directions with that additional requirement as well.

The common root gives an integral representation of the primitive orbit on a cyclic coefficient ring.
For \(G_p>1\) and large \(p\), take \(a\) attaining it and reduce modulo \(G_p\).
In the nonreciprocal case the scalar \(a\) satisfies \(Q(a)=\Phi_p(a)=0\) and has order \(p\), since \(\Phi_p(1)=p\) is a unit.
In the reciprocal case let
\[
                           A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(A+A^{-1}=aI\), so the trace identities give \(Q(A)=\Phi_p(A)=0\).
Thus \(A^p=I\), and \(A\ne I\) because of its unit off-diagonal entries.
This realizes order exactly \(p\) over the single modulus with the growth in (1).
