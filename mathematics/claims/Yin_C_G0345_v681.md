# Prime powers in Salem constructions at integer seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## The seed and the possible loss of Mahler measure

Let \(a\ge3\) and \(n\ge7\) be integers, \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), and define
\[
         G(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1),\qquad
         A=a(a-1)(a+1).
\]
There is just one exterior root \(r\), it is Salem, and \(a-1<r<a+1\).
Let \(P\) be its monic minimal polynomial and \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\).
For any odd prime \(p<n\) and integer \(e\ge1\),
\[
                r^{1/p^e}\in K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                           p^{e+1}\mid A .                              \tag{1}
\]
If \(a\equiv2\bmod4\), then \(r^{1/2}\notin K\).
All radicals here are the positive real ones.

Suppose \(a\equiv2\bmod4\) and
\[
                 n>\tfrac12\log(a+1)\log^3(3(n+1)).                   \tag{2}
\]
Write \(H=\prod_{p\mid A,\ p\ {\rm odd}}p^{v_p(A)-1}\).
Then every positive integer \(l\) with \(r^{1/l}\in K\) divides \(H\).
For every substitution \(X\mapsto X^h\), each noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(G(X^h)\) has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log(r)/H\).
If the odd parts of the three consecutive integers \(a-1,a,a+1\) are squarefree, then \(P(X^h)\) itself is irreducible and has multiplicative Mahler measure \(r\) for every \(h\ge1\).
The integers \(a\equiv2\bmod4\) satisfying this squarefreeness condition have lower natural density at least \(1/24\) among all positive integers.
Thus the conclusion covers both signs and unbounded seeds and delays satisfying (2).
The [family approaching two](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#statement) treats the smaller integer seed at its stated sign and every delay in its range.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Roots of unity and the exterior root

At \(a-1\) the polynomial \(G\) is negative, since \((a-1)^n>a(a-1)+1\), and at \(a+1\) it is positive.
On the circle its equation is \(z^n=\epsilon(az-1)/(a-z)\).
The right side is a signed Blaschke factor with argument derivative \((a^2-1)/|a-z|^2\le(a+1)/(a-1)\).
It winds once, so the argument difference is strictly increasing with total change \(2\pi(n-1)\).
This gives \(n-1\) simple circle roots, as in the [circle count at seed two](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier).
Reciprocity up to sign gives the other real root \(1/r\).
Since \(G\) is monic with unit constant term, the factor of \(r\) must also contain \(1/r\) by the absolute norm, and every other irreducible factor is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

For a torsion root \(\zeta\), every conjugate of \(\zeta^n-\epsilon\zeta\) has modulus at most \(2/a<1\), by the equation \(a(\zeta^n-\epsilon\zeta)=\zeta^{n+1}-\epsilon\).
Its integral norm forces \(\zeta^n=\epsilon\zeta\), and substituting back gives \(\zeta^2=1\).
Consequently \(G=P U\), where \(U\) is a product of distinct factors from \(X-1,X+1\).
In particular \(P\) has degree at least \(n-1\) and has neutral conjugates, as required for \(r\) to be Salem.

<a id="traces"></a>
## Detecting prime powers from the first delayed trace term

For a monic polynomial \(F\) let \(s_j(F)\) be the sum of the \(j\)-th powers of its roots.
Suppose \(\theta=r^{1/p^e}\in K\).
It is an algebraic unit generating \(K\), and the integral companion matrix of its minimal polynomial has the conjugates of \(\theta\) as eigenvalues.
The [characteristic-polynomial congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence) for exponents \(p^{e+1}\) and \(p^e\), applied also to the \(j\)-th power of this matrix, gives
\[
                  s_{pj}(P)\equiv s_j(P)\pmod{p^{e+1}}
                              \qquad(j\ge1).
\]
For odd \(p\) the roots of \(U\) are fixed by the \(p\)-th power, so the same congruence holds for \(G\).

The reversed polynomial is \(1-az+\epsilon z^n(a-z)\).
The formal identity \(-\log(z^{n+1}G(1/z))=\sum_{j\ge1}s_j(G)z^j/j\) gives
\[
 s_j(G)=a^j\quad(1\le j<n),\qquad
 s_j(G)=a^j-\epsilon j c_{j-n}\quad(n\le j<2n),
 \qquad c_0=a,\quad c_t=(a^2-1)a^{t-1}\quad(t\ge1).
\]
Indeed the first correction to \(-\log(1-az)\) is \(-\epsilon z^n(a-z)/(1-az)\) through degrees below \(2n\).
At \(p<n\) the trace congruence first gives \(a^p\equiv a\bmod p^{e+1}\).
If \(p\mid a\), then \(v_p(a^p-a)=v_p(a)\), proving (1).

For \(p\nmid a\) choose an integer \(j\) with \(n<pj<2n\) and \(p\nmid j\).
If \(p<n<2p\) take \(j=2\).
If \(n=2p\) take \(j=3\), since \(n\ge7\) then gives \(p\ge5\).
If \(n>2p\), the interval \((n/p,2n/p)\) has length greater than two and contains consecutive integers.
In each case \(j<n\).
Subtracting the two trace formulas and using \(a^{pj}\equiv a^j\bmod p^{e+1}\) gives \(p^e\mid(a^2-1)a^{pj-n-1}\).
Thus \(a\equiv b\bmod p\) for some \(b\in\{-1,1\}\).
The polynomial \(X^p-X\) vanishes at \(b\) and has derivative \(-1\bmod p\), so its integer value at \(a\) has valuation \(v_p(a-b)\).
This proves (1).

For the square test take \(p=2,e=1\) and let \(B\in\{0,1\}\) be the multiplicity of \(-1\) in \(U\).
For \(j=1,3\) the congruence for \(P\) becomes \(a^{2j}-a^j\equiv2B\bmod4\), since \(2j<n\).
When \(a\equiv2\bmod4\), the two left sides are respectively two and zero modulo four, which excludes the square.

<a id="dilation"></a>
## Field degrees and a set of admissible seeds

If \(r^{1/p}\in K\), its degree is \(d=\deg P\le n+1\) and its logarithmic measure is \(\log(r)/p\), by the moduli at the \(d\) embeddings.
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) gives \(p<\log(a+1)\log^3(3(n+1))/2\).
Under (2), every prime divisor of a field radical index is therefore less than \(n\).
Removing its other prime factors by taking powers and applying (1) and the square test proves \(l\mid H\).
For squarefree odd parts, each odd prime divides at most one of the three neighbors because \(a\) is even, so \(H=1\).

Consider a monic irreducible factor of \(P(X^h)\), and choose a conjugate \(\omega\) of a root with \(\omega^h=r\).
Over the real field \(K\) the minimum of \(\omega\) divides \(X^h-r\), so its \(t=[K(\omega):K]\) roots all have modulus \(r^{1/h}\).
The real constant coefficient gives \(r^{t/h}\in K\) after taking its sign.
Bezout's identity then gives \(r^{1/l}\in K\) for \(l=h/\gcd(t,h)\), as in the [real-field substitution argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Thus \(t/h\ge1/H\).
Exactly the \(t\) extensions of the exterior embedding yield exterior roots of the rational factor, giving it logarithmic measure \(t\log(r)/h\).
When \(H=1\) this forces \(t=h\), and the tower degree proves irreducibility.
Substitution in \(U\) contributes only roots of unity.

Among \(a\le X\) with \(a\equiv2\bmod4\), an odd prime square \(p^2\) can divide one of the three neighbors in at most \(3(X/(4p^2)+1)\) cases, by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Only \(p\le\sqrt{X+1}\) occur once \(a\ge3\).
The total of the additive errors is \(O(\sqrt X)\).
The sum of \(p^{-2}\) over odd primes is bounded by the sum over all odd integers at least three, which is at most \(1/9+\int_1^\infty(2x+1)^{-2}\,dx=5/18\).
Subtracting the resulting upper count from \(X/4+O(1)\) gives the claimed lower density.
