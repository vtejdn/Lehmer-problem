# A degree threshold at measure 1.089 and the two-value test through 233

<a id="statement"></a>
## The degree and prime conclusions

Every nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(1<M(P)\le1089/1000\) has degree at least 64.
For an algebraic unit \(\alpha\) in the same measure range the bound also applies to \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\) for every positive integer \(k\).
For the [original fixed-budget test](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement), \(N_p\le1\) at every prime through 233, for both signs.
At 239 the only remaining ordered degree pair, with the smaller measure first, is \((64,56)\).
At 241 the possibilities are \((64,56),(64,58),(66,56)\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Reduction to the two remaining enumerations

Put \(T=1089/1000\).
A nonzero integer polynomial with \(1<M(P)\le T<2\) has an irreducible factor \(F\) with \(1<M(F)\le T\), by multiplicativity and the lower bound one on the measure of every nonzero integer polynomial.
The leading and constant coefficients of this factor have absolute value one by the root product, excluding the factor \(X\) of measure one.
It is a reciprocal unit minimum after normalizing the leading coefficient, by [Smyth's nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
A reciprocal polynomial of odd degree has root \(-1\) and an antireciprocal polynomial has root 1.
Thus the degree is even.
The [published strict sub-Lehmer degree bound](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives \(\deg F\ge56\), since \(T\) lies below Lehmer's measure.
The [complete first census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v3/readme.md#counts) excludes degrees 56 and 58.
If an irreducible of degree 60 or 62 is \(S(X^q)\) with \(q\ge2\), the same root product gives \(M(S)=M(F)\) at degree at most 31, already excluded.
Consequently it suffices to enumerate primitive reciprocal representatives without power substitutions in degrees 60 and 62, up to changing the sign of \(X\).

For the elliptic bounds, any vanishing auxiliary of degree at most five at \(\pm(\alpha^k+\alpha^{-k})\) would give a power field of degree at most ten.
To exclude this throughout \(1<M(\alpha)\le T\), let \(L=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\), \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):L]\), and \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/L}(\alpha)\).
Then \(u^k=\alpha^{ke}\), so its absolute logarithmic height is \(e h(\alpha)>0\), and
\[
       0<\log M(u)=[\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\,e h(\alpha)
                       \le[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]h(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha).
\]
The published degree bound applies to \(u\in L\), proving the required nonvanishing for either sign and every power.
The [degree-60 and degree-62 ellipses](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v6/readme.md#boxes) therefore supply necessary bounds through the central coefficient in both degrees.
The [finite tests](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v6/readme.md#census) enumerate integral Newton sequences and recover the unique reciprocal polynomial at the center.
They compare the reflected power sums through 120 with \(D-2+T^k+T^{-k}\) in degree \(D\), then exclude cyclotomic divisors and exact violations of the elementary-symmetric coefficient bounds after root squaring.
After the first fifteen sums and their cyclotomic tests, the enumeration covers 8,299,613,827 representatives in degree 60 and 29,164,063,775 in degree 62.
The reflected-sum and endpoint tests leave 2,805,677 and 5,756,465 respectively.
In degree 60, initial cyclotomic divisibility eliminates 2,461,671, exact Graeffe bounds eliminate 344,005, and the last polynomial is \(\Phi_{122}\).
In degree 62, the same two stages eliminate 5,157,164 and 599,301, exhausting the candidates.
Both enumerations therefore exclude every possible positive-measure factor in their respective degrees and prove the degree bound.
Applying the preceding norm computation a second time to any such \(\alpha\), now with the full finite degree bound, gives \([L:\mathbb Q]\ge64\).

<a id="primes"></a>
## Applying the degree bound to the signed rows

The [local zero estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#statement) settles all primes through 211.
At \(p=223,227,229,233,239,241\), suppose two values are represented by distinct qualifying minima \(P_1,P_2\).
The [resultant and width calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#primes) proves that their total degree is at most \(114,116,117,118,121,122\), respectively.
It uses the double-contact resultant valuation to bound the residue multiplicity by 65, so that the lower derivative congruences force the reduced product to divide the primitive row.
The same calculation uses the strict coefficient budget and Voutier's bound to put both measures strictly below Lehmer's, and the smaller multiplicative measure below \(T\).
Thus both degrees are at least 56, and the smaller-measure factor has degree at least 64 by the threshold just proved.
Their sum would then be at least 120, excluding two values through prime 233.
At the last two primes the even degrees give exactly the alternatives in the statement.
These finite exclusions alone give no unrestricted lower bound.
