# Many occupied coefficients in each row of an odd-exponent test

<a id="statement"></a>
## A prescribed family of graphs

For large integers \(L\) put \(K=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\), \(w=\lfloor L/(100K^3)\rfloor\), and let \(X_0\) be the least odd integer at least \(L\).
Define \(h_i=3K^2i+i^2\) and
\[
 S_i=\{X_0+8wh_i+2u:0\le u<w,\ u\in\mathbb Z\},\quad
 S=\bigcup_{i=1}^K S_i,\quad r=Kw,\quad \Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p .
\]
There is a computable \(j=j(L)\le K\) tending to infinity with the following property at degree bound \(b=rj\).
The test takes two nonzero integer polynomials \(U,V\) of degrees at most \(b\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(U(Y)U(Y^{-1})-V(Y)V(Y^{-1})\ne0\), and cost \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\le 2^\Sigma\).
A single monic reciprocal irreducible minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion unit must divide every graph \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\), for \(p\in S\), with multiplicity at least two.
This uses odd integers as graph exponents and imposes the same seed throughout, modifying the [prime-exponent test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common).
For all sufficiently large \(L\), **each** of \(U,V\) has more than \(\lfloor w/2\rfloor\) nonzero coefficients, hence \(\gg L/(\log L)^3=L^{1-o(1)}\) occupied coefficients.
Existence for every sufficiently large \(L\) of such rows with \(\log H/\Sigma\to0\) is equivalent to vanishing of the unrestricted logarithmic Lehmer infimum.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Periods with many consecutive odd representations

The blocks are disjoint subsets of \([L,2L]\) by their spacing and \(8w(3K^3+K^2)+2w+1<L\) eventually, so \(LKw\le\Sigma\le2LKw\).
The sums \(h_i+h_j\) with \(i<j\) are distinct.
Equality first forces equal \(i+j\) since the sum of the two squares is less than \(3K^2\), and then equal sums of squares force the same unordered pair.
For each pair take the periods
\[
 n=2X_0+8w(h_i+h_j)+2v,\qquad \lceil w/2\rceil\le v\le w-1.
\]
These lists are disjoint by spacing, and every period has the representations \(p=X_0+8wh_i+2u\), \(q=n-p\in S_j\) for \(0\le u\le v\).
Write \(\mathcal N\) for their union and \(T=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\varphi(n)\).
Within each list the integers \(m=n/2\) form an interval of \(N=\lfloor w/2\rfloor\) consecutive integers between \(L\) and \(2L\).
Since \(\varphi(2m)/(2m)\ge\varphi(m)/(2m)\ge(1-\sum_{\ell\mid m,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}1/\ell)/2\), the sum of these ratios is at least \((N(1-\sum_{t\ge2}t^{-2})-O(\log L))/2\).
Indeed each prime occurs at most \(N/\ell+1\) times and the sum of its endpoint errors is bounded by the harmonic sum through \(2L\).
The series from two is at most \(1/4+\int_2^\infty x^{-2}dx=3/4\), and \(w/\log L\to\infty\).
Thus \(T\gg LK^2w\gg K\Sigma\).

<a id="width"></a>
## Choosing the width before the rows

For each \(2\le j\le K\) enumerate the reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P\) with degree \(e\le j\) and \(M(P)\le2\), using the [finite coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width).
Put \(D_n\) equal to the part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n)|\) prime to \(n\).
Require for every listed polynomial and every selected period
\[
 M(P)^K\ge 2^{ej},\qquad D_n^j\ge M(P)^{(j-1)\varphi(n)}.
\]
Take the largest passing integer, or one if none passes.
The comparisons are exact algebraic tests, and every fixed \(j\ge2\) eventually passes by positivity of the finitely many seed measures and the [fixed-seed primitive norm asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) for \(n\to\infty\).
For an actual common seed the [tangency argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(re\le b\) and \(\Sigma h\le\log H\) with \(h=\log M(P)\).
Indeed for \(R=-U/V\) and \(z=x^p\) the root and derivative identities give \(R(z)=x\), \(pzR'(z)=x\), recovering both the root and the exponent, so the powered minima preserve degree and are distinct for distinct positive integers.
Their squares divide the same reciprocal elimination polynomial as in the cited proof.
Thus the actual seed passes both width tests since \(e\le j\) and \(h\le\log2\).
Also \(b\le K^2 w<L\le n/2\).

<a id="support"></a>
## Evaluations of a primitive row in one component

Fix either actual row \(W\), write \(W=cA\) with positive integer content \(c\) and primitive \(A\), and suppose its support has size \(t\le\lfloor w/2\rfloor\), at exponents \(0\le d_1<\cdots<d_t\le b\).
Fix a prime \(\ell\), a selected \(n\) prime to \(\ell\), and an unramified cyclotomic component at a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\xi\) above \(\ell\); its reduction has exact order \(n\).
For the first \(t\) consecutive odd exponents in the representations above, the evaluations of \(A(\xi^p)\) modulo \(\ell\) form a Vandermonde system on \(\bar\xi^{2d_i}\), with invertible column multipliers \(\bar\xi^{p_0d_i}\) from the first exponent \(p_0\).
The nodes are distinct because \(d_t-d_1<n/2\), and the coefficient vector is nonzero in the residue field by primitivity.
At least one evaluation is therefore a unit in this component.
For every one of these exponents the [odd-period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement) gives \(W(\xi^p)\in(P(\xi)^2)\), whether or not \(p,q\) are relatively prime.
Hence \(v_\ell(c)\ge2v_\ell(P(\xi))\) on each component, normalizing \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
At a given prime the distinct residual irreducibles with positive valuation divide \(P\bmod\ell\) and have total degree at most \(e\), across all these periods by their distinct exact orders.
The [component norm identity](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#layers) then gives \(e v_\ell(c)\ge2\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}v_\ell(D_n)\).
Multiplying the prime contributions and using the two width estimates yields
\[
 \log H\ge\log c\ge 2(1-1/j)hT/e\gg hK\Sigma/e\gg j\Sigma .
\]
This contradicts the cost cutoff for large \(L\) for either choice of row.

<a id="equivalence"></a>
## Interpolation for the odd integers

The pointwise lower bound \(\Sigma h\le\log H\) gives one implication.
When the unrestricted infimum vanishes, the [reciprocal reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) supplies reciprocal nontorsion unit minima with arbitrarily small positive measure \(h\).
Replacing a root by its [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) preserves \(h\) and the unit property, and inversion carries its conjugacy class of products onto itself by reciprocity of the original seed.
Distinct new conjugates have nontorsion quotient, so powering by any positive integer preserves the new degree \(e\).
The powered minima for different \(p\) are distinct by their measures \(ph\).
Use all these nodes in the [integral Hermite construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) with multiplier \(J=\prod_{p\in S}p\).
Each \(J/p\) is integral even for composite exponents, and the value and first derivative at each node give a double factor in its graph.
The coprimality and endpoint adjustments there depend only on the distinct nodes and the monic reciprocal product of their minima.
The same coefficient estimate gives degree at most \(2er+2\) and \(\log H\le2h\Sigma+2er\log2+\log(5Jer)\).
For fixed seed the width fits eventually, and \(\log J\le r\log(2L)\), so \(\limsup\log H/\Sigma\le2h\).
Given any positive tolerance, choose a fixed seed with \(2h\) below both the tolerance and \(\log 2\).
For every sufficiently large \(L\) its rows meet the cost cutoff and place the normalized minimum below that tolerance.
