# Locating every noncyclotomic factor after a multiplier creates separated blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## The common divisor of the block coefficients

Let \(H\in\mathbb Z[z^{\pm1}]\) be nonzero and, up to a monomial, write
\[
          H(z)=\sum_{j=0}^R z^{jn}A_j(z),\qquad
          \deg A_j\le w<n,\quad A_j\in\mathbb Z[z],\quad A_0 A_R\ne0,\quad R\ge1 .
\]
Let \(G=\gcd(A_0,\ldots,A_R)\) include their common integer content, up to sign and a Laurent monomial, and let \(k\) be the number of nonzero coefficients of \(H\).
Use logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), and the [separated-block constants](Yin_C_G0345_v159.md#statement)
\[
 c_R=[2e^{16384}(\log(3R))^{48}]^{-1},\qquad
 \Delta=C R^2(w/n)\{R+k\log(2Rkn/w)\}.
\]
Use \(\Delta=0\) when \(w=0\).
The block representation is unambiguous since the intervals are disjoint.
If \(H/G\) has any noncyclotomic irreducible factor, then
\[
                             m(H)-m(G)\ge c_R-\Delta .                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular if \(m(H)-m(G)<c_R-\Delta\), every noncyclotomic irreducible of \(H\), with its **whole multiplicity**, divides \(G\).
This also follows under the stronger hypothesis \(m(H)<c_R-\Delta\), since \(G\) is integral.
Here and below cyclotomic products are taken up to sign and Laurent monomials.
The coordinator proposed the block-gcd obstruction and the dense shifted-Eisenstein family; the incremental measure bound and the common limit in (3) are developed here.

To prove (1), form \(F(X,Y)=\sum A_j(X)Y^j\) and \(F_0=F/G\); the coefficients of \(F_0\) have gcd one.
The comparison \(m_2(F)-m(H)\le\Delta\) is exactly \(m_2(F_0)-m(H/G)\le\Delta\), by additivity.
Thus the error for the original support remains available even when division inserts additional coefficients.
One may take the smaller of this error and the same error computed from \(F_0\)'s own widths and support.
The bound is increasing in its width argument on \(0<w<n\); if the actual \(X\)-width is zero, substitution preserves measure exactly.

If every geometric component of \(F_0\) depending on \(Y\) is a torsion coset, the [partial-degree factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) expresses \(F_0\) as a product of cyclotomic polynomials in Laurent monomials, up to a base factor and a unit.
Any nonunit base factor would divide all coefficients, so it is absent.
As in the [specialization argument](Yin_C_G0345_v159.md#arithmetic), each \(Y\)-dependent factor specializes to a nonconstant cyclotomic product: its \(X\)-width is at most \(w<n\), so distinct \(Y\)-layers cannot cancel at specialization.
Conversely, a noncyclotomic factor of \(H/G\) therefore requires a nontorsion horizontal component of \(F_0\), which gives \(m_2(F_0)\ge c_R\) by the [partial-degree gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement).
This proves (1).
More precisely, factor \(F_0\) over the integral Laurent ring and let \(U_i\) be its distinct irreducibles with such a component, occurring with multiplicity \(h_i\) and \(Y\)-width \(R_i\ge1\).
Applying that gap to the individual rational factors and adding their measures gives \(m(H)-m(G)\ge\sum_i h_i c_{R_i}-\Delta\).
Every other integral factor has nonnegative measure, and the specialization error is subtracted only once.

<a id="multipliers"></a>
## Multipliers, recurrences and successive block decompositions

Let \(H=P Q\), with \(P\) an irreducible noncyclotomic integral polynomial of degree greater than \(w\) after monomial normalization, and \(Q\) any nonzero integral Laurent polynomial.
The width of \(G\) is at most \(w\), so \(P\) divides \(H/G\) and (1) gives
\[
                         m(Q)\ge c_R-\Delta+m(G)-m(P).                         \tag{2}
\]
There is no bound on the degree, multiplicities or coefficient size of \(Q\).
In particular, along a shrinking sequence \(m(P)\to0\), fixed \(R\) and \(\limsup\Delta<c_R\) exclude **every** cyclotomic multiplier giving the specified blocks once \(\deg P>w\).
This width condition is necessary for the conclusion about \(P\): multiplying any base polynomial \(P\) of width at most \(w\) by \(z^n-1\) gives two such blocks with gcd \(P\).

In coefficients, write \(P(z)=\sum p_a z^a,\ Q(z)=\sum q_bz^b\), extending both sequences by zero.
Support is contained in the stated intervals exactly when the recurrence \(\sum_b q_b p_{a-b}=0\) holds outside them, with a common integer translation of all intervals allowed.
For the specified span in the block index, each of the two end intervals must also contain a nonzero defect.
The nonzero recurrence defects within each interval give its \(A_j\).
Thus (2) tests an input even when **every** coefficient of \(P\) between its ends is nonzero.

The incremental form (1) also adds across nested decompositions.
Given \(H_0,\ldots,H_t\) where \(H_{i+1}\) is the block gcd at stage \(i\), with that stage's parameters \(R_i,\Delta_i\), one has
\[
              m(H_0)\ge m(H_t)+
                   \sum_{\{i:H_i/H_{i+1}\ {\rm noncyclotomic}\}}
                                  (c_{R_i}-\Delta_i)_+ .
\]
This follows by telescoping and nonnegativity of all integral quotient measures.
In particular total measure below every positive stage threshold, when all thresholds are positive, retains the entire noncyclotomic factorization in \(H_t\).

<a id="dense"></a>
## Dense irreducible inputs for the second-difference recurrence

Fix a prime \(p\), let \(n=p^\ell,\ \ell\ge2\), and put \(A_n(z)=1+\cdots+z^{n-1}\).
Take any symmetric integral Laurent polynomial \(T\) with nonnegative coefficients, exact span parameter \(s=\max\{|j|:[z^j]T\ne0\}<n-1\), and \(p\nmid T(1)\).
Then
\[
                        P(z)=A_n(z)^2+p z^{n-1}T(z)
\]
is monic reciprocal, irreducible, noncyclotomic and strictly positive in every coefficient from zero to \(2n-2\).
Indeed \(A_n(z)\equiv(z-1)^{n-1}\pmod p\) by Frobenius.
Thus \(P(z+1)\equiv z^{2n-2}\pmod p\), and its constant term \(n^2+pT(1)>p\) has \(p\)-adic valuation exactly one.
Eisenstein proves irreducibility.
For \(m>1\), \(\Phi_m(1)\) is the prime dividing \(m\) when \(m\) is a prime power, and is one otherwise, by induction from \(m=\prod_{t\mid m,\,t>1}\Phi_t(1)\).
Also \(\Phi_1(1)=0\), so the given value rules out every cyclotomic polynomial.
The square \(A_n^2\) supplies all coefficients and the added term misses both ends.

The second difference has the exact form
\[
       (z-1)^2P=z^{2n}+1+z^n\big[-2+p(z+z^{-1}-2)T(z)\big].
\]
The central Laurent span is \(s+1\); translating the three blocks by \(s+1\) gives widths bounded by \(2(s+1)\), applicable in (1) when \(2(s+1)<n\).
The endpoint layers are monomials and the middle block has nonzero coefficient at its lower edge, so their Laurent gcd is one.
The multiplier is cyclotomic, hence its block gap transfers to the original dense polynomial whenever the error is sufficiently small.
The [central-block estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v158.md#statement) also applies directly to this centered form.

For an explicit measure calculation, choose any \(1\le j<n\) coprime to \(p\) and let \(T=A_j(z)A_j(z^{-1})\).
This gives \(P=A_n^2+p z^{n-j}A_j^2\).
On the circle its centered value is \(|A_n|^2+p|A_j|^2>0\), since \(n,j\) are coprime and neither summand vanishes at one.
Thus \(P\) has exactly \(n-1\) exterior conjugates, and its second difference is supported on just \(0,n-j,n,n+j,2n\).
For every such choice,
\[
  \log(1+p)\le m(P)\le\log(2(1+p)),\qquad
  m(P)\longrightarrow \frac4\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}
                     \operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt p\,\sin t)\,dt                 \tag{3}
\]
as \(n=p^\ell\to\infty\), uniformly over \(j\).
For the lower bound apply the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean with weights \(1/(1+p),p/(1+p)\) to \(|A_n|^2+p|A_j|^2\) and integrate the logarithm, using \(m(A_n)=m(A_j)=0\).
After multiplication by \((z-1)^2\) the circle modulus becomes \(|1-z^n|^2+p|1-z^j|^2\).
Its mean is \(2(1+p)\), proving the upper bound by concavity of the logarithm.
The distributions of \((z^n,z^j)\) for uniform \(z\) converge to Haar probability on the two-torus uniformly in the allowed \(j\).
Indeed for each fixed nonzero character with indices \(a,b\), the equality \(an+bj=0\) forces \(n\mid b\) and so is impossible at all sufficiently large \(n\).
Trigonometric approximation gives the assertion for continuous functions.
For the logarithm of \(|1-z^n|^2+p|1-z^j|^2\), the lower truncation at \(-K\) has mean error bounded by the mean of \((-\log|1-z^n|^2-K)_+\), which tends to zero uniformly because \(z^n\) is uniform.
The same bound holds on the two-torus, so the logarithmic means converge.
At fixed \(w=e^{i\theta}\), Jensen's formula in the other variable gives the circle mean of \(\log(|1-v|^2+p|1-w|^2)\) as
\(\operatorname{arcosh}(1+2p\sin^2(\theta/2))=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt p\,|\sin(\theta/2)|)\).
Integrating this over \(\theta\) proves (3).
