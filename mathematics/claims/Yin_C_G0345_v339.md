# Irreducible two-band measures oscillating about the plastic threshold

<a id="statement"></a>
## The threshold is approached from below by irreducible inputs

Let \(f(X)=X^3-X-1\), let \(g(X)=X^3f(1/X)=1-X^2-X^3\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the real zero of \(f\).
For \(n>3\) set
\[
                    G_n(X)=X^n g(X)+f(X),\qquad L=\log\theta.
\]
Each \(G_n\) is a reciprocal six-term integer polynomial of degree \(n+3\), supported on the two end intervals of width three.
For all sufficiently large \(n\equiv111\pmod{120}\), \(-G_n\) is the monic minimal polynomial of a [complex Salem number](../audits/literature/PT21/Yin_U001.md#pt21-u001).
Within this progression, each of \(0<m(G_n)<L\) and \(m(G_n)>L\) has relative natural density \(1/2\), where \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
In both sets \(m(G_n)\to L\).
In particular the [certified examples](#examples) \(G_{111}\) and \(G_{231}\) are irreducible and satisfy \(m(G_{231})<L<m(G_{111})\).
Thus the fixed positive margin in the [two-band threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#statement) is needed even for irreducible polynomials with two end intervals of width three.

More precisely, let \(\beta\) be the zero of \(g\) in the upper half-plane, write \(r=|\beta|=\sqrt\theta\) and \(\psi=\arg\beta\), and put
\[
                  c=-\frac{f(\beta)}{\beta g'(\beta)},\qquad A=2|c|>0.
\]
Along all sufficiently large integers one has
\[
                m(G_n)=L+2r^{-n}\operatorname{Re}(ce^{-in\psi})
                                      +O(nr^{-2n}).                          \tag{1}
\]
On every fixed arithmetic progression of positive indices the empirical distribution of \(r^n(m(G_n)-L)\) converges to the arcsine probability measure on \([-A,A]\), of density
\[
                        \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{A^2-x^2}},\qquad -A<x<A.          \tag{2}
\]
The irreducibility assertion is for the specified progression.

<a id="roots"></a>
## The two nonreal exterior roots

The irreducible cubic \(f\) has discriminant \(-23\), real root \(\theta\), and two nonreal roots of modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\).
Thus \(g\) has just one root inside the unit circle, the real number \(1/\theta\), and the two simple exterior roots \(\beta,\bar\beta\).
The two cubics are coprime because \(f+g=-X(X+1)\) and \(f(0)=f(-1)=-1\).
In particular \(c\ne0\).

The [Salem–Boyd circle argument](../audits/literature/Hir06/Yin_U001.md#hir06-u001) counts the exterior roots of the delayed polynomial with multiplicity.
Here \(|f|=|g|>0\) on \(|X|=1\).
By Rouché's theorem \(X^ng+t f\) for \(0\le t<1\) has \(n+1\) roots inside the circle and hence two outside.
Passing to \(t=1\) leaves at most two strictly exterior roots, since any such zeros persist with their total multiplicity in small disjoint exterior disks under a sufficiently small perturbation.
On a fixed small closed disk about \(\beta\) lying strictly outside the circle and disjoint from the real axis and the other roots of \(g\), the function \(g(z)+z^{-n}f(z)\) tends uniformly to \(g(z)\).
Rouché on its boundary gives exactly one zero \(\beta_n\) counted with multiplicity, and shrinking the disk shows \(\beta_n\to\beta\).
Conjugation gives the other exterior root \(\bar\beta_n\).
Both are simple.
Reciprocity under reversal follows from \(g=f^*\), so the only strictly interior roots are their inverses.
The leading coefficient is \(-1\), giving \(m(G_n)=2\log|\beta_n|\).

<a id="irreducibility"></a>
## Transporting a root of unity to the forward cubic

The [forward progression](Yin_C_G0336_v1.md#argument) gives an irreducible Salem polynomial \(X^{n'}f+g\) at every \(n'\ge9\) with \(n'\equiv9\bmod120\).
Suppose a root of unity \(\zeta\) of order \(t\) were a zero of \(G_n\) with \(n\equiv-9\bmod120\).
Choose \(n'=j\operatorname{lcm}(120,t)-n\) with \(j\) large enough that \(n'\ge9\).
Then \(n'\equiv9\bmod120\), and multiplication of \(G_n(\zeta)=0\) by \(\zeta^{-n}\) gives
\[
                        \zeta^{n'} f(\zeta)+g(\zeta)=0.
\]
The forward polynomial on this progression has no torsion root, a contradiction.

For large \(n\) in the reverse progression, a monic irreducible factor of \(-G_n\) containing \(\beta_n\) also contains its nonreal conjugate \(\bar\beta_n\).
These simple roots use both exterior roots of the entire polynomial.
Every remaining monic factor has nonzero constant coefficient and all roots of modulus at most one, so its roots would be roots of unity by Kronecker's theorem.
This proves irreducibility.
Since \(n+3>4\), the inverses of the exterior pair leave at least one conjugate on the unit circle, as the definition of a complex Salem number requires.
The two end blocks \(f\) and \(g\) differ even up to sign, as their linear coefficients show.

<a id="examples"></a>
## Two explicit irreducible inputs on opposite sides

For \(n=111\) and \(231\) take the Gaussian rational centers \(z=(a+ib)/10^{25}\) given below and radius \(e=10^{-19}\).
Both disks lie in the upper half-plane and \(1<|X|<6/5\) on each.
In each disk \(|G_n''(X)|\le B=6(n+3)^2(6/5)^{n+3}\) by the six monomials.
The exact checks below show \(|G_n(z)|<ek/4\), \(|G_n'(z)|>k\) and \(Be<k/2\) for the listed integer \(k\).
On the boundary, the Taylor remainder after the linear term has modulus at most \(Be^2/2\).
Thus \(G_n'(z)(X-z)\) dominates the constant and remainder together, giving a unique strictly exterior zero in the disk.
The exterior count and torsion exclusion above already apply at these two indices, so this zero and its conjugate give the entire measure and prove irreducibility.
Writing \(s\) for the listed sign, the last check gives \(s f(|z|^2-s5e)>0\).
The squared modulus of the zero lies within \(5e\) of \(|z|^2\), and \(f\) is strictly increasing above one, so \(s\) is the sign of \(M(G_n)-\theta\).

~~~python
from sympy import QQ, QQ_I

e = QQ(1, 10**19)
def norm2(z):
    return z.x**2 + z.y**2

def f(z):
    return z**3 - z - 1

tests = [
    (111, -8774388688845727635002504, 7448618073647491646163337, 10**6, 1),
    (231, -8774388331233454807491607, 7448617666197418494330327, 10**12, -1),
]
for n, a, b, k, sign in tests:
    z = QQ_I(a, b) / 10**25
    assert z.y > e and 1 < norm2(z) - 5*e < norm2(z) + 5*e < QQ(36, 25)
    g = 1 - z**2 - z**3
    v = z**n*g + f(z)
    deriv = n*z**(n-1)*g + z**n*(-2*z-3*z**2) + 3*z**2-1
    B = 6*(n+3)**2 * QQ(6,5)**(n+3)
    assert norm2(v) < (e*k/4)**2 and norm2(deriv) > k**2
    assert 2*B*e < k
    assert sign*f(norm2(z)-sign*5*e) > 0
~~~

<a id="displacement"></a>
## From a complex displacement to the logarithmic measure

Write \(\Delta_n=\beta_n-\beta\).
The root equation is \(g(\beta_n)=-\beta_n^{-n}f(\beta_n)\).
For any fixed \(1<q<r\), convergence, the simplicity of \(\beta\) and boundedness of \(f\) near \(\beta\) first give \(\Delta_n=O(q^{-n})\).
Choose the local logarithm of \(\beta_n/\beta\) tending to zero.
Then \(n|\log(\beta_n/\beta)|\to0\), so \(|\beta_n^{-n}|=O(r^{-n})\).
Using the equation again gives \(\Delta_n=O(r^{-n})\) and
\[
                     \beta_n^{-n}=\beta^{-n}(1+O(nr^{-n})).
\]
Expand \(g\) and \(f\) at \(\beta\) to obtain
\[
           \beta_n=\beta-\frac{f(\beta)}{g'(\beta)}\beta^{-n}
                                    +O(nr^{-2n}).
\]
Finally \(2\log|\beta_n|=2\log r+2\operatorname{Re}(\Delta_n/\beta)+O(|\Delta_n|^2)\) and \(2\log r=L\), proving (1).

<a id="distribution"></a>
## The limiting distribution on each progression

The ratio \(\beta/\bar\beta\) is nontorsion.
Indeed an automorphism of the splitting field of the irreducible cubic \(g\) sends \(\beta\) to the real root \(1/\theta\) and sends \(\bar\beta\) to a different root of modulus \(r\).
The resulting quotient has modulus \(1/(\theta r)<1\), which is impossible for a conjugate of a root of unity.
Therefore \(\psi/\pi\) is irrational.
On any progression \(n=a+qj\) of positive step \(q\), the average of \(e^{ikn\psi}\) tends to zero for every nonzero integer \(k\), by summing the geometric series with ratio \(e^{ikq\psi}\ne1\).
Trigonometric approximation gives the uniform angular distribution of \(n\psi\) modulo \(2\pi\).
The image of this uniform measure under \(t\mapsto2\operatorname{Re}(ce^{-it})\) has density (2) by the cosine substitution on its two monotone half-periods.
Multiplying (1) by \(r^n\) leaves an error \(O(nr^{-n})\to0\), which leaves the limits of continuous tests unchanged by uniform continuity on a common compact interval.
The distribution has no atom at zero and gives mass \(1/2\) to each open half-line.
Consequently the sign sets for the actual error have the asserted densities, and indices of exact equality have relative density zero.
The positive measures in both sign sets converge to \(L>0\).
