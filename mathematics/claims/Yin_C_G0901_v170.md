# The number of split real places needed for two Salem eigenvalues and the cost in translation length

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous realization over a totally real field

Let \(x,y>1\) be real Salem numbers of degree at least four, in their specified positive embeddings.
Write \(t_z=z+z^{-1}\), \(F_z=\mathbb Q(t_z)\), \(n_z=[F_z:\mathbb Q]\) for \(z=x,y\), and \(F=F_xF_y\).
Consider quaternion algebras over embedded totally real fields \(k\) with two reduced-norm-one elements whose larger eigenvalues, at the distinguished real place of \(k\), are \(x^a,y^b\) for positive integers \(a,b\).
Then \(F\subseteq k\).
The number \(r\) of real places of \(k\) at which the algebra is split satisfies
\[
                r\ \ge\ [k:F]\big([F:F_x]+[F:F_y]-1\big).                \tag{1}
\]
For every such \(k\supseteq F\) equality is attained by the norm-one group of a single order if the exponents may be chosen.
In particular minimizing also over \(k\) gives exactly \([F:F_x]+[F:F_y]-1\).

Equip the product of the \(r\) hyperbolic planes with the product Riemannian metric, each factor of curvature \(-1\).
The translation lengths of the two elements in this product are
\[
              L_x=2a\sqrt{[k:F_x]}\log x,\qquad
              L_y=2b\sqrt{[k:F_y]}\log y.                               \tag{2}
\]
If the embedded root fields \(\mathbb Q(x)\) and \(\mathbb Q(y)\) are different, there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
        L_x L_y \ \ge\
       c\,ab[k:F]\frac{\sqrt{n_xn_y}}{(\log(12n_x n_y))^{212}} .        \tag{3}
\]
Thus such a pair cannot have both lengths tend to zero even if the number of split real places is allowed to increase.
The product of lengths tends to infinity when either trace degree tends to infinity.
At fixed upper bounds for \(r\) and for \(x,y\), only finitely many distinct-root-field pairs are possible.

There is also a test using a single Salem number \(x\).
Take a different conjugate \(t'\) of its trace \(t=t_x\).
For simultaneous reduced traces given by positive powers of roots over \(t,t'\), the minimum split-place count is
\(2h\), where \(h=[\mathbb Q(t,t'):\mathbb Q(t)]\).
The two marked sets now lie at disjoint real places.
Even at the smallest base, each unpowered translation length is \(2\sqrt h\log x\), and
\[
             \sqrt h\log x\ \gg\
                     \frac{\sqrt{n_x}}{(\log(16n_x))^{18}}.              \tag{4}
\]
Integral powers used to enter a common order can only multiply these lengths.
In contrast a single \(x\) admits its own quaternion model with one split place and unpowered length \(2\log x\).
The estimates leave that single length uncontrolled as the trace field varies.

<a id="places"></a>
## Counting the marked embeddings and attaining the minimum

For a positive power \(z^a\), the trace \(z^a+z^{-a}\) lies in \(F_z\), and its only conjugate under embeddings of \(F_z\) exceeding two in absolute value is the value at the distinguished embedding.
Indeed all the other traces lie in \((-2,2)\) by the Salem conjugate description, including after taking powers because a conjugate of a Salem number cannot be torsion. [Salem trace fields](Yin_C_G0901_v157.md#compositum)
The powered trace generates \(F_z\) because any embedding of a proper subfield has at least two extensions to \(F_z\).
The reduced traces at the distinguished place force \(k\) to contain the two marked trace fields.

At a ramified real place a norm-one quaternion is a Hamilton quaternion of absolute value one, with reduced trace twice its real part, in \([-2,2]\).
Thus every real embedding of \(k\) extending either marked embedding must be split.
The two marked fibers contain \([k:F_x]\) and \([k:F_y]\) embeddings, with \([k:F]\) in their intersection, the embeddings fixing the entire compositum.
This proves (1).

For the converse put \(\Delta_z=t_z^2-4\) and use the Hilbert symbol algebra \((\Delta_x,\Delta_y)_k\) with generators \(I^2=\Delta_x,J^2=\Delta_y,IJ=-JI\).
At a real place this algebra is Hamilton precisely when both parameters are negative.
Indeed rescaling gives the ordinary Hamilton multiplication table in that case; if a parameter is positive the corresponding generator has two distinct real eigenvalues and the algebra is split.
Each parameter is positive exactly on its marked fiber, so the number of split real factors is the required size of the union.
The elements \(u=(t_x+I)/2\) and \(v=(t_y+J)/2\) have the indicated characteristic polynomials and norm one.
The span of \(1,I,J,IJ\) over \(\mathcal O_k\) is an order by the displayed multiplication rules.
The commutative rings \(\mathcal O_k[u]\), \(\mathcal O_k[v]\) are finite over \(\mathcal O_k\); their reductions modulo 2 are finite rings.
Both \(u,v\) are units in their respective rings, with inverse the difference of trace and element.
Choose positive powers congruent to one in the respective reductions.
Since \(2\mathcal O_k[u]\subseteq\mathcal O_k+\mathcal O_k I\), and likewise for \(v,J\), these powers and their inverses lie in the same order.
No bound on these powers is required for attainment of the split-place count.

For one input alone take \((\Delta_x,-1)_{F_x}\) with \(u=(t_x+I)/2\).
The span \(\mathcal O_{F_x}[u]+\mathcal O_{F_x}[u]J\) is an order because \(J^2=-1\) and \(Ju=(t_x-u)J\); it contains the unpowered unit.
These constructions concern the diagonal action on the whole product of split real factors.
They impose no discreteness requirement on a projection to one factor and no compactness assumption on a quotient.

<a id="lengths"></a>
## Translation lengths, height products and fixed-rank finiteness

On each marked real plane the eigenvalues of the first element are \(x^a,x^{-a}\) and the translation length is \(2a\log x\), since the diagonal determinant-one representative acts on the upper half plane by \(w\mapsto x^{2a}w\).
At the other split places its roots are nonreal of modulus one and the elliptic element has a fixed point in that plane.
Each factor infimum of the displacement is attained, and squared distances add in the product.
This proves (2).

Let \(D=[\mathbb Q(x,y):\mathbb Q]\) and \(f=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
The two root fields have degrees \(2n_x,2n_y\); each becomes at most quadratic over \(F\), so \(D\le4f\le4n_x n_y\).
The [two-unit height-product estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#comparison) applies to the two distinct embedded root fields because their positive powers keep the full root degree, by the unique largest-modulus conjugate.
It gives
\[
       (\log x)(\log y)\gg
            \frac{n_xn_y}{f(\log(12n_xn_y))^{212}} .
\]
Multiply by \(4ab[k:\mathbb Q]/\sqrt{n_xn_y}\) to obtain (3).
The factor \(\sqrt{n_xn_y}/(\log(12n_x n_y))^{212}\) is positive on the allowed integer products and tends to infinity with their product, hence has a positive absolute lower bound.
If \(x,y\le B\), the same estimate and (1) give
\[
           r+1\gg\frac{n_x+n_y}{(\log B)^2(\log(12n_x n_y))^{212}} .
\]
A fixed bound on \(r\) therefore bounds both root degrees, and the bound \(B\) on the house gives finitely many integral minima.

For a concrete example take \(t_x=(1+\sqrt{13})/2\), \(t_y=1+\sqrt2\).
Their real quadratic equations are \(t_x^2-t_x-3=0\) and \(t_y^2-2t_y-1=0\).
In each case the displayed value exceeds two while the other conjugate lies in \((-2,2)\), so \(Z^2-t_zZ+1\) is irreducible over its trace field by the negative conjugate discriminant.
The corresponding Salem minima are \(Z^4-Z^3-Z^2-Z+1\) and \(Z^4-2Z^3+Z^2-2Z+1\).
The trace compositum is biquadratic, with two marked embeddings for each trace and precisely one common marked embedding.
The minimum for suitable powers in one quaternion order is three split places.

<a id="conjugates"></a>
## The displacement when the second source is a conjugate of the first

Now put \(F'=\mathbb Q(t,t')\), \(n=n_x\), and choose a root \(y\) above \(t'\) conjugate to \(x\).
The marked embeddings for \(t\) and \(t'\) are those taking the specified trace to the unique trace value exceeding two.
No embedding of \(F'\) can do this simultaneously to two distinct elements.
Each marked set has \(h=[F':\mathbb Q(t)]=[F':\mathbb Q(t')]\) embeddings.
The trace-field, Hamilton and Hilbert-symbol arguments above therefore give the lower bound \(2h[k:F']\), attained at every \(k\supseteq F'\) for suitable positive powers of the two roots.
The traces here are algebraic elements of the marked base, so at the distinguished embedding the second source is elliptic.
In the full product over \(F'\) each unpowered length is \(2\sqrt h\log x\).

The two conjugates \(x,y\) are multiplicatively independent.
Taking moduli at the distinguished embedding in a relation \(x^iy^j=1\) gives \(i=0\), and \(y\) is nontorsion.
They have common degree \(2n\), total log measure \(m=\log x\), and joint degree \(J\le4nh\).
The [plane curve through two independent conjugates](Yin_C_G0901_v150.md#curve) therefore gives
\[
       \frac{m}{n}\ge
          \frac{1}{N(29920\log(4N))^{18}},\qquad
          N=\lceil\sqrt{2J}\rceil \le4\sqrt{nh}.
\]
Since \(h\le n\), this proves (4), for instance with the right side \(\sqrt n/[4(29920\log(16n))^{18}]\).
Consequently two conjugates of a single putative shrinking Salem number also have growing displacements in any such common model as its degree tends to infinity.

The available short-geodesic rigidity for irreducible arithmetic lattices in a fixed semisimple group constrains the squared lengths present in one quotient; in particular sufficiently short squared lengths there lie in a cyclic subgroup.
The threshold in that theorem depends on the ambient group. [Frączyk and Pham, Theorem C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02332#page=4)
Equations (1)–(4) explain the geometric cost of attempting to produce the requisite simultaneous short lengths from two fields or two trace conjugates.
