# A fixed positive Mahler measure with a vanishing invariant in the unramified family

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conductor-twelve examples

Let \(\alpha>1\) be the root of Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial \(P\), let \(w=e^{2\pi i/12}\), and put \(h=\log\alpha\).
For every positive \(n\) coprime to six there is a nontorsion unit \(\beta_n\) in the [unramified quadratic norm-one family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#statement) with
\[
    m(\beta_n)=4h,\qquad [\mathbb Q(\beta_n):\mathbb Q]=40n,\qquad
    r_2(F_n)=2n,\qquad F_n=\mathbb Q(\beta_n+\beta_n^{-1}).
\]
For the [positive algebraic integer from the nonreal pairs](Yin_C_G0901_v34.md#statement) of this generator one has
\[
       D_n=2n\,V_n,\qquad V_n=\alpha^{2/n}+\alpha^{-2/n}-2,\qquad
       [\mathbb Q(D_n):\mathbb Q]=5n .
\]
The positive real number \(V_n\) is an algebraic unit and \(n^2 V_n\to4h^2\).
Thus \(D_n\to0\) and \(r_2(F_n)D_n\to16h^2=m(\beta_n)^2\) even at the fixed positive total measure \(4h\).
Every conjugate of \(V_n\) is within \(2(e^{2h/n}-1)\) of the interval \([-4,0]\).
These are exact algebraic examples with unbounded degrees, shrinking \(D_n\) and a unit after division by the number of nonreal pairs.

<a id="tower"></a>
## Root extraction in the actual unramified quadratic family

More generally suppose \(\beta\) is in that family with \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})\), and \(r=r_2(F)\).
Assume \(X^n-\beta\) is irreducible over \(L\), and take any root \(\gamma\).
Then \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=L(\gamma)\) since \(\gamma^n=\beta\), so the minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) is the minimum of \(\beta\) composed with \(X^n\).
The resulting polynomial is reciprocal and \(\gamma\) is an algebraic unit.
The involution of \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) inverting \(\gamma\) restricts on \(L\) to the given inverter of \(\beta\), because \(\beta\) generates \(L\).
Its fixed field is \(F_\gamma=\mathbb Q(\gamma+\gamma^{-1})\) by the quadratic equation for \(\gamma\).
It fixes \(\sqrt3\) and negates \(i\), so \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=F_\gamma(i)\) and the [integral basis argument](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) applies.
Each embedding of \(L\) extends to \(n\) embeddings sending \(\gamma\) to the \(n\)-th roots of the image of \(\beta\).
This preserves total measure, and multiplies the number of off-circle conjugates and hence the number of nonreal pairs of the trace field by \(n\).
More precisely let \(a_s=\log|u_s|\) at one embedding in each nonreal pair of \(F\), using the [lift fixing \(i\)](Yin_C_G0901_v34.md#embeddings).
Each such lift extends to \(n\) lifts still fixing \(i\), all with radial logarithm \(a_s/n\).
Embeddings above its conjugate supply the negatives, while lifts of all real embeddings contribute zero.
Therefore
\[
                       D(\gamma)=4n\sum_s\sinh^2(a_s/n).
\]
Along any such unbounded sequence of full degrees \(n\), one has
\[
             r_2(F_\gamma)D(\gamma)\longrightarrow4r\sum_s a_s^2
                                            \ \ge\ m(\beta)^2 .
\]
The inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz with \(m(\beta)=2\sum_s|a_s|\).
Equality holds just when all the absolute logarithms on these pairs are equal.

## Degree, unit norm and the location of all conjugates

Set \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) in its positive real embedding, and \(E=\mathbb Q(w)\).
The intersection \(K\cap E\) is contained in the real field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
In the [Salem twist construction](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) the inverter on \(K\) acts on the intersection as complex conjugation on \(E\), so fixes it.
The fixed field in \(K\) is \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), of degree five, which cannot contain a quadratic subfield.
Thus \(K\cap E=\mathbb Q\).
In particular \(\beta=w\alpha\) generates \(KE\), has degree \(40\), and has total measure \(4h\).
Its trace field has two nonreal pairs, both with absolute radial logarithm \(h\).

Let \(\alpha_n=\alpha^{1/n}>0\).
The [irreducible power substitutions](Yin_C_G0901_v22.md#representatives) give \([K(\alpha_n):K]=n\).
Since \([KE:K]=4\) and \(n\) is odd, the degree of \(KE(\alpha_n)\) over \(K\) is at least \(4n\) by divisibility by both degrees and at most \(4n\) by compositum.
Choose an integer \(j\) with \(nj\equiv1\bmod12\) and put \(\beta_n=w^j\alpha_n\).
Its \(n\)-th power is \(\beta\), so it generates \(KE(\alpha_n)\) and \(X^n-\beta\) has full degree over \(KE\).
The preceding formula now gives \(r_2(F_n)=2n\) and \(D_n=2n(\alpha_n^2+\alpha_n^{-2}-2)\).

The positive unit \(v=\alpha_n^2\) generates \(K(\alpha_n)\).
Indeed \(v^n=\alpha^2\) generates \(K\) by uniqueness of the dominant Salem conjugate, and \((2,n)=1\) permits the expression \(\alpha_n=(\alpha_n^2)^a(\alpha_n^n)^b\) with integers \(a,b\).
It follows that the minimal polynomial of \(v\) is \(P_2(X^n)\), where \(P_2\) is the degree-ten minimum of \(\alpha^2\).
The polynomial \(P_2(X^n)\) is reciprocal, so \(\mathbb Q(v)\) has degree two over \(\mathbb Q(v+v^{-1})=\mathbb Q(V_n)\).
This gives the stated degree.
Furthermore \(|P_2(1)|=|P(1)P(-1)|=1\), by factoring the difference of squares over all ten conjugates of \(\alpha\) and evaluating the [explicit polynomial](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#statement).
Hence \(1-v\) is a unit, and \(V_n=(v-1)^2/v\) is a unit as well.
Every image of \(v\) can be written \(z=e^{a+i\theta}\) with \(|a|\le2h/n\).
The distance from \(z+z^{-1}-2\) to \(2\cos\theta-2\) is at most \(2(e^{2h/n}-1)\) by the triangle inequality.
Finally \(V_n=4\sinh^2(h/n)\) at the given embedding, proving all limits.
