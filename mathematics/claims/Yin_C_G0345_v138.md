# The longest prefix of a small Salem polynomial that can come from roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Cyclotomic completions at the original degree

Let \(P\) be a monic separable integer polynomial of degree \(d\to\infty\), with \(0<L=\log M(P)\le A\) for fixed \(A\).
For every sufficiently large \(d\) there is a monic reciprocal product \(Q\) of cyclotomic polynomials of degree exactly \(d\) having the same first \(k\) coefficients below the leading one as \(P\), where
\[
      k=\left\lfloor\frac{\log(d(1-e^{-L}))-d^{-1/4}}{L}\right\rfloor.   \tag{1}
\]
When \(P\) is reciprocal with constant term one the trailing \(k\) coefficients also agree.
The result applies to every exterior signature, and the product is constructed from the given coefficients.

Suppose \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma=e^a\), so \(L=a\).
Let \(K(P)\) be the largest possible \(k\) over all monic products of cyclotomic polynomials of degree \(d\) sharing these leading coefficients.
Then, uniformly for \(a\le A\),
\[
     aK(P)\le\log(d(1-e^{-2a}))+o(1).                               \tag{2}
\]
The same upper bound holds even if the comparison polynomial can have arbitrary roots on the unit circle without integrality, provided it is monic of degree \(d\).
Along every hypothetical Salem sequence with \(a\to0\), (1) and (2) give
\[
      -o(1)\ \le\ aK(P)-\log(da)\ \le\ \log 2+o(1).                 \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(a\sim\log(d)/K(P)\) on every bounded Salem sequence of increasing degree, and the existence of Salem numbers tending to one is equivalent to \(K(P)/\log d\) being unbounded among bounded Salem inputs.
For arbitrary exterior signatures (1) gives the necessary long cyclotomic prefixes along every sequence of irreducible reciprocal units with measure tending to one.
It does not assert the converse in that signature range.

<a id="packets"></a>
## Constructing a completion with the required integer coefficients

Write \(p_j=\sum_{P(\alpha)=0}\alpha^j\), with multiplicity.
For \(w^dP(1/w)\), determine integers \(c_j\) in succession by
\[
     w^dP(1/w)\equiv\prod_{j=1}^k(1-w^j)^{c_j}\pmod{w^{k+1}},
       \qquad j c_j=\sum_{e\mid j}\mu(j/e)p_e .                     \tag{4}
\]
The coefficient recursion and the identity from logarithmic differentiation are the [integral formal factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v136.md#threshold).
Use the polynomial \(1-w^j\) for each positive exponent and \((1-w^{j h})/(1-w^j)\), with \(h=\lfloor k/j\rfloor+1\), for each copy of a negative exponent.
This last polynomial agrees with \((1-w^j)^{-1}\) through order \(k\).
Both choices have degree \(j\) when \(j>k/2\), and degree at most \(k\) otherwise.
All their nonzero roots are roots of unity and they have constant one.
Reversing their product therefore gives a monic cyclotomic product \(U\) with the correct leading coefficients.
Its degree \(D\) satisfies
\[
         D\le \sum_{k/2<j\le k}|p_j|
                +2k\sum_{e\le k/2}\frac{|p_e|}{e}
                                      (1+\log(k/e)).                 \tag{5}
\]
In fact the high indices require at most \(\sum_{j>k/2}(\lvert p_j\rvert+\sum_{e\mid j,\ e<j}|p_e|)\).
Each such proper divisor \(e\le k/2\) occurs at most \(k/e\) times.
For the low indices sum \(k|c_j|\) by (4) and use the harmonic sum over multiples of \(e\).

<a id="cost"></a>
## Bounding the whole degree from the measure of the original input

For each root \(\alpha\) put \(\beta=\alpha\) when \(|\alpha|\le1\), including at zero, and \(\beta=1/\bar\alpha\) otherwise.
Set \(v_j=\sum\beta^j\).
The [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives
\[
                 \sum_{j\le d}\frac{|v_j|^2}{j}\ll_A d\log(2d).     \tag{6}
\]
Its proof of the weighted sum uses the monic discriminant, separability and \(\sum\log^+|\alpha|=L\).
It applies directly here also at a zero root, since the pairwise disk inequality there is \(|\alpha|\le\max(1,|\alpha|)\).
The later phase comparison for units in that reference is unnecessary.
An exterior root of logarithmic modulus \(u\) contributes at most \(2\sinh(ju)\) to \(|p_j-v_j|\).
Superadditivity of \(\sinh\) on the nonnegative half-line therefore gives
\[
                       |p_j-v_j|\le 2\sinh(jL)\le e^{jL}.           \tag{7}
\]

A positive-measure irreducible factor of \(P\) has degree at most \(d\).
The [degree bound of Dobrowolski](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001), together with the bounded-degree case, gives \(L\gg_A(\log(2d))^{-3}\) uniformly (a nonunit monic irreducible with nonzero nonunit constant has measure at least two).
Consequently (1) has \(1\le k\ll_A(\log(2d))^4\) and \(e^{kL}\le d\) eventually.
Cauchy–Schwarz in (6) gives
\[
 \sum_{j\le k}|v_j|\ll_A k\sqrt{d\log(2d)},\qquad
 \sum_{e\le k}\frac{|v_e|}{e}(1+\log(k/e))
          \ll_A \sqrt{d\log(2d)}(1+\log k)^{3/2}.
\]
In the second estimate the remaining squared weight sums to \(O((1+\log k)^3)\) by integral comparison.
The high indices in (5) contribute from (7) at most \(e^{kL}/(1-e^{-L})\).
The low-index error from (7) is at most \(O(k e^{kL/2}(1+\log k)^2)\).
It follows that, with an absolute exponent \(C\),
\[
            D\le \frac{e^{kL}}{1-e^{-L}}
                      +O_A(\sqrt d\,(\log(2d))^C)
              \le d e^{-d^{-1/4}}+O_A(\sqrt d\,(\log(2d))^C).
\]
In particular \(d-D>k\) eventually.
Multiplying \(U\) by \(z^{d-D}+U(0)\) pads the degree, preserves its first \(k\) coefficients, and makes the constant coefficient one.
The result is reciprocal since inversion permutes its roots with multiplicities.
This proves (1).

<a id="positive"></a>
## Positivity at all the matched moments

Let a degree-\(d\) polynomial with unit-circle roots match the first \(k\) coefficients of a Salem minimal polynomial.
Then their power sums agree at orders \(1,\ldots,k\).
The \(k\)-th sum of the source is \(\sigma^k\) plus at most \(d-1\) in absolute error, and that of the target has modulus at most \(d\).
Hence \(k\le\log(2d)/a\ll_A(\log(2d))^4\) when \(k\ge1\).
Write \(p_0=d,\ p_{-j}=p_j\) for the shared moments, which are real by the Salem input.
The matrix \((p_{i-j})_{0\le i,j\le k}\) is positive semidefinite since every target root contributes the outer product of its powers with their conjugates.

The \(d-2\) neutral source roots contribute a matrix \(N\) with diagonal \(d-2\).
Its off-diagonal operator norm after subtracting this diagonal is at most \(2\sum_{j\le k}|\sum_{\rm neutral}\alpha^j|\).
The neutral sum differs from \(v_j\) in (6) by \(2e^{-ja}\), so this norm is \(O_A(k\sqrt{d\log(2d)}+k)=o(d)\), uniformly in this range.
The two real source roots contribute the rank-two symmetric matrix \(u v^{\mathsf T}+v u^{\mathsf T}\), where
\[
       u_i=e^{a(i-k/2)},\quad v_i=e^{-a(i-k/2)},\qquad 0\le i\le k .
\]
Here \(\langle u,v\rangle=k+1\) and \(\|u\|^2=\|v\|^2=\sinh((k+1)a)/\sinh a\).
The eigenvector \(u-v\) gives eigenvalue \(k+1-\sinh((k+1)a)/\sinh a\).
By positivity of the sum of both matrices,
\[
              \frac{\sinh((k+1)a)}{\sinh a}\le d+o(d).
\]
Since \(d\sinh a\to\infty\), rewriting the hyperbolic sine gives \(e^{ka}\le (1+o(1))d(1-e^{-2a})\).
This proves (2).
For \(a\to0\) the floor in (1) loses at most \(a=o(1)\); also \(1-e^{-a}\sim a\), and \(1-e^{-2a}\sim 2a\), giving (3).
For all bounded Salem inputs \(|\log(1-e^{-a})|=O_A(1+\log\log(2d))\) by the input degree bound, proving the final asymptotic and equivalence.
