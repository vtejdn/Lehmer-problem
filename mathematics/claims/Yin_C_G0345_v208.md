# Coefficient length bounds the exponent of a degree-preserving power

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform bounds for powers in the same field

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(n\ge1\), and suppose \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^n)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Write \(\lambda(\beta)\) for the least sum of absolute coefficients of a nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(\beta\).
If \(\lambda(\alpha^n)\le L\) for an integer \(L\ge2\), then, with absolute implied constants,
\[
        \log(2n)\ll L^3\log(2L),\qquad
        q\ll L^3\log(2L)\quad\text{for each prime }q\mid n.           \tag{1}
\]
Only the displayed equality of fields at this one exponent is needed.
In particular, if every positive power of \(\alpha\) has its original degree, then
\[
 \lambda(\alpha^n)\gg
     \left(\frac{\log(2n)}{\log\log(4n)}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
 \lambda(\alpha^q)\gg
     \left(\frac{q}{\log(2q)}\right)^{1/3}\quad(q\ {\rm prime}).      \tag{2}
\]
The constants are independent of the degree and the Mahler measure.

<a id="internal"></a>
## The first radical outside a shortest relation

Let \(\beta\) be any nontorsion unit with \(\lambda(\beta)\le L\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and write \(h(\beta)=m(\beta)/[K:\mathbb Q]\) for its absolute logarithmic height.
Fix \(0<c\le1\) such that the [width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(T h(u)\ge c/s^2\) whenever a nonzero rational relation of width \(T\) and \(s\) terms vanishes at a nontorsion unit \(u\).
Define
\[
        H=(L-1)\log L,\qquad A=\max(1,H L^2/c),\qquad
        Q=\max(A,2L,2H,2).
\]
For \(\eta=\beta\) or \(-\beta\), suppose there is \(y\in K\) with \(y^{q^a}=\eta\) for a prime \(q\) and \(a\ge1\).
Then
\[
                    q\le Q,\qquad q^a\le A L\exp(H/q).                \tag{3}
\]
The sign change preserves the field, height and minimum length by substitution \(X\mapsto-X\).

Choose a shortest relation \(P\) for \(\eta\) and divide out its lowest power of \(X\).
Its \(s\le L\) nonzero terms have no proper nonempty vanishing subsum.
For each gap \(g\) between consecutive exponents the [gap norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v189.md#upper) gives \(g h(\eta)\le\log L\).
Thus \(T=\deg P\) satisfies \(T h(\eta)\le H\).
Put \(u_i=y^{q^{a-i}}\) for \(0\le i\le a\), so \(u_0=\eta\).
All these numbers are units, generate \(K\) by the tower inclusions, and have heights \(h(\eta)/q^i\).

Let \(j\) be the largest index such that \(P(u_i)=0\) for every \(0\le i\le j\).
The width bound at \(u_j\) gives \(q^j\le T h(\eta)s^2/c\le A\).
If \(j=a\), this proves (3).
Otherwise \(P(u_{j+1})\ne0\).
Apply the [one-step norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) with base \(u_j\), in-field \(q^{a-j}\)-th root \(y\), first \(q\)-th root \(u_{j+1}\), and relation \(P\).
Its bound is
\[
       q^{a-j}\le L\exp\!\left(T h(u_j)/q\right)
                   \le L\exp(H/q^{j+1}).
\]
This proves the prime-power estimate after multiplication by \(q^j\).
If \(j\ge1\) then \(q\le A\).
If \(j=0\) then \(q\le L\exp(H/q)\), which is incompatible with both \(q>2L\) and \(q>2H\).
This proves the prime restriction without any assumption on the other factors of \(P\).

<a id="exponents"></a>
## Summing over the primes of the exponent

For \(\beta=\alpha^n\) with \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), each \(q^a\Vert n\) has the actual \(q^a\)-th root \(y=\alpha^{n/q^a}\) in that field.
Sum the logarithm of (3) to get
\[
     \log n\le \pi(Q)\log(AL)+H\sum_{2\le t\le Q}1/t .
\]
Here \(\pi(x)\ll x/\log(2x)\) for \(x\ge2\).
Indeed the product of the primes in \((k,2k]\) divides \(\binom{2k}{k}\le4^k\), and summing on dyadic intervals gives \(\sum_{p\le x}\log p\ll x\).
The primes above \(\sqrt x\) then contribute at least \(\tfrac12\log x\) each, and at most \(\sqrt x\) primes remain.
Since \(Q\asymp L^3\log(2L)\), \(\log(AL)\ll\log(2L)\), and the harmonic sum is \(O(\log(2Q))\), (1) follows.
Inverting \(z\ll L^3\log(2L)\) gives \(L\gg(z/\log(2z))^{1/3}\) for \(z\ge1\), by considering \(L\ge z\) and \(L<z\) separately.
Apply this with \(z=\log(2n)\) for \(n\ge2\), and with \(z=q\) for a prime exponent, to obtain (2).
The case \(n=1\) is covered by decreasing the absolute constant.
