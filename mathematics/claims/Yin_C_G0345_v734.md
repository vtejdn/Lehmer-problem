# Ramification averaged over a nonnormal subfield and height growth at bounded relative degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound in terms of the degree of the original generator

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(m=\log M(\alpha)\).
Let \(F\) be any subfield of \(K\), with \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(D=d/s\), and let \(G\) be the Galois group of the normal closure of \(F/\mathbb Q\).
Fix a positive integer \(b\).
Suppose that every element of \(G\) has its \(b\)-th power in the center.
For \(m\le1\) one has
\[
           m\gg_b s\,g_b(D),\qquad
           g_b(x)=\frac{(\log\log(5x))^{b+2}}{(\log(2x))^{2b+1}}.       \tag{1}
\]
This extends the [normal subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#statement) to the actual nonnormal subfield, including primes at which only a small part of its degree ramifies.

At every height, if \(D\le D_0\) for a fixed positive integer \(D_0\), then
\[
                         m\gg_{b,D_0}(d\log(2d))^{1/(b+1)}.             \tag{2}
\]
In particular (2) applies with \(D_0=2\) to a Salem number \(\alpha\) whenever the normal closure of its trace field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) satisfies the hypothesis.
It requires no bound on the exponent of \(G\) itself.
If that exponent also divides \(b\), the [bounded-exponent relative height theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#statement) over the normal closure gives the stronger linear lower bound \(m\gg_{b,D_0}d\), since the degree of \(\alpha\) over that closure is at most \(D_0\).
The local sufficient conditions below only concern actual Frobenius lifts on one interval.

<a id="average"></a>
## Conjugacy and the total loss from inertia

Let \(L\) be the normal closure of \(F\) and \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\).
For each rational prime \(p\) put
\[
  \delta_p=1-\frac1s\sum_{v\mid p}f_v
          =\frac1s\sum_{v\mid p}(e_v-1)f_v,
\]
where \(e_v,f_v\) are the ramification indices and residue degrees in \(F\).
The [different exponent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#signed) and the [root discriminant bound from the power basis of \(\alpha\)](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#primes) give
\[
             \sum_p\delta_p\log p
        \le \frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}F|}{s}\le\log d+2m.         \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the local discriminant exponent is \(\sum_{v\mid p}f_v\,v_v(\mathfrak D_{F_v/\mathbb Q_p})\).
Root discriminants increase up the tower \(F\subseteq K\).
This estimate charges only the ramified part of the degree in \(F\), regardless of the local degree of \(L\).

Choose one prime \(P_0\) of \(L\) above an odd \(p\), a lift \(x\) there of the residue Frobenius \(z\mapsto z^p\), and an integer \(1\le k\le b\).
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), write
\[
                    u(C)=\max_{Hg\in H\backslash G}
                                  \frac{|C\cap Hg|}{|C|}.
\]
Define \(u_p\) as the maximum of \(u(C(x^k))\) over these \(x,k\); conjugacy makes it independent of \(P_0\).
Take a maximizing pair for each \(p\), denoting the power by \(k_p\).
Then the [Frobenius-power weight](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#powers) satisfies
\[
                              w_{p,k_p}\ge u_p-\delta_p.                \tag{4}
\]
To see this, choose \(h\) uniformly in \(G\), set \(P=hP_0\), and take \(Hg\) attaining the maximum for \(C(x^{k_p})\).
The element \(h x^{k_p}h^{-1}\) meets this coset with probability \(u_p\).
Whenever it does, \(\Phi_{P,k_p}\), the full coset of lifts of \(z\mapsto z^{p^{k_p}}\) at \(P\), meets \(Hg\) there.
The chance that a uniform member of \(\Phi_{P,k_p}\) lies in \(Hg\), conditionally on \(P\), is \(1/e_v\) if the coset is met and zero otherwise, with \(v=P\cap F\).
Weighting the first event by \(1/e_v\) loses at most the expectation of \(1-1/e_v\).
This expectation is \(\delta_p\), since the uniformly sampled \(P\) restricts to \(v\) with probability \(e_v f_v/s\).
This proves (4) without requiring all powers of residue lifts to fill the inertia coset.
Under the group hypothesis one may always take \(k=b\) and \(x^b\) central to get \(u_p=1\).

<a id="interval"></a>
## A local sufficient condition for small total measure

For \(0<\eta\le1\), \(A_\eta\) sufficiently large depending only on \(\eta\), and \(m\le1\), put
\[
    T=\left\lceil A_\eta\frac{\log(2d)}{\log\log(5d)}\right\rceil,
                         \qquad X=T^2\log(2T).
\]
It suffices for (1), with constant depending on \(b,\eta\), to have
\[
       \sum_{X\le p\le2X}u_p\log p\ \ge\
                  \eta\sum_{X\le p\le2X}\log p .                       \tag{5}
\]
Sums on these intervals run over odd primes.
The [interval prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice) gives \(\sum_{X\le p\le2X}\log p\asymp X\) and \(t=\#\{X\le p\le2X\}\asymp T^2\).
The upper bound \(\log d+2\) in (3) is at most half the lower bound in (5) for sufficiently large \(A_\eta\).
Thus \(\sum w_{p,k_p}\log p\gg\eta T^2\log(2T)\).
In the [relative determinant inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#powers) the savings, after multiplication by \(T\), dominate the archimedean expense \(O(T^2(\log(2d)+\log(2T)))\) by a factor of at least two after increasing \(A_\eta\).
All powers are at most \((2X)^b\), and their height coefficient is \(O_b(T^{4+2b}(\log(2T))^b)\).
As \(D h(\alpha)=m/s\), this gives \(m/s\gg_{b,\eta}g_b(d)\).
It follows from \(m\le1\) that \(s\ll_{b,\eta}1/g_b(d)\), whence \(\log(2D)\asymp_{b,\eta}\log(2d)\) and \(g_b(D)\asymp_{b,\eta}g_b(d)\), also across bounded degrees by enlarging the comparison constants.
This proves the local assertion and, with \(\eta=1\), (1).

<a id="growth"></a>
## A single useful prime when the height is unrestricted

Still permitting an arbitrary \(G\), now let \(m\) have any size, fix \(0<\eta\le1\), and put
\[
             X=A_\eta\big((2D)^{4/\eta}+\log(2d)+m\big).
\]
If (5) holds on this interval, then
\[
                             \frac{m}{s}\gg_{b,\eta}
                                    \frac{\log(2X)}{X^b}.               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the sum of the weights \(w_{p,k_p}\log p\) is at least \(\eta/2\) times the total prime weight by (3), (4) and the prime interval estimate, for large enough \(A_\eta\).
Hence some \(p\) there has \(w_{p,k_p}\ge\eta/2\).
With this prime alone and \(T=1\), the determinant gives
\[
       2(1+p^{k_p})\,m/s\ \ge\ w_{p,k_p}\log p-\log(2D).
\]
The right side is at least \((\eta/4)\log p\), since \(p\ge(2D)^{4/\eta}\).
This proves (6) and uses only the field at the test primes.

For \(D\le D_0\), fixed \(b,\eta\), and \(m\le\log(2d)\), (6) would yield \(d\ll_{b,\eta,D_0}(\log(2d))^{b+1}\), because \(X\ll_{\eta,D_0}\log(2d)\) and \(s\ge d/D_0\).
This is impossible for large \(d\).
Consequently \(m>\log(2d)\) there, \(X\asymp_{\eta,D_0}m\), and (6) yields \(m^{b+1}\gg_{b,\eta,D_0}d\log(2m)\).
In particular \(\log(2m)\gg_{b,\eta,D_0}\log(2d)\) at large \(d\).
For bounded \(d\), (6) supplies a positive lower bound whenever \(m\le1\), while \(m>1\) already suffices after changing the constant.
This proves (2) whenever (5) holds on this latter interval with fixed \(\eta\), in particular with \(\eta=1\) under the group hypothesis.
For the Salem application the two preimages of each trace are the distinct inverse conjugates, giving \(D=2\) in the original field.
The trace closure \(L\) is totally real, whereas the Salem number has nonreal conjugates, so it lies outside \(L\).
Let \(E\) be the splitting field of the Salem polynomial and \(J=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\).
The [complex conjugations in the Salem splitting field](Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) contain distinct elements \(c_i\) indexed by inverse pairs, with \(j c_i j^{-1}=c_{j(i)}\) for \(j\in J\).
Every central element fixes each index, hence fixes all traces, and \(L\subseteq E^{Z(J)}\).
Thus \(J/Z(J)\) surjects onto \(G\), whose whole exponent may exceed \(b\).
Applying the [central-extension height theorem](../audits/literature/ADZ14/Yin_U001.md#central-local-height-theorem) to \(E\) through bounded local degree at a fixed prime would in particular require that local bound for \(L\), beyond a bound in the field fixed by \(Z(G)\).
A proof that arbitrary small-measure inputs meet either local test is still missing.
