# Resultants and the height of a small factor at a moving power

<a id="statement"></a>
## The height at the long exponent

Fix nonzero coprime \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with no prime dividing all the coefficients of both polynomials.
Let \(k,N\) be positive integers, \(N\ge(1+\deg B)k\), and put
\[
               P=X^N A(X^k)+B(X^k),\qquad
               I=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A(z)|,|B(z)|)\,d\sigma(z),
\]
where \(\sigma\) is uniform probability on the circle.
If \(S\) is a primitive irreducible factor of degree \(d\) with sufficiently small positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), then
\[
              \frac{N m}{d}=I+O_{A,B}\!\left(m\sqrt{\log(2/m)}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
In particular the estimate allows \(k\) to vary with \(S\), without a power bound on \(k\) in terms of \(d\).

Suppose now \(A(0)\ne0\) and \(B=\pm A^*\), where \(A^*(Y)=Y^{\deg A}A(1/Y)\).
Then \(I=m(A)\).
For each primitive irreducible \(q\mid A\), of multiplicity \(e\), take disjoint sufficiently small fixed disks about the distinct exterior zeros of \(A\).
Sum over the roots \(x\) of \(S\) for which \(x^k\) belongs to one of the disks about an exterior zero of \(q\).
Then
\[
       \frac{N}{e d}\sum_{x\ {\rm in\ this\ sum}}\log|x|
                     =m(q)+O_A\!\left(m\sqrt{\log(2/m)}\right).             \tag{2}
\]
For small \(m\), these disks together account for every exterior root of \(S\).
In particular, if \(A\) has a positive-measure factor all of whose roots are in the closed unit disk, every positive-measure integral divisor in the indicated range has a uniform gap, even if \(A\) also has exterior roots.
The coefficient of the highest power of \(A\) may be any nonzero integer.

<a id="finite"></a>
## An integral bound for each factor resultant

The group underlying \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(A,B)\) is finite.
Indeed a nonzero integer \(D_0\) belongs to the ideal by rational coprimality and clearing denominators.
Modulo each prime \(p\mid D_0\), at least one of \(A,B\) is a nonzero polynomial, so the quotient modulo \(p\) is finite as an additive group.
The Chinese remainder decomposition and the successive quotients by powers of \(p\), each an image of the quotient modulo \(p\), give finiteness.
Write \(\Gamma(A,B)\) for its cardinality, taking the zero ring to have cardinality one.

Let \(S\) be any monic integral divisor of \(P\).
Since \(B(X^k)=-X^N A(X^k)\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\), there is a surjection
\[
 \mathbb Z[X]/(A(X^k),B(X^k))\longrightarrow
                    \mathbb Z[X]/(S,A(X^k)).
\]
The group on the left is a direct sum of \(k\) copies of \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(A,B)\), by the basis \(1,X,\ldots,X^{k-1}\) over \(\mathbb Z[X^k]\).
The group on the right is the cokernel of multiplication by \(A(X^k)\) on the free group \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\).
Its order is the absolute value of the determinant, hence
\[
               1\le|\operatorname{Res}(S,A(X^k))|\mid\Gamma(A,B)^k.       \tag{3}
\]
The same reasoning gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(S,q(X^k))|\mid\Gamma(q,B)^k\) for each primitive irreducible \(q\mid A\), since \(B(X^k)\) is also a multiple of \(q(X^k)\) modulo \(S\).
These bounds include primes dividing a leading coefficient.

<a id="height"></a>
## Equidistribution in the degree of the powered root

For sufficiently small \(m\), \(S\) is a monic reciprocal unit polynomial up to sign by the [endpoint and nonreciprocal bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer).
Write \(h=m/d\) and \(t=\max_{S(x)=0}\log|x|\).
All other exponents lie at least \(k\) below the leading exponent, so \(kt\ll_{A,B}1\) by the leading term at a largest root.
The [quadratic radial floor](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(dtm\ge1/20\).
Consequently \(k/d=O_{A,B}(m)\) and \(kh=O_{A,B}(m^2)\).
The width of \(P\), after removing any monomial, is at most \(N+k\deg A=O_{A,B}(N)\) and its term count is bounded.
The [width inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) thus gives \(Nh\gg_{A,B}1\), in particular \(N/k\gg_{A,B}m^{-2}\).

Choose a root \(\alpha\) of \(S\) and put \(\beta=\alpha^k,\ \delta=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
The norm \(\gamma=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(\beta)}\alpha\) satisfies \(\gamma^k=\beta^{d/\delta}\), so it is a nontorsion unit of height \((d/\delta)h\) and degree at most \(\delta\).
Its Mahler measure is at most \(m\).
The same radial floor gives \(\delta(2m)^2\ge1/10\), since the sum of the absolute logarithmic moduli of the conjugates of \(\gamma\) equals \(2m(\gamma)\).
In particular \(\log(2\delta)/\delta=O(m^2\log(2/m))\) for sufficiently small \(m\).

For fixed coprime integral polynomials \(U,V\), the [estimate for the logarithm of their maximum](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test), applied in degree \(\delta\) and at height \(kh\), gives
\[
 \frac1d\sum_{S(x)=0}\log\max(|U(x^k)|,|V(x^k)|)
 =\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\,d\sigma
       +O_{A,B,U,V}\!\left(m\sqrt{\log(2/m)}\right).                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed all \(\delta\) conjugates of \(\beta\) occur equally often, and its measure is \(\delta kh\).
The error in that estimate at fixed \(U,V\) is \(O(kh+\delta^{-1}+\sqrt{kh+\log(2\delta)/\delta})\).
For \(U=A,V=B\) the sum before division by \(d\) equals
\(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,A(X^k))|+Nm\), by the equation at each root.
Using (3) and \(k/d=O(m)\) proves (1).

<a id="allocation"></a>
## The exterior contribution from each irreducible edge factor

Since \(A\) is coprime to \(A^*\), it has no circle zero.
The identity \(|A|=|B|\) on the circle gives \(I=m(A)\) by Jensen's formula.
The numbers \(z=x^k\) lie in a fixed compact annulus by \(kt=O(1)\) and reciprocity.
In a neighborhood of the unit circle \(\log|B(z)/A(z)|\) is smooth and vanishes on the circle, hence its absolute value is bounded by \(C|\log|z||\).
The equation at \(x\) gives \((N/k)\log|z|=\log|B(z)/A(z)|\).
Once \(N/k>C\), every such \(z\) in that neighborhood lies exactly on the circle.

At every \(z\) beyond this neighborhood on the exterior side, \(A(z)=-x^{-N}B(z)\) tends uniformly to zero as \(m\to0\).
Thus each such \(z\) is in a prescribed disk about an exterior zero of \(A\) for sufficiently small \(m\).
On the interior side \(B(z)\) tends uniformly to zero, placing \(z\) close to an interior zero of \(B\).
Each exterior \(z\) satisfies \(\log|x|\gg_A1/k\), so the fraction of exterior conjugates is \(O_A(kh)\).

Fix \(q\mid A\) as in the statement.
At the exterior disks of \(q\) the functions \(B,A/q^e\) are bounded above and away from zero, giving
\[
                     \log|q(x^k)|=-(N/e)\log|x|+O_A(1).
\]
On the circle, at other exterior disks, and at the interior zeros of \(B\), the modulus of \(q\) is bounded away from zero by coprimality and the choice of disks.
Choose a fixed integer \(M\ge1\) so that \(1/M\) is below these lower bounds, shrinking the disks if needed.
The fixed truncated function \(\log\max(|q(z)|,1/M)\) is covered by (4) using \(U=Mq,V=1\) and subtracting \(\log M\).
Its circle integral equals \(m(q)\).
At the selected roots its value is bounded, and at all other roots it agrees with \(\log|q(x^k)|\).
Thus its empirical average differs from
\[
       d^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,q(X^k))|
                 +\frac{N}{ed}\sum_{\text{selected }x}\log|x|
\]
by \(O_A(kh)\).
Equation (3) for \(q\) proves (2).
If the selected set is empty and \(m(q)>0\), (2) excludes a sequence \(m\to0\).
Additivity and the positive cost of integer content pass the bound to all positive-measure divisors.

<a id="rectangle"></a>
## A family with a gap in every direction

Suppose in addition \(|A(0)|=|\operatorname{lc}A|\), and both \(A\) and \(A^*\) have a positive-measure factor with all roots in the closed disk.
Then for every pair of integer exponents \((k,N)\), every positive-measure integral divisor of a nonzero specialization of \(Y A(X)\pm A^*(X)\) at \((X,Y)=(Z^k,Z^N)\) has a uniform gap depending only on \(A\), after discarding a monomial.
To see this, apply the [direction theorem on a Newton polygon](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement).
Every edge of a summand in a Minkowski sum is parallel to an edge of the full polygon in dimension two, by exposing that edge of the summand.
If a two-dimensional irreducible component has an edge root off the circle, the full edge polynomial has that root as well, since the two exposed face polynomials multiply.
The full polygon here is a rectangle and both vertical edge binomials have all roots on the circle by the endpoint equality.
Any shrinking sequence therefore has a subsequence with \(|N|/|k|\to\infty\).
The direction theorem also excludes zero \(k\) along this sequence.
Simultaneously negating the exponents leaves the specialization unchanged up to a monomial and sign by reciprocity.
We may take \(k>0\), and for negative \(N\) we interchange \(A,A^*\) after removing a monomial and sign.
The preceding gap applies to each orientation.

For example take
\[
        A=(2X-1)(X+2)(X^3-6X^2+7X+1).
\]
The cubic \(C\) has \(C-C^*=-13X(X-1)\) and vanishes at neither zero nor one.
It vanishes at neither \(2\) nor \(-1/2\), so \(A,A^*\) are coprime.
Both endpoints have modulus two.
The factors \(2X-1\) of \(A\) and \(1+2X\) of \(A^*\) supply the two positive-measure closed-disk factors, while both horizontal edges also have exterior roots.
The specialization at \(k=N=1\) with the minus sign is
\[
    XA-A^*=(X-1)(X+1)(X^2-3X+1)(2X^2-X+2).
\]
In particular this family does contain a monic nontorsion unit factor.
