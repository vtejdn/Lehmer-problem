# Extracting cyclotomic factors whose conductors are divisible by a moving integer

<a id="statement"></a>
## Divisibility of exponents and conductors

Let \(K_d(\theta)=\log|1-e^{id\theta}|\) on the circle with normalized angular measure.
For a finitely supported real sequence \((b_d)_{d\ge1}\) and an integer \(q\ge1\), put
\[
 L=\sum_{d\ge1}b_d K_d,\qquad L_{[q]}=\sum_{u\ge1}b_{qu}K_u,\qquad
 W(m)=\prod_{p\mid m}\frac{p+1}{p-1},\quad W(1)=1,
\]
where the product runs over primes.
For nonempty support let \(g=\gcd\{d:b_d\ne0\}\).
Then
\[
                    \|L_{[q]}\|_1\le W(q/\gcd(q,g))\|L\|_1.        \tag{1}
\]
For empty support both norms vanish.
The bound is uniform in the valuations of \(q\), and in particular \(W(p^k)\le3\) for every prime power.

Let \(A=\prod_{n\ge1}\Phi_n^{e_n}\) be a nonconstant monic cyclotomic product with nonnegative integer multiplicities and let \(B=A/A(0)=\prod_d(1-X^d)^{b_d}\).
The polynomial
\[
               A_{[q]}(X)=\prod_{m\ge1}\Phi_m(X)^{e_{qm}}           \tag{2}
\]
has normalized binomial exponents exactly \(b_{qu}\) at \(u\), and its boundary logarithm equals \(L_{[q]}\).
Suppose along a sequence of such parents and choices of \(q\) that \(W(q/\gcd(q,g))\) and \(m(A(X)+aX^k)\) are bounded, with integer \(k\ge0\), complex \(|a|\ge1\), and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Every subsequence of \(A_{[q]}/A_{[q]}(0)\) has a further subsequence converging locally uniformly in the open disk to a finite product of \(1-X^u\) with signed integer exponents.
Along each such convergent subsequence, \(b_{qu}\) eventually equals the limiting exponent at every fixed \(u\).
Every finite signed binomial product normalized at zero occurs as such a limit even when the positive integers \(q\) are prescribed in advance.

In particular, fix a finite bound for \(W(q/\gcd(q,g))\).
If \(b_{qj}\ne0\) for every \(1\le j\le N\), then \(m(A+aX^k)\) tends uniformly to infinity as \(N\to\infty\), with unrestricted signs at these and all other indices.
More generally the same divergence holds along any sequence for which each index in a fixed infinite set of positive integers eventually has \(b_{qu}\ne0\).
If at most \(H\) of the parent's distinct conductors are divisible by \(q\), bounded shifted measure and bounded cost in (1) bound both \(\omega(n/q)\) and \(e_n\) for all those conductors, in terms of the two bounds and \(H\) alone.
Here \(\omega\) counts distinct prime divisors.

<a id="extraction"></a>
## Circle averages selecting all multiples of a prime power

Write \(D_h f(\theta)=f(h\theta)\) and let \(T_h f(\theta)=h^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}f((\theta+2\pi j)/h)\).
The dilation is an \(L^1\) isometry and the average is a contraction by change of variables.
With \(s=\gcd(h,d)\), the \(h\) terms in the average run through all \(h/s\)-th roots of unity with multiplicity \(s\) in the phase multiplier.
Factoring \(1-z^{h/s}\) thus gives, almost everywhere,
\[
                        T_h K_d=\frac{s}{h}K_{d/s}.
\]
For \(h=p^k,\ k\ge1\), every \(d\) with \(p^k\nmid d\) has \(\gcd(d,p^k)=\gcd(d,p^{k-1})\).
Its contribution cancels in \(T_{p^k}-p^{-1}T_{p^{k-1}}\).
For \(d=p^ku\) the contribution is \(K_u-p^{-1}D_pK_u\).
Therefore
\[
       (T_{p^k}-p^{-1}T_{p^{k-1}})L=(I-p^{-1}D_p)L_{[p^k]} .
\]
The inverse on the right is the operator-norm convergent series \(\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-j}D_{p^j}\).
This gives the norm bound \((1+p^{-1})/(1-p^{-1})\) for extraction at \(p^k\), independent of \(k\).
Successive extractions at \(v\) and \(w\) send the exponent at \(u\) to \(b_{vwu}\).
Applying the prime-power bound once per distinct prime divisor gives \(\|L_{[q]}\|_1\le W(q)\|L\|_1\).
For the refinement set \(s=\gcd(g,q)\), \(q'=q/s\), and \(\widetilde L=\sum_{v}b_{gv}K_v\).
Then \(L=D_g\widetilde L\) and \(L_{[q]}=D_{g/s}\widetilde L_{[q']}\), since \(q\mid gv\) exactly when \(q'\mid v\).
The two dilation isometries prove (1).

<a id="limits"></a>
## Normalized parents at the extracted conductors

By the [cyclotomic binomial formula](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity),
\[
        b_{qu}=\sum_{qu\mid n} e_n\mu(n/(qu))
              =\sum_{u\mid m}e_{qm}\mu(m/u).
\]
The right side is precisely the exponent at \(u\) of (2), with every cyclotomic normalized at zero, including \(\Phi_1\).
When the selected conductor set is empty the polynomial in (2) is one and all its binomial exponents vanish.
The [circle-root integral estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) at modulus \(|a|\ge1\) bounds \(\|L\|_1\) by \(2m(A+aX^k)+2\sqrt{\pi}\,\zeta(3/2)\), since \(L\) has mean zero.
Consequently (1) gives a common \(L^1\) bound for the boundary logarithms of all extracted parents in the sequence.
When \(A_{[q]}\) is nonconstant the upper circle-root estimate at modulus one gives \(m(A_{[q]}+1)\le\frac12\|L_{[q]}\|_1+\log2\).
The [normalized limit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v104.md#statement) now applies to the extracted monic parents.
Any subsequence containing infinitely many empty selections also admits the constant sublimit one.
For a locally uniformly convergent subsequence with empty selections, either there are only finitely many such terms or the limit is one.
In the latter case Cauchy's formula and integrality give eventual stabilization at each coefficient even on the mixed subsequence.
In every case the normalized binomial exponent at \(u\) is recovered successively from the coefficient of degree \(u\) and the earlier exponents, so the same stabilization holds for \(b_{qu}\).

Conversely take the nonconstant monic approximants \(P_i\) given by the converse normalized limit theorem for any prescribed finite signed binomial product \(R\).
These have bounded boundary logarithmic norm by the lower circle-root estimate and their bounded unit-modulus shifted measures.
For any prescribed \(q_i\), set \(A_i=P_i(X^{q_i})\).
Every root of these monic integer polynomials is a root of unity, so they are cyclotomic products.
Their normalized binomial supports are multiples of \(q_i\), so \(q_i\mid g_i\), the cost in (1) is one, and extraction at \(q_i\) recovers the normalized \(P_i\) exactly.
Angular dilation preserves their logarithmic norms, and the upper circle-root estimate bounds all their unit-modulus shifted measures uniformly.
The normalized extracted parents converge to \(R\).

<a id="obstructions"></a>
## Signed progressions and conductors divisible by the chosen integer

If shifted measures stayed bounded on a subsequence with \(N\to\infty\), choose a convergent further subsequence of the normalized extracted parents.
For each fixed \(u\), the signed integer \(b_{qu}\) is eventually nonzero there, and stabilization forces a nonzero exponent at \(u\) in the limit.
This contradicts finiteness of the limiting signed support.
The same reasoning works with each \(u\) in the stated infinite set.
This proves uniform divergence for complete initial progressions because a failure of uniformity would give a sequence with bounded measures and lengths tending to infinity.

Bounded cost and bounded shifted measure for the original sequence give a bounded logarithmic norm for the parents in (2).
If the extracted conductor set is nonempty and has cardinality at most \(H\), apply the [fixed-conductor-count theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#statement) to \(A_{[q]}\) with the shift \(+1\).
Its shifted measures are bounded as above, while that theorem forces them to diverge if the maximum of \(\omega(n/q)\) or of \(e_n\) over the selected conductors tends to infinity.
This gives the claimed local bounds irrespective of how many other conductors occur in \(A\).
In particular, if \(q\) is a conductor with no proper multiple among the parent's conductors, (2) is \(\Phi_1^{e_q}\) and (1) gives \(e_q\|K_1\|_1\le W(q/\gcd(q,g))\|L\|_1\) directly.
The progression test concerns normalized binomial exponents at multiples of \(q\), while the conductor count refers to the original positive cyclotomic multiplicities in (2).
