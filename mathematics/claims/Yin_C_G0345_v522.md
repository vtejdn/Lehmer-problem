# Each common row needs many occupied exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## A separate support bound irrespective of the other row

Use all primes \(S\) in \([L,2L]\), let \(r=|S|\), \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\), \(m=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\), and take the [full-interval width](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width) \(b=rj(L)\).
Suppose \(U,V\) pass the [common row test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width, and one reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) occurs with multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\) in every graph.
For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
 \min\{|\operatorname{supp}U|,|\operatorname{supp}V|\}>
 (1/2-\epsilon)\frac{\log L}{\log\log L}.                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular along any unbounded sequence of these rows the lower limit of the left side times \(\log\log L/\log L\) is at least \(1/2\).
Both rows may have arbitrary integer content subject to the common cost cutoff.

The local estimate used below also applies before that cutoff.
For either row \(W=cY^aW_0\), take \(c>0\) to be its coefficient content, \(a\) its smallest exponent and \(W_0\) primitive.
Write \(e=\deg P\) and let \(D_n\) be the part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n)|\) prime to \(n\).
Let \(R_n\) count ordered representations \(n=p+q\) by distinct primes in \(S\).
For any collection \(\mathcal G\) of such sums, assume that for each \(n\in\mathcal G\), every \(\ell\mid D_n\), and every root \(x\) of \(\bar P\) of order \(n\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell^\times\), at least one first prime \(p\) of a representation has \(\overline W_0(x^p)\ne0\).
Then
\[
            k\sum_{n\in\mathcal G} v_\ell(D_n)\le e\,v_\ell(c)
            \quad\text{for each rational prime }\ell,\qquad
            k\sum_{n\in\mathcal G}\log D_n\le e\log c.                   \tag{2}
\]
These assertions use graph divisibility itself, so (2) also holds at any width or coefficient cost under the stated local hypotheses for odd primes in \(S\).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Compressing the exponents on a group of roots of unity

Let \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\) be the occupied exponents of \(W_0\).
Suppose there is a positive integer \(u\) for which the residues \(ud_i\bmod n\) are distinct and can be placed in an interval of \(D\) consecutive steps on the residue circle, including its two endpoints.
If \(\ell\nmid n\), the number of zeros of \(\overline W_0\) on the group \(\mu_n\) of \(n\)-th roots in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\) is at most \(D\).
Indeed put \(d=\gcd(u,n)\) and take one representative \(\eta\) in each of the \(d\) cosets of \(\mu_n^u\) in \(\mu_n\).
On \(X\in\mu_n\), multiplying \(\overline W_0(\eta X^u)\) by a monomial and replacing exponents by representatives gives a polynomial of degree at most \(D\).
Primitivity leaves at least one nonzero coefficient in this characteristic, every \(\eta^{d_i}\) is a unit, and the distinct exponents remain distinct after the replacements, so the polynomial is nonzero.
Each zero of \(\overline W_0\) in that coset has \(d\) preimages, so the coset has at most \(D/d\) zeros.
Summing proves the assertion, also when \(u\) is a nonunit modulo \(n\).
For \(s=1\) the primitive monomial has no zero on \(\mu_n\).

<a id="periods"></a>
## Enough periods for every proposed small support

Fix \(\epsilon\) as in (1) and suppose \(s\le(1/2-\epsilon)\log L/\log\log L\).
The [localized Goldbach estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#goldbach) gives \(R_n\ge L/(16m^2)\) for all but \(o(L)\) even integers of \([5L/2,7L/2]\).
Write \(B=\lceil300m^2\rceil\) and \(U_*=\lfloor L^{1-\epsilon}\rfloor\).
We have \(B^{s-1}\le U_*\) for sufficiently large \(L\), since \(\log B=2\log\log L+O(1)\).
Also \(b<L\) for large \(L\), by \(j\le(\log_2 L)^{1/4}\) and the dyadic prime count \(r\ll L/\log L\).
Discard every \(n\) that divides \(u(d_i-d_j)\) for some \(i\ne j\) and \(1\le u\le U_*\).
The number discarded is at most
\[
      s^2 U_*\max_{1\le N\le L U_*}\tau(N)\le L^{1-\epsilon+o(1)}=o(L).
\]
For the divisor estimate, for any \(\delta>0\) and sufficiently large primes \(p\) one has \(a+1\le2^a\le p^{\delta a}\) for each \(a\ge0\).
Over the finitely many smaller primes the product of \(\sup_{a\ge0}(a+1)p^{-\delta a}\) is finite, giving \(\tau(N)\ll_\delta N^\delta\).

For every surviving even \(n\), partition \([0,1)^{s-1}\) into \(B^{s-1}\) equal boxes and consider the fractional parts of the vectors \(v((d_i-d_1)/n)_{i=2}^s\) for integers \(0\le v\le B^{s-1}\).
For \(s\ge2\), two vectors lie in the same box, so their positive index difference \(u\le U_*\) gives all \(u(d_i-d_1)\bmod n\) representatives of absolute value at most \(n/B\).
The deletion rule ensures the scaled residues are distinct, hence the [root count](#roots) is at most \(2n/B<R_n\) in every characteristic prime to \(n\).
For \(s=1\) the same strict inequality for the number of roots follows directly.
Write \(\mathcal G\) for these surviving even periods with the Goldbach lower bound.
For every order-\(n\) element \(x\), the \(R_n\) values \(x^p\) are pairwise distinct since the first primes determine the representations and their differences have absolute value less than \(n\).
Thus the hypothesis of (2) holds on \(\mathcal G\) simultaneously at all its required components.

The set \(\mathcal G\) has cardinality \(\gg L\), with uniform constants over the proposed supports.
The [totient estimate for positive-density subsets](Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#totients) gives \(\sum_{n\in\mathcal G}\varphi(n)\gg L^2\): the mean of \(\sum_{p\mid n}1/p\) over \(n\le4L\), with \(p\) prime, is bounded, so on a positive proportion of \(\mathcal G\) this sum is bounded and \(\varphi(n)\gg n\).

<a id="content"></a>
## Cost of the individual row content

For each \(n\in\mathcal G\) and \(\ell\mid D_n\), lift the distinct residual irreducible components of \(\Phi_n\) at which \(P\) vanishes, as in the [prime-to-order layer calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#layers).
Write \(\xi_i\) for the lifted root of unity, \(f_i\) for its residue degree and \(t_i=v_\ell(P(\xi_i))>0\), with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Choose \(p\) as in the hypothesis of (2) for its reduction \(x\).
The [integral sum-period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement) gives \(W(\xi_i^p)\in P(\xi_i)^k O_i\) in its unramified valuation ring \(O_i\).
For the reverse ordering, inversion in the sum-period ring interchanges the two powers and preserves the ideal of \(P^k\) by reciprocity.
Both \(\xi_i^{pa}\) and \(W_0(\xi_i^p)\) are units, whence \(v_\ell(c)\ge kt_i\).
Across all orders prime to this \(\ell\), the residual components are distinct and divide \(\bar P\), giving \(\sum_i f_i\le e\).
As \(\sum_i f_i t_i=\sum_{n\in\mathcal G}v_\ell(D_n)\), multiplication by residue degrees and summation prove (2), with its logarithmic form following on summing over primes.

For every qualifying seed the [width calibration](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width) gives \(\log D_n\ge(1-1/j)h\varphi(n)\) at all these orders, where \(h=\log M(P)\), and \(h\log L/e\gg j\).
Thus a proposed small support forces \(\log c\gg kh L^2/e\), giving \(\log c/\Sigma\gg kj\) since \(\Sigma\ll L^2/\log L\).
On the other hand \(c\le\|W\|_1\le H\le2^\Sigma\).
As \(j\to\infty\) this contradiction rules out the proposed support for either choice of row, proving (1).

The coordinating research agent for Sheng Yin derived the uniform individual support bound using compression on most central periods and conversion of a missed root into a coefficient content valuation.
