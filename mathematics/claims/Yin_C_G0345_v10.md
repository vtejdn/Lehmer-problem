# A uniform Pisot excess for either sign at every fixed Chebyshev offset

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed offsets with a varying sparse remainder

Fix integers \(d,s\ge1\) and \(\eta>0\).
For a positive-constant Pisot input on branch \(j=2\), let \(q\) be its constant, \(\theta\) its exterior root, \(C\) its [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) of degree \(h\), and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose \(\Pi\) has at most \(s\) terms and agrees through degree \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) with \(2-z^d\) or \(2+z^d\).
Then \(q\) is bounded, and \(\theta-q\) is bounded below by a strictly positive constant, in terms of \(d,s,\eta\) alone.
In particular the conclusion holds for \(C(x)=2U_h(x/2)\pm U_{h-d}(x/2)\), \(h\ge d\), for each fixed \(d\) and both degree parities.
Indeed these lifts are \(2\pm z^d+z^{2h+2-d}(\mp1-2z^d)\).
The uniformity in \(h\) permits the bound to depend on the offset \(d\).

The [fixed-minus theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#statement) proves the first sign.
Agent-S supplied the [positive-sign extension](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v56.md#statement), including the additional phase levels when the primitive offset is even.

<a id="phase"></a>
## The additional phase levels for the positive sign

Suppose \(q\to\infty\) for the plus sign and put \(A=2+z^d\).
The [sparse-prefix argument](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#harmonics) gives a limit \(K=A U/V\) with coprime squarefree cyclotomic products \(U,V\) of constant one.
The reduction uses the integer series \(A/K\), irreducibility of \(A\), and its coefficient one at degree \(d\), which excludes a numerator consisting of twice a cyclotomic product.
The cutoff estimates use \(|A|\ge1\) on the circle.
Put \(g=\gcd(d,\deg V-\deg U-d)\), \(a=d/g,\ b=(\deg V-\deg U-d)/g,\ w=z^g\), and \(\delta=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
Up to the factor \(U/V\) the continuous density \(\rho=2\Re K\) has bracket
\[
                  P(w)=(2+w^a)+\delta w^b(2w^a+1).
\]
Its odd-order circle zeros are torsion since \(\rho\) is a nonnegative nonzero Laurent polynomial, and \(M(\rho)=M(P)\) for multiplicative Mahler measure.
For odd \(a\), substituting \(-w\) yields the negative bracket with sign \(\delta(-1)^{b+1}\).
The [negative bracket calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets) then gives \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\), except for \(a=1\) with \(K=2+z^d\) or \(K=(2+z^d)/(1+z^d)\).
In the first case there are no torsion zeros of the bracket so neither cyclotomic product has roots.
In the second the bracket is \(3(1+w)\) and its simple roots lift to the \(d\) roots of \(1+z^d\).
Even density multiplicities put exactly those roots in \(UV\), and \(\deg V-\deg U=d\) puts them all in \(V\).

For even \(a\), \(b\) is odd, so \(w\mapsto-w\) exchanges the signs.
Write \(w=\exp(i((2r+1)\pi+t)/a)\) so \(w^a=-e^{it}\).
The zero equation is \(w^b(2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it})=\delta\).
The [torsion equation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#phase) allows only \(t=0,\pi/3,\pi,5\pi/3,2\pi\) in one turn at torsion zeros.
The phase \(bt/(2\pi)+a\arg((2e^{it}-1)/(2-e^{it}))/(2\pi)\), continuously lifted from zero, takes the values
\[
        0,\quad (a+l)/6,\quad l/2,\quad (5l-a)/6,\quad l,
        \qquad l=a+b,
\]
at these five points.
The target levels over the \(a\) sectors are the half-integers: the sector translations are \(rb+b/2\), the two signs differ by \(a/2\), and \(b\) is coprime to \(a\).
Consecutive values cannot strictly straddle a half-integer, since this would give an odd crossing in the open arc.
Hence \(|l|\le4,\ |a+l|\le6,\ |2l-a|\le6\), so \(a\le6\) and equality would force \(l=0\) against coprimality.
For \(a=2,4\), testing the odd integers \(l=-3,-1,1,3\) between these consecutive values leaves \((a,b)=(2,-3),(2,-1),(4,-5)\).
For sign \(+1\) the first bracket, after clearing the monomial, is \((w+1)(w^4-w^3+3w^2-w+1)\), of measure below \(5/2\) by the [reciprocal quartic computation](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets).
The second is \((w+1)(w^2+w+1)\), of measure one.
The third is \((w+1)(w^2+w+1)w^3(X^3-2X^2-X+3)\), \(X=w+w^{-1}\).
The cubic has a root in \((-2,-1)\), no rational root and discriminant \(-31\).
Its real root has nonreal conjugates and so cannot be a torsion trace.
Its simple preimages violate the odd-zero condition.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## The two exceptional envelopes and the prime specialization

The [harmonic comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#harmonics) on the common prefix gives integral Laurent polynomials \(H_k=(1+z^d/2)\sigma c_k\) for each positive conditional moment, with \(H_1\ne0\), \(\sigma=2\Re(2/K)\) away from atoms, and \(D=|1+z^d/2|\sigma\) bounding their moduli.
It uses the same pair of coefficient indices \(kn+l,kn+l-d\), and \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\) since \(M(K)=2\).
For \(M(P)<2\sqrt2\), the [separated-block argument](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) gives finitely many nonzero shapes up to monomial shift.
In the remaining cases the [exceptional envelopes](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#exceptions) are obtained by changing the variable \(z^d\) to \(-z^d\) in their real-valued formulas.
For \(K=2+z^d\), \(D^2\) has mean \(7/2\) and maximum four, attained only at \(z^d=\pm1\).
The same fourth-moment test leaves only unit monomials and unit binomials, whose exponent gap is at most \(2d\) by the number of alignment points.
Here \(M(D)=(2+\sqrt3)/2\).
For \(K=(2+z^d)/(1+z^d)\), \(D=3|1+z^d|^2/|2+z^d|\), and every \(H_k\) is divisible by \((1+z^d)^2\) by quadratic vanishing.
The squared norm of the integer quotient is strictly less than three, since equality in the integrated bound would make its product with \(2+z^d\) a nonzero monomial.
For a unit binomial quotient with gap different from \(d\) that product has squared norm ten, exceeding its pointwise squared bound nine.
This leaves finitely many shapes and gives \(M(D)=3/2\).

In all cases \(M(D)<2\).
For each nonzero shape \(L\), the phase ratio
\[
            \frac{L(z)(2+z^{-d})}{L(z^{-1})(2+z^d)}
\]
has a pole at any root \(\alpha\) of \(2+z^d\), since \(M(L)<2\) prevents divisibility by this irreducible polynomial and \(2+\alpha^{-d}=3/2\).
Since \(|\alpha|>1\), its successive squares are distinct, and the ratio cannot satisfy the rational squaring identity.
The [prime specialization and last-harmonic contradiction](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) apply verbatim to these integer shapes and positive conditional probabilities.
This bounds \(q\), and the [derivative estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives the claimed excess.
