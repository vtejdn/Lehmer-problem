# Finite Mahler measure spectra for classical subspace actions

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exclusion of all three classical subspace actions

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number, and let \(\gamma\) be the product of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) in its root-of-unity quotient class.
Write \(m=m(\alpha)=m(\gamma)>0\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure and \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
Use the [multiplicative labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) of \(\gamma\), with \(w\in\{1,2\}\) roots per label.
For fixed \(M>0\) and \(m\le M\), once \(d\) is sufficiently large depending only on \(M\), no block of \(b\ge2\) labels has induced action a primitive almost simple classical group \(T\le H\le\operatorname{Aut}(T)\) in a subspace action of Liebeck and Shalev.
This includes the single-subspace actions, the two linear configurations admitting graph automorphisms and the two even-characteristic symplectic actions with orthogonal stabilizer.
The field, natural dimension and subspace dimensions can all vary.
Consequently the positive measures admitting any such block form a finite set in each bounded interval. [Liebeck and Shalev, §1.1](../audits/literature/LS99/Yin_U001.md#subspace-action)

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Degrees of configurations inside a block

Suppose to the contrary that \(d\to\infty\) through examples at fixed \(M\).
Put \(u=d/(wb)\) and \(e=[H:T]\).
The two-root and group order bounds give \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) and \(\log |H|/\log b\to\infty\), uniformly in the block. [Block size](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) · [Induced group order](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#order)
In particular \(\log d\asymp\log b\).
Let \(Q\) be the cardinality of the natural module field and \(n\) its dimension, so the usual unitary group parameter is \(\sqrt Q\).
The classical outer automorphism tables give \(e\ll n\log(2Q)\), including diagonal, field and graph automorphisms.
Indeed the diagonal factor is at most \(\max(n,4)\), the field factor at most \(2\log_2 Q\), and the graph factor at most six.
Also \(|T|\le Q^{n^2}\) by its matrix realization up to a central quotient.
The least faithful permutation degree estimate recalled in the proof of Liebeck–Shalev Theorem 1.1 gives \(b\ge Q^{n/4}\) apart from finitely many classical groups, using this weaker exponent to allow either field convention for unitary groups.
Thus bounded \(n\) would give bounded \(\log |H|/\log b\), and we may take \(n\to\infty\).
In particular \(eQ^2=b^{o(1)}\), and \(\log d=O(n^2\log(2Q))\). [Liebeck and Shalev, p. 501](../audits/literature/LS99/Yin_U001.md#least-degree) · [Byott, §5, Tables 1–2](../audits/literature/Byo15/Yin_U001.md#classical-outer)

The normal subgroup \(T\) is transitive by primitivity and faithfulness.
For an ordered configuration of \(j\) independent labels suppose its \(T\)-orbit has size at most \(b^{j-1}c\).
There are at most \(e\) such orbits in its \(H\)-orbit, \(u\) possible images of the block and at most \(w^j\) choices of roots above the labels.
The joint degree over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is at most \(u w^j e b^{j-1}c\ll_j d^{j-1}ec\), and at most \(d^j\).
The product bound at the common height \(m/d\) gives
\[
                  m^j\gg_j \frac{d}{ec(\log(3d))^{K_j}}.                 \tag{1}
\]
We shall apply this for \(j=3,4\) and \(c\le Q^2+Q+1\), which contradicts bounded \(m\).
Every pair of different labels is also independent.
If the transitive \(T\)-set of size \(b\) projects equivariantly onto a \(T\)-set of size \(b_0>1\) with fiber size \(b/b_0>1\), a pair in one fiber has \(T\)-orbit size at most \(b^2/b_0\).
Its joint degree is at most \(uw^2e b^2/b_0\le e d^2/b_0\).
The two-coordinate bound gives \(m^2\gg b_0/(e(\log(3d))^{212})\).
Such a projection is impossible for these examples, as \(T\) is simple and the faithful degree bound gives \(b_0\ge Q^{n/4}\).
This last quantity dominates \(e(\log(3d))^{212}\) uniformly as \(n\to\infty\). [Independent height products](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) · [Two independent heights](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001)

<a id="patch"></a>
## Independent labels on subspaces with a common hyperplane

Write \(U=D\oplus\langle v\rangle\) with \(\dim U=k\), and let \(W\) be a natural-field space disjoint from \(U\).
Consider labels parametrized by
\[
                         U_t=D+\langle v+t\rangle,\qquad t\in W.          \tag{2}
\]
A second subspace in a label may be held fixed.
Assume the labels are distinct and their stabilizer as a set in \(T\) induces all translations of \(W\).
If \(|W|\to\infty\), the multiplicative rank of the roots over this set tends to infinity.
Indeed the preimage of its set stabilizer acts on the torsion-free lattice generated by those roots modulo torsion.
The lattice action has finite image, since the roots are permuted, and its line permutation image is transitive on the \(|W|\) distinct label lines.
The [injection modulo three](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) gives \(|W|\le3^{s^2}\) for lattice rank \(s\).

For \(Q\ge3\) use an ordinary line in this finite real projective set of labels.
Choose its two points \(L_a,L_b\), then a third parameter \(z\ne a,b\) on their natural-field affine line in \(W\).
The corresponding three labels are multiplicatively independent by ordinariness.
Any linear image of their subspaces has third subspace containing the intersection of the first two and contained in their sum.
The quotient of the sum by that intersection has dimension two, giving at most \(Q+1\) choices.
The fixed companion, if present, is already specified by the images of the first two labels.
The \(T\)-orbit has size at most \(b^2(Q+1)\).

For \(Q=2\) use the [binary parallelogram argument](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#affine) inside this set.
In the quotient of the multiplicative span by the origin label, take an ordinary line among the distinct projected labels and choose preimages with parameters \(a,b\).
The rank is at least four before projection.
The three original labels at \(0,a,b\) are independent, and translations by \(0,a,b,a+b\) permute the four triples transitively.
Thus the projection of the label at \(a+b\) is distinct from the two chosen projected points and lies outside their ordinary line.
All four labels are independent.
The last subspace contains the common intersection \(D\) and belongs to the sum of the first three, which has dimension \(k+2\).
The quotient by \(D\) has dimension three and at most \(Q^2+Q+1\) lines.
This bounds the \(T\)-orbit by \(b^3(Q^2+Q+1)\).
In each case only the matrix action of \(T\) was used for counting images, so (1) permits every almost simple extension actually inducing the action. [Ordinary lines in the multiplicative space](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#ordinary)

<a id="subspaces"></a>
## Single subspaces and the two linear configurations

For linear groups we may pass to annihilators in the dual module to assume \(k\le n/2\).
Choose \(W\) complementary to \(U\) in the full module.
The shears \(v\mapsto v+t\) fixing \(D+W\) have determinant one and act on (2) as translations.
For a linear pair \((U,A)\) choose the smaller dimension \(k\), allowing reversal of the underlying configuration by duality.
If \(U\oplus A=V\), keep \(A\) fixed and take \(W=A\).
If \(U\subseteq A\), keep \(A\) fixed and take for \(W\) a complement of \(U\) in \(A\), of dimension \(n-2k\).
In both cases extend the shears by the identity on a complementary space.
When \(k\ge n/4\) the number of configurations is at least half the number of all \(k\)-spaces, hence at least \(Q^{k(n-k)}/2\).
This follows from transitivity of \(\operatorname{SL}_n(Q)\) on \(k\)-spaces and the fact that an unordered configuration has at most two components of the given dimension.
The Gaussian coefficient lower bound also follows by counting graphs of all maps from a \(k\)-space into a complement.
Then \(|H|\le eQ^{n^2}\) contradicts \(\log|H|/\log b\to\infty\).
For \(k<n/4\) the components have different dimensions and \(W\) has dimension at least \(n/2\) in both cases.
This covers the graph-automorphism pairs including the equal-dimension possibilities.

For forms consider first an ambient space with nondegenerate polar or sesquilinear form.
Orthogonal complementation allows \(k\le n/2\) when we treat a nondegenerate subspace or a nonsingular line.
In either case the restricted polar radical has dimension at most one, and this property passes to its orthogonal complement.
In \(U^\perp\) take a complement to the polar radical of \(U^\perp\).
Its restriction is nondegenerate, it is disjoint from \(U\), and it has dimension at least \(n-k-1\).
It contains a totally singular \(W\) of dimension at least \((n-k-1)/2-1\), since finite-field symplectic, unitary and quadratic spaces have anisotropic part of dimension at most two.
Here and below totally singular for an alternating form means totally isotropic.
The maps fixing \(D\), translating \(v\) by \(t\in W\) and acting linearly on \(W\) by any member of \(\operatorname{GL}(W)\) preserve the restricted form on \(U+W\), including the quadratic values in characteristic two.
Witt's extension theorem realizes them in the full linear isometry group \(I\), since the ambient polar or sesquilinear radical is zero. [Taylor, Theorem 7.4](../audits/literature/Tay92/Yin_U001.md#witt-extension)
The induced group on (2) from these extensions contains \(W\rtimes\operatorname{GL}(W)\).
For \(\dim W\ge2\), commutators of elementary linear shears on \(W\) with translations give every coordinate translation.
Consequently the commutator subgroup of the preimage of this affine group induces every translation of (2).
It belongs projectively to \(T\), since \(I'\) is contained in the special unitary group in the unitary case, lies in the symplectic group in that case, and is the group \(\Omega\) in the orthogonal case.
Thus (2) applies within the actual simple-group orbit. [Taylor, finite-field Witt indices](../audits/literature/Tay92/Yin_U001.md#finite-indices) · [Taylor, Chapters 8, 10–11](https://mail.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/don/papers/gcg.pdf)

For totally singular \(U\), let \(N\ge(n-2)/2\) be the Witt index.
Extend \(U\) to a maximal totally singular space and take \(W\) complementary to \(U\) there, so \(\dim W=N-k\).
If \(N-k\ge n/8\), the same extensions and commutators apply.
Otherwise \(k\ge n/4\) for large \(n\).
Complete a basis of \(U\) to \(k\) hyperbolic pairs.
In their \(2k\)-space take all graphs from \(U\) into its paired dual given by symmetric matrices in the symplectic case, alternating matrices in the quadratic case and skew-Hermitian matrices in the unitary case.
In the quadratic case the requirement of zero diagonal gives total singularity also in characteristic two.
In the unitary case alternating matrices over the fixed subfield already suffice.
The graph shears fixing the dual space are isometries, extended by the identity outside the hyperbolic pairs.
There are at least \(Q^{k(k-1)/4}\) different graphs in the \(I\)-orbit of \(U\).
The abelian index of the natural preimage of \(T\) in \(I\) is at most \(2(Q+1)\) in these dimensions.
For the unitary group this follows from the determinant in the norm-one subgroup, for the symplectic group the projective image is already \(T\), and for the orthogonal group \([O:\Omega]\le4\).
For the last bound, Taylor's Corollary 11.42 gives generation by reflections in these dimensions.
Reflections on lines having the same quadratic value up to a square are conjugate by Witt extension after rescaling a generator.
Thus the abelianization is generated by at most two involutions.
Thus the degree of the \(T\)-orbit is at least \(Q^{k(k-1)/4}/(2(Q+1))\), giving again bounded \(\log |H|/\log b\).
This covers either family when a full isometry orbit of maximal singular subspaces splits in the simple group. [Taylor, finite orthogonal groups, Chapter 11](https://mail.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/don/papers/gcg.pdf)

## Quadratic refinements and the odd-dimensional model in characteristic two

Let the simple group be symplectic on a \(2a\)-space over an even field with alternating form \(B\).
For either type choose one nonsingular quadratic refinement \(F\) of \(B\).
Every other refinement is uniquely \(F_t=F+B(\,\cdot\,,t)^2\), since the difference is the square of a linear functional and \(B\) is nondegenerate.
The stabilizer of \(F\) in the symplectic group is precisely its orthogonal group, giving the stated coset action.
Choose a totally \(F\)-singular space \(W\) of dimension at least \(a-1\).
For \(t\in W\), the symplectic transvection \(x\mapsto x+B(x,t)t\) carries \(F\) to \(F_t\), fixes \(W\) pointwise and hence induces translation by \(t\) throughout the patch \(\{F_s:s\in W\}\).
The rank argument and binary parallelogram proof apply to the underlying additive group of \(W\), in every even field.
The fourth refinement is the pointwise sum of the first three as quadratic functions in characteristic two, a relation preserved by pullback by \(T\).
The \(T\)-orbit of this ordered quartet therefore has size at most \(b^3\), contradicting (1).

For completeness consider a regular odd-dimensional orthogonal model in characteristic two.
Its polar radical \(R\) is a line on which the quadratic form is nonzero, and its isometry group identifies with \(\operatorname{Sp}(V/R)\).
Indeed normalize a generator \(r\) by \(F(r)=1\), choose a vector-space complement to \(R\), and lift a symplectic map on the quotient to that complement.
The discrepancy of the quadratic values there is the square of a linear functional, which gives the unique correction in \(R\) preserving \(F\).
All isometries fix \(r\).
Map a subspace label \(U\) to its image \(Y\) in \(V/R\).
For a nonzero proper \(Y\), its symplectic orbit has size greater than one, so the projection criterion after (1) excludes every fiber of size greater than one.
In the singleton-fiber case one can count and transport configurations by the resulting \(T\)-equivariant identification.
The projected subspaces are totally isotropic for singular \(U\), and have polar radical of dimension at most one when the original restriction does.
The preceding symplectic-space arguments apply to these two types, allowing an orthogonal complement in the quotient for the latter.
The only subspace with zero image apart from zero is the fixed line \(R\).
A proper subspace projecting onto all of \(V/R\) is a complement to \(R\).
Its restricted quadratic form gives a refinement on \(V/R\), and all refinements arise uniquely since changing the complement adds the square of an arbitrary functional.
This identifies its simple-group orbit with one of the two refinement actions just excluded.

All exclusions bound the compressed degree at fixed \(M\).
The primitive integral polynomial of \(\gamma\) has coefficients bounded by \(2^d e^M\), so only finitely many such \(\gamma\), hence finitely many such measures, remain.
