# Torsion quotients and power fields for factors of prime blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Heights at two powers of the same unit

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\dots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\).
Let \(v_1,\dots,v_k\) be positive integers of gcd one, put \(A_0(Y)=\prod_i(Y^{v_i}-p_i)\) and \(V=1+\sum_i v_i\), and write \(A_0^*(Y)=Y^{V-1}A_0(1/Y)\).
Let \(g,n\) be positive coprime integers and let \(\alpha\) be any nontorsion root of
\[
        X^n A_0(X^g)\ \pm\ A_0^*(X^g).
\]
Put \(W=1+g(V-1)\) and assume \(n\ge CW^9\), with \(C\) sufficiently large in terms of the primes.
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(m=m(\alpha)=d h(\alpha)\), and let \(s\in\{1,\ldots,g\}\) be the integer in the [exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement).
Then
\[
     K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^g),\qquad
     m\asymp s/g,\qquad h(\alpha)\asymp 1/n,\qquad d\asymp ns/g .       \tag{1}
\]
All comparisons and constants below may depend on the fixed primes.
No two distinct nontorsion roots of this entire parent have a quotient which is a root of unity.
In particular, with \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), every power preserves both fields below.
\[
             K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^j),\qquad F(\alpha^j)=F(\alpha)\qquad(j\ge1).                       \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the abelian subfield and the minimum possible share satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
  [K\cap F:\mathbb Q]&\ll \frac{s}{g}\,
           \frac{(\log(2d))^4}{(\log\log(5d))^3},\\
  \frac{s}{g}&\gg [K\cap F:\mathbb Q]\,
           \frac{(\log\log(5d))^3}{(\log(2d))^4}.                      \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
In particular \(g\ll s(\log(2n))^4/(\log\log(5n))^3\), since \(d\le n+W\) and \(b(t)=(\log\log(5t))^3/(\log(2t))^4\) is eventually decreasing.
The lower bound permits a share tending to zero in the remaining range.

For arbitrary positive \(g,n\) in the same delay range set \(h_0=\gcd(g,n)\).
Among all nontorsion roots of the parent, two have a torsion quotient if and only if their \(h_0\)-th powers are equal.
For each such root \(\alpha\) the two intersections are
\[
  \bigcap_{j\ge1}\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^{h_0}),
  \qquad
  \bigcap_{j\ge1}F(\alpha^j)=F(\alpha^{h_0}).                         \tag{4}
\]
These assertions concern actual roots, including roots belonging to different irreducible factors.

<a id="heights"></a>
## The field at the block power and the measure of a factor

The root is an algebraic unit since the parent is monic with constant term of modulus one.
Writing \(\gamma=\alpha^g\) gives \(\alpha^n=\mp A_0^*(\gamma)/A_0(\gamma)\), with neither block zero since their roots lie on opposite sides of the circle.
Bézout's identity for \(g,n\) gives \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
The allocation formula gives
\[
       m=(s/g)\sum_i\log p_i+O(W\exp(-cn/W^2)),\qquad W=1+g(V-1).
\]
Here \(g\le W\), and by increasing \(C\) the error is at most \((2g)^{-1}\sum_i\log p_i\), proving \(m\asymp s/g\).
The coefficient length of \(A_0(X^g)\) and its reversal is bounded solely in terms of the primes.
The height estimate at all archimedean places, using the two integral blocks and that \(\alpha\) is a unit at the finite places, gives
\[
          n h(\alpha)\le (W-1) h(\alpha)+\log\prod_i(1+p_i).
\]
Conversely the [sparse divisor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) applied to this parent of width \(n+W-1\) with at most \(2^{k+1}\) terms shows \((n+W-1)h(\alpha)\gg1\).
These estimates prove the two remaining comparisons in (1).

<a id="twists"></a>
## A root-of-unity twist of a conjugate orbit

Suppose some other root of the parent is \(\zeta\alpha\), where \(\zeta\ne1\) is a root of unity of any order.
Eliminate \(\alpha^n\) between the two equations at \(\alpha\) and \(\zeta\alpha\).
With \(r=\mp A_0^*/A_0\) and \(\eta=\zeta^g\) this gives
\[
                  r(\eta\gamma)=\zeta^n r(\gamma).                    \tag{5}
\]
As an identity of rational functions in \(Y\), the equation \(r(\eta Y)=\zeta^n r(Y)\) would require equality of the pole sets.
The poles of \(r\) are simple and the moduli \(p_i^{1/v_i}\) are distinct by unique factorization of integers.
On the \(i\)-th circle the pole set consists of one rotated group of order \(v_i\), so equality implies \(\eta^{v_i}=1\) for every \(i\).
Their gcd gives \(\eta=1\), and the nonzero rational function would then require \(\zeta^n=1\).
This contradicts \(\gcd(n,g)=1\).
Hence (5) supplies a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2(V-1)\) over \(F\) vanishing at \(\gamma\).

Its existence would give \([F(\gamma):F]\le2V\).
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) gives, uniformly in this range,
\[
       h(\gamma)\gg \{V(\log(4V))^4\}^{-1}.
\]
Indeed \(\log\log(5t)\) has a positive absolute lower bound for \(t\ge1\).
On the other hand \(h(\gamma)=g h(\alpha)\ll g/n\) by (1).
These bounds are incompatible at \(n\ge C W^9\), since \(g V(\log(4V))^4\ll W^9\) with a constant independent of \(g,V\).
It follows that such a second root cannot exist.
For every \(j\ge1\) the \(j\)-th power map is therefore injective on the complete orbit of \(\alpha\).
The degree of \(\alpha^j\) equals the number of distinct \(j\)-th powers in that orbit by separability, so \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=K\).
Taking composita with \(F\) gives (2) as well.

For general \(g,n\), write the original parent as \(P_0(X^{h_0})\) by dividing both parameters by \(h_0\).
The new exponent and new block gcd are coprime.
The ninth-power threshold holds for \(P_0\) with the same \(C\), since for \(D'=g(V-1)/h_0\ge1\) one has \((1+h_0 D')/(1+D')\ge(1+h_0)/2\ge\sqrt{h_0}\).
If the quotient of two nontorsion roots is torsion, their \(h_0\)-th powers must coincide by the primitive result for \(P_0\).
The converse follows since all \(h_0\)-th roots of a nonzero number differ by \(h_0\)-th roots of unity and belong to the parent together.
Putting \(\beta=\alpha^{h_0}\), the coprime result gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\beta^j)\subset\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) for all \(j\), with equality of the extreme fields at \(j=h_0\).
The inclusion persists after adjoining \(F\).
This proves (4).

<a id="relative"></a>
## Relative degree and the size of the abelian subfield

Write \(\delta=[F(\alpha):F]\).
Since \(F/\mathbb Q\) is abelian, \(\delta=[K:K\cap F]\).
For example, this follows by restricting \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/F)\) to a finite Galois closure of \(K\) and using the fixed field of the resulting subgroup inside \(K\).
The relative height theorem cited above gives
\[
 \delta\,h(\alpha)\gg b(\delta),\qquad
             b(t)=\frac{(\log\log(5t))^3}{(\log(2t))^4}.
\]
For \(1\le\delta\le d\) one has \(b(\delta)\gg b(d)\), by eventual decrease and positivity on bounded intervals.
Hence \([K\cap F:\mathbb Q]=d/\delta\ll m/b(d)\).
Inserting \(m\asymp s/g\) proves both forms in (3).
