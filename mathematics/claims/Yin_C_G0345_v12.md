# A uniform Pisot gap for the full two-term Chebyshev family

<a id="statement"></a>
## Both signs, arbitrary offsets and sparse changes to the middle coefficients

Fix \(s\ge1\) and \(\eta>0\).
On the positive-constant Pisot branch \(j=2\), let \(q\) be the constant, \(\theta\) the exterior root, \(h\) the degree of the [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(C\), and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose \(\Pi\) has at most \(s\) terms and agrees through degree \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) with \(A=2+\tau z^d\), where \(\tau\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(1\le d\le h\) may vary.
Then \(q\) has a uniform upper bound and \(\theta-q\) has a uniform positive lower bound depending only on \(s,\eta\).
In particular these bounds are absolute for
\[
               C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+\tau U_{h-d}(x/2),\qquad 1\le d\le h ,
\]
in both degree parities, since the lift is \(A-z^{2h+2-d}(\tau+2z^d)\).
The bounds are qualitative.

The main researcher obtained the periodic coefficient relation at distances much larger than \(d\) and its use with a varying rational comparison series.
The [low-index vanishing and local support bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#interior) came from the separate two-coordinate investigation of the other residue classes.

<a id="scales"></a>
## Two different ranges of the trace denominator

Assume \(q\to\infty\) along a sequence and pass to one sign \(\tau\).
Then \(h\to\infty\).
The [first odd coefficient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#statement) gives \(d/h\to0\): on any subsequence with \(d\ge\epsilon h\) the given prefix is even through a positive fraction of the degree strictly below \(d\), which the theorem rules out.
The [fixed-offset theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v10.md#statement) also gives \(d\to\infty\).
Put \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})=\sum b_lz^l\) and \(J_q=2/K_q\), so \(2B^\sharp=\Pi J_q\).
Its [representing signed measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_q\) has mass one and negative part of mass \(1/[2(q-1)]\).
Throughout the common prefix,
\[
              b_l=[z^l](1+\tau z^d/2)J_q .
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular the coefficients in any initial interval \([0,Ld]\) for fixed \(L\) are bounded in absolute value by four for all sufficiently large inputs.

That interval also contains only \(O_L(1)\) nonzero \(b_l\).
Here is the applicability of the [seam test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#seam) to the allowed perturbations.
For every \(t=o(d)\), the comparisons at \(rt\) and \(d+rt\) for each fixed \(r\) use only (1), symmetry, integrality and vanishing negative mass.
Thus the [first-marginal argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) again forces the law of \(v^t\) to converge to Haar probability and \(b_t\) to vanish eventually.
Consequently for this sequence there is some \(c_0>0\) such that \(b_t=0\) throughout \(1\le t\le c_0d\) eventually, by diagonal extraction if the assertion fails.
The [Bessel estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) then allows at most four nonzeros per interval of diameter \(c_0d\) below \(d\) and eight above \(d\) through \(Ld\).
Its Gram matrix and the energy of \(2+\tau v^{-d}\) use only (1) in this range.
The single indices 0 and \(d\) contribute at most two more.

<a id="far-rows"></a>
## Farther coefficient windows have period \(6d\)

For any positive integers \(t\) along this sequence with \(d/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\), we claim
\[
                              b_{t+6d}=b_t
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
eventually.
Take a weak limit \(\Omega\) of the positive parts of \(2\omega_q\) pushed forward by \(v\mapsto(w,u)=(v^d,v^t)\).
Removing the negative mass changes the integral of every unit character by \(o(1)\).
The first marginal has mass two and is twice a probability with Herglotz transform \(J_0(w)\).
This transform must satisfy
\[
             (1+\tau w/2)J_0(w)=V(-\tau w)/U_0(-\tau w)
\]
for one of the [nine rational profiles](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#profiles).
Indeed (1) at multiples of \(d\) is an integer prefix of length tending to infinity for the measure pushed forward by \(v\mapsto v^d\).
The [prefix theorem for this projected measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix) gives the displayed identity along a subsequence with one fixed row.
In particular every atom of the first marginal satisfies \(w^6=1\), since the zeros of the listed \(U_0\) have orders dividing six and the possible rotation is by \(-1\).
Away from atoms the doubled marginal density is \(\sigma=2\Re J_0\).

For \(k\ge1\), weight \(\Omega\) by \(u^k\), project to \(w\), reflect \(w\) to \(\bar w\), and multiply by \(1+\tau w/2\), obtaining a finite complex measure \(\nu_k\).
With \(\widehat\nu_k(l)=\int w^{-l}\,d\nu_k(w)\), (1) gives
\[
                    \widehat\nu_k(l)=\lim b_{kt+ld}\in\mathbb Z
                    \qquad (l\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Both indices \(kt+ld\) and \(kt+(l-1)d\) are positive and strictly inside the common prefix eventually.
The constants from the zeroth Taylor term never enter these windows.
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) on \(\mathbb Z\) expresses these Fourier coefficients as a sum of singleton and arithmetic progression indicators with integer weights.
The singleton contribution is an integer Laurent polynomial density \(H_k\), and the progression contribution is a measure supported on finitely many roots of unity.
Uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition identifies these as the continuous and atomic parts of \(\nu_k\).
This gives integrality of each continuous row without prescribing the conditional moments at the atoms.
Disintegrating the reflected continuous part of \(\Omega\) over its first coordinate gives probability moments \(c_k(w)\) satisfying
\[
              H_k=(1+\tau w/2)\sigma c_k,\qquad
              |H_k|\le D_*:=|1+\tau w/2|\sigma .
\]
On regular points these moments have the stated continuous representatives, and positivity of every finite moment matrix extends from almost everywhere to every point with \(\sigma>0\) by continuity.

For the nine profiles the [bracket and measure bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#brackets), the [finite-shape criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) and the [two exceptional envelopes](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#exceptions) give \(M(D_*)<2\) and finitely many possible nonzero Laurent shapes up to monomial translation.
For \(\tau=1\) use \(w\mapsto-w\) in the rows, preserving integrality and all moment matrices.
If any \(H_k\ne0\), the [prime specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion) first forces all sufficiently late rows to vanish, then contradicts \(M(D_*)<2\) at the greatest nonzero row.
That argument only needs a nonzero row to ensure the greatest index exists after the tail has vanished.
Thus \(H_k=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).
In particular \(\nu_1\) is supported on the atoms of the first marginal, all sixth roots.
Its moments at indices 0 and 6 are equal, proving (2) by integrality and subsequence compactness.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## A rational series with bounded Fourier measure for its positive coefficients

It follows that there is a fixed integer \(L\ge8\), with \(1/L<\eta\), such that (2) holds for every integer \(Ld\le t\le h/L\) eventually on the sequence.
Otherwise one could take successive violating indices with \(t/d\to\infty,\ t/h\to0\).
Set \(p=6d,\ U=1-z^p\), and let \(Q\) be the truncation of \(UB^\sharp\) through \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\).
By (2) its terms have degrees at most \((L+6)d\).
By the local support bound above and the coefficient bound on this interval, \(\|Q\|_\infty\) and the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients are bounded independently of the input.
The rational series
\[
                   J_*=\frac{2Q}{(2+\tau z^d)(1-z^p)}
\]
agrees with \(J_q\) through \(H\) by (1).
All its positive-index Taylor coefficients are moments of a complex circle measure of uniformly bounded variation.
To check this last assertion, expand \(2/(2+\tau z^d)=\sum_{m\ge0}(-\tau/2)^m z^{md}\).
For any integer \(a\ge0\), the nonnegative-index coefficients of \(z^a/(1-z^p)\) are the indicator of \(n\equiv a\bmod p,\ n\ge a\).
The full congruence class is the Fourier transform of normalized Haar mass on the \(p\)-th roots weighted by a character, of variation one.
Removing its nonnegative indices below \(a\) uses at most \(1+a/p\) singleton corrections, each the transform of a unit monomial density.
No prescription at negative indices is needed.
For a term \(z^e\) of \(Q\), the resulting variation bound for \(a=e+md\) is \(2+(L+6+m)/6\).
Summation against the geometric weights and the absolute coefficients of \(Q\) proves the assertion.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Bounding the support of a cyclotomic multiple of the actual denominator

Use the polynomial identity
\[
             AUB^\sharp-Q\Pi=(AU/2)\Pi(J_q-J_*).
\]
The sparse bound on \(\Pi\) gives uniformly bounded circle supremum since all its roots have modulus one, \(M(\Pi)=2\), and the term count is bounded, by the [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound).
Also \(\|A\|_\infty\le3,\ \|U\|_\infty\le2\) and \(|A|\ge1\) on the circle.
The degrees on the left are \(O(h)\), its quotient series on the right has a zero prefix through \(H\) of length comparable to \(h\), and the strictly positive coefficients of \(J_q-J_*\) have a bounded Fourier measure by construction.
The [two interval cutoffs](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) therefore give a uniform \(L^1\) bound for the left side.
Explicitly the first cutoff agrees with the difference through three times the degree bound and has bounded \(L^1\) norm because of the zero prefix.
Multiplying by the bounded polynomial factors and applying the second cutoff removes precisely the high tail.
The boundedness of \(Q\Pi\) and \(|A|\ge1\) now give \(\|UB^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\).
By the [prime-grid support estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window), this integer polynomial has boundedly many terms.
But its Mahler measure equals that of \(B^\sharp\) since \(U=1-z^{6d}\) is cyclotomic up to sign.
This measure is strictly greater than one and tends to one by the [delay-one return and fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), which excludes a bounded number of terms.
This proves bounded \(q\).
The [fixed-branch derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(\theta-q\ge2/(q+3)>0\) on the second branch and hence the stated uniform gap.
