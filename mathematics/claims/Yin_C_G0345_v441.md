# The squarefree numerator controls the measure of genuine repeated factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Counting each rational-function zero and pole once

Let \(f=u/v\in\mathbb Q(Y)^\times\) be reduced, with \(f(Y)f(1/Y)=1\), and let \(d=\max(\deg u,\deg v)\).
Let \(R\) be the product, once each, of the primitive irreducible numerator factors other than \(Y\), choosing their leading coefficients positive and using \(R=1\) for an empty product.
For \(n\ge2\), let \(P\) run over the distinct primitive irreducible factors of \(u(X^n)-Xv(X^n)\) with a nonzero nontorsion root and multiplicity \(t_P\ge2\).
Then
\[
 \sum_P(t_P-1)\deg P\le2\deg R,\qquad
 n\sum_P(t_P-1)m(P)\le2m(R)+\log(1+nd),
 \qquad m=\log M .
\]
The additive term uses the degree with multiplicities, while the first terms on the right count each surviving numerator factor only once.

<a id="radical"></a>
## The polynomial obtained after removing the repeated zeros and poles

Write \(s=\operatorname{ord}_0 f\).
For each primitive irreducible factor \(F\ne Y\) of the numerator or denominator, let \(k_F\) be its signed multiplicity in \(f\), positive for zeros.
Let \(L=\prod_F F\) using positive leading coefficients.
Inversion interchanges the zeros and poles with their orders and their primitive reciprocal polynomials.
Coprimality excludes common factors, so \(\deg L=2\deg R\) and \(m(L)=2m(R)\).
The polynomial
\[
   K_n=L\left(nY\frac{f'}{f}-1\right)
       =(ns-1)L+n\sum_F k_FYF'\frac{L}{F}
\]
has integer coefficients and degree \(\deg L\).
Indeed its leading coefficient is that of \(L\) times \(n(s+\sum_F k_F\deg F)-1\), a nonzero integer.
At a nonzero root \(\alpha\) of a counted \(P\), both \(u\) and \(v\) are nonzero at \(\beta=\alpha^n\) by coprimality.
The [powered-node identity](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) makes \(\beta\) a zero of \(K_n\) of order \(t_P-1\), since \(L\) and \(uv\) are units there.
The [field recovery and distinctness](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) give distinct primitive powered minima of degrees \(\deg P\) and logarithmic measures \(nm(P)\).
Their product with these multiplicities divides \(K_n\) over the integers by Gauss's lemma.
This proves the degree bound.

There are no zeros or poles of \(f\) on the unit circle.
The [Poisson-kernel bound for the reduced function](Yin_C_G0345_v418.md#circle) gives \(\int_{\mathbb T}|Y f'/f|\le d\) for normalized circle measure.
Taking logarithmic measures in \(K_n/L=nY f'/f-1\) and using Jensen's inequality gives \(m(K_n)\le m(L)+\log(1+nd)\).
The asserted measure bound follows by multiplicativity.
Both arguments also include constant functions and monomials.

<a id="minimum"></a>
## A finite approximation using squarefree polynomials

For prime \(p\) take the [explicit parameters](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#statement) \(a=\max(2,\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor)\), \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), \(C_p=2^{\lfloor ap/2\rfloor}\).
Let \(B_p^{\rm sf}\) be the minimum of \(C_p\) and \(M(R)\le C_p\) over the functions above with \(d\le b\) and a counted \(t_P\ge a\) at \(n=p\).
Then \(B_p^{\rm sf}\) is effectively computable, and
\[
       \frac{2\log B_p^{\rm sf}+\log(1+pb)}{(a-1)p}
             \ \ge\ \lambda,\qquad
       \lim_{p\to\infty}
       \frac{2\log B_p^{\rm sf}+\log(1+pb)}{(a-1)p}=\lambda ,
\]
where \(\lambda\) is the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.

To enumerate the functions, first enumerate primitive squarefree \(R\) of degree at most \(b\), nonzero at zero, with \(M(R)\le C_p\), including 1.
The coefficient at position \(j\) has magnitude at most \(\binom{\deg R}{j}C_p\).
Factor \(R\), give each factor a positive multiplicity at most \(b\), and use the same multiplicities on their reciprocals in the denominator.
Multiply by \(Y^s\) with \(|s|\le b\) and either sign.
Indeed the product of the factor ratios \(F/F^*\), with \(F^*(Y)=Y^{\deg F}F(1/Y)\), already satisfies the inversion identity, so the leftover rational scalar has square one.
Discard cancellations that change the stated radical, degrees above \(b\), and functions failing the exact graph test.
The [factor recovery test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) applies to the resulting reduced function, and the algebraic Mahler measures can be compared exactly.
For an actual function the lower bound follows from the first part.
For the fallback it is the [same elementary bound](Yin_C_G0345_v418.md#minimum) as in the test charging the full numerator.
The new minimum is at most that full-numerator minimum because \(m(R)\le m(u)\) for a primitive integral presentation.
The [convergence of the full-numerator test](Yin_C_G0345_v418.md#statement) therefore gives the limit.

<a id="loss"></a>
## Uniform loss at a prescribed degree cutoff

Let \(\lambda_D\) be the least positive logarithmic measure at degrees at most \(D\), and consider a row of least one-norm \(H_{p,D}\) from the [degree-restricted signed test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#statement).
For every sufficiently large prime, uniformly over integers \(10\le D\le\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\), such a minimizing row exists below the fallback.
Indeed the uniform upper estimate there is eventually strictly below the normalized fallback, since \(\lambda_D\le\log\rho<\log2\) by the degree-ten example.
Reduce its signed ratio \(f=-\varepsilon A/A_b^*\) and take \(R\) as above.
Genuine contact makes the removed common polynomial nonzero at the designated powered node, so cancellation preserves the order of the graph zero.
Its individual logarithmic measure is at least \(\lambda_D\).
The squarefree bound gives \(2m(R)\ge(a-1)p\lambda_D-\log(1+pb)\).
On the other hand the uniform degree-restricted estimate gives
\[
       2\log H_{p,D}
          \le (a-1+2+o(1))p\lambda_D-\log(2pb+1).
\]
Since \(R\) divides the original numerator up to integral normalization, \(m(R)\le\log H_{p,D}\).
Subtracting proves the uniform loss bound
\[
        0\le\log H_{p,D}-m(R)\le(1+o(1))p\lambda_D .
\]
This bounds the combined cost of extra copies of numerator factors, factors canceled by reversal, and the difference between Mahler measure and the logarithmic row norm.
No degree-independent positive lower bound for \(m(R)/((a-1)p)\) has been established.
