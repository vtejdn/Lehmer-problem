## 1. A universal large-correction control, not a new gap mechanism

Let P be any nonconstant real polynomial of degree d with P(0)=1, p its coefficient vector on the ORIGINAL positions0,...,d, and e_j the j-th coordinate vector.
For any t>0 set

    Q_t=pp^T+t(e0e0^T-e1e1^T), R_t=t e1e1^T.

Every coordinate monomial has circle modulus1.
Thus Q_t has EXACT symbol |P|^2, R_t is rank-one PSD, and Q_t+R_t=pp^T+t e0e0^T is PSD.
Its constant entry is1+t and its corrected symbol is |P|^2+t.
The actual corrected income is

    F_t=(1/2)log(1+t)-E_P(sqrt(t))
       =m(P)-(1/2)integral log[(|P|^2+t)/(1+t)].       (1)

For fixed P the ratio inside the last integral tends uniformly to1 as t tends to infinity, so F_t tends to m(P).
The finite PSD/Jensen argument implies F_t<=m(P).
Consequently positive F_t exists if and only if m(P)>0; whenever m(P)>0 it remains positive for all sufficiently large t, while Q_t00=1+t is unbounded.
If P is integral and t is a positive integer, ALL matrices here are integral.
No original degree is changed, no coefficient optimizer is approximated and no field denominator is hidden.

This is a countercontrol to extending C125's raw-objective collapse to ALL corrected Grams.
It does not produce a new arithmetic lower bound: the exact E in(1) contains the original unknown m.
Writing H2=sum p_j^2, concavity of log gives

    F_t>=m(P)-(1/2)log[(H2+t)/(1+t)].                 (2)

A supplied old lower bound m>=mu can certify a positive F_t once the last loss is below mu, but the resulting numerical lower bound does not improve that supplied mu.
Correction size is not small: ||R_t||=Tr R_t=t.
This elementary control is retained as a method boundary, not a separate main Claim.

## 2. A genuine multiple-factor trace remainder and exact repair

Let P be monic real reciprocal of ORIGINAL degree d=2h>=2 with constant1, P(z)=z^h T(z+z^-1), T monic of degree h.
Choose1<=k<=h and REAL b_1,...,b_k>1, repetitions permitted.
Put s_i=b_i+b_i^-1 and divide in R[x]:

    D_x(x)=product_i(x-s_i), T=D_x S+R, deg R<k.

If R is zero use the zero polynomial convention.
Define

    D_z(z)=product_i(1-s_i z+z^2)=z^k D_x(z+z^-1),
    C(z)=z^(h-k) S(z+z^-1),
    e(z)=z^h R(z+z^-1),
    g(z)=product_i(b_i-2z+b_i^-1 z^2),
    f(z)=C(z)g(z), B=product_i b_i>1.                  (3)

These are ordinary polynomials.
C is reciprocal of degree d-2k and constant1; e is supported in positions h-k+1,...,h+k-1, hence has BOTH endpoint coefficients zero.
The identity P-e=C D_z is exact.
Each quadratic factor of g has the SAME circle modulus as the corresponding factor of D_z, by the C103/C123 quadratic identity.
Therefore |f|=|P-e| on the circle and f0=B.
Repeated nodes require no divided-difference denominator; ordinary monic division handles their multiplicities exactly.

Use the same letters f,e for their original-width coefficient vectors.
For ANY real u>0 set

    Q=ff^T+pe^T+ep^T-ee^T,
    R_u=u^-1 pp^T+(1+u)ee^T.                         (4)

Then Q has exact symbol |P|^2 and Q00=B^2.
R_u is PSD, and

    Q+R_u=ff^T+(u^(-1/2) p+u^(1/2) e)
                          (u^(-1/2) p+u^(1/2) e)^T>=0,
    v^*(Q+R_u)v=(1+u^-1)(|P|^2+u|e|^2),
    (Q+R_u)00=B^2+u^-1.                             (5)

All cross terms in(5) are checked by expanding |P-e|^2 and the second square; none is discarded or replaced by averaging.
Rank Q<=3, rank R_u<=2 and rank(Q+R_u)<=2.
On e-perpendicular, Q's quadratic form is (f^T x)^2, so it has at most one negative eigenvalue.
These facts alone give no small-correction conclusion.

Let

    E_(P,e)(u)=(1/2)integral log(1+u|e|^2/|P|^2).

The exact finite PSD/Jensen certificate is

    m(P)>=F_(P;e,B)(u)
       :=(1/2)log[(B^2u+1)/(u+1)]-E_(P,e)(u).        (6)

Circle zeros have measure0 and integrable logarithms.
If e is identically zero, E=0 and the uncorrected ff^T already gives m>=log B.
This exceptional case will be impossible in the far-node Salem application below, since e(b_i)=P(b_i) there is nonzero.

If e is nonzero, let V=M(e)>0.
Apply C125's supporting-line calculation to y=log|P|-log|e|, which is integrable, and whose mean is log(M(P)/V).
This gives

    E_(P,e)(u)>=(1/2)log(1+u V^2/M(P)^2).             (7)

The denominator minus numerator in the resulting ratio for exp(2F) is

    u[(1+u)V^2-M(P)^2(B^2-1)].                       (8)

Therefore positive ACTUAL F requires (1+u)V^2<M(P)^2(B^2-1).
In particular V^2>=M(P)^2(B^2-1) excludes EVERY scale u>0, with strict F<0 and supremum0.
For this last limit u decreases to0; domination by log(1+|e/P|^2), bounded above by a constant plus2|log|P||, justifies E->0.

This is a product construction with variable polynomial residual.
Its exact corrected symbol is not the positive-mixture A|P|^2+B_constant of C125.

## 7. Original coefficient, denominator and spectral expenses

For the general construction(3)--(6), suppose P is integral, b_i=a_i/c_i>1 in lowest positive terms, and u=r/s>0 in lowest terms.
Put

    L0=product_i a_i c_i, D0=L0^(h-k+1).

All coefficients of D_x and g have denominators dividing L0.
In monic long division of T by D_x, the quotient coefficient at depth j has denominator dividing L0^j, so S has common denominator L0^(h-k).
Substitution x=z+z^-1 preserves integrality of its basis polynomials.
It follows that both f and e have coefficient denominators dividing D0, and

    q=r s D0^2                                             (22)

clears EVERY entry of Q and R_u.
This is a supplied clearing denominator, not asserted least.
The case k=h is included: S=1 and D0=L0.
Repeated parameters do not require Vandermonde inversion or denominators of node differences.

For explicit coefficient sizes let T1 be the sum of absolute coefficients of the actual integer trace polynomial T, H1=sum|p_j|, and define

    V=product_i(1+|s_i|), W=product_i(b_i+2+b_i^-1),
    S1=T1(1+V)^(h-k+1), E1=2^(k-1)(T1+V S1),
    F1=2^(h-k)S1 W.

These are computable finite bounds.
The quotient coefficient at depth j is at most T1(1+V)^j in absolute value: the division recurrence has incoming coefficient at most T1 and total nonleading divisor coefficient norm at most V.
Induction gives this bound, and summing its finite geometric series gives ||S||_1<=S1.
Then ||R||_1<=T1+V S1.
Each (z+z^-1)^j has coefficient norm2^j, which proves ||e||_1<=E1 and ||f||_1<=F1.
Therefore

    ||Q||<=F1^2+2H1 E1+E1^2,
    Tr R_u=u^-1||p||_2^2+(1+u)||e||_2^2
           <=u^-1 H1^2+(1+u)E1^2.                  (23)

The norm is the ordinary Euclidean operator norm.
In particular any negative eigenvalue of Q has magnitude at most the displayed trace bound, by Q+R_u>=0, but no smallness or sharpness is claimed.
All matrix dimensions are exactly d+1.
Computing T, monic division, substitution and finite convolutions is rational finite algebra with these denominators and sizes; there is no unpriced field embedding or unknown optimizer.
No asymptotically efficient bit algorithm is needed for the obstruction theorem.

For the explicit full-factor Salem control(20), the bounds simplify without a trace computation.
Write B=b^h, so

    f=B^-1(b-z)^d,
    D_z=B^-1[b-(b^2+1)z+bz^2]^h,
    e=P-D_z, u=1/B.

    ||f||_1,||D_z||_1<=(b+2+b^-1)^h

    ||Q||<=19B^2, Tr R_u<=19B^2,
    |(qQ)_ij|, |(qR_u)_ij|<=19B^5.                 (24)

The correction is large and its full original coefficient energy is retained.
