# Computing the prime-indexed coefficient minimum at fixed row degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact minimum and attaining rows

Write \(M_{p,b}\) for the [coefficient minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) at an odd prime \(p\) with row degree at most \(b\).
For every fixed \(b\) one can compute \(M_{p,b}\), determine whether a genuine qualifying pair attains it, and return such a pair when one exists, in deterministic time polynomial in the numerical value \(p\) in the bit model.
In particular the exponential coefficient cutoff \(2^p\) need only enter by its bit length.
The input prime itself can have only \(O(\log p)\) bits, whereas the returned minimum can have \(p+1\) bits.

The same conclusion, with time polynomial in \(s=\sum_{p\in S}p\), holds for the [common minimum](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at a nonempty set \(S\) of distinct odd primes when \(b,C>0\) are fixed and
\[
                      s/\min S\le C\log(2+s).
\]
Here \(b\) is integral, and comparison with this promise is unnecessary for running the algorithm.
The running-time exponent may depend on \(b,C\).
In both statements the computation gives exact threshold decisions without enumerating the integer points of the coefficient box individually.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## A finite list of seed polynomials

For either minimum put \(s=p\) in the singleton case and keep its meaning as the sum for a prime set.
Suppose a genuine pair has cost \(H\le2^s\).
For every qualifying seed \(P\) at \(p\) the [measure and degree bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) give
\[
                e=\deg P\le b,\qquad m(P)\le (s/p)\log 2 .
\]
They also bound the number of primes by \(b\), since at least one positive-degree seed occurs at each prime.
If there are more primes, return \(2^s\) with no attaining pair.
The elementary symmetric functions give \(|[X^i]P|\le\binom ei\,2^{s/p}\).
For the singleton use the integer bound \(2\binom ei\).
For a prime set use \(\binom ei 2^{\lceil s/p\rceil}\), which is polynomial in \(s\) under the promise.
Enumerate the monic reciprocal unit polynomials in these boxes, retain the irreducible noncyclotomic ones, and choose one at each prime.
In fixed degree irreducibility can even be tested by trial monic factors, since each integral monic divisor has coefficients bounded by \(2^b\|P\|_1\) by monic division and the elementary symmetric bound.
Cyclotomic factors of degree at most \(b\) can be precomputed by inspecting orders \(n\le2b^2\).
Indeed \(\varphi(n)^2/n=\prod_{\ell^a\parallel n}\ell^{a-2}(\ell-1)^2\ge1/2\).
The number of tuples of seeds is constant at fixed degree for a singleton, and polynomial in \(s\) for the sets in the statement.
Extraneous irreducible seeds in the boxes do not affect the answer since actual double divisibility will be imposed.

<a id="decision"></a>
## A nonnegative weight detects qualifying pairs

Fix such a tuple \((P_p)\), an integer threshold \(1\le T\le2^s\), and actual degrees \(i,j\in\{0,\ldots,b\}\) for \(U,V\).
Write the coefficients through these degrees as variables.
In the quotient by \(P_p^2\) calculate the remainders of \(X^{pk}\) and \(X^{1+pk}\) through \(k=b\).
Equating every coefficient of the remainder of \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) to zero gives linear homogeneous integer equations in the variables.
At fixed \(b\), repeated multiplication by \(X\) in the monic quotient computes these columns with \(O_b(p\log(2+\|P_p\|_1))\) bits per entry.
Indeed the one-norm of each reduced monomial grows by a factor at most \(1+\|P_p^2\|_1\) in one multiplication.
All the data have polynomial bit size and are produced in polynomial time in \(s\).

Impose \(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1\le T\) using the inequalities for all choices of signs in each row.
Together with the remainder equations these inequalities specify a bounded rational polytope \(K\) in at most \(2b+2\) variables.
On its lattice points use the integer polynomial weight
\[
 f=(u_i v_j\operatorname{Res}_{i,j}(U,V))^2
                  \sum_{t=-b}^{b}\left([Y^t]\bigl(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\bigr)\right)^2 .
\]
Here \(u_i,v_j\) are the leading variables, \(\iota(Y)=1/Y\), and the Sylvester determinant uses the indicated formal degrees.
For two constant polynomials use the empty determinant one.
The leading variables exclude degree drops, and at the actual degrees a nonzero resultant is exactly coprimality over the rationals.
Consequently \(f\ge0\) throughout \(K\) and \(f>0\) at an integer point precisely when that point gives a qualifying pair with the specified seeds and degrees.
The number of variables, the degree of the weight and its coefficient sizes are bounded in terms of \(b\).

By the [fixed-dimensional weighted sum](../audits/literature/DHKW06/Yin_U001.md#sum), the exact integer \(\sum_{a\in K\cap\mathbb Z^{i+j+2}} f(a)\) is computable in polynomial time at fixed \(b\).
It is positive exactly when the specified problem is feasible, including when the polytope lies in a proper affine subspace.
Taking the disjunction over the listed degrees and seed tuples therefore decides the full threshold question.
First test the upper cutoff to distinguish a fallback without a witness.
Otherwise binary search in \(T\) needs at most \(s+1\) such decisions to find the exact least cost.
Retain a feasible tuple and degree choice at this threshold, and bisect the coefficient intervals successively using the same sum on each restricted polytope.
After \(O_b(s)\) further decisions all coordinates are fixed and the surviving lattice point supplies the attaining rows.

<a id="width"></a>
## An effective choice of increasing degree

There is a computable choice of diverging row degrees with a polynomial-time computation of the exact single-prime minimum in the numerical parameter \(p\).
Use the [integer lattice algorithm](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v245.md#bits), which returns the single-prime minimum and any attaining witness in at most \(f(b)(p+1)^4\) bit operations for a computable \(f\).
On a fixed deterministic bit machine run that algorithm successively at \(b=1,2,\ldots\), keeping the last completed degree and its output, until a total of \((p+1)^5\) steps has been used.
For every fixed \(B\), the prefix through degree \(B\) costs at most \((\sum_{b=1}^{B}f(b))(p+1)^4\) up to the fixed loop overhead, so it finishes before the budget for every sufficiently large \(p\).
For the finitely many inputs with no completed degree, use \(b=1\), whose minimum is \(2^p\) since a nontorsion reciprocal unit has degree at least two.
These effective degrees tend to infinity.
Their exact minima therefore give the same [limit of the normalized logarithms](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement).
The counter and bookkeeping can be included with \(O(\log(p+2))\) overhead per simulated step, still polynomial in \(p\).
This bound controls computation at a specified prime and supplies no rate of convergence to the infimum.
