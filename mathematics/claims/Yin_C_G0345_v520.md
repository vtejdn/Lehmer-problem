# All representations of a prime sum in the same Laurent row ideal

<a id="statement"></a>
## Multiplicity at each residue and simultaneous invariant factors

Use the [full-interval prime-field theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#statement) with \(g=1+\lfloor\log_2(1+\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor)\rfloor\), all primes \(S\) in \([L,2L]\), \(m=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\), and its width \(b=|S|j(L)\).
Suppose \(U,V\) pass the [common row test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width and one reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) occurs to multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\) in every graph \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\).
Divide both rows by their joint coefficient content to obtain \(u,v\), and put \(A=\mathbb Z[Y,Y^{-1}]/(u,v)\), whose additive group is [finite](Yin_C_G0345_v503.md#statement).
For sufficiently large \(L\), take any set of at least \(\lceil L/m^2\rceil\) certified primes \(\ell\) from the prime-field theorem and let \(Q\) be their squarefree product.
Then \(A\) admits an additive quotient
\[
          \prod_{i=1}^k(\mathbb Z/Q^i\mathbb Z)^{R_*},
          \qquad R_*=\left\lceil L/(16m^2)\right\rceil .                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular the \(kR_*\)-th largest additive invariant factor of \(A\) is divisible by \(Q\), and the \(R_*\)-th largest is divisible by \(Q^k\).
Here \(\log Q\gg L/\log L\).

At one such \(\ell\), write \(O=\mathbb Z_\ell,\ n=(\ell-1)/j\) and choose any reduced root \(z\) of \(P\) of order \(t\).
Let \(\alpha\in O\) be the root of \(P\) above \(z\), let \(\xi_z\in O\) be the \(n\)-th root of unity above \(z\), and put \(w=v_\ell(\alpha^n-1)\).
Let \(R_n\) count the ordered representations \(p+q=n\) by distinct primes of \(S\).
For each distinct \(y=z^p\) arising in these representations, let \(m_y\) count the representations with this value, put \(d_y=km_y\), and write \(\xi_y=\xi_z^p\).
This last lift depends only on \(y\).
At each \(y\) both rows lie in
\[
              (\ell^w,Y-\xi_y)^{d_y}\subset O[Y].                        \tag{2}
\]
In particular both reduced rows have a root of multiplicity at least \(d_y\) at \(y\).
For every \(0\le a<d_y\), the coefficient of \(T^a\) in either row at \(\xi_y+T\), its \(a\)-th Hasse derivative there, is divisible by \(\ell^{w(d_y-a)}\).
The \(\ell\)-primary additive group of \(A\) has a quotient isomorphic to
\[
           \prod_y\prod_{a=1}^{d_y}\mathbb Z/\ell^{wa}\mathbb Z.          \tag{3}
\]
Its base-\(\ell\) logarithmic order is \(\frac w2\sum_y(d_y^2+d_y)\), at least \(\frac w2(k^2R_n^2/\varphi(t)+kR_n)\), and some \(m_y\ge R_n/\varphi(t)\).
Equation (3) counts every representation, also when several first primes give the same root.

<a id="centers"></a>
## Separating the nodes above one residue

The prime-field theorem gives \(R_n\ge R_*\), \(t\mid n\), \(t>\sqrt\ell/g>2\), and order \(t\) for each \(y\).
Hensel lifting at a simple reduced root gives \(\alpha\), and reciprocity gives the root \(\alpha^{-1}\) above \(z^{-1}\).
Because \(n\mid\ell-1\), the \(n\)-th roots of unity reduce bijectively to the corresponding roots modulo \(\ell\).
The element \(\xi_z\) has order \(t\), and the unique lift of \(y\) is \(\xi_y=\xi_z^p=\xi_z^{-q}\).
Write \(\alpha=\xi_z(1+c)\).
The expansion of \((1+c)^n-1\) gives \(w=v_\ell(c)\ge1\), finite since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
For any nonzero integer \(a\) with \(t\mid a\) and \(\ell\nmid a\), the same argument gives \(v_\ell(\alpha^a-1)=w\), using inversion for negative \(a\).

The nodes \(x_p=\alpha^p\) and \(x'_q=\alpha^{-q}\) of a representation lie in \(\xi_y+\ell^w O\).
For a fixed \(y\), two different first primes have difference divisible by \(t\), with \(0<|p-p'|\le L<\ell\).
It follows that \(v_\ell(x_p-x_{p'})=w\).
The corresponding second primes \(q=n-p\) also differ by a nonzero multiple of \(t\) of absolute value at most \(L\), giving the same equality for their nodes.
Thus within each of the two lists, the \(m_y\) scaled centers \((x-\xi_y)/\ell^w\) have pairwise distinct reductions.

<a id="jets"></a>
## Reduction of the two rows on the smaller disk

Fix \(y\), and let \(W\) be the minimum valuation of all coefficients of both polynomials \(u(\xi_y+\ell^w T),v(\xi_y+\ell^w T)\).
After dividing by \(\ell^W\), denote the two polynomials by \(u_*(T),v_*(T)\).
At least one reduction is nonzero, and both reductions have degree at most \(\lfloor W/w\rfloor\) because the coefficients at degree \(a\) before division belong to \(\ell^{wa}O\).

For a first prime \(p\) and its scaled center \(a_p=(x_p-\xi_y)/\ell^w\), work modulo \((T-a_p)^k\).
In this quotient take
\[
 f_p(T)=\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{1/p}{i}
                 \bigl(\ell^w(T-a_p)/x_p\bigr)^i .
\]
Since \(p<\ell\), \(1/p\in O\), and the binomial coefficients at this argument are in \(O\) by approximating it by nonnegative integers in \(O\).
The truncated binomial identity gives \(f_p^p=\xi_y+\ell^wT\), and \(P(f_p)^k=0\) since \(f_p(a_p)=\alpha\).
The normalized graph is divisible by \(P^k\), as division by the joint content preserves divisibility by a monic polynomial.
Substituting \(f_p\) therefore gives \(\ell^W(u_*+f_pv_*)=0\) modulo \((T-a_p)^k\).
The monic quotient is free over \(O\), so this equality survives cancellation of \(\ell^W\).
All terms of \(f_p\) after its constant summand lie in \(\ell O[T]\).
Thus \(\bar u_*+z\bar v_*\) has a root of multiplicity at least \(k\) at \(\bar a_p\).
Using \(\alpha^{-1}\), \(q\), and \(x'_q\) in the same calculation gives \(m_y\) roots of multiplicity at least \(k\) for \(\bar u_*+z^{-1}\bar v_*\).
Each list consists of \(m_y\) distinct residues by the [scaled-center calculation](#centers).
If \(W<wkm_y\), the degree bound forces both combinations to vanish identically over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
Their distinct coefficients \(z,z^{-1}\) imply both rows vanish identically there, contradicting the normalization.
Consequently \(W\ge wd_y\).
Comparing coefficients before division proves all the Hasse derivative inequalities, which are exactly (2) on expanding in \(Y-\xi_y\).

## The local and global additive groups

In the quotient by \((\ell^w,Y-\xi_y)^{d_y}\), the successive powers \((Y-\xi_y)^a\) for \(0\le a<d_y\) are independent cyclic generators of orders \(\ell^{w(d_y-a)}\) over the integers.
Indeed the defining power is generated by the monomials \(\ell^{w(d_y-a)}(Y-\xi_y)^a\) for \(0\le a\le d_y\).
The class of \(Y\) is a unit because \(\xi_y\) is a unit and \(Y-\xi_y\) is nilpotent.
The defining ideals for distinct \(y\) are comaximal since their centers differ by units and powers of comaximal ideals remain comaximal.
The Chinese remainder theorem and (2) give a simultaneous map onto all the local quotients that kills both rows.
It is surjective from integer polynomials since all targets at this prime are killed by \(\ell^{w\max_y d_y}\), so any finite list of \(\ell\)-adic coefficients in a polynomial representative may be replaced by integers.
This proves (3).
The number of occupied residues is at most \(\varphi(t)\), and \(\sum_y m_y=R_n\).
Summing the cyclic exponents and using Cauchy–Schwarz gives the claimed order bound.
Averaging \(m_y\) over the occupied residues gives the largest multiplicity estimate.

At \(y\), group the \(km_y\) factors in (3) into consecutive blocks of size \(k\) in order of increasing exponent.
Each block has a quotient \(\prod_{i=1}^k\mathbb Z/\ell^i\mathbb Z\), since \(w\ge1\).
The blocks at this prime number \(R_n\ge R_*\).
Retain \(R_*\) blocks for every \(\ell\mid Q\) and combine their maps by the Chinese remainder theorem on the coprime rational prime powers.
This proves (1), and its invariant-factor consequences follow by counting cyclic factors at each prime and exponent.
The lower bound on \(\log Q\) follows from the number of prime factors and \(\ell>L\).
The [two-node Smith calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v519.md#smith) separately gives larger exponents at selected single representations.
The present rank estimate uses every representation simultaneously on the original row pair.
