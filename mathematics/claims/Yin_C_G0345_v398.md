# Recovering the rational quartic trace factors from one scalar at the prime three

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed-prime test for every sufficiently large exponent

For \(b\ge1\) coprime to three let
\[
 A_b(X)=1+X^3-X^{12}+X^bD(X),\qquad D(X)=X^{12}+X^9-1 .
\]
Remove the [known cyclotomic factors](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#simple) and write the monic remainder as \(P_b(X)=X^n H_b(X+X^{-1})\).
Define \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\) and
\[
 W=C_{12}+C_9-C_3-3,\qquad U(f)=(-1)^{\deg f}\operatorname{Res}(W,f)
\]
for a monic polynomial \(f\).
For every sufficiently large \(b\), the monic rational divisors of \(H_b\) can be identified among its divisors over \(\mathbb Z_3\) by the single test
\[
                    f\mid H_b,\qquad U(f)\in\{1,-1\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
More precisely, the reduction of \(H_b\) modulo three is squarefree.
Lift its distinct monic irreducible factors by Hensel's lemma.
The products of subsets of these lifts satisfying (1) are exactly the monic rational divisors of \(H_b\).
The minimal nonempty subsets passing (1) give the rational irreducible factors.
Only their scalar resultants have to be compared with \(1,-1\).
For odd \(b\) the single value \(+1\) suffices.

One may perform the test entirely in \(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z\).
For example set \(C=8\cdot6^{12}\) and take any \(k\) with
\[
               3^k > (1+C^n)^{\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}} .          \tag{2}
\]
Then the subsets in (1) are exactly those with \(U(f)\equiv 1\) or \(-1\bmod 3^k\).
The test is sufficient even at smaller precision if it already excludes every proper nonempty subset.
At \(b\ge26\) in the range of the theorem a proper nonempty subset passing the test at precision (2) exists if and only if one factor of \(P_b\) becomes the minimal polynomial of a Salem number less than \(9/8\) under \(X\mapsto-X\).
We call this the signed Salem factor below.
For even \(b\) in the split case, its trace subset has value \(-1\) and the other irreducible trace factor has value \(+1\).
This characterizes the actual factorization using one fixed prime for the whole family.
The sufficient bound (2) is large, and the occurrence of a split as \(b\) varies remains open.

<a id="independence"></a>
## Independent actions on real and nonreal traces

The [quartic exterior count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic) gives one negative exterior root and one nonreal exterior conjugate pair for \(b>18\).
For all sufficiently large \(b\), the [individual trace group theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#primes) gives one factor with exactly one nonreal trace pair and with group \(S_{n_c}\) on its traces.
In the split case the other factor is a sign-changed Salem polynomial, so its traces are all real and their group is \(A_{n_s}\) or \(S_{n_s}\) by [the full alternating action](Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#trace).
All these trace degrees tend to infinity uniformly by [primitive factor degree growth](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement), so take each greater than twelve.

Let \(L_c\) be the splitting field of the trace factor with a nonreal trace pair.
Ordinary complex conjugation acts there as a transposition.
The normal subgroup fixing \(L_c\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\), where \(\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) denotes the field of all totally real algebraic numbers, contains that transposition.
All transpositions generate \(S_{n_c}\) and are conjugate, so \(L_c\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}=\mathbb Q\).
The splitting field \(L_s\) of the other trace factor, when present, is totally real.
Thus \(L_c\cap L_s=\mathbb Q\), and the two finite Galois fields are linearly disjoint.
In particular their alternating groups act on the respective sets of traces independently, fixing all other traces.

<a id="subsets"></a>
## Integral scalar values and rational divisors

For each trace root \(t_i\) put \(\lambda_i=-W(t_i)\).
All are algebraic integers, and for any subset \(J\) of the roots the product \(\lambda_J=\prod_{i\in J}\lambda_i\) equals \(U(\prod_{i\in J}(Y-t_i))\), since \(W\) is monic of even degree twelve.
For a monic rational divisor of \(H_b\) the [signed pole identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#trace) identifies this scalar with the resultant of its monic reciprocal lift and \(D\), in the order \(D\) first.
The absolute resultant of \(D\) with the whole parent is one, so all these rational divisors have scalar \(\pm1\).
The \(\lambda_i\) are units as their full product is a unit.
For odd \(b\) the [sign at the real poles](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#sign) gives \(+1\) for all rational trace divisors.
For even \(b\) in the split case the signed Salem factor has pole resultant \(-1\) there.
The reciprocal lift of the other divisor has no real roots, hence takes positive values at both real zeros of \(D\), giving sign \(+1\).

Within any one rational trace factor the \(\lambda_i\) are distinct.
Equality of two would imply equality for every pair by double transitivity.
Then more than twelve distinct roots would have the same value under the nonconstant polynomial \(W\) of degree twelve.

Suppose now that \(\lambda_J=\pm1\) and that \(J\) meets one rational factor in a nonempty proper subset.
If this subset has at least two letters outside it in that factor, a three-cycle supported there sends one chosen letter inside to a chosen letter outside, leaving all other memberships unchanged.
If the complement is a singleton, a three-cycle with two letters inside instead changes just one membership in each direction.
The independent actions proved above permit this operation while fixing every letter of the other rational factor.
The scalar must be unchanged because it is rational, forcing equality of the values at two distinct letters after cancellation of nonzero factors.
This contradiction proves that \(J\) is a union of complete rational factors.
Together these two implications prove (1) even among all subsets in an algebraic closure, before taking local factorizations.

<a id="precision"></a>
## Squarefree lifting and a bound for identifying the signs

Modulo three the derivative of \(A_b\) is \(bX^{b-1}D(X)\), since the other exponents in \(D\) and the first block are multiples of three.
The parent has unit constant coefficient and its resultant with \(D\) is a unit, so this reduction is squarefree.
The monic factor \(P_b\) therefore has squarefree reduction.
If \(\bar H_b\) had a multiple root, the corresponding quadratic lifts in the reduction of \(P_b=X^nH_b(X+X^{-1})\) would also be multiple, which is impossible.
Hensel factorization thus supplies one monic lift of each distinct irreducible factor of the reduction, and every monic rational divisor corresponds locally to one of their subset products.

Fix an embedding into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_3\).
Each local subset represents an actual subset of the global roots, and its scalar \(\lambda_J\) lies in \(\mathbb Z_3\).
For every complex conjugate \(z\) of a root of \(P_b\), one has \(1/6\le |z|\le6\) from the coefficient length of the parent, its unit endpoints and reciprocity.
Thus \(|C_j(z+z^{-1})|\le 2\cdot6^j\) and every conjugate of every \(\lambda_i\) is bounded by \(C\).
For \(j=|J|\) the number of conjugates of \(\lambda_J\) is at most \(\binom nj\), since Galois permutes the trace roots.
Given \(\epsilon=\pm1\), if \(\lambda_J\ne\epsilon\) the algebraic integer \(\lambda_J-\epsilon\) has nonzero rational norm of absolute value at most
\[
                          (1+C^j)^{\binom nj}.
\]
If \(\lambda_J\equiv\epsilon\bmod3^k\) at the chosen embedding, this norm is divisible by \(3^k\), since all its other local conjugates are integral.
Inequality (2) rules out that possibility.
Polynomial resultants commute with reduction, so the lifts to precision \(3^k\) alone give the stated finite test.
