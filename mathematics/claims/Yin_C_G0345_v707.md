# Resultant bounds for small-measure polynomials in a coefficient class

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number of coprime polynomials and the modulus for uniqueness

Write \(\mathcal M(F)=\prod_{F(z)=0}\max(1,|z|)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of a monic polynomial, counting roots with multiplicity.
Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_k\) be monic squarefree polynomials in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), of the same degree \(d\ge1\), with identical coefficient residues modulo an integer \(M\ge2\).
Put \(b_i=\mathcal M(F_i)\) and \(\Delta_i=|\operatorname{disc}(F_i)|\), with discriminant one at degree one.
Then
\[
  M^{dk(k-1)}\prod_{i=1}^k\Delta_i
       \le (kd)^{kd}\left(\prod_{i=1}^k b_i\right)^{2(kd-1)}.          \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(b_i\le B\) for all \(i\) and \(\delta=\log(M/B^2)>0\), then
\[
                 k\delta\le\log(kdM),\qquad
                 k\le\frac{2}{\delta}
                     \left[\log(dM)+\max\{0,\log(2/(\mathrm e\delta))\}\right].
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
The bound applies to all distinct monic irreducibles in such a coefficient class, with arbitrary numbers of exterior roots.
For fixed \(M>B^2\) their count is \(O_{M,B}(\log(2d))\).
In particular, for \(M=2\) and degree-dependent bounds \(B_d\to1\), the count in any class is at most \((1+o(1))\log d/\log2\) as \(d\to\infty\), uniformly over classes.

For the entire class of monic squarefree polynomials of degree \(d\) and measure at most \(B\ge1\), including polynomials that share factors, there is at most one member whenever
\[
                          M>2d\,B^{4-2/d}.                              \tag{3}
\]
This also gives uniqueness under the simpler condition \(M>2dB^4\).
The estimates complement the [count for bounded numbers of exterior roots](Yin_C_G0345_v704.md#statement), which also covers modulus one.
For the [integer Salem seeds](#seeds), the same count gives logarithmically many prime radicals per residue class below any fixed fraction of the square root of the prime, and \(1/\sqrt8\) is a lower bound for the limit inferior of the ratio as the prime grows.

<a id="resultants"></a>
## The product discriminant

Write \(F_j=F_i+M H_{ij}\) with \(H_{ij}\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Evaluating at all roots of \(F_i\) gives \(\operatorname{Res}(F_i,F_j)=M^d\operatorname{Res}(F_i,H_{ij})\).
The second resultant is a nonzero integer by coprimality and monicity.
The product \(F=\prod_iF_i\) is squarefree, with
\[
      |\operatorname{disc}F|=\prod_i\Delta_i
                           \prod_{i<j}|\operatorname{Res}(F_i,F_j)|^2 .
\]
This yields the left side of (1).
The [polynomial discriminant inequality](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U003.md#smy08-discriminant-separation) applies at degree \(kd\).
Indeed each row of the Vandermonde matrix at a root \(z\) has norm at most \(\sqrt{kd}\max(1,|z|)^{kd-1}\).
Squaring Hadamard's determinant bound gives the right side of (1).
The same two-polynomial mechanism for a root and its degree-preserving prime power appears in the [finite-prime determinant](Yin_C_G0219_v1.md#finite-prime-discriminant-gap).

Dropping the factors \(\Delta_i\ge1\), taking logarithms and dividing by \(kd\) gives
\((k-1)\log M\le\log(kd)+(2-2/(kd))\sum_i\log b_i\).
Using \(\log b_i\le\log B\) gives the first inequality in (2).
The maximum of \(\log t-\delta t/2\) over \(t>0\) is \(\log(2/(\mathrm e\delta))\), giving the explicit second inequality.
For \(M=2\) and \(B_d\to1\), \(\delta\to\log2\), so first \(k=O(\log(2d))\), and then \(\log k=O(\log\log(3d))\) in the first inequality.
The stated asymptotic follows also when the classes vary with \(d\).

<a id="common"></a>
## Cancelling shared factors

Suppose \(F_1,F_2\) are distinct monic squarefree polynomials of degree \(d\) in the class.
Take their monic gcd \(G\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) and put \(U=F_1/G,\ V=F_2/G\).
These quotients are coprime and squarefree of a common degree \(e=d-\deg G\ge1\).
Since \(G\) is monic, multiplication by its reduction is injective on \((\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)[X]\), by the leading coefficient of a nonzero multiplier.
Hence \(U\equiv V\bmod M\), also for composite \(M\).
Applying (1) with \(k=2\) and these two quotients gives
\[
              M\le2e\big(\mathcal M(U)\mathcal M(V)\big)^{\,2-1/e}
                   \le2d B^{\,4-2/d}.
\]
Indeed \(\mathcal M(G)\ge1\), so each quotient has measure at most \(B\).
This proves (3).

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Prime radicals near the square root of the modulus

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers, \(\epsilon=\pm1\), \(r>1\) the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), and \(p>n\) a prime with \(\beta=r^{1/p}\in\mathbb Q(r)\), taking the positive root.
For each fixed \(0<c<1\), the number of seeds with \(\beta\le c\sqrt p\) for fixed \(n,p,\epsilon,a\bmod p\) is
\[
                             O_c(\log(2p)).                             \tag{4}
\]
For each fixed \(0<c<1/\sqrt8\) there are only effectively finitely many tuples \((a,n,\epsilon,p)\) with \(\beta\le c\sqrt p\).
More precisely, uniformly over admissible tuples as \(p\to\infty\),
\[
            \liminf\frac{\beta}{\sqrt p}\ge\frac1{\sqrt8},\qquad
            p\log p\le 2\log r+(\log 8+o(1))p.                         \tag{5}
\]
Thus for fixed \(t>0\), the range \(p\log p\ge2\log r+tp\) has the per-class count (4) with \(c=e^{-t/2}\).
If \(t>\log 8\) this range contains only effectively finitely many tuples, as does \(p\log p\ge(2+\eta)\log r\) for each fixed \(\eta>0\).

For (4), the [cofactor and residue argument](Yin_C_G0345_v703.md#application) puts \(\beta\) in a single coefficient class of Salem minimal polynomials for the fixed data.
Their degree is \(d=n+1-\deg U\le p\), with \(U\) the product of the deleted linear factors, and their measures equal \(\beta\).
Each such polynomial determines at most one seed because its unique exterior root gives \(r=\beta^p\) and \(a=(r^{n+1}-\epsilon)/(r^n-\epsilon r)\).
If \(c\sqrt p<1\) the range is empty.
Otherwise use (2) with \(M=p\), \(B=c\sqrt p\) and \(\delta=-2\log c>0\).
Since \(d\le p\), this gives (4).

For (5), the [small-resultant alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) shows for \(p\ge11\) that either \(\beta\) is an exterior root in the same family at an integer seed \(b\ge2\), or
\[
                  \beta\ge\left(\frac{p}{4j_p}\right)^{d/(n+1)},
       \qquad j_p=\min\{j\ge0:\ j\in\mathbb Z,\ 1+2j(j+1)>p\}.
\]
In the first case \(r=\beta^p\) is an [effectively finite seed-power coincidence](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement).
In the other case \(j_p=\sqrt{p/2}+O(1)\) by the defining quadratic inequality.
The [degree and exponent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) gives \(p\ll d^2\log^5(3d)\), with effective constants.
As \(p\to\infty\), this forces \(d\to\infty\) and \((\log p)/(n+1)=O(\log(3d)/d)\to0\).
Since \(0\le n+1-d\le2\), the factor incurred by replacing \(d/(n+1)\) by one in the lower bound for \(\beta\) tends to one uniformly and effectively.
Dividing by \(\sqrt p\) gives (5), including the logarithmic form since \(\log r=p\log\beta\).
All comparisons have effective error bounds, so \(\beta\le c\sqrt p\) with \(c<1/\sqrt8\) places an effective upper bound on \(p\).
Then \(n<p\) and \(a<1+r\le1+(c\sqrt p)^p\) leave an effective finite list whose positive radical memberships are decidable by factoring and real root isolation.
For the last assertion, \(p\log p\ge(2+\eta)\log r\) gives \(\beta/\sqrt p\le p^{1/(2+\eta)-1/2}\to0\).
For bounded \(p\) this same inequality bounds the seed.
