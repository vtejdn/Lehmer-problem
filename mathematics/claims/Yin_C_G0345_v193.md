# Degree and measure of factors of a polynomial with two fixed coefficient blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## The contribution of each exterior root

Fix coprime nonzero \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\), with \(A\) monic and \(B(0)=\pm1\).
Let \(P_n=X^nA+B\) and let \(S_n\) be any monic irreducible noncyclotomic factor whenever one exists.
Write \(d_n=\deg S_n\), \(t_n=d_n/n\), and let \(m\) denote logarithmic Mahler measure.
For each distinct zero \(\xi\) of \(A\) with \(|\xi|>1\), let \(k_{\xi,n}\) count the roots of \(S_n\) in a fixed sufficiently small disk about \(\xi\).
For large \(n\) any such choice of disjoint disks gives the same counts.
Put
\[
 J=\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+\left|\frac{B(e^{i\theta})}{A(e^{i\theta})}\right|
                         \frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Values at the finitely many circle zeros of \(AB\) do not affect this finite integral.
Along every sequence of such factors with \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 m(S_n)=\sum_{\xi} k_{\xi,n}\log|\xi|+t_n J+o(1).                    \tag{1}
\]
For every monic irreducible \(q\mid A\) of multiplicity \(e\),
\[
 t_n m(q)=\frac1e\sum_{q(\xi)=0,\ |\xi|>1}k_{\xi,n}\log|\xi|+o(1).   \tag{2}
\]
In fact the factor heights all have the same limit on multiplication by \(n\).
\[
 \frac{n\,m(S_n)}{d_n}\longrightarrow I=
      \int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A|,|B|)\,d\sigma=m(A)+J,                 \tag{3}
\]
where \(\sigma\) is normalized circle measure.
There is \(c(A,B)>0\) such that all these factors have \(d_n\ge c(A,B)n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
If \(m(A)>0\) or \(m(B)>0\), the possible accumulation points of the pairs \((t_n,m(S_n))\) belong to a finite set determined by the two fixed polynomials.
If a monic irreducible \(q\) occurs once in \(A\) and has exactly one exterior root or exactly one exterior complex conjugate pair and no other exterior roots, then for every sufficiently large \(n\) all factors of \(P_n\) are cyclotomic except for one simple irreducible factor, whose degree differs from \(\deg P_n\) by a bounded eventually periodic function of \(n\).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Equidistribution for the whole polynomial and its factors

For large \(n\), \(P_n\) is monic of degree \(n+\deg A\), with constant term \(\pm1\).
The sum of its absolute coefficients is bounded independently of \(n\).
This bounds all its root moduli and \(m(P_n)\) uniformly, and bounds each \(m(S_n)\) by additivity.
For bounded \(d_n\) this would leave only finitely many monic possibilities by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
A fixed nontorsion root cannot recur for two exponents since \(A\) and \(B\) do not vanish together and \(z^n=-B(z)/A(z)\).
Thus \(d_n\to\infty\).
The absolute heights of the roots of \(S_n\) are \(m(S_n)/d_n\to0\).
Their orbit measures converge weakly to Haar probability \(\sigma\) on the unit circle by [Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures).

Off the unit circle the bounded root multiset has only two types of limits.
Outside, \(X^{-n}P_n\to A\) uniformly on compact sets, and inside, \(P_n\to B\) uniformly on compact sets.
Rouché's theorem in disjoint disks at the exterior roots of \(A\) and the interior roots of \(B\) gives precisely their multiplicities there, with convergence to the respective centers.
Call the remaining multiset \(D_n\).
Its moduli tend to one uniformly.
The root counting measure of \(D_n\) divided by \(n\) also converges to \(\sigma\).
Indeed, for any fixed positive integer \(j\), the \(j\)-th power sum of all roots is bounded by Newton's identities since the first \(j\) coefficients after the leading one stabilize.
Removing boundedly many roots from a bounded disk preserves this bound.
The uniform convergence of moduli allows projection to the unit circle in the normalized moments, and trigonometric polynomial approximation gives the weak limit.

Pass to a subsequence with \(t_n\to t\) and all exterior counts constant.
The roots of \(S_n\) in \(D_n\), counted with weight \(1/n\), then converge as measures to \(t\sigma\).
The complementary multiset in \(D_n\), including remaining copies at multiple roots of \(P_n\), converges to \((1-t)\sigma\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## A continuous test for the factor height

At each root \(z\) of \(S_n\), coprimality and \(B(0)\ne0\) give \(A(z)B(z)\ne0\).
The exact equation \(z^n=-B(z)/A(z)\) yields
\[
 n\log^+|z|=\log\max(|A(z)|,|B(z)|)-\log|A(z)|.
\]
Since \(A\) is monic, \(\operatorname{Res}(A,P_n)=\operatorname{Res}(A,B)\ne0\), also with the usual empty-product convention if \(A=1\).
Thus the absolute resultant with \(S_n\) lies between one and \(|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|\).
The average of the second logarithm over the roots of \(S_n\) tends to zero since its sum is precisely the log absolute resultant.
The first logarithm is continuous and bounded on the common root disk because \(A,B\) have no common zero.
Bilu's theorem now gives (3).
The identity \(I=m(A)+J\) follows by subtracting \(\log|A|\) under the integral, and Jensen's formula in \(y\) identifies \(I\) with the two-variable measure of \(yA(x)+B(x)\).
The logarithmic singularities of the separate terms on the circle are integrable.

<a id="resultants"></a>
## Reading the factor degree from a fixed resultant

Fix a monic irreducible \(q\mid A\) of multiplicity \(e\).
The nonzero integer \(\operatorname{Res}(q,P_n)=\operatorname{Res}(q,B)\) is fixed.
The absolute resultants of \(q\) with \(S_n\) and with \(P_n/S_n\) are therefore integers between one and \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,B)|\).
For each factor their logarithms equal the sum of \(\log|q(z)|\) over its roots since both polynomials are monic.

At an exterior zero \(\xi\) of \(q\) write \(A(X)=(X-\xi)^e a(X)\), where \(a(\xi)B(\xi)\ne0\).
For every root \(z\) approaching \(\xi\), the equation
\[
      |z-\xi|^e=|z|^{-n}|B(z)/a(z)|
\]
first gives exponential convergence and hence \(n(\log|z|-\log|\xi|)=o(1)\).
Since \(q\) has simple roots, \(\log|q(z)|=-(n/e)\log|\xi|+O(1)\).
The other roots outside \(D_n\) converge to points where \(q\ne0\), by the disjointness of distinct root centers and coprimality with \(B\).

Write \(b=\sum_{q(\xi)=0,\ |\xi|>1} k_{\xi,n}\log|\xi|/e\) for the fixed allocation on this subsequence.
Subtracting the roots outside \(D_n\) from the two bounded log resultants shows that the sums of \(\log|q(z)|/n\) on the two parts of \(D_n\) tend to \(b\) and \(m(q)-b\).
On the common bounded root disk, \(\max(\log|q(z)|,-K)\) is continuous and bounds \(\log|q(z)|\) from above.
Weak convergence followed by \(K\to\infty\) gives \(b\le t\,m(q)\) and \(m(q)-b\le(1-t)m(q)\).
Both are equalities, proving (2) even if \(q\) has a zero on the unit circle.
Every subsequence has a further subsequence with stabilized counts and degree proportion, so (2) holds on the original sequence.
Summing (2) with multiplicity over the irreducible divisors of \(A\) identifies the exterior contribution as \(t_n m(A)+o(1)\).
Since \(t_n\) is bounded, (3) then gives (1).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Linear degree and finite limiting proportions

If \(t_n\to0\) on a subsequence, (3) would give \(m(S_n)\to0\), contrary to the [positive factor gap for a fixed specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#statement) applied to \(yA(x)+B(x)\).
This proves the uniform linear lower bound for the degrees.

If \(m(A)>0\), take a \(q\) with \(m(q)>0\).
In (2) each \(k_{\xi,n}\) is one of \(0,\ldots,e\), so only finitely many degree limits are possible.
Equation (1) then gives finitely many pairs.
If only \(m(B)>0\), reverse \(P_n\) to obtain \(X^{n+\deg A-\deg B}B^*+A^*\), where \(H^*=X^{\deg H}H(1/X)\).
Multiplying by the sign of \(B(0)\) restores a monic leading block and a trailing constant term of absolute value one.
The reversed blocks are coprime, reversal preserves factor degrees and measures, and the ratio of the new exponent to \(n\) tends to one.
This proves the same finiteness assertion.

Finally, for the stated \(q\) with one exterior root or one conjugate exterior pair and \(e=1\), the counts in conjugate disks coincide by real coefficients.
Thus (2) allows only the degree limits zero and one.
The lower bound excludes zero for noncyclotomic factors.
At least one such factor exists for all large \(n\) because of the exterior root localized at the zero of \(q\).
Two factors counted with multiplicity would have total degree \(2n+o(n)>n+\deg A\) along any infinite sequence.
This gives the asserted uniqueness and simplicity.

For the degree difference it suffices to count the torsion roots of \(P_n\).
All pairs \((z,z^n)\) from such roots belong to the fixed irreducible curve \(yA(x)+B(x)=0\) in the two-dimensional torus.
Its irreducibility follows from coprimality and linearity in \(y\).
A one-dimensional torsion translate in this torus has equation \(x^a y^b=\omega\) for coprime integers \(a,b\) and a root of unity \(\omega\).
The graph cannot equal such a curve because substitution of \(-B/A\) has a pole or zero at the exterior root of \(q\) when \(b\ne0\), and \(x\) varies on the graph when \(b=0\).
The [finiteness of maximal torsion subvarieties](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#torus-bogomolov-complement) thus gives a finite set of possible coordinates \(z\).
For each such \(z\), membership in \(P_n\) depends only on \(n\) modulo the order of \(z\).
When it occurs its multiplicity is eventually one since \(P_n'(z)=nz^{n-1}A(z)+O_{A,B}(1)\) and \(A(z)\ne0\).
This proves eventual periodicity.
