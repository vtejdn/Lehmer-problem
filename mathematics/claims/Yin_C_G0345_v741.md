# Frobenius overlap on subsets of conjugates and primitive Salem trace fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A height estimate from a set of conjugates

Let \(\alpha\) be any nonzero algebraic number of infinite order, of absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\) and degree \(d\).
Let \(\Omega\) be its set of conjugates, let \(L\) be their splitting field and fix a nonempty set \(A\subseteq\Omega\) of size \(r\).
For each prime \(p\) in a finite set choose \(k_p\ge1\), put \(q_p=p^{k_p}\), and choose \(B_p\subseteq\Omega\) such that the \(q_p\)-th powers within \(B_p\) are distinct.
Choose a uniformly random prime \(P\) of \(L\) above \(p\), then a uniform element \(y\) of the full coset in its decomposition group acting by \(z\mapsto z^{q_p}\) on the residue field.
Set
\[
                 \xi_p=r^{-1}\mathbb E\,|A\cap yB_p|.
\]
For every integer \(T\ge1\), with \(S=Tr+\sum_p|B_p|\), one has
\[
 Tr\sum_p\xi_p\log p\ \le\
 \tfrac12\big(T^2r+\sum_p|B_p|\big)\log S
       +(S-1)\big(Tr+\sum_p q_p|B_p|\big)h.                          \tag{1}
\]
The sets can have different sizes and the set \(A\) need not be the orbit of a subgroup.
Only the specified injectivity within each individual powered set is required.
It holds for every choice in a Salem orbit, since an automorphism carrying one of two distinct equal-power conjugates to the unique exterior root would give another conjugate with the same exterior modulus.

For a fixed integer \(b\ge1\) put \(g_b(x)=(\log\log(5x))^{b+2}/(\log(2x))^{2b+1}\).
Suppose \(|B_p|=r\), \(1\le k_p\le b\), and the mean overlap weighted by \(\log p\) over all primes in the interval specified [below](#interval) is at least \(\eta>0\).
Then
\[
                             rh\gg_{b,\eta} g_b(r).                    \tag{2}
\]
The estimate is at arbitrary height and uses the actual uniform lifts at ramified primes.
For algebraic integers of total log measure \(m=dh\le1\) an alternative uses one Frobenius lift and its conjugates at each prime, at the explicit original-degree cost in (4) below.

There exist totally real fields \(F_n\) of degrees \(n^3\), for arbitrarily large primes \(n\), with no subfield strictly between \(\mathbb Q\) and \(F_n\), such that some Salem number has trace field \(F_n\).
Every Salem number with this trace field satisfies
\[
                   \log\alpha\gg n\left(\frac{\log\log(5n)}
                                                   {\log(2n)}\right)^3. \tag{3}
\]
At all the prescribed test primes the largest individual Frobenius coset probability on the embeddings of \(F_n\) is exactly \(1/(3(n-1)^2)\) for every residue power from one to \(n-1\).
Thus the small individual probabilities in this example coexist with a large overlap between sets of conjugates.
The result asserts no supply of such overlaps in an arbitrary trace field.

<a id="edges"></a>
## The contribution of a pair at a ramified prime

Use \(v_P(p)=1\) and \(|p|_P=p^{-1}\).
Fix \(b_0\in B_p,\ a\in A\), and put \(v=P\cap\mathbb Q(b_0)\), with ramification index \(e_v\).
At fixed \(P\), the conditional chance that \(yb_0=a\) is either zero or \(1/e_v\).
Indeed the full lift coset equals \(xI_P\) for any one lift \(x\), and its images of \(b_0\) form a translate of the inertia orbit of size \(e_v\), all occurring with equal frequency by the [local inertia count](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#powers).
If a lift carries \(b_0\) to \(a\) and \(|b_0|_P\le1\), choose the representative \(z\) of the residue of \(b_0\) in \(\mathbb Q(b_0)_v\) satisfying \(z^{p^{f_v}}=z\).
The residue degree is \(f_v\) and simple-root lifting gives \(v_P(b_0-z)\ge1/e_v\).
The same uniqueness in \(L_P\) gives \(y(z)=z^{q_p}\).
Thus \(v_P(a-b_0^{q_p})\ge1/e_v\).
When \(|b_0|_P>1\), apply the calculation to \(b_0^{-1}\) and \(a^{-1}\), since \(y\) preserves the valuation.
In both cases the local factor
\[
  \frac{|a-b_0^{q_p}|_P}{\max(1,|a|_P)\max(1,|b_0^{q_p}|_P)}
\]
is at most \(p^{-1/e_v}\) at a hit and at most one everywhere.
Consequently its negative logarithm averaged over \(P\mid p\) is at least \(\mathbb P(yb_0=a)\log p\).
Summing over distinct ordered pairs gives \(r\xi_p\log p\).
Each factor occurs once here, even if several pairs have the same residue or several lifts realize one pair.

Use the Vandermonde determinant with multiplicity \(T\) at the nodes \(a\in A\) and multiplicity one at the nodes \(b_0^{q_p}\) for \(b_0\in B_p\).
Its nodes are distinct within the indicated sets, and the different positive heights \(h,q_ph\) separate different sets.
Each of the cross factors above has multiplicity \(T\).
For \(S\) columns the archimedean divided-derivative row of order \(j\) at \(z\) has norm at most \(S^{j+1/2}\max(1,|z|)^{S-1}\).
At the finite places the ordinary differences are bounded by the products of the two maxima, so every node of multiplicity \(m_z\) incurs exponent at most \(m_z(S-1)\) in those maxima.
Summing the logarithms of the row factors gives \((T^2r+\sum_p|B_p|)\log S/2\).
The normalized place weights of \(L\) over each rational prime are equal.
The product formula for this nonzero determinant together with the height charges gives (1), as in the [confluent estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#local), with no rationality requirement on either subset.

<a id="interval"></a>
## Actual overlap and a version using a single lift

For (2) put \(T=\lceil C_\eta\log(2r)/\log\log(5r)\rceil\), \(X=T^2\log(2T)\).
Take the interval \([X,2X]\) and choose the constant sufficiently large in terms of \(\eta\).
There are \(t\asymp T^2\) primes there, with total logarithmic weight \(\asymp X\), by the [prime interval estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice).
After division by \(r\) the arithmetic term in (1) is \(\gg\eta T^3\log(2T)\).
The first expense is \(O(T^2(\log(2r)+\log(2T)))\), at most half the arithmetic term by choice of \(C_\eta\).
The coefficient of \(rh\) is \(O_b(T^{4+2b}(\log(2T))^b)\).
Subtraction gives \(rh\gg_{b,\eta}T^{-(2b+1)}(\log(2T))^{1-b}\gg_{b,\eta}g_b(r)\).
One can equivalently use a subset of the interval supplying the stipulated lower fraction of its full logarithmic prime weight in overlap, by omitting all other nodes in (1).

Alternatively choose at one prime above each \(p\) a lift \(x_p\) of ordinary Frobenius.
For \(g\) uniform in the splitting group put \(\widetilde\xi_p=r^{-1}\mathbb E_g|A\cap g x_p^{k_p}g^{-1} B_p|\), still with \(|B_p|=r\).
For each fixed \(b_0\in B_p\) the uniformly conjugated lift at \(gP\) gives an image of \(b_0\) in one of the \(e_v\) possible positions of the full lift coset.
If the image belongs to \(A\), that coset sends \(b_0\) into \(A\) with probability at least \(1/e_v\).
The loss from the indicator of the first event to the full-coset probability is bounded above by \(1-1/e_v\).
Averaging, the restriction of \(gP\) to a place \(v\) of \(\mathbb Q(b_0)\) has weight \(e_vf_v/d\).
The expected loss is thus at most \(\delta_p=1-\sum_{v\mid p} f_v/d\), the same for all \(b_0\) since the root fields are conjugate.
Consequently \(\xi_p\ge\widetilde\xi_p-\delta_p\).
If \(\alpha\) is integral the [discriminant budget](Yin_C_G0345_v734.md#average) gives \(\sum_p \delta_p\log p\le\log d+2m\).

At \(m\le1\), take
\[
 T=\left\lceil C_\eta\max\left\{\frac{\log(2r)}{\log\log(5r)},
                    \sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}{\log\log(5d)}}\right\}\right\rceil,
       \qquad X=T^2\log(2T).
\]
If the weighted mean of \(\widetilde\xi_p\) in \([X,2X]\) is at least \(\eta\), the ramification budget costs at most half the asserted weight for large enough \(C_\eta\), since \(X\gg C_\eta^2\log(2d)\).
The same determinant calculation yields
\[
                   m\gg_{b,\eta}\frac{d}{r}\,
                         T^{-(2b+1)}(\log(2T))^{1-b}.                  \tag{4}
\]
The dependence on \(d\) in the interval length here pays for replacing the actual full lift distributions.

## A primitive trace field with small individual probabilities

Let \(n\) be a sufficiently large prime and \(r=n(n+1)\).
Take the interval for (2) with a fixed small absolute \(\eta\), say \(1/100\).
Use its primes \(p\equiv1\bmod7\) as the test set.
They supply asymptotically one sixth of the interval's logarithmic prime weight by the [prime number theorem in this progression](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz), using partial summation.
The fixed cyclic totally real cubic field \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1})\) splits completely at each of them since the primitive seventh roots lie in \(\mathbb Q_p\).

Construct a monic degree-\(n\) polynomial \(f\) over \(E\).
At the three places of \(E\) over each test prime, prescribe respectively two separable irreducible degree-\(n\) reductions and a complete split into different local roots.
For the latter use an arbitrarily close coefficientwise \(p\)-adic approximation to \(\prod_{j=1}^n(X-j)\), which need not have distinct residues.
The [strong Hensel inequality](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) at its finitely many distinct integer roots gives distinct roots in disjoint local neighborhoods for sufficiently close coefficients.
For the irreducible reductions the roots lift from their unramified extension, giving a single \(n\)-cycle under residue Frobenius.
Over three more distinct split primes larger than \(n\) prescribe squarefree Frobenius cycle types at one place respectively an \(n\)-cycle, an \((n-1)\)-cycle with one fixed point, and a single transposition, and prescribe complete splitting at their other two places.
Distinct finite-field factors of these degrees exist by the [finite-field construction](../audits/literature/Hun74/Yin_U006.md#hun74-u006), with enough distinct linear factors at the auxiliary primes.
At all real embeddings of \(E\) require \(f\) to have \(n\) different real roots, an open coefficient condition.
These requirements can be met simultaneously.
For each nonleading coefficient choose an integral element satisfying the necessary residue congruences by the Chinese remainder theorem in \(\mathcal O_E\).
With \(J\) the product of the chosen prime ideal powers, add an element of \(J/M\) for an arbitrarily large positive integer \(M\) prime to all the selected primes.
The local congruences persist and the real embeddings of \(J/M\) are a full lattice of mesh tending to zero, allowing all real open conditions to hold.

Let \(L_0\) be the splitting field over \(E\) of the three \(\mathbb Q\)-conjugate polynomials.
It is totally real and Galois over \(\mathbb Q\).
Its Galois group embeds into \(S_n^3\rtimes C_3\) by acting on the three labeled root lists, with cyclic action on the labels, and surjects onto \(C_3=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\).
At each prescribed split prime the whole local splitting extension is unramified, and the three components of its Frobenius have the types prescribed at the three places, in cyclic order.
The auxiliary primes give kernel elements supported on a single coordinate with each of the three specified nonidentity types.
By conjugating one may place each such element at any given coordinate.
The coordinate projection of the kernel is transitive by the \(n\)-cycle, and its \((n-1)\)-cycle gives a transitive stabilizer on the other points, hence double transitivity.
Conjugating the single-coordinate transposition by elements of the kernel now gives every transposition supported at that coordinate.
This proves that the kernel contains the full \(S_n^3\) and that the splitting group is \(G=S_n^3\rtimes C_3\).

Act on \(\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,n\}^3\) by the product action and let \(F_n\) be fixed by the stabilizer \(H=(S_{n-1})^3\rtimes C_3\) of \((1,1,1)\).
This faithful action has degree \(n^3\) and \(L_0\) is the normal closure of \(F_n\).
There is no proper intermediate field: a subgroup strictly larger than \(H\) contains, after correction by a coordinate rotation, an element \((u_1,u_2,u_3)\) moving \(1\) in at least one coordinate.
Conjugating the single-coordinate \(S_{n-1}\) in that coordinate gives the stabilizer of a different point.
The two stabilizers together generate \(S_n\) for \(n\ge3\), as their transpositions form a connected graph.
Coordinate rotation then gives every coordinate copy.

At each test prime the Frobenius law on these embeddings is uniform over the conjugates of \((c,c',1)\), where \(c,c'\) are \(n\)-cycles.
Every power with exponent \(1\le k<n\) has the same conjugacy law because \(n\) is prime.
The fixed coordinate is uniform among three, and the other two permutations are independent uniform \(n\)-cycles given that coordinate.
The image of an individual point under a uniform \(n\)-cycle is uniform among its \(n-1\) other possible images.
The three choices of fixed coordinate give disjoint sets of images, proving the stated largest probability \(1/(3(n-1)^2)\).
Take the triangular subset \(\mathcal A=\{(i,j,1):1\le i\le j\le n\}\).
Its only constant coordinate is the third, so a setwise stabilizer cannot rotate the coordinate labels.
Its row sizes \(n,n-1,\ldots,1\) and column sizes \(1,\ldots,n\) are all different within each list, forcing each of the first two coordinate permutations in a stabilizer to be the identity.
The third permutation must fix \(1\), so the stabilizer fixes the triangle pointwise.
In particular this nonsingleton triangle is no subgroup orbit, and none of the indicated Frobenius elements maps it onto itself.
With probability \(1/3\) the third coordinate permutation is the identity.
Conditionally on that event the image of \((i,j,1)\) in the first two coordinates is uniform among the \((n-1)^2\) pairs avoiding row \(i\) and column \(j\).
At least \(|\mathcal A|-2n\) of those pairs belong to the triangle, a fraction at least \(1/3\) for sufficiently large \(n\).
The normalized expected self-overlap is therefore at least \(1/9\).
The whole containing slice is a subgroup orbit and is another possible test using auxiliary fields.
The triangular estimate uses partial intersections and only compares to individual transition probabilities on the stated trace embeddings.

## Realization by a Salem number and the height of every such generator

In the real embeddings of \(F_n\), the image of \(\mathcal O_{F_n}\) is a full lattice.
Take a centered rectangular box of sufficiently large first side and with all other half-lengths \(1/4\).
It contains a nonzero lattice point: a half-sized copy has volume greater than the lattice covolume, so reduction modulo the lattice identifies two distinct points of that copy, whose difference is in the original box.
For the corresponding nonzero integer \(t\), its nonzero integral norm gives \(|t_1|\ge4^{n^3-1}>2\).
After changing its sign, precisely one embedding has \(t_1>2\), while all others have modulus less than one.
This unique exterior value forces \(t\) to generate \(F_n\), since each value from a proper subfield repeats in the embedding list.
The equation \(X^2-tX+1=0\) is irreducible over \(F_n\), because its discriminant is negative at every other real embedding.
Its larger root is a Salem number of degree \(2n^3\) with trace field \(F_n\).

For any Salem number with trace field \(F_n\), the inverse pairs of its conjugates map equivariantly and two-to-one onto the embeddings of \(F_n\).
Take \(A=B_p\) to consist of both members of every pair over the triangle \(\mathcal A\), so \(|A|=r\).
Even if the full Salem splitting field ramifies at a test prime, every residue Frobenius lift projects to the unique Frobenius at the restricted prime of the unramified trace closure.
Uniform primes upstairs restrict to uniform primes there by Galois transitivity.
The normalized overlap for \(k_p=1\) is therefore at least \(1/9\) at each test prime.
Their combined weight suffices for the chosen \(\eta\).
Use (2) with \(b=1\), \(d=2n^3\) and \(h=\log\alpha/d\), obtaining (3) with an absolute constant for all sufficiently large prime \(n\).
Neither the local realization nor (3) controls individual Salem trace fields outside the prescribed class.
