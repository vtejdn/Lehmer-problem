# A uniform energy bound from polynomial division at both ends

<a id="statement"></a>
## Integral Laurent numerators of arbitrary width

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no zero on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\log M(P)>0\), \(t=e^{-m}\) and \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\).
For every \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) indivisible by \(P\) in the Laurent ring, let \(E=\|A/P\|_2^2\) for Haar probability measure on the circle.
With \(k=\lfloor(\log 2)/(2m)\rfloor\), one has
\[
 E\ \ge\ \min\{C-\tfrac12,\ 2k e^{-2mk}\}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
 E\ge \frac1{2\sinh(2m)}-\frac12
       \quad (0<m\le\log2-\tfrac12),\qquad
 \liminf_{m\downarrow0}mE\ge\frac14.                                \tag{2}
\]
The limit inferior applies along every such sequence, including reducible denominators and numerators of unbounded width.
The leading constant improves the [one-ended coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#statement) for squared energy.
For width less than \(d\), the [short-numerator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement) gives \(E\ge C\).

<a id="division"></a>
## Choosing a remainder window with balanced quotient supports

Multiplication of \(A\) by a monomial preserves its energy and Laurent divisibility.
Normalize \(A\) to have smallest exponent zero and degree \(D\).
If \(D<d\), the short-numerator bound proves (1).
Suppose \(D\ge d\) and let \(q=D-d\).
For each \(j\) from \(0\) to \(q+1\), form the unique decomposition
\[
          X^{-j}A=PQ+R,\qquad Q\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}],\quad
                          R\in\mathbb Z[X],\quad \deg R<d.             \tag{3}
\]
To construct it, take the coefficients of \(A/P\) at the exponents \(0,\ldots,j-1\) from its expansion at zero and those at \(j,\ldots,q\) from its expansion at infinity.
Their sum as a polynomial, multiplied by \(X^{-j}\), gives \(Q\).
Both expansions have integral coefficients because \(P(0)=\pm1\) and \(P\) is monic.
Subtracting \(PX^jQ\) from \(A\) leaves only exponents in \([j,j+d-1]\), proving existence.
A nonzero Laurent multiple of \(P\) has width at least \(d\), which proves uniqueness.
Indivisibility gives \(R\ne0\).

Let \(n_-\) and \(n_+\) count the occupied exponents of \(Q\) strictly below zero and at least zero, respectively.
As \(j\) increases, \(n_-\) gains zero or one and \(n_+\) loses zero or one, according to the two fixed expansions at the crossed exponent.
The difference \(n_--n_+\) starts nonpositive, ends nonnegative, and increases by at most two in one step.
Choose \(j\) with \(|n_--n_+|\le1\) and put \(n=\max(n_-,n_+)\).
If \(b=\|Q\|_2\), integrality gives \(b^2\ge n_-+n_+\ge 2n-1\).
The first occupied coefficient of \(R\) is a nonzero integer, so the [short-numerator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement) gives \(r^2=\|R/P\|_2^2\ge C\).

<a id="projections"></a>
## Orthogonal estimates for the two ends of the quotient

Use the [exterior polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) \(O\) with \(|O|=|P|\) on the circle, \(O(0)=e^m\) and leading coefficient of modulus \(e^{-m}\).
Write \(O^*(z)=z^d\overline{O(1/\bar z)}\) and put
\[
            U=P/O,\qquad V=O^*/P,\qquad J=O^*/O=UV.
\]
The functions \(U,V,J\) are analytic on the closed disk and unimodular on its boundary.
Indeed \(O\) has only exterior roots, while \(O^*\) contains every interior root of \(P\) with at least its multiplicity by the construction of \(O\).
The endpoint coefficients give \(|U(0)|=|V(0)|=t\).

Let \(H^2\) be the closed span of nonnegative Fourier modes in circle \(L^2\), and let \(H^2_-\) be its orthogonal complement.
The function \(Y=R/O\) belongs to \(H^2\), and \(\bar JY=R/O^*\) on the circle has only strictly negative modes by its expansion at infinity.
Thus \(Y\) is orthogonal to the two mutually orthogonal spaces \(H^2_-\) and \(JH^2\).
Write \(\Pi\) for the orthogonal projection onto their sum and \(Z=UQ\), so that \(\|Z\|_2=b\), \(\|Y\|_2=r\) and \(E=\|Y+Z\|_2^2\).

To estimate the component of \(Z\) in \(H^2_-\), take any \(l\le n_-\) of the negative occupied positions starting from the smallest exponent.
The coefficients of \(UQ\) at these positions are the product of a lower triangular matrix and the corresponding coefficient vector of \(Q\).
All earlier occupied inputs are included and \(U\) has only nonnegative modes.
The matrix is a compression of multiplication by \(U\) to those monomials, hence a contraction, with diagonal \(U(0)\).
Its least singular value is at least \(t^l\), by its determinant and the upper bound one on every singular value.
For the component in \(JH^2\), take \(l\le n_+\) nonnegative occupied positions starting from the largest exponent.
The coefficients there of \(\bar JZ=\bar VQ\) are given by an upper triangular contraction with diagonal \(\overline{V(0)}\), since \(\bar V\) has only nonpositive modes.
Their squared sum is bounded by the squared norm of the \(JH^2\) component of \(Z\).
These two estimates may use different values of \(l\).

Taking all occupied positions on both sides gives
\[
      \|\Pi Z\|_2^2\ge t^{2n_-}\|Q_-\|_2^2+
                          t^{2n_+}\|Q_+\|_2^2\ge t^{2n}b^2,          \tag{4}
\]
where \(Q_-\) and \(Q_+\) are the negative and nonnegative parts of \(Q\).
An empty part contributes zero.
Since \(\Pi Y=0\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(Y\) and \(Z-\Pi Z\) yields
\[
 E\ge r^2+b^2-2urb,\qquad u=\sqrt{1-t^{2n}}.                        \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(E\ge t^{2n}r^2\) by completing the square in \(b\), for every decomposition (3), even before balancing.
Taking \(l\) positions on each side and using their nonzero integral coefficients gives a second bound,
\[
           E\ge\|\Pi Z\|_2^2\ge 2l\,t^{2l}
                  \qquad (1\le l\le\min(n_-,n_+)).                  \tag{6}
\]

<a id="estimate"></a>
## Combining the bounds at the balanced window

If \(n>k\), balancing gives at least \(k\) occupied coefficients on each side.
Equation (6) gives \(E\ge2kt^{2k}\), with the zero bound immediate when \(k=0\).
If \(n=0\), then \(Q=0\) and \(E=r^2\ge C\).
It remains to consider \(1\le n\le k\), for which \(t^{2n}\ge1/2\) and \(u^2\le1/2\).
When \(ub\ge\sqrt C\), completing the square in \(r\) in (5) gives \(E\ge(1-u^2)b^2\ge u^2b^2\ge C\).
When \(ub<\sqrt C\), the right side of (5) increases for \(r\ge\sqrt C\), giving \(E\ge C+b^2-2u\sqrt C\,b\).
Using \(C=t^2/(1-t^4)\), one obtains
\[
 4Cu^2=4C(1-t^{2n})\le4Cn(1-t^2)
                        =\frac{4nt^2}{1+t^2}<2n.
\]
The function \(b\mapsto b^2-\sqrt{2n}\,b\) increases for \(b\ge\sqrt{2n-1}\).
At the left endpoint it equals \(2n-1-\sqrt{(2n-1)2n}>-1/2\) by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
This proves the other term of (1).

Finally \(2kt^{2k}\ge k\ge(\log2)/(2m)-1\).
For \(m\le\log2-1/2\) this is at least \(1/(4m)-1/2\ge C-1/2\), which proves (2).
In particular the same constant \(1/4\) bounds the scaled least energy after [nearest-integer rounding](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#rounding) in any nonzero Laurent residue class.
The estimate applies to the unrounded reciprocal squares used in [cyclotomic matching](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#matching) and also to every integral representative obtained by rounding them.
