# Counting algebraic units by the minimum coefficient length of a multiple

<a id="statement"></a>
## Sharp growth with the degree

For an algebraic unit \(\beta\), let \(m(\beta)\) denote the logarithmic Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial, and let \(\lambda(\beta)\) be the least sum of absolute coefficients of any nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(\beta\).
Count units up to conjugacy over \(\mathbb Q\), equivalently by their monic minimal polynomials.
For every \(x>0\) and integer \(L\ge2\), the number of classes of degree at most \(D\), measure \(m(\beta)\ge x\), and \(\lambda(\beta)\le L\) is
\[
                              O_{L,x}(D^{L-1}) .                       \tag{1}
\]

For each integer \(k\ge2\), write \(\theta\) for the positive root of \(X^3-X-1\) and set
\[
               I_k=[\log\theta,\tfrac12\log(k+1)].
\]
Among units with measure in \(I_k\), there are \(\asymp_k D^k\) conjugacy classes of degree at most \(D\) with \(\lambda(\beta)=k+1\), as \(D\) tends to infinity.
This order of growth already holds among nonreciprocal units having a real conjugate \(\beta>1\) of strictly largest modulus.
Every such unit is power-stable, meaning that each positive power has its original degree.
One can require the degrees in the lower bound to lie between \(c_kD\) and \(D\) for a constant \(c_k>0\).
Counting all conjugates individually therefore gives \(\asymp_k D^{k+1}\) units at this exact length in \(I_k\).
The upper endpoint of \(I_k\) grows with \(k\), so these examples leave open the existence of unbounded minimum lengths in one fixed bounded interval.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Gaps in a shortest relation

Let \(P\) be a minimal polynomial counted in (1), let \(d=\deg P\), and choose a relation of length \(\lambda(\beta)\).
No nonempty proper subset of its nonzero terms vanishes at \(\beta\), by the minimality of its length.
Divide out the smallest power of \(X\) and choose the sign with positive leading coefficient to obtain \(F\) with exponents \(0=e_0<\cdots<e_{s-1}\), where \(2\le s\le L\).
For any two consecutive exponents write \(F=A+X^{e_{i+1}}H\), collecting terms up to \(e_i\) in \(A\), and put \(g=e_{i+1}-e_i\).
The algebraic integer \(H(\beta)\) is nonzero by the proper-subset condition.
For each conjugate \(\gamma\) with \(|\gamma|>1\), the equation gives \(|H(\gamma)|=|\gamma|^{-e_{i+1}}|A(\gamma)|\le L|\gamma|^{-g}\).
For every remaining conjugate, direct evaluation gives \(|H(\gamma)|\le L\).
Since \(P\) is monic, multiplication over all conjugates yields
\[
         1\le |\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\beta)/\mathbb Q}H(\beta)|
             \le L^d\exp(-g m(\beta)).
\]
Summing this bound on the gaps gives
\[
                    \deg F\le (s-1)d\log L/x .                        \tag{2}
\]
The same argument applies to any normalized relation of length at most \(L\) with the proper-subset condition, even if it does not attain \(\lambda(\beta)\).

There are \(O_{L,x}(D^{s-1})\) choices for the \(s-1\) positive exponents in (2) and \(O_L(1)\) choices for the signed coefficients.
Each resulting \(F\) has at most \(\lfloor(\log L)/x\rfloor\) distinct irreducible factors of measure at least \(x\), since integer factors have nonnegative measure, measure is additive on products, and \(m(F)\le\log L\).
Summing over \(s\) proves (1).

<a id="family"></a>
## A dominant root with a relation of prescribed length

For integers \(1\le r_1<\cdots<r_k\le D\) with \(\gcd(r_1,\ldots,r_k)=1\), consider
\[
                   F_{\boldsymbol r}(X)=X^{r_k}-1-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}X^{r_j}.   \tag{3}
\]
For \(t>0\) the function \(t^{-r_k}+\sum_{j<k}t^{r_j-r_k}\) strictly decreases from infinity to zero and has value \(k>1\) at \(1\).
Its unique value of \(t\) at which it equals \(1\) is the unique positive root \(\beta>1\) of (3).
For any complex root \(z\), the triangle inequality gives \(|z|^{r_k}\le 1+\sum_{j<k}|z|^{r_j}\), hence \(|z|\le\beta\).
If equality holds then all \(z^{r_j}\) for \(j<k\) have the argument of the constant term \(1\) on the right, as does \(z^{r_k}\).
Thus \((z/|z|)^{r_j}=1\) for every \(j\le k\), and the gcd condition forces \(z=\beta\).
For each \(n\ge1\), an embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) over \(\mathbb Q(\beta^n)\) must send \(\beta\) to a root of the same modulus.
There is just one such embedding, which proves power stability by separability.

The monic polynomial (3) has constant term \(-1\), so \(\beta\) is a unit.
Its inverse lies in \((0,1)\), where (3) has no zero, so its minimal polynomial is nonreciprocal.
The [nonreciprocal measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) therefore gives \(m(\beta)\ge\log\theta\).
Jensen's inequality for the circle mean of \(\log|F_{\boldsymbol r}|^2\), followed by Parseval, gives \(m(\beta)\le m(F_{\boldsymbol r})\le\tfrac12\log(k+1)\).
At the positive root all the terms on the right of \(\beta^{r_k}=1+\sum_{j<k}\beta^{r_j}\) are strictly positive.
A proper subset omitting the leading term cannot vanish, and a subset containing it would need every term on the right to reach equality.
Consequently (3) is a normalized nondegenerate relation of length \(k+1\) at \(\beta\).
The [uniform bound for normalized relations](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#relations) limits the number of different polynomials (3) sharing this root to \(B_{k+1}\), independently of its degree.
The strictly dominant root is unique in its conjugacy class, so the same bound holds per minimal polynomial.

<a id="lower"></a>
## Removing the shorter relations

For integer \(D\), start with all \(D^k\) ordered tuples in \([1,D]^k\).
The number with a common prime divisor is at most
\[
   D^k\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{-k}
   \le D^k\left(\tfrac14+\int_2^\infty t^{-2}\,dt\right)
   =\tfrac34D^k .
\]
Tuples with repeated coordinates contribute \(O_k(D^{k-1})\), and tuples with every coordinate at most \(D/4\) contribute at most \(D^k/4^k\).
After these exclusions at least a positive proportion remains, including for \(k=2\).
Sorting divides their count by \(k!\), giving \(\gg_k D^k\) distinct relations (3) with \(r_k>D/4\).
The uniform bound per minimal polynomial gives \(\gg_k D^k\) classes with measure in \(I_k\) and \(\lambda(\beta)\le k+1\).
Estimate (2), applied to (3) with \(x=\log\theta\), gives
\[
           D\ge d\ge \frac{r_k\log\theta}{k\log(k+1)}>
                         \frac{D\log\theta}{4k\log(k+1)}.
\]
By (1) with \(L=k\), at most \(O_k(D^{k-1})\) of these classes have a shorter relation.
Removing them gives the asserted lower bound at exact length \(k+1\).
The matching upper bound follows from (1) with \(L=k+1\), and each counted class contributes exactly \(d\) distinct conjugates.
