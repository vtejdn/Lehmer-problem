# Large residue characteristics for two-prime rows of vanishing cost

<a id="statement"></a>
## Removing the small primes and both exponent primes

Let \(X(L)\ge1\) be any computable integer cutoff on integers \(L\ge2\) such that \(X(L)\to\infty\) and \(\log X(L)=o(\log L)\).
One example is \(2^{\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor}\rfloor}\).
Let \(\mathcal P_L,\mathcal Q_L\) consist of the primes in \([L,5L/4]\) and \([3L/2,2L]\), respectively.
For a positive integer \(N\) and a pair \((p,q)\) from these sets, put
\[
 N^*=\prod_{\substack{\ell>X(L)\text{ prime}\\ \ell\nmid pq}}\ell^{v_\ell(N)}.
\]
There are a computable integer width \(b(L)\to\infty\) and computable sets \(\mathcal G_L\subseteq\mathcal P_L\times\mathcal Q_L\) of relative size tending to one with the following property.
For all sufficiently large \(L\), each \((p,q)\in\mathcal G_L\), and every integral pair \(U,V\) passing the [two-prime test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at width \(b=b(L)\), put \(\Sigma=p+q,\ H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1),\ \eta=\log H/\Sigma\).
Write \(B=B(U,V)\) for the positive generator of \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\).
If \(0<\eta\le10^{-3}\) and \(T_\eta>\mathrm e\) is the larger solution of \(T/\log T=(4\eta)^{-1/3}\), then
\[
            \frac{\log B^*}{\log H}>\frac{1-1/b}{2}\exp(T_\eta).       \tag{1}
\]
For **every** choice of one qualifying seed at each prime there is a prime \(\ell>X(L)\), distinct from \(p,q\), at which their two powered minima and \(U,V\) share a nonzero root of degree at most the smaller seed degree over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
The quantitative bound (1) counts prime powers with their full multiplicities.

On every such sequence with \(L\to\infty\) and \(\eta\to0\), both \(\log B^*/\log H\) and \(\log B^*/\Sigma\) tend to infinity, and
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log(\log B^*/\log H)}{\eta^{-1/3}\log(1/\eta)}
                  \ge \frac{4^{-1/3}}3 .                              \tag{2}
\]
Existence of a vanishing-cost sequence on these good pairs is equivalent to vanishing of the unrestricted Lehmer infimum \(\lambda\).
If \(\lambda=0\), one may prescribe any sequence of scales tending to infinity and any good pair at each sufficiently large scale, and then select the rows.

<a id="small"></a>
## The contribution from all characteristics below the cutoff

Fix an ordered pair of nontorsion reciprocal unit minima \(P_1,P_2\) with degrees \(e_1,e_2\) and roots \(a_i,c_j\), allowing equal seeds.
Take the compositum \(K\) of their splitting field and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\), and write \(D=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
For all sufficiently large \(L\) the powered lists have full degrees and are disjoint on the entire rectangle by the [fixed-seed power criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#gcd).
Thus \(d_{ij}=a_i^p-c_j^q\ne0\), and \(C=\prod_{ij}d_{ij}\) is a nonzero rational integer.
The [divisor from the local conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local) satisfies \(B_0\mid |C|^3\) and \(B_0\mid B\) whenever the original seed contacts hold.
Extension of the splitting field preserves algebraic integrality of each quotient defining \(B_0\).

For a prime ideal \(\mathfrak l\mid\ell\) in \(K\), use its integral valuation \(v\) and set \(W_{\mathfrak l}=(\log N\mathfrak l)/D\).
Since \(c_j\) is a unit, \(v(d_{ij})=v(a_i^p c_j^{-q}-1)\).
Apply [Yu's explicit two-logarithm bound](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-input-logarithmic-bound) with bases \(a_i,c_j\) and exponents \(p,-q\).
Both bases are units, their product with these exponents differs from one, and \(K\) contains \(\zeta_3,\zeta_4\), as required at \(2\) and at odd primes.
The displayed introductory consequence in Yu99 bounds the integral valuation by \(O_{P_1,P_2}(\ell^D\log(2L))\), uniformly in the prime ideal.
Indeed the ramification index and residue degree \(f\) are at most \(D\), the factor \(\ell^f\) is at most \(\ell^D\), and the product of the two factors \(\max(h_{\rm abs}(a_i),\log\ell)\), \(\max(h_{\rm abs}(c_j),\log\ell)\) divided by \((f\log\ell)^2\) is uniformly bounded.
Here \(h_{\rm abs}\) denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.
This application uses the published inequality at the source unit's stated inspection depth.
Summing \(W_{\mathfrak l}v(d_{ij})\) over all indices and primes above \(\ell\) gives \(v_\ell(C)\log\ell\), since \(C\) is rational and the ramification indices times residue degrees sum to \(D\).
Also \(\sum_{\mathfrak l\mid\ell}W_{\mathfrak l}\le\log\ell\).
Consequently, writing \(X=X(L)\),
\[
        \sum_{\ell\le X}v_\ell(C)\log\ell
         \ll_{P_1,P_2} X^{D+1}\log(2X)\log(2L)=o_{P_1,P_2,X(\cdot)}(L). \tag{3}
\]
The last limit takes \(L\to\infty\) for fixed seeds and the prescribed cutoff.

<a id="moving"></a>
## Spacing of the partners at which an exponent prime contributes

For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), all but \(O_{P_1,P_2,\epsilon}(|\mathcal P_L|+|\mathcal Q_L|)\) pairs satisfy
\[
             v_p(C)\log p\le\epsilon L,\qquad
             v_q(C)\log q\le\epsilon L.                               \tag{4}
\]
Fix \(p\) first.
If the first bound fails, then for some indices \(i,j\) and some prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\mid p\),
\[
          W_{\mathfrak p}v_{\mathfrak p}(a_i^p-c_j^q)
                   >\epsilon L/(e_1e_2D),
\]
since the sum giving \(v_p(C)\log p\) contains at most \(e_1e_2D\) nonnegative terms.
Fix one such triple and consider all \(q\) satisfying this strict inequality there.
If \(q_1<q_2\) both do, subtraction of the two differences and the unit property of \(c_j\) give the same strict lower bound for \(W_{\mathfrak p}v_{\mathfrak p}(c_j^{q_2-q_1}-1)\).
For any positive integer \(t\), \(c_j^t-1\) is a nonzero algebraic integer.
Its local term is bounded by \(D^{-1}\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(c_j^t-1)|\le t h_{\rm abs}(c_j)+\log 2\), by multiplying \(|\sigma(c_j)^t-1|\le2\max(1,|\sigma(c_j)|)^t\) over the embeddings of \(K\).
Hence for sufficiently large \(L\), the separation \(q_2-q_1\) is at least \(\epsilon L/(2 e_1e_2D h_{\rm abs}(c_j))\).
The interval has length at most \(L\), and the number of triples is bounded independently of \(p\), proving \(O_{P_1,P_2,\epsilon}(1)\) exceptions per \(p\).
With \(q\) fixed, subtract at two distinct choices of \(p\) and use \(a_i^{|p_2-p_1|}-1\) in the same norm bound.
This gives the second count in (4).
The [prime count at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) shows that both prime sets have size tending to infinity, so the relative exceptional count tends to zero.

<a id="width"></a>
## A simultaneous finite comparison on a set of relative density one

For the fixed seeds let \(k\) be the smaller of their two torsion-quotient class sizes, and take \(G\) from the [weighted norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#size).
That estimate gives \(\log B_0\ge G/(2k)-o_{P_1,P_2}(L)\) throughout this rectangle, where \(p<q\le2p\) and \(\Sigma\asymp L\).
The [lower bound for \(G\)](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#width) gives \(G/(2k)\ge cL\) for a constant \(c>0\) depending on the fixed seeds.
Since \(B_0\mid |C|^3\), the loss \(\log B_0-\log B_0^*\) is at most three times the sum of the left side of (3) and the two terms from (4).
For every fixed integer \(j\ge2\), choose \(6\epsilon<c/(2j)\).
Outside the exceptions in (4), absorbing the two \(o(L)\) errors gives \(\log B_0^*\ge(1-1/j)G/(2k)\) for all sufficiently large \(L\).

At each scale list the monic irreducible nontorsion reciprocal unit polynomials of degree at most \(j\) and multiplicative Mahler measure at most \(Q^3\), with \(Q=(1091/1000)^{1/3}\), as in the [finite seed enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width).
Call a prime pair good for \(j\) if for all ordered seeds in this list the powered lists have full degrees and are disjoint and
\[
                      (B_0^*)^{2kj}\ge \exp(G)^{j-1}.                 \tag{5}
\]
The quantities \(B_0,k,\exp(G)\) are computable by the [integer and real algebraic tests](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#width) and (3) of [the local divisor construction](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local).
Removing the rational prime powers indicated by \(X(L),p,q\) is an exact integer operation.
Finiteness of the list at fixed \(j\) and the estimates just proved imply that the fraction good for that \(j\) tends to one.
Take \(b(L)\) to be the largest integer \(2\le j\le L\) for which at least \(1-1/j\) of the rectangle is good, and \(\mathcal G_L\) its good pairs.
If the rectangle is empty or there is no passing integer, take \(b(L)=1\) and \(\mathcal G_L=\varnothing\).
Each fixed \(j\) eventually passes, so \(b(L)\to\infty\) and the relative size of \(\mathcal G_L\) tends to one.

## The surviving divisor and its residue root for each pair of contacts

For every row and choice of seeds in the statement, the [two contact constraints](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) give \(e_1+e_2\le b\) and \(ph_1+qh_2\le\eta\Sigma\), with \(h_i=\log M(P_i)\).
Thus both seeds occur in the width list, because \(q\le2p\) and each \(h_i\le3\eta<3\log Q\).
The fixed choice satisfies \(B_0^*\mid B^*\) and (5) at \(j=b\).
For the seed of smaller logarithmic measure \(h\le\eta\), let \(d\) be its degree divided by its torsion-quotient class size.
The [compressed degree and height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#width) gives \(G/(2k)\ge\Sigma dh/2\) and \(dh>\eta\exp(T_\eta)\).
It proves (1) even with \(B_0^*\) on the left in place of \(B^*\).
The [large-branch asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v502.md#inversion) gives (2) and both divergences.

In particular \(B_0^*>1\).
For each prime divisor \(\ell\) of this integer, the [exact local description of \(B_0\)](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local) gives a prime ideal above \(\ell\) and two labels whose condition in that description requires a positive rational valuation.
Write \(z=a_i^p,\ w=c_j^q,\ d_{ij}=z-w\), and use the integral valuation \(v\) there.
For unequal labels we have \(3v(d_{ij})>v(2)+3v(pq(a_i-c_j))\), in particular \(v(d_{ij})>v(a_i-c_j)\).
The two graph values are \(U(z)=-a_i V(z)\) and \(U(w)=-c_j V(w)\).
Reducing the second identity modulo the ideal generated by \(d_{ij}\) and comparing at \(z\) gives \((a_i-c_j)V(z)\equiv0\bmod d_{ij}\).
Hence \(v(V(z))>0\) and \(v(U(z))>0\).
For equal labels the positive condition gives \(v(d_{ij})>0\), and the [equal-label derivative calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local) puts both values in \((d_{ij})\).
In either case \(z,w\) are units with the same nonzero residue.
This residue is a common zero of the row reductions and of the two reduced monic powered minima of degrees \(e_1,e_2\), so its degree over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) is at most \(\min(e_1,e_2)\).

Finally every vanishing-cost sequence gives \(\lambda=0\) from the contact inequality.
Conversely, for any prescribed sequence of good pairs with scales tending to infinity, the [limit of the common-row minima](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) applies with two primes and \(b(L)/2\to\infty\).
If \(\lambda=0\) the normalized minima tend to zero and are eventually attained by actual rows, since the initial alternative has normalized cost \(\log 2\).
The existence of such sequences remains open.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed removing the small primes by Yu's theorem and the two exponent primes by spacing their exceptional partners before selecting the width.
A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin verified the estimates at ramified primes, the density and width quantifiers, and the common residue root for each contact choice.
