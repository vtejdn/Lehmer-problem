# Finite prime-ideal mass of translates across successive degree records

Under failure of the [unrestricted Lehmer gap](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract), the estimates below locate the norm of a near-integer translate among relative places and bound its overlap with the preceding field.

<a id="records"></a>
## Which primes carry the norm of a translate between adjacent records?

Suppose the unrestricted gap is zero.
Use [two adjacent strict degree records](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement), put \(A=A_i,\ B=A_{i+1},\ b=\log B,\ K=\mathbb Q(A),\ F=K(B)\), and write \(n=[F:K],\ D=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
For \(z=2-A^aB\) take the second closest allowed exponent from below.
The known norm calculation gives \(D^{-1}\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}z|\to\log 2\).
We ask where this mass can occur among the primes of \(F\).

For a prime ideal \(Q\) of \(F\) over \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\), let \(s_Q=[F_Q:K_{\mathfrak p}]=e_Q f_Q\), where both local indices are relative to \(K\).
Give \(Q\) weight \(w_Q=D^{-1}v_Q(z)\log NQ\), using integer ideal valuations and the absolute ideal norm.
For sufficiently late rows one has
\[
   \sum_Q w_Q\,\frac{s_Q-1}{n-1}\ \ll\ \sqrt{b\log(1/b)}. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, at any fixed \(\delta>0\) the primes with \(s_Q\ge\delta n\) carry \(o(1)\) mass, while the total mass tends to \(\log 2\).
Here the [relative degrees](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) tend to infinity.
The estimate weights each prime by its contribution to this particular translate.

To prove (1), take a normal closure of \(F/K\) and list the \(n\) relative images \(B_j\) of \(B\).
For \(j\ne k\), \(B_j/B_k\) is a nontorsion unit of absolute height at most \(2b\).
Indeed an embedding taking \(B_j\) to the strictly dominant \(B\) takes \(B_k\) to a different conjugate, and all conjugates of \(B\) have modulus at most \(B\).
Set \(z_j=2-A^aB_j\).
The common ideal \((z_j,z_k)\) divides \((B_j/B_k-1)\) since \(A,B_k\) are units.
The [cyclic norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) bounds its log norm divided by the absolute degree of the normal field by \(O(\sqrt{b\log(1/b)})\), uniformly in \(j,k,a\).
This is the same two-conjugate estimate used for the [window of translates](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v326.md#descent).

Average over ordered distinct embeddings \(j,k\).
At a fixed prime of the normal field above \(\mathfrak p\), exactly \(s_Q\) of the \(n\) embeddings induce the place \(Q\).
This follows by decomposing \(F\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak p}\) into its field factors and counting the embeddings of each factor.
When both embeddings induce \(Q\), their valuations of the translates are equal, so the common ideal retains their whole local contribution.
Given the first embedding, the fraction of other embeddings with the same place is \((s_Q-1)/(n-1)\).
Summing the normalized log norms over the primes upstairs and averaging the first embedding recovers \(w_Q\) by the local degree formula.
Pairs inducing different places contribute nonnegative terms.
This proves (1).

The estimate singles out a difficulty in seeking a contradiction by showing that translate norms are large.
Away from primes dividing the index of \(\mathcal O_K[B]\) in \(\mathcal O_F\), a prime dividing \(z\) already has \(f_Q=1\), since \(B\bmod Q=2A^{-a}\bmod\mathfrak p\) generates its residue field over that of \(K\).
Away also from the relative discriminant, such primes have \(s_Q=1\) and contribute zero to the left side of (1).
A comparison between different record rows would need information on these degree-one primes or on the exceptional prime contributions.
The upper bound (1) supplies neither a positive lower bound on the exceptional mass nor an exclusion of the one-sided record limit.

### How much ideal norm can earlier rows recover?

In row \(j\), write \(z_j=2-A_j^{a_j}A_{j+1}\) with the exponent chosen above.
Let \(K\) be the compositum of the splitting fields of any finite run of sufficiently late consecutive sources ending at source \(j\).
Write \(B=A_{j+1}\), \(n=[K(B):K]\), and choose a finite normal field \(E/\mathbb Q\) containing these fields and all images of \(B\).
For a nonzero algebraic integer \(x\) in \(E\), put \(H(x)=[E:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\log|N_{E/\mathbb Q}x|\).
For two such integers define \(g(x,y)\) by replacing the principal ideal in this formula with the ideal gcd, that is, by summing \(\min(v_Q(x),v_Q(y))\log NQ/[E:\mathbb Q]\) over the prime ideals of \(E\).
Both normalizations are invariant under enlarging \(E\).
For every nonzero \(x\in\mathcal O_K\) we obtain
\[
 g(x,z_j)^2\le H(x)\left(\frac{H(z_j)}{n}+O\!\left(\sqrt{m_{j+1}\log(1/m_{j+1})}\right)\right),
 \qquad m_{j+1}=\log B .
\]
The constant is absolute and independent of the length of the run.

Indeed, for each relative image \(B_k\) write \(y_k=2-A_j^{a_j}B_k\).
The two-conjugate calculation above gives \(g(y_k,y_l)\le\epsilon_j\) for \(k\ne l\), with \(\epsilon_j\ll\sqrt{m_{j+1}\log(1/m_{j+1})}\).
Give \((Q,t)\), with \(Q\) a prime ideal of \(E\) and \(t\ge1\) integral, mass \(\log NQ/[E:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(I_x(Q,t)\) indicate \(t\le v_Q(x)\).
Then \(\int I_x=H(x)\) and \(\int I_x I_y=g(x,y)\).
For \(u=n^{-1}\sum_k I_{y_k}\) we have \(\int u^2\le H(z_j)/n+(1-1/n)\epsilon_j\).
As \(x\) is fixed by \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\), each \(g(x,y_k)\) equals \(g(x,z_j)\).
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(\int I_xu\) gives the inequality.

In particular \(x=z_i\) is allowed in every later row \(j>i\), including \(j=i+1\) where the two translates use the same middle record.
The [relative block orbit](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) gives \(n\ge b_{j+1}\gg d_{j+1}/(\log(3d_{j+1}))^{212}\) even over all the preceding fields in the run.
Since \(H(z_i)\to\log 2\), all pairwise gcd masses for \(j>i\ge s\) are bounded by some \(\eta_s\to0\).
Choose integers \(R_s\to\infty\) so slowly that \(R_s\eta_s\to0\).
In a common normal field for rows \(s,\ldots,s+R_s-1\), assign each prime in their supports to one row with maximal valuation there.
In any row the mass at primes assigned elsewhere is bounded by the sum of its pairwise gcd masses, at most \(R_s\eta_s\).
This leaves pairwise coprime ideal divisors of the respective translates, each carrying normalized log norm \(\log 2+o(1)\), uniformly in the window.
Equivalently in this window, the sum of the absolute finite valuations of \(\prod_j z_j^{k_j}\), weighted by \(\log NQ/[E:\mathbb Q]\), is \((\log2+o(1))\sum_j|k_j|\) for arbitrary integer exponents.
For the lower bound use the triangle inequality on the primes assigned to each row, subtracting the other rows' lost masses with weights \(|k_j|\); the upper bound is the ordinary triangle inequality.
This extends the [single-row calculation for different exponents](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v326.md#descent) to translates of different records.
Projection to rational primes merges distinct ideals, so this argument leaves open how the corresponding rational norms overlap and how deeply they can be supported above any fixed prime.
