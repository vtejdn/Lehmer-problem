# Gaps for individual factors of specializations with a nonvanishing leading coefficient

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed two-variable polynomial

Let \(F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^R B_j(X)Y^j\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]\) be fixed, with \(R\ge1\) and \(B_R\ne0\).
For \(n\ge1\) write \(F_n(X)=F(X,X^n)\) and consider only nonzero \(F_n\).
There is \(c_F>0\) such that every irreducible integral factor \(A\) of \(F_n\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
                 m(A)\ge c_F/\log(2n).
\]
If \(B_R\) has no zero on the unit circle, there is a uniform positive lower bound \(m(A)\ge c_F'\) for all these factors and all \(n\).
This applies separately to every positive-measure factor, even when the rest of the specialization has much larger measure.
In particular, every positive-measure factor of either sign of the [reciprocal binary construction](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#binary) has a uniform positive gap as \(q=2^k\) varies.

More precisely, if \(A\) is monic with \(0<m(A)\to0\) along a sequence of these specializations, the leading coefficient must have a circle zero.
Each root \(\alpha\) of largest modulus of \(A\) then satisfies
\[
              \operatorname{dist}\bigl(\alpha,\{\xi:B_R(\xi)=0,\ |\xi|=1\}\bigr)
                         \ \le\ C_F\exp(-b_F/m(A))                   \tag{1}
\]
for fixed \(C_F,b_F>0\) and all sufficiently large indices.
If \(r(A)\) counts the roots of \(A\) outside the unit circle, a shrinking sequence therefore requires
\[
                        r(A)\ge c_F m(A)\exp(b_F/m(A)).
\]
In particular, for each fixed \(r_0\) the positive measures of all factors with at most \(r_0\) exterior roots have a uniform gap depending only on \(F,r_0\).
This includes every Salem factor of every nonzero specialization of the fixed \(F\), also when its leading coefficient has circle zeros.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The degree cost of one irreducible factor

An irreducible factor with absolute leading coefficient or constant coefficient at least two has \(m(A)\ge\log 2\), by the root formula applied also to the reciprocal.
An irreducible with zero constant coefficient is associated to \(X\) and has measure zero.
It therefore suffices to treat monic noncyclotomic units \(A\), with degree \(a>1\).
Write \(w=\max_j\deg B_j\), omitting zero coefficients from this maximum, and let \(s\) be the number of monomials of \(F\).
For \(n>w\), distinct monomials remain distinct and \(\deg F_n\le w+Rn\).
If \(F\) is a monomial there are no such factors.
Otherwise [Dobrowolski's divisor bound, Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible), applied to this particular \(A\mid F_n\), gives
\[
       M(A)\ge1+\frac{0.31\,a}{s!\deg F_n},\qquad
       m(A)\ge c_0(F)\,\frac{a}{n}.                               \tag{2}
\]
Here \(a\le\deg F_n\), so the logarithm changes the displayed increment by at most an absolute factor.
Small \(n\) produce only finitely many factors of the nonzero \(F_n\) and can be absorbed into the final constants.

<a id="house"></a>
## The root of largest modulus and the poles of the fiber

Let \(H=|\alpha|\) be the largest root modulus of \(A\), and write \(x=n\log H\).
The [house bound of Dimitrov](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives \(\log H\ge(\log2)/(4a)\).
If \(H\ge1+\eta\) for a fixed \(\eta>0\), then \(m(A)\ge\log(1+\eta)\).
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(\eta>0\) so that \(B_R\) has no zeros in \(1<|z|\le1+\eta\).
For \(1<H<1+\eta\) and \(\beta=\alpha^n\), the relation \(F(\alpha,\beta)=0\) and \(|\beta|\ge1\) imply
\[
          |B_R(\alpha)|\,|\beta|
              \le \sum_{j<R}|B_j(\alpha)|\le C_F .                  \tag{3}
\]
Let \(t\) be the number of roots of \(B_R\) on the unit circle counted with multiplicities.
Shrinking the annulus if needed, the other root factors are bounded away from zero there, giving \(|B_R(\alpha)|\ge c(H-1)^t\).
For \(t=0\), (3) bounds \(x\) by a constant.
Consequently \(a\gg_F n\) by the house bound, and (2) gives the positive gap.
For \(t>0\) and \(x\ge1\), the inequality \(H-1\ge x/n\ge1/n\) gives \(x\le t\log n+O_F(1)\) by (3).
The same upper estimate with \(O_F(\log(2n))\) includes \(x<1\).
It gives \(a\gg_F n/\log(2n)\) and proves the general measure estimate by (2).

If \(m(A)\to0\), then \(H\le M(A)\to1\).
By (2) and the house bound, \(x\ge(\log 2)n/(4a)\gg_F1/m(A)\).
From (3), \(|B_R(\alpha)|\le C_F e^{-x}\).
At every point of the annulus this leading coefficient has modulus at least a fixed positive constant times the \(t\)-th power of the distance to its set of circle zeros, by its factorization.
This proves (1) for every choice of a conjugate of largest modulus.
For a monic unit the root formula gives \(m(A)\le r(A)\log H\).
The left side of (1) is at least \(H-1\ge\log H\), so \(m(A)/r(A)\le C_F e^{-b_F/m(A)}\).
This gives the exterior-root count, and rules out a shrinking sequence at every bounded count.

<a id="binary"></a>
## Both signs of the binary construction

For the polynomials \(P_q^\pm\) in the [two reciprocal lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#binary), put
\[
 F^\pm(X,Y)=(X-1)^2\bigl[(Y+X+1)(X+Y+XY)\ \pm2XY\bigr]+2X^2(Y-1)^2.
\]
Then \(F^\pm(X,X^q)=X(X-1)^2P_q^\pm(X)\), by the geometric sum identity.
The coefficient of \(Y^2\) is \(B=X^3+X^2-X+1\) for both signs.
Its reciprocal is \(B^*=X^3-X^2+X+1\).
A circle zero of the real polynomial \(B\) would be common to \(B\) and \(B^*\), while \(B-B^*=2X(X-1)\) and \(B(0)B(1)\ne0\).
The uniform gap therefore holds for every positive-measure factor of either sign.
In particular the unbounded-degree irreducible reciprocal factors with few short binary periods from [the sign-change construction](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#irreducible) cannot have measures tending to one.
