# Comparison of conjugate label fields in solvable extensions

<a id="statement"></a>
## Equality of the label fields or descent to the block field

For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(z\), let \(L_z\) be its rational splitting field, \(\gamma_z\) its [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels), and \(d_z=[\mathbb Q(\gamma_z):\mathbb Q]\).
Inside \(L_z\) let \(P_z\) be the fixed field of the kernel of the permutation action on the multiplicative labels of the conjugates of \(\gamma_z\).
Let \(E_z\) be the field fixed in \(P_z\) by the stabilizer of one label.
The \(\mathbb Q\)-embeddings of \(E_z\) into \(P_z\) correspond to all labels.
For a compressed reciprocal unit, \(P_z\) is the splitting field of its trace \(z+z^{-1}\), and one can take \(E_z=\mathbb Q(z+z^{-1})\).

Fix \(M,N>0\) and suppose \(0<m(x)\le M,\ 0<m(y)\le N\).
Assume that some finite normal extension of \(\mathbb Q\) containing \(L_x,L_y\) is solvable over \(L_x\).
Let \(d_x,d_y\) be sufficiently large in terms of \(M,N\), respectively.
Write \(b_x,u_x\) for the size and number of [minimal blocks of labels](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) of \(x\).
Let \(B_x\) be the field in \(P_x\) fixed by the setwise stabilizer of one such block and \(\widetilde B_x\) its normal closure.
Then exactly one of the following alternatives holds.

1. \(P_y=P_x\), the two label actions are isomorphic as Galois sets, and after a matching of labels \(E_y=E_x\) inside this common field.
   The matched conjugates of \(\gamma_x,\gamma_y\) are multiplicatively dependent, so a nonzero integral power of a conjugate of \(y\) equals a nonzero integral power of a conjugate of \(x\) up to a root of unity.
   In particular \(b_y=b_x,\ u_y=u_x\), and \(d_y/d_x\in\{1/2,1,2\}\).
2. \(P_y\subseteq\widetilde B_x\subsetneq P_x\), \(b_y\le u_x\), and
   \[
                d_y\ll_N u_x(\log(3u_x))^{212}
                 \ll_{M,N}(\log(3d_x))^{212}(\log\log(16d_x))^{212}.     \tag{1}
   \]

Consequently for primitive label action on \(x\), every bounded-measure \(y\) in the stipulated extensions of sufficiently large compressed degree has the very same label field and matched label stabilizer field.
For compressed reciprocal units this identifies the two trace fields up to conjugacy.
These statements allow the solvable extension and its degree to vary.
They apply in particular to elements of towers of radical adjunctions above \(L_x\), since the finitely many conjugate radicals and roots of unity in the tower lie in a normal extension solvable over \(L_x\).

<a id="quotient"></a>
## The common nonabelian quotient

Let \(J\) be the finite normal extension in the hypothesis.
The kernel of the action on the labels of \(x\) inside \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_x/\mathbb Q)\) is solvable, including the original torsion-quotient fibers, by the [two-kernel argument](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal).
Hence \(\operatorname{Gal}(J/P_x)\) is solvable.
Its image in \(G_y=\operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q)\) is a solvable normal subgroup.
The [trivial radical of the label action](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#statement) makes that image trivial.
Thus \(P_y\subseteq P_x\), with restriction map \(G_x\to G_y\), where \(G_x=\operatorname{Gal}(P_x/\mathbb Q)\).

The [unique minimal normal subgroup](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) of \(G_x\) is \(D_x=(A_{b_x})^{u_x}\), with independent natural factors on the blocks.
If the restriction kernel is nontrivial, it contains \(D_x\).
The kernel \(K_x\) of the permutation action on the blocks satisfies \(K_x/D_x\le(C_2)^{u_x}\), by taking the parity of each block permutation.
Its image in \(G_y\) is therefore abelian and normal, hence trivial.
This gives \(P_y\subseteq P_x^{K_x}=\widetilde B_x\).
The containment of \(\widetilde B_x\) in \(P_x\) is strict because \(K_x\) contains a nontrivial alternating factor.
The group of \(\widetilde B_x\) is a subgroup of \(S_{u_x}\).
Since \(A_{b_y}\) occurs in its quotient \(G_y\), orders give \(b_y\le u_x\) for large \(b_y\).
The [two-coordinate block bound](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) now gives \(d_y\ll_N u_x(\log(3d_y))^{212}\).
For large \(d_y\) it first gives \(u_x\gg_N\sqrt{d_y}\), and substituting this inside the logarithm gives the first inequality in (1).
The second follows from \(u_x\ll_M(\log(3d_x))^{212}\).
For \(u_x=1\) the normal closure of \(B_x\) is rational, excluding this alternative at large \(d_y\).

<a id="letters"></a>
## Recovering the matched labels when the fields agree

If restriction is injective, \(P_x=P_y\).
The common group \(G\) has a unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup \(D\).
In both actions \(D\) is a direct product of simple alternating groups, naturally supported on single blocks.
Its individual simple factors are precisely the minimal normal subgroups of \(D\).
Consequently the factor counts agree and the orders of the simple factors give equal \(b_x=b_y\), denoted by \(b\).
Use \(b\ge9\), as follows from the same uniform block bound.
For each factor, the two natural actions have the same collection of point stabilizers inside that factor, since every automorphism of \(A_b\) in these degrees comes from a permutation of its letters, as shown in the [triple-support argument](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition).
Including the other factors of \(D\) in a point stabilizer now gives exactly the same collection of subgroups of \(D\) for the two complete label sets.
Each such subgroup specifies a unique label: in the moving factor natural point stabilizers are distinct, and that factor is distinguished by its proper intersection.
Identification by equal stabilizers in \(D\) is therefore a bijection.
It commutes with \(G\), since conjugation transports the stabilizers in \(D\).
Taking fixed fields of corresponding stabilizers in \(G\) gives \(E_x=E_y\).
Finally \(d_z=w_z b_z u_z\) with \(w_z=1\) or \(2\), which gives the degree ratio.

Choose one actual root at each of two matched labels.
The field of the selected compressed root has degree \(w_z\le2\) over its label stabilizer field, because the root stabilizer has index exactly the size of its orbit under the label stabilizer.
The matched roots therefore have joint absolute degree \(J\le w_xw_y v\le4v\), with \(v=b_xu_x=b_yu_y\).
Their heights are at most \(M/(w_x v)\) and \(N/(w_y v)\).
If they were multiplicatively independent, the [two-coordinate theorem](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) would give
\[
                 MN\gg v/(\log(3v))^{212},
\]
which is impossible for large \(v\) in terms of \(M,N\).
The degree thresholds in the comparison can be increased in terms of the two fixed bounds (equivalently insist both degrees exceed their own thresholds, using \(MN\le\max(M^2,N^2)\) and comparable degrees in this case).
Each class product is a power of a representative times torsion, proving the assertion for the original numbers.
