# Uniform escape rates under Mahler iteration throughout the group of a strict degree record

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed, doubling and large-growth cases

Let \(a\) be a reciprocal strict degree record with bounded positive total log measure and sufficiently large degree in terms of that bound.
Use its \(u\) canonical blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs, so \(d=2ub\), and increase the threshold to have \(b\ge9\).
For any nontorsion \(x\in D(a)\) write \(L_j=\log M^j(x)\) for \(j\ge1\), where \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure and the superscript denotes iteration.
Let \(C=(b-1)/4\).

* If the record is real Salem up to sign, conjugation and inversion, every first measure \(M(x)\) is already fixed.
* If it has the [one exceptional oriented block with \(M(a)=r^2\)](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal), then \(L_{j+1}\ge2L_j\) at every step \(j\ge1\).
  On any step where the input iterate is off the rational lines of the original conjugates the stronger bound is \(L_{j+1}\ge C L_j\).
  If a coordinate line is ever reached after the first measure, all successive log measures double exactly.
* In every other case,
  \[
                        L_{j+2}\ge C L_j\qquad(j\ge1).                    \tag{1}
  \]
  If the record has no real conjugate, \(L_{j+1}\ge C L_j\) for every \(j\ge1\).

There are no restrictions on the original degree or height of \(x\).
In the last case the lower bound on the per-iteration growth rate of the logarithms, \(\liminf_{j\to\infty}L_j^{1/j}\ge\sqrt C\), tends to infinity with the degree of the source record.
For the nonreal-signature case one can use \(C\) instead of \(\sqrt C\).
In the square case the lower growth rate \(2\) is attained by the record itself.
These are orbit statements about any one such record; they do not lower-bound its first positive measure uniformly.

There is a version on the entire [rational field \(U_1\) defined by nonabelian chief-factor ranks](Yin_C_G0901_v107.md#statement), without any record hypothesis.
For every nontorsion unit \(w\in U_1\) of sufficiently small positive measure let \(a\) be its torsion-class product of degree \(d\).
Either \(a\) is signed Salem up to conjugation and inversion and every first measure in \(D(w)\) is fixed, or
\[
       m(M(w))m(w)\gg \frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{212}},\qquad
             L_{j+2}\ge \frac{d/2-1}{4} L_j\quad(j\ge1)                 \tag{4}
\]
for every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), with \(L_j=\log M^j(x)\) as above.
The square pattern does not enter this alternative.

<a id="projection"></a>
## A rank inequality for the largest absolute log modulus

For any nontorsion algebraic unit \(y\) let \(D=\deg y\), \(t=\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_y\), and \(P\) be the real orthogonal projection onto the space of evaluations of real linear functionals on \(V_y\) at all \(D\) conjugate classes.
This subspace in \(\mathbb R^D\) has dimension \(t\) since those classes span, and the embedding permutation action preserves the subspace.
By transitivity its projection has diagonal \(P_{ii}=t/D\); the Gram inequality for \(Pe_i,Pe_j\) gives \(|P_{ij}|\le t/D\).
This uses the [conjugate-frame dimension calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v64.md#degree) and permits repeated classes among the actual embeddings.
The vector \(f=(\log|y_1|,\ldots,\log|y_D|)\) of all log moduli is in this space, by linearity of log modulus on classes, so \(f=Pf\).
The log moduli sum to zero for a unit and \(\sum_i|f_i|=2m(y)\).
Therefore
\[
             m(y)\ \ge\ \frac{D}{2t}\max_i|\log|y_i|| .                 \tag{2}
\]
At an iterate \(y=M^j(x)>1\) the right side is at least \(D\log(y)/(2t)\).

In the [irreducible record representation](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) we have \(t=ub\) or \(u(b-1)\) for every nonzero class in \(D(a)\).
An off-coordinate class has orbit size at least \(u b(b-1)/2\) by the [signed and oriented orbit count](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#orbit); the degree of the actual number is at least this size.
Equation (2) gives \(m(y)\ge C\log y\) at any such positive iterate.

<a id="coordinate"></a>
## The next iterate after a coordinate measure

Every measure value \(y>1\) of a unit is a real Perron unit, with all distinct other conjugates strictly smaller in modulus, by the [exterior-product argument](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field).
If \([y]=t_0[a']\) for a nonzero rational \(t_0\) and a conjugate \(a'\) of the source, complex conjugation fixes \([a']\).
The record is compressed, so \(a'\) is real.
The stabilizer of \([y]\) fixes \(y\) itself by the strict Perron property and fixes \(a'\) by compression.
Conversely fixing \(a'\) fixes \([y]\), hence \(y\); thus their root fields and degrees coincide.
Homogeneity of the absolute height on rational multiplicative classes now gives
\[
           \log y=t_0\log|a'|,\qquad m(y)=|t_0|m(a),\qquad
                [M(y)]=|t_0|[M(a)].                                    \tag{3}
\]
The last equality follows as an equality of classes of positive real numbers from \(\log M(y)=|t_0|\log M(a)\).
Also \(|\log|a'||=\log H\), where \(H=\max_\sigma|\sigma a|\).
Indeed the conjugate moduli of \(y\) are the \(|t_0|\)-th powers of the conjugate moduli of \(a\), using reciprocity to reverse negative exponents, and \(y\) is their largest.

If \([M(a)]\) is a coordinate, the [strict-record exterior-product classification](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#records) gives \(M(a)=H\) at a real Salem record, or \(M(a)=H^2\) in the stated square pattern.
The [Salem orbit result](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#iteration) makes every first measure fixed in the former case.
In the latter case (3) shows that the iterate after any positive coordinate measure is again a coordinate and satisfies \(m(y)=2\log y\).
All off-coordinate steps expand the logarithm by at least \(C\ge2\).
For \(x=a\) the first measure is a coordinate and exact doubling follows.

If \([M(a)]\) is not a coordinate, (3) forces the successor of every positive coordinate iterate off all coordinate lines.
Thus of two consecutive input iterates at least one gives expansion by at least \(C\) and the other by at least one, since \(M(y)\ge y\) at \(y>1\).
This proves (1).
With no real conjugate there are no positive Perron coordinate iterates and the stronger one-step bound follows.
The growth-rate conclusions result by iteration from \(L_1,L_2>0\) and the divergence of \(b\) at large record degree. [Block size](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement)

<a id="primitive"></a>
## All sufficiently small inputs at the first universal field level

Compression gives \(D(w)=D(a)\), \(M(w)=M(a)\), and \(a\) is reciprocal at small positive measure by [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
Its degree tends uniformly to infinity by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
By the [field membership test](Yin_C_G0901_v107.md#level) it has a single block of size \(b=d/2\).
In the independent alternative the signed module is already irreducible.
In the oriented case the half product has degree at most two and no greater positive total measure if nontorsion.
Bounded-degree finiteness therefore makes that product torsion for all sufficiently small \(m(w)\).
The module is then the full sum-zero space, and the [oriented quadratic field test](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#gap) makes a real conjugate impossible in this alternative at \(m(w)<\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\).
Indeed the quadratic orientation field lies in the root field at the chosen embedding; a real root would make it totally real with its real conjugation different from the involution interchanging halves.

If \(M(a)\) is on a coordinate line, there must be a real conjugate, so the independent case applies.
The exterior coefficients then have just one nonzero entry and \(a\) is signed Salem up to conjugation and inversion.
Equivalently \(D(w)\) has the same group as a real Salem generator, and radial rank one with that fixed measure makes every first value fixed. [Common iteration](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#statement)
If \(M(a)\) is off every coordinate line, the two module descriptions satisfy the replacement hypotheses of the [mixed-measure product bound](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#statement) for this single input.
It gives (4)'s first estimate directly for \(x=M(a)\).
The geometric orbit count, the equal-field argument (3) for compressed coordinates, and the two-step argument above only use those same two module descriptions, so they give the second estimate on all of \(D(w)\).
