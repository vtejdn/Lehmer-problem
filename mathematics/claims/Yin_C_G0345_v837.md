# Recovering the smallest rational factor degree from two reductions of a steep section

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound from any two primes and equality for some pair

For the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) take positive primitive directions with \(k\to\infty\) and \(l/k\to\infty\).
Write \(n=l+2k\), \(P(X)=X^n Q(X+X^{-1})\), \(W=\max(16,2n)\), and put
\[
 B=\max\left(1,\left\lfloor\frac{l-4k}{8\log 27}
              \left(\frac{\log\log W}{\log W}\right)^3\right\rfloor\right).
\]
At a prime \(p\) where the monic trace polynomial \(Q\) has squarefree reduction, let \(D_p\) be the set of subset sums of the degrees of its irreducible factors modulo \(p\), including zero and \(n\).
For a nonempty tuple \(\mathbf p\) of such primes set
\[
       b(\mathbf p)=\min\Bigl(\{n\}\ \cup\
       \bigl([B,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]\cap\mathbb Z\cap\bigcap_{p\in\mathbf p}D_p\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
If \(m\) is the smallest degree of an irreducible rational factor of \(Q\), then \(b(\mathbf p)\le m\).
At every sufficiently late direction,
\[
                     m=\max_{p\ne q} b(p,q).                                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(Q\) has \(r\) rational factors, ordered pairs attaining equality have prime-tuple density at least \((2n)^{-2r}\).
This bound also holds relative to pairs with both primes congruent to one modulo any prescribed integer \(a\ge1\).
For each fixed section and modulus, densities here first let the common prime bound tend to infinity, drawing primes independently and discarding repetitions.

The smallest log Mahler measure of a rational irreducible factor of \(P\) is \((C+o(1))m/n\), where \(C=\log(\beta_3\beta_4)>0\) is the [single-root height constant](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement).
Thus an infinite sequence on this curve has a factor with log measure tending to zero exactly when the maximum in (1) is \(o(n)\).
A lower bound on \(b(p,q)/n\) at a chosen pair yields a corresponding factorwise lower bound without factoring either \(Q\) over the rationals or its discriminant.

<a id="height"></a>
## An explicit cutoff for each single section

In fact \(m\ge B\) at every primitive direction \(l\ge k\).
Take any root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), write \(u=\alpha^k,v=\alpha^l\), and denote its absolute logarithmic Weil height by \(h(\alpha)\).
It is an algebraic unit and is [nontorsion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
The algebraic integer \(A(u)\) is nonzero, since at a root of \(A\) one has \(u-u^{-1}=3\) or \(4\) and the curve equation forces \(v=-(u-u^{-1}+3)(u-u^{-1}+4)\), a rational nonunit.
At each complex embedding,
\[
        |A(u)|\max(1,|v|)\le 27\max(1,|u|)^4.
\]
For \(|v|\le1\) this follows from the coefficient sum \(26\) of \(A\) in absolute value.
For \(|v|>1\), divide the curve equation by \(v\) and use the same coefficient sum for \(A^*\).
The nonzero integral norm of \(A(u)\) has absolute value at least one.
Averaging logarithms therefore gives \((l-4k)h(\alpha)\le\log27\).
If \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) has degree \(t\), then \(\deg\alpha\le2t\).
The [uniform degree form of Voutier's lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#radius) gives \((\deg\alpha)h(\alpha)>\tfrac14(\log\log W/\log W)^3\), including degrees below sixteen.
Combining these bounds proves the cutoff when \(l>4k\), and \(B=1\) suffices otherwise.
The reduction of each monic rational trace factor has the same degree and divides \(\bar Q\).
If \(Q\) is reducible, its smallest factor has degree at most \(n/2\), proving the asserted inequality for every prime tuple.

<a id="two-primes"></a>
## Two cycle patterns recover the degree exactly

Eventually the original factors are reciprocal and squarefree, and their trace factors have degrees \(n_1,\ldots,n_r\) summing to \(n\).
The [independent alternating actions](Yin_C_G0345_v806.md#statement) give \(\prod_i A_{n_i}\) inside the joint trace action, even over the maximal abelian extension.
Here \(B\gg n(\log\log n/\log n)^3\), so \(r\le n/B\), \(2r<B\) and \(\min n_i\ge6\) eventually.
Use two elements of the product, both acting by a full cycle on each odd block.
On an even block of size \(n_i\) prescribe cycle lengths \((1,n_i-1)\) for the first element and \((2,n_i-2)\) for the second.
All prescribed permutations are even.
At an unramified prime avoiding \(\operatorname{disc} Q\) the orbit lengths of Frobenius are the degrees of the reduced irreducibles, by lifting distinct residue roots.
The [Chebotarev density theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) realizes these patterns at distinct primes \(p,q\).

At \(p\) the short cycles account for at most \(r<B\) letters, so a degree at least \(B\) uses a long cycle of length at least \(m-1\).
For odd \(m\), every even block has size at least \(m+1\) and all long cycles have length at least \(m\).
For even \(m\), the only smaller value still to exclude is the odd integer \(m-1\).
At \(q\) every cycle on an even block has even length, while any cycle on an odd block has length at least \(m+1\), excluding this sum.
Since the true degree \(m\) occurs at both primes, this proves (1).

In \(\prod_i S_{n_i}\), the proportions of the prescribed cycle types on an odd block and on the two kinds of even block are \(1/n_i,\ 1/(n_i-1),\ 1/[2(n_i-2)]\), respectively.
Each full product of such classes belongs to \(\prod_i A_{n_i}\), so its proportion in the actual trace group is at least its proportion in the full symmetric product.
Conditioning a prime to split in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_a)\) replaces the trace group by the image fixing its intersection with that cyclotomic field, which still contains the same alternating product.
Each pattern thus has relative density at least \((2n)^{-r}\).
Independence in the limit follows by multiplying the finite counts of primes for each class, with only a vanishing diagonal from repeated primes.
Finally each original factor has degree \(2n_i\) and its log measure equals \(2n_i h(\alpha_i)\) for any root.
The uniform single-root height law gives \(lh(\alpha_i)\to C/2\) and \(n/l\to1\), proving the Mahler-measure assertion.

<a id="four-primes"></a>
## Four-prime certificates on irreducible sections

If \(P\) is irreducible at one of these sufficiently late directions, then four independently drawn squarefree-reduction primes satisfy \(b(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)=n\) with limiting density \(1-O(B^{-c})\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
This also holds relative to arbitrary prescribed invertible residue classes for the four primes, with their moduli allowed to depend on the section.
For a reducible section no tuple can give \(n\).
Consequently along any steep sequence of irreducible sections the four-prime success densities tend to one.

Indeed in the irreducible case the trace action is \(A_n\) or \(S_n\).
Chebotarev in its compositum with the specified cyclotomic field gives a uniform element in a coset of a subgroup containing \(A_n\) at each prescribed residue class, for conjugacy-stable tests.
This follows because the fiber over a fixed cyclotomic automorphism projects onto the appropriate trace coset with all fibers of equal size.
The four independent choices have joint probabilities bounded above by sixteen times the probabilities for four unrestricted uniform elements of \(S_n\).
For four such permutations the [common invariant-size estimate of Eberhard, Ford and Green](../audits/literature/EFG17/Yin_U001.md#efg17-u001), summed over dyadic intervals, bounds by \(O(B^{-c})\) the probability of a common member of \([B,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]\).
Outside this event the minimum defining \(b\) equals \(n\), proving the assertion.
These densities concern the auxiliary primes for a fixed input and give no estimate for how many directions are irreducible or for the largest prime needed at a given direction.
