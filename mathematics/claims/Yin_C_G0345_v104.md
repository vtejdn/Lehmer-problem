# Cyclotomic rational limits of parents with bounded shifted Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Limits in the fixed coefficient positions

Let \(A_i\) be nonconstant monic integer polynomials with all roots on the unit circle.
Write \(B_i=A_i/A_i(0)\), so \(B_i(0)=1\) and \(A_i(0)=\pm1\).
For integers \(k_i\ge0\), integers \(g_i\ge1\) and complex \(a_i\) with \(|a_i|\ge1\), suppose
\[
                    m\bigl(A_i(X^{g_i})+a_i X^{k_i}\bigr)
                        \quad\text{is bounded},\qquad m=\log M.
\]
Every subsequence of the undilated \(B_i\) has a further subsequence converging locally uniformly in the unit disk.
Every coefficientwise limit is a finite signed binomial product
\[
                       R(X)=\prod_{u\ge1}(1-X^u)^{b_u},
                          \qquad b_u\in\mathbb Z ,
                          \quad b_u=0\text{ for all but finitely many }u.
\]
Conversely every such \(R\) occurs as a limit of normalized nonconstant parents with bounded shifted measures at \(|a_i|=1\).
The bound in this realization may depend on \(R\).

Let \(b_{i,u}\) denote the signed binomial exponents of \(B_i\).
Along a coefficientwise convergent subsequence, these exponents are eventually equal to \(b_u\) at each fixed \(u\).
Thus if their supports increase by inclusion and \(\sum_u|b_{i,u}|\to\infty\), the shifted measures tend to infinity, uniformly over the dilations \(g_i\), shift positions \(k_i\) and scalars \(|a_i|\ge1\) for this sequence of parents.
For nonnegative \(b_{i,u}\) there is also a conclusion for moving supports.
If for some integer \(g\ge1\) all \(b_{i,jg}\ge1\) for \(1\le j\le N\), then the shifted logarithmic measure is at least \(c\log\log N-C\) for large \(N\), with absolute \(c>0,\ C\), independently of \(g\) and the other exponents.

<a id="disk"></a>
## Bounded characteristic of the limiting parent

The coordinator proposed applying Cantor's theorem directly to the undilated parent and testing positive exponents at a common multiple.
The [previous rational-prefix argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v2.md#rational-prefix) obtains bounded characteristic from signed trace residues and imposes a shrinking measure condition for a zero-free limit.
Here bounded shifted measure controls the logarithm of a polynomial already zero-free in the open disk.

Integrate in normalized angular measure, and let \(L_i(\theta)=\log|B_i(e^{i\theta})|\).
The mean of \(L_i\) is zero, its two parts have equal integrals, and composing its argument with \(\theta\mapsto g_i\theta\) preserves its distribution.
The [circle-root integral estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) therefore gives
\[
           m\bigl(A_i(X^{g_i})+a_iX^{k_i}\bigr)
                  \ge\tfrac12\|L_i\|_1-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2).
\]
This comparison needs no divisibility relation between \(g_i\) and \(k_i\).
Consequently \(\|L_i\|_1\le C_1\) with \(C_1\) fixed for the sequence.
The [Poisson formula for circle-root polynomials](Yin_C_G0345_v101.md#poisson) gives \(\log|B_i(\rho e^{i\theta})|=(P_\rho*L_i)(\theta)\).
For \(0<\rho<1\), positivity and \(\sup P_\rho\le(1+\rho)/(1-\rho)\) bound both \(B_i\) and \(1/B_i\) in modulus on the closed disk of radius \(\rho\) by \(\exp(C_1(1+\rho)/(1-\rho))\).
The same formula yields
\[
              \int \log^+ |B_i(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,d\nu(\theta)
                         \le \int L_i^+\,d\nu\le C_1/2
\]
because \((P_\rho*L_i)^+\le P_\rho*L_i^+\).

Normal-family compactness gives a subsequential holomorphic limit \(R\) throughout the open disk.
The reciprocal bounds keep it zero-free, and its constant coefficient is one.
By Cauchy's formula every Taylor coefficient is the limit of integers and is eventually constant on that subsequence.
At each fixed radius the last integral passes to the limit by uniform convergence, so \(R\) satisfies the [holomorphic criterion for bounded characteristic](../audits/literature/Can63/Yin_U001.md#bounded-characteristic).
Consequently [Cantor's Theorem 1](../audits/literature/Can63/Yin_U001.md#cantor-rationality) makes \(R\) rational.
If a subsequence initially converges coefficientwise, any locally uniform sublimit has exactly those coefficients, so this conclusion applies to its coefficientwise limit as well.

<a id="classification"></a>
## Which rational functions occur

A rational function analytic in the disk with integral Taylor series has only roots of unity as finite poles, by the [integral shift-lattice argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v2.md#cyclotomic-prefix).
In that argument the shifts of the integral coefficient sequence span a finite-dimensional rational vector space, since its Hankel matrix has finite rank.
Evaluation at finitely many coordinates embeds the lattice of integral sequences in this space into a finite-rank integer lattice.
The shift acts integrally on it, and its spectral radius is at most one by the Taylor coefficient bounds on smaller disks.
Its nonzero eigenvalues are roots of unity by Kronecker, as all their conjugates occur among the eigenvalues.
The resolvent of the shift supplies a rational denominator whose zeros are reciprocals of these eigenvalues.
Thus the reduced rational denominator over \(\mathbb Q\) is a product of cyclotomics up to a constant.
Apply the result both to \(R\) and to \(1/R\), which is analytic with integral Taylor coefficients since \(R(0)=1\).
It follows that \(R\) is a quotient of cyclotomic products normalized at zero.
The Möbius formula gives the stated finite signed binomial product, with sign fixed by its constant coefficient.

The normalized \(B_i\) also have such presentations by Kronecker and the Möbius formula.
In a normalized product, once the exponents below \(u\) are known, the coefficient at degree \(u\) is the corresponding coefficient of the lower product minus \(b_u\).
All higher factors are \(1\) modulo \(X^{u+1}\).
Induction proves uniqueness of the signed exponents and their eventual agreement at each fixed index under coefficientwise convergence.

Conversely, in the finite product for \(R\), replace each copy of \((1-X^u)^{-1}\) by
\[
                         1+X^u+\cdots+X^{(t-1)u}
                                 =\frac{1-X^{ut}}{1-X^u}
\]
as \(t\to\infty\), retaining the positive powers.
The resulting normalized polynomials converge locally uniformly to \(R\) and have all roots on the circle.
Their leading coefficients are \(\pm1\), so they are normalized forms of monic integer polynomials.
For the empty product \(R=1\), use \(1-X^t\).
The \(L^1\) norm of \(\log|1-e^{iv\theta}|\) is a fixed finite constant for all \(v\ge1\) by angular dilation.
Hence the boundary logarithms of the approximants have bounded \(L^1\) norm in terms of the fixed exponents of \(R\), since every replacement uses only two such logarithms.
The upper circle-root integral bound gives uniformly bounded shifted measures for \(|a_i|=1\), with arbitrary \(g_i,k_i\).

<a id="nested"></a>
## Nested binomial supports

Suppose the binomial supports of the undilated parents increase by inclusion and \(\sum_u|b_{i,u}|\to\infty\).
A bounded shifted-measure subsequence for any choice of dilations, offsets and scalars would have a sublimit of the preceding form.
If the union of the supports is infinite, each index in that union has a nonzero exponent eventually and hence a nonzero limiting exponent.
This contradicts the finite support of the limit.
If the union is finite, stabilization at its finitely many indices bounds the total exponent mass on the subsequence, again a contradiction.
This proves the asserted divergence and includes successively adjoining positive binomial factors with arbitrary repetitions.

Nesting of the cyclotomic divisor sets alone has a different consequence.
For example \(\prod_{n\mid 2^j}\Phi_n=X^{2^j}-1\) has increasing cyclotomic divisor sets, but its normalized binomial support is just \(\{2^j\}\).
Its unit-amplitude shifts have bounded logarithmic measure by the same circle integral estimate.
The coefficient limit alone allows nonzero exponents to move to infinity.

<a id="progression"></a>
## Positive exponents on a homogeneous progression

For one normalized parent \(B=\prod_u(1-X^u)^{b_u}\) write \(L=\log|B|\) on the circle.
By the [Fourier coefficient formula](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity), for each integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
                  -\widehat L(m)=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{u\mid m} b_u u .
\]
When all \(b_u\ge0\), any selected divisors give a lower bound on \(\|L\|_1\) through this identity.
Suppose \(b_{gj}\ge1\) for \(1\le j\le N\).
Choose \(y=\lfloor\sqrt{\log N}\rfloor\) and \(D=\prod_{p\le y}p\).
For all large \(N\), \(D\le y^y\le N\).
At \(m=gD\) every \(g j\) with \(j\mid D\) divides \(m\) and has coefficient at least one.
Therefore
\[
                 \|L\|_1\ge-\widehat L(gD)
                     \ge\frac12\sum_{j\mid D}\frac{j}{D}
                     =\frac12\prod_{p\le y}(1+1/p)
                     \ge\frac14\sum_{j\le y}\frac1j .
\]
For the last inequality, the finite Euler product \(\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p)^{-1}\) includes every term \(1/j\) with \(j\le y\), while \(\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p^2)\ge\prod_{j\ge2}(1-1/j^2)=1/2\).
Since \(\log y=\tfrac12\log\log N+O(1)\), the lower circle-root estimate proves the claimed quantitative divergence uniformly over all the other nonnegative exponents, the step \(g\), any further common dilation and every allowed shift.
For signed exponents the same Fourier coefficient contains contributions of both signs, so the progression bound uses positivity.
The preceding results do not give a uniform gap for arbitrary moving binomial supports.
