# Torsion translates of roots in critical six-term sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Power maps and separation between sections

Fix positive constants \(C,K\) and use the [critical six-term hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement): \(s\to\infty\), \(a,b\) are relatively prime positive integers, \(a\ge2\), \(s\le b/a\le Cs^3\), and \(P\) is a monic nontorsion irreducible rational factor of \(F_{s,a,b}(X)=g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a)\) with at most \(Ka\) exterior roots.
Here \(g_s(T)=T^s-T-1\) and \(B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\).
For all sufficiently large \(s\), if \(x\) is a root of \(P\), the only root of the entire parent \(F_{s,a,b}\) in \(x\mu_\infty\) is \(x\) itself, where \(\mu_\infty\) denotes all roots of unity.
Consequently \(\mathbb Q(x^n)=\mathbb Q(x)\) simultaneously for every nonzero integer \(n\).

More generally, fix \(0<\delta<3\).
Suppose \(3\le s'\le s^{3-\delta}\), \(b'=b+ta>0\) and \(|t|\le s^{3-\delta}\) with \(t\) integral.
For all sufficiently large \(s\), if \(F_{s',a,b'}(\xi x)=0\) for a root of unity \(\xi\), then \((s',b',\xi)=(s,b,1)\).
This separates distinct seed degrees and nearby slopes within one residue class of the long exponent, including roots of the second section with unrestricted exterior count.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Elimination over the cyclotomic field

Put \(y=x^a\).
The [critical height and degree estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) give \(h_{\rm abs}(y)=a\log M(P)/\deg P\ll_{C,K}s^{-3}\).
For every \(\eta>0\) the [rank-one relative height bound](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002) therefore implies
\[
 [L(y):L]\gg_{C,K,\eta}s^{3-\eta}
\]
uniformly over all finite abelian number fields \(L/\mathbb Q\), by choosing a sufficiently small fixed positive exponent in the cited theorem.
The unit \(y\) is nontorsion because \(x\) is nontorsion.

Write \(R_j=-g_j/B_j\) and set \(L=\mathbb Q(\xi)\), \(c=\xi^a\) and \(e=\xi^{b'}\).
The denominators are nonzero at the two section roots: a common nonzero zero of \(g_j,B_j\) has \(T^j=1+T\) and hence \(0=T B_j(T)=-1-T-T^2\), which is impossible for a nontorsion argument.
The two section equations imply
\[
 R_{s'}(cy)=e\,y^t R_s(y).
\]
If the corresponding rational functions of \(T\) differ, clearing both denominators and any negative power produces a nonzero polynomial over \(L\) of degree at most \(s+s'+|t|\) vanishing at \(y\).
For the second assertion this contradicts the relative degree bound with \(0<\eta<\min(2,\delta)\) once \(s\) is large.
For the first assertion use \(s'=s\) and \(t=0\), with polynomial degree at most \(2s\).

It remains to classify rational identities \(R_{s'}(cT)=eT^t R_s(T)\).
Both rational functions \(R_j\) have constant term one and linear coefficient one at the origin for \(j\ge3\).
The order at zero, constant term and linear term successively force \(t=0\), \(e=1\), and \(c=1\).
For \(j\ge3\) the first nonzero coefficient of \(R_j(T)-(1+T)\) occurs at degree \(j-1\), since its numerator over \(B_j\) is \(T^{j-1}(1+T+T^2)\).
Thus \(s'=s\), and \(\xi^a=\xi^b=1\) forces \(\xi=1\).
Finally two conjugates with equal nonzero \(n\)-th powers differ by a root of unity, so every power has the same number of distinct conjugates as \(x\).
