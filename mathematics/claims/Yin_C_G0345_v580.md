# Slow Fourier decay at bounded conductor index for units with neutral conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A power saving in the negative logarithm

Let \(P\) be the monic minimum of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), \(d=\deg P\), \(m=\log M(P)>0\), and suppose \(u>0\) of its conjugates have modulus one, counting both embeddings of each complex pair.
Set \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(J_n=(\alpha^n-1)R\), and use the [binary Fourier amplitude and conductor ideal](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#statement) on \(R/J_n\).
For arbitrarily large \(n\) there is a nontrivial character \(\chi\) with
\[
        [\mathfrak a_\chi:J_n]\le C_P,\qquad
        |\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\ge \exp(-C_P n^{1-\epsilon}),
        \qquad \epsilon=\frac{1}{u^2 D}.
\]
One may take \(D=d!\) for every such input, or \(D=d\) when \(\alpha\) is a Salem number.
In particular the exponent can be chosen in terms of degree alone without a height bound.
The periods and constants in the estimate depend on the input.

<a id="multipliers"></a>
## Choosing an integral multiplier and a period

Write \(w_\beta=|\log|\beta||/m\) for each nonneutral conjugate.
The unit norm gives \(\sum_{|\beta|\ne1} w_\beta=2\) and \(w_\beta\le1\).
Take \(\eta=1/(4uD)\).
There are arbitrarily large \(n\) with \(\max_{|\beta|=1}|\beta^{n-1}-1|\ll_P n^{-1/u}\).
Indeed among \(Q^u+1\) points of the simultaneous rotation two lie in a common box of side \(1/Q\), giving a return \(1\le n-1\le Q^u\).
No nonzero time is an exact return because none of the conjugates is a root of unity.
Along these periods all neutral denominators \(|\beta^n-1|\) stay bounded away from zero.

For each such \(n\) choose a nonzero \(a\in R\), with image \(a_\beta\) at each embedding, satisfying
\[
 |a_\beta|\le C_P n^{\eta w_\beta}\quad(|\beta|\ne1),\qquad
 |a_\beta|\le C_P n^{-2\eta/u}\quad(|\beta|=1).
\]
In the real and complex embedding space use intervals and disks of these radii.
Their product volume is constant in \(n\), since the real dimension of each complex coordinate counts both conjugates.
Choose \(C_P\) so the half-sized body has volume greater than the covolume of \(R\).
Two distinct points of the half-sized body then agree modulo the lattice, by integrating its multiplicity over a fundamental domain.
Their nonzero difference gives the required \(a\).
Multiplication of the embedding bounds gives \(1\le |N_{K/\mathbb Q}a|\ll_P1\), where \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).

Put \(b=(\alpha-1)a\) and use the trace character with parameter \(b/(\alpha^n-1)\).
Its amplitude is the product of \(|\cos(\pi t_j)|\) for \(0\le j<n\), where \(t_j=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(b\alpha^j/(\alpha^n-1))\).
The [trace-dual inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#slow) gives \([\mathfrak a_\chi:J_n]\le[R^\vee:R]|N_{K/\mathbb Q}b|\ll_P1\).
Along the selected returns \(|R/J_n|\asymp_P e^{nm}\), giving a nontrivial character for all sufficiently large selected \(n\).

<a id="bulk"></a>
## The middle of the product

Take \(L=\lceil\eta\log n/m\rceil+L_0(P)\).
On \(L\le j\le n-L\) the contribution of a contracting conjugate to \(t_j\) is at most \(C_P |\beta|^{j-L+L_0}\), using \(n^{\eta w_\beta}=\exp(|\log|\beta||\,\eta\log n/m)\).
For an expanding conjugate the corresponding bound is \(C_P|\beta|^{-(n-j-L+L_0)}\).
Their sum is uniformly small for sufficiently large \(L_0\), and its squared modulus summed over all middle positions is \(O_P(1)\) by the geometric series.
The neutral terms together are \(O_P(n^{-2\eta/u})\) at every position.
Thus the middle phases have \(|t_j|\le1/4\) for sufficiently large \(n\), with
\[
               \sum_{L\le j\le n-L}|t_j|^2
                   \ll_P 1+n^{1-4\eta/u}.
\]
On this interval of phase values \(-\log|\cos(\pi t_j)|\ll t_j^2\).

<a id="ends"></a>
## Integral comparison at both ends

At each initial position \(0\le j<L\) compare \(t_j\) with
\[
 x_j=-\sum_{|\beta|<1}(\beta-1)a_\beta\beta^j
                     +\sum_{|\beta|=1}a_\beta\beta^j .
\]
At each terminal position \(j=n-\ell\), \(1\le\ell<L\), compare it with
\[
 x_j=\sum_{|\beta|>1}(\beta-1)a_\beta\beta^{-\ell}
                     +\sum_{|\beta|=1}a_\beta\beta^{1-\ell}.
\]
Both are real algebraic integers in the splitting field by complex conjugate pairing and the unit property.
For each neutral term replace \(\beta^n\) by \(\beta\) in the denominator at the initial end and in both numerator and denominator at the terminal end.
The uniform error is \(O_P(n^{-1/u})\), as the denominators stay separated from zero and the remaining powers of \(\beta\) have modulus one.
The errors from the geometric terms off the circle are exponentially small in \(n\), up to factors polynomial in \(n\), uniformly over these \(O_P(\log n)\) positions.
Thus \(|t_j-x_j|\ll_P n^{-1/u}\).
Under any embedding, each \(a_\beta\) has modulus \(O_P(n^\eta)\) and each indicated power of a conjugate has modulus \(O_P(n^\eta)\), since \(\max_\beta|\log|\beta||\le m\).
All conjugates of \(x_j\) therefore have modulus \(O_P(n^{2\eta})\).

For Salem \(\alpha\), both endpoint comparisons belong to \(K\) itself.
Subtracting the initial \(x_j\) from \(\operatorname{Tr}(a\alpha^j)\) leaves \(a_\alpha\alpha^j+a_{\alpha^{-1}}\alpha^{-(j+1)}\).
Subtracting the terminal \(x_{n-\ell}\) from \(\operatorname{Tr}(a\alpha^{1-\ell})\) leaves \(a_\alpha\alpha^{-\ell}+a_{\alpha^{-1}}\alpha^{\ell-1}\).
The two evaluations lie in \(K\) since \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) is a field automorphism.
Hence \([\mathbb Q(x_j):\mathbb Q]\le D\) with either indicated choice of \(D\).

If \(s\) is an odd integer nearest to \(2x_j\), then \(2x_j-s\) is a nonzero algebraic integer since \(s/2\) is not integral.
Its norm and the upper bounds at all its other embeddings give
\[
                 |2x_j-s|\gg_P n^{-2\eta(D-1)}.
\]
This exponent is less than \(1/(2u)\), so the uniform error from replacing \(t_j\) by \(x_j\) preserves a lower bound of the same order for its distance to the nearest nonintegral half-integer.
Each endpoint factor is consequently bounded below by an inverse power of \(n\).
Their total negative logarithm is \(O_P((\log n)^2)\), absorbed in the middle bound with \(4\eta/u=1/(u^2D)\).
This proves the stated amplitude estimate.
