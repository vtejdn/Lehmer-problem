# Local power tests for reciprocal minima and their exact residue frequencies

<a id="statement"></a>
## A coefficient test, including the dyadic norm condition

For a monic polynomial \(S\) of degree \(d\) put \(\Psi_p S=\prod_j(X-\alpha_j^p)\), with roots repeated according to multiplicity, where \(p\) is a rational prime.
This operation has integral coefficients on \(\mathbb Z_p[X]\).
If \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is the monic minimum of a unit \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha\) has a \(p^e\)-th root in its own field, \(e\ge1\), then
\[
                          \Psi_p S\equiv S\pmod {p^{e+1}}.                \tag{1}
\]
This is simultaneous in all coefficients, including at ramified primes.
It strengthens the one-step [necessary Frobenius defect divisibility](Yin_C_G0901_v161.md#defect) by testing higher powers without factoring \(S(X^{p^e})\).
For an irreducible nontorsion input the content of the nonzero polynomial \(\Psi_p S-S\) gives a finite upper bound \(v_p({\rm content}(\Psi_p S-S))-1\) on the in-field exponent \(e\).

For any monic \(S\in\mathbb Z_p[X]\) with squarefree reduction and unit constant, let \(\alpha\) denote the class of \(X\) in \(\mathbb Q_p[X]/(S)\).
For odd \(p\), (1) is **equivalent** to \(\alpha\) being a \(p^e\)-th power in this local algebra.
For \(p=2\) the necessary and sufficient conditions are (1) and, for each monic local irreducible factor \(F\) of \(S\),
\[
                         (-1)^{\deg F} F(0)\equiv1\pmod{2^{e+2}}.        \tag{2}
\]
In particular good reduction at two rules out the exceptional quartic binomial obstruction \(\alpha\in-4K^4\) for an algebraic unit in its global root field \(K\), since its valuation in each unramified component is zero and that of \(-4\) is two.

Fix a squarefree monic reciprocal reduction of degree \(2n\), with constant one, and consider all monic reciprocal lifts of degree \(2n\) with constant one.
The exact fraction of lifts for which the local root is a \(p^e\)-th power is
\[
                       p^{-en}\quad(p\text{ odd}),\qquad
                       2^{-en-r}\quad(p=2),                              \tag{3}
\]
where \(r\) counts the unordered pairs of distinct mutually reciprocal irreducible factors of the binary reduction, with each reciprocal normalized to be monic.
The fractions refer to equally weighted lifts of the fixed reduction modulo \(p^{e+1}\) or \(2^{e+2}\), respectively.
For several primes the fractions multiply in the corresponding coefficient residue boxes.
These are local frequencies; deciding a global power requires a global argument.

<a id="contraction"></a>
## Contraction of the polynomial that raises every root to its \(p\)-th power

Writing \(\zeta\) for a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity, one has
\[
               (\Psi_p S)(X^p)=\pm\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}S(\zeta^jX).
\]
The sign is determined by the common degree and leading coefficient.
Reduction at \(\zeta-1\), followed by comparison in \(\mathbb F_p[X]\), gives \(\Psi_p S\equiv S\bmod p\) for integral \(p\)-adic coefficients.
If \(P,Q\) are monic of the same degree and \(P=Q+p^k H\), \(k\ge1\), expanding the product shows
\[
                   \Psi_p P\equiv\Psi_p Q\pmod {p^{k+1}}.                \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, after extracting \(p^k\), the linear term consists of the sum of \(p\) products with one occurrence of \(H\).
Its coefficients lie in \(\mathbb Z_p\) by Galois invariance, and reduce to zero at \(\zeta-1\) because the products have identical reductions.
The contraction of this prime to \(\mathbb Z_p\) is \(p\mathbb Z_p\); higher terms already carry \(p^{2k}\).
This includes \(p=2\).

In each monic residue class successive iterations converge coefficientwise to a unique fixed polynomial \(S_*\), by (4) and completeness.
Its roots are the Teichmüller representatives of the residue roots with multiplicities: each nonzero residue root in a finite field has a unique lift of the same prime-to-\(p\) order by simple-root lifting, zero lifts to zero, and powering by \(p\) permutes the lifts.
Their product polynomial is fixed under unramified Frobenius and lies in \(\mathbb Z_p[X]\).
Equation (4) shows that (1) is equivalent to \(S\equiv S_*\bmod p^{e+1}\), by iterating from \(S\) or comparing it to \(S_*\).
If \(\alpha=\rho^{p^e}\) in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), then \(\rho\) is an integral unit generating the same field.
Its monic minimum \(Q\) has \(S=\Psi_p^e Q\) without degree loss.
Iteration of (4) from \(Q\equiv S_*\bmod p\) proves (1).
Finally \(\Psi_p S=S\) would make the finite multiset of nonzero roots invariant under \(p\)-th powering, so every root would have finite order.

<a id="local"></a>
## Principal units in each unramified component

For squarefree reduction, Hensel lifting identifies the algebra with a product of unramified fields \(L\) of degrees equal to the residue factor degrees, whose rings of integers are generated by the indicated roots.
Write \(\omega\) for the Teichmüller lift of the residue of the root \(\alpha_L\) in one component.
The coefficient congruence \(S\equiv S_*\bmod p^{e+1}\) is equivalent to \(\alpha_L\equiv\omega\bmod p^{e+1}\) in all components.
In fact \(S_*(\alpha_L)\) differs from \(\alpha_L-\omega\) by a unit factor, because the other Teichmüller roots have different residues.
Conversely componentwise root congruences imply the coefficient congruences by taking all conjugates of every component root and multiplying.

The Teichmüller part always admits any \(p^e\)-th root in its own cyclic group of order prime to \(p\).
For odd \(p\), the series for logarithm and exponential are inverse on \(1+p\mathcal O_L\) and \(p\mathcal O_L\), respectively, and carry powering by \(p^e\) to multiplication by \(p^e\).
This gives exactly (1).
At \(p=2\) these inverse series apply on \(1+4\mathcal O_L\) and \(4\mathcal O_L\).
For \(y\in\mathcal O_L\) the binomial theorem gives
\[
       (1+2y)^{2^e}\equiv1+2^{e+1}(y+y^2)\pmod {2^{e+2}}.
\]
After (1), write \(\alpha_L/\omega=1+2^{e+1}c\).
The image of \(y\mapsto y+y^2\) on the finite residue field is precisely the kernel of its trace to \(\mathbb F_2\), by the trace identity and the two-element kernel.
Thus the necessary next-bit condition is \({\rm Tr}(\bar c)=0\).
It suffices as well: after matching this residue, the remaining factor lies in \(1+2^{e+2}\mathcal O_L\), the \(2^e\)-power image of \(1+4\mathcal O_L\) by the logarithm.
Finally the norm of \(\omega\) in \(\mathbb Q_2\) is one, and the norm of \(\alpha_L/\omega\) modulo \(2^{e+2}\) is \(1+2^{e+1}{\rm Tr}(\bar c)\).
This norm equals the signed constant term of the local factor, proving (2).

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## Counting the reciprocal lifts and attaining the power bound

The fixed polynomial \(S_*\) is reciprocal with constant one, since inversion preserves its residue roots and their lifts, and its constant term is the Teichmüller lift of the specified constant one in even degree (also at two).
There are \(n\) freely variable middle and paired coefficients of a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(2n\).
Fixing their values modulo \(p^{e+1}\) above their given residues costs the fraction \(p^{-en}\).
This proves (3) for odd \(p\) and supplies the first factor at two.

A squarefree reciprocal binary polynomial of even degree has no root one: the derivative at one equals \(n\) times its value there by pairing coefficients in characteristic two.
Every self-reciprocal irreducible binary factor therefore has degree at least two, and its inverse residue roots pair without any fixed root.
Uniqueness of Hensel factorization makes its local monic factor self-reciprocal, with the same inverse pairing of its lifted roots and norm exactly one.
For two distinct paired factors, the two local norms are inverse, so (2) is one condition.
Each such condition removes exactly one independent bit among the lifts modulo \(2^{e+2}\) which already agree with \(S_*\bmod 2^{e+1}\).
Indeed in the coprime Hensel factorization change the constant coefficient of either chosen monic factor by \(2^{e+1}\), replace its partner by its normalized reciprocal, and leave the other factors fixed.
These operations define commuting involutions modulo \(2^{e+2}\), using the same chosen member of each labeled residue pair.
Each toggles exactly the indicated norm bit while preserving monicity, reciprocity and the original lift modulo \(2^{e+1}\).
Multiplying the two members of a pair gives constant one; the self-reciprocal factors also have constant one by inverse pairing.
This proves the dyadic count, and coefficientwise Chinese remaindering proves the assertion for several primes.

The bound from the polynomial content can be attained at every positive exponent even for global reciprocal units.
Let \(\epsilon=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) and \(\alpha_e=\epsilon^{2^e}\) for \(e\ge1\).
Its quadratic minimum is \(S_e=X^2-t_eX+1\), with \(t_1=3\) and \(t_{e+1}=t_e^2-2\).
All \(t_e\equiv3\bmod4\), so \(v_2(t_e+1)=e+1\) by the recurrence \(t_{e+1}+1=(t_e-1)(t_e+1)\).
Since \(t_{e+1}-t_e=(t_e-2)(t_e+1)\), the coefficient content of \(\Psi_2 S_e-S_e\) has valuation exactly \(e+1\).
The element \(\epsilon\) belongs to the same quadratic field and realizes this bound.
