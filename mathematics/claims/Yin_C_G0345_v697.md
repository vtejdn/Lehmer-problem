# Transferring a height bound to signed relations in scalar quotients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound uniform in the signature

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit of degree \(d\), let \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and let \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero of degree \(W\) and with nonzero constant term.
Write \(H=\max_{|z|=1}|G(z)|\).
For a positive integer \(q\), assume that \(G(\alpha)/q\) is an algebraic integer.
This includes the full scalar congruence \(G(\alpha)\in q\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Put
\[
 y=\frac{G(\alpha)G(\alpha^{-1})}{q^2},\qquad
 b(t)=\operatorname{arcosh}(\max(1,t)),\qquad c_0=0.427800 .
\]
If \(G(\alpha)\ne0\), at least one of the following alternatives holds:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  &\frac{Wm}{d}+b(H/q)\ \ge c_0,\\
  &y=1,\qquad \frac{Wm}{d}\ \ge b(q/H).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(c_0\) uses the [published auxiliary-polynomial calculation](../audits/literature/GIM%2B10/Yin_U001.md#gim%2B10-u001) at its recorded numerical scope.
The alternatives hold also for unit-circle conjugates, real hyperbolic inputs and nonreal exterior conjugates.

If \(q>H\) and \(\alpha\) has any unit-circle conjugate, modular vanishing with \(Wm<c_0d\) implies exact vanishing.
In particular this holds for every Salem number and every degree.
For any reciprocal unit, a false positive when \(q>H\) requires
\[
                    Wm/d\ \ge\ \min\{c_0,\operatorname{arcosh}(q/H)\}.
\]
If \(y<0\) at a real embedding, only the first alternative is available, even when \(q<H\).
For Salem inputs and an even alternating word of \(r\) unit coefficients this gives \(Wm/d\ge c_0-b(r/q)\).
For \(q/H\) tending to one from above at Salem inputs the bound \(c_0\) improves on the limiting value \(\tfrac12\log2\) obtained from the [discriminant estimate with the same circle cap](Yin_C_G0345_v693.md#determinant) using only \(\Delta\ge1\) as the degree tends to infinity.
The latter estimate retains its use at other modulus ratios and includes the discriminant of the particular trace order.

<a id="growth"></a>
## An archimedean estimate for a reciprocal Laurent polynomial

Consider a Laurent polynomial \(V\) of exponents in \([-W,W]\) whose values on the circle are real and lie in \([-\cosh B,\cosh B]\), for \(B\ge0\).
For \(z\ne0\) let \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) be the two roots of \(X^2-2V(z)X+1\).
Then
\[
             |\log|\lambda||\le W|\log|z||+B.
\]
Indeed \(2T_k(V)\), where \(T_k\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, has Laurent exponents in \([-kW,kW]\) and circle modulus at most \(2 e^{kB}\).
By the maximum modulus principle, multiplying by \(z^{kW}\) gives the corresponding bound \(2e^{kB}|z|^{-kW}\) for \(2T_k(V(z))\) inside the circle.
Applying the same argument to \(V(1/z)\) bounds it by \(2e^{kB}|z|^{kW}\) outside.
Now \(2T_k(V(z))=\lambda^k+\lambda^{-k}\).
If \(|\lambda|\ne1\), taking \(k\)-th roots and the limit gives the assertion.
For \(|\lambda|=1\) it is immediate.

<a id="lift"></a>
## A unit congruent to one modulo two

Reciprocity gives an automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) carrying \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
Hence \(y\) is integral.
Define \(\theta,\theta^{-1}\) by
\[
                   \theta+\theta^{-1}=2-4y.
\]
Both are algebraic units, and \((\theta-1)/2\) is an algebraic integer since it satisfies \(Z^2+2yZ+y=0\).
If \(\theta\) is nontorsion, the [integral-congruence transfer](Yin_C_G0246_v1.md#gim%2B10-integral-congruence-transfer) gives \(h(\theta)\ge c_0\), for the absolute logarithmic height.

Apply the growth estimate to \(V(z)=1-2G(z)G(1/z)/q^2\).
Its circle values lie in \([1-2H^2/q^2,1]\), so one may use \(B=2b(H/q)\).
In a common number field containing \(\alpha,\theta\), average over all embeddings \(\sigma\).
At units, half the mean of \(|\log|\sigma u||\) equals \(h(u)\) by the norm formula.
Each \(\sigma\theta\) is one of the two roots for \(z=\sigma\alpha\).
It follows that \(h(\theta)\le W h(\alpha)+b(H/q)=Wm/d+b(H/q)\), proving the first alternative in the nontorsion case.

<a id="exception"></a>
## The only nonzero torsion case

If \(\theta\) is torsion, all conjugates of the algebraic integer \(y=(2-\theta-\theta^{-1})/4\) lie in \([0,1]\).
A nonzero such integer has absolute norm at least one, so all its conjugates equal one.
If \(y=0\), then \(G(\alpha)=0\) by reciprocity.
For \(y=1\), apply the growth estimate to \(V(z)=2G(z)G(1/z)/H^2-1\), now with \(B=0\).
At each conjugate \(z=\sigma\alpha\) its value is \(2(q/H)^2-1\).
When \(q>H\) the larger quadratic root has logarithm \(2\operatorname{arcosh}(q/H)\).
Averaging gives the second inequality, and for \(q\le H\) it is automatic.
A unit-circle conjugate and \(q>H\) give \(|G(z)|^2/q^2<1\), excluding \(y=1\).
An even alternating word at a real root \(\alpha>1\) has evaluations of opposite signs at \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), again excluding \(y=1\).
