# A Galois obstruction to non-Salem descent at the first integer trace level

<a id="statement"></a>
## An alternating composition factor in a strict non-Salem descent

Consider Salem inputs \(\sigma\) of degree \(2m\) with \(a=\log\sigma\to0\), trace minimal polynomial \(S\), and the [unique exterior trace above two](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#statement) of \(S+1\).
Let \(\tau>1\) be its positive outer lift, with minimal polynomial \(F\) of degree \(d\).
Suppose \(M(F)<\sigma\) and \(\tau\) is not Salem.
Then for every sufficiently large such input \(F\) is reciprocal and the splitting group of the minimal polynomial of \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\) has an alternating group \(A_t\) as a composition factor, where
\[
       t\gg\sqrt m\left(\frac{a}{\log(2m)}\right)^{3/2}
         \gg \frac{\sqrt m}{(\log(2m))^6}.                              \tag{1}
\]
In particular, in the solvable trace-group case a strict descent at this distinguished root must be Salem.
The estimate imposes a necessary condition on a proposed smaller-measure output; it does not establish its existence.

The underlying separation estimate applies independently of the shift.
Let \(F\) be any monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(d\) with a positive real root \(\tau>1\).
Put \(L=\log M(F)\le1,\ E=L-\log\tau\), and \(\ell=\log(2d)\).
If \(E>0\) and the splitting group of its trace polynomial is solvable, then with an absolute implied constant
\[
              \log(1/E)\ll d^{2/3}\ell+d^{3/4}L^{1/4}\ell^{3/4}.         \tag{2}
\]
The number and signs of all other real exterior roots are unrestricted in (2).

<a id="pair"></a>
## The discriminant and the degree of a radial product

Assume \(E>0\) and write \(Y=\log(1/E)\).
Let \(k\) be the number of exterior roots of \(F\) other than \(\tau\).
For each such root \(\beta\), pair it with \(w=1/\overline\beta\).
Both are roots by reciprocity and real coefficients, and the \(k\) pairs are disjoint.
Their distances satisfy \(|\beta-w|=2\sinh(\log|\beta|)\le 2eE\) since \(0<\log|\beta|\le E\le1\).
In the ordinary Vandermonde determinant at the \(d\) conjugates subtract the row at \(w\) from the row at \(\beta\), for all these pairs.
For \(0\le j<d\), the difference of the \(j\)-th powers has modulus at most \(d|\beta-w|\max(1,|\beta|)^{d-1}\).
The original discriminant is a nonzero integer and every row without subtraction has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt d\max(1,|\alpha|)^{d-1}\) at its root \(\alpha\).
Hadamard therefore gives
\[
       1\le d^{d/2}e^{(d-1)L}(2edE)^k,\qquad
       k(Y-\log(2ed))\le (d/2)\log d+(d-1)L.                           \tag{3}
\]

For any one of these \(\beta\) let \(\eta=(\beta\overline\beta)^{-1}\in(0,1)\) and \(D=[\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]\).
This is an algebraic unit, \(1-\eta\le 2E\), and \(2\le D\le d^2\): degree one would make it a rational unit, and a conjugate is specified by the images of the two roots.
The [absolute height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) and the product inequality give \(h(\eta)\le2L/d\).
In particular the log measure \(U\) of its minimal polynomial \(H\) satisfies \(U\le 2DL/d\le D\).
Integrality gives \(|H(1)|\ge1\).
Every coefficient of \(H\) has modulus at most \(\|H\|_{\mathbb T}\) by Fourier inversion.
Bounding \(H'\) on the real segment \([\eta,1]\) by the differentiated coefficient sum thus gives
\[
                1\le (1-\eta)D^2\|H\|_{\mathbb T}.                     \tag{4}
\]

For clarity, apply the [confluent determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) with confluence order \(1\le j\le D\).
The terms in that bound, after division by \(j\), give
\[
         \log\|H\|_{\mathbb T}\ll D(\log(2D)+U)/j+j\log(2D),
\]
using \({D+j-1\choose i}\le (2D)^i\).
Choose \(j\) to be the ceiling of \(\sqrt{D(1+U/\log(2D))}\), truncated at \(D\).
This integer is within absolute constant factors of the unrounded positive quantity for all \(D\ge2,\ 0\le U\le D\).
Substitution in (4) yields the following bound uniformly even when \(D<d\):
\[
       Y\ll \sqrt D\,\log(2D)+D\sqrt{L\log(2D)/d}.                     \tag{5}
\]
This use of the actual degree of the radial product will allow the trace-group action to enter the estimate.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## The trace block containing the conjugate pairs

The \(n=d/2\) inverse pairs of roots correspond to the distinct conjugates of \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\), since the roots different from \(\pm1\) with the same trace are exactly an inverse pair.
Indeed the reciprocal involution on the roots implies that the degree of \(\tau\) over its trace is two.
Let \(G\) be the transitive permutation group of the trace splitting field on these \(n\) labels.
Usual complex conjugation induces \(c\in G\).
Every nonreal trace label gives precisely one exterior root, necessarily different from \(\tau\), so \(c\) moves at most \(k\) labels.
If all other exterior roots are real, their radial products have the form \(\eta=\beta^{-2}\) and \(D\le d\).
Otherwise there is a nonreal trace: for a nonreal \(\beta\) off the unit circle, \(\operatorname{Im}(\beta+\beta^{-1})\ne0\).

In this latter case take a chain of \(G\)-invariant partitions from singletons to the whole set with no invariant partition properly between consecutive ones.
Let the first partition whose every block is fixed setwise by \(c\) have block size \(s\).
This partition has an immediate predecessor, because \(c\) moves a label.
Write \(u\) for the common size of the smaller blocks in that predecessor and \(t=s/u\).
On the \(t\) children of some block \(B\), \(c\) acts nontrivially and moves at most \(k/u\) children, so \(2u\le k\).
The permutation group \(J\) induced by the setwise stabilizer of \(B\) on its children is transitive by the transitivity of \(G\).
It is primitive, since a nontrivial invariant intermediate partition within \(B\) could be transported by \(G\) to all its translates to refine the partition chain.

For every secondary exterior root \(\beta\) with nonreal trace, the two different labels of \(\beta,\overline\beta\) lie in the same \(s\)-block.
This remains true for the images of those labels at every embedding of the full splitting field, since their restrictions act on the invariant partition.
Each unordered label pair gives at most four products of the two possible root choices at each label.
Consequently
\[
                   D\le4(n/s){s\choose2}=d(s-1).                      \tag{6}
\]

If \(G\) is solvable, then \(J\) is solvable and every nonidentity element of \(J\) moves at least \(t/2\) children.
For the latter assertion, a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of a faithful solvable primitive group is elementary abelian and transitive (its orbits form an invariant partition).
A transitive abelian subgroup is regular, so the point set identifies with this finite vector space and the group acts by affine transformations.
For a nonidentity affine transformation the fixed points are empty or a proper affine subspace, of size at most half the space.
Applied to \(c\), this gives \(s=tu\le2k\) and therefore \(D\le2dk\) in (6).
If \(c\) is trivial, any secondary exterior root is real and already gives \(D\le d\).
Thus there is an \(\eta\) with \(D\le2dk\) in either case.

To obtain (2) one may suppose \(Y\ge2\log(2ed)\), since the complementary case already satisfies (2).
Now (3) gives \(k\ll d\ell/Y\), and (5), with \(D\le\min(d^2,2dk)\), gives
\[
       Y\ll \sqrt{dk}\,\ell+k\sqrt{dL\ell}
         \ll \frac{d\ell^{3/2}}{\sqrt Y}
                    +\frac{d^{3/2}L^{1/2}\ell^{3/2}}Y .
\]
At least one term on the right is a fixed multiple of \(Y\) or larger, proving (2).

<a id="level"></a>
## The displacement at the first integer level

Return to the Salem input in the statement.
Its degree tends to infinity by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness, and the [Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(a\gg(\log(2m))^{-3}\).
Put \(T=2\cosh a,\ \tau=e^p\).
At level \(-1\), [signed displacement](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#location) gives \(T-2\cosh p=(1+o(1))/S'(T)\) and \(p<a\).
The [exterior slope estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v119.md#slope) gives \(\log S'(T)=ma+O(\sqrt m\log(2m))=(1+o(1))ma\).
In particular \(T-2\cosh p=o(a^2)\), which implies \(p/a\to1\) as \(a\to0\), by the quadratic expansion of \(\cosh\).
The mean value theorem at \(p,a\) now gives \(-\log(a-p)=(1+o(1))ma\), because \(|\log a|=o(ma)\).

Under the proposed strict descent \(L<a\), the [nonreciprocal gap](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-u002) makes the irreducible unit polynomial \(F\) reciprocal eventually.
The first-level trace has only one real root outside \([-2,2]\), above two, by [the endpoint count](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#factors).
A rational distinguished trace is impossible eventually since it would be an integer in \((2,T)\) with \(T\to2\).
If \(\tau\) is not Salem, its factor must therefore have at least one nonreal trace, \(k>0\), and \(0<E=L-p<a-p\).
In what follows \(D\) may refer to the radial product of **any** secondary exterior root.
Writing \(H_0=\log(2m)\), the estimates (3) and (5) give
\[
   Y\ge(1-o(1))ma,\qquad k\ll\frac{d H_0}{ma}\ll\frac{H_0}{a},
   \qquad \frac Dd\gg\sqrt{\frac{ma}{H_0}}.                          \tag{7}
\]
For the last bound use \(d\le2m,\ D\le d^2,\ L<a\) in (5).
Either \(D/d\gg (ma)^2/(dH_0^2)\gg ma^2/H_0^2\), or \(D/d\gg m\sqrt{a/(dH_0)}\gg\sqrt{ma/H_0}\).
In the first case the ratio of \(ma^2/H_0^2\) to the desired square root tends to infinity by \(a\gg H_0^{-3}\).
This proves (7) without a lower bound assumption on \(D/d\) or on \(d/m\).
It also excludes a solvable trace group directly by (2), whose right side is \(o(ma)\) as \(d\le2m,\ L<a\).

<a id="alternating"></a>
## A large alternating composition factor

Use the consecutive partitions from the block argument.
Every nonreal trace pair gives (6), so \(s\gg\sqrt{ma/H_0}\) by (7), while \(k^2\ll H_0^2/a^2=o(s)\).
We use the minimum-degree theorem of Praeger and Shalev for a finite quasiprimitive group of degree \(t\): unless it is the natural \(A_t\) or \(S_t\), every nonidentity permutation moves at least \((\sqrt t-1)/2\) points.
This is Theorem 7.2 on printed p. 255 of their published article, with the definitions in Section 7; the preceding paragraph identifies the primitive cases due to Babai and Manning.
The theorem is accepted here at its checked statement, without an audit of its original proof.
It applies to \(J\) because the orbits of a normal subgroup in a primitive faithful permutation group are an invariant partition, hence every nontrivial normal subgroup is transitive. [Praeger and Shalev, Theorem 7.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700002895) · [Inspected edition, p. 255](../literature/pdf/PS01--published.pdf#page=13)

If \(J\) were neither \(A_t\) nor \(S_t\), the action of \(c\) in the selected block would imply \(t\le(2k/u+1)^2\).
This gives \(s=tu\le4k^2/u+4k+u\ll k^2\), contrary to the preceding bounds.
Hence \(J\) contains the natural \(A_t\) and
\[
        t=s/u\ge 2s/k\gg\sqrt m\,(a/H_0)^{3/2}.
\]
In particular \(t\ge5\) eventually.
Let \(K\triangleleft G\) be the kernel on the full partition into \(s\)-blocks.
It contains \(c\); its image on the children of \(B\) is therefore a nontrivial normal subgroup of \(J\).
Every such subgroup of \(A_t\) or \(S_t\) contains \(A_t\) for \(t\ge5\), by simplicity and the trivial centralizer of \(A_t\) in \(S_t\).
Its composition factors thus include \(A_t\), which is then a composition factor of \(K\) and of \(G\).
The bound \(a\gg H_0^{-3}\) gives the second inequality in (1).
