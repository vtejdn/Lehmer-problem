# Coefficient growth from the norms of the row values at powered conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower rate for every fixed unit seed and multiplicity

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, of degree \(e\), with conjugates \(x_i\).
Put \(\ell_i=\log|x_i|\), \(h=m(P)=\sum_i\max(0,\ell_i)>0\) and
\[
                 D(P)=\sum_{i<j}|\ell_i-\ell_j|.
\]
Fix a positive integer \(m\), let \(t=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and take an integer \(p\ge2\).
Suppose \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have degrees at most \(b\ge2t\), \(P^m\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\), and \(V(x_1^p)\ne0\).
Let \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) in coefficient one-norm.
As \(p\to\infty\), every such pair satisfies
\[
     \log H \ \ge\ \frac{p}{e}\bigl(m D(P)-(b-2t)h\bigr)-o_{P,m}(p).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The error is uniform in the rows and in the supplied width \(b\).
The seed need not be reciprocal, and torsion ratios of distinct conjugates are allowed.
For coprime rows the required nonvanishing follows from the graph equation.
For each choice of seed the estimate concerns all sufficiently large integer exponents admitting such rows.

The [prime-indexed coefficient minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) with \(b\le5\) is \(2^p\) for all sufficiently large odd primes, and there is no genuine pair attaining this cutoff.
Previously at width five a [quadratic with distinct roots](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v249.md#endpoints) remained possible.
The exclusion follows from (1) at the original nonzero value of a single row.

<a id="contact"></a>
## Divisibility of the value at one powered conjugate

Write \(z_i=x_i^p\) and fix a splitting field \(L\) of \(P\) in \(\mathbb C\), of degree \(n\).
Every \(x_i=-U(z_i)/V(z_i)\), so the \(z_i\) are distinct, have the same degree as \(x_i\), and have monic integral minimum \(Q_p\).
At a finite prime ideal of \(L\) use its integer valuation \(\nu\).
Fix \(i\) and \(j\ne i\), and put \(r_{ij}=\nu(z_i-z_j)\), \(c_{ij}=\nu(x_i-x_j)\).
With \(A=p^{m-1}(m-1)!\) one has
\[
                  \nu V(z_i)\ \ge\ m r_{ij}-m\{\nu A+c_{ij}\}.         \tag{2}
\]
To prove this put \(B(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{1/p}{k}T^k\).
The coefficients of \(AB\) are integers.
In \(L[Y]/(Y-z_j)^m\) the local inverse of the substitution \(Y=X^p\) at \(X=x_j\) is \(x_j B((Y-z_j)/z_j)\).
Indeed its \(p\)-th power is \(Y\) there and its constant value is \(x_j\); the substitution induces an isomorphism of the two truncated rings because its derivative at \(x_j\) is nonzero.
Consequently the polynomial \(A[U(Y)+x_j B((Y-z_j)/z_j)V(Y)]\) is divisible by \((Y-z_j)^m\).
Its coefficients and the quotient are algebraic integers, by the unit property and monic division.
Evaluation at \(z_i\) gives valuation at least \(m r_{ij}\).
If \(r_{ij}>\nu A+c_{ij}\), the difference \(B((z_i-z_j)/z_j)-1\) has valuation greater than \(c_{ij}\).
Thus \(x_jB((z_i-z_j)/z_j)-x_i\) has valuation exactly \(c_{ij}\); the graph value at \(z_i\) gives (2).
For smaller \(r_{ij}\), integrality of \(V(z_i)\) proves (2) directly.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## Adding the collisions without counting one prime twice

For nonnegative numbers \(r_j\), \(\sum_j r_j-\max_j r_j\le\sum_{j<k}\min(r_j,r_k)\).
At each place use (2) for an index giving the maximum.
Then add over finite places with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/n\).
The total cost of \(\nu A\) and the finitely many \(c_{ij}\) is \(O_{P,m}(\log(2p))\).
For any three distinct indices \(i,j,k\), the weighted sum of \(\min(r_{ij},r_{ik})\) is \(o_P(p)\).

To check this, first suppose any two of \(x_i,x_j,x_k\) have root-of-unity ratio.
The distinctness of the \(z_i\) ensures the corresponding \(p\)-th power of the ratio differs from one.
Its difference from one takes only finitely many nonzero values.
The common valuation is at most the valuation of that difference, since \(r_{jk}\ge\min(r_{ij},r_{ik})\) and all powered conjugates are units.
This contributes \(O_P(1)\) after summation.
In the remaining case consider in \(\mathbb R^n\) the vectors \((\log|\sigma x_l|)_\sigma\) over all embeddings of the fixed splitting field.
They have equal Euclidean norm by Galois permutation and are distinct for \(l=i,j,k\), by Kronecker's theorem applied to the unit ratios.
Three distinct points on one sphere cannot be collinear.
The differences from the \(i\)-th point are therefore linearly independent, which implies multiplicative independence of \(x_j/x_i,x_k/x_i\).
Their \(p\)-th powers give distinct independent unit pairs of heights \(O_P(p)\).
The [Corvaja–Zannier bound for the common divisor of two unit differences](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) gives the claimed sublinear sum.
All comparisons are in the same fixed number field.

The sum of the \(r_{ij}\) over \(j\ne i\) is \(\nu Q_p'(z_i)\).
The normalized logarithmic norm of \(Q_p'(z_i)\) is \(e^{-1}\log|\operatorname{disc} Q_p|\).
Taking the product of its conjugates proves this identity, and the field degree ratio gives the same normalization in \(L\).
Thus
\[
 \frac{1}{n}\log|N_{L/\mathbb Q} V(z_i)|
       \ \ge\ \frac{m}{e}\log|\operatorname{disc}Q_p|-o_{P,m}(p).     \tag{3}
\]
The same norm is obtained for \(U(z_i)=-x_i V(z_i)\).

For each pair, \(\log|z_j-z_i|=p\max(\ell_j,\ell_i)+O_P(\log(2p))\).
For unequal moduli the smaller term has exponentially decreasing relative size.
For equal moduli the ratio is either torsion, already covered by distinctness, or a nontorsion algebraic number on the circle.
In the latter case use the [complex logarithmic form bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) for \(p\log(x_j/x_i)-2k\log(-1)\), choosing the nearest integer \(k\) to the argument divided by \(2\pi\) after multiplication by \(p\), and taking \(\log(-1)=i\pi\).
The nonzero form has magnitude at most \(\pi\), and its exponential differs from one by at least \(2/\pi\) times that magnitude.
Since \(\sum_i\ell_i=0\), twice the sum of the pairwise maxima is \(D(P)\).
It follows that \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q_p|=pD(P)+O_P(\log(2p))\).

<a id="ends"></a>
## Saving the first and last layers in the archimedean norm

Let \(x\) be a conjugate outside the circle and \(z=x^p\).
Using any supplied width \(b\), the two top terms at level \(k\) in the graph have exponents \(p(b-k)+s\), \(s=0,1\).
For \(0\le k<t\), isolate either one of these terms among all the top \(2t\) positions by applying the product of \(\mathcal E-a\) over the other \(2t-1\) exponents \(a\), where \(\mathcal E=X\,d/dX\).
Because \(2t-1<m\), the result vanishes at \(x\).
The isolated multiplier is a nonzero integer, since \(p\ge2\).
For an exponent on level \(l\ge t\) the multiplier is bounded by \(C_m p^{2t-1}(l+1)^{2t-1}\), independently of \(b\).
For \(|z|\ge2\), \(\sup_{l\ge t}(l+1)^{2t-1}|z|^{t-l}\) is bounded in terms of \(m\).
After division by \(|z|^{b-t}\), the sum of all lower terms is therefore \(O_{P,m}(p^{2t-1}H)\).
This bounds each top term at \(x\) on the same scale.
Summing the \(t\) top terms of either row and its remaining terms gives
\[
           \max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\ll_{P,m}p^{m}|z|^{b-t}H.
\]
If \(t=0\) the usual one-norm bound suffices without applying a differential operator.

For an interior conjugate apply the same argument outside the circle to the graph reversed about \(pb+1\) and its root \(x^{-1}\).
Its rows are \(V_b^*,U_b^*\) with \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\).
Returning to \(z=x^p\) gives \(\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\ll_{P,m}p^m |z|^t H\).
At a unit-circle conjugate the bound is simply \(H\).
Since the positive log moduli sum to \(h\) and the negative ones to \(-h\), their product bound reads
\[
 \frac1n\log|N_{L/\mathbb Q}V(z_i)|
       \ \le\ \log H+\frac{b-2t}{e}ph+O_{P,m}(\log(2p)).
\]
Together with (3) this proves (1).

<a id="salem"></a>
## Consequences for Salem and other fixed seeds

A Salem seed has one exterior root of modulus \(\rho\), its inverse and \(e-2\) roots on the circle.
Hence \(D(P)=(2e-2)\log\rho\).
At multiplicity two the lower rate in units of \(\log\rho\) is \((4e-2-b)/e\).
More generally if a reciprocal unit has \(g\) exterior conjugates and \(e-2g\) on the circle, the pairs with one positive and one negative log modulus contribute \(2gh\) to \(D(P)\).
The pairs with a zero and a nonzero log modulus contribute \(2(e-2g)h\), so \(D(P)\ge2(e-g)h\).

At widths through five the [seed classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short) leaves only two quartic Salem polynomials up to sign change within \(H\le2^p\).
For either one the lower rate is \((14-b)\log\rho/4\).
Both satisfy \(\rho^{3/2}>2\) by the [exact trace comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v247.md#five), so neither seed can qualify even at width eight for sufficiently large primes.
The second seed, with exterior trace \(1+\sqrt2\), has \(\rho>7/4>2^{4/5}\) by comparison with \(7/4+4/7\), and remains excluded at width nine.
These last two assertions concern the given quartics when other seed degrees are allowed.
Taking the larger of the two seed thresholds proves the full width-five result.
The estimates give no numerical onset and their rate is proportional to a seed statistic which can tend to zero with \(h\).
