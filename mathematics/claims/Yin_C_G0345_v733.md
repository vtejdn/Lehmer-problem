# Neutral root crowding and the boundary logarithm of a Salem polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound on the fraction of roots in crowded arcs

Let \(P\) be a complex polynomial of degree \(d\ge3\), and write \(d\nu(t)=dt/(2\pi)\), \(m(P)=\int\log|P(e^{it})|\,d\nu(t)\), and \(A(P)=\int\log^+|P(e^{it})|\,d\nu(t)\).
Let \(\Theta\) consist of the arguments of its \(s\) unit-circle roots, with multiplicity.
For \(\theta\in\Theta\), let \(n(\theta)\) count the members of \(\Theta\) within angular distance \(1/d\) of \(\theta\), including that root.
For every \(b>0\) and real \(R>2\pi\),
\[
 \frac{\#\{\theta\in\Theta:n(\theta)>b\}}{d}\log\frac R{2\pi}
 \ \le\ 2A(P)-m(P)+\frac{3Rs}{bd}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The boundary term \(2A(P)-m(P)\) is the \(L^1(\nu)\) norm of \(\log|P|\).

For a sequence of Salem minimal polynomials with \(m(P)\to0\), bounded \(A(P)\) therefore implies tightness of the number of neutral neighbors within \(1/d\) of a uniformly chosen neutral root.
At the [critical return bridge](Yin_C_G0345_v725.md#statement), the fraction of conditional covariance eigenvalues below any threshold \(\eta\to0\), averaged over blocks and pasts, then tends to zero.
The regularized determinant has lower limit at least \(x_{1/2}\) in the normalization of that claim.
Conversely, on any subsequence where this fraction has positive lower limit, \(A(P)\to\infty\) and \(m(P^2+X^d)\to\infty\).
The measure \(m(P^2+X^d)\) tests the condition using a single monic integer polynomial.

<a id="local"></a>
## Jensen's formula on circles centered in occupied bins

Partition the argument circle into \(d\) consecutive half-open bins of equal length \(2\pi/d\).
Write \(a_j\) for the number of unit-circle root arguments in bin \(j\), with indices taken modulo \(d\).
Let \(E\) be the union of the bins containing a root \(\theta\) with \(n(\theta)>b\).
All neighbors counted for such a root lie in its bin or in the two adjacent bins, since \(1/d<2\pi/d\).
Thus \(a_{j-1}+a_j+a_{j+1}>b\) for each selected bin.
Summing over all bins counts each root three times, so \(\nu(E)\le 3s/(bd)\).

For almost every \(t\) in bin \(j\), Jensen's formula on the disk centered at \(e^{it}\) with radius \(R/d\) gives
\[
 a_j\log\frac R{2\pi}
 \ \le\int \log^+|P(e^{it}(1+(R/d)e^{i\phi}))|\,d\nu(\phi)
            -\log|P(e^{it})|.                                            \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the chord from the center to every bin root has length at most \(2\pi/d\), and all the remaining roots in the disk give nonnegative Jensen contributions.
A zero on the disk boundary contributes zero, by the circle-mean formula for each linear factor.
The finitely many center angles at which \(P(e^{it})=0\) do not affect the integral.

For each \(\phi\) put \(r=|1+(R/d)e^{i\phi}|\).
If \(r\le1\), the full-circle mean of the nonnegative subharmonic function \(\log^+|P|\) at radius \(r\) is at most \(A(P)\).
Thus its integral over the rotated copy of \(E\), with the original normalization \(dt/(2\pi)\), is also at most \(A(P)\).
If \(r>1\), apply the same bound to \(P^*(z)=z^d\overline{P(1/\bar z)}\) at radius \(1/r\).
The boundary moduli of \(P^*\) and \(P\) agree, and \(\log^+|P(r e^{iv})|\le d\log r+\log^+|P^*(e^{iv}/r)|\).
Since \(d\log^+r\le R\), the integral over \(E\) is at most \(A(P)+R\nu(E)\) for each \(\phi\).
The other term in (2) has integral over \(E\) at most \(\int\log^-|P|\,d\nu=A(P)-m(P)\).
On the left of (2), integration over each selected bin assigns \(a_j/d\) to its roots before multiplication by the logarithm.
All roots counted on the left of (1) belong to selected bins, which proves (1).

## A logarithmic Mahler measure criterion at the critical scale

For Salem minimal polynomials \(s=d-2\), and \(d\to\infty\) on a shrinking sequence by finiteness in bounded degree at bounded Mahler measure.
Take \(R=b\) for \(b>2\pi\) in (1).
For bounded \(A(P)\), the upper bound for the fraction of neutral roots with more than \(b\) neighbors is \(O(1/\log b)\), uniformly at large degree, since \(d/s\) is bounded.
In the notation \(N=kd\), \(k\sim1/(2m(P))\) of the [crowding criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v725.md#statement), the choice \(U=k\to\infty\) gives \(U/N=1/d\).
The tightness just proved supplies hypothesis (3) there, with its conclusions on eigenvalue counts and regularized conditional determinants.
If a subsequence has a positive lower limit for the eigenvalue fraction and \(A(P)\) fails to tend to infinity there, a further subsequence with bounded \(A(P)\) contradicts the same conclusion.

For a real reciprocal \(P\) of even degree \(d\), \(e^{-idt/2}P(e^{it})\) is real.
On the unit circle this gives \(|P(e^{it})^2+e^{idt}|=1+|P(e^{it})|^2\).
Consequently
\[
         2A(P)\le m(P^2+X^d)\le 2A(P)+\log 2 .                         \tag{3}
\]
Writing \(P=\sum_{j=0}^d p_jX^j\), concavity of the logarithm and Parseval give \(m(P^2+X^d)\le\log(1+\sum_j |p_j|^2)\) under the same symmetry hypotheses.
A bounded coefficient \(\ell^2\) norm is therefore also sufficient.
These bounds leave open the size of \(A(P)\) along an arbitrary shrinking Salem sequence and the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
