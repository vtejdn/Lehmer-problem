# Mahler gaps from the number of binomial exponents in each dyadic interval

<a id="statement"></a>
## A norm comparison without divisibility conditions

Let \(d_1<\cdots<d_N\) be distinct positive integers with \(N\ge1\), and let \(b_i\) be nonzero real numbers.
Let \(w\) be the maximum number of the \(d_i\) in any one interval \([2^j,2^{j+1})\), \(j\ge0\).
With normalized angular measure, put
\[
        L(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^N b_i\log|1-e^{id_i\theta}|,
          \qquad \|b\|_2=\left(\sum_i b_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
There are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
                 c\,w^{-5/2}\|b\|_2\le\|L\|_1
                           \le \|L\|_4\le C\sqrt w\,\|b\|_2.          \tag{1}
\]
In particular the two-sided \(L^1\) comparison applies to supports with bounded occupancy in every interval \([x,2x)\).
The two occupancy conventions agree up to a factor of two, as each such interval intersects at most two standard dyadic intervals.
This extends the [divisibility-chain comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v105.md#statement) to sets with arbitrary divisibility relations.

Suppose \(A=\pm\prod_i(1-X^{d_i})^{b_i}\) is a nonconstant polynomial with integer exponents \(b_i\), and write \(S=\sum_i|b_i|\).
For integers \(k\ge0\) and complex \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\), the full logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=\log M\) satisfies
\[
                  m(A+aX^k)\ge (c/2)\,w^{-5/2}\sqrt S
                                           -\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2).     \tag{2}
\]
Thus bounded shifted measure forces \(w\gg_B S^{1/5}\) when \(m(A+aX^k)\le B\).
For fixed \(w\), the measures tend to infinity with \(S\), uniformly in the shift, and the positive logarithmic measures for nonzero integer \(a\) have a uniform gap depending only on \(w\).

In any sequence of shifted integer cyclotomic products with \(0<m(A+aX^k)\to0\) and nonzero integer \(a\), the mass \(S\), the number of occupied standard dyadic intervals, their maximum occupancy \(w\), and the number of distinct cyclotomic factors of \(A\) must all tend to infinity.

<a id="inversion"></a>
## An \(L^2\) lower bound by Möbius inversion

The coordinator supplied the inversion estimate and the frequency-block fourth-moment argument.
Each individual logarithm is in \(L^p\) for every finite \(p\), by its logarithmic behavior at finitely many points.
At a positive frequency \(v\), its [Fourier expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#fourier) gives
\[
           -2v\widehat L(v)=\sum_{d_i\mid v} d_i b_i .
\]
The [divisor inversion](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity) is linear and applies equally to these real coefficients, giving
\[
            b_i=-2\sum_{v\mid d_i}\mu(d_i/v)\frac{v}{d_i}\widehat L(v).
\]
Let \(K\) be the resulting matrix without the factor \(-2\), with row index \(i\), positive column index \(v\le d_N\), and entry zero when \(v\nmid d_i\).
Writing \(g=\gcd(d_i,d_j)\), the row Gram matrix satisfies
\[
       |(KK^*)_{ij}|\le\frac{\sum_{v\mid g}v^2}{d_i d_j}
          \le\zeta(2)\frac{g^2}{d_i d_j}
          \le\zeta(2)\frac{\min(d_i,d_j)}{\max(d_i,d_j)} .
\]
For a fixed row, indices in the same dyadic interval cost at most \(w\) in the sum of the last ratio.
Intervals whose indices differ by \(t\ge1\) cost at most \(2w\,2^{1-t}\) jointly on the two sides.
Thus every absolute row sum, and by symmetry every column sum, is at most \(5\zeta(2)w\) for \(KK^*\).
The Schur bound on its operator norm and Parseval's identity give
\[
                          \|b\|_2\le 2\sqrt{5\zeta(2)w}\,\|L\|_2.    \tag{3}
\]

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Fourth moment of each block of positive frequencies

For \(s\ge0\) define
\[
                 g_s(\theta)=\sum_{2^s\le l<2^{s+1}}
                                             \frac{e^{il\theta}}{l}.
\]
The block has \(\|g_s\|_\infty\le1\) by its length and \(\|g_s\|_2^2\le2^{-s}\) by orthogonality.
Consequently \(\|g_s\|_4\le2^{-s/4}\).
Fix \(s\) and write \(h_i(\theta)=b_i g_s(d_i\theta)\), \(F_i=\sum_{j\le i}h_j\), \(F_0=0\).
For every \(m\le N\),
\[
                    F_m^2=\sum_{i=1}^m h_i(F_i+F_{i-1}).
\]
All frequencies of the \(i\)-th summand lie in \([2^s d_i,4\cdot2^s d_i)\), since the frequencies are positive and \(d_j\le d_i\) for \(j\le i\).
For a fixed frequency \(v>0\) the possible \(d_i\) therefore lie in \((v/(4\cdot2^s),v/2^s]\), which meets at most three standard dyadic intervals.
Applying Cauchy–Schwarz at each Fourier coefficient and then Parseval gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
       \|F_m\|_4^4
         &\le 3w\sum_{i\le m}\|h_i(F_i+F_{i-1})\|_2^2\\
         &\le 12w\|g_s\|_4^2\,\|b\|_2^2
                                  \max_{j\le m}\|F_j\|_4^2 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second inequality uses Hölder and invariance of the angular distribution under multiplication by the integer \(d_i\).
Taking an index \(m\) at which \(\|F_m\|_4\) is maximal, and dividing if this maximum is positive, yields
\[
              \left\|\sum_{i=1}^N b_i g_s(d_i\theta)\right\|_4
                            \le \sqrt{12w}\,\|b\|_2\,2^{-s/4}.
\]
These bounds are summable over \(s\).
The negative real part of the sum over all blocks equals \(L\) in \(L^2\), by its Fourier coefficients and zero mean.
Convergence also holds in \(L^4\) by the summable bound, and the two limits agree because the circle has probability measure.
This proves the fourth-moment upper bound in (1).

Interpolating \(\|L\|_2\le\|L\|_1^{1/3}\|L\|_4^{2/3}\) gives \(\|L\|_1\ge\|L\|_2^3/\|L\|_4^2\) whenever \(\|b\|_2>0\), by (3).
Substituting (3) and the fourth-moment upper bound gives the asserted power \(w^{-5/2}\).

<a id="mahler"></a>
## Shifted measures and necessary growth of both support counts

For the stated polynomial signed binomial product, clearing its denominator shows that it is an integer polynomial with all its roots on the circle and leading coefficient \(\pm1\).
Indeed the denominator has unit leading coefficient and divides the integer numerator as a polynomial, and every root of the quotient is a root of the numerator.
The boundary logarithm is \(L\) almost everywhere and has mean zero.
Since \(|a|\ge1\), the [circle-root integral estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) bounds \(m(A+aX^k)\) below by \(\int L^+-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)=\|L\|_1/2-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\).
Integer \(b_i\) satisfy \(\sum_i b_i^2\ge S\), proving (2) and its occupancy consequence.

If \(w\) and the shifted measure are bounded, (2) bounds \(S\).
Write \(A=\pm U/V\) with \(U,V\) the products of numerator and denominator binomials repeated according to their multiplicities, using \(S\) binomials altogether.
For nonzero integer \(a\), the integer polynomial \(V(A+aX^k)=\pm U+aX^k V\) has at most \(2^{S+1}\) terms and the same measure, since \(M(V)=1\).
It is nonzero because \(A\) is nonconstant with a nonzero circle root.
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes any sequence with these bounds and positive logarithmic measures tending to zero.
This proves the gap for fixed \(w\), and the clearing argument alone forces \(S\to\infty\) on every such shrinking sequence.

An arbitrary monic cyclotomic product has a unique signed binomial presentation with constant term normalized to one, by the [binomial expansion and its normalization](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement).
Along a shrinking sequence its maximum occupancy must grow at least as a constant times \(S^{1/5}\) by (2).
If only boundedly many standard dyadic intervals were occupied on a subsequence, the same exponents on that subsequence would lie in boundedly many intervals of the form \([x,2x]\), violating the [gap for boundedly many multiplicative intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement).
A bounded number of distinct cyclotomic factors on a subsequence is excluded by the [gap for boundedly many distinct factors](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#statement).
This proves all four necessary conditions.
