# Common residue roots of nearly linear order and characteristic at a repeated seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## Large orders at the sum of the exponent primes

For positive integers \(L\) let \(\mathcal P_L,\mathcal Q_L\) be the primes in \([L,5L/4]\) and \([3L/2,2L]\), respectively.
There is a computable width \(b(L)\to\infty\) and, for each \(p\in\mathcal P_L\), a computable subset of \(\mathcal Q_L\) containing at least a fraction \(1-1/b(L)\) of its members, for all sufficiently large \(L\).
Write \(n=p+q\), and let \(\tau(n)\) be the number of its positive divisors.
Set
\[
 T(n)=\tau(n)\lceil\log_2(2n)\rceil,
 \qquad \mathcal D(n)=\{d\mid n:d> n/T(n)\}.
\]
For every retained pair and every [common row](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) of width \(b=b(L)\) and cost \(H\le 2^n\), suppose the same reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) occurs at both exponents, with multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\) in each graph.
Write \(e=\deg P\), \(h=\log M(P)\), \(B>0\) for the generator of \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\), and \(D_d^{\circ}\) for the prime-to-\(d\) part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_d)|\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{d\in\mathcal D(n)}\log D_d^{\circ}\ge (1-1/b)nh,
 \qquad \log D_n^{\circ}\ge (1-1/b)\varphi(n)h.                  \tag{1}
\]
Each rational prime \(\ell\mid D_d^{\circ}\) for \(d\in\mathcal D(n)\) exceeds \(L^{1-1/b}\) and differs from \(p,q\).
Over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), both rows and the two powered minima of this seed have a common nonzero root of exact order \(d\) and degree \(\operatorname{ord}_d(\ell)\le e\).
In particular exact order \(n\) occurs.
The product \(\prod_{d\in\mathcal D(n)}(D_d^{\circ})^k\) divides the order of \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(Y^n-1,U,V)\).
If \(B'\) denotes the part of \(B\) supported at the prime divisors of this product, then
\[
                 (B')^e\ \text{is divisible by}\
                         \prod_{d\in\mathcal D(n)}(D_d^{\circ})^k.       \tag{2}
\]
All orders in this product exceed \(n^{1-o(1)}\) in the sense that their logarithms divided by \(\log n\) tend uniformly to one.
The estimates count the full prime powers in the indicated group orders.

For any prescribed sequence of retained pairs with scales tending to infinity, the unrestricted [Lehmer infimum](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) is zero if and only if common rows with the *same* repeated seed at each pair can be chosen along the sequence with \(\log H/n\to0\).
The seed may change along the sequence.

<a id="layers"></a>
## Almost all the logarithmic norm lies on long primitive orders

Fix \(P\), let \(\alpha\) be a root, and take ideal norms in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
For \(d>2(2^e-1)\), [Stewart's imprimitive-ideal estimate](../audits/literature/Ste77/Yin_U001.md#ste77-imprimitive-bound) bounds the valuation of \(\Phi_d(\alpha)\) at each imprimitive prime ideal by that of \(d\).
No prime ideal above a divisor of \(d\) can give order \(d\), by the order of the multiplicative group of its residue field.
At a prime away from \(d\), the roots of \(\Phi_d\) have exact order \(d\) by separability of \(X^d-1\).
Hence the entire removed part of the cyclotomic norm divides \(d^e\).
The [fixed-seed cyclotomic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) gives
\[
        \log D_d^{\circ}=\varphi(d)h+
              O_P\big((2^{\omega(d)}+1)\log(2d)\big).                    \tag{3}
\]
For each \(\epsilon>0\) one has \(\tau(m)\ll_\epsilon m^\epsilon\): at primes \(t\ge2^{1/\epsilon}\) use \(u+1\le 2^u\le t^{\epsilon u}\), and for the finitely many smaller primes use the finite maxima of \((u+1)t^{-\epsilon u}\) over \(u\ge0\).
Thus \(T(n)=n^{o(1)}\), and every long divisor eventually meets the threshold in (3).
The missing orders have total totient at most \(\tau(n)n/T(n)\le n/\log_2(2n)\).
Since \(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n\) and \(2^{\omega(d)}\le\tau(n)\), summing (3) gives \(\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(n)}\log D_d^{\circ}=nh+o_P(n)\).
For \(d=n\) the error in (3) is \(n^{o(1)}\), whereas \(\varphi(n)\ge n/(1+\log_2 n)\) by the [elementary totient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#explicit).
This proves both estimates in (1) up to a fixed arbitrarily small relative error for every sufficiently large \(n\) at a fixed seed.

<a id="partners"></a>
## Excluding small characteristics for almost every partner of each first prime

Fix an integer \(j\ge2\).
For each first prime \(p\), discard every partner \(q\) for which some \(d\in\mathcal D(n)\) divides \(\ell^f-1\) for a rational prime \(\ell\le L^{1-1/j}\) and \(1\le f\le j\).
For each \(\ell,f\) the integer \(\ell^f-1\) has \(L^{o_j(1)}\) divisors by the preceding bound, uniformly in \(\ell\), where the exponent tends to zero at fixed \(j\).
For each candidate \(d\) the integer \(n/d<T(n)\) takes at most \(L^{o(1)}\) values since \(2L<n<4L\).
Each resulting \(n\) determines at most one partner of \(p\), so at most \(L^{1-1/j+o_j(1)}\) partners are discarded, uniformly in \(p\).
Also discard when some such \(d\) divides \(p^{2f}-1\) with \(1\le f\le j\).
This costs at most another \(L^{o_j(1)}\) partners by the same argument.
Because \(q\equiv-p\bmod d\), this test excludes both \(p\) and \(q\) as characteristics whose multiplicative order modulo \(d\) is at most \(j\).
The [prime interval estimate at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) gives \(|\mathcal Q_L|\asymp L/\log L\), so the discarded proportion tends uniformly to zero.

At each scale test all integers \(2\le j\le L\).
Require both prime intervals to contain primes, and for each \(p\) require that at least \(1-1/j\) of its partners pass the preceding finite exclusions.
For each retained pair require both inequalities (1) with \(b=j\) for every monic reciprocal irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial of degree at most \(j\) and measure \(M(P)\le2\).
This finite list follows from the coefficient bounds \(|P_{e-i}|\le2\binom ei\), with cyclotomicity and multiplicative measure decidable as in the [bounded-degree enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width).
Checks of the inequalities are exact comparisons of positive real algebraic numbers after exponentiating and raising to \(j\).
The exclusions involving the cutoff are exact as well, by comparing \(\ell^j\) with \(L^{j-1}\).
Take the largest passing \(j\) as \(b(L)\), using \(b(L)=1\) and empty partner sets if no value passes.
Every fixed \(j\) eventually passes by the uniform count and (3) on its finite list, so \(b(L)\to\infty\).
In each actual same-seed row the [tangency bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(e\le b\) and \(nh\le\log H\le n\log2\), so its seed occurs in the selected tests.

<a id="exponent"></a>
## The order and exponent of the actual row quotient

Let \(\ell\mid D_d^{\circ}\).
The reductions of \(P\) and \(\Phi_d\) then have a common root \(t\) of exact order \(d\).
Its Frobenius orbit has length \(f=\operatorname{ord}_d(\ell)\le e\) since its minimum divides \(P\bmod\ell\).
The partner exclusions give \(\ell>L^{1-1/b}\) and \(\ell\nmid pq\).
As \(p\) is coprime to \(n\), \(t^p\) has the same order and orbit length.
By [sum-period divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement), \(U(t^p)=V(t^p)=0\).
The reductions of the two powered minima both vanish at \(t^p\): the first contains the powers \(t^p\) of reduced roots of \(P\), and the second contains \((t^{-1})^q=t^p\) since \(P\) is reciprocal and \(d\mid n\).
The powered degrees are preserved by the original tangencies.

For each fixed \(\ell\), use just the contributing orders \(d\in\mathcal D(n)\) with \(\ell\nmid d\).
The corresponding \(\Phi_d\) reduce to squarefree polynomials with disjoint root sets of exact order \(d\).
Hensel lifting their irreducible factors yields disjoint unramified rings of integers \(O_i\) over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), with residue degrees \(f_i\).
More explicitly the product of these monic cyclotomic polynomials divides \(X^n-1\), and the quotient by their product maps isomorphically over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) to the product of all the lifted factors' rings.
This surjection from the entire period ring works also if \(\ell\mid n\).
In each component with root \(\xi_i\) set \(a_i=v_\ell(P(\xi_i))\), using \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Retain only components with \(a_i>0\).
Since all their distinct residual irreducibles divide \(P\bmod\ell\), one has \(\sum_i f_i\le e\).
Taking norms in these unramified fields gives
\[
       \sum_i f_i a_i=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(n)}v_\ell(D_d^{\circ}).
\]
The substitution \(Y\mapsto X^p\) identifies the cyclic rings, and the integral inclusion of both rows in \((P^k)\) gives a surjection from their period quotient over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) onto \(\prod_i O_i/\ell^{ka_i}O_i\).
The source has finite additive order by rational coprimality of the rows and the finite period rank.
Its order thus contains the full \(\ell\)-part claimed, and its additive exponent is divisible by \(\ell^{k\max_i a_i}\) whenever there is a retained component.
The integer \(B\) annihilates that quotient, since it belongs to the integral row ideal.
Therefore \(e v_\ell(B)\ge k\sum_i f_i a_i\), which proves (2) after multiplication over characteristics.
Individual valuations can have multiplicity greater than one, and a rational prime can support several orders on different residue orbits.

<a id="return"></a>
## Choosing the same factor at both primes

For any chosen sequence of retained pairs with \(L\to\infty\), one has \(b(L)\to\infty\), \(p,q\to\infty\), and two primes at every scale.
The [common Hermite interpolation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) puts the same fixed reciprocal seed at each prime once both preserve its power degree.
For each fixed seed this holds eventually, and the [coefficient estimate in the gap proof](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) gives width bounded in terms of its degree alone and normalized log cost at most twice its log measure plus a quantity tending to zero.
If \(\lambda=0\), nontorsion reciprocal unit minima of arbitrarily small positive log measure exist by the [single-prime reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
For each such fixed seed with \(2h<\log2\) its interpolated rows eventually satisfy the cost cutoff.
Applying the fixed-seed upper estimate successively at each desired positive cost threshold gives a choice of same-seed rows with cost tending to zero along the prescribed sequence.
The reverse implication follows directly from \(\lambda\le h\le\log H/n\).
The existence of rows with that limiting cost remains open.

A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin obtained the estimate by summing the primitive cyclotomic layers over long orders and counting exponent sums with a small residue characteristic.
