# Sharp double-contact cost for a finite list of reciprocal factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Cost at every exponent separating the original roots

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_s\) be distinct monic reciprocal irreducible polynomials of nontorsion algebraic units.
Write \(h_i=\log M(P_i)>0\) and \(S_0=\sum_i h_i\).
Call an integer \(n\ge2\) admissible if the map \(c\mapsto c^n\) is injective on the union of their complex root sets.
Equivalently, \(n\) is divisible by none of the orders of the torsion quotients of distinct roots in this union.
Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\) and a sequence of integer widths \(b(n)\to\infty\).
At every sufficiently large admissible \(n\) there is an integer row \(A\), of degree at most \(b=b(n)\), satisfying
\[
 \prod_i P_i^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
 A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y),\qquad A(c^n)\ne0\quad(P_i(c)=0).
\]
The logarithm of the least possible one-norm divided by \(n\) tends to \(S_0\) along all admissible exponents.
For each positive excess over \(S_0\), a single fixed width suffices to attain that upper slope eventually on the entire admissible set.
At an inadmissible exponent, two distinct roots with the same image would have to equal the same value of \(-\varepsilon A/A_b^*\), so a genuine row cannot exist.

In particular, fix any finite list of distinct positive irreducible logarithmic Mahler measures with sum strictly less than \((\log 2)/8\).
There are representatives of those exact measures which occur together on one passing row in the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at every sufficiently large integer \(n\), for the specified sign and any widths tending to infinity with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\).
For given original representatives in that range, the same assertion holds at every sufficiently large admissible exponent.
The input list is fixed before \(n\) varies.

<a id="small-coefficients"></a>
## Power sums with coefficients of sublinear height

In a fixed number field \(K\) choose elements \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_u\ne0\) of modulus one at a fixed complex embedding.
Assume each quotient of distinct elements has infinite multiplicative order.
Along an infinite sequence of positive integers \(t\), let \(a_i(t)\in K^\times\) have absolute logarithmic heights \(o(t)\).
For every \(\eta>0\),
\[
                \left|\sum_i a_i(t)\theta_i^t\right|>e^{-\eta t}
                  \quad\text{eventually on this sequence}.                 \tag{1}
\]
For \(u=1\) this follows from the local height bound and the fixed number field.
For \(u\ge2\) use the [number-field Subspace Theorem and almost-unit equation](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations).
Take \(S\) containing the archimedean places and all places where any \(\theta_i\) fails to be a unit.
Write \(x_i=a_i(t)\theta_i^t\) and use the product-formula normalization of the absolute values.
The projective height \(h([x])\) is \(O(t)\), and outside \(S\) the sum of \(\lvert\log|x_i|_v\rvert\) over all \(i,v\) is at most \(2\sum_i h(a_i(t))=o(t)\).
Thus the sum over \(S\) of the proximities of the \(u\) coordinate forms, \(\sum_{v,i}\log(\|x\|_v/|x_i|_v)\), equals \(u h([x])+o(t)\) by the product formula.

If the sum in (1) is nonzero but exponentially small on infinitely many \(t\), at the chosen place replace the first coordinate form by the sum of all coordinates.
The forms remain linearly independent, and this substitution increases the proximity sum by at least \(\eta' t-o(t)\) for some \(\eta'>0\), since \(\log |x_1|_v=o(t)\).
The Subspace Theorem puts these projective points in a finite union of fixed \(K\)-hyperplanes.
An infinite subsequence therefore satisfies a fixed nontrivial relation \(\sum d_i x_i=0\).
If the sum in (1) vanishes on an infinite subsequence, that relation is already available.

From either relation take a minimal vanishing subsum on an infinite subsequence, fixing its index set by finiteness.
Its support has at least two elements, since each term in the support is nonzero.
Dividing by \(-d_jx_j\) for one index \(j\) yields \(\sum_{i\ne j} y_i=1\) on that support, with no nonempty vanishing proper subsum.
Enlarge \(S\) by the finitely many places of the constants \(d_i\).
The affine tuple \(y\) and its inverse have combined outside-\(S\) height \(o(t)\), since each \(y_i=-(d_i a_i)/(d_j a_j)\cdot(\theta_i/\theta_j)^t\).
For every retained \(i\ne j\), \(h(y_i)\ge t h(\theta_i/\theta_j)-o(t)\), with a strictly positive coefficient by the height-zero criterion.
Thus \(h(y)\gg t\).
The tuples are almost \((S,\delta)\)-units for every fixed sufficiently small \(\delta>0\) eventually.
Xiao's Lemma 2.11 makes the set of these tuples finite, contrary to their growing height.
For a subsum of two terms the single coordinate \(y_i=1\) directly gives the same contradiction.
This proves (1).
When coefficients may be zero, an exponentially small sum on an infinite sequence forces all the coefficients to be zero eventually there, by extracting a constant nonempty support from any infinite sequence of exceptions.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Propagating the leading coefficients across shared radii

We prove a strict lower bound for any two integral rows after lowering a specified factor \(P_i\) to multiplicity one and keeping the others double.
Set \(S_i=S_0-h_i\), and choose one exterior root \(c_*\) of \(P_i\).
Such a root exists since \(h_i>0\) and the polynomial is monic.
The following reasoning applies to any sequence of admissible exponents.

At a radius \(R>1\) put \(z_c(n)=(R/c)^n\) for each node \(c\) there.
The [exact double-node matrices](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#matrices) apply to the exterior roots of any retained factors at this radius.
In the [recurrence estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#recurrences), choose the grouping modulus also divisible by all orders of torsion quotients of distinct original roots.
Every class containing an admissible \(n\) then has an admissible positive representative \(a_0\) in \(\{1,\ldots,q\}\), in the sense that its powered roots are distinct, also when \(a_0=1\).
The constant-in-\(X\) matrix at \(z_c(a_0)\) is invertible by the same value and derivative constraints.
Expansion in phase monomials, grouping by equal \(q\)-th powers and [Xiao's Lemma 4.1](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) consequently give the same subexponential inverse bound at every sufficiently large admissible exponent.
In particular, each radius with \(m\) double nodes forces an index jump of at least \(m\), irrespective of the row widths.
The unweakened list therefore costs at least \(S_0\) in limiting normalized log one-norm by summing over the exterior roots.

Suppose the weakened list had a sequence of nonzero two-row graphs of normalized log norm at most \(S_i+o(1)\), where the graph is written \(U_0(X^n)-XV_0(X^n)\) and its norm is the larger row one-norm.
Reverse both rows and remove common initial zeros.
For their remaining integral coefficient pairs \(w_j\) use the [finite coefficient-index limits](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) and the upper envelope \(L(s)=\max_j(\gamma_j-js)\), where \(\gamma_j=\lim n^{-1}\log|w_j|\) on a subsequence.
The sum of the positive breakpoints times their index jumps is \(L(0)-\gamma_0\).
The required jumps at the double nodes already contribute \(S_i\).
Thus \(\gamma_0=0\), \(L(0)=S_i\), every required jump is exactly its double-node count, and there are no further positive breakpoints.
In particular \(w_0\) is a nonzero rational integer pair of size \(\exp(o(n))\).

Visit the distinct radii of double nodes in decreasing order down to and including \(|c_*|\), inserting this final radius if necessary.
At radius \(R\) let \(m\) be its number of double nodes, \(k\) the sum of the earlier counts, and \(g\) the product of \(R'^{-nm'}\) over earlier radii \(R'\) with counts \(m'\).
The maximizing interval has endpoints \(k,k+m\), with a single maximizer \(k\) if \(m=0\).
Its first slope satisfies \(\gamma_k=\sum_{R'>R}m'\log R'\), by successively equating the two extreme lines at each earlier breakpoint.
Write
\[
          y_\ell=g R^{-n\ell}w_{k+\ell}\quad(0\le\ell\le m)
\]
and use the two coordinates of these vectors as the coefficients of polynomials \(U,V\).
The last pair \(y_m\) at one radius is exactly the first pair at the next.
At each double node the equations for this block are
\[
    U(z)-cV(z)=O(e^{-\rho n}),\qquad
    nz(U'(z)-cV'(z))+cV(z)=O(e^{-\rho n}), \qquad z=z_c(n).              \tag{2}
\]
They follow by subtracting \(nk\) times the reversed value equation from the differentiated one and multiplying both by \(g/(c^{-n})^k\).
For an index \(j\) the normalized slope is \(\gamma_j-(j-k)\log R-\gamma_k\), which is zero at the extreme indices and strictly negative outside the interval.
The [geometric tail](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) remains exponentially small, since the normalizing factor has slope \(-L(\log R)\le0\).
Finite extraction and decreasing \(\rho>0\) handle all the radii and the factors \(nj\).
The value equation alone holds at the simple node \(c_*\) in its block.

For a block with \(m>0\), solve the exact homogeneous versions of the \(2m\) double conditions in (2) for \(y_1,\ldots,y_m\) in terms of \(y_0\), replacing \(n\) by \(X\) in the derivative rows.
Call its coefficient determinant \(D_R(X,z)\).
At \(X=0\), with the phases evaluated at any admissible positive exponent, the solution polynomials are
\[
           (U(Y),V(Y))=y_0\,L_R(Y)/L_R(0),\qquad
           L_R(Y)=\prod_{\text{double }c}(Y-z_c).
\]
Indeed the \(m\) distinct phases are zeros of both coordinates, and their constant coefficients are prescribed.
This proves \(D_R(0,z)\ne0\), and the same grouped recurrence argument gives \(|D_R(n,z(n))|\ge\exp(-o(n))\).
All minors have polynomial growth at these phases, hence solving (2) changes its exponentially small errors by at most \(\exp(o(n))\).
At \(m=0\) take determinant one and retain \(y_0\) unchanged.

At the last block use its solution in the value equation for \(c_*\).
Composing these finitely many linear maps from \(y_0\) and clearing their Cramer determinants gives a row vector \(p(X,z)\) polynomial in \(X\) and the phase coordinates, with fixed algebraic coefficients, such that
\[
        |p(n,z(n))w_0|\le e^{-\rho' n}\quad\text{for some }\rho'>0.
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
All cleared denominators are \(O(n^C)\) there for a fixed \(C\).
For every positive exponent \(a_0\) separating the roots,
\[
                     p(0,z(a_0))=\kappa(1,-c_*),\qquad \kappa\ne0.       \tag{4}
\]
Each preceding block sends its first pair to \(y_0/L_R(0)\) at its last coefficient.
The last value row supplies the scalar \(L_R(z_{c_*})/L_R(0)\), nonzero since the simple phase differs from every double phase there.
The determinant factors cleared in obtaining \(p\) are also nonzero at this specialization.
These formulas include a simple node above all double radii and an empty double list.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Rationality forced by a simple node at the limiting cost

Expand \(p(X,z(n))=\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n\) with algebraic polynomial rows \(p_\beta\) and unit-modulus algebraic bases.
Enlarge \(q\) to kill all torsion ratios of these bases as well as the original collision orders.
Extract an infinite class \(n=a_0+qt\), \(1\le a_0\le q\), from (3).
For each distinct \(\theta=\beta^q\) put \(r_\theta(X)=\sum_{\beta^q=\theta}\beta^{a_0}p_\beta(X)\).
The remaining distinct \(\theta\)'s have nontorsion quotients.
The coefficients \(r_\theta(n)w_0\) have height \(o(t)\) when nonzero, since \(n=a_0+qt\), the polynomial rows are fixed, and the coordinates of \(w_0\) are integers of size \(\exp(o(n))\).
Apply (1) to (3) with these coefficients.
It follows that \(r_\theta(n)w_0=0\) for every \(\theta\) eventually on the extracted sequence.

Let \(r(X)=\sum_\theta r_\theta(X)\).
For each automorphism \(\tau\) of a normal number field containing the coefficients, both \(r(n)\) and \(\tau r(n)\) now annihilate the same nonzero rational pair \(w_0\).
Their determinant as a polynomial in \(X\) vanishes on infinitely many integers and hence at zero.
But \(r(0)=p(0,z(a_0))\), and (4) makes this determinant at zero a nonzero scalar times \(c_*-\tau c_*\).
Thus \(c_*\) is rational, contradicting an algebraic unit of modulus greater than one.
The weakened list therefore has normalized log norm at least \(S_i+\eta_i\) at every sufficiently large admissible exponent for some fixed \(\eta_i>0\), uniformly in the row width.
Indeed failure would supply exactly the sequence of graphs just excluded.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Fixed-width interpolation and numerical representatives

Choose the positive interpolation excess smaller than the desired slack and all of the \(\eta_i\).
The [integral two-jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) gives a fixed width \(w\) and nonzero two-row graphs with all the required double factors and normalized log norm at most \(S_0\) plus this excess for every sufficiently large \(n\).
Here \(J=2\sum_i\deg P_i\) and \(l=2(w+1)>J\) give the leading bound \(S_0l/(l-J)\) from the separate field embeddings.
For admissible \(n\), the powered minima \(Q_{i,n}\) remain distinct of unchanged respective degrees, with log measures \(nh_i\).
If both rows contained \(Q_{i,n}\), removing it would leave at least a simple \(P_i\) and all the other double factors, because \(Q_{i,n}(X^n)\) has simple roots and misses the other selected orbits.
The largest quotient row has log measure at least its log norm minus \(w\log 2\).
Restoring the factor therefore forces normalized log norm at least \(S_0+\eta_i-w\log(2)/n\), contradicting the chosen excess.
Consequently both row values are nonzero at each powered node by the graph value equation.
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) preserve all contacts and numerator nonvanishings at the chosen sign and width \(w+1\), at a bounded norm cost.
The [reciprocal width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) pads to any larger width at a norm cost of at most two.
The integer one-norms have a least value whenever the rows exist.
Taking arbitrarily small fixed slack and the unweakened lower bound proves the limit for every diverging width sequence.

For the numerical assertion, the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) gives monic reciprocal unit minima.
Independently choose an [all-power norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) for each of the distinct measures.
Within each orbit all conjugate powers remain distinct.
Across orbits a collision would force equality of the powered measures \(nh_i,nh_j\), contrary to the distinct original values.
Thus every exponent is admissible for that choice.
For either these representatives or the admissible exponents of a given list, \(2S_0<(\log2)/4\) leaves room in the strict budget for the fixed-width slack and \(\log(2nb+1)=o(n)\).
This proves the simultaneous count assertion.
