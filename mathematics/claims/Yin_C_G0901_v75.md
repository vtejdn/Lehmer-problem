# Explicit Frobenius bounds at fixed width and the necessity of a relation at one reciprocal pair

<a id="statement"></a>
## A resultant in the base Laurent polynomial ring

Let \(F(X,Z)\) be a primitive irreducible integer Laurent polynomial, with any finite number of base variables \(X\), and positive width \(D\) in the powers of \(Z\).
Normalize those powers to run from \(0\) to \(D\).
Suppose \(F\) is not cyclotomic in a Laurent monomial, up to sign and monomial factors.
For every rational prime \(p\),
\[
                 m(F)\ \ge\ \frac{\log p-D\log2}{p+1}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) is the ordinary logarithmic Mahler measure on the whole torus.
The prime may be chosen independently of the coefficients, dimension and widths in the base.
The bound complements the [bounded partial-degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement); it gives explicit sizable cutoffs at small \(D\).
An integral polynomial with any such factor of width at most \(D\) inherits (1).

In particular, for an integral symmetric Laurent polynomial \(H(X)\) in one variable and any integer \(a>0\) with the two exponents \(\pm a\) absent from \(H\), put
\(P(X)=H(X)+b(X^a+X^{-a})\) with \(b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
When \(|b|=1\), exclude from \(H\) the constants of absolute value at most two and the polynomials \(\pm(X^j+X^{-j})\), \(j\ge1\).
There exists a positive explicit cutoff independent of the total term count such that, below it, the selected frequency \(a\) satisfies a bounded additive relation with the remaining support; the bound on the length depends on the term count.
Concretely the criterion below applies as soon as
\[
                 m(P)\le \frac{\log(11/4)}{24}.                           \tag{2}
\]
For the excluded two-pair remainders and \(|b|=1\), the whole specialization is cyclotomic up to Laurent monomial factors; a constant remainder with absolute value at most two likewise gives measure zero.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## Divisibility and the archimedean cost

Let \(A(X)\) be the leading coefficient in \(Z\), and form
\[
       R(X)=\operatorname{Res}_{Z}\big(F(X,Z),F(X^p,Z^p)\big).
\]
The factor notation \(X^p\) means raising every base coordinate to \(p\).
The freshman's dream modulo \(p\) gives \(F(X^p,Z^p)=F(X,Z)^p+pB(X,Z)\) with \(B\) integral of \(Z\)-degree at most \(pD\).
In the root formula for the resultant, the first factor \(A^{pD}\) remains even if \(B\) has smaller degree.
If \(R\ne0\) and \(b_0=\deg_Z B\), the identity therefore gives
\[
             R=p^D A^{pD-b_0}\operatorname{Res}_{Z}(F,B).
\]
Thus \(R/p^D\) is a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial and \(m(R)\ge D\log p\).
An integral Laurent polynomial has nonnegative measure, by iterating the one-variable leading-coefficient bound.

For almost every base point on the torus, let \(\alpha_i\) be the \(D\) roots over \(X\) and \(\beta_j\) the \(D\) roots over \(X^p\), including multiplicities.
One has
\[
 R=A(X)^{pD}A(X^p)^D\prod_{i,j}(\alpha_i^p-\beta_j).
\]
Use \(|u-v|\le2\max(1,|u|)\max(1,|v|)\) on all \(D^2\) differences.
The leading coefficients combine with the root terms to give, by Jensen in \(Z\) and invariance of Haar measure under the power map,
\[
              m(R)\le D^2\log2+D(p+1)m(F).
\]
The fiber formula is used away from the zero sets of the nonzero end coefficients, and the logarithms of the nonzero Laurent polynomials are integrable.
Together these inequalities give (1).

<a id="nonzero"></a>
## Excluding a common horizontal factor

If \(R=0\), irreducibility and primitivity over the base Laurent polynomial ring show that \(F\) divides \(F(X^p,Z^p)\), by Gauss's lemma after passing to the rational function field.
The zero set of \(F\) in the torus is then mapped into itself by simultaneous \(p\)-th powers.
Choose the [products of primitive prime grids](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#grid), with all base primes distinct from \(p\) and tending to infinity.
The full rational orbit of such a grid point avoids the zeros of both end coefficients for all sufficiently large choices.
On each of these grids the \(p\)-th power map permutes the base orbit.
Over each base point all \(D\) roots are nonzero and their union over the orbit is finite.
Iteration sends \((X,z)\) to \((X^{p^j},z^{p^j})\) inside this finite set, so every \(z\) in these fibers is a root of unity.
For no base variables the same argument applies to the one-point base directly.

For clarity, these torsion points are Zariski dense in the hypersurface, or in its conjugate components jointly over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed any rational Laurent polynomial \(B_1\) not divisible by \(F\) has a nonzero \(Z\)-resultant with \(F\), after monomial normalization.
On sufficiently large grids this nonzero rational resultant does not vanish, by the grid-avoidance argument cited above.
Thus no such \(B_1\) vanishes on all the torsion fibers.
The Zariski closure over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) of all torsion points on this rational hypersurface is Galois stable and descends to \(\mathbb Q\), so it too is the whole hypersurface.
The [torsion-coset theorem and horizontal factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) now imply that \(F\) is cyclotomic in a Laurent monomial, contradicting the hypothesis.
This proves \(R\ne0\) for each prime, including the case of a nonmonic leading coefficient.

<a id="frequency"></a>
## One selected pair at a quantitative cutoff

Unfold \(P\) to \(G(X,Z)=H(X)+b(Z+Z^{-1})\).
If \(|b|\ge2\), Jensen in \(Z\) gives \(m(G)\ge\log2\).
For \(|b|=1\), the [trace quadratic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#trace) gives \(m(G)>0\) outside the stated exceptions, multiplying by a sign if necessary.
After multiplying by a sign and \(Z\), the polynomial is monic with constant one in \(Z\).
Thus its integral Laurent factorization has no base-only nonunit factor.
At least one positive-width irreducible factor must have positive measure, because the measure is additive.
It is not cyclotomic in a monomial, and has width at most two.
Applying (1) with \(p=11\) proves \(m(G)\ge c=\log(11/4)/12\) in every required case.

Let \(S\) be the support of \(G\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\) and \(k=|S|\).
With \(\delta=c/3\), set \(\eta=(\delta/(48k^2))^{4(k-1)}\) and \(N=\lceil(1+k^2/\eta)/\delta\rceil\).
If the map \((h,v)\mapsto h+av\) has trivial kernel on \(N(S-S)\), the [one-variable moment transfer](Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#transfer) gives \(m(P)\ge c-\delta>c/2\).
This contradicts (2).
Therefore there is a sum of \(N\) support differences equal to \((h,v)\) with \(v\ne0\), \(h+av=0\) and \(|v|\le2N\).
Its first coordinate is an integer combination of remaining support exponents with total absolute coefficient at most \(2N\).
When the remainder is one of the excluded reciprocal pairs, \(X^a+X^{-a}\pm(X^j+X^{-j})\) factors into two cyclotomic binomials up to signs and a monomial.
For an excluded constant the conclusion follows from the roots of the associated quadratic.
As in the [two-pair bound](Yin_C_G0901_v69.md#frequency), the small-measure hypothesis concerns the whole polynomial.
The same hypothesis on a specified factor suffices when its cofactor has logarithmic measure zero, equivalently multiplicative measure one.
