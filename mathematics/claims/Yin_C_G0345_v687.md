# Localizing prime radicals of Salem numbers near roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform bounds on the radical and the exceptional seeds

Fix integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and a sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Let \(r\) be the Salem root of \(G(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), with minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), and put \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\).
If \(p>n\) is prime and \(\beta=r^{1/p}\) belongs to \(K\), then
\[
                         \beta<5p,\qquad a<1+(5p)^p.                  \tag{1}
\]
Thus each fixed pair \(n,p\) allows only finitely many seeds.
In the case of a neutral conjugate lifting near a root of \(A\) in the construction below, one also has
\[
                         \log(a-1)<(n+1)\log(n-1).                    \tag{2}
\]

For fixed \(n\) the number of \(a\le X\) admitting any such prime for either sign is \(O_n((1+\log X)^{1+\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor})\).
More generally, for \(7\le n\le N(X)\) with \(N(X)=o(\log X/\log\log X)\), the number of such seeds over all these delays and both signs is at most \(X^{o(1)}\).
The [all-delay count](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#statement) applies also to the larger delays.
Since a radical index exceeding the [seed divisor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#statement) in the sense of nondivisibility requires a prime radical with \(p>n\), the same improved exceptional counts apply to that failure over the indicated delay ranges.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## An integer resultant bounded by four

Write \(L=n-1,\ U=G/P,\ A(X)=(X^L-\epsilon)/U(X)\), and \(D=\deg A=d-2\).
The [torsion classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) says that \(U\) consists exactly of the distinct factors at \(1,-1\) among the roots of \(X^L-\epsilon\).
In particular \(4\le D\le L\).
All roots of \(A\) lie on the circle.
For \(\zeta\) among them,
\[
                    P(\zeta)=\epsilon(\zeta^2-1)/U(\zeta),
\]
by evaluating \(G\) at \(\zeta^L=\epsilon\).
For \(\epsilon=1,L\) even, \(U=X^2-1\), giving \(P(\zeta)=1\).
For \(\epsilon=1,L\) odd, \(U=X-1\), giving \(P(\zeta)=\zeta+1\) with \(A(-1)=1\).
For \(\epsilon=-1,L\) odd, \(U=X+1\), giving \(P(\zeta)=1-\zeta\) with \(A(1)=1\).
For \(\epsilon=-1,L\) even, \(U=1,\ A=X^L+1\), and the product of the absolute values of \(\zeta^2-1\) is \(A(1)A(-1)=4\).
In particular \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}A(r)|=|\operatorname{Res}(P,A)|\) equals one in the first three cases and four in the last case.

For \(p>n\), its coprimality with \(2L\) implies that \(p\)-th powering permutes the roots of \(A\), since it permutes the roots of \(X^L-\epsilon\) and fixes the deleted linear roots.
Thus \(A(X)\mid A(X^p)\) over \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
If \(\beta\in K\), then \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=K\), and \(\beta\) is a Salem unit by the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
The integral norm of \(A(\beta)\) divides that of \(A(r)\).
Consequently
\[
                         1\le|N_{K/\mathbb Q}A(\beta)|\le4.            \tag{3}
\]

<a id="angles"></a>
## Pairing neutral conjugates with the torsion points

For a root \(z=e^{it}\) of \(G\) on the unit circle its equation gives
\[
            e^{iLt}=\epsilon\frac{1-e^{-it}/a}{1-e^{it}/a}.
\]
The continuous periodic branch of \(\arg(1-e^{it}/a)\) has modulus at most \(\arcsin(1/a)\).
The lift \(Lt+2\arg(1-e^{it}/a)\) is strictly increasing, since its derivative is \(n-(a^2-1)/|a-e^{it}|^2>0\), and it increases by \(2\pi L\) in one turn.
Every angle \(t_0\) with \(e^{iLt_0}=\epsilon\) therefore labels exactly one root on the circle, by equating that lift to \(Lt_0\) modulo \(2\pi L\).
Their circular angular distance is at most \(2\arcsin(1/a)/L\le\pi/(aL)\).
At a deleted linear root the two points coincide.
Hence the \(D\) neutral conjugates of \(r\) pair bijectively with the roots of \(A\) at this angular distance.

For a neutral conjugate \(w\) of \(\beta\), the conjugate \(z=w^p\) has such a partner \(\zeta\).
Choose the \(p\)-th root \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) nearest to \(w\).
The angular distance from \(w\) to \(\rho\) is at most \(\pi/(apL)\).
These choices respect complex conjugation by uniqueness.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Two bounds according to the chosen lift

In each of the four cases for \(U\), the sum of absolute coefficients of \(A\) is at most \(L\), by the geometric sum for division by a linear factor or by \(X^2-1\).
Thus \(|A(w)|\le L\) and the angular derivative has modulus at most \(L^2\) everywhere on the circle.
Also \(|A(\beta)A(\beta^{-1})|\le L^2\beta^D\).

Suppose some lift \(\rho\) is itself a root of \(A\).
Then \(|A(w)|\le\pi L/(ap)\).
The conjugate \(\bar w\) contributes the same estimate, and it is distinct from \(w\) since all neutral conjugates of a Salem number are nonreal.
Using the lower bound in (3) and the other \(D-2\) neutral factors gives
\[
                     1\le \beta^D L^{D+2}\frac{\pi^2}{a^2p^2}.
\]
As \(\beta^p=r<a+1\le4a/3\) and \(p>n\ge7\), it follows that \(\beta^{2p-D}< L^{D+2}\).
Since \(2p-D\ge D+2\) and \(p/(2p-D)\le1\), this proves \(\beta<L\) and \(\log(a-1)<p\log\beta<(D+2)\log L\), giving (2).

Now suppose none of the chosen lifts belongs to \(A\).
Then \(\epsilon\rho^L\) is a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity.
Indeed \(\rho^L=\epsilon\) would put \(\rho\) among the roots of \(A\) or among the deleted linear roots, and a deleted linear root would have \(\rho^p=\rho\) excluded from \(A\).
Therefore \(|\rho^L-\epsilon|\ge2\sin(\pi/p)\ge4/p\).
The angular distance estimate gives \(|w^L-\rho^L|\le\pi/(ap)\), so \(|w^L-\epsilon|>2/p\).
Since \(|U(w)|\le4\), we obtain \(|A(w)|>1/(2p)\) at every neutral conjugate.

It suffices to consider \(\beta\ge2\) here.
The reciprocal symmetry of \(A\) up to sign gives \(|A(\beta)A(\beta^{-1})|=\beta^D|A(\beta^{-1})|^2\).
From its defining quotient, \(|A(\beta^{-1})|\ge(1-2^{-L})/(1+1/2)^2>1/3\).
The upper bound in (3) consequently gives \((\beta/(2p))^D<36\).
Since \(D\ge4\), we have \(\beta<2p\sqrt6<5p\).
Both lift cases now give (1), using \(a-1<r\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the seeds with a prime radical above the delay

Let \(X\ge3\).
For \(7\le n\le N\), put \(Z=\log(X+1)\) and \(Y=\max\{2,Z\log^3(3(N+1))\}\).
At any seed counted here, \(\beta\) has degree \(d\le N+1\), and \(\log M(\beta)=\log r/p<Z/p\).
The [explicit logarithmic Mahler bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(p\le Y\), so (1) gives \(\beta<5Y\).
For fixed \(n\), take \(N=n\).
A monic reciprocal Salem polynomial of degree \(d\) has \(d/2\) free coefficients, each of absolute value at most \(2^d\beta\) by its root moduli.
This gives \(O_n(Y^{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor})\) possible \(\beta\).
For each sign, delay, \(\beta\) and \(p\), the seed is uniquely determined by
\[
                         a=\frac{r^{n+1}-\epsilon}{r^n-\epsilon r},
                         \qquad r=\beta^p>1.
\]
Summing over the at most \(Y\) prime choices proves the fixed-delay count.

For varying \(N\), the [uniform Salem count](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#statement) bounds the number of \(\beta\) of degree through \(N+1\) by \((N+1)(5Y)^{(N+1)/2}\exp(O(N+1))\).
Multiplying by \(2NY\) accounts for the signs, delays and primes.
When \(N=o(\log X/\log\log X)\), we have \(\log Y=O(\log\log X)\) for large \(X\).
The logarithm of this upper bound is \(o(\log X)\), giving the claimed \(X^{o(1)}\) estimate.
