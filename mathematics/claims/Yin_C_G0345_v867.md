# Coefficient growth and exponent span of a nonreciprocal four-term multiple

<a id="statement"></a>
## A height threshold independent of the exponents

Let \(P\) be a four-term integer polynomial, normalized to primitive coefficients and with its lowest monomial removed.
Write \(\mathcal H\ge1\) for the maximum of its four absolute coefficients and assume \(P^*(X)=X^{\deg P}P(X^{-1})\ne\pm P(X)\).
Write its exponents as \(0,gk_1,gk_2,gW\), where \(0<k_1<k_2<W\) and \(\gcd(k_1,k_2,W)=1\).
Let \(z\) be a nonzero nontorsion root, \(d=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\), \(m=d h(z)\) its logarithmic Mahler measure, and \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).

For every fixed \(0<\rho<1/2\), if \(z\) is reciprocal then
\[
 \min_{j\ge1}[H(z^j):H]\ll_\rho (1+\log\mathcal H)^{1/\rho}.
\]
For all such parents and all their nontorsion roots one has
\[
 m\gg \frac{(\log\log(5(1+\log\mathcal H)))^3}{(\log(2(1+\log\mathcal H)))^3}.
\]
In particular the coefficient height along a shrinking sequence satisfies
\[
 \mathcal H\ge \exp\!\left(\exp\!\left(c m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\right)\right)
\]
eventually, with an absolute \(c>0\).
This quantifies the [bounded-coefficient four-term gap](Yin_C_G0345_v866.md#parents).

For every \(0<\rho<1/2\) there is a threshold \(b_\rho>0\), depending only on \(\rho\), such that \(0<m<b_\rho\) also implies
\[
 \log\mathcal H\gg_\rho W^\rho,\qquad
 W\gg_\rho \left(\frac{d}{g m}\right)^{1/(1-\rho)} .
\]
Thus, for fixed \(C>0\) and \(0\le\tau<1/2\), every positive-measure integral factor of every such parent satisfying \(\log\mathcal H\le C W^\tau\) has a uniform positive lower bound depending only on \(C,\tau\).
For a primitive exponent pattern the power of \(d/m\) in the necessary span condition can be taken arbitrarily close to two from below.
Reciprocal and antireciprocal parents are outside the hypothesis.

<a id="intersection"></a>
## A hyperplane and its inverse

Put \(\beta=z^g\), \(u=(k_1,k_2,W)\) and \(x=\beta^u\).
In exponent order, set \(L(X)=a_0+a_1X_1+a_2X_2+a_3X_3\).
If \(z^{-1}\) is conjugate to \(z\), the rational equations \(L(X)=L(X^{-1})=0\) hold at \(x\).
Both Laurent polynomials are absolutely irreducible, and their Newton simplices are not translates of one another, so the common zero set has codimension at least two.
After clearing monomials these equations have ordinary degree at most three and logarithmic coefficient height \(\log\mathcal H+O(1)\).

If a positive-dimensional translate of a subtorus through \(x\) lies in that intersection, split the four terms of \(L\) by their restricted characters on the translate.
Distinct characters are linearly independent as functions on the subtorus, so the terms in each class sum to zero there.
They cannot all have the same character since their exponent differences span the full character lattice.
As no class can be a singleton, there are two pairs.
Evaluation at \(x\) gives \(\beta^{u_i-u_j}\in\mathbb Q^\times\) for one pair, with \(u_0=0\) and \(u_i-u_j\ne0\).
Thus some positive power of \(z\) has relative degree one.

Otherwise \(x\) is outside the positive-dimensional translates contained in any geometric component of the intersection through \(x\).
Apply [Amoroso, Sombra and Zannier, Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U001.md#intersection) in dimension three over \(\mathbb Q\) with \(\eta=1-2\rho\).
The vector \(u\) is primitive and \(x\) lies on the one-parameter subgroup with that direction.
The theorem gives either \(W^\rho\ll_\rho 1+\log\mathcal H\) or a nonzero vector \(v\in\mathbb Z^3\) of norm bounded solely in terms of \(\rho\) with \(v\cdot u=0\).
In the first case \([H(\beta):H]\le W\) by the primitive four-term equation.
In the second, appending \(-\sum v_i\) at the zeroth term and dividing by the common divisor gives a primitive affine relation of bounded norm among the four exponents.

If this relation is not a permutation of \((1,1,-1,-1)\), the [four-term area estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v866.md#statement) gives bounded relative degree for some power of \(z\).
In the remaining case the [unit-square argument](Yin_C_G0345_v866.md#parents) bounds the least relative degree by two whenever \(P^*\ne\pm P\).
Indeed a reducible bilinear polynomial splits into two binomials, and an irreducible bilinear polynomial can share its inverse only when its two pairs of opposite coefficients agree up to the same sign.
Thus either a power has relative degree \(O_\rho(1)\) or \(W^\rho\ll_\rho1+\log\mathcal H\).
This gives the first degree assertion.

<a id="measure"></a>
## The measure and the height of the primitive coefficient vector

For sufficiently small positive \(m\), the leading and constant coefficients of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(z\) have absolute value one.
[Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) makes \(z\) reciprocal in this range.
If any positive power of \(z\) has relative degree \(D\), the [cyclic compression bound](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#compression) gives \(m\gg G_3(D)\) in the integral range \(m\le1\), where \(G_3(t)=(\log\log(5t)/\log(2t))^3\).
The [normalized-width argument](Yin_C_G0345_v864.md#width) gives \(G_3(D)\gg G_3(B)\) for \(1\le D\le B\).
Taking \(\rho=1/4\) in the first assertion proves the displayed measure bound, since \(G_3(Ct^4)\asymp_C G_3(t)\) for \(t\ge1\) and fixed \(C\ge1\).
Larger \(m\) satisfies the bound after reducing the absolute constant, as \(G_3\) is bounded above.
For \(t=1+\log\mathcal H\) and \(m\to0\) this forces \(\log(2t)/\log\log(5t)\gg m^{-1/3}\).
First \(\log(2t)\gg m^{-1/3}\), and substituting this into the iterated logarithm gives the double exponential after adjusting \(c\).

Fix \(\rho\) and take \(b_\rho\) small enough to exclude the bounded-degree branch and [all one-, two- and three-term rational annihilators](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement).
The four elements \(1,\beta^{k_1},\beta^{k_2},\beta^W\) have rank exactly three over \(\mathbb Q\).
Their given relation shows the rank is at most three, and a dependence on a proper subset would give a polynomial with at most three nonzero integer coefficients annihilating \(z\) by the substitution \(X\mapsto X^g\).

In a normal closure the matrix of the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) applied to these four elements also has rank three, since the separable embedding matrix on a rational basis is invertible.
Choose three independent rows.
Their signed maximal minors span the kernel and represent the primitive coefficient vector \((a_0:a_1:a_2:a_3)\).
At each place a three-by-three determinant is bounded by the product of the three maximum row norms, with an additional factor at most six at archimedean places in the usual absolute values.
Each row has absolute projective height \(W h(\beta)\) by the product formula and the two extreme exponents.
Thus the height of the primitive integer coefficient vector satisfies
\[
                  \log\mathcal H\le 3W h(\beta)+\log6 .
\]
As \([H(\beta):H]\le W\), further reducing \(b_\rho\) can require \(W\) to exceed any fixed threshold by the compression bound.
The surviving inequality \(W^\rho\ll_\rho1+\log\mathcal H\) therefore gives both \(\log\mathcal H\gg_\rho W^\rho\) and \(h(\beta)\gg_\rho W^{\rho-1}\).
Substituting \(h(\beta)=gm/d\) proves the necessary span estimate.

Finally choose \(\tau<\rho<1/2\).
Under the coefficient growth hypothesis the small-measure inequality bounds \(W\) in terms of \(C,\tau\), taking \(\rho=(\tau+1/2)/2\).
Compression then supplies a uniform positive gap.
An integral divisor with positive measure either has nontrivial content or contains a positive-measure irreducible factor.
