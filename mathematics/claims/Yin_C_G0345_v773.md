# Binary collisions at critical span without a test of prescribed sparsity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reciprocal units with shrinking house and a binary entropy deficit

For each fixed integer \(r\ge2\) there is a sequence of positive reciprocal algebraic units \(\gamma_n>1\) of degrees \(d_n\to\infty\), depending on \(r\), with the following properties.
Their houses, the largest moduli of their conjugates, tend to one, and the proportion of their conjugates on the unit circle tends to one.
Their absolute heights tend to zero, while their total logarithmic Mahler measures satisfy
\[
                0<c_r\le m(\gamma_n)=d_n h(\gamma_n)\le C_r<\infty .
\]
Each \(\gamma_n\) is a zero of a nonzero integer polynomial with coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), a fixed number \(S\) of nonzero coefficients, and span \(T_n\) satisfying \(T_n h(\gamma_n)\asymp_r1\).
The exponential growth rate of its length-\(N\) binary sumsets is at most \(\log 2-\delta_r\) for one \(\delta_r>0\) independent of \(n\).

Nevertheless, for every fixed \(C\ge0\) and \(0<\eta\le1\), some \(\epsilon>0\) has the following property for all sufficiently large \(n\).
For any exponents \(0=a_1<\cdots<a_s=W\), \(1\le s\le r\), with \(Wh(\gamma_n)\le C\), and any complex column \(c\) of Euclidean norm one, fewer than \(\eta d_n\) embeddings satisfy
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=1}^s c_j(\sigma\gamma_n)^{a_j}\right|
       \le\epsilon\left(\sum_{j=1}^s|(\sigma\gamma_n)^{a_j}|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
This also excludes exact rational annihilators of at most \(r\) terms at these spans and excludes a positive density of neutral conjugates at vanishing absolute thresholds.
The house, binary collisions and bounded positive measure in this construction leave the case \(m(\gamma_n)\to0\) open.

<a id="seed"></a>
## A signed polynomial with several positive conjugates

We construct a monic irreducible integer polynomial \(P\) of odd prime degree \(D\) with constant coefficient one, all other coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), at least \(r\) distinct positive roots exceeding one and at least one nonreal root.
Take a sufficiently large prime \(D\) and set \(N_j=\lfloor D^{j/(r+2)}\rfloor\) for \(1\le j\le r+1\).
The intervals of integers \([N_j/100,100N_j]\) are disjoint and lie strictly between zero and \(D\) for large \(D\).
There is a monic irreducible binary polynomial of degree \(D\) whose support in every interval \([N_j/2,N_j]\) has at least \(N_j/10\) elements.
Indeed the proportion of irreducibles among monic binary polynomials of prime degree \(D\) is \((2^D-2)/(D2^D)\), by counting degree-\(D\) elements of \(\mathbb F_{2^D}\).
For a uniform such monic polynomial before the irreducibility restriction, the \(b\) coefficients in one interval are independent fair bits.
If \(Z\) is their sum, then \(\mathbb P(Z\le b/4)\le(2^{1/4}\,3/4)^b\) by applying Markov's inequality to \(2^{-Z}\).
Here \(b\ge N_j/2\), so failure of the required count at one of the finitely many intervals has total probability \(o(1/D)\).

Lift this support to an integer polynomial \(P_0\) with alternating choices of a constant sign on successive blocks \([N_j/100,100N_j]\) and coefficient \(+1\) at every other occupied position.
Its leading and constant coefficients are one and irreducibility follows from reduction modulo two.
At \(t_j=e^{-1/N_j}\), the designated block contributes at least \(e^{-1}N_j/10\) in absolute value with its assigned sign.
All terms outside that block together contribute at most \(N_j/100+1+2N_j e^{-100}\), using the geometric tail.
Thus the signs alternate at the increasing \(t_j\in(0,1)\), giving \(r\) distinct positive roots in \((0,1)\).
Take \(P(X)=X^D P_0(1/X)\).
If all its roots were real, the constant coefficient would give \(\sum_\lambda \lambda^2\ge D\) by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
But the first two coefficient identities give \(\sum_\lambda\lambda^2=a_{D-1}^2-2a_{D-2}\le3\).
In particular every real root \(\lambda>1\) of \(P\) generates a prime-degree field which is not totally real.
The polynomials \(P\) and \(P^*=P_0\) are coprime, since an odd-degree reciprocal or antireciprocal polynomial has a root at \(1\) or \(-1\).
No zero of \(P\) is on the unit circle, since such a zero would also annul \(P^*\).

<a id="sections"></a>
## A critical binary relation and almost all conjugates on the circle

Fix one root \(\lambda>1\) of \(P\).
For every sufficiently large \(n>D\), choose a positive real root \(\beta_n>1\) of \(F_n=X^nP-P^*\) tending to \(\lambda\).
Such roots exist because \(X^{-n}F_n\) converges uniformly to \(P\) near its simple real zero on a closed interval in \((1,\infty)\).
The two coefficient blocks are disjoint.
The monic polynomial \(F_n\) has constant \(-1\), coefficient length \(S=2\|P\|_1\) and exactly \(S\) nonzero coefficients.
Every \(\beta_n\) is a unit with \(\log\beta_n\le m(\beta_n)\le m(F_n)\le\log S\).
A positive root larger than one cannot recur at two different \(n\), since the two equations would force \(P=P^*=0\) at that root.
Northcott's theorem therefore gives \([\mathbb Q(\beta_n):\mathbb Q]\to\infty\).
The fixed rational map \(P^*/P\) has degree \(D\); its height upper bound \(h(P^*(z)/P(z))\le D h(z)+O_P(1)\) follows by the triangle inequality on the two homogeneous coordinates at every place.
Thus \( (n-D)h(\beta_n)\ll_P1\), whereas \( (n+D)h(\beta_n)\ge m(\beta_n)\) by the degree bound.

On the unit circle \(P^*/P\) has modulus one, has neither poles nor zeros, and admits a smooth argument along one full turn with bounded derivative.
Let \(w\) be its winding number.
For \(n\) larger than that derivative bound the difference of the arguments of \(X^n\) and \(P^*/P\) increases strictly through \(2\pi(n-w)\).
This gives \(n-w\) simple unit-circle roots and hence only \(D+w\) off-circle roots of \(F_n\), counted with multiplicity.
The same constant bounds the number in the minimal polynomial of the selected \(\beta_n\), regardless of how \(F_n\) factors.
Its neutral proportion therefore tends to one.
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the existence of one unit-circle conjugate forces that minimal polynomial to be reciprocal by irreducibility and complex conjugation.
On \(K_n=\mathbb Q(\beta_n)\) there is consequently an involution \(\tau_n\) sending \(\beta_n\) to \(\beta_n^{-1}\).

<a id="fields"></a>
## Bounded subfields and the positive conjugates of the limit

Suppose a fixed number field \(E\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}\) is contained in infinitely many \(K_n\).
On that subsequence put \(L_n=E\,\tau_n(E)\subset K_n\).
There are finitely many conjugate images of \(E\), so after passage to a subsequence \(L_n=L\) is fixed and \(\tau_n|_L=\tau\) is fixed.
Every embedding \(\sigma\) of \(L\) has \([K_n:L]\to\infty\) extensions to \(K_n\).
Only \(O_P(1)\) embeddings of the entire \(K_n\) are nonneutral on \(\beta_n\), so at least one extension \(\widetilde\sigma\) above each \(\sigma\) is neutral.
On such an embedding \(\widetilde\sigma\circ\tau_n=\overline{\widetilde\sigma}\) on \(K_n\), since they agree on its generator.
It follows that \(\overline{\sigma x}=\sigma\tau x\) for every \(x\in L\) and every embedding \(\sigma\).
Complex conjugation is central in the Galois group of the normal closure \(L'\) of \(L\): on each generator \(\sigma x\), composing any automorphism \(g\) with conjugation in either order gives \(g\sigma\tau x\).
In particular every real subfield of \(L'\) is totally real.

The field \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) has prime degree and is real with a nonreal conjugate.
Its intersection with \(L'\) is therefore \(\mathbb Q\).
Since \(L'/\mathbb Q\) is Galois, the two fields are linearly disjoint and all \(D\) conjugates of \(\lambda\) remain conjugate over \(L'\), hence also over \(E\).
In particular \(\lambda\) cannot annul any nonzero polynomial over \(E\) with at most \(r\) terms.
Otherwise that polynomial would vanish at all the \(r\) positive roots of \(P\).
Multiplying by a suitable complex scalar and taking real parts gives a nonzero real polynomial with at most \(r\) terms vanishing at these same positive numbers, contrary to Descartes' rule of signs.

Now consider a relation for \(\beta_n\) with at most \(r\) terms, coefficients in the fixed \(E\subset K_n\) and a fixed finite set of possible coefficient tuples, while the exponents may vary freely.
No such nonzero relation can hold on an infinite subsequence.
Indeed retain one tuple with nonzero coefficients, order the exponents, and divide out the highest power of \(\beta_n\).
After a further subsequence each difference from the largest exponent is either a fixed nonnegative integer or tends to infinity.
Since \(\beta_n\to\lambda>1\), precisely the bounded differences survive in the limit, including the zero difference.
Their distinct exponents give a nonzero Laurent polynomial over \(E\) with at most \(r\) terms vanishing at \(\lambda\), which was excluded above.

## Shrinking every conjugate modulus and excluding sparse tests

Choose primes \(k_n\to\infty\) coprime to \(R_S\) of the [bounded-length radical theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement), and put \(\gamma_n=\beta_n^{1/k_n}>0\).
Then \([\mathbb Q(\gamma_n):K_n]=k_n\) and \(m(\gamma_n)=m(\beta_n)\).
Every conjugate of \(\beta_n\) has all \(k_n\) distinct preimages among the conjugates of \(\gamma_n\), so the neutral proportion is preserved and the new units are reciprocal.
The roots of every monic \(F_n\) have modulus at most two by the geometric series bound for coefficients of absolute value at most one.
Hence the house of \(\gamma_n\) is at most \(2^{1/k_n}\).
The signed polynomial \(F_n(X^{k_n})\) now has span \(T_n=k_n(n+D)\) and \(T_n h(\gamma_n)=(n+D)h(\beta_n)\).

For prescribed \(C,\eta\) apply the [critical small-value reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#statement) for each cardinality \(2\le s\le r\), using the smallest of its positive thresholds and choosing it less than one to cover a monomial.
If the stated small-value test were to hold infinitely often, there would be a fixed number field \(E\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma_n)\) of bounded degree and a fixed nonzero coefficient tuple over \(E\) giving an exact relation on a subsequence.
Discard terms with zero coefficients.
For all large \(n\), \([E:\mathbb Q]<k_n\), so \([E K_n:K_n]\le[E:\mathbb Q]<k_n\).
It divides the prime \([\mathbb Q(\gamma_n):K_n]=k_n\), which forces \(E\subset K_n\).
Group the relation by the original exponents modulo \(k_n\).
For each residue \(b\) its contribution has the form \(\gamma_n^b Q_{n,b}(\beta_n)\) with coefficients in the fixed tuple.
The powers \(1,\gamma_n,\ldots,\gamma_n^{k_n-1}\) are independent over \(K_n\), so \(Q_{n,b}(\beta_n)=0\) for each occupied residue.
Within an occupied residue the quotient exponents are distinct, giving a nonzero polynomial with at most \(r\) terms, and there are only finitely many possible ordered coefficient subtuples.
The preceding limiting argument excludes this on every infinite subsequence, proving the claimed absence of critical tests.

<a id="entropy"></a>
## An actual deficit in binary trajectory growth

For fixed \(n\), let \(D_N(\gamma_n)\) count the distinct sums \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}e_i\gamma_n^i\) with \(e_i\in\{0,1\}\).
The \(S\)-point support of \(F_n(X^{k_n})\) gives two distinct binary assignments with the same sum, by separating the positive and negative coefficients.
Among translates of this support contained in \([0,N-1]\), a greedy selection finds at least \((N-T_n)/S^2\) disjoint translates for \(N>T_n\).
Indeed each selected translate excludes at most \(S^2\) shifts, one for each difference of support elements.
Each selected block has at most \(2^S-1\) distinct sums and each remaining position has two choices.
Consequently
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log D_N(\gamma_n)}N
   \le\log2+\frac1{S^2}\log(1-2^{-S})<\log2 .
\]
This compares one specified binary trajectory with sparse forms on the complete conjugate orbit.
The total algebraic entropy of the unit remains bounded below by the positive measure in the statement.
