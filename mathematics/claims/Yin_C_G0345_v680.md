# Every power substitution preserves the irreducible Salem factor in a family approaching two

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility and total Mahler measure

For \(n\ge4\), let \(r_n\in(1,2)\) be the unique exterior root of \(F_n(X)=X^n+1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X^i\), and write \(P_n\) for its minimal polynomial.
Then
\[
 F_n(X)=P_n(X)\,\Phi_2(X)^{[n\ {\rm odd}]}\,
                      \Phi_6(X)^{[n\equiv2\pmod6]} .                 \tag{1}
\]
For every integer \(q\ge1\), \(P_n(X^q)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(M(P_n(X^q))=r_n\).
In particular \(r_n\) is never a proper positive integer power of a Salem number.
These results settle the [inverse-power question for this family](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v305.md#digits).
The real root \(r_n^{1/q}\) can approach one, while its minimal polynomial has \(q\) exterior roots, each of modulus \(r_n^{1/q}\).
The other factors of \(F_n(X^q)\) are cyclotomic.

<a id="factors"></a>
## The cyclotomic factors before substitution

The [circle-root count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier) gives exactly two roots of \(F_n\) off the unit circle, \(r_n\) and its reciprocal, both in the same Salem minimal polynomial.
Indeed \((X-1)F_n=X^n(X-2)+2X-1\), and the phase derivative of \((2z-1)/(2-z)\) on the circle is \(3/|2-z|^2\le3<n\).
Thus all the circle roots are simple.
Every remaining irreducible factor is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

If \(\zeta\) is a root of unity of order \(j>1\) with \(F_n(\zeta)=0\), then \(2-\zeta\) divides \(2\zeta-1\) in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\), hence divides \(3=2(2-\zeta)+(2\zeta-1)\).
Its norm \(\Phi_j(2)\) is therefore a power of three, and \(\Phi_j(2)>1\) since \(|2-\zeta'|>1\) at all its conjugates.
Write \(j=3^a m\) with \(3\nmid m\).
Modulo three, the roots of \(\Phi_j\) are the primitive \(m\)-th roots, by separability of \(X^m-1\) and, for \(a>0\), the identity \(\Phi_{3^am}(X)=\Phi_m(X^{3^a})/\Phi_m(X^{3^{a-1}})\).
Since \(2=-1\) in this field, \(m=2\).
For \(a\ge1\), \(\Phi_{2\cdot3^a}(2)=b^2-b+1\) with \(b=2^{3^{a-1}}\equiv-1\bmod3\).
This integer is three modulo nine and exceeds three for \(a\ge2\).
The only remaining orders are two and six.
Directly \(F_n(-1)=0\) exactly for odd \(n\).
For a primitive sixth root \(\zeta\), the quotient \((2\zeta-1)/(2-\zeta)=\zeta^2\), giving precisely \(n\equiv2\bmod6\).
Also \(F_n(1)=3-n\ne0\), proving (1).

<a id="congruence"></a>
## A necessary congruence for inverse prime powers

For a monic integer polynomial \(A\), let \(A^{[p]}\) denote the monic polynomial whose roots are the \(p\)-th powers of its roots, counted with multiplicity.
If \(P=S^{[p]}\) for a monic integer polynomial \(S\) of the same degree and a prime \(p\), then
\[
                             P^{[p]}\equiv P\pmod{p^2}.                \tag{2}
\]
Indeed each coefficient of \(A^{[p]}\) is a universal integer polynomial in the coefficients of \(A\), by the fundamental theorem on symmetric polynomials.
Modulo \(p\) this universal polynomial is the \(p\)-th power of the corresponding coefficient, by Frobenius on the elementary symmetric polynomials of the roots.
For integer coefficient vectors congruent modulo \(p\), their images are therefore congruent modulo \(p^2\), since \(a^p\equiv b^p\bmod p^2\) when \(a\equiv b\bmod p\).
Now \(P\equiv S\bmod p\), so \(P^{[p]}\equiv S^{[p]}=P\bmod p^2\).
Newton sums are integer polynomials in the coefficients.
Consequently (2) forces \(s_{pj}(P)\equiv s_j(P)\bmod p^2\) at every \(j\ge1\), where \(s_j\) is the sum of the \(j\)-th powers of the roots.

If \(r_n=\beta^p\) for a Salem number \(\beta\), the fields of \(\beta\) and \(\beta^p\) agree.
Every embedding fixing \(\beta^p\) must send \(\beta\) to its unique conjugate of modulus \(\beta>1\).
Thus \(P_n=S^{[p]}\) for the minimal polynomial \(S\) of \(\beta\).
For \(p\ge5\), the two possible cyclotomic factors in (1) are fixed by \(p\)-th powering on their roots, so (2) also gives \(F_n^{[p]}\equiv F_n\bmod p^2\).

<a id="small"></a>
## Excluding prime exponents below the family index

For \(p=2\), (2) yields \(P_n(X^2)\equiv P_n(X)P_n(-X)\bmod4\), since \(d=\deg P_n\) is even.
Write \(a_j\) for the descending coefficient at degree \(d-j\), with \(a_0=1\).
Comparison at degrees \(2d-2\) and \(2d-4\) gives
\[
           a_1\equiv 2a_2-a_1^2,\qquad
           a_2\equiv2a_4-2a_1a_3+a_2^2\pmod4 .
\]
For \(P_n=F_n\), \(a_1=a_2=-1\) violates the first congruence.
For odd \(n\), division by \(X+1\) gives \((a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(-2,1,-2,1)\), violating the second.
For \(n\equiv2\bmod6\), division by \(X^2-X+1\) gives \((0,-2,-3,-2)\), again violating the second.

Write \(s_j=s_j(F_n)\).
The reciprocal generating function gives
\[
  \log F_n(z)=\log(1-2z)-\log(1-z)
                +\log\!\left(1+z^n\frac{2-z}{1-2z}\right)
             =-\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{s_j}{j}z^j .
\]
Hence \(s_j=2^j-1\) for \(1\le j<n\), while for \(n\le j<2n\),
\[
            s_j=2^j-1-jc_{j-n},\qquad c_0=2,\quad
                          c_a=3\cdot2^{a-1}\quad(a\ge1).              \tag{3}
\]
For \(p=3\), \(s_3-s_1=6\) excludes this exponent unless \(P_n=F_n/\Phi_6\), since cubing fixes the possible root \(-1\).
In that case \(n\ge8\) and \(s_6-s_2=60\), of which the primitive sixth roots account for \(2-(-1)=3\).
The difference \(57\) for \(P_n\) again violates (2).

Now let \(5\le p<n\) be prime.
If \(2^p\not\equiv2\bmod p^2\), the difference \(s_p-s_1\) already violates (2).
Otherwise choose \(j\) with \(n\le pj<2n\) and \(p\nmid j\).
For \(p<n<2p\) take \(j=2\), and for \(n\ge2p\) the half-open interval \([n/p,2n/p)\) contains two consecutive integers.
Then \(j<n\), and (3) gives
\[
              s_{pj}-s_j\equiv-pj c_{pj-n}\not\equiv0\pmod{p^2}.
\]
This also excludes primes satisfying \(2^p\equiv2\bmod p^2\).

<a id="remaining"></a>
## The large exponents and the finite range

If \(r_n=\beta^p\) with \(\beta\) Salem, then \(d=\deg\beta=\deg P_n\le n\) and \(\log M(\beta)=\log(r_n)/p\).
The [explicit Mahler bound of Voutier, Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives
\[
                  p<\tfrac12(\log 2)(\log(3n))^3 .                    \tag{4}
\]
Here the cited theorem is used at its inspected statement scope.
For \(n\ge40\) the right side is smaller than \(n\), since \(t/(\log(3t))^3\) is increasing there and \((0.7/2)(4.8)^3<40\) with \(\log120<4.8\).
For \(4\le n\le39\) the same upper estimate is below 39, leaving only primes \(n\le p\le37\).

The following finite Newton-sum computation checks all 162 pairs in this enlarged range using \(F_n\), since every such prime is at least five.
For 154 pairs \(s_p\ne s_1\bmod p^2\).
The eight pairs that agree at \(j=1\) are
\[
  (11,13),(17,19),(18,31),(23,31),(25,37),(28,31),(29,29),(37,37).
\]
All eight have \(s_{2p}\ne s_2\bmod p^2\).
The [executable Newton-sum check](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0181/readme.md#traces) gives the coefficients, recurrence and exact range.

<a id="substitution"></a>
## Radical extensions and the exterior roots

Fix \(K=\mathbb Q(r_n)\) in its distinguished real embedding.
No prime radical \(r_n^{1/p}>0\) belongs to \(K\).
Indeed such a radical would generate \(K\), be an algebraic integer, and have one exterior conjugate, since its \(p\)-th powers give the distinct conjugates of \(r_n\).
It is a unit and has a neutral conjugate because \(r_n\) is Salem.
The inverse of a neutral conjugate is its complex conjugate, so the reciprocal minimal polynomial shares a root with the minimal polynomial.
Irreducibility forces reciprocity and identifies the positive radical as a Salem number, which the preceding congruences exclude.

Let \(q\ge2\).
If \(X^q-r_n\) had a proper monic factor over \(K\) of positive degree \(t<q\), its constant coefficient would have modulus \(r_n^{t/q}\), as every complex root has modulus \(r_n^{1/q}\).
Since this coefficient lies in the real field \(K\), its absolute value belongs to \(K\).
For \(g=\gcd(q,t)\), Bézout's identity gives \(r_n^{g/q}\in K\) using \(r_n\) and \(r_n^{t/q}\).
Raising it to \((q/g)/p\) for any prime \(p\mid q/g\) would put \(r_n^{1/p}\) in \(K\).
Thus \(X^q-r_n\) is irreducible over \(K\), and the tower degree \(q\deg P_n\) proves irreducibility of \(P_n(X^q)\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Each conjugate of \(r_n\) has \(q\) distinct \(q\)-th roots of equal modulus, giving the stated exterior root count and measure.
The substituted cyclotomic factors in (1) have only roots of unity.
