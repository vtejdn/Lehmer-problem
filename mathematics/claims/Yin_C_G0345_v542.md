# Sparse Mahler measure and directional widths after removal of torsion hypersurfaces

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower bound in terms of the actual coefficients

For a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(F=\sum_{a\in A}c_a X^a\) on \((\mathbb C^*)^r\), put
\[
 K(F)=\frac{(\sum_{a\in A}|c_a|)^2}{\sum_{a\in A}|c_a|^2},\qquad
 W_v(F)=\max_{a\in A}a\cdot v-\min_{a\in A}a\cdot v .
\]
Separate off all factors \(\Phi_j(X^u)\) with primitive integer \(u\ne0\), including repetitions, and denote the remaining integral Laurent polynomial by \(R\), up to a Laurent monomial unit.
If \(|A|\ge2\), the absolute constant \(c>0\) from the [univariate factor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) satisfies
\[
 m(F)\ \ge\ \frac{c}{K(F)^2}\sup_{\substack{v\in\mathbb Z^r\ \mathrm{primitive}\\ W_v(F)>0}}
                    \frac{W_v(R)}{W_v(F)}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular, every nonmonomial \(F\) with no torsion hypersurface component satisfies \(m(F)\ge c/K(F)^2\ge c/|A|^2\), including products of any number of nontorsion irreducible factors.
The same full bound holds if some primitive direction is orthogonal to all removed factor directions and has positive width on \(R\).
Applying the full bound to \(R\) itself also gives \(m(F)\ge c/K(R)^2\) whenever \(R\) is nonmonomial, since each removed factor has measure zero by Haar integration in primitive monomial coordinates.
For a scalar times a monomial the exact measure is \(\log|c_a|\); for a scalar times a product of the removed factors and a monomial it is the logarithm of the absolute scalar.
Both estimates can tend to zero on a family with only six original monomials.
For \(L\ge3\) put \(F_L=(X^{L^2}-1)(X^L-2X+1)\).
At a root of unity of the second factor the equality \(2X=X^L+1\) forces \(X=X^L=1\) by equality in the triangle inequality, and the root at one is simple since the derivative there is \(L-2\).
Thus \(R_L=(X^L-2X+1)/(X-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}X^j-2\) has \(K(R_L)=L\), whereas \(W_1(R_L)/W_1(F_L)=(L-1)/(L^2+L)\).
The full polynomial has fixed \(K(F_L)\) because its two disjoint coefficient blocks have absolute coefficients \(1,2,1\).
Consequently neither right-hand side supplies a uniform positive lower bound for these six-term inputs.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Torsion roots of a one-parameter specialization

Suppose \(r\ge2\) and let \(G\) be an integral Laurent polynomial with at least two terms which has none of the indicated factors.
Set \(n=(1,N,\ldots,N^{r-1})\) and \(G_N(T)=G(T^{n_1},\ldots,T^{n_r})\).
Distinct support points give distinct exponents for large \(N\), since each difference evaluates to a nonzero polynomial in \(N\).
The number of roots of unity of \(G_N\), counted with multiplicity, is \(O_G(N^{r-2})\) with the coordinate system fixed.

Indeed at a torsion root \(\zeta\), partition the terms into inclusion-minimal vanishing subsums.
Take all differences of support points within each block, and write \(D\) for their union.
This set contains a nonzero vector because no singleton vanishes.
If its rational rank were one, write every difference as an integral multiple of one primitive \(u\).
Put \(\omega=\zeta^{u\cdot n}\).
In each block divide by the monomial at one of its support points; the remaining expression is a Laurent polynomial in \(X^u\) vanishing at \(\omega\).
It follows that the whole polynomial vanishes on \(X^u=\omega\).
In unimodular coordinates making \(X^u\) the first variable, division in that variable shows that \(X^u-\omega\) divides \(G\) over \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\).
Galois conjugation then gives the integral Laurent divisor \(\Phi_{\operatorname{ord}(\omega)}(X^u)\), contradicting the hypothesis.
This argument also identifies the listed rational factors with all codimension-one torsion cosets contained in the zero set.

Choose independent \(u,v\in D\), possibly from different blocks, and put \(f_w(Z)=\sum_{i=1}^r w_i Z^{i-1}\).
By the [rational minimal-relation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#orders), the order of \(\zeta\) divides both \(M f_u(N)\) and \(M f_v(N)\), where \(M\) is the product of the primes at most the number of terms of \(G\).
The coordinate polynomials are linearly independent, so their primitive polynomial gcd \(g\) in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) has degree at most \(r-2\).
After dividing by \(g\), the two integral quotients are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Z]\) and admit a Bézout identity equal to a fixed nonzero integer after clearing denominators.
Hence \(\gcd(f_u(N),f_v(N))\ll_{u,v}|g(N)|\ll_{u,v}N^{r-2}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
For this pair all possible roots thus lie in a single cyclic group of order \(M\gcd(|f_u(N)|,|f_v(N)|)\).
There are only finitely many pairs in the fixed support difference set.
Finally each nonzero root has multiplicity at most the number of terms minus one by the [Euler-derivative Vandermonde argument](Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#multiplicity).
This proves the claimed bound with multiplicities.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Recovering the directional degree share

Fix a primitive \(v\) with \(W_v(F)>0\).
Complete \(v\) as the last row of a unimodular change on exponent vectors, so the last coordinate of each new support vector is its old pairing with \(v\).
This preserves \(K(F)\), toric divisibility and Haar measure.
Apply the preceding specialization in these coordinates to \(F\) and its factors.
For every fixed nonzero Laurent polynomial \(H\), the width of \(H_N\) is \(N^{r-1}W_v(H)+O_H(N^{r-2})\), using the support in the original coordinates to define \(W_v\).
After multiplying by a monomial to make the least exponent zero, width is ordinary degree and zero is not a root.
Width is additive under multiplication since the extreme faces of two nonzero Laurent polynomials multiply without annihilation.
For each removed factor, the specialized monomial has exponent \(u\cdot n\ne0\) for large \(N\) in the changed coordinates, so every specialized nonzero root of the factor is torsion.
All nontorsion roots of \(F_N\) therefore come from \(R_N\), with their multiplicities.
By the torsion count their number is \(N^{r-1}W_v(R)+O_F(N^{r-2})\); the same relation holds for monomial \(R\), with count zero.
All implied constants may depend on the fixed coordinates and direction.

Apply the [univariate factor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) to each primitive nontorsion irreducible factor of the normalized \(F_N\), using the coefficients of the *whole* specialization as its sparse relation.
Their measures add with multiplicities, and the nonzero integral content contributes nonnegative log measure.
The resulting bound is \(c/K(F)^2\) times the fraction of the degree occupied by nontorsion roots, because the specialization eventually preserves all the coefficients at distinct exponents.
The [Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(m(F_N)\to m(F)\): any finite set of nonzero integer relation vectors eventually has nonzero pairing with \(n\), by the same polynomial argument.
Taking the limit and then the supremum proves (1).
When \(r=1\), the degree of the specialized polynomial after monomial normalization is already \(W_1(F)\), and the nontorsion degree is exactly \(W_1(R)\).
Summing the same factor bound proves (1) directly.
