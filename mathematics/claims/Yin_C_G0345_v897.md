# The index of the short power and the trace factor at even long exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## Restrictions on every realizing polynomial

Let \(q,n\ge2,\ p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ L=p+q\).
Suppose the monic integral \(H\) has degree \(n\), one root \(w\) in \((0,1/M)\), its other roots distinct in \((-4,0)\), and \(M^nH(1/M)=L^2\).
Use the [Salem lift](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) and the [two coprime powers](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion)
\[
 T+T^{-1}=2+w,\qquad T=\beta^u,\qquad
 Y=\frac{pT-q}{p-qT}=\beta^v,\qquad \beta>1,\quad 1\le u<v .
\]
The following identities hold at every parity:
\[
 \mathbb Z[\beta]/\mathbb Z[T]\simeq\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z
 \quad\text{as additive groups},\qquad
 \mathbb Z[\beta+\beta^{-1}]=\mathbb Z[w],\qquad
 \gcd(u,\operatorname{disc} H)=1 .
\]
In particular \(u\ge2\).

For even \(v\), define the monic polynomial \(V_v\) by
\[
 X^v-1=(X^2-1)X^{(v-2)/2} V_v(X+X^{-1}-2).
\]
Over \(R=\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\) there is a monic \(J\) of degree \(n-1\) such that
\[
 H(W)=(W+4)J(W),\qquad J\mid V_v .
\]
Consequently \(v\ge2n\) and \(\gcd(L,4^n-1)\mid v\).
If \(3\mid L\), even \(v\) requires \(3\mid v\), while odd \(v\) requires \(3\mid u\) and \(3\nmid\operatorname{disc}H\), by the [odd-exponent obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#orders).
For instance, at \(q=40\) and even \(v\), the required divisor of \(v\) is \(\gcd(81,4^n-1)\), which equals \(81\) whenever \(27\mid n\).
These conditions apply across all separating grids.

<a id="index"></a>
## Adjoining the long power adds one cyclic quotient

Write \(d=2n,\ O=\mathbb Z[T],\ A=\mathbb Z[\beta]\), and put \(E=p-qT,\ E'=p+qY\).
The [pole identities](Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#split) give \(EE'=L\) and \(N(E)=L^2\).
Bézout's identity and the fact that inverses of algebraic units belong to their power orders give \(A=\mathbb Z[T,Y]\).
Both orders have rank \(d\), since \(Y\) is a rational function of \(T\).
At primes outside \(L\), the norm makes \(E\) invertible in the localization of \(O\), so \(Y\) already belongs to this order.

At a prime \(\ell\mid L\), write \(D=\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}\).
Here \(q\) is invertible, so \(DO=D[E]\) and \(DA=D[E,E']\).
The minimal polynomial of \(E\) has the form
\[
 f(Z)=Z^d+a_1 Z^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{d-1}Z+L^2 .
\]
The trace \(\operatorname{Tr}(E')=-a_{d-1}/L\) is integral.
The identity \(f(E)=0\) gives
\[
 E'=-\frac{E^{d-1}+a_1E^{d-2}+\cdots+a_{d-1}}L,\qquad
 (E')^2=-\bigl(E^{d-2}+a_1E^{d-3}+\cdots+a_{d-2}\bigr)
         -\frac{a_{d-1}}L E'.
\]
Together with \(EE'=L\), these identities show that \(DO+DE'\) is a ring and equals \(DA\).
In the power basis of \(E\) the leading coordinate of \(E'\) is \(-1/L\), so the class of \(E'\) has annihilator exactly \(LD\) in \(DA/DO\).
The class of \(Y=(E'-p)/q\) generates the same quotient.
Combining these local calculations gives \(A=O+\mathbb ZY\) and \(A/O\simeq R\).

<a id="real"></a>
## The two real power orders coincide

For an invertible element \(s\) write \(\tau(s)=s+s^{-1}\) and set \(B=\mathbb Z[w]\), \(\delta=1-Mw\).
This element has norm \(L^2\) in the trace field, so its characteristic polynomial gives \(L^2/\delta\in B\).
By the graph trace identity, \(w_Y=\tau(Y)-2=wL^2/\delta\) belongs to \(B\).
Reduction of \(\delta(L^2/\delta)=L^2\) modulo \(qB\) gives \(L^2/\delta\equiv1\), since \(\delta\equiv L^2\equiv1\).
Thus \(w_Y-w\in qB\).
The identities \(qTY=q+p(Y-T)\) and \(q(TY)^{-1}=q+p(Y^{-1}-T^{-1})\) now give \(\tau(TY)=2+(p/q)(w_Y-w)\in B\).

Knowing \(\tau(T),\tau(Y),\tau(TY)\) gives every \(\tau(T^aY^b)\) in their generated ring.
Indeed, apply the recurrence \(\tau(sr^{j+1})=\tau(r)\tau(sr^j)-\tau(sr^{j-1})\) in both directions, first along the rows \(b=0,1\) and then along each column in \(b\).
It follows by Bézout that \(z=\tau(\beta)\in B\).
Conversely \(\tau(T)=D_u(z)\), where \(D_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\) defines an integral polynomial, so \(B=\mathbb Z[z]\).

For each prime \(\ell\mid u\), one has \(D_u(z)=D_{u/\ell}(z)^\ell\) in \(B/\ell B\).
Since \(D_u(z)-2=w\) generates this algebra, Frobenius is surjective and, by finiteness, injective.
There are therefore no nonzero nilpotents.
The presentation \(B/\ell B=\mathbb F_\ell[W]/(H)\) shows that \(H\bmod\ell\) is squarefree, proving the discriminant assertion.

<a id="even"></a>
## The characteristic polynomial on the quotient by the other pole factor

The abelian group \(A/EA\) has order \(L^2\) by the norm and is killed by \(L\).
The image of \(T\) there is the scalar \(p/q=-1\) in \(R\), since \(q\) is a unit modulo \(L\).
Thus \(A=O+\mathbb ZY\) makes \(1,Y\) span \(A/EA\) over \(R\).
Counting elements proves \(A/EA\simeq R^2\).
In particular the integer Smith invariant factors of multiplication by \(E\) on \(A\) are \(1\) repeated \(d-2\) times and \(L\) repeated twice.
Since multiplication by \(E'\) is \(L\) times the inverse rational matrix, its Smith factors are \(1\) twice and \(L\) repeated \(d-2\) times.
Consequently \(A/E'A\) is free of rank \(d-2\) over \(R\).
Multiplication by \(E'\) gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A/EA\longrightarrow A/LA\longrightarrow A/E'A\longrightarrow0 .
\]
The quotient is free, hence the sequence splits as \(R\)-modules and the characteristic polynomial of \(T\) factors along it.

On \(A/E'A\) the element \(Y=\beta^v\) equals \(-p/q=1\) in \(R\).
It follows that \(T^v=1\) and that \(T\) generates this quotient as an algebra.
Its characteristic polynomial \(D_T\) therefore divides \(X^v-1\).
Indeed, in each local factor of \(R\) the first \(d-2\) powers of \(T\), starting at one, span by Cayley–Hamilton and form a basis since the module is free of that rank.
Monic division in this basis identifies the ideal of relations with \((D_T)\).
The characteristic polynomial of \(T\) on \(A/LA\) is consequently
\[
 X^n H(X+X^{-1}-2)=(X+1)^2D_T(X).
\]
Evaluation at \(-1\) yields \(H(-4)=0\) in \(R\), hence \(H(W)=(W+4)J(W)\) there and \(D_T=X^{n-1}J(X+X^{-1}-2)\).

If \(v\) is even, the monic cofactor \((X^v-1)/D_T\) is anti-reciprocal of even degree by reciprocity of \(D_T\).
It vanishes at \(1,-1\) since \(2\) is invertible in \(R\).
After dividing by \(X^2-1\) its remaining factor is reciprocal of even degree.
Pairing opposite Laurent powers after centering at the middle degree expresses the remaining factor as a power of \(X\) times a monic polynomial in \(W=X+X^{-1}-2\).
Applying the same pairing over \(\mathbb Z\) to the geometric sum \((X^v-1)/(X^2-1)\) defines \(V_v\).
Comparison gives \(J\mid V_v\), since substitution of \(W\) is injective by the highest Laurent coefficient.
Thus \(n-1\le(v-2)/2\), and \(V_v(-4)=(-1)^{(v-2)/2}v/2\) by the geometric sum at \(X=-1\).

Even \(v\) gives \(H(-4)=(-1)^nL^2\) over the integers by the [mixed-parity endpoint theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#parity).
Since \(1/M+4=L^2/M\), Taylor expansion of the pole equation divided by \(L^2\) gives
\[
 1\equiv (-M)^n+M^{n-1}H'(-4)\pmod L .
\]
Here \(-M\equiv 4^{-1}\pmod L\) and \(J(-4)=H'(-4)\) in \(R\).
It follows that \(\gcd(L,4^n-1)=\gcd(L,H'(-4))\) divides \(v/2\), and hence \(v\).
