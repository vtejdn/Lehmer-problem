# A sublinear obstruction modulo the branch index for positive-constant Pisot inputs

<a id="statement"></a>
## The branch index may grow

Consider any sequence of positive-constant Pisot inputs with constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\) and \(\theta-q\to0\).
Let \(j\ge1\) be the branch index, \(C\) the [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) with degree \(h\) and leading coefficient \(j\), and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
If the circle supremum \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\) stays bounded, some coefficient of \(\Pi\) eventually fails divisibility by \(j\), and its first such index \(D\) satisfies
\[
                              D/h\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equivalently, for every \(M<\infty\) and \(\eta>0\) there is a uniform positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\) under the two conditions
\[
     \|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M,\qquad
     [z^k]\Pi\in j\mathbb Z\quad(0\le k\le\lfloor\eta h\rfloor).
\]
The bound covers varying \(j\), arbitrarily large coefficient heights, and dense numerators under the displayed norm bound.
In particular the conclusion about \(D\) applies whenever the number of terms of \(\Pi\) stays bounded, uniformly in \(j\).
It uses the many positive atoms of the actual inverse measure.
The [abstract half-integral prefix theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#prefix) treats signed measures without this finite-input hypothesis.

<a id="integrality"></a>
## Long divisible prefixes give integer moments

Suppose both displayed conditions hold but \(\delta=\theta-q\to0\).
The [derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(j=\delta(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0))\) with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\), without a bounded-\(j\) hypothesis.
Thus \(q\to\infty\) and \(j/(q-1)\to0\).
The lifted monic denominator \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\) has measure strictly greater than one tending to one by the [delay-one construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
In particular \(h\to\infty\), since a bounded degree would itself give bounded support.
We have \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_q\), where \(J_q=j/K_q\) has constant one and its [signed measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_q\) has total mass one and negative atom of size \(j/[4(q-1)]\) at 1.
Its positive part charges both lifts of each of the \(h\) simple interior roots of \(C\), by the positive weights of the [inverse representation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse).
Since \(\Pi/j\) has unit constant and integer coefficients on the given prefix, division in the ring of truncated series gives \(j_l=[z^l]J_q\in\mathbb Z\) through that prefix.

<a id="atoms"></a>
## Integer norm zero would force too many roots

Test any one or two positive integer indices \(n_1,n_2=o(h)\) along the sequence.
As in the [moving-index torus limit](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#limit), extract the eventual zero relations of their integer combinations, with quotient character group \(\Gamma\) free abelian and nonzero.
The weak limit \(\mu\) of the images of \(2\omega_q^+\) on its dual torus has mass two and integer Fourier coefficients.
Indeed each nonzero character tests \(j_l\) at a positive index \(l=o(h)\) after taking the absolute value, and removal of the negative atom changes all coefficients by \(o(1)\).
We show that no nonzero integer Laurent polynomial on the torus can vanish \(\mu\)-almost everywhere.

If \(P\) were one, fix a nonzero character \(a\in\Gamma\) and put \(Q=(1-X^a)P\).
This is nonzero since the Laurent polynomial ring of \(\Gamma\) is an integral domain.
Substitute the actual indices in its finitely many characters using representatives in the original integer lattice, then shift exponents by a monomial so the least is zero.
The resulting integer polynomial \(Q_q\) is eventually nonzero, because the finitely many distinct characters have distinct exponents eventually.
Its degree is \(o(h)\) and \(Q_q(1)=0\).
The last condition gives the exact equality
\[
        2\int |Q_q|^2\,d\omega_q^+
                   =2\int |Q_q|^2\,d\omega_q\ \in\mathbb Z.
\]
Integrality follows by expanding the square, since each positive difference of exponents lies in the integral prefix and the zeroth doubled moment is two.
By convergence of the finitely many character moments these integers tend to \(\int|Q|^2\,d\mu=0\), so they eventually vanish.
Then \(Q_q\) vanishes at every one of the \(2h\) distinct interior lifts charged by \(\omega_q^+\), impossible for its degree.
This establishes the required nonvanishing property for all polynomial tests on the limiting torus.

<a id="model"></a>
## A sparse rational comparison for the actual integer prefix

Apply the [mass-two classification](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#classification) to each such \(\mu\).
Its hypotheses are precisely positivity and mass two, symmetric integer Fourier coefficients and the polynomial nonvanishing property just proved.
For one test index this bounds its moment by one in absolute value.
At two distinct, nonopposite characters with nonzero moments, the signs are those of a subgroup homomorphism to \(\{\pm1\}\).
Using the [uniform pair and difference argument](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#local), there is \(c>0\) on the sequence such that on a prefix of length \(\lfloor ch\rfloor\), \(J_q\) agrees with one of
\[
           J_*=1,\qquad 1+\epsilon z^r,\qquad (1-\epsilon z^r)^{-1},
                    \qquad \epsilon=\pm1,\quad 1\le r\le ch .
\]
Indeed if the one-index bound or the sum, difference and doubling laws failed on every such prefix, diagonal extraction would give violating indices \(o(h)\) within the original integral prefix, contrary to their torus limits.
With the local laws on a uniform prefix, subtraction of the least nonzero index and addition of its multiples give exactly these three models.
Each is the Herglotz transform of a probability and can be written \(V/U\) with \(U,V\) bounded on the circle by two and each either one or a unit binomial.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## The normalized numerator has bounded circle norm

Use the identity
\[
             UB^\sharp-V\Pi/j=U(\Pi/j)(J_q-J_*).
\]
The positive-index coefficients of the last difference are represented by measures of uniformly bounded variation.
The zero prefix has length comparable to \(h\), the degree on the left is \(O(h)\), and the other factors on the right have uniformly bounded suprema.
The [two bounded interval cutoffs](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) yield a uniform \(L^1\) bound for the left side by first matching the series through three times the degree bound and then cutting the product down to the actual polynomial.
The coefficients of the left side may be rational, as this norm estimate only uses the degrees, measures and circle norms.
Since \(V\Pi/j\) is bounded, this controls \(\|UB^\sharp\|_1\).
Now \(UB^\sharp\) has integer coefficients, hence bounded support by the [prime-grid support bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
Its Mahler measure equals \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) because \(U\) is one or cyclotomic up to sign.
Convergence to one contradicts the [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This proves the two equivalent assertions, treating a numerator entirely divisible by \(j\) as having infinite first exceptional index.
Finally all roots of \(\Pi\) are on the unit circle and its constant term is \(j\), so \(M(\Pi)=j\).
The [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds \(\|\Pi\|_\infty/j\) solely in terms of the number of terms.
