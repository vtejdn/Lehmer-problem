# Reflections forced on sparse supports by two double contacts

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coefficient congruences on prime progressions

Let \(P_1,P_2\) be the distinct monic minima of a reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\gamma\) and its square, both of degree \(d\).
For \(p>3\) prime and an integer width \(b\ge0\), put \(A^{*b}(Y)=Y^bA(Y^{-1})\) for rows \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\).
For each sign \(\sigma=\pm1\) under consideration assume
\[
       U(X)=(P_1(X)P_2(X))^2\mid A_\sigma(X^p)+\sigma X A_\sigma^{*b}(X^p).
\]
Choose any integer modulus \(q\ge2\).
For any degree bound \(D\) there is an effective positive integer \(T\), independent of the coefficients of the minima, with the following property simultaneously for every \(d\le D\).
If \(p\equiv a\bmod T\), where \(a=1\) or \(-1\), and \(b+1<4d\), then the rows satisfy the following Laurent polynomial identity over \(\mathbb Z/q\).
\[
             A_\sigma(Y)=-\sigma Y^{b+a} A_\sigma(Y^{-1}).                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus every coefficient lacking its reflected partner is divisible by \(q\).
For odd \(q\), the same divisibility holds for the central coefficient of a plus row.
For odd \(q\), each prime progression therefore excludes a primitive plus row supported on any set without a distinct pair summing to \(b+a\), at arbitrary coefficient height.
A primitive minus row must have such a pair or a central position.

For a genuine transverse pair as in the [determinant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement), both signs obey (1) with the same center.
Its nonzero determinant \(D_A=A_+A_-^{*b}+A_-A_+^{*b}\) has every coefficient divisible by \(q\), and
\[
                    2\|A_+\|_1\|A_-\|_1\ \ge\ q M(\gamma)^{6p}.            \tag{2}
\]
The [radical support restriction](#radicals) below also excludes unpaired residue classes for unbounded degrees over any fixed radical base at every prime.

<a id="progressions"></a>
## Specialization at the exponents one and minus one

In the finite ring \(R=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(U)\), the class \(x\) of \(X\) is a unit because \(U(0)=1\).
This ring has \(q^{4d}\) elements by monic division.
The order of \(x\) is at most \(q^{4D}\), so \(T=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,q^{4D})\) works, or one may use the actual order for a single seed.
At \(p\equiv1\bmod T\) the polynomial \(A_\sigma(X)+\sigma X A_\sigma^{*b}(X)\) vanishes in \(R\).
At \(p\equiv-1\bmod T\), multiply the specialized Laurent equation by \(X^b\) to obtain \(A_\sigma^{*b}(X)+\sigma X A_\sigma(X)=0\) in \(R\).
Both polynomials have degree at most \(b+1<\deg U\), so their coefficients vanish modulo \(q\), proving (1) in both cases.
The two progressions contain infinitely many primes by [Dirichlet's theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#sut21-u001).
For example, choosing \(D\) and odd \(q\) tending to infinity and then increasing primes in the corresponding class of one gives the exclusion simultaneously for all seeds up to the chosen degree on that subsequence.

With the convention that coefficients outside the row are zero, (1) gives \(c_j=-\sigma c_{b+a-j}\bmod q\).
At an integral fixed position the plus sign gives \(2c_j=0\bmod q\).
If all allowed coefficients vanish modulo \(q\), no primitive row exists, and dividing out the content of an integral row preserves its support and both double contacts.
For odd \(q\), among the \(\binom{b+1}{s}\) supports of exactly \(s\) positions the proportion still possible for the plus sign is at most \(\binom{s}{2}/b\) for \(b\ge1\).
Indeed two distinct random positions sum to the required value with probability at most \(1/b\).
The minus sign additionally allows the unique possible center, contributing at most \(s/(b+1)\).
For \(s=o(\sqrt b)\) both proportions tend to zero.

For both signs (1) implies \(D_A=0\bmod q\) by substitution in the Laurent polynomial ring.
Let \(Q\) be the product of the minima of \(\gamma^p\) and \(\gamma^{2p}\).
The [transverse determinant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement) gives \(Q^2\mid D_A\) and \(M(Q)=M(\gamma)^{3p}\).
Since \(Q^2\) is monic and \(D_A/q\) is a nonzero integral polynomial, its quotient by \(Q^2\) is integral and has Mahler measure at least one.
Now \(qM(Q)^2\le M(D_A)\le\|D_A\|_1\le2\|A_+\|_1\|A_-\|_1\), as required.

<a id="radicals"></a>
## Every occupied residue class has a partner for radical targets

Consider the [radical presentations](Yin_C_G0345_v486.md#radicals) with \(\gamma^k=\xi\), where \(k\) is odd, the minima of \(\xi,\xi^2\) are \(S_1,S_2\) of degree \(f\), and \(P_i(X)=S_i(X^k)\) have degree \(d=fk\).
Assume for the selected row that \(A(\gamma^p)\) and \(A(\gamma^{2p})\) are nonzero.
At every prime \(p>3\) and every \(b<4d\) its set \(J\) of actual nonzero positions satisfies
\[
             J\bmod k=(b+p^{-1}-J)\bmod k.                                 \tag{3}
\]
For \(k=1\) the assertion just concerns the unique class.

Indeed the graph equation recovers \(z\) from \(z^p\) at each \(z=\gamma,\gamma^2\), because its two summands are nonzero.
Hence both \(z^p\) have degree \(fk\).
If \(p\mid k\), the polynomial \(S_1(X^{k/p})\) of degree \(fk/p\) annihilates \(\gamma^p\), a contradiction.
Moreover \(\xi^p,\xi^{2p}\) each have degree \(f\), since adjoining the corresponding \(k\)-th root has degree at most \(k\).
Their minima are distinct because their positive absolute logarithmic heights differ by a factor of two.

Write \(A(Y)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}Y^r A_r(Y^k)\).
The graph is divisible by \(U_0(X^k)\) with \(U_0=(S_1S_2)^2\).
In the unique decomposition \(\sum_{v=0}^{k-1}X^v B_v(X^k)\) of the graph, this implies \(U_0\mid B_v\) for each \(v\), by decomposing the quotient in the same way.
At \(v\equiv pr\bmod k\), the numerator comes only from \(A_r\), while the opposite layer can come only from the class \(b+p^{-1}-r\bmod k\).
If that partner is missing, \(B_v(Y)=Y^{(pr-v)/k}A_r(Y^p)\).
The substitution \(Y\mapsto Y^p\) preserves orders of vanishing at nonzero points in characteristic zero, so \(A_r\) vanishes twice at each of \(\xi^p,\xi^{2p}\).
Their two distinct degree-\(f\) minima squared have total degree \(4f\), while \(\deg A_r\le\lfloor b/k\rfloor<4f\).
This forces \(A_r=0\) and proves (3).

In particular, for \(k\ge3\) and \(k\le b<4fk\) the proportion of size-\(s\) supports satisfying (3) is at most
\[
       \frac{\lceil(b+1)/k\rceil}{b+1}
           +(s-1)\frac{\lceil(b+1)/k\rceil}{b}
                  \ \le\ \frac{3s}{k}.                                    \tag{4}
\]
In an ordered sample without replacement, the first position either lies in the unique fixed residue class of the reflection, or some other position must be in its partner class.
There are at most \(\lceil(b+1)/k\rceil\) points in any residue class, which gives the bound by summing these probabilities.
Thus at every prime, for sparse rows with \(s=o(k)\), the proportion still possible tends to zero uniformly in the base and prime as \(k\to\infty\).

<a id="radical-progression"></a>
## Coefficient divisibility at unbounded radical degrees

Fix the radical base \(S_1,S_2\), the modulus \(q\), and let \(T\) be the order of \(Y\) in the unit group of \((\mathbb Z/q)[Y]/(U_0)\).
Take any prime \(p>T\) with \(p\equiv1\bmod T\).
For every \(1\le h\le\lfloor(p-1)/T\rfloor\), take permissible radical indices \(k\equiv-(Th)^{-1}\bmod p\) and put \(u=(1+Thk)/p\), an integer between one and \(k\).
At every \(b\) with \(b+u<4fk\), the identity and determinant conclusions (1) and (2) hold with \(a=u\) in (1).
In particular every \(b<3d\) qualifies.
These are \(\lfloor(p-1)/T\rfloor\) distinct nonzero classes of \(k\bmod p\), a proportion tending to \(1/T\) of the nonzero classes as \(p\to\infty\) on the indicated prime progression.
At each such prime coprime to the fixed modulus of the [radical index restrictions](Yin_C_G0345_v486.md#radicals), each class contains arbitrarily large permissible \(k\), by the Chinese remainder theorem applied also to oddness and those fixed coprimalities.

In \(R_k=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(U_0(X^k))\) one has \(x^{kT}=1\).
Writing \(y=x^p\) gives \(y^k=x^k\) since \(T\mid p-1\), and \(y^u=x\) since \(pu=1+Thk\).
Thus there is a surjective homomorphism from \((\mathbb Z/q)[Y]/(U_0(Y^k))\) onto \(R_k\) mapping \(Y\) to \(y\).
Both rings have \(q^{4fk}\) elements, so it is an isomorphism.
In the former ring the original graph equation consequently becomes \(A_\sigma(Y)+\sigma Y^u A_\sigma^{*b}(Y)=0\).
Its polynomial degree is at most \(b+u<4fk\), which proves the identity coefficientwise, and the same determinant argument proves (2).
These exclusions allow coefficient lists with the required symmetries and divisibilities, and leave the unrestricted Lehmer gap open.
