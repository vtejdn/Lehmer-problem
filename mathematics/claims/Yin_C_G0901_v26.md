# Radial perturbations of primitive roots and the limits of cyclotomic norm inequalities

2026-11-24T20:40 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## All-power inequalities at arbitrarily small total measure

Let \(Q\) run through the primorials, put \(d_Q=\varphi(Q)\), and write \(\zeta_Q=e^{2\pi i/Q}\).
For a parameter \(A>0\) and \(x=A/Q\), form the real monic reciprocal polynomial
\[
 P_{Q,A}(X)=\prod_{a\in(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times}
              (X-e^x\zeta_Q^a)(X-e^{-x}\zeta_Q^a).
\]
Its \(2d_Q\) distinct roots are all off the unit circle, its constant coefficient is one, and its total logarithmic Mahler measure is \(d_Q A/Q\).
At each fixed \(A\) this tends to zero by the divergence of the Euler product for the harmonic series.
The coefficient below the leading one is \(-2\mu(Q)\cosh(A/Q)\), since the sum of primitive \(Q\)-th roots is \(\mu(Q)\) by Möbius inversion.
For large \(Q\) its absolute value lies strictly between two and three, so these polynomials have nonintegral coefficients.

For \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(P_{Q,A},X^n-1)|\) the inequalities
\[
                   R_n\ge1,\qquad R_{kn}\ge R_n                         \tag{1}
\]
hold simultaneously for all positive integers \(k,n\) when \(A\ge4\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)\).
This threshold for their conjunction is sharp for each even \(Q\).
For every integer \(r\ge0\), taking
\[
                 A\ge\max\{4\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2),\
                               1+(r+1)2^r\log4\}
\]
also gives, simultaneously for every \(n,h\ge1\) with at most \(r\) distinct primes dividing \(h\),
\[
                      |\operatorname{Res}(P_{Q,A},\Phi_h(X^n))|\ge1.    \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\Phi_h\) denotes the \(h\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.
Nevertheless, for fixed \(A>0\) one has
\[
 \frac{1}{2d_Q}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P_{Q,A},\Phi_Q)|
     =\log A-\sum_{p\mid Q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}+o_A(1)
       \longrightarrow-\infty.                                       \tag{3}
\]
In particular a diagonal choice \(r\to\infty\), \(A=A(r)\), \(Q=Q(r)\) gives (1) and (2), while \(m(P_{Q,A})\to0\) and the left side of (3) tends to \(-\infty\).
Indeed after choosing \(A(r)\) it suffices to take a primorial \(Q\) large enough that \(\omega(Q)>r\), \(d_QA(r)/Q<1/r\) and the normalized log in (3) is less than \(-r\), for \(r\ge1\).
Thus the absolute-value consequences of divisibility in (1), and even all the tests in (2), can hold with small measure in arbitrary degree over the reals.
An integral polynomial coprime to \(\Phi_Q\) would have nonzero integer resultant there, so (3) displays an arithmetic inequality that separates this family from the inputs to Lehmer's problem.

## Cyclic resultants and every divisibility quotient

For \(|z|=|v|=1\) there is the identity
\[
 |e^xz-v|\,|e^{-x}z-v|
      =2\big(\cosh x-\operatorname{Re}(z\bar v)\big)
      =|z-v|^2+4\sinh^2(x/2).
\]
Thus for any monic polynomial \(G\) with all roots on the unit circle, the paired factors in its resultant with \(P_{Q,A}\) increase with \(x\ge0\).
If \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) and \(\Phi_Q\nmid G\), their product is at least the nonzero integer \(|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_Q,G)|^2\).

Taking \(G=X^n-1\) proves the first inequality of (1) unless \(Q\mid n\).
If \(Q\mid n\), each pair contributes \(4\sinh^2(nx/2)\ge4\sinh^2(A/2)\).
Hence \(A\ge2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)\) suffices for all the bare floors and is necessary at \(n=Q\).

Taking \(G=(X^{kn}-1)/(X^n-1)\) gives the second inequality of (1) whenever \(Q\nmid kn\) or \(Q\mid n\), as this \(G\) is monic integral and coprime to \(\Phi_Q\) in these cases.
In the remaining case \(Q\mid kn,\ Q\nmid n\), we have \(k\ge2\).
For each \(a\) the numerator pair contributes \(4\sinh^2(knx/2)\) and the denominator pair is at most \(4\cosh^2(nx/2)\).
Since \(nx/2\le knx/4\) and \(knx\ge A\), each ratio is at least \(4\sinh^2(knx/4)\ge1\) at the claimed threshold.
For even \(Q\), putting \(n=Q/2,\ k=2\) attains the ratio \(4\sinh^2(A/4)\) at every pair because \((\zeta_Q^a)^n=-1\).
This proves sharpness.

## Unbounded conductors with a bounded number of prime divisors

Write \(g=(n,Q)\).
Unless \(h=Q/g\), the polynomial \(\Phi_h(X^n)\) is coprime to \(\Phi_Q\) since each \((\zeta_Q^a)^n\) has exact order \(Q/g\).
The [pointwise comparison](#powers) then proves (2).
At \(h=Q/g\) these powers run equally often through the primitive \(h\)-th roots by surjectivity of \((\mathbb Z/Q)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/h)^\times\) and coprimality of \(n/g\) with \(h\).
Put \(v=hnx=A n/g\ge A\).
For every divisor \(D\) of \(h\), write
\[
 L_D(y)=\frac{1}{\varphi(D)}
       \sum_{b\in(\mathbb Z/D)^\times}
               \log\!\big(2(\cosh y-\cos(2\pi b/D))\big),
\]
using the single root \(1\) for \(D=1\).
Apply \(\Phi_h(Z)=\prod_{D\mid h}(Z^{h/D}-1)^{\mu(D)}\) separately at \(Z=e^{\pm nx}\zeta_Q^{an}\).
Averaging over \(a\), each \(h/D\)-th power of the phase runs equally often over the primitive \(D\)-th roots.
The resulting exact equality is
\[
 \frac{1}{d_Q}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P_{Q,A},\Phi_h(X^n))|
             =\sum_{D\mid\operatorname{rad}(h)}\mu(D)L_D(v/D).          \tag{4}
\]
For \(D>1\) we have \(L_D(y)\ge L_D(0)=2\log|\Phi_D(1)|/\varphi(D)\ge0\).
Also \(L_D(y)-y\) is the average of \(2\log|1-e^{-y}\zeta_D^b|\).
It is at most \(\log4\) and for \(y\ge1\) it is at least \(2\log(1-e^{-1})>-1\).
For \(0<y<1\) nonnegativity gives \(L_D(y)-y\ge-1\).
Thus \(|L_D(y)-y|\le\log4\) for all \(D>1,\ y>0\), and the same bound holds for \(D=1\) at \(y=v\ge1\).
If \(s=\omega(h)\le r\), (4) is consequently at least
\[
            v\frac{\varphi(h)}h-2^s\log4\ \ge\ \frac{A}{r+1}-2^r\log4 .
\]
Indeed the \(j\)-th smallest prime divisor is at least \(j+1\), giving \(\varphi(h)/h\ge\prod_{j=1}^s j/(j+1)=1/(s+1)\).
This proves (2), including \(h=1\).

## The single cyclotomic test that fails

Put \(u_a=\zeta_Q^a\) for primitive residues \(a\).
In the resultant with \(\Phi_Q\), each diagonal pair contributes \(4\sinh^2(x/2)\).
For \(a\ne b\) the contribution is
\[
 |u_a-u_b|^2
 \left(1+\frac{\sinh^2(x/2)}{\sin^2(\pi(a-b)/Q)}\right).
\]
The product of the leading factors over ordered \(a\ne b\) is \(|\operatorname{disc}\Phi_Q|^2\).
Hence the normalized logarithm in (3) equals
\[
 \log(2\sinh(x/2))+\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}\Phi_Q|}{d_Q}
 +\frac1{2d_Q}\sum_{a\ne b}
       \log\!\left(1+\frac{\sinh^2(x/2)}{\sin^2(\pi(a-b)/Q)}\right).
                                                                         \tag{5}
\]
For fixed \(A\) and a signed nonzero residue difference \(j\), represented by \(0<|j|\le Q/2\) with any tie represented only once, the summand is \(O_A(j^{-2})\).
This follows from \(\sinh(A/(2Q))\ll_A Q^{-1}\) and \(|\sin(\pi j/Q)|\ge2|j|/Q\).
Among primitive \(a\), the fraction for which \(a+j\) is also primitive is
\[
                \prod_{\substack{p\mid Q\\ p\nmid j}}\frac{p-2}{p-1}
\]
by the Chinese remainder theorem.
For each fixed nonzero \(j\) this tends to zero since \(\sum_p1/p=\infty\).
Dominated convergence in the sum over signed differences now gives \(o_A(1)\) for the last term of (5).

For completeness, at a primitive root \(u\) differentiation of the Möbius product gives
\[
             |\Phi_Q'(u)|=Q\prod_{\substack{D\mid Q\\D>1}}
                                      |u^{Q/D}-1|^{\mu(D)} .
\]
Taking the geometric mean over primitive \(u\), the geometric mean at a divisor \(D>1\) is \(|\Phi_D(1)|^{1/\varphi(D)}\).
For squarefree \(D>1\), \(\Phi_D(1)=p\) at \(D=p\) and equals one when \(D\) has two or more primes.
These evaluations follow by taking the limit at one in the same Möbius product, or from \(\prod_{d\mid D,\,d>1}\Phi_d(1)=D\) by induction.
It follows that \(\log|\operatorname{disc}\Phi_Q|/d_Q=\log Q-\sum_{p\mid Q}\log p/(p-1)\).
Since \(\log(2\sinh(A/(2Q)))=\log(A/Q)+o_A(1)\), (5) proves (3).
The sum in (3) diverges by comparison with \((\log2)\sum_{p\mid Q}1/p\).

## A degree-explicit bound for genuine integer norms

The triangular average in the [circle characterization](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#power) has an exact finite maximum.
Indeed the Vandermonde determinant on \(1,z,\ldots,z^N\) and Hadamard's inequality give
\[
       \prod_{j=1}^N |1-z^j|^{N+1-j}\le(N+1)^{(N+1)/2}
                        \quad(|z|=1).
\]
Equality holds precisely when the rows of the square Fourier-type matrix are orthogonal, which requires and suffices that \(z\) be a primitive \((N+1)\)-st root.
In fact orthogonality of the first two rows gives \(z^{N+1}=1,\ z\ne1\), and equality requires all the points \(1,z,\ldots,z^N\) distinct.
For \(|z|\le1\) the inequality extends by maximum modulus, and for \(|z|>1\) reversal contributes \(|z|^{N(N+1)(N+2)/6}\).

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer of degree \(d\), with total logarithmic measure \(m(\alpha)\), and set \(R_k(\alpha)=|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(1-\alpha^k)|\).
Apply the last bound at \(z=\sigma(\alpha)^k\) for each original embedding \(\sigma\).
Since \(R_k(\alpha)\) divides \(R_{kj}(\alpha)\) for every \(j\ge1\) by the integral geometric sum, the product estimate gives
\[
        \log R_k(\alpha)
             \le \frac{d\log(N+1)}N+\frac{N+2}{3}\,k\,m(\alpha)
                        \qquad(k,N\ge1).                              \tag{6}
\]
The pointwise sharpness on the circle concerns the Vandermonde estimate prior to the norm and divisibility steps.
The real polynomials in (1) obey those same monotonicities at all powers, although the genuine integral test (3) fails at a moving conductor with increasingly many prime factors.
