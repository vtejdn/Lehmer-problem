# Several prescribed primes can lose the entire isolated-pair contribution

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous exceptions in the fourth-logarithmic window

Put \(y(d)=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil\), let \(\theta>1\) solve \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\), and write \(m(P)=h(P)=\log M(P)\).
For a degree-preserving prime \(p\) and a root \(\alpha\) of a unit minimal polynomial \(P\), let \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]]\) and let \(E_p\) be the [good-prime isolated-pair weight](Yin_C_G0345_v368.md#statement).
Fix \(C>0\).
For every sufficiently large real \(Y\) and every nonempty set \(S\) of primes in \([5Y/4,7Y/4]\) with
\[
                         n=\prod_{p\in S}p\ \le C\exp(Y^{1/4}),
\]
each of the following constructions gives a monic irreducible unit polynomial of its own degree \(d\).
For every \(p\in S\) simultaneously, the power preserves degree and \(y(d)<p\le2y(d)\).

* There is a reciprocal nontorsion input with \(I_p=1,\ E_p=0\) throughout \(S\) and \(0<h(P)\le\log\theta+o(1)\).
  The error tends to zero with \(Y\) uniformly over these sets.
* There is a nonreciprocal input \(x^d-x-1\) with \(I_p=1,\ E_p=0\) throughout \(S\) and \(\log\theta\le h(P)\le\frac12\log3\).
  Its support differences generate \(\mathbb Z\).
* There is a genuine Salem input \(S_d=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1\), also with support differences generating \(\mathbb Z\), such that
  \[
                  E_p=0,\qquad I_p=3^{(d-2)(p-1)/2}
                           \quad (p\in S),\qquad h(S_d)\longrightarrow\log2 .
                                                                    \tag{1}
  \]
  In particular all the index growth occurs at a prime of the discriminant.

The size condition holds, for example, when \(|S|\le Y^{1/4}/(3\log(2Y))\) for large \(Y\).
These examples rule out a uniform positive lower estimate for \(E_p/(pd\,h(P))\) on a list of this size chosen in advance, even for genuine Salem inputs.
They also limit the specified collision bound for double-root witnesses as calculated below.
The fixed-input limit in v368 takes a different order of quantifiers, and other eligible primes in the same window remain available.

<a id="seed"></a>
## A reciprocal seed with the same order after powering

Set \(g(X)=X^3-X-1,\ g^*(X)=X^3g(1/X)\), and
\[
                       A_n(x)=g(x^n)+xg^*(x^n).
\]
The reversal about degree \(3n+1\) exchanges the summands, and both extreme coefficients are \(-1\).
The cubic \(g\) is irreducible by the rational root test.
Its discriminant is \(-23\), so its real root \(\theta>1\) and its two conjugate roots of modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\) give \(m(g)=\log\theta\).
The identity \(g^*=(X-1)g-X^4\) shows that \(g^*(z)=-z^4\) at each root of \(g\).
Their product gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(g,g^*)|=1\), so an integral polynomial combination of the two rows equals one.

For any root \(\alpha\) of \(A_n\), set \(\beta=\alpha^n\).
The Bézout identity and \(g(\beta)=-\alpha g^*(\beta)\) make \(g^*(\beta)\) a unit in \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
The inverse of any algebraic integer unit is an integral polynomial in the unit itself, by its monic minimal polynomial with unit constant coefficient.
In particular \(g^*(\beta)^{-1}\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\), giving \(\alpha=-g(\beta)/g^*(\beta)\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Thus \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]=\mathbb Z[\alpha^n]\).

For the fixed bivariate polynomial \(g(X)+Zg^*(X)\), the mean in \(Z\) of its logarithmic modulus on \(|X|=1\) is \(\log|g(X)|\) by Jensen's formula, since \(|g^*|=|g|\).
The [fixed-polynomial specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) applies at \((n,1)\), whose shortest orthogonal integer relation has maximum norm \(n\).
Consequently \(m(A_n)\to\log\theta\).
Since \(n\ge5Y/4\) for every admissible set, this convergence gives a uniform upper error in the statement.
For large \(Y\) we have \(0<m(A_n)<2\log\theta\), so \(-A_n\) has a nontorsion factor.
Every such irreducible factor is reciprocal.
Indeed any nonreciprocal factor would occur with its distinct reversed factor, and [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) would charge each at least \(\log\theta\).

<a id="lift"></a>
## Lifting through square roots and choosing the degree

Choose a root \(\alpha\) of any nontorsion factor \(P_0\) just obtained.
Choose \(\gamma_0=\alpha,\ \gamma_{k+1}^2=\gamma_k\), and write \(P_k\) for the monic minimal polynomial of \(\gamma_k\).
All \(\gamma_k\) are nontorsion units.
With \(q=2^k\), the polynomial \(P_0(x^q)\) is reciprocal and has total logarithmic measure \(h(P_0)\le m(A_n)<2\log\theta\).
It contains \(P_k\), so the same reversed-factor argument gives reciprocity of \(P_k\) and \(0<h(P_k)\le m(A_n)\).
Indeed if all conjugates of a unit had modulus one, every positive power would have bounded degree and elementary symmetric coefficients bounded solely in terms of that degree, forcing two powers to coincide.

Now \(\gamma_k^q=\alpha\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[\alpha^n]\subseteq\mathbb Z[\gamma_k^n]\).
The two units \(\gamma_k^q,\gamma_k^n\) have inverses in that last ring.
Since \(n\) is odd, Bézout's identity for \(q,n\) gives \(\gamma_k\in\mathbb Z[\gamma_k^n]\).
For each divisor \(p\in S\) the intervening order \(\mathbb Z[\gamma_k^p]\) therefore equals \(\mathbb Z[\gamma_k]\).

Put \(T=\exp(Y^{1/4})/3\).
The degrees \(d_k=\deg P_k\) are nondecreasing and \(d_{k+1}\le2d_k\).
They are unbounded, since the \(\gamma_k\) are distinct by nontorsion, whereas bounded degree and bounded measure give only finitely many monic integral polynomials by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
The first \(d_k\ge T\) satisfies \(T\le d_k\ll_C T\), since \(d_0\le3n+1\).
For every such \(d\), we have \(Y\le y(d)\le Y+O_C(Y^{3/4})\), so \(S\subset(y(d),2y(d)]\) eventually.

This reciprocal construction uses square roots to adjust the degree and gives no assertion that the resulting support differences generate \(\mathbb Z\).
If its outputs for arbitrarily large \(Y\) have logarithmic measures arbitrarily close to zero, they give a sequence contradicting a Lehmer gap.
Otherwise a fixed positive lower bound holds throughout this constructed family for sufficiently large \(Y\).
The upper bound near \(\log\theta\) alone does not decide between these possibilities.

<a id="trinomial"></a>
## A nonreciprocal comparison with consecutive support indices

Choose \(d=n\lceil T/n\rceil\), so \(T\le d\ll_C T\), and take \(P=x^d-x-1\).
If \(z,z^{-1}\) were both roots, multiplication of \(z^d=z+1\) and \(z^{-d}=z^{-1}+1\) would yield \(z+z^{-1}=-1\).
Then \(z\) would be a primitive cube root while \(z^d=z+1=-z^2\) would have order six, a contradiction.
Thus all irreducible factors of this monic unit polynomial are nonreciprocal and nontorsion.
By Smyth's theorem each contributes at least \(\log\theta\).
Parseval and the logarithmic mean inequality give \(M(P)\le\sqrt3<\theta^2\), where \(\theta>33/25\) follows by substituting \(33/25\) in its cubic.
There is exactly one irreducible factor, counted with multiplicity.
Selmer established this irreducibility in [Theorem 1, p. 289 of his 1956 paper](../audits/literature/Sel56/Yin_U001.md#sel56-u001).
For each \(p\in S\) we have \(\alpha=(\alpha^p)^{d/p}-1\), giving the asserted equality of orders.

<a id="salem"></a>
## A Salem family with the whole power index at three

Choose \(d=6n\lceil T/(6n)\rceil\), again with \(T\le d\ll_C T\).
The [Salem family theorem](Yin_C_G0022_v1.md#salem-first-window-precise-statement) gives the asserted irreducible \(S_d\), with one exterior conjugate and \(h(S_d)\to\log2\).
Write \(F=(x-1)S_d=(x-2)x^d+2x-1\), and let \(\xi_i\) run through its \(d+1\) distinct roots, including one.
For \(p\in S\), a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\) satisfies \(\zeta^d=1\), giving
\[
               F(\zeta\xi_i)=(\zeta-1)\xi_i(\xi_i^d+2).
\]
The last factor never vanishes since \(F(x)=3\) whenever \(x^d=-2\).
Thus the \(p\)-th powers of the \(\xi_i\) are distinct, proving degree preservation for \(S_d\).
Moreover \(\prod_i|\xi_i|=1\) and \(\prod_i|\xi_i^d+2|=3^d\) by the monic resultant formula.

Let \(J=\prod_{i<j}|(\xi_i^p-\xi_j^p)/(\xi_i-\xi_j)|\).
Multiplying \(|F(\zeta\xi_i)|\) over all \(i\) and \(\zeta\ne1\) gives \(p^{d+1}3^{d(p-1)}\).
Expanding \(F\) into its linear factors in the same product gives \(p^{d+1}J^2\), since the diagonal factors yield \(p^{d+1}\) and each unordered off-diagonal pair occurs twice.
The factors involving \(\xi_i=1\) in \(J\) have product \(\prod_{\zeta\ne1}|S_d(\zeta)|=3^{p-1}\), as \(F(\zeta)=3(\zeta-1)\).
The [power-index formula](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) now gives \(I_p=J/3^{p-1}\), as in (1).
Modulo three, \(F=(x+1)(x^{d/3}-1)^3\), so after division by \(x-1\) at least a double root at one remains.
Hence \(3\mid\operatorname{disc}S_d\), and the whole index is excluded from \(E_p\).
Along these varying inputs \(\log I_p/(pd)\to(\log3)/2\), while the fixed-input archimedean expression for each \(S_d\) in v368 is \((1-1/d)h(S_d)\to\log2\), by its single reciprocal exterior-interior pair.

<a id="collision"></a>
## Consequence for the available double-root bound

For \(e=2\), consider the specific arithmetic choice \(B=B_0+ d\log p\) at \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\), and \(B=B_0\) otherwise, in the [resultant-to-measure inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v366.md#arithmetic).
Here \(B_0=4\sum_{\ell\ne p}(\log\ell)\sum_b w_{\ell,b}\) uses the maximum two-group collision weight above each powered residue.
For either family with \(I_p=1\) all these nonnegative weights vanish by the [index decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#index).
At a support prime avoiding the discriminant, the [local row divisibility](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v152.md#resultant) forces at least one stripped row of any primitive witness for \(P^2\) to have degree at least \(d\).
Indeed the nonzero reduction of that row is divisible by the monic degree-\(d\) powered minimal polynomial modulo \(p\), whose nonzero constant term permits the monomial removal.
Thus for the sum \(N\) of the two stripped row degrees one has \(B/N\le\log p\) for all such witnesses, including the case \(B=0\).

For the Salem family the single-root witness \(F=(x-1)S_d\) has rows \(-2X^{d/p}-1,\ X^{d/p}+2\) at modulus \(p\).
They are coprime, have nonzero constant terms, and their absolute resultant is \(3^{d/p}\) by evaluation at the roots of the second row.
The [two-group lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#branches) with multiplicity one gives \(B_0\le4(d/p)\log3\) for the input weights appearing in the double-root estimate.
Also \(F'\equiv x^d+2\bmod p\), and a common zero with \(F\) would give \(3=0\bmod p\).
Since \(p>3\), the discriminant of \(S_d\) is prime to \(p\).
The same \(N\ge d\) bound therefore gives \(B/N\le\log p+4(\log3)/p\).
All these normalized bounds are \(o(p)\) at every listed prime, even though the [finite double-root witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) exist there.
This conclusion concerns the specified arithmetic choice of \(B\).
Additional contributions to the actual resultant of a double-root witness and primes outside \(S\) remain possible.
