# Local degree bounds at both coefficients of reciprocal tetranomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary offsets, coefficient valuations and both reciprocal signs

Let \(D>2k>0\), \(\gcd(D,k)=1\), \(\sigma=\pm1\), and let \(A,B\) be relatively prime nonzero integers.
Put
\[
 P=A(X^D+\sigma)+B(X^{D-k}+\sigma X^k),\qquad
 M=D-2k,\qquad K=2+2|B/A|.
\]
For any prime \(p\mid AB\) set
\[
 (T,w)=
 \begin{cases}
 (D,v_p(B))&p\mid B,\\
 (M,v_p(A))&p\mid A,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad N=p^{v_p(T)},\qquad h=T/N.
\]
Let \(F=\operatorname{ord}_{2^{v_2(T)+1}}(p)\) if \(p\) is odd and \(\sigma=1\), and let \(F=1\) otherwise.
Thus \(1\le F\le h\), including the order one modulo two for odd \(p\).
Every rationally irreducible monic unit factor \(S\) of \(P\) has degree
\[
                     d\le2\qquad\text{or}\qquad d\gg\frac{NF}{w p^w}.       \tag{1}
\]
There is an absolute implied constant.
For \(p\mid B\), (1) holds for every rational irreducible factor, since the leading and constant coefficients of \(P\) are \(p\)-adic units.

Every positive-measure integral divisor has logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\) satisfying
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
    m\gg F/(w p^w h) & (p\mid B),\\[2pt]
    m\gg\min\{1/\sqrt{\log K},\, F/(w p^w h)\} & (p\mid A),
 \end{array}                                                            \tag{2}
\]
again with absolute constants.
If the original exponents have a common divisor \(g=\gcd(D,k)\), apply (2) to the reduced exponents \(D/g,k/g\) and divide its lower bound by the [radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) \(R_L\) with \(L=2|A|+2|B|\).
For fixed coefficients a sequence with positive measures tending to zero must therefore have \(h/F\to\infty\) at every prime dividing the inner coefficient and also at every prime dividing the outer coefficient, using the respective reduced exponents.
For odd fixed \(p\) and the plus sign, \(F\gg_p 2^{v_2(T)}\), so in this case the part of \(T\) prime to \(2p\) must tend to infinity.

If \(|A|\ge|B|\), all roots of \(P\) lie on the circle, so every positive-measure integral divisor has measure at least \(\log2\).
Indeed for \(|z|>1\) one has \(|Az^k+B|^2-|A+Bz^k|^2=(A^2-B^2)(|z|^{2k}-1)\ge0\) and \(Az^k+B\ne0\).
The two terms in \(P=z^{D-k}(Az^k+B)+\sigma(A+Bz^k)\) therefore cannot cancel there, and reciprocal symmetry also excludes interior roots.
The root formula now gives the assertion for integral divisors.
In the remaining primitive case \(|B|>|A|\) at least one prime divides \(B\), so the first estimate of (2) always applies.
For example \(A=1,\ B=-3,\ \sigma=1\) gives the reciprocal family \(X^D+1-3(X^{D-k}+X^k)\), with a real root greater than one and a uniform positive gap for all its positive-measure divisors whenever the part of the reduced \(D\) prime to six is bounded.
The bound holds at every allowed offset.

<a id="centers"></a>
## Centers and multiplicities for the two coefficient choices

At \(p\mid B\) write \(T=D,\ j=k,\ C=A,\ E=B\), and at \(p\mid A\) write \(T=M,\ j=-k,\ C=B,\ E=A\).
In both cases the Laurent polynomial
\[
                 H=C(X^T+\sigma)+E(X^{T-j}+\sigma X^j)
\]
equals \(P\) up to a monomial, \(\gcd(T,j)=1,\ v_p(E)=w\), and \(C\) is a \(p\)-adic unit.
We only need the roots that are \(p\)-adic units.
For a monic rational unit factor they are all units, because both the roots and their inverses are algebraic integers.
For \(p\mid B\) this also holds for every root of \(P\) by its endpoint units.

In the residue torus \(H\) reduces to a nonzero scalar times \(X^T+\sigma\), with each root of multiplicity \(N\).
Let \(\xi\) be the prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift of a residue root in an unramified extension, and normalize \(v_p(p)=1\).
For odd \(p\) one has \(\xi^T=-\sigma\), because the Teichmüller lift preserves multiplication and both signs have order prime to \(p\).
In the plus case the order of every such \(\xi\) is divisible by \(2^{v_2(T)+1}\), since \(\xi^T=-1\).
The Frobenius orbit of its reduction therefore has size at least \(F\) and is a multiple of \(F\).
For \(p=2\) all lifts have \(\xi^T=1\).
In every case the orbit has size at least one.

Take \(u=v_p(T)\).
If \(u\le w\), every factor meeting a unit class already has degree at least its orbit size \(F\ge NF/p^w\).
The same argument suffices for \(p=2,\ u=w+1\) with an extra factor two.
In the remaining range put \(J=p^{u-w+1}-1\), so \(J\ge7\) and \(J-2\gg N/p^w\).
Use \(X=\xi(1+Z)\).
The constant and the first coefficients below are those of \(H\), expanded as a power series at \(Z=0\).
In degrees \(1,\ldots,J\) every coefficient has valuation at least \(w\), since \(v_p\binom{T}{i}\ge u-v_p(i)\ge w\) for \(1\le i\le J\) and the other nonconstant terms carry \(E\).
Generalized binomial coefficients of negative integral exponents are integers.

For odd \(p\) the constant term is \(\sigma E(\xi^j-\xi^{-j})\).
Unless \(\bar\xi^{2j}=1\), it has valuation \(w\).
In the exceptional case \(\xi^{2j}=\xi^{2T}=1\) implies \(\xi^2=1\) by coprimality.
The constant is then zero and the linear coefficient is
\[
               \sigma\{-CT+E\xi^j(2j-T)\}.
\]
Its valuation is \(w\) since \(u>w,\ p\nmid 2j\).
Thus at every center there is a coefficient of valuation \(w\) in degree zero or one, and degree one is needed at most at the two rational sign centers.

For \(p=2,\ \sigma=-1\) the constant term is \(E(\xi^{-j}-\xi^j)\), of valuation \(w\) unless \(\xi=1\), since the order of \(\xi\) is odd and coprime to \(j\).
At \(\xi=1\) the constant vanishes and the linear coefficient is \(CT+E(T-2j)\), of valuation \(w+1\) because \(u\ge w+2\) and \(j\) is odd.
The quadratic coefficient has valuation \(w\).
Indeed \(v_2\binom T2=u-1>w\), and \(\binom{T-j}{2}-\binom j2\equiv\binom{-j}{2}-\binom j2\equiv j\bmod2\), since \(4\mid T\).
Only this one center requires degree two.

For \(p=2,\ \sigma=1\) the constant is \(2C+E(\xi^{-j}+\xi^j)\).
If \(w\ge2\), it has valuation one at every center.
If \(w=1\), it has valuation one except when \(\bar\xi^j+\bar\xi^{-j}=1\), which means \(\bar\xi^j\) has order three.
Since \(\xi\) has order dividing \(T\), coprimality forces \(\xi\) itself to have order three.
At either of these at most two centers the linear coefficient \(CT+E((T-j)\xi^{-j}+j\xi^j)\) has valuation one, by division by two and reduction modulo two.
Here \(j\) is odd and the displayed trace is one.
This completes the list of exceptional centers in both signs.

<a id="ramification"></a>
## Large ramification away from at most two roots

First take \(p=2,\ \sigma=1,\ w\ge2\).
Translate \(P\) at the center using \(X=\xi(1+Z)\).
Its constant has valuation one and the coefficients of positive degrees below \(N\) have valuation at least one by the residual multiplicity.
The degree-\(N\) coefficient is a unit, so the lower Newton polygon through abscissa \(N\) is the single segment of slope \(-1/N\).
Consequently every root in the class has relative ramification at least \(N\) over the unramified field.
This argument applies in this case even for the small \(u\) previously set aside.

In the other cases every constant has valuation at least \(w\).
Multiplying \(H(\xi(1+Z))\) by the monomial which gives \(P(\xi(1+Z))\) preserves divisibility by \(p^w\) through degree \(J\) and the first index \(i_0\le2\) having valuation exactly \(w\).
Indeed the multiplier is an integral power of \(1+Z\) times a constant unit, with inverse also having integral power-series coefficients.
At \(i_0\) all contributions from lower indices have valuation greater than \(w\).
The reduction of the translated polynomial vanishes at zero to exact order \(N\).

Discard any initial zero coefficients, counting their zero roots with multiplicity among the first \(i_0\).
The lower Newton polygon has negative slopes through abscissa \(N\), there attaining height zero for the first time.
At \(i_0\) its height is at most \(w\).
Any negative-slope segment extending strictly to the right of \(i_0\) ends at an integer \(q>J\), because it must end at height strictly below \(w\) and all coefficients through \(J\) have valuation at least \(w\).
Its absolute slope is at most \(w/(J-i_0)\) if the segment contains \(i_0\).
If it starts to the right of \(i_0\), the same bound follows by monotonicity of the slopes, using the first segment extending to the right of \(i_0\).
Thus all but at most \(i_0\) roots in this class have
\[
                    0<v_p(X/\xi-1)\le w/(J-2).
\]
This uses the Newton polygon theorem only for roots reducing to \(\bar\xi\), including when the leading coefficient of \(P\) is divisible by \(p\).
The local degree of a root meeting this bound is at least \((J-2)/w\) over the unramified field by its value group.
If the residue orbit has size \(f_\xi\), the degree of its rational factor is at least \(f_\xi(J-2)/w\), since it contains the conjugate blocks in all \(f_\xi\) classes and these valuations are preserved under unramified Frobenius.
The sum of \(i_0\) over all classes is at most two by the preceding cases.
Thus a factor containing no root meeting the ramification bound has degree at most two when all its roots are units at \(p\).
This proves (1).

For the asserted growth of \(F\), if \(e=v_2(T)\ge1\) then \(p\) odd gives
\[
          v_2(p^{2^j}-1)=v_2(p^2-1)+j-1\qquad (j\ge1)
\]
by successive squaring.
The order modulo \(2^{e+1}\) is a power of two, and the displayed identity bounds it below by a fixed positive multiple of \(2^e\) for fixed \(p\), including the bounded initial moduli.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Combining the two arithmetic tests with the outer gap

It suffices to consider a positive-measure primitive irreducible factor of sufficiently small logarithmic measure \(m\).
The [endpoint and nonreciprocal reductions](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer) make it a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit factor.
The [quadratic radial floor](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(d(2m)^2\ge1/10\) by bounding the sum of squares by the square of the sum of absolute logarithmic moduli.
It gives an absolute gap in the alternative \(d\le2\).
In the other alternative the [width estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(m\gg d/D\), with an absolute constant since there are four terms.

For \(p\mid B\) this proves (2) directly.
For \(p\mid A\) it gives the alternative \(m\gg F/(w p^w h)\) as soon as \(k\le D/4\), since then \(M\ge D/2\).
If \(k>D/4\), let \(t>0\) be the largest log modulus of a conjugate.
Leading-term domination gives \(kt\le\log K\), and reciprocity gives \(dtm\ge1/20\) by the same quadratic floor.
Thus \(d\ge k/(20m\log K)\), and \(m\gg d/D\) implies \(m\gg1/\sqrt{\log K}\).
The lower bounds have bounded right sides since \(w\ge1,\ 1\le F\le h\) and \(K\ge2\), so factors above the initial absolute cutoff satisfy them as well.
Integral content greater than one costs at least \(\log2\), and logarithmic measures of nonzero integral factors are nonnegative and additive.
For a common exponent dilation, the reduced root \(\beta=\alpha^g\) has measure bounded above by \(R_L\,m(\alpha)\) by the cited radical result.
This proves all measure assertions.
