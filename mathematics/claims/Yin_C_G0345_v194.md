# Factor heights and exterior roots when both coefficient blocks vary

<a id="statement"></a>
## Quantitative estimates for individual factors

Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero and coprime, with \(A\) monic and \(B(0)=\pm1\).
Write \(W=1+\max(\deg A,\deg B)\), \(L=\max(2,\|A\|_1,\|B\|_1)\), and \(\ell=\log(2L)\), where the coefficient norm is the sum of the absolute coefficients.
Suppose \(n\ge1\) and \(n+\deg A>\deg B\).
For every monic irreducible factor \(S\) of \(P=X^nA+B\), with \(d=\deg S\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(S)\), one has
\[
 \left|\frac{n\,m(S)}d-\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A|,|B|)\,d\sigma\right|
 \ \ll\ W\sqrt{\frac{\ell\{W\ell+\log(2d)\}}d}.                       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\sigma\) is normalized circle measure and the implied constant is absolute.
Thus for bounded \(L\) the error tends to zero whenever \(W=o(d^{1/3})\).
The exponent \(n\) need not exceed the widths of the blocks.

Now suppose \(A(0)\ne0\) and \(B=\pm A^*\), with \(A^*(X)=X^{\deg A}A(1/X)\), retaining the coprimality hypothesis.
Write \(A=\prod_q q^{e_q}\) over its distinct monic irreducible divisors.
At each exterior root \(z\) of \(S\), assign \(z\) to one \(q\) minimizing \(|q(z)|\), with arbitrary tie breaking.
For every such \(q\),
\[
 \left|\frac{n}{e_q d}\sum_{z\ {\rm assigned\ to}\ q}\log|z|-m(q)\right|
 \ \ll_L\ W^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{W^2+\log(2d)}d}+\frac{W^4}{d}.           \tag{2}
\]
No separation between roots of \(A\), linear lower bound on \(n\), or reciprocity of \(S\) is assumed.
There is \(C_L>0\) such that whenever \(d>C_LW^5\), the factor \(S\) has at least as many exterior roots as \(A\) has distinct irreducible factors.

In particular, fix distinct primes \(p_1,\dots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\), and a real \(K\ge1\).
Take arbitrary positive integers \(u_1,\dots,u_k,n\) with \(\max_i u_i\le K\min_i u_i\) and \(n\ge\min_i u_i\), and put \(A=\prod_i(X^{u_i}-p_i)\).
There is a uniform positive Mahler gap for all positive-measure irreducible factors with fewer than \(k\) exterior roots of all \(X^nA\pm A^*\) in this family.
For \(k=2\) this separates every Salem factor from one with three varying exponents.
It gives no gap for factors with an unrestricted number of exterior roots.

<a id="test"></a>
## Averaging a polynomial maximum

Let \(U,V\) be nonzero coprime integral polynomials of maximum degree \(D\), let \(w=1+D\), and suppose their coefficient norms are at most \(H\ge2\).
Put \(h=\log(2H)\), \(f(z)=\log\max(|U(z)|,|V(z)|)\), and \(g(z)=f(z)-D\log^+|z|\).
For the uniform probability \(\mu\) on the roots of any monic irreducible integer polynomial of degree \(d\) and logarithmic measure \(m\), we first prove
\[
 \left|\int f\,d(\mu-\sigma)\right|
 \ll \frac{wm+1}{d}+w\sqrt{\frac{h\{m+wh+\log(2d)\}}d}.              \tag{3}
\]

On the closed unit disk, \(\max(|U|,|V|)\ge\exp(-Cwh)\) for an absolute \(C\).
Indeed the Sylvester matrix of \(U,V\) represents the coefficient map \((s,t)\mapsto sU+tV\) with \(\deg s<\deg V,\ \deg t<\deg U\).
Its determinant is a nonzero integer; applying its adjugate to the constant basis vector gives \(sU+tV=\pm\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\).
Each coefficient in \(s,t\) is bounded by \(H^{2w}\) by Hadamard's inequality, since the columns before taking minors are coefficient shifts of one of the two polynomials.
If either polynomial is constant, its absolute value already gives the lower bound.
The reversed pair \(X^DU(1/X),X^DV(1/X)\) is again coprime because at least one polynomial has degree \(D\).
Applying the disk bound to both pairs yields \(\|g\|_\infty\ll wh\).
On the disk the two polynomial derivatives are bounded by \(wH\), so the logarithm of their maximum modulus is Lipschitz with constant at most \(\exp(C'wh)\).
The reversed disk estimate and \(|1/z-1/z'|\le|z-z'|\) for exterior points give the same bound for \(g\) outside; splitting a segment at the circle handles points on opposite sides.

The subharmonic function \(f\) has Riesz measure \(\nu=(2\pi)^{-1}\Delta f\) of mass \(D\), by its logarithmic growth \(D\log|z|+O(1)\) at infinity and Jensen's formula.
Thus \((2\pi)^{-1}\Delta g=\nu-D\sigma\).
The [Dirichlet energy identity](Yin_C_G0345_v172.md#disk) gives
\[
 (2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb C}|\nabla g|^2
       =\int g\,d(D\sigma-\nu)\ll w^2 h.
\]
Integration by parts applies by smoothing, since \(g\) is Lipschitz on the disk and is Lipschitz in the coordinate \(1/z\) near infinity.
For a signed measure \(\eta\) of zero mass and finite logarithmic energy \(I(\eta)=-\iint\log|z-z'|\,d\eta(z)d\eta(z')\), the same identity and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(|\int g\,d\eta|\ll w\sqrt{h I(\eta)}\).

Replace each point of \(\mu\) by uniform probability on the circle of radius \(\epsilon\) around it, keeping its original weight, and call the resulting probability \(\mu_\epsilon\).
The self-interaction of each circle for the positive logarithm is \(\log\epsilon\).
For two distinct centers the averaged logarithm is at least the logarithm of their distance by the submean inequality applied twice.
The product of squared distances between distinct roots, taken over unordered pairs, is the absolute value of a nonzero integer discriminant.
Since the logarithmic potential of \(\sigma\) with the positive sign is \(\log^+|z|\), a \(1\)-Lipschitz function, it follows that
\[
 I(\mu_\epsilon-\sigma)\le
     d^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)+2m/d+2\epsilon .
\]
Choose \(\epsilon=(2d)^{-1}\exp(-C'wh)\) with \(C'\) large enough for the Lipschitz estimate above.
Replacing \(\mu_\epsilon\) by \(\mu\) in the integral of \(g\) costs at most \(1/d\).
Restoring \(D\log^+|z|\) contributes \(Dm/d\), proving (3).

<a id="height"></a>
## The resultant giving the common height

Our condition on the leading degree makes \(P\) monic with unit constant term, and \(m(S)\le m(P)\le\log(2L)\).
At every root \(z\) of \(S\), neither block vanishes, and
\[
 n\log^+|z|=\log\max(|A(z)|,|B(z)|)-\log|A(z)|.
\]
The sum of the last logarithm over \(S\) is the logarithm of \(|\operatorname{Res}(A,S)|\).
This is between zero and \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(A,B)|\ll W\ell\), since the monic \(A\) gives \(\operatorname{Res}(A,P)=\operatorname{Res}(A,B)\), and the Sylvester determinant has columns of norm at most \(L\).
The constant case \(A=1\) has log resultant zero.
Applying (3) to \(A,B\) and dividing the resultant bound by \(d\) proves (1).

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Assigning exterior roots to the leading block

Assume now \(B=\pm A^*\) and \(A(0)\ne0\).
The assertion about allocations is empty for \(A=1\), so suppose \(A\) is nonconstant.
Coprimality excludes unit-circle roots of \(A\), since every such root of a real polynomial is also a root of its reversal.
For \(c>1\), Rouché's theorem on the unit circle gives \(cX^nA+B\) the same number of interior roots as \(X^nA\), and hence at most \(\deg A\) exterior roots.
Letting \(c\) decrease to one preserves this upper bound on strictly exterior roots by continuity with multiplicity.
Reversal of \(P\) gives \(\pm P\), so at most \(\deg A\) roots are strictly interior as well.
All its root moduli are at most \(1+2L\) by the monic coefficient bound.

Each monic divisor \(Q\) of \(A\) has \(\|Q\|_1\le 2^{\deg Q}\exp(m(Q))\le2^W L\), by expanding its root product.
For any two distinct irreducible divisors \(q,q'\), the disk Bézout bound in the proof of (3), evaluated on \(|z|\le1+2L\), gives
\[
             \max(|q(z)|,|q'(z)|)\ge \exp(-C_L W^2).
\]
Indeed the same Bézout coefficients cost at most the additional factor \((1+2L)^{2W}\) there.
The corresponding bound for \(A,A^*\) is \(\exp(-C_LW)\), and every divisor of \(A\) has modulus at most \(\exp(C_LW)\) on that disk.
At an exterior root of \(P\), \(|A^*(z)|=|z|^n|A(z)|\ge |A(z)|\), so \(|\log|A^*(z)||\ll_L W\).
The rule of assignment makes \(|\log|q(z)||\ll_L W^2\) for every unassigned \(q\).
For the assigned \(q\), taking logarithms in the equation of \(P\) and using \(\sum_{q'} e_{q'}\le W\) gives
\[
                e_q\log|q(z)|=-n\log|z|+O_L(W^3).
\]
At every interior root, \(|A(z)|\ge|A^*(z)|\) instead, so \(|\log|q(z)||\ll_L W\) by the upper bound for the complementary divisor \(A/q\).

The polynomials \(q,q^*\) are coprime because \(A,A^*\) are.
The continuous test \(f_q=\log\max(|q|,|q^*|)\) equals \(\log|q|\) on the unit circle and has mean \(m(q)\) there by Jensen's formula.
Applying (3) with \(h\ll_L W\) and \(m(S)\le\log(2L)\) gives
\[
 \frac1d\sum_{S(z)=0} f_q(z)
       =m(q)+O_L\left(W^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{W^2+\log(2d)}d}\right).
\]
The disk Bézout bound and the coefficient upper bounds give \(|f_q(z)|\ll_L W^2\) on the full root disk.
Deleting at most \(2W\) noncircle roots therefore costs \(O_L(W^3/d)\).
Finally \(\operatorname{Res}(q,S)\) divides \(\operatorname{Res}(q,A^*)\) up to sign, since \(q\) is monic and divides \(A\).
Its absolute logarithm is \(O_L(W^2)\) by the same column bound.
Sum \(\log|q(z)|\) over \(S\) using this resultant, the circle mean just obtained, and the bounds at interior, assigned exterior and unassigned exterior roots.
There are at most \(W\) roots of each strict radial sign, giving (2) after division by \(d\).

<a id="gap"></a>
## How many exterior roots a small factor must have

Every \(q\) here has nonzero constant term and is nonreciprocal, since a reciprocal \(q\) would divide both \(A\) and \(A^*\).
Consequently [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives \(m(q)\ge\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) is the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
The error in (2) is less than \(\log\theta\) for \(d>C_L W^5\) with \(C_L\) sufficiently large.
Indeed \(\log(2d)/d\) decreases for \(d\ge2\); at \(d=C_L W^5\) the bound is \(O_L(\sqrt{(1+\log(2C_L))/C_L}+1/C_L)\), uniformly in \(W\).
Each \(q\) must then receive an exterior root.

In the prime family the displayed factors are irreducible by Eisenstein, have distinct constant coefficients, and have all roots outside the circle.
Thus \(A,A^*\) are coprime, \(A\) has \(k\) distinct irreducible factors, and \(L\) is bounded in terms of the primes.
Write \(b=\min_i u_i\), \(p=\max_i p_i\), and consider any exterior root \(z\) of \(P\).
If \(|z|^b\ge2p\), each \(x=z^{u_i}\) has \(|x|\ge2p\), so \(|1-p_i x|/|x-p_i|\le2(p+1)\).
The root equation then implies \(|z|^b\le |z|^n\le [2(p+1)]^k\).
Together the two cases give \(b\log|z|\ll_{p,k}1\).
For a positive-measure factor with fewer than \(k\) exterior roots this yields \(m(S)\ll_{p,k}1/b\), and hence \(W\ll_{p,k,K}1/m(S)\), enlarging the constant using \(m(S)\le\log(2L)\).
Such a factor must have \(d\le C_LW^5\) by (2).
If its measures tended to zero, its degrees would tend to infinity by finiteness of monic polynomials of bounded degree and measure.
The [Dobrowolski lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(1/m(S)\ll(\log(2d))^3\), contradicting \(d\ll_{p,k,K}(\log(2d))^{15}\) as \(d\to\infty\).
This proves the stated uniform gap.
