# Independent even sign changes on the roots of all cubic factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## The splitting group over the maximal abelian field

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers, set \(N=\max(a,b)\), and put
\[
 g(T)=T^3-T-1,\qquad B(T)=1-T^2-T^3,\qquad
 A(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a).
\]
For every sufficiently large \(N\), let \(P_1,\ldots,P_r\) be the distinct monic nontorsion irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\) of \(-A\).
Each \(P_i\) is reciprocal, and its roots form \(n_i=\deg(P_i)/2\) pairs \(\{z,z^{-1}\}\).
Choose an ordering of these pairs and a representative in each pair.
Write \(V_i=\{v\in\mathbf F_2^{n_i}:\sum_j v_j=0\}\) for the sign changes that exchange the members of an even number of pairs.
Let \(M\) be the compositum of the splitting fields of the \(P_i\), and put \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
Then in the natural action on all the roots,
\[
 \operatorname{Gal}(MF/F)=\prod_{i=1}^r (V_i\rtimes A_{n_i}),\qquad
 [MF:F]=\prod_{i=1}^r 2^{n_i-1}\frac{n_i!}{2}.
\]
In particular, every even choice of exchanges on each factor can be made independently of all other factors.
Adjoining the torsion roots of \(A\) leaves the same field over \(F\).
The formula applies to the actual factor degrees and does not determine the number of factors or their shares of the exterior pairs.

<a id="four-letters"></a>
## When three-cycles determine two blocks of roots

Consider a set of \(n\ge5\) pairs of points, and a group \(D\) preserving the collection of pairs and inducing \(A_n\) on them.
Suppose its kernel on the pairs is contained in the group of order two generated by the simultaneous flip of all pairs.
Each three-cycle \(\sigma\) has a unique lift of order three in \(D\).
Indeed the cube of any lift lies in this central kernel, so its fourth power is a lift of order three.
If there is a second lift, it differs from the order-three lift by the simultaneous flip and has order six.
An order-three lift fixes both points in every pair off the support of its cycle.

For different labels \(j,k\), take any three-cycle carrying \(j\) to \(k\).
Its unique order-three lift induces a bijection \(t_{jk}\) between their two-point fibers that is independent of the third letter.
To check this, take two such cycles and choose a four-letter set containing the union of their supports.
The subgroup generated by all the order-three lifts on these four letters projects onto \(A_4\).
It fixes the complement pointwise, so its kernel is trivial and it is isomorphic to \(A_4\).
The stabilizer of a letter in \(A_4\) has order three, hence its action on the two points above that letter is trivial.
The two lifts sending \(j\) to \(k\) therefore give the same bijection.

The inverse lift shows \(t_{kj}=t_{jk}^{-1}\).
For three distinct letters \(j,k,l\), the lift of \((j\,k\,l)\) and its square show \(t_{kl}t_{jk}=t_{jl}\).
Taking \(t_{jj}\) to be the identity gives two classes of size \(n\), each consisting of one point in a chosen fiber and all its images under these bijections.
Every permutation preserving the collection of pairs and normalizing \(D\) preserves the unordered partition into these two classes, since conjugation preserves the unique order-three lifts.

<a id="product"></a>
## Isolating sign changes on one rational factor

Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\) and \(J=G'\).
Restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(MF/F)\) with \(J\), since \(M\cap F\) is the maximal abelian subextension of \(M/\mathbb Q\).
Every rational automorphism preserves each factor, giving an embedding \(G\subseteq\prod_i(\mathbf F_2^{n_i}\rtimes S_{n_i})\).
In each coordinate both total flip parity and permutation parity are homomorphisms to \(\mathbf F_2\).
Hence \(J\subseteq\prod_i(V_i\rtimes A_{n_i})\).
The [joint trace action](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v177.md#product) gives the image of \(J\) on the pairs as the entire product \(\prod_i A_{n_i}\), including when degrees coincide.
The [individual trace theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#pairs) ensures uniformly that \(n_i>6\) once \(N\) is large enough.

Fix \(i\), and let \(H_i\le J\) be the inverse image of the copy of \(A_{n_i}\) acting trivially on every other set of pairs.
It is normal in \(G\), since \(J\) is normal and each rational factor is preserved individually.
Then \(D_i=H_i'\) is normal in \(G\) and projects onto the perfect group \(A_{n_i}\).
Away from factor \(i\), elements of \(H_i\) act solely by commuting sign changes, so \(D_i\) fixes every root there.
Thus we may regard \(D_i\) as a subgroup of \(V_i\rtimes A_{n_i}\) on factor \(i\) alone.

Suppose its kernel on the \(n_i\) pairs consists only of constant sign vectors.
The [four-letter argument](#four-letters) gives two classes of size \(n_i\) on these roots, and normality of \(D_i\) makes the rational action preserve their unordered partition.
Irreducibility of \(P_i\) makes this action transitive, so the two classes are proper blocks.
For a root \(\alpha\) in one block, its stabilizer is contained in the setwise stabilizer of the block, with index \(n_i\) by transitivity.
The fixed field \(E\) of the latter stabilizer therefore satisfies \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):E]=n_i>6\).
This contradicts the [intermediate-field bound from finite inertia](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields).

The kernel thus contains a vector \(v\) with both a zero and a one.
Pick three coordinates on which the entries are not all equal, and let \(\sigma\) cycle them.
Conjugating by a lift of \(\sigma\) permutes the coordinates of \(v\), so the kernel contains \(v+\sigma v\), a vector of weight two.
The action of \(A_{n_i}\) is transitive on pairs of coordinates, and the vectors of weight two span \(V_i\).
Hence \(D_i\) contains \(V_i\) and projects onto \(A_{n_i}\), giving \(D_i=V_i\rtimes A_{n_i}\).
The subgroups \(D_i\) act on disjoint rational factors and lie in \(J\), proving the product and degree formulas.
