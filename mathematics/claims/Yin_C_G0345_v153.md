# A large power sum after bounded earlier energy forces many exterior conjugates at a common radius

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound on the number beyond a radius of order \(1/(dL)\)

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic integer unit, with degree \(d\), logarithmic Mahler measure \(L\), and power sums \(p_j=\sum_{\alpha}\alpha^j\) over its conjugates.
Fix \(0<\delta\le1,\ \eta>0,\ B\ge0\).
Suppose an integer \(q\) satisfies
\[
 \delta d\le q\le d,\qquad |p_q|\ge\eta d,\qquad
                            \sum_{j=1}^{q-1}|p_j|^2\le B d^2.                  \tag{1}
\]
There are positive constants \(a,c,L_*\), depending only on these fixed parameters, such that for \(0<L<L_*\),
\[
    \#\{\alpha:d\log|\alpha|>a/L\}
                        \ \ge\ \frac{c}{L\log^2(2/L)}.                         \tag{2}
\]
This includes the first nonzero coefficient at depth \(q\ge\delta d\), where Newton's identities give (1) with \(B=0,\ \eta=\delta\).
The earlier powers in (1) may also be nonzero.

More generally, for each \(t\ge0\) write \(I(t)=\{\alpha:\log|\alpha|>t\}\) and \(g_\alpha=\log|\alpha|-t\) on \(I(t)\).
There is an absolute \(\epsilon>0\) such that, for sufficiently small \(L\) under (1), the existence of positive integers \(k_\alpha\) with
\[
       \sum_{I(t)} k_\alpha\le q/2,\qquad
       \sum_{I(t)} e^{-k_\alpha g_\alpha}\le\epsilon                          \tag{3}
\]
implies \(L(1+dt)\gg_{\delta,\eta,B}1\).
In particular the empty set is allowed in (3).
This criterion retains the individual distances to the chosen radius.
For example, with \(G=\sum_{I(t)}1/g_\alpha>0\) and \(w_\alpha=(g_\alpha G)^{-1}\), condition (3) holds whenever
\[
 |I(t)|+G\left(\log(1/\epsilon)+\sum_{I(t)} w_\alpha\log(1/w_\alpha)\right)\le q/2.
                                                                            \tag{4}
\]
Indeed one can round \(g_\alpha^{-1}\log(1/(\epsilon w_\alpha))\) upward.

The coordinator proposed removing exterior powers with a polynomial, varying the common radius, and applying the construction to bounded earlier energy.
The contributor supplied the energy comparison by Fejér and Poisson kernels, the weighted choice (4), and the endpoint and degree estimates below.

<a id="remnant"></a>
## Bounding the moments left by the polynomial

For \(t\ge0\) put \(\gamma_\alpha=e^{-t}\alpha\).
For each \(\alpha\notin I(t)\), take the positive circle probability with positive Fourier moments \(\gamma_\alpha^j\).
This is Poisson measure at \(\gamma_\alpha\) in the open disk and unit point mass at \(\gamma_\alpha\) on the circle.
Denote their sum by \(\mu\), its moments by \(u_j\), and its mass by \(u_0\le d\).
For any positive finite circle measure define
\[
        T_m=\sum_{|\ell|<m}|\widehat\mu(\ell)|^2,\qquad
        F_m(z)=m^{-1}|1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1}|^2 .
\]
For integers \(H,m\ge1\), one has \(F_{Hm}\le H F_m\) by factoring the geometric sum.
The double integral against \(\mu\) of \(F_m(z\bar w)\) is \(\sum_{|\ell|<m}(1-|\ell|/m)|\widehat\mu(\ell)|^2\le T_m\).
Consequently whenever \(Hm\ge2s\) one has \(T_s\le2H T_m\).
In particular for \(m=\lfloor q/2\rfloor\), \(q\ge4\), this gives \(T_q\le12 T_m\).
It also gives
\[
              u_0^2+2\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-j/d}|u_j|^2\ll_\delta T_m,              \tag{5}
\]
by summing over blocks \(vd\le j<(v+1)d\), since \(T_{(v+1)d}\ll_\delta(v+1)T_m\).
The zero Fourier coefficient on the left is included once.

Now suppose (3) holds and define
\[
   A(z)=\prod_{\alpha\in I(t)}(1-(z/\gamma_\alpha)^{k_\alpha})
            =1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{D} A_\ell z^\ell,\qquad D\le q/2 .
\]
Then \(\sum_{\ell=1}^{D}|A_\ell|\le e^\epsilon-1=\epsilon_1\).
For \(1\le j<m\), evaluation at the scaled roots gives
\[
 u_j+\sum_{\ell=1}^{D}A_\ell u_{j+\ell}
       =\sum_{\ell=0}^{D}A_\ell e^{-t(j+\ell)}p_{j+\ell},\qquad A_0=1.
\]
All indices on the right are positive and strictly below \(q\), including when \(q\) is even and \(D=q/2\).
The \(\ell^2\) triangle inequality for these shifted data gives
\[
 \Big(\sum_{1\le j<m}|u_j|^2\Big)^{1/2}
       \le (1+\epsilon_1)\sqrt B\,d+\epsilon_1\sqrt{T_q}.
\]
Using \(u_0\le d\) and \(T_q\le12T_m\), we obtain \(T_m\ll(1+B)d^2\) by choosing the absolute \(\epsilon\) so that \(\sqrt{24}\epsilon_1<1/2\).
This argument also covers \(I(t)=\varnothing\) and a zero remnant measure.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Comparing the Poisson kernels before and after reflection

Let \(\beta_\alpha=\exp(-|\log|\alpha||+i\arg\alpha)\) be the reflected conjugates, and \(v_j=\sum_\alpha\beta_\alpha^j\).
First use only \(\alpha\notin I(t)\), writing \(v_j^0\) for this partial sum.
For such an index the two nonnegative radial logarithms \(t-\log|\alpha|\) and \(|\log|\alpha||\) differ by at most \(t\).
With \(x=1/d\), the smoothed energies, including the squared mass, for the scaled remnant and the reflected remnant are sums over ordered pairs of Poisson kernels of the form
\[
    \frac{1-e^{-2u}}{|1-e^{-u+i\phi}|^2}
     =\frac{1}{p\cos^2(\phi/2)+p^{-1}\sin^2(\phi/2)},\qquad p=\tanh(u/2).
\]
Here \(\phi\) is the phase difference and \(u\) is \(x\) plus the sum of the two relevant radial logarithms.
Both choices of \(u\) are at least \(x\) and differ by at most \(2t\), so their ratio and its inverse are at most \(1+2dt\).
The same comparison holds for the two values of \(p\) by monotonicity and concavity of the hyperbolic tangent on the positive axis.
It therefore holds for the two kernels at the fixed \(\phi\).
Summing the positive kernels and using (5) proves
\[
       \sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_j^0|^2\ll_{\delta,B} (1+dt)d^2 .                    \tag{6}
\]
For the remaining reflected roots of logarithmic modulus \(-b_\alpha\), with \(b_\alpha=\log|\alpha|>t\), their contribution to the \(\ell^2\) norm in (6) is at most
\[
   \sum_{\alpha\in I(t)}(e^{2b_\alpha}-1)^{-1/2}
        \le \left(\frac{|I(t)|}{2}\sum_{I(t)}\frac1{b_\alpha}\right)^{1/2}
        \ll d.
\]
Indeed (3) implies \(g_\alpha k_\alpha\ge\log(1/\epsilon)\) for each term and \(b_\alpha\ge g_\alpha\), while \(|I(t)|\le D\le d\).
Thus the same energy bound holds for the full \(v_j\).

<a id="spike"></a>
## Using the large power sum at the original scale

Apply the [finite Blaschke coefficient identity](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#filter) with every exterior root selected.
For its coefficients \(f_\ell\) one has \(f_0=1,\ \sum_{\ell\ge1}|f_\ell|^2=e^{2L}-1\).
Keeping the earlier powers in that identity gives
\[
   p_q=-q f_q+v_q+\sum_{\ell=1}^{q-1} f_\ell(v_{q-\ell}-p_{q-\ell}).
\]
The [moving reflected moment bound](Yin_C_G0345_v149.md#powers) gives \(|v_q|/d=o(1)\) uniformly as \(L\to0\).
Dividing this identity by \(d\), Cauchy–Schwarz and (1), (6) yield
\[
          \eta\le o(1)+O_{\delta,B}\bigl(\sqrt{L(1+dt)}\bigr).
\]
This proves the criterion (3), uniformly in \(t\) and the chosen integers.
Bounded degrees can be omitted by the positive minimum of \(L\) for bounded-degree nontorsion monic inputs, from finiteness at bounded measure.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Choosing a radius and counting the conjugates

Choose \(a>0\) so small that \(t_0=a/(dL)\) satisfies \(L(1+2dt_0)=L+2a\) below the lower bound proved under (3) for all sufficiently small \(L\).
Write \(r=\#\{\alpha:b_\alpha>t_0\}\).
If \(r=0\), (3) already holds at \(t_0\), a contradiction.
For \(r\ge1\) delete from \([t_0,2t_0]\) all points within \(t_0/(8r)\) of any of those \(r\) logarithms.
The retained set has length at least \(3t_0/4\).
The integral there of \({\bf1}_{t<b_\alpha}/(b_\alpha-t)\) is \(O(\log(2+r))\) per selected logarithm.
Indeed the domain has span at most \(t_0\) and the denominator is at least \(t_0/(8r)\), giving an integral at most \(\log(1+8r)\).
Thus at some retained \(t\) one has
\[
                \sum_{b_\alpha>t}\frac1{b_\alpha-t}
                     \ll \frac{r\log(2+r)}{t_0}.
\]
Taking \(k_\alpha=\lceil\log(r/\epsilon)/(b_\alpha-t)\rceil\) makes the exponential sum in (3) at most \(\epsilon\), and its degree cost is at most
\[
                r+C r\log^2(2+r)/t_0.
\]
If \(r\log^2(2+r)\le c_1/L\), the second term is at most \(q/4\) by taking \(c_1\) sufficiently small in terms of \(a,\delta\).
The first term is at most \(q/4\) eventually since \(dL\to\infty\).
For this last fact the [degree lower bound under bounded earlier energy](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#energy) gives \(L\gg_{\delta,\eta,B}\log\log(3d)/\log(2d)\).
This contradicts the choice of \(t_0\).
Hence \(r\log^2(2+r)>c_1/L\); considering \(r\ge1/L\) and \(r<1/L\) separately proves (2).
