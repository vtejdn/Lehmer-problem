# Real conjugates, the action of complex conjugation and a quantitative height bound

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound for the proportion of real conjugates

Let \(w\ne0\) be an algebraic number of infinite multiplicative order, \(d=[\mathbb Q(w):\mathbb Q]\), \(h=h(w)\) its absolute logarithmic Weil height, and \(m(w)=dh\) its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
If a proportion \(p>0\) of its conjugates are real, then
\[
                             h>\frac{p^2}{100\log(8/p)} .                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(p\ll\sqrt{h\log(2/h)}\) as \(h\to0\).
The [real-conjugate bound of Garza](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) gives \(h\ge(p/2)\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/p})\) on the same class.

There is also a bound for complex conjugation acting on the conjugates when \(w\) itself has no real image.
In a finite Galois field containing all the conjugates, write \(G\) for the Galois group and \(c\) for complex conjugation.
For a fixed conjugate \(a\) and any conjugate \(b\) define
\[
                        p_b=\Pr_{g\in G}\big(g^{-1}cg(a)=b\big)
\]
with uniform probability.
For every \(b\ne a^{-1}\) with \(p_b>0\), (1) holds with \(p=p_b\).
Put \(C=[G:C_G(c)]\), let \(\delta\) be the proportion of all conjugates of \(w\) off the unit circle, and put \(D=[T(w):T]\), where \(T=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{tr}}\) is the field of all totally real algebraic numbers.
If \(\delta>0\), then
\[
           m(w)>\frac{d}{100 C^2\log(8C)},\qquad
           m(w)>\frac{d\,\delta^2}{100 D^2\log(8D/\delta)} .             \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(\delta>0\) for every nontorsion algebraic unit.
The second bound gives a uniform positive Mahler gap whenever the off-circle fraction is bounded below and \(D=O(\sqrt{d/\log(2d)})\).
The first gives such a gap whenever \(C=O(\sqrt{d/\log(2d)})\), with no hypothesis on the radial rank or the number of off-circle pairs.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Saving the real-place contribution in a Vandermonde product

For any nontorsion nonzero algebraic number \(z\) let \(H=h(z)>0\), and let \(R>0\) be the fraction of its embeddings taking positive real values.
For integers \(N\ge2\) put \(B_N=N(N-1)/2\) and \(J=(N+1)/3\).
We claim
\[
        R\log^+\!\left(\frac{R}{2JH}\right)
                  \le(1-R)\frac{\log N}{N-1}+JH.                         \tag{3}
\]
Use the [consecutive-power auxiliary polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#auxiliary)
\[
           A_N(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(X^j-1)^{N-j},\qquad
           \deg A_N=B_N J .
\]
The source gives \(|A_N(v)|\le N^{N/2}\max(1,|v|)^{B_NJ}\) for all complex \(v\), by the Vandermonde determinant on the unit circle and the maximum principle.
At every finite place the same bound holds with factor one in place of \(N^{N/2}\), since \(A_N\) has integral coefficients.

At a positive real value \(v\), write \(s=|\log v|>0\).
Each normalized factor satisfies
\[
              \frac{|v^j-1|}{\max(1,v)^j}=1-e^{-js}\le\min(1,js).
\]
With probability weights \((N-j)/B_N\) the mean of \(j\) is \(J\).
By concavity of \(s'\mapsto\log\min(1,s')\) on \(s'>0\), the log modulus of the normalized product divided by \(B_N\) is at most \(\log\min(1,Js)\).
Across the positive real embeddings the mean of \(s\) is at most \(2H/R\), since the sum of the absolute logarithms over all archimedean embeddings divided by the degree is at most \(h(z)+h(z^{-1})=2H\).
Applying concavity a second time bounds the total normalized contribution from the positive real embeddings by \(R\log\min(1,2JH/R)\).
The others contribute at most \((1-R)\log N/(N-1)\) after division by \(B_N\).
The product formula for \(A_N(z)\ne0\) and the extracted local factors \(\max(1,|z|_v)^{B_NJ}\), of total normalized log \(B_NJH\), now give (3).
The argument retains the archimedean saving at the indicated embeddings in addition to the [finite-place estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) supplied by the same auxiliary polynomial.

Suppose \(z\) has height \(H\le2h\) and a proportion at least \(p\) of positive real conjugate images, counted in any common containing field.
The same proportion is obtained using its own degree, by uniform extension of embeddings.
Put \(L_0=\log(8/p)\) and \(N=\lceil16L_0/p\rceil\).
Then \(N-1\ge15L_0/p\), \(J\le6L_0/p\), and \(\log N\le2L_0\), since \(N\le17L_0/p\) and \(\log(17/8)+\log L_0\le L_0\) for \(L_0\ge\log8\).
If \(h\le p^2/(100L_0)\), then \(JH\le12p/100\).
The left side of (3) is at least \(p\log(25/6)>p\), and its right side is at most \(2p/15+12p/100<p\).
This contradiction gives (1) under these conditions on \(z\).
For the first assertion take \(z=w^2\), which at every real conjugate of \(w\) is positive and satisfies \(H=2h\).
For the asymptotic form, if \(p\ge\sqrt h\), then \(\log(8/p)\le\log(8/\sqrt h)\).
The case \(p<\sqrt h\) already has the stated bound.

<a id="action"></a>
## Individual images under conjugates of the complex conjugation

Fix \(a,b\) as in the statement with \(p_b>0\).
For every \(g\) counted by \(p_b\), one has \(g(ab)=g(a)\overline{g(a)}=|g(a)|^2>0\).
If \(ab\) were torsion, this positive real image would be one, forcing \(ab=1\).
Thus \(b\ne a^{-1}\) makes \(z=ab\) nontorsion without any assumption on torsion quotients of other conjugates.
It satisfies \(h(z)\le2h\), and the positive real images account for at least \(p_b\) of its embeddings.
The preceding proof gives (1) for this probability.

For the first part of (2) choose \(a\) off the circle and \(b=c(a)\).
Then \(b\ne a^{-1}\), and at least the elements of the centralizer of \(c\) are counted, giving \(p_b\ge1/C\).
For the other part, the probability of the inverse image \(b=a^{-1}\) is precisely \(1-\delta\), since it occurs exactly when \(|g(a)|=1\).
If the inverse is not a conjugate, the same observation gives zero probability and \(\delta=1\).
All the images \(g^{-1}cg(a)\) belong to the orbit of \(a\) under the normal subgroup generated by \(c\).
This orbit has size \(D\), by the [fixed-field description over \(T\)](Yin_C_G0901_v54.md#signs) and conjugacy of the fields \(T(a),T(w)\).
One of the other images therefore has \(p_b\ge\delta/D\).
Applying the monotonicity of \(p^2/\log(8/p)\) gives (2).

If positive unit measures tend to zero, bounded-degree finiteness forces \(d\to\infty\), and (1) forces every noninverse \(p_b\) to be \(o(\sqrt{\log d/d})\), uniformly in the selected roots.
Otherwise along a subsequence \(p_b\ge\epsilon\sqrt{\log d/d}\), giving a positive lower bound on \(d p_b^2/\log(8/p_b)\).
Consequently \(C\sqrt{\log d/d}\to\infty\), and with a positive lower bound on \(\delta\) one also needs \(D\sqrt{\log d/d}\to\infty\).
This includes the case of no circle conjugates.

<a id="signature"></a>
## A signature range in the orthogonal branch

For \(r>0\) real conjugates, (1) says
\[
                         m(w)>\frac{r^2}{100d\log(8d/r)} .
\]
Every class with \(r\ge\epsilon\sqrt{d\log(2d)}\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\) therefore has a uniform positive gap.
For reciprocal units with totally real trace, all off-circle conjugates are real.
In particular any shrinking sequence with totally real trace has only \(o(\sqrt{d\log d})\) off-circle roots.
The same necessary bound on its number of real roots applies in every signature.

There are actual orthogonal units in the stated signature range.
For arbitrarily large even \(n\) take totally real trace fields \(F\) of degree \(n\) with natural \(S_n\)-action, as in the [symmetric-group construction](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#bases).
For any \(2\le a<n\), choose an integral \(b\in F\) positive at exactly \(a\) real places and negative at the others by the open-orthant lattice argument in the [quadratic-extension construction](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#extensions).
Then \(K=F(\sqrt b)\) is quadratic, and the relative norm-one unit rank is \(a\), by the unit theorem in signatures \((2a,n-a)\) and \((n,0)\) and the fact that the norm image contains the squares of the base units.
Take a nontorsion relative unit \(w\).
At the split real places its two embeddings are real reciprocals distinct from signs, whereas at the others both have modulus one and are nonreal.
Thus \(w+w^{-1}\in F\) has conjugate values both outside and inside \([-2,2]\), so it is not rational.
The natural \(S_n\)-action is primitive, giving no proper intermediate field between \(\mathbb Q\) and \(F\).
It follows that \(F=\mathbb Q(w+w^{-1})\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(w)\), since inversion is nontrivial on \(w\).
The [primitive mixed-trace signature test](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs) puts \(w\) in the Salem-orthogonal branch because there are \(a\ge2\) real exterior roots.
The bound applies with \(d=2n\) and \(r=2a\), including choices of \(a\) of order \(\sqrt{n\log n}\).
These exclusions leave the unrestricted orthogonal infimum open.
