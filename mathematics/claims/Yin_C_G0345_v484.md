# Every passing degree at bounded term count and width proportional to the degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact degree sets for the two coefficient budgets

For a fixed integer \(s\ge2\) there is an effective \(p_0(s)\) with the following property.
Fix a prime \(p>\max(3,p_0(s))\).
Use either [narrow-width coefficient test](Yin_C_G0345_v473.md#statement), with at most \(s\) positions independently in each row \(A_+,A_-\).
The two signs must have genuine double zeros at a common reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit \(\gamma\) and its square, both of the same degree \(d\).
In particular on each row both \(A_\pm(\gamma^p)\) and \(A_\pm(\gamma^{2p})\) must be nonzero.
Use common formal width \(b=5d/2-1\).
For the individual budget take \(V=\max(\|A_+\|_1,\|A_-\|_1)^8\).
For the transverse budget take \(V=(\|A_+\|_1\|A_-\|_1)^4\) and require
\[
                      A_+ A_-^{*b}+A_- A_+^{*b}\ne0,\qquad
                      A^{*b}(Y)=Y^bA(Y^{-1}).
\]
The strict cutoff is \(V<2^p\), optionally strengthened to \((2pb+1)^4V<2^p\).

The set of all passing degrees is effectively computable as a finite union of one-sided arithmetic progressions and finite sets.
Its natural density on the positive integers is a computable rational number.
The finitely many distinct positive logarithmic Mahler measures occurring among those presentations can also be listed, with the degrees realizing each.
There is no bound on the normalized span of the occupied positions, and powers of the unit may have smaller degrees.
In the coefficient-only test at \(b=5d/2-1\), every nonempty degree set has positive density.

The same classification, including the measure lists, applies if the admissible pairs \((d,b)\) are specified by a finite Boolean combination of integer linear equalities, inequalities and fixed-modulus congruences, with \(0\le b\le Ld+L\) for a supplied integer \(L\).
This includes a fixed rational affine width with rounding, or the whole range \(2d\le b<3d\).
The positive-density statement above uses the specified exact width and coefficient-only budget.

<a id="bases"></a>
## Recovering every target from finitely many algebraic units

Both budgets give \(1\le H_\pm=\|A_\pm\|_1\le2^p\).
Fix a genuine row and partition its terms at the two evaluation points into minimal simultaneous zero subsets.
Its positive-label terms have nonzero total at \(\gamma\), so at least one minimal subset \(I\) meets both residue classes.
For the actual exponents \(a_i\) of its terms let \(g=\gcd_{i,j\in I}|a_i-a_j|\).
The [mixed subsum estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v483.md#statement), with \(m=2s,\ \delta=1\), gives an effective \(p_0(s)\) and a bound \(R(s,p)\) for the range of \((a_i-\min_{j\in I}a_j)/g\).
The difference of the two residues is a unit modulo \(p\).
Thus \(x=\gamma^g\) is a root of a nonzero integral polynomial of degree at most \(R(s,p)\) and one-norm at most \(2^{p+1}\), by summing this subset with its normalized exponents.
These polynomials can all be listed and factored.
Keep the nontorsion algebraic units among their roots, using their minimum polynomials or exact algebraic-number representatives.
This finite list contains a suitable \(x\) for every passing target.

For each such \(x\) take the [effective radical modulus](Yin_C_G0345_v475.md#statement) \(M=M_x\).
Run through the divisors \(m\mid M\) and all \(\xi\) such that \(\xi^m=x\).
Keep the reciprocal \(\xi\) of degree \(f=[\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q]=[\mathbb Q(\xi^2):\mathbb Q]\).
For these data the permitted indices are exactly
\[
                     k\ge1,\qquad k\text{ odd},\qquad \gcd(k,M/m)=1.       \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(m=\gcd(mk,M)\) is equivalent to the last condition, and for \(g=mk\) every root \(\gamma^k=\xi\) has minimum \(P_\xi(T^k)\) by the modulus theorem.
Its [reciprocity and square-degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v475.md#square) gives the filter on \(\xi\) and the oddness of \(k\).
All roots for (1) are reciprocal nontorsion units of degree \(d=fk\) with square degree \(d\).
Conversely the original \(\gamma^g=x\) is recovered with \(m=\gcd(g,M)\), \(k=g/m\) and \(\xi=\gamma^k\).
Its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(m(P_\xi)\), unchanged under \(T\mapsto T^k\).
Here \(m(P)\) denotes the measure of a polynomial separately from the positive divisor \(m\).

<a id="placement"></a>
## Encoding all coefficient placements and the two conjugate orbits

Fix one set of data \(x,m,\xi\) from the finite list.
Enumerate the lengths at most \(s\) and all ordered lists of nonzero integer coefficients for the two rows that pass the coefficient cutoff.
Use integer variables \(k,b,j_{\sigma,i}\) with (1), \(d=fk\), the width conditions and strictly increasing positions \(0\le j_{\sigma,1}<\cdots<j_{\sigma,t_\sigma}\le b\) for \(\sigma=+,-\).
For each position of row \(\sigma\), put into its graph the two exponents and coefficients
\[
            (a,c)=(pj_{\sigma,i},c_{\sigma,i}),\qquad
            (a,c)=(pb+1-pj_{\sigma,i},\sigma c_{\sigma,i}).
\]
The two labels are retained to identify the numerator terms.
The graphs have no coincident exponents within one row, since the two lists have different residues modulo \(p\).

Every graph exponent is between zero and \(p(Lfk+L)+1\).
For each graph term enumerate its possible quotient \(w=\lfloor a/k\rfloor\) in the finite interval \(0\le w\le pL(f+1)+1\).
Partition the labels of each graph into classes \(\ell\), assigning a variable \(v_\ell\) to each class with \(0\le v_\ell<k\) and imposing distinct remainders for distinct classes within the same graph.
Impose \(a=w k+v_\ell\) for each of its terms.
Since \(w\) is fixed on the enumerated choice, every new condition is linear in the remaining integer variables.
All actual placements have one of these patterns.

For \(u=1,2\), the powers \(1,\gamma^u,\ldots,(\gamma^u)^{k-1}\) form a basis of \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^u)\) over \(\mathbb Q(\xi^u)\).
In fact \((\gamma^u)^k=\xi^u\), and the ratio of the two degrees is \(fk/f=k\) for each \(u\).
A graph evaluated at \(\gamma^u\) therefore vanishes exactly when, in every class \(\ell\),
\[
                        \sum_{i\in\ell}c_i(\xi^u)^{w_i}=0.
\]
For its Euler derivative the analogous class sum has the extra coefficient \(a_i=w_i k+v_\ell\).
Once the value vanishes, this derivative condition reduces precisely to
\[
                        \sum_{i\in\ell} w_i c_i(\xi^u)^{w_i}=0 .
\]
Each of these is an equality of known algebraic numbers.
Impose both equalities for every class, every graph and both values of \(u\).
They give the required double zeros, since the nodes are nonzero and characteristic zero makes the two minimum polynomials separable.
The minima for \(u=1,2\) are distinct because \(h(\gamma^2)=2h(\gamma)>0\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height.

For each graph and each \(u\), compute the first class sums again using only terms \(a=pj_{\sigma,i}\) from the numerator.
Require at least one such sum to be nonzero, with the witnessing class allowed to depend on the graph and on \(u\).
The same basis argument makes this exactly the needed numerator test.
For the transverse condition, the monomials in the determinant have exponents \(b+j_{+,i}-j_{-,j}\) and \(b+j_{-,j}-j_{+,i}\), each with coefficient \(c_{+,i}c_{-,j}\).
Enumerate equality classes of these exponents, impose the equalities within each class and inequalities between distinct classes, and retain a pattern exactly when at least one combined coefficient is nonzero.
All of these exponent tests are linear.
For the optional width charge, at fixed \(p,V\) one can compute the largest allowed \(b\) by the strict integer inequality \((2pb+1)^4 V<2^p\), or discard the pattern if no nonnegative \(b\) works.

<a id="projection"></a>
## Effective projection to the radical index

After the finite enumerations the remaining tests form a finite disjunction of systems of linear inequalities and congruences of fixed modulus over integers.
The coprimality in (1) is a finite list of residue classes.
The existential projection of such a system in the variable \(k\) is computable in the same language.
For completeness, eliminate an integer variable \(z\) one conjunction at a time, expressing strict inequalities and equalities as non-strict integer bounds and splitting finite disjunctions.
Partition variables into residue classes for the fixed moduli in the conjunction and substitute each variable as its residue plus that common modulus times an unrestricted integer.
This handles the congruences, keeping the corresponding residue information when returning to the original coordinates.
For the resulting inequalities in \(z\), take the least common multiple \(D\) of the nonzero absolute coefficients of \(z\), using \(D=1\) for an empty list.
Multiplying each inequality involving \(z\) by its needed positive integer makes it an upper or lower bound on \(Dz\) with an integral linear right side in the remaining variables.
With both kinds present, branch on which lower bound is largest and its residue modulo \(D\).
The smallest multiple of \(D\) exceeding or equalling that bound is then a known integer increment above it, and testing it against every upper bound eliminates \(z\).
A one-sided collection admits a multiple of \(D\) in its unbounded direction.
The transformation back from the fixed residue classes retains linear inequalities and congruences by clearing denominators.
Repeating this procedure leaves linear inequalities and fixed-modulus congruences in \(k\) alone.
They determine a finite set and finitely many one-sided arithmetic progressions effectively.

Every point produced by this projection gives actual rows at the indicated width and a genuine common unit of degree \(fk\), since any \(k\)th root of \(\xi\) has the bases used to certify the graph tests.
Enlarging the initial finite list of base units beyond those arising from actual mixed blocks therefore introduces no passing degrees without witnesses.
Conversely each actual witness recovers a listed base and passes all of the finite pattern and integer tests.
Multiplying the resulting \(k\)'s by \(f\) and taking the union gives the complete degree set.
One can combine the periods to compute its density and can collect the degree sets for each equal measure.
Equality of the multiplicative Mahler measures is decidable because each is a positive real algebraic number obtained from the conjugates of \(\xi\).

At the exact width \(5d/2-1\) every coefficient-only witness of degree \(d\) supplies witnesses of degrees \(dt\) for an entire residue class of positive \(t\) by the [common substitution and translation](Yin_C_G0345_v473.md#lift).
In the notation of that result one may take \(t\equiv1\bmod pB_\gamma\).
The substitution and translation preserve each term count and the coefficient budget, and they preserve the determinant condition when required.
This gives the stated positive density in the nonempty case.
No period or first passing degree has been numerically evaluated here.

A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin developed the passage from a minimal mixed block to a finite list of radical bases and the integer projection for unrestricted placements at bounded width ratio.
