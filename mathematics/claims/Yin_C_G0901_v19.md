# Exact cyclotomic multiplicities at prescribed orders

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which of the prescribed orbits contribute a resultant

Let \(B\) be a finite set of positive integers and \(Q\) any fixed monic cyclotomic product, now allowing factors at orders in \(B\).
Forbid every factor \(\Phi_b,\ b\in B\), in the additional cyclotomic multiplier \(C\).
The limit of \(a^{-1}\log\min_C\|Q^aC\|_1\) is
\[
   \max\bigl(\{0\}\cup
      \{\,\varphi(b)^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,Q)|:
                               b\in B,\ \Phi_b\nmid Q\,\}\bigr).        \tag{1}
\]
One may impose any cofactor degree budget \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
In particular, to impose specified positive integer multiplicities \(a t_b\) at every one of finitely many orbits, the least logarithmic coefficient cost is \(o(a)\) for fixed \((t_b)_{b\in B}\).
The multiplicities here are exact.

There is also a bound uniform over all choices of the finite forbidden set for general inputs.
Let \(P\) be a nonzero real polynomial of degree \(d\ge1\) with no root at those orbits.
For positive integers \(t_b\), let \(\Gamma_B(P;t)\) denote the limit of the normalized infima of the log coefficient norm of \(P^a C\) over cyclotomic products \(C\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_b}C=a t_b\) for every \(b\in B\).
The same limit holds with \(W(a)/a\to\infty\) as a budget for \(\deg C\), for each fixed choice of input data.
For every integer \(M\ge2\), if every \(t_b\ge d M^2\), then
\[
             m(P)\le\Gamma_B(P;t)\le m(P)+C d M^{-1/64},                \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute \(C\).
The limit defining \(\Gamma_B(P;t)\) as \(a\to\infty\) is taken for each fixed \(B,t\).
In particular for fixed \(P\), as the smallest prescribed multiplicity per copy tends to infinity, the limiting cost tends to \(m(P)\), uniformly over finite nonempty \(B\) avoiding its roots and over the proportions of the prescribed multiplicities.

<a id="dual"></a>
## Finite forbidden-order duality for an input with specified zeros

For a nonzero real polynomial \(G\), use the compact space of even probabilities on the circle.
Let \(\mathcal D_B\) consist of those satisfying \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for all \(n\notin B\).
Then
\[
 \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac1a\log\inf_C\|G^a C\|_1
                 =\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal D_B}\int\log|G|\,d\mu,           \tag{3}
\]
where all orders in \(B\) are forbidden in \(C\).
At a finite degree budget there are finitely many such products, so the infimum there is a minimum.
For nonzero integer inputs positive coefficient one-norms are integers and the unrestricted minimum is also attained.
Indeed every admissible product has log supremum at least its integral against each dual probability.
If the coefficient rate exceeded the supremum, the [finite separation argument](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#dual), exhausting the allowed orders, would give a dual probability strictly exceeding that supremum.
In detail, at every target strictly above the supremum there must be finitely many nonnegative weights which, when added on the allowed logarithms to \(\log|G|\), give circle supremum at most the target.
Otherwise the compact convex argument with finite truncations produces the contradictory probability.
Their [upward rounding](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#integer) attains this bound asymptotically with degree \(O(a)\) for each fixed tolerance, proving (3) also under the stated width budgets.
The supremum is finite and at least \(m(G)\), by the Haar test.

For \(G=Q\), a dual probability whose objective is finite has no mass on any primitive orbit that is a zero of \(Q\).
It has no atoms at allowed roots of unity either, by the dual constraints.
In the [coprime power average](Yin_C_G0901_v18.md#measures) choose the modulus divisible by all orders in \(B\) and \(Q\).
For each averaging exponent \(j\), the quotient \(Q(X^j)/Q(X)\) is a cyclotomic product avoiding all forbidden factors, including the shared orders: powering permutes the primitive roots at each such order and preserves their individual multiplicities.
Thus monotonicity of the logarithmic integral still holds.
The limiting Haar part has integral zero, and only forbidden orbits disjoint from \(Q\) can carry mass in the limiting test.
The same truncation upper bound as in that power average proves the upper bound in (1).
For each unoccupied forbidden order the uniform measure on its primitive roots belongs to \(\mathcal D_B\) by the nonzero integral resultant with every allowed factor.
Together with Haar these measures give the reverse inequality.
Taking \(Q=\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{t_b}\) proves the stated zero limiting cost.

<a id="prescription"></a>
## The uniform estimate when all exceptional orbits are occupied

Apply (3) with \(G=P\prod_{b\in B}\Phi_b^{t_b}\).
If a total cofactor degree budget is used, the compulsory factors take \(a\sum_b t_b\varphi(b)\) degrees, a fixed multiple of \(a\), so the remaining budget divided by \(a\) still diverges.
Every dual probability with finite objective now has no torsion atoms at all.
Each \(I_b=\int\log|\Phi_b|\,d\mu\), including for \(b\in B\), is finite: the summands in \(\log|G|\) are bounded above and all \(t_b>0\).
For each \(b\in B\) the quotient \(\Phi_b(X^j)/\Phi_b(X)\) in the coprime power average avoids every forbidden factor.
The weak limit of the power images is Haar because there are no torsion atoms.
It follows that \(I_b\le0\) by [the same upper semicontinuity argument](Yin_C_G0901_v18.md#measures).
For allowed \(n\) it also follows that \(I_n=0\), using the nonnegative dual lower bound.

If \(I_n\ge-M^{-2}\) for all \(1\le n\le M\), the [finite cyclotomic moment estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v15.md#inversion) bounds \(\int\log|P|\,d\mu\) by \(m(P)+C d M^{-1/64}\).
Adding the nonpositive terms \(t_b I_b\) preserves that upper bound.
If some one of these tests fails, it is at a forbidden order.
Its contribution to the objective is less than \(-d\) by the prescribed multiplicity, and every other forbidden contribution is nonpositive.
The pointwise upper bound \(\log|P|\le m(P)+d\log2\) gives \(\int\log|G|\,d\mu\le m(P)\) in this case.
These alternatives give the upper bound in (2).
The lower bound is the Haar integral of \(\log|G|\).
This result permits all prescribed orders to carry high linear multiplicity and leaves open the cost of making every prescribed multiplicity zero for a general nontorsion input.
