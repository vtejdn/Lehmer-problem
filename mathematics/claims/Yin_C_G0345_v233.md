# A resultant bound for individual factors at a separated binomial end

<a id="statement"></a>
## The degree bound and the product over binomial roots

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have coefficient length \(\|P\|_1\le L\) and a decomposition
\[
 P=A(X)+\sum_{i=1}^k c_iX^{e_i}+X^D B(X^{-1}),\qquad
 B=B_0+B_1X^b,\qquad
 a=\deg A< e_1<\cdots<e_k<D-b<D .
\]
Assume \(A(0)B_0B_1\ne0,\ k\ge1\), and each \(c_i\ne0\), and put \(\Delta=\min(e_1,D-e_k)\) and \(\mu=-B_0/B_1\).
Let \(S\mid P\) be a monic reciprocal irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial, with degree \(d\), logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), and absolute logarithmic height \(h=m/d\).
If \(|\mu|\ge1\), then \(d\gg_L\Delta\).
If \(|\mu|<1\), put
\[
 r=\deg\gcd(A,B),\qquad
 V=\prod_{\substack{B(\eta)=0\\ A(\eta)\ne0}}|A(\eta)|,\qquad
 \kappa=\max(0,-\log V).
\]
An empty product is one.
Then
\[
          1\ll_L\frac{d}{\Delta}(1+\kappa/b)+\frac{dr}{b^2},
          \qquad 0\le\kappa\le a\log|B_1| .                         \tag{1}
\]
Neither the degree of \(A\) nor the positions of its individual coefficients relative to \(b\) are restricted.

In the coprime case with \(|\mu|<1\) put \(W=1+\kappa/b\), and for \(|\mu|\ge1\) put \(W=1\) without requiring coprimality.
In these cases \(d\gg_L\Delta/W\), and every positive-measure integral divisor of \(P\) has logarithmic Mahler measure \(\gg_L\Delta/(DW)\).
For the particular unit factor \(S\), an independent bound \(\Delta h\ge\delta>0\) gives \(m\gg_L\delta/W\).
Thus a uniform individual-factor gap follows if \(\Delta/D\) is bounded below and \(W\) is bounded above.
For \(|\mu|<1\) and \(r>0\), (1) still controls the number of common roots needed if \(d(1+\kappa/b)/\Delta\) is small.

For example, whenever \(0<\ell<\ell+b<n<q<D-b<D\) and \(D/3\le n<q\le2D/3\), the seven-term polynomial
\[
             1+X^\ell+3X^{\ell+b}-11X^n+X^q+3X^{D-b}+X^D
\]
has an absolute positive gap for all positive-measure integral divisors.
Indeed \(B=1+3X^b\) and \(A=1+X^\ell B\), so \(A(\eta)=1\) on every root of \(B\) and \(W=1\), irrespective of \(\ell/b\).
The coefficient length is 21 and \(\Delta\ge D/3\).
This monic family has a real zero greater than one because its value at one is \(-1\).
It permits unbounded \(\ell/b\), for instance with \(D=12(\ell+b),\ n=D/3,\ q=D/2\).

<a id="ends"></a>
## Exterior roots near the binomial end

Write \(t=\max_{S(x)=0}\log|x|>0\), and put \(u=\log|x|\) for an exterior root.
Dominance of the leading term at such a root gives \(bt\le\log L\), since all remaining powers are at most \(D-b\).
Also \(m\le\log L\), by multiplicativity of Mahler measure and the coefficient length bound.
The [quadratic floor in the original degree](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement), together with the reciprocal pairing, gives
\[
                   1/20\le d\sum_{u>0}u^2\le dtm .
\]
In particular \(d/b\gg_L1\), which proves the degree bounds and (1) whenever \(\Delta/b\) stays below a sufficiently large constant depending on \(L\).

For \(|\mu|\ge1\) the end block \(X^bB(X^{-1})\) has all roots in the closed unit disk.
Its actual separating gap is \(G=D-b-e_k\ge\Delta-b\).
When \(\Delta/b\) is sufficiently large the [capacity estimate at the actual gap](Yin_C_G0345_v232.md#gaps) gives \(d\gg_L G\gg\Delta\).
Indeed the degree of the lower block is \(e_k\), and the [containing-region proof](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#application) uses the lower block only through its coefficient length and this actual gap at the radial containment step.

Assume now \(|\mu|<1\) and \(\Delta/b\) sufficiently large in terms of \(L\).
The equations \(P(x)=P(1/x)=0\) imply
\[
                   |B(1/x)|+|A(1/x)|\le 2L e^{-\Delta u}.             \tag{2}
\]
In the first equation all powers outside the binomial end are at most \(e_k\), and in the second all powers outside \(A\) are at least \(e_1\).
There are finitely many possible \(\mu\) for fixed \(L\).
Thus every root with \(\Delta u>C_L\), for \(C_L\) sufficiently large, satisfies \(c_L/b\le u\le C'_L/b\), since (2) puts \(|x|^{-b}\) between \(|\mu|/2\) and \((1+|\mu|)/2\).
For such a root there is a center \(\xi\) with \(\xi^b=\mu^{-1}\) and
\[
                      |x-\xi|\ll_L e^{-c_L\Delta/b}/b.
\]
This follows by taking the logarithm of \(\mu x^b\) near one and dividing it by \(b\).

Distinct roots above the cutoff have distinct centers.
Indeed the [one-root disks](Yin_C_G0345_v232.md#mesh) of radius \(\epsilon_L/b\) around the centers are disjoint, with \(\log |X|\ge c'_L/b\) on each closure and \(|B(1/X)|\gg_L1\) on each boundary.
These estimates hold for every \(b\ge1\), since the proof using \((1+v/b)^b\to e^v\) is uniform for large \(b\), and for the finitely many remaining \(b\) one can decrease the radii and take the minimum over the boundaries.
On each boundary the sum of the terms of \(P\) outside the binomial end, divided in absolute value by \(|X|^D\), is at most \(L|X|^{-(D-e_k)}\).
Rouché's theorem gives one root of \(P\), counted with multiplicity, in each disk for \(\Delta/b\) sufficiently large.
Every root above the cutoff is in its indicated disk by the preceding exponential estimate.

<a id="product"></a>
## Bounding the number of small values at distinct centers

Put \(\eta=1/\xi\) for a center reached by such a root.
The segment joining \(1/x\) to \(\eta\) lies in \(|y|\le\exp(-c''_L/b)\), shrinking \(c''_L>0\) if necessary.
Hence
\[
 |A'(y)|\le L\sup_{j\ge1} j\,\exp(-c''_L(j-1)/b)\ll_L b.
\]
By (2) and \(|1/x-\eta|\ll_L e^{-c_L\Delta/b}/b\), it follows that \(|A(\eta)|\ll_L e^{-c_L\Delta/b}\).
This derivative estimate is independent of \(a/b\).

At all binomial roots \(|A(\eta)|\le L\), since \(|\eta|<1\).
Let \(N\) count reached centers with \(A(\eta)\ne0\).
Taking the product over all centers where \(A\) is nonzero gives
\[
              e^{-\kappa}\le V\le L^b(C_L e^{-c_L\Delta/b})^N .
\]
For \(\Delta/b\) sufficiently large this gives \(N\ll_L b(b+\kappa)/\Delta\).
At most \(r\) additional centers can be reached, as \(B\) has simple roots.
The roots below or at the cutoff contribute at most \(C_Lm/\Delta\) to the exterior quadratic sum, and every root above it contributes at most \((C'_L/b)^2\).
The quadratic floor now gives (1), using \(m\le\log L\).

Take the primitive integral factor \(B_{\rm reg}\) of the primitive part of \(B\) whose roots are exactly the nonshared roots, with positive leading coefficient.
It exists by division by the primitive greatest common divisor, and its leading coefficient divides \(|B_1|\) by Gauss's lemma.
The nonzero integer resultant has absolute value \(\operatorname{lc}(B_{\rm reg})^a V\); this formula also holds for \(B_{\rm reg}=1\).
It follows that \(\kappa\le a\log |B_1|\), as asserted.

Finally, the [width bound for a sparse multiple](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(Dm/d\gg_L1\) since \(P\) has at most \(L\) nonzero terms.
Multiplying by the degree bound proves the measure conclusion for the reciprocal unit.
All remaining positive-measure irreducible factors have an absolute gap by the [nonunit and nonreciprocal reductions](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer), which suffices since \(\Delta/(DW)\le1\).
A positive-measure divisor contains such a positive-measure irreducible factor or has integer content at least two.
For the alternative using \(\Delta h\ge\delta\), directly multiply \(d\gg_L\Delta/W\) by \(h\).
