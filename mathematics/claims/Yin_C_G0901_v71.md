# Cyclotomic multiplicities forced by a small circle maximum

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound for all integral auxiliaries

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero, \(a=\operatorname{ord}_1 F\ge1\), \(D=\deg F\), \(L=D/a\), and \(H=\max_{|z|=1}|F(z)|\).
For every sequence with \(L\to\infty\),
\[
 \liminf\frac{D\log H}{a^2}\ \ge\
 C_*=\frac12\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{k!}{(2k-1)!}
       =0.6942224\ldots .                                                 \tag{1}
\]
The leading coefficient, noncyclotomic factors and all cyclotomic multiplicities are unrestricted.
The estimate also holds with the coefficient one-norm in place of \(H\).
The [near-circle multiplicity estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v12.md#statement) already gives \(D\log H/a^2\ge1/(\pi\mathrm e^2)\) at all \(L\), using \(m(F)\ge0\).
Here the larger asymptotic constant comes from integer resultants at distinct primitive roots of unity.

Write \(b_m=\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_m}F\) for the multiplicity of the \(m\)th cyclotomic polynomial, with \(b_1=a\).
Suppose along a sequence that \(L\to\infty\) and \(\kappa_L=D\log H/a^2\to\kappa<\infty\).
For any fixed \(k\ge1\), the squarefree conductors with exactly \(k\) prime factors satisfy
\[
  \liminf\frac1D\sum_{\substack{m\ {\rm squarefree}\\\omega(m)=k}}
             \varphi(m)b_m
       \ \ge\ \frac{k!}{2\kappa(2k-1)!}.                                  \tag{2}
\]
In particular a positive fraction of the degree is spent on prime conductors of size comparable to \(L\log L\), and a further positive fraction on products of two distinct growing primes at that scale.
The following local form specifies the conductors and their required multiplicities.

For a product \(m=p_1\cdots p_k\) of distinct primes put
\[
 \theta_i=\frac{\log p_i}{\log L},\qquad x_m=\frac{m}{L\log L}.
\]
For every fixed \(k\), \(V>0\) and \(\epsilon>0\), uniformly when \(x_m\le V\) and all \(\theta_i\ge\epsilon\),
\[
 \frac{mb_m}{a}\ \ge\
      \left(k!\prod_{i=1}^k\theta_i-\kappa_L x_m\right)_+-o(1).           \tag{3}
\]
The uniform error in (3) concerns sequences with bounded \(\kappa_L\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## A derivative estimate at each squarefree conductor

Let \(m>1\) be squarefree and \(\zeta\) a primitive \(m\)th root of unity.
Write \(F=\Phi_m^{b_m}S\) with \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) and \(S(\zeta)\ne0\).
Each \(\Phi_{m/p}^{b_{m/p}}\), \(p\mid m\), divides \(S\), and the remaining integral factor has a nonzero integral norm at \(\zeta\).
The two norms needed here, written as logarithms divided by \(\varphi(m)\), are
\[
  \frac{\log|N\Phi_{m/p}(\zeta)|}{\varphi(m)}
       =\frac{\log p}{p-1},\qquad
  \frac{\log|N\Phi_m'(\zeta)|}{\varphi(m)}
       =\log m-\sum_{p\mid m}\frac{\log p}{p-1}.                          \tag{4}
\]
For the first identity interchange the two root sets in the absolute resultant and write \(s=m/p\).
At each primitive \(s\)th root \(\eta\), the identity \(\Phi_m(X)=\Phi_s(X^p)/\Phi_s(X)\) gives
\(\Phi_m(\eta)=p\eta^{p-1}\Phi_s'(\eta^p)/\Phi_s'(\eta)\).
The powered roots permute the denominator set, giving absolute resultant \(p^{\varphi(s)}\), also for \(s=1\).
For the second identity differentiate the factor \(X^m-1\) in \(\Phi_m(X)=\prod_{d\mid m}(X^d-1)^{\mu(m/d)}\) at \(\zeta\).
The geometric mean of \(|\zeta^d-1|\) over the primitive roots is \(\Phi_{m/d}(1)^{1/\varphi(m/d)}\) for \(d<m\).
At a prime \(p\) the value \(\Phi_p(1)\) is \(p\), while at a squarefree composite \(t=ps\), \(s>1\), it is \(1\) by the same quotient formula at \(1\).
This proves (4).

The identity \(F^{(b_m)}(\zeta)/b_m!=\Phi_m'(\zeta)^{b_m} S(\zeta)\) and the [unit-disk derivative bound](Yin_C_G0901_v12.md#disk) give
\[
 \sum_{p\mid m} b_{m/p}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
   \le \log H+\log\binom{D}{b_m}
        -b_m\left(\log m-\sum_{p\mid m}\frac{\log p}{p-1}\right).         \tag{5}
\]
This includes \(b_m=0\).
The argument keeps every other nonvanishing norm as an integer of absolute value at least one.

For \(L>1\) put \(u_m=mb_m/a\), \(u_1=1\), and \(E_m=\sum_{p\mid m}\log p/(p-1)\).
Use \(\binom{D}{b}\le(\mathrm eD/b)^b\) for \(b>0\), with the zero contribution at \(b=0\).
Multiplying (5) by \(m/(a\log L)\) gives
\[
 u_m\ \ge\ \sum_{p\mid m}\frac{p}{p-1}\frac{\log p}{\log L}u_{m/p}
                   -\kappa_L x_m-\frac{\exp(E_m)}{\log L}.                \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(u(1+E_m-\log u)\le\exp(E_m)\) for every \(u\ge0\), with \(0\log0=0\).
In the range of (3), all \(\theta_i\) are bounded above by \(1+o(1)\), and \(E_m=o(1)\) for fixed \(k,\epsilon\).
For every proper divisor \(s\mid m\), \(x_s\le V L^{-\epsilon}\).
Successive applications of (6) to these divisors therefore give \(u_s\ge j!\prod_{p\mid s}(\log p/\log L)-o(1)\) for \(s\) with \(j\) prime factors, starting with \(u_1=1\).
Each of the \(k\) terms for \(m\) now contributes at least \((k-1)!\prod_i\theta_i-o(1)\).
Use \(u_m\ge0\) to obtain (3).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the forced degrees

Let \(\Delta_{k-1}\) denote \(\theta_i>0\), \(\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i=1\), parametrized by the first \(k-1\) coordinates with Lebesgue measure \(d\theta\).
For \(k=1\) use a unit mass at \(\theta_1=1\).
For \(k\) fixed, the counting measure of ordered distinct prime tuples, divided by \(L\), in the variables \(x=x_m,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{k-1}\) converges on compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\times\Delta_{k-1}\) to
\[
               dx\,\frac{d\theta}{\prod_{i=1}^k\theta_i}.                  \tag{7}
\]
To verify this, fix a rectangular box inside this region with \(x\in[s,t]\), \(0<s<t\), and let \(P=p_1\cdots p_{k-1}\).
Write \(\theta_k'=1-\log P/\log L\), bounded below on the box.
The [prime number theorem at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) counts possible last primes between \(sL\log L/P\) and \(tL\log L/P\) by
\((1+o(1))(t-s)L/(P\theta_k')\), uniformly since the lower endpoint exceeds a fixed power of \(L\) and the endpoint ratio is fixed.
Omitting at most \(k-1\) primes per tuple has negligible relative cost under the same lower bound.
For each preceding prime the measures \(\sum_p p^{-1}\delta_{\log p/\log L}\) converge on compact intervals of \((0,\infty)\) to \(d\theta/\theta\), by partial summation of the same prime count.
Coincident preceding primes have negligible weight there, since \(\sum_{p\ge L^\epsilon}p^{-2}=o(1)\) and the other coordinate masses are bounded.
Also \(\log p_k/\log L=\theta_k'+o(1)\) uniformly on the box.
This proves (7) by rectangular approximation, including the ordinary prime count for \(k=1\).

On such compact regions \(\varphi(m)/m=1-o(1)\) uniformly.
Each unordered squarefree conductor has exactly \(k!\) orderings.
Applying (3) and (7) against continuous symmetric cutoffs between zero and one, and increasing the cutoffs to one, bounds the liminf of the degree fraction in (2) below by
\[
 \frac1{k!}\int_{\Delta_{k-1}}\int_0^\infty
       \frac{(k!\prod_i\theta_i-\kappa x)_+}{\prod_i\theta_i}\,dx\,d\theta .
\]
The case \(\kappa=0\) is impossible already for \(k=1\), since the integral over an arbitrarily long bounded \(x\)-interval would exceed the total degree fraction one.
For \(\kappa>0\) the integral is \(k!/(2\kappa)\) times \(\int_{\Delta_{k-1}}\prod_i\theta_i\,d\theta=1/(2k-1)!\), by the simplex monomial integral.
This proves (2).
Summing (2) for \(1\le k\le K\) uses disjoint irreducible factors with total degree at most \(D\).
Let \(K\to\infty\) and pass to a subsequence realizing any finite liminf to obtain (1).
Restriction to a closed box with nonempty interior in (7) on which \(x>0\) and \(k!\prod_i\theta_i>\kappa x\) gives a positive degree fraction in the corresponding conductor range.

## Consequences for real-place height auxiliaries

If \(\Phi_q\nmid F\) for a prime \(q\), (5) gives \(\log H/a\ge\log q/(q-1)\).
In particular a product of positive integral powers of \(X^j-1\) with \(j\le N\) misses every prime order \(q>N\).
The prime number theorem supplies \(N<q\le2N\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Thus such products satisfy \(\log H/a\ge(\log N)/(2N)\), which prevents an \(O(1/L)\) upper bound for \(N\asymp L\to\infty\).
For the [consecutive-power Vandermonde](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#powers) the value at a primitive \(N\)th root attains the Hadamard upper bound \(N^{N/2}\), since the Vandermonde matrix there is a Fourier matrix.
The finite-forbidden-order [coefficient-rate theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v29.md#statement) allows arbitrary excess degree.
Equations (2) and (3) constrain simultaneous multiplicities with the degree charged, even in the presence of arbitrary integral noncyclotomic cofactors.

To see the relevance to a possible sharper height bound, take any nonzero algebraic number \(z\) with \(F(z)\ne0\), absolute logarithmic height \(h>0\), and positive-real conjugate proportion \(p>0\).
For positive \(v\le1\), Taylor's integral remainder and the same derivative bound give \(|F(v)|/H\le\min(1,\binom Da|1-v|^a)\).
For \(v\ge1\) apply this to \(X^DF(1/X)\) at \(1/v\), which preserves the circle maximum and the multiplicity at one.
Consequently the archimedean bound after dividing by \(H\max(1,v)^D\) at every positive place is \(\min(1,\mathrm e L|\log v|)^a\).
The mean of \(|\log v|\) at those places is at most \(2h/p\).
Concavity and the product formula, with the integral nonarchimedean bound, give
\[
      p\log^+\!\left(\frac{p}{2\mathrm e Lh}\right)
                  \le \frac{\log H}{a}+Lh .                               \tag{8}
\]
If one could supply for every small \(p\) and each input an auxiliary avoiding that input with \(1/p\le L\le A/p\) and \(\log H/a\le C/L\) for absolute \(A,C\), then (8) would give \(h\gg_{A,C}p^2\).
Indeed \(h\le c p^2\) would force \(\log(1/(2\mathrm e Ac))\le C+Ac\), which fails for sufficiently small \(c>0\).
For merely real conjugates one can first square a nontorsion input, as in the [real-place bound](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#powers).
The necessary multiplicities in (2) and (3) leave existence of these auxiliaries open, as well as the unrestricted Mahler gap.
