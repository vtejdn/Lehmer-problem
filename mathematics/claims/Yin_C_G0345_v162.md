# Which spacings can support a multiple of a prescribed algebraic unit?

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finite search for cyclotomic cofactors and the possible spacings for arbitrary multiples

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\).
Fix integers \(R\ge1,\ 1\le w<d\) and put \(K=(R+1)(w+1)\).
For an integer \(n>w\), call a multiple admissible if up to a Laurent monomial it has the form \(F(X,X^n)\), where \(0\ne F\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]\) is supported in \([0,w]\times[0,R]\).
Coefficients, support and cofactor may vary with \(n\).

If there is an admissible multiple \(PQ\) with \(Q\) a product of cyclotomic polynomials, then
\[
              (d-w)/R\ \le\ n\ \le\ d+25w.                                  \tag{1}
\]
At that same spacing there is such a multiple with \(\deg Q\le24Rw\).
Thus existence for an individual \(P,R,w\) is decided by a finite search even with no prior bound on the cyclotomic cofactor.
More generally, for an arbitrary integral cofactor of an admissible multiple, the degree of its noncyclotomic factors, counted with multiplicity and excluding powers of \(X\), is at least
\[
                 \max(0,rn-s-24rs-d)                                         \tag{2}
\]
for some \(1\le r\le R,\ 0\le s\le w\) belonging to a factor of the lift which carries \(P\).

With an arbitrary integral cofactor, every \(n>w\) admits a multiple if \(K>d\).
Every admissible multiple has the cofactor cost
\[
       m(Q)\ge c_R-m(P)-C R^2(w/n)\{R+K\log(2RKn/w)\},                        \tag{3}
\]
where \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure and \(c_R=[2\exp(16384)(\log(3R))^{48}]^{-1}\).

Suppose \(K\le d\).
Let \(e\) be the exponent of the torsion subgroup of the multiplicative group generated by all conjugates of \(\alpha\), with \(e=1\) for a torsion-free group.
The spacings admitting any multiple consist of full residue classes modulo \(e\), restricted to \(n>w\), together with a finite set.
The full classes are determined by finitely many ranks within each class, and their proportion is at most
\[
           1-\frac1e\min\left(e,1+\left\lfloor\frac{d-K}{R}\right\rfloor\right).
                                                                                \tag{4}
\]
For a Salem input there are only finitely many spacings when \(K\le d\).
In either case the finiteness of the sporadic set gives no effective last sporadic spacing here.
There is also an explicit forbidden progression for every such \(P\), obtained from its reduction modulo two.
The coordinator asked whether separated multiples could be forced on arbitrary small-measure inputs.

<a id="cofactors"></a>
## Removing cyclotomic factors in the lift

Normalize \(F\) by monomials and factor over the rational Laurent ring, taking primitive integral representatives.
Widths are additive on products, since the two extreme coefficients multiply in the integral domain of the other variable.
Each factor has specialized exponents distinct on its support because its \(X\)-width is at most \(w<n\).
Some rational irreducible factor \(H\) specializes to a multiple of \(P\).
A base-only factor has degree at most \(w<d\) after monomial normalization, so this \(H\) depends on \(Y\).
Its \(Y\)-width is \(r\in[1,R]\) and its \(X\)-width is \(s\in[0,w]\).
A geometric component which is a torsion translate would give the rational factor \(\Phi_\ell(X^vY^b)\) up to monomials by the [torsion factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal).
Here \(b\ne0\) and \(v+bn\ne0\) by distinctness of the specialized terms.
Such a factor can specialize only to roots of unity, so \(H\) has no torsion-translate component.

Shift \(H\) to have minimum exponent zero in each variable and put \(h(X)=H(X,X^n)\).
The [Beukers–Smyth theorem](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) bounds its distinct roots of unity by \(22sr\).
Indeed the corresponding points \((\zeta,\zeta^n)\) are distinct torsion points of \(H=0\) and the Newton polygon is contained in a rectangle of area \(sr\).
To count multiplicities put \(D_n=X\partial_X+nY\partial_Y\).
The polynomials \(H,D_nH\) share no geometric factor.
In characteristic zero \(H\) is geometrically squarefree, so a common irreducible factor \(G\) would divide \(D_nG\) by the product rule.
The total degree of \(D_nG\) is at most that of \(G\), hence \(D_nG=cG\) for a constant, including the case \(c=0\).
All monomials of \(G\) would then have the same weight \(i+nj\).
Its \(X\)-width is at most \(s<n\), so it would be a single monomial, which the normalization of \(H\) excludes.

Consequently \(A(X)=\operatorname{Res}_Y(H,D_nH)\ne0\) and its Sylvester determinant has degree at most \(2sr\) in \(X\).
The polynomial Bézout identity for this resultant and \(D_nH(X,X^n)=Xh'(X)\) give \(\gcd(h,Xh')\mid A\) in \(\mathbb C[X]\).
Every nonzero root of multiplicity \(l\) thus costs at least \(l-1\) in the degree of \(A\), with costs adding across roots.
The total cyclotomic degree of \(h\) is at most \(24sr\), including when \(s=0\).

The occupied bottom and top \(Y\)-layers of \(H\) show that the difference of the extreme exponents of \(h\) lies between \(rn-s\) and \(rn+s\).
All nonzero roots of \(h\), counted with multiplicity, occur in the original multiple.
After counting at most \(24sr\) cyclotomic roots and one copy of \(P\), any remaining degree supplies (2).
If the cofactor is cyclotomic, \(h\) itself is \(P\) times a cyclotomic product of degree at most \(24sr\) up to a monomial and sign: the normalized integral factors of a monic specialization have unit leading coefficients.
Then \(d\le rn+s\) and \(rn-s\le d+24rs\), proving (1).
For a finite decision procedure enumerate the indicated \(n>w\) and cyclotomic products of total degree at most \(24Rw\).
There are finitely many orders to test since \(\varphi(q)\ge\sqrt{q/2}\), as follows prime power by prime power.
With \(P,Q\) of nonzero constant term, the interval test is simply whether the support of \(X^aP Q\) lies in \(\bigcup_{j=0}^R[jn,jn+w]\) for some \(0\le a\le w\): the selected normalized \(H\) has its first term in the bottom layer.
Every successful such test gives an admissible multiple directly.

<a id="dimension"></a>
## Existence by linear dependence and its cost

In the \(d\)-dimensional rational vector space \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) take the \(K\) columns \(\alpha^{i+jn}\), \(0\le i\le w,\ 0\le j\le R\).
Dependence is equivalent to an admissible multiple by clearing rational denominators and using the minimal polynomial.
Distinctness of the \(K\) integer exponents for \(n>w\) makes the resulting polynomial nonzero.
Thus \(K>d\) suffices at every spacing.

Every resulting lift contains the horizontal nontorsion component identified above, so the [bounded partial-degree gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(m_2(F)\ge c_R\).
For positive actual \(X\)-width, the [separated specialization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v159.md#statement) applies with support size at most \(K\), partial degree at most \(R\) and \(X\)-width at most \(w\).
These substitutions enlarge the error, since \(v\log(2RK/v)\) is increasing on \(0<v\le1\).
At actual \(X\)-width zero the specialization has exactly its vertical Mahler measure by invariance of circle measure under powering.
Now additivity \(m(PQ)=m(P)+m(Q)\) proves (3), with all monomial shifts harmless.
Thus increasing separation in the dimension construction has an explicit cost even when no restriction is put on coefficients.

<a id="spacings"></a>
## Rank on residue classes of the torsion exponent

When \(K\le d\), map the column matrix into the splitting field by all \(d\) embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Its rank there equals the original rational rank, since the embedding matrix on \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}\) is invertible Vandermonde.
In any \(K\)-row minor its determinant is a sum \(\sum_\lambda c_\lambda\lambda^n\) with at most \(K!\) terms.
The bases are products of the selected conjugates with powers from \(0,\ldots,R\), from expanding the determinant and absorbing the powers \(i\) into the coefficients.
On the class \(n=a+et\), combine equal bases \(\lambda^e\), weighting coefficients by \(\lambda^a\), and discard zero coefficients.
Distinct remaining bases have nontorsion ratios by the definition of \(e\).

Such a combined sum is either empty or has only finitely many nonnegative integer zeros.
This is the number-field case of the Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem, as stated and proved by \(p\)-adic interpolation in [Lech, pp. 417–418](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/116/6892-11512_2007_Article_BF02590997.pdf).
For the needed simple-base case choose a finite place over an odd prime \(p\) where the finitely many bases \(\gamma\) are units.
A common positive power \(b\) puts each \(\gamma^b\) in \(1+p^2\mathcal O\) in the completion, by finiteness of the unit group modulo \(p^2\).
In a fixed class \(t=c+bu\), the sum interpolates on \(\mathbb Z_p\) by \(\sum_\gamma a_\gamma\gamma^c\exp(u\log(\gamma^b))\).
The exponential series converges on a disk of radius greater than one in \(u\) because \(|\log(\gamma^b)|_p\le p^{-2}\).
An infinite set of integer zeros accumulates in the compact \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Expansion at the accumulation point has no first nonzero coefficient, since a lowest nonzero term would dominate sufficiently close to that point.
The expansion converges on the whole unit disk, so the function vanishes there.
Evaluating at consecutive integers from zero through the number of bases minus one gives an invertible Vandermonde system in the distinct \(\gamma^b\), contradicting the nonzero coefficients.
There are finitely many classes modulo \(b\), proving the assertion.

It follows that rank loss is universal on a class modulo \(e\) if every minor has an empty combined sum.
Otherwise one nonempty minor permits only finitely many exceptional spacings in that class.
Universal loss can be tested from \(K!\) consecutive samples in \(t\) by the same Vandermonde argument for each minor.
Samples may be chosen with \(n>w\).
For \(w+1\le n\le\lfloor(d-1-w)/R\rfloor\), all column exponents are distinct and below \(d\), so the columns are independent.
These consecutive samples occupy at least \(\min(e,1+\lfloor(d-K)/R\rfloor)\) distinct classes, giving (4) and the asserted natural density.
For Salem inputs the [inverse-pair independence](Yin_C_G0165_v1.md#off-pair-salem-ratio-proof) gives \(e=1\).

There is a direct effective exclusion with no splitting field.
In \(\mathbb F_2[X]/(\bar P)\) the class of \(X\) has a finite multiplicative order \(b_2\) since \(P\) has constant coefficient \(\pm1\).
If \(n\equiv w+1\bmod b_2\), the columns reduce to the first \(K\) consecutive powers, which are independent there for \(K\le d\).
A rational relation lifted to primitive integer coefficients would give a nonzero binary relation, an impossibility.

Full admissible classes really can occur for an algebraic unit.
Take \(\beta=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(\alpha=\beta^{1/4}>0\), \(w=1\) and \(R=2\).
The norm of \(\beta\) is \(-1\), excluding a square in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
In the quadratic extension obtained by adjoining \(\sqrt\beta\), the embedding sending \(\sqrt\beta\) to its negative is real, excluding a square root of \(\sqrt\beta\) in that extension.
Hence \(X^8-X^4-1\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\), with \(K=6<8\).
For every positive multiple \(n\) of four the element \(\alpha^n\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), so its polynomial of degree at most two gives an admissible relation.
This mechanism permits large cofactor degrees as in (2).
The finite reduction for cyclotomic cofactors and the density bound for arbitrary cofactors do not supply a separated multiple of small cost for every small-measure input.
