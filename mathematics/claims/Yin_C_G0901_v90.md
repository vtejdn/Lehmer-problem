# Argument-rank lower bounds on the complete small-measure catalogue

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two degree and measure ranges

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\), let \(k\) denote the rational multiplicative rank modulo roots of unity of the arguments \(a/|a|\) of all its algebraic conjugates, and let \(M(w)\) be the multiplicative Mahler measure.
The following implications hold.

* If \(\deg w\le45\) and \(M(w)\le13/10\), then \(k\ge3\).
* If \(\deg w\le55\) and \(M(w)\le3049/2500=1.2196\), then \(k\ge4\).

The second conclusion includes the four primitive cores below \(601/500=1.202\).
Both thresholds can be written with weak inequality since the multiplicative measure of an algebraic unit is an algebraic integer: it is the product of its exterior conjugates up to sign, using complex conjugate pairs and the signs of real factors.
Thus no such unit has measure equal to any of these three nonintegral rational numbers.
The same conclusions hold for all nonzero nontorsion algebraic integers in these measure ranges, since any nonunit has measure at least two.
In particular a hypothetical unit of argument rank at most two below Lehmer's number must pass the degree-55 frontier, in addition to the [all-degree finite reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v88.md#statement).

<a id="dimension"></a>
## The dimension budget of a neutral signature

Both thresholds are below the plastic number, so [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes the minimum polynomial reciprocal.
Its degree is \(2n\), since an odd-degree reciprocal or antireciprocal irreducible has a root at \(-1\) or \(1\).
Let \(u\) be the number of inverse pairs on the unit circle and \(l=n-u>0\) the number of exterior conjugates.
Let \(b\) be the rational rank of all multiplicative conjugate classes, and \(r\ge1\) the rational rank of their log moduli.
Kronecker gives the positive radial rank, and the [two eigenspace identities](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection) give \(b=r+k\).
The [projected-frame inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v64.md#degree) gives \(nr\le lb\).
Therefore
\[
                  k\ge\left\lceil\frac{u\,r}{l}\right\rceil,\qquad
          b\le\left\lfloor\frac{nk}{u}\right\rfloor\quad(u>0).
\]
The first inequality requires neither a faithful class action nor distinct classes among the original roots.

<a id="order"></a>
## Faithful low-dimensional class actions through degree 45

If a unit in the first range had \(k\le2\), take the [product in each torsion class](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) of its conjugates.
The resulting unit has the same measure and the same rational conjugate span and \(c\)-action, because each class of equal multiplicity \(e\) is replaced by its \(e\)-fold multiple.
Its degree is no larger, and its conjugates are distinct modulo torsion.
In particular its minimum cannot equal \(S(X^j)\) with \(j>1\), which would give different roots having a root of unity as quotient.
The compressed polynomial is reciprocal, has even degree at most 44 and occurs up to variable sign in the [complete primitive list](../audits/literature/Mos26/readme.md#complete-small-cores).
Variable sign has no effect on the class space, measure or neutral signature.

For this compressed unit the splitting group acts faithfully on its rational class span.
An element fixing every class fixes the unique root in that class and hence the splitting field pointwise.
In dimension at most three, the order of a finite-order rational matrix belongs to \(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).
In dimension at most four the list is \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12\}\).
Indeed the minimal polynomial of such a matrix is a product of distinct cyclotomic polynomials, and the order is the least common multiple of their indices.
An index of totient four uses the full four dimensions, while the smaller nontrivial indices \(2,3,4,6\) have totients \(1,2,2,2\).
This proves both lists after allowing an identity summand.
Squarefree reduction of the monic minimum at a prime gives a Frobenius element whose root-cycle lengths are precisely the irreducible factor degrees; its order on the class space must lie in the appropriate list.
Replacing \(X\) by \(-X\) preserves these degrees and squarefreeness.

The [integer and rational enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0191/readme.md#exhaustion) considers every one of the 944 source rows through degree 44.
Exactly 810 have \(u>2l\), already forcing \(k\ge3\).
On the remaining 134 rows, \(k\le2\) would bound \(b\) by three in 132 cases and by four in two cases.
All 134 full reciprocal polynomials have a squarefree reduction with a forbidden order: 123 are certified at two, ten further rows at three, and the final row at eleven.
This rules out a compressed counterexample, and compression proves the conclusion for the original degree range.
The proof uses faithfulness for the hypothetical compressed representative, without assuming it separately for every originally presented polynomial.

<a id="fourteen"></a>
## The fourteen low cores through degree 55

For the second implication, reduce to the exponent-primitive core by removing substitutions \(X\mapsto\pm X^j\) up to sign, as in the [bounded-degree core comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#catalogue).
Taking \(j\)th powers maps conjugates onto those of the core up to sign and scales their classes by \(j\).
The rational class space with its conjugation action, hence the argument rank, is preserved.
The Mahler measure is also unchanged by the power substitution.
The even primitive degree is at most 54.
The [exact cut through degree 44](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#cut) leaves fourteen primitive cores below \(3049/2500\).
In degrees \(46,48,50,52,54\) the listed minimizing polynomials are already above the cut, using the [published per-degree minima](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) with their primitivity convention.
This step uses no completeness of a full list below \(1.3\) beyond degree 44.

The [fourteen exact signatures](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#signatures) give five rows with one real exterior conjugate in degree at least ten.
They have \(l=1,n\ge5\), forcing \(k\ge n-1\ge4\).
Eight other rows have one pair of nonreal exterior conjugates in degree at least eighteen, hence \(l=2,n\ge9\) and \(k\ge\lceil(n-2)/2\rceil\ge4\).
The remaining degree-eighteen row, source line 197, has \(b=9\) and \(r=2\) by the [independent sign characters on its nine pairs](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#group), so its exact argument rank is seven.
This exhausts the second range, including the [smaller four-core cut](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0187/readme.md#cut).
