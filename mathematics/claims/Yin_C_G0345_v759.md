# The neutral conjugates and limiting height of a critical four-term relation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Signatures distinguished by the coefficient field

Use the [four-term critical test on arbitrary shrinking inputs](Yin_C_G0345_v754.md#statement).
Let \(\alpha\) be the reciprocal unit in its eventual conclusion, of degree \(d\), height \(h\) and positive logarithmic measure \(m=dh\to0\).
Let \(L=\max_\sigma\log|\sigma\alpha|\) and write
\[
 X=\alpha^a,\quad Y=\alpha^b=R(X),\quad
 R(Z)=\gamma\,\frac{Z-\mu}{Z-\lambda},\quad
 \mu=1/\iota(\lambda),\quad\gamma=\nu\lambda,\quad \iota(\nu)=1/\nu .
\]
Here \(\lambda,\mu,\gamma\ne0\), \(\lambda\ne\mu\), and \(\iota\) sends \(\alpha\) to its inverse.
Put \(E=\mathbb Q(\lambda,\iota(\lambda),\nu)\) and \(e=[E:\mathbb Q]\).
The possibilities for these data and the action on \(E\) form a finite list under the fixed width and density bounds.

Call an embedding \(\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) compatible if \(\overline{\tau(x)}=\tau(\iota x)\) on \(E\), and write \(j\) for the number of such embeddings.
If \(n_0\) is the number of unit-circle conjugates among the original \(d\) embeddings, then eventually
\[
        0\ \le\ \frac je-\frac{n_0}{d}\ \ll\ m^2,\qquad
        a\ll dm,\quad ah\ll m^2,\quad b/a\gg m^{-2},\quad bL\gg m^{-2}.       \tag{1}
\]
The constants throughout the finite list depend only on the critical width and density bounds.
In particular a shrinking sequence with such a test and \(n_0=o(d)\) eventually has \(n_0=0\).
If \(n_0/d\to1\), eventually either \(\iota\) fixes the totally real \(E\) pointwise, or \(E\) is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of its totally real fixed field with \(\iota\) as complex conjugation.

For a fixed choice from the list, let \(\mu_{\mathbb T}\) be normalized angular measure and set
\[
                  A_R=\frac1e\sum_\tau\int_{\mathbb T}
                                    \log^+|R^\tau(z)|\,d\mu_{\mathbb T}(z).
\]
The radius distribution and its upper tail satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
     bh&\longrightarrow h(\lambda)+A_R>0,\\
     \lim_{U\to\infty}\lim_{m\to0}\frac1d\sum_{\sigma}
                         (b\log^+|\sigma\alpha|-U)_+ &=h(\lambda).            \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
The limits are taken along the selected sequence and absolute height includes the finite places.
Given a coefficient embedding \(\tau\), the empirical distribution of \(Y\) over its extensions tends weakly on the Riemann sphere to \(R^\tau_*\mu_{\mathbb T}\).
At each incompatible embedding the limiting proportion of exterior conjugates in that block is \(\mu_{\mathbb T}\{z:|R^\tau(z)|>1\}\).
At compatible embeddings that proportion tends to zero.

At every finite place \(w\) of \(E\) one necessarily has
\[
                  |\gamma|_w\max(1,|\mu|_w)=\max(1,|\lambda|_w).             \tag{3}
\]
For every embedding \(\sigma\) attaining the largest modulus,
\[
                         |(\sigma\alpha)^a-\sigma\lambda|\ll e^{-bL}.
\]
If \(|\sigma\lambda|=1\) at even one such embedding, then \(a\ll dm\exp(-c/m^2)\) for some fixed \(c>0\).
Finally, whenever \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^{\gcd(a,b)})=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the [complex rational graph radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#statement) gives a uniform bound for \(\gcd(a,b)\) times every order of an in-field root of \(\alpha\).

<a id="slope"></a>
## The short gap at a largest root

The exact relation obtained by clearing the denominator has four nonzero coefficients, and the possible coefficients and their conjugates form a fixed finite set.
At a conjugate of modulus \(e^L>1\), the top exponent \(a+b\) is separated from the remaining three by at least \(a\).
The triangle inequality gives \(aL\ll1\).
The [square logarithm bound for units](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(d\sum_\sigma\log^2|\sigma\alpha|\ge1/10\).
The sum of absolute logarithmic radii is \(2m\), and reciprocity makes \(L\) their largest absolute value.
Hence \(L\ge1/(20dm)\), giving \(a\ll dm\) and \(ah\ll m^2\).
If \(bh\to0\) on a subsequence, the exact four-term relation, normalized, would have width times \(h\) tending to zero while vanishing on all embeddings fixing \(E\).
Their fraction \(1/e\) stays bounded below.
This contradicts the [subcritical bound at zero](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement), giving \(bh\gg1\) by the same argument on any failing sequence at the fixed parameters.
Now \(b/a\gg m^{-2}\) and \(bL\gg m^{-2}\).
All conjugates of \(X\) stay in a fixed compact annulus since \(aL\ll1\).
The exact graph equation at a largest conjugate bounds the distance of \((\sigma\alpha)^a\) to \(\sigma\lambda\) by \(O(e^{-bL})\).
For \(|\sigma\lambda|=1\) this distance is at least \(e^{aL}-1\ge aL\), giving the last short-gap estimate.

<a id="signature"></a>
## Counting embeddings above each coefficient place

At a neutral embedding \(\sigma\) of the root field, complex conjugation agrees with \(\sigma\iota\) on the generator and hence on its entire field.
Its restriction is therefore compatible, proving \(n_0/d\le j/e\) with all multiplicities.
For compatible \(\tau\), the map \(R^\tau\) preserves the unit circle by \(R^\iota(1/Z)R(Z)=1\).
It has no pole or zero there, because \(\tau(\mu)=1/\overline{\tau(\lambda)}\) and \(\lambda\ne\mu\).
On a fixed annulus about the circle the smooth function \(\log|R^\tau(z)|\) vanishes on the circle, so its absolute value is at most a constant times \(|\log|z||\).
Since \(b/a\to\infty\) and \(\log|Y|=(b/a)\log|X|\) under every embedding, all \(X\)-values in that annulus above \(\tau\) must be exactly neutral.
The total fraction of \(X\)-values outside the annulus is \(O(ah)=O(m^2)\) by \(\sum_\sigma|\log|\sigma X||=2dah\).
There are only finitely many annuli needed, proving (1).

Conversely if \(R^\tau\) preserves the circle, the reflection identity with ordinary complex conjugation pairs its pole at \(\tau(\lambda)\) with a zero at \(1/\overline{\tau(\lambda)}\) and gives \(|\tau(\gamma)|=|\tau(\lambda)|\).
Thus \(\overline{\tau(\lambda)}=\tau(\iota\lambda)\) and \(|\tau(\nu)|=1\), which certify compatibility on the displayed generators.
Otherwise the image circle on the sphere is distinct from the unit circle and meets it in at most two points.
The conjugates of \(X\) above each \(\tau\) equidistribute on the circle since \(h(X)\to0\), by [relative small-point equidistribution](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution).
The distinct-point hypothesis follows by Northcott and nontorsion, and the equal multiplicities of the relative \(X\)-values preserve the normalized distribution even if the power lowers degree.
Continuity of the fractional linear map on the sphere yields the weak law for \(Y\).
In an incompatible block the circle boundary has limiting probability zero, giving the claimed exterior proportion.
The bound (1) handles each compatible block since \(e\) is bounded.
The signature conclusions follow from the finite possible fractions \(j/e\).
When \(j=e\), every image of the fixed subfield is real, and for a nontrivial involution an embedding of \(E\) into \(\mathbb R\) would contradict compatibility, proving the quadratic assertion.

<a id="height"></a>
## Local coefficient norms and the part of the first moment in the tails

At a finite place of \(E\) let \(G_A,G_B\) be the coefficient maxima of \(A(Z)=\gamma(Z-\mu)\) and \(B(Z)=Z-\lambda\).
All evaluations have equal absolute value at each extending place since \(X,Y\) are units.
If \(G_A>G_B\), the polynomial \(P=A/c\) obtained by dividing by a coefficient of modulus \(G_A\) has nonzero reduction, yet \(|P(X)|\le G_B/G_A<1\) at all extending places.
As in the [finite-place calculation for a rational map](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height), for some fixed \(N\) this implies \(|X^N-1|\le\max(|\pi|,G_B/G_A)<1\) at all those places, with \(\pi\) a uniformizer.
Their normalized weights sum to the fixed positive weight of the place of \(E\).
This contradicts the [vanishing cyclic norm loss](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) at \(h(X^N)\to0\).
Interchanging \(A,B\) rules out the other strict inequality.
For fixed data the places at which the coefficient maxima could differ are finite; hence (3) holds for each surviving choice eventually.

Apply the [rational-map height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height) to \(X\).
At infinity the circle integral of the logarithm of the homogeneous maximum splits into the mean of \(\log|z-\tau(\lambda)|\) and that of \(\log^+|R^\tau(z)|\).
The identity holds away from finitely many points, with integrable logarithms even when a pole lies on the circle.
The first integral equals \(\log^+|\tau(\lambda)|\).
At each finite place (3) gives the common maximum \(\max(1,|\lambda|)\) for the height limit.
Together these terms give \(h(\lambda)+A_R\).
The actual first moment of \(\log^+|\sigma Y|\) equals \(bh\) since \(Y\) is a unit.
For each \(U>0\), its truncation at \(U\) is continuous on the sphere, with value \(U\) at infinity.
Its average therefore tends to the mixture of the corresponding truncated circle integrals.
Subtract from the actual limiting first moment, and let \(U\) increase using monotone convergence, to obtain (2).
The radical assertion follows because the maps come from a finite list and the exponent slopes diverge.
