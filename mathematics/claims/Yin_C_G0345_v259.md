# Ideal classes couple the primitive dyadic orbits at a prime conductor

<a id="statement"></a>
## A condition on the labels of a rational factor

Let \(q>3\) be prime and \(0<l<q\).
Put \(P_l=X^{2q}-2X^{q+l}+X^q-2X^{q-l}+1\), \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3q})^+\), and
\[
                  f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2),\qquad I=(q-1)/f .
\]
The primitive roots of order \(3q\) in characteristic two form \(I\) orbits under squaring and inversion, each of size \(2f\).
Consider a reciprocal monic rational divisor \(S=X^{\deg T}T(X+X^{-1})\) of \(P_l\).
Let \(J\) denote the primitive orbits it selects modulo two, identified below with primes of \(L\) over two.
The condition also allows a separate selection at order three.

For any subfield \(K\) of \(L\) in which two splits completely, write \(n_{\mathfrak p}\) for the number of selected orbit labels above \(\mathfrak p\mid2\) in \(K\).
Then the ordinary ideal class group of \(K\) satisfies
\[
                      \prod_{\mathfrak p\mid2}
                             [\mathfrak p]^{\,f n_{\mathfrak p}}=1.       \tag{1}
\]
At \(L\) itself the product of the selected prime ideals, each to the first power, is principal.
When order three is omitted, the generator obtained by taking the norm to \(K\) has residue \(T(-1)^{[L:K]}\) at the unique prime over \(q\), with \(T(-1)=\pm1\).
The [unit calculation](#ray) shows that imposing this residue on an otherwise unspecified generator adds no ideal-class condition to (1).

In particular suppose \(I\) is even, so there is a quadratic subfield \(K\) in which two splits.
Split the orbits according to the Legendre symbol of their normalized exponents modulo \(q\), and let \(u_+,u_-\) be the selected counts on the two sides of size \(I/2\).
If a prime over two in \(K\) has ordinary ideal class of order \(c\), then
\[
                       \frac{c}{\gcd(c,f)}\ \mid\ u_+-u_- .             \tag{2}
\]
This concerns membership in the two sets of labels even when all selected orbits have the same degree and order.
It holds simultaneously for every \(l\) in the given range.

For \(q=257\) there are 16 primitive labels, eight of each sign, and \(u_+\equiv u_-\bmod3\).
For \(q=5419\) there are 258 labels, 129 of each sign, and \(u_+\equiv u_-\bmod5\).
In the latter example the selection of all 129 labels of one sign and none of the other gives a degree-5418 reciprocal dyadic divisor which has no rational lift for any \(l\).
A selection of 67 and 62 labels has the same degree and satisfies the quadratic test.
Its rationality is undetermined by (2).

## Valuations of the sampled trace

Put \(s=3q\), choose a complex primitive \(s\)-th root \(\zeta\), and put \(\tau=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
The Galois group of \(L\) is \((\mathbb Z/s)^\times/\{\pm1\}\).
Represent each class by the exponent equal to one modulo three and read its residue modulo \(q\).
This identifies the group with the cyclic group \((\mathbb Z/q)^\times\), sending the class of two to \(-2\bmod q\).
As two is unramified, its decomposition group is generated by squaring and has order \(f\).
The distinct conjugate traces reduce to distinct inverse pairs of primitive \(s\)-th roots.
Indeed odd-order roots retain their orders, and equality of two traces forces their roots to solve the same quadratic with product one.
This identifies the primes over two in \(L\) with the orbits of these residue traces.

The reduction of \(P_l\) is \((X^{3q}-1)/(X^q-1)\) and is squarefree.
It has precisely the levels of orders three and \(3q\).
The trace of the parent is \(W=C_q+1-2C_l\), with \(C_j(x+x^{-1})=x^j+x^{-j}\).
The divisor \(S\) is integral by monic factorization.
Its reciprocal complement is also an integral trace polynomial times a monomial, since the parent has no roots at \(1,-1\), and its reduction is disjoint from the roots of \(\bar S\).
At \(\tau\) one has
\[
              W(\tau)=-2(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}).
\]
Here \(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}\) is a unit.
Indeed \(\zeta^{2l}\ne1\) has odd order, so \(1+\zeta^{2l}\) is an integral unit by taking the quotient \((1-\zeta^{4l})/(1-\zeta^{2l})\) of conjugates of equal norm.
This is the positive-argument evaluation of the [paired cyclotomic calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#norms), which here works for either parity of \(l\).
Consequently \(T(\tau)\) has valuation one at precisely the selected primes above two and zero at all other finite primes.
In the norm to \(K\), a prime of \(L\) above \(\mathfrak p\mid2\) contributes \(\mathfrak p^f\), since \(K\) has residue degree one there.
Thus \(\gamma=N_{L/K}T(\tau)\) generates the ideal in (1).
The norm to \(\mathbb Q\) of that ideal is \(2^{f|J|}\).

For the quadratic field in (2), the two primes above two have inverse classes since their product is \((2)\).
The two sets of labels are the cosets of the squares in the indicated cyclic group, up to swapping their names.
This proves (2).
The unique quadratic subfield is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) with \(D=q\) for \(q\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(D=3q\) for \(q\equiv3\bmod4\).
Indeed the quadratic Gauss sum in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)\) has square \((-1)^{(q-1)/2}q\) and changes by the Legendre symbol under exponentiation.
This follows by the cyclic Fourier sum and conjugation as in the [quadratic period calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v249.md#orbits).
For \(q\equiv3\bmod4\) multiply by \(\sqrt{-3}\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) to obtain a real square root of \(3q\).
Uniqueness follows from cyclicity.

## The residue of a generator at the ramified prime

In the full cyclotomic field the inertia at \(q\) consists of all exponents equal to one modulo three.
It maps isomorphically onto \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), so \(q\) is totally ramified in \(L\) and all its subfields, with residue field \(\mathbb F_q\).
The reduction of \(\zeta\) has order three, whence \(\tau\equiv-1\).
Write \(j=[L:K]\) and \(g=[K:\mathbb Q]=(q-1)/j\).
Complete splitting of two means \(f\mid j\).
The actual generator therefore satisfies \(\gamma\equiv T(-1)^j\bmod\mathfrak q\) at the prime \(\mathfrak q\) of \(K\) over \(q\).
When order three is omitted, \(T(-1)\) is odd and divides \(W(-1)=-2C_l(-1)\in\{2,-4\}\), giving \(T(-1)=\pm1\).

Suppose just that the ideal \(\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid2}\mathfrak p^{f n_{\mathfrak p}}\) is principal, generated by \(\lambda\).
For every \(b\in\mathbb F_q^\times\) it has a generator congruent to \(b^j\bmod\mathfrak q\).
This assertion concerns possible generators of the ideal.
To prove it set \(M=\sum n_{\mathfrak p}\).
Then \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\lambda=\pm2^{fM}\), so
\[
            \bar\lambda^g=\overline{N_{K/\mathbb Q}\lambda},\qquad
            \bar\lambda^{2g}=1 ,
\]
since \((-2)^f\equiv1\bmod q\).
For \(a\not\equiv0\bmod q\), the real cyclotomic unit
\[
      U_a=\frac{\zeta_q^a-\zeta_q^{-a}}{\zeta_q-\zeta_q^{-1}}
                         \ \in\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)^+
\]
reduces to \(a\bmod q\) at the ramified prime by the geometric sum.
It is a unit since \(1-\zeta_q^{2a}\) and \(1-\zeta_q^2\) are conjugate and their quotient is integral.

If \(j\) is even, the subgroup of order \(j\) fixing \(K\) contains the unique involution.
Its fixed field is contained in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)^+\), since that real field has index two in \(L\).
The norms of the \(U_a\) from \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)^+\) to \(K\) reduce to \(a^{j/2}\), covering the group of \(2g\)-th roots in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\).
Multiplication by a unit therefore realizes the desired residue.

If \(j\) is odd, \(f\) is odd, and the inertia at three in \(L\) survives in \(K\) with order two.
Indeed in the full cyclotomic field the nonidentity inertia element at three negates the exponent modulo three and fixes it modulo \(q\).
It gives the unique involution in \(L\), which does not belong to the subgroup of order \(j\).
For an element prime to three, its rational norm modulo three is then a square in \(\mathbb F_3^\times\).
In fact the residue norm from each prime over three enters with its ramification index two, as seen by filtering \(\mathcal O_K/3\mathcal O_K\) by successive powers of those primes and taking the determinant of multiplication.
Thus \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\lambda\equiv1\bmod3\), which forces its sign to be \((-1)^{fM}\).
It follows that \(\bar\lambda^g=(-2)^{fM}=1\) in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\).
The norms of the \(U_a\) from \(L\) to \(K\) have residues \(a^j\), covering the group of \(g\)-th roots, which contains the desired residue \(b^j\).
This proves the assertion.

## Two nontrivial class orders

For \(q=257\), \((-2)^8\equiv-1\bmod q\), hence \(f=16,\ I=16\).
The quadratic field is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{257})\).
The integer \((17+\sqrt{257})/2\) has norm eight and is nonzero modulo \(2\mathcal O_K\), since its coefficient at \((1+\sqrt{257})/2\) is odd.
Consequently exactly one prime over two occurs in its ideal and its class has order dividing three.

It is not principal.
If \(\alpha\in\mathcal O_K\) had norm \(\pm2\), multiply it by a suitable power of the positive unit \(U=16+\sqrt{257}\) of norm \(-1\) so that \(\sqrt{2/U}\le|\alpha|\le\sqrt{2U}\) in the displayed real embedding.
The conjugate \(\alpha'\) has absolute value \(2/|\alpha|\).
Writing the new element as \((x+y\sqrt{257})/2\) gives
\[
              |y|\sqrt{257}\le\sqrt2(\sqrt U+1/\sqrt U)<\sqrt{257}.
\]
Thus \(y=0\), impossible for norm \(\pm2\).
The class order is three, which is coprime to \(f\).
For instance two labels in one quadratic class fail (2), whereas one in each meets it.

For \(q=5419\), one has \(q-1=2\cdot3^2\cdot7\cdot43\).
A primality check is \(3^{q-1}\equiv1\bmod q\), with residues \(5418,5291,64,4406\) at exponents \((q-1)/p\) for \(p=2,3,7,43\), respectively.
Each residue minus one is coprime to \(q\), so modulo every prime divisor of \(q\) the order of three is \(q-1\), proving primality.
Since \(2^{21}+1=387q\) and neither \(2^3+1\) nor \(2^7+1\) is divisible by \(q\), one has \(f=21,\ I=258\).
Here \(D=16257\) and \((127+\sqrt D)/2\) has norm \(-32\) with odd coefficient at \((1+\sqrt D)/2\).
A prime above two therefore has class order dividing five.

To exclude norm \(\pm2\), use the positive unit \(U=43351+340\sqrt D\) of norm one.
As above any such element can be chosen with \(|y|\sqrt D\le\sqrt2(\sqrt U+1/\sqrt U)<4\sqrt D\), using \(U<87000\).
The coefficient \(y\) must be odd, since an element with even \(y\) is congruent to a rational integer modulo \(2\mathcal O_K\), and its norm is then odd or divisible by four.
For \(|y|=1,3\) none of \(Dy^2\pm8\) is a square, ruling out the required integer \(x\).
Thus \(c=5\), coprime to \(f=21\).
The example in the statement follows since \(129\not\equiv0\bmod5\) and \(67-62=5\).
Each stated union of primitive labels has a unique monic dyadic lift by squarefree factorization.
It is reciprocal because its reduction is reciprocal and the monic reciprocal normalization preserves the lifted divisor by uniqueness, with constant term one since \(P_l(1)\ne0\).

Among all subsets of these 258 labels, the exact fraction satisfying this quadratic test is
\[
                    \frac15\sum_{v=0}^{4}\cos^{258}(\pi v/5).
\]
Indeed the difference of the two counts for a uniform subset has the distribution of the difference of two independent binomial variables with parameters \(129,1/2\).
Apply the fifth-root-of-unity filter to their generating functions.
The analogous expression at any even \(I\) uses \(I\) and \(c/\gcd(c,f)\) in place of the two constants.
This counts a necessary condition for rationality at a single conductor, supplementing the [degree bound from the number of primitive orbits](Yin_C_G0345_v258.md#statement).
