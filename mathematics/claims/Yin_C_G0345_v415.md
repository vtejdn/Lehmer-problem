# Congruence classes for the equal-width coefficient test

<a id="statement"></a>
## One fixed seed and a dichotomy across all seeds

Fix an integer \(k\ge1\).
Call a prime successful for a seed \(P\) when the two integer rows in the [Lehmer coefficient test](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) may each have degree at most \(\deg P\), the repeated factor is \(P\), and their cost is at most \(2^{\lfloor p/k\rfloor}\).
Here \(P\) ranges over all monic reciprocal irreducible minima of nontorsion algebraic units, with no independence hypothesis.
For each fixed \(P\), success for all sufficiently large primes is specified by a union of reduced residue classes modulo a fixed integer \(M_P\).
Given a splitting-field representation and a certified basis for the full multiplicative relation lattice modulo torsion, the union is determined by finite algebraic operations.

Exactly one of the following holds.

1. Finitely many fixed seeds suffice to give success at every sufficiently large prime.
2. There is a sequence of fixed reduced congruence classes, each contained in its predecessor, such that for every integer \(d\) some class excludes all seeds of degree at most \(d\) at all sufficiently large primes in that class.

The second alternative is equivalent to the existence of a degree budget \(B(p)\to\infty\) and infinitely many primes at which every seed of degree at most \(B(p)\) fails the equal-width test.
This characterizes the minimum with each seed tested at its own degree.
The [unrestricted criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) permits both rows to have the full budget even when the seed has smaller degree.

<a id="one"></a>
## Strict algebraic comparisons on exponent progressions

For each \(P\), choose one representative from each pair of inverse roots and let \(M_P\) kill the torsion in the multiplicative group they generate.
On every class \(p=a+M_P n\), the [powered-node comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v257.md#progressions) either rules out the rows by a permanent collision or gives two generically one-dimensional signed kernels on the torus parametrized by a basis of ordinary units.
In a noncolliding class, take the maximal minors of each signed system and remove their common factors after substituting the actual multiplicative relations.
The [projective height calculation on that torus](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v257.md#height) gives a limit \(R_{P,a}\) for \(p^{-1}\log H_p(P)\), where \(H_p(P)\) is the least cost before the cutoff and is attained at every sufficiently large exponent of this class.
It is the larger of the two signed rates.
With \(L\) the splitting field, \(\exp(2M_P[L:\mathbb Q]R_{P,a})\) is a positive real algebraic unit, since each extremal squared modulus occurring in the product is an algebraic unit.
The [algebraic comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v257.md#cutoff) therefore also rules out \(R_{P,a}=(\log2)/k\): raising this positive unit to \(k\) would give \(2^{2M_P[L:\mathbb Q]}\).
If the rate is below \((\log2)/k\), the linear margin eventually absorbs the floor; if above, success eventually fails.
Discarding residues not coprime to \(M_P\) removes only finitely many primes.
This gives a set \(A_P\subset(\mathbb Z/M_P\mathbb Z)^\times\) of successful classes for each fixed \(P\), without giving a numerical onset.

<a id="cover"></a>
## Passing from finite residue tests to a prime subsequence

By [field recovery and reflection](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower), success implies \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\le p(\log2)/k\), hence \(M(P)\le2^{1/k}\).
There are only finitely many such seeds at each bounded degree by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
List them in order of nondecreasing degree, breaking ties arbitrarily.
The same argument below applies to a finite list by keeping its last finite stage thereafter.
If the list is empty the second alternative holds using the class modulo one.

At stage \(j\) take the common modulus \(D_j\) to be the least common multiple of the first \(j\) seed moduli.
Mark the reduced classes modulo \(D_j\) whose reduction belongs to none of the first \(j\) sets \(A_P\).
Reduction sends a marked class to a marked parent at every earlier stage.
If some stage has no marked class, all primes outside the finitely many prime divisors of \(D_j\) and the finitely many exceptional exponents are covered by those fixed seeds.
Conversely, any fixed finite collection covering every sufficiently large prime covers every reduced residue class of its common modulus, since [Dirichlet's theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#sut21-u001) gives arbitrarily large primes in every such class.
Supersets of a finite cover still give empty marked sets.

In the absence of a finite cover, every stage has at least one marked class.
The reduction tree has finite branching and nodes at arbitrarily large levels.
Starting at its root, successively choose a child with descendants at arbitrarily large levels, obtaining nested marked classes.
These classes avoid the successful residues of every finite initial segment, and every bounded degree is contained in such a segment after the measure restriction.
At its stage take the maximum prime onset over this finite segment.
All primes in the marked class beyond that onset exclude every seed up to the prescribed degree.
If a finite cover existed, a class excluding all its degrees would contain arbitrarily large primes by Dirichlet's theorem, a contradiction.

For increasing \(d\), take an increasing qualifying prime \(p_d\) in the corresponding nested class beyond the onset for degree \(d\).
The step function \(B(p)=d\) on \(p_d\le p<p_{d+1}\) tends to infinity and has the stated exclusions at \(p_d\).
Conversely, if a finite cover existed, its degrees would eventually lie below any diverging budget, so the asserted prime subsequence could not exist.
This proof supplies neither effective onsets nor a choice between the two alternatives for the unrestricted collection of seeds.
