# Valuation depths above rational primes for translates of adjacent measures

<a id="depth"></a>
## The prime-ideal mass lies on a small fraction of local embeddings

Assume the unrestricted gap fails and take a late [adjacent-record translate](Yin_C_G0901_v200.md#across-records) \(z=2-A^aB\) with the second closest exponent from below.
Write \(F=\mathbb Q(A^aB)\), \(D=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(b=\log B\), \(n=[\mathbb Q(A,B):\mathbb Q(A)]\), and \(H=D^{-1}\log |N_{F/\mathbb Q}z|\to\log2\).
For each rational prime \(p\), use \(v_p(p)=1\) on \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) and let \(F_p(t)\), \(t>0\), be the fraction of embeddings \(\sigma:F\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) with \(v_p(\sigma z)\ge t\).
Taking \(u=H/n+C\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}\) for an absolute sufficiently large \(C\), one has \(u\to0\) and
\[
 \sum_p\log p\int_0^\infty F_p(t)\,dt=H,\qquad
 \sum_p\log p\int_0^\infty F_p(t)^2\,dt\le u .
\]
At **every** \(p\), keep just the \(\lceil D\sqrt u\rceil\) deepest embeddings (or all if fewer).
Summed over all primes, these choices retain normalized log mass at least \(H-\sqrt u/4\).
Equivalently, when most of the norm descends to a small set of rational primes, the logarithm of that norm must be carried at exceptional local depths.
For any finite set \(S\) and caps \(c_p\ge0\) on the normalized depths, the total mass above \(S\) up to those caps is at most \((u\sum_{p\in S}c_p\log p)^{1/2}\).
In particular, if \(S\) carries mass at least \(h>0\), then
\[
 \sum_{p\in S}(\log p)\max_{\sigma:F\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_p} v_p(\sigma z)\ \ge\ h^2/u .
\]

For the first identity, the local degree formula counts every prime of \(F\) with its multiplicity among embeddings.
For the second, condition on the embedding of \(\mathbb Q(A)\) at \(p\), using \(F=\mathbb Q(A,B)\) by [strict dominance of both factors](Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#energy).
Within a fiber the square of the conditional fraction is the probability that two independently chosen relative images of \(z\) meet the same valuation threshold.
Averaging the thresholds and summing over \(p\), the diagonal choices contribute \(H/n\).
For off-diagonal choices, the integrated indicators are the normalized masses of the ideal gcds of pairs of distinct relative translates, averaged over the embeddings of a common normal field.
Each gcd has mass at most \(C\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}\) by the [two-conjugate estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v200.md#records).
Equivalently one may choose the two relative images first and then transport the entire pair by a uniform global embedding, which averages each gcd at its correct absolute local weights.
Jensen's inequality for the conditional fractions gives the desired bound for the marginal \(F_p\).
Removing the largest fraction \(\rho\) of depths at each prime leaves tail mass at most \(\sum_p\log p\int(F_p-\rho)_+\le u/(4\rho)\), since \((x-\rho)_+\le x^2/(4\rho)\).
Take \(\rho=\sqrt u\), rounding the number retained upwards.
Cauchy–Schwarz on the intervals \(0<t\le c_p\) proves the cap inequality and the final assertion.

## A single rational prime can carry the full norm for a strict Perron unit

There exist strict Perron units \(1<\beta_n<2\) of degrees \(n\to\infty\) with \(\beta_n\to2\), such that \(z_n=2-\beta_n\) has absolute norm \(3^{k_n}\), \(k_n/n\to\log2/\log3\).
Exactly one of the \(n\) embeddings into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_3\) gives positive depth, equal to \(k_n\); the corresponding local field is \(\mathbb Q_3\).
Even in a common normal closure any two distinct conjugates of \(z_n\) have coprime principal ideals.
Thus the real approximation, strict dominance, unit condition, limiting normalized translate norm and pairwise ideal separation allow a fixed rational prime to carry all the mass.
The example does not supply the factorization \(A^aB\) by successive strict record measures.

Let \(n=2^s\) grow, and let \(k=k_n\) be the largest positive odd integer with \(3^k\le2^{n-\sqrt n}\).
The integer \(D_0=(2^n-1-3^k)/4\) has binary expansion \(\sum_{j\ge1} b_j2^{j-1}\).
Set \(f(X)=X^n-1-2\sum b_jX^j\), so \(f(2)=3^k\).
Since \(D_0=2^{n-2}-(1+3^k)/4\), the entries \(b_{n-2},b_{n-3},\ldots,b_{n-16}\) all equal one for sufficiently large \(n\).
Choose five even indices from \(n-4,n-6,\ldots,n-14\).
At any chosen index \(j\), moving one digit from \(j\) to two copies at \(j-1\) changes \(f\) by \(2X^{j-1}(X-2)\).
The moves have disjoint supports, preserve nonnegative digits and \(f(2)\), reduce \(f(1)\) by two and increase \(f'(2)\) by \(2^j\equiv1\bmod3\).
Choose a number of moves between zero and five so that \(f(1)\equiv2\bmod4\) and \(f'(2)\not\equiv0\bmod3\), possible by the two coprime congruence moduli.
Here \(f(1)\) was even before the moves.
The resulting \(f(X+1)\) is Eisenstein at two because \(f(X)\equiv X^n+1\bmod2\), and \(n\) is a power of two.
Thus \(f\) is irreducible and its roots are units by its constant term.

Write the final polynomial as \(X^n-\sum_{j<n}c_j X^j\), with \(c_j\ge0\), \(c_0=1\) and \(c_{n-2},c_{n-3}>0\).
The positive root \(\beta_n\) lies in \((1,2)\) and is strictly dominant, since equality in the triangle inequality on its spectral circle would force the phase to have both square and cube one.
The decreasing function \(U(x)=\sum_{j<n}c_j x^{j-n}\) satisfies \(U(\beta_n)=1\), \(U(2)=1-3^k/2^n\) and \(U(\beta_n)\ge(2/\beta_n)U(2)\).
Hence \(\beta_n\to2\).
At three the residue \(2\) is a simple root of \(f\), so precisely one root reduces to \(2\), and that root lies in \(\mathbb Q_3\) by Hensel's lemma.
All other \(3\)-adic depths of the translate vanish; the norm identity \(f(2)=3^k\) gives depth \(k\) at the remaining embedding and excludes every other rational prime.
At any prime of the normal closure over three, simplicity of the same residue again prevents two conjugates from contributing there.
The choice of \(k\) gives the asserted norm growth.
