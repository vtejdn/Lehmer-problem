# A degree obstruction for a Salem polynomial taking a monomial value twice on a torsion grid

<a id="statement"></a>
## Classification at small residual degree

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma\), with \(\log\sigma\le A\) for fixed \(A\), degree \(2m\) and trace polynomial \(S\), so \(P(z)=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose for an integer \(n\ge1\), a sign \(\eta\), and an integer \(u\) one has
\[
                 P(z)\equiv\eta z^u\pmod{(z^n-1)^2}
                        \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}].                      \tag{1}
\]
Then \(u=m,\ \eta=-1,\ n\le m\).
Write \(D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\) and \(e=m-n\).
There is a monic \(T\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(e\) such that \(S=(D_n-2)T-1\).
There is a constant \(c_A>0\) such that for every sufficiently large \(n\), either
\[
              e=1,\qquad T=X-c\quad\text{for an integer }c\ge3,                 \tag{2}
\]
or
\[
                  e> c_A\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log(3n)}\right)^2.              \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) classifies every possible residual polynomial in an infinite bounded-measure family with bounded residual degree, up to finitely many indices as \(n\to\infty\).
Each \(c\ge3\) occurs, with measures tending from above to \(\beta_c=(c+\sqrt{c^2-4})/2\).
The smallest limit in this bounded residual degree regime is \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
At the larger scale, there is \(c'_A>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(n\), either
\[
       e\ge c'_A(n/\log(2n))^{1/3},                                         \tag{4}
\]
or the full shifted lift \(P+z^m\) is a single reciprocal quadratic \(z^2-cz+1\), \(c\ge3\) integral, times a cyclotomic product, with \(\beta_c<\sigma\).
In particular (4) holds on every bounded input with \(\sigma\le (3+\sqrt5)/2\) and sufficiently large \(n\), including every hypothetical shrinking Salem sequence.
The conclusion concerns the original Salem polynomial at the double congruence.
The contributor derived the negative-value mesh, ellipse count and bounded-shape classification.
The coordinator supplied the comparison of long power sums and the paired discriminant argument for the polynomial scale, and the contributor obtained the uniform quadratic alternative from that argument.

<a id="shape"></a>
## The forced trace quotient and its negative values

Reciprocity gives \(P'(1)=mP(1)\), and (1) gives \(P(1)=\eta,\ P'(1)=u\eta\).
The unique exterior trace exceeds two, with all remaining traces strictly between minus two and two, so \(P(1)=S(2)<0\).
These identities give the stated exponent and sign.
Now \(D_n(x)-2=z^{-n}(z^n-1)^2\) at \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
The quotient \((S(x)+1)/(D_n(x)-2)\) is integral Laurent in \(z\) by (1), and invariant under inversion.
Every invariant integral Laurent polynomial lies in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) by grouping terms into \(z^j+z^{-j}=D_j(x)\).
Leading terms give the degree and monicity of \(T\).

For each node \(x_j=2\cos(2\pi j/n)\) with \(0<2\pi j/n<\pi\), \(D_n(x_j)=2\) and \(D_n'(x_j)=0\).
Thus \(S(x_j)=-1,\ S'(x_j)=0\).
As \(S\) is monic with distinct real roots, its derivative has one simple zero between each consecutive pair of roots and no others.
Its critical points with negative value are strict local minima, and every local maximum has positive value.
Between each two adjacent interior nodes there is therefore a point \(x\) with \(S(x)>0\).
At such a point \((D_n(x)-2)T(x)>1\) and \(-4\le D_n(x)-2\le0\), whence \(T(x)<-1/4\).
The angular points \(\theta\in(0,\pi)\) so obtained, \(x=2\cos\theta\), are within \(O(1/n)\) of every point of \([0,\pi]\), for \(n\) large.
Indeed the interior nodes have spacing \(2\pi/n\), and the first and last nodes lie within \(2\pi/n\) of the respective endpoints.
Reflection gives the same density on the whole circle.
In particular \(T\) cannot equal one at large \(n\).

<a id="size"></a>
## A uniform bound on the residual polynomial in the circle variable

Put \(a=\log\sigma\) and \(T^\#(z)=z^eT(z+z^{-1})\), which is monic integral of degree \(2e\).
Assume \(1\le e\le(\log n)^2\), so \(m=n+e\sim n\).
The [positive measure degree estimate](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(a\gg_A(\log(2m))^{-3}\), and hence \(a>2C_A\log(2m)/\sqrt m\) eventually for the constant of the [negative level separation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#statement).
Apply that theorem with level \(-1\) and logarithmic level zero.
The shifted lift \(P+z^m=(z^n-1)^2 T^\#\) has a single root beyond radius \(\exp(C_A\log(2m)/\sqrt m)\), and that root is positive and less than \(\sigma\).
Since only the \(2e\) residual roots can contribute to the measure, one obtains
\[
             \log M(T^\#)\le A+2e\,C_A\log(2m)/\sqrt m\le A+1                  \tag{5}
\]
eventually.
Every root of \(S+1\) has multiplicity at most two, since \(S'\) is simple.
The lift \(z+z^{-1}\) has local degree at most two on the nonzero complex numbers, so every root of \(T^\#\) has multiplicity at most four, including at \(z=\pm1\).

By collecting one copy of each integral monic irreducible factor with multiplicity at least \(i\) for each \(1\le i\le4\), split \(T^\#\) into at most four monic separable integer factors.
Each factor has logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(A+1\).
For such a factor of degree \(v\ge2\), the [confluent determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) with parameter \(\lfloor\sqrt v\rfloor\) gives a circle supremum with logarithm \(O_A(\sqrt v\log(2v))\).
Factors of degree zero or one satisfy the corresponding bounded estimate directly from monicity and (5).
As their degrees sum to \(2e\), this proves
\[
                 U=\max_{|z|=1}|T^\#(z)|
                          \le \exp(C'_A\sqrt e\log(2e)).                        \tag{6}
\]

<a id="ellipse"></a>
## Counting the roots inside a thin ellipse

Choose \(c_A\le1\) sufficiently small that \(e\le c_A(\log n/\log\log(3n))^2\) implies \(U\le n^{1/4}\) in (6) at all sufficiently large \(n\).
Let \(W(z)=z^{-e}T^\#(z)\).
All its \(2e+1\) Laurent coefficients have modulus at most \(U\) by integration on the circle.
For \(|\log|z||\le n^{-1/2}\) this gives
\[
          |z W'(z)|\le (2e+1)e\,\exp(e/\sqrt n)U .
\]
Along either angle or logarithmic radius the right side bounds the absolute derivative.
Moving from a nearby sampled angle of the preceding negative-value mesh and then radially to any such \(z\) changes \(W\) in absolute value by \(O(e^2 n^{1/4}(n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}))=o(1)\).
Consequently \(\operatorname{Re}W(z)<-1/8\) throughout that annulus for sufficiently large \(n\).

The image under \(z\mapsto z+z^{-1}\) of \(|z|=\exp(n^{-1/2})\) is the ellipse with semiaxes \(2\cosh(n^{-1/2}),2\sinh(n^{-1/2})\), enclosing \([-2,2]\).
On its boundary the estimate just proved gives \(\operatorname{Re}T<-1/8\), and the maximum principle gives the same strict upper bound throughout the interior.
Further,
\[
                 |D_n(x)-2|\ge \exp(\sqrt n)-\exp(-\sqrt n)-2
\]
on that boundary, by its circle parametrization.
It follows that \(|(D_n-2)T|>1\) there.
All \(n\) zeros of \(D_n-2\), counted with multiplicities by the identity with \((z^n-1)^2\), lie in \([-2,2]\).
Since \(T\) is nonvanishing throughout the ellipse, Rouché's theorem counts precisely \(n\) zeros of \(S=(D_n-2)T-1\) inside it.
But the original Salem trace contributes at least \(m-1\) roots in \((-2,2)\), so \(m-1\le n\).
The constant quotient has already been excluded, whence \(e=1\).
Writing \(T=X-c\) and using \(T(2)<-1/8\) gives the integral inequality \(c\ge3\).
This proves the dichotomy.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Realization of all small-degree residual shapes

For even \(n\) the [linear residual Salem family](Yin_C_G0345_v145.md#family) already proves that each \(c\ge3\) gives an irreducible Salem lift with this double congruence and the indicated limit.
The same proof works for odd \(n\ge3\): the mesh indexed by \(0\le j\le n\) has \(n\) sign changes for either parity, and the argument that a neutral-only factor would make \(c-x\) a unit with all conjugates greater than one is independent of parity.
The exterior trace \(x_n\) obeys \(0<x_n-c\le1/(\beta_c^n+\beta_c^{-n}-2)\) by the same increasing real Chebyshev identity.
In any bounded-measure instance of the linear branch, \(c<x_n=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\le 2\cosh A\), so only finitely many such integers occur.
These facts give the stated fixed-shape limit and its optimal lower bound.

<a id="secondary"></a>
## Long shared power sums after removing the distinguished roots

For the polynomial scale it suffices to treat \(1\le e\le(n/\log(2n))^{1/3}\), so \(m=n+e\sim n\).
The same Dobrowolski comparison permits the actual level \(-1\) in [the displacement theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#statement).
There is a simple distinguished exterior root \(\tau<\sigma\) of \(P+z^m\).
Its trace \(t=\tau+\tau^{-1}\) and \(t_0=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) obey
\[
       0<t_0-t=\exp(-ma+O_A(\sqrt m\log(2m))).
\]
This follows from the displacement \((1+o(1))/S'(t_0)\) and the [slope estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v119.md#slope).
In particular \(\sigma-\tau\le\sqrt{\sigma(t_0-t)}\), because \((t_0-t)/(\sigma-\tau)=1-1/(\sigma\tau)\ge(\sigma-\tau)/\sigma\).
For \(j\le n/4\), the mean value bound \(j\sigma^{j-1}(\sigma-\tau)\) is \(o(1)\), uniformly, by \(ma\gg_A m/(\log(2m))^3\).

Write \(Q=T^\#\).
The reversed polynomials of \(P\) and \(Q\) have the same initial coefficients through order \(n-1\) by \(P=(z^n-1)^2Q-z^m\).
Hence their \(j\)-th power sums agree through that order.
The root \(\tau\) belongs to \(Q\) and has multiplicity one.
After subtracting its \(j\)-th power and comparing with \(\sigma^j\), the \(2e-1\) remaining roots of \(Q\), counted with multiplicities, have joint \(j\)-th power sum of modulus at most \(2m+1\) for \(1\le j\le n/4\).
Let \(b\) be the largest positive logarithmic modulus among these remaining roots, or zero when there is none.
Then uniformly
\[
                              b\ll_A e\log(2n)/n.                               \tag{7}
\]
Indeed for \(b>0\) divide the remaining \(s=2e-1\) roots by one of modulus \(e^b\), putting the resulting points in the unit disk with one equal to one.
Apply the [consecutive complex power inequality (3)](Yin_C_G0345_v143.md#powers) with its starting parameter \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor-s\).
At some \(j\) in the final \(s\) positions through \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), the original power sum thus has modulus at least \(\exp(jb-C s\log(2ns))\) for an absolute \(C\).
As \(j\ge n/5\) eventually, comparison with \(2m+1\) proves (7).
It follows that \(\log M(Q)\le A+2e b\le A+1\) eventually throughout this range.

<a id="paired"></a>
## Pairing the roots of the odd-multiplicity trace factors

Remove from \(T\) the powers of \(X-2,X+2\) and take the product \(J\) of its other distinct integral monic irreducibles of odd multiplicity.
The quotient by the removed endpoint powers and \(J\) is a square.
Since \(t\) is a simple root, \(J\) contains it.
The lift \(F(z)=z^{\deg J}J(z+z^{-1})\) is separable, reciprocal and integral, has even degree \(D\le2e\), divides \(Q\), and contains \(\tau,\tau^{-1}\).
At each strict interior grid node \(x_j\), \(T(x_j)<0\).
Indeed \(D_n-2\) has a double zero and a strict maximum there, whereas \(S+1\) has a double zero and a strict minimum because \(S'\) is simple.
Thus the square just removed is positive at every such node, and the endpoint factors have constant sign on \((-2,2)\).
The values \(J(x_j)\) consequently have the same nonzero sign at all these nodes.

All \(D-2\) roots of \(F\) other than \(\tau,\tau^{-1}\) admit disjoint pairs at distances at most
\[
                              \Delta=C_A e\log(2n)/n.                           \tag{8}
\]
An off-circle exterior root \(\beta\ne\tau\) pairs with \(1/\bar\beta\), at distance \(2\sinh(\log|\beta|)\ll b\) by (7).
For the neutral roots in \(0<\arg z<\pi\), each interval between successive strict interior grid angles contains an even number, since \(J\) has constant endpoint sign and all its roots are simple.
Pair these roots within the interval of length \(2\pi/n\), and do the same in the reflected lower half.
The two residual arcs containing \(1,-1\) have length at most \(4\pi/n\) each, are invariant under conjugation, and have no real roots of \(F\), since both endpoint traces were removed.
Pairing each remaining root there with its conjugate exhausts them.

If \(D\ge4\), use the Vandermonde matrix for the roots of \(F\), subtracting one row from its partner for each of the \((D-2)/2\) pairs, as in the [paired discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v133.md#pair).
Each unmodified row at \(\alpha\) has norm at most \(\sqrt D\max(1,|\alpha|)^{D-1}\).
The difference row is bounded by the larger-modulus row bound times \(D\Delta\), using the difference of powers at each coordinate and retaining the smaller-modulus row of the pair.
The discriminant is a nonzero integer and Hadamard's bound yields
\[
       1\le D^{D/2}M(F)^{D-1}(D\Delta)^{(D-2)/2}
          =D\,M(F)^{D-1}(D^2\Delta)^{(D-2)/2}.
\]
As \(M(F)\le M(Q)\le \exp(A+1)\), \(D^{-2/(D-2)}\ge1/4\) for \(D\ge4\), and \(2(D-1)/(D-2)\le3\), this forces \(D^2\Delta\ge\exp(-3(A+1))/4\).
Together with \(D\le2e\) and (8) it proves (4).

The only alternative is \(D=2\), giving \(J=X-c\) with the integer \(c=t>2\).
Every remaining nonendpoint trace root of \(T\) apart from \(t\) has even multiplicity.
Any such root is a zero of \(S'\) and therefore real.
The [real trace count at level minus one](Yin_C_G0345_v125.md#statement) gives exactly one root of \(S+1\) above two and none below minus two.
Thus all the remaining trace roots lie in \([-2,2]\).
After removal of \(z^2-cz+1\) the monic integral shifted lift has only unit-circle roots, hence is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
This proves the quadratic alternative and the bound for all \(\sigma\le(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
