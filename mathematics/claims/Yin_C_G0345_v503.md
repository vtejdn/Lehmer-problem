# Divisors forced by two contacts in the Laurent polynomial quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## The order of the quotient

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be nonzero and coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\).
Let \(c>0\) be the gcd of all their coefficients together, put \(u=U/c,\ v=V/c\), and set
\[
 A=\mathbb Z[Y]/(u,v),\qquad A_Y=\mathbb Z[Y,Y^{-1}]/(u,v),
 \qquad D=|A|,\quad D_Y=|A_Y|.
\]
Here the bars denote the orders of the additive groups, with order one for the zero ring.
Both groups are finite and
\[
                      D_Y\mid D\mid |\operatorname{Res}(u,v)|.             \tag{1}
\]
The affine order \(D\) depends only on the normalized polynomial ideal, and \(D_Y\) depends only on its extension to the Laurent polynomial ring.
In particular, changing the two Laurent generators, or multiplying them separately by signed powers of \(Y\), preserves \(D_Y\).
For every rational prime \(\ell\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_\ell(D)=v_\ell(\operatorname{Res}(u,v))
   &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \ell\nmid\gcd(\operatorname{lc}u,\operatorname{lc}v),\\
 v_\ell(D_Y)=v_\ell(D)
   &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \ell\nmid\gcd(u(0),v(0)).
\end{aligned}                                                           \tag{2}
\]
Both gcds concern the actual polynomial generators used in the comparison.
For example, \(u=1+tY,\ v=1+2tY\) generate the unit ideal since \(2u-v=1\), while their ordinary resultant has absolute value \(t\) for each positive integer \(t\).
For \(u=Y,\ v=Y+t\), the affine quotient has order \(t\) and its localization at \(Y\) has order one.

Under the [distinct-seed contact hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#statement), the integer \(|C|^4/\gcd(|C|^4,(\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3)^d)\) there divides \(D_Y\).
Under the [single-seed contact hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#statement), \(|B|^4|D_{\rm diag}|^2\) divided by its gcd with the correction in that result divides \(D_Y\), where \(D_{\rm diag}\) is the product of its diagonal differences.
Thus every [calibrated divisor \(W\)](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#divisors) for a qualifying pair of factors divides \(D_Y\).
Their least common multiple divides the absolute ordinary resultant of every pair of nonzero polynomial generators of the same normalized Laurent ideal, even when the new pair itself has no prescribed contacts.

<a id="affine"></a>
## What the Sylvester cokernel counts

Write \(m=\deg u,\ n=\deg v\), and \(r=|\operatorname{Res}(u,v)|\).
If \(m=n=0\), the normalized constants generate the unit ideal and the empty Sylvester determinant is one.
Otherwise the integral Sylvester identity gives \(r\in(u,v)\), so \(rA=0\).
For each prime \(\ell\mid r\), the group \(A/\ell A=\mathbb F_\ell[Y]/(\gcd(\bar u,\bar v))\) is finite because at least one row survives reduction.
The \(\ell\)-primary part of \(A\) is killed by \(\ell^{v_\ell(r)}\), and its successive quotients by powers of \(\ell\) are images of \(A/\ell A\).
The primary decomposition for modules killed by \(r\) now gives finiteness of \(A\).

The ordinary Sylvester map sends \((a,b)\) to \(au+bv\) with \(\deg a<n,\ \deg b<m\) and target the polynomials of degree below \(m+n\).
Its cokernel \(S\) has order \(r\).
The target maps to \(A\), killing the Sylvester image.
For each prime \(\ell\), the powers of \(Y\) below \(m+n\) span \(A/\ell A\) since the reduced gcd has degree at most \(\max(m,n)\).
The cokernel of \(S\to A\) is therefore a finite group whose reduction at every prime vanishes, so \(S\to A\) is onto.

Suppose \(\operatorname{lc}u\) is an \(\ell\)-adic unit.
Over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) division by \(u\) identifies \(S\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\) with \(A\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\).
Indeed if a target polynomial belongs to \((u,v)\), reduce its coefficient of \(v\) modulo \(u\) to degree below \(m\).
The remaining multiple of \(u\) then has degree below \(m+n\), so its coefficient has degree below \(n\).
The proof with \(v\) in place of \(u\) gives the other unit case.

If both leading coefficients vanish modulo \(\ell\), put \(g=\gcd(\bar u,\bar v)\) and let \(\delta\) be the minimum of the two drops in degree, assigning infinite drop to a zero reduction.
Then \(\delta\ge1\), and \(\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell} S/\ell S=\deg g+\delta\).
Indeed for two nonzero reductions every relation is a multiple of \((\bar v/g,-\bar u/g)\), with the multiplier of degree below \(\deg g+\delta\) by the two Sylvester degree bounds.
If \(\bar u=0\), only the coefficient of \(u\) is free and the dimension of the kernel is \(n=\deg g+\delta\), with the exchanged case giving \(m\).
The reduced Sylvester map is square, so its kernel and cokernel have equal dimensions.
Since \(\dim A/\ell A=\deg g\), the surjection \(S\to A\) must have a nontrivial \(\ell\)-primary kernel.
This proves the first criterion of (2).

The images of multiplication by \(Y^j\) on finite \(A\) stabilize, and \(Y\) acts bijectively on the stable image.
The localization map onto \(A_Y\) is consequently surjective, with kernel the elements killed by some power of \(Y\).
For any prime \(\ell\), put \(g=\gcd(\bar u,\bar v)\).
On the finite \(\ell\)-primary part multiplication by \(Y\) is invertible exactly when it is onto modulo \(\ell\).
The latter condition means that \(Y\) is a unit in \(\mathbb F_\ell[Y]/(g)\), equivalently that at least one of \(\bar u(0),\bar v(0)\) is nonzero.
This gives the second criterion and finishes (1).
Joint content normalization is intrinsic as well, since \(c\) is the largest positive integer whose principal ideal contains the original ideal, in either coefficient ring.
Without division by \(c\), every prime dividing \(c\) gives a quotient onto the whole polynomial or Laurent polynomial ring over its residue field.

<a id="contacts"></a>
## The contact lengths come from the Laurent quotient

Dividing both rows by \(c\) preserves all graph divisibilities by monic factors, as is seen by taking the remainder after monic division.
For distinct seeds let \(M_\ell=\mathbb Z_\ell[Y]/(A_p^2,B_q^2)\) and \(N=M_\ell/(u,v)\) with the powered minima of the [integral length comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#length).
The two powered minima have unit constant terms, so \(Y\) is invertible in \(M_\ell\).
Thus \(N\) is a quotient of \(A_Y\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\), of length at most \(v_\ell(D_Y)\).
The [annihilator computation](Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#tensor) and the generator bound for the image of the two rows give
\[
 4v_\ell(C)-d\,v_\ell(\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3)
      \ \le\ \operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}N\ \le\ v_\ell(D_Y).
\]
This proves the claimed integral divisor, including at the exceptional primes.

For the repeated seed take the splitting valuation ring \(O\) and the simultaneous image \(Z\) in the [comparison of the labeled contacts](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#comparison).
This image is a quotient of \(O[Y]/(A_m^2,A_n^2,u,v)\), hence of \(A_Y\otimes O\) because \(A_m\) has unit constant term.
At the prime over \(\ell\) with integral valuation \(v_O\), the last group has \(O\)-length \(v_O(D_Y)\), by decomposing the finite abelian group into cyclic factors.
Using this bound on the length of \(Z\) in the same comparison preserves every [diagonal and off-diagonal lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#local) and gives the stated divisor after descent.
Consequently the [distinct-seed asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) and [single-seed asymptotic and Salem specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#rate) hold with \(\log D_Y\) on the lower-bound side wherever they estimate \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\) from below.

## Bounds at the cost of the original pair

Take the [computable width](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width) \(b=b(p)\) for \(Q=(1091/1000)^{1/3}\).
For sufficiently large prime \(p\), prime \(p<q\le2p\), and any passing pair with \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)<Q^{p+q}\), put \(\Sigma=p+q,\ \eta=\log H/\Sigma,\ K_Y=\log D_Y/\log H\).
The positive contact costs give \(H>1\).
For every qualifying choice the relative estimate for \(W\) gives
\[
  (1-1/b)\Gamma\le\log W\le\log D_Y
       \le (m+n)\log(H/c).
\]
The upper bound follows from (1) and Hadamard's inequality for the normalized Sylvester matrix.
More generally any two nonzero polynomial generators \(u',v'\) of the same Laurent ideal give the upper bound
\(\log D_Y\le(\deg u'+\deg v')\log\max(\|u'\|_1,\|v'\|_1)\) by (1), since their Laurent ideal has finite quotient and joint content one.
Such generators may share a power of \(Y\) as their only common polynomial factor.
In that case first remove the full power of \(Y\) from each generator, which preserves its coefficient norm and only decreases its degree.
In the ordinary-resultant divisibility above such a common power simply gives resultant zero.

The [mixed and repeated seed estimates at the actual cost](Yin_C_G0345_v501.md#resultant) use the row resultant only through \((1-1/b)\Gamma\).
The weighted contact inequality uses the original \(H\).
It follows for \(0<\eta<\min(\log Q,2/81)\) that
\[
 K_Y\ \ge\ (1-1/b)\frac{4\sqrt2}{3}
                   \exp\!\left(\left(\frac{2}{3\eta}\right)^{1/3}\right).
\]
In particular both \(K_Y\) and \(\log D_Y/\Sigma\) diverge on every such sequence with \(p\to\infty,\ \eta\to0\).
For \(0<s\le10^{-3}\) let \(T_s>\mathrm e\) solve \(T_s/\log T_s=(4s)^{-1/3}\) and put \(c_b=(4/3)(1-1/b)\).
The [smaller-contact estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v502.md#contact) likewise gives \(K_Y\ge c_b d h/\eta\) for its seed of degree \(d\) and log measure \(h\).
Its [strict inversion](Yin_C_G0345_v502.md#inversion) yields, for \(0<\eta\le10^{-3}\),
\[
 K_Y>c_b\exp(T_\eta),\qquad L_Y=\log(K_Y/c_b)>T_\eta,\qquad
 \eta>\tfrac14(\log L_Y/L_Y)^3 .
\]
Thus \(K_Y>\mathrm e^{18}>64{,}000{,}000\) when \(b\ge4\), and
\[
  \liminf_{p\to\infty,\ \eta\to0}
       \frac{\log K_Y}{\eta^{-1/3}\log(1/\eta)}\ \ge\ 4^{-1/3}/3
\]
along every sequence of passing pairs in this range.
The [equivalence with the vanishing of the unrestricted infimum](Yin_C_G0345_v501.md#gap) still concerns the existence of those passing pairs at their original costs.
