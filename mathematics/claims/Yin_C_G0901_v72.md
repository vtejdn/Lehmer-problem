# A height obstruction for concentration of conjugate arguments near rotating rays

<a id="statement"></a>
## Ratios and translated short arcs

Let \(w\) be a nonzero algebraic number of degree \(d\) and absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\).
Use angular distance in radians modulo \(2\pi\).
For \(\varepsilon\ge0\), let \(p\) be the number of ordered pairs of distinct conjugates of \(w\) at angular distance at most \(\varepsilon\), divided by \(d^2\).
If \(p>0\), then
\[
     \varepsilon>\frac{p}{100\log(8/p)}
        \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
     h>\frac{p^2}{200\log(8/p)}.                                         \tag{1}
\]
This includes pairs whose quotient is a nontrivial root of unity.
More precisely the same conclusion holds with \(p\) the probability that two *prescribed distinct* conjugates, transported by the same uniformly chosen Galois automorphism, have arguments within \(\varepsilon\).

Suppose a fraction \(\sigma>0\) of the conjugates is covered by \(T\ge1\) arcs, each of length at most \(\varepsilon\), with arbitrary centers.
If \(u=\sigma^2/T-\sigma/d>0\), (1) holds with \(u\) in place of \(p\), when \(\sigma d\) conjugates are selected from the cover.
In particular for a sequence of nontorsion units with positive total measures \(m=dh\to0\), a fixed positive fraction cannot be covered by \(O(\sqrt{d/\log d})\) arcs of lengths \(o(1/\sqrt{d\log d})\).
The number of rays needed to cover this fraction, even with centers chosen separately for every input, must grow faster than \(\sqrt{d/\log d}\).

<a id="near"></a>
## Localizing the consecutive-power determinant near the positive ray

Take any algebraic \(z\ne0,1\), including a root of unity.
Let \(p>0\) be the fraction of conjugates with principal argument of absolute value at most \(\varepsilon\).
We show that \(\varepsilon\le p/(100L)\), \(L=\log(8/p)\), implies \(h(z)>p^2/(100L)\).
Choose \(N=\lceil16L/p\rceil\), \(B=N(N-1)/2\) and \(J=(N+1)/3\).
Use \(A_N(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(X^j-1)^{N-j}\), of degree \(BJ\).
If \(z\) is torsion of order \(2\le q<N\), all its conjugates have arguments at distance at least \(2\pi/q>2\pi/N\) from zero.
But \(N\le17L/p\) makes \(\varepsilon<2\pi/N\).
This contradicts \(p>0\), so \(A_N(z)\ne0\) also in the torsion case.

For an embedding with \(v=\sigma z\), put \(s=|\log|v||\) and \(\theta=\arg v\in[-\pi,\pi]\).
Each normalized factor satisfies
\[
    \frac{|v^j-1|}{\max(1,|v|)^j}\le j(s+|\theta|).
\]
Indeed one can invert a value of modulus greater than one and take absolute values, then use \(1-e^{-js}\le js\) and \(|1-e^{ij\theta}|\le j|\theta|\).
The weighted mean of \(j\), with weights \((N-j)/B\), is \(J\).
First average logarithms over these weights, then over the selected embeddings using concavity and the bound \(2h(z)\) for the overall average absolute log modulus.
The normalized logarithmic contribution of the selected places divided by \(B\) is at most \(p\log\big(J(2h(z)/p+\varepsilon)\big)\).
At the remaining archimedean places use the [global Vandermonde bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#auxiliary) \(N^{N/2}\) on the normalized product; finite places need factor one since the coefficients are integral.
The product formula consequently gives
\[
 p\log\frac{1}{J(2h(z)/p+\varepsilon)}
       \le (1-p)\frac{\log N}{N-1}+J h(z).
\]
If \(h(z)\le p^2/(100L)\), the [elementary estimates for this choice of \(N\)](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#powers) give \(J\le6L/p\), \(N-1\ge15L/p\), and \(\log N\le2L\).
The left side is at least \(p\log(50/9)>p\) and the right side is at most \(2p/15+6p/100<p\).
The case of zero logarithm denominator would require an embedding \(v=1\), already excluded.
This proves the claimed inequality.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## A common rotation and the original root count

For two distinct roots \(a,b\) take \(z=a/b\).
The angular distance of \(g(a),g(b)\) is exactly the distance of \(\arg g(z)\) from zero.
Uniform extensions of embeddings give every conjugate of \(z\) the same multiplicity.
Since \(h(z)\le2h\), the preceding inequality gives (1) for the corresponding probability.
Averaging these probabilities over all ordered pairs with the diagonal assigned probability zero yields the fixed complex-embedding value \(p\) in the statement, because each automorphism permutes the full root set.
Some off-diagonal pair therefore has probability at least \(p\).
Both lower thresholds in (1) increase with the probability, proving (1) for the average as well.

Assign each of the \(\sigma d\) selected conjugates to one covering arc, giving counts \(n_1,\ldots,n_T\).
The ordered distinct pairs in a common cell give
\[
      p\ \ge\ d^{-2}\sum_i n_i(n_i-1)\ \ge\ \sigma^2/T-\sigma/d .
\]
For fixed positive covered fraction and \(T\le C\sqrt{d/\log d}\), this is at least a positive constant times \(\sqrt{\log d/d}\) at sufficiently large \(d\).
Then the width threshold in (1) is at least a constant times \(1/\sqrt{d\log d}\), and the height threshold multiplied by \(d\) is bounded below by a positive constant.
This contradicts a shrinking sequence at the stated widths, since bounded-degree finiteness ensures \(d\to\infty\).
Taking width zero gives the assertion for rays.
The bound permits widely spread angles and thus leaves the unrestricted gap question open.
