# A congruence count for polynomials with a growing number of exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quadratic prediction window and subexponential growth

Let \(d\ge1\) and \(0\le r\le d\) be integers, and let \(B\ge1\).
Count monic squarefree integer polynomials \(X^d+a_1X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d\) with multiplicative Mahler measure at most \(B\) and at most \(r\) roots of modulus strictly greater than one.
For an integer \(M\ge1\), prescribe one residue modulo \(M\) for each \(a_j\).
Set
\[
  w=\max(1,64r^2),\qquad A=d\log(2dB)+r^2\log(2d),\qquad
  W=6400 B^4 w A .
\]
The number in the prescribed class is at most
\[
                 \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}\frac{\sqrt W}{M}\right).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
Within that class the exact values of the first \(\min(d,\lfloor W/M^2\rfloor)\) descending coefficients after the leading coefficient determine the polynomial.
Each class has at most one member when \(M>\sqrt W\).
These conclusions permit arbitrary integer constant term, including zero, and every real or complex signature.

At fixed \(B\), if \(r=r(d)=o(\sqrt{d/\log(2d)})\), the total count at degree \(d\) is \(\exp(o(d))\).
Indeed \(M=1\) counts all the polynomials, \(A=O_B(d\log(2d))\) in this regime, and \(\sqrt w=O(r+1)\).
The [shorter recurrence for bounded exterior count](Yin_C_G0345_v704.md#prediction) controls its coefficient sum exponentially in \(r\).
The longer recurrence here has a coefficient square sum bounded solely in terms of \(B\).

<a id="filter"></a>
## A bounded polynomial annihilating exterior exponentials

We first give numerical bounds for the [polynomial with one prescribed circle zero](Yin_C_G0345_v627.md#circle-zero).
For an integer \(N\ge16\), put \(D_N(z)=N^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}z^k\) and \(V_N(t)=1-|D_N(e^{it})|^2\).
For \(|t|\le2\pi/N\), use the integers \(k\in[N/8,N/4]\) in the identity
\[
      V_N(t)=\frac2{N^2}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)(1-\cos kt).
\]
There are at least \(N/16\) such integers, each with \(N-k\ge3N/4\), \(k\ge N/8\) and \(1-\cos(kt)\ge2(kt)^2/\pi^2\).
It follows that \(V_N(t)\ge cN^2t^2\) with \(c=1/(1024\pi^2)\).
For \(2\pi/N\le|t|\le\pi\), the geometric sum gives \(|D_N(e^{it})|\le\pi/(N|t|)\le1/2\), and hence \(-\log V_N(t)\le4\pi^2/(3N^2t^2)\).
Integration on the first arc with \(s=N|t|\) and on the second with its upper endpoint extended to infinity gives
\[
  \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}-\log V_N(t)\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}
       \le \frac{2(\log256+2)+2/3}{N}<\frac{20}{N}.
\]

The scalar factorization used in the circle-zero lemma gives a polynomial \(p_N\) of degree at most \(N-1\), zero-free in the open disk, with \(|p_N(e^{it})|^2=V_N(t)\).
In the elementary factorization, take the exterior member of each reciprocal-conjugate pair of roots of \(z^{N-1}(1-D_N(z)D_N(1/z))\), and half the multiplicity at each boundary root, which has even order by nonnegativity.
The squared boundary modulus of the resulting polynomial is proportional to \(V_N\), with positive proportionality constant determined at any point where \(V_N>0\).
The single-factor circle mean of \(\log|1-az|\) is zero for \(|a|\le1\), including the integrable logarithmic singularity at equality.
Thus \(\log|p_N(0)|=\tfrac12\int\log V_N(t)\,dt/(2\pi)\ge-10/N\).
Since \(V_N(0)=0\) and \(V_N\le1\), the polynomial \(f_N=p_N/p_N(0)\) has \(f_N(0)=1,\ f_N(1)=0\), and disk supremum at most \(\exp(10/N)\).

Let \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\) have modulus at least one and product of moduli at most \(B^2\), with repetitions allowed.
For \(m\ge1\) take \(N=16m\) and write \(f_N(z)=(1-z)S_N(z)\).
The [reflection of a prescribed zero](Yin_C_G0345_v627.md#degree) on the unit circle gives the polynomial
\[
             L(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^m(1-\lambda_\nu z)
                                  S_N(z/\overline{\lambda_\nu}).
\]
It has constant term one, degree at most \(m(N-1)\le16m^2\), and vanishes at every \(1/\lambda_\nu\) with at least the listed multiplicity.
On \(|z|=1\), the identity \(|1-\lambda z|=|\lambda||1-z/\bar\lambda|\) makes the modulus of each factor equal to \(|\lambda||f_N(z/\bar\lambda)|\), also at any boundary zero.
Therefore \(\max_{|z|=1}|L(z)|\le B^2\exp(10m/N)<2B^2\).
For \(m=0\) take \(L=1\).
Writing \(L(z)=1+\sum_{\ell\ge1}c_\ell z^\ell\), Parseval's identity gives \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}|c_\ell|^2\le4B^4\).
Every sequence \(v_i=\lambda_\nu^i\), for all integer \(i\), satisfies \(v_j+\sum_{\ell\ge1}c_\ell v_{j-\ell}=0\) by substitution at \(1/\lambda_\nu\).
Linear combinations of these sequences obey the same recurrence.

<a id="prediction"></a>
## Fitting the preceding traces in square norm

For a counted polynomial let \(s_i=e_i+u_i\) be its \(i\)-th full power sum for \(i\ge1\), where \(e_i\) sums the exterior roots and \(u_i\) sums the roots in the closed disk.
The [disk-root energy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v704.md#energy) gives \(U=\sum_{i=1}^d |u_i|^2/i\le45A\), including when zero is a root.
At \(i\le0\) put \(s_i=0\), retain the exterior power sum \(e_i\), and set \(u_i=-e_i\), so \(|u_i|\le r\).

For step \(j\), fit a conjugation-invariant list of at most \(r\) complex numbers of modulus at least one with product of moduli at most \(B\).
For a candidate list let \(b_i\) denote its real \(i\)-th power sum, with zero for the empty list.
Minimize \(\sum_{i=j-w}^{j-1}|s_i-b_i|^2\), then take \(y_j\) to be the smallest possible \(b_j\) among the minimizers.
These extrema exist because each permitted fixed length gives a compact set of ordered lists, all entries are nonzero and the power sums are continuous.
The value \(y_j\) depends only on the given parameters and the decoded past.
Writing \(I_j=\{j-w,\ldots,j-1\}\), comparison with the true exterior list and the triangle inequality give
\[
              \left(\sum_{i\in I_j}|e_i-b_i|^2\right)^{1/2}
                         \le 2\left(\sum_{i\in I_j}|u_i|^2\right)^{1/2}
\]
at a minimizing list with \(b_j=y_j\).

Combine the selected and true exterior lists.
Their joint length \(m\le2r\) gives \(16m^2\le w\), and their joint product of moduli is at most \(B^2\).
Apply the recurrence to \(e_i-b_i\), and use Cauchy–Schwarz on its preceding coefficients.
When both lists are empty the difference vanishes directly.
It follows that
\[
                 |s_j-y_j|
                     \le |u_j|+4 B^2\left(\sum_{i\in I_j}|u_i|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Each positive \(i\) in these windows occurs for at most \(w\) values of \(j>i\), with \(1/j\le1/i\).
Each \(j\le\min(w,d)\) incurs at most \(w\) synthetic-past contributions, all bounded by \(r^2\), and later windows contain only positive indices.
Hence, with \(H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{j=1}^d\frac{|s_j-y_j|^2}{j}
  &\le 2U+32 B^4 w\big(U+r^2H_{\min(w,d)}\big)\\
  &\le1594 B^4 w A\le W/4 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second line uses \(H_d\le2\log(2d)\) and \(B,w\ge1\).

<a id="decoding"></a>
## Coefficient recovery and the count

Apply the [Newton decoding and signed partition count](Yin_C_G0345_v704.md#coding) to these predictions through the full degree.
Given the earlier traces and coefficients, Newton's identity and the prescribed residue of \(a_j\) determine \(s_j\bmod jM\).
Take the nearest integer \(t_j\) in this class to \(y_j\), choosing the smaller one at a tie, and put \(z_j=(s_j-t_j)/(jM)\).
Then \(jM|z_j|\le2|s_j-y_j|\) and the past and \(z_j\) determine the next trace and coefficient.
Thus the encoding is injective and satisfies
\[
              \sum_{j=1}^d j|z_j|\le \sum_{j=1}^d j z_j^2\le W/M^2.
\]
Every digit after \(\lfloor W/M^2\rfloor\) is zero, giving the asserted determination by the exact prefix and the separation for large \(M\).
The signed partition estimate bounds the number of such vectors by \(\exp((2\pi/\sqrt3)\sqrt W/M)\), proving (1).
