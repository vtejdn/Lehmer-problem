# Prime trace degree and ambient obstructions to one or two exterior roots

The coordinating researcher proposed the conjugation-commutator quotient, its ambient Salem obstruction, and the extension using transposition components.
The agent-S author developed the complete group and field arguments below, including the normalizer, the elementary symmetric-group checks, and the numerical output bound.
An additional author-side checker challenged the two group arguments; this is development provenance, not independent review.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The quotient and the ambient unit conclusions

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2p\), where \(p\) is an odd prime, with nontorsion roots.
Let \(F\subset\mathbb C\) be its splitting field, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), and \(\tau\) ordinary complex conjugation.
Let \(c\) be the number of complex-conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots of \(P\), and assume \(c\ge1\).
Equivalently, the degree-\(p\) trace polynomial \(Q\), defined by \(P(X)=X^pQ(X+X^{-1})\), has a nonreal conjugate pair.
Exterior and neutral mean modulus greater than one and equal to one, respectively.

Let \(D\) be the normal subgroup of \(G\) generated by all commutators of two conjugates of \(\tau\).
Then
\[
 D=G',\qquad |G/G'|\mid 2(p-1),\qquad
                      \exp(G/G')\mid p-1.                         \tag{1}
\]
For any Galois subfield \(E/\mathbb Q\) of \(F\), the conjugates of complex conjugation in \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\) commute if and only if \(E/\mathbb Q\) is abelian.
Every totally real subfield of \(F\) is itself Galois abelian over \(\mathbb Q\), and its degree divides \(2(p-1)\).

Write
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal O_F^\times}
 =\{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:
        y^n\in\mu_\infty\mathcal O_F^\times\text{ for some }n\ge1\}.
\]
All its elements are algebraic units.
For every reciprocal nontorsion \(y\) in this group whose exterior conjugates are all real, \(y\) is totally real and
\[
                         M(y)\ge\varphi^2=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},
             \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular this entire ambient radical saturation contains no Salem number of degree at least four.
Reciprocal quadratic units are not included in that exclusion.

If \(c\ge2\), so necessarily \(p\ge5\), every reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde{\mathcal O_F^\times}\) with at most two exterior conjugates has no neutral conjugate.
Its degree is at most four and
\[
                         M(y)\ge\kappa:=\frac{11+\sqrt{57}}8>2.     \tag{3}
\]
There is no input measure bound, independent-flip hypothesis, or restriction on the exponents or radical denominators in these assertions.

For all sufficiently large primes, the [five-term family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement)
\[
                   P_p(X)=X^{2p}-X^{p+1}-X^p-X^{p-1}+1
\]
therefore has the following property: its measure is below the plastic number, but the radical saturation of the units of its entire splitting field contains no reciprocal nontorsion output with at most two exterior conjugates and measure less than \(\kappa\), and contains no Salem number at any measure.
The family has a limiting measure strictly greater than one.

## A transitive subgroup forced by complex conjugation

Choose one root from each inverse pair.
The group \(G\) embeds faithfully in \(B_p=C_2^p\rtimes S_p\), and its unsigned image \(H\le S_p\) is transitive.
Distinct pairs have distinct traces: \(x+x^{-1}=z+z^{-1}\) implies \(z=x\) or \(x^{-1}\).
The trace polynomial is monic integral and irreducible, since a factorization would yield one of \(P\).

On the pair labels, complex conjugation exchanges precisely the two labels corresponding to each nonreal exterior conjugate pair.
It fixes every real exterior label and every neutral label.
Indeed, writing \(z=r e^{i\theta}\) gives
\[
               \operatorname{Im}(z+z^{-1})=(r-r^{-1})\sin\theta,
\]
which vanishes exactly on real roots or the unit circle.
Thus the image \(t\in H\) of \(\tau\) is a nonidentity involution with \(c\) transpositions.
Since \(p\) is odd, it has a fixed label.

Put \(T=\langle g\tau g^{-1}:g\in G\rangle\).
The subgroup \(T'\) is normal in \(G\), contains the generating commutators for \(D\), and is contained in \(D\) because \(T/D\) is abelian.
Consequently \(D=T'\).
Its unsigned image is \(L'\), where \(L=\langle hth^{-1}:h\in H\rangle\).

A transitive permutation group of prime degree is primitive: the equal-sized blocks of any block system have sizes dividing \(p\).
The orbits of a normal subgroup form such a block system.
Hence every nontrivial normal subgroup of \(H\) is transitive.
In particular \(L\) is transitive.
It cannot be abelian: in a faithful transitive abelian action an element fixing one label fixes all labels, whereas \(t\ne1\) fixes a label.
Thus \(L'\ne1\), and normality in \(H\) makes \(L'\) transitive as well.
It follows that \(p\mid |D|\).

Since \(|G|\) divides \(2^p p!\), its \(p\)-adic valuation is exactly one.
Choose a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(S\le D\), of order \(p\); it is also a Sylow subgroup of \(G\).
For any \(g\in G\), the subgroups \(S\) and \(gSg^{-1}\) lie in \(D\) and are conjugate there by the Sylow theorem.
It follows that
\[
                              G=D N_G(S).                          \tag{4}
\]

<a id="normalizer"></a>
## The prime-cycle normalizer and the exact quotient

An order-\(p\) generator of \(S\) projects to a \(p\)-cycle.
The product of its signs around that cycle is \(+1\), since its \(p\)-th power is the identity.
Changing representatives within the inverse pairs therefore makes \(S\) a pure unsigned cycle group.

The normalizer of a \(p\)-cycle group in \(S_p\) is \(C_p\rtimes\mathbb F_p^\times\): identify its labels with \(\mathbb F_p\) and its generator with translation by one.
A permutation normalizing it then has the form \(x\mapsto ax+b\).
In the signed group, a normalizing element has such an unsigned permutation.
Its diagonal sign vector must be constant, because conjugating a pure transitive cycle to another pure cycle requires that vector to be fixed by the cycle.
Consequently
\[
 N_{B_p}(S)=\{\pm I\}\times(C_p\rtimes\mathbb F_p^\times),
 \qquad
                         N_{B_p}(S)/S\cong C_2\times C_{p-1}.       \tag{5}
\]
By (4), \(G/D\) is a quotient of \(N_G(S)/S\), which is a subgroup of the last group in (5).
It is therefore abelian, its order divides \(2(p-1)\), and its exponent divides \(p-1\).
Since \(D\subset G'\), abelianity proves \(D=G'\) and (1).

The same Sylow argument proves a useful fact about every quotient of \(G\):
\[
 \text{a normal subgroup containing }S
                    \text{ has abelian quotient}.                 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed use that normal subgroup instead of \(D\) in (4) and then apply (5).
Thus any nonabelian quotient retains the order-\(p\) subgroup \(S\subset G'\).

If \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois inside \(F\), the images of all conjugates of \(\tau\) commute precisely when \(D\) fixes \(E\).
By (1) this is equivalent to the quotient being abelian.
For any totally real subfield \(K\subset F\), its normal closure inside \(F\) is totally real, so complex conjugation and all its conjugates act trivially on that closure.
The closure is consequently abelian, and every subfield of an abelian Galois extension is Galois abelian.
It lies in \(F^{G'}\), proving the asserted degree divisibility.

<a id="real-output"></a>
## All-real exterior outputs in the ambient saturation

First let \(w\in F\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit all of whose exterior conjugates are real.
Reciprocity makes all interior conjugates real as well.
In the splitting field \(E\subset F\) of \(w\), complex conjugation fixes each real inverse pair pointwise and inverts each neutral inverse pair.
It thus belongs to the diagonal sign subgroup of the signed permutation action on the inverse pairs of \(w\).
That subgroup is abelian and normal, so all conjugates of complex conjugation commute.
The quotient result makes \(E/\mathbb Q\) abelian.

A nontorsion unit has an exterior conjugate.
For completeness, if all conjugates had modulus at most one, the unit norm would make every modulus one.
The monic polynomials on the powers of all its conjugates would then have integer coefficients uniformly bounded by binomial coefficients.
There are only finitely many such polynomials, so the powers of the unit belong to a finite set and the unit is torsion.
An exterior conjugate of \(w\) is real by hypothesis.
In an abelian splitting field, complex conjugation fixing that conjugate fixes all its conjugates and hence the whole field.
Thus \(w\) is totally real.

For \(y\in\widetilde{\mathcal O_F^\times}\), raise its defining relation to the order of its torsion factor.
This gives \(w=y^N\in\mathcal O_F^\times\) for some \(N\ge1\), and also proves that \(y\) and its inverse are integral.
If \(y\) is reciprocal and nontorsion, so is \(w\).
Every conjugate of \(w\) is the \(N\)-th power of a conjugate of \(y\).
Thus all-real exterior conjugates of \(y\) imply the same property for \(w\), so \(w\) is totally real.
A neutral conjugate of \(y\) would now have \(N\)-th power equal to \(1\) or \(-1\), making \(y\) torsion.
There are therefore no neutral conjugates of \(y\), and reciprocity makes all its conjugates real.

Here is an elementary measure bound in the needed totally real reciprocal setting.
Let the degree of \(y\) be \(2k\), and let its exterior logarithmic moduli be \(s_1,\ldots,s_k>0\), with \(\mu=\sum_i s_i=\log M(y)\).
The nonzero algebraic integer \(y-y^{-1}\) has a nonzero integer norm, whence
\[
 1\le |N_{\mathbb Q(y)/\mathbb Q}(y-y^{-1})|
    =4^k\prod_i\sinh^2s_i
    \le4^k\sinh^{2k}(\mu/k).
\]
The last inequality is concavity of \(\log\sinh\).
It gives \(\mu\ge k\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=k\log\varphi\), the reciprocal-unit form of the [classical totally real bound](../audits/literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#linear-height).
For \(k\ge2\) this proves (2).
For \(k=1\), the minimal polynomial is \(X^2-aX+1\), with integer \(|a|\ge3\), which gives \(M(y)\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\) directly.
There is no degree-one reciprocal nontorsion unit.
This proves (2) in every degree.
A Salem number of degree at least four has a real exterior conjugate and neutral conjugates, so it has already been excluded before the numerical estimate.

<a id="two-exterior"></a>
## A quotient with one transposition forces the original involution type

Assume now \(c\ge2\), and suppose a reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde{\mathcal O_F^\times}\) has at most two exterior conjugates and at least one neutral conjugate.
Choose \(w=y^N\in F\) as above.
It retains a neutral conjugate, remains nontorsion, and has at most two exterior conjugates.
The all-real exterior conclusion just proved excludes all-real exterior roots of \(w\).
Its exterior roots must therefore be exactly one nonreal complex-conjugate pair.

Let \(J\) be the splitting group of \(w\), a quotient of \(G\), and let \(A\) be its unsigned action on the \(q\) inverse-pair labels.
The image \(t_w\) of complex conjugation in \(A\) is a single transposition.
The group \(J\) is nonabelian.
Indeed, if it were abelian and \(u\) were a neutral conjugate, then \(\tau u=u^{-1}\) and centrality of \(\tau\) would give \(\tau gu=(gu)^{-1}\) at every conjugate.
All conjugates would have modulus one, contrary to nontorsion by the preceding argument.
By (6), the Sylow subgroup \(S\subset G'\) survives in \(J'\).
The kernel of \(J\to A\) is a 2-group, so \(p\) divides the order of the image of \(D=G'\) in \(A\).

Draw an edge between the two labels exchanged by each conjugate of \(t_w\).
The connected components of this graph have a common size \(s\), because \(A\) is transitive and preserves the graph.
Transpositions on the edges of a connected graph generate its full symmetric group: along a path, conjugating successive edge transpositions produces a transposition from its first vertex to each later vertex.
Thus, if there are \(r\) components, the normal subgroup generated by the conjugates of \(t_w\) is \(\prod_{i=1}^r S_s\), and its derived group is \(\prod_{i=1}^r A_s\).
Normal generation and taking the derived group commute with the surjective maps here, so that last group is exactly the image of \(D\).
Since its order is divisible by \(p\), one has \(s\ge p\).
Each factor then contributes at least one factor of \(p\), while \(v_p(|G|)=1\).
Consequently \(r=1\), and
\[
                                  A=S_s,\qquad s\ge p.             \tag{7}
\]

We use the elementary fact that \(S_s\) has no nontrivial normal abelian subgroup for \(s\ge5\).
To check it directly, let such a subgroup contain \(a\ne1\).
For a transposition \(b=(i,j)\), the commutator \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}=(a i,a j)(i,j)\) belongs to the subgroup.
If \(a\) has a cycle of length at least three, choose \(j=a i\) in that cycle to obtain a 3-cycle.
If \(a\) has both a moved point and a fixed point, choose them for \(i,j\), again obtaining a 3-cycle.
The only remaining possibility is a fixed-point-free involution, so \(s\ge6\); choosing \(i,j\) in different 2-cycles gives a double transposition with fixed points, reducing to the preceding case.
Normality would then include all 3-cycles, which do not commute, a contradiction.

The original sign kernel \(\ker(G\to H)\) is abelian and normal.
Its image in (7) is trivial by this fact.
Hence \(S_s\) is a quotient of \(H\le S_p\), and \(s!\le |H|\le p!\).
Together with \(s\ge p\), this forces \(s=p\), \(H=S_p\), and the quotient map on the unsigned groups to be an automorphism of \(S_p\).

An automorphism of \(S_p\) preserves the size of an involution's conjugacy class.
For \(k\) disjoint transpositions the ratio of that size to the class size of a single transposition is
\[
                       \frac{(p-2)!}{2^{k-1}k!(p-2k)!}.            \tag{8}
\]
For \(k\ge2\) and odd \(p\ge2k+1\), this is at least
\[
                         \frac{(2k-1)!}{2^{k-1}k!}>1.
\]
The value is \(3/2\) at \(k=2\), and its ratio at \(k+1\) to that at \(k\) is \(k(2k+1)/(k+1)>1\).
Thus a single transposition cannot be the image of the original involution with \(c\ge2\) transpositions.
This contradiction excludes the assumed neutral conjugate of \(y\).

<a id="small-degree"></a>
## The remaining degree-two and degree-four outputs

For a reciprocal nontorsion unit without neutral conjugates, the degree is twice the number of exterior conjugates.
It is therefore at most four under the preceding hypothesis.
If all roots are real, (2) gives \(M(y)\ge\varphi^2>\kappa\).
Otherwise the degree is four and the exterior roots form a nonreal conjugate pair \(z,\overline z\), with common modulus \(r>1\).
The trace \(t=z+z^{-1}\) has a monic irreducible integral quadratic polynomial and a nonreal conjugate.
Its negative integer discriminant is congruent to zero or one modulo four, so its absolute value is at least three.
Therefore
\[
 \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\le|\operatorname{Im}t|
       \le r-r^{-1},\qquad
 M(y)=r^2\ge\left(\frac{\sqrt3+\sqrt{19}}4\right)^2
                         =\frac{11+\sqrt{57}}8>2.
\]
This proves (3); no optimality claim for this quartic constant is needed.

## Application and comparison with the retained obstructions

The [prime family calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) supplies irreducibility, exactly one real exterior root, total exterior count \(p/3+O(1)\), and a limiting measure \(L\) strictly between one and the plastic number.
Its number of nonreal exterior pairs is \(p/6+O(1)\), hence at least two for all sufficiently large primes.
Applying (2)–(3) proves the stated ambient conclusions.
No assertion about a nonreal trace pair for every small prime in that family is required.

The [normal-closure growth theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v260.md#statement) uses bounded measure and quantitative height bounds, and its [solvable prime-trace case](Yin_C_G0345_v260.md#trace) gives an eventual measure gap.
The quotient calculation (1) has no measure or solvability hypothesis.
The [Salem sign commutators](Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#inversions) concern inversion subgroups in the splitting field of a Salem input; here a nonreal trace of the input obstructs a Salem number in any subfield of its entire splitting field.
The [ambient-unit criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v307.md#ambient) allows ambient units to improve root-generated exterior minima in other fields.
The [conjugate-root-field result](Yin_C_G0345_v308.md#statement) controls the saturation of units from all conjugate root fields, which need not equal all splitting-field units.
The present argument directly covers the latter full unit group and its radical saturation, but its count conclusion is limited to one or two exterior roots under the stated involution types.

In particular the result is not an unbounded lower bound for the exterior count of all ambient outputs, nor a positive input measure gap.
The example family approaches \(L>1\); the unrestricted possibility of input measures tending to one remains unresolved.
