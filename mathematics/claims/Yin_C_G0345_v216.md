# A one-share degree survives every lift at 2 and 3 on simultaneous splitting delays

<a id="statement"></a>
## Local factors at unbounded block exponents

Let \(T\) be a product of \(t\ge1\) distinct primes greater than five and take any \(g\ge2\) with \(g\equiv1\pmod T\), for example \(g=T+1\).
There are arbitrarily large primes \(q\), with \(n=q-g\ge C(2g+1)^9\), for which the following holds at both signs \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
Put
\[
      P=X^n(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g),
      \qquad Q=P/C_0,\qquad d=(q-1)/g,
\]
where \(C_0\) is the [full cyclotomic part](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#statement).
For \(\ell=2,3\), set \(t_\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(\ell))\) and \(J_\ell=(q-1)/t_\ell\).
Both \(J_\ell\) are divisible by \(g\).

In each characteristic choose any \(J_\ell/g\) of the \(J_\ell\) Frobenius and inversion orbits on the primitive roots of \(X^q+\varepsilon\), meaning the \(q-1\) roots other than \(-\varepsilon\).
The choices at 2 and 3 are independent.
For each choice there is a unique monic factorization \(Q=F_\ell H_\ell\) in \(\mathbb Z_\ell[X]\) whose first reduction is the product of \(X-u\) over the selected roots and whose second reduction is the complementary quotient.
The factor \(F_\ell\) is reciprocal with constant coefficient one and has degree \(d\).
For all \(a,b\ge1\) these choices give a factorization of \(Q\) into two monic reciprocal polynomials modulo \(2^a3^b\) with the same first degree \(d\).

They realize zero exceptional degrees in both [residue counts](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#degrees), with \(k_\ell/J_\ell=1/g\) and
\[
               \frac{d}{n}-\frac1g=\frac{g-1}{gn}.
\]
The two selected sets of primes in the [cyclotomic evaluation ideal](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#ideals) give a squarefree ideal of norm \(6^d\).
This is the norm prescribed there for a hypothetical rational factor with both exceptional degrees zero.

At the third residue characteristic \(5\), put \(m=n/5^{v_5(n)}\).
The same delays satisfy
\[
                \omega(m)\ge t,\qquad
                \frac{m}{\operatorname{ord}_m(5)}\ge2^{t-1}.          \tag{1}
\]
Thus one can take \(t,g\to\infty\) while all the stated local factorizations persist and the normalized lower bound in the [shifted order estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v213.md#statement) tends to zero.
The conclusions concern actual factorizations over the two local coefficient rings and the size of that particular estimate at 5.
Existence of a rational factor of degree \(d\), principality of the chosen ideal, and existence of a factor at 5 of the prescribed degree remain undecided by this construction.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Splitting and the shifted delay

Apply the [primitive-element construction for splitting delays](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#limits) to
\[
                    K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2gT},2^{1/g},3^{1/g}).
\]
More explicitly, choose a nonzero integral primitive element and exclude primes dividing \(6gT\) or the denominators of the three displayed generators in its power basis.
Infinitely many primes divide nonzero integer values of its monic minimal polynomial by the argument in that construction.
At each such prime \(q\) outside the exclusions there is a homomorphism from the algebra generated by the three elements to \(\mathbb F_q\), by substituting the root of the minimal polynomial and inverting the denominators.
The root of unity retains order \(2gT\) because \(X^{2gT}-1\) has distinct roots modulo \(q\).
Consequently \(q\equiv1\pmod{2gT}\) and both 2 and 3 are \(g\)-th powers modulo \(q\).
Each \(\operatorname{ord}_q(\ell)\) divides \((q-1)/g\), which is even, so \(t_\ell\mid(q-1)/g\) and \(g\mid J_\ell\).
For each fixed \(g,T\), take \(q\) beyond any necessary large-delay threshold.

The congruences \(q\equiv g\equiv1\pmod T\) imply \(T\mid m\).
For a number \(m\) prime to 5 and divisible by at least \(t\) distinct odd primes, \(\operatorname{ord}_m(5)\) divides the least common multiple of the \(\varphi(p^u)\) for \(p^u\Vert m\).
At least \(t\) of these factors are even, so their least common multiple is at most \(\varphi(m)/2^{t-1}\).
This proves (1).
At \(5\) the [equal-block reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v213.md#five) uses exactly the effective delay \(n\).
Thus the factor \(\operatorname{ord}_m(5)/m\) in the lower bound is at most \(2^{1-t}\), in addition to its penalty from \(\omega(m)\).

<a id="lifts"></a>
## Prescribing both factors over the local integers

By the [prime-grid factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#degrees), after reducing \(Q\) modulo \(\ell\) the \(q-1\) roots in question are simple, disjoint from the exceptional roots, and partitioned into \(J_\ell\) orbits of size \(t_\ell\).
The chosen product \(\bar F\) has degree \((J_\ell/g)t_\ell=d\), is monic over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), and is coprime to its monic complement \(\bar H\).
It is reciprocal with constant one because its root set consists of inverse pairs, none equal to \(1,-1\).
The integral polynomial \(Q\) is itself monic reciprocal with constant one, since its noncyclotomic irreducible factors have this property by the same result.
Thus \(\bar H\) is also reciprocal with constant one.

We give the coprime factor lifting argument explicitly to retain uniqueness at every precision.
The existence statement is the factorization form of [Hensel's lemma in the Stacks Project, Section 59.32, item (f)](../literature/pdf/The26b/03QD--20261117.html).
Suppose monic representatives \(F,H\) of the specified degrees multiply to \(Q\bmod\ell^j\), \(j\ge1\), and let \(E=(Q-FH)/\ell^j\bmod\ell\).
It has degree less than \(\deg Q\).
To lift one step, solve
\[
        U\bar H+V\bar F=E,\qquad \deg U<\deg\bar F,\qquad\deg V<\deg\bar H .
\]
Bézout's identity makes \(\bar H\) invertible modulo \(\bar F\), determining \(U\) uniquely by reduction.
Then \(V=(E-U\bar H)/\bar F\) is determined with the required degree.
Adding \(\ell^j U,\ell^j V\) to \(F,H\), using coefficient representatives, gives the unique monic lift modulo \(\ell^{j+1}\).
The compatible systems therefore give the stated factors over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).

For a monic factor \(L\) with unit constant, its normalized reversal is \(L^\dagger=X^{\deg L}L(1/X)/L(0)\).
Reversal yields another monic factorization \(Q=F_\ell^\dagger H_\ell^\dagger\) with the same reductions.
Uniqueness gives \(F_\ell=F_\ell^\dagger\), and evaluation at one gives \(F_\ell(0)=1\) because \(F_\ell(1)\) is a unit.
Thus \(F_\ell\) is reciprocal in the ordinary sense, as is \(H_\ell=Q/F_\ell\).
The two local factorizations have the same degrees at both primes, so the coefficientwise Chinese remainder theorem combines their reductions for every \(a,b\).

For \(\xi=-\varepsilon\zeta_q\), the [prime ideal computation](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#ideals) gives \(Q(\xi)=6\) times a unit.
At each completion above \(\ell=2,3\) the valuation of \(Q(\xi)\) is one with the integral normalization, since the primes are unramified.
For a selected residue root, \(F_\ell(\xi)\) reduces to zero and \(H_\ell(\xi)\) is a unit by coprimality, so the entire valuation one occurs in \(F_\ell(\xi)\).
At the other roots its valuation is zero.
The total residue degree of the selected primes is \(d\) at each \(\ell\), which proves the ideal norm assertion.
Finally the displayed identity for \(d/n\) follows from \(n=q-g\).
Its positive error is smaller than \(1/n\) and hence below the tolerance \(1/(3(2g+1)^2)\) of the [archimedean allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) at the chosen delays.
It is the degree comparison for one exterior root from each block if a rational factor with this degree exists.

<a id="shifts"></a>
## Several prescribed shifts

Fix a finite set of nonzero integer shifts \(c\) including \(-1\).
For each \(c\) take a product \(T_c\) of \(t\) distinct primes greater than five not dividing \(c\), choosing all these primes distinct across the shifts.
The Chinese remainder theorem supplies arbitrarily large \(g\) satisfying \(g\equiv-c^{-1}\bmod T_c\) for every \(c\).
Use the same splitting construction with \(T\) there replaced by \(\prod_cT_c\).
All conclusions about local factors at 2 and 3 still hold.
One can take \(q\) large enough that every \(q+cg\) is positive, and \(T_c\mid q+cg\) follows from \(q\equiv1\bmod T_c\).
In particular the 5-free part of each shifted integer satisfies (1) with the same \(t\), and the shift \(c=-1\) is the actual effective delay at 5.
The other shifts describe an arithmetic restriction on possible order-ratio tests, without asserting a factorization or a Mahler bound from those integers.
