# Twice the least quotient energy is the threshold for escaping residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rounded energy and translation of classes

Let \(P\) be the monic irreducible integer polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(d\), with no root on the unit circle.
Set \(m=\log M(P)\), \(R=\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]/(P)=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and use normalized circle measure.
For an integral Laurent polynomial \(A\) and a residue class \(x\in R\), write
\[
 e(A)=\|A/P\|_2^2,\qquad E(x)=\min_{[A]=x}e(A),\qquad
 E_*=\min_{x\ne0}E(x).
\]
The [coefficient rounding formula](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#rounding) attains the first minimum by subtracting nearest integers from the Laurent coefficients of any representative divided by \(P\).
The [finite minimum theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#finite-search) attains the second and isolates \(E_*\) among the energies \(e(A)\) with \([A]\ne0\).
Translation of a class means multiplication by an integral power of \(\alpha\).
It preserves \(E(x)\).

For every \(\varepsilon>0\) the nonzero classes with \(E(x)\le 2E_*-\varepsilon\) belong to finitely many translation orbits, with the explicit bound below.
For some \(\eta=\eta(P)>0\), every class with \(0<E(x)\le2E_*+\eta\) outside finitely many translation orbits has the following stronger property.
Every numerator attaining \(E(x)\) splits across a gap in its support into two numerators of energy exactly \(E_*\).
In particular a sequence leaving every finite union of translation orbits has lower limit of its energies at least \(2E_*\).
There are sequences attaining this limit even under the additional requirement \(\lvert N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(x)\rvert\to\infty\).
For any fixed nonzero \(x\in R\),
\[
                 \liminf_{|k|\to\infty,\ k\in\mathbb Z} E(kx)\ge2E_* .
\]
These are fixed-input limits.
The [universal energy floor](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) gives \(2E_*\ge 2\max_{j\ge1,\ j\in\mathbb Z} j e^{-2jm}\).

For \(P=X^2-aX+1\), \(a\in\mathbb Z,\ a\ge3\), choose \(\alpha>1\) and put \(L_n=\alpha^n+\alpha^{-n}\).
Then
\[
       \liminf_{|k|\to\infty,\ k\in\mathbb Z} E(k)
             = 2E_* = \frac{2\min(a,2a-4)}{(a^2-4)^{3/2}} .
\]
With the same \(\eta(P)\), all sufficiently large integer multipliers in absolute value satisfying \(E(k)\le2E_*+\eta\) are exactly the following, up to sign.
The index \(n\) ranges over nonnegative integers.

| \(a\) | Multipliers up to sign |
| --- | --- |
| \(3\) | \(L_{n+1}-L_n\) |
| \(4\) | \(L_n\) and \(L_{n+1}-L_n\) |
| \(a\ge5\) | \(L_n\) |

<a id="separation"></a>
## What a large gap costs in one residue class

Let \(M=\sum_j|p_j|\) for \(P=\sum_j p_j X^j\).
Set \(\mu=\prod_{P(\rho)=0}||\rho|-1|>0\) and \(V=2\sum_j j|p_j|/\mu^3\).
The [Fourier coefficient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#finite-search) for \(w=|P|^{-2}\) gives \(|\widehat w(s)|\le V/|s|\) at each nonzero integer \(s\).
If \(e(A)\le T\), its integer coefficients satisfy
\[
        \sum_j|a_j|\le\sum_j a_j^2\le M^2 e(A)\le M^2T .
\]
Let \(H=\lfloor M^2T\rfloor\).
If two consecutive occupied positions differ by \(g\), let \(B,C\) consist of the terms to the left and right of this gap, respectively.
Expanding against \(w\) gives
\[
 \big|e(A)-e(B)-e(C)\big|
      \le 2(V/g)\|B\|_1\|C\|_1\le VH^2/(2g),
                                                        \tag{1}
\]
where the one-norms sum the absolute coefficients.
Every nonzero integral numerator has energy at least \(M^{-2}\) by the same coefficient bound.

Take a nonzero class \(x\) and a numerator \(A\) attaining \(E(x)\).
If the right side of (1) is less than \(M^{-2}\), then both \(B\) and \(C\) represent nonzero classes.
Indeed, if \(C\) were divisible by \(P\), the representative \(B\) of \(x\) would have smaller energy since \(e(C)\ge M^{-2}\).
Interchanging the parts handles a divisible \(B\).

For the sublevel \(T=2E_*-\varepsilon\) it suffices to consider \(T\ge E_*\).
Put
\[
   G=\max\{1,\lceil VH^2/(2\min(\varepsilon,M^{-2}))\rceil\},
   \qquad W=(H-1)G .
\]
A gap exceeding \(G\) would give two nonzero classes and \(e(A)>2E_*-\varepsilon\) by (1).
The numerator therefore has at most \(H\) occupied positions and width at most \(W\).
Translation puts its entire support in \([0,W]\), where the coefficient bound leaves finitely many choices.
In particular \(|N(x)|\le H^d e^{mW}\), since translation has absolute norm one and each embedding bounds a normalized representative by \(H\max(1,|\rho|)^W\).
Thus for any nonzero \(x\) and integer \(k\) with \(|k|^d|N(x)|>H^de^{mW}\) one has \(E(kx)>2E_*-\varepsilon\).
This proves the scalar lower limit and the finiteness assertions below \(2E_*\).

For the upper neighborhood choose \(\sigma>0\) such that a numerator of nonzero class with \(e(B)<E_*+\sigma\) has \(e(B)=E_*\), using the isolation theorem.
Take \(\eta=\sigma/3\), \(T=2E_*+\eta\) and the corresponding \(H\).
Outside a finite list of translates of numerators there must be a gap \(g\) with \(VH^2/(2g)<\min(\eta,M^{-2})\), by the same width and coefficient count.
The classes of those exceptional numerators lie in finitely many translation orbits.
For every minimizing numerator of a class outside these orbits, both parts at this gap have energies at least \(E_*\).
By (1) their sum is less than \(2E_*+2\eta\), so each has energy less than \(E_*+\sigma\).
This proves the asserted splitting.

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Classes attaining the limit

Fix a numerator \(A\) of energy \(E_*\) and nonzero class.
Put \(x_n=[(1+X^n)A]\).
The absolute field norm satisfies
\[
          |N(x_n)|=|N([A])|\prod_{P(\rho)=0}|1+\rho^n|
                     \sim |N([A])|e^{nm},
\]
because the factors from the interior roots tend to one and the factors from the exterior roots have ratio tending to one to \(|\rho|^n\).
These classes leave every finite union of translation orbits since norm is constant in each such orbit.
The two copies of \(A\) separate by a gap tending to infinity, and their coefficient one-norms are fixed.
Equation (1), or its first bound with those fixed norms, gives \(e((1+X^n)A)\to2E_*\).
Rounding can only decrease this upper estimate, while sublevel finiteness gives \(\liminf E(x_n)\ge2E_*\).
Hence \(E(x_n)\to2E_*\).

<a id="quadratics"></a>
## Integer multipliers for reciprocal quadratics

The [quadratic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#quadratic) gives the displayed value of \(E_*\).
Up to sign and translation, the minimizing numerators are \(1-X\) when \(a=3\), \(1\) when \(a\ge5\), and both when \(a=4\).
Every exceptional translation orbit in the splitting conclusion meets the rational integers in a finite set because its absolute field norm is fixed and \(|N(k)|=k^2\).
Thus a sufficiently large scalar of energy at most \(2E_*+\eta\) is the sum of the values of two signed translated minimizers with disjoint ordered supports.

Write \(D_j=\alpha^j-\alpha^{-j}\) for every integer \(j\).
The function \(j\mapsto D_j\) is strictly increasing and odd.
Two monomial terms \(\epsilon\alpha^u+\delta\alpha^v\), with signs \(\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}\), give a rational scalar only if \(\epsilon D_u+\delta D_v=0\), by conjugation.
The exponents are distinct, so the signs agree and \(v=-u\), giving \(\pm L_{|u|}\).

Two terms of the other type have sum \((1-\alpha)(\epsilon\alpha^u+\delta\alpha^v)\).
Conjugating and using \(1-\alpha^{-1}=-\alpha^{-1}(1-\alpha)\) gives the necessary relation
\[
   \epsilon(\alpha^u+\alpha^{-u-1})
        +\delta(\alpha^v+\alpha^{-v-1})=0 .
\]
Both parenthesized values are positive, so the signs are opposite.
Their common value is \(2\alpha^{-1/2}\cosh((u+1/2)\log\alpha)\) and likewise at \(v\).
As \(u\ne v\), this gives \(v=-u-1\).
Choosing the nonnegative exponent as \(n\) gives the scalar \(\pm(L_{n+1}-L_n)\).

At \(a=4\) a mixed sum would require \(|D_u|=|D_{v+1}-D_v|\), by subtracting its conjugate.
Replacing \(v\) by \(-v-1\) preserves the right side, so take \(v\ge0\).
For \(v\ge1\), the recurrence \(D_{v+1}=4D_v-D_{v-1}\) gives \(D_v<D_{v+1}-D_v<D_{v+1}\).
This excludes \(|D_u|\) for integer \(u\).
For \(v=0\) one has \(|u|=1\), and the original \(v=0,-1\) give only bounded values.

For the converse \(L_0=2,\ L_1=a\) and \(L_{n+1}=aL_n-L_{n-1}\) show that all the listed values are integers.
The two translated monomials at \(n,-n\) give \(L_n\).
The two oppositely signed translates of \(1-X\) at \(n,-n-1\) give \(L_n-L_{n+1}\).
In each applicable row both constituents have energy \(E_*\) and their supports separate as \(n\to\infty\).
Equation (1) bounds the rounded energies above by \(2E_*+o(1)\).
The listed scalar sequences tend to infinity in absolute value, so the scalar lower limit proves convergence to \(2E_*\) along each.
This also proves the stated classification for all sufficiently large \(|k|\).
