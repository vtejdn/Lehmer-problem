# Failure of the unrestricted Lehmer gap would already give arbitrarily fast second iterates on two strict records

<a id="statement"></a>
## A two-factor equivalence with freely prescribed growth

Fix any positive function \(G(s)\) locally bounded for \(s>0\).
Failure of a uniform Lehmer gap for nonzero integer polynomials is equivalent to the existence of a sequence of squarefree products \(P_j=f_jg_j\) of exactly two distinct irreducible strict degree-record minima such that, writing \(C_j=M(P_j)\),
\[
  1<C_j\to1,\qquad M(C_j)>C_j^2,\qquad
                 \log M(C_j)>G(\log C_j),\qquad \log M(C_j)\to\infty .
\]
One may simultaneously require \(\log M(g_j)=o(\log M(f_j))\), the degrees of both factors to tend to infinity, and each of the two angular probability measures weighted by the positive or absolute negative log moduli of the conjugates of \(C_j\) to tend to normalized arclength.
Even if the original hypothetical examples all have bounded second iterates, their existence forces examples in the class just displayed.
Thus ruling out this restricted fast-growth sequence alone suffices for the full gap.
The first measures in an example must still approach one; a sequence with a fixed larger accumulation point does not establish failure.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Perturbing one factor by small absolute height and large relative degree

Fix a real positive strict Perron algebraic unit \(A>1\), so all its other distinct conjugates are smaller in modulus.
Take real positive strict Perron units \(B_i>1\) with absolute Weil heights \(h(B_i)\to0\) and degrees tending to infinity.
There is no hypothesis of linear disjointness, and \(B_i\) need not converge to one here.
Put \(C_i=AB_i\), \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), \(D_i=\deg C_i\).
An automorphism fixing \(C_i\) must send both factors to conjugates attaining their individual maximal moduli, and hence fixes both factors.
Consequently \(\mathbb Q(C_i)=K(B_i)\), \(C_i\) is strict Perron, and the relative degree \(q_i=[K(B_i):K]\) tends to infinity since \(K\) is fixed.
The height triangle inequality and its reverse give
\[
  \left|\log M(C_i)-\frac{D_i}{\deg A}\log M(A)\right|
                 \le D_i h(B_i).
\]
In particular \(\log M(C_i)\sim D_i h(A)\to\infty\).

Under uniform embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(C_i)\), the argument of \(C_i\) becomes uniformly distributed conditional on each fixed embedding of \(K\).
Indeed each fiber of \(B_i\) is given by one embedding of its monic integral minimum over \(K\), of degree \(q_i\).
The product of the absolute discriminants of all these polynomials is at least one by the integral norm.
For each fixed nonzero Fourier order \(k\), the [reflected fiber estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#fibers) bounds its normalized phase coefficient in every fiber by
\[
      O_k\!\left(\sqrt{[K:\mathbb Q]h(B_i)+[K:\mathbb Q]\log(3q_i)/q_i}
                           +[K:\mathbb Q]h(B_i)\right)=o(1).
\]
Only the monic integral minimum, unit norm and discriminant norm are used in this estimate.
Multiplying by the fixed image of \(A\) rotates that entire fiber by a fixed angle.
The average absolute difference between \(\log|\sigma C_i|\) and \(\log|\sigma A|\) is \(2h(B_i)\), because \(B_i\) is a unit and its embeddings occur equally often.
Replacing either positive or negative log weight by the corresponding weight of the fixed \(A\) costs at most this difference in the mean against any phase test of modulus at most one.
The latter weight is constant in each fiber, and its mean is \(h(A)>0\) for each sign.
Hence both normalized weighted angular measures of \(C_i\) tend to arclength.
Equivalently at the unweighted level the radial log and argument converge jointly to the product of the discrete conjugate log-modulus law of \(A\) and uniform angle.

<a id="records"></a>
## Choosing both irreducible inputs

If the unrestricted gap fails, take the successive new positive minima over integer polynomials of bounded degree, at the degrees where they decrease strictly.
Such minima exist below any fixed measure ceiling by coefficient finiteness (powers of \(X\) are harmless), and they decrease to one.
At a degree giving a strict decrease, a polynomial attaining the new value cannot split into lower-degree factors: at least one factor has positive measure no greater than the attained value.
The same argument excludes a positive-degree cyclotomic or zero factor.
Near one both extreme nonzero coefficients are units, so after choosing the leading sign these are distinct monic irreducible unit minima, of increasing degrees.
Their measures \(A_j>1\) are strict Perron units by the [exterior-product property](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field).
They have house \(A_j\to1\) and absolute height at most \(\log A_j\).
Their degrees as actual values also tend to infinity, since bounded-degree nonzero algebraic integers of bounded house are finite.

Choose any successive subsequence of first factors going to infinity in this list, each time beyond the indices already used, and for the moment hold its value \(A>1\) fixed.
Use as \(B_i\) the measures of still later records.
Their absolute height tends to zero and their degree tends to infinity, so the fixed-factor calculation applies.
The first measure \(AB_i\) of the product approaches \(A\) from above.
At the \(j\)-th choice take \(\log B_i<(\log A)/j\), and select the later index large enough that \(\log M(AB_i)\) exceeds \(j+2\log(AB_i)\) and the supremum of \(G\) on \([\log A,2\log A]\).
The supremum is finite by local boundedness.
At the same time require the first \(j\) nonconstant Fourier coefficients of both normalized angular measures to have modulus at most \(1/j\); this is possible by their convergence at the fixed \(A\).
The resulting diagonal sequence has all the claimed properties by trigonometric approximation.
The converse follows immediately from the positive measures tending to one for these particular integer polynomials.

<a id="example"></a>
## A fixed positive accumulation point where the perturbation is explicit

For the [five-term family of prime indices](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#statement), write \(B_p=M(P_p)\) and \(D_p=\deg B_p\).
Its asymptotic gives \(h(B_p)\sim \sqrt{I_2/(6\pi p)}\to0\) while \(D_p\to\infty\) and \(B_p\to L>1\).
For any fixed real Salem number \(A>1\), the product of its minimum with \(P_p\) thus has first measure \(AB_p\to AL\) and second log measure asymptotic to \([\mathbb Q(A,B_p):\mathbb Q]h(A)\).
Both weighted angles tend to arclength although almost every conjugate of the first value has radial log tending in probability to one of the finitely many conjugate radial logs of the fixed Salem number, in the precise joint-law sense above.
This instance illustrates the mechanism at a positive distance from one.
