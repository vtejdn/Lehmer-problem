# A logarithmic Fourier lower bound in every geometric interval for Salem Bernoulli convolutions

<a id="statement"></a>
## The real Fourier transform at a prescribed scale

Fix a Salem number \(\alpha>1\) of degree \(d=2s+2\), put \(m=\log\alpha\), and let \(\nu\) be the law of \(\sum_{j\ge0}\epsilon_j\alpha^{-j}\) for independent fair signs \(\epsilon_j=\pm1\).
Use the Fourier transform with kernel \(e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\).
There is a constant \(c_\alpha>0\) such that for every sufficiently large real \(T\),
\[
   \max_{T\le\xi\le\alpha T}|\widehat\nu(\xi)|
       \ \ge\ c_\alpha(\log T)^{-\Lambda},\qquad
   \Lambda=\frac{2d\log 2}{m}\bigl(1+(2d-1)(d-1)\bigr).              \tag{1}
\]
In particular every eventual upper bound \(C(\log|\xi|)^{-p}\) must have \(p\le\Lambda\).
An upper estimate with a logarithmic exponent increasing to infinity is impossible for the fair law.
Marshall-Maldonado and Solomyak give a fixed-input upper estimate of iterated-logarithm type, and in Appendix B, Proposition B.3, restrict the possible growth of an exponent for the fair law. [Source statement and scope](../audits/literature/MMS26/Yin_U001.md#mms26-u001)
Estimate (1) is a lower bound over real frequencies.
Its implied constant and starting scale may depend on \(\alpha\).
The displayed exponent also grows with the degree and inverse measure.

<a id="strip"></a>
## A common transition interval for a family of frequencies

Let \(N=\lceil\log T/m\rceil\), \(H=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor\), \(K=2^H\), and \(r=\alpha^{-1}\).
By the [unit construction](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#units) with \(\varepsilon=N^{-2}\), choose an order unit \(u\) positive at both real embeddings, with neutral moduli \(O_\alpha(N^{-2})\) and both real moduli \(O_\alpha(N^{2s})\).
Write \(P\) for the minimal polynomial and \(b=u/P'(\alpha)\).
All \(\operatorname{Tr}(b\alpha^t)\) are integers for \(t\in\mathbb Z\) by the [trace-dual formula](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#conductor).
The norm of \(u\) has magnitude one, so the neutral upper bounds together with the bound at \(r\) also give \(b_\alpha\gg_\alpha N^{2s}\).
Here subscripts denote embeddings, and \(b_\alpha>0\) since \(P'(\alpha)>0\).

For each \(0\le h<H\) choose an integer \(L_h\) so that
\[
                      \xi_h=2^{h-1}b_\alpha\alpha^{L_h}
                              \ \in [T,\alpha T).
\]
Thus \(L_h=N+O_\alpha(\log N)\) uniformly.
By independence and convergence of the sign series,
\[
      |\widehat\nu(\xi_h)|=\prod_{t\le L_h}
                                |\cos(\pi 2^h b_\alpha\alpha^t)|.
\]
Put \(J=\lceil d\log N/m\rceil\).
The interval \([-J,J]\) is present in every one of these products once \(T\) is sufficiently large.

For \(t<-J\), \(2^h b_\alpha\alpha^t\ll_\alpha N^{1+2s}\alpha^t\), whose squared sum is \(O_\alpha(N^{-2})\).
For \(J<t\le L_h\), subtract \(2^h\operatorname{Tr}(b\alpha^t)\).
The remaining phase in absolute value is at most
\[
             O_\alpha(N^{1+2s}\alpha^{-t}+N^{-1}).
\]
Its squared sum over this range is \(O_\alpha(1)\), using the geometric series and \(L_h\le 2N\).
In both ranges the distance to an integer is less than \(1/4\) for large \(N\), uniformly in \(h\).
Consequently the negative logarithm of the product outside \([-J,J]\) is \(O_\alpha(1)\), by the quadratic bound for \(-\log|\cos(\pi x)|\) near integers.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Arithmetic separation for the last doubling

For \(-J\le t\le J\), let \(x_t=b_\alpha\alpha^t\).
The two real embeddings of \(b\alpha^t\) have opposite signs, since the derivatives of the monic polynomial at its two simple real roots have opposite signs.
Thus \(Kx_t\) cannot be rational.
A fixed integer \(D>0\) makes \(D/P'(\alpha)\) an algebraic integer.
For an integer \(p_t\) nearest to \(Kx_t\), the nonzero algebraic integer \(D(Kb\alpha^t-p_t)\) has absolute norm at least one.
Every conjugate of \(Kb\alpha^t\) has modulus \(O_\alpha(N^{1+2s+d})=O_\alpha(N^{2d-1})\), using \(\alpha^{J}\ll_\alpha N^d\).
The same bound applies to \(|p_t|\), so the other \(d-1\) factors of the norm give
\[
            \operatorname{dist}(Kx_t,\mathbb Z)
                      \gg_\alpha N^{-(2d-1)(d-1)}.                    \tag{2}
\]

<a id="average"></a>
## Selecting one scalar for the infinite product

Use the [dyadic sine identity](Yin_C_G0345_v675.md#telescope) at each \(x_t\), with initial exponent zero.
By (2) the last sine is nonzero, and
\[
 \frac1H\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}-\log|\cos(\pi 2^h x_t)|
 \le \log 2+\frac{(2d-1)(d-1)\log N+O_\alpha(1)}{H}
 =\bigl(1+(2d-1)(d-1)\bigr)\log2+O_\alpha(1/\log N).
\]
Here \(|\sin(\pi Kx_t)|\ge 2\operatorname{dist}(Kx_t,\mathbb Z)\), and the initial sine has modulus at most one.
Sum over all \(2J+1\) values of \(t\).
Some common scalar \(2^h\) has negative logarithmic cost at most \(\Lambda\log N+O_\alpha(1)\) on the transition interval.
The two uniform tail estimates give the same upper bound for the whole negative logarithm.
Since \(N=\log T/m+O(1)\) and this particular \(\xi_h\) lies in the prescribed interval, exponentiation proves (1).
This choice of scalar supplies no input-uniform starting frequency or positive Mahler gap.
