# The Mahler cost of many conjugates of small dyadic degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A discriminant bound for partial splitting

For a monic squarefree \(F\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) of degree \(d\ge2\), write \(M(F)=\prod_{F(z)=0}\max(1,|z|)\).
For a rational prime \(p\) and an integer \(B\ge1\), let \(n_{p,B}\) be the number of its distinct unit roots in a fixed \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) with \([\mathbb Q_p(z):\mathbb Q_p]\le B\).
Let \(a_{p,r}\) count the nonzero elements of exact degree \(r\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), and put
\[
 S_{p,B}=\sum_{r=1}^B\lfloor B/r\rfloor a_{p,r}\le5p^B.
\]
At one prime the count satisfies
\[
 \frac{n_{p,B}^2}{S_{p,B}}-n_{p,B}
 \ \le\ v_p(\operatorname{disc}F)
 \ \le\ d\log_p d+(2d-2)\log_p M(F).                          \tag{1}
\]
One may also sum the positive parts of the left side times \(\log p\) over any finite set of primes, with separately chosen cutoffs, and bound the sum by \(d\log d+(2d-2)\log M(F)\).

In particular, at \(B=\lfloor\frac12\log_2d\rfloor\) and bounded Mahler measure, \(n_{2,B}=O(d^{3/4}\sqrt{\log d})=o(d)\) as \(d\to\infty\).
More generally the proportion tends to zero whenever \(2^B\log d=o(d)\) and \(M(F)\) is bounded.
If instead \(n_{2,B}\ge\delta d\) at the half-logarithmic cutoff, for fixed \(\delta>0\), then \(\log M(F)\gg_\delta\sqrt d\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).
This last growth order is sharp even for the minimum of a Salem number with **all** conjugates below or at that cutoff.
For growing odd \(B\), there exist Salem numbers \(T\) of degree \(d=2^{B+1}(2^B-2)\), with every dyadic conjugate in the unramified extension of degree \(B=\lfloor\frac12\log_2d\rfloor\), and
\[
                         c\sqrt d\le \log T\le C\sqrt d
\]
for absolute constants and all sufficiently large such \(B\).

For an integral Salem realization of the [exact trace pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), \(M(F)=T<1+1/q\), so the first upper bound applies before imposing the pole value or the graph power equation.
The graph equation supplies the sharper [\(O(2^B)\) upper bound](Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement).
The high-measure Salem family here addresses the cost of splitting; it has no near-one exterior root.
Fili and Pottmeyer study heights under splitting into a prescribed finite normal local extension at each selected prime, with all conjugates included. [Quantitative height bounds under splitting conditions, introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.01498)
In (1) only the counted roots have a degree restriction, and they may generate different local fields.

<a id="packing"></a>
## Counting pairs above each residue element

For a counted root \(z\), write \(e,f\) for the ramification index and residue degree of \(\mathbb Q_p(z)\).
If \(\bar z=\alpha\) has exact degree \(r\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), then \(r\mid f\) and \(ef\le B\), so \(e\le\lfloor B/r\rfloor\).
The unique \((p^r-1)\)-st root of unity \(\xi_\alpha\) above \(\alpha\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q_p(z)\) by simple-root lifting.
With \(v_p(p)=1\), this gives \(v_p(z-\xi_\alpha)\ge 1/e\), allowing the value \(+\infty\).
Thus every pair of counted roots of residue \(\alpha\) has difference valuation at least \(1/\lfloor B/r\rfloor\), even when its two local fields differ.

Let \(n_\alpha\) be the number of roots in the group above \(\alpha\), and \(w_\alpha=1/\lfloor B/r\rfloor\).
All other differences have nonnegative valuation because \(F\) is monic and integral.
The squared Vandermonde formula and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz give
\[
  v_p(\operatorname{disc}F)
  \ \ge\sum_\alpha w_\alpha(n_\alpha^2-n_\alpha)
  \ \ge\frac{(\sum_\alpha n_\alpha)^2}{\sum_\alpha1/w_\alpha}
             -\sum_\alpha n_\alpha .
\]
The sum may run over all nonzero residues of degree at most \(B\), so its denominator is \(S_{p,B}\).
Using \(a_{p,r}\le p^r\) and expanding the floor gives
\[
 S_{p,B}\le\sum_{k=1}^B\sum_{r\le B/k}p^r
        \le 2p^B+2B p^{B/2}\le5p^B .
\]
Here \(B2^{-B/2}\le3/2\), for instance by checking \(B\le3\) and the decreasing ratio thereafter.
The archimedean [Vandermonde bound](Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#cluster) follows by bounding each row norm by \(\sqrt d\max(1,|z|)^{d-1}\).
This proves (1), and the nonzero integer \(\operatorname{disc}F\) gives the simultaneous estimate by prime factorization.
Substituting \(n_{2,B}\le d\) on the right of \(n_{2,B}^2\le5\cdot2^B(n_{2,B}+d\log_2d+(2d-2)\log_2 M(F))\) gives the upper bounds.
Conversely \(n_{2,B}\ge\delta d\) implies \((2d-2)\log_2 M(F)\ge \delta^2 d^2/(5\cdot2^B)-d-d\log_2d\), as claimed.

<a id="local"></a>
## A reciprocal family of points in a growing unramified field

Fix an odd integer \(B\ge3\), let \(Q=2^B\), and let \(K/\mathbb Q_2\) be the unramified extension of degree \(B\) with residue field \(\mathbb F_Q\).
Write \([b]\) for the Teichmüller representative of \(b\), including \([0]=0\).
On \(\mathbb F_Q^\times\setminus\{1\}\), every Frobenius orbit and its inverse orbit are disjoint.
Indeed, if a Frobenius power sends \(a\) to \(a^{-1}\), its square fixes \(a\); its order on the field is odd, so this power itself fixes \(a\), giving \(a^2=1\).

For each pair of inverse orbits choose one orbit.
For each \(a\) in that orbit and each \(b\in\mathbb F_Q\), include the two elements
\[
            x_{a,b}=[a](1+2[b]),\qquad 5x_{a,b}
\]
and the two inverses over \(a^{-1}\).
This set is stable both under Frobenius and inversion.
Above a chosen \(a\) there are \(2Q\) points, with \(Q\) different classes modulo \(4\) of size two.
In each pair the difference has valuation exactly two.
Different classes above the same \(a\) give difference valuation exactly one, because \([b]-[b']\) is a unit for \(b\ne b'\).
Inversion preserves these distances between units.
The \(d=2Q(Q-2)\) points therefore form the simple roots of a monic reciprocal \(P\in\mathbb Z_2[Z]\) with constant one.
For any root \(x\), exactly \(2Q-2\) others contribute valuation one and one contributes two, hence \(v_2(P'(x))=2Q\).
Put \(m=4Q+1\).
If an integral monic polynomial \(F\) of the same degree satisfies \(F\equiv P\bmod 2^m\), then \(v_2(F'(x))=2Q\) and \(v_2(F(x))\ge m>2v_2(F'(x))\).
Hensel's lemma in \(K\) yields a root within difference valuation at least \(m-2Q>2\) of each \(x\).
These \(d\) balls are disjoint.
Thus \(F\) splits in \(K\) with distinct roots, and all root degrees over \(\mathbb Q_2\) are at most \(B\).

## Integral Salem minima with the prescribed splitting and height

Let \(D=d/2\).
There is a monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(U\) of degree \(2D\) over \(\mathbb F_3\).
Indeed a primitive \((3^D+1)\)-st root of unity has a Frobenius orbit of length exactly \(2D\): this order of \(3\) modulo \(3^D+1\) divides \(2D\), and a proper divisor would be at most \(D\), whereas \(0<3^j-1<3^D+1\) for \(j\le D,\ j\ge1\).
The \(D\)-th Frobenius power gives inversion, so the minimum is reciprocal with constant one.
By the Chinese remainder theorem choose monic reciprocal \(F_0\in\mathbb Z[Z]\), constant one, representing \(P\bmod2^m\) and \(U\bmod3\); choose each independent nonleading coefficient with absolute value at most \(H=3\cdot2^m\).
Then \(\|F_0\|_1\le(d+1)H\).

Write \(F_0=Z^D H_0(Z+Z^{-1})\), where \(H_0\) is monic integral, and put \(\phi_j(Z+Z^{-1})=Z^j+Z^{-j}\).
This [Chebyshev trace change](Yin_C_G0901_v187.md#round) has integral coefficients in both directions, also for the variable translated by two.
Take \(A=(d+2)H\) and put
\[
     H_1(X)=H_0(X)-A\phi_{D-1}(X),\qquad F=Z^D H_1(Z+Z^{-1}).
\]
The two congruences persist, so \(F\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and splits in \(K\).
At \(X_j=2\cos(j\pi/(D-1))\) for \(0\le j\le D-1\), one has \(|H_0(X_j)|\le\|F_0\|_1\) and \(\phi_{D-1}(X_j)=2(-1)^j\).
Thus consecutive values of \(H_1\) have opposite signs, with \(H_1(2)<0\).
There is a root in each of the \(D-1\) intervening intervals and one real root above \(2\) by monicity.
They exhaust the degree and yield one exterior real root \(T>1\), its reciprocal, and \(D-1\) pairs on the circle.
Hence \(F\) is the minimum of a Salem number.
One has \(Q^2\le d<4Q^2\), so \(B=\lfloor\frac12\log_2d\rfloor\).
All \(d\) roots obey this cutoff, and the lower bound for \(\log T\) follows from (1).
For the upper bound \(T=M(F)\le\|F\|_1\le(d+1)H+2A\), so \(\log T=O(Q+\log d)=O(\sqrt d)\).
