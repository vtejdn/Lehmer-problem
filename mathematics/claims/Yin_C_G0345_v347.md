# Isolating the original factor in a prime-residue lift with cyclotomic padding

<a id="statement"></a>
## The surviving factor and its degree

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be a monic irreducible unit polynomial of degree \(D\), with gcd one among the differences of its occupied exponents.
Suppose \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\), where \(m=\log M\) and \(\theta>1\) solves \(z^3-z-1=0\).
Let \(C\) be any product of cyclotomic polynomials.
Suppose \(CP\) occupies \(k\ge2\) classes modulo an odd prime \(p\).
Remove the common row factor \(A(x^p)\) and form the centered lift of \(CP/A(x^p)\) as in [the row reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage) and [the prime lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift).
Thus, up to a monomial, its diagonal is \(F(x^p,x^b)\) with \(0<b<p\), and \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) has unit row gcd and fast width \(h\), the difference of its extreme \(Y\)-exponents.
Assume \(24h<p\), as holds eventually for the centered lifts at fixed \(k\) since \(h=O_k(p^{1-1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor})\).

Up to Laurent units, exactly one irreducible factor \(B\) of \(F\) has diagonal divisible by \(P\).
It occurs once and is reciprocal under simultaneous inversion of \(X,Y\).
Every other factor is a cyclotomic polynomial in a Laurent monomial.
In particular \(m(B)=m(F)\ge\eta_k\), where \(\eta_k>0\) is the [projected-support gap](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement).
Let \(s\) be the fast width of \(B\) and \(d\) the difference of the extreme exponents of \(B(x^p,x^b)\).
Then \(1\le s\le h\) and
\[
                    D\le d\le\frac{D}{1-24s/p}.                         \tag{1}
\]
If \(m(P)<\eta_k\), then
\[
                       D\ge \frac{p}{2s}-12.                            \tag{2}
\]
Consequently at fixed \(k\), every sufficiently large prime with such a representation in this smaller shell satisfies \(p=O_k((D+12)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor})\), independently of \(\deg C\).

For \(H=F\) or \(B\), define its fiber roots up to Laurent monomials by shifting its lowest \(Y\)-exponent to zero.
At each regular \(X\) on the unit circle, count the nonzero fiber roots with multiplicity.
With \(\mu\) normalized circle measure put
\[
 {\cal L}(H)=\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_{\substack{H(X,u)=0\\|u|=1}}
                         \log\frac{2}{|u^p-X^b|}\,d\mu(X).
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 {\cal L}(F)-{\cal L}(B)&=(h-s)\log2,\\
 m(P)&\ge
 \left(1-\frac{2(s-1)}p\right)m(B)
          +\frac{m(\operatorname{disc}_Y B_0)-2s\log s-{\cal L}(B)}p ,
 \end{split}                                                           \tag{3}
\]
where \(B_0\) denotes the shifted polynomial and its discriminant is nonzero.
The upper bound is \(m(P)\le m(B)+s\log2/p\).
Thus for such padded sequences at fixed \(k\) with \(m(P)\to0\) and \(p\to\infty\), one must have \(\liminf{\cal L}(B)/p\ge\eta_k\).
This locates the necessary neutral-root loss in the factor carrying \(P\) without bounding the degree of the initial cyclotomic multiple or the number of rows remaining after factorization.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## A torsion bound with the width measured before specialization

Let \(H\) be a primitive irreducible rational Laurent factor of \(F\), taken with integral coefficients, with fast width \(s_0>0\).
Write \(J(x)=H(x^p,x^b)\) up to a monomial and let \(d_0\) be its exponent span.
Because \(s_0\le h<p\), the exponents \(pi+bj\) are distinct for distinct support points \((i,j)\) of \(H\).
Thus \(d_0>0\) and each coefficient survives.
Assume \(H\) has no component over \(\mathbb C\) that is a subtorus translated by a torsion point.
We show that the number of roots of unity of \(J\), counted with multiplicity, is at most
\[
                              24d_0s_0/p.                               \tag{4}
\]

Introduce \(K(x,z)=H(x^p,x^b z)\) and multiply by a Laurent monomial to make its minimum exponents in both variables zero.
Its Newton polygon lies in the rectangle \([0,d_0]\times[0,s_0]\), and the coefficient of each occupied \(x\)-exponent is a single monomial in \(z\).
The map \((x,z)\mapsto(x^p,x^bz)\) on the complex torus is a finite surjective homomorphism.
It sends any one-dimensional translated subtorus to a translated subtorus of the same dimension and sends torsion points to torsion points.
Thus \(K\) has no torsion-translate component.
The [Beukers–Smyth bound in Section 4.1](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) gives at most \(22d_0s_0\) distinct torsion points of \(K\).
For each root of unity \(\xi\) of \(J\), all \(p\) points \((\omega\xi,\omega^{-b})\) with \(\omega^p=1\) are such points.
Their second coordinate determines \(\omega\) since \(p\nmid b\), and their first coordinate then determines \(\xi\).
This bounds the number of distinct torsion roots of \(J\) by \(22d_0s_0/p\).

We count excess multiplicity by adapting [the derivative-resultant argument for separated blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v162.md#cofactors).
The torus curve of \(H\) is geometrically reduced because \(H\) is irreducible over a characteristic-zero field.
Its pullback to the torus of \((x,z)\) is also reduced.
Indeed its coordinate algebra is obtained from the reduced algebra of \(H=0\) by adjoining \(x\) with \(x^p=X\), with \(z=Y/x^b\).
This monic extension is free over the base algebra, and embeds into the corresponding extensions of the product of the fraction fields of its components.
Each such extension is reduced by separability, as \(X\) is a unit.
The normalization of \(K\) by monomials introduces no factor \(x\) or \(z\).
There is also no nonconstant factor depending only on \(z\), since the nonzero coefficients in \(x\) are monomials and their common \(z\)-power has been removed.
Consequently \(K\) is squarefree of degree \(d_0\) in \(\mathbb C(z)[x]\), and \(R(z)=\operatorname{Res}_x(K,\partial_x K)\) is nonzero.
Its Sylvester determinant has \(2d_0-1\) rows, with entries of \(z\)-degree at most \(s_0\).
It follows that \(\deg R\le(2d_0-1)s_0\).

At every \(z=\omega^{-b}\) the polynomial \(K(x,z)\) is a nonzero scalar times the monomial-normalized \(J(\omega^{-1}x)\), with full degree and nonzero constant term.
Indeed \((\omega^{-1}x)^p=x^p\) and \((\omega^{-1}x)^b=x^b\omega^{-b}\).
The specialized Sylvester matrix therefore has corank \(e=\deg\gcd(J,J')\), taking \(J\) with its minimum exponent shifted to zero.
At each of the \(p\) distinct values of \(z\), \(R\) vanishes to order at least \(e\).
To see this, use invertible constant row operations to make \(e\) rows vanish there, then factor \(z-\omega^{-b}\) from each.
Thus the total excess multiplicity for all nonzero roots of \(J\), including the torsion ones, satisfies \(p e\le(2d_0-1)s_0\).
Together with the distinct-root count this proves (4).

## Removing the factors with cyclotomic diagonals

The [row-content argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage) shows that \(A\) is cyclotomic and \(A(x^p)\) divides \(C\).
In particular \(CP/A(x^p)\) is monic up to sign, has unit constant coefficient, and contains exactly one copy of \(P\) and only cyclotomics besides it.
It is reciprocal up to sign by [reciprocity below the plastic threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity).
The centered lift satisfies \(F(X,Y)=\pm X^a F(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\) for an integer \(a\), by the [injectivity on the common fast interval](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#reciprocal).
The row gcd excludes all nonunit base factors.
Gauss's lemma gives a product of primitive integral Laurent factors, with only a signed monomial left over.
Fast widths add, so each factor has width at most \(h\).
Each preserves all of its monomials under the diagonal map by the injectivity used above.
Its diagonal is thus nonconstant up to a monomial, and its extreme coefficients have absolute value one because their product over factors gives the extreme coefficients of \(CP/A(x^p)\).

Unique factorization on the diagonal singles out \(B\), with multiplicity one since \(P\) is simple.
Simultaneous inversion must preserve this factor up to a Laurent unit because \(P\) is reciprocal and hence divides the diagonal of the inverted factor as well.
A rational irreducible factor with a geometric torsion-translate component is, up to a Laurent unit, \(\Phi_m(X^uY^v)\) for a primitive character \((u,v)\), by the [rational torsion-factor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal).
Here \(v\ne0\) by row primitivity, and \(pu+bv\ne0\) by injectivity on the support.
Such a diagonal has only roots of unity, so \(B\) has no torsion-translate component.
For any other factor every diagonal root is torsion.
If this factor had no torsion-translate component, (4) would give \(d_0\le24d_0s_0/p<d_0\).
Hence every other factor is of the stated cyclotomic form and has independent Mahler measure zero.
Applying (4) to \(B\) bounds \(d-D\), precisely the degree of its diagonal cyclotomic cofactor, by \(24ds/p\), proving (1).

The [projected-support gap](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement) applies to the full \(F\) with its \(k\) occupied powers and unit row gcd.
Its zero-measure case would make every factor cyclotomic in a Laurent monomial, contrary to the diagonal of \(B\).
Therefore \(m(B)=m(F)\ge\eta_k\) even if \(B\) has more occupied rows than \(F\).

If the support of \(B\) is collinear, then \(B\) is a Laurent monomial times a one-variable polynomial evaluated at a primitive Laurent monomial.
That character restricts to a nonzero exponent on the diagonal by injectivity.
Independent and diagonal Mahler measures then coincide by power substitution, giving \(m(P)=m(B)\ge\eta_k\).
In the smaller shell of (2) the Newton polygon of \(B\) thus has area at least \(1/2\), since three noncollinear integer points span at least that area.
The linear map on exponent vectors \((i,j)\mapsto(pi+bj,j)\) has determinant \(p\), and the image polygon fits inside a rectangle of widths \(d,s\).
Hence \(ds\ge p/2\).
Combining with \(D\ge d(1-24s/p)\) proves (2).
Finally \(p/(2h)\le D+12\) and the centered width estimate give the asserted polynomial bound on \(p\).

## Neutral roots, including repeated roots on a padded lift

The \(Y\)-polynomial \(B_0\) is primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(X)\) by Gauss's lemma, and separable in characteristic zero.
Its reciprocal symmetry makes its fibers self-inversive on \(|X|=1\) away from the zeros of its extreme coefficients and discriminant.
More precisely, shifting the minimum \(Y\)-exponent to zero puts its simultaneous reversal in degree \(s\), and its scalar multiple on the circle has modulus one because the Laurent association is a signed monomial.
Thus the [normalized Vandermonde and cyclic grid argument](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#radial) applies directly to \(B_0\).
It uses only the paired radii, separability, the nonzero diagonal, and \(p\nmid b\).
The factor's own specialization has measure \(m(P)\), giving both inequalities of (3).
The discriminant is a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial, of nonnegative logarithmic measure.
All the grid logarithms are integrable by [the conditional-log argument](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#grid).

For each discarded factor \(V=\Phi_m(X^uY^v)\), all \(|v|\varphi(m)\) fiber roots are on the unit circle for every regular \(X\).
Its independent and diagonal measures are both zero.
The grid product consequently gives \({\cal L}(V)=|v|\varphi(m)\log2\) after integration, since the integrated sum of \(\log|u_0^p-X^b|\) over its fiber roots \(u_0\) is zero.
Adding with multiplicities gives the first identity of (3).
This remains valid with repeated cyclotomic factors even when the discriminant of the full lift vanishes identically.
As \(k\) is fixed, \(s\log s\le h\log h=o(p)\), so (3) gives the stated lower limit for the surviving neutral loss.
It leaves open the existence of such padded representations for arbitrary shrinking sequences and the estimation of the surviving loss.

For comparison, the Vandermonde estimate itself extends to a reciprocal integral Laurent lift \(H\) of width \(w<p\), with repeated factors and arbitrary row content, whenever it has a nonzero diagonal and \(p\nmid b\).
Shift the minimum \(Y\)-exponent to zero and separate the row content \(A_0(X)\).
In \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}][Y]\) group the primitive irreducible factors by their multiplicities, writing \(H=A_0\prod_{j\ge1}S_j^j\) up to a Laurent unit, using only nonconstant squarefree layers \(S_j\).
Simultaneous inversion preserves multiplicities, so each layer is reciprocal up to a signed Laurent monomial and is self-inversive on the circle.
Each \(\Delta_j=\operatorname{disc}_Y S_j\) is a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial, with the degree-one discriminant taken to be one.
Write \(w_j=\deg_Y S_j\), so \(\sum_j j w_j=w\).
Apply the grid and normalized determinant comparison to each layer and add with weights \(j\).
The content contributes exactly \(m(A_0)\) to both means and no fiber roots almost everywhere.
This gives
\[
 m(H(x^p,x^b))\ \ge\ m(H)+
 \frac{\sum_j j\{m(\Delta_j)-2(w_j-1)m(S_j)-2w_j\log w_j\}
                         -{\cal L}(H)}p .
\]
Here every \(m(S_j)\) and \(m(A_0)\) is nonnegative, as for all nonzero integral Laurent polynomials by successive applications of Jensen's formula.
Thus for \(w\ge1\) one may weaken the right side to \((1-2(w-1)/p)m(H)-(2w\log w+{\cal L}(H))/p\).
The upper bound is \(m(H(x^p,x^b))\le m(H)+w\log2/p\).
The narrow torsion estimate above identifies which layers actually carry independent measure when the diagonal contains just \(P\) and cyclotomic factors.
