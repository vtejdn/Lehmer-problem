## Statement

For a monic integer polynomial P of degree e>=1 with nonzero roots counted with full multiplicity, put F_q(t)=P(t^q), q>=1, and let P^[p] and F_q^[p] denote the complete powered-root polynomials, p>=2.

**A. Exact transfer and degree scale.** For all such p,q,

    |Res(F_q,F_q^[p])| = |Res(P,P^[p])|^q.

No coprimality or irreducibility is required.
When gcd(p,q)=1, one additionally has F_q^[p](t)=P^[p](t^q).
Nonzero resultants have log absolute resultant divided by eq equal to the base value divided by e.
Also M(F_q)=M(P).

**B. One-point limit.** For fixed P, the empirical root measures of F_q converge weakly to uniform circle probability as q tends to infinity, and every root modulus tends to one.
If the base resultant in A has absolute value greater than one, its normalized linear-scale energy remains strictly positive.
Along an irreducible subsequence the absolute height is m(P)/(eq); small absolute height and this one-point limit therefore do not imply log|Res(F_q,F_q^[p])|=o(eq).

**C. Two actual irreducible controls.** Let P_L=t^10+t^9-t^7-t^6-t^5-t^4-t^3+t+1 and Q=t^4-t^3-t^2-t+1.
For every prime q>40, P_L(t^q) and Q(t^q) are irreducible.
Their base absolute resultants at p=2,3 are respectively (2^10,3^10) and (144,1296).
Consequently their degree-normalized energies at p=2 are log2 and log2+(log3)/2, and at p=3 are log3 and log6.

Both families retain fixed positive unnormalized Mahler measure and exhibit a q-th-power degree drop.
They do not refute a theorem using m->0 or all-power degree preservation, or an unspecified K d m+E d bound.

#### Elementary radical transfer

Let P be a monic integer polynomial of degree e with nonzero roots alpha_i counted with multiplicity.
For q>=1 write F_q(t)=P(t^q).
For every integer p>=2, with full multiplicities,

    |Res(F_q,F_q^[p])|=|Res(P,P^[p])|^q.             (1)

Indeed, switching the two root indices changes at most the sign of the full product, so its absolute value is |product_beta F_q(beta^p)|.
Each root beta of F_q satisfies beta^q=alpha_i and F_q(beta^p)=P(alpha_i^p).
Each alpha_i occurs q times.
This proves (1) for all p,q, with no coprimality, irreducibility or power-degree assumption.
It is also a direct specialization of L59 equation (9), using Res(F_q(t),F_q(t^p)).
The initial coprimality guard in this working draft was unnecessary for the resultant identity.

For gcd(p,q)=1 one additionally has F_q^[p](t)=P^[p](t^q) by permutation of each radical fiber.
That polynomial identity needs its guard, even though (1) does not.
When the resultants are nonzero and d_q=eq,

    log|R_(1,p)(F_q)|/d_q=log|R_(1,p)(P)|/e.        (2)

Also M(F_q)=M(P).
If F_q is irreducible and beta^q=alpha, its absolute height is m(P)/(eq).

#### One-point limit and the unresolved pair scale

For each fixed nonzero alpha_i, its q-th roots have common radius |alpha_i|^(1/q), tending to one, and equally spaced angles.
The average of any bounded continuous test on these roots converges to its uniform-circle integral, by uniform continuity on a fixed compact annulus and ordinary Riemann sums.
Averaging over the e fibers proves the one-point weak convergence for F_q.
In fact every root approaches the unit circle in modulus.
No equidistribution source theorem is needed for this explicit family.

Nevertheless (2) preserves a nonzero linear-scale pair energy whenever the base resultant has absolute value greater than one.
Only division by d_q squared makes that energy tend to zero.
Thus a proof that discards the fiber correlations and retains only this one-point convergence or h_abs-to-zero cannot infer log|R|=o(d).
The logarithmic kernel log|z-w^p| is singular on its coincidence set; weak convergence of empirical product measures alone does not control this finer scale.

This is a proposed scope diagnostic, not an obstruction to a new estimate using unnormalized m(P)-to-zero, all-power degree preservation or finer pair arithmetic.
Here m(P) stays fixed and positive, and taking the q-th power lowers degree.
Such differences remain essential.

#### Exact actual controls

For the Lehmer polynomial P_L=t^10+t^9-t^7-t^6-t^5-t^4-t^3+t+1, use L159's established irreducibility of F_q=P_L(t^q) for every prime q>40.
Its measures remain fixed and positive.
The exact base values R_(1,2)=2^10 and R_(1,3)=3^10 agree with [C243](Yin_C_G0243_v1.md#reciprocal-trace-lehmer-control); they are independently formulated again in the finite computation below, not new base-control discoveries.

For Q=t^4-t^3-t^2-t+1, modulo two there are no linear factors and Q is not the square of the only irreducible quadratic t^2+t+1; thus it is irreducible.
The substitution y=t+t^(-1) gives Q=t^2(y^2-y-3).
Its two y-roots lie respectively in (2,5/2) and (-2,2), so Q is Salem with its positive exterior root between 1 and 2.
In particular 0<m(Q)<log2.
The same prime-binomial argument as L159 gives Q(t^q) irreducible for every prime q>40: if alpha=b^q in its degree-four field, then b is a unit of degree four and m(b)=m(Q)/q<log2/40, contrary to L80's accepted degree-four lower bound log2/16.
The retained proof of prime-binomial irreducibility then gives degree 4q.
This reuses that external degree-only input at its recorded statement scope; it is not a new source-proof audit.

For this quartic, the exact base values computed in J G0014 are |R_(1,2)|=144 and |R_(1,3)|=1296.
Hence the two irreducible radical families have identical limiting one-point circle distribution, while their degree-normalized full-resultant energies are

    p=2:  log2 for P_L lifts; log2+(log3)/2 for Q lifts,
    p=3:  log3 for P_L lifts; log6 for Q lifts.          (3)

Both sequences retain fixed positive unnormalized Mahler measure, and both have a q-th-power degree drop.
These are scope diagnostics for an argument using only absolute height or limiting root distribution, not counterexamples to a near-one or all-power-stable theorem.
In particular, (3) does not refute the prospective K d m + E d inequality with an unspecified K.
