# Eliminating rational exceptional slopes on the steep family

<a id="statement"></a>
## The remaining exceptional primes

Let \(M\le k<2M\), \(h=\lceil(\log M)^6\rceil\) and \(l=hk+1\), with the [two-pole certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#statement) \(U\) and its degree-56 exceptional form \(B\).
Write \(B_{\rm lin}\) for the product of the distinct rational linear factors of \(B\), using primitive integral representatives.
In particular this removes the four slopes defined by \(k\), \(l\), \(6l-7k\) and \(6l+7k\).
The [formula for \(B\)](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) exhibits the first two directly, and at \(Z=\pm2\) its polynomials \(E,G\) have a common root when \(6l=\pm7k\), respectively.
There is a constant \(C_F\ge1\) with the following property for all sufficiently large \(M\).
Outside \(O_F(M/\log M)\) directions, retain a bad prime \(p\mid B(k,l)\) only if
\[
 p>M,\qquad p\nmid B_{\rm lin}(k,l),\qquad
 \text{some multiple geometric residue root of }P_{k,l}\text{ has order }>C_F M.
\]
The discarded bad prime powers of \(U\) have total logarithm \(O_F(M\log M)\).
The same estimate holds for the actual complementary contacts at all discarded bad primes together with the contacts of order at most \(C_F M\) at retained bad primes.
These cutoffs can replace the bad-prime conditions of the [averaged certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#statement), with the same exceptional-set size, error and necessary lower bound for reducibility.

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Small orders and primes up to the interval length

Use the [geometric derivative weights](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#weights) that dominate bad prime powers and individual cross-factor contacts.
For \(p\le M^{1/20}\), all weights together are \(o(M)\) at each direction by the [discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#spacing).
At larger primes a disk of order \(g\) costs \(O_F(g\log p)\), with at most 32 roots in nonsingleton disks.
Since \(\log|B(k,l)|\ll_F\log M\), all disks of order at most \(C_F M\) at bad primes together cost \(O_F(M\log M)\) at each direction, for any fixed choice of \(C_F\).
At every remaining bad prime \(p\le M\) there are \(O_F(1)\) possible geometric pairs \((u,v)\) over the entire interval, by the [finite exceptional-slope fibers](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#weights).
Each pair determines \(x=v/u^h\), and when \(g>C_F M\) the equation \(x^k=u\) admits at most one \(k\) in the interval.
The Chebyshev upper bound for the number of primes up to \(M\) therefore excludes only \(O_F(M/\log M)\) directions with such an occurrence.

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## Removing all large primes on a rational exceptional slope

Fix a rational linear factor \(ak+bl\) of \(B\).
Its critical torus fiber is the finite set of points of \(F=D_{b,-a}F=0\) in characteristic zero, where \(D_{b,-a}=bU\partial_U-aV\partial_V\).
Finiteness follows from the [slope map of degree sixteen](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#map).
Choose a fixed finite Galois number field \(K\) containing the coordinates of those points.
Outside finitely many primes every geometric point of the reduced torus intersection is the reduction of one of these pairs, even if the original fiber has multiplicities.
Indeed in the Laurent coordinate ring over \(K\) the radical of the defining ideal is the intersection of the point ideals.
Choose generators of this radical, clear denominators in identities expressing powers of the generators in the defining ideal, and clear denominators in expressions of the products of the point ideals in the radical.
These inclusions give the asserted containment after reduction, with all coordinates units outside a fixed finite set.
This also covers an empty fiber by the unit ideal.
Exclude the ramified primes of \(K\) as well.

At a multiple residue of order \(g\) and a prime \(p>M\) dividing \(ak+bl\), lift the critical coordinates to one of these fixed pairs \((u,v)\) and choose a prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) realizing the reduction.
If \(u\) has finite order \(t\), then \(x^{kt}=1\) in the residue field, giving \(g\le 2tM\).
Choose \(C_F\) large enough for all such coordinates in the finitely many linear factors.
Suppose now that \(u\) has infinite order, and set \(m=(ak+bl)/p\).
For large \(M\) this is nonzero since \(ak+bl=(a+bh)k+b\), and \(|m|\ll_F h\).
Let \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\) be the arithmetic Frobenius at \(\mathfrak p\).
The identities \(x=v/u^h\) and \(x^{mp}=u^av^b\) in the residue field imply
\[
        (v/u^h)^m-\sigma^{-1}(u^av^b)\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak p}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(h\) this algebraic number is nonzero for every allowed nonzero \(m\) and every \(\sigma\).
Indeed the absolute logarithmic height of the first term is at least \(|m|(h\,h_{\rm abs}(u)-h_{\rm abs}(v))\), tending to infinity because \(h_{\rm abs}(u)>0\), while the second term has fixed height.
The difference has height \(O_F(1+|m|h)\) by the product and sum inequalities.
For any nonzero element of \(K\), the sum of \(\log p\) over rational primes below its positive valuations is at most its absolute logarithmic height times \([K:\mathbb Q]\), by applying the height formula to the inverse.
Summing over the fixed pairs, Frobenius elements and \(0<|m|\ll_F h\) thus bounds the total log of candidate primes by \(O_F(h^3)\) for the whole interval.
Every candidate \(p>M\) gives at most one \(k\) per linear factor since \((a+bh)k+b\equiv0\pmod p\) and \(0<|a+bh|<p\) eventually.
This excludes only \(O_F(h^3/\log M)\) further directions.
Together with the small-order bound this proves the assertions, including when the same prime divides more than one exceptional factor.
