# A Mahler gap when the binary period has a fixed set of prime divisors

<a id="statement"></a>
## All powers of any fixed finite set of primes

Fix a finite set \(\mathcal Q\) of primes at least five.
For every \(n>1\) supported on \(\mathcal Q\) and every \(0<l<n\), let
\[
                       P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1.
\]
There is a constant \(c_{\mathcal Q}>0\) such that every noncyclotomic monic irreducible rational factor \(S\) satisfies
\[
                  m(S)=\log M(S)\ge c_{\mathcal Q},\qquad
                  \deg S\ge c_{\mathcal Q} n.                          \tag{1}
\]
In particular this holds for every \(n=5^a7^b>1\), without parity or coprimality conditions on \(l\).
It gives a uniform gap for every positive-measure integral divisor.
An unrestricted shrinking sequence in this five-term family with \(n\) odd and prime to three would therefore have unbounded largest prime divisor of \(n\).
The argument uses actual Frobenius orbits and allows a proper selection at every represented order.

More generally fix an odd \(H\ge1\) and distinct odd primes \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\) outside \(H\), with \(r\ge1\).
Let \(B=H\prod q_i^{a_i}\) with nonnegative exponents, and let \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), without cyclotomic factors, such that \(\bar S\mid X^B-1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Put \(X_0=1+\log(B/d)\), which is at least one.
Write \(E(S)\) for the supremum of the nonnegative lower bounds from the [binary period inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement) over all periods and all doublings.
Then
\[
             m(S)\ge E(S)\ge \exp\{-C X_0^{\,1-1/r}\},                 \tag{2}
\]
where \(C\) depends only on \(H,\mathcal Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_r\}\).
If \(m(S)\le Kd/B\) for a fixed \(K>0\), (2) implies \(d\gg_{H,\mathcal Q,K}B\) and \(m(S)\gg_{H,\mathcal Q,K}1\).
For two varying prime powers the exponent in (2) is \(1/2\).
Its order is sharp for the period inequality on squarefree binary divisors, as the earlier [two-prime antichain](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#statement) shows.

<a id="box"></a>
## A common multiple capturing divisors of comparable size

The following estimate is purely numerical, and includes two among the allowable primes.
Fix \(H\ge1\), distinct primes \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\) coprime to \(H\), and \(K\ge1\).
Write \(B=H\prod q_i^{a_i}\).
On divisors \(s\mid B\) give nonnegative weights \(w_s\) with sum \(d>0\), where \(d\le K B\), \(w_s\le K s\), and every divisor carrying nonzero weight satisfies \(s\le Kd\).
Set \(X_0=1+\max(0,\log(B/d))\) and \(W=X_0^{1-1/r}\).
There is a nonempty collection of active divisors of total weight at least \(c d/W\) whose least common multiple \(J\) satisfies
\[
                              J\le C d\exp(CW).                         \tag{3}
\]
Constants in this argument may depend on \(K,H\) and the fixed primes.

To estimate the weight of small divisors, write \(v=B/s=v_0\prod q_i^{b_i}\) with \(v_0\mid H\).
For \(u\ge0\) the number of such products with \(u\le\log v<u+1\) is \(O((1+u)^{r-1})\), even if the upper bounds on the \(b_i\) are removed.
Indeed after fixing \(v_0\) and the first \(r-1\) exponents there are only \(O(1)\) choices for the last.
Summing the exponentially decreasing reciprocal weights in consecutive shells gives, for \(0<Y\le B\),
\[
          \sum_{s\mid B,\ s\le Y} w_s
               \le K B\sum_{v\mid B,\ v\ge B/Y} v^{-1}
               \ll Y(1+\log(B/Y))^{r-1}.
\]
Take \(Y=\eta d/X_0^{r-1}\) with sufficiently small fixed \(\eta>0\).
Then \(Y\le B\) and \(1+\log(B/Y)\ll_r X_0(1+\log(1/\eta))\).
Since \(\eta(1+\log(1/\eta))^{r-1}\to0\), at least \(d/2\) of the weight lies in \(Y<s\le Kd\).

For these divisors every \(b_i\log q_i\) belongs to \([0,C X_0]\).
Group them by \(v_0\), and partition each of their first \(r-1\) coordinates \(b_i\log q_i\) into intervals of length \(W\).
The number of groups is \(O((1+X_0/W)^{r-1})=O(W)\), including \(r=1\) when there are no such coordinates.
Within a group \(\log(B/s)\) has range at most \(\log(Kd/Y)=\log(K/\eta)+(r-1)\log X_0\).
The variation of the last coordinate is therefore \(O(W)\): for \(r=1\) this range is bounded, and for \(r\ge2\) one has \(\log X_0\ll_r W\).
Some group carries weight at least \(cd/W\).
To form its lcm take the minimum complement exponent in each coordinate, leaving the component of \(H\) fixed.
Starting from any \(s\) in this group, these changes multiply \(s\) by at most \(\exp(CW)\).
The assumption \(s\le Kd\) proves (3).

<a id="period"></a>
## Frobenius and the optimized binary period bound

The reduction of \(S\) is squarefree because \(B\) is odd.
For each represented order \(s\mid B\), let \(w_s\) be the number of its roots of exact order \(s\) in \(\bar S\), so \(w_s\le\varphi(s)\le s\).
A full Frobenius orbit has size \(\operatorname{ord}_s(2)\) for \(s>1\).
For each fixed \(q_i\), put \(c_i=v_{q_i}(2^{\operatorname{ord}_{q_i}(2)}-1)\).
If \(q_i^e\mid s\), then \(q_i^{\max(0,e-c_i)}\) divides \(\operatorname{ord}_s(2)\), by the binomial expansion at an odd prime.
The product of these powers divides the orbit size since they are powers of distinct primes.
Consequently \(s\le H(\prod q_i^{c_i})d\) for every represented order, also for \(s=1\).
Thus the hypotheses of (3) hold with constants independent of the exponents and of the selected orbits.

For the multiple \(J\) supplied by (3), \(r_J=\deg\gcd(\bar S,X^J-1)\ge c d/W\).
In the binary period inequality take \(i=\lceil2d/r_J\rceil\).
Its numerator before the factor \(\log2\) is \((i+1)r_J-d\ge d\), and \(i\ll W\).
Together with (3) this gives (2).
If \(m(S)\le Kd/B=K\exp(1-X_0)\), then \(X_0\le1+\log K+C X_0^{1-1/r}\), which bounds \(X_0\).
This proves the two uniform consequences.
For the cited antichain the full period is \(B=3n\), its degree is of order \(A\sqrt n\), and \(\log n\asymp A\).
Its supremum of the same numerical expressions is at most \(\exp(-c\sqrt A)\), so a smaller growth order than \(\sqrt{X_0}\) in (2) cannot hold for all such binary selections.

<a id="five"></a>
## Applying the comparison to every rational factor

For the stated \(P_l\), the [binary factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors) gives \(\bar P_l=(X^{3n}-1)/(X^n-1)\), independently of \(l\).
A noncyclotomic irreducible rational factor can be chosen monic integral; its constant coefficient is a unit.
Its reduction divides \(X^{3n}-1\), with \(H=3\) and with all varying prime powers from \(\mathcal Q\).
The [five-term gap estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) gives \(m(S)\le 2(\log7)d/n\) at every inner exponent, because every cut has a side of at most two terms and no such side vanishes at a nontorsion unit.
Thus \(m(S)\le6(\log7)d/B\), and (2) proves (1).
A positive-measure integral divisor of this monic parent contains at least one such factor.
For each fixed set of primes the number of noncyclotomic irreducible factors, counted with multiplicity, is consequently bounded as well.

This comparison resolves the mixed-order case left by the [one-prime-power gap](Yin_C_G0345_v239.md#statement) for the coefficients and middle exponents of (1).
Individual trace congruences can still distinguish which large factors occur.
A sequence of factors with measure tending to zero cannot occur anywhere in the fixed-prime family.
