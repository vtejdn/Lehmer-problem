# Critical six-term factors with multiplicatively dependent roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniqueness of the section up to a rational power

Fix positive constants \(C,K\) and write
\[
 F_{s,a,b}(X)=g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a),\quad g_s(T)=T^s-T-1,\quad B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s .
\]
A section and a root \(x\) satisfy the [critical hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) when \(a,b\) are relatively prime positive integers, \(a\ge2\), \(s\le b/a\le Cs^3\), and the irreducible nontorsion rational factor containing \(x\) has at most \(Ka\) exterior roots.
Consider a second such root \(x'\) with parameters \((s',a',b')\).
For sufficiently large \(\min(s,s')\), depending only on \(C,K\), if \(x'=\xi x\) for a root of unity \(\xi\), then \((s',a',b',\xi)=(s,a,b,1)\).
The same conclusion about parameters holds when \(x'=\xi/x\), in which case \(x'=1/x\).
Both exponent pairs can vary independently.

More generally allow arbitrary nonzero integers \(u,v\), varying with the two sections.
For sufficiently large \(\min(s,s')\), depending only on \(C,K\), suppose \((x')^u=\xi x^v\).
Then the parameter triples coincide, \(|u|=|v|\), \(\xi=1\), and \(x'=x\) if the signs agree or \(x'=1/x\) otherwise.
Thus two distinct critical sections cannot contribute the same factor or multiplicatively dependent roots modulo torsion.
Their separate existence remains open.

<a id="orthogonality"></a>
## Short sums of roots of unity on a small-height orbit

Let \(t\) range over nontorsion algebraic units, let \(q>0\) vary, and assume \(q h(t)=O(1)\), where \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic height.
Suppose the proportion of conjugates of \(t\) off the unit circle tends to zero.
Let \(A(T)\) be a Laurent polynomial formed from a bounded number \(N\) of monomials, each with a root of unity as coefficient, and assume that the difference of the largest and smallest exponents is \(o(q)\).
If \(A(t)=0\) throughout the sequence, then \(A=0\) as a Laurent polynomial eventually.

Average over all embeddings of a number field containing \(t\) and the coefficients, counted equally.
Write \(\langle z\rangle=z/|z|\) for a complex phase.
For two terms with different exponents the ratio of their phases at any embedding is the phase of the embedded unit \(\beta=\eta t^n\), where \(\eta\) is a root of unity and \(n\ne0\) is their exponent difference.
It is nontorsion and \(h(\beta)=|n|h(t)=o(1)\).
Its degree \(D\) tends to infinity, since among monic unit minima of bounded degree and bounded height the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds give only finitely many polynomials, and a nontorsion unit has positive height.
For the real and imaginary parts of phase times a fixed Lipschitz radial cutoff supported on \([1/2,2]\) and equal to one on \([3/4,4/3]\), the [compact-test discrepancy bound](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) at smoothing radius \(1/D\) tends to zero, and the circle mean is zero.
Removing the cutoff changes the mean by \(O(h(\beta))\), since the average absolute logarithmic radius is \(2h(\beta)\) for a unit.
Every conjugate occurs equally often in the field average.
Therefore every cross-exponent phase correlation is \(o(1)\), uniformly over these boundedly many pairs by passage to subsequences.

For an exponent \(i\) let \(c_i\) be the exact sum of its coefficients.
If \(c_i\ne0\), the field average of \(|\sigma c_i|^2\) is at least one by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and the nonzero integral norm.
Expanding the mean of \(\left|\sum_i(\sigma c_i)\langle\sigma t\rangle^i\right|^2\) now gives \(\sum_i\operatorname{avg}|\sigma c_i|^2+o(1)\).
But on each circle embedding that sum inside the squared modulus vanishes, and on the others its modulus is at most \(N\).
The mean tends to zero, proving the assertion.

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Elimination at the long exponent

It suffices to prove the rational-power statement by contradiction along a sequence with \(\min(s,s')\to\infty\).
Both factors are eventually reciprocal by the [critical scales](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#scales), so inversion of either root is allowed and we can take both exponents positive.
After extracting the common divisor of \(u,v\) into a torsion coefficient, take coprime positive \(u,v\) varying along the sequence with \((x')^u/x^v\) torsion.
Choose an algebraic unit \(t\) with \(t^u=x\), so \(x'=\omega t^v\) for a root of unity \(\omega\).
The proportion of embeddings off the circle is the same for \(t\) and \(x\), and tends to zero since \(2k/d=O(s^{-2})\) by the critical scales.
The [factorwise measure constant](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#statement) gives \(b h(x)\to\mu>0\) and \(b'h(x')\to\mu\).
Thus with \(q=ub\) one has \(q h(t)\to\mu\), \(vb'/q\to1\), \(usa/q\to0\) and \(vs'a'/q\to0\), using the critical slopes \(b/a\asymp s^3\) and \(b'/a'\asymp (s')^3\).

Put \(R_j=-g_j/B_j\), \(c=\omega^{a'}\), and \(e=\omega^{b'}\).
The denominators are nonzero at the section roots by the [graph calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#argument).
Dividing the two equations yields at \(T=t\) the relation
\[
 R_{s'}(cT^{va'})=e T^{vb'-ub} R_s(T^{ua}).
\]
After clearing denominators this is a Laurent polynomial with at most eighteen terms before collection, each a signed root of unity, and span at most \(usa+vs'a'+|vb'-ub|=o(q)\).
This span estimate uses only the ratios to \(q\), so it holds for unbounded \(u,v\) as well.
The preceding orbit argument promotes the displayed equality to an identity of rational functions.

At the origin both \(R_j\) have value one and linear coefficient one for \(j\ge3\).
The order and constant term force \(vb'=ub\) and \(e=1\).
Comparing the first nonconstant powers and their coefficients gives \(va'=ua\) and \(c=1\).
Thus the reduced fractions \(b'/a'=b/a\) give \((a',b')=(a,b)\) and \(u=v=1\) after reduction.
The [first distinguishing Taylor coefficient](Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#argument) gives \(s'=s\), and \(\omega^a=\omega^b=1\) gives \(\omega=1\).
Restoring the original signs and the common divisor gives the claimed root and torsion identities for the supplied \(u,v,\xi\).
