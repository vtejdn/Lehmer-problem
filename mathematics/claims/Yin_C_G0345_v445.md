# The dyadic precision needed to distinguish short rows with the same exterior faces

<a id="statement"></a>
## False positives at precision proportional to exponent times width

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit whose positive powers all generate its field.
Let \(P_1,P_2\) be the minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\), of common degree \(d\), with \(h_i=\log M(P_i)\), \(h_*=\min h_i\), \(S=h_1+h_2\), and \(F=P_1^2P_2^2\).
Assume that \(P_1\bmod 2\) is squarefree.
Fix a sign \(\varepsilon\), put \(n=2^k\), and prescribe \(b=b(n)\to\infty\) with \(b=o(n)\).
At formal width \(b\) put \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\), \(G_b(A)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n)\), and \(K_b=\{A\in\mathbb Z[Y]_{\le b}: F\mid G_b(A)\}\).
For integral precisions \(q=q(n)\ge1\) assume
\[
                       \limsup\frac{q}{nb}<\frac{h_*}{4d\log 2}.
\]
At every sufficiently large \(n\) there is an integral row \(B\) with the following properties.

* Its class modulo \(2^q\) occurs in \(K_b\bmod 2^q\), and \(\log\|B\|_1/n\to S\).
* The rows \(B,B_b^*\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), \(B(c^n)\ne0\), and \(G_b(B)(c)\ne0\) at every root \(c\) of \(F\).
* For some \(t_n\to\infty\), both end blocks of \(t_n\) coefficients agree exactly with those of a genuine row \(A\in K_b\) of slope \(S\) having \((A(0),A_b^*(0))\ne(0,0)\).
  Thus the row and its reversal have the same limiting exterior coefficient faces as this exact row, including both coordinates on the [consecutive maximizing intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v443.md#cost).

When \(S<(\log 2)/8\), these rows satisfy the strict budget \(((2nb+1)\|B\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) eventually.
For \(q=o(nb)\) there is also a choice with \(B(0)\ne0\) and the same maximizing indices of the paired coefficient envelope at every exterior radius.
The construction below specifies which individual endpoint coefficients this latter choice can change.

Conversely, any row whose class occurs in \(K_b\bmod 2^q\) and whose graph has neither target factor satisfies
\[
                  q\log 2\le \log(2\|B\|_1)+(nb+1)h_*/d .
\]
Thus \(nb\) is the order of precision at which such false positives of optimal slope can be excluded by size alone.
The constants between the two bounds remain undetermined.

<a id="small"></a>
## A short reciprocal polynomial in the dyadic remainder ideal

Write \(R=\mathbb Z[X]/(F)\), a free abelian group of rank \(D=4d\).
For \(l=b-2\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\), send each polynomial \(W\) of degree at most \(l\) with coefficients in \(\{0,\ldots,T\}\) to \(W(X^n)\bmod 2^{q+2k}R\).
Taking \(T=\lceil 2^{D(q+2k)/(l+1)}\rceil\) gives more polynomials than images.
Their differences therefore give a nonzero \(W\) in the kernel with \(\|W\|_1\le(l+1)T\).
Inversion of \(X\) induces an integral automorphism of \(R\), so \(W_l^*(X^n)\) is in the same ideal.
At least one of \(W+W_l^*,W-W_l^*\) is nonzero.
Strip its common formal endpoint zeros by dividing by \(Y^a\) and shortening the width by \(2a\), and divide out all factors \(Y-1,Y+1\).

These divisions retain membership after substitution in \(2^{q+2k}(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2)\).
Indeed the even-degree reciprocal polynomial \(\bar P_1\) cannot have 1 as a simple root, since its linear coefficient in the expansion at 1 vanishes whenever its value there does, by reciprocal symmetry in characteristic two.
The squarefree hypothesis gives \(\bar P_1(1)\ne0\), and \(\bar P_2=\bar P_1\) by the [squaring identity](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests).
Thus \(X^n\pm1\) are units in \(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2\), as their common reduction \((X-1)^n\) is coprime to \(\bar F\).
The remaining polynomial \(H\) has nonzero constant and top coefficients, \(H(1)H(-1)\ne0\), and \(H^*=H\) at its true degree \(m\le l\).
Indeed a sign of minus in its reversal identity would force \(H(1)=0\).
The coefficient bound for a divisor in terms of its Mahler measure gives
\[
         \log\|H\|_1\le \frac{D(q+2k)\log 2}{l+1}+O(b+\log(b+1)).
\]
Here every discarded integral factor has multiplicative measure at least one.
In particular \(\limsup \log\|H\|_1/n<h_*\).
The minimum of \(c^n\) has logarithmic measure \(nh_i\) for \(P_i(c)=0\), by the power-degree hypothesis.
Consequently \(H(c^n)\ne0\) at every target.

<a id="pencil"></a>
## Preserving both ends and removing common zeros

Use the [even-order construction](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#statement) at width \(v=\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\) to obtain a genuine exact signed row of slope \(S\).
Its separating-exponent hypothesis holds because distinct conjugates in either orbit have nontorsion quotient, and the two orbits have different absolute heights.
Remove all common initial monomials of the row and its reversal, shortening the formal width by twice the removed order.
From the resulting row and its reversal remove their entire common multiplicity at every root of \(H\).
Concretely take their primitive common divisor on those roots, using the gcd with \(H^{v+1}\) to restrict its support.
Its roots are stable under inversion with equal multiplicities, since inversion interchanges the two rows and preserves the roots of \(H\).
It is reciprocal with sign plus because it does not vanish at 1.
Shorten the width by its degree.
Division therefore preserves the signed graph divisibility and its genuine numerator values at every target, since the divisor has no zero at any \(c^n\).
The quotient has norm at most \(2^v\) times the original norm by the same coefficient bound.
Pad to width \(b\) by multiplying by the needed power of \(1+Y\).
This preserves the sign, introduces no common zero on \(H\), and costs at most \(2^b\) in norm.
The resulting exact row \(A\) still has slope \(S\) by the lower bound for genuine contacts, and at least one coefficient at its two formal endpoints is nonzero.

Take \(t=\lfloor(b-m)/2\rfloor\) and \(J=Y^tH\), so both \(J\) and \(J_b^*\) start with at least \(\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\) zero coefficients.
The signed graph perturbation is
\[
               G_b(J)=X^{nt}H(X^n)\bigl(1+\varepsilon X^{1+n(b-m-2t)}\bigr).
\]
It is nonzero at every target since \(H(c^n)\ne0\) and \(c\) is nontorsion.
Its remainder lies in \(2^{q+2k}(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2)\), so the [integral lattice lifting lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v274.md#lifting) gives \(J\bmod 2^q\in K_b\bmod 2^q\).
Here \(k\) eventually exceeds the fixed determinant bound in that lemma and \(b\ge2d-1\).

Choose \(B=A+uJ\) with a positive integer \(u\).
The polynomial \(AJ_b^*-A_b^*J\) is nonzero, since otherwise evaluation at \(c^n\) for any target would transfer the exact graph equation to \(J\).
Every common zero of \(B,B_b^*\) must be among its at most \(2b\) roots.
At a root where \(J,J_b^*\) do not both vanish, at most one \(u\) creates a common zero.
Their common zeros occur only at zero and roots of \(H\), where the preparation of \(A\) prevents a common zero for every \(u\).
At most \(2d\) further choices can make \(B(c^n)=0\) at the targets.
One can therefore take \(1\le u\le2b+2d+1\), and \(G_b(B)(c)=uG_b(J)(c)\ne0\).
The perturbation has strictly smaller norm exponent than \(A\), so \(B\) also has slope \(S\).
At every fixed distance from either formal endpoint its coefficients eventually equal those of \(A\).
The [finite truncation at any fixed exterior radius](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) therefore gives the same coefficient limits and faces: the common initial endpoint pair is nonzero and the tail of either row has bounded norm exponent.

If \(q=o(nb)\), the bound for \(H\) gives \(\log\|H\|_1=o(n)\).
When \(A(0)=0\) use \(J=H\) in the same pencil, so \(B(0)=uH(0)\ne0\).
The factorization above still holds with \(t=0\), and the top \(b-m\) coefficient positions remain unchanged.
The opposite endpoint has \(A_b^*(0)\ne0\).
For the joint envelope \(L(s)=\max_j(\gamma_j-js)\) of the endpoint pairs at \(s>0\), this ensures \(L(s)\ge0\).
Changing a coefficient at a positive index \(j\) by a quantity of slope at most zero can affect only terms strictly below \(L(s)\), unless its original slope is positive, in which case that slope is unchanged.
The index-zero pair retains its slope.
This proves the assertion on maximizing indices, while allowing the newly nonzero individual constant coordinate at index zero.
For \(A(0)\ne0\) the original interior perturbation already preserves it.

<a id="upper"></a>
## An upper bound from either missing factor

Lift \(B\bmod 2^q\) to an integral row in \(K_b\).
For each root \(c\) of \(P_i\) this gives \(G_b(B)(c)\in2^q\mathbb Z[c]\).
If \(P_i\) does not divide the graph, its resultant with the graph is a nonzero integer divisible by \(2^{dq}\).
At every conjugate, \(|G_b(B)(c)|\le2\|B\|_1\max(1,|c|)^{nb+1}\).
Taking the product gives the asserted precision bound for each missing factor.
