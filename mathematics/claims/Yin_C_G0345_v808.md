# Critical values and common divisors of distinct factors of a quadratic section

<a id="statement"></a>
## A local test at sixteen algebraic points

Let \(P(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) for coprime positive integers \(k,l\), and write \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\).
There is a fixed nonzero homogeneous polynomial \(B(K,L)\in\mathbb Z[K,L]\) with the following properties.
For \(B(k,l)\ne0\), the torus equations \(F=DF=0\) have sixteen distinct geometric points \(c=(u_c,v_c)\) over the rationals, each defined over a number field of degree at most sixteen.
For a prime \(p\nmid B(k,l)\) their reductions are distinct and lift uniquely from the residue algebraic closure to unramified extensions of \(\mathbb Q_p\).
Set \(w_c=v_c^k/u_c^l\).
Suppose \(P\) is squarefree, as holds along the [steep sequences](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
Then \(w_c\ne1\) for every critical point.

At a prime \(p\nmid B(k,l)\), consider each residue Frobenius orbit of critical points with \(w_c\equiv1\), let \(f\) be its size, and put \(b=v_p(w_c-1)\) with \(v_p(p)=1\).
The residue class over a chosen orbit point is a double root of the section, and these orbits account for all multiple roots modulo \(p\).
For every factorization \(P=ST\) into monic integral polynomials, a common residue class of \(S,T\) can occur only at an orbit with positive even \(b\).
If it does occur, its contribution to \(\mathbb Z_p[X]/(S,T)\) as an additive group is exactly \((\mathbb Z/p^{b/2}\mathbb Z)^f\).
There is a further quadratic residue test for splitting the two local roots given in the [local calculation](#local).
In particular a first-order critical congruence \(w_c\equiv1\pmod p\) with \(w_c\not\equiv1\pmod{p^2}\) contributes to the discriminant of the section and cannot join distinct rational factors.

<a id="critical"></a>
## The critical scheme of the slope

Write \(t=U-U^{-1}\), so the Laurent equation is \((t-3)(t-4)V^2+V+(t+3)(t+4)=0\).
On the torus this curve is smooth over \(\mathbb Q\): where the quadratic coefficient vanishes the linear coefficient is one, and where the quadratic has a double root its discriminant \(-4t^4+100t^2-575\) has a simple zero as a function of \(U\).
Indeed its two roots as a quadratic in \(t^2\) are distinct and neither equals \(0\) or \(-4\), the finite branch values of \(U\mapsto (U-U^{-1})^2\).
The same simple zeros and the unit middle coefficient give geometric irreducibility.
For \(kl\ne0\) in characteristic zero, the bihomogenizations of \(F\) and \(DF\) on \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) have no common boundary point.
At \(V=0,\infty\) the edge polynomials \(A^*,A\) have simple nonzero roots, where the logarithmic \(U\)-derivative of the edge does not vanish.
At \(U=0,\infty\) the edge polynomial is \(V^2+1\), with simple nonzero roots and nonvanishing logarithmic \(V\)-derivative.
Terms from differentiating a homogenization monomial are multiples of the edge polynomial and vanish at its zeros.
Nor is there a common component, since \(DF\) has the same bidegree bounds as \(F\) and its monomial weights are not constant.
Thus their intersection has length sixteen, by the intersection number of two divisors of bidegree \((4,2)\).

On the smooth torus curve the pair \((UF_U,VF_V)\) has no base point.
The map sending a point to the projective slope annihilating this pair has generic degree sixteen by the intersection count, and is generically separable in characteristic zero.
Consequently its generic fiber consists of sixteen distinct transverse intersections.
For precision about uniformity in the prime and slope, form the universal projective intersection of \(F=0\) with \((KU\partial_U+LV\partial_V)F=0\) over \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\), using the same bidegrees as above.
The images of its boundary locus and of its torus intersections with singular fiber Jacobian are contained in a proper closed subset of the base: take the projective closure of the Jacobian locus before projecting, and use properness and generic transversality.
After removing those images every intersection is in the torus, and the invertible two-variable Jacobian makes it étale over the base.
It is finite there by properness and quasi-finiteness.
Its degree is sixteen, either by the intersection number or by finite étaleness over this integral open base.
A nonzero homogeneous integer polynomial vanishes on the excluded closed subset, by taking a nonzero homogeneous equation for it; multiply this polynomial by \(2KL\) and call it \(B\).
This proves the asserted good reduction for every primitive direction, including uniqueness of the unramified lifts by Hensel's lemma.
The finitely many projective zeros of \(B\) in characteristic zero exclude only finitely many primitive positive directions.

<a id="local"></a>
## The double-root disk and its two branches

Choose integers \(a,h\) with \(ka+lh=1\) and use the integral torus coordinates \(x=U^aV^h\), \(y=V^k/U^l\).
Their inverse is \(U=x^ky^{-h}\), \(V=x^ly^a\).
The equation of the section is \(y=1\), the original parametrizing variable is \(x\), and \(D=x\partial_x\) in these coordinates.
Thus a repeated root of the section determines a unique critical point on \(y=1\), and conversely; all section roots are torus points because \(P\) has constant coefficient one.
This also excludes \(w_c=1\) when the section in characteristic zero is squarefree.

Fix a good prime and a residue point of degree \(f\) as in the statement, and let \(O\) be the unramified integer ring of degree \(f\) over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Write \((x_c,w_c)\) for the lifted critical coordinates, now in \(O^\times\), and \(G(x,y)=F(x^ky^{-h},x^ly^a)\).
At the critical point, \(G_x=0\) and the independent gradients of \(G,xG_x\) modulo \(p\) give \(G_y,G_{xx}\in O^\times\).
The formal implicit function theorem gives, in the disk with parameter \(t=x-x_c\),
\[
                 y=w_c+C t^2(1+t g(t)),\qquad
                 C=-G_{xx}/(2G_y)\in O^\times,\quad g(t)\in O[[t]],
\]
with derivatives evaluated at the lift.
Because \(p\ne2\), the series \(1+t g(t)\) has a square root with constant term one in \(O[[t]]\).
The resulting coordinate \(z=t\sqrt{1+t g(t)}\) is invertible on the formal disk.
There the section has equation \(z^2=(1-w_c)/C\).
It has multiplicity two in the residue fiber, and its two roots are individually defined over \(\operatorname{Frac}(O)\) precisely if \(b=2e\) and \((1-w_c)/(Cp^{2e})\) has square residue in \(O/p\).
This is the square criterion in a complete unramified ring of odd residue characteristic.
If it fails the two roots are conjugate over \(\operatorname{Frac}(O)\) and so cannot belong to distinct rational factors.
If it passes, the difference of the two \(z\)-coordinates has valuation \(e\), also the valuation of the difference of their \(x\)-coordinates because the inverse coordinate change has unit linear coefficient.

When the two roots belong to different factors \(S,T\), their common local quotient over \(O\) at this chosen residue point is \(O/p^e O\), by evaluation at one linear factor and then at the other.
At the corresponding closed point over \(\mathbb Z_p\), the lifted roots give two distinct degree-\(f\) factors, each generating \(O\): their residues have degree \(f\) because on the residue subtorus the coordinates are recovered from \(x\).
The \(f\) conjugates of each such root are distinct modulo \(p\), so all factors outside this closed point are units there, and only its matching conjugate in the other degree-\(f\) factor contributes valuation \(e\).
Evaluation therefore gives \(O/p^eO\) as the closed-point quotient over \(\mathbb Z_p\), with additive group as claimed.
Hensel factorization along distinct residue factors yields the direct sum over the shared closed points.

<a id="obstruction"></a>
## The square divisibility required by a small factor

For each good prime form
\[
       E_p=\sum_{c} f_c b_c/2,
\]
where the sum is over residue Frobenius orbits with positive even valuation and square unit in the local criterion.
Take one representative per orbit.
Then \(v_p\operatorname{Res}(S,T)\le E_p\), and every positive summand of the actual valuation is exactly the displayed contribution of some such orbit.
Only finitely many good primes give a nonzero sum, since the critical algebra has finitely many characteristic-zero points and their nonzero \(w_c-1\) have finitely many prime divisors.
For a factor of degree \(d=o(l)\) on the steep sequence this gives the necessary inequality
\[
 \frac12\sum_{p\mid B(k,l)}v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\log p
       +\sum_{p\nmid B(k,l)} E_p\log p
          \ \ge\ d\log\big((e^L-o(1))l/d\big),
\]
where \(L\) is the [mean derivative constant](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement).
Indeed \(\operatorname{Res}(S,T)^2\mid\operatorname{disc}P\), whereas the [individual derivative limit and Hadamard bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#resultant) give \(|\operatorname{Res}(S,T)|\ge((e^L-o(1))l/d)^d\).
Thus controlling the bad-prime discriminant part and the square congruences of these bounded-degree critical values would control the original factor obstruction.
The inequality supplies no general upper bound for either contribution.
