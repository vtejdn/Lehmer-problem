# Half of the short energy at a signed period and the spectrum that remains

<a id="statement"></a>
## Energy measured at a moving period

Use the hyperbolic monic unit polynomial \(P\), short integral numerator \(T\), \(H=T/P\), angular probability \(\mu\), and Haar probability \(\tau\) from the [moving Taylor word](Yin_C_G0345_v654.md#statement).
Assume \(m=\log M(P)\to0\), \(m\|H\|_2^2\to1/2\), and that the nonzero progression in that theorem occurs, with least positive index \(h\), sign \(\sigma\), and \(mh\to0\).
Equivalently here one can take any of the theorem's nonempty positive patterns and truncate its window at the first occupied index \(h\).
View the short energy on the circle under the map \(w=\sigma z^h\).
Every weak subsequential limit of the image of \(|H|^2d\tau/\|H\|_2^2\) has the form
\[
 \lambda=\tfrac12\delta_1+\tfrac12\eta,\qquad
 d\eta(w)=\left(1-\sum_{k\ge1}a_k\operatorname{Re}w^k\right)d\tau(w),
 \qquad a_k\ge0,\quad\sum_{k\ge1}a_k\le1.                           \tag{1}
\]
In particular exactly half of the limiting energy is the atom at \(1\).
The other half has a continuous density between zero and two before scaling.
All measures (1) pass the full collection of integer tests \(\int |B|^2d\lambda\ge1\) for \(0\ne B\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\).
This last assertion describes the limitations of those tests and does not assert realization by a sequence of polynomials.

<a id="atom"></a>
## Signed runs in the integer expansion

Choose \(N\) with \(N\to\infty,\ h/N\to0,\ mN\to0\), for example \(N=\lceil\sqrt{h/m}\rceil\).
Translate \(T\) so its least exponent is zero.
Use the first \(N\) Taylor positions \(u_i\) of \(H\) with coefficients \(q_i\).
The [column estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v654.md#coefficients) through \(G=h\) leaves at most \(2h\) indices at which the complete word fails.
Outside them \(u_{i+1}=u_i+h\), \(q_{i+1}=\sigma q_i\), and all first \(N+ h\) coefficients at occupied positions have modulus one.
For \(D_N=N^{-1}\sum_{i<N}q_i z^{-u_i}\), cancellation of consecutive terms except at breaks gives \(\|(1-w)D_N\|_\infty\le (4h+2)/N\), with \(\|D_N\|_\infty\le1\).
By the [weighted energy identity](Yin_C_G0345_v654.md#energy-law), writing \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\), we have \(\|HD_N\|_2^2/C\to1\).
On \(|w-1|\ge\epsilon\) the supremum of \(|D_N|\) tends to zero for each fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
Since \(\|H\|_2^2/C\to2\), the limiting probability puts mass at least one half on each closed arc neighborhood of \(1\), hence at \(1\).

Fix \(k\ge1\).
There exists a run of \(k\) consecutive good steps among the first \(N-1\), since the proportion of breaks tends to zero.
The normalized stripped polynomials \(S_i,S_{i+k}\) at its ends are distinct short integral polynomials with constant one.
Each stripped quotient differs from its signed translate of \(H\) by a prefix of norm at most \(\sqrt N\).
On the run the two translates have relative phase \(w^{\pm k}\).
The [minimum squared distance](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#packing) between the two short vectors is at least \(L\), where \(L/\|H\|_2^2\to1\).
As \(\sqrt N/\|H\|_2\to0\), it follows that \(\int|1-w^k|^2d\lambda\ge1\).
All these measures are conjugation symmetric, since the coefficients are real, so \(\widehat\lambda(k)\le1/2\) for each positive \(k\).

<a id="density"></a>
## The rest of the measure and its integer tests

Set \(\eta=2\lambda-\delta_1\).
It is a probability with real Fourier coefficients \(\widehat\eta(k)\le0\) for every positive \(k\).
Integrating the nonnegative Fejér kernel at \(1\) gives
\[
 0\le1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-k/n)\widehat\eta(k).
\]
Monotone convergence gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}-2\widehat\eta(k)\le1\).
The absolutely convergent Fourier series with \(a_k=-2\widehat\eta(k)\) is continuous and has the same Fourier coefficients as \(\eta\), which proves (1) by uniqueness.

Conversely the density in (1) is the mixture of Haar probability with weight \(1-\sum a_k\) and the densities \(1-\operatorname{Re}w^k\) with weights \(a_k\).
For an integral Laurent polynomial \(B\ne0\) the latter gives \(\tfrac12\|(1-X^k)B\|_2^2\ge1\) by Parseval, since the nonzero integral product vanishes at \(1\), so its squared coefficient norm is at least two.
If \(B(1)=0\), the product has a double zero at \(1\).
Squared coefficient norms two and three are impossible in that case.
Two unit coefficients summing to zero give only a simple zero, and three signed units cannot sum to zero.
Thus each constituent of \(\eta\) has integral at least two when \(B(1)=0\), including Haar by integrality of \(B\).
When \(B(1)\ne0\), \(|B(1)|^2\ge1\) and all constituents of \(\eta\) have integral at least one.
Equation (1) yields the asserted test for both cases.

<a id="balanced"></a>
## What the same atom requires of a balanced quotient

Suppose now that \(H=R/P\) is the short remainder in a balanced division \(F=X^{-j}A/P=Q+H\) at the [unrestricted limiting energy](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement), with constant \(K=5/2+\log2-2\sqrt2\).
Translations used to normalize \(R\) in the short argument preserve its energy measure.
For the images under the same map \(w=\sigma z^h\), take simultaneous weak limits \(\rho_H,\rho_Q,\rho_F\) of \(m|H|^2d\tau,\ m|Q|^2d\tau,\ m|F|^2d\tau\), respectively.
Put \(c=(1+\sqrt2)/2\).
There exists a positive measure \(\xi\) such that
\[
 \rho_Q=c^{-2}\rho_H+\xi,\qquad
 \rho_F=(1-1/c)^2\rho_H+\xi,\qquad \rho_H(\{1\})=\tfrac14.            \tag{2}
\]
In particular the mass of the balanced polynomial at the same signed period satisfies
\[
                  \frac{1}{4c^2}\ \le\ \rho_Q(\{1\})
                        \ \le\ \frac{\log2}{2}.                      \tag{3}
\]
Use the [projection reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v651.md#reconstruction), with \(D\) the orthogonal projection onto the short space after changing the boundary phase.
It gives \(\sqrt m\|H+cDQ\|_2\to0\).
For every fixed \(k\ge1\), [commutation across the finite Fourier block](Yin_C_G0345_v651.md#commutator) gives \(\sqrt m\|[X^{kh},D]Q\|_2\to0\).
The two components \((1-D)X^{kh}DQ\) and \(DX^{kh}(1-D)Q\) in that commutator are orthogonal, so each has norm \(o(1/\sqrt m)\).
They bound both cross inner products between \(DQ\) and \((1-D)Q\) after multiplication by \(w^k\).
The same conclusion at negative indices follows by conjugation of the interchanged inner products, and at zero it holds by orthogonality.
Taking \(\xi\) along a further subsequence as the weak limit of the image of \(m|(1-D)Q|^2d\tau\) proves (2) by density of trigonometric polynomials.
The atom of \(\rho_H\) follows from (1) and its total mass \(1/2\).

For each fixed \(k\ge1\), \((1-\sigma^kX^{kh})A\) remains indivisible by \(P\), since the binomial has only unit-circle roots.
The unrestricted estimate applied to this multiple gives \(\int |1-w^k|^2d\rho_F\ge K\), while \(\rho_F\) has total mass \(K\).
Thus \(\operatorname{Re}\widehat\rho_F(k)\le K/2\).
Averaging over \(1\le k\le n\) and then letting \(n\to\infty\) bounds \(\rho_F(\{1\})\le K/2\), by the pointwise Cesàro limit of powers on the circle and dominated convergence.
Now (2) and positivity yield \(\rho_Q(\{1\})\ge1/(4c^2)\) and \(\rho_Q(\{1\})\le K/2+(2/c-1)/4=(\log2)/2\).
The estimates require a nonzero period in the short word and do not exclude the zero pattern.
