# Simultaneous finite-prime splitting in an unrestricted Lehmer gap test

2026-11-24T21:07 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The restricted family and its Mahler infimum

Let \(\lambda\) be the infimum of the positive total logarithmic Mahler measures of nonzero integer polynomials.
Fix a finite set \(S\) of rational primes and positive integers \(f_p\) for \(p\in S\).
Optionally fix nonnegative integers \(a_p\) for these primes and a cyclotomic conductor \(D\ge1\).
Write \(w=e^{2\pi i/12}\).
Consider nontorsion algebraic units \(\beta\) with
\[
  L=\mathbb Q(\beta),\quad F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1}),\quad
  \sqrt3\in F,\quad L=F(i),\quad [L:F]=2,\quad N_{L/F}\beta=1 .
\]
Require \(\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_F[w]\), relative discriminant \(\mathcal O_F\), and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_D)\subseteq L\), \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_D)^+\subseteq F\), where the superscript denotes the maximal real subfield.
Impose the following at **every** prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(F\) above each \(p\in S\).

* The prime \(\mathfrak p\) splits in \(L/F\).
* Its residue degree \(f(\mathfrak p/p)\) is divisible by \(f_p\).
* If \(a_p\ge1\), its absolute ramification index \(e(\mathfrak p/p)\) is divisible by \(\varphi(p^{a_p})\).

Let \(\lambda_{\mathcal D}\) be the infimum of the positive total measures in this family, allowing \(+\infty\) for an empty family.
Choose
\[
    N=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(12,D,\ p^{3f_p}-1,\ p^{a_p}:\ p\in S\bigr),
    \qquad c=\log\theta_0,\quad \theta_0^3-\theta_0-1=0,\ \theta_0>1,
\]
where \(12,D\) are included also when \(S\) is empty.
Then
\[
       \frac{\min(c,\lambda_{\mathcal D})}{\varphi(N)}
                        \ \le\ \lambda\ \le\ \lambda_{\mathcal D}.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\lambda=0\) is equivalent to the existence of units in this family with positive measures tending to zero.
For the displayed \(N\) the construction even forces \(3f_p\mid f(\mathfrak p/p)\).
The conclusion permits finitely many prescribed cyclotomic subfields of \(L\) and real cyclotomic subfields of \(F\) by taking the least common multiple of their conductors in \(D\).
Via the Kronecker–Weber theorem this includes prescribed finite abelian extensions of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(L\) and prescribed totally real abelian extensions in \(F\).

More generally, when \(\lambda=0\), let \(S_j,D_j,f_{p,j},a_{p,j}\) be any sequence of such finite data and \(\epsilon_j>0\) any sequence tending to zero.
There exist \(\beta_j\) satisfying the full \(j\)-th conditions and \(0<m(\beta_j)<\epsilon_j\).
This allows \(S_j\) to increase to all rational primes and the prescribed residue degrees to grow.
The trace fields and generators may vary with \(j\).

<a id="generator"></a>
## A generator of the cyclotomic compositum with controlled total measure

Write \(m(\gamma)=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]h(\gamma)\), where \(h\) is absolute logarithmic height.
Start with a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) such that
\[
                              m(\alpha)<c/\varphi(N).                    \tag{2}
\]
These suffice to study \(\lambda\) below this threshold.
Indeed positive measure is additive over irreducible factors, a nonunit extreme coefficient of a primitive factor with nonzero roots costs at least \(\log2>c\), and the factors \(X\) and the cyclotomics have zero measure.
Thus every nonzero integer polynomial of positive measure below (2)'s threshold has a nontorsion unit factor of at most that measure.

By the [norm construction preserving the original measure](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) there is a unit \(\delta\) with \(m(\delta)=m(\alpha)\) such that \(K=\mathbb Q(\delta^k)\) is the same field for every \(k\ge1\), including \(k=1\).
Let \(\zeta_N=e^{2\pi i/N}\) and put \(\beta=\zeta_N\delta\).
The identity \(\beta^N=\delta^N\) gives \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and then \(\zeta_N=\beta/\delta\) gives
\[
        L=\mathbb Q(\beta)=K(\zeta_N),\qquad
        m(\beta)=[L:K]m(\alpha)\le\varphi(N)m(\alpha).                  \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\beta\) is a nontorsion unit since multiplying by a root of unity preserves absolute height and integrality of the number and its inverse.
The degree factor in (3) can exceed one.

Both \(\delta\) and \(\beta\) have positive measure less than \(c\), so [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) implies that their minimal polynomials are reciprocal.
The embedding \(\beta\mapsto\beta^{-1}\) is an automorphism \(\tau\) of \(L\) of order two since \(\beta^{-1}\) generates the same field and \(\beta\ne\beta^{-1}\).
It sends \(\delta^N\) to its inverse and hence preserves \(K=\mathbb Q(\delta^N)\).
On \(K\) the automorphism \(\delta\mapsto\delta^{-1}\) exists by reciprocity and agrees with \(\tau\) on the generator \(\delta^N\), so \(\tau(\delta)=\delta^{-1}\).
Consequently \(\tau(\zeta_N)=\zeta_N^{-1}\).
This identifies the action of \(\tau\) on the full cyclotomic field even when that field meets \(K\) nontrivially.

The involution fixes \(\beta+\beta^{-1}\), and \(\beta\) satisfies the corresponding quadratic over its trace field \(F\).
Thus \([L:F]=2\), \(F=L^\tau\), and \(N_{L/F}\beta=1\).
Its restriction to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\) is ordinary complex conjugation, proving the two required inclusions for conductor \(D\).

<a id="basis"></a>
## The integral basis at the finite primes

As \(12\mid N\), we have \(w\in L\), \(\tau w=w^{-1}\), \(w+w^{-1}=\sqrt3\) and \(w-w^{-1}=i\).
It follows that \(\sqrt3\in F\) and \(L=F(w)=F(i)\), since both \(w\) and \(i\) are moved by \(\tau\).
For each \(u\in\mathcal O_L\), the element \(y=(u-\tau u)/(w-w^{-1})\) is integral because the denominator is a unit, and is fixed by \(\tau\).
Then \(x=u-yw\) is also fixed and integral, giving \(x,y\in\mathcal O_F\).
This proves \(\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_F[w]\) with basis \(1,w\), whose discriminant \((w-w^{-1})^2=-1\) is a unit.
Equivalently, at each finite prime the residue algebra is the quotient by the separable quadratic \(X^2-\sqrt3 X+1\).
Thus \(L/F\) is unramified at every finite prime, including primes above two and three.
The [twelfth-root family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) uses the same integral basis without specifying the splitting set.

<a id="splitting"></a>
## Splitting and residue degrees at every prime above each prescribed prime

Fix \(p\in S\), put \(t=3f_p\ge3\), \(M=p^t-1\), and let \(\mathfrak P\) be any prime of \(L\) over \(p\).
Since \(M\mid N\), \(L\) contains the primitive root \(\zeta_M=\zeta_N^{N/M}\), on which \(\tau\) acts by inversion.
Its reduction at \(\mathfrak P\) has exact order \(M\).
Indeed \(p\nmid M\), and reduction of the factorization of \(X^M-1\) into all its linear factors in \(L\) has no repeated root in characteristic \(p\), so reduction is injective on these roots.

Suppose that \(\tau\) fixed \(\mathfrak P\).
It would induce an automorphism of the finite residue field over \(\mathbb F_p\), given by raising to \(p^b\) for some \(b\ge0\).
Its action on the reduction of \(\zeta_M\) would give \(p^b\equiv-1\pmod M\).
But \(p^t\equiv1\pmod M\), so one can replace \(b\) by \(0\le b<t\), and then \(p^b+1\le p^{t-1}+1<p^t-1=M\).
This contradiction shows \(\tau\mathfrak P\ne\mathfrak P\).
The quadratic Galois group has only two elements, so \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak P\cap\mathcal O_F\) splits and \(F_{\mathfrak p}=L_{\mathfrak P}\) via the inclusion.
The argument uses only the prime-to-\(p\) order \(M\) and therefore covers \(p\mid N\) as well.

The common residue field now contains an element of order \(p^t-1\), so \(p^t-1\mid p^{f(\mathfrak p/p)}-1\).
The order of \(p\bmod(p^t-1)\) is exactly \(t\), since \(0<p^a-1<p^t-1\) for \(0<a<t\).
Thus \(t=3f_p\mid f(\mathfrak p/p)\) for every prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) of \(F\).

<a id="ramification"></a>
## Absolute ramification indices and varying data

If \(a_p\ge1\), \(L\) also contains \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{p^{a_p}})\).
Its completion at \(p\) is totally ramified over \(\mathbb Q_p\) of degree \(\varphi(p^{a_p})\).
Indeed \(\Phi_{p^{a_p}}(1+X)\) is monic, reduces modulo \(p\) to \(X^{\varphi(p^{a_p})}\) by the prime-power geometric sum, and has constant term \(p\), so it is Eisenstein.
The completed cyclotomic field lies in \(L_{\mathfrak P}=F_{\mathfrak p}\).
Multiplicativity of ramification indices gives \(\varphi(p^{a_p})\mid e(\mathfrak p/p)\), as stated.
This controls the absolute ramification in the trace field while its quadratic extension splits at the same prime.

Each admissible \(\beta\) is an unrestricted input, giving the right side of (1).
If an unrestricted input has positive measure below \(c/\varphi(N)\), its selected unit factor and (3) produce an admissible \(\beta\) of measure at most \(\varphi(N)\) times the original measure.
All other positive inputs already cost at least \(c/\varphi(N)\).
Taking their infimum gives the remaining inequality of (1).
For varying data, choose the input separately below \(\min(c,\epsilon_j)/\varphi(N_j)\) and apply the construction.
Conversely, any sequence of positive measures tending to zero in any of the prescribed families forces \(\lambda=0\).
