# The first unequal coefficient of two monic polynomials and the relative discriminant

<a id="statement"></a>
## A relative discriminant bound

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), and let inversion on \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) induce a nonidentity automorphism \(\tau\) of a stable subfield \(F\).
Let \(q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(F\), put \(b=[K:F]\), and let \(j\) be the first unequal coefficient of \(q,q^\tau\) counted from their common leading coefficient.
Write \(r>0\) for the number of real embeddings of \(F\), \(s\) for the number of its nonreal embeddings counted separately, and \(\mathcal D\) for the absolute norm of the relative discriminant ideal of \(F/F^\tau\).
Then \(1\le j\le b\), and
\[
 \mathcal D\le [4\sinh(m/r)]^r
              \left(\frac{2b}{j}\right)^s e^{2m}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
 m\ \ge\ \frac r3\log\left(1+\frac{\mathcal D^{1/r}}2
                                      \left(\frac{j}{2b}\right)^{s/r}\right).
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for fixed \(0<\delta\le1\) and \(A\ge0\), all inputs with \(j\ge\delta b\) and \(s\le Ar\) satisfy \(m\ge(r/3)\log(1+(\delta/2)^A/2)\), uniformly in the relative degree.
This includes fixed mixed signatures with arbitrarily large \(b\).
In that range the earlier [coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v616.md#coefficient) has \(2b/\sqrt j\ge2\sqrt b\), leaving open vanishing measure in its torsion-constant case.
The estimate imposes no condition on the relative constant, but its application requires the actual first discrepancy \(j\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## The first nonzero moment of a finite sum

Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_N\) be complex numbers with nonzero complex weights \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\), and put \(S_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^N a_\ell z_\ell^k\).
Suppose \(S_0=\cdots=S_{j-1}=0\) and \(S_j\ne0\), where \(j\ge1\).
Then \(j<N\), and
\[
 |S_j|\le \left(\sum_{\ell=1}^N|a_\ell|\right)
               \max_{|I|=j}\prod_{\ell\in I}|z_\ell|.               \tag{3}
\]

Equip \(\mathbb C^N\) with its standard Hermitian norm and put \(T=\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_N)\), \(x_\ell=\sqrt{|a_\ell|}\), \(y_\ell=\overline{a_\ell}/x_\ell\).
Then \(S_k=y^*T^k x\) and \(\|x\|\|y\|=\sum_\ell|a_\ell|\).
The vectors \(x,Tx,\ldots,T^j x\) are independent.
Indeed, a dependence with last nonzero coefficient at index \(h\le j\), multiplied by \(y^*T^{j-h}\), would give that coefficient times \(S_j\) equal to zero.
In particular \(j<N\).

Write \(V=\operatorname{span}(x,Tx,\ldots,T^{j-1}x)\) and \(v=x\wedge Tx\wedge\cdots\wedge T^{j-1}x\ne0\).
Use the Euclidean volume norm on exterior powers, for which the coordinate wedges of an orthonormal basis are orthonormal.
The volume formula for the distance from a subspace gives
\[
 \|v\|\,\operatorname{dist}(T^j x,V)
 =\|x\wedge Tx\wedge\cdots\wedge T^j x\|
 =\|x\wedge(\bigwedge\nolimits^j T)v\|
 \le \|x\|\,\|\bigwedge\nolimits^j T\|\,\|v\|.
\]
The diagonal operator \(\bigwedge^j T\) has norm \(\max_{|I|=j}\prod_{\ell\in I}|z_\ell|\).
Since \(y\perp V\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|S_j|\le\|y\|\operatorname{dist}(T^j x,V)\), proving (3).

<a id="coefficient"></a>
## A sharp bound at an arbitrary complex embedding

Let \(f,g\) be monic complex polynomials of degree \(b\) and let \(w\ne0\) be their first coefficient difference, at index \(j\) from the top.
For the signed power sums \(S_k\) of the combined list of \(2b\) roots, use weight \(+1\) for each root of \(f\) and \(-1\) for each root of \(g\), with all multiplicities.
We have \(S_0=0\).
For \(U(z)=z^bf(1/z)\) and \(V(z)=z^bg(1/z)\), one has \(U/V=1+wz^j+O(z^{j+1})\).
Its logarithmic derivative equals \(-\sum_{k\ge1}S_k z^{k-1}\) as a germ at zero.
Thus \(S_k=0\) for \(k<j\) and \(S_j=-jw\).
Every product of \(j\) root moduli is at most the product of \(\max(1,|\lambda|)\) over the combined root list.
Writing \(m(f),m(g)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measures, (3) gives
\[
                    |w|\le \frac{2b}{j}e^{m(f)+m(g)}.              \tag{4}
\]
The factor \(2b/j\) is attained for each \(j\mid b\) by \(f=(X^j-1)^{b/j}\), \(g=(X^j+1)^{b/j}\), whose roots all have modulus one.

<a id="product"></a>
## Product over the real and nonreal embeddings

Write \(m_\sigma=m(q^\sigma)\).
The [relative order argument](Yin_C_G0345_v616.md#discriminant) gives \(\sum_\sigma m_\sigma=m\), shows that \(q\ne q^\tau\), and bounds the absolute norm of their first coefficient difference below by \(\mathcal D\).
Set \(\mu_\sigma=m_\sigma+m_{\sigma\tau}\).
At a real embedding the [reflected coefficient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v616.md#reflection) bounds the difference by \(4\sinh(\mu_\sigma/2)e^{\mu_\sigma}\).
At every nonreal embedding apply (4) to \(q^\sigma,q^{\sigma\tau}\), which retain the same first discrepancy index \(j\).
The map \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma\tau\) permutes both sets of embeddings, so \(\sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma=2m\) and \(\sum_{\sigma\ {\rm real}}\mu_\sigma/2\le m\).
At each real embedding \(\mu_\sigma>0\), as its coefficient difference is nonzero.
Concavity of \(\log\sinh x\) gives a product of the real hyperbolic sine factors at most \([4\sinh(m/r)]^r\).
The exponential factors give \(e^{2m}\), proving (1).

Taking \(r\)-th roots in (1) and putting \(y=m/r\) gives
\[
 \mathcal D^{1/r}(j/(2b))^{s/r}\le4\sinh(y)e^{2y}
       =2(e^{3y}-e^y)\le2(e^{3y}-1),
\]
which yields (2).
Using \(\mathcal D\ge1\) and \((j/(2b))^{s/r}\ge(\delta/2)^A\) proves the stated exclusion.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Ordered pairs of embeddings

Retain \(K,F,b,q,\tau\) and allow \(r=0\).
Let \(\Omega\) be a Galois orbit of ordered pairs of distinct embeddings of \(F\) in the splitting field of the minimum of \(\alpha\).
Put \(o=|\Omega|/[F:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(t>0\) count the pairs \((\sigma,\bar\sigma\circ\tau)\) in \(\Omega\), where the bar denotes composition with complex conjugation at the target.
Write \(j_\Omega\) for the common first discrepancy index between \(q^\sigma\) and \(q^\eta\) for \((\sigma,\eta)\in\Omega\).
Then
\[
 1\le[4\sinh(m/t)]^t
        \left(\frac{2b}{j_\Omega}\right)^{|\Omega|-t}e^{2om}.        \tag{5}
\]
Indeed the [ordered-pair argument](Yin_C_G0345_v616.md#orbit) gives the nonzero integer product, transitivity of the first discrepancy index, and the reflected bound at its \(t\) specified pairs.
Use (4) at every other pair.
The mean of \((m_\sigma+m_\eta)/2\) over the reflected pairs is at most \(m/t\), since each coordinate is distinct among them.
Each embedding occurs \(o\) times in each coordinate over the full orbit, giving (5).
In particular a sequence with \(j_\Omega\ge\delta b\) and \((|\Omega|-t)/t\le A\), for fixed \(0<\delta\le1\) and \(A\ge0\), cannot have \(m\to0\).
Indeed \(o/t\le A+1\), so the \(t\)-th root of the right side of (5) is at most \(4\sinh(m)(2/\delta)^A e^{2(A+1)m}\), which tends to zero.
