# The least possible rank of conjugate log moduli in a divisible group of units

<a id="statement"></a>
## A test on rational irreducible constituents

For an algebraic unit \(x\) define
\[
             r(x)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
                   \{\log|\sigma x|:\sigma:\mathbb Q(x)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\}.
\]
For reciprocal \(x\), the positive log moduli already span this space.
Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\), its complex splitting field \(L\), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), and ordinary complex conjugation \(c\in G\).
Let \(V\) be the rational vector space spanned by the multiplicative classes of all conjugates of \(w\) modulo torsion, inside \((\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}/\mu_\infty)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).
Write \(D(w)\) for the group of all units whose positive power is, up to torsion, a product of integer powers of those conjugates.
Then
\[
   \min_{x\in D(w),\ x\ \mathrm{nontorsion}}r(x)
     =\min_{U\ \mathrm{irreducible\ rational\ constituent\ of}\ V}
                       \dim_{\mathbb Q} U^c .                              \tag{1}
\]
The minimum is attained by an integral monomial in the original conjugates.
If \(V\) is irreducible, every nontorsion element of \(D(w)\) has the same rank \(r(w)\).

For a reciprocal unit \(x\), \(r(x)\) is at most the number of real exterior conjugates plus the number of conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior conjugates.
Thus a lower bound on (1) excludes every reciprocal witness with fewer exterior places from the entire divisible group.
In particular a minimum at least two excludes all real and complex Salem numbers.
Here the rank and both counts refer to the actual distinct conjugates of the chosen unit.

<a id="modules"></a>
## The absolute values at the chosen complex embedding

Use additive notation for multiplicative classes, and let \(V_x\) be the span of all conjugates of an element \(x\in D(w)\).
It is a submodule of \(V\): taking a positive power gives an integral monomial modulo torsion, and conjugation preserves this relation.
The action of any common normal closure factors on \(V\) through \(G\), so no choice of root in the divisible group or lift of \(c\) affects the module.
The \(c\)-fixed space is \((1+c)V_x\) since \(2\) is invertible in \(\mathbb Q\).
It is spanned by the classes of \((\sigma x)c(\sigma x)=|\sigma x|^2\), all positive real algebraic numbers.
A rational linear relation among their real logarithms, after clearing denominators, says that a product of their integral powers is one.
It is exactly the same as a relation among their multiplicative classes, since the only positive real root of unity is one.
Consequently \(\dim V_x^c=r(x)\), extending the [fixed-space calculation for a compressed reciprocal input](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#field) to every element of this group.

Every rational representation of the finite group \(G\) is completely reducible, by averaging projections.
For nonzero \(V_x\), a contained irreducible module \(U\) gives \(\dim V_x^c\ge\dim U^c\).
Conversely realize a constituent of least fixed dimension by an actual submodule \(U\subset V\).
Any nonzero vector of \(U\) spans it under \(G\); multiplying by a common positive denominator realizes that vector up to scaling by a monomial \(x\) in the original conjugates, with \(V_x=U\).
This proves (1), including attainment.
All constituent fixed dimensions are positive, since the realizing nontorsion monomial has a nonzero log modulus by Kronecker's theorem.
If \(x\) is reciprocal, interior conjugates contribute negatives of exterior logs, neutral conjugates contribute zero, and a nonreal exterior conjugate has the same modulus as its complex conjugate.
These observations give the exterior-place bound.

<a id="families"></a>
## Realizing arbitrarily large minimum ranks

For any \(n\ge5\) and \(1\le t<n\) there are reciprocal units \(w\) of degree \(2n\) for which every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) has \(r(x)=t\).
One can take a totally real trace field of degree \(n\) whose normal closure acts as \(S_n\) on its embeddings, with \(t\) real exterior traces and \(n-t\) traces in \((-2,2)\).

To construct the trace minimum \(Q\), fix three sufficiently large distinct rational primes and prescribe squarefree monic reductions with factor degrees \((n)\), \((n-1,1)\) and \((2,1,\ldots,1)\), respectively.
Finite fields admit irreducibles of each required degree, and the primes may be chosen larger than \(n\) to provide the linear factors.
The first reduction ensures irreducibility.
The Frobenius permutations of the other two give an \((n-1)\)-cycle and a transposition by the usual factorization-cycle correspondence at squarefree reduction.
A transitive group with an \((n-1)\)-cycle is doubly transitive, as the stabilizer of its fixed letter is transitive on the rest.
All transpositions are then conjugate under the group, yielding \(S_n\).

Choose distinct real \(u_1,\ldots,u_{n-t}\in(-2,2)\) and start from the real monic polynomial
\[
    Q_R(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-t}(X-u_j)\prod_{j=1}^{t}(X-jR).
\]
For each coefficient below the leading one choose the nearest integer in its required congruence class modulo the product of the three primes.
The error per coefficient is bounded independently of \(R\).
At fixed endpoints isolating each \(u_j\) strictly inside \((-2,2)\), the sign changes persist for large \(R\), since the values of \(Q_R\) there have size bounded below by a positive multiple of \(R^t\).
At endpoints \((j\pm1/4)R\) bounding the \(j\)-th exterior root, the values have size bounded below by a positive multiple of \(R^n\), whereas the perturbation is \(O(R^{n-1})\).
Hence all \(n\) roots are real with exactly the prescribed split.
Taking \(R\) to infinity gives polynomials with unbounded largest root.

For a root \(s\) take \(w\) solving \(w+w^{-1}=s\).
The element \(s^2-4\) has a negative conjugate in the totally real trace field, so it is not a square there, and \(w\) has the asserted degree.
In the rational signed permutation module on inverse pairs, the kernel of the permutation action acts diagonally by signs.
Because all traces are real, \(c\) belongs to that kernel and has sign \(+1\) at the \(t\) exterior labels and \(-1\) elsewhere.
The equivalence relation of equality of the kernel characters on two labels is invariant under the transitive \(S_n\)-action and cannot be universal by these two different signs.
Primitivity makes all classes singletons.
Character averaging over the kernel now projects to each individual rational coordinate, so any invariant subspace is a sum of coordinate lines; transitivity proves irreducibility, as in the [signed-action criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs).
Mapping each oriented basis vector to its actual root class surjects onto \(V\) and has nonzero image, hence is an isomorphism.
Its \(c\)-fixed dimension is \(t\), as required.

In particular for \(t>k\) these fields supply actual units whose divisible conjugate groups contain no member of the [class with at most \(k\) exterior conjugates](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#statement).
This construction prescribes rank, and supplies no sequence with measure tending to zero.
