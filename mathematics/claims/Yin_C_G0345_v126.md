# Integer-level Salem descent for powers and an obstruction at all rational neutral nodes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Complete Salem outputs at negative integer levels

Let \(\sigma>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2m\ge4\), and let \(S_n\) be the trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^n\), with exterior trace \(T_n=\sigma^n+\sigma^{-n}\).
There is a constant \(C=C(\sigma)>0\) such that for every sufficiently large \(n\) and every integer \(1\le h\le T_n/n^C\), the complete polynomial \(S_n+h\) is an irreducible Salem trace of degree \(m\).
Its exterior trace \(r_{n,h}\) satisfies \(2<r_{n,h}<T_n\) and
\[
       T_n-r_{n,h}=\frac{h}{T_n^{m-1}}\bigl(1+O_m(T_n^{-1})\bigr).
\]
For any prescribed nonnegative sequence \(H_n=o(T_n)\), the same conclusions hold simultaneously for all integers \(1\le h\le H_n\) on a set of exponents of natural density one.
Here the Salem degree stays fixed and the parent measures \(\sigma^n\) tend to infinity.

There are also Salem inputs of unbounded degree and bounded measure for which the level \(-1\) yields no smaller Salem factor, whereas the level \(-2\) gives an irreducible smaller Salem number of the full input degree.
More precisely, let \(N\ge3,\ p\ge1\) be integers, and define \(V_p(2\cos\theta)=\sin((p+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\).
For each nonzero integer \(|c|\le N-2\), the polynomial
\[
                 F_c(X)=(X-N)V_p(X)+c
\]
is an irreducible Salem trace of degree \(p+1\).
The exterior traces strictly decrease as \(c\) increases, and at fixed \(N,c\) tend to \(N\) as \(p\to\infty\).
For the parent \(F_{-1}\), the level \(-1\) produces the exterior trace \(N\) and only roots in \((-2,2)\) from \(V_p\).
The level \(-2\) produces \(F_1\).
When \(N\ge4\) and \(6\mid p+1\), **every** integer level \(b\) meeting \(F_{-1}\) at any rational node of \([-2,2]\) gives no Salem factor.
In particular \(N=4\) gives an infinite family in this last assertion.

The computation [on Boyd's 45 inputs](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0164/readme.md#boyd-descent-search) found a rational node at every successful level in that sample.
The examples here establish a different outcome for explicitly constructed inputs, including a successful level that avoids every rational neutral node.
The coordinator proposed testing the fixed level and the rational-node criterion, and the contributor supplied the power estimates and the Chebyshev families.

<a id="power-separation"></a>
## Separation from conjugates and torsion traces

Choose one neutral conjugate \(v_i\) from each inverse pair of \(\sigma\), \(1\le i\le m-1\), and set \(t(v)=v+v^{-1}\), \(x_{i,n}=t(v_i^n)\).
The [Salem power argument](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers) gives multiplicative independence of the \(v_i\) modulo torsion, degree preservation, and joint product-arcsine equidistribution of the \(x_{i,n}\).
Let \(E\) be the finite set of traces of roots of unity of trace degree at most \(m\), including \(-2,2\).
Finiteness follows from the [torsion trace degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#count).
Let \(d_n\) be the minimum of 1, all \(|x_{i,n}-x_{j,n}|\) with \(i<j\), and all \(|x_{i,n}-e|\) with \(e\in E\).
Then \(d_n>0\), and there is an exponent \(K=K(\sigma)\) with \(d_n\ge n^{-K}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Indeed for each fixed algebraic nontorsion \(\beta\) on the unit circle, the [reported logarithmic-form estimate](../audits/literature/Dim16/Yin_U001.md#dim16-u001) gives \(|\beta^l-1|\ge l^{-K_\beta}\) for sufficiently large \(l\).
To meet its bounded-height hypothesis, take a fixed positive integer \(a\ge\max(1,h_{\rm abs}(\beta))\) and a root \(\gamma^a=\beta\).
Then \(|\gamma|=1\), \(\gamma\) is nontorsion of degree at least two, and \(h_{\rm abs}(\gamma)=h_{\rm abs}(\beta)/a\le1\).
Apply the estimate to \(\gamma\) and the exponent \(al\ge2\), increasing \(K_\beta\) to absorb the constant.
On the circle one has \(|t(v)-t(w)|=|v-w|\,|v-w^{-1}|\).
For \(i\ne j\) the two factors for \(v_i^n,v_j^n\) are the distances from 1 of the \(n\)-th powers of \(v_i/v_j\) and \(v_iv_j\), both nontorsion.
If \(e=t(\xi)\) with \(\xi\) of order \(q\), both \(|v_i^n-\xi|\) and \(|v_i^n-\xi^{-1}|\) are at least \(q^{-1}|v_i^{nq}-1|\), by factoring the difference of \(q\)-th powers.
Only finitely many fixed bases and orders occur, which proves the bound on \(d_n\).

<a id="shifted-powers"></a>
## Root localization and irreducibility after the shift

At the two ends of each interval \([x_{i,n}-d_n/3,x_{i,n}+d_n/3]\), the values of \(S_n\) have opposite signs and absolute values at least \((T_n-2)(d_n/3)^{m-1}\).
These intervals are pairwise disjoint, lie inside \((-2,2)\), and miss \(E\).
Also \(S_n(2)\le-(T_n-2)d_n^{m-1}\).
Hence, whenever
\[
       0<h<(T_n-2)(d_n/3)^{m-1},
\]
adding \(h\) preserves the signs at the interval endpoints, and \(S_n(2)+h<0<S_n(T_n)+h\).
Degree gives one simple root in each indicated neutral interval and one in \((2,T_n)\).
If the integral polynomial \(S_n+h\) at an integer \(h\) were reducible, some positive-degree monic factor would have only neutral roots.
Its reciprocal lift would have all roots of modulus one, so [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) would put its trace roots in \(E\), which all the intervals avoid.
Thus \(S_n+h\) is irreducible.
The trace field is totally real and has a neutral embedding at which \(r_{n,h}^2-4\) is negative.
Consequently the quadratic \(z^2-r_{n,h}z+1\) doubles the trace degree, with one exterior real root and all other conjugates except its inverse on the unit circle.
Its Salem number is strictly smaller than \(\sigma^n\).
The separation bound proves the assertion for all sufficiently large exponents by taking \(C>(m-1)K\).

For the density assertion, given any \(\eta>0\) choose \(0<d<1\) so small that the product-arcsine probability of a coordinate within \(d\) of \(E\), or two coordinates within \(d\) of each other, is less than \(\eta\).
This follows because the finite union of the limiting singletons and diagonals has measure zero.
The boundary at fixed \(d\) also has measure zero, so joint equidistribution bounds the upper density of exponents with \(d_n<d\) by \(\eta\).
On the rest, \(H_n=o(T_n)\) eventually fits the displayed strict bound.
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) gives density one.
In either range the largest permitted \(h\) is \(o(T_n)\), whereas \(-S_n(T_n-1)\ge(T_n-3)^{m-1}\) for \(T_n>3\).
Thus \(T_n-1<r_{n,h}<T_n\) eventually, and \((T_n-r_{n,h})\prod_i(r_{n,h}-x_{i,n})=h\) gives the stated uniform displacement.

<a id="chebyshev-family"></a>
## Growing degree with one failed level and one full descent

The recurrence \(V_0=1,\ V_1=X,\ V_{j+1}=XV_j-V_{j-1}\) gives monic integer \(V_p\).
Put \(q=p+1\).
At the \(q\) points \(x_j=2\cos((j+1/2)\pi/q)\), \(0\le j<q\), the \(V_p(x_j)\) alternate in sign with absolute values at least one.
Since \(N-x_j>N-2\), every real \(|c|\le N-2\) preserves the alternating signs of \((x_j-N)V_p(x_j)\) after adding \(c\).
There is a real root between each pair of consecutive points.
Moreover \(F_c(2)=-(N-2)q+c<0\) and \(F_c(X)\to+\infty\) as \(X\to+\infty\).
Degree \(p+1\) now gives \(p\) simple neutral roots and a unique simple exterior trace \(T_c>2\).
If \(c_1<c_2\), then \(F_{c_2}(T_{c_1})>0\), so \(T_{c_2}<T_{c_1}\).
For \(c=0\) the exterior trace is exactly \(N\).
For fixed \(x>2\), writing \(x=2\cosh u\) gives \(V_p(x)=\sinh(qu)/\sinh u\to+\infty\).
Signs at \(N-\delta,N+\delta\) for each \(0<\delta<N-2\) show \(T_c\to N\) at fixed \(N,c\).

For an integer \(0<|c|\le N-2\), any proper factorization of \(F_c\) would give an algebraic integer \(t\) with every conjugate in \((-2,2)\) and \(V_p(t)=c/(N-t)\).
The nonzero algebraic integer \(V_p(t)\) would then have all conjugates of modulus strictly less than one, contradicting its nonzero integer norm.
This proves irreducibility, and the negative discriminant at a neutral embedding gives a genuine Salem number of degree \(2(p+1)\) as above.
At level \(-1\) of \(F_{-1}\) the trace polynomial is \((X-N)V_p\), whose other roots are \(2\cos(j\pi/q)\) for \(1\le j<q\).
The linear exterior factor gives a quadratic unit, and the neutral factors lift to roots of unity.
At level \(-2\) the whole trace polynomial is \(F_1\), with exterior trace strictly between \(2\) and \(T_{-1}\).

<a id="rational-nodes"></a>
## Simultaneous exclusion of the five rational neutral nodes

If \(F_{-1}(a)=b\in\mathbb Z\) at a rational \(a\in[-2,2]\), monicity gives \(a\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\).
When \(6\mid q\), the points \(-1,0,1\) are roots of \(V_p\) by their angles, hence all give the level \(-1\).
To handle the endpoints, write \(f(x)=(N-x)V_p(x)\), so \(F_{-1}=-f-1\).
Since \(p\) is odd, \(f(-2)=-(N+2)q\) and \(f(2)=(N-2)q\).
On \((-2,2)\) one has \(|V_p(x)|<q\) by the strict triangle inequality on the sum of \(q\) phases defining \(\sin(q\theta)/\sin\theta\).
This gives \(f(x)>f(-2)\) there.

For \(0\le x=2\cos\theta<2\), the bound \(|\sin(q\theta)|\le(q/2)|\sin(2\theta)|\) gives \(|V_p(x)|\le qx/2\).
For \(N\ge4\), the function \((N-x)x/2\) is strictly increasing on \([0,2]\), so \(f(x)<(N-2)q\).
For \(-2<x<0\), write \(x=-2\cos\theta\), \(0<\theta<\pi/2\).
If \(V_p(x)>0\), oddness gives \(\sin(q\theta)<0\), so \(\theta>\pi/q\).
Since \(q\ge6\), concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/6]\) yields \(q\sin(\pi/q)\ge3\), whence \(|V_p(x)|\le q/3\).
Now \((N+2)/3\le N-2\), so \(f(x)<(N-2)q\) also on this side.
In the remaining case \(V_p(x)\le0\) the strict upper bound holds directly.
Thus either endpoint level has no trace root in \((-2,2)\).
Every genuine Salem factor of a lifted trace polynomial requires a neutral trace strictly inside that interval, proving the simultaneous exclusion.
