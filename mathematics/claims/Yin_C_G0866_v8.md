## All-place normalization

All logarithms are natural unless a base is written.
For nonzero z of actual degree D_z and own field K_z, use the usual rational absolute values and their extensions to all places v, with local degrees n_v.
At a finite place over p, integer ord_v, ramification e_v and residue degree f_v give n_v=e_v f_v and log|z|_v=-(ord_v(z)/e_v)log p; a complex place has n_v=2 and the usual modulus.
Define

    h_abs(z)=(1/D_z)sum_v n_v log^+|z|_v,
    m(z)=D_z h_abs(z), M(z)=exp(m(z)),
    M_2(z)^2=D_z sum_v n_v(log|z|_v)^2.                 (N1)

Thus M(z) is the Mahler measure of its primitive irreducible polynomial, including the leading coefficient.
For a unit the finite-place terms vanish and M_2(z)^2=D_z sum_j log^2|z_j| over all actual complex embeddings, including both members of each nonreal pair.
The unit product formula gives sum_j |log|z_j||=2m(z).
All degree multipliers below are the actual own degrees, not ambient normal-closure degrees or normalized heights.

## Statement

### A. Reciprocal signatures and the actual endpoint field

Let alpha>1 be a genuine Salem number of degree d=2t>=4, put m=log alpha, and let beta be any nonzero algebraic number with M(beta)=alpha.
Let n=deg(beta), and apply the adopted v7 atom construction to its selected orientation beta_tilde in {beta,beta^(-1)}.
Write F for the common splitting field of beta and beta_tilde, Omega for the n conjugates of beta_tilde, Sigma for the d conjugates of alpha, and L_alpha for the splitting field of alpha.
Let Delta_+ and Delta_- be the positive and negative logarithmic-modulus subsets of Omega, and let Delta_a and membership signatures be as in that construction.
Then beta is a unit,

    |Delta_+|=|Delta_-|,
    Delta_(alpha^(-1))=Delta_-,
    Delta_a intersect Delta_(a^(-1))=empty for all a.    (S1)

The count equality does not assert that beta has a reciprocal minimal polynomial.

There is an atom endpoint gamma with actual degree b<=n, with

    gamma in Q(beta) intersect L_alpha,
    M(gamma)=alpha,
    M_2(gamma)<=M_2(beta),                              (S2)

whose membership signature S is an oriented choice of r distinct inverse pairs of Sigma, for some 1<=r<=t.
For G_alpha=Gal(L_alpha/Q),

    Stab_(G_alpha)(gamma)=Stab_(G_alpha)(S),
    b=|G_alpha.S|,
    Q(gamma) subset Q(u:u in S).                       (S3)

In fact Q(gamma) is exactly the field generated by the coefficients of product_(u in S)(T-u).
Thus gamma is the value of an input-dependent rational function with rational coefficients in these r selected original roots, with nonzero denominator at that tuple.
No fixed complexity or radical-monomial hypothesis is asserted.

### B. Exact radial support and both degree bounds

At an actual conjugate of gamma, the logarithmic modulus is positive when its signature contains alpha, negative when it contains alpha^(-1), and zero when it contains neither.
Consequently the positive and negative counts of gamma are each rb/d, and its off-circle count k_gamma satisfies

    k_gamma/b=2r/d.                                     (S4)

Whenever all r selected input images are neutral, the corresponding output has modulus one, verifying the exact varying-rational-function/neutral-orbit domain of C866 v3.
The actual endpoint degree satisfies

    b>=2^r t/r for r<t,
    b>=2^(t-1) for r=t,
    2^(r-1)<=b<=2^r binom(t,r)<=3^t.                    (S5)

These are actual signature-orbit bounds, not bounds on an ambient field substituted for b.

### C. A finite attaining class at a Salem value

Put H_t=max_(1<=s<=t) 2^s binom(t,s), so H_t<=min{3^t,binom(2t,t)}.
For fixed alpha let T_alpha be the class of all monic irreducible integer polynomials q of degree b<=H_t, constant coefficient plus or minus one, splitting in L_alpha, and satisfying M(q)=alpha.
It is finite and nonempty, with coefficient bounds |c_(b-j)|<=binom(b,j)alpha, and

    inf_(beta:M(beta)=alpha) M_2(beta)
      =min_(q in T_alpha) M_2(any root of q).             (S6)

At least one minimizer can be chosen to be a block endpoint with the signature and field properties above.
The exact transversal bound sharpens the corresponding arbitrary-subset count; the looser envelope 3^t need not improve the central-binomial number at every small degree.
The result does not produce a globally basal raw minimizer or a degree-at-most-d monotone reduction.

### D. Necessary degree costs for small raw norm

For every starting realization and the specified endpoint,

    M_2(beta)>=M_2(gamma)>=m sqrt(2d/r).                 (S7)

Hence, for every B>0, if M_2(beta)<=B then

    r>=2d m^2/B^2,
    n>=b>=2^(2d m^2/B^2-1).                            (S8)

If a fixed C>0 satisfies M_2(beta)<=C m, then

    r>=2d/C^2,
    n>=b>=2^(2d/C^2-1).                                (S9)

In particular C<2 is impossible.
For d>=exp(e), the fixed-B statement also gives the original-degree-only estimates

    r>=d/(2880000 B^2)(log log d/log d)^6,
    n>=2^[d/(2880000 B^2)(log log d/log d)^6-1].          (S10)

No further threshold depending on B is required for S10 beyond that numerical source domain.
These are necessary costs: they neither establish that a small-raw realization exists nor contradict the exponential field and endpoint degree still available in L_alpha.
They do not resolve the unrestricted G0001 total-Mahler objective.

## Argument

### Salem inverse-Mahler signatures and the degree cost of small raw norm

The question concerns every realization of an actual Salem Mahler value, rather than a prescribed Cayley formula or an assumed radical monomial.
It seeks a necessary inverse-fiber degree bound, not an automatic supply of a realization with small raw norm.

#### Exact adopted block input and notation

Let alpha>1 be a genuine Salem number of degree d=2t>=4, set m=log alpha, and let beta be any nonzero algebraic number with M(beta)=alpha.
Use the [adopted C866 v7 block descent](Yin_C_G0866_v7.md#decision-whole-independent-review-2026-11-05), including its [membership atoms](Yin_C_G0866_v7.md#membership-atoms), [actual relative norm](Yin_C_G0866_v7.md#relative-norm) and [same-measure/raw comparison](Yin_C_G0866_v7.md#mahler-and-raw-comparison).
It proves that beta is an algebraic integer unit.
Let beta_tilde be its selected orientation, beta or beta^(-1), and let F be its splitting field, G=Gal(F/Q), Omega its n=deg(beta) conjugates, and Sigma the d conjugates of alpha in F.
Write L_alpha for the splitting field of alpha; it is contained in F.
For xi in Omega put x_xi=log|xi| and define

    Delta_+={xi:x_xi>0}, p=|Delta_+|,
    Delta_-={xi:x_xi<0}, q=|Delta_-|.

The unit product formula and exact Mahler value give

    sum_(xi in Delta_+)x_xi=m,
    sum_(xi in Delta_-)x_xi=-m,
    p,q>=1.                                             (IF1)

For a in Sigma the adopted well-defined set Delta_a is g Delta_+ for any g taking alpha to a.
The membership signature of xi is S_xi={a in Sigma:xi in Delta_a}.
The atoms are the fibers of this signature map, have a common size e, and their distinct products are the actual b=n/e conjugates of the chosen endpoint gamma.
The fixed result gives

    gamma in Q(beta) intersect L_alpha,
    M(gamma)=alpha,
    M_2(gamma)<=M_2(beta),
    b=deg(gamma)<=n.                                    (IF2)

All quantities use their actual own degrees and full places; the finite terms vanish because the two numbers have been proved units.
No globally basal property or degree-at-most-d endpoint is assumed.

#### Reciprocity identifies the negative set exactly

Because alpha is reciprocal, choose g_0 in G with g_0(alpha)=alpha^(-1).
The product defining alpha has only a rational sign outside Delta_+, so

    sum_(xi in g_0 Delta_+)x_xi=-m.

Among all subsets of Omega, the smallest possible logarithmic sum is -m.
Equality requires containing every negative x_xi and no positive x_xi; zero terms may be included.
Since g_0 Delta_+ has size p, this shows p>=q.
Apply the same argument to beta_tilde^(-1), whose Mahler measure is again alpha and whose positive and negative counts are q and p.
It gives q>=p, so p=q.
The original equality can therefore include no extra zero terms, proving

    Delta_(alpha^(-1))=Delta_-.                         (IF3)

The conclusion p=q is equality of positive and negative logarithmic counts; it does not assert that the minimal polynomial of beta is reciprocal.

For any a=h(alpha) in Sigma, Galois equivariance and h(alpha^(-1))=a^(-1) give

    Delta_a=h Delta_+,
    Delta_(a^(-1))=h Delta_-,
    Delta_a intersect Delta_(a^(-1))=empty.              (IF4)

Consequently each membership signature contains at most one root from each inverse pair of Sigma.
If S is the signature of the atom defining gamma and r=|S|, the adopted incidence count nr=dp together with IF3 gives

    1<=r<=t=d/2.                                        (IF5)

Thus S is an oriented selection of r distinct original inverse pairs, not an arbitrary r-subset which might contain both endpoints of a pair.

#### Exact stabilizer and the rational-function domain

The stabilizer in G of the chosen atom is exactly the set stabilizer of its signature S.
The adopted distinctness of atom products identifies this with the stabilizer of gamma.
The subgroup Gal(F/L_alpha) fixes Sigma pointwise, and hence fixes S and gamma.
After passing to G_alpha=Gal(L_alpha/Q), one therefore retains the exact equality

    Stab_(G_alpha)(gamma)=Stab_(G_alpha)(S),
    b=|G_alpha.S|.                                      (IF6)

The pointwise stabilizer of every element of S is contained in its set stabilizer.
Galois correspondence gives

    Q(gamma) subset Q(u:u in S).                        (IF7)

More precisely, the field generated by the coefficients of product_(u in S)(T-u) is Q(gamma), since its stabilizer is the same set stabilizer in IF6.
In particular gamma=F_r(u_1,...,u_r) for some rational function F_r in Q(X_1,...,X_r), with a denominator nonzero at the selected tuple S={u_1,...,u_r}.
This rational function may depend on the entire original realization and input; no fixedness, coefficient bound or degree-of-expression assertion is made.
Field containment does not assert that gamma is a radical monomial in these roots.

#### Exact radial support of the endpoint

For an atom with signature S', membership of alpha means every root in that atom belongs to Delta_+, so its product has logarithmic modulus strictly positive.
Membership of alpha^(-1) means every root belongs to Delta_- by IF3, so the product has logarithmic modulus strictly negative.
If neither belongs to S', every root of the atom lies outside both Delta_+ and Delta_-, and each has logarithmic modulus zero.
Thus the product has modulus one exactly in the latter case.

For an actual g in G_alpha, the signature of g(gamma) is g(S).
If all selected input images g(u_i) are neutral, g(S) contains neither alpha nor alpha^(-1), so |g(gamma)|=1.
This is precisely the actual-orbit hypothesis of [C866 v3 Part A](Yin_C_G0866_v3.md#statement), now verified for the general block endpoint together with its rational-function and actual-unit hypotheses.

In fact the support count is exact.
Every selected root has probability 2/d of mapping to the real inverse pair, and these events are disjoint because S contains no inverse pair twice.
Writing k_gamma for the number of off-circle conjugates at gamma's actual degree b, restriction of G_alpha to Q(gamma) has constant fibers and gives

    k_gamma/b=2r/d.                                     (IF8)

Its positive and negative counts are each rb/d.
The original realization has the same radial proportion: IF3 and nr=dp give (p+q)/n=2r/d.
No root angles or signs have been averaged using a hypothetical larger Galois group.

#### Both exact orbit bounds

The actual inverse-pair kernel contains all even flips, from the products of the conjugate complex conjugations already retained in [C383](Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population).
For r<t, every sign choice on the selected r pairs extends to an even flip by using one unselected pair, giving 2^r different signatures on that support.
The orbit of its support covers all t inverse pairs by transitivity, and therefore has at least t/r different supports.
For r=t, the actual even flips give 2^(t-1) distinct signatures.
Using the exact output stabilizer in IF6 yields

    b>=2^r t/r for r<t,
    b>=2^(t-1) for r=t,
    b>=2^(r-1) in every case.                           (IF9)

No full symmetric top group, splitting of the Galois extension or full sign kernel is assumed.
The total number of oriented r-subsets of distinct inverse pairs is 2^r binom(t,r), so also

    b<=2^r binom(t,r)<=3^t.                             (IF10)

The last inequality follows by comparison with sum_(j=0)^t 2^j binom(t,j)=3^t.
The exact transversal count sharpens the arbitrary r-subset count; 3^t is only a convenient uniform exponential envelope and need not improve the central-binomial bound at every small degree.
The raw minimum for a fixed Salem alpha can be attained by such an endpoint: apply the monotone block construction to an attained minimizer from v7, which cannot lower the attained infimum strictly.
For an explicit finite class, put H_t=max_(1<=s<=t) 2^s binom(t,s), and let T_alpha consist of all monic irreducible integer polynomials q of degree b<=H_t, constant coefficient plus or minus one, splitting in L_alpha and satisfying M(q)=alpha.
The transversal count is at most binom(2t,s), so H_t<=min{3^t,binom(2t,t)}.
Every coefficient c_(b-j) obeys |c_(b-j)|<=binom(b,j)alpha, since every j-fold product of roots has modulus at most M(q)=alpha.
Thus T_alpha is finite.
Every original realization has a no-more-expensive endpoint represented in this class, and every root represented in this class is itself an allowed realization.
Consequently

    inf_(beta:M(beta)=alpha) M_2(beta)
      =min_(q in T_alpha) M_2(any root of q).            (IF10a)

At least one minimizer can be chosen to be an endpoint with the exact signature properties above.
No exact-logarithm comparison algorithm or globally basal designation is needed for this existence assertion.
Neither IF7 nor IF10 proves a degree-at-most-d monotone reduction or identifies the output as globally basal.

#### The exact same-measure cost is stronger than a generic arity bound

The unit endpoint satisfies sum_j |log|gamma_j||=2m because M(gamma)=alpha.
Cauchy--Schwarz on its k_gamma nonzero logarithms, with the actual degree b and IF8, gives

    M_2(beta)>=M_2(gamma)
      >=2m sqrt(b/k_gamma)=m sqrt(2d/r).                (IF11)

This is exactly the direct actual-support inequality in C866 v3, with the additional same-measure identity from v7 retained.
For any specified B>0 and realization with M_2(beta)<=B,

    r>=2d m^2/B^2,
    deg(beta)>=b>=2^(r-1)>=2^(2d m^2/B^2-1).            (IF12)

If a fixed C>0 satisfies the sought original-measure comparison M_2(beta)<=C m, the same inequality cancels the original m without a new height theorem:

    r>=2d/C^2,
    deg(beta)>=b>=2^(2d/C^2-1).                         (IF13)

In particular C<2 is impossible because r<=d/2; no realization with any C>=2 is supplied by this necessary bound.

For a bound depending only on B and the original degree, use exactly the [accepted C147 v5 numerical interface](Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#reuse-and-source-scope) on the original Salem alpha, at its degree d>=exp(e):

    m>=(1/2400)(log log d/log d)^3.

This is applied to alpha at its original degree, not to gamma at the possibly much larger output degree b.
Its accepted source-statement and original-proof limits are unchanged.
Combining with IF12 gives

    r>=d/(2880000 B^2)(log log d/log d)^6,
    deg(beta)>=2^[d/(2880000 B^2)
                      (log log d/log d)^6-1].           (IF14)

These inequalities hold on the stated actual-degree numerical-source domain, without an additional threshold depending on B.
For comparison, the already adopted [C866 v3 BA8](Yin_C_G0866_v3.md#p2-bounded-coupling-arity) also applies and gives, for fixed B and sufficiently large d,

    r>=[1/(1200sqrt(2)14^3 B)]^(2/7)d^(1/7).

Its separate d/r<exp(e) branch and actual output-degree domain remain at that fixed proof.
The stronger IF12--IF14 use the exact original measure of the endpoint instead of forgetting it before applying a height lower bound.

#### Consequence and continuing gap

Every bounded-raw realization of a Salem Mahler value with growing original degree must have the large degree in IF14, and every fixed-C original-measure realization must have at least the exponential degree in IF13.
The general block descent still places an equally good endpoint in the original splitting field, whose degree can itself be exponentially large; no contradiction follows from these two facts alone.
The argument supplies neither an automatically inexpensive inverse realization nor a universal upper bound M_2<=C m.
It also supplies no radical-monomial classification and no globally basal raw-minimizer identification.
The unrestricted G0001 total-Mahler objective remains unresolved.
