# The two summands at the primitive absolute mean \(4/\pi\)

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded conductor or two signed roots of the original order

Fix \(K\ge1\) and let \(\alpha\) be a sum of at most \(K\) signed \(n\)-th roots of unity, \(n\ge2\).
Write \(U_n(\alpha)\) for the [primitive absolute mean](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#statement).
Let \(b_n(\alpha)\) be the least divisor \(c\mid n\) such that \(\alpha=\zeta_n^a\beta\) with \(\beta\in\mathbb Z[\zeta_c]\), requiring \(a=0\) when \(\alpha\) is real.
There exist \(C_K<\infty\) and \(\delta_K>0\) such that
\[
 U_n(\alpha)\le 4/\pi+\delta_K,\quad b_n(\alpha)>C_K
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha=\eta+\theta,\qquad
                              \eta,\theta\in\{\pm\zeta_n^a:a\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
For a real \(\alpha\) one can take \(\theta=\eta^{-1}\).
In particular, for every sequence of these bounded-length sums with \(b_{n_i}(\alpha_i)\to\infty\) and \(\limsup U_{n_i}(\alpha_i)\le4/\pi\), the two-term conclusion holds for all sufficiently large indices.
The ratio \(\eta_i/\theta_i\) then has order tending to infinity, the least signed length is eventually two, and the means tend to \(4/\pi\).
For a real sum the relevant ratio is \(\eta_i^2\).
The [odd-prime examples](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#sharp) realize this limit from below.

<a id="equality"></a>
## Equality after averaging along a torus fiber

Use the [compact group limit for signed sums](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#limit) on any sequence with \(b_{n_i}(\alpha_i)\to\infty\) and \(\limsup U_{n_i}(\alpha_i)\le4/\pi\), passing first to a subsequence of real sums or of nonreal sums.
After further extraction, the eventual relation group is \(T\oplus\mathbb Z^s\) with \(T\) cyclic of fixed order \(c\mid n_i\).
Its map to \(\mathbb Z/n_i\mathbb Z\) is eventually injective on \(T\).
The primitive mean converges to the mean of \(|F(\tau,z)|\) over faithful characters \(\tau\) of \(T\) and the Haar torus, where \(F(\tau,z)=\sum_v h_v(\tau)z^v\).
The coefficients at the faithful characters are conjugate cyclotomic integers, so their nonzero support is common to these fibers.
The empty and single-frequency cases of the [bounded-conductor argument](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#endpoints) would give \(b_{n_i}(\alpha_i)\le c\) eventually, including the untwisted conclusion for real sums.

Choose a real linear functional \(\lambda\) injective on \(\mathbb Z^s\), for example one whose coordinates are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(v_-,v_+\) minimize and maximize it on the support, and put \(w=v_+-v_-\ne0\).
At a fixed \(\tau\), the conditional average of \(G(z)=z^{-v_-}F(\tau,z)\) on the fibers of \(z\mapsto z^w\) is \(B(z)=a+bz^w\), where \(a=h_{v_-}(\tau)\), \(b=h_{v_+}(\tau)\).
The [two-endpoint bound](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#endpoints) gives
\[
 \int |G|\ \ge\ \int |B|\ \ge\ (2/\pi)(|a|+|b|).
\]
For each extreme coefficient, the arithmetic mean of its absolute conjugates is at least their geometric mean, which is at least one by its nonzero integer norm.
The upper bound \(4/\pi\) on the total limiting integral forces equality throughout.
In particular, each of the two endpoint coefficients has all conjugates of modulus one, so both are roots of unity by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization).
The equality \(\int|G|=\int|B|\) holds at every faithful \(\tau\).
On \(B\ne0\) the nonnegative function \(|G|-\operatorname{Re}(G\overline B/|B|)\) has zero integral by the conditional-average identity, since \(B=0\) has Haar measure zero.
Continuity and full support of Haar measure give \(G(z)/B(z)\in[0,\infty)\) at every such point.

At \(z_0^w=-a/b\), choose \(u\in\mathbb R^s\) with \(u\cdot w=1\) and take \(z_j(t)=(z_0)_j e^{it u_j}\).
Along this path \(B(z(t))=a(1-e^{it})\), whose two one-sided limiting phases are opposite.
By continuity \(G(z_0)\) lies on both resulting closed rays and hence vanishes.
Write \(w=k w_0\) with \(w_0\) primitive and \(k>0\), and choose Laurent coordinates with first variable \(y_1=z^{w_0}\).
For every root \(\rho\) of \(a+b y_1^k\), \(G(\rho,y_2,\ldots,y_s)\) vanishes for all remaining circle coordinates.
Uniqueness of Laurent coefficients makes this specialization identically zero, including when there are no remaining coordinates.
The \(k\) roots are distinct and nonzero, so \(a+bz^w\) divides \(G\) in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(\mathbb C\).
The difference of the maximal and minimal \(\lambda\)-values adds under multiplication, since each extreme is unique.
The binomial already has the full width of \(G\), so its quotient has width zero and is a single monomial.
Both minimum frequencies are zero with the same coefficient \(a\), so \(G=B\).
This argument works for every faithful \(\tau\) and proves that all middle coefficients vanish.

<a id="orders"></a>
## Descent to the actual conductor and uniformity

Every root of unity in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_c)\) has order dividing \(\operatorname{lcm}(2,c)\).
Indeed a root of order \(e\) there gives \(\varphi(\operatorname{lcm}(e,c))=\varphi(c)\) by taking the compositum with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_e)\).
Euler's product allows only one new factor \(2\) when \(c\) is odd.
At the original substitution the restriction to \(T\) is faithful for large \(i\).
Evaluating the two surviving monomials therefore gives two signed \(n_i\)-th roots, including the signs from the endpoint coefficients.
For real samples the limiting \(F\) is real throughout each faithful torus fiber by the [conjugation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#endpoints).
Its two nonzero frequencies must be opposites and their coefficients must be conjugates.
Thus the two evaluated monomials are inverse roots and \(\alpha_i=\epsilon_i(\zeta_{n_i}^{a_i}+\zeta_{n_i}^{-a_i})\) with \(\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}\).

If the uniform assertion failed, for every \(j\ge1\) there would be a counterexample to the stated representation with \(b_n(\alpha)>j\) and \(U_n(\alpha)\le4/\pi+1/j\).
The preceding extraction produces just that representation at all sufficiently large indices of a subsequence, a contradiction.
For any such two-term representation with ratio of bounded order \(e\), extracting the first power of \(\zeta_n\) leaves a signed constant and a signed power of \(\zeta_n\) of order at most \(2e\) after removal of its sign.
Hence \(b_n(\alpha)\le2e\) in the nonreal case.
In the real representation \(\eta+\eta^{-1}\), use \(\eta=\pm\zeta_n^a\) and \(\operatorname{ord}(\zeta_n^a)\le2\operatorname{ord}(\eta^2)\), giving the same bound without a twist.
A sum of two roots with ratio of order tending to infinity eventually has modulus different from zero and one, since those moduli require a ratio of order two or three, respectively.
This proves the least-length assertion.
The primitive substitutions on a signed power restrict uniformly to the embeddings of the field of its actual order.
Hence \(|\eta+\theta|=|1+\eta/\theta|\) has primitive mean converging to the circle mean \(4/\pi\) when the ratio order tends to infinity, by the [primitive-character limit](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#limit).

<a id="parity"></a>
## Which orders approach the endpoint from below

For a two-term value put \(M=\operatorname{ord}(\eta/\theta)\), so \(M=\operatorname{ord}(\eta^2)\) in the real representation.
The mean \(V(M)\) of \(|1+\zeta_M^u|\) for uniform \(u\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times\) is its exact primitive mean.
It never equals \(4/\pi\), and
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}\bigl(V(M)-4/\pi\bigr)=
 \begin{cases}
 (-1)^{\omega(M)},&4\nmid M,\\
 (-1)^{\omega(M)+1},&4\mid M.
 \end{cases}
\]
Indeed the full \(j\)-grid sum \(H(j)=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}2|\cos(\pi k/j)|\) is \(2\csc(\pi/(2j))\) when \(j\) is odd and \(2\cot(\pi/(2j))\) when \(j\) is even, by summing the geometric series symmetrically about zero (the endpoints for even \(j\) contribute zero).
Möbius inversion gives \(\varphi(M)V(M)=\sum_{j\mid M}\mu(M/j)H(j)\), whose linear contribution from \(4j/\pi\) is \(4\varphi(M)/\pi\).
The [positive coefficients of \(1-x\cot x\)](Yin_C_G0345_v93.md#cotangent) give strictly negative coefficients of each inverse odd power of \(j\) in \(2\cot(\pi/(2j))-4j/\pi\).
The identity \(\csc x=\cot(x/2)-\cot x\) gives strictly positive coefficients of these powers in \(2\csc(\pi/(2j))-4j/\pi\).
The series converge even at \(j=1\).
For each \(u>0\), the sum \(\sum_{j\mid M}\mu(M/j)j^{-u}=M^{-u}\prod_{p\mid M}(1-p^u)\) has sign \((-1)^{\omega(M)}\).
This proves the formula when \(M\) is odd, and when \(4\mid M\) every contributing grid is even.
For \(M=2m\) with \(m\) odd, pair the grids \(j,2j\) for \(j\mid m\).
Their difference \(H(2j)-H(j)=2\cot(\pi/(2j))\) has negative inverse-power coefficients, yielding \((-1)^{1+\omega(m)}=(-1)^{\omega(M)}\).
Thus the hypothesis \(U_n(\alpha)\le4/\pi\) on the two-term branch selects exactly the negative signs in the formula.

<a id="wrapping"></a>
## Reciprocal interpolation when the two exponents lie outside the degree

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero and reciprocal of even degree \(d=2m\le\varphi(n)+H\), with \(H\ge0\), \(n\ge3\).
Assume the centered value \(\zeta_n^{-m}P(\zeta_n)\) is \(\epsilon(\zeta_n^a+\zeta_n^{-a})\).
Choose \(0\le a\le n/2\), with \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
When \(n\) is even, replacing \(a\) by \(n/2-a\) and changing \(\epsilon\) permits \(a\le n/4\).
For \(a\le m\), the difference \(P-\epsilon(X^{m-a}+X^{m+a})\) is divisible by \(\Phi_n\) with zero quotient or a quotient of degree at most \(H\).
For \(a>m\) set \(k=a-m\).
The polynomial \(1+X^{2a}-\epsilon X^kP\) is monic reciprocal of exact degree \(2a\) and vanishes at \(\zeta_n\).
Therefore \(N=2a-\varphi(n)=2k+d-\varphi(n)\ge0\) and
\[
 Q=\frac{1+X^{2a}-\epsilon X^kP}{\Phi_n}
    \quad\text{is monic reciprocal of degree }N,\qquad
 Q\equiv\frac1{\Phi_n}\pmod {X^k}.
\]
For \(\ell=\min(k,\lfloor(N+1)/2\rfloor)\) put \(T=[1/\Phi_n]_{<\ell}\), the terms of degree less than \(\ell\) in the power series at zero.
Then
\[
 Q=T(X)+X^N T(1/X)+X^\ell W(X).
\]
If \(N-2\ell=-1\), take \(W=0\).
Otherwise \(W\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is reciprocal of formal degree \(N-2\ell\le\max(0,d-\varphi(n))\le H\), meaning \(W(X)=X^{N-2\ell}W(1/X)\).
The formula follows by reading the low \(\ell\) coefficients and reflecting them to the high end, with disjoint ranges.
For \(k>\ell\) the remaining low congruences still require \(Q\equiv1/\Phi_n\bmod X^k\), including any overlap with the reflected end.
Conversely any monic reciprocal \(Q\) of this form and degree \(N\) satisfying the full congruence yields the integer polynomial
\[
        P=\epsilon X^{-k}(1+X^{2a}-\Phi_n Q).
\]
It is reciprocal of formal degree \(d\), and has actual degree \(d\) exactly when \(P(0)\ne0\).
Since \(\ell<n\), the prefix \(T\) can equally be read from the polynomial \((1-X^n)/\Phi_n(X)\).

For fixed \(\omega(n)\le r\), bounded \(U_n(P)\) gives bounded signed length by the [first-mean length bound](Yin_C_G0345_v87.md#statement).
Along a sequence with \(U_n(P)\le4/\pi\) and \(1<M(P)\to1\), the bounded-conductor alternative is excluded by the [bounded-field interpolation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v88.md#lift) and the [fractional-margin theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v86.md#statement).
Thus \(b_n(\zeta_n^{-m}P(\zeta_n))\to\infty\) and the real two-term description holds eventually.
If \(a\le m\), or if \(a>m\) with \(k\) bounded, the quotient by \(\Phi_n\) above has bounded degree, using \(N\le2k+H\) in the latter case.
Multiplication by the denominator binomials in the Möbius product for \(\Phi_n\) then gives a bounded-term integer polynomial of the same measure, also allowing the monomial \(X^k\).
The [bounded-term gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes both cases on any such subsequence.
The remaining possibility has \(a-m\to\infty\) with the prefix and reflection specified above.
