# Quantitative cost of a mixed monomial in arbitrarily many large strict degree records

<a id="statement"></a>
## An estimate in the largest degree actually used

Fix \(M>0\).
Take a collection of reciprocal strict degree records of distinct degrees and positive total logarithmic Mahler measures at most \(M\).
A strict record first attains the running minimum of positive integer-polynomial measures at its own degree.
Take the degrees sufficiently large in terms of \(M\).
For any nontorsion \(x\) in the divisible group of the conjugates of this collection, its class modulo torsion uses a nonempty finite set of the irreducible record modules, by the [direct-sum description](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#families).
Let \(a\) be the record of largest degree \(d\) actually used, and write \(m(y)=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]h(y)\).
If \(x\) is not a torsion twist of a rational power of one conjugate of \(a\), then for an absolute \(c_0>0\)
\[
                    m(x)m(a)\ \ge\ c_0\,\frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{212}}.       \tag{1}
\]
This covers every mixture using at least two records and every noncoordinate direction inside one record module.
There is no bound on the number of records, the original degree of \(x\), or the measure of \(x\).
On any single coordinate line the positive measures are exactly the positive integer multiples of the record measure. [Line saturation](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)

The same estimates hold if the records are replaced by pairwise unmatched compressed reciprocal units of bounded measure for which the class module at each input is either the full independent signed module or the sum of the blockwise sum-zero spaces in the oriented alternative, with all half-block products torsion in the latter.
For this version select any input of largest active degree if tied, and on its coordinate lines replace the record measure by the primitive line measure.
In particular these hypotheses hold for the large compressed unit with one nonreal exterior pair from the [exterior-pair theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v94.md#orbit).

<a id="fields"></a>
## Modules, the compressed representative and its orbit

The [record representation theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) gives both asserted models at a large reciprocal record, with \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\) blocks of size \(b=d/(2u)\).
A record is compressed by first attainment.
The degree bound and signed actions of the [alternating block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) and its [actual-root alternatives](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) apply also under the replacement hypotheses.
In particular \(b^7/d^6\to\infty\) uniformly for fixed \(M\).
The source modules are absolutely irreducible: even sign changes separate coordinates in the full signed case, and the independent block actions and transitivity on blocks give irreducibility in the sum-zero case.
They are pairwise nonisomorphic because unmatched large compressed inputs of bounded measure have different kernel fields. [Kernel-field comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)

Work only with the finite support of \([x]\).
Let \(L\) be the compositum of the splitting fields of these compressed inputs, and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
The rational class \([x]\) has a unique decomposition along the direct sum, and its projection to the module of \(a\) is nonzero.
Take \(\gamma\) to be the product of the torsion class of \(x\).
It belongs to \(L\): its splitting field is the kernel field of \(V_x\), the compositum of the active fields.
The degree \(D=\deg\gamma\) is the orbit size of its class, its stabilizer fixes the actual number, \(m(\gamma)=m(x)\), and \([\gamma]\) is a nonzero multiple of \([x]\). [Compression and composita](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#families)
Write \(v\) for the projection of \([\gamma]\) to the module of \(a\).
The \(G\)-orbit of \(v\) has size at most \(D\), and its images are exactly those from the full splitting group of \(a\).

<a id="few"></a>
## A polynomial-size orbit marks a bounded set of inverse pairs

Suppose \(D\le d^6\).
In the oriented case represent \(v\) by the unique vector of sum zero in each block.
In both models let \(t\) be the number of blocks with a nonzero component of \(v\).
The orbit provides at least \(b^t\) possibilities with those blocks occupied.
In the oriented case each nonzero component is nonconstant and the supported alternating group produces at least \(b\) arrangements.
Indeed a nonempty proper color class of size \(j\) moves to every \(j\)-subset, and there are at least \(b\) of them.
These alternating groups act independently.
In the signed case a nonempty proper support of size \(j\) likewise has at least \(b\) possible supports by the independent label actions.
On a fully supported block the even sign changes give \(2^{b-1}\ge b\) possibilities, independently of all the other blocks; after realizing prescribed partial supports apply these flips.
It follows from \(b^7>d^6\) that \(1\le t\le6\).

In the signed case each occupied block actually has at most six supported labels.
For \(7\le j\le b-7\) its support orbit has size at least \(\binom b7>d^6\) for large \(d\).
For \(j>b-7\), independently negating coordinates in even subsets gives at least \(2^{j-1}>d^6\) possibilities.
In the oriented case each occupied block has one value repeated at least \(b-6\) times, leaving between one and six exceptional coordinates.
Otherwise some union of color classes has size in \([7,b-7]\): choose any single class in this interval, or, if all classes have size at most six, accumulate classes until at least seven positions are present.
The stabilizer of the actual block vector fixes the chosen union, so the alternating orbit of the vector contains at least \(\binom b7\) distinct arrangements.
This contradicts its orbit bound.
The majority in the surviving case is unique and remains the majority when all signs on a transported block change.

Thus in either model the stabilizer of \(v\) preserves a nonempty set of at most 36 labels, given by the supports or by the exceptional coordinates on the occupied blocks.
Choose one actual root \(a'\) above one of these labels.
The stabilizer of \(\gamma\) fixes \(v\) and hence has orbit of size at most 72 on \(a'\).
This gives \([\mathbb Q(\gamma,a'):\mathbb Q]\le72D\).
The two units are multiplicatively independent by the excluded coordinate-line condition.
The [rank-two height product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) therefore implies
\[
          m(x)m(a)=Dd\,h(\gamma)h(a')
                    \ \gg\ \frac{d}{(\log(216D))^{212}}
                    \ \gg\ \frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{212}} .
\]
All implied constants here are absolute.

<a id="many"></a>
## A large orbit has an independent conjugate over a small relative extension

Suppose now \(D>d^6\).
The [unmatched-field theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) applied to \(a\) through degree \(d\) ensures that the group fixing all other active splitting fields still maps onto a subgroup of the label group containing every supported alternating factor of \(a\).
Indeed the other active inputs are unmatched and have compressed degrees at most \(d\), including ties.
Choose in \(G\) an element fixing all those fields and inducing one three-cycle on one block of \(a\) with all other labels fixed.
Its fourth power \(\tau\) still induces this three-cycle and fixes every actual root outside its three inverse pairs.
On the three pairs its original lift has order dividing six, since the cube acts by inversions; on all other pairs its order divides two.
Thus \(\tau\) has order three on their compositum.
All conjugates of \(\tau\) fix the other active fields since these fields are normal, and are determined by their actions on the roots of \(a\).
Each acts over just three labels of \(a\), with boundedly many signed permutations above a chosen triple.
The size of its conjugacy class is consequently at most \(C d^3\) for an absolute constant \(C\).

Some conjugate \(\sigma\) of \(\tau\) sends \([\gamma]\) to a linearly independent vector.
Otherwise every such element fixes that rational class, since its rational proportionality scalar has cube one.
Their normal closure then fixes the nonzero projection \(v\), and fixes its entire irreducible module by normality.
But the three-cycle acts nontrivially in both indicated models, a contradiction.
Over \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), the orbit of \(\sigma\gamma\) has size at most \(Cd^3\), because an element fixing \(\gamma\) sends \(\sigma\gamma\) to a conjugate of \(\sigma\) applied to \(\gamma\).
The joint degree of the independent units \(\gamma,\sigma\gamma\) is thus at most \(Cd^3D\).
Applying the same height product bound gives
\[
                    m(x)^2\gg\frac{D}{d^3(\log(3D))^{212}},
\]
where \(D>d^6\) absorbs the additional \(d^3\) inside the logarithm by an absolute constant factor.
For sufficiently large \(D\), \(D/(\log(3D))^{212}\) is increasing.
Consequently \(m(x)\gg d^{3/2}/(\log(3d))^{106}\).
The [Voutier lower bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m(a)\gg(\log(3d))^{-3}\) at large \(d\).
Multiplication proves (1) also in this regime.
The result imposes a cost on mixtures but gives no degree-independent gap on the individual primitive coordinate lines.
