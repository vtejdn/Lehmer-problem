# A product of original conjugates with two nonreal exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reduction with logarithmic measure cost at most two

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial with \(M(P)>1\), and suppose the logarithms of the moduli of its exterior roots span a line over \(\mathbb Q\).
Form the [reflection system of its conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v63.md#reflection), applying the [equal-measure torsion-class reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) first if needed.
Suppose the system has \(t\) components, all of type \(A_{N-1}\) with \(N\ge3\), or all of type \(D_N\) with \(N\ge5\).
There is a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial \(Q\) of degree exactly \(2Nt\) with precisely two exterior roots, which are a nonreal complex-conjugate pair, and
\[
                            1<M(Q)\le M(P)^2 .
\]
Each root of \(Q\) is a product of a subset of the roots of the original \(P\), each used once.
The construction allows the vector of a root of \(P\) to have nonzero projections in several components.
The exponent two is attained by this construction already in \(A_2\).

<a id="products"></a>
## The units selected by the natural coordinate directions

Write \(\Omega\) for the roots after torsion-class reduction, \(G\) for the Galois group of their splitting field, and \(H\) for the group acting on \(\langle\Omega\rangle\) generated by \(G\) and inversion.
Inversion commutes with \(G\), so every element of \(H\) is either a Galois action or a Galois action followed by inversion.
Write \(V\) for the real span of the units modulo torsion and \([x]\) for the vector of a unit.
Use the invariant inner product of the reflection construction.
It gives a distinguished root \(a\) of squared length two and a constant \(r>0\) such that \(\log|x|=r(a,[x])\) throughout \(\langle\Omega\rangle\).
Complex conjugation \(c\) acts on \(V\) as \(-s_a\), where \(s_a\) is the reflection perpendicular to \(a\).
The group induced by \(H\) contains the product of the Weyl groups of the components, and the components span \(V\) and are permuted transitively.

In each \(A_{N-1}\) component, take the \(2N\) directions represented by the unit vectors parallel to \(\pm(e_i-N^{-1}\sum_j e_j)\) in the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane.
In each \(D_N\) component take the \(2N\) vectors \(\pm e_i\).
Their union \(\mathcal B\) is intrinsic to the components and hence is \(H\)-invariant by the [classical coordinate description](Yin_C_G0345_v64.md#coordinates).
In the first model the directions are given by the shortest dual lattice vectors, and in the second model the doubled coordinate vectors are distinguished among length-two lattice vectors by the number of roots pairing to two with them.
In \(D_4\) this latter count also occurs for four-coordinate vectors, which is the reason for the rank restriction here.
The Weyl group acts by all coordinate permutations in the first model and all coordinate permutations with an even number of sign changes in the second.
These actions, inversion and transitivity on components make \(\mathcal B\) one \(H\)-orbit of size \(2Nt\).

For \(b\in\mathcal B\), Agent-S proposed selecting the actual conjugates whose vectors have positive pairing with \(b\), giving
\[
             \gamma_b=\prod_{\substack{x\in\Omega\\([x],b)>0}}x,\qquad
             S_b=[\gamma_b]=\sum_{\substack{x\in\Omega\\([x],b)>0}}[x].
\]
The group \(H\) permutes the actual roots, preserves the inner product and acts multiplicatively on their group.
Thus \(h(\gamma_b)=\gamma_{hb}\) exactly, including \(\gamma_{-b}=\gamma_b^{-1}\).
Each \(\gamma_b\) is an algebraic unit, and expanding class products recovers a product over disjoint fibers of the original roots when compression was used.

The stabilizer of \(b\) in the product Weyl group fixes precisely \(\mathbb R b\).
The other component groups have zero fixed space since their root normals span their components.
In the component of \(b\), fixing the distinguished coordinate still permits all permutations of the others in type \(A\), while in type \(D\) it permits flipping the signs of any two other coordinates.
In the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane the first condition leaves only the line of \(b\), and the second forces all other coordinates to vanish in type \(D\).
Each vector-stabilizing Weyl action lifts to an element of \(H\), so equivariance fixes \(S_b\) under this stabilizer.
Some \([x]\) pairs positively with \(b\), since the classes span and reciprocity gives both signs.
Hence \((S_b,b)>0\), and \(S_b=k b\) for \(k>0\) common to all \(b\) by \(H\)-transitivity and unit normalization.
Distinct members of \(\mathcal B\) therefore give distinct actual products.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The Galois orbit and its exterior products

Choose coordinates with \(a=e_1-e_2\) in its component.
There is a direction \(b_0\in\mathcal B\) orthogonal to \(a\), for example either natural direction indexed by the third coordinate.
Then \(c b_0=-b_0\), so \(G b_0\) contains both \(b_0\) and \(-b_0\).
Since inversion is central, \(G b_0=H b_0=\mathcal B\).
Equivariance and the distinctness of the products show that their polynomial is the minimal polynomial \(Q\) of any one of them, of degree \(2Nt\).
Its coefficients are rational algebraic integers, it is monic, and its roots pair with their reciprocals, giving reciprocity.

Only two directions pair positively with \(a\).
They are the positive direction indexed by \(1\) and the negative direction indexed by \(2\) in the distinguished component.
All directions from other components are orthogonal to \(a\).
The two positive directions have the same pairing, and \(c=-s_a\) exchanges them.
Since \(\log|\gamma_b|=rk(a,b)\), the two corresponding distinct products are exactly the exterior roots and form a nonreal conjugate pair.
Each has modulus at most the product of all exterior root moduli of \(P\), since each is a product over a subset of its distinct roots.
Multiplying the two bounds proves the asserted measure cost.

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## A sextic attaining the square

Let \(\lambda>1,\beta,\overline\beta\) be the roots of \(X^3-X-1\), and denote them by \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\) in this order.
The rational root test and the discriminant \(-23\) give an irreducible cubic with Galois group \(S_3\).
The roots are units of product one, with \(|\beta|=\lambda^{-1/2}\).
Any torsion monomial with exponents \(h_i\) must satisfy \(2h_i=h_j+h_k\) for each permutation of the indices, by placing its \(i\)-th factor at the real embedding and taking absolute logarithms.
Thus the only rational exponent relations modulo torsion are multiples of \((1,1,1)\).
The six ordered ratios \(\alpha_i/\alpha_j\), \(i\ne j\), are therefore distinct even modulo torsion and form one Galois orbit.
Take \(P\) to be their monic irreducible reciprocal sextic.
Its positive log moduli equal \(\tfrac32\log\lambda\), so the distinguished unit of the reflection construction is \(\lambda^3\).
The vectors \(u_i=[\alpha_i]\) of the cubic units span a plane with \(u_1+u_2+u_3=0\), so their differences span the same plane.
The reflection roots are \(\pm3u_i\), a system of type \(A_2\).
By permutation invariance the \(u_i\) have equal length and equal pairwise scalar products, so the natural directions, perpendicular in \(A_2\) to one of the three root lines, are the directions of the six differences \(u_i-u_j\).
For \(b\) in the direction \(u_1-u_2\), the three positive pairings select \(\alpha_1/\alpha_2,\alpha_1/\alpha_3,\alpha_3/\alpha_2\).
Their product is \((\alpha_1/\alpha_2)^2\).
All conjugates are therefore the six squared ratios, and \(M(Q)=M(P)^2\).
This equality concerns the specified product construction.
