# How many torsion values certify all reciprocal coefficients?

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact integrality threshold for a window of orders

Let \(P(Z)=Z^n S(Z+Z^{-1})\) be a monic reciprocal rational polynomial of degree \(2n\), with \(n\ge1\), and suppose its first \(j\) coefficients below the leading one are integers, \(0\le j<n\).
Write \(h=n-j-1\).
For \(m\ge1\) put \(\tau_m=\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}\) for a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity, and let \(\Psi_m\) be its monic rational minimal polynomial.
Thus \(\Psi_1=T-2,\ \Psi_2=T+2\), and \(\deg\Psi_m=\varphi(m)/2\) for \(m\ge3\).
Testing a value means requiring \(P(\zeta_m)\) to be an algebraic integer.

For all orders \(1\le m\le J\), \(J\ge1\), the tests force \(P\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) for every such input if and only if
\[
                       \sum_{J/2<m\le J}\deg\Psi_m > h.                   \tag{1}
\]
The least window length is asymptotic to \((2\sqrt{2}\pi/3)\sqrt{n-j}\) as \(n-j\to\infty\).
If (1) fails, a monic rational counterexample of denominator exactly two can be chosen irreducible of degree \(2n\), with all tested values nonzero.

More generally, for any finite set of positive orders \(\mathcal T\), put
\[
       a_p(\mathcal T)=\deg\operatorname{lcm}_{m\in\mathcal T}
                          (\Psi_m\bmod p)
\]
over \(\mathbb F_p\), with empty least common multiple one.
The tests force coefficient integrality exactly when \(a_p(\mathcal T)>h\) for every rational prime \(p\).
Each prime violating the inequality can serve as the exact denominator of an irreducible counterexample.
In particular a single full value suffices exactly when \(\deg\Psi_m>h\).
The [norm tests](Yin_C_G0345_v284.md#statement) require only rational integrality of the individual resultants, whereas here all conjugate evaluations at a tested order have algebraic integrality.
For the [rational models with one exterior root](Yin_C_G0345_v282.md#statement), (1) bounds how far their growing window can extend at a given degree and integral prefix.
The converse here allows arbitrary archimedean measure and root signature.

<a id="value"></a>
## The obstruction at a denominator prime

The coefficients of \(S\) are recovered from the upper coefficients of \(P\) by triangular integer operations with diagonal one, comparing the successive leading terms of \(Z^n(Z+Z^{-1})^k\).
Thus \(S\) is monic rational with the same integral prefix, and \(P\) is integral exactly when \(S\) is.
Since \(P(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^n S(\tau_m)\), testing either value is equivalent.
The [cyclotomic integer ring](../audits/literature/Bel17/Yin_U002.md#bel17-u002) is \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_m]\).
For \(m\ge3\) this ring is \(\mathbb Z[\tau_m]+\zeta_m\mathbb Z[\tau_m]\), by the quadratic equation for \(\zeta_m\) and the fact that \(\tau_m\) already belongs to the ring.
The two components are independent over the real trace field since \(\zeta_m\) is nonreal.
Taking invariants under complex conjugation shows that all integers in the trace field are in \(\mathbb Z[\tau_m]\).
The same trace-ring assertion for \(m=1,2\) is immediate.

Let \(A\) be the least positive common denominator of the coefficients of \(S\), and suppose \(p\mid A\).
Then \(Q=A S\) is integral and \(\bar Q\) modulo \(p\) is nonzero of degree at most \(h\), since all coefficients at larger indices are integers before clearing denominators.
If the tests pass, \(Q(\tau_m)\) belongs to \(p\mathbb Z[\tau_m]\).
The presentation \(\mathbb Z[\tau_m]=\mathbb Z[T]/(\Psi_m)\) shows that \(\bar\Psi_m\) divides \(\bar Q\) for every tested order, including its full multiplicity in characteristic \(p\).
This proves the necessary inequality \(a_p(\mathcal T)\le h\) at any denominator prime.

Conversely, lift the monic least common multiple at a prime with \(a_p\le h\) to a monic \(H\in\mathbb Z[T]\) of that degree.
Choose \(S=S_0+H/p\) for a monic integral \(S_0\) of degree \(n\).
All tested values of \(H(\tau_m)/p\) are integral by the same presentation.
The first \(j\) coefficients below the leading coefficient remain integral and the denominator of \(S\) and \(P\) is exactly \(p\).
To arrange irreducibility choose an auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\).
Prescribe \(pS\equiv p(T-2)^n+\ell\bmod\ell^2\) coefficientwise, which is possible by choosing the nonleading coefficients of \(S_0\) modulo \(\ell^2\).
The integral polynomial \(pP(Z)\) reduces to \(p(Z-1)^{2n}\bmod\ell\), and \(pP(1)=pS(2)\) is divisible by \(\ell\) exactly once.
Eisenstein at \(\ell\) after translating by one proves irreducibility over the rationals.
A nonintegral monic rational irreducible polynomial cannot have a torsion root, so all tested values are nonzero.

<a id="window"></a>
## Reduction of the complete order window

Fix \(p\).
Group the orders \(m=p^a t\le J\) by their prime-to-\(p\) part \(t\).
In characteristic \(p\) their trace polynomials have as roots exactly the traces of primitive \(t\)-th roots.
Indeed reduction kills the \(p\)-part and preserves the exact order of a root of unity of order prime to \(p\).
All primitive \(t\)-th roots occur on reduction, by lifting the exponent from the units modulo \(t\) to the units modulo \(m\).
Two traces of roots of unity in a field agree only when the roots are equal or inverses, by their quadratic equation.
The sets of reduced traces are therefore disjoint for distinct \(t\).
Within each group the reduced roots occur with equal multiplicities: the exponent units modulo \(m\) permute the lifts transitively and induce a transitive permutation on the reduced primitive roots up to inversion.
These multiplicities are nondecreasing with \(a\), since they equal the degree of \(\Psi_{p^a t}\) divided by the fixed number of distinct reduced traces.
The degrees themselves are nondecreasing along the group, including the initial orders one and two.
Its contribution to the least common multiple is consequently the final member, which is characterized by \(J/p<m\le J\).
It follows that
\[
                a_p(\{1,\ldots,J\})=\sum_{J/p<m\le J}\deg\Psi_m .
\]
This is smallest at \(p=2\), giving (1) and the claimed denominator.
For the asymptotic, the totient identity \(\varphi(m)=m\sum_{k\mid m}\mu(k)/k\) gives \(\sum_{m\le x}\varphi(m)=3x^2/\pi^2+O(x\log(2x))\) by summing the integers up to \(x/k\) and using \(\sum\mu(k)/k^2=1/\zeta(2)\).
Thus the sum in (1) is \(9J^2/(8\pi^2)+O(J\log(2J))\), and inversion gives the stated least length.
