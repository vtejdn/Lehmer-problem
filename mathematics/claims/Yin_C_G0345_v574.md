# Common factors in a finite collection of dense exponent sets

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cost of testing prescribed sets

For an exponent set \(S\subseteq[0,N)\cap\mathbb Z\), let \(L_S\) be the squarefree product of the nontorsion unit minima that divide some nonzero ternary polynomial supported on \(S\).
Ternary coefficients are \(0,1,-1\).
The [unrestricted threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#polynomial) tests the greatest common divisor of these products over every set of the specified size.
Fix \(h\ge1\) and \(0<\delta\le1\).
For any \(h\) exponent sets of size at least \(\delta N\), and all sufficiently large \(N\), their products have at least \(c_{\delta,h}N\) distinct common factors of the form \(H(X^q)\), where \(H\) is Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial.
Each is reciprocal and irreducible and has the same Mahler measure as \(H\).
Their product has degree \(\gg_{\delta,h} N^2\).
In particular restricting the lists to reciprocal factors of measure at most Lehmer's still leaves this obstruction.

For the unrestricted factor lists there is also a lower bound allowing \(h\) to vary.
If all exponent sets have size at least \(s\ge1\) and their products have no common factor, then
\[
 h\ \ge\ \frac{\log(N/r_3(N))}{\log((2N-1)/s)}
 \ \gg\ \frac{(\log N)^{1/9}}{\log((2N-1)/s)}\qquad(N\ge3).
\]
Here \(r_\ell(N)\) is the largest size of a subset of \([0,N)\cap\mathbb Z\) containing no nonconstant arithmetic progression of length \(\ell\).
In contrast, the actual value of every unrestricted threshold \(R_k(t)\) is unchanged if one tests only irreducible minima that are not a polynomial in \(X^q\) for any \(q>1\).

<a id="alignment"></a>
## Finding the same step in different sets

For sets \(S_i\subseteq[0,N)\) choose independent integer shifts \(b_i\) uniformly from \([1-N,N-1]\).
For each \(x\in[0,N)\cap\mathbb Z\), exactly \(|S_i|\) shifts place \(x\) in \(S_i+b_i\).
Thus for some choices the intersection of all shifted sets with \([0,N)\) has size at least
\[
                    N\prod_{i=1}^h\frac{|S_i|}{2N-1}.
\]
If this exceeds \(r_\ell(N)\), the sets all contain an \(\ell\)-term progression with the same positive step, allowing different initial terms.
For \(\ell=3\) and step \(q\), each supports a monomial times \(X^{2q}-X^q-1\).
Its positive real root is a nontorsion unit, so the asserted lower bound for \(h\) follows from the [three-term progression bound of Bloom and Sisask](../audits/literature/BS23/Yin_U001.md#bs23-u001).

To count distinct common steps, fix \(\ell\ge3\) and choose \(L\ge\ell\) with \(r_\ell(L)<L(\delta/4)^h\), possible by [Szemerédi's density theorem](../audits/literature/Sze75/Yin_U001.md#sze75-u001).
For each \(1\le u\le\lfloor\delta N/(2L)\rfloor\), partition the interval into residue classes modulo \(u\).
Within each class take consecutive disjoint blocks of \(L\) positions, discarding its last incomplete block.
Fewer than \(uL\) positions are discarded, so each \(S_i\) occupies at least \(\delta L/2\) positions in some full block.
Compress the chosen block for each set to \([0,L)\) and apply the shift calculation there.
The compressed sets have a common progression step \(v\) with \(1\le v\le (L-1)/(\ell-1)\).
This gives the common step \(q=uv\) in the original sets.
Choosing one such \(v\) for each \(u\) produces at least \(\lfloor\delta N/(2L)\rfloor/\lfloor(L-1)/(\ell-1)\rfloor\) distinct steps, since a fixed \(q,v\) determines \(u\).
An \(\ell\)-term progression of step \(q\) supports a translate of \(F(X^q)\) for every ternary \(F\) of degree below \(\ell\).

<a id="lifts"></a>
## Irreducible substitutions of record measures

Let \(\beta\) be a nontorsion unit of degree \(d\) whose log measure \(m(\beta)\) is smallest among the positive log measures of units of degree at most \(d\).
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), take \(\alpha^q=\beta\), put \(e=[K(\alpha):K]\) and let \(y\) be the relative norm of \(\alpha\).
Then \(e\le q\), \(y\) is a unit in \(K\) and \(y^q=\beta^e\).
Sum \(\log^+|\sigma y|=(e/q)\log^+|\sigma\beta|\) over the embeddings of \(K\).
This yields
\[
         [K:\mathbb Q(y)]\,m(y)= (e/q)m(\beta)>0 .
\]
If \(e<q\), the unit \(y\) has smaller positive measure and degree at most \(d\).
Therefore \(e=q\), and the monic minimal polynomial \(P(X^q)\) of \(\alpha\) has degree \(dq\), where \(P\) is the minimum of \(\beta\).
The \(q\)-th roots of each root of \(P\) contribute its original log measure in total, so substitution preserves measure.

The [degree bound of Mossinghoff, Rhin and Wu](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#mrw08-u001) gives this record property for the [degree-ten Lehmer unit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v287.md#separation).
Its polynomial \(H\) is reciprocal and ternary of degree ten.
Apply the step count with \(\ell=11\).
Distinct steps give irreducibles \(H(X^q)\) of distinct degrees \(10q\).
The sum of any \(b\) distinct positive steps is at least \(b(b+1)/2\), proving the quadratic degree bound.

The same obstruction operates below any proposed small-measure cutoff \(0<\varepsilon\le\log 2/k\) at which a nontorsion unit with \(m<\varepsilon\) exists.
Start with such a unit of degree \(d_0\) and minimize the positive measure in degrees at most \(d_0\), which is possible by finiteness at bounded degree and bounded measure.
The resulting minimum \(P\) has the record property and \(k m(P)<\log2\).
The [count of binary subsets modulo \(P^k\)](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#density) supplies a nonzero ternary multiple \(F\) on a finite full interval.
Take \(\ell=\max(3,1+\deg F)\).
Each of the linearly many common steps then gives a distinct irreducible \(P(X^q)\) of the same positive log measure below \(\varepsilon\), whose \(k\)-th power divides a ternary polynomial on each tested set.
The constants here depend also on \(F\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Removing substitutions for the complete threshold

Let \(P(X)=Q(X^q)\) be irreducible with \(q>1\).
Then \(Q\) is itself an irreducible unit minimum, and \(Q\) is noncyclotomic when \(P\) is.
Write \(a_N(P,k)\) for the maximum size of an exponent set on which no nonzero ternary multiple of \(P^k\) is supported, and put \(a_0=0\).
For \(0\le j<q\), let \(n_j\) be the number of integers in \([0,N)\) congruent to \(j\) modulo \(q\).
Grouping a ternary polynomial as \(\sum_{j<q}X^j F_j(X^q)\) shows that \(P^k\) divides it if and only if \(Q^k\) divides every \(F_j\).
Indeed polynomial division of each \(F_j\) by \(Q^k\) gives an overall remainder of degree below \(\deg(P^k)\), with the residue classes disjoint.
Thus admissibility means that every compressed residue class is admissible, and
\[
                  a_N(P,k)=\sum_{j<q}a_{n_j}(Q,k)\ \ge\ a_N(Q,k).
\]
The inequality follows by partitioning a single admissible set for \(Q\) into consecutive intervals of lengths \(n_j\) and translating each to zero.
It follows that \(a_N(P,k)\le t\) implies \(a_N(Q,k)\le t\) at exactly the same \(N,t,k\).
Iterating to the largest common divisor of the exponents in \(P\) proves the stated reduction for \(R_k(t)\), including its vacuous case.
The same formula gives \(D_k(P)=D_k(Q)\) by taking \(N\to\infty\) with \(q\) fixed.

There is a corresponding explicit transfer for a restricted family of tests.
If a family of \((t+1)\)-point sets in \([0,N)\) excludes every minimum with exponent gcd one, use its members and, for each \(2\le q\le\lfloor t/(2k)\rfloor\), also use their images under the following permutation.
Cut \([0,N)\) into successive intervals of lengths \(n_0,\ldots,n_{q-1}\) and send position \(i\) within interval \(j\), counting from zero, to \(j+qi\).
A set admissible for \(Q\) has an admissible image for \(Q(X^q)\) by the preceding residue-class identity.
Include the consecutive test \(\{0,\ldots,t\}\) when \(N>t\).
It already excludes \(P\) whenever \(k\deg P>t\), and \(\deg Q\ge2\) for nontorsion unit minima.
These tests therefore cover every minimum, with at most \(1+\max(1,\lfloor t/(2k)\rfloor)\) times the original number of tests when that number is positive and \(N>t\).
