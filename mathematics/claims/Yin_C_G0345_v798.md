# Independence of exterior roots on steep sections and the degree of their product

<a id="statement"></a>
## Joint degrees at both exterior poles

Use the [quadratic curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement)
\[
 F(U,V)=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U),\quad
 A(U)=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1),\quad A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U).
\]
Consider positive coprime integers \(k,l\) with \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\), and write \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\).
If
\[
                  \frac{s}{\varphi(k)\log(2l)}\longrightarrow\infty,      \tag{1}
\]
then for each fixed \(t\ge1\) **every \(t\) distinct exterior roots** \(z_1,\ldots,z_t\) of \(P\) are multiplicatively independent at all sufficiently large indices.
The roots may come from different rational factors.
Their joint field has degree
\[
 [\mathbb Q(z_1,\ldots,z_t):\mathbb Q]\gg_t
             \frac{l^t}{(\log(2l))^{a_t}},\qquad
 a_t=t\big((t+1)((t+1)!)^t-t\big).                                      \tag{2}
\]

Let \(S\) be a monic rational irreducible factor, \(d=\deg S\), and \(H\) its number of exterior roots.
Put \(D_M=[\mathbb Q(M(S)):\mathbb Q]\) for its multiplicative Mahler measure.
Under (1), whenever \(H\ge t\) for fixed \(t\),
\[
                 D_M\gg_t\frac{(l/H)^t}{(\log(2l))^{a_t}} .               \tag{3}
\]
The same bound holds for the degree of every positive power of \(M(S)\).
In particular the following two conclusions apply.

* Along a sequence with fixed \(H\), one always has \(\log D_M/\log d\to H\), since the [bounded-signature slope bound](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#statement) implies (1).
* Under (1), if \(H\to\infty\), then \(D_M/d^B\to\infty\) for every fixed \(B>0\).
  This applies in particular when \(H\to\infty\) and \(H=o(k(\log\log k/\log k)^3)\), without imposing (1) separately.

<a id="relations"></a>
## Polynomial size of a possible relation

The section is monic with unit constant coefficient, so its roots and their inverses are algebraic integers.
For \(t\) chosen exterior roots put \(E=\mathbb Q(z_1,\ldots,z_t)\) and \(D=[E:\mathbb Q]\le(4k+2l)^t\).
Their [section heights](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) are \(O(1/l)\), so each height is at most one for large indices.
For a unit \(u\) in \(E\), take the vector of \(\log|\sigma(u)|\) over all its \(D\) embeddings, with norm the sum of absolute coordinates divided by \(2D\).
By the product formula the vector has norm \(h(u)\), its absolute logarithmic height.
A torsion unit has zero vector.
For a nontorsion unit [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) and the absence of rational nontorsion units give
\[
                  h(u)>\frac{2}{D\log^3(3D)}>\frac{1}{20D^4}.
\]

If the chosen roots are multiplicatively dependent, their log vectors span a real space of dimension at most \(t-1\).
For integers \(0\le b_i\le L\) the vector of \(\prod_i z_i^{b_i}\) is in a ball of radius \(tL\).
Distinct vectors in this set are separated by at least \(1/(20D^4)\).
Comparing the volumes of disjoint balls of half that radius with a containing ball bounds the number of distinct vectors by \((1+40tLD^4)^{t-1}\), also in dimension zero.
For \(L=\lceil(2+40tD^4)^t\rceil\) the \((L+1)^t\) choices cannot all give distinct vectors.
A collision gives a nonzero integral relation modulo a root of unity of order \(q\) in \(E\).
Then \(\varphi(q)\le D\) and \(q\le 2D^2\), since the prime-power factors in \(\varphi(q)^2/q\) are all at least one except for \(2^1\), which contributes \(1/2\).
Multiplying the coefficients by \(q\) therefore gives an exact relation
\[
            \prod_{i=1}^t z_i^{b_i}=1,\qquad
            (b_1,\ldots,b_t)\ne0,\qquad
            B_0=\max(2,|b_1|,\ldots,|b_t|)\le (2l)^{O_t(1)}.             \tag{4}
\]

<a id="series"></a>
## Successive coefficients at a fixed pole

For \(j=3,4\) put \(\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\).
At \(\beta=\beta_j\) the [analytic exterior branches](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#lifts) give \(z^k=u(T)\) with \(T=\nu^{-l}\) and \(\nu^k=1\).
Each phase at a given pole belongs to exactly one exterior root by coprimality.
Write \(f_s(T)=\log(u(T)/\beta)\), taking the branch with \(f_s(0)=0\).

Let \(U(w)\) solve \(A(U)+U^2w+A^*(U)w^2=0\) near \(U(0)=\beta\).
The simple zero of \(A\) makes \(g(w)=\log(U(w)/\beta)\) analytic, with \(g(0)=0\) and \(a=g'(0)=-\beta/A'(\beta)\ne0\).
The local parameter \(w=T u(T)^{-s}\) satisfies \(x=\beta^{-s}T=w e^{s g(w)}\), and \(f_s(T)=g(w)\).
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
 [T^n]f_s(T)=\beta^{-ns}P_{\beta,n}(s),\qquad
 P_{\beta,n}(s)=\frac1n[w^{n-1}]\,g'(w)e^{-ns g(w)}.                     \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(n[x^n]g(w(x))\) is the residue of \(x^{-n}d(g(w(x)))\), and the change of variable \(x=w e^{s g(w)}\) gives the coefficient in (5) multiplied by \(n\).
This polynomial in \(s\) has exact degree \(n-1\), with leading coefficient \(a(-na)^{n-1}/n!\).
For fixed \(N\ge0\) and every \(|T|=1\), the tail starting at \(n=N+1\) is \(O_N((1+s)^N\beta^{-(N+1)s})\).
This follows from the same branch's Cauchy bound \(O((1+s)^{-1}R^{-n})\) at \(R=c\beta^s/(1+s)\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Separation of phase sums by cyclotomic norms

Assign each chosen root to its unique pole, with index set \(I_j\) and phase \(T_i\) at \(\beta_j\).
Let \(b^{(j)}=\sum_{i\in I_j} b_i\).
Raise (4) to the \(k\)-th power and take logarithms of absolute values.
The bound for \(f_s\) gives
\[
          |b^{(3)}\log\beta_3+b^{(4)}\log\beta_4|
                     \ll_t B_0\, e^{-s\log\beta_3}.                        \tag{6}
\]
The two positive quadratic units are multiplicatively independent.
The automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13},\sqrt5)\) which negates \(\sqrt{13}\) and fixes \(\sqrt5\) changes the modulus of \(\beta_3\) to its reciprocal and fixes \(\beta_4\).
Thus any relation first has zero exponent at \(\beta_3\) and then at \(\beta_4\).
For a nonzero form the [Baker–Wüstholz theorem](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#wus02-u001), applied to these fixed numbers and real logarithms, bounds its absolute value below by \((2tB_0)^{-C}\) for a constant depending only on the two units.
As \(\log B_0=O_t(\log(2l))=o(s)\), (6) forces \(b^{(3)}=b^{(4)}=0\).
The exact product in (4) now implies \(\sum_{j,i\in I_j}b_i f_{j,s}(T_i)\in2\pi i\mathbb Z\), using \(f_{j,s}\) at \(\beta_j\).
Its modulus tends to zero, so this sum is zero.

For each \(j,n\ge1\) with \(j\in\{3,4\}\), put \(Z_{j,n}=\sum_{i\in I_j} b_i T_i^n\).
This is an algebraic integer in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_k)\) with all conjugates bounded by \(tB_0\).
If nonzero it therefore has absolute value at least \((tB_0)^{1-\varphi(k)}\).
Choose a fixed cutoff \(Y=t\log\beta_4\), and list in increasing order the positive weights \(\lambda=n\log\beta_j\le Y\).
All weights are distinct by independence of the two units.
The next larger weights at either pole are separated from \(Y\) by a positive constant, since the cutoff is fixed.
Substituting (5) into the vanishing sum gives a term \(P_{\beta_j,n}(s)Z_{j,n}e^{-s\lambda}\) for each listed weight.
The tail estimate at each pole bounds the sum of all omitted terms by \(O_t(B_0(1+s)^{O_t(1)}e^{-s(Y+\epsilon_t)})\) for some \(\epsilon_t>0\).
If any listed moment were nonzero, take the smallest of its weights.
Every higher listed weight and each tail have a fixed positive separation from it.
After division by its exponential and polynomial factors, whose leading coefficient in (5) is nonzero, the equality would give
\[
           |Z_{j,n}|\ll_t B_0(1+s)^{O_t(1)}e^{-c_t s}
\]
for some \(c_t>0\).
This contradicts the cyclotomic norm bound because \(\varphi(k)\log(tB_0)=o(s)\) by (1) and (4).
Thus every listed moment vanishes, including \(1\le n\le |I_j|-1\) for each nonempty \(I_j\).
The zeroth moment vanishes by (6).
The Vandermonde matrix on the distinct phases at each pole is nonsingular, so these moments force all its \(b_i\) to vanish, contradicting (4).
The bounds depend only on \(t\) and the fixed curve, proving the asserted uniformity.

<a id="measure"></a>
## The field of the complete exterior product

For fixed \(t\) the [Amoroso–David height product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) applied to the independent roots gives
\[
             \prod_{i=1}^t h(z_i)\gg_t D^{-1}(\log(3D))^{-a_t}.
\]
The bounds \(h(z_i)\ll1/l\) and \(D\le(4k+2l)^t\) give (2).
For a factor \(S\) let \(B\) be the full set of its \(H\) exterior roots in its splitting field \(K\).
Complex conjugation preserves \(B\), so \(M(S)\) is the product of its elements multiplied by a rational sign.
The product of the moduli over any other \(H\)-element subset is strictly smaller.
Thus fixing any positive power of \(M(S)\) is equivalent for \({\rm Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\) to preserving \(B\) as a set, which also proves equality of the fields of all positive powers.
Each translate of \(B\) contains at most \(H^t\) possible ordered images of a tuple drawn from it.
There are \(D_M\) translates, so (2) gives (3).

For fixed \(H\), the [pole count](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#statement) gives \(d/s\to H\), and the necessary slope in (2) of [the degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#statement) gives \(\log k=o(\log s)\).
In particular (1) holds, \(\log l/\log d\to1\), and (3) with \(t=H\) and the upper bound \(D_M\le\binom dH\) give the stated limit.
For \(H\to\infty\) under (1), the [total exterior count](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) gives \(H\le2k\), so \(l/H\ge s/2\).
Since \(\varphi(k)\ge\sqrt{k/2}\), (1) implies \(k\le2s^2\) and \(d\le4k+2l=O(s^3)\) eventually.
For any fixed \(B>0\) choose fixed \(t>3B\) in (3), which gives \(D_M/d^B\to\infty\).
Finally, if \(H=o(k(\log\log k/\log k)^3)\), the [finite exterior lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#statement) rules out \(l\le k^b\) on any infinite subsequence at each fixed \(b\).
Taking \(b=4\) already gives \(s/(\varphi(k)\log(2l))\to\infty\), by the monotonicity of \(l/\log(2l)\).
These degree restrictions still allow exterior products of large degree and leave that case open.
