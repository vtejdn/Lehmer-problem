# The Pisot excess at every sufficiently large even degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## The limit at the prescribed square constant

For each even \(d\ge4\), let \(\eta_d\) be the infimum of \(\theta-d^2\) for Pisot numbers \(\theta\) whose minimal polynomial is monic of degree \(d\) and constant term \(+d^2\), with value \(+\infty\) for an empty set.
For all sufficiently large even \(d\) the infimum is attained, and
\[
             \lim_{\substack{d\to\infty\\2\mid d}}\eta_d
                      =\inf_{\sigma\ {\rm Salem}}(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular the right side vanishes if and only if one can realize an excess tending to zero along **every** sufficiently large even degree with its prescribed constant.

For any fixed Salem minimal polynomial \(P_0\) of degree \(d_0\) and exterior trace \(T\), every sufficiently large even \(d\) admits such an inverse \(f\) whose entire return has \(P_0\) as its sole noncyclotomic factor and whose root satisfies
\[
            0<\theta-d^2-(T-2)
                      =O_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{d^{3/2}}\right).
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
The [single-grid construction](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#square-padding) established this estimate on a subsequence whose endpoint ratio was unchanged.
A bounded additional factor of odd-order roots supplies the missing degrees.
The bound's constant and its onset may depend on the supplied \(P_0\).

<a id="bounded-gaps"></a>
## Bounded gaps between suitable grid exponents

Let \(\mathcal U\) contain all neutral roots of \(P_0\) and put \(g=(\zeta^4)_{\zeta\in\mathcal U}\).
The closure \(\mathcal K\) of the integral powers of \(g\) is a compact subgroup of the finite torus.
The nonnegative powers are already dense in \(\mathcal K\).
Indeed thinning a convergent sequence of positive powers gives positive exponents \(n\to\infty\) with \(g^n\to1\) by taking consecutive exponent differences, so each negative power is approximated by nonnegative ones.
Let \(\mathcal V\subset\mathcal K\) be the relative open neighborhood of the identity on which \(|1+v_\zeta|>1\) for every \(\zeta\).
The translates \(g^k\mathcal V,\ k\ge0\), cover \(\mathcal K\), because each nonempty open set \(y\mathcal V^{-1}\) meets the nonnegative powers.
A finite subcover uses indices at most some \(K=K(P_0)\).
Consequently for each integer \(n\ge K+1\) there is \(0\le k\le K\) such that \(g^{n-k}\in\mathcal V\).

Given an even \(d\), take \(n=\lfloor(d-d_0)/4\rfloor\) and, when \(n\ge K+1\), set
\[
               J=4(n-k),\qquad L=d-d_0-J .
\]
Then \(4\mid J,\ |1+\zeta^J|>1\) for every \(\zeta\in\mathcal U\), and \(L\) is a nonnegative even integer at most \(4K+2\).
In particular \(J=d-O_{P_0}(1)\).

<a id="degree-filler"></a>
## Filling the remaining degree without a repeated root

Put
\[
 G_L(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^L,\qquad
 P(z)=P_0(z)G_L(z)(z^J+1).
\]
Take \(G_0=1\).
For \(L>0\) all roots of \(G_L\) are nontrivial roots of unity of odd order dividing \(L+1\), and all are simple.
Every root of \(z^J+1\) has even order and is simple.
The two padding factors therefore have disjoint roots, neither has a root at \(1\) or \(-1\), and neither meets the nontorsion roots of \(P_0\).
The new polynomial is monic reciprocal of degree exactly \(d\), with precisely the original exterior pair and simple neutral roots.
Its evaluations change by the explicit factors
\[
 P(1)=2(L+1)P_0(1),\qquad P(-1)=2P_0(-1),\qquad
 P(i)=2G_L(i)P_0(i).
\]
Thus the ratio \(P(-1)/|P(1)|\) acquires the factor \(1/(L+1)\), which equals one when \(L=0\).

Let \(S_L=P_0G_L\), belonging to a finite set of polynomials for this fixed input.
For each old neutral root \(\xi\) of \(S_L\) we have a common positive lower bound for \(|1+\xi^J|\).
For a root of \(P_0\) this is the choice of \(J\).
If \(\xi\) is a root of \(G_L\), all its powers have odd order and form a finite group that excludes \(-1\), uniformly over the finitely many possible \(L\).
The same bound holds at \(\xi=-1\) since \(J\) is even.

Apply the [neutral-node calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#square-padding) with the finitely many \(S_L\) as the old factors.
More explicitly, each old contribution to \(\Gamma(P)\) is bounded, because \(P'(\xi)=S'_L(\xi)(1+\xi^J)\) and all old neutral roots are simple and different from \(-1\).
At a grid root \(z^J=-1\), \(|(z+1)P'(z)|=J|(z+1)S_L(z)|\).
The latter fixed factor in \(z\) has simple circle zeros at \(-1\) and the neutral roots \(\xi\) of \(S_L\).
The lower bounds on \(|1+\xi^J|\) give \(|z-\xi|\gg_{P_0}1/J\) from \(|z^J-\xi^J|\le J|z-\xi|\).
Summing inverse chord distances over the equally spaced grid costs \(O_{P_0}(J\log J)\) at each such zero, by the same harmonic sum as in the single-grid estimate.
Finiteness of the choices of \(S_L\) gives \(\Gamma(P)=O_{P_0}(\log d)\) uniformly in \(d\).
The [rounding estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#square-interpolation) and its [strict stability proof for a reducible return](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) now give (2) at \(q=d^2\), with a degree-\(d\) Pisot minimal polynomial and the stated full return.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Taking the minimum over Pisot inputs

For each fixed Salem number the construction proves \(\limsup_{d\to\infty,\,2\mid d}\eta_d\le T-2\).
Taking the infimum over the Salem numbers is valid after this individual upper bound, with no uniform choice of \(K\) or other constants.
A single fixed Salem input already gives nonemptiness for every sufficiently large even \(d\).
At each such \(d\), restricting to Pisot roots no larger than one available root bounds the Mahler measure of their monic minimal polynomials, which equals the exterior root.
The elementary symmetric coefficient bounds then leave finitely many possibilities and give a minimum.

The [converse for every candidate](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#infimum) bounds its excess below by the infimum in (1), using the actual exterior factor of its return and the integer-trace bound for the possible quadratic factor.
This proves (1) and both implications in the zero-limit assertion.
Whether the infimum is positive remains open.
