# Testing Salem orthogonality inside the root field of a unit with totally real trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## The relative norms that detect every Salem contribution

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit whose trace \(t=w+w^{-1}\) is totally real.
Choose a real exterior conjugate of \(w\) as the given embedding and set \(K=\mathbb Q(w)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\).
Such an embedding exists because a nonzero exterior root with real trace is real.
For a primitive positive Salem number \(\alpha\) let \(K_\alpha=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) in its positive real embedding.
Some conjugate of \(\alpha\) has nonzero [logarithmic projection](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#coefficient) against \(w\) if and only if
\[
                   K_\alpha\subseteq K,\qquad N_{K/K_\alpha}w\ne\pm1.    \tag{1}
\]
In fact only the two real conjugates \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) can have nonzero coefficient for this real embedding of \(w\).
Whenever (1) holds, \(N_{K/K_\alpha}w=\pm\alpha^k\) for a nonzero integer \(k\), and
\[
                               |k|\log\alpha\le m(w).
\]
There is no bound on the degrees, measures or number of exterior roots.
The criterion stays valid if \(w\) is totally real, in which case it excludes all the indicated subfields.
Thus the [Salem-orthogonal branch](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#infimum) on this entire class can be tested by the ordinary relative norms in its real root field.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## Distinct quadratic extensions give zero conditional norm

If a coefficient against a conjugate \(\gamma\) of \(\alpha\) is nonzero, the two log functions are simultaneously nonzero at some group element in a common normal closure.
At this embedding the image \(w'\) is real exterior or interior since its trace is real; the image of \(\gamma\) is \(\alpha^{\pm1}\).
Work first in this embedding.
Put \(F'=\mathbb Q(w'+w'^{-1})\), \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), \(T=F'B\), a totally real field.
The quadratic extension \(K_\alpha T/T\) is nontrivial: a place of \(B\) where its trace is interior extends to a real embedding of \(T\).
If \(w'\) is totally real, \(K_\alpha\mathbb Q(w')/\mathbb Q(w')B\) remains quadratic by the same reasoning.
The norm \(\rho=N_{K_\alpha\mathbb Q(w')/K_\alpha}w'\) is then fixed by the inverter of \(\alpha\), since this inverter lifts to an automorphism fixing \(\mathbb Q(w')\) in that compositum.
Its projection coefficient is zero.

If \(w'\) is not totally real, it has both a real split and an interior trace place, since its trace is totally real and a nonreal conjugate with real trace has modulus one.
The quadratic \(T(w')/T\) also remains nontrivial.
If \(T(w')\) and \(T(\alpha)\) are distinct, their compositum over \(T\) admits an involution fixing \(w'\) and inverting \(\alpha\).
Functoriality of the norm again shows that the inverter fixes \(\rho\), contradicting nonzero projection.
Consequently \(T(w')=T(\alpha)\).

<a id="descent"></a>
## Descending the equal square classes to a Salem subfield

The two quadratic radicands \(d_w=(w'+w'^{-1})^2-4\) and \(d_\alpha=(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})^2-4\) have square ratio in \(T\).
In particular their signs agree at every real embedding of \(T\).
At the displayed embedding they are both positive.
In a finite normal closure of \(T\), every automorphism fixing \(F'\) preserves positivity of \(d_w\), hence of \(d_\alpha\).
For a Salem trace the latter positivity occurs precisely when the embedding fixes \(B\) pointwise, since the positive trace is its unique conjugate outside \([-2,2]\).
Thus the stabilizer of \(F'\) is contained in that of \(B\), giving \(B\subseteq F'\) and \(T=F'\).
The quadratic equality now gives \(K_\alpha\subseteq\mathbb Q(w')\).
Transporting back to the original embedding shows \(\gamma\in K\).
Since \(K\) is a subfield of the reals here, \(\gamma\) must be \(\alpha\) or \(\alpha^{-1}\), giving the required literal subfield inclusion.

Conversely suppose \(K_\alpha\subseteq K\).
The field \(K\) cannot be totally real.
At an interior trace embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K\) ordinary complex conjugation induces the inverter \(\tau\) of \(w\) on \(K\) by \(\bar\sigma=\sigma\tau\).
If \(\sigma(\alpha)\) were real, this would force \(\tau\) to fix \(\alpha\), putting \(K_\alpha\) in the totally real fixed field \(F\), an impossibility.
Hence \(\sigma(\alpha)\) has modulus one and \(\tau\alpha=\alpha^{-1}\).
In particular \(K_\alpha\) is stable under \(\tau\), which acts there as its Salem inverter.
The relative norm \(\rho=N_{K/K_\alpha}w\) is then a norm-one unit since \(\tau(\rho)=N_{K/K_\alpha}(w^{-1})\).
The [Salem unit kernel](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) gives \(\rho=\pm\alpha^k\).
The conditional norm formula shows that the real-pair projection is nonzero precisely when \(k\ne0\).
Finally \(h(\rho)\le[K:K_\alpha]h(w)\); multiplying by \([K_\alpha:\mathbb Q]\) gives the total-measure inequality.
