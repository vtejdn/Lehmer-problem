# Inverse pairs on the genus-two quotient of the two-pole curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two exceptional fibers

Let \(C\) be the smooth projective [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement), and let \(T=V^k/U^l\) for a primitive signed pair with \(kl\ne0\).
Inversion \(\iota(U,V)=(U^{-1},V^{-1})\) gives an unramified double cover of the genus-two curve
\[
 D:\quad W^2=(Z^2-4)H(Z),\qquad H(Z)=1-4(Z^2-13)(Z^2-20).
\]
The function \(S=T+T^{-1}\) on \(D\) has degree \(n=2|l|+4|k|\).
Over each of \(S=2,-2\) it has \(n/2\) points of index two, and its pole orders are \(|l|,|l|,|k|,|k|,|k|,|k|\).
Outside the [exceptional directions of the monodromy theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v855.md#statement), it has eight other branch values with one point of index two each, and geometric monodromy \(S_n\).

Let \(E_+,E_-\) be the reduced divisors over \(2,-2\), respectively, and put \(P_a=(a,0)\) for each finite branch coordinate of the hyperelliptic map \(Z\).
For every such direction,
\[
 [E_+-E_-]=\delta=[P_2-P_{-2}],\qquad \operatorname{Jac}(D)[2](\mathbb Q)=\langle\delta\rangle\simeq\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Both elliptic quotients given by \((Z^2,W)\) and \((Z^2,ZW)\) kill this class under pushforward.
The closed points of each divisor correspond to the rational irreducible factors of the trace polynomial of the corresponding signed section.
Their individual resultants satisfy the [prime valuation restriction below](#factors), and their products across the two fibers satisfy the [squareclass identity](#comparison).

<a id="cover"></a>
## The quotient and its branch values

Put \(t=U-U^{-1}\), \(Z=U+U^{-1}\), and \(a(t)=(t-3)(t-4)\).
The torus equation after division by \(U^2\) is \(a(t)V^2+V+a(-t)=0\).
Thus \(y=2a(t)V+1\) satisfies \(y^2=1-4a(t)a(-t)=H(Z)\), and inversion sends \((t,y)\) to \((-t,-y)\) and fixes \(Z\).
The invariant \(W=ty\) gives the stated model since adjoining \(t\), with \(t^2=Z^2-4\), recovers \(U=(Z+t)/2\), \(y=W/t\) and \(V\) rationally.
The six distinct branch coordinates give genus two, since the roots of \(H\) have squares \((33\pm5\sqrt2)/2\) and \(H(\pm2)=-575\).
A fixed torus point would have \(U,V\in\{1,-1\}\), excluded by the [torsion-point calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion), and inversion exchanges the [boundary zeros and poles](Yin_C_G0345_v854.md#critical) of each coordinate.
This proves that the double cover is étale.

The degrees in the two towers from \(C\) to the \(S\)-line give \(\deg S=\deg T=n\).
Base change from \(S\) to \(T\) recovers \(C\) because \(T\) is moved by inversion and generates the quadratic extension of the function field of \(D\).
Thus geometric monodromy on the \(S\)-line contains the geometric monodromy on the \(T\)-line in the same degree-\(n\) action.
The fibers at \(T=\pm1\) are simple by [squarefreeness](Yin_C_G0345_v852.md#statement), while \(T\mapsto T+T^{-1}\) has local degree two there.
At infinity the inverse boundary pairs consist of one zero and one pole of matching order.
Elsewhere the two vertical double covers are unramified, and the sixteen distinct finite nonzero branch values of the [generic directions](Yin_C_G0345_v855.md#statement) form eight inverse pairs, none fixed by inversion.
These observations give the asserted profiles.

<a id="class"></a>
## The common two-torsion class

On \(C\) the functions \(h=(T-1)/(T+1)\) and \(j=t/(Z+2)\) are anti-invariant and satisfy
\[
 h^2=(S-2)/(S+2),\qquad j^2=(Z-2)/(Z+2).
\]
Their ratio descends to \(D\), with divisor \(E_+-E_--P_2+P_{-2}\), by squaring and using the indicated ramification indices.
The distinct points \(P_2,P_{-2}\) differ by a nonprincipal divisor since a function with one simple pole would give a degree-one map from a genus-two curve to the line.
The square of \(j\) shows that this difference has order two.

Over the algebraic closure the fifteen differences of unordered pairs of distinct Weierstrass points give all fifteen nonzero two-torsion classes.
Indeed their doubles are principal by the branch-coordinate ratios, and possible coincidences between disjoint pairs would give two distinct linearly equivalent degree-two sums of distinct Weierstrass points, after using \(2P_a\sim2P_b\).
A moving degree-two divisor is canonical by Riemann–Roch, and its members are precisely the fibers of \(Z\), which excludes such sums.
Pairs sharing a point are distinguished by the degree-one argument above.
The quartic \(H\) is irreducible because \((33+5\sqrt2)/2\) generates \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) and its norm \(1039/4\) is nonsquare, so its square root has degree four.
Therefore the only Galois-stable unordered pair is \(\{P_2,P_{-2}\}\).
The maps \((Z,W)\mapsto(x,w)=(Z^2,W)\) and \((Z^2,ZW)\) lead to the squarefree cubic and quartic models \(w^2=(x-4)(1-4(x-13)(x-20))\) and \(w^2=x(x-4)(1-4(x-13)(x-20))\), respectively, each with rational point \((4,0)\).
Both send \(P_2,P_{-2}\) to that point.

<a id="factors"></a>
## A quartic-field resultant for each rational factor

For \(\epsilon=\pm1\) choose signs \(\xi,\eta\) with \(\eta^k/\xi^l=\epsilon\), possible by primitivity.
Then \((U,V)=(\xi X^k,\eta X^l)\) parametrizes \(T=\epsilon\) on the ambient torus, with inverse a Laurent monomial in \(U/\xi,V/\eta\) by Bézout.
Inversion acts as \(X\mapsto1/X\), so \(r=X+1/X\) distinguishes its pairs and generates each quotient point's residue field.
The cleared signed section has unit leading and constant coefficients by its four unit corner coefficients and the unique extremal corners in a nonaxis direction.
After monic normalization it is reciprocal since its roots form distinct inverse pairs.
Writing it as \(X^{n/2}Q_\epsilon(X+1/X)\) gives a monic integral squarefree trace polynomial whose rational irreducible factors are the closed points of \(E_\epsilon\).
Let \(B_m\) be the integral polynomial determined by \(B_m(X+1/X)=X^m+X^{-m}\), for \(m\ge0\), so \(Z=\xi B_{|k|}(r)\).

For any root \(b\) of \(H\), put \(L=\mathbb Q(b)\).
For every monic rational factor \(q\mid Q_\epsilon\), the nonzero resultant
\[
 R_b(q)=\operatorname{Res}_r(q(r),\xi B_{|k|}(r)-b)
\]
has even valuation at every prime of \(L\) away from \(2,5,23,1039\).
Indeed each \(r\) is integral and \(b\) is nonintegral since its monic minimal polynomial is \(Z^4-33Z^2+1039/4\).
For an irreducible \(q\), work in each field component of \(L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[r]/(q)\), where the quotient point provides \(W\).
At the stated primes the quartic \(H\) has unit leading coefficient and squarefree reduction, because the two squared roots differ by \(5\sqrt2\) and have product \(1039/4\).
They give branch coordinates distinct from \(\pm2\) since \(H(\pm2)=-575\).
If \(Z-b\) has positive valuation, both \(Z^2-4\) and \((H(Z)/(Z-b))\), evaluated as polynomials at \(Z\), are units there.
The equation for \(W^2\) forces the valuation of \(Z-b\) to be even.
Taking the product of component norms gives the resultant and preserves even valuations, as does multiplication over the irreducible factors of \(q\).

<a id="comparison"></a>
## Comparing the two factorizations

Take distinct quartic branch coordinates \(b,c\) and put \(M=\mathbb Q(b,c)\).
Using the signs belonging to each section in its corresponding resultant, one has in \(M^\times/M^{\times2}\)
\[
 \frac{R_b(Q_+)/R_c(Q_+)}{R_b(Q_-)/R_c(Q_-)}
 =\frac{(2-b)(-2-c)}{(2-c)(-2-b)}.
\]
Indeed \(f=(Z-b)/(Z-c)\) has divisor \(2(P_b-P_c)\), supported away from \(E_\pm,P_{\pm2}\) by the integrality argument.
Writing \(g=h/j\), Weil reciprocity gives \(f(\operatorname{div}g)=g(\operatorname{div}f)=(g(P_b)/g(P_c))^2\).
Evaluation on the divisor computed [above](#class) yields the identity through the product expression for the resultants.
These restrictions apply to proposed factors and to the product over each sign without determining the number of rational factors.
