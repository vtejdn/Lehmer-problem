# The opposite sign between adjacent Chebyshev traces forces two specific coefficient blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## The constant block and the first block that moves with the degree

Write \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
Suppose a sequence of Salem numbers tends to one and each monic trace minimal polynomial \(S_i\), of degree \(m_i=k_i+1\), is strictly interlaced by \(2D_{k_i}-D_{k_i-1}\).
Put \(P_i=z^{m_i}S_i(z+z^{-1})\).
Then \(P_i\to1-z\) coefficientwise.
If \(n_i\) is the first index at which \(P_i\) differs from \(1-z\), then \(n_i\to\infty\), \(n_i\le m_i\) eventually and \(p_i=[z^{n_i}]P_i\) is eventually a sign.
For every fixed \(J\ge2\), eventually
\[
       [z^{n_i+j}]P_i=p_i[z^j](1-z^2)\qquad(0\le j\le J).
\]
In particular this specifies the entire first block even when its index tends to infinity arbitrarily slowly.

<a id="profiles"></a>
## The endpoint condition with the sign reversed

Use the [positive trace weights and symmetric circle lift](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) with \(C=2D_k-D_{k-1}\).
Now \(|C(-2)|=6\), so the inverse endpoint integral is at most six, and the limiting positive-real function \(G\) still vanishes at \(-1\).
Here \(G_i=(1-z^2)C^\sharp/P_i\) with \(C^\sharp=2-z+z^{2k-1}(2z-1)\).
After taking a weakly convergent subsequence as there, the integer coefficients of \(G_i\) and then those of \(P_i\) stabilize in each fixed degree.
Their limits satisfy \(G P_0=(1-z^2)(2-z)\).
Put \(\widehat G(z)=G(-z)\), so its lower polynomial is \(2+z\) and \(\widehat G(1)=0\).

Apply the [numerator and phase argument](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#phase) to \(\widehat G\).
The reflected prefix \(P_0(-z)\) is integral with constant one, and reflection preserves the positive real part and the rationality with simple cyclotomic poles.
Hence \(\widehat G=(2+z)E/F\) with coprime squarefree cyclotomic products normalized at zero.
Writing \(r=-1-\deg E+\deg F\), all their roots belong to those of \(H_r=2+z+\eta z^r(1+2z)\), and \(-3\le r\le1\).
Since \(E(1)=0\), we have \(\eta=-1\).
For \(r=-3,-2,-1,0,1\) the cleared polynomials are respectively, up to scalar,
\[
 (z-1)(z+1)^3,\quad (z-1)(z^2+3z+1),\quad
 (z-1)(z+1),\quad z-1,\quad (z-1)(z+1).
\]
The middle quadratic at \(r=-2\) has no unit roots.
The degree difference and the mandatory factor \(1-z\) therefore allow only \(E/F=1-z^2,\ 1-z,\ (1-z)/(1+z)\), respectively at \(r=-3,-2,-1\).
Returning to the physical variable these give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 P_0 & G & \text{limiting circle measure}\\ \hline
 1 &(2-z)(1-z^2)& |(1-z)^2(1+z)|^2\,dm/2\\
 1-z &(2-z)(1+z)& (2+x)(3-x)\,dm/2\\
 (1-z)^2 &(2-z)(1+z)/(1-z)& |1+z|^2\,dm/2+\delta_1 ,
\end{array}
\]
where \(m\) is Haar probability and \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
The measures follow by comparison of Fourier coefficients, including the mass at one in the last row.

<a id="prediction"></a>
## Excluding the first and third rows

For each row let \(n\) be the first coefficient at which \(P_i\) departs from \(P_0\).
Such a departure occurs by degree \(m_i\) eventually.
Otherwise reciprocity prescribes \(P_i=P_0(z)+z^{2m_i}P_0(1/z)\), which here is \(P_0\) times a binomial with all roots on the circle.
Thus \(n\to\infty\) and coefficient comparison at \(n\) takes place before the upper band of \(C^\sharp\).
The coefficients \(g_{i,j}=[z^j]G_i\) agree with \(g_j=[z^j]G\) for \(j<n\), and \(g_{i,n}-g_n=-2p\) for a nonzero integer \(p\).

In the first and third rows write the continuous density as \(|h|^2/2\) with \(h(0)=1\) as displayed.
There is a real polynomial \(q\) with \(q(0)=1,\ q(1)=0\) and \(\int|hq|^2dm<2\).
Indeed \(h(z)(1-z)/((1-az)h(az))\to1\) in \(L^2\) as \(a\uparrow1\).
Each ratio \((1\pm z)/(1\pm az)\) is uniformly bounded on the circle for \(1/2<a<1\), and the product tends to one almost everywhere.
For fixed \(a<1\) approximate \(1/((1-az)h(az))\) by its Taylor polynomials and multiply by \(1-z\).

Use the [pairing at the first varying coefficient](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#departure) with this \(q\).
In both rows \(g_{n-1}\) stays bounded, giving \(t_i\sigma_i^n=O(1)\) for the exterior trace weight \(t_i\) as in that argument.
The baseline cross pairing at lags from \(n-2\deg q\) to \(n\) vanishes for large \(n\), since the continuous density has finite Fourier support and \(q(1)=0\) kills the possible atom.
The true pairing for the interior measure therefore has modulus at least \(1-o(1)\), using \(q(1)=0\) also in the discarded exterior term.
The squared norms tend to \(\int |hq|^2dm/2<1\), a contradiction.
Only the middle row survives on every extracted subsequence, proving the first assertion.

<a id="moving"></a>
## The moments at the first varying index

Use \(P_0=1-z\), \(G=(2-z)(1+z)\) and let \(w=(2+x)(3-x)/2\) be the circle density.
Write \(\lambda_i\) for the symmetric circle lift of the neutral weights.
The exterior mass \(t_i\) tends to zero since it would otherwise give an atom at one in the full limit.
Consequently \(\lambda_i\to w\,dm\).
Again \(t_i\sigma_i^n\) is bounded, and \(p_i\) is bounded by the coefficient identity at \(n\) and the neutral mass bound.
On any subsequence extract a constant nonzero \(p_i=p\), a limit \(s=\lim t_i\sigma_i^n\), and a weak limit \(\Omega\) of the lifts of \(\lambda_i\) by \(z\mapsto(z,z^n)\).
Define a complex measure \(\eta\) on the first circle by integrating the second coordinate against \(\Omega\).
Its variation is dominated by the marginal \(w\,dm\).
For every fixed integer \(j\) we have
\[
          \lim_i g_{i,n+j}=2\int z^j\,d\eta(z)+s .
\]
For \(j<0\) the left side is zero, by agreement with \(G\) at \(n+j\).
As \(j\to-\infty\) the integrals vanish by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, so \(s=0\).
All the numbers \(u_j=2\int z^j d\eta\) are integer limits and vanish as \(|j|\to\infty\).
Consequently \(U(z)=\sum_j u_j z^j\) is an integer polynomial with \(U(0)=-2p\).
By Fourier uniqueness it is the reflected density of \(2\eta\), and hence satisfies
\[
                 |U(z)|\le (2+x)(3-x)\qquad (|z|=1).
\]
The bound extends from almost every point to the whole circle by continuity.
In particular the double zero of \(2+x\) at \(z=-1\) implies \((1+z)^2\mid U\).

Write \(P_i=P_0+z^n F_i\).
Comparing \(G_iP_i\) with \(GP_0\) at the degrees \(n+j\), for every fixed \(j\ge0\), gives coefficientwise stabilization of \(F_i\) and
\[
                        (2-z)(1+z)F=-(1-z)U,\qquad F\in\mathbb Z[[z]].
\]
Indeed \((G_i-G)/z^n\to U\), \(G_i(0)=2\), and the two numerators agree to degree \(2k_i-2\), beyond \(n+j\) eventually.
Evaluate in the dyadic disk at \(z=2\) to obtain \(U(2)=0\).
Thus \(U=(1+z)^2(2-z)V\) for an integer polynomial \(V\) with \(V(0)=-p\).
On the unit circle,
\[
  |V|^2\le\frac{(3-x)^2}{5-2x}
           =-\frac{x}{2}+\frac74+\frac1{4(5-2x)}.
\]
The integral of \((5-2x)^{-1}=|2-z|^{-2}\) is \(1/3\), by the geometric series at \(1/2\).
Parseval gives \(\sum_j |[z^j]V|^2\le 11/6<2\).
Since \(V(0)\) is a nonzero integer, \(p=\pm1\), \(V=-p\) and \(F=p(1-z^2)\).
Every initial subsequence has a further subsequence with this conclusion, giving the stated eventual block equalities.
