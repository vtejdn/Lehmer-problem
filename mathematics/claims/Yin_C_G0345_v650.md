# Circle moments of short remainders and integral representatives of the same class

<a id="statement"></a>
## Short remainders and unrestricted numerators

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\log M(P)>0\), \(r=e^{-2m}\), \(\sigma=1-r^2\), and use circle Haar probability measure \(\mu\).
For a nonzero polynomial \(T\) of degree less than \(d\), write \(a\) for its lowest nonzero coefficient.
For \(2\le p\le\infty\), with \(1/p+1/p'=1\), one has
\[
   \|T/P\|_p\ge
       \frac{|a|e^{-m}}{(1+r)^{2/p}\sigma^{1/p'}}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus in particular \(\|T/P\|_\infty\ge |a|e^{-m}/(1-e^{-4m})\).
An integral short numerator has \(|a|\ge1\).

For \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) with \(P\nmid A\), take any division \(X^{-j}A=PQ+T\) with integral Laurent quotient and nonzero ordinary remainder of degree less than \(d\).
One may first take a [balanced division](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#division).
Shifting the identity by the lowest exponent of \(T\) gives a division of the same type with \(T(0)=a\), preserving all circle norms.
In that normalization there is a probability \(\nu_P\), depending only on \(P\), for which
\[
 |a|e^{-m}\le (1+r)^{2/p}\sigma^{1/p'}\|A/P\|_p
                           +\sigma\int |Q|\,d\nu_P .                  \tag{2}
\]
For example, if \(\sigma\|Q\|_\infty\le(1-\epsilon)|a|e^{-m}\), then the bound (1) multiplied by \(\epsilon\) holds also for \(A/P\).
Along any sequence with \(m\downarrow0\) and \(\|A/P\|_p=o(m^{-1/p'})\), every such integral quotient must satisfy \(\sigma\int |Q|\,d\nu_P\ge |a|e^{-m}-o(1)\).
The [angular form of this condition](#angular) identifies where the weighted cancellation must occur when \(\sigma\|Q\|_\infty\) stays bounded.

There is a converse construction within each fixed nonzero residue class.
Take any integral Laurent representative \(T\) and write \(h_n\) for the real Laurent coefficients of \(T/P\) on the circle.
Put \(d_n=\operatorname{dist}(h_n,\mathbb Z)\) and \(V=\sum_n d_n(1-d_n)\).
For each fixed \(2\le p<\infty\), the class has an integral Laurent representative \(A\) with
\[
       \|A/P\|_p^p\le C_p(V+V^{p/2}),\qquad
       C_p=(2\lceil p/2\rceil)^{2\lceil p/2\rceil}.                  \tag{3}
\]
At \(p=4\) one can use \(V+3V^2\) as the whole right side.
A [finite frequency interval estimate](#uniform) gives a corresponding upper bound for the supremum norm using the absolute sum of the centered tail.
In particular, if \(p>2\) is fixed and some sequence of classes with \(m\downarrow0\) has \(V=o(m^{-2/p'})\), it has representatives with \(\|A/P\|_p=o(m^{-1/p'})\).
If \(V=O(1/m)\), the upper bound is \(O_p(m^{-1/2})\), matching the order of the [unrestricted quadratic lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement).
Whether either variance condition occurs on a shrinking sequence remains open.

<a id="kernel"></a>
## A squared analytic test

Use the [exterior polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) \(O\) with \(|O|=|P|\) on the circle and \(O(0)=e^m\).
Put \(U=P/O\), \(J=O^*/O\), where \(O^*(z)=z^d\overline{O(1/\bar z)}\), and \(b=J(0)\).
Both functions are analytic and unimodular on the circle, and \(|b|=r\).
After shifting the lowest exponent of the short numerator to zero, \(Y=T/O\) is analytic on the closed disk and \(\bar JY=T/O^*\) has only strictly negative Fourier modes.
It follows that \(Y\) is orthogonal to \(J\) and \(J^2\), since \(J\) is analytic in the disk.
With the circle inner product linear in its first argument, set
\[
 w=(1-\bar bJ)^2,\qquad
 \langle Y,w\rangle=Y(0)=ae^{-m}.
\]
The circle mean of \(J\) is \(b\), so \(\|w\|_1=1-|b|^2=\sigma\).
Also \(\|w\|_\infty\le(1+r)^2\), giving \(\|w\|_{p'}\le(1+r)^{2/p}\sigma^{1/p'}\) by direct integration.
Hölder's inequality proves (1).
For a normalized division, \(UX^{-j}A/P=Y+UQ\).
Taking its inner product with \(w\) gives
\[
   |\langle UQ,w\rangle+ae^{-m}|
           \le (1+r)^{2/p}\sigma^{1/p'}\|A/P\|_p .
\]
The measure \(d\nu_P=|w|\,d\mu/\sigma\) is a probability.
Since \(|U|=1\) on the circle, the last inequality proves (2), including \(p=\infty\).

<a id="angular"></a>
## A common angular set for the remainder and the quotient

Under \(J\), the pushforward of \(\mu\) has density \(\sigma/|1-\bar b\zeta|^2\) with respect to Haar probability measure on the target circle.
Indeed the averages of \(J^k\) are \(b^k\) for \(k\ge0\), and the conjugate moments follow by complex conjugation.
These are the Fourier moments of the displayed density by the two geometric series, and trigonometric polynomials are dense in continuous circle functions.
Consequently \(J\) pushes \(\nu_P\) to Haar probability measure exactly.

For \(0<\delta<1\), take the arc of normalized length \(\delta\) centered at \(b/r\), and let \(E_\delta\) be the preimage under \(J\) of its complement.
Then
\[
 \nu_P(E_\delta^c)=\delta,\qquad
 \mu(E_\delta)\le\frac{\sigma}{4r\sin^2(\pi\delta/2)}.
\]
The second bound uses \(|1-\bar b\zeta|^2=(1-r)^2+4r\sin^2(\arg(\zeta r/b)/2)\).
Therefore the same set has Haar measure \(O_\delta(m)\) and carries \(1-\delta\) of the test weight.
For (2) with the integral restricted to \(E_\delta\), one need only add \(\sigma\delta\|Q\|_\infty\) to its right side.
In particular, on a sequence evading the short rate with \(\sigma\|Q\|_\infty\le D\), the weighted mass \(\sigma\int_{E_\delta}|Q|\,d\nu_P\) is at least \(|a|e^{-m}-D\delta-o(1)\).
For arbitrary quotient size the full weighted identity above gives the cancellation requirement.

A short numerator at the order in (1) also carries part of its norm on \(E_\delta\).
Indeed \(\|w\,1_{E_\delta^c}\|_{p'}\le(1+r)^{2/p}(\sigma\delta)^{1/p'}\).
If \(\|T/P\|_p\le D|a|\sigma^{-1/p'}\) on a shrinking sequence, the contribution to \(\langle Y,w\rangle\) from \(E_\delta^c\) is at most \(D|a|(1+r)^{2/p}\delta^{1/p'}\).
Choosing \(\delta>0\) sufficiently small in terms of \(p,D\) leaves at least \(|a|/2\) for the absolute value of the integral on \(E_\delta\) for all sufficiently small \(m\).
Hölder there yields \(\|1_{E_\delta}T/P\|_p\ge |a|/[2(1+r)^{2/p}\sigma^{1/p'}]\), including the local supremum for \(p=\infty\).

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Unbiased rounding of a fixed Laurent class

Mean-preserving independent Bernoulli rounding is classical and was previously used for finite modular laws. [Raghavan and Thompson, equation (1.1)](../audits/literature/RT87/readme.md#independent-bernoulli-rounding-attribution) [Finite modular rounding](Yin_C_G0054_v1.md#modular-rounding-finite-construction)
Here the variables are all Fourier coefficients of one quotient, and the correction must represent an integral Laurent polynomial in its class.
The rational function \(T/P\) is analytic on an annulus around the circle, so its real coefficients \(h_n\) decay exponentially in both tails.
Independently at every \(n\), choose either adjacent integer \(b_n\) with mean \(h_n\), using \(b_n=h_n\) for an integral coefficient.
Then \(\xi_n=h_n-b_n\) has mean zero, satisfies \(|\xi_n|\le1\), and has variance \(v_n=d_n(1-d_n)\).
Outside a finite set \(|h_n|<1/2\) and \(\Pr(b_n\ne0)=|h_n|\), whose sum is finite.
Thus almost surely only finitely many \(b_n\) are nonzero, and \(A=T-P\sum_n b_nX^n\) is an actual integral Laurent polynomial in the prescribed nonzero class.
Adding an integral Laurent polynomial to \(T/P\) leaves all \(d_n\) unchanged.
The [quadratic minimization by nearest integers](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#rounding) uses the same distances and gives the exact least energy \(\sum_n d_n^2\).
The present random choice controls higher moments of representatives, allowing corrections to both tails as well as the [middle coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails).

For a finite sum \(S(z)=\sum_n\xi_n z^n\) and \(k\ge1\), expand \(|S(z)|^{2k}=S(z)^k\overline{S(z)}^k\).
Every index that occurs once has zero expectation by independence.
For a block of repeated indices its absolute expected contribution is at most \(v_n\), since \(|\xi_n|\le1\) and its multiplicity is at least two.
At most \(k\) blocks occur, and for each partition into \(j\) blocks the sum over distinct assignments of indices is at most \(V^j\).
There are at most \((2k)^{2k}\) partitions.
This bounds the \(2k\)-th moment by \((2k)^{2k}\max(V,V^k)\), uniformly in \(z\).
For \(k=2\) only the fourfold block and three pairings occur, which give \(V+3V^2\).
For \(k=1\) the second moment is exactly the sum of the variances in the finite sum.
Almost surely the Laurent error series converges absolutely, so Fatou's lemma passes the bounds to the full series and Haar integration preserves them.
Interpolation of the second and \(2k\)-th moments on the joint probability space gives (3) for \(k=\lceil p/2\rceil\) (using the second moment directly at \(p=2\)).
In detail the bound between those moments is \(C_p V\) for \(V\le1\) and \(C_p V^{p/2}\) for \(V\ge1\).
Some finite integral representative attains at most the corresponding expected integral.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## Uniform control from a finite frequency interval

For any interval \(I\) of integer frequencies with span \(D\ge0\), let \(V_I=\sum_{n\in I}v_n\), \(\Delta_I=\sum_{n\notin I}d_n\), \(M=\lceil4\pi(D+1)^2\rceil\), and \(L=\log(8M)\).
There exists a representative in the same class satisfying
\[
  \|A/P\|_\infty\le
       \Delta_I+1+2\sqrt2\bigl(\sqrt{V_I L}+L\bigr).                 \tag{4}
\]
Use nearest integers outside \(I\), where the resulting tail has absolute sum \(\Delta_I\), and unbiased rounding on \(I\).
Shift the random error polynomial by a monomial to frequencies \(0,\ldots,D\).
Its angular derivative has absolute value at most \(D(D+1)/2\), so its supremum is at most one plus its maximum on the \(M\) equally spaced angles.
At a given angle each real or imaginary part of the error sum, with either sign, is a sum of independent real centered variables of absolute value at most one and total variance at most \(V_I\).
For \(0<\lambda\le1\), the inequality \(e^x\le1+x+x^2\) for \(|x|\le1\) bounds the moment generating function at \(\lambda\) by \(\exp(\lambda^2V_I)\).
The probability that any of the \(4M\) signed parts exceeds \(\lambda V_I+L/\lambda\) is at most \(4M e^{-L}=1/2\).
Take \(\lambda=\min(1,\sqrt{L/V_I})\), using one for \(V_I=0\).
The modulus is at most \(\sqrt2\) times the larger absolute part, proving (4).

<a id="example"></a>
## Exact supremum in one quadratic residue class

Take \(P=X^2-3X+1\) and \(T=X\).
Writing \(X=e^{it}\) gives \(T/P=-1/(3-2\cos t)\), whose supremum norm is one.
The representative \(X+P=(1-X)^2\) gives \((2-2\cos t)/(3-2\cos t)\), with supremum \(4/5\).
For every integral Laurent polynomial \(B\), its values \(B(1)\) and \(B(-1)\) have the same parity.
If \(\|X/P+B\|_\infty<4/5\), evaluation at \(1\) forces \(B(1)=1\).
At \(-1\), the distance between the odd integer \(B(-1)\) and \(1/5\) is at least \(4/5\), a contradiction.
Thus \(4/5\) is the exact minimum over this class at a fixed positive measure.
The [quadratic energy of classes](Yin_C_G0345_v633.md#statement) and [two-tail determinant estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#statement) address the squared norm.
Obtaining a variance or tail estimate strong enough to decide the unrestricted higher-moment rate as \(m\downarrow0\) remains open.
