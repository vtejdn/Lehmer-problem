# Exterior compression with independent inverse-pair flips

This fixed account concerns [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The coordinating agent proposed the exact perfect-matching formula, the support-count proof, the explicit Salem output and the extension to even flips.
The agent-S contributor checked the actual orbit and measure counts, derived the complete radial-relation test and the odd-exterior refinement, closed the degree-six even-flip case, and wrote this account.
The purpose is to evaluate the general signed-orbit reduction in [C345v304](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#statement) on a concrete large-kernel class.
The matching proof below is self-contained at its stated flip hypotheses; it does not assume the general reduction has supplied a favorable output.

<a id="statement"></a>
## An exact matching formula

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with nontorsion unit roots and exactly \(e\ge1\) exterior roots.
Exterior roots may be real or nonreal, and \(e\le m\).
Choose one root \(x_i\) from each inverse pair, taking its exterior member when the pair is not neutral.
Let \(I=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), let \(E\subset I\) be the exterior labels, and put
\[
 \ell_i=\log|x_i|\ge0,\qquad
 \mu=\log M(P)=\sum_i\ell_i>0.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Let \(G\) be the splitting-field Galois group and \(H\le S_I\) its transitive action on the inverse-pair labels.
Assume initially that \(G\) contains every independent inversion of the \(m\) pairs, fixing all other roots.
Equivalently, its pair kernel contains \(C_2^m\).

Let \(\mathfrak M\) be the set of \(H\)-invariant perfect matchings on \(I\).
For \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M\), define

- \(N_{\rm equal}(\mathcal M)\): the number of matching edges joining the two distinct labels of one nonreal complex-conjugate exterior pair;
- \(N_{\rm single}(\mathcal M)\): the number of matching edges with exactly one exterior endpoint.

Define
\[
 D=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup
     \{N_{\rm equal}(\mathcal M)-N_{\rm single}(\mathcal M):
                                      \mathcal M\in\mathfrak M\}\bigr),
 \qquad \nu=e-D.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(D=0\) when no invariant perfect matching exists.
If \(\Gamma\) is generated by all roots of \(P\), and
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma=
 \{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:y^N\in\mu_\infty\Gamma
                              \text{ for some }N\ge1\},
\]
then
\[
 \min_{y\in\widetilde\Gamma\text{ nontorsion}}
                b(y)=\nu,                                      \tag{3}
\]
where \(b(y)\) counts distinct exterior conjugates of the actual minimal polynomial.
No reciprocity, reality, degree, exponent or radical-denominator restriction is imposed on the output \(y\).

If \(D=0\), an original root attains (3).
If \(D>0\), choose a maximizing matching and any one of its edges \(\{i,j\}\).
An ordinary product \(\beta=x_i x_j\) attains (3), has a reciprocal minimal polynomial of degree exactly \(2m\), and satisfies
\[
 \log M(\beta)=2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal M}\max(\ell_i,\ell_j)
                       \le2\mu.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
Thus this compression may decrease the exterior count while retaining the original degree.

The same assertions (2)–(4) hold under the weaker even-flip hypothesis provided
\[
                  m\ge3,\qquad 1\le e<m:
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
it is enough that \(G\) contain all inversions of an even number of inverse pairs.
The proof of this extension is given separately below.
The degree-six case is included for both reciprocal and unrestricted radical outputs.
The case \(e=m\) without the full-flip hypothesis remains outside this even-flip extension.

<a id="independence"></a>
## Independence, conjugation and the only radial relations

Under full flips, any torsion monomial relation \(\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\in\mu_\infty\) becomes, after applying the \(i\)-th flip and dividing, a torsion power \(x_i^{2a_i}\).
No root is torsion, so every \(a_i=0\).
The inverse-pair representatives are therefore multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
Different integer exponent vectors give different actual monomials, even modulo torsion.
Every Galois orbit of monomials can consequently be counted by its signed exponent vectors.

The same independence holds with even flips when \(m\ge3\).
For distinct \(i,j\), a double flip gives \(x_i^{2a_i}x_j^{2a_j}\) torsion.
Using the pairs \(\{i,j\},\{i,k\},\{j,k\}\) for three distinct indices, multiply the first two relations and divide by the third.
This makes \(x_i^{4a_i}\) torsion, forcing \(a_i=0\).

Let \(\tau\) be ordinary complex conjugation and let \(c\) be its permutation on exterior labels.
It fixes each real exterior label and exchanges exactly the two labels of each nonreal exterior conjugate pair.
On these labels \(\tau x_i=x_{c(i)}\), because both chosen roots are exterior.
For rational coefficients \(a_i\) supported on \(E\), clear denominators and use the positive real product with its conjugate to obtain the following identity, where \(e_i\) denotes the coordinate basis vector:
\[
 \sum_{i\in E}a_i\ell_i=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{i\in E}a_i(e_i+e_{c(i)})=0
                       \quad\text{in }\mathbb Q^I.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Indeed a zero logarithm means that \(\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\) has modulus one, so its product with its complex conjugate equals one; independence gives the vector identity.
Conversely that identity makes the positive product one and hence its logarithm zero.
Thus the logarithms of the real exterior radii, together with one logarithm from each nonreal exterior pair, are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
The only exterior radial relations are equality within these nonreal pairs.
In particular, for distinct exterior labels and positive integers \(a,b\),
\[
 a\ell_i=b\ell_j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 c(i)=j,\ c(j)=i,\ a=b.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

For an output \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\), raise to a positive power to remove both the radical denominator and the torsion multiplier:
\[
                   w=y^N=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\in\Gamma.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
If \(y\) is nontorsion, its exponent vector is nonzero.
Every exterior conjugate of \(w\) lifts to an exterior conjugate of \(y\), so \(b(y)\ge b(w)\).
Both are algebraic units, since integrality of a unit and its inverse passes to any algebraic radical.
Under full flips, inversion of all coordinates also makes the actual orbit of \(w\) reciprocal.
The lower bounds below count positive logarithms directly, so this last observation will not be needed for the even-flip extension.

<a id="support"></a>
## Supports of at least three labels cannot improve the input count

Let \(s\) be the support size of the exponent vector in (8).
For full flips, on each support in its \(H\)-orbit all \(2^s\) independent sign choices occur, with fixed positive coefficient magnitudes on that support.
There may be more coefficient assignments, which only increase the actual orbit.
Different supports or different exponent vectors give distinct conjugates by independence.

Fix one such support \(A\), and let \(\ell=|A\cap E|\).
If \(\ell=0\), all these values are neutral.
If \(\ell>0\), condition on every sign except one active sign.
At most one of its two choices can make the logarithmic sum zero.
Thus at most \(2^{s-1}\) sign sums are zero.
The distribution is symmetric under reversing all signs, so at least \(2^{s-2}\) sums are positive.
For \(s\ge4\),
\[
                           2^{s-2}\ge s\ge\ell.                  \tag{9}
\]
For \(s=3\) and \(\ell\le2\), the same bound gives at least \(2\ge\ell\) positives.
For \(s=\ell=3\), (6) makes a zero sum impossible: three active coordinates cannot be partitioned into nonreal conjugate pairs with cancelling coefficients.
There are then four positive sums, in particular at least three.
Alternatively the weaker elementary bound of at most two zero sums among three positive summands already suffices.

The \(H\)-orbit of \(A\) is a regular set system on the \(m\) labels.
Let \(d_A\ge1\) be its common incidence number, the number of these supports containing any one label.
Summing the lower bound \(\ell\) over supports gives
\[
 b(w)\ge\sum_{A'\in H A}|A'\cap E|=d_A e\ge e.
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
For \(s=1\), transitivity and both inversion signs give exactly one exterior conjugate on each exterior label, and hence \(b(w)=e\).

There is an exact zero-count refinement of this argument.
At fixed coefficient magnitudes, if the \(\ell>0\) active labels cannot be partitioned into nonreal conjugate pairs with equal magnitudes within each pair, no sign sum is zero by (6).
If they do so partition, write \(\ell=2k\).
Each pair must have opposite signs, so precisely \(2^{s-k}\) of the \(2^s\) sums are zero and
\[
               2^{s-1}(1-2^{-k})
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
are positive.
This refinement is not needed to assume any extra independence: it follows from the actual flip subgroup and conjugation.

<a id="edges"></a>
## Two-label supports and invariant perfect matchings

Suppose \(s=2\).
The \(H\)-orbit of its unordered support is a simple regular graph of some valency \(d\ge1\) on \(I\).
Fix one weighted coefficient assignment on each edge and all its four sign choices.
For an edge with \(\ell\) exterior endpoints, the positive count is

| Exterior endpoints | Positive sign choices |
| --- | --- |
| None | \(0\) |
| Exactly one | \(2\) |
| Two with unequal weighted logarithms | \(2\) |
| Two with equal weighted logarithms | \(1\) |

In all cases this is at least \(\ell/2\).
If \(d\ge2\), summing over edges therefore gives
\[
                              b(w)\ge de/2\ge e.                 \tag{12}
\]
Thus any improvement must come from \(d=1\), which is exactly an \(H\)-invariant perfect matching \(\mathcal M\).

For such a matching, equality of two weighted exterior logarithms is permitted only on an edge counted by \(N_{\rm equal}\), and only with equal coefficient magnitudes, by (7).
Relative to the original exterior-label count, a single-exterior edge contributes an extra one, an equal edge can save at most one, and every other edge saves nothing.
Hence every weighted monomial supported on this matching has
\[
              b(w)\ge e+N_{\rm single}(\mathcal M)
                                -N_{\rm equal}(\mathcal M).
                                                               \tag{13}
\]
Together with (10) and (12), this proves \(b(y)\ge\nu\) for every radical output.
Taking nonreciprocal radicals cannot avoid it, because (8) passes to a counted monomial before imposing any reciprocal assumption on the output.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Products attaining the bound and their exact measure

An invariant perfect matching is a single \(H\)-orbit of edges.
Indeed transitivity on labels carries the unique edge at one label to the unique edge at any other label.
For one edge choose \(\beta=x_i x_j\).
Its exponent orbit consists exactly of the four sign choices on every edge of \(\mathcal M\).
There are \(m/2\) edges, hence \(2m\) distinct actual conjugates.
They are closed under inversion, so the minimal polynomial is irreducible, monic integral and reciprocal of degree \(2m\).
The unweighted product realizes equality in every row of the edge table, by (7).
Consequently
\[
                 b(\beta)=e+N_{\rm single}-N_{\rm equal}.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
Choose a maximizing matching when \(D>0\); if \(D=0\), use an original root instead.
This proves attainment in (3).

The four logarithms over one matching edge are
\[
       \ell_i+\ell_j,\quad\ell_i-\ell_j,
              \quad-\ell_i+\ell_j,\quad-\ell_i-\ell_j.
\]
Their positive parts sum to
\[
        \ell_i+\ell_j+|\ell_i-\ell_j|=2\max(\ell_i,\ell_j).
\]
Summing proves (4), using the actual degree and all distinct conjugates.
The formula covers neutral endpoints and the two neutral sign choices on an equal exterior edge.

<a id="even"></a>
## The same formula from even flips and a neutral pair

Assume (5) and only the even-flip subgroup.
Independence and the radial criterion (6) were already proved above for \(m\ge3\).
Whenever \(s<m\), an unused coordinate can absorb the parity of any chosen sign assignment on the support.
All \(2^s\) sign choices therefore occur, and the entire support and edge argument remains valid.
In particular pair products attaining (14) have exactly the same orbit because \(2<m\).

It remains to examine \(s=m\).
Choose one orbit under the even-flip subgroup; it contains exactly \(2^{m-1}\) distinct exponent vectors.
Since \(e<m\), at least one neutral coordinate can absorb parity.
Every sign assignment on the \(e\) active coordinates occurs exactly \(2^{m-1-e}\) times in this parity class.
The active logarithmic distribution is therefore symmetric.
The one-active-sign argument from the support proof gives at least
\[
                               2^{m-3}
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
positive logarithms in this orbit.
For \(m\ge5\), this is at least \(m-1\ge e\).
For \(m=4,e\le2\) it is again at least \(e\).
For \(m=4,e=3\), (6) excludes a zero active sign sum, so four of the eight active assignments are positive, more than the required three.
Thus a full-support monomial cannot improve the original count when \(m\ge4\).
This reasoning does not assert global inversion in the actual Galois group when \(m\) is odd.
It uses parity absorption and counts positive logarithms directly.

<a id="degree-six"></a>
## Complex conjugation closes the degree-six even-flip case

It remains to treat \(m=3\) with \(1\le e<3\).
For \(e=1\), each sign on the active coordinate occurs twice in a full-support even-flip orbit, so that orbit has two exterior values, at least the required one.
Proper supports were already covered above.

Now let \(e=2\).
If both exterior roots are real, complex conjugation fixes them and flips just the one neutral pair.
Conjugating this single flip by the transitive label action gives all three individual flips.
The full-flip result then applies.

Otherwise choose \(x_1,x_2\) to be the nonreal conjugate exterior roots and \(x_3\) on the neutral pair.
Thus \(\ell_1=\ell_2=L>0\), \(\ell_3=0\), and on exponent vectors
\[
                         \tau(a,b,u)=(b,a,-u).                    \tag{15a}
\]
Consider a full-support vector, so \(abu\ne0\).
If \(|a|\ne|b|\), its four-vector even-flip orbit realizes all four active sign choices.
The four logarithms \(L(\pm|a|\pm|b|)\) are nonzero, and exactly two are positive.
This already gives two distinct exterior conjugates.

If \(|a|=|b|\), that one parity orbit can have only one positive logarithm.
However, (15a) preserves the coordinatewise absolute values and changes the sign of the product \(abu\).
It therefore supplies the other parity class on the same three coefficient magnitudes.
Together with the even flips, the actual Galois orbit contains all eight sign vectors.
Their logarithms have two positive, four zero and two negative values, counted as distinct monomials by independence.
Hence the actual orbit again has at least two exterior conjugates.
This resolves the loss in the single-parity estimate without assuming an odd flip in the pair kernel.

Proper supports are covered by the preceding support and edge bounds, and three labels admit no perfect matching.
The original reciprocal input has two exterior conjugates and attains the bound.
Consequently at \(m=3,e=2\) the minimum is exactly two, both over all nontorsion radical outputs and over the reciprocal subfamily.
There is no nonreciprocal one-exterior exception.
This completes (3)–(4) throughout the even-flip range (5).

When \(e=m\), there is no neutral coordinate to absorb parity, and that case remains outside the even-flip extension.

<a id="consequences"></a>
## No bounded-exterior compression when the input count grows

Each equal edge uses two exterior labels, so \(D\le\lfloor e/2\rfloor\).
Under either stated flip hypothesis,
\[
                              \nu\ge\lceil e/2\rceil.            \tag{16}
\]
If \(e\ge3\) is odd, every perfect matching has \(N_{\rm single}\ge1\), and
\[
 N_{\rm equal}\le(e-N_{\rm single})/2.
\]
Thus the stronger odd-count bound is
\[
                              \nu\ge(e+3)/2.                    \tag{17}
\]
When no perfect matching exists the conclusion follows from \(\nu=e\).
In particular three exterior roots cannot be compressed to two in this class.

If the label action \(H\) is primitive and \(m>2\), an invariant perfect matching would be a nontrivial block system.
It cannot occur, so \(\nu=e\).
The same conclusion holds whenever \(m\) is odd.
These conclusions concern all radical outputs, not only products with bounded exponents.

A one-exterior output exists exactly in the following cases:

- \(e=1\), when the original root already has that count;
- \(e=2\), the exterior roots are a nonreal conjugate pair, and their two labels form an edge of an \(H\)-invariant perfect matching.

In the second case the matching product has measure exactly \(M(P)\), by (4), and is positive at its unique exterior conjugate.
A reciprocal unit with one exterior root is a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit after a rational sign change.
Thus this gives the precise Salem-or-quadratic criterion in the stated classes, with no claim of a smaller-degree output.

Likewise a radical output with at most two exterior conjugates exists exactly when \(e\le2\), or when \(e=4\) and an invariant perfect matching simultaneously joins both nonreal conjugate exterior pairs.
For the latter case the matching product has two exterior roots and measure exactly \(M(P)\).
Equations (16)–(17) exclude every other exterior count.

Consequently any hypothetical sequence in this class with \(e\to\infty\) remains outside every bounded-exterior compression target in the general reduction.
This is an obstruction conditional on the supplied inputs, not a construction of inputs with Mahler measure tending to one.
Neither a universal positive gap nor automatic presence of the assumed flip subgroup is asserted.

## The earlier degree-eight input and its explicit Salem output

The [degree-eight example in L462v20](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v20.md#nonreal-block-example) already supplies
\[
 T(t)=((t-1)^2+2)^2-5,\qquad P(z)=z^4T(z+z^{-1}),
                                                               \tag{18}
\]
with irreducibility, all four independent pair flips, and two nonreal exterior conjugates.
Its other two trace conjugates are in \((-2,2)\).
The necessary input argument is retained below from that ledger example, with its accepted Hensel statement used at the same scope.
The ledger's earlier independent review concerns its fixed input and is not an independent review of this new complete account.
The trace blocks form an invariant perfect matching; the nonreal exterior pair occupies one edge, the neutral labels the other.
Thus \(N_{\rm equal}=1\), \(N_{\rm single}=0\), and \(\nu=1\).
The ledger already proves that the edge product has degree eight and measure exactly \(M(P)\), so these numerical counts and costs are not new examples.

<a id="example-input"></a>
### Irreducibility and the full flip kernel of the input

The trace roots are \(1\pm\sqrt{-2+\sqrt5}\) in \((-2,2)\) and \(1\pm i\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}\).
For the first real root \(t_1=1+\sqrt{-2+\sqrt5}\), the field \(\mathbb Q(t_1)\) contains \(\sqrt5\).
The norm of \(-2+\sqrt5\) to \(\mathbb Q\) is \(-1\), so it is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
This proves irreducibility of \(T\) and gives the block partition according to the two values of \((t-1)^2+2\).

Let \(F\) be the splitting field of \(T\), and put \(u_i=t_i^2-4\).
Modulo \(29\), the polynomial has four distinct roots \(-2,4,-3,5\).
By the [simple-root Hensel theorem](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001), these lift to four roots in \(\mathbb Q_{29}\), giving an embedding of \(F\) there.
Only one \(t_i\) reduces to \(-2\), and none reduces to \(2\).
Since \(T(-2)=116=4\cdot29\), that factor \(t_i+2\) has valuation one, by taking the product over all four roots.
Thus precisely one \(u_i\) has valuation one and the others have valuation zero.
Conjugating this valuation under the transitive trace group gives an individual odd valuation for every \(u_i\).
Their four square classes in \(F^\times/F^{\times2}\) are therefore independent.

Adjoining their square roots gives every independent sign flip.
Indeed square-root monomials for an independent list form a basis with distinct sign characters.
If an element of that extension has nonzero square in \(F\), every existing sign flip takes it to itself or its negative, so it lies in one character line and is a scalar times one basis monomial.
An additional independent square class cannot already have a square root in the extension.
This proves the assertion inductively.
The roots \(z=(t_i\pm\sqrt{u_i})/2\) are thus interchanged independently.
Trace transitivity and these individual flips give irreducibility of \(P\).
Its reciprocal roots are integral units.
The nonreal trace pair supplies precisely two nonreal exterior roots; the real trace pair lies in \((-2,2)\) and supplies four neutral roots.

Only the simple-root specialization of [Con26e-U001](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) is used: Theorem 4.1 on p. 6, equivalently Theorem 9.1 on p. 18 over \(\mathbb Q_{29}\), with a unit derivative at each distinct residue root.
Its retained statement/applicability acceptance is unchanged; no new full proof audit of Hensel's lemma is claimed.

### The explicit product polynomial

The product's minimal polynomial can be written explicitly.
Within a trace block let \(t_1+t_2=2\) and \(p=t_1t_2=3\pm\sqrt5\).
For pair representatives \(x_1,x_2\), the two product traces
\[
 Y_1=x_1x_2+(x_1x_2)^{-1},\qquad
 Y_2=x_1/x_2+x_2/x_1
\]
have sum \(p\) and product \(t_1^2+t_2^2-4=-2p\).
They satisfy \(Y^2-p(Y+2)=0\).
Taking both values of \(p\) gives
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{tr}}(Y)
 =[Y^2-3(Y+2)]^2-5(Y+2)^2
 =Y^4-6Y^3-8Y^2+16Y+16.
                                                               \tag{19}
\]
The actual product orbit already has eight elements, so its minimal polynomial is the degree-eight lift
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q(z)&=z^4Q_{\mathrm{tr}}(z+z^{-1})\\
     &=z^8-6z^7-4z^6-2z^5+6z^4-2z^3-4z^2-6z+1.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{20}
\]
For \(p_-=3-\sqrt5\in(0,1)\), both roots of \(Y^2-p_-(Y+2)\) lie in \((-2,2)\), as its values at \(-2,0,2\) have signs \(+,-,+\).
For \(p_+=3+\sqrt5>5\), its negative root is in \((-2,0)\), and its positive root exceeds two.
Thus (20) is a genuine Salem polynomial.
Its degree is the same as that of (18), and its measure is not close to one.
If \(\lambda>1\) is the common exterior radius, then a nonreal trace satisfies \(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\ge|\Im t_i|=\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}>2\), so \(M(P)=\lambda^2>(1+\sqrt2)^2\).
This explicit control identifies the already available matching output; it is not a new shrinking family.

## Scope relative to retained results

The [C345v54 matching argument](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#matching) already proves the sufficient Salem-or-quadratic reduction for two equal exterior moduli when the exterior-edge orbit is a matching.
The present calculation adds necessity under the flip hypotheses and excludes every larger support and every radical escape, uniformly over arbitrary \(e\).
The [balanced imaginary-quadratic class in C345v69](Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#field) has a nonzero full exponent relation and therefore lies outside the independent-flip situation proved here.
Its ramification mechanism and examples are not premises of the matching formula.

The [Salem monomial counts in C866](Yin_C_G0866_v1.md#salem-monomial-orbits) and the [Salem flip kernel in C383](Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) already contain the signed-support and unused-coordinate parity method for the one-real-exterior signature.
The [L462v20 block calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v20.md#salem-blocks) records its actual degree and measure cost, and its separate nonreal example gives the benchmark above.
Here the complex-conjugation pair structure supplies the exact weighted cancellation criterion and hence the full matching evaluation for general exterior signatures.

The adjacent [C345v304](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#statement) gives a finite signed-subset minimization for arbitrary reciprocal inputs at its own fixed scope.
This account evaluates that minimum in the full-flip class and, under (5), the even-flip class, while also giving the same lower bound for nonreciprocal radical outputs.
It identifies a substantial regime in which growing exterior count cannot be reduced to a bounded count by any root-generated radical construction.
The remaining task is not another enumeration of larger monomial exponents: it is to overcome this obstruction, leave the radical group, or obtain information ruling out shrinking inputs in the indicated class.
