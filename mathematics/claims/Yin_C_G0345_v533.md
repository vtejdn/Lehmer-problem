# A ceiling on cyclotomic norm transfer through arbitrary powered orders

<a id="statement"></a>
## The available norm per output degree

Let \(P\) be a monic nontorsion unit minimum of degree \(e\), and put \(h=\log M(P)\).
Let \(S\) be any nonempty finite set of positive integer exponents, \(r=|S|\) and \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\).
Write \(D_n\) for the part prime to \(n\) of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n)|\).
For each input order \(n\) and output order \(t\), let \(m_{nt}\) count the distinct residues modulo \(n\) represented by exponents \(p\in S\) with \(n/\gcd(n,p)=t\).
This is the full number of distinct primitive \(t\)-th roots obtainable by powering one primitive \(n\)-th root with the available slots.
For any finitely supported nonnegative real weights \(a_t\), put \(B=\sum_t a_t\varphi(t)>0\).
Then
\[
 \frac1B\sum_{n,t} a_t m_{nt}\log D_n
       \le h\Sigma+er\log 2.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The same bound holds for the complete absolute resultants, including primes dividing their orders.
One may choose the orders, weights and contributing opposite pairs after seeing the seed and the exponent schedule.

In particular, consider the [weighted component argument](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#mass) which credits each input component its number of distinct output roots and uses the common seed degree \(e\) to account for overlaps.
Even before subtracting rational content or the error in the [circle estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#circle), its credited lower-bound term for primitive logarithmic measure, normalized by \(\Sigma\), is at most
\[
                k h/e + k(r/\Sigma)\log 2.                          \tag{2}
\]
Here \(k\) is the contact multiplicity and each output resultant is used at a nonvanishing order.
Indeed the idealized credit is at most \(k\sum a_t m_{nt}\log D_n/(eB)\): each component has at most \(m_{nt}\) allowed images, and the coefficient of the unknown measure in the weighted archimedean resultant estimate is \(B\).
This also covers projection to proper divisors of sum periods and arbitrary common dilations.
In the double-contact case \(k=2\), along schedules with \(\min S\to\infty\) and \(h/e\to0\), this term tends to zero even when arbitrarily many projected orders are used simultaneously.
The conclusion concerns precisely this source of a lower bound with the global degree loss. Component-specific overlap estimates or independent restrictions on the rows require a separate argument.

<a id="pullback"></a>
## Counting every input slot before taking unions

For each \(p,t\) the roots of \(\Phi_t(X^p)\) are simple and regroup by exact order to give
\[
         \Phi_t(X^p)=\prod_{n:\,n/\gcd(n,p)=t}\Phi_n(X).
\]
The set of indices is finite since every such \(n\) divides \(pt\).
The residue count \(m_{nt}\) is at most the number of slots in this fiber.
All the logarithmic norms are nonnegative because the resultants are nonzero integers.
Counting slots first and using multiplicativity therefore bounds the sum for each \(t\) by \(\sum_{p\in S}\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_t(X^p))|\).
For every conjugate \(\alpha\) and every primitive \(t\)-th root \(\zeta\), one has \(|\alpha^p-\zeta|\le 2\max(1,|\alpha|)^p\).
Taking the product yields the upper bound \(\varphi(t)(\Sigma h+er\log 2)\) and proves (1).

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Sharpness of the slot count and a fixed-order example with opposite pairs

Fix \(P\) and any finite collection of positive output orders with fixed weights as above.
Take any nonempty set of primes \(p\in[L,2L]\) for \(S\).
For \(L\) larger than every output order, the indices \(tp\) for all pairs \((t,p)\) are distinct, and the exponent \(p\) sends order \(tp\) to order \(t\).
The [prime-to-order norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) gives \(\log D_{tp}=\varphi(t)(p-1)h+O_{P,t}(\log(2p))\), since \(p\nmid t\) and the number of distinct prime factors of \(tp\) stays bounded.
Consequently the left side of (1) equals \((h+o(1))\Sigma\), uniformly in the choice and number of these primes.
This establishes sharpness of the leading coefficient in the unrestricted slot count.

For an example using actual opposite pairs, choose an even multiple \(M\ge4\) of all these fixed output orders and take the two odd exponents \(p,(M-1)p\) for an odd prime \(p\to\infty\).
For every selected \(t\) the order \(tp\) divides their sum \(Mp\), and the first exponent has projected order \(t\).
Thus even counting only this first image in each genuine [opposite-pair inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement) supplies normalized input mass \((h/M+o(1))\Sigma\) after division by \(B\).
In both examples the seed is fixed throughout the limit.
