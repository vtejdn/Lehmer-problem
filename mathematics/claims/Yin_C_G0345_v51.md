# Positive trace residues occupy every open set of independent moving phases

<a id="statement"></a>
## An integer density and a quantitative lower bound for the neutral mass

Let \(\sigma_i\to1\) be Salem numbers with monic trace minimal polynomials \(S_i\).
For each \(i\), let an integer polynomial \(A_i\) strictly interlace \(S_i\), with degree \(\deg S_i-1\) and fixed positive leading coefficient \(c\).
Set \(c_x=A_i(x)/S_i'(x)>0\) at every trace root, \(T_i=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\), and \(t_i=c_{T_i}\).
The weights sum to \(c\).
For each neutral trace \(x=u+u^{-1}\), put mass \(c_x/2\) at each of its two circle lifts \(u,u^{-1}\), defining a measure \(\lambda_i\).

Take any fixed \(r\ge1\) and vector of integer exponents \(\mathbf n_i\in\mathbb Z^r\).
Assume that for each nonzero \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^r\), the integer \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n_i\) is eventually nonzero.
Write \(B_i=\|\mathbf n_i\|_\infty\) and map \(u\) to \(\mathbf z=(u^{n_{i,1}},\ldots,u^{n_{i,r}})\).
In either of the following cases, every weak limit of the pushforwards of \(2\lambda_i\) has a nonzero absolutely continuous part \(f\,dm\), where \(m\) is Haar probability on \(\mathbb T^r\) and \(f\) is a nonnegative Laurent polynomial with integer coefficients.

1. \(B_i\log\sigma_i\to0\).
2. \(n_{i,1}=1\), the measures \(\lambda_i\) converge to a measure with bounded density against circle Haar probability, and for every fixed \(j\ge1\) there is a finite \(a_j\ge0\) with \(t_i\sigma_i^{jB_i}=O_j((\sigma_i-1)^{-a_j})\).

In both cases \(\int\log f\,dm\ge0\).
In particular for every fixed open set \(V\subseteq\mathbb T^r\) with \(0<\alpha=m(V)<1\), the actual neutral masses satisfy
\[
 \liminf_{i\to\infty}\lambda_i\{u:(u^{n_{i,j}})_j\in V\}
 \ \ge\ \frac{\alpha}{2}
       \left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2c}\right)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}.
\]
The bound depends only on the leading coefficient and the Haar measure of the test set.
The exponents in the first case need no degree bound, and neither case requires sparse interlacers.

<a id="integrality"></a>
## Integral moments on the torus

Start with a convergent subsequence and extract also \(t_i\to t\in[0,c]\).
As in the [positive trace identity](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit), the weighted sum of any integer polynomial in \(x\) is an integer.
Indeed it is the coefficient of degree \(\deg S_i-1\) in the remainder of \(A_i\) times that polynomial modulo \(S_i\).
In particular for every \(h\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
 g_{i,h}=2\int u^h\,d\lambda_i(u)+t_i(\sigma_i^h+\sigma_i^{-h})
               \in\mathbb Z ,
\]
including \(g_{i,0}=2c\).
Write \(\Lambda\) for the weak torus limit of \(2\lambda_i\).
In case 1, every Fourier coefficient of \(\Lambda+2t\delta_{\boldsymbol1}\) is integral, by taking \(h=\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n_i\) for each fixed \(\mathbf v\).
Here \(\sigma_i^h\) and \(\sigma_i^{-h}\) both tend to one.

In case 2 all Fourier coefficients of \(\Lambda\) itself are integers.
Fix a character in the last \(r-1\) coordinates and project its product with \(\Lambda\) to the first circle.
Write \(b(z)\) for the resulting density, bounded because the first marginal has bounded density.
Use either sign convention for the character, and put \(h_i=\sum_{j=2}^r v_j n_{i,j}\) for the exponent it contributes.
For a sufficiently large fixed integer \(M\), every Fourier coefficient of \((1-z)^M b\) is the limit of an integer linear combination of \(g_{i,h_i+l}\) with \(l\) in a fixed finite interval.
Indeed expansion of the filter gives the corresponding neutral coefficients exactly.
Its exterior error is a sum of terms bounded by a constant times \(t_i\sigma_i^{|h_i|}|\sigma_i-1|^M\).
The power bound, with \(j\ge\max(1,\sum_{j'=2}^r|v_{j'}|)\), makes this error vanish.
The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma now makes all but finitely many of these integer coefficients zero.
By Fourier uniqueness \((1-z)^M b\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
Its zero at one has multiplicity at least \(M\) since \(b\) is bounded.
Division by \((1-z)^M\) in the integral Laurent ring yields integer Fourier coefficients for \(b\), proving the assertion for every character.
This is the finite-difference argument for [higher trace moments](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#rows) with an arbitrary finite vector of exponents.

<a id="nonvanishing"></a>
## The absolutely continuous part is nonzero

Apply the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) on the discrete group \(\mathbb Z^r\) to the measure with integer Fourier coefficients in each case.
The singleton cosets give an integer Laurent polynomial density \(f\).
Every other coset term has inverse transform supported on the annihilator of a nonzero subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^r\), as in the [torus decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#classification).
These are Haar-null and there are only finitely many.
Positivity gives \(f\ge0\) and \(\int f\,dm\le2c\).
Removing the point mass added in case 1 does not change the density.

If \(f=0\), choose one nonzero vector \(\mathbf v\) from each of the finitely many nonzero subgroups and put \(Q(\mathbf z)=\prod_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf z^{\mathbf v}-1)\).
The product vanishes on the singular support of the limiting measure and hence on that of \(\Lambda\).
Set \(Q_i(u)=Q((u^{n_{i,j}})_j)\) and define \(L_i(x)=Q_i(u)Q_i(u^{-1})\) for \(x=u+u^{-1}\).
This is an integer polynomial since it is a symmetric integer Laurent polynomial in \(u\).
Every factor of \(Q_i\) is nonzero at both \(\sigma_i\) and \(\sigma_i^{-1}\) eventually by the hypothesis on the exponents.
For any integer \(D\ge1\),
\[
              \sum_{x:S_i(x)=0} c_x(2-x)^{2D} L_i(x)^2
\]
is a strictly positive integer by the remainder formula and the exterior term.
In case 1 use instead \(D=0\).
The neutral contribution then tends to zero by weak convergence, and the exterior contribution tends to zero because \(Q_i(\sigma_i^{\pm1})\to Q(\boldsymbol1)=0\) for a nonempty product.
If the singular support is empty in case 1, the augmented measure would have zero total mass, which is impossible.

In case 2 the neutral integrand is a fixed continuous function of the torus coordinates, using \(z_1=u\), and it again vanishes in the weak limit.
The product of absolute values of all binomials in \(L_i(T_i)^2\) is bounded by \(O_Q(\sigma_i^{jB_i})\) for some fixed \(j\).
Since \(T_i-2=(\sigma_i-1)^2/\sigma_i\), choose \(D\) large enough for the exterior term to tend to zero as well.
The same argument for an empty product uses \(L_i=1\).
This contradicts integrality in both cases and proves nonvanishing.

<a id="mass"></a>
## A bound in terms of the measure of the test set

The logarithmic Mahler measure of a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial is nonnegative.
For the present \(f\) this follows by iterated one-variable Jensen inequalities down to a nonzero integer leading coefficient, after clearing Laurent monomials.
Thus \(0\le\int\log f\,dm\), and \(\int f\,dm\le2c\).
If \(b=\int_V f\,dm\), Jensen's inequality separately on \(V\) and its complement gives
\[
    0\le\alpha\log(b/\alpha)+(1-\alpha)\log(2c/(1-\alpha)).
\]
Solving for \(b\), dividing by two and applying the open-set inequality for weak convergence yields the assertion.
This holds on a subsequence realizing the lower limit because every further weakly convergent subsequence obeys the same bound.
