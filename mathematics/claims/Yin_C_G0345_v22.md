# Uniform Pisot excess near every nonconstant real trace prefix with exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## A coefficient neighborhood in fixed maximum degree

Fix \(N\ge1\), \(M<\infty\) and \(\eta>0\).
Let \(S\in\mathbb R[w]\) be nonconstant of degree at most \(N\), with \(S(0)=1\) and all roots of modulus strictly greater than one.
There are \(\varepsilon,c>0\), depending only on \(S,N,M,\eta\), with the following property.
Let \(S_i\in\mathbb R[w]\) have constant one, degree at most \(N\) and maximum coefficient distance less than \(\varepsilon\) from \(S\), padding both coefficient vectors by zeros through \(N\).
Consider any monic Pisot minimal polynomial of positive constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\), branch index \(j\ge1\), and [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(C\) of degree \(h\).
Put \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M\) on the unit circle and the Taylor coefficients of \(\Pi/j\) through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) agree with those of
\[
                     G(z)S_i(z^g),\qquad 1\le g\le h,\qquad
                     G\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]].
\]
Then \(\theta-q\ge c\), with arbitrary \(G\), either parity of the input degree, and no height or degree restriction on the input beyond these hypotheses.
The conclusion holds uniformly on a neighborhood of any compact set of such centers in the fixed real coefficient space.
For an exact rational \(S\) with a fractional coefficient, the [fixed rational criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) in addition permits roots on the unit circle.

<a id="seam"></a>
## Local coefficients at the first nonintegral term

Use the [real-coefficient argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#exterior) and suppose that \(S_i\to S\) while \(\theta-q\to0\).
The [varying-index theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) puts the first nonintegral coefficient of \(\Pi/j\) at \(o(h)\), hence within the prefix.
Below \(g\) the prefix is integral, so \(g=o(h)\).
The [inverse representation](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) gives \(h\to\infty\), an integer polynomial \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_i\) of degree \(2h+2\) and Mahler measure tending to one strictly from above.
The Taylor constant of \(J_i\) is one and its positive coefficients are doubled moments of a symmetric signed measure \(\omega_i\) of mass one, with negative mass tending to zero.
The formal quotient \(X_i=B^\sharp/G\) is integral and agrees with \(S_i(z^g)J_i\) on the prefix.

The leading coefficient of \(S\) has modulus strictly between zero and one by the product of its root moduli.
Let \(\lambda\) be its first nonintegral coefficient, at index \(l\), and \(A\) its truncation before \(l\).
Divide \(X_i\) by the integral formal unit \(A(z^g)\).
In \(S_i/A\) the coefficients before \(l\), except the constant one, tend to zero, and the coefficient at \(l\) tends to \(\lambda\).
For \(g\to\infty\) and any positive \(t'=o(g)\), take a probability limit of \((v^{t'},v^{lg})\) under \(\omega_i\), and denote its doubled \((r,k)\)-moment by \(D(r,k)\).
At \(rt'\) for \(r>0\) and \(lg+rt'\) for every fixed \(r\) the integral coefficients give
\[
\begin{split}
 D(r,0)&\in\mathbb Z\quad(r>0),\qquad D(r,1)\in\mathbb Z\quad(r<0),\\
 D(0,1)+\lambda&\in\mathbb Z,\qquad D(r,1)+\lambda D(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\quad(r>0).
\end{split}
\]
The earlier offsets cost \(o(1)\) by the bounded raw moments, and the zeroth Taylor coefficient contributes one at \(lg\).
The mixed sequence \(D(r,1)\) has finite range by these relations and is Fourier–Stieltjes.
Interpolation of its level indicators and the integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem give a common periodic tail in both directions, so \(\lambda D(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\) for all sufficiently large \(r>0\).

The [real seam classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#real-seam) now gives Haar first marginal.
Indeed a pure atomic integral marginal is supported on finitely many torsion points, contradicting the fractional condition at zero by periodicity.
With a continuous part its positive-real transform is \(1\), \(1+\delta w^u\), or \((1+\delta w^u)^{-1}\), where \(\delta=\pm1\).
The reciprocal has unit coefficients on an infinite progression, contradicting the tail condition.
For the binomial \(D_0=1+\delta w^u\), the mixed row density \(F\) satisfies \(|F|\le|D_0|^2\), and \(H=F+\lambda D_0\) is an integer Laurent polynomial by the congruences and Fourier decay.
Double vanishing at every zero of \(D_0\) makes \(H/D_0\) integral Laurent and equal to \(\lambda\) at each of those zeros.
Its integral remainder of degree less than \(u\) modulo \(D_0\) would be the constant \(\lambda\), a contradiction.
Consequently the exactly integral coefficient \([z^{t'}]J_i\) eventually vanishes for every such test, and a diagonal choice gives zero coefficients through \(\kappa g\) for some fixed \(\kappa>0\) along the sequence.
On blocks of that diameter the signed character Gram matrix is the identity.
For any fixed number of characters the correction to the positive Gram norm is \(o(1)\).
The [local Bessel estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) applied to the finitely many uniformly bounded active partial sums of \(2S_i\) gives bounded absolute coefficient sum of \(X_i\) through \(Cg\) for each fixed \(C\), counting the offset indices separately.
For bounded \(g\) the raw moment bound gives the same conclusion directly.

<a id="rows"></a>
## The coefficient field forced by a surviving slope

Fix a limiting probability \(\mu\) of \(w=\bar v^g\) and write \(J_0\) for its Herglotz series.
Then \(B_0=SJ_0\) is integral by testing the multiples of \(g\), so \(\mu\) cannot be Haar.
For \(g/t,t/h\to0\), take any further joint limit \(\Omega\) with \(u=v^t\).
For each \(k>0\) the projection \(\nu_k\) of \(2S(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) to the base has integral Fourier coefficients, by testing \(X_i\) at \(kt+rg\) for every fixed \(r\in\mathbb Z\).
Their absolute values are bounded uniformly by \(2\|S\|_\infty\).
The transform of \(2|S|^2\Omega\) has finite range on the full lattice.
On the zero layer it equals \(S^*B_0+SB_0^*\), with star denoting conjugate reversal and \(B_0\) a bounded integer coefficient sequence.
Every other layer follows by multiplying the integral row by \(S^*\) or reflecting.
Finite interpolation and the [coset decomposition](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) apply to this finite range even for transcendental coefficients of \(S\).
The positive weighted measure has trigonometric polynomial densities on finitely many torsion translates of connected subtori.
Each \(\nu_k\) has an integer Laurent polynomial density on its absolutely continuous part, since only singleton frequency cosets contribute to that part.
The zero-layer decomposition and strict positivity of \(|S|\) confine all marginal singular mass to a fixed finite torsion set.

After division by \(2|S|^2\), group proper components projecting onto the full base by the slope in their graph equations \(u^b=\zeta w^a\).
For each group's projected nonnegative density \(f\), the [slope separation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#slopes) gives an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=2Sf\).
Indeed at large common multiples of the denominators and torsion orders, the graph moments are translates of \(2Sf\), while the full-torus term has no such harmonics.
Shifting to one slope and using Fourier decay of all others gives integer coefficients for \(2Sf\), which vanish outside a finite set by decay.
If \(f\ne0\), then \(M(H)=2M(f)<2\), since \(M(S)=1\) and equality in Jensen for \(f\) would exhaust the marginal as Haar probability.
Here \(M\) applied to a function denotes its multiplicative Mahler measure.
Reality of \(f\) gives, for \(n=\deg S\),
\[
                 \frac{H}{H^*}=\frac{w^n S(w)}{w^n S(1/w)}.
\]
The left side is in \(\mathbb Q(w)\).
On the right the denominator has only strictly interior roots, is nonzero at zero, and is coprime to the numerator.
Uniqueness of the reduced fraction and the coefficient one at index \(n\) in the numerator force \(S\in\mathbb Q[w]\).
The rational function \(H/S\) is real on the circle and has no nonzero finite pole, since an exterior pole would have a reciprocal conjugate interior pole.
It is thus rational-coefficient Laurent polynomial, and for the least denominator \(m\) of \(S\) the primitive polynomial \(mS\) divides \(H\) in the integer Laurent ring by Gauss's lemma.
As \(S\) has a fractional coefficient, \(M(H)\ge M(mS)=m\ge2\), a contradiction.

Only the full-torus component can now contribute to the absolutely continuous positive rows, and its harmonic degree is finite because division was by a base function.
At a last nonzero row \(k\), the \(k\)-th power pushforward of the conditional probabilities on the absolutely continuous base has only constant and first harmonics.
Its first moment has modulus at most \(1/2\) by positivity.
The integer Laurent density \(H_k\) then has \(M(H_k)\le M(S)M(f_0)<1\), where \(f_0\) is the absolutely continuous marginal density and equality in Jensen is again excluded by non-Haar marginal.
This contradiction leaves every positive row supported on the fixed finite set of torsion atoms.

<a id="return"></a>
## The actual denominator and coefficient stability

Choose \(T\ge1\) divisible by all those orders, and put \(W_i=1-z^{Tg}\).
At each intermediate scale \(t\) the coefficient of \(W_i X_i\) is integral and tends to zero by the support of \(\nu_1\).
Extraction of violating indices otherwise gives simultaneous vanishing on \(Lg\le t\le h/L\) eventually for some fixed \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\).
The truncation \(Q_i\) of \(W_i X_i\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) thus has degree \(O(g)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum by the local estimate.
In \(J_*=Q_i/(W_i S_i(z^g))\) the coefficients agree with \(J_i\) through that truncation.
There is \(R>1\) such that \(S\) is zero-free on \(|w|\le R\), and the uniform lower bound there for \(|S_i|\) gives \(|[w^r](1/S_i)|=O(R^{-r})\) by Cauchy's estimate, including when the degree changes.
After the \(rg\) shift in this expansion, a monomial of \(Q_i\) divided by \(W_i\) contributes a progression modulo \(Tg\) with \(O(1+r)\) initial exceptions.
A character times uniform measure on the \(Tg\)-th roots gives the progression, and monomial densities correct its initial exceptions.
Summing their variations gives a uniform positive-index representing measure for \(J_*\), since \(\sum_{r\ge0}(1+r)R^{-r}<\infty\).
The [denominator comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#return) uses
\[
 S_i(z^g)W_i B^\sharp-Q_i\Pi/j
          = S_i(z^g)W_i(\Pi/j)(J_i-J_*).
\]
The left polynomial has degree \(O(h)\), the right multipliers have bounded circle suprema, and the remaining series has bounded representing variation with a zero prefix of length comparable to \(h\).
The [two interval cutoffs](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) give bounded circle \(L^1\) norm for the difference, and adding the bounded \(Q_i\Pi/j\) and using the circle minimum of \(|S_i|\) bounds \(\|W_iB^\sharp\|_1\).
The [integer support estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) then bounds its term count, whereas \(M(W_iB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one, contradicting the [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This excludes every sequence converging in coefficients to the given center with excess tending to zero, which gives the two positive constants.
A finite subcover and the minimum of the finitely many lower bounds give the compact-set assertion.

<a id="bounded-offsets"></a>
## When the reduced lower exponents of the actual lift are bounded

Write \(n=2h+2\), and let \(d_1<\cdots<d_s\) be all nonzero support indices of \(\Pi\) in \([1,h]\).
Fix \(K\ge1\).
For \(s>0\) put \(g=\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_s)\) and assume \(d_s/g\le K\).
Define \(A_i(w)=1+\sum_{r=1}^K a_{i,r}w^r\) by \([w^{d_l/g}]A_i=[z^{d_l}]\Pi/j\), with the other positive coefficients zero.
Anti-reciprocity and the zero midpoint give the exact identity
\[
                  \Pi/j=A_i(z^g)-z^n A_i(z^{-g}).
\]
Allow \(s=0\) as well, with \(A_i=1,\ g=1\).
For each fixed \(K,J\) there is \(c(K,J)>0\) such that every such input with \(1\le j\le J\) satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c(K,J)\), and \(q\) is bounded in terms of \(K,J\).
With unbounded branch permitted, any sequence of these inputs with \(\theta-q\to0\) has \(j\to\infty\).
Its coefficient vectors are bounded, and every subsequential limit of \(A_i\) is nonconstant, has no open-disk zero, and has at least one unit-circle zero.

Indeed the [trace construction](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) gives \(M(\Pi)=j\).
The support size is at most \(2K+2\), so the [sparse coefficient-height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds every coefficient of \(\Pi/j\) and its circle supremum solely in terms of \(K\).
The full normalized interior coefficient sum is exactly \(2\sum_{r=1}^K|a_{i,r}|\).
The [endpoint dominance bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v64.md#dichotomy) bounds this sum away from zero along an escaping sequence, excluding both an empty lower support and constant limiting profiles.
The first nonintegral normalized coefficient occurs at \(o(h)\) by C345v13 and must eventually be one of the \(d_l\), since the entire lower half ends at \(h\) and \(h\to\infty\).
This gives \(g=o(h)\), so every lower offset is at most \(Kg=o(h)\).
The exact lower polynomial agrees with the normalized lift through \(h\), since each reflected term has index at least \(h+2\).

Take a coefficient limit \(A_i\to A\).
If \(A\) had an interior zero, choose \(0<b<1\) enclosing one such zero with \(A\) nonzero on \(|w|=b\).
On \(|z|=b^{1/g}\), the reflected term is \(O_K(b^{n/g-K})=o(1)\), whereas \(|A_i(z^g)|\) is bounded below there.
By Rouché first on \(|w|=b\) and then on \(|z|=b^{1/g}\), the actual \(\Pi\) would have an interior zero, contrary to the unit-circle location of its roots.
If all roots of \(A\) were strictly exterior, the neighborhood criterion with \(G=1,\eta=1,N=K\) would contradict \(\theta-q\to0\).
This proves the asserted limit restrictions.
If \(j\) stayed bounded on a subsequence, after extraction \(j\) and all of \(A_i\) would be fixed by the bounded normalized height and integrality of \(j A_i\).
That fixed \(A\) has a nonintegral coefficient by the first nondivisible index and has no interior roots.
The [exact rational criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) applies with \(G=1\) even at its circle roots, excluding this subsequence.
This proves the fixed-\(J\) uniform gap by contradiction, and the [branch derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(q\le J/c(K,J)+2\).
