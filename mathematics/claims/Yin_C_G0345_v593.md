# Primitive exponent patterns at strict records with few expanding or contracting conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## The count and its signature condition

Take a sequence of [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement) among nontorsion algebraic units with \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(L=\log(1/m)\), and choose \(1\le B\le d/4\) with \(Bm\to0\).
Let \(k\) be the larger of the numbers of expanding and contracting complex embeddings of \(K\), counted individually.
For the fixed ordered outgoing coefficient tuple and witness weight \(r\to\infty\) in the [finite-window theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement), let \(V_r\) count the primitive marked lists obtained by dividing the realized sorted exponents by their positive gcd.
If \(\log(2k)=o(rL)\), then
\[
                  \log V_r\ge (1-o(1))(r-2)L.
\]
This counts realizations in the same integral height and exponent windows under the full degree-\(B\) terminality test.
The signature condition holds whenever \(k\le m^{-C}\) for a fixed \(C\), and more generally whenever \(\log(2k)=o(L^{5/4}/(\log(2+L))^{1/4})\), by the [witness weight bound](Yin_C_G0345_v589.md#statement).
Conversely if \(V_r\le A^r\) for a fixed \(A\), then \(\liminf\log(2k)/(rL)>0\), since a subsequence with ratio tending to zero contradicts the count.
When \(\log B=o(L)\), the [degree restriction for capped witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v589.md#statement) applies simultaneously.

<a id="groups"></a>
## Sorted groups and separated evaluations

Retain the most frequent pattern of equalities and signs among the finite-window lists, losing at most \(2^{r-2}(r+1)^2\) as in the [grouping argument](Yin_C_G0345_v591.md#groups).
The surviving logarithmic count is at least \((r-2)L-O(r)\).
Each exponent group has a fixed nonzero sum of coefficients, since a vanishing group would be a proper zero subsum of the marked relation.
The group sizes and exponents determine the entire sorted list.
For a bounded number of nonzero groups the [mixed embedding argument](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings) bounds the number of lists by \(O(B)\): it allows arbitrary degrees for the fixed grouped coefficients, includes a zero group if present, and requires only that the output have degree at most \(B\) and that the outgoing terms have no zero subsum.
Here the minimum positive power-field degree is \(d\) by the [record norm argument](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm).
Since \(Bm\to0\), a bounded group count contradicts the retained count on every subsequence.
Thus the least size \(g\) of a nonzero-exponent group satisfies \(g=o(r)\).

Fix a retained primitive list with grouped coefficients \(b_w\) at its distinct exponents \(w\), and take \(b_0=0\) if no constant group occurs.
At a fixed embedding choose a side of nonzero exponents, with coefficient mass \(S\) equal to the sum of their embedded moduli.
When the base for that side (the embedded unit or its inverse) has logarithmic modulus \(H>0\), the [separated evaluation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v591.md#dilations) gives at most \(r\lceil\log(64r^2(1+A))/H\rceil\) positive dilations at which the side evaluates to a value of modulus at most \(AS\), for \(A\ge1\).
Its matrix bound uses only the base modulus, so also applies to a nonreal base.

<a id="fiber"></a>
## A neutral extension and a contracting extension in the same fiber

Suppose first that \(d>2k B^r\).
Choose an embedding \(\sigma\) of maximal expanding log modulus \(H\ge m/k\), since the sum of all expanding log moduli is \(m\).
Choose the side of larger coefficient mass at \(\sigma\), resolving ties in favor of the positive side.
For each realized dilation \(q\) with output \(c_q\), the field \(E=\mathbb Q(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1},c_q)\) has degree at most \(B^r\).
The extension fiber above \(\sigma|_E\) has more than \(2k\) members, including a neutral embedding.
Since \(E\) is proper, the [relative norm](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm) is torsion; the sum of signed log moduli of the unit in this fiber is zero.
It contains \(\sigma\), hence also contains a contracting embedding \(\tau\) with \(-\log|\tau(\alpha)|\ge H/k\), because there are at most \(k\) contracting embeddings in total.
The coefficients and the output have identical values across this fiber.
In particular neutral evaluation bounds \(|\sigma(c_q-b_0)|\le 2S\).
For the positive side evaluate at \(\sigma\), or for the negative side at such a \(\tau\) and use \(\tau(\alpha)^{-1}\) as expanding base.
In either case the opposite side is bounded by \(S\), hence \(A=3\) suffices.
Although the fiber may depend on \(q\), the negative-side hits are covered by at most \(k\) fixed contracting embeddings, each with expanding inverse log modulus at least \(m/k^2\) whenever selected.
The total number of dilations is at most \(kr\lceil k^2\log(256r^2)/m\rceil\), with logarithm \(O(L+\log(2k)+\log(2r))\).

<a id="height"></a>
## Height cost when the degree is bounded by the signature and coefficient degrees

Suppose instead \(d\le 2kB^r\).
Choose the sign of one group of minimum size and evaluate at a fixed extremal embedding for that sign, taking an inverse base for a negative exponent.
The expanding or contracting log modulus has absolute value at least \(m/k\) because each side of the product formula has total mass \(m\).
Every original coefficient and the output has absolute logarithmic height at most \(Y=O(\log(2d))\) and degree at most \(B\), so their moduli at this embedding are at most \(e^{BY}\).
The coefficient of the chosen group has degree at most \(B^g\) and height at most \(gY+\log g\); its inverse has the same height.
Therefore \(S\ge\exp(-B^g(gY+\log g))\).
The embedded output, constant group and decaying opposite terms together have modulus at most \(r e^{BY}\) by the triangle inequality on the original individual terms.
Thus we can use \(A=1+r\exp(BY+B^g(gY+\log g))\).
Taking the logarithm of the separated-evaluation count gives an upper bound
\[
 O\big(L+\log(2k)+\log(2r)+\log(2+Y)\big)+g\log B.
\]
Indeed \(g\ge1\), so \(BY\le B^g(gY+\log g)\); only the logarithm of \(\log(64r^2(1+A))\) enters the cost.
Since \(d\le2k B^r\) and eventually \(\log B\le L\), we have \(\log(2+Y)=O(\log(2+\log(2k))+\log(2r)+\log(2+L))\).
Both branches now cost \(o(rL)\) per primitive list on the logarithmic scale under the stated hypothesis, because \(g=o(r)\).
Division into primitive lists gives the claimed lower bound.
