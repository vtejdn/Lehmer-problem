# Real-trace generators and fixed dimensions for the normal closure of complex conjugation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small total measure and the action of the conjugate involutions

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\), use the rational conjugate module \(V\), splitting group \(G\), and complex conjugation \(c\).
Write \(C=\langle gcg^{-1}:g\in G\rangle\).
Suppose a rational irreducible constituent \(W\) of \(V\) contains a nonzero rational \(C\)-invariant subspace \(U\) of dimension \(s\).
Let \(a=\dim W^c/\dim W\) and \(q=|\operatorname{im}(C\to\mathrm{GL}(U))|\).
Then \(q\le3^{s^2}\), and when \(a>0\),
\[
                   m(w)\ \ge\ (a/q)\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-q/a}).
\]
In particular if \(m(w)\to0\) and \(s\) stays bounded, then \(a\to0\), uniformly over the eligible constituents.
If the conjugates of \(c\) commute on \(W\), one may use \(a\) itself in place of \(a/q\).
In this case \(m(w)>a^2/(50\log(8/a))\) when \(a>0\).

There is also an exact description when all conjugates of \(c\) commute on \(V\).
This holds exactly when the divisible group \(D(w)\) can be generated, allowing all torsion and roots, by totally real units and reciprocal units of totally real trace belonging to \(D(w)\).
The latter can be chosen with at least one real exterior conjugate and one conjugate on the unit circle.
A finite set of generators of the rational class space suffices, each an ordinary signed product of the original conjugates of height at most \(m(w)\).
In fact at most \(\deg w\) such products suffice, and the exponent of the quotient of the original integral class lattice by their lattice divides the least common multiple of the sizes of the \(C\)-orbits on the actual roots.
For the equivalent field formulation, the splitting field of the [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) has commuting conjugates of complex conjugation.
On a shrinking sequence satisfying this condition, the rank of conjugate log moduli divided by the multiplicative rank tends uniformly to zero for every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\).
The individual generated units need not have small total measure because their degrees can grow.

<a id="small"></a>
## A common fixed vector among a positive fraction of conjugations

On \(W\), the average of \(g^{-1}(1+c)g/2\) over \(G\) is \(aI\), by the [rational projection argument](Yin_C_G0901_v76.md#lattice).
Each conjugate of \(c\) lies in \(C\), so these operators restrict to \(U\).
Taking traces there gives mean fixed dimension \(a s\) for its conjugate involutions.
Thus a fraction at least \(a\) act on \(U\) with a nonzero fixed vector.
The finite image of \(C\) on \(U\) preserves a full lattice, obtained by intersecting the lattice of conjugate classes with \(U\).
Reduction modulo three is injective on such finite groups, giving \(q\le3^{s^2}\).
At least one image with a rational fixed vector occurs with probability at least \(a/q\).
Choose a nonzero rational vector in its fixed space.
The [subset product selected by that vector](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection) is nontorsion, has height at most \(m(w)/2\), and is real at all these embeddings.
The [real-conjugate height bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) proves the first conclusion.

When the images on \(W\) of the conjugates of \(c\) commute, the image of \(C\) is an elementary abelian \(2\)-group.
The rational representation splits into common eigenspaces with signs as eigenvalues.
Choose any one-dimensional line in a nonzero common eigenspace.
The trace identity on this line says that a fraction exactly \(a\) of conjugates of \(c\) act as \(+1\).
They fix the entire line, so no division by the group order is needed.
In addition the [real-place determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement) applied to the same selected unit gives the displayed rational-logarithmic bound.
A nonzero constituent has \(a>0\) because the positive products of conjugates detect a nontorsion element on it by the [fixed-space rank identity](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules).

<a id="generation"></a>
## A set of generators with prescribed signs under every complex conjugation

Assume \(C\) acts through an elementary abelian \(2\)-group on \(V\).
For a nonzero rational vector \(v\) in a common eigenspace \(V_\chi\), write \(\chi:C\to\{\pm1\}\) for its character.
Use the invariant rational inner product and form the actual unit
\[
      z(v)=\frac{\prod_{(v,[b])>0,\ b\in\Omega} b}
                       {\prod_{(v,[b])<0,\ b\in\Omega} b},
               \qquad \Omega=\{\text{conjugates of }w\}.
\]
The pairings of \(v\) with these roots do not all vanish.
Their absolute values sum to \((v,[z(v)])>0\), so \(z(v)\) is nontorsion.
Each conjugate occurs at most once, giving \(h(z(v))\le m(w)\).
For \(u\in C\) the sign conditions are preserved or interchanged according to \(\chi(u)\); hence \(u z(v)=z(v)^{\chi(u)}\) exactly, including all possible torsion in the original root classes.
Thus \([z(v)]\in V_\chi\).
These vectors span \(V_\chi\): if their span were proper, its orthogonal complement within this rational eigenspace would contain a nonzero rational \(v\), contradicting its positive pairing with \([z(v)]\).
Selecting a finite basis across the eigenspaces gives the generation claim.

For the assertion on integral classes use each actual \(C\)-orbit \(O=C b\) separately.
For each character \(\chi\) of the elementary abelian image of \(C\) on \(V\) which is trivial on the stabilizer of \(b\), form
\[
           z_{O,\chi}=\prod_{t b\in O}(t b)^{\chi(t)} .
\]
The sign is independent of the representative \(t\), the height is at most \(|O|h(w)\), and \(s z_{O,\chi}=z_{O,\chi}^{\chi(s)}\) for each \(s\in C\).
Such characters number at most \(|O|\), since they factor through the elementary abelian image modulo the image of the stabilizer.
If \(e_\chi=|C|^{-1}\sum_{t\in C}\chi(t)t\) is the rational character projection, then \([z_{O,\chi}]=|O|e_\chi[b]\).
For any character nontrivial on the stabilizer this projection of \([b]\) vanishes by cancellation.
The projections sum to the identity on \(V\); for each \(b'=s b\) their signed sum expresses \(|O|[b']\) in the integral lattice of the displayed products, since \(s e_\chi[b]=\chi(s)e_\chi[b]\).
Torsion products have zero class and may be omitted.
The bound on the quotient exponent and the total number of generators follow, with the asserted trace types by their exact sign action just as for \(z(v)\).

For trivial \(\chi\) all conjugates of \(z(v)\) are real since \(C\) fixes it and is normal.
For nontrivial \(\chi\), some conjugate of \(c\) acts as \(-1\) because these elements generate \(C\).
Consequently \(z(v)\) is reciprocal and has a neutral conjugate.
Every conjugate of \(c\) fixes \(z(v)+z(v)^{-1}\), so this trace is totally real.
At least one conjugate of \(z(v)\) is off the circle by Kronecker's theorem, and with totally real trace every such conjugate is real.
Reciprocity supplies a real exterior value.
Conversely each totally real generator has all conjugation involutions trivial on its conjugate span.
For any reciprocal generator with totally real trace, every conjugate of \(c\) sends each actual conjugate to itself or its inverse, since it fixes the trace of that conjugate.
Those involutions commute on the classes of the generators and their conjugates, hence on their entire rational span, including all roots and torsion factors.
Finally the compressed roots are distinct modulo torsion and have the same rational span \(V\), so their splitting group acts faithfully there, proving the field equivalence.

For any nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), its rational conjugate span is a nonzero submodule of \(V\).
The ratio of its radial rank to its dimension is the fixed dimension fraction of \(c\), a weighted average over its constituents.
The uniform bound from the commuting case proves the last assertion.
For a sequence of strict degree records with shrinking measures and commuting conjugates of \(c\) on each input's \(V\), this raises the lower bound on argument ranks throughout \(D(w)\) to \((2/\log2-o(1))\log(\deg w)\), by the [constituent rank estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v80.md#records).
