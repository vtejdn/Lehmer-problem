# Lower-degree Salem numbers obtained by evaluating the two halves of a square endpoint

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational evaluations and unrestricted polynomial evaluations

Take a half polynomial \(f\) as in the [delay-one reconstruction](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#statement), of degree \(b\ge3\) over \(K/E\), where \(E\) is totally real of degree \(u\ge2\).
It is monic, \(f(0)=\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(f^\tau=\epsilon f^*\) for the relative involution \(\tau\).
At the marked place the roots of \(f\) are \(r>1\) and \(b-1\) strictly interior roots, and at each nonsplit place they are on the unit circle.
Write \(F(A,B)=B^bf(A/B)\).
At a rational projective point with nonzero denominator of the following quotient put
\[
                   y=\frac{F^\tau(A,B)}{F(A,B)},\qquad (A,B)=1 .
\]
It has relative norm one when nonzero, hence all complex values have modulus one and its two real values are mutual inverses.
Every nontorsion such \(y\), including nonintegral values, has full multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(y)\ge3+2\sqrt2\).
For \(x=A/B>r\), the oriented real value satisfies
\[
       1<\sqrt y\le\frac{x-r^{-1}}{x-r},\qquad
       N\operatorname{den}(y)\ge
          \frac{|AB|^{2u}}{4^{u-1}(\sqrt y-1/\sqrt y)^2}.                \tag{1}
\]
At each fixed rational \(x>1\), as \(r\to1\) the real value tends to one but its denominator norm grows at least as a positive constant depending only on \(x\) times \((\log r)^{-2}\).
If \(y\) is a unit at some \(x>r\), then \(x-r\le(r-r^{-1})(2/|AB|)^u\).
The measure bound also holds for the whole group generated by evaluations with \(AB\) even, and for the group of all rational evaluations when \(f(1)\) is a unit at every dyadic prime.

For a canonical square endpoint the [half field](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields) satisfies \(K\subset L=\mathbb Q(r)\), with \(f\) the minimum over \(K\).
Replace the rational evaluation by the quotient of the two resultants against any rational polynomial \(g\) for which they are nonzero.
Taking positive integral roots of these quotients, with signs allowed, gives as its real Salem outputs **exactly** the Salem numbers in the marked \(K\).
Every one is obtained using a single integral \(g\) of degree less than \([L:\mathbb Q]\) and an exponent at most \(b\).
There is a single quartic field \(K\) and an explicit infinite family of irreducible square endpoints \(r_b\), with unbounded half degrees, for which every one of these Salem outputs is strictly greater than the full measure \(M(r_b)=r_b^2\).
This rules out a degree descent with no increase of measure by this operation, even with unrestricted auxiliary polynomials and exponents.
The constructed measures tend to a value greater than one, leaving the shrinking case open.

<a id="real"></a>
## The real value at a point beyond the exterior root

Write \(m=\log r\), and write each of the interior roots as \(e^{-t_i+i\theta_i}\), with \(t_i>0\).
Their total inward logarithmic displacement is \(\sum_i t_i=m\) by the unit constant.
Let \(D_j\) be the sum of the \(j\)-th powers of the roots of \(f\) minus that of its conjugate \(f^\tau\).
At the marked real embedding, pairing complex conjugates gives
\[
              D_j=2\sinh(jm)-2\sum_i \sinh(jt_i)\cos(j\theta_i).
\]
For every \(j\ge1\), \(0<D_j\le4\sinh(jm)\), since there are at least two strictly positive \(t_i\) and \(\sinh(s+t)>\sinh s+\sinh t\) for \(s,t>0\).
All roots of both polynomials have modulus at most \(r\).
Their monic values are positive at real \(x>r\), and expansion of the logarithms there gives
\[
         0<\log y=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{D_j}{jx^j}
           \le 2\log\frac{1-r^{-1}/x}{1-r/x}.
\]
This proves the archimedean inequality in (1) without a factor depending on the degree.

<a id="denominators"></a>
## A congruence at both homogeneous coordinates

The slopes \(0,\infty,1,-1\) give signs whenever defined, by the endpoints and reciprocity.
At all remaining rational slopes \(Q=|AB|\ge2\).
Modulo each prime-power component of \(A\mathcal O_K\), the denominator \(F(A,B)\) is congruent to \(\epsilon B^b\), a local unit, and modulo \(B\mathcal O_K\) it is congruent to \(A^b\).
The numerator has the same reductions, so \(y\equiv1\bmod Q\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}\) for all \(\mathfrak p\mid Q\).
Write \(D=N_{K/\mathbb Q}\operatorname{den}(y)\) for the norm of the integral denominator ideal in lowest terms.
Then \((y-1)/Q\) has exactly the same denominator ideal as \(y\), also at primes away from \(Q\).
If \(y\ne1\), its numerator ideal has norm at least one, whence
\[
                D\,|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(y-1)|\ \ge\ Q^{2u}.                  \tag{2}
\]
For nontorsion \(y\) put \(H=\max(|y|,|y|^{-1})>1\) at the mark.
Its unique embedding of maximal modulus implies \(\mathbb Q(y)=K\), so the archimedean and finite places give \(M(y)=HD\).
The \(u-1\) complex pairs each contribute at most four to the absolute norm in (2).
For \(y>0\) the two real factors contribute \((\sqrt H-1/\sqrt H)^2\).
This proves the denominator estimate in (1).
If \(D=1\), then \(\sqrt y-1/\sqrt y\ge 2(Q/2)^u\) at \(x>r\), and \(\sqrt y-1\ge(Q/2)^u\).
The real upper bound gives the restriction on \(x-r\).
For fixed rational \(x>1\) the same upper bound gives \(\sqrt y-1/\sqrt y=O_x(\log r)\) as \(r\to1\), while \(Q^{2u}/4^{u-1}\ge4\).

For positive \(y\), (2) yields \(M(y)\ge\max(H,4H^2/(H-1)^2)\).
The two expressions meet at \(H=3+2\sqrt2\), one increasing and the other decreasing.
If \(Q\) is even, apply the same reasoning modulo two to the positive real sign of \(y\), which is still congruent to one there.
If \(y<0\) and \(Q\) is odd, then \(Q\ge3\), and the two real norm factors contribute \(H+2+H^{-1}\).
Hence \(D\ge(81/4)/(H+2+H^{-1})\) since \(u\ge2\).
For \(1<H\le6/5\) the right side exceeds five, so the integer \(D\) is at least six.
For \(H\ge6/5\), \(HD\ge(81/4)(H/(H+1))^2\ge729/121>6\).
This proves the asserted bound for all signs and denominator ideals.
The condition of being one modulo two at all dyadic places persists under products and inverses.
If \(f(1)\) is a dyadic unit everywhere, then for odd \(A,B\) the denominator reduces modulo two to this same unit, and the numerator reduces to \(\tau f(1)=\epsilon f(1)\).
Thus the argument modulo two also proves both group assertions.

<a id="resultants"></a>
## All auxiliary polynomials after taking roots

Now suppose \(f\) is the minimum of \(r\) over \(K\subset L=\mathbb Q(r)\).
Writing \(z=N_{L/K}g(r)=\operatorname{Res}(f,g)\), the new output is \(\tau z/z\in K\).
Conversely, take any \(\eta\in K^\times\) of norm one.
If \(\eta\ne-1\) put \(k=1+\eta^{-1}\), so \(\tau k/k=\eta\); for \(\eta=-1\) take any nonzero anti-invariant \(k\in K\).
Choose \(g\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree less than \([L:\mathbb Q]\) such that \(g(r)=ck\) for a nonzero rational integer \(c\), by the power basis of \(L\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Then the resultant ratio is \(\eta^b\).
If a real Salem number \(\eta>1\) in the marked \(K\) were fixed by \(\tau\), it would lie in the totally real \(E\), impossible because it has a nonreal conjugate.
Thus \(K=E(\eta)\) and the pair of roots over each real embedding of \(E\) is distinct.
Over the nonsplit places they are nonreal conjugate pairs, since otherwise they would coincide.
The real value \(\eta\) can therefore occur only once among the embeddings of \(K\), proving full degree and \(\tau\eta=\eta^{-1}\).
Finally if a real Salem \(\theta>1\) has a signed positive power equal to a resultant ratio, then \(\theta^j\in K\).
Every positive power of a Salem number has full degree, since distinct conjugates coalescing after powering could be transported to two of maximal modulus.
Hence \(\theta\in K\).
The same conclusion holds for products of ratios and for root-of-unity twists of a real power, whose twist must itself be real.

<a id="field"></a>
## A half field whose least Salem number exceeds the original measure

Take \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) at its positive embedding, \(c=3+2\sqrt2\), \(\nu=2+\sqrt2\), and let \(a>a'\) solve \(Y^2-cY+\nu=0\).
Put \(\delta=c^2-4\nu=9+8\sqrt2\) and \(K=E(\sqrt\delta)\).
The discriminant is positive at the mark and negative at the other place.
Here \(c>1+\nu\), giving \(a>1>a'>0\), while the other two values have modulus \(\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}<1\).
Also \(a<(6+5)/2=11/2\), since \(c<6\) and \(\delta<25\).

Any Salem unit \(\sigma>1\) in the marked \(K\) has \(s=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\in\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) with \(s>2\), \(-2<s'<2\) and \(K=E(\sqrt{s^2-4})\), by the preceding field argument.
The norm of \(\delta\) is \(-47\), so its principal ideal is prime of norm \(47\) and \(\sqrt2\equiv-7\) there.
Since \(s^2-4\) has the squareclass of \(\delta\) in \(E\), its integral valuation there is odd and positive.
Writing \(s=l+k\sqrt2\) therefore gives \(l-7k\equiv\pm2\bmod47\).
If \(s\le32\), then \(1\le k<(32+2)/(2\sqrt2)<13\) and \(|l-k\sqrt2|<2\).
In this range \(l-7k\) lies strictly between \(-70\) and \(-3\), forcing \(-45\) or \(-49\).
The inequalities \(|(7-\sqrt2)k-45|<2\) or \(|(7-\sqrt2)k-49|<2\) leave respectively \((l,k)=(11,8),(14,9)\).
In the first case \(N(s^2-4)=(9^2-128)(13^2-128)=-47\cdot41\), incompatible with the required squareclass.
In the second case \((s^2-4)/\delta=18+12\sqrt2=6(1+\sqrt2)^2\), while \(6\) is nonsquare in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).
Hence \(s>32\), so \(\sigma>31>a^2\).
The relative norm-one units of \(K/E\) form a rank-one group modulo signs, since the unit norm image contains the squares in \(\mathcal O_E^\times\) and the two Dirichlet ranks differ by one.
Every positive norm-one unit greater than one is Salem and generates \(K\) by its unique exterior embedding.

For \(b\ge3\) set \(f_b=X^{b-1}(X-a)-(1-a'X)\).
At the mark \(|X-a|^2-|1-a'X|^2=(a-a')(c-2\operatorname{Re}X)>0\) on the unit circle.
The [split and complex root counts](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#roots) give \(b-1\) strictly interior roots and one real \(1<r_b<a\) there, since \(f_b(1)<0<f_b(a)=\nu-1\).
At either complex embedding all roots are neutral by the same disk inequality for \(|\iota a|<1\).
If \(f_b\) were reducible over \(K\), a positive-degree monic factor omitting \(r_b\) would have integral unit constant \(h\) with \(1/r_b\le |h|<1\) at the mark.
The neutral roots in a complex copy give \(|\iota h|^2=1\), whence \(h\tau h=1\).
Choosing the sign of \(1/h\) that is positive at the mark gives a norm-one Salem unit in \((1,r_b]\), contradicting the bound just proved.
Thus \(f_b\) is irreducible over \(K\).
The opposite real polynomial has the inverse roots; each marked interior root has modulus strictly above \(1/r_b\) since their product of moduli is \(1/r_b\) and there are at least two.
The two real root sets are therefore disjoint: the lone exterior \(r_b\) cannot belong to the opposite set and the lone opposite interior \(1/r_b\) cannot belong to the marked set.
The complex copies have only neutral roots and are themselves disjoint by transporting their pair to the two real copies.
All four coefficient conjugates are separable and irreducible over their respective fields, so the rational minimum contains all \(4b\) roots, by extension of embeddings.
Consequently \([\mathbb Q(r_b):\mathbb Q]=4b\), \(K\subset\mathbb Q(r_b)\) since \([K(r_b):\mathbb Q]=4b\), and \(M(r_b)=r_b^2<a^2\).
The strict largest modulus also ensures compression, and for all sufficiently large prime \(b\) the four halves give the two canonical blocks by the [prime-block argument](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#roots).
At each fixed \(1<x<a\) one has \(f_b(x)<0\) for large \(b\), so \(r_b\to a\).
The attainable Salem numbers in this fixed half field all exceed the original measures throughout the family.
