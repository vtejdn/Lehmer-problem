# Interlacing inverse images of Salem traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## Individual Salem factors and the measure of the full inverse image

Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(A\) monic of degree \(s\ge1\), and \(B\) of degree \(s-1\) and leading coefficient \(k\ge1\).
Consider the interlacing rational maps
\[
        g(X)=A(X)/B(X)=X/k+c-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1} w_i/(X-b_i),
                    \qquad b_1<\cdots<b_{s-1},\quad w_i>0 .          \tag{1}
\]
The poles and weights are real.
For a monic trace polynomial \(S\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree \(m\), the full inverse trace polynomial and its reciprocal lift are
\[
       H(X)=B(X)^m S(g(X)),\qquad
       P_g(Z)=Z^{sm} H(Z+Z^{-1}).
\]
Both are monic integer polynomials, of degrees \(sm\) and \(2sm\).
At each pole the value of \(H\) is \(A(b_i)^m\ne0\).

* Let \(S\) be irreducible and let \(u=\tau+\tau^{-1}\) for a specified Salem number \(\tau\).
  The minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) divides \(P_g\) for some map (1), with all poles even rational and strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), **if and only if** some root of \(S\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(u)\).
  More generally every individual trace factor has as many real and complex places as those of the original trace field multiplied by its relative degree.
* If \(S\) is the trace polynomial of a reciprocal nontorsion unit of degree \(d=2m\) and bounded logarithmic Mahler measure, and all \(b_i\in[-2,2]\), then for every sufficiently large \(d\) and every \(g\ne X\) in (1), the reciprocal lift has at least \(c_0d\) distinct roots of modulus exceeding \(\exp(\operatorname{arcosh}(21/20))\).
  The constant \(c_0>0\) is absolute and only the degree threshold depends on the input measure bound.
  If all source traces are real the same result allows arbitrary real poles.
  Over all nonlinear maps (1), and also affine maps in (1) other than the identity, only finitely many original Salem numbers can have full inverse images of logarithmic Mahler measure at most any prescribed \(M\).
* For every totally real Pisot algebraic integer \(\theta\) of degree at least two, the Salem number \(\sigma\) of trace \(1+\theta\) has a sequence of such full inverse images with strictly smaller positive measure.
  Each has a unique Salem factor, with root \(\tau_h\) increasing to \(\theta<\sigma\) and trace field containing \(\mathbb Q(\theta)\) with unbounded relative degrees.
  The maps use the quotients of consecutive Chebyshev polynomials described below.
  For these quotients of degree \(h\ge2\) and every integral translation, all positive full inverse measures of Salem inputs have a uniform gap.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Which individual Salem factors occur

Each real fiber \(g(X)=y\) has one simple real root in each of the \(s\) intervals cut out by the poles.
On each interval the derivative is \(1/k+\sum_i w_i/(X-b_i)^2>0\) and the endpoint limits are \(-\infty,+\infty\).
Conversely, suppose \(A,B\) are coprime as above with \(\deg B<s\) and positive leading coefficient, and \(A-yB\) has only real roots for every real \(y\).
Then \(A/B\) has no pole in the upper half-plane, since a punctured neighborhood of a pole takes sufficiently large real values.
Its imaginary part is nonzero throughout that half-plane and therefore has constant sign.
If \(s-\deg B\ge2\), the leading power at infinity gives both signs along rays in the upper half-plane, so this difference is one and the sign is positive.
The same ray test at each real pole shows it is simple, and evaluation immediately above it gives a negative residue.
Thus (1) describes all these monic linear pencils with only real roots in every real fiber.
For \(z=x+iv\) with \(v\ne0\),
\[
       \Im g(z)=v\left(1/k+\sum_i w_i/|z-b_i|^2\right).              \tag{2}
\]
Consequently for an irreducible trace factor with a root \(u\), \(y=g(u)\) is a source trace and \(F=\mathbb Q(y)\subseteq K=\mathbb Q(u)\).
The embedded value of \(u\) is real exactly when the embedded value of \(y\) is real.
Each embedding of \(F\) has \(\ell=[K:F]\) extensions, hence \(r_1(K)=\ell r_1(F)\) and \(r_2(K)=\ell r_2(F)\), with \(r_2\) counting complex pairs.
In particular a source with a nonreal trace conjugate has no Salem factor in any such lift.
Every nonreal trace conjugate of a selected factor contributes one exterior root to its reciprocal lift.

For the converse, write \(T\) for the monic minimal polynomial of the specified Salem trace \(u>2\), of degree \(r\), whose other roots are \(x_1<\cdots<x_{r-1}\) in \((-2,2)\).
Choose any \(y\in\mathbb Q(u)\) and \(V\in\mathbb Q[X]\) with \(V(u)=y\).
Choose rational numbers \(-2<b_1<x_1<b_2<\cdots<x_{r-1}<b_r<2\).
Scale \(\prod_i(X-b_i)\) by a positive integer to obtain \(B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) such that \(VB\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
The remainder \(C\) of \(VB\) modulo the monic \(T\) is integral.
Put \(A=(X+\lambda)T+C\), with \(\lambda\) an integer.
It is monic of degree \(r+1\), and \(A(u)/B(u)=y\).
The alternating roots imply \(T(b_i)/B'(b_i)<0\) at every \(i\), since there are \(r-i+1\) roots of \(T\) above \(b_i\) and \(r-i\) roots of \(B\) there.
For all sufficiently large positive \(\lambda\), each residue \(A(b_i)/B'(b_i)\) is therefore strictly negative.
In particular \(A,B\) are coprime, and polynomial division and the simple-pole expansion give (1).
Taking \(y\) to be any root of the prescribed \(S\) in this field proves the sufficiency, including every possible relative degree of the field inclusion.

For example, take \(u=(1+\sqrt{13})/2,\ y=u+2\), with respective trace polynomials \(X^2-X-3\) and \(Y^2-5Y+3\).
Their other conjugates are strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), so adjoining a root of \(Z^2-uZ+1\) or \(Z^2-yZ+1\) doubles the real trace degree and yields a Salem number.
The map with \(A=X^3+9X^2+8X-3\), \(B=2X^2+3X\) has residues \(-1,-5/8\) at \(0,-3/2\), and
\[
        A^2-5AB+3B^2=(X^2-X-3)(X^4+9X^3+16X^2+2X-3).
\]
The factor at \(u\) gives a strictly smaller Salem number.
The norms of \(u^2-4\) and \(y^2-4\) from the common trace field are \(-3\) and \(-51\), so their quotient is not a square there.
The quadratic Salem fields differ, and no positive powers of these numbers coincide, since the unique exterior conjugate ensures that every positive power of a Salem number preserves its degree and field.

<a id="barriers"></a>
## Many conjugate fibers have an exterior inverse root

Set \(t=21/10,\ \eta=1/20\).
For \(k=1\) and \(s\ge2\), dividing the monic integer \(A\) by the monic integer \(B\) shows that \(\sum_i w_i\) is an integer, since its negative is the coefficient of \(1/X\) in the Laurent expansion at infinity.
It is positive, hence at least one.
For poles in \([-2,2]\), let
\[
  p_+=c+t-\sum_i w_i\frac{t-b_i}{(t-b_i)^2+\eta^2},\qquad
  p_-=c-t+\sum_i w_i\frac{t+b_i}{(t+b_i)^2+\eta^2}
\]
when \(k=1,\ s\ge2\).
On the right vertical line \(\Re z=t\), one has \(\Re g(z)\le p_+\) whenever \(|\Im g(z)|\le\eta\), since (2) forces \(|\Im z|\le\eta\).
Likewise \(\Re z=-t\) gives \(\Re g(z)\ge p_-\) within that horizontal band.
Each \(a=t\pm b_i\) is at least \(1/10\), so \(a/(a^2+\eta^2)\ge4/(5a)\).
Since \(1/(t-b_i)+1/(t+b_i)\ge2/t\), we have
\[
             p_+-p_-\le 2t-8/(5t)<18/5.                            \tag{3}
\]
If \(k\ge2\), take \(p_\pm=c\pm t/k\).
On the right line all real reciprocal-pole contributions are negative and on the left all are positive, so the corresponding inequalities hold at every imaginary height, and \(p_+-p_-\le t<18/5\).

It follows that \(p_+<9/5\) or \(p_->-9/5\).
In the first case take any source value with \(\Re y\ge9/5\) and \(|\Im y|\le\eta\).
For the real fiber at \(\Re y\) the rightmost root exceeds \(t\), since \(g(t)\le p_+<\Re y\).
Continuously varying the imaginary part from zero to \(\Im y\) leaves the number of roots in \(\Re z>t\), counted with multiplicity, unchanged.
There are no roots on the separating line, and the coefficients of the monic fiber polynomial stay bounded on this segment.
The second case gives a root in \(\Re z<-t\) for every \(\Re y\le-9/5,\ |\Im y|\le\eta\).
If \(s=k=1\), then \(g=X+c\) with integral \(c\), and for \(c\ne0\) the band of the opposite sign gives \(|\Re(y-c)|>t\) directly.

For entirely real source traces, a pole with \(|b_i|\ge t\) supplies a root beyond that outer pole in every real fiber.
If all \(|b_i|<t\), use the real endpoints \(g(\pm t)\) in place of \(p_\pm\).
Their difference is at most \(2t/k-(2/t)\sum_i w_i\), which is less than \(18/5\) in the same cases as before.
This proves the band conclusion for arbitrary real poles and real source traces.

Write the original reciprocal unit conjugates as \(e^{a+i\phi}\).
Their absolute logarithms sum to twice the bounded input measure, so only boundedly many have \(|a|>1/100\).
By the [short-arc conjugate count](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), each fixed arc of angular half-width \(1/10\) centered at \(0\) and at \(\pi\) contains at least a fixed positive fraction of the original \(d\) roots for all sufficiently large \(d\).
The corresponding traces have real parts \(2\cosh a\cos\phi\) and imaginary parts \(2\sinh a\sin\phi\).
After omitting the bounded exceptional set these give a positive fraction in each of the two bands above, even after dividing counts by two for the inverse-pair repetitions.
Different source values have disjoint inverse fibers.
For each resulting inverse trace \(z\) with \(|\Re z|>t\), the root \(\lambda\) of \(Z+Z^{-1}=z\) outside the unit circle satisfies \(|\Re z|\le 2\cosh(\log|\lambda|)\).
This proves the asserted number of exterior roots and a full logarithmic measure lower bound \(c_0d\operatorname{arcosh}(21/20)\).
Any divisor of bounded measure must omit at least \(c_0d-O(1)\) of these roots.
For a trace factor of relative degree \(\ell\), the remaining trace degree is exactly \(m(s-\ell)\), since the selected factor has degree \(m\ell\).
The field criterion above explains why no analogous lower bound on the measure of every individual factor follows.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Finitely many original Salem inputs at bounded full cost

Let the full inverse of a Salem input have logarithmic measure at most \(M\).
All inverse traces are real and have absolute value less than \(T=1+2\cosh M\), since their reciprocal lifts are monic units and occur in inverse pairs.
The same interval contains all poles by the ordering of each real fiber.
On the exterior branches, every original trace \(y\) therefore satisfies \(g(-T)<y<g(T)\), whence \(|y-c|<T/k\le T\) by (1).
At least one original trace lies in \((-2,2)\), so \(|c|<2+T\), and the distinguished trace is less than \(2+2T\).
This bounds the original Salem number using only \(M\).
The large-degree lower bound for arbitrary real poles then bounds its degree in terms of \(M\), for \(g\ne X\).
Bounded degree and bounded Salem number bound all coefficients of its monic minimal polynomial, proving the finiteness assertion.

<a id="chebyshev"></a>
## An explicit family of full strict descents above a totally real Pisot number

Write \(V_j(X)=U_j(X/2)\), the monic second-kind Chebyshev polynomials with \(V_0=1,\ V_1=X,\ V_{j+1}=XV_j-V_{j-1}\).
For \(h\ge2\), \(g_{h,c}=c+V_h/V_{h-1}\) satisfies (1) for each \(c\in\mathbb Z\).
Indeed \(V_{h-1}\) has simple roots \(2\cos(j\pi/h)\), \(1\le j<h\), strictly interlacing the roots of \(V_{h-2}\), so \(V_{h-2}/V_{h-1}\) has strictly positive residues and \(V_h/V_{h-1}=X-V_{h-2}/V_{h-1}\).
The endpoint values for \(c=0\) are \(\pm(1+1/h)\).
For \(z>1\), direct substitution in the recurrence gives
\[
    g_{h,0}(z+z^{-1})=
       \frac{z^{h+1}-z^{-(h+1)}}{z^h-z^{-h}}
        =z+\frac{z-z^{-1}}{z^{2h}-1}.                              \tag{4}
\]

Let \(\theta>1\) be a totally real Pisot integer of degree at least two and take the original trace \(y=1+\theta\).
Its remaining conjugates lie in \((0,2)\).
The discriminant \(y^2-4\) is negative at those real embeddings, so \(Z^2-yZ+1\) generates a quadratic extension of the trace field and its root \(\sigma>1\) is Salem.
Since \(1+\theta>\theta+\theta^{-1}\), one has \(\sigma>\theta\).
Use \(g_{h,1}\) and take \(h\ge2\) with \(h>1/(\theta-1)\).
Every nondistinguished source conjugate gives \(h\) inverse traces strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), by the endpoint values and the real fiber ordering.
The distinguished fiber has exactly one root above \(2\) and none below \(-2\).
Thus the entire reciprocal lift has one exterior root \(\tau_h\) and one interior root \(\tau_h^{-1}\).
Its irreducible factor containing \(\tau_h\) must also contain the only available interior root, since its constant coefficient has absolute value one.
The remaining factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker.
Moreover \(\theta=g_{h,0}(\tau_h+\tau_h^{-1})\), so \(\mathbb Q(\theta)\) lies in the new trace field, excluding a reciprocal quadratic and proving that \(\tau_h\) is Salem.
Equation (4) gives \(\tau_h<\theta\), and its right side strictly decreases with \(h\) for \(z>1\) and strictly increases with \(z\) at fixed \(h\).
It follows that \(\tau_h\) strictly increases, and every \(1<z<\theta\) eventually satisfies \(g_{h,0}(z+z^{-1})<\theta\).
Hence \(\tau_h\to\theta\).
Distinct bounded Salem numbers have degrees tending to infinity by coefficient finiteness, giving the claimed unbounded relative trace degrees.
For instance, \(\theta=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) gives the original Salem trace \((3+\sqrt5)/2\), and the construction works for every \(h\ge2\).

Finally suppose positive full measures of \(g_{h,c}\) at some Salem inputs tend to zero with varying integral \(c\) and \(h\ge2\).
The finiteness result bounds the inputs to a finite set, and its bound on the constant at infinity also bounds \(c\).
Fix an input and translation by passage to a subsequence.
Bounded \(h\) leaves a finite set of full measures, so we may assume \(h\to\infty\).
Some conjugate \(y\) of the input trace has \(|y-c|>1\), for otherwise the algebraic integer \(y-c\) has every conjugate in \([-1,1]\), forcing \(y-c=0,\pm1\) by Kronecker and realness, contrary to trace degree at least two.
By (4) and oddness of \(V_h/V_{h-1}\), the corresponding exterior root modulus tends to \(|y-c|>1\).
This contradicts shrinking full measure and proves the uniform positive gap in this family.
The explicit strict descents give positive limiting measures and the field criterion permits arbitrary noncyclotomic cofactors.
Neither assertion constructs Salem numbers tending to one.
