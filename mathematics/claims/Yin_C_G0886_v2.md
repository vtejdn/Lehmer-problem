# Canonical Coxeter covers for standard and E8-block cusp words

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statements and exact domains

For a positive definite integral lattice \(H\), put \(L=H\oplus U\), where \(e^2=f^2=0\) and \((e,f)=1\).
Extend each positive integral isometry to fix \(e,f\), and for every \(v\in H\) of even squared norm use
\[
T_{e,v}(x)=x-(v,x)e+(e,x)v-\tfrac12(v,v)(e,x)e,
\]
with \(T_{f,v}\) defined by exchanging \(e,f\).
Write \(\Gamma(H)\) for the group generated by all these positive isometries and both fixed-cusp transvection groups.
For \(A\in\Gamma(H)\), let \(P_A(z)=\det(zI-A)\).
Let \(\lambda_{\rm L}>1\) be the Salem root of
\[
z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1.
\]

**Scalar standard lattices.**
For every pair of positive integers \(c,g\), every \(A\in\Gamma(cI_g)\) satisfies
\[
M(P_A)=\rho(A),\qquad
M(P_A)=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}.       \tag{1}
\]
The bound is independent of \(c,g\), word length, all vector norms and coordinates, and the number and orders of the positive-isometry factors.
All even-norm parameters, including zero and negative parameters, are included.

**Retained E8-block statement.**
For \(H=E_8^{\oplus r}\), \(r\ge1\), take a specified word for \(A\), multiply the permutations of the positive E8 blocks in the order of its positive-isometry factors, and let \(d\) be the order of that product permutation.
Then the [adopted v1 theorem](Yin_C_G0886_v1.md#decision-e8-block-canonical-cover) gives
\[
M(P_A)=\rho(A),\qquad
M(P_A)=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}^{1/d}. \tag{2}
\]
This permutation remains a property of the specified presentation; no homomorphism from the entire generated group to the block permutation group is asserted.
Every subfamily admitting presentations with \(d\le D\) has the gap \(\log\lambda_{\rm L}/D\), independently of \(r\), word length and parameter norms.
For \(r=3\), the bound is \(\lambda_{\rm L}^{1/3}\).
In a sequence with \(1<M(P_A)\to1\), every such choice of presentation must have \(d\to\infty\).
These assertions retain exactly the v1 scope.

**The strict search interval.**
For every scalar standard lattice in (1), and also for every E8-block presentation in (2) with \(d=1\),
\[
1<M(P_A)<6/5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(P_A)=\lambda_{\rm L}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus these domains contain no other complete Mahler value in the specified open interval.
No new value, unrestricted positive-to-one family, or resolution of the full Lehmer-gap Task is asserted.

No concrete source objection affecting these stated interfaces is known.

For a norm-two vector \(a\), write
\[
s_a(x)=x-(a,x)a.
\]
A real basis of norm-two vectors with off-diagonal pairings \(-2\cos(\pi/m)\), including \(-2\) for \(m=\infty\), is exactly the source's canonical convention.
The basis need not be an integral basis of the original lattice.
A real change of basis preserves the entire characteristic polynomial and spectral radius; integrality of \(A\) is already supplied by the original generators.

<a id="standard-rank-two-up"></a>
### Standard positive lattices of rank at least two

Let \(g\ge2\), and let \(u_1,\ldots,u_g\) be the orthonormal integral basis of \(I_g\).
Choose the following \(g+2\) real vectors in \(L_{\mathbb R}\):
\[
a_i=u_i-u_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<g),\qquad
a_g=\sqrt2\,u_g,\qquad
b=e-u_1-u_2,\qquad c_0=-e-f.                              \tag{4}
\]
The \(a_i\)'s span the positive part, \(b\) adds the \(e\) direction, and \(c_0\) adds the \(f\) direction.
They are therefore a basis of the entire real space.
All their squared norms are two.

Among the \(a_i\), adjacent pairings are minus one except for
\((a_{g-1},a_g)=-\sqrt2\); all other distinct pairings are zero.
Also \((c_0,b)=-1\) and \((c_0,a_i)=0\).
When \(g=2\), one has \((b,a_1)=0\) and \((b,a_2)=-\sqrt2\).
When \(g\ge3\), the only nonzero pairing between \(b\) and the \(a_i\)'s is \((b,a_2)=-1\).
Thus (4) has precisely a canonical Coxeter Gram matrix, with exponents \(2,3,4\) according to the pairings \(0,-1,-\sqrt2\).
Let \(W_g\) be the subgroup of real linear transformations generated by its reflections; it is the actual image of that canonical geometric representation.

For \(i<g\), \(s_{a_i}\) swaps \(u_i,u_{i+1}\), and \(s_{a_g}\) changes the sign of \(u_g\).
These generate every signed permutation of the positive basis.
Every integral \(I_g\)-isometry is such a signed permutation, since each of its integer columns has Euclidean squared norm one.
Consequently all permitted positive isometries belong to \(W_g\).

Put \(\theta=u_1+u_2\).
Signed permutations take \(a_1=u_1-u_2\) to every vector \(\pm u_i\pm u_j\), \(i\ne j\), so their norm-two reflections lie in \(W_g\), including \(s_\theta\).
As \(b=e-\theta\), direct substitution gives
\[
s_b s_\theta=T_{e,\theta}.                               \tag{5}
\]
Conjugation by positive signed permutations and the [addition and inverse identities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#short-word-reduction) therefore put \(T_{e,v}\) in \(W_g\) for every integral sum of the roots \(\pm u_i\pm u_j\).

The allowed even-norm parameters are exactly
\[
D_g=\left\{\sum_i v_i u_i:\ v_i\in\mathbb Z,\ 
                             \sum_i v_i\equiv0\pmod2\right\},              \tag{6}
\]
because \(v_i^2\equiv v_i\pmod2\).
These roots generate all of (6), including when \(g=2\).
Indeed, writing \(s=\sum_i v_i\), one has
\[
v=\sum_{i<g}v_i(u_i-u_g)+s u_g,\qquad
2u_g=(u_1+u_g)-(u_1-u_g),
\]
and \(s\) is even.
Thus every permitted e-transvection belongs to \(W_g\), with no restriction on its norm.

The reflection \(s_{c_0}\) sends \(e\) to \(-f\), sends \(f\) to \(-e\), and fixes the positive summand.
Conjugating the transvection formula gives
\[
s_{c_0}T_{e,v}s_{c_0}=T_{f,-v}.                          \tag{7}
\]
Hence every permitted f-transvection is also in \(W_g\).
All specified words over \(I_g\), of any length, are therefore covered in the original full real dimension.

<a id="standard-rank-one"></a>
### The rank-one endpoint

For \(g=1\), write \(u^2=1\).
The even-norm integral parameters are exactly \(2\mathbb Z u\).
Choose the full real basis
\[
a=\sqrt2\,u,\qquad b=\sqrt2(e-u),\qquad c_0=-e-f.          \tag{8}
\]
Its vectors have squared norm two, with pairings
\[
(a,b)=-2,\qquad (b,c_0)=-\sqrt2,\qquad (a,c_0)=0.
\]
These are exactly canonical exponents \(\infty,4,2\), including the required value minus two for the infinite edge.
The reflection \(s_a\) changes the sign of \(u\), so it supplies the full positive integral isometry group.
For every \(x\),
\[
s_b s_a(x)
=x+2(e,x)u-2(u,x)e-2(e,x)e
=T_{e,2u}(x).                                           \tag{9}
\]
Addition and inverse give all e-transvections, and (7) gives all f-transvections.
This proves the same full-dimensional canonical coverage at rank one.
An empty positive summand, if allowed, contributes only the identity action and is neutral.

<a id="standard-gap"></a>
### Complete measure and the standard-lattice gap

Every permitted generator is an integral isometry by the [even-norm transvection calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#integer-generators).
The form has negative index one.
The [complete generalized-eigenspace bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#negative-index-bound) gives at most one expanding eigenvalue counted with algebraic multiplicity.
The spectrum is reciprocal, so \(\rho(A)\ge1\) and the complete monic Mahler measure is exactly
\[
M(P_A)=\rho(A).                                         \tag{10}
\]
This includes the case when every eigenvalue is on the unit circle.
By (4)--(9), the actual action of \(A\) is the action of an element of the canonical Coxeter group in its full space, up to real similarity.
McM02 Theorem 1.1 therefore gives \(M(P_A)=1\) or \(M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}\) for all \(g\) when \(c=1\).
No isometry-order or word-length cost is introduced.

### Scalar standard lattices through an exact neutral embedding

Let \(H=cI_g\), with any positive integer \(c\).
Define an integer embedding
\[
V:\mathbb Z^g\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{cg},\qquad
Vv=(v,\ldots,v),
\]
using \(c\) identical blocks.
Then \(V^{\mathsf T}V=cI_g\), so it is an isometric embedding of the stated positive integral lattice into \(I_{cg}\).
Keep the same hyperbolic plane \(U\).

Every positive integral \(cI_g\)-isometry is again a signed permutation \(R\).
Replace it by \(R'=\operatorname{diag}(R,\ldots,R)\) on the standard positive lattice, fixing \(U\).
Replace each transvection parameter \(v\) by \(Vv\).
Its squared norm is exactly the original value \(c\sum v_i^2\), so the even-norm condition is unchanged.
The resulting word \(A'\) belongs to \(\Gamma(I_{cg})\).

The rational subspace \(V(\mathbb Q^g)\oplus U_{\mathbb Q}\) is invariant under each new generator, and the restricted action is exactly the original generator on \(H_{\mathbb Q}\oplus U_{\mathbb Q}\).
The positive orthogonal complement is
\[
K=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_c)\in(\mathbb Q^g)^c:\ \sum_jx_j=0\}.
\]
All transvections fix \(K\) pointwise.
A positive factor acts there as \(c-1\) copies of \(R\), for example in the coordinates \(x_1,\ldots,x_{c-1}\), with \(x_c=-\sum_{j<c}x_j\).
If \(R_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is the ordered product of the original positive factors, the full characteristic polynomials therefore satisfy
\[
P_{A'}(z)=\chi_{R_{\mathrm{tot}}}(z)^{c-1}P_A(z).         \tag{11}
\]
For \(c=1\), the complement is zero and the exponent is zero.
The signed permutation \(R_{\mathrm{tot}}\) has finite order, so its complete characteristic polynomial is cyclotomic.
Equation (11) gives exactly
\[
M(P_{A'})=M(P_A),\qquad \rho(A')=\rho(A),                 \tag{12}
\]
using \(\rho(A)\ge1\).
No primitive integral splitting of this rational orthogonal decomposition is needed for the characteristic-polynomial identity.
The already proved standard-lattice result for \(A'\) proves (1) for every \(c,g\).

\widetilde A^d\in W,\qquad

## Search consequences and remaining domains

Neither increasing rank nor allowing arbitrarily large transvection parameters or arbitrarily long words over \(cI_g\) can yield a sequence of positive complete measures tending to one.
In the strict interval \(1<M<6/5\), these entire domains can only reproduce the Lehmer value.
This includes the standard scalar active lattices occurring in the already excluded growing-cycle commutator construction, without replacing its stronger family-specific lower bound.

The result does not identify \(\Gamma(cI_g)\) with the full indefinite integral isometry group.
It does not cover an arbitrary positive integral Gram matrix merely by allowing a real diagonalization: the actual integral parameters and positive-isometry extensions must satisfy the proved generator interface.
The E8-block domain retains its permutation-order cost and does not acquire an absolute all-word bound when that order is unbounded.
The degree-18 model of complete measure approximately \(1.188368\) belongs to its separate block-permutation extension, consistently with these limitations.

For general positive lattices the [bounded-count and bounded-norm theorem](Yin_C_G0885_v3.md#decision-bounded-word-bounded-norm-gap) remains a different restriction, including rootless lattices.
A counterexample investigation outside the present covers still needs an actual integer construction and complete nonneutral measure estimate.
None is supplied by merely leaving a canonical group or increasing a presentation parameter.
The unrestricted [G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) remains unresolved.
