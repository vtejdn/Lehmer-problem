# Computing the complete measure spectrum of a given divisible conjugate group of radial rank one

<a id="statement"></a>
## All exponents, their eventual periods and the attained minimum

Let \(w\) be any nontorsion algebraic unit of radial rank one.
Use \(V,D(w),G,L\) as in the [measure-field description](Yin_C_G0901_v96.md#statement) and let \(\eta>1\) be the primitive positive unit on the measure line in \(F=\mathbb Q(M(w))\) from the [common-field theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#statement).
The input to the following finite determination is the minimal polynomial of \(w\) and its chosen embedding; no list of generators for the unbounded radical extension is required.
Define the indices
\[
              E(w)=\{j\ge1:M(x)=\eta^j\text{ for some }x\in D(w)\}.
\]
Then \(E(w)\) is a finite union of effectively determined additive subsemigroups of the positive integers.
For each semigroup one computes an attained positive index \(p\) and a list of pairs \((a,j_a)\) for some residue classes modulo \(p\).
Its indices are exactly
\[
                        \bigcup_a\{j_a+np:n=0,1,\ldots\}.                \tag{1}
\]
One can in particular find the least measure in the entire radical group, a concrete unit of measure equal to it, and decide whether \(\eta\) itself is attained.

For each semigroup one also obtains an integer \(b>0\) as the greatest common divisor of a linear functional on a computed lattice basis.
The semigroup contains only multiples of \(b\) and contains all sufficiently large multiples of \(b\).
Thus outside an explicitly computable finite range, membership in \(E(w)\) is just divisibility by at least one of this finite list of integers \(b\).
In particular the spectrum contains every sufficiently large power of \(\eta\) if and only if at least one of the computed integers is one.
These assertions include the case where iteration after the first step has exponent greater than one.
The minimum and the eventual periods can depend on \(w\).

<a id="lattices"></a>
## Finite fields that realize all measures exactly

We first give an exact reduction valid even outside radial rank one.
For any \(x\in D(w)\), form \(y\) as the product of all conjugates of \(x\) belonging to its class modulo roots of unity, including \(x\) itself.
The [class-product compression](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) gives \(M(y)=M(x)\), and \(y\) is a unit whose distinct conjugates have distinct classes.
Every element of the absolute Galois group fixing \([x]\) permutes this entire class fiber, hence fixes its actual product.
Since \([y]\) is a positive integral multiple of \([x]\), any automorphism fixing \(y\) must fix \([x]\) as well.
Consequently \(y\in L^{H}\), where \(H\le G\) is the stabilizer of its class, and \(\mathbb Q(y)=L^H\).

Enumerate the subgroups \(H\le G\) and set \(K_H=L^H\).
Write \(\Lambda_H\) for the image in \(V\) of the units of \(K_H\) belonging to \(D(w)\), modulo the torsion of \(K_H\).
For each \(H\), use precisely the nonzero \(v\in\Lambda_H\) whose \(G\)-stabilizer is \(H\).
Every such \(v\) has an actual unit representative \(y\in K_H\); its stabilizer as a number lies in the class stabilizer and contains \(H\), so it has degree \([G:H]\).
All representatives in \(K_H\) differ by torsion and have the same height and degree for this class.
Conversely every value from any torsion or radical extension has already occurred in this list, by the preceding product construction.

These fields and lattices can be obtained by finite number-field arithmetic.
For details take the original normal closure \(L_0\), its unit group with its torsion, and represent its units modulo torsion in an integral basis of the free unit group.
The classes of the conjugates of \(w\) give the rational subspace \(V\) in these coordinates.
The action of the finite Galois group on \(V\) gives its kernel, \(L\) and the fixed fields \(K_H\).
Inclusion of a unit basis of \(K_H\) in the unit coordinates of \(L_0\) followed by integral linear algebra computes its intersection with \(V\) and actual unit representatives of a basis of \(\Lambda_H\).
Equivalently the intersection is the integer kernel of the rational equations for \(V\) on those coordinates.
Here the usual effective arithmetic of number fields includes the computation of a full system of fundamental units, with a certified index or a full exhaustive unit-group algorithm.
The definition using all roots of unity introduces no additional rational directions in these lattices.

<a id="cones"></a>
## Rational conditions on an actual fixed-field unit lattice

The field \(F\) and the actual product \(M(w)\) can be obtained by deciding which algebraic conjugates have modulus greater than one.
Unit coordinates of \(M(w)\) in \(F\), divided by their greatest common divisor and oriented positively at the embedding, give \(\eta\).
For \(a\) a representative of a basis vector of \(\Lambda_H\), the number \(\log|g a|/\log\eta\) is rational for every \(g\in G\).
Indeed both logarithms belong to the nonzero rational line of conjugate log moduli of \(w\).
These rational numbers are computable exactly: enumerate integer pairs with positive denominator and test \((g a\,\overline{g a})^{n}=\eta^{2m}\) in the algebraic closure, stopping at a match to get the ratio \(m/n\).
This test includes zero and negative numerators and provably terminates for every entry.

In the coordinates of a basis of \(\Lambda_H\), impose the condition \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(v)=H\).
Each unwanted stabilizer gives the excluded rational linear space \(\ker(g-1)\) for some \(g\notin H\).
Take the finite central hyperplane arrangement given by the coordinate equations for all these kernels, the finitely many forms \(\ell(gv)/\log\eta\), and the individual basis-coordinate hyperplanes.
Discard identically zero forms, the origin and the relatively open faces on which an unwanted element fixes every point.
On each remaining face every lattice vector has exactly the required stabilizer, and the signs of all the log moduli are fixed, including exact zeros.
For the resulting relatively open rational cone \(C\), let \(E_C\) denote its linear span and choose one representative of each left coset \(gH\) of \(H\) in \(G\).
In \(C\) the index of the measure of an actual representative of \(v\) is the value of a single rational linear functional
\[
                  f_C(v)=\sum_{\substack{gH\in G/H\\\ell(gv)>0}}
                                       \ell(gv)/\log\eta .                \tag{2}
\]
There is one term for each exterior conjugate, since the degree and the actual stabilizer have already been established.
On \(\Lambda_H\cap C\), (2) is always a positive integer by the common-field theorem and Kronecker's theorem.
All nonempty rational cones retained meet the lattice in their span, by scaling a rational relative-interior point.

<a id="indices"></a>
## From each cone to a finite list of progressions

Put \(\Lambda=\Lambda_H\cap E_C\), \(S=f_C(\Lambda\cap C)\).
The relatively open cone is closed under addition and gives \(S+S\subset S\).
Choose one lattice point \(v_0\in C\), and set \(p=f_C(v_0)>0\).
For any \(u\in\Lambda\), both \(N v_0\) and \(N v_0+u\) belong to \(\Lambda\cap C\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Thus the additive group generated by \(S\) is \(f_C(\Lambda)=b\mathbb Z\); the functional is integral on this entire lattice and the positive integer \(b\) is its gcd on a basis.
In particular \(b\mid p\).
The image of \(S\) modulo \(p\) is a nonempty finite additive semigroup in a group, hence a subgroup, and is the image of \(b\mathbb Z\).
For each multiple \(a\) of \(b\) modulo \(p\) there is a least attained index \(j_a>0\) in that class.
All \(j_a+np\) are attained by adding copies of \(v_0\), and every member of that class in \(S\) equals such a sum.

For a direct finite procedure, the search for \(j_a\) enumerates the positive integers in that residue class in increasing order, testing \(f_C(v)=j\) with \(v\in\Lambda\cap C\).
This is integer linear feasibility in a computed lattice basis: equalities and rational strict inequalities describing the face become integer equalities and weak inequalities with integral bounds after clearing denominators.
A standard integer linear feasibility algorithm terminates and can return a point on a feasible instance.
Even a bounded enumeration suffices here: in \(C\) a representative \(y\in K_H\) of a solution has \(h(y)=j\log\eta/[G:H]\).
The archimedean logarithm embedding of the finite unit lattice bounds the norm of its coordinate vector in terms of this height.
The same bound can be obtained by inverting a nonsingular minor of the unit logarithm matrix to sufficient precision.
Existence of \(j_a\) proves termination of the outer search in each selected residue.
Lifting the returned lattice point gives an actual algebraic unit of the requested measure.
After taking the finite union over the cones and subgroups, (1) gives the whole spectrum.
Beyond the maximum of the finitely many \(j_a\), the union contains exactly the multiples of one of the numbers \(b\).
If each \(b>1\), primes above the maximum of these numbers and of the thresholds are excluded.
This proves the criterion for containing every sufficiently large power and completes the effective determination.
