# A quadratic lower bound for the logarithmic row resultant on a full dyadic prime interval

<a id="statement"></a>
## The totient sum and the resultant bound

For an integer \(L\ge2\), let \(S\) be the set of all primes in \([L,2L]\), write \(r=|S|\) and \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\), and let \(B\) be the set of sums of two distinct elements of \(S\).
As \(L\to\infty\), Euler's totient satisfies
\[
             \sum_{n\in B}\varphi(n)\asymp L^2,\qquad
             \sum_{n\in S+S}\varphi(n)\asymp L^2 .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The implied constants are absolute, and either sum divided by \(\Sigma\) is of order \(\log L\).

Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) with monic reciprocal minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(e\), and write \(h=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Suppose nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), satisfy
\[
                     P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\qquad(p\in S).
\]
Their ordinary resultant \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) satisfies
\[
          e\log|R|\ \ge\ 4h\sum_{n\in B}\varphi(n)-O_P(L^{3/2}\log L).
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular every such pair has \(\log|R|\gg_P L^2\) and \(\log|R|/\Sigma\gg_P\log L\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
For each fixed seed as above and all sufficiently large \(L\), there exist such pairs with nonzero reflected determinant, degrees at most \(2er+2\) and \(\log H\le(2h+o(1))\Sigma\), where \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) and \(\|\cdot\|_1\) sums the absolute values of the coefficients.

<a id="fourth-moment"></a>
## A fourth moment for the primes in the interval

Write \(e(t)=\exp(2\pi it)\) in this section, \(F(\theta)=\sum_{p\in S}(\log p)e(p\theta)\), and \(D=(\log L)^{20}\).
The [prime number theorem at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) gives \(F(0)\sim L\), \(r\sim L/\log L\) and \(\Sigma\asymp L^2/\log L\).
On the unit circle take the union of arcs \(\theta=a/q+\beta\) with \(1\le q\le D\), \(a\bmod q\) reduced, and \(|\beta|\le 2D/(qL)\), with distances taken modulo one.
Outside this union, Dirichlet approximation with \(Q=\lfloor L/D\rfloor\) gives a reduced \(a/q\) with \(D<q\le Q\) and \(|\theta-a/q|\le1/(qQ)\le q^{-2}\), choosing a lift of \(\theta\) to the real line.
Indeed any denominator at most \(D\) would put \(\theta\) in the union because \(1/Q\le 2D/L\) for large \(L\).
The [Vaughan bound for one frequency](../audits/literature/Vau77/Yin_U001.md#prime-exponential-sum) at \(X=L-1,2L\) bounds each initial von Mangoldt sum by
\[
          O\!\left((\log L)^7(Lq^{-1/2}+L^{4/5}+\sqrt{Lq})\right).
\]
Subtract the two initial sums and remove the prime powers of exponent at least two, whose total weight is \(O(\sqrt L(\log L)^2)\) by counting bases separately for each exponent.
The denominator range then gives \(|F(\theta)|\ll L/(\log L)^2\) outside the arcs.
As \(\int_0^1|F|^2=\sum_{p\in S}(\log p)^2\ll L\log L\), the contribution of their complement to \(\int_0^1|F|^4\) is \(O(L^3)\).

On an arc put \(I(\beta)=\int_L^{2L}e(\beta t)\,dt\).
For \(t\in[L,2L]\) and each reduced class \(b\bmod q\), [Siegel–Walfisz for the von Mangoldt sum](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) gives the initial sum \(t/\varphi(q)+O(L/(\log L)^{60})\), uniformly in \(q\le D\).
Removing higher prime powers costs \(O(\sqrt L(\log L)^2)\) in each class and is absorbed by this error.
All primes in \(S\) are coprime to \(q\) since \(q<L\).
Partial summation against \(e(\beta t)\) multiplies the error by at most \(O(1+L|\beta|)\), including the endpoint prime at \(L\) in the error.
Summing over \(b\) with weights \(e(ab/q)\) gives
\[
                 F(a/q+\beta)=\frac{\mu(q)}{\varphi(q)} I(\beta)
                                  +O(L/(\log L)^{40}).
\]
Here \(q(1+L|\beta|)\le3D\), and the reduced-class exponential sum equals \(\mu(q)\) by expanding the coprimality indicator with Möbius inversion and summing the resulting geometric series.
The sum of all arc lengths is \(O(D^2/L)\).
Since \(|I(\beta)|\ll\min(L,|\beta|^{-1})\), its fourth power integrates over \(\mathbb R\) to \(O(L^3)\).
For \(j=\omega(q)\), the \(i\)-th smallest distinct prime factor of \(q\) is at least \(i+1\), so \(\varphi(q)\ge q/(j+1)\ge q/(1+\log_2 q)\).
Thus \(\sum_{q\ge1}\varphi(q)^{-3}<\infty\).
Using \(|x+y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) and summing over the \(\varphi(q)\) choices of numerator bounds the fourth moment on the union by
\[
       O\!\left(L^3\sum_{q\ge1}\varphi(q)^{-3}
                  +\frac{D^2}{L}\left(\frac{L}{(\log L)^{40}}\right)^4\right)
         =O(L^3).
\]

<a id="totients"></a>
## Positive density and the totient weight

Let \(b_n\) be the coefficient of \(e(n\theta)\) in \(F(\theta)^2\).
These coefficients are nonnegative and supported on \(S+S\), with sum \(F(0)^2\sim L^2\).
By Parseval, \(\sum_n b_n^2=\int_0^1|F|^4\ll L^3\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|S+S|\gg L\), and removing the at most \(r=o(L)\) sums that require equal summands gives \(|B|\ge cL\) for an absolute \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(L\).

Put \(A(n)=\sum_{\ell\mid n,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}1/\ell\).
Summing first over multiples of each prime gives \(\sum_{n\le4L} A(n)\le4L\sum_{\ell\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{-2}\).
Choose a fixed \(T>8c^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{-2}\).
At least \(cL/2\) of the elements of \(B\) satisfy \(A(n)\le T\).
For these \(n\), the inequality \(\log(1-1/\ell)\ge-2/\ell\) gives \(\varphi(n)\ge n\exp(-2T)\ge 2L\exp(-2T)\).
This proves the lower bound in (1) for each set of sums.
The upper bound follows from \(S+S\subset[2L,4L]\) and \(\varphi(n)\le n\).

<a id="resultant"></a>
## Applying the simultaneous cyclotomic divisor

For large \(L\) there are at least two distinct primes \(p,q\in S\).
Their nodes \(\alpha^p,\alpha^q\) are distinct since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion, and at both nodes \(V\ne0\) by coprimality.
The rational function \(-U/V\) takes the value \(\alpha\) at both nodes.
It is nonconstant because \(\alpha\) is irrational, as every rational algebraic unit is torsion.
If \(\deg U+\deg V\le1\), this nonconstant function is fractional linear and hence injective, a contradiction.
The degree hypothesis of the [fourth-power resultant divisor](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#statement) therefore holds for every common pair here.

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(L_S=\prod_{p\in S}p\).
Take the absolute norm of the divisor and retain just the indices \(n\in B\), since the other cyclotomic norms are nonzero integers.
This yields
\[
        e\log|R|\ge4\sum_{n\in B}\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Phi_n(\alpha)|
                       -8e\log L_S-4\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})|.
\]
The [Möbius estimate for each cyclotomic norm](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) gives main term \(h\varphi(n)\) with error \(O_P(2^{\omega(n)}\log(2n))\).
Since \(2\le\sqrt\ell\) for primes \(\ell\ge5\), the two smaller primes cost at most a fixed factor in \(2^{\omega(n)}\ll\sqrt n\).
There are at most \(2L+1\) indices \(n\in B\), all at most \(4L\), and \(\log L_S\le r\log(2L)=O(L)\).
The resulting total error is \(O_P(L^{3/2}\log L)\), proving (2).

For each fixed \(P\), only finitely many primes \(p\) satisfy \([\mathbb Q(\alpha^p):\mathbb Q]<e\).
Once \(L\) exceeds these exceptional exponents, the [common interpolation construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) gives both rows with degrees at most \(2er+2\) and \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(W^\iota(Y)=W(1/Y)\).
Its bound reads \(\log H\le2h\Sigma+2er\log2+\log(5L_Ser)\).
The two excess terms total \(O_P(L)=o(\Sigma)\), proving the asserted coefficient cost for the full interval.
