# A finite positive lower bound on the optimized numerator loss

<a id="statement"></a>
## Approaching zero loss at a fixed polynomial

Let \(P\) satisfy the hypotheses of the [integer-numerator transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v605.md#bound), put \(u=|P|\) on the unit circle and use normalized angular measure \(\mu\).
Write \(G\) for the real polynomial with no zero on the closed disk, \(G(0)>0\), and \(|G|^2=4u\) on the circle.
It is obtained by taking the exterior root from each pair \(\beta,1/\bar\beta\) of roots of \(P\), keeping conjugate roots together, and scaling the constant term by a positive real number to match the modulus at one point.
Indeed \(|z-\beta|=|\beta|\,|z-1/\bar\beta|\) on the circle, so the squared modulus of that product differs from \(4u\) by a positive constant only.
Its coefficients are real algebraic numbers.
For a germ \(f=\sum_{j\ge0}f_jX^j\), let \(L_n(f)=(f_{i-j})_{0\le i,j<n}\) with \(f_j=0\) for \(j<0\).
Use the Euclidean operator norm and define
\[
 s_n(P)=\min_{\substack{a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb Z\\a_0\ne0}}
 \left\|L_n\!\left((a_0+\cdots+a_{n-1}X^{n-1})/G\right)\right\|.
\]
For every nonzero integer polynomial \(A\), let \(\ell_P(A)=\int D(|A|^2/u)\,d\mu\), with \(D\) as in the transfer.
Then at every depth \(n\),
\[
                    \ell_P(A)\ \ge\ \frac{2}{n}\log^+ s_n(P).
\]
There exists a numerator satisfying \(|A|^2\le4u\) everywhere if and only if \(s_n(P)\le1\) at every depth.
Each \(s_n(P)\) is exactly computable, so a failed [pointwise certificate test](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#statement) yields a finite depth and an explicit strictly positive lower bound valid for every numerator at that input.
Equivalently, the infimum of the losses over admissible numerators at fixed \(P\) is zero precisely when an admissible numerator attains zero loss.
These componentwise bounds can vary along a sequence of inputs.

<a id="compression"></a>
## Bounding multiplication by its positive logarithm

For a function \(f\) analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk put \(\delta=\int\log^+|f|\,d\mu\), interpreting \(\log^+0=0\).
We prove \(\|L_n(f)\|\le e^{n\delta}\).
Put \(v=\log^+|f|\), a continuous nonnegative function, and let
\[
 h(z)=\int \frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}v(\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta),\qquad |z|<1 .
\]
Its real part is the Poisson integral of \(v\), whose boundary values are attained uniformly as the radius tends to one by continuity.
Consequently \(b=f e^{-h}\) has modulus at most one in the disk by the maximum principle on concentric disks.
Multiplication by \(b\), followed by discarding all coefficients from degree \(n\) onwards, has norm at most one.
Indeed Parseval on circles of radius tending to one bounds the squared coefficient sum of \(bp\) by that of \(p\) for every input polynomial \(p\).
Truncation is multiplicative for the matrices \(L_n\), and \(L_n(e^h)=\exp(L_n(h))\) by the convergent exponential series, or by its finite Taylor formula after extracting the constant term.
The Hermitian part of \(L_n(h)\) has diagonal \(\delta\) and entry \(\int\bar\zeta^{\,i-j}v(\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta)\) in position \((i,j)\).
It is positive semidefinite by integration of squared moduli, so its largest eigenvalue is at most its trace \(n\delta\).
For a vector \(x\), differentiation of \(\|\exp(tL_n(h))x\|^2\) from \(t=0\) to \(1\) bounds that squared norm at time one by \(e^{2n\delta}\|x\|^2\).
Multiplying by \(L_n(b)\) proves the estimate.

After removing the initial monomial from \(A\), apply the estimate to \(f=A/G\).
The identity \(D(|A|^2/u)=2\operatorname{arcosh}(|f|)\) when \(|f|>1\) and its vanishing otherwise give \(2\delta\le\ell_P(A)\).
Thus \(\|L_n(A/G)\|\le e^{n\ell_P(A)/2}\), which proves the lower bound.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Integer prefixes and finite exclusion

When \(\|L_n(A/G)\|\le C\), applying this matrix to the vector of the first \(n\) coefficients of \(G\) gives
\[
 \sum_{j<n}|a_j|^2\le C^2\sum_{j<n}|G_j|^2\le 4 C^2\int u\,d\mu.
\]
Taking \(C=\|L_n(1/G)\|\) bounds the finite enumeration for the minimum, with matrix norms and comparisons computable by real algebraic arithmetic.
A certificate gives prefix norms at most one by the multiplication estimate.
Conversely, if \(s_n(P)\le1\) at every depth, the feasible integral prefixes form a finitely branching tree with nonempty levels, since shortening a prefix takes a principal compression.
An infinite branch has bounded integral squared coefficient sum by the displayed bound with \(C=1\), hence is a nonzero polynomial \(A\).
All its matrices \(L_n(A/G)\) are contractions.
For any polynomial \(p\), padding its coefficients with zeros and taking limits bounds \(\|(A/G)p\|_{L^2(\mu)}\le\|p\|_{L^2(\mu)}\) by Parseval.
Taking the normalized geometric sums whose squared moduli are Fejér kernels concentrating at each chosen circle point gives \(|A/G|\le1\) there by continuity.
The [automatic coprimality and strict crossing](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#coprimality) make this certificate admissible for the neutral transfer.
If it fails to exist, enumerating \(s_n(P)\) until the first value above one gives the claimed obstruction even for unrestricted numerator degree.
