# Which edge factors can occur at a limiting slope of algebraic-unit sections?

<a id="statement"></a>
## Visiting noncyclotomic boundary points away from the unit circle

Let \(C,U,V,K,D,Q,e,\lambda\) have the meanings in the [branch contribution formula](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement), now allowing any finite nonzero \(\lambda=U(Q)\) of positive height.
Let \(P_i\) eventually avoid every finite subset of the curve, with
\[
 U(P_i)=\xi_i z_i^{k_i},\qquad V(P_i)=\eta_i z_i^{l_i},\qquad
      l_i\ne0,\quad k_i/l_i\to0,\quad h(V(P_i))=O(1).
\]
Here \(z_i\) is nontorsion, the two phases are roots of unity, and \(V(P_i)\) is an algebraic unit.
In particular \(z_i\) is a unit.
At each embedding \(\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) with \(|\tau\lambda|\ne1\), take a neighborhood \(B_\tau\) as in that formula, and at every embedding with \(|\tau\lambda|=1\) put \(B_\tau=\varnothing\).
Then the same allocation holds:
\[
 \lim_i\frac1{[E_i:\mathbb Q]}\sum_{\sigma:E_i\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
 \mathbf1_{B_{\sigma|K}}(P_i^\sigma)\,
           \big|\log|\sigma V(P_i)|\big|=\frac{e\,h(\lambda)}{D}.          \tag{1}
\]
As before the fields \(E_i\) may be any number fields containing \(K\) and the coordinates of \(P_i\).
In particular, if \(Q\) is a pole and \(k_i/l_i>0\), at least one conjugate of \(\lambda\) must have modulus strictly greater than one.
For \(k_i/l_i<0\) at least one must have modulus strictly less than one.
At a zero the two requirements are reversed.
If \(k_i=0\) throughout, no such boundary point of positive height is possible.

For a fixed primitive rationally irreducible integral Laurent polynomial of rank-two support in coordinates \(U,V\), write \(F=\sum_{j=0}^J B_j(U)V^j\) up to a monomial, with \(B_0 B_J\ne0\).
Along any sequence of distinct dependent unit pairs on this curve whose short-to-long exponent ratio tends to zero through positive values, **every** noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(B_J\) with a nonzero root must have an exterior root, and every such factor of \(B_0\) must have an interior root.
Here cyclotomic refers to a polynomial proportional to a cyclotomic polynomial.
For negative ratios exchange interior and exterior.
The two extreme polynomials must also be primitive.

In a fixed direction there is an exact criterion.
There are infinitely many distinct torus points on \(F=0\) with \(U\) a root of unity and \(V\) an algebraic unit if and only if \(B_0,B_J\) are each, up to sign and a monomial, products of cyclotomic polynomials, allowing an empty product.
In that case, for every pair of sufficiently large distinct primes \(p,q\) and every primitive \(pq\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), **every** root of \(F(\zeta,V)\) is an algebraic unit.
A unimodular change of coordinates gives this test for any fixed primitive direction of multiplicative dependence modulo torsion.

<a id="truncation"></a>
## The part of the defect supported away from circle values

We have \(h(U(P_i))=|k_i/l_i|h(V(P_i))\to0\).
Use \(\bar L,\bar Q,g_v,\mu_v,n_v\) and the weak convergence at each place from the [height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#defect).
All its finite-place conclusions still apply since \(V(P_i)\) is a unit.
For each large fixed \(T\) the same subtraction of bounded tests now gives
\[
 \lim_i\sum_{v\mid\infty}n_v\operatorname{avg}_{P_i,v}(g_v-T)^+
   =\frac{h(\lambda)}{D}+
         \sum_{v\mid\infty} n_v\int(g_v-T)^+\,d\mu_v.                   \tag{2}
\]
The integrals tend to zero as \(T\to\infty\) by the integrability of \(g_v\) in the [intersection formula](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#point-defect).
For the embeddings with \(|\tau\lambda|\ne1\), the integrals are zero for sufficiently large \(T\), and replacing \(e(g_v-T)^+\) by the localized summand in (1) costs \(o_i(1)\) for each such fixed \(T\), by the [local order calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#branch).
It remains to show that at each archimedean place with \(|\lambda|=1\) the upper limit of the orbit mean of \((g_v-T)^+\) tends to zero as \(T\to\infty\).

<a id="moving"></a>
## Logarithmic tails when the short exponent is nonzero

First restrict to \(k_i\ne0\), and set \(s=|l_i/k_i|\to\infty\).
Near \(Q\) take a parameter \(w\) with \(U-\lambda=w^r f\), \(V=w^{\pm e}b\), where \(r\ge1\) and \(f,b\) are holomorphic units.
Then \(g_v=-\log|w|+O(1)\), and on a sufficiently small neighborhood with \(g_v\) large,
\[
 e g_v-O(1)\ \le\ \big|\log|V|\big|
       =s\big|\log|U|\big|\ \ll\ s\,|U-\lambda|
       \ \ll\ s e^{-r g_v}
\]
at the sampled points.
The equality uses the phases and the middle bound uses \(|\lambda|=1\).
Thus the sampled \(g_v\) is at most \(C\log(2s)\) there, and is bounded outside this neighborhood.

The absolute orbit of \(U(P_i)\) has height \(O(1/s)\) and degree \(\gg\sqrt{s}\) by the [degree-height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts), since it is nontorsion on this subsequence.
For large \(T\), a visit with \(g_v>T\) places \(U\) in a disk of radius \(Ce^{-rT}\) about the fixed circle value \(\lambda\).
The [disk discrepancy calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v778.md#tails) uses only the stated degree and height bounds and the [compact Lipschitz test estimate](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002).
With disk cutoff radius \(\max(Ce^{-rT},s^{-1/8})\) and smoothing radius \(s^{-1/4}\) it bounds the absolute orbit proportion by \(O(e^{-rT}+s^{-1/9})\).
The relative orbit over a chosen embedding of \(K\) costs at most a factor \([K:\mathbb Q]\), since its contribution is one term in the full absolute embedding mean of this disk indicator.
Larger fields repeat conjugates with their extension multiplicities.
Integration of this bound from \(T\) to \(C\log(2s)\) gives the required vanishing upper limit as \(T\to\infty\).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Logarithmic tails on an exact torsion fiber

On the subsequence \(k_i=0\) the orders of \(U(P_i)=\xi_i\) tend to infinity since a finite morphism has finite fibers and the \(P_i\) escape finite sets.
At each circle conjugate of \(\lambda\), the [archimedean logarithmic limit for torsion orbits](../audits/literature/BIR08/Yin_U001.md#torsion-logarithm) applies because \(\lambda\) has positive height and is therefore not a root of unity.
Over the fixed base \(K\), both the local orbit means of \(\log|\xi_i-\lambda|\) and those of each fixed continuous truncation converge to the respective Haar integrals on the circle.
It follows that the limiting upper means of \((-\log|\xi_i-\lambda|-A)^+\) tend to zero as \(A\to\infty\), by integrability of the circle logarithm.
Near \(Q\) the expansion of \(U-\lambda\) gives \(g_v\le-r^{-1}\log|U-\lambda|+O(1)\).
Away from the neighborhood \(g_v\) is bounded.
The subtraction therefore controls \((g_v-T)^+\) for the lifted orbit as well, since its projection repeats each relative conjugate of \(\xi_i\) equally often.
Combining the two subsequences in (2) proves (1).
At a pole restrict the off-circle neighborhoods to \(|V|>1\).
There \(\log|U|=(k_i/l_i)\log|V|\), so the positive right side of (1) forces an off-circle conjugate on the indicated side.
For a zero apply the argument with \(V^{-1}\).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Extreme face factors and the converse on a fixed direction

For a sequence of distinct dependent unit pairs the [projected height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#statement) bounds both coordinate heights.
The [finite-place condition](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) makes each extreme face primitive on a limiting coordinate axis.
After ignoring the finite torsion set one may choose a nontorsion generator modulo torsion and lift the distinct pairs to the normalization of a fixed geometric component after passing to a subsequence.

Every nonzero root \(\lambda\) of \(B_J\) gives a point \((\lambda,\infty)\) on the closure in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), with a pole of \(V\) on a branch above it.
Indeed the bihomogenized primitive equation, after removal of monomials, cuts out that closure without a vertical or horizontal divisor at infinity.
The corresponding assertion for \(B_0\) uses \((\lambda,0)\).
The geometric components are conjugate by rational irreducibility.
By conjugating the sequence to the component carrying the chosen branch and enlarging the fixed ground field to define that branch, (1) applies to each selected \(\lambda\).
Shifting by a monomial or replacing the polynomial by an associate preserves all the nonzero roots.
This gives the factor conditions, and for \(U\) torsion it forces every finite nonzero extreme root to have height zero.
By Kronecker's theorem, primitivity then gives the two factorizations asserted in the criterion.

Conversely let \(p,q\) be distinct primes greater than all the cyclotomic indices occurring in the two faces.
For any such index \(j\), \(\zeta^j\) still has order \(pq\), and \(1-\zeta^j\) is a unit since \(\Phi_{pq}(1)=1\).
As an integral divisor of \(\zeta^j-1\), the nonzero number \(\Phi_j(\zeta)\) is a unit too.
Thus \(B_0(\zeta),B_J(\zeta)\) are both units.
Division by the leading coefficient gives a monic equation with algebraic-integer coefficients, and the reciprocal equation gives integrality of each inverse root.
There are roots since \(J>0\), and varying the orders yields infinitely many distinct pairs.
A primitive direction can be completed to a unimodular character basis in which its constant character modulo torsion is \(U\); the coordinate changes preserve algebraic units.
