# Estimating the smallest rational factor degree from one auxiliary prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single reduction detects the smallest factor up to relative error tending to zero

Take any sequence of [primitive two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) with positive coprime directions \((k,l)\), \(k\to\infty\) and \(l/k\to\infty\).
Write \(P(X)=X^nQ(X+X^{-1})\) with \(n=l+2k\), and let \(n_1,\ldots,n_r\) be the degrees of the distinct irreducible rational factors of the eventually squarefree trace polynomial \(Q\).
Put \(m=\min_i n_i\) and use the [height cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v837.md#height)
\[
 B=\max\left(1,\left\lfloor\frac{l-4k}{8\log27}
 \left(\frac{\log\log W}{\log W}\right)^3\right\rfloor\right),\qquad W=\max(16,2n).
\]
At each prime \(p\) of squarefree reduction of \(Q\), form the subset sums of its factor degrees modulo \(p\).
Let \(b(p)\) be the smallest such sum in \([B,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]\), with value \(n\) if this interval contains no such sum.
At every sufficiently late direction and in every invertible residue class modulo any integer \(a\ge1\), the primes satisfying
\[
             (1-B/(4n))m\ \le\ b(p)\ \le\ m                              \tag{1}
\]
have relative natural density at least \((B/(8n))^r\).
Here the prime bound tends to infinity with the section, modulus and class fixed.
The lower bound is independent of the class and modulus, which may vary between sections.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting long cycles in a prescribed residue class

The cutoff gives \(m\ge B\), and \(B/n\sim(8\log27)^{-1}(\log\log n/\log n)^3\), so \(B/n\to0\) and \(B^2/n\to\infty\).
Write \(t_i=n_i B/(4n)\), which is at least \(B^2/(4n)\) and eventually exceeds four on every block.
On block \(i\) require one cycle of length \(n_i-j\) for some integer \(2\le j\le\lfloor t_i\rfloor\).
All these lengths exceed \(n_i/2\), so the events for different lengths are disjoint and the long cycle is unique.
For fixed \(j\), the fraction in either sign class of \(S_{n_i}\) realizing that cycle is \(1/(n_i-j)\).
Indeed there are \(\binom{n_i}{j}(n_i-j-1)!\) ways to choose and order the long cycle, and exactly \(j!/2\) permutations of the remaining letters give the required sign since \(j\ge2\).
The resulting fraction in either sign class, summed over \(j\), is at least \((\lfloor t_i\rfloor-1)/n_i\ge t_i/(2n_i)=B/(8n)\).

In the compositum of the trace splitting field and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_a)\), condition on the cyclotomic automorphism of the chosen class.
Its fiber projects uniformly onto a coset of the trace group over the cyclotomic field, which contains \(\prod_i A_{n_i}\) by the [alternating actions over the maximal abelian extension](Yin_C_G0345_v806.md#statement).
Each coset of this product inside the projected fiber consists of independent uniform permutations of prescribed signs on the blocks when sampled uniformly.
Thus the conditional fraction meeting all the cycle requirements is at least \((B/(8n))^r\), even when the signs on different blocks are correlated.
The cycle requirements are invariant under conjugation, so [Chebotarev's theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) gives the same lower bound among primes in the class, after ignoring the finitely many bad primes.
At these primes the cycle lengths are the degrees of the factors of the reduction.

Outside the long cycles there are at most \(\sum_i t_i=B/4<B\) letters in total.
Any subset sum at least \(B\) must therefore include a long cycle and has size at least \(\min_i(n_i-t_i)=(1-B/(4n))m\).
The default value \(n\) also satisfies this lower bound.
The [upper bound](Yin_C_G0345_v837.md#height) \(b(p)\le m\) holds at every good prime, including when \(Q\) is irreducible and \(m=n\).
This proves (1), whose full event also has a density since it depends only on the cycle type.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Detecting a positive lower bound for all factor measures

Let \(\mu\) denote the least log Mahler measure of an irreducible rational factor of \(P\).
Along the given sequence the [uniform height law](Yin_C_G0345_v837.md#statement) gives \(\mu=(C+o(1))m/n\) with fixed \(C>0\).
Consequently \(\liminf\mu>0\) if and only if there exist constants \(d,H>0\) such that at every sufficiently late direction the primes with \(b(p)\ge dn\) have relative density at least \((\log n)^{-H}\) in every prescribed invertible residue class.
Indeed, if eventually \(m/n\ge\delta>0\), then \(r\le R=\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor\) since \(\sum_i n_i=n\).
We may take \(d=\delta/2\) and \(H=3R\), since eventually \(B/(8n)\ge(\log n)^{-3}\).
Conversely, just one witnessing prime per late section forces \(m/n\ge d\).

For convergence to zero, let \(b_{\max}\) be the maximum of \(b(p)\) over good primes in any selected invertible residue class at that section.
Positive density in (1) and the universal upper bound give \((1-B/(4n))m\le b_{\max}\le m\).
Thus \(\mu\to0\) is equivalent to \(b_{\max}=o(n)\), or equivalently to the eventual absence, for each fixed \(d>0\), of primes in the selected class with \(b(p)\ge dn\).
All these conclusions concern limiting prime densities or existence at each fixed section and give no bound on the least witnessing prime.
