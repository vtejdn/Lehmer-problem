# The exceptional atoms in every finite cyclotomic avoidance problem

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform weights on every forbidden primitive orbit

Let \(B\) be any finite set of positive integers and let \(\mu\) be an even probability measure on the unit circle such that \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for every \(n\notin B\).
Every atom is at a forbidden root of unity.
For each \(b\in B\), the masses at all primitive \(b\)-th roots are equal.
No regularity of the nonatomic part is required for this equality.
For a probability without the evenness assumption the same statement holds for its conjugation symmetrization.

Consequently, for arbitrary \(B\), an even feasible probability satisfying the [Fourier summability condition](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#statement) on its nonatomic part is exactly a convex combination of normalized arc length and the uniform measures on the forbidden primitive orbits.
For a nonzero real polynomial avoiding these orbits, any strict excess over the maximum of its log Mahler measure and its forbidden-orbit log averages requires a nonatomic part failing that summability condition.
The possibility of such a part is still open.

<a id="atoms"></a>
## The atomic part satisfies the allowed inequalities by itself

By [atom exclusion](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#regularity), all atoms lie on the forbidden orbits.
If \(B=\varnothing\) the atomic assertion is immediate.
Otherwise take \(L\) to be the least common multiple of the members of \(B\).
Write \(A\) for the atomic part, supported on the \(L\)-th roots, and \(\eta\) for normalized arc length.
Average the images of \(\mu\) by \(z\mapsto z^j\) for \(1\le j\le T,\ j\equiv1\bmod L\).
Every image is feasible since \(\Phi_n(X^j)\) for allowed \(n\) has only allowed cyclotomic factors: powering fixes every forbidden root.
By the [power-average argument](Yin_C_G0901_v18.md#measures) the weak limit is \(A+(1-A(\mathbb T))\eta\).
Irrational points give arc length and the progression fixes every atom.
Each allowed logarithmic integral is upper semicontinuous, and its Haar integral is zero.
Hence \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,dA\ge0\) for every allowed \(n\).

Let \(G=(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^\times\) act on the \(L\)-th roots by powers, and let \(\bar A\) be the average of the translates of \(A\) under \(G\).
For each allowed \(n\), put \(f_n=\log|\Phi_n|\).
We have
\[
                       \int f_n\,dA=\int f_n\,d\bar A .
\]
Indeed for any \(u\in G\) choose a positive integer \(j\equiv u\bmod L\) coprime to \(nL\).
The quotient \(\Phi_n(X^j)/\Phi_n(X)\) is a product of allowed cyclotomics, so \(\int f_n(z^j)\,dA\ge\int f_n(z)\,dA\).
The same inequality holds for \(j^{t+1}\) compared to \(j^t\), since the quotient after substituting \(X^{j^t}\) still avoids all forbidden roots.
A positive power of \(j\) is one modulo \(L\), closing the inequalities to equalities.
Average over \(u\).
The signed even measure \(v=A-\bar A\) therefore annihilates each allowed \(f_n\), and has total mass zero on each primitive orbit.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Detecting the individual masses by a finite Fourier transform

For each integer \(s\ge1\) define \(g_s(z)=\log|1-z^s|\) at \(L\)-th roots with \(z^s\ne1\) and define \(g_s(z)=0\) at the others.
For \(j>0\) coprime to \(L\), the polynomial \((1-X^{sj})/(1-X^s)\) is a product of allowed cyclotomics.
At forbidden points with \(z^s=1\) its logarithmic modulus is \(\log j\), constant on each such orbit.
At the others its logarithmic modulus is \(g_s(z^j)-g_s(z)\).
Using the two properties of \(v\) gives \(\int g_s(z^j)\,dv=\int g_s(z)\,dv\).
Averaging over \(G\) gives
\[
                            \int g_s\,dv=0\qquad(s\ge1).                 \tag{1}
\]

If \(v\ne0\), choose an order \(b\) maximal under divisibility among the primitive orbits carrying a nonzero part of \(v\).
Write \(v_a\) for its masses at \(e^{2\pi i a/b}\) for \(a\in G_b=(\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z)^\times\).
The sequence in \(s\) on the left of (1) is \(L\)-periodic.
For each \(j\in G_b\) take its normalized additive Fourier coefficient at the additive character \(s\mapsto e^{2\pi i j s/b}\).
A component from another primitive orbit of order \(c\) is periodic modulo \(c\), hence contributes zero unless \(b\mid c\).
Maximality leaves only the orbit of order \(b\).
Define \(g(k)=\log|1-e^{2\pi i k/b}|\) if \(k\ne0\bmod b\), and \(g(0)=0\).
With \(\widehat g(j)=b^{-1}\sum_{k\bmod b}g(k)e^{-2\pi i j k/b}\) the resulting equations are
\[
                     \sum_{a\in G_b}v_a\,\widehat g(ja^{-1})=0
                                \qquad(j\in G_b).                        \tag{2}
\]
The orbit sum already handles \(b=1,2\).
In general the principal multiplicative character coefficient of \(v_a\) vanishes by that sum, and its odd coefficients vanish by evenness.

Fix a nonprincipal even character \(\chi\) of \(G_b\) and extend it by zero off the unit residues.
Replace \(g(k)\) in the additive transform by \(g_r(k)=\log|1-r e^{2\pi i k/b}|\), \(0<r<1\).
The divergent value at \(k=0\) adds the same scalar to every additive coefficient, so it contributes zero when summed against \(\chi(j)\).
Every other entry converges to its value in \(g\).
Expanding \(g_r\) in its absolutely convergent series and summing on \(G_b\) gives
\[
   \sum_{j\in G_b}\chi(j)\widehat g(j)
       =-\lim_{r\uparrow1}\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{\chi(\ell)r^\ell}{\ell}
       =-L(1,\chi)\ne0 .                                                \tag{3}
\]
The two residue classes \(j,-j\) give the same contribution by evenness.
The Abel limit equals the convergent Dirichlet series by bounded partial sums of the nonprincipal character.
The [nonvanishing theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21b/Yin_U001.md#sut21b-u001) applies to every nonprincipal character, including imprimitive ones.
Multiplying (2) by \(\chi(j)\) and summing gives \(\sum_a v_a\chi(a)=0\) by (3).
All character coefficients of \((v_a)\) are therefore zero, contradicting the choice of \(b\).
Thus \(A=\bar A\), and the transitive action of \(G\) on each primitive orbit gives the claimed uniformity.

<a id="rate"></a>
## Consequence for the coefficient rate

With a residual part satisfying the [divisor-weighted Fourier condition](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#differences), its evenness forces it to equal its mass times \(\eta\).
The preceding argument classifies the atoms for every finite set \(B\).
Conversely every convex combination in the statement is feasible, by the integral resultant on each forbidden orbit and the zero Haar integrals.
In the [dual coefficient formula](Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#dual), a maximum is attained by an even probability by upper semicontinuity and compactness.
The supremum on the classified measures is exactly the maximum of the indicated Haar and finite-orbit averages.
This proves the stated necessary condition on any maximizer giving a strict excess.
