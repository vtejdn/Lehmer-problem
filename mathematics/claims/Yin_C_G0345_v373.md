# The prime three forces a resultant cost for double Salem zeros

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound for every pair of rows

Let \(6\mid d\), let \(p>3\) be a prime dividing \(d\), and set
\[
 S_d(x)=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1,\qquad q=3^{v_3(d)},\qquad d=qps .
\]
Thus \(3\nmid s\), and \(p\) may divide \(s\).
For two nonzero integral Laurent rows in distinct classes modulo \(p\), write their sum as \(F(x)=x^{u_0} U_0(x^p)+x^{u_1} V_0(x^p)\), where \(U_0,V_0\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have nonzero constant terms.
Suppose \(U_0,V_0\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(S_d^2\mid F\) over \(\mathbb Q[x^{\pm1}]\).
Write \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|\) and \(t_+=\max(t,0)\).
Then, without a bound on either row degree,
\[
                    v_3(H)\ \ge\ \frac{d}{4p}(p-2-\log_3 d)_+^2 .          \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(p\to\infty\) and \(\log d=o(p)\), the lower bound is \((1/4-o(1))pd\), uniformly over the two classes and all their witnesses.
For every such pair of classes there is also a primitive witness with exactly two copies of \(S_d\) and \(v_3(H)\le dp+d\log_3 p\).
In the [Salem family with prescribed primes](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem), the entire power index is \(3^{(d-2)(p-1)/2}\).
Thus (1) recovers at least a fraction \(1/2-o(1)\) of its valuation in this range, including along the fourth-logarithmic prime windows there.

<a id="packets"></a>
## Original residues and their powered nodes

Normalize \(v(3)=1\) in a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_3\) containing the roots of \(S_d\), the \(ps\)-th roots of unity and an element \(\tau\) with \(\tau^q=3\).
Bars denote reduction in its residue field.
The sparse identity
\[
       (x-1)S_d(x)=(x-2)x^d+2x-1\equiv (x+1)(x^{ps}-1)^q\pmod 3
\]
shows that exactly \(q\) roots \(\alpha\) of \(S_d\) reduce to each \(\bar\omega\in\mu_{ps}\) other than \(1,-1\).
The multiplicities at \(1,-1\) are \(q-1,q+1\), respectively.
Indeed all characteristic-zero roots are integral units and the reductions of the \(ps\)-th roots of unity are distinct.
The Salem roots are simple by [irreducibility of this family](Yin_C_G0022_v1.md#salem-first-window-precise-statement).
Their powers \(\beta=\alpha^p\) are also distinct, since \(\alpha^d=\beta^{qs}\) determines \(\alpha\) by \((\alpha^d+2)\alpha=2\alpha^d+1\), and \(\alpha^d=-2\) cannot satisfy this equation.

For a Teichmüller lift \(b\in\mu_s\), the \(p\) residues with \(\bar\omega^p=\bar b\) include at least \(p-1\) other than \(1,-1\), since these last two have different \(p\)-th powers.
Use only these groups of \(q\) roots and the disk \(X=b+\tau Y\) with \(v(Y)\ge0\).
For a root in any chosen group,
\[
               z=\frac{\beta^{qs}-1}{3}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha-2}
\]
is a unit.
Write \(\delta=v(\beta/b-1)>0\).
In \((1+T)^{qs}-1\) the coefficients at \(1\le j<q\) are divisible by three, and the coefficient at \(q\) is \(s\bmod3\) by Frobenius.
If \(\delta<1/q\), the term at \(q\) has uniquely smallest valuation \(q\delta<1\), while if \(\delta>1/q\), every term has valuation greater than one.
Consequently \(\delta=1/q\).
After substituting \(X=b+\tau Y\), the polynomial \((X^{qs}-1)/3\) is integral and reduces to \(s\bar b^{-q}Y^q\).
Thus the \(q\) nodes of a group have one common scaled residue \(\bar y\), determined by
\[
                  s\bar b^{-q}\bar y^q=\frac{1-\bar\omega}{\bar\omega+1}.
\]
Distinct chosen groups have distinct \(\bar y\), because the fractional linear function on the right is injective away from its pole and the \(q\)-power map on the residue field is injective.
Each of the \(s\) disks therefore contains at least \(q(p-1)\) distinct powered nodes, with \(q\) nodes at each of the selected scaled residues.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## From the disk valuations to the resultant

Remove common \(3\)-content from the rows and put \(k=u_1-u_0\), so \(p\nmid k\).
At a particular disk let \(g\) be the minimum valuation of the coefficients of \(U_0(b+\tau Y),V_0(b+\tau Y)\).
Divide both by a scalar of valuation \(g\), reduce to \(A,B\), and let \(n=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\) with \(\deg 0=-\infty\).
At least one row survives, and \(n/q\le g\) because the coefficient at degree \(j\) before normalization has valuation at least \(j/q\).

If the reduced ratio is constant in the projective line, the [common inverse branch for each group](Yin_C_G0345_v372.md#groups) makes \((Y-\bar y)^{2q}\) divide \(A+\bar\omega^k B\).
The \(\bar\omega^k\) are distinct above \(\bar b\) since \(p\nmid k\).
At most one group makes the combination identically zero, so \(n\ge2q(p-2)\) and \(g\ge2(p-2)\).
If the reduced ratio is nonconstant, the [mixed-characteristic derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v372.md#derivative) counts each characteristic-zero node even when \(q\) of them coincide after reduction.
Using multiplicity two, depth \(1/q\), and at least \(q(p-1)\) nodes gives
\[
                    2g\ge p-1+1/q-\log_3 n .
\]
When \(g<p/2\), the inequality \(n\le qg\) gives \(n<qp/2\le d\).
When \(g\ge p/2\), no bound on \(n\) is needed.
In either case \(g\ge(p-2-\log_3 d)_+/2\).

The \(s\) centers have different original residues.
The [separated-disk resultant inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#resultant) therefore adds \(qg^2\) over these disks, using the common minimum as a lower bound for each individual row valuation.
It allows one row to have nonunit scalar content.
Restoring the common content multiplies the resultant by that content to the sum of the two row degrees.
Since \(sq=d/p\), the sum gives (1).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Extra Mahler measure under the finite degree cap

Suppose now \(F\) is a primitive ordinary polynomial of degree at most \(p(16d+1)\).
Let \(R\) be the product with multiplicities of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/S_d^2\) other than \(x\), and let \(m=\log M\).
Set \(N=\deg U_0+\deg V_0\).
The two original residue classes are distinct, so at most one row reaches degree \(16d+1\) before removing its power of \(X\), giving \(N\le32d+1\).
The exponent span \(D\) is at most \(p(16d+1)\).
With \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(z)\) for normalized Haar measure, the [arithmetic row inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v366.md#arithmetic) and (1) give
\[
 m(R)\ \ge\ (1-4/p)\frac{d\log3}{4p(32d+1)}(p-2-\log_3d)_+^2
                 -2m(S_d)-\frac2p\log\!\big(Cp(16d+1)\big).
\]
The Salem measure \(m(S_d)\) tends to \(\log2\).
Along any sequence as in (1) with \(p\to\infty\) and \(\log d=o(p)\), this yields
\[
                           \liminf\frac{m(R)}p\ \ge\ \frac{\log3}{128}.
\]
The arithmetic numerator here uses only the valuation of the actual resultant at three, where the [single-pair estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#collision) see at most \(4(d/p)\log3\) for multiplicity two.

<a id="witness"></a>
## An upper bound for each prescribed offset

Put \(h=d/p,\ X=x^p,\ V=X^h+2,\ W=2X^h+1,\ A=xV,\ B=W\).
Then \(A-B=(x-1)S_d\).
For \(1\le r<p\) use the integral polynomial
\[
\begin{split}
 F_r&=rA^p+(p-r)B^p-pA^r B^{p-r}=U_r(X)+x^r V_r(X),\\
 U_r&=(p-r)W^p+rXV^p,\qquad V_r=-pW^{p-r}V^r .
\end{split}
\]
Multiplication by a monomial places these rows in any prescribed pair of classes with difference \(r\bmod p\), preserving the stripped rows.
Expanding in \(A-B\), the constant and linear terms vanish and the quadratic coefficient is \(rp(p-r)B^{p-2}/2\).
The polynomials \(V,W\) have no common zero, so \(B\ne0\) at every zero of \(S_d\), and \(S_d(1)=3-d\ne0\).
Thus \(F_r\) contains \(S_d\) with multiplicity exactly two.
The row \(U_r\) has constant term \(p-r\) and leading coefficient \(r\), since its two summands have degrees \(d\) and \(d+1\).
As \(\gcd(r,p-r)=1\), the witness is primitive.
The degree of \(F_r\) is \(p(d+1)\), and a translation by at most \(p-1\) preserves the upper bound \(p(16d+1)\).

At a zero of \(V\), one has \(W=-3\), so \(|\operatorname{Res}(V,U_r)|=(p-r)^h3^{hp}\).
At a zero of \(W\), one has \(V=3/2\), and the product of the moduli of these \(h\) zeros is \(1/2\).
The leading coefficient \(2\) of \(W\) contributes \(2^{d+1}\), giving \(|\operatorname{Res}(W,U_r)|=r^h3^{hp}\).
In particular the rows are coprime, and both have nonzero constant terms.
Multiplicativity gives
\[
        |\operatorname{Res}(U_r,V_r)|
           =p^{d+1}r^{h(p-r)}(p-r)^{hr}3^{dp}.
\]
Consequently \(v_3(H)\le dp+d\log_3p\) for this witness, and the order \(pd\) of (1) is attained within an absolute factor in the stated asymptotic range.
