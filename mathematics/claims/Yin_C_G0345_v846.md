# Horizontal discriminant weights throughout a rectangle of primitive directions

<a id="statement"></a>
## A simultaneous bound at all prime divisors of the horizontal exponent

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) and let \(1\le k\le K,\ 1\le l\le L\) with \(\gcd(k,l)=1\).
Put \(S=\{2,3,5,23,1039\}\), and let \(\omega(k)\) count the distinct prime divisors of \(k\).
After omitting at most \(16\pi(K)\) primitive directions from the entire rectangle, every remaining squarefree section satisfies
\[
 \sum_{p\mid k,\ p\notin S}v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\log p
                \le 16\omega(k)\log(2KL).                               \tag{1}
\]
Outside a further fixed finite set of directions, the sum over all \(p\mid k\) is
\[
             O_F\big(\omega(k)\log(2KL)+\log^3(3+K+L)\big).             \tag{2}
\]
The same upper bound holds for the horizontal part of the [integer certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#statement) \(U\) whenever it is defined, and for the horizontal part of the absolute complementary resultant in any monic integral factorization.
For every fixed \(A>0\), every primitive graph \(l=l(k)\le (2M)^A\) on \(M\le k<2M\) therefore has horizontal logarithmic weight \(O_{F,A}((\log M)^3)\) at its squarefree sections outside \(O_F(M/\log M)\) indices.
This includes \(l=k^2+1\), where all sufficiently large sections are squarefree by the [steep-root description](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).

<a id="critical"></a>
## The critical value on a fixed horizontal residue disk

Fix \(p\mid k\) outside \(S\), normalize \(v_p(p)=1\), and set \(r=k/l\in p\mathbb Z_p\).
By the [horizontal collision calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v845.md#coordinate), the possible pairs \((u,v)=(x^k,x^l)\) at multiple residue roots lie among eight geometric pairs independent of the direction.
For each pair use the unramified integer ring \(\mathcal O\) with residue field \(\mathbb F_p(u)\) and denote Teichmüller lifts by brackets.
Write \(\alpha\) for the root of \(D=U^4-4AA^*\) lifting \(u\), and \(\beta=-\alpha^2/(2A(\alpha))\) for the lift of \(v\).
At \((\alpha,\beta)\), \(F_V=0\), \(F_{VV}=2A(\alpha)\) is a unit, and \(F_U=-D'(\alpha)/(4A(\alpha))\) is a unit by the simple reduction of \(D\).
The last identity follows by differentiating \(F(U,-U^2/(2A(U)))=-D(U)/(4A(U))\).
The integral implicit function theorem, or successive coefficient comparison using this unit derivative, writes the curve as \(U=f(V)\) with \(f\in\mathcal O[[V-\beta]]\), \(f(\beta)=\alpha\), \(f'(\beta)=0\) and \(f''(\beta)=-F_{VV}/F_U\) a unit.
This parametrizes the whole open residue disk because the remaining factor in \(F(U,V)=(U-f(V))G(U,V)\) has unit constant term in the expansion about \((\alpha,\beta)\).

The equation \(V f'(V)/f(V)=r\) has a solution \(V=V(r)\in\mathcal O[[r]]\) with \(V(0)=\beta\), by the same integral recursion since the left derivative at \(\beta\) is \(\beta f''(\beta)/\alpha\), a unit.
Thus \(V'(0)\) is a unit, and \(U(r)=f(V(r))\) solves \(F=rUF_U+VF_V=0\) together with \(V(r)\).
For \(r\in p\mathbb Z_p\) define
\[
 H(r)=\log(U(r)/[u])-r\log(V(r)/[v]).
\]
Whenever \(u^l=v^k\), the unique residue coordinate \(x\) is given by the [Bézout formula](Yin_C_G0345_v845.md#coordinate).
On its open disk use \(X=[x]\,(V/[v])^{1/l}\), giving \(X^k=[u]\,(V/[v])^r\) by the integral binomial expansions.
This coordinate preserves distances in valuation because it and its inverse have integral coefficients and unit linear terms.
The section roots in that disk are exactly the zeros of
\[
 J(V)=\frac{f(V)}{[u]\,(V/[v])^r}-1.
\]
The logarithmic derivative of \(1+J\) is \(f'/f-r/V\), so \(J'(V(r))=0\), and \(J''(V(r))\) reduces to \(f''(\beta)/u\ne0\) since \(p\mid r\) in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
At this unramified critical point \(J(V(r))\in p\mathcal O\) and \(\log(1+J(V(r)))=H(r)\), by the binomial and logarithm identities on principal units.
Their valuations agree, including an infinite valuation at zero.
The expansion at \(V(r)\) is integral, with a unit quadratic coefficient and vanishing linear coefficient.
The [integral square-root coordinate](Yin_C_G0345_v845.md#distances) therefore gives exactly two roots counted with multiplicity, whose distance has valuation \(v_p(H(r))/2\).
For a squarefree section this yields derivative weight \(v_p(H(r))\log p\) for the disk, with half that weight in a complementary resultant when the two factors separate its roots.

<a id="centers"></a>
## Two centers for arbitrary depth

The critical equation cancels the terms involving \(V'(r)\) in the first derivative of \(H\), giving
\[
       H'(r)=-\log(V(r)/[v]),\qquad H''(r)=-V'(r)/V(r)\in\mathcal O[[r]].
\]
In particular \(H'(0)\in p\mathcal O\) and \(H''(0)\) is a unit.
Writing \(H=\sum h_n r^n\), we have \(n(n-1)h_n\in\mathcal O\) for \(n\ge2\).
For \(n\ge3\), \(n-2-v_p(n(n-1))\ge1\) and the left side tends to infinity, since at most one of the consecutive integers is divisible by \(p\ge5\), and \(v_p(n(n-1))\le\log_p n\) (check \(n=3,4\) directly).
It follows that if \(v_p(H(0))=1\), then \(v_p(H(r))=1\) throughout \(p\mathbb Z_p\).
Otherwise \(H(pT)/p^2\) is an integral restricted series whose reduction has degree exactly two, its leading coefficient being the reduction of \(H''(0)/2\).

For completeness, such a restricted series is a unit times a monic quadratic over \(\mathcal O\).
Start modulo \(p\) with the leading scalar and a monic quadratic; at precision \(p^m\) divide the reduced error divided by \(p^m\) by the reduced monic quadratic.
The error reduction is a polynomial because the series is restricted.
Correct the unit factor by \(p^m\) times a lift of the quotient, and correct the quadratic by \(p^m\) times a lift of the remainder divided by the leading scalar.
The product gains one digit, the quadratic stays monic, and completeness gives the factors in the restricted series ring, with the first reducing to a nonzero constant.
Thus its values on integral arguments are units.
If \(t_1,t_2\) are the two integral roots of the quadratic in an algebraic closure, put \(\lambda_i=pt_i\).
For every \(r\in p\mathbb Z_p\),
\[
                v_p(H(r))=v_p(r-\lambda_1)+v_p(r-\lambda_2).            \tag{3}
\]
The two centers depend only on the prime and geometric horizontal pair and may coincide or belong to a ramified extension.

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Counting primitive directions near the centers

For each center omit directions with \(p\mid k\) and \(v_p(k/l-\lambda_i)>\log_p(KL)\).
If two distinct primitive directions met this test at the same center, their nonzero integer determinant \(kl'-k'l\) would have valuation greater than \(\log_p(KL)\), since both denominators are units.
Its absolute value is at most \(KL\), a contradiction.
Each of the at most sixteen centers at a prime excludes at most one direction, and every prime dividing a horizontal exponent lies below or at \(K\).
On the remaining directions a disk of the second kind costs at most \(2\log(KL)\) by (3), and one of the first kind costs \(\log p\le\log K\).
There are at most eight disks at each prime since each compatible pair determines a unique residue coordinate.
Simple residue roots contribute zero, so summation proves (1).
The [all-prime discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#statement) gives the fixed-prime term in (2) outside its fixed finite exceptional set of primitive directions.
The discriminant product identity bounds complementary resultants, and the [trace discriminant identity](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#weights) bounds the certificate by the full discriminant prime by prime.
Taking \(K=2M\) and \(L=(2M)^A\) in (2) gives the graph bound by \(\pi(2M)\ll M/\log M\) and \(\omega(k)\ll\log M\).
