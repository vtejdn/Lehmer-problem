# The relative discriminant when inversion disagrees with complex conjugation

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound for subfields of a reciprocal root field

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit, \(P\) its monic minimum, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(\iota\) the automorphism of \(K\) taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
Let \(F\subseteq K\) be totally real or CM, stable under \(\iota\), of degree \(e\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Write \(\kappa\) for its canonical conjugation, the identity in the totally real case, and \(\tau=\iota|_F\).
Suppose \(\rho=\kappa\tau\ne1\).
Canonical conjugation commutes with every automorphism of \(F\), so \(\rho\) is an involution.
Put \(F_0=F^\rho\) and let \(\mathcal D\) be the absolute norm of the relative discriminant ideal of \(F/F_0\).
Let \(q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(F\) and \(a=q(0)\).
Then the logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
 m(P)\ \ge\
 \begin{cases}
   e\,\operatorname{arsinh}(\mathcal D^{1/e}/4),& a\text{ is a root of unity},\\[2pt]
   (e/2)\,\operatorname{arsinh}(\mathcal D^{1/e}/2),& a\text{ is nontorsion}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The estimate refines the norm-one constant in the [relative non-self-inversive bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#gap) for these subfields and does not require an oriented block partition.
In particular if \(m(P)<\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\), inversion agrees with canonical conjugation on every totally real or CM subfield of \(K\), even without an initial stability hypothesis.
Thus every totally real subfield is contained in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), and that trace field contains no CM subfield.

<a id="field"></a>
## Norm polynomial and the norm of a difference

Since \(F(\alpha)=K\), the norm polynomial \(\prod_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}q^\sigma=P\) collects every rational conjugate of \(\alpha\) exactly once.
Write \(m_\sigma=m(q^\sigma)\), so \(m(P)=\sum_\sigma m_\sigma\).
The coefficients of \(q\) are integral and generate \(F\), since over their field the same irreducible polynomial still has degree \([K:F]\) and \(\alpha\) still generates \(K\).
The constant \(a\) is a unit, and \(\tau\) transforms \(q\) into its monic reciprocal \(X^{\deg q}q(1/X)/a\), by the action on \(\alpha\).
In particular \(\tau a=a^{-1}\).

For any integral \(b\in F\) with \(b\ne\rho b\), the order \(\mathcal O_{F_0}[b]\) has relative discriminant generated by \((b-\rho b)^2\), by the two-element basis \(1,b\).
The field discriminant divides this ideal, as is seen locally at each prime of \(F_0\) by squaring the determinant of the inclusion of the order in the full integral lattice.
Consequently \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(b-\rho b)|\ge\mathcal D\), since its relative norm is \(-(b-\rho b)^2\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## The constant term and the first differing coefficient

If \(a\) is nontorsion then \(a\ne\rho a\), since equality would give \(|\sigma a|=1\) for all \(\sigma\) and Kronecker's theorem would make \(a\) torsion.
Indeed \(\sigma(\rho a)=1/\overline{\sigma a}\), since every embedding intertwines \(\kappa\) with ordinary complex conjugation.
Therefore
\[
 |\log|\sigma a||=\operatorname{arsinh}\bigl(|\sigma(a-\rho a)|/2\bigr).
\]
The bound \(m_\sigma\ge\log^+|\sigma a|\) follows from the root product, and summation gives \(m(P)\ge\tfrac12\sum_\sigma|\log|\sigma a||\) by the unit norm.
The function \(x\mapsto\operatorname{arsinh}(e^x/c)\) is increasing and convex for each \(c>0\), as follows by differentiation.
Jensen's inequality and the discriminant estimate with \(b=a\) now prove the nontorsion bound.

If \(a\) is torsion, \(q\) and \(q^\rho\) have common constant \(a\), but differ since the coefficients of \(q\) generate \(F\).
For the first unequal coefficient \(b\) of \(q\), the first nonconstant coefficient of \(q/q^\rho\) is \(w=(b-\rho b)/a\), so \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}w|\ge\mathcal D\).
The [first-discrepancy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#gap) gives \(|\sigma w|\le4\sinh m_\sigma\).
Its hypotheses here follow from the monic reciprocal identity for \(q^\tau\) and the common unit-modulus constant.
Explicitly the embedded quotient is a quotient of two finite Blaschke products with the same value \(e^{-m_\sigma}\) at zero, obtained by reflecting the exterior roots and cancelling any common circle factors.
Every positive-index coefficient of either product is bounded in modulus by \(1-e^{-2m_\sigma}\), by averaging over roots of unity of that index and applying Schwarz–Pick at zero.
Their first differing coefficient is \(e^{-m_\sigma}\sigma w\), giving the asserted bound.
Convexity as before yields \(m(P)\ge e\operatorname{arsinh}(\mathcal D^{1/e}/4)\).

<a id="compatibility"></a>
## Removing the stability hypothesis

If a totally real or CM subfield \(E\subseteq K\) is given, take \(F=E\,\iota(E)\).
This finite compositum is stable under \(\iota\) and is totally real or CM.
For the latter assertion, the conjugations on its generating fields are restrictions of ordinary complex conjugation, and after any embedding they are again restrictions of ordinary complex conjugation.
Their common action on the compositum has totally real fixed field, and is nonidentity at every embedding if a CM generator field occurs.
The canonical conjugation on \(F\) restricts to that on \(E\).
If \(\iota\) differs from canonical conjugation on \(E\), it does so on \(F\).
Then \(e\ge2\), \(\mathcal D\ge1\), and the two lower bounds are at least \(2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)\) and \(\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\), respectively.
The former exceeds the latter by the strict concavity of \(\operatorname{arsinh}\) on the positive half-line.
Below the stated threshold \(\iota\) must consequently preserve each \(E\) and act on it by its canonical conjugation.
The trace field is the fixed field of \(\iota\), since \(\alpha\) satisfies a quadratic over it with distinct conjugates \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\), proving both subfield restrictions.
