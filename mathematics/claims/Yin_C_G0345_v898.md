# Finiteness for perturbations of the Chebyshev trace grid

<a id="statement"></a>
## Node displacements in the first nonunit pole problem

Fix integers \(q,n\ge2\), and put \(p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ h=1/M,\ K=4M+1,\ N=n-1\).
An ordered grid \(0=t_0>\cdots>t_N=-4\) is realized if there is a monic integral polynomial \(H\) of degree \(n\) with \(M^nH(h)=K\), \(H(0)<0\), and strictly alternating signs on the grid.
Compare it with a second ordered grid \(0=s_0>\cdots>s_N=-4\).
Write \(d_i=|t_i-s_i|\) and set
\[
             L=\max_{0\le i\le N}\ \sum_{j\ne i}\frac{d_j}{|s_i-s_j|}.
\]
For each fixed bound on \(L\), the following conclusions hold uniformly over the real nodes \(t_i\).

1. If deleting one \(s_i\) leaves a monic integral node polynomial, there is an upper bound on every realizable height \(q\), independent of \(n\) and both grids.
2. If \(s_i=2\cos(i\pi/N)-2\), only finitely many pairs \((q,n)\) are realizable, and the number of realizable degrees at each \(q\) is bounded independently of \(q\).

For the Chebyshev extrema let \(m\) count the displaced nodes and let \(\varepsilon=\max_i d_i/\min_{j\ne i}|s_i-s_j|\).
For every sequence of realized grids with distinct pairs \((q,n)\), both
\[
              \varepsilon\log(2+m)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
              N^2\max_i d_i\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
In particular one may round every Chebyshev node to the nearest multiple of \(1/D_N\), with any integer denominators \(D_N\ge cN^2\) for a fixed \(c>0\).
Among the rounded grids whose nodes remain distinct, only finitely many height-degree pairs are realizable.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Transferring the residues between two real grids

Put \(Q_t(x)=\prod_i(x-t_i)\), \(Q_s(x)=\prod_i(x-s_i)\), and \(r_j=-H(t_j)/Q_t'(t_j)\).
The alternating signs give \(r_j>0\).
Partial fractions give \(H/Q_t=1-\sum_jr_j/(x-t_j)\), and evaluation at \(h\) gives \(\sum_jr_j/(h-t_j)<1\).
In particular \(\sum_jr_j<4+h\).
The residues \(u_i=H(s_i)/Q_s'(s_i)\) can have either sign.
They satisfy
\[
   V:=\sum_i|u_i|
   \le e^L\left(\sum_i d_i+(1+L)\sum_jr_j\right)
   \le e^L\bigl(4L+(1+L)(4+h)\bigr).
\]

Indeed evaluate the polynomial identity \(H=Q_t-\sum_jr_j Q_t/(x-t_j)\) at \(s_i\) and divide by \(Q_s'(s_i)\).
Each product of factors \((s_i-t_k)/(s_i-s_k)\) over a subset of \(k\ne i\) has absolute value at most \(e^L\), by \(1+x\le e^x\).
The \(Q_t\) term has the additional factor \(s_i-t_i\).
The term indexed by \(j=i\) consists solely of the bounded product, and the term indexed by \(j\ne i\) has the additional factor \((s_i-t_i)/(s_i-s_j)\).
Hence the absolute column sum for each \(r_j\) is bounded by \(e^L(1+\sum_{i\ne j}d_i/|s_j-s_i|)\).
These polynomial evaluations also cover common nodes.
Summing gives the first bound on \(V\), and using the endpoint \(s_0=0\) in \(L\) gives \(\sum d_i\le4L\).

If \(B=Q_s/(x-s_e)\) is integral, the proper residues of \(H/B\) are \((s_i-s_e)u_i\) at \(i\ne e\).
Their total variation is at most \(4V\).
After translation by two, monic division puts \(H/B\) in the [whole-lift residue theorem](Yin_C_G0880_v1.md#statement), with a budget bounded in terms of \(L\) since \(h\le1/6\).
On the other hand the [root-location and Salem lift argument](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) gives the reciprocal polynomial \(S(z)=z^nH(z+z^{-1}-2)\) logarithmic Mahler measure strictly between zero and \(\log(1+1/q)\).
The residue theorem supplies a uniform positive lower bound on this measure, proving the height assertion.

<a id="cyclic"></a>
## Counting degrees with signed evaluations on the extrema

Take the Chebyshev reference grid and \(N\ge2\), since degree two has empty sign region.
Its node polynomial \(Q_s=x(x+4)U_{N-1}((x+2)/2)\) is monic integral, so the height conclusion applies.
Let \(D=2N\) and let \(R=\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}a_jz^j\) represent \(-z^{-1}S\) modulo \(z^D-1\).
At the \(D\)-th roots of unity its absolute evaluations equal \(|H(s_i)|\), with each interior node appearing twice and the endpoints once.
The [derivatives of the node polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v890.md#masses) have absolute values \(2N\) at interior nodes and \(4N\) at the endpoints, by differentiation of the sine expression for \(U_{N-1}\).
Thus the Fourier algebra norm of the integer coefficient function \(a\) on the cyclic group is
\[
           D^{-1}\sum_{\zeta^D=1}|R(\zeta)|=2V.
\]
The sum of the reference nodes is \(-2n\), and the expansion of \(H/Q_s=1+\sum_i u_i/(x-s_i)\) at infinity gives \([x^{n-1}]H=2n+\sum_i u_i\).
Substitution \(x=z+z^{-1}-2\) therefore gives \([z]S=\sum_i u_i\).
In particular \(|[z]S|\le V\).

These two estimates suffice for the [signed-coset construction](Yin_C_G0345_v894.md#cyclic).
Indeed Sanders' theorem gives a bounded number of signed cosets for \(a\) in terms of the bound on \(L\).
Clearing their denominators gives the identity from that construction for \(S W\), where \(W\) is a product of a bounded number of binomials \(1-z^d\).
Its right side, expanded with repeated exponents allowed, has boundedly many terms and bounded integer coefficients since the quotient of \(S+zR\) by \(z^D-1\) is \(z^2+([z]S)z-1\).
Applying the [mixed unit-equation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v894.md#units) now gives a bound in terms of \(L\) on the number of degrees for each fixed \(q\).
In fact \(q^{2n}S(p/q)=K\), and the homogeneous degrees on the two sides after clearing are \(2n+\deg W\) and \(\deg W\).
As \(W(p/q)\ne0\), a minimal vanishing subsum meets both sides.
Its normalized unit ratios in \(\langle p,q\rangle\) determine \(2n\) from a cross-side ratio by coprimality of \(p,q\).
At each \(q\) the number of normalized coefficient patterns and the rank of the tuple group have bounds depending only on \(L\), so the cited counting argument is uniform in \(q\).
Together with the height assertion this proves finiteness.

<a id="mesh"></a>
## Two bounds in terms of the mesh

For \(m\ge1\) write \(\mathcal H_m=\sum_{k=1}^m1/k\), and set \(\mathcal H_0=0\).
On the Chebyshev reference grid one has
\[
  L\le \min\left\{2\pi\varepsilon\mathcal H_m,\
                         \frac{\pi^2}{12}N^2\max_i d_i\right\}.
\]
To see the first bound, write \(\theta_j=j\pi/N\).
At an interior node the smaller adjacent gap is at most half the distance between its two neighbors, hence at most \(2(\pi/N)\sin\theta_j\).
The exact separation is \(4\sin((\theta_i+\theta_j)/2)\sin(|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2)\).
Concavity of sine gives \(\sin\theta_j\le2\sin((\theta_i+\theta_j)/2)\), and the chord bound on \([0,\pi/2]\) gives \(\sin(|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2)\ge|i-j|/N\).
This gives \(d_j/|s_i-s_j|\le\pi\varepsilon/|i-j|\).
Summing independently on either side of \(i\) gives the harmonic bound.

For the second bound the midpoint angle lies in \([|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2,\pi-|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2]\).
Its sine is at least \(\sin(|\theta_i-\theta_j|/2)\), so \(|s_i-s_j|\ge4|i-j|^2/N^2\).
Summing the reciprocal squares on both sides proves the estimate.
Bounded \(L\) occurs at only finitely many realizable pairs, so both quantities on the right must diverge along every sequence of distinct realizable pairs.
Rounding with \(D_N\ge cN^2\) has \(\max_i d_i\le1/(2cN^2)\), proving the stated consequence.
