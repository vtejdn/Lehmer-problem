# Mahler measure of polynomial expressions in distinct conjugate traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound uniform in the coefficients

Fix a positive integer \(s\) and a measure bound \(M>0\).
Consider compressed reciprocal nontorsion units of degree \(d\to\infty\) and logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(M\), with \(u\) minimal blocks of \(b=d/(2u)\) inverse-pair labels as in the [alternating action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
Write \(v_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\) for the trace attached to a label, and choose any \(s\) distinct labels.
Let \(H\in\mathbb Z[T_1,\ldots,T_s]\) depend on every displayed variable, with individual degrees at most \(D\ge1\), and put \(y=H(v_1,\ldots,v_s)\).
Define \(S=\max_{[-2,2]^s}|H|\).
Uniformly over the labels and coefficients, if \(D\sqrt{(2M+u(\log(2b)+4))/b}\to0\), then eventually
\[
 h(y)\gg_s D^{-s}\log S,\qquad
 m(y)\gg_s (b/D)^s\log S,\qquad S\ge\sqrt{2}.
\]
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic height and \(m\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the minimal polynomial.
In particular, even polynomials depending on the seed and having unbounded coefficients cannot give a bounded-measure sequence at these degrees.
This includes, for example, the difference or product of any two distinct conjugate traces, with \(m(y)\gg b^2\).
It leaves the measure of the original unit itself unrestricted within the assumed bound.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Formal stabilizers and actual field degrees

Suppose \(b\ge2s+2\) and \(D\le b-2s\).
View \(H\) as a polynomial with named variables for the chosen labels in the polynomial ring on all labels.
Its formal stabilizer in the label Galois group is exactly the stabilizer of its value.
To see this, any difference between \(H\) and a permuted copy uses at most \(k=2s\) labels and has individual degree at most \(D\).
For any polynomial in at most \(k\) distinct labels that vanishes at their traces, the alternating actions give vanishing at every tuple of distinct labels with the same ordered block assignments.
Every such partial injection in each block extends to an even permutation because at least two positions are unused.
The traces at different labels are distinct because the two roots over a trace are inverses.
Fix all but the last label in an allowed tuple.
At least \(b-k+1>D\) different traces are available for the final position, so every coefficient in the final variable vanishes at the preceding tuple.
Inducting on the number of variables shows the polynomial is zero.
Applying this observation to the difference proves the stabilizer assertion.
If the support uses \(s_B\) variables in block \(B\), even permutations within the blocks give at least \(\prod_B\binom b{s_B}\gg_s b^s\) distinct formal supports and hence distinct conjugates.
This degree estimate applies throughout the range of the statement because the block sizes tend to infinity.

<a id="height"></a>
## A neighborhood of a maximum on the torus

Put \(F(z)=H(z_1+z_1^{-1},\ldots,z_s+z_s^{-1})\), a Laurent polynomial of coordinate degrees between \(-D\) and \(D\).
Its supremum on the unit torus equals \(S\).
The substitution is injective, for the cube contains a real open set, so \(F\) is nonconstant.
A nonzero nonconstant Fourier coefficient appears together with its opposite by inversion symmetry, and both are integers.
Orthogonality on the torus gives \(S^2\ge2\).

On the region \(e^{-1/D}\le|z_i|\le e^{1/D}\), one has \(|F(z)|\le e^s S\).
Indeed, for each coordinate separately the maximum modulus principle on the unit disk, applied to \(z^D F(z)\) or to \(z^D F(1/z)\) with other coordinates fixed, gives the factor \(\max(|z|,|z|^{-1})^D\).
Applying the estimate successively gives the indicated bound.
Choose a torus maximum \(z^*\).
For every point within coordinate distance \(1/(8D)\) of \(z^*\), the coordinate circles of radius \(1/(4D)\) lie inside the region, since \(1-3/(8D)\ge e^{-1/D}\) and \(1+3/(8D)\le e^{1/D}\).
Cauchy's estimate thus bounds each partial derivative there by \(4De^s S\).
Fix \(0<\eta_s\le1/8\) so small that \(4s e^s\eta_s\le 1-2^{-1/4}\).
When each coordinate lies within \(r=\eta_s/D\) of its target \(z_i^*\), integrating derivatives gives \(|F|\ge 2^{-1/4}S\ge S^{1/2}\).

By the [block occupancy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v539.md#statement), the disk at each target contains representatives of at least \(br/(8\pi)\) labels from every block for large enough degree.
Here \(r\) satisfies the shrinking-radius hypothesis and \(br\to\infty\).
Average over the label group by first fixing a coset of the product of the alternating groups and then acting by a uniform member of that product on the left.
Within each resulting block the images of the ordered labels are uniform without replacement since at least two positions are unused.
At each exposure at most \(s-1\) satisfactory labels were used before, so the conditional probability of hitting its target disk is at least \(r/(16\pi)\) for sufficiently large degree.
Each satisfactory label has an orientation in the disk, and \(F\) is invariant under coordinate inversions.
Consequently at least \((r/(16\pi))^s\) of the embeddings have \(\log^+|y|\ge(\log S)/2\).
The algebraic integer \(y\) has height equal to the average of these archimedean positive logarithms.
Multiplying by the degree bound proves the assertion.
