# A logarithmic lower bound for the circle mean of an integral quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## The mean and the Mahler measure

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and irreducible with \(|P(0)|=1\), no root on the unit circle and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=m(P)>0\).
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) satisfy \(P\nmid A\), and put \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}|A/P|\,d\mu\), where \(\mu\) is Haar probability measure.
For every positive integer \(k\),
\[
 C\ \ge\
 \frac{e^{-m}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^k (i+1)^{-1}}
      {26\bigl(1+e^{km}\sqrt{k(1-e^{-2m})}\bigr)}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Consequently every sequence of such pairs with \(m\to0\) satisfies
\[
             \liminf_{m\to0}\frac{C}{\log(1/m)}\ \ge\ \frac1{26}.      \tag{2}
\]
For each individual pair one has the explicit bound
\[
                 m\ \ge\ \tfrac12\exp\!\bigl(-26\sqrt e\,
                                  (1+e\sqrt2)\,C\bigr).               \tag{3}
\]
All three bounds also apply with \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}|B|^2/|P|\,d\mu\) for every \(B\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) with \(P\nmid B\), in any width.

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Restoring the initial Taylor coefficients

Choose the integer \(a\) for which \(H=X^a A/P\) is regular and nonzero at zero.
Write \(0=s_1<s_2<\cdots\) for the exponents of its nonzero Taylor coefficients \(h_i=[X^{s_i}]H\).
The [integral quotient and its inner factor](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) give infinitely many such coefficients, all integers.
The finite Blaschke product \(I\) of the interior poles, normalized by \(I(0)=e^{-m}\), makes \(F=IH\) analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk.
On the circle \(|F|=|A/P|\), so \(\|F\|_1=C\).

Fix \(k\) and put \(T=\sum_{i=1}^k h_i X^{s_i}\).
The [compression inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) gives
\[
 e^{-2km}\sum_{i=1}^k |h_i|^2\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^k |[X^{s_i}]F|^2
                                      \ \le\ k C^2 .
\]
The last inequality uses the bound \(C\) on the modulus of each Fourier coefficient of \(F\).
Orthogonality therefore gives \(\|T\|_2\le e^{km}\sqrt{k}\,C\).
Since \(|I|=1\) on the circle, \(\|I-I(0)\|_2^2=1-e^{-2m}\).
Define
\[
                        G=F-(I-I(0))T.
\]
This function is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
               \|G\|_1\ \le\ C\bigl(1+e^{km}\sqrt{k(1-e^{-2m})}\bigr).
                                                                        \tag{4}
\]
At zero one has \(G=I(0)T+I(H-T)\) as an identity of Taylor germs.
The second term vanishes to order greater than \(s_k\).
Thus the first \(k\) nonzero Taylor coefficients of \(G\) are exactly \(e^{-m}h_i\) at the exponents \(s_i\), including all the intervening gaps.

<a id="littlewood"></a>
## Applying the ordered Littlewood inequality

For integer frequencies \(n_1<\cdots<n_N\) and arbitrary complex amplitudes \(b_j\), the [ordered inequality of Jaming, Kellay and Saba](../audits/literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) gives
\[
                  \Big\|\sum_{j=1}^N b_j X^{n_j}\Big\|_1
                          \ \ge\ \frac1{26}\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{|b_j|}{j+1}.
\]
This uses Theorem 1.1(i) on page 3 of arXiv:2303.10919v2 and the integer-period identification on page 4, at the source unit's statement and applicability scope.
Apply it to the Taylor polynomial of \(G\) through degree \(M\ge s_k\), listing only its nonzero coefficients in increasing order of exponent.
The first \(k\) terms in the lower bound contribute at least \(e^{-m}\sum_{i=1}^k (i+1)^{-1}/26\), since each \(h_i\) is a nonzero integer.
Every later contribution is nonnegative.
The Taylor polynomials converge uniformly on the unit circle, as \(G\) is analytic on a disk of radius greater than one.
Passing to the limit gives the same lower bound on \(\|G\|_1\), which proves (1) by (4).

<a id="consequences"></a>
## Limits and squares of arbitrary width

For small positive \(m\) choose \(k=\lfloor 1/(m\log(1/m))\rfloor\).
Then \(km\to0\), \(k(1-e^{-2m})\le2km\to0\), and \(\log k/\log(1/m)\to1\).
The harmonic sum in (1) equals \(\log k+O(1)\), proving (2).

For \(0<m\le1/2\), take \(k=\lfloor1/m\rfloor\).
Now \(km\le1\), \(e^{-m}\ge e^{-1/2}\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^k(i+1)^{-1}\ge\log((k+2)/2)\ge\log(1/(2m))\).
Substitution in (1) gives (3).
For \(m>1/2\) the right side of (3) is at most \(1/2\).

For the square assertion take \(A=B^2\).
Irreducibility makes \(P\) prime in the Laurent polynomial ring, so \(P\nmid B^2\), and \(|B^2/P|=|B|^2/|P|\) on the circle.
In particular a uniform upper bound for this integral would give a positive Mahler gap on any class admitting such numerators, including reciprocal inputs with base width at least half the degree.
Constructing a numerator with a uniform upper bound remains an arithmetic question.
