# Alternating and symmetric trace actions give units orthogonal to the Salem branch

2026-11-24T22:17 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## A splitting-field criterion and a degree-eighteen example

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with irreducible reciprocal minimum \(P(X)=X^nQ(X+X^{-1})\), of degree \(2n\) with \(n\ge5\).
Assume that \(Q\) has a nonreal conjugate pair and that its splitting group on the \(n\) trace roots contains the natural alternating group \(A_n\).
Write \(L\subset\mathbb C\) for the splitting field of \(P\), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), and \(c\in G\) for ordinary complex conjugation.
Let \(D\) be the subgroup generated by the commutators of pairs of conjugates of \(c\).
Then
\[
                D=G',\qquad G/D\ \text{is elementary abelian of order at most four}.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(L^D\) is the maximal abelian subfield of \(L\).

Let \(T=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) be the field of all totally real algebraic numbers in the specified complex embedding, and put \(M=L T(i)\).
The largest Galois subfield of \(M\) over \(\mathbb Q\) on which all conjugates of complex conjugation commute is exactly \(T(i)\).
All finite abelian number fields are already in \(T(i)\).
For the unit group of this possibly infinite field \(M\), consider its radical saturation
\[
         \mathcal U=\{x\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:
              x^h\in\mu_\infty\mathcal O_M^\times
                         \text{ for some integer }h\ge1\}.
\]
Every element is an algebraic unit, and this group contains no Salem number of degree at least four.
Every nontorsion member of \(\mathcal U\) is orthogonal to every conjugate of every primitive Salem unit in the sense of the [Salem projection criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#algebra).
These conclusions impose no bound on the radical exponents, the auxiliary totally real fields, or the degrees of their units.
The [prime trace-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v309.md#statement) has a different group hypothesis and works for prime trace degree without requiring \(A_n\); (1) applies also in composite degree.

The degree-eighteen polynomial given by the trace
\[
          Q(X)=X^9+X^8-8X^7-8X^6+20X^5+19X^4-16X^3-13X^2+2X+1
\]
satisfies these hypotheses with trace group \(S_9\).
Its splitting field has maximal abelian subfield \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7},\sqrt{-5537520119})\).
In particular the [orthogonal-branch infimum](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement) satisfies
\[
                         \lambda_\perp<\log(1.20143521).                 \tag{2}
\]
This single strictly positive measure gives an upper bound for that infimum; its vanishing remains open.

## The group generated by complex-conjugation commutators

Choose one root from each inverse pair above a trace label.
The action on the roots embeds \(G\) in the signed permutation group \(\mathbb F_2^n\rtimes H\), where \(H\) is the image on the trace labels.
The map onto \(H\) is denoted by \(\pi\), and its kernel \(V\le\mathbb F_2^n\) is \(H\)-invariant under coordinate permutation.
Here \(H=A_n\) or \(S_n\).
Let \(C\) be the normal closure of \(c\) in \(G\); then \(D=C'\), since commuting all the generators of \(C\) makes its image abelian and their commutators are stable under conjugation by \(G\).

The nonreal trace pair implies \(\pi(c)\ne1\).
The nontrivial normal subgroup \(\pi(C)\) of \(H\) contains \(A_n\).
Indeed \(A_n\) is simple and nonabelian for \(n\ge5\); if \(\pi(C)\cap A_n\) were trivial, \(\pi(C)\) would centralize \(A_n\).
The centralizer of the natural \(A_n\) in \(S_n\) is trivial, as any permutation commuting with every three-cycle preserves every three-element support and must fix each label.
Perfection of \(A_n\) and \(\pi(D)=\pi(C)'\) now give \(A_n\le\pi(D)\).
Also \(\pi(D)\le H'\le A_n\), so \(\pi(D)=A_n\).

We claim \([V:V\cap D]\le2\).
If \(V\) contains only constant sign vectors then \(|V|\le2\).
Otherwise choose \(v\in V\) with different entries on two labels, and choose a three-cycle on these labels and a third.
Lift it to \(d\in D\).
The commutator of \(v\) and \(d\) is the sign vector \(v+\pi(d)v\); it lies in \(V\cap D\) and has exactly two nonzero entries.
Transitivity of \(A_n\) on unordered pairs and normality of \(V\cap D\) put all weight-two vectors in \(V\cap D\).
These span the whole even-sum sign subspace.
This proves the claim, and the map to \(H/A_n\) shows that \(|G/D|\le4\).
This quotient is abelian, so \(G'=D\).

If \(H=A_n\) the quotient has order at most two.
If \(H=S_n\), it is generated by the image of \(V\) and one lift \(g\) of a transposition.
In the nonconstant-vector case, \(g^2\in V\) has even sign sum since its sign vector is the sum of a vector and its permutation, hence \(g^2\in D\).
In the constant-vector case, \(g^2\) has zero entries at every label fixed by the transposition, forcing this constant vector to be zero.
Thus in either case the abelian quotient has exponent at most two, proving (1).

## Adjoining every totally real number and taking radicals

The field \(T\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\), since it is stable under every embedding and is closed under field operations.
Complex conjugation is central on \(T(i)\): every automorphism preserves \(T\), on which conjugation is trivial, and acts on \(i\) by a sign.
If \(E/\mathbb Q\) is finite abelian, \(E(i)\) is also abelian.
For \(z\in E\), its real and imaginary parts in \(E(i)\) are fixed by complex conjugation, and all their conjugates are real by centrality.
Thus \(E\subseteq T(i)\), in particular \(L^D\subseteq T(i)\).
Also \(T(i)\cap\mathbb R=T\), by writing each element in the form \(a+bi\) with \(a,b\in T\).

The compositum \(M\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(\widetilde D\) be generated by the commutators of pairs of conjugates of complex conjugation in its Galois group.
Every such commutator fixes \(T(i)\) by centrality.
Restriction to \(L\) on \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/T(i))\) is injective, and restriction from \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\) to \(G\) is onto, by normality and extension of embeddings.
It follows that \(\widetilde D\) is finite and restricts isomorphically to \(D\), since all pairs of conjugates used to define \(D\) lift.
Conversely, every automorphism of \(M\) fixing \(T(i)\) fixes \(L^D\) and therefore restricts to \(D\).
This proves \(\widetilde D=\operatorname{Gal}(M/T(i))\).
A Galois subfield on which the conjugates commute must be fixed by \(\widetilde D\), and \(T(i)\) itself has this property.

The splitting field of a Salem number of degree at least four has commuting conjugates of complex conjugation.
All its trace labels are real, so conjugation acts only by sign flips on the reciprocal pairs.
All its conjugates remain in this abelian normal subgroup.
If a Salem number \(\sigma>1\) belonged to \(M\), its splitting field would be in \(M\), hence in \(T(i)\) by what we just proved.
The real number \(\sigma\) would lie in \(T\), contrary to its nonreal unit-circle conjugates.

The group \(\mathcal U\) is Galois-stable and closed under products, inversion and taking radicals, and each member and its inverse are integral since positive powers of them are integral.
If \(\sigma\in\mathcal U\) were Salem, some power \(\sigma^h\) would lie in \(M\) because all roots of unity are in \(T(i)\).
Every positive power of a Salem number generates its field: an embedding fixing that power must fix its real exterior root, its only conjugate of that modulus.
Thus \(\sigma\in M\), a contradiction.
The saturated group generated by all conjugates of any \(x\in\mathcal U\) consequently contains no Salem number.
The [equivalence of saturation and nonzero projection](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#algebra) now proves the asserted orthogonality without a degree-record assumption.

## A modular certificate for the trace group on nine labels

The [degree-eighteen trace control](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0144/readme.md) gives the displayed \(Q\), the reciprocal lift \(P=X^9Q(X+X^{-1})\), seven distinct real trace roots in \((-2,2)\), and one nonreal conjugate pair.
The [rational-arithmetic program](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0144/trace_control.py) checks those seven sign brackets and a Rouché disk in the upper half-plane, and bounds the full product of the exterior root moduli by
\[
                           1.2013475236<M(P)<1.20143521 .
\]
The trace roots are all accounted for: each interior trace contributes two circle roots, whereas the conjugate pair gives two exterior roots of the same modulus.
We give an additional exact group and irreducibility check.

In the table, coefficient lists are in ascending powers, \(r_j\) is the remainder of \(X^{p^j}\) modulo \(Q\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and the last column is the monic gcd of \(Q\) and \(r_j-X\).
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l|l}
p&j&r_j&\gcd(Q,r_j-X)\\ \hline
2&3&X^8&1\\
2&9&X&Q\bmod2\\
17&1&(15,3,2,2,4,13,14,1,15)&1\\
17&7&(12,6,2,1,5,5,10,0,7)&(6,7,2,2,4,14,7,1)
\end{array}
\]
These entries can be reproduced by starting at \(r_0=X\), taking \(r_{j+1}=r_j^p\bmod Q\) by repeated squaring, and running the polynomial Euclidean algorithm in the last column.
The derivative gcd \(\gcd(Q,Q')\) equals one modulo \(2,7,17\) by the same algorithm.
Modulo two the entire polynomial divides \(X^{2^9}-X\) and has no factor of degree dividing three.
The finite-field factorization of \(X^{p^j}-X\) therefore makes \(Q\bmod2\) irreducible of degree nine, giving rational irreducibility and transitivity on its roots.
Modulo seventeen the degree-seven gcd in the last row is a single irreducible factor, since seven is prime and the preceding row excludes all linear roots.
Its quotient in \(Q\) is \(X^2+11X+3\), also irreducible by the absence of linear roots.

The squarefree reduction modulo seventeen gives a Frobenius permutation of cycle type \((2,7)\): distinct residue roots are permuted in cycles of their irreducible degrees.
Its square has order seven.
An imprimitive transitive group of degree nine preserves three blocks of size three and embeds in \(S_3\wr S_3\) of order \(6^4\).
That order excludes seven, so the trace group is primitive.
Ordinary complex conjugation transposes exactly the two nonreal traces, fixing the seven real ones.
In a primitive group containing a transposition the graph whose edges are its conjugate transpositions is connected, since its connected components give invariant blocks and there is an edge.
Transpositions along a connected graph generate the symmetric group.
Thus the trace group is \(S_9\).

Over the field generated by any trace root \(t\), the discriminant \(t^2-4\) of \(X^2-tX+1\) is negative at a real embedding taking \(t\) into \((-2,2)\).
It cannot be a square there, and rational irreducibility of \(Q\) now gives irreducibility of the degree-eighteen lift.
Hence the full positive measure above is the measure of any one root and proves (2).

Finally \(Q(2)=1,\ Q(-2)=-7\).
Choosing one oriented root \(w_j\) in each inverse pair gives
\[
                 \left(\prod_{j=1}^9(w_j-w_j^{-1})\right)^2
                          =\prod_j(t_j^2-4)=Q(2)Q(-2)=-7.
\]
The trace Vandermonde supplies \(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}Q}\), with
\(\operatorname{disc}Q=\operatorname{Res}(Q,Q')=-5537520119\) by the Sylvester determinant (the degree-nine discriminant sign is positive).
Its squareclass is nontrivial since the trace group contains odd permutations, and has valuation zero at seven by the derivative gcd there.
Thus these two quadratic fields are distinct.
Their compositum lies in the maximal abelian subfield \(L^D\) and its degree four exhausts (1).
