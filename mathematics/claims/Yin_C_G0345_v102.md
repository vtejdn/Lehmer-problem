# A discriminant bound for cofactors of central cyclotomic perturbations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Transfer beyond the square root of the degree

Let \(A\) be any monic product of cyclotomic polynomials of even degree \(d\ge2\), allowing arbitrary multiplicities and the factor \(X-1\).
Put \(G=A+aX^{d/2}\) for a nonzero integer \(a\), and factor \(G=FQ\) into monic integer polynomials, including constant factors.
Write \(m=\log M\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, \(Y=1+m(G)\), and \(\Delta=\log(2d)\).
Remove all cyclotomic factors of \(Q\), with their multiplicities, and let \(f\) be the degree of the remaining polynomial \(Q_0\).
Every noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(G\) occurs with multiplicity one.
With an absolute implied constant one has
\[
  0\le m(G)-m(F)=m(Q)
   \ll \frac{Y f}{d}
       +\sqrt{\frac{Y}{d}}\,f^{3/4}(Y+\Delta)^{1/4}.                 \tag{1}
\]

As \(d\to\infty\), if \(f=o(d^{2/3}/\Delta^{1/3})\), then \(m(Q)=o(1+m(G))\).
In this range bounded \(m(F)\) forces bounded \(m(G)\) and \(m(G)-m(F)\to0\).
Any family with a uniform gap for positive \(m(G)\) therefore has a uniform gap for positive \(m(F)\) under any prescribed bound \(f\le b(d)=o(d^{2/3}/\Delta^{1/3})\).
In particular one may use \(\deg Q\le Kd^{2/3-\epsilon}\) for fixed \(K,\epsilon>0\).

At the full scale \(f\le Kd^{2/3}/\Delta^{1/3}\), for any fixed \(K>0\), one still has \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\) whenever \(d\to\infty\) and \(m(G)\to\infty\).
For the [class of boundedly many multiplicative intervals of binomial exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement), bounded \(m(F)\) at this full scale forces \(Q\) to be a product of cyclotomic polynomials and \(m(F)=m(G)\) eventually as \(d\to\infty\).
In this class there is a uniform gap for positive \(m(F)\) depending only on \(K\) and the interval parameters.

<a id="simple"></a>
## Multiple roots of a central shift

The coordinator supplied the centered derivative argument, the use of the reflected discriminant energy and the Poisson estimate with variable smoothing.
Write \(s=A(0)=\pm1\), so \(X^d A(1/X)=sA(X)\).
On the circle \(e^{-id\theta/2}A(e^{i\theta})\) is real for \(s=1\) and purely imaginary for \(s=-1\).
After multiplication by a constant of modulus one it is a real periodic differentiable function, since \(d\) is even.
At each distinct root angle of multiplicity \(m_j\) its derivative has a zero of order \(m_j-1\) if \(m_j>1\).
Rolle's theorem gives one more zero in each open gap, including the gap returning to the first angle on the next period.
This counts at least \(d\) zeros with multiplicity in one period.
The derivative is a nonzero constant times \(e^{-id\theta/2}H(e^{i\theta})\), where \(H(X)=2XA'(X)-dA(X)\) has degree exactly \(d\).
Thus all roots of \(H\) are on the circle.
The central monomial cancels identically in \(2XG'-dG=H\).
If an irreducible factor is repeated in \(G\), all its roots therefore lie on the circle, and it is cyclotomic by the [measure-one characterization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
Consequently \(Q_0\) is squarefree, monic and has constant term of modulus one because \(G(0)=s\).

<a id="energy"></a>
## Discriminant energy of the noncyclotomic roots

Put \(v=m(Q)=m(Q_0)\) and omit the case \(f=0\), for which \(v=0\).
For each root \(\alpha_i\) of \(Q_0\) put \(t_i=|\log|\alpha_i||\), \(R_i=\max(1,|\alpha_i|)\) and \(w_i=\alpha_i/R_i^2=e^{-t_i+i\theta_i}\).
The unit constant term gives \(\sum_i t_i=2v\), and \(v\le m(G)\) by multiplicativity.
Apply the [reflected-root discriminant calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy) to the full squarefree \(Q_0\), with \(\eta=d^{-3}\) and \(\rho=e^{-\eta}\).
In this setting its energy is
\[
  \mathcal E=\sum_{l\ge1}\frac{\rho^l}{l}\Big|\sum_i w_i^l\Big|^2
            =-\sum_{i,j}\log|1-\rho w_i\overline{w_j}|.
\]
For distinct roots one has \(|1-w_i\overline{w_j}|\ge|\alpha_i-\alpha_j|/(R_iR_j)\).
This is equality when exactly one root is outside, and for two roots on the same side it follows from \(|1-x\bar y|^2-|x-y|^2=(1-|x|^2)(1-|y|^2)\) on the reflected points in the disk.
Also \(|1-\rho z|^2\ge\rho|1-z|^2\) for \(|z|\le1\), since the difference equals \((1-\rho)(1-\rho|z|^2)\).
The product of all ordered off-diagonal distances is the absolute discriminant of \(Q_0\), a positive integer even if \(Q_0\) is reducible.
The factors \(R_i\) contribute \(2(f-1)v\) to the logarithmic bound, and the diagonal terms cost at most \(f\log(1/(1-\rho))\).
Therefore
\[
 \mathcal E\le 2(f-1)v+\frac{\eta f(f-1)}2
                     +f\log\frac1{1-\rho}\ll f(v+\Delta).                \tag{2}
\]
Here \(f\le d\) and \(1-e^{-\eta}\ge\eta/2\).

<a id="poisson"></a>
## Summing the radial displacements

Let \(L=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\), let \(\nu\) be normalized angular measure, and put \(d\mu=L^-\,d\nu\) with \(L^-=\max(0,-L)\).
The mean of \(L\) is zero, so \(\mu(\mathbb T)=\|L\|_1/2\).
The [circle-root estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives \(\max(\mu(\mathbb T),\log|a|)\le m(G)+\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\ll Y\).
For every \(t_i>0\) the [Poisson identity for a central perturbation](Yin_C_G0345_v101.md#poisson) gives
\[
 \frac{d t_i}{2}\le\log|a|+
       \int P_{e^{-t_i}}(\theta_i-\theta)\,d\mu(\theta),
 \qquad P_r(x)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos x+r^2} .                         \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(\log|A(w_i)|=\log|a|-dt_i/2\) inside the circle, directly when \(w_i=\alpha_i\) and by signed reversal and conjugation when \(w_i=1/\overline{\alpha_i}\).
Taking the negative part of the boundary logarithm in the Poisson integral yields (3) in both cases.

Fix \(\eta\le\tau\le1\).
If \(t\ge\tau\), then \(P_{e^{-t}}(x)\le2P_{e^{-(t+\tau)}}(x)\) at every angle.
The ratio takes its maximum at \(x=0\), by the explicit kernel formula, and its value there is \(\tanh((t+\tau)/2)/\tanh(t/2)\le\tanh(t)/\tanh(t/2)\le2\).
The sum of the smoothed kernels over all roots is bounded uniformly in \(\theta\) by
\[
 \sum_i P_{e^{-(t_i+\tau)}}(\theta_i-\theta)
 \le f+2\sqrt{\mathcal E}
          \left(\sum_{l\ge1}l e^{-(2\tau-\eta)l}\right)^{1/2}
 \le f+C\sqrt{\mathcal E}/\tau .                                        \tag{4}
\]
The first bound expands the kernels in Fourier series and applies Cauchy–Schwarz with the weights in (2).
The last bound follows from \(2\tau-\eta\ge\tau\) and the geometric derivative sum.
The small \(t_i<\tau\) contribute at most \(f\tau\) to \(2v\).
Sum (3) for the remaining indices, extend their smoothed kernel sum to all indices using positivity, and apply (2) and (4) to obtain
\[
          v\ll f\tau+\frac{Y}{d}
                \left(f+\frac{\sqrt{f(Y+\Delta)}}{\tau}\right).          \tag{5}
\]

Set \(\tau_0^2=Y\sqrt{Y+\Delta}/(d\sqrt f)\).
Since \(Y\ge1\) and \(f\le d\), one has \(\tau_0\ge d^{-3/4}\ge\eta\).
For \(\tau_0\le1\) its use in (5) balances the two terms involving \(\tau\), giving (1).
For \(\tau_0>1\) the \(t_i<1\) contribute at most \(f\), and for \(t_i\ge1\) the original kernel in (3) is bounded by \((1+e^{-1})/(1-e^{-1})\).
This yields \(v\ll f+Yf/d\), which gives (1) as \(f\le f\tau_0\).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Consequences for the remaining factor

Since \(Y\ge1\), division of (1) by \(Y\) gives
\[
 \frac vY\ll \frac fd+
    \frac{f^{3/4}}{\sqrt d}\frac{(Y+\Delta)^{1/4}}{\sqrt Y}.
\]
The last factor is \(O(\Delta^{1/4})\) for all \(Y\ge1\), proving \(v=o(Y)\) under the stated little-oh bound on \(f\).
At \(f\le Kd^{2/3}/\Delta^{1/3}\) the second term is \(O_K(Y^{-1/4})\), since \((Y+\Delta)/\Delta\ll Y\).
Thus \(v=o(Y)\) at this full scale whenever \(Y\to\infty\), and \(m(F)=m(G)-v\) gives the relative transfer.
In particular bounded \(m(F)\) excludes every subsequence with \(Y\to\infty\) in either range.
Under the little-oh hypothesis this also gives \(v\to0\).
If \(0<m(F)\to0\) while the parent family has a gap, bounded \(d\) is excluded by finiteness of bounded-degree integer polynomials of bounded measure.
On a subsequence with \(d\to\infty\), one would have \(0<m(G)=m(F)+v\to0\), contradicting the parent gap.

Finally suppose the signed binomial exponents \(b_j\) of \(A=\pm\prod_j(1-X^j)^{b_j}\) are supported in at most \(h\) intervals \([x,Cx]\) with fixed \(h,C\), and \(f\le Kd^{2/3}/\Delta^{1/3}\).
If \(m(F)\) is bounded then so is \(m(G)\), hence \(\log|a|\) and \(\|L\|_1\) are bounded.
The [multiplicative-interval theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement) bounds \(S=\sum_j|b_j|\).
The [root bound from the binomial factors](Yin_C_G0345_v101.md#classes) now gives \(|\log|\alpha||\le (2/d)(\log|a|+S\log(4d))=O(\Delta/d)\) at every root of \(G\).
If \(Q_0\ne1\), a monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor of it has degree \(g\le f\), with \(g>1\) since its constant term is a unit.
The [house bound of Dimitrov](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#house-and-its-relation-to-the-gap) gives one root with \(g\log|\alpha|\ge(\log2)/4\), whereas \(f\Delta/d\to0\).
This contradiction gives eventual \(Q_0=1\), hence exact equality of measures.
The whole-polynomial gap from C345v98 transfers at the full stated scale by the same bounded-degree and unbounded-degree alternatives.
