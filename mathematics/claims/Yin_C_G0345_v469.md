# Common strides and power fields of simultaneous signed witnesses

<a id="statement"></a>
## Necessary conditions on the rows in a degree count

Use the [joint degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#statement) at a prime \(p\) and width \(2d\le b<3d\).
The reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\gamma\) and its square have distinct minima of degree \(d\), with product \(G\).
On each selected sign write the nonzero integer row, after division by its scalar content, as
\[
 A(Y)=Y^w B(Y^k),\qquad c=\deg B,\qquad
 r=1+p(b-2w-kc),\qquad g=\gcd(k,r),\qquad \ell=k/g .
\]
Here \(w\) is the smallest exponent in the support and \(k\) is the gcd of its differences.
Every exact row with both required double contacts and nonzero numerators has at least two terms, so \(k,c\ge1\).
Write \(h=m(\gamma)>0\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure and \(e\) for the degree of the [all-power field](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) \(F=\bigcap_{n\ge1}\mathbb Q(\gamma^n)\).
Then
\[
 \ell\in\{1,2\},\qquad e\le c,\qquad
 3p\ell h\le\log\|W_{\rm pr}\|_1
             \le 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2pb+1),                              \tag{1}
\]
where \(W_{\rm pr}\) is the primitive derivative polynomial in the degree test.
If \(\bar G\) is squarefree in characteristic \(p\), then \(g=k\) and \(kc\ge2d\).
The same two conclusions hold at every \(p>10c/3+1\), including primes of bad reduction.
At \(p>10c/3+1\), or when \(\bar G\) is squarefree and \(p>2c+2\), one also has \(2e\le c\).

For two genuine rows on the same pair, let \(t=\gcd(g_+,g_-)\).
Put \(a_i=c_i/2\) if the relevant large-prime condition just stated holds for row \(i\), and \(a_i=c_i\) otherwise.
Then
\[
 d e\le t a_+a_-,\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(\gamma^{2t}):\mathbb Q]\le a_+a_-/e,\qquad
 b/t<3a_+a_-/e.                                                            \tag{2}
\]
In particular bounded normalized spans bound both the degree of this common power and the rescaled width, even for arbitrarily large input degree.
These conclusions hold before a bound on the row norms.

Under the strict budget \(((2pb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^p\), one has \(c\ge86\) at every prime, and \(c\ge92\) if \(\ell=2\).
At every prime \(p\ge347\) of squarefree reduction, or every prime \(p>571\), a passing row has \(c\ge172\).
Both counts \(J_j(w)\) of the degree test are unchanged if a candidate pair violating any applicable bound is discarded.
For its score counts \(N_j(\tau)\), restrictions independent of the strict budget still apply, with the additional cut
\[
       e>\tfrac13\exp\!\left((2/\tau)^{1/3}\right).                       \tag{3}
\]
In particular these restrictions apply to arbitrary coefficients on either of the normalized spans four in the [five-term modular construction](Yin_C_G0345_v467.md#statement), independently of the proposed target pair.
The span cut imposes no uniform bound on rows with five terms at arbitrarily spaced exponents.

## Recovering power fields from one row

A monomial gives a monomial times a signed binomial whose exponent difference is \(1\bmod p\); all its nonzero roots are simple.
For a row with at least two terms, write \(B^*(Y)=Y^cB(1/Y)\) and \(q=pk\).
After cancellation of a monomial the graph in the Laurent ring is \(B(X^q)+\varepsilon X^rB^*(X^q)\).
For \(x=\gamma\) or \(\gamma^2\) and \(z=x^q\), both \(B(z)\) and \(B^*(z)\) are nonzero and the graph recovers \(x^r=-B(z)/(\varepsilon B^*(z))\).
As \(p\nmid r\), one has \(\gcd(q,r)=g\) and Bézout gives \(\mathbb Q(x^g)=\mathbb Q(z)\).
Denote these two field degrees by \(e_1,e_2\) in the order \(x=\gamma,\gamma^2\).
They satisfy \(e\le e_2\le e_1\le2e_2\) by the square map, and each is at least \(d/g\) because \(x\) solves \(T^g-x^g=0\) over its power field.

Double contact and the graph equation eliminate \(x^r\) from the Euler derivative and give \(W_0(z)=0\), where
\[
 W_0(Y)=qY(B'B^*-B(B^*)')-rBB^* .
\]
Its constant term is nonzero because both endpoints of \(B\) and \(r\) are nonzero.
The minima of \(\gamma^q,\gamma^{2q}\) are distinct since their positive absolute logarithmic heights differ by a factor of two.
Their product therefore divides \(W_0\), and \(e_1+e_2\le2c\).
It follows that \(e_2\le c\), \(e_1\le4c/3\), \(c\ge d/g\) and \(\ell d\le kc\le b<3d\).

For \(V=\varepsilon Y^bA(1/Y)\), direct differentiation gives
\[
  pY(A'V-AV')-AV=\varepsilon Y^{b-kc}W_0(Y^k).
\]
The primitive part of \(W_0\) retains both monic minima and has the same one-norm as \(W_{\rm pr}\).
Their logarithmic measures sum to \((q/d)(e_1+2e_2)h\ge3p\ell h\), using multiplicativity of absolute height under powers in logarithmic form.
Mahler measure is bounded above by coefficient one-norm, and the displayed derivative of the original row has one-norm at most \((2pb+1)\|A\|_1^2\).
These facts prove (1), also with the norm of a row before its scalar normalization.

## Multiplicities in fibers at a prime of squarefree reduction

If \(\bar G\) is squarefree, its \(2d\) distinct roots \(\xi\) lift to roots \(x\) of \(G\) in a common finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\).
Group them by \(y=\xi^q\); write \(f_y\) for the number in a fiber.
Reduction of the Euler derivative and then the graph equation gives \(\bar B^*(y)=\bar B(y)=0\), since \(r\) is a unit modulo \(p\).
The Frobenius map is injective, so \(f_y\le k\).
If \(p\mid k\), then \(\ell\le2\) and \(p\nmid g\) force \(p=\ell=2\); in this case \(f_y\le g\) by injectivity of the square map in the residue field.

If \(f_y>g\), we thus have \(p\nmid k\), and two different residues in the fiber have different \(r\)-th powers by \(\gcd(q,r)=g\).
Let \(D=Y\,d/dY\).
The characteristic-zero Euler derivative at each lifted root, with the graph value substituted, is
\[
 (r+qc) B(x^q)=q\big((DB)(x^q)-\varepsilon x^r (x^q)^c(DB)(x^{-q})\big).
\]
For every lift in this fiber \(x^q\) is congruent modulo \(p^2\) to the unique prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift of \(y\): writing \(x=\omega(1+pz)\) gives this congruence since \(p\mid q\).
Consequently \(B(x^q)/p\bmod p\) depends only on \(y\).
Divide the last equation by \(p\), subtract for the two residues, and use \(p\nmid k\) to obtain \((D\bar B)(1/y)=0\).
Reciprocity of \(G\) gives \(f_{1/y}=f_y\); the same conclusion there gives \((D\bar B)(y)=0\).
This also covers the self-inverse fibers.
Thus \(\operatorname{ord}_y\bar B\ge\lceil f_y/g\rceil\), since \(f_y\le k\le2g\).
The reduction of the content-normalized \(B\) is nonzero.
Summation over the fibers gives \(c\ge 2d/g\), hence \(2\ell d\le b<3d\), proving the assertion.

## Local contacts after division by the stride

Suppose first \(p>10c/3+1\), or suppose \(\bar G\) is squarefree and \(p>2c+2\).
Then \(p\) is odd and \(\ell\) is a \(p\)-unit.
For \(u=r/g\), the two minima of \(\gamma^g,\gamma^{2g}\) both have double contact with
\[
 B(X^{p\ell})+\varepsilon X^u B^*(X^{p\ell}),
\]
since substitution \(X\mapsto X^g\) preserves order of vanishing at a nonzero point in characteristic zero.
Their degrees are \(e_1,e_2\), and their positive absolute heights distinguish them.
We give the change needed in the [local disk and multiplicity proof](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#disk) for the two possibly different and signed exponents.

In a disk with residue \(\xi\ne0\) and prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift \(\omega\), let \(E\) be the largest residue multiplicity of either minimum there.
Every selected root in that disk satisfies \(v_p(X/\omega-1)\ge1/E\) by [Hensel factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#degree).
Take \(E=1\) instead when \(\bar G\) is squarefree, since every selected root is then a power of a unit in an unramified extension.
In the first case \(E\le4c/3\), by the field-degree bound above.
Expand \(B,B^*\) at \(\omega^{p\ell}(1+pZ)\); let \(s\) be the least valuation of all coefficients of these two expansions.
Then \(s\le\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^{p\ell}}\bar B\le c\), and division by \(p^s\) gives two integral polynomials with reductions of degree at most \(s\), at least one nonzero.
Our prime conditions imply \(2s+1<p-E\).

On \(v_p(X/\omega-1)\ge1/E\) use \(Z=((X/\omega)^{p\ell}-1)/p\).
Raising to the \(\ell\)-th power preserves valuation of displacement from one and is injective in that disk, since \(\ell\) is a \(p\)-unit.
The [disk calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#disk) for the remaining \(p\)-th power thus preserves distances from the center and separates the selected roots.
In these coordinates \(X^u=\omega^u(1+pZ)^{a/p}\), where \(a=u/\ell\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\).
Indeed the convergent binomial branch agrees at the center and is the inverse power on this disk, as follows by first taking the indicated \(p\)-th root and then the integral \(p\)-adic exponent \(u/\ell\).
Its coefficient at index \(n\) is \(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(a-jp)/n!\), of valuation at least \(-v_p(n!)\), and for \(n<p\) it reduces to \(\bar a^n/n!\).
The constant factor \(\omega^u\) is absorbed in the second polynomial.
In the falling-factorial argument of the disk calculation the coefficient at degree \(n\), multiplied by \(n!\bar a^{-n}\), has the same linear equations with the \(j\)-th coefficient of that second polynomial multiplied by \(\bar a^{-j}\).
These nonzero scalings preserve the proof of a unit coefficient in degrees through \(2s+1\); the factorial inequality and convergent-series zero count are unchanged.
The coordinate derivative is nonzero on the disk, so the sum of contact multiplicities of the selected roots is at most \(2s+1\).

For a fixed \(y=\xi^{p\ell}\) there can be two occupied disks only when \(\ell=2\), in which case their residues are opposite.
The two centers give the same \(\omega^{p\ell}\), hence the same normalized polynomial expansions, whose reductions we denote by \(L,M\) before absorbing the constant.
The constants \(\varepsilon\xi^u\) on the two disks are distinct, since \(\gcd(p\ell,u)=1\).
Write \(m\le n\) for the first nonzero reduced coefficient indices of their respective normalized series, using coefficients below \(p\); both exist through degree \(2s+1\).
Subtract the two series through index \(m-1\).
Since the reduced exponential series is a unit at zero, \(Z^m\) divides \(M\) and hence \(L\).
At least one reduction is nonzero, so \(m\le s\).
After division of \(L,M\) by \(Z^m\), the same falling-factorial calculation through degree \(2(s-m)+1\) gives \(n\le m+2(s-m)+1\).
Thus \(m+n\le2s+1\).
In each disk the first unit coefficient bounds its contact count by that index, by the same factorial estimate since the index is at most \(2s+1<p-E\).
Consequently the total contact count over a given \(y\), including the one-disk case, is at most \(2s+1\).
Each selected root contributes at least two, so at most \(s\le\operatorname{ord}_y\bar B\) selected roots lie over \(y\).
Summing gives \(2d/g\le e_1+e_2\le c\), \(e_2\le c/2\) and \(2\ell d\le b<3d\).

## The degree after extracting a common stride

For each sign let \(K_i=\mathbb Q(\gamma^{2g_i})\).
The one-row field recovery gives \([K_i:\mathbb Q]=e_{2,i}\le a_i\), and both fields contain \(F\).
Bézout gives \(K_+K_-=\mathbb Q(\gamma^{2t})\).
Any basis of \(K_-\) over \(F\) spans this compositum over \(K_+\), so the compositum degree is at most \(a_+a_-/e\).
Over the compositum, \(\gamma^2\) solves \(T^t-\gamma^{2t}=0\).
Its degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(d\), giving (2) since \(b<3d\).
For example, any candidate with \(g_+,g_-\) coprime requires \(a_+a_-\ge de\).
The quotient degrees and the bound on \(b/t\) do not require \(b/t\) to be an integer.

## Consequences for the exact degree counts

The [all-power norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) in \(F\) is a unit of degree \(e\) and logarithmic measure exactly \(h\).
By (1) the strict budget gives \(h<(\log2)/(12\ell)\).
The [degree bound at 1.06](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree) gives \(e\ge86\), since \(2^{1/12}<1.06\).
For \(\ell=2\) apply the [bound at 1.036](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#statement) to get \(e\ge92\) from \(2^{1/24}<1.036\).
If \(c\le171\), the condition \(p>571\) implies \(p>10c/3+1\); at a prime of squarefree reduction \(p\ge347\) implies \(p>2c+2\).
The local contact result in each case implies \(c\ge2e\ge172\), a contradiction.
The primitive and raw scores both exceed or equal \(\ell h\) by (1) and the nonnegative other terms.
At a strict cutoff \(\tau\), [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(2/\log^3(3e)<h<\tau\) and hence (3).
The canonical field and its degree are computable from the original minimum using the finite exponent in the norm construction.
Each cut excludes only impossible rows, preserving all degrees counted by the corresponding exact test.
In particular the normalized span alone excludes exact passing witnesses of the form \(Y^wB(Y^k)\) with \(\deg B\le85\), including both five-term rows at every precision and every modulus in the cited construction.
The remaining degree counts with larger admissible spans are open.
