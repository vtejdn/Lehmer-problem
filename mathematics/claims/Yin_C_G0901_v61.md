# A sharp Mahler bound for three independent reciprocal pairs and its one-variable obstruction

<a id="statement"></a>
## The three-pair bound

Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure with probability Haar normalization, and \(M=\exp m\).
Let \(Y\) denote any finite list of torus variables, \(H(Y)\) any complex Laurent polynomial, and \(u_1,u_2,u_3\) Laurent monomials in \(Y\) with coefficients of absolute value one.
In three additional variables put
\[
 F(Y,Z)=H(Y)+\sum_{j=1}^3\bigl(u_j(Y)Z_j+u_j(Y)^{-1}Z_j^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
 m(F)\ \ge\ c_3:=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\cos v)\,dv
       \ >\ \log(4/3).                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The case \(H=0\) attains \(c_3\).
With two additional variables the corresponding infimum is zero, attained when \(H=0\).
Indeed Jensen in the final variable gives a nonnegative conditional measure, and \(x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}=(x+y)(1+(xy)^{-1})\).

There is a one-variable consequence with an explicit dependence on the number \(k\) of nonzero coefficients.
For \(0<\delta<1\) set
\[
 \eta=\left(\frac{\delta}{48k^2}\right)^{4(k-1)},\qquad
 N(k,\delta)=\left\lceil\frac{1+k^2/\eta}{\delta}\right\rceil .          \tag{2}
\]
Let \(S\subset\mathbb Z^r\) be the exponent support of \(F\), in all its variables, and let \(A:\mathbb Z^r\to\mathbb Z\) be an integer linear map.
Write \(F_A\) for the Laurent polynomial obtained by mapping each monomial of exponent \(s\) to \(X^{A(s)}\).
For \(N=N(k,\delta)\), let \(N(S-S)\) consist of all sums of \(N\) differences of elements of \(S\), including zero differences.
If
\[
                     \ker A\cap N(S-S)=\{0\},                            \tag{3}
\]
then \(m(F_A)\ge c_3-\delta\).
The test (3) is sufficient also when the dimension, coefficient sizes and exponents vary with the input.
At fixed \(k\) its length is uniform, and growing \(k\) carries the explicit increasing cost in (2).
The bound concerns the entire specialized polynomial.
It passes to an individual irreducible unit factor when removing one occurrence of that factor leaves a cofactor of multiplicative measure one.

## Minimization over the remaining terms

Conditional on \(Y\), the phases \(u_j(Y)\) rotate independent uniform circle variables.
It suffices to bound \(\mathbb E\log|t+S_1+S_2+S_3|\) for every complex constant \(t\), where \(S_j=2\cos\theta_j\) and the \(\theta_j\) are independent uniform angles.
Since the \(S_j\) are real, replacing \(t\) by its real part gives a lower bound pointwise.
For real \(v\), Jensen applied to \(X^2+vX+1\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\log|v+S_3|=g(v),\qquad
 g(v)=
 \begin{cases}0,&|v|\le2,\\ \operatorname{arcosh}(|v|/2),&|v|>2.\end{cases}
\]
All the logarithmic integrals used here are well-defined, including at the endpoints, by the logarithmic integrability of a nonzero Laurent polynomial on its torus.

The sum \(S=S_1+S_2\) has an even density decreasing on its positive support.
Indeed \((U,V)\mapsto(U+V,U-V)\) preserves probability Haar measure as a surjective torus homomorphism, so \(S\) has the law of \(4\cos U\cos V\).
Each \(|\cos U|\) has density \(2/(\pi\sqrt{1-x^2})\) for \(0<x<1\).
The density of \(|S|/4\) at \(0<t<1\) is therefore
\[
 \frac4{\pi^2}\int_t^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}\sqrt{x^2-t^2}}
 =\frac4{\pi^2}\int_0^{\pi/2}
        \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{t^2+(1-t^2)\sin^2\theta}} .
\]
The substitution is \(x^2=t^2+(1-t^2)\sin^2\theta\), and the last integrand decreases with \(t\).
Among all intervals of a fixed length the centered interval thus has largest \(S\)-probability, by integrating the centered interval superlevel sets of its density.
Consequently for all real \(t\) and \(s\ge0\) one has \(\Pr(|S+t|>s)\ge\Pr(|S|>s)\).
Since \(g\) increases with absolute value, \(\mathbb E g(S+t)\ge\mathbb E g(S)\).
Integration in the remaining variables proves that \(H=0\) gives the exact minimum.

To identify this minimum take the [evaluated three-variable polynomial](Yin_C_G0224_v1.md#three-variable-kernel-limit)
\[
 P(x,W,z)=1+xW^2-W(x+1)(z+z^{-1}).
\]
Put \(x=r^2\), \(W=t/r\), and divide by the unit-modulus monomial \(t\).
The result is \(t+t^{-1}-(r+r^{-1})(z+z^{-1})\).
The first substitution preserves Haar probability, as does \((r,z)\mapsto(rz,r/z)\), under which the product of the two reciprocal sums is the sum of two reciprocal pairs.
Rotating both of the latter variables by \(-1\) absorbs their signs.
The cited evaluation and its strict rational lower bound now give (1).

## Transfer by matching finitely many constant coefficients

We prove \(m(G_A)\ge m(G)-\delta\) for any complex Laurent polynomial \(G\) with \(k\ge2\) terms whose support satisfies (3).
Divide by the largest coefficient modulus, so \(G\) has maximum coefficient modulus one and coefficient length \(B\le k\).
Condition (3) prevents collisions even among the individual support monomials, whence \(G_A\) has the same \(k\), maximum modulus of a coefficient, and length.
The division adds the same constant to both logarithmic measures upon rescaling.

On each torus put \(R_\eta(G)=\tfrac12\log(|G|^2+\eta)\).
For the one-variable polynomial \(G_A\), the [regularization estimate, Proposition 4.10](../audits/literature/BGMP24/Yin_U001.md#bgmp24-u001), gives
\[
 0\le\int R_\eta(G_A)-m(G_A)
       \le24 k^2\eta^{1/(4(k-1))}=\delta/2 .
\]
Here both the source and target tori of the proposition have dimension one, the matrix is the identity and its least nonzero kernel relation has infinite norm.
Thus its required inequality with the exponent diameter holds even for an arbitrarily large specialized degree.
The parameters are maximum coefficient modulus one and \(\alpha=1/2\).

Write \(V=(B^2-|G|^2)/(B^2+\eta)\), so \(0\le V\le b=B^2/(B^2+\eta)<1\).
The convergent logarithm series gives
\[
 R_\eta(G)=\tfrac12\log(B^2+\eta)-\tfrac12\sum_{j\ge1}V^j/j .
\]
Cutting after \(N\) terms gives an upper approximation with error at most
\[
 \frac{b^{N+1}}{2(N+1)(1-b)}
 \le\frac{1+k^2/\eta}{2(N+1)}\le\delta/2 .
\]
The same approximation and error bound hold for \(G_A\).
On the torus the frequencies of \(|G|^2\) lie in \(S-S\).
Every retained power therefore has its frequencies in \(N(S-S)\), and (3) makes its constant coefficient identical before and after specialization.
The integrated upper approximations coincide.
The one-variable regularized integral is at least this common value minus \(\delta/2\), and the common value is at least \(\int R_\eta(G)\ge m(G)\).
The one-variable regularization cost proves the asserted transfer, and (1) supplies the bound for the three reciprocal pairs.

## A necessary relation for each selected triple in one variable

Consider directly a one-variable reciprocal Laurent polynomial in which three distinct positive frequencies \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) have symmetric coefficients \(\sigma_j\in\{1,-1\}\).
Write it as \(H(X)+\sum_j\sigma_j(X^{a_j}+X^{-a_j})\) by removing the six selected terms, and write \(k\) for its number of terms.
Unfold just those three pairs into new variables, retaining the one-variable \(H\) exactly.
The support of this comparison polynomial is
\[
 S=\{(h,0):h\in\operatorname{supp}H\}
     \ \cup\ \{(0,\pm e_j):1\le j\le3\}\ \subset\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^3 .
\]
If the original polynomial has \(M\le601/500\), then for \(N=N(k,1/12)\) there must be a relation
\[
 (b,v)\in N(S-S),\qquad v\ne0,\qquad b+v_1a_1+v_2a_2+v_3a_3=0 .       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed in the absence of (4), (3) holds for \(A(b,v)=b+v\cdot a\), since a kernel element with \(v=0\) also has \(b=0\).
The previous sections would give \(M>(4/3)e^{-1/12}>11/9>601/500\).
This contradiction applies to every choice of the three pairs.
For example if \(|h|\le D\) on \(\operatorname{supp} H\), every displayed relation has \(|b|\le2ND\) and \(\|v\|_1\le2N\).
Thus the elementary inequalities \(|v\cdot a|>2ND\) for all \(0<\|v\|_1\le2N\) suffice to exclude this measure range, also for moving inputs when their own \(k,D,N\) are used.

The degree-eighteen [orthogonal example](Yin_C_G0901_v56.md#statement) has centered positive unit-coefficient frequencies \(7,8,9\), with remainder \(-1-X^2-X^{-2}-X^4-X^{-4}\).
Here \(7+9=2\cdot8\) supplies (4) already in \(2(S-S)\).
The same relation for the three distinct frequencies \(a,a+1,a+2\), \(a\ge1\), gives a product of measure one when \(H=0\), since their sum of reciprocal pairs equals
\((X^{a+1}+X^{-a-1})(1+X+X^{-1})\).
Large degree alone therefore allows collapse even for the template attaining (1).
