# Explicit integral rows with independent first derivatives at every degree-preserving power

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two rows with an absolute leading coefficient in the height bound

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with monic reciprocal minimum \(P\) of degree \(e=2m\ge4\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(h=m(P)\).
Let \(p\ge2\) be any integer for which \(z=\alpha^p\) has degree \(e\), let \(|w|\le p\) be an integer, and fix a sign \(\sigma=\pm1\).
Put \(B=4e,\ K^+=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) and \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\).
There are two explicit integer polynomial pairs \((a_i,b_i)\), where \(a_i\) has degree \(B\) and both endpoint coefficients one, \(b_i\ne0\) has degree \(<B\) and zero constant term, and \(\gcd(a_i,Y^{B-1}b_i(1/Y))=1\).
For \(f_i=Y^{-2e}a_i,\ g_i=Y^{-2e}b_i\), both rows have the same nonzero value \(u\) and satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
     (f_i(z),\mathcal E f_i(z))&=(u,\ell_i),\\
     (g_i(z),\mathcal E g_i(z))&=\sigma\alpha^{-w}(-u,\ell_i+w u/p).
 \end{split}                                                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(u,\ell_i\in K^+\), both \(\ell_i/u\) are irrational and \(u(\ell_2-\ell_1)\ne0\).
Each \(a_i(x^p)+\sigma x^{pB-w}b_i(x^{-p})\) is divisible by \(P(x)^2\) in the Laurent polynomial ring.
The sum of absolute coefficients satisfies
\[
          \log\max_i(\|a_i\|_1,\|b_i\|_1)
                \le 3ph+3e\log2+O(\log(2ep))                          \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute implied constant.
In particular the minimum in the [one-prime integer test](Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement) is finite at every even degree \(e\ge4\), and its logarithm is at most \((3+o(1))p_eh\) uniformly as \(e\) tends to infinity.

<a id="transport"></a>
## Transporting a value to the other row over the integers

Let \(\alpha_j\) be the conjugates of \(\alpha\), let \(z_j=\alpha_j^p\), and let \(Q=\prod_j(Y-z_j)\).
It is a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(2m\) with constant term one.
Write
\[
    q(Y)=Y^{-m}Q(Y),\quad D=\mathcal E q,\quad
    \delta=Y-Y^{-1},\quad S=Y+Y^{-1}.
\]
Inversion fixes \(q\) and negates \(D,\delta\).
At \(z\) one has \(D(z)=z^{1-m}Q'(z)\ne0\).
Choose \(\eta=1\) if \(w>0\) and \(\eta=0\) otherwise, so \(c=p\eta-w\) belongs to \([0,p]\).
Set
\[
  T(Y)=\sum_j\alpha_j^c\prod_{k\ne j}(Y-z_k),\qquad V=Y^{1-m-\eta}T(Y).
\]
The coefficients of \(T\) are algebraic integers fixed by the Galois group of a splitting field, and hence integers.
Substitution at any \(z_j\) gives \(V(z_j)=\alpha_j^{-w}D(z_j)\).
All exponents of \(q,D,V\) lie in \([-m,m]\).
With \(M=2^e\exp(ph)\) one has \(\|q\|_1\le M,\ \|D\|_1\le mM,\ \|V\|_1\le eM\).
For the last inequality the missing factor in each product absorbs the weight, since \(|\alpha_j|^c\le\max(1,|z_j|)\).
The [two-column correction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v239.md#direction) uses the same value transport at offset one.

<a id="rows"></a>
## An integral choice of the value and both first derivatives

Let \(A=D^3\delta,\ W=D^2 V\delta\).
The polynomial \(A\) is fixed by inversion, so \(A(z)\in K^+\) and \(A(z)\ne0\).
Here \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and the fixed field of inversion is \(K^+=\mathbb Q(z+z^{-1})\) of degree \(m\).
For a choice of \(L\) from \(1,S,2,2S\) form integral Laurent polynomials
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f_L &=p\{A-q(\mathcal EA)/D\}+q D L,\\
 g_L &=\sigma\{-p(W-q(\mathcal EW)/D)+wqDV\delta+q V L\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{3}
\]
Both displayed divisions take place in the integral Laurent ring.
For instance \((\mathcal EW)/D=2(\mathcal ED)V\delta+D(\mathcal EV)\delta+DV(\mathcal E\delta)\), and \((\mathcal EA)/D=3D(\mathcal ED)\delta+D^2(\mathcal E\delta)\).
For \(J=A,W\), the correction \(J-q(\mathcal EJ)/D\) takes value \(J(z)\) with first Euler derivative zero at \(z\).
Consequently (3) gives (1) with \(u=pA(z)\) and \(\ell=D(z)^2 L(z)\).
The term \(wqDV\delta\) contributes \(w\alpha^{-w}A(z)\) to the second derivative datum in (1), before multiplication by the sign.
The element \(\kappa=D(z)^2/u\) is nonzero and fixed by inversion.
At least one of \(\kappa,\kappa S(z)\) is irrational, since \(S(z)\) has degree \(m\ge2\).
Choose \(L=1\) or \(S\) accordingly for the first row and twice this \(L\) for the second.
The two derivatives are nonzero and distinct.

For completeness, divide the seed graph by \(x^{2ep}\) to obtain \(f_i(x^p)+\sigma x^{-w}g_i(x^{-p})\).
At \(\alpha\), inversion gives \(g_i(z^{-1})=-\sigma\alpha^w u\) and \((\mathcal E g_i)(z^{-1})=\sigma\alpha^w(\ell_i+w u/p)\).
Both the value and the Euler derivative of the graph therefore vanish at \(\alpha\), and its Galois conjugates give the asserted multiplicity.

<a id="bounds"></a>
## Unit endpoints, coprimality and the coefficient estimate

Every Laurent exponent in (3) belongs to \([-3m-1,3m+1]\).
Multiplication by \(Y^{2e}\) thus produces \(a,b\) supported strictly inside \([0,B]\), since \(3m+1\le4m-1\).
Add \(Q(Y)^2(1+Y^{2e})\) to \(a\) to set both endpoints to one.
This leaves its value and derivative at \(z\) unchanged.
As \(b(z)\ne0\) and \(Q\) is reciprocal, \(Q\) has no common root with \(Y^{B-1}b(1/Y)\).
At its nonzero roots the further change \(a\mapsto a+tY^e Q^2\) has nonzero slope.
At most \(B-1\) scalars \(t\) are forbidden, and \(a(0)=1\) excludes a common zero at zero.
Checking \(0\le t\le B-1\) therefore achieves coprimality on each row and preserves its endpoints and jets.

An Euler derivative on support \([-m,m]\) increases the coefficient one-norm by a factor at most \(m\).
The displayed products and the expanded quotients in (3) consist of at most three factors from \(q,D,V\) and their derivatives, together with factors of bounded one-norm from \(\delta,S,L\) and their derivatives.
The triangle and product inequalities give a bound of \((2ep)^{C}M^3\) for some absolute \(C\) on both one-norms.
The endpoint and gcd corrections add at most \((B+2)M^2\), proving (2).
All choices involve only symmetric polynomial arithmetic, exact field evaluation and a finite scalar test.
The [height lower bound at degree \(e\)](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) makes \(e+\log(2ep_e)=o(p_eh)\) at \(p_e>16e^4\), uniformly in the seed.

More generally the same test with any degree-only prime \(p(e)>e+1\) satisfying \(p(e)/e\to\infty\) is equivalent to a uniform Lehmer gap.
The [norm argument for powering](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#primes) ensures degree preservation: a loss at a prime not dividing \(e\) forces \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\subset\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and hence \(p-1\mid e\).
The [individual coefficient lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#integer) applies verbatim.
For the reverse implication (2) divided by \(p(e)\) tends to zero on any shrinking sequence, because \(e/p(e)\to0\).
In particular one may take the least prime greater than \(e\log(2e)\).
This last equivalence uses the actual seed graphs and requires no choice of a second prime.
The remaining absolute lower bound on the coefficient rate is open.
