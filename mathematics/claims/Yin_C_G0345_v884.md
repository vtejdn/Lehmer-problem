# A norm-one Salem trace forces nonreal zeros in its auxiliary polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on the inverse construction

Let \(q\ge2\) be an integer and \(M=q(q+1)\).
Suppose a monic \(H\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(n\ge2\) has one root \(w_0\in(0,2/q)\), its remaining roots in \((-4,0)\), and \(M^nH(1/M)=1\).
Count roots with multiplicity and let \(a=-\sum_i w_i\).
There is a unique integer polynomial \(G\) such that
\[
 H(x)=x^n+(Mx-1)G(x).
\]
Its degree is \(n-2\) and its leading coefficient is the positive integer \(a/M\).
Whenever \(a\ge30\), in particular for every \(q\ge5\), \(G\) has nonreal roots.
This excludes every real-rooted auxiliary polynomial in the signed norm-one branch of the [Salem trace construction](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion), including products of real-rooted factors with any leading coefficient.

For every nonzero integer \(j\) with \(|j|\le a/(64\pi)\) there is a distinct nonreal zero \(x_j\) of \(G\) with
\[
 \left|x_j-\frac{a}{2\pi i j}\right|<4 .
\]
Thus \(G\) needs at least \(2\lfloor a/(64\pi)\rfloor\) nonreal roots, a positive proportion of its degree along any unbounded-degree sequence.
Either \(q\to\infty\) or \(n\to\infty\) forces \(a\to\infty\).
These are necessary conditions on the sought polynomials, without an existence assertion.

<a id="moments"></a>
## The sum of squares of the auxiliary roots

The norm identity is \(\prod_i(1-Mw_i)=1\).
Its factors at the negative roots exceed one, so \(0<w_0<1/M\le1/6\).
The nonzero integer \(H(0)\) gives \(\prod_{i>0}|w_i|\ge1/w_0>1\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality then gives \(a>n-1-w_0\), and \(a>0\) since at least one negative root has absolute value greater than one.
Also \(a<4(n-1)\).
The primitive linear polynomial \(Mx-1\) divides \(H(x)-x^n\) over the rationals by evaluation at \(1/M\), hence over the integers by Gauss's lemma.
The coefficient of \(x^{n-1}\) is \(a>0\), proving the degree and leading coefficient statements and \(a\ge M\).

Write \(s_k=\sum_i w_i^k\).
We have \(s_2\le4(a+w_0)+w_0^2<4a+1\) and \(s_3\le w_0^3<1\).
If \(a\ge30\), then \(a<4(n-1)\) ensures \(n\ge9\).
Let \(b,c\) be the coefficients at degrees \(n-2,n-3\) in \(H\).
Newton's identities give
\[
 b=(a^2-s_2)/2,\qquad c=(a^3-3as_2-2s_3)/6.
\]
Writing the monic normalization of \(G\) with next two coefficients \(b',c'\), comparison gives \(b'=b/a+1/M\) and \(c'=c/a+b'/M\).
Consequently the sum of the squares of the roots of \(G\) is
\[
 b'^2-2c'=-\frac{a^2}{12}+\frac{s_2}{2}+
             \frac{s_2^2}{4a^2}+\frac{2s_3}{3a}-\frac{1}{M^2}.
\]
This is less than \(-a^2/12+2a+6\) when \(a\ge30\), using \(s_2<4a+1\) and \(s_3<1\).
The upper bound is negative at 30 and strictly decreasing thereafter.
Real roots alone would have a nonnegative sum of squares.

<a id="zeros"></a>
## Location of individual nonreal zeros

On the closed disk \(|t|\le1/16\) use the analytic logarithm given by its Taylor series at one and write
\[
 F(t)=a^{-1}\sum_i\log(1-w_i t).
\]
Since \(|w_i t|\le1/4\), the terms beyond the linear term in each series have sum of absolute values at most \(2|w_i t|^2/3\).
The first moment gives \(F'(0)=1\), and \(s_2/a<4+1/a\le4+1/6\), so
\[
 |F(t)-t|\le3|t|^2 .
\]
For each \(j\) in the stated range set \(s_j=2\pi i j/a\), so \(0<|s_j|\le1/32\).
On the boundary of the disk, \(|F(t)-s_j-t|\le3/256+1/32<1/16\).
[Rouché's theorem on a disk](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk) gives exactly one solution \(t_j\) of \(F(t)=s_j\) in this disk, counted with multiplicity.
Different \(j\) have different solutions, and none of these solutions is real, since every summand of \(F\) is real on the real diameter.
Exponentiating \(aF(t_j)\) gives \(\prod_i(1-w_i t_j)=1\).
Consequently \(x_j=1/t_j\) is a zero of \(H(x)-x^n\), and its nonreal value rules out the linear factor \(Mx-1\).
Finally \(|s_j|\ge(1-3/16)|t_j|\), whence
\[
 \left|\frac1{t_j}-\frac1{s_j}\right|
 \le\frac{3|t_j|^2}{|t_j||s_j|}\le48/13<4 .
\]
The count is a positive proportion asymptotically because \(a>n-1-w_0\).
