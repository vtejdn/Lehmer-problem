<a id="single-chain-prescribed-target-obstruction"></a>
### 1. A full-core single-chain obstruction for the prescribed degree-six input

For any integral upper-unitriangular rank-four core let

    p=t^4+A t^3+B t^2+A t+1,
    q=t^3+C t^2+C t+1

be its determinant and one principal deletion.
Appending a three-vertex unit chain at that vertex gives the L203 determinant C_3 p-t C_2 q, where C_j=1+t+...+t^j.
Its coefficients from degrees7 to4 are

    1, A, A+B-C, 2A+B-2C.

The prescribed (t+1)P has these coefficients1,0,-1,-1.
Matching them forces A=0, B=-1, C=0.
Thus its deletion would have q=t^3+1.
For a general integral upper-unitriangular rank-three matrix with strict upper entries a,b,c, direct expansion gives C=3-(a^2+b^2+c^2)+abc.
The equation C=0 would require a^2+b^2+c^2-abc=3.
This is impossible modulo4: with zero odd entries it is0; with one odd entry it is1; with two odd entries it is0 or2; with three odd entries it is0 or2.
This is the retained L190 rank-three residue obstruction applied to the forced deletion.
Therefore no arbitrary integral rank-four core with one appended three-vertex unit chain realizes exactly(t+1)P.
The restriction is the displayed architecture and exact padding; it is not nonrealizability of P with other core sizes, couplings, chain lengths or cyclotomic products.

<a id="two-chain-complete-determinant"></a>
### 2. Independent unit-chain attachments

For an integral upper-unitriangular core V and distinct vertices i,j, let p be its determinant, q_i and q_j its single-deletion determinants, and r the determinant with both deleted.
Append disjoint unit chains of lengths a,b at i,j, all new vertices ordered after the core.
Expansion at the path endpoints, or the two-variable diagonal Schur complement, gives exactly

    p_(a,b)=C_a C_b p-t C_(a-1) C_b q_i
             -t C_a C_(b-1) q_j+t^2 C_(a-1) C_(b-1) r.    (1)

Here C_(-1)=0, so empty chains are included.
Each chain's finite continued fraction subtracts t C_(a-1)/C_a at its attaching diagonal, and the determinant is affine in each of these two distinct diagonal variables; clearing C_a C_b proves the full polynomial identity, including every point where a denominator vanishes.
This direct calculation imposes no sign condition on the core.
The two independent deletion polynomials and their intersection are the changed data, not simply a longer fixed chain.
The all-negative absolute presentation obeys the same identity with the actual absolute core and its same-index deletions.
No factor may be omitted before comparing with a target.

<a id="minimal-padding-pendant-forest-obstruction"></a>
### 3. Every three-vertex unit pendant forest misses the minimally padded target

The following is a complete all-integer obstruction, not a conclusion from the finite search.
Let P=t^6-t^5-t^3-t+1.
Its irreducible Salem status and beta in(3/2,151/100) are fixed in [complete C476](Yin_C_G0476_v1.md#degree-six-return-core-fixed-scope).
Its full logarithmic measure is log(beta), and P(-1)=5.
The retained Pfaffian condition already rules out an exact rank-six realization with no multiplier.
The smallest possible cyclotomic padding by degree is Phi2=t+1, giving the exact degree-seven target

    F=(t+1)P=t^7-t^5-t^4-t^3-t^2+1.                  (2)

No claim that this padding is sufficient is made.
In fact:

**Pendant-forest obstruction.** Begin with any integral upper-unitriangular rank-four core, with no bound on its entries.
Add exactly three vertices whose induced graph is a forest, each connected component joined by exactly one unit-magnitude edge to one core vertex; all edges inside the added forest have unit magnitude, and there are no other new edges.
Different components may attach at the same core vertex.
Then the complete determinant is never F.
This includes every rooted forest shape on the three added vertices and every attachment choice.
The original core's induced order is retained; the new vertices may be ordered after it.
The argument also applies to any ordering of the added forest with that same core order, since each new edge is a bridge and only its squared weight enters the leaf elimination.

Unit edge signs on the added trees do not restrict this assertion: vertex sign changes at new vertices make those edges all negative without changing the core or determinant.
Thus all formulas below use squared edge magnitude one.

For use in the proof, write p=t^4+A t^3+B t^2+A t+1.
A principal three-vertex deletion has q_i=t^3+C_i t^2+C_i t+1; deleting two vertices gives r_ij=t^2+D_ij t+1.
If the remaining edge has weight w, D_ij=2-w^2.
A three-vertex core with weights u,v,w has coefficient3-u^2-v^2-w^2+uvw, independently of its vertex order.
Also the middle coefficient of any rank-four core obeys

    B = sum_(perfect matchings) product_(e in matching) w_e^2 (mod2). (3)

Indeed identity terms contribute6, edge-transposition terms contribute-2w_e^2, each triangle contributes twice its edge product, and a four-cycle contributes either only to degrees1 and3 or twice its product to degree2.
Only the two-disjoint-transposition terms remain modulo2.
This argument covers zeros, signed entries and every ordered complete core.

**One three-vertex path attached at an endpoint.** Section1 already rules it out: coefficient matching forces an impossible rank-three deletion C=0.
This was derived for a completely arbitrary core, not merely the earlier four-cycle support.

**A length-one and a length-two chain at distinct vertices i,j.** The coefficient of t^6 in(1) is A; its coefficients of t^5 and t^4 are

    2A+B-C_i-C_j,
    3A+2B-2C_i-3C_j+D_ij.

Matching(2) gives

    A=0, B=C_i+C_j-1, D_ij=C_j+1.                  (4)

Let k,l be the other two core vertices, and denote their edge by w, the i-k and i-l edges by u,v, the j-k and j-l edges by y,z, and the i-j edge by x.
Since C_j=3-u^2-v^2-w^2+uvw and D_ij=2-w^2, (4) implies

    u^2+v^2-uvw=2.                                (5)

Exactly one of u,v odd is impossible modulo2.
Both even is impossible modulo4.
Thus u,v are odd, and(5) modulo2 forces w even.
Formula(3) now gives B=y^2+z^2 modulo2.
But C_i=3-y^2-z^2-w^2+yzw is1+y^2+z^2 modulo2, whereas(4) gives B=C_i-w^2, hence B=C_i modulo2.
This contradiction excludes every integral choice, independently of the finite entry box or ordering.

**A length-one and a length-two chain at the same vertex.** With q=t^3+C t^2+C t+1, affine dependence on its one changed diagonal gives

    F_candidate=(t+1)C_2 p-t[C_2+(t+1)^2]q.

The degree6,5,4 coefficients are A, 2A+B-2C-1, 3A+2B-5C-1.
Matching(2) forces A=0, B=2C and2B=5C, hence C=0.
Section1's rank-three congruence again excludes it.

**Three single leaves at three distinct core vertices.** Write s=t+1.
Repeated diagonal expansion gives

    F_candidate=s[s^2 p-t s sum_i q_i+t^2 sum_(i<j) r_ij-t^3],  (6)

where the sums run over the three attachment vertices.
Matching the quotient in brackets to P gives

    A=0, B+1=sum_i C_i, sum_(i<j) D_ij=2.           (7)

Let l be the unattached core vertex.
Each r_ij consists of l and the one remaining attached vertex.
The last equality in(7) therefore says the three squared weights incident to l sum to4.
Exactly one of those integer weights is plus or minus2, and the other two are zero.
Thus the core is an arbitrary triangle on the three attachment vertices, with one weight-two leaf l.
Let x,y be the two triangle weights incident to the neighbor of l, and z the opposite triangle edge.
Direct determinant expansion gives

    A=-(x^2+y^2+z^2)+xyz,
    B=6-2(4+x^2+y^2+z^2)+2xyz+4z^2,
    sum_i C_i=1-(x^2+y^2+z^2).

Using A=0, equations(7) reduce to

    x^2+y^2+z^2=xyz,
    x^2+y^2+5z^2=2.                               (8)

The second equation forces z=0; the first then forces x=y=0, contradicting the second.
This excludes all integer cores, including disconnected ones and zeros.

**Three single leaves with repeated attachment sites.** If all three attach to one vertex, the full determinant is s^2(sp-3tq), hence divisible by s^2.
But F has precisely one s factor because P(-1)=5.
If two attach at i and one at a distinct j, it is

    s[s^2 p-t s(2q_i+q_j)+2t^2 r_ij].              (9)

Matching its quotient to P forces A=0 and B=2C_i+C_j.
Its middle coefficient then equals2D_ij, which is even, whereas the middle coefficient of P is-1.
This is impossible.

**One rooted three-vertex fork.** The only remaining shape is a new central vertex joined to the core and to two new leaves.
Its complete determinant is

    s[(s^2-2t)p-t s q]=s[(t^2+1)p-t s q].          (10)

The quotient's coefficient of t^5 forces A=0, after which its middle coefficient is-2C, again unable to equal-1.
A three-vertex path rooted at its middle is exactly this case.
These cases exhaust partitions3,2+1,1+1+1 and all root positions and repeated attachment sites, proving the assertion.

The conclusion concerns this entire unit pendant-forest architecture at exactly the minimal degree-one padding.
It does not exclude a general rank-seven matrix, weighted new edges of magnitude greater than one, a new cycle or multiple attachment edges, a larger core, or higher-degree cyclotomic padding.
In particular it is not an obstruction that survives every allowed stabilization, and it does not infer universal nonrealizability from a fixed architecture.

### 4. General independently variable chains have bounded full measure at a fixed core

The multiple-chain construction itself remains useful even though Section3 excludes the specified minimal target.
Fix any integral upper-unitriangular core V of rank d, and a set I of r distinct attachment vertices.
Give vertex i a unit chain of any length ell_i>=0.
For S contained in I, let p_S be the full determinant of the core with exactly S deleted, with empty determinant1.
Let C_n=1+t+...+t^n and C_(-1)=0.
Expansion in the distinct changed diagonals proves the exact generalization of(1):

    p_ell=sum_(S subset I) (-t)^|S| p_S
             product_(i in S) C_(ell_i-1)
             product_(i in I\S) C_(ell_i).          (11)

It is the determinant of the displayed integral unitriangular matrix of rank d+sum_i ell_i, not a selected factor.
An ell_i=0 contributes no vertex and terms with i in S vanish.
Clearing denominators gives the sparse expression

    (t-1)^r p_ell=sum_(S subset I) (-t)^|S| p_S
             product_(i in S)(t^ell_i-1)
             product_(i in I\S)(t^(ell_i+1)-1).     (12)

Both identities hold as polynomial identities, including t=1.
The divisor(t-1)^r is written explicitly and contributes zero Mahler measure; its removal merely recovers the actual determinant already defined in(11).

Define the positive integer

    A(V,I)=sum_(S subset I) ||p_S||_1,

where the norm is the sum of absolute coefficients of the complete polynomial p_S.
On the unit circle each binomial in(12) has modulus at most2, so Jensen's elementary integral bound gives

    0<=m(p_ell)<=r log2+log A(V,I).                 (13)

Zeros on the circle cause no gap: every nonzero polynomial has integrable logarithm, m(t-1)=0, and log of its modulus is bounded above by log of the stated constant almost everywhere.
Thus(13) holds for all independent lengths simultaneously; it is not a limit theorem, an empirical bound or a removal of neutral factors.
The determinant is always monic integral, so its Mahler measure is nonnegative.
Depending on the chosen core, every member may be cyclotomic; unbounded positive-measure irreducible degree is not asserted for an arbitrary core.
L203 remains one already proved one-chain subclass with that additional property.

Write V^- for the actual all-negative absolute core and p_S^- for its same principal deletions.
The new chain edges have the same squared magnitudes after absolute replacement, so the full absolute determinant p_ell^- obeys(11)--(13) with V replaced by V^-.
In particular

    m(p_ell p_ell^-)<=2r log2+log[A(V,I)A(V^-,I)].   (14)

The [D9 signed-cover identity](Yin_C_G0002_v1.md#signed-cover-identity) realizes exactly this product in rank2(d+sum ell_i).
If E=sum_(i<j)V_ij^2 and H=max(1,max_(i<j)|V_ij|), then the original and absolute matrices have entry height H and energy E+sum ell_i; the double cover has the same entry height and energy2E+2sum ell_i.
All diagonal entries remain one.
These are complete costs, including the absolute factor rather than only its largest expanding eigenvalue.

There is no unnamed cyclotomic multiplier in(11): p_ell itself is the complete realized polynomial of exact degree d+sum ell_i.
If a target factor P0 is isolated later, any identity p_ell=P0 C must retain C and prove it cyclotomic before calling it neutral; its degree would then be d+sum ell_i-deg(P0).
Section3 considered exactly C=Phi2 of degree1 and proved failure.
No cyclotomic classification for general V,I,ell is supplied by the upper bounds.
Their constants depend on the entire chosen core and r; they do not control m(p_ell^-)/m(P0) uniformly over small-measure targets or give unrestricted coefficient prescription.
