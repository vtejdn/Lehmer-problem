# Squarefree torsion translates in signed directions and transcendental horizontal centers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Global directions and local centers

Let \(F=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\) be the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
For every primitive signed pair \((k,l)\) with \(N=\max(|k|,|l|)\ge390\) and every pair of roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\), the Laurent polynomial \(F(\xi X^k,\eta X^l)\) has only simple zeros in the torus in characteristic zero.

In the [ordinary critical disks](Yin_C_G0345_v847.md#spacing), including the [horizontal disks](Yin_C_G0345_v846.md#centers) and [exceptional ordinary fibers](Yin_C_G0345_v849.md#centers), and in the [ramified cubic disks](Yin_C_G0345_v848.md#centers), take the centers arising from **zeros** of the prepared logarithmic critical value under the prime and chart hypotheses of the respective constructions.
Across all these primes, each such projective direction algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\) is rational and admits a primitive representative of height \(N\le389\).
In particular every deep horizontal center at \(p\ge390\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb Q\), including the centers of the [two computed deep primes](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#horizontal-depth) 8447 and 17167.
At 8447 at least one of these transcendental slopes lies in \(\mathbb Q_{8447}\).
The centers in the statement lie in finite extensions of their local fields by preparation.
The auxiliary slope used in a shallow cubic disk for spacing need not be a zero of a logarithmic critical value.

<a id="elimination"></a>
## The height of a point and its projected conjugates

Write \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\) and \(E=DF-(2k+l)F\).
The polynomial \(F\) is geometrically irreducible, with no boundary component, by the [critical-intersection calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#critical).
For every nonzero projective direction, including either axis and directions over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), its monomials have unequal weights since the four corners of the bidegree rectangle occur.
Thus \(F\) cannot divide \(DF\), which has the same bidegree bounds, and intersection with \(E\) has total length at most sixteen by the bidegrees \((4,2),(4,2)\).
A multiple section root gives such an intersection at \((U,V)=(\xi X^k,\eta X^l)\) by the chain rule.
An identically zero section would give infinitely many intersections since its parametrization is nonconstant.
For a rational direction the field \(K=\mathbb Q(U,V)\) has degree at most sixteen, since its embeddings give distinct points of the fiber.

Expanding \(A=U^4-7U^3+10U^2+7U+1\) gives \(\|F\|_1=53\), where the norm sums the absolute coefficients.
Each monomial of \(E\) gains the signed weight \((i-2)k+(j-1)l\), so \(\|E\|_1\le 3N\cdot53\).
Choose either projected coordinate \(Z=U,V\) and eliminate the other coordinate \(W\), putting \(d=2,4\) respectively.
Form the integer polynomial \(P_Z\) by the Sylvester determinant for \(F,E\) in \(W\), padding both to degree \(d\).
Over \(\mathbb Q(Z)\), \(F\) has full degree and shares no root with \(E\), so the padded determinant is nonzero even when \(E\) has smaller degree.
Indeed its homogeneous form then acquires only extra roots at infinity, where the leading coefficient of \(F\) is nonzero over this function field.
On \(|Z|=1\) each of the \(d\) shifted coefficient rows from either polynomial has Euclidean norm bounded by its respective coefficient sum.
Hadamard's bound and integration around the circle give
\[
                  M(P_Z)\le (3\cdot53^2 N)^d.                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(M\) is the multiplicative Mahler measure, with the integer content included.

Let \(z\) be the chosen projected coordinate at the point, let \(m_z\) be its primitive integral minimal polynomial, and put \(e=[K:\mathbb Q(z)]\).
The \(e\) embeddings above \(z\) give distinct finite \(W\)-coordinates.
Evaluation at each of these coordinates annihilates every Sylvester row specialized at \(Z=z\), since the rows are shifted multiples of the two polynomials.
These \(e\) evaluation vectors on polynomials of degree below \(2d\) are independent by Vandermonde, as \(e\le d\): the specialization of \(F\) is nonzero because a zero specialization would give a line component.
The matrix therefore loses rank at least \(e\) there.
Constant invertible row operations over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) give \(e\) rows divisible by \(Z-z\), so the determinant vanishes to order at least \(e\).
Conjugation and Gauss's lemma give \(m_z^e\mid P_Z\) in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\).
The remaining integral factor has measure at least one, hence
\[
             [K:\mathbb Q]h(z)=e\log M(m_z)\le\log M(P_Z),              \tag{2}
\]
where \(h\) denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## A uniform bound independent of the translating torsion

At a tangency to a torsion translate, \(U^l/V^k\) is torsion.
Choose integers \(a,b\) with \(ak+bl=1\) and put \(\gamma=U^a V^b\in K^\times\).
Then \(U/\gamma^k=(U^l/V^k)^b\) and \(V/\gamma^l=(U^l/V^k)^{-a}\) are roots of unity.
The [exclusion of torsion points](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion) makes \(\gamma\) nontorsion.
Its primitive integral minimal polynomial has measure greater than one by Kronecker's theorem, also for degree one and nonintegral inputs.
Its degree is at most sixteen, so the [Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives \(M(\gamma)\ge\rho>7/6\), where \(\rho\) is Lehmer's number.
Indeed the Lehmer polynomial multiplied by \(6^{10}\) at \(7/6\) has value \(-13481789\) and its unique exterior root is \(\rho\).
Choose \(z=U\) when \(|k|=N\) and \(z=V\) otherwise.
In either case \(h(z)=Nh(\gamma)\), giving by (1) and (2)
\[
            (7/6)^N < M(\gamma)^N \le (3\cdot53^2 N)^4.                \tag{3}
\]
At \(N=390\) the reverse strict inequality holds by integer exponentiation, \(7^{390}>6^{390}(3\cdot53^2\cdot390)^4\).
The ratio \((7/6)^N/N^4\) increases for all integers \(N\ge390\) since \(7\cdot390^4>6\cdot391^4\).
This proves the asserted uniform squarefreeness.

<a id="logarithms"></a>
## Algebraic slopes and the logarithms of critical coordinates

In the ordinary chart \(r=k/l\) a deep center is a zero of \(H(r)=\log(U/[u])-r\log(V/[v])\), at a point of \(F=rUF_U+VF_V=0\).
Indeed the analytic inverse giving the point converges for the integral roots of the prepared polynomial scaled into \(v_p(r-r_0)\ge1\), also over ramified extensions.
The two coordinates divided by their Teichmüller lifts differ from one by elements of valuation at least one by the integral coordinate expansions.
Interchanging coordinates gives the other ordinary chart.
In the cubic chart each center has \(s=g(b)\) at a zero \(b\) of the [prepared critical value](Yin_C_G0345_v848.md#centers), so \((U,V)=(b,f(b))\) satisfies \(F=UF_U+sVF_V=0\) and \(\log(V/[v])=s\log(U/[u])\).
Here \(v_p(b-\beta)\ge1/2\), and both normalized coordinates differ from one by elements of valuation at least \(1/2\) by integrality at the unramified lift.
The logarithms converge there, including on ramified cubic centers.

If the slope is algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\), the finite critical intersection over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) puts both coordinates in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) in the chosen embedding into \(\mathbb C_p\).
Their Teichmüller lifts are roots of unity, so the two principal units are algebraic.
Their logarithm vector is nonzero since simultaneous vanishing would make both coordinates torsion.
In either chart the logarithm line is defined over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), so the [torus application of the p-adic analytic subgroup theorem](../audits/literature/FP15/Yin_U001.md#fp15-u001) gives a rational line.
Writing the projective direction as \([k:l]\) in primitive integers, we have \(l\log(U/[u])-k\log(V/[v])=0\).
The logarithm kernel on principal units is torsion, hence \(U^l/V^k\) is torsion.
The Bézout construction in the preceding section now puts the critical point on a torsion translate in this direction, so \(N\le389\).
This provides one finite set of possible algebraic directions independent of the prime and chart.

On a horizontal disk \(r=k/l\) has \(v_p(r)\ge1\), so a nonzero rational center has \(p\mid k\) and \(N\ge p\).
At \(r=0\) the critical point has \(U=\alpha\) a root of \(D(U)=U^4-4AA^*\), and \(H(0)=0\) would make \(\alpha\) torsion.
All eight roots of \(D\) are real of modulus different from one by the [horizontal collision formula](Yin_C_G0345_v844.md#collision).
This excludes zero and proves the stated transcendence.
At 8447 the [compatible first residue orbit](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#horizontal-depth) gives primitive directions in the same disk with unbounded root-distance valuation.
By the horizontal two-center formula a subsequence of their rational slopes tends to one fixed center in the local metric, and completeness of \(\mathbb Q_{8447}\) puts that center in \(\mathbb Q_{8447}\).
The algebraic classification yields no upper bound for how closely the rational directions can approach a transcendental center.
