# Limits on complete dyadic separation with a simple residue order

<a id="statement"></a>
## Excluding a single orbit of large prime order

Let \(F\) be a [genuine integral trace-pole minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) of degree \(d\), and let \(A\) be the multiplicative order of \(Z\) modulo \((2,F)\).
If \(\bar F\) is irreducible and \(d\) tends to infinity, the least prime divisor of \(A\) is \(d^{o(1)}\).
In particular no unbounded-degree family can have irreducible binary reduction of prime order or of prime-power order with bounded exponent.

There is also a restriction when \(A\) is prime and the binary polynomial is allowed to split.
Write \(t\) for the number of its nontrivial irreducible factors, excluding \(Z-1\).
For each fixed \(0<\eta\le 1/4\) and all sufficiently large \(d\), it satisfies
\[
             t>\exp\!\left(\frac{c_\eta\log d}{\log^2(2q+1)}\right) ,
\]
with a suitably chosen effective positive constant.
These restrictions apply to the Frobenius orbits of distinct roots; neither supplies a lower bound of two roots with the same residue.

<a id="orders"></a>
## Combining the period estimate with cyclotomic factor degrees

Put \(P_d=(\log(3d))^3\log\log(9d)\).
The [degree cap for the binary binomial](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#statement) gives \(A\ll dP_d\).
The [period repulsion](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) for \(b=1\) gives \(|A-nd|>d^{1-\eta}\) for every integer \(n\).
When \(\bar F\) is irreducible of degree \(d>1\), any root \(\alpha\) has odd order \(A>1\), and its first return under repeated squaring has length \(d\).
Hence \(d=\operatorname{ord}_A(2)\) divides \(\varphi(A)\), and \(A\ge d+1\).
Write \(r\) for the least prime divisor of \(A\).
The product formula for Euler's totient gives
\[
 d^{1-\eta}<A-\varphi(A)\le A\sum_{\ell\mid A,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{-1}
 \le A(\log_2 A)/r .
\]
Thus \(r\ll d^\eta P_d\log(3d)\) for every fixed \(\eta\) in the specified range and sufficiently large \(d\) depending on \(\eta\).
This implies the subpower assertion and rules out \(A=r^a\) with bounded \(a\), since \(r\ge(d+1)^{1/a}\).

For prime \(A\) and growing \(d\), the prime is odd since \(\bar F\mid Z^A-1\) forces \(d\le A\).
The latter polynomial has simple roots in characteristic two.
The nonidentity \(A\)-th roots all have Frobenius orbit length \(f=\operatorname{ord}_A(2)\).
Consequently \(d=tf+\delta\) with \(\delta\in\{0,1\}\), and \(I=(A-1)/f\) is an integer at least \(t\).
This gives
\[
             |tA-Id|=|t-I\delta|\le I\ll tP_d ,
\]
using \(d-\delta=tf\) in the last bound.
Reduce the positive constant in the displayed cutoff so its value is at most \(d^{1/8}\) and it is within the range of period repulsion.
If \(t\) were within the cutoff, taking \(b=t,\ n=I\) in that estimate would give \(|tA-Id|>d^{1-\eta}\), contrary to \(tP_d\le d^{1/8}P_d=o(d^{1-\eta})\).
In particular the low-degree tests at the prime two and the real pin constrain a completely separated reduction even when they do not force a collision.
