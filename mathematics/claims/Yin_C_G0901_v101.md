# The bounded spectrum of one small unit and of arbitrarily many large strict degree records

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which multiplicative lines can have bounded total measure

Write \(m=\log M\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure and \(V_x\) for the rational multiplicative span of all conjugates modulo torsion.
For any collection \(S\) of units, \(D(S)\) consists of torsion twists of radicals of finite monomials in their conjugates, with integer exponents of either sign.
Two units match if some of their conjugates have nonzero powers equal up to torsion.
The compressed unit attached to an input is the product over the conjugates in its class modulo roots of unity.
Use its degree for the compressed degree.

Fix an arbitrary ceiling \(T>0\).
The following statements concern all actual units below this ceiling, with no restriction on their degrees or radical denominators.

1. For all sufficiently small \(m(w)>0\), form the [chain of successively compressed block products](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent) \(z_1,\ldots,z_s\).
   Every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) with \(m(x)\le T\) matches exactly one \(z_i\), and \(V_x=V_{z_i}\).
   In particular only the terminal sums \(W_i\oplus\cdots\oplus W_s\) in that decomposition can occur at bounded measure, although every nonempty subset of the summands is realized somewhere in the divisible group.
   Choose a primitive unit \(\rho_i\) on the line of \(z_i\) modulo torsion in \(\mathbb Q(z_i)\).
   The entire positive spectrum up to \(T\) is
   \[
           (0,T]\ \cap\ \bigcup_{i=1}^s m(\rho_i)\mathbb Z_{>0}.          \tag{1}
   \]
   The smallness threshold depends only on \(T\).

2. Fix also \(M_0>0\).
   Let \(S\) be any family of pairwise unmatched nontorsion units of measures \(m(a)\le M_0\), with each \(V_a\) rationally irreducible.
   Assume every compressed degree exceeds a threshold depending only on \(M_0,T\).
   Then every nontorsion \(x\in D(S)\) with \(m(x)\le T\) matches exactly one input, even for infinitely many inputs with no upper bound on their degrees.
   Its class is supported on that input alone in the direct sum of the \(V_a\).
   The spectrum is (1) with \(i\) ranging over \(S\), using the primitive line unit of each compressed input.
   An empty family gives an empty positive spectrum.

In particular take any set of reciprocal strict degree records of sufficiently large distinct degrees and bounded measure.
Here a record first attains the running minimum of positive integer-polynomial measures at its own degree.
In the second statement the primitive measures are the original record measures, and every bounded positive value in the combined group is an integer multiple of one record value.
Every finite monomial or radical with nonzero class components from two or more such records exceeds \(T\).
Thus the infimum of the measures of such mixtures tends to infinity as the minimum degree of the records being combined tends to infinity, at a fixed upper bound on their individual measures.
The conclusions allow small measures on new input lines as their fields vary.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Compression, kernel fields and the comparison of two unmatched inputs

For a rational multiplicative module \(V\) with finite Galois action, write \(L(V)\) for the fixed field of the kernel of the absolute Galois action on it.
For any algebraic unit \(a\), the [product in its torsion class](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) gives a unit \(\gamma_a\) of the same total measure and the same rational conjugate span, and \(\gamma_a\) is a positive power of \(a\) up to torsion.
The conjugates of \(\gamma_a\) have distinct classes.
Thus fixing \(V_a\) pointwise is equivalent to fixing each actual conjugate of \(\gamma_a\), and \(L(V_a)\) is its rational splitting field.
More particularly the stabilizer of \([a]\) fixes the actual product \(\gamma_a\), and an automorphism fixing \(\gamma_a\) must fix \([a]\).
So its point stabilizer has fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\gamma_a)\), with the chosen embedding.

We use the following consequence of the [unmatched-field theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement).
Two bounded-measure inputs with equal kernel fields must match as soon as the larger of their compressed degrees is sufficiently large in terms of the common measure bound.
Indeed work with their compressed representatives, and choose the representative of larger degree as target, breaking a tie arbitrarily.
If they are unmatched, the rational splitting field of the other representative is one of the fields in the unmatched base through the target degree.
That field would already contain the target, in contradiction with the theorem, which keeps a nontrivial alternating action after adjoining that entire base.
It is important here that the bound is on the original total measures, which compression preserves.

If \(x\) matches a compressed unit \(z\), choose the conjugate of \(z\) on whose rational line \([x]\) lies.
The stabilizers of these nonzero proportional vectors agree, so the compressed unit \(\gamma_x\) lies in the actual root field of this conjugate.
Its class there is an integer multiple of the primitive unit class on the line, by [saturation in the root field](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#primitive).
Every representative of a nonzero integer multiple of this primitive class in the field has that full degree: an automorphism fixing it fixes its class, hence fixes the given compressed conjugate \(z\).
It follows from the degree-times-height formula that
\[
         m(x)=m(\gamma_x)=j\,m(\rho),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.           \tag{2}
\]
Conversely \(\rho\) belongs to \(D(z)\), and each \(\rho^j\) attains \(j\,m(\rho)\).

<a id="chain"></a>
## The leading summand detects the entire compressed splitting field

At each step of the small-measure chain, \(W_i\) is faithful for the splitting group of the compressed \(z_i\).
In the independent inversion alternative, \(W_i\) is the full signed permutation representation on inverse-pair coordinates.
An element acting as the identity therefore fixes each root.
In the oriented case it is the direct sum of the coordinate-sum-zero spaces of the blocks, with ordinary permutations and a uniform sign on each transported block.
An element acting identically must preserve every block.
On a fixed block of at least three labels, no permutation is minus the identity on its sum-zero space: it would have to interchange every distinct pair \(j,k\), by applying it to \(e_j-e_k\).
The identity action with positive sign requires the permutation itself to be the identity, by those same differences.
Thus all oriented roots are fixed.
The [signed alternatives on the actual roots](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) and the [block representation in the chain](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent) give
\[
                       L(W_i)=L(V_{z_i}).                                \tag{3}
\]

For nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), \(V_x\) is the sum of a nonempty subset of the distinct irreducible \(W_i\).
Let \(i\) be its first index, so \(W_i\subseteq V_x\subseteq V_{z_i}\).
These inclusions and (3) imply \(L(V_x)=L(V_{z_i})\).
Choose \(m(w)\) small enough that the chain exists, \(m(w)\le1\), and *all* its degrees exceed the threshold in the field comparison at the common bound \(\max(1,T)\).
The last condition is uniform by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness, since \(0<m(z_i)\le m(w)\) at every step.
If \(m(x)\le T\), the comparison forces \(x\) to match \(z_i\), hence \(V_x=V_{z_i}\).
Matching to two different \(z_i\) is impossible because their compressed degrees are distinct and matching identifies the point stabilizers up to conjugation.
Equation (2) gives (1), including attainment.

<a id="families"></a>
## Comparing the largest compressed degree in an arbitrary finite support

Compress every family member first.
This changes neither its measure nor its rational span or divisible group.
By the two-input comparison, distinct unmatched family members have distinct kernel fields.
Their irreducible absolute Galois modules are therefore pairwise nonisomorphic, since isomorphic modules have the same kernel.
In every finite subset these irreducible subspaces form a direct sum, by semisimplicity for the action factoring through one finite Galois group.
If the class of \(x\) belongs to that sum, \(V_x\) is precisely the direct sum of the summands with nonzero projection of \([x]\).
This follows because there is only one summand of each simple type.
In particular \(L(V_x)\) is the compositum of the kernel fields of its active inputs, by intersecting the action kernels.
This field equality is for compressed splitting fields and does not presume \(x\) itself lies in the finite compositum.

Suppose \(0<m(x)\le T\) and that \(x\) matches none of these active inputs.
Use only this nonempty finite support, and consider the largest compressed degree among its members and \(x\).
This degree exceeds the threshold in v87 at the common bound \(\max(M_0,T)\).
If \(x\) has largest degree, the unmatched base for its compressed representative contains the splitting fields of every active compressed input.
Their compositum contains \(\gamma_x\), which is impossible by v87.
If a family member has largest degree, the unmatched base for that member contains the splitting field of \(\gamma_x\).
The field equality just proved puts the target input in it, giving the same contradiction.
Either argument is valid at equal maximal degrees.
Thus \(x\) matches one active input, and equality of their rational spans leaves just that summand.
Distinct input orbits of lines cannot both match \(x\), and (2) gives all stated values and their realization.

For reciprocal strict records of large bounded measure degree the [absolute irreducibility theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) supplies rational irreducibility.
Strict first attainment forces trivial torsion-class fibers, since compression would otherwise attain the same measure at a smaller degree.
Matching different records is excluded by their distinct compressed degrees.
If the saturation of their coordinate line in the root-field units were nonprimitive, (2) would give a smaller positive measure at that same degree, contrary to the defining minimum.
The second assertion now applies directly, and its uniformity for every fixed \(T\) gives the divergence statement.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## Consequence for the unique nonreal exterior pair

In the [large compressed one-pair setting](Yin_C_G0901_v94.md#statement), at bounded \(m(\alpha)\), \(V_\alpha\) is irreducible in both signed alternatives.
In the independent case this is the signed representation separated by the even inversions.
In the oriented case every block product is torsion by the one exterior pair and its opposite logs, so the whole span is the irreducible sum of blockwise sum-zero spaces. [Block products and relations](Yin_C_G0901_v94.md#orbit)
Apply the family statement to the one input with the ceiling also at least its original measure.
Its smallest positive measure in the whole divisible group is exactly that of the primitive coordinate \(\rho\), and every bounded positive value is an integer multiple of \(m(\rho)\) for sufficiently large input degree in terms of the ceiling and original bound.
In particular the formal exceptional case \(k=b\) in the [oriented saturation calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v99.md#statement) cannot occur in sufficiently large degree at fixed measure bound.
Indeed it would produce the strictly smaller value \((b-1)m(\rho)/b\).
This extra degree threshold comes from comparing unmatched fields; the local integer and group calculations in that saturation result also apply with their own thresholds.
