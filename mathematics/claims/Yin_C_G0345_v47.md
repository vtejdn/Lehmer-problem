# A uniform Salem gap for the difference of two adjacent Chebyshev traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## The family with coefficients two and minus one

Write \(D_j(u+u^{-1})=u^j+u^{-j}\).
There is an \(\epsilon>0\) such that every Salem number \(\sigma\) whose monic trace minimal polynomial of degree \(k+1\) is strictly interlaced by \(C=2D_k-D_{k-1}\), \(k\ge2\), satisfies \(\sigma\ge1+\epsilon\).
Together with the [positive-sign gap](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#statement), this gives one positive lower bound on \(\sigma-1\) for both signs.

<a id="rows"></a>
## Integral Fourier coefficients at every multiple of the first varying index

Suppose there is a sequence \(\sigma_i\to1\) of the stated kind.
Write \(S_i\) for the trace polynomial, \(m=k+1\), \(P_i(u)=u^m S_i(u+u^{-1})\), and \(T=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\).
The positive residues \(c_j=C(x_j)/S_i'(x_j)\) at the ordered roots \(x_1<\cdots<x_m=T\) sum to two by the expansion of \(C/S_i\) at infinity.
Write \(t=c_m\) and \(\lambda_i\) for the positive symmetric circle measure lifting the weights \(c_j\) at the interior trace roots \(x_j=u+u^{-1}\), \(j<m\).
Let \(y_1<\cdots<y_{m-1}\) be the roots of \(C\).
They all belong to \((-2,2)\), since \(C(2\cos(j\pi/k))=(-1)^j(4-2\cos(j\pi/k))\) for \(0\le j\le k\).
Strict interlacing gives \(y_{j-1}<x_j\) for \(2\le j<m\), so
\[
             t=2\prod_{j<m}\frac{T-y_j}{T-x_j}
               \ge\frac{2(T-y_{m-1})}{T-x_1}
               \ge\frac{2(T-2)}{T+2}.
\]

Take the [first varying index](Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#statement) \(n=n_i\to\infty\) of \(P_i\) relative to \(1-u\), and extract a subsequence with its coefficient \(p=\pm1\) fixed.
The [moving moment calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#moving) gives \(t\sigma_i^n=O(1)\) and the weak limiting marginal of \(\lambda_i\).
For consistency with its reflected Fourier convention, let \(\Phi\) be a weak limit of twice \(\lambda_i\) under the map to the two circles
\[
                             z=u^{-1},\qquad w=-p u^n .
\]
Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(dm\) for Haar probability on the first circle, and \(B_j(z)\,dm(z)\) for the projection of \(w^j d\Phi\) to that circle.
These complex measures have densities because their variations are dominated by the marginal.
The same moving moment calculation, including its polynomial \(U=-p(1+z)^2(2-z)\) for the reflected first coordinate, gives
\[
                B_0=(x+2)(3-x),\qquad B_1=(x+2)z(2-z).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

We show that every \(B_j\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
The bounds just established and \(T-2=(\sigma_i-1)^2/\sigma_i\) give
\[
      t\sigma_i^{jn}=(t\sigma_i^n)^j t^{1-j}
                 = O_j\big((\sigma_i-1)^{-2(j-1)}\big)\qquad(j\ge1).
\]
The trace moments \(g_{i,h}=\sum_l c_lD_h(x_l)\) for \(h\ge1\) are integers by the [moment identity](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) applied with this \(C\).
Fix \(j\ge1\), \(M=2j\), and any integer \(r\), and use the Fourier convention \(\widehat f(r)=\int z^{-r} f(z)\,dm\).
The coefficient \(\widehat{(1-z)^MB_j}(r)\) is the limit of the integer sums
\[
         (-p)^j\sum_{l=0}^M(-1)^l\binom Ml g_{i,jn+r-l}.
\]
Indeed all indices are positive eventually, the neutral part is precisely the coefficient test for the weak limit, and the exterior part up to its sign is
\[
    t\sigma_i^{jn+r}(1-\sigma_i^{-1})^M+
    t\sigma_i^{-jn-r}(1-\sigma_i)^M=o(1).
\]
The limiting coefficients are integers and tend to zero as \(|r|\to\infty\) by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, since \(B_j\) is bounded by \(B_0\).
Thus \((1-z)^M B_j\) agrees almost everywhere with an integer Laurent polynomial.
The bound by \(|1-z|^M B_0\) forces this polynomial to vanish to order at least \(M\) at one.
Division by \((1-z)^M\) within \(\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\) proves the assertion.
Negative \(j\) follow by conjugation, and \(B_0\) is already given in (1).

<a id="graphs"></a>
## A positive measure on two circles with two possible values of its second coordinate

Set \(d\Lambda=|1-w|^2d\Phi\).
Its \(j\)-th projected density is \(L_j=2B_j-B_{j-1}-B_{j+1}\).
By (1) its marginal is
\[
                  H=L_0=(x+2)(2-x)^2=|(1+z)(1-z)^2|^2 .
\]
For every \(j\), \(L_j\) is an integer Laurent polynomial bounded in modulus by \(H\), first almost everywhere and hence everywhere.
The zeros at \(-1,1\) force divisibility of \(L_j\) by \(H\) in the integral Laurent ring, since \(H\) is a monomial unit times \((1+z)^2(1-z)^4\).
The quotient has circle modulus at most one.
Parseval gives a squared sum of its integer coefficients at most one, so the quotient is zero or a signed monomial.

Condition the measure \(\Lambda\) over \(z\) using the positive marginal \(H\,dm\).
Outside a single null set the conditional \(j\)-th moments are \(L_j/H\) for all integers \(j\).
An index with nonzero quotient has moment of modulus one, so \(w^j\) is constant on almost every such fiber under the conditional probability.
The set of these indices is a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\), by multiplication and conjugation of the resulting constants.
If the subgroup is zero, all conditional laws are Haar by Fourier uniqueness.
That would make recovery of the finite measure \(\Phi\) away from \(w=1\) impossible, since \(\int |1-w|^{-2}dm(w)=\infty\).
Otherwise write the subgroup as \(d\mathbb Z\), \(d\ge1\), and its moment at \(d\) as \(W(z)=\eta z^a\), \(\eta=\pm1\), \(a\in\mathbb Z\).
The conditional law is uniform on the \(d\) roots of \(w^d=W(z)\) by comparison of all its Fourier coefficients.
We have \(W\ne1\) almost everywhere because \(\Lambda\) gives no mass to \(w=1\).

In \(\Phi\) the fiber at \(w=1\) contributes nothing to \(B_0-B_1\).
On its complement, averaging \((1-w)/|1-w|^2=(1-\overline w)^{-1}\) in the conditional law of \(\Lambda\) gives
\[
           \frac1{1-\overline W}
              =\frac{B_0-B_1}{H}
              =\frac{3-x-z(2-z)}{(2-x)^2}
              =\frac{z}{z-1}.
\]
The root average on the left follows by expanding \((1-\overline W)/(1-\overline w)\) as a geometric sum of length \(d\).
Thus \(W=z\).
The conditional average of \(|1-w|^{-2}\) is \(d/|1-z|^2\), since the squared modulus of that geometric sum averages to \(d\) by orthogonality of the \(d\) roots.
The projection of the part of \(\Phi\) off \(w=1\) therefore has density \(d(4-x^2)\).
The remaining density \((x+2)(3-x-d(2-x))\) must be nonnegative, and sending \(x\downarrow-2\) after division by \(x+2\) gives \(d\le5/4\).
We obtain the full law
\[
     d\Phi=(x+2)\,dm(z)\,\delta_1(dw)+(4-x^2)\,dm(z)\,\delta_z(dw).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

<a id="trace"></a>
## Returning the two equalities to a positive integer trace

In the actual circle coordinate \(u\) the two equalities in (2) read \(u^n=-p\) or \(u^{n+1}=-p\).
Put \(Q_i(u)=(u^n+p)(u^{n+1}+p)\) and define the integer polynomial \(A_i\) by
\[
             A_i(u+u^{-1})=Q_i(u)Q_i(u^{-1}).
\]
It is integral because every symmetric integral Laurent polynomial is an integer combination of \(1,D_1,D_2,\ldots\).
The number
\[
                 J_i=\sum_l c_l(2-x_l)^4 A_i(x_l)^2
\]
is a positive integer.
For any integer polynomial \(f\), \(\sum_l c_l f(x_l)\) is the coefficient of degree \(m-1\) in the integral remainder of \(C f\) modulo the monic \(S_i\), by Lagrange interpolation.
Here all summands are nonnegative and the term at \(T>2\) is positive, since the two binomials are nonzero at \(\sigma_i\) and \(\sigma_i^{-1}\).

On the circle the neutral summand integrates \((2-x)^4|Q_i(u)|^4\) against \(\lambda_i\).
Under \(z=u^{-1},\,w=-p u^n\) this is the fixed continuous test \((2-x)^4 |(1-w)(1-w/z)|^4\), which vanishes on both parts of (2).
The neutral contribution tends to zero by the weak convergence defining \(\Phi\).
The exterior contribution is at most a constant times
\[
             t(T-2)^4\sigma_i^{4n+2}
                    =O\big((\sigma_i-1)^8(\sigma_i-1)^{-6}\big)=o(1)
\]
by the bound for \(j=4\), since \(Q_i(\sigma_i^{-1})\) stays bounded.
Thus \(J_i\to0\), in contradiction with \(J_i\ge1\).
