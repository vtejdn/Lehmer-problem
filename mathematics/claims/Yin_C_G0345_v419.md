# Growing numbers of distinct repeated factors on one prescribed signed row

<a id="statement"></a>
## The same Lehmer limit with many different factor degrees

Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
For each prime \(p\) put \(a=\max(2,\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor)\), \(b_0=\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), \(b=b_0+1\), and let \(r=r(p)\) be a positive integer.
For \(0\ne A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\), set \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\) and \(G_A(X)=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^p)\).
A primitive irreducible polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with positive leading coefficient qualifies for this row if \(P^a\mid G_A\) and a root \(\alpha\) is nonzero, nontorsion and satisfies \(A(\alpha^p)\ne0\).
These conditions are invariant under conjugacy.
Let \(H_{p,r}\) be the minimum of \(C_{p,r}=2^{\lfloor apr/2\rfloor}\) and the coefficient one-norms at most \(C_{p,r}\) of rows with at least \(r\) distinct qualifying factors.
Let \(H^{\mathrm{deg}}_{p,r}\) impose the additional requirement of \(r\) pairwise distinct degrees.
Both tests use the same prescribed sign, and each irreducible polynomial contributes only once regardless of its number of conjugates.

For either choice \(H\), writing \(\lambda\) for the infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures of nonzero integer polynomials, one has at every prime
\[
         T_{p,r}:=\frac{\log((2pb+1)H^2)}{(a-1)pr}\ \ge\ \lambda .
\]
If \(r\to\infty\) and \(r=o(p^{3/8})\) along the primes, then
\[
              \lim_{p\to\infty} T_{p,r}
                 =\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{2\log H}{apr}=\lambda.          \tag{1}
\]
Examples include \(r=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2p)/4\rfloor)\) and \(r=\max(1,\lfloor p^{3/8}/\log(2p)\rfloor)\).
For the distinct-degree test, the exponent \(3/8\) is the largest possible in power order because every actual qualifying row satisfies
\[
                   (a-1)\,r(r+1)/2\le2b.                                \tag{2}
\]
At sufficiently large primes either test has a minimizing row below its fallback.
From any such row one can recover \(r\) distinct primitive factors, with distinct degrees for the second test.
For any qualifying selection of size \(r\) their mean logarithmic measure lies in \([\lambda,T_{p,r}]\).
In particular, if \(\lambda=0\), one row at every sufficiently large prime supplies \(r(1-o(1))\) distinct factors whose measures tend uniformly to zero.
Different factors may have the same measure.

<a id="product"></a>
## A product of powered minima divides the derivative polynomial

Put \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon A_b^*\) and \(W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV\).
The [single-row elimination identity](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) gives \(0\ne W\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), \(\deg W\le2b\) and \(\|W\|_1\le(2pb+1)\|A\|_1^2\).
For a qualifying root \(\alpha\) and its power \(z=\alpha^p\) one has \(V(z)\ne0\), \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_zW=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha G_A-1\).
Write \(P,Q\) for the primitive minima of \(\alpha,z\) respectively.
Field recovery and the [absolute height normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) give \(\deg Q=\deg P\) and \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\) also for nonmonic primitive minima.
If two powered nodes were conjugate, applying the same rational recovery map to both would give conjugate original roots.
Consequently distinct \(P_i\) give distinct primitive \(Q_i\), and Gauss's lemma gives the integral divisibility
\[
     \prod_{i=1}^r Q_i^{a-1}\mid W,\qquad
     (a-1)p\sum_{i=1}^r m(P_i)\le 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2pb+1).              \tag{3}
\]
Every \(m(P_i)\) is positive by nontorsion and thus at least \(\lambda\).
If the minimum is the fallback, \(2\lfloor apr/2\rfloor\ge(a-1)pr\) and \(\lambda\le\log2\) give the asserted pointwise bound.
The degree sum in (3) is at most \(2b/(a-1)\), which gives (2) for \(r\) distinct positive degrees.

For each row in the finite norm box factor just \(W\), keeping its primitive noncyclotomic factors other than \(Y\) of multiplicity at least \(a-1\).
For each such \(Q\), in the field with \(z=Y\bmod Q\), test \(V(z)\ne0\) and \((-U(z))^p=z V(z)^p\).
The exact order identity makes these tests necessary and sufficient.
Successful factors recover distinct original minima by \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), since conjugate original roots likewise have conjugate powers.
One can therefore count successful factors or their distinct degrees using only factorizations through degree \(2b\).
No seed list is required for either test.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Fixed equal-measure substitutions and a simultaneous jet count

Fix a reciprocal unit minimum \(P_0\) of a nontorsion algebraic number, of degree \(e\) and positive measure \(h\).
For this choice only, the [gcd classification of power substitutions](Yin_C_G0345_v390.md#statement) gives a positive integer \(L_0\) such that \(P_0(X^n)\) is irreducible whenever \((n,L_0)=1\).
Thus for any \(N\ge1\) the \(N\) polynomials \(P_i=P_0(X^{1+iL_0})\), \(1\le i\le N\), have pairwise distinct degrees and total degree \(D=O_{P_0}(N^2)\).
They are monic reciprocal unit minima of the same measure \(h\), since taking all roots of a power substitution preserves Mahler measure.
Below \(N=O(r)\), so each individual degree is \(O_{P_0}(r)<p-1\) eventually.
The [degree-preserving power calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power) gives distinct powered conjugates for each seed.
Their monic powered minima \(Q_i\) have their respective original degrees and measure \(ph\), hence are pairwise distinct.

Choose the number of initial seeds and contact order before any choice of coefficients.
Set
\[
 \chi=a^{-1}+r^2p^{-3/4},\qquad \eta=\chi^{1/3},\qquad
 N=\lceil(1+3\eta)r\rceil,\qquad t=a+\lceil\eta a\rceil .
\]
Then \(\eta\to0,\ N/r\to1,\ t/a\to1\), and \(D=O_{P_0}(r^2)\).
Let \(n=2(b_0+1)\) be the number of coefficient positions in the two classes \(0,1\bmod p\), and let \(L=p(b_0+1)\) bound their exponents.
We have \(Dt/n=O_{P_0}(r^2p^{-3/4})=o(1)\).

Apply the [divided derivative box argument](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) in the direct product of the \(N\) seed fields, using one chosen root of each seed and all its derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,t-1\).
For a coefficient box \([0,K]^n\), the number of possible tuples is at most
\[
          (6nK)^{Dt}(1+L)^{Dt(t-1)/2}\exp(tLNh).
\]
Indeed each individual field gives the bound with its own degree and measure, and multiplying the counts is an upper bound even if some fields intersect.
A difference of coefficient vectors gives a nonzero
\(F_0=U_0(X^p)+X V_0(X^p)\) divisible by all \(P_i^t\), with degrees of the rows at most \(b_0\) and
\[
   \log\|F_0\|_1\le
   \frac{tLNh+Dt\log(6n)+Dt(t-1)\log(1+L)/2}{n-Dt}+\log(2n).          \tag{4}
\]
Indeed take \(K\) equal to one plus the integer part of the exponential of the fraction, exactly as for one field.
The one-norm costs at most \(nK\).
Since \(L/n=p/2\), (4) gives
\[
     \frac{2\log\|F_0\|_1}{Nph}
                \le t+O_{P_0}(tDt/n)+o_{P_0}(1).                       \tag{5}
\]
The two terms free of \(h\) in the fraction are \(O((Dt/n)(1+t)\log p)\) once \(Dt/n\le1/2\).
Together with \(\log(2n)\), they are \(o(Nph)\) because \(P_0\) is fixed.
Both rows must occur, since a single nonzero row would be divisible before substitution by \(\prod_iQ_i^t\), and would have one-norm at least \(\exp(tNph)\), contrary to (5).

<a id="retention"></a>
## Retaining enough primitive factors after division and sign selection

Let \(J\) be the primitive integral polynomial gcd of the two nonzero rows, and put \(c_i=\operatorname{ord}_{z_i}J\) for \(z_i=\alpha_i^p,\ P_i(\alpha_i)=0\).
Set \(\widetilde U=U_0/J,\ \widetilde V=V_0/J\).
For the unsigned derivative polynomial \(W_0=pY(U_0'V_0-U_0V_0')-U_0V_0\) the least product term has multiplier \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0U_0-\operatorname{ord}_0V_0)-1\ne0\), so \(W_0\ne0\).
Factoring the gcd in both rows gives \(W_0=J^2\widetilde W\), with the same derivative expression on the quotients.
Removing \(J(X^p)\) loses exactly \(c_i\) orders at the nonzero \(\alpha_i\).
The [derivative identity](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#construction) therefore gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{z_i}\widetilde W\ge\max(0,t-c_i-1)\), and
\[
       \prod_i Q_i^{\,t+c_i-1}\mid W_0,\qquad
       \frac1N\sum_i c_i\le1+O_{P_0}(tDt/n)+o_{P_0}(1)=O_{P_0}(a\chi). \tag{6}
\]
For the inequality use (5), \(m(Q_i)=ph\), and \(\|W_0\|_1\le(2pb_0+1)\|F_0\|_1^2\).
At most \(O_{P_0}(\eta^2N)\) indices have \(c_i>t-a\), since \(t-a\ge\eta a\) and \(\chi=\eta^3\).
This is \(o(\eta N)\), whereas \(N-r\ge3\eta r\), so at least \(r\) seeds retain contact order at least \(a\).
Choose any \(r\) of them.
For each selected seed, coprimality and the graph value equation give \(\widetilde U(z_i)\widetilde V(z_i)\ne0\).
Division increases either row norm by a factor at most \(2^{b_0}\), using \(M(J)\ge1\) and \(\|E/J\|_1\le2^{b_0}M(E/J)\le2^{b_0}\|E\|_1\) for either original row \(E\), where \(M=\exp m\).

For sign \(\delta=\pm1\) take \(B_\delta=\widetilde U+\delta\widetilde V^*_{b_0}\).
Its sign-\(\delta\) graph is the quotient graph plus \(\delta\) times the formal reversal of that graph, so all \(r\) reciprocal powers \(P_i^a\) divide it.
Set \(E_\delta=(Y+1)B_\delta\) when \(\delta=\varepsilon\), and \(E_\delta=(Y-1)B_\delta\) otherwise.
Both graphs now have the same prescribed sign at width \(b\), and all the divisibilities persist, by the [width-one sign change](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#construction).
At each \(z_i\), the sum \(B_+(z_i)+B_-(z_i)=2\widetilde U(z_i)\) is nonzero, and neither multiplier vanishes at the nontorsion \(z_i\).
Thus at most one integer \(k\) can solve \(E_+(z_i)+kE_-(z_i)=0\) for a given \(i\).
Among \(0,\ldots,r\) choose a common \(k\) avoiding all forbidden values.
The row \(A=E_++kE_-\) has the prescribed sign, qualifies at all \(r\) selected factors, and satisfies
\[
                     \|A\|_1\le4(1+r)2^{b_0}\|F_0\|_1 .
\]
Since \(b_0+\log(1+r)=o(apr)\), (5) and \(t/a,N/r\to1\) give
\(\limsup 2\log\|A\|_1/(apr)\le h\).

For the logarithmic choice of \(r\), one may instead prescribe all \(r\) dilations in advance with prime substitution exponents.
Choose a fixed integer \(B\) past the [irreducible prime-substitution threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers), and choose one prime in each interval \((2^jB,2^{j+1}B)\), \(0\le j<r\), by [Bertrand's postulate](../audits/literature/Erd32/Yin_U001.md#erd32-u001).
Now \(N=r,\ D=O_{P_0}(2^r)=O_{P_0}(p^{1/4})\).
With \(t=a+r\), (4) gives \(2\log\|F_0\|_1/(ph)\le rt+o_{P_0}(1)\), since \(rt\,Dt/n=O_{P_0}(rp^{-3/8})=o(1)\) and the remaining box terms are \(o(ph)\).
The same product divisibility then gives \(\sum_i c_i\le r+o(1)\), hence \(\sum_i c_i\le r\) by integrality.
All \(r\) prescribed factors retain multiplicity \(a\) before the common choice of \(k\).

<a id="limit"></a>
## From the fixed construction to recovered near-minimizing factors

The infimum \(\lambda\) can be approached by fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P_0\) of positive measure \(h<\log2\), by the [irreducible reduction and degree-ten benchmark](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#comparison).
For each such choice the constructed row eventually has norm below \(C_{p,r}\) since its upper rate is at most \(h\).
It is feasible even for the distinct-degree version.
Taking the limit superior first at fixed \(P_0\), and then the infimum of these \(h\), gives the upper bound in (1) for either test.
The correction vanishes at the indicated scales since \(a\to\infty\) and \(\log(2pb+1)=o(apr)\).
The pointwise lower bound proves both limits.
This order of limits gives no rate for approximation to the unrestricted \(\lambda\), and neither the seed threshold nor the size of \(L_0\) is needed by the finite test.

The fixed gap between the benchmark and \(\log2\) puts the minimum strictly below the fallback eventually.
For any minimizing row and any qualifying selection of \(r\) factors, (3) bounds their mean measure by \(T_{p,r}\).
Let \(\Delta_p=T_{p,r}-\lambda\ge0\).
If it is positive, at most \(r\sqrt{\Delta_p}\) of these factors can exceed \(\lambda+\sqrt{\Delta_p}\) in measure, and if it is zero all \(r\) attain \(\lambda\).
This proves the simultaneous near-minimality assertion.
The constructed witnesses use distinct substitutions of one fixed seed of equal measure, so the argument supplies no count of distinct measure values.
