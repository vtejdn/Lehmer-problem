# The second residue period on one-denominator models of the trace pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## Candidate statement

Fix a sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, a positive integer \(h\) and an odd prime \(\ell\nmid q(q+1)\).
Put \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ J=q/2+1,\ u=2^h,\ C=\operatorname{lcm}(q,2^h),\ K_0=(2q+1)^2\) and \(\theta=u\log p/\log s\).
Use the [even-modulus models](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#statement) with \(h\) as their fixed binary precision.
Each model is a monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) of even degree \(d=2D\), with constant one, zero coefficients at offsets \(1,\ldots,J\) from either endpoint, and \(q^dF(s)=K_0\).
Its unique root outside the closed unit disk is \(1<T<s\).
Write \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\), let \(A\) be the common order of \(Z\) in the quotients by \(F\) modulo two and modulo \(q\), and set \(v=A+u,\ N=v+u=A+2u\).
For any monic polynomial over a finite coefficient ring with constant one, the order is the least \(k>0\) for which the polynomial divides \(Z^k-1\) over that ring.

There is a [coefficient and factorization criterion](#trace) for a specified row of this construction to admit the full order \(N\) modulo \(p\).
For each \(\varepsilon>0\) there are arbitrarily large rows on which \(F\bmod p\) has a [divisor of degree less than \(\varepsilon d\)](#short-period-divisor) with exact order \(N\).
There is also a [sequence of rows](#period-completion) with such a divisor of degree \(d-o(d)\).
At each \(r^\nu\parallel p\) the latter sequence has full order \(N r^{b_r}\) where \(r^{b_r}=o(d)\), and every geometric root of \(F\bmod r\) has order dividing \(N\).
Here and below a divisor carrying order \(N\) is monic and reciprocal with constant one.
These sequences retain the even-modulus graph congruence, the dyadic degree bounds and the [real bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#statement) on \(s-T,\ |A-\theta d|\) and \(|v\log T-u\log Y|\).
On the small-divisor sequence the constants for the last two bounds may also depend on \(\varepsilon\).
Whether a full degree-\(d\) word attains exact order \(N\) at every prime power of \(p\) on arbitrarily large rows remains open.
One conditional obstruction occurs when \(N=Rw\) with \(R\) fixed and coprime to \(p\), \(w\) prime tending to infinity, and \((w-1)/\operatorname{ord}_w(r)\) bounded at some prime \(r\mid p\).
In this case every full word of order \(Nr^b\) at \(r^\nu\parallel p\) has \(r^b\gg_{q,h,R,j}d\), where \(j\) bounds the index.

For the parameters in the proofs, fix the odd auxiliary prime \(t>p\) chosen in the [construction of the even period](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical).
Take \(a\to\infty\) with \(a\equiv-1\bmod 2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot(t-1)\) and choose \(j,k\ge1\) maximal with \(10\cdot11^{j-1},3\cdot7^{k-1}\), respectively, at most \(2^a/(100a^2)\).
Then \(n=15\cdot11^j7^k(2^a-1)(2^{a+2}-1)\), \(A=tn\) and \(D=tS\), where \(S\) is chosen in the indicated degree progression close to \(n/(2\theta)\).
The standard even-modulus coefficient word uses the substitution \(V(Z^t)\).

## The rowwise coefficient and order criterion

Fix a sufficiently large row of the [full even-modulus construction](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#combine) at the parameters above.
Retain the row's coefficient word modulo \(C\), which has order \(A=v-u\) modulo \(q\).
Also retain the exact pole \(K_0\), the real root conditions and zero low and high offsets through \(J\).
The required order at \(p\) for a [genuine integral solution](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#statement) is \(N=A+2u\).

For this row the one-denominator control extends to this second order exactly when there exists a monic reciprocal degree-\(d\) polynomial \(W\) over \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) with constant one, zero offsets \(1,\ldots,J\), order \(N\) for \(Z\bmod W\), and coefficient
\[
 [Z^{J+1}]W\equiv
 q^{-(d-J-1)}\frac{K_0-q^d}{p^{J+1}}\pmod p. \tag{1}
\]
The numerator is divisible by \(p^{J+1}\) on the chosen row.
Expanding \(q^d F(p/q)\) modulo \(p^{J+2}\) proves necessity, since exactly the constant term and offset \(J+1\) can contribute.

For sufficiency glue \(W\) and the given word modulo \(C\) coefficientwise by the Chinese remainder theorem with the endpoints and zero prefix fixed over the integers.
At the \(q\)-side the stronger modulus \(Cq^{J+1}\) still gives the old pole congruence because changing coefficients by multiples of \(C\) in the free positions changes the evaluation by multiples of \(C(pq)^{J+1}\).
At the \(p\)-side (1) gives the pole congruence modulo \(p^{J+2}\).
The two conditions give divisibility of the pole discrepancy by \(Cp(pq)^{J+1}\).
The [gcd of the values of the free reciprocal basis](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) is \((pq)^{J+1}\).
Correcting by \(Cp\) times this lattice therefore hits the pole exactly.
The [one-prime approximation and irreducibility argument](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) with residue modulus \(Cp\) preserves both words throughout the same nonempty real neighborhood.
In particular any word satisfying the coefficient conditions lifts this way, whatever its order.
Thus the remaining existence question for this model is a finite-ring question at each specified row.
The [finite-place height obstruction](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement) forces multiplicative independence of \(T\) and \(Y\) on these particular models even if their two residue periods can be matched.

The substitution used on the even side cannot simply be used on the other side.
Choose a prime \(r\mid p\).
If \(W\bmod r=V(Z^t)\) with \(V\) nonconstant and with nonzero constant term, its nonzero roots include \(\alpha,\zeta_t\alpha\) in an algebraic closure, for \(\zeta_t\) of order \(t\), since \(r\ne t\).
Divisibility by \(Z^N-1\) forces \(t\mid N\), whereas \(N=tn+2u\) is prime to \(t\).
A second-period word must therefore break this substitution, even after reduction at each prime of \(p\).
For composite \(p\), a direct test over the entire ring is \(W\mid Z^N-1\) and \(W\nmid Z^{N/s}-1\) for every prime \(s\mid N\), with (1) imposed.
Single-prime reductions alone can miss the order at a prime power.

## A divisor of small degree carrying the entire second period

Fix the eligible \(q,h,\ell,t\) above and \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and put \(\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)\).
There are arbitrarily large rows with all the even-modulus, dyadic-degree and real tests above in which the reduction of the same \(F\) modulo \(p\) contains a monic reciprocal divisor of degree less than \(\varepsilon d\) and exact order \(N\).
Thus \(N\) divides the order of \(Z\) in the full degree-\(d\) quotient modulo \(p\).
The bound on the real-period error is uniform along each chosen sequence and may depend on \(\varepsilon\).

First choose a prime \(\rho>\max(p,t,11,J+1,2\theta/\varepsilon)\) dividing some \(15t\,11^{j_0}-4u\), \(j_0\ge1\).
To see that such primes are unbounded, start with any positive value of this expression and keep its valuation at every prime up to the proposed bound fixed by restricting \(j\) to an arithmetic progression.
For each such prime \(s\ne11\) use the order of \(11\) modulo \(s^{1+v_s(15t\,11^{j_*}-4u)}\), where \(j_*\) is the starting exponent.
The valuation at \(11\) is always zero.
The values on the progression grow without bound.
Let \(e=v_\rho(15t\,11^{j_0}-4u)\), \(R=\rho^e\) and \(Q=\rho^{e+1}\).
Use the [original period construction](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical), with \(a\equiv-1\) modulo both its prescribed modulus and \(\operatorname{ord}_Q(2)\).
Also require the original maximal exponents \(j,k\) there to satisfy
\[
   j\equiv j_0\bmod \operatorname{ord}_Q(11),\qquad
   k\equiv0\bmod \operatorname{ord}_Q(7).
\]
Since \((2^a-1)(2^{a+2}-1)\equiv-1/2\bmod Q\), these choices give \(2N\equiv4u-15t\,11^{j_0}\bmod Q\).
Hence \(R\mid N\) and \(\rho\nmid N/R\).

To verify that the maximal exponents attain the required residues, write \(a=Hm-1\) in the chosen progression, \(x=a\log2-2\log a-\log100\), \(b_1=\operatorname{ord}_Q(11)\) and \(b_2=\operatorname{ord}_Q(7)\).
The defining formulas are \(j=1+\lfloor(x-\log10)/\log11\rfloor\) and \(k=1+\lfloor(x-\log3)/\log7\rfloor\).
The points \((x/(b_1\log11),x/(b_2\log7))\) modulo one are equidistributed with weights \(1/m\).
Indeed a nontrivial Fourier character has frequency \(\lambda=c_1/(b_1\log11)+c_2/(b_2\log7)\ne0\) for \((c_1,c_2)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\), since \(7\) and \(11\) are multiplicatively independent.
If \(\lambda H\log2\notin\mathbb Z\), the geometric sums in \(m\) are bounded and the successive differences of \((Hm-1)^{-4\pi i\lambda}/m\) are summable.
If \(\lambda H\log2\in\mathbb Z\), the character sum with these weights differs by \(O(1)\) from a constant times \(\int_1^X y^{-1-4\pi i\lambda}\,dy\), which is bounded.
Thus each nonconstant weighted Fourier mean tends to zero after division by \(\sum_{m\le X}1/m\).
Trigonometric approximation gives positive limiting mass to the interiors of the intervals specifying both floor residues, so the original block sizes occur on infinitely many such rows.

In the [degree progression](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#period) strengthen the endpoint congruence to \((1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^{J+2}\).
The [principal-unit construction](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) still gives a progression \(D_0\bmod P\) whose prime divisors in \(P\) are at most \(p\), and (1) now prescribes zero.
Choose \(S\) nearest \(n/(2\theta)\) subject to \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P\) and \(R\mid S\).
These congruences are compatible and give \(d=2tS\), \(R\mid d\), and \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tPR\).
For this fixed \(R\) the additional rounding error is bounded as \(a\) grows, so the original [dyadic block filling](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#binary) and [real-period estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) apply.

Write \(w=N/R\) and \(E_b(Z)=1+Z+\cdots+Z^{b-1}\).
The integer reciprocal polynomial \(U=E_wE_R\) divides \(Z^N-1\), since \(\gcd(w,R)=1\) and the two geometric sums have disjoint cyclotomic factors.
Over \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\), \(E_b\) has exact order \(b\) for \(b\ge3\) because it divides \(Z^b-1\), has degree \(b-1\), and differs from \(Z^{b-1}-1\) in an intermediate coefficient.
Consequently \(U\bmod p\) has exact order \(N\), even at composite \(p\).
Its degree is \(w+R-2\), with \((w+R-2)/d\to\theta/R<\varepsilon\).
For sufficiently large rows choose a monic reciprocal \(G\bmod p\) of the remaining even degree whose coefficients through \(J+1\) agree at zero with \(1/U\).
This is possible by setting the low coefficients and reflecting them into the disjoint high positions.
The polynomial \(W=UG\) now has the required zero offsets through \(J+1\).
The coefficient gluing and exact pole correction above, followed by approximation with denominator prime \(\ell\nmid Cp\), preserve \(W\bmod p\) while retaining the full order \(A\) modulo \(q\).

For this prescribed divisor \(U\), test whether the monic reciprocal factors of \((Z^N-1)/U\bmod p\) include a \(G\) of degree \(d-\deg U\) with those specified low coefficients.
Such a factor would give the full order \(N\).
The free completion just constructed gives divisibility of the full order by \(N\).
An additional sufficient test uses \(R\mid d,N\).
A monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(d/R\), constant one and exact order \(N/R\) modulo \(p\) yields a complete word by substituting \(Z^R\).
Its first \(J+1\) offsets vanish, and the substitution multiplies the order by \(R\), as is seen from the free basis \(1,Z,\ldots,Z^{R-1}\) over the quotient in \(Z^R\).

## Matching the second period on all but a vanishing fraction of the degree

Fix \(q,h,t,\ell\) as in the [one-denominator construction](#trace).
There is a sequence with the same full period \(A\) modulo \(q\), dyadic degree bounds, exact pole and bounded real-period error on which \(F\bmod p\) has a reciprocal divisor of degree \(d-o(d)\) and exact order \(N=A+2u\).
For every \(r^\nu\parallel p\), all roots of \(F\bmod r\) satisfy \(Z^N=1\), and the order of \(Z\) in the full quotient modulo \((r^\nu,F)\) is \(N r^{b_r}\) with \(r^{b_r}=o(d)\).
Here an order on a full quotient includes the multiplicities and the precision of the coefficient modulus.

First fix \(0<\eta<1\) and let \(\omega\) be the number of distinct primes dividing \(p\).
Choose \(k_0\) so large that \(2^{\omega+1-k_0}<\eta/(4\theta)\).
The [prime-divisor and exponent argument](#short-period-divisor) can prescribe \(k_0\) distinct exact prime powers in \(N\) at once.
Indeed, for \(B_j=15t\,11^j-4u\), keep the valuations of a positive \(B_j\) at all primes up to a chosen bound fixed on a progression of exponents.
As \(B_j\) grows on that progression it acquires a prime above the bound.
Repeat with the bound at least the last selected prime, keeping the old valuations each time.
At the final exponent \(j_0\) this gives primes \(\rho_i>\max(p,t,11,J+1)\) with \(e_i=v_{\rho_i}(B_{j_0})>0\).
Put \(R=\prod_i\rho_i^{e_i}\), \(Q=\prod_i\rho_i^{e_i+1}\).
Apply the same logarithmic equidistribution argument with this modulus \(Q\), using the orders of \(2,11,7\bmod Q\) for the congruences on the original exponents \(a,j,k\), respectively.
This produces arbitrarily large rows with \(N=Rw\) and \(\gcd(R,w)=1\).
Use the strengthened endpoint progression giving zero in (1), and choose \(S\) nearest \(n/(2\theta)\) subject only to \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P\).
Then \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tP\) with the same \(P\) for every choice of \(R\).
The [binary blocks](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#binary) and the [real-period estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) therefore retain uniform bounds as \(\eta\) varies.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the subgroup of \((\mathbb Z/R\mathbb Z)^\times\) generated by \(-1\) and the primes \(r\mid p\), and write \(g=|\Gamma|\).
The unit group modulo its squares has order \(2^{k_0}\) by the Chinese remainder theorem at odd prime powers, while the image of \(\Gamma\) has order at most \(2^{\omega+1}\).
Consequently \(g\le R\,2^{\omega+1-k_0}\).
Label the \(R\)-th roots of unity at each \(r\mid p\) by the powers of a primitive root.
Each \(\Gamma\)-orbit of labels is Frobenius-stable at every \(r\), and is closed under inversion.
Each orbit except that of the identity root gives a reciprocal factor of \(Y^R-1\) of even degree at most \(g\) and constant one.
At \(r^\nu\) use the corresponding factor lifted by Hensel's lemma, which applies since \(r\nmid R\) and the whole reduction is squarefree.
Uniqueness of the monic lift in the factorization preserves reciprocity, as is also seen by pairing inverse lifted roots.
The same set of labels at all \(r\) gives factors of the same degree, so their coefficientwise Chinese remainder combination divides \(Y^R-1\bmod p\).

Choose one of the primes \(H=\rho_i\).
For each \(r^\nu\parallel p\) fix a power \(L_r=r^{a_r}\) with \(a_r\ge\nu+\lfloor\log_r(J+1)\rfloor\), and put \(K=2H\max_{r\mid p}L_r\).
Include the orbit of a primitive \(R\)-th root, then include other nonidentity orbits until the next would exceed the degree budget \((d-K)/w\).
For sufficiently large rows the first orbit fits, since \(wg/d\to\theta g/R<\eta/4\), and the combined available degree \(R-1\) exceeds the budget since \(d/w\to R/\theta\).
Write \(V\) for the product of the selected factors modulo \(p\), and \(U=V(Z^w)\).
Then \(U\mid Z^N-1\), and its degree deficit \(\Delta=d-\deg U\) is even and satisfies \(K\le\Delta<K+wg<\eta d\) on sufficiently large rows.
The primitive orbit gives \(V\) exact order \(R\) over each \(r^\nu\).
The free basis over the quotient in \(Z^w\) gives \(U\) exact order \(wR=N\) there.
As \(w\to\infty\), the coefficients at offsets \(1,\ldots,J+1\) of \(U\) all vanish.

For \(r^\nu\parallel p\), express \(\Delta/2=Hx+L_r y\) with \(x,y\) nonnegative integers by taking \(0\le y<H\) in the required residue class modulo \(H\).
The definition of \(K\) ensures \(x\ge0\).
Use the remaining-degree factor
\[
   G_r(Z)=(Z^H-1)^{2x}(Z-1)^{2L_r y}\pmod {r^\nu}.
\]
This factor is reciprocal with constant one and zero offsets through \(J+1\).
Indeed \(H>J+1\), and \(v_r\binom{2L_r}{j}\ge a_r-v_r(j)\ge\nu\) for \(1\le j\le J+1\).
Every geometric root of \(G_r\bmod r\) has order dividing \(H\), and \(G_r\) divides \((Z^N-1)^\Delta\) over \(r^\nu\) since \(H\mid N\).
Glue the \(UG_r\) into a full word modulo \(p\) and apply the [pole correction and one-prime approximation](#trace), which only require the specified coefficients of this word.
The order at \(r^\nu\) is a multiple of \(N\) by the divisor \(U\).
It divides \(N r^E\) for \(E=\nu-1+\lceil\log_r(\Delta+1)\rceil\), since \((1+Y)^{r^E}-1\) is divisible by \(Y^{\Delta+1}\bmod r^\nu\).
For the last assertion use \(v_r\binom{r^E}{j}\ge E-v_r(j)\) for \(1\le j\le\Delta\) and substitute \(Y=Z^N-1\).
Thus \(r^{b_r}\le r^\nu(\Delta+1)\).
Choosing successively larger degrees for \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the claimed sequence, since the degree progression and its real-error constant were independent of \(R\).

For a fixed \(R\), even after adding \(o(d)\) roots to a union of whole fibers under \(Z\mapsto Z^w\), the resulting degree differs from \(d\) by at least \((\operatorname{dist}(R/\theta,\mathbb Z)-o(1))w\).
Indeed \(\theta\) is irrational, since a rational relation between \(\log(p/q)\) and \(\log p\) would contradict coprimality of \(p,q>1\).
A stronger restriction holds if \(w\to\infty\) is prime and the index \(j=(w-1)/\operatorname{ord}_w(r)\) is bounded at some fixed \(r\mid p\).
For any full word with order \(Nr^b\) at \(r^\nu\), let \(z\) count the roots of its reduction modulo \(r\) with orders dividing \(R\), including multiplicities.
Once \(w>p\), each of those at most \(R\) distinct roots has multiplicity at most \(r^b\) by divisibility by \(Z^{Nr^b}-1\bmod r\).
All remaining Frobenius orbits have length divisible by \(f=\operatorname{ord}_w(r)\), so \(d-z\) is divisible by \(f\).
For each of the finitely many possible \(j\), \(d/f\to Rj/\theta\) along its rows, with a nonintegral limit.
It follows that \(Rr^b\ge z\gg_{q,h,R,j}d\).
This excludes exact completion in that factorization regime, without asserting that such prime cofactors occur infinitely often.
For the full trace-period test the remaining question is whether the residual \(r\)-power excess can be eliminated at every \(r^\nu\parallel p\) on the same rows.
