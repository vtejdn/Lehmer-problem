# Mahler measure of monomials in conjugates with independent inverse-pair flips

<a id="statement"></a>
## When a monomial preserves the measure

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(M(P)>1\), where \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure.
Assume that for each inverse pair the splitting-field Galois group contains the permutation interchanging its two roots and fixing all other roots.
Choose one root \(v_i\) in each pair, put \(a_i=|\log|v_i||\), and write \(L=\log M(P)=\sum_i a_i\).
For \(b\in\mathbb Z^m\setminus\{0\}\), set \(\gamma=\prod_i v_i^{b_i}\) and let \(s\) be the number of its nonzero exponents.
Use \(M(\gamma)\) for the measure of its minimal polynomial.
Then
\[
                    \log M(\gamma)\ \ge\ \frac{2^{s-1}}s L .
\]
In particular every monomial with \(s\ge3\) strictly increases the measure.
For \(s=1\) its degree is \(2m\), the number of exterior conjugates is unchanged, and \(\log M(\gamma)=|b_i|L\) for its one nonzero exponent.

For \(s=2\) equality with \(L\) holds exactly when both nonzero exponents have absolute value one, the translates of their two labels form a matching on the \(m\) labels, and \(a_i=a_j\) on every edge of the matching.
In this case \(\gamma\) has degree \(2m\) and exactly half as many exterior conjugates as \(P\).
These assertions permit an arbitrary number of exterior roots and make no assumption on their arguments.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Summing over signs in the actual conjugate orbit

Agent-S proposed the estimate and equality classification by counting all signs at every absolute exponent vector.
This extends the [independent flips in the degree-eight example](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v20.md#nonreal-block-example) and the [exponent-orbit calculation for Salem numbers](Yin_C_G0866_v1.md#salem-monomial-orbits) to the stated Galois hypothesis and arbitrary exterior moduli.
All roots of \(P\) are nontorsion algebraic units, and irreducibility gives a transitive action on inverse pairs.
If a monomial in the \(v_i\) is torsion, dividing it by its image under the individual \(i\)-th flip gives a torsion power \(v_i^{2b_i}\), so \(b_i=0\) for each \(i\).
Thus distinct exponent vectors give distinct values, and the Galois orbit of the exponent vector enumerates every conjugate of \(\gamma\) once.

Let \(\mathcal W\) be the set of distinct coordinatewise absolute exponent vectors in this orbit.
Over every \(w\in\mathcal W\) all \(2^s\) sign vectors on its support occur, since the individual flips commute.
The orbit is closed under global negation, so the polynomial of \(\gamma\) is reciprocal and its logarithmic measure is half the sum of the absolute logarithms of its conjugates.
Summing the signs also allows the replacement of every \(\log|v_i|\) by \(a_i\).
Consequently
\[
       \log M(\gamma)
       =2^{s-1}\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}
          \mathbb E_\epsilon\left|\sum_i\epsilon_i w_i a_i\right|,
\]
where each sign on the support is independently uniform.
For any one supported coordinate \(j\), fixing the other signs gives a conditional average at least \(w_j a_j\), by the triangle inequality applied to the two sums.
The expectation is therefore at least \(\max_i w_i a_i\), hence at least \(s^{-1}\sum_i w_i a_i\).
By transitivity the integer \(d=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}w_i\) is the same for every \(i\).
It is at least the largest original absolute exponent, as an automorphism can send a label with that exponent to any prescribed label.
We have proved the stronger bound
\[
                 \log M(\gamma)\ \ge\ \frac{2^{s-1}}s\,d L.
\]
For \(s\ge3\) the coefficient \(2^{s-1}/s\) exceeds one.
For \(s=1\) the orbit consists of the two opposite exponents on each of the \(m\) labels, giving the asserted degree, measure and exterior count directly.
For \(s=2\) any absolute exponent greater than one gives \(d\ge2\) and hence at least \(2L\), including when both exponents exceed one.

<a id="matching"></a>
## The graph on pairs of labels

It remains to take \(s=2\) and both absolute exponents one.
The supports in \(\mathcal W\) are the distinct edges of a simple graph, the orbit of the original unordered pair.
Its valency \(\Delta\) is positive and constant by transitivity.
On an edge \(\{i,j\}\), the four logarithms are \(a_i+a_j,a_i-a_j,-a_i+a_j,-a_i-a_j\) as a multiset.
Their positive parts sum to \(a_i+a_j+|a_i-a_j|\), giving the exact identity
\[
            \log M(\gamma)=\Delta L+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal W}|a_i-a_j|.
\]
Since \(L>0\), equality with \(L\) requires and suffices that \(\Delta=1\) and that the difference vanish on every edge.
At valency one there are \(m/2\) edges, each furnishing four distinct conjugates, giving degree \(2m\).
On a matched edge of common positive weight \(a\) precisely one of the four logarithms, \(2a\), is positive, whereas the original two inverse pairs furnished two exterior roots.
On every other edge both weights vanish and all four conjugates are neutral.
This proves the exterior count and explains the measure preservation in the [degree-eight example](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v20.md#nonreal-block-example).
