# Consecutive dyadic completion with sharp exterior coefficient sizes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed-width rows passing the local tests

Let \(\delta\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic integer unit whose every positive power generates its field, of degree \(d\).
Write \(P,Q\) for the minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\), assume \(\bar P\) squarefree modulo two and set \(F=P^2Q^2\).
In the exterior roots of \(PQ\) list the logarithms of the absolute values, with one entry per root, as \(s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_L>0\).
Put \(\gamma_j=\sum_{i=1}^j s_i\), including \(\gamma_0=0\), and \(S=\gamma_L=3\log M(P)\).
Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\), let \(n=2^k\), and write \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y)\) and \(G_A(X)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n)\).
The integral kernel \(K_b\) consists of the rows \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) for which \(F\mid G_A\).

At every sufficiently large \(k\), for every \(b\ge2d-1\) and every \(q\ge1\), one may prescribe all the coefficients outside **any** interval of \(2d\) consecutive positions.
They admit a unique completion modulo \(2^q\) to a class of \(K_b\).
In particular, suppose \(b\ge 2L+2d+1\), \(\log b=o(n)\) and \(\limsup q\log2/n\le S\).
These conditions allow a single fixed width.
There exist integer rows with endpoints \(a_0=a_b=1\), coprime to their reversals, lying in \(K_b\bmod 2^q\), with
\[
 \frac{\log\|A\|_1}{n}\longrightarrow S,\qquad
 \frac{\log|a_j|}{n},\ \frac{\log|a_{b-j}|}{n}\longrightarrow\gamma_j
                    \quad(0\le j\le L).
\]
Both numerator and reversal are nonzero at \(\delta^n,\delta^{2n}\), and neither \(P\) nor \(Q\) divides the graph.
Nevertheless the value and first derivative equations have exponentially small relative errors at **every** exterior root, in the precise normalization [below](#errors).
When \(6\log M(P)<(\log2)/4\), the rows also satisfy \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) eventually.

<a id="completion"></a>
## Projection of the integral kernel and preservation of endpoints

The [dyadic rank formula](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v274.md#rank) identifies the kernel reduction modulo two with all multiples of \(\bar P^2\) of degree at most \(b\).
It also gives rank \(b+1-2d\), including the zero-dimensional case \(b=2d-1\).
A multiple supported on a deleted interval has the form \(Y^i B(Y)\) with \(\deg B<2d\).
As \(\bar P(0)=1\), this forces \(B=0\).
The projection to the remaining coordinates is consequently an isomorphism modulo two by equality of dimensions.
A basis of the saturated integral kernel projects to a square matrix with odd determinant, which is invertible modulo every \(2^q\).
This proves the completion assertion as a statement about residue classes of integral solutions.

When the deleted interval is interior and the prescribed endpoints are one, choose centered integer representatives on it to form \(A_0\).
Choose an index \(i\) there with \(2i\ne b\), and vary \(A_t=A_0+2^q tY^i\).
A shared zero \(y\) of \(A_t,A_{t,b}^*\) must be nonzero and satisfy
\[
                y^{b-i} A_0(y)-y^i A_{0,b}^*(y)=0.
\]
This polynomial is nonzero because its two terms have different orders at zero, and it has degree at most \(2b\).
Each of its nonzero roots can spoil at most one value of \(t\).
For each \(c\in\{\delta,\delta^2\}\), the two row values at \(c^n\) have nonzero slopes as functions of \(t\).
The graph value at \(c\) also has nonzero slope, since \(c\) is nontorsion and \(n(b-2i)+1\ne0\).
Thus some integer \(0\le t\le 2b+6\) avoids all the forbidden values.
The cost on the entire deleted interval is at most \((d+2b+6)2^q\), and every prescribed outside coefficient is kept as an integer.

<a id="faces"></a>
## Simultaneously realizing both coefficient sizes at every exterior radius

Order the distinct exterior radii \(R\) from largest to smallest and let \(m\) be the number of roots at the current radius, with \(k_0\) roots counted at earlier radii.
The quotient of two distinct roots within either list has infinite order since every power preserves degree.
Across the two lists the absolute Weil heights are unequal, which rules out a torsion quotient.
Start with the real vector \(w_0=(1,-\varepsilon)\).
We construct real pairs \(w_j\), which after rounding will become \((a_{b-j},-\varepsilon a_j)\).
At the current radius normalize them by
\[
       y_\ell=\exp(-n\gamma_{k_0}) R^{-n\ell}w_{k_0+\ell}
                                       \quad (0\le\ell\le m).
\]
The first normalized vector is precisely the last one from the preceding radius.
Take the coordinates of the \(y_\ell\) as coefficients of \(U,V\) and at every node \(c\) of radius \(R\) put \(z_c=(R/c)^n\).
Determine \(y_1,\ldots,y_m\) by the \(2m\) equations
\[
        U(z_c)-cV(z_c)=0,\qquad
        nz_c(U'-cV')(z_c)+cV(z_c)=0.
\]
The [transfer determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v436.md#transfer) is invertible for all sufficiently large \(n\), with inverse norm \(\exp(o(n))\).
Complex conjugation permutes its equations, so uniqueness gives real solutions.
There are finitely many blocks, and all normalized entries have size at most \(\exp(o(n))\).

For a matching lower bound on *each* entry, substitute a formal \(X\) for \(n\) in the derivative multipliers and evaluate all phases at a positive exponent \(a\).
At \(X=0\) the two output polynomials on a block are the two incoming constants times \(D_R(Y)/D_R(0)\), with \(D_R(Y)=\prod_{|c|=R}(Y-(R/c)^a)\).
The phases are distinct at every positive \(a\).
Every coefficient of \(D_R\) is nonzero for all sufficiently large \(a\).
Indeed each elementary symmetric function is a sum of powers of products of distinct members of the fixed set \(\{R/c\}\).
On its multiplicative group modulo torsion choose a real linear functional with distinct values on the members.
Among subsets of a given size there is a unique maximum, so the corresponding base survives after grouping all torsion ratios, on every progression.
The [recurrence bound](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives eventual nonvanishing there.
The constant and leading coefficients are already nonzero.

The last normalized pair at \(X=0\) is the incoming pair times the nonzero scalar \(1/D_R(0)\).
Consequently, after composition through all prior blocks, every single coordinate of the current normalized block has nonzero specialization at \(X=0\) for all sufficiently large positive phase exponents \(a\).
Clear the Cramer determinants of the blocks used so far.
Every resulting numerator has the form \(\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n\) with algebraic polynomial coefficients and algebraic unit-modulus bases.
On each residue class modulo the orders of its torsion ratios, choose a sufficiently large \(a\) in that class.
The nonzero specialization just proved at \(X=0\) shows that at least one grouped polynomial survives.
The [grouped estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#recurrences) gives numerator modulus at least \(\exp(-o(n))\) at \(X=n\) on every class.
The cleared denominators have polynomial upper bounds at the actual phases.
Hence each coordinate of \(w_j\) has absolute value \(\exp(n\gamma_j+o(n))\).

<a id="errors"></a>
## Rounding and completing the middle coefficients

Round the pairs at \(j=0,\ldots,L\) componentwise to nearest integers, keeping \(w_0\) exact.
Prescribe the corresponding outside coefficients and set all other coefficients outside the interval \(L+1,\ldots,L+2d\) to zero.
The width hypothesis ensures that the two end blocks and this interval are disjoint.
Apply the [dyadic completion and perturbation](#completion).
Since \(q\log2\le nS+o(n)\) and \(\log b=o(n)\), the completed row has norm at most \(\exp(nS+o(n))\).
The two prescribed entries at \(j=L\) give the matching lower bound, and rounding preserves all the stated coordinate limits.

Write \(\mathcal U(Y)=\sum_j a_{b-j}Y^j,\ \mathcal V(Y)=-\varepsilon\sum_j a_jY^j\).
At each root \(c\) of radius \(R>1\) set \(z=c^{-n}\) and \(\Lambda_R=\gamma_{k_0}-k_0\log R\).
For some \(\eta>0\) depending only on the fixed roots, at all sufficiently large dyadic exponents the full row satisfies
\[
 e^{-n\Lambda_R}\left(
 |\mathcal U(z)-c\mathcal V(z)|
 +|nz(\mathcal U'-c\mathcal V')(z)+c\mathcal V(z)|\right)
 \le e^{-\eta n}.
\]
Indeed \(\gamma_j-j\log R\) has its maximum \(\Lambda_R\ge0\) exactly on \(k_0,\ldots,k_0+m\) for \(0\le j\le L\), by the order of the slopes \(s_i\).
Before rounding, the block cancels in the two displayed expressions, because the extra derivative term from its initial monomial is \(nk_0\) times its zero value equation.
The finitely many other prescribed indices up to \(L\) have a strict exponent deficit.
The rounding error vanishes at index zero and its contribution elsewhere is \(O(n R^{-n})\).
For \(j>L\) use \(\|A\|_1\le\exp(nS+o(n))\) and \(\max_{j>L}jR^{-nj}=(L+1)R^{-n(L+1)}\) eventually.
As \(S-L\log R\le\Lambda_R\), this tail also has a strict deficit.
Taking a smaller common positive deficit absorbs all polynomial factors at the finitely many radii.
