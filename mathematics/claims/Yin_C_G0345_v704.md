# Counting polynomials with a bounded number of exterior roots in coefficient congruence classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A subexponential count at bounded Mahler measure

Let \(d\ge1,\ 0\le r\le d\) be integers and \(B\ge1\).
Consider all monic squarefree integer polynomials \(P=X^d+a_1X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d\) of multiplicative Mahler measure at most \(B\), with at most \(r\) roots of modulus strictly greater than one.
Fix an integer \(M\ge1\) and a residue modulo \(M\) for every \(a_j\).
Put
\[
 \begin{split}
 A&=d\log(2dB)+r^2\log(2d),\\
 W&=2000(1+2r)\,16^r B^4 A .
 \end{split}
\]
The number of distinct polynomials in that class is at most
\[
                  \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt3}\frac{\sqrt W}{M}\right).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
Within the class a polynomial is determined by the exact values of its first \(\min(d,\lfloor W/M^2\rfloor)\) descending coefficients after the leading one.
In particular, if \(M>\sqrt W\), each class contains at most one polynomial.
For fixed \(B,r\), taking \(M=1\) bounds the total number at degree \(d\) by \(\exp(O_{B,r}(\sqrt{d\log(2d)}))\).
The same order of upper bound with \(D\) replacing \(d\) holds through degree \(D\), by summation.

All monic irreducible integer polynomials satisfy the squarefree hypothesis, including polynomials of nonunits and those with no unit-circle conjugate.
The estimate counts polynomials, allowing all complex conjugate pairs and either sign for real roots.
The [congruence count for Salem polynomials](Yin_C_G0345_v703.md#encoding) uses a single positive real exterior root and reciprocity to recover the polynomial from half its coefficients.
Here the prediction uses finitely many previous full power sums and all \(d\) coefficients are recovered.

<a id="energy"></a>
## Power sums of the roots in the closed unit disk

Write \(s_j=\sum_{\alpha:P(\alpha)=0}\alpha^j=e_j+u_j\) for \(j\ge1\), where \(e_j\) is the sum over the exterior roots and \(u_j\) the sum over the roots in the closed unit disk.
These sums are real because both sets are closed under conjugation.
Let \(\gamma_\alpha=\alpha\) for \(|\alpha|\le1\), and \(\gamma_\alpha=1/\overline\alpha\) otherwise.
We use the discriminant argument for [reflected power sums](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) and verify its domain here.
For distinct roots \(\alpha,\alpha'\),
\[
 |\alpha-\alpha'|
 \le \max(1,|\alpha|)\max(1,|\alpha'|)
                         |1-\overline{\gamma_\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha'}|.
\]
For two roots in the closed disk this follows by squaring, with difference \((1-|\alpha|^2)(1-|\alpha'|^2)\).
For two exterior roots apply the disk inequality after reflection, and for a mixed pair there is equality.
The inequality also holds when a root equals zero.

Put \(\rho=\exp(-1/d)\) and \(t_j=\sum_\alpha\gamma_\alpha^j\).
For \(|z|\le1\), squaring gives \(|1-z|\le\rho^{-1}|1-\rho^2z|\).
The absolute discriminant is the product of \(|\alpha-\alpha'|\) over all ordered distinct pairs and is a positive integer by squarefreeness and monicity.
Taking logarithms in the pair estimate, applying the last inequality, and bounding each added diagonal term by \(-\log(1-\rho^2)\) yield
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2j}}j|t_j|^2
 =-\sum_{\alpha,\alpha'}\log|1-\rho^2\overline{\gamma_\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha'}|
 \le 2(d-1)\log B+(d-1)+d\log\frac1{1-\rho^2}.
\]
Here the equality follows by the absolutely convergent logarithm series.
This argument permits an arbitrary integer constant term.
The last right side is at most \(3d\log(2dB)\), since \(1/(1-e^{-2/d})\le1+d/2\le2d\).
On \(1\le j\le d\), one has \(\rho^{2j}\ge e^{-2}\) and \(|t_j-u_j|\le r\).
With \(H_d=\sum_{j=1}^d1/j\le2\log(2d)\), it follows that
\[
                     U:=\sum_{j=1}^d \frac{|u_j|^2}{j}
           \le 6e^2d\log(2dB)+2r^2 H_d\le45A.                       \tag{2}
\]

<a id="prediction"></a>
## Predicting exterior powers from a finite sequence of traces

Let \(w=\max(1,2r)\), \(K=4^r B^2\), and extend \(s_i=0\) at the indices \(i\le0\) used below.
For these indices define \(e_i\) by the same exterior sum with exponent \(i\), and put \(u_i=-e_i\), so \(|u_i|\le r\).
For each \(j\ge1\), consider all conjugation-invariant lists of at most \(r\) complex numbers of modulus at least one whose product of moduli is at most \(B\), allowing repetitions and the empty list.
Each member has modulus at most \(B\), and the lists of each permitted length form a compact set upon taking all orderings.
For such a list write \(f_i\) for its real power sum at any integer \(i\), taking the empty sum to be zero.
Among the lists minimizing
\[
                      \max_{j-w\le i<j}|s_i-f_i|
\]
take \(y_j\) to be the smallest value of \(f_j\) at a minimizer.
The extrema exist by compactness and continuity, including for the negative exponents.
Thus \(y_j\) is determined by \(d,B,r\) and the past traces.
The true exterior list is admissible, so any chosen minimizer satisfies
\[
          \max_{j-w\le i<j}|e_i-f_i|\le 2\max_{j-w\le i<j}|u_i|.     \tag{3}
\]

Form the monic polynomial whose roots are the entries of the true and selected lists taken together with multiplicities.
Its degree \(m\) is at most \(2r\), and the sum of absolute coefficients is at most the product of \(1+|z|\) over the combined list.
Since \(|z|\ge1\) and the two products of moduli are each at most \(B\), that coefficient sum is at most \(2^{2r}B^2=K\).
Each individual sequence \(z^i\) satisfies the polynomial recurrence at all integer indices, since \(z\ne0\).
Consequently \(e_i-f_i\) satisfies that same recurrence, including when some entries coincide.
For \(m=0\) the difference is identically zero.
For \(m\ge1\), the earlier \(m\) values needed at \(j\) all belong to the window in (3).
This proves
\[
                |s_j-y_j|\le |u_j|+2K\max_{j-w\le i<j}|u_i|.          \tag{4}
\]
The recurrence proof uses no bound on the separation of exterior roots.

Each earlier positive index \(i\) occurs in at most \(w\) windows with \(j>i\), where \(1/j\le1/i\).
A nonpositive index can occur only for \(j\le w\), and throughout those positions its residual is at most \(r\) in absolute value.
Bounding each maximum squared by the sum of the positive-index squares in the window plus \(r^2\) if a nonpositive index occurs, one obtains
\[
     \sum_{j=1}^d\frac1j\max_{j-w\le i<j}|u_i|^2
           \le w U+r^2 H_{\min(w,d)} .
\]
Using (2) and (4) now gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^d\frac{|s_j-y_j|^2}{j}
   \le 2U+8K^2(wU+r^2H_{\min(w,d)})
   \le466wK^2 A\le W/4 .                                              \tag{5}
\]

<a id="coding"></a>
## Recovery in a specified coefficient class

At step \(j\) Newton's identity fixes \(s_j=-j a_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i s_{j-i}\) modulo \(jM\), given the previous coefficients and sums and the prescribed residue of \(a_j\).
Let \(b_j\) be the nearest integer in this known class to \(y_j\), choosing the lower integer at a tie.
Encode \(z_j=(s_j-b_j)/(jM)\in\mathbb Z\).
The class and past determine \(b_j\), so decoding \(z_j\) recovers \(s_j\) and then \(a_j\).
Induction through \(d\) gives an injection into the integer vectors \(z\).
Since \(s_j\) is itself in the prescribed class, \(jM|z_j|\le2|s_j-y_j|\), and (5) yields
\[
               \sum_{j=1}^d j|z_j|\le\sum_{j=1}^d j z_j^2
                              \le W/M^2 .                              \tag{6}
\]
In particular \(z_j=0\) for \(j>W/M^2\).
If the initial coefficients through \(\min(d,\lfloor W/M^2\rfloor)\) are given, so are their traces, and the zero digits uniquely determine the suffix by this same recursion.

For \(V=W/M^2\) and \(v>0\), the [signed partition estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#count) bounds the number of vectors of weight at most \(V\) by
\[
 e^{vV}\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-vj})^{-2}
 \le \exp\!\left(vV+\frac{\pi^2}{3v}\right).
\]
Indeed the logarithm of the product is \(2\sum_{\ell\ge1}1/(\ell(e^{v\ell}-1))\le 2v^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell^{-2}\).
Choosing \(v=\pi/\sqrt{3V}\) proves (1).

Squarefreeness matters for separation at this scale.
For every prime \(p\), the distinct polynomials \((X-1)^p\) and \(X^p-1\) are congruent coefficientwise modulo \(p\), both have degree \(p\), measure one and zero exterior roots.
For all sufficiently large \(p\) this modulus exceeds \(\sqrt W\) at \(r=0,\ B=1\).
