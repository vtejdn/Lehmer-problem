# Occupied exponents of two rows with a common seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## A linear bound in the number of tested exponents

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(e\), let \(U,V\) be nonzero rational polynomials and put \(s=|\operatorname{supp} U\cup\operatorname{supp} V|\).
Suppose \(U(\alpha^p)+\alpha V(\alpha^p)=0\) at every exponent in a finite set \(S\) of distinct nonnegative integers.
For any place of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) over 2 let \(a\) be its ramification index, \(f\) its residue degree and \(d\) the multiplicative order of the reduction of \(\alpha\).
Set \(h=d2^{1+\lceil\log_2 a\rceil}\).
If \(S\ne\varnothing\), then \(s\ge2\) and
\[
 \max_{c\bmod h}|S\cap(c+h\mathbb Z)|\le 2s-3,
 \qquad |S|\le h(2s-3)<8e(2^e-1)s.                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular at the [full-interval widths](Yin_C_G0345_v515.md#statement), every actual common pair with a single qualifying seed has at least \(L^{1-o(1)}\) occupied exponents across its two rows.
The bound applies at every coefficient cost, even with only simple incidence at the seed.

<a id="disk"></a>
## A uniform bound in one residue class

Normalize the local valuation by \(v(2)=1\).
We have \(v(\alpha^d-1)\ge1/a\), since the seed is a unit and its reduction has order \(d\mid 2^f-1\).
Squaring an element \(1+x\) with \(v(x)>0\) raises the valuation of its difference from 1 to at least \(\min(2v(x),1+v(x))\).
After \(\lceil\log_2 a\rceil\) squarings the valuation is at least 1, and after one more it is at least 2.
Thus \(\theta=\log(\alpha^h)\) satisfies \(v(\theta)=v(\alpha^h-1)\ge2\), and \(\exp(\theta)=\alpha^h\) by the convergent inverse power series on this disk.
Nontorsion gives \(\theta\ne0\).

A rational algebraic unit is \(\pm1\), so \(\alpha\) is irrational.
Consequently \(U(Y)+\alpha V(Y)=\sum_{i=1}^s w_iY^{u_i}\) has a nonzero coefficient at each exponent of the union.
If \(s=1\), it has no nonzero root.
For a representative \(0\le c<h\), set \(b_i=w_i\alpha^{cu_i}\) and \(T_n=\sum_i b_i u_i^n\), using \(u_i^0=1\) also at zero.
The first \(s\) moments cannot all vanish because their Vandermonde matrix on the distinct \(u_i\) is invertible.
Put \(M=\min_{0\le i<s}v(T_i)\).
The monic integer polynomial \(\prod_i(X-u_i)\) gives an integral linear recurrence for these moments, whence \(v(T_n)\ge M\) for every \(n\ge0\).
On the local closed unit disk form the convergent series
\[
 F_c(Z)=\sum_i b_i\exp(\theta u_i Z)=\sum_{n\ge0} A_n Z^n,
 \qquad A_n=T_n\theta^n/n!.
\]
At some \(i<s\) we have \(v(A_i)\le M+(s-1)v(\theta)\).
For \(n\ge2s-2\) use \(v_2(n!)\le n-1\) to get
\[
 v(A_n)\ge M+n(v(\theta)-1)+1>M+(s-1)v(\theta),
\]
since \(v(\theta)\ge2\).
The last index attaining the largest coefficient absolute value is therefore at most \(2s-3\).
For completeness, a restricted series with last such index \(N\) has at most \(N\) distinct zeros on this disk.
Indeed division at a zero \(z\) has quotient coefficients \(B_i=\sum_{j>i} A_j z^{j-1-i}\), which tend to zero.
If \(N=0\) the constant term dominates the rest throughout the disk, while for \(N>0\) the quotient has the same maximum at index \(N-1\) and a strict drop thereafter.
Induction proves the assertion.
At every nonnegative integer \(t\), the value is \(F_c(t)=U(\alpha^{c+ht})+\alpha V(\alpha^{c+ht})\).
Counting distinct such zeros gives the first inequality in (1).
Summation over the classes gives the second, since \(h\le4a(2^f-1)\), \(a\le e\) and \(f\le e\).

<a id="interval"></a>
## Actual rows throughout a prime interval

For the [same-seed rows](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common), each graph vanishes at the same \(\alpha\), so (1) applies without taking resultants or reducing the rows modulo a prime.
At the [selected widths](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width) the seed degree satisfies \(e\le j\le\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil^{1/4}\).
Thus \(e(2^e-1)=L^{o(1)}\), while the prime number theorem gives \(|S|\sim L/\log L\).
Substitution in (1) proves the stated support estimate.
In particular one of the two rows has at least half as many occupied exponents as the union.
Their common width \(b=|S|j\) can accommodate these supports, so the estimate alone gives no fixed positive lower bound for \(\log H/\sum_{p\in S}p\).
