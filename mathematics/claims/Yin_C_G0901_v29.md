# Every finite cyclotomic avoidance cost is given by arc length and the forbidden orbits

<a id="statement"></a>
## All feasible probabilities and the exact coefficient rate

Let \(B\) be a finite set of forbidden positive orders, let \(\eta\) denote normalized arc length on \(\mathbb T\), and let \(\eta_b\) denote the uniform probability on primitive \(b\)-th roots.
An even probability \(\mu\) satisfies \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for every \(n\notin B\) if and only if it is a convex combination of \(\eta\) and the measures \(\eta_b\), \(b\in B\).
For arbitrary probabilities the same equivalence holds for the conjugation symmetrization, since the test functions are even.

Consequently, for every nonzero real polynomial \(P\),
\[
 \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac1a\log\inf_C\|P^a C\|_1
   = \max\left(\{m(P)\}\,\cup\,
          \left\{\int\log|P|\,d\eta_b:b\in B\right\}\right),              \tag{1}
\]
where \(C\) runs over monic cyclotomic products avoiding all orders in \(B\), including the empty product, and \(\|\cdot\|_1\) is the coefficient one-norm.
One may impose any additional degree bound \(\deg C\le W(a)\) with \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
An orbit containing a zero of \(P\) has average \(-\infty\).

The proof uses a mean over pairs of distinct primes to control the logarithmic singularity at every power of the identity, after removing the atoms.
Write \(e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)\), \(\|x\|=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|x-k|\) and \(H(x)=\log(1/\|x\|)\), with \(H(0)=+\infty\) on \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).

<a id="prime"></a>
## A bounded average of truncated logarithms at prime multiples

Let \(N\) be a sufficiently large positive integer, let \(L=\log N\), and use the primes \(N<p\le2N\) with weights \(w_p=\log p/S_N\), where \(S_N=\sum_{N<p\le2N}\log p\).
The [prime count with von Mangoldt weights](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) at modulus one gives \(S_N\sim N\), after omitting proper prime powers, whose total weight up to \(2N\) is \(O(\sqrt N\log^2(2N))\).
We prove the absolute bound
\[
 \sup_{\|\theta\|\ge1/(8N)}
       \sum_p w_p \min(H(p\theta),\log(8N))\ll1 .                         \tag{2}
\]

First truncate \(H\) at \(3\log L\), writing \(h(x)=\min(H(x),3\log L)\) with its continuous value at zero.
Its Lipschitz constant for circle distance is at most \(L^3\).
Let \(T=\lceil L^{12}\rceil\).
Convolution with the Fejér kernel of degree \(T\) gives a trigonometric polynomial \(h_T\) with the same Haar mean and uniform error \(O(L^3\log(2T)/T)\).
Indeed the kernel is bounded by \(C\min(T,1/(T\|x\|^2))\), whose integral against \(\|x\|\) is \(O(\log(2T)/T)\).
Each coefficient of \(h_T\) is \(O(1+\log L)\) by the supremum bound on \(h\).

For each \(1\le k\le T\) use rational approximation with \(Q=\lfloor N/L^{100}\rfloor\) to choose a reduced \(a_k/d_k\) such that
\[
             1\le d_k\le Q,\qquad |k\theta-a_k/d_k|\le1/(d_k Q).
\]
This follows by the pigeonhole principle applied to the first \(Q+1\) multiples, followed by reduction of the fraction.
If every \(d_k\ge L^{100}\), the [single-frequency Vaughan estimate](../audits/literature/Vau77/Yin_U001.md#prime-exponential-sum) at \(X=N,2N\) gives
\[
      \left|\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)e(k\theta n)\right|
        \ll L^7\big(Xd_k^{-1/2}+X^{4/5}+\sqrt{Xd_k}\big)
        \ll N L^{-40}.
\]
Its approximation hypothesis holds since \(d_k\le Q\).
Taking the difference at the endpoints, removing proper prime powers and dividing by \(S_N\) bounds each nonconstant mode in the weighted prime average by \(O(L^{-40})\), including the conjugate modes.
Summing the \(2T\) coefficient errors and using the uniform Fejér approximation gives
\(\sum_p w_p h(p\theta)\le \int H\,d\eta+O(1)\).

In the other case some \(d_k<L^{100}\), so reducing \(a_k/(k d_k)\) writes \(\theta=b/r+\beta\) with \((b,r)=1\), \(r\le2L^{112}\), and \(N|\beta|\le2L^{100}\).
For sufficiently large \(N\) all the sampled primes are coprime to \(r\).
On a reduced residue class \(u\bmod r\), the test is \(h(bu/r+\beta p)\).
The [Siegel–Walfisz estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) for \(\psi(x;r,u)\), after removing proper prime powers, gives the weighted count up to \(x\) as \(x/\varphi(r)+O(N/L^{400})\) uniformly for \(N\le x\le2N\).
The supremum of the test is \(O(1+\log L)\) and its variation on \([N,2N]\) is \(O(L^3|\beta|N)=O(L^{103})\).
Partial summation over at most \(2L^{112}\) classes and division by \(S_N\) therefore give
\[
 \sum_p w_p h(p\theta)
  =\frac{N}{S_N}\frac1{\varphi(r)}
       \sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/r)^\times}\int_1^2 h(bu/r+\beta Nt)\,dt+o(1).
                                                                            \tag{3}
\]
For \(r=1\) use the single residue class.

For every nonintegral \(y\) and real \(v\),
\[
                      \int_1^2 H(y+vt)\,dt\le C+H(y).                      \tag{4}
\]
If \(|v|\ge1/8\), integration over an interval of length \(|v|\) and periodicity bound the average by an absolute multiple of \(\int H\,d\eta\), also for integral \(y\).
If \(|v|<1/8\) and \(\|y\|>4|v|\), the distance to the nearest integer along the interval is at least \(\|y\|/2\).
In the remaining case \(v\ne0\), and the average of \(H\) on any interval of length \(|v|\) is at most \(C+\log(1/|v|)\).
This follows by integrating the level set bound \(\min(|v|,2e^{-t})\) for \(H>t\) on that interval.
Since \(\|y\|\le4|v|\), this proves (4).
For \(r>1\), multiplication by \(b\) permutes the unit classes, and \(|1-e(y)|\le2\pi\|y\|\) gives
\[
  \frac1{\varphi(r)}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/r)^\times}H(bu/r)
    \le\log(2\pi)-\frac{\log|\Phi_r(1)|}{\varphi(r)}
    \le\log(2\pi).
\]
Here \(\Phi_r(1)\) is a nonzero integer.
For \(r=1\), the assumption \(\|\theta\|\ge1/(8N)\) implies \(N|\beta|\ge1/8\).
Thus (3) gives a bounded average in both cases.

To raise the truncation from \(3\log L\) to \(\log(8N)\), fix \(0<y\le L^{-3}<1/8\).
If \(\|p\theta\|<y\), the nearest integer \(a_p\) to \(p\theta\) is coprime to \(p\), since \(p\mid a_p\) would give \(\|\theta\|<y/p<1/(8N)\).
The distinct reduced fractions \(a_p/p\) lie in a real interval of length \(2y/N\) about a fixed representative of \(\theta\) and are mutually separated by at least \(1/(4N^2)\).
There are at most \(1+8Ny\) of them.
Since \(\max_p w_p\ll L/N\), the weighted contribution between the two truncations is bounded by
\[
        \int_{3\log L}^{\log(8N)} C(L/N+Le^{-t})\,dt=O(1).
\]
This completes (2).
The bounds use the cited prime estimates at theorem-statement scope and require no effective value of their sufficiently large threshold.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## The mean of the cyclotomic logarithms of order \(pq\)

Let \(U_N(z)\) be the weighted mean of \(\log|\Phi_{pq}(z)|\) over ordered distinct primes \(p,q\in(N,2N]\), assigning weights \(w_p w_q/(1-\sum_t w_t^2)\).
The weights have sum one and each of their two marginals is bounded by \(2w_p\) for large \(N\), since \(\max_p w_p\to0\).
There is an absolute \(C\) such that, for every \(z=e(\theta)\),
\[
 U_N(z)\le
 \begin{cases}
   C,&\|\theta\|<1/(8N),\\
   C+\log|1-z|,&\|\theta\|\ge1/(8N).
 \end{cases}                                                              \tag{5}
\]
For \(t=\|\theta\|<1/(8N)\) and \(t>0\), both \(pt,qt<1/4\).
In the identity
\[
             \Phi_{pq}(z)=\frac{(1-z^{pq})(1-z)}{(1-z^p)(1-z^q)}
\]
the numerator has modulus at most \(4\pi^2pq\,t^2\), and the denominator has modulus at least \(16pq\,t^2\).
This also handles the entire range \(N^{-2}\le t<1/(8N)\).
At \(z=1\) the polynomial equals one.

Now suppose \(t\ge1/(8N)\).
At most one of \(\|p\theta\|,\|q\theta\|\) can be smaller than \(1/(8N)\).
Otherwise their nearest integers \(a,b\) satisfy \(|qa-pb|<1\), hence \(p\mid a\) and \(t<1/(8Np)\), a contradiction.
If both distances exceed the threshold or are equal to it, the identity and \(|1-z^p|\ge4\|p\theta\|\) bound the log by
\[
    C+\log|1-z|+\min(H(p\theta),\log(8N))+\min(H(q\theta),\log(8N)).
\]
If, for example, \(\|p\theta\|\) is smaller, the factor \((1-z^{pq})/(1-z^p)\) has modulus at most \(q\le2N\) by the geometric sum, including its polynomial value at a zero of the original denominator.
This gives the same bound with the indicated truncation.
Averaging, the marginal bound and (2) prove (5).

<a id="diffuse"></a>
## Integrability for every power after the atoms are removed

Remove from \(\mu\) its positive atomic part \(A\), leaving \(\nu\).
The [exclusion of all other atoms](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#regularity) says that \(A\) is supported on forbidden primitive roots and \(\nu\) is atomless.
For every fixed \(k\ge1\) and every \(p,q>\max(B\cup\{1\})\) in the prime average, every root of \(\Phi_{pq}(X^k)\) has order divisible by \(pq\).
The polynomial is a monic product of allowed cyclotomic factors, so \(\int U_N(z^k)\,d\mu(z)\ge0\).
This is a finite integral since the factor logarithms are bounded above and integrable under the constraints.
By (5) the integral against the removed atoms is at most \((C+\log2)A(\mathbb T)\), independently of \(N,k\).
Integrating (5) also against \(\nu\) gives
\[
       0\le C+\log2+
          \int_{\|kx\|\ge1/(8N)}\log|1-e(kx)|\,d\nu(x).
\]
The domains increase to the complement of finitely many points of zero \(\nu\)-mass.
The positive part is bounded by \(\log2\), so monotone convergence of the restricted negative part gives a uniform lower bound on \(\int\log|1-e(kx)|\,d\nu(x)\) for every \(k\).
In particular
\[
                     \sup_{k\ge1}\int H(kx)\,d\nu(x)<\infty .
\]

If \(\nu\ne0\), apply the [Cesàro bound for the logarithm of a rotation](Yin_C_G0901_v27.md#rotation) to the atomless probability \(\nu/\nu(\mathbb T)\).
Writing \(A_n=\int\log|1-e(nx)|\,d\nu(x)\), it gives \(N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}|A_n|\to0\).
This also holds with \(A_{dn}\) for each fixed \(d\), by comparison with the sum up to \(dN\).
The finite divisor identity for \(X^s-1\) makes \(I_s(j)=\int\log|\Phi_s(z^j)|\,d\nu(z)\) a finite integer combination of \(A_{dj}\), \(d\mid s\).
All factor integrals are finite, since they sum to a finite integral and their integrands are bounded above.
Hence for every fixed \(s,k\), \(N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}|I_s(kn)|\to0\).
For any \(\epsilon>0\) the prohibited choices \(|I_s(kn)|\ge\epsilon\) for any finite set of fixed multipliers \(k\) have density zero.
Every fixed residue class used in the [two- and three-difference proof](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#differences) has positive density.
Thus the successive coprime choices with all required nonempty subproducts small exist.
That proof gives \(I_s(1)=0\) for every \(s\), since its two polynomial differences have nonnegative integrals and logarithm zero at every removed atom.
The [unrestricted moment characterization](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement) identifies the symmetrization of \(\nu\) with \(\nu(\mathbb T)\eta\).
For \(\nu=0\) this identity already holds.

For even \(\mu\) the [finite-orbit uniformity theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v23.md#statement) makes its atom masses uniform within each forbidden primitive orbit.
Conversely arc length gives zero for every cyclotomic logarithm, and \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\eta_b=\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,\Phi_n)|/\varphi(b)\ge0\) at every allowed \(n\).
This proves the full classification.
The [finite forbidden-order dual formula](Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#dual) now gives (1) by taking the maximum over these measures, also at the stated degree budgets.

For example, let \(P\) be the monic irreducible minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit, and fix \(B\ne\varnothing\).
The orbit averages in (1) are \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,P)|/\varphi(b)\) with nonzero integer resultants.
If \(m(P)<\log2/\max_{b\in B}\varphi(b)\), the coefficient rate equals \(m(P)\) exactly when all those resultants have absolute value one.
Otherwise the rate is at least \(\log2/\max_{b\in B}\varphi(b)\) within this small-measure range.
The [exceptional-unit reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v17.md#statement) describes how these unit requirements compare across finite sets at a vanishing infimum.
Existence of such representatives on an unrestricted shrinking sequence remains a separate Lehmer question.
