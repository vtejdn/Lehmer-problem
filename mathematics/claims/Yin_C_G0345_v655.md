# The number of large adjacent gaps in terminal witnesses of small Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Restrictions on the spacings of an actual witness

Let \(\alpha\) run through the degree-preserving representatives of a sequence of nontorsion units with a unit-circle conjugate and \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\).
Here degree preservation means \(\deg(\alpha^Q)=\deg\alpha\) for every \(Q\ge1\).
For each input use a tuple of \(r\) signed coefficients and a collection of at least \((c/m)^{r-2}\) distinct marked terminal lists in the [finite witness window](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement), where \(c>0\) is fixed and \(r\to\infty\).
Include the marked term when sorting the \(r\) positions.
Put \(L=\log(1/m)\), and fix \(0\le\theta<1\) and \(0<\epsilon<1\).
At each of the \(r-1\) adjacent gaps prescribe a set of at most \(m^{-\theta}\) allowed positive integers.
The prescribed sets may depend on the input, the tuple, the index of the gap and the position of the mark in the full order.

Except for a proportion at most \(\exp(-c_{\epsilon,\theta}rL)\) of the lists, the number of gaps outside their respective sets exceeds
\[
       \left\lfloor
        \frac{(1-\epsilon)(1-\theta)rL}{(1-\theta)L+2\log r}
       \right\rfloor                                                   \tag{1}
\]
for all sufficiently late inputs, with \(c_{\epsilon,\theta}>0\).
Taking the same initial interval of positive integers for every gap counts gaps longer than \(m^{-\theta}\).
When \(\log r=o(L)\), the proportion of gaps longer than \(m^{-\theta}\) tends to one in probability for the uniform measure on the witness collection.
When \(\log r\le A L\) for fixed \(A>0\), any fixed fraction below \((1-\theta)/(1-\theta+2A)\) holds outside an exponentially small exceptional proportion.

Every Salem number has the degree preservation used here.
Over \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^Q)\) all conjugates of a Salem number \(\alpha>1\) have modulus \(\alpha\), and \(\alpha\) is the only conjugate of that modulus.
For a general sequence with a unit-circle conjugate the [degree-preserving representatives](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) have exactly the original Mahler measures.
On the original inputs, even without degree preservation, a proportion \(1-\exp(-\Omega_\theta(rL))\) of the actual lists has more than
\[
   \left\lfloor b_\theta\min\left\{r-1,
        \left(\frac{rL}{\log(2+rL)}\right)^{1/5}\right\}\right\rfloor    \tag{2}
\]
gaps outside the prescribed sets, for a constant \(b_\theta>0\).

<a id="trees"></a>
## Counting simultaneous monomial identities by labeled binary trees

Suppose \(n\ge2\) and \(b_1,\dots,b_n\in\mathbb C(X)^\times\) are fixed.
There are at most \((2n-3)!!\) integer arrays with \(u_n=0\) such that
\[
               \sum_{i=1}^n b_i(X)X^{u_i}=0
\]
and no nonempty proper subsum vanishes.
At a given array, choose two current terms of equal largest degree at infinity and merge them, representing the group by its least label.
At each stage the sum is zero, so two such terms exist until the last merge.
A group other than the full set has a nonzero sum by nondegeneracy.
The merges construct a rooted binary tree on the labeled leaves with unordered children.

The tree alone recovers all shifts up to translation.
At a leaf the normalized coefficient is \(b_i\).
For children \(A,B\) with representatives \(i,j\) and normalized coefficients \(b_A,b_B\), equality of degrees forces
\[
                u_j-u_i=\deg b_A-\deg b_B .
\]
If \(i<j\), the normalized coefficient at their parent is \(b_A+X^{u_j-u_i}b_B\), and the symmetric rule applies if \(j<i\).
Thus the reconstruction works from the leaves upwards regardless of the chronological order of merges on disjoint sets.
The root gives all relative shifts, and \(u_n=0\) fixes their translation.
There are \((2n-3)!!\) trees, by inserting a new labeled leaf along any existing edge or above the root.
For unrestricted labeled arrays the factorial order of growth is necessary.
Taking \(b_1=\cdots=b_{n-1}=1\) and \(b_n=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X^j\), all \((n-1)!\) permutations of the shifts \(1,\ldots,n-1\) satisfy the identity nondegenerately.

Consequently, for fixed nonzero complex coefficients at a nontorsion algebraic unit, the number of normalized nondegenerate \(n\)-term lists is at most
\[
                 Q^{n-1}(2n-3)!!
     \qquad\text{if}\qquad \log M(\alpha^Q)>(n/2)\log n.              \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, on each of the \(Q^{n-1}\) residue classes the [nondegenerate rational-function lift](Yin_C_G0345_v592.md#lifting) gives common coefficients for any finite selection of rows, so the tree bound applies.
This improves the count from multiplying the numbers of available pairs over all successive merges, which also counts different interleavings on disjoint sets.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Translating consecutive groups of terms

For a fixed ordered tuple of nonzero algebraic coefficients, consider nondegenerate relations at a nontorsion algebraic unit, up to simultaneous translation and without any span restriction.
Prescribe sets of at most \(H\) possible values for the respective gaps, where \(H\ge1\).
The number of lists with at most \(k\) gaps outside their sets is at most
\[
             \binom{r-1}{k} H^{r-1-k} B_k,\qquad 0\le k\le r-1,        \tag{4}
\]
where \(B_0=1\).
For \(k\ge1\) one can take
\[
 B_k=(8k)^{4k^4(2k+1)},\qquad
 \text{or}\qquad B_k=Q^k(2k-1)!!
 \quad\text{if}\quad \log M(\alpha^Q)>\tfrac{k+1}{2}\log(k+1).
\]
Choose \(k\) cuts containing all gaps outside their sets.
Each other gap has at most \(H\) possibilities.
These internal gaps determine the coefficient of each of the \(k+1\) consecutive groups when its first exponent is translated to zero.
If any group coefficient vanishes, there is no counted solution for these data when \(k\ge1\).
Otherwise all free translations give a single nondegenerate equation on the \(k+1\) group coefficients, since a zero subsum of groups expands to a proper zero subsum of the original relation.
Normalize one group position to zero.
The \(k\) other positions inject into the \(k\)-fold product of \(\langle\alpha\rangle\), a tuple group of rank \(k\).
Dividing by the nonzero coefficient of the normalized group gives the first bound from [Amoroso and Viada, Theorem 6.2](../audits/literature/AV09/Yin_U001.md#retained-primary-statement) with dimension and rank both \(k\).
Alternatively, (3) gives the second bound.
For \(k=0\) all the gaps are already fixed once their values are chosen.
This proves (4).

<a id="witnesses"></a>
## Comparing the exceptional count with the witness multiplicity

A complete order of the coefficients determines the coefficient pattern in (4).
The marked term gives at most \(r\) order choices, and translation classes with that mark correspond to uniquely normalized lists.
Terminality excludes every nonempty proper zero subsum, and the positions are distinct for sufficiently large \(r\).
Thus the proportion with at most \(k\) gaps outside their sets is bounded by \(r\) times (4) divided by \((c/m)^{r-2}\).

On a degree-preserving input, \(\log M(\alpha^Q)=Qm\).
For \(k\ge1\) use \(Q=1+\lfloor(k+1)\log(k+1)/(2m)\rfloor\).
For an absolute constant \(C\), the logarithm of the second bound for \(B_k\) is at most
\[
             k\big[L+2\log(k+1)+\log\log(2k+2)+C\big]
\]
when \(m\le1\), by \((2k-1)!!\le(2k)^k\).
Take \(H=m^{-\theta}\) and \(k\) equal to the integer in (1), which tends to infinity and is less than \(r-1\) eventually.
Using \(\binom{r-1}k\le2^{r-1}\), the logarithm of the exceptional proportion is at most
\[
 -(1-\theta)rL+
 k\big[(1-\theta)L+2\log r\big]+O\big(r+L+k\log\log(3r)\big).
\]
The last error is \(o(rL)\), since \(r,L\to\infty\) and \(k=O_\theta(rL/(L+\log r))\).
The leading terms sum to at most \(-\epsilon(1-\theta)rL\), proving (1).

Without degree preservation take \(k\) equal to the integer in (2).
The first bound for \(B_k\) has logarithm at most \(C' k^5\log(8k)\) for an absolute constant \(C'\).
For \(b_\theta\le1\) this is \(O(b_\theta^5 rL)\), with an absolute implied constant.
Taking \(b_\theta\) small enough makes it at most \((1-\theta)rL/2\), whereas the remaining logarithmic cost in the numerator of the proportion is at most \(\theta rL+O(r+\log r)\).
This proves (2).
The constraints describe the distribution of spacings in actual witnesses and leave open the total number of admissible exponent lists with these many unrestricted gaps.
