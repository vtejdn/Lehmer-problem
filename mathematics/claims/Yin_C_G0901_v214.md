# Reciprocal local factors at the deep primes of a Salem translate

<a id="graph"></a>
## A grading on the incidences of deep local factors

Let \(f,g\) be monic separable reciprocal integer unit polynomials of degrees \(D,d\), and fix an odd prime \(p\).
Write \(s_p=v_p(2^{p-1}-1)\), with \(v_p(p)=1\), and put
\[
 T_0=s_p+\lfloor\log_p(Dd)\rfloor+\lfloor\log_p(D+d)\rfloor .
\]
Use the monic irreducible factors of \(f\) and \(g\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\) as two disjoint vertex sets.
Write \(i^*\) for the factor of the inverses of the roots of \(i\), in the same vertex set.
At any precision \(T>T_0\), draw an arrow \(j\to i\) if \(i\mid f,\ j\mid g\) have roots \(y,b\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) with \(v_p(2-yb)\ge T\).
For each such arrow also draw its reversed image under inversion, \(i^*\to j^*\).
Each component has an integer grading: the grade increases by exactly one on every arrow.
A component cannot contain self-reciprocal factors from both vertex sets, even if they are connected only through intermediate factors.

To prove this, take \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Dd)\).
At a factor \(i\) of degree \(u\) on the \(f\) side attach the unit \(a_i=N_{\mathbb Q_p(y)/\mathbb Q_p}(y)^{L/u}\).
At a factor \(j\) of degree \(v\) on the \(g\) side attach \(a_j=N_{\mathbb Q_p(b)/\mathbb Q_p}(b)^{-L/v}\).
These labels are inverted under \(i\mapsto i^*\).
If \(w=[\mathbb Q_p(y,b):\mathbb Q_p]\), every conjugate of \(yb/2\) equals one to precision \(T\).
Taking their product and raising to the integer \(L/w\) shows that \(a_i/a_j\) equals \(2^L\) to this same precision, meaning \(v_p(a_i/a_j-2^L)\ge T\).
This property holds on the reflected arrow as well, with the quotient always taken as the head label divided by the tail label.

On a simple circuit let \(h\) be the number of arrows traversed forwards minus the number traversed backwards.
The same argument covers two opposite parallel arrows.
If \(h\ne0\), then \(1\le|h|\le D+d\) and \(v_p(2^{L|h|}-1)\ge T\).
For every positive integer \(n\) the valuation \(v_p(2^n-1)\) is at most \(s_p+v_p(n)\), since \(2^{(p-1)n}-1\) has exactly that valuation by the binomial formula at an odd prime.
Also \(v_p(L)=\lfloor\log_p(Dd)\rfloor\).
This contradicts \(T>T_0\).
Consequently all circuits have signed length zero, and signed lengths along paths from a fixed vertex define the integer grading.
To check the assertion for self-reciprocal factors, their labels have square one.
A simple path between the two sides has odd length and hence nonzero signed length \(h\), with \(|h|\le D+d\).
Squaring the label comparison gives \(v_p(2^{2L|h|}-1)\ge T\), again impossible since \(p\) is odd.

## Paths of large-degree factors

Write \(c_f=v_p(\operatorname{disc} f),\ c_g=v_p(\operatorname{disc} g)\), and \(C=\max(c_f,c_g)\).
For the same graph, every component containing a degree \(u>2C/T\) vertex and an edge is a directed path of factors of that same degree.
At each original arrow on such a path every root of the earlier factor has exactly one conjugate root of the later polynomial matching it to depth \(T\), and conversely.

Indeed the [local derivative bound](Yin_C_G0901_v212.md#local) applies to any such pair by the same argument, with the full separable polynomials in place of the rational minima: the valuations of the remaining pairwise differences are nonnegative.
If the embedded local fields differed, the local compositum of degree \(w\ge u\) would have \(wT\le2C\).
Thus the embedded fields coincide and both factors have degree \(u\).
For any fixed root \(y\) of the first factor, every matching factor provides a root \(b\) close to \(2/y\), by extending the embedding carrying the first root to \(y\).
If two distinct roots \(b,b'\) did so, then \(v_p(g'(b))\ge T\) and all \(u\) conjugates of \(b\) give the same derivative valuation.
Their product in the discriminant would give \(c_g\ge uT\), a contradiction.
The symmetric argument applies with \(f\).
Thus the original arrows form a matching on these vertices and their reflected arrows also form a matching.
Each vertex has in-degree and out-degree at most one.
The grading forbids a directed cycle, proving the path assertion.

## Which individual trace fields can be nonsplit?

On the [shrinking adjacent Salem rows](Yin_C_G0901_v212.md#records), apply the construction to the rational minima of \(Y=A^a\) and \(B\) at the prescribed second exponent below two.
Let \(n=[\mathbb Q(A,B):\mathbb Q(A)]\).
Above a fixed finite prime set the total normalized norm mass at places where *both* individual Salem quadratic extensions are nonsplit over their own trace fields tends to zero.
More precisely it is \(O_S(\sqrt{\log(2d)\log(2n)/n})\), with \(D=\deg A<d=\deg B\).

For either coordinate \(x=Y,B\), its local factor is fixed by inversion exactly when \(\mathbb Q_p(x)/\mathbb Q_p(x+x^{-1})\) has degree two.
Indeed the two roots of the trace quadratic are distinct and each is contained in \(\mathbb Q_p(x)\); an embedding taking \(x\) to \(x^{-1}\) then fixes its trace.
Positive powers of a Salem number generate its field, and the traces of \(A^a\) and \(A\) generate the same fixed field of the inversion involution.
The global and hence the embedded local coordinate and trace fields are therefore unchanged on passing to \(Y\).
At a place where both quadratics are nonsplit the two incident factors would be self-reciprocal.
By the first section its depth is at most \(T_0\).
The prime two gives no mass since \(YB\) is a unit there.
Apply the [capped-depth mass inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v208.md#cost) with these \(O_S(\log(2d))\) caps and \(U=O(\log(2n)/n)\) as in its [record application](Yin_C_G0901_v208.md#records).
The adjunction lower bound there and in the [local field dichotomy](Yin_C_G0901_v212.md#records) makes the displayed bound tend to zero.
This concerns splitting over the two individual trace fields and imposes the incidence and matching restrictions even when their completions happen to agree.
Concentration on the surviving directed paths still needs to be decided.
