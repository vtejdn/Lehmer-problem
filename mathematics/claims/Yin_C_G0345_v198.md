# A unique noncyclotomic factor for widely separated products of prime binomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Factorization with independently varying exponents

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\).
For positive integers \(u_1,\ldots,u_k,n\) and either sign put
\[
 A(X)=\prod_{i=1}^k(X^{u_i}-p_i),\qquad
 P(X)=X^n A(X)\pm X^{D}A(1/X),\qquad
 D=\sum_i u_i,\quad W=D+1,\quad g=\gcd(u_1,\ldots,u_k).
\]
There are constants \(C,c>0\) depending only on the primes such that if \(n\ge C W^9\) and \(g=1\), then \(P\) has exactly one monic irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(S\).
This factor occurs once, and its degree and logarithmic Mahler measure satisfy
\[
       0\le n+D-\deg S\le10D,\qquad
       \left|m(S)-\sum_i\log p_i\right|\le C W\exp(-cn/W^2).          \tag{1}
\]
The block exponents need not be comparable.
In particular this is an individual-factor Mahler gap with no restriction on the number of exterior roots.

For arbitrary \(g\) and the same range \(n\ge CW^9\), every noncyclotomic irreducible factor is simple.
The factors are indexed by the parts of a partition of \(g\) into positive integers, including repeated part sizes when they occur.
The factor with part \(s\) contains exactly \(s u_i/g\) exterior roots near the circle of radius \(p_i^{1/u_i}\), for each \(i\), and satisfies
\[
             m(S)=\frac{s}{g}\sum_i\log p_i+O(W\exp(-cn/W^2)).         \tag{2}
\]
The neighborhoods are the unions of disks specified below.
The total degree of cyclotomic factors, counted with multiplicity, is at most \(10D\) also for arbitrary \(g\).
No assertion of a uniform gap for unbounded \(g\) follows from (2).

<a id="disks"></a>
## Each exterior root lies in its own disk

Write \(q_i=X^{u_i}-p_i\) and \(A^*=X^DA(1/X)\).
The \(q_i\) are irreducible by Eisenstein, and all their zeros are simple and strictly exterior; the zeros of \(A^*\) are strictly interior.
Different \(q_i\) have distinct root radii, since \(p_i^{u_j}\ne p_j^{u_i}\).
The separation between any two different zeros of \(A\) is at least \(\exp(-C_1 W)\) for a constant depending only on the primes.
Indeed, roots on one circle have spacing at least \(2\sin(\pi/u_i)\) when \(u_i\ge2\).
Between different circles, the difference of the logarithms of their radii is at least \(1/(u_i u_j p^W)\), where \(p=\max_i p_i\): the two distinct integers \(p_i^{u_j},p_j^{u_i}\) differ by at least one and are at most \(p^W\).
The actual difference of radii is at least this difference of logarithms since the radii exceed one.

Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(c>0\), set \(\delta=\exp(-cn/W^2)\), and take the disk of radius \(\delta\) at each zero of \(A\).
For \(n\ge CW^9\) with \(C\) sufficiently large these disks are disjoint and their boundaries satisfy \(\log|z|\ge(\log2)/(2W)\), since every center has log modulus at least \((\log2)/W\).
There \(|A(z)|\ge\delta^D\) by the monic root product, while \(|A^*(z)|\le\exp(C_2W)\) because \(|z|\le p+1\) and \(\|A^*\|_1\le\prod_i(1+p_i)\).
Taking \(c<(\log2)/4\) gives \(|z^n A(z)|>|A^*(z)|\) on every boundary.
Rouché's theorem puts exactly one root of \(P\), counted with multiplicity, in each disk.
The [exterior count for a reciprocal pair of blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#allocation) is at most \(D\), so these are all the exterior roots.

For a root in the disk around a zero of \(q_i\), one has \(|q_i(z)|\le\delta\exp(C_3W)\) by its root product.
Every root of \(q_j\), \(j\ne i\), is at distance at least \(\tfrac12\exp(-C_1W)\) from \(z\), so \(|q_j(z)|\ge\exp(-C_4W^2)\).
After increasing \(C\), \(q_i\) is the unique minimizer of \(|q_j(z)|\).
Also \(\log|z|=(\log p_i)/u_i+O(\delta)\), uniformly over the disks.

<a id="integers"></a>
## Integer root counts determine the allocation

Let \(S\) be any monic irreducible factor with \(d=\deg S\), and let \(b_i\) count its exterior roots in the disks of \(q_i\).
The [moving-block allocation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#statement) applies because \(A,A^*\) are coprime and their coefficient norms are bounded in terms of the fixed primes.
The preceding disk estimates identify its assignment exactly, while \(m(q_i)=\log p_i\) and every exponent of \(q_i\) in \(A\) is one.
Multiplying that estimate by \(d/n\) and dividing by \(\log p_i\) gives
\[
 \left|\frac{b_i}{u_i}-\frac dn\right|
 \ll W\delta+\frac{W^{3/2}\sqrt{d\,\{W^2+\log(2d)\}}+W^4}{n}.
\]
All implied constants here and below may depend on the primes.
Since \(d\le n+D\le2n\), the right side is at most
\[
      C_5\left(W\delta+
         W^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{W^2+\log(4n)}n}+\frac{W^4}{n}\right).
\]
For \(n\ge CW^9\) this is less than \(1/(3W^2)\) on increasing \(C\).
Indeed \(\log(4n)/n\) decreases in this range, and after multiplication by \(W^2\) the middle term is \(O(\sqrt{(1+\log(4C))/C})\), uniformly in \(W\).
Two different rational numbers \(b_i/u_i,b_j/u_j\) differ by at least \(1/(u_i u_j)>1/W^2\).
All the fractions must therefore be equal, with common value \(s/g\) for an integer \(0\le s\le g\).

A noncyclotomic factor of this monic unit polynomial has an exterior root by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization), so \(s>0\) for each such factor.
Each exterior root of \(P\) is simple; hence each of its noncyclotomic factors occurs once.
There are exactly \(u_i\) exterior roots of \(P\) in the disks of \(q_i\).
Summing their counts over the noncyclotomic factors shows that the corresponding positive integers \(s\) sum to \(g\).
Summing their logarithmic moduli within a factor gives (2).
For \(g=1\) there is just one such factor, its measure differs from \(\sum_i\log p_i\) by \(O(W\delta)\), and increasing \(C\) makes this error smaller than half that sum uniformly.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## The degree occupied by roots of unity

We use the sign changes and squaring from [Beukers and Smyth's method for torsion points on curves](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area), keeping the sign fixed by this particular graph.
First suppose \(\gcd(n,u_1,\ldots,u_k)=1\).
Let \(r=A^*/A\); this rational function is finite and nonzero on the unit circle.
At a torsion root \(\zeta\) of \(P\) we have \(r(\zeta)=e\zeta^n\), with \(e\in\{1,-1\}\) fixed by the choice of \(P\).
Write \(m\) for its order.
When \(m\) is odd, a Galois automorphism takes \(\zeta\) to \(\zeta^2\).
When \(m=2M\) with \(M\) odd, the exponent \(M+2\) is coprime to \(m\) and takes \(\zeta\) to \(-\zeta^2\).
When \(4\mid m\), the exponent \(1+m/2\) is coprime to \(m\) and takes \(\zeta\) to \(-\zeta\).
Since \(r\) has rational coefficients, in the first two cases \(\zeta\) is a solution of the corresponding one of two equations
\[
             r(X^2)=e r(X)^2,\qquad r(-X^2)=e(-1)^n r(X)^2 .
\]
Neither is an identity: all finite poles of \(r\) are simple and nonzero, so those on the left are simple whereas the right has double poles.
Each equation has at most \(4D\) solutions after clearing denominators.

In the last case \(r(-\zeta)=(-1)^n r(\zeta)\).
If some \(u_i\) is odd, the negative of the positive pole \(p_i^{1/u_i}\) is not a pole, because its circle belongs only to \(q_i\).
Thus the equation \(r(-X)=(-1)^n r(X)\) is not an identity and has at most \(2D\) solutions.
If all \(u_i\) are even, the gcd assumption forces \(n\) odd, so \(r(-X)=r(X)\) contradicts the required sign at the nonzero value \(r(\zeta)\).
Altogether \(P\) has at most \(10D\) distinct torsion roots.

They are simple for \(n>3D\).
Indeed \(r=\prod_i r_i\), where \(r_i(z)=(z^{u_i}-p_i^{-1})/(1-z^{u_i}/p_i)\), and on the unit circle
\[
             \frac{z r_i'(z)}{r_i(z)}
             =\frac{u_i(p_i^2-1)}{|p_i-z^{u_i}|^2}\le3u_i .
\]
A multiple circle root would require \(n=z r'(z)/r(z)\le3D\) by differentiation of \(z^n\mp r(z)\).
For arbitrary common divisor \(h=\gcd(n,u_1,\ldots,u_k)\), the polynomial is \(P_0(X^h)\) with all exponents divided by \(h\) in \(P_0\).
The coprime argument bounds the torsion roots of \(P_0\) by \(10D/h\), all simple since \(n/h>3D/h\).
Their \(h\)-th roots are distinct, simple and account for every torsion root of \(P\).
This proves the claimed total degree and completes (1).
