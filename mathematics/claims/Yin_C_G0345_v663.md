# Angular spacing improves the energy bound for integral numerators

<a id="statement"></a>
## Short and unrestricted widths

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|P(0)|=1\), no root on the unit circle and \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
All circle norms and integrals use Haar probability.
Let \(\ell_0>2\) be the unique solution of
\[
             (\ell_0-2)\sqrt{\ell_0}=\frac{2}{9\pi},
             \qquad r_0=\frac{\sqrt{\ell_0}}2 .
\]
Along every sequence with \(m\to0\), any nonzero integral Laurent numerator \(T\) of width less than \(d\) and any integral Laurent numerator \(A\) indivisible by \(P\) satisfy
\[
 \liminf m\|T/P\|_2^2\ge\ell_0/4=0.5123527\ldots,\qquad
 \liminf m\|A/P\|_2^2\ge r_0^2+2\log(2r_0)-4r_0+2=0.3667523\ldots.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular the earlier short threshold \(1/2\) cannot occur, for any distribution of the root angles.
These bounds place a necessary energy cost on numerators for inputs of small measure.
They leave the existence of such inputs open.

<a id="envelope"></a>
## A common lower estimate for the moduli of short quotients

Put \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\) and \(L=\min_{0\ne T\in\mathbb Z[X],\,\deg T<d}\|T/P\|_2^2\).
The [integral packing estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#packing) gives \(\liminf L/C\ge2\), and \(mC\to1/4\).
It suffices to prove the short bound on an arbitrary subsequence with \(l=L/C\to\ell\le2.05\), since \(2.049<\ell_0<2.05\) by direct substitution.
Let \(S_*\) be the [short complex minimizer with constant one](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation).
Use the functions
\[
         s=\frac{S_*}{P\sqrt C},\qquad H_T=\frac{T}{P\sqrt C},
         \qquad e_T=\|H_T\|_2^2 .
\]
In particular \(\|s\|_2=1\), and \(s\) is orthogonal to every normalized short quotient whose numerator has constant zero.
We claim that for every nonzero short integral numerator
\[
          \big\|(|s|-|H_T|)_+\big\|_2^2\le e_T-1-l/2+o(1),            \tag{2}
\]
with a uniform error on this subsequence.
Translate the least exponent of \(T\) to zero, preserving its boundary modulus.
When \(e_T\ge7/2\) the left side is at most one, which proves (2) for all sufficiently late inputs in this range.

For \(e_T<7/2\), take \(N=\lfloor C^{1/3}\rfloor\) and use the [successive divisions](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#remainders).
They give occupied Taylor positions \(u_i\), digits \(q_i\), and pairwise distinct short integral polynomials \(S_i\) normalized to have positive constant, for \(0\le i<N\).
The maximum of their normalized norms is at most \(\sqrt{e_T}/(1-\sqrt{(N-1)/C})\), by the triangle inequality in that division estimate with starting numerator \(T\).
Since evaluation gives \(q_i^2\le\|H_{S_i}\|_2^2\), all \(q_i\) are signed units eventually, uniformly in \(T\) in this range.
Then \(S_i(0)=1\) for every \(i\).
The stripping identity gives a unit phase function \(x_i=\operatorname{sign}(P(0)q_i)X^{-u_i}\) such that
\[
           \|H_{S_i}-H_T x_i\|_2\le\sqrt{N/C}=o(1),                   \tag{3}
\]
because the discarded prefix has at most \(N\) distinct unit coefficients.

Write \(B=N^{-1}\sum_i H_{S_i}\).
Each \(H_{S_i}-s\) is orthogonal to \(s\), and every distinct pair is at squared distance at least \(l\).
The variance identity gives
\[
 \|B-s\|_2^2
   =\frac1N\sum_i\|H_{S_i}-s\|_2^2
       -\frac1{2N^2}\sum_{i,j}\|H_{S_i}-H_{S_j}\|_2^2
   \le e_T-1-\frac{N-1}{2N}l+o(1).
\]
By (3), \(B\) differs in norm by \(o(1)\) from \(H_T N^{-1}\sum_i x_i\), whose modulus is bounded pointwise by \(|H_T|\).
The triangle inequality now gives (2).
All errors above depend only on \(C\) and the fixed bound \(7/2\).

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Distances between the phases of stripped minimizers

Take \(T\) attaining \(L\), translated to have nonzero constant.
Write \(H=H_T\) and construct \(S_i,x_i,B\) as above, so that \(e_T=l\).
For \(i\ne j\) write \(H_{ij}=H_{S_i-S_j}\).
This is a nonzero short integral numerator divided by \(P\sqrt C\).
Let \(\mathbb E\) denote the uniform average over ordered distinct pairs.
Since \(\|B\|_2^2=1+\|B-s\|_2^2\ge1\), the variance identity and (3) give
\[
      \mathbb E\|H_{ij}\|_2^2
       =\frac{2N}{N-1}\left(\frac1N\sum_i\|H_{S_i}\|_2^2-\|B\|_2^2\right)
       \le 2l-2+o(1).
\]
Apply (2) to the differences, shifting their lowest exponent when necessary.
By (3), \(\|H_{ij}-H(x_i-x_j)\|_2=o(1)\) uniformly over the pairs.
The map \(u\mapsto(|s|-|u|)_+\) is a contraction in \(L^2\), and its outputs have norm at most one.
Consequently
\[
      \mathbb E\int (|s|-|H|\,|x_i-x_j|)_+^2
                     \le \tfrac32(l-2)+o(1).                          \tag{4}
\]

Fix a point \(z\) of the circle and list the \(N\) angles of \(x_i(z)\) in cyclic order, retaining repetitions.
Extend their ordered real representatives by \(\theta_{j+N}=\theta_j+2\pi\).
For each \(1\le k<N\), the \(N\) differences \(\theta_{j+k}-\theta_j\) all lie in \([0,2\pi]\) and have mean \(2\pi k/N\).
On this interval the function \(t\mapsto(|s(z)|-2|H(z)|\sin(t/2))_+^2\) is convex, since \(\sin(t/2)\) is concave and the squared positive part is convex and nondecreasing.
Jensen's inequality over \(j\), and then averaging over \(k\), gives a pointwise lower bound in (4) by the average at the equally spaced angles \(2\pi k/N\).
For any \(a\ge0\), the inequality \(|s|\le (|s|-a|H|)_++a|H|\) gives \(\|(|s|-a|H|)_+\|_2\ge(1-a\sqrt l)_+\).
After integration and passage to the Riemann integral, (4) yields
\[
 \tfrac32(\ell-2)\ \ge\ \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
        (1-2\sqrt\ell\sin(t/2))_+^2\,dt
 \ \ge\ \frac1\pi\int_0^{1/\sqrt\ell}(1-\sqrt\ell\,t)^2\,dt
 =\frac1{3\pi\sqrt\ell}.                                               \tag{5}
\]
The last comparison uses symmetry and \(2\sin(t/2)\le t\) on \([0,\pi]\).
The integrands for \(l\to\ell\) converge uniformly, which justifies the moving Riemann sums.
Monotonicity of \((\ell-2)\sqrt\ell\) on \([2,\infty)\) proves the first assertion of (1).

<a id="unrestricted"></a>
## Using the improved short bound in a balanced division

Let \(B_0=r_0^2+2\log(2r_0)-4r_0+2\), and suppose along a subsequence that \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to F<B_0\).
Set \(a=\tfrac12\log(7/3)\).
We will use \(B_0<0.367\) and \(2a-4(1-e^{-a})^2>0.370\).
For example \(2.049<\ell_0<2.05\) gives \(r_0<0.716\), and \(u^2+2\log(2u)-4u+2\) increases for \(u>0\) since its derivative is \(2(u-1)^2/u\).
The displayed rational comparisons follow using \(1.5275<\sqrt{7/3}<1.5276\) and the first six terms of \(\log v=2\sum_{j\ge0} t^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\), \(t=(v-1)/(v+1)\), with tail at most \(2t^{13}/[13(1-t^2)]\) for \(v>1\).

In the [balanced division](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#division) write \(X^{-j}A/P=Q+R/P\) with \(R\ne0\) short and \(n=\max(n_-,n_+)\) the larger of the two occupied counts.
The [weighted prefix argument](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#localization) applies through \(\lfloor a/m\rfloor\) since \(e^{-a}>1/2\).
If \(n>\lfloor a/m\rfloor\) infinitely often, that argument gives \(F\ge2a-4(1-e^{-a})^2\), contradicting \(F<B_0\).
With \(n\le\lfloor a/m\rfloor\), take convergent further subsequences for the bounded quantities in the [complementary projection estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#defect).
Write \(x=\lim mn\), \(B_Q=\lim m\|Q\|_2^2\), \(r=\lim\sqrt m\|R/P\|_2\), and \(w\) for the limiting scaled norm of the complementary projection of the quotient.
The prefix recursion in that estimate requires \(2(e^{2a}-1)F<1\), which holds since \(e^{2a}-1=4/3\) and \(F<0.367\).
The same determinant, row and orthogonality inequalities therefore give
\[
 B_Q\ge2x,\qquad w^2\le(4/3)F,\qquad
 w\le2(1-e^{-x})+\tfrac{\sqrt2}3(B_Q-2x),\qquad
 F\ge r^2+B_Q-2rw .
\]
By the short bound \(r\ge r_0\), whereas \(w^2<(4/3)0.367<1/2<r_0^2\).
Thus the last expression increases with \(r\ge r_0\).
Substitution and \(1-2\sqrt2\,r_0/3>0\) give
\[
 F\ge r_0^2+2x-4r_0(1-e^{-x}).
\]
The minimum over real \(x\) occurs at \(x=\log(2r_0)\) and equals \(B_0\), a contradiction.
This proves the second assertion of (1), including reducible denominators and repeated roots.
