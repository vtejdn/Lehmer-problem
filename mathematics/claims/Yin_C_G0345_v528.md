# Few monomial shifts cannot certify a small primitive output

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coefficient cost and the number of shifts in a rational identity

Let \(U,V,P,e,h,k,L,K,w,g,\Sigma,j,b,\mathcal N,T\) be as in the [dilated odd-exponent test](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#statement) and its [retained periods](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#width).
Write \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
Take any nonzero combination \(F U+G V\) with rational Laurent polynomial multipliers.
Let \(s_U,s_V\) count the nonzero terms of \(F,G\), respectively, and put
\[
        J=s_U\log\|U\|_2+s_V\log\|V\|_2 .
\]
Multiply the combination by a rational scalar and a monomial so that the resulting \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) has primitive coefficients and nonzero constant term.
Its degree \(d\) is the exponent span of the output before normalization.
The input shifts can be arbitrarily large in either direction.
When \(d<\min_{n\in\mathcal N}\varphi(n)\), one has absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
       m(A)+C\frac{d\log(2L)}{\sqrt L}+C\frac{wJ}{T}
                           \ \ge\ c k g w h/e.                         \tag{1}
\]
Consequently at any output span \(d<\min\varphi(n)\), every representation with \(J\) at most a sufficiently small absolute constant times \(kghT/e\) forces \(m(A)\gg kgwh/e\) for large \(L\).
Indeed \(d\le4L\) and \(\sqrt L\log L=o(w/K)\), while \(h/e\gg j/K\).

For a nonzero monomial output an exact inequality holds,
\[
                 eJ\ \ge\ k\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log D_{gn},          \tag{2}
\]
where \(D_{gn}\) is the part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_{gn})|\) prime to \(gn\).
Every rational Laurent Bézout identity for \(U,V\) therefore needs
\[
         s_U+s_V\ \gg\ \frac{kghT}{e\log H}
                           \ \gg\ \frac{kj\Sigma}{\log H}.             \tag{3}
\]
This bounds the number of nonzero multiplier coefficients even if their degrees, denominators and magnitudes are unrestricted.

<a id="minor"></a>
## An integer multiple controlled by the selected columns

After the output shift, use as columns the coefficient vectors of the selected translates of \(U,V\).
Index rows by the finite union of their supports; each column is a translate of one input and keeps its coefficient Euclidean norm.
The primitive integer vector \(A\) belongs to the rational column span.
Choose linearly independent columns spanning that space and a nonsingular square minor on those columns with integer determinant \(\Delta\).
Solving the equations on the selected rows by Cramer's rule shows that \(C_A A\), \(C_A=|\Delta|\), lies in the integer span of these same translates.
The solution on the selected rows is the unique rational solution for these independent columns, so it holds on all rows.
Hadamard's inequality gives \(\log C_A\le J\), since each retained column occurs once and each nonzero input has norm at least one.
This conclusion includes dependent columns and gaps of arbitrary length in the exponents.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Valuations transferred to an output of prescribed span

At any lifted component \(\xi_i\) and any of its selected coprime first exponents \(p\), both inputs at \(\xi_i^{gp}\) are multiples of \(P(\xi_i)^k\) by the [sum-period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement).
Their Laurent shifts take unit multipliers there.
Thus \(C_A A(\xi_i^{gp})\) has valuation at least \(kt_i\), where \(t_i=v_\ell(P(\xi_i))\).
With \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_n,A)|>0\), the [weighted component count](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#mass) gives, exactly as for a scalar content,
\[
     e\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log R_n
       \ \ge\ c_0w\left(k\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log D_{gn}-e\log C_A\right).
\]
The bound uses only integrality of the multiplier, independently of the particular representation of \(A\).
The [circle estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#circle) gives
\(\sum_n\log R_n\le Tm(A)+O(Td\log(2L)/\sqrt L)\).
The width calibration gives \(\sum_n\log D_{gn}\gg hgT\), proving (1).

For a monomial output \(A=\pm1\), all the \(R_n\) equal one, giving (2).
Now \(J\le(s_U+s_V)\log H\), \(T\gg K\Sigma/g\), and \(Kh/e\gg j\), proving (3).
The same minor argument applies whenever an independently obtained divisor bounds the integer needed to represent a monomial by Laurent shifts.
In particular, for the [undilated full prime interval](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#remove), the retained divisor lower bound there applies to every integer represented by the Laurent rows.
Indeed the common evaluation at each witnessing root of unity is integral and its powers are units, as in the [order-layer proof](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#layers).
It follows there too that \(s_U+s_V\gg kj\Sigma/\log H\).
