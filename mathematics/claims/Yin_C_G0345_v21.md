# A fixed rational prefix without interior roots forces positive Pisot excess

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary rational coefficients and degree

Fix a polynomial \(S\in\mathbb Q[w]\) with \(S(0)=1\), no root in the open unit disk and at least one nonintegral coefficient.
Fix \(M<\infty\) and \(\eta>0\).
Consider positive-constant Pisot inputs with branch index \(j\ge1\), constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\) and normalized trace lift \(\Pi/j\) of trace degree \(h\), as in the [varying-branch theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
Suppose \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M\) on the unit circle and its coefficients through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) agree with \(G(z)S(z^g)\), where \(1\le g\le h\) and \(G\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]]\).
Then \(\theta-q\ge c(S,M,\eta)>0\), uniformly in \(j,q,g,h,G\).
The rational polynomial may have noncyclotomic factors with all roots on the unit circle.
Examples include \(S(w)=1+w/a+w^2\) for every fixed integer \(a\ge2\); its circle roots have trace \(-1/a\), which cannot be the trace of a root of unity.

<a id="seam"></a>
## A nonintegral rational coefficient forces an initial interval of zeros

Write \(P=mS\) primitive in \(\mathbb Z[w]\), so \(m=P(0)\ge2\) is the least common denominator.
Assume towards contradiction that \(\theta-q\to0\).
The [inverse-measure bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) give \(h\to\infty\), a real symmetric signed measure of mass one with negative mass tending to zero, and its Herglotz series \(J_i\) with \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_i\) a monic integer denominator of degree \(O(h)\) and Mahler measure tending to one strictly from above.
The normalized numerator is integral below \(g\) wherever prefix agreement applies, so the [sublinear first nondivisible index](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) gives \(g=o(h)\).
The series \(X=B^\sharp/G\) is integral and agrees with \(S(z^g)J_i\) through the stated prefix.

Let \(a/b\) in lowest terms, \(b\ge2\), be the first nonintegral coefficient of \(S\), at index \(l\).
Up to index \(lg-1\) the coefficients of \(J_i\) are integers by recursive division.
When \(g\to\infty\), test at positive indices \(t'=o(g)\) and pass to a joint limit on \((v^{t'},v^{lg})\) of the inverse measures.
Write \(M(r,k)\) for its doubled moments as in the [half-coefficient calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#seam).
The earlier integral offsets contribute only integral coefficients at indices \(lg+rt'\), since the offsets differ by at least \(g\) and all earlier inverse-series indices used there are strictly between zero and \(lg\).
Thus \(M(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\) for \(r>0\), and
\[
 M(r,1)\in\mathbb Z\ (r<0),\qquad M(0,1)+a/b\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 M(r,1)+(a/b)M(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\ (r>0).
\]
The integral sequence \(bM(r,1)\) is Fourier–Stieltjes, hence periodic outside a finite set with the same periodic tail on both sides by the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes).
The negative tail consists of multiples of \(b\), implying \(b\mid M(r,0)\) for all large positive \(r\).
A purely atomic marginal is impossible as in the [integer marginal classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar): its finitely many torsion atoms would make \(M(r,1)\) periodic, conflicting with its value at zero.
The same classification of a positive-real series with integral coefficients leaves \(1,\ 1\pm w^u,\ 1/(1\pm w^u)\) when a continuous part is present.
The reciprocal binomials violate eventual divisibility by \(b\).
For a binomial \(1+\delta w^u\), \(\delta=\pm1\), the integer Fourier sequence \(bM(r,1)\) gives an integer Laurent density \(H\), bounded in modulus by \(b|1+\delta w^u|^2\) by domination by the first marginal.
Hence \((1+\delta w^u)^2\mid H\) over the integers, whereas modulo any prime dividing \(b\) its coefficients give \(H\equiv-a(1+\delta w^u)\).
This is impossible since \(a\) is a unit in that residue field.
Only the Haar marginal remains, so the integer coefficient of \(J_i\) at \(t'\) is eventually zero.
By choosing a violating index if necessary, all positive coefficients up to \(\kappa g\) vanish eventually on the sequence for some \(\kappa>0\).
On intervals of that length the signed Gram matrix of the characters is the identity, and the [Bessel estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) with the finitely many bounded partial coefficient multipliers of \(2S\) bounds the number of nonzero integer coefficients of \(X\) through \(C g\) for each fixed \(C\).
Their individual sizes are bounded by the moment bounds.
If \(g\) is bounded, the same local absolute-sum bound follows directly.

<a id="rows"></a>
## Removing the absolutely continuous mixed rows

Use the [fixed-pair Fourier argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#fixed) on the full polynomial \(P\) with the following normalization.
For the marginal of \(w=\bar v^g\) in the limit, let \(J\) be its constant-one Herglotz transform.
The coefficients of \(SJ\) are integers by testing \(X\) at the multiples of \(g\), so the marginal cannot be Haar.
For \(g/t,t/h\to0\) take a joint limit \(\Omega\) with \(u=v^t\).
At every positive harmonic \(k\), projection of \(2S(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) has integer Fourier coefficients, by testing the positive coefficients of \(X\) at \(kt+rg\), \(r\in\mathbb Z\).
On the zero layer \(2|P|^2\) times the marginal has integer coefficients by \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\) and integrality of \(SJ\).
On positive layers multiply the integer coefficients just obtained by \(mP^*\); negative layers follow by reflection.
Thus \(2|P|^2\Omega\) has integer Fourier transform.
Its coset decomposition gives finitely many torsion translates of subtori with trigonometric polynomial densities, and each projected mixed row has integer Laurent polynomial as its absolutely continuous density.

Divide the positive decomposition by \(2|P|^2\) where \(P\ne0\).
Any extra measure at the zeros of \(P\), whether torsion or not, has zero contribution to the rows weighted by \(2S\).
For the proper components projecting over the full base, group their graph slopes as in the [separated-slope argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction).
On high common multiples of the slope denominators and translation orders, each slope gives a translated density \(2S f\) where \(f\) is its nonnegative marginal density.
The full-torus term vanishes at these high harmonics, and Riemann–Lebesgue separation of the slopes makes each Fourier coefficient of \(2S f\) integral.
Their decay then gives an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=2P f/m\).
Reality of \(H/P\) on the circle makes its possible nonzero poles reciprocal conjugate pairs.
Since \(P\) has no interior zero, any such pole lies on the circle, where a genuine pole is incompatible with integrability of \(f\).
Thus \(H/P\) is Laurent polynomial, with rational coefficients by polynomial division.
Primitivity of \(P\) and Gauss's lemma imply \(H/P=2f/m\in\mathbb Z[w,w^{-1}]\).
For a nonzero slope density this would give \(\int f\ge m/2\).
When \(m>2\) this exceeds the total mass; when \(m=2\) it forces total mass one and an integral nonnegative Laurent probability density.
All its nonconstant Fourier coefficients then have modulus strictly below one by strict triangle inequality, forcing the already excluded Haar marginal.

Only the full-torus component contributes to the remaining absolutely continuous positive rows, and these vanish above its finite \(u\)-degree.
If there were a last nonzero row, its density \(H_k\) would be an integer Laurent polynomial with \(|H_k|\le|2S|f_0/2\), where \(f_0\) is the absolutely continuous marginal density.
Indeed the conditional measures at that harmonic have no nonzero higher multiples, and the power pushforward gives first harmonic at most \(1/2\) in modulus by positivity.
Jensen gives \(M(f_0)<1\), since its mass is at most one and equality would force the excluded Haar marginal; this includes zero geometric mean.
As \(M(S)=1\), this contradicts \(M(H_k)\ge1\).
All positive weighted rows are therefore supported on the finitely many torsion atoms of the marginal away from the zeros of \(P\).
These atoms depend only on the fixed limiting marginal, by the coset decomposition on its zero layer.
Choose a common multiple \(T\ge1\) of their orders and put \(V_i=1-z^{Tg}\).
On every intermediate scale the integer coefficient of \(V_i X\) vanishes eventually.
Diagonal extraction gives vanishing throughout \(Lg\le t\le h/L\) for a fixed \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\).

<a id="division"></a>
## Dividing a bounded sparse polynomial by a fixed noncyclotomic factor

Fix a primitive irreducible noncyclotomic polynomial \(E\in\mathbb Z[w]\) with \(E(0)\ne0\).
If integer polynomials \(H_i\) have bounded term counts and coefficient sizes and are divisible over the integers by \(E(z^{g_i})\), the quotients also have bounded term counts and coefficient sizes.
Let \(\xi\) be a root of \(E\).
There is a place with \(|\xi|>1\), since absence of any such place would make \(\xi\) an algebraic integer with all conjugates of modulus at most one and hence torsion by Kronecker.
Group the exponents of \(H_i\) by residue modulo \(g_i\); in each class the corresponding polynomial in \(w=z^{g_i}\) is divisible by \(E\), since a multiple of \(E(z^{g_i})\) has this property in every class.
Partition its terms at \(\xi\) into minimal nonempty vanishing blocks.
In each block \(n_1>\cdots>n_s\), the differences \(n_1-n_j\) are bounded successively.
Once the differences up to \(n_j\) are bounded, the nonzero normalized partial sums \(\sum_{l\le j}c_l\xi^{n_l-n_j}\), for \(j<s\), belong to a fixed finite set.
The norm of their product with \(\xi^{n_j}\) is bounded above by a constant times \(|\xi|^{n_{j+1}}\) using the remaining terms.
Their positive minimum modulus therefore bounds \(n_j-n_{j+1}\).
After a monomial is removed, each block polynomial belongs to a fixed finite set divisible by \(E\) over \(\mathbb Z[w]\) by primitivity.
Reassembling the resulting bounded number of shifts of fixed quotients proves the assertion, including cancellations.

<a id="return"></a>
## Recovering the denominator of the original input

Factor \(P=EF\) integrally, placing each irreducible factor having a circle root in \(E\), with its full multiplicity.
Such a factor shares a root with its reciprocal by complex conjugation and irreducibility, so all of its roots are on the circle by the absence of interior zeros of \(P\).
All roots of \(F\) are strictly exterior.
Truncate \(V_i X\) at \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to get \(Q_0\) of bounded absolute coefficient sum and degree \(O(g)\) by the local bound and intermediate vanishing.
Set \(E_i=E(z^g),\ F_i=F(z^g)/m\).
Then \(Q_0/F_i\) agrees with \(E_iV_i J_i\) through the cutoff.
The coefficients of \(1/F_i\) are uniformly absolutely summable by strict exteriority.
The positive coefficients of \(E_iV_i J_i\) have a bounded-variation representation: each of its finitely many shifts of the two-sided inverse moments requires correction only at the initial \(O(g)\) indices, at bounded total cost because \(J_i=X/S(z^g)\) in the prefix and only finitely many Taylor coefficients of \(1/S\) enter that range.
This is the [denominator cutoff argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#fixed-return), with \(X\) from the integral formal factor.
Apply it to the polynomial identity
\[
  E_iF_i V_i B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/j
       = F_i(\Pi/j)(E_i V_i J_i-Q_0/F_i).
\]
The two-cutoff estimate applies since the prefix has positive fractional length, the degree is \(O(h)\), the multipliers have bounded supremum norm and both positive-index representations have bounded variation.
Since \(\inf_{|z|=1}|F_i(z)|\) is bounded below, \(E_iV_i B^\sharp\) has bounded circle \(L^1\) norm.
The [integer support estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) gives bounded term count, and every coefficient is bounded by the same norm.
Remove all noncyclotomic factors of \(E_i\) one at a time using the preceding division result on their fixed factors before substitution.
The remaining integer polynomial has bounded support and Mahler measure \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, since the retained factors and \(V_i\) are cyclotomic up to sign.
The [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) excludes this sequence.
