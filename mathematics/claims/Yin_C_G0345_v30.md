# A uniform Mahler gap for Salem's construction at all delays at least two

<a id="statement"></a>
## Both signs and every Pisot seed

There is an absolute real \(c>0\) with the following property.
For every monic minimal polynomial \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of a Pisot number, every integer \(N\ge2\), and both signs,
\[
             M(z^N f\pm f^*)=1
       \quad\hbox{or}\quad
             M(z^N f\pm f^*)\ge1+c,\qquad f^*(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z).
\]
Here \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure.
The gap is qualitative and is uniform in all degrees and coefficients.
Consequently any sequence of Salem construction polynomials of strictly positive logarithmic Mahler measure tending to zero must have delay one eventually.
The [delay-one representation](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#closedness-and-delay-one) includes every Salem number, so the gap for Salem numbers themselves remains open.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## Exact contact at the first scale and moment approximation at the next

By the [earlier gap at larger delays](Yin_C_G0345_v27.md#statement) it suffices to exclude a delay-two sequence with \(1<M_i\to1\).
The [dyadic concentration theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v28.md#statement) then gives unit Pisot seeds, the matched sign \(z^2f+f(0)f^*\) and convergence of \(F=f(0)f/f^*\) to \(1/(1-2z)\).
The [first discrepancy](Yin_C_G0345_v29.md#statement) occurs at \(n\to\infty\) with sign \(a=\pm1\), which we fix on a subsequence.
Put \(p=n+1\) and \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2 F)\).
Its Taylor coefficients are integral, and it has a genuine interior pole at \(\tau=1/M_i\).
For the [positive measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v114.md#positive) \(\mu\) of each input, the integer coefficients approximate doubled moments uniformly through every fixed multiple of \(n\) at positive indices.
At zero the bilateral sequence takes the value two and at negative indices it uses reflection.
Extract the limiting law of \(v^p\) under \(\mu\), where \(v\) is the circle coordinate.
The [power-marginal classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v123.md#laws) gives arclength with density \(1+a\operatorname{Re}v\), or half arclength plus a half-atom at \(a\).

Their respective comparison germs are \(H=1+a z^p\) and \(H=(1-a z^p)^{-1}\).
On every fixed multiple of \(n\) one has [exact coefficient agreement](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v125.md#window) between \(K^0\) and \(H\).
At a moving index outside the multiples of \(p\), integer conditional moments in the density case either vanish or force a forbidden signed period.
In the half-atom case the atomic and continuous parts have integral moments separately, and a nonzero continuous moment forces concentration on two binomial root sets.
The [cyclotomic recurrence argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#annihilator) excludes this concentration.
Moving an index by a bounded multiple of \(p\) then identifies the atomic contribution using the initial zero block.

Both models \(H\) are analytic in the disk, whereas \(K^0\) has its interior pole.
Hence there is a first different Taylor coefficient at \(q\) and \(q/p\to\infty\).
The [Fejér estimate on this full prefix](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v124.md#fejer) gives \(M_i+M_i^{-1}-2=O(q^{-2})\) for both signs and both models.
Together with the original Gram bound on blocks of \(p-1\) moments, this gives [uniform integer approximation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v124.md#window) on every fixed multiple of \(q\), with error \(O_C(p^{-1/2})\).

<a id="exclusion"></a>
## The first different coefficient is impossible in both cases

Extract a joint limit of \((x,y)=(v^p,v^q)\).
For fixed \(r\in\mathbb Z\), the doubled mixed moment \(m_r=2\int x^r y\) is the integral limiting coefficient at \(q+rp\).
For \(r<0\) that index is positive and less than \(q\) eventually, so the model still supplies its value.
At \(r=0\) the actual coefficient differs from the model's coefficient.

In the [density case](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v128.md#continuous), projecting the measure weighted by \(2y\) to the \(x\)-circle gives an integer Laurent polynomial density bounded by \(|1+ax|^2\).
Dividing by the double zero at \(-a\) gives an integer Laurent polynomial of norm at most one.
The discrepancy forces it to be a signed monomial.
Thus \(y=c x^b\) almost surely with \(b\in\mathbb Z,\ c=\pm1\), forcing a single signed period of degree \(q-bp=O(q)\).
In the [half-atom case](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v128.md#mixed), the negative tail of \(m_r\) determines the entire atomic contribution.
The continuous component then has a conditional mean of circle norm at most one, integral Fourier coefficients and nonzero constant coefficient.
It equals a fixed sign \(c\), so the joint measure is supported on \((x-a)(y-c)=0\).
This gives concentration on a product of two signed binomials of degree \(p+q=O(q)\).

For either polynomial \(D\), the squared circle norm against the actual measures tends to zero.
Uniform integer approximation rounds the resulting convolution equations to an exact bilateral recurrence on every fixed multiple of \(q\).
The unit end coefficients and reciprocity of \(D\) extend its symmetric initial data to a rational comparison \(S\) with \(S(1/z)=-S(z)\) and poles only on the circle.
The [inverse interpolation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v127.md#inverse) matches the Taylor jet of the signed Pisot quotient long enough for finite Blaschke uniqueness to force \(K^0=S\).
The interior pole contradicts this identity.
Thus no such sequence exists, proving the gap at delay two and completing the all-delay assertion.
