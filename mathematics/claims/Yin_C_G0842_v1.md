## Statement

### A. A computable gap from a fixed finite reversal family

<a id="fixed-reversal-auxiliary-class"></a>
**Definition.** Fix an integer modulus m>=2 and a nonempty finite set Gset of monic polynomials in Z[x], with k=max_(G in Gset)deg G and M(G)<m for every G.
For k_G=deg G define G*(x)=x^(k_G)G(1/x), retaining this degree in the reversal even when G has zero constant coefficient.
The fixed-reversal auxiliary class consists of monic irreducible nontorsion integer-unit polynomials P of original degree D>=2 for which some G in Gset satisfies

    F_(D,G)=x^D G-G* in(P,m) subset Z[x].                (ST1)

Here(P,m) is the ideal generated by P and m over Z[x].
The class does not require P reciprocal, and its constant term can be either1 or-1.
Use total logarithmic Mahler measure m(P)=log M(P), natural logarithms and Haar probability throughout.
The modulus m is distinct from the function m(P).

There is a terminating algorithm from the finite input(m,Gset) to a positive rational g such that EVERY P in this class satisfies

    m(P)>g>0.                                          (ST2)

The polynomial F_(D,G) is nonzero of degree D+k_G, but its value at a root alpha of P may be zero.
Ideal membership makes F_(D,G)(alpha)/m integral, and the proof explicitly handles that zero-value case before using a nonzero norm.
All original conjugates, scalar denominators and actual original degrees remain in the estimates.

### B. The finite certificate and its complete costs

<a id="finite-logarithmic-reversal-certificate"></a>
**Definition.** For H_G(z,w)=wG(z)-G*(z), f_G=H_G/m and a real rational Laurent polynomial f, put f^dagger(z,w)=f(z^(-1),w^(-1)).
This dagger is conjugation on the torus and differs from the polynomial reversal G*.
Choose S=max(1,ceil(2 max_G||G||_1/m)), B=S^2+1 and at each integer r>=1 define

    delta_r=2^(-r), J_r=ceil(B*2^(3r)),
    Y_(G,r)=1-(f_G f_G^dagger+delta_r^2)/B,
    Phi_(G,r)=(log B)/2-(1/2)sum_(j=1..J_r)Y_(G,r)^j/j,
    c_(G,r)=CT_(z,w)Phi_(G,r).                         (ST3)

The constant c_(G,r) is(log B)/2 minus an exactly computable rational.
Rational upper intervals for log B give a terminating search for common r and positive rational eta<=1 with c_(G,r)<-2eta for every G.
No test for exact equality of an undecided real number is needed.
For the returned delta,J and EVERY D>kJ,

    CT_x Phi_(G,r)(x,x^D)=c_(G,r).                      (ST4)

This exact coefficient identity follows from the full finite bounds |a|<=kJ, |b|<=J on its two-variable Fourier exponents.
The case k=0 is included.

Its original degree is T=D+k_G, the full frequency cost is2^J, and the finite matrix interpretation has size2JT+1 if used.

For the accepted increasing angular function

    c0=log2/4, h0=c0 exp(-20),

    epsilon(v)=min(1,15sqrt(b(v))+4v), 0<v<=h0,

put K=2(k+1), C=1+k/2.
A terminating rational search chooses a dyadic u>0 with

These tests first exclude a vanishing auxiliary by C92 and then give the original nonzero-norm contradiction through C137 whenever m(P)<=u/C and D>kJ.

### D. Actual nonvacuity in every compatible degree

### FG2. A finite fixed reversal-auxiliary criterion

Fix an integer modulus m>=2 and a nonempty finite set Gset of monic polynomials G in Z[x], each of degree k_G<=k, with M(G)<m.
The nonnegative integer k and the entire set Gset are fixed independently of the original degree D.
Define reversal with the ACTUAL degree of G, including possible zero constant coefficient:

    G*(x)=x^(k_G)G(1/x),
    F_(D,G)(x)=x^D G(x)-G*(x).                         (FG1)

Let P range over monic irreducible nontorsion integer-unit polynomials of degree D>=2 such that, for some G in Gset,

    F_(D,G) in(P,m) subset Z[x].                       (FG2)

Then this class has a positive uniform total logarithmic Mahler gap.
The gap depends only on m and Gset, not on D, the coefficients of P or the chosen member G.
Reciprocity is not required for this intermediate criterion.

Indeed, write F_(D,G)=A P+mB with integral A,B.
At a root alpha of P, F_(D,G)(alpha)/m=B(alpha) is an algebraic integer, with zero allowed.
The auxiliary is a NONZERO polynomial of degree D+k_G: its leading coefficient is1 and G* has degree at most k_G<D+k_G.
Possible overlap of the two coefficient blocks does not increase either support or length, so

    #supp F_(D,G)<=2(k+1),
    ||F_(D,G)||_1<=2 max_(G in Gset)||G||_1,
    1<=deg F_(D,G)<=D+k<=(1+k/2)D.                    (FG3)

Let h=log m-max_(G in Gset)log M(G)>0 and eta=min(1,h/2).
The following torus-limit proof gives limsup_(D->infinity)m(F_(D,G))<=log M(G) for each fixed G.
Since the set is finite and eta<h, there is one D_0>=2 such that for all D>=D_0 and all its members,

    m(F_(D,G))-log m<-eta.                             (FG4)

Take K=2(k+1), S=max(1,ceil(2 max||G||_1/m)) and C=1+k/2 in C140.
For D>=D_0, equations FG2--FG4 verify every hypothesis of its final theorem, including the branch F_(D,G)(alpha)=0.
It supplies a positive lower bound depending only on these fixed parameters.

For the remaining degrees2<=D<D_0, restrict first to M(P)<=2.
The accepted coefficient estimate |a_j|<=binom(D,j)M(P) gives a finite set of integral monic polynomials in this bounded degree range.
Each allowed polynomial has M(P)>1 by the exact Kronecker characterization, because it is irreducible, noncyclotomic and has unit constant term.
The finite set therefore has a positive minimum of log M(P) if it is nonempty.
Taking the minimum of that number, log2 and the C140 lower bound handles every degree; an empty finite-set minimum is interpreted as infinity.
This is a qualitative uniform positive gap, not a claimed numerical value or an explicit D_0.

### FG3. The sufficient one-sided torus limit, with zeros retained

Fix one real integral G as in FG2 and put

    H(z,w)=wG(z)-G*(z),
    nu_D=(theta ->(exp(2pi i theta),exp(2pi i D theta)))_*dtheta.

The polynomial H is nonzero, and F_(D,G)(z)=H(z,z^D).
For each integer character(r,s) of T^2,

    integral z^r w^s dnu_D
       =1 if r+D s=0, and0 otherwise.                 (FG5)

For every fixed nonzero(r,s), the first case occurs for at most one positive D when s!=0 and never when s=0.
Thus these integrals converge to the Haar character integrals.
Finite trigonometric-polynomial approximation gives nu_D converging weakly to Haar probability lambda_2 on T^2.
One direct justification is convolution of a continuous test with the two-variable product Fejer kernels: positivity, total mass1 and concentration at the identity give uniform convergence by uniform continuity, and each convolution is a finite trigonometric polynomial to which FG5 applies.
This is the standard one-sided Boyd/Lawton specialization mechanism, not a new equidistribution theorem or an algebraic-orbit claim.

For T>0 define phi_T=max(log|H|,-T), assigning value -T at zeros of H.
It is continuous and bounded on T^2, because it equals log(max(|H|,exp(-T))).
The nonzero one-variable polynomial F_(D,G) has integrable logarithm, including its circle zeros.
Consequently

    m(F_(D,G))<=integral phi_T dnu_D,
    limsup_D m(F_(D,G))<=integral phi_T dlambda_2.       (FG6)

On |z|=1, real coefficients imply G*(z)=z^(k_G)conjugate(G(z)), hence |G*(z)|=|G(z)|.
Except at finitely many z, write u(z)=G*(z)/G(z), so |u(z)|=1 and

    log|H(z,w)|=log|G(z)|+log|w-u(z)|.

The first summand is integrable in z by one-variable Jensen.
The absolute integral in w of the second is finite and independent of u(z), by rotation invariance and the ordinary integrability of log|w-1|.
Thus Fubini is legitimate and scalar Jensen in w gives

    integral_(T^2)log|H| dlambda_2
       =integral_T log|G| dlambda_1=log M(G).          (FG7)

The nonnegative bound B=log(2||G||_1) for log|H| makes B-phi_T increase monotonically to B-log|H|.
Monotone convergence and FG7 therefore allow T to tend to infinity in FG6, proving

    limsup_(D->infinity)m(F_(D,G))<=log M(G).           (FG8)

There is no direct application of weak convergence to a singular logarithm, no exchange of the D and T limits, and no uniform near-zero estimate asserted.
The full equality limit would also follow from the accepted Lawton statement, but the upper inequality alone supplies FG4.

### FG4. All reciprocal period-three constraints and the exact endpoint identity

Fix m>=2 and residues r_0,r_1,r_2 modulo m.
Let

    P(x)=x^D+sum_(j=1..D-1)a_j x^j+1

be monic, reciprocal, irreducible over Q and noncyclotomic, with a_j congruent to r_(j mod3) modulo m.
All degrees are even: an odd-degree reciprocal polynomial vanishes at -1 and cannot be irreducible noncyclotomic.
For D>=4 all three residue classes occur among the inner indices.
Reciprocity forces r_(j mod3)=r_((D-j)mod3), and therefore exactly the following necessary equalities:

| D modulo3 | Forced equality | Exact integral comparison lifts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | r_1=r_2 | c_0=A, c_1=c_2=B |
| 1 | r_0=r_1 | c_0=c_1=A, c_2=B |
| 2 | r_0=r_2 | c_0=c_2=A, c_1=B |

A class with a failed necessary equality contains no such P of that degree.
For D=4, j=1 and j=3 force precisely r_1=r_0 and j=2 is fixed; no absent inner coefficient is used.
For D=2 only j=1 occurs, and no constraint on unused residues is imposed; FG6 handles that case separately.

Choose lifts as in FG5, and put

    Q_D(x)=x^D+sum_(j=1..D-1)c_(j mod3)x^j+1,
    G(x)=x^3+c_1 x^2+c_2 x+(c_0-1),
    G*(x)=1+c_1 x+c_2 x^2+(c_0-1)x^3.                (FG9)

The exact lift equalities make Q_D reciprocal and Q_D congruent to P modulo m.
For every D>=4,

    (x^3-1)Q_D=x^D G-G*.                              (FG10)

To check every endpoint, the coefficients at powers0,1,2,3 on the left are -1,-c_1,-c_2,1-c_0.
All coefficients from4 through D-1 cancel by period three, with an empty interval allowed.
The coefficients at powers D,D+1,D+2,D+3 are c_0-1,c_2,c_1,1, since q_(D-j)=q_j for j=1,2,3.
The low and high ranges are disjoint already at D=4.
These are exactly the two coefficient blocks on the right; G* uses degree-three reversal even when c_0-1=0 lowers its ordinary degree.
Writing P=Q_D+mR, with integral R of degree below D, gives

    F_D=x^D G-G*=(x^3-1)P-m(x^3-1)R in(P,m).          (FG11)

The case R=0 is permitted; it is handled by C140's zero-value branch, not excluded by a resultant assumption or a second lift.

### FG5. A fixed finite cubic lift set with M(G)<m

First assume m>=3.
When D=0 modulo3, choose B congruent to r_1=r_2 with |B|<=m/2 and E congruent to r_0-1 with |E|<=m/2, then set A=E+1.
The coefficient energy of G is

    ||G||_2^2=1+2B^2+E^2<=1+3m^2/4<m^2.              (FG12)

When D=1 modulo3, choose A congruent to r_0=r_1 minimizing |A-1/2|, and B congruent to r_2 minimizing |B|.
The lattice spacings give |A-1/2|<=m/2 and |B|<=m/2, including ties.
When D=2 modulo3 make the same choices for A congruent to r_0=r_2 and B congruent to r_1.
The two cases merely interchange the x and x^2 coefficients, and both satisfy

    ||G||_2^2=1+A^2+(A-1)^2+B^2
              =3/2+2(A-1/2)^2+B^2
              <=3/2+3m^2/4<m^2.                     (FG13)

Parseval and Jensen give M(G)<=||G||_2, so FG12--FG13 prove M(G)<m.

For m=2 and D=0 modulo3 choose B in{0,1} in its residue class and E in{0,-1} in its required residue class, again with A=E+1.
Then ||G||_2^2=1+2B^2+E^2<=4.
If it is strictly below4, M(G)<2 follows immediately.
Equality4 requires B=1 and E=-1, so G=x^3+x^2+x-1 is not a monomial.
For any nonmonomial polynomial G of degree s, |G|^2 is nonconstant on the circle: if ell is its smallest nonzero coefficient index, its Fourier coefficient at the difference s-ell is the nonzero product of the leading and smallest coefficients.
Strict concavity of log, with the usual integrable logarithmic zeros, therefore makes Jensen strict, yielding M(G)<||G||_2=2 in this equality case as well.
A monomial G here has M(G)=1.

For m=2 and D=1 or2 modulo3 choose A and B in{0,1} in their required residue classes.
Then A^2+(A-1)^2=1 and ||G||_2^2=2+B^2<=3<4, proving M(G)<2.
These choices do not change any congruence or reciprocal equality.

Choose one permitted representative at each tie once and for all.
There are at most3m^2 labeled choices over all compatible residue pairs and the three degree classes, hence a fixed finite cubic set Gset_m with

    max_(G in Gset_m)M(G)<m.                           (FG14)

Duplicates cause no problem.
For fixed m this finite set may be chosen simultaneously for every period-three residue triple; incompatible triples simply contribute no polynomial in that degree class.
There is no assertion of a margin uniform as m varies.
