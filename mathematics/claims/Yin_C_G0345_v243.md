# Two prime powers and the binary period bound for reciprocal five-term factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## A squarefree divisor for which every binary period gives a small bound

For a positive integer \(A\) put \(L=5^A\), \(b_a=\lfloor(A-a)\log_7 5\rfloor\) for \(0\le a\le A\), and \(n=5^A7^{b_0}\).
For even \(a\) in this interval set \(t_a=5^a7^{b_a}\).
Write \(\Phi_j\) for the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial and use a bar for reduction modulo two.
Consider
\[
 P_{n,l}=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1,\qquad
 B_A=\prod_{\substack{0\le a\le A\\2\mid a}}\bar\Phi_{3t_a}
        \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[X],\qquad d=\deg B_A .
\]
For every \(0<l<n\), \(B_A\) divides the squarefree reduction of \(P_{n,l}\).
Its represented orders form a divisibility antichain and \(d\asymp A5^A\asymp A\sqrt n\).
For \(D\ge1\) let \(r_A(D)=\deg\gcd(B_A,X^D-1)\).
Then
\[
  r_A(D)=\sum_{\substack{0\le a\le A,\ 2\mid a\\3t_a\mid D}}\varphi(3t_a),
  \qquad
  \sup_{1\le D\le Kd}\frac{r_A(D)}d\ll_K\frac{\log(2A)}A
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
for every fixed \(K>0\) as \(A\to\infty\).
With \(x_+=\max(0,x)\), put
\[
 E_A=\sup_{\substack{D\ge1,\ i\ge0\\D,i\ {\rm integers}}}
       \frac{((i+1)r_A(D)-d)_+\log2}{2^iD}.
\]
There are absolute \(C>c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(A\),
\[
                        e^{-C\sqrt A}\le E_A\le e^{-c\sqrt A}.          \tag{2}
\]
This computes the scale of the best lower bound supplied by the [binary period inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement) for a hypothetical integral factor with this reduction and no cyclotomic factor.
Only the gcd degree formula in (1) is an exact equality for an individual period.
The [single additional prime power](Yin_C_G0345_v239.md#tower) gives a uniform gap by capturing all represented orders at one period comparable to the factor degree.
Here two independent prime powers allow every such period to capture a vanishing fraction of the specified modular divisor.

For every even \(l\), the characteristic-zero parent \(P_{n,l}\) has no cyclotomic factor.
The binary divisor lifts uniquely to a monic divisor over \(\mathbb Z_2\), and the lift is reciprocal.
If \(l\) is even and a monic divisor \(Q\) of \(P_{n,l}\) over \(\mathbb Q\) reduces to \(B_A\), then, writing \(k=n-l\) and \(e(Q)\) for its number of exterior roots with multiplicities,
\[
                   d\gg k,\qquad m(Q)\gg k/d,\qquad e(Q)\gg k^2/d .    \tag{3}
\]
For offsets \(k\asymp n^{3/4}\) with \(k\equiv1\bmod70\), there is no rational divisor with this reduction for sufficiently large \(A\).
For \(k\asymp\sqrt n\) in the same congruence class, (3) requires \(e(Q)\gg\sqrt n/A\).
No rational lift is asserted in this second range.
The binary estimates and the local lifting statement apply to both choices of offset.

<a id="conductors"></a>
## Counting the orders dividing a given period

Since \(n\) is odd and coprime to three, \(\bar P_{n,l}=(X^{3n}-1)/(X^n-1)\) is squarefree.
Its roots have orders \(3t\) for \(t\mid n\), as follows also from the [residual order calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v235.md#orbits) with \(u=v=0\).
For each odd such order the reduced cyclotomic polynomial has all the primitive roots of that order, each once, by the separable factorization of \(X^{3n}-1\).
The integer \(t_a\) divides \(n\), and \(L/7<t_a\le L\).
The exponent of five increases with \(a\), while \(b_a\) strictly decreases between consecutive even indices since \(2\log_7 5>1\).
This proves the antichain assertion.
Each represented order contributes \(\varphi(3t_a)=2\varphi(t_a)\asymp L\), since all prime divisors of \(t_a\) belong to \(\{5,7\}\).
There are \(\lfloor A/2\rfloor+1\) orders and \(L^2/7<n\le L^2\), proving both comparisons for \(d\).
Separability gives the gcd equality in (1), even for even \(D\), because \(B_A\) itself is squarefree.

If two represented orders dividing \(D\) have indices \(a\le a'\), their least common multiple is
\[
                  3\cdot5^{a'}7^{b_a}=3t_a5^{a'-a}>(3L/7)5^{a'-a}.
\]
Using the least and greatest selected indices, the number of orders dividing \(D\) is at most \(1+\log_5(7D/(3L))\) whenever \(r_A(D)>0\).
In particular \(D>3L/7\) then.
Summing the degree contributions \(O(L)\) and using \(d\asymp AL\) gives
\[
     r_A(D)/d\ll (1+\log(7D/(3L)))/A\qquad (r_A(D)>0).
                                                                        \tag{4}
\]
This proves the bounded-period assertion in (1).

<a id="optimization"></a>
## Optimizing over the period and every doubling

For \(D\ge L\exp(\sqrt A)\), the expression defining \(E_A\) is at most \(d\log2/D\ll A\exp(-\sqrt A)\), since \((i+1)/2^i\le1\).
For smaller \(D\) with nonzero gcd, (4) gives \(r_A(D)/d\ll A^{-1/2}\).
A positive numerator then requires \(i+1\gg\sqrt A\), and \(d/D\ll A\).
The bound \((i+1)2^{-i}\ll2^{-i/2}\) shows that this entire range also contributes at most \(e^{-c\sqrt A}\) for some absolute \(c>0\) and large \(A\).

For the other direction choose \(s=\lfloor\sqrt A\rfloor\) and take the first \(s\) even indices \(0,2,\ldots,2s-2\), all at most \(A\) for large \(A\).
Their least common multiple of orders is \(D=3\cdot5^{2s-2}7^{b_0}\le 3L5^{2s}\).
The captured degree is at least a constant times \(sL\), so \(i=\lceil2d/r_A(D)\rceil\ll A/s\).
At this \(i\) the numerator before multiplication by \(\log2\) is at least \(d\).
The resulting lower bound for \(E_A\) is at least a constant times \(A\,5^{-2s}2^{-O(A/s)}\), which gives (2).
Thus even the optimized inequality can lose a uniform gap on divisors of the specific squarefree binary parent, while rational divisibility imposes further conditions.

<a id="lifts"></a>
## Local factors and the obstruction over the rationals

The complement of \(B_A\) in \(\bar P_{n,l}\) is relatively prime to \(B_A\).
Monic Hensel factorization therefore gives a unique monic divisor of \(P_{n,l}\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\) with the specified reduction.
Both \(B_A\) and the parent are reciprocal with constant one.
If \(F\) denotes the lifted divisor, \(F(0)\) is a unit and \(X^d F(X^{-1})/F(0)\) is another monic divisor with the same reduction, so it equals \(F\).
This gives \(F(0)^2=1\).
The choice \(F(0)=-1\) would imply \(F(1)=0\), contrary to \(P_{n,l}(1)=-1\).
Thus \(F(0)=1\) and \(F\) is reciprocal.

Suppose now that \(l\) is even and a root of unity of order \(2^j s\), \(s\) odd, is a root of \(P_{n,l}\).
For \(j\ge1\), the reduction of \(\Phi_{2^j s}\) at two is \(\bar\Phi_s^{\,2^{j-1}}\).
Indeed substitution \(X^{2^{j-1}}\) into \(\Phi_{2s}\) gives \(\Phi_{2^j s}\) by the orders of its complex roots, and negation of the variable identifies \(\Phi_{2s}\) with \(\Phi_s\) up to sign.
Consequently squarefreeness forces \(j\le1\), and in either case the simple residual orders require \(s=3t\), \(t\mid n\).
For a root \(\zeta\) of order \(3t\) or \(6t\), its \(n\)-th power has order three or six, respectively, since \(n\) is coprime to six.
Thus \(\zeta^n+\zeta^{-n}\) is \(-1\) or \(1\).
The equation for \(P_{n,l}(\zeta)=0\), divided by \(\zeta^n\), requires \(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}=0\) or \(1\), respectively.
Those values require order four or six, whereas \(\zeta^l\) has odd order.
This excludes every cyclotomic factor.

Set \(k=n-l\).
For every exterior root \(x\) of \(P_{n,l}\), the gap \(k\) from its leading term to the next term gives \(|x|^k\le6\) by the triangle inequality.
Reciprocity then gives \(|\log|x||\le t=(\log6)/k\) for every root.
Every monic rational divisor \(Q\) is integral by monic factorization, and its irreducible factors are algebraic unit polynomials because the parent is monic with constant one.
They all have nontorsion roots for even \(l\).
For any irreducible factor \(S\) of \(Q\), the [quadratic radial bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives
\[
 \tfrac1{10}\le(\deg S)\sum_{S(x)=0}(\log|x|)^2
     \le(\deg S)^2t^2,\qquad
 \tfrac1{10}\le2(\deg S)t\,m(S).
\]
The second inequality uses the unit identity \(\sum|\log|x||=2m(S)\).
Using \(\deg S\le d,\ m(S)\le m(Q)\le e(Q)t\) proves (3).
Taking \(k=70\lfloor n^\gamma/70\rfloor+1\) for \(\gamma=3/4\) or \(1/2\) gives the stated asymptotic scales, \(l=n-k\) even, and \(\gcd(n,l)=1\) for all sufficiently large \(A\).
In the first case \(k/d\to\infty\), excluding the rational lift.
In the second case \(k^2/d\asymp\sqrt n/A\to\infty\), excluding a bounded exterior count for such a lift.
