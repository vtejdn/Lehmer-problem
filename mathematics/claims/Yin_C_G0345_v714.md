# Dyadic cyclotomic intersections and small Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Packing twists and powers in one coefficient class

Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be a nontorsion algebraic integer such that every positive power generates \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Write \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(m=\sum_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\log^+|\sigma\alpha|>0\).
Put \(E_a=\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^a})\) for \(a\ge1\), where \(\mu_w\) denotes all \(w\)-th roots of unity.
Let \(F=K\cap\bigcup_{a\ge1}E_a\), \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(A\ge1\) be least with \(F\subseteq E_A\).
The intersections with \(E_a\) form an ascending chain of subfields of bounded degree, so this least level exists.
Then \(h=[E_A:F]\) is one or two.
Set \(k=2s\) if \(h=1\) and \(k=2s-1\) if \(h=2\).
For every integer \(J\ge1\),
\[
 (kJ-1)\log 2\ \le\ \log\big((k/s)Jd\big)
       +\left(2-\frac{2}{kJhd}\right)kh(2^J-1)m .                    \tag{1}
\]
There is no restriction on the number of exterior conjugates.

Along every sequence of these integers with \(m\to0\),
\[
 \limsup\frac{k\log(1/m)}{\log d}\le1,\qquad s=o(\log d).             \tag{2}
\]
For each \(\delta>0\), the condition \(s\ge\delta\log(2d)\) gives an effective positive lower bound on \(m\) depending only on \(\delta\).
For general nonzero nontorsion algebraic integers the same conclusions apply to the [products of conjugates with torsion quotients](#scope), which preserve the original total measure.
The intersection degree and the field degree in this application refer to the field of a class product.

<a id="twists"></a>
## Fields of the twists and their distinct polynomials

Write \(w=2^A\), \(E=E_A\), \(L=KE\), and \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\).
Restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) with \(H\), since \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois and \(K\cap E=F\).
At \(A=1,2\), minimality gives \(F=E\), so \(h=1\).
For \(A\ge3\) the group \((\mathbb Z/2^A\mathbb Z)^\times=\{\pm1\}\times\langle5\rangle\) has \(\operatorname{ord}(5)=2^{A-2}\), as follows by successive squaring starting with \(v_2(5-1)=2\).
The kernel of restriction to \(E_{A-1}\) is generated by \(u=1+2^{A-1}\), the involution of \(\langle5\rangle\).
Minimality gives \(u\notin H\).
The last nonidentity power obtained by successive squaring of any element of order at least four is \(u\), and the whole subgroup of elements killed by squaring has order four and contains \(u\).
Consequently \(h\le2\).
When \(h=2\) its involution is \(-1\) or \(-u\), congruent to three modulo four, so it fixes exactly \(1,-1\) in \(\mu_w\).
In both cases \(w=2sh\).

For any root of unity \(\zeta\) of order \(t\) and integer \(b\ge1\), raising \(\zeta\alpha^b\) to the \(t\)-th power recovers a generator of \(K\).
Hence \(\mathbb Q(\zeta\alpha^b)=K(\zeta)\).
Fix \(b=2^j\) with \(0\le j<J\).
For \(h=1\) take every \(\zeta\in\mu_w\).
For \(h=2\) take one representative from each \(H\)-orbit in \(\mu_w\setminus\{1,-1\}\).
Each chosen \(\zeta\alpha^b\) generates \(L\), of degree \(hd\).
If two such elements at the same level are conjugate, an embedding taking one to the other fixes \(\alpha^{bw}\) and hence fixes \(K\).
It must act on \(E\) through \(H\), so the representatives coincide.
This gives \(k=w\) or \(k=(w-2)/2\) distinct monic irreducibles as asserted.
Their logarithmic measures are \(hb m\), since each embedding of \(K\) extends \(h\) times and \(|\sigma\zeta|=1\).
Thus the polynomials at distinct levels are also distinct.

<a id="packing"></a>
## Coefficients, discriminants and the quantitative gap

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
At any prime over two in a common splitting field, every element of \(\mu_w\) reduces to one.
The reduction of a twist polynomial thus has roots \(\overline{\sigma\alpha}^{\,2^j}\) with each embedding of \(K\) counted \(h\) times.
Frobenius preserves the root multiset of \(\overline P\in\mathbb F_2[X]\), with multiplicities, including in inseparable reduction.
The \(kJ\) polynomials are consequently congruent to \(P^h\) modulo two.

Each of their absolute discriminants is at least the absolute field discriminant of \(L\), by the integral power-basis index formula.
The relative discriminant formula gives \(|D_L|^{1/(hd)}\ge |D_E|^{1/[E:\mathbb Q]}=sh\).
For the final equality write \(v=w/2=[E:\mathbb Q]=sh\).
It is immediate for \(A=1\).
For \(A\ge2\) the discriminant of \(X^v+1\) has absolute value \(v^v\) by its derivative.
The order generated by a primitive \(w\)-th root \(\xi\) therefore has index prime to every odd prime.
At two, \(\pi=\xi-1\) satisfies the Eisenstein polynomial \((X+1)^v+1\).
In the degree-\(v\) completion its valuation with \(v_2(2)=1\) is \(1/v\).
The nonzero terms of any expansion \(\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}a_i\pi^i\), \(a_i\in\mathbb Q_2\), have distinct valuations modulo one, so an integral sum forces every \(a_i\in\mathbb Z_2\).
This gives the full order also at two and proves the field discriminant formula.

Apply the [resultant packing inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v707.md#statement) to these \(kJ\) polynomials of degree \(hd\) with modulus two.
Their summed logarithmic measure is \(kh(2^J-1)m\).
After dividing the logarithmic inequality by \(kJhd\), the average discriminant contribution on the left is at least \(\log(sh)\).
Subtracting it from \(\log(kJhd)\) gives (1).

When \(m\to0\) take \(J=\lfloor\log(1/m)/\log2\rfloor\), which tends to infinity and satisfies \(2^Jm\le1\).
Using \(k/s\le2\) and \(h\le2\) in (1) gives
\[
 k\log(1/m)\le\log d+\log(2J)+\log2+k(\log2+4).
\]
All terms on the right beyond \(\log d\) are \(o(k\log(1/m))\).
This forces \(d\to\infty\) and proves (2), since \(s\le k\).
For the effective assertion choose \(J\) with \(J\log2\ge1+\delta^{-1}+\log(4J)\).
Then \(\log d\le k/\delta\) and \((kJ-1)\log2-\log((k/s)Jd)\ge k\).
Inequality (1) yields \(m\ge1/[4(2^J-1)]\).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Reduction and the limit of degree-preserving twists

For a general nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer \(\theta\), partition its conjugates by root-of-unity quotient, as in the [reciprocal case](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).
Galois transitivity makes all classes the same size \(f\).
If \(N\) kills every quotient within a class \(C\), its product \(\alpha_C\) satisfies \(\alpha_C^N=\gamma^{fN}\) for each \(\gamma\in C\).
Products from different classes therefore have nontorsion quotient.
The products form one orbit of distinct algebraic integers, and every positive power still separates the orbit, so each power generates the same field as its product.
Within a class the moduli agree, so summing \(\log^+|\alpha_C|\) over the classes gives precisely the total logarithmic measure of \(\theta\).
This proves the unrestricted class-product formulation.

For a generator all of whose powers retain its field, the equality \(\mathbb Q(\zeta\alpha^b)=K(\zeta)\) shows that exactly the roots of unity in \(K\) preserve the degree.
If \(K\subset\mathbb R\), those twists are only the two signs.
The quadratic extension used in (1) also handles real dyadic intersections, but the intersection itself can stay small in arbitrarily large degrees.
For example, take the exterior root of the [seed-two polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#statement) with \(n=4j+3\), \(j\ge1\).
Its degree is \(d=n-1\equiv2\pmod4\), since the only cofactor at these odd delays is \(X+1\), and its measure lies strictly between one and two.
Its unique exterior conjugate has a singleton class under torsion quotient, so all classes are singletons.
Here \(s\) is a power of two dividing \(d\), whence \(s\le2\).
Thus small dyadic intersections are compatible with unbounded degree and bounded Mahler measure.

The [finite powered-conjugate determinant of Matveev](../audits/literature/Mat96/Yin_U001.md#mat96-u001) already uses powers with degree conditions, and [Amoroso and Dvornicich's cyclotomic argument](../audits/literature/AD00/Yin_U001.md#ad00-u001) uses a global Frobenius automorphism in an abelian field.
Here only the auxiliary extension \(E/\mathbb Q\) is assumed abelian.
The factor \(k\) in (1) counts distinct irreducibles supplied at each power by its dyadic twists, including quadratic orbits over \(K\).
