# Repeated integral remainders double the short-numerator energy floor

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least energy below the denominator degree

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\) with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Use Haar probability measure there, and write \(m=\log M(P)>0\), \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\) and
\[
 L=L(P)=\min_{\substack{S\in\mathbb Z[X]\setminus\{0\}\\ \deg S<d}}\|S/P\|_2^2 .
\]
For every positive integer \(N\) with \(N-1<C\),
\[
 L\ge
 \frac{C}{(1-\sqrt{(N-1)/C})^{-2}-(N-1)/(2N)}.                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(L\ge2C(1-O(C^{-1/3}))\) as \(C\to\infty\), with an absolute implied constant, and
\[
       \liminf_{m\downarrow0} mL(P)\ge \tfrac12 .                    \tag{2}
\]
This applies to reducible denominators and every integral Laurent numerator of width less than \(d\) after translation.
The corresponding minimum with complex coefficients and prescribed constant one is exactly \(C\), by [short-width evaluation](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation).

For an indivisible integral Laurent numerator \(A\) of arbitrary width, balanced division consequently gives
\[
             \liminf_{m\downarrow0}m\|A/P\|_2^2
                    \ge \tfrac12 e^{-1/2}>\tfrac14 .                \tag{3}
\]
In particular no such sequence realizes the premise \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to1/4\) of the [earlier conditional angular classification](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#statement).

<a id="remainders"></a>
## Stripping a coefficient and translating the remainder

The quadratic form \(\|S/P\|_2^2\) on coefficient vectors of length \(d\) is positive definite.
More explicitly it is at least \(\|S\|_2^2/\max_{\mathbb T}|P|^2\).
Thus each sublevel contains finitely many integral vectors, and the minimum \(L\) is attained.
Remove any initial monomial factor of a minimizer to get an ordinary polynomial \(T_0\) with nonzero constant, degree less than \(d\), and energy \(L\).

For each \(i\ge0\) put \(q_i=T_i(0)/P(0)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
The polynomial \(T_i-q_iP\) has degree \(d\) and zero constant coefficient.
Let \(g_i\ge1\) be its order at zero and define \(T_{i+1}=X^{-g_i}(T_i-q_iP)\).
It has nonzero constant and degree less than \(d\).
Write \(u_0=0\) and \(u_{i+1}=u_i+g_i\).
Iteration gives
\[
                 X^{u_i} T_i/P=T_0/P-\sum_{j<i}q_jX^{u_j}.          \tag{4}
\]
The exponents of the monomials on the right are all distinct.

Normalize \(S_i=\operatorname{sign}(T_i(0))T_i\) so that \(a_i=S_i(0)\ge1\).
No two \(S_i\) coincide.
Indeed equality at \(i<j\), together with (4), would imply \(P\mid (X^{u_j-u_i}\pm1)T_i\) in the Laurent ring.
All the possible roots of the binomial have modulus one.
Coprimality would give \(P\mid T_i\), also for repeated or reducible \(P\), impossible since \(T_i\ne0\) has degree less than \(d\).

Fix \(N\) as in (1) and put \(B=\max_{0\le i<N}\|T_i/P\|_2\).
The [lowest-coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement) gives \(C q_i^2\le \|T_i/P\|_2^2\).
From (4) and orthogonality of its monomials we get
\[
 B\le \sqrt L+\sqrt{(N-1)/C}\,B,\qquad
 B^2\le L(1-\sqrt{(N-1)/C})^{-2}.                                  \tag{5}
\]
These estimates allow large gaps between the exponents and arbitrary signs of the original coefficients.

<a id="packing"></a>
## Pairwise distances around the complex minimizer

Let \(S_*\) be the degree-less-than-\(d\) polynomial of constant one with least weighted norm.
The [orthogonal minimization identity](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#extremizer) gives \(\|S_*/P\|_2^2=C\) and makes \((S_i-a_i S_*)/P\) orthogonal to \(S_*/P\).
It follows that for \(v_i=(S_i-S_*)/P\),
\[
       \|v_i\|_2^2=\|S_i/P\|_2^2-(2a_i-1)C\le B^2-C .
\]
The difference \(S_i-S_j\) of two distinct normalized remainders is a nonzero integral polynomial of degree less than \(d\), so \(\|v_i-v_j\|_2^2\ge L\).
In the Hilbert space of circle functions,
\[
 \tbinom N2 L\ \le\ \sum_{i<j}\|v_i-v_j\|_2^2
              =N\sum_i\|v_i\|_2^2-\left\|\sum_i v_i\right\|_2^2
              \le N^2(B^2-C).
\]
Combining with (5) proves (1).
Take \(N=\lfloor C^{1/3}\rfloor\) for sufficiently large \(C\).
The denominator in (1) is \(1/2+O(C^{-1/3})\), and \(mC\to1/4\).
This proves (2).

<a id="allwidth"></a>
## A consequence for both ends of an unrestricted numerator

For width at least \(d\), use the [balanced division and its two projection estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#projections).
Its nonzero short remainder has energy at least \(L\).
Write \(n\) for the larger of the two quotient support counts, which differ by at most one.
The projection estimates imply \(E=\|A/P\|_2^2\ge e^{-2mn}L\), and \(E\ge 2j e^{-2mj}\) whenever both counts are at least \(j\ge1\).
Take \(k=\lfloor1/(4m)\rfloor\), which is positive for all sufficiently small \(m\).
For \(n\le k\) we have \(E\ge e^{-2mk}L\), and for \(n>k\) both counts are at least \(k\), giving \(E\ge2k e^{-2mk}\).
For width less than \(d\), \(E\ge L\) already.
Now (2) and \(mk\to1/4\) give (3).
An upper bound below the new threshold on hypothetical shrinking inputs would contradict it.
The existence of a shrinking sequence of measures remains open.
