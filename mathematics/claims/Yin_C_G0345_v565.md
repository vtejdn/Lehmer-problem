# Simultaneous residue orders for a long exponent with many prime divisors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Recovering the prime powers of the long exponent at one place

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit root of the [four-term polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v561.md#statement) with exponents \(n>k>0\), and consider a sequence with \(k/n\to0\).
Write \(n=LH\), where \(L=3^{v_3(n)}\) and \(3\nmid H\).
If \(H\to\infty\), then at each sufficiently late index there is a place above three in \(\mathbb Q(x)\) whose ramification index \(e\) and residue order \(\ell=\operatorname{ord}(\bar x)\) satisfy \(e\ge L/3\) and
\[
             \log\bigl(H/\gcd(H,\ell)\bigr)=o(\log H).
\]
Consequently \(\deg(x)\ge (L/3)\operatorname{ord}_H(3)H^{-o(1)}\) for arbitrarily many distinct prime divisors of \(H\).
For a fixed squarefree \(B\mid H\), a place with both \(e\ge L/3\) and \(H\mid\ell\) exists eventually whenever
\[
              \sum_{q\mid H,\ q\nmid B}\sqrt{\log(2q)/q}\longrightarrow0,
\]
where sums indexed by \(q\) throughout run over primes.
In that case \(\deg(x)\ge (L/3)\operatorname{ord}_H(3)\), with \(\operatorname{ord}_1(3)=1\).
For instance, the exact recovery criterion permits an unbounded number \(r\) of primes outside \(B\) if their least member \(Q\) tends to infinity and \(r=o(\sqrt{Q/\log Q})\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## A bound for each exceptional prime

Use uniform probability on the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) in \(\mathbf C_3\), with \(v(3)=1\).
Use the orientation \(u\in\{1,-1\}\) and sign \(c\) of the [tame fibre calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v561.md#fibres), and set \(z=c x^{un}\).
Call an embedding good if \(1/2<v(z-1)<3/2\).
Write \(E_q\) for the good embeddings at which \(q^{v_q(H)}\nmid\ell\).
Then for one absolute constant \(C\), uniformly for all \(q\mid H\),
\[
                       \Pr(E_q)\le C\sqrt{\log(2q)/q}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(\ell\mid 2n\) at a good embedding, since \(x^{2un}=z^2\) reduces to one.
For an embedding in \(E_q\) we therefore have \(\ell\mid 2n/q\), including when \(q=2\).
The unit \(t=x^{2un/q}\) reduces to one, and \(v(t-1)=v(t^q-1)=v(z^2-1)>1/2\), since \(q\ne3\) and \(z\equiv1\).
Its absolute logarithmic height satisfies \(h(t)\le6\log(6)/q\) by the [four-term cut bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement).

For any integer \(J\ge2\), form the nonzero algebraic integer \(D=\prod_{0\le i<j<J}(t^j-t^i)\).
Every factor has valuation at least \(1/2\) on \(E_q\).
At a complex embedding the Vandermonde matrix on \(1,t,\ldots,t^{J-1}\) has row \(i\) of norm at most \(\sqrt J\max(1,|t|)^{i(J-1)}\).
Hadamard's inequality and the product formula thus give
\[
  \tfrac12\log(3){J\choose2}\Pr(E_q)
     \le \tfrac J2\log J+(J-1){J\choose2}h(t).
\]
Taking \(J=\max(2,\lceil\sqrt{q\log(2q)}\rceil)\) gives (1).

<a id="packet"></a>
## A positive proportion with prescribed small prime powers

For a fixed squarefree \(B\mid H\) put \(\beta=x^{un/B}\).
The [fixed tame fibre distribution](Yin_C_G0345_v561.md#fibres) gives mass \(1/B\) to each of the \(B\) disk points indexed by \(r^B=c\).
Choose the roots whose reduction has order divisible by every \(q\mid B\).
For \(c=1\) exactly \(\varphi(B)\) qualify.
For \(c=-1\) the possible numerators in \(r=\exp(2\pi i j/(2B))\) run over the odd residues modulo \(2B\) after identifying the roots of unity in both fields.
Avoiding multiples of the odd primes dividing \(B\) leaves at least \(\varphi(B)\) choices, and the order is automatically even if \(2\mid B\).
Reduction at three preserves these orders.
The disjoint open neighborhoods used in that calculation are good and give \(\bar\beta=\bar r\).
Consequently the good embeddings at which all \(q^{v_q(H)}\) for \(q\mid B\) divide \(\ell\) have lower limiting probability at least \(\varphi(B)/B\), by the [residue-order argument](Yin_C_G0345_v561.md#degree).
The same local argument proves \(e\ge L/3\) at every good embedding.
Subtracting the probabilities (1) on the remaining primes proves the exact recovery assertion.

<a id="loss"></a>
## The logarithmic loss without a restriction on the prime divisors

Fix an integer \(K\ge5\) and take \(B=\prod_{q\mid H,\ q\le K}q\).
Only finitely many \(B\) are possible, so the preceding lower bound applies on each subsequence with constant \(B\).
For all sufficiently late indices the mass of good embeddings recovering these prime powers is at least \(c_0 K^{-1/3}\) for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Indeed \(\varphi(B)/B\) is bounded below by half the product of \(1-1/j\) over \(5\le j\le K\) coprime to six.
The sum of \(1/j\) over those integers is \(\frac13\log K+O(1)\) by grouping residues modulo six, and the sum of \(1/j^2\) converges.

On this set the logarithmic loss \(\log(H/\gcd(H,\ell))\) is bounded by \(\sum_{q\mid H,\ q>K} v_q(H)\log(q)\mathbf1_{E_q}\).
Its integral over the set is at most \(C\sqrt{\log(2K)/K}\log H\) by (1).
Some embedding in the set has loss at most the integral divided by the mass, giving a relative loss \(O(K^{-1/6}\sqrt{\log(2K)})\).
Letting \(K\to\infty\) bounds the limsup of the minimum relative loss over good embeddings by zero.
Taking a minimizing embedding gives the statement with simultaneous ramification.
For \(g=\gcd(H,\ell)\), reduction from the unit group modulo \(H\) to the unit group modulo \(g\) has kernel of size at most \(H/g\), by counting lifts of one residue class.
Hence \(\operatorname{ord}_H(3)\le(H/g)\operatorname{ord}_g(3)\), while the residue degree is a multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_g(3)\).
The local degree is at least \((L/3)\operatorname{ord}_H(3)g/H\), as asserted.
