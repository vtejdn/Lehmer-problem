# Explicit radical exclusions for the positive delay-seven Salem polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exclusions at every integer seed

Let \(a\ge3\) be an integer, let \(r\) be the exterior Salem root of \(X^7(X-a)+(aX-1)\), and write \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\).
For each \(p\in\{2,5,7,11,13\}\) one has \(r^{1/p}\notin K\).
Thus \(P(X^m)\) is irreducible for every \(m\) whose prime divisors lie in this set, where \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of \(r\).
For each fixed \(q\ge2\) coprime to six, four univariate polynomial gcds, followed by finite factor tests, determine all seeds admitting \(r^{1/q}\in K\).
This test treats arbitrary composite \(q\) coprime to six as well.

<a id="trace"></a>
## The cubic for the radical trace

The [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(P(X)=(X^8-aX^7+aX-1)/(X^2-1)\).
Writing \(t=X+X^{-1}\) gives \(P(X)=X^3 f_a(t)\), where
\[
        f_a(t)=t^3-at^2-2t+a=(t^2-1)(t-a)-t,\qquad f_a(1)=-1,\quad f_a(-1)=1.
\]
Let \(D_q\) be the integral polynomial with \(D_q(X+X^{-1})=X^q+X^{-q}\), given by \(D_0=2,\ D_1=y,\ D_{j+1}=yD_j-D_{j-1}\).
If \(\beta=r^{1/q}\) belongs to \(K\), it is a Salem number of degree six by the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Its three inverse pairs of conjugates therefore give three distinct values \(y_i=w+w^{-1}\), one larger than two and the other two in \((-2,2)\).
Their monic cubic \(g\) is integral because the conjugates are algebraic integers and the pairing is Galois invariant.
The three values \(D_q(y_i)\) are precisely the roots of \(f_a\), since the \(q\)-th powers of the six conjugates of \(\beta\) run bijectively through those of \(r\).

<a id="square"></a>
## The two unit values exclude squares

Write \(g(y)=y^3+By^2+Cy+D\).
For \(q=2\), the identity \(D_2(y)=y^2-2\) gives
\[
 f_a(y^2-2)=-g(y)g(-y)
            =(y^3+Cy)^2-(By^2+D)^2 .
\]
At \(y=1\) the left side is \(1\), hence \(g(1)=-g(-1)=\pm1\).
Adding and subtracting gives \(B+D=0\) and \(C\in\{0,-2\}\).
The constant coefficient and the coefficient of \(y^4\) give \(D^2=3a+4\) and \(B^2=a+6+2C\), respectively.
Consequently \(a=1+C\in\{-1,1\}\), which excludes every seed in the stated range.

<a id="pencils"></a>
## Four cubic pencils at exponents coprime to six

For \(\gcd(q,6)=1\), we have \(D_q(1)=1\) and \(D_q(-1)=-1\), as follows from \(D_q(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(q\theta)\).
The monic \(g\) divides \(f_a(D_q(y))\) in \(\mathbb Z[y]\).
Thus \(g(1)\) and \(g(-1)\) are each in \(\{-1,1\}\).
Since \(B+D\) and \(1+C\) are their half-sum and half-difference, respectively, \(g\) must take one of the four forms
\[
        g_{B,c,e}(y)=(y^2-1)(y+B)+cy+e,\qquad
           (c,e)\in\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)\}.
\]
For each pair form the monic cubic
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_y(g_{B,c,e}(y),\,t-D_q(y))
                   =t^3+v(B)t^2+w(B)t+u(B).
\]
A candidate \(B\) must be an integral root of \(\gcd(w+2,u+v)\) over \(\mathbb Q[B]\), with candidate seed \(a=-v(B)\).

This gcd is always a nonzero polynomial.
Indeed for real \(B\to+\infty\) the two bounded roots of the pencil near \(s=\pm1\) satisfy
\[
        y_s=s-\frac{cs+e}{2sB}+O(B^{-2})
\]
by the simple-root expansion after division by \(B\).
Both leading displacements are nonzero for every listed pair.
The remaining root is \(-B+O(1)\), so \(|v(B)|\asymp_q B^q\) since \(D_q\) is monic.
If both polynomials in the gcd vanished identically, each transformed root \(t_s=D_q(y_s)\) would satisfy \(f_{-v(B)}(t_s)=0\).
Then \((t_s^2-1)(t_s+v(B))=t_s\) would imply \(t_s-s=O_q(B^{-q})\).
However \(D_q'(s)\ne0\) because \(q\) is coprime to six, by differentiation of the cosine identity at angles \(\pi/3\) and \(2\pi/3\).
The displayed root expansion therefore gives \(|t_s-s|\asymp_q B^{-1}\), a contradiction.

The four nonzero gcds have finitely many integral roots that can be found by polynomial factorization.
At each candidate \(a\ge3\), factor \(P(X^q)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and isolate its positive root \(r^{1/q}>1\).
Its factor has degree six precisely when \(r^{1/q}\) belongs to \(K\), because it generates an extension containing \(K\).
These tests decide every surviving candidate.

<a id="examples"></a>
## The first four prime exponents coprime to six

The entries below are the monic gcds in \(\mathbb Q[B]\).

| \(q\) | \((1,0)\) | \((-1,0)\) | \((0,1)\) | \((0,-1)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | \(1\) | \(B(B-1)(B+1)\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| 7 | \(1\) | \(B^2\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| 11 | \(1\) | \(B(B-1)(B+1)\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| 13 | \(1\) | \(B(B-1)^2(B+1)^2\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |

For reproduction, the following operations use polynomial remainders in \(y\) modulo the monic \(g=g_{B,c,e}\) over \(\mathbb Z[B]\) and an ordinary polynomial gcd over \(\mathbb Q\).

~~~text
d0 = 2
d1 = y
for j from 2 through q:
    d0, d1 = d1, remainder(y*d1 - d0, g, variable=y)
t^3 + v*t^2 + w*t + u = resultant(g, t - d1, variable=y)
return monic_gcd(w + 2, u + v, variable=B)
~~~

The only candidates have \(c=-1,\ e=0,\ B\in\{0,-1,1\}\).
Then \(g(y)=y^3+By^2-2y-B\) has alternating signs at \(-2,-1,1,2\), since \(g(\pm2)=\pm4+3B\), \(g(-1)=1\), and \(g(1)=-1\).
All three roots are in \((-2,2)\), contrary to the required radical trace greater than two.
The [substitution criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now gives the asserted irreducibility.
