# Integral Schur models require large irreducible stable factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact feasibility and restrictions on two stable stages

Fix an even degree \(d\ge4\), an integer \(q\ge2\), and \(N=q^2-1\).
There is an exact correspondence between monic Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) of degree \(d\) and positive constant \(q\), and polynomials
\[
 A(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_i z^i\in\mathbb Z[z],\qquad
 a_0=-N,\quad a_{d-1}=N+j,\quad j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},                 \tag{1}
\]
whose roots are all strictly inside the unit disk and which satisfy
\[
                 q a_i+a_{d-i}\equiv0\pmod N
                        \qquad(1\le i\le d-1).                     \tag{2}
\]
The maps are
\[
 A=f^*-qf,\qquad
 f=-\frac{qA+zA^*}{N},\qquad
 f^*(z)=z^df(1/z),\quad A^*(z)=z^{d-1}A(1/z).                         \tag{3}
\]
No Salem polynomial is an input to this correspondence.

Write \(\theta\) for the exterior root of \(f\), and \(\delta=\theta-q\).
If \(A\) is reducible over \(\mathbb Q\), then
\[
                           j\ge2q+1.                               \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(A\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) whenever \(\delta\le1\).

There is a further restriction on constructing \(A\) by one inverse Schur step.
Put \(L=N+j\), \(K=L^2-N^2=j(2N+j)\), and
\[
                    B=\frac{LA+NA^*}{Kz}.                            \tag{5}
\]
This is a monic rational polynomial of degree \(s=d-2\), all of whose roots are strictly inside the unit disk.
Let \(V=cB\) be its primitive integral numerator, with positive leading coefficient \(c\), and let \(t\) be the multiplicity of zero as a root of \(B\).
Then \(c\mid K\).
If \(0<j<N\), then
\[
 t\le\frac{d-2}{2},\qquad
 \max_{G\mid V,\,G\ne z}\deg G
                 \ge\frac{d-2}{2\log_2 K},                           \tag{6}
\]
where the maximum is over primitive irreducible integral factors of \(V\), normalized to have positive leading coefficient.
These nonzero-root factors all have leading coefficient at least two.

Consequently, when \(q=d^2\) and \(0<j\le q\), one such factor has degree at least
\[
                 \frac{d-2}{12\log_2 d+2\log_2 3}.                   \tag{7}
\]
Along any proposed sequence at \(q=d^2\) with \(\delta\to0\), \(A\) is eventually irreducible, and the bound (7) applies to its Schur descendant.
This excludes constructions of that descendant using only factors of bounded degree, even with arbitrary multiplicities and an arbitrary zero block.
There is also an exact restriction on power substitutions.
If \(0<j<N\) and \(B=z^tD(z^k)\), where \(k\ge2\), \(D\in\mathbb Q[z]\) is monic of positive degree and \(D(0)\ne0\), then necessarily \(t=0\) and \(k=2\).

<a id="correspondence"></a>
## Stability and the complete coefficient congruences

First suppose that \(f\) is a Pisot minimal polynomial as above.
On the unit circle, \(|f^*|=|f|\), so Rouché's theorem applied to \(f^*-qf\) gives exactly \(d-1\) roots in the open disk.
Its degree is at most \(d-1\), since its degree-\(d\) coefficient cancels.
It therefore has exactly that degree and all its roots strictly inside.
Also \(A(1)=(1-q)f(1)>0\).
For a real polynomial with all roots inside the disk, its value at one has the sign of its leading coefficient; real factors and conjugate pairs both give positive factors at one.
Thus the leading coefficient \(L\) of \(A\) is positive.
The product of its root moduli is \(N/L<1\), so \(L=N+j\) for an integer \(j\ge1\).
Reversal in the indicated degrees gives \(zA^*=f-qf^*\), proving (3) and (2).

Conversely suppose that \(A\) satisfies (1), (2) and strict stability.
Formula (3) gives a monic polynomial of degree \(d\), constant \(q\), and integral intermediate coefficients by (2).
On the unit circle, \(q|A|>|zA^*|\), so it has precisely \(d-1\) roots inside and one outside.
Since \(A(1)>0\), it satisfies \(f(1)=-(q+1)A(1)/N<0\).
The unique exterior root is therefore real and greater than one.
Any proper monic integral factor excluding that root would have all roots strictly inside and nonzero integral constant of modulus less than one, which is impossible.
Thus \(f\) is irreducible and is the required Pisot minimal polynomial.

An independent set of the congruences (2) consists of those with \(1\le i<d/2\), together with
\[
                            q-1\mid a_{d/2}.                         \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(q^2\equiv1\pmod N\) supplies the reversed member of each pair, and the middle congruence is \(N\mid(q+1)a_{d/2}\).
These are the full divisibility conditions; stability alone does not imply them.

<a id="branch"></a>
## Branch size is asymptotically the Pisot excess times the constant

At zero one has
\[
 [z](f/f^*)=[z]f-q[z^{d-1}]f=L,
\]
so \(j\) is exactly the branch index used in the [positive-real-part normalization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
Its derivative identity holds without fixing the branch:
\[
           j=\delta\bigl(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0)\bigr),
                    \qquad |H_q'(0)|\le2.                            \tag{9}
\]
Since \(\theta>q\), for \(q>2\) this gives
\[
       \frac{j}{q+2+q^{-1}}<\delta<\frac{j}{q-2}.                     \tag{10}
\]
The left inequality remains valid at \(q=2\).
In particular, for \(q\to\infty\), \(j=o(q)\) is equivalent to \(\delta\to0\).
If \(\delta\le1\), (9) gives \(j<q+2+q^{-1}<2q+1\); thus the integer \(j\) is at most \(2q\).

<a id="first-factor"></a>
## A factorization costs more than twice the constant

Suppose that \(A\) factors over \(\mathbb Q\) into two positive-degree factors.
Gauss's lemma writes \(A=c_0GH\), where \(c_0\) is a positive integer and \(G,H\) are primitive integral polynomials of positive leading coefficients \(a,b\).
Write \(u,v\ge1\) for their absolute constant coefficients.
All their roots lie strictly inside the unit disk, so \(a>u\), \(b>v\), and hence \(a\ge u+1\), \(b\ge v+1\).
It follows that
\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=c_0uv,\\
 j&=c_0(ab-uv)
       \ge c_0(u+v+1)
       \ge2\sqrt{c_0N}+c_0
       \ge2\sqrt N+1>2q.
 \end{split}
\]
The last strict inequality holds for \(q\ge2\), since \(\sqrt{q^2-1}>q-1/2\).
Integrality proves (4), and (9) proves the stated consequence for \(\delta\le1\).
More explicitly every reducible input has \(\delta>(2q+1)/(q+2+q^{-1})>1\).

The threshold (4) is sharp if only stable integral \(A\) is required.
For positive integers \(r,u\) with \(r+u=d-1\), the polynomial
\[
          (qz^r-(q-1))((q+2)z^u+(q+1))
\]
has all roots strictly inside, constant \(-N\), and leading coefficient \(N+2q+1\).
This example is not asserted to satisfy (2) or to produce an integral Pisot inverse.

<a id="descendant"></a>
## One Schur step still requires a large irreducible factor

On the unit circle \(L|A|>N|A^*|\), so \(LA+NA^*\) has \(d-1\) strict interior roots.
Its constant vanishes and its leading coefficient is \(K=L^2-N^2>0\).
Dividing by \(Kz\) proves the degree, monicity and stability in (5).
Its coefficients have denominators dividing \(K\), so the least common denominator \(c\) divides \(K\).
The polynomial \(cB\) is primitive: a common divisor of its coefficients would divide its leading coefficient \(c\) and give a smaller common denominator.

Reversing (5), then eliminating \(A^*\), gives
\[
              A=LzB-NB^*,\qquad
 f=z(z-qL/N)B+(q-Lz/N)B^*.                                           \tag{11}
\]
Here the reversal of \(B\) uses degree \(s=d-2\).
Write \(B=z^tD\), with \(D\) monic of degree \(e=s-t\), \(D(0)\ne0\), and reversal \(D^*\) in degree \(e\).
Equation (11) becomes
\[
 f=z^{t+1}(z-qL/N)D+(q-Lz/N)D^*.                                    \tag{12}
\]
If \(t>e\), the second summand has degree at most \(e+1<t+1\), so the two coefficient blocks are disjoint.
Integrality of \(f\) then makes \((z-qL/N)D\) a monic integral polynomial.
Its rational root \(qL/N\) must be an integer.
Since \(\gcd(q,N)=1\), this forces \(N\mid j\), contrary to \(0<j<N\).
Therefore \(t\le e\), proving the zero-multiplicity bound and \(e\ge(d-2)/2\).

Factor the primitive polynomial \(V/z^t\) over the integers, taking every irreducible factor with positive leading coefficient.
Each has nonzero integral constant and all roots strictly inside, so its leading coefficient is an integer at least two.
The product of these leading coefficients, counting multiplicity, is \(c\).
Consequently there are at most \(\log_2 c\le\log_2 K\) factors with multiplicity.
Their degrees sum to \(e\), proving (6).
Equivalently, if every such factor has degree at most \(k\), then
\[
                       K\ge2^{(d-2)/(2k)}.                            \tag{13}
\]
For \(q=d^2\), \(0<j\le q\), one has \(K=j(2N+j)<3q^3=3d^6\), which proves (7).

<a id="substitution"></a>
## Power substitutions cannot hide the large factor

Write \(B_i=[z^i]B\), \(s=d-2\), and \(\rho=L/N\).
Equation (11) gives the exact coefficients
\[
 \begin{split}
 [z^{d-1}]f&=B_{s-1}-q\rho-\rho B_0,\\
 [z^2]f&=B_0-q\rho B_1+qB_{s-2}-\rho B_{s-1}.
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{14}
\]
Suppose \(B=z^tD(z^k)\) as in the statement.
If \(t\ge1\), then \(B_0=B_{s-1}=0\), since \(k\ge2\).
The first coefficient in (14) equals \(-qL/N\), so its integrality forces \(N\mid j\), contrary to \(0<j<N\).
Thus \(t=0\).
If \(k\ge3\), then \(B_1=B_{s-1}=B_{s-2}=0\), and the second coefficient in (14) equals \(B_0\).
Strict stability and monicity give \(0<|B_0|<1\), which contradicts its integrality.
Only \(k=2\) remains.
The omitted constant-\(D\) case would give \(B=z^s\), already excluded by the zero-multiplicity bound.
In particular an irreducible binomial of degree at least three, or a substitution of exponent at least three into a lower-degree stable polynomial, cannot supply an escape from (6).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequences for direct stable constructions

The [uniform prescribed-constant inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v292.md#prescribed) permits \(q=d^2\) in the direction from any supplied Salem sequence tending to one.
The present conditions concern a direct construction without such a supplied output.
Any successful sequence at that constant has \(j=o(q)\) by (9), so eventually \(j\le q<N\).
It must therefore use an irreducible first stable polynomial and a next stable polynomial with a nonmonic irreducible factor of degree bounded below as in (7).
Products of fixed-degree rational stable factors, repeated rational roots and added zero blocks cannot meet these requirements.
Nor can their degrees be increased merely by a power substitution of exponent at least three; the quadratic substitution remains outside this exclusion.
The earlier [trace-numerator obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) concerns nonmonic factors of a real interlacer on bounded branches.
The present bounds concern strict disk stability, allow \(j\) to grow with \(q\), and quantify the necessary factor degree for the Schur descendant.

The obstruction does not exclude irreducible stable numerators of growing degree, does not prove a lower bound for their excess, and does not produce a new Salem sequence.
The exact congruences and strict stability remain simultaneous requirements.
The contributor studying direct Schur constructions supplied the factorization bound (4), its sharp stable-only example and the coefficient congruences.
Agent-S supplied the denominator budget, the disjoint-block exclusion for excess zero multiplicity, and the quantitative descendant factor bound.
The coordinator supplied the power-substitution exclusion, whose two coefficient tests Agent-S checked.
The contributors checked these interfaces together before submission; this collaboration is author work rather than independent review.
