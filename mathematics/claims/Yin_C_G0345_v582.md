# Min-entropy of repeated binary sums approaches Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A rate from the expanding conjugates

Let \(\alpha\) be an algebraic integer with monic minimum \(P\) and \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
Let \(h_{\infty,k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}-n^{-1}\log\max_x\Pr(T_{n,k}=x)\), where \(T_{n,k}\) is the sum of \(k\) independent copies of \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\alpha^j\) with independent fair binary digits.
Among the expanding conjugates put \(h=\min_{|\beta|>1}\log|\beta|\), \(H=\max_{|\beta|>1}\log|\beta|\).
Then as \(k\to\infty\)
\[
       0\le m-h_{\infty,k}\ll_P
          \frac{k^{-h/(2(H+h))}}{\log k}.
\]
The same bound therefore holds for the deficit of every Rényi entropy rate of order at least one, including the collision rate.
For precisely one expanding conjugate with \(0<m\le\log2\), an absolute constant suffices in
\[
               0\le m-h_{\infty,k}\ll
                         \frac{m\,k^{-1/4}}{\log k}
                  \qquad(k\ge 256).
\]
In particular the relative error bound here is uniform in the degree and in small Salem measure.
The [repeated-sign Shannon estimate](Yin_C_G0305_v1.md#quantitative-convolution-entropy-convergence) applies to the Shannon rate with its sharper dependence on \(k\).

<a id="cells"></a>
## Integral cells at regularly spaced blocks

Let \(s\) count the expanding conjugates with both embeddings counted in each complex pair.
Identify their real embedding space with \(\mathbb R^s\), using real and imaginary parts for each pair.
The evaluations of \(1,X,\ldots,X^{s-1}\) at the expanding roots form an invertible real matrix \(V\) in these coordinates by the Vandermonde determinant.
Write \(A\) for multiplication by the \(q\)-th power of the roots in this space, expressed in the coefficient basis via \(V\), and \(B=A^{-1}\).
Then \(|\det A|=e^{qm}\), \(\|A\|_\infty\ll_P e^{qH}\) and \(b=\|B\|_\infty\ll_P e^{-qh}\).
The matrix norm is the maximum absolute row sum.

For integer \(q\ge s\) sufficiently large, round each entry of \((1-(s+2)b)A\) to its nearest integer, giving an integral matrix \(M\).
The rounding error has norm at most \(s/2\).
Hence \(\|BM\|_\infty\le 1-(s/2+2)b<1-b\), and \(BM=I+O_P(b)\) in norm.
In particular \(M\) is nonsingular for all sufficiently large \(q\), with
\[
           \log|\det M|=qm+O_P(b),\qquad \|M\|_\infty\ll_P e^{qH}.
\]
Partition \(\mathbb Z^s\) by the half-open parallelotopes \(M(\ell+[0,1)^s)\), \(\ell\in\mathbb Z^s\).
Each contains exactly \(|\det M|\) integer vectors because \(M[0,1)^s\) supplies one representative of each coset of \(M\mathbb Z^s\).

Use digits at times \(qi,\ldots,qi+s-1\) for each full length-\(q\) block \(i=0,\ldots,\lfloor n/q\rfloor-1\).
Fix the unused digits and the cell at every chosen block.
Then different lists of the remaining digit vectors give distinct sums.
Indeed if a relation occurs, let \(t\) be the final differing block and denote its nonzero integer difference vector by \(v_t\).
After division at every expanding embedding and change back to the coefficient basis,
\[
                   v_t=-\sum_{i<t} B^{t-i} v_i .
\]
For each difference within a single cell, \(v_i=M z_i\) with \(\|z_i\|_\infty\le1\).
The sum on the right has norm at most \(\|BM\|_\infty/(1-b)<1\), which excludes the nonzero integer vector.

<a id="concentration"></a>
## Summing the largest probabilities in the cells

Let \(p(j)=2^{-k}\binom{k}{j}\) on \(0\le j\le k\) and zero elsewhere, and let \(p_*=\max_j p(j)\ll k^{-1/2}\) by the central binomial estimate.
For the block law \(p^{\otimes s}\), write \(W\) for the sum over cells of the largest point probability in each cell.
Put \(R_0=\lceil\|M\|_\infty\rceil\).
The probability at a maximizing point of the cell containing \(x\) is bounded by \(\prod_{j=1}^s\max_{|v-x_j|\le R_0}p(v)\).
Summing this bound over all \(x\in\mathbb Z^s\) gives
\[
       W\le\frac{(1+2R_0p_*)^s}{|\det M|}.
\]
Here each nonempty superlevel set of the unimodal \(p\) is an integer interval, whose enlargement by \(R_0\) adds \(2R_0\) points.
Integration over levels from zero to \(p_*\) gives the one-dimensional sum \(1+2R_0p_*\).

For each fixed unused digit list and each choice of the cells, at most one tuple of block vectors can give a specified endpoint.
Independence and summation over these choices prove \(\max_x\Pr(T_{n,k}=x)\le W^{\lfloor n/q\rfloor}\).
The min-entropy rate exists by the product lower bound on the largest atom on concatenating independent blocks.
The usual [embedding box count](Yin_C_G0305_v1.md#finite-digit-mahler-upper) bounds the support rate by \(m\) for each fixed \(k\).
For orders \(1<a<\infty\) the rates also exist since merging the pair outcomes after concatenation increases their sum of \(a\)-th powers, while ordinary entropy is subadditive at order one.
All those rates lie between the min-entropy and the support rate.

Take \(q=\lfloor\log k/(2(H+h))\rfloor\), large enough in terms of \(P\).
The errors \(b\) and \(R_0p_*\) are then \(O_P(k^{-h/(2(H+h))})\).
Dividing the determinant and probability estimates by \(q\) gives the first conclusion.

<a id="scalar"></a>
## An absolute constant with one expanding conjugate

In this case the root \(\beta\) is real with \(|\beta|=e^m\).
Take \(q=\lfloor\log k/(4m)\rfloor\) and partition the integer digits of each selected one-coordinate block into intervals of \(U=\lfloor e^{qm}\rfloor\) successive integers.
The maximum difference in each interval is \(U-1\), so all earlier differences contribute strictly less than one at the final differing position after scaling, even if \(U=e^{qm}\), by the finite geometric series.
The same counting bound now gives
\[
          h_{\infty,k}\ge
             q^{-1}\bigl(\log U-\log(1+2U p_*)\bigr).
\]
For \(k\ge256\) and \(m\le\log2\), \(q\ge\log k/(8m)\), \(e^{qm}>k^{1/4}/2\), and \(U\ge k^{1/4}/4\).
Therefore \(0\le qm-\log U\le 1/U\ll k^{-1/4}\) and \(U p_*\ll k^{-1/4}\), proving the uniform bound.
For even numbers of walks there is also an exact identification \(h_{\infty,2k}=h_{k,2}\), where \(h_{k,2}\) is the collision entropy rate of \(k\) walks.
Reflection of a binary walk about half the sum of all its powers preserves its law, so the largest atom for \(2k\) walks equals the largest atom of the difference of two independent \(k\)-walk sums.
Cauchy–Schwarz puts its maximum at zero, giving the collision probability before taking rates.
