# Quantitative torsion collisions in sparse rational relations

The coordinating researcher and the sparse-relation researcher developed the quantitative estimates and examples together.

<a id="statement"></a>
<a id="relations"></a>
## Projective and individual height bounds

Let \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\) be nonzero algebraic numbers, \(s\ge2\), satisfying \(\sum_i c_i u_i=0\) for nonzero rational coefficients.
Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height, and put
\[
 A=h(u_1:\cdots:u_s),\qquad \Delta=\max_i h(u_i/u_1),\qquad
 \rho=\frac{(\sum_i|c_i|)^2}{\sum_i|c_i|^2}.
\]
For \(x\ge1\) write \(\ell(x)=\log(e+x)\), \(j(x)=\log(e+\ell(x))\) and \(b(x)=j(x)^3/(x^2\ell(x)^4)\).
Then either two terms have quotient a root of unity of squarefree order \(q\) with \(\varphi(q)\le2(\rho-1)\), or both
\[
          A\gg b(\rho),\qquad
          \Delta\gg\min\!\left\{\frac{\ell(s)}{\rho s},\, b(\rho)\right\}.
                                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The constants are absolute, the order \(q=1\) is allowed, and \(1<\rho\le s\).
In particular \(\Delta\gg b(s)\) in the second alternative.
For \(\rho\) bounded the individual height scale \(\log(2s)/s\) is sharp, as shown [below](#examples).

For a Laurent relation on any number of nonzero algebraic coordinates \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\), let \(w_j\) be the difference between the largest and smallest exponents in coordinate \(j\) of its actual support.
For its monomial values \(u_i\), one has
\[
                         A\le B_*=\sum_{j=1}^r w_jh(x_j).            \tag{2}
\]
Indeed divide all terms by the common monomial of coordinatewise minimum exponents.
At every place the new maximum term modulus is at most \(\prod_j\max(1,|x_j|)^{w_j}\).
Thus when there is no indicated torsion quotient, \(B_*\gg b(\rho)\), also for varying rank, coefficients and support.
In particular \(K\sum_jh(x_j)\gg 1/(s^2\log^4(2s))\) for \(K=\max_j w_j\).

The [bounded-relation theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#statement) already gives a qualitative coefficient-independent threshold and a bounded-order collision for fixed \(s\).
The [two-variable phase test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v114.md#sparse) gives the effective coefficient parameter and the totient bound on units, with the stronger height-only first moment in its [discriminant argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v114.md#discriminant).
For one nontorsion unit with a rational annihilator, the [one-variable degree and width argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) already gives the stronger lower bound \(w_1 h(x_1)\gg\rho^{-2}\).

## A first harmonic in terms of height

Let \(w\ne0\) be algebraic of degree \(n\), and average its phases \(\operatorname{ph}(z)=z/|z|\) over its complex conjugates.
For \(t=h(w)\) the [degree-sensitive first harmonic](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v114.md#discriminant) gives
\[
   |\mathbb E\operatorname{ph}(w)|
           \le C\sqrt{t+\log(3n)/n}+2t.                            \tag{3}
\]
For a nontorsion unit the [Dobrowolski estimate](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) yields \(nt\gg(j(n)/\ell(n))^3\), using finiteness in bounded degree and bounded measure for the finitely many small degrees.
For a nonunit the leading or constant coefficient of its primitive integral minimal polynomial has absolute value at least two, so \(nt\ge\log 2\).
In either case, for \(0<t\) sufficiently small,
\[
 |\mathbb E\operatorname{ph}(w)|
         \ll g(t):=\sqrt t\,\frac{\ell(1/t)^2}{j(1/t)^{3/2}}.       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed if \(n\ge 1/t\), monotonicity gives \(\log(3n)/n\le t\log(3/t)\).
If \(n<1/t\), the function \(j(x)/\ell(x)\) decreases for \(x\ge1\), so
\[
          1/n \ll t\,\ell(1/t)^3/j(1/t)^3,\qquad
          \log(3n)\le\log(3/t).
\]
These estimates in (3) give (4).
For a root of unity of exact order \(q\), the conjugates are the primitive \(q\)-th roots and their mean is \(\mu(q)/\varphi(q)\) by divisor inversion.
All these means remain the same in a joint number field, since each embedding of the generated field has the same number of extensions.

## Radial error with unrestricted coefficients

Choose a number field containing every \(u_i\), write \(\mathbb E\) for its uniform embedding average, and normalize the coefficients by \(\sum_i|c_i|^2=1\).
This common real rescaling preserves the rational relation at each embedding.
Let \(D=\mathbb E|\sum_i c_i\operatorname{ph}(\sigma u_i)|^2\).
At an archimedean place set \(M=\max_i|\sigma u_i|\) and \(r_i=|\sigma u_i|/M\).
The relation divided by \(M\) makes the phase sum equal to \(\sum_i c_i\operatorname{ph}(\sigma u_i)(1-r_i)\).
For every \(i\), the embedding mean of \(\log(M/|\sigma u_i|)\) is at most \(A\), since including all finite places gives exactly \(A\) by the product formula and each added summand is nonnegative.
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and \((1-r)^2\le-\log r\) for \(0<r\le1\) therefore give
\[
              D\le \|c\|_1\sum_i |c_i|\,\mathbb E(1-r_i)^2
                       \le \rho A.                                  \tag{5}
\]

For the bound in terms of individual heights, divide the tuple by \(u_1\), which multiplies each sampled phase sum by a common factor of modulus one.
For \(t_i=\log|\sigma(u_i/u_1)|\), the two parts of the height formula give \(\mathbb E|t_i|\le2\Delta\).
Set \(T=\frac12\log(1+s)\).
The event \(\max_i|t_i|>T\) has probability at most \(2s\Delta/T\), and the squared phase sum there is at most \(\rho\).
On the complementary event use the relation with moduli \(e^{t_i}\).
Convexity on \([0,T]\) gives \(|1-e^t|^2\le(e^T-1)^2 |t|/T\) for \(|t|\le T\).
The same weighted Cauchy–Schwarz bound on this event costs at most \(2\rho\Delta(e^T-1)^2/T\).
Since \((e^T-1)^2\le s\), we obtain
\[
                         D\le 8\rho s\Delta/\log(1+s).               \tag{6}
\]

Let \(v\) bound the heights of all pairwise ratios.
Both \(v=A\) and \(v=2\Delta\) are available, by projective coordinate projection and the height of a quotient, respectively.
For sufficiently small \(v>0\), \(g\) is increasing on \((0,v]\), as follows by differentiation, and (4) bounds the absolute phase correlation of each nontorsion pair by \(C g(v)\).
If the collision in (1) is absent, a torsion pair either contributes zero or has absolute correlation less than \(1/(2(\rho-1))\).
The ordered off-diagonal coefficient weights sum to \(\rho-1\), and the diagonal sum equals one.
Expanding \(D\) and bounding the torsion and nontorsion contributions separately gives
\[
                        \tfrac12 \le D+C(\rho-1)g(v).               \tag{7}
\]
At \(v=0\) all the pairs are torsion and one can take \(g(0)=0\).
For every sufficiently small absolute \(a>0\), \(\sup_{x\ge1}xg(a b(x))\ll\sqrt a(1+|\log a|)^2\).
Indeed \(\ell(1/(a b(x)))\ll(1+|\log a|)\ell(x)\) and \(j(1/(a b(x)))\gg j(x)\), by the definition of \(b\).
Also \(x b(x)\) is bounded on \([1,\infty)\).
Taking \(a\) small, (5) and (7) exclude \(A<a b(\rho)\), and (6) and (7) exclude \(\Delta<a\min\{\ell(s)/(\rho s), b(\rho)\}\).
For \(1<\rho\le s\), \(b(\rho)\gg b(s)\) and \(\ell(s)/(\rho s)\gg b(s)\), which proves the individual bound in \(s\).

## Units with few off-circle conjugates

Let \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r\) be multiplicatively independent Salem numbers of any degrees \(d_j\), and put \(B=\max_j\log\sigma_j\).
For any nonzero rational Laurent relation at this tuple with distinct support vectors, coefficient parameter \(\rho\), and maximum coordinate width \(K\), one has
\[
                K\rho^2 (1+B+r)\sum_{j=1}^r d_j^{-1}\gg 1.         \tag{8}
\]
The implied constant is absolute.
In particular, for two independent Salem numbers of degrees at least \(d\) and bounded logarithmic measures, every such relation satisfies \(K\gg_B d/\rho^2\) throughout the full range of supports.
More generally, (8) holds for an arbitrary Salem tuple if its monomial values on the given support are pairwise distinct.
Indeed every value is positive at the given real embedding, so a torsion ratio would equal one.
For a dependent tuple violating (8), two different support vectors must therefore give the same monomial value.

For arbitrary algebraic units \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) define \(t=\sum_j h(x_j)\) and
\[
       p=\sum_j\frac{\#\{\text{off-circle conjugates of }x_j\}}{[\mathbb Q(x_j):\mathbb Q]},
       \qquad H=\sum_j w_j h(x_j)\le Kt .
\]
For a rational Laurent relation the squarefree torsion collision in (1) occurs or
\[
         K\rho^2\{t+(r+1)p\}\gg1,\qquad
         H+p\log(3/p)\gg\rho^{-2}\quad (0<p\le1).                   \tag{9}
\]
These alternatives allow arbitrary multiplicative dependencies.
If all distinct support terms have nontorsion ratios, both lower bounds hold in their indicated ranges.

The width \(K\) is at least one since a single nonzero Laurent monomial cannot vanish.
For \(p>1\) the first bound is immediate.
At most a fraction \(p\) of the joint embeddings put any input off the unit circle.
At every other embedding the phase sum itself vanishes, so \(D\le\rho p\).
Every nontorsion pair ratio is a unit with an off-circle conjugate by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
All its off-circle embeddings lie among those exceptional embeddings, so if \(n\) is its degree then \(1/n\le p\).
Its height is at most \(H\), and for \(0<p\le1\) monotonicity of \(\log(3n)/n\) in (3) bounds its correlation by \(C\sqrt{H+p\log(3/p)}+2H\).
In the absence of the indicated collision, the torsion contribution to the normalized Gram expansion is at most \(1/2\), as in (7).
For \(p=0\) every term is torsion and \(D=0\), so that expansion forces the collision.
For \(0<p\le1\) it yields
\[
 \tfrac12\le \rho p+(\rho-1)\{C\sqrt{H+p\log(3/p)}+2H\}.
\]
For \(H+p\log(3/p)\le1\) the right side is bounded by an absolute multiple of \(\rho\sqrt{H+p\log(3/p)}\), giving the second bound of (9), which holds directly for larger values.

Put \(D_0=1/p\), so \(D_0/\rho^2\ll Kt/p+\log(3D_0)\).
The exponent vectors lie in a translate of \([0,K]^r\), hence \(\rho\le s\le(K+1)^r\).
Writing \(v=D_0/(\rho^2 K)>0\), it follows that
\[
 \log(3D_0)/K
   \le \{\log3+(2r+1)\log(K+1)+\log^+v\}/K
   \ll 1+r+\log^+v .
\]
Thus \(v\le C(1+t/p+r+\log^+v)\) for an absolute \(C\), and \(\log^+v\le v/(2C)+O_C(1)\) gives the first bound of (9).
For the Salem tuple \(p=\sum_j2/d_j\) and \(t=\sum_j(\log\sigma_j)/d_j\le Bp/2\), proving (8) in the absence of a collision.
The argument uses the degrees of the individual ratio fields, even if the input fields have a large intersection.

## Sharp dependence on term count at bounded effective support

For even \(n\ge4\), let \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) be the roots of \(X^n-nX^{n-1}-2\).
This is Eisenstein at two, and on the unit circle its middle monomial of modulus \(n\) dominates \(X^n-2\).
Rouché's theorem places exactly \(n-1\) roots inside, while evaluation at \(n\) and \(n+1\) locates the remaining real root \(\alpha\in(n,n+1)\).
No root is torsion by irreducibility.
If the quotient of distinct roots were torsion, an automorphism carrying the numerator to \(\alpha\) would carry the denominator to a different root of the same exterior modulus, which is impossible.
The trace relation \(-n+\sum_i a_i=0\) therefore has \(s=n+1\), \(\rho=4n/(n+1)<4\) and no torsion ratio.
With the constant monomial as reference, \(\Delta=\log(\alpha)/n\), since every root is integral and has the same single exterior conjugate.
The projective height is \(A=\log\alpha\), since every embedding permutes the whole root set and the tuple includes one.
This gives the upper bound matching the \(\ell(s)/s\) individual height floor for bounded \(\rho\).

For the relation \(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta_p^i=0\) at a primitive \(p\)-th root for prime \(p\), all relative heights vanish and every pair ratio has order \(p\), with \(\varphi(p)=p-1=\rho-1\).
Thus a bound on the collision order must allow linear growth in support even for a nondegenerate relation.
The [Amoroso–Viada hyperplane result](../audits/literature/AV12/Yin_U001.md#av12-u001) also permits arbitrary rational coefficients.
At its smaller dimension-dependent cutoff, a nondegenerate relation forces all ratios to be torsion.
Their [equal-coefficient example](../audits/literature/AV12/Yin_U001.md#example) has \(\rho=s\), no torsion ratios and projective height \(O(1/s)\).
These examples leave the optimal growing-\(\rho\) projective floor open.
