# Reconstructing an integral delay-one seed from the half polynomial of a square endpoint

<a id="statement"></a>
## Odd half degrees and the exact obstruction in even degree

Let \(E\) be totally real of degree \(u\ge2\) and let \(K/E\) be quadratic, split at one real place and complex at all others, with nonidentity automorphism \(\tau\).
Fix one of its real embeddings above the split place.
Suppose \(f=\sum_{i=0}^b f_iX^i\in\mathcal O_K[X]\) is monic of degree \(b\ge2\), \(f_0=\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(f^\tau=\epsilon f^*\), where \(f^*(X)=X^bf(1/X)\).
At the fixed embedding suppose \(f\) has one simple exterior root \(r>1\) and \(b-1\) strictly interior roots.
At all the complex embeddings suppose its roots are distinct and have modulus one.
These are the [half-polynomial conditions of the square endpoint](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#moments).

There exists a monic \(P\in\mathcal O_K[X]\) of degree \(b-1\) with
\[
                         f=XP+\epsilon(P^\tau)^*
\]
that satisfies all the [nondegenerate relative seed conditions](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#statement) if and only if either \(b\) is odd or
\[
                   f_{b/2}\in (1+\epsilon\tau)\mathcal O_K .
\]
The seeds can have arbitrarily large positive exterior root at the fixed embedding while giving the very same \(f\) at delay one.
They have nonzero constant, and every root at **every** complex embedding is strictly inside the circle.
For even \(b\) the same coefficient condition is necessary for any representation \(f=X^kP+\epsilon(P^\tau)^*\) at any positive delay, with \(P\) integral monic of degree \(b-k\).

In particular, a uniform gap for the [bounded-second-iterate class of integer polynomials](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) is equivalent to a gap for both of the following classes: all nondegenerate relative constructions at delay one, measured by their rational norm, and square endpoints of even half degree with \(f_{b/2}\notin(1+\epsilon\tau)\mathcal O_K\).
For this assertion the latter endpoints are the compressed canonical ones in the small-measure reduction and have \(u\ge2\).
The first class includes rational norms equal to the squares of real Salem minima.
No degree bound is imposed on its seeds.
The remaining integer polynomial inputs with unbounded second iterates are outside this reduction.

<a id="integrality"></a>
## Integral solutions of the coefficient equations

Write \(P=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}p_iX^i\) with \(p_{b-1}=1\).
At intermediate indices the equations are \(p_{i-1}+\epsilon\tau(p_{b-1-i})=f_i\), \(1\le i<b\).
The equations at \(i\) and \(b-i\) are equivalent by relative reciprocity.
Whenever these indices differ one can set \(p_{i-1}=f_i,\ p_{b-1-i}=0\) on one choice from the pair.
For even \(b\) the middle equation is exactly the indicated image condition.
Thus the condition characterizes existence of an integral solution before imposing inequalities.
At delay \(k\le b/2\) the middle coefficient is still \(p_{b/2-k}+\epsilon\tau(p_{b/2-k})\), and it is zero when \(k>b/2\).

When an integral solution exists, all of them form its translate by a full lattice of homogeneous solutions at the Archimedean places.
Indeed on each pair of distinct indices a homogeneous coefficient can vary freely over \(K\), determining its partner, and at a middle index the kernel of \(1+\epsilon\tau\) is a one-dimensional \(E\)-line.
The homogeneous solutions over \(E\) have dimension \(b-1\).
Their integral members contain an \(\mathcal O_E\)-span of an \(E\)-basis by clearing denominators, and embed discretely in the ordinary Minkowski coefficient lattice.
At the marked split place the \(b-1\) lower coefficients of the first real \(P\) can be varied arbitrarily over \(\mathbb R\); the other real copy is determined by the equations.
This uses \(K\otimes_{E,*}\mathbb R=\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R\), with \(\tau\) interchanging the two factors.

<a id="direction"></a>
## A strictly positive direction at the split real place

All coefficients in this section are taken at the chosen real embedding.
Write \(f=(X-r)\prod_{j=2}^b(X-a_j)\), where \(|a_j|<1\), and define
\[
     O(X)=r(1-X/r)\prod_{j=2}^b(1-\bar a_j X),\qquad
     U(X)=\frac{\epsilon O(X)+O^*(X)}{r+r^{-1}} .
\]
The multiset of interior roots is closed under conjugation, so \(O\) is real.
On the unit circle \(|O|=|f|\), since \(|z-a|=|1-\bar a z|\) there and \(r|1-z/r|=|z-r|\).
Also \(O(0)=r\), and its leading coefficient is \((-1)^b\prod_{j=2}^b\bar a_j=\epsilon/r\) by \(f(0)=\epsilon\).
It follows that \(U(0)=\epsilon\), \(U\) is monic of degree \(b\), and
\[
                       |U/f|\le 2/(r+r^{-1})<1
\]
on the circle.
Let \(N=f-U\).
It has zero constant and degree at most \(b-1\).
By Rouché it has the same \(b-1\) roots inside the disk, counted with multiplicity, as \(f\).
Consequently \(H=N/X\) has exact degree \(b-2\), all its roots strictly interior, and \(\operatorname{Re}(zH(z)/f(z))>0\) on the entire circle.
Since \(f(1)<0\), also \(H(1)<0\), so \(H\) has negative leading coefficient.
All these conditions are open among real polynomials of degree \(b-2\); perturb within them if necessary to arrange \(H(0)\ne0\).

<a id="approximation"></a>
## Simultaneous rounding with bounded errors at the complex places

The monic rational-coefficient operation \(P_{\rm ref}=f'/b\), with coefficients in \(K\), solves the identity \(f=XP_{\rm ref}+\epsilon(P_{\rm ref}^\tau)^*\) by differentiating the reciprocal relation, or by comparing each intermediate coefficient.
At each complex place its roots are strictly inside the disk because \(f\) has distinct circle roots there. [Strict critical-point bound](Yin_C_G0901_v130.md#critical)
Use this solution as the desired point at every nonsplit place.
At the split place prescribe \(P_{\rm ref}+\lambda H\) on the chosen real side and the uniquely determined partner on the opposite side.

Choose a totally positive unit \(v\) of \(E\) with \(v_*>1\) at the marked place and \(0<v_\sigma<1\) elsewhere, by the [logarithmic unit lattice](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#realization).
Set \(\lambda=v_*^{2n}\) for integers \(n\to\infty\).
Starting from one integral particular solution, approximate the difference to these simultaneous targets in the real homogeneous solution space by the lattice of integral homogeneous solutions scaled by \(v^n\).
To see the errors, divide that difference componentwise by \(v^n\), round to a point of the fixed lattice in a bounded fundamental domain, then multiply back.
The correction is integral because \(v\) is an algebraic-integer unit of \(E\).
At each nonsplit place the coefficient error is \(O(v_\sigma^n)\to0\).
At the marked embedding the error is \(O(v_*^n)=o(\lambda)\).
The resulting monic integral \(P_n\) therefore has all its roots strictly interior at the complex places, while on the marked side \(P_n/\lambda\to H\) coefficientwise.
In particular its constant is nonzero eventually.

For a circle strictly inside the disk enclosing all the roots of \(H\), Rouché gives \(b-2\) roots of \(P_n\) inside that circle for large \(n\).
The remaining root \(\beta_n\) is real since the others form a conjugation-stable multiset and the coefficients are real.
The coefficient of \(X^{b-2}\) in \(P_n\) divided by \(\lambda\) tends to the negative leading coefficient of \(H\), so the sum of the roots shows \(\beta_n\to+\infty\).
At \(|z|=1\) the identity with \(f\) gives
\[
    |P_n(z)|^2-| (P_n^\tau)^*(z)|^2
            =2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{f(z)}\,zP_n(z))-|f(z)|^2 .
\]
Dividing by \(\lambda\) yields a uniformly strictly positive limit since \(f\) has no circle root and \(\operatorname{Re}(zH/f)>0\) there.
This proves strict circle domination and completes the reconstruction.

<a id="gap"></a>
## The remaining class for the bounded-second-iterate problem

In the [bounded-second-iterate reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement), a shrinking sequence yields either shrinking real Salem numbers or shrinking compressed Perron square endpoints with torsion half products and one nonneutral block.
For such an endpoint the marked real half has product \(\pm1\), and its other half is reciprocal; the half fields have the indicated signatures by the [action of complex conjugation](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#moments).
There are at least two blocks eventually: at \(u=1\) the [small-measure oriented case](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) would force an imaginary quadratic subfield of the real field of the endpoint.
The roots are distinct and the hypotheses just proved apply.
Unless the even middle coefficient obstructs reconstruction, the rational norm of the reconstructed polynomial is exactly the original minimum by the [half-field norm identity](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields).
Every real Salem input likewise gives a delay-one rational norm of measure equal to its square by the [linear realization](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#realization).
Here squaring preserves convergence to one.

Conversely each obstructed square endpoint is itself an input with bounded second measure on a shrinking sequence since \(M(r)=r^2\) and powers have full degree by compression.
For any delay-one relative norm polynomial \(F\) with marked exterior root \(R\), the [norm and root count](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#delays) gives \(M(F)=R^2\).
The minimum of \(R\) divides \(F\), so \(M(R)\le R^2\); passing to squares can only identify some conjugates, hence \(M(R^2)\le M(R)^2\le R^4\).
Thus a shrinking sequence in either of the stated classes also gives one for the bounded-second-iterate problem.
