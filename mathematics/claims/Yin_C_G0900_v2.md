# Equality at a vertex of modulus two for four-term Laurent polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## All support dimensions at least two

Let \(P\) be a four-term integer Laurent polynomial in finitely many variables, with a vertex coefficient of modulus two.
Suppose the affine span of its exponents has dimension at least two.
Normalize the marked term to the constant \(2\) by a signed monomial, and use the lattice generated by the differences of the exponents.
Then \(M(P)=2\) holds exactly for the following polynomials in two independent characters \(x,y\) which form a basis of this lattice:
\[
                         2+a x+y(b+c x^k).
\]
All entries in the table are nonzero integers, the two magnitudes in braces may be interchanged, and the indicated sign is required.

| \(k\) | \(|a|\) | \(\{|b|,|c|\}\) | \(\operatorname{sgn}(bc)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \(1\) | \(\{1,1\}\) or \(\{1,2\}\) | \(\operatorname{sgn}a\) |
| \(1\) | \(2\) | \(\{1,1\}\) or \(\{2,2\}\) | \(\operatorname{sgn}a\) |
| \(2\) | \(1\) or \(2\) | \(\{1,1\}\) | \(-1\) |

Embeddings of the support lattice into a larger lattice preserve the torus measure.
Every other polynomial under the hypotheses has \(M(P)\ge 2+\varepsilon(4,2)\) by the [sparse vertex gap](Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement).
The classification does not require the polynomial to arise from a dynamical system or an integer trace construction.

<a id="geometry"></a>
## Supports without a pair of parallel edges

Write the support as \(0,v_1,v_2,v_3\), with a functional strictly positive on the last three vectors by the vertex hypothesis.
If the affine span of \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) omits zero, there is an integral functional taking a common nonzero value there.
This includes the rank-three case and the rank-two case of three collinear nonconstant terms.
The [common-phase calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#common-phase) then gives \(M(P)>2\), since the [minimum supremum norm](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#norm) of the three-term integer sum is at least \(\sqrt5\).
If two vectors are collinear with zero while the third is independent, integrate in the independent character.
This gives the mean of \(\log\max(|T|,|c|)\) where the trinomial \(T\) has constant two at a vertex, and \(c\) is a nonzero integer.
Equality with \(\log2\) would give \(M(T)=2\) and \(\min|T|\ge1\), excluded by [minimum-modulus rigidity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v101.md#minimum).

It remains to consider rank two with no collinear triple.
Choosing coordinates along the two extreme rays as in the [circle-slice calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v23.md#slices) gives
\[
                  2+a x^p+b y^q+c x^r y^s,\qquad p,q,r,s>0 .
\]
This calculation preserves Haar measure and applies to any such polynomial.
If \(r\ne p\) and \(s\ne q\), equality of the measure with two first forces \(|a|=|b|=1\) by the slices with one coordinate fixed.
Slicing at \(a x^p=-1\) produces a constant-one trinomial of measure one and leading modulus at least one.
Its conjugate reversal forces \(s/q=1/2\) or \(2\), and interchanging variables gives \(r/p=1/2\) or \(2\).
A doubled ratio on one axis forces the other ratio to be doubled as well, by the leading coefficient condition at every root of \(a x^p=-1\) or \(b y^q=-1\).
The simultaneous halves make three points collinear.
The simultaneous doubles reduce by sign rotations to \(2+X+Y+X^2Y^2\).
The [diagonal specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v23.md#diagonal) gives measure strictly above two, by testing \(2+2z+z^4\).
Thus one must have \(r=p\) or \(s=q\), which gives a convex quadrilateral with parallel sides.

<a id="edges"></a>
## The parallel sides and the converse

Orient the side incident to the marked vertex toward its other endpoint, and use the same orientation on the opposite side.
In independent characters the polynomial becomes \(2+a z^m+u(b+c z^n)\), where \(m,n>0\).
Jensen's formula in \(u\) gives the circle mean of \(\log\max(|2+a z^m|,|b+c z^n|)\).
By the [parallel-side computation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#jensen), the mean is \(\log2\) exactly when the displayed table holds with \(n=km\).
In particular the primitive character \(z\) of the edge direction contributes only through \(z^m\).
The characters \(x=z^m,y=u\) are independent and generate the support lattice, giving precisely the claimed forms.
