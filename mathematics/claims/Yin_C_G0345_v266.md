# Ordinary lines among conjugates and the orbits of a two-point stabilizer

<a id="statement"></a>
## A pair for which every third label has a large orbit

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number and let \(\gamma\) be the product in its class of conjugates with root-of-unity quotient.
Write \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\) and \(m=m(\alpha)=m(\gamma)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure.
The [multiplicative labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) of conjugates of \(\gamma\) identify two roots when their quotient or product is torsion.
Each label has \(w\le2\) roots.
Take any block \(B\) of \(b\ge2\) labels for this Galois action, including the entire label set, and let \(H\) be the group induced on \(B\) by its setwise stabilizer.

For fixed \(M>0\), if \(m\le M\) and \(d\) is sufficiently large depending on \(M\), there are distinct labels \(a,b_0\in B\) such that every third label \(z\in B\setminus\{a,b_0\}\) satisfies
\[
           |H_{a,b_0}z|\ \ge\ \frac{c\,d}{m^3(\log(3d))^{K_3}} .          \tag{1}
\]
Here \(H_{a,b_0}\) fixes the two labels individually, \(c>0\) is absolute and \(K_3\) is the absolute logarithmic exponent for three independent heights.
The same pair works for all third labels.
In particular a large permutation group with just one short orbit outside **each** pointwise fixed pair can be excluded even when its order grows faster than every power of its degree.

For each of the following classes admitting at least one block \(B\) as specified, the positive values of \(m(\alpha)\) in every bounded interval form a finite set.
All ground set sizes and dimensions in the conditions can vary.

1. \(H\) preserves a system of subsets of \(B\), each of size \(k\) with \(3\le k<b\), in which each pair of distinct labels occurs exactly once.
   This includes projective points in dimension at least two over any finite field and affine points in dimension at least two over fields of size at least three, with any transitive group preserving their lines.
2. \(B\) is the collection of \(k\)-subsets of an \(n\)-element set, \(2\le k\le n/2\), and the action is induced by permutations of that set, also allowing complementation for \(n=2k\).
3. \(B\) is a Cartesian power of \(a\ge2\) copies of an \(s\)-element set, \(s\ge2\), and \(H\) acts by permuting coordinates and applying permutations within individual coordinates.
4. \(H\) is triply transitive on \(B\) and preserves subsets of size \(k\), \(4\le k<b\), in which each triple of distinct labels occurs exactly once.
   This includes the full affine action on a binary vector space of dimension at least three, with its affine planes of four points.

In each case only finitely many class products \(\gamma\) can occur below a fixed measure bound.
The statement concerns the indicated actions on the labels of conjugates.
For example the natural action of a full symmetric group on individual letters is still possible under (1).

<a id="ordinary"></a>
## An ordinary line in the rational vector space of multiplicative relations

Map the conjugates over \(B\) into the group they generate modulo torsion and tensor it with \(\mathbb Q\).
Its dimension \(r\) is the multiplicative rank there.
The labels give distinct rational projective points in this vector space.
Indeed proportional nonzero vectors would give a multiplicative dependence, and the equal positive heights force the proportionality factor to be \(1\) or \(-1\).
The [block rank and two-height estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) give \(r\gg\sqrt{\log d}\) when \(m\le M\) and \(d\) is large, uniformly over \(B\).

For any finite set of distinct real projective points of rank at least three, some line through two points contains no third point of the set.
This is the Sylvester–Gallai ordinary-line theorem.
For completeness, choose an affine chart containing all the points and project linearly to a real plane, preserving all distinct points and all originally noncollinear triples.
Such maps exist because each of the finitely many failures is a proper polynomial condition on the map.
In the plane minimize the positive distance from a point \(P\) to a line \(L\) spanned by two other points.
If \(L\) contained three marked points, two of them, \(B_1,C_1\), could be chosen on the same closed side of the perpendicular foot, with \(B_1\) the nearer.
Then \(|B_1-C_1|<|P-C_1|\), and the distance from \(B_1\) to \(PC_1\) equals the minimum distance times \(|B_1-C_1|/|P-C_1|\).
It is positive and smaller, a contradiction.
This gives the ordinary line also in the original projective set.

Apply the result to all the labels over \(B\).
For the two labels \(a,b_0\) on the resulting ordinary line, any third label \(z\) gives a multiplicatively independent triple of root representatives.
There are at most \(u=d/(wb)\) possible images of the block, \(b(b-1)\) of the ordered pair in one block, and \(|H_{a,b_0}z|\) of the third label after the ordered pair is fixed.
Each of the three labels has at most \(w\) choices of actual root.
The joint degree \(J\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is at most the absolute joint degree, hence
\[
             J\le u b(b-1)w^3 |H_{a,b_0}z|
               \le 4d^2 |H_{a,b_0}z|,\qquad J\le d^3.
\]
All three heights are \(m/d\).
The [product lower bound of Delsinne, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) gives \((m/d)^3\gg1/(J(\log(3J))^{K_3})\), proving (1).

<a id="pairs"></a>
## A short orbit for each ordered pair in three actions

In the first class let \(L\) be the unique subset through the given pair.
The pair stabilizer preserves \(L\), so each third point of \(L\) has orbit size at most \(k-2\).
Through any point there are \(t=(b-1)/(k-1)\) subsets.
A point outside \(L\) lies on \(k\) distinct subsets joining it to the \(k\) points of \(L\), since two points determine \(L\).
Thus \(t\ge k\), giving \(k(k-1)\le b-1\).
The short orbit has size \(O(\sqrt b)\).

For the subset action take distinct \(k\)-subsets \(A,D\).
If \(|A\setminus D|\ge2\), replace one element of \(A\setminus D\) by one of \(D\setminus A\) to obtain a third \(k\)-subset.
The pair stabilizer induced by ground set permutations gives at most \(k^2\) possible images.
If the two differ in just one element, replace one of the \(k-1\) common elements of \(A\) by the unique one in \(D\setminus A\).
The resulting subset is again distinct from both and has at most \(k-1\) images.
Allowing complementation increases each orbit by at most two as a multiplicative factor, by passing to a subgroup of index at most two.
Since \(b=\binom nk\ge\binom{2k}k\ge2^k\), this supplies a third orbit of size at most \(2(\log_2 b)^2\) for every pair.

In the Cartesian power, for two tuples that differ in at least two coordinates, change the first tuple to the second in exactly one of those coordinates.
There are at most \(a\) possible images of the resulting third tuple under the pair stabilizer.
If the tuples differ in just one coordinate, change the common value at one other coordinate to a different value.
The third tuple then has at most \((a-1)(s-1)\) possible images.
As \(b=s^a\) and \(a\ge2\), both bounds are \(O(\sqrt b\log(2b))\).

In each case (1) for its ordinary pair is incompatible with these upper bounds as \(d\to\infty\) at fixed \(M\), because \(b\le d\).
The degree of \(\gamma\) is therefore bounded.
For bounded \(d\) the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) has all coefficients bounded by \(2^d e^M\), giving the asserted finiteness.

<a id="triples"></a>
## Projection from one label in a triply transitive action

For the fourth class take \(d\) large enough that the multiplicative rank over \(B\) is at least four.
Every triple is independent because one independent triple exists and \(H\) is triply transitive on labels.
Fix any projective label \(o\) and quotient the vector space by its one-dimensional span.
The other projected labels are nonzero and pairwise distinct as projective points, by independence of triples containing \(o\).
Their span in the quotient has rank at least three.
An ordinary line there supplies two labels \(a,b_0\) such that \(o,a,b_0,z\) are multiplicatively independent for every other \(z\) in the block.

The unique \(k\)-subset through this triple has \(k-3\) remaining points, invariant as a set under the three-point stabilizer.
The sets through \(o\), with \(o\) removed, give the pair design from the first class on \(b-1\) points of subset size \(k-1\), so \(k=O(\sqrt b)\).
For one of the fourth points the joint relative degree is bounded by \(u b^3 w^4(k-3)\ll d^{7/2}\), also by \(d^4\).
At four independent roots the same height product gives \(m^4\gg d^{1/2}/(\log(3d))^{K_4}\) for an absolute exponent \(K_4\).
This again bounds \(d\) for fixed \(M\).
In a binary affine space three distinct points span a unique affine plane, and their two independent differences can be sent to any other such pair by an invertible linear map.
This checks the plane system and triple transitivity in the stated example.
