# The elementary divisors of the power suborder for trinomial sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## The additive quotient at the first exponent

Let \(s\ge3\), and let \(a,b\) be relatively prime positive integers.
Write \(g=T^s-T-1\), \(B=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and \(F(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\).
In \(O=\mathbb Z[X]/(F)\) put \(x=X\bmod F\) and \(O_0=\mathbb Z[x^a]\).
As a finite abelian group the additive quotient is
\[
 O/O_0\simeq
 \begin{cases}
  0, &s\equiv0,1\pmod3,\\
  \displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{a-1}(\mathbb Z/2^j\mathbb Z)^2,&s\equiv2\pmod3.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
This includes the empty direct sum when \(a=1\) and allows repeated roots of \(F\).
For \(s\equiv2\pmod3\) the index is \(2^{a(a-1)}\) and the exponent is \(2^{a-1}\).
For every nonconstant monic divisor \(P\) of \(-F\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), including reducible divisors, let \(z=X\bmod P\).
The additive group \(\mathbb Z[z]/\mathbb Z[z^a]\) is a quotient of (1), by the surjection from \(O\).
Thus \(\mathbb Z[z^a]=\mathbb Z[z]\) in the first two residue classes; in the third, \(2^{a-1}\mathbb Z[z]\subseteq\mathbb Z[z^a]\), with equality of the two orders after inverting two.

<a id="change"></a>
## Changing the two coefficients in the presentation

Put \(R=\mathbb Z[T,T^{-1}]\) and \(r=a-1\).
For \(a=1\) the assertion is immediate, so assume \(r\ge1\).
The [block-power presentation](Yin_C_G0345_v217.md#presentation) applies to the monic polynomial \(-F=X^b(-B)(X^a)-g(X^a)\): both seed polynomials have degree \(s\), their constants are units, and they have no common complex root since the identity below would force \(C=T^2+T+1=0\) while \(g\) is nonzero at each such root.
Taking \(T=x^a\), that presentation has generators \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\) and relations
\[
 B e_1=0,\qquad g e_i+B e_{i+1}=0\quad(1\le i<r),\qquad g e_r=0.
\]
Its relation matrix with relations as columns is the transpose of the matrix for multiplication by \(B U+g V\) from binary forms homogeneous of degree \(r-1\) to those of degree \(r\), in monomial bases ordered by increasing exponent of \(V\).
Every invertible linear substitution of the two variables over \(R\) induces invertible substitutions on both spaces of forms.
Thus any invertible linear change of the coefficient pair gives equivalent matrices, including after transposition, and leaves the presented module unchanged up to isomorphism.

Since \(TB+(1+T)g=-C\) and \(T\) is a unit, we may replace the pair by \((g,C)\).
Modulo \(C\) one has \(T^3=1\) and \(T+1=-T^2\).
For \(s\equiv0,1,2\pmod3\), respectively, the polynomial \(g\) is thus congruent to \(-T,-1,2T^2\).
Subtracting a multiple of \(C\) and multiplying by a Laurent monomial unit replaces the first entry by one or two accordingly.
With first entry one the relation at \(e_1\) and the successive middle relations kill every generator.

<a id="invariants"></a>
## The cyclic factors at two

With first entry two the relations become
\[
        2e_1=0,\qquad C e_i=-2e_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<r),\qquad C e_r=0.
\]
First use these relations over \(\mathbb Z[c]\), with an indeterminate \(c\) in place of \(C\), and call the resulting module \(M\).
The relations imply \(2^i e_i=0\) inductively, and reduction of powers of \(c\) shows that the \(e_i\) generate its additive group.
Conversely on \(\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb Z/2^i\mathbb Z\) with generators \(v_i\), define \(cv_i=-2v_{i+1}\) for \(i<r\) and \(cv_r=0\).
The assigned images have orders dividing \(2^i\); they define a \(\mathbb Z[c]\)-action satisfying the presentation.
The maps carrying corresponding generators to one another identify this group with \(M\), and \(c^rM=0\).

Map \(c\) to \(C(T)\).
The ring \(\mathbb Z[T]\simeq\mathbb Z[c][T]/(T^2+T+1-c)\) is free with basis \(1,T\) over \(\mathbb Z[c]\), so the presented module over \(\mathbb Z[T]\) is additively \(M\oplus M\).
On this module \(T(T+1)=c-1\) acts invertibly since \(c\) acts nilpotently.
Consequently \(T\) is invertible there, and localization from \(\mathbb Z[T]\) to \(R\) preserves the group.
This proves (1).
