# Small conditional eigenvalues of Salem carries and the neutral phase Gram matrix

<a id="statement"></a>
## A determinant bound at individual bridge pasts

Let \(\alpha>1\) be Salem of degree \(d\), put \(s=d-2\) and \(m=\log\alpha\), and take integers \(k,L\ge1\).
Use the [exact return law](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#cost) of the independent binary difference digits at time \(T=Lkd\), with \(N=kd\) digits per block.
Write \(\langle Z_i\rangle=L^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^L\mathbb E_{Q_T} Z_i\), where \(Q_T\) is the return law.
Let \(V\) have columns \(v_j=\pi(\alpha^{j-N})\) for \(0\le j<N\), with \(\pi\) the scaled real neutral embedding of [v688](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram), and set \(W_N=VV^{\mathsf T}\).
Let \(\Sigma_i\) be the conditional covariance of \(W_N^{-1/2}\pi(C_{iN})\) given all digits before block \(i\), as in the [conditional prediction problem](Yin_C_G0345_v700.md#salem).
Write \(\lambda_{ia}\) for its \(s\) eigenvalues, including zeros.
Define
\[
 \delta=\max_{0\le j<N}v_j^{\mathsf T}W_N^{-1}v_j,\qquad
 h=\frac d s\left(km+\frac{\log(4T)}{L}\right),\qquad
 \phi(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 (2x-1-\log(2x))/2,&0<x<1/2,\\
 0,&x\ge1/2.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(H\ge0\) let \(x_H\in(0,1/2]\) solve \(2x_H-1-\log(2x_H)=2H\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), put \(t=1/(2\varepsilon)\) and
\[
 E(t,\delta)=\log\!\left(1+2\sqrt{1+t}\,
                  \exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{4t\delta}\right)\right).
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
    \frac1s\left\langle\sum_{a=1}^s\phi(\lambda_{ia}+\varepsilon)\right\rangle
            &\le h+E(t,\delta),\\
    \exp\!\left(\frac1s\langle\log\det(\Sigma_i+\varepsilon I_s)\rangle\right)
            &\ge x_{h+E(t,\delta)} .                                  \tag{1}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The logarithm in (1) is taken at the individual past, before averaging.

For an integer \(b\ge d\), let \(W_b\) be the Gram matrix for \(b\) consecutive neutral columns and put
\(\Delta_b=\log b-s^{-1}\log\det W_b\), independently of their initial exponent.
For every \(b\ge d\) with \(4b\le N\),
\[
                \delta\le \min\!\left(1,\frac{4s}{N}
                                            (\log4+\Delta_b)\right).    \tag{2}
\]
In particular along a shrinking Salem sequence at \(k\sim1/(2m)\) and \(\log(4T)=o(L)\), the condition \(\Delta_d=o(k)\) implies \(\delta\to0\).
The simpler condition \(m\log d\to0\) suffices.
More generally the same conclusion holds when \(\Delta_b=o(k)\) for some \(d\le b\le N/4\) along the sequence.

Under any of these conditions choose \(\varepsilon=\sqrt\delta\).
Then the lower limit of the geometric mean in (1) is at least \(x_{1/2}\), approximately \(0.07930\).
For every \(\eta\ge0\) tending to zero, the mean number of \(\lambda_{ia}\le\eta\), divided by \(s\), tends to zero.
The [earlier critical-scale bound](Yin_C_G0345_v700.md#salem) allows a fraction \(1/(2\log2)\) with no restriction on \(\Delta_b\).
The regularization permits the terminal cut, whose conditional covariance is zero.

<a id="phase"></a>
## A restriction from the large eigenvalues of the deterministic Gram matrix

Without any condition on \(\Delta_b\), take the same shrinking sequence, critical scale and horizon.
For every \(R\to\infty\) and \(\eta\ge0\) tending to zero,
\[
 \frac1s\langle\#\{a:\lambda_{ia}\le\eta\}\rangle
       \le \frac1s\#\{\lambda(W_N)<Rs\}+o(1).                          \tag{3}
\]
In particular, even if the largest column leverage fails to vanish, a positive limiting fraction of near-zero conditional eigenvalues requires a correspondingly large count of phase Gram eigenvalues small relative to their mean \(N\), by taking \(1\ll R\ll k\).
The count on the right can be computed from the neutral conjugates alone.

<a id="laplace"></a>
## A Gaussian bound with a matrix parameter

Let \(A\) be any \(u\)-by-\(N\) real matrix with \(AA^{\mathsf T}=I_u\), \(u\ge1\), and columns \(a_j\) of squared norm at most \(\delta\).
For the unrestricted block \(B\) of difference digits, any \(z\in\mathbb R^u\) and any symmetric \(0\preceq H\preceq tI_u\),
\[
 \log\mathbb E\exp[-(AB-z)^{\mathsf T}H(AB-z)]
       \le -\tfrac12\log\det(I_u+H)+uE(t,\delta).                     \tag{4}
\]
To prove this, represent the exponential by the characteristic function of a standard \(u\)-dimensional Gaussian \(G\) evaluated at \(\sqrt2H^{1/2}(AB-z)\).
The [difference digit characteristic function](Yin_C_G0345_v700.md#laplace) gives the product of the nonnegative terms \(\cos^2(\langle H^{1/2}a_j,G\rangle/\sqrt2)\).
Taking absolute values discards the center phase.
Multiply and divide by \(\exp(-G^{\mathsf T}HG/2)\) in the Gaussian integral and make the change of variables \(G=(I_u+H)^{-1/2}g\).
The determinant factor is \(\det(I_u+H)^{-1/2}\).
Under the remaining standard Gaussian law of \(g\), put
\[
 b_j=(I_u+H)^{-1/2}H^{1/2}a_j,\qquad
 \rho=\frac{t}{1+t},\qquad c_j=\|b_j\|^2/\rho,\qquad
 u_j=b_j/\|b_j\|
\]
for nonzero \(b_j\), omitting zero terms.
Since \(\sum_j b_jb_j^{\mathsf T}=H(I_u+H)^{-1}\preceq\rho I_u\), we have \(0<c_j\le\|a_j\|^2\le\delta\), \(\sum c_j\le u\), and \(\sum c_j u_j u_j^{\mathsf T}\preceq I_u\).
The positive exponential introduced by the division factors as \(\prod_j\exp(\langle b_j,g\rangle^2/2)\).
Thus the integrand is \(\prod_j f_j(\langle u_j,g\rangle)^{c_j}\), with
\[
                  f_j(x)=e^{\rho x^2/2}
                    |\cos(\sqrt{\rho c_j/2}\,x)|^{2/c_j}.
\]
Complete the frame to the identity using eigenvectors of the positive remainder with their eigenvalues as weights and constant functions one.
The [geometric Gaussian Brascamp–Lieb bound](../audits/literature/BCCT08/Yin_U001.md#gaussian-frame) applies first to bounded truncations, then by monotone convergence to the functions just defined.

For a real standard normal \(g_1\), on \(|g_1|\le\pi/\sqrt{2\rho c_j}\) the elementary bound \(|\cos y|\le e^{-y^2/2}\) on \(|y|\le\pi/2\) gives \(f_j(g_1)\le1\).
On the complementary set bound the cosine by one and integrate against the Gaussian density of exponent \(-(1-\rho)x^2/2\).
The two-sided Gaussian tail bound gives
\[
 \mathbb E f_j(g_1)\le1+\frac2{\sqrt{1-\rho}}
                e^{-\pi^2(1-\rho)/(4\rho c_j)}
       \le e^{E(t,\delta)}.
\]
Multiplying with weights \(c_j\) proves (4), also for singular \(H\).

<a id="entropy"></a>
## Applying the bound at each conditional spectrum

At a realized past let \(D_i\) be the relative entropy of the next block law against its unrestricted product law.
The [bridge entropy chain rule](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#cost) gives \(\langle D_i\rangle\le sh\).
The neutral carry at the new cut differs from the new block contribution by a known vector.
For \(A=W_N^{-1/2}V\), use its conditional mean as \(z\) in (4).
The [relative entropy inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v700.md#conditional) gives, at the fixed past,
\[
       D_i\ge\tfrac12\log\det(I_s+H)
                      -\operatorname{tr}(H\Sigma_i)-sE(t,\delta).
\]
Choose \(H\) in the eigenbasis of \(\Sigma_i\) with entries
\(\max\{0,1/(2(\lambda_{ia}+\varepsilon))-1\}\), all at most \(t\).
Replacing \(\Sigma_i\) in the negative trace by \(\Sigma_i+\varepsilon I_s\) weakens the lower bound.
The resulting determinant and trace terms sum to \(\sum_a\phi(\lambda_{ia}+\varepsilon)\).
Averaging proves the first line of (1).
The function \(y\mapsto\phi(e^y)\) is convex, with left derivative zero where it joins its constant branch.
Jensen's inequality over the cuts, pasts and eigenvalues gives the second line.

At \(\delta\to0,\ \varepsilon=\sqrt\delta\), we have \(t\delta=\sqrt\delta/2\), so \(E(t,\delta)\to0\).
At the critical scale \(h\to1/2\), since a shrinking Salem sequence has \(d\to\infty\).
Continuity of \(x_H\) yields the determinant assertion.
The near-zero fraction is at most \((h+E(t,\delta))/\phi(\eta+\varepsilon)\) whenever the denominator is positive, and that denominator tends to infinity.

For (3) compress the output isometry to an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(W_N\) at eigenvalues at least \(Rs\).
Write \(u\) for the retained dimension.
Every unwhitened column has squared length \(s\), so the compressed isometry has squared column lengths at most \(1/R\).
Its conditional covariance is the corresponding compression of \(\Sigma_i\).
For \(u>0\), applying the first inequality of the argument in this section in dimension \(u\), before normalizing, gives
\[
 \left\langle\sum_{a=1}^u \phi(\lambda_{ia}^{\rm comp}+\varepsilon)\right\rangle
                        \le sh+uE(t,1/R).
\]
With \(\varepsilon=R^{-1/2}\) the compressed near-zero count divided by \(s\) tends to zero, uniformly in \(u\).
The same count is zero when \(u=0\).
Eigenvalue interlacing adds at most \(s-u\) to this count for the full matrix, proving (3).

<a id="leverage"></a>
## Comparing the endpoints of overlapping column windows

For all window lengths use base columns \(v_j=\pi(\alpha^j)\).
Translating every exponent by the same integer applies a common orthogonal rotation, preserving Gram determinants and all column leverages.
For \(l\ge d\) let \(W_l=\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}v_jv_j^{\mathsf T}\).
These matrices are positive definite by the [full row rank of the degree block](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram).
The first-column leverage \(g_l=v_0^{\mathsf T}W_l^{-1}v_0\) is nonincreasing in \(l\) by positive semidefinite order.
For \(l>d\) the rank-one determinant identity gives
\[
              1-g_l=\det W_{l-1}/\det W_l .
\]
Indeed removing the first column leaves a translated window of length \(l-1\).
Removing the last column has the same determinant, so its leverage also equals \(g_l\).

Put \(M=\lceil N/2\rceil\).
Every column in the full window can be an endpoint of a consecutive subwindow of length \(M\), since at least one side including that column has length \(M\).
The full-window leverage is at most its leverage in that subwindow, hence at most \(g_M\).
Take \(j_0=b\lfloor M/(2b)\rfloor\) under \(N\ge4b\).
Then \(M/4\le j_0\le M/2\).
Summing the rank-one identity gives
\[
 (M-j_0)g_M\le\sum_{l=j_0+1}^M -\log(1-g_l)
                         =\log(\det W_M/\det W_{j_0}).
\]
The trace bound \(\operatorname{tr}W_M=Ms\) gives \(\det(W_M)^{1/s}\le M\).
The window of length \(j_0\) is the sum of \(j_0/b\) rotated copies of the \(b\)-column Gram.
Concavity of \(\log\det\) gives \(\det(W_{j_0})^{1/s}\ge(j_0/b)\det(W_b)^{1/s}\).
Consequently the logarithmic determinant ratio is at most \(s(\log4+\Delta_b)\), and (2) follows since \(M-j_0\ge N/4\).
The same trace argument gives \(\Delta_b\ge0\).

Finally the [power-basis discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram) gives
\[
  \det(W_d)^{1/s}\ge (d e^{m(d-1)})^{-2/s},\qquad
  \Delta_d\le \log d+\frac{2}{s}\big(\log d+m(d-1)\big).
\]
This proves the stated sufficient condition on \(m\log d\).
Without control of the Gram spectrum or its determinant defect, these estimates leave open whether the critical near-zero fraction vanishes for every shrinking Salem sequence.
