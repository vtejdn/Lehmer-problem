# All exponents of a fixed signed row for a unit and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## An aggregate bound and an effective house estimate

Fix a nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\), write \(V=Y^b A(1/Y)\), \(H=\|A\|_1\) and fix \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
For \(n\ge2\) put \(G_n(X)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X V(X^n)\).
Count every pair \((n,[\delta])\), once per Galois orbit of nontorsion algebraic units, for which \(\delta\) and \(\delta^2\) are zeros of \(G_n\) with \(A(\delta^n)A(\delta^{2n})\ne0\).
Let \(m_\delta\) be the logarithmic Mahler measure of the minimum of \(\delta\), \(d_\delta=[\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]\), and \(t\) the smaller of the two orders of vanishing in this pair.
Then, simultaneously over all exponents,
\[
             \sum_{(n,[\delta])} t d_\delta\le4b,\qquad
             \sum_{(n,[\delta])} t n m_\delta\le3\log H .                 \tag{1}
\]
All pairs with \(t\ge2\) can be found by one factorization of degree at most \(4b\) and exact arithmetic in the resulting number fields.

For any one such double pair suppose that a conjugate of \(\delta\) has modulus \(1<\rho\le2\).
With no restriction on \(b\) one has
\[
 \begin{cases}
 H\ge \rho^{3n}/(1200n^2), & \rho^n\ge1152n^2,\\
 H\ge \rho^{3n}/(1152n^2)^3, & \text{in all cases}.
 \end{cases}                                                            \tag{2}
\]
In particular a passing row of the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) satisfies
\[
              \log\rho < \frac{\log2}{24}
                           +\frac{\log(1152n^2)}{n}.
\]
For a fixed Salem number \(\delta\le2\), the first line of (2) eventually bounds \(\log H\) below by \(3n m_\delta-2\log n-\log1200\).
The fixed-input rate \(3m_\delta\) is attained in the limit at either sign and any widths \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\) by the [signed cost theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#statement).

<a id="square"></a>
## One polynomial for all exponents

In \(\mathbb Z[Y]\) form
\[
  K(Y)=\frac{A(Y^2)V(Y)^2+\varepsilon V(Y^2)A(Y)^2}{2}.
\]
The two summands have equal reductions modulo two by Frobenius, so \(K\) is integral and \(\|K\|_1\le H^3\).
Write \(f=-\varepsilon A/V\).
If \(K=0\), the identity \(f(Y^2)=f(Y)^2\) holds in \(\mathbb Q(Y)\).
Such a nonzero rational function has no zero or pole in \(\mathbb C^\times\), because every \(2^j\)-th preimage of any such zero or pole would again be one of the same type.
It is \(cY^a\) for \(a\in\mathbb Z\), and the identity gives \(c=1\).
At a genuine contact this would give \(\delta=\delta^{na}\), which contradicts nontorsion for \(n\ge2\).
Thus the zero case has no pairs.

Otherwise set \(\beta=\delta^n\).
At both specified points the value equation makes \(A,V\) nonzero and gives \(f(\beta)=\delta,\ f(\beta^2)=\delta^2\).
In particular \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
The polynomial identity
\[
  2K(X^n)=G_n(X^2)V(X^n)^2+
       \varepsilon V(X^{2n})G_n(X)\bigl(A(X^n)-\varepsilon X V(X^n)\bigr)
\]
gives order at least \(t\) at \(X=\delta\) on the left, since squaring is unramified there.
Powering by \(n\) is likewise unramified there, so the minimum \(S\) of \(\beta\) divides \(K\) to order at least \(t\).
For a given orbit of \(\beta\), the formula \(\delta=f(\beta)\) determines its seed orbit.
Two possible exponents would then satisfy \(f(\beta)^{n_1}=f(\beta)^{n_2}=\beta\) for the same embedding, forcing equality by nontorsion.
Distinct pairs therefore consume distinct irreducible factors of \(K\).
Their degrees are \(d_\delta\), and equality of the fields gives \(\log M(S)=n m_\delta\) by the absolute-height power law.
Monic division, multiplicativity of measure, \(\deg K\le4b\) and \(M(K)\le\|K\|_1\) give (1).

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Recovering the exponent and the two contacts

For each repeated monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor \(S\) of \(K\), work at \(\beta=Y\bmod S\).
Discard a factor if either row vanishes at \(\beta\) or \(\beta^2\).
Set \(u=f(\beta)\).
The graph has a double zero at \(u\) exactly when \(u^n=\beta\) and \(n\beta f'(\beta)=u\), since its local equation is \(X-f(X^n)=0\).
The exponent is thus uniquely prescribed by
\[
       n=\frac{A(\beta)V(\beta)}
               {\beta(A'V-AV')(\beta)} .
\]
Discard a zero denominator and any value outside the rational integers at least two.
Check \(u^n=\beta,\ f(\beta^2)=u^2,\ n\beta^2 f'(\beta^2)=u^2\).
These are sufficient for genuine double contacts at both \(u,u^2\).
Since \(\beta\) is a nontorsion unit and \(u^n=\beta\), \(u\) is a nontorsion unit too.
The [exact implementation and controls](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/versions/v3/readme.md#test) work with these residue fields.

## Using two distinct exterior radii

Choose an embedding in which \(c=\delta\) has modulus \(\rho\).
Reverse the two rows at width \(b\), absorbing the sign in the denominator row so that their ratio is \(f(1/T)\).
Remove a common power of \(T\), obtaining polynomials \(u(T)=\sum a_jT^j,\ v(T)=\sum b_jT^j\), each of one-norm at most \(H\), with \(C_0=\max(|a_0|,|b_0|)\ge1\).
Put \(C_1=\max(|a_1|,|b_1|)\), allowing missing coefficients to be zero.
Let \(z=c^{-n}\), \(x=|z|\).
At \((T,s)=(z,c),(z^2,c^2)\), the two contacts give the equations
\[
 \sum_j(a_j-s b_j)T^j=0,\qquad
 n\sum_j j(a_j-s b_j)T^j=-s\sum_j b_jT^j.                              \tag{3}
\]
The negative sign comes from reversing the independent variable.

Suppose \(x\le1/2\), so \(1\le|s|\le4\).
At \(T=z^2\), solve the second equation for \(b_0\), then the first for \(a_0\).
Using \(j|T|^{j-2}\le2\) for \(j\ge2\) gives
\[
 |b_0|\le(2n+1)C_1x^2+(4n+1)Hx^4,\qquad
 C_0\le24n(C_1x^2+Hx^4).                                                \tag{4}
\]
At \(T=z\), subtract \(n\) times the first equation from the derivative sum in (3), removing the \(j=1\) term in that sum.
Since \((j-1)x^{j-2}\le1\) for \(j\ge2\), solving for \(b_1\), then using the value equation for \(a_1\), gives
\[
 |b_1|x\le(2n+1)(C_0+Hx^2),\qquad
 C_1\le24n(C_0/x+Hx).
\]
Consequently \(C_0\le576n^2x C_0+(576n^2+24n)Hx^3\), using \(x\le1\).
When \(x\le1/(1152n^2)\), the first term can be absorbed and \(1\le C_0\le1200n^2 Hx^3\) since \(n\ge2\).
For \(x>1/(1152n^2)\) one has \(H\ge1>(1152n^2)^{-3}x^{-3}\).
This proves (2), including the asserted consequence under \(H<2^{n/8}\).
For a Salem number, its unique exterior conjugate is real positive and gives \(m_\delta=\log\rho\).
Every power retains full degree since no other conjugate can have that same exterior powered value.
The two dyadic levels therefore satisfy the hypotheses of the [all-index construction](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests), whose least-cost assertion applies before imposing any budget.
