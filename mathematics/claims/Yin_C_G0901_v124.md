# The signs of a square root of the pole unit at all coefficient primes and real places

<a id="statement"></a>
## A simultaneous restriction in the positive branch

Let \(q\ge2\), \(D=q(q+1)\), \(L=2q+1\).
Use the [norm-one Salem trace data](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion): \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\) is totally real of degree \(n\), one conjugate of \(t\) is \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}>2\) with \(\alpha>1\), the others belong to \((-2,2)\), and \(\delta=1-D(t-2)\) is a totally positive unit of norm one.
Suppose that \(\delta=\gamma^2\) in \(F\).
The actual positive-sign, short-exponent-one section has this property whenever its long exponent \(v\) is odd. [Square condition](Yin_C_G0901_v110.md#squares)
The following tests do not require knowledge of \(v\).

At every odd prime \(\ell\mid D\), choose an extension of the normalized \(\ell\)-adic valuation to a common normal closure.
Each conjugate of \(\gamma\) is congruent to \(+1\) or \(-1\) to valuation at least \(e_\ell=v_\ell(D)\).
Write \(\Delta_\ell\) for the difference of the two counts divided by \(n\).
At \(2\), if \(e_2\ge3\), use the signs at valuation at least \(e_2-1\) and the same definition.
Set \(a_\ell=e_\ell\) at odd primes and \(a_2=\max(e_2-2,0)\), omitting zero weights.
Then
\[
       \sum_{\ell\mid D,\ a_\ell>0}a_\ell\log\ell\,\Delta_\ell^2
              \le\frac{\log D+2\log(36n)+2\log\alpha}{n}.                \tag{1}
\]
In particular both signs have proportion \(1/2+o(1)\) simultaneously at every tested prime as \(n\to\infty\), uniformly for these data.
A place of \(F\) above such a prime uses just one of the signs; its local degree is at most \(n(1+|\Delta_\ell|)/2\).

At the real places \(\gamma\) cannot have constant sign when \(n\ge10\).
In particular \(\delta\) is not a fourth power in \(F\) at those degrees.
Along every sequence with \(n\to\infty\) and \(1/D\to a\), the conjugates of \(\gamma/\sqrt D\) converge in distribution to the equally signed square roots of \(a+2-2\cos\theta\), with \(\theta\) uniform on \([0,\pi]\).
Thus each sign occurs asymptotically half the time even after restricting \(t\) to any fixed interval of positive arcsine mass with endpoints in \([-2,2]\).
These are restrictions on the signed norm and square test, and do not establish the existence of a section.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Forced valuations in the discriminant

The \(n\) conjugates \(\gamma_i\) are distinct algebraic integers since \(t=2+(1-\gamma^2)/D\) generates \(F\).
For each of them \(v_\ell((\gamma_i-1)(\gamma_i+1))\ge e_\ell\).
At odd \(\ell\) exactly one factor has positive valuation.
At \(2\), if the smaller of the two valuations is less than one, they coincide since the difference is 2.
If the larger exceeds one, the smaller is exactly one.
Consequently all \(\gamma_i-1\) have valuation at least \(e_2/2\) when \(e_2=1,2\).
For \(e_2\ge3\) the two sign classes are disjoint, all pair differences have valuation at least one, and differences within a class have valuation at least \(e_2-1\).
The sign classes are fixed by the local absolute Galois group since the centers are rational, so every group of embeddings from a single place uses a single sign.
Changing the prime of the normal closure just permutes the roots.

Write \(\mathfrak d=\prod_{i<j}(\gamma_i-\gamma_j)^2>0\), the nonzero integer discriminant of the monic minimum.
The number of unordered pairs of the same sign is \((n^2(1+\Delta_\ell^2)/2-n)/2\).
Using it in the preceding valuation inequalities gives
\[
 \log\mathfrak d\ge
   \binom n2\log D+\frac{n^2}2
       \sum_{\ell\mid D,\ a_\ell>0}a_\ell\log\ell\,\Delta_\ell^2
                              -\frac n2\log D .                           \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the valuation at each tested prime is at least \(e_\ell\binom n2+
a_\ell(n^2\Delta_\ell^2-n)/2\); for the untested power of two only the baseline is needed.

<a id="real"></a>
## Matching archimedean determinant bounds

Use the monic Chebyshev trace polynomials \(C_j(Z+Z^{-1})=Z^j+Z^{-j}\) for \(j\ge1\), putting \(C_0=1\) for this basis only.
Take monic polynomial columns of successive degrees \(2j,2j+1\) in the Vandermonde determinant:
\[
       U_{2j}(X)=(-D)^j C_j(2+(1-X^2)/D),\qquad U_{2j+1}(X)=XU_{2j}(X).
\]
For all neutral trace places \(1<|\gamma_i|<L=\sqrt{1+4D}\); at the one remaining place \(0<|\gamma_i|<1\), and its Chebyshev evaluation of index \(j\) is at most \(2\alpha^j\).
After dividing degree \(k\)'s column by \(D^{k/2}\), all neutral entries have modulus at most six, since \(L/\sqrt D<3\).
All entries of the distinguished row have modulus at most \(6\alpha^{n/2}\).
Hadamard's inequality therefore gives
\[
                 \log\mathfrak d\le\binom n2\log D+n\log(36n)+n\log\alpha.
\]
Combining with (2) proves (1).
The [trace coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v884.md#moments) gives \(D\le c_1<4(n-1)\) for the positive first coefficient \(c_1\) of the minimum of \(t-2\), because \(D\mid c_1\).
Also \(\alpha<(q+1)/q\) by \(0<t-2<1/D\) at the distinguished place.
These facts give the asserted uniform decay in (1).

If all \(\gamma_i\) have one real sign, they lie in an interval of length \(L\).
The monic Chebyshev columns on this interval have suprema at most \(2(L/4)^k\) in degree \(k\ge1\), by scaling the usual trace polynomials from \([-2,2]\).
They give \(\mathfrak d\le(4n)^n(L/4)^{n(n-1)}\).
Comparison with the baseline of (2) would require
\[
                 (n-1)\log(4\sqrt D/L)\le\log(4n)+\tfrac12\log D.
\]
For \(D\ge6\), \(4\sqrt D/L\ge4\sqrt6/5>1.95\).
As \(D<4n\), this inequality fails for \(n\ge10\): it already fails at 10 with \(1.95\), and the left side increases faster than \(\frac32\log(4n)\).
Both real sign choices of a square root of a fourth power would have constant sign, proving the exclusion.

<a id="limit"></a>
## The real limiting measure, including both signs

Put \(x_i=\gamma_i/\sqrt D\).
Their support is uniformly bounded.
Apart from the distinguished root their squares belong to \([1/D,4+1/D]\).
Any weak subsequential limit \(\nu\) at \(1/D\to a\) is supported on \(E_a=\{x:a\le x^2\le4+a\}\).
By (2), after subtracting the scaling contribution \(\binom n2\log D\), the average over ordered distinct pairs of \(\log|x_i-x_j|\) has lower limit at least zero.
Here \(\log D=O(\log n)\).
Truncate the logarithm below at \(-H\) to get a bounded continuous kernel on the compact interval; the diagonal contributes only \(O(H/n)\).
Pass first to the weak limit and then let \(H\to\infty\).
Thus the logarithmic energy in the convention \(\iint\log|x-y|\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y)\) is at least zero.

The probability \(\mu_a\) described in the statement has constant log potential zero on \(E_a\).
Averaging the two signs gives half the log potential of the arcsine distribution on \([a,a+4]\) evaluated at \(x^2\), which vanishes there by the circle integral for \(2\cos\theta\).
For completeness, a signed measure \(\xi\) of mass zero on a compact real interval and finite mutual logarithmic energies has nonpositive energy, equal to zero only when \(\xi=0\).
Indeed first use the kernel \(\frac12\log((x-y)^2+\epsilon^2)\).
Integrating \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon t}(1-\cos(t(x-y)))\,dt/t=\frac12\log(1+(x-y)^2/\epsilon^2)\) against \(\xi\otimes\xi\) gives \(-\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon t}|\widehat\xi(t)|^2dt/t\), since constants integrate to zero.
The signed double integral has the required limit by applying monotone convergence separately to its finite positive pairings after subtraction from a common upper bound.
Equality forces the continuous Fourier transform to vanish identically and hence the measure to be zero.
Apply this to \(\nu-\mu_a\); both self-energies are finite and the cross energy is zero by the potential identity.
It follows that \(\nu=\mu_a\), establishing the limiting law and the conditional sign balance.
