# Degree from two exponent scales and exclusion of a specified rational factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two-prime divisor at every admissible offset

For a positive integer \(A\) put \(L_0=5^A\), \(b_a=\lfloor(A-a)\log_7 5\rfloor\), and \(n=5^A7^{b_0}\).
For odd \(k\) with \(0<k<n,\ \gcd(n,k)=1\), put \(l=n-k\) and
\[
      P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1,\qquad
      B_A=\prod_{\substack{0\le a\le A\\2\mid a}}
                     \bar\Phi_{3\cdot5^a7^{b_a}}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[X] .
\]
For all sufficiently large \(A\), **no admissible \(k\)** permits a rational factor of \(P_l\) reducing to \(B_A\) after monic normalization.
This binary divisor has a unique reciprocal monic lift over \(\mathbb Z_2\) for every one of these offsets.
The obstruction applies to this specified selection of complete conductors.
It leaves open the measures of factors with other reductions.

More generally, for the same five-term formula at any odd \(n>1\) prime to three and any admissible \(k\), every nonconstant rational divisor has degree
\[
                                d\gg\sqrt{nk}                              \tag{1}
\]
with an absolute implied constant.
This degree restriction does not require the reduction to be a union of complete conductors.

<a id="two"></a>
## A height and degree bound for separated powers

Let \(F(U,V)=\sum_{j=0}^s A_j(U)V^j\) be a nonzero polynomial in \(\mathbb Z[U,V]\), put \(B=\deg_U F\), and let \(L\ge2\) bound the sum of the absolute values of all its coefficients.
Suppose \(n\ge (B+1)k>0\) with \(n,k\) integers, and consider the specialization \(P=F(X^k,X^n)\).
For a monic irreducible nontorsion unit divisor \(S\) of degree \(d\), put \(m=m(S)>0\), with logarithmic Mahler measure.
If the \(A_j\) do not all vanish at \(\alpha^k\), where \(S(\alpha)=0\), then
\[
        m\ge\frac{k}{20d\log L},\qquad
        (n-Bk)m\le d\log L,\qquad
        d\ge\frac{\sqrt{k(n-Bk)}}{\sqrt{20}\log L}.                      \tag{2}
\]
There is no restriction on \(s\).
If all \(A_j\) vanish there, then \(B\ge1\) and \(m\ge1/\sqrt{40B}\).
The lower measure bound in (2) still holds in this case.

Indeed the sets of exponents from two consecutive nonempty powers of \(V\) are separated by at least \(n-Bk\ge k\).
Within each such set adjacent exponents differ by at least \(k\).
There are no colliding terms.
On an exterior root of \(S\), leading-term dominance gives \(k\log|\alpha|\le\log L\).
Applying the same argument at the other end, after removing a monomial, gives \(-k\log|\alpha|\le\log L\) on an interior root.
Thus the sum of squares of all the logarithmic displacements of the conjugates is at most \(2m(\log L)/k\), using the unit condition.
The [quadratic radial inequality](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) bounds this sum below by \(1/(10d)\).
This proves the first bound.

If the specialized coefficients are not all zero, let \(j\) be the largest index with \(A_j(\alpha^k)\ne0\).
It is the same index at every conjugate \(x\) of \(\alpha\).
Since \(P(\alpha)=0\) and \(\alpha\ne0\), one has \(j\ge1\).
Put \(u=x^k,\ z=x^n\).
The equation at \(x\) for \(|z|>1\), and the bound by the sum of the coefficients for \(|z|\le1\), give
\[
          |A_j(u)|\max(1,|z|)
              \le \sum_{i=0}^s |A_i(u)|
              \le L\max(1,|u|)^B .
\]
The product of \(A_j(x^k)\) over the roots of \(S\) is a nonzero rational integer.
Summing the logarithms gives the second bound in (2); combining it with the first gives the third.
If the specialized coefficients all vanish, the nonzero coefficient polynomial of smallest degree already shows \(\delta=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\le B\).
The norm of \(\alpha\) into that field is a nontorsion unit of degree at most \(\delta\) and measure at most \(m\), by the [norm computation at the powered root](Yin_C_G0345_v240.md#height).
The same radial inequality then gives \(40\delta m^2\ge1\).

<a id="five"></a>
## The degree in the five-term family

The parent \(P_l\) is monic, has constant term one, and has no cyclotomic factor for the stated \(n,l\), by the [odd-order and sixth-order test](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts).
All its irreducible factors therefore meet the nontorsion unit conditions above.
For a root \(x\) put \(U=x^k,\ V=x^n\).
Multiplication by \(U\) gives the relation
\[
              (U-2)V^2+UV+U(1-2U)=0 .                                  \tag{3}
\]
Its coefficients never vanish simultaneously for \(U\ne0\); the coefficient sum in absolute value is seven and its \(U\)-degree is two.
When \(k\le n/4\), (2) yields \(d\gg\sqrt{nk}\) for each irreducible factor.

For \(k>n/4\) the first and last gaps of \(P_l\) itself still equal \(k\).
At either end the coefficient length gives \(|\log|x||\le(\log7)/k\) on all conjugates.
The same radial inequality gives \(m\ge k/(20d\log7)\), and \(m\le m(P_l)\le\log 7\) by the coefficient length and Jensen's formula.
It follows that \(d\gg k\gg\sqrt{nk}\) in this range.
Every nonconstant rational divisor contains an irreducible factor, so (1) follows without a restriction on its number of exterior roots.

<a id="exclusion"></a>
## Comparing the allowed degrees with the trace obstruction

The degree of \(B_A\) is \(O(A\sqrt n)\) by the [complete conductor count](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors).
If a monic rational divisor reduced to \(B_A\), it would have exactly that degree, since the leading coefficient survives reduction.
Equation (1) would therefore give \(k\ll A^2\) with an absolute implied constant.
For sufficiently large \(A\), this implies \(k<5^A/490\).
At every such admissible offset the [two trace congruences](Yin_C_G0345_v246.md#statement) preclude a rational lift.
This proves the all-offset exclusion.
Squarefreeness and the [reciprocal Hensel lift](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) still supply the indicated dyadic divisor.
