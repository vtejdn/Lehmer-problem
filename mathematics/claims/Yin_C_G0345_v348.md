# A finite test for cyclotomic multiples supported on few prime residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Deciding the existence of an unrestricted multiplier

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic and irreducible, with unit constant coefficient, support differences of gcd one and degree \(D\).
Assume \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\), where \(m=\log M\) and \(\theta>1\) solves \(z^3-z-1=0\).
Fix \(k\ge2\), set \(t=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\), and take an odd prime \(p>k\).
Put
\[
 H=\begin{cases}
 2,&t=1,\\
 \lfloor 2p/\lfloor(p-1)^{1/t}\rfloor\rfloor,&t\ge2 .
 \end{cases}
\]
If \(24H<p\), one can decide in finitely many exact arithmetic steps whether some product \(C\) of cyclotomic polynomials makes \(CP\) occupy exactly \(k\) residue classes modulo \(p\).
The test can produce a multiplier in every affirmative case and takes no upper bound on its degree as input.

For \(m(P)<\eta_k\), with \(\eta_k\) the [projected-support gap](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement), the same test over all primes satisfying \(24H<p\) is finite.
In fact a prime admitting a multiplier satisfies \(p\le(4(D+12)+1)^t+1\) in that range.
For \(k=2,3\), throughout the full interval \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\), any prime admitting a multiplier satisfies
\[
                              p\le D+48.                              \tag{1}
\]
For instance no cyclotomic multiple of the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#minus-delay-progression) has just two or three occupied residues at a prime \(p>58\).
These are individual-input tests and upper bounds on admissible primes.
The existence of suitable growing primes and multipliers along arbitrary sequences of increasing degrees and decreasing positive measures remains open.

<a id="carrier"></a>
## A finite list of possible factors carrying the original input

The [centered lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift) of a successful multiple, after removing [the common row factor](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage), has fast width \(h\le H\) and diagonal \(X=x^p,\ Y=x^b\) for some \(0<b<p\).
By [the factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#statement), exactly one irreducible factor \(B\) contains \(P\) on this diagonal, and the other factors are of the form \(\Phi_m(X^aY^v)\) up to Laurent units.
The fast width \(s\) of \(B\) belongs to \([1,H]\).
Its diagonal, shifted to minimum exponent zero and made monic, equals \(J=P C_0\) for a product of cyclotomics with
\[
              \deg J\le d_{\max}=\left\lfloor\frac{Dp}{p-24H}\right\rfloor .
\]
There are finitely many possible \(C_0\), all enumerable from this degree budget including repetitions.
Indeed \(\varphi(m)^2\ge m/2\) by multiplicativity, since \(\varphi(\ell^a)^2/\ell^a=\ell^{a-2}(\ell-1)^2\) for each prime power, with every factor at least one except for \(2^1\), whose factor is \(1/2\).

Normalize \(B\) to minimum \(Y\)-exponent zero and multiply by a power of \(X\) so the smallest diagonal exponent \(r\) belongs to \([0,p-1]\).
For each \(b,r,J\), reconstruct a possible \(B\) from its monomials.
For a term of degree \(e\) of \(J\), find \(j\in[0,H]\) satisfying \(bj\equiv e+r\bmod p\).
There is at most one choice because \(H<p\).
If any term lacks such a \(j\), discard the candidate.
Otherwise place its coefficient at \(X^{(e+r-bj)/p}Y^j\), require minimum \(j=0\), and read the fast width \(s\).
This gives a finite list containing every possible normalized carrier up to sign.
It is harmless to retain additional candidates with the same prescribed diagonal, since an affirmative witness below is checked by its actual product.

<a id="collisions"></a>
## Resolving the unbounded exponents of the cyclotomic factors

Choose an ordered finite list of pairs \((m_i,v_i)\) with \(v_i>0\) and
\[
                       \sum_i v_i\varphi(m_i)\le H-s .
\]
This covers every other factor with multiplicity, since widths add, the row gcd excludes pure base factors, and reversing the primitive character only changes a Laurent unit.
The number of lists is finite by the totient bound, with the empty list allowed.
The base exponents \(a_i\in\mathbb Z\) remain unrestricted.
The product to examine is
\[
                   B(X,Y)\prod_i\Phi_{m_i}(X^{a_i}Y^{v_i}).
\]
In its formal expansion a term has an integer coefficient, a fixed fast exponent \(j\), and a base exponent given by an integral affine form in the \(a_i\).
For each fast exponent separately, enumerate every partition of its terms according to equality of their base exponents.
Within a block impose equalities of affine forms, and between distinct blocks in the same row impose inequalities of the form \(f\ne0\).
A block survives exactly when the sum of its integer coefficients is nonzero.
Thus the number of surviving rows is determined by the partitions before the exponent values are found.

The equalities are a finite linear Diophantine system.
Integer row and column operations give either inconsistency or the full solution \(a=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^r n_j v'_j\) with integer \(n_j\).
Discard a partition if an inequality vanishes identically on that lattice.
Every other partition with consistent equalities is realizable: for \(r=0\) check the unique point, and for \(r>0\) put \((n_1,\ldots,n_r)=(N,N^2,\ldots,N^r)\).
Each remaining forbidden equality becomes a nonzero univariate polynomial, so testing successive nonnegative integers \(N\) finds one avoiding all of them.
Keeping exactly the realizable partitions with \(k\) surviving rows decides the test.

Indeed distinct rows in the fast strip of width \(H<p\) remain distinct residues, and distinct terms in each row remain distinct on the diagonal.
For every added factor \(pa_i+bv_i\ne0\) since \(0<v_i<p\).
Its specialization \(\Phi_{m_i}(x^{pa_i+bv_i})\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials up to a signed Laurent monomial, also for negative exponents by reversal.
After normalizing the full product, its diagonal is therefore \(P\) times an actual product of cyclotomics.
This proves sufficiency and gives a witness.
Necessity follows from the factorization and width bound for a successful representation.
Removing the original common row factor is permissible because the quotient retains \(P\) and exactly the same occupied residues.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Bounds on the support prime and the two smallest row counts

If \(m(P)<\eta_k\), the [original-degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#factor) yields \(p/(2h)\le D+12\).
For \(t\ge2\), combine this with \(h\le2p/\lfloor(p-1)^{1/t}\rfloor\) to obtain the stated bound.
For \(t=1\) use \(h=2\) to obtain \(p\le4(D+12)\).

There is also a geometric distinction independent of the possibly small value of \(\eta_k\).
If the carrier's support is collinear, it has the form \(f(X^uY^v)\) up to a Laurent unit with \((u,v)\) primitive and \(v\ne0\).
Its one-variable polynomial \(f\), normalized at minimum exponent zero, is irreducible by the irreducibility of \(B\), and it has no root of unity because \(B\) carries \(P\).
Since \(pu+bv\ne0\), its diagonal has no cyclotomic root either.
It therefore equals \(P\) up to a signed monomial and possible inversion of its argument.
The support differences of \(P\) have gcd one, so \(|pu+bv|=1\).
In this case \(D=\deg f\le |v|\deg f=s\).
In the noncollinear case, the lattice area argument giving \(D\ge p/(2s)-12\) applies without a small-shell assumption.

For \(k=2,3\) and \(p>48\), the lift has \(h=2\), even for two opposite classes with no central row.
Here \(s\le2\), so when \(d=\deg J<p\), the support of \(J\) has at most three terms.
Indeed the carrier occupies at most three rows, and an interval of length less than \(p\) contains at most one exponent per residue.
The carrier is reciprocal up to a Laurent unit, hence \(J\) is reciprocal up to sign with unit endpoints.
A binomial with unit endpoints has zero logarithmic measure.
A trinomial of this kind must be \(x^{2e}+a x^e+1\) with integer \(a\), since reversal fixes the middle term and forces the positive sign.
Its measure is zero for \(|a|\le2\), and for \(|a|\ge3\) its multiplicative measure is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>\theta\) by the quadratic formula and power substitution.
Thus \(m(J)=m(P)\) in the stipulated interval forces \(d\ge p\).
The [degree bound for the carrier](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#statement) gives \(D\ge d(1-24s/p)\ge p-48\).
Primes at most \(48\) already satisfy (1), proving the all-prime assertion.
