# A uniform Mahler gap with bounded fast Fourier degree

The strategy researcher for [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) developed the bounded-fast-shape extension below.
It uses the critical-scale packing and local analytic estimates of [the relative cosine comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#packing), with an explicit finite-order replacement for the fast cosine tests and a separate global estimate for large slow energy.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative comparison uniform in the fast coefficients

Fix an integer \(m\ge1\).
Let
\[
 A(v)=\sum_{j=-m}^m a_j e^{ijv},\qquad a_{-j}=\overline{a_j},
 \qquad \lambda=\max_{1\le |j|\le m}|a_j|>0,
\]
and let \(b(t)\) be any real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\), where \(1\le d<n\) are integers.
Coefficients of both polynomials may vary without a height bound.
With normalized circle measure \(d\nu=dt/(2\pi)\), put
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mu&=\int\log|A(nt)-b(t)|\,d\nu(t),\\
 I&=\iint\log|A(v)-b(t)|\,d\nu(v)d\nu(t),\\
 D&=n/d,\qquad \beta_m=2m\log2,\qquad J=I-\log\lambda+\beta_m.
 \end{split}
\]
Then \(J\ge0\), and there are constants \(D_m,C_m>0\), depending only on \(m\), such that for \(D\ge D_m\),
\[
          |\mu-I|\le\frac{C_m}{D}
                         \{1+J+\log(2D)\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
All phase shifts of the fast polynomial are included in its coefficients.
Multiple zeros and reducible specializations are allowed.
For constant \(b\), the two means agree exactly.

Consequently the absolute discrepancy tends to zero uniformly when \(d/n\to0\) and the normalized diagonal means \(\mu-\log\lambda\) are bounded above.
Multiplying both \(A\) and \(b\) by a positive scalar shifts the two means by the same logarithm.
In the [endpoint Taylor family](Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#statement) with \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\), scaling both terms by \(e^{-\mu_{n,d}}\) makes the diagonal mean zero and preserves a discrepancy bounded away from zero as \(D\to\infty\).

For the integral consequence, let \(\mathcal A(Y),\mathcal B(X)\) be symmetric integer Laurent polynomials, with
\[
 1\le s=\max\{|j|:[Y^j]\mathcal A\ne0\}\le m,
 \qquad [Y^s]\mathcal A=[Y^{-s}]\mathcal A=1,
 \qquad \max\{|j|:[X^j]\mathcal B\ne0\}\le d<n.
\]
Define the monic reciprocal integer unit polynomial
\[
                   P(z)=z^{sn}\{\mathcal A(z^n)-\mathcal B(z)\}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
There are \(\epsilon_m,\eta_m>0\), depending only on \(m\), such that
\[
              d\le\epsilon_m n,\qquad M(P)>1
                       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                          \log M(P)\ge\eta_m.                 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(d=0\) is also permitted and handled by exact equality of the means.
The assertion concerns the entire polynomial, without a bound on the number or sizes of the slow coefficients and without separability or irreducibility assumptions.
In this integral class, (1) also gives absolute convergence at bounded original diagonal measure when \(d=o(n)\).

<a id="normalization"></a>
## Normalization and the slow logarithmic energy

Replace \(A\) by \(\widehat A=(A-a_0)/\lambda\), and replace \(b\) by \(\widehat b=(b-a_0)/\lambda\).
Their difference has been divided by \(\lambda\), so both logarithmic means decrease by \(\log\lambda\) and their discrepancy is unchanged.
In the analytic proof write \(A,b,\mu,I\) for these normalized quantities.
Thus \(A\) has zero mean, its largest nonconstant Fourier coefficient has modulus one, and \(\|A\|_\infty\le2m\).

For any real \(c\), the polynomial \(z^m(A(v)-c)\), with \(z=e^{iv}\), has degree at most \(2m\) and still has a coefficient of modulus one at a noncentral exponent.
For a polynomial of degree \(h\), each coefficient has modulus at most \(2^h\) times its multiplicative Mahler measure: expand its elementary symmetric functions and bound every root factor by \(\max(1,|\alpha|)\).
It follows that the fast fiber mean
\[
               j(c)=\int\log|A(v)-c|\,d\nu(v)
                           \ge-\beta_m.                       \tag{4}
\]
This also proves the claimed \(J\ge0\) before normalization.

If \(|c|\ge4m\), then \(|A(v)-c|\ge |c|/2\), so \(j(c)\ge\log|c|-\log2\).
If \(|c|<4m\), use (4).
Consequently
\[
 \log^+|c|\le j(c)+\beta_m+\log(4m),\qquad
 E:=\int(1+\log^+|b|)\,d\nu
                    \le1+I+\beta_m+\log(4m).                  \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(E\ge1\) and \(E\le C_m(1+J)\), with \(J=I+\beta_m\) in normalized notation.

<a id="fast"></a>
## Uniform finite-order tests on a fast period

Put \(R=2m\).
There is \(\kappa_m>0\) such that every normalized fast polynomial satisfies
\[
             \max_{1\le k\le R}|A^{(k)}(v)|\ge\kappa_m
                       \quad\text{at every real }v.            \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the vector of these derivatives is obtained from
\((a_j e^{ijv})_{j\in\{-m,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,m\}}\)
by the fixed matrix with entries \((ij)^k\), \(1\le k\le2m\).
This matrix is invertible: factor one nonzero frequency from each column and use the Vandermonde determinant for the distinct frequencies.
The input vector has maximum norm one.
The inverse matrix norm therefore supplies \(\kappa_m\), even when some extreme coefficients vanish.

The derivatives of \(A\) through order \(R+1\) have uniform bounds depending only on \(m\).
Choose a sufficiently fine fixed mesh on one period.
At each mesh interval, select a derivative order from (6) at its center.
The derivative bound through order \(R+1\) makes this selected derivative keep its sign and have modulus at least \(\kappa_m/2\) throughout that interval.
The period is thus covered by \(O_m(1)\) intervals with this property.

Choose \(\delta_m>0\) sufficiently small that \(\delta_m\le\kappa_m/4\) and \(2\pi\delta_m\le1/4\).
If a real function \(h\) on a fast period satisfies
\[
                    |h^{(k)}|\le\delta_m,\qquad 1\le k\le R,
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
then on every selected interval a derivative of \(A-h\) of order at most \(R\) has modulus at least \(\kappa_m/4\).
For a real \(C^k\) function with \(|g^{(k)}|\ge\rho>0\) on an interval, its sublevel set \(|g|\le u\) has length at most \(C_k(u/\rho)^{1/k}\).
To see this, if that set has length \(l>0\), choose \(k+1\) points in it separated by at least a fixed multiple of \(l/k\), and apply the divided-difference mean-value formula.
The divided difference is at least \(\rho/k!\) in absolute value and at most \(C_k u/l^k\).
Applying this on the selected intervals yields
\[
       \frac1{2\pi}|\{v\text{ in a period}:|A(v)-h(v)|\le u\}|
                           \le C_m u^{1/R},\qquad 0<u\le1.     \tag{8}
\]
No derivative of \(h\) of order \(R+1\) is required.

For any constant \(c\), the supremum of \(|A-c|\) on a period is at least one, by its Fourier coefficient of modulus one.
Fixing one value of \(h\), (7) bounds its variation over the period by \(1/4\).
Thus the same good period has the uniform anchor
\[
                          \sup|A-h|\ge3/4.                    \tag{9}
\]
In particular (8) holds for every constant \(h=c\), uniformly in that constant and in the fast shape.

<a id="quadrature"></a>
## Clipped comparison without an amplitude loss

Let \(F(t)=A(nt)-b(t)\), and clip each logarithm below at \(\log u\), \(0<u<1\), writing \(\mu_u,I_u\).
Set \(B=2m+2\).
For a real \(a\in[-2m,2m]\), the function
\[
                    x\longmapsto
          \log\frac{\max(u,|a-x|)}{B+|x|}
\]
has range in \([\log(u/(3B)),0]\) and an absolute bounded number of monotone pieces.
Split at zero and at \(a-u,a+u\); each remaining ratio is a quotient of affine functions or a constant numerator divided by an affine function.
A nonconstant real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\) has at most \(2d\) critical points.
Composing with \(b(t)\) therefore gives variation at most \(C_m d\log(C_m/u)\).

Condition on \(v=nt\bmod2\pi\).
The slow variable is then averaged on a shifted mesh of \(n\) equally spaced points, whose error for a function of bounded variation is at most its variation divided by \(n\).
The subtracted term \(\log(B+|b|)\) has identical means in the diagonal and independent integrals.
Hence
\[
                   |\mu_u-I_u|\le C_m D^{-1}\log(C_m/u).        \tag{10}
\]
Integrating (8) for constant \(h=b(t)\) against \(du/u\) gives
\[
                            0\le I_u-I\le C_m u^{1/R}.         \tag{11}
\]

<a id="global"></a>
## A global estimate for the large-energy branch

For nonconstant \(b\), divide the circle into periods of length \(2\pi/n\).
Use fixed amplitude thresholds \(\pm B\) and derivative thresholds
\(\pm\delta_m n^k\), \(1\le k\le R\).
Flag any period meeting an amplitude threshold or a point where the amplitude is within its thresholds and the first derivative quantity to exceed its threshold has order \(k\in\{1,\ldots,R\}\).

The \(2R+2\) threshold equations have \(O_m(d)\) zeros in total.
None is identically zero: \(b\) is nonconstant, and a periodic derivative cannot be a nonzero constant.
On a component where a derivative first exceeds its allowed thresholds, that derivative has constant sign.
On a component where the first excessive derivative is \(b^{(k)}\), the preceding derivative varies in a range of length \(2\delta_m n^{k-1}\) if \(k>1\), or at most \(2B\) if \(k=1\).
Its derivative has magnitude at least \(\delta_m n^k\), so this component has length \(O_m(1/n)\).
Threshold points add only \(O_m(d)\) more period intersections.
There are therefore \(O_m(d)\) flagged periods.

Each unflagged period either has \(|b|>B\) throughout, so \(|F|>1\), or satisfies (7) after rescaling to a fast period.
It has the sublevel bound (8) and an anchor of size at least \(3/4\).
For \(D\) larger than a constant depending on \(m\), some period is unflagged.
Adjoin a neighboring unflagged period to every maximal flagged run.
The enlarged intervals have total length \(O_m(d/n)\), and each has supremum at least \(3/4\).

Let \(K\) be the number of distinct nonzero Fourier terms of \(F\); then \(2\le K\le2d+2m+1\).
On each enlarged interval apply the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001).
Its exponential factor is one because all frequencies are imaginary.
The anchor gives a relative sublevel bound \(C_m u^{1/(K-1)}\), whose logarithmic integral up to \(u\le1\) costs \(O_m(K)\) times the interval length.
The good periods instead cost \(O_m(u^{1/R})\).
Together with (10)–(11), this proves
\[
          |\mu-I|\le C_m\{u^{1/R}
                       +D^{-1}\log(C_m/u)+K/D\}.              \tag{12}
\]
Taking \(u=D^{-R}\) gives \(C_mD^{-1}\{K+\log(2D)\}\).
In particular, whenever \(E\ge\gamma d\), for any fixed \(\gamma>0\), the bound is
\[
                       |\mu-I|\le
                      C_{m,\gamma}D^{-1}\{E+\log(2D)\}.       \tag{13}
\]
This proof establishes the needed large-energy branch for arbitrary normalized \(A\); it does not invoke the cosine-only term-count estimate as a general theorem.

<a id="local"></a>
## The critical-scale argument with finitely many derivative levels

It remains to treat \(E<\gamma d\), with the sufficiently small absolute \(\gamma\) used in [C325's packing construction](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#packing).
Apply that construction to \(f=1+\log^+|b|\).
It gives covering arcs with centers \(x_i\), radii \(r_i\), and \(M_i=dr_i\ge1\), such that
\[
 (P_{r_i}*f)(x_i)=dr_i,
 \qquad \sum_i r_i\ll1,
 \qquad \sum_i M_i^2\ll dE.                                  \tag{14}
\]
The [strip majorant](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#analytic) uses only the slow polynomial and gives
\[
               \sup_{|z-x_i|\le16r_i}\log^+|b(z)|\ll M_i.      \tag{15}
\]

Fix a center and abbreviate \(r=r_i,M=M_i\).
For \(0\le k\le R\), Cauchy's formula bounds
\(g_k=b^{(k)}/n^k\) on the disk of radius \(8r\) by
\[
              \log^+|g_k|\le C_m M,
\]
because \(nr=DM\ge D\), \(k\le2m\), and \(M\ge1\).
For \(g_0\), choose a positive threshold in \([B,B+1]\) and a negative threshold with magnitude in that interval.
For each \(g_k\), \(1\le k\le R\), choose positive and negative thresholds with magnitudes in \([\delta_m/2,\delta_m]\).
Choose each from the endpoints of its interval so its distance from \(g_k(x_i)\) is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(m\).
The Jensen argument of [C325's local level count](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#levels) then gives \(O_m(M)\) crossings of all these finitely many thresholds on \([x_i-4r,x_i+4r]\), counting multiplicities.

Use the same first-excessive-derivative rule as above with these local thresholds.
The preceding derivative is bounded and the next one has fixed sign and magnitude at least a fixed positive multiple of \(n^k\), so each component has length \(O_m(1/n)\).
There are at most \(C_mM\) locally flagged periods.
Every unflagged full period has (8)–(9), or \(|F|>1\) from the amplitude threshold.
Thus only the number of derivative levels and the constants have changed from the cosine construction.

Increase \(D_m\) until \((C_mM+6)2\pi/n\le r/100\).
As in [C325's run construction](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#clusters), every flagged run meeting the covering arc has a neighboring good period inside the larger counting interval.
Adjoining one neighbor gives intervals of total length \(O_m(M/n)\), each of length \(l\le r/100\) and with a real anchor \(\xi\) satisfying \(|F(\xi)|\ge3/4\).
On its complex disk of radius \(8l\), (15) bounds the slow logarithmic modulus by \(C_mM\), and
\[
               |A(nz)|\le 2m\exp(mn|\operatorname{Im}z|)
\]
bounds the fast one by the same quantity, since \(nl=O_m(M)\).

The disk Jensen–Blaschke argument in that same [local logarithm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#clusters) therefore gives
\[
                      \int_{\text{run}}\log^-|F(t)|\,dt
                                             \le C_mMl.       \tag{16}
\]
Its hypotheses are an analytic logarithmic upper bound, a real anchor bounded away from zero, and a fixed ratio of disk to interval radii.
Replacing anchor one by \(3/4\) costs only an additive constant in the Jensen and Harnack estimates, absorbed because \(M\ge1\).
Each removed zero still has logarithmic integral \(O(l)\), so no factor \(\log M\) is introduced.

The exceptional loss at this center is \(O_m(M^2/n)\).
The good periods cost \(O_m((r+1/n)u^{1/R})\).
Sum these nonnegative losses over the cover, using (14) and \(1/n\le r\), to obtain
\[
                       0\le\mu_u-\mu
                         \le C_m u^{1/R}+C_m dE/n.             \tag{17}
\]
Taking \(u=D^{-R}\) in (10), (11), (17), and combining with the large-energy case (13), proves
\[
                       |\mu-I|\le
                           C_mD^{-1}\{E+\log(2D)\}.
\]
Equation (5) and restoration of the original scale give (1).
If \(\mu-\log\lambda\) is bounded above, (1) absorbs its own coefficient of \(J\) for sufficiently large \(D\), proving the stated uniform absolute convergence.

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Monic integral fibers and the zero case

Now work with the original integral Laurent polynomials in (2), before any real normalization, and set
\[
                         \mathcal F(X,Y)=\mathcal A(Y)-\mathcal B(X).
\]
After multiplying by \(Y^s\), this is monic of degree \(2s\) in \(Y\), with constant coefficient one.
The [bounded partial-degree gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives
\[
                I=m_2(\mathcal F)=0\quad\text{or}\quad
                 I\ge c_{2m},\qquad
        c_{2m}=\frac1{2\exp(16384)(\log(6m))^{48}}.              \tag{18}
\]
Indeed the actual fast width is \(2s\le2m\), and the source bound decreases with that width.

If \(I=0\), [C156's horizontal-component argument](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) excludes every nontorsion component depending on \(Y\).
Its factorization consequently expresses \(\mathcal F\), up to a Laurent unit, as a product of factors \(\Phi_h(X^aY^b)\) with \(b\ne0\).
There is no nonunit factor in \(X\) alone, since it would divide the leading \(Y\)-coefficient one in the Laurent coefficient ring.
Under \(X=z,Y=z^n\), each character becomes \(z^{a+bn}\).
For nonzero exponent its cyclotomic factor is, up to a Laurent unit and sign, a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials.
For zero exponent it becomes the integer \(\Phi_h(1)\).
No such factor vanishes: \(d<n\) leaves the extreme coefficients of \(P\) equal to one, so the specialization is nonzero.
The monicity of \(P\) then forces the product of all these integer constant factors to have absolute value one.
Each constant factor is a unit, and \(P\) has measure zero.
This argument allows constant specialized factors; it does not require every character to retain nonzero exponent.

Thus \(M(P)>1\) implies the positive alternative of (18).
In this integral class \(\lambda\ge1\), so the original quantity in (1) satisfies \(J\le I+\beta_m\).
Choose a fixed sufficiently large \(D_m'\) such that, for \(D\ge D_m'\),
\[
 \frac{C_m}{D}\le\frac14,
 \qquad
 \frac{C_m}{D}\{1+\beta_m+\log(2D)\}\le\frac{c_{2m}}4.
\]
Then (1) gives \(\mu\ge c_{2m}/2\).
Taking \(\epsilon_m=1/D_m'\) and \(\eta_m=c_{2m}/2\) proves (3).
When \(d=0\), substitution gives \(\mu=I\) and (18) applies directly.
For bounded integral diagonal measure, \(\mu-\log\lambda\le\mu\), so the general normalized convergence conclusion also applies to the original measure.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Limits of the extension

The fast Fourier degree \(m\) is fixed; the constants and the admitted positive fraction of slow width may deteriorate with \(m\).
The theorem treats a fixed fast shape \(A(v)\) within each input and one real slow scalar \(b(t)\), with both allowed to vary between inputs.
It does not treat arbitrary slowly varying coefficients in \(\sum_j A_j(t)e^{ijnt}\): those coefficients need not retain a uniform nonzero fast shape at each base point, so the derivative and anchor arguments above do not supply that extension.
The gap is for the whole specialization, and supplies no lower bound for an arbitrary selected factor of a larger-measure product.
