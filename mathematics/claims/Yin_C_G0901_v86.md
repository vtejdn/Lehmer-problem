# How many terms above the vertices are necessary in a low-measure exponent lift?

<a id="statement"></a>
## An obstruction with increasing dimension and increasing term count

For an integer Laurent polynomial \(P\) in one variable with \(k\ge2\) nonzero terms, let \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) denote its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
For \(0<\delta<1\), use the [lift by all short relations](Yin_C_G0901_v78.md#lift) at \(N=N(k,\delta)\) as specified in the [dimension bound](Yin_C_G0901_v78.md#statement).
Its dimension \(r\) is \(k-1\) minus the rational rank of the balanced exponent relations of positive and negative mass at most \(N\).
Write \(q=k-2^r\) and let \(\theta>1\) solve \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
For every sequence with
\[
        0<m(P),\qquad m(P)+\delta<\log\theta,\qquad r\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
one has \(q\ge1\) and
\[
       \liminf\frac{\log(q+1)}{\sqrt{r\log r}}\ \ge\ \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{12}}.       \tag{1}
\]
Both \(k\) and \(\delta\) may vary, with the indicated relation length on each input.
Thus the term count must exceed \(2^r\) by at least \(\exp((\pi/\sqrt{12}-o(1))\sqrt{r\log r})-1\).
The bound concerns the support of the whole input, including any cyclotomic factors.

For an individual input with \(m(P)+\delta<\log\theta\), \(1\le q<2^{r-1}\) and \(r\ge2\), one has the following finite bound.
Put
\[
 h=\log(q+1),\qquad d=\max\{0,r-2-2\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\}.
\]
Let \(S_d=\sum_{i=1}^d \log|1+Z_i|\) for independent Haar circle variables, with \(S_0=0\).
Then
\[
                       m(P)+\delta\ \ge\ \mathbb E(-S_d-h)_+.             \tag{2}
\]
In particular bounded \(q\) and bounded positive measure in the indicated range cannot occur as \(r\) grows.
The hypotheses do not cover every possible support of a sequence of increasing degrees.

<a id="box"></a>
## The vertex coefficients in common box coordinates

Write \(F\) for the lifted polynomial, so \(m(F)\le m(P)+\delta\) by the [finite constant-coefficient comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#transfer).
The strict bound below \(\log\theta\) makes every face polynomial signed reciprocal up to a monomial.
Its Newton polytope is a zonotope and every vertex coefficient is a unit sign by the [face and vertex argument](Yin_C_G0901_v78.md#faces).
The positive original measure rules out \(q=0\) by the [equality case](Yin_C_G0901_v78.md#corners).
If \(q<2^{r-1}\), there are fewer than \(3\cdot2^{r-1}\) terms, so the zonotope is a parallelotope by the [vertex count](Yin_C_G0901_v78.md#zonotope).

Translate a corner to zero and use its independent edge vectors as a real basis.
The remaining support points then have rational coordinates in \([0,1]^r\), since the edge vectors and original exponents are lattice vectors.
Multiplying these new coordinates by one common positive denominator gives an integer exponent map of full rank on the support-difference lattice.
The dual map on the torus is surjective and preserves Haar probability, so the transformed polynomial has the same measure.
Its corners are the vectors with coordinates \(0\) or \(D\) for a common \(D\ge1\).
The \(q\) remaining distinct support points have at least one coordinate strictly between \(0\) and \(D\).

On each two-dimensional face the opposite corner products agree, since reflection interchanges opposite corners and uses one sign on the whole face.
Extra support on that face preserves this implication.
Consequently the ratios of the corner coefficients along each axis are independent of the other corner coordinates, by changing one of those coordinates at a time.
The complete sum of the vertex terms is
\[
                    c_0\prod_{i=1}^r(1+\epsilon_i X_i^D),\qquad
                    c_0,\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
All \(q\) other coefficients may be arbitrary nonzero integers.

<a id="slices"></a>
## Two estimates on the same conditional Mahler measure

We will integrate over a set \(J\) of at most \(2+2\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\) coordinates.
For a fixed choice \(Y\) of all other torus coordinates write \(m_J(F;Y)\) for the logarithmic integral over the coordinates in \(J\).
All statements about a fixed \(Y\) are for almost every \(Y\).
Nonzero Laurent coefficients vanish on null sets, and the logarithms of the nonzero Laurent polynomials are integrable on their tori.

First choose some coordinate \(i\) having an interior exponent, and select the smallest exponent strictly between \(0\) and \(D\) there.
In that variable there are at most \(q+2\) distinct exponents, and the selected coefficient is the second in order, after the exponent zero.
For a one-variable complex Laurent polynomial with \(\ell\) nonzero coefficients in exponent order, one has
\[
                 |a_j|\le\binom{\ell-1}{j}M(A),
                 \qquad 0\le j\le\ell-1.                                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, translate the exponents to \(0=n_0<\cdots<n_{\ell-1}=b\).
The two extremes satisfy (3) by Jensen.
For an interior index, differentiate the polynomial and its reversal, each of which loses one term.
Induction on \(\ell\) and [Mahler's derivative inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0461_v1.md#normalized-derivative-descent) bound \(n_j|a_j|\) by \(b\binom{\ell-2}{j-1}M(A)\) and \((b-n_j)|a_j|\) by \(b\binom{\ell-2}{j}M(A)\).
Addition proves (3).
Thus integration over \(i\) bounds the conditional integral below by the logarithm of the modulus of the selected coefficient polynomial minus \(\log(q+1)\).

This coefficient consists entirely of nonvertex terms, of which there are at most \(q\).
If more than one remains, choose a further coordinate on which their exponents differ.
Of the two extreme levels in that coordinate select the one containing fewer terms, with arbitrary choice in a tie.
Its size is at most half the current size.
Jensen's leading- or trailing-coefficient inequality in this coordinate bounds the current integral below by the log modulus of the selected extreme coefficient.
Repeat until a single term is left, using at most \(\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\) further coordinates.
Call the set of these axes together with \(i\) the set \(J_1\).
The remaining single coefficient times a monomial has modulus at least one on the remaining torus.
Integrating the successive conditional inequalities therefore gives
\[
                       m_{J_1}(F;Y)\ge-\log(q+1)
\]
with \(Y\) here running over the coordinates outside \(J_1\).

Separately, choose endpoints on a set \(J_2\) of axes in such a way that none of the \(q\) extra support points agrees with all chosen endpoints.
If \(a>0\) points still agree, at least one of them has an interior coordinate on an unused axis.
At that axis choose the endpoint with fewer of the remaining points.
The two endpoint counts sum to at most \(a-1\), so at most \(\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor\) survive.
After at most \(1+\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor\) choices none survives.
Now take \(J=J_1\cup J_2\), extending the chosen endpoints on \(J_2\) to arbitrary endpoints on all of \(J\).
The coefficient at this corner of the \(J\)-coordinates comes only from the vertex terms.
Its modulus at the remaining variables is
\[
                         \prod_{i\notin J}|1+\epsilon_i Y_i^D|.
\]
Successive Jensen inequalities in the \(J\)-coordinates bound \(m_J(F;Y)\) below by its logarithm.
Integrating the previous \(J_1\) inequality over \(J\setminus J_1\) also bounds this same \(m_J(F;Y)\) below by \(-h\).
Thus the two lower bounds hold with the same conditioned variables \(Y\).

The logarithm of the displayed product has the law of \(S_{r-|J|}\), since the coordinates are independent and rotation by \(\epsilon_i\) and the power map preserve Haar probability.
It follows that \(m(F)\ge\mathbb E\max(S_{r-|J|},-h)\).
Each summand has mean zero by Jensen, so this expectation equals \(\mathbb E(-S_{r-|J|}-h)_+\).
The expectation is nondecreasing in the number of independent centered summands by conditional Jensen for the convex positive-part function.
Since \(r-|J|\ge d\), the measure transfer proves (2).

<a id="tail"></a>
## A lower tail at thresholds of order \(\sqrt{d\log d}\)

Put \(U=-\log|1+Z|\) for one Haar circle variable.
Its mean is zero, and \(\sigma^2=\mathbb E U^2=\pi^2/12\).
Indeed \(\log|1+\rho e^{i\theta}|\) has cosine coefficients \((-1)^{j-1}\rho^j/j\) for \(0<\rho<1\) and zero constant coefficient.
Their square summability gives an \(L^2\) limit as \(\rho\uparrow1\), identified by pointwise convergence away from \(-1\).
Parseval gives \(\sigma^2=\tfrac12\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}\).
Also \(\mathbb E e^{u|U|}<\infty\) for \(0<u<1\), since \(|1+e^{i\theta}|\) vanishes to order one at \(\theta=\pi\) and is bounded elsewhere.
In a fixed neighborhood of zero this permits three differentiations of \(\psi(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{tU}\), with bounded third derivative.
Hence
\[
 \psi(t)=\tfrac12\sigma^2t^2+O(|t|^3),\qquad
 \psi'(t)=\sigma^2t+O(t^2),\qquad \psi''(t)\longrightarrow\sigma^2 .
\]

Let \(T_d=-S_d\), and fix any \(c<\sigma\) with \(c>0\).
We show that \(\mathbb E(T_d-h)_+\to\infty\) uniformly for \(0\le h\le c\sqrt{d\log d}\).
Choose a fixed \(B>2+4\sigma\), and put \(t=(h+B\sqrt d)/(\sigma^2 d)\).
Give the \(d\) independent copies of \(U\) the probability density \(e^{tT_d-d\psi(t)}\) relative to their original joint law.
Under this new law the mean of \(T_d\) is \(d\psi'(t)=h+B\sqrt d+O(\log d)\) and its variance is \(d\psi''(t)\le2\sigma^2d\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).
By Chebyshev's inequality the interval \([h+\sqrt d,h+2B\sqrt d]\) has new probability at least \(1/2\), uniformly over the stated \(h\).
On this interval the original density relative to the new one is bounded below by \(\exp(d\psi(t)-t(h+2B\sqrt d))\).
The Taylor estimate, with \(d|t|^3=o(1)\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E(T_d-h)_+
    \ \ge\ \tfrac12\sqrt d\,
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{h^2}{2\sigma^2d}-O(1+h/\sqrt d)\right).
\]
The right side tends to infinity since \(c^2/\sigma^2<1\) and the error in the exponent is \(O(\sqrt{\log d})\).

Suppose (1) failed on a subsequence.
Then for some \(c_0<\sigma\) it would satisfy \(h=\log(q+1)\le c_0\sqrt{r\log r}\), with \(r\to\infty\).
In particular \(q<2^{r-1}\) eventually, \(r-d=O(1+\log(q+1))=o(r)\), and \(\sqrt{r\log r}/\sqrt{d\log d}\to1\).
For some \(c_0<c<\sigma\) the preceding estimate applies.
It would force the right side of (2) to diverge while \(m(P)+\delta<\log\theta\), a contradiction.
This proves (1) without asserting an optimal asymptotic constant or a gap on supports satisfying this necessary bound.
