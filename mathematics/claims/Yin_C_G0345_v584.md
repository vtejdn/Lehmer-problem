# Terminal relations after splitting through algebraic coefficients of bounded degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Many exponent lists with the same prescribed coefficients

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and
\[
                 d_*=\min_{q\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^q):\mathbb Q].
\]
Fix an integer \(1\le B\le d_*/4\).
Allow the intermediate terms in a split to be \(c\alpha^h\), where \(h\in\mathbb Z\), \(0\ne c\in K\), and \([\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]\le B\), with no height bound.
Call a zero sum of \(r\) such terms terminal if it has no nonempty proper zero subsum and no sublist of size from two through \(r-2\) sums to an allowed intermediate term.
Exponents may repeat.

For \(r\ge3\) and fixed nonzero \(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1}\in K\) of degrees at most \(B\), count the integer lists \(t_1\le\cdots\le t_{r-1}\) for which
\[
             \sum_{i=1}^{r-1} a_i\alpha^{t_i}=c
\]
with \(c\ne0\) of degree at most \(B\) and with the relation obtained by appending \(-c\) terminal.
Thus translation is removed by placing a distinguished term at zero, and its coefficient is determined by the other terms.
Write \(W_r(\alpha,B)\) for the maximum of these counts over the prescribed coefficient lists.
There are constants \(H_r\) depending only on \(r\) such that \(W_r(\alpha,B)\le2BH_r\).

On any sequence of units with \(m\to0\), choose \(B\) subject to \(B\le d_*/4\) and \(Bm\to0\).
For some \(r\to\infty\), with \(r\le\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil+1\), one has
\[
                 W_r(\alpha,B)\ \ge\ (c_0/m)^{r-2}
\]
for an absolute \(c_0>0\) and all sufficiently late inputs.
In particular one may allow \(B\to\infty\).
Even for these enlarged splitting rules, a uniform exponential upper bound on the terminal counts for some such choice of \(B\) at strict degree records would prove the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
This count keeps integer dilations and holds the entire ordered list of outgoing coefficients fixed for each application.

<a id="embeddings"></a>
## Fixed coefficients and many embeddings of a low-degree value

Fix a complex realization of \(K\).
At most \(2d/d_*\) embeddings \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) have \(\alpha,\sigma(\alpha)\) multiplicatively dependent.
Indeed a relation between nonzero powers and invariance of the positive absolute Weil height under conjugation force equal absolute exponents.
Thus \(\sigma(\alpha)\) differs from \(\alpha\) or \(\alpha^{-1}\) by a root of unity.
There is one common positive power \(Q\) killing all such roots of unity.
Each of the at most two target values of \(\sigma(\alpha^Q)\) occurs for at most \([K:\mathbb Q(\alpha^Q)]\le d/d_*\) embeddings.

For clarity take any fixed \(s\ge1\) and \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\in K^\times\).
Suppose \(\sum_i a_i\alpha^{t_i}\) has no nonempty zero subsum and is fixed by one specified embedding \(\sigma\) for which \(\beta=\sigma(\alpha)\) is multiplicatively independent of \(\alpha\).
Then the possible ordered integer tuples \((t_i)\), even without sorting, number at most a constant depending only on \(s\).
To see this, partition the labeled equality
\[
          \sum_i a_i\alpha^{t_i}-\sum_i\sigma(a_i)\beta^{t_i}=0
\]
into minimal zero subsums.
Each block meets both sides, since a zero sublist on either side would give a zero sublist in the original sum.
In each block divide by a chosen term on the \(\beta\)-side, moving it to the right to obtain a nondegenerate equation with right side one.
The remaining multiplicative variables lie in the Cartesian power of \(\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\) with one coordinate per remaining term, with tuple rank at most twice the number of these terms.
For this partition and pivot the [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt bound](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) gives finitely many ratio tuples with a bound depending only on the block size, uniformly in the field, embedding and coefficients.
A ratio \(\alpha^{t_i}\beta^{-t_j}\) to the pivot determines both exponents by multiplicative independence.
Every index \(i\) occurs on the \(\alpha\)-side of a mixed block, so the collection of ratio tuples determines the entire exponent list.
Multiplying the bounds and summing over the finitely many labeled partitions proves the assertion, with a uniform bound denoted \(H_{s+1}\).

When the value \(c\) has degree at most \(B\), at least \(d/B\) embeddings fix it.
At least \(d/B-2d/d_*\ge d/(2B)\) of them form independent pairs as above.
Counting pairs of a tuple and a fixing embedding gives at most \(2B H_{s+1}\) possible tuples for the fixed coefficients.
This proves the asserted bound for \(W_r\), and shows that its maximum over coefficient lists exists because the bounded counts are integers.

<a id="trees"></a>
## Recovering the intermediate coefficients from the leaves

Use the [finite witness for small measures](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale), which supplies \(j\le\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil+1\) with at least \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\) translation classes of minimal ternary supports.
Orient a vanishing sign choice on each such support.
Whenever an allowed partial sum equals \(c\alpha^h\), adjoin \(-c\alpha^h\) to that sublist and \(c\alpha^h\) to its complement.
Each child is nondegenerate, since a zero proper sublist on either side of its new term would give one avoiding that term by complementation.
Both are shorter than their parent.
Iteration gives a tree of terminal relations with the original \(j\) terms as leaves, as in the [splitting tree construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#trees).
The internal degrees \(r_v\ge3\) satisfy \(\sum_v(r_v-2)=j-2\).

Root at the leaf of smallest exponent and order the outgoing edges at each internal vertex by their exponents, resolving ties arbitrarily.
For a fixed plane tree and a sign at each leaf, count the possibilities from its furthest vertices toward the root.
When the subtrees below a vertex have been filled, the coefficients contributed by its outgoing edges are known, including the change of sign at each previously cancelled edge.
Translation changes only the exponents when all terms, including the input term, are scaled by the same power of \(\alpha\).
With the input exponent placed at zero, each child exponent list is therefore counted by \(W_{r_v}\) for the known outgoing coefficients.
Its sum determines the coefficient on the input edge exactly.
Its relative exponents position the already determined subtrees relative to that edge, and at the root leaf the choice of exponent zero fixes the whole translate.
Multiple descriptions of one support are harmless.

There are at most \(4^{2j}\) plane rooted trees with at most \(2j-2\) vertices and at most \(2^j\) sign choices.
Hence, for \(V_j=\max_{3\le r\le j}W_r^{1/(r-2)}\),
\[
                   A_j\le 32^j V_j^{j-2}.
\]
Combining both counts gives \(V_j\ge c_0/m\) for an absolute \(c_0>0\) when \(m\) is sufficiently small, for example \(c_0=\log(2)/(32\cdot32^3)\).
Take a maximizing \(r\).
For every fixed \(r\ge3\) the bound \(W_r\le2BH_r=o(1/m)\) precludes this lower bound eventually, so \(r\to\infty\).
The [norm to a power field](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent) is a nontorsion unit of degree at most \(d_*\) and log measure at most \(m\) when the minimizing power is taken.
Finiteness at bounded degree and bounded measure gives \(d_*\to\infty\).
For example \(B=\lfloor\min(d_*/4,m^{-1/2})\rfloor\) tends to infinity and meets both conditions.
On strict degree records \(d_*=d\) by the same norm descent.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Which powers can participate in a split

For a specified nonzero partial sum \(z\in K\), at most \(2B\) integers \(h\) satisfy \([\mathbb Q(z\alpha^{-h}):\mathbb Q]\le B\).
For each such \(h\) at least \(d/(2B)\) independent embeddings fix \(z\alpha^{-h}\).
Each fixed independent embedding can serve at most one \(h\), since it requires
\[
                     z/\sigma(z)=(\alpha/\sigma(\alpha))^h
\]
and the base is nontorsion.
This also gives an effective finite test.
For each independent pair its ratio is a nontorsion algebraic unit, so some absolute conjugate modulus of the ratio differs from one.
At that embedding absolute values give one possible integer exponent from logarithms, testable by interval approximation to width less than one followed by exact algebraic comparison.
It suffices in an algorithm to test all embeddings whose ratio is nontorsion, detected by the unit's minimum polynomial, and then check the degree of each candidate directly.

The restriction on scalar degree has mathematical content.
For a reciprocal unit of degree \(d>2\), all values \(\alpha^t+\alpha^{-t}\) have degree at most \(d/2\), since inversion fixes them.
At \(B=d/2\), the prescribed outgoing coefficients \(1,1\) therefore admit infinitely many sorted exponent pairs \((-t,t)\), \(t\ge1\).
Their sums are nonzero, their three-term relations have no proper vanishing subsum and admit no split of the allowed sizes.
This example concerns the count retaining dilations defined above.
