# Different total measures at indistinguishable house asymptotics and identical prefixes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two irreducible families at the same degrees

Fix integers \(1\le h<s\).
For \(n=3^j\to\infty\) there are two explicit monic irreducible reciprocal polynomials \(P_{1,n},P_{2,n}\) of degree \(2n\), each with a unique positive root of largest modulus and exactly \(n\) exterior roots.
In each polynomial every quotient of distinct roots is nontorsion.
The two polynomials agree in exactly the first \(n-h-1\) coefficients below the leading one, including a nonzero coefficient at \(z^{n+s}\).
For each the maximum length of agreement with a cyclotomic product of degree \(2n\) is exactly \(n-s-1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Write \(b_{i,n}\) for the logarithmic house and \(L_{i,n}\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure.
There is a common \(c>0\) for which
\[
 nb_{i,n}\longrightarrow c,\qquad
 n^{2h+1}(b_{2,n}-b_{1,n})\longrightarrow
                     (-1)^h c^{2h}/\sinh c.                              \tag{1}
\]
Nevertheless \(L_{i,n}\) have distinct finite positive limits.
Thus even the degree, exterior count, complete list of optimal cyclotomic companions, and house expansion up to any prescribed finite order can agree while the limiting total costs differ.
The [earlier Perron family](Yin_C_G0345_v142.md#family) shows failure of a long prefix alone to imply small total cost.
The comparison here is between two actual irreducible inputs with the same first-order house constant and the same total number of exterior conjugates.

<a id="construction"></a>
## The central Laurent polynomials

Set \(A=2\cdot4^h+4,\ C=2sA+3\), and define
\[
\begin{split}
 B_1(z)&=C+A\sum_{l=1}^s(z^l+z^{-l}),\\
 B_2(z)&=B_1(z)+2(2-z-z^{-1})^h,\\
 P_{i,n}(z)&=z^{2n}+1-z^n B_i(z).
\end{split}                                                             \tag{2}
\]
Take \(n>3s\).
The sum of absolute coefficients of the added term is at most \(2\cdot4^h\).
Both \(B_i\) thus have a positive odd central coefficient and positive even coefficients at every nonzero exponent of absolute value at most \(s\).
On the circle \(B_2(e^{i\phi})\ge B_1(e^{i\phi})\ge3\), with strict first inequality away from \(1\).
In particular \(P_{i,n}\) has no unit-circle roots, since the root equation is \(z^n+z^{-n}=B_i(z)\).
The degree and reciprocal symmetry give exactly \(n\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity.
Reduction modulo two gives \(z^{2n}+z^n+1=\Phi_{3^{j+1}}(z)\), irreducible there by the [multiplicative order of two](Yin_C_G0345_v142.md#family).
This gives irreducibility over the rationals and separability.

The coefficient at \(z^{n+s}\) in both polynomials is \(-A\).
The [sharp zero-prefix classification](Yin_C_G0345_v146.md#statement) therefore gives the asserted optimal length.
All optimal comparators are exactly its list (1) with parameter \(s\), since every polynomial on that list attains this common zero block.
The difference \(P_{2,n}-P_{1,n}\) has degree exactly \(n+h\), proving the longer mutual agreement.

<a id="house"></a>
## A unique largest conjugate and high-order agreement

For either \(B(z)=a_0+\sum_{l=1}^s a_l(z^l+z^{-l})\), all \(a_l>0\).
The ratio \(B(e^u)/(2\cosh(nu))\) is strictly decreasing for \(u>0\), exceeds one at zero, and tends to zero at infinity.
Indeed each \(\cosh(lu)/\cosh(nu)\) is strictly decreasing for \(0\le l<n\) by logarithmic differentiation.
There is a unique \(b>0\) with \(2\cosh(nb)=B(e^b)\), giving the real root \(e^b\).

At any exterior root \(e^{u+i\phi}\), divide the real and imaginary coordinates of \(z^n+z^{-n}=B(z)\) by \(2\cosh(nu)\) and \(2\sinh(nu)\), respectively.
The left vector has Euclidean norm one, and the right is the sum of a positive horizontal vector of length \(a_0/(2\cosh(nu))\) and the vectors
\[
 a_l\left(\frac{\cosh(lu)}{\cosh(nu)}\cos(l\phi),
          \frac{\sinh(lu)}{\sinh(nu)}\sin(l\phi)\right).
\]
Since \(\tanh(lu)<\tanh(nu)\), their respective lengths are at most \(a_l\cosh(lu)/\cosh(nu)\).
This gives \(1\le B(e^u)/(2\cosh(nu))\), hence \(u\le b\).
Equality forces the \(l=1\) vector to have maximal length and point in the positive horizontal direction, so \(e^{i\phi}=1\).
The unique largest root is consequently a Perron unit.
An automorphism sending one element of a torsion quotient to the largest root would send the other to a distinct root of the same modulus, which is impossible.

As \(n\to\infty\) the decreasing ratio tends to zero at each fixed \(u>0\), so \(b_{i,n}\to0\) and \(nb_{i,n}\to c=\operatorname{arcosh}(B_1(1)/2)\).
Here \(B_2(1)=B_1(1)\).
For \(G_n(u)=2\cosh(nu)-B_1(e^u)\) one has \(G_n(b_{1,n})=0\) and
\[
 G_n(b_{2,n})=2(2-2\cosh b_{2,n})^h
              \sim 2(-1)^h(c/n)^{2h}.
\]
For every \(u\) between the two logarithmic houses, \(G_n'(u)/n\to2\sinh c>0\) uniformly.
The mean value theorem proves (1).
The expansions themselves exist since \(2\cosh x-B_i(e^{t x})=0\) at \((t,x)=(0,c)\) has nonzero \(x\)-derivative \(2\sinh c\).
The analytic implicit function at \(t=1/n\) gives \(x=nb_{i,n}\) by uniqueness.

<a id="radii"></a>
## Full distribution of the exterior logarithmic moduli

Write \(\rho_i(\phi)=\operatorname{arcosh}(B_i(e^{i\phi})/2)\).
Uniformly over all exterior roots \(z=e^{u+i\phi}\) of \(P_{i,n}\),
\[
                   nu-\rho_i(\phi)\longrightarrow0.                       \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(0<u\le b_{i,n}=O(1/n)\), so \(B_i(z)-B_i(e^{i\phi})\to0\) uniformly.
Along every convergent subsequence of \((e^{i\phi},z^n)\), the second limit \(w\) has \(|w|\ge1\) and solves \(w+w^{-1}=B_i(e^{i\phi_*})\) at the limiting phase.
The right side is at least three, so the only admissible solution is \(e^{\rho_i(\phi_*)}\).
The pairs lie in a fixed compact set, proving (3).

The exterior phases with weight \(1/n\) become uniformly distributed on the circle.
To see this, the first \(n-s-1\) full power sums are zero by Newton's identities.
For each fixed \(l\ge1\), replacing \(z^l\) by \(e^{il\arg z}\) at all roots changes their normalized mean by \(O_{i,l}(1/n)\), since \(\left|\log|z|\right|\le b_{i,n}\).
The full phase measure equals the exterior phase measure since \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) pairs exterior and interior roots at the same phase.
Vanishing of the nonconstant limiting Fourier coefficients gives uniform distribution by approximation by trigonometric polynomials.
Consequently the phases and scaled exterior logarithms jointly converge in distribution to \((\phi,\rho_i(\phi))\) for uniform \(\phi\).
In particular
\[
 L_{i,n}=\frac1n\sum_{|z|>1} n\log|z|
      \longrightarrow \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\rho_i(\phi)\,d\phi.
\]
The two finite limits are positive, and the second strictly exceeds the first by the circle inequality in (2).
Since \(L_{i,n}/(n b_{i,n})\) is the mean exterior logarithm divided by the largest, the normalized exterior logarithm distributions themselves have different means.
