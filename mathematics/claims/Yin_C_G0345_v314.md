# Endpoint Taylor remainders obstruct a wider uniform trace comparison

The author-side strategy researcher for [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) constructed the integer family and derived the comparison obstruction and total-measure growth below.
The motivating question was whether the coefficient-independent [short-width specialization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#statement) could extend uniformly to every central trace block of degree \(o(n)\).

<a id="statement"></a>
## The family, comparison loss and growing measure

Let \(C_n\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be the monic trace polynomial defined by
\[
 C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}.
\]
For integers \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le d<n\), write
\[
 \begin{split}
 C_n(2+u)&=\sum_{j=0}^n a_{n,j}u^j,\qquad a_{n,0}=2,\\
 a_{n,j}&=\frac{2n}{n+j}\binom{n+j}{2j}\quad(1\le j\le n),\\
 B_{n,d}(T)&=\sum_{j=0}^d a_{n,j}(T-2)^j,\\
 P_{n,d}(z)&=z^n\{C_n(z+z^{-1})-B_{n,d}(z+z^{-1})\}.
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
All \(a_{n,j}\) and coefficients of \(B_{n,d}\) are integers.
The polynomial \(P_{n,d}\) is monic, integral and reciprocal of degree \(2n\), with constant coefficient one.
Its root \(1\) has multiplicity exactly \(2d+2\).
When \(n\ge16d\), it also has a root off the unit circle.

For a real number \(b\), put
\[
 g(b)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,& |b|\le2,\\
  \operatorname{arcosh}(|b|/2),& |b|>2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Use logarithmic Mahler measure throughout, and set
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mu_{n,d}&=m(P_{n,d}),\\
 I_{n,d}&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
                      g(B_{n,d}(2\cos\theta))\,d\theta\\
 &=m_2\bigl(X+X^{-1}-B_{n,d}(Y+Y^{-1})\bigr).
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For every pair \(n,d\) in (1),
\[
 I_{n,d}-\mu_{n,d}\ \ge\
 \frac{d+1}{4e\pi n}
       \left((d+1)\log16+\log\frac{15}{8}\right)-\log4.           \tag{3}
\]
In particular, if \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\), then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}(I_{n,d}-\mu_{n,d})
 \ge\frac{25\log16}{4e\pi}-\log4
 >\frac{43}{66}\log2>0.                                        \tag{4}
\]
For \(d=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor\), the difference in (3) tends to infinity, although \(d=o(n)\).

This failure does not arise on a sequence of bounded total measures.
For every sequence \(1\le d=d(n)=o(n)\),
\[
       \mu_{n,d}\sim\log a_{n,d}
                  \sim2d\log(n/d)\longrightarrow\infty.         \tag{5}
\]
An explicit lower bound, requiring only \(n\ge4d\), is
\[
 \begin{split}
 U&=\log a_{n,d}+d\log4+\log3,\qquad \ell=\frac{U}{n-d},\\
 \mu_{n,d}&\ge
 \frac13\left(1-\frac{2Ue^\ell}{n}\right)_+
                         (\log a_{n,d}-\log4),                 \tag{6}
 \end{split}
\]
where \(x_+=\max(x,0)\).
The last factor is positive in this range.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Exact integer Taylor coefficients

The trace recurrence \(C_{n+1}=TC_n-C_{n-1}\), with \(C_0=2\) and \(C_1=T\), gives \(C_n\in\mathbb Z[T]\).
Consequently its Taylor coefficients at the integer \(2\) are integers.
The differential equation
\[
          (4-T^2)C_n''-TC_n'+n^2C_n=0
\]
follows directly by differentiating \(C_n(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(n\theta)\), first on the open interval and hence as a polynomial identity.
Substituting \(T=2+u\) gives
\[
 \frac{a_{n,j}}{a_{n,j-1}}
       =\frac{n^2-(j-1)^2}{2j(2j-1)}
       \quad(1\le j\le n).                                    \tag{7}
\]
Together with \(a_{n,0}=2\), this proves the formula in (1), positivity of every coefficient and
\[
 a_{n,j}
   =\frac{2\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}(n^2-r^2)}{(2j)!}
   \le2\binom{n+j}{2j}
   \le2\left(\frac{en}{j}\right)^{2j}.                          \tag{8}
\]
For the last inequality, use \(n+j\le2n\) and
\(\binom{N}{k}\le(eN/k)^k\), which follows from \(k!\ge(k/e)^k\).

The remainder \(C_n(T)-B_{n,d}(T)\) has a zero of exact order \(d+1\) at \(T=2\), because \(a_{n,d+1}>0\).
The identity
\[
                       z+z^{-1}-2=\frac{(z-1)^2}{z}
\]
therefore gives the exact multiplicity \(2d+2\) at \(z=1\).
Since \(d<n\), the terms \(z^{2n}\) and \(1\) in \(P_{n,d}\) cannot cancel with the central block, proving its degree and end coefficients.

<a id="pinch"></a>
## A small arc contributes a quadratic loss

Abbreviate \(B=B_{n,d}\) and \(P=P_{n,d}\).
If
\[
                         |\theta|\le\frac{d+1}{4en},           \tag{9}
\]
then \(u=2\cos\theta-2\) satisfies \(|u|\le\theta^2\).
For every \(j\ge d+1\), (8) gives
\[
 a_{n,j}|u|^j
       \le2\left(\frac{en|\theta|}{j}\right)^{2j}
       \le2\cdot16^{-j}.
\]
Thus the exact Taylor remainder, without estimating the retained terms separately, satisfies
\[
 |P(e^{i\theta})|
   =|C_n(2\cos\theta)-B(2\cos\theta)|
   \le\frac{32}{15}\,16^{-(d+1)}.                               \tag{10}
\]

For every real \(b,\phi\),
\[
                  \log|2\cos\phi-b|-g(b)\le\log4.              \tag{11}
\]
When \(|b|\le2\), this is immediate from \(|2\cos\phi-b|\le4\).
When \(|b|>2\), use
\[
 e^{g(b)}=\frac{|b|+\sqrt{b^2-4}}2,\qquad
 \frac{|b|+2}{e^{g(b)}}\le4.
\]
Jensen's formula for \(X+X^{-1}-b\) gives the equality between the two expressions for \(I_{n,d}\) in (2).
All integrals are finite: the nonzero Laurent polynomials have only finitely many zeros on each specialized circle, and the logarithmic singularities are integrable.

On the arc (9), \(g(B)\ge0\), so (10) gives
\[
       g(B(2\cos\theta))-\log|P(e^{i\theta})|
                    \ge(d+1)\log16-\log(32/15).
\]
Its normalized length is \((d+1)/(4e\pi n)\).
On the complementary arc, (11) gives the lower bound \(-\log4\).
Integrating these two inequalities proves
\[
 \begin{split}
 I_{n,d}-\mu_{n,d}
 &\ge \frac{d+1}{4e\pi n}
           \bigl((d+1)\log16-\log(32/15)+\log4\bigr)-\log4,
 \end{split}
\]
which is (3).
In particular the correction linear in \(d+1\) is positive, with coefficient \(\log(15/8)\).
For (4), use \(e<3\) and \(\pi<22/7\) to get
\[
 \frac{25\log16}{4e\pi}-\log4
  >\frac{175}{66}\log2-2\log2
  =\frac{43}{66}\log2.
\]

<a id="noncyclotomic"></a>
## The large-gap range has a noncyclotomic factor

Assume \(n\ge16d\).
For \(1\le j\le d\), (7) gives
\[
                 \frac{a_{n,j}}{a_{n,j-1}}
                              \ge\frac{3n^2}{16d^2}.           \tag{12}
\]
For \(x\ge64d^2/n^2\), the successive magnitudes in
\(B(2-x)=\sum_{j=0}^d(-1)^j a_{n,j}x^j\) therefore increase by at least a factor of twelve.
The last term dominates the sum of the preceding terms, giving
\[
        |B(2-x)|\ge\frac{10}{11}a_{n,d}x^d
                         \ge\frac{20}{11}12^d>2.               \tag{13}
\]
For \(-2\le T\le2\), one has \(|C_n(T)|\le2\).
Consequently every zero of \(C_n-B\) which lies in \([-2,2]\) is larger than
\[
                         2-\frac{64d^2}{n^2}\ge\frac74.
\]
If all its \(n\) zeros lay in \([-2,2]\), their sum would be positive.
But their sum is zero: the coefficient of \(T^{n-1}\) in \(C_n\) is zero, and \(\deg B=d\le n-2\).
This contradiction gives a trace zero outside \([-2,2]\) or a nonreal trace zero.
Its two reciprocal lifts cannot both lie on the unit circle.
Thus \(P\) has an exterior root and \(\mu_{n,d}>0\).

<a id="explicit-growth"></a>
## A coefficient bound gives a root annulus and an explicit measure lower bound

Assume \(n\ge4d\).
Then (12) still holds, and its right side is at least three.
Put
\[
                         L=\sum_{j=0}^d a_{n,j}4^j.
\]
The successive summands increase by at least a factor of twelve, so
\[
                 L\le\frac{12}{11}a_{n,d}4^d,\qquad
                 \log(2+L)\le U.                              \tag{14}
\]
The Laurent coefficient length of \(B(z+z^{-1})\) is at most \(L\), since the length of \((z+z^{-1}-2)^j\) is \(4^j\).
Therefore Jensen's inequality gives
\[
                            \mu_{n,d}\le\log(2+L)\le U.         \tag{15}
\]

At a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\) with \(r=|\alpha|\ge1\),
\[
                     r^n\le |B(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})|+r^{-n}
                                      \le Lr^d+1.
\]
It follows that \(r^{n-d}\le L+1\).
Reciprocity gives, at every root,
\[
                             |\log r|\le\ell.                  \tag{16}
\]
Roots are counted with multiplicity in the following sums.
For a monic reciprocal polynomial with constant coefficient one,
\[
                         \sum_{P(\alpha)=0}|\log|\alpha||=2\mu_{n,d}. \tag{17}
\]

The coefficient of \(z^{2n-1}\) in \(P\) is zero, so \(\sum\alpha=0\).
Writing \(\alpha=r e^{i\theta}\), (16)–(17) imply
\[
 \left|\sum e^{i\theta}\right|
      \le\sum|r-1|
      \le e^\ell\sum|\log r|
      \le2e^\ell U.                                            \tag{18}
\]
Let \(K\) count roots with \(\cos\theta\le1/2\).
The other roots contribute at least \(1/2\) to the cosine sum, whereas each of the \(K\) roots contributes at least \(-1\).
Hence
\[
                           K\ge\frac23(n-2e^\ell U)_+.         \tag{19}
\]

At any such root,
\[
 |\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2|
       =r+r^{-1}-2\cos\theta\ge1.
\]
By (12), the magnitudes of the terms of \(B\) at this complex argument increase by at least a factor of three.
The triangle inequality in the direction of its last term gives
\[
                       |B(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})|\ge a_{n,d}/2.
\]
On the other hand the root equation gives
\[
           |B(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})|
                         =|\alpha^n+\alpha^{-n}|
                         \le2e^{n|\log r|}.
\]
It follows that
\[
                           n|\log r|\ge\log a_{n,d}-\log4.
\]
Here \(a_{n,d}\ge a_{n,1}=n^2\ge16\), so the right side is positive.
Sum this inequality over the \(K\) roots, then use (17) and (19), to obtain (6).

<a id="asymptotic-growth"></a>
## The total measure has an exact first-order asymptotic

Suppose \(1\le d=o(n)\).
For \(d\le n/2\), the product formula in (8) and \((2d)!\le(2d)^{2d}\) give
\[
 2\left(\frac{\sqrt3\,n}{4d}\right)^{2d}
       \le a_{n,d}
       \le2\left(\frac{en}{d}\right)^{2d}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \log a_{n,d}=2d\log(n/d)+O(d),\qquad
 U\sim\log a_{n,d},\qquad U/n\longrightarrow0,\quad\ell\longrightarrow0. \tag{20}
\]

For every integer \(k\) with \(1\le k<n-d\), all of the first \(k\) coefficients after the leading coefficient of \(P\) are zero.
Newton's identities therefore give \(\sum\alpha^k=0\).
For each fixed positive \(k\), (16)–(17) show
\[
 \left|\frac1{2n}\sum e^{ik\theta}\right|
   \le\frac{k e^{k\ell}}{2n}\sum|\log r|
   \le\frac{k e^{k\ell}U}{n}\longrightarrow0.                   \tag{21}
\]
The same holds for negative \(k\) by complex conjugation.
Approximating continuous periodic functions by trigonometric polynomials now proves convergence of the root-argument probability measures to uniform circle measure.
In particular, for every fixed \(0<\delta<\pi\), a proportion \(1-\delta/\pi+o(1)\) of the roots have \(\delta\le|\theta|\le\pi\).

For these roots, put \(c_\delta=2-2\cos\delta>0\).
Then
\[
                     |\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2|\ge c_\delta.
\]
By (12), the ratio of the magnitude of any term preceding the next one in \(B\) is at most
\[
                         q_{n,d,\delta}=\frac{16d^2}{3n^2c_\delta}
                                      \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over the selected roots,
\[
 |B(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})|
   \ge a_{n,d}|\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2|^d
                 \left(1-\frac{q_{n,d,\delta}}{1-q_{n,d,\delta}}\right)
   \ge\frac12 a_{n,d}c_\delta^d.
\]
The root equation then implies
\[
                     n|\log r|\ge\log a_{n,d}+d\log c_\delta-\log4.
\]
Since \(\log a_{n,d}/d\to\infty\), summing over those roots and using (17) gives
\[
              \liminf\frac{\mu_{n,d}}{\log a_{n,d}}
                                      \ge1-\frac{\delta}{\pi}.
\]
Let \(\delta\) decrease to zero.
The upper bound \(\mu_{n,d}\le U\sim\log a_{n,d}\) proves (5).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequence for the trace-delay approach

For a general integer block \(B\) of degree \(d\), apply [C161](Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#statement) to
\[
                    F(X,Y)=X+X^{-1}-B(Y+Y^{-1}),\quad (H,N)=(n,1).
\]
When \(n>2d\), its width is \(R=2d\) and its term count is at most \(2d+3\).
That result gives a coefficient-independent comparison error \(O(d^2\log(2n)/n)\), and hence convergence for \(d=o(\sqrt{n/\log n})\).
The present family prevents replacing that window by every \(d=o(n)\), or even asserting uniform convergence throughout \(d=O(\sqrt n)\).
Its quadratic loss has the same \(d^2/n\) scale, up to the logarithm and absolute constants.

The construction uses actual integer coefficients and actual noncyclotomic polynomials in its asymptotic range.
Its unavoidable repeated root at one and the divergence (5) are material limitations.
It does not refute a comparison theorem restricted to bounded total measure or irreducible polynomials, and it gives no lower bound for each individual noncyclotomic factor.
Dividing out the high power of \(z-1\) preserves total measure but does not preserve the displayed trace-delay support.
The [separable bounded-measure analysis](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#statement) therefore remains a distinct possible source of stronger information.
No claim about new measures approaching one follows from this obstruction.
