# A unique large factor when the longer binomial exponent is a power of three

<a id="statement"></a>
## The factor degree for all four sign choices

Let \(L=3^a\) with \(a\ge2\), let \(0<k<L\) with \(3\nmid k\), and write \(D=L+k\).
For \(s,\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\) put
\[
 P=X^{D}+2s(X^L+\sigma X^k)+\sigma,\qquad r=s\sigma.
\]
There is a rational irreducible factor \(H\) occurring once with
\[
 \deg H\ge e,\qquad e=\begin{cases}L+1&r^k=s,\\L-1&r^k=-s.\end{cases}
\]
It is noncyclotomic.
If \(D/k>3\log6/\log(3/2)\), then \(P=C H\) with \(H\) monic irreducible and
\[
 C=\begin{cases}1& k\text{ odd},\ \sigma=1,\\
 X^2-1& k\text{ odd},\ \sigma=-1,\\
 X+\sigma& k\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
In particular \(14k\le D\) suffices, and \(k\) can tend to infinity along any sequence satisfying these hypotheses.
For instance one can take \(k=3^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}+2\) for all sufficiently large \(a\).
For this odd choice of \(k\), both reciprocal parents are irreducible.
The [coefficient-prime degree bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#statement) and [residual-period estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#statement) use the prime two for these parents.
The assertion here uses the auxiliary prime three and imposes no condition on the prime divisors of the total degree.

<a id="cluster"></a>
## The factor over the 3-adic integers

Reduction modulo three gives
\[
 \bar P=(X^k-s)(X^L-r)=(X^k-s)(X-r)^L.
\]
If \(r^k=s\), the root \(r\) has multiplicity exactly \(L+1\), since \(3\nmid k\).
By coprime Hensel factorization there is a monic factor \(G\in\mathbb Z_3[X]\) with reduction \((X-r)^{L+1}\) and complementary factor a unit at \(r\).
Since \(P(r)=6\sigma\), the constant term of \(G(X+r)\) has valuation one, and all its intermediate coefficients are divisible by three.
Thus \(G\) is Eisenstein after translation.

If \(r^k=-s\), then \(P(r)=0\) and \(rP'(r)=\sigma(L-3k)\) has valuation one.
Apply the same Hensel factorization to \(P/(X-r)\), whose reduction has multiplicity exactly \(L-1\) at \(r\) and whose value at \(r\) is \(P'(r)\).
Again the resulting factor \(G\) of degree \(e\) is Eisenstein after translation.
In both cases the rational irreducible factor \(H\) containing \(G\) occurs only once because \(G\) occurs only once locally.
A root of unity reducing to \(r\) has prime-to-three part of order one or two, since reduction preserves prime-to-three torsion orders.
Its local degree is therefore one or \(2\cdot3^j\) for some \(j\ge0\), by the cyclotomic Eisenstein polynomial \(\Phi_{3^{j+1}}(1+X)\), with a possible change of sign of the root.
But \(e>2\) is prime to three, so \(H\) is noncyclotomic.

<a id="complement"></a>
## Exclusion of nontorsion roots in the complementary factors

After removing \(G\) and, in the second case, the factor \(X-r\), the local quotient has reduction dividing \(X^k-s\).
Consequently any noncyclotomic rational irreducible factor \(S\ne H\) has \(\bar S\mid X^k-s\).
Write \(d=\deg S\), \(m=\log M(S)\), and let \(\alpha\) be a root.
Division by the monic polynomial \(S\) shows that \(\alpha^k-s\in3\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Its norm is nonzero and divisible by \(3^d\), whereas the upper estimate obtained over all conjugates is \(2^d\exp(km)\).
Thus \(km\ge d\log(3/2)\).
Since \(P\) has four terms and coefficient length six, the [sparse cut estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \(Dm\le3d\log6\) for every nontorsion unit factor.
All factors of \(P\) have unit endpoints, so the two bounds give the claimed exclusion of nontorsion factors.

For a cyclotomic factor in the local quotient, the same norm comparison forces \(\alpha^k=s\) exactly, since all conjugates have modulus one.
Substitution in \(P\) then gives \(\alpha^L=-r\).
Conversely every common root of \(X^k-s\) and \(X^L+r\) is a root of \(P\), and occurs once since the local quotient has squarefree reduction and these roots reduce away from \(r\) (the equality \(\alpha^L=-r\) implies this in characteristic three).
Since \(\gcd(k,L)=1\), the two power identities force \(\alpha^2=1\), hence \(\alpha=-r\) as \(L\) is odd.
The candidate \(-r\) contributes exactly when \((-r)^k=s\).
Together with the simple root \(r\) occurring when \(r^k=-s\), this gives the three displayed cyclotomic parts.
