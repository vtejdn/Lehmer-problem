# Distinct Mahler values at every large exponent and simultaneous congruences at factorial exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed total budget along any unbounded sequence of integers

Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\) and \(B=(\log2)/4\).
For every integer \(n\ge2\) take \(a=\max(2,\lfloor n^{1/8}\rfloor)\), \(b_0=\lfloor n^{7/8}\rfloor\) and \(b=b_0+1\).
Count distinct positive logarithmic Mahler values exactly as in the [prime test](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement), now in the signed graph
\[
 G_n(X)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y).
\]
Thus a nonzero integer row of degree at most \(b\) must satisfy
\[
          \big((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2\big)^4<2^{(a-1)n}.
\]
An irreducible primitive factor must occur at least \(a\) times and have a nonzero nontorsion root \(\alpha\) with \(A(\alpha^n)\ne0\).
Let \(N_n\) be the largest number of different qualifying values on one row, or zero if no value occurs.
Let \(K(B)\) be the supremum of the cardinalities of finite sets of different positive measures of irreducible primitive integer polynomials with sum strictly below \(B\).
Then \(N_n\) is computable, \(N_n\le K(B)\) for every \(n\), and \(N_n\to K(B)\) along **all integers**.
A finite limit is attained eventually.
If \(K(B)=+\infty\), every fixed count is met at every sufficiently large exponent.
Consequently the unrestricted Lehmer infimum \(\lambda\) is positive exactly when \(N_n\) is bounded on some infinite set of integers, and \(\lambda=0\) is equivalent to divergence along any prescribed unbounded increasing sequence, including \(2^j\) and \(j!\).
For \(N_n>0\) a maximizing row recovers a positive measure strictly below \(B/N_n\).

There is also a criterion for prescribing the original polynomials.
For any fixed list of representatives of distinct positive values with sum below \(B\), let \(\mathcal O\) be the set of orders of torsion ratios of **distinct** roots in their combined root set.
For all sufficiently large \(n\), a passing row contacts every original representative if and only if no element of \(\mathcal O\) divides \(n\).
The threshold may depend on the list.
Every list of these numerical values admits representatives for which \(\mathcal O\) is empty.

<a id="norm"></a>
## An all-power representative with exactly the original measure

For any nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\), let \(M\) be a positive multiple of the orders of all root-of-unity ratios of distinct conjugates.
One can choose this exponent effectively from the degree alone.
Such a ratio lies in the compositum of two degree-\(d\) fields, so its order \(k\) has \(\varphi(k)\le d^2\).
The elementary inequality \(\varphi(k)^2\ge k/2\) follows by multiplying the prime-power factors \(\varphi(q^e)^2/q^e=q^{e-2}(q-1)^2\), of which only \(q^e=2\) has value below one.
The least common multiple of the integers through \(2d^4\) therefore suffices.

Set \(z=\alpha^M,\ F=\mathbb Q(z),\ s=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):F]\), and \(\delta=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/F}(\alpha)\).
Any two conjugates of \(z\) with torsion ratio arise from two conjugates of \(\alpha\) with torsion ratio and hence are equal.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(z^k)=F\) for every \(k\ge1\).
In particular \(F=\bigcap_{k\ge1}\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\), because \(z^k\) belongs to each respective power field and \(F\) itself is one of the fields.
The [ordinary norm calculation](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#all-positive-power-existence-descent) gives \(\delta^M=z^s\), since the \(M\)-th powers of all \(s\) factors in the relative norm are \(z\).
For every \(k\ge1\), \((\delta^k)^M=z^{sk}\) therefore generates \(F\), so \(\mathbb Q(\delta^k)=F\).
The norm differs from \(\alpha^s\) by a root of unity.
Consequently its absolute logarithmic height is \(s h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)\), including at finite places, and its degree is \(d/s\).
The [primitive-minimum height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) gives \(m(\delta)=m(\alpha)\).
Choosing \(M\) to kill all torsion ratios is what makes the residual field index in the ordinary norm formula equal to one.
Units remain units under the norm.
If \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) are conjugate, the field automorphism sending one to the other preserves \(F\), by the power-field description, and sends the norm to its inverse.
Thus reciprocity is preserved as well.

<a id="realization"></a>
## The precise obstruction for a list of original factors

For any qualifying root put \(z=\alpha^n\).
The graph equation recovers \(\alpha=-A(z)/(\varepsilon A_b^*(z))\), with nonzero denominator.
Consequently the \(n\)-th power map must be injective on all distinct roots in any qualifying list.
Failure of injectivity is equivalent to divisibility of \(n\) by a member of \(\mathcal O\).
The [finite derivative test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) works for every \(n\ge2\), since the least-term multiplier \(n(\operatorname{ord}_0 A-\operatorname{ord}_0 A_b^*)-1\) is nonzero, and substitution is unramified at nonzero roots.
The recovery map gives both equality of fields and distinct powered minima for distinct qualifying orbits.
The [derivative product](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) consequently gives \((a-1)n\sum_i m(P_i)\le 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)\).
It proves the pointwise bound, the extraction bound and the computability by the same finite integer box, number-field factor tests and exact real algebraic comparisons as at prime exponents.

Conversely fix a nonempty list with sum \(S<B\) and take exponents \(n\) avoiding the orders in \(\mathcal O\).
The [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) makes the representatives reciprocal units, and injectivity gives distinct powered minima of the same respective degrees and of measures \(n h_i\), where \(h_i=m(P_i)\).
These are the only uses of primality in the [fixed-list construction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#supply).
In detail, choose \(K>S/\min_i h_i+1\) integral, \(t=a+K\), \(D=\sum_i\deg P_i\), and \(l=2(b_0+1)\) coefficient positions.
The simultaneous divided-derivative boxes have exponent bound \(L=n(b_0+1)\), so \(L/l=n/2\) and \(Dt^2/l=O_D(n^{-5/8})\) at fixed \(K\).
They give an unsigned two-row graph of log one-norm at most \(n(tS+o(1))/2\).
The common divisor of the two rows has powered-root orders \(c_i\) satisfying \(\sum_i c_i h_i\le S+o(1)\) by the same differential product with exponents \(t+c_i-1\).
Thus each \(c_i\le K\) eventually.
Division, the two reciprocal projections, and one integral slope avoiding the finitely many forbidden values preserve all the required contacts with the prescribed sign.
Their additional log cost is \(O(b_0+\log(1+\#\{i\}))=o(n)\).
The resulting normalized cost has limit superior at most \(S\), proving the strict cutoff at every sufficiently large admissible integer exponent.

For each numerical value choose the representative from the norm construction.
Within each representative there are no distinct conjugates with torsion ratio.
A torsion ratio across two representatives would give an equality of powered conjugates at some exponent \(k\).
Their measures after powering would then agree, whereas they are \(k h_i\) and \(k h_j\) by preservation of degree.
This proves \(\mathcal O=\varnothing\) for the combined list.
Every fixed list of different values with sum below \(B\) is therefore realized at all sufficiently large integers.
An integer supremum, when finite, is attained as a cardinality, including the empty list for zero.
An infinite supremum permits a separate fixed list for every required integer count.
These facts prove the limit and the gap equivalences by the [fixed-budget factorization argument](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#consequence).

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## Exact local multiplicities for every prime divisor of the exponent

Dividing a row by its integer content preserves all its contacts and can only reduce its cost, so take a primitive row.
Fix a prime \(q\mid n\), put \(Q=q^{v_q(n)}\), \(m=n/Q\), and use bars for reduction in \(\mathbb F_q[Y]\).
Let \(J=\gcd(\bar A,\overline{A_b^*})\) be monic and put \(U=\bar A/J,\ V=\overline{A_b^*}/J\).
Both original reductions are nonzero, and \(U,V\) are coprime.
Frobenius gives
\[
 \bar G_n(X)=J(X^m)^Q R(X),\qquad
 R(X)=U(X^m)^Q+\varepsilon X V(X^m)^Q,\quad R'=\varepsilon V(X^m)^Q.
\]
The derivative and \(R\) are coprime by Bézout for \(U,V\), so \(R\) is squarefree.
At any nonzero \(\xi\) in the algebraic closure of the residue field, the exact order of \(\bar G_n\) is \(Q\operatorname{ord}_{\xi^m}J+\mathbf1_{R(\xi)=0}\), since \(q\nmid m\).
If \(F\) is a product of distinct qualifying unit minima then \(F^a\mid G_n\) integrally.
For every root \(\xi\) of \(\bar F\) of multiplicity \(r_\xi\) one obtains
\[
       \operatorname{ord}_{\xi^m}J
             \ \ge\ \left\lceil\frac{a r_\xi-1}{Q}\right\rceil .        \tag{1}
\]
All these roots are nonzero by the unit constant coefficients.
Define \(D_q\) by taking each distinct image \(\beta=\xi^m\) with the maximum of the right sides of (1) over its fiber.
The monic polynomial with these roots and multiplicities belongs to \(\mathbb F_q[Y]\) by Frobenius invariance and divides both signed rows after reduction.

For \(n=2^j\), \(j\ge1\), one has \(m=1\).
For a fixed row and width the binary recursion is
\[
 \bar G_{2^{j+1}}=\bar G_{2^j}^{\,2}
                   +(X^2+X)\overline{A_b^*}(X)^{\,2^{j+1}}.
\]
For a root \(\xi\ne0\) of degree \(s\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) the excess above \(2^j\operatorname{ord}_\xi J\) is zero if \(V(\xi)=0\).
Otherwise the excess is one exactly when \((U(\xi)/V(\xi))^{2^j}=\xi\).
The excess is identically zero if the ratio is outside the Frobenius orbit of \(\xi\), and otherwise equals one on exactly one class \(j\bmod s\).
This recurrence concerns the same row and width while the exponent varies.
For example, take \(T=1+Y+\cdots+Y^6,\ A=YT,\ b=7\).
Here \(A_b^*=T\) and \(\bar G_{2^j}=\bar T^{2^j}(X^{2^j}+X)\).
The primitive seventh roots in characteristic two have degree three, and each has exact multiplicity \(2^j+\mathbf1_{3\mid j}\).

<a id="factorial"></a>
## Simultaneous integer tests and growing endpoint divisibility

For any monic nonconstant \(T\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) coprime to \(A\) over \(\mathbb Q\), (1) yields the simultaneous bound
\[
       \prod_{q\mid n} q^{\deg\gcd(\bar T,D_q)}
              \ \mid\ |\operatorname{Res}(T,A)|
              \ \le\ \|A\|_1^{\deg T} M(T)^b.                          \tag{2}
\]
In (2), divisibility concerns the first two positive integers.
Indeed the determinant of multiplication by \(A\) on \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(T)\) is the resultant, and its nullity modulo \(q\) is \(\deg\gcd(\bar T,\bar A)\).
Smith form gives at least that many factors of \(q\), and valuations at the distinct primes combine by multiplication.
The upper bound follows by evaluating \(A\) at the roots of \(T\) with multiplicities.
In particular (2) applies simultaneously to all primes through \(j\) when \(n=j!\).
For a cyclotomic \(T\) the upper bound is \(\|A\|_1^{\deg T}\), extending the [single-prime resultant test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v263.md#resultant).
If such a \(T\) divides \(A\), reciprocity up to sign makes it divide the other row as well.
Its removal leaves each qualifying contact, since \(A(\alpha^n)\ne0\) there, and reduces formal width by \(\deg T\), with the reversal sign adjusted if necessary.
Thus common cyclotomic divisors must have total degree at most \(b-(a-1)\deg F/2\) by the derivative degree bound after removal.

More explicitly, suppose a factorial-exponent graph contacts even one unit minimum of degree at most \(D\) to order \(a\) with nonzero numerator.
For \(j\ge2\) put
\[
    C_{j,D}=\prod_{\substack{q\ {\rm prime}\\q\le(j+1)^{1/D}\\q\le j}}q,
    \qquad k_j=\left\lceil\frac{a-1}{2^j}\right\rceil,\qquad n=j!.
\]
For each such \(q\), every root of this minimum modulo \(q\) has degree \(s\le D\) and multiplicative order dividing \(q^s-1\le j\).
Its order is coprime to \(q\) and hence divides \(j!/q^{v_q(j!)}\), so its image under the power \(m\) is one.
Also \(v_q(j!)\le j/(q-1)\) and \(q^{1/(q-1)}\le2\), whence \(Q\le2^j\).
Equation (1) gives \((Y-1)^{k_j}\mid J\) for every prime in the product.
Consequently \(C_{j,D}\) divides every coefficient of degrees \(0,\ldots,k_j-1\) in both \(A(1+Y)\) and \(A_b^*(1+Y)\).
At fixed \(D\) both \(C_{j,D}\) and \(k_j\) tend to infinity, the latter because \((j!)^{1/8}/2^j\to\infty\).
Every fixed list of distinct values with sum below budget admits representatives of some fixed degree bound, so the all-integer existence theorem realizes these joint congruences on actual rows at every sufficiently large factorial.
The congruences are necessary for the stated contacts and give no unconditional bound on the counts along factorials.
