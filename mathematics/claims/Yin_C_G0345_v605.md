# Optimizing the numerator in the neutral specialization

<a id="bound"></a>
## A family of upper bounds for the neutral infimum

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2h>0\), with no unit-circle root, and put \(u=\pm X^{-h}P\), choosing the sign positive on the circle.
Write \(\gamma\) for the infimum of total logarithmic Mahler measures of nontorsion algebraic units with a unit-circle conjugate.
For a nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\) coprime to \(P\), put \(B=A(X)A(X^{-1})\), and assume \(0<B<4u\) somewhere on the circle.
With normalized Haar measure \(d\mu\), let \(D(s)=0\) for \(0\le s\le4\) and \(D(s)=\operatorname{arcosh}(s/2-1)\) for \(s>4\).
Then
\[
               \gamma\le m(P)+\int D(B/u)\,d\mu.
\]
Thus the infimum of the right side over admissible integer polynomials is an upper bound; \(A=1\) recovers [the quarter-level transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v602.md#statement).
In particular the concrete certificate \(|A|^2\le4u\) throughout the circle gives \(\gamma\le m(P)\).
Equality \(B=4u\) identically is impossible by coprimality, and zeros of \(A\) are isolated, so this certificate supplies the required strict crossing arc.
For the same inputs with \(m(P)<\log4\), the [complementary upper-tail estimate](#dual) gives \(\gamma\le\int D(u)\,d\mu\).

<a id="components"></a>
## Removing the torsion obstruction

Apply the quadratic fiber computation to \(F=B-u(2-Y-Y^{-1})\).
The leading coefficient in \(YF\) is \(u\), and the sum of its roots is \(2-B/u\), giving the displayed integral by Jensen's formula and the [scalar specialization limit](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U002.md#luc22-abelian-matrix-approximation).
A vertical component at \(X=\xi\) would require \(u(\xi)=B(\xi)=0\), impossible since reciprocity and coprimality imply that \(P\) shares no nonzero root with either factor of \(B\).
On a binomial component parametrized by \(X=\xi t^a,Y=\eta t^b\) with \(a\ne0\), choose \(t\) giving \(X\) equal to a root of \(P\).
There \(F=B\ne0\), again impossible.
Consequently the [torsion point theorem](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) bounds the total number of torsion pairs on this fixed curve.
On a strict crossing arc the real values of \(F(X,X^N)\) alternate in sign at the successive points where \(X^N=1,-1\).
For all sufficiently large \(N\) this yields a nontorsion circle root.
The outer coefficients after clearing the monomial are \(\pm1\) for \(N\) sufficiently large relative to the fixed widths, so the root belongs to an irreducible unit factor of positive measure, bounded by the measure of the specialization.

<a id="dual"></a>
## A complementary upper-tail test

Taking the numerator \(B=u^2\) instead introduces a common factor \(u\).
After cancelling it, the remaining Laurent polynomial is \(G=u-(2-Y-Y^{-1})\), and its log measure is exactly \(\int D(u)\,d\mu\) since the quadratic has leading coefficient one.
If \(m(P)<\log4\), continuity supplies an arc with \(0<u<4\).
For this second construction a binomial component would give \(u(\xi t^a)=2-\eta t^b-\eta^{-1}t^{-b}\).
If \(a=0\) or \(b=0\), precisely one side is nonconstant.
If \(ab\ne0\) and \(\xi,\eta\) have modulus one, choose a unit-circle \(t\) with \(\eta t^b=1\), contradicting strict positivity of \(u\).
Thus there are only finitely many torsion pairs, and the same alternating signs give neutral nontorsion factors of normalized specializations with extreme coefficients one for \(N>h\).
It follows that \(\gamma\le\int D(u)\,d\mu\).
Both this tail and the optimized numerator bound must accommodate any positive neutral gap on a hyperbolic sequence of vanishing measure.

<a id="example"></a>
## An irreducible input where the numerator removes the whole loss

Set \(u=(2-X-X^{-1})+(X^3+X^{-3}-1)^2\).
It is strictly positive because the first summand can vanish only at \(X=1\), where the square is one.
At \(X=e^{i\pi/9}\) the square vanishes, and \(u=4\sin^2(\pi/18)<1/4\), since \(\sin(\pi/18)<\pi/18<1/4\).
Thus the constant numerator incurs positive loss on an open arc, whereas \(A=1-X\) satisfies \(B\le u\) everywhere and incurs zero loss.
The polynomial \(P=X^6u=X^{12}-2X^9-X^7+5X^6-X^5-2X^3+1\) is irreducible modulo 19.
For reproduction in \(\mathbb F_{19}[X]/(P)\), start with \(s_0=X\) and iterate \(s_{j+1}=s_j^{19}\bmod P\).
The remainders at indices 4 and 6 are respectively \(4X^{11}-8X^{10}-8X^9+5X^8-5X^7-7X^6-5X^5-3X^3+4X^2+7X+3\) and \(-X^{11}+2X^8+X^6-5X^5+X^4+2X^2\).
Both \(s_4-X\) and \(s_6-X\) have gcd one with \(P\); also \(s_{12}=X\).
The finite-field irreducibility criterion now applies since 2 and 3 are the prime divisors of 12.
This example establishes strict improvement of the loss functional; it gives no uniform estimate as \(m(P)\to0\).
