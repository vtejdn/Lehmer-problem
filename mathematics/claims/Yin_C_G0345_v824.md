# Large rational primes in the denominators of dyadic block evaluations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Prime support on a full interval of the ray

Use the [reversed exterior block](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#statement) of a least-degree witness, with block size \(b\), absolute degree \(d=2ub\), log measure \(m\) and \(R=e^{m/2}\).
Fix an integer \(Q\ge2\) and write \(q_j=2^jQ\) and \(D_j=D(q_j)\) for the [reduced denominator ideal](Yin_C_G0345_v818.md#denominator) in the oriented block field \(K\).
Use absolute ideal norms, and write \(I_N=\{N,\ldots,2N-1\}\) for an integer \(N\ge1\).
Suppose that \(q_N>R\) and
\[
       d\log((q_N-R)/(2b))-(4b-2)m\ \ge\ \tfrac12 dN\log 2.              \tag{1}
\]
This holds for all sufficiently large \(N\) at each fixed witness and \(Q\).
For any finite set \(\mathcal P\) of rational primes and \(0<\eta\le1\), at most
\[
       (d+4/\eta)\,|\mathcal P|                                           \tag{2}
\]
indices in \(I_N\) receive at least the fraction \(\eta\) of \(\log ND_j\) from primes above \(\mathcal P\).
In particular, for any \(J\subseteq I_N\), the union of rational prime divisors of the integers \(ND_j\) for \(j\in J\) has size at least \(|J|/(d+4)\).
Every prime so counted has a divisor in the block field \(E\) that splits in \(K/E\).

For fixed witness and \(Q\), if \(Y_N\ge2\) and \(\pi(Y_N)=o(N)\), then on all but \(o(N)\) indices in \(I_N\) primes larger than \(Y_N\) supply \((1-o(1))\log ND_j\).
For example any fixed \(Y_N=cN\) is allowed by the elementary density zero of the primes.
The bound concerns rational primes at the original starting point of the ray and allows the witness to vary whenever (1) holds and the right side of (2) is small enough.

<a id="local"></a>
## The closest dyadic value to each local root

Put \(g=\tau f\) for the conjugate of the oriented polynomial and let \(P=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}g\) be the signed-root minimal polynomial of degree \(d\) from the [oriented factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement).
Here signing the roots means multiplying them by the rational sign used in v809.
Since \(D_j\) divides the integral ideal \((g(q_j))\), its norm divides \(|P(q_j)|\).
The constant term of \(P\) is a unit, so primes dividing \(2Q\) have valuation zero in every such integer for \(j\ge1\).
For every other rational prime \(p\), embed the roots of \(P\) into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).
For each root \(a\), choose an index \(i(a)\in I_N\) maximizing \(v_p(q_i-a)\).
All these valuations are finite since the roots are algebraic units and \(q_i>1\).
Away from this chosen index, the ultrametric inequality gives
\[
       v_p(q_j-a)\ \le\ v_p(q_j-q_{i(a)})=v_p(2^{|j-i(a)|}-1).
\]
Let \(t\) be the multiplicative order of \(2\) modulo \(p\).
Only differences divisible by \(t\) contribute.
For \(h\ge1\), binomial expansion for the odd prime \(p\) gives
\(v_p(2^{th}-1)=v_p(2^t-1)+v_p(h)\): raising a principal unit to a prime-to-\(p\) exponent leaves its distance valuation unchanged, and raising it to the \(p\)-th power increases that valuation by one.
On the two sides of \(i(a)\), the contributing differences have the forms \(th\) with \(1\le h\le H_-\) and \(1\le h\le H_+\), respectively, where \(H_-+H_+\le(N-1)/t\).
Use \(v_p(2^t-1)\log p\le t\log 2\) and \(v_p(H!)\le H/(p-1)\).
Since \(\log p/(p-1)\le\log 2\), summing the distance valuations costs at most \(2N\log 2\) per root after multiplication by \(\log p\).
Thus for some \(B_p\subset I_N\) with \(|B_p|\le d\),
\[
     \sum_{j\in I_N\setminus B_p}v_p(ND_j)\log p\ \le\ 2dN\log 2.          \tag{3}
\]
Indeed take the union of the chosen indices over the roots and use the monic factorization of \(P(q_j)\).
For \(p\mid2Q\) take \(B_p\) empty.

<a id="mass"></a>
## Passing from each prime to the total denominator

The lower bound in [v818](Yin_C_G0345_v818.md#denominator) and (1) give \(\log ND_j\ge dN\log(2)/2\) throughout \(I_N\).
The union of the \(B_p\) for \(p\in\mathcal P\) costs at most \(d|\mathcal P|\) indices.
On its complement (3) bounds the total logarithmic contribution from \(\mathcal P\) by \(2dN\log(2)|\mathcal P|\).
Each remaining index counted in (2) consumes at least \(\eta dN\log(2)/2\), proving (2).
Apply it with \(\eta=1\) and with \(\mathcal P\) equal to the joint support of \(J\) for the support bound.
The [involution on the fractional ideal](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#ideals) puts every prime of \(D_j\) above a split prime of \(E\).
Finally take all rational primes through \(Y_N\) and \(\eta=\sqrt{\pi(Y_N)/N}\) for sufficiently large \(N\) in the fixed-witness regime.
Both \(\eta\) and the fraction of indices discarded by (2) tend to zero.
