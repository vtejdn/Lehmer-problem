# Frequency concentration for sparse interlacers of small Mahler measure targets

<a id="statement"></a>
## All surviving trace harmonics have degree asymptotic to the full interlacer degree

Let \(P_i\) be monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomials of degree \(2m_i\) with \(M(P_i)>1\) and \(M(P_i)\to1\).
Let \(A_i\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degrees \(k_i\to\infty\) have real symmetric Laurent expansions
\[
 f_i(z)=A_i(z+z^{-1})=\sum_{|n|\le k_i}a_{i,n}z^n,\qquad a_{i,-n}=a_{i,n}.
\]
Assume bounded term counts and bounded coefficients for \(f_i\).
The distinct zeros of \(f_i\) on the unit circle divide it into open arcs, counting also the arc across angle zero.
Suppose there are \(2k_i-o(k_i)\) distinct circle zeros and only \(o(k_i)\) of the arcs contain no circle root of \(P_i\).
If \(k_i/m_i\to1\), then
\[
            \min_{a_{i,n}\ne0}|n|/k_i\longrightarrow1 .
\]
Moreover the length in radians of the arc containing a uniform circle angle, multiplied by \(k_i\), converges in probability to \(\pi\).
The same holds under uniform sampling of the arguments of the conjugates of \(P_i\), assigning length zero to a boundary point.
No commensurability assumption is imposed on the limiting frequency ratios.

In particular fix \(J,K\ge1\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\).
There is a uniform positive Mahler gap for Salem numbers whose trace minimal polynomial admits a strict integer interlacer \(A\) of positive leading coefficient at most \(J\), if its circle Laurent expansion has at most \(K\) terms and a nonzero harmonic of absolute index at most \((1-\varepsilon)\deg A\).
Thus on an escaping sequence any such bounded-leading, bounded-term full interlacers must eventually have two bands of exponents of width \(o(\deg A)\) at the ends of their reciprocal lifts.

<a id="phases"></a>
## The common limit from sampling angles and from sampling conjugates

Pass to a subsequence fixing the coefficients, the presence or absence of a constant, and the number \(r\ge1\) of distinct positive exponents \(n_{1,i}<\cdots<n_{r,i}=k_i\).
Write \(\mathbf n_i=(n_{1,i},\ldots,n_{r,i})\) and pass further so that \(\mathbf n_i/k_i\to\boldsymbol\lambda\), with largest coordinate one.
By diagonal extraction each fixed integer relation \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n_i=0\) holds eventually or fails eventually.
The eventual relations form a saturated lattice \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^r\).
Let \(G\subset\mathbb T^r\) be its annihilator, a connected torus with Haar probability \(\mu\) and character lattice \(\mathbb Z^r/\Lambda\).
For all large \(i\) the whole angular curve \(X_i(\theta)=(e^{in_{j,i}\theta})_j\) lies in \(G\), by taking a finite basis of \(\Lambda\).
The flow \(\phi_t=(e^{i\lambda_jt})_j\) also lies in \(G\).
The fixed Laurent polynomial \(F(X)=a_0+\sum_j a_j(X_j+X_j^{-1})\) on \(G\) satisfies \(F(X_i(\theta))=f_i(e^{i\theta})\).
Its characters with nonzero coefficients are distinct, since the original signed exponents are distinct at every index.

For uniform \(\theta\), \(X_i(\theta)\) tends in distribution to \(\mu\) by integrating each character.
The same holds if \(\theta\) is the argument of a uniformly chosen root of \(P_i\).
Indeed for a root \(\alpha_i\) the joint point \((\alpha_i^{n_{j,i}})_j\) lies in the algebraic torus with character lattice \(\mathbb Z^r/\Lambda\), eventually.
In any fixed basis its height is \(O(k_i\log M(P_i)/m_i)=o(1)\) when \(k_i/m_i\) is bounded, by the height inequalities for fixed monomials.
Every nontrivial character eventually takes a value different from one since \(\alpha_i\) is nontorsion and its integer exponent is eventually nonzero.
Thus [Bilu's torus theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives the same Haar limit for the joint phases.
The joint orbit is counted with equal multiplicities in the full root orbit by separability.
Below both angular samples can consequently be used to test any bounded statistic of \(X_i\) having the same locally stable limit at \(\mu\)-almost every phase.
Precisely, stability means convergence along every sequence of points on the angular curves tending to such a phase.
The assertion follows from weak convergence on the compact torus, by covering a compact set of good phases of probability arbitrarily close to one by continuity neighborhoods, or by the almost-sure coupling of weakly convergent probabilities on a compact metric space.

<a id="regular"></a>
## Local stability of the distinct zeros under the limiting flow

For \(X_i(\theta_i)\to X\), the functions \(f_i(e^{i(\theta_i+t/k_i)})\) converge locally uniformly in complex \(t\) to \(F(X\phi_t)\).
We verify that for almost every \(X\), the distinct real zeros on each bounded \(t\)-interval with nonzero endpoint values persist individually, even if the Laurent polynomial has repeated factors.
Choose character coordinates on \(G\), so its complexification is \((\mathbb C^\times)^p\).
Factor \(F\) into Laurent irreducibles over \(\mathbb C\).
The derivative along the flow multiplies each character by \(i\) times its real frequency.
An irreducible factor dividing its own derivative is an eigenfunction of this derivative, allowing eigenvalue zero.
Indeed the derivative has support within the factor's support, and in a Laurent product the Newton polytope is the Minkowski sum, as seen by taking extremal terms in generic directions.
Any nonzero quotient must therefore be a constant.
Such a factor either never vanishes on \(X\phi_t\) or vanishes identically there, and its value at \(X\) is nonzero for almost every \(X\).

Let \(Q\) be the product, without multiplicities, of the other irreducible factors, with \(Q=1\) for the empty product.
For nonconstant \(Q\) it is coprime to its flow derivative, by the product rule and the choice of its factors.
Their common complex zero set has dimension at most \(p-2\), since a codimension-one component would give a common irreducible factor.
The real trajectories meeting that set form a Haar-null set of starting phases.
To see this, decompose the complex zero set into countably many smooth complex charts, taking the smooth locus and then the successively lower-dimensional singular loci.
On each chart a projection onto at most \(p-2\) complex coordinates has a local holomorphic inverse on the chart image.
Points also on \(G\) project into that many real unit circles.
Sweeping their inverses by one real time parameter gives a countable union of locally Lipschitz images of subsets of dimension at most \(p-1\) in real coordinates, hence zero \(p\)-dimensional Haar measure.
For \(p=1\) the common zero set is empty.
It follows that all real zeros of \(Q(X\phi_t)\) on a typical trajectory are simple.

The involution conjugating coefficients and inverting the characters preserves \(F\), and permutes the nonstationary factors because it preserves the eigenfunction condition.
It thus carries \(Q\) to a Laurent unit times \(Q\).
Along any real angular curve in \(G\), the complex zeros in the time variable are consequently invariant under complex conjugation.
At good \(X\) all zeros of \(F\) on a bounded real interval come from \(Q\).
Near each simple zero of \(Q(X\phi_t)\), convergence of this fixed Laurent polynomial along \(X_i(\theta_i+t/k_i)\) gives exactly one nearby zero by Rouché's theorem, and uniqueness makes it real.
The excluded eigenfactors stay nonzero near the whole compact real segment, and away from the chosen neighborhoods \(Q\) stays nonzero.
This gives the asserted persistence of distinct nodes.
For any prescribed pair of endpoints the exceptional starting phases with a zero there also have measure zero, since \(F\) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.

<a id="gaps"></a>
## Sampling one root in each gap forces constant rescaled length

When \(k_i/m_i\to1\), all but \(o(k_i)\) circle intervals contain exactly one circle root of \(P_i\), and all but \(o(k_i)\) of its \(2m_i\) roots are accounted for in these intervals.
For fixed \(L>0\), at most \(2\pi k_i/L\) intervals can have length greater than \(L/k_i\).
Thus among target roots the proportion seeing no template node in the forward angular segment of length \(L/k_i\) is at most \(2\pi k_i/(2m_i L)+o(1)\), and the same holds backwards.
Endpoint coincidences among the unaccounted target roots cost \(o(1)\); using an open segment and non-strict comparison for its length gives the same bound.
By local stability and the target phase limit, the limiting probability of no forward node through time \(L\) is at most \(\pi/L\), and likewise backwards.
Letting \(L\) tend to infinity proves that nearest neighbors exist on both sides of almost every starting phase.
Translation invariance gives this conclusion simultaneously at all rational time translates of a typical phase.

Let \(\ell(X)\) denote the time between the nearest bracketing real zeros of \(F(X\phi_t)\) at a typical phase.
The local result, on a large enough fixed segment for that phase, shows convergence to \(\ell(X)\) of the scaled lengths \(\ell_i=k_i|I|\) of the template arcs containing the respective origins.
For any continuous \(h\) of compact support in \((0,\infty)\), uniform angular sampling gives the exact sum \(\sum_I\ell_i h(\ell_i)/(2\pi k_i)\).
Sampling \(\ell_i h(\ell_i)\) at the target arguments instead gives the sum \(\sum_I\ell_i h(\ell_i)/(2m_i)+o(1)\), since the integrand is bounded and all but \(o(k_i)\) roots and intervals are matched one to one.
The two Haar limits from the preceding sections therefore satisfy
\[
          \int_G h(\ell)\,d\mu=\frac1\pi\int_G \ell h(\ell)\,d\mu .
\]
Since \(0<\ell<\infty\) almost surely, \(\ell=\pi\) almost surely.
Sampling any bounded continuous function of the scaled lengths now gives the two claimed convergences in probability.

<a id="frequencies"></a>
## The zero progression and the intermediate harmonics

At a typical \(X\) every rational translate sees a gap of length \(\pi\), and its simple distinct nodes have no finite accumulation and extend unboundedly in both directions.
Every open gap contains a rational time, so the full zero set on that trajectory is \(t_0+\pi\mathbb Z\) for some real \(t_0\).
Write \(F(X\phi_t)=\sum_{\omega}b_\omega(X)e^{i\omega t}\), grouping equal limiting frequencies \(-1\le\omega\le1\).
Each \(b_\omega\) is a nonzero sum of distinct characters with fixed nonzero coefficients and hence is nonzero at almost every \(X\).
Evaluation at \(t_0+\pi l\) for integer \(l\) gives a linear combination of geometric sequences with ratios \(e^{i\pi\omega}\) equal to zero for all \(l\).
Only the two extremes \(\omega=-1,1\) have the same ratio.
The Vandermonde determinant then forces \(b_\omega(X)=0\) for every interior \(\omega\), contradicting its almost sure nonvanishing if any interior frequency was present.
Thus all normalized active exponents cluster at the two ends, on every extracted subsequence.

For the Salem corollary \(k_i=m_i-1\) and all interlacer roots except possibly the last are in \((-2,2)\), strictly between consecutive trace roots.
The last is below the exterior trace and above the largest neutral trace.
Its omission and the seam arcs cost only a bounded number of nodes or intervals on the circle.
The lifted interlacer has Mahler measure at most \(J\sigma_i\) because at most this last trace can have a lift outside and its exterior modulus is at most \(\sigma_i\).
The [bounded-term coefficient inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) then bounds all coefficients uniformly.
Bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure allow only finitely many monic targets, so degrees must tend to infinity on a putative escape.
The frequency conclusion contradicts the prescribed central harmonic, proving the uniform gap.
