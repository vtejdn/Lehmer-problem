# Cancellation among the first dyadic evaluations of a reversed block

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound after combining the valuations

Use the [reversed block](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#statement), put \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]=2u\), \(R=e^{m/2}\), and write \(w(X)=f(X)/(\tau f)(X)\).
For an integer \(q>R\) and \(s\ge1\), define
\[
 C_s(T)=\prod_{h=1}^{s-1}(2^h-T)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_jT^j,\qquad
 W_s(q)=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}w(2^jq)^{c_j},\qquad B_s=2^{s(s-1)/2}.
\]
Write \(D_s(q)\) for the integral denominator ideal of \(W_s(q)\) in lowest terms.
At the distinguished real place put \(H_s=\log W_s(q)\).
Then
\[
 0<H_s\le B_s\frac{2mR}{q-R},\qquad
 ND_s(q)\ge\frac{(q^s/2)^n}{\sinh^2(H_s/2)}.
\]
In particular, for each fixed \(s\) as \(m\to0\),
\[
 ND_s(2)\gg_s 2^{(s-1)n}m^{-2}.
\]
For \(s=2\) this applies to the denominator remaining in \(w(2)^2/w(4)\) after cancellation, even if the two evaluations have identical prime support.
More precisely, for each split prime of the block field choose either prime \(\mathfrak P\) of \(K\) above it.
Then \(\log ND_s(q)\) is the sum over these choices of
\[
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_j v_{\mathfrak P}(w(2^j q))\right|\log N\mathfrak P.
\]
This follows from the [opposite valuations in each split pair](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#ideals).
The bound permits this sum to grow through higher multiplicities at old primes, so a degree-independent lower bound for \(m\) still requires additional control.

<a id="proof"></a>
## Vanishing at zero and strict positivity at the real evaluation

The [constant terms](Yin_C_G0345_v830.md#congruence) give \(w(0)=1\), and both numerator and denominator have unit constant terms.
Form the rational functions \(W_s(X)\) by the displayed product with \(q\) replaced by \(X\).
Their numerators and denominators can be chosen integral with unit constant terms, so their expansions belong to \(\mathcal O_K[[X]]\).
We have \(W_s(X)=1+O(X^s)\) as a formal series.
Indeed this holds for \(W_1=w\), and
\[
 W_{h+1}(X)=W_h(X)^{2^h}/W_h(2X).
\]
If the coefficient of \(X^h\) in \(W_h\) is \(a\), both numerator and denominator on the right have expansion \(1+2^haX^h+O(X^{h+1})\).
Thus for an integral presentation \(W_s=A/B\) with unit constant terms, \(X^s\) divides \(A-B\) in \(\mathcal O_K[X]\).
At every prime dividing \((q)\), \(B(q)\) is a local unit and \(W_s(q)-1\) belongs to \(q^s\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak P}\).

Write the [positive real expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#expansion) as \(\log w(q)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_k q^{-k}\) with \(a_k>0\).
Absolute convergence gives \(H_s=\sum_{k\ge1}a_k q^{-k} C_s(2^{-k})\).
Since \(0<C_s(2^{-k})\le B_s\), the same expansion gives the asserted bounds on \(H_s\).
In particular \((W_s(q)-1)D_s(q)\) is a nonzero integral ideal divisible by \((q^s)\).
The two real embeddings contribute \(4\sinh^2(H_s/2)\) to the absolute norm of \(W_s(q)-1\), while the remaining embeddings each contribute at most two by unit modulus of every evaluation there.
Comparing the resulting bound \(2^n\sinh^2(H_s/2)ND_s(q)\) with \(q^{sn}\) proves the claim.
