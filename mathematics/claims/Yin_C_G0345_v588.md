# Heights and exponents in terminal trees at strict degree records

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite window for marked terminal lists

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and \(h=h(\alpha)=m/d\), using absolute logarithmic height.
Assume that every nontorsion unit of degree strictly less than \(d\) has logarithmic Mahler measure strictly greater than \(m\).
Call such an input a strict degree record.
Along any sequence of strict degree records with \(m\to0\), take integers \(1\le B\le d/4\) with \(Bm\to0\).
Put \(T=\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil\) and \(N=\lfloor T\log(2)/(4m)\rfloor\).
For all sufficiently late inputs there are integers \(3\le r\le j\le T+1\), with \(r\to\infty\), for which the following holds.
Put
\[
        Y=\log j+Nh,\qquad S=\lfloor 4Y/h\rfloor .
\]
There is one fixed ordered list of nonzero algebraic integers \(a_1,\dots,a_{r-1}\in K\), each of absolute degree at most \(B\) and height at most \(Y\), admitting at least \((c_0/m)^{r-2}\) sorted exponent lists \(-S\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_{r-1}\le S\) for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
For every list, \(\sum_i a_i\alpha^{t_i}=c\) for a nonzero algebraic integer \(c\in K\) of degree at most \(B\) and height at most \(Y\).
The relation after appending \(-c\) is terminal under the [full degree-\(B\) splitting rule](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement), which tests all coefficients of the permitted degree at all integer exponents.
The estimates \(Y=O(\log(2d))\) and \(S=O(d\log(2d)/m)\) are uniform.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Relative norm to a proper subfield

For any proper subfield \(F\subset K\) containing \(\mathbb Q\), put \(e=[K:F]\) and \(u=N_{K/F}(\alpha)\).
The norm is a unit and \(h(u)\le e h\), by the product inequality for height applied to the \(e\) conjugate factors in a normal closure.
The [degree-height identity](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) therefore gives \(\log M(u)\le [F:\mathbb Q]e h=m\).
Since \(\deg u<d\), the record hypothesis forces \(u\) to be a root of unity.
In particular if \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\) for \(k\ge1\), then \(u^k=(\alpha^k)^e\) is nontorsion, so this \(F\) cannot be proper.
Thus the minimum power-field degree in the terminal count equals \(d\).

More generally, suppose \(0\ne z=c\alpha^n\) with \(c\in K\) of degree less than \(d\) and \(n\in\mathbb Z\).
Taking \(F=\mathbb Q(c)\) gives \(N_{K/F}(z)=c^e u^n\), hence
\[
       h(c)=h(N_{K/F}(z))/e\le h(z),\qquad
       |n|h=h(z/c)\le2h(z).
\]
The same bounds hold with either sign on the edge value.
If \(z\) is integral, so is \(c=z\alpha^{-n}\), since all integer powers of the unit are integral.

<a id="edges"></a>
## Edge sums in the original interval

Take a minimal ternary relation on \(j\) original exponents in \([0,N)\), translated so its least exponent is zero.
Split repeatedly as in the [terminal tree](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees), keeping the original exponent coordinates until all splits have been made.
Each vertex relation sums to zero and the values on opposite ends of every internal edge cancel.
Removing any edge divides the tree into two components.
Summing the vertex relations on either side with internal vertices cancels every edge internal to that component and expresses its cut value, up to sign, as the sum of the signed original leaves on that side.
For an edge incident to a leaf this also follows directly from its original term; the complementary sum has the opposite sign because the original leaf sum is zero.
Thus the conclusion includes edges introduced by splits of a vertex that already contained earlier intermediate terms, and the eventual root leaf.

Each such value \(z\) is integral and nonzero.
At any archimedean embedding \(\sigma\) the sum is at most \(j\max(1,|\sigma(\alpha)|)^N\) in modulus; all finite places contribute zero to its logarithmic height.
Averaging gives \(h(z)\le \log j+Nh=Y\).
Every edge coefficient has degree at most \(B<d\), including the leaf signs, so the norm estimate puts all absolute edge exponents in \([-2Y/h,2Y/h]\) and all coefficient heights at most \(Y\).
At a vertex, placing its input exponent at zero subtracts that edge exponent from all outgoing exponents, giving the integer window \([-S,S]\); the coefficients retain their absolute heights and integrality.
These bounds apply to every complete splitting of such an original witness, with any allowed representation of each cut value.

<a id="count"></a>
## Reconstructing with the finite window

The [finite-scale support count](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale) gives one \(j\le T+1\) with at least \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\) translation classes of minimal supports of span less than \(N\).
Each class has the required translate in \([0,N)\); choose a vanishing sign choice and a complete splitting for each.
For \(3\le k\le j\), define \(W'_k\) as the maximum over fixed outgoing coefficient lists of the number of sorted local exponent lists obeying all integrality, degree, height, window and full terminality conditions in the statement with arity \(k\).
A list satisfying these restrictions has at most \((2S+1)^{k-1}\) exponent choices, so a positive maximum is attained whenever a realization exists; set an empty count to zero.
Root at the original leaf of exponent zero and order outgoing edges by increasing exponent, breaking ties arbitrarily.
In reconstruction from the leaves, their signs fix their coefficients, and each completed child determines the coefficient presented to its parent, with the edge sign reversed.
For these now fixed outgoing coefficients there are at most \(W'_k\) possible normalized exponent lists at a vertex of degree \(k\), since every completion corresponding to an original witness meets the edge bounds.
Each list fixes the incoming coefficient by summation and translates the child subtrees into the incoming edge coordinates.
The root leaf fixes the translate of the reconstructed support.

The [plane tree and sign count](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees) therefore gives
\[
       A_j\le32^j\Bigl(\max_{3\le k\le j}(W'_k)^{1/(k-2)}\Bigr)^{j-2},
\]
using \(\sum_v(\deg v-2)=j-2\) over internal vertices.
Combining the two inequalities as in that count gives \(W'_r\ge(c_0/m)^{r-2}\) at a maximizing \(r\), for example with \(c_0=\log(2)/(32\cdot32^3)\) for sufficiently small \(m\).
For each fixed \(k\), the [unrestricted bound](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement) gives \(W'_k\le2BH_k=o(1/m)\) under \(Bm\to0\), so these maximizing arities tend to infinity.
Finally, \(j\le T+1\) and \(Nh\le T\log(2)/(4d)\) give the stated uniform estimates for \(Y,S\).

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Primitive shapes and the possible witnessing arities

Divide each nonzero marked exponent list by the positive gcd of its entries and retain its order and the same fixed outgoing coefficient list.
Let \(V_r\) count the resulting primitive lists, including one additional shape if the zero list occurs.
For each nonzero primitive list at least one entry has magnitude at least one, so the window contains at most \(S\) positive dilations; the zero list contributes at most one.
Thus, for the preceding witness and all sufficiently late inputs,
\[
        V_r\ge (c_0/m)^{r-2}/S .
\]
Write \(L=\log(1/m)\).
Since \(\log S\le L+O(\log(2d))\), whenever \(\log d=o(rL)\) this yields \(\log V_r\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)L\), for every allowed sequence of \(B\) and without conditions on the conjugate radii or signatures.
Conversely, if along these witnesses \(V_r\le A^r\) with fixed \(A\ge1\), then \((r-2)(L+\log c_0)\le r\log A+L+O(\log(2d))\).
As \(L\to\infty\), this forces \(r=O(1+\log d/L)\).
Both conclusions concern the finite-window witnesses at the selected arities and coefficients; a uniform degree-independent upper bound for their primitive counts remains open.

<a id="polynomial"></a>
## A dilation bound from the coefficient field

There is a second bound on each positive dilation \(q\) that uses the height window itself.
For a nonzero primitive direction append the marked exponent zero and let \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) denote the outgoing coefficients and the signed output \(-c\) for this particular dilation.
Write \(u_i\) for the resulting primitive exponents, \(u_0=\min_i u_i\), and \(E=\mathbb Q(b_1,\ldots,b_r)\), of degree \(D\le\min(d,B^r)\).
The polynomial \(G(X)=\sum_{i=1}^r b_i X^{u_i-u_0}\) has integral coefficients in \(E\) and vanishes at \(\alpha^q\).
There are at least two distinct exponents because the direction is nonzero and the marked exponent is zero.
Each group at a common exponent is a proper nonempty sublist, and its coefficient sum is nonzero by nondegeneracy.
Thus \(G\ne0\), even allowing repeated exponents.

The product \(P_G=\prod_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\tau(G)\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), since its coefficients are algebraic integers fixed by every automorphism of a normal closure.
It has \(\alpha^q\) as a root using the given embedding for one factor.
Bounding each factor on the unit circle by \(r\prod_i\max(1,|\tau(b_i)|)\) and summing logarithms over embeddings gives
\[
      \log M(P_G)\le D\bigl(\log r+\textstyle\sum_i h(b_i)\bigr)
                      \le D(\log r+rY).
\]
The monic irreducible polynomial of \(\alpha^q\) divides \(P_G\) integrally, and the nonzero integral quotient has Mahler measure at least one.
Degree preservation proved [above](#norm) gives \(\log M(\alpha^q)=d qh=qm\).
It follows that each dilation obeys
\[
           q\le Q_B:=\min(d,B^r)(\log r+rY)/m .
\]
When \(B=1\), all \(b_i\) are rational integers and \(|b_i|\le e^Y\); the same circle bound using their maximum gives \(q\le Q_1'=(\log r+Y)/m\).
These inequalities hold separately for every realized output, even when the output varies along a direction.

In the primitive count one may therefore replace \(S\) by \(\min(S,Q_B)\), also including \(Q_1'\) in the minimum when \(B=1\), for all sufficiently late inputs.
For sequences with \(\log B=o(L)\), the estimate \(\log Q_B\le L+r\log B+O(\log(2r)+\log\log(3d))\) gives the full lower rate \(\log V_r\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)L\) whenever \(\log\log(3d)=o(rL)\).
If instead \(V_r\le A^r\) with fixed \(A\) at those witnesses, then
\[
 (r-2)(L+\log c_0)\le r\log A+L+r\log B+O(\log(2r)+\log\log(3d)).
\]
Absorbing the terms of size \(o(rL)\) as \(L\to\infty\) yields \(r=O(1+\log\log(3d)/L)\).
This sharper arity constraint in particular applies with fixed rational outgoing and varying rational output coefficients.
