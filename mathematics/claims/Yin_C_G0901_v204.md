# Splitting at the primes that carry the norm of an adjacent-record translate

<a id="splitting"></a>
## Orienting the prime divisors in intermediate Galois extensions

Under failure of the unrestricted gap use the [adjacent records and second closest exponent](Yin_C_G0901_v200.md#records).
In particular \(A,B>1\) are their strictly dominant unit measures, \(b=\log B\to0\), \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), \(F=K(B)\), \(D=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and \(z=2-A^aB\) has normalized absolute log norm \(H(z)\to\log2\).
Give a prime ideal \(Q\) of \(F\) weight \(w_Q=v_Q(z)\log NQ/D\), with integer ideal valuations.
For every finite subgroup \(G\le\operatorname{Aut}_K(F)\) one may keep at most one prime of \(F\) over each prime of \(F^G\), all over primes splitting completely in \(F/F^G\), with retained weight at least
\[
       H(z)-C(|G|-1)\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}
\]
for an absolute \(C\) on sufficiently late rows.
In particular this keeps \(\log2-o(1)\) mass if the product of \(|G|-1\) and the square-root term tends to zero.

Indeed for any \(\tau\ne1\) in \(G\), \(\tau B\ne B\) because \(F=K(B)\).
The two finite principal ideals generated by \(z,\tau z\) have gcd dividing the ideal generated by \(1-\tau B/B\), since \(A,B\) are units and \(\tau A=A\).
The quotient \(\tau B/B\) has infinite order by strict dominance at the specified real embedding, and its absolute logarithmic height is at most \(2b\).
The [cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) bounds the normalized log norm \(g(z,\tau z)\) of this gcd by \(C\sqrt{b\log(1/b)}\).
At a prime with nontrivial stabilizer choose any nonidentity \(\tau\) fixing it.
Its entire weight occurs in \(g(z,\tau z)\).
On each free orbit of primes retain a prime of maximal valuation.
For each discarded prime \(Q\) in this orbit choose \(\tau\ne1\) taking the retained prime to \(Q\).
Then \(v_Q(\tau z)\ge v_Q(z)\), so the term of \(g(z,\tau z)\) there also covers the entire discarded weight.
Summing over the discarded primes costs at most \(\sum_{\tau\ne1}g(z,\tau z)\).
In the Galois extension \(F/F^G\) a free orbit is exactly a completely split prime, including the condition of no ramification.

<a id="salem"></a>
## The later record is a Salem number

If the later source is Salem, \(B\) is its exterior root.
Put \(L=K(B+B^{-1})\).
For all sufficiently late such rows \(F/L\) is genuinely quadratic.
Indeed the [relative orbit](Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) gives \([K(B):K]>2\).
Only two of the conjugates of \(B\) are real, so some embedding fixing \(K\) takes \(B\) to a nonreal conjugate \(B'\) on the unit circle.
Extend the embedding to an automorphism \(s\) of a common normal field over \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb C\).
Complex conjugation \(c\) fixes \(K\) pointwise in the distinguished embedding since \(A\) is real.
Thus \(s^{-1}cs\) fixes \(K\) and sends \(B\) to \(B^{-1}\).
It restricts to a nonidentity involution of \(F\) fixing \(L\), and the quadratic equation over \(L\) gives the degree.

Consequently almost all the normalized log norm of \(2-A^aB\) occurs at split primes of this explicit quadratic extension, on at most one of the two primes above each prime of \(L\).
Taking \(|G|=2\) bounds the combined mass on inert and ramified primes by \(O(\sqrt{b\log(1/b)})\), even at their fixed relative degree two.
The previous [depth concentration](Yin_C_G0901_v202.md#depth) can be imposed at the same time by taking the intersection and adding the two discarded masses.
This is a restriction over \(K(B+B^{-1})\).
Many split prime pairs of that field may still lie over one rational prime, so exclusion of concentration above a fixed list of rational primes remains open.
