# All short rational functions taking a given unit to a power

<a id="statement"></a>
## One rational generator for all delays

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m>0\), fix an integer \(W\ge1\) and \(L\ge2\).
Consider all nonzero rational functions \(f\in\mathbb Q(X)\) with a reduced representation by integral polynomials of degrees at most \(W\) and sums of absolute coefficients at most \(L\), such that \(f(\alpha)=\alpha^n\) for an integer \(n\).
Only defined nonzero evaluations are included.
There is an absolute \(C\) such that if
\[
                     d>CW(1+\log L/m),                                  \tag{1}
\]
then either every such function is a power of \(X\), or there are a single nonconstant rational function \(H\in\mathbb Q(X)\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_0 H=0\) and an integer \(N\) such that \(H(\alpha)=\alpha^N\) and every such \(f\) has the form
\[
 f=X^a H^b,\qquad n=a+bN,\qquad
 |a|\le W,\qquad |b|\le\min(W,2\log_2 L).                               \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the number of different attainable integer exponents is \(O(W\min(W,\log(2L)))\), independently of \(d,\alpha\) and the size of the largest exponent.
The same absolute bound holds in the monomial case.
A sufficient condition for (1) is \(d>C'W(1+\log L)(\log(2d))^3\), by the classical degree-dependent lower bound for \(m\).
There are examples of arbitrarily high degree satisfying (1) and attaining at least \(cW\min(W,\log(2L))\) distinct exponents, with an absolute \(c>0\) for every \(W,L\) in the stated ranges.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## From a pair of delays to a rank bound

If \(f(\alpha)=1\), then \(f=1\) as a rational function since the numerator of \(f-1\) has degree at most \(W<d\).
One can invert a nonzero function to reverse the sign of its exponent without changing its bounds.
For two functions \(f,g\) at nonzero exponents, arrange the larger absolute exponent as \(n>0\).
If the [two-delay identity](Yin_C_G0345_v199.md#statement) fails, the compression and height estimates give
\[
 d\le C_1(W+\sqrt{nW}),\qquad n\le W+d\log L/m .
\]
For \(d>2C_1 W\), squaring the first inequality gives \(d^2\le 4C_1^2W(W+d\log L/m)\).
Thus \(d\le C_2 W(1+\log L/m)\), excluded by (1) for sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
Reversing any orientations, there must be integers \(u,v\), not both zero, and \(j\) with \(f^u g^v=X^j\) identically.
The power functions themselves also satisfy such an identity with any other function.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the subgroup of \(\mathbb Q(X)^\times\) generated by all the specified functions including \(X\).
The finite coefficient and width bounds make this a finitely generated abelian group.
Every member evaluates at \(\alpha\) to an integer power of \(\alpha\).
It has no torsion because the only possible nontrivial torsion element is the constant \(-1\), which cannot be an integer power of a nontorsion unit.
In fact a rational constant in \(\Gamma\) is a rational algebraic unit, so is one.
Map \(\Gamma\) to itself by \(f\mapsto X^{-\operatorname{ord}_0 f}f\).
The image consists of elements with neither pole nor zero at zero.
In that image the integer relation for every pair of generators becomes \(\tilde f^u\tilde g^v=1\), by taking the order at zero in the identity and canceling the powers of \(X\).
If one normalized generator is nontrivial, it has infinite order and each of the others lies in its one-dimensional rational span after tensoring the group with \(\mathbb Q\).
The normalized group is therefore either trivial or infinite cyclic, by the structure theorem for finitely generated torsion-free abelian groups.
In the second case choose a generator \(H\).
It belongs to \(\Gamma\), hence \(H(\alpha)=\alpha^N\) for one integer \(N\).
Its order at zero is zero, and the constant calculation above ensures it is nonconstant.

<a id="exponents"></a>
## The size of the exponent of the generator

For an allowed function \(f\), its order \(a\) at zero satisfies \(|a|\le W\).
Normalizing at zero merely removes a monomial from its numerator or denominator.
After removing any common integer content, the normalized function still has a jointly primitive integral numerator and denominator with lengths at most \(L\), degrees at most \(W\), nonzero constant terms and no common polynomial factor.
Represent \(H=U/V\) by such a jointly primitive pair (with no bound yet on sizes).
By Gauss's lemma, raising both polynomials to a common positive power preserves coprimality and joint primitivity, also when one polynomial has nonunit content.
The reduced primitive pair for \(H^b\) is therefore \(U^{|b|},V^{|b|}\) in one order or the other, up to a common sign.
Since at least one of \(U,V\) has positive degree and both have nonzero constant term, \(|b|\le W\) for any admitted \(b\ne0\).
At least one of these polynomials has two or more nonzero integer coefficients.
Its circle supremum is at least \(\sqrt 2\) by Parseval, and the supremum of its \(|b|\)-th power is at most the coefficient length \(L\).
This proves the remaining exponent bound in (2); the case \(b=0\) is immediate.
Evaluation proves the formula for \(n\) by nontorsion.
Counting integer pairs in the displayed ranges gives the upper bound regardless of collisions.
In the trivial normalized group the only exponents are between \(-W\) and \(W\).

<a id="examples"></a>
## Matching order of growth

Let \(\alpha\) be the real root greater than one of \(X^D-X-1\) with \(D>2W\).
These units have degree \(D\) and measures tending to a positive limit by the [trinomial calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v191.md#statement), so \(D\) can be taken arbitrarily large to satisfy (1).
For integers \(0\le b\le\min(\lfloor W/2\rfloor,\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor)\) and \(0\le a\le\lfloor W/2\rfloor\), the polynomial \(X^a(1+X)^b\) has degree at most \(W\), coefficient length \(2^b\) and value \(\alpha^{a+Db}\).
These exponents are distinct since \(D>2W\).
The product of the two list sizes is at least \(c W\min(W,\log(2L))\), with \(W=1\) also covered after decreasing \(c\).
This supplies the asserted lower bound within algebraic units for the very same coefficient and width limits.
