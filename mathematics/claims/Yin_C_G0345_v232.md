# A gap for individual factors of five-term polynomials away from reciprocal inputs

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed coefficient height and arbitrary exponents

Fix \(H\ge1\).
Consider integer polynomials of five nonzero terms, after removing content and a monomial, with primitive coefficient height at most \(H\).
Write
\[
 P=A(X)+cX^n+X^D B(X^{-1}),\qquad
 A=A_0+A_1X^a,\quad B=B_0+B_1X^b,\qquad 0<a<n<D-b<D .
\]
There is a constant \(c_H>0\) such that every positive-measure integral divisor of \(P\) has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(c_H\), with only the following families excluded:

* the reciprocal inputs \(D=2n,\ A=B\);
* the [product in which the middle-power quadratics have a common nonmonomial root](Yin_C_G0345_v230.md#statement) and its reversal;
* the [product from an identically equal pair of binomial and trinomial norms](Yin_C_G0345_v231.md#splits) and its reversal.

In both product families every nontorsion unit factor divides a tetranomial reciprocal up to sign and of coefficient height bounded in terms of \(H\), after rational scalar and monomial normalization.
There is no bound on the number of exterior conjugates in the theorem.
Together with the [four-term alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#statement), it reduces the gap problem for individual factors of integral polynomials with at most five terms and bounded coefficient height to bounded-height four-term polynomials reciprocal up to sign and bounded-height reciprocal five-term polynomials.
The reduction concerns each fixed coefficient bound, allowing the bound to change with normalization.

For example, for every \(0<a<n<D-b<D\), all positive-measure divisors of
\[
                    1+2X^a-8X^n+3X^{D-b}+X^D
\]
have an absolute positive gap.
The two end binomials differ.
The first product family has unequal absolute endpoint coefficients, with ratio \(|z|^{\pm1}\ne1\) in the displayed parametrization, whereas both endpoints here are one.
The second would require the middle coefficient to be minus an endpoint coefficient in one of the orientations.
The example is monic with constant one and has a real root greater than one since its value at one is \(-1\).

<a id="gaps"></a>
## The two long gaps in a hypothetical sequence

If the asserted bound failed, taking a positive-measure irreducible factor would give a sequence of such factors \(S\) with logarithmic measures \(m>0\) tending to zero.
An integer content greater than one already contributes at least \(\log2\).
After passing to a subsequence the five primitive coefficients are fixed.
All constants below may depend on these coefficients, for which there are only finitely many choices.
For large indices \(S\) is, up to sign, a monic reciprocal unit polynomial of degree \(d\).
Put \(t=\max_{S(x)=0}\log|x|>0,\ w=\max(a,b)\), and \(\Delta=\min(n,D-n)\).
The [two end and radial bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer) and the [two binomial-trinomial splits](Yin_C_G0345_v231.md#splits) give
\[
          wt=O(1),\qquad dtm\ge1/20,\qquad \Delta m/d\gg1 .               \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(wm/d\ll m^2\) by the first two bounds and the two exceptional trace identities have been excluded.
In particular \(\Delta t\gg1/m^2\), \(w=o(\Delta)\), and \(d/\Delta=O(m)\).
For every exterior root \(x\) of \(S\) write \(u=\log|x|\).
Both equations for \(x\) and \(x^{-1}\) give the uniform estimates
\[
       |x^{-a}-\lambda|+|x^{-b}-\mu|\ \ll\ e^{-\Delta u},\qquad
            \lambda=-A_0/A_1,\quad\mu=-B_0/B_1.                         \tag{2}
\]
This is the [two-orientation endpoint calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v231.md#endpoint), using \(wu\le wt=O(1)\).

Suppose \(|\mu|\ge1\).
The upper end block \(U=X^bB(X^{-1})\) has all its zeros in the closed unit disk.
In \(P=X^{D-b}U+(A+cX^n)\) the actual separating gap is \(G=D-b-n\ge\Delta-b\).
Thus \(G/\Delta\) is bounded below and \(b=o(G)\).
The [radial segment argument for a separated block](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#application) gives \(d\gg G\) directly at this actual gap.
Specifically, its exponent \(j_0=\lfloor(G-5b)/2\rfloor\) satisfies \(j_0+3b\asymp G\), and \((j_0+3b/2)/(j_0+3b)\ge1/2\) eventually.
Inside the containing region, the union of the radial segments has log capacity at most
\[
           \frac{\log T}{j_0+3b}-
              \frac{1}{768s},\qquad s\le 2d/k,\quad T=2L_0^{10k},
\]
by the [capacity deficit](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#geometry).
Here \(L_0\) bounds the coefficient lengths and \(k\) is the dyadic power used in the [irreducible segment construction](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#dyadic).
Their capacity is at least one, hence \(d\gg G/\log(2L_0)\).
This contradicts \(d/\Delta=O(m)\).
If \(|\lambda|\ge1\) apply the same argument to the reversal, with block width \(a\) and actual gap \(n-a\).
We can consequently assume \(0<|\lambda|,|\mu|<1\).

For a root of largest modulus, (2) and \(\Delta t\to\infty\) give
\[
                at\longrightarrow-\log|\lambda|,\qquad
                bt\longrightarrow-\log|\mu| .
\]
In particular \(a\asymp b\), \(t\asymp1/b\), \(b\to\infty\) since \(t\le m\), and \(\Delta/b\to\infty\).
Let \(f=\gcd(a,b)\).
When \(\lambda^{b/f}=\mu^{a/f}\), the two end binomials have a common nontorsion root by the [rational root extraction](Yin_C_G0345_v231.md#common), since these two rational numbers \(\lambda,\mu\) have moduli strictly less than one.
At fixed height the gap proved there already excludes this sequence.
It remains to consider \(\lambda^{b/f}\ne\mu^{a/f}\).

<a id="mesh"></a>
## Counting exterior roots near both sets of binomial roots

Choose a sufficiently large fixed constant \(C_0\).
Every exterior root with \(\Delta u>C_0\) satisfies \(c_1/b\le u\le c_2/b\) for fixed positive \(c_1,c_2\).
Indeed (2) places \(e^{-bu}\) between \(|\mu|/2\) and \((1+|\mu|)/2\).
For such a root, (2) also gives
\[
                    |x^b-\mu^{-1}|+|x^{-a}-\lambda|
                                  \ll E,\qquad E=e^{-c_1\Delta/b}.
\]
Inverting the first power in (2) costs only a fixed constant on the indicated compact interval of moduli.
There is a root \(\xi\) of \(X^b=\mu^{-1}\) within \(O(E/b)\) of \(x\).
Indeed \(x^b\mu=1+O(E)\), so taking the logarithm in a neighborhood of one and dividing it by \(b\) gives \(x/\xi=1+O(E/b)\) for a suitable \(b\)-th root.
Since \(a/b\) is bounded, \(|\xi^{-a}-\lambda|\ll E\) as well.

Each center \(\xi\) can account for at most one such root.
To prove this choose a fixed \(\kappa>0\) small enough that the disks of radius \(\kappa/b\) at these centers are disjoint and have \(\log|X|\ge c_3/b\) on their closures for some \(c_3>0\).
This is possible since the center modulus is \(\exp(-\log|\mu|/b)\) and adjacent centers have spacing asymptotic to \(2\pi/b\).
On every boundary \(|B(X^{-1})|\) is bounded below by a fixed positive constant.
Indeed write \(X/\xi=1+v/b\), where \(|v|=\kappa/|\xi|\to\kappa\).
Then \((1+v/b)^b\) tends uniformly to \(e^v\) for bounded \(v\), and \(|e^v-1|\) is bounded away from zero on the annulus \(\kappa/2\le|v|\le2\kappa\) when \(2\kappa<2\pi\).
This gives the claimed lower bound by \(B(\xi^{-1})=0\) and the fixed nonzero coefficients.
On each boundary
\[
                    \frac{|A(X)+cX^n|}{|X^D B(X^{-1})|}
                                  \ll |X|^{-(D-n)}=o(1)
\]
uniformly because \((D-n)/b\ge\Delta/b\to\infty\).
Rouché's theorem now gives exactly one root of \(P\) in the disk, counted with multiplicity.

Among all \(b\) centers, the numbers \(\xi^{-a}\) form \(b/f\) equally spaced points on a circle, each repeated \(f\) times.
Their common modulus \(|\mu|^{a/b}\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero.
The condition \(|\xi^{-a}-\lambda|\ll E\) restricts their angles to an interval modulo \(2\pi\) of length \(O(E)\), whenever there is a point satisfying it.
This follows from the chord length bound on a circle of radius bounded away from zero, taking any one admissible point as a reference.
The number of exterior roots satisfying \(\Delta u>C_0\) is therefore
\[
                                      O(f+bE).                           \tag{3}
\]

<a id="mass"></a>
## The full quadratic sum and the signed power comparison

The exterior roots with \(\Delta u\le C_0\) contribute at most \(C_0m/\Delta\) to \(\sum_{u>0}u^2\).
Every other one contributes \(O(1/b^2)\).
By reciprocity and the [quadratic floor in the original degree](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement),
\[
               \frac1{20}\le d\sum_{u>0}u^2
                  \ \ll\ \frac{dm}{\Delta}
                          +\frac{df}{b^2}+\frac db e^{-c_1\Delta/b}.
\]
The first term is \(O(m^2)\) by (1).
The last is \(O(m(\Delta/b)e^{-c_1\Delta/b})=o(1)\).
Consequently \(df/b^2\gg1\).

At a root of largest modulus set \(\beta=x^{-1}\).
The distinct rationals \(\lambda^{b/f},\mu^{a/f}\) differ by at least \(H^{-(a+b)/f}\), since the reduced denominators of \(\lambda,\mu\) are at most \(H\).
Both \(\beta^a,\beta^b\) lie in the open unit disk, as do \(\lambda,\mu\).
Applying the difference-of-powers formula to (2) compares both signed rational powers with \(\beta^{ab/f}\), giving
\[
            H^{-(a+b)/f}
                    \ \le\ |\lambda^{b/f}-\mu^{a/f}|
                    \ \ll\ ((a+b)/f)e^{-\Delta t}.
\]
It follows that \(\Delta t\ll_H b/f\) because \(a\asymp b\) and \(b/f\ge1\).
Since \(bt\) is bounded below, \(\Delta/b\ll_H b/f\).
The preceding lower bound for \(df/b^2\) now gives \(d/\Delta\gg_H1\), contradicting (1).

The exclusions and the statement about the factors of the two product families follow from their displayed factorizations.
All remaining cases of at most four terms use the [four-term alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#statement) and the [trinomial gap](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement).
This proves the claimed reduction at bounded height.
