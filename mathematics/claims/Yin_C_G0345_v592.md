# Lifting nondegenerate exponent arrays to a rational function field

<a id="statement"></a>
## Labeled arrays with one exponent fixed

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit and fix \(k\ge2\).
For fixed nonzero complex coefficients \(a_i\), count the labeled integer arrays \((t_1,\ldots,t_{k-1},0)\) for which \(\sum_i a_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) with no nonempty proper zero subsum.
Write \(P_k=\prod_{n=2}^k\binom n2=k!(k-1)!/2^{k-1}\).
For every positive integer \(Q\) with \(\log M(\alpha^Q)>(k/2)\log k\), the count is at most \(Q^{k-1}P_k\).
There is also a bound when the last coefficient varies over nonzero rational integers \(c\) of absolute value at most \(C\ge1\), while all other coefficients stay fixed.
For \(\log M(\alpha^Q)>(k/2)\log k+\log C\), the count in this case is at most \(Q^{k-1} k!(k-1)!/2\).
Each original exponent array determines its own output uniquely.
As in the [single-direction determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v590.md#statement), such a modulus exists and at strict degree records \(\log M(\alpha^Q)=Q\log M(\alpha)\).
These estimates do not require the stronger absence of intermediate single powers used in the terminal count.

<a id="lifting"></a>
## A common lift for each residue class

Choose any finite set of distinct solutions in one coordinatewise residue class, writing \(t_{ji}=s_i+Qu_{ji}\) with \(s_k=u_{jk}=0\).
Form the matrix over \(\mathbb Q[X,X^{-1}]\) whose ordinary columns have entries \(X^{u_{ji}}\).
For fixed coefficients the last column is one, while for varying output its entries are the actual signed nonzero integers \(c_j\).
At \(X=\gamma=\alpha^Q\) there is a common kernel vector with coordinates \(a_i\alpha^{s_i}\) in the ordinary columns and last coordinate respectively \(a_k\) or one.
Every nonzero minor of size \(n\) has circle modulus at most \(n^{n/2}\) by Hadamard when all its columns have monomial entries, or at most \(Cn^{n/2}\) if it includes the output column.
Its integral Laurent polynomial cannot vanish at \(\gamma\) by minimal-polynomial divisibility and the Mahler measure threshold, as in the [residue determinant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v590.md#determinant).
Thus the rank equals its specialization at \(\gamma\).
Select a maximal nonzero square minor, keep the free coordinates of the kernel vector equal to their actual complex values and solve the pivot equations over \(\mathbb C(X)\).
The pivot determinant is nonzero at \(\gamma\), so the resulting vector is regular there and specializes to the original vector.
All remaining rows vanish on it since the pivot rows span the row space over \(\mathbb C(X)\).
No lifted proper subsum can vanish identically, since its specialization would give a forbidden original subsum.
The coefficient functions are common to all rows in this finite set, including the common factor of the varying output column.

<a id="merge"></a>
## Counting by the maximal degree at infinity

For fixed coefficients in \(\mathbb C(X)^\times\), consider the resulting identities \(\sum_i b_i(X)X^{u_i}=0\) with \(u_k=0\).
At least two of the integers \(\deg b_i+u_i\) are maximal, since a unique maximum cannot cancel at infinity.
For any candidate pair this fixes the difference of its exponents.
Retain one index, always retaining the marked index if present, and absorb the other using this fixed difference into its coefficient.
At arity greater than two the new coefficient is nonzero by nondegeneracy, and every proper zero subsum after merging would expand to a forbidden original subsum.
The retained exponents and the chosen pair recover the omitted exponent.
At arity two the difference fixes the only unmarked exponent; iterating therefore gives at most \(P_k\) arrays.

For varying output, allow the marked coefficient to have the form \(f(X)c+g(X)\) with fixed rational functions, \(f\ne0\), where the parameter \(c\) may differ by row and is a complex scalar.
Only rows where the marked coefficient is nonzero are considered before merging.
The degree of this coefficient takes at most two values, since a leading coefficient cancellation between \(cf\) and \(g\) is possible at only one exceptional scalar if their degrees agree; the possible scalar zero also obeys this two-value bound by considering separately \(\deg f>\deg g\).
More explicitly the generic degree is \(\max(\deg f,\deg g)\), and its leading coefficient is a nonzero affine polynomial in \(c\), with at most one root; take \(\deg 0=-\infty\).
In the first step \(g=0\) and \(c\ne0\), so only one degree occurs.
An ordinary pair has one possible difference, and a pair involving the marked term has at most two.
Absorbing an ordinary term into the marked term adds a fixed rational function to \(g\), preserving the stated affine dependence.
Thus after the first merge at arity \(n\) there are at most \(\binom{n-1}2+2(n-1)=(n-1)(n+2)/2\) branches, also bounding the final two-term step.
In particular there are at most \(2\binom n2\) branches per subsequent arity, giving the stated \(2^{k-2}P_k\) bound per residue class.
The reconstruction counts exponent arrays even when several different parameter choices give the same reduced array.
The finite set was arbitrary, and there are at most \(Q^{k-1}\) residue classes.

<a id="example"></a>
## Factorial growth for arbitrary fixed coefficients

For the larger class of arbitrary complex coefficients, even sorted positive exponents can realize at least \((k-1)!\) distinct marked primitive shapes with a single full coefficient tuple for every \(k\ge3\).
Choose \(\alpha>1\) the larger root of \(x^2-Nx+1\) at an integer \(N\ge3\) so large that \(\log M(\alpha)>(k/2)\log k\).
Take ordinary coefficients \(a_i=\alpha^{-ki}\) for \(1\le i<k\), and marked coefficient \(-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\alpha^j\).
Every permutation \(\pi\) of \(1,\ldots,k-1\) gives the strictly increasing exponents \(t_i=ki+\pi(i)\).
Their terms are positive and add up to the absolute value of the unique negative term, so every proper nonempty subsum is nonzero.
The sum of the ordinary exponents is a fixed positive integer, so two different arrays cannot be positive dilations of one another.
This example uses algebraic outgoing coefficients of unrestricted height and concerns nondegenerate relations; it leaves open the exponential question for terminal rational ones.
