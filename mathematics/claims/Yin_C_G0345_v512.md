# Prime divisors of the least row integer across varying partners

<a id="statement"></a>
## Removing prescribed sets of primes at each exponent

For each integer \(L\ge2\), let \(\mathcal P\) and \(\mathcal Q\) be computable sets of primes in \([L,5L/4]\) and \([3L/2,2L]\), respectively, of sizes \(M,N\) tending to infinity.
To each \(p\in\mathcal P\) assign a computable finite set of rational primes \(E_p\), and to each \(q\in\mathcal Q\) another such set \(F_q\).
Allow their members to be arbitrarily large and suppose
\[
         I_L=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}|E_p|+\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}|F_q|=o(MN).
\]
For a positive integer \(B\), write \(B^\circ_{p,q}\) for the part of \(B\) supported outside \(E_p\cup F_q\), keeping all valuations there.

There exist a computable width \(b(L)\to\infty\) and computable sets of pairs of relative size tending to one such that the following holds on every retained pair for all sufficiently large \(L\).
Let \(U,V\) satisfy the [common two-prime test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width, \(B\) be the positive generator of \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\), \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), and \(\eta=\log H/(p+q)\).
When \(0<\eta\le10^{-3}\), let \(T_\eta>\mathrm e\) satisfy \(T_\eta/\log T_\eta=(4\eta)^{-1/3}\).
Then
\[
             \frac{\log B^\circ_{p,q}}{\log H}>
                        \frac{1-1/b(L)}{2}\exp(T_\eta).                 \tag{1}
\]
At these costs, for each choice of one qualifying seed at each exponent, some prime outside \(E_p\cup F_q\) divides \(B\) and gives a nonzero common root of the two powered minima and both rows, of residue degree at most the smaller seed degree.
Along a sequence of retained pairs of cost \(\eta\to0\), both \(\log B^\circ_{p,q}/\log H\) and \(\log B^\circ_{p,q}/(p+q)\) tend to infinity.
Such a sequence exists exactly when the unrestricted Lehmer infimum is zero, in which case one may prescribe any sequence of retained pairs with \(L\to\infty\).

In the two full prime intervals one may simultaneously remove \(p,q\) and **all** primes at most any computable \(X(L)=o(L)\), including the integer cutoff in the proof below.
For fixed seeds the same estimates also force a linear number of distinct witness primes as one varies a partner with the other exponent fixed.

<a id="one"></a>
## The total valuation at one prime as one exponent varies

Fix nontorsion reciprocal unit minima \(P_1,P_2\), allowing equality, and a common Galois splitting field \(K\) of degree \(D\).
Write \(a_i,c_j\) for their roots.
For sufficiently large \(L\) the [powered lists](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#gcd) are full and disjoint, so
\[
           C(p,q)=\prod_{i,j}(a_i^p-c_j^q)
\]
is a nonzero rational integer.
For every rational prime \(\ell\), uniformly in \(p,q,\ell,L\), one has
\[
 \sum_{q'\in\mathcal Q}v_\ell(C(p,q'))\log\ell\ll_{P_1,P_2} L,\qquad
 \sum_{p'\in\mathcal P}v_\ell(C(p',q))\log\ell\ll_{P_1,P_2} L .          \tag{2}
\]
Here \(K\) can be fixed as the splitting field of the two polynomials.

To prove the first estimate fix \(a=a_i,\ c=c_j\), a place \(\mathfrak l\mid\ell\) with integral valuation \(v\), ramification index \(e\), and weight \(W=\log N\mathfrak l/D\).
Let \(t\) be the order of \(c\) in the multiplicative group of its residue field.
For each positive integer \(n\),
\[
              Wv(c^{tn}-1)\le A_c t+eWv_\ell(n)                         \tag{3}
\]
with \(A_c\) independent of the place.
Indeed, put \(u=c^t\) and choose an \(\ell\)-power \(m\) with \(v(u^m-1)>e/(\ell-1)\).
Starting with \(v(u-1)\ge1\), each \(\ell\)-th powering increases the valuation by at least one by binomial expansion, so such \(m\) exists.
Take \(m=1\) for \(\ell>D+1\).
There are only finitely many remaining places, so \(m\) is bounded uniformly for this \(c\).
Above the indicated threshold the linear term has strictly least valuation on \(\ell\)-th powering, and a power coprime to \(\ell\) preserves positive valuation.
Thus \(v(u^n-1)\le v(u^{mn}-1)=v(u^m-1)+e v_\ell(n)\).
The ordinary norm bound for the nonzero algebraic integer \(c^{tm}-1\) gives \(Wv(u^m-1)\le tm h_{\rm abs}(c)+\log 2\), where \(h_{\rm abs}\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
This proves (3), including at the places above two.

If all the local valuations in the first sum vanish, their sum is zero.
Otherwise choose \(q_0\) maximizing \(v(a^p-c^{q_0})\) and having positive valuation.
Its weighted valuation is at most \(p h_{\rm abs}(a)+q_0 h_{\rm abs}(c)+\log 2\) by the same integral norm comparison.
Every other \(q'\) with positive valuation satisfies \(q'-q_0=\pm tn\) for \(1\le n\le L/t\).
The ultrametric inequality and maximality give
\[
          v(a^p-c^{q'})\le v(c^{q'}-c^{q_0})=v(c^{tn}-1).
\]
Each positive \(n\) occurs at most twice.
The \(A_ct\) terms in (3) therefore cost at most \(2A_cL\).
The remaining sum is bounded by \(2eW\sum_{n\le L/t}v_\ell(n)\le 2 (L/t)\log\ell/(\ell-1)\), using \(eW\le\log\ell\) and the sum over multiples of \(\ell^j\).
Together with the maximal term this gives \(O_{a,c,K}(L)\).
Summing over the finitely many labels and the at most \(D\) places above \(\ell\) recovers rational valuations, since \(\sum_{\mathfrak l\mid\ell}eW=\log\ell\).
Interchanging the labels and the variable exponents proves the second inequality.

## The loss from the prescribed prime sets and the width

Let \(B_0(p,q)\) be the [integer divisor forced by these two seeds](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local).
It divides \(|C(p,q)|^3\), and divides \(B\) whenever the corresponding contacts occur.
Summing (2) over each prescribed set gives
\[
   \sum_{p,q}\big(\log B_0(p,q)-\log (B_0(p,q))^\circ_{p,q}\big)
                         \ll_{P_1,P_2} L I_L .                           \tag{4}
\]
In particular, for each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the fraction of pairs with loss exceeding \(\epsilon L\) is \(O_{P_1,P_2}(I_L/(\epsilon MN))=o(1)\).

For fixed seeds, the [weighted difference estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#size) gives \(\log B_0\ge G/(2k)-o_{P_1,P_2}(L)\), with \(G/(2k)\gg_{P_1,P_2}L\) by the [crossed degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#width).
Here \(k\) is the smaller size of a class of conjugates whose pairwise quotients are roots of unity, and \(G\) is the weighted sum defined in the cited estimate.
For each fixed integer \(j\ge2\), (4) implies \(((B_0)^\circ_{p,q})^{2kj}\ge\exp(G)^{j-1}\) on a fraction tending to one.
Impose this exact inequality and full disjoint powered lists for every ordered pair in the finite list of reciprocal nontorsion unit minima of degree at most \(j\) and Mahler measure at most \(1091/1000\).
All comparisons are computable by the [finite algebraic tests](Yin_C_G0345_v508.md#width).
Take the largest passing \(2\le j\le L\) for which at least \(1-1/j\) of the rectangle meets these requirements, using \(b=1\) and the empty set if none passes or the rectangle is empty.
Each fixed \(j\) eventually passes, so this gives the stated width and density.
The [contact and compressed height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v508.md#rows) now applies to each choice of qualifying seeds, giving (1) already for their retained \(B_0\), and a common nonzero residue at every prime dividing that retained integer.
Its proof of the limiting assertions and the [common-prime limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) apply to any prescribed retained pair sequence.

For the full intervals \(M,N\asymp L/\log L\) by the [prime count at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz).
Any \(X=o(L)\) has \(\pi(X)=o(L/\log L)\) by the same estimate, separating \(X\le\sqrt L\) if needed.
One may put all primes up to \(X\) in each \(E_p\), add \(p\) there and take \(F_q=\{q\}\).
For an example using integer operations only, take \(X=\lfloor L/(1+\lfloor\log_2(1+\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor)\rfloor)\rfloor\).

## Distinct primes for different partners

For fixed \(P_1,P_2\), there is a positive integer \(J=J(P_1,P_2)\) with the following property for every sufficiently large \(L\) and every \(p\in\mathcal P\).
To each \(q\in\mathcal Q\) one can assign a prime divisor of \(B_0(p,q)\) so that each prime is used at most \(J\) times.
In particular this assignment gives at least \(|\mathcal Q|/J\) distinct primes using a single witness for each pair.
The same statement holds with the exponents interchanged.

Indeed, \(\log B_0(p,q)\ge cL\) for every pair, for some \(c>0\) depending on the seeds.
For each prime \(\ell\), the sum of its logarithmic contributions over any subset of partners is at most \(C_1L\) by (2) and the cubic upper multiple for \(B_0\).
Thus the union of supports of \(B_0(p,q)\) for any subset of \(n\) partners contains at least \(cn/C_1\) primes.
Give each prime \(\lceil C_1/c\rceil\) copies.
Every subset of partners then has at least its cardinality in neighboring copies, so the finite marriage theorem gives the assignment.
For actual contacts the [residue calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v508.md#rows) applies to each assigned prime.
The assignment bounds reuse across partners and gives no growing count of primes for an individual pair.
