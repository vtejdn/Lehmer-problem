# A relative cubic height bound from ramification and Frobenius classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary heights and unbounded base-field degree

Let \(F/\mathbb Q\) be finite Galois and let \(\alpha\) be any nonzero algebraic number of infinite multiplicative order.
Write \(D=[F(\alpha):F]\) and let \(h(\alpha)\) be the absolute logarithmic Weil height, including its finite-place contributions.
At an odd prime \(p\), choose a place \(v\) of \(F\) over \(p\), write \(e_p\) for its ramification index, and choose an element \(\sigma_p\) of its decomposition group inducing \(x\mapsto x^p\) on the residue field.
Write \(b_p\) for the size of the conjugacy class of this lift in \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\).
Suppose the choices can be made with \(e_pb_p\le H\) at all but at most \(u\) odd rational primes, where \(H\ge1,\ u\ge0\).
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
 D h(\alpha)\ \ge\
 \frac{c}{H\left(\sqrt{u+1}
            + H\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\right)^3}.                       \tag{1}
\]
The base degree, the total Mahler measure of \(\alpha\), and the number of finite places where \(\alpha\) is nonintegral are unrestricted.

For an abelian base take \(b_p=1\).
If it ramifies at at most \(u\) odd rational primes, (1) holds with \(H=1\) even if the ramification indices there are large.
In particular the cubic relative estimate
\[
 D h(\alpha)\gg \left(\frac{\log\log(5D)}{\log(2D)}\right)^3          \tag{2}
\]
holds with a constant depending only on \(C\) whenever \(u\le C(\log(2D)/\log\log(5D))^2\).
If instead \(F\) is any abelian extension of group exponent dividing \(b\), possibly infinite, the same cubic estimate holds with lower-bound constant \(c'/b^4\) for an absolute \(c'>0\).
This includes the compositum of all quadratic fields, uniformly over all input degrees and heights.
For an integral input with \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), multiplication by \([F:\mathbb Q]\) converts (1) into a lower bound on the original total logarithmic Mahler measure.
The [small-total-measure theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#statement) separately gives the cubic estimate over all abelian bases in its stated height range.

<a id="relative-compression"></a>
## Removing power collisions over the chosen field

Partition the \(D\) distinct \(F\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\) by the equivalence relation \(z_1/z_2\) a root of unity.
The relative Galois action is transitive, so every class has the same size \(l\).
Let \(\beta\) be the product of the class of \(\alpha\).
A class containing \(z\) has product \(z^l\) times a root of unity.
If the products of two classes had torsion quotient, then the representatives would themselves have torsion quotient.
Hence distinct classes have distinct products with nontorsion quotient.
The conjugates of \(\beta\) over \(F\) are exactly these products, and every positive power of \(\beta\) retains their distinctness.
In particular \(D'=[F(\beta):F]=D/l\), \(h(\beta)=l h(\alpha)\) and \(D'h(\beta)=D h(\alpha)\).
The height identity follows by summing the absolute local heights, since a root of unity has absolute value one at every place.
This is the [class-product construction](Yin_C_G0345_v714.md#scope) on relative conjugates.
It removes all degree-lowering primes without a count depending on the base field and without an integrality assumption.

<a id="local"></a>
## The local saving at a ramified place

Fix a finite set \(\mathcal P\) of odd primes and use the choices in the statement.
With \(t=|\mathcal P|\), \(T\ge1\) an integer and \(S=D'(T+t)\), one has
\[
 T\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac{\log p}{e_pb_p}
 \le \tfrac12(T^2+t)\log S+
              (T+t)\left(T+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p\right)D h(\alpha).  \tag{3}
\]
To prove (3), form the divided-derivative Vandermonde on the relative conjugates \(a_i\) of \(\beta\) with multiplicity \(T\), and at each \(p\) on the nodes \(b_{pi}=(\sigma_p^{-1}a_i)^p\) with multiplicity one, using any extension of the automorphism.
The previous paragraph gives distinct nodes within a given set, and their respective positive absolute heights distinguish different sets.
Their determinant is nonzero.

At the chosen place \(v\), scale the monic relative minimal polynomial \(f\) of \(\beta\) to \(g=c_v f\) of Gauss norm one, taking \(c_v\) to be the inverse of a coefficient of largest local absolute value.
Then \(\sigma_p^{-1}g\) has the same Gauss norm and the coefficients of \(g(X^p)-(\sigma_p^{-1}g(X))^p\) belong to the maximal ideal.
This follows by the residue action and the characteristic-\(p\) binomial identity.
With \(|p|_v=p^{-1}\), their absolute values are at most \(p^{-1/e_p}\).
At \(z_i=\sigma_p^{-1}a_i\) the second term vanishes, so
\[
          |g(b_{pi})|_v
                  \le p^{-1/e_p}\max(1,|b_{pi}|_v)^{D'} .
\]
Gauss norm multiplicativity gives
\(|c_v|_v\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_v)=1\).
Multiplying the evaluations therefore gives the extra factor \(p^{-D'/e_p}\) for the cross differences between the original and the \(p\)-powered sets, relative to the usual products of the local maxima.
Each difference in this cross term occurs to power \(T\).
This accounts for possible denominators at the same place.

Every place obtained from \(v\) by an element commuting with \(\sigma_p\) has the same local estimate: this automorphism \(\sigma_p\) still induces its residue Frobenius.
If \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\) and \(C_G(\sigma_p)\) is its centralizer, at least \(|C_G(\sigma_p)|/|G|=1/b_p\) of the places over \(p\) occur in this orbit.
Indeed the map \(G\to\{w\mid p\}\) has equal fibers everywhere, and its restriction to the centralizer has fibers of at most that size.
All places of \(F\) over \(p\) have the same local degree, and in a common splitting field the local degrees above each add to its original normalized weight.
This gives the left side of (3) after dividing by \(D'\).
The other local bounds, the archimedean row norms and the sum of the normalized heights are as in the [relative determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#determinant).
Explicitly the archimedean factor from the row lengths is \(S^{D'(T^2+t)/2}\), and at each place the total exponent in the local maxima at a node of multiplicity \(m_z\) is bounded by \((S-1)m_z\).
The height contribution after division by \(D'\) is at most \(D'h(\beta)(T+t)(T+\sum p)\).
The product formula now gives (3).

<a id="consequences"></a>
## Prime ranges and abelian extensions of bounded exponent

Put
\[
 T=\left\lceil A\left(\sqrt{u+1}
                 +H\frac{\log(2D)}{\log\log(5D)}\right)\right\rceil,
                     \qquad X=T^2\log(2T).
\]
For a sufficiently large absolute \(A\), the [interval prime estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice) leaves \(\asymp T^2\) primes in \([X,2X]\) after deleting at most \(u\) exceptions.
Use these in (3).
Its left side is \(\gg T^3\log(2T)/H\), while its first right-hand term is \(O(T^2(\log(2D)+\log(2T)))\).
Because \(T\gg A H\) and \(T\log(2T)\gg A H\log(2D)\), the latter is at most half the left for sufficiently large \(A\).
The coefficient of \(D h(\alpha)\) is \(O(T^6\log(2T))\).
It follows that \(D h(\alpha)\gg1/(HT^3)\), which gives (1) and the stated dependence in (2).

For the bounded-exponent assertion first take \(F\) finite abelian.
At every odd \(p\nmid b\), its inertia group has order prime to \(p\), because the finite group's exponent divides \(b\).
This inertia is cyclic.
Indeed its action on a local uniformizer \(\pi\) gives the tame character \(g\mapsto g(\pi)/\pi\bmod\pi\) into the cyclic multiplicative group of the residue field.
The kernel is a \(p\)-group: the successive higher ramification quotients embed in the additive residue field via the first nonzero coefficient of \(g(\pi)-\pi\), and the filtration ends at the identity since inertia fixes the maximal unramified field and a uniformizer generates the remaining totally ramified extension.
Thus \(e_p\le b\).
All Frobenius lifts have class size one.
The primes dividing \(b\) contribute at most \(b\) exceptions, so in (1) one can take \(H=b,\ u\le b\).
This proves the \(c'/b^4\) estimate since \(\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\) is bounded away from zero.
For infinite abelian \(F\), the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(F\) is defined over a finite subextension, and irreducibility persists over that smaller field.
The subextension is abelian of exponent dividing \(b\) and has the same relative degree for \(\alpha\), giving the assertion.
