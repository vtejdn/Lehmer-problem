# Simultaneous signed relations on every coordinate line of a dense mask

<a id="statement"></a>
## Common binary fibers, support and matrix rank

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), with monic minimal polynomial \(P\).
Fix integers \(L\ge2\), \(J\ge1\), and a mask \(S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^J\).
A binary array vanishes outside \(S\) and takes values in \(\{0,1\}\) on \(S\).
On each coordinate line record \(\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}x_i\alpha^i\), where \(i\) is the varying coordinate and \(x_i\) denotes the array entry there.
Some family \(\mathcal F\) of arrays has the same values on every recorded line and satisfies
\[
 \log|\mathcal F|\ \ge\ |S|\log2-
    J L^{J-1}\bigl((L-1)m+d\log(8L)\bigr).                            \tag{1}
\]
Let \(w_L\) be the minimum number of nonzero coefficients of a nonzero ternary polynomial of degree less than \(L\) vanishing at \(\alpha\), with \(w_L=\infty\) if none exists.
Here ternary coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
Two distinct arrays in any common fiber differ in at least \(w_L^J\) entries.
Over the full grid the minimum distance among all distinct pairs with equal line values is exactly \(w_L^J\), with the same infinity convention.
Every common fiber also satisfies
\[
                    \log|\mathcal F|\le (L-d)_+^J\log2 ,
 \qquad (L-d)_+=\max(0,L-d).                                        \tag{2}
\]
Thus a common fiber of rate \(L^{-J}\log|\mathcal F|\ge c\), where \(0<c<\log2\), requires
\[
 L\ge\frac{d}{1-(c/\log2)^{1/J}}
        \ge\frac{Jd}{\log((\log2)/c)} .
\]

Suppose \(|S|\ge\delta L^J\), with \(0<\delta\le1\) and \(\kappa=\delta\log2-Jm>0\).
For \(J\ge2\) and all sufficiently large \(L\) there is a subfamily in a single fiber of size at least \(\exp(\kappa L^J/2)\) such that each pairwise difference has matrix rank at least
\[
                   \frac{\kappa}{16\log3}\,L^{\lfloor J/2\rfloor}       \tag{3}
\]
over \(\mathbb Q\) for every grouping of \(\lfloor J/2\rfloor\) coordinates as row indices and the remaining coordinates as column indices.
In particular any expression for such a difference as a sum of products of one-variable arrays requires at least the number of summands in (3).

For any nonzero difference \(u\) in one fiber, the single-variable polynomial
\[
        Q(X)=\sum_{\mathbf i}u_{\mathbf i}
                      X^{i_0+Li_1+\cdots+L^{J-1}i_{J-1}}
\]
is ternary and divisible by \(P(X^{L^j})\) for every \(0\le j<J\).
Each nonempty coordinate line of its signed support already gives an exact ternary relation at the original \(\alpha\).

If the Lehmer gap fails, take the [shrinking units with increasing minimum coefficient length](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#statement) and denote this length by \(\lambda(\alpha)\).
For any fixed \(\delta>0\) with \(\delta\le1\), take \(J=\lfloor\delta\log2/(2m)\rfloor\).
For all sufficiently late units, every sufficiently large side length for that unit gives a family of size at least \(\exp(\delta(\log2)L^J/4)\) on every mask of density at least \(\delta\).
Distinct members differ in at least \(\lambda(\alpha)^J\) entries and satisfy (3), with \(\kappa\ge\delta\log2/2\).
At the displayed positive rate, (2) requires the side length itself to be at least a constant depending on \(\delta\) times \(d/m\) along this sequence.
Bound (1) suffices when \(dJ\log(8L)/L\le\kappa/4\).
The flattened exponent span can be as large as \(L^J-1\), so this gives no fixed upper bound on its product with \(h(\alpha)\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting all coordinate-line values

At an embedding \(\sigma\), a binary line sum has modulus at most \(R_\sigma=L\max(1,|\sigma\alpha|)^{L-1}\).
Following the [integral subset-sum count](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#count), partition each real coordinate or the real and imaginary parts at a complex place into intervals of length \(1/2\).
Each coordinate requires at most \(8R_\sigma\) intervals.
Two sums in the same box differ by an algebraic integer of modulus less than one under every embedding, and its integer norm forces that difference to vanish.
The number of possible line sums is at most \((8L)^d\exp((L-1)m)\), counting each complex place twice.
Multiplying the bound for a single line over the \(J L^{J-1}\) lines bounds the number of joint records.
Pigeonholing the \(2^{|S|}\) arrays proves (1).
At density \(\delta\) the right side of (1) is at least \(3\kappa L^J/4\) once \(dJ\log(8L)/L\le\kappa/4\).

<a id="dimension"></a>
## A lower bound on the side length at positive rate

Put \(V=\mathbb Q^L\), and let \(U\) be the kernel of \((a_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i\alpha^i\) from \(V\) to \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The powers \(1,\ldots,\alpha^{\min(d,L)-1}\) are independent over \(\mathbb Q\), and every higher power belongs to the span of the first \(d\).
Hence \(\dim U=(L-d)_+\).
Identify rational arrays with \(V^{\otimes J}\).
For any given direction the zero-line equations require membership in the tensor product with \(U\) in that slot and \(V\) in the others, by expansion in the standard basis in the other slots.
The row and column description and the dimension and distance product rules also occur in the [finite-field tensor-code construction](../audits/literature/BV09/Yin_U001.md#bv09-u001).
In a basis of \(V\) extending a basis of \(U\), the intersection of these subspaces has precisely the basis tensors using \(U\) in every slot.
It has dimension \((L-d)_+^J\).
A common fiber lies in an affine translate of this rational space.
Projection to a set of \((L-d)_+^J\) independent coordinate positions is injective there, and a binary array offers at most two choices per position.
This proves (2), also with a restricted mask.
Rearrange (2) for \(c>0\) and use \(1-\exp(-x)\le x\) with \(x=J^{-1}\log((\log2)/c)\) to get the two side-length bounds.

<a id="distance"></a>
## Sharp signed distance and the flattened polynomial

Every nonzero line of a signed difference contributes at least \(w_L\) entries by definition.
For \(J\ge2\), take one nonzero line in the last direction.
At least \(w_L\) slices at fixed last coordinate are nonzero, and every line in the other \(J-1\) directions inside such a slice still has zero weighted sum.
Induction from \(J=1\) proves the distance bound.
If \(w_L=\infty\), already a single nonzero entry would give a prohibited nonzero line.
Otherwise choose a minimizing ternary word \(t\), and on the full grid take the signed array \(\prod_{j=0}^{J-1}t_{i_j}\).
The positive and negative parts give two binary arrays with equal line records and exactly \(w_L^J\) differences.
Every ternary weight is its coefficient length, whence \(w_L\ge\lambda(\alpha)\).

To check divisibility for a signed difference, fix \(j\) and group the flattened sum by all coordinates except \(i_j\).
Each resulting summand is a monomial times \(T(X^{L^j})\), with \(T\) an integer line polynomial satisfying \(T(\alpha)=0\).
Monicity of \(P\) gives \(P\mid T\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), including a zero line, so \(P(X^{L^j})\mid Q\).
Distinct grid positions have distinct exponents by uniqueness of base-\(L\) digits.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Selecting separated matrices in the same fiber

Set \(a=L^{\lfloor J/2\rfloor}\), \(b=L^{\lceil J/2\rceil}\), so \(a\le b\) and \(ab=L^J\).
For a fixed grouping of coordinates, the number of ternary \(a\)-by-\(b\) matrices of rank at most \(R\) is at most \(2^a3^{R(a+b)}\).
Indeed, at rank \(t\le R\) choose \(t\) basis rows and specify their \(tb\) entries.
Choose the first independent set of \(t\) pivot columns for these rows in lexicographic order, which requires no further data.
Every remaining row in the span is uniquely determined by its \(t\) entries in those columns.
Summing \(\binom at3^{tb+t(a-t)}\) over \(t\le R\) proves the bound.

With \(R=\lfloor\kappa a/(16\log3)\rfloor\), the number of ternary differences of rank at most \(R\) in at least one such grouping is at most \(2^{J+a}3^{R(a+b)}\).
Its logarithm is at most \(\kappa ab/4\) for all sufficiently large \(L\), since \(R(a+b)\log3\le\kappa ab/8\).
From the common fiber of size at least \(\exp(3\kappa ab/4)\), select one array and delete every array whose difference from it belongs to that set, including the selected array itself.
Each selection removes at most \(\exp(\kappa ab/4)\) arrays, giving the claimed subfamily with rank strictly greater than \(R\) on every grouping.
A product of one-variable arrays has rank at most one under each grouping.
This proves (3) and the lower bound for sums of such products.
