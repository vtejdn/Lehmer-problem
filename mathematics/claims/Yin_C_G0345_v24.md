# Sparse trace lifts need unbounded coefficient denominators at sublinear exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform gap when the denominators in an initial interval are bounded

Fix positive integers \(K,D\) and a real number \(\eta>0\).
Let \(\theta\) be a Pisot number whose monic minimal polynomial has positive integer constant \(q\ge2\), and let \(j\ge1\), \(h\) and \(\Pi\) be its branch index, trace degree and [trace numerator lift](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace).
If \(\Pi\) has at most \(K\) nonzero coefficients and each coefficient of \(\Pi/j\) through degree \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) has reduced denominator at most \(D\), then
\[
                              \theta-q\ \ge\ c(K,D,\eta)>0.
\]
The branch and the denominators of all later coefficients are unrestricted.
Zeros have denominator one.
Consequently on any sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\) and bounded term count, for every fixed \(D\) a coefficient of reduced denominator greater than \(D\) eventually exists and its least index is \(o(h)\).
Equivalently, along such a sequence one can choose an index \(n_i=o(h_i)\) at which the reduced denominators tend to infinity, by choosing increasing thresholds sufficiently slowly.

On every fixed branch \(j\), the term count must therefore tend to infinity on escape.
For each fixed \(j,K\) the positive constants \(q\) are bounded by the [branch derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
The same conclusion about term growth holds on any bounded set of branches.
The constants are qualitative.

<a id="moving"></a>
## Passing to a limiting numerator when the far coefficients vary

Suppose the conclusion fails and \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\).
Write \(F_i=\Pi_i/j_i\) and \(B_i=B_i^\sharp\) for the monic integral trace denominator, whose constant is one.
The [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives uniform coefficient and circle supremum bounds because \(M(F_i)=1\) and the support size is bounded.
The [inverse representation](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) gives \(B_i=F_iJ_i\), \(M(B_i)>1\) tending to one, \(h_i\to\infty\), and symmetric signed measures for \(J_i\) of mass one and negative mass tending to zero.
The [first nonintegral coefficient](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) has index \(d_i=o(h_i)\).
Thus \(D=1\) is already impossible.
We may shrink \(\eta\) to be at most \(1/2\).

Fix the ordered support size and pass to a limit of its bounded real coefficient tuple.
On the prescribed initial interval the choices form a finite rational set.
Pass to a subsequence fixing interval membership and every coefficient in it, and let \(m\) be the least common denominator of those coefficients, a divisor of \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D)\).
One has \(m\ge2\).
Stabilize all persistent relations on the ordered exponents as in the [face construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#face).
Let \(F_\infty\) be the fixed Laurent polynomial on the resulting free lattice with the limiting coefficients, retaining the constant one even if some other limiting coefficients are zero.
We claim that \(M(F_\infty)=1\).

Let \(G_i\) be \(F_\infty\) evaluated at the original indexed exponents.
Then \(\|G_i-F_i\|_\infty\to0\) by the finite term count, and both polynomials have circle supremum at least one by the constant term.
For either \(R_i=G_i\) or \(F_i\), the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) on one whole period gives
\[
      \mu\{|R_i|\le t\}\ \le\ A_0\, t^{1/(K-1)}\qquad(0<t\le1)
\]
with normalized angular measure \(\mu\), as its frequencies are distinct and purely imaginary in the exponential-polynomial parameter.
We can assume \(K\ge2\) since \(\Pi_i\) has two nonzero endpoints, and a monomial with constant one would have zero small-value measure for \(t<1\).
For \(0<\delta<1\), replacing \(\log|R_i|\) by \(\log\max(\delta,|R_i|)\) costs at most \(A_0(K-1)\delta^{1/(K-1)}\) in mean, by integrating the sublevel bound against \(dt/t\).
The two regularized means differ by at most \(\delta^{-1}\|G_i-F_i\|_\infty\).
Letting \(i\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) shows that \(\log M(G_i)-\log M(F_i)\to0\).
The [Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) on the stabilized lattice applies to the fixed polynomial \(F_\infty\) and gives the claim.

<a id="faces"></a>
## A fixed integer face at each sublinear scale

Separate the lower exponents into finitely many scales from the first nonintegral index \(d_i\) up to \(h_i\), by taking subsequential limits of all pairwise ratios.
Use \(d_i\) and \(h_i\) themselves for the first and last representatives.
Adjacent representatives \(s,S\) have \(s/S\to0\).
For each such pair form the integer polynomial \(A_i\) by multiplying by \(m\) the terms of \(F_i\) at all exponents \(O(s)\), including the constant.
All these exponents are \(o(h_i)\) and their rational coefficients were fixed in the preceding step.
Every omitted exponent is at least a fixed positive fraction of \(S\).
The [separating functional for an initial group](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#intermediate) exposes these terms in the full lattice.
Its rational cone argument uses the positions of terms alone and also works with zero limiting coefficients outside the selected face.
Jensen along this functional bounds the Mahler measure of the normalized face by \(M(F_\infty)=1\).
A sufficiently late evaluation exposes its constant one as a vertex, giving the reverse bound.
Lawton applied to the face on its own lattice now gives \(M(A_i)\to m\).

A product \(U_i\) of a bounded number of cyclotomic binomials of degree \(O(s)\) satisfies \([z^t]U_i B_i=0\) on \(Ls\le t\le S/L\) after extraction, for some fixed \(L\).
To check the [intermediate-row argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#rows) with varying branch and tail, take the primitive part \(P\) of the fixed lattice face of \(A_i\).
Its positive constant \(m'\) is at least two because the coefficient of \(F_i\) at \(d_i\) is nonintegral and belongs to the face.
It has \(M(P)=m'\).
The tests of \(B_i\) in that argument use only the coefficients of \(P_i/m'=A_i/m=F_i\) within the common prefix at \(O(s)\) and at \(kt+O(s)\), for fixed \(k>0\) and \(s/t,t/S\to0\).
They therefore give the same integer transforms using the actual signed measures for \(J_i\).
The [real-quotient division](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#division) removes the graph densities, and the last-harmonic bound and [torsion multipliers](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v105.md#multiplier) give the vanishing interval.

<a id="propagation"></a>
## A bounded-term product at the full degree

At the initial scale \(s=d_i\), use the [integral-prefix estimate on every interval](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial).
It gives integer polynomials \(P_i\), equal to one or to one signed binomial with constant one, of degree \(O(s)\), for which the total absolute coefficient sum of \(P_i B_i\) is bounded on every interval of length \(s\), uniformly in its location.
Indeed the coefficients of \(J_i\) below \(d_i\) are integral by formal division.
The [integer-prefix models](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low) and the Bessel estimate on the finitely many pieces cut out by the whole support only require the actual signed measure, integrality on this initial interval and a bounded sum of magnitudes of the full normalized numerator coefficients.
All hold here, including for the late real limits before they are attained.

Suppose this all-interval estimate holds at \(s\) with \(\deg P_i=O(s)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum for \(P_i\).
Suppress the index \(i\) and put \(W=PU,\ C=WB,\ V=WA\) at the next scale \(S\).
Then \(C\) has bounded absolute sum through \(S/L\): use the translated all-interval estimate up to \(Ls+\deg P\) and the intermediate zero interval after that.
On the prefix below the first omitted offset one has \(C=(V/m)J\) exactly.
Writing \(V^*(z)=V(z^{-1})\), the two-sided moments of \(2|V|^2\omega\) for \(|k|\le S/(4L)\) are the coefficients of
\[
                          m(V^* C+ V C^*) .
\]
This uses \(J+J^*=2\widehat\omega\) with constant two and only coefficients through \(S/L\), by \(\deg V=O(s)\) and enlargement of \(L\).
These moments have bounded absolute sum on the stated interval, giving a uniform Gram matrix bound for the characters weighted by \(V\) on blocks of this diameter.

Off the finitely many intervals \([e,e+\deg V]\) adjacent to offsets \(e\) of the whole numerator, each coefficient of \(VB\) is the doubled signed moment of its weighted character against the fixed-on-the-piece partial sum of the active terms of \(F_i\).
These partial sums need only have bounded circle supremum, supplied by the term and coefficient bounds even for the later coefficients with varying denominators.
On each fixed number of tested indices the change to the positive part of the measure is \(o(1)\) in the Gram matrix and in the moments.
Thus the [block Bessel estimate and transition calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#gram) bound the number of nonzero integer coefficients on each block.
In a transition interval, \(VB=(UA)(PB)\), so the all-interval bound already proved at \(s\) covers its length \(O(s)\) after boundedly many shifts.
The coefficients themselves are bounded, giving the all-interval estimate at \(S\) with the integer multiplier \(V\).
In particular the integrality tested here is that of the original \(VB\), independently of denominators in the tail of \(F_i\).

There are finitely many scale steps, say \(b\) after extraction.
At \(h_i\) a bounded number of intervals covers the whole final product \(P_i B_i\), whose degree is \(O(h_i)\).
It has bounded term count and integer coefficients.
Each step multiplies the constant by \(m\), and multiplies the measure by \(M(A_i)\to m\) since the other factors are cyclotomic binomials.
The initial multiplier has constant and measure one.
Thus \(P_i B_i\) has constant \(m^b\) and measure tending to \(m^b\) strictly from above, using \(M(A_i)\ge m,\ M(B_i)>1\).
The [sparse vertex gap](Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement) forbids this sequence.
