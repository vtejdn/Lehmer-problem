# Reciprocal radii and the discriminant in the fixed-coefficient count

<a id="statement"></a>
## Conditions for retaining the weighted count

Consider reciprocal nontorsion algebraic units of degree \(d\) and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\to0\).
Write \(P\) for the monic minimum, \(\Delta=|\operatorname{disc}(P)|\), \(H=\max_\beta\log|\beta|\), \(\delta=\min_\beta|\log|\beta||\), \(L=\log(1/m)\), and
\[
 \Lambda=1+\log^+(d/\Delta^{1/d}),\qquad
 T=\lceil128d\Lambda\rceil,\qquad N=\lfloor T\log(2)/(4m)\rfloor,
 \qquad U=N+\log(T+1)/H.
\]
Here \(\log^+x=\max(0,\log x)\).
Put \(R_0=(L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\).
If \(N\delta=o(L)\) and \(U\delta=o(LR_0)\), then for each fixed \(\lambda\ge0\) there are \(r\gg R_0\) tending to infinity and one prescribed tuple \(\mathbf b\) of \(r\) nonzero rational integers, including the output at exponent zero, such that the number \(W\) of distinct sorted marked lists satisfies
\[
                \log W-\lambda\log H_2(\mathbf b)
                      \ge (1-o(1))(r-2)L.
\]
Here \(H_2\) is the [product over all but the two largest coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement), and terminality tests all rational multiples of all integer powers.
One may take \(r\le T+1\), marked exponents of magnitude at most \(2U\), and coefficients of magnitude at most \((T+1)e^{N\delta/2}\).
If \(U\delta=O(1)\), one obtains \(W\ge H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda(c_\lambda/m)^{r-2}\), with \(c_\lambda>0\) depending also on the fixed bound.
Under either set of hypotheses the displayed logarithmic lower bound also holds after division by the exponent gcd when each positive power preserves degree or there is a real conjugate.

In particular \(\delta=O(m/(d\log(2d)))\) suffices for the constant-base conclusion with no discriminant assumption.
Alternatively \(\Lambda=o(L)\) and \(m\log(2d)=o(LR_0)\) suffice for the logarithmic conclusion with no extra hypothesis on the closest conjugate.
The latter test includes \(\Delta^{1/d}\gg d\) and \(m\log(2d)=O(1)\).
Both criteria concern the original full rational splitting rule on these inputs.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## A subset-sum interval using the power-basis lattice

Every set of \(s=T+1\) distinct exponents in \([0,N)\) supports a nonzero ternary relation when \(m\) is sufficiently small.
To see this, sum the corresponding powers with independent uniform binary coefficients.
In the integral power basis \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}\) the resulting vector \(Z\) is integral.
If all subset sums were different, adding independent uniform noise on the centered unit cube in these coordinates would give differential entropy \(s\log2\), by disjoint cube interiors.
Map the new vector to all real embeddings and to the real and imaginary parts of one embedding from each complex pair.
The absolute determinant of this real linear map is \(2^{-r_2}\sqrt{\Delta}\), where \(r_2\) is the number of complex pairs, by the Vandermonde determinant and the real and imaginary decomposition of each pair.
Entropy increases by the logarithm of this determinant by change of variables.

At an embedding \(\beta\), each of the real coordinates has variance at most \((s/4+d/12)\max(1,|\beta|)^{2N}\), adding independent bit and cube-coordinate variances, since \(N\ge d\) eventually.
A vector with these marginal variances has entropy at most the sum of \(\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,\mathrm{Var})\) over its coordinates.
This follows from nonnegativity of relative entropy against the product of normals with matching means and variances. [Gaussian entropy comparison](Yin_C_G0321_v1.md#argument)
Counting each complex pair twice would therefore give
\[
 s\log2\le Nm+\tfrac d2\log(2\pi e(s/4+d/12))+r_2\log2-\tfrac12\log\Delta .
\]
Since \(s\le130d\Lambda,\ r_2\le d/2\), the three terms after \(Nm\) are at most \(\tfrac d2(\log(4\pi e\cdot130)+\log\Lambda+\Lambda-1)\le 6d\Lambda\).
Together with \(Nm\le T\log(2)/4\) this contradicts \(s=T+1\).
The [random deletion estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v290.md#shapes) now gives some \(3\le j\le T+1\) with at least \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\) minimal ternary supports up to translation.
Here \(N\ge2T\) and \(N/(4T)\ge\log(2)/(32m)\) eventually.

<a id="radii"></a>
## Two integer labels for each cut

Take a complete tree of rational splits for each such support, retaining original coordinates in \([0,N)\) for the leaves.
Every actual edge value \(c\alpha^n\) is up to sign a sum of signed descendant leaves, and its nonzero rational coefficient \(c\) is integral. [Cut identity](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#edges)
At conjugates of radii \(e^H,e^{-H}\) the two evaluations give \(n\le N+\log j/H\) and \(-n\le\log j/H\), since \(|c|\ge1\).
Thus \(|n|\le U\) throughout the original tree.

Choose inverse conjugates \(\beta,\beta^{-1}\) with \(|\beta|=e^\delta\), including \(\delta=0\).
For a cut with \(s_e\) leaves, multiply the bounds on its two moduli at these embeddings.
Their product is exactly \(|c|^2\); the bounds \(s_e e^{N\delta}\) and \(s_e\) respectively yield \(|c|\le s_e e^{N\delta/2}\).
Apply the binary bracketing of [the integer triangle count](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#lengths) separately to each evaluation of this tree.
Label each original or auxiliary edge by the two ceilings of its evaluated moduli.
For one set of labels, the possible root-weighted assignments for prescribed leaf labels are bounded by the product of their weights \(2x+1\).
Each leaf label lies between zero and \(\lceil e^{N\delta}\rceil\).
Summing the weights over all those choices for both embeddings gives at most \(\exp(Cj(1+N\delta))\) joint labelings for an absolute \(C\).

For an original edge the actual moduli \(x,y\) have \(\sqrt{xy}=|c|\) and \(\sqrt{x/y}=e^{n\delta}\).
Given the two ceilings \(X,Y\), the nonnegative difference between \(\sqrt{XY}\) and \(|c|\) is at most
\[
 \frac{(x+1)(y+1)-xy}{\sqrt{XY}+\sqrt{xy}}
 \ \le\ e^{n\delta}+e^{-n\delta}+1/|c|\ \le\ 2e^{U\delta}+1.
\]
Thus only \(O(e^{U\delta})\) signed integer coefficients are possible for that edge.
This bound stays independent of the coefficient magnitude even when it greatly exceeds the number of terms at the adjacent vertex.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the ambiguous labels only at large weights

For each vertex omit the parent and a child having the most descendant leaves.
Its coefficient product \(H_2\) is at most the product of \(1+s_e e^{N\delta/2}\) over the other children.
The [dyadic leaf count](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#budget) bounds the product of \(1+s_e\) on all those edges by \(e^{Cj}\).
The number of edges is at most \(2j-3\), so the corresponding bound for the product of these local majorants is \(\exp(Cj(1+N\delta))\), independently of all coefficient choices.

Set \(R=\lfloor a R_0\rfloor\) with sufficiently small absolute \(a>0\).
For arity \(k\le R\), the [fixed outgoing coefficient bound with variable output](Yin_C_G0345_v589.md#statement) allows at most \(\exp((k-2)L/2)\) lists once the child subtrees are filled.
Its power-degree hypothesis holds eventually by [power-field descent](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent).
At larger arities maximize \(\log(W/H_2^\lambda)/(k-2)\) over complete tuples with positive count in the stated windows for this \(j\), and let \(X_0\) be the larger of this maximum and \(L/2\), taking \(X_0=L/2\) if the set is empty.
Given its two integer labels, choosing the parent coefficient costs at most \(C e^{U\delta}\) possibilities.
For each choice the upper bound on its exponent lists is \(e^{(k-2)X_0}\) times the \(\lambda\)-th power of the local majorant just obtained.
This also bounds the number of compatible placements when the child coefficients are already known.
The root leaf fixes the translation.
The tree identity \(\sum_v(k_v-2)=j-2\) allows at most \((j-2)/(R-2)\) large vertices.
Including plane trees, leaf signs, both sets of rounded labels and all local majorants gives
\[
       \log A_j\le (j-2)X_0+O_\lambda(j(1+N\delta))
                         +(j-2)O(1+U\delta)/(R-2).
\]
Consequently \(X_0\ge L-O_\lambda(1+N\delta+(1+U\delta)/R)\).
Under the hypotheses this exceeds \(L/2\) eventually, so some \(r>R\) and one complete tuple give the claimed count, including the constant-base gain when \(U\delta=O(1)\).
The [full-tuple dilation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) costs \(o((r-2)L)\) in the logarithm under either of its two stated hypotheses.

Finally, \(\Delta\) is a positive integer, so \(\Lambda=O(\log(2d))\).
For reciprocal inputs the sum of all absolute logarithmic radii is \(2m\), giving \(\delta\le2m/d\).
The [reciprocal house bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v311.md#spread) gives \(dH\ge(\log2)/4\).
Together these bounds give \(N\delta=O(\Lambda)\) and \(U\delta\le N\delta+O(m\log(2d))\).
If instead \(\delta=O(m/(d\log(2d)))\), they give \(N\delta=O(1)\) and \((\delta/H)\log(T+1)=O(m)\).
These estimates imply the stated sufficient tests.
