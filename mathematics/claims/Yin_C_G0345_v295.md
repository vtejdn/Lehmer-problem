# A positive excess gap for unit monomial Schur defects

<a id="statement"></a>
## The unit-defect model at a prescribed constant

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\), of even degree \(d=2\ell+2\ge4\) and positive constant \(q\ge2\).
Use the [Schur correspondence](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#correspondence) and its [descendant](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#descendant) to define
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N=q^2-1,\qquad A=f^*-qf,\qquad L=\operatorname{lc}(A)=N+j,\qquad
 K=L^2-N^2=j(2N+j),\\
 B=\frac{LA+NA^*}{Kz}=D(z^2)+zE(z^2),\qquad
 \deg D=\ell,\quad D\text{ monic},\quad\deg E\le\ell-1,\\
 \Delta=\frac KN(qE+E^*).
 \end{gathered}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(j\) is a positive integer.
The reversals of \(f,A,B,D\) use degrees \(d,d-1,d-2,\ell\), respectively; the reversals of \(E,\Delta\) use formal degree \(\ell-1\).
The polynomial \(B\) is monic and strictly stable, meaning that all its roots have modulus less than one.
The defect \(\Delta\) is integral.

There exists an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that every such input satisfying
\[
 q=d^2,\qquad
 \Delta=\varepsilon w^r,\qquad
 \varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},\quad0\le r\le\ell-1                         \tag{2}
\]
has
\[
                              \theta-q\ge\eta.                      \tag{3}
\]
The argument proves a qualitative positive gap; it supplies no explicit numerical value for \(\eta\).
It uses no finite enumeration.

The complete integer coefficient formulation for arbitrary defects is given below.
For the unit model, if \(d\ge6\), \(q=d^2\), and \(0<\theta-q\le1\), then only \(r=0\) and \(r=\ell-1\) are possible.
These two endpoints have the exact odd parts
\[
 E=\frac{\varepsilon(q-w^{\ell-1})}{K}
       \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 E=\frac{\varepsilon(qw^{\ell-1}-1)}{K},                              \tag{4}
\]
respectively.
In either case the least common denominator of \(B\) is exactly \(K\), and the greatest common divisor of its support-exponent differences is one.
These properties evade the [power-substitution restriction](Yin_C_G0345_v294.md#statement), but do not remove the excess gap (3).

<a id="parity"></a>
## The complete parity formulation and its stability boundary

Write
\[
 f(z)=U(z^2)-zV(z^2),\qquad \rho=L/N.
\]
The inverse Schur identity gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 U&=wD+qD^*-\rho w(qE+E^*),\\
 V&=\rho(qD+D^*)-(wE+qE^*).
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{5}
\]
The stars on \(D,V\) use formal degree \(\ell\), while those on \(E\) use formal degree \(\ell-1\).
Thus \(U\) is monic of degree \(\ell+1\) and constant \(q\), and integrality of \(f\) is equivalent to integrality of both \(U,V\).
If \(S=[w^\ell]V\), the endpoint coefficients satisfy
\[
                              V(0)=qS-L.                            \tag{6}
\]

Strict stability of the full \(B\) does not imply strict stability of its even part \(D\).
For example \(B=(z-\tfrac12)^4\) is strictly stable, whereas
\[
                       D(w)=w^2+\tfrac32w+\tfrac1{16}
\]
has a root in \((-2,-1)\), since \(D(-2)>0>D(-1)\).
This example checks the proposed implication only; it is not an integral Pisot inverse.
No stability of \(D\) or \(V\) is assumed in the proof below.

Define
\[
                    \mathcal F(V)=(q^2-w)V^*+q(w-1)V .
\]
Expansion of (5) gives
\[
                  \mathcal F(V)-LU=w\Delta,\qquad
                  \Delta=\frac KN(qE+E^*).                          \tag{7}
\]
The left side is integral, its constant vanishes, and the displayed identity gives \(\deg\Delta\le\ell-1\); hence \(\Delta\in\mathbb Z[w]\).
Also \(\Delta=0\) if and only if \(E=0\): reverse \(qE+E^*=0\) in formal degree \(\ell-1\) and eliminate \(E^*\).

Conversely, take integer \(V,\Delta\) of formal degrees \(\ell,\ell-1\), with (6), and require
\[
                         \mathcal F(V)\equiv w\Delta\pmod L         \tag{8}
\]
coefficientwise.
Set
\[
 E=\frac{q\Delta-\Delta^*}{K},\qquad
 D=\frac{qV-V^*+NE^*}{L},\qquad
 U=\frac{\mathcal F(V)-w\Delta}{L}.                                 \tag{9}
\]
Then \(D\) is monic of degree \(\ell\), because \(qS-V(0)=L\).
Reversal verifies (5) and (7); \(U\) is integral, monic of degree \(\ell+1\), and has constant \(q\).
If the reconstructed full \(B=D(z^2)+zE(z^2)\) is strictly stable, then \(A=LzB-NB^*\) is strictly stable by \(L>N\) and Rouché's theorem.
Its inverse \(f\) is integral by (5), and \(A(1)=jB(1)>0\).
The strict Schur correspondence therefore gives a Pisot minimal polynomial of exactly degree \(d\) and positive constant \(q\).
Replacing the full stability condition by a condition on \(D\) or \(V\) would change this equivalence.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## A unit monomial must occupy an endpoint

Evaluation of (7) at one gives
\[
                         \gcd(N,j)\mid\Delta(1).
\]
For (2), it follows that
\[
                              \gcd(N,L)=1.                          \tag{10}
\]
Reverse \(\mathcal F(V)\) in formal degree \(\ell+1\).
The identity
\[
 q(1-w)\mathcal F(V)-(q^2-w)\mathcal F(V)^*=-N^2wV
\]
and (8) imply
\[
             N^2V\equiv(q^2-w)\Delta^*-q(1-w)\Delta\pmod L.          \tag{11}
\]
In particular \(N\) is invertible modulo \(L\).

Suppose \(q=d^2\), \(d\ge6\), and \(0<\theta-q\le1\).
Since \(S=-[z^{d-1}]f\) is the trace of \(f\), its \(d-1\) interior roots give
\[
                        q-d+1<S<q+d<L.
\]
If \(0<r<\ell-1\), the coefficient of \(w^\ell\) on the right of (11) is zero.
This would force \(L\mid S\), a contradiction.
The two remaining cases are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 r&E&N^2S\pmod L\\ \hline
 0&\varepsilon(q-w^{\ell-1})/K&-\varepsilon\\
 \ell-1&\varepsilon(qw^{\ell-1}-1)/K&\varepsilon q .
 \end{array}                                                       \tag{12}
\]
The assumption \(d\ge6\) makes the endpoint positions distinct.

Each displayed \(E\) has a coefficient \(1/K\) or \(-1/K\).
Every coefficient denominator of \(B\) divides \(K\) by (1), so its least common denominator is exactly \(K\).
Moreover \(B\) has nonzero coefficients at both degrees \(2\ell\) and \(2\ell-1\), proving the support assertion in the statement.

If also \(0<j<q-1\), then
\[
                              S\in\{q,q+1\}.                        \tag{13}
\]
Indeed put \(t=B(0)=D(0)\), \(v=[z^{2\ell-1}]B=[w^{\ell-1}]E\).
The leading coefficient in (5) is \(S=\rho(q+t)-v\).
Strict stability gives \(|t|<1\), while (12) gives \(|v|\le q/K\).
Consequently
\[
 q-1+\frac{j}{q+1}-\frac qK<S
       <q+1+\frac{j}{q-1}+\frac qK .
\]
For \(q\ge2,j\ge1\), one has \(q/K<j/(q+1)\) and \(q/K<1/(q-1)\).
Since the integer \(j<q-1\) is at most \(q-2\), this places the integer \(S\) strictly between \(q-1\) and \(q+2\), proving (13).

<a id="growth"></a>
## Necessary branch growth along a shrinking sequence

Suppose, toward a contradiction to (3), that actual inputs in (2) have
\(\delta=\theta-q\to0\).
The [branch inequalities](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#branch), with \(j\ge1\), force \(q\to\infty\), hence \(d\to\infty\).
They also give \(j/q\to0\), so eventually \(j<q-1\), \(d\ge8\), and (12)--(13) apply.

In fact
\[
                               j/d\longrightarrow\infty.           \tag{14}
\]
To see this, use the [degree requirement](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#degree) for the positive delay-one return.
If \(r_{\rm out}\) is the degree of its exterior Salem root, then eventually
\[
 \log r_{\rm out}\gg X\log X,\qquad X=(q/j)^{1/6},
 \qquad r_{\rm out}\le d+1.
\]
The constants in this degree requirement do not depend on the input.
If \(j/d\) did not tend to infinity, some subsequence would have \(j\le Md\) for a fixed \(M\).
Since \(q=d^2\), the lower bound would then exceed a constant times \(d^{1/6}\log d\), contradicting \(\log r_{\rm out}\le\log(d+1)\).
This proves (14), using the degree of the returned Salem factor rather than identifying it with the seed degree.

Put \(s=d-2=2\ell\), and write
\[
 t=B_0,\quad u=B_1,\quad x=B_2,\quad
 y=B_{s-2},\quad v=B_{s-1},\qquad B_i=[z^i]B .
\]
For the endpoint models, \(|u|,|v|\le q/K\), where \(K\ge2q^2-1\).
Newton's identity and strict stability of the \(s\) roots give
\[
            |y|<\frac{s+v^2}{2},\qquad |x|<\binom{s}{2}<q/2.       \tag{15}
\]
The first bound uses \(\sum\beta_i^2=v^2-2y\); it does not assume the roots occur in opposite pairs.
For \(\ell\ge3\), the endpoint \(E\) has zero coefficient at degree \(\ell-2\), so (5) yields
\[
                   V_{\ell-1}=\rho(qy+x)-qu=O(qd).                  \tag{16}
\]
This bound is uniform along the supposed sequence.

<a id="low-endpoint"></a>
## Excluding the constant endpoint

For \(\Delta=\varepsilon\), (11)--(12) give
\[
                  V_{\ell-1}\equiv-q^2S\equiv(j-1)S\pmod L.
\]
Here \(V_{\ell-1}/L\to0\) by (16), and \((j-1)S/L\to0\) because \(j/q\to0\) and \(S=q\) or \(q+1\).
Their difference is an integer multiple of \(L\), so eventually
\[
                            V_{\ell-1}=(j-1)S.
\]
Equation (16) then gives \(j=O(d)\), contradicting (14).
Thus this endpoint cannot occur along a shrinking subsequence.

<a id="two-schur"></a>
## An inequality from two strict Schur steps

For any real monic strictly stable \(B\) of degree \(s\ge4\), with the five coefficients above, put
\[
             a=1-t^2,\qquad c=u-tv,\qquad e=v-tu .
\]
The polynomial
\[
                       C=\frac{B-tB^*}{z}
\]
is strictly stable of degree \(s-1\), leading coefficient \(a>0\), constant \(c\), next-to-leading coefficient \(e\), and coefficient of \(z\) equal to \(x-ty\).
Its next Schur transform \((aC-cC^*)/z\) is strictly stable of degree \(s-2\), with leading coefficient \(a^2-c^2>0\) and constant \(a(x-ty)-ce\).
The root-modulus product therefore gives
\[
              \left|x-ty-\frac{ce}{a}\right|
                         <a-\frac{c^2}{a}.                         \tag{17}
\]
Both applications follow from Rouché's theorem on the circle and exact cancellation of the constant term.
Reversal uses the actual degrees \(s,s-1\), respectively.
The strict leading inequalities remain valid if a Schur constant is zero.

<a id="high-endpoint"></a>
## Excluding the top endpoint

For \(\Delta=\varepsilon w^{\ell-1}\), equations (11)--(12) imply
\[
                   V_{\ell-1}\equiv-S\pmod L.
\]
Equation (16) and \(S/L\to0\) force
\[
                              V_{\ell-1}=-S                         \tag{18}
\]
eventually.
For \(\ell\ge3\), the coefficient of \(w\) on the right of (11) is \(-\varepsilon\).
Combining it with the leading coefficient gives
\[
              N^2V_1\equiv-\varepsilon,\qquad
              qV_1+S\equiv0\pmod L.                                \tag{19}
\]
The coefficient of \(w\) in \(E^*\) vanishes.
Thus (9), (18), and (19) give the important integrality assertion
\[
                x=D_1=\frac{qV_1+S}{L}\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
                x(j-1)\equiv S\pmod q.                             \tag{20}
\]
No stability of \(D\) or \(V\) enters this step.

The endpoint coefficients and (18) also give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 u=-\varepsilon/K,\quad v=\varepsilon q/K,\quad
 t=\frac{NS-qL+\varepsilon qN/K}{L},\\
 y=-R-\frac{x}{q},\qquad
 R=\frac{NS}{qL}+\frac{\varepsilon N}{KL}\longrightarrow1,\\
 c=-\frac{\varepsilon(1+qt)}K,\qquad
 e=\frac{\varepsilon(q+t)}K .
 \end{gathered}                                                     \tag{21}
\]
In (17), the central expression can therefore be written as
\[
                x(1+t/q)+tR-ce/a.                                 \tag{22}
\]

First suppose \(S=q+1\) on a subsequence.
Then \(t\to1\), and
\[
 1-t=\frac{(q+1)j-\varepsilon qN/K}{L}\asymp j/q,
 \qquad a=1-t^2\asymp j/q.
\]
The comparisons follow from \(N/K<1/(2j)\), and their constants are absolute.
Also \(ce=O(q^2/K^2)\), so \(ce/a=O(1/(qj^3))\to0\).
The right side of (17) is at most \(a\to0\).
Equation (22) first bounds \(x\), then forces \(x\to-1\).
By its integrality, \(x=-1\) eventually.
But (20) would give \(-(j-1)\equiv1\pmod q\), or \(j\equiv0\pmod q\), contrary to \(0<j<q-1\).

It remains to consider \(S=q\).
Now
\[
 t=-\frac qL\left(j-\frac{\varepsilon N}{K}\right)<0,\qquad
 \frac{qj}{2L}\le |t|\le\frac{3qj}{2L},\qquad t\to0.                 \tag{23}
\]
Thus \(a\to1\), and (17)--(22) give \(|x|\le1+o(1)\).
Eventually \(x\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
In this case \(ce=O(qj/K^2)\), and
\[
                         \frac{|ce|}{a|t|}=O(1/(q^2j^2))\to0 .
\]
If \(x=-1\), then (21) gives \(y\to-1\), so eventually \(ty\ge|t|/2\).
The left side of (17) would exceed one, while its right side is less than one.
This excludes \(x=-1\).

If \(x=1\), (20) forces \(j=1\).
If \(x=0\), then \(V_1=-1\) by (20), and (19) gives
\[
                           j^2\equiv N^2\equiv\varepsilon\pmod L.
\]
Since \(1\le j<q-1\), one has \(0\le j^2-1<L\) and \(0<j^2+1<L\).
The congruence therefore forces \(\varepsilon=1\) and \(j=1\).
Both remaining cases contradict (14).
This excludes the top endpoint as well.

<a id="scope"></a>
## The qualitative conclusion and its limits

A failure of (3) would supply a sequence of actual unit-defect inputs with \(\delta\to0\).
The branch inequality forces its degrees to infinity, and one of the finitely many endpoint and leading-coefficient cases would occur on an infinite subsequence.
Each such case has been excluded above.
Hence the infimum of the positive excesses in this restricted model is positive, proving the existence of \(\eta\).
No ineffective constants have been converted into a numerical value.

The result concerns unit monomial defects at \(q=d^2\).
It does not exclude defects with larger coefficients, several terms, or different parameter scales.
It also does not prove a Mahler gap for all Salem outputs: the positive-return inequality bounds their trace excess above by the Pisot excess, so a lower bound for this input excess alone cannot be reversed.
The unrestricted G0001 problem remains unresolved.

The full parity formulation and integer defect were developed by the coordinator.
The trace and two-Schur tests were developed in parallel author work, with Agent-S checking the endpoint bounds and branch split.
The coordinator identified the final coefficient congruence reducing the surviving top-endpoint case to \(j=1\).
These are collaborating author calculations, not independent review.
