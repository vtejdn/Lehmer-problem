# Ramification in trace fields of reciprocal units with a quadratic subextension

<a id="statement"></a>
## Local degree fractions and the trace discriminant

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit, \(t=x+x^{-1}\), \(A=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(n=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(d=2n\), and \(m=m(x)\) its logarithmic Mahler measure.
For a rational prime \(p\), write \(e_\mathfrak p,f_\mathfrak p\) for the absolute ramification index and residue degree of a prime \(\mathfrak p\mid p\) in \(F\).
For \(Y\ge1\) let \(\delta_p(Y)\) be \(1/n\) times the sum of \(e_\mathfrak p f_\mathfrak p\) over primes with \(e_\mathfrak p\le Y\) that ramify in \(A/F\).
Then
\[
    \delta_p(Y)\ \le\ \frac2d+
                4\sqrt{\frac{Y(\log d+2m)}{d\log p}}.                    \tag{1}
\]
The estimate allows arbitrary simultaneous variation of \(p,Y\) and the fields.
In particular a positive fixed fraction of such primes, weighted by their local degrees, with bounded \(e_\mathfrak p\), forces logarithmic measure growing at least linearly in \(d\) as \(d\to\infty\).

Suppose \(A=FK\) for a quadratic number field \(K\not\subset F\).
This is the [oriented alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields) for a primitive inverse-pair action at bounded measure and sufficiently large compressed degree.
Put
\[
   a_p=\sqrt{\frac{\log d+2m}{d\log p}},\qquad D_F=|\operatorname{disc}F|.
\]
At every \(p\) ramified in \(K\), all primes of \(F\) over \(p\) with odd \(e_\mathfrak p\) ramify in \(A/F\).
Consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac1n\sum_{e_\mathfrak p\ {\rm odd}} f_\mathfrak p
      &\le 2/d+8a_p,\\
 \frac{\nu_p(D_F)}n&\ge \tfrac12(1-2/d-4a_p),\\
 \frac{\nu_2(D_F)}n&\ge 1-2/d-8a_2\qquad\text{if dyadic ramification occurs in }K .
 \end{split}                                                              \tag{2}
\]
The sum in the first line is over the primes of \(F\) above the given \(p\).
In particular, for every fixed \(\eta>0\), the positive measures of these reciprocal units in each bounded interval form a finite set if some ramified \(p\) of \(K\) satisfies \(\nu_p(D_F)/n\le1/2-\eta\), or if \(2\) ramifies in \(K\) and \(\nu_2(D_F)/n\le1-\eta\).
For bounded \(m\) and large \(d\), every rational prime ramified in \(K\) must divide \(D_F\).

<a id="cluster"></a>
## Conjugates near the two roots of unity

The element \(x\) satisfies \(X^2-tX+1=0\).
Reciprocity and nontorsion provide the distinct conjugate \(x^{-1}\) over \(F\), so \(A/F\) is quadratic with involution \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\).
At a ramified prime of this quadratic extension the involution acts trivially on the residue field.
Thus \(x^2-1\) reduces to zero, and its valuation normalized by \(\nu_p(p)=1\) is at least \(1/(2e_\mathfrak p)\).
For every local embedding above \(\mathfrak p\), at least one of \(x-1,x+1\) has valuation at least \(1/(4e_\mathfrak p)\), by their product.
This includes \(p=2\).
Each prime counted in \(\delta_p(Y)\) gives \(2e_\mathfrak p f_\mathfrak p\) embeddings of \(A\) into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\).
Among the resulting \(d\delta_p(Y)\) conjugates at least \(r\ge d\delta_p(Y)/2\) lie within valuation \(1/(4Y)\) of one and the same sign.
Every difference in this cluster has valuation at least \(1/(4Y)\).

For the monic rational minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(x\), all conjugate differences are integral and its nonzero discriminant is the squared Vandermonde product up to sign.
Consequently \(\nu_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\ge r(r-1)/(4Y)\).
At an archimedean embedding, each Vandermonde row for a root \(z\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt d\,\max(1,|z|)^{d-1}\).
Hadamard's inequality gives
\[
     \frac{r(r-1)\log p}{4Y}
        \le\log|\operatorname{disc} P|
        \le d\log d+2(d-1)m .
\]
Taking \(r\le1+\sqrt{4Yd(\log d+2m)/\log p}\) gives (1).
For fixed \(\delta_p(Y)\ge\eta>0\), \(Y\le C\) and \(d\ge4/\eta\), rearrangement gives \(m\ge d\eta^2\log p/(128 C)-(\log d)/2\).

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## A fixed quadratic extension in the root field

In the oriented case of [C274](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields), the field \(E\) of the inverse-pair block lies in \(F\), because the root-pair stabilizer preserves that block.
The involution of \(A/F\) takes the oriented set containing \(x\) to its inverse set under any extension to the splitting field, so it acts nontrivially on the quadratic orientation field \(K/E\).
Thus \(K\cap F=E\) and \(A=FK\).
For any prime of \(E\) ramifying in \(K/E\), a prime of \(F\) above it with odd **relative** ramification index must ramify in \(A/F\), by multiplicativity in the two towers.
Formula (1) applies there with the **absolute** ramification indices of its definition.
For \(u=1\), \(E=\mathbb Q\), and this proves the asserted ramification at all odd-index primes.
The same tower argument applies whenever \(A=FK\) with \(K\) rational quadratic, without the block assumptions.

At a ramified rational prime of \(K\), restrict \(\delta_p(Y)\) to the odd indices, obtaining \(\delta_p^{\rm odd}(Y)\le\delta_p(Y)\).
Since \(\int_e^\infty Y^{-2}\,dY=1/e\) for \(e\ge1\), integration of (1) gives
\[
       \frac1n\sum_{e_\mathfrak p\ {\rm odd}} f_\mathfrak p
           =\int_1^\infty \delta_p^{\rm odd}(Y)Y^{-2}\,dY
           \le 2/d+8a_p .
\]
For the discriminant take the trace pairing on \(\mathcal O_F/p\mathcal O_F\).
Its component at \(\mathfrak p\) has a filtration of \(e_\mathfrak p\) successive copies of its degree-\(f_\mathfrak p\) residue field.
Thus multiplication by an element there has trace \(e_\mathfrak p\) times the residue-field trace of its reduction.
The pairing factors through the residue field, and it vanishes on the entire component if \(p\mid e_\mathfrak p\).
In an integral basis the nullity modulo \(p\) gives a lower bound on the valuation of the determinant, whence
\[
           \nu_p(D_F)\ge n-\sum_{p\nmid e_\mathfrak p} f_\mathfrak p .
\]
In particular every summand of index at least two costs at least half its local degree in the discriminant.
The total fraction with index one is at most \(\delta_p(1)\), giving the second line of (2).
At \(p=2\) only odd indices can contribute to the subtracted sum, proving the third line.
For bounded \(m\) all errors tend to zero uniformly in \(p\), since \(\log p\ge\log2\).
In either of the stated discriminant-deficit classes the degree is bounded at bounded \(m\), and the elementary coefficient bound \(2^d e^m\) then proves finiteness.

<a id="parity"></a>
## Odd trace degree and the scope of its parity condition

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\Delta})\) for a squarefree integer \(\Delta\), and let \(W\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(t\).
The two generators \(x-x^{-1}\) and \(\sqrt\Delta\) of the anti-invariant line of \(A/F\) differ by a factor in \(F^\times\).
Thus \(t^2-4=\Delta z^2\) with \(z\in F^\times\), giving
\[
         W(2)W(-2)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(t^2-4)=\Delta^n N_{F/\mathbb Q}(z)^2 .
\]
For odd \(n\) the two endpoint values recover \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{W(2)W(-2)})\), and for even \(n\) their product is a square.
An odd \(n=\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p} e_\mathfrak p f_\mathfrak p\) forces at least one odd summand.
The fraction of trace embeddings at odd-index primes can be exactly \(1/n\) even when \(n\) is prime and the trace group is symmetric.

Fix any odd prime \(n\) and any rational prime \(p\).
Let \(D/\mathbb Q_p\) be \(\mathbb Q_p(\sqrt p)\) at odd \(p\), and \(\mathbb Q_2(i)\) at \(p=2\).
We construct a totally real degree-\(n\) field \(F\) with trace Galois group \(S_n\) and local algebra \(\mathbb Q_p\times D^{(n-1)/2}\).
Start with a monic integral polynomial having roots zero and \((n-1)/2\) distinct translated conjugate pairs of the chosen quadratic generator, using different integer translations.
The roots are simple and all lie in \(D\).
All monic polynomials sufficiently close to this polynomial modulo a power of \(p\) have one root in each of disjoint small disks around the model roots in \(D\), by Hensel lifting at simple roots.
Choose the disks invariant as a collection under the involution, with only the disk around zero fixed.
The lifted roots then give exactly the required local algebra, including when several roots initially agree modulo \(p\).

At a second prime require the polynomial to be Eisenstein, and at a third prime larger than \(n\) require one irreducible quadratic factor and \(n-2\) distinct linear factors in its squarefree reduction.
The three conditions are monic coefficient congruences modulo a fixed integer \(C\).
To impose real roots, start with \(\prod_{j=1}^n(X-jL)\) with \(L\) a large positive multiple of \(C\), and add a fixed integral correction of degree at most \(n-1\) attaining the required congruences.
After writing \(X=LZ\) and dividing by \(L^n\), the corrected polynomial converges coefficientwise to \(\prod_{j=1}^n(Z-j)\).
The signs at the ends of disjoint small intervals about \(1,\ldots,n\) therefore give \(n\) distinct real roots for large \(L\).
Irreducibility and prime degree give a primitive group, and the third prime supplies a transposition by Frobenius.
The graph of the conjugate transpositions is connected, since its components form blocks and some component has an edge.
The edge transpositions generate \(S_n\).

Choose an odd prime \(\ell\ne p\) unramified in the normal closure of \(F\) and set \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p\ell})\).
This real quadratic field ramifies at \(p\) and at \(\ell\), so it is disjoint from the normal closure.
The totally real compositum \(FK\) has degree \(2n\).
By Dirichlet's unit theorem the group of units in \(FK\) of relative norm one over \(F\) has rank at least \((2n-1)-(n-1)=n\).
The squares of these units are totally positive and have the same rank.
There are only finitely many quadratic subfields in \(FK\), and their units each have rank at most one.
Choose a totally positive norm-one unit \(x\) outside them and outside \(\mathbb Q\), possible because finitely many proper rational subspaces do not cover a rational vector space.
Since \(t=x+x^{-1}\in F\) and \(x\) has degree greater than two, \(t\notin\mathbb Q\), so \(\mathbb Q(t)=F\) by prime degree.
The involution in \(FK/F\) interchanges the distinct roots \(x,x^{-1}\), showing that \(\mathbb Q(x)=FK\).
Its splitting field is the compositum of the normal closure of \(F\) and \(K\), with Galois group \(S_n\times C_2\), and the sign kernel has order two.
All roots are positive, so distinct roots have nontorsion quotient.
The trace field has one unramified local degree-one factor at \(p\).
For odd \(p\) the ramified quadratic factor has discriminant valuation one and for \(p=2\) it has valuation two, from the local bases \(1,\sqrt p\) and \(1,i\), respectively.
Thus \(\nu_p(D_F)=(n-1)/2\) for odd \(p\) and \(\nu_2(D_F)=n-1\) for \(p=2\).
These examples settle what prime-degree parity alone can force in the reciprocal setting.
The construction places no uniform upper bound on their Mahler measures.
