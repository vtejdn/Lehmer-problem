# Salem splitting fields unramified at any prescribed finite set of primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Avoiding discriminant primes while retaining local congruences

Use the [irreducible height-one Salem polynomials](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#statement)
\[
 P_h(Z)=1+a(Z)B_h(Z),\qquad
 a(Z)=Z^5-Z^3-Z^2,\qquad
 B_h(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{12h-1}Z^{5j},\qquad h\ge1.
\]
Their degrees are \(60h\), and their unique exterior roots increase to the plastic number from below.
For any finite set \(\mathcal P\) of rational primes, there is an explicit positive integer \(H_0\) such that every positive multiple \(h\) of \(H_0\) satisfies
\[
                         p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P_h
                                      \quad(p\in\mathcal P).             \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the entire rational splitting field of \(P_h\) is unramified at every prime in \(\mathcal P\).

One can simultaneously prescribe any finite list of the [local polynomial tests](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#finite-data).
Namely, for integers \(A_i\ge2\) and monic \(G_i\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) of degrees \(g_i\ge1\) with \(\gcd(A_i,G_i(0))=1\), the same progression can satisfy
\[
                              P_h\equiv1\pmod{(A_i,G_i)}
                                       \quad\text{for every }i.          \tag{2}
\]
Powers of the testing polynomials are allowed.
An adequate period obeys
\[
                     H_0\le
                       \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^7\prod_i A_i^{g_i+1}.     \tag{3}
\]
Thus arbitrarily large actual Salem inputs meet every such finite combination.

There are also growing windows.
With no additional tests, take \(\mathcal P=\{p:p\le B\}\), where \(B\) tends to infinity through powers of two, and take \(h=H_0\) as constructed below.
For \(d=60h\),
\[
                      \log d\le\log60+14B\log2 .                       \tag{4}
\]
In particular the full splitting field is unramified at every prime up to
\((\log d-\log60)/(14\log2)\).
The degrees are unbounded and the Mahler measures tend to the plastic number.
This controls the absolute splitting field, in addition to the relative quadratic extension treated by the endpoint argument in C286.
The entire polynomial discriminant also has the exact fixed-degree resultant expression in [the calculation below](#discriminant).

<a id="derivative"></a>
## A bounded-degree test for every repeated residue root

Put \(L=60h\) and \(Q=Z^5-1\), so \(Q B_h=Z^L-1\).
Differentiating this polynomial identity and then \(P_h=1+aB_h\) gives the exact identity
\[
 Q P_h'=B_h W+aLZ^{L-1},\qquad
 W=a'Q-aQ'
   =Z(2Z^6+3Z^5-5Z^3+3Z+2).                                      \tag{5}
\]
Suppose \(p\mid h\).
Then \(L=0\) in characteristic \(p\).
A root \(z\) of \(\bar P_h\) is nonzero because \(P_h(0)=1\).
It cannot satisfy \(z^5=1\), since the geometric sum defining \(B_h\) then has value \(12h=0\), giving \(P_h(z)=1\).
At a remaining root \(a(z)B_h(z)=-1\), so both factors are nonzero.
If that root were repeated, (5) would imply \(\bar W(z)=0\).

The coefficients of \(W\) have greatest common divisor one, so its reduction is nonzero at every prime.
Remove its maximal power of \(Z\) in \(\mathbb F_p[Z]\), and divide by the leading coefficient.
This yields a monic polynomial \(w_p\) of degree \(g_p\le6\), with nonzero constant coefficient.
Every possible repeated root above is a root of \(w_p\).
Let \(G_p\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) be any monic lift of \(w_p\).
If
\[
                         P_h\equiv1\pmod{(p,G_p)},                    \tag{6}
\]
these candidates are excluded.
Hence \(\bar P_h\) is squarefree, which is equivalent to (1) for a monic integral polynomial.
This step uses the full polynomial discriminant and requires no assumption about an index of orders.

<a id="small-primes"></a>
## The characteristics two, three and five

The reductions of the exact polynomial \(W\) in (5) are
\[
 \bar W=
 \begin{cases}
  Z^2(Z^2+Z+1)^2,&p=2,\\
  2Z(Z+1)^6,&p=3,\\
  2Z(Z-1)^6,&p=5.
 \end{cases}
\]
In characteristics two and three, \(p\mid L\) and \(p\mid12h\) hold for every \(h\).
The candidate roots of \(Q\) are therefore excluded by \(P_h=1\), as above.
In characteristic two every remaining candidate has order three, so \(z^{60h}=1\) and the geometric quotient gives \(B_h(z)=0\).
In characteristic three the remaining candidate is \(-1\), where \(P_h(-1)=1\).
Thus \(P_h\) is squarefree modulo two and three for every \(h\).

In characteristic five all candidates from \(W\) lie at \(Z=1\), where \(P_h(1)=1-12h\).
There are no repeated roots when \(h\not\equiv3\pmod5\).
For the converse, reciprocity in even degree gives \(P_h'(1)=30hP_h(1)\), so a zero at one is repeated in characteristic five.
Thus squarefreeness modulo five is exactly the condition \(h\not\equiv3\pmod5\).
The sufficient choice \(5\mid h\) used in (1) meets this condition.

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## The full discriminant as a resultant with a cubic trace polynomial

Write \(f(Z)=Z^3-Z-1\), \(f^*(Z)=1-Z^2-Z^3\), \(V=W/Z\), \(L=60h\), and
\[
 H_L(Z)=L f(Z)f^*(Z)-V(Z)
       =Z^3 C_L(Z+Z^{-1}),
\]
where
\[
 C_L(T)=-(L+2)T^3-(L+3)T^2+(4L+6)T+5L+11.                         \tag{7}
\]
These identities follow by multiplication.
If \(S_h\) is the monic trace polynomial of \(P_h\), then the signed polynomial discriminant is
\[
 \operatorname{disc}P_h
   =\frac{\operatorname{Res}(P_h,H_L)}
           {(1-12h)(144h^2+36h+1)^2}
   =\frac{\operatorname{Res}(S_h,C_L)^2}
           {(1-12h)(144h^2+36h+1)^2}.                              \tag{8}
\]
In particular it is negative.

To prove (8), at any root \(z\) of \(P_h\) the factor \(a(z)\) is nonzero since \(P_h=1+aB_h\).
The factor \(Q(z)\) is also nonzero by the exact endpoint and fifth-order values in C286.
The equation \(P_h(z)=0\) gives \(B_h(z)=-1/a(z)\) and
\[
                     z^L=\frac{a(z)-Q(z)}{a(z)}
                         =\frac{f^*(z)}{a(z)}.
\]
Substitution into (5) yields
\[
                              P_h'(z)=\frac{zH_L(z)}{a(z)Q(z)}.
\]
Now \(\operatorname{Res}(P_h,Z)=1\), since the degree is even and the constant coefficient is one.
Also \(\operatorname{Res}(P_h,a)=1\).
Indeed \(a=Z^2f\), and \(P_h\) is one at every root of the monic \(f\); switching the two resultant arguments has positive sign because \(\deg P_h\) is even.
Finally the [exact torsion norms](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#norms) give
\[
              \operatorname{Res}(P_h,Q)
                    =(1-12h)(144h^2+36h+1)^2.
\]
Multiplying the derivative identity over the roots proves the first expression in (8), since
\((-1)^{d(d-1)/2}=1\) at \(d=60h\).
Pairing the roots \(z,z^{-1}\) in \(H_L=Z^3C_L(Z+Z^{-1})\) gives the second expression.
At primes dividing \(h\), \(H_L\) reduces to \(-V\), agreeing with the repeated-root elimination above.
The coordinator supplied the resultant formula and the trace cubic.

<a id="period"></a>
## Choosing the period and the degree

In \(\mathbb F_p[Z]/(w_p)\), the class of \(Z\) is a unit.
Let \(e_p\) be the order of \(Z^5\) in its finite unit group.
Then \(e_p\le p^{g_p}\le p^6\).
If \(p e_p\mid h\), then \(p\mid h\) and \(p e_p\mid12h\).
Partitioning the geometric sum into blocks of length \(e_p\) makes it zero in this ring, since the number of equal blocks is divisible by \(p\).
This proves (6).

For the additional tests let \(e_i\) be the order of \(Z^5\) in
\(((\mathbb Z/A_i\mathbb Z)[Z]/(G_i))^\times\), so \(e_i\le A_i^{g_i}\).
The same block argument proves (2) when \(A_i e_i\mid h\).
It is therefore enough to take
\[
               H_0=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(
                    \{p e_p:p\in\mathcal P\}\cup\{A_i e_i:i\}\bigr),
\]
with empty least common multiple one.
The product bound gives (3).

For the prime window without extra tests, put
\(\psi(B)=\sum_{p^j\le B}\log p\).
Then
\[
       \log H_0\le7\sum_{p\le B}\log p\le7\psi(B)\le14B\log2
\]
at powers of two, by the [elementary binomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v286.md#local).
This proves (4).
The periods \(H_0\) tend to infinity, since they are divisible by every prime at most \(B\).
If a fixed finite list of extra tests is retained, the right side of (4) increases only by the constant \(\sum_i\log(A_i e_i)\).

<a id="splitting"></a>
## Unramified local splitting and the remaining scope

Fix a prime \(p\) satisfying (1).
The squarefree reduction of \(P_h\) splits over a finite residue field.
Take the unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) with that residue field.
Hensel lifting at each simple residue root splits the full polynomial in this unramified extension.
Thus every completion of its rational splitting field at a prime above \(p\) is unramified.
This is the same local passage used in the [simple trace reduction argument](Yin_C_G0345_v272.md#five), now applied directly to the original Salem minimal polynomial.

The endpoint argument alone controls ramification in
\(\mathbb Q(\beta_h)/\mathbb Q(\beta_h+\beta_h^{-1})\).
Equation (5) makes it possible to exclude ramification in the entire splitting field while preserving prescribed local polynomial data.
A diagonal choice can make every fixed rational prime eventually unramified.
The measures continue to approach the positive plastic limit, so the construction establishes no sequence of Mahler measures tending to one.
The coordinator supplied the bounded-degree derivative elimination and its simultaneous use with the finite-ring construction.
