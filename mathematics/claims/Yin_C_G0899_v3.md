# A prescribed dyadic order on irreducible power substitutions and its two prime residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## The odd part of one primitive resultant

Let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(D\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m(Q)\le1\).
Let \(p\equiv1\bmod4\) be prime such that \(P(z)=Q(z^p)\) is irreducible, and put \(d=Dp,\ J=2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\).
Require \(D\le\log(d)/(4\log\log(3d))\).
Let \(h\) be the multiplicity of \(1\) in \(Q\bmod2\), allowing zero, and let \(W\) be the odd part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_J)|\).
Uniformly on these inputs as \(d\to\infty\),
\[
 W=2^{-h}|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{J/2}+1)|,\qquad
 \log W=(1+o(1))(J/2)m(P).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(d\), every prime \(\ell\mid W\) is prime to \(\operatorname{disc}P\) and lies in one of the two residue classes
\[
       \ell\equiv\pm1\bmod B,\qquad B=J/2^{\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor}\ge p .
\]
The odd prime ideals at which a root of \(P\) first returns to one at exponent \(J\) have the same residue fields and valuation contributions as the corresponding prime ideals for \(\alpha\).
Their product, each with its valuation in the corresponding \(J\)-th power minus one, has absolute norm exactly \(W\).

The unrestricted positive Lehmer gap is equivalent to the existence of \(c>0\) such that \(\log W\ge cd\) for all sufficiently large \(d\) in this class.
This equivalence still holds under any additional restriction \(D\le g(d)\) with \(g(d)\to\infty\).
In particular the order and the two classes are prescribed by the degrees before any prime factorization.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The norm estimate and the prime classes

Raising to the odd power \(p\) permutes the primitive \(J\)-th roots, so the absolute primitive resultant equals that for \(Q\) and is \(|N(\alpha^{J/2}+1)|\).
The [local multiplicity calculation](Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#local) with \(u=1,\ell=2,\varphi(J)=J/2>D\) gives the exact valuation \(h\).
By the [signed resultant estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) the absolute difference between the log norm and \((J/2)m(Q)\) is at most \(C D^3\log(2D)\log(2J)(1+m(Q))\).
The degree bound \(m(Q)\gg(\log(2D))^{-3}\) from the [positive-measure estimate](Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer), the stated upper bound on \(D\), and \(h\le D\) make both this error and \(h\log2\) uniformly \(o(Jm(Q))\).
Taking all \(p\)-th roots of every conjugate gives \(m(P)=m(Q)\).

Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
An odd prime divisor \(\ell\) of \(W\) has a prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of \(K\) at which \(\alpha^{J/2}=-1\) in the residue field, hence \(\alpha\) has order exactly \(J\) there.
For its residue degree \(f\le D\) it follows that \(J\mid\ell^f-1\).
Write \(s=v_2(f)\).
For odd \(f\) the valuation equals \(v_2(\ell-1)\).
For \(s\ge1\) repeated difference of squares gives \(v_2(\ell^f-1)=v_2(\ell-1)+v_2(\ell+1)+s-1\), since \(\ell^{2^a}+1\equiv2\bmod8\) for \(a\ge1\).
One of the first two valuations is one, so in both cases \(\max(v_2(\ell-1),v_2(\ell+1))\ge\log_2J-\lfloor\log_2 D\rfloor\).
This proves the congruences, and \(B\ge J/D\ge p\).
Eventually \(B\ge4\), and the only possible positive odd prime at most \(B\) in those classes is \(B-1\equiv3\bmod4\), so \(\ell\ne p\).

By the [discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#discriminant), \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|\le D\log D+2(D-1)m(Q)\le(\log d)/3\) eventually under our size restriction.
All the primes in question are at least \(B-1\ge d/D-1>d^{1/3}\) eventually.
Finally differentiation at the roots of \(Q(z^p)\) and the unit constant term give \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=p^d|\operatorname{disc}Q|^p\).
Thus all odd contributors give separable reductions of both polynomials.

<a id="transport"></a>
## The residue fields and valuations above the base

Take a root \(\gamma\) of \(P\) with \(\gamma^p=\alpha\), and put \(L=K(\gamma)\).
For an odd prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of \(K\) with residue order \(J\), the group generated by \(\bar\alpha\) has a unique solution \(y\) of \(y^p=\bar\alpha\) in that group.
This solution has order \(J\); all other solutions in the algebraic residue closure differ by a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity and have order \(pJ\).
Indeed \(\ell\ne p\) and \(\gcd(p,J)=1\).
Hensel's lemma gives a unique lift of \(y\) to a root in \(K_{\mathfrak q}\).
The irreducible polynomial of \(\gamma\) over \(K\) is \(z^p-\alpha\), since the absolute degrees are \(Dp,D\).
The factorization over the completion has exactly one linear factor reducing to \(y\), and every other local factor has roots reducing to the other solutions by separability.
Hence exactly one prime \(\mathfrak P\) above \(\mathfrak q\) gives order \(J\), with \(L_{\mathfrak P}=K_{\mathfrak q}\).
Conversely every order-\(J\) prime for \(\gamma\) contracts to one for \(\alpha=\gamma^p\).
At the matching primes the quotient \((\gamma^{pJ}-1)/(\gamma^J-1)\) reduces to \(p\), a unit.
Consequently the respective normalized integral valuations of \(\gamma^J-1\) and \(\alpha^J-1\) agree.
At odd order-\(J\) primes \(\alpha^{J/2}-1\) is a unit, so the valuation on the base is exactly that of \(\alpha^{J/2}+1\).
The factorization of the principal ideal of this element accounts for its entire odd norm, proving the product statement.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## Why the restricted class tests every unit

If \(m(Q)\) has a uniform positive lower bound, the uniform estimate above and \(J\ge d\) give \(\log W\gg d\).
Conversely fix an arbitrary nontorsion unit with \(0<m(Q)\le1\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\) the [irreducible power-substitution lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) applies.
There are arbitrarily large primes \(1\bmod4\), since any prime divisor of one plus the square of twice the product of finitely many such primes is a new prime \(1\bmod4\).
With \(D\) fixed and \(d=Dp\to\infty\), both size restrictions eventually hold and \(J<2d\).
Thus \(\log W\ge cd\) forces \(m(Q)\ge c\) by the asymptotic estimate.
Units of logarithmic measure larger than one and nonunit algebraic integers already have positive absolute lower bounds in their respective classes.
This proves the equivalence and leaves the degree-proportional lower bound for the indicated odd norm open.
