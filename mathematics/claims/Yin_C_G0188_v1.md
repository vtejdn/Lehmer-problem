## Exact theorem statement

Let F be a totally real number field, let a,b,c be nonzero algebraic integers in F, and let s in{1,-1}.
Assume

    2c^2=3a^5,
    b^2-a^3+s(c^3-2b^5)=0,
    g=c^3-2b^5!=0.

Then

    Q(a,b,c)=Q(a).                                 (1)

In particular b,c belong to the field generated by the totally positive coordinate a.
The conclusion concerns the actual primitive triple field; it does not assert that an arbitrarily enlarged ambient F is generated by a.
It gives no exponential norm floor for g and does not establish Q(a)=Q(g).

The proof uses the following full real-pair estimate.
For A>0 put C=sqrt(27/8) A^(5/2) and q(x)=2x^5-x^2.
Whenever nonzero real x,y satisfy q(x)=C-A and q(y)=-C-A, their sum T obeys

    -113/100<T<8/5,
    -1/2<T<0 if A>=2/3.                            (2)

These estimates hold for every allowed pair, not only a selected continuation of two branches.

## Complete proof

Put H=Q(a,b,c), and first prove H=Q(a,c).
The equation2c^2=3a^5 makes a totally positive.
Since a is a nonzero algebraic integer, its full norm is a positive integer, so some real embedding of Q(a,c) has a>=1.
At that embedding replace b,c by B=s b,C=s c, without changing a or the generated fields.
Then

    2B^5-B^2=C^3-a^3,
    abs(C)^3=(3/2)^(3/2) a^(15/2).

If C>0, the right-hand level is positive; if C<0, it is below-1.
The polynomial q(X)=2X^5-X^2 has a unique real root at either such level: its negative half-line increases from-minus infinity to0, and its positive local minimum is -3/5^(5/3)>-1.
Every embedding of H over this fixed embedding of Q(a,c) is real and must send b to that same unique root.
Because H=Q(a,c)(b) and characteristic0 gives separability, there can be only one such embedding.
Thus b belongs to Q(a,c), proving H=Q(a,c).
The relation c^2=3a^5/2 now gives [H:Q(a)]<=2.
Suppose for contradiction that the degree is2, and put k=Q(a).
The nontrivial k-automorphism exchanges c and-c.
Replace b,c by s b,s c in the equations, and denote the replaced b again by b.
This preserves integrality and the field H, and fixes the sign in the residual equation to1.
Then

    T=Tr_(H/k)(b) in O_k.

At any real embedding of k put A=a^3>0 and C=sqrt(27/8) A^(5/2)>0.
The two b values lie respectively at the two levels

    q(b)=C-A and q(b)=-C-A,
    q(x)=2x^5-x^2.                                 (R36)

They need not be selected by a presumed continuation of root branches.
The bounds below cover EVERY possible choice of one root at each level.
Put q_0=2^(-1/3), K=sqrt(27/8), and f(x)=x^2+2x^5 for x>=0.
The function f is strictly increasing.
The polynomial q has a unique negative root at every negative level, is negative on(0,q_0), and has its positive local minimum -3/5^(5/3)>-1.

#### All levels A>=2/3

Here C>=A, so the first level has one nonzero real root B>=q_0, while the second, being at most-4/3, has only a negative root-D.
They satisfy

    q(B)=C-A, f(D)=C+A.

Since q(sqrt(A))=2A^(5/2)-A>C-A and sqrt(A)>q_0, one has B<sqrt(A).
Thus f(D)-f(B)=2(A-B^2)>0, proving D>B.
One also has B>(9/10)sqrt(A).
If the proposed lower endpoint is at most q_0 this follows immediately.
Otherwise q is increasing there, and the required comparison follows from

    [K-2(9/10)^5] A^(3/2)>19/100.

Indeed K>9/5, the bracket is greater than3/5, and A^(3/2)>1/2 for A>=2/3.

    0<D-B<2A/(10B^4)
           <1/[5(9/10)^4 A]
           <=3000/6561<1/2.

Consequently every actual trace in this range satisfies

    -1/2<T=B-D<0.                                  (R37)

At A=2/3 the zero root of the first level is not allowed, since b is a nonzero field element; its nonzero root is still q_0.

#### All levels 0<A<2/3

Any positive root belongs to(0,q_0), so the trace is always less than2q_0<8/5.
Let-D and-U be the unique negative roots at the levels -C-A and C-A respectively.
They are characterized by

    f(D)=A+C, f(U)=A-C, D>U>0.

#### The complete integral-norm contradiction

The preceding estimates put EVERY k-conjugate of the integral trace T in the interval(-113/100,8/5).
Let

    W(X)=X(X+1)(X-1)(X-2)
        =(X^2-X-1)^2-1.                            (R41)

For X in that interval, u=X^2-X lies between-1/4 and24069/10000.
The function u(u-2) is at least-1.
At the left endpoint of this u interval its value is9/16<1, and at the right endpoint it is also below1, because

    (14069/10000)^2<2,
    197936761<200000000.

Convexity of u(u-2) then gives abs(W(X))<=1 throughout the whole trace interval.
The element a is a nonzero totally positive algebraic integer, so at least one embedding of k has a>=1, hence A>=1.
At that embedding(R37) gives T in(-1/2,0), where W(T) lies strictly between-15/16 and0.
Thus W(T) is nonzero, every conjugate has absolute value at most1, and at least one has absolute value strictly less than1.
Its full k-norm is consequently a nonzero rational integer of absolute value strictly less than1, a contradiction.
This excludes the assumed degree2 and proves(1).

The conclusion concerns the actual primitive triple field H, which may be a proper subfield of the original ambient F.
For any element g of H the original norm remains

    abs(Norm_F(g))=abs(Norm_H(g))^[F:H],

The argument neither bounds Norm_H(g) from below nor proves H=Q(g).
