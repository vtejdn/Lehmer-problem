# A rational-prime test on the compositum of two Salem trace fields

<a id="degree"></a>
## Absolute local degrees when the earlier record is Salem

Suppose there are [shrinking adjacent strict records](Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement) whose earlier measure \(A\) is the exterior root of a Salem source, of degree \(d_A\).
Let \(B\) be the later measure, \(b=\log B\), \(x=A^aB\) at the second closest exponent below two, \(z=2-x\), and \(F=\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(A,B)\) of degree \(D\).
Write \(w_Q=v_Q(z)\log NQ/D\), with \(v_Q\) the integer valuation at a prime ideal of \(F\).
Their sum tends to \(\log 2\).
Put \(h_*=(\log 2)/d_A+h(B)\), using absolute logarithmic height; this tends to zero since \(h(B)\le b\).
Uniformly over all rational primes \(p\), summed together, one has
\[
  \sum_p\ \sum_{Q\mid p}w_Q
     \frac{[F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]-1}{D-1}
     \ll \sqrt{h_*\log(1/h_*)}.
\]
Here the local degree is absolute; a large contribution at a single rational prime requires a place of small absolute degree relative to \(D\).

Indeed \(h(x)\le a\log A/d_A+h(B)\le h_*\), since \(A\) is Salem and \(x<2\).
For any two distinct conjugates \(x_j,x_k\), their quotient has infinite order, because an embedding carrying the first to the distinguished strictly dominant \(x\) makes the second strictly smaller in modulus.
Its height is at most \(2h_*\).
The common ideal of \(2-x_j\) and \(2-x_k\) divides that of \(1-x_j/x_k\) since the roots are units.
The [small-height norm inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) bounds the normalized log norm of each such gcd by the quantity on the right up to an absolute factor.
Average this bound over ordered distinct conjugate embeddings into a common normal closure.
At a fixed prime above \(p\) in that closure, exactly \([F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]\) of the \(D\) embeddings induce \(Q\), by the field decomposition of \(F\otimes\mathbb Q_p\).
When both embeddings induce the same place, their absolute valuations of the translate agree.
The contribution of the first embedding at \(Q\) is then retained with conditional probability \(([F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]-1)/(D-1)\).
Summing with the absolute local norm weights and discarding the remaining nonnegative terms gives the bound, as in the [relative-place calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v200.md#records).

<a id="trace"></a>
## Both records are Salem

Now also assume that the later measure \(B\) is the exterior root of a Salem source, of degree \(d_B\), so \(h(B)=b/d_B\).
Write \(L=\mathbb Q(A+A^{-1},B+B^{-1})\), the totally real compositum of the two trace fields.
On sufficiently late rows \(F/L\) is biquadratic and its four automorphisms independently invert \(A\) and \(B\).
Indeed the [relative inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v204.md#salem) fixes \(A,B+B^{-1}\) and inverts \(B\).
As \(L(A)=\mathbb Q(A,B+B^{-1})\), the extension \(F/L(A)\) has degree two.
Also \(A\notin L\), since \(A\) has nonreal conjugates, and its trace gives a quadratic equation over \(L\).
Both quadratic equations split upon adjoining the roots, so \(F/L\) is Galois of degree four with the stated sign changes.

Up to total weight \(O(\sqrt{h_*\log(1/h_*)})\), all prime weight of \(z\) can be retained on one prime per completely split quartet over \(L\).
For a nonidentity automorphism the images of \(x\) are distinct, since \(F=\mathbb Q(x)\).
The gcd estimate in the first section applies also to automorphisms moving the earlier root.
Apply the [orbit selection argument](Yin_C_G0901_v204.md#splitting) to these three gcds.
In a split quartet one has \([F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]=[L_{\mathfrak l}:\mathbb Q_p]\) for each \(Q\) above \(\mathfrak l\).
For instance, the total weight above any set of rational primes for which **every** local factor of \(L\) has degree at least \(\delta[L:\mathbb Q]\) is \(O_\delta(\sqrt{h_*\log(1/h_*)})\) for fixed \(\delta>0\).
This follows directly from the first section and \([F:L]=4\), and includes primes inert in \(L\).

<a id="conic"></a>
## An exact trace equation for the prime support

Put \(T=A^a+A^{-a}\), \(S=B+B^{-1}\), and \(W=N_{F/L}(2-A^aB)\).
The four conjugates give the identity
\[
 W=4T^2-10TS+4S^2+9,\qquad
 4W=(4T-5S)^2-9(S^2-4)=(4S-5T)^2-9(T^2-4).
\]
Indeed the product for \(u=A^aB,u^{-1}\) is \(5-2(u+u^{-1})\), and the other pair is obtained by replacing \(B\) by \(B^{-1}\).
The two traces in these factors have sum \(TS\) and product \(T^2+S^2-4\).
In particular a rational prime divides the absolute norm exactly when this conic equation vanishes modulo an actual prime of \(L\).

Let \(\mathfrak l\mid p\) divide \(W\), with \(p>3\).
If \(T^2-4\) and \(S^2-4\) are both nonzero modulo \(\mathfrak l\), the two square identities make them both nonzero squares in the residue field.
Hensel lifting makes both quadratics split over \(L_{\mathfrak l}\).
Here \(L(A^a)=L(A)\): every positive power of a Salem number preserves its field since its unique exterior conjugate has just one preimage among the conjugates.
Thus \(\mathfrak l\) splits completely, and exactly one prime of \(F\) above it divides \(z\).
In fact if one signed product is \(2\) in the residue field, inversion of just one coordinate could preserve the value only at a double root of its quadratic.
Inversion of both would give \(2^{-1}=2\), impossible for \(p>3\).
The valuation of \(z\) at the unique contributing prime then equals \(v_{\mathfrak l}(W)\).
The exceptional residue pairs occur only at \(S=\pm2,\ T=5S/4\), or \(T=\pm2,\ S=5T/4\), in the residue field.
This computation and the local-degree estimate still allow the conic to vanish to arbitrarily large depth at small local factors above a fixed rational prime.
