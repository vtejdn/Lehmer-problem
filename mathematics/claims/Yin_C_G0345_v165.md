# When a short modular lift keeps all exponents on one line

<a id="statement"></a>
## A doubling threshold for the exponents

Let \(S\subset\mathbb Z\) have \(k\ge2\) elements, with minimum zero and greatest common divisor one.
Write \(S+S=\{s+t:s,t\in S\}\).
In any [modular lift](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#compression) write \(s=H x_s+N y_s\) with \(x_s,y_s\in\mathbb Z\), \(H\ge2\), \((N,H)=1\), and put \(R=\max y_s-\min y_s\).
If \(2R<H\) and
\[
                          |S+S|<3k-3,                               \tag{1}
\]
there are integers \(u,v\) with \(Hu+Nv=1\) such that
\[
                    (x_s,y_s)=(x_0,y_0)+s(u,v)
                       \qquad(s\in S).                              \tag{2}
\]
In particular this holds for every normalized support in \([0,d]\) with \(k>(2d+4)/3\).
It also holds for every two-point normalized support, directly by (2).
Thus a polynomial with such a support lifts, up to a monomial, to \(P(X^uY^v)\).
For an irreducible primitive integral \(P\) with \(P(0)\ne0\), the sum of the absolute logarithmic heights of **all** the roots in each torsion fiber of this lift is exactly \(m(P)\), for every torsion order.
Consequently summing more relative orbits inside one such fiber supplies exactly the original logarithmic measure even if the number of orbits grows.

<a id="edges"></a>
## Equal pair sums and a planar triangulation

Put \(A=\{(x_s,y_s):s\in S\}\).
The projection \((x,y)\mapsto Hx+Ny\) maps \(A+A\) onto \(S+S\).
If \(s+t=s'+t'\), the differences of the corresponding \(y\)-sums are divisible by \(H\), and have absolute value at most \(2R<H\).
Thus the \(y\)-sums agree, and the \(x\)-sums agree as well.
This gives \(|A+A|=|S+S|\).

The planar case of [Freiman's dimension lemma, Lemma 1.14](https://doi.org/10.1090/mmono/037) gives the needed bound on a noncollinear sumset.
To see it directly, if \(A\) is noncollinear, triangulate its convex hull using every point of \(A\) as a vertex, subdividing edges at any intervening points.
Such a triangulation is obtained by first triangulating the extreme vertices and then inserting each remaining point into its face or edge.
With \(b\) boundary vertices, Euler's formula and the edge count of the triangular faces give \(3k-3-b\ge2k-3\) edges.
Their midpoints are distinct and disjoint from the vertices because the triangulation has disjoint open edges containing no vertices.
Twice these midpoints and twice the \(k\) vertices give at least \(3k-3\) different elements of \(A+A\).
This contradicts (1).

On a line the projection is nonconstant since \(S\) has at least two elements.
Hence the coordinates of its inverse are affine rational functions of \(s\).
Their slopes are integers because all coordinate differences from \(s=0\) are integers and the positive \(s\) have gcd one.
Projection gives \(Hu+Nv=1\), so \(v\ne0\).
For \(S\subset[0,d]\) the sumset has at most \(2d+1\) elements, giving the stated density threshold.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## What the torsion fibers measure

Write \(P(Z)=\sum_{s\in S}a_s Z^s\).
Equation (2) gives \(\sum_{s\in S}a_s X^{x_s}Y^{y_s}=X^{x_0}Y^{y_0}P(X^uY^v)\).
For a root of unity \(\xi\) and each root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), the equation \(\xi^uY^v=\alpha\) gives \(|v|\) distinct nonzero roots, each of height \(h(\alpha)/|v|\) by invariance under torsion and the power rule for height.
The roots from different \(\alpha\) are disjoint.
For primitive irreducible \(P\) of degree \(d\), the sum is \(d h(\alpha)=m(P)\) by [the height normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization).
The one-variable archimedean Mahler measure of each such fiber is likewise \(m(P)\), by invariance of circle measure under rotations and nonzero power maps.

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Equality in the doubling threshold

Take \(h\ge2\), \(H=2h-1\), \(N=1\), and
\[
                   S=[0,h-1]\ \cup\ [H,H+h-1]
\]
with integer intervals.
The lift consists of the two rows \((x,y)=(0,j),(1,j)\), \(0\le j<h\), so it is noncollinear and \(2R=2h-2<H\).
The three sum intervals start at \(0,H,2H\) and each contains \(2h-1=H\) integers.
Consequently \(|S+S|=3(2h)-3\) and \(k=(2d+4)/3\), with \(d=3h-2\).
Both strict thresholds are attained by a noncollinear lift in the stated range of \(R/H\).
