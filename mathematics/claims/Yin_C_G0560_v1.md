### 1. Original collision mass in an explicit typical lattice box

Let alpha be a nonzero algebraic integer with actual monic irreducible polynomial P of degree d.
Algebraic units, both torsion and nontorsion, are included.
Put m=log M(P)>=0.
Let tau:Q(alpha)->R^d be the real Minkowski embedding: each real embedding supplies one coordinate, and each nonreal conjugate pair supplies the real and imaginary parts of one representative.
Every coordinate i is assigned the modulus rho_i of that conjugate; a complex pair has two coordinates with the same modulus.
Write q for the number of coordinates with rho_i=1.

Define the real invertible matrix B by its columns tau(1),tau(alpha)...,tau(alpha^(d-1)), and set

    Delta=|det B|>0,
    a_i=(1/2) sum_(j=0)^(d-1) |B_(i,j)|.             (1)

Invertibility is the usual separability/Vandermonde fact, after the invertible conversion of conjugate complex coordinates to real and imaginary parts.
Thus Lambda=tau(Z[alpha])=B Z^d is a full lattice.
The half-open parallelepiped B[-1/2,1/2)^d has volume Delta and lies in the coordinate box with half-widths a_i.
Its lattice translates are disjoint up to measure-zero boundaries.

Let

    W_n=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) xi_j alpha^j,
    p_n=Pr{W_n=W_n'},                                  (2)

where all xi_j and their primed copies are independent fair bits.
No digit law is changed.
Center tau(W_n) at its actual mean mu_n; that mean need not be a lattice point.

For rho_i!=1, the corresponding centered coordinate is bounded deterministically by

    b_i(n)=rho_i^n/[2(rho_i-1)]    if rho_i>1,
    b_i(n)=1/[2(1-rho_i)]         if rho_i<1.            (3)

Indeed every centered digit has absolute value1/2, and each real or imaginary coordinate of tau(alpha^j) is bounded by rho_i^j.
Sum the geometric series.
For a neutral coordinate, independence and centering give variance at most n/4.
If q>=1, Chebyshev at radius sqrt(qn), followed by a union bound over the q coordinates, shows that all neutral coordinates simultaneously have magnitude at most sqrt(qn) with probability at least3/4.
If q=0 no random restriction is needed and the probability is one.
Correlations between different embedding coordinates are allowed throughout.

Let C_alpha be the explicit positive constant

    C_alpha=Delta^(-1)
      product_(rho_i>1) [1/(rho_i-1)+2a_i]
      product_(rho_i<1) [1/(1-rho_i)+2a_i]
      product_(rho_i=1) [2(sqrt(q)+a_i)].              (4)

The final product is empty when q=0.
For n>=1 the box just constructed contains at most

    C_alpha exp(nm) n^(q/2)                            (5)

lattice points.
To check this with an arbitrary center mu_n, translate the fundamental parallelepiped by every lattice point in the box.
Their disjoint volumes add, and their union lies in the box enlarged by a_i in coordinate i.
For expanding coordinates,

    2[b_i(n)+a_i]
       <=[1/(rho_i-1)+2a_i] rho_i^n;

for contracting coordinates the corresponding factor is exactly bounded by the factor in(4).

Division by Delta proves(5).
No projected coordinate is treated as an integer, and the actual order Z[alpha] is not replaced by a larger ring without its covolume.

    p_n >= c_alpha exp(-nm) n^(-q/2),

The cap by one is harmless and convenient for n=0 comparisons.

### 2. Stationary classes need not contain zero

    I(pi,Q)=sum_x pi(x) D(q_x||p),  0<=I<=log4.         (4)

    I_C>=log z-pi_C(x)log F_x(z)>=log z.               (5)

### 5. A strict arithmetic profile at every nonzero state

Now assume m=log M(alpha)>0.
There is at least one expanding embedding sigma, with rho=|sigma(alpha)|>1.

    L_(sigma,x)=floor(1/[(rho-1)|sigma(x)|])+1,
    kappa_x=min_(rho>1) cos(pi/(L_(sigma,x)+1)).       (16)

    u_(n,x)<=kappa_x p_n.                             (17)

Here is a complete proof.
Set v=(1-alpha^n)x, which is nonzero.
Partition the finite support of the binary law nu_n into cosets modulo the infinite cyclic additive subgroup Z v.
On each such coset the nonnegative values of nu_n form a finite sequence.
Break its support into consecutive nonzero runs.
If one run has l positions, its endpoint embedding distance is (l-1)|sigma(v)|.
The diameter of the entire binary support at this embedding is at most sum_(j=0)^(n-1)rho^j=(rho^n-1)/(rho-1), whereas

    |sigma(v)|=|1-sigma(alpha)^n| |sigma(x)|
               >=(rho^n-1)|sigma(x)|.

Therefore l<=L_(sigma,x).

    sum_j b_j b_(j+1)<=cos(pi/(L_(sigma,x)+1))sum_j b_j^2.

Sum over the disjoint runs and cosets.
