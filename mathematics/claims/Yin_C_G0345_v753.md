# Large prime divisors of the exponent indices forced by a pole reduction

<a id="statement"></a>
## Consequences for a shrinking four-term Salem sequence

In the [critical four-term Salem setting](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) pass to a subsequence with fixed pole \(B>1\) and fixed sign \(\epsilon\).
Write \(E=\mathbb Q(B)\), \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), \(u=a/g\), \(v=b/g\), \(\beta=\alpha^g\), and \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\).
At a fixed finite prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(E\), let \(p\) be the residue characteristic, \(q=|\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak p|\), and \(v_{\mathfrak p}\) the additive valuation.
Each applicable row forces the largest prime divisor of the indicated positive index \(N\) to tend to infinity.

| Pole at \(\mathfrak p\) | Index \(N\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(v_{\mathfrak p}(B)>0\) | \(v+u\) |
| \(v_{\mathfrak p}(B)<0\) | \(v-u\) |
| \(v_{\mathfrak p}(B)=0\) and \(B\equiv 1\) or \(-1\bmod\mathfrak p\) | \(v\) |

There is also an order restriction when the number of distinct prime divisors is bounded.
For \(M=2N\) write \(M'=M/p^{v_p(M)}\) and
\[
                 \kappa_q(M)=\frac{M'}{\operatorname{ord}_{M'}(q)},
                 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_1(q)=1.
\]
For every fixed \(t\), on the indices with at most \(t\) distinct prime divisors of \(M\) one has, eventually,
\[
                         \kappa_q(M)\gg_{B,\mathfrak p,t,C} M/d,         \tag{1}
\]
where \(C\) is the critical width bound.
In particular \(M/d\to\infty\) here.
For every rational pole \(B=m/n>1\) in lowest positive terms, primes dividing \(m\) and \(m+n\) therefore force both \(v+u\) and \(v\) to have greatest prime divisor tending to infinity.
When \(n>1\), a prime dividing \(n\) forces the same conclusion for \(v-u\).
At \(B=2\) one can use \(p=q=2\) for \(v+u\) and \(p=q=3\) for \(v\), for either sign.
The general table asserts its conclusions only for the indicated residue behavior at an actual prime of the pole field.
For example, the largest root of \(Z^3-2Z^2-Z+1\) is a totally real algebraic number \(B>1\) with \(B,B-1,B+1\) all units.
The cubic has no rational root, changes sign on \((-1,0),(0,1),(2,3)\), and has values of absolute value one at \(0,1,-1\), giving these unit assertions by norms.
Such a pole has none of the three reductions; this example is only a pole-field calculation.

<a id="periods"></a>
## Covering a finite-field divisor by short binomials

Let \(M\ge1\) be an integer, \(q\) a power of a prime \(p\), and \(A\) a monic divisor of \(X^M-1\) in \(\mathbb F_q[X]\) with \(\deg A\le d\), \(d\ge1\).
Let \(T=\omega(M)\), the number of distinct prime divisors of \(M\), and suppose \(W=M/(p\kappa_q(M)d)>1\) and \(T\ge1\).
Then \(A\) divides a product of at most \(T\) polynomials \(X^{n_i}-1\) with
\[
                         n_i\le M W^{-1/T}.                             \tag{2}
\]
Consider an irreducible factor of \(A\) of degree \(f\) and multiplicity \(j\), and let \(r\) be the multiplicative order of any root.
Then \(r\mid M'\) and \(f=\operatorname{ord}_r(q)\) by the finite-field Frobenius orbit.
The quotient \(\operatorname{ord}_{M'}(q)/\operatorname{ord}_r(q)\) is the order of \(q^{\operatorname{ord}_r(q)}\) in the kernel of reduction of residue-class units from modulus \(M'\) to \(r\).
That kernel has at most \(M'/r\) elements, by counting residues in a single congruence class modulo \(r\).
Consequently \(r\le\kappa_q(M) f\).
The least exponent of a binomial \(X^n-1\) divisible by the full \(j\)-th power of this irreducible factor is
\[
                       L=r\,p^{\lceil\log_p j\rceil}.
\]
Indeed the prime-to-\(p\) part of the binomial is separable and each of its roots has multiplicity \(p^{v_p(n)}\).
Thus \(L\mid M\), and \(L\le p rj\le p\kappa_q(M)d\), using \(fj\le d\).
In particular \(M/L\ge W\).

For some prime \(\ell\mid M\) one has \(\ell^{v_\ell(M/L)}\ge W^{1/T}\).
For each \(\ell\mid M\) use the least positive integer \(b_\ell\) with \(\ell^{b_\ell}\ge W^{1/T}\), omitting the primes with \(b_\ell>v_\ell(M)\).
The prime just found was not omitted, and its \(n_\ell=M/\ell^{b_\ell}\) is a multiple of \(L\).
The binomial of this exponent therefore absorbs the full irreducible power under consideration.
Using all the indicated primes covers every irreducible power in \(A\) and proves (2).
The primes dividing \(M\) may vary between indices.

For later use, if \(M\) has prime divisors in a fixed finite set \(S\), then \(\kappa_q(M)\) is bounded in terms of \(q,S\).
Indeed for each odd \(\ell\in S\) different from \(p\), put \(h_\ell=v_\ell(q^{\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)}-1)\).
The order modulo \(\ell^b\) is divisible by \(\ell^{(b-h_\ell)_+}\), by the binomial theorem and the increase by one in valuation on raising an element of \(1+\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\) to its \(\ell\)-th power.
If \(\ell=2\ne p\), use \(q^2\equiv1\bmod8\) and \(h_2=v_2(q^2-1)\); successive squarings give the same lower divisor \(2^{(b-h_2)_+}\) for the order of \(q^2\), hence for that of \(q\).
For each prime dividing \(M'\) the indicated prime power therefore divides \(\operatorname{ord}_{M'}(q)\).
These divisors are pairwise coprime across different primes, giving \(\kappa_q(M)\le\prod_{\ell\in S,\ell\ne p}\ell^{h_\ell}\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Local divisibility of a projected minimal polynomial

The [common-exponent estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v747.md#statement) gives \(g=O(1)\) and \(u\to\infty\); also \(v/u=b/a\) and \(v/(ud)\) stays bounded above and bounded away from zero by [the four-term limits](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement).
Thus each of \(v+u,v-u,v\) tends to infinity with \(d=o(N)\).
One also has \((u+v)h(\beta)=(a+b)h(\alpha)\le C\), where \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
In particular \(u h(\beta)\to0\) and \(M h(\beta)\le 2C\).

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) over \(E\).
The root is a unit, as is its inverse, so \(P\in\mathcal O_E[X]\) and its constant term is a unit.
Its degree is at most \(d\).
It divides
\[
            (X^{u+v}-\epsilon)-B(X^v-\epsilon X^u)
\]
over \(E\).
The same divisibility holds at the valuation ring of \(\mathfrak p\) for any integral scaling of this expression, by monic division.
In the first table row its reduction is \(X^{v+u}-\epsilon\).
In the second row scale by \(B^{-1}\); the resulting reduction is \(-X^u(X^{v-u}-\epsilon)\).
The reduction of \(P\) has nonzero constant term, so the monomial can be removed.
In both cases \(\overline P\mid X^{2N}-1\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), including characteristic two.

In the last row choose \(\delta=\pm1\) representing \(B\bmod\mathfrak p\).
The reduction factors as \((X^u-\delta)(X^v+\epsilon\delta)\), which divides \((X^{2u}-1)(X^{2v}-1)\).
Taking the monic gcd of \(\overline P\) with \(X^{2u}-1\) leaves a quotient dividing \(X^{2v}-1\).
This uses multiplicities and remains valid even when the two binomials share roots.
Thus it suffices in that row to adjoin the one short binomial \(X^{2u}-1\) to any cover of the remaining monic divisor of \(X^{2N}-1\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Finite-place norm cost of a bounded number of short periods

Suppose a product \(Q=\prod_i(X^{n_i}-1)\) with positive exponents is divisible modulo \(\mathfrak p\) by \(\overline P\).
Dividing \(Q\) by the monic \(P\) over the local valuation ring yields a remainder with coefficients in its maximal ideal.
For every place \(w\mid\mathfrak p\) of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), integrality of \(\beta\) therefore gives \(|Q(\beta)|_w\le p^{-1/e_{\mathfrak p}}\), where \(e_{\mathfrak p}=e(E_{\mathfrak p}/\mathbb Q_p)\) and \(|p|_w=p^{-1}\).
Here \(E\subseteq K\) holds by the exact pole equation, and all factors at \(\beta\) are nonzero.
With the normalized local weights of \(K\), summing \(-\log|Q(\beta)|_w\) over just these places gives at least
\[
             \frac{[E_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb Q_p]\log p}{[E:\mathbb Q]e_{\mathfrak p}}
                        =\frac{\log q}{[E:\mathbb Q]}>0.                \tag{3}
\]
All the finite-place contributions are nonnegative and add across the binomials, since \(\beta\) is a unit.
By the [small-height cyclic norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement), \(L_1(\beta^{n_i})\to0\) whenever \(n_i h(\beta)\to0\).
In particular (3) forbids a bounded number of binomials with all \(n_i=o(M)\), since \(M h(\beta)\le2C\).
The extra exponent \(2u\) in the last row also has vanishing height cost.

If \(\omega(M)\le t\) and \(\kappa_q(M)d/M\to0\) on a subsequence, then \(W\to\infty\) in (2), yielding exactly such a forbidden product.
This proves (1).
The implied lower constant depends only on the listed local data, \(t\) and \(C\): the upper envelope in v730 depends only on the height of each binomial argument, \(n_i h(\beta)\le2C W^{-1/t}\), while \(L_1(\beta^{2u})\to0\) can be made smaller than half of (3) by taking the index sufficiently large.
If the greatest prime divisor of \(N\) were bounded on an infinite subsequence, then \(M=2N\) there would have its prime divisors in a fixed finite set.
Both \(\omega(M)\) and \(\kappa_q(M)\) would be bounded, contradicting (1) and \(M/d\to\infty\).
This proves the table and its integer-pole specializations.
