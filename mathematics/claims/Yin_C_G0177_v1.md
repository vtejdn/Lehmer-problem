## Exact theorem statement

Let P be a monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion polynomial in Z[z], of even degree d=2r and constant coefficient1.
Let alpha be a root, K=Q(alpha) and F=Q(alpha+alpha^(-1)), and assume F is totally real.
Use the actual nonzero integral trace quotients a,b,c of C153v2 and their defects e=b^2-a^3, f=2c^2-3a^5 and g=c^3-2b^5, which may individually vanish.
Define

    H23=9P(z^2)^3-8P(z^3)^2,
    H25=75P(z^2)^5-64P(z^5)^2,
    H35=250P(z^3)^5-243P(z^5)^3,
    E=-z^(-3d)H23/72, F_0=-z^(-5d)H25/800,
    G=-z^(-15d/2)H35/30375,
    A_0=(||H23||_1/72)^2+(||H25||_1/800)^2,
    G_1=||H35||_1/30375, J_G=integral_T log G^2 dmu,

where mu is normalized circle Haar measure.
For any rational c>sup_T G^2 and integer N>=0, let B_N be the actual rational polynomial-shear symbol

    B_N=(E^2+F_0^2)(1-G^2/c)^(2N+2)+2G^2,
    I_N=integral_T log B_N dmu,
    L_N=(1/d)log Norm_K B_N(alpha), n=N+1.

The polynomial-shear identity and full norm floor2^d are the exact C176 interfaces, with the dependence on this general c justified in the argument below.
All original embeddings, degrees and rational normalizations are retained.

**A. Actual original-conjugate asymptotics.**
Formula(T1) is the exact mean over all r trace embeddings.
If g=0 it is constant as stated there; if e=f=0 it equals(T2) for every N.
In every remaining case, partitioning the original trace embeddings by g_i^2<2c, g_i^2=2c and g_i^2>2c gives exactly(T3), including its explicit nonnegative exponential remainder and actual rate gamma_c.
The coefficient-paid choice c_*=1+G_1^2 R^(15d), R=1+max_(j<d)|p_j|, contains every original conjugate and removes all expanding and boundary embeddings.
When g!=0 its limiting original/Haar discrepancy is exactly

    (2/r)log|Norm_F g|-J_G
       =2[(1/d)log|Res(P,H35)|-m(H35)].

The g=0 case has the separate constant original mean and makes no use of a zero norm or resultant logarithm.
The choice c_* pays its actual height and degree cost and is not asserted minimal.

**B. Necessary Fourier expense for every near-infimum shear sequence.**
Assume additionally that G has at least one circle zero.
For every sequence of real or rational polynomial pairs S_j,T_j whose symbols

    B_(S_j,T_j)=(E+S_j(z+z^(-1))G)^2
                 +(F_0+T_j(z+z^(-1))G)^2+2G^2

have Haar mean tending to log2+J_G, one has W_*(log B_(S_j,T_j))->infinity, where W_*(f)=sum_(k!=0)|fhat(k)| with fhat(k)=integral_T f(z)z^(-k)dmu and infinity is allowed.
This is a conclusion about actual logarithms and not a degree estimate.
It concerns the analytic real-parameter class as well; its arithmetic norm theorem is only invoked on the rational-coefficient domain.

**C. Explicit lower rate and actual controls.**
Under the same hypothesis as part B that G has at least one circle zero, put gamma_0=(7/30375)^2 and n_0=ceil(max{16,(2c/gamma_0)^2}).
For every integer n>=n_0, the actual geometric family satisfies

    W_*(log B_(n-1))
       >=log n-log log n+log2+J_G-log(A_0+2c).

Both actual J97 controls P=z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1 and P=z^10+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1 have a circle zero of this G, by the exact integer evaluations in the complete argument.
No root approximation, parameter scan or new integral is used for that assertion.
These results do not exclude finite-degree nonoptimal savings, do not construct a near-zero-measure input sequence, and do not establish a degree-uniform transfer or covering Mahler gap.

### General positive c and the full original-field average

The geometric identities of L438 hold for ANY rational c>sup_circle G^2, with the same A_0 and with c=a/b in its exact denominator and endpoint formulas.
The proof there used the particular rational choice1+G_1^2 only to guarantee this strict circle bound.
Replacing it by another rational c satisfying the displayed inequality preserves the finite polynomial identities, full norm proof, exact degree, denominator, coefficient upper bound and error proof with that actual c.
Here G_1=||H35||_1/30375.
This use follows from those actual proof steps rather than silently changing the printed choice in L438.

Put n=N+1 and enumerate all r embeddings sigma_i of the actual trace field F into R.
Let

    e_i=sigma_i(e), f_i=sigma_i(f), g_i=sigma_i(g),
    a_i=e_i^2+f_i^2,
    u_i=|1-g_i^2/c|,
    L_N=(1/d)log Norm_K(B_N(alpha)).

The full original norm, with d=2r, is exactly

    B_N,i=a_i u_i^(2n)+2g_i^2,
    L_N=(1/r)sum_(i=1)^r log B_N,i.                 (T1)

The factor1/r follows because Norm_K B_N=Norm_F B_N squared and d=2r; no original conjugate has been omitted.
All terms are positive by the same two arithmetic cases as L438.

If g=0 as a field element, every g_i=0 and the pairwise exclusions give e,f nonzero.
Then B_N,i=a_i for every N, and L_N=(1/r)log Norm_F(e^2+f^2) is constant.
If e=f=0 as field elements, the pairwise exclusions give g!=0, every a_i=0 and

    L_N=log2+(2/r)log|Norm_F g|                    (T2)

for every N.
These global cases cannot be replaced by a different vanishing choice at different embeddings.

In the remaining case g!=0 and (e,f)!=(0,0), every g_i is nonzero and every a_i>0.
Partition the embeddings into

    I_>={i:g_i^2>2c}, I_={i:g_i^2=2c},
    I_<={i:0<g_i^2<2c}.

Define

    gamma_c=(2/r)sum_(i in I_>)log(g_i^2/c-1),
    K_c=(1/r)[sum_(I_>)log a_i
                   +sum_(I_=)log(a_i+2g_i^2)
                   +sum_(I_<)log(2g_i^2)].

Then the exact large-N expansion, with a positive controlled remainder, is

    L_N=n gamma_c+K_c+E_N,
    0<=E_N<=(1/r)[sum_(I_>) (2g_i^2/a_i)u_i^(-2n)
                   +sum_(I_<) (a_i/(2g_i^2))u_i^(2n)].
                                                           (T3)

Indeed factor a_i u_i^(2n) in each expanding term and2g_i^2 in each contracting term of (T1), and use0<=log(1+x)<=x.
At u_i=1 the term is exactly a_i+2g_i^2 and contributes no remainder.
At u_i=0 the contracting remainder is exactly zero because n>=1.
Thus E_N tends to zero, and L_N/n tends to gamma_c.
This is the actual original norm growth rate; it is not an upper bound obtained from the polynomial width.
If at least one g_i^2>2c, gamma_c>0 and the original mean grows linearly while its Haar counterpart tends to the fixed log2+J_G.
If every g_i^2<2c, the limit is (T2), including the already separated e=f=0 case.
The equality case2c has the additional exact terms displayed in K_c.

### A coefficient-paid choice avoiding expanding original embeddings

Write P(z)=z^d+sum_(j<d)p_j z^j and put

    R=1+max_(j<d)|p_j|,
    c_*=1+G_1^2 R^(15d).                            (T4)

Every original conjugate beta and its inverse have modulus at most R.
For the first assertion, if |beta|>R the lower leading term |beta|^d exceeds sum_(j<d)|p_j||beta|^j by the geometric-series bound, contradicting P(beta)=0.
The reciprocal polynomial gives the same conclusion for beta^(-1).
The centered support of G is [-15d/2,15d/2], so |G(beta)|<=G_1 R^(15d/2).
Every g_i is obtained from a pair of these original values.
Consequently c_* is rational, exceeds the circle maximum and satisfies g_i^2<c_* at every embedding.

For g!=0 the construction with this actual c_* has no expanding or boundary embedding and

    lim_(N->infinity) L_N=log2+(2/r)log|Norm_F g|,
    lim_(N->infinity) [L_N-I(S_N,T_N)]
       =(2/r)log|Norm_F g|-J_G
       =2[(1/d)log|Res(P,H35)|-m(H35)].             (T5)

The last identity uses the exact centered G(alpha)=-alpha^(-15d/2)H35(alpha)/30375, Norm_K alpha=1 and Norm_K g=(Norm_F g)^2.
Thus (2/r)log|Norm_F g|=(2/d)log|Res(P,H35)|-2log30375, and the same scalar cancels against J_G=2m(H35)-2log30375.
The resultant is nonzero here because g!=0.
No division by a smaller own degree or primitive-content normalization occurs.
When g=0, (T1) gives the constant original mean instead; no logarithm of a zero norm or resultant is taken.

This choice controls the actual exponential growth, but it pays the height-and-degree factor R^(15d) in c_* and therefore in the explicit sufficient N and denominator of L438.
It does not prove that a cheaper c or N is impossible.
For g!=0, a transfer estimate for these near-infimum choices would have to bound the exact original resultant discrepancy in (T5), not merely their circle approximation error.
In particular J_G<=-eta<0 forces the limiting discrepancy to be at least eta because Norm_F g is a nonzero integer.
This is an obstruction to an unpriced transfer for this selected class, not a constructed sequence of original measures tending to zero.

### Approaching the full infimum forces an actual Wiener cost at circle zeros

Let W_*(f)=sum_(k!=0)|fhat(k)| for an integrable real circle function, allowing the value infinity.
Fix an original P for which G has at least one circle zero.
For ANY sequence of polynomial shears S_j,T_j, real or rational, with

    I(S_j,T_j) -> log2+J_G,

one has

    W_*(log B_(S_j,T_j)) -> infinity.               (T6)

This conclusion concerns the actual logarithms, not their degree bounds or a coefficient upper estimate.
Indeed f_j=log B_(S_j,T_j) satisfies f_j>=f_*=log(2G^2) almost everywhere, and

    ||f_j-f_*||_(L1)=I(S_j,T_j)-(log2+J_G) ->0.

If an infinite subsequence had W_*(f_j)<=K, its absolutely summable Fourier series would represent f_j almost everywhere by a continuous function with f_j>=I(S_j,T_j)-K.
For completeness, the absolutely convergent series has the same coefficients as f_j, so their difference has zero Fourier coefficients and every Fejer convolution vanishes.
At any point where B_(S_j,T_j)>0 that difference is continuous on a neighborhood and is integrable on the whole circle.
The Fejer kernel of order m is (1/m)|sum_(a=0)^(m-1)exp(iat)|^2, whose constant coefficient and Haar integral are1 by character orthogonality.
Outside an arc of radius delta its supremum is at most1/[m sin(delta/2)^2], by the finite geometric sum and squared-sine formula.
Local continuity controls the near integral, while this vanishing supremum times the finite L1 norm controls the complement.
The Fejer convolutions therefore converge to the difference's value at every such point, proving equality there and hence almost everywhere.
This is the elementary uniqueness mechanism already used in [C171](Yin_C_G0171_v1.md#two-block-singular-phase-divergence), with the explicit integrable-tail bound replacing its bounded-tail specialization.
The bounded convergent means would therefore give a common finite lower bound on that subsequence.
L1 convergence implies that its limit f_* has the same lower bound almost everywhere.
This is impossible: at a zero of the nonzero Laurent polynomial G, log(2G^2) tends to minus infinity through positive-measure arcs.
Thus for every K only finitely many sequence entries can have Wiener norm at most K, proving (T6).
If an individual B has circle zeros, the same continuous-representative argument already makes its Wiener norm infinite.

### Explicit logarithmic lower rate for the geometric family

For the L438 geometric shears, retain any rational c>sup_circle G^2 and set

    gamma_0=(7/30375)^2,
    n_0=ceil(max{16,(2c/gamma_0)^2}).

The leading Laurent coefficient of G has modulus7/30375, hence sup_circle G^2>=gamma_0.
For n>=16, log n<=sqrt(n): this holds at16 and the difference sqrt(n)-log n is increasing for n>=16.
Therefore for every integer n>=n_0,

    0<c log n/n<=gamma_0/2<=sup_circle G^2.

Since G^2 is continuous and has a zero, the intermediate value theorem gives a circle point z_n with G(z_n)^2=c log n/n.
At that point the exact geometric formula and1-x<=exp(-x) give

    B_(n-1)(z_n)
      <=A_0/n^2+2c log n/n
      <=(A_0+2c)log n/n.

If the Wiener norm is finite, absolute Fourier convergence bounds the deviation of this positive point value from the mean; if it is infinite the following inequality holds automatically.
Using I(S_(n-1),T_(n-1))>=log2+J_G yields

    W_*(log B_(n-1))
       >=log n-log log n+log2+J_G-log(A_0+2c)
       for every integer n>=n_0.                  (T7)

There is no simple-root hypothesis or root-location computation in this estimate.
The assertion is a lower bound on the actual Wiener expense for this actual construction, rather than a necessary lower bound on every degree needed for a fixed positive saving.

### Both prescribed controls have the required zero

For the quartic P=z^4-z^3-z^2-z+1, P(1)=-1 and P(-1)=3.
Its centered G has

    G(1)=7/30375>0,
    G(-1)=-27(250*9-243)/30375<0,

so it has a circle zero on an arc joining1 to-1.
For the degree-ten Lehmer input, the same exact substitution gives P(1)=-1 and P(i)=3i.
Since its trace center is r=5, P(i)=i^5 T(0) implies T(0)=3, whereas T(2)=P(1)=-1.
The centered trace formula is

    G_0(x)=-[250T(C_3(x))^5-243T(C_5(x))^3]/30375.

At x=0 both C_3 and C_5 are zero, so G(i)=-27(250*9-243)/30375<0, while G(1)=7/30375>0.
Thus the second control also has a circle zero.
These are exact evaluations of the retained input polynomials; no root isolation or mathematical program was executed.
Consequently both (T6) and (T7) apply to the same controls on which L438 constructs sub-log2 shears.

### Consequence for the continuing transfer question

The original-conjugate formula separates three genuine costs.
An insufficiently large c can give the positive actual growth rate gamma_c in (T3); enlarging c to(T4) avoids that rate but exposes the exact resultant/Haar discrepancy(T5).
On both controls, approaching the class infimum also necessarily incurs the actual Fourier divergence(T6), with the constructive lower rate(T7).
Neither vanishing circle approximation error nor preserving the2^d norm floor gives a free comparison of the two distributions.

No assertion here rules out a fixed nonoptimal saving achieved with finite shear degree or a different arithmetic class carrying a bounded useful transfer.
A covering improvement would have to supply an actual bound on the resultant discrepancy or on the relevant Fourier/radial budget at a useful finite shear choice, with original total m, degree and every denominator retained.
Those arithmetic inputs are not supplied by the present exact asymptotics, and D36 remains open at that obligation.
