# A degree-independent matrix bound for square endpoints and the obstruction to diagonal normalization

<a id="statement"></a>
## All exterior powers at all places

Let \(A\in{\rm GL}_b(K)\) for a number field \(K\), with determinant a root of unity.
Assume that the directed graph \(i\to j\) for \(A_{ij}\ne0\) is strongly connected and that \(A\) is not monomial.
At each place \(v\) choose any invertible diagonal matrix \(D_v\) over \(K_v\), equal to the identity at almost all places, and put \(A_v=D_v A D_v^{-1}\).
Use the usual absolute values extending those of \(\mathbb Q\), weights \(n_v=[K_v:\mathbb Q_v]/[K:\mathbb Q]\), and define
\[
 T_v=\max_{0\le k\le b}\|\textstyle\bigwedge^k A_v\|,\qquad
                   \mathcal T=\prod_v T_v^{\,n_v}.
\]
The norm at infinity is the operator norm for the Euclidean exterior-power inner product; at finite places it is the maximum absolute value of the matrix entries in the exterior basis.
Then for all these choices
\[
                               \mathcal T\ \ge\ \sqrt2 .                 \tag{1}
\]
The estimate uses the whole set of exterior powers and has no field or matrix-size constant.

In particular let \(F\) be an irreducible half of degree \(b\ge3\) in the [relative square-endpoint family](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#statement), with \(K/E\) quadratic over a totally real field of degree \(u\ge2\) and marked positive exterior modulus \(R\).
Its marked root has total measure \(M(R)=R^2\).
Every matrix over \(K\) with characteristic polynomial \(F\) satisfies (1), including after any \(K\)-rational change of basis.
The product using the eigenvalue moduli in place of the singular values at infinity is \(R^{1/u}\), with all finite spectral factors one.
In particular an integral realization that is diagonally similar to a normal matrix at every archimedean place gives \(M(R)\ge 2^u\).
More generally the ratio \(\mathcal T/R^{1/u}\) measures the loss when using these matrix norms and can be computed for any given model and scalings.
Over all \(K\)-rational changes of basis, the infimum of the archimedean part alone is **exactly** \(R^{1/u}\).
Any sequence reaching this archimedean infimum has limiting lower finite contribution at least \(\sqrt2/R^{1/u}\).

At any fixed measure ceiling and all sufficiently large degrees, **no** matrix over the half field with this characteristic polynomial can be made normal by local diagonal similarities at every infinite place, even with denominators and independently chosen similarities at those places.
The proof uses the first and second exterior powers of an eigenbasis.
These results give an absolute lower bound for the matrix expression; they leave the possibility \(R\to1\) for the eigenvalues open.

<a id="minors"></a>
## The complementary minors under a putative small bound

Write \(\Delta_I(v)=\prod_{i\in I}|(D_v)_{ii}|_v\).
For subsets \(I,J\) of equal size \(k\), consider
\[
        B_{IJ}=\det A_{I,J}\det(A^{-1})_{J,I}\in K .
\]
This product is unchanged by the diagonal similarities, and \(\sum_J B_{IJ}=1\) by Cauchy–Binet.
The largest exterior norm of \(A_v^{-1}\), over all orders, is also \(T_v\).
At infinity \(T_v\) is the product of the singular values greater than one, and all singular values have product one.
At a finite place complementary minors of the inverse are signed minors divided by the determinant of absolute value one.
Consequently at infinity, using the \(I\)-th row of \(\bigwedge^k A_v\) and \(I\)-th column of its inverse, the triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities give
\[
       |B_{IJ}|_v+|1-B_{IJ}|_v\le\sum_J |B_{IJ}|_v\le T_v^2 .
\]
Thus \(|B_{IJ}(1-B_{IJ})|_v\le T_v^4/4\).
At a finite place each minor product has absolute value at most \(T_v^2\), and the ultrametric inequality gives the product bound \(T_v^4\) since \(T_v\ge1\).
The archimedean weights sum to one, so if \(\mathcal T<\sqrt2\) the product formula forces every \(B_{IJ}\) to be zero or one.

At order one the row and column sums are one, hence the ones form a permutation \(j=\pi(i)\).
At every order the unique nonzero entry in row \(I\) occurs at \(J=\pi(I)\).
To see this expand the principal minor on \(I\) of \(A\,{\rm diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_b)\,A^{-1}\) as \(\sum_J B_{IJ}\prod_{j\in J}z_j\).
It is a single monomial since the coefficients are zero or one with sum one.
At \(z_1=\cdots=z_b=1\) its derivative in \(z_j\) equals \(\sum_{i\in I} A_{ij}(A^{-1})_{ji}\) by the first-order determinant expansion at the identity.
This is precisely \({\bf1}_{j\in\pi(I)}\), which determines the entire monomial.
In particular \(\det A_{I,\pi(I)}\ne0\) for every \(I\).

<a id="cycle"></a>
## Closing a directed cycle through a nonmatching entry

Set \(d_i=\prod_v |(D_v)_{ii}|_v^{n_v}\).
The product formula for the nonzero minor on \(I,\pi(I)\), bounded after local scaling by \(T_v\), gives
\[
                  \prod_{i\in I}\frac{d_i}{d_{\pi(i)}}\le\mathcal T
                    \quad\text{for every }I .                            \tag{2}
\]
For a nonmatching nonzero entry \(A_{ij}\), the inverse entry \((A^{-1})_{ji}\) vanishes.
At an archimedean place each singular value \(s\) of \(A_v\) lies in \([1/T_v,T_v]\), and the singular value decomposition gives
\[
               |(A_v)_{ij}|\le\|A_v^*-A_v^{-1}\|
                    =\max_s |s-s^{-1}|\le T_v-T_v^{-1}.
\]
At finite places use the bound \(T_v\).
There is at least one nonmatching entry, so the preceding inequality implies \(T_v>1\) at every infinite place.
Write \(T_\infty=\prod_{v\mid\infty}T_v^{n_v}\).
By concavity of \(\log(2\sinh t)\) for \(t>0\), the weighted geometric mean of the archimedean bounds is at most \(T_\infty-T_\infty^{-1}\).
The product formula for \(A_{ij}\) now gives
\[
           d_i/d_j\le(\mathcal T/T_\infty)(T_\infty-T_\infty^{-1})
                      \le\mathcal T-\mathcal T^{-1}.
\]
In a strongly connected finite graph any nonmatching edge occurs in a simple directed cycle, allowing a loop.
Around that cycle the ratios \(d_i/d_j\) multiply to one.
By (2) all the matching edges on it together contribute at most \(\mathcal T\).
If \(\mathcal T<\sqrt2\), each nonmatching edge contributes at most \(\mathcal T-\mathcal T^{-1}<1\), with at least one such edge.
This would give \(1\le\mathcal T(\mathcal T-\mathcal T^{-1})<1\), proving (1).

<a id="places"></a>
## The comparison with eigenvalues and the role of the finite places

Irreducibility of \(F\) forces the support graph of any representing matrix to be strongly connected, since otherwise a permutation of the coordinates makes it properly block triangular.
A strongly connected monomial matrix is supported on a single cycle and has characteristic polynomial \(X^b-c\), with \(|c|=1\) at the mark by the unit constant sign of \(F\).
This contradicts the marked exterior root.
At a nonsplit embedding all eigenvalues are neutral; at the two split embeddings the lists are mutually inverse and at each the product of the exterior moduli is \(R\).
All eigenvalues are algebraic units at finite places.
The product of the \(k\) largest eigenvalue moduli at infinity is bounded by \(\|\bigwedge^k A_v\|\), using the spectral radius.
This gives the normalized spectral lower bound \(R^{2/[K:\mathbb Q]}=R^{1/u}\) for the infinite factors.
If the local matrix is normal, equality holds there for the largest exterior norm.
For integral \(A\) without finite scalings, all finite exterior norms are at most one and \(T_v=1\).
This proves the integral normal-model assertion even when the archimedean diagonal similarities vary.

For each infinite place a simple-spectrum complex matrix is similar to a diagonal matrix.
At a real place one may take a real normal form with real lines and real \(2\)-by-\(2\) rotation-dilation blocks for the nonreal eigenvalues.
Approximate the corresponding finite tuple of invertible similarity matrices by matrices over \(K\), simultaneously at all infinite places.
Such approximation follows from the density of \(\mathbb Q^{[K:\mathbb Q]}\) in the real coefficient space of a rational basis in the Minkowski embedding; invertibility is open.
Continuity of singular values gives the claimed infinite-place infimum, already with identity diagonal scalings there.
Applying (1) to each rationally conjugated matrix gives the asserted lower limit for its finite factors.
In particular arbitrarily accurate approximation to normal forms at infinity by itself provides no upper bound for \(\mathcal T\) close to the eigenvalue product.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Why the two sets of spectral weights cannot both be uniform

Fix the measure ceiling and let the rational degree \(2ub\) of the marked root be sufficiently large.
Its rational minimum is the product of the distinct conjugate half polynomials, with all root sets disjoint. [Half-field norm](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#factors)
Its powers preserve degree and its conjugates are distinct modulo torsion: the unique root of largest modulus \(R\) would otherwise have a second conjugate of that modulus after transport.
The two inverse halves over each embedding of \(E\) give a block of \(b\) inverse-pair labels, and the stabilizer of the chosen half fixes \(K\), since distinct coefficient embeddings give disjoint root sets.
The [canonical alternating action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) on these labels has a unique partition into minimal nontrivial blocks, each of size \(b_0\) tending to infinity and numbering \(u_0\ll(\log(6ub))^{212}\).
Each original block contains one minimal nontrivial block, by descending inclusion, so this partition refines the original one by transitivity and \(u\le u_0\).
There must be a nonreal marked root.
Otherwise both marked halves would consist entirely of real roots, giving a fraction \(1/u\ge1/u_0\) of real embeddings, in contradiction with the [bounded-measure real-place bound](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement) of order \(\sqrt{\log(6ub)/(2ub)}\).

In the full rational splitting field let \(N\) be the normal subgroup generated by the conjugates of ordinary complex conjugation.
At a nonreal marked root of modulus different from one, its conjugate is neither itself nor its inverse.
Thus \(N\) has nontrivial normal image on the labels, containing the unique minimal normal product of alternating groups from the canonical action.
Choose a canonical block \(B_0\) inside the original marked block.
Every supported three-cycle on \(B_0\) has a lift in \(N\).
The lift preserves the entire original block and either fixes or exchanges its two halves.
Its fourth power fixes the chosen half, acts there by the prescribed three-cycle, and fixes the other labels there.
It therefore fixes \(K\).
These elements act transitively on singletons and on unordered pairs in the chosen half over \(B_0\).

Suppose now that a matrix with characteristic polynomial \(F\) is diagonally similar to a normal matrix at every infinite place.
Choose a matrix \(V\) of eigenvectors in the splitting field, with columns indexed by the chosen half.
For coordinate subsets \(I\) and equally sized spectral subsets \(S\) put
\[
                  w_{I,S}=\det V_{I,S}\det(V^{-1})_{S,I}.
\]
These numbers are independent of eigenvector scalings and invariant under diagonal change of the matrix coordinates.
At every embedding their images are nonnegative real numbers: diagonal normalization and eigenvector rescaling allow a unitary eigenbasis there, giving squared absolute minors.
Hence \(N\) fixes every \(w_{I,S}\).
An automorphism over \(K\) transports the columns by the induced root permutation and nonzero column scalars, so it transports the weights with \(S\).
In particular \(w_{\{i\},\{s\}}\) is constant on \(B_0\) for each coordinate row \(i\), and all these weights are positive.
Indeed a zero at one root is transported to zero at every root by irreducibility of \(F\) over \(K\), whereas their sum is one.
Similarly \(w_{\{i,j\},\{s,t\}}\) is constant over distinct \(s,t\) in \(B_0\) for every pair of coordinate rows \(i,j\).

At the chosen embedding use a diagonally normalized unitary eigenbasis \(U\).
Its columns on \(B_0\) have rank \(b_0\), so for some two rows \(i,j\) at least one of the corresponding two-column minors is nonzero.
All such minors with columns in \(B_0\) now have the same positive absolute value.
The entries of row \(j\) on \(B_0\) have a common nonzero modulus.
It follows that the complex numbers \(U_{is}/U_{js}\), \(s\in B_0\), have a common positive pairwise distance, by the two-by-two determinant formula.
At most three points in the Euclidean plane have this property: subtract one point and the Gram matrix of the differences has diagonal the squared distance and off-diagonal half that value, hence full rank \(b_0-1\le2\).
This contradicts \(b_0\ge4\).
The argument uses no integrality of the representing matrix and permits the original half to contain more than one canonical block.
