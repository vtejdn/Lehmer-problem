# A uniform share of the powered-order index at every good auxiliary prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two rows and double roots

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(d\), and suppose that \(\beta=\alpha^p\) retains degree \(d\) for the prime \(p\).
Write \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\).
Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero coprime polynomials over \(\mathbb Q\) with nonzero constant terms and put \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\).
Suppose \(P^2\) divides \(U(x^p)+x^k V(x^p)\) in the Laurent polynomial ring, where \(k\) is an integer prime to \(p\).
This includes the two occupied classes of an ordinary integral witness after stripping their monomial factors.
At every prime \(\ell\nmid p\operatorname{disc}P\),
\[
           v_\ell H\ \ge\ \frac{2}{9}\,v_\ell I_p .                         \tag{1}
\]
If \(2\ell\ge\min(d,p)+1\), the coefficient \(2/9\) can be replaced by \(1/2\).
Both bounds allow constant and inseparable reductions of the row ratio, repeated collisions, and arbitrary row degrees.

<a id="paths"></a>
## A change of residue on a path of disks

Normalize \(v(\ell)=1\).
All conjugates \(\alpha_i\) lift distinct roots of \(P\bmod\ell\) in a finite unramified extension, by simple-root lifting after splitting the reduction.
Thus the distinct \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) have integral mutual distances in the valuation scale.
Inside a single positive-depth disk the values \(U(\beta_i)/V(\beta_i)=-\alpha_i^k\) are units with pairwise distinct residues.
Indeed neither row vanishes there and the quotients of different original residues above the same powered residue are nontrivial \(p\)-th roots of unity.
There are at most \(\min(d,p)\) marked points in that disk.

A disk of depth \(t\) about a marked center \(b\) has common row valuation
\[
 g_b(t)=\min_{j\ge0}\bigl\{v([Z^j]U(b+Z))+jt,\ v([Z^j]V(b+Z))+jt\bigr\}.
\]
At a rational depth, rescale to \(X=b+\tau Y,\ v(\tau)=t\), divide by the common scalar of valuation \(g_b(t)\) and reduce both rows to \(A,B\).
Set \(n=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\) and call a disk nonconstant when \(A/B\) is nonconstant after cancellation.
The zero row is allowed in a proportional pair.
Enlarge the coefficient field for these changes of scale.
On all paths under consideration choose a single generic pencil \(G=U+cV\), and a second \(G'=U+c'V\) with different residue, as in the [two-pencil argument](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#pencil).
At each of finitely many marked centers and each Taylor coefficient index it suffices to avoid cancellation of the smaller valuation when the two valuations coincide.
Then both pencils have valuation function \(g_b(t)\) at every depth and exactly \(n\) roots in each closed disk at rational depth.
This permits finitely many further exclusions on the two residues below.
Off the finitely many breakpoints, \(g_b'(t)\) is the number of pencil roots in that disk, by its linear factors or the minimizing exponent.

For two marked points take the path of concentric disks about the first up to their smallest common disk and back down to the second.
Endpoints may be truncated at sufficiently great finite depths where the row ratios have the endpoint residues.
Some disk on this path is nonconstant.
To prove this, let \(\lambda=U(\beta_i)/V(\beta_i)\) at the first point and compare the valuation function of \(W=U-\lambda V\) with \(g_b\).
Their difference is continuous, nonnegative, positive near the first endpoint and zero near the second.
Here \(W\ne0\) because the residues at the two endpoints differ.
At a boundary of its positive set with zero value, the normalized reduction of \(W\) is \(A-\bar\lambda B\ne0\).
If \(A,B\) are proportional, this reduction has exactly the common polynomial factor of the pair up to a nonzero scalar.
Around that disk, on the side of smaller depth its degree and on every incident side of greater depth its order at the corresponding residue are the same as those of a generic pencil.
Indeed these are respectively the largest and smallest minimizing exponents in the Taylor coefficient formula, after centering in the incident direction.
The difference would therefore stay zero on both adjacent path segments.
The boundary is rational since the functions are piecewise affine with rational intercepts and integer slopes.
This proves the assertion, including a change of center at the joining disk.
There are only finitely many nonconstant disks on the finite union of marked paths, since nonconstancy requires a tie between different coefficient indices in the formula for \(g_b\).

For later use, choose a nonconstant disk on the path for every pair of distinct residues in a closed disk.
The maximal selected disks are disjoint and cover all but at most one of the marked points.
A missing pair would have a selected disk containing at least one of its points.
If every selected disk has separable reduction, the [critical polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#jets) gives \(2n_B-2\ge m_B\) there, where \(m_B\) counts the marked points in \(B\).
For a starting disk with \(m\ge2\) this gives \(2n\ge m+1\), by counting generic pencil roots in the disjoint maximal disks.
If any selected disk is inseparable, its reduced rational function has degree at least \(\ell\), so \(n\ge\ell\).
Consequently \(2n\ge m+1\) whenever \(2\ell\ge\min(d,p)+1\).

<a id="integer"></a>
## What one unit of depth contributes

For an integer \(s\ge1\) let \(D=\{v(X-b)\ge s\}\) contain \(m\ge2\) marked points, and let \(E^+=\{v(X-b)>s-1\}\).
They contain exactly the same marked points.
Write \(a_D=g_b(s)-g_b(s-1)\), the average root count of either generic pencil along this unit of depth.
It is at least \(n_D\) by monotonicity.
When the reduction on \(D\) is nonconstant,
\[
                         m+1\le 2a_D+\log_\ell n_D\le3a_D .                \tag{2}
\]
Here is the change of scale needed in the [mixed characteristic critical estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v372.md#derivative).
First translate at \(b\), rescale at depth \(s-1\), and divide by the common scalar there.
The two polynomials now have integral coefficients, and the second rescaling has depth one and common valuation \(a_D\).
After joint normalization on \(D\) their coefficients of degree \(j\) have valuation at least \(j-a_D\).
The critical polynomial \(K=pX(a'b_1-ab_1')-\tau k ab_1\), for normalized rows \(a,b_1\), still has at least \(m\) zeros in the disk by the double jets.
In this expression \(X=b+\tau Y,\ v(\tau)=s\ge1\), so its coefficient of degree \(j\) has valuation at least \(j+1-2a_D\).
The generic Taylor coefficient of the twisted quotient gives minimum coefficient valuation of \(K\) at most the logarithm to base \(\ell\) of the inseparable degree, at most \(\log_\ell n_D\), by the cited calculation.
Counting the zeros of the normalized critical polynomial proves (2), since \(\log_\ell n_D\le n_D\).
In particular \(m+1\) is at most three times the number of roots of \(G\) in \(E^+\).

<a id="constant"></a>
## Counting the remaining residue changes on a constant disk

Suppose the reduction on the integer-depth disk \(D\) is constant.
Among its proper integer-depth subdisks containing a mark and having nonconstant reduction, take the maximal disks \(B\).
These are finite by the Taylor minimum along every marked path, including the rays to the individual points.
For depth \(s_B\) let \(E_B^+\) be the open disk of depth \(s_B-1\) with the same center.
These open disks lie in \(D\), have the same marks as their respective \(B\), and are pairwise disjoint.
In fact if two intersect, the center of the smaller open disk lies in the larger, hence in its closed integer-depth disk by integrality of the mutual distances, contradicting disjointness of the \(B\).
Let \(R_1\) be the total root count of \(G\) in these open disks.
Equation (2), whose proof also holds for one mark, gives
\[
                            \sum_B(m_B+1)\le3R_1 .                         \tag{3}
\]

Let \(u\) be the number of marks left in \(D\).
For \(u\ge2\) form their finite connecting tree by joining the two truncated ends of every pair as in the path argument.
Every disk on it contains a remaining mark, and every branching point has integer depth, the mutual valuation of two marks.
No integer-depth disk on this tree has nonconstant reduction.
All its nonconstant disks are therefore at fractional depths, where the tree has at most two incident directions.
Deleting their finitely many points leaves at most one more component than the number deleted.
Each component contains at most one marked end by the path argument.

Each deleted point accounts for a different root of \(G\) in \(D\) outside all \(E_B^+\).
In coordinates centered at a remaining mark, every original mark in its fractional disk lies in direction \(Y=0\bmod\mathfrak m\), by integrality of mutual valuations.
The other direction on the path goes outside the disk.
For the nonconstant reduced quotient \(\phi=A/B\) after cancellation, choose \(-\bar c\) different from \(\phi(0)\) and \(\phi(\infty)\) and from the images of the zeros of the cancelled common factor.
A nonconstant rational function over the algebraic closure takes this chosen value, in some finite nonzero direction.
The reduced pencil therefore has a root in this direction and \(G\) has a root there by the normalized linear-factor count, with no separability assumption.
The corresponding open directions are pairwise disjoint as open disks.
Indeed a direction lying off the connecting tree contains no mark, and two such intersecting directions from distinct points of the tree would put the center mark of the deeper point in the shallower direction.
They also miss every \(E_B^+\).
A direction containing \(E_B^+\) would contain its mark, and in the reverse inclusion the fractional disk's center mark would belong to \(E_B^+\), since its valuation from the chosen direction equals the fractional depth while the boundary depth of \(E_B^+\) is integral.
All excluded pencil values form a finite set and can be avoided in one residue field extension.
Thus \(u\le1+n_D-R_1\), also when \(u\le1\).
If any \(B\) was selected, adding (3) gives \(m\le3n_D\).
If none was selected, \(m\le1+n_D\le 3n_D\) for \(m\ge2\).
This proves \(a_D\ge m/3\) also on constant disks.

<a id="sum"></a>
## Summation over primes and the finite degree window

For each integer \(s\ge1\) group the powered nodes according to \(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)\ge s\), retaining groups of size \(m\ge2\).
Only finitely many groups occur.
The [Vandermonde quotient](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#index) gives \(v_\ell I_p=\sum_{s,D}\binom{m_D}{2}\) since all original residues are distinct.
The local intersection of the two pencils pays at least the integral of their common root count squared on each corresponding unit segment, added over all these segments.
Indeed one may subdivide at every root-distance breakpoint on the finitely many paths and apply the [nested-disk inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#length) to the resulting tree.
Above any fixed integer depth the segments for different groups separate immediately, and each pair of pencil roots contributes at most its mutual valuation over the entire tree.
By the integral Cauchy inequality each unit segment pays at least \(a_D^2\ge m_D^2/9\), proving (1).
Under the large-characteristic condition the path bound gives \(n_D^2\ge m_D(m_D-1)/4\) at every end disk and the pointwise count on its incoming segment is at least \(n_D\), proving the second coefficient.

Write \(I_p^{\rm good}\) for the divisor of \(I_p\) supported away from \(p\operatorname{disc}P\).
In the [finite window](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) the primitive adjacent-row witness of degree at most \(p(16d+1)\) with extra measure at most \(2p\,m(P)\) has stripped degree sum at most \(32d+1\).
Combining (1) with the [resultant-to-measure bound](Yin_C_G0345_v366.md#statement) yields
\[
 (2p+2)m(P)\ \ge\
       (1-4/p)\frac{2\log I_p^{\rm good}}{9(32d+1)}
                 -\frac2p\log\!\big(Cp(16d+1)\big).
\]
Thus \(\liminf\log I_p^{\rm good}/(pd)\ge\kappa>0\) gives \(\liminf m(P)\ge\kappa/288\) as \(d\to\infty\) through such inputs.
The argument supplies no positive lower bound on the good part for a general varying input.
For the [Salem family supported at three](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) the repeated original residues require a separate estimate at that prime.
