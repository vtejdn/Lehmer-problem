# Transferring hyperbolic inputs by signed cyclotomic approximation of exterior phases

<a id="statement"></a>
## An angular discrepancy bound for the neutral infimum

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2h>0\), with no unit-circle root, and write \(m=m(P)\) for its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
Write its exterior roots as \(e^{\ell_j+2\pi i\theta_j}\), \(1\le j\le h\), with \(\ell_j>0\) and \(\theta_j\in\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).
Let \(A,B\) be coprime monic products of cyclotomic polynomials, allowing repeated factors and empty products.
For such a product \(V\) let \(\sigma_V\) count its root angles on \(\mathbb T\) with multiplicity.
Define
\[
 \nu=\sigma_A-\sigma_B-\sum_{j=1}^{h}\delta_{\theta_j},\qquad C'=\nu-\nu(\mathbb T)\,dt,\qquad \int_{\mathbb T}C(t)\,dt=0.
\]
Here \(dt\) has total mass one and the derivative is distributional, so \(C\) is the centered periodic cumulative count with its linear drift removed.
Let \(\gamma\) be the infimum of the total logarithmic measures of nontorsion algebraic units having a unit-circle conjugate.
Then
\[
 \gamma\le m+\frac{4\pi^2}{\log4}\int_{\mathbb T}C(t)^2\,dt.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, a sequence with \(m\to0\) transfers to neutral irreducible units of vanishing total measure if signed cyclotomic root counts can be chosen with \(\int C^2\to0\).
All counts here retain their original multiplicities without division by degree.

<a id="denominator"></a>
## A cyclotomic denominator in the quadratic specialization

Put \(u(X)=\pm X^{-h}P(X)\), choosing the sign for which \(u=|P|>0\) on the circle, and write \(V^*(X)=V(X^{-1})\).
Consider the integral Laurent polynomial
\[
 F(X,Y)=AA^*-uBB^*(2-Y-Y^{-1}).
\]
For \(s=|A|^2/(u|B|^2)\), define \(D(s)=0\) on \([0,4]\) and \(D(s)=\operatorname{arcosh}(s/2-1)\) for \(s>4\).
Off the finite zero set of \(AB\), the quadratic \(YF\) has root product one and root sum \(2-s\).
Jensen's formula on that fiber gives \(\log u+2\log|B|+D(s)\).
The positive part of \(\log |F|\) is bounded on the torus and the fiber means are bounded below by the integrable function \(\log u+2\log|B|\), which justifies integration of the negative parts by Tonelli.
Since \(m(B)=0\), the two-variable measure is \(m(F)=m+\int D(s)\,dt\).
The logarithm of \(s\) has integral \(-m<0\) because \(m(A)=m(B)=0\), so there is an open arc avoiding the zeros on which \(0<s<4\).

A vertical component at \(X=\xi\ne0\) would require both \((AA^*)(\xi)=0\) and \((uBB^*)(\xi)=0\), impossible since \(A,B\) are coprime, their root sets are closed under inversion, and \(P\) has no circle roots.
Every nonvertical torsion binomial component is parametrized by \(X=\xi t^a,Y=\eta t^b\) with \(a\ne0\).
At a parameter value making \(X\) a root of \(P\) the value of \(F\) is \((AA^*)(X)\ne0\), excluding the component.
Thus the [torsion-point theorem](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) gives only finitely many torsion pairs on this curve.
On a closed subarc of positive length in the crossing arc, the signs of \(F(X,X^N)\) at successive mesh points with \(X^N=1,-1\) alternate.
The number of distinct circle roots therefore tends to infinity, and only finitely many can be torsion since \(X\mapsto(X,X^N)\) is injective.
For \(N\) sufficiently large, the outer coefficients at \(\pm(N+h+\deg B)\) have absolute value one, coming solely from the shifted extreme terms of \(uBB^*\).
After monomial and sign normalization each such specialization has an irreducible unit factor with a nontorsion circle root, of positive measure at most the whole measure, as in the [numerator transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v605.md#components).
The [scalar specialization limit](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U002.md#luc22-abelian-matrix-approximation) consequently gives \(\gamma\le m+\int D(s)\,dt\).

<a id="fourier"></a>
## The Fourier estimate at the projected roots

Put \(H(X)=\prod_j(X-e^{2\pi i\theta_j})\).
Pairing each exterior root with its inverse complex conjugate gives
\[
 u(e^{2\pi it})=\prod_j(2\cosh\ell_j-2\cos(2\pi(t-\theta_j)))\ge |H(e^{2\pi it})|^2.
\]
Thus for \(x(t)=\log|A/(BH)|^2\), outside a finite set we have \(s\le e^x\).
If \(s>4\), then \(D(s)\le\log s\le x\le x^2/\log4\), and the same final upper bound holds when \(s\le4\).
Use the convention \(\widehat\nu(n)=\int e^{-2\pi int}\,d\nu(t)\).
The kernel \(\log|1-e^{2\pi it}|^2\) has mean zero and Fourier coefficient \(-1/|n|\) at each \(n\ne0\).
Indeed, inserting a radius \(r<1\) gives these coefficients multiplied by \(r^{|n|}\) by the logarithmic series, and their square-summability gives convergence in \(L^2\) to the displayed kernel by pointwise convergence away from zero.
Summing its translates gives \(\widehat x(n)=-\widehat\nu(n)/|n|\) for \(n\ne0\) and \(\widehat x(0)=0\).
Since \(2\pi in\widehat C(n)=\widehat\nu(n)\), Parseval gives \(\int x^2=4\pi^2\int C^2\), proving (1).

<a id="parity"></a>
## An odd Newton sum obstructs small discrepancy

For \(n\ge1\), let \(S_n=\sum_{P(\beta)=0}\beta^n\in\mathbb Z\) and \(T_n=\sum_j e^{2\pi in\theta_j}\in\mathbb R\), the latter by conjugation of exterior roots.
The paired roots above give \(S_n=2\sum_j\cosh(n\ell_j)e^{2\pi in\theta_j}\).
The addition law for \(\cosh\) gives \((\cosh a-1)+(\cosh b-1)\le\cosh(a+b)-1\) for \(a,b\ge0\).
Hence \(|S_n/2-T_n|\le\cosh(nm)-1\), since \(\sum_j\ell_j=m\).
Each of \(\widehat\sigma_A(n),\widehat\sigma_B(n)\) is a rational integer, being a Galois-invariant sum of algebraic integers.
If \(S_n\) is odd, then
\[
 |\widehat\nu(n)|\ge b_n:=\max\{0,\tfrac12-(\cosh(nm)-1)\},\qquad
 \int C^2\,dt\ge \frac{b_n^2}{2\pi^2n^2}.
\]
The two conjugate frequencies \(\pm n\) give this lower bound in Parseval's formula.
For a fixed \(n\) with odd \(S_n\) along a shrinking sequence, the discrepancy thus has lower limit at least \(1/(8\pi^2n^2)\) for every choice of signed cyclotomic counts.
This obstruction bounds the discrepancy surrogate alone and leaves the actual Jensen loss undetermined.
Counting the input roots twice would make all its Newton sums even, but would require a separate analysis of approximation to the doubled phase counts.
