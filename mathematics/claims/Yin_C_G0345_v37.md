# A one-third branch bound and arithmetic restrictions on Salem inverse residues

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small branches on every hypothetical sequence

Let \(\sigma_i\to1\) be any sequence of Salem numbers, with minimal polynomial \(P=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\), degree \(d=2m\), exterior trace \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), and field \(K=\mathbb Q(T)\), suppressing sequence indices.
There exist same-degree monic Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) with \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\), positive constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\) and integer branch \(j=[z](f/f^*)-q^2+1\), such that
\[
   \theta-q\longrightarrow0,\qquad
   \limsup\frac{j}{d}\le\frac13,\qquad
   \limsup\frac{q\log\sigma}{d}\le\frac1{12}.
\]
For these choices the positive trace residue \(w_T\in K\) at \(T\) eventually has neither \(w_T\) nor \(2w_T\) in the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_K\).
For every fixed \(b\le5\), \(b\ge1\) integral, any \(\mathbb Z[x]\) representative of \(bw_T\) on any infinite subsequence has degree at least a positive constant times \(\sqrt{d/\log(2d)}\) eventually on that subsequence.

For an arbitrary same-degree exact Pisot inverse with \(q=f(0)\ge2\) on the same Salem sequence, let \(b\ge1\) be any integer such that \(\gamma=bw_T\) is an algebraic integer, and put \(e=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
The residue in this notation is taken at the distinguished real embedding \(T>2\).
One has
\[
   \gamma\ge(1-o(1))mu,\qquad
   e\,\frac{bj}{m}\ge(1-o(1))mu .
\]
In particular \(bj/m=O(1)\) forces \(e\to\infty\).
If \(\tau\) denotes the lower limit of the normalized traces of totally positive algebraic integers as their degrees tend to infinity, then for any such integral scalings
\[
                     \liminf \frac{bj}{m}\ \ge\ \tau\ \ge\ \frac{97}{70}.
\]

<a id="siegel"></a>
## A bounded trace in the codifferent of the maximal order

For \(m\ge2\) put \(r_m=\lfloor m/6\rfloor+\mathbf1_{m\not\equiv1\pmod6}\).
Siegel's special-value formula gives a totally positive element of \(\mathcal O_K^\vee\) of trace between one and \(r_m\) for every totally real field of degree \(m\).
Indeed at weight two the nonzero \(\zeta_K(-1)\) is a finite linear combination over \(1\le l\le r_m\) of sums over such elements of trace \(l\).
If every one of those trace sets were empty the whole combination would vanish.
The different and trace in this formula are those of the maximal order.
The functional equation and \(\zeta_K(2)>0\) give nonvanishing.
The general totally real formula, including its weight-\(2m\) cutoff and indexing of the Fourier coefficients, is given in Zag76, §1, printed pp. 58–59, equations (6), (10), (13)–(17). [Published text](../literature/pdf/Zag76--published.pdf#page=6)
Yat19, Theorem 4.3, gives the same formulation, numbered Theorem 21 on p. 8 in the [author's preprint](../literature/pdf/Yat19--arxiv-v1.pdf#page=8).
We use the established special-value theorem at statement and application scope.

<a id="zeta"></a>
## Transfer to the trace order and the inverse polynomial

The inclusion \(\mathbb Z[T]\subseteq\mathcal O_K\) reverses on trace duals, so the least positive trace \(t(S)\) of the order codifferent satisfies \(t(S)\le r_m\), whether or not the order is maximal.
The correspondence with integer interlacers is Yat19, Proposition 3.5 and the following observation on p. 228, or Proposition 12 in the same preprint.
The [factor-four construction](Yin_C_G0345_v34.md#statement) gives \(q\sim t(S)/\log\sigma\), \(\theta-q\to0\) and \(j\le(4+o(1))t(S)\).
The degrees tend to infinity since bounded degree and bounded measure allow finitely many integer coefficients.
Thus \(r_m/m\to1/6\), giving both upper limits.
For each fixed \(b\le5\) the scaled upper limit \(bj/d\le b/3+o(1)\) lies strictly below two.
Any low-degree representation along a subsequence would contradict the [sharp denominator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v33.md#statement), since Galois conjugation gives all the required trace values.
This proves the degree assertion by extraction.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The distinguished conjugate and the full degree of the residue

Write \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) for the neutral traces and \(\gamma_l\) for the conjugate values there, with repetitions allowed.
The [actual positive residues](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) give \(\sum_{l<m}\gamma_l+\gamma=bj\).
The [endpoint identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v134.md#weights), obtained by evaluating the trace transform at two, holds for these scaled values before any polynomial representative is chosen.
It gives
\[
       \frac{\gamma}{u}=b(q-1)+\sum_{l<m}\frac{\gamma_l}{2-x_l}
          \ \ge\ (m-1)
           \left(\frac{N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)\,u}{\gamma\,|S(2)|}\right)^{1/(m-1)} .
\]
Here \(\gamma,\gamma_l>0\) and \(\prod_{l<m}(2-x_l)=|S(2)|/u\).
Multiplication after taking the \((m-1)\)-st power yields
\[
       \left(\frac{\gamma}{u}\right)^m
            \ge (m-1)^{m-1}\frac{N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)}{|S(2)|}.
\]
The norm is a positive integer.
The [endpoint logarithm estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) gives \(\limsup m^{-1}\sum_{l<m}\log(2-x_l)\le0\) by truncation and the arcsine limit.
Since \(u\to0\) and \(|S(2)|\) is a nonzero integer, \(|S(2)|^{1/m}\to1\).
This proves the lower bound on \(\gamma\).
Each field conjugate occurs \(m/e\) times, whence the normalized trace in \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) equals \(bj/m\), and \(e bj/m\ge\gamma\).
The [degree bound of Voutier](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives \(u\ge(\log\sigma)^2>\frac1{16}(\log\log d/\log d)^6\) for large \(d\), so \(mu\to\infty\).
On any subsequence of bounded normalized traces the degrees \(e\) therefore tend to infinity, which proves the bound by \(\tau\) via extraction.

<a id="integrality"></a>
## An elementary separation from trace four-thirds

For \(x>0,\ x\ne1\), define \(H(x)=x-\frac45\log x-\frac35\log|x-1|\).
Its derivative has numerator \((x-2/5)(x-2)\) over \(x(x-1)\), so its two interval minima are at \(2/5\) and \(2\).
At the first minimum apply \(\log y\ge2(y-1)/(y+1)\) for \(y\ge1\) to \(y=5/2,5/3\), giving \(H(2/5)\ge2/5+(4/5)(6/7)+(3/5)(1/2)=97/70\).
At the second minimum \(H(2)=2-(4/5)\log2>7/5\), since \(e^{3/4}>2\) by its first three Taylor terms.
For a totally positive algebraic integer \(\alpha\ne1\), both \(|N(\alpha)|\) and \(|N(\alpha-1)|\) are positive integers.
Summing the pointwise lower bound over its conjugates gives normalized trace at least \(97/70>4/3\).
This elementary constant suffices here.
For the selected inverse seeds, \(j/m\le2/3+o(1)\), so integrality on an infinite subsequence with \(b=1\) or \(2\) contradicts the preceding normalized trace bound, including the possible degree-one cases through the endpoint estimate.
The existence of a Salem sequence approaching one remains an assumption throughout.
