# A cubic obstruction and the radical degrees of two exterior poles

<a id="statement"></a>
## Moving sections of the reciprocal quadratic curve

Let \(q_j(X)=X^2-jX-1\) and \(\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\) for \(j=3,4\).
Write \(A=q_3q_4,\ A^*(X)=X^4 A(1/X)\) and take the [quadratic curve](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#example)
\[
                 F(U,V)=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U).
\]
Let \(k,l\) be positive coprime integers along a sequence with \(k\to\infty\) and \(l/k\to\infty\).
Choose a noncyclotomic rational irreducible factor \(S\) of \(F(X^k,X^l)\).
Put \(d=\deg S\), let \(H\) count its roots outside the unit circle, and suppose \(H\) stays bounded.
For all sufficiently large indices \(H=2b\), with \(b\ge1\) roots whose \(k\)-th powers approach each of \(\beta_3,\beta_4\).
One has, with constants uniform for bounded \(H\),
\[
 \frac{kd}{2l}=b+o(1),\qquad
 k\,m(S)=b\log(\beta_3\beta_4)+O_H(e^{-c l/k})
 \quad\text{for some }c>0.                                               \tag{1}
\]
On the indices with \(3\mid k\), the number \(H\) is eventually divisible by six.

Suppose now \(H=2\).
The two exterior roots are real.
For either of them take its distinguished real root field \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\).
For all sufficiently large indices one has \(3\nmid k\) and, **simultaneously for every positive integer \(a\)**,
\[
 [K(\beta_3^{1/a}):K]=2a,\qquad
 [K(\beta_4^{1/a}):K]=\frac{2a}{\gcd(a,3)},                              \tag{2}
\]
using positive real radicals.
In particular both quadratic fields \(\mathbb Q(\beta_j)\) are separately linearly disjoint from \(K\).
Both \(q_j(X^a)\) are irreducible over \(K\) for all \(a\mid k\) at these indices.
These are necessary conditions on the proposed two-exterior factors; they leave the coprime directions with \(3\nmid k\) available.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Counts at the two exterior values

The normalized section is monic with unit constant coefficient for large \(l/k\).
Every root of \(S\) is therefore an algebraic unit.
By the [individual section height](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement), \(l h(z)\to m(F)/2\), so \(h(z^k)\to0\).
The powered points leave every finite set because the primitive directions are distinct after passage to a subsequence tending out of any bounded range.
More explicitly, the rank-one nonzero coordinate pair modulo roots of unity determines its primitive direction up to sign.
On the unit circle all fibers of \(F\) have both \(V\)-roots on the circle, as proved in the [quadratic example](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#example).
The [radial localization theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v776.md#statement) puts the \(k\)-th power of each exterior conjugate within \(O(e^{-c l/k})\) of either \(\beta_3\) or \(\beta_4\), since these are the only exterior roots of the top face \(A\).
Write \(b_j\) for these two counts using disjoint fixed small disks.

There is a unique simple pole of \(V\) above each of the four roots of \(A\) on the normalization.
Indeed in the coordinate \(w=1/V\) the equation is \(A(U)+U^2w+A^*(U)w^2=0\).
The derivative in \(U\) at each such point is nonzero, so \(w\) is a local parameter.
Sum the [boundary formula](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement) over the two conjugate poles above each \(q_j\).
The conjugate of \(\beta_j\) is \(-1/\beta_j\), whose interior neighborhood cannot be visited by \(U=z^k,V=z^l\) with \(|V|>1\).
Every exterior conjugate with \(z^k\) near \(\beta_j\) has \(V\to\infty\) uniformly there and its lift approaches the unique simple pole.
Summing over the conjugate pair of poles counts all visits over the absolute embeddings of the root field with their ordinary multiplicities.
Since the projection onto \(U\) has degree two and \(h(\beta_j)=\tfrac12\log\beta_j\), this gives
\[
 \frac{l}{dk}\,b_j(\log\beta_j+O(e^{-c l/k}))\longrightarrow \tfrac12\log\beta_j .
\]
In particular both counts are eventually positive.
Their ratio tends to one, so they agree eventually by boundedness.
This proves the degree estimate, and summing the exterior logarithms proves the measure estimate in (1).

<a id="pullback"></a>
## Exterior counts on a fixed cover

For a fixed positive divisor \(h\) of the moving \(k\) restrict to the indices with \(h\mid k\).
Set \(T=z^{k/h}\).
The curve \(F(T^h,V)=0\) is absolutely irreducible and has degree two over \(T\).
Indeed the function \(U\) has a simple zero at each point above \(U=0,V=\pm i\), since \(F(0,V)=V^2+1\) has two simple roots there.
Thus \(X^h-U\) is Eisenstein in the valuation ring of the curve at one such point and has degree \(h\) over its complex function field.
The extension obtained by adjoining its root has degree two over \(\mathbb C(T)\) by the tower law.
The coefficients of the quadratic in \(V\) have no common factor, which proves the assertion about the whole equation.
Each root \(c\) of \(q_j(T^h)\) still gives exactly one simple pole \(Q_c\), by the same derivative calculation at \(w=0\).
The short-coordinate heights tend to zero, its values are nonzero, and the powered points lift generically to this fixed cover since their images on the original curve leave finite sets.

For \(h=3\) let \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), so \(\beta_4=\phi^3\).
The six roots of \(q_4(T^3)\) form two rational orbits of sizes two and four, those of \(\phi\) and \(\phi\zeta_3\) with \(\zeta_3\) a primitive cube root of unity.
The field of \(\phi\zeta_3\) contains \(\beta_4\), hence \(\phi\), and also contains \(\zeta_3\), giving degree four.
Both representative heights are \(h(\phi)\), and every exterior value of either orbit has modulus \(\phi\).
On the cubic cover, the boundary formula summed over each orbit gives two positive limits in the ratio \(2:4\), with interior values again unable to contribute.
At a visited pole the actual logarithm of \(|V|\) is \((3l/k)(\log\phi+o(1))\) uniformly in both orbits.
Indeed \(T^3=z^k\) is exponentially close to \(\beta_4\) in the exterior disks, and \(V\) tends to infinity there.
Thus the ratio of the two orbit counts tends to \(1:2\), while their sum is exactly \(b_4\).
The bounded positive counts have this ratio exactly for large indices.
It follows that \(3\mid b_4\), giving the divisibility by six.

<a id="branches"></a>
## Disjointness detected at individual geometric points

When \(H=2\), only one conjugate lies above each of the two disjoint exterior disks after the \(k\)-th power.
Complex conjugation fixes each of those conjugates because the disks have real centers, so both are real.
To prove disjointness for \(j\), choose as distinguished root \(z\) the one approaching the \(j\)-th disk after the \(k\)-th power.
Fix \(h\), restrict to \(h\mid k\), and take a fixed normal splitting field \(M\) of \(q_j(T^h)\).
After taking a subsequence \(z^{k/h}\) tends to a single real \(c_0\) with \(c_0^h=\beta_j\), since it is real and its \(h\)-th power tends to \(\beta_j\).

Apply the boundary formula on the fixed cover separately to **each** marked pole \(Q_c\) over \(M\).
Its right side is \(h(c)/2>0\), and all conjugates of \(c\) are off the unit circle.
Take disjoint neighborhoods of the \(2h\) poles, over disjoint neighborhoods of their coordinate values, requiring \(|V|>1\).
For the absolute average over \(KM\), the neighborhood for \(\sigma\) is the one belonging to the image of \(Q_c\) under \(\sigma|_M\).
Consequently for each \(c\) and at every sufficiently large eligible index some embedding of \(KM\) visits the required neighborhood.
The only possible restriction of such an embedding to \(K\) is the identity.
Indeed \(|V|>1\) forces an exterior conjugate, and the other exterior conjugate has its \(k\)-th power tending to \(\beta_{7-j}\), outside the neighborhoods of all roots of \(q_j\).
Since \(z^{k/h}\to c_0\), the visiting lift must therefore send \(c\) to \(c_0\).
Every root of \(q_j(T^h)\) is thus conjugate over \(K\) to \(c_0\), giving irreducibility over \(K\) eventually for this fixed \(h\).
The subsequence argument also proves eventual irreducibility on all eligible indices for this fixed \(h\), since a failing subsequence would have a further one of the type just considered.
At \(h=1\) this shows \([K\mathbb Q(\beta_j):K]=2\).
The disjointness holds in the other exterior root field as well, since it is a conjugate root field and each quadratic pole field is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).

<a id="radicals"></a>
## All positive radicals in the real pole compositum

For \(H=2\), (1) gives \(d\to\infty\) and \(m(S)\to0\).
There are neutral conjugates for all large indices: otherwise all conjugates would be exterior or interior, and reciprocity at sufficiently small measure follows from [Smyth's nonreciprocal gap](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001), leaving just four roots.
Thus \(S\) is reciprocal, \(K\) has an involution \(\iota(z)=1/z\), and \(T_0=\mathbb Q(z+1/z)\) is totally real of degree \(d/2\).
Exactly two embeddings of \(T_0\) correspond to real off-circle inverse pairs, and each other embedding of \(T_0\) extends to embeddings of \(K\) on which \(\iota\) acts as complex conjugation.

Put \(H_j=\mathbb Q(\beta_j)\), \(D_j=KH_j\), \(M_j=T_0H_j\).
Disjointness proved above gives \([D_j:M_j]=2\), \([M_j:H_j]=d/2\), and \(\iota\) extends to \(D_j\) fixing \(H_j\).
The field \(M_j\) is totally real.
Suppose \(u=\beta_j^{1/a}>0\) lies in \(D_j\).
The image of \(\iota(u)\) is real and equals \(u\) or \(-u\), whence \(u^2\in M_j\).
All \(H_j\)-conjugates of \(u^2\) are real and solve \(X^a=\beta_j^2\), giving \([H_j(u^2):H_j]\le2\).
For \(d>8\) at least three embeddings of \(M_j\) over \(H_j\) fix \(u^2\).
At most two can induce the exceptional trace embeddings, since an embedding over \(H_j\) is determined by its restriction to \(T_0\).
Choose one of the others and extend it to an embedding \(\sigma\) of \(D_j\).
There \(\sigma\iota=\overline{\sigma}\), as one sees on \(K\) by neutrality and on \(H_j\) by reality.
The alternative \(\iota(u)=-u\) would make \(\sigma(u)\) purely imaginary with square \(\sigma(u^2)=u^2>0\).
Thus \(u\in M_j\).
The negative quadratic conjugate of \(\beta_j\) precludes an even \(a\) for such a totally real radical.
For odd \(a\) all the \(H_j\)-conjugates of \(u\) are real roots of the same odd power equation, so \(u\in H_j\).
This extends the [trace-field argument for a Salem pole](Yin_C_G0345_v751.md#relative-real) to the two exceptional embeddings here.

In \(H_j\) the positive radical \(u>1\) is an algebraic unit, and its odd power has norm \(-1\).
Thus \(u-1/u\) is its integral trace and is at least one, so \(u\ge\phi\).
Since \(\beta_3<\phi^3=\beta_4\), the only possible exponent for \(\beta_3\) is one, whereas for \(\beta_4\) the possibilities are one and three, both attained.
Write \(e_3=1,e_4=3\).
For any \(a\ge1\), set \(c=\beta_j^{1/a}\) and \(s=[D_j(c):D_j]\).
The conjugates of \(c\) over this real field have modulus \(c\), so the absolute value of the real constant coefficient of its monic minimal polynomial is \(c^s\in D_j\).
Together with \(c^a=\beta_j\) this gives \(c^{\gcd(a,s)}\in D_j\).
Hence \(a/\gcd(a,s)\mid e_j\), and \(s\ge a/\gcd(a,e_j)\).
Conversely \(c^{a/\gcd(a,e_j)}=\beta_j^{1/\gcd(a,e_j)}\in H_j\), giving equality.
Multiplication by \([D_j:K]=2\) proves (2) for every \(a\) at once.
The cubic count on the indices \(3\mid k\) excludes \(H=2\), so (2) gives the asserted full degrees for every \(a\mid k\).
