# Uniform height bounds for integral trace grids and finiteness on the Chebyshev extrema

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two finiteness statements

Use the [sign simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#statement) with integers \(q,n\ge2\), \(p=q+1\), \(M=pq\), \(h=1/M\), and pole value \(K=(p+q)^2\).
For nodes \(-4=t_0<\cdots<t_{n-1}=0\), let \(Q(x)=\prod_i(x-t_i)\).

* If deleting one node leaves a monic polynomial in \(\mathbb Z[x]\), integral realization is impossible for every sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly in the degree and in the grid.
  This includes every integral \(Q\), since the endpoint \(0\) can then be deleted.
* On the exact shifted Chebyshev extrema \(2\cos(j\pi/(n-1))-2\), only finitely many pairs \((q,n)\) admit integral realization.
  There is an absolute bound on the number of possible degrees for each \(q\).

The first conclusion allows the deleted node to vary with the degree and the grid.
The arguments give neither a numerical list of surviving pairs nor an exclusion of general rational separating grids.

<a id="delete"></a>
## Deleting one node puts the reciprocal lift in a class with a Mahler gap

Suppose \(H\) realizes the grid and set \(r_i=-H(t_i)/Q'(t_i)\).
The [interpolation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#simplex) gives \(r_i>0\), \(H/Q=1-\sum_i r_i/(x-t_i)\), and \(\sum_i r_i/(h-t_i)<1\).
In particular \(\sum_i r_i<4+h\).
Choose a node \(t_e\) such that \(B(x)=Q(x)/(x-t_e)\) is integral.
The proper residues of \(H/B=(x-t_e)H/Q\) at the remaining nodes are \(-(t_i-t_e)r_i\).
Their total absolute value is smaller than \(4(4+h)<17\).
In the variable \(t=x+2\), the monic integral polynomials \(H(t-2)\) and \(B(t-2)\) have degrees \(n\) and \(n-1\), respectively.
Monic division gives integral quotient \(t-a\) and integral proper remainder.
The poles are distinct and in \([-2,2]\).
Thus the [whole-lift residue theorem](Yin_C_G0880_v1.md#statement), applied with residue budget \(17\), gives an absolute \(\eta>0\) below every positive logarithmic Mahler measure in this class.
The [root-location argument](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) places the reciprocal lift \(S(z)=z^nH(z+z^{-1}-2)\) in the strict range \(0<m(S)<\log(1+1/q)\).
These inequalities exclude all sufficiently large \(q\).

<a id="cyclic"></a>
## A bounded number of cosets on the exact grid

Write \(N=n-1\) and number the extrema in decreasing order as \(u_j=2\cos(j\pi/N)-2\), \(0\le j\le N\).
The case \(n=2\) has empty sign region, so assume \(N\ge2\).
The exact node polynomial is integral, since
\[
 Q(x)=x(x+4)U_{N-1}((x+2)/2),
\]
where \(U_k\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind and the shifted factors are monic integral by the three-term recurrence.
In particular the height bound just proved applies.

Set \(D=2N\), \(s_1=[z]S\), and let \(R(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}a_jz^j\) represent \(-z^{-1}S(z)\) modulo \(z^D-1\).
Its coefficients are integers.
For each \(D\)-th root \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i j/D)\), the value \(R(\zeta)=-(-1)^j H(2\cos(2\pi j/D)-2)\) is positive by strict alternation, including the reflected nodes.
Fourier inversion gives
\[
 \|a\|_{A(\mathbb Z/D\mathbb Z)}
 =D^{-1}\sum_{\zeta^D=1}|R(\zeta)|=a_0=-2s_1 .
\]
The last equality uses reciprocity of \(S\), since exactly its terms of indices \(1\) and \(D+1\) contribute to \(a_0\).
The node sum is \(-2n\) by reflection.
Consequently the coefficient of \(x^{n-1}\) in \(Q\) is \(2n\), that in \(H\) is \(2n-\sum_i r_i\), and \(s_1=-\sum_i r_i\).
This is an integer strictly between \(-(4+h)\) and zero, giving \(\|a\|_A\le8\).

By [Sanders' finite abelian group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#finite-abelian-fourier-algebra-norm), \(a\) is a sum of at most \(b\) signed subgroup-coset indicators for an absolute \(b\).
Thus for some \(l\le b\), signs \(\epsilon_i\), divisors \(d_i\mid D\), and \(0\le v_i<d_i\),
\[
 R(z)=\sum_{i=1}^l\epsilon_i z^{v_i}(1-z^D)/(1-z^{d_i}).
\]
The quotient of \(S+zR\) by \(z^D-1\) is \(E(z)=z^2+s_1z-1\), as seen from the two highest coefficients and the constant term.
With \(W(z)=\prod_i(1-z^{d_i})\) and \(d=\sum_i d_i\), we obtain the polynomial identity
\[
 S W=(z^D-1)E W-
 z\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{v_i}(1-z^D)\prod_{k\ne i}(1-z^{d_k}).
\]
On the right keep terms separate when equal exponents occur.
There are at most \((6+b)2^b\) terms, with coefficients in \(\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm4\}\) and exponents between \(0\) and \(2n+d\).

<a id="units"></a>
## The homogeneous pole equation distinguishes degrees

The pole equation is \(q^{2n}S(p/q)=K\), since \(p/q+q/p-2=h\).
Evaluate the preceding sparse identity at \(z=p/q\) and multiply by \(q^{2n+d}\).
It gives an equality between a bounded number of terms \(c p^\alpha q^\beta\) of degree \(\alpha+\beta=2n+d\) from the sparse expression and at most \(2^b\) terms of degree \(d\) with coefficients \(\pm K\) from \(Kq^d W(p/q)\).
The latter side is nonzero because \(p/q>1\).
Partition the zero sum formed by taking the difference into minimal vanishing subsums.
At least one such subsum uses both sides, since otherwise the entire latter side would vanish.
In this subsum select a term from the degree-\(d\) side, move it across the equality, and divide all other terms by it.
This gives a nondegenerate equation
\[
 c_1 x_1+\cdots+c_m x_m=1,\qquad
 (x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in\langle p,q\rangle^m ,
\]
with the coefficients separated from the bare monomial ratios \(x_j\).
For fixed \(q\) each coefficient belongs to \(\{\pm1,\pm1/K,\pm2/K,\pm3/K,\pm4/K\}\).
The number of coefficient patterns is bounded absolutely, because \(m\le(7+b)2^b\).
The rank of the tuple group is \(2m\), as \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime integers greater than one.
The [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt bound](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) gives an absolute bound, depending only on \(m\), on the number of tuples for each pattern.
For \(m=1\) there is at most one solution directly.
At least one coordinate comes from the higher-degree side.
Its uniquely determined exponent vector \((A,B)\) in \(x_j=p^A q^B\) has \(A+B=2n\).
Every admissible degree is therefore recovered from one of an absolutely bounded number of coordinates for that \(q\).
Combining this count with the uniform height bound proves finiteness of the pairs.
