# A quantitative alternative for all Mahler values near one

<a id="statement"></a>
## A growing threshold for the second iterate

Let \(A=M(w)>1\) be the multiplicative Mahler measure of an algebraic number and put \(m=\log A\).
Once \(m\) is sufficiently small, there are the following alternatives.

1. \(A=r^{jE}\) with \(j\in\{1,2\}\), an integer \(E\ge1\), and a reciprocal real Perron unit \(r>1\) satisfying \(M(r)=r^j\). All positive powers of \(r\) generate \(\mathbb Q(A)=\mathbb Q(r)\). In particular \(M(A)=A^j\).
2. For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), once \(m\) is sufficiently small in terms of \(\eta\), the total logarithmic measure of the value itself satisfies
   \[
             m(A)=\log M(A)\ \ge\
                \exp\!\big(\eta\,m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).                 \tag{1}
   \]

In the first case \(j=1\) makes \(A\) a real Salem number; \(j=2\) makes \(A\) an even power of a unit in its own field.
Thus every nonreciprocal \(A=M(w)\) tending to one obeys (1), as does every primitive non-Salem value tending to one, where primitive means primitive modulo torsion on its rational unit line in \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
Equivalently for these two exclusions and \(B\to\infty\), uniformly for all such \(A\) with \(m(A)\le B\),
\[
            \log A\ \ge\ \bigl(\tfrac14-o(1)\bigr)
                  \left(\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}\right)^3.             \tag{2}
\]
The bounds concern values of the map \(M\) on individual algebraic numbers.

<a id="leading"></a>
## A quantitative comparison at the first nonzero constituent

There is also an estimate for any nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) at small \(m\).
Use the [block-product chain](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent), its degrees \(d_i=\deg z_i\), and the first active summand \(W_i\) of \([x]\).
If \(x\) does not match a coordinate of \(z_i\), then for an absolute \(c>0\)
\[
                    m(x)m(z_i)\ \ge\
                           \frac{c\,d_i}{(\log(3d_i))^{212}} .           \tag{3}
\]
This extension to chain terms permits a nonzero lower space of block constants in \(V_{z_i}\).
To check it, compress \(x\) to \(\gamma\) and set \(D=\deg\gamma\), \(d=d_i\).
Then \(m(\gamma)=m(x)\), \(L_\gamma\subseteq L_{z_i}\) for the rational splitting fields, and the projection of \([\gamma]\) to \(W_i\) is nonzero.
Indeed \(V_x\subseteq V_{z_i}\) and the splitting field of a compressed unit is the kernel field of its class span. [Kernel fields](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)
The projection is equivariant and has orbit size at most \(D\).
It belongs to the full signed representation or the blockwise sum-zero representation, irrespective of the lower summand.

For \(D\le d^6\), the [small-orbit count](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#few) for that projection marks a nonempty set of at most 36 inverse pairs preserved by its stabilizer.
Hence a chosen root \(a\) over those labels has joint degree with \(\gamma\) at most \(72D\).
They are independent by the nonmatching condition.
The two-unit height product bound gives (3).
For \(D>d^6\), choose in the splitting group of \(z_i\) a lift of a three-cycle supported on one block of labels, and raise it to the fourth power.
This order-three element acts on at most six actual roots of \(z_i\) and nontrivially on \(W_i\); its conjugacy class has size \(O(d^3)\).
Some member sends \([\gamma]\) to an independent vector, since otherwise the normal closure fixes its nonzero projection and hence all of the irreducible \(W_i\).
The two independent conjugates of \(\gamma\) then have joint degree \(O(Dd^3)\).
The [large-orbit height calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#many) gives \(m(\gamma)\gg d^{3/2}/(\log(3d))^{106}\).
Multiplying by [Voutier's bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) for the original \(z_i\) gives (3) here too.
The uniform large-degree requirements hold at all stages by coefficient finiteness and \(0<m(z_i)\le m\).

<a id="iterate"></a>
## The second iterate and the inverse bound

At small \(m\), integrality of the two extreme coefficients reduces the algebraic input to a unit.
Apply (3) to the actual signed exterior product \(x=A\in D(w)\).
If \(A\) matches a coordinate of its first active \(z_i\), the [exterior-product argument along the chain](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#line) applies directly.
At every earlier level the leading projection vanishes, so the half-block products incur zero loss and \(M(w)=M(z_i)^E\).
At the selected level the exterior coefficients are \(1,0,-1\); their difference on a marked block shows \(M(z_i)=r^j\) with \(j=1\) or \(2\), for a real signed conjugate or inverse \(r>1\) of \(z_i\).
This portion of the argument needs the specified match, with no prior upper bound on \(m(A)\).
The compressed \(r\) is reciprocal, \(M(r)=M(z_i)\), all positive powers retain its degree, and the Perron property of \(A\) gives the same property for \(r\).
When \(j=1\) this large reciprocal unit has only one exterior root and hence is Salem; so is its positive integral power \(A\).

Otherwise (3) gives \(m(A)\gg d_i/(m(\log(3d_i))^{212})\).
Write \(t=\log d_i\) and \(a=(4m)^{-1/3}\).
Voutier's estimate at \(m(z_i)\le m\) gives \(t>a\log t\); uniformly at small \(m\) this implies first \(t>a\) and then \(t>a\log a\).
The lower bound for \(\log m(A)\) is \(t-212\log(t+\log3)-\log m-O(1)\).
For large \(t\) the \(t\)-dependent part is increasing.
At \(t=a\log a\) it is
\[
         \bigl(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)+o(1)\bigr)m^{-1/3}\log(1/m).
\]
This proves (1).
For (2), in this same case \(m(A)\le B\) and \(m(z_i)\le m<1\) imply \(d_i\ll B(\log(3d_i))^{212}\).
Hence \(d_i\ll B(\log(3B))^{212}\) by taking logs first.
Use the monotonicity for large degree of \(\log\log d_i/\log d_i\) and the same Voutier estimate once more.
Inputs above the fixed smallness threshold satisfy (2) automatically at large \(B\).
In the first case of the theorem \(A\) is reciprocal, and if \(A\) is primitive one must have \(jE=1\); this proves both exclusions.

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Nonnegative recurrences and degrees of preimages of a primitive Salem value

For every fixed \(C\), let \(P(X)=X^n-\sum_{i<n}c_iX^i\) have nonnegative integer \(c_i\), \(c_0\ge1\), and \(2\le\sum c_i\le C\).
Its unique positive root \(R>1\) cannot be a value of \(M\) once \(R\) is sufficiently close to one in terms of \(C\) alone.
Indeed the equation \(1=\sum c_iR^{i-n}\) has a strictly decreasing right-hand side, greater than one at \(R=1\); no positive inverse \(R^{-1}\) is a root.
Thus the minimal polynomial of \(R\) is nonreciprocal.
Its multiplicative measure is at most \(M(P)\le1+C\), by monicity, multiplicativity and the circle integral bound.
This contradicts (1) near one, uniformly in all exponents.

Conversely fix a primitive Salem \(\sigma>1\) with \(\log\sigma\) sufficiently small.
For any single-number preimage \(M(w)=\sigma\), compress \(w\) to \(z_1\) of degree \(d_1\).
Either \(z_1\) is a signed conjugate of \(\sigma\) or its inverse, or
\[
                 \log(3d_1)\gg
                    \left(\frac{\deg\sigma}{(\log\sigma)^2}\right)^{1/212}.  \tag{4}
\]
Indeed (1)'s growing right-hand side excludes the unmatched alternative because \(m(\sigma)=\log\sigma\).
In \(A=r^{jE}\) primitivity gives \(j=E=1\), so \(\deg\sigma=d_i\) for the matched stage.
If \(i>1\) then \(d_i\le d_2\le2u_1\).
The [two-label estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) gives \(u_1\ll m(w)^2(\log(3d_1))^{212}\), proving (4).
All preceding half-block transfers have multiplicity one and zero loss since their positive integer multiplicities multiply to \(E=1\).
These degree restrictions allow preimages independent of the Salem coordinate at much larger degrees.
