# Averaging the prime and residue-order cutoffs for the trace discriminant certificate

<a id="statement"></a>
## The part of the certificate still needed on a steep family

Let \(M\ge3\) be an integer, \(h=\lceil(\log M)^6\rceil\), \(M\le k<2M\) and \(l=hk+1\).
For the two-pole section \(P=P_{k,l}\), use the [integer irreducibility certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#statement) \(U\), its degree-56 exceptional form \(B=B(k,l)\), and the full monic trace polynomial \(Q\).
For all sufficiently large \(M\) every section here is squarefree and \(B\ne0\), by [squarefreeness](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) and the finite set of projective zeros.
At \(p\nmid B\) take the critical residue disks with positive even contact as in the [eight invariant factors](Yin_C_G0345_v823.md#statement).
At \(p\mid B\) take all multiple geometric residue roots of \(P\).
In either case call their section coordinates \(x\) and use their multiplicative orders in the residue field.

Define \(U_*\) by keeping the full \(p\)-part of \(U\) only if there is a chosen \(x\) of order \(>M^{1/2}\) and the following size condition holds:
\[
                p>M^{1/5}\quad(p\nmid B),\qquad p>M^{2/9}\quad(p\mid B).
\]
For all but \(O(M/\log M)\) of the \(k\) in this interval,
\[
                          \log(U/U_*)\ll_F M\log M.                    \tag{1}
\]
For the same directions and any factorization, the actual logarithmic complementary resultant supplied by contacts failing either size or order condition is also \(O_F(M\log M)\), including within primes having both low- and high-order disks.
Here the factorization is taken into monic reciprocal integer factors, as holds for every grouping of the irreducible factors eventually.
This error is \(o(d)\) uniformly for the degrees \(d\) of all irreducible factors in the interval.
In particular all reducible sections outside the indicated exceptional set satisfy
\[
                     \log U_*\gg l(\log\log l)^4/(\log l)^3.          \tag{2}
\]
The remaining prime powers in \(U_*\) are unrestricted above the cutoffs.

<a id="weights"></a>
## A set of at most a constant times \(p\) residue points

At any prime embed the characteristic-zero roots in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_p\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).
Charge each chosen geometric class \(x\) the nonnegative weight
\[
        (\log p)\sum_{z\bmod p=x}v_p(P'(z)).
\]
Every actual complementary contact lies in a chosen class by the [quadratic local test](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#statement) at good primes, and the weight dominates the sum of its cross-factor distances times \(\log p\).
At good primes these weights also dominate \(v_p(U)\log p\) when summed over the chosen disks.
Indeed an octic orbit of degree \(f\) and positive even valuation \(b\) contributes \(fb\) to the upper divisor from the eight integers.
Its \(2f\) geometric section disks have two roots each, and the two roots in each disk have distance valuation \(b/2\), by the same local calculation.
At a bad prime all repeated classes together give precisely \(v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\log p\), which dominates \(v_p(U)\log p\).
For the latter assertion, writing \(P\) as a product of the quadratics \(X^2-yX+1\) over the roots of \(Q\) gives \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=|Q(2)Q(-2)|\,|\operatorname{disc}Q|^2\) by their pairwise resultants.

At good primes the two roots of each chosen disk lie in an unramified extension, since a positive even contact reduces the local quadratic to adjoining the square root of a unit at an odd prime.
Both roots therefore differ from the Teichmüller lift of \(x\) by a multiple of \(p\).
It follows that \(P\) evaluated at that lift vanishes modulo \(p^2\).
Consequently the pair \((u,v)=(x^k,x^l)\) belongs to the [slope-independent set \(Z_p\)](Yin_C_G0345_v813.md#lift), of size at most \(8p\) by the proper intersection estimate there.
For \(p\mid B(k,l)\), all such pairs belong to the union of the torus intersections \(F=D_{a,b}F=0\) over the projective zeros \([a:b]\) of \(\bar B\), where \(D_{a,b}=aU\partial_U+bV\partial_V\).
Except at the fixed primes dividing the content of \(B\), there are at most 56 such directions, each with [at most sixteen geometric points](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#ramification).
We may work outside the fixed finite set containing those content primes and the primes of singular reduction of the torus curve in the [smoothness test](Yin_C_G0345_v813.md#density).
They will be included below among the small primes.
Thus the possible pairs, taken over the entire interval at one prime, number \(O_F(p)\) in all and \(O_F(1)\) for the bad-prime part.

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Two congruences for the same short exponent

Fix \(h,p,u,v\) outside those fixed primes.
Any occurrence must have \(x=v/u^h\) since \(l=hk+1\).
Write \(g\) for the order of this \(x\).
The condition \(x^k=u\) determines \(k\) modulo \(g\) whenever it has a solution.
The tangency equation at this point is
\[
          k(uF_U+h vF_V)+vF_V=0.
\]
At a smooth torus point admitting a solution the coefficient of \(k\) is nonzero modulo \(p\), since otherwise both partial derivatives would vanish.
This fixes \(k\) modulo \(p\), and \(p\nmid g\).
Thus each pair can occur at most \(1+M/(pg)\) times.
Conversely we need no sufficiency of these two necessary congruences.
The [inverse-orbit bound](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#orbit) gives \(g\le p^8+1\) for every occurring pair.

For \(p\le M^{1/20}\), the [all-prime discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#statement) bounds the sum of all weights by \(O_F(M^{9/20}(\log M)^5)\) at one direction, since \(3+k+l=M^{1+o(1)}\).
Hence their total over the interval is \(o(M^2)\).
For \(p>M^{1/20}\) the ratio \(\log(3+k+l)/\log p\) is bounded absolutely for large \(M\).
The [order-sensitive proof](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#valuation) then bounds \(v_p(P'(z))\ll_F g\) for each root of a chosen disk.
Indeed in rank two its bound is \(O_F(g(1+\log(3+k+l)/\log p)^4)\), and in rank at most one the height term and the exponent term there give \(O_F(g)\) in this range.
At most 32 roots per section and prime are in nonsingleton classes.
Thus we may assign \(O_F(g\log p)\) per occurrence.
Using the number of pairs and their spacings, the sum of good-prime weights through \(Y\), above the already treated primes, is
\[
 \ll_F \sum_{p\le Y} p\,(p^8+1+M/p)\log p
 \ll_F Y^{10}+MY\qquad(Y\ge2),
\]
by the elementary Chebyshev bound.
For bad-prime weights replace the factor \(p\) counting pairs by a constant, obtaining \(O_F(Y^9+M\log(2Y))\) by partial summation.
The respective cutoffs \(Y=M^{1/5},M^{2/9}\) each cost \(O_F(M^2)\) over the interval.

<a id="order"></a>
## Low orders over all remaining primes

For \(p>M^{1/20}\) and any one direction, order \(g\) requires that \(p\) divide the positive integer \(|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_g,P)|\).
Its logarithm is \(O_F(\varphi(g))\) by [torsion exclusion and the complex product](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#torsion).
There are at most 32 contributing roots at one prime, so orders \(g\le G=M^{1/4}\) cost \(O_F(G^3)\) per direction.
For \(G<g\le T=M^{1/2}\) first consider the pairs with \(pg\le M\).
Their number of occurrences per pair is at most \(2M/(pg)\).
The possible pairs at \(p\) number \(O_F(p)\), and \(p\le M/G\) here.
Their total weight is \(O_F(M\sum_{p\le M/G}\log p)=O_F(M^2/G)\).

For \(pg>M\) each pair occurs at most twice.
At a given \(g\) sort all pairs by the residue \(a=k\bmod g\), using \(0\le a<g\).
Then \(u=x^a,\ v=x^{ha+1}\), so each geometric pair at this \(g,a,p\) is specified by one primitive \(g\)-th root \(x\) satisfying \(F(x^a,x^{ha+1})=0\).
The integer
\[
                  C_{a,g}=|\operatorname{Res}_X(\Phi_g(X),F(X^a,X^{ha+1}))|
\]
is nonzero by torsion exclusion for \(F\) and has logarithm \(O_F(\varphi(g))\), uniformly even at \(a=0\).
Because \(p\nmid g\), the primitive \(g\)-th roots lift distinctly to an unramified extension containing the cyclotomic roots.
The product defining \(C_{a,g}\) contains a factor of valuation at least one for every such geometric solution.
It follows that the sum of \(\log p\) over these solutions, with each geometric \(x\) counted at each prime, is at most \(\log C_{a,g}\).
Multiplying by \(O_F(g)\) for their weights and occurrences and summing over \(a,g\) gives \(O_F(\sum_{g\le T}g^2\varphi(g))=O_F(T^4)\).
Together these three order ranges cost \(O_F(MG^3+M^2/G+T^4)=O_F(M^2)\).

The total of the weights failing a prime or order cutoff is therefore \(O_F(M^2)\).
Outside \(O_F(M/\log M)\) directions it is at most \(M\log M\), which proves (1) and the assertion about contacts by domination.
The [minimum degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#degrees) is \(\gg l(\log\log l/\log l)^3\gg M(\log M)^3(\log\log M)^3\).
This absorbs the discarded terms even at the smallest factor degree, and the [splitting threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#statement) proves (2).
