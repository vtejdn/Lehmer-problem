# Exact relations forced by small values on many conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## Critical exponent spans and a finite list of relations

Fix an integer \(r\ge2\), \(C\ge0\) and \(0<\eta\le1\).
Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be algebraic of degree \(d\), with absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\), and let \(0=a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_r=W\) be integer exponents with \(Wh\le C\).
At every complex embedding of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), put
\[
            v_\sigma=(1,(\sigma\alpha)^{a_2},\ldots,(\sigma\alpha)^W).
\]
There are constants \(\epsilon_0>0\) and \(M_0\ge1\), depending only on \(r,C,\eta\), with the following property.
Suppose some complex coefficient column \(c\) of Euclidean norm one satisfies
\[
    |v_\sigma c|\le\epsilon_0\|v_\sigma\|_2
                    \quad\hbox{at at least }\eta d\hbox{ embeddings}.  \tag{1}
\]
Then there is a field \(E\subseteq K\) of degree \(M\le M_0\), and a nonzero form with coefficients \(b_j\in E\) such that
\[
             \sum_{j=1}^r b_j\alpha^{a_j}=0,\qquad
             \max_j h(b_j)\le 2\big(\log(r!)+rC\big).                 \tag{2}
\]
Some \(b_j\) equals one and some others may vanish.
The coefficients in (2) need not equal \(c\).
There is a proper subspace defined over \(E\), containing the row of \(\alpha\), whose \(M\) distinct Galois conjugates partition all \(d\) labeled rows into sets of size \(d/M\).
All rows in the original set satisfy the same form (2).
Both its field of definition \(E\) and the possible normalized tuples in (2) range over finite lists depending only on the fixed parameters.

In particular the ordinary absolute sublevel condition \(|v_\sigma c|\le\epsilon_0\) suffices.
The same holds if the counted rows are unit-circle conjugates, with their count still compared to the full \(d\).
The norm of the form gives an integer polynomial divisible over \(\mathbb Q\) by the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\), of degree at most \(MW\) and with at most \(\binom{M+r-1}{r-1}\) nonzero terms.
It comes from a finite list of fixed integer polynomials in \(r-1\) variables by substituting \(X^{a_2},\ldots,X^{a_r}\).
If \(K\) has no proper nontrivial subfield of degree at most \(M_0\) and \(d>M_0\), then (2) itself has rational coefficients on the original support.

Conversely, a nonzero form on this support with coefficients in a degree-\(M\) subfield of \(K\) that vanishes at \(\alpha\) vanishes at at least \(d/M\) of the embeddings, and normalization gives (1) at zero threshold for any \(\eta\le1/M\).
For a potential sequence of algebraic units with total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\to0\), these tests permit every \(W=O(d/m)\).
The [subcritical sublevel bound](Yin_C_G0345_v738.md#statement) already forces vanishing proportions at shrinking thresholds when \(Wh\to0\) for the ordinary absolute sublevel sets.

<a id="minors"></a>
## The cost of small nonzero minors

Use ordered \(r\)-tuples \(I\) of embeddings with replacement and the determinant \(\Delta_I\) of their rows.
In a normal closure, every nonzero determinant has absolute height at most
\[
                             h(\Delta_I)\le \log(r!)+rWh .             \tag{3}
\]
At a finite place each determinant is bounded in modulus by \(\prod_{i\in I}\max(1,|i\alpha|)^W\), with factors repeated according to the tuple.
At an infinite place the factor \(r!\) suffices by the determinant expansion.
Taking positive logarithms, with the usual normalized global weights, proves (3) since every conjugate has height \(h\).
This also applies to any square minor of size \(k\le r\), with \(k!,kWh\).
No integrality assumption is used.

Write \(\log^- x=\max(0,-\log x)\) for \(x>0\).
For nonzero algebraic \(\Delta\), the mean of \(\log^-|g\Delta|\) over the Galois group is at most \(h(1/\Delta)=h(\Delta)\).
The set of nonzero tuples is invariant under simultaneous Galois permutation.
Averaging over the group before summing (3) thus bounds \(d^{-r}\sum_{\Delta_I\ne0}\log^-|\Delta_I|\) by \(\log(r!)+rC\).
Also \(d^{-1}\sum_\sigma\log\|v_\sigma\|_2\le\tfrac12\log r+C\).
Writing \(b_I=|\Delta_I|/\prod_{\sigma\in I}\|v_\sigma\|_2\), Hadamard and the two estimates give
\[
              d^{-r}\sum_{\Delta_I\ne0}\log(1/b_I)
                 \le A,\qquad A=\log(r!)+2rC+\tfrac r2\log r .        \tag{4}
\]
In particular repeated or proportional rows enter only with their actual labeled multiplicities.

Let \(S\) be the set in (1).
For tuples entirely in \(S\), \(b_I\le r\epsilon_0\): multiply the matrix of normalized rows by a unitary matrix of first column \(c\), expand along that column, and bound each cofactor by one using Hadamard.
Choose \(\epsilon_0=r^{-1}\exp(-1-A(2/\eta)^r)\).
If every proper subspace contained fewer than \(\eta d/2\) of all rows, there would be at least \((\eta d/2)^r\) independent ordered tuples in \(S\), by choosing each successive row outside the proper span so far.
This would contradict (4).
Hence some proper subspace contains at least \(\eta d/2\) rows.
This argument does not require that all minors be nonzero.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Extracting a partition over a bounded-degree subfield

Put \(\tau_{r-1}=\eta/2\) and, proceeding downwards, \(\tau_{j-1}=\tau_j^2/8\) for \(2\le j\le r-1\).
Choose the smallest \(1\le k<r\) for which a \(k\)-dimensional complex subspace \(V\) contains a proportion \(u\ge\tau_k\) of all rows.
A proper subspace from the preceding paragraph extends to a hyperplane, so the choices are nonempty.
The rows lying in \(V\) span \(V\), since no smaller subspace has this many rows.
The zero subspace contains none.
In particular \(V\) is defined in the normal closure of \(K\).
Its \(M\) distinct Galois conjugates have the same incidence proportion \(u\).
The intersection of two distinct conjugates contains a proportion at most \(\tau_k^2/8\), by minimality of \(k\), also for \(k=1\).

Sample a uniform embedding and count the conjugate spaces containing its row.
Cauchy–Schwarz for this count gives
\[
             M^2u^2\le Mu+M(M-1)\tau_k^2/8,\qquad M\le 2/u.
\]
The union covers every row by transitivity.
In fact the number of spaces containing each labeled row is the same positive integer, again by transitivity.
The expected number of pairs containing that row is at most \(\binom M2\tau_k^2/8<1\), so the common number of spaces must equal one.
Thus the conjugate spaces give a partition and \(Mu=1\).
In particular \(M\le M_0=\lceil2/\tau_1\rceil\).
Replace \(V\) by its unique conjugate containing the original row.
Every automorphism fixing \(\alpha\) stabilizes \(V\), so the stabilizer's fixed field \(E\) is contained in \(K\) and \([E:\mathbb Q]=M\).

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Bounded heights and integer polynomials

Choose \(k\) rows forming a basis of \(V\) and any pivot set of \(k\) columns on which they are independent.
Left multiplication by the inverse pivot matrix gives a basis matrix that has the identity on these columns and is uniquely determined by \(V\) and the pivot set.
All entries belong to \(E\); conversely a Galois element fixing all the entries must stabilize \(V\), so the entries generate \(E\).
Cramer's rule and (3) bound every entry's height by \(2(\log(k!)+kC)\), taking \(h(0)=0\).
A nonpivot coordinate is an \(E\)-linear combination of the pivot coordinates on \(V\).
Taking its coefficient to be one gives (2) with the specified heights.
There are only finitely many algebraic entries with these bounded degrees and heights: the primitive minimal polynomial has bounded degree and Mahler measure, and its coefficients are bounded by the binomial coefficients times that measure.
This proves the finite-list assertion for the basis matrices, fields and coefficient tuples.

For each tuple form the norm from \(E\) to \(\mathbb Q\) of the multivariate polynomial \(b_1+\sum_{j=2}^r b_jY_{j-1}\) by multiplying all its conjugates.
After clearing denominators it is an integer polynomial of total degree at most \(M\).
Under the power substitution the product is nonzero, since every conjugate linear form has at least one nonzero coefficient and the \(a_j\) are distinct.
It vanishes at \(\alpha\) and has the asserted bounds on terms and degree.
For the converse use the \(d/M\) embeddings of \(K\) over the identity on the coefficient field.
The argument gives the existence of these exact relations; a bound on the Mahler measure of a whole sparse multiple by itself need not bound the measure of each individual factor below.
