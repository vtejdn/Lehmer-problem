# Common double factors of signed rows at two exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## One elimination for every exponent at a prescribed distance

Fix nonzero integer rows \(A,B\) of formal widths \(b,d\), two signs \(\varepsilon,\sigma\), and an integer \(k\ge1\).
Write \(V=Y^bA(1/Y)\), \(T=Y^dB(1/Y)\), \(U=-\varepsilon A\), \(S=-\sigma B\), \(f=U/V\), \(g=S/T\), and \(H_A=\|A\|_1,\ H_B=\|B\|_1\).
For each \(n\ge2\), count every Galois orbit of nontorsion algebraic units \(c\) giving a double zero of both
\[
 A(X^n)+\varepsilon X V(X^n),\qquad B(X^{n+k})+\sigma X T(X^{n+k}),
\]
with \(A(c^n)B(c^{n+k})\ne0\).
For each counted pair \((n,[c])\) write \(t\ge2\) for the smaller vanishing order, \(D_c\) for the algebraic degree and \(m_c\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of its minimum.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum tD_c &\le b+d+kbd,\\
 \sum tn m_c &\le (kd+1)\log H_A+\log(2H_B),\\
 \sum t(n+k)m_c &\le (kb+1)\log H_B+\log(2H_A).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each sum ranges over all exponents and all counted orbits for these same two rows and signs.
The pairs can be recovered by one univariate factorization and exact arithmetic in its factor fields.

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Following a graph from one powered node to the other

Homogenize \(S,T\) to degree \(d\), writing \(S_h(C,D)=D^d S(C/D)\) and similarly for \(T_h\).
Define the integer polynomial
\[
 K(Y)=V(Y)S_h(YU(Y)^k,V(Y)^k)-U(Y)T_h(YU(Y)^k,V(Y)^k).
\]
Its degree is at most \(b+d(kb+1)\), and its one-norm is at most \(2H_A^{kd+1}H_B\) by submultiplicativity.
At \(\beta=c^n\) the nonvanishing value equations give \(c=f(\beta)=g(c^{n+k})\) and all four numerator and denominator values are nonzero at their respective nodes.
There \(K\) differs from \(g(Yf(Y)^k)-f(Y)\) by a holomorphic factor nonzero at \(\beta\).

Take a local coordinate \(X\) at \(c\) and put \(Y=X^n\).
The first graph gives \(f(X^n)=X+O((X-c)^t)\).
It follows that \(X^n f(X^n)^k=X^{n+k}+O((X-c)^t)\).
The second graph gives \(g(X^{n+k})=X+O((X-c)^t)\).
Composition with \(g\), holomorphic at \(c^{n+k}\), now shows that \(K(X^n)\) vanishes to order at least \(t\).
Since \(c\ne0\), the minimum of \(\beta\) divides \(K\) to at least this order.
For the reverse direction use \(Z/g(Z)^k\) at \(Z=c^{n+k}\).
The identical computation with the two graphs exchanged uses \(1/g(Z)^k\) for the change of argument and subtracts \(g(Z)\) from \(f(Z/g(Z)^k)\).
Clearing its denominators gives a polynomial of degree at most \(d+b(kd+1)\) and one-norm at most \(2H_B^{kb+1}H_A\).

<a id="identity"></a>
## Ruling out an identically vanishing elimination at a double unit

If \(K=0\), let \(F(Y)=Yf(Y)^k\) and \(J(Z)=Z/g(Z)^k\).
The map \(F\) cannot be constant at a counted pair, since the double equation gives \(\beta f'(\beta)/f(\beta)=1/n\), hence \(\beta F'(\beta)/F(\beta)=1+k/n\).
For nonconstant \(F\) the polynomial identity gives \(g\circ F=f\) as rational functions and \(J\circ F=Y\).
Thus \(F,J\) are inverse Möbius maps.
If the reverse elimination vanishes instead, the corresponding identity is \(F\circ J=Z\), with \(J\) nonconstant since its logarithmic derivative at the second node is \(n/(n+k)\).
The same Möbius conclusion follows.

The reciprocity \(f(1/Y)=1/f(Y)\) makes \(F\) commute with inversion.
The equation \(YF'(Y)/F(Y)=(n+k)/n\) for a rational Möbius map has a nonzero numerator of degree at most two after denominators are cleared.
Indeed an identically constant logarithmic derivative forces all zeros and poles into \(0,\infty\), and a Möbius monomial has logarithmic derivative \(1\) or \(-1\).
Consequently \(\beta\), and also \(c=f(\beta)\), have degree at most two.
A rational unit is torsion, and a nonreal quadratic unit has both conjugates of modulus one and is torsion by Kronecker's theorem.
We may therefore use a real conjugate of \(c\) with \(|c|>1\).

An inversion-commuting Möbius map permutes the two fixed points \(1,-1\).
In the coordinate \((Y-1)/(Y+1)\) it acts by multiplication by a constant or by a constant divided by the coordinate.
Taking a logarithmic differential gives \(F'(Y)/(F(Y)^2-1)=\pm1/(Y^2-1)\).
At \(\beta=c^n\) we have \(F(\beta)=c^{n+k}\).
With \(x=\log|c|>0\) this yields
\[
 \left|\frac{\beta F'(\beta)}{F(\beta)}\right|
 =\frac{\sinh((n+k)x)}{\sinh(nx)}>\frac{n+k}{n}.
\]
The inequality follows from strict increase of \(\sinh u/u\) for \(u>0\).
This contradicts the double equation and excludes both identities whenever any pair is counted.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting factors and recovering the exponent

The equalities \(c=f(c^n)=g(c^{n+k})\) identify both powered fields with \(\mathbb Q(c)\).
The minimum of \(c^n\) therefore has degree \(D_c\) and logarithmic measure \(nm_c\) by the power law for absolute height.
An orbit of \(c^n\) recovers that of \(c\) equivariantly by \(f\); for a fixed embedding of the powered node its recovered nontorsion unit permits at most one exponent.
The reverse nodes likewise give distinct factors with logarithmic measures \((n+k)m_c\).
Degree, multiplicativity of Mahler measure and \(M(K)\le\|K\|_1\) give the three bounds using the two eliminations.
If one elimination is zero there are no counted pairs by the preceding argument.

For nonzero \(K\), take its repeated monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factors and work at their residue class \(\beta\).
Discard vanishing numerator or denominator values, and compute \(c=f(\beta)\).
The double equation recovers the sole candidate \(n=c/(\beta f'(\beta))\), discarding a zero denominator or a value outside the rational integers at least two.
Test \(c^n=\beta\) and at \(z=\beta c^k\) test \(S(z)T(z)\ne0,\ g(z)=c,\ (n+k)z g'(z)=c\).
These tests suffice because the local graph equations are \(X-f(X^n)=0\) and \(X-g(X^{n+k})=0\).
As \(c^n=\beta\) is a nontorsion unit, so is \(c\).
The exact orders can then be read from the two original polynomials.

For example, if \(c\) and \(c^2\) both occur at the same pair of exponents and generate the same field, their different positive measures distinguish their orbits.
Their contributions alone require \(4D_c\le b+d+kbd\) and \(6nm_c\le(kd+1)\log H_A+\log(2H_B)\).
In the fixed-double test the widths and the rows may both vary with \(n\), so these inequalities do not supply a uniform exclusion.
