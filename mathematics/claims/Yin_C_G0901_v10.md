# Short rows attaining both local divisibility bounds after increasing the width

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quantitative transfer for an ordinary double-contact pair

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(e\).
At an odd prime \(p\), let \(m_C\) be the multiplicities of the distinct roots of \(\bar P\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), and suppose \(p\ge3\max_Cm_C\).
Write \(y=\alpha^p,\ Q=\min_{\mathbb Q}(y)\) and \(J_p=\sum_Cm_C^2\).
At an integer width \(B\ge e+1\), suppose an ordinary qualifying pair \(G=(U,V)\) is given.
Thus \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) are nonzero and coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), their degrees are at most \(B\), \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\), and \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), with \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\).
Set \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) in coefficient one-norms and put
\[
     s=B+\min(B,2e)-1,\qquad d=2(s+1-e),\qquad
     A=\max\{H,\|Q\|_1^2\}.
\]
There is another ordinary qualifying pair \((U_1,V_1)\) for the original \(P,p\), of width at most \(s\) and height at most \(p d(d+3)A\), such that
\[
       v_p\!\left(N_{\mathbb Q(y)/\mathbb Q}V_1(y)\right)
           =v_p\operatorname{Res}(U_1,V_1)=J_p .                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(B=e+k,\ 1\le k<e\), permits width \(2e+2k-1\).
At \(B=e\) the [minimum-width theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v7.md#statement) gives a height comparison at width \(e\) itself.

For any sequence of reciprocal nontorsion unit minima of degrees \(e\to\infty\) and measures \(h=m(P)\to0\), the exact equalities (1) can therefore be imposed at width \((2+o(1))e\) and normalized log height \(o(1)\) at every prime \(p\ge ae\) for each fixed \(a>0\).
The normalization divides by \(p\), and the bounds are uniform in those primes on the original seeds.
The same conclusion holds in the [linear and sublinear range](Yin_C_G0901_v5.md#short) described below.

<a id="span"></a>
## A full rational spanning set of bounded height

The [local divided-difference calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v5.md#local) ensures that the \(e\) powered nodes \(y_i=x_i^p\), for conjugates \(x_i\) of \(\alpha\), remain distinct.
In \(S=\mathbb Q[Y]/Q^2\) there is a unique Hermite interpolation class \(T\) with \(T(y_i)=x_i\) and \(T'(y_i)=x_i/(p y_i)\) at every \(i\).
The values and derivatives determine the class over the splitting field, and their Galois equivariance gives rational coefficients.
The image of a contact pair in \(S^2\) obeys \(U+TV=0\), by both contact equations.
Conversely this equality suffices for the double contact.
All these classes form a free rank-one \(S\)-module, generated by the class of \(G\), since \(V(y_i)\ne0\) at every node by coprimality and the graph value.
In particular the shifts \(Y^jG,\ 0\le j<2e\), already span this module over \(\mathbb Q\).

We can use fewer shifts when \(B<2e\).
The swapped reversal \(G^*=(Y^BV(1/Y),Y^BU(1/Y))\) is another pair in the contact space, by reciprocity of \(P\).
The two polynomial pairs have nonzero determinant \(Y^B(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\).
Let \(W_j\subset S^2\) be the rational span of \(Y^iG,Y^iG^*\) for \(0\le i\le j\).
At \(j_0=2e-1-B\ge0\) these pairs are independent: a vanishing linear combination in \(S^2\) has components of degree below \(2e\), hence vanishes as polynomials, and independence of \(G,G^*\) over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\) gives all coefficients zero.
Thus \(\dim W_{j_0}=2(j_0+1)\).
If \(W_{j+1}=W_j\), then \(YW_j\subset W_j\), and this space contains the cyclic generator \(G\), so its dimension is already \(2e\).
It follows that \(W_{B-1}\) is the full module.
Together with the single-pair construction for \(B\ge2e\), this provides representatives of a rational spanning set with degrees at most \(s\) and height at most \(H\).

In the full polynomial contact space at width \(s\), the kernel of reduction to \(S^2\) consists exactly of the pairs of multiples of \(Q^2\).
Add the pairs \((Y^jQ^2,0),(0,Y^jQ^2)\), \(0\le j\le s-2e\), to span that kernel.
The resulting height bound is \(A\) and the dimension is \(2e+2(s-2e+1)=d\).
All these spanning vectors are integral.

<a id="profiles"></a>
## Residue profiles ensuring both equalities at the larger width

Let \(\Lambda\) be the lattice of integral contact pairs of width \(s\), initially without the coprimality and reflected-determinant requirements.
Its localization over \(\mathbb Z_p\) is the full integral kernel there, by the Smith form of the map taking the remainder modulo the monic \(P^2\).
The [integral top-coordinate isomorphism](Yin_C_G0901_v7.md#tops) at width \(e\) gives local pairs reducing to \((\bar Q,0)\) and \((0,\bar Q)\), since the polynomial of lifted centers used there reduces to \(\bar Q\).
Multiplying these two pairs by arbitrary integral lifts of polynomials of degrees at most \(s-e\) shows that all reductions \((\bar Q a,\bar Q b)\) with these degree bounds on \(a,b\) are accessible in \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Z_p\).

Take \(b=1\).
For each residue \(\xi\) of a root of \(P\) of multiplicity \(m\), prescribe the value of \(a(\xi^p)\) to avoid \(0\) and \((-1)^m\xi\) in \(\mathbb F_p(\xi^p)=\mathbb F_p(\xi)\).
At least one choice exists because \(p\ge3\).
Make the choices equivariantly on Frobenius orbits.
Also require \(a(0)=1\), where zero is distinct from all the powered residues.
The Chinese remainder theorem over \(\mathbb F_p\) now permits a monic \(a\) of degree exactly \(s-e\): the modulus \(Y\operatorname{rad}(\bar Q)\) has degree at most \(e+1\le s-e\), and a suitable monomial multiple of it can be added to the representative of lower degree.
This polynomial has nonzero constant term and positive degree.

We verify that these ordinary coefficient residues suffice for every contact pair with the given reduction, including the valuations beyond first order.
In a class with \(m=m_C\) and Teichmüller lift \(\omega\) of \(\xi\), use \(c=\omega^p\) and the scaled chart \(Y=c(1+pZ)\).
The [Taylor divisibilities](Yin_C_G0901_v6.md#jets) make \(p^{-m}U_1(c(1+pZ))\) and \(p^{-m}V_1(c(1+pZ))\) integral.
Their reductions \(\widehat U,\widehat V\) have degree at most \(m\), since all terms of order greater than \(m\) carry an additional \(p\).
Their degree-\(m\) coefficients have ratio \(a(\bar c):1\).
Indeed the unscaled Taylor coefficient in degree \(m\) reduces to \(\prod_{D\ne C}(\bar c-\bar c_D)^{m_D}\) times the value of the respective quotient by \(\bar Q\), and scaling multiplies both by \(\bar c^m\).
The resulting pair satisfies the reduced Hermite equation
\(\widehat U+\xi E(Z)\widehat V=0\bmod Z^{2m}\) with \(E(Z)=\sum_{j<2m}Z^j/j!\).
The [classification of its top lines](Yin_C_G0901_v7.md#lines) concerns precisely these local polynomials of degree at most \(m\).
Since \(a(\bar c)\ne(-1)^m\xi\), it gives \(\widehat V(0)\ne0\) and coprimality of \(\widehat U,\widehat V\).
Every original powered node in this class has \(v_p(Z)>0\), so \(V_1\) takes valuation exactly \(m\) at each of them, proving the norm equality in (1).

The first row has a unit leading coefficient in degree \(s\), and its reduction has exactly \(m\) roots at \(\bar c\), counted with multiplicity, because \(a(\bar c)\ne0\).
At any first-row root in that residue class one has \(v_p(Y-c)\ge1\): at a distance \(0<v_p(Y-c)<1\) its unit Taylor term in degree \(m\) would be strictly smallest in valuation, by the Taylor divisibilities in lower degrees and integrality in higher degrees.
Coprimality of \(\widehat U,\widehat V\) now gives valuation exactly \(m\) for \(V_1\) at each of these \(m\) roots.
All other first-row roots are integral and reduce to points outside \(\bar Q\) where \(\bar V_1=\bar Q\) is nonzero.
The product formula for the resultant gives its equality in (1) and shows coprimality over \(\mathbb Q\).
Finally \(\bar U_1\bar U_1^\iota-\bar V_1\bar V_1^\iota=\bar Q\bar Q^\iota(aa^\iota-1)\ne0\), since \(a\) has distinct occupied endpoints.
This proves the required properties solely from contact and the prescribed residues.

## The height of an integral lift

From the [short spanning set](#span) choose \(d\) independent integral vectors \(w_i\), all of height at most \(A\).
Use the norm on real pairs given by the maximum of their two coefficient one-norms.
Let \(\Lambda_i\) be the intersection of \(\Lambda\) with the rational span of \(w_1,\ldots,w_i\).
The quotient \(\Lambda_i/\Lambda_{i-1}\) is infinite cyclic.
Choose a generator whose coefficient on \(w_i\) is positive; this coefficient is at most one since \(w_i\in\Lambda_i\).
Subtract integer multiples of \(w_1,\ldots,w_{i-1}\) to make each of its other coefficients in this basis at most \(1/2\) in absolute value.
The resulting vectors form an integral basis of \(\Lambda\) by induction, and the \(i\)-th one has height at most \((1+(i-1)/2)A\).
The residue pair constructed above is realized in \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Z_p\), hence by an integer combination of this basis with coefficients of absolute value at most \((p-1)/2\) upon reduction.
The triangle inequality gives height at most \(p d(d+3)A/8\), which proves the stated bound.

## Coefficient size of the powered minimum

For a monic squarefree integer polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(e\), write \(m=m(Q)\), \(a=(\log(2e)+m)/e\).
Uniformly as \(a\to0\),
\[
                \frac{\log\|Q\|_1}{e}\ll\sqrt{a\log(2/a)} .             \tag{2}
\]
Here is a direct estimate for varying polynomials.
Set \(n=\lceil e\sqrt{a/\log(2/a)}\rceil\), \(r=n/e\), so \(n\to\infty,\ r\to0\).
For \(|z|=1,\ Q(z)\ne0\), append to the roots of \(Q\) the vertices of a regular \(n\)-gon of radius \(r\) around \(z\).
Rotate it so that the log of the product of \(|Q|\) on those vertices is at least \(n\log|Q(z)|\): the mean over the rotation has this property by the circle mean inequality for each linear factor.
The original Vandermonde determinant has modulus at least one because its square is a nonzero integer up to sign, and the polygon alone has determinant of modulus \(n^{n/2}r^{n(n-1)/2}\).
In the combined determinant the root-row norms are at most \(\sqrt{e+n}\max(1,|\gamma|)^{e+n-1}\) at each original root \(\gamma\), and each polygon row has norm at most \(\sqrt{e+n}(1+r)^{e+n-1}\).
Factoring the combined determinant and applying Hadamard's bound gives
\[
 \log|Q(z)|\le
 \frac{e+n}{2n}\log(e+n)+\frac{e+n-1}{n}m+
 (e+n-1)\log(1+r)+\frac{n-1}{2}\log(1/r)-\tfrac12\log n .
\]
After division by \(e\), these terms are \(O(a/(n/e)+(n/e)\log(2e/n))\), on dropping the negative term.
Our choice \(a\ge\log(2e)/e\) ensures the ceiling in \(n\) changes this order by at most an absolute factor.
Cauchy's coefficient bound on the unit circle now gives (2).

## Original witnesses of small measure

Consider any sequence of reciprocal nontorsion unit minima with \(h\to0\) and \(e\to\infty\).
Use either a fixed positive linear prime cutoff or the range \(p\ge e\theta\) with \(0<\theta\le1\), \(\theta\ge C\log\log(3e)/\log(3e)\) and \(h\log(2/h)=o(\theta)\) from the [original-seed construction](Yin_C_G0901_v5.md#short).
It gives, at each such prime, the original pair of normalized log height \(o(1)\), local threshold \(p\ge3\max_Cm_C\), and a common width allowance \(B=e+o(e)\), uniformly.
Enlarge the allowance to at least \(e+1\) if necessary.
The theorem gives \(s=2e+o(e)\) and \(d=O(e)\).
The prefactor \(p d(d+3)\) has log divided by \(p\) tending uniformly to zero.

It remains to bound \(\log\|Q\|_1/p\).
Here \(m(Q)=ph\) by degree preservation.
Writing \(\sigma=p/e\), along any subsequence where \(\sigma\to\infty\) the elementary estimate \(\|Q\|_1\le2^e\exp(ph)\) suffices.
Along a subsequence with bounded \(\sigma\ge\theta\), (2) applies since \(a=\log(2e)/e+\sigma h\to0\).
Eventually \(\theta\ge h\), so \(a\ge h^2\) and also \(a\ge\log(2e)/e\).
Taking square roots termwise in (2) therefore gives
\[
       \frac{\log\|Q\|_1}{p}\ll
          \sqrt{\frac{h\log(2/h)}{\theta}}+
                    \frac{\log(2e)}{\theta\sqrt e}\ \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
Any failure of uniform convergence would have a subsequence of one of the two kinds just treated.
This proves the asserted uniform shortness with both prime valuations exact.
Conversely a sequence of such qualifying pairs with normalized log heights tending to zero forces vanishing of the unrestricted infimum by the [individual measure bound](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
Thus even exact local sharpness on the original input at this enlarged width cannot by itself exclude a shrinking sequence.
This argument gives no comparison with the height minimum at the original width \(e+k\).
