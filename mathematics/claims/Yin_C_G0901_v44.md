# The complete positive spectrum below two in a Salem field

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small elements and the exact degrees of their rational expressions

Let \(K\) be generated by a Salem number of degree \(d\ge6\) in its embedding with Salem value greater than one.
There is a primitive Salem unit \(\epsilon>1\) generating \(K\), unique with this orientation among generators of the relative norm-one unit group modulo torsion.
Put \(h_0=m(\epsilon)=\log\epsilon\), \(B=\mathbb Q(\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1})\), and \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
The elements \(y\in K^\times\) with \(0<m(y)<\log2\) are exactly

* the signed powers \(\pm\epsilon^k\) with \(0<|k|h_0<\log2\);
* the four numbers \(\pm\phi,\pm\phi^{-1}\) when \(\sqrt5\in B\).

In particular every element in this range outside the trace field is a signed power of the same Salem generator.
No integrality or reciprocity hypothesis on \(y\) is needed.

Fix \(h_0\le M<\log2\), put \(k_*=\lfloor M/h_0\rfloor\), and suppose \(2k_*\le d\).
The least degree of a nonconstant rational function \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\), excluding all signed powers as functions, such that \(0<m(R(\epsilon))\le M\) is exactly
\[
 \begin{cases}
 d/2,&\sqrt5\in B\ \text{and}\ M\ge\log\phi,\\
 d-k_*,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                            \tag{1}
\]
All values under consideration are finite and nonzero.
For a fixed bound \(M<\log2\) and large \(d\) the condition on \(k_*\) holds automatically whenever \(h_0\le M\).
A putative sequence of Salem numbers tending to one can be replaced by these primitive generators in the same fields with no larger measures.
The fields in that existence question may vary.

<a id="subfields"></a>
## Every element outside the totally real trace field generates the Salem field

Begin with the given Salem number \(\alpha>1\), let \(\tau\) invert \(\alpha\), and put \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})=K^\tau\).
The field \(B\) is totally real because the two real images of \(\alpha\) are positive inverses and all the other images have modulus one.
For an element \(y\in K\setminus B\), its two real images in the chosen real embeddings of \(K\) are distinct, since they are \(y,\tau y\).
At each nonreal embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K\) one has \(\bar\sigma=\sigma\circ\tau\) on the generator and hence on \(K\).
Thus \(\sigma(y)\) is nonreal.
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(y)\) extends to \([K:\mathbb Q(y)]\) embeddings of \(K\).
The two distinct real images of \(y\) account for all embeddings giving a real value of \(y\), and each has only one extension.
It follows that \(\mathbb Q(y)=K\).
Every element of a proper subfield consequently lies in \(B\).

<a id="units"></a>
## Relative norm one and the single possible quadratic input

Write \(d=2n\).
The signatures of \(K\) and \(B\) are respectively \((2,n-1)\) and \((n,0)\).
By Dirichlet's unit theorem the ranks of their units are \(n\) and \(n-1\).
The relative norm of units contains all squares of units of \(B\), so its kernel has rank one.
Its torsion is \(\{1,-1\}\) because \(K\subset\mathbb R\).
Choose a representative \(\epsilon\) of a generator modulo torsion, using sign and inversion to make its chosen real value greater than one.
Its second real image is its positive inverse, and every nonreal embedding gives modulus one by the action of \(\tau\).
Since \(\epsilon\notin B\), it generates \(K\) and is a Salem number with the same trace field.
It is primitive even in \(\mathcal O_K^\times/\{1,-1\}\).
Indeed, if a positive power of a unit \(v\) lies in the kernel, then \(N_{K/B}(v)\) is a root of unity in \(B\).
This root is positive at every real place of \(B\) which becomes complex in \(K\), so it must be one.
Thus the kernel modulo torsion is saturated.
The original \(\alpha\) belongs to the kernel and is a positive power of \(\epsilon\) by its real value.
Every nonzero power of \(\epsilon\) generates \(K\), as follows either from its two distinct real images or from its unique dominant conjugate.

Let \(c=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)=\tfrac12\log\phi\).
For any nontorsion nonzero totally real number \(z\), [Garza's bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) gives \(h(z)\ge c\).
Here \(3c>\log2\) since \(\phi^3=2+\sqrt5>4\).
Every \(y\) in the stated range is an algebraic unit, since a nonunit nonzero extreme coefficient of its primitive integer minimum costs at least \(\log2\) by applying the Mahler bound at both ends.
If \(y\in B\), the real-conjugate bound forces its degree to be at most two.
Rational units are torsion.
For a degree-two nontorsion real unit of measure less than \(\log2\), its larger absolute conjugate \(R\) is in \((1,2)\).
At constant coefficient \(+1\) its absolute trace is \(R+1/R\) strictly between two and \(5/2\), which is impossible.
At constant coefficient \(-1\) its absolute trace is \(R-1/R\) strictly between zero and \(3/2\), hence equal to one.
This gives exactly the listed four quadratic units, present precisely when \(\sqrt5\in B\).

If \(y\notin B\), it generates \(K\).
The height of its norm \(y\tau(y)\in B\) is at most \(2h(y)<2\log(2)/d\le(\log2)/3<c\).
Hence the norm is torsion, and positivity at any real place that becomes complex gives norm one.
This proves the classification from the kernel above.

<a id="maps"></a>
## Matching the degree bounds by explicit rational maps

For any allowed \(R=A/D\) in reduced form taking the value \(\pm\epsilon^k\), clearing the denominator and any negative power gives a nonzero rational polynomial of degree at most \(\deg R+|k|\) vanishing at \(\epsilon\).
It is nonzero by the signed-power exclusion.
Thus \(\deg R\ge d-|k|\).
Conversely let \(1\le k\le d/2\) and split the monic minimal polynomial of \(\epsilon\) as \(P(X)=X^kD(X)+A(X)\) with \(\deg A<k\), \(\deg D=d-k\).
Then \(D(\epsilon)\ne0\) by degree, and \(-A(\epsilon)/D(\epsilon)=\epsilon^k\).
This rational function cannot be a signed power: agreement at the real \(\epsilon>1\) would force that function to be \(X^k\) identically, contrary to \(P\ne0\).
It is nonconstant since \(\epsilon^k\) has degree \(d\).
Reduction and optional sign or inversion therefore realize the lower bound for each signed power with \(1\le |k|\le d/2\).

For an allowed map taking a quadratic value, \(d\le 2\deg R\) follows by its equation over that quadratic field.
If \(\sqrt5\in B\), take the monic minimum of \(\epsilon\) over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) of degree \(d/2\).
Writing it as \(A(X)+\sqrt5\,D(X)\) with rational coefficients gives \(\deg A=d/2\), \(\deg D<d/2\), and \(D\ne0\) by the degree over \(\mathbb Q\).
Hence \(D(\epsilon)\ne0\) and \(-A(\epsilon)/D(\epsilon)=\sqrt5\).
Composing with the rational affine map giving \(\phi\) realizes degree \(d/2\), and the resulting function cannot be a signed power since the value has degree two.
These constructions prove (1).
Finally the [relative height bound at rational base](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base), specialized to the trivial abelian extension, gives \(h_0\gg(\log\log(5d))^3/(\log(2d))^4\).
Thus \(k_*=o(d)\) uniformly at fixed \(M\).
