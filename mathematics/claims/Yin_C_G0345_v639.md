# Near equality in the energy bound for integral numerators

<a id="statement"></a>
## Remainders and root angles at the limiting constant

Let \(P\) range over monic integer polynomials of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) be indivisible by \(P\), put \(m=\log M(P)\) and \(E=\|A/P\|_2^2\) for Haar probability measure on the circle, and suppose \(m\to0\).
The [two-ended division bound](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#statement) gives \(\liminf mE\ge1/4\).
We characterize sequences attaining this lower limit and derive a restriction on the angles of the roots.

Choose any [balanced division](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#division) \(X^{-j}A=PQ+R\) after translating the smallest exponent of \(A\) to zero.
Here \(R\ne0\) has degree less than \(d\), and the numbers \(n_-,n_+\) of occupied negative and nonnegative positions of \(Q\) differ by at most one.
For an input of width less than \(d\), use \(R=A\) after translation and \(Q=0\).
Write \(n=\max(n_-,n_+)\), \(h=\operatorname{ord}_0 R\), and \(a=[X^h]R\).
Let \(S=X^{-h}R/a\), and let \(S_*\) be the unique complex polynomial of degree less than \(d\) and constant coefficient one that minimizes \(\|S_*/P\|_2\).
Set \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\).
Then \(mE\to1/4\) if and only if
\[
 |a|=1\ \text{eventually},\qquad
 m\|Q\|_2^2\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 m\|(S-S_*)/P\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.                              \tag{1}
\]
When \(mE\to1/4\), put \(\Delta=m(E-C+1/2)\).
For all sufficiently late indices,
\[
 mn\le\sqrt{2\Delta},\qquad
 m\|Q\|_2^2\le2\sqrt{2\Delta}+2\Delta,\qquad
 m\|(S-S_*)/P\|_2^2\le2\Delta.                                    \tag{2}
\]

Place a probability measure \(\mu_P\) on the circle by giving each root \(\alpha\) mass \(\bigl|\log|\alpha|\bigr|/(2m)\) at \(\alpha/|\alpha|\), counting multiplicities.
The interior and exterior roots each contribute mass \(1/2\), since \(|P(0)|=1\).
If \(mE\to1/4\), then for each fixed \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \left|[z^k]\frac{P(0) S(z)}{P(z)}-\int e^{-ik\theta}\,d\mu_P(\theta)\right|
       =O_k(\sqrt{\Delta}+m).                                        \tag{3}
\]
Here the Taylor coefficient at zero is an integer once \(|a|=1\).
Every weak limit of \(\mu_P\) is Haar measure on the circle or the uniform measure on the \(N\) solutions of \(z^N=\sigma\) for some \(N\ge1\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\).
Along any subsequence with such a weak limit, the Taylor series of \(P(0)S/P\) agrees, to each fixed order eventually, with \(1\) in the Haar case or with \(1/(1-\sigma z^N)\) in the finite case.
These conditions apply to sequences attaining the energy floor and leave the existence of such a sequence open.

## Support and norm bounds for near equality

Use the scaled quantities \(x=mn\), \(u=\sqrt{1-e^{-2x}}\), \(\rho=\sqrt m\,\|R/P\|_2\), \(\beta=\sqrt m\,\|Q\|_2\), and \(c=\sqrt{mC}\).
The [projection inequalities](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#projections), integrality and the [short-width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement) give
\[
 mE\ge \rho^2+\beta^2-2u\rho\beta,\qquad
 \rho\ge |a|c,\qquad \beta^2\ge 2x-m .
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
They also hold for \(Q=0\).
We have \(c\to1/2\) with \(c\le1/2\).
Assume \(mE\to1/4\).
If \(n>\lfloor(\log2)/(2m)\rfloor=l\) infinitely often, then each side contains at least \(l\) occupied positions at those indices.
The two-ended projection estimate gives \(mE\ge2ml e^{-2ml}\to(\log2)/2>1/4\), a contradiction.
Thus \(x\le(\log2)/2\) eventually, and \(u^2\le1/2\).
Completing squares in (4) gives \(mE\ge(1-u^2)\max(\rho^2,\beta^2)\), so the scaled quantities are bounded.

Pass to any convergent subsequence of \((x,\rho,\beta)\), denoting limits by \(x_0,\rho_0,\beta_0\) and \(u_0=\sqrt{1-e^{-2x_0}}\).
The completed square gives \(u_0\beta_0\le\sqrt{1-u_0^2}\,\beta_0\le1/2\).
Thus the limiting quadratic in (4) is increasing for \(\rho_0\ge1/2\), and
\[
 \tfrac14\ge\tfrac14+\beta_0(\beta_0-u_0).
\]
If \(x_0>0\), then \(\beta_0^2\ge2x_0>1-e^{-2x_0}=u_0^2\), a contradiction.
Hence \(x_0=u_0=\beta_0=0\), and (4) gives \(\rho_0=1/2\).
This holds for every convergent subsequence.
In particular \(|a|=1\) eventually by (4).

For all sufficiently late indices \(u\beta\le c/2\), so \(\rho+c-2u\beta\ge(\rho+c)/2\).
Subtract \(c^2\) in (4), use \(2c\le1\), and write \(2\beta(\beta-u)=(\beta-u)^2+\beta^2-u^2\).
This gives
\[
 2\Delta\ge(\rho^2-c^2)+(\beta-u)^2+
         \bigl(\beta^2-2x+m\bigr)+\bigl(2x-1+e^{-2x}\bigr).         \tag{5}
\]
Every term on the right is nonnegative.
On \(0\le x\le(\log2)/2\) the last term is at least \(x^2\), since it and its first derivative vanish at zero and its second derivative is \(4e^{-2x}\ge2\).
This proves the first two inequalities of (2) and \(\rho^2-c^2\le2\Delta\).

<a id="extremizer"></a>
## Distance to the short extremizer and its Taylor coefficients

Take the [reflected polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) \(O\) with \(|O|=|P|\) on the circle, \(O(0)=e^m\), and only exterior roots.
Put \(O^*(z)=z^d\overline{O(1/\bar z)}\), \(J=O^*/O\), and \(b_0=J(0)\), so \(|b_0|=e^{-2m}\).
The extremizer from that calculation is
\[
 S_*=\frac{O-\overline{b_0}O^*}{O(0)(1-|b_0|^2)}.
\]
Its quotient by \(O\) is orthogonal to every \(H/O\) with \(\deg H<d\) and \(H(0)=0\), by the evaluation identity there.
Uniqueness follows, and
\[
  \|S/P\|_2^2=C+\|(S-S_*)/P\|_2^2.                                \tag{6}
\]
For \(|a|=1\) the left side equals \(\rho^2/m\), so (5) gives the last inequality of (2).
Conversely, under (1), (6) yields \(m\|R/P\|_2^2\to1/4\).
The triangle inequality and its reverse applied to \(Q+R/P\) give \(mE\to1/4\).

Write \(H^2\) for the nonnegative Fourier modes in circle \(L^2\), and \(K_J=H^2\ominus JH^2\).
For \(\deg H<d\), the quotient \(H/O\) belongs to \(K_J\), because \(\bar J H/O=H/O^*\) on the circle has only strictly negative modes.
The projection of \(z^j\), \(j\ge0\), on \(K_J\) has norm at most \(\sqrt{1-|b_0|^2}\), since its component along the unit vector \(Jz^j\) has modulus \(|b_0|\).
Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to \((S-S_*)/O\) and this projection.
By (2) and \(1-e^{-4m}\le4m\),
\[
  \left|[z^j]\frac{S-S_*}{O}\right|\le\sqrt{8\Delta}
                   \qquad(j\ge0).                                   \tag{7}
\]
The function \(U=P/O\) is analytic on the closed disk and has modulus one on the circle, with \(|U(0)|=e^{-m}\).
Its Taylor coefficients have modulus at most one, and \(|U(0)|\ge1/2\) eventually.
Formal division by \(U\) in (7) therefore bounds each fixed coefficient of \((S-S_*)/P\) by \(O_j(\sqrt\Delta)\), by induction on \(j\).

<a id="angles"></a>
## Taylor expansion in the radial displacements

Set \(V=O^*/P\), \(F=U/U(0)\) and \(G=V/V(0)\).
Since \(J=UV\), the formula for \(S_*\) becomes
\[
        \frac{P(0)S_*}{P}=\frac{F^{-1}-e^{-4m}G}{1-e^{-4m}}.         \tag{8}
\]
Let \(I\) consist of the interior roots of \(P\), and \(T\) of the points \(1/\bar\alpha\) for its exterior roots, each with multiplicity.
Cancellation of the factors of \(P/O\) and \(O^*/P\) gives
\[
    F(z)=\prod_{\zeta\in I}\frac{1-z/\zeta}{1-\bar\zeta z},
     \qquad G(z)=\prod_{\zeta\in T}\frac{1-z/\zeta}{1-\bar\zeta z}.
\]
For \(\zeta=e^{-\ell+i\theta}\) in either multiset, \(0<\ell\le m\), and each multiset has total \(\ell\) equal to \(m\).
For fixed \(k\ge1\) the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(-\log F\) is
\[
     D_{I,k}=\sum_{\zeta\in I}\frac{2\sinh(k\ell)}{k}\,e^{-ik\theta}
            =2\sum_{\zeta\in I}\ell e^{-ik\theta}+O_k(m^3).
\]
The error follows from \(\sum\ell^3\le m^3\).
The same formula defines \(D_{T,k}=[z^k](-\log G)\).
Through any fixed order all these logarithmic coefficients are \(O_k(m)\).
Exponentiation gives \([z^k]F^{-1}=D_{I,k}+O_k(m^2)\) and \([z^k]G=-D_{T,k}+O_k(m^2)\), because all terms beyond the linear one have at least two such factors.
Using \(1-e^{-4m}=4m+O(m^2)\) in (8) gives
\[
    [z^k]\frac{P(0)S_*}{P}
       =\frac1{2m}\sum_{\zeta\in I\cup T}\ell e^{-ik\theta}+O_k(m),
\]
where the sum counts both multisets with multiplicity.
Reflection preserves the angle of each exterior root, so this sum divided by \(2m\) is \(\widehat\mu_P(k)\).
The fixed-coefficient consequence of (7) proves (3).
For reciprocal \(P\) the two multisets agree and \(P(0)=1\).
At \(k=1\), where exponentiation has no higher-order terms, (8) then gives the exact identity
\[
   [z]S_*-[z]P
       =2\coth(2m)\sum_{\zeta\in I}\sinh(\ell)\cos\theta .
\]

<a id="limits"></a>
## Classification of the limiting measures

The coefficients of \(P(0)S/P\) at zero are integers for all sufficiently late indices because \(S\) is then integral and \(P(0)=\pm1\).
If \(\mu_P\) converges weakly to \(\mu\) along a subsequence, (3) gives \(\widehat\mu(k)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) for every \(k\ge1\).
The coefficients at negative indices are their complex conjugates and \(\widehat\mu(0)=1\).
Let \(H=\{k\in\mathbb Z:|\widehat\mu(k)|=1\}\).
For \(k\in H\), equality in the triangle inequality makes \(z^{-k}\) constant \(\mu\)-almost everywhere with value \(\widehat\mu(k)\).
Multiplication and complex conjugation show that \(H\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\) and its coefficient values form a character.
If \(H=\{0\}\), all nonconstant coefficients vanish, so \(\mu\) is Haar measure by density of trigonometric polynomials.
Otherwise \(H=N\mathbb Z\) and \(\sigma=\widehat\mu(N)\in\{-1,1\}\).
Now \(\widehat\mu(jN)=\sigma^j\), while the other coefficients vanish.
The uniform measure on \(z^N=\sigma\) has exactly these coefficients, proving the classification.
Finally, an integer sequence converging to an integer is eventually constant.
Applying this at the finitely many positions of each fixed Taylor truncation proves the asserted exact stabilization.
