# Alternating sections and the Mahler measures of numbers solvable by radicals

<a id="statement"></a>
## Local finiteness of measures when a simple section is excluded

Let \(G_\alpha\) be the Galois group of the splitting field of the rational minimal polynomial of a nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\).
A *section* of a finite group is a quotient of one of its subgroups.
For every fixed integer \(s\ge5\) and real \(M>0\), the set
\[
 \bigl\{m(\alpha):\ 0<m(\alpha)\le M,\quad A_s
               \text{ is not a section of }G_\alpha\bigr\}
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]
is finite, where \(A_s\) is the alternating group and \(m(\alpha)=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]h(\alpha)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular this spectrum is locally finite for **all algebraic numbers solvable by radicals**, with no bound on the derived length, the original degree or the coefficients.
There is a positive uniform Mahler gap in each of these classes.
The same conclusions hold throughout towers mixing arbitrary radical adjunctions with arbitrary algebraic adjunctions of bounded degree, with the bound on degree fixed for the class and no bound on tower length.

More precisely, group the rational conjugates of \(\alpha\) into classes under root-of-unity quotient, and let \(\gamma\) be the product of the conjugates in the class of \(\alpha\).
Then \(m(\gamma)=m(\alpha)\), and distinct conjugates of \(\gamma\) have nontorsion quotient.
Under the hypotheses of (1), \(\gamma\) ranges over a finite set depending only on \(M,s\).
This allows the original degrees to be unbounded.
For example \(2^{1/n}\) has solvable splitting field, degree \(n\) and constant measure \(\log2\).

The quantitative group constraint uses \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\) and
\[
    V(d)=\sqrt{\frac{\log\log(16d)}{\log(2+\log\log(16d))}} .
\]
For \(0<m(\alpha)\le M\) and sufficiently large \(d\) depending on \(M\), every minimal block of size at least two in the [action on multiplicative dependence classes](#labels) has an induced primitive group with a section \(A_j\), where \(j\ge c V(d)\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
The section also occurs in \(G_\alpha\) and in \(G_\alpha^{(\infty)}/(G_\alpha^{(\infty)}\cap R(G_\alpha))\), where \(G^{(\infty)}\) is the eventual term of the derived series and \(R(G)\) the largest solvable normal subgroup.
Along a sequence with \(0<m(\alpha)\to0\), the compressed degrees \(d\) tend to infinity, so this forces alternating sections of unbounded degree.

<a id="labels"></a>
## Products of conjugates and multiplicative dependence

Assume \(\alpha\) is nontorsion and let \(f\) be the common size of its torsion-quotient classes, which are permuted transitively.
Every class product is \(z^f\) times a root of unity for any member \(z\) of that class.
If the products of two classes had torsion quotient, their representatives would also have torsion quotient.
The products therefore form exactly \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]/f\) distinct conjugates of \(\gamma\).
As \(h(\gamma)=f h(\alpha)\), this proves \(m(\gamma)=m(\alpha)\) for arbitrary algebraic numbers, including nonunits.
The splitting group of \(\gamma\) is a quotient of \(G_\alpha\).
This is the [class-product compression](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers), with the height identity accounting for finite places as well.

On the conjugates of \(\gamma\), identify \(x,y\) if \(x/y\) or \(xy\) is a root of unity.
This is the relation of belonging to the same pair \(\{v,-v\}\) after mapping into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}/\mu_\infty\), written additively.
Since there are no torsion quotients between distinct conjugates, each label contains at most two actual roots.
All labels have the same size \(w\in\{1,2\}\) by Galois transitivity, and their number is \(d/w\).
Two roots with different labels are multiplicatively independent.
Indeed a dependence would give \(x^a=y^b\) for nonzero integers \(a,b\), and equality of the positive heights gives \(|a|=|b|\).
This is precisely the quotient or product condition just excluded.
In the reciprocal unit case these labels are the ordinary inverse pairs.

Write \(G\) for the splitting group of \(\gamma\).
A nonempty set \(B\) of labels is a block if each \(g\in G\) either preserves it or carries it to a disjoint set.
Take **any** such block of size \(b\ge2\), allowing the entire label set, and put \(u=d/(wb)\) for its number of translates.
Its setwise stabilizer is transitive on \(B\).
Let \(H\) be the permutation group it induces on these \(b\) labels.
If \(B\) is minimal among blocks of size at least two, \(H\) is primitive: a smaller nontrivial block for its action would again be a block for \(G\), since elements outside the stabilizer take \(B\) to a disjoint set.

<a id="block"></a>
## Rank and joint degrees inside one block

Choose two roots of \(\gamma\) on different labels of \(B\).
Once the first image is chosen, the second image must belong to the same translated block, giving at most \(wb\le2b\) possibilities.
Their joint absolute degree \(J_2\) is at most \(2db\), and also at most \(d^2\).
The [two-coordinate height product inequality](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) at these independent roots gives
\[
 \left(\frac{m(\gamma)}{d}\right)^2
     \gg \frac1{J_2(\log(3J_2))^{212}},
 \qquad b\gg \frac{d}{m(\gamma)^2(\log(3d))^{212}} .                    \tag{2}
\]
This adapts the [inverse-pair partition bound](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) to every such block, allowing torsion products and arbitrary signatures.
Thus \(\log b\gg\log d\) for \(m(\gamma)\le M\) and large \(d\).

Let \(\Gamma_B\) be the group generated in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\) by all \(wb\) roots over \(B\), and let \(r\) be its rank.
The image \(H^\sharp\) of the setwise stabilizer on those actual roots surjects onto \(H\).
It acts faithfully on the lattice \(\Gamma_B/\mathrm{torsion}\).
Indeed an element acting trivially sends each root to one with torsion quotient and therefore fixes that root.
The action on \(\Gamma_B\) is well defined from the actual root permutation since these roots generate it.
Every finite subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\) injects under reduction modulo three.
To see this, an element of prime order \(\ell\) in the kernel would have the form \(I+3^aT\) with \(a\ge1\) and some entry of \(T\) prime to three.
Its \(\ell\)-th power differs from \(I\) modulo \(3^{a+1}\) if \(\ell\ne3\), or modulo \(3^{a+2}\) if \(\ell=3\), by the binomial expansion.
Thus the kernel has no nontrivial torsion.
We obtain \(b\le |H|\le |H^\sharp|\le3^{r^2}\), and (2) implies \(r\gg\sqrt{\log d}\) for large \(d\).

For any \(1\le k\le r\), select \(k\) independent generators \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) from the actual roots over \(B\).
There are \(u\) possibilities for the image of \(B\).
After fixing one element taking \(B\) to each such translate, the images of the selected labels there have at most \(|H|\) possibilities.
At each selected label there are at most \(w\le2\) actual roots.
Therefore
\[
      [\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_k):\mathbb Q]\ \le\ 2^k u |H|.             \tag{3}
\]
In particular elements acting independently in other blocks add no cost to the degree in (3).

<a id="order"></a>
## The order needed by the local permutation group

Let \(J\) be the joint relative degree of the chosen roots over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), so that \(J\le2^k u|H|\).
All roots have height \(h=m(\gamma)/d\).
The [product bound of Delsinne, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) gives
\[
 k\log(1/h)\ \le\ \log J+\log c_2(k)+\kappa_2(k)\log\log(3J).
\]
In this theorem \(\log c_2(k)\) and \(\kappa_2(k)\) are both at most \(\exp(Ck^2\log(2k))\) with absolute \(C\), as follows from the [explicit constants](Yin_C_G0345_v260.md#intermediate).
Choose \(k=\lfloor c_1 V(d)\rfloor\) with \(c_1>0\) sufficiently small and absolute.
For large \(d\), \(k\) tends to infinity, is at most \(r\), and the last two constants are at most \((\log d)^{1/3}\).
If \(|H|\le d^{k/2}\), then (3) and \(u\le d\) give \(\log J\le(3k/4)\log d\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).
It follows that
\[
 \frac{k}{4}\log d\ \le\ k\log M+
 O\!\left((\log d)^{1/3}(1+\log\log(16d))\right).
\]
For fixed \(M\) this is impossible for large \(d\).
We have proved
\[
                     \log |H|\gg V(d)\log d                              \tag{4}
\]
uniformly for **every** block of size at least two.
The joint degree in (3) is the step beyond the [normal-closure obstruction for an intermediate field](Yin_C_G0345_v260.md#statement), which controls the order of the whole permutation action after a projection.

<a id="permutations"></a>
## Alternating sections and solvable extensions

For a finite group \(K\) acting primitively on \(b\ge2\) letters, the primitive-group order bound gives \(\log|K|\ll\max(1,j)\log b\), where \(j\) is the largest degree of an alternating group occurring as a section of \(K\).
Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 on p. 6 of Babai's 2018 ICM author manuscript (pp. 3307–3308 of the author-hosted typeset copy) specify the section convention and state this quantitative formulation, attributing the qualitative theorem to Babai–Cameron–Pálfy and the linear dependence to Pyber.
The original 1982 paper also gives a polynomial bound with fixed exponent whenever the alternating degrees and classical dimensions of the nonabelian composition factors of the primitive group are bounded.
These established permutation-group results are used at statement scope. [Babai's thickness and order theorem](../audits/literature/Bab18/Yin_U001.md#thickness) · [Author manuscript, §4](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/icm18-babai-prepub.pdf) · [Babai–Cameron–Pálfy, original publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(82)90323-4)

Apply the bound to \(H\) for a minimal block and combine it with (4) and \(b\le d\).
This gives a section \(A_j\) with \(j\gg V(d)\).
The group \(H\) is a section of \(G_\alpha\), so \(A_j\) is also a section of \(G_\alpha\).
For large \(d\) one may take \(j\ge5\).
If \(A_j\) is a quotient of a subgroup \(U\) of \(G_\alpha\), the last derived subgroup \(U^{(\infty)}\) still surjects onto \(A_j\) by perfectness.
It lies in \(G_\alpha^{(\infty)}\).
Its intersection with \(R(G_\alpha)\) is solvable and normal, so its image is trivial in the nonabelian simple group \(A_j\).
This proves the asserted section in the quotient of the last derived subgroup.

If \(A_s\) is excluded, then \(j<s\), since \(A_s\) embeds in every \(A_j\) for \(j\ge s\).
Consequently the compressed degree \(d\) is bounded in terms of \(s,M\).
When the label set has size one this already follows from \(d\le2\).
The primitive minimal polynomials of \(\gamma\) now have bounded degree and their coefficients satisfy \(|a_{d-i}|\le\binom di e^M\), by expansion in their roots with the leading coefficient included.
This leaves finitely many possible \(\gamma\) and proves (1).
Taking \(M=1\) gives the positive gap, and if \(0<m(\alpha)\to0\) the same coefficient bound forces \(d\to\infty\).
For a solvable group one can take \(s=5\).

The property of having no section isomorphic to a given nonabelian simple group \(A_s\) is preserved under subgroups, quotients and extensions.
For the extension assertion let \(N\lhd G\) and suppose \(H\le G\) surjects onto \(A_s\).
The image of \(H\cap N\) is normal in \(A_s\), so either \(N\) already has an \(A_s\) section or the map factors through the subgroup \(HN/N\) of \(G/N\).
This also proves closure under finite direct products.
Fix a bound \(n\) for the degrees of the allowed algebraic adjunctions and take \(s=\max(5,n+1)\).
Then \(|A_s|>n!\), so \(S_n\) has no \(A_s\) section.
At a degree-at-most-\(n\) step over a number field \(K\), start from the normal closure of \(K/\mathbb Q\) and split all conjugates of the defining polynomial.
The extra Galois group embeds in a finite product of copies of \(S_n\).
At a radical step all conjugates split after adjoining the needed roots of unity and radicals of the conjugate radicands, with an abelian cyclotomic step followed by an abelian Kummer step.
Induction applies the section exclusion at every step and gives it to all subfields, including the field generated by the selected number.
The same reasoning permits composita of such towers since each given number lies in a finite compositum.

The [Amoroso–Delsinne relative height estimate](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) uses relative degree over an abelian base, and the [central-extension result](../audits/literature/ADZ14/Yin_U001.md#adz14-u001) imposes its bounded-local-degree hypothesis.
Neither of those inputs gives the conclusion here for arbitrary lengths of radical towers by substituting a solvable base for the required base.
The finiteness in (1) concerns measures as values.
The example \(2^{1/n}\) has absolute height \((\log2)/n\), so the absolute Weil height on the same class can approach zero.
