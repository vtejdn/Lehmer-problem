# Eventual irreducibility of the noncyclotomic part on sections of a plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The degree of a factor from its exterior roots at one face

Let \(F(U,V)=\sum_{j=0}^D A_j(U)V^j\) be a primitive integral polynomial, absolutely irreducible in the Laurent ring, with two-dimensional Newton polygon, \(D>0\), \(A_0A_D\ne0\).
Suppose the leading coefficient of \(A_D\) and the lowest nonzero coefficient of \(A_0\) both have absolute value one.
Let \(P_n(X)\) be \(F(X,X^n)\) with its lowest monomial and leading sign removed, for all sufficiently large positive integers \(n\).
Let \(q\) be a monic irreducible factor of \(A_D\), of multiplicity \(e\), with positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(q)\).
Fix sufficiently small disjoint closed disks outside the unit circle about its exterior roots \(\lambda\), each containing only that root of \(A_D\) and with root-free boundary, using conjugate disks at conjugate roots.
For every monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(S_n\) of \(P_n\) write \(d_n=\deg S_n\) and let \(k_{\lambda,n}\) count its roots in the corresponding disk.
Then uniformly in the factors as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{n}{d_n}\sum_{q(\lambda)=0,\ |\lambda|>1} k_{\lambda,n}\log|\lambda|
       =\frac{e\,m(q)}{D}+o(1),\qquad
 \frac{n\,m(S_n)}{d_n}=\frac{m(F)}{D}+o(1).                           \tag{1}
\]
For large \(n\), each \(k_{\lambda,n}\) is between \(0\) and \(e\), every noncyclotomic factor occupies at least one of the disks, and \(d_n\gg_F n\).
More precisely, every limit of \(d_n/n\) lies in the intersection over the positive-measure irreducible factors \(q\) of the finite sets
\[
 \left\{\frac{D}{e\,m(q)}
    \sum_{q(\lambda)=0,\ |\lambda|>1} b_\lambda\log|\lambda|
    : b_\lambda\in\{0,\ldots,e\},\quad b_{\bar\lambda}=b_\lambda,\quad
                       \sum_\lambda b_\lambda>0\right\}.
\]
At each such limit the corresponding measure limit is the degree ratio times \(m(F)/D\).

If \(e=1\) and either a single root or a single nonreal conjugate pair accounts for all exterior roots of \(q\), then \(P_n\) has exactly one noncyclotomic irreducible factor for all sufficiently large \(n\), with multiplicity one.
The difference between its degree and \(\deg P_n\) is bounded and eventually periodic.
This extends the [factor degree and uniqueness theorem for two coefficient blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v193.md#statement) to any \(V\)-degree \(D\).
The [quadratic example](#example) gives infinitely many distinct reciprocal irreducible factors with exactly two exterior conjugates, both positive, although the set of Salem numbers across all its translated sections is finite.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Adding the geometric branches over the chosen factor

For sufficiently large \(n\), different \(V\)-levels have disjoint exponent ranges after substitution.
The endpoint hypotheses make \(P_n\) monic with constant term of absolute value one, so every root is a unit.
Take any sequence of such noncyclotomic factors with \(n\to\infty\) and a root \(z_n\) of each.
The points \((z_n,z_n^n)\) avoid every finite set, since for a fixed nontorsion \(z\) all its positive powers are distinct.
Omitting finitely many singular points, they lift uniquely to the normalization \(C\) over their coordinate fields.
The projection \(U\) has degree \(D\), and the projected width at \((1,n)\) is \(Dn+O_F(1)\).
The [uniform height formula for individual sections](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(n h(z_n)\to m(F)/D\).
This proves the second assertion of (1) and bounds \(h(z_n^n)\).

Take the whole set of poles \(Q\) of \(V\) above the zeros of \(q\) on \(C\), defined over a common finite Galois extension.
For each zero \(\lambda\) of \(q\), the sum of \(|\operatorname{ord}_Q V|\) over the poles above \(\lambda\) is \(e\).
Indeed \(w^{-D}F(U,w)\to A_D(U)\) uniformly on a small circle about \(\lambda\) as \(w\to\infty\).
For generic large \(w\) there are \(e\) solutions in the circle counted with multiplicity by Rouché's theorem.
These approach precisely the poles over \(\lambda\), each contributing the local degree of \(1/V\) at that branch.

Apply the [off-circle boundary height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v784.md#statement) with short coordinate \(z_n\), long coordinate \(z_n^n\), and sum it over all these geometric poles.
The sum of its right sides is \(e(\deg q)h(\lambda)/D=e\,m(q)/D\).
Take disjoint small neighborhoods of the branches, with complex conjugate choices, over short-coordinate disks about the corresponding roots.
The formula omits the neighborhood at a circle value.
At an interior value the neighborhood can be taken in \(|U|<1, |V|>1\), which a point \((z,z^n)\) never visits.
For exterior values choose the neighborhoods within \(|V|>1\) above the interiors of the prescribed disks.
Since the collection of poles over \(q\) is Galois invariant, summing the neighborhoods appropriate to each embedding gives exactly the same union for every embedding of the field of definition, with the circle values omitted.
Extending each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(z_n)\) to the common compositum just repeats each visit equally often.

On any fixed exterior disk, \(X^{-Dn}F(X,X^n)\to A_D(X)\) uniformly.
Rouché's theorem gives precisely \(e\) roots of the whole section there for large \(n\), counted with multiplicity, all converging to the center.
Their long coordinates tend uniformly to infinity, so their lifts all lie in the chosen pole neighborhoods for large \(n\) by compactness.
Thus the boundary formula gives
\[
        \frac{n}{d_n}\sum_{\substack{S_n(z)=0\\z\ \mathrm{in\ the\ disks}}}
                          \log|z|\ \longrightarrow\ e\,m(q)/D>0 .
\]
In particular the disks are occupied.
Every summand is bounded below by a fixed positive constant, whence \(d_n\gg_F n\) from the bounded limit.
There are at most \(e\deg q\) summands, and each differs from its center's logarithmic modulus by \(o(1)\), proving (1).
These deductions hold along every choice of factors and hence uniformly.
The counts at conjugate exterior roots agree by conjugation of the rational polynomial \(S_n\).
Their finite range and the two limits in (1) give the claimed intersection for all positive-measure factors of the same face.

<a id="period"></a>
## A simple exterior root and the periodic degree loss

For \(e=1\), each exterior disk contains one simple root of \(P_n\), which is nontorsion.
If the unique exterior root of \(q\) is real, every noncyclotomic factor contains the same single root of \(P_n\) in its disk.
In the nonreal pair case use conjugate disks.
A rational factor visiting either contains both conjugate roots, since the entire polynomial is real and both disks have only one root.
In either case there is only one such factor and its multiplicity is one.

All torsion roots come from the finite set of torsion pairs of \(F\) by the [torsion point bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area), since the irreducible curve has rank-two support.
Each such pair \((\zeta,\eta)\) occurs precisely at the indices with \(\zeta^n=\eta\), a periodic condition.
Let \(H_r(t,v)\ne0\) be the first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term of \(F(\zeta e^t,\eta e^v)\) at the origin, of degree \(r\).
At these indices the order of \(F(\zeta e^t,(\zeta e^t)^n)\) is \(r\) whenever \(H_r(1,n)\ne0\).
This is a nonzero polynomial in \(n\), by homogeneity, so for all sufficiently large admissible indices the multiplicity is exactly \(r\).
Summing over the finite torsion set gives bounded eventually periodic cyclotomic degree.

<a id="example"></a>
## A quadratic curve with two positive exterior conjugates

Set
\[
 \begin{split}
   A(U)&=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1),\qquad A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U),\\
   F(U,V)&=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U).
 \end{split}
\]
This curve has only finitely many Salem numbers on all sections \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\), including arbitrary torsion translations and common dilations.
Nevertheless the ordinary sections \(P_n\) supply infinitely many distinct irreducible reciprocal polynomials, each with precisely two exterior conjugates.
Both exterior roots are positive and converge respectively to \(\beta_3=(3+\sqrt{13})/2\) and \(\beta_4=2+\sqrt5\).
Their two reciprocals are the only interior conjugates and the other conjugates have modulus one.
In particular the positive logarithmic measures of these factors tend to \(\log(\beta_3\beta_4)\).

The discriminant of the quadratic in \(V\) is \(U^4-4AA^*\).
It equals \(-4\) at zero and is strictly positive at \(\beta_3\), so it has a real zero of odd multiplicity.
It cannot be a square in \(\mathbb C(U)\).
The three coefficients have no common factor as \(A(0)=1\), proving absolute irreducibility.
For \(|U|=1\), one has \(|U^2-jU-1|=|U-U^{-1}-j|\ge j\) for \(j=3,4\), giving \(|A|\ge12\).
Putting \(a=A(U)/U^2\), the fiber equation is \(aV^2+V+\bar a=0\).
After the rotation \(w=(a/|a|)V\), it becomes \(w^2+w/|a|+1=0\), with two distinct roots of modulus one.

Both monic quadratic factors of \(A\) are irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with simple positive exterior roots \(\beta_3,\beta_4\) and one interior root each.
Thus the single-factor conclusion above applies, and Rouché gives a simple root tending to each \(\beta_j\).
These roots are real by conjugation and positive for large \(n\).
By the [unit-circle fiber estimate for actual conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v776.md#statement), with \(k=1,l=n\), every exterior root of \(P_n\) stays a fixed distance from the circle and approaches some exterior root of \(A\).
The two disks therefore exhaust the exterior roots.
The polynomial \(P_n=X^{2n}A(X)+X^{n+2}+A^*(X)\) is reciprocal of degree \(2n+4\).
Its unique noncyclotomic factor is consequently reciprocal, and contains both exterior roots and their reciprocals.
Its degree is \(2n+4-O(1)\), so infinitely many distinct factors occur.

For the assertion over all translated sections, the Newton polygon is the rectangle \([0,4]\times[0,2]\) with corner coefficients one.
The edges at \(U\)-degree zero and four both have polynomial \(V^2+1\), of measure zero.
Hence the [archimedean Salem criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v774.md#statement) leaves only the two normals to the pair of edges with polynomials \(A,A^*\).
The exponent differences generate \(\mathbb Z^2\) since each outer row includes consecutive exponents and the middle term lies at \(V\)-degree one.
The translation stabilizer is therefore trivial.
At either remaining ray the [single-orbit Salem condition](Yin_C_G0345_v785.md#statement) requires the nonzero top roots to form one rational Galois orbit.
Both \(A\) and \(A^*\) have two distinct irreducible factors, also after reversing the short character or removing a monomial.
This excludes both rays and proves finiteness.
