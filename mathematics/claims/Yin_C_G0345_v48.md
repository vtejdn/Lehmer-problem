# A uniform Salem gap for two Chebyshev traces with a growing separation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Both signs and all sufficiently large separations

Define \(D_j(v+v^{-1})=v^j+v^{-j}\), including \(D_0=2\).
There exist \(h_0\ge1\) and \(\epsilon>0\) such that every Salem number \(\sigma\) whose monic trace minimal polynomial \(S\) of degree \(k+1\) is strictly interlaced by
\[
                         C=2D_k+eD_{k-h},\qquad
                       e\in\{-1,1\},\quad h_0\le h\le k,
\]
satisfies \(\sigma\ge1+\epsilon\).
Here strict interlacing places one root of \(C\) in each open gap between consecutive roots of \(S\).
In particular both signs exclude simultaneous limits \(\sigma\to1\) and \(h\to\infty\), with no assumption on the first coefficients of the Salem polynomials.

<a id="marginal"></a>
## The limiting residue measure and its integer Fourier coefficients

Failure of the uniform assertion gives a sequence with \(h\to\infty\) and \(\sigma\to1\), on which we may fix \(e\).
Write \(m=k+1\), \(P(v)=v^m S(v+v^{-1})\), and \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\).
The [frequency concentration for full Salem interlacers](Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#statement) applies since the leading coefficient is two and the lifts have at most four terms.
It excludes the constant harmonic when \(h=k\), and forces \((k-h)/k\to1\) on the remaining sequence.
Hence \(h=o(k)\) and
\[
                      C^\sharp=2+e v^h+e v^{2k-h}+2v^{2k}.
\]
Let \(x_1<\cdots<x_m=T\) be the trace roots and put \(c_j=C(x_j)/S'(x_j)\).
The weights are positive and sum to two by interlacing and partial fractions.
All circle lifts of the first \(m-1\) traces lie on the unit circle.
Let \(\lambda_i\) give half the corresponding weight to each of these two lifts, and write \(t=c_m\).
The [residue generating identity](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) gives
\[
 G_i(v)=\frac{(1-v^2)C^\sharp(v)}{P(v)}
       =2+\sum_{j\ge1}g_{i,j}v^j,\qquad
 g_{i,j}=2\int u^j\,d\lambda_i(u)+t(\sigma^j+\sigma^{-j}).
\]
In particular the coefficients are integers and are even in all positive degrees below \(h\), since \(P(0)=1\).
Take a weak limit \(\mu\) of \(\lambda_i+t\delta_1\) on the circle.
For each fixed \(j>0\), \(g_{i,j}\to2\widehat\mu(j)\) by symmetry and \(\sigma\to1\).
Thus \(\mu\) has integer Fourier coefficients, including its mass two, and the coefficients of \(G_i\) stabilize in every fixed degree.

For the full trace weights \(\nu_i=\sum_jc_j\delta_{x_j}\), partial fractions give
\[
                 \int \frac{d\nu_i(x)}{x+2}
                    =-\frac{C(-2)}{S(-2)}\le 6 .
\]
Indeed \(|C(-2)|\le6\), and \(S(-2)\) is a nonzero integer.
Since \(T\to2\), the measures \(\nu_i\) tend to the pushforward of \(\mu\) under \(u\mapsto u+u^{-1}\).
Truncation at the singular endpoint therefore gives \(\int (2+u+u^{-1})^{-1}d\mu\le6\).

By [Sanders's integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes), \(\mu\) is the sum of an integer Laurent polynomial density \(f\) against normalized arclength \(d\theta\) and a measure on a finite set of roots of unity.
Indeed singleton indicators in the Fourier coefficients give monomials in the density, and congruence classes give finite torsion support.
Both parts are nonnegative by uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition, and \(f\) is real and symmetric.
The density cannot vanish identically.
Otherwise take a common order \(L\) of the finitely many torsion points supporting \(\mu\) and set \(A(x)=D_L(x)-2\).
For any \(F\in\mathbb Z[x]\), \(\sum_j c_j F(x_j)\) is the coefficient of \(x^{m-1}\) in the integral remainder of \(CF\) modulo \(S\), by Lagrange interpolation.
Taking \(F=A^2\) gives a positive integer because \(A(T)>0\), yet its limit is zero by the fixed trace test and the choice of \(L\).

The positive integer mean of \(f\) is at most two.
At mean one, strict triangle inequality on an open arc where \(f>0\) bounds every nonconstant Fourier coefficient in absolute value by less than one, so \(f=1\).
Any positive constant density contradicts the inverse endpoint integral, which also forces \(f(-1)=0\) by continuity.
The [classification of nonnegative integer symbols of mean two](Yin_C_G0403_v1.md#integer-mean-two-classification) now gives \(f=2+\eta(v^a+v^{-a})\) with \(a\ge1\) and \(\eta=\pm1\), leaving no singular mass.
The zero at \(-1\) fixes \(\eta=-(-1)^a\).
Writing \(b=(-1)^a\) and \(H(v)=1-bv^a\), we have
\[
             d\mu=|H|^2d\theta,\qquad
             G_i\longrightarrow G=2H,\qquad
             P\longrightarrow P_0=\frac{1-v^2}{H}
                         \quad\text{coefficientwise}.
\]
The last limit follows by formal division, and \(P_0\in\mathbb Z[[v]]\).
The limiting marginal is continuous, so positivity forces \(t\to0\) and \(\lambda_i\to\mu\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Integer Laurent polynomials at the first changing scale

Let \(n\) be the first index at which \(P\) differs from \(P_0\).
It exists eventually since \(P_0\) is fixed while the degrees of \(P\) tend to infinity, and \(n\to\infty\).
Extract either \(h-n\to+\infty\), in which case put \(l=n\), or \(n\ge h-K\) for some fixed \(K\ge0\), in which case put \(l=h\).
Set \(K=0\) in the first case.
All differences in \(P-P_0\) and \(G_i-G\) start at degrees at least \(l-K\), by the numerator identity.
Thus \(g_{i,l-K-1}=0\) eventually since \(G\) is a fixed polynomial.
The positive moment formula gives \(t\sigma^l\le4\sigma^{K+1}\).

Push \(2\lambda_i\) to \((z,w)=(u^{-1},u^l)\) and take a joint weak limit \(\Phi\), extracting also \(t\sigma^l\to s\).
The projection of \(w\,d\Phi\) to the first circle has a complex density \(U(z)\,d\theta\), with \(|U|\le2|H|^2\) by the first marginal.
For each fixed integer \(j\) the convention \(\widehat U(j)=\int z^{-j}U(z)d\theta\) gives
\[
                      \lim_i g_{i,l+j}=\widehat U(j)+s.
\]
Indeed \(t\sigma^{-l-j}\to0\) and \(t\sigma^{l+j}\to s\).
For \(j<-K\) the left side is zero.
Since \(U\) is integrable its Fourier coefficients tend to zero as \(j\to-\infty\), proving \(s=0\).
All \(\widehat U(j)\) are consequently integers by eventual stabilization, and they tend to zero in both directions.
Fourier uniqueness identifies \(U\) with an integer Laurent polynomial, supported in exponents at least \(-K\).
At each simple root of \(H\) the bound by \(2|H|^2\) forces a zero of order at least two.
Clearing a monomial and dividing by the monic polynomial \(H^2\) proves
\[
                       U=H^2 V,\qquad V\in\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}].
\]

<a id="early"></a>
## An earlier denominator change would force a torsion relation

Suppose \(h-n\to+\infty\).
Here \(U\) has no negative powers, and its constant coefficient is \(-2p\), where \(p=[v^n](P-P_0)\) stabilizes to a nonzero integer.
This follows at degree \(n\) by subtracting \(GP_0\) from \(G_iP\), since the two numerators still agree there.
Consequently \(V\) is an ordinary polynomial with \(V(0)=-2p\) and circle modulus at most two.
The maximum modulus principle forces \(|p|=1\) and \(V=-2p\).
Since \(H(z^{-1})=-bz^{-a}H(z)\), the first moment on each conditional probability for \(w\) is
\[
                     \frac{U(z)}{2|H(z)|^2}=pb z^a
\]
almost everywhere for the first marginal.
Its modulus one forces \(w=pb z^a\) almost surely.

For the actual circle variable define \(Q_i(u)=u^{n+a}-pb\) and \(A_i(u+u^{-1})=Q_i(u)Q_i(u^{-1})\).
The latter belongs to \(\mathbb Z[x]\) as a symmetric integer Laurent polynomial in \(u\).
By the remainder formula the sum \(J_i=\sum_jc_j(2-x_j)^4 A_i(x_j)^2\) is an integer, strictly positive at \(x_m=T>2\).
Its neutral part tends to zero by joint weak convergence, since on the circle \(|Q_i(u)|=|w-pbz^a|\).
To estimate the exterior part let \(y_1<\cdots<y_{m-1}\) be the nodes of \(C\).
They all lie in \((-2,2)\) since \(C(2\cos(j\pi/k))\) has the sign of \((-1)^j\) for \(0\le j\le k\).
Strict interlacing gives \(y_{j-1}<x_j\) for \(2\le j<m\), so cancellation of successive factors yields
\[
 t=2\prod_{j<m}\frac{T-y_j}{T-x_j}
        \ge\frac{2(T-y_{m-1})}{T-x_1}\ge\frac{2(T-2)}{T+2}.
\]
As \(a\) is fixed and \(t\sigma^n=O(1)\), the exterior part is \(O(t(T-2)^4\sigma^{2n+2a})=O((T-2)^3)\).
Here \(Q_i(\sigma^{-1})\) stays bounded.
This contradicts \(J_i\ge1\).

<a id="parity"></a>
## A denominator change at or after the numerator scale fails modulo two

It remains to take \(l=h\) and \(n\ge h-K\).
Put \(\Delta G=G_i-G\) and \(\Delta P=P-P_0\).
Their product starts at least at \(2h-2K\).
For any fixed integer \(j\), the coefficient at \(h+j\) in \(G_iP-GP_0\) is eventually the coefficient at \(j\) of \(e(1-z^2)\), since \(2k-2h\to\infty\).
The coefficients of \(\Delta G\) at \(h+j\) stabilize to \(\widehat U(j)\).
In \(P_0\Delta G\) the corresponding limits are the coefficients of the formal Laurent series \(P_0U\), since only offsets at least \(-K\) enter.
From \(G\Delta P=G_iP-GP_0-P_0\Delta G-\Delta G\Delta P\) and \(G(0)=2\), induction on offsets beginning at \(-K\) shows that \([v^{h+j}]\Delta P\) also stabilizes at every fixed \(j\).
Its values form an integer Laurent series \(F\) bounded below in exponent.
The exact limiting identity is
\[
               P_0U+2HF=e(1-z^2),\qquad
               (1-z^2)HV+2HF=e(1-z^2).
\]
Reduction modulo two leaves an identity of finite Laurent polynomials, so canceling the nonzero \(1-z^2\) gives \(HV=1\) over \(\mathbb F_2[z,z^{-1}]\).
But \(H=1-bz^a\) reduces to \(1+z^a\) and vanishes at one.
This final contradiction excludes the sequence for either sign.
