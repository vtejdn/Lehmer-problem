## 2. Exact field, order and norm domain

Let alpha>1 be an actual Salem number of original degree d=2s>=4, K=Q(alpha), F=Q(tau) with tau=alpha+alpha^-1, and sigma(alpha)=alpha^-1.
The trace field F is totally real of degree s.
Label its embeddings rho_0,...,rho_(s-1), with rho_0 the unique exterior trace place and the remaining s-1 places arising from the original unit-circle pairs.

Let O be a full order in K containing 1 and stable under sigma.
Put

    B=O intersection F.

This is a full order in F.
It is a subring containing 1 and an additive subgroup of the finite free Z-module O, hence finitely generated.
A Q-basis of F expressed in a Q-basis from O can be multiplied by one positive integer to lie in O.
Those scaled basis elements lie in B, so B spans F and has rank s.
No maximal-order equality is assumed.
In particular Dirichlet's theorem applies directly to B.

Assume an ACTUAL unit w in O^times satisfies

    Norm_(K/Q)(w)=-1,
    c_-=w sigma(w) in B^times,
    Norm_(F/Q)(c_-)=-1.                             (1)

The relative norm and its inverse belong to B because O is sigma-stable and w is an O-unit.
At the circle trace places c_j=rho_j(c_-) is |w(z_j)|^2>0.
The signed full product in (1) then forces

    c_0<0, c_j>0 for 1<=j<s.                        (2)

Every original conjugate contributes; the sign is not replaced by an absolute norm.
A negative unit known only in O_K^times is not enough for this theorem: passing to a power to enter O can erase its negative sign.
The hypothesis is the actual w in O, with its inverse in O.
The result applies to O_K itself when that is the specified order.

## 3. Selecting one actual trace-unit direction per place

Dirichlet's checked actual-order form gives a full lattice

    Lambda={ (log|rho_j(epsilon)|)_(j=0)^(s-1) : epsilon in B^times }
        in H_0={x in R^s : sum_j x_j=0}.            (3)

For each i choose the vector z_i in H_0 whose i-th coordinate is s-1 and all other coordinates are -1.
A full lattice has bounded fundamental parallelepiped: express Tz_i in a lattice basis and round its coefficients to integers.

Thus choose once and for all actual units epsilon_0,...,epsilon_(s-1) in B^times with

    |rho_i(epsilon_i)|>1,
    |rho_j(epsilon_i)|<1 for j!=i.                  (4)

Only the existence of the finitely many units is used; their size is input dependent.

For integers N>=1 define actual O-unit directions and their relative norms by

    u_i(N)=epsilon_i^N,       A_i(N)=epsilon_i^(2N), 0<=i<s,

Because sigma fixes F, each A_i is exactly u_i sigma(u_i), and A_- is exactly u_- sigma(u_-).

## 4. The finite positive-weight construction

**Theorem 1.** Under (1), for EVERY prescribed E in Q with E>0 there is N_0, depending on E, w and the chosen units, such that for every integer N>=N_0 there are strictly positive rational weights lambda_0,...,lambda_(s-1),lambda_- satisfying

    sum_(i=0)^(s-1) lambda_i+lambda_-=1,
    sum_(i=0)^(s-1) lambda_i A_i(N)+lambda_- A_-(N)=E in F.
                                                               (6)

There are s+1 actual O-unit directions, and the complete profile equality holds at all s trace embeddings.
No uniform N_0 or height/denominator estimate is asserted.

*Real existence and strict positivity.* For i>=1 introduce analysis variables mu_i=a_ii lambda_i, leaving lambda_0 and lambda_- unscaled.
Divide only the place-zero equation by a_00.
The full system is

    lambda_0+c_0 lambda_-+
        sum_(i=1)^(s-1) [a_i0/(a_ii a_00)] mu_i = E/a_00,
    lambda_0+lambda_-+sum_(i=1)^(s-1) mu_i/a_ii = 1,
    a_0j lambda_0+c_j a_0j lambda_-+
        sum_(i=1)^(s-1) [a_ij/a_ii] mu_i = E,  1<=j<s.
                                                               (7)

These are precisely all embeddings of the original field equation plus the normalization, with no dropped row.
As N tends to infinity, every first-row sum coefficient and its right-hand side tend to zero, every 1/a_ii tends to zero, and the circle-row matrix a_ij/a_ii tends to the identity.
Also a_0j tends to zero for j>=1.
The limiting system is

    lambda_0+c_0 lambda_-=0,
    lambda_0+lambda_-=1,
    mu_j=E, 1<=j<s.                                 (8)

Its determinant, in the displayed row/variable order, is 1-c_0>0.
Its unique solution is

    lambda_0^*=(-c_0)/(1-c_0)>0,
    lambda_-^*=1/(1-c_0)>0,
    mu_i^*=E>0.                                     (9)

Finite-dimensional determinant continuity makes the matrices in (7) invertible for all sufficiently large N.
The inverse, expressed by cofactors divided by the nonzero determinant, and the right-hand side converge to their limits.
Hence their unique solutions converge to (9).
Choosing the error smaller than half the minimum of those finitely many positive limit coordinates makes lambda_0,lambda_- and every mu_i strictly positive.
Since a_ii>0, every original lambda_i=mu_i/a_ii is strictly positive too.
The normalization is exact, not merely asymptotic.
The dependence on fixed E matters: this argument does not select one N that works uniformly down to E=0.

*Exact rationality.* Scaling was used only for asymptotic analysis; the mu_i need not be rational.
In the original unknowns lambda_i,lambda_-, express each A_i,A_- in the Q-basis 1,tau,...,tau^(s-1) of F.
Equality to rational E gives s rational linear equations, and the weight sum gives one more.
Their square coefficient matrix R_N has rational entries.
The s-by-s matrix taking this basis to its embeddings is a Vandermonde matrix on the s distinct trace conjugates, so it is invertible.
Multiplication by it transforms those s rational equations into exactly the embedding equations used in (7), before the invertible row/column scalings.
Thus R_N is invertible for the same finite N.
Its right-hand side is rational, so its UNIQUE solution is rational.
This is not an approximation of real weights by rational weights; (6) is an exact field identity.
Together with strict positivity already proved, this completes the theorem.
