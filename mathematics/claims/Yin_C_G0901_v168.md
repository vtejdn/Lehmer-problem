# The Mahler cost per copy of an eigenvalue in a realizing tree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A tree realization cannot spread the cost of a small Salem trace over many copies

For a finite simple graph \(G\) on \(N\) vertices write \(A_G\) for its adjacency matrix and
\[
 P_G(z)=z^N\det((z+z^{-1})I-A_G),\qquad E(G)=\log M(P_G).
\]
For a real \(t>2\) occurring in some finite tree define
\[
 c(t)=\inf\{E(T):T\text{ a finite tree with adjacency eigenvalue }t\}.
\]
This is finite for every totally real algebraic integer \(t>2\) by [Salez's realization theorem](../audits/literature/Sal15/Yin_U001.md#sal15-u001).
For any forest \(F\) in which \(t\) has multiplicity \(r\), and any graph \(G\) in which \(t\) has multiplicity \(m\) and removal of \(f\) vertices leaves a forest, one has
\[
 E(F)\ge r c(t), \qquad E(G)\ge (m-f)_+c(t).                         \tag{1}
\]
At an individual connected tree of multiplicity \(r>1\) the first lower bound improves to \((r+1)c(t)\).
Through realizable \(t\) approaching 2 from above,
\[
                         c(t)\longrightarrow\infty .                 \tag{2}
\]
Yet for each fixed \(t\) the lower coefficient in (1) is sharp even with connectivity and prescribed multiplicity:
\[
 \lim_{r\to\infty}\inf_{\substack{T\ {\rm tree}\\{\rm mult}_T(t)=r}}
                        \frac{E(T)}r=c(t).                           \tag{3}
\]

If a Salem number \(\tau\) tends to one, take \(t=\tau+\tau^{-1}\), and let \(p_\tau\) denote its monic minimum.
In every realizing tree with multiplicity \(r\) the complementary polynomial satisfies
\[
         r^{-1}\log M(P_T/p_\tau^r)\ge c(t)-\log\tau\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
Thus replacing one occurrence by arbitrarily many still forces divergent extra logarithmic measure per occurrence.
These statements give a constraint on graph realizations without imposing a lower bound on \(\tau\) itself.

<a id="fragmentation"></a>
## Fragmentation at a multiple eigenvalue

We give the needed deletion argument, a special case of the Parter–Wiener theorem. [Johnson, Leal Duarte and Saiago, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479801393320)
For any two real \(t\)-eigenvectors \(u,v\) on a tree, put \(J_{ij}=u_i v_j-v_i u_j\) on each oriented edge.
The sum of this skew-symmetric flow out of a vertex vanishes by the two eigenvalue equations.
Summing over one side of each edge cut gives \(J_{ij}=0\) on every edge.
Hence adjacent nonzero coordinate evaluation functionals on the real eigenspace are proportional.
If every functional is nonzero they are all proportional, and the injective map recording all coordinates implies that the eigenspace has dimension at most one.

In a connected tree of multiplicity \(r\ge2\) choose a vertex \(i\) where all eigenvectors vanish adjacent to a vertex \(j\) with a nonzero evaluation.
Write \(B=A_{T-i}\) and \(b\) for the vector of former neighbors of \(i\).
The \(t\)-eigenvectors on \(T\) correspond exactly to the vectors of \(\ker(B-tI)\) annihilated by \(b^{\mathsf T}\).
This functional is nonzero there: take an original eigenvector nonzero at \(j\) and restrict it to the component of \(T-i\) containing \(j\), extending it by zero on the other components.
It remains a \(t\)-eigenvector on the forest and its pairing with \(b\) is its value at \(j\), since only \(j\) in that component was adjacent to \(i\).
Therefore the total multiplicity increases by exactly one upon deletion.
Repeat on any component having multiplicity at least two.
After \(s\) deletions there is no such component, and \(r+s\) separate trees each contain one occurrence.
Termination follows from the strictly decreasing number of vertices.

Each adjacency eigenvalue \(\lambda\) contributes \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|\lambda|/2)\) for \(|\lambda|>2\) and zero otherwise to \(E\).
Under principal deletion both positive and negative spectral tail counts can only decrease by interlacing.
Integrating these counts against \(du/\sqrt{u^2-4}\) on \(u>2\) gives monotonicity of \(E\).
It is additive on disconnected graphs.
Consequently \(E(F)\ge(r+s)c(t)\); in a connected tree with multiplicity greater than one, \(s\ge1\).
For a general graph, deletion of \(f\) vertices reduces the multiplicity by at most \(f\), either by interlacing or by restricting the eigenspace to vectors vanishing at those vertices.
The resulting forest proves (1).

<a id="edge"></a>
## Why the minimum cost diverges at the spectral edge

For any fixed \(B\), the [induced subgraph and degree bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v197.md#graphs) bound the number of vertices of degree at least three in every connected simple graph with \(E(G)\le B\), independently of its size.
In a tree with that branch bound the [individual eigenvalue estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v128.md#statement) excludes all eigenvalues in \(2<t<2\cosh\eta(B)\) for a positive \(\eta(B)\).
Hence all realizing trees in this interval have cost greater than \(B\); varying \(B\) proves (2).
The multiplicity of \(p_\tau\) in the reciprocal characteristic polynomial is the multiplicity of its trace eigenvalue, since \(z+z^{-1}\) has nonzero derivative at \(\tau>1\).
Multiplicativity gives the stated complementary bound.

The limit is meaningful even independently of the existence of Salem numbers tending to one.
Take a path on \(N\) vertices and attach one leaf at every vertex.
Eliminating the leaf coordinates at an eigenvalue \(t\ne0\) gives the path eigenvalue equation at \(t-t^{-1}\).
The positive solutions of \(t-t^{-1}=2\cos(j\pi/(N+1))\), \(1\le j\le N\), are therefore tree eigenvalues.
Choosing the cosines so that \(2\cos(j\pi/(N+1))\) approaches \(3/2\) strictly from above gives realizable \(t\to2^+\).
This example only tests the spectral edge.

<a id="amortization"></a>
## Attaining the limiting cost with connected trees

The infimum defining \(c(t)\) is unchanged when restricted to trees with a simple occurrence.
Indeed fragmentation of any realizing tree leaves a component with a simple occurrence of cost no greater than the original.
Fix such a tree \(T\) of size \(N\) and choose a vertex \(v\) where its \(t\)-eigenvector is nonzero.
Join \(k\ge2\) disjoint copies of \(T\) to one new hub, connecting the hub to \(v\) in each copy.
The subspace of vectors whose coordinates sum to zero across the copies at every original vertex, with hub value zero, carries \(k-1\) copies of \(A_T\).
On its orthogonal complement the matrix is \(A_T\) bordered at \(v\) by an edge of weight \(\sqrt{k}\) to the hub.
It has no eigenvalue \(t\): pairing its equation on \(T\) with the simple \(t\)-eigenvector forces the hub coordinate to vanish, and the hub equation then forces the old eigenvector coefficient to vanish.
Thus the new tree has exactly \(k-1\) copies of \(t\).
The bordered block has fixed dimension \(N+1\), and norm at most \(\|A_T\|+\sqrt{k}\); the sum of the logarithmic contributions of all its eigenvalues is \(O_T(\log(2k))\).
The total cost divided by \(k-1\) tends to \(E(T)\).
Letting \(E(T)\) approach the infimum gives (3) for all integer multiplicities, with the reverse bound from (1).
