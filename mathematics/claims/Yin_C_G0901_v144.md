# Almost all logarithmic Mahler mass on each strict side is nonreal off the coordinate lines

<a id="statement"></a>
## Real exterior and interior contributions at a record

Let \(w\) be a reciprocal strict degree record at bounded positive total logarithmic Mahler measure, and let its degree \(d=2ub\) tend to infinity, with \(u\) canonical blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs.
For any nontorsion \(x\) in the divisible group of its conjugates, put \(m(x)=\log M(x)\).
If the class of \(x\) modulo torsion is not proportional over \(\mathbb Q\) to a single conjugate of \(w\), then
\[
          \sum_{\sigma x\ {\rm real}}\big|\log|\sigma x|\big|
                    \ \le\ b^{-1/2+o(1)}\, m(x).                         \tag{1}
\]
Here and below the sums count distinct conjugates, the error is uniform in \(x\) at the fixed source ceiling, and \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}=d^{-1/2+o(1)}\).
Thus the nonreal exterior roots contribute \((1-o(1))m(x)\) in positive log mass, and the nonreal interior roots contribute \((1-o(1))m(x)\) in absolute negative log mass.
There is no assumption about neutral conjugates, radical denominators, reciprocity of \(x\), or its degree.
The same estimate holds for a nontorsion terminal compressed block product of bounded measure and growing degree with the same pure signed or oriented representation, in place of the record.

In particular, along shrinking reciprocal strict records \(w\), put \(A=M(w)\).
If \(A\) is off those coordinate lines, then for any fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) and small \(m=m(w)>0\), the nonreal exterior log mass of \(A\), and separately its absolute nonreal interior log mass, are at least
\[
                    \exp\!\big(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).
\]
These masses concern the algebraic value \(A\) itself.
Either one remaining bounded along such records forces the record eventually to be real Salem or a genuine square endpoint, up to the signed conjugate and inverse conventions of the [record classification](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#records).
The estimate on an off-coordinate value controls the size of the root contributions as well as their [number](Yin_C_G0901_v142.md#statement).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## From the real mass to a permutation estimate

Use the [pure signed or oriented representation](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) on the actual class space \(W\).
Its dimension \(t\) is at most \(ub\), with \(u\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\).
Write \(v=[x]\), let \(g\) be uniformly distributed on a finite common normal group, and write \(\lambda(v)=\log|x|\) by rational homogeneity.
Real embeddings of \(x\) require \(cgv=gv\) for ordinary complex conjugation \(c\); this implication allows torsion twists.
Writing \(D=\deg x\), one has \(\mathbb E|\lambda(gv)|=2m(x)/D\) and
\[
        \max_g|\lambda(gv)|\le t\,\mathbb E|\lambda(gv)|
\]
by the [orbit projection bound](Yin_C_G0901_v114.md#projection).
The number \(a\) of real pair labels of \(w\), over all blocks, is \(O(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=b^{1/2+o(1)}\) at the fixed measure ceiling. [Real places](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement)

In the signed space mark a block's nonzero positions.
In an oriented sum-zero block let its number of exceptions be \(b\) minus the largest multiplicity of a coefficient; in a sparse block mark the positions outside its unique majority.
Let \(e\) be the sum of these counts.
The class in (1) has \(e\ge2\).
Condition on any transport of the blocks, then apply the supported alternating label permutations independently on them, with lifts on roots as in the [signed-action alternatives](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement).
In the oriented case pure supported permutations are available.
In the independent case the normal kernel also supplies all supported even sign changes.
Probability upper bounds uniform on these cosets carry over to the group average.
On any list of at most \(b-2\) distinguishable source positions in a block their destination labels are uniformly distributed as ordered draws without replacement; the alternating subgroup alone suffices.
We first show that when \(e\ge3\),
\[
                       \Pr(cgv=gv)\le b^{-3/2+o(1)} .                    \tag{2}
\]

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Sparse colors and sparse supports

Put \(L=\lceil(\log b)^2\rceil\).
If every block has fewer than \(L\) exceptions, the total \(e\le uL=b^{o(1)}\).
Fix a choice of destination blocks and distinguish the individual marks for counting.
For \(cgv=gv\), conjugation permutes their positions by an involution.
This condition is necessary also when \(c\) exchanges blocks or reverses an oriented block, because the unique majority transports with its sign.
A fixed marked label must be real: its nonzero coefficient in the independent case, or its nonzero difference from the majority in the oriented case, must be preserved with positive sign.
For an involution with \(f\) fixed points and \(p\) transpositions, expose the required labels by first exposing one member of each transposition and then the other.
Each forced partner has conditional probability at most \(1/(b-e)\) and each fixed point at most \(a/(b-e)\).
Incompatible block destinations only lower the probability.
Thus the bound per involution is \((a/(b-e))^f(b-e)^{-p}\).
There are at most \(e^e\) choices and \(f+2p=e\), so the total is at most \(b^{-(1/2-o(1))e}\), uniformly in \(3\le e\le uL\).
This gives (2) in the sparse case.

We will also use a subset estimate on one fixed block.
Suppose a random subset of size \(12\le j\le b^{3/4}\) must be preserved by \(c\), and every fixed label in the subset must lie in an allowed set of at most \(a\) positions.
Its probability of meeting these requirements is at most \(b^{-3+o(1)}\).
Distinguish twelve draws from the subset.
Split them into \(f\) fixed draws, \(p\) paired internally by \(c\), and \(q\) whose distinct partners must be included among the remaining \(j-12\) draws.
All such patterns of the twelve have bounded total number.
The probability per pattern is
\[
            O\!\left((a/(b-12))^f(b-12)^{-p}
                            (j/(b-24))^q\right),
\]
by exposure without replacement, with constraints on the final \(q\) partners imposed after the first twelve draws.
It is \(b^{-3+o(1)}\) or smaller since \(f+2p+q=12\).
Uniform subset or partition distributions used here and below are unchanged on passing from the symmetric to the alternating group when a transposition of two identical colors is available.
For the twelve draws or an individual subset it suffices to interchange two unused or indistinguishable labels.

<a id="dense"></a>
## Dense blocks at complex conjugation

Suppose a block with at least \(L\) exceptions is transported to \(B\).
If \(cB\ne B\), condition on all outside data at \(cB\).
The vector on \(B\) must equal a prescribed vector transported from \(cB\).
It has at least \(\binom b3\) possible images under the supported group acting on \(B\) alone by the [three-exception orbit count](Yin_C_G0901_v141.md#small).
In the independent case, supported label motions may first be lifted with unwanted signs outside \(B\); one may instead count support sets when \(3\le j\le b-3\), and use independent even flips when \(j>b-3\).
The support sets are moved independently of all labels outside \(B\), and equality forces the prescribed support, while in the latter case the flips fix all the other roots.
Each method gives conditional probability \(O(b^{-3})\) when conditioning on the other support sets, or on all the other signs in the dense-flip case, respectively.

Now suppose \(cB=B\).
In the independent case its support, of size \(j\ge L\), must be preserved.
Fixed labels in the support must be real, so the subset estimate applies up to \(j=b^{3/4}\).
For larger \(j\), at most \(a\) occupied labels can be fixed if the vector is \(c\)-fixed.
On each of the resulting at least \((j-a)/2\) occupied transpositions the two signs must have a prescribed relation.
Even flips permit every sign assignment on the support, with at worst one total parity condition, so success has probability at most \(2^{1-(j-a)/2}=O(b^{-3})\).

In the oriented case with positive sign, \(c\) must preserve each coefficient color and has at most \(a\) fixed labels.
Some union of color classes has size \(L/2\le j\le b/2\): take the complement of a majority larger than \(b/2\), or a single color in the range, or accumulate smaller colors.
If \(j\le b^{3/4}\), apply the subset estimate.
Otherwise expose the membership of the two ends of \(\lfloor b^{1/3}\rfloor\) disjoint transpositions of \(c\), available since at most \(a\) labels are fixed.
At each pair, conditioned on all preceding memberships, the probability of separating the pair is at least a constant times \(b^{-1/4}\), because at least \(j-2b^{1/3}\) selected and \(b-j-2b^{1/3}\) unselected positions remain.
Preservation is consequently \(O(\exp(-c_1 b^{1/12}))\) for some absolute \(c_1>0\).

With negative orientation sign, all nonzero colors must pair with their negatives and their total support \(j\) is at least \(L\).
It can contain no fixed labels.
Up to \(b^{3/4}\) the same subset bound applies.
For larger \(j\) there must be \(j/2\) positive entries and at least \(j/2\) transpositions of \(c\).
Expose \(\lfloor b^{2/3}\rfloor\) disjoint pairs.
The subset of positive positions is uniform, and the conditional probability that both ends are positive is at least a constant times \(b^{-1/2}\), since \(j/2-2b^{2/3}\gg b^{3/4}\).
One such pair precludes invariance, so success is \(O(\exp(-c_2 b^{1/6}))\) for an absolute \(c_2>0\).
This proves (2) in all remaining cases.
For \(e\ge3\) the maximum-log bound and \(t\le ub=b^{1+o(1)}\) now bound the fraction of absolute log mass on real classes by \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}\).

<a id="two"></a>
## Two exceptional coordinates

With exactly two marks write \(v=sE_i+t'E_j\), \(i\ne j\), using signed basis coordinates or their projections onto each oriented sum-zero space.
Both scalars are nonzero.
On the orbit put \(X=\lambda(gE_i)\), \(Y=\lambda(gE_j)\).
They have the same absolute first moment by signed-coordinate transitivity.
Condition on the signed image of the first coordinate.
In the independent model \(\mathbb E(Y\mid gE_i)=0\), by a double flip on the second label and a spare one.
In the oriented model with the labels in different blocks it is zero by averaging pure permutations in the second block, since the projected coordinates sum to zero there.
In the same oriented block it is \(-X/(b-1)\) since the common transport sign is fixed and the other \(b-1\) positions are equiprobable.
The same assertions hold on interchanging the two labels.
Conditioning on the coordinate with the larger absolute scalar and using Jensen's inequality therefore gives
\[
  \mathbb E|sX+t'Y|\ \ge\
           (1-1/(b-1))\max(|s|,|t'|)\,\mathbb E|X|.
\]

Conditional on either single signed coordinate image, the probability that \(cgv=gv\) is at most \((a+1)/(b-1)\).
If its label moves, the second mark must occupy its specific image under \(c\).
If the first label is fixed, the second must also be fixed and must have positive sign on the deviation from the majority or on its signed entry; only real labels qualify.
One can first condition on the destination block of the other mark when the blocks differ.
The second position is uniform in that block or among the \(b-1\) remaining labels, by the stabilizer action on labels.
In the independent case any change to the first sign in lifting a stabilizing label permutation can be corrected by an even flip fixing that first label as a label.
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left(|sX+t'Y|{\bf1}_{cgv=gv}\right)
      \le \frac{a+1}{b-1} (|s|+|t'|)\mathbb E|X|.
\]
Together these estimates prove (1) also for \(e=2\), accounting for both signs of the log and for the common embedding multiplicities.

<a id="escape"></a>
## Where the growing measure of the first value occurs

At a strict reciprocal record the [quantitative off-coordinate inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#records) gives \(m(A)m(w)\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) on that branch.
At small \(m(w)\), [Voutier's degree inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#iterate) therefore gives the exponential lower bound on \(m(A)\) with any exponent strictly between the stated \(\eta\) and \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
The absolute real contribution from (1) takes a vanishing fraction, and the sum of all positive logs and the sum of the absolute negative logs both equal \(m(A)\).
Reducing the exponent to \(\eta\) proves the two assertions on strict sides.
The coordinate alternatives are exactly the Salem and square cases by the same record classification.
