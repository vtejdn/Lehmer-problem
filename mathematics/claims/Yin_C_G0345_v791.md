# The actual size of a common fiber of weighted binary line sums

<a id="statement"></a>
## A necessary logarithmic factor in the side length

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) and let \(L\ge d\).
For binary arrays on \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^j\), record \(\sum_i b_i\alpha^i\) on every coordinate line.
Let \(M_j\) be the largest cardinality of a fiber of the entire record, \(h_j=L^{-j}\log M_j\), and \(h_0=\log 2\).
For a mask \(S\) of size \(s\) in dimension \(J\), the corresponding largest fiber \(M_S\) with zeros outside \(S\) is at most \(M_J\).
Use natural logarithms and put \(H_2(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\), with the continuous endpoint convention.

Write \(k=\lceil L/d\rceil\).
For \(0<a<1\) such that \(k^{-a}\le1/2\), put
\[
 u=H_2(k^{-a}),\qquad b=\frac{(1-a)\log k}{2k\log2}.
\]
Then
\[
                 h_J\le u+(\log2-u)(1+b)^{-J}.                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular if \(J\to\infty\) and \(L/(Jd)\to c\in(0,\infty)\), then
\[
                     h_J\le k^{-1/(1+2c\log2)+o(1)}.                    \tag{2}
\]
The estimate holds uniformly in the algebraic unit and requires no bound on its Mahler measure.
More generally \(h_J\ge h>0\) along a sequence with \(J\to\infty\) requires \(L\gg_h Jd\log(2J)\).
For masks with \(s\ge\delta L^J\), a rate \(\log M_S/s\ge h>0\) requires the same lower bound with constants depending on \(h,\delta\).
Thus the logarithmic cost is necessary for an actual positive-rate common fiber.
The [discriminant-sensitive construction](Yin_C_G0345_v786.md#discriminant) reaches this order under its fixed entropy slack and root-discriminant lower bound.

There is a corresponding restriction on the [parities admitting a signed lift](Yin_C_G0345_v788.md#statement).
On the full grid put \(n=L^J\).
If \(L/(Jd)\to c>1/\log2\) with \(c\) finite, the fraction of the binary joint kernel admitting a lift with entries in \(\{0,1,-1\}\) is at most \(\exp(-c' n)\) for all sufficiently large \(J\), with \(c'>0\) depending on \(c\).
For masks of density at least \(\delta>0\), the same exponential bound holds for the fraction in the shortened binary kernel if \(L/(Jd)\) stays between fixed positive constants and
\[
             1-(1-d/L)^J\le \delta/3-\epsilon
\]
for some fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
These results permit subexponential fibers at the critical side length.

<a id="biased"></a>
## The concentration cost of one line with arbitrary marginals

Let \(P\) be any probability law on binary strings of length \(L\) for which the weighted sum on the line is fixed at \(y\).
Let \(p_i\) be the probability of a one at site \(i\), and let \(Q\) be the law of independent bits with those marginals.
The dependence within the line satisfies
\[
 \sum_i H_2(p_i)-H(P)=D(P\Vert Q)\ge-\log Q\{\text{sum}=y\}.             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed restricting \(Q\) to the fiber and dividing by its probability gives a probability law there, whose relative entropy from \(P\) in the order \(D(P\Vert\,\cdot\,)\) is nonnegative.
This also handles deterministic coordinates.

Represent the powers by integer columns \(v_i\) in the power basis and use Fourier inversion on the \(d\)-torus as in the [degree-block bound](Yin_C_G0345_v786.md#marginal).
Bound the atom on the right of (3) by the integral of the product of the characteristic function moduli.
Discard positions outside \(k^{-a}\le p_i\le1-k^{-a}\), leaving \(t\) positions.
Partition into \(k\) consecutive blocks of size at most \(d\).
Hölder's inequality with exponent \(k\) in every block bounds the integral by the product of the \(k\)-th roots of the block integrals with each modulus raised to power \(k\).
Every selection of columns in a single block is rationally independent because the corresponding exponents lie in an interval of length at most \(d\).
The corresponding coordinate frequencies \(\langle v_i,\theta\rangle\bmod1\) under Haar measure have joint product Haar distribution, by testing integer Fourier characters.
Thus the one-dimensional integrals multiply in each block.

For a retained site one has \(2p_i(1-p_i)\ge k^{-a}\).
Using \(\sin(\pi t)\ge2t\) for \(0\le t\le1/2\) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
  \int_0^1 |1-p_i+p_i e^{2\pi i x}|^k\,dx
  &\le \int_0^1 \exp[-k^{1-a}\sin^2(\pi x)]\,dx\\
  &\le \frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\,k^{-(1-a)/2}
   \le k^{-(1-a)/2}.
 \end{split}
\]
The atom probability is therefore at most \(k^{-(1-a)t/(2k)}\).
Meanwhile \(H(P)\le\sum_i H_2(p_i)\le t\log2+Lu\).
Substitution in (3) proves the line inequality
\[
                    \sum_i H_2(p_i)-H(P)\ge b\,[H(P)-Lu].               \tag{4}
\]

<a id="slices"></a>
## Entropy lost between adjacent dimensions

Choose an array uniformly in a fiber with \(M_j\) elements, and slice it by its last coordinate into \(L\) arrays \(X_i\) of dimension \(j-1\).
Each slice belongs to a prescribed fiber of its own line records, so \(\sum_iH(X_i)\le L\log M_{j-1}\).
At \(j=1\) each slice is just one bit with entropy at most \(h_0\).
Order the \(L^{j-1}\) sites of a slice in an arbitrary fixed way.
For each site \(v\), condition on the full array of bits at all earlier sites of all slices, denoted by \(B_v\).
Let \(P_v\) be the conditional joint law of the row \((X_i(v))_i\).
It is supported on the specified line sum in the last direction since the original record was fixed.

The chain rule within slice \(i\) conditions only on that slice's earlier sites.
Conditioning also on all other earlier sites can only decrease its conditional entropy.
Subtracting the chain rule for the joint array consequently gives
\[
 \sum_i H(X_i)-\log M_j
 \ \ge\ \sum_v \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i H_2(p_{i,v})-H(P_v)\right],
\]
where \(p_{i,v}\) are the marginals under \(P_v\).
The joint chain rule also gives \(\sum_v\mathbb E H(P_v)=\log M_j\).
Applying (4) inside each conditional law, and dividing by \(L^j\), gives
\[
                     h_{j-1}\ge (1+b)h_j-bu .
\]
Iteration proves (1).
For \(k/J\to c\), \(u=O_a(k^{-a}\log k)\) and \((1+b)^{-J}=k^{-(1-a)/(2c\log2)+o(1)}\).
Taking \(a=1/(1+2c\log2)\) gives (2).

For arbitrary \(L\ge d\), the [rational kernel dimension](Yin_C_G0345_v782.md#dimension) gives \(h_J\le(1-d/L)^J\log2\le e^{-J/k}\log2\).
Hence if \(h_J\ge h>0\), \(k\gg_h J\).
Taking \(a=1/2\) in (1) now gives \(u\to0\) and \(J\log(1+b)=O_h(1)\), so \(k\gg_h J\log(2J)\).
Since \(L>(k-1)d\), this implies the stated side-length bound.
Every masked fiber is a subset of a full-grid fiber, and the claimed extension follows.

<a id="parity"></a>
## The scarcity of signed lifts

Set \(K=L-d\) and \(q=n-K^J\).
By the [integral and binary kernel splitting](Yin_C_G0345_v788.md#kernel), the full binary code \(C\) is the \(J\)-fold tensor product of the one-dimensional kernel \(\bar W\) and has dimension \(K^J\).
We first control the weight \(w\) of a uniform word of \(C\).
Let \(b_0=\lfloor L/d\rfloor\ge2\) and \(\theta=(1-1/b_0)^J\).
Partition the first \(b_0d\) positions into \(b_0\) consecutive blocks of size \(d\).
Each of these is a basis of the reduced line map because multiplication by \(\alpha\) is invertible modulo two.
Projection from \(\bar W\) to the positions outside any chosen block is therefore bijective.

In each axis independently remove a uniformly chosen one of these blocks, forming a Cartesian product of the complementary position sets.
The bits on that product are independent and fair for a uniform word of \(C\), since tensoring the coordinate projections gives an isomorphism.
Every grid site belongs to this random product with probability at least \(\theta\), including sites using a position after the last complete block.
Thin the product by independent deletions with site-specific probabilities so that each site is retained with probability exactly \(\theta\).
The resulting set \(T\) is independent of the word, and the bits on it remain independent for each fixed \(T\).

For each fixed word \(z\), the conditional mean over \(T\) of \(\sum_{t\in T}(z_t-1/2)\) is \(\theta(w(z)-n/2)\).
Jensen's inequality followed by the fair-bit moment bound \(\cosh(v/2)\le e^{v^2/8}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_z e^{v\theta(w-n/2)}
   \le \mathbb E_T \cosh(v/2)^{|T|}
   \le e^{n v^2/8}.
\]
Optimizing the exponential Markov bound yields
\[
                        \Pr(w>n/2+x)\le e^{-2\theta^2x^2/n}.
\]
When \(L/(Jd)\to c>1/\log2\), both \(K^J/n\) and \(\theta\) tend to \(e^{-1/c}>1/2\).
Choose \(\epsilon>0\) with \(K^J/n>1/2+2\epsilon\) eventually.
By [the signed collision count](Yin_C_G0345_v788.md#statement), the fraction of liftable words of weight at most \((1/2+\epsilon)n\) is at most \(M_J2^{(1/2+\epsilon)n-K^J}\).
This fraction is exponentially small by (2), as is the weight tail just bounded.
Adding them proves the full-grid conclusion.

For a mask of size \(s\), denote its kernel dimension by \(D_S\) and the fraction of its liftable words by \(p>0\).
The [shortened-code inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v788.md#masks) gives
\[
 \log_2 M_S\ge 3D_S/2-s-\sqrt{D_S\log(2/p)/2}+\log_2(p/2).
\]
Here \(D_S\ge s-q\), so under the stated hypothesis \(3D_S/2-s\ge3\epsilon n/2\).
Equation (1) with \(a=1/2\) gives \(\log M_S=o(n)\) uniformly when \(L/(Jd)\) is bounded above and below by positive constants.
If \(\log(1/p)\le\eta n\) for sufficiently small \(\eta=\eta(\epsilon)>0\), then \(D_S\le n\) bounds the square-root and last terms in magnitude by less than \(\epsilon n\) in total for large \(n\).
The resulting positive fiber rate contradicts the uniform upper bound, proving the asserted exponential scarcity.
