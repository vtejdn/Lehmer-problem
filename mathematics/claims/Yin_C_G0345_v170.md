# A capacity deficit for radial segments and a factor gap for separated polynomial blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Factors of two separated blocks

Fix \(L\ge2\) and \(0<\delta\le1\).
Let \(B,C\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero polynomials of coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm at most \(L\), and suppose all zeros of \(B\) lie in the closed unit disk.
Put \(b=\deg B\) and consider
\[
 f(X)=X^N B(X)+C(X),\qquad g=N-\deg C,\qquad n=N+b .
\]
If
\[
                        g-2b\ge\delta n,\qquad n\ge1,                 \tag{1}
\]
then every monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor \(A\) of \(f\) satisfies \(\deg A\ge c(L,\delta)n\).
Every irreducible integral factor of positive logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies \(m(A)\ge c'(L,\delta)>0\).
Both blocks and all their exponents may vary.
The constants allow arbitrarily many moving zeros of \(B\), with multiplicities, and the leading coefficient of \(B\) may vary within the norm bound.
The underlying [capacity estimate (2)](#geometry) applies to arbitrary complex zeros in the disk and arbitrary radial segments in its containing region.

For example, for positive integers \(u,v,h\) with \(u,v\le h/10\), the reciprocal polynomials
\[
             (1+X^{2u})(1+X^{2h})
                   -3X^{h+u-v}-3X^{h+u+v}
\]
have this individual-factor gap uniformly in all three parameters.
Here \(N=2h,\ B=1+X^{2u},\ b=2u,\ L=8\), and \(g-2b=h-5u-v\ge 2h/5\ge (N+b)/6\).
Their values at \(1\) are negative and their leading coefficients are positive, so each has a real root greater than one.

<a id="geometry"></a>
## The capacity lost between radial segments

Let \(Q\) be any monic complex polynomial of degree \(d\ge0\) with all zeros in the closed unit disk.
For an integer \(j\), write \(P(z)=z^jQ(z)\) on \(|z|\ge1\).
Assume
\[
           D=j+d>0,\qquad \lambda=(j+d/2)/D>0 .
\]
In particular \(0<\lambda\le1\).
Choose \(T>\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\) and put
\[
          E=\{|z|\le1\}\ \cup\ \{|z|\ge1:\ |P(z)|\le T\}.
\]
If \(K\subset E\) is the union of \(s\ge1\) radial segments from zero to nonzero endpoints, then
\[
               \log\operatorname{cap}K
                  \le \frac{\log T}{D}-\frac{\lambda^2}{192s}.        \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(j\ge0\) gives a deficit at least \(1/(768s)\) for polynomial lemniscates, independently of the positions and number of zeros.

For \(z=re^{i\theta}\), \(r>1\), and \(|b_0|\le1\),
\[
        \partial_{\log r}\log|z-b_0|
          =\operatorname{Re}\frac{z}{z-b_0}
          =\frac12+\frac{r^2-|b_0|^2}{2|z-b_0|^2}\ge\frac12 .
\]
Consequently \(u(t,\theta)=\log|P(e^{t+i\theta})|\) has \(u_t\ge\lambda D\) for \(t>0\).
The boundary of \(E\) has the smooth polar parametrization \(z_\theta=R(\theta)e^{i\theta}\), determined uniquely by \(u(\log R,\theta)=\log T\) with \(R>1\).
Outside \(E\) its Green function with pole at infinity is \(G_E=(\log|P|-\log T)/D\), by its boundary values and logarithmic growth.
In particular \(\log\operatorname{cap}E=(\log T)/D\).
The equilibrium measure on its boundary is the outward normal derivative of \(G_E\) times arclength divided by \(2\pi\).
In this parametrization it is
\[
       d\mu_E=\frac{u_t+u_\theta^2/u_t}{2\pi D}\,d\theta
                    \ \ge\ \frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,d\theta ,
\]
since \((\log R)'=-u_\theta/u_t\).

Remove from the angular circle the intervals of radius \(1/(4s)\) about the directions of the segments of \(K\).
Their total length is at most \(1/2\), so at least half the circle remains.
For each remaining \(\theta\), the open disk of radius \(R(\theta)/(8s)\) about \(z_\theta\) misses \(K\), since its distance to every segment is at least \(R(\theta)\sin(1/(4s))\ge R(\theta)/(8s)\).
The Green function \(G_K\) is positive and harmonic on that disk.
Set \(h=1/(16s)\).
At \((1+h)z_\theta\), domain monotonicity and the radial derivative give
\[
                G_K\ge G_E\ge\lambda\log(1+h)\ge\lambda/(32s).
\]
The disk form of Harnack's inequality at half its radius gives \(G_K(z_\theta)\ge\lambda/(96s)\).
Integrating over the retained angles therefore gives \(\int G_K\,d\mu_E\ge\lambda^2/(192s)\).
The equilibrium potential \(\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu_E(z)\) equals \(\log\operatorname{cap}E\) throughout \(E\).
Integrating the equilibrium-potential representation of \(G_K\) against \(\mu_E\) and interchanging the measures gives
\[
              \int G_K\,d\mu_E=\log\operatorname{cap}E-\log\operatorname{cap}K .
\]
Both compact sets are regular for the exterior Dirichlet problem, since \(E\) has smooth boundary and \(K\) is a finite union of segments.
This proves (2).

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## All rotations in a degree drop

Let \(A\) be a monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor of \(f\), of degree \(a\).
Take the largest power of two \(k\) for which \(A(X)=A_0(X^k)\) with \(A_0\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
The polynomial \(A_0\) is monic, irreducible, noncyclotomic, and has degree \(a_0=a/k\).
Its constant coefficient is a unit, so \(a_0>1\).
The squares of its roots are distinct.
Indeed a collision would give two distinct roots \(\beta,-\beta\) and hence \(A_0(-X)=(-1)^{a_0}A_0(X)\) by irreducibility.
Comparison of constant terms would then force even degree and a polynomial in \(X^2\), contrary to maximality of \(k\).
The Galois group is transitive on those distinct squares, so their orbit polynomial is irreducible.
By Dimitrov's arithmetic capacity criterion in the form stated by [van Ittersum, Ringeling and Zudilin, Section 2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972721000654), the union \(K\) of the radial segments ending at \(\beta^2,\beta^4\) for \(A_0(\beta)=0\) has capacity at least one.
There are at most \(2a_0\) segments.

For \(q\in\{2,4\}\) let \(Q_q\) be the monic polynomial determined, up to division by the leading coefficient on the right, by
\[
                  Q_q(X^{qk})\ \propto\
                    \prod_{\rho^{qk}=1} B(\rho X).
\]
The product is invariant under all \(qk\)-th root rotations of \(X\), and its leading coefficient has modulus at least one.
Thus \(Q_q\) has degree \(b\), all its zeros lie in the closed unit disk, and \(\|Q_q\|_1\le L^{qk}\).
If \(\alpha\) is any root of \(A\) with \(r=|\alpha|\ge1\), then \(\rho\alpha\) is a root of \(f\) for every \(\rho^k=1\).
For each of these \(k\) rotations the original equation gives \(|B(\rho\alpha)|\le Lr^{-g}\).
For all remaining \(qk-k\) rotations one has \(|B(\rho\alpha)|\le Lr^b\).
Consequently
\[
      |Q_q(\alpha^{qk})|\le L^{qk}r^{k[-g+(q-1)b]} .                \tag{3}
\]

<a id="application"></a>
## One containing region for both powers

Set \(Q=Q_2Q_4^2,\ d=3b,\ j=\lfloor(g-5b)/2\rfloor\) and \(T=2L^{10k}\).
Then \(\|Q\|_1\le L^{10k}\).
At the endpoint \(z=\alpha^{2k}\), use (3) on \(Q_2\) and \(|Q_4(z)|\le L^{4k}|z|^b\) on the two copies of \(Q_4\).
This gives
\[
                     |z^j Q(z)|\le L^{10k}r^{k(2j-g+5b)}
                                 \le L^{10k}\qquad(r\ge1).
\]
At \(z=\alpha^{4k}\), use (3) on the two copies of \(Q_4\) and \(|Q_2(z)|\le L^{2k}|z|^b\).
This gives \(|z^jQ(z)|\le L^{10k}r^{k(4j-2g+10b)}\le L^{10k}\) for \(r\ge1\).
Every endpoint for \(A_0\) occurs this way since its roots are the distinct \(\alpha^k\).

For \(n\ge4/\delta\), (1) gives
\[
             j+d/2\ge (g-2b)/2-1\ge\delta n/4,\qquad
             j+d\le(g+b)/2\le n/2 .
\]
Hence \(D=j+d\ge\delta n/4\) and \(\lambda\ge\delta/2\) in (2).
The corresponding \(E\) contains the unit disk and every exterior endpoint by the two displayed estimates.
Its radial sections are intervals, so it contains every complete segment of \(K\).
Apply (2) with \(s\le2a/k\) and \(\operatorname{cap}K\ge1\).
It yields
\[
                      a\ge \frac{k\lambda^2D}{384\log T}
                        \ge \frac{\delta^3 n}{6144(10\log L+\log2)} .
\]
For \(n<4/\delta\), \(\deg A\ge1\) supplies a uniform degree fraction.
Since \(f\) has at most \(2L\) nonzero integral coefficients, the [sparse divisor estimate of Dobrowolski, Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) now gives the asserted positive Mahler gap for the unit factors.
An irreducible factor with absolute leading or constant coefficient at least two has \(m(A)\ge\log2\), by the root formula applied in both orders.
The irreducible factor \(X\) has measure zero.
These cases cover the assertion for all positive-measure factors.
