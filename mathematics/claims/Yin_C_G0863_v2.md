## Statement

Let K consist of the connected subtori H of T^4 whose four restricted coordinate characters chi_j=z_j|H are nontrivial and distinct up to inversion.
Equivalently, their saturated annihilator lattices L(H) in Z^4 contain none of e_j or e_i+/-e_j for i different from j.
Give K the topology of pointwise membership of fixed integer vectors in L(H), equivalently weak convergence of the associated Haar measures.
Put

    F_epsilon=sum_(j=1..4)epsilon_j(chi_j-chi_j^(-1)),
    m_H(F_epsilon)=integral_H log|F_epsilon|,
    g(H)=min_(epsilon in {+1,-1}^4) m_H(F_epsilon),
    gamma=9/(4sqrt11),
    eta_large=49/72-gamma>0,
    eta_0=11/12150,
    b_0=log2-eta_0/2.

All integrals use normalized Haar measure and natural logarithms, with the ordinary integrable Laurent-polynomial zero convention.
The assertions are as follows.

1. The space K is compact and g is upper semicontinuous.
   If g(H)<log2 for every H in K, there is a single positive margin valid for every H in K and hence for every four-tuple of distinct positive integer frequencies.
   This conditional assertion does not supply its universal strict-inequality hypothesis.
2. On the full four-dimensional torus, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<23/50.
3. If one character is outside the rational span of the other three, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=gamma<log2-5/1296.
4. If L(H) annihilates some delta in {+1,-1}^4, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=log2-1/512.
5. Let X=Z^4/L(H), and let X_j be the subgroup generated by the three coordinate characters other than j.
   If q_j=[X:X_j] is finite, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=gamma+2(log2)/q_j.
   In particular q_j>=128 gives m_H(F_epsilon)<=log2-eta_large, at every rank.
6. At rank three write L(H)=Zc with c primitive.
   For each nonzero c_j, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<23/50+2(log2)/|c_j|.
   Consequently max_j|c_j|>=6 gives m_H(F_epsilon)<log2-eta_0.
7. Every H in K with dim H>=3 satisfies g(H)<log2-eta_0.
   More precisely, for every full-support primitive relation whose absolute coefficients are at most five and have no equal-weight partition, there is a sign with m_H(F_epsilon)<17/25<log2-23/2025.
   At rank three there are at most two sign-measure classes, and only one if the coefficient sum of a primitive relation is odd.
8. On the rank-two torus with character vectors e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2,e_1-e_2, all sixteen signs have the same measure and satisfy m_H(F_epsilon)<log2-log(93/92).
   This is a Haar-torus bound, without the same asserted bound for every one-variable specialization.
9. There are finitely many rank-two tori H_1,...,H_r in K and finitely many rank-one tori E_1,...,E_s in K, all with g>=b_0, such that every rank-one H in K satisfying g(H)>=b_0 is contained in some H_i or equals some E_j.
   The E_j may be chosen outside every H_i.
   Equivalently, every circle outside these host families and exceptions has g(H)<b_0.

Part 7 strengthens the old finite rank-three reduction; Part 9 is new.
No effective enumeration of the hosts or exceptional circles is asserted.
The statement leaves general dependent rank-two configurations, the circles inside the possible bad hosts, the exceptional circles and arbitrary original-input auxiliary supply unresolved.

## The exact certificate for forty-two rank-three classes

### Canonical inputs and signs

Coordinate permutations and inversions reduce the sign-minimum problem to

    c=(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4), 1<=c_1<=c_2<=c_3<=c_4<=5,
    gcd(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=1,
    sum_j delta_j c_j !=0 for every delta in {1,-1}^4.   (MC1)

These operations can change which sign is favorable, but preserve the existence of one and its Mahler mean.
The [native case input](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0134/inputs/rank-three-cases.json) lists exactly forty-two tuples satisfying (MC1).
The [program](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0134/rank-three-moments.py) regenerates them by all nondecreasing four-tuples from {1,...,5}, then checks the gcd and every one of the sixteen signed sums before comparing the entire resulting list with that native input.
This is a finite exhaustive enumeration under (MC1), not a sample of relation coefficients.

Let H_c={z in T^4:z^c=1} and put

    F_epsilon=sum_j epsilon_j(z_j-z_j^(-1)),
    G_epsilon=F_epsilon/i=2sum_j epsilon_j sin(theta_j),
    sigma=product_j epsilon_j^(c_j).

The real function G_epsilon, rather than the imaginary-valued F_epsilon, is used in the nonnegative even moments.
Its absolute value equals that of F_epsilon and is at most eight.
The earlier parity argument shows that only sigma in {1,-1} matters for the sign orbit.
When sum_j c_j is odd, global negation identifies both values of sigma.
When that sum is even, a representative for sigma=-1 is obtained by negating one epsilon_j having odd c_j; such an index exists because c is primitive.
The all-positive signs represent sigma=1.

### Integer walk coefficients and the sine phase

For v in Z^4, let A_n(v) be the coefficient of z^v in (sum_j(z_j+z_j^(-1)))^n.
Let B_t(u,v) count the two-dimensional nearest-neighbor walks of length t ending at (u,v).
The change of endpoint coordinates (u,v)->(u+v,u-v) sends each of the four possible steps bijectively to a pair of independent signs.
Therefore

    B_t(u,v)=binom(t,(t+u+v)/2)*binom(t,(t+u-v)/2),
    A_n(v)=sum_(t=0..n) binom(n,t)
              B_t(v_1,v_2) B_(n-t)(v_3,v_4).           (MC2)

A binomial with a noninteger index or an index outside [0,t] is zero.
The outer binomial chooses which steps use the first coordinate pair; the two B factors then count all choices in the two pairs.
In particular A_n(v)=0 unless sum_j|v_j|<=n and sum_j v_j has the parity of n.
The coefficients are unchanged by independent sign changes of the endpoint coordinates.

For a walk of even length 2k with p positive-direction steps and 2k-p negative-direction steps, the expansion of G has step coefficients -i epsilon_j and i epsilon_j respectively.
Since 2p-2k=sum_j v_j, its total phase is

    (-i)^p i^(2k-p)=(-1)^(sum_j v_j/2).

Every walk ending at v has this same phase, and its sign multiplier is product_j epsilon_j^(v_j), because inverse and positive powers of epsilon_j agree.
Haar integration on H_c keeps exactly exponents v in Zc.
Pairing ell*c and -ell*c gives the exact nonnegative moments

    M_k=integral_(H_c) G_epsilon^(2k)
       =A_(2k)(0)
        +2sum_(1<=ell<=2k/(sum_j c_j), ell*sum_j c_j even)
             (-1)^(ell*sum_j c_j/2) sigma^ell A_(2k)(ell*c). (MC3)

The restriction on ell is essential: its excluded odd-parity terms have zero walk coefficient and do not have an integer phase exponent.
When sum_j c_j is odd, only even ell occur, so sigma disappears from (MC3), agreeing with the torus sign-orbit argument.
For every tuple in (MC1), the first three moments are M_0=1, M_1=8 and M_2=168.

### The finite logarithm certificate

Set X=G_epsilon^2/64, so 0<=X<=1, and fix J=128.
For j>=1 define

    r_j=integral_(H_c)(1-X)^j
       =sum_(k=0..j)(-1)^k binom(j,k) M_k/64^k,
    R_j=64^j r_j in Z,
    S_J=sum_(j=1..J) R_j/(j*64^j).                     (MC4)

The polynomial F_epsilon is nonzero on H_c because its four coordinate characters are nontrivial and distinct up to inversion; a full-support rank-three primitive relation cannot identify two such characters or kill one.
Thus its logarithm is Haar integrable.
For 0<X<=1 the finite positive logarithm series gives log X<=-sum_(j=1..J)(1-X)^j/j, with the extended value minus infinity at X=0.
Integration therefore yields the upper bound

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<=1/2(log64-S_J).                 (MC5)

This uses no remainder estimate and no replacement of a logarithmic integral by quadrature.
The exact certificate is simply S_128>14/5.
Writing S_128=N/D in lowest terms with D>0, the program checks the integer inequality 5N-14D>0.
It also checks 0<=R_j<=64^j and R_j<=64R_(j-1), as required by 0<=1-X<=1.

For explicit logarithm constants, the atanh series at1/3 gives

    56/81=2/3+2/81<log2
      <2/3+2/81+1/540=1123/1620<52/75.

The upper tail bound replaces every denominator 2n+1 for n>=2 by five and sums the resulting geometric series.
Hence log64<104/25, and a passing certificate gives

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<1/2(104/25-14/5)=17/25
                     <log2-23/2025.                   (MC6)

The last comparison uses 56/81-17/25=23/2025.

## Assembly of the dimension-at-least-three bound

For a rank-three H, its annihilator has rank one and is generated by a primitive c.
If some c_j is zero, there is no relation with a nonzero coefficient of the j-th character.
That character is outside the rational span of the other three, so Part 3 applies.
If c has full support and max_j|c_j|>=6, Part 6 applies.
If c has full support, all |c_j|<=5 and some delta in {+1,-1}^4 has c dot delta=0, Part 4 applies.

Every remaining c has full support, coefficient moduli at most five and no equal-weight partition.
To check the canonical reduction explicitly, put d_j=|c_j| and w_j=z_j^(sgn(c_j)).
Then z^c=1 is equivalent to w^d=1, and

    epsilon_j(z_j-z_j^(-1))
       =epsilon_j sgn(c_j)(w_j-w_j^(-1)).

This torus automorphism preserves Haar measure and permutes the sixteen sign choices.
A coordinate permutation sorts d without changing the sign minimum or primitivity.
The resulting tuple therefore belongs to the exact list (MC1), and the certified sign pulls back to an actual sign on the original H.
Changing the generator c to -c has no effect on this existence assertion.

The relevant margins obey

    5/1296>eta_0,   1/512>eta_0,
    23/2025=138/12150>eta_0,   eta_0=11/12150.

Thus every rank-three branch gives g(H)<log2-eta_0, with strictness also in the common-phase branch because its margin is strictly larger.
At rank four H is the whole torus, so Part 2 applies; alternatively each character is a coloop and Part 3 directly supplies a larger margin.
This proves Part 7 for every dim H>=3.

## Finitely many bad rank-two hosts and exceptional circles

Put

    B={H in K:g(H)>=b_0},   b_0=log2-eta_0/2.

Part 1 makes B closed and hence compact.
Part 7 excludes all dimensions at least three from B.

For 1<=d<=4 define K_(>=d)={H in K:dim H>=d}.
This is closed in K.
Indeed its complement means rank L(H)>=5-d, which is witnessed by membership of 5-d linearly independent fixed integer vectors.
The requirement that finitely many specified vectors lie in L is open and closed in the product membership topology.
Taking the union over such independent sets makes the complement open.

For a torus H of dimension d, choose an integer basis c_1,...,c_(4-d) of its saturated lattice L(H).
Let

    U_H={H' in K:c_1,...,c_(4-d) belong to L(H')}.

This is a clopen neighborhood of H, and L(H) subset L(H') implies H' subset H.
If H' also has dimension at least d, the two annihilator lattices have the same rank and rational span.
Their inclusion is equality: for any v in L(H'), some positive integer multiple belongs to L(H), and saturation of L(H) then gives v in L(H).
Thus U_H intersect K_(>=d) consists only of H.
Each dimension-d point is isolated in K_(>=d).

Now B intersect K_(>=2) is compact, consists only of dimension-two points and is discrete by the preceding isolation argument.
A compact discrete space is finite, since the cover by its singleton open sets must have a finite subcover.
Write these finitely many tori as H_1,...,H_r.
For each H_i use the clopen neighborhood U_(H_i) determined by its two basis relations.
Every torus in that neighborhood is contained in H_i.

The residual

    B_0=B outside union_(i=1..r) U_(H_i)

is compact because it is a closed subset of B.
It contains no torus of dimension at least two.
Dimension zero is excluded from K by the nontrivial-character condition, so B_0 consists of circles.
Each circle is isolated in all of K by its three basis relations, using the same saturation argument with d=1.
Hence B_0 is finite, say E_1,...,E_s.

A circle in B either belongs to one of the chosen U_(H_i) and is contained in that host, or belongs to B_0 and equals an E_j.
An exceptional E_j is outside every host: if E_j were contained in H_i, its annihilator would contain the two chosen host relations, putting it in U_(H_i).
All hosts and exceptions belong to B by construction.
This proves every assertion of Part 9, including the strict inequality outside the listed families.

The proof supplies qualitative finiteness only.
It neither computes the hosts or exceptions nor controls the sign minimum of every circle contained in a bad host.
Finitely many circles means finitely many rank-one subtori, without treating all scalar multiples of an integer frequency tuple as different exceptional tori.
