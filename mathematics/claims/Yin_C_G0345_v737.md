# Small values of sparse polynomials on moving algebraic conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sublevel bound for every small-height orbit

Let \(\alpha_i\) be nonzero nontorsion algebraic numbers with absolute logarithmic heights \(h_i\to0\) and degrees \(d_i\).
Fix \(r\ge2\).
For each \(i\) take any nonzero complex Laurent polynomial with at most \(r\) terms and exponent span \(W_i\), where \(W_i h_i\to0\).
Divide by the lowest monomial power and the coefficient \(\ell^2\) norm, writing the result as \(Q_i\).
For every \(t\ge0\), with an absolute \(A\ge1\),
\[
 \limsup_i\frac1{d_i}\#\{\sigma:|Q_i(\sigma\alpha_i)|\le t\}
       \ \le\ \min(1,A t^{1/(r-1)}).                                    \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\sigma\) runs over the \(d_i\) complex embeddings of the original field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)\).
The same bound holds at the phases \(\sigma\alpha_i/|\sigma\alpha_i|\).
In particular, a bounded number of such polynomials, each with its own coefficients and exponents and tested on any assigned subset, can take values tending uniformly to zero on only \(o(d_i)\) distinct conjugates in total.
The hypotheses include all signatures, nonunits and nonintegral algebraic numbers.
The normalization by a monomial matters for the version at the actual complex conjugates.

For a Salem number \(\alpha\), write \(m=\log\alpha\), \(d=\deg\alpha\), and let \(\Theta\) denote all \(d-2\) neutral arguments.
For a polynomial normalized as above with span \(W\), one has at every \(t\ge0\)
\[
 \frac{\#\{\theta\in\Theta:|Q(e^{i\theta})|\le t\}}{d-2}
 \ \le\ C_r\left(t+
       \left(\frac{Wm+\log(2d)}{d}\right)^{1/(2(r+1))}\right)^{1/(r-1)}. \tag{2}
\]
Consequently for a positive fraction of neutral roots to have values tending to zero along a shrinking Salem sequence, a single bounded-term witness must have \(W\gg d/m\), with a constant depending on the fraction and the number of terms.
Estimate (2) can also be summed over different witnesses when their number grows.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## The character averages for moving supports

Write \(Q_i(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{l} c_j X^{a_j}\) with \(0=a_0<\cdots<a_l=W_i\), \(l\le r-1\) and \(\sum |c_j|^2=1\), suppressing \(i\) in the coefficients and exponents.
For a monomial the normalized modulus is one and the assertions follow immediately.
Use normalized Haar probability on every circle.
Consider the empirical distribution \(\mu_i\) of the vectors
\[
       (e^{ia_1\theta},\ldots,e^{ia_l\theta}),
               \qquad e^{i\theta}=\sigma\alpha_i/|\sigma\alpha_i|,
\]
and let \(\nu_i\) be the distribution of the same vector for uniform \(\theta\).
For a frequency \(v\in\mathbb Z^l\) put \(q=\sum v_j a_j\).
For \(q=0\) both Fourier coefficients are one.
When \(q\ne0\), the \(\nu_i\) coefficient is zero and the \(\mu_i\) coefficient, up to conjugation, is the mean phase of \(\alpha_i^{|q|}\).
Every conjugate of the latter has the same number of preimages among the original embeddings, by separability and the extension count in a tower.
In every fixed box \(\|v\|_\infty\le M\),
\[
              \max_v|\widehat\mu_i(v)-\widehat\nu_i(v)|\longrightarrow0. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed any selected nonzero \(q\) in the box gives \(h(\alpha_i^{|q|})\le l M W_i h_i\to0\).
All these powers are nontorsion, so in dimension one the sequence is strict: every proper algebraic subgroup consists of roots of unity.
[Bilu's equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) applied to the bounded continuous phase function on \(\mathbb C^*\) gives the asserted convergence, also along subsequences of maximizing frequencies.

Possible relations between support differences are included in (3).
For example, after fixing \(l\) on a subsequence and making a diagonal extraction, each relation \(v\cdot a=0\) is either eventually true or eventually false.
The eventually true relations form a saturated sublattice because \(n(v\cdot a)=0\) implies \(v\cdot a=0\) for any nonzero integer \(n\).
Their annihilator in the compact torus is connected, as is seen by extending a basis of the saturated sublattice to an integral basis.
Its Haar Fourier coefficients are the indicators of these relations.
Thus \(\mu_i\) and \(\nu_i\) converge weakly along this extraction to the same subtorus Haar measure by trigonometric approximation, even when the exponent differences satisfy exact relations throughout.
The estimates below work directly with the varying \(\nu_i\).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## From ordinary circle measure to the original conjugates

Put \(F(z)=c_0+\sum_{j=1}^{l}c_j z_j\) on the \(l\)-torus.
For each \(i\), the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) gives
\[
            \nu_i\{|F|\le x\}\le\min(1,A x^{1/(r-1)})\qquad(x\ge0).      \tag{4}
\]
In fact on the one-variable parametrization the polynomial has \(l+1\) distinct purely imaginary frequencies, and its supremum is at least its coefficient norm one by Parseval.
Applying the cited estimate on one period to the sublevel set gives (4) for \(0<x<1\), with exponent \(1/l\ge1/(r-1)\).
The zero set has measure zero by analyticity, and the other thresholds use the trivial probability bound.

Given any \(\delta>0\), form a cutoff \(f\) which is one for \(|F|\le t\), decreases linearly to zero at \(t+\delta\), and vanishes thereafter.
Its change between two angle tuples is at most \(\delta^{-1}\sum_j |c_j|\operatorname{dist}(\theta_j,\theta'_j)\).
Convolve \(f\) in each angle with the normalized square of the Fejér kernel of order \(J\ge2\), obtaining a polynomial \(f_J\) of coordinate degrees at most \(2J\).
The normalized squared kernel has first absolute angular moment \(O(1/J)\).
Indeed the Fejér kernel on \([-\pi,\pi]\) is \(O(\min(J,1/(Jx^2)))\) by its sine quotient, and its squared integral is \(\gg J\) by its lower bound on \(|x|\le1/J\).
The normalized squared kernel is consequently \(O(\min(J,1/(J^3 x^4)))\), giving the moment bound by integration.
It follows that \(\|f-f_J\|_\infty\ll_r1/(\delta J)\).
Since \(0\le f_J\le1\), Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval bound the sum of its absolute Fourier coefficients by \((4J+1)^{l/2}\).
Thus
\[
 \mu_i\{|F|\le t\}\le \nu_i\{|F|\le t+\delta\}
    +O_r\left(\frac1{\delta J}
        +J^{l/2}\max_{\|v\|_\infty\le2J}
                       |\widehat\mu_i(v)-\widehat\nu_i(v)|\right).       \tag{5}
\]
The same argument works with \(\mu_i\) formed using just the neutral arguments of a Salem number.
For (1) on the phases first fix \(\delta,J\), apply (3) and (4), and then let \(J\to\infty\) followed by \(\delta\downarrow0\).

The mean of \(|\log|\sigma\alpha_i||\) over the original embeddings is at most \(2h_i\), by the height sums for \(\alpha_i\) and \(\alpha_i^{-1}\) including the finite places.
Thus \(W_i|\log|\sigma\alpha_i||\to0\) in probability.
With \(z=\sigma\alpha_i\), the difference \(|Q_i(z)-Q_i(z/|z|)|\) is at most \(\sqrt r(\exp(W_i|\log|z||)-1)\).
This proves (1) at the actual conjugates by enlargement of \(t\) by any positive amount and passage to the limit.
For thresholds tending to zero one can dominate by any fixed positive threshold before taking the limit.
A union bound proves the asserted conclusion for boundedly many witnesses, including polynomials chosen differently for every input.

<a id="salem"></a>
## A finite estimate for neutral Salem phases

For every positive integer \(j\), the \(j\)-th powers of distinct Salem conjugates are distinct.
Otherwise an automorphism of the splitting field sending one of two colliding roots to \(\alpha\) would send the other to a distinct root of modulus \(\alpha\), contradicting uniqueness of the exterior root.
The minimum of \(\alpha^j\) therefore has degree \(d\) and total logarithmic measure \(jm\).
Apply the first harmonic of the [discriminant energy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) to that monic irreducible unit polynomial.
In its sum of reflected points, the two off-circle roots both contribute \(e^{-jm}\) and the others contribute \(e^{ij\theta}\).
The damping multiplier \(e^{-2/d}\) at the first harmonic is bounded below by an absolute constant.
Hence for positive or negative \(j\ne0\)
\[
 \frac1{d-2}\left|\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}e^{ij\theta}\right|
       \ll\sqrt{\frac{|j|m+\log(2d)}{d}},                               \tag{6}
\]
where the two removed terms are absorbed using \(d\ge4\).
For \(\mu\) built from neutral phases set \(a=(Wm+\log(2d))/d>0\).
For \(\|v\|_\infty\le2J\) the frequency \(|q|\le 2lJW\), so (6) bounds the maximum error in (5) by \(O_r(\sqrt{Ja})\).
The angular error in (5) is now \(O_r((\delta J)^{-1}+J^{r/2}\sqrt a)\).
When \(a\le1\), take \(\delta=a^{1/(2(r+1))}\) and \(J=\max(2,\lceil\delta^{-2}\rceil)\).
Both errors are \(O_r(\delta)\), which together with (4) proves (2) for \(t+\delta\le1\).
The other cases, including \(a>1\), follow by increasing \(C_r\) in the trivial bound.
Solving (2) for \(Wm/d\) at a fixed positive fraction and \(t\to0\) gives the stated span restriction as \(d\to\infty\).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Which sparse relations can test phase crowding

The [whole-orbit width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) already excludes any rational polynomial with boundedly many terms, span \(o(d/m)\), and the whole minimal polynomial as a factor for a shrinking sequence of algebraic units.
The [sector count for sparse polynomials](Yin_C_G0879_v1.md#angular-count) would give a bounded number of roots per arc of width \(O(1/W)\) for such a multiple of span \(W\).
The width restriction precludes deducing the [critical covariance criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v725.md#statement) from the existence of that multiple at \(W=o(kd)\), \(k\sim1/(2m)\).
Equations (1) and (2) now also exclude replacing the multiple by boundedly many complex short polynomials, each only approximately vanishing on part of the orbit, to cover a positive fraction.
The argument allows \(W\) greater than \(d\) and arbitrary coefficient sizes before normalization.
It leaves open neutral-root crowding itself and sparse witnesses on the scale \(d/m\) or longer.
The coordinator proposed testing sparse multiples against the critical angular scale and extending the resulting obstruction from Salem numbers to unrestricted small-height orbits.
