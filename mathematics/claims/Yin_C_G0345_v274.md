# Inversions or quadratic fields of oriented blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two alternatives for compressed reciprocal units

Let \(x\) be a reciprocal algebraic unit of logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m\le M\) with no torsion quotient between distinct conjugates.
For \(d=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]\) sufficiently large depending only on \(M\), let the \(v=d/2\) inverse pairs form the [canonical partition](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) into \(u\) blocks of size \(b=v/u\).
Write \(L\) for the splitting field of \(x\), \(T\) for the splitting field of \(x+x^{-1}\) and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
Exactly one of the following occurs.

1. The commutator subgroup \(G'\) contains all even sign changes supported on each individual block.
   Thus \([L:T]\ge2^{v-u}\), and one root from each of the \(v\) pairs gives a multiplicatively independent set.
2. The \(2b\) roots over each block split canonically into two inverse sets of \(b\) roots, each containing one root from every pair in the block.
   The \(2u\) resulting sets are permuted transitively by \(G\), and \([L:T]\le2^u\).
   The selected set containing \(x\) gives fields \(E\subset K\subset\mathbb Q(x)\) with \([E:\mathbb Q]=u,\ [K:E]=2\), and a monic integral polynomial \(f\) of degree \(b\) over \(K\) with precisely those selected roots.
   Its coefficients generate \(K\), its norm polynomial is the rational minimal polynomial of \(x\), and its conjugate under the nonidentity \(\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)\) is its monic reciprocal.
   The product \(\beta\) of the selected roots is a unit of degree at most \(2u<d\) and logarithmic measure at most \(m\).
   At a least-degree witness among all algebraic numbers of logarithmic measure in \((0,m]\), the product is torsion and the rank of the \(v\) representatives is exactly \(v-u\).

In the second case suppose \(K\) is totally real or CM and write \(\kappa\) for its canonical complex conjugation, the identity in the totally real case.
If \(\kappa\ne\tau\), then \(m\ge u\log\varphi\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
If in addition \(\beta\) is torsion, then
\[
                   m\ \ge\ 2u\log\bigl((1+\sqrt{17})/4\bigr).             \tag{1}
\]
If \(\kappa=\tau\), then \(K\) is CM and \(\beta\) is automatically torsion.
In particular, when \(u=1\) and \(m<\log\varphi\), the second case forces an imaginary quadratic subfield of \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
Hence an input with a real conjugate and primitive inverse-pair action must be in the first case at these bounds.

<a id="cycles"></a>
## Lifts of three-cycles supported on their own pairs

Label the pairs by selecting one of their two roots as the positive representative.
The kernel \(N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/T)\) is a subgroup of the elementary abelian group of sign changes on the \(v\) pairs.
Suppose an element of \(N\) has a nonconstant sign vector on some block \(B\).
Choose three of its coordinates in \(B\) on which both signs occur.
The alternating factor on \(B\) supplies a lift in \(G\) of the three-cycle fixing all other labels.
Conjugating the sign vector by this lift permutes just those three entries, so their commutator flips exactly two of the chosen pairs and fixes every other root.
By conjugation and two-transitivity on each block these double flips generate all the asserted even sign groups in \(G'\).
A nonzero relation on the representatives, subtracted from its transform by a double flip meeting a nonzero coordinate, would give a nonzero relation supported on at most two positions.
The [whole-block support theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank) excludes it.

It remains that every sign vector of \(N\) is constant on each block, so \(|N|\le2^u\).
For a three-cycle \(p\) supported on one block, any lift \(h\) has order dividing six because its underlying permutation is a three-cycle and its kernel signs have exponent two.
The fourth power \(h^4\) still has permutation \(p\), has order three and fixes every root outside the three pairs.
There is only one such supported lift, since two would differ by an element of \(N\) supported on at most three pairs and \(b\ge9\).

Within one block number the pairs \(1,\ldots,b\).
Let \(g_i\) be the supported lift of \((1\,2\,i)\), for \(3\le i\le b\), and use rightmost-first composition.
In the provisional orientation denote its edge signs on \(1\to2\), \(2\to i\) and \(i\to1\) by \(a_i,b_i,c_i\).
Their product is \(+1\) because \(g_i^3=1\).
For \(i\ne j\), the permutation of \(g_i g_j\) is \((1\,i)(2\,j)\), so \((g_i g_j)^2\) lies in \(N\) and is supported on at most four pairs.
It is therefore trivial.
Starting at label \(1\), its sign along \(1\to i\to1\) is \(a_j b_i c_i=a_j a_i\), proving that \(a_i\) is independent of \(i\).
Choose the representative at 1 arbitrarily, at 2 by this common transport, and at \(i\) by transport from 2 under \(g_i\).
All \(g_i\) now act as pure permutations of the selected roots and fix every root over the other blocks.
The cycles \((1\,2\,i)\) generate \(A_b\), so the lifted subgroup acts naturally on the selected roots and on their inverses.
In these orientations its lift of every three-cycle is supported on the three pairs, hence is the unique supported lift already identified.
The subgroup is therefore intrinsically generated by all the supported lifts on \(B\).
Its two orbits over \(B\) are the asserted selections, and conjugation by \(G\) transports them to the corresponding orbits over image blocks.
Transitivity on the roots gives transitivity on the \(2u\) selections.
The two alternatives are disjoint since \(v-u>u\).

<a id="fields"></a>
## Fields and relative norms of the oriented selections

Let \(H_B\) stabilize the pair block \(B\) containing the label of \(x\), and \(H_+\) stabilize the selection containing \(x\).
The subgroup \(H_+\) has index two in \(H_B\), since \(G\) is transitive on all the selections.
It contains the stabilizer of \(x\).
The corresponding fixed fields \(E=L^{H_B}\) and \(K=L^{H_+}\) have the stated degrees and inclusions.
The coefficients of \(f=\prod_{y\ {\rm selected}}(X-y)\) are integral and have common stabilizer \(H_+\), since the root set determines the selection.
The polynomial is irreducible over \(K\), either by its transitive alternating subgroup or by \([\mathbb Q(x):K]=b\).
Its conjugates run once through the \(2u\) disjoint selections, giving the asserted norm polynomial.
Writing \(a_0=f(0)\), the automorphism \(\tau\) satisfies
\[
          f^\tau(X)=X^b f(1/X)/a_0,\qquad \tau(\beta)=\beta^{-1}.
\]
The unit \(\beta=(-1)^b a_0\) belongs to \(K\) and satisfies \(h(\beta)\le b h(x)\).
Thus its logarithmic measure is at most \(2ub h(x)=m\).
If \(\beta\) is torsion, all blocks give torsion products by transitivity, hence \(u\) linearly independent relations with disjoint supports.
The block rank bound gives rank at least \(v-u\), so equality follows.
These products exist here without the rank-defect hypothesis in the [earlier descent](Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#descent).

<a id="gap"></a>
## The relative Mahler measure of the orientation polynomial

For a totally real or CM field \(K\), each embedding \(\sigma:K\to\mathbb C\) intertwines \(\kappa\) with ordinary complex conjugation.
The monic polynomial with roots \(1/\bar y\) for \(f(y)=0\) is therefore \((f^\tau)^\kappa\).
Equality to \(f\) is equivalent to \(\kappa=\tau\), since its coefficients generate \(K\).
For \(\kappa\ne\tau\), Schinzel's relative non-self-inversive inequality in the monic integral case gives
\[
          m=\sum_{\sigma:K\to\mathbb C}m(f^\sigma)
                     \ge [K:\mathbb Q](\log\varphi)/2 .
\]
The equality uses the norm polynomial with no repeated roots proved above. [Schinzel's 1975 addendum, Corollary 1′](../audits/literature/Sch75/Yin_U001.md#sch75-monic-integral-gap)

For completeness, the stronger torsion-constant specialization (1) is the corresponding unit-constant bound in Schinzel's 1973 Theorem 2.
The coefficient argument below uses only a first discrepancy and the absolute integral norm. [Schinzel, pp. 385–386](../audits/literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#theorem-2) · [Smyth's inner functions](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-inner-products)
Put \(g=(f^\tau)^\kappa\).
When \(a_0\) is torsion one has \(g(0)=1/\kappa(a_0)=a_0\).
Thus \(f/g\) has constant coefficient one, its series coefficients are in \(\mathcal O_K\), and its first nonconstant term is \(cX^j\) with \(c\ne0\) when \(\kappa\ne\tau\).
At each embedding write \(m_\sigma=m(f^\sigma)\).
The ratio \(f^\sigma/g^\sigma\) is a quotient \(B_1/B_2\) of finite Blaschke products with \(B_1(0)=B_2(0)=A=e^{-m_\sigma}\).
Indeed roots on the unit circle cancel, \(B_1\) uses the interior roots of \(f^\sigma\), and \(B_2\) uses the reflected exterior roots \(1/\bar y\).
The product of the moduli of the interior roots equals \(A\) since the constant coefficient has modulus one, so both normalizations are possible and the quotient has the specified value one at the origin.

For a holomorphic function \(B\) bounded by one on the disk with \(B(0)=A\), every coefficient \([X^j]B\) with \(j>0\) has modulus at most \(1-A^2\).
Average \(B(\zeta X)\) over the \(j\)-th roots of unity, regard the resulting bounded function as a series in \(X^j\), and apply Schwarz–Pick at zero, with the constant case immediate.
Comparing the coefficient of \(X^j\) in \(B_1-B_2\) gives
\[
              |\sigma c|\le 2(A^{-1}-A)=4\sinh m_\sigma .
\]
The nonzero integral norm of \(c\) has absolute value at least one.
In particular every \(m_\sigma>0\), and concavity of \(\log\sinh t\) on \(t>0\) gives \(4\sinh(m/[K:\mathbb Q])\ge1\).
This proves (1).
If \(\kappa=\tau\), one has \(\beta\kappa(\beta)=1\) in \(K\).
All conjugates of this algebraic unit then have modulus one, so Kronecker's theorem gives torsion.
For a single pair block \(K\) is quadratic, and its real possibility satisfies the lower bound \(m\ge\log\varphi\).
In the remaining imaginary quadratic case a real conjugate of \(x\) would give a real embedding of its subfield \(K\), which is impossible.
