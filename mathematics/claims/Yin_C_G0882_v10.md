## Exact original-input statement

Use the complete original construction and hypotheses of [C882v9](Yin_C_G0882_v9.md#statement): conductor \(p=19683\), trace degree \(n=9841\), cut \(k=1990\), integral \(h\), the prescribed strict signs and absolute resultant \(2^{4391}\), \(K=3,L=4,N=7\), strict endpoint, and complete \(M(P)\le101/100\).

\[
 \mathcal R=\{(10,18,27),(11,20,33),(11,20,34),(11,21,36),(11,21,37)\}.
\]
All roots and factors retain their original multiplicities.

The following conclusions hold.

1. Every one of these five original branches has the exact integer value
   \[
                         h(-1)=32.                         \tag{1}
   \]
2. For every mandatory-root selection and residual factor
   \(Q=FZ\), \(Z(t)=(t-s)^2-d\), as in C882v9, put \(y=-161/100\).
   Then
   \[
    \bigl((s+1)^2-d\bigr)\bigl(1-3s^2-d+2ys\bigr)
                  >\frac{9(-1-y)32}{19683}.                \tag{2}
   \]
3. If \((O,V,W)=(10,18,27)\), then \(M(P)=1\).
   Equivalently, the original first triple has no input with
   \[
                          1<M(P)\le101/100.                \tag{3}
   \]

The third conclusion is a strict-positive-shell exclusion.
It does not exclude a measure-one input with a real residual pair, or assert that such an input exists.
The four other triples remain unresolved in this shell.
No all-conductor statement or arbitrary-radius existence result is claimed.

## The exact integer value at the rational pole

The order-three layer consists of the single pole \(b_{6561}=-1\).
It is on the positive side of the cut.
By the adopted layer argument, its positive integer value is \(h(-1)=2^{t_1}\), with
\[
 t_1\equiv1\pmod2,\qquad
 \sum_{\ell=1}^9 3^{\ell-1}t_\ell=4391,\qquad t_\ell\ge0.
\]
Reducing the second equality modulo three gives \(t_1\equiv2\pmod3\).
Therefore
\[
                         t_1\equiv5\pmod6.                \tag{4}
\]
The first layer has only the units \(1,-1\), and its positive sign selects \(+1\); no unknown unit modulus remains in this value.

For a compact scalar bound, define the positive weights
\[
 \sigma_j=\frac{(b_j-y)r_j}{2+b_j}>0.
\]
Their sum is \(K-(2+y)U=261/100\), whereas the pole at \(-1\) contributes
\(183h(-1)/(100p)\).
Other nodes contribute positively, so
\[
 h(-1)<\frac{87p}{61}<2^{17}.
\]
Together with (4), this leaves only \(h(-1)\in\{32,2048\}\).
These are necessary possibilities, not two asserted realizations.

To exclude the second value, put
\[
 R_0=3,\ R_1=4,\ R_2=7,\ R_3=O,\ R_4=V,\ R_5=W,
 \quad \mu_j=(b_j-y)r_j>0,
\]
and form
\[
 H_{ab}=R_{a+b+1}-yR_{a+b}\quad(0\le a,b\le2),
 \qquad v=(1,-1,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad \Gamma=v^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}v.
\]
The matrix \(H\) is strictly positive definite: its quadratic form is the positive sum of a quadratic polynomial's square over all 9841 distinct nodes.
For the polynomial with coefficient vector \(H^{-1}v\), its value at \(-1\) is \(\Gamma>0\), and its squared norm is \(\Gamma\).
It cannot vanish at all the other nodes, so their contribution makes the pole-mass inequality strict:
\[
 \frac{3(-1-y)h(-1)}p\,\Gamma^2<\Gamma,
 \qquad h(-1)<\frac{p}{3(-1-y)\Gamma}.                     \tag{5}
\]
This proves the required matrix bound directly by a squared polynomial; it uses actual original grid masses.
The general point-mass mechanism is the Christoffel variational bound, with source attribution checked at [Sim08 Theorems 2.1 and 9.2](../audits/literature/Sim08/Yin_U001.md#point-mass-christoffel).
The source's global setup excludes finite support, so the present finite-support proof and its strictness are supplied above rather than asserted as a literal application of those global hypotheses.

Exact rational substitution gives the following upper bounds in (5).

| --- | --- |
| \((10,18,27)\) | \(1890993681/3916078\) |
| \((11,20,33)\) | \(35503342470/58516507\) |

Each displayed rational lies strictly between 32 and 2048, as checked by integer multiplication.
Thus 2048 is impossible in every row, proving (1).
The native rational calculation solves the five systems exactly and checks every matrix-vector identity; floating point is not used for these exclusions.

## Passing the fixed integer value to the exceptional pair

Take any permitted mandatory factor \(F\) and residual quadratic \(Z\).
The strict signs \(Z(b_j)>0\) are supplied by the adopted original-root argument, including repeated roots.
Since \(F/B\) is proper with simple poles, its partial fractions have coefficients
\[
 \frac{F(t)}{B(t)}=\sum_j\frac{\alpha_j}{t-b_j},
 \qquad \alpha_j=\frac{r_j}{Z(b_j)}.
\]
Indeed \(Q=FZ\) and \(Q(b_j)/B'(b_j)=r_j\).
At infinity, use \(Q/B=t+2+3/t+O(t^{-2})\) and \(Z=t^2-2st+s^2-d\).
Comparison of the first three Laurent coefficients yields
\[
 \sum_j\alpha_j=1,\qquad
 \sum_jb_j\alpha_j=2+2s,\qquad
 \sum_jb_j^2\alpha_j=3+4s+3s^2+d.
\]
The actual separator makes \(\beta_j=(b_j-y)\alpha_j>0\).
Therefore
\[
 \sum_j(2-b_j)\beta_j=1-3s^2-d+2ys.
\]
At \(b_j=-1\), the summand equals
\[
 \frac{9(-1-y)h(-1)}{pZ(-1)},\qquad Z(-1)=(s+1)^2-d>0.
\]
Every other summand is positive.
Substituting (1) and multiplying by the positive \(Z(-1)\) proves (2).
This step retains the integer value at one original pole, which the earlier continuous occupation relaxation did not impose.

## Complete fixed cover of the first positive shell

Assume the first triple and \(1<M(P)\le101/100\).
By C882v9 the residual pair is nonreal and lies in the safe closed rectangle
\[
 (s,d)\in[-6/5,-1/3]\times[-1/10100,0],
 \quad c\in[-1609663/10^6,-1609662/10^6].                  \tag{6}
\]
The extra \(d=0\) boundary enlarges the exclusion domain; it does not replace the actual requirement \(d<0\).

Partition the \(s\) interval into 4096 equal closed rational cells.
The fixed native verifier first applies the previous four rational duals, in their retained order, to each whole cell and the entire \(d,c\) ranges in (6).
Their positive-part penalties use exact rational root isolation and 256-bit outward Arb integration, while the symmetric-power recurrence encloses both residual roots without choosing a square root.
A positive dual gap contradicts the adopted continuous necessary inequality on that entire box.
For every cell not excluded by those four tests, the verifier instead encloses
\[
 \frac{9(-1-y)32}{p}
       -\bigl((s+1)^2-d\bigr)\bigl(1-3s^2-d+2ys\bigr).
\]
A positive lower bound contradicts the strict necessary inequality (2).
All powers crossing zero use interval multiplication, all results must be finite, and no sampled sign substitutes for an interval comparison.

The complete fixed calculation returns
\[
 \text{first-success counts}=(3739,203,66,17,71),\qquad
 \text{unresolved cells}=0.
\]
The last 71 cells are precisely those left by the previous four-dual cover, and each has pole-gap lower bound greater than \(0.0043619\).
The counts sum to 4096, so every point of (6) is excluded by at least one valid inequality.
This contradicts the assumed positive shell and proves (3).
As the complete monic reciprocal polynomial has \(M(P)\ge1\), the first triple under the stated \(M(P)\le101/100\) hypothesis can only have \(M(P)=1\).
The proof makes no test of the small positive real-pair strip that may occur at measure one, and does not claim its exclusion.
