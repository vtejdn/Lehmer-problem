# The fraction of Mahler measure carried by each factor of a Möbius graph

<a id="statement"></a>
## Allocation at large slope

Fix nonzero algebraic numbers \(\gamma,\mu,\lambda\) with \(\mu\ne\lambda\).
Put \(R(Z)=\gamma(Z-\mu)/(Z-\lambda)\), \(E=\mathbb Q(\gamma,\mu,\lambda)\), and \(e=[E:\mathbb Q]\).
Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[U,V]\) be the primitive integer multiple with positive leading \(U^eV^e\) coefficient of
\[
           \prod_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
              \big[V(U-\tau\lambda)-\tau\gamma(U-\tau\mu)\big].
\]
For positive \(a,b\) with \(b/a\to\infty\), put \(P(Z)=F(Z^a,Z^b)\).
For every irreducible nontorsion factor \(S\) over \(\mathbb Q\), represented by its primitive integer polynomial, and every root \(z\) of \(S\),
\[
       b h(z)\longrightarrow H(R)=m(F)/e>0,\qquad
       \frac{m(S)}{m(P)}
         =(1+o(1))\,\frac{\deg S}{e(a+b)}.                              \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure, \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height, and the relative error is uniform over all such factors and common exponent divisors.
The sum of the degrees of the nontorsion factors, with multiplicity, is \((1+o(1))e(a+b)\).
A factor of vanishing positive measure therefore has degree \(o(b)\) and has at least one distinct nontorsion companion factor for all sufficiently large indices.
The [two-sided degree bounds for a fixed curve](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) apply at arbitrary primitive directions and dilations.
Formula (1) identifies the limiting constant and the share of each factor at these large slopes.

Consequently, if a critical four-term test of fixed positive density and bounded width in height units persists along any sequence of shrinking positive measures, Schinzel's rational specialization conjecture fails for one fixed \(F\) of the displayed form.
For each given width and density only a finite list of such \(F\)'s is needed.
This extends the [Salem pole implication](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#statement) to all root signatures.

<a id="height"></a>
## A common limiting height for arbitrary factors

Write \(A(Z)=\gamma(Z-\mu)\), \(B(Z)=Z-\lambda\).
Use the quantity \(H(R)\) from the [rational graph height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height), namely the mean over the \(e\) complex embeddings of the circle integrals of \(\log\max(|A^\tau|,|B^\tau|)\), plus the normalized finite-place sum of \(\log G_w\), with \(G_w=\max(1,|\lambda|_w,|\gamma|_w,|\gamma\mu|_w)\).
We first show \(h(R(X))\to H(R)\) for *every* sequence of nonzero nontorsion algebraic numbers with \(h(X)\to0\).
Northcott's theorem ensures the degrees grow and the sequence eventually avoids all poles and zeros.
The archimedean projective test \(\log\max(|A^\tau(X)|,|B^\tau(X)|)-\log^+|X|\) is continuous on the sphere.
The complete relative orbits converge to circle measure at every embedding by [Baker and Rumely](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution).
Restoring the projective terms over all places costs precisely \(h(X)\).

At a finite place \(w\) of \(E\), divide both forms by a coefficient with modulus \(G_w\).
The quotient of the maximum of their values by \(\max(1,|X|)\) is at most one and at least a constant \(c_w>0\), by invertibility of their two-by-two coefficient matrix.
It is one identically outside the finitely many places where the normalized determinant is a nonunit.
All these bounds hold over every extension place with absolute values extending \(w\).
Fix one exceptional \(w\), write \(\pi\) for its uniformizer and first take \(|X|=1\).
One of the normalized forms has nonzero reduction.
After removing its initial monomial this reduction divides \(Z^N-1\) for some fixed \(N\ge1\), since the residue field is finite.
As in the [unit calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v758.md#height), reduction and lifting imply \(|X^N-1|\le\max(|\pi|,|A(X)|/G_w,|B(X)|/G_w)\).
Together with \(c_w>0\) this bounds the loss in the normalized log maximum by a fixed multiple of \(-\log|X^N-1|\) on units.
Its weighted sum there tends to zero by the [finite-place estimate for \(L_1(X^N)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement), since all losses in that estimate are nonnegative.

When \(|X|<1\), a unit constant coefficient gives normalized maximum one.
If both constants are nonunits, some linear coefficient is a unit, so the maximum is at least \(|X|\) for \(|\pi|<|X|<1\).
For \(|X|\le|\pi|\), the lower bound \(c_w\) makes the logarithmic loss \(O_w(|\log|X||)\) as well.
For \(|X|>1\) the same estimates apply to the reversed linear forms at \(1/X\).
The weighted sum of \(|\log|X||\) over all places equals \(2h(X)\) by the product formula.
This proves that the finite corrections tend to the asserted Gauss terms, including for nonunits.

Every root \(z\) in (1) satisfies \(z^b=R^\tau(z^a)\) for some coefficient embedding, since the two linear forms are coprime.
The projective height inequality \(h(R^\tau(x))\le h(x)+O_R(1)\) gives \((b-a)h(z)\ll_R1\).
Thus \(X=z^a\) has height tending to zero along every such selection.
The preceding limit gives \(bh(z)\to H(R)\), invariant under coefficient conjugation, uniformly by the sequential criterion.

<a id="mass"></a>
## Whole measure and factor degrees

Write \(F=l\prod_\tau [VB^\tau-A^\tau]\) with \(l>0\).
Gauss norm multiplicativity at each finite place and primitivity of \(F\) give \(\log l=e\sum_{w\nmid\infty}[E_w:\mathbb Q_p]\log G_w/[E:\mathbb Q]\), by computing in a normal closure and summing over rational primes.
Archimedean Jensen integration first in \(V\) gives \(m(F)=eH(R)\).
The archimedean integrand equals \(\log|B^\tau|+\log^+|R^\tau|\) almost everywhere, and \(G_w\ge\max(1,|\lambda|_w)\).
Hence \(H(R)\ge h(\lambda)+e^{-1}\sum_\tau\int_{\mathbb T}\log^+|R^\tau|\,d\mu_{\mathbb T}\), where \(\mu_{\mathbb T}\) is angular probability measure.
This is positive when \(h(\lambda)>0\).
Otherwise \(\lambda\ne0\) is a root of unity, and the genuine pole on the circle makes the integral positive.

The shortest nonzero relation perpendicular to \((a,b)\) has sup norm \(b/\gcd(a,b)\ge b/a\) at \(b>a\).
The [Boyd–Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) therefore gives \(m(P)\to m(F)\).
Projection is eventually injective on the finite support, so \(P\) is primitive.
Its highest term and its constant term are nonzero, and \(\deg P=e(a+b)\).
Using \(m(S)=\deg S\,h(z)\) now gives (1).
Summing its uniform relative estimate over all factors with multiplicity gives the total nontorsion degree assertion, since cyclotomic factors have zero measure.
A nonzero root of a polynomial with \(t\) distinct exponents has multiplicity at most \(t-1\), by applying \((Z\,d/dZ)^j\) for \(0\le j<t\) and inverting the Vandermonde matrix of the exponents.
Here \(t\le|\operatorname{supp}F|\) is fixed, so a shrinking \(S\) and all its copies together have degree \(o(b)\).
They cannot exhaust the nontorsion degree.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## A fixed rational conjectural instance for each surviving graph

The [critical four-term classification](Yin_C_G0345_v754.md#statement) gives maps from a finite list and a nontorsion witness root with \(m(z)\to0\).
The [short-gap estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v759.md#statement) gives \(b/a\to\infty\).
Along a subsequence the coefficient graph is fixed, and (1) requires two distinct nontorsion factors of its rational section.
Moreover the [bounded denominator for every radical branch](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) applies to the primitive-direction root \(z^{\gcd(a,b)}\), since the length of its specialized parent is at most the coefficient length of \(F\).
Thus a shrinking branch also gives a shrinking primitive-direction factor.

For each geometric component of \(F(U^n,V^n)\), Eisenstein over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[U]\) applies at a simple root of \(U^n-\tau\mu\), where \(U^n-\tau\lambda\ne0\).
The coefficients are relatively prime, and neither variable divides the polynomial.
Composing any nonsingular integral monomial substitution with its signed adjugate gives diagonal powering, so every such pullback of each component is absolutely irreducible in the Laurent ring.
The components have the same finite support and leading \(UV\) coefficient one; their different triples give different coefficient vectors.
These properties keep their pullbacks pairwise nonassociate, and Galois transitivity makes the rational product irreducible, exactly as in the [pole graph argument](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#pullback).
Its Newton polygon has rank two, so it is noncyclotomic.
[Schinzel's rational conjecture in the formulation of Amoroso and Sombra](../audits/literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) would therefore give at most one nontorsion rational irreducible factor of every section outside finitely many exceptional lines.
The directions with \(b/a\to\infty,\ a>0\) eventually avoid them all, even before removing a common divisor.
This contradicts the unconditional companion factor.
The implication assumes a persistent critical test; the existence of such a test for every shrinking sequence remains open.
