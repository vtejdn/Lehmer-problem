# Every radical preserves the first measure of the large five- and seven-term factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility at every common dilation

Consider either of the integral Laurent polynomials
\[
 \begin{split}
 F_5(X,Y)&=Y+Y^{-1}-1-X-X^{-1},\\
 F_7(X,Y)&=1+X+(1+X+X^2)Y+X(1+X)Y^2 .
 \end{split}
\]
Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers and \(s,t\in\{1,-1\}\).
For \(\max(a,b)\) sufficiently large, every nontorsion irreducible factor \(S\) of \(F_i(sZ^a,tZ^b)\), normalized to be monic and omitting monomials, has the following property:
\[
    S(Z^g)\text{ is irreducible over }\mathbb Q,\qquad
          M(S(Z^g))=M(S)\quad\text{for every }g\ge1 .                     \tag{1}
\]
Thus the root of **any** irreducible nontorsion factor of **any** common dilation has exactly the measure of its primitive power.
The cutoff is uniform in the signs and primitive directions.
No irreducibility of the full primitive specialization is required.

In particular the [five-term prime polynomials](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#statement)
\[
       P_p(Z)=Z^{2p}-Z^{p+1}-Z^p-Z^{p-1}+1
\]
are irreducible at all dilations for every sufficiently large prime \(p\).
Their positive exterior real generators are primitive on their lines in their own fields.
Consequently their actual measures \(A_p=M(P_p)\) are themselves multiplicatively independent atoms of the full integer-polynomial measure monoid, after discarding finitely many primes.
They converge to the original five-term limit \(L\) strictly between one and the plastic number.
This determines the saturation index left in the [atom accumulation theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v158.md#degree).
For the smaller [Boyd–Mossinghoff seven-term limit](Yin_C_G0345_v312.md#statement), a reduction of the first measure by an individual factor must already occur before common dilation, including in the case of reducible primitive specializations.
Neither family gives first measures tending to one.

<a id="defect"></a>
## A necessary common factor in characteristic \(\ell\)

For a prime \(\ell\) and an integral Laurent polynomial in any number of variables define
\[
       \delta_\ell F(\mathbf X)=
          \frac{F(\mathbf X)^\ell-F(\mathbf X^\ell)}{\ell}.
\]
This is integral by reduction of the two terms modulo \(\ell\).
Suppose \(\beta\) is an algebraic unit with monic minimum \(S\) and is an \(\ell\)-th power in \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), say \(\beta=\rho^\ell\).
Then \(\rho\) is an algebraic unit generating \(K\), with monic minimum \(Q\) of the same degree.
The distinct roots of \(S\) are the \(\ell\)-th powers of the roots of \(Q\).
Their elementary symmetric functions give \(\bar Q=\bar S\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) by Frobenius.
For a primitive \(\ell\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\),
\[
        \pm S(X^\ell)=Q(X)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}Q(\zeta^jX).
\]
The product over \(j\ne0\) is integral and rational by Galois invariance; reducing at \(1-\zeta\) shows it is congruent to \(Q(X)^{\ell-1}\) modulo \(\ell\) as an integer polynomial.
Evaluating at \(\beta\), both factors on the right therefore belong to \(\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
It follows that \(\delta_\ell S(\beta)=-S(\beta^\ell)/\ell\in\ell\mathbb Z[\beta]\), equivalently \(\bar S\mid\overline{\delta_\ell S}\).
This argument permits repeated roots of \(\bar S\) and an index at \(\ell\) in the power order.

For a multiple \(G=SH\), the exact identity
\[
       \delta_\ell G=S^\ell\delta_\ell H+H(X^\ell)\delta_\ell S
       \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]
\]
gives the same divisibility by \(\bar S\).
Write \(F=F_i(sX,tY)\); then the defect of its specialization is the specialization of its defect.
The map from the two-variable Laurent ring to \(\mathbb F_\ell[Z^{\pm1}]\) via \((Z^a,Z^b)\) is surjective by coprimality.
Hence the quotient by \((\bar F,\overline{\delta_\ell F})\) maps onto \(\mathbb F_\ell[Z^{\pm1}]/(\bar S)\), of dimension \(\deg S\).
If these two plane equations have no common component in the torus, the dimension of their quotient is at most \(d_F^2\ell\) by plane Bézout with multiplicities, where \(d_F\) is the total degree after clearing monomials.
Indeed clearing a monomial from \(F\) multiplies its defect by the \(\ell\)-th power of that monomial, so the two polynomial degrees can be taken to be \(d_F,\ell d_F\); boundary monomial factors may be dropped in the torus.
Here \(d_F\le4\) in both families.
Thus in-field \(\ell\)-th powering would force \(\deg S\le16\ell\).

<a id="boundary"></a>
## A first-order branch at the boundary excludes a common component

Use integral monomial torus coordinates \(z,w\) and a monomial factor so that the polynomial in a boundary chart has the expansion
\[
                         F=A(z)+B(z)w+O(w^2).
\]
Integral monomial changes commute with \(\delta_\ell\), and monomial factors have the harmless effect described above.
Suppose \(A\) has a nonzero simple root \(\xi\) modulo \(\ell\) with \(B(\xi)\ne0\).
There is a unique formal branch \(z=\xi+c w+O(w^2)\) at this point over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), with \(c=-B(\xi)/A'(\xi)\ne0\), by successive solution at the invertible derivative.
The constant and linear terms in \(w\) of the integral defect are \(\delta_\ell A\) and \(A^{\ell-1}B\).
On the branch, the linear term of the reduced defect is
\[
              c\,(\delta_\ell A)'(\xi)
                 =-c\,\xi^{\ell-1}A'(\xi^\ell)\ne0 ,
\]
since \(A'(\xi^\ell)=A'(\xi)^\ell\) in the residue field.
This follows by differentiating the integral formula for \(\delta_\ell A\) before reduction.
Therefore the component carrying this branch cannot divide the defect, also in characteristic two.

For \(F_5\) take \(z=X/Y,\ w=1/Y\), and multiply by \(w\).
The two lowest rows are \(A=t-sz\) and \(B=-1\), so the branch test applies at every prime.
The reduction of \(F_5\) is absolutely irreducible in the torus at every prime, as seen after removing the signs by substitution.
For odd \(\ell\), as a quadratic in \(Y\) its discriminant is
\[
                (X^2-X+1)(X^2+3X+1)/X^2 .
\]
The factors are coprime in odd characteristic, and their discriminants \(-3,5\) cannot both vanish there.
The discriminant is therefore nonsquare in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell(X)\).
In characteristic two put \(C=1+X+X^{-1}\).
Solvability of \(Y^2+CY+1=0\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2(X)\) would represent \(1/C^2\), hence also \(1/C\), as \(z^2+z\).
But \(1/C=X/(X^2+X+1)\) has simple poles, whereas every pole of a rational \(z^2+z\) has even order.
No vertical factor is possible from the unit coefficient of \(Y^2\) in the Laurent ring.

For \(F_7\) the chart at \(Y=0\) has \(A=1+sX,\ B=t(1+sX+X^2)\); at \(Y=\infty\) put \(w=1/Y\) and multiply by \(w^2\), giving \(A=sX(1+sX)\) and the same \(B\).
Both simple boundary roots \(X=-s\) have \(B\ne0\) modulo every prime.
For odd \(\ell\), the discriminant of the unsigned quadratic in \(Y\) is \(X^4-2X^3-5X^2-2X+1\).
In a possible monic quadratic square the cubic and linear coefficients force the middle coefficient to be \(-1\) and the constant one.
The quadratic coefficient would then be \(3\), different from \(-5\) in every odd characteristic.
There is no vertical factor since \(1+X\) and \(1+X+X^2\) are coprime.
In characteristic two the factorization in the torus is exactly
\[
             ((1+X)Y+1)(XY+X+1).
\]
These are distinct irreducible linear equations in \(Y\) with coprime coefficient pairs.
The branch at \(Y=0,\ X=1\) lies on the second, and the branch at \(1/Y=0,\ X=1\) lies on the first, where its reversed equation is \(1+X+w\).
Thus both components are excluded separately by the first-order test.
This proves the Bézout hypothesis at all primes for both signed families.

<a id="dilations"></a>
## One cutoff for every radical and the exact accumulation value

Both polygons have only binomial edges with roots of unity, also after the sign substitutions.
Their nontorsion unit factors have degree at least \(c\max(a,b)\) for an absolute constant \(c>0\) common to these finitely many shapes by the [edge-polynomial degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement).
For large positive coprime directions the normalized parent has both endpoint coefficients of absolute value one: for \(F_5\) the larger exponent is unique, and \(F_7\) has unique extreme vertices in a strictly positive direction.
Thus every nontorsion factor is monic up to sign with unit constant.
The parent has coefficient length at most seven.
By the [bounded radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement), if \(\beta\) has an \(\ell\)-th root in its own field then \(\ell\mid R_7\).
The same applies to \(-\beta\), which is a root of another specialization obtained by changing the two signs by \((-1)^a,(-1)^b\).
The bound \(\deg S\le16\ell\) excludes every prime divisor of this one fixed integer as soon as \(c\max(a,b)>16R_7\).
In particular neither \(\beta\) nor \(-\beta\) is an in-field square.
The possibility \(\beta\in-4K^4\) would make \(-\beta\) a square there.
The [full binomial criterion and composition theorem](../audits/literature/JP18/Yin_U001.md#jp18-u001) now give (1) simultaneously for all \(g\).
The equality of measures also follows by taking all \(g\) roots over each original conjugate.

For \(P_p\) the original minimum is irreducible and has a positive real exterior root \(r_p\).
The only torsion in \(\mathbb Q(r_p)\subset\mathbb R\) is a rational sign, so the positive primitive line unit of v152 has exponent exactly one by the in-field power exclusion.
At any fixed ceiling between \(L\) and the plastic number the [bounded-ceiling generator theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v158.md#independence) makes the measures \(M(r_p)\) themselves distinct independent atoms for all sufficiently large \(p\), using the [nonzero exterior projection](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#orbit) and the mutually different compressed degrees.
Consequently these atoms accumulate at \(L\), and no positive radical of any of the five-term roots can lower this limit by a fixed or varying denominator.
