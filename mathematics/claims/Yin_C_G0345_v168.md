# A uniform gap for every positive-measure factor of a fixed two-variable specialization

<a id="statement"></a>
## Individual factors

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be fixed and nonzero.
There is a constant \(c_F>0\) such that for every integer \(n\ge1\) with \(F(X,X^n)\ne0\), every integral Laurent polynomial divisor \(A\) of \(F(X,X^n)\) satisfies
\[
                         m(A)=0\quad\text{or}\quad m(A)\ge c_F .
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
The end coefficients of \(F\), when viewed as a polynomial in \(Y\) after multiplication by a monomial, may vanish on the unit circle.
This removes the end-coefficient hypothesis in the [earlier individual-factor gap](Yin_C_G0345_v167.md#statement).
The constant may depend on the exponents and coefficients of \(F\).

For a fixed \(F\) of positive \(Y\)-width there exist \(\varepsilon_F,c'_F,C_F>0\) with the following more precise implication.
If a monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit polynomial \(A\) divides \(F(X,X^n)\) and \(m(A)<\varepsilon_F\), write \(d=\deg A\) and let \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\) be its conjugate roots.
Then
\[
            \frac1d\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigl(n\log|\alpha_i|\bigr)^2\le C_F,
            \qquad d\ge c'_F n.                                      \tag{1}
\]

<a id="fiber"></a>
## Root bounds near the end coefficients

Monomials are units in the Laurent ring and have measure zero.
After multiplication by one such unit, write \(F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^R B_j(X)Y^j\) with \(B_j\in\mathbb Z[X]\) and \(B_0B_R\ne0\).
For \(R=0\) the positive-measure irreducible factors come from one fixed polynomial.
Assume \(R\ge1\) and let \(w\) be the maximum degree of the nonzero \(B_j\).
An irreducible factor other than a rational prime can be taken primitive with nonzero constant coefficient after removing any monomial.
When its leading or constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, the root formula for it or its reversal gives \(m(A)\ge\log2\).
It suffices to consider a monic noncyclotomic unit \(A\), since rational primes also have measure at least \(\log 2\).
Put \(m=m(A)\) and \(u_i=\log|\alpha_i|\).
The identity \(\sum_i u_i=0\) gives \(|u_i|\le m\) for every \(i\).

Consider both \(F\) and \(F^\vee(X,Y)=X^wY^RF(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\).
Their leading coefficients in \(Y\) are \(B_R(X)\) and \(X^w B_0(X^{-1})\), respectively.
Choose \(0<\varepsilon_F<\min(1,\log 2)\) so that these two nonzero leading coefficients have no zero in \(1<|z|\le e^{\varepsilon_F}\).
For \(m<\varepsilon_F\) and \(u_i>0\) take \(z=\alpha_i\), \(G=F\), and for \(u_i<0\) take \(z=\alpha_i^{-1}\), \(G=F^\vee\).
In both cases \(1<|z|=e^{|u_i|}\le e^{\varepsilon_F}\) and \(G(z,z^n)=0\).
Write \(D_G\) for the leading coefficient of \(G\) and \(Z_G\) for its set of distinct circle zeros.
The lower coefficients of \(G\) are bounded on the closed annulus, so division of the fiber equation by \(|z|^{n(R-1)}\) gives
\[
                          |D_G(z)|\,e^{n|u_i|}\le C(F).                \tag{2}
\]
Every factor of \(D_G\) with a zero off the unit circle is bounded away from zero on this annulus.
If \(b_G\) counts its circle zeros with multiplicity and \(Z_G\ne\varnothing\), this gives
\[
                      |D_G(z)|\ge c(F)\operatorname{dist}(z,Z_G)^{b_G}.
\]
If \(Z_G\) is empty use \(|D_G(z)|\ge c(F)\).

Write \(y_i=n|u_i|\).
Whenever \(y_i\ge1\), every circle zero is at distance at least \(|z|-1\ge y_i/n\ge 1/n\).
It follows from (2), including the case \(y_i<1\), that
\[
                              y_i\le C_1(F)\log(2n).                  \tag{3}
\]
Let \(\Theta\) consist of the arguments of the circle zeros of \(B_R\) and the negatives of the arguments of the circle zeros of \(X^wB_0(X^{-1})\), taken modulo \(2\pi\).
The sign accounts for using \(z=\alpha_i^{-1}\) on interior roots.
For \(|z|\ge1\), \(|\zeta|=1\) one has \(|z-\zeta|\ge|z/|z|-\zeta|\), and the latter is at least \(2/\pi\) times the shorter angular distance.
With \(b=\max(1,b_F,b_{F^\vee})\), equation (2) therefore gives constants \(s_0,K>0\), depending only on \(F\), such that for every \(s\ge s_0\)
\[
       y_i>s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \arg\alpha_i\ \text{lies within }K e^{-s/b}
                         \text{ of some point of }\Theta.            \tag{4}
\]
Increase \(s_0\) to handle the constant bound when \(Z_G=\varnothing\) and to ensure \(K e^{-s_0/b}\le 1/4\).
Roots on the unit circle have \(y_i=0\).

<a id="angular"></a>
## Averaging over small arcs

The [discriminant estimate for a triangular angular weight](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), equation (4) there, applies to every monic irreducible unit and every center \(\theta\).
Put \(e_d=(\log(2d)/d)^{1/2}\).
For \(m\le1\) and \(0<\delta\le1/4\) it implies
\[
        \frac1d\#\{i:\operatorname{dist}(\arg\alpha_i,\theta)\le\delta\}
                            \ll \delta+e_d,                           \tag{5}
\]
where the distance is on the circle and the constant is absolute.
Indeed, when \(e_d\le1/2\), use the triangular weight of half-width \(h=2\max(\delta,e_d)\le1\).
It is at least \(1/2\) throughout the arc, has mean \(h/(2\pi)\), and its error in that estimate is \(O(e_d+(1+\log(2d))/(dh))=O(e_d)\).
When \(e_d>1/2\) the trivial bound suffices.

The universal [all-root square bound in Part A](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) applies to a root of \(A\) with its actual degree \(d\).
All finite places contribute zero because the root is a unit, and both embeddings of each nonreal pair occur in the archimedean sum.
The resulting inequality is
\[
                              d\sum_{i=1}^d u_i^2\ge 1/10.            \tag{6}
\]
The selected Part A incorporates degree descent for powers and includes nonreciprocal units.
Combining (3) with (6) first gives \(d\gg_F n/\log(2n)\).
In particular \(e_d\log^2(2n)\ll_F1\), because \(d\le\deg F(X,X^n)\le w+Rn\) after the chosen monomial normalization.

Apply (5) to the finitely many arcs in (4).
For \(s\ge s_0\) the proportion of roots with \(y_i>s\) is \(O_F(e^{-s/b}+e_d)\).
For \(s>C_1(F)\log(2n)\) it is zero by (3).
Integrating the proportion against \(2s\,ds\) gives
\[
 \frac1d\sum_i y_i^2
 \ \ll_F\ 1+\int_{s_0}^\infty s e^{-s/b}\,ds+e_d\log^2(2n)
 \ \ll_F\ 1 .
\]
Combining this estimate with (6) gives \(1/10\le d\sum_i u_i^2\ll_F d^2/n^2\), proving (1).

<a id="gap"></a>
## From degree to Mahler measure

Let \(k\) count the monomials of \(F\).
After specialization and collection, the resulting nonzero integral polynomial has at most \(k\) terms and degree at most \(w+Rn\).
If it has a positive-measure monic irreducible unit divisor \(A\), it has at least two terms.
The [divisor bound of Dobrowolski in Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) gives
\[
                        M(A)\ge1+\frac{0.31d}{k!(w+Rn)} .
\]
For \(m(A)<\varepsilon_F\), (1) makes the increment bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(F\).
Together with the complementary \(m(A)\ge\varepsilon_F\) and nonunit cases, this proves the assertion for irreducibles.
Any integral Laurent divisor factors into such irreducibles and a Laurent unit of measure zero.
Logarithmic measures add and are nonnegative for each integral factor, which proves the stated divisor result.
The source use is the published divisor estimate at its retained statement and applicability scope, together with the two project estimates at their selected scopes.
