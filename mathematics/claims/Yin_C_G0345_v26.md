# The two scales required for sparse positive-constant Pisot traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## The first fractional coefficient and a later nonvanishing coefficient

Consider a sequence of Pisot numbers \(\theta_i\) whose minimal polynomials have positive constant \(q_i\ge2\) and \(\theta_i-q_i\to0\).
Suppose their [trace lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) have bounded term count, and write \(F_i=\Pi_i/j_i\) for the normalized lifts and \(h_i\) for their trace degrees.
The branch indices \(j_i\) may vary and both degree parities are allowed.
Let \(d_i\) be the first index where \(F_i\) has a nonintegral coefficient.
Then \(d_i=o(h_i)\), the reduced denominator at \(d_i\) tends to infinity, and for each fixed \(A\ge1\)
\[
       \max_{0\le t\le A d_i}
          \operatorname{dist}([z^t]F_i,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Every subsequence admits a further subsequence and lower exponents \(e_i\le h_i\) with \(e_i/d_i\to\infty\) and \(\liminf_i |[z^{e_i}]F_i|>0\) there.
For each fixed term bound \(K\) and \(A\ge1\) there are \(\varepsilon(K,A),c(K,A)>0\) such that if \(d\) exists and every normalized coefficient with \(A d<t\le h\) has modulus at most \(\varepsilon(K,A)\), then \(\theta-q\ge c(K,A)\).
These conclusions do not require bounded reduced exponents or a fixed coefficient limit.

<a id="first-scale"></a>
## Why all coefficients on the first scale approach integers

The [first index theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) gives \(d_i=o(h_i)\), since sparse lifts have bounded normalized circle norm.
If a coefficient at \(O(d_i)\) remained a positive distance from the integers, freeze the normalized coefficient tuple and stabilize all relations of the finite exponent set.
The [logarithmic truncation](Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#moving) gives Mahler measure one for the full limiting polynomial, and each [initial face](Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#faces) from the scale \(d_i\) onward has measure one and contains the selected nonintegral coefficient.
At each pair of consecutive scales the ordered germ \(SJ\) for that face \(S\) has integer coefficients.
A surviving graph component projects to a nonnegative density \(f\) dominated by the base probability, with \(2Sf\) integral Laurent.
The [torus density lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#lattice) excludes such a component: \(2f\) is Laurent by slice division, and an exposed coefficient forces either a constant or the full density \(2\pm(X^b+X^{-b})\).
Both alternatives would imply \(S\) integral.
The [vanishing of the other continuous rows](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#scales) gives a product of binomials killing an intermediate interval of the actual integer denominator \(B_i^\sharp\).
Starting with the integral moments below \(d_i\), the [real coefficient induction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#propagation) bounds the term count of a multiple of \(B_i^\sharp\) with coefficients from a fixed finite real set and constant one.
Its measure approaches one strictly from above, contradicting [endpoint isolation for a fixed coefficient tuple](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations).
This proves convergence to integers on every fixed multiple of the first fractional index.
At \(d_i\) a noninteger rational of reduced denominator \(D\) is at distance at least \(1/D\) from every integer, so its denominator diverges.

<a id="later-scale"></a>
## A normalized coefficient bounded away from zero beyond the first scale

On each subsequence pass further to limits of all coefficient tuples and of all lower exponents divided by \(d_i\).
The normalized coefficients whose indices are \(O(d_i)\) then have integral limits.
The [rounded first face](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v112.md#round) evaluated at those same indices is an integer polynomial \(A_i\) with constant one, degree \(k_i=O(d_i)\), bounded term count and measure tending to one by Lawton.
Thus \(M(A_i)=1\) eventually by the fixed-support gap and \(A_i\) is reciprocal up to sign, including \(A_i=1\).
If every later coefficient tended to zero, the full normalized \(F_i\) would approach \((1-\sigma_i z^{p_i})A_i\) in coefficient absolute sum, with \(\sigma_i=\operatorname{lc}(A_i)=\pm1,\ p_i=2h_i+2-k_i\).
The [full-period fold](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v112.md#fold) then represents the integer remainder of \(B_i^\sharp\) modulo \(z^{p_i}-\sigma_i\) by one sequence of bounded-variation Fourier transforms on the cyclic group, up to a uniformly vanishing error.
Rounding and clearing the resulting progression denominators gives a bounded-term cyclotomic multiple of the remainder.
The lower truncation of \(B_i^\sharp\) through \(k_i\) has a bounded-term binomial multiple by the integral moments below \(d_i\), including the extra upper edge of the truncated product.
Polynomial division restores a bounded-term integer multiple of \(B_i^\sharp\) by cyclotomic factors, contradicting its measure tending to one strictly from above.
Some later coefficient limit is therefore nonzero on this extraction.

If the last uniform assertion in the statement failed for some \(K,A\), one could take a vanishing-excess sequence with every coefficient beyond \(A d_i\) in the lower half of modulus at most \(1/i\).
On its further extracted subsequence every coefficient at \(e_i/d_i\to\infty\) would then tend to zero, contradicting the preceding conclusion.
In particular the largest lower exponent divided by \(d_i\) tends to infinity along the original sequence.
