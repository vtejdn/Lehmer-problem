# Quadratic periods distinguish partial conductors of a reciprocal five-term factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reciprocal selections at mixed conductors

Let \(n=5^A7^B>1\) with \(A,B\ge0\), let \(0<k<n\) be odd and prime to \(n\), let \(l=n-k\), and put
\[
             P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1 .
\]
Its squarefree reduction modulo two has exactly the primitive roots of orders \(3t\) for \(t\mid n\).
At each mixed level \(t=5^a7^b,\ a,b\ge1\), the four Frobenius orbits form two pairs under inversion.
Each pair consists of the exponent classes of one sign of the quadratic character
\[
            \chi(j)=\left(\frac j3\right)\left(\frac j5\right)
                                      \left(\frac j7\right).
\]
Their cyclotomic reference polynomials have coefficient field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{105})\).
At each pure prime power \(t>1\), a reciprocal selection takes the whole conductor or none of it.

Consider a monic rational divisor whose reduction is reciprocal and omits order three.
Count one for each selected pure prime power and one for each mixed conductor at which just one pair is selected.
This total is even at every offset.
Moreover the divisor has the form \(X^{D}T(X+X^{-1})\) with \(T(-2)=T(-1)=T(1)=T(2)=1\).
Thus a single mixed pair by itself can never lift to a rational divisor.

The following selections have degrees proportional to \(n\) and satisfy the parity condition.
Let \(n=5^a7^b,\ a,b\ge2,\ h=n/35\) and \(k=h+2\).
Fix a primitive root \(\rho\) of order 105 in characteristic two for which \(\sum_{\chi(j)=1}\rho^j=0\), with exponents taken modulo 105.
Label a primitive root \(\eta\) of order \(3n\) by the sign \(\chi(j)\) for \(\eta^h=\rho^j\).
This convention is independent of the choice of \(\rho\) satisfying the normalization.
Take the top pair of one sign together with a pure conductor \(3t\).

* For \(t=5\), the plus selection fails the two required trace congruences at \(\pm1\) modulo four, and the minus selection passes both.
  If \(h\equiv1\bmod3\), both fail at \(\pm2\).
  If \(h\equiv2\bmod3\), the minus selection passes at all four points.
* For \(t=25\) and \(a\ge3\), the minus selection fails at \(\pm1\), and the plus selection passes at all four points for either residue of \(h\).

The degrees for \(t=5,25\) are \(24h+8,24h+40\), respectively.
Each failed congruence excludes the unique reciprocal dyadic lift from \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
Passing these congruences leaves rationality undecided.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## The two quadratic halves over the integers

At a mixed level, the orders of two modulo \(3,5^a,7^b\) are \(2,4\cdot5^{a-1},3\cdot7^{b-1}\), respectively.
For the last two formulas the initial orders are four and three, and \(v_5(2^4-1)=v_7(2^3-1)=1\).
The binomial expansion raises the valuation by exactly one at each successive powering by the prime.
Hence \(\langle2\rangle\) has index four in the units modulo \(3t\).
Inversion is outside this subgroup because two is a square modulo seven and minus one is not.
Both two and minus one belong to \(\ker\chi\), which therefore equals \(\langle2,-1\rangle\).
For \(t=5^a\), the only possible exponent of two giving minus one modulo \(5^a\) is even modulo its order, hence cannot give minus one modulo three.
For \(t=7^b\), use the same square test at seven.
The orders above give \(\langle2,-1\rangle\) equal to the whole unit group in both pure cases.

At conductor 105 let \(\zeta\) be primitive, \(G=\sum_{j\bmod105}\chi(j)\zeta^j\), and define
\[
        V_\sigma(Y)=
        \prod_{\substack{1\le j\le52\\ \chi(j)=\sigma}}
                      (Y-\zeta^j-\zeta^{-j})\qquad(\sigma=\pm1).
\]
The character is extended by zero on nonunits.
The sum \(G\) is real since \(\chi(-1)=1\), and \(G^2=105\).
Indeed its Fourier coefficient at \(u\) is \(\chi(u)G\) if \(u\) is a unit and is zero otherwise, by summing over the omitted prime component using the Chinese remainder theorem.
Parseval on the cyclic group gives \(\varphi(105)|G|^2=105\varphi(105)\).
The two polynomials are interchanged by the automorphisms with negative character and have disjoint root sets, giving the stated fixed field.

The exact reference values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
        & 2 & -2 & 1 & -1\\ \hline
 V_\sigma & 3361-328\sigma G & 1 & 1 & -41-4\sigma G .
\end{array}                                                            \tag{1}
\]
Here is a verification by integer polynomial arithmetic.
Write \(2V_\sigma=U+\sigma G W\).
The coefficient lists of \(U,W\), from degree twelve down to zero, are
\[
\begin{split}
 U &: (2,1,3,28,-75,-143,251,160,-323,28,100,-32,2),\\
 W &: (0,-1,-1,6,1,-13,13,8,-27,8,10,-4,0).
\end{split}
\]
To reproduce them use \(e_0=1,\ e_j=-j^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^j e_{j-i}p_i\) for the coefficients of the monic polynomial, with
\[
       p_i=\frac14\sum_{v=0}^i {i\choose v}
         \big[c_{105}(i-2v)+\sigma\chi(i-2v)G\big],\qquad
       c_{105}(w)=\sum_{d\mid(105,w)}d\mu(105/d).
\]
These are the power sums by expansion of the inverse-paired traces and the Fourier formula for \(\chi\).
Direct substitution gives (1).
For \(t=5^a7^b\) put \(H=5^{a-1}7^{b-1}\).
Every \(H\)-th root of a primitive 105-th root has exact order \(105H\), by the prime exponents at five and seven.
Writing \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\), the reference trace polynomial of either half is \(V_\sigma(C_H(Y))\).
The four values (1) are preserved because \(H\) is prime to six.

<a id="parity"></a>
## The four values required of a rational divisor

Work at a dyadic embedding of the cyclotomic field.
Since both halves are stable under squaring, their monic reference polynomials belong to \(\mathbb Z_2[Y]\).
For a prescribed reciprocal selection omitting order three, take \(V\) the product of its reference trace polynomials, with one or both factors from each chosen mixed level.
Each mixed half has trace degree \(12H\) and \(V_\sigma(2)\equiv V_\sigma(-2)\equiv1\bmod8\) by (1).
Each pure conductor has even trace degree and takes value one at \(\pm2\) by the [full-conductor endpoint evaluation](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces).
Thus \(D=\deg V\) is even, \(V(2)\equiv V(-2)\bmod8\) and \(V'(0)\equiv0\bmod2\).
The unique dyadic lift is reciprocal by squarefreeness and \(P_l(1)=-1\), as in the [reciprocal lifting argument](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts).
Its trace polynomial \(T_l\) has the same derivative parity.
For a rational lift, the [endpoint sign and conductor-three evaluations](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces) now apply without requiring full mixed conductors.
Indeed \(T_l(2)\equiv T_l(-2)\bmod8\), \(T_l(2)\mid-1\), and \(T_l(-2)\) is a positive divisor of five.
This forces both values to be one, and omission of order three gives \(T_l(\pm1)=\pm1\) with both signs forced positive modulo three.

At either trace argument \(v=-1,1\), the local value modulo four is
\[
        T_l(v)\equiv V(v)(1+2\epsilon_l)\pmod4,\qquad
        \epsilon_l=\sum_{\eta}\eta^l\,g(\eta),\quad
        g(z)=\frac{1+z^2}{1+z+z^2},                                    \tag{2}
\]
with \(\eta\) running over the selected binary roots.
The [simple-root calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#digit) works for each inverse pair separately relative to its cyclotomic trace.
The denominator \(v-(\eta+\eta^{-1})\) has the same nonzero reduction at both arguments.
At the even arguments \(v=\pm2\), the same calculation gives the correction \(\sum_\eta\eta^l\), since the trace factor in the root displacement cancels its denominator.
In all these formulas the sums belong to \(\mathbb F_2\) by Frobenius stability.

For each half (1) gives \(V_\sigma(1)=1,\ V_\sigma(-1)\equiv-1\bmod4\).
Each complete pure five- or seven-power level also gives \(V(1)=1,\ V(-1)\equiv-1\bmod4\) by the [prime-power evaluations](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#reference).
The common correction in (2) cancels from the ratio at the two odd arguments.
This proves the stated parity rule, since a full mixed conductor contributes its two halves.

<a id="selection"></a>
## The two choices at offset \(n/35+2\)

Define \(\Pi_\sigma(w)=\sum_{\chi(j)=\sigma}\rho^{jw}\) over the units modulo 105.
Both periods at one are binary because \(\chi(2)=1\), and their sum is the reduction of \(c_{105}(1)=-1\).
Changing \(\rho\) by an exponent of negative character interchanges them, proving existence and independence of the normalization in the statement.
For any integer \(w\) one has
\[
\Pi_\sigma(w)=
\begin{cases}
 {\bf1}_{\sigma\chi(w)=-1}, & (105,w)=1,\\
 1, & (105,w)\in\{3,7\},\\
 0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}                                                             \tag{3}
\]
For the nonunit case, project the exponents onto the components at primes not dividing \(w\).
Each choice there has \(\tfrac12\prod_{p\mid(105,w)}(p-1)\) lifts of the desired sign.
This multiplicity is odd precisely when the only omitted prime is three or seven.
The projected sum over all units is then one modulo two, by the Ramanujan sum at a unit of a squarefree conductor.

For the top half in (2) group the roots over \(y=\eta^h=\rho^j\).
Here \(\eta^l=y^{35}\eta^{-k}\), and in characteristic two
\[
    g(\eta)=
    \frac{(1+\eta+\eta^2+\eta^3)\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\eta^{3i}}{1+y^3}.
\]
The sum of \(\eta^w\) over these \(h\) roots is zero unless \(h\mid w\), when it is \(y^{w/h}\), since \(h\) is odd.
With \(k=h+2\), the only possible integers \(q=(3i+j'-k)/h\), for \(0\le j'\le3\), are \(-1,0,1\).
At \(q=-1\) just \(j'=2\) occurs.
For \(q=0,1\), the indices \(j'\equiv k+qh\bmod3\) give one term unless the residue is zero, when the two terms cancel.
Consequently the contribution at \(y\) equals \(y^{34}(1+y^e)/(1+y^3)\), with \(e=1\) for \(h\equiv2\bmod3\) and \(e=2\) for \(h\equiv1\bmod3\).
Using \((1+y^3)^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{16}y^{3(2i+1)}\) on these roots, the two sums of (3) at \(37+6i\) and \(37+6i+e\) give the following integer counts before reduction.

| \(e\) | Plus counts | Minus counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(8,11\) | \(9,5\) |
| 2 | \(8,12\) | \(9,12\) |

Thus the top corrections in the order (plus, minus) are \((1,0)\) for \(h\equiv2\), and \((0,1)\) for \(h\equiv1\bmod3\).
At the even arguments they vanish for both halves, since \(h\nmid l=34h-2\).

For the pure conductor use the [Ramanujan formula](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#digit).
For \(j'=0,1,2,3\) count \(0\le i<(t-1)/2\) for which \(c_{3t}(l+3+6i+j')\) is odd.
For \(3t=3\cdot5^u\) this requires the argument to be prime to three and have exact five-adic valuation \(u-1\).
The row for \(t=25\) uses \(a\ge3\) so that \(25\mid h\).

| \(t\) | \(h\bmod3\) | \(l\bmod 3t\) | Four counts | Odd-argument correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 2 | 3 | \(0,2,2,0\) | 0 |
| 5 | 1 | 8 | \(2,0,2,1\) | 1 |
| 25 | 2 | 48 | \(0,1,2,0\) | 1 |
| 25 | 1 | 23 | \(1,0,2,3\) | 0 |

At the even arguments the pure correction is \(c_{3t}(l)\bmod2\).
It occurs only for \(t=5,\ h\equiv1\bmod3\), since \(5\nmid l\) and \(l\equiv h+1\bmod3\).
The combined reference value is one modulo four at all four arguments, by (1) and the pure reference values.
Adding the corrections proves each stated exclusion and each surviving residue.
For example \(n=1225,\ h=35,\ k=37,\ t=5\) gives two binary selections of degree 848, with only the minus choice passing all four congruences.
These results concern partial selections of degree asymptotic to \(24n/35\), whereas the [all-offset exclusion for the two-prime antichain](Yin_C_G0345_v247.md#statement) treats the specified product of whole conductors of smaller degree.
