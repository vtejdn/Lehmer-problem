# Stability of every cyclotomic completion of a Salem prefix near minimum degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Multiplicities at every primitive order

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(e^a\), with \(0<a\le A\) and degree \(d\to\infty\).
Fix \(\delta>0,\ B>1/2+\delta\), and take integers \(k\) with \((1/2+\delta)\log d\le ak\le B\log d\).
Set \(H=\lfloor k^{7/8}\rfloor,\ J=\{k-H+1,\ldots,k\},\ R=k^{1/40}\), and \(V=e^{ak}/(1-e^{-a})\).
Let \(Q=\prod_n\Phi_n^{b_n}\) be any monic cyclotomic polynomial product of degree \(D\) with the same first \(k\) coefficients below the leading one as \(P\), and suppose \(D/V\to1\).
This is precisely asymptotic minimum degree by [the Salem completion theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement).
Call \(n\) assigned to \(j\) when \(n\mid j,\ j\in J,\ j/n<R\).
An order is assigned to at most one index, since such an \(n\) exceeds \(H\) eventually.
For \(j\in J,\ u\mid j\), let \(m_{j,u}=\varphi(u)b_u\) when \(u\) is assigned to \(j\), and \(m_{j,u}=0\) otherwise.
Then
\[
 \sum_{n\ {\rm unassigned}}\varphi(n)b_n
 +\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{u\mid j}
    \left|m_{j,u}-\frac{e^{aj}}{j}\varphi(u)\right|=o(D).                    \tag{1}
\]
All the absolute values can be taken simultaneously, including at the small divisors shared by many \(j\)'s.
For every fixed \(\eta>0\), the degree at assigned orders with \(j/n\ge k^\eta\) is \(o(D)\).

In particular the explicit monic polynomial
\[
              C_{a,k}(X)=\prod_{j\in J}(X^j-1)^{\lfloor e^{aj}/j\rfloor}      \tag{2}
\]
satisfies \(\deg\gcd(Q,C_{a,k})=(1-o(1))D\), counting multiplicities.
Any two such near-minimum comparators also have a common divisor of degree \((1-o(1))D\).
Along a hypothetical sequence \(a\to0\), this applies to every degree-\(d\) comparator at the longest feasible prefix \(k=K(P)\).
For every fixed integer \(q\ge1\), as \(a\to0\) the exact orders of the roots of any such near-minimum comparator satisfy
\[
                  \frac1D\sum_{q\mid n}\varphi(n)b_n
                     \longrightarrow \frac1q\prod_{p\mid q}\frac p{p+1}.       \tag{3}
\]
Here (3) holds throughout the specified range of \(k\), and the distinct-prime divisibility events have independent limiting probabilities.
These multiplicities are forced in the completions of the original coefficients.
The original Salem polynomial itself is irreducible and has no cyclotomic factor.

<a id="weighted"></a>
## Testing every divisor of a terminal index at once

Choose real \(g_j(u)\) arbitrarily in \([-1,1]\) for \(j\in J,\ u\mid j\).
For \(l\mid j\) set \(G_j(l)=\sum_{u\mid l}\varphi(u)g_j(u)\), so \(|G_j(l)|\le l\).
Let \(f(t)=\max(0,\min(1,1-\log t/\log R))\).
Use the Euler weight \(w_l=f(j/l)G_j(l)\) when \(l\) is assigned to \(j\) and set \(w_l=0\) otherwise.
Always \(|w_l|\le l\), and \(w_j=G_j(j)\) for \(j\in J\).
For a primitive order define
\[
                         T_n(g)=\sum_{l\mid n}\mu(n/l)w_l .
\]
For \(n\le kR\) all assigned divisors \(l\mid n\) use the same \(j\), even when a weight is zero.
Indeed the denominator of \(n/j=(n/l)/(j/l)\) in lowest terms is at most \(R\), and its possible variation across \(J\) is \(O(RH/k)=o(R^{-2})\).
When there is at least one assigned divisor, one therefore has
\[
       T_n(g)= f(j/n)\sum_{u\mid j} g_j(u)B_{n,j}(u)+O(\omega_k\varphi(n)),
\quad B_{n,j}(u)=\varphi(u)\!\!\sum_{\substack{u\mid l\\l\mid(n,j)}}\!\!\mu(n/l),       \tag{4}
\]
where \(\omega_k=o(1)\) is independent of \(n,j,g\).
In fact sum first over \(l\mid(n,j)\), since the extra terms with \(j/l\ge R\) have \(f(j/l)=0\), and replace \(f(j/l)\) by \(f(j/n)\).
The error is at most \(n(\log R)^{-1}\sum_{v\mid n}|\mu(v)|\log(v)/v\), using \(|G_j(l)|\le l\).
The [uniform divisor estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#divisors) bound this by \(O(\varphi(n)(1+\log\log(3k))^6/\log R)\).
Interchanging the remaining two divisor sums gives (4).
With no assigned divisor \(T_n(g)=0\).
For \(n>kR\) the same estimates give \(|T_n(g)|\le k\tau(n)=o(\varphi(n))\) uniformly.

For all \(n,j\),
\[
               \sum_{u\mid j}|B_{n,j}(u)|\le\varphi(n).                        \tag{5}
\]
To check this, factor the expression by prime powers, writing \(h=v_p(n),\ v=v_p(j),\ t=v_p(u)\).
The local sum before the totient is \(\sum_{a=t}^{\min(h,v)}\mu(p^{h-a})\), interpreted as zero for an empty range.
If \(h\le v\), only \(t=h\) survives and its value is one.
If \(h=v+1\), every \(0\le t\le v\) has value minus one, and the total absolute mass is \(\sum_{t=0}^v\varphi(p^t)=p^v\le\varphi(p^{v+1})\).
For \(h>v+1\) all terms vanish.
Multiplication proves (5), including the empty prime product, and when \(n\mid j\) it gives precisely the single atom \(B_{n,j}(n)=\varphi(n)\).
For \(g=1\) the unweighted sum of \(B_{n,j}\) is the Ramanujan sum \(c_n(j)\), by summing \(\varphi(u)\) over \(u\mid l\).
The same prime-power computation gives \(c_n(j)/\varphi(n)=\mu(m)/\varphi(m)\), where \(m=n/(n,j)\).
For \(n\nmid j\) this real number is at most \(1/2\), since the only \(m>1\) with \(\varphi(m)=1\) is two and its Möbius value is negative.

<a id="saturation"></a>
## Recovering the entire multiplicity distribution

Let \(c_l\) be the integral exponents in the initial formal product \(\prod_{l\le k}(1-X^l)^{c_l}\) for the reversal of the input.
Matching the prefix gives \(c_l=\sum_{l\mid n}b_n\mu(n/l)\).
The [Salem Euler exponent bound and terminal sum](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#degree) give \(\sum_{l\le k}|lc_l-e^{al}|=o(e^{ak})\), \(aH\to\infty\), and \(k\ll_{A,B}(\log(2d))^4\).
Since \(|w_l|\le l\) and \(\sum_{l\le k-H}e^{al}\le Ve^{-aH}\), there is a uniform identity for every test,
\[
 \sum_n b_nT_n(g)=\sum_{l\le k}c_l w_l
     =\sum_{j\in J}\frac{e^{aj}}{j}\sum_{u\mid j}\varphi(u)g_j(u)+o(V).       \tag{6}
\]

First take \(g=1\).
The right side is \((1+o(1))V\).
Every assigned order \(n\) contributes \(\varphi(n)f(j/n)+O(\omega_k\varphi(n))\) by (4).
If \(n\le kR\) has an assigned divisor but \(n\mid j\), then \(j/n<R\) and \(n\) itself is assigned to \(j\).
Consequently every unassigned order with an active divisor in this range gives at most \((1/2+o(1))\varphi(n)\), by (4) and the Ramanujan estimate.
Inactive or larger conductors satisfy the same upper bound.
Summing the deficits from \(D=\sum_n b_n\varphi(n)\sim V\) gives
\[
   \tfrac12\sum_{n\ {\rm unassigned}}b_n\varphi(n)
      +\sum_{n\ {\rm assigned\ to}\ j}(1-f(j/n))b_n\varphi(n)=o(D),            \tag{7}
\]
where the second sum includes all terminal \(j\)'s.
This proves the assertion on \(j/n\ge k^\eta\), since such an assigned ratio incurs loss at least \(\eta\log k/\log R\).

For arbitrary \(g\), the bounds (4) and (5) make the entire contribution of unassigned orders \(o(D)\) uniformly.
At each assigned order the single atom in (4) gives \(f(j/n)b_n\varphi(n)g_j(n)\) up to the same summed \(o(D)\) error, and (7) removes the scalar \(f(j/n)\).
It follows from (6) that the difference between the integrals of \(g\) against \(m_{j,u}\) and \(e^{aj}\varphi(u)/j\) is \(o(D)\), uniformly over all such arrays.
Taking \(g_j(u)\) to be the sign of that difference of masses at each separate coordinate proves (1).

<a id="consequences"></a>
## A common divisor and the limiting local order statistics

On adding the coordinates with the same \(u\) and reinstating the \(o(D)\) unassigned mass, (1) gives total absolute degree difference \(o(D)\) from the ideal multiplicities \(\sum_{j\in J,\ u\mid j}e^{aj}/j\).
Rounding each summand down before addition changes the total degree by at most \(\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{u\mid j}\varphi(u)=\sum_{j\in J}j\le k^2=o(D)\).
The rounded multiplicities are exactly those of (2).
Thus \(\sum_u\varphi(u)|b_u-\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_u}C_{a,k}|=o(D)\).
Taking componentwise minima proves the common divisor assertion, and the triangle inequality gives its pairwise version.
At the longest prefix of a shrinking Salem input the [sharp original-degree cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#rounding) gives \(aK(P)=\log(da)+o(1)\sim\log d\).
Hence \(V=(1+o(1))d\) at that index.

The ideal coordinates constitute an \(e^{aj}\)-weighted mixture of the uniform distributions on all \(j\)-th roots of unity.
For each fixed modulus \(m\), the \(j\)'s in this mixture tend to the uniform distribution modulo \(m\) if \(a\to0\).
Indeed the weights at \(j=k-h\) are proportional to \(e^{-ah}\) for \(0\le h<H\).
The geometric tail past \(H\) has relative mass \(e^{-aH}=o(1)\), and each residue modulo \(m\) under the full geometric distribution has probability \((1-e^{-a})e^{-ah_0}/(1-e^{-am})\to1/m\) uniformly for \(0\le h_0<m\).
Consequently the valuations of \(j\) at any fixed finite set of primes converge jointly to independent geometric laws with \(\Pr(v_p(j)=v)=(1-1/p)p^{-v}\).
This follows first for bounded truncations by the Chinese remainder theorem and then for the full laws by letting the truncation bound grow, since the finite-prime tails have limiting mass at most \(\sum_p p^{-T}\) at level \(T\).

For fixed \(j\) with \(v_p(j)=v\), a uniform \(j\)-th root has order divisible by \(p^t\), \(t\ge1\), with probability zero if \(v<t\) and \(1-p^{t-1-v}\) if \(v\ge t\).
These conditional events are independent over different primes by the direct product decomposition of the cyclic group.
Averaging at one prime gives \(\sum_{v\ge t}(1-1/p)p^{-v}(1-p^{t-1-v})=p^{1-t}/(p+1)\).
Multiplication over the prime powers in \(q\), followed by (1), gives (3).
