# Simultaneously coprime pole moduli across sections and factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational norm intersections and divisors for every factor

For the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement), write \(c_3=42,c_4=56\), \(K_j=\mathbb Q(\beta_j)\) and
\[
 N_j(k,l)=\left|N_{K_j/\mathbb Q}((-c_j)^k-\beta_j^l)\right|.
\]
For positive integers \(k,l,t,n\), put \(T=\max(k+l,t+n)\).
As \(T\to\infty\), uniformly in these exponents,
\[
 \log\gcd(N_i(k,l),N_j(t,n))=o(T)
 \quad\text{if }i\ne j\text{ or }kn\ne tl.                         \tag{1}
\]
There is a computable strictly increasing sequence \(h_m\) such that for every \(m\ge1\), every \(m\le a\le2m\), every monic irreducible rational factor \(S\) of \(P_{a,h_ma+1}\), and each pole \(j=3,4\), one can assign a positive integer \(M_{m,a,S,j}\) dividing \(|\operatorname{Res}(S,q_j(X^a))|\).
All the assigned moduli are pairwise coprime, including between different batches, and each is supported on split rational primes of \(K_j\) greater than \(m\).
Writing \(d=\deg S\), uniformly over each batch as \(m\to\infty\),
\[
 \log M_{m,a,S,j}=\big(\tfrac12\log\beta_j+o(1)\big)d.              \tag{2}
\]
For each modulus there is one unit residue \(x\) satisfying \(S(x)=q_j(x^a)=0\) and \(x^{h_ma+1}=-c_j\) modulo that modulus.
Thus for all sufficiently large batches every factor has a distinct rational witness prime at each pole, with distinct choices even across batches.
The retained prime powers may have growing exponents, and an assigned prime may also divide another *full* pole norm.
The construction gives neither a single prime of comparable logarithmic size nor irreducibility of the sections.

<a id="intersection"></a>
## Comparing conjugate unit differences

Use the fixed biquadratic field \(L=K_3K_4\) and the places above infinity and \(2,3,7\).
For either choice of conjugate \(\eta\) of \(\beta_i\), the number \(u=(-c_i)^k\eta^{-l}\) is an integral \(S\)-unit for that set of places, with height \(O(T)\).
Define \(v=(-c_j)^t\theta^{-n}\) with either conjugate \(\theta\) of \(\beta_j\).
For distinct poles, the valuation over three in a relation \(u^r v^s=1\) first kills the exponent on pole three, and the valuation over two then kills the other exponent.
For the same pole, taking a valuation over two gives \(kr+ts=0\).
Since the two conjugates are \(\beta_j\) and \(-\beta_j^{-1}\), taking their exponents modulo signs gives \(\epsilon lr+\epsilon' ns=0\), with the two signs determined by the chosen embeddings.
For like signs the determinant is nonzero by \(kn\ne tl\), and for opposite signs it is nonzero by positivity.
Hence all four orientation pairs are multiplicatively independent in the cases of (1).

Apply [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) to each pair.
Its finite-place bound gives \(\log N_{L/\mathbb Q}((u-1,v-1))=o(T)\), using the norm of the integral ideal generated by the two differences.
The finitely many exceptional pairs for any fixed tolerance have bounded exponents, since the valuations at two recover \(k,t\) and the chosen non-torsion units then recover \(l,n\).
Multiplication by the integral units \(\eta^l,\theta^n\) preserves all these valuations.
At each prime of \(L\), the valuation of each rational norm integer is the sum of the two valuations of its conjugate factors.
For nonnegative numbers the minimum of two such sums is at most the sum of the four pairwise minima.
Summing with prime norm weights and dividing by \([L:\mathbb Q]\) proves (1), including primes in the chosen set since the source inequality sums over all places.

<a id="batches"></a>
## Assigning primes over an increasing collection of sections

Fix \(m\) temporarily and use \(l=ha+1\) for \(m\le a\le2m\), with \(h\to\infty\).
The directions are primitive, and \(a(hb+1)-b(ha+1)=a-b\) separates distinct slopes.
By (1), the sum of the logarithmic pairwise gcds of the \(2(m+1)\) norm integers in this batch is \(o_m(h)\).
For each individual norm, the [split-branch and factor allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#statement) supplies moduli for the individual factors already coprime within that norm, with total logarithmic loss \(O_m(1+\log h)\) relative to their resultants.
At every rational prime keep the largest assigned power across all norms and discard the other powers, with any fixed tie rule.
For each discarded power, its logarithm is bounded by the local contribution to the gcd of its originating norm and the winning norm.
Each winner-loser norm pair occurs at most once at a given prime because assignments within a norm are coprime.
Thus this step loses \(o_m(h)\) in total.

Also exclude every prime in a finite set \(A_m\) consisting of the primes used in earlier batches and the primes at most \(m\).
For \(p\in A_m\), fix \(e_p>\max_{a,j}v_p(N_j(a,1))\), finite since \((-c_j)^a\) is rational and \(\beta_j\) irrational.
Require \(h\) to be a multiple of all the orders of the units \(\beta_j\) in the finite groups \((\mathcal O_{K_j}/p^{e_p})^\times\).
Then \(N_j(a,ha+1)\) agrees up to sign with \(N_j(a,1)\) modulo \(p^{e_p}\), so the discarded contributions at all primes of \(A_m\) are bounded independently of \(h\) for this batch and its fixed past.
On this progression the combined logarithmic loss from all the resultants remains \(o_m(h)\).

The [fixed-cover degree formula](Yin_C_G0345_v828.md#statement) gives \(d\ge h\) eventually for every factor in the fixed batch, since \(d/(2l)\ge 1/a+o_a(1)\).
Choose \(h_m>\max(m^2,h_{m-1})\), starting with \(h_0=0\), on the specified progression with all factor degrees at least \(h_m\) and total logarithmic loss at most \(h_m\log(2)/m\).
This is an effective terminating search: one can factor the polynomials and integers, enumerate the divisors and residue classes satisfying the individual congruences and coprimality restrictions, and test the loss exactly by raising the product of resultant-to-modulus ratios to the power \(m\) and comparing with \(2^{h_m}\).
The preceding estimates prove existence for all sufficiently large candidates on this progression without an effective threshold from the Subspace Theorem.
All selected directions now have \(a\to\infty\) and \((h_ma+1)/a\to\infty\).
By the [archimedean pole size](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#size), each original log resultant divided by its factor degree tends uniformly to \(\tfrac12\log\beta_j\).
The individual loss divided by that degree is at most \(\log(2)/m\), proving (2), and reduction of the original unit residue gives the claimed congruences.
