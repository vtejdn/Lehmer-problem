# A common Pell exponent at two, three and five

<a id="statement"></a>
## A condition linking two different residue characteristics

Fix a positive odd multiple \(h\) of \(39\) prime to five.
Use the [fixed degree with numerator eight](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#statement), writing \(g=q-r,\ b=4g/h,\ d=8(q-1)/h\), with either sign \(\varepsilon\) in \(Q\).
For a monic rational divisor \(S\) of degree \(d\) the element
\[
 U(S)=5^{-b/2}\operatorname{Res}(S,X^{g/2}-\sqrt3)
\]
is a [totally positive norm-one unit](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#units) in \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\).
Put \(\lambda=2+\sqrt3\), so \(U(S)=\lambda^n\) for some integer \(n\).
In \(\mathbb F_3\) write \(T_3\) for the sum of \(\bar x^{g/2}\) over the roots of \(\bar S\) at three, including multiplicities.
The single exponent requires
\[
        n\equiv0\pmod2,\qquad n\equiv T_3\pmod3,\qquad
        \overline{U(S)}=(2+a)^{T_3}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{25},\quad a^2=3.   \tag{1}
\]
Here the bar in the last equation denotes reduction at five and sends \(\sqrt3\) to \(a\).
The right side is well-defined since \(2+a\) has order three.

There are arbitrarily large prime pairs of the stated type with the following properties, for each choice of positive precisions \(a_2,a_3\).
For each sign, fix local degree-\(d\) divisors at two and three meeting the scalar unit tests through these precisions, with \(U\equiv1\) through the same precisions.
On the very same pair there are three choices of local divisor at five with
\[
         F(\varepsilon)\equiv5^{-b}N_2(F)\equiv5^{-b}N_3(F)\equiv1\bmod5,
         \qquad N_j(F)=\operatorname{Res}(F,X^g-j).
\]
The three residues of \(U(F)\) have orders six, three and one, respectively.
The first violates the totally positive unit condition at five.
The second satisfies that local condition but violates (1) for the fixed divisor at three.
The third matches the residue of the single unit \(\lambda^0\) at all three chosen precisions \(a_2,a_3,1\).
The existence of a monic rational divisor of degree \(d\) remains open.

<a id="exponents"></a>
## Reduction of the same exponent in the three rings

The reduction of \(\mathcal O\) modulo three is \(\mathbb F_3[\pi]/(\pi^2)\), where \(\pi\) denotes \(\sqrt3\).
The constant term of \(S\) is one, its degree is even and its roots pair under inversion.
Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Res}(S,X^{g/2}-\pi)
   =\prod_x(1-\pi x^{-g/2})
   \equiv1-\pi T_3\pmod3.
\]
Here the elementary symmetric coefficients in the inverses are integers and the linear coefficient uses inversion invariance.
Since \(b/2=2g/h\) is even, the normalizing power of five is one modulo three.
As \(\lambda^n=(-1)^n(1-n\pi)\) in this quotient, the two coefficients give the first two relations of (1), also for negative \(n\).
At five the stated order of \(2+a\) now gives the final relation.

More generally, for \(i=2,3,5\) and \(t\ge1\), put \(o_i(t)=\operatorname{ord}_{(\mathcal O/i^t)^\times}(\lambda)\).
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 o_2(1)&=2,\qquad o_2(2)=4,\qquad o_2(t)=2^{t-1}\quad(t\ge3),\\
 o_3(t)&=2\cdot3^t,\qquad o_5(t)=3\cdot5^{t-1}.                         \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
At two \(\lambda^2=7+4\sqrt3\) has residue \(-1\bmod4\), and \(\lambda^4=97+56\sqrt3\) differs from one by an element of \(8\mathcal O\setminus16\mathcal O\).
For \(k\ge2\), squaring \(1+2^k z\) with \(z\notin2\mathcal O\) raises the exact power of two in its difference from one by one.
Together with \(\lambda\bmod2=\sqrt3\ne1\) this proves the first line.
At three the expansion above gives order six modulo three and \(\lambda^6=1351+780\sqrt3\) differs from one by an element of \(3\mathcal O\setminus9\mathcal O\).
At five \(\lambda^3=26+15\sqrt3\) differs from one by an element of \(5\mathcal O\setminus25\mathcal O\).
For an odd prime \(p\) the binomial expansion of \((1+p^k z)^p\) increases \(k\ge1\) by exactly one for \(z\notin p\mathcal O\), since the new leading coefficient is \(z\bmod p\).
This proves the other orders.

The subgroup generated by \(\lambda\) at \(5^t\) consists exactly of the norm-one residues whose reduction modulo five has order dividing three.
Indeed the norm-one subgroup of \(\mathbb F_{25}^\times\) has size six, by cyclicity of the multiplicative group.
Each of its elements has exactly \(5^{t-1}\) norm-one lifts modulo \(5^t\): at each step multiplying an arbitrary lift by \(1+5^k z\) alters the next norm digit by the trace of \(z\), a surjective linear map \(\mathbb F_{25}\to\mathbb F_5\) with five-element kernel.
Thus the claimed inverse image has size \(3\cdot5^{t-1}\), equal to \(o_5(t)\).
At \(3^t\) the subgroup generated by \(\lambda\) is the whole norm-one group.
For each \(y\bmod3^t\) the equation \(x^2=1+3y^2\bmod3^t\) has exactly two solutions, lifting the two simple roots modulo three one digit at a time, so its size is \(2\cdot3^t\).

Given a tuple of residues in the three respective cyclic subgroups generated by \(\lambda\), let \(n_i\) be its exponents modulo \(o_i(t_i)\).
The tuple comes from a single integer exponent if and only if
\[
                       n_2\equiv n_3\bmod2,\qquad n_3\equiv n_5\bmod3 .           \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the three pairwise gcds of the orders for \((2,3),(3,5),(2,5)\) are \(2,3,1\), and these are the compatibility conditions of the Chinese remainder theorem.
The criterion concerns the specified unit residues; realizing them by a common polynomial is a further condition.

<a id="choices"></a>
## Fixing the factors at two and three

In the [fixed-prime-pair construction](Yin_C_G0345_v226.md#parameters) enlarge \(M\) by a fixed multiple so that for each \(H=h,3h,21h\) we have \(8H\mid r-1\) and \(24\mid B=g/(2H)\).
For instance \(M\) can be taken divisible by \(24\cdot21\) as well as by every earlier fixed requirement.
For each \(\ell=2,3\) add the residue of \(\operatorname{Res}(F_O,X^{g/2}-\sqrt3)\) in \((\mathcal O/\ell^{a_\ell})^\times\) to the three scalar labels of each lifted primitive orbit \(F_O\).
Its norm equals \(N_3(F_O)\), a unit by disjointness from the exceptional residues.
Hence this new label is a unit, also at the ramified primes.
Take a common exponent \(E\) of the two resulting finite label groups and enlarge \(M\) by a multiple of \(6E\).
Choose \(M(h-8)\) larger than \(E\) times each group size.
The [equal-label grouping](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#precision) still gives exactly \(8J_\ell/h\) selected orbits with every product label one.
Also \(E\mid b/2\), and the normalizing \(5^{b/2}\) reduces to one at both primes, since the group exponents include the scalar unit groups.
Keep these factors fixed; in particular \(T_3=0\).
At five keep the [common primitive factor](Yin_C_G0345_v226.md#parameters) with all four labels one.
The [two exceptional choices](Yin_C_G0345_v226.md#orders) give the residues of orders six and three.
The latter is different from the residue \((2+a)^0=1\) required by (1).

<a id="identity"></a>
## An exceptional choice with quadratic unit residue one

Use \(H=21h\) and the [exceptional fibers](Yin_C_G0345_v226.md#fibers) \(x^{4B}=-\xi^4\), with \(\eta=a^{H^{-1}\bmod8}\).
Their derivation uses only that \(H\) is odd and prime to five, \(g=2HB\), \(24\mid B\), \(8H\mid r-1\), and that the chosen \(H\)-th roots are closed under Frobenius and inversion.
Take the union of all primitive \(m\)-th roots for the following \(m\), choosing the row by \(\eta^2\):
\[
 \begin{array}{c|l}
   \eta^2 & m\\ \hline
   3 & 13,\ 117\\
   2 & 7,\ 9,\ 21,\ 39,\ 63 .
 \end{array}
\]
Each \(m\) divides \(21\cdot39\), hence divides \(H\).
The sets are disjoint, have total cardinalities \(12+72=84\) and \(6+6+12+24+36=84\), and yield exceptional degree \(4B\cdot84=8g/h=2b\).
Since \(m>1\) is odd, \(\Phi_m(-1)=1\): the product over divisors of \(m\) other than one of these positive values equals \(((-1)^m-1)/(-2)=1\).
Fourth powers permute the primitive roots at each \(m\).
Thus the product of \(1+\xi^4\) is one.

For each row in the following table the final column is the remainder of \(\Phi_m(T)\) modulo \(T^2-\eta^2\) in characteristic five:
\[
\begin{array}{r|r|l}
 m & \eta^2 & \Phi_m(T)\\ \hline
 13&3&-T-2\\
 117&3&T-2\\
 7&2&2T\\
 9&2&2T-1\\
 21&2&-2\\
 39&2&2T-2\\
 63&2&2 .
\end{array}
\]
The entries follow by replacing \(T^2\) by its displayed value in
\(\Phi_m(T)=\prod_{e\mid m}(1-T^e)^{\mu(m/e)}\); the denominators are units since \(T\) has order eight and all \(e\) are odd.
In both rows of the choice the product of the table entries at \(T=\eta\) is one: for the first it is \(4-\eta^2\), and for the second use \((2\eta-1)(2\eta-2)=4\eta^2-6\eta+2=-\eta\) in characteristic five.
By reciprocity this product equals \(\prod_\xi(1-\eta\xi)\).
The exceptional-fiber formula gives \(U(F)\equiv1\bmod5\), with exact valuation \(b/2\) before normalization, and gives \(F(\varepsilon)\equiv1\).
Taking the quadratic norm gives \(5^{-b}N_3(F)\equiv1\).
The [five-adic ratio of the two binomial norms](Yin_C_G0345_v226.md#orders) is one modulo five for every such selection of degree \(d\) and exceptional degree \(2b\), so \(5^{-b}N_2(F)\equiv1\) as well.
The same local factors at two and three now satisfy the quadratic congruences for this choice with exponent zero.
None of these finite tests provides a rational divisor.
