# When a common power preserves the noncyclotomic factor of prime blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Common powers of a normalized polynomial

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) with \(k\ge2\), and positive integers \(b_i\) with \(\gcd_i b_i=1\).
Set \(D=\sum_i b_i\), \(W=D+1\), \(A(X)=\prod_i(X^{b_i}-p_i)\), \(A^*(X)=X^D A(1/X)\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
For \(N\ge C W^9\), [the prime-block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) gives
\[
 P_N(X)=X^N A(X)+\varepsilon A^*(X)=S_N(X)U_N(X),
\]
where \(S_N\) is the unique monic irreducible noncyclotomic factor, it occurs once, and \(U_N\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials of total degree at most \(10D\).
Write \(d=\deg S_N\) and \(B=m(S_N)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular \(B=\sum_i\log p_i+O(W\exp(-cN/W^2))\) and, increasing \(C\) if necessary, \(B\ge\tfrac12\sum_i\log p_i\).
We consider \(P_N(X^h)\) for every positive integer \(h\); the size condition is on the normalized exponents \(N,b_i\).

1. For every fixed finite set \(T\) of primes there is a union of residue classes of \(N\), of positive natural density, on which \(S_N(X^h)\) is irreducible **simultaneously** for every \(h\) supported on \(T\).
   The union depends on the normalized blocks, sign and \(T\), not on \(h\).
   More precisely, one can take \(\ell-1\) classes modulo \(\ell(\ell-1)\) for a prime \(\ell\) specified in the proof, giving density \(1/\ell\).
2. At every \(N\) in the stated range, if some \(b_i\) is even then \(S_N(X^{2^j})\) is irreducible for every \(j\ge0\).
   Also, for an absolute \(C_1>0\), any odd prime \(q>C_1 B(\log(2d))^3\) may be used to arbitrary multiplicity without splitting.
   More precisely, take any \(H\mid h\) all of whose odd prime divisors satisfy this bound, allowing \(2\mid H\) only when some \(b_i\) is even.
   Then \(S_N(X^H)\) is irreducible.
3. For every divisor \(H\mid h\) for which \(S_N(X^H)\) is irreducible, each irreducible noncyclotomic factor of \(P_N(X^h)\) has measure a positive integer multiple of \(B/(h/H)\), and those integers sum to \(h/H\).
   In particular if \(H=h\), the unique noncyclotomic factor has degree \(hd\), occurs once and has measure \(B\).
   For arbitrary \(h\), even without an irreducibility certificate, each such factor has measure at least \(c_1/(\log(2d))^3\), with an absolute \(c_1>0\).

The last bound is independent of the common dilation but still depends on the normalized degree.
The result does not exclude small individual measures at unbounded normalized degrees and uncontrolled dilations.

<a id="tower"></a>
## Which prime powers can be combined

Let \(\beta\ne0\) belong to a field \(K\), and let \(h\) be an integer whose prime divisors are different from the characteristic of \(K\).
If for every prime \(q\mid h\) neither \(\beta\) nor \(-\beta\) is a \(q\)-th power in \(K\), then \(X^h-\beta\) is irreducible in \(K[X]\).
For odd \(q\), the two tests are equivalent.
Indeed the [elementary prime-binomial criterion](Yin_C_G0376_v2.md#irreducible-lehmer-radical-control) follows by taking the product \(v\) of the roots of a monic proper factor of \(X^q-u\) of degree \(e\): then \(v\in K\), \(v^q=u^e\), and \(\gcd(e,q)=1\) implies \(u\in K^q\).
Suppose inductively that \(X^t-\beta\) is irreducible, and take a root \(\theta\).
Its norm from \(K(\theta)\) to \(K\) is \((-1)^{t+1}\beta\), which by hypothesis is not a \(q\)-th power in \(K\) for any prime \(q\mid h\).
Thus \(\theta\) is not a \(q\)-th power in \(K(\theta)\).
Adjoining a \(q\)-th root of \(\theta\) gives degree \(q\) over \(K(\theta)\), hence degree \(tq\) over \(K\).
Starting at \(t=1\) and using the prime factors of \(h\) with their multiplicities proves the assertion.

For a root \(\beta\) of \(S_N\), set \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
Irreducibility of \(X^h-\beta\) over \(K\) implies irreducibility of \(S_N(X^h)\) over \(\mathbb Q\): any root \(\alpha\) above \(\beta\) generates a field containing \(K\), so its degree is \(hd\).
To certify the prime 2, take a block with even exponent.
The disjoint exterior disks in [the localization proof](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#disks) centered at \(p_i^{1/b_i}\) and \(-p_i^{1/b_i}\) each contain just one root, real by complex conjugation and of the same sign as its center.
Both belong to \(S_N\), since their absolute values exceed one.
Consequently \(\beta\) has a negative real embedding and a positive real embedding, so neither \(\beta\) nor \(-\beta\) can be a square in \(K\).

The [classical degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v199.md#height) supplies \(m(\gamma)\ge c_1/(\log(2d'))^3\) for a nontorsion unit \(\gamma\) of degree \(d'\).
If \(\gamma^q=\beta\) in \(K\), then \(\gamma\) is itself a nontorsion unit, \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K\), and \(m(\gamma)=B/q\) by the power rule for absolute logarithmic height.
This excludes every odd \(q>B(\log(2d))^3/c_1\) and proves the simultaneous assertion in part 2 by the tower argument.

<a id="residue"></a>
## A residue certificate for every dilation on a fixed set of primes

Take a prime \(\ell\equiv1\pmod4\) with \(q\mid\ell-1\) for every \(q\in T\), and \(\varphi(\ell-1)>10D\).
There are arbitrarily large such primes: put \(m=4\prod_{q\in T}q\) and, given any finite list of primes, take \(z\) a sufficiently large multiple of them and of \(m\).
Then \(\Phi_m(z)>1\) is prime to \(z\), since \(\Phi_m(0)=1\).
Any prime \(\ell\mid\Phi_m(z)\) is outside the list and prime to \(m\); the polynomial \(X^m-1\) is separable modulo \(\ell\), so \(z\bmod\ell\) has exact order \(m\), giving \(m\mid\ell-1\).
Finally \(\varphi(t)\to\infty\) with \(t\): bounded \(\varphi(t)\) bounds every prime divisor \(p\) and its exponent \(a\), since \(p^{a-1}(p-1)\mid\varphi(t)\) for \(p^a\Vert t\).

The cyclic group \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\) has \(\varphi(\ell-1)>2D\) generators.
Choose a generator \(a\) with \(A(a)A^*(a)\ne0\): these are nonzero polynomials modulo \(\ell\) of degree at most \(D\) each because \(A\) is monic and \(A^*(0)=1\).
There is one class \(N_0\bmod{\ell-1}\) for which \(a^{N_0}=-\varepsilon A^*(a)/A(a)\).
Then \(P_N(a)=0\) for every \(N\equiv N_0\bmod{\ell-1}\).
Within that progression the derivative at \(a\) is
\[
   P_N'(a)= N a^{N_0-1} A(a)+a^{N_0}A'(a)+\varepsilon (A^*)'(a)
                \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_\ell .
\]
The coefficient of \(N\) is nonzero, so exactly one residue class of \(N\bmod\ell\) makes this derivative vanish.
Keeping the other \(\ell-1\) classes and intersecting with \(N\equiv N_0\bmod{\ell-1}\) yields the stated union and density; removing \(N<CW^9\) does not change the density.

No cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_j\) of \(P_N\) can vanish at \(a\) modulo \(\ell\).
Indeed \(\Phi_j\mid X^j-1\) would force \(\ell-1\mid j\), whereas \(\varphi(j)\ge\varphi(\ell-1)>10D\) for such a multiple.
This contradicts the cyclotomic degree bound on \(U_N\).
Thus \(a\) is a simple residue root of \(S_N\).
It lifts to a root in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\): at each power \(\ell^r\), the unit derivative determines the unique correction modulo \(\ell^{r+1}\).
This gives an embedding of \(K\) in \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) taking \(\beta\) to a unit of residue \(a\).

For each \(q\in T\), the generator \(a\) is not a \(q\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\), since \(q\mid\ell-1\).
The element \(-1\) is a \(q\)-th power for odd \(q\), and is a square since \(4\mid\ell-1\).
Neither \(a\) nor \(-a\) is therefore a \(q\)-th power.
The [tower argument](#tower) in the finite field in particular shows \(X^h-a\) irreducible over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) for every \(h\) supported on \(T\).
Equivalently for the required test over \(K\), a \(q\)-th root of \(\beta\) or \(-\beta\) in \(K\) would be an \(\ell\)-adic unit whose reduction gives a forbidden \(q\)-th root of \(a\) or \(-a\).
The same tower over \(K\) proves part 1, for arbitrary multiplicities of all primes in \(T\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Degrees and measures of the individual factors

Every zero of \(U_N(X^h)\) is a root of unity, whereas every zero of \(S_N(X^h)\) is nontorsion.
The latter polynomial has simple zeros, since \(S_N\) is separable in characteristic zero and does not vanish at zero.
For any one of its irreducible factors take a root \(\alpha\), put \(\beta=\alpha^h\) and write \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\beta)]\).
The [radical measure identity](Yin_C_G0206_v1.md#statement) gives \(m(\alpha)=eB/h\), since the degree of \(\alpha\) is \(ed\) and its absolute logarithmic height is \(1/h\) times that of \(\beta\).
If \(H\mid h\) and \(S_N(X^H)\) is irreducible, then \(\alpha^{h/H}\) has degree \(Hd\); the tower of fields shows \(H\mid e\).
The sum of all the relative degrees \(e\) is \(h\) by the degree of \(S_N(X^h)\), proving the asserted partition and the measure and degree at \(h=H\).

For the unconditional lower bound use the norm \(\eta=\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/K}\alpha\), with \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
It is a nontorsion unit, because \(\eta^h=\beta^e\).
Thus \(\deg\eta\le d\), and the height power rule gives \(m(\eta)=(\deg\eta/d)m(\alpha)\).
The degree estimate above proves \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\eta)\ge c_1/(\log(2d))^3\).
In particular there are at most \(B(\log(2d))^3/c_1\) noncyclotomic factors for every \(h\).
