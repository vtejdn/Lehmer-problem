# A gap above two for every eigenvalue of a tree with boundedly many branch vertices

<a id="statement"></a>
## Individual eigenvalues and Salem trace factors

For each integer \(b\ge0\) there exists \(\eta_b>0\) such that every adjacency eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of a finite tree with at most \(b\) vertices of degree at least three satisfies
\[
                |\lambda|>2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                \operatorname{arcosh}(|\lambda|/2)\ge\eta_b .
\]
The degrees of the branch vertices, the number of leaves and the lengths of all paths may vary without bound.
One can take \(\eta_1=\tfrac12\log(43/42)\).
For a prescribed bound on the number of leaves, a finite recursion below gives a computable positive lower bound.
The recursion also applies to individual eigenvalues of all independently chosen edge subdivisions of any fixed finite simple graph, at either spectral endpoint.

For a tree \(T\) with adjacency matrix \(A_T\), put \(P_T(z)=z^{|T|}\det((z+z^{-1})I-A_T)\).
Every monic integer factor of \(P_T\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure has logarithmic measure at least \(\eta_b\) under the branch bound.
This estimate allows arbitrary complementary factors, including other eigenvalues outside \([-2,2]\).
For every Salem number \(\tau\), the trace \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\) occurs as an eigenvalue of some finite tree by [Salez's realization theorem](../audits/literature/Sal15/Yin_U001.md#sal15-u001).
Indeed the trace is an algebraic integer and all its conjugates are real, since every conjugate of \(\tau\) other than \(\tau,\tau^{-1}\) has modulus one.
Its realization puts the minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) in \(P_T\).
Thus if Salem numbers tend to one, the **minimum** number of branch vertices among all realizing trees tends to infinity.
The same argument applies to every reciprocal algebraic unit whose trace is totally real, choosing a conjugate with modulus greater than one whenever the measure is positive.

The proof uses the energy at the endpoint \(2\) of each path after eliminating its interior vertices.
Related limits for simultaneous uniform edge subdivision are studied by [Kumar, Mohar, Pragada and Zhan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2025.01.044).

<a id="paths"></a>
## Elimination at a spectral endpoint

For \(t\ge0\) put \(x=2\cosh t\).
Keep a nonempty set of vertices so that all remaining components are paths.
The matrix \(xI-A\) on those components is positive definite even at \(t=0\), since a path on \(h\) vertices has eigenvalues \(2\cos(j\pi/(h+1))\), \(1\le j\le h\).
Let \(K(t)\) be the Schur complement on the kept vertices.
Then \(x\) is an eigenvalue exactly when \(K(t)\) is singular.
Its quadratic form is the minimum of the full quadratic form over interior values with fixed kept values.
In particular \(K(t)\succeq K(0)+(2\cosh t-2)I\).

An edge path of length \(\ell\ge1\) joining two kept vertices contributes a two-by-two matrix \(E_\ell(t)\) in addition to the common diagonal \(xI\).
Solving \(xf_j=f_{j-1}+f_{j+1}\) between endpoint values \(a,b\) gives
\[
 f_j=\frac{\sinh((\ell-j)t)a+\sinh(jt)b}{\sinh(\ell t)} .
\]
Substitution at the endpoints gives diagonal entries \(-\sinh((\ell-1)t)/\sinh(\ell t)\) and off-diagonal entries \(-\sinh t/\sinh(\ell t)\).
In the symmetric and antisymmetric directions the eigenvalues are respectively
\[
   -\cosh t+\sinh t\,\tanh(\ell t/2),\qquad
   -\cosh t+\sinh t\,\coth(\ell t/2).
\]
At \(t=0\) the block is \(-I+\ell^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so it decreases in the order of quadratic forms as \(\ell\) grows.
For \(0<t\le1/2\), \(\|E_\ell(t)-E_\ell(0)\|\le4t\) uniformly in \(\ell\).
Indeed \(0\le\tanh u\le1\), \(0\le\coth u-1/u\le1\) for \(u>0\), and the term from \(1/u\) in the second channel contributes an error \(2(\sinh(t)/t-1)/\ell\).
Using \(\sinh t\le2t,\ \cosh t-1\le t^2,\ \sinh(t)/t-1\le t^2\) gives the stated bound.

A pendant path of \(r\ge1\) edges starting at a kept vertex contributes a diagonal entry \(-a_r(t)\), where
\[
    a_r(t)=\frac{\sinh(rt)}{\sinh((r+1)t)},\qquad
    a_r(0)=\frac r{r+1},\qquad
    1\ge a_r(t)\ge\frac1{2\cosh t},\quad
    a_r(t)\ge e^{-t}a_r(0).
\]
This follows by imposing zero one step past the leaf in the path formula.
The ratio increases with \(r\), as is also seen from \(e^{-t}(1-u^r)/(1-u^{r+1})\), \(u=e^{-2t}\).
The last inequality follows since the mean of the first \(r\) terms of \(1,u,u^2,\ldots\) is at least the mean of the first \(r+1\).

<a id="branches"></a>
## Unbounded degrees at finitely many branch vertices

Trees without branch vertices are paths, so assume there are between one and \(b\) branch vertices and keep exactly these vertices.
The compressed paths between branch vertices form a tree on the kept vertices, with all other edges lying in pendant paths.
For a hypothetical sequence of tree eigenvalues \(2\cosh t_n>2\) with \(t_n\to0\), pass to a subsequence on which this finite labeled tree is fixed.
Let \(K_n(t)\) denote the reduced matrix of the \(n\)-th tree.
Pass to a further subsequence on which every connecting path length is nondecreasing, using the fact that a sequence of positive integers has a nondecreasing subsequence.
At each kept vertex the number of pendant paths may be made constant or divergent.
At each constant-count vertex order the finitely many arms by length and pass to a subsequence on which all those individual lengths are nondecreasing.
At the other vertices take all pendant counts positive and at least tripling at each successive member of the sequence.

For any \(n>j\) and \(0\le t\le1/2\), the explicit blocks give
\[
             K_n(0)\preceq K_j(0),\qquad K_n(t)\preceq K_j(0)+CtI.    \tag{1}
\]
For a connecting path the first comparison is monotonicity and the second uses the uniform \(4t\) estimate.
At constant-count vertices an individual new arm contributes at most \(-e^{-t}a_{r_{\rm old}}(0)\), at most \(t\) above its previous zero-energy contribution.
At a tripling vertex the new sum of the \(a_r(t)\)'s is at least the new count divided by \(2\cosh(1/2)<3\), hence at least the old count, which dominates the old sum at zero.
Finally the base diagonal changes by \(2\cosh t-2\).
These bounds give (1) with \(C\) depending only on the fixed connecting tree and the constant pendant counts of this subsequence.

The number of negative eigenvalues of \(K_n(0)\) is nondecreasing and bounded by \(b\), so it eventually equals a fixed integer \(h\).
The inequality \(K_n(t_n)\succeq K_n(0)+(2\cosh t_n-2)I\) makes all its ordered eigenvalues after the first \(h\) strictly positive.
If \(h>0\), fix \(j\) after stabilization and let \(-\gamma<0\) be the largest negative eigenvalue of \(K_j(0)\).
By (1) and min–max, the first \(h\) eigenvalues of \(K_n(t_n)\) are strictly negative for all sufficiently large \(n\).
This excludes a zero eigenvalue, including when \(h=0\), and proves the positive-endpoint gap.
A tree is bipartite, so the negative endpoint follows by changing signs on one bipartition class.
Each off-circle root of a reciprocal factor lies over a real adjacency eigenvalue with absolute value \(2\cosh(|\log|z||)>2\).
This proves the factor assertion using one outer root alone.

<a id="effective"></a>
## A finite computation for every fixed subdivision topology

Let \(H\) be a finite simple graph and retain its original vertices when each edge \(e\) is replaced by a path of arbitrary positive integer length \(\ell_e\).
For the upper endpoint the path-block sum gives
\[
      K_{\boldsymbol\ell}(0)=\operatorname{diag}(2-\deg_H v)
              +\sum_{e=uv}\ell_e^{-1}(e_u-e_v)(e_u-e_v)^{\mathsf T},
\]
where \(e_v\) denotes the coordinate vector at the vertex.
For the lower endpoint apply the same argument to \(-A\).
Changing signs on internal path vertices moves all edge signs on that path to one end, leaving its product \(\sigma_e=(-1)^{\ell_e}\).
In the zero-energy formula replace \(e_u-e_v\) by \(e_u-\sigma_e e_v\), and in the two-by-two block conjugate by \(\operatorname{diag}(1,\sigma_e)\).
All norm and positivity estimates continue to hold.
It suffices to treat the finitely many sign patterns, allowing arbitrary lengths at each one.

In a recursion node fix the lengths of a subset of edges \(F\), leaving the remaining ones free.
Let \(K_*\) be the rational zero-energy matrix obtained by setting the reciprocals of all free lengths to zero.
Each completion has \(K_{\boldsymbol\ell}(0)\succeq K_*\).
If \(K_*\) has no negative eigenvalues, the strict increase at \(t>0\) excludes every upper-endpoint outlier at this node.
Otherwise compute a positive rational \(\gamma\) no larger than the absolute value of the negative eigenvalue of \(K_*\) nearest zero, for instance by rational isolation of the characteristic roots.
Choose an integer \(N\ge1\) so that \(2|E(H)|/N<\gamma/4\).
All completions with every free length at least \(N\) obey
\[
 K_{\boldsymbol\ell}(t)\preceq K_*+
       \big(2|E(H)|/N+(2+4|E(H)|)t\big)I,\qquad 0<t\le1/2 .
\]
This follows from the rank-one terms at zero and the uniform path estimate.
Take a positive rational \(t_*\le1/2\) with \((2+4|E(H)|)t_*<\gamma/4\).
The negative part of the ordered spectrum stays strictly negative for \(0<t\le t_*\), and the rest becomes strictly positive by \(K_{\boldsymbol\ell}(t)\succeq K_*+(2\cosh t-2)I\).
For every free edge and each possible length \(1,\ldots,N-1\), recur with that edge fixed at that length.
Together these children cover all completions missed by the long-length case.
There are finitely many children per node and the number of free edges falls at every step.
Taking the minimum of \(1/2\) and the resulting finite list of positive \(t_*\)'s gives an effective endpoint gap.
For a tree with at most \(l\) leaves and at least one branch vertex, suppressing all degree-two vertices gives at most \(2l-2\) vertices, by summing degrees.
There are finitely many such trees to enumerate and paths can be handled separately.
Thus this procedure computes a positive threshold for each prescribed leaf bound, independently of the original tree size.

<a id="one"></a>
## A numerical lower bound with one branch vertex

For arm lengths \(r_i\) at one branch vertex, an outlying eigenvalue \(2\cosh t\) satisfies \(2\cosh t=\sum_i a_{r_i}(t)\).
Strict increase of the scalar Schur complement implies \(\sum_i r_i/(r_i+1)>2\).
This sum is at least \(2+1/42\).
Indeed for five or more arms it is at least \(5/2\), and for four arms positive excess requires an arm of length at least two, giving excess at least \(1/6\).
For three arms write \(2\le k_1\le k_2\le k_3\) for their lengths plus one.
The excess is \(1-\sum1/k_i>0\).
Every such triple dominates one of \((2,3,7),(2,4,5),(2,5,5),(3,3,4),(3,4,4),(4,4,4)\) coordinatewise, by considering successively the first and second entries.
Each listed triple has excess at least \(1/42\).
The pendant estimate therefore gives \(2e^t\cosh t\ge2+1/42\), or \(t\ge\tfrac12\log(43/42)\).
