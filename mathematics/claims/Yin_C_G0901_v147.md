# Exact dyadic degrees and a coefficient-independent gap on positive reciprocal sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Removing the even coefficient and two prime powers from the degree estimate

Let \(0<u<v\) be coprime, let \(a,b\) be coprime integers of absolute value at least two, and take
\[
                    P(X)=a(1+X^{u+v})+b(X^u+X^v).
\]
Assume one coefficient is even, and set \(N=v-u\) if \(a\) is even, \(N=v+u\) if \(b\) is even.
Write \(r\) for the valuation at two of that coefficient, \(s=v_2(N)\), \(e=2^s\), and \(h=N/e\).
For every fixed odd prime \(\ell\), write \(h=H\ell^k\) with \(\ell\nmid H\), and put \(c_\ell=v_\ell(2^{\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)}-1)\).
Every nontorsion algebraic-unit root \(\beta\) of \(P\) satisfies the total logarithmic Mahler bound
\[
                   m(\beta)\ \ge\ \frac{\log2}{2H\ell^{c_\ell}} .        \tag{1}
\]
There is no bound on \(r,s,k,u,v,|a|,|b|\).
This also gives a uniform gap for all positive-measure integral divisors of \(P\) at fixed \(H,\ell\).
For a nontorsion unit root \(z\) of a dilation \(P(X^g)\), (1) applies to the primitive power \(\beta=z^g\).

More precisely, at \(s\ge2\) the following is the complete list of local factors over \(\mathbb Q_2\) on unit roots.
Each Frobenius orbit of roots of \(X^h=1\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), of length \(f\), gives one factor of residue degree \(f\) and ramification index \(e\), with a single possible exception.
If \(r=1\) and \(3\mid h\), the orbit of the two primitive cube roots gives instead two distinct factors of residue degree two, of ramification indices \(1\) and \(e-1\).
In particular the degree \(D\) of a monic irreducible unit factor satisfies \(e\mid D\) away from the exception, and \(D\equiv0,2,-2\bmod e\) in its presence.
In both cases \(D\ge e\) unless \(D=2\).
The [earlier dyadic polygon bound](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#ramification) gives the full width at \(r\ge2\) and isolates the exceptional centers at \(r=1\).
The two exact exceptional factors here allow (1) also for \(r=1\) with arbitrarily large \(s\).

On every sequence with \(\delta=|b/a|-1\to0^+\) and a nontorsion unit root at any common dilation, let \(J\) be the number of exterior conjugates of \(\beta\).
Then
\[
 h\ge(1-o(1))\frac uJ\frac{\log |b|}{\log(1+\delta)},\qquad
 H\ge\left(\frac{\log2}{2\ell^{c_\ell}}-o(1)\right)
                  \frac{u}{J\log(1+\delta)}                               \tag{2}
\]
for every fixed odd \(\ell\).
In particular the second estimate forces the cofactor outside \(2,\ell\) to grow even if \(\delta\log |b|\) does not tend to zero.
The [fixed-set period bound](Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#statement) for two selected primes has a logarithmic coefficient-height loss in this comparison.

<a id="polygons"></a>
## The exceptional residue factors have degrees two and \(2(e-1)\)

Use the [Laurent normalization at either coefficient](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#centers)
\[
        C(1+X^N)+E(X^j+X^{N-j}),
\]
where \((C,E,j)=(a,b,u)\) if \(b\) is even and \((C,E,j)=(b,a,-u)\) if \(a\) is even.
In the latter case this equals \(X^{-u}P\).
We have \(C\) odd, \(v_2(E)=r\), and \(\gcd(j,N)=1\).
In the unit residue torus every root is an \(h\)-th root with multiplicity \(e\).
Let \(\zeta\) be its odd-order lift in its unramified residue field and use \(X=\zeta(1+T)\).
The constant in this coordinate is \(2C+E(\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j})\).
It has valuation one if \(r\ge2\).
If \(r=1\), the same holds unless \(\bar\zeta^j+\bar\zeta^{-j}=1\), which says that \(\bar\zeta^j\) has order three.
Since \(j\) is coprime to its order, this is precisely the primitive-cube-root orbit, present exactly when \(3\mid h\).
At every ordinary center all coefficients below degree \(e\) vanish modulo two and the degree-\(e\) coefficient is a unit.
The negative part of the Newton polygon thus has just one edge from \((0,1)\) to \((e,0)\), also for \(s=0,1\) at an ordinary center.

At a primitive cube root in the exceptional case the constant is \(2C-E\), nonzero by coprimality and \(|C|\ge2\), and \(t=v_2(2C-E)\ge2\).
If \(s\ge2\), the linear coefficient is
\[
                CN+E\big(j\zeta^j+(N-j)\zeta^{-j}\big).
\]
After division by two its residue is one, since \(4\mid N\) and \(j\) is odd.
All degrees below \(e\) still vanish modulo two.
Thus there are two negative edges, through \((0,t),(1,1),(e,0)\); their slopes are distinct since \(e\ge4\).
The monomial used to clear a negative Laurent exponent is an integral invertible series in \(T\) of unit constant and preserves all negative edges, as in the [weighted polygon argument](Yin_C_G0901_v123.md#polygon).
For instance both \((a,b,u,v)=(2,3,5,17)\) and \((3,2,5,7)\) have \(N=12\) and exceptional vertices \((0,2),(1,1),(4,0)\).

The first exceptional edge gives just one root over the unramified center field.
For each remaining edge of width \(w=e\) or \(e-1\) the distance of a root to its lift has normalized valuation \(1/w\).
Hence the ramification index over that center field is divisible by \(w\), and its degree is at most \(w\) by the polygon count of all conjugates at that distance.
It has exact degree and ramification index \(w\).
The field of the root over \(\mathbb Q_2\) already contains its odd-order lift by Hensel's lemma, which proves the full list including its residue degrees.
A rational monic unit factor selects whole local factors.
All ordinary degrees are divisible by \(e\); the exceptional degrees contribute respectively \(2\) and \(-2\bmod e\).
Only the exceptional degree-two factor by itself can have a positive total degree below \(e\).

<a id="period"></a>
## A common residue period and the largest represented \(\ell\)-power

Let \(S\) be the monic minimum of \(\beta\).
Write \(h_0\) for the least common multiple of the orders of the roots of \(\bar S\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\).
All these orders divide \(h\); all multiplicities are at most \(e\) by the reduced parent, since \(\bar S\mid\bar P\) and \(\bar S(0)=1\).
Therefore \(\bar S\mid X^{e h_0}-1\), including at the exceptional orbit.
Monic division gives \(\beta^{e h_0}=1+2y\) with \(y\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\), and \(\beta^{2e h_0}-1\) is divisible by four in this order.
Its nonzero integral norm from \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) has absolute value at least \(4^D\).
Bounding each archimedean factor by twice the \(2e h_0\)-th power of \(\max(1,|\sigma\beta|)\) gives
\[
                         m(\beta)\ge \frac{D\log2}{2e h_0}.
\]

Let \(i=v_\ell(h_0)\); one represented residue order has this same valuation.
If \(i>c_\ell\), that order is not three and its center is ordinary.
Its Frobenius degree is divisible by \(\ell^{i-c_\ell}\): successive raising of \(2^{\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)}\) to the \(\ell\)-th power increases the valuation of its difference from one by exactly one, while raising to a power prime to \(\ell\) preserves the valuation.
The ordinary local degree at this center gives \(D\ge e\ell^{i-c_\ell}\).
When \(i\le c_\ell\) one has \(D\ge e\) for \(D>2\) by the polygon result at \(s\ge2\), and automatically at \(s\le1\).
Now \(h_0\mid H\ell^i\), so both cases yield (1).
A quadratic nontorsion unit already has measure at least the golden ratio by its integer trace and norm \(\pm1\), which also suffices for (1) in logarithmic units.
Nonunit extreme coefficients or nontrivial content cost at least \(\log2\), while every integral factor has nonnegative log measure, proving the divisor assertion.
For (2) the [individual near-pole estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement) give \(m(\beta)=(1+o(1))(J/u)\log(1+\delta)\), \(v h(\beta)=\log|b|+o(1)\), and \(v/u\to\infty\), with \(h(\beta)\) the absolute Weil height.
Degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Use \(h=N/e\ge N/D\) and \(N/v\to1\) for the first bound, and combine (1) with the first-measure equivalent for the second.

<a id="examples"></a>
## Whole polynomial irreducibility and a simultaneous exclusion

If \(N=2^s,\ s\ge1\), the sole residue center is one, ordinary at every even coefficient valuation.
When \(b\) is even this yields the entire degree \(N\) of \(P\), hence \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_2\) and \(\mathbb Q\).
When \(a\) is even, \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) whenever
\[
                         r(v-u)\log2>2u\log(|a|+|b|).                     \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the two nonhorizontal edges of the polygon of \(P\) at zero are of slopes \(\pm r/u\), and its unique unit-root factor over \(\mathbb Q_2\) has multiplicity one.
In a proper rational factorization one irreducible factor would omit this unit-root factor.
All its conjugates at two would have absolute normalized valuation \(r/u\), so any root \(\kappa\) of it satisfies \(2h(\kappa)\ge (r/u)\log2\) by the product formula and the sum of the absolute local logarithms.
The equation \(\kappa^v=-(a+b\kappa^u)/(b+a\kappa^u)\) gives \((v-u)h(\kappa)\le\log(|a|+|b|)\) via the height of the two linear forms; their determinant is nonzero because the two coefficients are coprime of absolute value at least two.
This contradicts (3).
In either irreducible case the primitive polynomial is nonmonic up to sign and hence has no algebraic-unit root, also ruling out nontorsion units at every dilation.

For an explicit family set \(|a|=q=2^r,\ |b|=q+1,\ u=5\), with \(r\ge7\) odd and \(3\nmid r\).
Choose any \(s,t\ge2\), set \(Q=2^s3^t\), and take \(Q\le v<31Q\) with
\[
 v\equiv 5+2^s\bmod 2^{s+1},\qquad
 v\equiv-5+3^t\bmod 3^{t+1},\qquad v\equiv1\bmod5 .
\]
The Chinese remainder theorem gives one such representative modulo \(30Q\).
Then no \(P(X^g)\) has a nontorsion unit root for any \(g\ge1\) and either sign of each coefficient.
Indeed \(v\) is odd, coprime to five, and \(2^s\mid D\) by the ordinary dyadic result since \(4\mid a\).
Here \(v_3(2^r+1)=1\), by expanding \((-1+3)^r\) modulo nine.
At three, the [odd-prime degree congruence](Yin_C_G0901_v123.md#statement) for \(v+5\) has no exceptional correction, since the sign is positive and the endpoint is even.
Thus \(3^t\mid D\) and \(D\ge Q\).
The [finite height and exterior-sum bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement) give \(D\log((q+1)/2)\le3(v+5)/q\), contradicting \(v+5<32Q\) and \(q\ge128\).
The respective cofactors \((v-5)/2^s\) and \((v+5)/3^t\) can both grow arbitrarily fast relative to \(q\), by choosing both powers after \(r\).
The exclusion uses only the prime two on the outer coefficient, even when its valuation is unbounded.
In each excluded family every positive-measure integral divisor of a dilation has log measure at least \(\log2\).
