# Simultaneous approximation of Laurent coefficients by integer traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exponent of a full coefficient block

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and write \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
Let \(\Omega\) be the roots in a splitting field \(L\subset\mathbb C\), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), and \(I=\{\rho\in\Omega:|\rho|<1\}\).
Define the integer
\[
 r=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_{gI}-\mathbf1_I:g\in G\}.
\]
Fix any integral Laurent polynomial \(A\) with \(A(\alpha)\ne0\), and let \(h_j\) be the real Laurent coefficients of \(A/P\) on the unit circle.
For any \(d\) consecutive integers \(B\), write \(D(t)=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mathbb Z)\) and \(\Delta_B(k)=\max_{j\in B}D(kh_j)\).
Then \(r\ge1\), the images of \(\{h_j:j\in B\}\) span a space of dimension exactly \(r\) in \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Q\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and
\[
        \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\Delta_B(k)}{\log k}=\frac1r .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Here \(k\) ranges over positive integers.
In particular the upper bound on the exponent includes exceptional scalar sequences.
For \(0\le\delta\le1/2\), the upper natural density of multipliers satisfying \(\Delta_B(k)\le\delta\) is at most \((2\delta)^r\).
For \(T>0\) the upper natural density satisfying \(\sum_{j\in B}D(kh_j)^2\le T\) is at most \(\min\{1,(2\pi\mathrm e T/r)^{r/2}\}\).

There is a nontorsion unit \(\beta\) of degree \(D_0\le 2^r\) with \(0<\log M(\beta)\le m\).
Consequently \(r\ge\log_2 d\) at every strict degree record for positive unit Mahler measure.
For all inputs one has
\[
 r>\frac{(2/m)^{1/3}-\log 3}{\log 2}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For sufficiently small \(m\), putting \(a=(4m)^{-1/3}\) gives the stronger \(r>a\log a/\log 2\).
Thus the optimal scalar exponent in (1) tends to zero on every shrinking sequence.
These exponents concern a fixed input and block at a time.
They give no onset uniform in the input and no upper bound for the energy of both infinite tails.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Galois conjugates of the partial trace

Write \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), which contains \(\alpha^{-1}\), and \(b=A(\alpha)/P'(\alpha)\ne0\).
The [trace dual](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#conductor) is \(R^\vee=R/P'(\alpha)\), so \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(b\alpha^n)\in\mathbb Z\) for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\).
For \(y\in K\), denote by \(y_\rho\) its embedding at \(\alpha\mapsto\rho\), and put \(F(y)=\sum_{\rho\in I}y_\rho\).
Complex conjugation preserves \(I\), so this sum is real.
Replace \(A\) by its integral polynomial remainder of degree less than \(d\), which changes the Laurent coefficients of the quotient by integers.
The resulting proper rational function has residues \(b_\rho\).
Its [partial fraction expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails) has coefficient at \(-n-1\) equal to \(F(b\alpha^n)\) for \(n\ge0\), and to minus the exterior sum for \(n<0\).
The full trace is integral, hence on the whole axis
\[
                  h_{-n-1}\equiv F(b\alpha^n)\pmod{\mathbb Z}.
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]

Consider \(F\) as a rational linear map \(K\to L/\mathbb Q\), and denote its kernel by \(W\).
Its rank is \(r\).
Indeed, after extending scalars to \(L\), the \(d\) embeddings give coordinates on \(K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}L\), as follows from the invertible Vandermonde matrix for the powers of \(\alpha\).
Likewise the coordinates on \(L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}L\) are its distinct embeddings, using a primitive element for \(L\).
In the target modulo the diagonal copy of \(L\), subtract the identity coordinate from each other coordinate.
Since \(gF(y)=\sum_{\rho\in gI}y_\rho\), the matrix of the extended map in these coordinates has exactly the rows defining \(r\).
Scalar extension preserves rank, and transitivity with \(I\) nonempty and proper ensures that at least one difference row is nonzero.
In any \(d\) consecutive powers the elements \(b\alpha^n\) form a rational basis of \(K\).
This proves the dimension assertion by (3).

There is also an exact finite cancellation when the residue class may be chosen.
Given \(N\) prescribed indices with \(Nr<d\), there is a nonzero class for which all their centered coefficients vanish.
Indeed requiring \(b\alpha^n\in W\) has rational codimension \(r\) for each prescribed \(n\), so the common conditions have a nonzero solution in \(K\).
Multiply \(b\) by a positive integer to put it in \(R^\vee\) and all the specified rational sums \(F(b\alpha^n)\) in \(\mathbb Z\).
Then \(bP'(\alpha)\) is a nonzero integral residue class realizing the claim.

<a id="approximation"></a>
## Scalar multipliers, including exceptional subsequences

Choose \(r\) coefficients \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\) in \(B\) whose images form a basis modulo \(\mathbb Q\).
They are real algebraic numbers, and \(1,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\) are rationally independent.
Write every \(h_j\) in \(B\) as a rational combination of these \(r+1\) numbers with one common positive denominator \(q\).
For each integer \(H\ge2\), partition the unit \(r\)-cube into \(H^r\) equal boxes and compare the \(H^r+1\) multiples of the vector \(\theta\) starting at zero.
Two in one box give \(1\le n\le H^r\) with \(\max_iD(n\theta_i)\le H^{-1}\le n^{-1/r}\).
These \(n\) are unbounded by irrationality.
Taking \(k=qn\) and using the integer coefficients of the cleared relations gives \(\Delta_B(k)\ll_{B,A,P}k^{-1/r}\) infinitely often.

Conversely, suppose that for a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and unbounded \(k\) all \(D(k\theta_i)\le k^{-1/r-\epsilon}\).
Take nearest integers \(p_i\) and the rational vector \(v=(k,p_1,\ldots,p_r)\).
Use the [Subspace Theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations) over \(L\) with all archimedean places.
For each such place choose a representing automorphism \(g\) in the fixed complex embedding of \(L\).
Use the independent forms \(X_0\) and \(X_i-g^{-1}(\theta_i)X_0\) for \(1\le i\le r\) at that place.
Their absolute values on rational vectors before the local normalizing exponent are \(k\) and \(|p_i-k\theta_i|\).
The archimedean exponents sum to one, so the sum of the logarithms of the ratios of the vector norm to these form values is at least \((r+1+r\epsilon)\log k-O(1)\).
The rational projective height of \(v\) is at most \(\log k+O(1)\), since its integral coordinates are \(O(k)\).
Thus the approximation vectors lie in finitely many proper hyperplanes, allowing bounded projective heights as well since there are only finitely many such rational points.
The rational points in each proper \(L\)-hyperplane satisfy a nonzero rational linear equation, obtained by expanding the coefficients in a rational basis of \(L\).
Along an infinite subsequence in one such hyperplane, division by \(k\) and passage to the limit gives a rational dependence of \(1,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\).
This contradiction proves (1).

The sequence \(k\theta\bmod1\) is Haar equidistributed on the whole \(r\)-torus by the [character criterion](../audits/literature/Tao10/Yin_U001.md#tao10-u001) and the finite geometric sum.
The simultaneous condition restricts these coordinates to a box of Haar volume \((2\delta)^r\).
For the energy condition they lie in the intersection of the centered unit cube with the Euclidean ball of squared radius \(T\).
Both sets have null boundary.
For \(s>0\) the ball volume is at most \(e^{sT}\int_{\mathbb R^r}e^{-s\|u\|^2}\,du=e^{sT}(\pi/s)^{r/2}\).
Taking \(s=r/(2T)\) proves the stated density bounds.

<a id="descent"></a>
## A smaller unit from equal interior memberships

Center every vector \(\mathbf1_{gI}\) by subtracting \((|I|/d)\mathbf1_\Omega\).
Their span equals the difference-row span because their average is zero by transitivity.
Choose \(r\) centered vectors forming a basis, and label each root by its membership bits for their \(r\) underlying subsets.
Two roots with the same label belong to precisely the same subsets in the entire orbit of \(I\), since equality of their coordinates on the basis implies equality on every centered row.
Thus the partition by labels is \(G\)-invariant and has at most \(2^r\) parts.
Each part lies wholly in the interior or wholly in the exterior.
The products of the roots in the parts form one Galois multiset of algebraic units, because \(G\) acts transitively on the parts.
Choose one of these products \(\beta\).
The product in every exterior part has modulus greater than one, and that in every interior part has modulus less than one.
If each distinct conjugate of \(\beta\) occurs \(t\) times in the multiset, then \(t\log M(\beta)=m\), and \(2\le D_0=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\le 2^r\).
This proves the compression and implies \(D_0=d\) at a strict record.

[Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m\ge\log M(\beta)>2/(\log(3D_0))^3\), proving (2).
In particular \(D_0\to\infty\) as \(m\to0\).
When \(\log D_0\ge\mathrm e\), the function \(z\mapsto(\log z)/z\) is decreasing for \(z\ge\log D_0\).
Voutier's theorem then gives \(m>\tfrac14(\log z/z)^3\) for \(z=r\log 2\ge\log D_0\).
Thus \(z>a\log z\ge a\), which implies \(z>a\log a\) for sufficiently small \(m\).

<a id="flips"></a>
## Reciprocal pairs and exact rational coefficients

Suppose \(P\) is reciprocal of degree \(d=2h\) and its splitting group contains each single inverse-pair flip, or contains every double flip and \(h\ge3\).
Write \(K^+=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), the fixed field of the nontrivial involution \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) on \(K\).
Then \(W=K^+\), \(r=h\), and at most one coefficient on the entire integer axis can be rational for each fixed nonzero class.
Furthermore every block of length \(1\le N\le d\) has at least \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) rationally independent coordinates modulo \(\mathbb Q\).
In particular for fixed \(C>0\), the density bound from one full block at \(T=C/m\) is at most \(\min\{1,(4\pi\mathrm e C/(md))^{d/4}\}\).
On any shrinking sequence in this class, \(md\to\infty\) by Voutier's Corollary 2 on the original input.

Exactly one root per inverse pair lies in \(I\).
For \(y\in K^+\) its partial trace is the full trace from \(K^+\), hence rational.
Conversely suppose \(F(y)\) is rational and put \(z_i=y_{\rho_i}-y_{\rho_i^{-1}}\) for the interior choices.
Invariance of the sum under every single flip gives \(z_i=0\), while under every double flip it gives \(z_i+z_j=0\) for every \(i\ne j\).
With at least three labels the latter equations also imply all \(z_i=0\).
Thus \(y\) is fixed by the involution, proving \(W=K^+\).
Rational coefficients at two distinct positions would give \(b\alpha^n,b\alpha^{n'}\in K^+\) by (3).
Their ratio is fixed by inversion, forcing \(\alpha^{2(n-n')}=1\), which is impossible.
For an \(N\)-block set \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{b\alpha^n,\ldots,b\alpha^{n+N-1}\}\) and \(U=V\cap K^+\).
The sum \(U+\alpha U\) is direct because \(\alpha\notin K^+\).
It lies in \(V+\alpha V\), of dimension at most \(N+1\).
Hence \(2\dim U\le N+1\), and (3) gives block rank \(N-\dim U\ge\lfloor N/2\rfloor\).
