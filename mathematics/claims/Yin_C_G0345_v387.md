# Arbitrary power substitutions preserve every large primitive cubic factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility for every substitution exponent at once

Let \(g(T)=T^3-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), and \(A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\) for coprime positive integers \(a,b\).
Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of any nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(A_{a,b}\), set \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(d=\deg P\).
For every \(q\ge1\), \(P(X^q)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and has degree \(qd\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)\) whenever at least one of
\[
                 d\ge1024,\qquad a\ge5717,\qquad |b-3a|\ge2859          \tag{1}
\]
holds.
In particular, outside a finite explicit set of primitive directions, the nontorsion irreducible factors after any common dilation are exactly the individual substitutions of the original factors, with the same multiplicities and measures.
This conclusion does not assume a bound on \(b/a\) or on \(q\).

More precisely, if \(\alpha\) is a \(p\)-th power in \(K\) for any odd prime \(p\), then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d\le6p+12,\qquad d<1024,\qquad p\le167,\\
 a<d(\log3)(\log(3d))^3/p,\qquad
 |b-3a|<d(\log3)(\log(3d))^3/(2p).                                    \tag{2}
 \end{gathered}
\]
The odd-power cases inside these bounds are not decided by (1).

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## The first Frobenius correction at simple residues

Suppose \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) in \(K\).
Then \(\gamma\) is an algebraic integer unit of the same degree, since \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) are integral, both \(\gamma,\gamma^{-1}\) satisfy monic equations over the integers of \(K\), and \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\gamma)\subseteq K\).
Choose a prime above \(p\) in the splitting field of \(-A_{a,b}\), which also contains all conjugates of \(\gamma\).
The [collision bound in characteristic \(p\)](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#inertia) shows that the residues of multiplicity at least two in the reduction of the entire polynomial account for at most twelve roots counted with multiplicity.
Thus at least \(d-12\) conjugates of \(\alpha\) have simple and pairwise distinct residues in that polynomial.
For each such conjugate, inertia fixes it because there is only one root in its residue class.
Its local root field, and hence every element of that field, lies in the maximal unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\).
In the following computation \(\alpha,\gamma\) denote the corresponding conjugates, with valuation normalized by \(v(p)=1\).

Set \(u=\gamma^a,\ v=\gamma^b,\ F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\).
Then \(F(u^p,v^p)=0\), so \(F(u,v)\) has zero residue by Frobenius.
Unramifiedness gives \(v_p(F(u,v))\ge1\) in this normalization, with \(v_p(0)=+\infty\).
The integral polynomial
\[
             J_p(U,V)=\frac{F(U^p,V^p)-F(U,V)^p}{p}
\]
therefore vanishes modulo the prime at \((u,v)\).
The identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) makes \(B(\bar u)\ne0\) at every point of \(\bar F=0\), so \(\bar v=-g(\bar u)/B(\bar u)\).
Substitution on the graph and multiplication by \(B(U)^p\) show that \(\bar u\) is a root of the reduction of
\[
             J_p^*(U)=
             \frac{g(U^p)B(U)^p+(-g(U))^p B(U^p)}p\ \in\mathbb Z[U].    \tag{3}
\]
Integrality follows either from the substitution into \(J_p\) or by polynomial Frobenius.
The degree is at most \(6p\), and the coefficient of \(U\) equals one.
Indeed the first summand in the numerator has no linear term and the linear term of \((-g(U))^p B(U^p)\) is \(pU\).
In particular (3) stays nonzero modulo every such prime.

The simple residues of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) give distinct residues of \(\gamma\), because their \(p\)-th powers are already distinct.
On the graph, \(\bar u\) determines \(\bar v\), and these two nonzero powers recover \(\bar\gamma\) by the integral Bézout relation between \(a,b\).
Thus at least \(d-12\) distinct roots of (3) occur, proving \(d\le6p+12\).
This argument also allows the same rational factor to have other conjugates at ramified primes over \(p\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## Bounding the prime and both exponents

Since \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) with equal field degrees, \(m(\gamma)=m(P)/p\).
The parent has coefficient norm at most \(\sqrt8\) in \(\ell^2\), and Jensen's inequality gives \(m(P)\le\log\sqrt8\).
For \(d\ge16\) all six parent exponents are distinct, since a coincidence requires \(b=a,2a\) or \(3a\), which by primitivity gives \(a=1\) and total degree at most six.
In that range \(m(P)\le\log\sqrt6\).
By [Voutier's theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height),
\[
                 p<4\log\sqrt6\,(\log d/\log\log d)^3\qquad(d\ge16).
\]
The function \((\log d/\log\log d)^3/d\) decreases for \(d\ge1024\), by logarithmic differentiation.
At \(d=1024\), using \(\log 1024<6.94,\ \log\log 1024>1.93,\ \log\sqrt6<0.9\), one has \(12+6\cdot3.6(6.94/1.93)^3<1024\).
The bound \(d\le6p+12\) is therefore impossible for \(d\ge1024\).
On \(16\le d<1024\), the ratio \(\log d/\log\log d\) increases, giving \(p<3.6(6.94/1.93)^3<168\).
For \(2\le d<16\), Corollary 2 of the same source gives \(p<(\log\sqrt8)(\log(3d))^3/2<30\).
A rational algebraic unit is torsion, so these ranges exhaust the possibilities.

Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic height.
The [two height estimates for this graph](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#frobenius) give
\[
                 |b-3a|h(\alpha)\le\log3,\qquad
                 a h(\alpha)\le2\log3 .
\]
Their proofs use the unit graph identity and the monic equation for \(\alpha^a\) for every nontorsion root, before inserting any degree bound.
Corollary 2 gives \(h(\alpha)=p h(\gamma)>2p/(d(\log(3d))^3)\).
Substitution proves the last two inequalities of (2).
Since \(d/p\le6+12/p\le10\), \(\log3<1.1\) and \(\log(3072)<8.04\), they require \(a<5717,\ |b-3a|<2859\).

<a id="substitutions"></a>
## Adjoining roots of arbitrary composite order

Under (1) the preceding argument excludes every odd prime power condition \(\alpha\in K^p\) with \(p\) prime.
The [dyadic theorem for the same graph](Yin_C_G0345_v380.md#statement) excludes both \(\alpha\in K^2\) and \(-\alpha\in K^2\) for every primitive direction.
For a field of characteristic zero and a nonzero element \(z\), a reducible binomial \(X^p-z\) with \(p\) prime implies \(z\) is a \(p\)-th power.
Indeed the product of the roots of any proper monic factor of degree \(j\), \(1\le j<p\), belongs to the field and has \(p\)-th power \(z^j\).
Since \((j,p)=1\), Bézout's identity gives a \(p\)-th root of \(z\) in the field.

Induct on the number of prime factors of \(q\), with repetition.
If \(X^m-\alpha\) is already irreducible over \(K\) and \(\eta\) is a root, its norm to \(K\) is \((-1)^{m+1}\alpha\).
If \(\eta\) were a \(p\)-th power in \(K(\eta)\), its norm would be a \(p\)-th power in \(K\).
For odd \(p\) the sign can be absorbed into the power, and for \(p=2\) both signs have been excluded.
It follows that adjoining a \(p\)-th root of \(\eta\) has degree \(p\) over \(K(\eta)\), proving the induction step for \(mp\).
Over \(\mathbb Q\) the full degree is \(dq\) because the \(q\)-th power generates \(K\).
This proves irreducibility of \(P(X^q)\), and taking every \(q\)-th root of each original conjugate preserves its total logarithmic measure.
Distinct base factors stay disjoint after substitution, and all lifts of torsion roots are torsion, giving the stated factorization conclusion.
