# Archimedean upper bounds for dispersed terminal exponent lists

<a id="statement"></a>
## A count without a span restriction

Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be algebraic and suppose some conjugate \(\gamma\) has \(H=|\log|\gamma||>0\).
Fix an ordered tuple \(\mathbf b\) of \(r\ge3\) nonzero rational integers.
Let \(N\) count strictly increasing integer lists, up to translation, for which \(\sum_i b_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) and no nonempty proper subsum vanishes.
Write \(H_2\) for the product of \(1+|b_i|\) after omitting the two largest magnitudes.
For every \(2\le k\le r\),
\[
       N\le C^r H_2^2
          \left(1+\frac{2\log 2}{H}\right)^{r-k} B_k,\qquad
       B_2=1,\quad B_k=(8(k-1))^{4(k-1)^4(2k-1)}\quad(k\ge3),       \tag{1}
\]
with absolute \(C\).
This includes every placement of the exponents in widely separated intervals.

Consider now the [coefficient-weighted witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement) with weight exponent two on a sequence of units of shrinking total measure \(m=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Allow either an exact unit-circle conjugate or the [near-neutral hypothesis](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement) with fixed \(K\).
Suppose some conjugate satisfies \(H\ge\eta m\) with fixed \(\eta>0\), as holds with \(\eta=1\) at Salem numbers.
Write \(L=\log(1/m)\).
Every such witness collection of size at least \(H_2^2(c/m)^{r-2}\), with fixed \(c>0\) and \(r\to\infty\), satisfies
\[
                  r\gg_{c,\eta} L^{5/4}/(\log(2+L))^{1/4}.              \tag{2}
\]
Set \(q=\lceil 2\log(2)/H\rceil\) and count occupied classes modulo \(q\) on each original list.
For some \(\delta>0\) depending only on \(c,\eta\), all but a proportion at most \(e^{-r}\) occupy at least \(\delta\min(r,q)\) classes for all sufficiently late inputs.
If \(r=o(q)\), the fraction of the \(r\) positions represented by distinct classes, namely the number of occupied classes divided by \(r\), tends to one in probability for a uniformly chosen list.
These counts allow the occupied classes to vary with the list.

<a id="forest"></a>
## Merging comparable summands

For fixed nonzero algebraic coefficients in the chosen embedding, first count labeled exponent arrays modulo simultaneous translation.
In every nondegenerate zero sum there are two terms whose moduli differ by a factor of at most two.
Indeed, if all successive moduli in increasing order had ratios greater than two, the sum of all but the largest modulus would be strictly smaller than the largest.
Merge any such pair and repeat until \(k\) groups remain.
A group is represented by its least label \(i\) and its value has the form \(A\gamma^{t_i}\), with \(A\) determined by the relative exponents within the group.
Every group coefficient is nonzero, and any proper zero subsum of the remaining groups would be a proper zero subsum of the original list.

The merges give a forest of unordered rooted binary trees on the \(r\) labeled leaves, with \(k\) components.
Every forest occurs by pruning a full rooted binary tree at a subset of its nodes, as one can complete its roots by binary merges.
The number of such forests is at most \(2^{2r}(2r-3)!!\) by the [labeled tree enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v655.md#trees).
For a fixed forest, reconstruct its components upwards.
Given nonzero child coefficients \(A,D\) and representatives \(i,j\), the condition on their moduli gives
\[
           \left|\log|D/A|+(t_j-t_i)\log|\gamma|\right|\le\log 2 .
\]
The interval for the integer difference has length \(2\log(2)/H\), so there are at most \(1+2\log(2)/H\) choices.
Each choice recovers the parent coefficient, and there are \(r-k\) such nodes.

Once their internal differences have been fixed, the \(k\) roots have fixed nonzero algebraic coefficients.
Divide by the term of one root and move it to the right.
The other \(k-1\) powers form a tuple in \(\langle\gamma\rangle^{k-1}\), of rank \(k-1\), and no nonempty subsum of the left side vanishes.
[Amoroso and Viada, Theorem 6.2](../audits/literature/AV09/Yin_U001.md#retained-primary-statement) bounds its nondegenerate solutions by \(B_k\) for \(k\ge3\).
This uses the inspected theorem statement with the proof limits recorded there, as in the [count for translated groups](Yin_C_G0345_v655.md#blocks).
At \(k=2\) the single relative exponent is unique because \(|\gamma|\ne1\).
The relative exponents of all roots and all internal differences recover the entire array up to translation.

For integer coefficients let \(n_b\) denote the multiplicity of signed value \(b\).
Permuting labels of equal coefficient in a strictly increasing list gives \(\prod_b n_b!\) different labeled arrays modulo translation, disjoint for different original translation classes.
A finite nonempty set of positions has no nonzero translation fixing it.
The [multinomial estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v662.md#multiplicities) with one interval gives
\[
            r!/\prod_b n_b!\le r(r-1) Z^{r-2}H_2^2,\qquad
            Z=\sum_{b\ne0}(1+|b|)^{-2}.
\]
Since \((2r-3)!!\le 2^{r-1}(r-1)!\), division of the labeled bound by the permutation count gives (1).

<a id="weight"></a>
## Choosing how many groups to retain

The marked term can occupy at most \(r\) places in the full coefficient order.
At a fixed place, a translation class recovers the marked list by putting the marked term at zero.
For sufficiently late inputs the [terminal positions are distinct](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive).
Compare the witness lower count with \(r\) times (1).
Since \(H\ge\eta m\), there are constants \(A=A(c,\eta)>0\) and absolute \(D>0\) such that simultaneously for all \(2\le k\le r\),
\[
                       (k-2)L\le Ar+Dk^5\log(2k).                       \tag{3}
\]
Taking \(k=r\) and using \(r\to\infty\) first gives \(r\gg_{c,\eta}(L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\), by separating \(r\ge L\) and \(r<L\).
Now take \(k=\lfloor a(L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\rfloor\) with a sufficiently small fixed \(a>0\).
Then \(k\le r\) and \(Dk^5\log(2k)\le kL/4\) eventually.
Thus (3) gives \(Ar\ge kL/2\) for large \(L\), proving (2).

<a id="classes"></a>
## Counting lists with few occupied residue classes

Fix any \(v\) residue classes modulo \(q\), with representatives in \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\).
Count translation classes of original lists having some translate in their union.
For each labeled array with this property choose one such translate, and denote by \(s_i\) its residues and by \(u_i=(t_i-s_i)/q\) its quotient exponents.
There are at most \(v^r\) choices for the vector of residues.
Once it is chosen, the equation has fixed coefficients \(b_i\gamma^{s_i}\) and base \(\gamma^q\).
Its labeled quotient arrays modulo translation recover the original labeled arrays modulo translation by restoring all \(s_i\).
The forest bound at \(k=2\) applies to these algebraic coefficients before any permutation division, and \(1+2\log(2)/(qH)\le2\).
Every permutation of labels of equal original coefficient is still counted, including permutations across different residue vectors.
Consequently the same division as above bounds the original sorted lists for this fixed union by \((C_1 v)^rH_2^2\).

There are \(\binom qv\le(eq/v)^v\) possible unions and any union of fewer than \(v\) classes can be enlarged to one of size \(v\).
The count of occupied classes is invariant under translation.
Since \(\alpha\) is a unit, \(H\le m\) by the product formula, and \(H\ge\eta m\) gives \(\log q=L+O_\eta(1)\).
After comparison with the marked witness lower bound, the logarithm of the proportion occupying at most \(v\) classes is at most
\[
                    -(r-v)\log(q/v)+O_{c,\eta}(r)+2L,\qquad
                    1\le v\le\min(r,q).
\]
By (2), \(L=o(r)\).
For \(v=\lfloor\delta\min(r,q)\rfloor\), the negative term is at most \(-(1-\delta)r\log(1/\delta)\).
Choosing \(\delta\) small proves the exponential exception bound.
For \(r=o(q)\), take \(v=\lfloor(1-\epsilon)r\rfloor\) at any fixed \(0<\epsilon<1\).
The negative term divided by \(r\) tends to \(-\infty\), as required.
The possibility of large weights occupying many classes remains compatible with a hypothetical shrinking Salem sequence.
