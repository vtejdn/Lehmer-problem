# Fully separated dyadic roots with a period at bounded distance from the real target

<a id="statement"></a>
## One-prime models satisfying the irrational period constraint

Fix a sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, an odd prime \(\ell\), and a positive integer \(B\).
Put \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ J=q/2+1,\ u=2^B\), and \(\theta=u\log p/\log s\).
There exist arbitrarily large degrees \(d=2D\) and monic irreducible reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) satisfying all the [joint moment, pole and capacity conditions](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement).
Write \(T\) for the unique exterior root, \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\), and \(A\) for the order of \(Z\) modulo \((2,F)\).
Simultaneously,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
   c q p^{-d}<s-T<C q p^{-d},\qquad
   |A-\theta d|\le C_{q,B},\\
   |(A+u)\log T-u\log Y|\le C'_{q,B}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The constants in the pinning interval are absolute.
Every pair of distinct dyadic roots has distance one, so every closed disk of radius \(1/8\) contains at most one root.
With \(v=A+u\), the exponents \(u,v\) are coprime and \(Z^v=((pZ-q)/(p-qZ))^u\) in the full quotient modulo \((2^B,F)\).
At sufficiently large degrees they obey the [local reconstruction size bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities), and the periods satisfy the [small-denominator repulsion inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) for each fixed \(0<\eta\le1/4\), with an effective choice of its constant.

The precision \(B\) is specified before the degree and the constants in the two period estimates can depend on \(q,B\).
The primitive minimum has Mahler measure \(\ell^a T\), with the admissible denominator exponents of the joint construction.
In particular \(T,Y\) are multiplicatively independent by the [finite-place height obstruction](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement).
These controls test the effect of the real period estimate on concentration after releasing integrality at one odd prime.

<a id="period"></a>
## Choosing the period and an admissible degree

Let \(M=pq\) and \(K=(2q+1)^2\).
As in the [strengthened pole congruences](Yin_C_G0901_v184.md#word), require \(p^D\equiv1+2q\bmod q^{J+1+B}\), \((1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^{J+1}\), and the rotation conditions of the joint construction.
The principal-unit and Chinese remainder argument provides a fixed progression \(D_0\bmod P\) of admissible degrees, and all prime divisors of \(P\) are at most \(p\).
Take a fixed odd prime \(t>q+B+2\).
Choose unbounded odd primes \(a>t\) and for each choose a prime \(a<b<2a\).
Set \(r=ab,\ n=2^r-1\), and choose the integer \(S\) nearest \(n/(2\theta)\) among those that satisfy \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P\).
This is possible since \(t\) is prime to \(P\), with
\[
             |S-n/(2\theta)|\le P/2 .
\]
We will realize \(D=tS\) and \(A=tn\), giving \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tP\).

Inside \(\mathbb F_{2^r}^{\times}\) group the nonidentity elements under Frobenius and inversion.
The Frobenius degrees are \(a,b,r\), because the only maximal proper subfields have degrees \(a,b\) and their intersection is \(\mathbb F_2\).
No nonidentity element is conjugate to its inverse: inversion on a Frobenius orbit that it preserves is a commuting permutation of order dividing two, hence a shift of order dividing two in an odd cycle.
Such a shift is the identity, and \(z=z^{-1}\) forces \(z=1\) in characteristic two.
Thus the blocks have respective sizes \(2a,2b,2r\), with counts
\[
       \frac{2^a-2}{2a},\qquad \frac{2^b-2}{2b},\qquad
       \frac{2^r-2^a-2^b+2}{2r}.
\]
A primitive \(n\)-th root lies in one of the blocks of size \(2r\).

We select blocks of total size \(2S\) including this primitive block.
Use \(j=\lfloor(S-r)/r\rfloor\) of the largest blocks, including the required one.
There are enough for all large \(a\): \(j\sim n/(2r\theta)\), the count of these blocks is asymptotic to \(n/(2r)\), and \(\theta>1\).
The remainder \(E=S-rj\) lies in \([r,2r)\).
Choose \(0\le i<b\) with \(ai\equiv E\bmod b\), then \(E-ai\ge r-a(b-1)>0\).
Thus \(E=ai+bj'\) with \(i,j'\) nonnegative and at most \(2r\).
For sufficiently large \(a\) each of the two smaller block counts exceeds \(2r\), since \(a<b<2a\).
Taking these blocks gives the required total.

<a id="lift"></a>
## The residue word, its integral pole representative and the graph congruence

Lift the selected residue elements to the unique \(n\)-th roots of unity with those residues in the unramified degree-\(r\) extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\).
These lifts exist by simple-root Hensel lifting, and commute with Frobenius and inversion by uniqueness.
The monic product \(V\) of their linear factors lies in \(\mathbb Z_2[Z]\) by Frobenius invariance.
It has degree \(2S\), constant one and is reciprocal by the inverse pairing.
It divides \(Z^n-1\).
Consequently \(U(Z)=V(Z^t)\) has degree \(d=2tS\) and divides \(Z^{tn}-1\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\).
Modulo two it is squarefree, since \(tn\) is odd.
It contains a primitive \(tn\)-th root among its residual roots.
Indeed \(t\nmid n\), because otherwise the order of \(2\bmod t\) would divide both \(ab\) and \(t-1\), hence be one.
On the cyclic group of \(n\)-th roots the \(t\)-th power is therefore a bijection, so a primitive \(n\)-th root in the chosen block has a primitive \(n\)-th root as a preimage.
Multiplying this preimage by a primitive \(t\)-th root supplies the required primitive \(tn\)-th root.
This proves that the full binary period equals \(A=tn\).

Lift \(V\bmod2^B\) coefficientwise to an ordinary monic reciprocal integer polynomial with constant one, and substitute \(Z^t\) to obtain \(U_0\).
All offsets \(1,\ldots,t-1\) from either end vanish.
Thus \(q^dU_0(s)\) reduces to \(p^d\bmod q^{J+1+B}\) and \(q^d\bmod p^{J+1}\), which give \(K\) by the degree choices.
In particular \(q^d U_0(s)\equiv K\bmod 2^B M^{J+1}\).
The [pole increments with \(J\) prescribed zeros](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) have gcd \(M^{J+1}\).
Correct the pole exactly by adding \(2^B\) times an integral combination of free reciprocal coefficient pairs and the center.
This integral pole representative has the prescribed prefix and residue \(U\bmod2^B\).
Apply the [one-prime approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) with this residue and with the real neighborhood given by the [two-sided pinning estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v184.md#pin).
The resulting \(F\) has the stated irreducibility, root locations, zero initial moments, powered capacities and primitive denominator cost.
As its binary reduction is squarefree, distinct roots have distinct residues.

In the free quotient modulo \((2^B,F)\), writing \(z\) for the image of \(Z\), we have \(z^A=1\), since the entire \(U\) divides \(Z^A-1\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\).
Both \(z\) and \(p-qz\) are units, and \(W=(pz-q)/(z(p-qz))\equiv1\bmod2\).
Repeated squaring gives \(W^u=1\) in this quotient for \(u=2^B\).
Hence \(z^{A+u}=((pz-q)/(p-qz))^u\), with \(\gcd(A,u)=1\) since \(A\) is odd.
For fixed \(q,B\) and sufficiently large \(d\), the short exponent meets \(u<(\log(3d))^3/(2q)\), the quotient \((v-u)/A=1\) meets \(1<2\log_2(2q+1)\), and \(A\log T/(ud)\to\log p\), above the norm threshold \((\log 2)/2\).
The uniqueness regime of the dyadic reconstruction theorem requires the precision to exceed a degree-dependent bound; the present modulus is fixed before choosing \(d\).

<a id="arch"></a>
## Bounded real error and exclusion of small-denominator slopes

The bounds \(1<pT-q<3\) and \(p-qT=q(s-T)\asymp q^2p^{-d}\) give
\[
                 \log Y=d\log p-2\log q+O(1).
\]
The term \((A+u)|\log T-\log s|\) tends to zero at fixed \(q,B\) by the pinning interval and \(A=O_{q,B}(d)\).
Combining \(\theta\log s=u\log p\) with \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tP\) proves the claimed bounded real logarithmic error.

To check the repulsion at the same growing denominator range, fix \(0<\eta\le1/4\), and suppose \(|b_0 A-md|\le d^{1-\eta}\) with \(1\le b_0\le d^{c_\eta/\log^2(2q+1)}\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\).
Then
\[
 |m\log s-b_0u\log p|
       \le (\log s)\big(d^{-\eta}+b_0\theta tP/d\big).
\]
The [two-logarithm lower-bound argument](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#forms) applies to this same nonzero form with \(k=1\).
Indeed at each fixed \(q,B\) and all sufficiently large degrees we have \(u,q,\theta\le(\log(3d))^4\), so \(|m|,b_0u\ll b_0(\log(3d))^4\).
The modified heights remain \(O(\log(2q+1))\).
The same effective choice of \(c_\eta\), decreased if needed, ensures a lower bound \(d^{-\eta/2}\) and \(b_0\le d^{1/8}\).
The displayed upper bound is \(o_{q,B}(d^{-\eta/2})\), which is a contradiction.
Thus this squarefree family also survives the period-slope exclusion at every fixed height and precision for sufficiently large degrees.
In particular a sequence with \(q\) and the prescribed precision both increasing can be formed by choosing the degrees successively after these parameters.
The construction addresses fixed-precision equations and bounded real error; the nonzero common-power obstruction at the missing prime persists at every degree.
