# A uniform factor gap for nonreciprocal integer polynomials of coefficient length at most six

<a id="statement"></a>
## The remaining reciprocal multiples

For a nonzero integer polynomial \(F\) put \(\ell(F)=\sum_j|[X^j]F|\), remove any common monomial, and put \(N=\deg F\) after this removal.
Write \(J^*(X)=X^{\deg J}J(1/X)\) for a polynomial with nonzero constant term.
There is an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that if \(\ell(F)\le6\) and \(F^*\ne\pm F\), then every integral divisor of \(F\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure has log measure at least \(\eta\).

More precisely, suppose \(\ell(F)\le6\) along a sequence and a monic irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(P\) has log measure \(m\to0\).
Eventually, up to an overall sign,
\[
 F=A(X)+\epsilon X^{N-t}A^*(X),\qquad
 \ell(A)=3,\quad t=\deg A>0,\quad t/N\to0,\quad \epsilon=\pm1.       \tag{1}
\]
The low block \(A\) has either three distinct terms of coefficient modulus one or two terms of coefficient moduli one and two.
In both cases, with \(d=\deg P\),
\[
        d/N\to0,\qquad Nm/d-\log M(A)\longrightarrow0.              \tag{2}
\]
For a binomial block the comparison value is always \(\log 2\).
Conversely, for any sequence of parents of the form (1) with \(0<t<N/2\), a noncyclotomic irreducible **unit** factor with \(d/N\to0\) has \(m\to0\).
Thus the degree test on these two families covers the small-factor existence question for all integer parents of coefficient length at most six, with arbitrary exponents and any number of exterior conjugates.
The [Salem reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v174.md#statement) also restricts the signs and the scale using its real exterior root; no Salem hypothesis is used here.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Two short blocks of length three

It suffices to treat a sequence of irreducible factors with \(m\to0\); every positive-measure integral divisor contains such a factor of no greater measure or has content of absolute value at least two.
For \(m<\log 2\), a positive-measure irreducible factor has leading and constant coefficients of absolute value one, after removing any monomial and normalizing the sign.
By [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) it is eventually reciprocal.
The [length-five gap](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) implies \(\ell(F)=6\) eventually.
Represent each coefficient by copies of its sign, ordered with repetition of exponents, so the six positions are \(0=e_0\le e_1\le\cdots\le e_5=N\).

For \(i=0,1,3,4\), if \(e_{i+1}-e_i\ge\delta N>0\), then one side of that gap has length at most two.
Viewed from the appropriate end it is an integer monomial after shifting, or a signed binomial of the form \(\pm(X^v\pm1)\).
An integer monomial gives a constant after shifting, including when both positions agree.
The [one-block gap for products of signed binomials](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) gives a positive lower bound depending only on \(\delta\), contradicting \(m\to0\) along any such subsequence.
Consequently \(W=\max(e_2,N-e_3)=o(N)\) and the middle gap eventually separates the first three positions from the last three.
Write \(F=A+X^n B\), with \(\ell(A)=\ell(B)=3,\ A(0)B(0)\ne0,\ n=e_3\), and \(\max(\deg A,\deg B)=W\).
Both blocks are normalized at their first occupied exponent.

<a id="correlation"></a>
## A short relation between the two blocks

Let \(x\) be a root of \(P\).
Apply \(F=0\) at \(x\) and \(1/x\), which are conjugate, and multiply to obtain
\[
        A(x)A(1/x)=B(x)B(1/x).
\]
Write \(T_J=J(X)J(1/X)\).
The Laurent difference \(T_A-T_B\) has width at most \(2W\) and at most eighteen nonzero terms.
If this difference is nonzero, the [width bound for a sparse annihilator](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) yields \(2Wm/d\gg1\).
But at sufficiently small \(m\) the [cut bound for at most seven terms](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \(Nm/d\le5\log6\), since both the coefficient length and the number of nonzero terms are at most six.
This contradicts \(W/N\to0\), proving \(T_A=T_B\) identically for all sufficiently late indices.

We recover a polynomial of length three from this Laurent product up to sign and reversal.
Its constant Laurent coefficient is \(9,5,3\) in the monomial, binomial and trinomial cases respectively.
A monomial normalized at zero is a constant.
For a binomial the largest distance and its coefficient determine the width and the sign of the endpoint product; the two endpoint magnitudes are one and two in either order.
For a trinomial write its support as \(0,p,t\) with \(0<p<t\).
The largest positive Laurent exponent is \(t\) and its coefficient is the endpoint product.
If \(p\ne t-p\), both intermediate distances \(p,t-p\) have nonzero coefficients, and after a possible reversal their positions and the two products determine all signs up to an overall sign.
If \(p=t/2\), the only possible intermediate coefficient is the middle sign times the sum of the endpoint signs.
It determines the middle sign after choosing one endpoint sign when the endpoints agree.
For opposite endpoints the two choices of middle sign are exchanged by reversing and negating.
These cases are distinguishable because unequal intermediate distances always give two nonzero intermediate coefficients.
It follows that \(B=\pm A\) or \(B=\pm A^*\).

If \(B=\pm A\), every nontorsion factor divides \(A\), contradicting the [individual-factor gap for trinomials](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement).
For a monomial \(A\), both alternatives are signed copies.
This leaves \(B=\pm A^*\) with a nonconstant block, proving (1) and the claimed uniform gap by contradiction.

<a id="binomial"></a>
## The limiting measure of a binomial block

In the trinomial case the [degree law for reciprocal six-term parents](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#statement) proves (2) and the converse.
In the binomial case write \(A=c_0+c_1X^t\) and \(y=x^t\), where \(\{|c_0|,|c_1|\}=\{1,2\}\).
The endpoints of \(F\) both have modulus \(|c_0|\); the assertion about degrees in the converse is restricted to unit factors, as required for vanishing positive measure.
Take such a factor and set \(U=c_0+c_1y,\ V=c_1+c_0y\).
The four-term [cut bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \(Nm/d\le3\log6\), independent of \(t\).
The [sparse divisor bound](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#dob06-u001) gives \(m\gg d/N\), hence the asserted two-way comparison for unit factors.
At \(t=o(N)\), one has \(h(y)\to0\).
Since \(U=-\epsilon x^{N-t}V\), their ideals agree and \(V\) divides both \(c_1(y^2-1)=yU-V\) and \(c_0(y^2-1)=yV-U\).
The two coefficients are coprime, so \(V\mid y^2-1\).
The [cyclotomic truncation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#norm) applies to \(y^2-1\): its circle log integral is zero, its norm is a nonzero integer, and its truncated logarithm converges by one-dimensional equidistribution.
It follows that \(0\le\mathbb E\log|V|\le\mathbb E\log|y^2-1|\to0\).
Here \(\mathbb E\) averages over embeddings of the root field.
The functions \(U,V\) have no common complex zero since \(c_0^2-c_1^2=\pm3\).
Their maximum modulus is bounded below by \(3/(|c_0|+|c_1|)=1\), by eliminating \(y\).
Its positive logarithm is at most \(\log3+\log^+|y|\), so its mean tends to the circle integral of \(\log|c_0+c_1y|\), namely \(\log2\), by the same truncation at infinity.
The identity \((N-t)h(x)=\mathbb E\log\max(|U|,|V|)-\mathbb E\log|V|\) proves (2).
