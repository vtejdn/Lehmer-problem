# Rational approximation to the logarithmic critical slopes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds for every rational approximant

Let \(\lambda\) be any zero in the slope coordinate of the [ordinary](Yin_C_G0345_v847.md#spacing), [horizontal](Yin_C_G0345_v846.md#centers) or [exceptional ordinary](Yin_C_G0345_v849.md#centers) preparation, or one of the three slope images of zeros in a [deep cubic disk](Yin_C_G0345_v848.md#centers).
Use the chart in which the center was constructed and normalize \(v_p(p)=1\).
For any finite rational approximant \(r\) in that chart, write its direction as a primitive signed pair \((k,l)\), so \(r=k/l\) or \(l/k\) respectively, and put \(N=\max(|k|,|l|)\), \(L=\log(2+N)\).
Write \(g\) for the order of the subgroup generated by the two coordinates of the geometric residue point of the disk.
Uniformly in the prime, disk and direction,
\[
 v_p(r-\lambda)\ll_F \min\left\{
 p^{16}(1+L/\log p)^2(L+\log p),\quad g(1+L/\log p)^4\right\}.       \tag{1}
\]
The same bounds apply to the sum of the distances in valuation over all the centers of one preparation, with multiplicity.
Every rational direction in the slope disk is allowed, including those for which the two residue coordinates cannot arise from a section root.
In particular, for each fixed \(p\) there is a constant \(C_{F,p}\) such that all these transcendental centers satisfy
\[
                   |r-\lambda|_p\ge \exp\{-C_{F,p}\log^3(3+N)\}.
\]

<a id="evaluation"></a>
## Evaluating the logarithms at a rational slope

Each center satisfies \(v_p(\lambda-r_0)\ge1\) at its unramified base slope \(r_0\), by the ordinary rescaling or the cubic expansion \(s(b_i)-r_0=(b_i-\beta)^2e(b_i-\beta)\).
For \(r\) outside this disk the ultrametric inequality gives \(v_p(r-\lambda)=v_p(r-r_0)<1\).
Inside the disk the chart denominator is a \(p\)-adic unit by primitivity.
At an ordinary critical point let \((U,V)\) be the lifted coordinates at \(r=k/l\) and \((u,v)\) their reductions, and write \([u],[v]\) for the Teichmüller lifts.
Set \(W=U^l/V^k\).
Since both Teichmüller lifts have order dividing \(g\),
\[
 \log W^g=gl\big(\log(U/[u])-r\log(V/[v])\big)=glH(r).
\]
Here \(W^g\) is an unramified principal unit and \(p\) is odd, so \(v_p(W^g-1)=v_p(H(r))\) because \(p\nmid gl\).
The reciprocal chart interchanges the two coordinates and replaces \(W\) by its inverse, giving the same valuation.
The ordinary preparation gives \(\sum_i v_p(r-\lambda_i)=v_p(H(r))+m-2\), with \(m=2\) except for the allowed degree three at \(p=3\).

In a deep cubic disk write \(r=l/k\), \(V=f(U)\), and \(s(U)=Uf'(U)/f(U)\).
Let \(c_1,c_2\) be the two roots in the disk of \(s(U)=r\), counted with multiplicity, and evaluate \(W_j=c_j^l/f(c_j)^k\).
The [cubic preparation](Yin_C_G0345_v848.md#centers) writes the zeros of \(B(U)=\log(f(U)/[v])-s(U)\log(U/[u])\) in the closed ball \(v_p(U-\beta)\ge1/2\) as \(b_1,b_2,b_3\), with \(\lambda_i=s(b_i)\).
On this ball its prepared product has \(v_p(B(c_j))=\sum_i v_p(c_j-b_i)\), since rescaling the three factors cancels the scalar of valuation \(3/2\).
Conversely evaluating the monic quadratic for \(s(U)-r\) at each \(b_i\) gives \(v_p(\lambda_i-r)=\sum_j v_p(b_i-c_j)\), as its formal unit evaluates to a unit.
Consequently
\[
 \sum_i v_p(r-\lambda_i)=\sum_{j=1}^2 v_p(B(c_j))
                         =\sum_{j=1}^2 v_p(W_j^g-1).                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(\log W_j^g=-gkB(c_j)\), and the normalized units of both coordinates lie in the ball \(v_p(x-1)\ge1/2>1/(p-1)\), where the logarithm preserves valuations of increments.
Both preparations concern the curve and the critical equations at the chosen slope, so this evaluation also works when \(u^l\ne v^k\).
Every distance valuation on these slope disks is nonnegative, and hence the sum bounds each individual term.

<a id="heights"></a>
## Two estimates for the critical coordinates

At every critical point just used the algebraic coordinates satisfy \(F=kUF_U+lVF_V=0\).
The [two projected resultants](Yin_C_G0345_v851.md#elimination) give \([K:\mathbb Q]\le16\) for \(K=\mathbb Q(U,V)\) and \(h(U),h(V)\ll_F L\), for both signs and the axes.
The two coordinates are units at this place, with residue degree \(f\le16\) and \(g\mid p^f-1\).
If \(W\) were torsion, Bézout applied to the primitive direction would put the point on a torsion translate of \((X^k,X^l)\), where the critical equation would give a multiple section zero, contrary to [squarefreeness on all torsion translates](Yin_C_G0345_v852.md#statement).
Thus \(W^g-1\ne0\) even for incompatible residues and for axis directions.

Apply the [displayed consequence of Yu's Theorem 1](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-input-logarithmic-bound) to \(U,V\) with signed exponents \(gl,-gk\) and \(B=\max(3,gN)\).
For odd \(p\) the field condition holds when \(p^f\equiv3\pmod4\), and otherwise adjoining \(i\) preserves the chosen completion since \(-1\) has a simple square root in the residue field.
The global degree is then at most 32, and the normalized valuation divides out the single factor of the ramification index in the two-input bound.
The height product divided by \((f\log p)^2\) is \(O_F((1+L/\log p)^2)\), and \(\log B\ll L+\log p\) since \(g<p^{16}\).
This proves the first bound of (1) for \(v_p(W^g-1)\).

For independent \(U,V\) and \(kl\ne0\) apply [Bugeaud and Laurent, Theorem 3](../audits/literature/BL96/Yin_U001.md#bl96-u001) to the bases \(U^{\operatorname{sgn}l},V^{\operatorname{sgn}k}\) and the positive exponents \(b_1=g|l|, b_2=g|k|\).
The difference of the two powers has valuation \(v_p(W^g-1)\), and the residual parameter of the bases is still \(g\).
Take both logarithmic height majorants \(L_1,L_2\) to equal a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(L+\log p\).
With \(D=[K:\mathbb Q]/f\in[1,16]\), the parameter \(b'=b_1/(DL_2)+b_2/(DL_1)\) satisfies \(\log b'\le \log(gN)+O_F(1)\).
Thus the squared maximum in Theorem 3 costs \(O_F((L+\log p)^2)\), and its prefactor costs \(O_F(g(L+\log p)^2/(\log p)^4)\).
For an axis the nonzero exponent has absolute value one, and the local height inequality directly gives \(v_p(W^g-1)\log p\le16(g h(W)+\log 2)\).

For dependent coordinates, nontorsion of \(W\) forces their subgroup to have rank one.
Choose \(\gamma\) in the subgroup generating its quotient modulo torsion, write \(U=\zeta_1\gamma^i, V=\zeta_2\gamma^j\), and let \(m\) be the order of the torsion subgroup, bounded absolutely in degree at most sixteen.
Then \(h(\gamma)\ll_F L\) and \(|i|+|j|\ll_F L\) by the positive lower bound for the height of a nontorsion element of degree at most sixteen.
The integer \(n=il-jk\) is nonzero, and \(\gamma^g\) is a principal unit.
The local coordinates lie in an unramified field in the ordinary case and in an extension of degree at most two over an unramified field in the cubic case by the prepared quadratic for \(c_j\).
Hence \(v_p(\gamma^g-1)>1/(p-1)\) in both cases, since cubic disks have \(p\ge17\).
Raising \(W^g\) to \(m\) and using the logarithm isometry gives
\[
 v_p(W^g-1)\le v_p(\gamma^{gmn}-1)=v_p(\gamma^g-1)+v_p(mn).
\]
The first term times \(\log p\) is at most \(16(g h(\gamma)+\log 2)\) by the local height inequality, and the second is \(O_F(L/\log p)\) since \(|n|\ll_F NL\).
This proves the second bound of (1) also in rank one.
Applying the two estimates to the ordinary identity or (2) absorbs the possible additive one and the two cubic terms into the constant.

<a id="lifting"></a>
## Heights needed for the prescribed distances at 8447

In the [first horizontal orbit at 8447](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#horizontal-depth), let \(N_a\) be the minimum of \(\max(|k|,|l|)\) over compatible primitive directions in the horizontal disk realizing distance valuation \((a+2)/2\) between the two section roots, for \(a\ge1\).
The ordinary identity gives \(v_p(H(k/l))=a+2\), twice the mutual distance valuation, so (1) yields \(\log(2+N_a)\gg_F (a+2)^{1/3}\) at this fixed prime.
Conversely the cited Hensel construction takes \(l=27\) and \(k=pt\), with \(t\) specified modulo \(p^{a+1}\) and \(t\equiv1\pmod{8448}\).
Taking the least positive Chinese remainder representative gives \(N_a\le8448p^{a+2}\) for \(p=8447\).
The resulting interval between cube-root and linear growth for \(\log(2+N_a)\) leaves open a bound linear in \(\log(2+N)\) for the valuation of approximation to a center at a fixed prime.
