# Irreducible reciprocal quotients at a positive proportion of nearby prime pairs

<a id="statement"></a>
## Many disjoint pairs with a growing block exponent

For primes \(x\le r<q\le2x\), put \(g=q-r\) and, for either \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), define
\[
 Q_\varepsilon(X)=\frac{X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g)}
                          {X+\varepsilon}.
\]
There are absolute constants \(c_1,c_2,c_3>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(x\), there are at least \(c_1 x/\log x\) disjoint pairs with
\[
 c_2\log x\le g\le c_3\log x,\qquad
 \gcd(q-1,r-1)=2,\qquad q\equiv r\equiv3\pmod4.
\]
For both signs at every such selected pair, \(Q_\varepsilon\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), is the entire noncyclotomic part of its numerator, and has logarithmic Mahler measure
\[
              m(Q_\varepsilon)=\log6+O\!\left(g\exp(-c r/(2g+1)^2)\right) .       \tag{1}
\]

For each fixed \(C>0\), consider all prime pairs in the same interval with \(g\le C\log x\), allowing repeated endpoints.
For every \(\eta>0\), the number of pairs at which some \(Q_\varepsilon\) has a monic irreducible rational factor of logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(\eta\) is
\[
                    O_C\!\left(\eta\,\frac{x}{\log x}\right)
                         +o_C\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).                     \tag{2}
\]
The bounds are uniform in \(\eta\).
In particular, in any subcollection of \(\gg x/\log x\) pairs, the proportion admitting such a factor tends to zero if \(\eta=\eta(x)\to0\).

<a id="sieve"></a>
## Two prime values in one residue class

Write \(F(n)=(n/\varphi(n))^2\), with \(F(1)=1\), and fix \(C>0\).
For \(1\le m,j\le C\log x\), the number of integers \(n\) with \(x\le mn+1\le2x\) and both \(mn+1,m(n+j)+1\) prime is at most
\[
                     C_0\,\frac{x}{m(\log x)^2} F(m)F(j)                       \tag{3}
\]
for an absolute \(C_0\) and sufficiently large \(x\) depending on \(C\).
Here is a direct upper-bound sieve proof, keeping the constants independent of the residue class modulus.

Set \(z=x^{1/20}\) and use the primes \(p\le z\) not dividing \(2mj\).
There are exactly two excluded residue classes of \(n\bmod p\), the roots of the two distinct linear forms.
Both prime values exceed \(z\).
Let \(\mathcal D\) be the squarefree \(d\le z\) supported on the sieve primes, including \(1\).
For a squarefree product \(a\) of sieve primes, set \(w(a)=2^{\omega(a)}/a\) and \(b(a)=\prod_{p\mid a}2/(p-2)\), where \(\omega(a)\) counts distinct prime divisors.
The number of \(n\) in the given interval for which \(a\) divides the product of the two forms equals \((x/m)w(a)+O(2^{\omega(a)})\), by the Chinese remainder theorem and counting each class in a real interval of length \(x/m\).
For any real weights \(\lambda_d\) indexed by \(\mathcal D\) with \(\lambda_1=1\), majorize the prime-pair indicator by the square of the sum of \(\lambda_d\) over the divisors of the product of the forms.
Its main term, divided by \(x/m\), is
\[
 \sum_{d,e\in\mathcal D}\lambda_d\lambda_e w([d,e])
       =\sum_{k\in\mathcal D}\frac{Y_k^2}{b(k)},\qquad
 Y_k=\sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D\\ k\mid d}}w(d)\lambda_d .
\]
This identity uses \(w([d,e])=w(d)w(e)/w((d,e))\) and \(1/w((d,e))=\sum_{k\mid(d,e)}1/b(k)\).
Put \(G=\sum_{k\in\mathcal D}b(k)\) and choose \(Y_k=\mu(k)b(k)/G\), where \(\mu\) is the Möbius function.
Inversion on the divisor-closed set \(\mathcal D\) gives \(\lambda_1=1\) and
\[
 |\lambda_d|
  =\frac{b(d)}{w(d)G}
       \sum_{\substack{a\le z/d,\ (a,d)=1\\a\in\mathcal D}}b(a)
  \le \frac{b(d)}{w(d)}\le d .
\]
The main term is \(x/(mG)\), and the total absolute remainder is \(O(z^6)\), since \(|\mathcal D|\le z\), each \(|\lambda_d|\le z\), and \(2^{\omega([d,e])}\le de\le z^2\).

To bound \(G\), put \(y=z^{1/16}\) and consider the full product of \(1+b(p)\) over sieve primes up to \(y\).
Normalize the associated squarefree divisor weights \(b(d)\) by their total.
The expected \(\log d\) is \(\sum_{p\le y,\,p\nmid 2mj}(2/p)\log p\le4\log y\).
Indeed \(\log(N!)\ge\sum_{p\le N}\lfloor N/p\rfloor\log p\) and \(\lfloor N/p\rfloor\ge N/(2p)\) give \(\sum_{p\le y}(\log p)/p\le2\log y\) at \(N=\lfloor y\rfloor\).
At least half the product weight is therefore at \(d\le z\) and contributes to \(G\).
As \(1+b(p)=(1-2/p)^{-1}\ge(1-1/p)^{-2}\) for \(p\ge3\), this proves
\[
 G\ \gg\ \left(\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p)^{-1}\right)^2
                 \prod_{p\mid 2mj}(1-1/p)^2
      \ \gg\ \frac{(\log x)^2}{F(m)F(j)} .
\]
The full prime product is at least \(\sum_{n\le y}1/n\), and \(F(2mj)\le4F(m)F(j)\).
The error \(O(x^{3/10})\) is absorbed uniformly since \(m\le C\log x\), proving (3).

<a id="common-divisors"></a>
## A tail estimate for the common divisor

The function \(F\) has bounded mean.
Indeed \(F(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}a(d)\), where \(a(d)\) vanishes on nonsquarefree \(d\) and equals \(\prod_{p\mid d}((1-1/p)^{-2}-1)\) otherwise.
Thus \(\sum_{n\le Y}F(n)\le Y\prod_p(1+a(p)/p)\ll Y\) since \(a(p)=O(1/p)\).
Summation on dyadic intervals then gives \(\sum_{m\ge D} F(m)/m^2\ll1/D\) for \(D\ge1\).

Let \(1\le H\le C\log x\).
The number of all prime pairs \(x\le r<q\le2x\) with \(q-r\le H\) and \(\gcd(r-1,q-1)\ge D\), for \(D\ge1\), is
\[
                           \ll \frac{xH}{D(\log x)^2}.                         \tag{4}
\]
In fact their common divisor \(m\) satisfies \(D\le m\le H\) and \(g=mj\).
For each such \(m\) bound the union by (3) summed over \(1\le j\le H/m\).
The mean bound contributes \(O(H/m)\) to the sum of \(F(j)\), and the tail sum over \(m\) gives (4).
The constant is absolute after \(x\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(C\).
Independently (3) with \(m=1\) bounds the total number of pairs of gap at most \(H\) by \(O(xH/(\log x)^2)\).

<a id="supply"></a>
## Selecting the two endpoints

Fix \(B\ge3\) for the moment.
Use primes \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) with \(p\not\equiv1\bmod\ell\) for all odd primes \(\ell\le B\).
Their relative prime density is
\[
                  \Delta_B=\frac12\prod_{3\le\ell\le B,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}
                                             \left(1-\frac1{\ell-1}\right).
\]
To compute it by inclusion-exclusion, each intersection of complementary congruences is \(p\equiv1\bmod a\), with \(a\) a product of distinct odd primes and optionally \(4\).
The [prime number theorem for each fixed reduced class](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#fixed-progression-pnt) gives relative density \(1/\varphi(a)\) for this class, including \(a=1\).
Multiplicativity gives the product formula.
Moreover \(B\Delta_B^2\to\infty\).
Indeed at \(s=1+1/\log B\) one has \(\sum_{p\le B}1/p\le e\log\zeta(s)\le e\log(1+\log B)\) by the Euler product and the integral upper bound for the zeta series.
Since \(-\log(1-1/(\ell-1))=O(1/\ell)\), this gives \(\Delta_B\gg(1+\log B)^{-K}\) for an absolute \(K\).

Remove the primes at which one of the orders of \(2,3,5\) is at most \(\sqrt{2x}(\log x)^5\) in \([x,2x]\).
The [unconditional rank-one bound](../audits/literature/Pap96/Yin_U001.md#rank-one-orders) removes \(o(x/\log x)\) primes.
For fixed \(B\), the prime number theorem and relative density therefore leave at least \(\Delta_B x/(2\log x)\) primes for large \(x\).
Take disjoint pairs of consecutive entries in their ordered list, starting with the first and second.
The sum of their gaps is at most \(x\).
With \(H=16\log x/\Delta_B\), at least \(\Delta_B x/(8\log x)\) of the pairs have gaps at most \(H\) for all sufficiently large \(x\).
Every such pair has \(\gcd(q-1,r-1)\) exactly divisible by two, and any odd prime dividing it exceeds \(B\).
If this gcd exceeds two it therefore exceeds \(B\).
By (4) the number of these failures is \(O(x/(B\Delta_B\log x))\) with an absolute constant.
Choose \(B\) once sufficiently large that this loses at most half the lower bound.
The resulting absolute positive proportion still persists after removing every \(g<c_2\log x\) with \(c_2>0\) sufficiently small, by the unrestricted gap count following (4).
This proves the supply claimed in the statement.

<a id="exceptional-endpoints"></a>
## Prime pairs meeting a sparse set of endpoints

For \(H=C\log x\), let \(D(p)\) count the other primes in \([x,2x]\) within distance \(H\) of \(p\).
We prove
\[
                    \sum_{x\le p\le 2x,\ p\ {\rm prime}} D(p)^2
                                     \ll_C \frac{x}{\log x}.                       \tag{5}
\]
The sum of \(D(p)\) counts twice the number of pairs and satisfies this bound after (4).
The remaining sum of \(D(p)(D(p)-1)\) counts ordered triples of distinct primes with one distinguished endpoint.
Their smallest prime is \(n\), and the others are \(n+i,n+j\) for \(1\le i<j\le2H\).
Each unordered triple has at most six possible orderings.

For fixed \(i,j\), omit the primes dividing \(L=6ij(j-i)\).
At each other sieve prime \(p\le z=x^{1/20}\), the three linear forms exclude exactly three classes.
The same square-weight calculation proving (3) now uses \(w(p)=3/p\) and \(b(p)=3/(p-3)\).
The identities for \(Y_k\), the main coefficient \(1/G\), and the bound \(|\lambda_d|\le d\) hold since all sieve primes are at least five.
The total remainder is again \(O(z^6)\), since the CRT error \(3^{\omega([d,e])}\le de\).
For \(y=z^{1/16}\) the expected logarithm of a divisor in the small-prime product is at most \(6\log y<(\log z)/2\).
Now \(1+b(p)\ge(1-1/p)^{-3}\), so \(G\gg(\log x)^3(\varphi(L)/L)^3\).
Consequently the number of triples for these shifts is \(O(x(L/\varphi(L))^3/(\log x)^3)\), uniformly in \(i,j\).

Put \(V(k)=(k/\varphi(k))^3\).
The factor \(V(L)\) is at most \(V(6)V(i)V(j)V(j-i)\).
The same divisor-sum proof used for \(F\) shows \(\sum_{k\le Y} V(k)^3\ll Y\), since each prime divisor now supplies \((1-1/p)^{-9}=1+O(1/p)\).
Hölder's inequality on the shift pairs \(1\le i<j\le2H\) bounds \(\sum V(i)V(j)V(j-i)\) by the product of three cube-rooted sums of cubes.
Each such sum is \(O(H^2)\), because each integer occurs in each position for at most \(2H\) choices of the other shift.
This proves the total triple bound \(O(xH^2/(\log x)^3)\) and hence (5).
By Cauchy–Schwarz, a set of \(o(x/\log x)\) exceptional prime endpoints therefore meets \(o_C(x/\log x)\) pairs, even when endpoints are reused.

<a id="measures"></a>
## The share denominator and the measure of an individual factor

For a pair whose two endpoints satisfy the order bounds used above, the [orbit-length argument](Yin_C_G0345_v221.md#rounding) applies directly with \(g\le C\log x\).
It uses only those order bounds and the bound on \(g\), so the choice of the two primes within the interval imposes no further hypothesis.
Write \(s\ge1\) for the common exterior count of an irreducible factor of \(Q_\varepsilon\) near each of the two binomials.
Its reduced share denominator for \(s/g\) divides each of the three indices \(J_2,J_3,J_5\) on the grids of sizes \(q-1,q-1,r-1\).
In particular it divides \(\gcd(q-1,r-1)\).
For the selected pairs with gcd two and \(q\equiv3\bmod4\), the orbit length \(t_2=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(2))\) is even, so \(J_2=(q-1)/t_2\) is odd.
The share denominator is therefore one.
Every constituent has a positive share and their counts sum to \(g\), so exactly one occurs.
The [cyclotomic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion) removes just \(X+\varepsilon\), since \(g\) is even, \(q\) is prime and \(r\ge C'(2g+1)^9\) for arbitrarily large fixed \(C'\) once \(x\) is sufficiently large.
The [individual measure formula](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) now gives (1) with \(s=g\).

For every other pair passing the same order bounds, any irreducible constituent satisfies
\[
 m(S)=\frac{s}{g}\log 6+O\!\left((2g+1)e^{-c r/(2g+1)^2}\right)
                              \ \ge\ \frac{s}{2g}\log6.
\]
Indeed the absolute error is uniformly \(o_C(1/g)\) on \(g\le C\log x,\ r\ge x\), whereas \(s\ge1\).
As the reduced denominator of \(s/g\) divides \(\gcd(q-1,r-1)\), a factor with \(m(S)\le\eta\) requires \(\gcd(q-1,r-1)\ge (\log6)/(2\eta)\).
Apply (4) with this lower threshold or with \(D=1\) if the threshold is smaller.
The number of qualifying pairs with good orders is \(O_C(\eta x/\log x)\), including the union over the two signs since the necessary condition on the gcd is the same.
The [rank-one order bound](../audits/literature/Pap96/Yin_U001.md#rank-one-orders) gives \(o(x/\log x)\) exceptional endpoints.
Equation (5) bounds the pairs meeting them by the second term of (2).
