# Mahler measure and short relations for delays between two cyclotomic products

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform comparison for moving pairs

Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic products of cyclotomic polynomials, allowing the constant one.
For \(g\ge1,\ n\ge0,\ \sigma\in\{1,-1\}\), suppose \(F(X)=X^nA(X^g)+\sigma B(X^g)\) is nonzero.
Write \(d_A=\deg A,\ d_B=\deg B\) and \(\epsilon_A=A(0),\ \epsilon_B=B(0)\).
Take all circle integrals in normalized angular measure, write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, and put
\[
      H(A,B)=\tfrac12\int\big|\log|A(e^{it})/B(e^{it})|\big|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi},
      \qquad \Delta=2n+g(d_A-d_B).
\]
Values at zeros or poles in the boundary integral are immaterial.
For \(\Delta\ne0\),
\[
                 H(A,B)-\log2\le m(F)\le H(A,B)+\log2 .                \tag{1}
\]
If \(\Delta=0\) and \(\epsilon_A\ne\epsilon_B\), the sharper bounds \(H(A,B)\le m(F)\le H(A,B)+\tfrac12\log2\) hold.
The upper bound \(m(F)\le H(A,B)+\log2\) also holds in the remaining case.
All these estimates are uniform in both products and the two exponents.
In the cases with a lower bound, every nonzero nontorsion root of \(F\) has a nonreciprocal minimal polynomial, and hence has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(X^3-X-1=0\).

Use the reduced signed binomial exponents \(a_j\) specified by
\[
            \frac{A(X)}{B(X)}=\epsilon_A\epsilon_B
                        \prod_{j\ge1}(1-X^j)^{a_j},\qquad a_j\in\mathbb Z .
\]
Their support is finite.
Let \(w\ge1\) bound the number of active indices in each interval \([2^v,2^{v+1})\), \(v\ge0\).
For every shift, if \(H(A,B)\le h\), the minimum coefficient length of a nonzero integer relation at any nonzero nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(F\) satisfies
\[
                    \lambda(\alpha)\le 1+2^{Cw^5 h^2}                 \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute \(C\).
In (1) and in the perpendicular case \(\Delta=0,\ \epsilon_A\ne\epsilon_B\), one may use \(h=m(F)+\log2\).
Consequently a bounded full measure in those cases also bounds minimum length at each individual root under the stated occupancy assumption.
No estimate of \(H(A,B)\) from a single centered shift with \(\epsilon_A=\epsilon_B\) is required for (2).

<a id="phase"></a>
## The relative phase on the unit circle

The reversal \(X^{d_A}A(1/X)\) equals \(\epsilon_A A(X)\), since conjugation pairs the roots on the unit circle, and likewise for \(B\).
At \(X=e^{it}\), away from the finitely many zeros, \(e^{-igd_At/2}A(e^{igt})\) is real for \(\epsilon_A=1\) and purely imaginary for \(\epsilon_A=-1\).
Thus the relative argument of the two summands of \(F\), modulo \(\pi\), is \(\Delta t/2+\phi\) for a fixed \(\phi\), equal to zero modulo \(\pi\) for equal reversal signs and \(\pi/2\) modulo \(\pi\) for opposite signs.
Projection perpendicular to either summand shows that
\[
  |\sin(\Delta t/2+\phi)|\max(|A(e^{igt})|,|B(e^{igt})|)
       \le |F(e^{it})| \le 2\max(|A(e^{igt})|,|B(e^{igt})|).
\]
For nonzero integral \(\Delta\), the logarithmic mean of the sine factor is \(-\log2\), by \(2|\sin s|=|1-e^{2is}|\) and the vanishing logarithmic mean of the unit binomial.
The mean of \(\log\max(|A|,|B|)\) is \(H(A,B)\), since \(\max(u,v)=(u+v+|u-v|)/2\) and both products have measure zero.
It is unchanged by angular dilation by \(g\).
This gives (1).
In the perpendicular case \(|F|^2=|A|^2+|B|^2\) on the circle, giving the claimed constants.

At a nontorsion root \(\alpha\ne0\), neither \(A(\alpha^g)\) nor \(B(\alpha^g)\) vanishes.
If its inverse is also a root, substitution of the first root equation into the one at the inverse gives
\[
                            \alpha^\Delta=\epsilon_A/\epsilon_B .
\]
This is impossible for \(\Delta\ne0\) or for opposite signs.
Hence every such primitive irreducible factor is nonreciprocal.
If its positive leading coefficient is one, the [nonreciprocal bound of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) applies to its algebraic integer root.
If the leading coefficient is at least two, the root formula for the logarithmic Mahler measure gives the stronger lower bound \(\log 2>\log\theta\).
This extends the [inverse-root calculation for one cyclotomic block](Yin_C_G0345_v99.md#reciprocity).

<a id="length"></a>
## Clearing the signed binomial quotient

The Möbius product for each cyclotomic polynomial gives the displayed expansion of \(A/B\).
The quotient normalized to have constant coefficient one determines all the integers \(a_j\), since changing the first differing exponent \(a_j\) changes the coefficient of \(X^j\).
Common cyclotomic factors of \(A\) and \(B\) cancel in this expansion.
Set \(S=\sum_j|a_j|\) and \(u(t)=\sum_j a_j\log|1-e^{ijt}|\), so \(\|u\|_1=2H(A,B)\).
The [dyadic norm comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#statement) gives \(\|a\|_2\le c^{-1}w^{5/2}\|u\|_1\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
Its [Möbius inversion estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#inversion) bounds \(\|a\|_2\) by \(O(\sqrt w)\|u\|_2\).
In its [elementary fourth-moment argument](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#blocks), the positive harmonic block with \(2^s\le l<2^{s+1}\) has fourth norm at most \(2^{-s/4}\).
Squaring its dilated sum and grouping products by the larger dilation leaves at most \(3w\) groups at each frequency, so Parseval and Hölder bound the fourth norm of the sum by \(O(\sqrt w)2^{-s/4}\|a\|_2\).
Summing in \(s\) and using \(\|u\|_2^3\le\|u\|_1\|u\|_4^2\) yields the stated comparison.
The empty support gives \(S=0\) directly.
For integral exponents the resulting estimate is
\[
                        S\le\|a\|_2^2\le 4c^{-2}w^5 H(A,B)^2 .
\]

Let \(U=\prod_{a_j>0}(1-X^j)^{a_j}\), \(V=\prod_{a_j<0}(1-X^j)^{-a_j}\), and \(S_+,S_-\) be their respective numbers of binomial factors counted with multiplicity.
Then \(A V=\epsilon_A\epsilon_B B U\).
The integer polynomial
\[
                     W(X)=\epsilon_A\epsilon_B X^n U(X^g)+\sigma V(X^g)
\]
satisfies \(B(X^g)W(X)=V(X^g)F(X)\).
It is nonzero, vanishes at \(\alpha\), and has the full measure \(m(W)=m(F)\) since the two multiplying polynomials have measure zero.
Its coefficient length is at most \(2^{S_+}+2^{S_-}\le1+2^S\).
This proves (2) with \(C=4c^{-2}\).
If instead only a bounded number of intervals exceed a fixed occupancy, the [exceptional-interval theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v107.md#statement) gives a qualitative bound for \(S\) and hence for the same explicit multiple in terms of \(H(A,B)\) and the two occupancy bounds.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Fixed-pair convergence and a varying sequence of delays

Jensen's formula in \(Y\) gives \(m_{X,Y}(Y A(X)+\sigma B(X))=H(A,B)\).
For a fixed pair \(A,B\) and fixed sign, [Lawton specialization](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) therefore gives \(m(F)\to H(A,B)\) whenever \(\max(g,n)/\gcd(g,n)\to\infty\).
Indeed the primitive integral relation for the specialization \((X,Y)=(Z^g,Z^n)\) has sup norm \(\max(g,n)/\gcd(g,n)\).
In particular \(g=1,\ n\to\infty\) gives this limit.

The additive error in (1) cannot be replaced by an error tending uniformly to zero as \(n\) and \(|\Delta|\) grow.
For \(q\to\infty\) take \(g=1,\ n=2q,\ \sigma=1\) and the coprime products
\[
        A=1+X^q+X^{2q},\qquad B=1+X^q .
\]
Both have only roots of unity, and \(B=0\) implies \(X^q=-1\), where \(A=1\).
Here \(\Delta=5q\) and \(F=1+X^q+X^{2q}+X^{3q}+X^{4q}\) has measure zero.
In contrast \(H(A,B)\) is independent of \(q\) by angular dilation and is positive since \(|A(1)/B(1)|=3/2\).
Thus the fixed-pair limit and the uniform comparison give different information when both blocks move.
