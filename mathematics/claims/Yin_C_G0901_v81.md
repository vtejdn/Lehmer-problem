# Separation of the Lehmer infimum by norms to proper normal subfields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Units indexed by their normal fields

For a finite Galois extension \(F/\mathbb Q\) in a fixed algebraic closure set
\[
 B(F)=\{u\in\mathcal O_F^\times:\ N_{F/E}(u)\text{ is a root of unity
      for every proper Galois subfield }E/\mathbb Q\text{ of }F\}.
\]
The notation for proper subfields includes \(E=\mathbb Q\) if \(F\ne\mathbb Q\).
Let \(\mu_F\) be the least positive total logarithmic Mahler measure in \(B(F)\), with \(+\infty\) for no nontorsion element.
The minimum exists whenever finite.
For any nonempty collection \(\mathcal F\) of such fields, form all products of integer powers of units from the \(B(F)\), with arbitrary torsion twists and arbitrary positive roots.
The infimum of the positive total measures over this group is exactly
\[
                               \inf_{F\in\mathcal F}\mu_F .                \tag{1}
\]
For a finite collection with a nontorsion element the value is attained.
No condition on intersections or inclusions among the fields is imposed.
More generally (1) holds for chosen Galois-stable divisible subgroups of the saturated groups generated by \(B(F)\), using their own infima on the right.

Every nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\) has a nonempty selection of its conjugates whose product lies in a \(B(F)\) and has positive measure at most \(m(w)\), with \(F\) a normal subfield of its splitting field.
Consequently the unrestricted Lehmer infimum equals the infimum of the \(\mu_F\) over all finite Galois fields.
Every nontorsion algebraic unit that is a strict degree record lies in \(B(L)\) for its splitting field \(L\).
These separate number-field minima still require a bound uniform over varying fields.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Averaging the conjugate classes

Work in a common finite Galois field \(N\) containing the finitely many actual units and all auxiliary elements in a given expression.
For any Galois \(E\subseteq N\) write \(P_E\) for averaging over \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/E)\) on the rational span of multiplicative classes modulo torsion.
The image of a unit class is a rational multiple of the class of its relative norm.
In particular on any classes coming from a Galois \(F\subseteq N\), \(P_E\) acts by averaging over \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/(F\cap E))\): restriction has precisely this image by Galois theory.
It follows that \(P_E\) fixes a class from \(B(F)\) if \(F\subseteq E\), and kills it otherwise.
The same conclusion applies to rational linear combinations of such classes, and every finite product with roots and twists has a class of just this form.

The resulting spaces for different \(F\) form a direct sum.
Indeed if a sum of nonzero terms from distinct fields vanished, choose a field \(F_0\) minimal by inclusion among them.
Applying \(P_{F_0}\) isolates exactly its term, giving a contradiction.
In a common normal closure the functions \(g\mapsto\log|g(u)|\) from distinct fields' groups are also orthogonal for the uniform inner product.
For \(F\not\subset E\) the conditional mean of this function for \(u\in B(F)\) given restriction to \(E\) is zero by norm averaging; a function from \(E\) is constant on those cosets.
If \(F\subsetneq E\), interchange their roles.

Now let \(y\) be a nontorsion element of the group in (1), and choose \(F_0\) minimal among the nonzero terms of its class.
Take the product \(z\) of the distinct conjugates of \(y\) in its orbit under \(J=\operatorname{Gal}(N/F_0)\).
This product belongs to \(F_0\) and \([z]=eP_{F_0}[y]\), where \(e\) is the orbit size.
In particular \(z\) is nontorsion and lies in \(B(F_0)\), since all averages on its class to proper normal subfields vanish.
For a chosen divisible subgroup in the generalization, the isolated term belongs to the rational class space of that subgroup, so the product belongs to it up to torsion; if needed the group is understood to include the allowed torsion twists.
Normality of \(J\) gives orbit size \(e\) on all conjugates of \(y\).
The conjugates of \(z\) therefore number at most \((\deg y)/e\), and \(h(z)\le e h(y)\).
Thus \(m(z)\le m(y)\).
Conversely every allowed original unit contributes to the combined group.
For the full group from a single \(B(F)\) the same product removes radical denominators at no larger total measure, and bounded-degree coefficient finiteness gives the minimum inside \(F\).
This proves (1) and its stated variants.

For an arbitrary nontorsion \(w\) take \(N\) to be its splitting field.
Choose a normal subfield \(F\) minimal under inclusion such that \(P_F[w]\ne0\); such a choice exists since \(P_N\) is the identity.
For a proper normal \(E\subsetneq F\), \(P_EP_F=P_E\), hence \(P_EP_F[w]=0\).
The same orbit product gives the required \(z\in B(F)\).
If \(w\) is a strict record, every nontrivial normal subgroup of its splitting group moves some root and so, by normality and transitivity, has orbit size at least two at every root.
A nonzero proper projection would give a lower-degree unit of no greater positive measure.
Thus \(w\in B(N)\).
For the original integer-polynomial question, positive measures below \(\log2\) reduce to nontorsion unit minima by multiplicativity and the integer end coefficients; the established Lehmer example already has measure below that threshold.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Deciding which normal fields contribute

Write \(G_F=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\) and \(c\in G_F\) for complex conjugation.
For \(F\ne\mathbb Q\), \(B(F)\) contains a nontorsion unit exactly when \(G_F\) has a faithful irreducible complex representation with a nonzero \(c\)-fixed vector.
Equivalently \(G_F\) must possess a faithful complex irreducible representation, and either \(F\) is totally real or \(c\) is not central.
In particular a noncyclic center excludes such a field.
Normal fields with central nontrivial complex conjugation also cannot contribute a proper-field minimum here.

By the logarithmic form of [Dirichlet's unit theorem](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002), the real span of all unit classes of \(F\) is the hyperplane of weighted sum zero on its archimedean places.
For a normal field these places form \(G_F/\langle c\rangle\), with common weight, and the logarithm map identifies the real representations equivariantly.
Its complexification is therefore the permutation module on these places with one trivial copy removed.
A nontrivial irreducible representation occurs exactly when it has a nonzero \(\langle c\rangle\)-fixed vector, by the permutation character inner product or Frobenius reciprocity.
For any normal subgroup, its averaging projection on an irreducible representation is the identity if it acts trivially, and zero otherwise, because the subspace of invariants is \(G_F\)-stable.
Imposing zero projection for every nonidentity normal subgroup therefore selects exactly the faithful constituents.
The simultaneous kernel is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) on the rational span of actual units, so a positive complex dimension yields a nonzero rational vector realized after clearing denominators by an actual unit in \(B(F)\).
A faithful irreducible has \(c\) acting as \(-I\) only when \(c\) is central, whereas a nonidentity central involution acts as \(-I\) by Schur's lemma and faithfulness.
The center in a faithful complex irreducible consists of scalar matrices, so is cyclic.
The case \(F=\mathbb Q\) has only torsion units.

There is also a direct integer rank formula.
For each normal \(E\subseteq F\), let \(r_1(E),r_2(E)\) count real places and pairs of complex places.
Every rational irreducible component of the unit space of \(E\) has a unique kernel and hence a unique normal fixed field \(K\subseteq E\).
Its projection space with a given kernel is the rational class space of \(B(K)\): invariance under \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) puts it in the span of \(K\)-units by averaging the norms, and the proper normal averages vanish precisely on the faithful part for \(K\).
Thus
\[
         r_1(E)+r_2(E)-1=\sum_{K\subseteq E,\ K/\mathbb Q\ {\rm Galois}}
                                   \operatorname{rank}B(K).
\]
Subtraction over proper normal subfields computes the contribution of each \(E\), equivalently by Möbius inversion on the normal subfield lattice.
