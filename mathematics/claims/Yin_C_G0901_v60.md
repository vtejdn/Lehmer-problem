# A Frobenius obstruction from the signs of a reciprocal trace discriminant

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least ideal norm representing the signs at infinity

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(w)\), \(t=w+w^{-1}\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(n=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and \(m=m(w)\) its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
Put \(\Delta=t^2-4\).
Take any finite elementary abelian \(2\)-extension \(E/F\) unramified at every finite prime.
For each real embedding \(\sigma\) of \(F\), let \(c_\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\) be its complex conjugation, equal to the identity when the embedding stays real.
Put
\[
 u=\prod_{\sigma\ {\rm real},\ |\sigma(t)|<2} c_\sigma,\qquad
 D_E(u)=\min\{\,\mathrm N I:\ I\subseteq\mathcal O_F
      \text{ a nonzero squarefree ideal},\ \operatorname{Art}_{E/F}(I)=u\,\},
\]
allowing the unit ideal with norm one.
Then \(u\) is represented, and for every integer \(T\ge2\),
\[
 \log D_E(u)\le\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}\Delta|
 \le 2n\left(\frac{\log T}{T-1}+\frac{T+1}{3}\frac{m}{n}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(u\) is in the subgroup generated by the Frobenius elements of the primes of norm at most \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}\Delta|\).
If \(m_j\to0\) through positive measures, then \(\log D_{E_j}(u_j)=o(\sqrt{n_j\log n_j})\) for every choice of these extensions, even as their ranks vary.

For every \(r\ge6\) with \(r\equiv2\bmod4\), put \(n=3r\).
For every \(Y>0\) there are infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic totally real fields \(F\) of degree \(n\), with the following properties.
Each field has a prime of norm \(27\) and a distinguished set \(S\) of \(2r\) real embeddings.
Consider all reciprocal units with trace field \(F\) whose trace has exactly four exterior embeddings \((|\sigma(t)|>2)\), an odd number of them in \(S\).
Every such unit is orthogonal to every conjugate of every primitive Salem number and satisfies \(m(w)>Y\).
Every set of four embeddings meeting \(S\) in odd cardinality occurs, in infinitely many distinct quadratic extensions \(K/F\).
In particular one can take \(Y=n\) at each degree.
Here \(n-4\) is even, so the unrestricted product of the real signs of \(\Delta\) is positive.

<a id="artin"></a>
## Factoring the principal discriminant ideal

Reciprocity of \(w\) gives an involution of \(K\) over \(F\) taking \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\), and its quadratic equation gives \([K:F]=2\).
Thus \(\Delta\) is a nonzero algebraic integer and \(N_{K/F}(w^2-1)=-\Delta\).
For a quadratic quotient of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\), the [principal-ideal Artin sign formula](../audits/literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#artin-sign) identifies the Artin image of \((\Delta)\) with the product of the signs of \(\Delta\) over the real places becoming complex in that quotient.
There are no finite ramified places, including above two.
At a real embedding \(\sigma\) the sign is negative exactly when \(|\sigma(t)|<2\).
An endpoint value \(t=\pm2\) at any embedding would force \(w=\pm1\).
The quotient therefore sends \(\operatorname{Art}_{E/F}((\Delta))\) and \(u\) to the same element.
The quadratic characters separate elements of the elementary abelian group, giving equality in that group.

Writing \(I=\prod_{v:\,v(\Delta)\ {\rm odd}}v\) with integral prime ideal exponents normalized in \(\mathbb Z\), one has \(\operatorname{Art}_{E/F}(I)=u\), since each prime Frobenius has order dividing two.
Also \(I\mid(\Delta)\), which proves the first inequality of (1) and the assertion about the generated subgroup.
The [complete cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) for \(w^2\) in the containing field \(K\) uses \([K:\mathbb Q]=2n\) and \(h(w^2)=m/n\).
Norm transitivity gives the second inequality of (1), regardless of any degree drop in \(w^2\).
On a shrinking sequence, bounded-degree finiteness gives \(n_j\to\infty\).
The small-height estimate at the same locator bounds the right side after optimization by \(O(\sqrt{n_jm_j\log(n_j/m_j)})\).
Division by \(\sqrt{n_j\log n_j}\) proves the stated limit, because \(m_j+m_j\log(1/m_j)/\log n_j\to0\).

## A quadratic extension with mixed real signs and no finite ramification

Let \(D=\mathbb Q(\theta)\) with \(\theta^3-4\theta-1=0\), and put \(a=1+4\theta\).
The cubic \(f(X)=X^3-4X-1\) has no rational root, and sign changes in \((-2,-1)\), \((-1,-1/4)\), and \((2,3)\) give its three roots.
Thus \(a\) has two negative embeddings and one positive embedding.
It is a unit since \(N_{D/\mathbb Q}a=-64 f(-1/4)=1\).
The extension \(D'=D(\sqrt a)\) is quadratic.
The monic polynomial of \((1+\sqrt a)/2\) over \(D\) is \(X^2-X-\theta\) with unit discriminant \(a\).
Its reduction at every finite prime is separable, so the local extension is unramified, including at the dyadic primes.
This also proves finite-place unramifiedness after any base change by the same polynomial.

The cubic modulo three is \(X^3-X-1\), separable with no root in \(\mathbb F_3\), so three is inert in \(D\) and its unique prime \(\mathfrak l\) has norm \(27\).
The norm of the residue of \(a\) to \(\mathbb F_3\) is the reduction of its absolute norm one.
Thus \(a^{13}=1\) in \(\mathbb F_{27}\), making its residue a square there by cyclicity.
The prime \(\mathfrak l\) splits in \(D'/D\).

Fix \(r,Y\) as in the statement and set \(X=4^n\exp(2Y)\).
At every prime of \(D\) of norm at most \(X\) that is inert in \(D'/D\), prescribe an irreducible monic reduction of degree \(r\) for a polynomial over \(\mathcal O_D\).
At \(\mathfrak l\) prescribe squarefree factor degrees \(1,r-1\).
To distinguish a new field from a finite list of previously obtained fields, take a rational prime exceeding \(X\) unramified in \(D\) and all fields in that list.
At one prime of \(D\) above it impose the Eisenstein conditions, using coefficient classes modulo the square of that prime.
These finitely many independent prescriptions are possible by the Chinese remainder theorem.

As in the [totally real construction with prescribed reductions](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#bases), start with \(\prod_{j=1}^r(Z-jR)\) for a large positive integer \(R\).
The coefficient changes needed for the prescriptions can be chosen bounded at every embedding of \(D\), uniformly in \(R\), since there are only finitely many residue classes modulo the fixed ideal of conditions.
At each embedding the monic polynomial after \(Z=Rz\) and division by \(R^r\) tends to \(\prod_{j=1}^r(z-j)\), hence has \(r\) real roots for sufficiently large \(R\) by sign changes on disjoint intervals.
Eisenstein gives an extension \(F/D\) of degree \(r\) ramified at the chosen new rational prime, so the fields are pairwise nonisomorphic on iteration.
All squarefree reductions have the specified residue degrees by local factorization of a monic polynomial with unit discriminant.
In particular \(F\) has a prime of norm \(27\).

Let \(E=F(\sqrt a)\).
It is a quadratic extension since some embeddings of the totally real field \(F\) send \(a\) to a negative number.
Its complexified real places form precisely the set \(S\) of the \(2r\) embeddings above the two negative embeddings of \(a\) in \(D\).
Every finite prime \(v\) of \(F\) of norm at most \(X\) splits in \(E/F\).
Indeed the underlying prime of \(D\) has norm at most \(X\), and if it splits in \(D'\) the splitting persists after base change.
If it is inert in \(D'\), its prescribed residue degree in \(F\) is the even integer \(r\), so the residue field of \(F\) contains the quadratic residue extension and the two distinct roots of \(Z^2-Z-\theta\) lift there.

For a trace with the four prescribed exterior embeddings, the number of negative \(\sigma(\Delta)\) on \(S\) is \(2r\) minus the odd number of exterior embeddings in \(S\).
Hence \(u\) is the nonidentity element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\).
Every squarefree ideal representing it contains some inert prime, so \(D_E(u)>X\).
At \(T=2\), (1) gives \(\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}\Delta|\le n\log4+2m\).
The choice of \(X\) now forces \(m>Y\).
The [endpoint parity test for Salem inputs](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#parity) uses the inequality \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}\Delta<0\).
Here the full archimedean sign product is positive and the minimum of prime ideal norms congruent to three modulo four is at most \(27\).
The [unit-sign relations for reciprocal polynomials with unit endpoints](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v218.md#classes) apply the Artin formula to units in a trace splitting field.
In (1) the ideal of the actual radicand in the original trace field accounts for its signs even when that ideal has nontrivial prime divisors.

## Realizing each exterior set and excluding all Salem projections

Given four such embeddings, choose \(b\in F^\times\) positive there and negative at every other real embedding.
As in the [marked-place quadratic construction](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#extensions), \(b\) can simultaneously have valuation one at a fresh odd prime unramified in any finite list of earlier quadratic extensions.
Indeed one coset of the square of that prime imposes valuation one, and its real image is a translated full lattice meeting the required open orthant.
Then \(K=F(\sqrt b)\) has signature \((8,n-4)\), with exactly the specified split real places, and the new ramification gives distinct quadratic extensions on iteration.
The unit ranks of \(K\) and \(F\) differ by four, and the relative norm image contains the squares of all \(F\)-units.
Consequently the rank of the relative norm-one units is four.
Their archimedean logarithms form a full lattice in the four coordinates given by one real lift of each split place.
Indeed the other four real coordinates are their negatives, the complex coordinates vanish, and the lattice in the full logarithmic embedding is discrete by [Dirichlet's unit theorem](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002).
Choose a lattice vector with four strictly increasing positive coordinates by going sufficiently far inside that open cone.
For the corresponding unit \(w\) the maximum modulus occurs at exactly one embedding of \(K\).
Hence \(w\) generates \(K\), since restriction to a proper subfield would repeat the largest modulus.
The relative involution sends \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\), so \(w\) is reciprocal and \(t=w+w^{-1}\) generates \(F\) by the quadratic degree.
At each split place it has \(|\sigma(t)|>2\), and at every other place it has \(|\sigma(t)|<2\), proving nonemptiness for each required set.

For any reciprocal unit with this trace field and exterior set, take a real exterior conjugate as the working embedding, transporting the fields and places with it.
If it detected a primitive positive Salem number \(\alpha\), [the descent to a Salem subfield](Yin_C_G0901_v57.md#descent) would give \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\subset F\) and \(K=F\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) in this embedding.
The latter equality also follows because the Salem field contained in \(K\) has nonreal conjugates and \(F\) is totally real, while \([K:F]=2\).
The quadratic Salem radicand is positive at exactly one embedding of \(B\).
After base change to \(F\) the number of real split places is therefore \([F:B]\).
It would equal four, which cannot divide \(n=3r\equiv2\bmod4\).
This excludes every Salem projection, by invariance of detection under conjugation.
The bound concerns the prescribed trace fields and exterior sets.
For general reciprocal units the element \(u\) can be trivial, in which case \(D_E(u)=1\).
