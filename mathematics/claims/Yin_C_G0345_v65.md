# A Mahler gap for three or more exterior roots with commensurable log moduli

<a id="statement"></a>
## The degree bound and the two remaining families

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial with \(M(P)>1\), whose exterior log moduli span a one-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb Q\).
Replace each maximal class of roots having root-of-unity quotients by the product of its members, as in the [torsion-class construction](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).
The resulting monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial has the same measure, and its distinct roots have nontorsion quotient.
Write \(d\) for its degree.
If it has at least three exterior roots, then for an absolute constant \(c>0\),
\[
                  \log M(P)\ \ge\
                  c\,\frac{d^{1/4}}{(\log(3d))^{106}} .
\]
In particular this logarithmic measure tends to infinity with the compressed degree, and the class with at least three exterior roots after compression has a uniform positive logarithmic gap.

There is a positive logarithmic gap for the entire class of \(P\) in the hypothesis if and only if there is one for Salem numbers and one for irreducible reciprocal algebraic units with exactly two exterior conjugates that form a nonreal conjugate pair.
Both the exterior count in the estimate and the degree \(d\) refer to the same compressed polynomial.

<a id="height"></a>
## Heights of two independent units in the reflection system

Use the compressed polynomial throughout the proof, with root set \(\Omega\).
All torsion classes had equal cardinality, so compression multiplied the log modulus of each class representative by that common cardinality and preserved the dimension-one hypothesis.
Write \(r>0\) for the generator of the group of log moduli and \(\kappa=e^{2r}\).
In the [reflection construction](Yin_C_G0345_v63.md#reflection), the real span \(V\) of \(\Omega\) modulo torsion carries a finite simply laced root system \(\Phi\) spanning \(V\).
Its roots are the signed Galois images of \(a=[\kappa]\), with squared length two.
The [uniqueness of lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v63.md#lifts) supplies an actual unit \(x_b\) for each \(b\in\Phi\), equivariantly under Galois, and every \(x_b\) is conjugate to \(\kappa\) or its inverse.
In particular their absolute logarithmic heights all equal \(h(\kappa)\le 2\log M(P)/d\).
The weight set \(\mathcal O=\{[\alpha]:\alpha\in\Omega\}\) has exactly \(d\) elements by compression.
Each original root has \(\log|\alpha|=r(a,[\alpha])\), so counting weights whose pairing with \(a\) is positive counts the actual exterior roots.

The group generated by Galois and inversion preserves \(\mathcal O\), acts transitively on it and permutes the \(t\) isomorphic components of \(\Phi\) transitively.
It contains each root reflection, hence acts on \(\mathcal O\) by the Weyl group in each component independently.
In particular \(t\le\dim V\le d\).
For independent roots \(b,b'\), their lifts are multiplicatively independent since an integral relation would give a linear relation in \(V\).
Writing \(D=[\mathbb Q(x_b,x_{b'}):\mathbb Q]\), [Amoroso and David, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
             h(\kappa)^2\ \ge\ \frac{c_2}{D(\log(3D))^{212}} .
\]
Here the dimension is two and the source exponent before multiplication by the dimension is \(3(3!)^2-2=106\).
It suffices to find a pair with \(D\ll d^{3/2}\), with an absolute implied constant.
Its degree is the size of the Galois orbit of the ordered pair, which the uniqueness of lifts allows us to count using the vectors themselves.

<a id="classical"></a>
## The size of the original weight orbit in types \(A\) and \(D\)

Consider \(A_{N-1}\) with \(N\ge3\) or \(D_N\) with \(N\ge5\).
Choose roots of pairing \(-1\) in one component.
An ordered image pair is in one component and has the same pairing.
In \(A_{N-1}\) there are \(N(N-1)\) roots and \(2(N-2)\) choices of the second root for each first root.
In \(D_N\) the corresponding counts are \(2N(N-1)\) and \(4(N-2)\), as in the [adjacent-root count](Yin_C_G0345_v64.md#gap).
Thus \(D\le 8tN^3\).

Define the support of a weight to be the set of components on which its orthogonal projection is nonzero.
Transitivity gives a common support size \(p\ge1\).
If \(B\) distinct supports occur, then \(Bp\ge t\), since their union is invariant and meets every component.
Each nonzero projection has a component Weyl orbit of size at least \(N\).
Indeed, the \(A_{N-1}\) model permutes the coordinates of a nonconstant vector of coordinate sum zero, giving at least \(N\) permutations by choosing the positions of a proper nonempty level set.
In \(D_N\), a vector with \(j<N\) nonzero coordinates admits \(\binom Nj\) choices of their positions and all \(2^j\) sign choices there, because a zero coordinate allows correction to even parity.
When all coordinates are nonzero, even sign changes alone give \(2^{N-1}\) choices.
The Weyl groups in the \(p\) components act independently without changing the support.
Consequently \(d\ge BN^p\ge tN^p/p\), which is at least \(tN^2/2\) when \(p\ge2\).

Suppose \(p=1\), so the \(t\) supports are individual components.
In \(A_{N-1}\), if one coordinate of a weight differs from the other \(N-1\) equal coordinates, it lies on a line generated by \(e_i-N^{-1}\sum_j e_j\).
These lines are intrinsic as the lines of the shortest dual-lattice vectors, by the [classical coordinate calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v64.md#coordinates).
The whole orbit would lie on these lines and all its vectors have the same length.
In the component of \(a=e_1-e_2\), just two of the resulting oriented vectors at that length have positive pairing, and every other component is orthogonal to \(a\).
This gives at most two exterior roots.
In all remaining nonzero patterns the component orbit has at least \(\binom N2\) elements.
If all coordinates differ there are \(N!\) permutations, and otherwise a level of multiplicity \(j\) with \(2\le j\le N-2\) supplies at least \(\binom Nj\ge\binom N2\) placements.

In \(D_N\), the coordinate axes are intrinsic for \(N\ge5\), by the same coordinate calculation distinguishing \(\pm2e_i\) among root-lattice vectors of squared length four.
If a weight lies on an axis, the entire weight orbit would again have at most two positive pairings with \(a=e_1-e_2\).
For \(2\le j\le N-2\) nonzero coordinates the orbit size is at least \(2^j\binom Nj\ge\binom N2\).
For \(j=N-1\) or \(N\) it is at least \(2^{N-1}\ge\binom N2\).
Thus in both classical types the assumption of at least three exterior roots forces \(d\gg tN^2\) also when \(p=1\).
Together with \(D\le 8tN^3\) this gives \(D\ll d^{3/2}\).

<a id="axes"></a>
## Components of bounded rank and independent axes

For \(D_4,E_6,E_7,E_8\) choose two roots of pairing \(-1\) in one component.
There are at most 240 roots in each component, by the \(D_4\) coordinate model and the [exceptional lattice model](Yin_C_G0345_v64.md#exceptional) with 112 integral and 128 half-integral vectors of squared length two in the ambient \(E_8\) lattice.
The ordered pair therefore has \(D\le240^2t\le240^2d\).

In type \(A_1\) each component is an axis.
If \(p=1\), at the common orbit length only one point of the axis of \(a\) has positive pairing with \(a\), and no other axis contributes.
For \(p\ge2\), choose roots on two different axes in a single weight support.
They are independent, and every ordered Galois image pair lies on two axes of one of the \(B\) supports.
Counting positions and signs gives \(D\le4Bp(p-1)\).
Independent sign changes on each support give \(d\ge B2^p\).
Since \(p(p-1)/2^p\) is absolutely bounded, \(D\ll d\).

These cases exhaust the simply laced classification.
The height inequality now gives
\[
       \log M(P)\ \ge\ \tfrac d2h(\kappa)
       \ \gg\ d\,D^{-1/2}(\log(3D))^{-106}
       \ \gg\ d^{1/4}(\log(3d))^{-106}.
\]
The rightmost degree factor is positive and tends to infinity, so its infimum over positive integer degrees is positive.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Identification of the remaining exterior conjugates

If the compressed polynomial has exactly one exterior root, that root is real.
After changing its sign if needed, it is a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one, since all conjugates besides it and its inverse have modulus one.
In degree two its positive trace is an integer at least three, which gives a uniform gap.
If exactly two exterior roots are real, write them \(\alpha,\beta\).
The rational ratio of their positive log moduli gives \(\alpha^{2u}=\beta^{2v}\) for positive integers \(u,v\).
Their equal positive heights force \(u=v\), so their quotient is torsion, contrary to compression.
Thus with two exterior roots these form a nonreal conjugate pair.
All Salem numbers and all reciprocal units with exactly one such exterior pair satisfy the original rational-rank hypothesis.
These inclusions, the equality of measures in compression and the estimate for three or more exterior roots prove the gap equivalence.
