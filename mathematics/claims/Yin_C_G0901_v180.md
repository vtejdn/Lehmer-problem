# Bounded support remains effectively finite after arbitrary cyclotomic completion

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree estimate for multiples with no bound on the cofactor degree

Let \(F\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of degree \(2D\) from a [genuine integral trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) at the integer height \(q\ge2\).
Write \(M(G)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of \(G\), and count only nonzero coefficients when writing \(|\operatorname{supp}G|\).
Let \(G\ne0\) be any integer multiple of \(F\).
Remove its initial monomial, and assume that at least one of its two end coefficients is not divisible by \(q\).
In particular this holds for any unit constant term, any monic multiple, or whenever \(M(G)<q\).
For \(s=|\operatorname{supp}G|\) one has an absolute effective bound
\[
 D\ \le\ C\big(1+s+\log M(G)\big)\big(Cq\log(2q)\big)^s.             \tag{1}
\]
There is no degree, support, multiplicity or reciprocity condition on the cofactor.
The right side uses the Mahler measure of the whole multiple.

Define \(s_{\rm cyc}(F)\) as the minimum support among \(F\) multiplied by any finite product of cyclotomic polynomials, including the empty product and repetitions.
Then with \(s=s_{\rm cyc}(F)\),
\[
     \frac{\log(3D)}{\log\log(9D)}\ll s,\qquad
           D\le \exp(C s\log(2s)),\qquad q\le C s^3 .                 \tag{2}
\]
Thus bounded support after the best such completion permits effective finite enumeration of all pairs \((q,F)\) over all heights and degrees, in the sense of listing a finite candidate superset and testing actual trace-pole realization.
If one does not supply a completion, this enumeration by itself does not decide whether a given candidate admits a completion of the specified sparsity.
Multiplication by any number of factors \(1-X^a\), with arbitrary positive lags, is covered.
Consequently even a dense coefficient string cannot be reduced to bounded support by such iterated differences along unbounded degrees in this branch.
The [uncompleted bound](Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#statement) corresponds to taking \(G=F\).

<a id="inside"></a>
## Pinning a zero of the multiple inside the unit disk

Put \(p=q+1\), \(M_0=pq,\ K=(2q+1)^2=4M_0+1,\ r=q/p\), and let \(\lambda>1\) be the Salem root of \(F\).
Its inverse \(\rho=\lambda^{-1}\) is also a zero of \(G\).
For \(x=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}-2\) and \(h=M_0^{-1}\), the [exterior pinning estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#windows) gives \(0<h-x<K/M_0^{D+1}\).
Since
\[
       h-x=(p/q-\lambda)\big(1-q/(p\lambda)\big)
                  \ \ge\ (p/q-\lambda)/p,
\]
we have \(0<\rho-r\le pK/M_0^{D+1}\).
For \(D\ge3\) this is at most \(1/(2p)\), since \(2p^2(4M_0+1)\le M_0^4\) for \(q\ge2\).
Thus \(1-\rho\ge1/(2p)\).
The desired bound (1) for \(D<3\) is immediate.

Write \(L=\sum_i |[X^i]G|\).
At every \(y\in[r,\rho]\) and \(b\ge1\), \(b y^{b-1}\le\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}y^i\le(1-y)^{-1}\le2p\).
Using the mean value theorem with \(G(\rho)=0\) gives
\[
                        |G(r)|\le 10p^2 L M_0^{-D}.                 \tag{3}
\]
This estimate uses no norm of a cyclotomic cofactor and does not require evaluation of such a cofactor at \(r\).
In particular, inserting a cyclotomic product of huge degree cannot evade the derivative estimate at this interior point.

<a id="prefixes"></a>
## Every truncated numerator has a nonzero lower bound

Because \(F\) is reciprocal, reversal of the normalized \(G\) also gives an integer multiple with the same measure and support.
We may therefore arrange \(q\nmid G(0)\).
If \(0=i_0<i_1<\cdots<i_{s-1}\) are its occupied exponents, the prefix through \(w=i_b\) at \(r=q/p\) has absolute value at least \(p^{-w}\).
Indeed its numerator after multiplication by \(p^w\) is \(G(0)p^w\bmod q\), which is nonzero since \(p\) is invertible modulo \(q\).
This also excludes \(G(r)=0\).

If \(20p^2L\ge p^{D/2}\), we already have \(D\ll1+\log L\).
Otherwise (3) gives \(|G(r)|<p^{-D/2}/2\).
Whenever \(w<D/2\), there is a next occupied exponent \(j\), since \(\deg G\ge 2D\).
The tail after that prefix must contribute at least \(p^{-w}/2\) in absolute value.
Its value is bounded above by \(L(q/p)^j\), so
\[
        j\le \frac{w\log p+\log(2L)}{\log(p/q)}
                      \le p\big(w\log p+\log(2L)\big).
\]
Starting at zero, apply this inequality until the occupied exponent first reaches \(D/2\).
With \(W=1+\log(2L)\), each step bounds \(j+W\) by \((1+p\log p)(w+W)\).
There are at most \(s-1\) steps, giving \(D\le2W(1+p\log p)^{s-1}\).
For \(s\ge2\) the [sparse coefficient estimate of Dobrowolski and Smyth](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#ds17-u002) gives \(L\le s\,2^{s-2}M(G)\), independently of \(\deg G\).
Here \(s\ge2\) follows from the nontorsion root.
Both alternatives now prove (1).
For the endpoint formulation note that every nonzero integral end coefficient is at most \(M(G)\) in absolute value, using reversal for the constant coefficient.

<a id="completion"></a>
## All cyclotomic completions and the role of the exact pole

A cyclotomic product has unit end coefficients and measure one.
In (1) its product with \(F\) has \(M(G)=\lambda<1+1/q\).
Hence \(\log(3D)\ll s\log(2q)\).
The [two degree estimates of Voutier](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) give \(q<(\log(6D))^3/2\) and \(q<4(\log(2D)/\log\log(2D))^3\) at the root of degree \(2D\).
The first bounds \(\log(2q)\) by \(O(\log\log(9D))\).
The second gives the height bound in (2), after absorbing the bounded small degrees.
Inversion of the resulting degree inequality gives the effective exponential bound, as in the [bounded-support enumeration](Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#gaps).
At bounded degrees the coefficient bounds for \(F\) and the [exact realization tests](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#statement) suffice to enumerate the candidate pairs; no bound on a chosen completion degree is needed for this enumeration.

For arbitrary integral polynomials with few nonzero terms the [general sparse gap](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#dob06-u001) already bounds their full measure away from one, even with cyclotomic factors.
The present degree control also applies at a fixed height.
There are actual [Salem minima with six-term cyclotomic completions](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family) of unbounded degree and measures tending to the plastic number below \(4/3\).
They meet the radial upper bound \(\lambda<1+1/q\) for \(q=2\).
Thus that radial bound on its own permits bounded completed support at unbounded degree; the pinning at the rational point supplies the extra exclusion in (2).
