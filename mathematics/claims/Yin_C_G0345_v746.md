# Radical degrees at the short exponent of a four-term Salem relation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility at every divisor

Let \(\alpha_i\) be Salem numbers with \(m_i=\log\alpha_i\to0\), let \(d_i=[\mathbb Q(\alpha_i):\mathbb Q]\), and write \(K_i=\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)\subset\mathbb R\) and \(F_i=\mathbb Q(\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1})\).
Let \(a_i,b_i\) be positive integers, and let \(B_i\in F_i\) run through a fixed finite set of real numbers greater than one.
Suppose \(a_i m_i\) is bounded and, uniformly along the sequence,
\[
 |\alpha_i^{a_i}-B_i|\ll e^{-b_i m_i},\qquad
 b_i m_i/d_i-h(B_i)\longrightarrow0,                                  \tag{1}
\]
where \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
In particular these hypotheses hold for the exponents and seeds of [the four-term Salem theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement).
Then at every sufficiently large index, **simultaneously for all positive divisors** \(k\mid a_i\), the polynomial \(X^k-B_i\) is irreducible over \(K_i\).
Putting \(E_i=\mathbb Q(B_i)\), one has
\[
 [K_i(B_i^{1/k}):K_i]=[E_i(B_i^{1/k}):E_i]=k
\]
and \(E_i(B_i^{1/k})\) and \(K_i\) are linearly disjoint over \(E_i\).
All real radicals of positive numbers specified here are the positive ones.

For any real number field \(D\) in the chosen embedding containing \(B>1\), put
\[
                    e_D(B)=\max\{j\ge1:B^{1/j}\in D\}.
\]
The same conclusion gives \(\gcd(a_i,e_{K_i}(B_i))=1\) eventually.
There is an explicit field test for this integer.
For any Salem field \(K\) with trace field \(F\), \(B\in F\) with \(B>1\), \(E=\mathbb Q(B)\) and \([F:E]>2\), one has
\[
 e_K(B)=e_F(B)=
 \begin{cases}
 2e_E(B),& B^{1/(2e_E(B))}\in F,\\
 e_E(B),& B^{1/(2e_E(B))}\notin F .
 \end{cases}                                                           \tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(a_i\) is eventually coprime to \(e_{E_i}(B_i)\) and is odd whenever that integer doubles in (2).
The assertions are necessary conditions on a shrinking Salem sequence under (1).

<a id="capacity"></a>
## The degree of a fixed radical

Bounded degree and bounded total Mahler measure allow only finitely many integer minimal polynomials, so \(d_i\to\infty\).
For a Salem number every positive power generates its full root field.
Indeed, if two distinct conjugates have the same positive power, an automorphism of the normal closure sending the first to the positive exterior root takes the other to a different conjugate of the same exterior modulus, contradicting uniqueness.
Thus \(\alpha_i^{a_i}\ne B_i\) for all large indices, since the degrees of \(B_i\) are bounded.

Fix \(k\ge2\), and pass to a subsequence on which \(k\mid a_i\), \(B_i=B\) is fixed and \([K_i(c):K_i]=s\) is fixed, where \(c=B^{1/k}>1\).
There are only finitely many possibilities for \(B\) and \(1\le s\le k\).
Put \(Z_i=\alpha_i^{a_i/k}\), \(T_i=K_i(c)\), \(H=\mathbb Q(c)\).
Then \(T_i=H(Z_i)\) has degree \(sd_i\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
At the distinguished real embedding,
\[
 0<|Z_i-c|\ll e^{-b_i m_i},
\]
because the other factor in \(Z_i^k-c^k=(Z_i-c)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_i^{k-1-j}c^j\) is at least one.
Apply the product formula to \(Z_i-c\) over \(T_i\), counting each complex embedding separately in the sum over infinity.
The one distinguished summand is at most \((-b_i m_i+O(1))/(sd_i)\).
At every finite place, \(|Z_i|_v=1\) since \(Z_i\) is an algebraic unit, so \(\log|Z_i-c|_v\le\log^+|c|_v\).
Their total normalized contribution is at most the finite-place part of \(h(c)\).

For each embedding \(\tau:H\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), the images of \(Z_i\) under embeddings of \(T_i\) extending \(\tau\) form a full orbit over \(\tau H\), each member counted once since \(T_i=H(Z_i)\).
Their absolute height is \(a_i m_i/(kd_i)\to0\).
Degrees of these points over \(\mathbb Q\) tend to infinity, so on every subsequence one can pass to pairwise distinct points.
For each \(\tau\), the [equidistribution theorem over a fixed number field](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution) for \(z\mapsto z^2\) gives normalized angular measure on the unit circle in the limit.
All these conjugates lie in a fixed compact disk because \(a_i m_i\) is bounded.
For \(U>0\) the function \(\max(\log|z-\tau(c)|,-U)\), taking value \(-U\) at \(\tau(c)\), is continuous on that disk and has a continuous bounded extension to the sphere.
Deleting the distinguished embedding from the truncated normalized sum changes it by \(O_{U,c}(1/(sd_i))\).
Hence the sum of the remaining untruncated archimedean logarithms, normalized by \(sd_i\), has upper limit at most the average over \(\tau\) of the circle integrals of these truncated functions.
Every conjugate of \(B\) is real and differs from \(1,-1\), since \(B\) is in a totally real field and \(B>1\).
It follows that \(|\tau(c)|\ne1\) for every \(\tau\).
For sufficiently large fixed \(U\) the cutoff leaves the circle integrals unchanged, and the linear-factor mean formula gives \(\log^+|\tau(c)|\) for each of them.
The resulting average is the archimedean part of \(h(c)\).

The product formula now gives
\[
         \limsup_i \frac{b_i m_i}{sd_i}\le h(c)=\frac{h(B)}k .
\]
By (1) its left side is \(h(B)/s\), with \(h(B)>0\).
Thus \(s=k\).
The subsequence argument proves eventual equality for each fixed \(k\) at every index where \(k\mid a_i\).
In particular that assertion holds simultaneously on any fixed finite list of primes.

<a id="seed"></a>
## Positive radicals in a Salem root field

Let \(K,F,E,B\) be as in (2) and denote by \(\iota\) the automorphism of \(K/F\) sending \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
The field \(F\) is totally real.
Of its real embeddings precisely one has real extensions to \(K\), and on either extension \(\sigma\) of any other embedding one has \(\sigma\iota=\overline{\sigma}\), as seen on the generator \(\alpha\).
Let \(u=B^{1/j}\in K\).
Since the chosen field \(K\) is real and \(\iota(u)^j=B\), one has \(\iota(u)=\pm u\).
Therefore \(u^2\in F\).
All conjugates of \(u^2\) over \(E\) are real roots of \(X^j-B^2\), so \([E(u^2):E]\le2\).
Under \([F:E]>2\) the distinguished embedding of \(E(u^2)\) has at least two extensions to \(F\).
Choose one which extends to a nonreal embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K\).
If \(\iota(u)=-u\), then \(\overline{\sigma(u)}=-\sigma(u)\), giving \(\sigma(u)^2<0\).
But \(\sigma(u^2)=u^2>0\) by construction.
It follows that \(u\in F\).
Every conjugate of \(u\) over \(E\) is now a real root of \(X^j-B\), hence is among \(u,-u\).
Thus \(u^2\in E\), and if \(j\) is odd then \(u\in E\).

For any fixed \(D,B\) in the definition of \(e_D(B)\), the possible exponents are finite by bounded degree and \(h(B^{1/j})=h(B)/j\).
They are exactly the positive divisors of \(e_D(B)\).
Indeed the set is closed under divisors, and Bézout's identity on exponents shows it is closed under least common multiples, using positivity of the real radicals.
For \(j\) odd with \(B^{1/j}\in K\) this gives \(j\mid e_E(B)\).
For \(j\) even we have \(B^{2/j}\in E\) and \(j/2\mid e_E(B)\).
Since every such radical in \(K\) lies in \(F\), these divisibilities prove (2).

<a id="all-divisors"></a>
## Uniformity for unbounded divisors

Since \(d_i\to\infty\) and \(B_i\) ranges through a finite set, \([F_i:E_i]>2\) eventually.
By (2) every prime factor of \(e_{K_i}(B_i)\) belongs to the fixed finite set of primes dividing at least one of \(2e_{\mathbb Q(B)}(B)\) for the allowed seeds \(B\).
For any such prime \(p\) dividing \(a_i\), the [fixed radical degree](#capacity) yields \([K_i(B_i^{1/p}):K_i]=p\) at all sufficiently large indices, so \(p\nmid e_{K_i}(B_i)\).
This proves the eventual coprimality.

At any of those indices and for any \(k\mid a_i\), put \(u=B_i^{1/k}\) and \(s=[K_i(u):K_i]\le k\).
The constant term of the monic minimal polynomial of \(u\) over \(K_i\) is real, and every one of its \(s\) roots in \(\mathbb C\) has modulus \(u\).
The absolute value of that constant term is therefore \(u^s\in K_i\).
Writing \(g=\gcd(s,k)\), Bézout's identity with \(u^k=B_i\) gives \(u^g=B_i^{1/(k/g)}\in K_i\).
If \(s<k\), the integer \(k/g>1\) divides both \(a_i\) and \(e_{K_i}(B_i)\), contrary to coprimality.
Hence \(s=k\), proving irreducibility for all the divisors at once.
Irreducibility over the larger field implies irreducibility over \(E_i\), and preservation of the degree of this simple extension after adjoining \(K_i\) is exactly linear disjointness.
