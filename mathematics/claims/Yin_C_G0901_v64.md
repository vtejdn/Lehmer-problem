# Finite tests for bounded exterior conjugates in the divisible group of an arbitrary unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree and measure bounds for the witnesses

Use the [divisible group \(D(w)\) and the reciprocal, neutral class \(\mathcal B_k\)](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#statement) for a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\) and \(k\ge1\).
Let \(V_w\) be the rational span of its conjugates modulo torsion, \(b=\dim V_w\), and \(d_w=\deg w\).
For every nontorsion reciprocal \(x\in D(w)\), let \(l(x)\) be its number of exterior conjugates and \(r(x)\) its [rank of conjugate log moduli](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#statement).
Then
\[
       (\deg x)\,r(x)\ \le\ 2 l(x)\dim V_x\ \le\ 2 l(x)b.                \tag{1}
\]
Here \(V_x\) is the corresponding rational span for \(x\).
In particular every witness in \(\mathcal B_k\), even before replacing it by a small one, has degree at most \(2kb\).
Always \(b\le d_w-1\) by the unit norm relation, and \(b\le d_w/2\) when \(w\) is reciprocal.

There is a finite test for \(D(w)\cap\mathcal B_k\ne\varnothing\) that does not require a basis of the unit group of any field.
Enumerate reciprocal monic integer polynomials of degree at most \(2kb\) and total logarithmic measure at most \(2k\,m(w)\), retaining irreducible inputs with a neutral conjugate and at most \(k\) exterior conjugates.
The preceding bounds on \(b\) can replace \(b\) throughout.
For each conjugate \(a\) of each retained input use its root field \(K=\mathbb Q(a)\), its reciprocal involution \(\tau\), and compute
\[
                     v=N_{K(w)/K}w,\qquad v/\tau v .
\]
A witness exists exactly when one of these quotients is nontorsion.

The analogous finite test for \(D(w)\) to contain a complex Salem number uses just complex Salem inputs, degree at most \(4b\) and total measure at most \(4m(w)\); at the [small-measure threshold](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#statement) for \(k=2\), the measure cutoff can be \(2m(w)\).
An alternative rational linear-algebra test below determines the minimum exterior count and the real or complex Salem condition directly when the \(G\)-module \(V_w\) with complex conjugation is supplied.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Counting nonzero projections in a conjugate orbit

View \(V_x\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R\) in a finite common normal closure, with \(c\) its ordinary complex conjugation.
Index the vectors \(v_i=[\sigma_i x]\) by the \(d=\deg x\) embeddings, allowing vectors equal modulo torsion to occur more than once.
Put \(a=\dim V_x\) in this paragraph.
The surjection from \(\mathbb R^d\) sending the \(i\)-th standard basis vector to \(v_i\) is equivariant for the permutation action on embeddings.
Give the codomain the Euclidean structure from the orthogonal complement of the kernel, on which the surjection is now an isometry.
Writing this map as \(T\), one has \(TT^*=I\), or \(\sum_i v_i v_i^*=I\).
The action preserves the inner product and is transitive on the embeddings, so taking traces gives \(\|v_i\|^2=a/d\) for every \(i\).

For the orthogonal projection \(P=(1+c)/2\) the [fixed-space formula](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules) gives \(\operatorname{tr}P=r(x)\).
The equality \(Pv_i=0\) is equivalent to \(|\sigma_i x|=1\), since \((\sigma_i x)c(\sigma_i x)\) is positive real and its class then vanishes exactly when it equals one.
There are precisely \(2l(x)\) nonneutral embeddings by reciprocity.
Consequently
\[
     r(x)=\operatorname{tr}(PTT^*)=\sum_i\|Pv_i\|^2
             \le 2l(x)a/d .
\]
This proves (1) with no condition of irreducibility on the module.

## Exhausting the norm tests

A nontorsion quotient at any retained \(a\) belongs to the ordinary group generated by conjugates of \(w\), has relative norm one and has neutral and exterior counts bounded as in the [relative norm construction](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#detection).
Conversely, if a witness exists, [bounded-measure transfer](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#cost) produces a witness \(x\) of measure at most \(2k\,m(w)\).
By (1) it is in the enumerated degree range.
The inner-product argument applied to \(x\in D(w)\) gives a conjugate \(a\) of this very witness with nontorsion quotient in the test.

For a complex Salem witness \(a\), the nontorsion relative norm-one output has nonneutral values only above its lone complex trace place.
It has either a complex conjugate pair of exterior conjugates or a single repeated real value in the root field of \(a\).
As in the [complex Salem transfer](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#limits), select its exterior conjugate in the first case, or a complex quadratic lift of its positive signed real Salem value in the second case.
Both lie in \(D(w)\) at the same total measure as the norm-one output.
This argument works also with \(v/\tau v\) at unrestricted input measure, giving \(4m(w)\), and with \(v\) at the smaller threshold, giving \(2m(w)\).
Apply (1) to the resulting complex Salem number itself for the degree bound.
Conversely a nontorsion quotient at any complex Salem test input gives such an output by the same argument.

The integer coefficient of order \(j\) of a monic polynomial of degree \(d\) has absolute value at most \(\binom{d}{j}\exp(m)\) by its elementary symmetric expression.
Hence one can enumerate a finite integer box, using any upper rational estimate on the multiplicative cutoff if desired; accepting a nontorsion norm quotient at an extra test input of the required signature is still sufficient.
Root counts and modulus comparisons use algebraic real numbers, and whether an algebraic number in \(K\) is a root of unity can be decided by its minimum polynomial and Kronecker's theorem.
The bounds prove termination and do not assert practical cost at large degree.

<a id="arrangement"></a>
## Computing the minimum count from rational subspaces

Let \(G\) now be the Galois group of the splitting field of \(w\).
For \(0\ne q\in V_w\) write
\[
 H(q)=\{g:gq=q\},\quad I(q)=\{g:gq=-q\},\quad
 Z(q)=\{g:(1+c)gq=0\},\quad R(q)=\{g:(1-c)gq=0\}.
\]
Realize a positive multiple of \(q\) as a monomial \(y\) in the conjugates of \(w\).
Raise \(y\) to a common even multiple of the order of the finite group of roots of unity of the splitting field.
For the resulting \(x\), distinct conjugates correspond exactly to the points of \(Gq\): equality of two classes means they differed only by torsion before powering.
By the same argument inverse classes give actual inverse values, and classes fixed by \(c\) give actual real values.
Neutral values are exactly those indexed by \(Z(q)\).
Thus \(x\) is reciprocal exactly when \(I(q)\ne\varnothing\).
For such a vector with \(Z(q)\ne\varnothing\), the number of exterior conjugates and the number of real exterior conjugates of \(x\) are respectively
\[
             \frac{|G|-|Z(q)|}{2|H(q)|},\qquad
             \frac{|R(q)|}{2|H(q)|}.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the \(H(q)\) cosets count the distinct conjugates, and inversion pairs exterior and interior values and preserves reality.
A real neutral conjugate would equal \(1\) or \(-1\), impossible for a nontorsion unit.

Minimize the first count in (2) over these \(q\), taking the minimum to be infinite when there are none.
This returns precisely the minimum exterior count over all reciprocal elements of \(D(w)\) having a neutral conjugate.
For the converse comparison, an arbitrary such element has a positive power in the monomial group, and all conjugates of any further power are powers of original conjugates.
The distinct exterior count can only decrease; reciprocity and a neutral conjugate persist.
Similarly \(D(w)\) contains a complex Salem number exactly when a pattern has first count one, or first count two and real count zero.
A complex Salem input remains complex Salem on powering and identifying torsion classes unless its two exterior values become the same real value, in which case the first count becomes one.
In the converse direction one exterior root gives a signed real Salem value and its complex Salem lift.

For each \(g\in G\) form the four rational subspaces cut out by the linear equations defining \(H,I,Z,R\).
There are finitely many intersections of subcollections of these subspaces, including the whole space.
For each nonzero intersection \(W\), take a rational point outside the union of its intersections with defining subspaces not containing \(W\), also excluding zero.
Such a point exists since finitely many proper linear subspaces cannot cover a rational vector space.
Ordinary enumeration of rational vectors can find it.
Every nonzero point of \(V_w\) has the same incidence pattern as one such chosen point, by intersecting exactly the defining subspaces containing it.
This gives an alternative finite test and the asserted minimum.
