# An inverse bound for the Mahler measure of an arbitrary integer polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reducible polynomials and primitive values

Let \(P\ne0\) be an arbitrary integer polynomial, \(A=M(P)>1\) its multiplicative Mahler measure, and \(m=\log A\).
There is no bound on its number of irreducible factors, their multiplicities, their degrees or the coefficients of \(P\).
For sufficiently small \(m>0\), either

* \(A=\rho^j\), where \(j\ge1\) and \(\rho>1\) is a reciprocal Perron unit primitive modulo torsion on its rational line in \(\mathbb Q(A)\). Every positive power of \(\rho\) generates this same field, and \(m(\rho)=\log M(\rho)\) is at most the positive logarithmic measure of one irreducible factor of \(P\); or
* for any fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), at sufficiently small \(m\) in terms of \(\eta\) one has
  \[
                         m(A)\ \ge\
             \exp\!\big(\eta\,m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).                     \tag{1}
  \]

In particular (1) holds for nonreciprocal values and for primitive non-Salem values tending to one, even for reducible polynomials.
For either exclusion, under the bound \(m(A)\le B\),
\[
        \log A\ \ge\ \bigl(\tfrac14-o(1)\bigr)
                \left(\frac{\log\log B}{\log B}\right)^3
                    \qquad(B\longrightarrow\infty).                     \tag{2}
\]
If \(A\) is primitive and the first alternative applies, \(A\) is real Salem and exactly one factor of \(P\) has positive log measure, with multiplicity one.
The nonnegative-recurrence exclusion for [single-number preimages](Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#inverse) therefore excludes integer-polynomial preimages as well, uniformly at bounded coefficient sum of the recurrence.

<a id="modules"></a>
## The combined constituent spaces of all the small factors

Ignore powers of \(X\).
At \(1<A<2\) the integer content and both outside nonzero coefficients have absolute value one, by the inequalities of Mahler measure with the absolute values of those coefficients.
Up to sign all factors are monic with constant coefficient a unit.
The cyclotomic factors have measure one and do not affect \(A\).
Choose one root \(w_j\) for each noncyclotomic factor, repeating according to multiplicity.
Then \(\sum_j m(w_j)=m\), so all the [small-measure constituent chains](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent) apply with a common threshold.
The product \(A=\prod_j M(w_j)\) lies in the divisible group of all the conjugates of the inputs, since each measure is a signed exterior product.
It is a positive Perron unit: if a rational Galois automorphism preserves the modulus of this product, it must preserve the maximal modulus of every factor \(M(w_j)\) and therefore fixes each factor as a number. [Perron property for an exterior product](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field)
Consequently \(\deg A\) is the orbit size of its class, and every positive power generates the same field as \(A\).

Associate to each chain term \(z\) its leading absolutely irreducible module \(W\), on which the Galois group of the splitting field \(L_z\) acts faithfully. [Leading kernel field](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#chain)
Two isomorphic leading modules in this collection have the same kernel field.
The [two-field comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields) at a fixed common bound of one then makes their corresponding \(z,z'\) match, since their measures are bounded by \(m\) and every chain degree tends uniformly to infinity as \(m\to0\).
Matching identifies the actual rational class spaces \(V_z=V_{z'}\).
Their leading subspaces coincide as actual subspaces: this common module is multiplicity free and its unique simple summand of largest dimension is leading in either chain.
Conversely matching chain terms have the same leading subspace by the same argument.
Keep one representative \(z\) for each distinct leading module.
The combined span of all conjugates of the \(w_j\) is therefore a direct sum of actual pairwise inequivalent irreducible subspaces.
This uses semisimplicity for their joint finite Galois action and is independent of the number of inputs.

In this sum take just the summands with nonzero component of \([A]\).
The Galois span \(V_A\) is their full direct sum because each type occurs only once.
Consequently \(L(V_A)\), the fixed field of the kernel of the action on \(V_A\), is the compositum of the splitting fields \(L_z\) attached to these active summands.
It equals the splitting field of \(A\), which is already compressed by its strict Perron property.
Choose one active \(z\) of largest compressed degree \(d\), and denote its nonzero component by \(v\in W\).
The positive log measure \(m(z)\) is at most that of one original factor.

<a id="quantitative"></a>
## An unmatched product has large total measure

Suppose \(A\) does not match this \(z\).
We claim
\[
                       m(A)m(z)\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}.                  \tag{3}
\]
Let \(D=\deg A\).
For \(D\le d^6\), the orbit of \(v\) has at most \(D\) elements.
Irrespective of the other components it marks a nonempty set of at most 36 inverse pairs in the representation of \(z\): apply the [signed support or majority-coordinate argument](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#few) to \(W\).
Thus some conjugate \(z'\) of \(z\) has degree at most \(72\) over \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
The nonmatching hypothesis gives multiplicative independence, and the rank-two height product at joint degree at most \(72D\) proves (3).

For \(D>d^6\), the other active representatives are unmatched to \(z\) and have degrees at most \(d\).
The [unmatched-field theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) gives a lift of a supported label three-cycle on \(z\), fixing their entire splitting fields.
In their common compositum its fourth power is an order-three element with conjugacy class of size \(O(d^3)\), as it is determined by its action on just three inverse pairs of \(z\) and fixes the other normal fields.
It acts nontrivially on \(W\), both in the full signed and in the sum-zero representation.
Some conjugate of this element therefore sends \([A]\) to an independent vector; otherwise its normal closure would fix \(v\) and the irreducible space it generates.
The resulting two conjugates of \(A\) have joint degree \(O(Dd^3)\).
Applying the [large-orbit estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#many) to these independent units and the [Voutier estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) to \(z\) proves (3).
All constants are absolute and all the block-size cutoffs use \(m(z)\le m<1\).
With (3), the [degree inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v117.md#iterate) applies verbatim to \(m(z)\le m\), giving (1) and (2) in this case.

<a id="match"></a>
## Primitive values admit no splitting of their positive measure

If the chosen \(z\) matches \(A\), take a conjugate \(z'\) whose class is proportional to \([A]\).
It is real, since ordinary conjugation fixes that class and \(z\) is compressed.
The stabilizers of \([A]\) and of \([z']\) agree, and they fix the corresponding actual values by the Perron property and compression, respectively.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(A)=\mathbb Q(z')\) in this embedding.
Take the primitive unit \(\rho>1\) on this rational line in the common real field, choosing its sign and orientation there.
Its roots of unity are just the signs.
The [line saturation argument](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#primitive) gives \(A=\rho^j\) with \(j\ge1\), \(m(\rho)\le m(z)\), and full degree of all powers.
The Perron property of \(A\) makes \(\rho\) Perron too.
Since \(m(\rho)\le m\), [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes \(\rho\) and hence all its powers reciprocal at the stated smallness threshold.

If \(A\) is primitive, then \(j=1\).
For the factor \(w_h\) supplying \(z\) we have
\[
          \log A\le m(\rho)\le m(z)\le m(w_h)
                         \le\sum_j m(w_j)=\log A .
\]
The first inequality is the contribution of the real exterior root \(\rho=A\).
Equality throughout makes \(w_h\) the sole positive-measure factor counted with multiplicity, and \(M(A)=A\).
The degree tends to infinity in this small case because the matched chain degrees do.
A large reciprocal fixed unit has one real exterior root, its inverse interior root and all other conjugates neutral; it is Salem.
This proves both exclusions and the irreducible-factor assertion.
