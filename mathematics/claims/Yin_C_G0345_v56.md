# First powers admitting torsion interlacing, with a fixed-degree obstruction

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound for an actual exponent

Let \(\sigma\) be a Salem number of degree \(2m\), put \(k=m-1\), and write \(d\) for the degree of its splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\).
Put \(L=\lceil9k^{3/2}\rceil\).
With \(\delta=1-\cos(\pi/(4k))\), some integer
\[
  1\le n\le 1+\left\lceil 8k L^k(1+\sigma^L)^{d-1}/\delta\right\rceil
\]
has the property that \(D_k\) strictly interlaces the trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^n\).
One may replace \(d\) by \(2^m m!\).
Consequently the least successful power has a polynomial upper bound in \(\sigma\) for each fixed degree.
The dependence on the size of \(\sigma\) cannot be removed even if the degree of the monic integer torsion interlacer can be chosen freely between two and \(k\).

More precisely, for every fixed \(m\ge3\) there are Salem numbers \(\sigma\to\infty\) of degree \(2m\) for which none of the powers with \(1\le n\le c_m\sigma^{1/(2(m-1))}\) admits a monic integer partial interlacer of degree at least two made from distinct torsion trace nodes.
Here partial interlacing requires at least one trace in each of the open intervals cut out by the nodes.
These examples concern large Salem numbers at fixed degree.
The upper bound has no uniformity in the degree that could give a gap at one by itself.

<a id="test"></a>
## A finite trigonometric test supported in frequency

Choose one unit conjugate \(u_j=e^{2\pi i\theta_j}\) from each neutral inverse pair.
The [Salem power calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers) gives their independence modulo roots of unity, degree preservation under powering, and the \(k\) arcs on the upper semicircle in which we need a conjugate.
Their normalized angular lengths are \(1/(4k)\) for the bin next to \(-2\), and \(1/(2k)\) for the other \(k-1\) bins.
Assign one such interval to each coordinate and write \(x_j\) for its midpoint in \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).
If every \(n\theta_j\) is at circular distance less than \(s=1/(8k)\) from \(x_j\), then strict interlacing follows, the exterior trace supplying the final interval.

Set \(a_l=\sin((l+1)\pi/(L+1))\) for \(0\le l<L\) and
\[
 K(\theta)=\frac{\left|\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}a_l e^{2\pi i l\theta}\right|^2}{\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}a_l^2}.
\]
This is a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial of integral one whose Fourier coefficients have absolute sum at most \(L\), by Cauchy–Schwarz on \(\sum a_l\).
Its cosine moment is \(\cos(\pi/(L+1))\).
Indeed \(a_{l-1}+a_{l+1}=2\cos(\pi/(L+1))a_l\) upon setting \(a_{-1}=a_L=0\), and integration against \(\cos(2\pi\theta)\) gives \(\sum_{l=0}^{L-2}a_la_{l+1}/\sum a_l^2\).

In coordinates \(v_j\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), take the test function
\[
 F(\mathbf v)=
 \prod_{j=1}^k K(v_j-x_j)
 \left(\delta-\sum_{j=1}^k(1-\cos(2\pi(v_j-x_j)))\right).
\]
A positive value implies membership in the required open box.
Its frequencies satisfy \(\|\mathbf l\|_\infty\le L\), and its sum of absolute Fourier coefficients is at most \(2k L^k\), since \(0<\delta\le k\).
Its integral is \(\delta-k(1-\cos(\pi/(L+1)))\), greater than \(\delta/2\).
Indeed \(\delta\ge1/(8k^2)\) from the chord bound on sine, while \(k(1-\cos(\pi/(L+1)))\le k\pi^2/(2(L+1)^2)<1/(16k^2)\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Separation of a nontrivial character value from one

For every nonzero frequency in this box, the unit \(\gamma=\prod u_j^{l_j}\) differs from one.
Under any automorphism of the splitting field, at most one of the selected conjugates can land in the exterior inverse pair, because the inverse pairs are permuted.
Hence \(|g\gamma|\le\sigma^L\).
The nonzero integer norm of \(1-\gamma\) gives
\[
                    |1-\gamma|\ge(1+\sigma^L)^{-(d-1)}.
\]
The degree bound follows since the Galois action embeds into the group of signed permutations of the \(m\) inverse pairs.
For the first \(N\) powers the absolute mean of any nonconstant such character is at most \(2/(N|1-\gamma|)\), by the finite geometric sum.
The empirical mean of \(F\) therefore exceeds its integral minus \(4kL^k(1+\sigma^L)^{d-1}/N\).
At the stated integer upper bound it is positive, which proves that a successful exponent occurs in this range.

<a id="examples"></a>
## A family in every fixed degree at least six

Agent-S found the fixed-degree obstruction by placing neutral traces close to two in an Eisenstein cubic.
For each \(m\ge3\) choose distinct positive integers \(c_1,\ldots,c_{m-1}\) with \(c_1=2\) and all the others odd.
For even integers \(b\to\infty\) define the trace polynomial by
\[
            S_b(2+y)=y^m-\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(by+c_j).
\]
It is irreducible by Eisenstein at two: every nonleading coefficient is even and the constant term is not divisible by four.
At \(y=v/b\) the expression tends uniformly on compact sets to \(-\prod(v+c_j)\).
Opposite signs on the sides of each simple root give \(m-1\) neutral trace roots \(2-(c_j+o(1))/b\).
The remaining root is real and is \(2+b^{m-1}+O(1/b)\) by the coefficient of \(y^{m-1}\).
The irreducible totally real trace field has a neutral embedding where \(x^2-4<0\), so \(x^2-4\) is not a square in the field.
Consequently the reciprocal lift is an irreducible Salem polynomial of degree \(2m\), with exterior root \(\sigma_b\sim b^{m-1}\).

The largest neutral angle is \(O_m(b^{-1/2})\) after fixing the \(c_j\) in terms of \(m\).
There are finitely many torsion traces of degree at most \(m-1\), by the [cyclotomic degree count](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#count).
Let \(x_*<2\) be the largest of those strictly below two.
For a sufficiently small fixed \(c_m'>0\), every neutral trace of every power with \(n\le c_m'\sqrt b\) lies strictly above \(x_*\).
Its exterior trace also lies above \(x_*\).
A monic partial torsion interlacer in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) can have degree at most \(m-1\), so each node has algebraic degree at most \(m-1\), and at degree at least two its least node is at most \(x_*\).
No powered trace lies in the interval below that node.
This proves the asserted obstruction after adjusting the constant.

The scale of angular confinement at two in this construction is sharp up to a constant for fixed \(m\).
For any degree-\(2m\) Salem number with exterior trace \(T\) and neutral traces \(x_j=2\cos\theta_j\), the nonzero integer \(|S(2)|=(T-2)\prod_j(2-x_j)\) is at least one.
Since \(2-x_j\le\theta_j^2\), the maximum neutral angle is at least \((T-2)^{-1/(2(m-1))}\).
This bounds the confinement mechanism only.
