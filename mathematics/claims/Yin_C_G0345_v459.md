# Compressing the width of signed rows while preserving contact multiplicities

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finite widths for prescribed coefficients and contact conditions

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_s\) be a nonempty finite list of monic irreducible integer polynomials with nonzero constant terms and Mahler measures \(M(P_i)>1\).
Set \(\eta=\min_i\log M(P_i)/\deg P_i>0\) and fix a positive integer \(a\).
Given an exponent \(n\ge1\), a formal width \(b\ge0\), and a nonzero integer polynomial \(A(Y)=\sum_{j\in J}c_jY^j\) supported on \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,b\}\), write
\[
 K=|J|,\qquad H=\|A\|_1,\qquad
 U=A(X^n),\qquad V=X^{nb}A(X^{-n}),\qquad F_\pm=U\pm XV .
\]
All \(c_j\) on \(J\) are nonzero.
For an integer polynomial \(W\), let \(\nu_i(W)\) be the multiplicity of \(P_i\) in \(W\), with \(\nu_i(0)=+\infty\).
Choose an integer \(D\ge2\) satisfying
\[
 (nD-1)\eta>\log(2H)+(a-1)\log(1+2nKD).                    \tag{1}
\]
There is a width \(b'\le\min\{b,(2K-1)D\}\) and a row \(A'\) with the same ordered list of nonzero coefficients as \(A\).
Form \(U',V',F_\pm'\) with \(A',b'\) at the same \(n\).
For every \(i\) and each corresponding pair \(W,W'\) in these four families,
\[
                     \min\{a,\nu_i(W)\}=\min\{a,\nu_i(W')\}. \tag{2}
\]
If preservation of \(b\bmod 2\) is also required, the same conclusions hold with \(b'\le\min\{b,(2K-1)D+1\}\).

In particular all divisibilities \(P_i^{a_i}\mid F_\pm\) for \(1\le a_i\le a\) and all numerator conditions \(A(c^n)\ne0,\ A(c^{-n})\ne0\) at roots \(c\) of the respective \(P_i\) survive simultaneously in both directions.
Indeed conjugate roots of the same irreducible polynomial have the same multiplicity, and \(c^{nb}\ne0\).
The coefficient norm, cardinality and content of the row are preserved, and every upper budget nondecreasing in its formal width still holds.
This includes the genuine signed contacts for reciprocal unit minima, with either sign and without any restriction on quotients between distinct roots.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## Splitting at a gap after taking derivatives within each block

We use the product-formula gap argument of [Lenstra, Proposition 2.3, as previously applied to integral unit factors](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts).
For the required integral version let \(\alpha\ne0\) have monic minimum \(P\) of degree \(d\), and suppose \(L(X)+X^tR(X)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), where \(\deg L\le u<t\) and both coefficient norms are at most \(B\ge1\).
If \(R(\alpha)\ne0\), its norm is a nonzero rational integer.
At an exterior conjugate \(z\) one has \(|R(z)|=|z|^{-t}|L(z)|\le B|z|^{u-t}\), and at each other conjugate \(|R(z)|\le B\).
Multiplying gives \(1\le B^d M(P)^{u-t}\).
Thus \((t-u)\log M(P)/d>\log B\) forces both pieces to vanish.
The estimate applies to every prefix cut of a sum of disjoint blocks using the same bound on the total coefficient norm.
All the resulting prefix values vanish, so taking consecutive differences gives vanishing of the individual block values.

List the elements of \(S=J\cup(b-J)\) in increasing order and split at every consecutive difference at least \(D\).
Write \(l_q,r_q\) for the first and last positions in block \(q\).
Each block spans fewer than \(2KD\) positions in the sense \(r_q-l_q<2KD\), since \(|S|\le2K\).
For any of \(W=U,V,F_+,F_-\) write
\[
                    W(X)=\sum_q X^{nl_q} B_q(X)
\]
by assigning the terms from position \(s\in S\) to its block and removing that block's initial monomial \(X^{nl_q}\).
The forward term from \(j\) is at \(s=j\), and the reversed term is at \(s=b-j\).
Depending on \(W\), the actual exponents are \(ns\) or \(ns+1\).
In particular each \(B_q\) has degree at most \(n(r_q-l_q)+1\) when nonzero, and \(\sum_q\|B_q\|_1\le2H\).
Coefficient cancellations within a block are allowed.
The gap between the last possible actual exponent of a block and the first possible exponent of the next is at least \(nD-1\).

For each \(i\) and \(0\le k\le a\) we claim
\[
         P_i^k\mid W\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad P_i^k\mid B_q
                                  \ \text{for every }q .       \tag{3}
\]
The reverse implication holds by summation.
The forward implication is automatic for \(k=0\).
Suppose it holds for \(k<a\), and \(P_i^{k+1}\mid W\).
At a root \(\alpha\) of \(P_i\) all Euler derivatives \(E^j B_q(\alpha)\) for \(j<k\) vanish by induction, where \(E=X\,d/dX\).
At a nonzero root in characteristic zero, vanishing of the Euler derivatives through order \(k\) is equivalent to multiplicity at least \(k+1\), by the triangular relation with ordinary derivatives.
Using \(E^k(X^mB)=X^m(E+m)^kB\), we obtain
\[
               0=E^k W(\alpha)=\sum_q \alpha^{nl_q} E^kB_q(\alpha).
\]
The polynomial on the right before evaluation has the same block envelopes, and its total coefficient norm is at most \(2H(1+2nKD)^k\).
Only the exponents within the normalized blocks enter this bound.
Condition (1) and the gap argument force each \(E^k B_q(\alpha)=0\), proving (3) by induction.

<a id="geometry"></a>
## Moving the blocks and their reflected partners

Map the least element of \(S\) to zero and replace each successive difference \(g\) by \(\min\{g,D\}\).
Denote the resulting increasing map by \(\phi\) and put \(b'=\phi(\max S)\).
Reflection about \(b/2\) reverses the ordered list of \(S\), so its consecutive differences form a palindrome.
The new differences retain this symmetry, giving \(\phi(b-s)=b'-\phi(s)\).
The two empty margins of size \(\min S\) have both been removed, and each new difference is bounded by its old value and by \(D\).
This proves the asserted width bound even when \(S\) is a singleton.

Take \(A'(Y)=\sum_{j\in J}c_jY^{\phi(j)}\).
Within each block the map is a translation, and the reflection identity places every reversed term at \(\phi(b-j)\).
Hence each \(W'\) has exactly the same normalized polynomials \(B_q\) as \(W\), now translated by \(n\phi(l_q)\).
The spaces between successive blocks still give actual exponent gaps of at least \(nD-1\).
Applying (3) in both layouts proves (2).

Paired differences contribute an even sum to either width, as do the removed margins.
When there is a single central difference \(g\ge D\), parity can therefore be preserved by replacing it with \(D\) or \(D+1\), whichever has the same parity as \(g\).
That choice is at most \(g\) and keeps the cut intact.
In every other case the original construction already preserves parity.

<a id="budget"></a>
## A computable uniform cutoff under an exponential norm budget

For fixed \(n,K,H,\eta,a\), condition (1) holds for all sufficiently large integers \(D\), because its left side grows linearly and its right side grows at most logarithmically in \(D\).
More strongly, suppose \(\log H\le Cn\) for some fixed \(C\ge0\).
One can use any integer \(D\ge2\) satisfying
\[
          (D-1)\eta>C+\log 2+(a-1)\bigl(\log(3D)+1+C\bigr).  \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(K\le H\), \(1+2nKD\le3nKD\), \(\log n\le n\), and \(nD-1\ge n(D-1)\).
These inequalities give (1) for every \(n\ge1\), even as \(K\) varies.
A supplied positive rational lower bound for \(\eta\) and an upper bound for \(C\) suffice to find such a \(D\) by increasing the trial values with certified numerical bounds.
For listed integer minima with \(M(P_i)>1\) a positive lower bound follows effectively by isolating exterior roots and bounding their moduli from below.

For fixed \(K\) and an exponential norm budget there are therefore just finitely many widths and position sets to test, uniformly over all exponents at which the budget holds.
For individual \(n\) the coefficient budget is finite as well, so existence at any width subject to the stated contact, numerator and width-monotone upper bounds is equivalent to existence in the finite range.
If only a norm bound \(H\le H_0\) is supplied at this exponent, the search can take \(K\le H_0\) and use \(H_0\) in (1) for both \(K\) and \(H\).

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed folding the support, splitting at gaps by the norm inequality and centering the Euler derivatives within each block.
A collaborating agent developed the simultaneous equivalence for both numerators and both signs, the parity adjustment and the uniform cutoff.
