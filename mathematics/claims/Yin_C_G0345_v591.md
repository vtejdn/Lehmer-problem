# Primitive marked patterns with bounded-degree coefficients at Salem numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The primitive count for growing coefficient degree

Let \(\alpha>1\) run through any sequence of Salem numbers with \(m=\log\alpha\to0\), put \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\), and choose integers \(1\le B\le d/4\) with \(Bm\to0\).
For weights \(r\to\infty\) there is a single fixed ordered tuple of \(r-1\) nonzero algebraic integers in \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), each of degree at most \(B\), with at least
\[
 \exp\big((1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m)\big)
\]
distinct primitive marked exponent lists.
Each list has a positive integral dilation giving a relation with these outgoing coefficients, a nonzero integral output of degree at most \(B\) at exponent zero, and full [degree-bounded terminality](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
The outgoing exponents are sorted, and their primitive list is obtained by dividing by their positive gcd.
The coefficients, output and dilated exponents can all satisfy the [height and exponent windows](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement).

<a id="trace"></a>
## Subfields and the finite-window count

Write \(\iota(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}\) for the nontrivial automorphism over \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
For every neutral embedding \(\sigma\) (one with \(|\sigma(\alpha)|=1\)), complex conjugation after \(\sigma\) equals \(\sigma\iota\), as seen on \(\alpha\).
If \(c\in K\) and \(c\ne\iota(c)\), all neutral embeddings send \(c\) to nonreal values.
The two real embeddings give two distinct real values of \(c\).
Each real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(c)\) must extend to \([K:\mathbb Q(c)]\) embeddings of \(K\), so the real value \(c\) itself has just one extension and \(K=\mathbb Q(c)\).
Consequently every proper subfield of \(K\) lies in \(F\), and the relative norm of \(\alpha\) to any such field is 1 by transitivity from \(N_{K/F}(\alpha)=1\).
In particular every positive power of \(\alpha\) generates \(K\), since \(\iota\) moves it.

The [edge estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#edges) and [tree count](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#count) therefore apply to these Salem inputs, since the proper-subfield norm bound and the equality of the power fields were the two consequences of the strict degree record used there.
More explicitly for an edge \(z=c\alpha^n\), taking the norm to \(\mathbb Q(c)\) gives \(h(c)\le h(z)\), hence \(|n|h(\alpha)\le2h(z)\).
The same original support interval, marked trees and full splitting rule now yield \(r\to\infty\), coefficient and output heights at most \(Y=O(\log(2d))\), and at least \((c_0/m)^{r-2}\) sorted lists for one fixed tuple, for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Here \(h\) denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.

<a id="groups"></a>
## Distinct nonzero exponents in a large family

Keep one pattern of consecutive equalities and signs of the sorted exponents with the largest number of lists.
The equality cuts have at most \(2^{r-2}\) possibilities, and specifying the two boundaries between negative, zero and positive groups costs at most \((r+1)^2\).
Thus the retained family has logarithmic size at least \((r-2)\log(1/m)-O(r)\).
Each group has a fixed nonzero coefficient equal to the sum of its original coefficients, since a vanishing group would give a proper zero subsum after including the marked term.
Let \(k\) count its distinct nonzero exponent groups, and retain the zero group when present.
The [mixed embedding count](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings) for the grouped outgoing terms bounds the number of such lists by \(2B\max_{1\le j\le k+2}H_j\), taking unused constants to be at least one.
Indeed all the grouped coefficients are fixed, a nonempty zero subsum of outgoing groups would give a zero subsum of original terms, and the degree restriction in this count is on the output alone.
The group exponents determine the original list because the group sizes are fixed.
Consequently \(k\to\infty\), since bounded \(k\) would give \(O_k(B)=o(1/m)\) lists.
Let \(g\) be the least size among the nonzero exponent groups, so \(g\le(r-1)/k=o(r)\).

<a id="dilations"></a>
## Counting dilations on a retained ray

Fix a primitive list in the retained family and consider only dilations giving one of its realizations.
Write \(b_w\) for the grouped coefficient at primitive exponent \(w\), and put \(b_0=0\) when the constant group is absent.
For \(w\ne0\) let \(S\) be the larger of the positive and negative sides' sums of \(|b_w|\) at the identity embedding.
Evaluate at the identity if the positive side wins, or at \(\iota\) if the negative side wins, reversing the signs of the exponents in the latter case.
All original coefficients and the output belong to \(F\), so both real embeddings agree on them.
The chosen side is now a sum with positive exponents evaluated at \(\alpha\), and the opposite side has absolute value at most \(S\).

For every \(A\ge1\), the [separated evaluation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#matrix) bounds the number of positive dilations at which the chosen sum has modulus at most \(AS\) by
\[
 r\left\lceil \log(64r^2(1+A))/m\right\rceil.
\]
Indeed with this separation the inverse evaluation matrix has entries of modulus at most \(4\exp(-m\lceil\log(64r^2(1+A))/m\rceil)\), so inversion at separated hits would give a total coefficient mass strictly smaller than \(S\).
The greedy selection argument in the cited estimate gives the bound for arbitrary hits.

If \(B^r<d/2\), adjoining the outgoing coefficients and each particular output to \(\mathbb Q\) yields a field of degree at most \(B^r\).
Its identity embedding has more than two extensions to \(K\), including a neutral one.
At that embedding the equality bounds \(|c-b_0|\le\sum_{w\ne0}|b_w|\le2S\), since the embedding fixes all coefficients and the output \(c\).
Thus \(A=3\) suffices at every hit, regardless of which embedding was used.

If \(B^r\ge d/2\), a neutral embedding fixing the output alone exists because \([K:\mathbb Q(c)]\ge4\).
At every embedding each original coefficient has modulus at most \(e^{BY}\) by its degree and height, hence \(|c|\le(r-1)e^{BY}\) from the neutral evaluation.
Also \(|b_0|\le(r-1)e^{BY}\).
A group of minimum size has coefficient \(b\ne0\) of degree at most \(B^g\) and height at most \(gY+\log g\) by the height inequality for a sum.
Thus \(S\ge |b|/2\ge\exp(-B^g(gY+\log g))/2\), using the height bound for \(1/b\).
One can take \(A=1+4r\exp(BY+B^g(gY+\log g))\).
Writing \(L=\log(1/m)\), the logarithm of the dilation bound is consequently at most
\[
 O\big(L+\log(2r)+\log(2+Y)+\log(2g)\big)+g\log B.
\]
Here \(\log(2+Y)=O(\log(2r)+\log(2+\log B))\) by \(d\le2B^r\).
Since \(\log B\le L\) eventually and \(g=o(r)\), this logarithmic cost is \(o(rL)\).
The same conclusion follows immediately from \(A=3\) in the first case.
Dividing the retained count by this uniform bound for the number of dilations per primitive list proves the statement.
