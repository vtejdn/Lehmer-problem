# Mahler measure from ranks at several primes and periods

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite inequality at arbitrary sample exponents

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), with nonzero constant and no root of unity, and let \(m=\log M(P)\).
For a positive integer \(q\) and prime \(p\), put
\[
 r_p(q)=\deg\gcd(P\bmod p,X^q-1),\qquad
 \lambda(q)=\frac1d\sum_p r_p(q)\log p .
\]
The gcd counts multiplicities, including at primes dividing \(q\).
For any \(n\ge2\) distinct integer exponents \(a_1<\cdots<a_n\), put \(D=\sum_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\lambda(a_j-a_i)\ \le\ \frac n2\log n+\frac{mD}{d}.       \tag{1}
\]
Thus a sample that uses several different periods can charge each occurrence of a difference at every contributing prime.

<a id="proof"></a>
## Divisibility and the Vandermonde determinant

On the free rank-\(d\) ring \(\mathbb Z[X]/P\), multiplication by \(X^q-1\) has nonzero determinant of absolute value \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,X^q-1)|\).
Modulo \(p\), the kernel consists of the multiples of \(\bar P/\gcd(\bar P,X^q-1)\) modulo \(\bar P\), so its dimension is \(r_p(q)\).
At least this many Smith invariants are divisible by \(p\).
In particular the sum defining \(\lambda(q)\) is finite and \(d\lambda(q)\le\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,X^q-1)|\).

The polynomial \(V(X)=\prod_{i<j}(X^{a_j-a_i}-1)\) has degree \(D\).
On the unit circle its absolute value is the absolute Vandermonde determinant on the points \(X^{a_i}\).
Each of the \(n\) rows of the power matrix has Euclidean norm \(\sqrt n\), so Hadamard's inequality gives \(|V|\le n^{n/2}\).
The maximum modulus principle applied to \(V\) and \(X^D V(1/X)\) gives \(|V(z)|\le n^{n/2}\max(1,|z|)^D\) everywhere.
Multiplying over the roots of \(P\), with their multiplicities, proves (1).
For consecutive sample exponents the circle maximum is exactly \(n^{n/2}\), attained at a primitive \(n\)-th root, where the rows are orthogonal.

<a id="cover"></a>
## Covering a positive proportion of the degree

Define
\[
 B(x)=\sup_{n\ge2}\frac{3}{n+1}
             \left(x-\frac{\log n}{n-1}\right)_+ .
\]
This function is convex and increasing in the weak sense, and \(B(x)>0\) for \(x>0\).
For \(a_i=(i-1)q\) every difference is divisible by \(q\), so its rank at each prime is at least \(r_p(q)\).
Since \(D=q n(n^2-1)/6\), (1) yields \(qm/d\ge B(\lambda(q))\).
For any positive periods \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\), summing and applying convexity gives
\[
 \frac{m}{d}\sum_{j=1}^k q_j
      \ \ge\ \sum_{j=1}^k B(\lambda(q_j))
      \ \ge\ k B\!\left(\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda(q_j)\right).      \tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(k\le k_0\), the sum of the periods is at most \(Ad\), and their total rank weight is at least a fixed \(\delta>0\), then \(m\ge B(\delta/k_0)/A>0\).
No particular prime need contribute at every period.
If \(\bar P\) divides \(\prod_j(X^{q_j}-1)\) at a single prime \(p\), then \(\sum_j r_p(q_j)\ge d\).
Indeed for each irreducible factor of \(\bar P\) of multiplicity \(t\), the sum of the individual multiplicities truncated at \(t\) is at least \(t\).
In this case the right side of (2) is at least \(kB((\log p)/k)\).

For example take coprime odd \(a,b>1\) and \(G_h=1+X+\cdots+X^{h-1}\).
The binary reduction \(G_a G_b\) has degree \(d=a+b-2\) and is squarefree.
Its two factors have disjoint roots, with primitive orders \(a\) and \(b\) occurring.
The least common period is \(ab\), but the two periods \(a,b\) have total cost \(d+2\).
Thus (2) gives a uniform positive bound for all monic lifts with no root of unity, even for comparable \(a,b\) tending to infinity.
Such lifts exist with reciprocal symmetry: \(G_aG_b+KX^{d/2}\) works for any even integer \(K>ab\), by the strict circle bound \(|G_aG_b|\le ab\).
For this binary two-binomial case a [specialized estimate](../audits/literature/HB26/Yin_U001.md#hb26-u001) gives an explicit larger constant.

<a id="orders"></a>
## Joint residue orders and the outstanding existence question

Suppose \(P(0)=\pm1\).
At each prime in a nonempty finite set \(\mathcal P\), let \(s_p\) be the number of distinct roots of \(\bar P\) over the algebraic closure.
If \(q\) is the least common multiple of their multiplicative orders at all these primes, then \(r_p(q)\ge s_p\).
It follows from (2) that
\[
 q\ \ge\ \frac d m B\!\left(\frac1d\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}s_p\log p\right).
\]
For a prescribed subset of roots the same proof uses the number of selected distinct roots at each prime.
The [two-exponent completion count](Yin_C_G0345_v645.md#statement) uses joint residue orders at prime divisors of two erased coefficients.
The lower bound above uses all contributing roots and permits arbitrary ramification, but its size still has to be compared with the actual witness span.
The [fixed-period estimate of Silverman](../audits/literature/Sil12/Yin_U001.md#sil12-u001) weights successive multiples of a given period by their nonarchimedean contributions.
Formula (1) instead keeps the degree of each gcd on all pairwise differences of a supplied set.
Neither calculation constructs bounded-cost covers for unrestricted inputs.
