# The limiting projective heights of multiplicatively dependent points on a plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The Newton support gives one height limit

Let \(F\) be a primitive integer Laurent polynomial irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) with support \(A\subset\mathbb Z^2\) of affine dimension two.
For a nonempty finite support \(B\subset\mathbb Z^2\) define the absolute projective height
\[
               h_B(x,y)=h\big([x^i y^j:(i,j)\in B]\big).
\]
For \(u=(u_1,u_2)\ne0\) write \(w_B(u)=\max_{c\in B}c\cdot u-\min_{c\in B}c\cdot u\).
Along every sequence of distinct algebraic torus points \(P=(x,y)\) on \(F=0\) whose coordinates are multiplicatively dependent,
\[
                            h_A(P)\longrightarrow m(F)>0.                 \tag{1}
\]
This also includes sequences on one fixed direction of multiplicative dependence modulo roots of unity, with the actual torsion phases varying.
The [individual factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) addresses directions of growing primitive norm, with arbitrary common exponent divisors.

More generally, fix any finite list \(B_1,\ldots,B_l\) of such nonempty supports.
The full set of attainable limits of the corresponding joint height vector along distinct dependent points is
\[
 \left\{\,m(F)\left(\frac{w_{B_1}(u)}{w_A(u)},\ldots,
                          \frac{w_{B_l}(u)}{w_A(u)}\right):
                                 u\in\mathbb R^2,\ \|u\|_\infty=1\,\right\}.       \tag{2}
\]
Every point of this set is obtained along untwisted monomial sections.
Thus the two individual coordinate heights have joint limiting values \(m(F)(|u_1|,|u_2|)/w_A(u)\).
In particular the supremum over directions of the possible departure of an individual coordinate height from \(m(F)\) can be read from the polygon \(\operatorname{conv}(A)\).

For \(F=X+Y-1\), (1) gives the qualitative projective-height conclusion of [Habegger's Theorem 1](../audits/literature/Hab09/Yin_U001.md#hab09-u001), for which his Theorem 4 gives a degree-dependent error.
Here \(w_A(u)=\max(0,u_1,u_2)-\min(0,u_1,u_2)\), and (2) gives the interval \([m(F)/2,m(F)]\) for the maximum of the two coordinate heights, also obtained by Habegger on that line.

<a id="width"></a>
## Exact height of the projected support

The torsion points of \(F=0\) form a finite set by the [finite torsion argument](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#calibration).
Outside that set the subgroup generated by \(x,y\) modulo roots of unity is infinite cyclic.
Choose a generator represented by \(z\) in the group generated by \(x,y\).
Then \(x=\xi z^p,\ y=\eta z^q\) with \(\xi,\eta\) roots of unity and \((p,q)\) primitive.
The vector is unique up to sign.
The product formula gives the exact identities
\[
           h_B(x,y)=w_B(p,q)h(z),\qquad
           h_B(x,y)=\frac{w_B(p,q)}{w_A(p,q)}h_A(x,y).                   \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, at places where \(\log|z|_v\ge0\) the local maximum uses the largest projected exponent, and at all other places it uses the smallest.
Both finite and infinite places enter, and the phases have modulus one at all of them.
Thus (1) follows immediately from the [all-direction factor-height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) along every subsequence with \(\max(|p|,|q|)\to\infty\).

<a id="fixed"></a>
## A fixed direction with varying phase

It suffices to consider a remaining subsequence with the same primitive \((p,q)\).
Choose a fixed geometric component, transporting the points there by embeddings from a fixed finite Galois field and extensions if necessary.
They still avoid finite sets, since rational conjugate orbits of a finite set are finite.
Take its smooth projective normalization \(C\) over a number field of definition.
The character \(f=x^q y^{-p}\) is nonconstant on \(C\) and gives a finite map to \(\mathbb P^1\).
Its pullback \(\overline L\) of the standard metrized line bundle has positive degree and is semipositive.
Its heights are \(h(f(P))\), which are nonnegative on the full projective curve and zero along the chosen sequence because the character takes torsion values.
Thus the absolute and essential minima are zero.
The monomial map with support \(A\) on the open curve extends to a morphism to projective space on \(C\), since the target is proper and the source is a nonsingular curve.
The pullback of the standard sup metric gives a semipositive bundle whose height on these points is \(h_A\).

The [global convergence theorem](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height) yields the same limit of \(h_A\) on all generic sequences with \(h(f(P))\to0\).
To determine it, choose primitive integer directions \((p_n,q_n)\) with sup norm \(N_n\to\infty\) whose normalized vectors tend to \((p,q)/\|(p,q)\|_\infty\).
Such a sequence exists even on a rational ray, for example by adding a fixed integer vector of determinant one with \((p,q)\) to increasing multiples of \((p,q)\).
Choose any nontorsion root \(z_n\) of the untwisted section in each of these directions and transport its powered point to the same component as above.
The [torsion fiber estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#calibration) ensures existence, and the distinct primitive relations ensure genericity.
One has \(N_n h(z_n)=O_F(1)\) and
\[
 h\big(f(z_n^{p_n},z_n^{q_n})\big)=|q p_n-p q_n|h(z_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
On these auxiliary points (3) and the growing-direction theorem give \(h_A\to m(F)\).
This fixes the same limit on the original sequence with its constant primitive direction, proving (1).

<a id="locus"></a>
## Every direction is attained

By (3), compactness of the unit sup-norm sphere and positivity of \(w_A\) there, every subsequential joint limit belongs to (2).
Conversely each real direction is a limit of distinct primitive directions of increasing norm, using rational approximation for irrational rays and the determinant-one construction for rational rays.
Untwisted nontorsion roots exist along each such sequence and their points are distinct after passing to a subsequence.
Applying (1) and (3) gives the prescribed joint limit.
On the line \(X+Y-1\), the ratio for the maximum coordinate height is \(1/w_A(u)\) when \(\|u\|_\infty=1\).
The width ranges continuously from one to two there, giving the stated interval.
