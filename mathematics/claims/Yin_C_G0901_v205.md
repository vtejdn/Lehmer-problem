# Full periods, large residue orders and the degree of a zero-prefix factor

<a id="statement"></a>
Use the [strengthened trace rows](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#statement) with \(p=3^\nu,\ q=p-1\) sufficiently large, \(\nu\) positive and even, and fixed binary precision \(h\).
Write \(u=2^h,\ L=(p+3)/2,\ \theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)\), \(A\) for the even-side period, \(d\) for the degree and \(N=A+2u\).
Then \(N\) is odd and prime to three, \(|A-\theta d|=O_{q,h}(1)\) on the synchronized rows used below, and \(\theta>2\).
Write \(\tau(n)\) for the number of positive divisors of \(n\).

There are three tests at the second modulus.
The [projection to divisors](#projection) gives a necessary quantitative inequality at every divisor of \(N\) for a full reciprocal word to have order dividing \(N3^b\).
The [orbit-degree calculation](#degrees) gives a sufficient test for the exact full order on the original rows by discarding the prefix coordinates after a substitution.
Unconditionally the [zero-sum construction](#large-roots) gives actual rows with a degree-\(d-o(d)\) zero-prefix divisor of exact order \(N\) in which every residue root has multiplicative order at least \(N/(8\tau(N))\) and residue degree at least \(\log_3N-O(1)\).
Furthermore, given any function \(\omega(d)\to\infty\), the [completion](#filler) can leave at most \(\omega(d)\) roots of the whole word, counted with multiplicity, in a fixed finite subgroup, with the minimum residue degree of all the other roots tending to infinity.
The full finite-ring order of the constructed word is \(N3^b\) with \(3^b=o(d)\).
Positivity of the [exact full-degree count](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#count) on infinitely many original rows is still open.

<a id="projection"></a>
## How a large orbit on a divisor of the period restricts the degree

Let \(W>1\) be any divisor of \(N\), write \(N=RW\), and let \(f\) be the order of the subgroup generated by \(3\) and \(-1\) in \((\mathbb Z/W)^\times\).
Define \(B=R\varphi(W)/f\) and \(E=N-R\varphi(W)\).
For any monic reciprocal degree-\(d\) word of constant term one over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\) with order dividing \(N3^b\), \(b\ge0\), one has
\[
                 d\bmod f\ \le\ 3^b E ,
\]
where the remainder is least nonnegative.
On the actual rows, for every fixed \(0<\eta\le 1/4\) there exists \(\delta=\delta(q,h,\eta)>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
        B\le d^\delta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                B3^b(E+2u)\gg_{q,h} d^{\,1-\eta}.
\]
No coprimality of \(R,W\) is needed.
For example if \(W\) is prime, \(E=R\), giving the lower bound \(3^b\gg_{q,h}d^{1-\eta}/(R^2 J)\) when \(RJ\le d^\delta\), where \(J=(W-1)/f\).
This includes growing complementary factors and growing indices.

Indeed modulo three the word divides \((X^N-1)^{3^b}\).
Cubing and inversion preserve its residue roots and multiplicities.
The map \(x\mapsto x^R\) on \(\mu_N\) has exactly \(R\) preimages per point of \(\mu_W\).
The preimage of the nonprimitive \(W\)-th roots contains \(E\) distinct roots.
Its \(z\) roots of the word counted with multiplicity therefore satisfy \(z\le3^bE\).
The other orbits project onto an orbit of length \(f\), giving \(d-z=kf\) for an integer \(k\ge0\).
The exact integer relation is
\[
                 k A - B d = k(E-2u)-Bz .
\]
If \(E\ge N/2\) or \(k=0\), the asserted lower bound follows directly from \(N\asymp_{q,h} d\) or \(3^b E\ge d\), respectively.
Otherwise \(0<k\le d/f=d B/(N-E)\ll_{q,h}B\).
Apply the [real-period repulsion](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) with denominator \(k\) and numerator \(B\), choosing \(\delta\) smaller than the exponent allowed there so the fixed multiplicative constant in the bound for \(k\) is absorbed.
Its lower bound \(|kA-Bd|>d^{1-\eta}\) and the relation prove the inequality.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## A residue test for the factors obtained by substitution

Take an odd \(H>L\) dividing both \(N,d\), write \(K=N/H>1\) and \(m=d/H\), and now put \(f=|\langle3,-1\rangle\bmod K|\).
Such a fixed common divisor occurs on [unbounded rows](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#short-period-divisor), using the strengthened zero-boundary degree progression there.
Reserve one primitive orbit in \(\mu_K\) under cubing and inversion.
It has size \(f\).
For each \(e\mid f\), let \(a_e\) count the other nonidentity orbits of size \(e\).
Use the central range
\[
              f(1+\tau(f))\ \le m\le\ K-1-f\tau(f).
\]
A reciprocal factor on \(\mu_K\) of degree \(m\) containing the reserved orbit and excluding the identity exists precisely when
\[
 \sum_{e\mid f} e\,s_e\equiv m\pmod f,\qquad
 0\le s_e\le \min(a_e,f/e-1),\qquad s_e\in\mathbb Z .
\]
In particular \(f\mid m\) suffices, as does \(e\mid m\) for any \(e\mid f\) with \(a_e\ge f/e-1\).
Either test gives a positive full-degree count at the original modulus.

In fact all orbit sizes divide \(f\), and a choice with the stated degree gives the congruence by reducing the number selected at each size modulo \(f/e\).
Conversely reserve the prescribed orbit and \(s_e\) others at each size.
Their total degree is congruent to \(m\bmod f\) and is at most \(f+f\tau(f)\).
In the remainder form groups of \(f/e\) orbits of size \(e\), each of total degree \(f\).
At most \(f\tau(f)\) degrees are left outside these groups and the reserved choices.
There are \(K-1\) nonidentity degrees in total, so the upper range ensures enough groups to reach exactly \(m\).
The orbit product lifts uniquely to a divisor \(V\) of \(X^K-1\) over \(\mathbb Z/3^\nu\), with constant term one, reciprocity and order \(K\).
Then \(V(X^H)\) has degree \(d\), zero prefix through \(L\), and exact order \(HK=N\) by the free basis over the ring in \(X^H\). [Original substitution and gluing](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#short-period-divisor)
Other full words can exist outside the indicated substitution and primitive-orbit class.

There is an exact variant for the whole substitution class, allowing the full order to come from different orbits.
Write \(\omega_0(K)\) for the number of distinct prime factors of \(K\) and take \(m\ge f(\omega_0(K)+\tau(f))\) with the same upper bound.
Try each collection of at most \(\omega_0(K)\) nonidentity orbits whose root orders have least common multiple \(K\).
If its total degree is \(c\), count the orbits left at each size for \(a_e\), and use \(m-c\) in place of \(m\) on the right of the congruence.
An exact-order substitution exists precisely when at least one such test succeeds.
Any full-order selection contains a collection of this size by choosing a witness for each prime power exactly dividing \(K\).
Conversely the reserved cost is at most \(f\omega_0(K)\), so the same grouping reaches the prescribed degree without changing the order.

<a id="large-roots"></a>
## Choosing only large residue orders in a divisor of nearly full degree

Keep \(q,h\) and the auxiliary and denominator primes fixed as above.
Let \(G\) be the [finite group of lifted prefix digits](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#count).
Addition of its orbit classes describes multiplication of the corresponding reciprocal factors, and zero class gives a zero prefix at the full coefficient precision.
By the [synchronization of prime powers](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion) we may impose \(k_0\) distinct odd prime divisors of \(N\), including a fixed prime \(H_0>L\) and one more prime \(H\) as large as desired.
The degree is chosen by rounding in the original fixed progression, so the real-error bound is uniform as these primes vary.
At each stage take \(d\) arbitrarily large after specifying these divisors.

Let \(f_N=|\langle 3,-1\rangle\bmod N|\).
The quotient of the unit group modulo its squares has at least \(2^{k_0}\) elements, by reduction to the distinct odd primes.
The two generators occupy at most four elements of this quotient.
Consequently there are \(\varphi(N)/f_N\ge2^{k_0-2}\) primitive orbits, and every orbit in \(\mu_N\) has size at most \(f_N\le 2^{2-k_0}N\).

Discard all roots with order less than \(N/(8\tau(N))\) or with residue degree over \(\mathbb F_3\) at most \(\lfloor\log_3(N/16)\rfloor\).
The former number at most \(N/8\), by summing \(\varphi(n)\le n\) over the eligible divisors of \(N\).
The latter number at most \(\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\log_3(N/16)\rfloor}3^j< N/8\).
These conditions preserve whole orbits, and every primitive root survives because a root of degree \(j\) has order dividing \(3^j-1\).
In particular more than \(3N/4\) degrees remain.
The order threshold is \(N^{1-o(1)}\).
For instance, for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the factors \(a+1\) of \(\tau(N)\) at prime powers \(r^a\) are at most \(r^{a\varepsilon}\) for \(r\ge 2^{1/\varepsilon}\).
The product over smaller primes is \(O_\varepsilon((1+\log N)^{2^{1/\varepsilon}})\), giving \(\log\tau(N)=o(\log N)\).

Any \(|G|\) orbits contain a nonempty subset with zero total prefix class, by comparing the \(|G|+1\) partial sums in any order.
Choose \(k_0\) sufficiently large that there are at least \(|G|\) primitive orbits and first reserve such a zero-class subset of primitive orbits.
Partition the remaining good orbits into further nonempty zero-class subsets of at most \(|G|\) orbits each until fewer than \(|G|\) orbits remain.
Each subset has degree at most \(u_N=|G|f_N\), also bounding the unused degree.
Given \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), increase \(k_0\) to ensure \(u_N<\epsilon d/4\) for all large rows.
Fix an even positive target margin \(M_0\) for this stage.
Starting with the primitive subset and adding successive subsets up to the budget \(d-M_0\) gives a product \(U\) of the lifted monic factors satisfying
\[
       M_0\le\Delta=d-\deg U<M_0+u_N<\epsilon d
\]
for sufficiently large rows.
The available total exceeds the budget since \(N/d\to\theta>2\).
Every chosen orbit is nonidentity, so \(\Delta\) is even.
The resulting \(U\) has zero prefix, order exactly \(N\), and the asserted root orders and residue degrees.

<a id="filler"></a>
## Concentrating the remaining small residue degrees

Choose \(Q=3^v\) with \(v\ge\nu+\lfloor\log_3 L\rfloor\).
The reciprocal polynomial \(\Phi_H(X)^Q\) has degree \(D_H=Q(H-1)\), and all its residue roots have order \(H\).
It has zero prefix at the required precision because \(\Phi_H(0)=1\) and \(v_3\binom{Q}{i}=v-v_3(i)\ge\nu\) for \(1\le i\le L\).
Use \(M_0=2H_0Q+D_H\) above and write
\[
      \Delta=aD_H+r,\qquad 2H_0Q\le r<2H_0Q+D_H,\qquad a\ge1.
\]
The even integer \(r\) has a representation \(r/2=H_0 x+Q y\) with \(x\ge0\), \(0\le y<H_0\), by reduction modulo \(H_0\).
Adjoin to \(U\) the factor
\[
            \Phi_H(X)^{Qa}(X^{H_0}-1)^{2x}(X-1)^{2Qy}.
\]
All factors have constant term one after taking the indicated powers and the product is reciprocal.
The last two factors of combined degree \(r\) have zero prefix as in the [fixed-subgroup completion](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#construction).
All residue roots of the new part have order dividing \(N\).
In the full coefficient ring, this new part divides \((X^N-1)^\Delta\), since \(\Phi_H,X^{H_0}-1,X-1\) each divide \(X^N-1\) integrally and their total exponent is at most \(\Delta\).
Together with \(U\mid X^N-1\), the whole word divides \((X^N-1)^{\Delta+1}\) and its order is a multiple of \(N\).
The binomial expansion of \(X^{N3^s}=(1+(X^N-1))^{3^s}\) for \(s=\nu+\lceil\log_3(\Delta+1)\rceil\) gives an order \(N3^b\) with \(3^b=O_\nu(\Delta+1)\).
Apply the [exact pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) to this full word at its prescribed degree, retaining the original even-side, dyadic and real conditions.

Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and increase \(H\) through the stages.
Take degrees so large at each stage that \(r\le 2H_0Q+D_H\le\omega(d)\) and \(N/(8\tau(N))>H\).
The roots in \(\mu_{H_0}\) now occur only in the degree-\(r\) part.
Outside it the multiplicative orders are at least \(H\), so all the corresponding residue degrees are at least \(\log_3(H+1)\).
The retained divisor \(U\) accounts for degree \(d-o(d)\) and has the stronger bounds above.
This proves the claims for the whole word and its finite-ring order, while the remaining degree corrections may still introduce a nontrivial power of three.
