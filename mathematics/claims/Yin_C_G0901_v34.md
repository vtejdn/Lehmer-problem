# An integer invariant of the nonreal embeddings in the norm-one reduction

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound in terms of the conjugates of a single algebraic integer

Use the [quadratic norm-one family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#statement) with \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)=F(i)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(t=\beta+\beta^{-1}\) and \(y=(\beta-\beta^{-1})/i\in\mathcal O_F\).
Here \(t^2+y^2=4\), \([L:F]=2\) and \(\beta\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit.
Fix \(i=\sqrt{-1}\) in the complex numbers.
For every embedding \(s:F\to\mathbb C\) put \(u_s=(s(t)+i\,s(y))/2\), and write \(\bar s\) for the conjugate embedding.
Choose one embedding in each of the \(r=r_2(F)\) nonreal pairs, with index set \(J\).
The real number
\[
      D=\sum_{s\in J}(|u_s|^2+|u_s|^{-2}-2)
\]
is a positive algebraic integer, independent of the choices in \(J\).
Write \(k=[\mathbb Q(D):\mathbb Q]\) and \(m=m(\beta)\).
Then \(r\ge1\), and, when \(m\le1\),
\[
      m\ \ge\ 2\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\tfrac12(6r)^{-(k-1)/2}\right).
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]
In particular a rational \(D\) forces \(m\ge2\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) with no upper hypothesis on \(m\).
In the [Salem construction](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis), a sequence of inputs tending to one would give \(r\le2\) and \(k\to\infty\), even when the original Salem degrees are unrestricted.

<a id="embeddings"></a>
## Archimedean coordinates and conjugation

For each \(s\) there is exactly one extension to \(F(i)\) taking \(i\) to the chosen \(i\); the other takes it to \(-i\).
Their images of \(\beta\) are \(u_s\) and \(u_s^{-1}\) by \(t^2+y^2=4\).
In particular all \(u_s\) are algebraic units.
One has \(u_{\bar s}=1/\overline{u_s}\), including at a real embedding where \(|u_s|=1\).
Put \(a_s=\log|u_s|\).
The total measure counts each of the two extensions, giving
\[
              m=\sum_s |a_s|=2\sum_{s\in J}|a_s|,\qquad
              D=4\sum_{s\in J}\sinh^2 a_s\le4\sinh^2(m/2).
\]
The inequality follows by superadditivity of \(\sinh\) on nonnegative arguments and squaring.
Positive \(m\) implies that \(r\) and \(D\) are positive.
Equivalently \(m\) is the sum, over all embeddings \(s\) of \(F\), of
\[
     \operatorname{arsinh}\left(\tfrac12
                 \sqrt{(\operatorname{Im}s(t))^2+(\operatorname{Im}s(y))^2}\right).
\]
Indeed for \(u_s=e^{a_s+i\theta}\) the two imaginary parts are \(2\sinh(a_s)\sin\theta\) and \(-2\sinh(a_s)\cos\theta\).

<a id="integer"></a>
## Bounding every conjugate and using the integer norm

For each \(s\in J\), \(|u_s|^2=u_s/u_{\bar s}\), so the summand defining \(D\) is the sum of an algebraic unit and its inverse minus two.
This proves integrality.
Extend any embedding of \(\mathbb Q(D)\) to the normal closure of \(F(i)\).
The automorphism \(g\) sends \(u_s\) to \(u_{gs}\) if it fixes \(i\), and to \(u_{gs}^{-1}\) otherwise.
Thus the conjugate of \(D\) is a sum of \(r\) terms \(v+v^{-1}-2\) with \(v=u_{gs}/u_{g\bar s}\); replacing all ratios by their inverses has no effect.
The pairs of indices remain disjoint.
Writing \(b=\log|v|\) for each ratio therefore gives \(\sum |b|\le\sum_s|a_s|=m\).
In particular each term has modulus at most \(2+2\cosh m<6\) if \(m\le1\).
All conjugates of \(D\) then have modulus at most \(6r\).
The absolute norm of the nonzero algebraic integer \(D\) is at least one, so \(D\ge(6r)^{-(k-1)}\).
Its upper bound in the preceding section gives (1).
If \(D\) is rational it is a positive integer and \(4\sinh^2(m/2)\ge1\) directly at any \(m\).
Finally the Salem transfer has \(r\le2\) and \(m\) at most four times the original log measure, proving the last assertion.
