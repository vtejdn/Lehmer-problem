# At least half the horizontal directions are lost at a repeated-factor obstruction

<a id="statement"></a>
## The dimension of the remaining exponent patterns

Under the [fixed-support and near-degree hypotheses](Yin_C_G0903_v8.md#statement), assume \(p\ge3\) and put \(n=k-2\).
There is a constant \(D(k,K)\) such that either a positive power of the nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) has degree at most \(D(k,K)\), or the \(n\) normalized horizontal exponent differences satisfy at least \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) independent integer relations from a finite list depending only on \(k,K\).
In the second case one of the [bounded quotient substitutions](Yin_C_G0903_v8.md#iteration) giving those relations has dimension
\[
                         2\le N\le\lfloor (k-2)/2\rfloor .
\]
Its horizontal determinant has a repeated geometric factor through the powered point.
In particular every seven-term obstruction outside bounded power degree occurs in one of finitely many two-variable exponent patterns.
No bound on the coefficient heights is required.
The resulting two-variable repeated-factor case remains open.

<a id="normal"></a>
## The gradient on a divisor of the quotient torus

Use the independent horizontal variables and the two pivot-normalized linear forms \(P,Q\) of [the horizontal construction](Yin_C_G0903_v8.md#field).
Include a symbol \(z_i\) for each of the \(k\) slots, assigning \(z_i=1\) at the two pivots.
The coefficients \(c_i\) are the nonzero original coefficients in their rows.
Take any quotient subtorus of dimension \(N\) arising in the iteration, with \(\ell=n-N\) independent defining character relations.
Let \(J\) be a repeated irreducible Laurent factor of the restricted determinant through the point, and use its divisor \(Z\) in the quotient torus over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
Its dimension is \(N-1\).
The four values \(P,Q,P^\iota,Q^\iota\) are nonzero at the specified point, so all are generically nonzero on \(Z\).
The logarithmic gradient of \(\Delta=PP^\iota-QQ^\iota\) along the quotient torus vanishes on \(Z\) because \(J^2\) divides the restriction.
Thus the \(n\) ambient gradient coordinates at its generic point lie in the constant \(\ell\)-dimensional space spanned by the defining relations of the subtorus.
Extend each vector to the \(k\) slots by requiring that the sum in each row be zero, and denote this extended space by \(L\).
For the gradient this extension supplies the actual pivot derivative, since \(PP^\iota\) and \(QQ^\iota\) are invariant under a common scalar multiplication of all slots in their respective rows.

Write \(\tau=-P/Q\) and in a finite function-field extension take \(\omega^2=P/P^\iota\).
On \(Z\) one has \(PP^\iota=QQ^\iota\).
Set \(T_i=z_i/\omega\) in the first row and \(T_i=\tau z_i/\omega\) in the second.
Dividing the extended gradient by \(P^\iota\omega\) gives
\[
               w_i=c_i(T_i-T_i^{-1}),\qquad w=(w_i)\in L .
\]
Indeed the two row derivatives are \(c_i(P^\iota z_i-P/z_i)\) and \(-c_i(Q^\iota z_i-Q/z_i)\), respectively.
The horizontal coordinates of the quotient torus are recovered from ratios of the \(T_i\) within each row: these give all nonpivot \(z_i\), whose characters generate the quotient lattice.
Since each \(T_i\) is quadratic over the field generated by \(w\), the \(w_i\) have transcendence degree at least \(N-1\).
They lie in a finite extension of the function field of \(Z\), so that degree is exactly \(N-1\).
In particular \(N-1\le\ell\).

<a id="equality"></a>
## Equality forces a reflection of the polynomial

It suffices to rule out \(N-1=\ell\) for \(N\ge2\).
Then \(w\) is generic in the whole linear space \(L\).
The functions \(u_i=w_i/c_i\) are homogeneous linear forms on \(L\).
In addition to the two row sums of \(w\) being zero, one has \(\sum_i c_i T_i=(P+\tau Q)/\omega=0\).
It follows that
\[
        \sum_i c_i(T_i+T_i^{-1})=0,\qquad
                  (T_i+T_i^{-1})^2=u_i^2+4.                            \tag{1}
\]
Group the nonzero forms \(u_i\) up to equality and negation.
For different groups the polynomials \(u_i^2+4\) have disjoint pairs of irreducible linear factors over the algebraic closure: a shared factor would identify \(u_i\) and \(u_j\) up to sign by comparing their constant terms.
Their squareclasses are therefore independent over the rational function field of \(L\).
Their square roots, together with the constant \(1\), are linearly independent over that field.
For instance each square root admits a sign change fixing the others in the multiquadratic extension, by independence of the squareclasses.
Consequently each group in (1) cancels separately, as do the terms with \(u_i=0\).

These identities can be evaluated over the original powered point.
On the open set where the four row values are units, adjoining \(\omega\) is a finite cover, and the component chosen over the generic point surjects onto this open subset of \(Z\) by integrality.
There each \(T_i\) is a unit.
If \(a_i\) denotes the original exponent of slot \(i\) and \(j_0\) the first row pivot, its value there is
\[
                              T_i=\alpha^{a_i-pj_0}/\omega .
\]
Indeed \(\tau=\alpha^{1+p(j_1-j_0)}\) at that point.
In particular distinct \(T_i,T_j\) there cannot agree up to sign, since the exponents are distinct and \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.

If \(u_i=0\), the corresponding \(T_i\) is identically \(1\) or \(-1\), so there can be at most one such slot.
Its contribution to (1) is nonzero and cannot cancel alone.
Thus there are no zero forms.
For any one group choose \(i\) and write \(u_j=\sigma_j u_i\) with \(\sigma_j=\pm1\).
The quadratic relation implies that \(T_j=\sigma_j T_i\) or \(T_j=-\sigma_j/T_i\).
For \(j\ne i\) the first option is excluded after evaluation, and two different partners in the second option would have values equal up to sign.
There are thus at most two indices per group.
There must be two because a nonconstant \(u_i\) makes \(T_i+T_i^{-1}\) nonzero and the group cancels in (1).
For its pair \(i,j\), with \(u_j=\sigma u_i\), one has \(T_iT_j=-\sigma\) and (1) gives \(c_j=\sigma c_i\).
Comparing any two pairs at the powered point gives an integral power of \(\alpha\) equal to the ratio of their two signs.
Nontorsion forces all exponent sums \(a_i+a_j\) to have a common value \(a\), and all signs to coincide.
Thus \(X^a F(X^{-1})=\sigma F(X)\).
For \(p\ge3\) such a reflection must interchange the two residue classes, since fixing both would require \(p\mid2\).
Comparing the rows then gives \(A(Y)=\sigma Y^{(a-1)/p} C(Y^{-1})\), contrary to the original nonzero determinant.
This excludes equality and yields \(N\le\ell\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## The number of bounded relations

The [successive quotient construction](Yin_C_G0903_v8.md#iteration) can terminate in dimension one only with power degree bounded in terms of \(k,K\).
In every other terminal case there is a repeated-factor divisor of the type analyzed above, with \(N\ge2\) and \(N\le n-N\).
All \(n-N\) independent relations used by the construction come from its finite list of integral basis changes.
Lifting them successively to the original horizontal lattice preserves their independence and bounds their lengths in terms of \(k,K\).
This proves the statement, including the asserted dimension for \(k=7\).
