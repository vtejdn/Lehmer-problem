# The noncyclotomic degree of two-class multiples of the five-term trace polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Dependence on the distance between occupied residues

For a prime \(p>10\) let \(P_p(x)=x^{2p}-x^{p+1}-x^p-x^{p-1}+1\).
For any nonzero multiple \(F\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of \(P_p\), write \(R\) for the product of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P_p\) other than \(x\), counting multiplicities.
Suppose \(F\) occupies at most two exponent classes modulo \(p\).
Then
\[
                \deg R\ \ge\ (1-10/p)\frac{p^2+1}{2}-2p.                 \tag{1}
\]
If exactly two classes occur, let \(1\le s\le(p-1)/2\) be their least cyclic distance.
Writing \(N_p(s)\) for the minimum of \(\deg R\) over these multiples with prescribed distance \(s\), one has
\[
 (1-10/p)\bigl[p(p-s)-s\bigr]-2p
        \ \le\ N_p(s)\ \le\ p^2+s-2p.                                  \tag{2}
\]
The same minimum applies to any prescribed pair of classes at that distance, since monomial multiplication translates the support without changing \(\deg R\).
Thus \(N_p(s)/p^2\to1\) for any sequence of these primes and distances with \(p\to\infty\) and \(s=o(p)\), including adjacent classes.
For one occupied class the minimum is exactly \(2p^2-2p\).

These estimates allow arbitrary coefficient heights and Mahler measures.
With the extra hypothesis \(m(R)=o(p)\), the [Lucas resultant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v356.md#statement) gives the larger uniform lower limit \(\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)/\log 2\) for \(\deg R/p^2\).
The two estimates concern this particular [five-term family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement), whose Mahler measure has a limit strictly greater than one.

<a id="base"></a>
## A common row factor and the one-class minimum

Fix a root \(\alpha\) of \(P_p\) and put \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
The [irreducibility and nontorsion](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) and the [base-field identity](Yin_C_G0345_v356.md#existence) give
\[
 [\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]=2p,\qquad
 \alpha+\alpha^{-1}=\beta+\beta^{-1}-1.
\]
Let \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
It has \(2p\) distinct nonzero nontorsion roots.
If an integral Laurent row factor \(G(x^p)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), then \(Q\) divides \(G\) over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
The diagonal factor \(Q(x^p)\) contributes \(2p^2\) nonzero nontorsion roots counted with multiplicity, including the \(2p\) roots of one copy of \(P_p\).
Consequently \(\deg R\ge2p^2-2p\) whenever such a row factor divides \(F\).
A one-class multiple is a monomial times a polynomial in \(x^p\), so this bound applies and \(F=Q(x^p)\) attains it.

For two classes, remove a Laurent monomial in \(x\) so that the rows have offsets \(0,r\) with \(r\equiv\pm s\bmod p\) and \(|r|=s\).
This uses a transfer of an integral power of \(X=x^p\) into the second row to implement the chosen signed representative.
Divide their Laurent coefficients by their common gcd \(G(X)\) in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Rational scalar factors do not affect roots or degrees.
When \(G(\beta)=0\), the preceding bound implies both lower bounds (1) and (2).
Otherwise the remaining specialization
\[
                     E(x)=U(x^p)+x^r V(x^p)
\]
still vanishes at \(\alpha\), so \(P_p\) divides it in the rational Laurent ring.
Here \(U,V\) are nonzero and coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Multiply both by one power of \(X\) to put the union of their exponent supports in \([0,b]\) with both endpoints attained.
Their specialized terms occupy distinct classes, so all survive.
If \(d\) is the exponent span of \(E\), comparison of a term based at \(b\) with a term based at zero gives \(d\ge pb-s\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Intersections with the quadratic trace relation

Write \(T_s(z+z^{-1})=z^s+z^{-s}\), where \(T_s\) is the monic integral polynomial of degree \(s\) obtained from \(T_0=2,\ T_1(t)=t,\ T_{j+1}=tT_j-T_{j-1}\).
Consider the Laurent polynomial
\[
       H(X)=U(X)^2+U(X)V(X)T_s(X+X^{-1}-1)+V(X)^2.
\]
At \(X=\beta\) this is
\((U(\beta)+\alpha^r V(\beta))(U(\beta)+\alpha^{-r}V(\beta))=0\).
Because the coefficients are rational, \(H\) vanishes at every conjugate of \(\beta\).

To see that \(H\) is nonzero, take \(X\) transcendental and adjoin \(Y\) with \(Y+Y^{-1}=X+X^{-1}-1\).
The discriminant of this quadratic is
\[
                   \frac{(X^2-3X+1)(X^2+X+1)}{X^2}.
\]
The two quadratics have simple, disjoint roots, so the discriminant is nonsquare even in \(\mathbb C(X)\).
The nonidentity automorphism sends \(Y\) to \(Y^{-1}\).
If \(Y^r\) belonged to \(\mathbb C(X)\), it would equal \(Y^{-r}\), contrary to the nonconstant trace of \(Y\) and \(r\ne0\).
Thus \(U+Y^rV\) is nonzero and its field norm \(H\) is nonzero.
The exponents of \(H\) lie in \([-s,2b+s]\).
A nonzero Laurent polynomial vanishing at all \(2p\) conjugates of \(\beta\) has span at least \(2p\), by clearing its lowest power of \(X\).
It follows that \(b\ge p-s\) and
\[
                    d\ \ge\ p(p-s)-s\ \ge\ (p^2+1)/2,                  \tag{3}
\]
where the last bound uses \(s\le(p-1)/2\).

<a id="strip"></a>
## Counting the noncyclotomic roots on the diagonal

The Laurent polynomial \(B(X,Y)=U(X)+YV(X)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by the coprimality of its coefficients and linearity in \(Y\).
It has \(Y\)-width one.
A cyclotomic polynomial in a Laurent monomial, up to a Laurent unit, could specialize on \((x^p,x^r)\) only to a monomial times a constant or a polynomial whose nonzero roots are all roots of unity, or vanish identically.
All possibilities are excluded by \(E(\alpha)=0\), the nontorsion of \(\alpha\), and \(E\ne0\).
Hence \(B\) has no factor to remove when applying the [strip estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v350.md#strip).
Its diagonal is primitive since \(\gcd(p,r)=1\), and the estimate holds for either sign of \(r\).
It bounds the number of torsion roots of \(E\), with multiplicities, by \(10d/p\).
The span \(d\) counts all nonzero roots of \(E\) with multiplicities.
Its noncyclotomic factors therefore contribute degree at least \((1-10/p)d\) to \(F\), including the \(2p\) degrees of the distinguished copy of \(P_p\).
Since \(p>10\), substitution of (3) proves the asserted lower bounds.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Witnesses with prescribed distance

For \(1\le s\le(p-1)/2\), consider the \(\mathbb Q\)-linear map
\[
 \mathbb Q[X]_{\le p}\oplus\mathbb Q[X]_{\le p}\longrightarrow\mathbb Q(\beta),
 \qquad (U,V)\longmapsto U(\beta)+\alpha^s V(\beta).
\]
Its target contains \(\alpha\) and has dimension \(2p\), while its domain has dimension \(2p+2\).
Choose a nonzero kernel vector.
Neither component can be zero since evaluation at \(\beta\) is injective in degrees at most \(p\) and \(\alpha\ne0\).
After clearing denominators, \(F=U(x^p)+x^s V(x^p)\) is a nonzero integral multiple of \(P_p\) occupying both prescribed residues \(0,s\), with degree at most \(p^2+s\).
Removing one copy of \(P_p\) proves the upper bound in (2).
Translating the residue pair by multiplication by a nonnegative power of \(x\) gives every pair of the same cyclic distance.
This dimension argument controls degree alone.
The [reciprocal interpolation construction](Yin_C_G0345_v354.md#statement) separately provides monic reciprocal unit witnesses at distance one.
