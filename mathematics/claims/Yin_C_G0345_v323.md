# Tame inertia prevents any reduction in exterior count below the five-term benchmark

The ambient-unit researcher developed the endpoint-seven factor selection, the characteristic-seven calculation, the single inversion and the Frobenius obstruction to a matching.
The balanced specialization and its measure estimates come from [C345v313](Yin_C_G0345_v313.md#balanced).

<a id="statement"></a>
## An unconditional family of irreducible inputs

Let \(p>1680\) run through primes and put
\[
 b=420\lfloor p/840\rfloor,\qquad a=p-b,\qquad
 H_p(z)=(1+z^a)(1+z^b)(1+z^p)-z^p.                                 \tag{1}
\]
There is a unique monic irreducible factor \(S_p\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of \(H_p\) such that \(S_p(1)=7\), and it occurs once.
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), it is reciprocal of degree \(2d_p\), all its roots are nonreal, and its numbers of exterior roots \(e_p\) and neutral inverse pairs both grow at least linearly in \(p\).
There is a fixed \(c_0>0\) such that
\[
 c_0\le\log M(S_p),\qquad
 \limsup_{p\to\infty}\log M(S_p)\le I_{23}<6/25<I_{306},            \tag{2}
\]
where \(\exp I_{23}\) is the Boyd–Mossinghoff value approximately \(1.255433\) and \(I_{306}\) is the logarithmic limit of the five-term prime family.
The argument requires no primality assumption on \(d_p\).

Let \(\Gamma_p\) be the multiplicative subgroup generated by all conjugates of a root of \(S_p\), and let \(\widetilde\Gamma_p\) consist of algebraic numbers having a positive power in \(\mu_\infty\Gamma_p\).
Then for every sufficiently large prime \(p\),
\[
        \min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma_p\\y\ {\rm nontorsion}}}
              \#\{\text{exterior conjugates of }y\}=e_p.             \tag{3}
\]
There is no reciprocity or measure restriction on \(y\).
In particular these irreducible inputs of upper limiting measure at most \(\exp I_{23}\) do not admit any decrease in exterior count under root-generated products, quotients, powers and radicals.
This conclusion holds independently of whether the full \(H_p\) is irreducible.
It concerns the radical of the root-generated group; the remaining units in the splitting field are outside (3).

In the quadratic extension of the trace field generated by an actual root of \(S_p\), the only finite ramified place is a degree-one place above seven, with absolute ramification index one in the trace field and relative discriminant exponent one.
The splitting field of the trace polynomial is unramified at seven.
These local assertions force all inverse pairs of \(S_p\) to admit independent sign changes.
The same unramified Frobenius rules out an invariant perfect matching of those pairs.

<a id="factor"></a>
## Selecting the irreducible factor and controlling its measure

Let
\[
 F(X,Y)=(1+X)(1+Y)(1+XY)-XY.
\]
Equation (1) is its specialization \(F(z^a,z^b)\), using the two positive signs.
Here \(a,b>0\) are coprime since \(a+b=p\) is prime, \(b\) is divisible by 420 and \(a\) is odd.
The [torsion exclusion for all four sign choices](Yin_C_G0345_v313.md#torsion) gives no cyclotomic factors.
The [positive-sign real-root test](Yin_C_G0345_v313.md#real) excludes real roots of modulus greater than one.
Reciprocity of \(H_p\) excludes real roots in the open unit disk as well, and \(H_p(1)=7,\ H_p(-1)=1\).
Indeed \(F(1,1)=7\) and \(F(-1,1)=1\).
Each monic irreducible factor has no real zero and is positive everywhere on the real line by conjugate pairing of its roots.
Its values at \(1,-1\) are therefore positive integers.
Multiplicativity of the two evaluations proves uniqueness, multiplicity one and \(S_p(-1)=1\).

By the [balanced factor estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v313.md#balanced), all these irreducible factors are reciprocal for large \(p\), each has logarithmic measure at least \(c_0\), and \(\log M(H_p)\to I_{23}\).
The [integral comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#statement) gives the strict inequalities in (2).
Every exterior root has \(\log|z|\le\log6/\min(a,b)\).
Since \(\min(a,b)>p/4\), this proves \(e_p\ge c_0p/(4\log6)\) as well as \(d_p\gg p\).
Additivity of logarithmic measure on monic factors proves the upper bound in (2).

<a id="neutral"></a>
## A fixed neutral arc for the positive signs

We give the argument with the signs of (1).
Put \(k=a-b\), an odd integer with \(1\le k\le839\), and write \(z=e^{i\theta}\), where \(\theta=u+iv\).
Direct expansion shows that at any root
\[
 0=z^{-p}H_p(z)=1+2\cos(p\theta)+2\cos(a\theta)+2\cos(b\theta)
                =4y^2+4ty-1,\quad
          y=\cos(p\theta/2),\qquad t=\cos(k\theta/2).
\]
The root bound and reciprocity give \(|v|\le4\log6/p\).
For \(|u-\pi|\le h=1/4000\), oddness of \(k\) gives
\[
 |t|^2=\cos^2(ku/2)+\sinh^2(kv/2)
    \le(839h/2)^2+\sinh^2(1678\log6/p)<1/4
\]
for all sufficiently large \(p\).
It follows from the quadratic that \(|y|\le(|t|+\sqrt{1+|t|^2})/2<\sqrt3/2\).
Since \(|\cos(pu/2)|\le |y|\), one has \(|\sin(pu/2)|>1/2\).
The identity \(t=1/(4y)-y\) gives \(|\Im y|\le|\Im t|\), whence
\[
              \tfrac12\sinh(p|v|/2)\le\sinh(k|v|/2).
\]
For \(p>2k\) this forces \(v=0\), by monotonicity of \(\sinh(s)/s\).
Thus the entire fixed arc contains only neutral roots.
The [angular estimate for irreducible units](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) applies to \(S_p\) of degree \(2d_p\gg p\) and bounded measure.
The normalized average of the triangular function supported on this arc is \(h/(2\pi)+o(1)\), giving at least a fixed positive fraction of the conjugates there.
Each neutral inverse pair contains only two roots, which proves the claimed linear count.

<a id="seven"></a>
## The full trace polynomial at seven

Define \(T_p\) by \(H_p(z)=z^p T_p(z+z^{-1})\).
It is the monic integer polynomial
\[
                         T_p(T)=1+C_p(T)+C_a(T)+C_b(T),
\quad C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}.
\]
We show that its reduction at seven is squarefree and its only rational residue root is \(T=2\).

All roots of \(\overline H_p\) are nonzero.
At a multiple root \(z\) set \(x=z^a,\ y=z^b\).
Differentiation gives \(0=z\overline H_p'(z)=axF_X(x,y)+byF_Y(x,y)\).
Since \(7\mid b\) and \(a\not\equiv0\bmod7\), both \(F(x,y)\) and \(F_X(x,y)\) vanish.
The value \(y=-1\) is impossible because \(F(x,-1)=x\).
Writing \(F\) as a quadratic in \(X\) gives
\[
                 y(1+y)X^2+(1+y+y^2)X+1+y .
\]
Its discriminant in \(X\) is
\[
       y^4-2y^3-5y^2-2y+1
          =y^2\{(y+y^{-1})^2-2(y+y^{-1})-7\}.
\]
Over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_7\) it vanishes only at \(y=1\) or \(y^2=-1\).
At \(y=1\), \(F(x,1)=2(x-1)^2\) in characteristic seven, implying \(x=1\); coprimality of \(a,b\) gives \(z=1\).
If \(y^2=-1\), the repeated \(X\)-root is \(x=-1/(2(1+y))=(y-1)/4\).
It has \(x^2=-y\) in characteristic seven, so \(x\) has order eight and \(y\) has order four.
Since \(a\) is odd, the two-primary part of the order of \(z\) equals that of \(x=z^a\), namely eight.
Then \(4\mid b\) makes the two-primary part of the order of \(z^b\) at most two, a contradiction.
Thus \(1\) is the sole possible multiple root of \(\overline H_p\).

We have \(T_p(2)=7\) and \(C_j'(2)=j^2\), the latter from the Chebyshev recurrence or the second-order expansion at \(z=1\).
Thus \(T_p'(2)=p^2+a^2+b^2\equiv2p^2\not\equiv0\bmod7\).
Also \(T_p(-2)=-1\), since \(p\) is odd and \(H_p(-1)=1\).
Any repeated trace root other than \(2\) would lift to a repeated root of \(\overline H_p\) away from \(1\).
Hence \(\overline T_p\) is squarefree, including at \(2\).

If \(u\in\mathbb F_7\) is a trace root, take \(z\) with \(z+z^{-1}=u\).
Seventh powering fixes \(z\) or takes it to \(z^{-1}\), so the order of \(z\) divides six or eight.
Since \(6\mid b\) and \(4\mid b\), \(y=z^b\) is \(1\) or \(-1\).
The case \(y=-1\) has already been excluded, and at \(y=1\) the equality \(F(x,1)=2(x-1)^2=0\) forces \(x=1\).
Consequently \(z=1\), which gives \(u=2\) as asserted.

<a id="inertia"></a>
## A single coordinate flip and a Frobenius with one fixed point

For sufficiently large \(p\), write \(S_p(z)=z^{d_p}Q_p(z+z^{-1})\) with \(Q_p\) the monic irreducible trace polynomial.
Indeed inversion is a nontrivial conjugacy over the trace of a nontorsion reciprocal root, so the individual trace field has degree \(d_p\).
The polynomial \(Q_p\) divides \(T_p\) over \(\mathbb Z\), since \(T_p\) vanishes at its trace root.
By the endpoint calculation \(Q_p(2)=7\) and \(Q_p(-2)=(-1)^{d_p}\).
Thus \(\overline Q_p\) is squarefree and has the single \(\mathbb F_7\)-root \(2\).

For \(t\) a root of \(Q_p\), the field \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\) has a unique place dividing \(t-2\), of norm seven with valuation one, and \(t+2\) is a unit.
The relevant place of \(K\) has absolute ramification index one since the entire splitting field \(T\) of \(Q_p\) is unramified at seven: choose a finite unramified local extension with residue field containing all roots of \(\overline Q_p\), and lift the distinct roots by [simple-root lifting](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001).
In the quadratic root extension \(K(\sqrt{t^2-4})/K\), the valuation of \(t^2-4\) at the indicated place is one.
The resulting quadratic extension is tamely ramified, and its relative discriminant has exponent one since the discriminant of the integral root order has valuation one.
The [order-discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative) excludes every other finite ramified place.

Write \(t_i\) for all roots of \(Q_p\), and fix a place of \(T\) above seven.
Exactly one \(t_i\) reduces to \(2\), all \(t_i+2\) are units, and \(\prod_j(2-t_j)=7\).
Consequently the distinguished \(t_i^2-4\) has valuation one and all other \(t_j^2-4\) have valuation zero there.
The full splitting field \(L\) of \(S_p\) over \(T\) is obtained by adjoining their square roots.
In odd residue characteristic square roots of units can all be obtained in an unramified extension, by simple-root lifting after extending the residue field.
The remaining square root still gives a ramified quadratic extension over this unramified base.
Therefore a relative inertia element changes the sign of this one square root while fixing every other square root and every trace.
On the roots of \(S_p\) it inverts exactly one pair and fixes every other pair pointwise.
Transitivity on the \(d_p\) trace labels now gives each independent pair inversion by conjugation.

Frobenius of \(T/\mathbb Q\) at the same place permutes the distinct trace roots as seventh powering permutes their residues.
It therefore fixes exactly one label, since \(2\) is the only rational residue root of \(\overline Q_p\).
If the trace group preserved a perfect matching, this element would fix the partner of its fixed label as well.
So no invariant perfect matching exists.
With all individual pair inversions and this matching exclusion, the [exact exterior minimum theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v305.md#statement) gives (3).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequences at the smaller limiting measure

For each fixed \(A>\exp I_{23}\) and fixed exterior budget \(B\), all sufficiently large \(p\) provide an irreducible unit polynomial \(S_p\) with \(M(S_p)<A\) whose root-generated radical has no nontorsion output with at most \(B\) exterior conjugates.
The construction in fact preserves the full minimum \(e_p\) and has just one original finite ramified place with local degree one among the \(d_p\) trace embeddings.
Its ramified local degree fraction at seven is therefore exactly \(1/d_p\).
This establishes persistence of a growing exterior count at the smaller benchmark without an irreducibility conjecture for the specialized polynomial.
The positive lower bound in (2) leaves the unrestricted small-measure supply question open.
