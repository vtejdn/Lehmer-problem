# A Mahler gap from torsion trace nodes over fields of bounded degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Interlacing over a coefficient field

Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic of degree \(m\) and put \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\).
Let \(A\) be monic of degree \(g<m\) with distinct roots \(y=\xi+\xi^{-1}\) for roots of unity \(\xi\).
Write \(K\) for the field generated over \(\mathbb Q\) by the coefficients of \(A\), with \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\le D\).
Those coefficients are algebraic integers and all conjugates of every node are again torsion traces.
For each embedding \(\sigma\) of \(K\), order the nodes of \(A^\sigma\) as \(y_1<\cdots<y_g\).
Suppose that \(S\) has real roots satisfying
\[
                         t_0<y_1<t_1<\cdots<y_g<t_g .
\]
The selected roots of \(S\) may depend on \(\sigma\), and its unused roots are unrestricted.

If \(h=m-g-1\le H\), there is a positive gap depending only on \(D,H\) for \(\log M(P)>0\).
Alternatively suppose each selection has at most one \(t_j\) outside \([-2,2]\), and that
\[
             \prod_\sigma\int_0^{2\pi}|J^\sigma(2\cos\theta)|
                                      \frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\le U ,
\]
where \(J\) is the polynomial quotient of \(S\) by \(A\).
There is then a gap depending only on \(D,U\), with no restriction on \(h\).
In both cases, whenever \(M(P)\le2\), an integer polynomial with bounded term count in the stated parameters has measure exactly \(M(P)^d\).
The nodes need not lie in \(K\), and no disjointness between \(K\) and the splitting field of \(S\) is required.

<a id="product"></a>
## A product bound without interlacing

More generally, keep the monic input polynomials, torsion nodes and degree bound on \(K\), and define \(Q(z)=J(z+z^{-1})\).
For each embedding use the partial fractions
\[
           J^\sigma(x)-S(x)/A^\sigma(x)
                =\sum_{y\,:\, A^\sigma(y)=0}\frac{\lambda_{\sigma,y}}{x-y},
       \qquad B_\sigma=2\sum_y|\lambda_{\sigma,y}|.
\]
Residues at common roots may be zero.
Suppose \(Q\) has at most \(T\) nonzero Laurent coefficients and \(\prod_\sigma B_\sigma\le B\).
Then an integer polynomial of positive degree and bounded term count in \(D,T,B\) has measure \(M(P)^d\), with no interlacing requirement and no bound on the measure of the input.
In particular the positive values of \(\log M(P)\) in this class have a uniform gap.
We allow \(A=1\), which has no poles.

Agent-S proposed passing from field-valued periodic moments to the indicators of their values.
The coordinating agent proposed the product bound using division by a nonzero moment.
Choose a common period \(N\) divisible by the orders of all the circle lifts of the nodes.
At the given embedding write \(\lambda_y=\lambda_{\mathrm{id},y}\) and set
\[
                  F(k)=\sum_y \lambda_y(\xi_y^k+\xi_y^{-k}),
               \qquad \xi_y+\xi_y^{-1}=y .
\]
The expansion at infinity of \(J-S/A\) has coefficients in \(\mathcal O_K\), by monic division, so \(\sum_y\lambda_y y^j\in\mathcal O_K\) for every \(j\ge0\).
Since \(\xi^k+\xi^{-k}\) is an integer polynomial in \(y\) for \(k\ge0\), all entries of the periodic \(F\) lie in \(\mathcal O_K\).
Under \(\sigma\) its entries are the corresponding sums at the nodes of \(A^\sigma\), independently of the choice of either lift.
Thus on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) the Fourier algebra norm of \(\sigma F\) is at most \(B_\sigma\), by summing the absolute masses at these characters, including coincident lifts at the endpoints.
In particular \(|\sigma F(k)|\le B_\sigma\).

If \(F\) vanishes identically, use the empty sum of indicators below.
Otherwise choose \(a=F(k_0)\ne0\) and let \(n=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}a|\).
This is a positive integer and \(n/a\in\mathcal O_K\), by the integral characteristic polynomial of multiplication by \(a\).
For every embedding the ratio \(B_\sigma/|\sigma a|\) is at least one and their product is at most \(B/n\).
Hence \(H(k)=nF(k)/a\) is integral, its conjugates all have modulus at most \(B\), and its Fourier algebra norm at the given embedding is at most \(B\).
This statement allows an individual \(B_\sigma\) to be arbitrarily large subject to the product bound.

The set of algebraic integers of degree at most \(D\) and with every conjugate of modulus at most \(B\) is finite, since the elementary symmetric functions bound the integer coefficients of their monic minimal polynomials.
For each member of this finite set, Lagrange interpolation supplies a polynomial that is one at this member and zero at all other members.
Applying it to \(H\) gives the indicator of a level set on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\).
Its Fourier algebra norm is bounded in terms of \(B,D\), because the Fourier transform of a product is a convolution, the norm of the constant one is one, and the list of interpolation polynomials is fixed by \(B,D\).
By [Sanders's finite-group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) each of these integer-valued indicators is a sum of boundedly many signed congruence indicators.
The level sets are also those of \(F\).
For each nonempty level use its actual value of \(F\) as coefficient, obtaining
\[
                 F(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{s} c_j\,{\bf1}_{\,k\equiv r_j\bmod n_j},
          \qquad c_j\in\mathcal O_K,\quad n_j\mid N,\quad 1\le r_j\le n_j ,
\]
with \(s\) bounded solely in \(B,D\) and signs absorbed in the coefficients.
Choosing the positive representative \(n_j\) for residue zero gives the geometric series starting at positive \(k\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Clearing denominators and taking the field norm

Put \(r=m-g\), \(b=\sum_y\lambda_y\in\mathcal O_K\), \(V=\prod_j(1-z^{n_j})\), and \(V_j=V/(1-z^{n_j})\).
The [geometric-series identity at the torsion poles](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#sparse) is algebraic in the residues and gives the explicit polynomial
\[
 W=(1-z^2)V z^r Q(z)
          -z^{r+1}\Big(bV+\sum_j c_j z^{r_j}V_j\Big),\qquad
 W z^g A(z+z^{-1})=(1-z^2)V P .
\]
Indeed \(\sum_y\lambda_y/(z+z^{-1}-y)=z(b+\sum_{k\ge1}F(k)z^k)/(1-z^2)\).
The same identity applies when the set of poles or the list of indicators is empty.
The polynomial \(W\) is in \(\mathcal O_K[z]\), has constant coefficient one by monicity of \(J\), and has at most \((2T+1+s)2^s\) terms.
Under each embedding its polynomial identity has the same rational right side, and its other left factor is monic with all roots on the circle.
The factors \(1-z^2\) and \(V\) likewise have measure one.
Thus \(M(W^\sigma)=M(P)\) for every \(\sigma\).
The product \(W_{\mathbb Q}=\prod_\sigma W^\sigma\) has rational algebraic-integer coefficients and constant one.
Its term count is at most \(((2T+1+s)2^s)^d\), and its measure is \(M(P)^d\).
The identity gives \(\deg W=2(m-g)+2+\deg V>0\).
The [bounded-term integer polynomial theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives the gap for the norm polynomial whenever \(M(P)>1\).
Division of its positive logarithmic bound by at most \(D\) gives the gap for \(P\).

<a id="supply"></a>
## How the geometric hypotheses bound the residues

It suffices to treat \(M(P)\le2\), since the other positive logarithms exceed \(\log2\).
As in the [real partial-fraction calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#poles), all roots of \(S\) then have modulus at most \(5/2\).
At each embedding select \(S_0=\prod_{j=0}^g(x-t_j)\).
Interlacing gives positive weights in \(A^\sigma/S_0=\sum_j p_j/(x-t_j)\) with sum one.
The reciprocal expansion \(S_0/A^\sigma=x-c-\sum_y\rho_y/(x-y)\) has \(\rho_y>0\) and \(\sum_y\rho_y\) equal to the variance of the selected \(t_j\) under those weights, at most \(25/4\).
Hence \(\lambda_{\sigma,y}=\rho_y(S/S_0)(y)\) and \(B_\sigma\le(25/2)(9/2)^H\) if \(h\le H\).
These are identities of real polynomials at the given embedding and require no field of definition for \(S_0\).
The Laurent polynomial \(Q\) has at most \(2h+3\) terms by its degree, proving the first assertion with the product bound.
When \(g=0\) the residue estimate is vacuous and the same term count holds.

Under the integral hypothesis on the quotient put \(U_\sigma=\|Q^\sigma\|_{L^1(\mathbb T)}\) for normalized arclength.
The outer Fourier coefficient of \(Q^\sigma\) is one, so \(1\le U_\sigma\le U\).
Each Laurent coefficient is integral in \(K\) and has modulus at most \(U\) under every embedding.
The norm of a nonzero coefficient is a nonzero integer, so its modulus at the given embedding is at least \(U^{-(D-1)}\).
For \(T\) nonzero coefficients, the [Jaming–Kellay–Saba inequality](../audits/literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) gives \(U\ge U^{-(D-1)}\sum_{j=1}^T(26(j+1))^{-1}\), which bounds \(T\) in terms of \(D,U\).
The supremum of \(|J^\sigma|\) on \([-2,2]\) is then at most \(TU\).
At a selection with at most one exceptional \(t_j\), the [real interpolation and endpoint estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v40.md#endpoint) bounds \(\sum_y|\lambda_{\sigma,y}|\) by \(15TU/2\).
The row orthogonality and the sign of the exterior column in that estimate concern only the selected real nodes and roots, so they apply at each embedding before the integer-valued steps of the rational-coefficient argument.
The product criterion now proves the second assertion, also for \(g=0\) by the empty residue sum.

In particular any hypothetical sequence \(M(P)\to1\) with \(M(P)>1\) eventually has no simultaneous torsion interlacer of bounded defect over a field of bounded degree.
This assertion includes auxiliary fields varying independently of the inputs.
The product criterion separately permits embeddings without interlacing when the actual residue and quotient bounds hold.
