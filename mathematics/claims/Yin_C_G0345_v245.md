# Congruence classes of conductors in a rational five-term factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## A parity condition at every offset

Let \(n>1\) be odd and prime to three, let \(k\) be odd with \(0<k<n\) and \(\gcd(n,k)=1\), and put \(l=n-k\).
Consider
\[
       P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1 .
\]
For a nonempty subset \(\mathcal T\subseteq\{t:t\mid n,\ t>1\}\), prescribe the reduction modulo two of a monic divisor to be
\(\bar B\), where \(B=\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}\Phi_{3t}\).
Let \(T_l\) be the trace polynomial of the unique monic dyadic lift of that divisor, so the lift is \(X^{\deg T_l}T_l(X+X^{-1})\).
A rational lift must have \(T_l(-1)=T_l(1)=1\) by the [four trace values](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces).

Let \(E\) count the elements \(t=p^j\) of \(\mathcal T\) for which \(p\equiv3\bmod4\), and let \(F\) count those for which \(p\equiv2\bmod3\).
Each selected prime power counts separately, with \(j\ge1\).
A necessary condition for a rational lift at **any** such \(k\) is
\[
                E+F\equiv0\bmod2.                                         \tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, an even number of selected levels must be powers of primes congruent to five or seven modulo twelve.

Put \(H_t=t/\operatorname{rad}(t)\), with \(\operatorname{rad}(t)\) the product of the distinct prime divisors.
Whenever \(0<k<\min_{t\in\mathcal T} H_t\), the actual residues of the unique dyadic lift satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
 T_l(-1)&\equiv 1+2\{E\,{\bf1}_{3\mid n-k}+F\,{\bf1}_{3\mid n+k}\}\pmod4,\\
 T_l(1)&\equiv 1+2\{E\,{\bf1}_{3\nmid n-k}+F\,{\bf1}_{3\nmid n+k}\}\pmod4 .
 \end{split}                                                             \tag{2}
\]
In particular for the [two-prime family](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#statement) with \(L=5^A,\ n=5^A7^{\lfloor A\log_7 5\rfloor}\) and its specified even indices, both values are \(3\bmod4\) for **every** admissible \(k<L/245\) not divisible by three, for all sufficiently large \(A\).
Neither can then be the value required over the rationals.
In particular in the fixed interval \([\sqrt n/1000,\sqrt n/500]\) the specified binary divisor has no rational lift for a proportion tending to \(2/3\) of admissible offsets from this congruence alone, in the sense that all admissible offsets not divisible by three are excluded.
For offsets divisible by three both values in (2) are one and this calculation leaves rationality undecided.

For \(n=p^2\) with a prime \(p\equiv5,7\bmod12\), suppose that \(2,-1\) generate \((\mathbb Z/(3p^j)\mathbb Z)^\times\) for \(j=1,2\).
Then the whole polynomial \(P_l\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for every admissible offset.
This applies, in particular, to \(n=25,49\).
The assertion uses only the indicated finite conditions on \(p\).

<a id="reference"></a>
## The two integral reference values

Write \(B=X^{\deg T_B}T_B(X+X^{-1})\).
For a single \(t>1\), write \(T_t\) for the analogous trace polynomial of \(\Phi_{3t}\), of degree \(\varphi(t)\), which is even.
Fix a complex primitive cube root \(z\).
Since \(3\nmid t\), \(\Phi_{3t}(X)=\Phi_t(X^3)/\Phi_t(X)\), by the orders of the roots.
At \(-z\), the numerator is \(\Phi_t(-1)=1\) since \(t>1\) is odd and \(\Phi_{2t}(1)=1\).
In the divisor product for \(\Phi_t(-z)\), each \(1+z^d\) is minus a cube root of unity and \(\sum_{d\mid t}\mu(t/d)=0\).
Thus \(\Phi_t(-z)\) is a cube root of unity.
Since \((-z)^{-\varphi(t)}\) is also a cube root of unity, \(T_t(1)\) is a real cube root of unity, hence one.

At \(z\), the numerator is \(\Phi_t(1)>0\).
Put \(a_t=\sum_{d\mid t,\ d\equiv2\bmod3}\mu(t/d)\).
Then \(\Phi_t(z)=(-z^2)^{a_t}\), by \((1-z^2)/(1-z)=1+z=-z^2\).
The reality of \(T_t(-1)\) gives \(T_t(-1)=(-1)^{a_t}\Phi_t(1)\).
For \(t=p^j\), \(\Phi_t(1)=p\), and \(a_t\) is odd exactly when \(p\equiv2\bmod3\), by the two nonzero terms.
If \(t\) has two or more distinct prime divisors, \(\Phi_t(1)=1\) and \(a_t\) is even.
Indeed modulo two the nonzero terms correspond to the subsets of the primes of \(t\).
If one of those primes is one modulo three, pairing subsets by that prime preserves their residue.
Otherwise each of the two residues occurs \(2^{\omega(t)-1}\) times, where \(\omega(t)\ge2\) is the number of distinct prime divisors.
Multiplication over \(\mathcal T\) now gives
\[
                         T_B(1)=1,\qquad T_B(-1)\equiv1+2(E+F)\pmod4.     \tag{3}
\]

Let \(\epsilon_l\in\mathbb F_2\) denote the [first correction at trace minus one](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#digit).
It is the sum of \(\eta^l g(\eta)\) over the primitive roots \(\eta\) of the selected orders \(3t\) in characteristic two, with \(g(Z)=(1+Z^2)/(1+Z+Z^2)\).
Since \(T_l\equiv T_B\bmod2\), write \(T_l=T_B+2V\) with \(V\in\mathbb Z_2[Y]\).
The residues \(V(1),V(-1)\) coincide modulo two.
Both reference values are odd, so the correction is the same multiplicative factor \(1+2\epsilon_l\bmod4\) at both arguments.
Equation (3) gives (1) by the two prescribed rational values.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Summing the correction at each prime power

Fix a level \(t\) and write \(R=\operatorname{rad}(t)\), \(H=t/R\).
Powering by \(H\) maps the primitive roots of order \(3t\) onto those of order \(3R\), with all \(H\) roots of \(Z^H=\xi\) above each such \(\xi\).
Indeed every prime dividing \(H\) divides \(R\), and comparison of the order at each prime gives \(3t\) for every preimage.
All these roots are simple as \(H\) is odd.
Let \(\omega\) run over the two primitive cube roots in characteristic two.
The partial fractions identity is
\[
                    g(Z)=1+\sum_{\omega}\frac{1}{1+Z/\omega}.
\]
For \(0<k<H\), the constant part contributes zero to \(\sum_{\eta^H=\xi}\eta^{-k}g(\eta)\).
For each remaining term expand the numerator of
\[
             \frac1{1+\eta/\omega}
              =\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}(\eta/\omega)^j}{1+\xi/\omega^H}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero because \(R>1\).
Summing \(\eta^{j-k}\) in this fiber leaves just \(j=k\), with sum one in characteristic two.
Thus the contribution to \(\epsilon_l\) is
\[
             \sum_{\xi\ {\rm primitive}\ 3R}\xi^{n/H}
                         \sum_\omega\frac{\omega^{-k}}{1+\xi/\omega^H}.    \tag{4}
\]
Agent-S independently derived this fiber computation for the two primes five and seven.

Write \(\xi=zv\) with \(z\) primitive of order three and \(v\) primitive of order \(R\).
Then \(\xi^{n/H}=z^{n/H}\), since \(R\mid n/H\).
For \(u\) a cube root, define \(K_R(u)=\sum_{v\ {\rm primitive}\ R}(1+uv)^{-1}\).
At \(u=1\), pairing \(v\) with \(v^{-1}\) gives \(K_R(1)=\varphi(R)/2\bmod2\).
This is zero for composite squarefree \(R\), and for \(R=p\) it equals \({\bf1}_{p\equiv3\bmod4}\).
For \(u\ne1\) the divisor decomposition for primitive roots gives, in characteristic two,
\[
                  K_R(u)=\sum_{s\mid R}(1+u^s)^{-1}
                         =\sum_{s\mid R}u^s .
\]
Indeed summing \((1+uv)^{-1}\) over all \(v^s=1\) gives \(1/(1+u^s)\) by the finite geometric sum, and all Möbius coefficients of the squarefree \(R\) are one modulo two.
For nontrivial cube roots \(u^s\), the last reciprocal equals \(u^s\).
The sum vanishes for composite \(R\), by the same subset pairing or parity count used in (3).
For \(R=p\) it equals \({\bf1}_{p\equiv2\bmod3}\).

For a prime \(R=p\), the term \(z=\omega^H\) in (4) contributes
\({\bf1}_{p\equiv3\bmod4}\sum_\omega\omega^{n-k}\), and \(z=\omega^{-H}\) contributes
\({\bf1}_{p\equiv2\bmod3}\sum_\omega\omega^{-n-k}\).
A sum of the two cube-root powers is one precisely when the exponent is not divisible by three.
Combining these contributions with (3) proves (2).
For the specified antichain, \(t_a>L/7\) and \(R\le35\), so every \(H_t>L/245\).
Exactly one selected level is a pure power of seven and one is a pure power of five for large \(A\), since \(b_a=0\) only for \(A-a=0,1\).
All remaining levels are mixed and do not contribute.
Thus \(E=F=1\), which gives the stated congruences as \(3\nmid n\).
In the indicated interval admissibility means \(k\) is prime to \(70\), and the proportion of these offsets prime to three tends to \(2/3\) by counting residue classes modulo \(210\).
Since \(\sqrt n\le L\), the entire interval is below \(L/245\).

## A criterion at a prime square

Under the hypotheses for \(n=p^2\), the squarefree reduction has three levels, of orders \(3,3p,3p^2\).
All rational factors are monic integral up to scalar and have constant coefficient of modulus one.
The monic reciprocal normalization permutes the irreducible factors and fixes the one containing \(\bar\Phi_3\), since this residual factor appears just once.
That rational factor cannot have degree two.
Indeed \(P_l\) has no cyclotomic factor by the [even inner exponent argument](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts), whose proof uses only odd \(n\) prime to three and even \(l\).
It has no negative real roots as in the [trace sign argument](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces), and no positive real roots at least two.
For \(x\ge2\), \(x^n-2x^l\ge0\) as \(n\ge l+1\), while \(1+x^{-n}-2x^{-l}>0\).
A monic irreducible quadratic stable under inversion is reciprocal, as constant \(-1\) in that case would give the torsion roots \(1,-1\).
A noncyclotomic reciprocal unit quadratic has a real root of absolute value at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2>2\), by its integer trace.
This proves the assertion about degree two.

Any other factors together form a monic reciprocal divisor \(S\), as their product is invariant under monic reciprocal normalization and cannot have a root at one.
Its binary roots are stable under Frobenius and inversion.
By the generation hypothesis each primitive order \(3p^j\) is a single orbit under those two operations.
If the complementary divisor is nonconstant it contains at least one whole level and cannot contain both, since that would leave only degree two for the first factor.
It therefore contains exactly one of the levels \(p,p^2\), contradicting (1).
This proves irreducibility.
For \(p=5\), modulo \(5,25\) the orders of two are \(4,20\) since \(2^4=16\not\equiv1\bmod25\); inversion is outside the cyclic group generated by two modulo \(3\cdot5^j\), since the exponent giving \(-1\bmod5\) is even, while \(-1\bmod3\) requires odd exponent.
For \(p=7\) the orders modulo \(7,49\) are \(3,21\) since \(2^3=8\not\equiv1\bmod49\); inversion is outside the generated group already modulo seven.
Together with the order two modulo three these computations give the full groups for \(j=1,2\).
