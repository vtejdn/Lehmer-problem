# Powers of reciprocal units with two exterior conjugates and dependent neutral phases

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive density of interlacing powers

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with exactly two roots of modulus greater than one.
Write \(q=m-2\) for the number of neutral inverse pairs and \(P_n\) for the polynomial obtained by raising every root of \(P\) to the \(n\)-th power.
If the two exterior moduli are equal and the [orbit of the exterior pair](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#matching) is a matching on the inverse-pair labels, \(M(P)\) is a Salem number or a real reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one.
In all other cases the following assertions hold.

Every \(P_n\) is irreducible of degree \(2m\).
For \(q\ge2\) there is a set of powers of positive natural density for which the \(q\) neutral traces occupy, one each, the \(q\) open intervals cut out on the real line by the roots of
\[
                     A(x)=U_{q-1}(x/2).
\]
Here \(U_l(\cos\theta)=\sin((l+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\).
The polynomial \(A\) is a monic torsion trace interlacer of the powered trace polynomial, with the two traces of the off-circle inverse pairs unused.
More precisely, the set of **all** positive integers satisfying this neutral occupancy condition has a natural density.
For independent neutral representatives modulo roots of unity it equals \(q!/q^q\).
The [construction below](#bins) gives a positive lower bound when they are dependent.

For \(q=1\) the constant \(A=1\) supplies the same partial interlacing at every positive power using the unique neutral trace.
For \(q=0\) the input has degree four, and its measure is uniformly bounded away from one by bounded-degree finiteness.
Outside the equal-modulus matching case at \(q=0\) the two exterior moduli must differ, since a graph on just the two pair labels has only one possible simple edge.
The exterior roots are then real, so \(A=1\) also supplies partial interlacing with one unused trace at every power.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree preservation and the neutral character torus

The matching conclusion follows from the [exterior-product reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#matching).
If two distinct roots become equal after raising to the same positive power \(n\), transitivity on the original roots makes every fiber of the power map have the same size, at least two.
The image of either exterior root must then have both exterior roots as preimages, since it has modulus greater than one.
Thus the two moduli agree and the exterior quotient is torsion, forcing the matching by the same result.
Outside that case every power map on the roots is injective.
All \(2m\) images are conjugate algebraic units, hence \(P_n\) is irreducible, monic integral and reciprocal.
Each trace has exactly its two mutually inverse lifts, giving the trace minimal polynomial of degree \(m\).
As in the [general reciprocal power construction](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#reciprocal), \(M(P_n)=M(P)^n\).
For degree four, \(M(P)\le2\) bounds all coefficients by the root-product inequality, leaving only finitely many possibilities and proving the small-degree gap.

Choose representatives \(v_1,\ldots,v_q\) of the neutral inverse pairs, with \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_q)\in\mathbb T^q\).
The [signed orbit equations](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations) give independence modulo roots of unity for unequal exterior moduli, and for equal moduli if the signed graph is unbalanced or connected.
In the remaining case the graph has \(c\ge2\) balanced components of the same size \(b\) by transitivity.
Each vertex is incident to an edge.
Thus \(b=2\) would be a matching, already treated, and \(b\ge3\).
Writing \(t=c-1\), we have \(q=bt+(b-2)\).
There are \(t\) disjoint blocks of size \(b\) among the neutral representatives, one for each component disjoint from the exterior pair, and \(b-2\) additional neutral coordinates from the component containing that pair.
The complete lattice of relations modulo torsion has one generator \(a^{(B)}\) per block \(B\), with entries \(\pm1\) along that block and zero elsewhere.
Write \(\zeta_B=\prod_{j\in B}v_j^{a^{(B)}_j}\), and let \(L\) be a positive common multiple of the orders of these roots of unity.
In the independent case take \(L=1\) and no blocks.

The subtorus \(K\) of \(\mathbb T^q\) on which \(\prod_{j\in B}z_j^{a^{(B)}_j}=1\) for every block is connected.
Indeed all but one coordinate in each block, and all coordinates outside the blocks, can be chosen freely, and then the last coordinate in each block is determined by an integral monomial.
These free coordinates identify \(K\) with their product of circles and identify Haar probability with product Haar probability.
For any \(r\) fixed, the points \(v^{Lj+r}\) are equidistributed on \(v^rK\).
To see this, a nontrivial character of \(K\) is represented by an integer vector outside the block relation lattice, by the same free-coordinate description.
Its value at \(v^L\) differs from one, since equality would give a torsion-valued relation for \(v\).
The geometric sums over \(j\) tend to zero for every such character, giving Haar measure on the coset by trigonometric approximation.

<a id="bins"></a>
## Placing one phase in each equal angular interval

The monic polynomials \(U_l(x/2)\) have initial terms \(1,x\) and recurrence \(U_{l+1}(x/2)=xU_l(x/2)-U_{l-1}(x/2)\).
The nodes for \(A\) are the \(q-1\) distinct torsion traces \(2\cos(j\pi/q)\), \(1\le j<q\).
Thus each open folded-angle interval of width \(\pi/q\) specifies one bin for the neutral traces, in reverse trace order.
Every coordinate projection of \(K\) is onto the circle, including the last coordinate in each block since \(b\ge3\).
Consequently each translated Haar measure gives zero mass to every coordinate boundary angle.
The neutral occupancy event therefore has a density on each residue class modulo \(L\) and a natural density on all positive integers, given by their average.
In the independent case the \(q\) folded angles are independent and uniform, giving \(q!/q^q\).
We give a positive-measure open box on \(K\) for the remaining case.

Use units of \(\pi/q\) for folded angles in \((0,q)\), so bin \(j\) has center \(c_j=j-1/2\).
For a chosen positive angle in a bin either sign can be used for the actual circle argument.
Thus each coefficient of a block sum of folded angles can be made \(+1\) or \(-1\) independently by combining the chosen argument sign with the prescribed sign of \(a^{(B)}\).
It suffices to assign all bins once and adjust their centers inside their own bins until every signed block sum is zero over the reals.

For even \(b\ge4\) assign \(b\) consecutive bins to each constrained block, leaving the last \(b-2\) bins free.
Split a block into adjacent pairs and give the two centers in each pair opposite signs.
Each pair can contribute \(1\) or \(-1\), so the sum \(r\) of the \(b/2\) contributions can be chosen with \(|r|\le1\).

For odd \(b\ge3\), reserve bins \(1,\dots,3t\) to give three to each constrained block.
For block \(j\), \(1\le j\le t\), use indices \(j,\ t+a_j,\ t+d_j\), where the pairs \(a_j<d_j\) partition \(1,\ldots,2t\) and \(d_j-a_j\) equals either \(j\) or \(j-1\).
To produce them, if \(t=2s\) split the \(2t\) positions into two successive segments of length \(2s\), and for \(t=2s+1\) use lengths \(2s+2\) and \(2s\).
Pair the positions in each segment symmetrically about its midpoint.
An even segment of length \(2l\) has one pair of each odd distance \(1,3,\ldots,2l-1\).
Assign distance \(2r-1\) from the first segment to block \(2r-1\), and the same distance from the second to block \(2r\) whenever that block exists.
This includes \(t=1\) with its empty second segment.
The signed triple of centers \(c_j+c_{t+a_j}-c_{t+d_j}=j-(d_j-a_j)-1/2\) is therefore \(\pm1/2\).
Assign \((b-3)/2\) adjacent pairs of the next unused bins to each block, leaving exactly \(b-2\) bins free.
Their signed contributions \(\pm1\) can sum to zero for an even number of pairs, or to the unit of opposite sign to the triple for an odd number.
Thus each completed block has \(|r|=1/2\).

In either construction, if the signs on a block are \(e_i=\pm1\), change its center in coordinate \(i\) by \(-e_i r/b\).
This sets the signed sum exactly to zero, leaves every coordinate in its assigned bin and puts it at least \(1/4\) bin-width from either endpoint.
The unconstrained coordinates can remain at their bin centers.
Choosing the actual argument signs now gives a point of \(K\) with the required occupation.
In each of the \(q-t\) free circle coordinates of \(K\), allow an angular perturbation of magnitude less than \(\pi/(8bq)\).
The dependent angle in a block changes by at most the sum of the perturbation magnitudes there, less than \(\pi/(8q)\), so all coordinates remain in their bins.
The Haar probability of this box is \((8bq)^{-(q-t)}\).
The density of successful powers among multiples of \(L\) is at least this number, and their density among all powers is at least \((8bq)^{-(q-t)}/L\).

Every occupied bin supplies its own distinct neutral trace of \(P_n\).
The two off-circle inverse pairs have traces outside \([-2,2]\) or nonreal traces, and neither is needed in the alternating list.
The trace degree \(m\) and node degree \(q-1\) give defect \(m-(q-1)-1=2\) in the [partial torsion interlacing condition](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement).
