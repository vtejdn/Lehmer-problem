# The maximum Fourier amplitude in the periodic quotients of an algebraic unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## A limit that distinguishes neutral conjugates

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of monic minimum \(P\), let \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and put \(G_n=R/(\alpha^n-1)R\).
For independent fair binary digits let \(F_n\) be the largest absolute Fourier coefficient of \(\sum_{j<n}\xi_j\alpha^j\) on \(G_n\) among nontrivial characters, taking zero if the group is trivial.
Then \(F_n\) has a limit \(F(P)\).
It is zero exactly when \(P\) has a conjugate on the unit circle.
In that case the convergence to zero has a computable modulus for each fixed \(P\), yet
\[
                            \limsup_{n\to\infty}F_n^{1/n}=1 .
\]
The subexponential lower bound is realized along a subsequence by characters of asymptotically full conductor, in the sense that \(\log[R:\mathfrak a_\chi]/(nm)\to1\), where \(m=\log M(P)>0\) and \(\mathfrak a_\chi=\{v\in R:\chi(vy)=1\ \forall y\in R\}\).

For hyperbolic \(P\), \(F(P)\) is the attained maximum of a two-sided product over nonzero square-summable solutions to the recurrence modulo one described below.
For binary entropy rate \(h\), limiting dissociated density \(D=D_1(P)\), and \(g,C\) from the [conductor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v578.md#statement), one has
\[
                         Dg(F(P))\le m-h\le C(m).
\]
In particular \(F(P)=O(m)\) along every sequence with \(m\to0\), with an absolute implied constant and no restriction on degree.
The digit order \(R\) matters here since [nontrivial characters on a larger order](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v298.md) can restrict trivially to all digit sums.

<a id="compactness"></a>
## Character orbits and localized limits

A character corresponds exactly to an \(n\)-periodic sequence \(t_j\bmod1\) satisfying \(P(S)t=0\bmod1\), where \((St)_j=t_{j+1}\), via \(\chi(\alpha^j)=\exp(2\pi i t_j)\).
Indeed evaluation on polynomial coefficients defines a character on \(\mathbb Z[X]/(P,X^n-1)=G_n\), with the two relations precisely as stated.
Choose centered representatives in \([-1/2,1/2]\).
Its amplitude is \(\prod_{j<n}|\cos(\pi t_j)|\).
A nonzero orbit has some \(|t_j|\ge c_P=1/\sum|P_i|\).
Otherwise all entries of the integer sequence \(P(S)t\) vanish, so the real periodic sequence vanishes because \(P\) has no root of unity.

Consider increasing periods whose amplitudes stay bounded below by a positive constant.
Since \(|\cos(\pi x)|\le\exp(-\pi^2x^2/2)\) on the centered interval, the sum of squares over a whole period is bounded.
Shift to put a coordinate of modulus at least \(c_P\) at zero.
Diagonal compactness gives a two-sided limit \(t\) with \(|t_0|\ge c_P\), \(t\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), and \(P(S)t\) integral.
Its product over all integer times is at least the upper limit of the original amplitudes after extracting a subsequence realizing this upper limit, by first taking products on each fixed central window.

The defect \(b=P(S)t\) is square summable and integral, so it has finite support.
With the convention that \(S\) is multiplication by \(X\) in the Fourier transform \(\sum_j t_j X^{-j}\), write \(B(X)=\sum_j b_j X^{-j}\).
Almost everywhere on the circle the transform of \(t\) equals \(B/P\).
If \(\beta\) is a unit-circle root of \(P\), square integrability forces \(B(\beta)=0\), since a pole is not square integrable there.
Irreducibility and monicity give \(B/P\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\).
Its coefficients are integers, forcing the centered \(t\) to vanish, a contradiction.
This proves the asserted decay, giving a direct proof of the square-summable case of [Lind and Schmidt's homoclinic result](../audits/literature/LS99b/Yin_U001.md#ls99b-u001).

For an effective upper bound, on \([-L,L]\) maximize the product of the absolute cosines under the centered bounds, \(|t_0|\ge c_P\), and all the integer recurrence constraints whose entries lie in this window.
Take zero when infeasible.
The feasible set is a finite union of compact rational polytopes, since each defect integer is bounded by \(\sum|P_i|/2\).
The maximum is computable to any absolute precision by triangulation and uniform approximation on the polytopes.
These upper bounds decrease with \(L\) and bound \(F_n\) for \(n>2L\), since the central window then has no repetitions.
A positive limiting upper bound would give the preceding nonzero square-summable orbit by compactness and the same cosine estimate.
When there is a unit-circle conjugate, a search for a certified upper bound below any given positive tolerance therefore terminates.

<a id="hyperbolic"></a>
## Periodizing an orbit off the unit circle

If \(P\) is hyperbolic, \(B/P\) is analytic on an annulus around the circle.
Thus every two-sided square-summable solution above has exponentially decaying coefficients.
For a nonzero centered solution its defect satisfies \(B(\alpha)\ne0\), since divisibility by \(P\) would again give integer coefficients for \(t\).
Set \(v^{(n)}_j=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}t_{j+\ell n}\).
Periodization gives a periodic solution modulo one, nontrivial for large \(n\), since some coordinate of \(t\) is nonintegral.
Its whole-period product converges to the two-sided product of \(t\).
Indeed on a centered interval of length \(n\) the sum of all aliasing errors is exponentially small by the tail bound, and \(|\cos(\pi x)|\) is Lipschitz.
A positive lower limit for \(F_n\) is furnished by the fixed integral trace multiplier one from the [two-sided trace calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v578.md#traces), now evaluated on \(R\).
The character is nontrivial for large \(n\), since a trivial character would put \(1/(\alpha^n-1)\) in the fixed trace dual \(R^\vee\), contradicting its norm tending to zero.
Together with the compactness argument and periodization this proves convergence and attainment of the positive maximum.

The periodicized character has asymptotically full conductor.
If \(z=Z(\alpha)\) is in its conductor, then \(Z(S)v^{(n)}\) is integer-valued by testing on every \(\alpha^j\).
Applying \(P(S)\) shows that \(Z(X)B(X)\) is a multiple of \(P\) modulo \(X^n-1\) over the integers, using the same Laurent coefficient convention.
Consequently \(zB(\alpha)\in(\alpha^n-1)R\), and
\[
          [R:\mathfrak a_\chi]\ \ge\ |G_n|/|N_{K/\mathbb Q}B(\alpha)|.
\]
The [finite conductor proof](Yin_C_G0345_v578.md#frequencies) works in \(R\): it uses its fixed lattice for the fiber count and the norm bound \([R:\mathfrak a_\chi]\le[R:zR]=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}z|\) when \(0\ne z\in\mathfrak a_\chi\).
Applying it to the periodization of an attaining orbit gives \(Dg(F(P))\le m-h\).
This controls the maximum over all nontrivial characters, even though the maximizing finite characters need not be selected by conductor.

<a id="slow"></a>
## Slow decay along periods with a simultaneous return

Suppose there are neutral conjugates and fix a small \(\varepsilon>0\).
Take \(t\ge1\) with \(|1-\beta^t|<\varepsilon\) at every neutral \(\beta\); simultaneous recurrence of a rotation gives such integers by the pigeonhole principle on a torus.
Put \(b_t=1-\alpha^t\ne0\) and take an unbounded sequence of \(n\) with \(\beta^n\to\beta\) at every neutral \(\beta\), using the same recurrence for \(n-1\).
The additive character with trace parameter \(b_t/(\alpha^n-1)\) on \(G_n\) has amplitude
\[
 \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\cos\!\left(\pi\,\operatorname{Tr}
                         \frac{b_t\alpha^i}{\alpha^n-1}\right)\right|.
\]
At neutral embeddings the total absolute contribution to each trace is at most \(C_P\varepsilon\) for sufficiently large \(n\), since every \(|\beta-1|>0\).
For \(i\) and \(n-i\) both larger than a fixed \(L(P,t,\varepsilon)\), the other embeddings together contribute at most \(\varepsilon\), by geometric decay.
Every middle factor is then at least \(\exp(-C'_P\varepsilon^2)\) for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).

At each of the finitely many initial and terminal positions the trace has a finite real algebraic integer limit.
At a fixed initial \(i\) the contracting contribution tends to \(-\sum_{|\beta|<1}(1-\beta^t)\beta^i\), and the expanding contribution tends to zero.
At \(i=n-\ell\) for fixed \(\ell\ge1\) the expanding contribution tends to \(\sum_{|\beta|>1}(1-\beta^t)\beta^{-\ell}\) and the contracting one vanishes.
The neutral terms at the two ends tend to sums of \((1-\beta^t)\beta^i/(\beta-1)\) and \((1-\beta^t)\beta^{1-\ell}/(\beta-1)\), respectively.
Here \((1-\beta^t)/(\beta-1)=-(1+\cdots+\beta^{t-1})\), and all negative powers are algebraic integers as well.
None of these real trace limits can be a nonintegral half-integer.
The end factors thus have a positive joint lower bound with \(P,t,L\) fixed while \(n\) tends to infinity.

Finally \(b_t\mathfrak a_\chi\subseteq(\alpha^n-1)R^\vee\) gives
\[
             [R:\mathfrak a_\chi]\ \ge\
          |G_n|/\bigl([R^\vee:R]\,|N_{K/\mathbb Q}b_t|\bigr)
\]
by lattice covolumes.
For each \(t\) the conductor fraction tends to one and the character is nontrivial eventually.
The middle and endpoint bounds give a lower limit of the \(n\)-th root of this amplitude at least \(\exp(-C'_P\varepsilon^2)\) along the selected periods.
Taking a diagonal sequence as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves both the subexponential assertion and its conductor qualification.
