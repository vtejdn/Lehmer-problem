# A trace value modulo eight for small offsets divisible by three

<a id="statement"></a>
## All admissible offsets in a square-root interval

Let \(n>1\) be odd and prime to three, let \(0<k<n\) be odd and coprime to \(n\), and write \(l=n-k\).
Consider
\[
       P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1.
\]
For a nonempty set \(\mathcal T\) of divisors \(t>1\) of \(n\), let \(T_l\) be the trace polynomial of the unique monic dyadic divisor with reduction \(\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}\bar\Phi_{3t}\) modulo two.
Here \(\Phi_m\) denotes the \(m\)-th cyclotomic polynomial, and a reciprocal divisor with trace polynomial \(T\) is \(X^{\deg T}T(X+X^{-1})\).
For each \(t\) put \(R_t=\operatorname{rad}(t)\), the product of its distinct prime divisors, and \(H_t=t/R_t\).
Fix a primitive cube root \(\omega\) in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), set \(x=-\omega\), and put \(v_t=x^{H_t}\).
If \(3\mid k\) and \(2k<\min_{t\in\mathcal T}H_t\), then
\[
             T_l(1)\equiv
                 \prod_{t\in\mathcal T}v_t^{-\varphi(R_t)}
                      \Phi_{3R_t}(3v_t)\pmod8 .                         \tag{1}
\]
Every normalized term with composite \(R_t\) equals one modulo eight.
For \(R_t=p\) prime it equals
\[
             (-1)^{f_p}p\{1+4(e_p+f_p)\}\pmod8,\quad
             e_p={\bf1}_{p\equiv3\bmod4},\qquad f_p={\bf1}_{p\equiv2\bmod3}.
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular the residues for \(R_t=5,7,35\) are \(7,3,1\), respectively.

Take the [two-prime family](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#statement) with \(L=5^A\), \(b_a=\lfloor(A-a)\log_7 5\rfloor\), \(n=5^A7^{b_0}\), and \(\mathcal T=\{5^a7^{b_a}:0\le a\le A,\ 2\mid a\}\).
For all sufficiently large \(A\) and every admissible \(k<L/490\), the specified binary divisor has no monic rational lift dividing \(P_l\).
In particular the exclusion holds for **every** admissible \(k\) in \([\sqrt n/1000,\sqrt n/500]\).
At offsets divisible by three the obstruction is \(T_l(1)\equiv5\bmod8\), whereas the [modulo-four trace values](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#statement) already exclude all other admissible offsets in this interval.
The conclusion concerns this prescribed reduction and permits other rational factors of the same parent.

<a id="fiber"></a>
## A monic local factor modulo eight

Treat one selected \(t\) and abbreviate \(R=R_t,\ H=H_t,\ M=n/H\).
Over an unramified extension containing the \(3t\)-th roots of unity, let \(\xi\) run over the primitive \(3R\)-th roots.
The residue classes satisfying \(X^H=\bar\xi\) for these \(\xi\) partition the roots of \(\bar\Phi_{3t}\).
Indeed \(H\) is odd and prime to three, and every prime dividing \(H\) divides \(R\).
An element whose \(H\)-th power has order \(3R\) therefore has order \(3RH=3t\), by considering each prime power in its order.
Conversely every primitive \(3t\)-th root has \(H\)-th power of order \(3R\).
The full reduction of \(P_l\) is squarefree by the [binary factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors).
Thus each \(X^H-\bar\xi\) specifies a unique monic factor of degree \(H\) by Hensel lifting.

Put \(U=X^H,\ Z=X^k,\ W=X^{H-k}\), and use the polynomials in \(U\)
\[
             f(U)=U^{2M}+U^M+1,\qquad L_0(U)=U^{2M-1}W+Z .
\]
Then \(P_l=f(U)-2L_0(U)\), because \(n=HM\) and \(n+l=2n-k\).
Write \(u=\xi^M\), a primitive cube root since \(M=R(n/t)\) and neither \(R\) nor \(n/t\) is divisible by three.
In particular \(f(\xi)=0\), and \(D=f'(\xi)=M\xi^{M-1}(1+2u)\) is a unit.
With \(E\) the coefficient of \(V^2\) in \(f(\xi+V)\), set
\[
          a=\frac{L_0(\xi)}D,\qquad b=\frac{L_0'(\xi)a-Ea^2}D ,
\]
where \(Z,W\) are held fixed in the derivative.
Taylor expansion gives \(f(\xi+2a+4b)-2L_0(\xi+2a+4b)\equiv0\bmod8\).
The polynomial \(a\) is linear in \(Z,W\) and \(b\) is quadratic.
In \(b\) replace \(ZW=X^H\) by \(\xi\), and \(W^2=X^HX^{H-2k}\) by \(\xi X^{H-2k}\), obtaining \(b_0\).
Then
\[
                         G_\xi=X^H-\xi-2a-4b_0                         \tag{3}
\]
is monic of degree \(H\) because \(0<2k<H\).
In the quotient by (3) modulo eight, \(U\equiv\xi\bmod2\), so \(4b=4b_0\) there.
The Taylor identity therefore makes \(P_l=0\) in this quotient.
This proves that \(G_\xi\) is the reduction modulo eight of the specified monic Hensel factor.
The reduction of the quadratic monomials before evaluation accounts for the degree constraint in (1).

<a id="evaluation"></a>
## Evaluation at a primitive sixth root

Put \(c\in\{1,3\}\) equal to \(M\bmod4\) and \(e=(c-1)/2\).
Expanding the displayed formulas for \(a\) and \(b_0\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 a&\equiv c(1-2u)(\xi^{1-M}Z+uW)\pmod4,\\
 b_0&\equiv(\xi^{1-M}+e\xi^{1-2M})X^{2k}+\xi+
                    ((1+e)u^2+1)X^{H-2k}\pmod2 .
\end{split}                                                            \tag{4}
\]
For the first congruence use \(M^{-1}\equiv c\bmod4\).
For the second, the coefficients of \(L_0'(\xi)\) and \(E\) modulo two give \(L_0'(\xi)=\xi^{2M-2}W\) and \(E=\xi^{2M-2}+e\xi^{M-2}\) there.
After multiplication by \(D^{-1}\equiv\xi^{1-M}\), the coefficient of \(ZW\) in \(b\) is one, and the coefficient of \(W^2\) is \((1+e)\xi^{2M-1}+\xi^{-1}\).
The replacements defining \(b_0\) yield (4).

At \(x=-\omega\) one has \(x+x^{-1}=1\).
Write \(v=x^H\).
The assumptions that \(k\) is odd and divisible by three give \(x^k=-1,\ x^{H-k}=-v,\ x^{2k}=1,\ x^{H-2k}=v\).
Substitution of (4) into (3), using \(u^3=1\), gives \(G_\xi(x)\equiv vA(u)-\xi B(u)\pmod8\), where
\[
       A(u)=-3+2cu-4eu^2,\qquad
       B(u)=1+(4-2c)u^2+4eu .
\]
For \(c=1\) these have sum \(-2+2(u+u^2)=-4\), and for \(c=3\) their sum is \(-2+10u-6u^2\equiv4\bmod8\) since \(u+u^2=-1\).
Thus \(A+B\equiv4\bmod8\) and \(B\equiv1\bmod2\) give \(A/B\equiv3\bmod8\).
It follows that \(G_\xi(x)\equiv B(u)(3v-\xi)\bmod8\).

Among the primitive \(3R\)-th roots \(\xi\), the two possible values of \(u=\xi^M\) each occur \(\varphi(R)\) times, by the coprime parts of orders three and \(R\).
The product \(B(\omega)B(\omega^2)\) is an odd rational integer, and \(\varphi(R)\) is even for odd \(R>1\).
Hence the product of all the scalar factors \(B(u)\) is one modulo eight.
Multiplication of (3) at \(x\) over these \(\xi\) now gives \(\Phi_{3R}(3v)\bmod8\).
The local factor for the full conductor has degree \(2H\varphi(R)\), so passage to its trace polynomial contributes precisely \(x^{-H\varphi(R)}=v^{-\varphi(R)}\).
Multiplication over all selected conductors proves (1).

<a id="antichain"></a>
## The weight of a prime power and the rational obstruction

Write \(v=-y\) with \(y=\omega^H\) a primitive cube root.
Then \(3v\equiv5y\bmod8\), so Taylor expansion in (1) gives the contribution
\[
  y^{-\varphi(R)}\Phi_{3R}(y)(1+4L_R)\pmod8,\qquad
  L_R=\frac{\bar y\,\bar\Phi'_{3R}(\bar y)}{\bar\Phi_{3R}(\bar y)}
\]
where any integral lift of the residue \(L_R\) may be used inside the multiple of four.
The denominator is a unit since \(\bar y\) has order three and \(R>1\).
The [integral evaluation at trace minus one](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#reference) is \(y^{-\varphi(R)}\Phi_{3R}(y)=1\) for composite squarefree \(R\) and \((-1)^{f_p}p\) for \(R=p\).
To compute the logarithmic derivative, write the primitive \(3R\)-th roots in the residue field as \(\eta=zr\), with \(z\) primitive of order three and \(r\) primitive of order \(R\).
Each contributes \((1+\eta/\bar y)^{-1}\) to \(L_R\).
The two ratios \(z/\bar y\) are one and a nontrivial cube root.
The [two sums over primitive \(R\)-th roots](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#fibers) at these arguments both vanish for composite squarefree \(R\), and for \(R=p\) they equal \(e_p\) and \(f_p\), respectively.
This proves (2), whose evaluations at five and seven are \(7\) and \(3\) modulo eight.
The coordinating agent contributed the logarithmic derivative evaluation for arbitrary \(R\).

For the specified two-prime family exactly one even index has radical seven, namely \(a=0\) for large \(A\).
Exactly one has radical five, the greatest even \(a\le A\), because \(b_a=0\) precisely for \(A-a\in\{0,1\}\).
The other radicals are \(35\), so (1) gives \(7\cdot3\equiv5\bmod8\).
A monic rational lift must satisfy \(T_l(1)=1\) by the [exact trace values](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces).
Since \(t>L/7\) and \(R\le35\), each \(H_t>L/245\).
For every admissible \(k<L/490\), the condition \(2k<\min H_t\) holds.
The residue five excludes all those \(k\) divisible by three, and the [parity formula](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#statement) excludes the others.
Finally \(\sqrt n\le L\) gives \(\sqrt n/500<L/490\), proving the interval assertion.
