# A uniform positive Pisot excess for every four-term trace lift

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary branch index, integer coefficient and offset

There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that every positive-constant Pisot input with constant \(q\ge2\) and exterior root \(\theta\) satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c\) if its [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(C\), of degree \(h\), has lift
\[
                 \Pi(z)=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})
\]
with at most five nonzero terms.
Equivalently this covers \(C=jU_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)\) for all branch indices \(j\ge1\), all integers \(a\), and every \(1\le d\le h\), as well as the single term \(jU_h\).
The lift is anti-reciprocal of even degree \(N=2h+2\), so its midpoint coefficient vanishes and all its support comes in pairs.
If it has an interior pair, it has the exact form
\[
                 \Pi=j(1-z^N)+a(z^d-z^{N-d}).
\]
The bounds on \(a,j,d,h\) are absent from the constant in the conclusion.
The theorem concerns these trace lifts and does not bound the measure of every integer polynomial.

The main researcher combined interior-ratio compactness with signed finite-grid folding when \(|a|/j\) tends to one.
Agent-S supplied the rational-ratio profiles and a separate verification of the finite-prefix and folding arguments.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## The possible limiting coefficient ratios

Suppose \(\delta=\theta-q\to0\).
Use \(p=N-d\), \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(a)\) for \(a\ne0\), and \(r_i=|a|/j\) on the sequence.
The [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds \(r_i\) absolutely since \(M(\Pi)=j\) and the support has size at most four.
Extract so that \(s\) stays fixed and \(r_i\to r\).
The case \(a=0\), and also \(r=0\), contradict the [small interior coefficient bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v64.md#dichotomy).
The normalized lift has bounded circle supremum, and its coefficients are integral after division by \(j\) through \(d-1\).
The [divisibility obstruction for arbitrary branches](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) therefore gives \(d=o(h)\), with \(h\to\infty\).
If \(r>1\), fix \(b\) with \(1/r<b<1\) and take a circle of radius \(R_i=b^{1/d}\).
There \(|1+s r_i z^d|\ge r_i b-1\), bounded below, whereas the other two terms in \(\Pi/j\) have sum in modulus at most \((1+r_i)R_i^{N-d}=o(1)\).
Rouché's theorem forces \(d\) zeros in the open disk, contrary to the trace root locations.
It remains to treat \(0<r<1\) and \(r=1\).

<a id="interior"></a>
## The interior of the ratio interval

The actual signed measure \(\omega_i\) of \(J_i=j/K_q\) has mass one and only a negative atom at \(1\) of size \(\gamma_i=j/[4(q-1)]=o(1)\).
The lifted denominator \(B^\sharp=\sum_{l=0}^N b_l z^l\) is reciprocal with \(b_0=b_N=1\) and \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one by the [delay-one return](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
We have \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_i\), so below \(p\) its coefficients are those of \((1+s r_i z^d)J_i\).

First consider \(d\to\infty\), \(t=o(d)\), and the mixed moments \(M(l,k)=2\int x^l y^k\,d\Omega\) of the limit probability for \((x,y)=(v^t,v^d)\), as in the [fixed-ratio argument](Yin_C_G0345_v14.md#seam).
Here \(M(l,0)\) is integral and
\[
 M(l,1)\in\mathbb Z\ (l<0),\qquad M(0,1)+s r\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 M(l,1)+s r M(l,0)\in\mathbb Z\ (l>0).
\]
All estimates use fixed test characters and permit \(r_i\to r\).
The Fourier–Stieltjes sequence \(M(l,1)\) takes finitely many values because \(M(l,0)\) is a bounded integer.
Polynomial interpolation of its level indicators and the [integer transform theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) give a common periodic part on both tails.
Thus \(sr M(l,0)\) is integral for all sufficiently large \(l>0\).
The [cyclotomic first-marginal argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) gives \(J_x=1,1+\epsilon x^u\) or \(1/(1+\epsilon x^u)\).
A purely atomic marginal is excluded before this classification by periodicity at zero and the nonintegral value of \(M(0,1)\).
The reciprocal binomials contradict eventual integrality of \(sr M(l,0)\).
For a polynomial binomial \(J_x=1+\epsilon x^u\), the reflected mixed measure with Fourier coefficients \(M(l,1)\) has a density \(F\) bounded by \(|1+\epsilon x^u|^2\).
Its coefficients tend to zero and belong to a finite set, so it is a Laurent polynomial.
The displayed moment conditions give
\[
                    F=P-sr(1+\epsilon x^u),\qquad P\in\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}].
\]
Double vanishing of \(F\) at the roots of \(1+\epsilon x^u\) first makes \(P\) divisible by that binomial over the integer Laurent polynomials.
The integral quotient then takes the constant value \(sr\) at every root.
Its remainder modulo the binomial is an integer polynomial of degree less than \(u\), using \(x^u=-\epsilon\) also to reduce negative exponents.
Subtracting \(sr\) gives a polynomial of degree less than \(u\) with \(u\) distinct zeros, so \(sr\) is an integer, impossible.
This leaves the Haar marginal for all \(0<r<1\).
By the same diagonal argument and Bessel estimate as in the [fixed-ratio seam](Yin_C_G0345_v14.md#seam), only boundedly many \(b_l\) are nonzero up to \(Ld\) for each fixed \(L\).
The multipliers are 2 and \(2+2s r_i v^{-d}\), now uniformly bounded by four eventually.
Testing a fixed number greater than 16 of purported nonzero coefficients in each interval of controlled diameter suffices.
For bounded \(d\) the support bound is immediate.

For \(d/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\) take the far mixed limit under \((w,u)=(v^d,v^t)\).
The projected probability transform \(J_0\) satisfies \((1+s r w)J_0=B_0\) with integral coefficients by extraction in the prefix.
For rational \(r\) its profiles are those of the [rational-ratio classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#measure), including the [nine profiles at one half](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix).
For irrational \(r\), the [finite-valued transform and Galois-orbit argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v67.md#conjugacy) gives the eight profiles under the same necessary range restrictions.
In all cases the atoms have order dividing six, after the sign rotation as well.
At every harmonic \(k\ge1\) the tested limits of \(b_{kt+ld}\) are the Fourier coefficients of \(1+s r w\) times the reflected weighted measure.
Replacing \(r_i\) by \(r\) in these limits is valid by uniform boundedness of the original moments.
Their integrality gives an integer Laurent density for the continuous part as in the [far-row splitting argument](Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows).
Such a family vanishes by the [rational-ratio envelope and specialization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v66.md#envelope), the [half-ratio specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#torsion), or the [irrational-ratio specialization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v67.md#extinction).
The remaining atom moments force \(b_{t+6d}=b_t\) eventually.

Extract a fixed window \(Ld\le t\le h/L\) for this period as in the [arbitrary-offset proof](Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#comparison).
Set \(U=1-z^{6d}\) and let \(Q\) be the truncation of \(UB^\sharp\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\).
It has a bounded number of bounded coefficients through degree \((L+6)d\) and is zero above that degree.
With \(A_i=1+s r_i z^d\), \(J_*=Q/(A_i U)\) matches \(J_i\) through the truncation index and has a uniformly bounded Fourier measure for its positive coefficients.
Indeed the congruence-class expansion from that proof uses the convergent majorant \(\sum_{m\ge0}(1+m)(\sup r_i)^m\) after discarding finitely many inputs.
In \(A_i U B^\sharp-Q\Pi/j=A_i U(\Pi/j)(J_i-J_*)\) the degrees are \(O(h)\), all polynomial factors on the right are uniformly bounded and \(|A_i|\ge1-\sup r_i>0\).
The [two-cutoff and integer support argument](Yin_C_G0345_v14.md#cutoff) bounds the support of the cyclotomic multiple \(UB^\sharp\), contrary to its measure tending to one strictly from above.

<a id="prefix-clearing"></a>
## A cyclotomic multiple of the short initial segment

It remains to handle \(r_i\to1\), allowing approach from either side.
Let \(P_d(z)=\sum_{l=0}^d b_l z^l\).
There is a cyclotomic multiplier \(U\), either one or a unit binomial, such that \(UP_d\) has boundedly many terms on a subsequence.
For bounded \(d\) take \(U=1\).
For \(d\to\infty\), the strictly positive moments \([z^l]J_i\) equal the integers \(b_l\) for \(l<d\).
The [moving-index argument using the actual positive atoms](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#atoms) applies to every one or two indices \(o(d)\), since the polynomial in its proof has degree \(o(d)\le o(h)\) and its energy uses only these integral moments.
The [local sign and subgroup rules](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#local) therefore give one of the models \(1,\ 1+\epsilon z^u,\ (1-\epsilon z^u)^{-1}\) for the Taylor series of \(J_i\) through a fixed positive fraction \(c_0d\), with \(u\) allowed to vary.

In the first two models the positive model measure has density bounded by two.
On any finite collection of exponents in a block of diameter at most \(c_0d\), the Gram matrix against \(\omega_i\) equals the model Gram matrix and has operator norm at most two.
Against \(\omega_i^+\) the difference is the rank-one matrix with all entries \(\gamma_i\).
Through \(1\le l<d\), the \(b_l\) are twice the corresponding moments.
Bessel's inequality on a fixed collection of more than eight purported nonzeros bounds their squared sum by \(8+o(1)\), although the integer coefficients give at least nine before the \(o(1)\) correction.
Partitioning into boundedly many blocks yields the assertion with \(U=1\), counting \(b_0,b_d\) separately.
If the geometric model has \(u>c_0d/4\), its first \(c_0d/4\) positive coefficients vanish, and the same proof uses the Haar model on that shorter prefix.

Otherwise put \(U=1-\epsilon z^u\).
For \(u<l<d\) the integer coefficient \([z^l]UP_d=b_l-\epsilon b_{l-u}\) is twice the signed moment of \(v^l(1-\epsilon v^{-u})\).
On any block of diameter at most \(c_0d/2\), the Gram matrix of these weighted characters against \(\omega_i\) agrees with that of the geometric model, since the tested differences and their shifts by \(u\) belong to the prefix.
Its operator norm is at most four: the model measure is half Haar and half the uniform measure on \(v^u=\epsilon\), and the weight kills the atomic part.
On any fixed number of characters, adding the atom \(\gamma_i\) changes this bound and their moments by \(o(1)\).
The squared norm of the constant function two against \(\omega_i^+\) tends to four.
Bessel now permits only boundedly many nonzero difference coefficients on each block, by testing any fixed number greater than 16.
The indices up to \(u\) contribute only boundedly many terms because \(b_l=0\) for \(0<l<u\).
For the upper edge of the product \(UP_d\), one needs the original coefficients \(b_l\) on \(d-u<l<d\).
Their exponents have positive differences strictly below \(u\), where the model coefficients vanish, so the unweighted Haar Gram argument bounds their number of nonzeros as well.
The exceptional coefficient \(b_d\) and the index \(l=d\) each add at most a bounded number of terms.
This proves the short-segment assertion for all models.

<a id="folding"></a>
## Rounding the long folded segment at the limiting ratio one

Fold \(B^\sharp\) modulo \(z^p-s\) with \(p=N-d>d\), and denote the polynomial remainder of degree less than \(p\) by \(I\).
Extend its coefficients \(I_l\), \(0\le l<p\), to the cyclic group of order \(2p\) by \(I_{l+p}=s I_l\).
On \(0\le l\le d\), reciprocity gives \(I_l=b_l+s b_{d-l}\), while on \(d<l<p\) one has \(I_l=b_l\).
Write \(M_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i(v)\) for all \(l\), so \(M_0=2\).
The folded integers satisfy
\[
                   I_l=M_l+sM_{l-d}+O(|r_i-1|)
                           \qquad(0\le l<p)
\]
uniformly.
For \(0<l<d\) the identity is exact, and for \(d<l<p\) the actual multiplier in the second summand is \(s r_i\).
At \(l=0,d\) use \(b_0=1,\ b_d=M_d+s r_i\) to obtain the same estimate.

At every support point of the inverse measure, \(\Pi(v)=0\), giving
\[
                |1+s v^d|\,|1-s v^p|\le2|r_i-1| .
\]
Project \(v\) to a nearest root \(\zeta^p=s\), splitting tied choices symmetrically to preserve conjugation.
For \(l<p\), the bound \(|v^l-\zeta^l|\le(\pi/2)|v^p-s|\) follows by taking angular distance to the grid.
Weighting the signed measure by \(2(1+s v^{-d})\) before projection gives a real Fourier transform on \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\) of uniformly bounded algebra norm.
Its error from \(I_l\) is \(O(|r_i-1|)\) on the first \(p\) indices by the product bound.
Since \(\zeta^p=s\), both functions have the same signed extension to the other \(p\) indices.
The [near-integer rounding theorem](../ledger/Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#rounding) expresses \(I_l\) as a bounded sum of signed coset indicators on the full cyclic group for large \(i\).
On \([0,p-1]\) each coset becomes one ordinary finite arithmetic progression or the empty set.
Its polynomial times \(1-z^a\), with \(a\mid 2p\) the coset step, has at most two terms.
The product \(V\) of these boundedly many binomials therefore makes \(VI\) have bounded support.

Finally \(B^\sharp=I+(z^p-s)T\) where \(T=z^dP_d(1/z)\), by reciprocity.
The reciprocal \(U^*\) of the short-segment multiplier from the preceding section makes \(U^*T\) have bounded support.
Thus \(U^*V B^\sharp\) has bounded support by the ordinary polynomial identity, since \(U^*,V,z^p-s\) themselves have bounded support.
The multiplier is cyclotomic up to sign, giving the same contradiction to the [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
Every limiting ratio has now been excluded, proving the uniform excess.
