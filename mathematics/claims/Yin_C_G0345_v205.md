# The least Mahler measure of a radical of a positive real unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least factor measure at each dilation

Let \(S\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a positive real nontorsion algebraic unit \(\beta\), and fix the resulting real embedding \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\subset\mathbb R\).
Put \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\) and \(B=m(S)>0\), using logarithmic Mahler measure.
Every real radical of \(\beta\) written below denotes its positive root.
There is a largest integer \(r\ge1\) such that \(\beta^{1/r}\in K\); the full set of such exponents is exactly the divisors of \(r\).
For any \(h\ge1\) put \(t=\gcd(h,r)\) and \(s=h/t\).
Then
\[
    \min_{F\mid S(X^h)} m(F)=\frac Bt,\qquad
    [\mathbb Q(\beta^{1/h}):\mathbb Q]=ds,\qquad
    S(X^h)\ \text{is irreducible over }\mathbb Q
            \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \gcd(h,r)=1,                       \tag{1}
\]
where the minimum is over monic irreducible factors in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
Each factor occurs once and its measure is an integer multiple of \(B/t\).
In particular the minimum over every factor of every dilation is \(B/r\), attained in the original field by the degree-\(d\) unit \(\beta^{1/r}\).

There is also a description of the other factors.
Let \(\mu_t\) be the set of \(t\)-th roots of unity, and take one \(\xi\) in each orbit of \(\operatorname{Gal}(K(\mu_t)/K)\) on \(\mu_t\).
Write \(f_\xi=[K(\xi):K]\).
Each orbit contributes either one factor of degree \(ds f_\xi\) and measure \(B f_\xi/t\), or two factors of degree \(ds f_\xi/2\) and measure \(B f_\xi/(2t)\) each.
If \(s\) is odd, or if \(\beta\) also has a negative real conjugate, every orbit contributes just one factor.
In that case the total number is
\[
                  \sum_{j\mid t}[K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j):\mathbb Q],  \tag{2}
\]
where \(\zeta_j\) denotes a primitive \(j\)-th root of unity.
The two-factor alternative can occur for a totally positive unit.

<a id="positive-norm"></a>
## A positive norm gives the exact minimum

If \(\gamma=\beta^{1/k}\in K\), then \(\gamma\) is a unit, \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K\) and \(m(\gamma)=B/k\).
Only finitely many such positive units exist, because their degrees are \(d\) and their measures are bounded: the absolute value of each coefficient of their minimal polynomials is at most \(2^d\exp B\) by the root product.
Distinct \(k\) give distinct \(\gamma\), since \(\beta\ne1\).
If \(k,l\) are two admissible exponents and \(v=\operatorname{lcm}(k,l)\), then \(v/k\) and \(v/l\) are coprime.
An integral combination equal to one expresses \(\beta^{1/v}\) as a product of integral powers of \(\beta^{1/k}\) and \(\beta^{1/l}\).
Positivity fixes the choice of each radical in this identity.
The admissible exponents are closed under divisors and least common multiples, proving the assertion about \(r\).

For any irreducible factor \(F\) of \(S(X^h)\), choose its root \(\alpha\) so that \(\alpha^h=\beta\).
This is possible because the \(h\)-th powers of its conjugates include every conjugate of \(\beta\).
Write \(e=[K(\alpha):K]\).
The [radical norm and height identities](Yin_C_G0206_v1.md#statement) give
\[
      m(F)=\frac eh B,\qquad
      w^h=\beta^e,\qquad w=\operatorname{Norm}_{K(\alpha)/K}\alpha.
\]
Here \(w\in K\subset\mathbb R\), even when \(\alpha\) is not real, so \(|w|\in K\) and \(|w|=\beta^{e/h}\).
Write \(e/h=a/b\) in lowest terms.
Bézout's identity for \(a,b\) gives \(\beta^{1/b}\in K\) from \(|w|=\beta^{a/b}\) and \(\beta\).
Thus \(b\mid r\) as well as \(b\mid h\), so \(e\) is a positive multiple of \(s=h/t\).
This argument extends the [positive norm computation for the golden ratio](Yin_C_G0345_v195.md#count) to all selected factors, including those without a real root.

The positive \(\alpha_0=\beta^{1/h}\) satisfies \(X^s-\beta^{1/t}=0\) over \(K\); its relative degree is at most \(s\) and must therefore equal \(s\).
This proves attainment, the stated absolute degree and the irreducibility equivalence in (1).
The zeros of \(S(X^h)\) are simple, since \(S\) is separable and \(\beta\ne0\).
Taking \(h=r\) proves the global minimum assertion.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## The possible degrees in a root-of-unity orbit

Write \(u=\beta^{1/t}\in K\).
In the fiber \(\alpha^h=\beta\), each \(\alpha\) has \(\alpha^s=u\xi\) for one \(\xi\in\mu_t\), and all these equations occur.
Irreducible factors of \(S(X^h)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) correspond bijectively to \(K\)-conjugacy classes in this fiber: a \(\mathbb Q\)-automorphism sending one root over \(\beta\) to another fixes \(\beta\), and every \(\mathbb Q\)-class meets the fiber.
Over a representative \(\xi\) of an orbit put \(L=K(\xi)\).
The stabilizer of \(\xi\) fixes \(L\), so the factors arising from this orbit correspond exactly to the irreducible factors of \(X^s-u\xi\) over \(L\).
If such a factor has degree \(j\), its corresponding absolute degree is \(d f_\xi j\), since \(\xi=\alpha^s/u\) belongs to \(K(\alpha)\).

Let \(E=K(\mu_\infty)\) be the compositum with all roots of unity.
For a root of the relative factor of degree \(j\), its norm \(v\) to \(L\) satisfies \(v^s=(u\xi)^j\).
It follows that \(v/u^{j/s}\) is a root of unity, so \(u^{j/s}\in E\).
Writing \(j/s=a/b\) in lowest terms and using Bézout again gives \(y=u^{1/b}\in E\).
The extension \(E/K\) is abelian: each finite cyclotomic compositum over \(K\) acts faithfully on its roots of unity and has abelian Galois group.
Since \(K\subset\mathbb R\), complex conjugation belongs to this group at every finite level and commutes with every \(K\)-automorphism.
Consequently all \(K\)-conjugates of the real \(y\) are real and satisfy \(Y^b=u\).
For odd \(b\) this forces \(y\in K\); for even \(b\) the two real possibilities differ by sign and hence \(y^2\in K\).
In the first case \(tb\mid r\), and in the second \(tb/2\mid r\), by the description of the admissible exponents.
But \(b\mid s\) and \(\gcd(s,r/t)=1\); thus \(b=1\) or \(2\).
As \(1\le j\le s\), this means \(j=s\) or \(j=s/2\).
In the latter case a complementary factor must have the same degree, and it is distinct by separability.
Multiplying degrees by \(d f_\xi\) and using \(m(F)=[K(\alpha):K]B/h\) gives both stated possibilities.

An odd \(s\) excludes \(b=2\).
If \(\beta\) has a negative real conjugate, \(r\) is odd, because an even root in \(K\) would make that conjugate a square in \(\mathbb R\).
Thus \(t\) is odd and the real embedding of \(K\) in question takes \(u\) to a negative number.
The positive square root of \(u\) cannot lie in \(E\): if it belonged to \(K(\zeta_M)\), extend this real embedding of \(K\) to an embedding of \(K(\zeta_M)\) in \(\mathbb C\).
The extension commutes with complex conjugation on \(K\) (both images are real) and on \(\zeta_M\) (its image is a root of unity).
It would take the real square root to a real square root of a negative number.
This rules out \(b=2\) also in the mixed-sign case.

All primitive \(j\)-th roots generate the same field \(K(\zeta_j)\) over \(K\), so each orbit among them has size \([K(\zeta_j):K]\).
Restriction to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)\) identifies the Galois group of the compositum over \(K\) with the subgroup fixing \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)\): its image has exactly that fixed field because the compositum is Galois over \(K\).
Dividing \(\varphi(j)\) by the orbit size and summing proves (2).

<a id="example"></a>
## A cyclotomic orbit that splits in two

Put \(w=11+2\sqrt{30}=(\sqrt5+\sqrt6)^2\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{30})\), and \(\beta=w^5\).
Both conjugates of \(w\) are positive and their product is one, so \(\beta\) is a totally positive unit of degree two: \(w>1\) and its conjugate is \(1/w\).
The positive square root \(y=\sqrt5+\sqrt6\) is not in \(K\), since \(y-y^{-1}=2\sqrt5\) and \(\sqrt5\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt{30})\).
Thus \(5\mid r\) but \(2\nmid r\), since \(\sqrt{\beta}=w^2 y\).
At \(h=10\) one has \(t=5\), \(s=2\) and \(u=w\).
For primitive \(\xi\in\mu_5\), the field \(K(\xi)\) contains \(\sqrt5\) since \(1+2(\xi+\xi^{-1})\) has square \(5\).
It contains \(\sqrt6=\sqrt{30}/\sqrt5\), hence \(y\), and \(\xi\) itself is a square in \(\mu_5\).
Therefore \(X^2-w\xi\) splits into two distinct linear factors over \(K(\xi)\), realizing the two-factor alternative.

<a id="prime-blocks"></a>
## Consequence for prime blocks

The unique noncyclotomic factor in [the normalized prime-block family](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) has a positive real root: the exterior disk at any positive binomial root is fixed by complex conjugation and contains a single root.
If a normalized exponent is even, the corresponding negative disk also yields a real root of this factor, so (2) counts all noncyclotomic factors of the block polynomial after every common dilation.
The [simultaneous irreducibility tests](Yin_C_G0345_v202.md#statement) consequently certify coprimality of \(r\) with the selected dilation primes.
For a sequence of such normalized seeds with fixed block primes, \(B\) stays between two positive constants.
Formula (1) then shows that common dilations can produce individual measures tending to zero along a subsequence if and only if \(r\) is unbounded along the normalized seeds: the forward implication uses \(t\le r\), and the reverse implication takes \(h=r\) on a subsequence with \(r\to\infty\).
This characterization neither proves nor disproves unboundedness of \(r\) in the family.
