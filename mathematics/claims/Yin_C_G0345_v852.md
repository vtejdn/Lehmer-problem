# Squarefreeness of every torsion translate and transcendence of critical slopes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Torsion translates and critical slopes

Let \(F=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\) be the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
For every primitive signed pair \((k,l)\) and all roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\), the Laurent polynomial \(F(\xi X^k,\eta X^l)\) has only simple zeros in the characteristic-zero torus.
When \(kl\ne0\), the conclusion also holds over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) for arbitrary translating constants \(\xi,\eta\) of dyadic valuation zero.

At every prime covered by the [ordinary](Yin_C_G0345_v847.md#spacing), [horizontal](Yin_C_G0345_v846.md#centers), [exceptional ordinary](Yin_C_G0345_v849.md#centers) or [ramified cubic](Yin_C_G0345_v848.md#centers) construction, every projective slope arising from a zero of its prepared logarithmic critical value is transcendental over \(\mathbb Q\).
This statement concerns the zeros in the respective charts with their stated hypotheses.

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## The critical equations in characteristic two

Write \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\).
Reduction of the [curve equation](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#reduction) gives \(F=a(V^2+1)+U^2V\) with \(a=U^4+U^3+U+1\) in characteristic two.
Every torus point of \(F=0\) satisfies \(U\ne1\) and \(V\ne1\), since \(F(1,V)=V\) and \(F(U,1)=U^2\).
For the three possible parity classes of a primitive direction, direct differentiation gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 (k,l)\bmod 2 & \text{polynomial nonzero at every torus point of }F=0\\ \hline
 (0,1) & DF=U^2V\\
 (1,0) & DF=U(U^2+1)(V^2+1)\\
 (1,1) & DF+F=(U^4+1)(V^2+1).
\end{array}
\]
Thus \(F=DF=0\) has no torus point over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\).

For \(kl\ne0\), the support of \(F\) lies in the rectangle \([0,4]\times[0,2]\) and contains its four corners, all with coefficient one.
The signed weight \(ki+lj\) has unique minimum and maximum corners, so after clearing a monomial the section has integral dyadic coefficients and unit constant and leading coefficients for all dyadic-unit twists.
The unit leading coefficient makes every root integral, and the unit constant coefficient gives integrality of its inverse by reversing the polynomial.
At a multiple root the logarithmic derivative of the section gives \(DF=0\), and both coordinates reduce to a common torus zero of the two reduced critical equations, an impossibility.
For roots of unity over characteristic zero the coefficients and all section roots are algebraic, so a dyadic embedding applies.

<a id="axes"></a>
## The two coordinate directions

Put \(t=U-U^{-1}\), so that
\[
  F/U^2=(t^2-7t+12)V^2+V+(t^2+7t+12).
\]
For fixed torsion \(U\), choose a complex embedding, where \(t\) is purely imaginary.
The leading and constant coefficients of this quadratic in \(V\) are conjugate and the former has modulus \(|t-3||t-4|\ge12\).
Its discriminant is \(1-4|(t-3)(t-4)|^2\ne0\).

For fixed torsion \(V\), a multiple torus root in \(U\) would either satisfy \(U^2=-1\), where the map \(U\mapsto t\) ramifies, or give a multiple root in \(t\).
In the first case both coordinates are roots of unity, hence dyadic units, and the [dyadic critical equations](#dyadic) exclude the direction \((1,0)\).
In the second case the polynomial in \(t\) is
\[
  (V^2+1)t^2+7(1-V^2)t+12(V^2+1)+V.
\]
When \(V^2=-1\) the coefficient of \(t\) is 14.
Otherwise its discriminant is \(V^4-4V^3-194V^2-4V+1\), which divided by \(V^2\) is \(s^2-4s-196\) with \(s=V+V^{-1}\in[-2,2]\).
This is strictly negative throughout the interval, excluding a repeated root.
The primitive axis exponents are \(\pm1\), so rescaling and inversion of the moving coordinate preserve simplicity.

<a id="centers"></a>
## Transcendence at zeros of the local critical value

If a slope at one of the stated zeros were algebraic, the [logarithm-line argument](Yin_C_G0345_v851.md#logarithms) would give a rational direction \([k:l]\) and a characteristic-zero critical point \((U,V)\) on a torsion translate in that direction.
Indeed the embedded algebraic coordinates satisfy \(F=DF=0\) and \(U^l/V^k\) is torsion by the analytic subgroup theorem and the kernel of the local logarithm.
Taking a primitive representative and expressing the point by Bézout on the translate produces a torus root where the section and its logarithmic derivative vanish.
The preceding squarefreeness excludes every such root.
