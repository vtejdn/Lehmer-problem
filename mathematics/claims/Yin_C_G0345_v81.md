# A Mahler gap from samples on several grids of roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## The total number of independent samples

Fix a nonempty finite list \((a_i,b_i)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\times\mathbb Z\) and an integer \(H\ge0\).
For a positive integer \(N\) put \(n_i=a_iN+b_i\), taking only \(N\) for which every \(n_i>0\).
Let \(d_N\) be the number of distinct complex numbers in the union of the grids \(\{\xi:\xi^{n_i}=1\}\).
For \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) define
\[
       A_n(P)=\frac1n\sum_{\xi^n=1}|P(\xi)|.
\]
For each fixed \(B\ge0\), any nonzero \(P\) with
\[
          \deg P\le d_N+H,\qquad \max_i A_{n_i}(P)\le B
\]
has a nonzero integer polynomial multiple of bounded term count with exactly the same Mahler measure.
The bound depends only on the list, \(H\) and \(B\).
In particular \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\ge\eta>0\) uniformly, without any restriction on the coefficients, signatures or factors.
The same conclusions hold if at each grid one can subtract an integer polynomial of at most \(H\) terms before testing its average, independently for each grid.
No bound on the coefficients or exponents of those corrections is needed.

For example, for fixed \(r\) and \(n_i=N+i\), \(0\le i<r\), the union has \(rN+O_r(1)\) points.
The conclusion tests degree \(rN+O_r(1)\) using orders comparable to \(1/r\) of that degree.
At any allowed unbounded excess over \(d_N\), even the condition that every sampled value vanish retains the entire unrestricted Mahler spectrum.
This last assertion is about all integer polynomials, including reducible ones.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Recovering short expressions for blocks of cyclic coefficients

Say that a Laurent expression has bounded complexity here if it is a sum of a bounded number of Laurent monomials with rational coefficients divided by products of boundedly many factors \(1-X^h\), \(h\ge1\).
The monomial exponents, binomial exponents and rational coefficients need no bound.
Sums and products of boundedly many such expressions still have this property.

The coefficients of \(P\bmod(X^n-1)\) in positions \(0,\ldots,n-1\) give an integer function on the cyclic group of order \(n\).
With the [finite Fourier convention](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) its Fourier algebra norm equals \(A_n(P)\).
By Sanders's theorem the cyclic coefficient function of each corrected polynomial is a sum of boundedly many signed indicators of residue classes modulo divisors of \(n\).
The correction itself adds at most \(H\) point masses of arbitrary integer sizes to these coefficients.
In the ordinary polynomial of any consecutive block of these coefficients, a single residue class contributes either zero or a translated finite geometric progression.
Its generating expression is \(X^u(1-X^{jh})/(1-X^h)\) for some \(j,h\ge1\).
Thus any block, shifted to any origin, has bounded complexity.
A bounded number of splits, wraparounds or individual coefficient changes preserves that assertion.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Symbolic interpolation of the coefficient blocks

Use two indeterminates \(X,W\).
Let \(c\) be the degree in \(W\) of the monic least common multiple in \(\mathbb Q(X)[W]\) of \(W^{a_i}-X^{-b_i}\).
For \(g_i=\gcd(a_i,|b_i|)\), group the pairs by their common primitive direction \((a,b)=(a_i/g_i,b_i/g_i)\).
For each direction let \(E\) be the union of the sets of positive divisors of its \(g_i\).
Within that direction the distinct factors, up to Laurent monomial units in \(X\), are \(\Phi_e(X^b W^a)\), \(e\in E\).
Indeed \((X^b W^a)^{g_i}-1\) is their product over \(e\mid g_i\), and different orders have disjoint roots.
At any complex \(X\ne0\) which is not a root of unity, distinct directions have no common root in \(W\), since a common root would imply \(X^{a_i b_j-a_j b_i}=1\).
These facts also hold for indeterminate \(X\).
Consequently \(c=\sum_{(a,b)}a\sum_{e\in E}\varphi(e)\), and the specialized least common multiple has this same degree at every such complex \(X\).

On the actual grids the contribution from one direction consists of the distinct roots of the polynomials \(\Phi_e(X^{aN+b})\), \(e\in E\).
These have combined degree \((aN+b)\sum_{e\in E}\varphi(e)\) for large \(N\).
Two grids of distinct directions share at most \(|a_i b_j-a_j b_i|\) points since their common orders divide this nonzero integer.
Thus \(d_N=cN+O(1)\), with the constant fixed by the list.
Remove from \(P\) the terms of degrees at least \(cN\), calling their sum \(T\).
There are \(O(1+H)\) such terms.
They count as additional sparse corrections in the cyclic coefficient argument.
For the remaining polynomial write
\[
          P-T=\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} X^{jN}P_j(X),\qquad \deg P_j<N,
          \qquad U(W)=\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}P_j(X)W^j .
\]
The assertion for the finitely many small \(N\) follows directly from the degree bound.

For each \(i\), the remainder of \(U\) modulo \(W^{a_i}-X^{-b_i}\) has coefficients
\[
          U_{i,s}(X)=\sum_{\substack{0\le j<c\\j\equiv s\bmod a_i}}
                     X^{-b_i\lfloor j/a_i\rfloor}P_j(X),\qquad 0\le s<a_i .
\]
Their exponents lie in \([-C,N-1+C]\) for a constant depending only on the list.
The cyclic representative of \(P-T\) modulo \(X^{n_i}-1\) is obtained by folding \(\sum_s X^{sN}U_{i,s}\) modulo \(n_i\).
In the interval \(C'<t<N-1-C'\), the coefficient at \(sN+t\) is the coefficient at \(t\) of \(U_{i,s}\) alone.
Indeed adjacent blocks extend at most \(C\) beyond their ends, and the cyclic seam moves by only \(b_i\) positions relative to \(a_iN\).
Here one may take \(C'=2C+2\max_i|b_i|+3\), for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Only a bounded number of coefficients of each \(U_{i,s}\) lie outside this interval.
The consecutive-block result therefore gives bounded complexity for every \(U_{i,s}\).

The matrix \(L(X)\) of the displayed remainder maps has \(c\) columns and entries in \(\mathbb Q[X,X^{-1}]\).
It has rank \(c\) at each nonzero non-torsion complex \(X\), because a polynomial in \(W\) of degree less than \(c\) in its kernel would be divisible by the entire specialized least common multiple.
The greatest common divisor \(D\) of its maximal minors thus has only roots of unity as nonzero roots.
In the Laurent polynomial ring over \(\mathbb Q\) choose \(D\) monic with nonzero constant, so it is a product of cyclotomic polynomials or is one.
Bézout's identity expresses it as a linear combination of the minors with fixed rational Laurent polynomial coefficients.
Multiplying the adjugate of each square row submatrix by its row selector and combining with these same coefficients gives a fixed matrix \(V\) with \(VL=D I_c\).
Hence each \(DP_j\) has bounded complexity.
Summing the shifted blocks and adding \(DT\) gives the same property for \(DP\).
Clear all displayed binomial denominators and shift to nonnegative powers.
The resulting nonzero polynomial is a monomial times \(DP\) times a product of \(1-X^h\).
This identity makes its coefficients integral even though the intermediate Bézout coefficients are rational.
Its term count is bounded and every extra factor has Mahler measure one.
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives the claimed gap.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## What the samples lose beyond the degree bound

The polynomial \(V_N=\operatorname{lcm}_i(X^{n_i}-1)\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) is monic, has degree \(d_N\), and is a product of distinct cyclotomic polynomials.
For any nonzero \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) the polynomial \(V_NQ\) vanishes on every grid and satisfies \(M(V_NQ)=M(Q)\).
If the allowed excess is unbounded along admissible \(N\), choose one with excess at least \(\deg Q\).
This realizes each original measure within the zero-sample class.
In particular a positive gap for that class with unbounded excess is exactly as strong as the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
