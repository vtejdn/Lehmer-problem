# Uniqueness above an arbitrary trace field and rigidity of the original trace field

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quadratic extension and a single line of norm-one units

Fix \(M>0\).
For any number field \(B\) of sufficiently large degree \(n\) in terms of \(M\), consider the nontorsion algebraic units \(x\) in a fixed algebraic closure satisfying
\[
    \mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})=B,\quad [\mathbb Q(x):B]=2,\quad
                                  0<m(x)\le M .                           \tag{1}
\]
The measure is total logarithmic Mahler measure.
If the set is nonempty, every such \(x\) generates the same quadratic extension \(K/B\), and all these units are multiplicatively dependent in pairs.
In the relative norm-one unit group of \(K/B\), let \(\epsilon\) lift a primitive generator of the rational line of any one of them modulo torsion, and let \(\mu_1\) be the torsion in this group.
The precise set in (1) is
\[
  \left\{\xi\epsilon^k:\ \xi\in\mu_1,\ k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\
       \mathbb Q(\xi\epsilon^k)=K,\quad 2n|k|h(\epsilon)\le M\right\},    \tag{2}
\]
where \(h\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
There is no condition on the signature of \(B\).

If a member of (1) generates \(K\) in every positive power, then every element \(\xi\epsilon^k\) in (2) before imposing the degree and measure conditions also generates \(K\) in every positive power.
In this case \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(B)\) is trivial.
In particular the unrestricted Lehmer infimum vanishes if and only if it vanishes on primitive reciprocal unit generators of full power degree whose original trace fields have trivial rational automorphism group.
Here primitive means primitive modulo torsion in the full unit group of the generated field.
The quadratic extension itself then has exactly two rational automorphisms for sufficiently large degrees under a fixed measure bound.

<a id="extensions"></a>
## Independence in different extensions of the same embedded base

For two units \(x,y\) satisfying the field conditions in (1), their two quadratic extensions have compositum of degree at most \(4n\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Whenever they are multiplicatively independent, the [rank-two height product](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
                          m(x)m(y)\gg n/(\log(12n))^{212} .
\]
The root degrees in both factors on the left are \(2n\).
Thus bounded measures rule out independence for sufficiently large \(n\).
If the quadratic extensions are different, their intersection is precisely \(B\).
Nonzero powers agreeing in the intersection would lie in \(B\); the nontrivial automorphism over \(B\) inverts the unit and hence that same power, which is impossible for a nontorsion unit.
This proves uniqueness and dependence, without needing a separate power-degree condition.

The unit group of relative norm one modulo its torsion is a lattice, by Dirichlet's unit theorem and the subgroup theorem for finitely generated abelian groups.
Its intersection with the rational line of a nonzero vector is generated by the primitive vector on that line.
The resulting representation and the degree-times-height formula give the necessity of all conditions in (2).
Conversely, if \(v=\xi\epsilon^k\) generates \(K\), its trace lies in \(B\) and \([K:\mathbb Q(v+v^{-1})]\le2\).
The two-to-one degree of \(K/B\) forces equality of the trace field with \(B\).
The measure cutoff in (2) then suffices.
If an initial all-power member is \(x=\omega\epsilon^s\) with \(s\ne0\), a power of each \((\xi\epsilon^k)^j\), \(j\ge1\), agrees with a nonzero signed power of \(x\) after killing the torsion, proving the asserted full degree.

<a id="automorphisms"></a>
## Lifting automorphisms and selecting the unrestricted sequence

Let \(g\) be a rational automorphism of the embedded base \(B\).
Extending \(g\) to the algebraic closure takes \(x\) to another member of (1), with the same measure and the same embedded trace field.
By uniqueness of the extension, the image of \(K\) is \(K\).
Hence restriction from the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(K)\) preserving \(B\) onto \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(B)\) is surjective.
For an all-power generator of bounded measure, [the automorphism theorem for its reciprocal field](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#subfields) gives only the identity and inversion.
Both fix \(B\) pointwise, proving the assertion.
This application concerns the field before adjoining cyclotomic constants; the automorphisms after that operation are described by the [twisted-field calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v42.md#fields).

Under the unrestricted zero hypothesis the [saturation and norm descent](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#selection) supply nontorsion reciprocal, primitive, all-power generators \(x\) of positive measures tending to zero and degrees tending to infinity.
Their trace fields have half their degrees, so the uniqueness and lifting conclusions apply eventually for any fixed positive measure bound.
Conversely, such units already belong to the unrestricted domain.

<a id="real"></a>
## Counting trace polynomials with any number of exceptional real embeddings

As a further consequence let \(B\) be totally real of sufficiently large degree \(n\) for fixed \(M>0\).
Among generators \(t\in\mathcal O_B\) consider those for which
\[
            0<H(t)=\sum_{\sigma:B\hookrightarrow\mathbb R}
                   \operatorname{arcosh}\big(\max(1,|\sigma(t)|/2)\big)
                         \le M .
\]
If the set is nonempty, there is \(t_0\) in it and the complete set is
\[
           \{\ \pm T_k(t_0):1\le k\le\lfloor M/H(t_0)\rfloor,\
                       k\text{ odd if }-t_0\text{ is conjugate to }t_0\ \},
\]
where \(T_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
All these traces have the same exceptional embeddings, at which \(|\sigma(t)|>2\).
The [single-place case](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#base) has no restriction on the parity.

Indeed any \(x\) with \(x+x^{-1}=t\) is a unit with \(B\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\).
If every \(|\sigma(t)|>2\), then \(x\) is totally real and \(h(x)=H(t)/(2n)\): the two inverse roots have equal absolute radial logarithms over each embedding, even if the quadratic splits over \(B\).
This contradicts the [absolute height bound for totally real elements](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) for sufficiently large \(n\).
Equality \(|\sigma(t)|=2\) cannot occur for a degree-\(n>1\) generator.
Thus there are both an exceptional and an interior embedding.
The interior embedding forces \([K:B]=2\), and counting both roots in each fiber gives \(m(x)=H(t)\).
The exceptional embedding supplies a real embedding of \(K\), so \(\mu_1=\{1,-1\}\).
Since a member \(x=\pm\epsilon^s\) generates \(K\), the primitive \(\epsilon\) of (2) also generates \(K\); its own trace \(t_0=\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1}\) generates \(B\) and \(H(t_0)\le M\).

Every exterior conjugate of \(\epsilon\) is real, since its trace lies in the totally real field \(B\).
If distinct conjugates have a root-of-unity quotient, use transitivity to send the numerator to an image of maximal modulus, greater than one.
The denominator then has the same modulus and must be its negative, so the torsion quotient was \(-1\).
Consequently all nonzero powers generate \(K\) unless \(-\epsilon\) is conjugate to \(\epsilon\), in which case exactly the odd powers do.
The latter condition is equivalent to \(-t_0\) being conjugate to \(t_0\), by extending an embedding and using both inverse roots in each fiber.
For any power the nontrivial inverter still restricts to inversion on its generated field, so the powered trace generates \(B\) exactly when the power generates \(K\).
This proves the counting formula and the identical exceptional sets.
In fact every automorphism of \(B\) sends \(t_0\) to \(\pm t_0\) by preservation of \(H(t_0)>0\); it is determined by that image.
When \(-t_0\) is conjugate to \(t_0\), the sign change does give a rational automorphism because the two elements generate the same field.
