# An all-conductor reduction at small primitive absolute mean

<a id="statement"></a>
## Central cyclotomic perturbations at the critical degree

Put \(b_0=\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}-1>1\).
Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal integer polynomial of even degree \(d=2m\ge2\).
At any \(n\ge3\) for which the [mean of \(|P|\) on primitive \(n\)-th roots](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#statement) is strictly below \(b_0\), there exists \(v\in\{-1,0,1\}\) such that
\[
                         \Phi_n(X)\mid P(X)-vX^m .
\]
Conversely this divisibility gives primitive absolute average \(|v|\).
In particular \(d\ge\varphi(n)\), and at \(d=\varphi(n)\) the only possibilities are
\[
                    P(X)=\Phi_n(X)+vX^{\varphi(n)/2},\qquad v=0,\pm1 .
\]
The positive Mahler spectra of this critical-degree class at all conductors and of the two central perturbations at odd squarefree conductors \(q\ge3\) coincide.
Here the positive spectrum comprises measures strictly greater than one.
Any sequence of these positive measures tending to one must involve unbounded numbers of distinct prime divisors in the odd squarefree conductors.
For general \(d\le\varphi(n)+H\), the classification allows the quotient \((P-vX^m)/\Phi_n\) to be an arbitrary compatible monic reciprocal polynomial of degree at most \(H\).

<a id="absolute"></a>
## A first absolute moment bound for totally real integers

Let \(\beta\) be any totally real algebraic integer other than \(0,1,-1\).
Take the uniform distribution on its conjugates and write \(t\) for the absolute value of a sampled conjugate, \(U=\mathbb Et\).
The nonzero integer norms of \(\beta\) and \(\beta^2-1\) give \(\mathbb E\sqrt t\ge1\) and
\[
           1\le\bigl(\mathbb E|t-1|\bigr)\bigl(\mathbb E(t+1)\bigr).
\]
Indeed the geometric mean of \(\sqrt t\) is at least one, and the product of the geometric means of \(|t-1|\) and \(t+1\) is at least one.
By Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(|\sqrt t-1|(\sqrt t+1)\),
\[
     (\mathbb E|t-1|)^2
        \le (U+1-2\mathbb E\sqrt t)(U+1+2\mathbb E\sqrt t)
        \le (U+1)^2-4 .
\]
Consequently \((U+1)^2((U+1)^2-4)\ge1\), which implies \(U\ge b_0\).
No optimality of this constant is needed.

By reciprocity the algebraic integer \(\beta=\zeta_n^{-m}P(\zeta_n)\) is real.
The same calculation at each primitive root shows that it is totally real, and the average of \(t\) is exactly the primitive average for \(P\), since each distinct conjugate of \(\beta\) occurs equally often.
Thus that average below \(b_0\) forces \(\beta=v\in\{0,1,-1\}\).
The defining minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_n\) gives the divisibility, and the converse follows by evaluation.
The numerator \(P-vX^m\) remains monic, reciprocal and of degree \(2m\).
Its quotient by the reciprocal \(\Phi_n\) is therefore also monic reciprocal with integer coefficients, and equals one at the critical degree.

<a id="conductors"></a>
## Which conductors can supply small positive measures

Let \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), \(s=n/q\).
The equality \(\Phi_n(X)=\Phi_q(X^s)\) follows because the \(s\)-th powers of the primitive \(n\)-th roots are the primitive \(q\)-th roots and each fiber has size \(s\).
If \(q\ge3\), then \(\varphi(q)\) is even and the central perturbation at \(n\) is exactly the substitution \(X\mapsto X^s\) into the one at \(q\).
This preserves the Mahler measure by integration on the circle.
If \(q=2\) and \(n\ge3\), \(n\) is a power of two and the perturbed polynomials have the form \(Y^2+vY+1\) at \(Y=X^{\varphi(n)/2}\).
All have measure one.
If \(q=2u\) for \(u\ge3\) odd and squarefree, \(\Phi_q(X)=\Phi_u(-X)\) and \(\varphi(q)=\varphi(u)\).
Rotation \(X\mapsto-X\) permutes the two central signs up to the sign of the central monomial and preserves the measure.
The \(v=0\) branch always has measure one, and each odd squarefree example is itself a critical-degree input, proving the asserted equality.

Finally at a squarefree conductor \(q\) having at most \(r\) primes, the Möbius identity \(\Phi_q(X)=\prod_{a\mid q}(X^a-1)^{\mu(q/a)}\) has at most \(2^r\) factors.
Multiplying either central perturbation by the denominator factors yields an integer polynomial with boundedly many terms and the same measure, exactly the [central-shift clearing argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0457_v2.md#bounded-prime-count-completion).
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes a shrinking positive sequence at bounded \(r\).
This reduction identifies the precise remaining families under the first-mean and critical-degree hypotheses.
