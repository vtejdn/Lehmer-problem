# Bounding modular returns by a short relation on a two-dimensional torus

<a id="torus"></a>
## Intersection length for a primitive specialization

Let \(a,b\) be coprime integers, \(j\ge1\), and \(p\) a prime.
Let \(F\) be a Laurent polynomial in two variables over \(\mathbb F_p\).
Consider any nonzero \((u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\) with \(au+bv\equiv0\bmod j\).
Put \(k=\gcd(|u|,|v|)\) and \((u_0,v_0)=(u/k,v/k)\).
Assume \(F\) is nonzero as a function on every curve \(U^{u_0}V^{v_0}=\xi\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) with \(\xi^k=1\).
Here nonzero means its restriction is not identically zero.
Then
\[
 \deg\gcd\!\left(F(X^a,X^b),X^j-1\right)
 \ \le\ k\,\left(\max_{(s,t)\in\operatorname{supp}F}(u_0t-v_0s)
             -\min_{(s,t)\in\operatorname{supp}F}(u_0t-v_0s)\right).
\]
Multiplying the specialization by a power of \(X\) to clear negative exponents has no effect on this gcd.

Work over the algebraic closure and write \(W=U^{u_0}V^{v_0}\).
Complete its exponent vector to an integral basis of determinant one, giving Laurent coordinates \(W,Z\).
The \(Z\)-exponent of \(U^sV^t\) is \(u_0t-v_0s\).
For every \(\xi^k=1\), the nonzero Laurent polynomial \(F(\xi,Z)\) has quotient-ring dimension at most the displayed width in \(Z\), by clearing its lowest exponent.
If \(\xi\) has multiplicity \(e\) in \(W^k-1\), filter the quotient by \((F,(W-\xi)^e)\) by the powers of \(W-\xi\).
Multiplication by \((W-\xi)^i\) makes its \(i\)-th successive quotient a quotient of the specialization modulo \(F(\xi,Z)\).
Thus the full quotient by \((F,W^k-1)\) has dimension at most \(k\) times the width, by the Chinese remainder theorem and \(\sum e=k\).
Finally \(U\mapsto X^a,\ V\mapsto X^b\) maps that ring onto the quotient of the Laurent polynomial ring in \(X\) by \((F(X^a,X^b),X^j-1)\).
Indeed the short relation makes \(W^k=1\) there, and \(X\) is generated by the two images and their inverses by coprimality.
The dimension of the target is the indicated gcd degree.
This proof counts repeated roots when \(p\mid j\).

## An upper bound at every prime for one fixed hyperbolic exterior

For the [closed fibers of the simplest hyperbolic braid](Yin_C_G0883_v5.md#hironaka), take the complete polynomials \(P_{a,b}\) at coprime \(|a|<b,\ b\ge6\).
For every prime and every \(j\ge1\),
\[
       \deg\gcd(\overline P_{a,b},X^j-1)\ \le\ 2\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor .
\]
Indeed the whole polynomial divides \(X^bF(X^a,X^b)\) over the integers with
\[
                 F(U,V)=U+U^{-1}+V+V^{-1}-1 .
\]
Reduction modulo any prime keeps all five monomials.
On \(W=\xi\) the four nonconstant terms have \(Z\)-exponents \(-v_0,v_0,u_0,-u_0\).
If \(|u_0|\ne|v_0|\), a term of extremal nonzero exponent occurs only once.
If \(|u_0|=|v_0|\), both are nonzero and the constant term is \(-1\).
Thus the required specialization never vanishes identically, even in characteristic two.
Take any nonzero relation and use the first section to get an upper bound of \(2\max(|u|,|v|)\).
Among the pairs in \(\{0,\dots,\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor\}^2\) two have the same value of \(au+bv\bmod j\).
Their difference proves the stated numerical bound.

The estimate is attained at \((a,b,p,j)=(1,6,2,31)\).
Here \(P_{1,6}=P_{\rm Lehmer}(-X)\), which modulo two equals the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial and divides \(X^{31}-1\).
The quotient is the sum of the monomials of exponents \(0,1,2,5,7,9,10,11,12,14,16,19,20,21\), by multiplication in \(\mathbb F_2[X]\).
Nevertheless along fibers of growing degree \(d\), for every fixed \(A>0\) the maximal returned fraction over all primes and \(j\le Ad\) is at most \(2\sqrt{A/d}\).
The realization volumes are bounded above by the volume of one exterior, and the degrees grow already for \(a=1,\ b\to\infty\).
Thus uniformly bounded realization volume for whole polynomials is consistent with vanishing short-return fractions at every rational prime.
The family has a five-term cyclotomic completion and supplies no sequence of shrinking strict records.
