# Degree at least 86 at measure 1.06 and bounds for three or more factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Individual degrees, degree sums and prime tests

Every nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(1<M(P)\le53/50\) has degree at least 86.
For an algebraic unit of measure in this interval every positive power generates a field of degree at least 86.
More generally, let \(r\ge3\) primitive irreducible integer polynomials have positive logarithmic measures with sum less than \(B=(\log2)/4\).
Their total degree \(D\) satisfies
\[
                 D\ge86r-48,\qquad
                 D>\frac r3\exp\!\left((2r/B)^{1/3}\right).
\]

For the signed [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), a reciprocal nontorsion unit \(c\) whose positive powers generate its field and its square cannot occur together on a passing row at any prime \(p\le353\).
At \(359\) such a pair requires degree 86.
Three distinct qualifying values are impossible at every prime through 443.
At 449 the only possible degrees, in increasing order of measure, are \(86,68,56\).
The three-value assertions also apply in the [original count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement), which uses the same multiplicity at these primes.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Nine reciprocal degrees and descent to power fields

The [degree bound at 1.091](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree) already excludes every degree below 68.
By multiplicativity any polynomial in the stated measure interval must have an irreducible factor in it.
The same small-measure reduction there makes that factor monic up to sign, reciprocal and of even degree.
A nontrivial power substitution in degrees 68 through 84 would preserve its positive measure in degree at most 42, already excluded.
The [certified Newton bounds](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v11/readme.md#boxes) and the [complete enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v11/readme.md#enumeration) cover the remaining reciprocal polynomials up to \(X\mapsto-X\).
The nine searches visit 2,302,738,123 representatives, and each of the 46,846 final candidates has an exact cyclotomic divisor. [Degree counts](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v11/readme.md#census)
An irreducible with such a divisor has measure one.
The [verification](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v11/readme.md#verification) checks the disjoint slice counts and compares all filter stages on selected prefix subtrees to independent integer calculations.

For \(L=\mathbb Q(c^k)\), the unit \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(c)/L}(c)\) has \(1<M(u)\le M(c)\) by the [relative norm calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree).
Its degree is at most \([L:\mathbb Q]\), giving the asserted bound on that field.

<a id="sums"></a>
## Sorting the measures in a fixed total budget

Write \(h_1\le\cdots\le h_r\) for the logarithmic measures and \(d_j\) for the corresponding degrees.
Each is the measure of a nontorsion unit of degree at least two, since the individual measures are positive and below \(\log 2\).
The inequalities \(h_{r-2}<B/3\) and \(h_{r-1}<B/2\) follow by summing the three and the two greatest measures, respectively.
Since \(53^{12}>2\cdot50^{12}\) and \(1091^8>2\cdot1000^8\), the first \(r-2\) degrees are at least 86 and the next is at least 68.

Suppose \(D\le86r-48\).
By [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) each of the first \(r-1\) logarithmic measures exceeds \(2/\log^3(258r)\), since \(3d_j<258r\).
The function \((x-1)/\log^3(258x)\) increases for \(x\ge3\), by differentiation and \(\log(258x)>3\).
Consequently
\[
   h_r < B-\frac{2(r-1)}{\log^3(258r)}
       \le B-\frac4{\log^3(774)}< B-\frac4{343}<\log\rho ,
\]
where \(\rho\) is Lehmer's measure.
Here \(e^7>(8/3)^7>774\), and the last inequality follows from the [verified bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite) \(\log2<0.69315,\ \log\rho>0.162356\).
The [Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives \(d_r\ge56\).
Thus \(D\ge86(r-2)+68+56=86r-48\), also when the initial supposition fails.
Equality forces every individual lower bound in this calculation to be attained.
For the second inequality the function \(x\mapsto2/\log^3(3x)\) is decreasing and strictly convex for \(x\ge2\), by differentiating twice.
Jensen's inequality and Voutier's bound give \(B>\sum_j h_j>2r/\log^3(3D/r)\), which rearranges as stated.

<a id="primes"></a>
## The next primes for the prescribed pair and for three values

The [previous prime calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#primes) excludes the unit and its square through 271.
At all subsequent primes under consideration the [half-prime width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#widths) forces \(M(c)<2^{1/12}\) and \(2d\le b\), where \(d=[\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]\).
The first inequality gives \(d\ge86\).
Since \(353^7<171^8\), the width through 353 is at most 171.
The next prime is 359, with \(172^8<359^7<173^8\), so \(2d\le173\) forces \(d=86\).

For three distinct values choose three distinct qualifying minima.
Their measure sum is below \(B\) by the [derivative product](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product).
The [two-value exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#statement) rules out three values through 241.
At 251 the same result allows only smaller-measure degrees 68 or 70 paired with companion degree 56 or 58.
Applying it to the pairs with measure indices \((1,2)\) and \((2,3)\) would give contradictory ranges for the middle degree.
At all further primes (2) of the [prime-width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement) gives \(D\le b\).
The degree-sum bound at \(r=3\) gives \(D\ge210\).
Since \(443^7<209^8\), the width is at most 209 through 443.
The next prime 449 satisfies \(209^8<449^7<210^8\), so \(b=210\) and equality is necessary in the degree-sum bound.
This gives the three specified degrees.
Since \(449<3^8\), the fixed and varying orders agree throughout these finite ranges.

The coordinating agent and computation contributors for Sheng Yin obtained these bounds while testing the unresolved small-measure cases for the [unrestricted Lehmer problem](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
