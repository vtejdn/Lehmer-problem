# Uniform specialization of a quadratic trace polynomial with a varying middle block

<a id="statement"></a>
## The specialization error and a positive measure gap

Let \(J(e^{it})\) be a real trigonometric polynomial of degree \(s\), let \(n>s\ge1\) be integers, and write \(u=s/n\).
The coefficients may be complex with the conjugate symmetry required for real values on the circle.
For every real \(\phi\) put
\[
   A_\phi(t)=2\cos(nt+\phi)+J(e^{it}),\qquad
   m_\phi=\int\log|A_\phi(t)|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi},\qquad
   m_* = m_2(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X)).
\]
Let \(k\) be the number of nonzero coefficients of \(A_\phi\) as a Laurent polynomial in \(e^{it}\), counting both outer terms.
With absolute constants,
\[
   -Cku\log(2k/u)\ \le\ m_\phi-m_*
                         \ \le\ Cu\log(2/u).                          \tag{1}
\]
Both bounds are uniform in the coefficients, the phase and all root multiplicities.

For an integral symmetric \(J\) and \(\phi=0\), write \(P(z)=z^{2n}+z^n J(z)+1\).
There are absolute \(c_0,c_1>0\) such that for every such \(P\) that has a noncyclotomic factor,
\[
          \frac{ks}{n}\log(2kn/s)\le c_0
                 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log M(P)\ge c_1.         \tag{2}
\]
In particular along every sequence in this class with \(L=\log M(P)\to0\), eventually \(ks\log(2n)\gg n\) and \(s\gg\sqrt{n/\log(2n)}\).
No separability hypothesis is required.
The one-sided comparison also bounds all phase specializations from above as \(s/n\to0\), regardless of the support size.

<a id="modes"></a>
## Uniform bounded variation of the vertical Fourier coefficients

Write \(a(t)=-J(e^{it})/2\).
For a real \(a\) let \(y_\pm\) be the roots of \(Y^2-2aY+1\).
Put \(g(a)=\log\max(|y_+|,|y_-|)\) and replace each exterior root by the reciprocal of its conjugate, leaving the other roots fixed.
Denote the two resulting disk points by \(w_\pm(a)\).
Conjugate symmetry makes their \(j\)-th mean \(c_j(a)=(w_+^j+w_-^j)/2\) real.
The formula is
\[
   c_j(a)=\begin{cases}
       \cos(j\arccos a),& |a|\le1,\\
       (\operatorname{sgn}a)^j e^{-j\operatorname{arcosh}|a|},& |a|>1 .
   \end{cases}
\]
It is continuous and has total variation at most \(2j+2\) on the full real line.
There are at most \(2s\) critical points on a period of the nonconstant real trigonometric polynomial \(a(t)\).
Consequently \(c_j(a(t))\) has variation at most \(C s j\) on a period, independent of coefficient size.
Integration by parts against the exponential of frequency \(jn\) thus gives
\[
       \left|\int c_j(a(t))e^{ijnt}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right|\le C s/n .
\]
On the circle \(v=e^{i(nt+\phi)}\) the exact factorization in modulus is
\[
         |A_\phi(t)|=e^{g(a(t))}\prod_{\pm}|1-\bar w_\pm(a(t))v|.
\]
Jensen's formula in the vertical variable gives \(m_*=\int g(a(t))\,dt/(2\pi)\).
Replacing \(v\) on the right of the modulus identity by \(r v\), \(0<r<1\), defines a regularized mean \(m_{\phi,r}\) by integrating the logarithm of that expression.
The logarithmic Taylor series converges absolutely and uniformly for fixed \(r\), hence
\[
                     |m_{\phi,r}-m_*|
                             \le C u\log(1/(1-r)).                    \tag{3}
\]

<a id="small"></a>
## The removed logarithmic singularities

Set \(\epsilon=1-r\le1/2\) and \(D=|1-\bar w_\pm v|\) for either factor, with any measurable ordering of the two disk points.
For \(|z|\le1\), the identity
\(|1-rz|^2-r|1-z|^2=(1-r)(1-r|z|^2)\ge0\) shows \(m_\phi-m_{\phi,r}\le-\log r\ll\epsilon\).
Combining with (3) and taking \(\epsilon=u/2\) proves the upper side of (1).

For the reverse bound the regularized logarithm of one factor exceeds the original by at most \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\).
If \(D<v_0\le1/4\), then \(|w_\pm|>1-v_0\).
Inside the elliptic interval the two moduli equal one, and outside it both equal \(e^{-g}\), so always \(e^g\le(1-v_0)^{-1}\) on this set.
The other factor is at most two and the exact identity yields \(|A_\phi|\le4v_0\).
The sup norm of \(A_\phi\) is at least one by its outer Fourier coefficient.
Apply the [measurable-set Turán--Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) to its \(k\) pure imaginary frequencies on the full period and the measurable subset where \(|A_\phi|\le4v_0\).
For normalized arc measure this gives
\[
                        \Pr(D<v_0)\le C v_0^{1/(k-1)} .
\]
The constant here is absolute, since the absolute base constant in that inequality is raised to \(k-1\) before taking the root.
For \(h=1/(k-1)\le1/2\), the layer integral of \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\) up to \(v_0=1/4\) is bounded by
\[
 C\int_0^{1/4}\frac{\epsilon v^{h-1}}{v+\epsilon}\,dv
      \le C\epsilon^h\left(\frac1h+\frac1{1-h}\right).
\]
The remaining layer integral is \(O(\epsilon)\), so both factors together cost at most \(C k\epsilon^{1/(k-1)}\).
This also proves logarithmic integrability directly at possible multiple zeros.
Use \(\epsilon=(u/(2k))^{k-1}\) in (3) to obtain the lower side of (1).

<a id="gap"></a>
## Consequence for small positive one-variable measures

If \(P\) contains a noncyclotomic factor, then \(m_*>0\).
Indeed zero would force \(|J|\le2\) on the entire circle.
The [integral classification](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#quadratic) uses only the trigonometric norm in this step: each nonconstant possibility is \(J=\pm(z^s+z^{-s})\), giving a product of binomials for \(P\).
By the [uniform torsion-fiber gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement), \(m_*\) is then bounded below by an absolute positive constant.
The lower side of (1) proves (2) after choosing \(c_0\) sufficiently small.
Finally \(k\le 2s+3\) and \(\log(2kn/s)\ll\log(2n)\), which give the stated consequences for growing spans.
