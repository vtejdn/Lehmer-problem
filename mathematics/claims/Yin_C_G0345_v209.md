# Cubic growth of exceptional cyclotomic orders at minimum length six

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound for orders without small prime divisors

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)\ge x>0\).
Let \(\ell\) be the least sum of absolute coefficients of a nonzero integral multiple of \(P\).
For an integer \(L\ge\ell\), set \(K=\prod_{p\le L,\ p\ {\rm prime}}p\) and
\[
  R_n(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_n,P)|^{1/\varphi(n)},\qquad
  T=\operatorname{lcm}\{n\ge1:(n,K)=1,\ R_n(P)>L\},
\]
with empty least common multiple one.
If the set is nonempty then \(\ell=2s\) is even.
For any shortest multiple \(F\) normalized to have nonzero constant term, its multiplicity \(j\) at one satisfies \(1\le j\le s\), and
\[
         T\le {s\choose j}(\deg F)^j,\qquad
         \deg F\le(\ell-1)d\log(\ell)/x .
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular the exponent of \(d\) is at most \(\lfloor L/2\rfloor\).

For \(L=6\) the exponent three is sharp.
There are nonreciprocal irreducible unit polynomials \(H\) of unbounded degree \(d\) and exact minimum length six with
\[
             \log\theta\le m(H)\le\tfrac12\log 6,
             \qquad T\asymp d^3 ,
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
where \(\theta>1\) solves \(X^3-X-1=0\).
In fact three pairwise coprime orders \(a,b,c\), each relatively prime to \(30\) and comparable to \(d\), have normalized resultants with a common limit at least \(13\).
The examples have measure bounded away from zero and fixed minimum length.

<a id="bound"></a>
## Congruences between exponents of opposite signs in the relation

For every order in the defining set for \(T\), the [norm and root-of-unity block argument](Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#orders) gives \(F(\zeta_n)=0\) and \(n\mid K(u-v)\) for any two exponents in one minimal vanishing block.
As \((n,K)=1\), all exponents in each block are equal modulo \(n\).
Its signed coefficients therefore sum to zero, and the same holds in every residue class.
In particular \(F(1)=0\).
Split the coefficients into signed unit terms, counted with repetitions, and denote the exponents of the positive terms by \(u_i\) and those of the negative terms by \(v_i\).
There are equally many of each sign, say \(s\).
For each exceptional order the multisets \(\{u_i\bmod n\}\) and \(\{v_i\bmod n\}\) coincide.
Consequently \(n\) divides every coefficient of
\[
                \prod_{i=1}^s(Y-u_i)-\prod_{i=1}^s(Y-v_i).
\]
This polynomial is nonzero because the integral multisets differ.
Let \(j\) be the first index at which the two elementary symmetric functions differ, counting degree one first.
Newton's identities make \(j\) the first positive index at which the sums of powers differ, and hence also the first index at which the sums of falling factorials differ.
The latter difference is \(F^{(j)}(1)\), proving the multiplicity assertion.
Each of the two elementary symmetric functions of degree \(j\) lies between zero and \({s\choose j}(\deg F)^j\).
The absolute value of their nonzero difference bounds the least common multiple and proves (1) by the [consecutive-gap estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v189.md#upper).

<a id="alternant"></a>
## A six-term polynomial and its values on three grids

Fix \(k=10,\ l=23,\ h=60\cdot13\cdot23\).
For positive integers \(t\) put
\[
         a=ht-1,\qquad b=ha+1,\qquad c=(b-a)/2 .
\]
These integers are pairwise coprime because \(a,b\) are coprime and odd and \(2c=b-a\).
They are each coprime to \(2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot13\cdot23\), since \(a,b,c\) are respectively \(-1,1,1\) modulo \(h/2\).
Define
\[
 \begin{split}
  B(U,V)&=(U^k-1)(V^l-1)-(U^l-1)(V^k-1),\\
  G(U,V)&=\frac{B(U,V)}{(U-1)(V-1)(V-U)},\\
  S(X)&=X^{-ka}B(X^a,X^b),\qquad
  Q(X)=X^{-(k-1)a}G(X^a,X^b).
 \end{split}
\]
The ratio of the alternating determinant with rows of powers \(0,k,l\) in three variables to the Vandermonde determinant is a symmetric homogeneous integer polynomial of degree \(k+l-3\).
Indeed each pairwise difference divides the alternating numerator, their product divides it in the integer polynomial ring, and the two determinants have the same sign change under transpositions.
Setting the first variable equal to one gives \(G(U,V)\).
The six support exponents of \(B(X^a,X^b)\) are
\[
                  ka,\ la,\ kb,\ la+kb,\ lb,\ ka+lb .
\]
The smallest exponent is \(ka\) and the largest is \(ka+lb\), both with unit coefficients.
Removing the common power gives the monic unit polynomial \(S\) of degree \(lb\), and
\[
                  S=(X^a-1)(X^b-1)(X^{b-a}-1)Q .
\]
Thus \(Q\) is monic of degree \((l-2)b\), with constant term of absolute value one.

On the diagonal sections the symmetric homogeneous formula gives
\[
  G(1,V)=D(V)=\frac{k(V^l-1)-l(V^k-1)}{(V-1)^2},\quad
  G(U,1)=D(U),\quad G(U,U)=U^{k+l-3}D(U^{-1}).
\]
The first formula follows by differentiating the numerator once in \(U\) at one.
The constant term of \(D\) is \(l-k=13\), so \(m(D)\ge\log13\) by Jensen's formula.
If a unit complex number \(v\ne1\) were a zero then \(kv^l+(l-k)=lv^k\).
Equality in the triangle inequality forces \(v^l=v^k=1\), impossible since \((k,l)=1\).
At one, \(D(1)=kl(l-k)/2>0\).
Thus \(\log|D|\) is continuous on the unit circle.

At primitive orders \(a,b,c\) respectively, the pairs \((U,V)=(X^a,X^b)\) have \(U=1,\ V=1,\ U=V\).
In each case the remaining parameter ranges over all primitive roots of that order, by coprimality.
Primitive roots of order \(n\) equidistribute as \(n\to\infty\).
Indeed their sum at a fixed nonzero frequency \(r\), before division by \(\varphi(n)\), is \(\sum_{e\mid (n,r)}e\mu(n/e)\) by inclusion-exclusion, bounded solely in terms of \(r\).
Trigonometric polynomial approximation therefore gives the assertion for every continuous circle function.
It follows that all three normalized resultants of \(Q\) tend to \(e^{m(D)}\).

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## Reciprocal pairs after the three binomials have been removed

Suppose \(z,z^{-1}\) both vanish in \(Q\), put \(U=z^a,V=z^b\), and use the triples
\[
                       x=(1,U^k,V^k),\qquad y=(1,U^l,V^l).
\]
The determinants for \(B(U,V)\) and \(B(U^{-1},V^{-1})\) vanish.
If the entries of either triple are all equal then \(z^k=1\) or \(z^l=1\), using \((a,b)=1\).
Otherwise, vanishing of the first determinant gives \(y_i=A x_i+B_0\) with \(A\ne0\), and the other gives \(y_i^{-1}=A'x_i^{-1}+B'_0\) with \(A'\ne0\).
If two \(x_i\)'s agree the corresponding two \(y_i\)'s agree, so \((k,l)=1\) gives \(U=1,\ V=1\) or \(U=V\).
Each condition makes \(z\) a root of unity because \(a,b,b-a\) are positive.
The diagonal formulas and the absence of circle zeros of \(D\) exclude all three possibilities.
If all \(x_i\)'s are distinct, multiplying the two affine equations and clearing \(x_i\) gives a quadratic polynomial vanishing at three different points.
Its leading and constant coefficients \(AB'_0,B_0A'\) vanish, so \(B_0=B'_0=0\).
The first entries give \(A=1\), hence \(U^{l-k}=V^{l-k}=1\) and \(z^{l-k}=1\).

Every reciprocal factor of \(Q\) therefore has its roots among the roots of \(X^{10}-1,\ X^{23}-1,\ X^{13}-1\).
The multiplicity of any nonzero root of \(S\) is at most five, by the nonsingular Vandermonde system obtained by applying the first six powers of \(X\,d/dX\) at its six distinct exponents.
These facts bound the total cyclotomic degree of \(Q\) independently of \(t\).
The root \(1\) is absent because \(D(1)>0\).

<a id="factor"></a>
## Uniqueness of the nonreciprocal irreducible factor

We use the [factor-reversal argument](Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#example) with the actual support of \(S\).
In the unshifted six-point support the consecutive pairs near \(0,kb,lb\) have widths \(13a,23a,10a\), respectively.
They give three distinct small positive distances.
Each pair of these clusters gives four more distances in one of the intervals \([mb-23a,mb+23a]\), \(m=10,13,23\).
The four are distinct because the two participating widths are positive and unequal.
The intervals and the small distances are disjoint since \(b>120a\).
Thus all fifteen positive differences are distinct, and the coefficient autocorrelation at each of them is \(1\) or \(-1\).

For an integral factorization \(S=AB\), write \(A^*(X)=X^{\deg A}A(1/X)\) and \(W=A^*B\).
Then \(WW^*=SS^*\), so \(W\) has squared coefficient sum six, and at least fifteen positive support differences.
Hence it has six signed unit coefficients with exactly the same fifteen distances.
In any six-point support with these distances, distance at most \(23a\) defines an equivalence relation since no distance lies in \((23a,46a]\).
Four classes would require a span of at least \(3(10b-23a)>lb\), which exceeds the span of \(W\).
If the class sizes are \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) with \(r\le3\), they satisfy \(\sum u_i=6\) and \(\sum \binom{u_i}{2}=3\).
Thus \(\sum u_i^2=12\), and Cauchy–Schwarz forces \(r=3\) and all sizes equal to two.
The four distances between any two classes vary by at most \(46a\), so belong to one of the three large intervals, whose successive gaps exceed \(46a\).
Each interval thus comes from one pair of classes.
The distance between the two outer classes includes the span \(lb\), placing them in the interval for \(23b\).
Reverse the support if necessary so that the first two classes give the interval for \(10b\).
The range of the four distances in each interval is the sum of the two participating widths.
These three pairwise sums determine each individual width.
Starting with the leftmost point at zero, the least distance in each adjacent interval then determines the next left endpoint.
The support of \(W\) is consequently the support of \(S\) or its reversal, since the endpoints of the factors of \(S\) are units.
The autocorrelations at distances from zero recover the signs up to an overall sign by uniqueness of each pair.
Thus \(W=\pm S\) or \(W=\pm S^*\), and cancellation gives \(A^*=\pm A\) or \(B^*=\pm B\).

For large \(t\), \(Q\) has at least one noncyclotomic irreducible factor because its degree tends to infinity and its cyclotomic degree is bounded.
Choose one such monic factor \(H\).
It cannot be reciprocal by the reciprocal-pair argument.
In the factorization \(S=H(S/H)\) the other side must be reciprocal.
If that side contained a nontorsion root, its inverse would also be a root of that side, and both would vanish in \(Q\) because the three removed binomials have only torsion zeros.
This contradicts the same reciprocal-pair argument.
Thus \(Q=HC\) with \(C\) monic cyclotomic of bounded degree and \(d=\deg H=(l-2)b+O(1)\).

<a id="length"></a>
## Resultants and exact minimum length of the surviving unit

Every irreducible factor \(\Phi_m\) of \(C\) has \(m>1\) dividing \(10,23\) or \(13\).
For \(n=a,b,c\) we have \(n>1,\ (m,n)=1\), and \(|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_m,\Phi_n)|=1\).
Indeed in \(\Phi_n(Z)=\prod_{e\mid n}(Z^e-1)^{\mu(n/e)}\) none of the factors vanishes at primitive \(m\)-th roots, and exponentiation by each \(e\) permutes those roots.
Their absolute product is therefore raised to the exponent \(\sum_{e\mid n}\mu(n/e)=0\).
It follows that \(R_n(H)=R_n(Q)\) for all three orders.
They all exceed six for sufficiently large \(t\), and their least common multiple \(abc\) is comparable to \(d^3\).

The [nonreciprocal bound of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives \(m(H)\ge\log\theta\).
Since all other factors of \(S\) are cyclotomic, \(m(H)=m(S)\le\tfrac12\log6\) by Jensen's inequality and the squared coefficient sum six.
The multiple \(S\) gives minimum length at most six.
If infinitely many \(H\) had minimum length at most five, (1) with \(L=6\) and \(x=\log\theta\) would force even length at most four and \(T=O(d^2)\) on that subsequence.
This contradicts \(T\ge abc\).
For the resulting exact length six, (1) also gives the upper bound in (2).
