# Integral numerator bounds from the poles of a hyperbolic polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## The energy bound and a necessary condition for small Mahler measure

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit, and assume that \(P\) has no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\sum_{|\rho|>1}\log|\rho|>0\), summing over the roots of \(P\), and set
\[
 b_\rho=\frac{1}{|P'(\rho)|\sqrt{|1-|\rho|^2|}},\qquad
 \mathcal E(P)=\inf_{\substack{A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\\P\nmid A}}
                  \int_{\mathbb T}|A/P|^2 .
\]
The circle integral uses Haar probability measure, and divisibility is in the integral Laurent polynomial ring.
For every \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(q\ge0\) with \(P\nmid Q\), one has
\[
 \mathcal E(P)\ \le\ \frac q4+
       \left(\sum_{|\rho|<1}b_\rho|Q(\rho)|\right)^2+
       \left(\sum_{|\rho|>1}b_\rho|\rho|^{-q}|Q(\rho)|\right)^2 .       \tag{1}
\]
An explicit integral numerator attains energy at most the right side.
For any sequence of such polynomials \(P_n\) with \(m_n=m(P_n)\to0\),
\[
             \sqrt{m_n}\sum_{P_n(\rho)=0}
             \frac{1}{|P_n'(\rho)|\sqrt{|1-|\rho|^2|}}
                    \ \longrightarrow\ +\infty .                     \tag{2}
\]
Consequently a common upper bound on the displayed quantity implies a positive infimum of \(m\) on any nonempty family satisfying that bound.
The conclusion concerns hyperbolic algebraic units and leaves the unrestricted Mahler gap open.

<a id="tails"></a>
## Centering the coefficients between the two tails

The roots of \(P\) are simple, so \(1/P(z)=\sum_\rho 1/[P'(\rho)(z-\rho)]\).
For \(|z|=1\), an interior pole contributes the terms \(\rho^k z^{-k-1}/P'(\rho)\), \(k\ge0\), and an exterior pole contributes \(-\rho^{-k-1}z^k/P'(\rho)\), \(k\ge0\).
Both series converge absolutely for the fixed \(P\).
Write \(t_j\) for the Laurent coefficients of \(Q/P\), which are real because \(P,Q\) are real and complex roots are paired by conjugation.
Convolving the expansion with \(Q\) gives two tails indexed by \(k\ge0\),
\[
 t_{-k-1}=\sum_{|\rho|<1}\frac{Q(\rho)\rho^k}{P'(\rho)},\qquad
 t_{q+k}=-\sum_{|\rho|>1}\frac{Q(\rho)\rho^{-q-1}(\rho^{-1})^k}{P'(\rho)} .
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
Choose a nearest integer \(e_j\) to \(t_j\) for \(0\le j<q\), with either choice allowed at a tie, and put \(B=\sum_{0\le j<q}e_jX^j\).
Then \(A=Q-PB\) is integral and \(P\nmid A\) since \(P\nmid Q\).
The quotient \(A/P\) has the two tails (3) and its \(q\) intervening coefficients have combined squared norm at most \(q/4\).
Each geometric series \((z^k)_{k\ge0}\) with \(|z|<1\) has norm \((1-|z|^2)^{-1/2}\).
Thus the triangle inequality on each tail and Parseval give (1), including \(q=0\) with \(B=0\).

<a id="kernels"></a>
## The angular dependence of the two tail norms

Write \(r_\rho=|\rho|\), \(u_\rho=\rho/r_\rho\), and \(s_\rho=\min(r_\rho,r_\rho^{-1})\).
Use \(\lambda_\rho=\rho=s_\rho u_\rho\) on the interior and \(\lambda_\rho=\rho^{-1}=s_\rho\overline{u_\rho}\) on the exterior.
The unit vector \(v(\lambda)=(\sqrt{1-|\lambda|^2}\,\lambda^k)_{k\ge0}\) has the inner product, taken linear in the first variable,
\[
 \langle v(\lambda_\rho),v(\lambda_\sigma)\rangle
 =\frac{\sqrt{(1-s_\rho^2)(1-s_\sigma^2)}}{1-\lambda_\rho\overline{\lambda_\sigma}} .
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
For two roots on the same side of the circle, the absolute value of (4) is
\[
 D_{\rho\sigma}=
 \frac{\sqrt{(1-s_\rho^2)(1-s_\sigma^2)}}
 {|1-s_\rho s_\sigma u_\rho\overline{u_\sigma}|}\le1 .
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
Complex conjugation of the whole denominator on the exterior preserves its absolute value.
Take \(Q=X^q-1\) for an integer \(q\ge1\).
Every such \(Q\) is admissible since all its roots lie on the unit circle.
In (3), the absolute value of the coefficient multiplying \(v(\lambda_\rho)\) is \(b_\rho f_{\rho,q}\) on both sides, where
\[
                     f_{\rho,q}=|1-s_\rho^q u_\rho^q|.
\]
Indeed \(|Q(\rho)|=f_{\rho,q}\) on the interior and \(r_\rho^{-q}|Q(\rho)|=f_{\rho,q}\) on the exterior.
For each side \(I=\{\rho:r_\rho<1\}\) or \(O=\{\rho:r_\rho>1\}\), squaring the corresponding tail norm in (3) and bounding its cross terms by (5) gives
\[
       m\mathcal E(P)\le\frac{mq}{4}
       +\sum_{J\in\{I,O\}}\sum_{\rho,\sigma\in J}
         (\sqrt m\,b_\rho)(\sqrt m\,b_\sigma)
                  f_{\rho,q}f_{\sigma,q}D_{\rho\sigma}.              \tag{6}
\]

<a id="divergence"></a>
## Concentration on common angles and simultaneous powers

Suppose (2) fails, and pass to a subsequence on which \(\sqrt{m_n}\sum_\rho b_\rho\) is bounded.
On each side \(J\), form the finite positive phase measure \(\mu_{n,J}=\sum_{\rho\in J}\sqrt{m_n}\,b_\rho\,\delta_{u_\rho}\).
The circle is compact and the masses are bounded, so after a common further subsequence both measures converge weakly to finite positive measures \(\nu_J\).
Since the constant coefficient of \(P_n\) has absolute value one, \(\prod_\rho r_\rho=1\) and hence \(\sum_{r_\rho<1}-\log r_\rho=m_n\).
In particular \(e^{-m_n}\le s_\rho<1\) on both sides.
For fixed \(q\), the difference between \(f_{\rho,q}\) and \(F_q(u_\rho)=|1-u_\rho^q|\) is therefore at most \(1-e^{-qm_n}\), uniformly in \(\rho\).
All these factors are bounded by two.

At any fixed chordal separation \(|u_\rho-u_\sigma|\ge\eta>0\), (5) tends uniformly to zero.
Indeed its numerator is at most \(1-e^{-2m_n}\), and its denominator is at least \(\sqrt{s_\rho s_\sigma}|u_\rho-u_\sigma|\), by squaring.
To bound the double sum for one side in (6), take a continuous cutoff supported on \(|u-v|\le2\eta\), equal to one on \(|u-v|\le\eta\), with values in \([0,1]\).
Outside the region where the cutoff is one, the uniform kernel bound and bounded masses make the omitted contribution tend to zero.
On its support use \(D_{\rho\sigma}\le1\), replace the two phase factors by \(F_q\) with vanishing error, and take the limit using \(\mu_{n,J}\otimes\mu_{n,J}\to\nu_J\otimes\nu_J\) weakly.
This product convergence follows by uniform approximation of continuous functions on \(\mathbb T^2\) by sums of products of continuous functions in the two variables.
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\), bounded convergence leaves the diagonal integral \(\sum_{u\in\mathbb T}F_q(u)^2\nu_J(\{u\})^2\), supported on the atoms.

The [integral quotient lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) gives \(\liminf_n m_n\mathcal E(P_n)\ge1/(2\mathrm e)\).
Consequently (6) forces, for every fixed positive integer \(q\),
\[
             \frac1{2\mathrm e}\le
             \sum_{J\in\{I,O\}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb T}
                        |1-u^q|^2\nu_J(\{u\})^2 .                    \tag{7}
\]
The two sums of squared atom masses are finite since each measure has finite mass.
Choose a finite set of their atoms leaving an arbitrarily small sum of squares outside it.
For any integer \(H\ge2\) and any \(h\ge1\) selected angles, partition \([0,1)^h\) into \(H^h\) equal boxes and consider the \(H^h+1\) simultaneous multiples of the angles divided by \(2\pi\), starting with zero and taking all coordinates modulo one.
The difference \(q\ge1\) of two indices in a common box then satisfies \(|1-u^q|\le2\pi/H\) for all selected phases \(u\).
If the selected set is empty, use \(q=1\).
Since \(|1-u^q|\le2\) elsewhere, the right side of (7) can be made arbitrarily small by choosing first the finite set and then \(H\).
The contradiction proves (2).
