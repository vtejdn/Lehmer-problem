# Which Salem numbers occur at delay one with a linear seed?

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two signs and the dyadic criterion

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2u\ge4\), let \(S_\alpha\) be its monic minimal polynomial, and put \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) and \(E=\mathbb Q(t)\).
Mark the embedding of this totally real field at which \(t>2\).
Write \(t_i\in(-2,2)\) for the other values of \(t\).
In the [relative construction](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#statement), take a linear seed \(X-a\) and delay one.
Its coefficient field \(K\) is quadratic over a totally real field, split at the marked place and complex at every other place, with at least one such place.
Write \(a'=\tau a\) for the nontrivial relative conjugate.
The seed has \(a\) integral and \(a>1\) at the marked embedding, with strict circle domination \(|z-a|>|1-a'z|\) there and \(|\iota a|\le1\) at each complex embedding.
Call the seed strict if all the last inequalities are strict.
Call it a unit seed if \(a\in\mathcal O_K^\times\).
The delayed polynomials and their positive exterior roots are
\[
 f_+(X)=X^2-(a+a')X+1,\quad f_-(X)=X^2-(a-a')X-1,\qquad R_+,R_->1 .
\]
For every such seed \(R_+\) and \(R_-^2\) are Salem numbers and their trace fields both equal the totally real base of the seed.

The plus sign attains \(R_+=\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\) with a strict integral seed, and its rational polynomial norm always equals \(S_\alpha(X)^2\).
The minus sign attains \(R_-^2=\alpha\) with an integral seed if and only if
\[
                   t-2\text{ is a square in }\mathcal O_E/4\mathcal O_E.       \tag{1}
\]
When (1) holds, infinitely many strict integral seeds attain the target with both marked real values positive.
The negative rational norm is always \(S_\alpha(X^2)\).
It is irreducible of degree \(4u\) precisely when \(t+2\) is nonsquare in \(E\).
Otherwise \(r=\sqrt\alpha\) is Salem and the two distinct factors are \(S_r(X)\) and \(S_r(-X)\).
Thus every Salem number occurs as \(R_-\) with a strict integral seed on this split branch, by taking its square as the target.
Lehmer's Salem number itself fails (1).
For either sign and a given target the unit seeds are specified by finite searches in \(\mathcal O_E\) below.
Over every totally real \(E\) of degree at least two with any given marked place, infinitely many distinct Salem targets have irreducible negative norm and strict unit seeds with \(a>1>a'>0\).

<a id="minus"></a>
## Integral lifts of a prescribed negative target

At the marked embedding and \(|z|=1\) the squared boundary difference is \((a-a')(a+a'-2\operatorname{Re} z)\).
Given \(a>1\), strict circle domination implies \(a>a'\), since \(a'\ge a\) would make the difference nonpositive at \(z=1\).
It then amounts to \(c=a+a'>2\).
Conversely \(a>a'\) and \(c>2\) give \(a>1\) and strict domination.
At every nonsplit place write \(\gamma=\iota a\), so \(\iota a'=\bar\gamma\).
The trace of \(R_+\) is \(c\), and the trace \(R_-^2+R_-^{-2}\) equals \(2+(a-a')^2\).
Both are integral and exceed two at the mark, while their other values \(2\operatorname{Re}\gamma\) and \(2-4(\operatorname{Im}\gamma)^2\) lie in \([-2,2]\).
Neither endpoint can be a conjugate of a number exceeding two, so these values lie in \((-2,2)\).
In either case the trace generates the base because its marked value occurs at exactly one embedding.
The quadratic over the base with this trace and constant one is irreducible by its negative discriminant at any other embedding, proving the assertions that these are Salem values.

In the negative case fix \(R_-^2=\alpha\) and put \(d=\sqrt{t-2}=\sqrt\alpha-1/\sqrt\alpha>0\).
The coefficient difference must be \(a-a'=d\).
Thus \(K=E(d)\), since \(t_i-2<0\) at the other places.
All seeds with this target are given exactly by
\[
 a=(c+d)/2,\qquad c\in\mathcal O_E,\quad c>2,\qquad
 n_c=\frac{c^2-t+2}{4}\in\mathcal O_E,\qquad c_i^2\le 2+t_i\quad(i\ne *) .  \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(c\) and \(n_c\) are the relative trace and norm, and conversely \(a\) is a root of the monic integral quadratic with these coefficients.
The bounds in (2) express \(\iota(n_c)=|\iota a|^2\le1\).
The seed is strict exactly when all these upper bounds are strict, and \(a'>0\) exactly when \(c>d\).

If (1) holds, choose an integral lift \(c_0\) of one square root, positive at the mark after adding a multiple of four.
The [logarithmic unit lattice](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002) gives a totally positive unit \(v\) with \(v>1\) at the mark and \(0<v_i<1\) elsewhere.
Indeed take a lattice point within bounded distance of a sufficiently large multiple of a vector in the sum-zero hyperplane having these strict logarithmic signs, and square the corresponding unit.
For any proper nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak a\), a positive power of \(v\) is one modulo \(\mathfrak a\), by finiteness of the unit group of the quotient.
Use such a power for \(\mathfrak a=4\mathcal O_E\).
Then \(c=c_0v^j\) eventually exceeds \(\max(2,d)\) at the mark and tends to zero at every other embedding, whose bound \(2+t_i\) is strictly positive.
These infinitely many lifts satisfy (2) strictly, proving both directions of (1) over the full ring of integers, including ramified dyadic places.

For a unit seed additionally require \(n_c\in\mathcal O_E^\times\).
At a nonmarked place \((2-t_i)/4\le (n_c)_i\le1\).
The absolute unit norm gives \(|n_c|\le\prod_{i\ne *}4/(2-t_i)\) at the mark.
Together with \(c^2=t-2+4n_c\) and (2), this explicitly bounds every embedding of \(c\).
The lattice of integers of \(E\) in this box gives a finite search for all unit seeds, allowing either strict or closed disk conditions and, if desired, positivity of \(a'\).

<a id="splitting"></a>
## The minimum of the positive square root

Set \(r=\sqrt\alpha\), \(s=r+r^{-1}=\sqrt{t+2}\) at the mark, and \(L=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
One has \(r=(s+d)/2\) and \(\mathbb Q(r)=E(s,d)\), since \(r^2=\alpha\) and \(d=r-r^{-1}\).
When the negative target is attained, the norm of \(f_-\) over \(K/E\) is
\[
           (X^2-1)^2-d^2X^2=X^4-tX^2+1 .
\]
Taking the norm over \(E/\mathbb Q\) gives \(S_\alpha(X^2)\).
If \(s\notin E\), then \(E(s)\) is totally real because \(t_i+2>0\) everywhere, whereas \(E(d)\) has a complex place.
The two quadratic extensions are distinct, so \([\mathbb Q(r):E]=4\).
Hence \(f_-\) is irreducible over \(K\) and \(S_\alpha(X^2)\) is the minimum of \(r\) of degree \(4u\), with exterior roots \(r,-r\) and \(M(r)=r^2\).
If \(s\in E\), then \(\mathbb Q(r)=E(d)=L\), since \(L\) is already quadratic over \(E\).
Here \(\tau r=(s-d)/2=r^{-1}\), and the nonmarked conjugates of \(r\) have unit modulus by squaring.
Thus \(r\) is Salem, \(M(r)=r\), and \(f_-=(X-r)(X+r^{-1})\) over \(K\).
The minima of \(r,-r\) are distinct because their unique exterior conjugates have opposite signs, and their degrees exhaust \(S_\alpha(X^2)\).
This also proves that \(t+2\) is square in \(E\) exactly when \(\alpha\) is square in \(L\).
For a Salem number \(\theta\), its square is Salem of full degree because distinct conjugates with equal squares could be transported to two conjugates of the maximal modulus \(\theta\).
Its trace \(t=(\theta+\theta^{-1})^2-2\) generates the same trace field, by equality of degrees and inclusion.
Hence \(t-2\equiv(\theta+\theta^{-1})^2\bmod4\) and \(t+2\) is a square in that field, giving the stated realization of \(R_-=\theta\).

<a id="plus"></a>
## The positive sign and positivity at the split place

For \(R_+=\alpha\) the trace of \(a\) must equal \(t\).
The possible seeds are exactly the larger marked roots of \(Y^2-tY+\nu\) with \(\nu\in\mathcal O_E\) satisfying
\[
                 \nu<t^2/4,\qquad t_i^2/4<\nu_i\le1\quad(i\ne *) .           \tag{3}
\]
These inequalities say that the two marked conjugates are distinct and real and each other pair is nonreal of modulus \(\sqrt{\nu_i}\le1\).
They also ensure the quadratic is irreducible and \(a>1\).
The field is \(K=E(\sqrt{t^2-4\nu})\), strictness means \(\nu_i<1\) everywhere off the mark, and \(a'>0\) means \(\nu>0\) at the mark.
The delayed polynomial is \(X^2-tX+1\), so it splits over \(K\) exactly when \(K=L\).
In either case the rational norm repeats \(S_\alpha\) twice, as its coefficients are already in \(E\).
Taking \(\nu=1-v^j\) for large \(j\) realizes the [strict integral positive-sign construction](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#realization).
The boundary choice \(\nu=1\) always gives a unit seed \(a=\alpha,a'=\alpha^{-1}\).
If equality \(\nu_i=1\) occurs at any other embedding, injectivity implies \(\nu=1\).

The unit restriction in (3) is exactly \(\nu\in\mathcal O_E^\times\).
None of the \(t_i\) vanishes because \(t\ne0\), so \(|\nu|\le\prod_{i\ne *}4/t_i^2\) follows from the absolute unit norm.
This and (3) give another finite lattice search.
For any strict integral seed with both marked values positive, \(0<\nu_i<1\) at every other place.
The nonzero absolute norms of \(\nu\) and \(1-\nu\) give \(\nu>1\) and then \(\nu-1>1\).
In particular \(t^2/4>\nu>2\), so this positive-sign realization requires \(\alpha>1+\sqrt2\).

## A dyadic exclusion and genuine lifts over every totally real base

For [Lehmer's Salem number](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#statement), the minimum of its trace is
\[
              F(T)=T^5+T^4-5T^3-5T^2+4T+3 ,
\]
as \(X^5F(X+X^{-1})\) is its degree-ten minimum.
Modulo two, \(F'\equiv T^4+T^2\), with \(F(0)=F(1)=1\bmod2\).
Thus \(F\) is squarefree modulo two and its discriminant is odd.
The index of \(\mathbb Z[t]\) in \(\mathcal O_E\) is odd as its square divides this discriminant, so the power basis computes \(\mathcal O_E/2\mathcal O_E\) and \(\mathcal O_E/4\mathcal O_E\).
The first ring is a product of finite fields and its squaring map is bijective.
For \(c=1+t+t^3\), direct reduction by \(F(t)=0\) gives
\[
               c^2-(t-2)=6+2t-8t^2+2t^3+8t^4 .
\]
Thus \(c\bmod2\) is the unique possible square root there, and every lift with this residue has the same square modulo four.
The displayed difference is nonzero modulo four in the power basis, excluding (1).

Conversely, fix any totally real field \(E\) of degree \(u\ge2\) and any marked place.
Fix a totally positive unit \(\nu>1\) with \(0<\nu_i<1\) at the other places, as in the logarithmic lattice argument above.
Put \(A=4(\nu-1)\).
Choose a sufficiently large positive even integer \(j\) that \(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(Aj^2-1)|>1\), possible because the norm is a polynomial in \(j\) of degree \(2u\) with nonzero leading coefficient.
Any prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) dividing \(Aj^2-1\) avoids \(2A\), and \(c'=Aj\) satisfies \(c'^2\equiv A\bmod\mathfrak p\).
Since \(2c'\) is a unit modulo \(\mathfrak p\), varying \(c'\) by elements of \(\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p^2\) gives every possible value of \(c'^2-A\) in \(\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p^2\).
Choose a lift \(c_0\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_0^2-A)=1\), positive at the mark after adding a sufficiently large rational integer lying in \(\mathfrak p^2\).
Take a totally positive unit \(v\) with the same logarithmic signs and \(v\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak p^2\).
For \(c=c_0v^h\) with \(h\) large, the roots \(a>a'\) of \(Y^2-cY+\nu\) are positive units, with \(a>1>a'>0\) at the mark and complex conjugates of modulus \(\sqrt{\nu_i}<1\) at all other places.
Indeed \(c\to+\infty\) while \(c_i\to0\), giving \(c>1+\nu\) and \(c_i^2<4\nu_i\).
The same seed also meets strict marked circle domination because \(c>2\).
Its negative target has trace \(t=2+c^2-4\nu\), with \(t>2\) and \(-2<t_i<2\) elsewhere.
The marked uniqueness gives \(\mathbb Q(t)=E\).
Now \(t+2=c^2-A\) has valuation one at \(\mathfrak p\), guaranteeing the genuine irreducible quadratic lift at all these distinct targets.
Their marked traces tend to infinity, and the same strict unit seeds give infinitely many positive-sign targets with traces \(c\).

For a concrete strict unit example take \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), \(c=3+2\sqrt3\), \(\nu=2+\sqrt3\) as in the [quartic seed field](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#field).
Then \(t=15+8\sqrt3\), and \(N_{E/\mathbb Q}(t+2)=97\) shows that \(t+2\) is nonsquare.
The irreducible negative norm at delay one is \(X^8-30X^6+35X^4-30X^2+1\).
