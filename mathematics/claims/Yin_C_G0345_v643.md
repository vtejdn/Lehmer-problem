# The energy of unrestricted integral numerators from the two ends of the quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform lower bound and necessary conditions for equality

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge1\), with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Let \(m=\log M(P)>0\), let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) be indivisible by \(P\), and let \(E=\|A/P\|_2^2\) for normalized Haar measure on the circle.
Every such sequence with \(m\to0\) satisfies
\[
    \liminf m E\ \ge\ K,\qquad
    K=\tfrac52+\log 2-2\sqrt2=0.364720\ldots .                       \tag{1}
\]
This strengthens the [arbitrary-width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#statement) by using the [short integral numerator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#statement) and the discreteness of the quotient coefficients at a balanced division.

Translate \(A\) by a monomial and take any [balanced division](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#division) \(X^{-j}A=PQ+R\), where \(R\ne0\) and \(\deg R<d\).
For an input of width less than \(d\), translate its support into \([0,d-1]\) and take \(Q=0\).
Let \(n_-\) and \(n_+\) count the negative and nonnegative occupied exponents of \(Q\), respectively, so \(|n_--n_+|\le1\).
If \(mE\to K\), then every such choice satisfies
\[
 m\max(n_-,n_+)\to\tfrac12\log2,\qquad
 m\big(\|Q\|_2^2-n_--n_+\big)\to0,\qquad
 m\|R/P\|_2^2\to\tfrac12.                                           \tag{2}
\]

<a id="localization"></a>
## Restricting the number of occupied positions at each end

Put \(t=e^{-m}\), \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\), and \(n=\max(n_-,n_+)\).
Use the [two orthogonal projections](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#projections) with \(Y=R/O\), \(Z=UQ\) and their projection \(\Pi\), where \(\Pi Y=0\).
In particular \(\|Y\|_2=\|R/P\|_2\), \(\|Z\|_2=\|Q\|_2\) and \(E=\|Y+Z\|_2^2\).
At the negative end take the occupied inputs in increasing order, and at the nonnegative end take them in decreasing order.
At each end the outputs at a prefix of those positions are obtained from its inputs by a triangular contraction with constant diagonal of modulus \(t\), and the squared output norms at the two ends sum to at most \(\|\Pi Z\|_2^2\).
A constant phase on each output normalizes both diagonals to \(t\).

Consider a subsequence on which \(mE\to L\le K\).
It suffices to show that \(L=K\) and establish (2) there.
Let \(a=\log(3/2)\) and \(l=\lfloor a/m\rfloor\).
If \(n>l\), each end contains at least \(l\) inputs.
For all sufficiently small \(m\), the numbers \(\gamma_i=(1+t)t^{i-1}-1\) are positive through position \(l\), since \(e^{-a}>1/2\).
The [weighted prefix estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#weights) applies to each of the two contractions with \(c_i=\gamma_i\) up to \(l\) and zero thereafter.
Its backward weights and telescoping use only these \(l\) rows, so a longer input is unnecessary.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives a squared output norm at least \(\sum_{i=1}^l\gamma_i^2\) at each end.
If \(n>l\) infinitely often, the Riemann sum consequently gives
\[
 L\ge2\int_0^a(2e^{-u}-1)^2\,du
   =2a-4(1-e^{-a})^2
   =2\log(3/2)-4/9>K.
\]
The last difference is \(\log(9/8)+2\sqrt2-53/18>0\), using \(\log(9/8)>2/17\) by integration or the logarithm series and \(\sqrt2>707/500\).
Thus \(n\le l\) eventually.
The determinant bound at both ends also gives \(E\ge t^{2n}\|Q\|_2^2\).

<a id="defect"></a>
## The part of the quotient outside the two projections

At either end, write \(q_i\) for its successive nonzero inputs, \(y_i\) for the corresponding outputs, and put
\[
      z_i^2=\sum_{j=1}^i(|q_j|^2-|y_j|^2),\qquad z_i\ge0,\quad z_0=0.
\]
Contractivity makes these squares nonnegative.
The same determinant bound at the first \(i\) rows gives
\[
 z_i^2\le (1-t^{2i})\sum_{j\le i}|q_j|^2
       \le (1-t^{2n})\|Q\|_2^2\le(t^{-2n}-1)E.                     \tag{3}
\]
Since \(mn\le a\), \(\limsup s^2 z_i^2\le2(e^{2a}-1)K=5K/2<1\) uniformly in the row and the end.
Here \(K<2/5\) follows from \(\log2<7/10\) and \(\sqrt2>7/5\).
For all sufficiently late indices, \(sz_{i-1}\le t\le t|q_i|\).
The [single-row estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#compression) gives \(|y_i|\ge t|q_i|-sz_{i-1}\).
Squaring this nonnegative lower bound and using the defining identity for \(z_i^2\) yields
\[
                  z_i\le t z_{i-1}+s|q_i|.
\]
Iteration and \(u-1\le(u^2-1)/3\) for every positive integer \(u\) therefore give, for either final count \(h=n_-\) or \(h=n_+\),
\[
 z_h\le s\frac{1-t^h}{1-t}+\frac{s}{3}\sum_{i=1}^h (|q_i|^2-1).   \tag{4}
\]
Both terms are zero for an empty end.

Pass to a further subsequence on which \(mn\to x\), \(m\|Q\|_2^2\to B\), \(\sqrt m\|R/P\|_2\to r\) and \(\sqrt m\|(1-\Pi)Z\|_2\to w\).
These quantities are bounded by the restriction on \(n\), the determinant bound and the triangle inequality.
Balancing and integrality give \(mn_\pm\to x\) and \(B\ge2x\).
The squared norm of \((1-\Pi)Z\) is at most the sum of the two final \(z_h^2\), since the selected outputs at the two ends account for part of the projection \(\Pi Z\).
Apply the Euclidean triangle inequality to the pair of bounds (4).
For their first terms use \(\sqrt m\,s/(1-t)\to\sqrt2\) and \(t^{n_\pm}\to e^{-x}\).
For their second terms bound the Euclidean norm by the sum and use \(s/\sqrt m\to\sqrt2\).
This gives
\[
               w\le 2(1-e^{-x})+\frac{\sqrt2}{3}(B-2x).              \tag{5}
\]
Independently, \(\|\Pi Z\|_2^2\ge t^{2n}\|Q\|_2^2\) gives \(w^2\le(e^{2a}-1)L\le 5K/4<1/2\).

<a id="optimization"></a>
## Combining the short remainder and the integer quotient

The [lower bound for nonzero short integral numerators](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#statement) gives \(r\ge1/\sqrt2\).
Since \(\Pi Y=0\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(L\ge r^2+B-2rw\).
This expression increases strictly in \(r\ge1/\sqrt2\) because \(w<1/\sqrt2\).
Substitution of (5) yields
\[
 L\ge\tfrac12+2x-2\sqrt2(1-e^{-x})+\tfrac13(B-2x).
\]
The function of \(x\) in the first three terms is strictly convex, with its unique minimum \(K\) at \(x=(\log2)/2\).
The last term is nonnegative, proving (1).
If \(L=K\), all the inequalities force \(x=(\log2)/2\), \(B=2x\) and \(r=1/\sqrt2\).
The same limits hold along every convergent further subsequence, giving (2).
