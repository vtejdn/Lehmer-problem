# Density-one irreducibility of the noncyclotomic part of high-slope reciprocal sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Counting reducible sections in a slope interval

Fix \(s\ge3\), \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and put \(F_{a,b}=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) for positive integers \(a,b\).
Use the monic associate and remove all cyclotomic factors to form the core.
There are constants \(c_s,C_s>0\) such that whenever \(R\) is sufficiently large and
\[
              1\le a\le c_s\sqrt{R/\log(2R)},
\]
the number of \(b\) with \((a,b)=1,\ R\le b/a\le 2R\) for which the core is reducible over \(\mathbb Q\) is at most
\[
                         C_s a^2\log(2aR).                              \tag{1}
\]
For each fixed \(s,a\) this gives \(O_{s,a}(\log R)\) exceptional exponents in the interval.
The proportion of exceptions among primitive directions tends to zero uniformly throughout the displayed first-exponent range.
On the complementary directions the unique nontorsion factor has logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(B)+O_s(e^{-cR})\) with \(c>0\) depending only on \(s\).
The estimate counts all ways the core can split, including factors with several exterior roots and directions with no real exterior root.
It leaves the existence of exceptional sequences of vanishing positive measure open.

<a id="poles"></a>
## A common set of pole lifts for many second exponents

Let \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_t\) be the exterior zeros of \(B\), \(L_j=\log|\tau_j|\), \(I=\sum_jL_j=m(B)>0\), and \(\omega_j=L_j/I\).
The [pole allocation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) gives simple poles, no circle zeros, simple parent roots for large slope, exactly \(a\) exterior roots near each pole after taking \(a\)-th powers, and exponential convergence there.
For fixed \(a\) distinguish the \(a\) different lifts \(\xi\) with \(\xi^a=\tau_j\).
Each lift accounts for exactly one exterior root from each parent, with the uniform bound
\[
             x=\xi\exp(u/a),\qquad |u|\ll_s|\tau_j|^{-b/a}.              \tag{2}
\]
Indeed substitution in the section gives \(B(\tau_j e^u)+\xi^{-b}e^{-(b/a)u}g(\tau_j e^u)=0\).
On \(|u|=K|\tau_j|^{-b/a}\) the first term dominates the second for a sufficiently large fixed \(K\), by simplicity and \((b/a)|\tau_j|^{-b/a}\to0\).
Rouché gives one zero in each disk, and the disks are disjoint by lifting the distinct logarithmic branches and choosing disjoint neighborhoods of the finitely many poles.
This is the [quartic lift argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v216.md#powers) at each pole of the fixed seed.

Call a lift \(\lambda\) and write \(\omega_\lambda=\omega_j\) there, so \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda=a\).
For an irreducible nontorsion factor \(P\) let \(h_P\) be the sum of the weights of its occupied lifts, \(k_P\) their number and \(d_P=\deg P\).
Write \(\omega_*=\min_j\omega_j>0\).
Kronecker's theorem and simplicity give \(1\le k_P,\ \omega_* k_P\le h_P\le k_P\).
Every such factor is reciprocal at high slope: its degree is \(\gg_s a\sqrt{b/a}\) by the [individual height theorem](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height), while the entire parent has only \(2ta\) off-circle roots.
It thus has a circle conjugate and is reciprocal by irreducibility.
For \(R\le r=b/a\le2R\), (2) and the same height theorem give uniformly
\[
 m(P)=\frac{I h_P}{a}(1+O_s(e^{-cR})),\qquad
 d_P=r h_P(1+O_s(\varepsilon_R)),\qquad
 \varepsilon_R=\sqrt{\log(2R)/R}.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the leading integral in that theorem is \(I\), since \(|g|=|B|\) on the circle.
Its initial error \(O_s(r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\log(2d_P)/d_P})\) tends uniformly to zero by the cited degree growth.
Combining the two measure expressions first yields \(d_P\asymp_s r h_P\gg_s R\), after which the same error is \(O_s(\varepsilon_R)\).
Summing (2) over the entire section gives \(m(F_{a,b})=I+O_s(e^{-cR})\).

<a id="collision"></a>
## The discriminant of a product from different directions

Consider a collection of \(m\ge1\) such factors from distinct second exponents in a common interval \(\rho\le b/a\le(1+\eta)\rho\) inside \([R,2R]\), with \(0<\eta\le1\).
Their product \(Q\) is monic and squarefree.
Indeed a shared root would have \(B(x^a)\ne0\) by coprimality of \(g,B\), and the two section equations would give \(x^{b-b'}=1\).
Write \(D=\deg Q\), \(W=\sum_P h_P\) and \(N_\lambda\) for the number of these factors occupying lift \(\lambda\).
Then \(\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda N_\lambda=W,\ D\asymp_s\rho W\).
We claim
\[
 \sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda N_\lambda^2
 \le \frac{W^2}{a}\bigl(1+O_s(\eta+\varepsilon_R)\bigr)
                    +O_s(W\log(2D)).                                   \tag{4}
\]

For two exterior roots \(x,z\) at the same lift of \(\tau_j\), (2) gives
\[
 \frac{|x-z|}{|x\bar z-1|}\ll_s|\tau_j|^{-\rho},
\]
since the numerator is \(O_s(|\tau_j|^{-\rho}/a)\) and the denominator is at least \(|x||z|-1\gg_s1/a\).
The reflected interior roots \(1/\bar x,1/\bar z\) give the same disk ratio.
For completeness, fold each root \(z_i\) of \(Q\) into the closed disk as \(w_i=z_i/\max(1,|z_i|)^2\).
After division by \(\max(1,|z_i|)\max(1,|z_j|)\), each distance equals \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|\) for opposite sides or a boundary point.
For two strictly same-side roots it equals this expression times \(|w_i-w_j|/|1-w_i\bar w_j|\le1\).
The product \(\prod_{i<j}|1-w_i\bar w_j|^2\) is at most \(D^D\), by successively maximizing in each closed disk and applying Hadamard's bound to the boundary Vandermonde determinant.
The discriminant is a nonzero integer and each ordered exterior collision has a matching interior collision.
The [reflected discriminant estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v216.md#discriminant) on this product thus gives
\[
 2\sum_{\lambda}(\rho I\omega_\lambda-C_s)N_\lambda(N_\lambda-1)
                   \le D\log D+2(D-1)m(Q).                              \tag{5}
\]
Since \(\omega_\lambda\ge\omega_*>0\), the left coefficients are at least \(2\rho I\omega_\lambda(1-O_s(1/R))\).
Formula (3) gives \(D\le(1+\eta)\rho W(1+O_s(\varepsilon_R))\) and \(m(Q)=(IW/a)(1+O_s(e^{-cR}))\).
Division in (5), followed by adding \(\sum\omega_\lambda N_\lambda=W\), proves (4).
The coefficient of \(W^2/a\) tends to one as both the relative width and the height error tend to zero.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting factors which occupy a common lift

A more general form holds for any \(a\).
For \(K\ge\omega_*\) with \(K\le a/2\) and \((K^2/a)\varepsilon_R\le c_s\), the number of primitive \(b\) in the stated interval with some factor of weight \(h_P\le K\) is
\[
            \ll_s \bigl(a\log(2+K)+K^2\bigr)\log(2+RK).                 \tag{6}
\]
Select one such factor per qualifying \(b\).
Partition its weights into \(O_s(\log(2+K))\) ranges \([H,2H)\), starting at \(\omega_*\) and doubling, always retaining \(h_P\le K\).
For this range split \([R,2R]\) into \(O_s(1+H^2/a)\) subintervals, each of relative width at most \(\eta=\kappa_s\min(1,a/H^2)\), for a sufficiently small fixed \(\kappa_s>0\).
In one subinterval and range, fix a lift of weight \(\omega\) and retain the \(m\) selected factors occupying it.
If it is the only lift, no such factor has weight at most \(a/2\).
Otherwise weighted Cauchy–Schwarz on the other lifts gives
\[
 \sum_{\lambda}\omega_\lambda N_\lambda^2
 \ \ge\ \omega m^2+\frac{(W-\omega m)^2}{a-\omega}
 \ =\ \frac{W^2}{a}+\frac{a\omega}{a-\omega}(m-W/a)^2
 \ \ge\ \frac{W^2}{a}+\frac{\omega_*}{4}m^2 .                          \tag{7}
\]
Here \(W\le am/2\) and \(W\le2Hm\).
In (4) the error times \(W^2/a\) is at most \(\omega_*m^2/8\) by the width choice and the hypothesis on \(K^2\varepsilon_R/a\), after reducing the constants independently of \(a,R,K\).
Consequently \(m^2\ll_s W\log(2D)\) and \(D\ll_s RHm\) give \(m\ll_s H\log(2+RH)\).
For the last implication divide by \(H\), use \(H\ge\omega_*\) and absorb \(\log(m/H)\) in the linear left side for large \(m/H\).
There are \(at\) choices of lift.
Each factor in the range occupies at least \(h_P\ge H\) lifts, so double counting bounds the number of qualifying exponents in this subinterval and range by \(O_s(a\log(2+RH))\).
Adding the subintervals and using the geometric sum of the \(H^2\) proves (6).

Whenever the core is reducible, its factor weights are positive and sum to \(a\), so some \(h_P\le a/2\).
If \(a/2<\omega_*\), reducibility is impossible.
Otherwise take \(K=a/2\) in (6) to obtain (1) in the stated range.
There are at least \((\lfloor R\rfloor-1)\varphi(a)\) primitive second exponents in the interval by counting whole residue systems modulo \(a\).
The elementary bound \(\varphi(a)\ge\sqrt{a/2}\) follows from the prime-power formula, comparing each odd prime-power totient with its square root and allowing a factor \(\sqrt2\) at two.
Thus the exceptional proportion is \(O_s(a^{3/2}\log(2aR)/R)=o_s(1)\) uniformly in the indicated range.
Every irreducible core carries the full positive limiting measure by (3).
