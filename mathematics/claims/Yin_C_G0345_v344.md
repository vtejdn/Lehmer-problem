# A discriminant bound for three reciprocal residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound independent of the number of terms in the rows

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) have nonzero constant term, degree \(n\), and \(P^*=\sigma P\) for \(\sigma=\pm1\), where \(P^*(x)=x^nP(1/x)\).
Suppose \(P\) occupies exactly three residues modulo an odd prime \(p\), with unit row gcd in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\).
Use the lift \(P(x)=x^s F(x^p,x^\ell)\) below, with
\[
 q_X(Y)=YF(X,Y)=a(X)Y^2+b(X)Y+c(X),\qquad \Delta=b^2-4ac .
\]
Here \(0<\ell<p\) is the residue displacement and \(m(F)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure on the two-dimensional torus.
Assume first that \(\Delta\) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
Define \(E=\{X\in\mathbb T:|b(X)|<2|a(X)|\}\).
On \(E\), both roots \(\alpha,\beta\) of \(q_X\) have modulus one.
For normalized arc measure \(\mu\), put
\[
 L_E=\int_E \sum_{u=\alpha,\beta}\log\frac{2}{|u^p-X^\ell|}\,d\mu(X).
\]
The sum counts the two roots separately and is independent of their labeling.
Then
\[
 \left(1-\frac2p\right)m(F)+\frac{m(\Delta)-L_E}{p}
       \ \le\ m(P)\ \le\ m(F)+\frac{2\log2}{p}.                        \tag{1}
\]
In particular the left side gains the nonnegative term \(m(\Delta)/p\) from the integer discriminant.
If \(|b|\ge2|a|\) on the circle, the loss \(L_E\) is zero, including when equality occurs at isolated points.
In this case \(m(P)>0\) implies \(m(P)\ge\min(\log2,\eta_3(1-2/p))\), where \(\eta_3>0\) is the [three-row projected-support gap](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement).
These bounds impose no restrictions on the degrees, heights or term counts of the rows.

Along a sequence with \(p\to\infty\), nonzero discriminant, and \(0<m(P)\to0\), one must have
\[
                       \liminf L_E/p\ge\eta_3 .                       \tag{2}
\]
Thus the unresolved contribution is an integrated logarithmic loss on the arcs where the fiber roots are on the unit circle.
The repeated-root and irreducible cases are described below.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Restoring the reciprocal symmetry after the residue shifts

Choose \(0\le s<p\) with \(2s\equiv n\bmod p\).
Since reflection \(r\mapsto n-r\) preserves the three residues, they are \(r_e\equiv s+e\ell\bmod p\) for \(e=-1,0,1\), with \(0\le r_e<p\) and \(0<\ell<p\).
Write the corresponding nonzero rows as \(U_{r_e}(X)\).
The [prime-modulus lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift) in these coordinates is
\[
 V_e(X)=X^{(r_e-s-e\ell)/p} U_{r_e}(X),\qquad
 F(X,Y)=\sum_{e=-1}^1 V_e(X)Y^e .
\]
The monomial shifts retain unit row gcd and give the stated identity on the diagonal.
With \(d=(n-2s)/p\), comparison of exponents \(s+e\ell+pj\) and \(n-(s+e\ell+pj)\) gives
\[
 V_{-e}(X)=\sigma X^d V_e(1/X).
\]
Put \(a=V_1,\ b=V_0,\ c=V_{-1}\).
On the unit circle \(c=\lambda\overline a,\ b=\lambda\overline b\), with \(\lambda=\sigma X^d\) of modulus one.
For either square root \(\omega\) of \(\lambda\), \(b/\omega\) is real, and on \(|Y|=1\),
\[
                 F(X,Y)/\omega=b/\omega+2\Re(aY/\omega).
\]
Outside the finitely many zeros of \(a\Delta\), this identifies \(E\) as the region with two distinct unit-circle roots.
On the strict complementary region the two roots are interchanged by \(u\mapsto1/\bar u\), with common argument and radii \(R,R^{-1}\), \(R>1\).
The equality \(|b|=2|a|\) gives a repeated root when \(a\ne0\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## The two losses in the cyclic norm

Write \(j(X)=m_Y(q_X)\), and let \(d_p(X)\) be the mean of \(\log|q_X(Y)|\) over \(Y^p=X^\ell\).
At fixed \(X=x^p\) the \(p\) choices of \(x\) give exactly this grid in \(x^\ell\).
Hence the means over \(X\) of \(j,d_p\) are \(m(F),m(P)\), respectively.
For \(a(X)\ne0\), Jensen's formula and the product on the grid give
\[
 p(d_p-j)=\sum_{u=\alpha,\beta}
        \log\frac{|u^p-X^\ell|}{\max(1,|u|)^p}.                       \tag{3}
\]
Each summand is at most \(\log2\).
The diagonal logarithm is integrable because its argument is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
The fiber mean is integrable by the torus Mahler formula, and \(\log|\Delta|\) is integrable because \(\Delta\ne0\).
On \(E\) the integrand defining \(L_E\) is nonnegative and equals \(2\log2-p(d_p-j)\) almost everywhere, so \(L_E\) is finite even in the presence of diagonal zeros.

On \(|b|>2|a|\), each normalized factor in (3) is at least \(1-R^{-p}\ge1-R^{-2}\).
The shared argument of the roots gives
\[
       |\Delta|^{1/2}=|a|(R-R^{-1}),\qquad j=\log(|a|R).
\]
Consequently \(p(d_p-j)\ge2\log(1-R^{-2})=\log|\Delta|-2j\).
On \(E\), where \(j=\log|a|\), the corresponding identity is \(\log|\Delta|-2j=2\log|\alpha-\beta|\le2\log2\).
Integrating both regions in (3) proves (1), in fact with the additional nonnegative term
\[
                    \frac2p\int_E\log\frac{2}{|\alpha-\beta|}\,d\mu
\]
on its lower side.
The exceptional circle points have zero measure.
Since \(\Delta\) is integral and nonzero, its Laurent Mahler measure is nonnegative.

For the primitive lift the [projected-support alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement) gives \(m(F)\ge\eta_3\) unless \(F\) is a signed monomial times a product of cyclotomics in Laurent monomials.
In that case the [specialization argument](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#gap) gives \(m(P)=0\) or \(m(P)\ge\log2\).
This yields the positive gap when \(E\) is empty and proves (2).

<a id="repeated"></a>
## Irreducibility and contact with the unit circle

If \(\Delta\) is a square in \(\mathbb Q(X)\), including zero, the quadratic splits into linear factors over that field.
By primitivity and Gauss's lemma those factors can be chosen in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}][Y]\), up to a signed Laurent monomial in \(X\).
Their leading and constant coefficients are nonzero because \(ac\ne0\) in the Laurent ring.
After specialization each binomial retains two distinct residue classes, so it gives a nonunit of \(\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1}]\).
It follows that an irreducible \(P\) satisfying the hypotheses has nonsquare discriminant.
When \(\Delta=0\), the two primitive linear factors are associates and the diagonal is a square up to a signed monomial.
Indeed the square of a reciprocal two-row input with coprime rows at odd \(p\) gives three primitive rows of this repeated-root type, since \(0,\ell,2\ell\) are distinct modulo \(p\).

If \(E\) is empty, \(b\) has no circle zero: such a zero would force \(a=b=c=0\), impossible for primitive integral Laurent rows.
On \(|x|=1\) the expression \(F(x^p,x^\ell)/b(x^p)\) is real and nonnegative by the symmetry and \(|b|\ge2|a|\).
Every zero of this real analytic circle function has even order.
The other factors relating it to \(P(x)\) are nonvanishing there, so an irreducible \(P\), being separable in characteristic zero, has no unit-circle root in this case.
In particular an irreducible Salem input with three residues requires a nonempty set \(E\).
Its integrated loss in (2) remains to be bounded.
