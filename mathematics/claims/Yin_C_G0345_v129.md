# Salem inputs at the plastic limit with no Salem factor at any nonzero integer level

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous exclusion at every nonzero integer level

Put \(f(z)=z^3-z-1,\ f^*(z)=z^3 f(1/z)\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(f\).
For every sufficiently large odd \(N\) with \(N\not\equiv7\bmod8\) and \(N\not\equiv21\bmod30\), the entire polynomial \(q_N(z)=z^N f(z)+f^*(z)\) is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma_N\).
These numbers are distinct, their degrees \(N+3\) tend to infinity, and \(\theta<\sigma_N<4/3\) with \(\sigma_N\to\theta\).
Write \(m=(N+3)/2\) and let \(S_N\) be the trace minimal polynomial, so \(q_N=z^m S_N(z+z^{-1})\).
For every sufficiently large such \(N\), **no** integer \(b\ne0\) gives a Salem factor of
\[
             Q_{N,b}(z)=z^m(S_N(z+z^{-1})-b)=q_N(z)-b z^m .
\]
Thus even permitting the level to vary over all integers and extracting individual irreducible factors gives no Salem descent on this family.
The input measures approach the fixed plastic number from above.

Write \(T_N=\sigma_N+\sigma_N^{-1}\).
For every integer \(b\) for which \(S_N(x)=b\) has a root in \((2,T_N)\), let \(r\) be its rightmost root in this interval and put \(\tau=(r+\sqrt{r^2-4})/2\).
Uniformly over these levels, the minimal polynomial \(F\) of \(\tau\) satisfies \(M(F)>\sigma_N\) for every sufficiently large admissible \(N\).
Thus lifting the rightmost smaller trace gives a strict *increase* in total Mahler measure, whatever the signature of its factor.

There is also an absolute \(c>0\) such that every monic irreducible nontorsion factor \(F\) of every \(Q_{N,b}\) above satisfies
\[
                \log M(F)\ \ge\ c\,\frac{\deg F}{N+3}.                \tag{1}
\]
For each fixed \(B<\infty\), all sufficiently large-degree such factors with \(\log M(F)\le B\) have at least \(c\deg F\) conjugates off the circle, after decreasing \(c\) if necessary.
In particular positive measures tending to one among these factors would require degrees tending to infinity, degree ratios \(\deg F/(N+3)\to0\), and a positive proportion of off-circle conjugates.
These conditions leave the existence of such factors open.

The coordinator proposed the plastic family and the exclusion of Salem factors using an arc and finiteness, and supplied the measure comparison for the rightmost root.
The contributor identified the exact existing torsion table, supplied the arc value, checked the recurrence argument, and extended the exclusion into a thin annulus to prove (1).

<a id="progression"></a>
## The residue classes of the full Salem inputs

The [plastic-number Salem construction](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family) gives the asserted bounds, convergence and distinctness for the unique Salem factor of every sufficiently large \(q_N\), with all remaining factors cyclotomic.
The [complete torsion table for this polynomial](Yin_C_G0248_v1.md#family-cyclotomic-factors) gives exactly \(\Phi_2,\Phi_8,\Phi_{12},\Phi_{18},\Phi_{30}\) at residues \(0\bmod2,\ 7\bmod8,\ 10\bmod12,\ 14\bmod18,\ 21\bmod30\), respectively.
Thus the odd exponents in the statement are exactly the odd exponents without a cyclotomic factor, including \(N\equiv1\bmod120\).
At sufficiently large \(N\) it is the full Salem minimal polynomial, and inverse pairing gives the displayed trace representation with the original irreducible \(S_N\).

<a id="arc"></a>
## A common arc missing every shifted unit root

For \(|z|=1,\ x=z+z^{-1}\), direct multiplication gives \(|f(z)|^2=5-x^2-x^3+4x\), which is \(1/8\) at \(x=-3/2\).
Choose a closed angular arc \(I\) of positive length around one such \(z\) and a constant \(\delta>0\) so that \(2|f(e^{i\phi})|\le1-\delta\) for \(\phi\in I\).
The absolute value of \(q_N\) there is at most \(2|f|\), so \(Q_{N,b}\) has no unit root with argument in \(I\) for any nonzero integer \(b\).
On the whole circle \(|q_N|\le6\).
Thus a shift having even one unit root must have \(1\le |b|\le6\).
For odd \(N>9\) the seven displayed monomials of \(Q_{N,b}\) have distinct exponents, so Jensen's inequality and Parseval give \(M(Q_{N,b})\le\|Q_{N,b}\|_2=\sqrt{6+b^2}\).
Consequently every Salem factor has log measure at most \(\log\sqrt{42}\).

The [angular estimate for irreducible units](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) shows that for each fixed \(B\) and all sufficiently large \(d\), every monic irreducible unit polynomial of degree \(d\) and \(\log M\le B\) has at least \(c_I d\) conjugate arguments in \(I\), with \(c_I>0\) depending only on the fixed arc.
In fact its tent-function estimate (4) on any fixed smaller arc has fixed positive mean and error tending uniformly to zero at bounded log measure.
For a Salem polynomial at most two conjugates are off the circle, so the missing unit arc gives an absolute degree bound for every possible Salem factor here.
The bound on measure then bounds its integer coefficients by the elementary symmetric coefficient estimates.
It follows that only finitely many candidate Salem numbers can occur, uniformly over \(N,b\).

<a id="recurrence"></a>
## Each fixed exterior root at each bounded level occurs at most twice

For \(j=(N-3)/2\) there is an exact identity at every \(z\ne0\),
\[
             z^{-m}q_N(z)=z^j f(z)+z^{-j} f(1/z).
\]
Fix a candidate Salem root \(\tau>1\) and a nonzero integer \(|b|\le6\).
If \(Q_{N,b}(\tau)=0\), then \(u=\tau^j\) solves
\[
                  f(\tau)u^2-bu+f(1/\tau)=0 .
\]
The coefficient \(-b\ne0\) makes this polynomial nonidentically zero, and the powers \(\tau^j\) are distinct.
Thus at most two odd \(N\) work for this pair.
The preceding finiteness gives one threshold after which no pair occurs.

<a id="radial-cost"></a>
## What the missing arc forces for other positive-measure factors

There is an absolute \(a>0\) such that \(Q_{N,b}\) has no root \(z=e^{t+i\phi}\) with \(\phi\in I,\ |t|\le a/(N+3)\) for any sufficiently large odd \(N\) and any nonzero integer \(b\).
Indeed the preceding Laurent identity has absolute value at most
\[
          e^{a/2}\bigl(|f(e^{t+i\phi})|+|f(e^{-t-i\phi})|\bigr).
\]
Choose \(a>0\) with \(e^{a/2}(1-\delta/2)<1\).
Uniform continuity and the real coefficients of \(f\) make the sum in parentheses at most \(1-\delta/2\) on the strip for all sufficiently large \(N\).
This contradicts equality to the integer \(b\).

Every monic irreducible factor \(F\) is a unit polynomial since \(Q_{N,b}\) has constant coefficient one.
If \(\log M(F)\le B\), the angular estimate therefore gives at least \(c_I d\) roots with \(|t|>a/(N+3)\) when \(d=\deg F\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(B\).
These roots are all off the circle, and \(2\log M(F)=\sum_F |t|\) makes their total radial cost at least \(a c_I d/(N+3)\) in this last sum.
To prove (1) apply the estimate at \(B=1\).
Larger log measures satisfy (1) since \(d\le N+3\).
The bounded degrees at log measure at most one give a finite set of monic integer polynomials by their coefficient bounds.
Among them every nontorsion unit polynomial has strictly positive log measure by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001), which supplies one positive constant for these remaining cases and proves (1).

<a id="rightmost"></a>
## The factor of the rightmost smaller trace costs more than the source

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) be the neutral roots of \(S_N\).
In the [integer-level profile](Yin_C_G0345_v119.md#statement), the levels meeting \((2,T_N)\) are negative.
To locate their rightmost root uniformly, put \(W=\sum_i(T_N-x_i)^{-1}\ge (m-1)/(T_N+2)\).
On \(2<x<T_N\) the logarithmic derivative of \(-S_N(x)=(T_N-x)\prod_i(x-x_i)\) equals \(\sum_i(x-x_i)^{-1}-(T_N-x)^{-1}\), a strictly decreasing function tending to \(-\infty\) on the right.
It is positive whenever \(x<T_N-1/W\).
As \(T_N\to\theta+\theta^{-1}>2\) and \(W\to\infty\), the maximum lies inside the interval at distance at most \(1/W\) from \(T_N\).
The rightmost root of every attained level is at least the position of this maximum.
Consequently \(0<T_N-r\le 1/W=O(1/N)\), and the smooth trace inverse near \(\theta+\theta^{-1}\) gives \(0<\log\sigma_N-\log\tau=O(1/N)\), uniformly in \(b\).
In particular \(\tau\to\theta\) uniformly.

Suppose for contradiction that \(M(F)\le \sigma_N\) for arbitrarily large admissible \(N\).
The degrees \(d=\deg F\) in any such sequence tend to infinity.
Indeed, bounded degree and bounded measure give finitely many candidate roots, so a bounded-degree subsequence would have \(\tau=\theta\) eventually.
At that point the level would be \(b=f^*(\theta)\theta^{-m}\), strictly between \(-1\) and \(0\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), because \(f^*(\theta)=-\theta^2-\theta\).

If \(F\) is reciprocal, its roots on each argument occur in pairs \(z,1/\overline z\) when their moduli differ from one.
The thin-strip exclusion and angular count give at least \(c_I d/2\) exterior roots on the arc, each contributing at least \(a/(N+3)\) to \(\log M(F)\).
The arc can be chosen disjoint from the positive real ray, so \(\tau\) makes a separate contribution.
It follows that \(\log M(F)\ge\log\tau+a c_I d/(2(N+3))>\log\sigma_N\) eventually as \(d\to\infty\).

If \(F\) is nonreciprocal, [Smyth's isolation and equality statement](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-nonreciprocal-isolation) and the convergence of \(\sigma_N\) to \(\theta\) from above force \(M(F)=\theta\) eventually.
Every root in this equality case is a conjugate of a radical \((\pm\theta)^{\pm1/k}\), \(k\ge1\).
Since the two nonreal cubic conjugates of \(\theta\) have modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}\), the positive root \(\tau>1\) has modulus of the form \(\theta^{1/k}\) or \(\theta^{1/(2k)}\).
All these values other than \(\theta\) are at most \(\sqrt{\theta}\).
Therefore \(\tau\to\theta\) forces \(\tau=\theta\) eventually, already excluded by the level at \(\theta\).
Both cases give the required strict increase in the distinguished factor's measure.
