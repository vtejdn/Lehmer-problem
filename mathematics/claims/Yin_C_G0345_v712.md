# Divisibility of cyclotomic multiplicities in mixed radical indices

<a id="statement"></a>
## A prime dividing the binomial exponent

Use the integer Salem family \(G_a(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\), \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7,\ \epsilon=\pm1\), with exterior root \(r\) and \(d=[\mathbb Q(r):\mathbb Q]\).
Suppose the positive radical \(\gamma=r^{1/q}\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(r)\).
Fix a prime \(\ell\mid q\) with \(a\equiv c\bmod\ell\) for \(c\in\{0,1,-1\}\).
Such a representative exists for every \(\ell\le n\) dividing \(q\), by the [cyclotomic specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#specialization).
For \(c=0\) put \(N=n+1,\ \eta=\epsilon\), and for \(c=\pm1\) put \(N=n,\ \eta=-\epsilon c\).
Thus \(X^N-\eta\) is the binomial in \(G_c\).

For every odd prime \(p\ne\ell\) dividing both \(q\) and \(N\), one has
\[
             v_p(q)+v_p(N)\ \le\ v_p(\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}-1).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
For \(2\mid q\) and \(\ell\ne2\), put \(k=v_2(N)+[\eta=-1]\).
If \(k\ge2\), then
\[
              v_2(q)+k\ \le\ \max\{v_2(\ell-1),v_2(\ell+1)\}.          \tag{2}
\]
The inequalities are uniform in both exponents, the other prime divisors of \(q\), the seed and the delay.
In particular (1) excludes \(p\mid q\) in this mixed setting whenever \(p\mid N\) and \(v_p(\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}-1)=1\).
For example an index divisible by six is impossible if \(a\) is even and \(3\mid n+1\), or if \(a\) is odd and \(3\mid n\), by taking \(\ell=2,p=3\).
For \(q=\ell^e p^f\) in (1), the [prime-power gap](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives \(\log\gamma\ge\delta/p^{w-v_p(N)}\), where \(w=v_p(\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}-1)\) and \(\delta=\log(5/2)/6\).
This bound is independent of the part of \(N\) prime to \(p\), even if its \(\ell\)-part is arbitrarily large.

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## Counting all preimages of one primitive order

Write \(t=q/\ell^{v_\ell(q)}\), \(U=\prod_{u=\pm1,\ u^{n-1}=\epsilon}(X-u)\) and \(P_c=G_c/U\).
The [mixed-index reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#specialization) shows that the roots of the reciprocal minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\) modulo \(\ell\), counted with multiplicities and raised to \(t\), give the roots of \(\overline P_c\).
For \(s\) prime to \(\ell\) put \(L_s=|\langle\ell,-1\rangle|\) in \((\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z)^\times\), with \(L_1=1\).
For a source orbit of primitive order \(m\) under Frobenius and inversion, its image has order \(s=m/\gcd(m,t)\).
As in the [orbit-size bound](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#local), this orbit contributes exactly \(L_m/L_s\) to the multiplicity of each of its image points, per copy of the source orbit.
Consequently a common divisor of all these costs for the orbits over a given target point must divide the entire target multiplicity.

For odd \(p\) in (1), put \(b=v_p(N)\ge1,\ f=v_p(q)\ge1\).
If \(\eta=1\) or \(\ell=2\), take \(s=p^b\).
If \(\eta=-1\) and \(\ell\) is odd, take \(s=2^{v_2(N)+1}p^b\).
Every primitive \(s\)-th root occurs in \(X^N-\eta\bmod\ell\) with multiplicity \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\).
Indeed removing the \(\ell\)-part of \(N\) gives a separable binomial, and the specified order gives the required signed value there.
Since \(s>2\), neither the extra possible factor \(X-c\) nor the deleted linear factors in \(U\) change this multiplicity.
It is prime to \(p\).

For every contributing source order \(m\), one has \(v_p(m)=b+f\) because \(v_p(s)=b>0\).
With \(w\) as in (1), the \(p\)-valuation of \(\operatorname{ord}_{p^j}(\ell)\) is \((j-w)_+\) for \(j\ge1\), where \(x_+=\max(x,0)\).
Indeed \(\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\) divides \(p-1\), and raising \(\ell^{\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)}\) to successive \(p\)-th powers increases its difference-from-one valuation by one, by the binomial theorem.
Powers prime to \(p\) leave that valuation unchanged.
The optional factor \(2^{v_2(N)+1}\) in \(s\) has a unit group of power-of-two order.
Adjoining inversion also costs at most two, so \(v_p(L_s)=(b-w)_+\).
Projection onto the units modulo \(p^{b+f}\) gives \(v_p(L_m)\ge(b+f-w)_+\).
If \(b+f>w\), every orbit cost \(L_m/L_s\) is divisible by \(p\), contrary to the target multiplicity.
This proves (1).

For (2) use the primitive order \(s=2^k\ge4\) in the signed binomial.
Its multiplicity is exactly \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\), which is odd.
Set \(w_2=\max\{v_2(\ell-1),v_2(\ell+1)\}\) and take the sign of \(\ell\) giving an integer \(v=\pm\ell\equiv1\bmod4\).
Then \(v_2(v-1)=w_2\ge2\), and repeated squaring increases this valuation by one at each step, since the factor \(v^{2^i}+1\) has valuation one.
Therefore for \(j\ge2\) the subgroup generated by \(v\) modulo \(2^j\) has order \(2^{(j-w_2)_+}\).
All its elements are one modulo four, so adjoining \(-1\) doubles the order, giving \(L_{2^j}=2^{1+(j-w_2)_+}\).
Every source above \(s\) has \(2\)-valuation \(k+v_2(q)\) in its order.
If \(k+v_2(q)>w_2\), its orbit cost is even, which contradicts the odd target multiplicity and proves (2).

<a id="smooth"></a>
## Uniform height for fixed sets of prime divisors

Fix finite sets of primes \(S,T\).
Suppose every prime in \(S\) divides \(a\), every prime divisor of \(n+1\) belongs to \(T\), and every prime divisor of \(q\) belongs to \(S\).
There is an effectively computable \(c(S,T)>0\) such that \(\log\gamma\ge c(S,T)\), uniformly in the exponents of all these primes, the delay and both signs.
The sets \(S,T\) may intersect.

The case \(q=1\) follows from \(r>a-1\), and a prime-power \(q\) has the uniform lower bound \(\delta\).
Otherwise every choice of \(\ell\mid q\) has \(c=0,\ N=n+1\).
If every \(\ell\mid q\) has \(v_\ell(N)\ge2\), choose any one of them as reference.
Equations (1) and (2) bound \(v_p(q)\) by effective constants depending only on \(S\) for each other \(p\mid q\).
It follows that \(t=q/\ell^{v_\ell(q)}\) is bounded and \(\log\gamma\ge\delta/t\) by applying the prime-power gap to \(\gamma^t\).

In the remaining case choose a reference \(\ell\mid q\) with \(v_\ell(N)\le1\).
Every primitive order \(s\) of \(\overline P_0\) divides \(2N\), and every root has multiplicity at most \(\ell\), since \(\overline P_0\mid X^N-\epsilon\bmod\ell\).
For a complementary \(p\mid t\), equation (1) bounds \(v_p(t)\) if \(p\) is odd and divides \(N\), and (2) bounds it if \(p=2\) and \(v_2(N)\ge2\).
For the other complementary primes, \(v_p(s)\le2\) for every occurring \(s\).
In fact it is zero at an odd such \(p\), and \(s\mid2N\) gives the stated bound at two.
As all the prime factors of \(s\) belong to \(T\cup\{2\}\), \(v_p(\varphi(s))\) is bounded solely in terms of \(S,T\).
Indeed the \(p\)-part from a prime \(u\ne p\) in \(s\) is \(p^{v_p(u-1)}\), independently of the exponent of \(u\), and the contribution from \(p\) is bounded because \(v_p(s)\le2\).
Thus \(v_p(L_s)\) is bounded as well.
For any locally admitted source order \(m\) in the refined test of v711, \(L_m/L_s\le\ell\), which bounds \(v_p(L_m)\) in terms of \(S,T\).
The reduction of \(\langle\ell,-1\rangle\bmod m\) onto the subgroup modulo \(p^{v_p(m)}\) is surjective.
The prime-power order formulas just used in (1) and (2) then bound \(v_p(m)\) by an effectively computable constant depending only on \(S,T\).
Hence the multiplier \(h_*=\gcd(t,\operatorname{lcm}_m m)\) of the [refined height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#local) is bounded at every complementary prime.
Taking the finite product of those bounds gives the claimed positive constant.

Let \(R\) be the largest positive field radical index, and put \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{i=-1,0,1}((a+i)/\operatorname{rad}(a+i))\).
If every prime of \(H(a)\) belongs to \(S\) and \(R\mid H(a)\), the uniform estimate applies to \(q=R\).
For example the [effective delay threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) ensures this divisibility when \(n\ge C_1\log(a+1)/\log\log(a+3)\).
The [positive-unit factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now bounds the logarithmic Mahler measure of each noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(G_a(X^k)\), for every \(k\ge1\), below by \(c(S,T)\).
Substitution into the removed factors in \(U\) contributes only roots of unity.

<a id="six"></a>
## An explicit bound for powers of two and three

Suppose \(6\mid a\) and \(N=n+1=5^j\) with \(j\ge2\).
Then for every field radical with \(q=2^e3^f,\ e,f\ge0\), one has
\[
                  \log\gamma\ \ge\ 0.427800\,\frac{N-1}{N}.            \tag{3}
\]
Here \(d=N-1\) since \(n\) is even and exactly one linear root is deleted at each sign.
For \(e>0\) take characteristic two, so \(c=0\), and \(\overline P_0\) divides \(X^N-1\), which is squarefree at two.
The \(3^f\)-th powering of the source multiset is consequently a bijection onto the target multiset.
Put \(K=2\cdot5^{j-1}\).
The relation \(2^K\equiv-1\bmod5^j\) follows by raising \(-4\equiv1\bmod5\) to the odd power \(5^{j-1}\), increasing its difference-from-one valuation at every fifth power.
The map \(z\mapsto z^{-2^K}\) preserves the source by Frobenius and inversion and fixes each target root since its order divides \(5^j\).
The bijective power map commutes with this map, so the map fixes every source root individually.
Every source root order thus divides \(2^K+1\), which is prime to three because \(K\) is even.
On that group \(3^f\)-th powering is invertible and preserves primitive target multiplicities, giving \(\overline S_\gamma=\overline P_0\) by the [cyclotomic inversion argument](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#orders).
In particular \(\gamma^N\equiv\epsilon\bmod2\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(r)}\).
The [scalar height estimate](Yin_C_G0246_v1.md#gim%2B10-arbitrary-prime-scalar-transfer) now gives \(N\log\gamma/d\ge0.427800\), at its accepted published numerical scope.
For \(e=0,\ f>0\), the [prime-power reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius) in characteristic three gives \(\gamma^N\equiv\epsilon\bmod3\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(r)}\), with the stronger scalar height constant \(\tfrac12\log(5/2)\).
For \(q=1\) the root interval gives (3) directly.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Seeds where the earlier divisor estimate tends to zero

For every sufficiently large integer \(j\), there is an integer \(a_j\) divisible by \(Q=6^j\) and at most \(256Q^2\) such that no prime \(v\ge5\) has its square dividing \((a_j-1)a_j(a_j+1)\).
To see this, try the seeds \(a=Qb\) with \(1\le b\le K=256Q\).
For each prime \(v\ge5\) the three terms \(Qb-1,Qb,Qb+1\) give three residue classes modulo \(v^2\), since \(Q\) is invertible there.
At most \(3(K/v^2+1)\) choices are excluded by that prime, and only \(v\le\sqrt{QK+1}\) need be tested.
The total excluded is at most
\[
          3K\sum_{m\ge5}m^{-2}+3\sqrt{QK+1}
                          \le 3K/4+3\sqrt{QK+1}<K ,
\]
using the integral of \(x^{-2}\) from four.
Thus a finite search also produces such a seed.
For these seeds \(H(a_j)\) has prime factors only in \(\{2,3\}\), and \(H(a_j)\ge6^{j-1}\) from the central term.
At \(n=5^j-1\) the effective delay threshold holds eventually, since \(\log(a_j+1)=O(j)\).
Both signs and every dilation therefore have the explicit factor gap (3), by applying it to the largest radical index.
Meanwhile \(\log r/H(a_j)\le\log(a_j+1)/6^{j-1}\to0\).
The sieve establishes growth of the seed divisor \(H(a_j)\), and (3) applies to every field radical index that actually occurs.
