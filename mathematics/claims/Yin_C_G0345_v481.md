# Bounded affine relations among the positions of sparse signed rows

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree alternative for arbitrary coefficients

Fix \(s\ge3\), and let \(A(Y)=\sum_{i=1}^s c_iY^{j_i}\), with \(c_i\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) and \(0\le j_1<\cdots<j_s\le b\).
Write \(H=\sum_i|c_i|\), take integers \(b\ge0,\ p\ge2\), and let \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Suppose that \(\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}\) has infinite order and that
\[
 F(X)=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X^{pb+1}A(X^{-p})
\]
vanishes at \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma^2\).
Write \(e(\gamma)=\min_{k\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\gamma^k):\mathbb Q]\).
There are effective constants \(L_s,K_s\) such that for \(p>L_s\) at least one of the two alternatives holds.
\[
\begin{split}
 &\text{there exists }0\ne q\in\mathbb Z^s,\quad
   \|q\|_\infty\le L_s,\quad \sum_i q_i=0,\quad\sum_i q_ij_i=0,\\
 &e(\gamma)\le K_s(1+\log H)^{2(s-1)}.
\end{split}                                                               \tag{1}
\]
The first alternative concerns the original \(s\) positions.
It is invariant under translation and common dilation of these positions.
The dichotomy includes rows whose two reflected parts vanish separately and rows with simple contacts.

If \(\gamma\) is Salem of degree \(d\), or if every positive power of \(\gamma\) has degree \(d\), then \(e(\gamma)=d\).
For the Salem assertion, all embeddings over \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^k)\) must send \(\gamma>1\) to a conjugate of the same modulus, and only \(\gamma\) itself has this modulus.
Thus for \(d\to\infty,\ p\asymp(\log d)^2,\ H\le\exp(Kp)\), the relation in (1) is necessary for every fixed \(s,K\), including each \(s\ge6\).
No restriction on the width \(b\) enters this conclusion.

<a id="subsum"></a>
## Simultaneous subsums and their character lattice

Put \(N=pb+1,\ M_i=2p j_i-N\) and choose \(\alpha^2=\gamma\).
The \(2s\) integers \(\pm M_i\) are distinct and nonzero for \(p>2\), since equality of oppositely signed exponents would require \(p(j_i+j_k)=N\), including the case \(i=k\).
Write \(E_i\) for the standard basis vectors of \(\mathbb Z^s\) and use \(y^v=\prod_i y_i^{v_i}\).
Give \(E_i\) coefficient \(a_{E_i}=c_i\) and \(-E_i\) coefficient \(a_{-E_i}=\varepsilon c_i\).
At \(y_i=\alpha^{M_i}\) the sum of these \(2s\) monomials is zero, since it is \(\alpha^{-N}F(\alpha^2)\).
The same sum at \(y^2=(y_i^2)_i\) is \(\alpha^{-2N}F(\alpha^4)=0\).
Choose an inclusion-minimal nonempty subset \(I\subseteq\{\pm E_1,\ldots,\pm E_s\}\) whose two sums both vanish.
It has at least three elements, since for two terms the equations force their monomial values to agree, contradicting distinct exponents and the infinite order of \(\alpha\).
Its affine rank is at least two, since a line contains at most two points of the Euclidean unit sphere.

Fix \(v_0\in I\), choose a basis \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) of the lattice generated by \(v-v_0,\ v\in I\), and write \(v-v_0=\sum_\ell h_{v,\ell}b_\ell\).
The differences of the \(h_v\) generate \(\mathbb Z^r\), with \(2\le r\le s\).
Set \(P(z)=\sum_{v\in I}a_v z^{h_v}\) and let \(X\) be cut out in \(\mathbb G_m^r\) by \(P(z)=P(z^2)=0\).
The point \(x=(\alpha^{b_\ell\cdot M})_{\ell=1}^r\) is in \(X\).
After clearing Laurent monomials, the two defining polynomials have ordinary degrees bounded in terms of \(s\) and logarithmic heights at most \(\log H+O_s(1)\).
Indeed the finitely many subsets and basis choices fixed once for each subset have bounded coordinates, and no two support terms coincide.

<a id="codimension"></a>
## Codimension for both signs

In the original \(s\) coordinates set \(S(y)=\sum_{v\in I}a_vy^v\).
We show that \(S(y)\) and \(S(y^2)\) are coprime in the geometric Laurent ring for every block that occurs at the given point.
Any common nonunit Laurent factor of \(P(z),P(z^2)\) would pull back to a common nonunit of \(S(y),S(y^2)\) under \(z_\ell=y^{b_\ell}\), up to monomial factors.
Linear independence of the \(b_\ell\) preserves distinct monomials under pullback.
Every component of \(X\) then has codimension at least two.

Suppose first that some axis contributes only one term.
After inverting that coordinate if necessary, write \(S=aY+U\), where \(U\) uses the other axes, and \(a\ne0\).
The second equation after elimination of \(Y\) implies \(U^2+aU(y^2)=0\).
To see that this is a nonzero Laurent polynomial, first consider terms from two different axes in \(U\).
They give a mixed monomial with twice the product of their coefficients.
If only one axis remains, \(U\) must contain its two terms, whose product gives a nonzero constant in \(U^2\).
In both cases the indicated monomial is absent from \(U(y^2)\).
Since \(Y\) is determined by \(U\), the common zeros have codimension at least two.

If every active axis is paired, write
\[
 S=a(Y+\varepsilon Y^{-1})+U,\qquad
 U=\sum_{\ell=2}^t d_\ell(Z_\ell+\varepsilon Z_\ell^{-1}),
\]
where \(t\ge2\) and all coefficients are nonzero.
The first equation gives at most two values of \(Y\) for fixed remaining coordinates.
For \(\varepsilon=1\), the second implies
\[
                      U^2+a U(z^2)-2a^2=0 .
\]
For \(t\ge3\) the coefficient of \(Z_2Z_3\) is \(2d_2d_3\).
For \(t=2\) the coefficient of \(Z_2^2\) is \(d_2(d_2+a)\).
If \(d_2=-a\), the first equation forces \(Y=Z_2\) or \(Y=Z_2^{-1}\), impossible at the given point.
For \(\varepsilon=-1\), square the second equation and use \((Y+Y^{-1})^2=(Y-Y^{-1})^2+4\) in the first to obtain
\[
                         U^4+4a^2 U^2-a^2 U(z^2)^2=0.
\]
For \(t\ge3\) the coefficient of \(Z_2^3Z_3\) is \(4d_2^3d_3\).
For \(t=2\) the left side becomes \(d_2^2(d_2^2-a^2)(Z_2-Z_2^{-1})^4\).
If \(d_2=-a\), the first equation forces \(Y=Z_2\) or \(YZ_2=-1\).
If \(d_2=a\), it forces \(YZ_2=1\) or \(Y=-Z_2\).
None can hold at the given point, since none of \(\alpha^{M_i\pm M_k}\) for different axes is torsion.
In every possible paired case the remaining coordinates therefore satisfy a nonzero Laurent equation and the finite fibers in \(Y\) give the required codimension.

<a id="cosets"></a>
## Positive-dimensional cosets and the degree of a power

No positive-dimensional coset through \(x\) is contained in \(X\).
Indeed, if \(xT\subset X\) for a subtorus \(T\), group the characters \(h_v\) by their restriction to \(T\).
Distinct characters are linearly independent as functions on \(T\), so the coefficients in each class, evaluated at \(x\), sum to zero.
The partition is the same for \(2h_v\) since the character group of a subtorus has no torsion.
The second equation therefore makes each class a simultaneous vanishing subsum.
By minimality there can be only one class, but the differences of the \(h_v\) generate every character of \(\mathbb G_m^r\).
Their triviality on \(T\) forces \(T\) to be trivial.

Set \(u_\ell=b_\ell\cdot M\).
These numbers are not all zero since the projected support exponents \(v\cdot M\) are distinct.
Put \(g=\gcd_\ell |u_\ell|>0,\ n=u/g,\ D=\|n\|_\infty\).
Then \(n\) is primitive and \(x=((\alpha^g)^{n_\ell})_\ell\) is nontorsion.
At any component through \(x\), both the codimension and the coset hypotheses of [Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U001.md#intersection) hold.
Apply them over \(\mathbb Q\) with \(\eta=1/2\).
On the degree branch \(D^{1/(2(r-1))}\ll_s 1+\log H\).
The Laurent polynomial \(P(t^n)\) is nonzero because its exponents \(h_v\cdot n=(v-v_0)\cdot M/g\) are pairwise distinct.
Its exponent range is at most \(O_s(D)\), and it vanishes at \(\alpha^g\).
Consequently \(e(\gamma)\le[\mathbb Q(\alpha^g):\mathbb Q]\ll_s D\), since \(\gamma^g=(\alpha^g)^2\).
This gives the second branch of (1).

On the other branch there is a bounded nonzero \(w\in\mathbb Z^r\) with \(w\cdot n=0\).
The nonzero vector \(q=\sum_\ell w_\ell b_\ell\in\mathbb Z^s\) has norm bounded in terms of \(s\) and satisfies
\[
                    2p\sum_i q_ij_i=(pb+1)\sum_i q_i .
\]
Reduction modulo \(p\) and \(\gcd(p,pb+1)=1\) show that \(p\mid\sum_i q_i\).
Choose \(L_s\) larger than two and than every possible bound on \(s\|q\|_\infty\) across the finitely many subset bases.
Then \(p>L_s\) gives \(\sum_i q_i=\sum_i q_i j_i=0\) as required.

<a id="count"></a>
## Number of surviving supports

For fixed \(s\) and \(b\ge1\), the first alternative in (1) leaves at most \(O_s((b+1)^{s-1})\) ordered increasing \(s\)-tuples, since each of finitely many nonzero \(q\)'s determines one position from the other \(s-1\).
If the two endpoints are fixed at \(0,b\), a balanced relation supported only on the endpoints would imply \(q_sb=0\) and then \(q_1=0\).
At least one internal position is therefore determined, giving \(O_s((b+1)^{s-3})\) possibilities.
In either convention the surviving fraction is \(O_s(1/b)\) as \(b\) grows.
For example, \(0,1,m,\ldots,m^{s-2}\) fails the first alternative for all integers \(m>L_s\), by successively reducing the weighted relation modulo \(m\) and using the zero sum for the coefficient of position zero.
The constants are effective through the cited theorem, but no numerical values for them are computed here.
Supports on any of the allowed hyperplanes still require further analysis.
