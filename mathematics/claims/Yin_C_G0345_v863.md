# Individual measures of generic torsion sections of toric hypersurfaces

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform exceptional locus for all dilations of a direction

Let \(r\ge2\), let \(F\) be a primitive integral Laurent polynomial irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) with full-dimensional Newton polytope \(N\subset\mathbb R^r\), and put \(H=\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)\).
Write \(c\) for the number of geometric components of \(V=\{F=0\}\) in the torus and \(I\) for the [saturation index](#lattice) of their coordinate characters.
For a primitive vector \(k\in\mathbb Z^r\), write \(u^k=(u^{k_1},\ldots,u^{k_r})\), let \(T_k=\mathbb G_m^r/\{u^k\}\), and let \(W_k\) be the width of \(N\) in direction \(k\).
There is a finite union of proper torsion cosets in \(T_k\) such that, whenever a torsion vector \(\xi\) projects outside it, every \(g\ge1\) and every nonzero root \(z\) of \(F(\xi z^{gk})\) satisfy
\[
 [H(z):H]\ge \frac{gW_k}{cI},\qquad m(z)\ge b_F[\mathbb Q(z)\cap H:\mathbb Q].
\]
Here \(m(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\) uses the absolute logarithmic height and \(b_F>0\) depends only on \(F\).
This extends the [plane component argument](Yin_C_G0345_v859.md#covers) and [single test dilation](Yin_C_G0345_v861.md#transfer) to a quotient torus of arbitrary dimension.
The exceptional locus may depend on \(F,k\), including whether it contains the identity parameter.
Outside it the primitive section has full width and all its roots have infinite order.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Characters on a hypersurface and components of its pullbacks

Work over \(B=\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and choose one geometric component \(C\), with function field \(K\).
After normalizing a monomial and coefficient, the geometric defining equations are distinct conjugates with identical supports, since \(F\) is irreducible in characteristic zero.
The Newton polytope of their product is the Minkowski sum, so each component has full-dimensional polytope and width \(W_k/c\).
A nonconstant ambient character constant on \(C\) would put this irreducible hypersurface inside a binomial hypersurface, forcing it to be a component of that hypersurface with a binomial defining equation.
Thus the coordinates generate a free rank-\(r\) subgroup \(L\subset K^*/B^*\).
Take the normalization of a projective closure of \(C\).
The divisor map embeds \(K^*/B^*\) in its group of Weil divisors, since a rational function without poles in codimension one on a normal projective variety is constant.
The finitely many prime divisors occurring in the coordinate divisors span a free integral group, in which the rational span of the \(r\) independent character divisors intersects in a lattice of rank \(r\).
Hence the saturation \(\Lambda\) of \(L\) in \(K^*/B^*\) has finite index \(I=[\Lambda:L]\) over \(L\), the same on conjugate components.

For scalar multiplication \([d]\) on the ambient torus, choose \(d\)-th roots \(u_i\) of the coordinate functions.
The Galois group of \(K(u_1,\ldots,u_r)/K\) is a subgroup \(D\subset\mu_d^r\), and the geometric components of \([d]^{-1}C\) correspond to its orbits on the generic choices of radicals, since the pullback is finite étale over \(C\).
Their number is the order of \(\mu_d^r/D\).
A character \(a\bmod d\) annihilating \(D\) gives the class of \(\prod_i u_i^{a_i}\in K^*\) in \(\Lambda/L\).
It is independent of representatives, and its triviality implies \(a\in d\mathbb Z^r\) by raising to the \(d\)-th power and using independence of the coordinates modulo constants.
The character group of the quotient therefore injects into \(\Lambda/L\), bounding the component count by \(I\).

<a id="covers"></a>
## Torsion fibers over the quotient torus

Complete \(k\) to a unimodular cocharacter basis and write the corresponding coordinates as \((X,T)\), where multiplication by \(u^k\) scales \(X\) by \(u\) and fixes the \(r-1\) quotient coordinates \(T\).
The projection of \(C\) onto the quotient is dominant of generic degree \(W_k/c\), by considering its irreducible equation as a Laurent polynomial in \(X\) over \(B(T)\).
Fix a positive integer \(G\), temporarily arbitrary, and for \(m\ge1\) pull \(C\) back by \((y,s)\mapsto(y^G,[m]s)\).
The deck translations of the ambient isogeny permute the components transitively and induce base translations on \(s\), so their degrees over \(s\) are equal and sum to \(GW_k/c\).
Their number \(q_m\) is at most \(I\), since the scalar pullback with \(d=\operatorname{lcm}(G,m)\) maps finitely onto this pullback.
Choose \(m_0\) attaining the maximum of these integers.
Pulling the components at \(m_0\) back by any scalar multiplication \([j]\) on the whole base gives \(q_{jm_0}\) components in total, with at least one over each original component.
Maximality gives one over each, which proves (PB) for each component cover of the \(s\)-torus.

Over a dense open base these generically separable covers are finite étale and their embedded affine points coincide with those of their normal models.
Indeed one may invert the leading and trailing coefficients in \(y\), the discriminant of the resulting generic polynomial and the denominators needed to separate the components and identify their normalizations.
This removes a proper closed locus, whose torsion points lie in finitely many contained proper torsion cosets by the [finite maximal torsion coset theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#torus-bogomolov-complement).
Define all finitely many covers over a number field \(M\).
[Zannier's Theorem 2.1](../audits/literature/Zan10/Yin_U001.md#zan10-u001) now gives the full component degree \(GW_k/(cq_{m_0})\) over \(M(\mu_\infty)\) for every point above a torsion parameter outside another finite union of proper torsion cosets.
The coordinates are \((y,s)\) with \(s\) torsion, hence \([H(y):H]\) is at least this degree.
The images of all discarded cosets under \([m_0]\) are proper torsion cosets, as dimension is preserved by an isogeny.
Take their union over components and exclude also the torsion in the zero loci of the two extreme coefficients of the original primitive section, viewed in quotient coordinates.
These loci are proper because the extreme coefficients are nonzero Laurent polynomials.
For a twist over a remaining parameter, choose any torsion lift \(s\) under \([m_0]\) for the component in question.
The \(X\)-coordinate of a section point is \(X(\xi)z^G\), and one may choose \(y=\rho z\) with torsion \(\rho^G=X(\xi)\).
This realizes every section root and gives \([H(z):H]\ge GW_k/(cI)\) at the fixed dilation \(G\).

<a id="uniform"></a>
## One test dilation and the height bound

The torsion points of \(V\) lie in finitely many maximal torsion cosets, all of dimension at most \(r-2\), since no component of \(V\) is a torus coset.
Their images in \(T_k\) are proper torsion cosets, so after excluding these images no torsion section root is possible at any dilation.
Moreover a point \(x=\xi\beta^k\) with \(\beta\) nontorsion on a contained torsion coset forces the entire translated one-parameter subtorus \(\xi\{u^k\}\) into that coset, since every defining character has torsion value at \(x\), so its pairing with \(k\) vanishes.
Thus on every nonidentically zero primitive section such a point lies in \(V^*\), the complement of all contained torsion cosets.

Let \(L_F\) be the sum of absolute coefficients of \(F\).
For a nontorsion root \(\beta\) of any nonidentically zero primitive section, \(D=[H(\beta):H]\le W_k\) and \(h(\beta)\le\log(2L_F)\).
Indeed the coefficients after clearing a monomial are algebraic integers and every archimedean coefficient maximum is at most \(L_F\).
The local root bound divides this maximum (at finite places at most one) by the absolute leading coefficient, with a factor two at infinite places, and the leading coefficient cancels by the product formula.
If \(\beta=\delta^e\) for \(\delta\in H(\beta)\), then \(H(\delta)=H(\beta)\).
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) gives a positive lower bound for \(h(\delta)\) depending only on \(W_k\), by taking the minimum of its lower bounds over the integers \(1\le D\le W_k\).
Since \(e h(\delta)=h(\beta)\), all such \(e\) are bounded by a fixed positive integer \(B_{F,k}\) for the direction and all twists.
Use \(G=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B_{F,k})\) in the fixed-dilation argument and keep its single exceptional union.
For \(z^g=\beta\) the cyclic radical formula gives \([H(z):H]/g=D/e_g\), where \(e_g\) is the largest divisor of \(g\) for which \(\beta\) is an \(e_g\)-th power in \(H(\beta)\).
Indeed automorphisms multiply \(z\) by a subgroup of \(\mu_g\) of order \(j\), so \(z^j\in H(\beta)\) and every admissible divisor divides \(g/j=e_g\).
As \(e_g\mid G\) it divides \(e_G\), so the bound at \(G\) gives \(D/e_g\ge D/e_G\ge W_k/(cI)\).

Put \(R_k=\max(0,k_1,\ldots,k_r)-\min(0,k_1,\ldots,k_r)\).
At every place the torsion coordinates have absolute value one, so summing over the places with \(|\beta|>1\) and \(|\beta|<1\) gives \(h(1:x_1:\cdots:x_r)=R_k h(\beta)\) by the product formula.
The [Amoroso-Viada bound](../audits/literature/AV12/Yin_U001.md#av12-u001) gives a constant \(a_F>0\) for this projective height at every point of \(V^*\).
The ratio \(W_k/R_k\) has a positive minimum \(d_N\) on real nonzero directions by homogeneity, continuity and full dimension of \(N\).
Finally \([H(z):H]=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q(z)\cap H]\), since \(H/\mathbb Q\) is Galois (apply the subgroup index formula in a finite common Galois closure, or pass to a sufficiently large finite cyclotomic field).
With \(\beta=z^g\), the preceding degree bound and \(h(\beta)=g h(z)\) prove the measure assertion with \(b_F=d_Na_F/(cI)\).

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Distinct exponents and the exact cyclotomic coefficient field

Fix \(s=r+1\ge3\) nonzero rational coefficients \(a_0,\ldots,a_r\) and distinct integer exponents.
Subtract the exponent of the zeroth term and divide by the positive gcd of the differences to write the resulting primitive exponents as \(0,k_1,\ldots,k_r\).
For phases \(\zeta_0,\ldots,\zeta_r\) of finite order put \(\xi_i=\zeta_i/\zeta_0\) and let \(w\) be the joint order of the \(\xi_i\).
Apply the theorem to the hyperplane \(F=a_0+\sum_{i=1}^r a_i X_i\), after clearing rational content, and take phase vectors outside the indicated exceptional locus in \(T_k\).
For all dilations \(g\ge1\), subtract the lowest exponent and divide by the leading coefficient of \(a_0\zeta_0+\sum_i a_i\zeta_i X^{gk_i}\) to form a monic polynomial \(f\) of degree \(gR_k\).
For every root \(z\) one then has
\[
 \mathbb Q(z)\cap H=\mathbb Q(\mu_w),\qquad [\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]=gR_k\varphi(w),\qquad
 m(z)\ge \bigl(935r^5\log(r^2)\bigr)^{-r(r+1)^2}\varphi(w).
\]
The product of the distinct coefficient conjugates of \(f\) is the monic rational minimal polynomial of \(z\).
The measure constant depends only on the number of terms.
The exceptional cosets may depend on the coefficients and primitive exponent vector.

Indeed this hyperplane has \(c=1\) and \(W_k=R_k\).
In its projective closure the coordinate hyperplanes \(X_i=0\) in the chart \(X_0=1\) give distinct prime divisors whose orders on the coordinate functions are the rows of the identity matrix, since every \(a_i\ne0\) and \(r\ge2\).
These valuations split the coordinate lattice, so \(I=1\).
The relative degree bound reaches \(\deg f\), proving irreducibility over \(H\).
The distinct exponents recover each ratio of phased coefficients from the monic coefficients, so their field is exactly \(A=\mathbb Q(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r)=\mathbb Q(\mu_w)\).
Putting \(J=\mathbb Q(z)\cap H\), the minimal polynomial over \(J\) has degree \([H(z):H]=\deg f\) and over \(H\) is divisible by \(f\), hence equals \(f\).
Thus \(A\subseteq J\), and \([\mathbb Q(z):A]\le\deg f=[\mathbb Q(z):J]\) forces equality of the fields.
All \(\varphi(w)\) conjugates of \(f\) are distinct because its coefficients generate \(A\), and their monic product has the rational degree just proved.
The [hyperplane height bound](../audits/literature/AV12/Yin_U001.md#av12-u001) has degree of definition one and projective dimension \(r-1\), giving the displayed constant uniformly over these rational coefficients and distinct exponents.
