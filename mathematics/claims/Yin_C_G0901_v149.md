# Angular distribution of the logarithmic mass of the exterior product at a degree record

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small arcs and the limit at growing support

Let \(w\) be a reciprocal strict degree record with bounded positive total log measure, of degree \(d=2ub\) tending to infinity and with \(u\) canonical blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs.
Put \(A=M(w)\), \(\mu=\log M(A)>0\).
Consider separately the positive and negative log moduli of the conjugates of \(A\), using their absolute magnitudes as weights at the arguments of the corresponding conjugates and dividing by \(\mu\).
These are probability measures \(\nu_+,\nu_-\) on the circle.
In the independent signed representation let \(e\) count all nonneutral inverse pairs of \(w\); in the oriented representation let it sum, over blocks, the number of exterior coefficients different from their most frequent value among \(1,0,-1\) in a chosen half.
This count is unchanged by reversing a half.

On the noncoordinate branch \(e\ge2\), for any arc \(I\) of normalized length \(|I|\) one has
\[
           \max(\nu_+(I),\nu_-(I))\ \ll\
                \sqrt{|I|+\left((1+\log M(w)+u\log(3b))/b\right)^{1/3}}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The implied constant can be taken uniform at any fixed source measure ceiling.
The same bound holds for moving arcs, and any weak limit along these records has bounded density with respect to normalized arclength (the density bound for a given limit may depend on a bounded subsequence of \(e\)).
If \(e\to\infty\), then each of \(\nu_+,\nu_-\) in fact converges weakly to normalized arclength.
Thus a shrinking sequence of strict records on the [large second-iterate branch](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#records) cannot confine a positive fraction of either growing log mass to finitely many shrinking arcs.
This concerns the actual exterior product and uses its bounded integral coefficients; no analogous angular assertion for arbitrary powers from its divisible group is needed.

<a id="phases"></a>
## Unweighted phases in a single block

Write \(m_0=\log M(w)\) and \(s=((1+m_0+u\log(3b))/b)^{1/3}\), which tends uniformly to zero by the [canonical block bound](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
In any full block of \(2b\) roots the fraction of arguments in any arc \(I\) is \(O(|I|+s)\).
In any one oriented half the normalized phase Fourier coefficient at each fixed nonzero integer \(h\) tends uniformly to zero; the same holds for a full block in either model.
Indeed the full blocks, or all the oriented halves, form a Galois-invariant partition into sets of size \(n=2b\) or \(b\).
The product of their absolute polynomial discriminants is the absolute value of a nonzero rational integer.
The [reflected energy calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#energy), with smoothing radius \(e^{-1/n}\), gives for the normalized phase sum on each set, for \(1\le h\le b\),
\[
 \frac1n\left|\sum_y e^{ih\arg y}\right|
    \ll \sqrt{\frac{h(m_0+u\log(3b))}{b}}+\frac{hm_0}{b}.
\]
Here one retains the \(h\)-th nonnegative energy term after summing over the partition, and replacing each reflected radial power by a phase power costs at most \(h|\log |y||\), of total at most \(2hm_0\).
For an arc centered at \(\phi\), a Fejér kernel of order \(H\) at \(\phi\) is \(\gg H\) throughout the arc when \(H|I|\le1/4\).
Its constant term is one and its other Fourier coefficients have modulus at most one.
Taking \(H\) comparable to \(\min(1/|I|,1/s)\) with a sufficiently small constant gives the asserted upper bound, with the trivial bound for long arcs.
Negative Fourier orders follow by conjugation.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Size of the logarithmic weight

Use the Galois group \(G\) of \(w\) and uniform \(g\in G\).
Choose one representative \(a_i\) of each inverse pair, using the canonical halves in the oriented case.
Up to a single rational sign,
\[
          A=\prod_i a_i^{c_i},\qquad c_i\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
The coefficient records whether the selected root is interior, neutral or exterior with its corresponding sign.
Write \(\ell_i=\log|ga_i|\), \(L=\log|gA|=\sum_i c_i\ell_i\), and \(h_1=\mathbb E|\ell_i|=2m_0/d\), the same for all \(i\).
We claim
\[
                              \mathbb E|L|\gg \sqrt e\,h_1.              \tag{2}
\]
In the independent model average further over all supported even sign changes in each block.
The resulting signs of the nonzero terms have uniform marginals on any four coordinates, since \(b\ge5\), and are independent across blocks.
For the signed sum \(S\), conditionally on \(g\), its second moment is \(\sum_{c_i\ne0}\ell_i^2\) and its fourth moment is at most three times the square of its second moment.
Hölder's inequality bounds \(\mathbb E|S|\) below by \(\sqrt{\sum_{c_i\ne0}\ell_i^2/3}\), at least \(\sum_{c_i\ne0}|\ell_i|/\sqrt{3e}\).
Averaging proves (2).

In the oriented model block products are torsion at the record, so the sum of the log moduli in each half is zero at every embedding. [Pure oriented space](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules)
In each occupied block reserve two labels of a most frequent color.
Pair as many remaining labels of that color with exceptions as possible, disjointly.
There are \(K\gg e\) such pairs in total and \(K\le e\), because each majority has at least \(b/3\) entries.
Swaps of any selection of these pairs can be implemented by pure alternating permutations: on each block compensate odd parity by transposing its reserved equal-color labels, which leaves the monomial unchanged.
With independent fair swap choices the conditional log is a constant plus a symmetric signed sum with coefficients \((c_i-c_j)(\ell_i-\ell_j)/2\).
Symmetry and convexity allow the constant to be dropped in a lower bound for its absolute first moment.
The same moment inequality gives a lower bound \(\gg K^{-1/2}\sum_{\rm pairs}|\ell_i-\ell_j|\).
For every pair used, conditioning on \(ga_i\) makes the other position uniform on the remaining labels in that half, of mean log \(-\ell_i/(b-1)\).
Thus \(\mathbb E|\ell_i-\ell_j|\ge h_1\) by Jensen's inequality, proving (2).

In the independent model the signed group also allows pure alternating permutations with respect to any chosen orientation.
For a supported label three-cycle take a lift and raise it to the fourth power: it still gives the three-cycle, fixes all roots outside the three pairs and has order three.
Its signs on the three labels have product one and can be corrected by supported even flips.
These pure cycles generate the required action. [Independent flips and lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles)
Thus in that model we may choose the orientations so the nonzero entries in each block are split as evenly as possible between the two signs.

<a id="arcs"></a>
## Moving one coefficient while retaining another

Choose a most frequent coefficient in every block as baseline, using zero in the independent model whenever it has at least \(b/3\) positions and is convenient.
In either model a baseline can be chosen with at least \(b/3-O(1)\) positions.
In the oriented case its contribution to \(L\) vanishes in each block.
In both models the signed differences from baseline have absolute value at most two.
When \(e\ge2\) there are at least two positions different from baseline in total; in the independent case this follows from the balanced orientation, using zero baseline in sparse blocks.

Fix one such position \(j\).
Let \(H\) act by pure alternating permutations on \(j\) and the baseline positions of its block.
On each coset \(gH\), this source position is placed uniformly among the transported positions of that set, while all other exceptional entries remain in place.
One other prescribed label may also be held fixed if distinct from \(j\), by omitting it.
Writing \(t_j\) for the nonzero difference from baseline, the log \(L_0=L-t_j\ell_j\) and the corresponding phase with \(t_j\arg(ga_j)\) subtracted are constant on the coset.
This holds even for a nonzero block baseline: the product of all the roots in that transported selection is unchanged under permutations.
There are \(\gg b\) available roots in a full block of \(2b\).
Hence the conditional probability of \(\arg(gA)\in I\) is at most \(p=\min(1,C(|I|+s))\), uniformly in all the fixed data.
The preimage under the power \(t_j\) of a translated arc is at most two arcs with the same total normalized length.

For the first estimate, bound \(|L|\) by \(\sum_{c_i\ne0}|\ell_i|\) in the independent model and by \(2\sum_{\rm exceptions}|\ell_i|\) in the oriented model.
For each term fix its own label and move a different exceptional position as above.
It follows that \(\mathbb E(|L|{\bf1}_I)\ll p e h_1\).
For the second estimate just move \(j\), keeping \(L_0\) fixed, and use \(\mathbb E|t_j\ell_j|\le2h_1\).
This yields
\[
       \frac{\mathbb E(|L|{\bf1}_I)}{\mathbb E|L|}
             \ll \min\{\sqrt e\,p,\ p+e^{-1/2}\}.
\]
The minimum is \(O(\sqrt p)\), by separating \(e\le1/p\) and \(e>1/p\).
Each strict sign of \(L\) has total weight \(\mathbb E|L|/2\) by the norm of the unit.
This proves (1).
For a sequence on which \(e\) is bounded, the first estimate gives a limiting interval bound \(O(\sqrt{\sup e}\,|I|)\); on a sequence where \(e\to\infty\) the second gives \(O(|I|)\).
The estimates apply to open arcs and extend to limiting measures by weak convergence and open-set approximation.
Every subsequence admitting a weak limit has a further subsequence of one of these two types, proving the bounded-density assertion.

<a id="fourier"></a>
## Vanishing weighted Fourier coefficients when the number of exceptions grows

Fix \(h\ne0\), and let \(L_+=\max(L,0)\), \(L_-=\max(-L,0)\); each is a 1-Lipschitz function of \(L\).
First suppose \(e\to\infty\) with \(e\le\sqrt b\).
Move one exception \(j\) as in the arc argument.
In the oriented model all but \(O(\sqrt b)\) of the positions in a half are available.
The fixed-order Fourier estimate above shows that the conditional mean of \(\exp(ih t_j\arg(ga_j))\) tends to zero, uniformly, using orders \(h t_j\).
In the independent case choose zero baselines; a further even sign flip on the chosen position and a spare zero position makes both inverse roots equiprobable, without affecting the remaining monomial.
Thus the same assertion follows from the full block estimate after at most \(2\sqrt b\) deleted roots.
Replacing \(L_\pm\) by the corresponding function of \(L_0\) in the weighted harmonic costs \(O(h_1)\), which is \(o(\mathbb E|L|)\) by (2).
The phase from the remaining monomial is constant under the conditional averaging (the extra independent flips here have zero baseline).
This proves vanishing of the normalized weighted Fourier coefficient in this range.

Now suppose \(e>\sqrt b\).
In either model the aligned orientations above give \(\gg e\) disjoint different-color pairs each between a baseline and an exception, reserving two equal-color labels per used block.
For the independent case this follows by splitting the \(j\) nonzero entries of each block evenly in sign: the number outside a largest color lies between a constant times \(j\) and \(j\) (with the evident sparse zero majority).
Use \(K=\lceil C_h\log(3d)\rceil\) of these pairs.
As in (2) average over independent compensated swaps; write \(L_0\) now for the mean of the log on this swap orbit.
Replacing \(L_\pm\) by \((L_0)_\pm\) costs \(O(Kh_1)\) in expectation, hence \(o(\mathbb E|L|)\).
The modulus of the conditional phase harmonic is exactly
\[
             \prod_{(i,j)}\left|
                   \cos\!\left(\tfrac12 h(c_i-c_j)
                                (\arg(ga_i)-\arg(ga_j))\right)\right|.
\]
Its expectation is at most \(\gamma_h^K\) for some \(\gamma_h<1\) independent of the record.
Indeed first fix all block transports, and randomize by pure supported permutations.
Expose the \(2K\) ordered labels pair by pair, possible uniformly without replacement since at least two spare labels remain on each block.
After any previous exposures and the first position of the current pair, there are \(b-O(K)\) choices for its second position, all distinct roots of a full block of \(2b\).
The angles making the displayed cosine greater than \(\gamma'_h\) in absolute value belong to a bounded number of arcs of arbitrarily small total length by choosing \(\gamma'_h<1\) sufficiently close to one.
The arc estimate puts conditional probability at most \(1/2\) on this event, uniformly in the previous positions and the first angle.
The product bound follows successively with \(\gamma_h=(1+\gamma'_h)/2\).
Finally \(\max |L_0|\le\max_g|L|\le ub\,\mathbb E|L|\) by the [transitive projection bound](Yin_C_G0901_v114.md#projection).
Choosing \(C_h\) large proves vanishing also in this range.
Trigonometric approximation now gives the asserted weak convergence separately for the two normalized measures.
