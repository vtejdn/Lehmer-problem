# Excluding 59 from every simultaneous pair of radical primes above the delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## The first possible smaller prime after the finite tests

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers, \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(r>1\) the exterior root of \(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Take positive real radicals in \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\).
If primes \(n<p<q\) satisfy \(r^{1/p},r^{1/q}\in K\), then
\[
                         61\le p<q<1500,\qquad r^{1/(pq)}<1.064 .
\]
In particular 59 can never occur together with another prime above the delay in the same field radical index.
The bounds apply without a restriction on the size of the seed.

<a id="range"></a>
## Why checking second primes through 410 is sufficient

Set \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
The [degree and prime restriction](Yin_C_G0345_v708.md#degree) already gives \(d\ge56,\ p\ge59\), using the published degree bound below the Lehmer measure.
Suppose \(p=59\).
Since \(n<p\) and \(d\le n+1\), the delay is one of \(55,56,57,58\), and the even degree satisfies \(56\le d\le58\).
The [complementary-index bound](Yin_C_G0345_v705.md#complement) gives
\[
                         q<4\log(59)(\log d/\log\log d)^3<410 .
\]
For the numerical inequality use \(\log59<4.08,\ \log58<4.07,\ \log\log56>1.39\), and \(4(4.08)(4.07/1.39)^3<410\).
For example the first two comparisons follow from the lower exponential series for \(e^{4.08}\) and \(e^{4.07}\).
For the last use \(e^{1.39}<4.02\) and \(e^{4.02}<56\).
These strict exponential comparisons may all be checked with the first twenty-one terms, bounding the tail after degree twenty by \(x^{21}/(21!(1-x/22))\) for \(0<x<5\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## The two exact computations

There are 63 primes \(59<q\le410\), partitioned by their multiplicative order modulo \(q\) for the base 59.
For the 47 orders greater than 58, the [finite-field inverse and integer trace search](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0182/readme.md#through410) covers the four delays, both signs and every residue of the seed modulo 59.
The [recovery theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v708.md#decoding) ensures that any field radical yields one of the complete integral lifts.
There were 5,640 such lifts over the 22,184 parameter choices, of which 1,081 passed the endpoint sign and irreducibility tests.
Each failed a necessary identity for the second trace, at modulus 101 or 103.
These are exact congruences on the powered candidate, after restoring the two possible linear cofactor roots.

For the remaining 16 primes the [small-order computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0182/exceptional-orders.md#result) covers all 128 choices of prime, delay and sign.
It first restricts the seed modulo the square of either 59 or the other prime.
At a surviving seed the reduced target is a product of cyclotomic factors.
For each primitive target order, the ratio of the sizes of the Frobenius and inversion orbits at a proposed larger preimage order and at the target order bounds the multiplicity needed for that preimage.
Where this excludes every larger order, the inverse is cyclotomic, and the [integral norm comparison](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0182/exceptional-orders.md#norm) rules out the positive Salem radical.
The computation excludes 114 choices in characteristic 59 and the remaining 14 in characteristic \(q\), in each case for every integral seed.
Together the two computations exhaust the required range.

Thus \(p\ge61\), and the upper bound \(q<1500\) follows from the [two-prime estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v705.md#cutoff).
Every smaller prime below 59 had already been excluded, so 59 cannot occur as the larger member either.
By the [first radical estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v702.md#statement), \(r^{1/(pq)}<p^{1/q}\).
When \(p=61\), the next prime is 67.
When \(p\ge67\), \(\log p/(p+2)\) decreases with \(p\).
Hence \(p^{1/q}\le\max(61^{1/67},67^{1/69})<133/125=1.064\), with the last two inequalities checked by taking integer powers.
This concludes the proof.
