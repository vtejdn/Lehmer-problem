# A sharp Mahler bound for nonnegative integral summands on the circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive Laurent polynomial with a decomposition

Let \(u\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) be real and strictly positive on the unit circle, with largest exponent \(h\ge1\) and coefficient \(u_h=\pm1\).
Write \(M(u)=\exp\int_{\mathbb T}\log u\,d\mu\), where \(\mu\) is Haar probability measure.
Suppose \(u=U+V\), where \(U,V\) are nonzero integral Laurent polynomials nonnegative on the circle.
If \(w\) is the larger of their largest exponents, then
\[
 M(u)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 (3+\sqrt5)/2,&w=h,\\
 4,&w>h .
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
The first constant is attained by \(u=3-X-X^{-1}=1+|1-X|^2\), where the squared modulus denotes evaluation on the circle.
In particular a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial \(P\) of degree \(2h\) with no circle roots satisfies \(M(P)\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\) whenever a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(B\) satisfies \(|B|^2\le |P|\) on the circle.
If the width of \(B\) exceeds \(h\), then \(M(P)\ge4\).

There is a coefficient-sensitive form for a positive integer \(q\).
If \(qu=U+V\) and \(a=U_w,\ b=V_w\), put \(x=\sqrt{M(U)}\), \(y=\sqrt{M(V)}\), where isolated zeros are allowed in these logarithmic integrals.
Then
\[
 T:=x^2+y^2+a^2/x^2+b^2/y^2,\qquad
 qM(u)\ \ge\ \frac{T+\sqrt{T^2-4(a+b)^2}}2 .                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular cancellation above degree \(h\) forces \(qM(u)\ge4|a|\).

<a id="factors"></a>
## A matrix of two spectral factors

For a nonzero nonnegative integral Laurent polynomial \(U\) of largest exponent \(j\), there is a real polynomial \(B\) of degree \(j\), zero-free in the open disk, such that \(B(0)>0\) and \(U=|B|^2\) on the circle.
Indeed \(U_{-i}=U_i\) follows from reality there.
If \(j>0\), the roots of \(X^jU\) off the circle occur in reciprocal-conjugate pairs, and each circle root has even multiplicity by nonnegativity along the circle.
Take the exterior member of each pair, and half of the multiplicity at each circle root, preserving complex conjugation.
The resulting real polynomial has a boundary square proportional to \(U\), with positive proportionality determined at any point where \(U>0\).
Rescale and choose its sign to give the stated normalization.
For \(j=0\), take \(B=\sqrt{U_0}\).
The elementary circle mean of \(\log|1-z/\rho|\) is zero for \(|\rho|\ge1\), including the integrable boundary zero.
Thus \(B(0)=\sqrt{M(U)}=x\).
The integer polynomial \(X^jU\) has nonzero integral leading coefficient, so its root product formula gives \(M(U)\ge1\).
These conclusions also give a factor \(C\) of \(V\) with \(C(0)=y\ge1\) and a factor \(D\) of \(qu\) with \(D(0)=\sqrt{qM(u)}\).
Strict positivity of \(u\) makes \(D\) zero-free on the closed disk.

In degree \(w\) write \(B^\#(z)=z^w B(1/z)\), padding with zero leading coefficients when necessary, and define \(C^\#\) the same way.
The coefficients of \(B,C\) at degree \(w\) are \(a/x,b/y\) respectively, by evaluating the top coefficient in the boundary squares.
On the unit circle the columns of
\[
  \Theta(z)=\frac1{D(z)}
       \begin{pmatrix}B(z)&-C^\#(z)\\ C(z)&B^\#(z)\end{pmatrix}
\]
are orthogonal with norm one because \(qu=|B|^2+|C|^2\).
Testing against two fixed unit vectors and applying the scalar maximum principle shows that \(\Theta(0)\) has operator norm at most one.
The real matrix \(D(0)\Theta(0)\) has columns \((x,y)^{\mathsf T}\) and \((-b/y,a/x)^{\mathsf T}\).
Its squared singular values have sum \(T\) and product \((a+b)^2\).
Its larger squared singular value therefore gives (2).

<a id="gap"></a>
## The leading coefficients and the sharp example

For \(w>h\) one has \(b=-a\ne0\).
The bound in (2) becomes \(qM(u)\ge T\ge4|a|\) by applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality separately to \(x^2+a^2/x^2\) and \(y^2+a^2/y^2\).
For \(w=h\) and \(q=1\), one has \(|a+b|=1\).
If both \(a,b\) are nonzero, then \(T\ge2(|a|+|b|)\ge6\) since these integers have opposite signs.
If one vanishes, the other has modulus one, contributing at least two to \(T\), and the remaining squared constant contributes at least one.
Thus \(T\ge3\) in every case and (2) yields (1).
The example has measure \((3+\sqrt5)/2\) by solving \(X^2-3X+1=0\).

For the pointwise certificate take \(u=\pm X^{-h}P>0\), \(U=B(X)B(X^{-1})\), \(V=u-U\).
Then \(M(u)=M(P)\), and the integrality and pointwise assumptions give two nonnegative integral summands.
The possibility \(V=0\) would factor \(P\), up to a Laurent unit, as \(B(X)B(X^{-1})\).
A nonconstant-width \(B\) makes both factors nonunits, contradicting irreducibility, while a monomial gives a constant \(U\).
This proves the stated application, with no restriction on the width of \(B\).
The [quadratic specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v617.md#quadratic) also treats the weaker pointwise majorant \(4|P|\) by a different estimate.
