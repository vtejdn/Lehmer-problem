# A cubic ideal-class obstruction to splitting at a moving prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## A prime with three primitive labels and a single noncyclotomic factor

For \(q=1831\) and every integer \(0<l<q\), the rational factorization of the five-term polynomial is
\[
  X^{2q}-2X^{q+l}+X^q-2X^{q-l}+1
         =\Phi_6(X)^{\,\epsilon} S_l(X),\qquad
  \epsilon=\mathbf1_{\gcd(l,6)=1},
\]
where \(S_l\) is irreducible, reciprocal and noncyclotomic of degree \(3662-2\epsilon\).
Here \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)=610\) and \(h=(q-1)/f=3\), so there are three primitive binary trace orbits.
In the cubic subfield \(K\) of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3q})^+\), each of the three primes above two has ideal class of exact order seven.
The obstruction therefore works even though \(\Phi_3\) splits modulo seven.
Schoof's table records a class-group factor of size seven at degree three and conductor 1831.
We identify the order of the specific dyadic class by a norm computation and residue characters at 29 and 379. [Schoof, table of prime conductors](https://reneschoof.github.io/realcyc.pdf)

More generally let \(q\ge29\) be prime with \(h=(q-1)/\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)=r\) prime, let \(K\) be the degree-\(r\) subfield, and put \(z=f[\mathfrak p]\) in its ordinary class group for a prime \(\mathfrak p\) over two.
The same factorization, with \(\deg S_l=2q-2\epsilon\), holds for \(r=3,5\) if \(z\ne0\), and for \(r=7\) if \(\operatorname{ord}(z)>2\).
For arbitrary prime \(r\), if a rational trace factor selects \(e\) of the \(r\) primitive labels with \(0<e<r\), then
\[
                 \operatorname{ord}(z)
                    \le \left(\frac{e(r-e)}{r-1}\right)^{(r-1)/2}.     \tag{1}
\]

<a id="masks"></a>
## Integer annihilators of a choice of labels

Index the primes over two by powers of a Galois generator \(\sigma\) of \(K/\mathbb Q\).
The [sampled trace ideal and its conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v263.md#module) give \(A(\sigma)z=\Phi_r(\sigma)z=0\), where \(A(X)=\sum_{i\in J}X^i\) represents the primitive labels \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,r-1\}\) selected by the rational trace divisor.
There is one label per prime since \(h=r\).
Thus evaluation at \(\sigma\) makes the group generated by the conjugates of \(z\) a module over \(R=\mathbb Z[\xi]\), for \(\xi\) a primitive \(r\)-th root of unity.
Every integer in the ideal \(A(\xi)R\) annihilates \(z\), giving
\[
                 A(\xi)R\cap\mathbb Z
                       \ \subseteq\ \operatorname{ord}(z)\mathbb Z .
\]
Complementing the labels replaces \(A(\xi)\) by its negative, and translating their indices multiplies it by a root of unity.
If \(0<e=|J|<r\), then \(A(\xi)\ne0\) since its coefficients are not all equal and its degree is at most \(r-1\).
Its field norm is a nonzero integer in \(A(\xi)R\).
Orthogonality on \(\mathbb Z/r\) gives \(\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}|A(\xi^j)|^2=e(r-e)\), by subtracting the value \(e^2\) at the trivial character from \(re\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality bounds the absolute norm and proves (1).

For \(r=3\) or \(5\), every nonempty proper selection can be complemented to have one or two elements.
One element gives a unit, and for a pair the remaining factor after translation is \(1+\xi^a=(1-\xi^{2a})/(1-\xi^a)\), an integral unit for odd \(r\) and \(r\nmid a\).
Hence a proper nonempty selection requires \(z=0\).
At \(r=7\) one must also consider triples.
After translation and rescaling by a nonzero residue, they have the form \(\{0,1,t\}\).
For \(t=2,4,6\) this is an arithmetic progression, whose sum of roots is a unit by the analogous quotient \((1-\xi^{3a})/(1-\xi^a)\).
The cases \(t=3,5\) are equivalent under \(x\mapsto1-x\).
All six nonzero differences of \(\{0,1,3\}\) occur exactly once, giving \(A(\xi)\overline{A(\xi)}=3+\sum_{j=1}^6\xi^j=2\), also after the allowed changes of indices.
Such a selection requires \(2z=0\).

Once only the empty and full selections are possible, exactly one irreducible trace factor takes all primitive labels by squarefreeness.
The [trace-to-reciprocal argument](Yin_C_G0345_v263.md#lift) then gives precisely the factorization in the statement for \(q\ge29\), including the irreducibility of the reciprocal lift at \(\epsilon=1\).
The parent takes value \(-1\) at one and is positive for sufficiently large positive arguments, so \(S_l\) has a root greater than one.

<a id="field"></a>
## Gaussian periods and a principal seventh power at conductor 1831

Trial division by the primes through 41 proves primality of \(q=1831\), and \(q-1=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot61\).
At the exponents \((q-1)/p\) for these four primes the residues of 3 are \(1830,1158,571,1287\).
Thus 3 generates \((\mathbb Z/q)^\times\).
One has \((-2)^{610}=1\bmod q\), and its powers at \(610/2,610/5,610/61\) give \(1830,571,1024\), proving \(f=610\).

Fix a primitive complex \(q\)-th root \(\zeta\), put \(H_i=3^i\langle3^3\rangle\) in \((\mathbb Z/q)^\times\), and set \(\eta_i=\sum_{a\in H_i}\zeta^a\) for \(i\bmod3\).
These are real since \(-1\in H_0\), and sum to \(-1\).
They are distinct because a polynomial of degree at most \(q-1\) vanishing at \(\zeta\) must be a multiple of \(\Phi_q\), whereas the difference of two coset sums has unequal coefficients.
Thus they generate the cubic field \(K\), with \(\sigma\eta_i=\eta_{i+1}\).
Two splits completely there by the [decomposition group calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v259.md#ideals).

For reproducibility work first with the basis of coset sums in the group ring of the additive group \(\mathbb Z/q\), using the four sets \(D_i=\{0\},H_0,H_1,H_2\), in that order.
Multiplication of the sums on sets \(D_i,D_j\) has coefficient \(\#\{a\in D_i:b-a\in D_j\}\) at \(b=0,1,3,9\), with subtraction modulo \(q\).
These representatives determine all coefficients by symmetry under \(H_0\).
Before evaluation at \(\zeta\), the coefficients of the square and cube of the \(H_0\) sum are respectively \((610,195,202,212)\) and \((118950,124383,123766,123756)\).
Writing \(Y=\eta_0\) and eliminating \(\eta_2\) gives
\[
 F(Y)=Y^3+Y^2-610Y+4408=0,\qquad
             10\eta_1=398-17Y-Y^2 .
\]
The cubic \(F\) is the minimal polynomial since \(Y\) has three conjugates.

Write \([a,b,c]=a+b\eta_0+c\eta_1\), and set
\[
       u=[-914453,72890,16496],\qquad \alpha=[-226,-13,-17].
\]
Both are algebraic integers.
For either, put \(H(Y)=10a+10bY+c(398-17Y-Y^2)\).
Its characteristic polynomial is \(10^{-3}\operatorname{Res}_Y(F(Y),10t-H(Y))\).
This gives \(N(u)=1\) and characteristic polynomial \(t^3+648t^2-4681t-128\) for \(\alpha\).
In particular \(\alpha\) has norm 128, and its characteristic polynomial reduces to \(t(t+1)^2\) modulo two.
Since two splits completely, precisely one of the three residues of \(\alpha\) vanishes.
Thus \((\alpha)=\mathfrak p^7\) for one dyadic prime and \(u,\sigma u\) are units.

<a id="characters"></a>
## Three residue characters rule out a principal dyadic prime

For a prime \(p\equiv1\bmod7\), a simple zero of \(F\) in \(\mathbb F_p\) gives an embedding \(K\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q_p\) by Hensel lifting.
Fix an element \(\omega\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) of order seven.
On elements that are units in this embedding use the additive character with value \(t\in\mathbb F_7\) determined by \(\bar x^{(p-1)/7}=\omega^t\).
It vanishes on local seventh powers.
The three rows below specify the embeddings by their period residues.

| \(p\) | \(\omega\) | \((\eta_0,\eta_1,\eta_2)\) | \((u,\sigma u,\alpha)\bmod p\) | Character values |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 29 | 16 | \(0,5,23\) | \(8,12,8\) | \(3,0,3\) |
| 29 | 16 | \(5,23,0\) | \(12,13,14\) | \(0,4,6\) |
| 379 | 125 | \(46,129,203\) | \(279,117,15\) | \(3,6,1\) |

The residues of \(F'(\eta_0)\) are \(28,26,145\), and \(10\eta_1=398-17\eta_0-\eta_0^2\) and the period sum give the indicated second and third coordinates.
The determinant of the character values on the three columns is \(1\bmod7\).
Thus the combined images of \(u,\sigma u,\alpha\) span a three-dimensional space over \(\mathbb F_7\).
The images of all global units span a space of dimension at most two, since \(K\) is totally real cubic with torsion units \(\{\pm1\}\) and unit rank two.
Equivalently, discreteness of the logarithmic embedding lattice in the plane of vectors of coordinate sum zero gives the needed upper bound on its rank.
If \(\mathfrak p=(\beta)\), then \(\alpha\) is a unit times \(\beta^7\).
The element \(\beta\) would be a unit at each of the three chosen places, so its seventh power would have zero image.
This contradicts the rank calculation.
Hence \([\mathfrak p]\) has exact order seven, and its two conjugates have the same order.
Since \(7\nmid610\), \(z=f[\mathfrak p]\ne0\), as required for the three-label conclusion.
