# Separated dyadic unit roots when the height parameter has more factors of two than the power difference

<a id="statement"></a>
## A regime in which a fourth root would contradict complete local separation

Take the [genuine trace-pole data](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) with \(p=q+1,\ q\ge2,\ L=2q+1\).
The real pair has \(T=\beta^u,\ Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)=\beta^v\), \(0<u<v\), \(\gcd(u,v)=1\). [Graph-coordinate criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion)
Put \(\epsilon=1,\ x=q/p\) at even \(q\) and \(\epsilon=-1,\ x=p/q\) at odd \(q\).
Let \(e=v_2(x)=v_2(pq)\), \(N=v-\epsilon u\), \(s=v_2(N)\), \(m=\epsilon u\), and normalize \(v_2(2)=1\).
Every dyadic conjugate of \(T\) occurs among the unit zeros of
\[
 z^N=V(x,z)^m,\qquad V(x,z)=\frac{1-x/z}{1-xz}.                         \tag{1}
\]
If \(s<e\), all distinct unit zeros satisfy \(v_2(z-z')\le1\), even in the algebraic closure.
Their full set is an isometric copy of the \(N\)-th roots of unity for the dyadic distance.
The splitting field of the unit zeros is abelian over \(\mathbb Q_2\).

For odd \(N\) the zeros reduce to distinct roots and are in canonical Galois-equivariant bijection with \(\mu_N\) in the unramified field \(\mathbb Q_2(\mu_N)\).
For \(e\ge s+2\) there is a canonical Galois-equivariant bijection \(\xi\mapsto z_\xi\) with \(v_2(z_\xi-\xi)\ge e-s\) and \(\mathbb Q_2(z_\xi)=\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\).
For the boundary \(e=s+1\), \(s\ge1\), each pair \(\{\xi,-\xi\}\subset\mu_N\) corresponds to two zeros at mutual distance of valuation exactly one.
A corresponding zero belongs to \(\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\) or its unramified quadratic extension, as determined below.
The pairwise labelling inside this last pair need not be canonical.
Thus not even two roots occupy one radius-\(1/8\) disk in the indicated regime.
In particular this covers opposite parities of \(u,v\) at every admissible height.
It does not rule out the global occurrence of these exponent patterns in a putative sequence of integral solutions.

<a id="count"></a>
## Reduction and the odd exponent

Over any dyadic field the numerator and denominator of \(V\) can be written \(z-x\) and \(z(1-xz)\).
Both are units at a unit \(z\), and each reduces there to \(\bar z\).
Clearing the denominators in (1), using the reversed ratio if \(m<0\), gives a polynomial over \(\mathbb Z_2\) with reduction \(z^{|m|}(z^N-1)\).
Its nonzero residue roots have total multiplicity \(N\).
This is exactly the total number of its unit roots in the algebraic closure, with multiplicity: in a finite splitting field normalize each linear factor to have Gauss norm one, then multiply their nonzero reductions.
Negative-valuation roots contribute only nonzero constants, positive-valuation roots contribute powers of \(z\), and the remaining roots give the unit factors.
This argument counts only the unit roots of the cleared equation.
The integral Salem minimum of \(T\) has all roots in this part by the [power relation in the dyadic order](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities).

When \(s=0\), all the residue roots of \(z^N-1\) are simple.
Each lifts by Hensel's lemma in the same unramified field as its Teichmüller representative.
The unique zero above a given residue generates that unramified field since its residue already generates its residue field.
This proves the odd case, also if \(e=1\).

<a id="deep"></a>
## Closer lifts at the nonsingular radius

Suppose \(a=e-s\ge2\) and take \(\xi\in\mu_N\).
In \(K=\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\), for \(v_2(w)\ge a\), the exponential \(z=\xi\exp(w)\) is defined and \(V(x,z)\in1+2^e\mathcal O_K\).
Consider
\[
                        w\longmapsto \frac mN\log V(x,\xi\exp w).
\]
Its image lies in \(2^a\mathcal O_K\).
For two arguments \(w,w'\) the two values of \(1-xz\), and also those of \(1-x/z\), differ by an element of valuation at least \(e+v_2(w-w')\), since exponential is isometric here.
The logarithms have the same difference bound by the convergent series on \(1+4\mathcal O_K\).
After multiplying by \(m/N\), differences gain at least \(a\) valuation units.
Completeness gives a unique fixed point in this ball.
Exponentiating the identity \(Nw=m\log V(x,z)\) gives (1) and \(v_2(z-\xi)\ge a\).

For any two distinct roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) their distance has valuation at most one: distinct odd parts have different residues, while \(v_2(1-\zeta)=1/2^{j-1}\) for a primitive root of order \(2^j\).
The latter follows by evaluating the \(2^j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial at \(1\) and using that the conjugates have the same valuation.
Thus the \(N\) distinct fixed points exhaust the unit zeros and preserve every pairwise valuation.
Uniqueness and rationality of (1) make the matching Galois-equivariant, so the stabilizers and the individual generated local fields agree.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## The quadratic residue equation when \(e=s+1\)

Assume \(s\ge1\), so that \(m\) is odd because \(\gcd(u,N)=1\).
For \(\xi\in\mu_N\) write \(z=\xi(1+2w)\) with \(w\) integral.
After subtracting the right side of (1) from the left and dividing by \(2^{s+1}\), the reduction in the residue field of \(\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\) is
\[
                        w+w^2-(\bar\xi+\bar\xi^{-1}).                    \tag{2}
\]
In fact \(N/2^s\) and \(\binom N2/2^{s-1}\) are odd.
For \(j\ge3\), \(v_2\binom Nj\ge s-v_2(j)\) by \(j\binom Nj=N\binom{N-1}{j-1}\); hence the \(j\)-th term of \((1+2w)^N\) has valuation at least \(s+2\).
On the other side \(V-1=x(z-z^{-1})/(1-xz)\) gives the displayed constant after dividing \(V^m-1\) by \(2^e\).
This argument works for negative \(m\) because \(V\) is a unit.
It also holds as a polynomial reduction after clearing the \(w\)-dependent unit denominators, which reduce to constants here.

The two roots of (2) are simple.
They both lift in \(\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\) precisely if the residue-field trace of \(\bar\xi+\bar\xi^{-1}\) to \(\mathbb F_2\) is zero, by the image of the map \(b\mapsto b^2+b\) in a finite field.
Otherwise the roots lift after the unramified quadratic extension and neither is rational over \(\mathbb Q_2(\xi)\).
In either case their two \(w\)-residues differ by one, giving valuation one for the difference of the two corresponding \(z\)'s.
The disks \(v_2(z-\xi)\ge1\) for \(\xi,-\xi\) agree.
Two distinct such pairs are at mutual valuation strictly less than one by the root-of-unity calculation above, so their lifts preserve that valuation.
This constructs exactly \(2(N/2)=N\) distinct units.
All lie in the unramified quadratic extension of \(\mathbb Q_2(\mu_N)\), which is abelian over \(\mathbb Q_2\): it is the compositum of that cyclotomic field and an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\) of degree twice its residue degree.

<a id="example"></a>
## A critical-radius example and the global limitation

For \(q=4,u=1,v=15\) one has \(e=2,\ N=14,\ s=1\).
The class above \(\xi=\pm1\) gives two rational unit zeros.
For each nonidentity seventh root \(\xi\), \(\bar\xi+\bar\xi^{-1}\) has trace one in \(\mathbb F_8\): its three Frobenius iterates together sum all six nonidentity seventh roots, whose sum is one in characteristic two.
Consequently the other twelve unit zeros occur over the unramified extension of degree six and do not lie in the degree-three unramified field.
The real-power equation has its unique solution \(1<T<5/4\), since \(v/u=15>2q+1\) and the real ratio increases continuously from \(2q+1\) to infinity. [Real ratio](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#real)
These exponent and root counts concern equation (1); they do not supply the global pole norm of a genuine realization.
Indeed a unit coordinate at \(u=1,\ q\ge3\) would have pole norm one by the [short-exponent result](Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#statement).

More generally every fixed \(q\ge2\) admits arbitrarily large even \(v\) with \(u=1\).
The positive solution then approaches \((q+1)/q\) from below to any prescribed real accuracy, and the odd-\(N\) unit roots still have pairwise distinct dyadic residues.
Here the accuracy may be prescribed at each \(q\); no claim is made at the degree-dependent rate of the exact global trace pole.
These controls and the [three-root bound](Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#statement) leave open whether there are genuine integral solutions at unbounded heights.
