# Multiplicative relations for reciprocal units with two exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exterior pair and its Galois translates

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with exactly two roots \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) of modulus greater than one.
Write \(R_s=|\alpha_s|\), let \(G\) be the Galois group of the splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\), and choose a representative \(v_i\) from each of the \(m\) inverse pairs of roots.
There are \(q=m-2\) neutral pairs, whose roots have modulus one.
A relation is a vector \(a\in\mathbb Z^m\) for which \(\prod_i v_i^{a_i}\) is a root of unity.
Neutral relations are the relations supported on the \(q\) neutral pairs.

If \(R_1\ne R_2\), there are no nonzero relations among all \(m\) representatives.
If \(R_1=R_2=R\), form the following signed graph on the inverse pairs.
For each \(g\in G\), write
\[
                   g(\alpha_1)=v_i^{\varepsilon_1},\qquad
                   g(\alpha_2)=v_j^{\varepsilon_2},\qquad
                   \varepsilon_s\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Join \(i\) to \(j\) with edge sign \(-\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\).
Retain both signs if the same endpoints occur with different signs.
Call a component balanced if the edge signs multiply to \(+1\) around every closed walk.
All components are balanced or all are unbalanced.
If they are unbalanced, all \(m\) representatives are independent modulo roots of unity.
If they are balanced and there are \(c\) components, the full relation lattice has one generator per component, with coordinates \(\pm1\) at its vertices and zero elsewhere.
Each sign vector is determined up to simultaneous negation on its component.
The neutral relation lattice has rank \(c-1\), generated by the components disjoint from the two exterior inverse pairs.

For equal exterior moduli, if the underlying simple graph is a matching, the multiplicative Mahler measure \(\beta=M(P)=R^2\) is itself a Salem number or a real reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one.
A root-of-unity quotient \(\alpha_1/\alpha_2\) forces this matching, in particular whenever both exterior roots are real with equal modulus.
If the two exterior moduli differ, both exterior roots are real.

<a id="relations"></a>
## Recovering the full integral relation lattice

The orbit graph and matching reduction for two exterior conjugates were proposed by agent-S.
The coordinating agent supplied the unequal-modulus extension for two real exterior roots.
Since \(P\) is monic reciprocal its constant is one, and all its roots are algebraic units.
They are distinct by irreducibility in characteristic zero.
None is torsion, since one torsion conjugate would make them all torsion.
In particular no inverse pair is a singleton, and the two exterior roots determine two distinct inverse pairs.
The transitive action on the roots induces a transitive action on inverse pairs because each automorphism commutes with inversion.

For an integer vector \(a\), the element \(b_a=\prod_i v_i^{a_i}\) is an algebraic unit, also for negative exponents.
For every \(g\) and indices and signs as above one has
\[
             \log |g^{-1}(b_a)|
                    =\varepsilon_1 a_i\log R_1+\varepsilon_2 a_j\log R_2 .
\]
Only the two indicated representatives can map to roots off the circle under \(g^{-1}\).
If \(b_a\) is torsion all these values vanish.
Conversely they test all embeddings of \(b_a\), since the splitting field is normal and separable.
Vanishing gives a nonzero algebraic integer with all conjugates on the unit circle, hence a root of unity by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
Thus the logarithmic equations describe the exact lattice of relations modulo torsion.

When \(R_1=R_2\), each equation says \(a_j=(-\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)a_i\).
Following a path propagates the sign and leaves the absolute value constant.
An inconsistent closed walk forces every coordinate in its component to be zero.
On a balanced component, propagation from an initial value one is well-defined and supplies the primitive generator of all integral solutions there.
For \(h\in G\), write \(h(v_i)=v_{hi}^{\delta_i}\).
The edge from \(hg\) has the sign of the edge from \(g\) multiplied by \(\delta_i\delta_j\).
The extra factors cancel around every closed walk, so transitivity on vertices makes balance the same in every component.
The same sign-switching argument shows independence of the choices of representatives.
The original two exterior labels lie in one component by the edge from the identity.
Requiring both coordinates there to vanish removes exactly that generator in the balanced case and leaves all others.

For \(R_1\ne R_2\), orient each edge from the label of \(g(\alpha_1)\) to the label of \(g(\alpha_2)\).
The equations now give \(|a_j|=(\log R_1/\log R_2)|a_i|\) on every such edge.
Every vertex has an outgoing edge by transitivity.
Starting at any vertex and following directed edges eventually reaches a directed cycle, where multiplication by a positive power of the fixed ratio different from one forces a zero.
Propagation back along the initial path forces its starting coordinate to be zero.
A nonreal exterior root would bring its complex conjugate as the second exterior root of the same modulus, proving the asserted reality in this case.

<a id="matching"></a>
## When the Mahler measure is a single exterior conjugate

Assume \(R_1=R_2=R\).
The two exterior roots are either complex conjugates or real roots of opposite signs.
Thus there is \(\delta\in\{-1,1\}\) with \(\beta=\delta\alpha_1\alpha_2=R^2\).
This is an algebraic unit and equals the Mahler measure by the root product.
Suppose distinct edges of the underlying simple graph are disjoint.
The image under any \(g\) of the exterior edge then either misses it or is that same edge.
In the first case both images of the exterior roots lie on the unit circle.
In the second case \(g(\beta)\) is a signed product choosing one root from each of the two original inverse pairs, with the fixed prefactor \(\delta\).
Its modulus is one if precisely one choice is exterior.
Choosing both exterior roots gives exactly \(\beta\), and choosing both interior roots gives exactly \(\beta^{-1}\).
Consequently \(\beta\) is the only distinct exterior conjugate, and the unit norm forces \(\beta^{-1}\) to occur as a conjugate.
The minimal polynomial is reciprocal since inversion then preserves the full orbit.
It has degree two if these are the only conjugates, and otherwise has a unit-circle conjugate and defines a Salem number.

If \(\alpha_1/\alpha_2\) is torsion, choose \(N\ge1\) for which their \(N\)-th powers coincide.
In the map from the distinct original roots to their \(N\)-th powers every fiber has the same size by transitivity.
The fiber of \(\alpha_1^N\) contains exactly the two exterior roots by modulus, so all fibers have size two.
Inversion exchanges the fibers over \(y\) and \(y^{-1}\).
These are distinct since \(y=y^{-1}\) would make the original roots torsion.
Their union consists of exactly two original inverse pairs and gives a two-element block on the set of pair labels.
These blocks form a \(G\)-invariant partition, and the original exterior pair of labels is one block.
All its translates are therefore blocks, and by transitivity they exhaust the partition, giving the required matching.
In the real equal-modulus case the distinct exterior roots have quotient \(-1\), so \(N=2\) works.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Applying the independence criterion to powers

There are no neutral relations if the exterior moduli differ, or if they are equal and the graph is unbalanced or connected.
In these cases the [power-interlacing result for neutral pairs](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#reciprocal) applies to the full polynomial having the powered roots with their original multiplicities.
For \(q\ge2\), the positive integers \(n\) for which each of the \(q\) bins cut out by the roots of \(D_{q-1}\) receives a powered neutral trace have natural density \(q!/[4(q-1)^q]\), where \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
These powers give partial torsion interlacing with deficit two.
For \(q=1\) the constant interlacer has the corresponding property at every positive exponent.
The logarithmic density bounds for unions in the cited result apply at fixed allowable deficit \(H\ge2\) in their stated range as \(q\to\infty\).
When the equal-modulus graph is balanced and disconnected, its \(c-1\) neutral relations describe exactly the obstruction to the independent-phase hypothesis of that result.
