# A lower bound for the first occupied pair of Schur defect coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## Pisot excess at a prescribed constant

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta>1\), of even degree \(d=2\ell+2\ge4\) and positive constant \(q=d^2\).
Write \(\delta=\theta-q\), and use the [Schur correspondence](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#correspondence) and the [parity decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v295.md#parity) to put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N=q^2-1,\qquad A=f^*-qf,\qquad L=\operatorname{lc}(A)=N+j,\qquad K=L^2-N^2,\\
 B=\frac{LA+NA^*}{Kz}=D(z^2)+zE(z^2),\qquad
 \Delta=\frac K N(qE+E^*),\qquad f(z)=U(z^2)-zV(z^2).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here \(j\) is a positive integer, \(B\) is monic and strictly stable, and \(\Delta\) has integer coefficients.
Reversal uses the formal degrees \(d,d-1,2\ell,\ell,\ell-1,\ell-1,\ell\) for \(f,A,B,D,E,\Delta,V\), respectively.
Write \(S=[w^\ell]V=-[z^{d-1}]f\).
Assume that both outer defect coefficients vanish, \(\Delta_0=\Delta_{\ell-1}=0\).

For \(\Delta\ne0\), let \(i\) be the least index at which the pair \(x=\Delta_i,\ y=\Delta_{\ell-1-i}\) is nonzero, and let \(H_0=\max(|x|,|y|)\).
If \(0<j<q-1\), then \(S\in\{q,q+1\}\), and
\[
                H_0>q^{1/3}\quad(S=q),\qquad
                H_0\ge\sqrt{(q+1)/2}\quad(S=q+1).                  \tag{1}
\]
There is no height restriction on the deeper coefficients.
More precisely, with \(W=qS-L\) and \(k=\gcd(L,S)\), either \(H_0\ge L/(|W|+S)>(q-1)/2\), or \((x,y)\) is a nonzero integer multiple of \((W/k,S/k)\).
If the whole nonzero defect satisfies \(\Delta^*=\varepsilon\Delta\) for \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), its content \(c_\Delta\), the positive greatest common divisor of its coefficients, satisfies
\[
              c_\Delta\ge\frac L{|W-\varepsilon S|}
                         \ge\frac L{2(q+1)}.                        \tag{2}
\]

For every \(A_0\ge1\), set \(Q_{A_0}=\max(A_0^3,2A_0^2)\).
Over all branch indices \(j\), every input with \(\Delta\ne0\) and \(H_0\le A_0\) satisfies
\[
                           \delta>\frac1{Q_{A_0}+3}.                \tag{3}
\]
On also admitting \(\Delta=0\), the lower bound is \(1/\max(327,Q_{A_0}+3)\).
These are bounds for the excess of the Pisot input at its prescribed constant.

<a id="pair"></a>
## The congruence at the first occupied pair

Suppose \(0<j<q-1\).
The [parity inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v295.md#parity) gives \(E=(q\Delta-\Delta^*)/K\), so both outer coefficients of \(E\) vanish.
The leading coefficient in the formula for \(V\) is therefore \(S=(L/N)(q+B(0))\).
Since \(|B(0)|<1\), we have
\[
          q-1<S<(1+j/N)(q+1)=q+1+j/(q-1)<q+2.
\]
This gives the two trace choices.

Use the [elimination identity](Yin_C_G0345_v295.md#endpoints), which follows by reversing the general coefficient congruence before any restriction to a monomial defect, to write
\[
              N^2V\equiv(q^2-w)\Delta^*-q(1-w)\Delta\pmod L.        \tag{4}
\]
The coefficient of \(w^\ell\) on the right is zero because both outer coefficients vanish.
Since \(N\equiv-j\pmod L\), this gives \(L\mid j^2 S\).
Put \(h=\gcd(L,j^2)=\gcd(L,N^2)\), so \(L/h\mid S\) and \(\gcd(h,q)=1\).
The coefficient at \(w^i\) on the right of (4) is \(q^2y-qx\), as all preceding coefficients of both \(\Delta,\Delta^*\) vanish.
Thus \(h\mid qy-x\), giving \(L\mid S(qy-x)\) and hence \(L\mid Wy-Sx\).
In both trace cases \(|W|<S\), since \(W=1-j\) or \(W=q+1-j\).
A nonzero multiple of \(L\) in the last congruence forces \(H_0\ge L/(|W|+S)>(q-1)/2\).
Otherwise \(Wy=Sx\), and \(\gcd(W,S)=k\) gives the primitive pair \((W/k,S/k)\) claimed above.

<a id="traces"></a>
## Bounds for the primitive pair

Consider first \(S=q+1\) and \(Wy=Sx\).
Then \(W=q+1-j>0\), so \(x,y\) are nonzero and have the same sign.
Here \(k=\gcd(j,q+1)\) divides \(N,L\).
Reducing (4) modulo \(k\), where \(q\equiv-1\), gives \((1-w)(\Delta^*+\Delta)\equiv0\).
The factor \(1-w\) has a unit leading coefficient, so \(k\mid x+y\).
We obtain \(k\le2H_0\) and \(S/k\le|y|\le H_0\), proving \(q+1\le2H_0^2\).

For \(S=q\) and \(Wy=Sx\), write \(q=k\beta\) and \(j-1=k\nu\), so \(\gcd(\beta,\nu)=1\).
In this case \(j=1\) would give \(q^2=L\mid S=q\), which is impossible.
Thus \(0<\nu<\beta\), and the primitive pair is \((-\nu,\beta)\).
The integer \(\lambda=L/k=k\beta^2+\nu=q\beta+\nu\) is coprime to \(\beta\), and \(L\mid qj^2\) gives \(\lambda\mid j^2\).
Reducing \(\beta^2 j=\beta^2(k\nu+1)\) modulo \(\lambda\) gives the residue \(\beta^2-\nu^2\).
Consequently
\[
                  q\beta<\lambda\le(\beta^2-\nu^2)^2<\beta^4,
\]
so \(H_0\ge\beta>q^{1/3}\).
In the alternative \(Wy\ne Sx\), the lower bound \((q-1)/2\) exceeds both thresholds in (1) because \(q\ge16\).

<a id="content"></a>
## Exact reversal and the excess bound

Under \(\Delta^*=\varepsilon\Delta\ne0\), the right side of (4) is \((\varepsilon q-1)(q+\varepsilon w)\Delta\).
It vanishes modulo \(h\), and \(q+\varepsilon w\) has unit leading coefficient, so \(h\mid(q-\varepsilon)c_\Delta\).
As \(L/h\mid S\), this gives \(L\mid c_\Delta S(q-\varepsilon)\).
Subtracting \(c_\Delta L\) gives \(L\mid c_\Delta(W-\varepsilon S)\), where \(|W|<S\) ensures \(0<|W-\varepsilon S|\le2(q+1)\).
This proves (2).

For the all-branch conclusion, the [Pisot branch inequalities](Yin_C_G0345_v293.md#branch) give \(\delta>j/(q+2+q^{-1})\).
If \(j\ge q-1\), the right side exceeds \(1/2\) since \(q\ge16\).
When \(0<j<q-1\), (1) and \(H_0\le A_0\) imply \(q\le Q_{A_0}\), and \(j\ge1\) gives \(\delta>1/(Q_{A_0}+3)\).
Finally \(\Delta=0\) forces \(E=0\) by the parity inverse, so the [even-descendant theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v294.md#statement) gives \(\delta>1/327\).
This proves the uniform bounds for both classes.
