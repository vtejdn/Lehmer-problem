# The first odd coefficient of a sparse Pisot trace numerator must occur at sublinear degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on all sparse numerators on the second branch

Let \(q\to\infty\) along positive-constant Pisot inputs on branch \(j=2\).
Write \(C\) for their [trace numerators](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace), \(h=\deg C\), and \(\Pi(z)=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose \(\Pi\) has at most \(s\) nonzero terms for a fixed \(s\).
Eventually it has an odd coefficient, and the least index \(D\) of such a coefficient satisfies \(D/h\to0\).
Arbitrary even coefficients can precede the first odd one.
In particular, for the two Chebyshev terms \(C(x)=2U_h(x/2)\pm U_{h-d}(x/2)\) with \(1\le d\le h\), every such sequence must satisfy
\[
                          d\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad d/h\longrightarrow0.
\]
The [fixed-offset theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v10.md#statement) gives the first restriction, and the lift \(2\pm z^d+z^{2h+2-d}(\mp1-2z^d)\) identifies \(D=d\) for the second.
The argument below gives a finite-prefix theorem for signed circle measures independently of the Pisot construction.

<a id="prefix"></a>
## The integral prefix before a half-integral coefficient

Let \(\omega\) be a symmetric real signed measure on the unit circle of mass one, with \(j_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega\) for \(l>0\) and \(J(z)=1+\sum_{l>0} j_lz^l\).
There are absolute \(c,\delta>0\) and \(D_0\) with the following property.
Suppose \(D\ge D_0\), the negative part has mass at most \(\delta\), and
\[
 j_l\in\mathbb Z\ (0<l<D),\qquad j_D\in \tfrac12+\mathbb Z,\qquad
 j_l\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z\ (D<l<2D).
\]
Then \(J\) agrees through degree \(\lfloor cD\rfloor\) with one of
\[
             1,\qquad 1+\epsilon z^r,\qquad (1-\epsilon z^r)^{-1},
                 \qquad \epsilon=\pm1,\quad 1\le r\le\lfloor cD\rfloor.
\]
The index \(r\) can vary between inputs.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Positive measures on the torus generated by the tested indices

Take a sequence with \(D\to\infty\), negative mass tending to zero, and one or two positive integer indices \(n_1,n_2=o(D)\), omitting the second for a single index.
Let \(t\in\{1,2\}\) be the number of indices.
Extract so that for each \(a\in\mathbb Z^t\), \(a\cdot n\) is either eventually zero or eventually nonzero.
The eventually zero relations form a saturated subgroup, so the quotient \(\Gamma\) is free abelian of rank at least one.
Push \(2\omega^+\) to the torus by \(v\mapsto (v^{n_1},\dots,v^{n_t})\) and take a weak limit \(\mu\).
It has mass two and is supported on the torus dual to \(\Gamma\), since every relation eventually holds.
Its coefficient at each nonzero character \(a\) is an integer, the eventual value of \(j_{|a\cdot n|}\), since \(|a\cdot n|=o(D)\) and removing the negative part changes all moments by \(o(1)\).

No nonzero integer Laurent polynomial on this torus can vanish \(\mu\)-almost everywhere.
To see this, use a primitive coefficient vector for such a polynomial \(P\) and choose lifts of its finitely many distinct characters to \(\mathbb Z^t\).
After substitution along the indices \(n\), its exponents are distinct eventually, and a monomial shift makes them nonnegative of span \(o(D)\).
Let \(s_0\) be the largest exponent with odd coefficient in the shifted polynomial \(P_n\).
Every index in the moment of \(v^{D-s_0}P_n(v)\) lies strictly between zero and \(2D\).
Terms below \(s_0\) have integer moments against \(2\omega\), the term at \(s_0\) has half-odd-integral moment, and terms above it have even coefficients multiplying half-integers.
Thus the modulus of the integral is at least \(1/2\).
Replacing \(2\omega\) by \(2\omega^+\) changes it by \(o(1)\), since the coefficient sum in absolute value is fixed.
Cauchy–Schwarz and the convergence of the masses to two give \(\int |P|^2\,d\mu\ge1/8\).
The monomial shift disappears upon taking squared moduli.

<a id="classification"></a>
## Classifying the mass-two Fourier coefficients

The integer Fourier coefficients of \(\mu\) form an integer-valued Fourier–Stieltjes transform on the discrete group \(\Gamma\).
By the [locally compact integer transform theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes), they are a finite integer linear combination of indicators of subgroup cosets.
Under inverse Fourier transform the singleton cosets give a Laurent polynomial density \(f\) with integer coefficients against Haar probability.
Each other coset gives a character times Haar probability on the annihilator of a nonzero subgroup of \(\Gamma\).
That annihilator is a proper Haar-null subgroup of the dual torus.
The resulting decomposition is the Lebesgue decomposition, so both \(f\) and the remaining singular measure are nonnegative.
Moreover \(f\) is real and symmetric by the symmetry of \(\mu\).
If \(f=0\), the product of \(1-X^a\) over one nonzero \(a\) from each of the finitely many subgroups would be a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial vanishing on the support of \(\mu\).
The preceding argument therefore gives \(f\ne0\).
Its mean is a positive integer at most two.

At mean two there is no singular part.
The [mean-two classification](Yin_C_G0403_v1.md#integer-mean-two-classification) gives \(f=2\) or \(f=2+\epsilon(X^a+X^{-a})\) for a single \(a\ne0\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Indeed in a basis of \(\Gamma\) one can specialize to one circle by an integer vector separating the finitely many exponents including zero, preserving nonnegativity, integrality and every coefficient.
At mean one every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of \(f\) has modulus strictly less than one, by the strict triangle inequality on the open set where \(f>0\).
Thus \(f=1\).
The remaining singular probability has integer Fourier coefficients and they are zero or signs.
The set of characters with modulus-one moment is a subgroup \(H\), since each such character is almost surely a constant sign for that probability.
The signs on \(H\) form a homomorphism into \(\{\pm1\}\), and outside \(H\) the coefficients vanish.
Consequently in every case the nonzero-frequency coefficients lie in \(\{0,1,-1\}\).
If they are nonzero at two distinct nonopposite characters, the subgroup alternative applies.

<a id="local"></a>
## From one or two indices to a whole interval

Uniformly for sufficiently large \(D\), sufficiently small negative mass and some absolute \(0<c<1/4\), put \(L=\lfloor cD\rfloor\).
Then \(|j_r|\le1\) for \(1\le r\le L\).
For any two indices \(1\le r<s\le L\) with \(j_rj_s\ne0\) one also has
\[
                 j_{s-r}=j_{s+r}=j_rj_s,\qquad j_{2r}=1.
\]
If no uniform choices existed, choose violations with \(D\ge i\), negative mass at most \(1/i\), and tested indices at most \(D/i\), taking a subsequence of a fixed type of violation.
Apply the preceding torus classification to those one or two indices.
In the two-index case the tested characters are distinct and nonopposite in \(\Gamma\), because \(0<r<s\) at every input.
The signs agree with their limiting coefficients eventually by integrality, including those of the sum, difference and double, which are also positive \(o(D)\).
Both claimed rules follow.

If at most one coefficient is nonzero on \([1,L]\), the constant or polynomial model in the prefix statement applies.
Otherwise let \(r\) be the first nonzero index and \(\epsilon=j_r\).
Repeated subtraction of \(r\) from any larger nonzero index shows that every nonzero index is divisible by \(r\), by minimality.
In particular \(2r\le L\), and \(j_{2r}=1\).
Applying the sum identity to \(r,kr\) successively for \(k\ge2\) gives \(j_{kr}=\epsilon^k\) at every multiple through \(L\).
This proves the asserted prefix agreement.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Applying the prefix to a sparse trace numerator

Write \(B^\sharp=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\) for the monic trace denominator lift, so \(2B^\sharp=\Pi J_q\) with \(J_q=2/K_q\).
Its [signed measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) has negative mass \(1/[2(q-1)]\).
We have \(h\to\infty\) by \(q-1\mid C(2)\le2\cdot4^h\).
If \(\Pi\) were even coefficientwise along a subsequence, so would be \(C\), by descending from the highest trace power in the identity modulo two.
Then \(C/2\) would be monic with all roots in \((-2,2)\), and the primitive nonmonic part in the [trace multiplier theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) would be empty, against the theorem with multiplier one.
Here \(\theta-q\to0\) follows from the [fixed-branch derivative estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).

With \(D\) the first odd index, \(J_q=2B^\sharp/\Pi\) has the prefix hypotheses above.
Indeed write \(\Pi/2=E+z^D O/2\) with integral polynomials \(E,O\), \(E(0)=1\) and \(O(0)\) odd.
The expansion \(1/E-z^D O/(2E^2)\) for \(2/\Pi\) is exact through degree \(2D-1\), and \(B^\sharp(0)=1\).
Suppose \(D\ge\eta h\) on a subsequence for some \(\eta>0\).
The prefix theorem supplies \(J_*=V/U\) agreeing with \(J_q\) through a positive fraction of \(h\), with each of \(U,V\) equal to one or a unit binomial of degree at most \(cD\).
Every \(J_*\) is the Herglotz transform of a positive measure of mass one, also when \(r\) varies.
For the polynomial model this follows from its nonnegative boundary real part, and for the inverse binomial it follows by averaging one and \((1+\epsilon z^r)/(1-\epsilon z^r)\).

The two cutoffs in the [sparse-prefix estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) therefore apply to
\[
                2UB^\sharp-V\Pi=U\Pi(J_q-J_*).
\]
Here \(\|\Pi\|_\infty\) is bounded in terms of \(s\) by the [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) since \(M(\Pi)=2\), and \(\|U\|_\infty,\|V\|_\infty\le2\).
The strictly positive coefficients of \(J_q-J_*\) are moments of measures of uniformly bounded variation and vanish through the common prefix.
For clarity, take the cutoff degree larger than the degrees on the left, hence \(O(h)\) since \(D\le2h+2\).
The first cutoff returns a polynomial of bounded \(L^1\) norm agreeing with \(J_q-J_*\) through three times that degree because the length of the zero prefix is comparable to \(h\).
After multiplication by \(U\Pi\) the second cutoff removes the tail exactly, leaving a bounded \(L^1\) norm for the left side.
Thus \(\|UB^\sharp\|_1\) is bounded and the [prime-grid support bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) gives boundedly many nonzero terms.
The binomial \(U\), when present, is cyclotomic up to sign.
Hence \(M(UB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one by the [delay-one return](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), contradicting the fixed-support gap there.
This proves \(D/h\to0\).
