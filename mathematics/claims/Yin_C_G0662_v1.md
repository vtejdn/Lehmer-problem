## Statement

    Theta(u)=u(X)^5/u(chi(X)),

    Phi(u,v)=ell(N_high(v))-(4/5)ell(u), Pi=Phi/2.

### M1: actual complete distributions and their completed limits

## Argument

### MD1: exact whole-source ratios before completion

Keep phi=X^2+X+1, chi=Xphi^2 and B_n as in C659.
For positive odd n and dyadic N>=2 define the rational function

    F_(n,N)=product_(a=0)^4 B_(n+2Na)/B_(5n),
    T_(n,N)=F_(n,N)/F_(n,2N).                            (MD1)

Exactly one numerator index n+2Na is divisible by5, since2N is invertible modulo5.
Every B_j is squarefree, has constant term1 and has exactly one phi factor when5 divides j.
Let a_0 be that unique index, and write D_(5j)=B_(5j)/phi=B_j(chi), with exact polynomial division.
Thus

    F_(n,N)=D_(n+2Na_0) product_(a!=a_0)B_(n+2Na)/D_(5n).

This is a ratio of actual complete-group units at BOTH X and phi, including when5 divides n or the quotient index of D has arbitrarily high five-adic order.
The same holds for T_(n,N), whose uncancelled numerator and denominator each have exactly one phi factor.
Every reduction belongs to the full G_r for every r, with inverses replaceable by nonnegative finite powers.
The assertion does not identify a global polynomial with a unit inverse or impose a new bound on its actual complete multiplicities or degree.

### MD2: the different low-primary branch and a completed limit

The companion matrix A=[[X,1],[1,0]] satisfies A^2+I=XA, hence

    A^N=I+X^(N/2)A^(N/2)

for dyadic N>=2.
Modulo X^N, its a-th power gives A^(Na)=I+(a modulo2)X^(N/2)A^(N/2).
Using A^j(1,1)=(B_(2j+1),B_(2j-1)) therefore yields

    B_(n+2Na)=B_n+(a modulo2)X^(N/2)B_(n+N) modulo X^N.

There are two odd a among0,1,2,3,4, so the product of the five entries is B_n^3(B_n+X^(N/2)B_(n+N))^2=B_n^5 modulo X^N.
In particular

    F_(n,N)=B_n^5/B_(5n)=Theta(B_n)/phi modulo X^N,
    T_(n,N)=1 modulo X^N,                               (MD2)

where Theta(u)=u(X)^5/u(chi(X)) is the actual C652 endomorphism of U=1+XF_2[[X]].
All denominators in this low-primary calculation are units; phi is not a low-primary nonunit.

At the high primary, the Laurent calculation in MD4 below proves

    F_(n,N)=X^(-2) modulo phi^(2N-1).

Consequently, along dyadic N tending to infinity, the actual complete ratios converge in the product completion to

    g_n=(Theta(B_n)/phi, X^(-2)) in closure(G),
    g_1=(phi^(-1),X^(-2)),
    g_n/g_1=(Theta(B_n),1).                              (MD3)

The high inverse X^(-2) is used only where X is a unit.
The two components refer to the X-adic and phi-adic completions, respectively; no global inverse of X is claimed.

### MD3: a uniform low-only completion interface

By adopted C654, finite rational products of all B_n are dense in U, and C652 proves that Theta(U)=ker ell is closed.
Continuity of Theta and MD3 give

    (ker ell,1) subset closure(G).

The order-three high residue must be separated before taking two-adic powers.

    (u, <X>^(-2ell(u))) in closure(G) for every u in U.    (MD4)

The powers can be approximated by nonnegative integers on the already cubed unit g_1^3.

### MD4: exact Laurent distribution and paid division precision

### MD5: all five source classes and the exact annular condition

Let b in{0,1,2,3,4} be uniquely specified by n=2Nb modulo5, so a_0=q_0^b.
Write epsilon_n=(n-1)/2 modulo2 and put

    p_b=E_0(a_0,q_0),
    d_b=a_0 partial_a E_0(a_0,q_0),
    e_b=a_0^2 partial_a^[2] E_0(a_0,q_0).

The second derivative here is the second Hasse derivative, namely the coefficient of u^2 in E_0(a+u,q_0).
Since a=a_0(1+s+epsilon_n s^2+O(s^3)) and

    D=s+epsilon_n s^2+epsilon_n s^3+O(s^4),
    D^(-1)=s^(-1)+epsilon_n+O(s^2),

the exact required expansion is

    E_0(a,q_0)/D=p_b/s+d_b+epsilon_n p_b+e_b s+O(s^2).
                                                               (MD8)

There is no s coefficient in D^(-1); the two epsilon_n d_b contributions cancel.
Set c=q_0+q_0^4, a nontrivial cube root in F_4 with trace1.
Using1+q_0+q_0^2+q_0^3+q_0^4=0, all five cases are

| b | p_b | d_b | e_b | Tr_(F_4/F_2)e_b |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | c | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 | c | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 | q_0^3+q_0^4 | c | 1 |
| 3 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 1 | q_0+q_0^2 | c | 1 |

This is a full symbolic residue-class calculation, not a numerical source-order cutoff.
For direct checking, after reducing q_0^5=1 one has

    E_0=a(q_0+q_0^3)+a^2(q_0+q_0^2)
                      +a^3(q_0^3+q_0^4)+a^4(q_0^2+q_0^4),
    d_b=a_0(q_0+q_0^3)+a_0^3(q_0^3+q_0^4),
    e_b=a_0^2(q_0+q_0^2)+a_0^3(q_0^3+q_0^4).

Let T be ANY finite product of T_(n,N) with arbitrary integer exponents c_n.
Products of two errors in MD7 have order at least4N-2>=2N+2, so the displayed first corrections add with exponents reduced modulo2, including for negative exponents.
Every T already equals1 modulo X^N by MD2, while its high component equals1 modulo phi^(2N) if and only if

    P=sum_n(c_n modulo2)p_(b(n))=0.                      (MD9)

Thus MD9 is exactly the condition T in Gamma_N.
When it holds, the sum of brackets in MD7 is regular, so k_N=1 modulo phi^2 can now legitimately be removed for the annular coefficient h=(T-1)/chi^N modulo phi^2.
The constant term belongs to F_4: the only two non-F_4 entries d_2,d_4 occur with total even parity, and d_2+d_4=1.
The coefficient of s is E=sum_n(c_n modulo2)e_(b(n)), always in F_4.
Since D_X=X d/dX corresponds to z d/dz and D_Xs=1+s, the derivative of that regular coefficient has residue E.
Equivalently s=xi^2(xi+1)phi+O(phi^2)=rho^2phi+O(phi^2), rho=xi+xi^(-1), and rho^3=1.
Teichmuller constants in F_4 have zero derivative.
Therefore the adopted C659 functional satisfies

    J_N(h)=Tr_(F_4/F_2)E=P=0.                            (MD10)

The N=2 endpoint is included because k_N was removed only after the pole vanished; both error inequalities used above hold there.
