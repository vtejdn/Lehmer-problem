# Hyperbolic reciprocal units with trivial cyclic quotients through logarithmic periods

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bounded-measure family with stable power degrees

There is an absolute \(c>0\) and a sequence of reciprocal algebraic units \(\alpha\) of degrees \(d\to\infty\) for which every positive power has degree \(d\), every conjugate lies off the unit circle, and
\[
       8<M(\alpha)\le57,\qquad
       \mathbb Z[\alpha]/(\alpha^D-1)=0
                  \quad (1\le D\le c\log(2d)).
\]
In particular all the [cyclic characteristics](Yin_C_G0345_v599.md#period) in this range equal one.
Bounded Mahler measure, hyperbolicity, reciprocity and stability of the power degrees together do not force growth of the short-period characteristics.
Here the measures stay strictly above eight, so growth at hypothetical records whose measures approach one remains open.

<a id="factor"></a>
## Isolating a reciprocal irreducible factor

For an integer \(J\ge2\) put
\[
       P_J(X)=(X^J-1)^2(X^2+12X+1)-X^{J+1}.
\]
It is monic reciprocal of degree \(2J+2\) with constant term one.
On writing \(X=e^{i\theta}\), its value divided by \(X^{J+1}\) equals
\[
          (2\cos(J\theta)-2)(12+2\cos\theta)-1 .
\]
This expression lies between \(-57\) and \(-1\), so there are no unit-circle roots and \(M(P_J)\le57\) by the circle integral.
On \(|X|=8\) one has \(|X^2+12X+1|\ge96-65=31\) and \(31(8^J-1)^2>8^{J+1}\).
Rouché's theorem therefore gives the same number of roots inside that circle as for \((X^J-1)^2(X^2+12X+1)\).
The latter has just one root outside, namely \(-6-\sqrt{35}\), and no root on the circle.
Thus \(P_J\) has a unique root outside, counted with multiplicity.
Denote it by \(\alpha_J\) and let \(Q_J\) be its monic minimal polynomial.
All factors of \(P_J\) are units up to sign at their constant term and have measure at least one.
If the monic reciprocal of \(Q_J\) were a distinct factor, then \(M(P_J)\ge M(Q_J)^2>64\), contradicting the bound 57.
Hence \(Q_J\) is reciprocal, with \(8<M(\alpha_J)\le57\).
Every conjugate in the fiber above \(\alpha_J^q\) has modulus \(|\alpha_J|>8\).
Uniqueness outside that circle gives fiber size one, so \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_J^q)=\mathbb Q(\alpha_J)\) for every \(q\ge1\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Size of the factor and simultaneous periods

Write \(d=\deg Q_J,\ B(X)=X^2+12X+1\).
The defining equation gives
\[
       0<|B(\alpha_J)|=
       \frac{|\alpha_J|^{1-J}}{|1-\alpha_J^{-J}|^2}
                  \le 2\cdot 8^{1-J}.
\]
The norm of \(B(\alpha_J)\) is a nonzero rational integer.
At all other conjugates \(\gamma\) one has \(|B(\gamma)|\le14\max(1,|\gamma|)^2\).
Multiplication gives \(1\le 2\cdot8^{1-J}14^{d-1}57^2\).
Thus \(d\gg J\) for large \(J\), and \(d\le 2J+2\).

For \(D\mid J\) the polynomial \(P_J(X)\) reduces to the unit \(-X^{J+1}\) modulo \(X^D-1\).
The factor \(Q_J\) is consequently a unit in the same quotient, proving \(\mathbb Z[\alpha_J]/(\alpha_J^D-1)=0\).
Choose \(y=2^s\to\infty\) and \(J=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)\).
To bound its size, write \(J_t=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,t)\).
Every prime gains at most one factor in \(J_{2t}/J_t\); when it does, a power of that prime lies in \((t,2t]\).
The corresponding term in the factorial valuation of \(\binom{2t}{t}\) is one, and all other terms are nonnegative.
Hence \(J_{2t}/J_t\) divides \(\binom{2t}{t}\le4^t\).
Iteration gives \(J\le4^{y-1}\), so \(d\le2J+2\) implies \(y\ge c\log(2d)\) with an absolute positive \(c\).
All periods through \(y\) divide this one \(J\), proving the assertion.
