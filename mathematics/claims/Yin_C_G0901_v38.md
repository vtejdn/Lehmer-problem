# A vanishing quadratic invariant after all positive powers already preserve the input field

<a id="statement"></a>
## Reciprocal units with every radial logarithm of order the inverse degree

Let \(T_n\) be the monic integer polynomial with \(T_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\) for \(n\ge1\).
For \(n=3^j\), \(j\ge1\), set
\[
    P_n(X)=X^{2n}-3X^{n+1}-15X^n-3X^{n-1}+1 .
\]
This is an irreducible reciprocal unit polynomial.
Its unique conjugate of largest modulus is positive, and for that root \(\alpha_n\) every positive power generates \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_n)\).
In particular no two distinct roots have torsion quotient.
For every root \(z\),
\[
   \operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)\le n\,|\log|z||\le\operatorname{arcosh}(27/2).
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]

The twist \(\beta_n=e^{2\pi i/12}\alpha_n\) is in the [unramified quadratic norm-one family](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#statement) with \(m(\beta_n)=4m(\alpha_n)\), degree \(8n\), and \(r_2(F_n)=2n\) for its trace field \(F_n\).
Let \(D_n\) be its [positive archimedean invariant](Yin_C_G0901_v34.md#statement), and let \(\mu_j\), for \(j=1,2\), be the arc-length mean of \(g(\theta)^j\), where
\[
             g(\theta)=\operatorname{arcosh}\big((15+6\cos\theta)/2\big).
\]
As \(n\) grows on the given sequence,
\[
                 m(\beta_n)\to4\mu_1>0,\qquad nD_n\to8\mu_2 .
\]
Thus \(D_n\to0\) even on the twelfth-root twists of inputs with all powers generating the original field.
In these examples \(r_2(F_n)D_n-m(\beta_n)^2\to16(\mu_2-\mu_1^2)>0\).

<a id="field"></a>
## Irreducibility over the rationals and the cyclotomic intersection

The degree-\(n\) trace polynomial is \(R_n(U)=T_n(U)-3U-15\).
The identities \(T_{ab}=T_a\circ T_b\) and \(T_3(U)=U^3-3U\) give \(T_n(U)\equiv U^n\bmod3\).
Since \(n\) is odd, \(T_n(0)=0\) and \(R_n(0)=-15\).
Thus \(R_n\) is Eisenstein at three.
For a root \(t\) its field \(H=\mathbb Q(t)\) has degree \(n\), and
\[
           N_{H/\mathbb Q}(t^2-4)=R_n(2)R_n(-2)=(-19)(-11)=209.
\]
This is neither a rational square nor three times a rational square.
Consequently \(K=H(\sqrt{t^2-4})\) has degree two over \(H\), and the roots of \(X^2-tX+1\) generate \(K\) over \(\mathbb Q\) since their sums with their inverses recover \(t\).
Their rational minimum is \(X^nR_n(X+X^{-1})=P_n\) by degrees.
Also \(\sqrt3\notin H\) by odd degree.
If \(\sqrt3\in K\), equality of the two quadratic extensions would give \(t^2-4=3v^2\) for some \(v\in H\), contradicting the square class of its norm since \(n\) is odd.

<a id="radial"></a>
## A unique dominant conjugate and full power fields

For \(A=2\cosh x\), \(x\ge0\), \(T_n(A)=2\cosh(nx)\) is increasing with derivative at least \(n^2\) on \([2,\infty)\), by \(\sinh(nx)\ge n\sinh x\) and the endpoint limit.
Thus \(T_n(A)=15+3A\) has a unique solution \(A_*>2\), and \(A_*<4\) since \(T_n(4)\ge T_3(4)=52>27\).
There is a positive real root \(r_*>1\) of \(P_n\) with \(r_*+r_*^{-1}=A_*\).
For any root \(z\) put \(r=|z|\), \(A=r+r^{-1}\), \(W=z^n+z^{-n}=15+3(z+z^{-1})\).
The identities \(|v+v^{-1}\pm2|=|v\pm1|^2/|v|\) at \(v=z^n\) give
\[
                 T_n(A)=\frac{|W-2|+|W+2|}{2}\le15+3A.
\]
The triangle inequalities in the last expression are strict unless \(z\) is positive real, since \(15-2>0\) and equality forces the terms \(z,z^{-1}\) to have the common argument zero.
Therefore \(A\le A_*\), and a root with \(|z|=r_*\) must be \(z=r_*\).
Take \(\alpha_n=r_*\) and use this real embedding of \(K\).
An embedding of \(K\) fixing any positive power of \(\alpha_n\) must fix \(\alpha_n\) by uniqueness at its modulus.
It follows that every positive power generates \(K\), excluding all torsion quotients of distinct conjugates by injectivity on embeddings.
Since \(A<4\), the lower bound \(T_n(A)\ge|W|\ge15-3A\) and the preceding upper bound give \(3\le T_n(A)\le27\).
The equality \(T_n(A)=2\cosh(n|\log r|)\) proves (1).

<a id="limit"></a>
## The trace signature and the limiting costs

Let \(E=\mathbb Q(e^{2\pi i/12})\).
Because \(K\) is contained in \(\mathbb R\) in its chosen embedding, \(K\cap E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\); the square-class calculation shows this intersection is \(\mathbb Q\).
Inversion on \(K\) and conjugation on \(E\) therefore agree on their intersection.
The [generation and compatibility proof](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#involution) now gives the claimed twisted generator with degree \(8n\) and total measure \(4m(\alpha_n)\).
All \(2n\) original roots are off the circle by (1), so the [signature count](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#basis) gives \(r_2(F_n)=2n\).
If \(b_z=\log|z|\) at the roots of \(P_n\), then \(D_n=4\sum_{P_n(z)=0}\sinh^2 b_z\).
Indeed in the archimedean formula each nonreal pair of \(F_n\) accounts for four embeddings of its quadratic extension with equal \(|b_z|\), while each original root has four lifts of unchanged modulus.

The empirical distribution of the arguments of the \(2n\) original roots tends to uniform arc length.
For every fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(n>k+1\), the first \(k\) coefficients below the leading term of \(P_n\) vanish, so Newton's identities give \(\sum_z z^k=0\).
Replacing \(z^k\) by \((z/|z|)^k\) in its normalized sum costs at most \(e^{k\operatorname{arcosh}(27/2)/n}-1\) by (1).
The negative Fourier coefficients follow by conjugation, and trigonometric approximation gives the assertion for every continuous test.

Writing \(\theta=\arg z\), the bounds on \(b_z\) give \(W=15+6\cos\theta+O(1/n)\) uniformly over the roots.
Using \((|W-2|+|W+2|)/4\) and the continuity of \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) on compact intervals bounded away from one gives \(n|b_z|-g(\theta)\to0\) uniformly.
Since \(m(\alpha_n)=\tfrac12\sum_z|b_z|\), the first-moment limit follows.
The uniform bound on \(n|b_z|\) also gives \(n^2\sinh^2 b_z-(n b_z)^2\to0\) uniformly.
The second-moment limit follows by the same averaging, and \(g\) is continuous and nonconstant, so \(\mu_2>\mu_1^2\).
