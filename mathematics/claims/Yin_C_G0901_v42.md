# Rational maps of nearly full degree and the automorphisms of small-measure fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound uniform in the rational coefficients

Let \(x\ne0\) be algebraic of degree \(d\), and \(y=R(x)\) finite and nonzero, with \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\) of degree \(t\ge1\).
Assume \(R\ne\pm X^k\) for all nonzero integers \(k\), and \(m(x),m(y)\le M\) for a fixed \(M>0\), where \(m\) denotes total logarithmic Mahler measure.
There is a positive constant depending only on \(M\) such that
\[
                       t\gg_M\frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{212}} .                \tag{1}
\]
In the multiplicatively dependent case one can use \(t\gg_M d\,b(d)\), where \(b(d)=(\log\log(5d))^3/(\log(2d))^4\).
This includes torsion inputs or outputs.
The [elementary good-reduction estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v41.md#degree) gives an alternative growing bound without height-product theorems.

There is also an intrinsic restriction on the fields of bounded-measure generators.
For all sufficiently large \(d\) in terms of \(M\), every automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) sends a nontorsion \(x\) with \(m(x)\le M\) to \(\xi x^{\pm1}\) for a root of unity \(\xi\).
If \(x\) is reciprocal and every positive power generates \(\mathbb Q(x)\), the only automorphisms are the identity and inversion of \(x\).

<a id="independent"></a>
## Independent inputs and outputs

Put \(d_y=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\).
The inclusion \(\mathbb Q(y)\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\) and the nonzero equation \(A(x)-yB(x)=0\), for a reduced expression \(R=A/B\), give \(d\le t d_y\).
For independent \(x,y\), the [rank-two height product theorem](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) at joint degree \(d\) gives
\[
                    m(x)m(y)=dd_y h(x)h(y)
                         \gg \frac{d_y}{(\log(3d))^{212}} .
\]
Here \(h\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
This proves (1) in this case.
It also shows that two independent elements generating the same field and both having bounded total measure cannot occur at unbounded degrees.

<a id="dependent"></a>
## One generator modulo torsion and its relative height

First let both coordinates be nontorsion and multiplicatively dependent.
Choose coprime integers \(a,b\) with \(b>0\), \(a\ne0\), such that \(y^b=\xi x^a\) with \(\xi\) a root of unity in \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\).
In the group modulo torsion choose integers \(u,v\) with \(bu+av=1\) and put \(z=x^u y^v\).
Then \(z^b\) equals \(x\) modulo torsion and \(z^a\) equals \(y\) modulo torsion.
Thus \(z\) is nontorsion with
\[
                         b h(z)=h(x),\qquad |a|h(z)=h(y).
\]
Let \(f=[\mathbb Q(\xi):\mathbb Q]\).
Since \(z\in K\), its degree \(D\) over the cyclotomic base \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) is at most \(d/f\).
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) implies \(D h(z)\gg b(d)\); here \(\min_{1\le D\le d}b(D)\gg b(d)\) by positivity and eventual monotonicity as in the [relative-degree comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#fiber).
It follows that
\[
                  f b\ll m(x)/b(d),\qquad
                  f |a|\ll (d/d_y)m(y)/b(d).
\]

The equation \(R(x)^b=\xi x^a\) supplies a polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) of degree at most \(bt+|a|\) vanishing at \(x\), by clearing the denominator and a possible negative power of \(x\).
If it were identically zero, \(R\) would have no zeros or poles in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
Then \(R=cX^k\) with \(c\in\mathbb Q^\times\), \(kb=a\) and \(c^b=\xi\), so \(c=\pm1\), contrary to the exclusion.
Thus \(d\le f(bt+|a|)\).
Together with \(d/d_y\le t\) the preceding bounds give \(d\,b(d)\ll t(m(x)+m(y))\).

If \(x\) is torsion and \(y\) is nontorsion then \(y\) belongs to the cyclotomic field \(K\).
Applying the relative bound over \(K\) with relative degree one gives \(h(y)\gg1\), whence \(d_y\ll_M1\) and \(d\ll_Mt\).
If \(y\) is torsion and \(x\) is nontorsion, use \(\mathbb Q(y)\) as the abelian base for \(x\), of relative degree \(d/d_y\le t\), to obtain \(t\,m(x)/d\gg b(d)\).
If both coordinates are torsion, all \(d\) embeddings give distinct torsion points on the graph \(A(X)-YB(X)=0\).
This graph is irreducible in the Laurent ring by coprimality and linearity in \(Y\).
A torsion translate graph would be \(Y=cX^k\) with \(c\) a rational root of unity, since its projection has degree one.
Otherwise the [Beukers–Smyth torsion point estimate](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) gives \(d\le22t\), using the containing Newton rectangle of width \(t\) and height one.
This accounts also for a monomial graph with nontorsion multiplier, which has no torsion point.
The dependent bounds imply (1) since \(b(d)\gg(\log(3d))^{-212}\).

<a id="fields"></a>
## The automorphism groups before and after the cyclotomic twist

For any automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\), the element \(y=\sigma(x)\) has \(d_y=d\) and \(m(y)=m(x)\).
The independent-case height product then fails for large \(d\) at bounded measure, so \(x,y\) are multiplicatively dependent.
For nontorsion \(x\), equality of their positive heights makes the coprime exponents in the dependence satisfy \(|a|=b=1\).
Therefore \(y=\xi x^{\pm1}\).
If all powers generate and \(x^{-1}\) is conjugate to \(x\), no two distinct conjugates have torsion quotient, since raising to the order of the quotient would identify their embeddings on a generator.
This gives exactly the two indicated automorphisms.

More specifically, let \(\delta\) be a nontorsion reciprocal unit with all its powers generating \(K=\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
Write \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\), \(N\) divisible by twelve, \(L=KE\), \(\beta=\zeta_N\delta\).
Assume the inverters on \(K\) and \(E\) agree on \(K\cap E\), so that \(\beta\) generates \(L\), and \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})\) has \(L=F(i)\) with nontrivial inverter \(\tau\).
These are the [compatible twists](Yin_C_G0901_v32.md#involution), also used in the [arbitrary-conductor reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v33.md#generator).
For \([L:\mathbb Q]\) sufficiently large in terms only of a bound for \(m(\beta)\),
\[
       \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(L)
          =\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\times\langle\tau\rangle,\qquad
       \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(F)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(E/(K\cap E)).
\]
Indeed each \(\sigma\) sends \(\beta\) to \(\xi\beta^{\pm1}\).
For a multiple \(a\) of \(N\) and the order of \(\xi\), \(\beta^a=\delta^a\) generates \(K\), so \(\sigma\) restricts there to the identity or the inverter.
The kernel on \(K\) is the Galois group of \(L/K\), identified by restriction with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/(K\cap E))\).
The involution \(\tau\) commutes with this kernel on \(K\) and on the abelian field \(E\), hence on \(L\), and is nontrivial on \(K\).
Thus the product is direct.
Every automorphism of \(F\) extends to \(F(i)\) fixing \(i\), since \(X^2+1\) is irreducible over \(F\).
Conversely every automorphism of \(L\) preserves \(F=L^\tau\) by centrality, and the kernel on \(F\) is exactly \(\langle\tau\rangle\).
Taking this quotient proves the second formula without assuming either \(F\) or \(L\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
