# Uniform comparison without a lower bound for any band coefficient

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound by the number of terms and the separation of the bands

Let \(m,d,n\) be positive integers, \(D=n/d\), and let \(F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^m a_j(X)Y^j\ne0\) have complex Laurent coefficients supported in \([-d,d]\).
Let \(K\) count the nonzero monomials of \(F\), and write \(I=m_2(F)\), \(\mu=m(F(X,e^{i\phi}X^n))\) for logarithmic measures with real \(\phi\).
For constants depending only on \(m\), whenever \(D\ge D_m\),
\[
                  |\mu-I|\le {C_m\over D}\{K+\log(2D)\}.                     \tag{1}
\]
This estimate allows common zeros of all coefficients, arbitrary coefficient sizes and simultaneous rescaling.
It gives uniform absolute convergence for \(d=o(\sqrt n)\) since \(K\le(m+1)(2d+1)\), and also when \(D\to\infty\) and \(K=o(D)\).
In general the square-root window is sharp in order for \(m\ge2\).
The [endpoint Taylor example](Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#statement) has \(F=Y^2-B_{n,d}(X+X^{-1})Y+1\), a positive lower limit for the discrepancy at \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\), and divergence at \(d=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor\).

For integer coefficients and \(\phi=0\), suppose the greatest common divisor of the rows in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\) has multiplicative measure one.
There are constants \(\alpha_m,\eta_m>0\) such that
\[
        D\ge D_m,\quad (K+\log(2D))/D\le\alpha_m,\quad \mu>0
                       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu\ge\eta_m .               \tag{2}
\]
In particular a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n\) is an admissible band half-width, uniformly in all coefficients and without a lower bound for their maximum modulus on the circle.
The gcd hypothesis holds for an irreducible Laurent diagonal with at least two occupied bands in this range.
A common base factor contributes the same logarithmic measure on both sides of (1).

<a id="periods"></a>
## Relative small-value estimates away from exceptional periods

Use \(d\nu=dt/(2\pi)\), \(G(t)=F(e^{it},e^{i(nt+\phi)})\), and \(A(t)\) equal to the largest of the absolute values of the real and imaginary parts \(v_s(t)\) of all the coefficients.
At their common zeros define \(V(t,v)\) arbitrarily in \([0,\sqrt2(m+1)]\), and elsewhere set \(V(t,v)=|F(e^{it},e^{iv})|/A(t)\).
The zero set of \(A\) contains at most \(2d\) points, being contained in the zeros of a nonzero coordinate.
The function \(\log A\) is integrable, since \(A\) is bounded above and bounded below by the absolute value of that nonzero coordinate.
The diagonal is a nonzero Laurent polynomial as soon as \(D>2\), because its frequency intervals are disjoint.
Thus both means lose the same finite integral when the logarithm of the baseline \(A\) is removed.

For \(0<u<1\), the [relative clipping and mesh argument](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#quadrature) gives \(O_m(D^{-1}\log(C_m/u))\) for the difference of the clipped means and \(O_m(u^{1/m})\) for the independent clipping loss.
Indeed zeros of \(A\) add only \(O(d)\) endpoints to the monotonicity partition for fixed \(v\).
A change of the assigned function value at such a point costs at most twice the clipped range in variation.
The fixed-fiber estimate uses the coefficients divided by \(A(t)\) only when it is positive.

For the diagonal use the [whole-circle chart count](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#charts).
Flag each fast period meeting a common zero as well, adding at most \(4d\) periods.
For each chart choose a point with positive \(A\) where that coordinate attains its maximum among the coordinates at the point, omitting charts for which no such point exists.
To count its levels globally one only needs the cleared trigonometric polynomials to be nonzero.
At the chosen point the chart denominator is nonzero, so at most one endpoint in any allowed threshold range can make the expression zero there.
This gives \(O_m(d)\) threshold points regardless of how small the chosen value is.
Away from common zeros the original chart count applies because all ratios cannot stay bounded up to a denominator zero unless every coordinate tends to zero there.
The piece on which the first excessive normalized derivative has fixed sign has length \(O_m(1/n)\), as its preceding derivative has bounded range.
We obtain \(B_0=O_m(d)\) flagged periods in all.
By the [frozen-coefficient derivative test](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#finite-type), on each other period the relative length where \(V(t,nt+\phi)\le w\) is \(O_m(w^{1/m})\), \(0<w\le1\).
In particular there is a fixed \(c_m>0\) such that at least half of each good period has \(|G|\ge c_m A\).
All these estimates concern ratios and use no absolute anchor for \(G\).

<a id="cover"></a>
## Intervals containing a fixed fraction of good periods

Label the \(n\) equal periods in linear order after cutting the circle at a period boundary.
If \(B_0=0\) the good-period bound suffices.
Otherwise take \(D_m\) large enough that \(B_0/n<1/4\).
Start from the full list.
At any list with at most one quarter flagged entries split it into two consecutive lists of lengths \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil\) if its length \(N\ge2\).
Stop at any list with flagged fraction greater than \(1/4\), and discard singleton lists with zero flags.
The stopped intervals are disjoint and cover every flag, and each has at least one quarter good entries.
Indeed its parent had at most a quarter flagged, and the ratio of parent to child length is at most three.
Its flagged fraction is therefore at most \(3/4\).
Also the sum of their lengths in the real parameter is at most \(4 B_0(2\pi/n)=O_m(d/n)\) because each has more than a quarter flags.
This argument works for every \(n\).

<a id="propagation"></a>
## Propagating the relative amplitude through each stopped interval

Let \(J\) be such an interval and \(A_J=\max_{t\in J} A(t)>0\).
On a measurable subset \(S\) of at least \(|J|/8\) there holds \(|G|\ge c_m A\), by the good periods inside \(J\).
Pick one complex coefficient \(a_j\) whose modulus somewhere in \(J\) is at least \(A_J\).
Its evaluation on the circle is an exponential polynomial with at most \(K\) terms.
Apply the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) to \(a_j(e^{it})\) on \(J,S\).
All exponents are imaginary, so the term involving their real parts is one.
Since \(|a_j|\le\sqrt2 A\), we obtain
\[
                    \sup_J|G|\ \ge\ e^{-C_m K} A_J .                         \tag{3}
\]
The dependence on the slow amplitude and on the length of \(J\) cancels.

The diagonal \(G\) has at most \(K\) terms.
For \(K\ge2\), a second application of the same inequality to \(G\) on \(J\) gives
\[
 {|\{t\in J:|G(t)|\le e^{-s}\sup_J|G|\}|\over |J|}
       \ \le\ \min(1,C e^{-s/(K-1)}),\qquad s\ge0 .
\]
A set of measure zero already satisfies the assertion.
Thus the integral of \(\log(\sup_J|G|/|G|)\) over \(J\) is \(O(K|J|)\) by integrating in \(s\).
By (3), and \(A(t)\le A_J\), the relative clipping loss on \(J\) is also \(O_m(K|J|)\).
This includes every flagged period and is uniform in the common dips of the coefficients.
For \(K=1\) both measures coincide directly.
The contribution from good periods outside the stopped intervals is \(O_m(u^{1/m})\) after integration in \(dw/w\).
Adding the mesh error and choosing \(u=D^{-m}\) proves (1).

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## The positive integral gap

In (2) use the same separation threshold \(D_m>2\) as in (1).
When the rows have gcd of measure one, the [horizontal component theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) and the [specialization of torsion components](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#arithmetic) give \(I\ge c_m'>0\) whenever \(\mu>0\).
For a single row its diagonal measure equals the gcd measure, so positive measure already requires two occupied rows.
When the Laurent diagonal is irreducible and at least two rows are present, disjoint frequency intervals make the quotient by the common factor a nonunit on specialization, forcing the gcd to be a unit.
With \(C_m\alpha_m\le c_m'/2\), (1) proves (2) with \(\eta_m=c_m'/2\).
Since \(K/D\le3(m+1)d^2/n\) and \(\log(2D)/D\) decreases to zero, \(d\le\epsilon_m\sqrt n\) guarantees the hypothesis for sufficiently small \(\epsilon_m>0\), using \(d\ge1\).

The coordinating researcher developed the propagation through intervals containing a definite proportion of good periods to remove the need for a lower bound on the coefficients.
