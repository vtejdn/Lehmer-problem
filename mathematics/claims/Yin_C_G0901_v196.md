# Large dyadic degrees with the exact residue period at the full modulus \(q\)

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous tests on the even side of the graph

Fix a sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, an odd prime \(\ell\nmid q\), and a positive integer \(B\).
Put \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ u=2^B,\ \theta=u\log p/\log s\).
There exist arbitrarily large degrees \(d\) and monic irreducible reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) satisfying the [pole, real-period and finite-precision tests](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#statement) and the [large local degree bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v193.md#statement).
With \(A\) the binary order and \(z\) the class of \(Z\), the same models satisfy
\[
  \operatorname{ord}_{(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)[Z]/(F)}(z)=A,\qquad
  z^{A+u}=\left(\frac{pz-q}{p-qz}\right)^u
      \quad\bmod\bigl(\operatorname{lcm}(q,2^B),F\bigr).
\]
In particular the entire even-side residue period is exactly \(v-u\) for the exponent \(v=A+u\) used at the real place.
Every dyadic conjugate has degree at least \(c d^{1/4}\), and all but \(O_{q,B}(\sqrt d)\) have degree at least \(c\sqrt d\), with absolute \(c>0\).
The sole permitted denominator prime can be chosen in advance subject to \(\ell\nmid2q\).
For any prescribed finite odd modulus \(m\) coprime to \(\ell\), taking unbounded multiples \(q\) of \(4m\) prime to \(\ell\) tests the graph modulo \(m\) on the same examples.
The [two-modulus period requirement for genuine solutions](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#statement) additionally requires the order modulo \(q+1\) to equal \(A+2u\) at these heights.
The construction makes no such assertion, and the characteristic-zero graph relation fails by the [one-prime height obstruction](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement).

<a id="practical"></a>
## A period with enough reciprocal integer divisors

Write \(J=q/2+1,\ B'=\max(B,v_2(q))\).
Take the [admissible half-degree progression](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#period) \(D_0\bmod P\) with the \(q\)-side congruence raised to \(q^{J+2+B'}\).
All prime divisors of \(P\) are at most \(q+1\).
Choose a prime \(t\) strictly between \(\max(31,q+B'+4)\) and twice that number.
As in the [large-degree construction](Yin_C_G0901_v193.md#degrees), \(t<\theta\) for all sufficiently large \(q\) uniformly in \(B\); here \(B'\le B+\log_2 q\).
Let \(a\to\infty\) in the class \(-1\bmod 2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot(t-1)\) and put \(b=a+2\).
Set \(W=2^a/(100a^2)\), and choose
\[
 m_1=10\cdot11^{j-1},\qquad m_2=3\cdot7^{k-1}
\]
as the largest respective terms at most \(W\).
Then \(m_1,m_2\asymp W\).
They are the orders of two modulo \(11^j,7^k\), respectively, since the first-prime orders are ten and three and the valuations of \(2^{10}-1,2^3-1\) at the corresponding primes are one.
The prime-power lifting calculation in the cited construction therefore applies.

Set \(N_0=15\cdot11^j7^k,\ n=N_0(2^a-1)(2^b-1)\).
The factors \(15,11^j,7^k,2^a-1,2^b-1\) are pairwise coprime by the first-prime orders and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\); also \(t\nmid n\).
The polynomial \(Z^n-1\) has a monic divisor in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) of every degree \(0,\dots,n\).
Indeed \(15\) already has this property, since its cyclotomic factor degrees are \(1,2,4,8\).
If \(m\) has this property and a prime \(r\nmid m\) satisfies \(r\le m+1\), then \(mr^e\) has this property for every \(e\ge1\). [Thompson, Lemma 4.3, p. 259](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.866)
For the sufficient direction used here, the new cyclotomic factors at each \(r\)-power level \(i\ge1\) realize all degree sums \(\varphi(r^i)h,\ 0\le h\le m\), by multiplicativity of the totient.
The earlier levels cover \([0,mr^{i-1}]\); since \(\varphi(r^i)\le m r^{i-1}+1\), the translated intervals cover \([0,mr^i]\) without gaps.
This first adjoins \(11^j,7^k\) to 15.
Next \(N_0\asymp 2^{2a}/a^4>2^b\) at sufficiently large \(a\), so all prime powers in the two remaining factors of \(n\) can be adjoined in turn.
Since \(n\) is odd, \(Z-1\) is the only odd-degree cyclotomic factor.
Every even-degree divisor chosen as a product of distinct cyclotomic factors therefore omits it and is reciprocal with constant one.

<a id="binary"></a>
## Binary factor degrees at this period

Use the [Frobenius and inverse blocks](Yin_C_G0901_v193.md#degrees) with the two new prime powers.
Let \(P_1\) be the products of a full-degree element of \(\mathbb F_{2^a}\) and a primitive \(11^j\)-th root, and let \(P_2\) be the products of a full-degree element of \(\mathbb F_{2^b}\) and a primitive \(7^k\)-th root.
Their orbit lengths are \(X=am_1,\ Y=bm_2\), which are coprime.
The mixed set now consists of \(P_1P_2\) times the primitive fifteenth roots.
The base products have degree \(L=XY\), and \(v_2(L)=1\).
Since \(\operatorname{ord}_{15}(2)=4\), the mixed set has orbit length \(2L\).
No orbit is its own inverse by projection to the odd full-degree component.
Consequently the paired blocks in the three sets have sizes \(2X,2Y,4L\).
There are at least \(2^a/(4a)\) and \(2^b/(4b)\) respective filling blocks for all large \(a\), since \(\varphi(11^j)=m_1,\ \varphi(7^k)=2m_2\).
The mixed set contains a primitive \(n\)-th root and its size divided by \(n\) tends to \((10/11)(6/7)(8/15)>1/\theta\), using the full-degree counts in the cited construction.

Choose \(S\) nearest \(n/(2\theta)\) in the class \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P\).
Use \(\lfloor(S-L)/(2L)\rfloor\) mixed blocks, including a primitive block, leaving a half-size remainder \(L\le E<3L\).
There are enough mixed blocks since \(L\asymp 2^{2a}/a^2,\ n\asymp2^{4a}/a^4\) and their density exceeds \(1/\theta\).
As in the [exact block filling](Yin_C_G0901_v193.md#fill), write \(E=Xi+Yi'\) with \(0\le i<Y,\ 0\le i'<3X\) by choosing \(Xi\equiv E\bmod Y\).
The two filling-block counts exceed \(Y,3X\) by the factor 100 in \(W\).
Lift the selected roots to a reciprocal Teichmüller product \(V_2\in\mathbb Z_2[Z]\) of degree \(2S\) dividing \(Z^n-1\), and put \(U_2=V_2(Z^t),\ A=tn,\ d=2tS\).
As \(t\nmid n\), the lifted polynomial has order \(A\) upon reduction modulo two, and already divides \(Z^A-1\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\).
The bound \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tP\) and the binary degree estimates follow exactly as in the filling argument: every root has residue degree at least \(\min(X,Y)\asymp n^{1/4}\), and only \(2tE=O_{q,B}(\sqrt d)\) preimages could have degree below \(2L\asymp\sqrt n\).
For sufficiently large \(a\), \(d\le n\).

## Combined congruences at the exact pole

Write \(q_o=q/2^{v_2(q)}\) and \(C=2^{B'}q_o=\operatorname{lcm}(q,2^B)\).
For all sufficiently large degrees \(2S<n\), so choose an integer reciprocal divisor \(V_o\mid Z^n-1\) of degree \(2S\) by the divisor result above.
Choose the coefficients of a monic reciprocal integer polynomial \(V_0\) of the same degree, with constant one, to agree with \(V_2\bmod 2^{B'}\) and \(V_o\bmod q_o\) by the Chinese remainder theorem.
When \(q_o=1\) only the dyadic congruence is required.
Then \(U_0=V_0(Z^t)\) has zero initial and final offsets through \(t-1\), and divides \(Z^A-1\) modulo \(C\) by monic polynomial division and the two component divisibilities.

Write \(M=pq,\ K=(2q+1)^2\).
Both endpoint congruences hold for \(q^dU_0(s)\equiv K\bmod CM^{J+1}\).
Indeed the strengthened degree progression gives \(p^d\equiv K\bmod q^{J+2+B'}\) and \(q^d\equiv K\bmod p^{J+1}\).
The vanishing offsets allow these pure end powers to be substituted for the evaluations at the respective moduli, since \(t>J+2+B'\).
Finally \(Cq^{J+1}\mid q^{J+2+B'}\) and \(Cq\) is prime to \(p\).
By the [pole lattice increment](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences), correction by \(C\) times a zero-prefix lattice element realizes the integer pole exactly and preserves these coefficient congruences.
The [one-prime approximation with an integral residue representative](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) works since \(\ell\nmid C\).
Use the same [archimedean pin and period envelope](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch) to obtain the full small-denominator repulsion and finite-precision test at \(2^B\), and to keep the full archimedean requirements of the period construction.
The resulting \(F\) still divides \(Z^A-1\bmod C\).
Modulo \(q\) its period divides \(A\), and reduction modulo two forces the reverse divisibility.

Modulo \(q\) the fractional linear transformation \(Y=(pz-q)/(p-qz)\) equals \(z\), since \(p\equiv1\bmod q\).
Modulo \(2^B\), the unit \(Y/z\) is congruent to one modulo two, so its \(u\)-th power equals one modulo \(2^B\).
The denominator \(p-qz\) is invertible at each component.
Together with \(z^A=1\) these give the graph equality modulo \(C\).
These congruences test all prime powers in \(q\) simultaneously while the one-prime cost remains at \(\ell\).
