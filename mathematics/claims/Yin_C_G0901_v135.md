# One delay-one construction for the entire bounded-second-iterate gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## Clearing a denominator of two

Let \(E\) range over totally real fields of degree \(u\ge2\), and let \(K/E\) range over quadratic extensions split at one real place and complex at all others.
At one chosen split embedding take \(P\in\mathcal O_K[X]\) of positive degree, leading coefficient \(2\) and nonzero constant.
Require \(P\) there to have a simple positive exterior root \(\beta>1\), all other roots strictly interior, and \(|P(z)|>|(P^\tau)^*(z)|\) on the unit circle, where \(\tau\) is the nonidentity automorphism of \(K/E\).
At every complex embedding require all the roots of \(P\) to be strictly interior.
Take either sign \(e=\pm1\) subject to the coefficient congruence
\[
                      XP+e(P^\tau)^*\equiv0\pmod {2\mathcal O_K[X]}.
\]
Then \(F=(XP+e(P^\tau)^*)/2\) is integral monic and has a unique exterior root \(R>1\) at the chosen embedding.
The rational norm \(H=N_{K/\mathbb Q}F\) is a monic integer polynomial with \(M(H)=R^2\) and \(M(M(H))\le R^4\).

A uniform positive gap above one for the measures of all these \(H\) is **equivalent** to a gap for integer polynomials along every sequence with bounded second Mahler iterates.
In particular any resolution of the real Salem gap applies to a subclass of this construction, and every canonical square endpoint in the small-measure reduction has its actual minimal polynomial represented here.
The single denominator works even for arbitrarily large coefficient degrees, half degrees and relative discriminants.

More precisely, for a fixed half polynomial \(f\) under the [reconstruction hypotheses](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#statement), let \(q\) be a positive integer.
Require an integral \(P\) of leading coefficient \(q\) with the same strict root conditions and \(qf=XP+\epsilon(P^\tau)^*\), \(\epsilon=f(0)\).
If its monic reconstruction is possible, all \(q\) work.
Otherwise exactly the even \(q\) work.
In both cases the real seed roots \(\beta\) are unbounded at each allowed \(q\), even with \(f\) fixed.

<a id="denominator"></a>
## The integral obstruction has exponent two

For fixed \(q\) the coefficient equations of the [integral reconstruction](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#integrality) have right-hand sides \(q f_i\) and leading coefficient \(q\).
All paired equations again have integral solutions.
At an even middle index write \(c=f_{b/2}\), so \(c=\epsilon\tau c\).
The obstruction is the class of \(qc\) in
\[
  \{x\in\mathcal O_K:x=\epsilon\tau x\}/(1+\epsilon\tau)\mathcal O_K .
\]
Every class is killed by two since \(2x=x+\epsilon\tau x\).
This gives the exact parity criterion for integral solutions, including existence at \(q=2\) for every \(f\).
When a solution exists, divide it by \(q\) to get one monic solution over \(q^{-1}\mathcal O_K\).
The permissible coefficients form a translate of the full homogeneous lattice scaled by \(q^{-1}\).
Use precisely the [strict real direction](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#direction) and [simultaneous rounding](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#approximation) in that translate: at every complex place the monic target is still \(f'/b\), and at the chosen split place its varying target is \(f'/b+\lambda H_0\) with \(H_0\) the strict direction.
The fixed lattice scaling changes only the constants in the rounding errors, so the roots and circle inequalities are as before, with exterior seed roots tending to infinity.
Multiplying the monic seeds by \(q\) yields the required integral \(P\), preserving all the strict inequalities and roots.

<a id="equivalence"></a>
## Recovering and recognizing the rational norm polynomials

For a seed at \(q=2\) divide by two before the Archimedean root argument.
The resulting monic seed has real coefficients at the split embedding, strict boundary domination and exactly one positive exterior zero of its own.
The [Rouché and exterior disk argument](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#delay) applies verbatim to these complex polynomial coefficients: it uses no arithmetic integrality when determining the roots.
It gives a unique real exterior root \(R>1\) for \(F\), the opposite real copy has reciprocal roots, and all nonsplit copies have only unit-circle roots.
The congruence gives the integral monic polynomial \(F\) with constant \(e\).
Thus \(H\) is integral monic and \(M(H)=R^2\), by multiplying the root moduli in the two split copies.
The minimum of \(R\) divides \(H\), so \(M(R)\le R^2\) and \(M(M(H))=M(R^2)\le M(R)^2\le R^4\).
Taking rational norm after the division is essential for this measure normalization.

Conversely the [bounded-second-iterate reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) supplies either shrinking real Salem inputs or shrinking canonical square endpoints from a hypothetical failure of its gap.
For real Salem numbers the [linear construction](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#realization) yields norms of measure equal to the square of the Salem number, from integral monic seeds with strictly interior roots at every complex embedding.
Multiplying these seeds by two gives exactly the same norm after the prescribed division.
For canonical square endpoints the [half-field verification](Yin_C_G0901_v134.md#gap) gives all reconstruction hypotheses, and the preceding parity calculation always permits \(q=2\).
The reconstructed relative polynomial is \(f\) itself, so its norm is the endpoint's actual minimal polynomial.
This proves the equivalence in both directions.

For unrestricted Lehmer the other possible shrinking sequence has, after passage to a subsequence, unbounded second iterates tending to infinity.
For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) its integer polynomial values \(A=M(P_{\mathbb Z})\) then eventually satisfy both
\[
 \log M(A)\ \ge\ \exp\!\big(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big),\qquad
 l(A)\ \ge\ \frac{\log M(A)}{m},\qquad m=\log A .
\]
These follow from the [second-iterate alternative](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) and the [exterior count for the value itself](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#values).
Neither the uniform lower bound for the single norm construction nor the exclusion of this remaining growth is established.
