# Small measures with commensurable exterior log moduli and the complex Salem conjecture

<a id="statement"></a>
## Equality of the irreducible measure spectra near one

There is an absolute \(\eta>0\) with the following property.
If an irreducible integer polynomial \(P\) has \(1<M(P)<1+\eta\) and the positive log moduli of its roots are rational multiples of one another, then some complex Salem number has a minimal polynomial \(Q\) with
\[
                    M(Q)=M(P),\qquad \deg Q\le 2\deg P .
\]
Here a [complex Salem number](../audits/literature/PT21/Yin_U001.md#pt21-u001) is a nonreal algebraic integer with precisely itself and its complex conjugate outside the unit circle and with at least one conjugate on the circle.
Every such number conversely has only one positive log modulus.
Thus the two sets of irreducible measures coincide in the interval \((1,1+\eta)\).
A positive Mahler gap for **all** integer polynomials with commensurable positive log moduli is equivalent to the complex Salem conjecture.
By the [short-geodesic equivalence](../audits/literature/PT21/Yin_U001.md#pt21-u001) this is also equivalent to a positive universal lower bound on closed geodesic lengths in arithmetic hyperbolic three-orbifolds.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Passing from a real Salem number to a nonreal pair

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a real Salem number with monic minimal polynomial \(A\) of degree \(n\).
Pham and Thilmany give the substitution \(A(-z^2)\) in their discussion of the [complex Salem conjecture](../audits/literature/PT21/Yin_U001.md#pt21-u001).
Indeed a root \(\beta=\sqrt{-\alpha}\) has \(\mathbb Q(\beta^2)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subset\mathbb R\), and \(-\alpha\) is negative in this embedding.
The quadratic extension therefore has degree two.
The monic normalization of \(A(-z^2)\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), of degree \(2n\).
Its exterior roots are exactly the conjugate pair \(\pm i\sqrt{\alpha}\), the inverse real Salem conjugate gives two interior roots, and each original unit-circle conjugate gives two more unit-circle roots.
The measure is \(\alpha\) exactly.
The two exterior roots have quotient \(-1\), and taking the products within the pairs over each root of \(A\) recovers that root.
This use of complex Salem numbers includes torsion collisions.

<a id="spectrum"></a>
## Compression, the degree bound and reducible polynomials

Choose \(1+\eta<2\), below the [nonreciprocal lower bound of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001), below the [uniform gap for at least three compressed exterior roots](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement), and below every positive measure greater than one for monic polynomials of degrees at most four.
The last choice is possible by the coefficient bounds for polynomials of bounded degree and bounded measure.
Any irreducible integer polynomial in this interval is monic up to sign, since its leading coefficient in absolute value is bounded by the measure.
Its constant coefficient is nonzero unless the polynomial is an associate of \(z\), whose measure is one.
Smyth's bound then makes the polynomial reciprocal, so its roots are algebraic units.

The [products of torsion classes](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) give an irreducible reciprocal polynomial with the same measure and no increase in degree.
Its exterior log moduli remain commensurable.
By the choice of \(\eta\) it has at most two exterior roots and degree greater than four.
In the one-exterior case, changing the sign of the root if necessary gives a real Salem number.
In the two-exterior case both roots cannot be real, since commensurability of their positive logarithms would give a multiplicative relation up to sign between two equal-height conjugates, hence a torsion quotient, as in the [residual case proof](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#reduction).
The two roots are a nonreal conjugate pair and the degree bound forces a neutral conjugate.
They are complex Salem numbers.
Applying the quadratic lift only in the real Salem case proves the claimed measure and degree comparison.

Finally an arbitrary integer polynomial of measure in this interval has content one up to sign.
Its monic irreducible factors with measure greater than one each lie in the same interval, and their positive log moduli belong to those of the input.
Each such factor can therefore be replaced at the very same measure by the minimal polynomial of a complex Salem number.
The product of the replacements, with original factor multiplicities, preserves the measure and has degree at most twice the original degree.
In particular a hypothetical sequence of such products with measure tending to one supplies complex Salem factors with measures tending to one.
The reverse implication follows by using their minimal polynomials themselves.
This reduction does not place a bound on the number of independent exterior logarithms of unrestricted polynomials.
