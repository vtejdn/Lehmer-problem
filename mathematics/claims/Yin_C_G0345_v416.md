# A single signed polynomial detects the Lehmer gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## One polynomial of bounded width

Fix either sign \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) once for all odd primes \(p\).
For \(b\ge1\), write \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\) for the reversal of a nonzero polynomial \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\).
Test whether
\[
                  G_A(X)=A(X^p)+\epsilon X A_b^*(X^p)
\]
has a repeated nonzero root \(\alpha\) which is not a root of unity and for which \(A(\alpha^p)\ne0\).
Set \(S_{p,b}\) equal to the least of \(2^p\) and the coefficient one-norms of the passing polynomials of norm at most \(2^p\).
This definition permits common divisors of \(A,A_b^*\) and arbitrary primitive minimal polynomials of the repeated root.

Let \(\lambda\) be the infimum of the positive logarithmic Mahler measures of integer polynomials.
For **every** sequence \(b(p)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(p)=o(p)\),
\[
 \frac{\lambda}{2}\ \le\ \liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log S_{p,b(p)}}p
 \ \le\ \limsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log S_{p,b(p)}}p\ \le\ \lambda.     \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(b(p)=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\) suffices for the following test.
A uniform positive gap exists if and only if there is an integer \(k\ge2\) for which infinitely many primes admit no passing polynomial of norm at most \(2^{\lfloor p/k\rfloor}\).
When \(\lambda=0\), passing polynomials below or at every such cutoff occur at every sufficiently large prime.
There is also a computable choice \(b(p)\to\infty\), with \(b(p)\le\sqrt p\), for which \(\log S_{p,b(p)}/p\) converges to \(\lambda\) itself.
Both statements use the same fixed sign.

<a id="derivative"></a>
## Eliminating the repeated root

Put \(U=A,\ V=\epsilon A_b^*\), \(H=\|A\|_1\), and
\[
          W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV .
\]
It has degree at most \(2b\) and norm at most \((2pb+1)H^2\).
It is nonzero because at the lowest exponent of \(UV\) the multiplier of the product coefficient is \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0U-\operatorname{ord}_0V)-1\).
If \(z=\alpha^p\) is a passing value then \(V(z)\ne0\), \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), and \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The Euler derivative \(XG_A'(X)\) at \(\alpha\) is \(W(z)/V(z)\).
Writing \(P,Q\) for the primitive integer minima of \(\alpha,z\) with positive leading coefficient, we have \(Q\mid W\) in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\).
The [absolute height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) gives \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\) since taking the \(p\)-th power multiplies the absolute height by \(p\) and the degrees agree.
The measure of a nonzero integral cofactor is nonnegative, and \(m(W)\le\log\|W\|_1\).
Thus every passing row satisfies
\[
                p\,m(P)\le 2\log H+\log(2pb+1).                       \tag{2}
\]
Its \(m(P)\) is positive by Kronecker's theorem for nonzero algebraic numbers of height zero.
The fallback satisfies \(\log 2\ge\lambda\), proving the first inequality of (1).

For finite computation factor just \(W\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
For each irreducible factor other than \(Y\) and cyclotomic factors, use \(z=Y\bmod Q\) in its number field.
The necessary and sufficient conditions are \(V(z)\ne0\) and \((-U(z))^p=zV(z)^p\).
Indeed these give the nonzero root \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), its nonzero numerator, the graph value zero and the derivative zero by \(W(z)=0\).
This test factors polynomials of degree at most \(2b\), even for nonintegral roots.

<a id="projection"></a>
## Selecting the prescribed sign

At each sufficiently large prime take a minimizing genuine pair \(U_0,V_0\) in the [two-row test](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) at width \(t=b(p)-1\).
The normalized logarithm of its cost tends to \(\lambda<\log(6/5)<\log2\) by that theorem and its [degree-ten benchmark](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks), so eventually the minimum is below the fallback.
At a reciprocal repeated root \(\alpha\) put \(z=\alpha^p\).
The original rows are coprime and \(U_0(z)\ne0\).
Reversing their graph in formal degree \(pt+1\) gives \(V_{0,t}^*(X^p)+XU_{0,t}^*(X^p)\), also divisible by the same reciprocal square.
For \(\delta=\pm1\) put \(A_\delta=U_0+\delta V_{0,t}^*\).
The signed graph at width \(t\) is the sum of the original graph and \(\delta\) times its reversal.
At least one \(A_\delta(z)\) is nonzero since their sum is \(2U_0(z)\).
Multiply this \(A_\delta\) by \(Y+1\) when \(\delta=\epsilon\) and by \(Y-1\) otherwise.
The multiplier has formal reversal sign \(+1\) or \(-1\), respectively.
The width-\(b\) graph with the prescribed sign is the corresponding product of the width-\(t\) graph by \(X^p+1\) or \(X^p-1\).
Neither multiplier vanishes at \(\alpha\), since \(z\) is nontorsion.
The resulting first row costs at most four times the original cost, hence eventually qualifies within \(2^p\).
This proves the upper bound in (1).
If \(\lambda>0\), choose \((\log2)/k<\lambda/2\) and use its strict margin in the lower bound.
If \(\lambda=0\), the upper bound gives a strict margin below \((\log2)/k\) even after the floor, proving the cutoff assertions.

<a id="budget"></a>
## A computable width giving the exact limit

For each \(j\ge1\), form a finite list \(\mathcal P_j\) of all monic reciprocal irreducible nontorsion unit polynomials of degrees \(d\le2j\) whose coefficient of \(X^i\) has absolute value at most \(8\binom di\) at every \(i\).
For a listed polynomial and a prime \(p\), compute the two positive integers \(D_\pm(P,p)\) from the [cyclotomic resultant and inverse denominators](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction).
Choose the largest \(1\le j\le\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\) for which every member of \(\mathcal P_j\) satisfies
\[
        2^{p^2}(D_+D_-)^j\ \ge\ \exp(jp^2m(P)).                        \tag{3}
\]
Use \(b(p)=j\), or one if there is no such \(j\).
All comparisons are exact real algebraic comparisons since \(\exp m(P)\) is a product of absolute values of algebraic numbers.
For each fixed \(j\), the [fixed-polynomial limits](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input) give \(\log(D_+D_-)/p^2\to m(P)\) on the entire finite list.
Consequently every fixed \(j\) eventually passes, \(b(p)\to\infty\), and the fallback in this width definition eventually becomes unnecessary.

Consider a passing polynomial of cost \(H\le2^p\) at this width.
Its repeated root has degree \(\deg P=\deg Q\le2b\) and \(m(P)\le 2\log2+o(1)<\log8\) by (2), uniformly over all passing rows.
If \(P\) is nonmonic or has nonunit constant coefficient, \(m(P)\ge\log2\) by applying the root product at both ends.
Then (2) alone gives \(\log H/p\ge(\log2)/2-o(1)>\lambda-o(1)\).

If \(P\) is a nonreciprocal monic unit polynomial, \(Q\) is also nonreciprocal.
In fact an automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(z)\) taking \(z\) to \(z^{-1}\) would take \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) by the signed reversal identity for \(U,V\).
Moreover \(Y^{2b}W(1/Y)=W(Y)\).
This follows from \(Y\,d(Y^bf(1/Y))/dY=bY^bf(1/Y)-Y^b(Yf')(1/Y)\) and exchanging the signed reversals of \(U,V\).
Thus \(W\) contains both \(Q\) and its distinct reciprocal, and the argument for (2) gives \(2p\,m(P)\le2\log H+\log(2pb+1)\).
This suffices for \(\log H/p\ge\lambda-o(1)\).

In the remaining case the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds put \(P\) in \(\mathcal P_b\).
Apply the [two-class contraction with multiplicity two](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v231.md#jets) to \(G_A\) and \(P^2\).
Its finite inequality is \(2\log(D_+D_-)\le(\deg G_A)\log 2+2p\,m(G_A)\), since the nontorsion cofactor there has degree and measure at most those of \(G_A\).
Using \(\deg G_A\le pb+1,\ m(G_A)\le\log(2H)\) and (3) gives
\[
       \frac{\log H}{p}\ge m(P)-\frac{\log2}{b}
                  -\frac{(pb+1)\log2}{2p^2}-\frac{\log2}{p}.
\]
All losses tend to zero uniformly for this chosen width.
The fallback cost also exceeds \(\exp(p\lambda)\), so (1) now gives the exact limit.
The construction does not determine whether that limit is positive.
