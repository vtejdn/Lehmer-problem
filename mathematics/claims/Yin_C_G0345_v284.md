# A sharp number of prime cyclotomic norm tests for coefficient integrality

<a id="statement"></a>
## Recovering the missing integral coefficients

Let \(n\ge1\), \(0\le j<n\), and let \(P(Z)=Z^nS(Z+Z^{-1})\) be monic reciprocal of degree \(2n\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(S\) monic of degree \(n\).
Suppose the first \(j\) coefficients below the leading coefficient of \(P\) are integers, and put \(k=n-j-1\).
For any \(n-j\) distinct odd primes \(\ell\), the conditions
\[
                         \operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_\ell)\in\mathbb Z       \tag{1}
\]
force \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\), equivalently \(P\in\mathbb Z[Z]\).
Zero resultants are allowed.

The number \(n-j\) is sharp uniformly over rational reciprocal polynomials.
For any set of \(t<n-j\) distinct odd primes there are separable nonintegral monic reciprocal polynomials of degree \(2n\) satisfying (1) with nonzero integer resultants for every prime in the set and with the first \(j\) coefficients integral.
No assertion of irreducibility or small Mahler measure is made for these examples.

If all coefficient denominator primes belong to a finite set \(\mathcal B\), a single odd prime \(\ell\) suffices whenever every irreducible factor of its trace polynomial over each \(\mathbb F_p\), \(p\in\mathcal B\), has degree greater than \(k\).
For \(p\ne\ell\), these factor degrees equal
\[
                       \min\{h\ge1:p^h\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\}.             \tag{2}
\]
For \(p=\ell\), the only factor is \(X-2\).
In particular, if all denominator primes are at most \(B\ge2\), any odd prime \(\ell>\max(B,B^k+1)\) suffices.

For an odd prime \(p\) and \(r\ge1\), put \(e=\varphi(p^r)/2\).
Uniformly for the same degree and prefix, a single integral resultant with \(\Phi_{p^r}\) forces \(p\)-integrality of the coefficients if and only if \(e>k\).
If \(e\le k\), an example with denominator only \(p\) passes this test with a nonzero integer resultant.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Triangular coefficients and trace norms

The transformation from leading coefficients of \(S\) to those of \(P\) is triangular over \(\mathbb Z\) with diagonal entries one.
Indeed \(Z^n(Z+Z^{-1})^{n-a}\) has leading term \(Z^{2n-a}\) and otherwise integral coefficients.
Induction proves both the prefix equivalence and the equivalence of full integrality.

For any odd prime power \(m\), let \(F_m\) be the monic trace polynomial whose roots are \(\zeta_m^a+\zeta_m^{-a}\), one for each inverse pair of primitive roots.
Its coefficients are rational algebraic integers, and
\[
                  \Phi_m(Z)=Z^{\varphi(m)/2}F_m(Z+Z^{-1}).
\]
Pairing inverse roots in the resultant gives
\[
       \operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_m)
                     =\left(\prod_{F_m(x)=0}S(x)\right)^2.              \tag{3}
\]
The product is rational, and a rational number whose square is integral is itself integral.

<a id="denominators"></a>
## A denominator has too few distinct trace zeros

If \(S\) is not integral, choose a denominator prime \(p\).
Let \(a>0\) be minimal such that \(Q=p^aS\) has coefficients in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Then \(\overline Q\ne0\), and the integral prefix implies
\[
                              \deg\overline Q\le k.                   \tag{4}
\]
An integral resultant for an odd prime \(\ell\) implies
\[
              v_p\left(\prod_{F_\ell(x)=0}Q(x)\right)
                                      \ge a(\ell-1)/2>0 .
\]
A zero product is assigned infinite valuation.
Reduction of this resultant implies that \(\overline Q\) and \(\overline F_\ell\) have a common root.

The trace root sets for distinct odd primes are disjoint over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
For \(\ell\ne p\) they are traces of elements of exact order \(\ell\).
Equality of traces forces equality of the unordered inverse pairs, since these are the roots of \(T^2-xT+1\); this argument includes characteristic two.
For \(\ell=p\), reduction of \(\Phi_p\) is \((Z-1)^{p-1}\), so the only trace root is \(2\).
That trace forces \(z=1\) and occurs for no other prime order.
Thus \(k+1=n-j\) tests force too many distinct roots of (4).

A single test suffices when every possible common irreducible factor has degree greater than \(k\).
Frobenius fixes a primitive trace after \(h\) iterations precisely when \(z^{p^h}\) is \(z\) or \(z^{-1}\), proving (2).
If \(\ell>\max(B,B^k+1)\), then for \(1\le h\le k\) both positive integers \(p^h-1,p^h+1\) are smaller than \(\ell\).
For \(k=0\) the factor-degree condition is automatic.
This proves the restricted-denominator criterion.

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Fewer tests can absorb a denominator

Fix \(t<n-j\) distinct odd primes \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_t\).
Choose a prime \(p\equiv1\pmod{\ell_i}\) for all \(i\).
For completeness this requires no prime-distribution theorem.
If \(M=\prod_i\ell_i>1\), any prime divisor \(p\) of \(\Phi_M(M)>1\) is coprime to \(M\), because \(\Phi_M(0)=1\).
Reduction of \(\Phi_M\) at such a prime has only primitive \(M\)-th roots, so the residue of \(M\) has order \(M\), proving \(p\equiv1\pmod M\).
For an empty set choose any prime.

Each \(F_{\ell_i}\) splits with distinct roots over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
Choose a root \(x_i\) and distinct integers \(u_i>2\) with
\(u_i\equiv x_i\pmod{p^{(\ell_i-1)/2}}\).
Set
\[
 A(X)=\prod_{i=1}^t(X-u_i),\qquad
 S(X)=(X^{n-t}+1/p)A(X),\qquad Q=pS.                                  \tag{5}
\]
Its first nonintegral coefficient has depth \(n-t>j\), being an integer plus \(1/p\).
The triangular map gives the same first nonintegral depth in \(P\).

All values of \(Q\) on roots of \(F_{\ell_i}\) are \(p\)-integral, and \(Q(x_i)\) is divisible by \(p^{(\ell_i-1)/2}\).
The trace norm of \(S\) is therefore \(p\)-integral and has no other denominator prime.
It is a rational integer, proving (1) by (3).
It is nonzero: the integers \(u_i>2\) are not torsion traces, and a torsion trace \(x\), being integral, cannot satisfy \(x^{n-t}=-1/p\).
The roots of \(A\) are distinct and greater than \(2\), while the roots of the other factor in (5) are distinct and of modulus less than one.
Thus \(S\) is separable and avoids \(2,-2\), so \(P\) is also separable.

These examples establish sharpness at fixed degree.
The [rational small-measure models](Yin_C_G0345_v282.md#statement) instead let the degree grow while passing any fixed finite list of torsion tests.

<a id="ramified"></a>
## The exact test at an odd prime power

The trace polynomial \(F_{p^r}\) has degree \(e\), with
\(F_{p^r}(2)=\Phi_{p^r}(1)=p\).
Reduction of \(\Phi_{p^r}\) modulo \(p\) is \((Z-1)^{2e}\), and
\(Z+Z^{-1}-2=(Z-1)^2/Z\).
It follows that \(F_{p^r}(2+Y)\) is Eisenstein: its reduction is \(Y^e\) and its constant coefficient is \(p\).
For a primitive trace \(x\), the extension \(\mathbb Q_p(x)\) is totally ramified of degree \(e\), with \(v_p(x-2)=1/e\).

Suppose \(p\) is a denominator prime and form \(Q=p^aS\) as above.
The reduction of \(Q(2+Y)\) is still nonzero of degree at most \(k\).
If \(k<e\), let \(b\le k\) be its least index with unit coefficient.
The degree-\(b\) term evaluated at \(Y=x-2\) has valuation \(b/e<1\).
Earlier terms and all nonunit terms have valuation at least one; later unit terms have larger valuation.
Hence \(v_p(Q(x))=b/e<1\).
Every conjugate has this valuation, because the Eisenstein trace polynomial gives a unique completion above \(p\).
The trace norm of \(S=p^{-a}Q\) has negative valuation, contradicting the integral resultant.

Conversely, if \(e\le k\), take
\[
                              S(X)=X^n+F_{p^r}(X)/p .
\]
Its first nonintegral coefficient has depth \(n-e>j\).
At each primitive trace \(x\), its value is \(x^n\), a nonzero algebraic integer.
The trace norm and its square in (3) are nonzero integers.
This proves the exact ramified criterion without an integral-basis assumption.
