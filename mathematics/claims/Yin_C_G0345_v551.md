# Complete sets of primitive roots in finite-field reductions of six-term sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Orders and numbers of roots

Let \(s\ge3\), let \(a,b\) be relatively prime positive integers, and write
\[
 G=X^{as+b}+X^{as+b-a}-X^{as}-X^b+X^a+1.
\]
Let \(p\) be a prime, and let \(n\ge1\) be prime to \(p\).
Suppose the reduction of \(\Phi_n\) divides \(G\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), or equivalently every primitive \(n\)-th root in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) is a root of \(G\).
Put \(e=n/\gcd(n,30)\).
Then at least one of the following assertions holds.

1. One has \(e\le 2s-1\), and hence \(n\le30(2s-1)\).
2. One has \(p=2\), \(s\equiv2\pmod3\), and \(n/\gcd(n,a)=3\), so every primitive \(n\)-th root maps under the \(a\)-th power to a primitive cubic root.

Conversely all the primitive roots specified by the second assertion vanish on \(G\) in characteristic two.
At each prime the union of all complete sets occurring in the hypothesis has \(O(s^2+a)\) distinct roots with an absolute constant.
For \(s\to\infty\) and \(b/a\asymp s^3\), this is \(o(b)\), uniformly in the first exponent for fixed slope comparison constants.
In particular every single irreducible factor of the reduction of degree \(\gg b\) in this regime eventually has a root order at which some primitive roots fail to vanish on \(G\).
This limits constructions of large Frobenius cycles using a prime primitive modulo a chosen root order, complementing the [simultaneous lower bounds on cycle lengths](Yin_C_G0345_v545.md#statement) on certain progressions.

<a id="classes"></a>
## Splitting a sparse relation according to its exponents

Work over \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
For each prime power \(q=\ell^j\Vert n\), choose a primitive \(q\)-th root \(u_q\).
The products \(z=\prod_{q}u_q\), taking one factor for each prime divisor, run bijectively through the primitive \(n\)-th roots as the choices vary.
Indeed the cyclic group of \(n\)-th roots is the direct product of its prime-power subgroups, and exact order \(n\) requires exact order \(q\) in each subgroup.
In coordinates obtained instead from powers of a single primitive root, the Chinese remainder projections differ only by unit multipliers modulo \(q\), which permute the primitive choices and preserve congruence of exponents.
Thus holding all coordinates but \(u_q\) fixed gives a relation vanishing at every primitive \(q\)-th root, even after adjoining the other roots to the coefficient field.

More generally consider any subsum of the original six signed monomials that vanishes identically on this product of primitive sets.
For fixed other coordinates write its specialization in the \(q\)-coordinate as \(A(U)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}c_i U^i\), reducing exponents modulo \(q\) and combining collisions.
Since \(p\nmid q\), \(\Phi_q(U)=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell-1}U^{h q/\ell}\) has distinct roots, precisely the primitive \(q\)-th roots, and divides \(A\) in \(k[U]\).
Its quotient has degree less than \(q/\ell\) if nonzero.
Consequently \(c_{r+hq/\ell}\) is constant for \(0\le h<\ell\) at fixed \(0\le r<q/\ell\), including when \(A=0\).
For each such \(r\) the corresponding subsum vanishes separately at all primitive \(q\)-th roots by the geometric sum.
If \(\ell>6\), at least one index in every such class receives no monomial at all, so its common coefficient is zero and the sums at individual residues modulo \(q\) vanish separately.
The other coordinates were arbitrary, so each resulting subsum vanishes on the original full product, and has at most six terms.
Iterating through all primes partitions the relation into subsums at fixed residues modulo the product of \(q/\ell\) for \(\ell\le5\) and \(q\) for \(\ell>5\), namely \(e\).
Each subsum vanishes independently on the full primitive set.
For \(n=1\) the empty iteration leaves the given relation intact.

<a id="reflection"></a>
## The two triples of exponents

Write \(-G=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) with \(g(Y)=Y^s-Y-1\) and \(B(Y)=1-Y^{s-1}-Y^s\).
Consider the six formal monomials including their signs, retaining them separately even if exponents happen to coincide.
When \(h=\gcd(e,a)>1\), the two triples have residues \(0\) and \(b\) modulo \(h\), which are distinct by coprimality.
Each triple must therefore vanish independently at every primitive root.
In either triple, splitting the three terms into more than one nonempty class modulo \(e\) would leave a singleton, whose nonzero value contradicts the class relation.
In particular \(e\mid a\), and for \(y=z^a\) both \(g(y)\) and \(B(y)\) vanish.
The identity \(yB(y)+(1+y)g(y)=-(y^2+y+1)\) gives \(y^3=1\) and \(y+1=-y^2\).
For \(s\equiv0,1,2\pmod3\), \(g(y)\) equals respectively \(-y,-1,2y^2\).
Thus \(p=2\), \(s\equiv2\pmod3\), and \(y\) has order three since \(1\) is not a root of \(Y^2+Y+1\) in characteristic two.
The same evaluations prove the converse, as \(y\) is a unit and the identity then implies \(B(y)=0\).

When \(\gcd(e,a)=1\), it suffices to treat \(e>s\).
On multiplying the exponents by \(a^{-1}\bmod e\), the two triples have residue sets \(A=\{0,1,s\}\) and \(t-A\), where \(t=s+b a^{-1}\bmod e\).
Each triple has three distinct residues, so the absence of singleton classes forces \(A=t-A\).
The involution \(x\mapsto t-x\) has a fixed point in this three-element set.
If it fixed all three, the points zero and one would give \(e\mid 2\).
Therefore it swaps the other two, whose sum must be twice the fixed point modulo \(e\).
For the fixed point \(0,1,s\), respectively, this gives \(e\mid s+1\), \(e\mid s-2\), or \(e\mid 2s-1\), proving the bound.

Finally the first alternative counts at most \(\sum_{n\le30(2s-1)}\varphi(n)=O(s^2)\) primitive roots in any characteristic, since \(\varphi(n)\le n\).
All roots of the second alternative solve \(X^{2a}+X^a+1=0\), contributing at most \(2a\) distinct roots in total.
At cubic slopes \((s^2+a)/b\ll 1/(as)+1/s^3\to0\).
An irreducible factor over a finite field is separable, and its Frobenius orbit has a single root order \(n\).
If \(p\) generates the units modulo \(n\), this orbit comprises every primitive \(n\)-th root, so its degree is bounded by the count above.
No simplicity of the whole reduction is required.
