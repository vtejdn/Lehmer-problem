# Residual periods and primitive pole sections whose factors have many exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## An individual prime-power obstruction and an excluded family

Use the [three reductions of a four-term Salem relation](Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#statement) along a shrinking sequence with fixed pole \(B\), coefficient field \(E=\mathbb Q(B)\), sign, and a fixed prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(E\) of residue cardinality \(q\) and characteristic \(p\).
Let \(u,v\) be the short and long primitive exponents, \(\beta=\alpha^{\gcd(a,b)}\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):E]\) and \(N\) the index prescribed by that reduction.
Put \(M=2N\).
There exists \(A\ge1\), depending only on the critical width bound and the specified local pole data, such that for all sufficiently large indices the following holds **without any bound on the number of primes dividing \(N\)**.
For each prime \(\ell>A\), \(\ell\ne p\), with \(\ell^b\parallel M\), one has
\[
                         \operatorname{ord}_{\ell^b}(q)\le D.            \tag{1}
\]
Also \(p^{v_p(M)}\le ApD\).
More precisely, the least period of the long binomial divisor in [the proof below](#period) has index at most \(A\) in \(M\).

At a rational prime pole \(B=p\ge5\), take the reduction at that prime, where \(N=u+v\).
Eventually the least positive \(n\) with \(\overline P\mid X^n-1\) is exactly \(N\) for sign plus and \(2N\) for sign minus, where \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
At \(B=p=3\) the same assertion holds for sign minus, and for sign plus the period is either \(N\) or \(N/2\), the latter only for even \(N\).

There are primitive positive integer pairs \((u_j,v_j)\), with \(u_j\to\infty\), \(v_j/u_j\to\infty\), and with the numbers of distinct prime divisors of **both** \(v_j\) and \(v_j+u_j\) tending to infinity, for which the following holds.
For both signs, every noncyclotomic irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q\) of
\[
                 X^{v_j}(X^{u_j}-2)+\epsilon(2X^{u_j}-1)               \tag{2}
\]
has at least \(c j\) roots of modulus greater than one for all sufficiently large \(j\), with an absolute \(c>0\).
Each polynomial has a positive exterior root.
In particular there is no Salem factor on this family for sufficiently large \(j\), even though the required largest prime factors of both long indices tend to infinity.

<a id="period"></a>
## The period of the whole residual divisor

Let \(P\in\mathcal O_E[X]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) over \(E\).
When \(v_{\mathfrak p}(B)\ne0\), take \(A_0=\overline P\); when \(B\equiv\pm1\bmod\mathfrak p\), take \(A_0=\overline P/\gcd(\overline P,X^{2u}-1)\).
In every case \(A_0\mid X^M-1\) over the residue field, with all multiplicities, by the [local reduction calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#reduction).
Let \(L\) be its least positive period, meaning \(A_0\mid X^L-1\), with \(L=1\) for the constant divisor.
It is the order of \(X\) in the unit group of \(\mathbb F_q[X]/A_0\) for a nonconstant divisor, and \(L\mid M\).

The polynomial \(\overline P\) divides \(X^L-1\), with the extra multiplier \(X^{2u}-1\) in the unit-residue row.
The normalized finite-place cost of this product is at least \(\log q/[E:\mathbb Q]\), by the [relative local degree sum](Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#norm).
The extra multiplier has cost tending to zero since \(u h(\beta)\to0\).
The [cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) bounds the remaining cost by a function of \(Lh(\beta)\) tending to zero with its argument.
Since \(Mh(\beta)\le2C\) at critical width \(C\), one has \(Lh(\beta)\le2C/(M/L)\).
Choose \(A\) so that for \(M/L>A\) this last cost would be less than half of \(\log q/[E:\mathbb Q]\).
It follows that \(M/L\le A\) eventually, independent of the number of prime divisors.

For an irreducible factor of \(A_0\) of multiplicity \(k\) and residue root order \(r\), its least period with multiplicity is \(r p^{\lceil\log_p k\rceil}\).
Its degree is \(\operatorname{ord}_r(q)\); these facts follow from Frobenius orbits and the separability of the prime-to-\(p\) binomial.
The period \(L\) is the least common multiple of these periods.
If \(\ell>A\) is distinct from \(p\), the entire power \(\ell^b\parallel M\) divides \(L\), hence divides one of the \(r\)'s.
This gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell^b}(q)\le\operatorname{ord}_r(q)\le\deg P=D\).
For the residue characteristic, the largest \(p\)-part of a constituent period is at most \(p\deg P\) because \(k\le\deg P\); the same bound holds for a constant divisor with \(L=1\).
This proves the assertions on individual prime powers.

<a id="signed"></a>
## Exact periods at a rational prime pole

For \(B=p\) the original monic polynomial reduces to \(X^N-\epsilon\), so \(\overline P\) divides that binomial.
The [critical height limits](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) give \(Nh(\beta)\to h(B)=\log p\), including the short exponent contribution \(u h(\beta)\to0\).
Whenever \(\overline P\mid X^n-\delta\), with \(\delta=\pm1\), the nonzero number \(\beta^n-\delta\) belongs to \(p\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\), by monic division.
Its absolute field norm and the archimedean triangle inequality give
\[
                           \log p\le \log2+n h(\beta).                   \tag{3}
\]
For sign plus the period \(n\) divides \(N\).
If the quotient \(N/n\) is at least \(k\), the upper limit of the right side of (3) is at most \(\log2+(\log p)/k\).
For \(p\ge5\) this is too small already at \(k=2\).
For \(p=3\) it is too small at \(k=3\), leaving the two stated possibilities.

For sign minus in odd characteristic, \(X^N=-1\) in the quotient ring by \(\overline P\).
The cyclic subgroup generated by \(X\) contains \(-1\), which has order two, and hence has even order \(2n\) with \(X^n=-1\).
Then \(N/n\) is odd.
If \(n<N\), this quotient is at least three, contradicting (3) for every \(p\ge3\).
This verifies the signed assertions with possible repeated factors in the residue polynomial.

<a id="construction"></a>
## Primitive directions with many prime factors in both long indices

For each \(j\ge1\) choose two relatively prime squarefree odd integers \(S_j,T_j\), each a product of \(j\) distinct primes, and another odd prime \(\ell_j>j\) coprime to both.
Write
\[
            w_j=v_{\ell_j}(2^{\operatorname{ord}_{\ell_j}(2)}-1).
\]
Choose a positive integer \(u_j\ge j S_j\ell_j^{w_j}\) satisfying \(u_j\equiv1\bmod S_j\ell_j\) and \(u_j\equiv S_j\bmod T_j\), using the Chinese remainder theorem.
Take \(b_j>w_j\) to be a sufficiently large multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{T_j}(\ell_j)\) that \(N_j=S_j\ell_j^{b_j}\ge(j+2)u_j^2\).
Put \(v_j=N_j-u_j\).
Then \(\gcd(u_j,v_j)=1\), \(T_j\mid v_j\), \(S_j\mid v_j+u_j\), and the required exponent and prime-divisor growth follows.
Lifting the order from \(\ell_j\) to \(\ell_j^{b_j}\) gives
\[
       \operatorname{ord}_{\ell_j^{b_j}}(2)
          \ \ge\ \ell_j^{\,b_j-w_j}\ \ge\ j N_j/u_j .                  \tag{4}
\]
The first bound follows by binomial expansion at this odd prime, as in the [prime-power order calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v753.md#periods).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Every noncyclotomic factor in the construction

Take any nontorsion root \(z\) of (2).
It is an algebraic unit and its monic rational minimal polynomial \(P_z\) divides (2) over \(\mathbb Z\).
Writing \(u=u_j,\ v=v_j,\ N=N_j\), the equation for \(z^v\) is a fixed fractional linear map of \(z^u\).
For \(x=z^u\) the denominator \(x-2\) does not vanish.
At every archimedean place the maximum of \(|2x-1|,|x-2|\) is at most \(3\max(1,|x|)\), and at each finite place at most \(\max(1,|x|)\).
The projective height of the quotient therefore gives \((v-u)h(z)\le\log3\).
Since \(v/u\to\infty\), the product \(Nh(z)\) is bounded uniformly in \(j,z\) and in the sign.

Reduction at two gives \(\overline P_z\mid X^N-1\) for either sign.
If \(\deg P_z<\operatorname{ord}_{\ell_j^{b_j}}(2)\), no irreducible factor of \(\overline P_z\) can have root order divisible by \(\ell_j^{b_j}\).
Consequently \(\overline P_z\mid X^{N/\ell_j}-1\); removing the odd prime \(\ell_j\) from the exponent preserves the multiplicities contributed by its \(2\)-part.
This gives \(z^{N/\ell_j}-1\in2\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(z)}\).
Yet \(h(z^{N/\ell_j})\to0\) uniformly by the preceding height estimate and \(\ell_j\to\infty\).
The normalized log norm must tend to zero by v730, contradicting its lower bound \(\log2\).
It follows that **every** noncyclotomic factor in either sign has degree at least the order in (4) for all sufficiently large \(j\).

To count its exterior roots, the [individual factor height theorem for a fixed plane curve](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives \(h(z)\gg1/v\), with an absolute constant after taking the minimum over the two signs.
Its hypotheses hold because the direction \((u,v)\) is primitive and the bilinear pole graph at two is rationally irreducible with support of rank two by the [pole graph calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#pullback).
Thus the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P_z\) is at least \(c_1 j N/(uv)\).
For any exterior root \(y\) of (2) the equation and \(v>u\) give \(|y|^{u+v}\le2|y|^v+2|y|^u+1\le5|y|^v\), whence \(\log|y|\le(\log5)/u\).
All contributions to the measure of this monic factor come from its exterior roots, so their number is at least \(c_1 j N/(v\log5)\ge c j\).
The [positive exterior root count](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#winding) supplies such a root of the section for both signs when \(v/u>3\), which holds eventually.
Increasing \(b_j\) in the construction to meet this inequality also gives an exterior root at every initial index.
These are actual sections with a uniform exclusion of Salem factors, obtained without an arithmetic specialization conjecture.
