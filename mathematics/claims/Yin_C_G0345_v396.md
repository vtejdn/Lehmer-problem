# Transfer of Mahler spectra over normal fields and a finite-place gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded-degree towers over a normal base

Work in one algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(F/\mathbb Q\) be any normal algebraic extension, possibly infinite.
Let \(F^{(2)}\) be the compositum of all extensions of \(F\) of degree at most two, and let \(F_q\) consist of the elements reachable from \(F\) by a finite tower of arbitrary solvable adjunctions and single algebraic adjunctions of relative degree at most \(q\), where \(q\ge1\) is an integer.
A solvable adjunction has finite solvable relative normal closure.
Write \(m(x)=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]h(x)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of a nonzero algebraic number.

For fixed \(M>0\) and \(q\), every \(x\in F_q\) with \(0<m(x)\le M\) has an equal-measure representative \(\gamma\) satisfying \([F(\gamma):F]\le2\), apart from a finite set of possible \(\gamma\) depending only on \(M,q\) and independent of \(F\).
Here \(\gamma\) is the [product of the torsion-quotient class of \(x\)](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels).
Consequently the following three sets of positive log measures have the same finite accumulation points, including possible accumulation at zero:
\[
   \{m(y):[F(y):F]\le2\},\qquad \{m(y):y\in F^{(2)}\},\qquad
   \{m(y):y\in F_q\}.                                               \tag{1}
\]
Only positive values are retained in each set.
On every interval \((0,M]\) the largest set differs from the smallest by at most the finite exceptional set of measure values above.
In particular local finiteness of these spectra on all bounded intervals, and the existence of a positive gap at zero, each transfer in both directions.

Fix a rational prime \(p\) and an integer \(s\ge1\).
Allow **all** such normal \(F\) that, under a fixed embedding of the algebraic closure into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), lie in some extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) of degree at most \(s\).
For every \(q\), the union of the positive spectra of their fields \(F_q\) has finite intersection with \((0,M]\) for each \(M\).
In particular there is a positive Mahler gap even after unrestricted solvable steps interspersed with arbitrarily many bounded-degree steps over the field of all totally \(p\)-adic numbers.
The original degrees in these towers need not be bounded.

<a id="normal"></a>
## Passage from an element in a tower to a quadratic representative

Let \(P\) be the rational normal label field of the class product, as in the [totally real case](Yin_C_G0345_v395.md#statement), and put \(d=\deg_{\mathbb Q}\gamma\).
For bounded measure and large \(d\), the rational label group has the [unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) \(D=(A_b)^u\), where \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
If \(P\not\subseteq F\), then \({\rm Gal}(P/(P\cap F))\) is a nontrivial normal subgroup of \({\rm Gal}(P/\mathbb Q)\).
It contains \(D\) and identifies with \({\rm Gal}(PF/F)\).
When \(b>q\), the [section argument for conjugate towers](Yin_C_G0345_v395.md#base-change) excludes \(x\in F_q\).
Indeed normality of \(F/\mathbb Q\) permits one conjugate tower over \(F\) for each rational conjugate of \(x\); the compositum of their normal closures excludes \(A_b\) as a section, and would otherwise contain \(PF\).

Thus \(P\subseteq F\) for each \(x\in F_q\) once \(d\) exceeds a threshold depending only on \(q,M\).
Over \(P\) the orbit of \(\gamma\) lies in its own label of size at most two, giving \([F(\gamma):F]\le2\).
When \(d\) is below the threshold there are finitely many possibilities for \(\gamma\) of bounded measure by the integral coefficient bounds for its primitive polynomial.
The three sets in (1) are nested, since every individual element of the quadratic compositum belongs to a finite elementary abelian extension of \(F\).
Compression preserves measure, proving the first assertion.

<a id="local"></a>
## A fixed finite-place condition bounds the compressed degree

For the fields at \(p\) in the statement, choose a local field \(E\) of degree at most \(s\) containing the image of \(F\).
For each \(\gamma\) obtained above with \([F(\gamma):F]\le2\), every rational conjugate has degree at most two over \(F\) by normality, and therefore degree at most \(2s\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\) after the chosen embedding.
The following elementary height estimate applies uniformly to all of them, including nonunits.

Let \(y\ne0\) be nontorsion and suppose all its \(p\)-adic conjugates have local degrees at most \(S\).
Put \(\lambda=(\log p)/S\), \(f_*=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,S)\) and \(N=(p^{f_*}-1)p^{4S}\).
Then
\[
        h(y)\ \ge\ \min\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{4},
                           \frac{2\log p-\log2}{N}\right)>0.           \tag{2}
\]
Use the absolute height with local degrees divided by the rational degree.
The weights above \(p\) sum to one.
At each nonunit place above \(p\) the absolute logarithm of the local modulus is at least \(\lambda\), and their weighted sum is at most \(h(y)+h(y^{-1})=2h(y)\).
If \(h(y)<\lambda/4\), the unit places have total weight at least \(1/2\).
In a completion of degree at most \(S\), each unit raised to \(p^{f_*}-1\) is one modulo the maximal ideal, by the order of the residue field.
With valuation normalized by \(v(p)=1\), a positive valuation is at least \(1/S\).
The binomial expansion gives \(v((1+z)^p-1)\ge v(z)+1/S\) whenever \(v(z)\ge1/S\).
At every such unit place \(v(y^N-1)\ge4\).
Since \(y^N-1\ne0\), its height equals that of its inverse and is at least \(2\log p\).
The inequality \(h(y^N-1)\le Nh(y)+\log2\) proves (2).
For \(\gamma\) use \(S=2s\), so \(d=m(\gamma)/h(\gamma)\) is bounded in terms of \(p,s,M\).
Together with the uniform exceptions and the coefficient bound this proves finiteness of the measures for the entire union.
The classical [theorem of Bombieri and Zannier](../audits/literature/BZ01b/readme.md) gives a height lower bound under local containment; (2) is a direct bound for the representatives needed after arbitrary towers.

<a id="primes"></a>
## A necessary size for a varying split prime in the reciprocal case

Let \(\gamma\) be any nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit of degree \(d\), put \(t=\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\), and let \(\rho_p\) be the fraction of rational conjugates of \(t\) that lie in \(\mathbb Q_p\) under the fixed embedding.
For every \(j\ge1\),
\[
       2p^j m(\gamma)\ \ge\ d\bigl(\rho_p j\log p-\log4\bigr).         \tag{3}
\]
In particular if \(p\ge5\) splits completely in the trace field, \(2p\,m(\gamma)\ge d\log(p/4)\).
This requires only that the traces lie in \(\mathbb Q_p\); the inverse-pair lifts may generate quadratic local extensions.

Let \(C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\) be the integral monic trace polynomial for \(n\ge1\), with \(C_1(X)=X\).
It has \(C_p(X)\equiv X^p\bmod p\) by Frobenius, so \(C_p(v)\equiv v\bmod p\) for every \(v\in\mathbb Z_p\), and \(C_p'\equiv0\bmod p\).
Taylor expansion then shows that a congruence modulo \(p^k\) for \(k\ge1\) gains one power of \(p\) under \(C_p\).
By iteration
\[
        C_{p^j}(v)-C_{p^{j-1}}(v)\in p^j\mathbb Z_p \qquad(v\in\mathbb Z_p).
\]
Set \(a=p^j,\ c=p^{j-1}\).
The algebraic integer \(\delta=C_a(t)-C_c(t)\) is nonzero, since \(\gamma^a\delta=(\gamma^{a-c}-1)(\gamma^{a+c}-1)\) and \(\gamma\) is nontorsion.
At the fraction \(\rho_p\) of embeddings the local divisibility above gives \(h(\delta)=h(1/\delta)\ge\rho_p j\log p\).
At each complex conjugate \(z\) of \(\gamma\), \(|C_a(z+z^{-1})-C_c(z+z^{-1})|\le4\max(|z|,|z|^{-1})^a\).
Integrality and the unit height formula therefore give \(h(\delta)\le\log4+2a h(\gamma)\), proving (3).

For fixed \(q\), suppose \(0<m(x_i)\to0\) and each \(x_i\) is obtained by the permitted towers over the totally \(p_i\)-adic numbers, with primes allowed to vary.
The compressed degrees \(d_i\) tend to infinity, and the [small-measure reciprocity and trace description](Yin_C_G0345_v395.md#spectrum) applies to the equal-measure representatives.
The normal-field argument forces their label fields into the respective totally \(p_i\)-adic fields, so \(\rho_{p_i}=1\) eventually.
The fixed-prime result forces \(p_i\to\infty\), and (3) gives
\[
                     \frac{p_i}{d_i\log p_i}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
This leaves open whether a suitable prime or a substantial fraction of locally rational traces can always be found at a smaller scale.
