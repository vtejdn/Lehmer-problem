# Local degree bounds for reciprocal four-term and five-term polynomials at odd primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two families with a uniform gap for individual factors

For integers \(0<l<n\), \(ab\ne0\), and possibly zero \(c\), put
\[
             P=a(X^{2n}+1)+b(X^{n+l}+X^{n-l})+cX^n .
\]
For every nonconstant rational divisor let \(d\) denote its degree.
The following conclusions do not require a bound on the coefficients.

* At three, assume \(3\nmid a,\ v_3(b)=1\), and write \(n=3^u2^v h\) with \(\gcd(h,6)=1\).
  Assume either \(9\mid c\) and \(v_2(l)\ne v\), or \(3\mid c\) and \(v_2(l)<v\).
  Then \(d\) is divisible by \(3^u\operatorname{ord}_{2^{v+2}}(3)\), which is at least \(n/h\).
  In particular this applies to reciprocal tetranomials with \(c=0\) and \(v_2(l)\ne v\).
  If \(h=1\) and \(v\ge1\), there are exactly two irreducible factors of degree \(n\) over \(\mathbb Q_3\), whereas for \(h=1,\ v=0\) the polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_3\).
* Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, \(p\nmid a,\ p\mid b,\ p\mid c-a\).
  Write \(n=p^u3^v h\) with \(\gcd(h,3p)=1\), let \(f=\operatorname{ord}_3(p)\), and put \(w=v_3(p^f-1)\).
  Assume either \(v_p(b)=1\) and \(v_3(l)\le v-w\), or \(p^2\mid b\) and \(v_p(c-a)=1\).
  Then \(d\) is divisible by \(p^u\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}}(p)=p^u f3^{\max(0,v+1-w)}\), in particular \(d\ge f3^{1-w}n/h\).
  For \(p\) primitive modulo nine one has \(f=2,\ w=1\), so every such divisor has degree at least \(2n/h\) and \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_p\) if \(h=1\).
  The first offset condition for these primes is exactly \(3^v\nmid l\), and the second coefficient condition permits every offset.

In the first family every positive-measure integral divisor has logarithmic Mahler measure \(\gg1/h\), with an absolute implied constant.
In the second family the corresponding bound is \(\gg f3^{1-w}/h\), again with an absolute implied constant.
For instance \(a=1,\ b=-3,\ c=0\) in the first family and \(a=1,\ b=5,\ c=-14,\ p=5\) in the second produce monic examples having a real root greater than one, since \(P(1)<0\).
With \(h=1\), each family permits unbounded \(n\) and offsets satisfying its first condition, for example \(l=1,\ v\ge1\).

<a id="local"></a>
## One irreducible local factor for each residual orbit

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z_p[X]\) have unit leading coefficient and suppose its monic normalization reduces to \(\prod_i g_i^{e_i}\) with distinct monic irreducibles \(g_i\in\mathbb F_p[X]\) having nonzero constant terms.
For each \(i\), choose a root of \(g_i\) and let \(\xi_i\) be its prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift in the corresponding unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\).
Normalize the valuation by \(v_p(p)=1\).
If \(v_p(F(\xi_i))=1\), the Hensel factor lifting \(g_i^{e_i}\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_p\) and has degree \(e_i\deg g_i\).

Indeed Hensel's lemma gives one monic factor for each of the coprime residual powers, and all the other factors take unit values at \(\xi_i\).
In any nontrivial factorization of the chosen Hensel factor over \(\mathbb Q_p\), both factors can be taken monic integral.
Their reductions have positive degrees and are powers of \(g_i\).
Both then take values divisible by \(p\) at \(\xi_i\), since its field is unramified, contradicting the valuation one of their product.
This also gives the residue-degree factor in the [valuation-one argument at a single residual root](Yin_C_G0345_v180.md#local).
If the test holds on every orbit, the degrees of the rational divisors are sums of the resulting local degrees whenever \(F\) has rational coefficients.

<a id="three"></a>
## The fourth roots at three

In the first family the reduction, up to the nonzero scalar \(a\), is \((X^{2^{v+1}h}+1)^{3^u}\).
The roots of the parenthesis are simple and have exactly the orders \(s=2^{v+2}t\) for \(t\mid h\).
Indeed the condition on the exponent of two in \(s\) is equivalent to raising a root of order \(s\) to an element of order two by the power \(2^{v+1}h\), with its odd part killed.
Each Frobenius orbit of order-\(s\) roots has cardinality \(\operatorname{ord}_s(3)\).
For any such Teichmüller lift \(\xi\), its power \(\xi^n\) has order four.
Consequently
\[
                  P(\xi)/\xi^n=c+b(\xi^l+\xi^{-l}).
\]
Put \(y=\bar\xi^l\) in the residue field.
If \(9\mid c\), having valuation one is equivalent to \(y+y^{-1}\ne0\), since \(v_3(b)=1\).
In odd characteristic \(y+y^{-1}=0\) exactly when \(y\) has order four.
The two-part of the order of \(y\) is \(2^{\max(0,v+2-v_2(l))}\), which differs from four if \(v_2(l)\ne v\).
If only \(3\mid c\) but \(v_2(l)<v\), the order of \(y\) is divisible by eight.
In this case \(y+y^{-1}\notin\mathbb F_3\), since membership would force \(y^3\) to equal \(y\) or \(y^{-1}\), and therefore its order would divide two or four.
The term \((b/3)(y+y^{-1})\) in the residue of \(P(\xi)/(3\xi^n)\) cannot cancel the scalar \(c/3\).
This verifies valuation one on every orbit in both cases.

By the local lemma all local degrees are \(3^u\operatorname{ord}_{2^{v+2}t}(3)\), with one factor for each orbit.
Each degree is a multiple of \(3^u\operatorname{ord}_{2^{v+2}}(3)\).
The order at modulus four is two, and \(\operatorname{ord}_{2^{v+2}}(3)=2^v\) for \(v\ge1\), since successive squaring gives \(v_2(3^{2^j}-1)=j+2\) for \(j\ge1\).
If \(h=1,\ v\ge1\), there are \(\varphi(2^{v+2})/2^v=2\) orbits, and at \(v=0\) there is just one.

<a id="odd"></a>
## The third roots at an odd prime other than three

Now the reduction up to scalar is
\[
                  \bigl(X^{2\cdot3^vh}+X^{3^vh}+1\bigr)^{p^u}.
\]
The roots of the parenthesis have exactly the orders \(s=3^{v+1}t,\ t\mid h\), and all are simple since \(p\nmid 3h\).
For each lift \(\xi\), the power \(\xi^n\) has order three, so that
\[
                    P(\xi)/\xi^n=c-a+b(\xi^l+\xi^{-l}).
\]
If \(p^2\mid b\) and \(v_p(c-a)=1\), this immediately has valuation one.
Otherwise assume \(v_p(b)=1\) and the stated bound on \(v_3(l)\).
The order of \(y=\bar\xi^l\) is divisible by \(3^{w+1}\).
If \(y+y^{-1}\in\mathbb F_p\), Frobenius must take \(y\) to \(y\) or \(y^{-1}\), whence its order divides \(p-1\) or \(p+1\).
Their largest three-part is \(3^w\), since one is prime to three and \(f\) is one for \(p\equiv1\bmod3\) and two otherwise.
Again the trace term divided by \(p\) cannot cancel the scalar \((c-a)/p\) in the residue field.
This proves valuation one on every orbit.

The local degrees are therefore \(p^u\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}t}(p)\), each divisible by \(p^u\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}}(p)\).
For all \(j\ge1\), factoring the prime-to-three part and successively cubing gives \(v_3(p^{fj}-1)=w+v_3(j)\), proving the asserted order formula.
A prime primitive modulo nine has order two modulo three and \(v_3(p^2-1)=1\), so the minimum degree is \(2p^u3^v\).

Finally any irreducible nontorsion unit factor of degree \(d\) has logarithmic measure \(m\gg d/(2n)\) by the [width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement), since \(P\) has at most five terms.
A primitive irreducible factor which is not a unit already has \(m\ge\log2\) by its leading or constant coefficient.
The degree bounds therefore give the asserted gaps, since \(h\ge1\) and \(f3^{1-w}\le2\).
Every positive-measure integral divisor contains a positive-measure irreducible factor or has content of absolute value at least two.
