# Linear recurrence complexity of dense coefficient windows at the trace pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree obstruction that permits every coefficient to be nonzero

For integers \(q,D\ge2\) put \(N=2D\) and \(K=(2q+1)^2\).
Let \(F\) be the reciprocal lift of a [genuine integral realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) of the first nonunit pole.
Write \(F(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}c_i X^i\), with \(c_{N-i}=c_i\), \(c_0=1\).
Call the order of the window \(c_w,\ldots,c_{j-1}\) at most \(r\), \(r\ge1\), if it agrees to that length with the Taylor series of \(P/Q\in\mathbb Q(X)\), \(\deg P<r,\ \deg Q\le r,\ Q(0)\ne0\).
This includes a solution of any homogeneous order-\(r\) linear recurrence with constant rational coefficients on that window and arbitrary first \(r\) terms.
There is no a priori bound on the coefficients of \(P,Q\).

For every such window \(0\le w<j\le N+1\) there is an absolute effective bound
\[
          j\ll (r+1)(w+r+1)[\log(3D)]^2\log(2q+1).                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular the least order for the first \(D\) coefficients is
\[
               r\gg \frac{\sqrt D}{\log(3D)\sqrt{\log\log(9D)}}.     \tag{2}
\]
If those first \(D\) coefficients can be partitioned into \(k\) intervals of order at most a fixed \(R\), with completely different recurrences on different intervals, then
\[
       \frac{\log(3D)}{\log\log(9D)}\ll_R k,\qquad
       D\le \exp(C_R k\log(2k)),\qquad q\ll_R k^3.                  \tag{3}
\]
The implied constants are effective.
This gives effective finiteness over all heights and all separating grids for bounded \(k,R\), including piecewise polynomial coefficient strings of bounded degree.
It extends the [successive support-gap test](Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#gaps) to long windows filled with nonzero entries.

<a id="reconstruction"></a>
## Recovering an approximant of controlled height from the start of a window

We prove a version also for monic reciprocal rational \(F\) with the same pole and archimedean root product at most \(1+1/q\).
Let \(d_0\) be its least integral coefficient denominator, \(E=\log d_0\), and set \(C(X)=d_0F(X)=\sum a_iX^i\).
Scaling all coefficients preserves window order.
All \(|a_i|\le d_0(1+1/q)\binom Ni\), by summing the appropriate products of roots.
It suffices to treat \(j-w\ge2r\), since shorter windows satisfy the bounds directly.
In particular \(r\le N\).
Choose an approximant to the window of \(C\), and for the moment normalize its denominator's constant term to one.
The \(r\) equations in the remaining \(r\) denominator coefficients saying that the coefficients of orders \(r,\ldots,2r-1\) of the product vanish are consistent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Their entries and right sides come from integers of absolute value at most \(L=2d_0 N^{w+2r}\).
Set the free unknowns to zero after choosing independent columns spanning the column space and a full rank minor on these columns.
Cramer's rule and expansion of determinants furnish a common positive denominator and integer numerators all bounded by \(r!\,L^r\), also at rank zero.
The numerator is obtained from the first \(r\) coefficients of the product; after clearing denominators it costs at most another factor \((r+1)L\) per coefficient.
The recovered rational function equals the original window approximant: their cross difference has degree at most \(2r-1\) and vanishes to order \(2r\) at zero.
It therefore represents the whole window, even if the system had free variables.

Take coprime integral representatives \(P,Q\) in \(\mathbb Q[X]\), meaning that common polynomial factors have been canceled, without requiring either leading coefficient to be one.
Canceling a primitive common factor increases either bound on coefficient length by at most \(2^r\): for an integral polynomial \(V=GW\) of degree at most \(r\), \(\|W\|_1\le2^r M(W)\le2^r M(V)\le2^r\|V\|_1\).
Here \(M\) is the ordinary multiplicative Mahler measure, and \(M(G)\ge1\) for nonzero integral \(G\).
When \(P=0\), use \(P=0,Q=1\).
Thus \(\log\max(2,\|P\|_1,\|Q\|_1)\ll(r+1)[E+(w+r+1)\log(3D)]\).
The numerator
\[
       U(X)=Q(X)\sum_{i<w}a_iX^i+X^wP(X)
\]
and denominator \(Q\) approximate \(C\) modulo \(X^j\).
They have no common root since \(Q(0)\ne0\) and \(P,Q\) were reduced.
Both degrees are at most \(b=w+r\), and the same logarithmic length estimate holds for them after enlarging the absolute constant, by the bound for \(\sum_{i<w}|a_i|\).

<a id="evaluation"></a>
## The partial sums at a common rational argument

Let \(u,t\) be the even and odd members of \(q,q+1\), so \(t\ge3\).
Reciprocity and the exact pole imply \(t^N C(u/t)=d_0 K\).
Put \(A=t^bU(u/t)\) and \(B=t^bQ(u/t)\), both integers.
Since \(X^j\mid Q C-U\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), clearing odd denominators gives
\[
                   v_2(d_0 K B-t^N A)\ge j v_2(u).                 \tag{4}
\]
The left side is allowed to be infinite for the moment.
There is an absolute effective bound
\[
 \log\max(2,|A|,d_0K|B|)\le H,\quad
 H=C_1(r+1)\{E+(w+r+1)[\log(3D)+\log(2q+1)]\}.                   \tag{5}
\]
This follows by homogenizing the length estimates at \(t,u\).
If the difference in (4) vanishes, coprimality prevents both evaluated integers from vanishing, so \(t^N\le d_0K|B|\) and \(j\le N+1\ll H\).
If just one evaluated integer vanishes, the valuation is at most \(H/\log2\).
If their nonzero terms \(d_0KB,t^N A\) have unequal valuations, the same bound holds for the smaller valuation, since \(t\) is odd.

In the remaining case \(A/(d_0KB)\) is a dyadic unit; the power difference \(t^N A/(d_0KB)-1\) is nonzero.
Apply [Yu's bound](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#yu99-u001) to these two rational local units in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), as in the [dyadic pole estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v174.md#dyadic).
The heights of the two units are at most \(\log(2q+1)\) and \(H\), their exponents \(N,1\), and the fixed field has ramification index one at two.
Adding back \(v_2(d_0KB)\le H/\log2\) gives, in every case,
\[
                        j\ll H\,\log(3D)\log(2q+1).                \tag{6}
\]
This also explains what a one-prime relaxation costs here.
A denominator \(\ell^a\) enters (5) as \(E=a\log\ell\), even when \(\ell=2\).
With large denominators, the [one-prime models at the same exact pole](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) need not satisfy the integral-case order estimate.

<a id="partition"></a>
## Prefixes and changing recurrences across a partition

For the genuine integral input \(E=0\) and \(q<(\log(6D))^3/2\) by [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) at its Salem root.
Substitution into (5) and (6) proves (1).
Taking \(w=0,\ j=D\) then gives (2), with bounded degrees absorbed in the absolute constant.
For intervals \([w,j)\) in a partition of the first \(D\) coefficients with \(r\le R\), (1) bounds \(j+R+1\) by \(w+R+1\) times \(C_R(\log(3D))^2\log\log(9D)\).
Starting at zero and ending at \(D\) in \(k\) steps gives the first inequality of (3) by taking logarithms.
Effective inversion and the stronger degree inequality \(q<4(\log(2D)/\log\log(2D))^3\) yield the other two, just as in the [bounded-support count](Yin_C_G0901_v175.md#gaps).
The archimedean bound on all coefficients leaves a finite effective list at each bounded degree.
A string polynomial in its index of degree \(d\) has a generating function with denominator \((1-X)^{d+1}\) and numerator of degree at most \(d\), so it contributes an interval of order at most \(d+1\).
