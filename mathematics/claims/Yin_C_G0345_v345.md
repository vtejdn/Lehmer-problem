# The loss on unit-circle fibers of a cyclic Mahler specialization

<a id="statement"></a>
## A term-independent comparison for any fixed number of occupied classes

Fix \(k\ge2\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be irreducible with \(P(0)\ne0\), and suppose its support occupies exactly \(k\) classes modulo an odd prime \(p\).
Write \(m=\log M\), let \(\theta>1\) solve \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\), and assume \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\).
For sufficiently large \(p\) use the [centered prime-modulus lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift)
\[
 P(x)=x^c F(x^p,x^b),\qquad q_X(Y)=Y^{h/2}F(X,Y),\qquad 0<b<p.
\]
The extreme exponents of \(Y\) in \(F\) are \(-h/2,h/2\), and \(h=O_k(p^{1-1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor})<p\).
Let \(\mu\) denote normalized circle measure, let \(\mathcal D(X)=\operatorname{disc}_Y q_X\), and define
\[
 L_p=\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_{\substack{q_X(u)=0\\|u|=1}}
             \log\frac{2}{|u^p-X^b|}\,d\mu(X).
\]
The sum is symmetric in the roots, with any exceptional fibers of measure zero omitted.
Then \(\mathcal D\ne0\), \(0\le L_p<\infty\), and
\[
 \left(1-\frac{2(h-1)}p\right)m(F)
       +\frac{m(\mathcal D)-2h\log h-L_p}{p}
 \ \le\ m(P)\ \le\ m(F)+\frac{h\log2}{p}.                            \tag{1}
\]
The estimate places no term, degree or height bound on the rows of \(P\).

Let \(\eta_k>0\) be the [free projected-support gap](Yin_C_G0345_v338.md#statement).
Along any such sequence with \(p\to\infty\) and \(m(P)\to0\), one must have
\[
                       \liminf L_p/p\ \ge\ \eta_k .                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular the subclass for which almost every fiber has no unit-circle root has a uniform positive gap for all sufficiently large \(p\).
This concerns the roots in the \(Y\) fiber of the indicated lift.
The existence of such a fixed-count representation for an arbitrary small-measure sequence remains open.

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## Self-inversive fibers and a nonzero discriminant

Below the plastic threshold \(P^*=\sigma P\), \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\), by the [whole-polynomial reciprocity result](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity).
In the centered lift the occupied fast exponents occur in opposite pairs, together with zero when \(k\) is odd, and \(2c\equiv\deg P\bmod p\).
The identity
\[
 F(X,Y)=\sigma X^{(\deg P-2c)/p}F(X^{-1},Y^{-1})
\]
holds because it holds after \(X=x^p,\ Y=x^b\) and substitution is injective on Laurent polynomials in the common interval of fast exponents of span \(h<p\).
Indeed different fast exponents give distinct residues modulo \(p\), and distinct base exponents within a row remain distinct.
Consequently \(q_X\) is self-inversive for \(|X|=1\), meaning its reversed conjugate differs from it by a scalar of modulus one.

The rows have unit gcd since \(P\) is irreducible and at least two classes are occupied.
The Laurent polynomial \(F\) is itself irreducible: in any integral Laurent factorization each factor has fast span at most \(h\) by additivity of widths.
The same injectivity preserves each factor's monomial count and coefficients under specialization.
A Laurent nonunit therefore remains a nonunit, contradicting the irreducibility of \(P\) in \(\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1}]\).
Gauss's lemma and the nonzero constant row now give irreducibility of \(q_X(Y)\) in \(\mathbb Q(X)[Y]\).
In characteristic zero it is separable, and its degree-\(h\) discriminant is a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial in \(X\).
In particular \(m(\mathcal D)\ge0\).
Except at finitely many \(X\), the extreme coefficients are nonzero and all fiber roots are simple.
Self-inversivity pairs each off-circle root \(Ru\), \(R>1,\ |u|=1\), with \(R^{-1}u\).

<a id="radial"></a>
## A normalized Vandermonde determinant for the radial pairs

For such a fiber, write \(q_X(Y)=A\prod_{i=1}^h(Y-z_i)\) and \(m_X=\log|A|+\sum_i\log^+|z_i|\).
Form the matrix with row \(i\) equal to
\[
             (z_i^j/\max(1,|z_i|)^{h-1})_{j=0}^{h-1}.
\]
Its squared determinant in absolute value is exactly \(|\mathcal D(X)|\exp(-2(h-1)m_X)\).
Each row has norm at most \(\sqrt h\).
For each off-circle pair put \(r=R^{-1}\) and subtract its interior row from its normalized exterior row.
The new entries have absolute values \(\left|r^{h-1-j}-r^j\right|\le h(1-r^2)\), by the geometric sum inequality on \([0,1]\).
The pairs are disjoint, so these row operations retain the determinant.
Hadamard's inequality gives, with \(s\le h/2\) pairs,
\[
 |\mathcal D(X)|e^{-2(h-1)m_X}
       \le h^{h+2s}\prod_{\rm pairs}(1-r^2)^2
       \le h^{2h}\prod_{\rm pairs}(1-r^2)^2 .                         \tag{3}
\]
The factor \(h^{2h}\) is important when \(h\) grows with \(p\).
The determinant argument is given here for the normalized radial distances used on the cyclic grid.
For related products of distances between roots, see Escorcielo and Perrucci, [*On the Davenport–Mahler bound*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06572).

<a id="grid"></a>
## Grid products and the contribution from unit-circle roots

Conditioning on \(X=x^p\) makes \(x^b\) uniform over the \(p\) roots of \(Y^p=X^b\).
For each fiber root \(z\), the sum of its logarithmic distances to those grid points is \(\log|z^p-X^b|\).
Its discrepancy from the independent fiber contribution is therefore
\(d(z)=\log|z^p-X^b|-p\log^+|z|\).
Each term is at most \(\log2\).
On an off-circle pair each term is at least \(\log(1-r^p)\ge\log(1-r^2)\).
For a unit root it equals \(\log2-\log(2/|z^p-X^b|)\).

All means in this computation exist.
The fiber mean \(m_X\) is bounded below by \(\log|A(X)|\) and above by a constant depending on the fixed polynomial.
The conditional diagonal log is integrable by power substitution and the integrability of \(\log|P|\).
The roots can be locally labelled away from the exceptional fibers, and their symmetric sums are measurable.
The nonnegative sum \(\sum_z(\log2-d(z))\) is integrable by the grid identity and these two integrability bounds.
Its sub-sum over the unit roots proves finiteness of \(L_p\).

Taking logs in (3) bounds the total off-circle discrepancy below by \(\log|\mathcal D(X)|-2(h-1)m_X-2h\log h\).
Adding the unit terms, discarding their nonnegative multiples of \(\log2\), and integrating proves the lower bound in (1).
The upper bound follows by summing \(d(z)\le\log2\).
In the quadratic case the [direct two-root calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v344.md#statement) saves the term \(2h\log h\) and describes the unit roots by an explicit middle-row inequality.

<a id="consequence"></a>
## A necessary concentration of the phases

The independent lift has \(m(F)\ge\eta_k\).
Otherwise its unit row gcd and the equality case in the projected-support theorem would factor it into cyclotomics in Laurent monomials.
The diagonal would have logarithmic measure zero or at least \(\log2\), as in the [specialization argument](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#gap).
Both alternatives contradict the input interval.
For fixed \(k\), the span bound gives \(h\log h=o(p)\).
Using \(m(\mathcal D)\ge0\) and the eventually positive coefficient of \(m(F)\) in (1) proves (2), even if the independent measures grow.

More precisely, fix \(0<\gamma<\eta_k\) and retain in \(L_p\) only the roots with \(|u^p-X^b|<2\exp(-\gamma p/h)\).
The discarded contribution is at most \(\gamma p\), since there are at most \(h\) roots in each fiber.
The retained integral divided by \(p\) consequently has lower limit at least \(\eta_k-\gamma\) along a shrinking sequence.
An isolated exact resonance alone has zero angular measure.
The unresolved estimate concerns the total logarithmic contribution of neighborhoods of such near coincidences.
