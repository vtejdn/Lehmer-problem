# Cyclotomic completion degree from the power sums of the exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## The full exterior power sums determine the degree cost

Let \(P\) be a monic separable integer polynomial of degree \(d\to\infty\), with \(0<L=\log M(P)\le A\) and exterior roots \(z_1,\ldots,z_s\), where \(1\le s\le r\le(\log d)^{1-\gamma}\) for fixed \(0<\gamma\le1\).
Put \(b=\log\max_i|z_i|>0\), \(s_j=\sum_{i=1}^s z_i^j\) and \(V(k)=\sum_{j=1}^k|s_j|\).
Complex conjugation preserves the exterior set, so all \(s_j\) are real.
Write \(D(P,k)\) for the least degree, at least \(k\), of a monic cyclotomic product matching the first \(k\) coefficients below the leading one of \(P\).
For fixed \(\delta>0,\ B>1/2+\delta\), uniformly when \((1/2+\delta)\log d\le bk\le B\log d\), one has
\[
 D(P,k)=(1+o(1))V(k)
       =(1+o(1))\sum_{j=k-H+1}^k |s_j|,\qquad
       H=\lfloor k^{1-\gamma/12}\rfloor.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The oscillations of different exterior roots enter inside the absolute value at each order.

For the largest prefix \(K=K(P)\) attainable at degree exactly \(d\),
\[
       bK=\log d+O_{A,\gamma}(r\log\log(3d)),\qquad
       V(K)\le(1+o(1))d,\qquad V(K+1)\ge(1-o(1))d.                          \tag{2}
\]
When \(br(2e)^r\to0\), in particular for bounded \(r\) and \(b\to0\), these two consecutive costs both equal \((1+o(1))d\).
In particular, for bounded exterior count and bounded total measure, a sequence has \(L\to0\) if and only if \(K(P)/\log d\to\infty\).
The input can be reducible and its exterior roots can have any arguments or relative moduli.
For a single positive exterior root the geometric sum recovers the [Salem completion degree](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement).
The contributor and the strategy collaborator \(\texttt{agent\_s\_reversal}\) independently observed that the [terminal divisor weights](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#divisors) admit individual signs.
The contributor supplied the complex-power cancellation estimate and its use for the degree and cutoff at bounded exterior count.
The separate collaborator supplied the growing-count range and the corresponding interval width and divisor cutoff.

<a id="powers"></a>
## An elementary bound for consecutive complex powers

For complex numbers \(y_1=1,\ |y_i|\le1\), \(1\le i\le s\), and every integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
           \max_{1\le h\le s}\left|\sum_i y_i^{m+h}\right|
                \ge \frac{(8s(m+1))^{1-s}}{s2^{s+1}}.                         \tag{3}
\]
Choose \(t=1-1/(8s(m+1))\).
For \(|y|\le1\), \(|1-y|\le 2|1-ty|\), by the triangle inequality and \(1-t\le|1-ty|\).
Since \(|y^{m+1}-1|\le(m+1)|1-y|\), replacing \(y_i^{m+1}\) by one in the numerator of \(\sum_i y_i^{m+1}/(1-ty_i)\) costs at most \(2s(m+1)\).
All the reciprocals \(1/(1-ty_i)\) have positive real part and the first equals \(1/(1-t)\).
The original rational sum therefore has modulus at least \(1/(2(1-t))\).
Multiplying by \(D(t)=\prod_i(1-ty_i)\) gives a polynomial value of modulus at least \((1-t)^{s-1}/2\).
Its degree is at most \(s-1\), and the coefficient at order \(j<s\) is \(\sum_{\ell=0}^j[t^\ell]D(t)\sum_i y_i^{m+1+j-\ell}\) by the power series at zero.
As the absolute coefficient sum of \(D\) is at most \(2^s\), these \(s\) coefficients prove (3).
No spacing of the \(y_i\)'s enters this estimate.

Order the original roots with \(|z_1|=e^b\).
Apply (3) to \(y_i=z_i/z_1,\ m=k-s\), where \(k\ge s\).
Restoring the powers of \(z_1\) costs a factor at worst \(e^{-Ar}\) relative to \(e^{bk}\).
Consequently an appropriate \(C_A>0\) gives
\[
 W=\sum_{j=k-H+1}^k|s_j|\ \ge e^{bk-C_A r\log(2rk)}
 \quad\text{once }H\ge s.                                                    \tag{4}
\]
The [positive measure degree bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) at a positive-measure irreducible factor gives \(L\gg_A(\log(2d))^{-3}\), as in [the general monic input estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#cost).
Thus \(L/r\le b\le L\le A\) and \(k\ll_{A,B}r(\log(2d))^4\ll_{A,B}(\log(2d))^5\) in the range of (1).
Also \(k\gg_{A,\delta}\log d\), whence \(H/r\to\infty\) uniformly.
Writing \(\epsilon=\gamma/12\) gives \(bH\gg_{A,B,\delta}(\log d)^{1-5\epsilon}\).
Here \(r\log(2rk)=O_{A,B}((\log d)^{1-\gamma}\log\log(3d))=o(bH)\) and \(r\log(2rk)=o(\log d)\).
In particular
\[
       0\le V(k)-W\le rk e^{bk-bH}=o(W).                                    \tag{5}
\]
This choice of width absorbs the cancellation loss in (4) uniformly.

<a id="signed"></a>
## Signed divisor weights and matching upper cost

Use the [divisor construction](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#divisors) with \(J=[k-H+1,k]\cap\mathbb Z\) and \(R=k^{\epsilon/4}\).
It assigns to \(l\) the weight \(w_l=l f(j/l)\) if \(j\in J,\ l\mid j,\ j/l\le R\), zero otherwise, where \(f(s)=\max(0,\min(1,1-\log s/\log R))\).
The multiple \(j\) is unique because \((k-H)/R>H\) eventually.
For arbitrary signs \(\epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}\), set \(\widetilde w_l=\epsilon_jw_l\) for these \(l\) and zero elsewhere.
Uniformly in \(n\ge1\) and in the signs,
\[
            \left|\sum_{l\mid n}\mu(n/l)\widetilde w_l\right|
                                      \le(1+o(1))\varphi(n).                    \tag{6}
\]
Indeed for \(n\le kR\) all the contributing terms have the same \(j\).
The reduced denominators of \(n/j\) are at most \(R\) and their variation over \(J\) is \(O(RH/k)=o(R^{-2})\), since \(R^3 H/k=O(k^{-\epsilon/4})\).
The unsigned sum in that proof is a scalar in \([0,1]\) times a Ramanujan sum of absolute value at most \(\varphi(n)\), with an error \(o(\varphi(n))\) in absolute value.
Its displayed error bound is \(O(\varphi(n)(1+\log\log(3n))^6/\log R)=o(\varphi(n))\) as \(\epsilon\) is fixed.
For \(n>kR\), the same divisor estimate \(k\tau(n)/\varphi(n)\ll_\nu k n^{-1+\nu}\) tends uniformly to zero on taking \(0<\nu<\epsilon/(4+\epsilon)\).
The estimates are uniform and \(|\widetilde w_l|\le l\), with \(\widetilde w_j=\epsilon_j j\) on \(J\).

Let \(p_l\) be the full power sums, and \(c_l\) the initial Euler exponents, so \(lc_l=\sum_{e\mid l}\mu(l/e)p_e\).
For a monic cyclotomic comparator of degree \(D\), each multiplicity \(b_n\) in the primitive factorization contributes \(b_n\mu(n/l)\) to \(c_l\) when \(l\mid n\).
As \(\sum_n b_n\varphi(n)=D\) with \(b_n\ge0\), (6) gives \(\sum_{l\le k}\widetilde w_l c_l\le(1+o(1))D\).
The [reflected power-sum estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#cost) replaces every exterior root by its reflection into the disk and bounds the absolute value of the resulting \(j\)-th sum by \(C_A\sqrt{jd\log(2d)}\).
Subtracting the reflected exterior terms therefore gives
\[
           |p_j-s_j|\le E_k=r+C_A\sqrt{kd\log(2d)},\qquad 1\le j\le k.
\]
Every proper divisor of \(l\le k\) is at most \(k/2\), so
\[
                  |lc_l-s_l|\le (k+1)(E_k+r e^{bk/2}).                         \tag{7}
\]
The sum of these errors over \(l\le k\), divided by (4), tends uniformly to zero.
Indeed \(e^{C_A r\log(2rk)}=d^{o(1)}\), \(e^{bk}\ge d^{1/2+\delta}\), and \(r,k\) are polylogarithmic in \(d\).
Choose \(\epsilon_j\) to have the sign of \(s_j\), either sign at zero.
Then (5) and (7) give \(\sum_l\widetilde w_l c_l\ge W-(V(k)-W)-o(W)=(1-o(1))W\).
This proves the degree lower bound.

For the upper bound, the [cyclotomic packet inequality (5)](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets) constructs a comparator with degree at most
\[
      \sum_{k/2<j\le k}|p_j|+
      2k\sum_{j\le k/2}\frac{|p_j|}{j}(1+\log(k/j)).
\]
The first sum is at most \(W+(V(k)-W)+kE_k\).
The second is \(O(k(1+\log k)^2(E_k+r e^{bk/2}))\) by the harmonic sum bound.
All the errors are \(o(W)\) by (4), (5) and the same degree range.
If a constructed reversal has degree less than \(k\), multiplying its monic polynomial by \(X^{k+1}-1\) preserves all matched reversal coefficients and adds \(k+1=o(W)\) to the degree.
This proves (1).

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Exact degree and the last two power sums

Use
\[
 k_-=\left\lfloor(\log d-Cr\log\log(3d))/b\right\rfloor,\qquad
 k_+=\left\lceil(\log d+Cr\log\log(3d))/b\right\rceil
\]
with \(C\) a sufficiently large fixed constant depending on \(A\).
Both satisfy the range of (1) with \(\delta=1/4,\ B=2\) eventually, since \(r\log\log(3d)=o(\log d)\) and each rounding costs at most \(A\) after multiplication by \(b\).
At \(k_-\), \(V(k_-)\le rk_-e^{bk_-}=o(d)\), so the minimal completion of degree \(D\) leaves \(d-D>k_-\).
Padding by \(X^{d-D}-1\) gives an actual degree-\(d\) comparator.
At \(k_+\), use \(2rk_+\ll_A(\log(2d))^6\), so \(\log(2rk_+)\le7\log\log(3d)\) eventually.
Taking \(C>7C_A+10\) and \(C>10\) gives \(V(k_+)\ge d(\log(3d))^2\) by (4), and also suffices for the preceding upper bound at \(k_-\).
There is no degree-\(d\) completion there, and prefix feasibility is decreasing in length.
The two integers enclose \(K\) and prove its estimate in (2).
In particular \(K,K+1\) are both in the range of (1).
Since \(D(P,K)\le d\), (1) gives \(V(K)\le(1+o(1))d\).
At \(K+1\) any minimum degree \(D<d-(K+1)\) would permit the same exact-degree padding.
It follows that \(D(P,K+1)\ge d-(K+1)\), proving the other estimate in (2).

When \(br(2e)^r\to0\), consecutive cumulative costs have ratio tending to one at this cutoff.
To see this, let \(q=\lfloor1/b\rfloor\), so \(q\to\infty\) and \(rq<K\) eventually by \(r=o(\log d)\) and (2).
For each \(1\le h\le q\), use the characteristic polynomial \(\prod_i(T-z_i^h)\) and multiply the identity at each \(z_i^h\) by \(z_i^{K+1-sh}\).
Summation gives a recurrence expressing \(s_{K+1}\) as a linear combination of \(s_{K+1-jh}\) for \(1\le j\le s\).
Each coefficient has modulus at most \(\binom{s}{j}e^{b h j}\le 2^r e^r\).
After summing the absolute bounds over \(h\), each earlier moment occurs at most \(r\) times, one \(h\) for each possible \(j\).
Thus \(q|s_{K+1}|\le r2^r e^r V(K)\) and \(V(K+1)=V(K)(1+o(1))\).
The two one-sided estimates in (2) now give the claimed limits.
Finally, for bounded \(r\), \(b\le L\le rb\) yields the stated equivalence between shrinking measure and the prefix-to-log-degree ratio tending to infinity.
