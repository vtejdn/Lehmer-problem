# Gaps between coefficients multiply the cost of repeated contacts

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sharp cost for two rows with separated nonzero coefficients

Fix distinct monic reciprocal irreducible unit minima \(P_i\) of positive log Mahler measures \(h_i\), and even orders \(2u_i\ge2\).
Assume that no quotient of two distinct roots in their combined list is a root of unity.
Put \(S=\sum_i u_i h_i\) and fix an integer \(r\ge1\).
In each of two integer rows \(A,B\) require successive nonzero coefficient indices to differ by at least \(r\), independently in the two rows.
For nonzero graphs \(A(X^n)-XB(X^n)\) containing \(\prod_iP_i^{2u_i}\) one has, without any width bound or numerator nonvanishing hypothesis,
\[
            \liminf_{n\to\infty} \frac1n\log\max(\|A\|_1,\|B\|_1)
                        \ \ge\ rS .
\]
For either sign this lower slope is sharp on genuine signed rows at formal widths \(r b(n)\) for any \(b(n)\to\infty\).
In particular rows of slope \(S+o(1)\) must eventually have adjacent nonzero coefficients in at least one row.

More locally, consider the limiting upper envelope of the reversed coefficient logarithms on a bounded-slope subsequence.
At a particular exterior radius \(R\) put \(m_R=\sum_{|c|=R}u(c)\), where \(u(c)=u_i\) at a root of \(P_i\).
Within the maximizing index interval, at least one of the two coordinates must itself attain the envelope at \(m_R+1\) distinct indices.
If the graph attains slope \(S\), then at each exterior radius one coordinate attains it at every index of a consecutive interval of length \(m_R+1\), after extraction of the coefficient limits.
The coordinate can depend on the radius.

<a id="minors"></a>
## A confluent evaluation minor for arbitrary distinct monomials

Let \(g_i\) be fixed algebraic numbers of complex modulus one, with pairwise nontorsion quotients.
For nonnegative integers \(v_i\) of sum \(k>0\) and distinct nonnegative exponents \(j_1<\cdots<j_k\), consider the square matrix
\[
                 \big(j_b^\ell g_i^{nj_b}\big)_{(i,\,0\le\ell<v_i),b}.
\]
Use \(j^0=1\).
Its determinant is a power sum of unit-modulus algebraic bases and has modulus \(\exp(o(n))\) at all sufficiently large integers.
To see noncancellation, take the finitely generated multiplicative group of the \(g_i\) modulo its torsion subgroup.
It is free abelian, and the images of the \(g_i\) are distinct.
Choose a real linear functional on the resulting lattice with distinct values on those images, by avoiding finitely many hyperplanes.
In the determinant expansion a base of maximal functional value must assign the \(j_b\) in increasing order to the nodes in increasing functional order, with \(v_i\) exponents assigned to each \(i\).
This allocation of column sets is unique by strict rearrangement.
Permuting those columns within each node leaves the actual base unchanged.
Their combined coefficient is up to sign a product of nonzero Vandermonde determinants in the assigned integers \(j_b\).
Every other allocation has a strictly smaller value, so no other base with torsion quotient can cancel this term.
The argument includes a single node, where the whole determinant has just one base.

After separating residue classes modulo the torsion orders of all base ratios, each class therefore has a nonzero term and its remaining distinct bases have nontorsion quotients.
The [local recurrence bound](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives the asserted rate and eventual nonvanishing on every class.
The empty evaluation minor will have value one.

<a id="cost"></a>
## How many coefficient indices a contact uses at one radius

Take limits of the normalized logarithms of both individual coefficient coordinates as well as of their maximum on the [finite truncation](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii).
After passing to a subsequence this uses only finitely many indices; a zero coordinate has logarithm \(-\infty\).
At \(s=\log R\) retain the positions in each coordinate whose limiting exponent, after subtraction of the index times \(s\), equals the upper envelope there.
All other positions and the tail are exponentially smaller than the largest retained coefficient after scaling as in the [even-order derivative equations](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#jets).
Shifting by the first maximizing index makes the retained sets of offsets \(J_A,J_B\) nonnegative.
The retained coefficient vector has maximum norm one after normalization, and at every required node the normalized jet equations have exponentially small residual.

Suppose both sets have size at most \(m_R\).
In the polynomial matrix of those equations with the [two-center derivative basis](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#jets), put \(X=0\) in the formal parameter replacing \(n\).
The rows of one type now act on \(J_A\) alone, and those of the other type on \(J_B\) alone.
For each coordinate choose at each node an initial segment of its Euler derivative orders, of length at most \(u(c)\), totaling the size of its own set.
The square submatrix on all retained columns at \(X=0\) has determinant equal to a nonzero constant times two of the preceding evaluation minors, with \(g_i=R/c_i\).
These phases have pairwise nontorsion quotients by hypothesis.
The determinant is therefore nonzero at every sufficiently large positive phase exponent.

At general \(X\) the determinant has the form \(\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n\) with algebraic polynomial coefficients and unit-modulus algebraic bases.
In any residue class modulo the orders of torsion ratios among these bases, choose a positive representative \(a_0\) large enough to make the specialization at \(X=0\) nonzero.
Grouping terms by equal powers of the bases leaves at least one nonzero polynomial coefficient, since the sum of their values at zero is the nonzero specialization at \(a_0\).
The [grouped recurrence estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#recurrences) now bounds the inverse of the exact square submatrix at \(X=n\) by \(\exp(o(n))\), because its cofactors have polynomial growth.
Applied to the small residual, this contradicts the norm-one retained vector.
Thus one coordinate has at least \(m_R+1\) retained indices.

Reversing and translating the rows preserves the gaps between indices in each coordinate.
When the gaps are at least \(r\), the extreme maximizing indices are consequently at distance at least \(r m_R\).
Integrating their jumps gives the stated lower slope \(r\sum_R m_R\log R=rS\).
In the unrestricted case of slope \(S\), the weighted jumps already exhaust the available slope.
Hence the jump at \(R\) equals \(m_R\) and the \(m_R+1\) indices in the chosen coordinate must be consecutive.
For sharpness apply the [unrestricted even-order construction](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#statement) at exponent \(rn\) and width \(b(n)\).
All exponents separate the roots.
Substitution \(Y\mapsto Y^r\) in its signed row yields the identical graph at exponent \(n\), the required formal width \(r b(n)\), the same norm and genuine values, and coefficient gaps at least \(r\).

<a id="dilation"></a>
## A finite field and measure bound for a dilated reduced ratio

There is a separate necessary condition at a single exponent, without the torsion-quotient assumption.
Let a reduced inversion-symmetric rational function satisfy \(f(Y)=Y^s h(Y^r)\), where \(r\ge1,\ s=\operatorname{ord}_0 f\) and \(h(0)\) is finite and nonzero.
Assume \(f\) has rational coefficients and \(h\in\mathbb Q(Z)\).
Then \(h(Z)h(1/Z)=1\), so its numerator and denominator in lowest terms have equal degree \(d_h\) and nonzero constant terms.
In particular \(\deg f=|s|+r d_h\).
Let \(R_h\) be the primitive squarefree numerator of \(h\), a product of its distinct primitive irreducible numerator factors.
For a nontorsion unit \(c\) giving a genuine graph contact of order \(t\ge2\), put \(\beta=c^n,\ z=\beta^r,\ e=[\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q(z)]\), and \(D_c=[\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]\).
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e&\le\gcd(r,ns-1),\\
 (t-1)D_c/e&\le2\deg R_h,\\
 nr(t-1)m(c)/e &\le 2m(R_h)+\log(1+n\deg f).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed \(c=f(\beta)\) gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(c)\) and \(\beta^{ns-1}=h(z)^{-n}\).
Together with \(\beta^r=z\) this places \(\beta^{\gcd(r,ns-1)}\) in \(\mathbb Q(z)\) by Bézout, giving the field bound.
The scaled logarithmic derivative is
\[
            nY f'/f-1=(ns-1)+nr Z h'/h,\qquad Z=Y^r .
\]
Clear just the simple poles of its right side by the product \(L_h\) of distinct primitive zeros and pole factors of \(h\), as in the [squarefree numerator argument](Yin_C_G0345_v441.md#radical).
The resulting nonzero integer polynomial has degree \(2\deg R_h\) and is divisible by the minimum of \(z\) to order \(t-1\), since substitution is unramified at \(\beta\) and no removed factor vanishes there.
Its leading multiplier is \(ns-1\ne0\), by equality of the numerator and denominator degrees of \(h\).
On the unit circle the [logarithmic derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v418.md#circle) bounds the mean absolute value of the right side by \(1+n(|s|+r d_h)\).
Jensen's inequality bounds the logarithmic measure of the cleared polynomial by \(m(L_h)+\log(1+n\deg f)\), with \(m(L_h)=2m(R_h)\).
The minimum of \(z\) has degree \(D_c/e\) and logarithmic measure \(nr\,m(c)/e\), which proves the remaining assertions.
For \(1<M(c)\le1.06\), every such power field has [degree at least 86](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree), forcing \(2\deg R_h\ge86(t-1)\) at every dilation and exponent.

For fixed input as in the first statement, a support dilation in the reduced ratio itself also costs at least \(rS\) at sublinear formal widths.
More precisely, suppose the signed ratios have a representation above with some integer dilation \(r_n\ge r\) at every selected \(n\), and \(b=o(n)\).
The reduced numerator and denominator are each supported on one residue class modulo \(r_n\).
They are primitive divisors up to sign of the original signed numerator and its reversal after common factors are removed.
Their log one-norms exceed their log Mahler measures by at most \(b\log 2\), and their logarithmic measures are at most the logarithm of the original row norm.
Genuine contact preserves the designated orders on cancellation.
Applying the gap estimate to the reduced rows gives the same lower slope \(rS\) for the original norms.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin derived the support constraint and power-field calculation while investigating how the [squarefree numerator cost](Yin_C_G0345_v441.md#loss) can be realized.
