# Residue roots at Salem seeds eight and nine and the limits of finite delay tests

<a id="statement"></a>
## Further exclusions and delays that match prescribed local data

For an integer seed \(a\ge3\), delay \(n\ge7\) and sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\), write \(G_{a,n,\epsilon}=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\) and \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}=G_{a,n,\epsilon}/U\) for the minimal polynomial of its exterior Salem root \(r\).
Here \(U\) consists of the distinct linear factors at \(u=\pm1\) with \(u^{n-1}=\epsilon\).
The following are additional necessary conditions for the [matching-prime radicals at seeds eight and nine](Yin_C_G0345_v716.md#statement).

| Seed and positive radical in \(\mathbb Q(r)\) | Sign | Additional excluded delays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(a=8,\ r^{1/2}\) | \(+\) | \(n\equiv4,14\bmod22\) |
| \(a=9,\ r^{1/3}\) | \(+\) | \(n\equiv4,16,22,28,34\bmod36\) |
| \(a=9,\ r^{1/3}\) | \(-\) | \(n\equiv7,10,13,22,25,31,34\bmod36\) |

The first line excludes \(2/11\) of the delays remaining at eight after v716.
At nine the two lines exclude respectively five and seven of the twelve classes modulo 36 allowed by \(n\equiv1\bmod3\).

Conversely fix any finite list \((b_i,F_i)\), where \(b_i\ge2\) is an integer and \(F_i\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is monic of positive degree with constant coefficient invertible modulo \(b_i\).
Use \(B_i=(\mathbb Z/b_i\mathbb Z)[X]/(F_i)\).
There is an effectively computable \(L\ge1\) such that, for each sufficiently large multiple \(M\) of \(L\), the following holds in all three lines.
Take \(n=n_0+M\), where \(n_0=2\) at eight and \(n_0=1\) at nine, and write \(p=2,3\) respectively.
There exists a monic irreducible Salem polynomial \(S\) of exactly \(\deg P_{a,n,\epsilon}\) such that its polynomial of \(p\)-th powers satisfies
\[
                         S^{[p]}=P_{a,n,\epsilon}\quad\text{in every }B_i.       \tag{1}
\]
Here powering raises each root with its multiplicity.
In fact both sides agree there with the explicitly determined reference \(P_{a,n_0,\epsilon}\) below.
For each finite list this gives an arithmetic progression of positive natural density.
It includes finite collections of residue roots, Frobenius orbits and prescribed finite jets at units, at composite moduli as well as prime moduli.

There is also a simultaneous growing range.
For each integer \(Y\ge11\), take \(M=6\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Y)^2\) and \(J=\lfloor(Y-2)/3\rfloor\).
For each prime \(h\le Y\) and each \(1\le e\le Y\) coprime to \(h\), (1) holds with \(b_i=h\), \(F_i=(X^e-1)^J\), simultaneously at those same delays.
Here \(n\le2+6\cdot16^Y\).
Thus unbounded example delays match characteristic, root order and jet depth all through constant multiples of \(\log n\).

<a id="obstruction"></a>
## A residue root whose multiplicity cannot come from powering

If a positive radical exists in the field, its minimal polynomial \(S\) satisfies \(S^{[p]}=P_{a,n,\epsilon}\) over the integers by the [degree-preserving field argument](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius).
Let \(h\equiv1\bmod p\) be prime and \(x\in\mathbb F_h^\times\) with \(x^{(h-1)/p}\ne1\).
For every \(p\)-th root \(y\) of \(x\), its Frobenius image is \(y^h=yx^{(h-1)/p}\).
The multiplier has exact order \(p\) and belongs to \(\mathbb F_h\), so the whole fiber is one orbit of size \(p\).
Its members have equal multiplicity in \(\overline S\), hence a necessary condition is that the multiplicity of \(x\) in \(\overline P_{a,n,\epsilon}\) be divisible by \(p\).

Assume \(h\nmid a(a^2-1)\) and \(x\ne0,\pm1\) is a root of \(\overline G_{a,n,\epsilon}\).
Neither \(x=a\) nor \(ax=1\) can occur.
The multiplicity of \(x\) is the same in \(\overline P_{a,n,\epsilon}\) and in \(L(X)-\epsilon\), where \(L(X)=X^n(X-a)/(1-aX)\).
Logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{XL'(X)}{L(X)}
       =n-\frac{a^2-1}{1+a^2-a(X+X^{-1})}.
\]
At \(x\) the denominator is nonzero.
Its derivative \(-a(1-x^{-2})\) is nonzero as well, so the displayed right side has a simple zero whenever it vanishes at \(x\).
A root of \(L-\epsilon\) of multiplicity at least three would force \(L'\) to vanish at least twice.
Thus the multiplicity is at most two.
Moreover all multiple roots outside \(0,\pm1\) have the same \(x+x^{-1}\), since the displayed rational function of that trace is injective away from its pole.
A noncube root therefore rules out \(p=3\), and two nonsquare roots with different traces rule out \(p=2\).

The following residues supply all the exclusions in the statement.
The column \(k\) is \(n\bmod(h-1)\).
In each row substitution gives \((x-a)x^k+\epsilon(ax-1)=0\bmod h\).

| \(a\) | \(\epsilon\) | \(h\) | \(k\) | \(x\) | \(x^k\bmod h\) | \(x^{(h-1)/p}\bmod h\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | \(+1\) | 23 | 4 | 7, 17 | 9, 8 | 22, 22 |
| 8 | \(+1\) | 23 | 14 | 5, 15 | 13, 6 | 22, 22 |
| 9 | \(+1\) | 13 | 4 | 6 | 9 | 9 |
| 9 | \(-1\) | 13 | 10 | 6 | 4 | 9 |
| 9 | \(+1\) | 19 | 4 | 2 | 16 | 7 |
| 9 | \(+1\) | 19 | 16 | 3 | 17 | 7 |
| 9 | \(-1\) | 19 | 13 | 2 | 3 | 7 |
| 9 | \(-1\) | 19 | 7 | 3 | 2 | 7 |

At 23 the traces of the two roots in the first row are 17 and 13, and in the second row they are 19 and 12.
Since the old even-seed restrictions depend on \(n\bmod24\) and all admissible delays are even, the two eliminated even classes modulo 22 remove two out of eleven equally frequent classes modulo 11 by the Chinese remainder theorem.
At nine take the union of the indicated classes modulo 12 and 18 at each sign, giving the list modulo 36 in the statement.

<a id="reference"></a>
## The three small-delay references

At delays one and two use the same polynomial quotient by the indicated \(U\) as an algebraic definition.
These delays lie outside the family under investigation.
Put \(S_0=X^2-3X+1\) and \(\theta=(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Since \(\theta+\theta^{-1}=3\), the reciprocal traces of \(\theta^2,\theta^3\) are 7 and 18, respectively.
Direct substitution gives
\[
 P_{8,2,+}=X^2-7X+1=S_0^{[2]},\qquad
 P_{9,1,-}=X^2-18X+1=S_0^{[3]},\qquad
 P_{9,1,+}=1.
\]
The first two powered roots generate \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) and have the indicated positive radical in that field.
In the third case \(G_{9,1,+}=X^2-1=U\); the quotient has no roots.

<a id="congruence"></a>
## Matching the powered polynomial at every specified unit

For a fixed seed and sign and delays \(n_0+M,n_0\), the difference of the two numerator polynomials is
\[
                      (X-a)X^{n_0}(X^M-1).                             \tag{2}
\]
If \(M\) is even the cofactor \(U\) is the same.
In the ring \((\mathbb Z/b_i\mathbb Z)[X]\), it is enough that \(X^M-1\) be divisible by \(UF_i\) for the quotient difference to vanish modulo \(F_i\).
Cancellation of the common monic \(U\) is valid even with zero divisors in the coefficient ring.

For each \(i\) put \(H_i=(X^2-1) F_i(X)F_i(X^2)F_i(X^3)\).
The residue of \(X\) is a unit in the finite ring modulo \(b_i,H_i\), because \(H_i\) is monic and its constant coefficient is a unit modulo \(b_i\).
Take \(L\) to be the least common multiple of six and the finite orders of these units.
For every \(M\) in the asserted progression, \(H_i\) divides \(X^M-1\) modulo \(b_i\).
Since all the possible \(U\) divide \(X^2-1\), (2) shows \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}=P_{a,n_0,\epsilon}\) in \(B_i\).

For the first two references take as comparison
\[
                       S=P_{4,M+2,+}.
\]
This is a monic irreducible Salem polynomial for \(M+2\ge7\) by the [integer-seed classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots).
Its degree is \(M+2\), also the degree of the respective target in these two cases.
Its reference at delay two is \(P_{4,2,+}=S_0\).
Since the auxiliary cofactor is \(X-1\), (2) gives \(S(Z)\equiv S_0(Z)\bmod (b_i,F_i(Z^p))\).
To pass this congruence through root powering, work in the free rank-\(p\) algebra \(B_i[Z]/(Z^p-X)\), where \(X\) denotes the residue of the variable in \(B_i\).
The two elements \(S(Z),S_0(Z)\) agree there, so multiplication by them has equal determinants.
For a monic polynomial \(A\) of even degree that determinant is \(A^{[p]}(X)\).
Indeed over \(\mathbb Z[X]\) it is \(\operatorname{Res}_Z(Z^p-X,A(Z))=(-1)^{(p+1)\deg A}A^{[p]}(X)\), by the root product, and determinants commute with coefficient reduction.
This proves (1) for both nontrivial references.

For \(a=9,\epsilon=+1\), the target at \(n=1+M\) has degree \(M\).
Choose an integer \(A\ge3\) divisible by every \(b_i\) and take \(S=P_{A,M,+}\).
At this even delay its cofactor is \(X-1\), so coefficientwise modulo \(b_i\),
\[
                      S\equiv D_M=(X^{M+1}-1)/(X-1).
\]
Since \(3\mid M\), cubing permutes all the nontrivial \((M+1)\)-st roots of unity and \(D_M^{[3]}=D_M\) over \(\mathbb Z\).
The coefficients of the powered polynomial are integral polynomials in the original coefficients, by the resultant formula.
It follows that \(S^{[3]}\equiv D_M\bmod b_i\).
In \(B_i\), \(D_M-1=X(X^M-1)/(X-1)=0\), proving (1) with the constant reference one.
The same seed classification gives the required irreducible Salem signature of \(S\).

For example, taking \(b_i=h,\ F_i=(X^e-1)^J\) with \(h\) prime and \(h\nmid e\) matches all Hasse derivatives of orders below \(J\) at every \(e\)-th root of unity in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_h\).
Taking \(J\ge3\) matches the exact root multiplicities there, since the reference polynomial has degree at most two, including when its reduction has a double root.
The finite-ring result also allows \(b_i=h^v\) with arbitrary prescribed finite \(v\).
It does not identify the full reductions of the degree-growing polynomials at even one fixed characteristic.
A test that factors the whole polynomial modulo a fixed prime can see roots of growing order, and an unbounded-precision local test or an archimedean restriction on the putative radical imposes other data.
The constructed comparison polynomials certify indistinguishability at the specified quotients; the existence of radicals for the genuine large delays remains unresolved.

<a id="window"></a>
## A growing range of simultaneously matched orders and multiplicities

Let \(B=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Y)\) and take \(M=6B^2\).
For every prime \(h\le Y\), the largest power of \(h\) at most \(Y\) is at least \(\sqrt Y\): this is true already of \(h\) if \(h>\sqrt Y\), while otherwise maximality gives \(h^{v_h(B)}>Y/h\).
Thus \(h^{v_h(M)}\ge Y\).
For \(e\le Y\) prime to \(h\) and \(p=2,3\), one has \(pe\mid M\).
All roots of \(Z^{pe}-1\) are roots of \(Z^M-1\) of multiplicity \(h^{v_h(M)}\) in characteristic \(h\).
Each has multiplicity at most \(pJ\) in \((Z^{pe}-1)^J\), since \(h\nmid e\).
As \(3J+2\le Y\), deletion of up to two linear factors, including collision in characteristic two, leaves enough multiplicity at each root.
Consequently (2) applies separately modulo \((X^e-1)^J\) to the target and modulo \((Z^{pe}-1)^J\) to the auxiliary source for the first two references.
The same determinant argument passes the latter congruence through powering.
In the third case take the auxiliary seed divisible by each \(h\le Y\); \(X^M-1\) supplies the required multiplicity after dividing out \(X-1\) in \(D_M-1\).
This proves the stated simultaneous range, with \(n\le2+6\cdot16^Y\) by the [elementary bound \(B\le4^Y\)](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#all).
As \(Y\) tends to infinity these delays are unbounded.
For instance \(n\le32^Y\) and \(J\ge Y/6\) here, so the claimed logarithmic window follows with absolute constants.
