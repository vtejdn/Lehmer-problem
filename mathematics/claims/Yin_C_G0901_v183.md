# A uniform three-root bound in a dyadic disk for an individual trace-pole solution

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on one complete orbit, with an absolute radius

Let \(F\) be the reciprocal Salem minimum of any genuine [integral trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), of any allowed degree and height.
Extend the dyadic valuation to the algebraic closure with \(v(2)=1\).
Every closed disk \(v(z-c)\ge3\) contains at most three distinct roots of \(F\).
In particular **at most eight roots of \(F\) belong to \(\mathbb Q_2\)**.
In any nonzero residue class of multiplicative order different from one and three, two distinct roots of \(F\) cannot even satisfy \(v(z_1-z_2)>1\).
These restrictions are internal to one polynomial, whereas the [cross-solution separation](Yin_C_G0901_v182.md#statement) compares different polynomials at a precision depending on their degrees.

For every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, every fixed odd prime \(\ell\), and every prescribed integer \(k\ge4\), there are single-prime rational models of arbitrarily large degrees meeting all the archimedean, exact pole and zero-moment conditions of the [joint construction](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) and having at least \(k\) distinct \(\mathbb Q_2\)-roots in \(3+8\mathbb Z_2\).
Hence the disk bound distinguishes whole orbits that pass those earlier tests, at the fixed good prime two.

<a id="disk"></a>
## Counting zeros by the derivative of a multiplicative relation

Write \(p=q+1,\ x=q/p,\ \epsilon=1\) if \(q\) is even, and \(p=q+1,\ x=p/q,\ \epsilon=-1\) if \(q\) is odd.
Set \(V(x,z)=(1-x/z)/(1-xz)\).
For the [coprime power exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) put \(m=\epsilon u,\ N=v_0-\epsilon u\), where \(v_0>u\) denotes the long integer exponent.
Every conjugate is a dyadic unit and satisfies \(z^N=V(x,z)^m\).
The parameter \(x\) lies in \(2\mathbb Z_2\).

If a disk contains a root \(z_0\), write every point in it as \(z=z_0(1+w)\), \(v(w)\ge3\).
We may work in a finite dyadic extension containing any selected roots.
Writing \(A=(1-x/z_0)^{-1}\) and \(B=xz_0/(1-xz_0)\) gives
\[
 \frac{V(x,z)}{V(x,z_0)}=\frac{1+Aw}{(1+w)(1-Bw)} .
\]
Both \(A,B\) are integral and \(A\) is a unit.
Taking convergent logarithms after division by the equation at \(z_0\), every other root in the disk, as well as \(w=0\), is a zero of
\[
 G(w)=(N+m)\log(1+w)-m\log(1+Aw)+m\log(1-Bw).
\]
Its derivative is a rational function \(P(w)/Q(w)\) with \(\deg P\le2,\ \deg Q\le3\), integral coefficients and \(Q(0)=1\).
Indeed logarithmic differentiation gives three simple fractions.
The derivative cannot vanish identically, since \(z^N/V(x,z)^m\) has a nontrivial zero or pole at \(z=x\) in characteristic zero, and \(x\notin\{0,1,-1\}\).

For \(G'(w)=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jw^j\), the least valuation of any \(h_j\) is already attained among \(h_0,h_1,h_2\).
This follows from the integral recurrence from \(QG'=P\) at all indices \(j\ge3\).
If this minimum is \(t\), at least one coefficient of \(G(8y)\) in degrees one through three has valuation at most \(t+9\).
For \(j\ge4\) its coefficient has valuation at least \(t+3j-v_2(j)>t+9\), tending to infinity.
Such a series has at most three zeros in the closed unit disk.
Indeed if its last coefficient of maximal absolute value is at index \(h\), division by \(y-a\) for an integral root \(a\) reduces that last index to \(h-1\): the new coefficient at \(j\) is the convergent sum \(\sum_{k>j}c_k a^{k-j-1}\) in terms of the old coefficients.
Iteration proves the bound.
This applies to any finite extension containing four hypothetical distinct roots, establishing the first assertion.

In \(\mathbb Q_2\) the odd units form four disks modulo eight.
Inversion preserves each disk because every odd square is one modulo eight.
The reciprocal roots occur in pairs with no fixed points there, since \(F\) has neither \(1\) nor \(-1\) as a root.
Thus each of these four disks contains at most two rational dyadic roots.

<a id="simple"></a>
## Simple separation outside two residue orders

Suppose \(z_0\) reduces to an element of multiplicative order different from one and three.
Set \(c=\min(v_2(N),v_2(mx))\).
The coefficient of \(w\) in \(G\) has exactly this valuation.
Indeed it is \(N-m(A-1+B)\), and \((A-1+B)/x\) reduces to \(\bar z_0+\bar z_0^{-1}\).
This residue is neither zero nor one: those two values would require \(\bar z_0=1\) or \(\bar z_0^2+\bar z_0+1=0\).
Thus even in the equal-valuation case the two leading terms cannot cancel, as the unit parts of the rational scalars reduce to one.
At degree \(j\ge2\) the logarithm expansions give a coefficient of valuation at least \(c-v_2(j)\): \(A^j-1\) and \(B^j\) are both divisible by \(x\).
For \(v(w)>1\) and \(w\ne0\), the linear term therefore has strictly smaller valuation than every later term.
It cannot vanish in their sum.
This proves the stronger separation.

<a id="control"></a>
## Exact-pole models with many roots in a single rational disk

Fix \(q,\ell,k\) as in the statement.
Use sufficiently large degrees \(2D\) admitted by the joint construction, with \(J=q/2+1\) and \(D-J-2\ge k\).
In its trace variable \(X=z+z^{-1}-2\), the integral affine lattice of the specified zero prefix and pole has the form \(H_1+(q(q+1)X-1)G\), with integral \(H_1,G\) and \(\deg G\le D-J-2\). [Pole and prefix lattice](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place)
Choose \(k\) distinct positive odd integers \(z_j=3+8j,\ 1\le j\le k\), and let \(a_j=z_j+z_j^{-1}-2\).
These are distinct dyadic integers.
Write \(C(X)=\prod_j(X-a_j)\).
In the finite free algebra \(\mathbb Z_2[X]/C\), \(q(q+1)X-1\) is invertible by reduction modulo two.
Choose a representative \(G_0\in\mathbb Z_2[X]\) of degree less than \(k\) so that \(C\) divides \(H_1+(q(q+1)X-1)G_0\).
Adding \(cC\) to \(G_0\) for a dyadic integer \(c\) leaves the prescribed zeros intact; each of the \(k\) derivative tests excludes at most one value of \(c\), since \((q(q+1)a_j-1)C'(a_j)\ne0\).
Thus a dyadic polynomial in this affine space has the \(a_j\) as simple roots and its lift has the \(z_j\) as simple roots, since \(z_j^2\ne1\).

Choose disjoint rational dyadic neighborhoods of the \(z_j\) within \(3+8\mathbb Z_2\).
Every sufficiently close coefficient vector for the reciprocal lift preserves a rational root in each of these neighborhoods by simple-root Hensel lifting (choose coefficient precision exceeding twice each derivative valuation and the prescribed neighborhood requirements).
Approximate the coefficients of \(G_0+cC\) by ordinary integers at this precision.
The resulting **integral** pole-and-prefix representative specifies a compatible residue class modulo \(2^b\) for some finite \(b\).
As \(\ell\) is odd, the [simultaneous one-prime approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) meets this class together with the original real tests, irreducibility, all powered capacities, exact pole and zero moments.
Its global coefficient denominator still contributes \(\ell^a\) to its primitive Mahler measure.
This gives the asserted controls on the same rational polynomial.
