# Finite-place distributions of individual plane-curve factors and an all-section gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## A Newton polygon test for an individual factor

Let \(F=\sum_{c\in A}f_c X^{c_1}Y^{c_2}\) be primitive integral, irreducible over the rationals in the Laurent ring, with rank-two support differences.
Write \(w_A\) for its projected width as in the [Newton-support height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#statement).
Fix a rational prime \(p\) and set \(A_p=\{c\in A:p\nmid f_c\}\).
For \(\|u\|_\infty=1\), form the lower convex hull of
\[
                          (c\cdot u,\ v_p(f_c)),\qquad c\in A,
\]
where \(v_p(p)=1\).
For each lower edge of positive horizontal length put an atom at the negative of its slope, of mass its horizontal length divided by \(w_A(u)\).
Call the resulting probability measure \(\nu_{p,u}\).

Take \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\), with \(\xi,\eta\) arbitrary roots of unity and \(z\) nonzero nontorsion, and put \(N=\max(|a|,|b|)\), \(g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)\).
If \(N/g\to\infty\) and \((a,b)/N\to u\), the empirical distribution of \(N v_p(\sigma z)\) over the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(z)\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) tends weakly to \(\nu_{p,u}\).
The same limit holds for every choice of root and translations along these directions.
Moreover, putting \(d=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\), one has
\[
     \frac{\#\{\sigma:v_p(\sigma z)=0\}}{d}
                      \longrightarrow\frac{w_{A_p}(u)}{w_A(u)}.            \tag{1}
\]
In particular this identifies the limiting proportion for each *individual* factor, including reducible rational norms with unbounded torsion orders.

Every accumulation direction \(u\) of a sequence of distinct multiplicatively dependent algebraic-unit pairs on \(F=0\), including sequences in one fixed primitive direction, satisfies
\[
                    w_{A_p}(u)=w_A(u)\qquad\text{for every }p.             \tag{2}
\]
Equivalently the coefficients on each of the two exposed faces perpendicular to \(u\) have greatest common divisor one, separately on each face.
If no direction satisfies this finite test, \(F=0\) contains only finitely many multiplicatively dependent algebraic-unit pairs.
In that case there is a constant \(c_F>0\) such that every \(z\) as above satisfies \(m(z)\ge c_F\), over **all** directions, common dilations and torsion translations, including the finitely many points that can recur under arbitrary dilations.
Here directions with an identically zero section are excluded; a rank-two irreducible \(F\) has no such section at \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\).

<a id="local"></a>
## Continuous tests of the absolute valuation

On a subsequence fix which signed coordinate attains \(N\).
Write \(y_*\) for the corresponding curve coordinate and \(s\in\{-1,1\}\) for its exponent sign.
At every \(p\)-adic embedding one has
\[
                         N v_p(z)=s\,v_p(y_*).
\]
Use the smooth projective curve and the semipositive directional real divisor of the [individual factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#metric).
Its proof gives a generic small sequence of points on a fixed geometric component over a fixed number field, with absolute and essential minimum zero, for every selection of nontorsion roots from the given directions.
The points may be brought to that component by coefficient conjugation, preserving the full absolute \(p\)-adic empirical distribution.

For a continuous function \(\psi\) on the compact extended real line, use the trivial real divisor with Green function \(\psi(s\,v_p(y_*))\) at every place over \(p\), and zero elsewhere.
Here \(v_p(y_*)=-\log|y_*|/\log p\) extends continuously to the extended line on the analytic curve, through the morphism to the projective line with the designated values at zero and infinity.
These finitely many bounded invariant continuous functions give an adelic divisor, and its absolute height at a point is the average of the test over all \(p\)-adic embeddings.
Indeed summing the place weights and local relative orbit means is the same as averaging all absolute embeddings of a common field, since the test is the same at every place above \(p\).
The [global height convergence theorem](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height) therefore gives one common limiting integral of this test for every choice of roots and translations.
The second test divisor only needs continuous metrics.
The same argument compares any such choices with the roots of the untwisted sections, where torsion roots account for \(O_F(g)\) out of \(w_A(a,b)\) roots, [including all multiplicities](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#calibration).
The negligible torsion roots may be included, since the test is bounded.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Computing the common measure from an entire section

For the untwisted section, shift the lowest exponent to zero.
The valuations of its roots with multiplicity are the negatives of the Newton polygon slopes, counted by horizontal length.
To see the normalization, at real \(t\) the function
\[
               \min_{e}\{v_p(c_e)+e t\}
\]
for a polynomial \(\sum c_e Z^e\) is additive on products by multiplicativity of the weighted Gauss norm.
This follows first for rational \(t\) by scaling the variable over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), then for real \(t\) by continuity.
For a linear factor \(Z-\rho\) the function is \(\min(v_p(\rho),t)\).
Thus its change of slope records each root valuation once, giving the lower-polygon rule.

For the valuations multiplied by \(N\), use horizontal coordinates \(c\cdot(a,b)/N\), which converge to \(c\cdot u\).
The support projects injectively eventually because \(N/g\to\infty\).
The polygon distributions converge to the asserted \(\nu_{p,u}\).
Indeed for each \(t\) not a break of \(\min_c(v_p(f_c)+(c\cdot u)t)\), all minimizing points there have the same horizontal coordinate.
The slopes at that \(t\) for the approximating minima, including both one-sided slopes at a possible break, converge to that coordinate by finiteness of \(A\).
Subtracting the minimum horizontal coordinate and dividing by the full projected width gives the fraction of root valuations above \(t\) at continuity points of the limiting distribution.
This proves weak convergence, including when several points project to the same limiting coordinate, since its extreme horizontal coordinates delimit a probability distribution of full mass.
The common-limit argument thus proves the assertion for each individual root field.

The coefficient valuations are integers independent of torsion translations, since no terms collide in these long directions.
Every nonzero slope of the scaled polygon has absolute value at least \(1/C_F\), where \(C_F=\max_{\|v\|_\infty=1}w_A(v)>0\), because any two distinct integer coefficient valuations differ by at least one.
This holds under every \(p\)-adic embedding of the coefficient phases.
All scaled root valuations, and the support of the limit, therefore avoid a fixed punctured neighborhood of zero.
One continuous test counts exactly the zero valuations.
The horizontal lower edge at height zero stretches from the first to the last projected exponent with \(p\nmid f_c\), with length zero when these coincide.
This proves (1).

<a id="finite"></a>
## All directions of multiplicative dependence and repeated points

For a unit pair outside the finite torsion set choose the primitive exponents \((a,b)\) of its coordinates modulo torsion as in the [exact support formula](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#width).
Its generator \(z\) there can be chosen as a product of integer powers of the two units and is itself a unit.
If the primitive norm tends to infinity, (1) gives (2).
In a fixed primitive direction \((a,b)\), the character \(x^b y^{-a}\) takes torsion values along the sequence.
Its sup-metric pullback on the normalization has absolute and essential minimum zero.
Compare the generic sequence with growing untwisted directions approaching the same ray as in the [fixed-direction height proof](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#fixed), now testing by the bounded continuous valuation functions above.
The coordinate \(y_*\) has valuation zero at every embedding of each unit point.
Thus the limit polygon distribution is the unit point mass at zero in this case as well.
This gives (2) for every accumulation direction by compactness and passage to subsequences.
The two extreme faces both meet \(A_p\) exactly when \(w_{A_p}(u)=w_A(u)\).
Primitivity of \(F\) makes \(A_p\) nonempty, and only the finitely many primes dividing a nonzero coefficient require testing.
Exposed faces are constant on the relatively open cones of the finite normal fan of the Newton polygon, so the test over directions is also finite.

If no direction passes, infinitely many distinct dependent unit pairs would yield an accumulation direction contradicting (2).
For an arbitrary section, a root which is a nonunit has \(m(z)\ge\log 2\), by its primitive minimal polynomial's leading or constant coefficient.
For a nontorsion unit root the powered pair belongs to the resulting finite set of rank-one unit pairs.
For each such pair \((x,y)\) choose a fixed unit \(\beta=x^i y^j\) generating its cyclic subgroup modulo torsion, using Bézout on the primitive exponents.
For any occurrence of the pair at any common dilation \(g\), one has \(z^{\pm g}=\omega\beta\) for some root of unity \(\omega\).
The [externally twisted radical bound for a fixed unit](Yin_C_G0345_v767.md#external) gives a positive lower bound depending only on this \(\beta\), uniformly in \(\omega,g\) and the branch.
The same measure is assigned to \(z^{-1}\).
Taking the minimum over the finite set and \(\log 2\) proves the all-section gap.

<a id="example"></a>
## An explicit reciprocal parent with a nontorsion unit point

Put
\[
 \begin{split}
 A_0(X)&=2(3X-1)(3X^5-47),\qquad A_0^*(X)=X^6 A_0(1/X),\\
 B_0(X)&=X(X-3)(3X-1)(X^2-3X+1),\\
 F(X,Y)&=A_0(X)Y^4+B_0(X)Y^2+A_0^*(X).
 \end{split}
\]
The polynomial is primitive and reciprocal under simultaneous inversion.
Its Newton polygon is the full rectangle \([0,6]\times[0,4]\).
Modulo two the nonzero support lies strictly inside the rectangle, entirely in the row \(Y^2\), so every nonzero direction fails (2) at two.
The reduction itself has torus zeros, for instance at \(X=1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The polynomials \(A_0,A_0^*\) have no common zero: their respective moduli of zeros lie in the two disjoint sets \(\{1/3,(47/3)^{1/5}\}\) and \(\{3,(3/47)^{1/5}\}\).
At \(X=3\) the constant coefficient in \(Y\) has a simple zero, the middle coefficient vanishes and the leading coefficient is nonzero.
Eisenstein and Gauss's lemma give absolute irreducibility.
For either root \(\beta\) of \(X^2-3X+1\), the middle coefficient vanishes and
\[
 \frac{A_0(\beta)+A_0^*(\beta)}{2\beta^3}
       =56(\beta^3+\beta^{-3})-144(\beta^2+\beta^{-2})
       =56\cdot18-144\cdot7=0 .
\]
Thus \((\beta,1)\) is an actual nontorsion unit point.
The finiteness and positive gap above apply to all torsion-translated sections of this explicit reciprocal parent.
No numerical lower bound for its gap is obtained from the compactness argument.
