# Torsion translates and division of units in consecutive-coefficient sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Unit roots generate the same field as each of their powers

Let \(0<u<v\) be coprime integers, \(g\ge1\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), \(|a|=q\) and \(|b|=q+1\), where \(a,b\) are integers and \(q\ge2\).
Write
\[
 P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v).
\]
If \(\beta\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit root of \(P\), every root \(\zeta\beta\) of \(P\) with \(\zeta\) a root of unity equals \(\beta\).
This applies to distinct factors of the parent as well as conjugates within one factor.
Consequently \(\mathbb Q(\beta^n)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) for every integer \(n\ne0\).

Let \(\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) be the field of all totally real algebraic numbers in \(\mathbb C\).
For every nontorsion algebraic unit \(z\) satisfying \(P(z^g)=0\), one has
\[
                   z^n\notin\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\qquad(n\ne0).
\]
Thus powers, roots and torsion multiples of cyclotomic units, and more generally of units from arbitrary CM or totally real fields, cannot give a construction at any \(q\ge2\).
Along any sequence of solutions with \(q\to\infty\), roots from a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group of algebraic numbers, even after adjoining roots of all orders and roots of unity, are excluded by the quantitative relative-degree bound below.
These restrictions allow other moving fields and leave existence at unbounded \(q\) open.

<a id="translate"></a>
## Two points on the graph differing by torsion in both coordinates

Put \(B=b/a\) and \(R(T)=-\epsilon(1+BT)/(T+B)\).
Its numerator and denominator have no common root because \(B^2\ne1\).
We first show that for every nontorsion algebraic unit \(T\) and roots of unity \(t,w\), the identity \(R(tT)=wR(T)\) implies \(t=w=1\).
Both sides are defined because the rational pole \(-B\) is not an algebraic unit.
Expanding the identity and dividing by \(T\) yields
\[
 ab\,t(1-w)(T+t^{-1}T^{-1})
    =-a^2(1-wt)-b^2(t-w).
\]
All terms are algebraic integers in the field \(K=\mathbb Q(T,t,w)\).
As \((a,b)=1\), reduction modulo \(b\mathcal O_K\) gives \(1-wt\in b\mathcal O_K\).
Every conjugate of \(1-wt\) has modulus at most two, and \(|b|\ge3\).
The norm of \((1-wt)/b\) therefore has absolute value less than one and is an integer, so \(wt=1\).

If \(t\ne1\), cancel \(1-w\ne0\) and substitute \(w=t^{-1}\) to obtain
\[
 T+t^{-1}T^{-1}=-(b/a)(1+t^{-1}).
\]
This gives \(1+t^{-1}\in a\mathcal O_K\).
For \(t\ne1\) every conjugate of \(1+t^{-1}\) has modulus strictly less than two.
Since \(|a|\ge2\), the same norm argument gives \(1+t^{-1}=0\).
Now \(t=-1\) and the displayed equation gives \(T^2=1\), contrary to the hypothesis.
Thus \(t=w=1\).

For \(P(\beta)=P(\zeta\beta)=0\), take \(T=\beta^u,\ t=\zeta^u,\ w=\zeta^v\) in this graph calculation.
The coprimality of \(u,v\) forces \(\zeta=1\).
Two distinct conjugates of \(\beta\) with equal \(n\)-th powers would contradict this conclusion applied to either conjugate.
Equality of the numbers of distinct conjugates of \(\beta\) and \(\beta^n\), together with field inclusion, proves the asserted field equality.

<a id="real"></a>
## Exclusion over totally real and CM fields

We first exclude a totally real primitive unit \(\beta\).
By [the radial inequality and reciprocal symmetry](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#radius), each conjugate \(x\) satisfies
\[
 u\,|\log|x||<\operatorname{arcosh}(1+1/q)\le\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2).
\]
For \(u\ge2\) this gives \(|\log|x||<\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\).
Since \(x\) is real, \(|x-x^{-1}|=2\sinh(|\log|x||)<1\).
The nonzero algebraic integer \(\beta-\beta^{-1}\) would have nonzero integer norm of absolute value less than one.

For \(u=1\), [the winding count](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#count) gives at most one exterior root counted with multiplicity, and reciprocity gives at most one interior root.
The totally real nontorsion unit and its conjugates avoid \(1,-1\), and its norm requires roots on both sides of the circle.
It therefore has exactly two conjugates \(x,x^{-1}\), since the reciprocal of the exterior root is the sole interior root of the parent.
After choosing \(r=|x|>1\), its trace up to sign is the integer \(r+r^{-1}\).
The strict radial inequality puts it between two and \(2(1+1/q)\le3\), a contradiction.

Now suppose \(\beta\in\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\) and let the bar denote complex conjugation.
Every embedding into \(\mathbb C\) of a finite field \(E(i)\) containing \(\beta\), with \(E\) totally real, commutes with the bar because it maps \(E\) into \(\mathbb R\) and \(i\) to \(i\) or \(-i\).
Thus every conjugate of the algebraic unit \(\beta/\bar\beta\) has modulus one.
By Kronecker's theorem this quotient is a root of unity, and \(P(\bar\beta)=0\), forcing \(\beta=\bar\beta\) by the torsion-translate result.
It then lies in \(E\), which was excluded above.
Every CM field is contained in \(\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\): its conjugation commutes with every embedding, so the real and imaginary parts of each element are totally real.
The field also contains all roots of unity.
If \(z^n\) belonged to it, then \(\beta^n=(z^n)^g\) for \(\beta=z^g\) would belong to it, and the power-field equality would give \(\beta\in\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}(i)\).
This proves the claimed exclusion, including all common dilations.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Relative degree required of a multiplicative construction

Write \(d=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\beta):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).
For every \(n\ne0\) and root of unity \(\zeta\), the degree of \(\zeta\beta^n\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is the same \(d\).
This follows by taking the compositum of the power-field equality with \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), which contains \(\zeta\).
Along any sequence with \(q\to\infty\), we have
\[
 d\gg\frac{v(\log\log v)^3}{\log(q+1)(\log v)^4}
\]
eventually, with an absolute implied constant.
Indeed \(vh(\beta)=\log(q+1)+o(1)\) by the [individual height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement).
The [relative height bound of Amoroso and Delsinne](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) yields
\[
 d\gg\frac{v}{\log(q+1)}
       \frac{(\log\log(5d))^3}{(\log(2d))^4}.
\]
We have \(d\le u+v\le2v\) and \(\log(q+1)\ll\log v\) by the [primitive slope estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement).
Retaining only a positive constant in the numerator first gives \(d\gg v/(\log v)^5\).
Thus \(\log\log(5d)\) is comparable to \(\log\log v\) for large \(v\), giving the assertion.

More generally, if a nonzero power of \(z\), up to a root of unity, belongs to a number field \(K\), then \(\beta^n\in K\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) for a nonzero \(n\), and
\[
              [K\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}:\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\ge d .
\]
For a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group one may take the single number field generated by its generators throughout.
The same bound applies to varying fields, so the degrees of their composita over the maximal abelian extension must grow at the indicated rate.
