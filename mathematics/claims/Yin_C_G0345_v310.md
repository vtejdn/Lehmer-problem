# Growing exterior count for small-measure radicals from an entire splitting field

<a id="statement"></a>
## An obstruction independent of the choice of units

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of odd prime half-degree \(p\) with nontorsion roots.
Let \(F\) be its splitting field, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), and \(\tau\) complex conjugation at the chosen embedding.
On the \(p\) inverse-pair labels, \(\tau\) is a product of \(c\) disjoint transpositions.
Here \(c\) is the number of complex-conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots.
Assume \(c>0\), and write \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).

Let \(y\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with some positive power in \(F\).
This includes the radical saturation of **all** units in \(F\), since a torsion multiplier disappears on taking a further power.
Let \(b(y)\) count its exterior conjugates.
Under either of the hypotheses

- \(M(y)<\varphi^2\), or
- \(y\) has a conjugate of modulus one,

the following bound holds:
\[
 b(y)\ \ge\ \frac{p-1}{\min(T_1,T_2)},\qquad
 T_1=\left\lceil p/\sqrt c\right\rceil+
                      \frac{p^2}{c\left\lceil p/\sqrt c\right\rceil},
 \qquad
 T_2=1+\frac{(p-1)\log(p-1)}{c}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
The \(T_1\) estimate uses only prime cyclic translations of one matching and elementary finite group arguments.
The \(T_2\) estimate also uses Burnside's prime-degree dichotomy.
In particular the lower bound tends to infinity whenever \(c\to\infty\), however slowly relative to \(p\).

For the [five-term prime family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement), \(c=p/6+O(1)\).
Uniformly over the indicated outputs, (1) gives
\[
                       b(y)\ \ge\ (1/6+o(1))\,p/\log p .           \tag{2}
\]
Its inputs eventually have \(M(P)<\theta<\varphi^2\), where \(\theta\) is the plastic number.
Every nontorsion output with \(M(y)\le M(P)\) is then reciprocal by Smyth's theorem and satisfies (2).
Thus expanding the available units to the entire splitting field and allowing arbitrary radicals cannot provide a bounded-exterior replacement of no greater measure along this concrete subplastic sequence.

<a id="abelian"></a>
## Exact measure under taking the least abelian power

Suppose a nontorsion algebraic unit \(u\) has some positive power in \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), the union of finite abelian extensions of \(\mathbb Q\).
This base contains all roots of unity.
If \(u^j\) belongs to the base, the extension obtained by adjoining \(u\) is finite Galois since it splits \(X^j-u^j\).
Its group injects by \(\gamma\mapsto\gamma(u)/u\) into \(\mu_j\), as every automorphism fixes \(\mu_j\).
With \(n\) the order of the image, \(w=u^n\) is in the base, and \(n\) divides every exponent giving a power there.
Over \(\mathbb Q(w)\), the degree of \(u\) is at least \(n\) by extension of scalars to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), and at most \(n\) by its displayed power.
The equality of degrees, together with \(h(w)=nh(u)\) for logarithmic Weil height, gives
\[
                               M(u)=M(w).                            \tag{3}
\]

For any such abelian unit \(w\), choose a finite abelian field containing it.
Every conjugate of \(w\overline w\) is positive real by commutativity with complex conjugation.
If \(w\) has a neutral conjugate, \(w\overline w=1\) by injectivity of the corresponding embedding, and all conjugates of \(w\) then have modulus one.
Kronecker's theorem for monic integer polynomials makes the unit torsion, a contradiction.
In particular a radical of a nontorsion abelian unit has no neutral conjugates.

The positive square root \(a\) of \(w\overline w\) is a totally real nontorsion unit.
Conjugate absolute values of \(a\) and \(w\) agree in the uniform embedding averages, so \(h(a)=h(w)\).
For any totally real nontorsion unit \(a\), put \(l_\sigma=|\log|\sigma a||\).
All \(l_\sigma\) are positive, and their average is \(2h(a)\) by the unit norm.
The nonzero integer absolute norm of \(a-a^{-1}\) is the product of \(2\sinh l_\sigma\).
Concavity of \(\log(2\sinh x)\) on \(x>0\) gives \(2\sinh(2h(a))\ge1\), hence \(h(w)\ge\frac12\log\varphi\).
This calculation gives the totally real unit case of [Schinzel's height bound](../audits/literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#linear-height) for \(a\).
If \(u\) is reciprocal, \(w=u^n\) is reciprocal as well.
Its degree is even, since its inverse pairs are distinct.
For degree at least four, (3) gives \(M(u)\ge\varphi^2\).
In degree two the reciprocal polynomial \(X^2-a_0 X+1\) has \(|a_0|\ge3\) by nontorsion, yielding the same bound directly.
The bound is attained by the positive root of \(X^2-3X+1\).
Both hypotheses in the statement therefore exclude any positive power of \(y\) in an abelian extension.

<a id="representation"></a>
## Detecting an element of order \(p\) in the output module

Choose \(w=y^j\in F\) for some \(j>0\).
Let \(W\) be the rational span of the conjugates of the class of \(w\) in \(\mathcal O_F^\times\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\), with multiplication written additively in this vector space.
The kernel of passage from units to classes is torsion.
For these classes, a logarithm of absolute value vanishes precisely when \(\tau v=-v\): after clearing denominators this says that a positive real number \(z\overline z\) is torsion, hence one.
Projecting the full conjugate orbit by \((1+\tau)/2\) spans \(W^\tau\).
Different nonzero rational fixed classes up to sign give different absolute logarithms because the logarithm is injective on the fixed space.
The conjugate orbit is closed under negation by reciprocity.
Thus each distinct nonzero projected pair supplies an actual exterior conjugate of \(w\) and then of \(y\), giving \(b(y)\ge\dim W^\tau\).
This is the argument of the [archimedean rank estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#rank) applied to the full orbit of a field unit.

Let \(S\) be any subgroup of order \(p\) in \(G\).
It is Sylow since \(G\) embeds in the signed permutation group on \(p\) labels of order \(2^p p!\).
The [prime-cycle normalizer calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v309.md#normalizer) gives \(N_G(S)/S\) abelian.
Consequently any quotient of \(G\) killing \(S\) is abelian.
Indeed for a normal kernel containing \(S\), conjugacy of Sylow subgroups inside the kernel shows that the image of \(N_G(S)\) is the entire quotient.
If \(S\) acted trivially on \(W\), the representation kernel \(K\) would have abelian fixed field \(F^K\).
For every \(k\in K\), \(k(w)/w\) is torsion, so one common positive power of \(w\) is fixed by all of \(K\).
This gives an abelian power of \(y\), excluded above.
Hence \(S\) acts nontrivially on \(W\), and
\[
                       \operatorname{codim}_W W^S\ge p-1           \tag{4}
\]
because \(\Phi_p\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).

Suppose that \(t\) conjugates \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_t\) generate a subgroup whose unsigned action on the \(p\) labels is transitive.
Its order is divisible by \(p\), so it contains a subgroup \(S\) as in (4).
The common negative eigenspace \(Z=\bigcap_i\ker(1+\tau_i)\) has codimension at most \(t\dim W^\tau\).
On \(Z\) each generator acts by \(-1\), hence the whole generated group acts by scalars in \(\{1,-1\}\), and the odd-order group \(S\) acts trivially.
The inclusion \(Z\subset W^S\) now yields
\[
                         p-1\le t\,\dim W^\tau\le t\, b(y).        \tag{5}
\]
It suffices to make the graph of the selected transpositions connected.
Its components are exactly the orbits of the group generated by the associated involutions.

<a id="cyclic"></a>
## A bound from cyclic shifts of one matching

Identify the labels with \(\mathbb F_p\) along a \(p\)-cycle in the unsigned permutation group.
Conjugation by powers of this cycle translates the matching of \(c\) transpositions of \(\tau\).
All these translates lift to conjugates of \(\tau\) in \(G\).
Suppose the graph of the matchings selected thus far has \(k>1\) components.
For a component \(A\) of size \(a\), each single edge of the original matching, translated in all \(p\) ways, gives the edges of a cycle of length \(p\).
Indeed its nonzero difference generates the additive group of \(\mathbb F_p\).
At least two edges of that cycle cross from \(A\) to its complement.
Under a uniform translation of the whole matching the expected number of crossing edges incident to \(A\) is therefore at least \(2c/p\).
There are at most \(a\) such edges in a matching, so the probability that \(A\) is joined to another component is at least \(2c/(pa)\).
Summing over components, the expected number touched is at least
\[
                           (2c/p)\sum_A 1/|A|\ge 2c k^2/p^2 .
\]
When \(s\) old components are touched, their new unions contain at least two old components each, and the number of components drops by at least \(s/2\).
Some translate therefore reduces \(k\) to \(k'\) with
\[
                           k-k'\ge c k^2/p^2 .
\]
In particular it reduces \(k\) by at least one.
Set \(k_0=\lceil p/\sqrt c\rceil\).
While \(k>k_0\), each step increases \(1/k\) by at least \(c/p^2\).
There are at most \(p^2/(c k_0)+1\) such steps, by applying this inequality to the steps preceding the last crossing of \(k_0\).
At most \(k_0-1\) further steps suffice.
This proves \(t\le T_1\), including the case \(k_0=p\).

<a id="transitive"></a>
## A faster bound from prime-degree permutation groups

The [prime-degree dichotomy](../audits/literature/Mu03/Yin_U001.md#burnside-affine) states that the transitive unsigned group is doubly transitive or can be labeled as a subgroup of the affine group of \(\mathbb F_p\).
In the affine case its nonidentity involution is \(x\mapsto d-x\), since \(p\) is odd.
The group contains the translations, by transitivity and the unique subgroup of order \(p\) of the affine group.
Two different translation conjugates of this involution have a nonzero translation as their product.
Thus two conjugates suffice, and \(2\le T_2\) since \(c\le(p-1)/2\) and \(p\ge3\).

In the doubly transitive case, a fixed unordered edge under a uniform group conjugation is uniform on all unordered pairs.
For a current component of size \(a\), the expected number of edges crossing to its complement is
\[
                        \frac{2c\,a(p-a)}{p(p-1)}.
\]
The probability of a crossing is at least this expectation divided by \(a\).
The expected number of touched components is therefore at least \(2c(k-1)/(p-1)\).
As above the number of components drops by at least half the number touched, so one can reduce \(k-1\) by a factor at most \(1-c/(p-1)\) in each step.
Starting from \(p\) singleton components, after \(\lfloor(p-1)\log(p-1)/c\rfloor+1\) steps the integer \(k-1\) is less than one, using \(1-x\le e^{-x}\).
This gives \(t\le T_2\) and finishes (1).

<a id="application"></a>
## The subplastic family and the remaining measure question

For (2), substitute \(c=(e_p-1)/2=p/6+O(1)\) from the [explicit trace-root count](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) into (1).
If the output is nontorsion and its measure is at most that of the input, the [strict nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) and the eventual input inequality \(M(P_p)<\theta\) give reciprocity.
This proves the claimed obstruction without any restriction on the chosen field unit or its radical exponent.
The limiting input measure \(L\) is strictly greater than one.
Consequently this sequence excludes a bounded-exterior replacement of no greater measure on any interval \(1<M<A\) with \(A>L\); it leaves open what happens on smaller intervals adjacent to one.
The argument would also apply to a hypothetical sequence of inputs tending to one with increasing \(c\) in prime half-degrees, but does not produce such a sequence or exclude it.

The coordinating researcher derived the matching bounds, the common negative eigenspace estimate and the abelian-power measure comparison.
