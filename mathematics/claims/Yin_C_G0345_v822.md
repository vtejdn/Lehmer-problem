# Cubic contacts at the branch values of the logarithmic slope

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exceptional contribution to the complementary resultant

Use the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#statement) with positive coprime exponents \(k,l\), and assume \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) is squarefree over the rationals.
Let \(E(Z;s)\) be the [critical octic](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) at \((k,l)=(1,s)\), and define
\[
 Q(s)=\frac{\operatorname{disc}_Z E(Z;s)}{2723676160000\,s^6(49s^2-66)^2},\qquad H(k,l)=k^{18}Q(l/k).
\]
There exists a fixed finite set of rational primes outside which the following holds, for \(H(k,l)\ne0\).
If \(p\nmid H(k,l)\), every multiple residue root of \(P\) has multiplicity two and obeys the [unramified critical-value test](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local), even when \(p\) divides the original degree-56 exceptional form.
If \(p\mid H(k,l)\), the same test applies off two inverse geometric points of the curve.
Each of these two points contributes a triple root if it lies on the residue section, and contributes nothing otherwise.
Put \(r=v_p(H(k,l))\).
At each triple root the local section equation can be prepared as a depressed cubic \(X^3+aX+b\) over the unramified local integer ring.
Its discriminant has valuation at most \(3r\), except possibly when
\[
                  v_p(a)=r,\qquad 2v_p(b)=3r.
\]
In particular the exceptional equality requires even \(r\ge2\).
For every monic integral factorization \(P=ST\), the total contribution to \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,T)|\) from these triple-root disks outside the displayed equality, over all primes outside the fixed set, is bounded by \(3\log|H(k,l)|\le54\log(k+l)+O(1)\).
Large valuations from the remaining double-root disks still require the even critical valuation and square-unit test.

## Branch values of the slope map

Write \(C\) for the smooth projective model of the two-pole curve and \(g=(dV/V)/(dU/U)\) for its logarithmic slope.
It has degree sixteen by the [intersection count](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#critical).
The map \(C\to\mathbb P^1_U\) is a double cover branched at the eight simple zeros of \(1-4((U-U^{-1})^2-9)((U-U^{-1})^2-16)\), with poles of order four at zero and infinity.
Hence \(C\) has genus three, and the [Riemann–Hurwitz formula](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B) gives total ramification degree \(2\cdot3-2+2\cdot16=36\) for \(g\).
These degree and genus counts remain valid outside a fixed finite set of primes, using the same smooth double cover and generic octic, and the map is tame for \(p>16\).

Here are reproducible polynomial checks for locating the whole ramification divisor.
For \(z=Z\), put \(h=z^2-4\), \(J=(h-9)(h-16)\), \(D=1-4J\), and \(G=zhJ(2h-25)D\).
The following exact Euclidean computations take place first in \(\mathbb Z[s,z]\) and then in \(\mathbb F_{101}[s]\).

```python
import sympy as sym
z, s = sym.symbols('z s')
h = z**2 - 4
J = (h-9)*(h-16)
D = 1-4*J
A = s*J-7*z*(12-h)
G = z*h*J*(2*h-25)*D
E = sym.exquo(D*A**2-z**2*h*(2*h-25)**2, J, z)
Q = sym.exquo(sym.discriminant(E,z), 2723676160000*s**6*(49*s**2-66)**2, s)
assert sym.Poly(Q,s,domain=sym.ZZ).degree() == 18
assert sym.LC(sym.Poly(Q,s)) == 65 and Q.subs(s,0) != 0
R = sym.resultant(E,G,z)*s*(49*s**2-66)*Q.diff(s)
reduce = lambda f: sym.Poly(f,s,modulus=101)
assert sym.gcd(reduce(Q),reduce(R)).degree() == 0
```

Since \(Q\) retains its leading degree modulo 101, the last check proves coprimality over the rationals and therefore outside a fixed finite set of primes.
Each of the eighteen branch candidates is a nonzero finite simple zero of the discriminant of the monic normalization of the octic, and the fiber is disjoint from \(G=0\).
Over the completed slope disk with algebraically closed residue field at such a zero, factor the octic by its distinct residue roots.
A monic factor reducing to \((Z-z_0)^m\) contributes at least \(m-1\) to the discriminant valuation, since multiplication by its derivative has kernel of dimension at least \(m-1\) after reduction.
A simple discriminant zero thus gives exactly one double residue root, and its lifted quadratic has discriminant of valuation one.
Completing the square presents a smooth branch with ramification index two over the slope line.
The octic reconstruction adjoins \(t^2=h\) étale over the disk and yields two inverse points of index two on \(C\).
These give all 36 ramification contributions, hence no other critical fiber of the slope map contains a ramified point.
The same argument in characteristic \(p\) uses the uniformizer \(s-s_0\) over the algebraic closure of the residue field, excluding only the finitely many primes where the polynomial checks or smooth cover fail.

At a torus point the logarithmic differentials of \(U,V\) do not vanish simultaneously because the torus curve is smooth and immersed.
In the coordinates \((x,y)=(U^aV^h,V^k/U^l)\) of the [local argument](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local), the section \(y=1\) is tangent precisely in the slope fiber \([k:l]\), and \(x\) is then a local parameter.
The differential \(dy/y\) vanishes there to order equal to the multiplicity of that slope fiber, using \(dU/U\) for finite slopes and \(dV/V\) for the reciprocal coordinate near infinity.
Since the orders are one or two and \(p>3\), the section multiplicities are two or three respectively.
At each simple critical point the transverse Jacobian lifts the unique critical point of the actual direction to the unramified local ring, so the quadratic calculation applies without conditions on other disks or boundary points.

## Depth of a cubic contact

At a triple residue root we have \(p\nmid k\), since the leading coefficient 65 is a unit away from the fixed primes.
Over the unramified ring \(O\) of its residue point, the ramified slope value lifts uniquely to a root \(s_0\) of \(Q\), and \(v_p(l/k-s_0)=r\) by simple reduction.
The corresponding ramification point lifts to a section \(c_0\) over \(O\): the simple zero of the derivative of the lifted slope map has a unique Hensel lift, whose image is the unique branch value in this residue class by the characteristic-zero calculation above.
Use \(t=x-x(c_0)\) and write \(y-1=c_0'+c_1t+c_2t^2+c_3t^3+\cdots\) in \(O[[t]]\).
Here \(dU/U\) has unit coefficient in \(dt\), since the slope is finite, and \(g=s_0+t^2u(t)\) with \(u(0)\) a unit by simple ramification.
The identity \(dy/y=k(g-l/k)dU/U\) gives \(v_p(c_1)=r\), \(v_p(c_2)\ge r\), and \(c_3\) a unit.
Set \(s'=v_p(c_0')>0\), allowing infinity for zero.

Weierstrass preparation factors the series as a unit times \(t^3+A_2t^2+A_1t+A_0\) with all \(A_j\) in the maximal ideal.
Comparing the constant, linear and quadratic coefficients in order gives \(v_p(A_0)=s'\), \(v_p(A_1),v_p(A_2)\ge\min(r,s')\), and \(v_p(A_1)=r\) if \(s'>r\).
Translate to remove the quadratic coefficient, giving \(a=A_1-A_2^2/3\) and \(b=A_0-A_1A_2/3+2A_2^3/27\).
For \(s'\le r\) one has \(v_p(b)=s'\), \(v_p(a)\ge s'\), and \(v_p(-4a^3-27b^2)=2s'\).
For \(s'>r\) one has \(v_p(a)=r\) and the discriminant valuation is at most \(3r\) unless the two terms have equal valuation.
The discriminant is nonzero by squarefreeness.
Over the unramified ring this cubic accounts for exactly the three roots of the original polynomial in the disk, with root differences preserved by the translation in \(x\).
The square of the local complementary resultant divides its discriminant.
Counting geometric residue roots counts each Frobenius orbit with its residue degree, and there are at most two triple residue roots at this prime.
Summing the resulting bound \(3r\log p\) over primes gives the stated estimate by homogeneity of \(H\).
