# Finite bounds for small values of sparse polynomials on algebraic conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound independent of the degrees of all powers

Fix an integer \(r\ge2\) and put \(l=r-1\).
Let \(\alpha\) be any nonzero nontorsion algebraic number of degree \(d\) and absolute logarithmic height \(h\).
Normalize a nonzero complex Laurent polynomial of at most \(r\) terms by dividing out its lowest monomial power and its coefficient \(\ell^2\) norm, and call the result \(Q\).
Write \(W\) for its exponent span and \(u=Wh\).
For \(0<u\le e^{-4}\) put
\[
 L=\log(1/u),\qquad E=u\,L^4/(\log L)^3 .
\]
For every \(t\ge0\), with \(C_r\) depending only on \(r\),
\[
 \frac1d\#\{\sigma: |Q(\sigma\alpha)|\le t\}
       \le C_r\big(t^{1/l}+E^{1/(l^2+2)}\big).                         \tag{1}
\]
The same estimate holds at the phases \(\sigma\alpha/|\sigma\alpha|\).
The two sides use the original embeddings even if a power used in the proof loses degree.
For a normalized monomial the sublevel fraction is zero for \(t<1\) and one otherwise.

For a Salem number of degree \(d\), \(m=\log\alpha\), the finite neutral-phase estimate (1) also holds with \(E\) replaced by \(E_0=(Wm+\log(2d))/d\), counting its \(d-2\) neutral roots and dividing by \(d-2\).
These finite estimates strengthen the [asymptotic arbitrary-orbit and finite Salem bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement).
To test several separately chosen polynomials at once one may sum their right sides, with the actual thresholds and spans.
For \(t=0\), each normalized polynomial with \(u\to0\) therefore has at most \(O_r(d E^{1/(l^2+2)})\) exact zeros among the original conjugates.

The exponent \(1/l\) on the threshold cannot be increased in a uniform limiting bound over all algebraic inputs of height tending to zero.
Indeed take \(\alpha_j=2^{1/j}\) and \(Q=(1-X)^l/\|(1-X)^l\|_{\mathrm{coeff},2}\), where the norm is the Euclidean norm of the coefficients.
Eisenstein at two gives degree \(j\); the phases form the complete \(j\)-th root grid and the radii \(2^{1/j}\) tend uniformly to one.
For every sufficiently small fixed \(t>0\) the sublevel fraction tends to the normalized length of the arc \(|1-e^{i\theta}|\le(t\|(1-X)^l\|_{\mathrm{coeff},2})^{1/l}\), a positive constant times \(t^{1/l}(1+o(1))\) as \(t\downarrow0\).
This example concerns small absolute height; its total logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\log2\).

<a id="harmonic"></a>
## The first harmonic for a primitive minimal polynomial

Let \(\beta\) be any nonzero nontorsion number of degree \(D\), height \(v\), and primitive integer minimal polynomial \(a\prod_j(X-z_j)\) with \(a>0\).
Write \(b_j=e^{-|\log|z_j||}z_j/|z_j|\), and \(\rho=e^{-1/D}\).
The [reflected discriminant energy argument](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) applies with its term \(2(D-1)m\) replaced by \(2D(D-1)v\).
In fact \(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|z_i-z_j|\ge-(2D-2)\log a\), by the nonzero integer discriminant.
The exterior contribution in that argument is \(2(D-1)\sum_j\log^+|z_j|\); adding the leading coefficient cost gives precisely \(2D(D-1)v\).
Its geometric reflection, damping and diagonal bounds give
\[
 \rho^2\Big|\sum_j b_j\Big|^2
 \le 2D(D-1)v+D(D-1)\log(1/\rho)+D\log(1/(1-\rho^2)).
\]
The mean radial difference between the \(b_j\) and the phases is at most \(D^{-1}\sum_j|\log|z_j||\le2v\), by the height sums for \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{-1}\).
Thus the absolute mean first phase harmonic is \(O(\sqrt{v+\log(2D)/D}+v)\).

For small \(v>0\), writing \(L_v=\log(1/v)\), one has
\[
                  \frac{\log(2D)}{D}\ll v\,L_v^4/(\log L_v)^3.        \tag{2}
\]
Rational nontorsion numbers have height at least \(\log2\).
For \(D\ge1/v\) use eventual monotone decrease of \(\log(2D)/D\).
Otherwise [Voutier, Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(Dv>2/\log^3(3D)\), whence \(D>v^{-1/2}\) for sufficiently small \(v\).
Now \(\log D\) lies between \(L_v/2\) and \(L_v\), and the full theorem \(Dv>\tfrac14(\log\log D/\log D)^3\) gives (2).
The normalized mean phase of \(\beta\) is consequently \(O(\sqrt{vL_v^4/(\log L_v)^3})\).
No integrality or degree stability of \(\beta\) is used.

<a id="majorant"></a>
## A positive polynomial majorant tested on two tori

Write \(Q(X)=c_0+\sum_{j=1}^s c_jX^{a_j}\) with \(1\le s\le l\), \(0<a_1<\cdots<a_s=W\).
On the full \(s\)-torus put \(F(z)=c_0+\sum_{j=1}^s c_j z_j\), \(B_x=\{|F|\le x\}\), and denote its normalized Haar measure by \(\lambda\).
The measure \(\nu\) is instead induced by \(z_j=e^{ia_j\theta}\) for uniform \(\theta\); let \(\mu\) be induced by the phases of the original conjugates.
The [circle sublevel bound](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#transfer) gives \(\nu(B_x)\ll_r x^{1/l}\).
On the full torus there is also a bound \(\lambda(B_x)\ll_r x\).
Indeed if every nonconstant coefficient has modulus \(<1/(2r)\), normalization gives \(|c_0|>3/4\) and \(\sum_{j>0}|c_j|<1/2\), so \(B_x\) is empty for \(x<1/4\).
Otherwise fix all angles except one whose coefficient has modulus at least \(1/(2r)\).
The remaining point is uniformly distributed on a circle of radius at least \(1/(2r)\).
Its portion inside any disk of radius \(x\) has relative length \(O_r(x)\), by the chord bound from any one point in the intersection for \(x\) small; the estimate at larger \(x\) follows from total measure one.

For an integer \(J\ge2\) use the product kernel \(K_J\) of the nonnegative Fejér kernels of order \(J\) on these \(s\) circles, each of normalized integral one.
For clarity the one-dimensional kernel is \(J^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}e^{ij\theta}|^2\).
The bound \(K_J^{(1)}\gg J\) on \(|\theta|\le1/J\) gives product mass at least \(c_r>0\) on the angular box of these side radii.
Put \(y=t+\sqrt r/J\) and \(f=\mathbf1_{B_y}*K_J\).
For \(z\in B_t\), moving each angle by at most \(1/J\) changes \(F\) by at most \(\sqrt r/J\), so \(f(z)\ge c_r\).
Also
\[
              \int f\,d\nu\ll_r (t+1/J)^{1/l}.
\]
Indeed at each translation in the convolution, the translated circle parametrization replaces the \(c_j\) by coefficients of the same moduli, and the same circle sublevel bound holds throughout.
Young's inequality and the full-torus sublevel bound give \(\|f\|_{L^2(\lambda)}\le \lambda(B_y)^{1/2}\ll_r y^{1/2}\).
There are at most \((2J+1)^s\) Fourier frequencies in \(f\), so Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the sum of its absolute Fourier coefficients by \(O_r(J^{s/2}y^{1/2})\).
Consequently, for \(\Delta_J=\max_{\|v'\|_\infty\le J}|\widehat\mu(v')-\widehat\nu(v')|\),
\[
      \mu(B_t)\ll_r (t+1/J)^{1/l}+J^{s/2}(t+1/J)^{1/2}\Delta_J.       \tag{3}
\]
The localized \(L^2\) estimate here uses the full torus regardless of linear dependencies among the exponents on the one-dimensional parametrization.

<a id="scales"></a>
## Choosing the degree and returning to the original moduli

When \(q=v'\cdot(a_1,\ldots,a_s)\) vanishes, the corresponding Fourier coefficients of the two distributions agree.
For nonzero \(q\) the difference in absolute value is the absolute mean first phase harmonic of \(\alpha^{|q|}\), since all its embeddings have equal multiplicity among those of \(\alpha\).
Its height is \(|q|h\le rJu\).
Write \(V(x)=x(\log(1/x))^4/(\log\log(1/x))^3\) near zero.
There \(V(x)\) is increasing and \(V(x)/x\) is decreasing, as follows by differentiation.
If \(rJu\) is sufficiently small, (2) bounds the harmonic by \(O_r(\sqrt{J E})\): for \(|q|h\le u\) use monotonicity of \(V\), and for \(|q|h>u\) use monotonicity of \(V(x)/x\).
For neutral Salem phases the [finite harmonic bound](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#salem) instead gives \(\Delta_J\ll_r\sqrt{JE_0}\).

Set \(a=1/l\) and \(z_0=E^{1/(l+2a)}\), or take \(z_0=E_0^{1/(l+2a)}\) in the Salem case.
Only small \(z_0\) and small \(t\) need proof, since otherwise a constant times the displayed upper bound exceeds one.
Put \(z=\max(t,z_0)\) and \(J=\lceil1/z\rceil\).
For the general case \(rJu\to0\) as \(u\to0\), since \(l+2a>1\) and \(E\ge u\) at small \(u\).
The error term in (3) is at most \(C_r z^{-l/2}\sqrt E\le C_r z^a\), using \(t+1/J\ll z\), \(s\le l\) and \(E\le z^{l+2a}\).
The same proof uses \(E_0\) in the neutral case.
This proves (1) for the phases since \(z_0^a=E^{1/(l^2+2)}\).
The positive continuous function \(E(u)\) is bounded away from zero on every compact subinterval of \((0,e^{-4}]\), so the remaining range of \(u\) is absorbed by the constant.

For the full orbit the fraction of embeddings with \(W|\log|\sigma\alpha||>z_0\) is at most \(2u/z_0\).
For small \(u\) this is \(O_r(z_0^a)\) because \(u\le E=z_0^{l+2a}\) and \(l+2a-1\ge a\).
At each remaining embedding the change of the normalized polynomial under replacement of the argument by its phase is at most \(\sqrt r(e^{z_0}-1)=O_r(z_0)\).
Applying the phase bound at \(t+O_r(z_0)\) proves (1) for the original conjugates.
