# A Mahler gap for products with boundedly many distinct cyclotomic factors plus one monomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform growth in the conductors and their multiplicities

Fix \(H\ge1\) and let
\[
         A(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{h}\Phi_{n_j}(X)^{e_j},\qquad 1\le h\le H,
\]
where the \(n_j\ge1\) are distinct and the \(e_j\ge1\) are integers.
The factor \(\Phi_1=X-1\) is allowed.
Put \(r=\max_j\omega(n_j)\), \(E=\max_j e_j\), and \(L(\theta)=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\), with norms taken in normalized angular measure.
For each \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \|L\|_1\gg_{H,\epsilon}r^{1/6-\epsilon}\quad(r\to\infty),
 \qquad
 \|L\|_1\gg_{H,\epsilon}\frac{E}{(1+r)^\epsilon}\quad(r\ge0).
\]
Consequently, uniformly over all nonzero integers \(a\) and all integers \(k\ge0\), the full logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(A+aX^k)\) tends to infinity when \(\max(r,E)\to\infty\).
There is a gap depending only on \(H\) for the positive logarithmic measures of this family.
The growth statement also holds for complex \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\).

The first norm bound has a further range when \(h\) varies.
On every sequence with \(r\to\infty\) and \(h^2\log(h+2)=o(\log r)\) one has \(\|L\|_1\ge r^{1/6-o(1)}\), in the sense that \(\liminf\log\|L\|_1/\log r\ge1/6\).

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## Recovering multiplicities from Fourier coefficients

The coordinator supplied the common prime valuations and small-prime adjustment used below, and agent-S supplied the inversion that controls arbitrary multiplicities.
Use \(\widehat L(t)=\int L(\theta)e^{-it\theta}\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
The Möbius formula gives \(L(\theta)=\sum_d b_d\log|1-e^{id\theta}|\) almost everywhere, with
\[
                     b_d=\sum_{j:\,d\mid n_j} e_j\mu(n_j/d).
\]
The sum is finite, and \(\log|1-e^{i\theta}|\) has nonconstant coefficient \(-1/(2|t|)\) at frequency \(t\), as in the [single-conductor Fourier calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#fourier).
For \(v\ge1\), inversion of \(-2v\widehat L(v)=\sum_{d\mid v}d b_d\) gives
\[
              b_v=-2\sum_{t\mid v}\frac{\mu(t)}{t}\widehat L(v/t),
      \qquad |b_{n_j}|\le2\|L\|_1\prod_{p\mid n_j}(1+1/p)
                 \ll_\epsilon (1+r)^\epsilon\|L\|_1 .
\]
Here \(1+1/p\le(1-1/p)^{-1}\) and the [uniform totient ratio bound](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#test) applies to the squarefree radical.
On the set of actual conductors the coefficient of \(e_j\) in \(b_{n_j}\) is one, and all other terms belong to strict multiples of \(n_j\).
Order the conductors in decreasing numerical order and recover the multiplicities successively.
At the \(j\)-th step the absolute value is at most \(\max_i|b_{n_i}|\) plus the sum of the preceding \(j-1\) absolute values, since \(|\mu|\le1\).
Hence
\[
                       E\le 2^{h-1}\max_j|b_{n_j}|
                           \ll_\epsilon 2^h(1+r)^\epsilon\|L\|_1 .
                       \tag{1}
\]

<a id="primes"></a>
## A common set of prime valuations

We prove the first norm bound for large \(r\), treating fixed \(h\) and the stated growing range together.
Choose a conductor with \(r\) distinct prime divisors and keep its largest \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\) prime divisors, all at least \(r/2\).
For each such \(p\), let \(I_p\) be the nonempty set of indices attaining \(\max_j v_p(n_j)\), which is positive.
One set \(I\) occurs on a set \(Q\) of \(u\ge\lfloor r/2\rfloor/2^h\) primes.
Thus for \(p\in Q\) every \(j\in I\) attains the same positive valuation, and every \(j\notin I\) has a strictly smaller one.
Choose \(j_0\in I\) maximizing \(e_j\varphi(n_j)\) over \(I\), and write \(n_0=n_{j_0}\), \(w_0=e_{j_0}\varphi(n_0)\).

Choose a positive integer \(D\) supported on the primes \(\ell\le4h\).
For each such \(\ell\) take \(v_\ell(D)\) from \(0,\ldots,h\), avoiding the at most \(h\) values \(v_\ell(n_j)-v_\ell(n_0)-1\), \(1\le j\le h\).
Then \(D\le(4h)^{4h^2}\).
In both ranges \(r/2>4h\) eventually, so \(D\) is coprime to every \(p\in Q\).
Put \(W=w_0/(Dn_0)\) and \(d_p=Dn_0/p\).

For any nonzero integral frequency \(t\) the roots of \(A\) give
\[
                   \widehat L(t)=-\frac{1}{2|t|}
                                \sum_j e_j c_{n_j}(t).
\]
Here \(c_n\) is the Ramanujan sum over primitive \(n\)-th roots and \(c_1(t)=1\).
Its local factor at \(\ell^b\parallel n\) is \(\varphi(\ell^b)\) if \(\ell^b\mid t\), \(-\ell^{b-1}\) if \(v_\ell(t)=b-1\), and zero otherwise.
This follows by subtracting the sum on all \(\ell^{b-1}\)-th roots from the sum on all \(\ell^b\)-th roots and then using the Chinese remainder theorem.
In particular \(|c_n(t)|\le(n,t)\).
For \(t=d_p\), the \(j_0\)-term in \(\sum_{j\in I}e_jc_{n_j}(t)\) is exactly \(-w_0/(p-1)\).
Every \(j\in I\) has a deficit of exactly one at \(p\), meaning \(v_p(t)=v_p(n_j)-1\).
If its contribution is positive, it must have another deficit of one at some \(\ell\ne p\).
No prime \(\ell\le4h\) can supply it by the choice of \(D\).
Hence each positive term is at most \(w_0/((p-1)4h)\), by the local factor formula and maximality of \(w_0\).
After dividing by \(-2d_p\), the contribution from the indices in \(I\) to \(\widehat L(d_p)\) is at least \(3W/8\).

<a id="test"></a>
## Controlling the other indices on all signed sums

The [prime selection for the odd products](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#selection) provides a subset of \(Q\) of size \(s=\lfloor(u/40)^{1/3}\rfloor\) which is either separated by successive ratios at least four or lies in one interval \([P,P(1+1/(4s))]\).
Indeed that selection uses only the number of available primes after its initial restriction to large primes, and here all \(u\) primes are available.
For any subset \(J\) of this selection of odd size \(j\) and signs \(\epsilon_p=\pm1\), put
\[
          t=\sum_{p\in J}\epsilon_p d_p=Dn_0 S_J,\qquad
          S_J=\sum_{p\in J}\epsilon_p/p .
\]
Then \(|S_J|\ge1/(2\min_{p\in J}p)\), by domination by the largest reciprocal in the separated case and by comparison with the odd integer \(\sum\epsilon_p\) after scaling by \(P\) in the short interval.
At every \(p\in J\) exactly its own summand has valuation \(v_p(n_0)-1\), so \(v_p(t)=v_p(n_0)-1\).
For \(i\in I\), the local factor formula therefore gives \(|e_i c_{n_i}(t)|\le w_0/\prod_{p\in J}(p-1)\).
With \(R=r/2-1\), the absolute contribution of all these indices to \(\widehat L(t)\) is at most
\[
                 \frac{hW}{2|S_J|\prod_{p\in J}(p-1)}
                            \le\frac{2hW}{R^{j-1}} .
\]

At every \(p\in Q\setminus J\), all the summands of \(t\) are divisible by \(p^{v_p(n_0)}\).
For \(i\notin I\), the integer \(|t|/(n_i,t)\) is therefore divisible by \(\prod_{p\in Q\setminus J}p\).
The total absolute contribution of indices outside \(I\) is at most \(hE(r/2)^{-(u-s)}/2\) at every signed frequency under consideration, including the singletons.

Use the real test \(G(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\prod_p(1+i s^{-1/2}\cos(d_p\theta))\) over the selected primes.
Its supremum is at most \(e^{1/2}\).
At a subset of size \(j\) the integral of \(L\) times its cosine product is the average of \(\widehat L(t)\) over the signed sums above.
For \(I\), the singleton contribution to \(\int LG\) is at least \(3W\sqrt{s}/8\), and the total absolute error from odd orders \(j\ge3\) is at most
\[
          2hWR\sum_{j\ge3}\frac{(\sqrt{s}/R)^j}{j!}
                           =O(hW s^{3/2}/r^2).
\]
The contribution of all indices outside \(I\), over all odd orders, is bounded by \(hE(r/2)^{-(u-s)}(1+s^{-1/2})^s/2\).
By (1) with \(\epsilon=1\), this is at most
\[
              C h2^h(1+r)e^{\sqrt{s}}(r/2)^{-(u-s)}\|L\|_1
                            =o(\|L\|_1).
\]
Indeed \(u\ge\lfloor r/2\rfloor/2^h\) implies \(\log u/\log r\to1\) in both ranges, and \(s=O(r^{1/3})\).
Also \(hs/r^2=o(1)\).
Together with \(|\int LG|\le e^{1/2}\|L\|_1\), these estimates give \(\|L\|_1\gg W\sqrt s\) with an absolute implied constant for all sufficiently large indices in either range.

Since \(e_{j_0}\ge1\), the totient ratio estimate used in (1) gives \(W\gg_\delta D^{-1}(1+r)^{-\delta}\) for every fixed \(\delta>0\).
For \(h\le H\), \(D\) is bounded in terms of \(H\) and \(\sqrt s\gg_H r^{1/6}\), proving the first bound.
If \(h^2\log(h+2)=o(\log r)\), then \(D=r^{o(1)}\) and \(\log\sqrt s/\log r\to1/6\).
Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero proves the additional range.

<a id="measure"></a>
## The complete measure after a monomial shift

The roots of the monic \(A\) are on the circle with the indicated positive multiplicities.
The [integral estimate for circle roots](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) at \(b=|a|\ge1\) therefore gives
\[
              m(A+aX^k)\ge\int\max(L,\log|a|)\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
                                  -\sqrt{\pi}\,\zeta(3/2)
                       \ge\tfrac12\|L\|_1-\sqrt{\pi}\,\zeta(3/2),
\]
since the angular mean of \(L\) is zero.
For \(h\le H\), the first norm bound forces \(r\) to stay bounded on any subsequence of bounded measure, and (1) then bounds \(E\).
This proves the asserted divergence.

It remains to exclude positive measures tending to zero for integer \(a\) when \(r\) and \(E\) are bounded.
Each \(\Phi_{n_j}\) is a quotient of products with at most \(2^r\) binomials in total by Möbius inversion, also for \(n_j=1\).
The numerator and denominator of their product with multiplicities together use at most \(HE2^r\) binomials.
Multiplying \(A+aX^k\) by the denominator gives a nonzero integer polynomial with boundedly many terms and the same Mahler measure.
It is nonzero because a nonconstant polynomial with a circle root cannot equal a monomial.
The [bounded-term Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes the proposed sequence.
