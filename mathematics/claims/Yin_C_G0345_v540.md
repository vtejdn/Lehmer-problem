# The phase spectrum near integer multiples of the long section exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## The limiting coefficients and their locations

Fix positive constants \(C,K\) and use the [critical six-term sections](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) as \(s\to\infty\).
Thus \(a,b\) are coprime positive integers, \(a\ge2\), \(s\le b/a\le Cs^3\), and \(P\) is a monic nontorsion irreducible rational factor of \(X^{as}-X^a-1+X^b(1-X^{a(s-1)}-X^{as})\) with at most \(Ka\) exterior roots.
For \(d=\deg P\) write \(T_P(n)=d^{-1}\sum_{P(x)=0}(x/|x|)^n\) for every integer \(n\).
Let \(du\,dv\) denote normalized Haar measure on the unit two-torus, and put
\[
 A=1+u,\qquad R(u,v)=\frac{A-v}{1-\overline A v},\qquad
 c_k(i,j)=\int u^i v^j R(u,v)^k\,du\,dv
\]
for integers \(k,i,j\), assigning value one to \(R\) at its two undefined points.
For each fixed triple,
\[
 T_P(kb+ia+jas)\longrightarrow c_k(i,j).
\]
For every fixed \(k\) and \(\epsilon>0\) there is a finite set \(D\subset\mathbb Z^2\) with the following uniform property.
Given any envelope \(E_s=o(b)\) along a critical sequence, the limsup of the largest \(|T_P(kb+\ell)|\) over \(|\ell|\le E_s\) outside \(\{ia+jas:(i,j)\in D\}\) is at most \(\epsilon\), with empty maxima taken as zero.
Equivalently, offsets of size \(o(b)\) escaping every fixed finite set of such pairs have vanishing response.
In particular the first long power has the nonzero limiting mean
\[
                   T_P(b)\longrightarrow \frac23-\frac{\sqrt3}{2\pi}.
\]

## Approximation against a varying character

The [critical scales](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#scales) give \(as=o(b)\) and an off-circle root fraction tending to zero.
The [height and torus limits](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#statement) give \(h(x)=O(1/b)\) and Haar convergence of \((x^a,x^{as})\) over circle roots with weight \(1/d\).
At such a root the section equation reads \(x^b=R(x^a,x^{as})\), since \(x^{a(s-1)}=x^{as}/x^a\).
Here \(|A-v|=|1-\overline A v|\) on the torus, and simultaneous vanishing requires \(|A|=1\) and \(v=A\).
The two possible \(u\) are primitive cube roots of unity, which cannot occur as powers of a nontorsion root.
Consequently \(R^k\) is bounded with only a Haar-null discontinuity set, for negative as well as positive \(k\).
Applying torus convergence to the fixed test \(u^iv^jR^k\) gives the first limit, after bounding the omitted phase average by the off-circle fraction.

Choose a trigonometric polynomial \(Q=\sum_{(p,q)\in F}A_{pq}u^pv^q\) with \(\int|R^k-Q|<\epsilon/2\).
Such polynomials approximate in \(L^1\) by cutting off near the two exceptional points and then using uniform trigonometric approximation on the torus.
The absolute error test is bounded and continuous almost everywhere, so its circle-root mean converges to that same integral.
Multiplication by \(x^\ell\) at circle roots does not change this error, uniformly in \(\ell\).
Each term of the resulting polynomial average differs by \(o(1)\) from \(A_{pq}T_P(\ell+pa+qas)\) on restoring the off-circle phases.
Take \(D\) to consist of all pairs \((-p,-q)\) from \(F\).
Outside the resulting offsets all appearing frequencies \(t=\ell+pa+qas\) are nonzero and satisfy \(|t|=o(b)\) uniformly in the envelope.
The nontorsion units \(x^t\) then have height \(|t|h(x)=o(1)\), so their mean phases tend to zero by the [small-height orbit estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#orthogonality).
This holds uniformly by selecting a violating sequence of frequencies if uniformity fails, and every conjugate of \(x^t\) occurs equally often in the average over embeddings of \(x\).
Summing the finitely many terms proves the claimed bound.
Distinct offsets from any fixed finite set of different pairs are distinct for sufficiently large \(s\), since \(i+js=i'+j's\) forces equal pairs once \(s>|i-i'|\).

## Conditional moments and rational multiples

For fixed \(u\) with \(|A|<1\), the function \(R\) of \(v\) is holomorphic on the closed unit disk, of modulus one on its boundary and with value \(A\) at zero.
For \(|A|>1\) the reciprocal \(1/R\) has these properties with value \(1/A\) at zero.
Taking the constant Taylor coefficient, and using conjugation on the boundary in the second case, gives for every integer \(k\ge1\)
\[
 \int R(u,v)^k\,dv=\begin{cases} A^k,& |A|<1,\\ (1/\overline A)^k,& |A|>1.\end{cases}
\]
The negative moments are their conjugates.
At \(k=1\) write \(u=e^{it}\), \(-\pi\le t\le\pi\).
The inner region \(|t|>2\pi/3\) contributes the real integral of \(1+\cos t\), normalized by \(2\pi\), namely \(1/3-\sqrt3/(2\pi)\).
In the complementary region the real part of \(1/(1+e^{-it})\) is \(1/2\), contributing \(1/3\), and the imaginary parts cancel by symmetry.

For fixed integers \(p\) and \(q\ge2\) with \(qn=pb+\ell\), \(\ell=o(b)\), the same result determines the moments \(T_P(hqn)\) for each fixed integer \(h\) by applying the theorem with \(k=hp\) and offset \(h\ell\).
In particular, if \(\ell=ia+jas\) for fixed \(i,j\), the law of the \(q\)-th power of the phase of \(x^n\) converges to the law of \(u^iv^jR^p\), by convergence of every integer moment.
Identifying the law before taking that power requires further information on its choice of roots.
All assertions are conditional on the existence of the critical factors.
