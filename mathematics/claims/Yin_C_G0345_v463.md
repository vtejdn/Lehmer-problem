# Finite prime windows for simultaneous double factors at almost the least width

<a id="statement"></a>
## One exponent suffices at each degree

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) whose positive powers all generate \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
Put \(h=\log M(\delta)\), \(P_a=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\delta^a)\), \(G=P_1P_2\), and \(m=2d\).
The targets are distinct, both have degree \(d\), and \(m(G)=3h\), where \(m(G)\) here denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
A prime \(p>3\) is good if it does not divide \(\operatorname{disc}(G)G(1)G(-1)\).
For \(1\le r\le d\) put \(b=m+r-1,\ q=\lceil r/2\rceil\), choose a sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), and write \(\Lambda\) for the integer rows \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) satisfying
\[
 G(X)^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X^{pb+1}A(X^{-p}).
\]
A genuine row has \(A(\delta^p)A(\delta^{2p})\ne0\).
At every good prime the lattice has rank \(r\).
For each of the two targets its numerator-vanishing subspace has codimension at least \(q\) in the rational span of the lattice.
There exists a genuine row with one-norm \(H\) satisfying
\[
 \log H\le \frac{(3b-2)ph+E}{q}+\log(3\sqrt{b+1}),                         \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
 E=\log(m!)+m\log(2(1+pb))+6h+
       \tfrac12\log\binom{b+1}{m}+\tfrac r2\log r .
\]
For \(h\le1\) a good prime always lies in the explicit window
\[
 L_d<p\le X_d,\qquad L_d=\max(d,10^6),\qquad
 X_d=20L_d\lceil\log_2(2L_d)\rceil.                                      \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(p=O(d\log d)\) as \(d\to\infty\).

For each even \(d\ge2\), each prescribed sign, and every prime in (2), search widths \(2d\le b<3d\) and nonzero integral rows of one-norm \(H\le2^p\).
Keep a row if its graph has genuine double factors \(P_1,P_2\), where \(P_1\) is the minimum of some degree-\(d\) reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit and \(P_2\) is the distinct degree-\(d\) minimum of its square.
This search needs neither a supplied seed nor a good-reduction test.
Let \(T_d\) be the minimum of \(\log 2\) and the following scores over the retained rows at all those primes and widths:
\[
 \frac{(b-2d+1)\log 2}{d}
       +\frac{2\log H+\log(2pb+1)}{3p}.                                    \tag{3}
\]
Every \(T_d\) is computable and bounds the unrestricted infimum \(\lambda\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measures from above.
Then
\[
      \lambda=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \liminf_{\substack{d\to\infty\\2\mid d}} T_d=0 .                    \tag{4}
\]
Every sequence of actual scores tending to zero supplies genuine factors of measure tending to zero at widths \(b=2d+o(d)\).
For a hypothetical sequence of seeds of measure tending to zero, a suitable row exists at every good prime in its prescribed window for each sufficiently large degree.
Thus the [fixed-seed iterated limit](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#gap) can be replaced by a single degree limit with a bounded search over exponents at each degree.
The [fixed-budget consequence](#budget) gives the conditional unrestricted lower bound \((\log 2)/145\) using only width \(3d-1\) in these windows.

<a id="digits"></a>
## The reciprocal quotient gives independent numerator values modulo a good prime

For each \(a=1,2\), let \(y=\xi^p\) with \(\xi\) running through the \(d\) roots of \(\bar P_a\).
The [exact integral kernel](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#rank) has rank \(r\), and its reduction modulo \(p\) identifies with \(\bar A=\bar G C\), \(\deg C<r\).
Its local hypotheses hold because all residue nodes are simple, disjoint and different from the endpoints.
Set \(t=r-1\) and \(C_t^*(Y)=Y^t C(1/Y)\).
In the unramified splitting field write \(\omega\) for the prime-to-\(p\) root of unity lifting \(\xi\) and \(x\) for the root of \(P_a\) above \(\xi\).
Then \(x^p\equiv\omega^p\bmod p^2\).
The [next numerator digit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#digits), with \(V=\varepsilon A_b^*\), gives
\[
   A(x^p)/p\bmod p
     = y\bar G'(y)\{C(y)+\varepsilon\xi C_t^*(y)\}.                       \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(\bar A_b^*=\bar G C_t^*\) by reciprocity, and \(\bar G(y)=0\).
The formula extends to all rows by linearity.

On the space \(C=C_t^*\) of dimension \(q\), the expression in braces is \((1+\varepsilon\xi)C(y)\).
If it vanishes at all \(d\) residues of \(P_a\), then \(C=0\), since \(t<d\) and Frobenius permutes the distinct residues.
The map in (5) for each target therefore has rank at least \(q\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), with the conjugate residue coordinates taken together.
The integral rows whose numerator at \(\delta^{ap}\) vanishes form a saturated subgroup of \(\Lambda\), being the kernel of a homomorphism into a characteristic-zero field.
Their reductions in \(\Lambda/p\Lambda\) have dimension equal to their rank and lie in the kernel of (5) by conjugacy.
This proves the rational codimension bound at the stated exponent.

<a id="height"></a>
## A finite bound for the shortest genuine row

Use the [value and Euler derivative matrix](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#rank) over the splitting field of \(G\), with one representative \(x\) per inverse pair and two rows per representative.
At an embedding with \(u_x=\log|\sigma(x)|\), either entry at column \(l\) has absolute value at most
\[
  2(1+pb)\max(1,|\sigma(x)|)
       \exp\bigl(p(bu_x/2+|l-b/2||u_x|)\bigr).
\]
Set \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_{m/2}\) equal to the sorted \(|u_x|\).
Their multiset is embedding-independent, \(\sum v_i=3h\) and \(v_1\le2h\).
In a maximal minor each column occurs once and each site occurs twice.
Assigning the largest column distances to the largest site weights gives the archimedean exponential contribution at most \(p(b\sum_xu_x+U_b)\), where
\[
 U_b=\sum_i(b-2i+2)v_i\le3bh-2(3h-v_1)\le(3b-2)h .
\]
The logarithm of the remaining factor is at most \(\log(m!)+m\log(2(1+pb))+6h\), since \(2\sum_x\max(0,u_x)\le6h\).
All entries are algebraic integers at finite places and \(b\sum_xu_x\) averages to zero by the unit product formula.
The [primitive complementary wedge](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#support) therefore has largest coordinate at most the exponential of \(pU_b+\log(m!)+m\log(2(1+pb))+6h\).
Its Euclidean norm is the determinant of the saturated lattice and costs at most the additional factor \(\sqrt{\binom{b+1}m}\).

For the Euclidean successive minima \(\lambda_i\), [Minkowski's second theorem](../audits/literature/Hen02/Yin_U001.md#hen02-u001) gives \(\prod_i\lambda_i\le r^{r/2}\det\Lambda\), since the unit ball contains a cube of side \(2/\sqrt r\) in the real span of the lattice.
Set \(j=r-q+1\).
All nonzero integer vectors have norm at least one, so \(\lambda_j^q\le\prod_i\lambda_i\).
Choose \(j\) independent rows of norm at most \(\lambda_j\).
For each of the two targets some row in this set has nonzero numerator by the codimension bound.
Choose \(A_1\) nonzero at the first target and \(A_2\) nonzero at the second.
At most two integers \(z\) are excluded by requiring \(A_1+zA_2\) to be nonzero at both sites, so a choice in \(\{0,1,2\}\) gives a genuine row of one-norm at most \(3\sqrt{b+1}\lambda_j\).
This proves (1).

<a id="primes"></a>
## Avoiding every bad residue with a bounded prime

The Vandermonde determinant at the \(m\) conjugate nodes and the row Euclidean norm estimate give
\[
 |\operatorname{disc}(G)|\le m^m M(G)^{2(m-1)}.
\]
Evaluation at the endpoints gives \(|G(1)G(-1)|\le2^{2m}M(G)^2\).
The product of primes dividing \(\operatorname{disc}(G)G(1)G(-1)\) is consequently at most \(m^m 2^{2m}\exp(6mh)\).

Put \(\theta(N)=\sum_{p\le N}\log p\).
The largest binomial coefficient of order \(N-1\) is at least \(2^{N-1}/N\) and divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)\).
Indeed the factorial valuation formula contributes at most one carry for each prime power at most \(N\).
Prime powers of exponent at least two in the logarithm of this least common multiple together contribute at most \(\sqrt N\log N\), by grouping terms at each prime up to \(\sqrt N\).
Hence \(\theta(N)\ge (N-1)\log2-\log N-\sqrt N\log N\), which exceeds \(N/2\) for \(N\ge10^6\).
This numerical bound follows since \(\log N/\sqrt N\) and \(\log(2N)/N\) decrease there.
Write \(L=L_d\) and \(X=X_d\).
Then \(\theta(X)-\theta(L)>X/2-L\log L>9L\log(2L)\).
For \(h\le1\) the logarithm of the bound on the bad-prime product is at most \(2L\log(2L)+(4\log2+12)L<4L\log(2L)\).
This proves (2).

<a id="finite"></a>
## Recovering the two factors from a finite search

At fixed \(d\), the windows, widths and height boxes in (3) are finite.
For each row one may factor its [derivative polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) of degree at most \(2b\), test the recovery equation there, and form each recovered minimum \(P\) and the minimum of the square of one of its roots.
Check that both distinct degree-\(d\) minima qualify, including reciprocity, the unit condition, double contact and nonzero numerators.
One may also check those conditions by factoring the graph itself.
All score comparisons reduce to comparisons of positive rational numbers by clearing the denominators of their logarithmic coefficients.
If \(h'\) is the positive logarithmic measure of a recovered degree-\(d\) seed, its degree-\(d\) square has measure \(2h'\).
The [derivative product bound](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) therefore gives \(3ph'\le2\log H+\log(2pb+1)\) at each successful prime, including primes of bad reduction.
This proves the assertion about every actual score and \(\lambda\le T_d\), using \(\lambda\le\log2\) for the fallback.

If \(\lambda=0\), factorization and the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) followed by the [power-field norm](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) produce seeds as above with \(0<h\to0\).
Their degrees tend to infinity by the coefficient bound in bounded degree.
Take \(r=\max(\lceil\sqrt d\rceil,\lceil d\sqrt h\rceil)\le d\) eventually.
In (2) we have \(d<p=O(d\log d)\), \(b<3d\), and \(E=O(d\log(2pd))\) with absolute implied constant for \(h\le1\).
Equation (1) at any good prime in that window yields
\[
 \frac{\log H}{p}
     =O\left(\sqrt h+\frac{\log(2d)}{\sqrt d}\right).
\]
Indeed \(q\ge r/2\), \(dh/r\le\sqrt h\), and \(r\ge\sqrt d\).
These rows eventually lie below the fallback norm cutoff, and \(r/d\to0\) and \(\log(2pb+1)/p\to0\).
They prove the forward implication of (4).
In the other direction the fallback is positive, and each actual score tending to zero forces both its nonnegative summands to tend to zero.
This proves (4), including the asserted width and the recovery of actual positive measures.

<a id="budget"></a>
## A single width for excluding a uniform positive interval

For \(r=d\) take \(b=3d-1,\ q=d/2\).
If \(h\le1\) and \(p\) is good in (2), (1) gives
\[
  \frac{\log H+\tfrac12\log(2pb+1)}p
       \le 18h+\frac{24\log(2p)}p.                                        \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\log(m!)/q\le4\log(2p)\) and \(m\log(2(1+pb))/q\le12\log(2p)\), using \(m=2d,\ b<3d,\ d<p\).
The quantities \(6h/q,\ \log\binom{b+1}{m}/(2q),\ r\log r/(2q)\) are each at most \(\log(2p)\) by \(d\ge2,\ \binom{b+1}m\le2^{3d}\).
Finally \(\log(3\sqrt{b+1})\le2\log(2p)\) and \(\tfrac12\log(2pb+1)\le2\log(2p)\).
For \(p\ge10^6\), the error in (6) is smaller than \((\log2)/(8\cdot145)\) by monotonicity of \(\log(2p)/p\).
Thus \(0<h\le(\log2)/145\) guarantees a genuine row satisfying the strict budget \(((2pb+1)H^2)^4<2^p\), at each good prime in the window and for either sign.

Suppose that for every even \(d\ge2\), one sign, possibly depending on \(d\), has no such passing row with a qualifying degree-\(d\) unit and its square at any prime in (2) at this single width.
Then \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/145\) by the same factorization and norm reduction.
Each degree exclusion is decidable by the finite search above.
The asserted exclusions across all degrees remain open.
