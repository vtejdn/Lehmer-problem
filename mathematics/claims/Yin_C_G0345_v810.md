# Endpoint squareclasses and finite ramification of the original root fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A parity test for the factor degree

Use the [primitive two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) along any sequence with \(k\to\infty\) and \(l/k\to\infty\).
Eventually each monic irreducible rational factor \(S\) is reciprocal and simple by the [trace-field theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#factors).
For a root \(z\) let \(t=z+z^{-1}\), \(B=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\), \(2n=\deg S\), and let \(H\) count the exterior roots of \(S\).
Put \(e=1\) for the unique factor whose value at \(-1\) is divisible by 23 when \(l\) is odd, and \(e=0\) otherwise.
Then
\[
 N_{B/\mathbb Q}(t^2-4)\in(-23)^e\mathbb Q^{\times2},\qquad n-H\equiv e\pmod2.
\]
In particular an even exterior count forces degree divisible by four, except in that single factor of degree two modulo four.
In the joint rational splitting group an odd number of exchanges of inverse pairs within a factor is possible exactly for the factor with \(e=1\), where the associated quadratic character cuts out \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})\).
Every dyadic place of \(B\) splits in \(K\).

For each \(\eta\in\{1,-1\}\) with \(\eta^l=1\), assume
\[
                         l\not\equiv2\eta^k k\pmod5.                    \tag{1}
\]
Then \(K/B\) is unramified at all finite places except, for the factor with \(e=1\), one residue-degree-one place over 23 with relative discriminant exponent one.
For each such endpoint \(\eta\), exactly one factor has a trace place with \(t\equiv2\eta\bmod 5\), that completion is \(\mathbb Q_5\), and its quadratic root extension is unramified and inert.
Consequently if \(l\) is even and (1) holds, every original root field is quadratic over its trace field with the same absolute root discriminant.

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## Splitting at two and the discriminant character

The values of the full section are \(P(1)=25\) and \(P(-1)=24+(-1)^l\), since \(A(\pm1)=A^*(\pm1)=12\).
All factors have nonzero endpoint values, and there is exactly one carrier of 23 when \(l\) is odd.
The trace polynomial \(Q\) of \(S\) satisfies \(S(X)=X^nQ(X+X^{-1})\), giving the [relative order discriminant](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative) with norm
\[
              N(t^2-4)=Q(2)Q(-2)=(-1)^n S(1)S(-1).
\]
The two [graphs in characteristic two](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#reduction) are exchanged by simultaneous inversion since \(U-U^{-1}\) is fixed there.
They are disjoint in the torus: coincidence of reciprocal graph values would force \(V=1\), where both graph equations equal one.
At any dyadic trace place, reduction of \((z^k,z^l)\) and its inverse in an algebraic closure of the residue field therefore lie on different graphs defined over the prime field.
If the local quadratic algebra were a field, its involution would permute the two reductions by a residue-field automorphism and preserve each graph, which is impossible.
Thus \(t^2-4\) is square in each dyadic completion and its rational norm is square in \(\mathbb Q_2\).
Up to rational squares this odd integer is \(\pm5^a23^e\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
An odd dyadic square is one modulo eight, forcing \(a=0\) and the displayed signed squareclass.
Precisely \(n-H\) of the real embeddings of \(B\) send \(t\) strictly between \(-2\) and 2, and only those contribute negative signs to the norm.
This gives the parity test.
Choosing one root \(z_i\) from each inverse pair gives a square root \(\prod_i(z_i-z_i^{-1})\) of the norm.
Permutation of the pairs leaves the product unchanged, and each internal exchange negates it, giving the stated rational character.

<a id="odd-places"></a>
## Odd places of the trace field

Only odd places dividing \(t^2-4\) can ramify, by the relative discriminant divisibility.
If \(e=1\), the rational norm has 23-adic valuation one, so one degree-one place occurs with valuation one.
Adjoining the square root there gives a tamely ramified quadratic extension with discriminant exponent one.
This also treats 23 without any condition on \(k,l\).

To analyze 5 write \(P(X)=X^{2k+l}Q_P(X+X^{-1})\).
At each marked endpoint \(\eta^l=1\), expand \(X=\eta\exp(h)\) formally to order two.
With \(u=X^k-X^{-k}\) the normalized section is
\[
       (12+u^2)(X^l+X^{-l})-7u(X^l-X^{-l})+1
         =25+c h^2+O(h^4),\quad c=8k^2+12l^2-28\eta^kkl.
\]
Since \(X+X^{-1}=2\eta+\eta h^2+O(h^4)\), one has \(Q_P(2\eta)=25\) and \(Q_P'(2\eta)=\eta c\).
Modulo five, \(c\equiv2(l-2\eta^k k)^2\), a nonsquare unit under (1).
Hensel lifting the simple root gives exactly one trace root \(t_0\equiv2\eta\) in the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_5\), and \(t_0\in\mathbb Q_5\).
Indeed the local factor associated with that residue root has degree one, and every local trace root is integral.
Taylor expansion gives \((t_0-2\eta)/25\equiv-1/(\eta c)\bmod5\), so \((t_0^2-4)/25\equiv-4/c\bmod5\) is nonsquare.
The corresponding trace place thus gives the unramified quadratic extension.
At an endpoint with \(\eta^l=-1\), \(Q_P(2\eta)\) is a signed 23, hence a unit at five.
Together with the endpoint norm identity these are all possible places, and the discriminant tower formula gives the root discriminant equality.
