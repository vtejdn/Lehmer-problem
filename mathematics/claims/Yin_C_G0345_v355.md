# Torsion cosets detect the minimum prime-residue support of a subplastic family

<a id="statement"></a>
## An obstruction measured on the original roots

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic with nonzero constant term, let \(p\) be a prime, and let \(\ell\ne p\) be another prime.
Work over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), write \(\overline P\) for the reduction, and fix \(\xi\ne0\) and a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\).
Index the elements \(x_j=\xi\zeta^j\) by \(j\in\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\).
Choose any nonempty set of indices \(S\) on which all the \(x_j\) have the same multiplicative order.
Set
\[
 e_j=\operatorname{ord}_{x_j}\overline P,\qquad
 E=\{j\in S:e_j>\min_{i\in S} e_i\},
\]
using multiplicity zero at every nonroot.

If \(E\) contains \(k\ge1\) consecutive positions in an arithmetic progression of nonzero step modulo \(p\), then every product \(CP\), with \(C\) any product of cyclotomic polynomials, occupies at least \(k+1\) exponent classes modulo \(p\).
In particular, two distinct positions in \(E\) exclude every representation on at most two classes.
This test uses the actual multiplicities in auxiliary characteristic \(\ell\).
Two simple roots of the original polynomial and one nonroot of the same order in one coset suffice for the two-class obstruction.
They can occur even when every root examined on this coset is simple.

For each prime \(p\ge5\), consider the integral polynomial
\[
             P_p(x)=x^{2p}-x^{p+1}-x^p-x^{p-1}+1 .
\]
The minimum number of exponent classes modulo \(p\) among **all** its cyclotomic multiples is exactly three.
The polynomials \(P_p\) are monic irreducible reciprocal units of degree \(2p\).
Their measures tend to a fixed \(L\) strictly between one and the plastic number \(\theta\), the positive root of \(x^3-x-1\).
Thus for each cutoff \(L<A<\theta\), inputs with \(M(P_p)<A\) attain this minimum at an unbounded sequence of primes.

<a id="coset"></a>
## Subtracting the common multiplicity on a coset

A cyclotomic factor has constant multiplicity on all roots of any one order in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell^\times\).
In fact \(\overline\Phi_n\) has simple roots of exact order \(n\) when \(\ell\nmid n\), and for \(a\ge1\) one has \(\overline\Phi_{\ell^a n}=\overline\Phi_n^{\,\ell^{a-1}(\ell-1)}\), by Frobenius and the cyclotomic factorization.
Consequently \(C\) adds one common multiplicity on \(S\), and for each \(j\in E\) the multiplicity of \(\overline{CP}\) at \(x_j\) strictly exceeds its minimum on the entire coset, regardless of what happens outside \(S\).

Suppose \(CP\) occupies at most \(k\) classes.
The nonzero reduction \(F=\overline{CP}\) has a decomposition
\[
              F(x)=\sum_{r\in R} x^r U_r(x^p),\qquad
                        R\subseteq\{0,\ldots,p-1\},\quad 1\le |R|\le k,
\]
in which all rows are nonzero.
Monicity ensures that at least one survives reduction.
Put \(b=\xi^p\) and \(m=\min_{r\in R}\operatorname{ord}_{b}U_r\), and write \(U_r(Y)=(Y-b)^m V_r(Y)\).
The residual polynomial \(F/(x^p-b)^m\) has values
\[
                 \sum_{r\in R}\xi^r V_r(b)\,\zeta^{jr}
\]
at \(x_j\).
At least one coefficient is nonzero.
The polynomial in \(\zeta^j\) giving these values has degree less than \(p\), so it cannot vanish at all \(p\) distinct grid points.
The factor \(x^p-b\) is simple at each \(x_j\) because \(\ell\ne p\).
Thus \(m\) equals the minimum multiplicity of \(F\) on the coset, and the residual sum must vanish at every index in \(E\).

Let \(r_1,\ldots,r_v\) be the exponents whose residual coefficients are nonzero, with \(1\le v\le k\).
At the first \(v\) forbidden indices \(j_0,j_0+a,\ldots,j_0+(v-1)a\), the coefficient matrix is the product of a Vandermonde matrix on the distinct nodes \(\zeta^{a r_i}\) and an invertible diagonal matrix with entries \(\zeta^{j_0r_i}\).
It is invertible since \(a\ne0\bmod p\), giving the desired contradiction.
For the nonzero residual word, this weight bound from consecutive zeros is the classical [BCH bound](../audits/literature/DL24/Yin_U001.md#dl24-u001).
This argument uses no separability assumption on \(\overline P\) away from the chosen points.

<a id="family"></a>
## A pair of simple roots at order twice the support prime

Let \(S_p(T)\) be the monic trace polynomial specified by \(\Phi_p(z)=z^{(p-1)/2} S_p(z+z^{-1})\).
All its complex roots lie strictly below two and are real, so \(S_p(3)>1\) is an integer.
Choose any prime divisor \(\ell\) of this integer.
It cannot be \(p\), since in characteristic \(p\) the identity \(\Phi_p(z)=(z-1)^{p-1}\) gives \(S_p(T)=(T-2)^{(p-1)/2}\).
For \(\ell\ne p\), every root of \(S_p\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\) is the trace of a primitive \(p\)-th root by the defining factorization and separability of \(z^p-1\).
In particular there is a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\) with \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=3\).
At \(\ell=2\), a root with this trace would solve \(z^2+z+1=0\) and have order three.
At \(\ell=5\), it would solve \((z+1)^2=0\) and have order two.
Thus every such \(\ell\) is odd and different from \(5,p\).

Take the coset \(-\mu_p\) and its subset of all points except \(-1\), which have common order \(2p\).
On this coset \(x^p=-1\), so the Laurent polynomial
\[
                   x^{-p}P_p(x)=x^p+x^{-p}-x-x^{-1}-1
\]
vanishes precisely when \(x+x^{-1}=-3\).
There are exactly two such points, \(-\zeta\) and \(-\zeta^{-1}\).
At them the logarithmic differentiation operator \(x\,d/dx\) applied to the displayed Laurent polynomial gives \(-(x-x^{-1})\ne0\), since \(x^p=x^{-p}=-1\) there and \(x\ne\pm1\).
These two roots are simple.
There are \(p-1>2\) points of order \(2p\) in the coset, so another such point has multiplicity zero.
The first part of the statement excludes every cyclotomic multiple on two or fewer classes.
The five exponents of \(P_p\) itself occupy exactly \(0,1,-1\bmod p\), proving the matching upper bound.

The [prime-half-degree family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) proves irreducibility for every \(p\ge5\).
Its degree-\(p\) trace polynomial reduces to the irreducible \(T^p-T-1\) in characteristic \(p\), and a real trace in \((-2,2)\) forces the further quadratic extension.
The [measure calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit) gives \(M(P_p)\to L\in(1,\theta)\).
This puts the family below any fixed cutoff between \(L\) and \(\theta\) for sufficiently large primes.
The simple roots found above concern the stated coset only.

## Earlier degree bounds and the small-measure question

The existing two-row estimates already exclude such a padding for all sufficiently large \(p\) in this family.
Indeed, after [removing common row content](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage), the [two-row degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#two) in the eventual subplastic interval forces \((p-10)\deg f+S\le2p\), with \(S>0\), on a coprime reciprocal pair \(f,f^*\) in the two-row normal form.
Here the support differences of \(P_p\) have gcd one since it has consecutive occupied exponents.
The seed is nonconstant since a constant gives a signed binomial of measure one.
For \(p>30\) its degree is at most two, and the two rows together have at most six terms.
The [two-row comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v342.md#statement) then gives \(m(P_p)\ge m(f)-O(\log(p)/p)\), with \(M(f)\ge\theta\) by the [nonreciprocal seed bound](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#rows).
This contradicts \(L<\theta\) along any unbounded sequence of these primes.

The auxiliary-characteristic test gives the exact minimum at every prime \(p\ge5\) and applies to other inputs through their own coset multiplicities.
The earlier [test in the characteristic of the support prime](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v147.md#multiplicities) uses different local data and gives a lower bound of at most two at squarefree reductions.
Here the obstruction uses the three different positions consisting of the two simple roots and a nonroot, with \(\ell\ne p\).
The family leaves open which primes and residue counts occur on arbitrary inputs with measures tending to one.
