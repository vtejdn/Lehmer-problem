# A factor gap for exponents on a quadratic progression

<a id="statement"></a>
## Factor measures and the shape of a set of exponents

For each fixed \(T\ge1\) there is an effectively computable \(c_T>0\) with the following property.
Let \(a,b,s\) be any integers and let \(P\) be a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial all of whose exponents belong to
\[
                 \{s+aj+bj^2:0\le j\le T,\ j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Every integer Laurent polynomial factor of \(P\) with positive logarithmic Mahler measure has logarithmic measure at least \(c_T\).
The exponents, the coefficients and the cofactor measure are unrestricted.

More generally let \(S\) be the support of a two-variable complex Laurent polynomial \(G\), and let \(N\) denote the sum of the horizontal and vertical widths of its Newton polygon, the convex hull of \(S\).
Suppose this polygon has at most one direction in which two distinct parallel edges occur.
Segments and points are also allowed.
Let \(a,b\) be integers, not both zero, and suppose \(G(z^a,z^b)\) is not identically zero.
For a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic integer \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\) put \(e_*=\min_{n\ge1}\deg(\alpha^n)\).
The fraction of its original conjugates \(z\) for which \(G(z^a,z^b)=0\) is at most
\[
                              \min(1,N/\sqrt{e_*}).                        \tag{1}
\]
The coefficients may vary arbitrarily in \(\mathbb C\).

If the polygon condition holds for the convex hull of each subset of \(S\), there is also a small-value estimate.
Let \(Q\) have \(r\ge2\) distinct exponents from \(\{ai+bj:(i,j)\in S\}\) up to translation, shift its true minimum exponent to zero and normalize its coefficients to Euclidean norm one.
Let \(W\) be the true exponent span and \(h=h(\alpha)\).
With \(L=\min(1,N/\sqrt{e_*})\), the original conjugate fraction at which \(|Q(z)|\le t\) is at most
\[
                 2L+
   \left(\frac{r\,2^{r-1}(\log\sqrt r+Wh)}
                    {\log(1/(rt))}\right)^{1/r}\qquad (0<t<1/r).          \tag{2}
\]
Thus every fixed support of this kind has vanishing small-value density at critical width \(Wh=O(1)\) on a shrinking positive-measure sequence as \(t\to0\).
The quadratic progression above has the required property on every subset.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Reciprocal curves require two pairs of parallel edges

Use the distinct orbit points \((z^a,z^b)\) for \(z\) a conjugate of \(\alpha\).
Its size is \(D=\deg(\alpha^{\gcd(|a|,|b|)})\ge e_*\).
Every point has the same number of preimages, and coordinatewise inversion permutes the orbit.
For complex \(G\) impose, on its coefficient vector at \(S\), the linear equations giving zero at all its current orbit zeros.
These equations have coefficients in the splitting field.
Preserving the support excludes finitely many coordinate hyperplanes, and keeping the specialization nonzero excludes a further proper linear subspace of the solution space.
The original vector witnesses all these exclusions.
Over the infinite splitting field the resulting nonempty open subset has a rational point there, by ordinary linear algebra.
Hence a polynomial over that field with the original support, nonzero specialization, and at least the original zeros suffices.

Factor this polynomial over the algebraic closure in the Laurent ring, ignoring the invertible monomial.
For each irreducible factor \(f\) let \(n_f\) be the sum of the two coordinate widths of its Newton polygon.
Widths add under products, including multiplicity, because the product of the initial forms in a fixed direction is nonzero.
More generally the polygon of the product is the Minkowski sum of those of the factors, by the same argument in every direction.
If one summand were a two-dimensional centrally symmetric polygon it would have pairs of opposite edges in at least two independent directions.
The exposed faces of the total sum with these outer normals each contain a translate of the corresponding edge, giving those same two pairs on the polygon of the product.
The hypothesis therefore excludes every such summand. [Newton polygons of products](Yin_C_G0345_v353.md#directions)

A one-dimensional irreducible factor is, up to a monomial, \(X^uY^v-\gamma\) for a primitive \((u,v)\) and a nonzero algebraic \(\gamma\).
Indeed its exponent differences are multiples of one primitive vector and an ordinary univariate polynomial splits over the algebraic closure.
If \(au+bv\ne0\), its orbit zeros have mass at most \(1/e_*\) by the degree of that power.
If \(au+bv=0\) the specialization is constant up to a monomial and nonzero because the whole specialization is nonzero.
In either case its zero mass is at most \(n_f/\sqrt{e_*}\).

For a factor with two-dimensional polygon let \(M\) count its distinct orbit zeros.
Work in a finite normal extension containing its coefficients and the full orbit.
Every coefficient conjugate \(f^\sigma\) has the original polygon.
The inverse curve defined by \(f(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\) has the negated polygon, which is not a translate of the original one by the exclusion just proved.
Thus the two absolutely irreducible torus curves have no common component.
After translating each support so that the coordinate minima are zero, their plane equations have degree at most \(n_f\).
Neither is divisible by a coordinate, hence there is no extra common coordinate component and Bézout's plane-curve bound gives at most \(n_f^2\) common points in the torus.
By inversion there are exactly \(M\) orbit points on the inverse curve.
For a uniform automorphism the probability that a specified orbit point belongs to \(f^\sigma=0\) is \(M/D\).
Consequently \(M^2/D\le n_f^2\), so the zero fraction of this factor is at most \(n_f/\sqrt{e_*}\).
Adding over factors proves (1), including a monomial with no zeros.

<a id="small"></a>
## Subsets, projected exponents and the height cost

Choose one source point of \(S\) for each distinct actual exponent of \(Q\), before the common shift.
A nonzero hyperplane functional on the monomial rows is the specialization of a polynomial supported on a nonempty subset of these points.
Its specialization is nonzero because the chosen projections are distinct.
Every such polygon satisfies the hypothesis and has sum of widths at most \(N\), so (1) bounds the mass of every hyperplane by \(L\).
Translate the actual exponents to minimum zero and maximum \(W\).
The [normalized determinant calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v745.md#volume) now applies with precisely these rows.
The total cost of nonzero ordered \(r\)-row determinants is at most \(r d^r(\log\sqrt r+Wh)\).
When the small-value mass is \(\delta>2L\), the probability of drawing \(r\) independent small-value rows is at least \(\delta^r/2^{r-1}\), and their normalized determinants have modulus at most \(rt\).
Comparing costs gives (2).
The divergence of the minimum powered degree and eventual reciprocity on shrinking positive-measure sequences follow from [norm descent](Yin_C_G0345_v739.md#volume) and [Smyth's gap](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001).

## Quadratic exponents and a computable gap

A finite subset of the graph of a strictly convex function at distinct abscissas has as its lower hull the successive segments joining its points in order.
Their slopes are strictly increasing.
Its upper hull is the single endpoint chord, so there is at most one pair of distinct parallel edges.
This applies to every subset of \(\{(j,j^2):0\le j\le T\}\) with width sum at most \(T+T^2\).
For repeated projected exponents choose one representative per surviving term, including after the cancellations in a specified polynomial.

For integer factors, discard powers of the variable and use multiplicativity of Mahler measure.
Integer content greater than one, or a primitive irreducible factor whose leading or nonzero constant coefficient has absolute value at least two, gives log measure at least \(\log 2\).
A nonreciprocal algebraic unit has the positive lower bound of Smyth.
For a remaining reciprocal nontorsion unit factor, every conjugate of any root \(\alpha\) is a zero of the projected polynomial.
Thus (1) forces \(e_*\le (T+T^2)^2\).
For a power realizing this minimum, the norm of \(\alpha\) to its powered field is a nontorsion unit \(\gamma\) of degree at most \(e_*\) and logarithmic measure at most that of \(\alpha\), by the [power norm identity](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#primitive-phase).
Among algebraic units of degree at most \((T+T^2)^2\) and positive logarithmic measure at most one the possible monic minimal polynomials form an effectively enumerable finite set, by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
Torsion can be removed by the cyclotomic criterion, and rational positive lower bounds for the remaining measures can be computed by isolating their algebraic roots.
Taking the minimum with one, \(\log 2\) and the nonreciprocal gap gives a positive computable bound.
If the projection has just one exponent, only its integer coefficient can contribute positive measure.
This proves the first assertion.
