# Counting finite-field inverse powers factor by factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## Inverse polynomials at all prime orders

Let \(p\ne q\) be primes, with \(q\) odd, and let \(F\in\mathbb F_p[X]\) be monic of degree \(d<q\) with nonzero constant term.
For a monic polynomial \(T\) write \(T^{[q]}\) for the polynomial obtained by raising its roots to the \(q\)-th power, with multiplicities.
Write \(F=\prod_g g^{m_g}\) over distinct monic irreducibles in \(\mathbb F_p[X]\).
The number of monic \(T\) with \(T^{[q]}=F\) is \(\prod_g C_g\), and the construction below lists their root multisets.
For a factor \(g\), set \(f=\deg g,\ m=m_g,\ Q=p^f\), and take \(z=X\bmod g\) in \(\mathbb F_Q\).

* If \(q\nmid Q-1\), put \(t=\operatorname{ord}_q(Q)>1\) and \(h=(q-1)/t\).
  Then
  \[
                     C_g=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/t\rfloor}
                                       \binom{h+j-1}{j}.                \tag{1}
  \]
  In particular this factor has a unique lift whenever \(m<t\).
* If \(q\mid Q-1\) and \(z^{(Q-1)/q}\ne1\), then \(C_g=0\).
* If \(q\mid Q-1\) and \(z^{(Q-1)/q}=1\), then \(C_g=\binom{q+m-1}{m}\).

When \(F\) is reciprocal and squarefree, there is a sharper product formula for lifts whose root multisets are closed under inversion.
Choose one factor from each pair \(g,g^*\) of distinct reciprocal monic factors and use \(C_g\) for each such pair, where the star denotes inversion of roots and normalization to monic form.
Each linear root \(1\) or \(-1\) contributes a factor one.
Each remaining self-reciprocal irreducible \(g\) has even degree \(f=2b\).
Set \(N=p^b+1\).
Its contribution is one if \(q\nmid N\), is zero if \(q\mid N\) and \(z^{N/q}\ne1\), and is \(q\) if \(q\mid N\) and \(z^{N/q}=1\).
In particular every simple self-reciprocal factor has a unique reciprocal lift whenever \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) is odd.
Here an inversion-stable multiset is allowed either sign of its resulting constant term when linear roots occur.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Multisets on the fiber of a Frobenius orbit

Work in the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_p\) and fix one root \(z\) of \(g\).
Choosing a lift above the entire \(p\)-Frobenius orbit of \(z\) is equivalent to choosing a multiset of size \(m\) on the \(q\) distinct solutions \(y^q=z\), invariant under \(\tau(y)=y^Q\).
Indeed the choices above the other \(f-1\) elements of the orbit are their successive \(p\)-th powers, and consistency after one turn is exactly \(\tau\)-invariance.
A Frobenius-invariant multiset gives a monic polynomial over the base field, including in the presence of multiplicities.
The fibers over distinct factors are disjoint, so their choices multiply.

If \(q\nmid Q-1\), raising to the \(q\)-th power bijects \(\mathbb F_Q^\times\).
For its unique preimage \(y_0\) of \(z\) in that field and a primitive \(q\)-th root of unity \(\xi\), the solutions are \(y_0\xi^j\) with \(j\bmod q\).
The action of \(\tau\) on these residues is multiplication by \(Q\).
It has one fixed point and \(h\) cycles of length \(t\).
If the sum of the common multiplicities assigned to these \(h\) cycles is \(j\), the fixed point takes the remaining multiplicity \(m-tj\).
Counting the nonnegative multiplicity vectors gives (1).

If \(q\mid Q-1\), the roots of unity of order \(q\) lie in \(\mathbb F_Q\).
For any chosen preimage \(y\), the action on the fiber is multiplication by \(y^{Q-1}=z^{(Q-1)/q}\), a \(q\)-th root of unity.
When this multiplier is nontrivial the fiber is one cycle of size \(q\), so \(0<m\le d<q\) excludes a lift.
When it equals one all \(q\) points are fixed, and distributing the multiplicity gives the last binomial in the statement.

<a id="reciprocity"></a>
## Inversion on squarefree factors

Assume \(F\) squarefree, so all the fibers must receive one point above each \(z\).
For a pair of distinct reciprocal factors the choices above the first factor prescribe those above the second by inversion.
This gives a bijection from the \(C_g\) choices to the admissible lifts of the pair.
At \(z=\pm1\) the selected point must itself be fixed by inversion and its \(q\)-th power must be \(z\).
The only choice is \(z\), also in characteristic two where the two possible targets coincide.

For a self-reciprocal orbit away from the two linear roots, inversion acts without a fixed point and commutes with the transitive cyclic Frobenius action.
It is thus a rotation of order two, forcing \(f=2b\) and \(z^{p^b}=z^{-1}\).
The sole selected root \(y\) over \(z\) must obey \(y^{p^b}=y^{-1}\), since both elements lie over \(z^{-1}\) in an inversion-stable lift.
Conversely every \(y\) with \(y^q=z\) and \(y^{p^b+1}=1\) supplies such a lift by taking its Frobenius orbit.
In fact \(y\) then lies in \(\mathbb F_{p^{2b}}\) and has degree \(2b\) since \(z=y^q\) has that degree.
The solutions form the fiber of exponentiation by \(q\) in the cyclic subgroup of order \(N=p^b+1\).
The displayed three cases follow from cyclicity, since \(z\) is already in that group.
If \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\) is odd then no power of \(p\bmod q\) is \(-1\), giving the stated simplification.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Applying the factor counts to simultaneous Salem radicals

In the [integral lifting test](Yin_C_G0345_v708.md#reduction) at primes \(n<p<q\), the reduced polynomial \(F=\overline P_a\) depends on \(n,\epsilon,a\bmod p\), its degree is at most \(p\), and its constant term is nonzero.
For each factor the finite tests above require only its degree, multiplicity, a power of its residue root and the integer order of \(p^f\bmod q\) when relevant.
A zero factor count eliminates that entire seed class.
If every factor has count one the inverse polynomial is determined even when \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\le d\).
Otherwise the orbit description lists all possibilities with multiplicity.
Reciprocity may be imposed while enumerating them, and in the squarefree case the sharper formula gives their exact number before reconstruction.
The [Newton trace bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v708.md#decoding) leave at most six integral candidates per inverse polynomial.
This tests the exceptional finite-field orders within the bounded range without enumerating integer seeds.
