# A common factor modulo an auxiliary integer for short rows at opposite prime residues

<a id="statement"></a>
## The forced factor and the partners that realize it

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(e\).
For an integer \(m\ge2\) coprime to \(P(1)P(-1)\), let \(T=T(P,m)\) be the multiplicative order of \(X\) in \((\mathbb Z/m)[X]/(P^2)\).
Take distinct primes \(p,q\) such that \(\gcd(p,T)=1\) and \(q\equiv-p\bmod T\).
Suppose the same nonzero integral polynomials \(U,V\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), satisfy
\[
                P(X)^2\mid U(X^t)+XV(X^t)\qquad (t=p,q).
\]
Write \(Q_p\) for the monic minimum of \(\alpha^p\).
Then \(\deg Q_p=e\), and reducing coefficients modulo \(m\) gives
\[
           \overline Q_p^{\,2}\mid\overline U,\qquad
           \overline Q_p^{\,2}\mid\overline V,\qquad
           m^{2e}\mid\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\ne0.                           \tag{1}
\]
The conclusion holds after removing the common integer content of the two rows.

Assume now that every positive power of \(\alpha\) has degree \(e\), and fix \(C>1\), an excess \(\delta>0\) and an exponent \(A>0\).
There exist a width \(w\) and a bound \(K\) depending only on the seed, \(C\) and \(\delta\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\) and every permitted auxiliary modulus \(2\le m\le(\log p)^A\), all but at most \(K\) primes \(q\in[p,Cp]\) with \(q\equiv-p\bmod T(P,m)\) admit one such coprime pair of degrees at most \(w\) with nonzero reflected determinant and
\[
       m(\alpha)\ \le\ \frac{\log H}{p+q}<m(\alpha)+\delta,\qquad
                   H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1).                                \tag{2}
\]
Here \(m(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha)\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure, whereas \(m\) alone denotes the auxiliary integer.
The number of primes in the indicated partner class is asymptotic to \((C-1)p/(\varphi(T)\log p)\), uniformly in this range of moduli, and tends to infinity uniformly.
The pair may depend on the two primes.
For fixed \(p\) the exceptional partners can be taken from one set of at most \(K\) primes in the entire interval, independent of \(m\).

<a id="opposite"></a>
## Comparing the graph equations over a finite ring

Write \(R=(\mathbb Z/m)[X]/(P^2)\) and \(x=X\bmod P^2\).
Its additive group is \((\mathbb Z/m)^{2e}\) and \(x\) is invertible because \(P(0)=1\).
Reciprocity of \(P\) therefore makes \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\) an involution on \(R\).
Put \(y=x^p\), so \(x^{-q}=y\).
Use the graph equation at \(p\), then apply the involution to the one at \(q\), to get
\[
                        U(y)+xV(y)=0,\qquad U(y)+x^{-1}V(y)=0.
\]
The factors \(x-1\) and \(x+1\) are units because \(P(X)^2-P(\pm1)^2\) is divisible by \(X\mp1\) and each \(P(\pm1)\) is a unit modulo \(m\).
Their product times \(x^{-1}\) is \(x-x^{-1}\).
Subtracting the equations gives \(V(y)=0\), whence \(U(y)=0\).

In characteristic zero, \(V(\alpha^p)\ne0\), since otherwise the graph equation gives a common root of \(U,V\).
The graph recovers \(\alpha=-U(\alpha^p)/V(\alpha^p)\), so \(Q_p\) has degree \(e\).
Since \(P(X)\mid Q_p(X^p)\) by monic division, \(\overline Q_p(y)^2=0\) in \(R\).
Evaluation at \(y\) is surjective onto \(R\), since an inverse to \(p\bmod T\) writes \(x\) as a nonnegative power of \(y\).
The resulting homomorphism
\[
            (\mathbb Z/m)[Y]/(\overline Q_p^{\,2})\longrightarrow R
\]
is an isomorphism because both rings have \(m^{2e}\) elements.
This proves the polynomial divisibilities, including at primes of bad reduction for \(P\).

<a id="resultant"></a>
## The Sylvester cokernel

Set \(a=\deg U,\ b=\deg V\).
Removing the common positive integer content preserves the divisibility of both graphs by the monic \(P^2\), and hence preserves the first two conclusions of (1).
The normalized pair has at least one coefficient nonzero modulo \(m\).
Since its two reductions are multiples of the monic degree-\(2e\) polynomial \(\overline Q_p^{\,2}\), it follows that \(\max(a,b)\ge2e\), also for the original pair.
The Sylvester map for the original pair sends polynomials \(B,C\) of respective degrees less than \(b,a\) to \(BU+CV\) in the free integer module of polynomials of degree less than \(a+b\).
Its determinant has absolute value \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\), which is positive by coprimality.
Evaluation at \(y\) kills its image and maps the target onto the additive group of \(R\), since the target includes the powers of \(Y\) through \(2e-1\).
Thus the finite cokernel of order \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\) has a quotient of order \(m^{2e}\), proving the last divisibility in (1).

<a id="partners"></a>
## Uniformity over auxiliary moduli and the degree-ten example

By the [two-prime fixed-seed result](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold), there are \(w,K\) as above such that for every sufficiently large \(p\) all but at most \(K\) prime partners in \([p,Cp]\) are distinct from \(p\) and admit a pair with the upper bound in (2).
The pair has \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), with \(F^\iota(Y)=F(Y^{-1})\).
The [common tangency inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) supplies the lower bound because \(P\) is repeated at both exponents.
Every congruence class of partners inherits the same exceptional set.

Since \(T\le |R|=m^{2e}\le(\log p)^{2eA}\), one has \(p>T\) eventually, so \(-p\bmod T\) is a reduced class.
Apply the [uniform prime count in reduced classes](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) at \(p,Cp\) with error \(O_{A',C}(p/(\log p)^{A'})\) for fixed \(A'>2eA+2\).
The main term is \((\operatorname{li}(Cp)-\operatorname{li}(p))/\varphi(T)\), asymptotic uniformly to the stated expression.
Changing an endpoint costs at most one prime, and the error is uniformly smaller than the main term since \(\varphi(T)\le(\log p)^{2eA}\).
The primes \(p,q\) are both larger than the auxiliary modulus throughout this range for large \(p\).

For the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#lehmer), \(P(1)=-1,\ P(-1)=1\), and its [Salem root has measure strictly below \(6/5\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks).
All its positive powers preserve degree because it is the only conjugate of its exterior modulus.
Choosing \(\delta<\log(6/5)-m(\alpha)\) gives \(H<(6/5)^{p+q}\).
Every fixed list of auxiliary moduli can be imposed simultaneously by using its least common multiple for \(m\), since the orders for its divisors divide \(T(P,m)\).
Consequently at this threshold the common factors and resultant divisibilities in (1) for all those moduli occur in actual qualifying pairs, for an unbounded number of partners at every sufficiently large first prime.

One can also let the normalized cost tend to the measure of this seed while the auxiliary modulus grows.
For any given \(A>0\), choose any integer function \(2\le m(p)\le(\log p)^A\) eventually, for example \(\lfloor(\log p)^A\rfloor\).
Take excesses \(1/j\) and any width budget \(b(p)\to\infty\).
For each \(j\), choose an increasing onset beyond which \(b(p)\) accommodates the width at that stage and the number of partners in the selected class exceeds its exception bound by at least \(j\).
On successive stages there are then at least \(j\) partners admitting (1) and (2) with this excess and the supplied width.
The argument proves existence of the onsets and gives no effective rate for shrinking the excess.
It gives no corresponding obstruction at thresholds below the measure of the chosen seed.
