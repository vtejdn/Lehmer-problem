# Local crowding of neutral Salem phases required for near-zero conditional eigenvalues

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction at every intermediate angular scale

Let \(\alpha>1\) run along a sequence of Salem numbers with \(m=\log\alpha\to0\), degree \(d\), and \(s=d-2\) distinct neutral arguments \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\), counting both conjugates.
Use angular distance in radians.
Take \(k\sim1/(2m)\), \(N=kd\), \(T=LN\) and \(\log(4T)=o(L)\).
At the \(i\)-th block of the [exact return bridge](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#cost), let \(\Sigma_i\) be the conditional covariance of its endpoint given the entire earlier digit past, in the neutral coordinates whitened by their unrestricted phase Gram \(W_N\) as in [v713](Yin_C_G0345_v713.md#statement).
Use \(\langle Z_i\rangle=L^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb E_{Q_T}Z_i\) for the mean under the return law.
For \(\eta\ge0\) tending to zero and \(U>0\) put
\[
 q_\eta=\frac1s\left\langle\#\{\lambda(\Sigma_i)\le\eta\}\right\rangle,\qquad
 c_U(\theta)=\#\{\theta'\in\Theta:\operatorname{dist}(\theta,\theta')\le U/N\}.
\]
For **every** \(U\to\infty\) and each fixed positive integer \(b\),
\[
       \limsup\left(q_\eta-
              \frac1s\#\{\theta\in\Theta:c_U(\theta)>b\}\right)\le0.   \tag{1}
\]
The count includes \(\theta\) itself.
In particular, along any subsequence where \(q_\eta\to\rho>0\), at least a limiting lower fraction \(\rho\) lies in these crowded arcs, for every fixed \(b\).
Taking \(1\ll U\ll k\) makes their width \(o(1/d)\).
Clusters of any bounded size with arbitrarily small internal gaps can be retained in the argument.

There is also a test using the actual power sums
\[
 u_j=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}e^{ij\theta}
   =\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^j)-\alpha^j-\alpha^{-j}.
\]
Whenever \(\liminf q_\eta>0\), one has, for every integer sequence \(H\) with \(H\ge c d\) for some \(c>0\) and \(H=o(N)\),
\[
                      \frac1{sH}\sum_{j=1}^{H}|u_j|^2\longrightarrow\infty .
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Thus a bounded second moment at even one such scale, including \(H=d\), forces \(q_\eta\to0\) for every \(\eta\to0\).

More generally, suppose that for some \(U\to\infty\) the counts at a uniformly chosen neutral root are tight, in the sense that
\[
             \lim_{b\to\infty}\limsup\frac1s\#\{\theta:c_U(\theta)>b\}=0. \tag{3}
\]
Then \(q_\eta\to0\), and there exists a deterministic \(\varepsilon\to0\) for which
\[
       \liminf\exp\!\left(\frac1s
                   \left\langle\log\det(\Sigma_i+\varepsilon I_s)\right\rangle\right)
                        \ge x_{1/2},                                    \tag{4}
\]
where \(0<x_{1/2}<1/2\) solves \(2x-1-\log(2x)=1\).
The bounded-moment condition above implies (3).
These criteria impose no growth condition on \(m\log d\).
Whether a shrinking Salem sequence can have the crowding required by (1) remains open here.

<a id="packets"></a>
## Time-coordinate leverage of separated clusters

The next deterministic estimate concerns the span of the rows \((e^{ij\theta})_{0\le j<N}\).
For fixed \(b\ge1,\ D\ge0\), partition \(u\) distinct selected phases into clusters of at most \(b\) phases each.
Assume every cluster has a chosen center among its phases and all its angles lie within \(D/N\) of that center.
There are constants \(G=G(b,D)\ge1,\ C=C(b,D)\) such that if different centers are at distance at least \(G/N\), the orthogonal projection onto the selected row space has every diagonal entry at most \(Cu/N\), for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The constants are independent of the number of clusters and of their smallest internal spacing.
For related work on angles between clustered Vandermonde subspaces see Batenkov, Diederichs, Goldman and Yomdin, [BDGY21](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2020.08.034).

To prove the estimate, lift the angles in a cluster as \(\theta_*+x_l/N\) with \(|x_l|\le D\), in any order.
For \(1\le l\le r\le b\), where \(r\) is its size, use the functions
\[
 f_l(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=D+1}
           \frac{e^{izt}}{\prod_{a=1}^l(z-x_a)}\,dz .
\]
At distinct nodes the residues give a triangular change from the exponentials \(e^{ix_l t}\) with nonzero diagonal.
Even when nodes coincide in the parameter limit, the derivatives \(f_l^{(a)}(0)\) vanish for \(0\le a<l-1\) and \(f_l^{(l-1)}(0)=i^{l-1}\), by integration of \((iz)^a/\prod(z-x_t)\) around the contour.
They are therefore independent as analytic functions for every parameter tuple.
The functions and their first two derivatives are bounded uniformly for \(0\le t\le1\) by the contour formula, with constants depending only on \(b,D\).

Take \(w(t)=t^3(1-t)^3\) on \([0,1]\), extended by zero outside.
The continuous weighted Gram of the functions \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\) on this interval is positive definite by independence and positivity of \(w\) in the interior.
Its entries vary continuously on the compact parameter cube, including coincident nodes.
Minimizing over that cube and the finitely many \(r\le b\) gives a uniform positive lower eigenvalue.
Uniform convergence of the Riemann sums, by the derivative bounds, gives a lower bound \(a=a(b,D)>0\) for each corresponding discrete diagonal block normalized by \(1/N\).

Use rows \(e^{ij\theta_*}f_l(j/N)\) from every cluster and call the resulting matrix \(B\).
For different centers at angular distance \(v\), an off-diagonal entry of \(N^{-1}B\operatorname{diag}(w(j/N))B^*\) is a normalized exponential sum sampled from a function \(w f_l\overline{f_{l'}}\) supported on \([0,1]\) with uniformly bounded second derivative after extension by zero.
The sum of the absolute second differences of its samples on the integer line is \(O_{b,D}(1/N)\), by two integrations over steps of length \(1/N\).
Multiplying the exponential sum by \((1-e^{iv})^2\) and shifting its finite sum twice therefore bounds the normalized entry by \(C_1/(Nv)^2\) for \(0<v\le\pi\).
On either side of a center the ordered center distances up to \(\pi\) are at least \(jG/N\) at position \(j\).
Each other center contributes at most \(b\) entries per row, so the off-block absolute row sums are at most \(2bC_1G^{-2}\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}\).
Choose \(G\) large enough to make this at most \(a/2\).
The off-block Hermitian matrix then has operator norm at most \(a/2\), and \(BB^*/N\succeq(a/2)I\), since \(0\le w\le1\).
Each column of \(B\) has squared norm \(O_{b,D}(u)\).
The diagonal entries of \(B^*(BB^*)^{-1}B\) have the asserted bound.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Selecting a common scale for most bounded components

Fix \(b\) and a positive integer \(J\).
Choose constants \(t_0=1,\ldots,t_J\) in increasing order with \(t_{j+1}\ge G(b,bt_j)\).
At scale \(j\) join any two angles at distance at most \(t_j/N\) and take the connected components.
A component of size greater than \(b\) at the last scale supplies each of its vertices with at least \(b+1\) vertices within distance \(bt_J/N\).
Indeed one can grow a connected set of \(b+1\) points starting from that vertex using at most \(b\) edges.
Discard all these last-scale components.
Their fraction is at most \(s^{-1}\#\{\theta:c_U(\theta)>b\}\) once \(U\ge bt_J\).

For each undiscarded vertex its nested components increase strictly at most \(b-1\) times.
At some common index \(0\le j<J\) no more than \(s(b-1)/J\) undiscarded vertices change component in the next step.
Retain the components of the remaining vertices at this index.
They are disjoint, have at most \(b\) points, lie within \(bt_j/N\) of any of their points, and are separated from every other retained component by more than \(t_{j+1}/N\).
The retained union is invariant under complex conjugation since each graph and the condition of an unchanged component are invariant under angle reversal.
If its size is \(u\), the previous estimate, maximized over these finitely many possible indices, gives a row projection with time-coordinate leverage at most \(C(b,J)s/N\), and
\[
          1-u/s\ \le\ \frac1s\#\{\theta:c_U(\theta)>b\}+(b-1)/J.       \tag{5}
\]
All constants in this selection are fixed before passing to the shrinking sequence.

<a id="bridge"></a>
## Compression of the actual conditional covariance

The complex Vandermonde rows of the retained conjugate pairs span the complexification of the space of their sine and cosine rows.
A common translation of all time exponents multiplies the complex rows by unit scalars.
Thus the estimate applies in the real row space of the [neutral carry embedding](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram), for its actual exponents \(j-N\).
Let \(A=W_N^{-1/2}V\) denote the isometry with orthonormal rows for all the phases, and let \(A_0\) have orthonormal real rows for the retained subspace.
Then \(A_0=S A\) with \(SS^{\mathsf T}=I_u\), by taking \(S=A_0A^{\mathsf T}\).
Its conditional covariance given the full past is \(S\Sigma_i S^{\mathsf T}\) at the very same cut, since the past contributes a known translation.

For fixed \(b,J\) every squared column norm of \(A_0\) is at most \(\delta=C(b,J)s/N\to0\).
When \(u>0\), the [pastwise entropy inequality for a compressed isometry](Yin_C_G0345_v713.md#entropy) at \(\varepsilon=\sqrt\delta\) gives
\[
 \left\langle\sum_{l=1}^u\phi\big(\lambda_l(S\Sigma_iS^{\mathsf T})+\varepsilon\big)
                         \right\rangle\le sh+o(s),                      \tag{6}
\]
with \(h\to1/2\) and \(\phi(x)=(2x-1-\log(2x))/2\) on \(0<x<1/2\), zero on \(x\ge1/2\).
Here the entropy cost is that of the original entire block and the error per retained row tends to zero.
Because \(\phi(\eta+\varepsilon)\to\infty\), the mean compressed near-zero count is \(o(s)\), uniformly in \(u\), also when \(u=0\).
Interlacing bounds \(q_\eta\) by \(1-u/s+o(1)\).
Apply (5) and then let \(J\to\infty\) at fixed \(b\) to obtain (1).

Under (3), choose successively fixed \(b,J\) and then sequence cutoffs so that (5) has right side tending to zero and \(\delta=C(b,J)s/N\to0\) along the resulting diagonal choice.
This is possible because \(U\to\infty\) and \(s/N\to0\) for each fixed choice of constants.
Thus \(u/s\to1\).
For the retained compression, (6) and convexity of \(y\mapsto\phi(e^y)\) give
\[
 \liminf\frac1u\left\langle\log\det(S\Sigma_iS^{\mathsf T}+\sqrt\delta I_u)\right\rangle
                      \ge\log x_{1/2}
\]
by the [determinant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v713.md#entropy).
Choose \(\varepsilon\ge\sqrt\delta\) tending to zero sufficiently slowly that \((1-u/s)|\log\varepsilon|\to0\).
Interlacing bounds the largest \(u\) eigenvalues of the full regularized covariance below by the corresponding compressed eigenvalues, and the others are at least \(\varepsilon\).
This proves (4).

<a id="moments"></a>
## A power-sum test and the effect of arbitrarily close pairs

The Fejér kernel \(F_H(x)=H^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}e^{ijx}|^2\) is nonnegative, with \(F_H(x)\ge c_0H\) for angular distance \(|x|\le1/H\) and an absolute \(c_0>0\), by the sine quotient.
Therefore
\[
 \frac{s^2+2\sum_{j=1}^{H-1}(1-j/H)|u_j|^2}{sH}
   =\frac1{sH}\sum_{\theta,\theta'\in\Theta} F_H(\theta-\theta')
   \ge \frac{c_0}{s}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}c_{N/H}(\theta).
\]
At \(U=N/H\to\infty\), (1) and \(\liminf q_\eta>0\) force the mean on the right to diverge, by taking arbitrarily large fixed \(b\).
Since \(s/H\) is bounded, this proves (2).
Conversely, a bounded normalized second moment bounds that mean, giving (3) by Markov's inequality.

For a deterministic comparison take \(s\) divisible by four and \(s/2\) equally spaced centers at angles \(2\pi(l+1/2)/(s/2)\).
Put two phases symmetrically about each center with total angular separation \(N^{-2}\), where \(N/s\to\infty\).
The resulting phases are closed under conjugation and the cluster estimate gives full-space maximum leverage \(O(s/N)\).
In contrast the difference of each pair of exponential rows has squared norm at most \(N^{-4}\sum_{j<N}j^2\le1/N\).
The compression of the ordinary \(s\)-by-\(s\) phase Gram to the \(s/2\) orthonormal pair differences therefore has trace at most \(s/(2N)\).
Every eigenvalue in that compression tends to zero, so at least \(s/2\) eigenvalues of the ordinary phase Gram tend to zero by interlacing.
This example concerns phase matrices alone.
It distinguishes the geometric restriction (1) from counting [small eigenvalues of the unconditioned phase Gram](Yin_C_G0345_v713.md#phase).
