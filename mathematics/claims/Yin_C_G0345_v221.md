# Exact exterior shares on short intervals of primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree and the three exceptional root counts

Fix an odd integer \(h\ge1\) prime to five.
As \(x\) tends to infinity there are \(\gg_h x/\log x\) disjoint pairs of primes \(x\le r<q\le2x\) with \(g=q-r\ll_h\log x\) and \(2h\mid g\) having the following properties.
Both endpoints split completely in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2h},2^{1/h},3^{1/h},5^{1/h})\).
Use either sign and set
\[
 Q=\frac{X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g)}{X+\varepsilon}.
\]
For any monic rational divisor \(S\) of \(Q\), let \(d=\deg S\) and let \(s\) count its exterior roots in the disks around the roots of \(X^g-2\).
The same \(s\) counts those in the disks for \(X^g-3\).
At \(\ell=2,3,5\) let \(\delta_\ell\) be the number of its exceptional roots modulo \(\ell\), counted with multiplicity outside the primitive grid of size \(q-1,q-1,r-1\), respectively.
Then
\[
 \delta_2=\delta_3,\qquad
 d=\frac{q-1}{g}s+\delta_2,\qquad
 \delta_5=\delta_2+s.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
More precisely, if \(J_2,J_3,J_5\) count the orbits of Frobenius and inversion on the three respective primitive grids, then the reduced denominator of \(s/g\) divides
\[
                           \gcd(g,J_2,J_3,J_5).                          \tag{2}
\]
Writing \(v=v_2(g)\), the shares satisfy
\[
                     s\equiv\delta_2\pmod {2^{v+2}},\qquad 2^v\mid s.    \tag{3}
\]
Exactly one irreducible constituent has \(v_2(s)=v\), and all other constituents have shares divisible by \(2^{v+1}\).
In particular the reduced denominator in (2) is odd and divides the odd part of \(\gcd(r-1,q-1)\).
If this greatest common divisor is a power of two, \(Q\) is irreducible for both signs.
The exact equalities hold for every divisor on the same pairs, without prescribing its degree or its local factors.

<a id="supply"></a>
## Many disjoint pairs with large orders at both ends

We use the [rank-one order bound for almost all primes](../audits/literature/Pap96/Yin_U001.md#rank-one-orders).
For each fixed \(a=2,3,5\) and each fixed \(K\), the primes \(p\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a)\le\sqrt p(\log p)^K\) have density zero among the primes.
Deleting the three exceptional sets in a dyadic interval removes \(o(x/\log x)\) primes.
For fixed \(h\), the [split prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#prime-pairs) leaves \(\gg_h x/\log x\) available there, also for \(h=1\) when the field is rational.
Take \(K=5\), and sort the remaining primes.
Pair the first with the second, the third with the fourth, and so on.
The sum of their gaps is at most \(x\), so at least half the pairs have gaps at most twice \(x\) divided by the number of pairs.
This gives the asserted count and \(g\ll_h\log x\).
Both endpoints are \(1\bmod 2h\), and \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) for all sufficiently large \(x\), for every fixed \(C\).
Neither the onset in \(x\) nor the constant in the gap estimate is uniform in \(h\).

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Rounding a multiple of the orbit length

First take \(S\) irreducible.
With \(W=2g+1\), the [degree estimate before rounding the exterior counts](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) at delay \(r\), multiplied by \(r\), gives
\[
 \left|d-\frac{rs}{g}\right|\ll rW e^{-c r/W^2}
             +W^{3/2}\sqrt{d\,\{W^2+\log(2d)\}}+W^4
            \ll_h \sqrt{x}(\log x)^{5/2}.
\]
Indeed \(d\le r+2g\ll x\), \(W=O_h(\log x)\), and the exponential term tends to zero.
For a given characteristic let \(N\) be the grid size and \(t\) the order of the subgroup generated by Frobenius and inversion on it.
The [two grids](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#three) give \(N=q-1\) at two and three and \(N=r-1\) at five.
There are \(J=N/t\) equal-sized orbits.
The selected roots on the grid are a union of these orbits, so \(t\) divides both \(N\) and \(d-\delta_\ell\).
Consequently
\[
 H_\ell=g(d-\delta_\ell)-Ns
\]
is an integer multiple of \(t\).
Here \(0\le\delta_\ell\le2g,\ 0\le s\le g\), and \(|N-r|\le g\), so
\[
 |H_\ell|\le g\,|d-rs/g|+3g^2\ll_h \sqrt{x}(\log x)^{7/2}.
\]
The orbit length \(t\) is at least the multiplicative order of the residue characteristic at the relevant endpoint.
The chosen orders exceed \(\sqrt{x}(\log x)^5\), hence \(|H_\ell|<t\) and \(H_\ell=0\) in all three cases.
Subtract the equations with \(N=q-1\) to get \(\delta_2=\delta_3\).
Subtract the one with \(N=r-1\) to get \(\delta_5-\delta_2=s\).
Also \((d-\delta_\ell)/t=J s/g\) is an integer, proving (2).
Degrees, shares and exceptional multiplicities add under products, so \(H_\ell=0\) and both assertions extend to every divisor, including one and the full polynomial.

<a id="odd"></a>
## The odd denominator of every exterior share

The [exceptional degree congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v220.md#statement) gives \(\delta_5/2\equiv\delta_2\equiv2^v\kappa\bmod 2^{v+1}\), with \(\kappa=v_2(S(-\varepsilon))\) equal to zero or one.
Substituting \(\delta_5=\delta_2+s\) gives (3) and shows \(s/2^v\equiv\kappa\bmod2\).
For \(S=Q\), \(\delta_2=g\), so \(\kappa=1\).
Evaluation at \(-\varepsilon\) multiplies over the integral irreducible factors, whose valuations are nonnegative integers.
Exactly one has \(\kappa=1\).
Any nonconstant divisor has \(s>0\), and its reduced denominator in \(s/g\) is odd by (3).
Equation (1) makes the denominator divide \(q-1\), as well as \(g\), so it divides the odd part of \(\gcd(g,q-1)=\gcd(r-1,q-1)\).
When this odd part is one, every nonconstant factor has \(s=g\), and the sum of their shares is \(g\).
This proves the asserted irreducibility criterion.
