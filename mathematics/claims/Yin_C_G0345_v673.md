# Optimizing the quotient window and the central cost of small tails

<a id="statement"></a>
## Estimates on the original polynomial

Let \(P\) be the monic irreducible polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\), let \(d=\deg P\), and put \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
For an integral Laurent numerator \(A\) indivisible by \(P\), write \(h_j\) for the circle Laurent coefficients of \(A/P\), \(E(A)=\sum_j|h_j|^2\), and \(T_W(A)=\sum_{j\notin[0,W-1]}|h_j|^2\) for an integer \(W\ge0\).
Write \(\mathcal E(P)=\inf_A E(A)\).
For a reciprocal \(P\) of degree \(d=2h\), let \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_h\) be its interior roots and set
\[
 \mathcal D=\prod_{i,j=1}^{h}|1-\alpha_i\overline{\alpha_j}|,\qquad
 \Delta=e^{-2m}\mathcal D^{-4/d},\qquad L_P=\log(m\Delta).
\]
Along every sequence of such reciprocal inputs with \(m\to0\), one has \(L_P\to+\infty\) and an integral numerator can be constructed with
\[
             \limsup\frac{24m E(A)}{d L_P}\le1.                       \tag{1}
\]
The full-dimensional Euclidean and torus ball-volume test, optimized over all windows, first passes at a squared ball radius asymptotic to \(d L_P/(24m)\).
Its energy certificate uses four times this squared radius.
Both estimates retain the actual stable-root product of the input.

Conversely, consider any sequence with \(m\to0\) on which positive powers of \(\alpha\) preserve degree, including [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
Reciprocity is unnecessary for this part.
Allow arbitrary indivisible integral Laurent numerators, put \(U=mT_W(A)\), \(C_{\rm mid}=m(E(A)-T_W(A))\), and assume
\[
             U\to0,\qquad B=\limsup mW/d<\infty .
\]
Then \(U^p C_{\rm mid}\to\infty\) for every fixed \(p>0\).
If in addition \(\ell=\log(1/U)=o(dm)\), then
\[
            \liminf\frac{\log C_{\rm mid}}{\ell^2}\ge\frac1{8B},       \tag{2}
\]
where the right side is \(+\infty\) if \(B=0\).
These assertions apply also after integer rounding in the window, since the correction preserves the residue class and both tails.

<a id="window"></a>
## The two-tail determinant for every window

Represent \(x\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) by its integral polynomial \(R\) of degree below \(d\) with coefficient vector \(a\).
Let \(H(W)\) be the real \(d\)-dimensional quadratic form \(a^{\mathsf T}H(W)a=T_W(R)\).
The two partial fractions for a proper quotient give the \(j=-1-k\) and \(j=W+k\) coefficients, for \(k\ge0\), as geometric sums with weights \(R(\rho)/P'(\rho)\) on the interior roots and \(-R(\rho)\rho^{-W-1}/P'(\rho)\) on the exterior roots.
The [full-degree kernel factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#roots) consequently uses the same square evaluation matrix and positive definite kernel matrices at every \(W\).
Changing \(W\) from \(d-1\) multiplies its determinant by \(e^{-2m(W-d+1)}\), since only the exterior diagonal weights change.
The [reciprocal determinant and product bound](Yin_C_G0345_v630.md#statement) give
\[
       (\det H(W))^{1/d}=\Delta e^{-2mW/d},\qquad
       \Delta\ge(2\sinh m)^{-2}.
\]
In particular \(m\Delta\to\infty\) as \(m\to0\).

A shortest nonzero vector \(H(W)^{1/2}a\) in the image of \(\mathbb Z^d\) has squared norm at most \(d\Delta e^{-2mW/d}\).
Indeed Minkowski's theorem applied to a dilated coordinate cube of side \(2(\det H(W))^{1/(2d)}\) gives this bound by a limit.
For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the [finite scalar multiplier estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v671.md#scalar) provides an input-independent integer \(Q_\epsilon\) and some \(1\le n\le Q_\epsilon\) for which rounding the \(W\) middle coefficients of \(nR/P\) costs at most \((1/12+\epsilon)W\).
For \(W=0\) take \(n=1\).
Subtracting \(P\) times the integral rounding polynomial gives an indivisible numerator, with
\[
        E(A)\le (1/12+\epsilon)W+
                         Q_\epsilon^2 d\Delta e^{-2mW/d}.              \tag{3}
\]
Take \(W=\lceil d L_P/(2m)\rceil\), so the second term is at most \(Q_\epsilon^2d/m\).
In the finite scalar estimate use \(N=\max(2,\lfloor L_P^{1/8}\rfloor)\) and \(s=\lceil\log_2 N\rceil\).
It gives \(\epsilon=O(1/N)\) and \(Q_\epsilon\le N^2\), hence \(Q_\epsilon^2=O(\sqrt{L_P})\).
Substitution in (3) proves (1).

<a id="volume"></a>
## The optimized mixed ball

Let \(D_W=\Delta^{d/2}e^{-mW}\) be the covolume of the tail lattice.
Attach to every lattice point the \(W\) middle coefficients of its proper quotient reduced modulo \(\mathbb Z^W\).
The resulting subgroup of \(\mathbb R^d\times(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^W\) has fundamental volume \(D_W\), by reducing the first coordinates in the lattice and using Haar probability on the torus.
Write \(V_d\) for Euclidean unit ball volume and let \(Y_j\) be independent uniform variables on \([-1/2,1/2]\).
With Euclidean distance on the first factor and squared circle distances added on the second, a ball of radius \(r\) has volume
\[
                  V_d\,\mathbb E (r^2-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^W Y_j^2)_+^{d/2}.
\]
If this exceeds \(D_W\), two distinct points in the ball project to the same class modulo the subgroup, as in the [finite packing test](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#packing).
Their difference has a nonzero integral residue and, after middle rounding, squared energy at most \(4r^2\).

Let \(s>0\) be determined by \(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-s y^2}\,dy=e^{-m}\).
The integral decreases continuously from one to zero and its derivative at zero is \(-1/12\), so \(s\sim12m\).
Bound the ball indicator by \(e^{s(r^2-\|x\|^2-\sum Y_j^2)}\).
Integrating over \(x\in\mathbb R^d\) and over the torus gives a volume bound \((\pi/s)^{d/2} e^{s r^2-mW}\) at every \(W\).
The test can therefore pass only if
\[
                 r^2>\frac{d}{2s}\log(s\Delta/\pi).
\]
This lower bound is asymptotic to \(d L_P/(24m)\).

For the reverse estimate take \(W=\lceil d L_P/(2m)\rceil\), which is positive for sufficiently small \(m\).
The sum of the \(Y_j^2\) has mean \(W/12\) and variance \(W/180\).
With probability greater than \(1/2\) the sum is at most \(W/12+\sqrt W\).
At \(r^2=W/12+\sqrt W+d/m\), the ball volume thus exceeds \(V_d(d/m)^{d/2}/2\).
The unit ball contains the cube of side \(2/\sqrt d\), so this last quantity is at least \(m^{-d/2}\) for \(d\ge1\).
The choice of \(W\) gives \(D_W\le m^{-d/2}\), and the test passes.
The extra \(\sqrt W+d/m\) is \(o(d L_P/m)\), proving the asserted asymptotic for the infimum of squared radii.
This volume calculation bounds the performance of the particular certificate even when an individual lattice vector is much shorter.

<a id="middle"></a>
## Growth of the middle energy when the degree-scale tails vanish

Write \(C=mE(A)=U+C_{\rm mid}\).
Both \(C\) and \(U\) are positive, since a quotient supported on a finite window would imply \(P\mid A\).
Use the finite [translated-section determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v669.md#volume) and its [projection estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v669.md#outside) at a positive integer \(k\le d\), with \(t_k=km,\ q=\lfloor d/k\rfloor,\ N=kq\).
The \(N\) translated sections have mean squared norm \(C\), geometric mean Gram eigenvalue at least \(t_ke^{-2t_k}\), and mean energy at most \(U\) on the complement of at most \(n\le W/k+q+1\) observed coordinates.
For \(\rho=n/N<1\), the finite projection inequality gives
\[
             t_k e^{-2t_k}\le 2 C^\rho U^{1-\rho}.                   \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the factor \(\rho^{-\rho}(1-\rho)^{-(1-\rho)}\) from the two traces is at most two, with endpoint values by continuity.
No uniform bound on \(C\) enters this finite inequality.

The [degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v669.md#translated) gives \(dm\to\infty\).
Whenever \(k=o(d)\), one has \(N/d\to1\), and the dimension bound implies
\[
       t_k\rho\le mW/N+m+t_k/N\le B+o(1).
\]
First fix \(p>0\) and choose \(t>(p+2)(B+1)\).
At \(k=\lfloor t/m\rfloor\) one has eventually \(\rho\le1/(p+2)\).
Since \(C/U\ge1\), (4) gives \(C\ge c_{p,B} U^{-(p+1)}\) eventually, for some positive constant.
Subtracting \(U\) proves the superpolynomial growth assertion.

For (2) take \(k=\lfloor\ell/(4m)\rfloor=o(d)\), so \(t_k\sim\ell/4\).
For any fixed \(B'>B\), eventually \(\rho\le B'/t_k<1\).
Taking logarithms of (4) yields
\[
 \log(C/U)\ge\frac{t_k}{B'}\big(\ell-2t_k+\log(t_k/2)\big).
\]
The term in parentheses is positive and asymptotic to \(\ell/2\).
Since \(C_{\rm mid}/C\to1\) by the first bound and \(\log U=-\ell\), division by \(\ell^2\) gives the lower bound \(1/(8B')\).
Letting \(B'\downarrow B\) proves (2).
The upper construction uses \(mW/d\sim L_P/2\to\infty\), whereas the middle-energy theorem concerns windows on the degree scale.
