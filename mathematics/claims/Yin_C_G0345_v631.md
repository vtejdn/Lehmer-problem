# Subquadratic circle moments of integral quotients and the location of their Taylor coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## Moment bounds and concentration in dyadic intervals

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and irreducible, with \(|P(0)|=1\), no root on the unit circle and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=m(P)>0\).
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) satisfy \(P\nmid A\), and use Haar probability measure \(\mu\) on the unit circle for all norms.
Multiply \(A/P\) by the unique monomial \(X^a\) that makes \(H=X^aA/P\) regular and nonzero at zero.
Write \(0=s_1<s_2<\cdots\) for the exponents of its nonzero Taylor coefficients.
For every fixed \(1<p\le2\),
\[
 \left\|\frac A P\right\|_p^p\ge K_p(m):=\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}} k^{p-1}e^{-pkm},
 \qquad
 K_p(m)\sim \left(\frac{p-1}{p\mathrm e m}\right)^{p-1}
 \quad(m\downarrow0).                                                   \tag{1}
\]

For \(k\ge1\), put \(D_k=\max_{\ell\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}}\#\{i\le k:\ell\le s_i\le2\ell\}\).
At the endpoint one has
\[
 (1+4D_k)\left\|\frac A P\right\|_1^2\ge k e^{-2km}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Thus if \(m\to0\) through such pairs and \(\|A/P\|_1\le C\) for a fixed \(C>0\), then for \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\) one has \(\liminf D_k/k\ge 1/(4\mathrm e C^2)\).
In particular \(D_k=o(k)\) along these prefixes forces \(\|A/P\|_1\to\infty\).

Bounded endpoint norm also controls how many different scales occur in a shorter prefix.
Set \(c=1/(64(1+C)^2)\).
If \(\|A/P\|_1\le C\) and \(0<m\le c\), the positive exponents among the first \(\lfloor c/m\rfloor\) occupied positions meet at most \(32C^2\) of the intervals \([2^j,2^{j+1})\), \(j=0,1,\ldots\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Testing the pole-cleared function on occupied coefficients

Take the finite Blaschke product \(I\) for the interior poles of \(H\), normalized by \(I(0)>0\), and let \(F=IH\).
The [quotient compression](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient) gives \(I(0)=e^{-m}\) and shows that \(F\) is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk.
The Taylor coefficients \(h_i=[X^{s_i}]H\) are nonzero integers and their list is infinite.
Writing \(f_n=[X^n]F\), the same compression gives, for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k |f_{s_i}|^2
       \ge e^{-2km}\sum_{i=1}^k|h_i|^2\ge k e^{-2km}.                 \tag{3}
\]
Unimodularity of the monomial and of \(I\) on the circle gives \(\|F\|_p=\|A/P\|_p\), including \(p=1\).

Let \(p'=p/(p-1)\) and \(T=\sum_{i=1}^k b_i X^{s_i}\).
Orthogonality and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(\|T\|_2=\|b\|_2\) and \(\|T\|_\infty\le\sqrt{k}\|b\|_2\).
For \(p'>2\), integrating \(|T|^{p'}\le\|T\|_\infty^{p'-2}|T|^2\) gives \(\|T\|_{p'}\le k^{1/2-1/p'}\|b\|_2\), which also holds at \(p'=2\) by orthogonality.
Take \(b_i=f_{s_i}\), so that \(\int F\overline T\,d\mu=\|b\|_2^2\).
Hölder's inequality and (3) give \(\|F\|_p\ge k^{1/p'}e^{-km}\), proving the bound in (1).
The maximum over positive real \(k\) occurs at \(k_*=(p-1)/(pm)\), and its value is \(((p-1)/(p\mathrm e m))^{p-1}\).
For fixed \(p>1\), rounding \(k_*\) down has relative error tending to zero in this value as \(m\downarrow0\).

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Splitting an analytic product at half the exponent

For a finite set \(S\) of positive integers let \(D(S)=\max_{\ell\ge1,\,\ell\in\mathbb Z}\#(S\cap[\ell,2\ell])\), with value zero for the empty set.
We show that
\[
                         \sum_{n\in S}|f_n|^2\le4 D(S)\|F\|_1^2.       \tag{4}
\]
Remove from \(F\) the finite Blaschke product \(J\) of its open-disk zeros, counting multiplicities.
The quotient \(Q=F/J\) extends analytically to a neighborhood of the closed disk and has no open-disk zeros.
It has an analytic square root \(v\) in the open disk, where \(|v|^2=|Q|\).
Put \(g=Jv\), so \(F=gv\).
Both factors have square-summable Taylor coefficients and
\[
                 \sum_{a\ge0}|g_a|^2=\sum_{b\ge0}|v_b|^2=\|F\|_1 .
\]
Indeed, apply Parseval on circles of radius \(r<1\) and let \(r\uparrow1\).
The radial squared moduli are \(|J|^2|Q|\) and \(|Q|\), which extend continuously to the closed disk and agree with \(|F|\) on the unit circle.
This proof includes zeros of \(Q\) on the circle.

Write \(f_n=U_n+V_n\) by splitting \(\sum_{a+b=n}g_a v_b\) into \(a\ge b\) and \(a<b\), respectively.
Cauchy–Schwarz on each sum gives
\[
 |U_n|^2\le\left(\sum_{\lceil n/2\rceil\le a\le n}|g_a|^2\right)\sum_{b\ge0}|v_b|^2,
 \qquad
 |V_n|^2\le\left(\sum_{\lceil n/2\rceil\le b\le n}|v_b|^2\right)\sum_{a\ge0}|g_a|^2.
\]
A fixed positive index \(\ell\) belongs to the interval in these sums exactly for \(n\in[\ell,2\ell]\).
Index zero never occurs in that interval for \(n>0\).
Summing over \(S\) and using \(|f_n|^2\le2|U_n|^2+2|V_n|^2\) proves (4).
The constant coefficient satisfies \(|f_0|\le\|F\|_1\).
Apply (4) to the positive occupied positions through \(s_k\), add the constant coefficient and use (3) to obtain (2).
For \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\) one has \(k\to\infty\) and \(e^{-2km}\to e^{-1}\), which gives the two limiting assertions after (2).

<a id="scales"></a>
## Few occupied scales at bounded endpoint norm

Assume \(\|F\|_1\le C\), so \(|f_n|\le C\) for every \(n\).
By (3), \(\sum_{i=1}^k|h_i|^2\le e^{2km}kC^2\).
Parseval for \(I\) gives \(\sum_{n>0}|[X^n]I|^2=1-e^{-2m}\le2m\).
At each \(s_i\) with \(i\le k\), the convolution for \(F\) involves \(h_i\) and the earlier occupied coefficients only.
The positive differences \(s_i-s_j\), \(j<i\), are distinct.
Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
\[
              |f_{s_i}-e^{-m}h_i|\le C\sqrt{2mk}\,e^{km}.
\]
For \(k=\lfloor c/m\rfloor\) and \(m\le c\) the right side is at most \(e^{1/64}\sqrt{2}/8<1/4\), while \(e^{-m}\ge e^{-1/64}>3/4\).
As \(|h_i|\ge1\), this gives \(|f_{s_i}|\ge1/2\) throughout the prefix.

Choose one positive exponent from each half-open dyadic interval met by the prefix, and call their set \(S\).
If \(2^j\le\ell<2^{j+1}\), then \([\ell,2\ell]\subset[2^j,2^{j+2})\), so \(D(S)\le2\).
Applying (4) gives \(\#S/4\le8C^2\), as asserted.

<a id="squares"></a>
## Reciprocal square numerators of arbitrary width

Suppose \(P\) is reciprocal of degree \(2h\), and let \(u=\pm X^{-h}P\) be positive on the unit circle.
For any integral Laurent polynomial \(B\) with \(P\nmid B\), let \(W=B(X)B(X^{-1})/u\), so \(W=|B|^2/|P|\) there.
Reciprocity of \(P\) gives \(P\nmid B(X^{-1})\) and hence \(P\nmid B(X)B(X^{-1})\) in the Laurent ring.
Consequently (1) applies to \(W\) in absolute value, and (2) and the dyadic interval count apply to the Taylor support of its monomial normalization.
In particular, the averaged bound \(\int |B|^2/|P|\,d\mu\le C\) imposes both restrictions on that support, with no width hypothesis on \(B\).
For \(1<p<2\) and \(t>0\) it also gives
\[
                  \int_{\{W>t\}} W^p\,d\mu\ge K_p(m)-Ct^{p-1},
\]
by bounding \(W^p\le t^{p-1}W\) on the complementary set.
Thus for \(t=\delta/m\) with \(C\delta^{p-1}<((p-1)/(p\mathrm e))^{p-1}\), the scaled tail moment \(m^{p-1}\int_{\{W>\delta/m\}}W^p\,d\mu\) has a positive lower limit along a shrinking sequence satisfying the averaged bound.

The [exact numerator condition](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#statement) \(|B|^2\le4u\) implies the averaged bound with \(C=4\) and bounds every circle moment of \(W\).
The [quadratic transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v617.md#quadratic) already gives an explicit positive measure gap for this pointwise condition.
For width less than \(h\), the [short-square estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v630.md#energy) bounds the integral itself below by \(|b|^2/(2\sinh m)\), where \(b\) is the first nonzero coefficient of \(B\).
For unrestricted widths, (2) and (4) give necessary support conditions for a bounded integral and leave open whether the integral must diverge as \(m\to0\).
