# A nearly square-root degree bound for residues at roots approaching the unit circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## Growth of the normalized residues

Let \(P\) be a monic complex polynomial of degree \(d\) with simple roots, \(|P(0)|=1\), and no zero on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\sum_{|\rho|>1}\log|\rho|\) and assume \(0<m\le1\).
For \(r_\rho=|\rho|\) put
\[
 T=\sqrt m\sum_\rho \frac1{|P'(\rho)|\sqrt{|1-r_\rho^2|}},\qquad R=1+T .
\]
There is an absolute \(C\ge1\) such that
\[
 d\le C R^2(1+\log R)\exp\!\left(C\sqrt{m\log(2+R)}\right).          \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for an absolute \(c>0\),
\[
 T\ \ge\ c\sqrt{\frac d{\log(2d)}}\exp\!\left(-C\sqrt{m\log(2d)}\right). \tag{2}
\]
This implies \(\liminf \log T/\log d\ge1/2\) as \(d\to\infty\), uniformly for \(0<m\le1\).
The [integral numerator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#statement) explains why an upper estimate on these residues is relevant to small Mahler measure in the hyperbolic unit problem.
The proof below uses complex interpolation, so (1) also applies when the coefficients are not integers.
The exponent \(1/2\) is attained at fixed measure even by irreducible reciprocal integer polynomials, as shown [below](#sharpness).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Which roots need to be controlled

Let \(y_\rho=|\log r_\rho|\), \(x_\rho=y_\rho/m\) and \(c_\rho=1/(\rho P'(\rho))\).
The product of the radii is one, giving \(0<x_\rho\le1\) and \(\sum_\rho x_\rho=2\).
For every polynomial \(F\) of degree less than \(d\), Lagrange interpolation at zero gives
\[
                   \sum_\rho c_\rho F(\rho)=-F(0)/P(0).              \tag{3}
\]
As \(y_\rho\le1\), one has \(|1-r_\rho^{-2}|\le 2e^2y_\rho\).
Thus, with \(C_0=\sqrt2 e\), the total \(\sum|c_\rho|\) is at most \(C_0T\), and
\[
 \sum_{x_\rho\le\eta}|c_\rho|\le C_0 T\sqrt\eta\qquad(0<\eta<1).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed each \(|c_\rho|\) is bounded by its summand in \(T\) times \(C_0\sqrt{x_\rho}\).
Taking \(F=1\) in (3) also shows \(T\ge1/C_0\).

<a id="groups"></a>
## Reflection on circles of different radii

Fix \(\lambda\ge2\), and set
\[
 B=\exp(4+(4\lambda+6)m),\qquad
 \varepsilon=(8e^2 C_0BR)^{-1},\qquad \eta=\varepsilon^2.
\]
Only roots with \(x_\rho>\eta\) are selected for interpolation.
Put \(t_j=2^j\eta\) and let \(J\) be the least nonnegative integer with \(t_J\ge m\).
Put the selected roots with \(y_\rho\le t_0\) in \(H_0\), and those with \(t_{j-1}<y_\rho\le t_j\) in \(H_j\) for \(1\le j\le J\).
Writing \(h_j=|H_j|\), we have \(t_0h_0\le2\) by \(\sum x_\rho=2\), and \(\sum_{j\ge1}t_j h_j\le4m\) by \(\sum y_\rho=2m\).

Use \(\lambda_0=1\), \(\lambda_j=\lambda\) for \(j\ge1\), and \(\sigma_j=\exp((1+\lambda_j)t_j)\).
The rational function
\[
 B_j(z)=\prod_{\rho\in H_j}
       \frac{1-z/\rho}{1-z/\beta_\rho},\qquad
 \beta_\rho=\sigma_j^2/\bar\rho
\]
is analytic on the closed disk of radius \(\sigma_j\), is one at zero and vanishes at every root in \(H_j\).
The boundary modulus of each quotient is \(\sigma_j/r_\rho\) by reflection in that circle.
Consequently \(\sup_{|z|\le\sigma_j}|B_j(z)|\le M_j=\exp((1+\lambda_j)t_jh_j+\sum_{\rho\in H_j}y_\rho)\), and \(\prod_{j=0}^J M_j\le B\).
Empty groups give the constant one.
The use of several radii permits the contour for a more distant group to grow with its members' distances from the circle.

<a id="truncation"></a>
## Controlling earlier polynomial factors at later roots

Let \(D_{-1}=0\).
For each \(j\) in increasing order set
\[
 n_j=\left\lceil\frac{D_{j-1}}{\lambda_j}+
 \frac{\log((J+1)/[\varepsilon(1-e^{-\lambda_jt_j})])}{\lambda_jt_j}
              \right\rceil,\qquad D_j=D_{j-1}+n_j .
\]
Let \(G_j\) be the Taylor polynomial of \(B_j\) through degree \(n_j\).
By the Cauchy coefficient bound and the geometric tail, on \(|z|\le e^{t_j}\) one has
\[
   |G_j-B_j|\le M_j\frac{\varepsilon}{J+1}e^{-t_j D_{j-1}},
   \qquad |G_j|\le M_j(1+\varepsilon/(J+1)).                         \tag{5}
\]
Define \(F=\prod_{j=0}^J G_j\), so \(F(0)=1\) and \(\deg F\le D_J\).
At a root with \(x_\rho\le\eta\) we have \(r_\rho\le e^{m\eta}\le e^{t_0}\), hence \(|F(\rho)|\le B e^\varepsilon\).
At a selected root \(\rho\in H_j\), the preceding partial product has circle supremum at most \(\prod_{i<j}\big[M_i(1+\varepsilon/(J+1))\big]\), with a factor \(1+\varepsilon/(J+1)\) for each index in the product.
Evaluating it at \(\rho\) enlarges this bound by at most \(e^{t_jD_{j-1}}\).
Indeed a polynomial of degree at most \(D\) and unit-circle supremum \(V\) has \(|p(z)|\le V\max(1,|z|^D)\), by applying the maximum principle inside and to \(z^Dp(1/z)\).
The \(j\)-th factor at this root is its error in (5), since \(B_j(\rho)=0\).
Each later factor is evaluated inside its own disk of radius \(e^{t_i}\).
Multiplication yields \(|F(\rho)|\le Be^\varepsilon\varepsilon/(J+1)\).
By (4), \(\sum_\rho |c_\rho F(\rho)|\le Be^\varepsilon C_0 T(\sqrt\eta+\varepsilon)<1\).
Equation (3) therefore forces \(d\le D_J\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree estimate

Write \(L=\log(1/\eta)\) and \(A=\log((J+1)/[\varepsilon(1-e^{-\eta})])\).
Since \(m\le1\), \(J\le1+L/\log2\), and \(1-e^{-\eta}\ge\eta/2\).
It follows that \(A\le C(1+L)\) with an absolute constant, which may change at each occurrence.
The degree recurrence gives \(D_0\le 1+A/\eta\) and, for \(j\ge1\),
\[
 D_j\le(1+1/\lambda)D_{j-1}+1+A/(\lambda t_j).
\]
Using \(\sum_{j\ge1}1/t_j\le1/\eta\), we obtain
\[
 d\le C(1+L)e^L(1+1/\lambda)^J,\qquad
 \log d\le L+\log(1+L)+C+(1+L/\log2)/\lambda .
\]
Here \(L=2\log R+O(1+\lambda m)\) with absolute implied constant.
Choose \(\lambda=\max\{2,\sqrt{\log(2+R)/m}\}\).
Then \(\lambda m+(\log R)/\lambda\le C(1+\sqrt{m\log(2+R)})\), and \(\log(1+L)\le\log(1+\log R)+C\).
This proves (1).
If \(R\le\sqrt{2d}\), rearranging (1) gives (2), since \(R\le(1+C_0)T\).
For \(R>\sqrt{2d}\) the same conclusion follows directly from this last comparison.

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## A reciprocal family at fixed positive measure

Let \(Q(X)=X^2-3X+1\), with larger root \(\alpha=(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
For \(q=3^k\), \(Q(X^q)\) is irreducible.
Indeed \(\alpha\) is not a cube in \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
A cube root in \(K\) would be an algebraic unit of norm one, since its cube has norm one.
Its trace would be \(\alpha^{1/3}+\alpha^{-1/3}\), strictly between two and three, an impossibility for an algebraic integer.
If \(X^{3^k}-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\) and \(\gamma=\alpha^{1/3^k}\), its relative norm is \(\alpha\) because its degree is odd.
A cube root of \(\gamma\) in \(K(\gamma)\) would therefore make \(\alpha\) a cube in \(K\).
The cubic \(X^3-\gamma\) is irreducible and induction gives \([K(\alpha^{1/q}):K]=q\).
As \(\alpha=(\alpha^{1/q})^q\), the claimed irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) follows.

The degree is \(2q\) and \(m(Q(X^q))=\log\alpha<1\).
Each root over a fixed root \(\zeta\) of \(Q\) has radius \(|\zeta|^{1/q}\) and derivative modulus \(q|\zeta|^{1-1/q}|Q'(\zeta)|\).
Thus this group of \(q\) roots contributes
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\log\alpha}}{|\zeta|^{1-1/q}|Q'(\zeta)|\sqrt{|1-|\zeta|^{2/q}|}}
\]
to \(T\), whose ratio to \(\sqrt q\) tends to a finite positive constant.
The exponent in (2) therefore admits no uniform increase on this class.
The example uses power substitutions and its measure stays positive.
