# A bounded arithmetic progression of exponents and one further term

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small values on reciprocal conjugates

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic integer of degree \(d\), height \(h\), and put \(e_*=\min_{q\ge1}\deg(\alpha^q)\).
Fix an integer \(T\ge1\).
Let the support of a complex Laurent polynomial lie in a translate of \(\{0,a,\ldots,Ta\}\cup\{b\}\), where \(a>0\) and \(b\) is an arbitrary integer.
Remove its lowest monomial power and normalize its coefficients to Euclidean norm one.
Let \(r\ge2\) be the actual number of terms, \(W\) their actual span, and \(\delta_t\) the fraction of original embeddings with \(|Q(\sigma\alpha)|\le t\).
Set \(L=\min(1,\max\{T/e_*,\sqrt{2T/e_*}\})\).
Then
\[
 \delta_0\le L,\qquad
 \delta_t\le 2L+
 \left(\frac{r\,2^{r-1}(\log\sqrt r+Wh)}
                  {\log(1/(rt))}\right)^{1/r}
 \qquad(0<t<1/r).                                                   \tag{1}
\]
A single normalized monomial has value one.
As the positive total logarithmic measure tends to zero, \(e_*\to\infty\) and the input eventually satisfies reciprocity by the [powered degree and nonreciprocal gap argument](Yin_C_G0345_v739.md#volume) and [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
Hence for fixed \(T\) the small-value fraction tends to zero at \(Wh=O(1)\) and \(t\to0\), and more generally when \(1+Wh=o(\log(1/t))\).
For exact zeros the bound is independent of the exponent span.
The estimate applies equally to subsets of the progression, negative outliers and complex coefficients varying with the orbit.

<a id="occupancy"></a>
## Intersections of a graph with its inverse

Use the original projective monomial rows on the true support, retaining each original embedding with equal weight.
After a common translation of the exponents, a hyperplane on these rows has equation
\[
                        A(z^a)+c z^b=0,\qquad \deg A\le T .
\]
If the outlier already lies in the progression, absorb it into \(A\).
For \(c=0\), a nonzero \(A\) fixes at most \(T\) distinct values of \(z^a\), and each has mass at most \(1/e_*\).
If \(c\ne0\) and \(A\) is one monomial, the equation fixes at most one value of a nonzero power of \(z\), of mass at most \(1/e_*\).
A single nonzero term has no zero on the torus.

It remains to consider \(c\ne0\), \(b\notin\{0,a,\ldots,Ta\}\) and \(A\) with at least two nonzero coefficients.
Use the distinct points \((x,y)=(z^a,z^b)\).
Their transitive Galois orbit has size \(D=\deg(\alpha^{\gcd(a,|b|)})\ge e_*\), each with multiplicity \(d/D\).
Coordinatewise inversion preserves this orbit.
Write \(M\) for the number lying on the graph \(y=P(x)=-A(x)/c\).
For an arbitrary complex \(P\), the linear equations of interpolation through these \(M\) points are defined over the splitting field of \(\alpha\).
Impose also the prescribed zero coefficients.
The resulting nonempty affine solution space has a point over that field retaining every prescribed nonzero coefficient, since the field is infinite and the finitely many coordinate hyperplanes being avoided do not contain the solution space.
Use this algebraic graph for the bound, writing \(M'\ge M\) for its actual number of orbit points.

Its inverse graph has equation \(yP(1/x)=1\) and contains exactly \(M'\) orbit points.
The image graph under a field automorphism has equation \(y=P^\sigma(x)\).
An intersection satisfies \(P^\sigma(x)P(1/x)=1\).
This is not an identity since the units in the Laurent polynomial ring over a field are monomials and \(P\) has at least two terms.
Multiplication by \(x^T\) gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(2T\), and \(y\) is fixed by \(x\) on the image graph.
The expected number of common orbit points, for a uniform automorphism, is \((M')^2/D\) by transitivity.
Thus \(M/D\le\sqrt{2T/D}\).
Together the cases prove that every proper projective hyperplane on the original rows has mass at most \(L\).

<a id="volume"></a>
## Determinants on the true support

Translate the true exponents once more to have minimum zero and maximum \(W\).
This multiplies each row by a nonzero scalar and preserves all hyperplane incidences.
Write \(v_i\) for these integral monomial rows and \(u_i=v_i/\|v_i\|_2\).
The product of the determinants of all ordered \(r\)-tuples with nonzero determinant is a nonzero rational integer, including the empty product.
Indeed the raw entries are algebraic integers and Galois automorphisms permute the ordered tuples.
Every row norm is at least one and \(\sum_i\log\|v_i\|_2\le d(\log\sqrt r+Wh)\).
By Hadamard's inequality the normalized determinant costs \(-\log|\det|\) are nonnegative.
Their sum over nonzero tuples is at most \(r d^r(\log\sqrt r+Wh)\) by counting the \(r\) possible positions of each row in the product.

Suppose \(\delta_t>2L\).
Sequential independent uniform sampling of \(r\) rows gives probability at least \(\delta_t(\delta_t-L)^{r-1}\ge\delta_t^r/2^{r-1}\) of an independent tuple of small-value rows.
At each step after the first, the span of the earlier independent rows is contained in a hyperplane.
Rotate the coefficient functional to the last coordinate by a unitary change of basis.
Its value has modulus at most \(t\) on every normalized small-value row.
Expansion in that column bounds the modulus of the determinant by \(rt\), since every complementary minor has row norms at most one.
Comparing this cost with the preceding sum proves (1).
The argument for \(t=0\) only uses hyperplane occupancy.
