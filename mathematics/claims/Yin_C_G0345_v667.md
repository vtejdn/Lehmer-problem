# Gram determinants of integral sections and energy outside moving intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## Energy outside intervals on the inverse-square scale

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\), so every conjugate lies off the unit circle.
Write \(m=\log M(P)>0\) and \(A/P=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}h_j X^j\) on the circle, where \(A\) is an integral Laurent polynomial indivisible by \(P\).
Consider a sequence with \(m\to0\), bounded scaled energy \(m\sum_j|h_j|^2\le C\), and \(\deg(\alpha^k)=d\) for every positive integer \(k\).
The power hypothesis holds at [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
Let \(I\) be a union of \(s\) intervals of consecutive integers of total cardinality \(W\), and set \(T_I=m\sum_{j\notin I}|h_j|^2\).
Their locations are arbitrary.

If \(W=O(m^{-2})\) and \(s=O(m^{-1})\), then \(\liminf T_I>0\), with a bound depending only on \(C\) and the two implied constants.
If \(W=o(m^{-2})\) and \(s=o(m^{-1})\), then
\[
 \liminf T_I\ \ge\ \frac1{2\mathrm e},\qquad
 \liminf T_I\ \ge\ \frac14\quad\text{when }\operatorname{width}(A)<d.       \tag{1}
\]
The unrestricted bounds apply to the nearest-integer distances of the coefficients with the boundedness assumption imposed on their own scaled energy.
In particular, they extend the [interval-length range](Yin_C_G0345_v665.md#statement) from \(o(\log(1/m)/m)\) to the inverse-square scale.
The proof gives finite estimates for the distance of the progression vectors from any common subspace and applies when only the chosen power preserves degree.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## A determinant bound from the first independent coefficient columns

Let \(Q\) be a monic integer polynomial of degree \(D\) with \(|Q(0)|=1\), no root on the unit circle and \(u=\log M(Q)>0\).
Take \(q\) integral Laurent polynomials \(B_1,\ldots,B_q\) linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(G_0\) be the Gram matrix of \(B_i/Q\) in circle \(L^2\) for Haar probability.
Then
\[
 (\det G_0)^{1/q}\ge e^{-2u},\qquad
 (\det G_0)^{1/q}\ge\frac1{2\sinh(2u)}
 \quad\text{if all }B_i\text{ lie in a common interval of }D\text{ exponents}.       \tag{2}
\]

A common monomial shift makes every row an ordinary polynomial without changing its Gram matrix.
Read their integer coefficient columns from the lowest exponent upward, selecting each column that increases the rank.
The selected \(q\) columns form a matrix \(M\) with nonzero integer determinant.
Multiplying the column of row polynomials on the left by \(M^{-1}\) gives polynomials \(U_i\) with first nonzero coefficient one at the successive selected exponents \(j_i\).
Indeed every column before \(j_i\) is in the span of the first \(i-1\) selected columns, and these become coordinate vectors.

Every polynomial \(F=U_i+\sum_{l>i}c_l U_l\) has first coefficient one at \(j_i\).
Reflect each interior factor \(X-\beta\) of \(Q\) to \(1-\bar\beta X\), leaving the exterior factors in place.
The resulting polynomial \(H\) has no zeros on the closed disk, \(|H|=|Q|\) on its boundary and \(|H(0)|=e^u\).
Thus \(X^{-j_i}F/H\) is analytic on the closed disk and its constant coefficient has magnitude \(e^{-u}\).
Parseval yields \(\|F/Q\|_2^2\ge e^{-2u}\).
In the common interval case \(F\) also has width less than \(D\), so the [short complex numerator estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) gives the second lower bound in (2) for \(\|F/Q\|_2^2\).
Orthogonalizing the normalized rows \(U_i/Q\) in descending order of \(j_i\) writes their Gram determinant as the product of the squared distances to the spans of the later rows.
Each factor satisfies the indicated bound for \(F\).
Returning to the original rows multiplies the determinant by \(|\det M|^2\ge1\), proving (2).

<a id="projection"></a>
## Progression vectors and the trace on a projected kernel

Fix an integer \(1\le k\le d\) with \(\deg(\alpha^k)=d\), put \(t=km\) and let \(P_k\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^k\).
Its log measure is \(t\).
For \(r=0,\ldots,k-1\), the [integral progression sections](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#sections) give \(B_r/P_k=\sum_j h_{kj+r}X^j\).
At \(\beta=\alpha^k\) they satisfy \(B_r(\beta)=b\alpha^{-r}\) with a common \(b\ne0\), by [Fourier inversion at this fiber](Yin_C_G0345_v665.md#separation).
A rational relation among the rows would give a polynomial of degree at most \(k-1<d\) vanishing at \(\alpha\), after multiplication by \(\alpha^{k-1}/b\).
The rows are therefore independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
For the short case first shift \(A\) to exponents in \([0,d-1]\) and shift \(I\) by the same amount.
Then the exponent collection defining the \(B_r\) puts them all in \([0,d-1]\), since \(\deg(A P_k(X^k)/P)<kd\).

Map the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^k\) to the vectors \(v_r=(\sqrt t\,h_{kj+r})_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) by a linear map \(V\).
Parseval and (2) show that \(G=V^*V\) satisfies
\[
 (\det G)^{1/k}\ge D_t,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(G)/k=m\sum_j |h_j|^2\le C,
 \qquad
 D_t=t e^{-2t}\quad\text{or, in the short case,}\quad
 D_t=\frac{t}{2\sinh(2t)}.                                             \tag{3}
\]
Let \(\Pi\) be an orthogonal projection of rank \(n<k\) in the real sequence space and put \(\rho=n/k\).
Choose \(k-n\) orthonormal vectors in \(\ker(\Pi V)\), and complete them to an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb R^k\).
In this basis the last \(n\) diagonal entries of \(G\) have sum at most \(kC\).
The sum of the first \(k-n\) entries is at most \(\|(1-\Pi)V\|_{\rm HS}^2\), because \(V=(1-\Pi)V\) on the chosen kernel vectors.
Hadamard's inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality within each block now give
\[
 \frac1k\sum_r\|(1-\Pi)v_r\|^2
 \ \ge\ (1-\rho)D_t
             \left(\frac{\rho D_t}{C}\right)^{\rho/(1-\rho)}.           \tag{4}
\]
At \(\rho=0\) the right side is \(D_t\), by continuity or the trace-determinant inequality.

Project onto the coordinates in \(J=\{\lfloor j/k\rfloor:j\in I\}\).
Every omitted coordinate of a row then corresponds to a coefficient outside \(I\), so \(T_I\) bounds the left side of (4) from above.
An interval of cardinality \(w\) meets at most \(w/k+2\) quotient indices, giving \(n\le W/k+2s\).

<a id="scales"></a>
## Choosing a power and applying the bound to centered witnesses

The [Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\).
Here \(d\to\infty\), since the coefficient bounds from \(M(P)\) leave only finitely many polynomials at bounded degree.
Consequently \(d\) eventually exceeds every fixed power of \(1/m\).
If \(W\le A_0/m^2\) and \(s\le B_0/m\), choose \(u>0\) with \(A_0/u^2+2B_0/u<1\) and take \(k=\lfloor u/m\rfloor\).
Then \(t\to u\) and \(\limsup \rho<1\), so (4) gives a uniform positive lower bound.
Under the two little-oh hypotheses in (1), taking \(u=1/2\) makes \(\rho\to0\) and gives \(1/(2\mathrm e)\).
For the short bound take instead \(u=\max\{(\log(1/m))^{-1/2},(m^2W)^{1/4},(ms)^{1/2}\}\).
Now \(t\sim u\to0\), \(W/k^2+2s/k\to0\) and the short value of \(D_t\) tends to \(1/4\).

For larger windows, suppose \(m\sqrt W\to\infty\), \(s=o(\sqrt W)\) and \(\lceil\sqrt{3W}\rceil\le d\).
Taking \(k=\lceil\sqrt{3W}\rceil\) gives \(\limsup \rho\le1/3\) and \(t\sim\sqrt3\,m\sqrt W\).
Since \(\log D_t=-2t+\log t\) for the unrestricted estimate, (4) yields
\[
                \liminf \frac{\log T_I}{m\sqrt W}\ \ge\ -3\sqrt3 .
\]
The degree condition here holds in particular when \(W\) is polynomially bounded in \(1/m\).

For centering, subtract the nearest integers from the coefficients of \(A/P\).
Only finitely many corrections are nonzero since the coefficients tend to zero in both directions, and this changes \(A\) by an integral Laurent multiple of \(P\).
Every nonzero square-summable centered Fourier witness for the hyperbolic recurrence has such an indivisible integral numerator, up to reversal of the coefficient indexing. [Recurrence representation](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#recurrence)
For such a witness the product of \(|\cos(\pi h_j)|\) over \(j\notin I\) is at most \(\exp(-\pi^2T_I/(2m))\) by the cosine bound there.
Thus a witness with bounded scaled energy has an exponentially small outside product even after deletion of any interval of length \(O(m^{-2})\).
The existence of inputs with \(m\to0\) remains open.
