# Adding the depths of nested power collisions in a two-row resultant

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sum over a tree of disks

Let \(p\ne\ell\) be primes and let \(v(\ell)=1\) in a finite extension \(K/\mathbb Q_\ell\) with valuation ring \(\mathcal O\) and residue field \(k\).
Suppose
\[
 F=x^{r_0}U(x^p)+x^{r_1}V(x^p),\qquad r_0\not\equiv r_1\bmod p ,
\]
where \(U,V\) are nonzero integral Laurent polynomials coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Write \(U=X^u U_0,\ V=X^w V_0\) with nonzero constant terms in the polynomial rows \(U_0,V_0\), and set \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|\).
Let \(\alpha_i\in\mathcal O^\times\) be distinct roots of \(F\) of multiplicity at least \(e\ge1\), and write \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\).
These \(\beta_i\) are distinct since at a nonzero root both rows are nonzero and their ratio determines \(x^{r_1-r_0}\) in the power fiber.

Choose a finite tree of closed disks \(D=\{X:v(X-b_D)\ge s_D\}\) with centers \(b_D\in\mathcal O^\times\) and positive depths \(s_D=v(\tau_D)>0\) in the value group.
Use the depth-zero unit ball as a formal root, require each child disk to be contained in its parent with strictly greater depth, and require siblings to be disjoint.
Take the disks in an algebraic closure when counting their roots.
Write \(\delta_D=s_D-s_{\mathrm{parent}(D)}\), and call \(D\) mixed if it contains at least two \(\beta_i\) whose \(\alpha_i\) have distinct residues.
Let \(m_D\) be the number of specified \(\beta_i\) in \(D\).

In each positive-depth disk substitute \(X=b_D+\tau_DY\) in \(U_0,V_0\), divide both by a common scalar of the minimum coefficient valuation across the two polynomials, and reduce to \(A,B\in k[Y]\).
At least one is nonzero.
Set \(n_D=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\), with degree \(-\infty\) for zero.
Then
\[
              v_\ell(H)\ \ge\ \sum_D\delta_D n_D^2
                         \ \ge\ e^2\sum_{D\ {\rm mixed}}\delta_D .       \tag{1}
\]
The second bound also applies when the two points witnessing mixing have the same residue in the scaled coordinate \(Y\).
If \(A/B\), after removing common factors, is nonconstant and separable over \(k\), then
\[
                      2n_D-2\ \ge\ (e-1)m_D.                            \tag{2}
\]
Thus a disk with this property counts every prescribed root it contains even when several roots have the same scaled residue or the same original residue.
For a nonconstant inseparable reduction one always has \(n_D\ge\ell\), which gives the same numerical lower bound on \(n_D\) whenever \(\ell\ge\lceil((e-1)m_D+2)/2\rceil\).

<a id="pencil"></a>
## Choosing two rows that count the roots in every selected disk

Remove any common \(\ell\)-content first.
This preserves all \(n_D\) and root multiplicities, and restoring it increases the resultant valuation by its valuation times \(\deg U_0+\deg V_0\).
Choose units \(c,c'\) with distinct residues in a sufficiently large finite unramified extension.
We can require both \(G=U_0+cV_0\) and \(G'=U_0+c'V_0\) to be primitive at \(\ell\).
Indeed at most one coefficient choice in the residue field makes the entire reduction of a combination zero.
In each selected disk, exclude also the at most one residue cancelling the coefficient of \(Y^{n_D}\) in \(A+cB\).
There are only finitely many disks, so one pair \(c,c'\) meets every requirement.

For either chosen row the scaled polynomial, after dividing by the common disk scalar, has nonzero reduction of degree exactly \(n_D\).
An integral polynomial with a nonzero reduction of degree \(j\) has exactly \(j\) roots in the closed unit disk counted with multiplicity.
To see this, factor into normalized linear factors over a splitting extension and use multiplicativity of coefficient Gauss norms.
Each factor with an integral root reduces to a polynomial of degree one, and each remaining factor reduces to a nonzero constant.
Consequently \(G\) and \(G'\) each have exactly \(n_D\) roots in \(D\), counted with multiplicities.

<a id="length"></a>
## Local intersection lengths and the edge increments

Fix an original residue class \(b\) containing any of the chosen disks and a lift \(b_0\in\mathcal O^\times\).
Enlarge the splitting field to contain all row roots and keep the same normalized valuation.
In its completed local ring \(\mathcal O[[T]]\), with \(X=b_0+T\), each nonzero polynomial \(W\) is a unit times a scalar of valuation equal to its content valuation \(c_W\) times the monic polynomial \(W_b\) with roots \(z-b_0\) for roots \(z\) of \(W\) in residue class \(b\), counted with multiplicities.
Indeed a normalized linear factor belonging to another residue, including a nonintegral root, has unit constant in this local ring.
The polynomial \(W_b\) reduces to \(T^{\deg W_b}\).

Assume \(V_0\) primitive, exchanging rows if necessary.
The length of \(\mathcal O[[T]]/(U_0,V_0)\), divided by the ramification index of the splitting extension, equals
\[
 c_{U_0}\deg (V_0)_b+
       \sum_{\substack{U_0(z)=0,\ V_0(z')=0\\ \bar z=\bar z'=b}}v(z-z') ,
\]
with multiplicities.
In fact division by the monic distinguished polynomial \((V_0)_b\) gives a free \(\mathcal O\)-module with basis the powers of \(T\) below its degree.
The cokernel of multiplication by the first row has length equal to the valuation in uniformizer units of its determinant, the product of the scalar and root contributions above.
For a constant distinguished factor the quotient has length zero.
Summed over the chosen original residues these normalized lengths are at most \(v_\ell(H)\).
The linear-factor formula for the original resultant counts every displayed pair with valuation \(v(z-z')\), and the scalar contribution \(c_{U_0}\deg V_0\) covers all the displayed scalar terms.
All remaining determinants of normalized forms have nonnegative valuations.

The change to \(G,G'\) has unit determinant \(c'-c\), so the two local generators define the same ideal at each \(b\).
Both new rows are primitive, hence the same normalized length is the sum of \(v(z-z')\) over their root pairs in this residue with multiplicities.
For any such pair, the selected disks containing both roots form a chain.
The sum of its increments is at most the depth of the last disk in the chain, hence at most \(v(z-z')\).
The two root counts in each disk give \(n_D^2\) pairs, proving the first inequality of (1) after summation.
This calculation takes place at the selected finite residues, so changes of degree or leading coefficient under the row transformation introduce no term at infinity.

<a id="jets"></a>
## Distinct values and critical points on a disk

Put \(k_0=r_1-r_0+p(w-u)\).
Near \(\beta_i\), substituting the integral inverse branch \(z_i(X)^p=X,\ z_i(\beta_i)=\alpha_i\), gives a zero of order at least \(e\) in \(U_0+f_i V_0\), where \(f_i=z_i^{k_0}\).
The coefficients of the branch in \(X-\beta_i\) are integral by recursive division by the unit \(p\alpha_i^{p-1}\), and its linear coefficient is a unit.
For \(\beta_i=b_D+\tau_D y_i\) in \(D\) the [scaled jet calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#jets) gives
\[
             (Y-\bar y_i)^e\mid A+\lambda_i B,\qquad
                 \lambda_i=\bar\alpha_i^{\,k_0}.
\]
Indeed on replacing \(X-\beta_i\) by \(\tau_D Z\), the nonconstant coefficients of \(f_i\) reduce to zero, and the two scaled rows stay integral after division by their common scalar.
Different original residues above the same powered residue have a nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity as quotient, and \(p\nmid k_0\), so their \(\lambda_i\) differ.
If \(n_D<e\), each indicated polynomial would vanish identically.
Two distinct values of \(\lambda_i\) would force \(A=B=0\), even if \(\bar y_i=\bar y_j\).
This proves the mixed-disk bound in (1).

Write \(a(Y),b(Y)\) for the two integral scaled rows before reduction and \(X=b_D+\tau_DY\).
The polynomial
\[
                K_D=pX(a'b-ab')-\tau_D k_0 ab
\]
vanishes to order at least \(e-1\) at each \(y_i\).
Indeed \(pX\,d f_i/dY=\tau_D k_0 f_i\), so substituting \(a=-f_i b+(a+f_i b)\) leaves only terms containing \(a+f_i b\) or its derivative.
Modulo the maximal ideal, \(K_D\) becomes \(p\bar b_D(A'B-AB')\).
For a nonconstant separable ratio this reduced polynomial is nonzero, also in the presence of common factors since the Wronskian contains their square.
The degree of the reduction is at most \(2n_D-2\) by cancellation of the terms of highest degree when the degrees of the two rows agree.
The integral-root count applied to \(K_D\) bounds all its zeros in the closed unit disk, giving (2).
For a nonconstant inseparable ratio, zero derivative places the ratio in \(k(Y^\ell)\), of degree at least \(\ell\).
Constant or inseparable reductions need not give a nonzero reduction of \(K_D\).

<a id="index"></a>
## Comparison with the original power index

For a monic unit minimal polynomial \(P\) whose \(d\) conjugates \(\alpha_i\) retain degree \(d\) on powering, take all of them as the specified roots when \(P^e\mid F\).
The [power-index identity](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) expresses \(v_\ell[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]]\) as the sum over unordered pairs of \(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)-v(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\).
For two equal original residues the geometric sum dividing the power difference by the original difference is a unit, since \(\ell\ne p\).
For different original residues only \(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)\) remains.

In each original powered residue class containing at least two nodes, take the disk of depth equal to the minimum valuation of the pairwise differences there, centered at any one of its nodes.
Partition the nodes by their residue after this rescaling and repeat on every part containing at least two nodes.
This yields a finite tree since the powered nodes are distinct.
For \(D\) in this tree let \(J_D\) count unordered pairs of its specified nodes with distinct original residues.
The depth of the last disk containing each pair equals \(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)\).
Thus
\[
            v_\ell[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]]
                            =\sum_D\delta_D J_D .
\]
For \(e\ge2\), whenever (2) holds in every mixed disk of this tree, \(n_D^2\ge(e-1)^2m_D^2/4\ge(e-1)^2J_D/2\).
In that case (1) gives at least \((e-1)^2/2\) times the full local index valuation, in particular one half for double roots.
The mixed-depth estimate (1) applies without this additional hypothesis.
An input-independent positive fraction of the full prime-to-\(p\) index for arbitrary mixed disks would also have to account for disks with constant reduction and the possible loss of separability in small residue characteristic.
