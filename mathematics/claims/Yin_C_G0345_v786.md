# Separate line entropies and the size of a common line-sum fiber

<a id="statement"></a>
## The loss at side length proportional to dimension times degree

Use the [coordinate-line records](Yin_C_G0345_v782.md#statement) of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Let \(S\) be a nonempty mask in \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^J\), put \(n=L^J\), and choose the entries on \(S\) as independent fair bits, with zero entries elsewhere.
Write \(Y_\ell\) for the weighted sum on line \(\ell\) and \(Y\) for the tuple of all these sums.
All entropies below are Shannon entropies with natural logarithms.
Set \(k=\lceil L/d\rceil\) and \(I_k=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}\).
For every mask,
\[
 \sum_\ell H(Y_\ell)\ \ge\ \frac{J|S|}{k}\log(1/I_k)
      \ \ge\ \frac{J|S|}{2k}\log(k+1),\qquad
 \sum_\ell H(Y_\ell)-H(Y)\ \ge\
       |S|\left(\frac{J}{2k}\log(k+1)-\log2\right).                   \tag{1}
\]
Thus when \(J\to\infty\) and \(L/(Jd)\) stays between fixed positive constants, the excess of the sum of the marginal entropies over the true joint entropy, per allowed entry, is at least a positive constant times \(\log J\).
To prove a positive lower bound on the logarithm of the largest fiber by subtracting the sum of the separate line entropies from \(|S|\log2\), one needs \(L\gg Jd\log(2J)\) as \(J\to\infty\).
This holds even with the exact entropy of every line and every allowed position known.

Suppose \(|S|\ge\delta n\), \(\delta\log2-Jm\ge\kappa>0\), and put \(\Delta=|\operatorname{disc}(P_\alpha)|\) for the discriminant of the minimal polynomial.
For a constant \(A_\kappa\) depending only on \(\kappa\), the same subtraction gives a fiber of size at least \(\exp(\kappa n/2)\) whenever
\[
 L\ \ge\ A_\kappa Jd\bigl(1+\log(2J)+\log^+(d/\Delta^{1/d})\bigr).   \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for \(\Delta^{1/d}\ge c_0d\) with fixed \(c_0>0\) this reaches the order \(Jd\log(2J)\).

There is also an upper bound using the actual joint record.
Let \(p_*=\max_y\mathbb P(Y=y)\), and put \(a_t=\int_0^1|\cos(\pi x)|^t\,dx\).
For each integer \(1\le t\le k\),
\[
 -\log p_*\ \ge\ \frac{|S|}{t}\bigl(1-(1-t/k)^J\bigr)\log(1/a_t).
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Consequently if \(z=k/J\ge2\), every common binary fiber \(\mathcal F\) satisfies
\[
       \log|\mathcal F|\ \le\ |S|\log2
                      -c|S|\,\frac{\log(1+z)}{z}                      \tag{4}
\]
with an absolute \(c>0\).
The fractional deficit in (4) tends to zero as \(z\to\infty\).
Estimate (1) leaves open whether a positive common-fiber rate is guaranteed on every fixed-density mask at \(L\asymp Jd\) and \(J\asymp1/m\).
The [rational dimension bound](Yin_C_G0345_v782.md#dimension) already requires a side length of this order at positive rate.

<a id="marginal"></a>
## Independent degree blocks on a masked line

Partition \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\) into \(k\) consecutive blocks of length \(d\), with a possibly shorter last block.
In each block the powers at any allowed positions are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), by division by the smallest power in the block.
Use the integral power basis of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) to identify the corresponding columns with integer vectors \(v_i\).
For any rationally independent subset of columns, the map on real tori taking \(\theta\) to the coordinates \(\langle v_i,\theta\rangle\bmod1\) sends Haar probability to product Haar probability.
Indeed every nonconstant Fourier character of the target pulls back to a nonconstant character by linear independence.

Apply the [degree-block Fourier bound](Yin_C_G0522_v1.md#original-binary-degree-block-holder) with only the occupied columns in each block.
For a line with \(s_\ell\) allowed positions, its collision probability is the integral over the torus of \(\prod_i|\cos(\pi\langle v_i,\theta\rangle)|^2\), by independence of the bits and character orthogonality.
Hölder's inequality with \(k\) equal exponents and the preceding Haar property give a bound of \(I_k^{s_\ell/k}\), since the integral for a block with \(b\) occupied columns raised to the \(k\)-th power is \(I_k^b\).
The same degree-block bound gives \(I_k\le(k+1)^{-1/2}\).
The negative logarithm of collision probability is at most Shannon entropy by Jensen's inequality.
Every allowed position lies on \(J\) lines and \(H(Y)\le|S|\log2\), proving (1).

A positive difference \(|S|\log2-\sum_\ell H(Y_\ell)\) requires \(k>J\log(k+1)/(2\log2)\).
This first gives \(k>J/2\), then \(k\gg J\log(2J)\).
Since \(L>(k-1)d\), the side-length necessity follows.
Upper bounding each line's entropy by the logarithm of its support incurs at least the same loss.

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## The polynomial discriminant in the upper bound

For \(L\ge d\) use the [power-basis smoothing estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v601.md#lattice) on each line.
After adding independent uniform noise on a unit cube to the integral coefficient vector, the resulting differential entropy equals the discrete entropy of the line sum, since the translated cube interiors are disjoint and the cube has volume one.
Under the map to all real embeddings and one real and imaginary coordinate pair per complex place the Jacobian has absolute value \(2^{-r_2}\sqrt\Delta\), with \(r_2\) the number of complex places.
At a coordinate belonging to a conjugate \(\beta\), the variance after smoothing is at most \((s_\ell/4+d/12)\max(1,|\beta|)^{2(L-1)}\).
The powers in the noise have exponents at most \(d-1\le L-1\).
Summing the Gaussian upper bounds on the coordinate entropies and accounting for the Jacobian gives
\[
 H(Y_\ell)\le (L-1)m+\tfrac d2\log(2\pi\mathrm e(s_\ell/4+d/12))
                 +r_2\log2-\tfrac12\log\Delta
 \le (L-1)m+\tfrac d2\log\!\left(\frac{4\pi\mathrm e L}{3\Delta^{1/d}}\right).
\]
There are \(Jn/L\) lines.
Writing \(x=L/(Jd)\) and \(T=1+\log(2J)+\log^+(d/\Delta^{1/d})\), the contribution of their logarithmic terms divided by \(n\) is at most \((T+\log(4\pi\mathrm e/3)+\log x)/(2x)\).
This is at most \(\kappa/2\) for \(x\ge A_\kappa T\) with a sufficiently large \(A_\kappa\), since \(T\ge1\) and \(\log x/x\to0\).
Subadditivity now gives \(|S|\log2-H(Y)\ge\kappa n/2\).
Conditional on \(Y=y\), the array is uniform on its fiber, so the logarithm of the maximum fiber size is at least this conditional entropy.

<a id="layers"></a>
## Independent sets of columns for the joint record

In each coordinate order the same \(k\) consecutive blocks by an arbitrary permutation specific to that coordinate.
For \(1\le h\le k\) put a grid cell in layer \(h\) when the earliest block index among its coordinates in the chosen orders is \(h\).
The joint record is an integer matrix applied to the bits, after writing every line output in the power basis.
Its columns from a single layer are rationally independent.
To see this, suppose a rational array on that layer has zero weighted sum on every line.
At a cell with exactly one coordinate in block \(h\), the line in that direction has its allowed entries only in that block, since all other coordinates have larger ordered indices.
The corresponding powers are independent, so all such entries vanish.
Induct on the number of coordinates in block \(h\).
Vary any coordinate in that block while fixing the others.
Values at an earlier block lie outside the layer, and values at a later block have one fewer coordinate in block \(h\) and already vanish.
Independence inside block \(h\) again forces the chosen entry to vanish.
This proves the assertion for the whole layer and for each subset of it.

Order the blocks independently and uniformly in the \(J\) coordinate directions.
Every allowed cell belongs to one of the first \(t\) layers with probability \(1-(1-t/k)^J\), including cells in a shorter last block.
Choose orders for which these layers together contain at least \(|S|(1-(1-t/k)^J)\) allowed cells.
Fourier inversion bounds any joint atom by the integral of the product of the absolute cosine factors of its allowed columns.
Dropping the remaining layers increases this integral.
Apply Hölder with \(t\) equal exponents to the first \(t\) layers and use the Haar argument above for their rationally independent integer columns.
The result is \(a_t\) raised to the number of retained columns divided by \(t\), proving (3).

Take \(t=\lfloor z\rfloor\) for \(z=k/J\ge2\).
Then \(1-(1-t/k)^J\ge1-\exp(-1/2)\), and \(a_t\le I_{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}\le(1+\lfloor t/2\rfloor)^{-1/2}\).
These inequalities give \(\log(1/a_t)\gg\log(1+z)\) and \(1/t\ge1/z\).
Multiplying the upper bound for \(p_*\) in (3) by \(2^{|S|}\) gives (4).
