# Rational elliptic quotients and endpoint values of the trace factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Mordell-Weil groups and trace factors

Let \(D:W^2=(Z^2-4)(1-4(Z^2-13)(Z^2-20))\) be the [genus-two inversion quotient](Yin_C_G0345_v857.md#statement) and write \(P=(2,0)\).
The elliptic quotients \(E_1,E_2\) defined by \((Z^2,W)\) and \((Z^2,ZW)\) have rational Mordell-Weil groups isomorphic respectively to \(\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2\) and \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\).
Under their rational two-isogeny descent maps, the two pushforwards of each rational degree-zero divisor class on \(D\) have the same square class, lying in \(\{1,-23\}\).
For every monic rational factor \(q\) of either signed trace polynomial of a primitive nonaxis direction, this implies
\[
 q(2)q(-2)\in\mathbb Z^2\ \cup\ (-23)\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Here \(\mathbb Z^2\) denotes the set of integer squares.
The restrictions apply separately to each factor, including reducible factors.
For irreducible \(q\) with negative endpoint product, \(X^{\deg q}q(X+X^{-1})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
If \(l\) is odd, each signed trace polynomial has exactly one such irreducible factor, namely the factor whose endpoint value is divisible by 23.
Its endpoint product is \(-23\) or \(-575\).

<a id="models"></a>
## Elliptic models

Write \(x=Z^2\) and \(E(a,b):Y^2=X(X^2+aX+b)\) with the point at infinity as identity.
Direct substitution gives the following rational changes of coordinates on the respective quotient curves.

| Curve | \((a,b)\) | \(X\) | \(Y\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(E_1\) | \((100,2300)\) | \(-4(x-4)\) | \(4W\) |
| \(E_2\) | \((1550,597425)\) | \(-575x/(x-4)\) | \(2300ZW/(x-4)^2\) |

Each change is invertible on a dense open set since \(X\) is fractional linear in \(x\) and the second coordinate is a nonzero rational multiple of the original ordinate.
The images of \(P\) are respectively \((0,0)\) and the identity.
At the good primes 3 and 7 the orders of the reduced groups are respectively 6 and 8 for \(E_1\), and 2 and 4 for \(E_2\), by counting the solutions for each abscissa and adding the point at infinity.
Prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion injects under good reduction, so these counts exclude all rational torsion beyond the displayed point of order two on each curve.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Two-isogeny descent

For \(E(a,b)\) put \(a'=-2a,\ b'=a^2-4b\), and write \(E'=E(a',b')\).
The descent homomorphism \(\alpha\) gives \(X\) modulo squares at a point of nonzero abscissa, \(b\) at \((0,0)\), and 1 at infinity.
Indeed a line \(Y=mX+c\) meets the cubic in abscissas with product \(c^2\), with the rule at \((0,0)\) obtained from \(X(P+(0,0))=b/X(P)\).
The isogeny \(\phi:E\to E'\) has coordinates \((Y^2/X^2,Y(1-b/X^2))\), and the reverse isogeny \(\psi\) uses the corresponding formula on \(E'\) followed by division of the coordinates by 4 and 8.
Their composite on \(E\) agrees with doubling up to sign by the tangent formula.
The kernel of \(\alpha\) is \(\psi E'(\mathbb Q)\): a reverse preimage with abscissa \(u\) must satisfy \(u^2+(a'-4X)u+b'=0\), with discriminant \(16Y^2/X\).
When \(X\) is a nonzero square, either root gives an ordinate \(\pm2u\sqrt{X}\) and its sign gives the required image.
The identical argument interchanges the curves, with the rational coordinate rescaling preserving square classes.
For the curves here the only rational nonidentity points with zero ordinate on either side are \((0,0)\), since \(b,b'\) are nonsquares.
Those points lie outside their respective kernels by the displayed values of \(\alpha\).
Writing \(A,A'\) for the two descent images, the indices of the isogeny images give \([E(\mathbb Q):2E(\mathbb Q)]=|A||A'|/2\), since the order-two kernel of \(\psi\) maps nontrivially to \(A'\).
Consequently \(2^{\mathrm{rank}\,E}=|A||A'|/4\).

For a squarefree signed representative \(d\) of an abscissa class, the equation forces \(d\mid b\), since a negative valuation of \(X\) or a positive one at a prime away from \(b\) must be even.
Substituting \(X=du^2/v^2\) gives the necessary equation
\[
 h^2=du^4+au^2v^2+(b/d)v^4,\qquad \gcd(u,v)=1.
\]
Conversely a solution with \(uv\ne0\) gives \(Y=duh/v^3\).
The following finite exclusions exhaust the squarefree signed divisors outside the last column.
For modulus \(M=16,32,25\), every primitive pair has a unit coordinate, so the complete check tests \((u,v)=(i,1),(1,i)\) for \(0\le i<M\) against the residues \(j^2\bmod M\), using homogeneity.
The real exclusion on either dual follows from \(a<0,b>0,d<0\).

| \((a,b)\) | Excluded by modulus 16 | Excluded by modulus 32 | Excluded by modulus 25 | Surviving classes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((100,2300)\) | \(5,-115\) | \(-5,10,-10,115,230,-230\) | | \(\pm1,\pm2,\pm23,\pm46\) |
| \((-200,800)\) | | | \(5,10\) | \(1,2\) |
| \((1550,597425)\) | \(-1,-5,23,1039,115,5195,-23897,-119485\) | | \(5,-115,-5195,119485\) | \(1,-23,-1039,23897\) |
| \((-3100,12800)\) | | | \(5,10\) | \(1,2\) |

Both dual images contain 2 from their point of order two.
On \(E_1\), torsion gives 23 and the points \((-36,12),(50,700)\) give \(-1,2\), independent modulo the torsion square class.
On \(E_2\), torsion gives \(23897=23\cdot1039\).
The other generator \(-23\) is certified by the quartic solution
\[
 (d,u,v,h)=(-23,783965,132828,184438601605).
\]
Substitution verifies the integer identity, so the point search used to find this solution is unnecessary for verification.
The table therefore gives both images exactly, and the index formula yields the stated ranks.

## Common descent on divisors

For either elliptic model the function \(X\) has divisor \(2(0,0)-2O\).
Its evaluation on a rational degree-zero divisor, taking residue-field norms and reducing modulo rational squares, gives the descent class of its point in the elliptic group.
Indeed evaluation on a principal divisor is a square by Weil reciprocity, and on a point minus \(O\) it gives the displayed formula for \(\alpha\).
At intersecting supports one uses the leading unit in a local parameter: at \(O\) the parameter \(X/Y\) gives leading unit 1 and at \((0,0)\) the parameter \(Y\) gives \(1/b\); changes of parameter affect these only by squares since the orders are even.
Equivalently one may move the divisor away from the finite supports by Riemann-Roch.
On \(D\) the two pulled-back functions satisfy
\[
 X_2/X_1=23\bigl(5Z/(2(Z^2-4))\bigr)^2.
\]
Evaluation on a degree-zero divisor away from zeros and poles removes the constant and yields equal descent images of its pushforwards, by the residue-field norm formula.
The images in the table intersect in \(\{1,-23\}\).

Let \(A_q\) be the degree-\(m\) rational divisor of trace roots of \(q\) on the [signed quotient fiber](Yin_C_G0345_v857.md#factors).
On it \(Z=\xi B_{|k|}(r)\), where \(r=s+s^{-1}\) is the trace parameter and \(\xi=\pm1\).
The values \(Z=0,\pm2\) would force a torsion torus coordinate \(U\), hence also a torsion \(V\) on the signed fiber, which is impossible.
For \(A_q-mP\), the first elliptic descent gives the square class of \((-23)^m\prod_{q(r)=0}(Z^2-4)\), by the factor \(-4\) in \(X_1\) and the torsion value 23 at the image of \(P\).
Since \(-23\) belongs to the common two-element subgroup, the product itself belongs to that subgroup.
The identity \(Z^2-4=(r^2-4)((s^{|k|}-s^{-|k|})/(s-s^{-1}))^2\) has its squared quotient in the form of a square of an integral polynomial in \(r\), by the geometric series or its second-order recurrence.
Taking the product over the trace roots gives \(q(2)q(-2)\) modulo squares, since both traces \(\pm2\) are absent.
The trace factors are monic integral and \(-23\) is squarefree, giving the claimed integer-square alternatives.
For irreducible \(q\), a negative norm prevents \(r^2-4\) from being a square in \(\mathbb Q(r)\), so adjoining a root of \(X^2-rX+1\) doubles the degree.
At the parameters \(s=1,-1\), the torus coordinates have \(U=\pm1\) and \(V=\eta s^l\), and the two-pole equation divided by \(U^2\) takes the value \(12V^2+V+12=24+V\).
Monomial clearing, monic normalization and passage to the trace polynomial preserve these absolute endpoint values.
When \(l\) is odd they are therefore 23 and 25 in some order.
Exactly one monic irreducible factor accounts for the prime 23, with valuation one.
Its endpoint product belongs to the negative square class, with absolute value 23 or 575, and every other factor belongs to the positive square class.
