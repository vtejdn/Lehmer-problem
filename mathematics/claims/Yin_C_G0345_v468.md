# A perfect pairing of the two signed kernels and transverse Lehmer tests

<a id="statement"></a>
## The determinant of two signed rows

Use the [finite paired-factor test](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#statement) with a prime \(p>3\), common formal width \(b\), and integer rows \(A_+,A_-\) of one-norms \(H_+,H_-\).
Their signed graphs \(A_\pm(X^p)\pm X A_\pm^{*b}(X^p)\), with \(A^{*b}(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\), must have as double factors the same reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P_1\) of degree \(d\) and the distinct degree-\(d\) minimum \(P_2\) of its square.
Call a row genuine when its numerator is nonzero at the two powered targets.
Call the pair transverse when the polynomial
\[
             D=A_+ A_-^{*b}+A_- A_+^{*b}
\]
is nonzero.
If \(h=m(P_1)>0\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure, every genuine transverse pair satisfies
\[
                         6ph\le \log(2H_+H_-).                            \tag{1}
\]
Indeed at a root \(x\) of either target the graph value recovers \(x\) as a rational function of \(x^p\), with nonzero denominator by genuineness.
This inverse is the same function on both orbits, so the powered nodes are all distinct, retain their fields, and their two minima have logarithmic measures \(ph,2ph\) by the [powered-factor calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product).
Multiplying the two graphs by the opposite reversed rows evaluated at \(X^p\) and adding eliminates the \(X\)-terms, giving \((P_1P_2)^2\mid D(X^p)\).
The power map is unramified at these nonzero roots, so the square of the product \(Q_p\) of their powered minima divides \(D\).
The cofactor is integral by monic division, and \(M(D)\le\|D\|_1\le2H_+H_-\) proves (1).

The [degree and block tests below](#density) characterize zero unrestricted infimum using (1) and the relative excess width.
The [fixed-width tests](#budgets) give conditional lower bounds \((\log2)/132\) at width \(3d-1\) and \((\log2)/120\) at width \(5d/2-1\), with different stated height budgets.
At arbitrary diverging widths the [normalized transverse heights](#limit) converge to the unrestricted infimum itself.

## Reduction at a good prime

Set \(G=P_1P_2,\ m=2d,\ b=m+r-1\) with \(r\ge1\), and call \(p>3\) good when \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(G)G(1)G(-1)\).
For \(1\le u<p\), write \(\Lambda_\pm\) for the two integral lattices given by \(G^2\mid A(X^p)\pm X^u A^{*b}(X^p)\).
At a good prime the roots of \(G\) are distinct modulo \(p\), and their \(p\)-th powers have pairwise distinct reductions by Frobenius.
Thus \(Q_p\) has degree \(m\), is reciprocal, reduces to \(\bar G\), and has logarithmic measure \(3ph\), even without a genuineness assumption on the rows.
The same elimination gives \(Q_p^2\mid D\) for every pair in these shifted lattices.
The [value and derivative rank calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#shift) works for every \(r\ge1\).
The characteristic-zero image has rank at most \(m\) by signed reciprocity, while the kernel modulo \(p\) consists exactly of \(\bar G C\), \(\deg C<r\), and has codimension \(m\).
The integral kernel is saturated, so reduction identifies each \(\Lambda_\pm/p\Lambda_\pm\) with the full space of these multiples.

Define an integral scalar pairing by \(\beta(A_+,A_-)=\left[Y^{r-1}\right]\bigl(D/Q_p^2\bigr)\).
If the reductions are \(\bar G C,\bar G E\), monic division and reciprocity of \(\bar G\) give the residual polynomial modulo \(p\) as \(CE^{*(r-1)}+EC^{*(r-1)}\).
In particular
\[
                \bar\beta(C,E)=2\sum_{i=0}^{r-1} c_i e_i .                 \tag{2}
\]
This pairing is perfect modulo \(p\) and nondegenerate over \(\mathbb Q\).
Any two rational subspaces from the opposite signs whose dimensions sum to more than \(r\) therefore contain a pair with \(\beta\ne0\).

For any fixed primitive nonzero row in one lattice the subspace in the other lattice with \(D=0\) has rational codimension at least \(q=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\).
Indeed the primitive row gives \(C\ne0\), and on the reversal eigenspace \(E^{*(r-1)}=\eta E\) the residual polynomial is \(E(\eta C+C^{*(r-1)})\).
For at least one \(\eta\in\{1,-1\}\) this map is injective, and both eigenspaces have dimension at least \(q\).
The polynomial map has rational rank at least its rank modulo \(p\).

## A height and width test on degrees

Fix any computable prime \(p_d>3\) tending to infinity on even \(d\), with optional computable support cap \(s_d\to\infty\) on each row.
At degree \(d\) minimize
\[
           R=\frac{(b-2d+1)\log2}{d}+
                         \frac{\log(2H_+H_-)}{6p_d}                        \tag{3}
\]
over genuine transverse pairs with \(2d\le b<3d,\ H_\pm\le2^{p_d}\), using \(\log2\) as an additional fallback value.
The minimum is finite and computable by bounded enumeration, exact graph factorization and the integer determinant test.
Every value is at least the unrestricted infimum \(\lambda\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measures, by (1) and the [Lehmer polynomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
Its degree liminf is zero exactly when \(\lambda=0\).

Alternatively put \(D_j=2^j\), take any computable primes \(p_j>3\) tending to infinity, and use \(p=p_j\) throughout \(D_j\le d<2D_j\).
Let \(N_j(\tau)\) count the degrees with an actual admissible pair and \(R<\tau\).
Then
\[
     \lambda=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \text{for every }\tau>0,\qquad
                \liminf_{j\to\infty}N_j(\tau)/D_j>0 .                     \tag{4}
\]
Cutoffs \((\log2)/n\) suffice, and comparison at rational multiples of \(\log2\) is exact after exponentiation to clear denominators.

For the forward implications choose, as in the [fixed-base block argument](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#density), a reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) of even degree \(e\) with all positive powers generating its field and with arbitrarily small positive \(h=m(\alpha)\).
As \(h\to0\) these degrees tend to infinity.
Set \(r=\max(2,\lceil\sqrt e\rceil,\lceil e\sqrt h\rceil),\ c=2e+r-1,\ q=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), so \(r\le e/2\) eventually.
At every sufficiently large \(p\), uniformly over \(1\le u<p\), the [shifted height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#shift) gives two numerator-vanishing subspaces of codimension at least \(q\) for each sign.
It also bounds the normalized logarithm of the successive minimum of index \(r-q+1\) by \(L+O_{e,h}(\log(2p)/p)\), where \(L=(3c+4)h/q\).
Select a genuine plus row within this bound, dividing out its content.
For the minus sign the determinant-zero subspace is a third subspace of codimension at least \(q\) by the polynomial rank calculation.
Choose \(r-q+1\) independent minus rows reaching that successive minimum.
For each of the three subspaces select one of these rows outside it, denoting the three selections \(A_1,A_2,A_3\).
Some \(z\in\{0,\ldots,6\}\) gives \(A_1+zA_2+z^2A_3\) outside all three, since a linear functional vanishing on each subspace can be chosen nonzero on its designated row and its resulting polynomial excludes at most two values.
The one-norm is at most \(43\sqrt{c+1}\) times the successive minimum.
Consequently both genuine transverse base rows have \(\log H_\pm/p\le L+O_{e,h}(\log(2p)/p)\).

For an eligible substitution degree \(d=ek,\ p\nmid k\), choose \(u\) with \(ku\equiv1\bmod p\) and \(t=(ku-1)/p\).
The [two-factor lift](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#lift) takes both rows to \(A_\pm(Y^k)\) at width \(kc+t\), giving determinant \(Y^tD(Y^k)\).
Thus both rows remain genuine and transverse, preserve their norms, and use at most \(c+1\) positions each.
The width term in (3) is at most \((r/e)\log2\), and its height term is at most \(L/3+O_{e,h}(\log(2p)/p)\).
Both \(r/e\) and \(L\) tend to zero as the base measures tend to zero, so for a given cutoff a fixed base suffices and the height cap eventually holds.
The [eligible odd substitutions](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#count) have block count \(\frac{\varphi(B)}{eB}(1-1/p_j)D_j+O_B(1)\), where \(B\) is the fixed even product of excluded primes in that construction.
This proves the forward direction of (4), including the optional support cap.
For the prescribed prime at each degree use the [fixed odd-prime tower](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#tower) and the same bound along its unbounded degrees, then take the cutoff arbitrarily small.
The converse in each setting follows from (1).

## Two fixed widths and their budgets

At \(b=3d-1\) let \(J_j\) count the degrees in a block admitting a genuine transverse pair for which \(((2p_jb+1)H_\pm^2)^4<2^{p_j}\) separately at both signs, without a support restriction.
If \(p_j/j\to\infty\), then
\[
       \liminf_j J_j/D_j=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                             \lambda\ge(\log2)/132.                        \tag{5}
\]
To see this take a fixed small base with \(h<(\log2)/132\) and every eligible inflated degree \(d\) in the preceding block count, so \(p=p_j\) is good for the inflated pair by the [good reduction under substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#count).
For either sign, put \(V=\pm A^{*b}\) and \(W=pY(A'V-AV')-AV\).
Every nonzero integral row gives \(W\ne0\), since \(pY(A/V)'/(A/V)\) has limit in \(p\mathbb Z\) at infinity.
At each genuine node \(W\) vanishes by the double graph equation, and at each node where \(A\) vanishes the value equation gives \(V=0\), so \(W\) vanishes there too.
Hence \(Q_p\mid W\), and \(3ph\le\log\|W\|_1\le\log(2pb+1)+2\log\|A\|_1\).
In particular the first Euclidean minimum has log at least \(3ph/2-\frac12\log((2pb+1)(b+1))\).
Here \(r=d,\ q=d/2\), and the two [unshifted numerator subspaces](Yin_C_G0345_v463.md#digits) have codimension at least \(q\).
The [finite covolume estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v463.md#height) bounds the log product of all successive minima by \((3b-2)ph+O(d\log(2pd))\), uniformly in these degrees and \(0<h<1\).
Since \(\lambda_{r-q+1}^q\lambda_1^{r-q}\le\prod_i\lambda_i\), the three-subspace selection above gives a transverse genuine pair with
\[
      \frac{\log H_\pm+\tfrac12\log(2pb+1)}{p}
         \le (33/2-10/d)h+O\bigl(\log(2pd)/p\bigr).
\]
The right side eventually lies below \((\log2)/8\), uniformly across the block, proving (5) by the positive proportion of eligible degrees.

At \(b=5d/2-1\), let \(J'_j\) count the degrees with a genuine transverse pair satisfying the single product budget \(((2p_jb+1)H_+H_-)^4<2^{p_j}\), with the optional diverging support cap.
If also \(p_j=o(D_j)\), then
\[
       \liminf_j p_j J'_j/D_j=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                             \lambda\ge(\log2)/120.                        \tag{6}
\]
At the fixed base degree \(e\), put \(c=5e/2-1,\ r=e/2\) and \(u=1\).
The [first numerator rank theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#rank) makes every nonzero row genuine at all sufficiently large primes, since its rank at each target equals \(r\).
For either lattice take independent vectors realizing its ordered Euclidean successive minima \(\lambda_i^\pm\).
For each \(i\), their respective initial spans of dimensions \(i,r+1-i\) have a pair of basis vectors with \(\beta\ne0\) by (2).
Taking the best such pair over \(i\) gives
\[
             H_+H_-\le(c+1)
               \left(\prod_{i=1}^r\lambda_i^+\lambda_i^-\right)^{1/r},    \tag{7}
\]
since the minimum of the products \(\lambda_i^+\lambda_{r+1-i}^-\) is at most their geometric mean.
The [fixed-width determinant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#norm) gives \(\limsup_p\log(H_+H_-)/p\le 2(15-10/e)h\) for the selected pairs.
At \(h\le(\log2)/120\) this is strictly less than \((\log2)/4\).
Use the [narrow-width substitutions and padding](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#narrow) for all eligible \(k\equiv1\bmod p_j\).
The common reciprocal padding multiplier multiplies the determinant by its square and each one-norm by at most two.
The support is bounded by \(2(c+1)\), and \(\log(2p_j b+1)/p_j\to0\) uniformly in the block.
The cited count gives a strictly positive lower bound on \(p_j J'_j/D_j\), proving (6).

## The limiting transverse height

Let \(b(p)\to\infty\) be any computable formal width on primes \(p>3\), with optional computable support bound \(s(p)\to\infty\).
At each prime take the minimum of \(\log(2H_+H_-)/(6p)\) over all genuine transverse pairs with the two target minima as above and \(H_\pm\le 2^p\), including the fallback \((\log2)/3\).
These finite minima converge to \(\lambda\).
The same conclusion holds on replacing \(H_+H_-\) in the score by \(\max(H_+,H_-)^2\).
The target degrees need no input bound since every nonzero graph has finite degree.

For a fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) with \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^n)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for all positive \(n\), fix \(S=3h,\ m=2e,\ w=m+r-1\).
For each sufficiently large prime take a pair from the complementary successive minima as in (7), at width \(w\), initially with no genuineness restriction.
The [determinant upper bound](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#norm) gives limsup normalized log product at most \(2U_w/r\), where \(U_w=(r-1)S+U_m\).
Every nonzero signed row at this fixed width has normalized log one-norm of liminf at least \(S\) by the [exterior double contacts](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#attainment).
Consequently the limsup of each selected normalized log one-norm is at most \(S+2(U_m-S)/r\).

For each target index \(a=1,2\), the same [weakened-contact bound](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#attainment) gives some \(\eta_a>0\) depending only on the seed and targets for graphs with the other target double and this target simple, with lower normalized slope at least \((3-a)h+\eta_a\).
If a selected row vanishes at \(\alpha^{ap}\), its reversed row vanishes there by the graph value equation, and the reciprocal minimum of \(\alpha^{ap}\) divides both.
Dividing it out preserves the other double contacts and leaves at least simple contact at this target by separability.
Restoring the factor adds \(pah\) to log Mahler measure, while the quotient row of larger one-norm has log Mahler measure at least its log one-norm minus \(w\log2\).
Thus any such nongenuine subsequence has normalized log one-norm of liminf at least \(S+\eta_a\).
Taking \(r\) sufficiently large forces the selected pair to be genuine eventually and gives both slopes at most \(S\) plus any prescribed positive excess.
For all sufficiently large primes pad both rows to \(b(p)\) by the common reciprocal polynomial \(Y^{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}(1+Y)^{t\bmod2}\), \(t=b(p)-w\).
The determinant remains nonzero, the numerators retain their nonzero values, the norm multiplier is at most two, and the support is at most \(2(w+1)\).

The [Lehmer polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) gives \(\lambda\le\log(6/5)<(\log2)/3\), so (1) bounds both finite minima below by \(\lambda\), including their fallbacks.
The [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) and [all-power representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) give fixed seeds arbitrarily close to \(\lambda\) from within \(3h<\log2\).
For each such seed choose the excess below the height-cap margin and apply the preceding construction, then let the excess tend to zero and the measures tend to \(\lambda\).
This gives the asserted upper limits.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed adding transversality to the common-prime degree tests and using the determinant to bound the recovered measure.
A collaborating agent derived the pairing modulo \(p\), used it to select the two rows and obtained the fixed-width and limiting height estimates.
