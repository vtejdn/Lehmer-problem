# What a cyclotomic prefix detects when several roots are exterior

<a id="statement"></a>
## Total measure, house and prefix length

Let \(P\) be monic, separable and integral with degree \(d\to\infty\), positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(L\le A\) and logarithmic house \(b=\max_{P(\alpha)=0}\log|\alpha|>0\).
Let \(K(P)\) be the greatest number of coefficients below the leading one that agree with a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials of degree exactly \(d\).
If
\[
             q=L/b=o\bigl(\log d/\log\log d\bigr),
\]
then
\[
                         bK(P)\sim\log d.                             \tag{1}
\]
The quotient \(q\) counts the total exterior logarithmic measure in units of the largest exterior logarithmic modulus.
The actual number of exterior roots can be much larger.
In particular, on any such family with bounded \(q\), \(L\to0\) is equivalent to \(K(P)/\log d\to\infty\).
The constructive lower bound uses reciprocal cyclotomic products, and for reciprocal \(P\) of constant one it also gives matching at the trailing end.

There are irreducible reciprocal Perron units of degrees \(d=2n\), \(n=3^j\to\infty\), for which
\[
      K(P)=n-2,\qquad b\sim\operatorname{arcosh}(5/2)/n,\qquad
                       0<c\le L\le\tfrac12\log 11.                    \tag{2}
\]
All their distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient.
Thus even within this class, long cyclotomic prefixes can accompany a uniformly positive total measure when the number of exterior roots grows and their individual logarithmic moduli are small.

<a id="lower"></a>
## A completion using the house and the full measure

Use the construction and notation of the [all-signature completion](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets), with \(p_j\) the original power sums and \(v_j\) the disk-reflected power sums.
For an exterior root of logarithmic modulus \(u\le b\), convexity gives \(\sinh(ju)\le(u/b)\sinh(jb)\).
Consequently \(|p_j-v_j|\le q e^{jb}\).
Under the hypothesis of (1), \(q=O(\log(2d))\) and \(b=L/q\gg_A(\log(2d))^{-4}\) by the [positive measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
Take
\[
        k_-=\left\lfloor\frac{\log(d(1-e^{-b})/q)-d^{-1/4}}{b}
                       \right\rfloor .
\]
This is positive and polylogarithmic in \(d\) for sufficiently large \(d\).
In the [degree estimate (5)](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets) the high indices cost at most \(q e^{k_- b}/(1-e^{-b})\) above the reflected terms.
The low-index excess costs \(O(q k_- e^{k_- b/2}(1+\log k_-)^2)\).
This and the [reflected contribution](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#cost) are \(O_A(\sqrt d(\log(2d))^C)\) for some absolute \(C\).
The main term leaves a margin \(d(1-e^{-d^{-1/4}})\) at degree \(d\), so the reciprocal degree padding applies.
Since \(\log((1-e^{-b})/q)=O_A(\log\log(3d))\) in absolute value, this proves \(bK(P)\ge\log d-O_A(\log\log(3d))\).

<a id="upper"></a>
## Separating a largest root from the unit circle

Take any attaining cyclotomic product.
It suffices to use its unit-circle roots and equal power sums through \(k=\min(K(P),\lceil2\log d/b\rceil)\).
We have \(kb\ge(1-o(1))\log d\) and \(k\) is polylogarithmic by the preceding argument.
Set
\[
       \epsilon=\sqrt{q\log\log(3d)/\log(2d)},\qquad
       \Gamma=\{\alpha:P(\alpha)=0,\ |\alpha|>e^{\epsilon b}\}.
\]
Then \(\epsilon\to0\) and \(1\le p=|\Gamma|\le q/\epsilon=o(\log d/\log\log d)\).
For \(1\le j\le k\), the power sums of \(\Gamma\) satisfy
\[
                         |s_j|\le 2d e^{j\epsilon b}
\]
by transferring all other roots to the unit-circle target side of the moment equality.
In particular \(p=o(k)\) since \(b\le A\).

Use the [rational numerator for consecutive moments](Yin_C_G0345_v137.md#rational) with \(n=k-p+1\), multiplicity one at each center, and the new bound for \(s_j\).
On \(|z|\le e^A+1\) its numerator is at most \(2d C_A^p e^{L+n\epsilon b}\) by the same coefficient estimate.
Around the centers take disks of a common generic radius in \([1/(32pk),1/(16pk)]\).
On the boundary of the component containing a root of modulus \(e^b\), the product denominator is at least \((32pk)^{-p}\) in modulus and \(|z|\ge e^b-O(1/k)\), so \(|z|^{-n}\le e^{-nb+O(1)}\).
The component stays away from zero, and its [signed count](Yin_C_G0345_v137.md#counts) is a positive integer since only roots in \(\Gamma\) enter the rational function and each has residue one after the weighting.
The total contour length is bounded.
Thus
\[
             (1-\epsilon)nb\le\log(2d)+O_A(p\log(2pk)+1)
                                            =(1+o(1))\log d.
\]
We obtain \(kb\le(1+o(1))\log d\), excluding the upper truncation in the definition of \(k\) and proving (1).

<a id="family"></a>
## An irreducible Perron family with many exterior conjugates

For \(n=3^j,\ j\ge1\), take
\[
       P_n(z)=z^{2n}-2z^{n+1}-z^n-2z^{n-1}+1.
\]
Modulo two it is \(\Phi_{3^{j+1}}(z)\), irreducible there because the multiplicative order of \(2\) modulo \(3^{j+1}\) is \(2\cdot3^j\).
Indeed an admissible exponent is even, and \(v_3(4^s-1)=1+v_3(s)\) follows by cubing \(1+3u\) successively and expanding a power prime to three.
Reduction proves irreducibility over the integers.

There is a unique \(b_n>0\) satisfying \(2\cosh(nb_n)=1+4\cosh b_n\).
The ratio \((1+4\cosh u)/\cosh(nu)\) decreases strictly for \(u>0\), as is seen by logarithmic differentiation, and goes from five to zero.
Thus \(e^{b_n}\) is a root.
At every root \(z=e^{u+i\phi}\) with \(u>0\), the real and imaginary parts of \(z^n+z^{-n}=1+2(z+z^{-1})\) yield
\[
       1=\frac{(1+4\cosh u\cos\phi)^2}{4\cosh^2(nu)}
                    +\frac{4\sinh^2 u(1-\cos^2\phi)}{\sinh^2(nu)} .
\]
The right side is strictly convex as a quadratic in \(\cos\phi\), since \(\tanh(nu)>\tanh u\).
Its unique maximum on \([-1,1]\) is at \(1\), where the ratio is \((1+4\cosh u)^2/(4\cosh^2(nu))\).
This proves \(u\le b_n\) and equality forces \(z=e^{b_n}\).
The unique largest conjugate makes the root a Perron unit.
A torsion quotient of distinct conjugates would, upon sending the first to the largest conjugate, give a different conjugate of the same maximal modulus.
The defining equation also gives \(b_n\to0\) (the ratio tends to zero at every fixed \(u>0\)) and \(n b_n\to\operatorname{arcosh}(5/2)\).

The coefficient square sum gives \(M(P_n)\le\sqrt{11}\) by integration on the circle and Jensen's inequality.
The [short-arc count](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) now places a positive fraction of the \(2n\) roots in a fixed narrow arc of arguments about zero where \(\cos\phi>3/4\).
There \(\operatorname{Re}(1+2(z+z^{-1}))=1+4\cosh(u)\cos\phi>4\).
Hence \(2\cosh(n|u|)\ge |z^n+z^{-n}|>4\).
The involution \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) preserves the arc, and exactly one of each resulting pair is exterior.
There are therefore \(\gg n\) exterior roots with \(u\gg1/n\), proving the lower bound on \(L\).

<a id="exact"></a>
## Exactly which initial zeros can be preserved

The monic cyclotomic product \(z^{2n}+1\) gives \(K(P_n)\ge n-2\).
If the coefficient of \(z^{n+1}\) also agreed, reciprocal or anti-reciprocal symmetry of every monic cyclotomic product would determine all terms except the center.
In the reciprocal case the polynomial must be
\[
       z^{2n}-2(z^{n+1}+z^{n-1})+c z^n+1 .
\]
Its monic trace polynomial is \(C_n(X)-2X+c\), where \(C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\).
Having all \(n\) roots in \([-2,2]\) would require nonnegative value at \(2\) and nonpositive value at \(-2\) since \(n\) is odd.
These values are \(c-2\) and \(c+2\), an impossibility.

In the anti-reciprocal case the only candidate is \(z^{2n}-2z^{n+1}+2z^{n-1}-1\).
After division by \(z^n(z-z^{-1})\) the root equation is \(U_{n-1}(X/2)=2\), where \(U_{n-1}(\cos\phi)=\sin(n\phi)/\sin\phi\).
The latter polynomial has \(n-1\) simple zeros in \((-2,2)\) in the variable \(X\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\), some positive lobe between consecutive interior zeros lies entirely where \(\pi/6<\phi<5\pi/6\).
On its closure \(\sin\phi>1/2\), so its maximum is strictly below two.
There is a unique critical point between each pair of consecutive zeros by Rolle's theorem and the derivative degree.
Each other positive interior lobe consequently supplies at most two intersections with level two, counted with multiplicity, the negative lobes supply none, and each exterior branch supplies at most one.
The missed positive lobe reduces the total number of real intersections below \(n-1\).
Thus the anti-reciprocal lift cannot be cyclotomic either, proving (2) for every sufficiently large \(n\).
