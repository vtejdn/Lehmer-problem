# Disk zeros and poles forced by even contacts at arbitrary algebraic nodes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Balanced inertia at large separating exponents

Fix distinct algebraic numbers \(c_1,\ldots,c_h\) with \(0<|c_i|<1\) in a fixed complex embedding and positive integers \(u_i\).
Put \(U=\sum_i u_i\), and call a positive integer \(n\) separating if the \(c_i^n\) are distinct.
At every sufficiently large separating integer \(n\), the Hermitian jet matrix of
\[
                         K_n(x,y)=\frac{1-x\bar y}{1-(x\bar y)^n}
\]
at the \(c_i\), with derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,2u_i-1\) at \(c_i\) on both sides, has exactly \(U\) positive and \(U\) negative eigenvalues.
The threshold depends only on the nodes and their prescribed orders.

For either sign let \(A\) be a nonzero integral polynomial of formal width \(b\), so \(\deg A\le b\), and set \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y)\).
Suppose \(A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n)\) vanishes to order at least \(2u_i\) at each \(c_i\) and \(A(c_i^n)\ne0\).
Then at every sufficiently large such \(n\) the reduced map \(f=-\varepsilon A/A_b^*\) has at least \(U\) zeros and at least \(U\) poles in the open unit disk, counted with multiplicity.
In particular \(\deg f\ge2U\) and \(b\ge2U\).
The [common-ray theorem](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v261.md#ray) also covers small exponents and odd orders for nodes on one ray.
Here the nodes may occupy any number of rays, including several at the same modulus.

<a id="leading"></a>
## The first \(2U\) columns of the kernel expansion

Use the Euler derivatives \((x\,d/dx)^j\), which give an invertible change of ordinary jets because \(c_i\ne0\).
Write \(v_s=(s^j c_i^s)_{i,\,0\le j<2u_i}\), with \(s^0=1\).
The two-variable geometric series gives the jet matrix
\[
       \sum_{q\ge0}\big(v_{qn}v_{qn}^*-v_{qn+1}v_{qn+1}^*\big).
\]
It may be differentiated termwise on compact subsets of the open disk.
Let \(P_n\) have columns \(v_{qn+e}\) in increasing order of \(q=0,\ldots,U-1\), and then of \(e=0,1\) within each pair.

At node \(c_i\), write \(v=u_i\) and choose the [two-center Hermite basis](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#jets) \(Q_{a,l}\), for \(a=0,1\) and \(0\le l<v\).
It consists of the polynomials of degree below \(2v\) specified by \(Q_{a,l}(s)\equiv(s-a)^l\bmod (s-a)^v\) and \(Q_{a,l}(s)\equiv0\bmod (s-(1-a))^v\).
Change the \(2v\) rows at \(c_i\) to \(n^{-l}Q_{a,l}(x\,d/dx)\).
This row operation is invertible for every \(n\ge2\).
Denote the resulting columns by \(w_s\) and the first square matrix by \(W_n\).
At a radius \(r=|c_i|\), an entry in column \(qn+e\) is
\[
       r^{nq}c_i^e z_i(n)^q\, H_{a,l,e}(n,q),\qquad
       z_i(n)=(c_i/r)^n,\qquad
       H_{a,l,e}(X,q)=X^{-l}Q_{a,l}(qX+e).
\]
Here \(H\) is a polynomial in \(X,q\), and \(H_{a,l,e}(0,q)=\mathbf1_{a=e}q^l\).
The modulus of its value for \(q\ge0,n\ge2\) is bounded by \(C(1+n)^C(1+q)^C\) for some constant depending only on the prescribed orders.

Order the rows by increasing radius, and write \(r_j\) for the radius of row \(j\), repeating it for each jet.
Write \(q_j\) for the sorted list \(0,0,1,1,\ldots,U-1,U-1\) and put \(E=\sum_{j=1}^{2U}q_j\log r_j\).
The rearrangement inequality makes \(E\) the maximal exponential rate of a term in \(\det W_n\).
For each distinct radius \(r\), put \(m_r=\sum_{|c_i|=r}u_i\).
Its \(2m_r\) rows receive \(m_r\) consecutive complete column pairs in every maximizing assignment.
Indeed, exchanging two unequal \(q\)'s assigned in reverse order to different radii strictly increases the rate.
Every boundary between radii comes after an even number of columns and separates distinct \(q\)'s.
Consequently the sum of all maximizing terms, after removing \(e^{nE}\), is the product of the square block determinants at the separate radii, up to the sign of row ordering.
All other terms have total size \(O(e^{nE-\eta n})\) for some \(\eta>0\), since there are finitely many permutations and all remaining factors have at most polynomial growth.
If there is only one radius, every term belongs to its block.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Lower bound on the block determinants

Let \(D_r(X,\boldsymbol z)\) be one block determinant after removing its factors \(r^{nq}\) and replacing the argument \(n\) of \(H\) by the formal parameter \(X\).
If \(a_0>0\) separates the nodes, \(D_r(0,\boldsymbol z(a_0))\ne0\).
Indeed the rows of type \(a\) then use only columns of type \(e=a\).
After dividing the nonzero row factors \(c_i^a\), these are the Euler derivatives through order \(u_i-1\) of \(Y^{q_0},\ldots,Y^{q_0+m_r-1}\) at the distinct nonzero phases \(z_i(a_0)\), where \(q_0\) is the first pair index in the block.
A polynomial \(Y^{q_0}B(Y)\) in their span satisfying the homogeneous equations would have \(\prod_i(Y-z_i(a_0))^{u_i}\mid B\) over the nodes at that radius.
Since \(\deg B<m_r\), it vanishes identically.

The algebraic phases have modulus one because \(r^2=c_i\bar c_i\).
Expanding the determinant gives \(D_r(X,\boldsymbol z(n))=\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n\) with fixed algebraic polynomial coefficients and finitely many algebraic bases of modulus one.
Choose \(Q\ge1\) divisible by the orders of all torsion quotients between the bases and all torsion quotients between distinct original nodes, for the finitely many blocks together.
On a separating class \(n=a_0+Qt\), choose \(1\le a_0\le Q\), so the \(a_0\)-th powers remain distinct.
Group terms with the same \(\theta=\beta^Q\), obtaining polynomial coefficients \(\sum_{\beta^Q=\theta}\beta^{a_0}p_\beta(X)\).
At least one is nonzero since the sum of their values at zero is \(D_r(0,\boldsymbol z(a_0))\ne0\).
After setting \(X=a_0+Qt\), the remaining coefficients are polynomials in \(t\) and distinct remaining bases have nontorsion quotients.
[Xiao's Lemma 4.1](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives \(\log|D_r(n,\boldsymbol z(n))|=o(n)\) along that class, using the direct polynomial upper bound.
Taking the finitely many separating classes and blocks gives
\[
                         |\det W_n|=\exp(nE+o(n))
\]
through the full separating set.
In particular the exponentially smaller permutations cannot cancel the block product for large \(n\).

<a id="tail"></a>
## Controlling all omitted columns

Replace any initial column with pair index \(q_*\le U-1\) by \(w_{qn+e}\) with \(q\ge U\).
After sorting, the new indices \(q'_j\) satisfy \(q'_j\ge q_j\) and \(\sum_j(q'_j-q_j)=q-q_*\).
With \(\rho=\max_i|c_i|<1\), the maximal rate in the new determinant is therefore
\[
       \sum_j q'_j\log r_j
         \le E+(q-q_*)\log\rho
         \le E+(q-U+1)\log\rho .
\]
Every term in the replacement determinant is bounded by \(C(1+n)^C(1+q)^C e^{nE}\rho^{n(q-U+1)}\).
Cramer's rule therefore gives, simultaneously for all omitted columns,
\[
       \|W_n^{-1}w_{qn+e}\|
             \le \exp(o(n))(1+q)^C\,\rho^{n(q-U+1)} .
\]
The factor \(\exp(o(n))\) is independent of \(q,e\), since the denominator is the same fixed initial determinant and all initial columns have bounded \(q_j\).
The left side equals \(\|P_n^{-1}v_{qn+e}\|\) by the common invertible row change.
The sum of its squares over \(q\ge U\) and both signs is \(O(\exp(o(n))\rho^{2n})=o(1)\), by summing the polynomially weighted geometric series.
After congruence by \(P_n^{-1}\), the full jet matrix is thus \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,\ldots,1,-1)\) plus a Hermitian matrix of norm \(o(1)\).
This proves the claimed inertia.

<a id="zeros"></a>
## Zeros, poles and reciprocal root pairs

At a genuine contact the graph equation gives \(f(c_i^n)=c_i\) with nonzero denominator, so genuine contact already implies separation.
Locally \(f(x^n)-x\) vanishes to order at least \(2u_i\).
Consequently the jets of \((1-f(x^n)\overline{f(y^n)})/(1-(x\bar y)^n)\) are those of \(K_n\) through the prescribed orders on each side.
Pullback by \(x\mapsto x^n\) is an invertible change of jets at each node, since \(nc_i^{n-1}\ne0\).
The [disk kernel decomposition](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v261.md#counts) bounds the positive and negative indices by the numbers of interior zeros and poles of the reduced \(f\), respectively.
It also gives \(\deg f\) as the sum of those two counts, proving the degree and width bounds.

In particular, take any fixed list of distinct reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P_j\), with genuine graph contacts of orders at least \(2v_j\) at all their roots.
Let \(e_j\) be the number of exterior roots of \(P_j\), counting both members of a nonreal conjugate pair.
Inversion gives \(e_j\) distinct interior roots of the same minimum.
Using every one of these interior nodes gives at least \(\sum_j v_j e_j\) disk zeros and as many disk poles, and \(b\ge 2\sum_j v_j e_j\) eventually.
If all the selected minima have no unit-circle roots this specializes to \(b\ge\sum_j v_j\deg P_j\).

The coordinating agent and a proof-development agent for Sheng Yin obtained the estimate by separating the finite leading columns from the tail of the disk kernel and applying the arithmetic derivative basis on each radius.
