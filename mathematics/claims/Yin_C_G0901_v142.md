# Nonreal exterior conjugates are necessary even for nonreciprocal Mahler values near one

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound on each open side of the unit circle

Let \(A=M(P)>1\) be the multiplicative Mahler measure of a nonzero integer polynomial, \(m=\log A\), and \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
For sufficiently small positive \(m\) in terms of \(\eta\), either \(A\) is a real Salem number or it has at least
\[
                       \exp\!\big(\eta\,m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big)
\]
distinct **nonreal exterior conjugates** and at least this many distinct **nonreal interior conjugates**.
There is no reciprocity condition on \(A\), no bound on the number of its real conjugates and no bound on the second Mahler iterate.
In particular, on any shrinking sequence with a bounded number of nonreal conjugates on either one of these two strict sides, the actual Mahler values eventually are Salem numbers.

The additional input for nonreciprocal values is a count in the divisible conjugate group of any family of units of uniformly sufficiently small positive total log measure.
Suppose a nontorsion \(x\) in that group has no neutral conjugate, and take an attached compressed term \(z\) of largest degree \(d=2ub\) among the active leading constituents of \([x]\).
Let \(a\) denote the total number of real inverse-pair labels of \(z\).
On each strict side there are at least \((b-a)/2-1\) distinct nonreal conjugates of \(x\).
In particular each count is at least \((1/2-o(1))b\) uniformly as the measure ceiling of the inputs tends to zero.
The statement permits unlimited degree and measure of \(x\).

<a id="cubic"></a>
## Vanishing of every averaged cubic on a leading space

Use a finite subfamily supporting the class of \(x\) and the [multiplicity-free joint decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules) of the rational span of its conjugates modulo torsion.
Let \(W\) be the constituent attached to \(z\), \(v\ne0\) the projection of \([x]\) on it, and \(G\) the group of a finite common normal field containing the units and their conjugates.
The real space \(W\) splits orthogonally as \(\bigoplus_B W_B\) over its \(u\) blocks.
Use the ordinary coordinate metric, restricted to the sum-zero subspaces in the oriented model.
The signed action permutes blocks and preserves this metric. [Two block models](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules)

Every \(G\)-invariant homogeneous cubic polynomial on \(W\) vanishes.
In the independent signed case the even flips in each block require that the parity of a monomial's exponent be constant across that block.
At degree three and \(b\ge5\) this forces all exponents even, impossible.
In the oriented case the supported \(A_b\) on each sum-zero block has no invariant linear form, so averaging kills all terms involving more than one block: some block then occurs to degree one.
On a single block, cubic invariants of \(A_b\) are symmetric.
Indeed pull back by the equivariant projection from \(b\) coordinates to their sum-zero space, and permute monomials of degree three: two unused labels allow every odd permutation to have the same effect on the monomial as an even permutation.
Restricted to sum zero, symmetric cubics are multiples of \(\sum_i X_i^3\), since the other two power-sum generators in degree three involve \(\sum_iX_i\).
The stabilizer of each block contains an element reversing its orientation, by transitivity on the two halves, and hence negates that symmetric cubic on that block.
An invariant polynomial restricts to zero on every single block too.

<a id="average"></a>
## A quadratic weight that has zero log-modulus average

Write \(\lambda(w)=\log|w|\) on multiplicative classes, extended linearly to their real span.
It is well defined since torsion has modulus one.
Let \(Q\) be any real quadratic form on \(W\) whose symmetric matrix has the same trace on each diagonal block \(W_B\).
Then
\[
                  \sum_{g\in G}\log|gx|\;Q(gv)=0.                       \tag{1}
\]
For the part of the sum from \(\lambda(gv)\) the assertion follows by averaging the cubic \(\lambda(w)Q(w)\) as above.
Let \(v'\) be the sum of the remaining projections of \([x]\).
Take the normal subgroup \(H\) fixing the splitting fields attached to every other active constituent, using \(G\) itself if there are none.
Its action fixes \(v'\) and every \(gv'\) by normality.
On the labels of \(z\), \(H\) induces at least the independent alternating groups on each block, by the [relative largest-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) for the unmatched active representatives, allowing ties.

The \(H\)-average of \(Q\) is a scalar times the coordinate norm squared on \(W\).
In the oriented case a lift in \(H\) of a supported three-cycle has a uniform sign on each fixed block; its fourth power gives the pure supported three-cycle with all signs positive.
Thus the independent alternating groups act on the \(W_B\), each with its absolutely irreducible sum-zero representation, and different blocks have inequivalent representations of their product.
An invariant quadratic form has scalar matrix on each block and no cross terms.
In the independent signed case \(H\) contains in its image the double flips as well.
Indeed commute a lift in \(H\) of a supported three-cycle with an even flip on one moved and one unused label of that block in the whole group.
The commutator belongs to the image of \(H\) by normality and flips exactly two labels.
Conjugation gives all the supported double flips. [Signed lift actions](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles)
Their distinct coordinate characters remove all off-diagonal matrix entries of an invariant form, and the alternating label actions make the diagonal constant within each block.
In either model the initially equal block traces are preserved by averaging, since every group element permutes isometric blocks and the other matrix entries remain cross terms under such permutations.
All block dimensions agree, so there is one scalar for all blocks.

Average \(Q(hgv)\) over \(h\in H\) first in the sum from \(\lambda(gv')\).
It is a constant independent of \(g\), since all \(gv\) have the same norm.
Also \(\sum_g gv'=0\), because the class of a unit has orbit sum zero and its projections are equivariant.
This proves (1), including contributions from every other active input.

<a id="count"></a>
## Placing the weight on nonreal conjugates

Let \(c\) be usual complex conjugation.
On a block fixed by \(c\), each transposition of two labels contributes one negative direction; each fixed label contributes a negative direction unless it is a real pair.
In the oriented sum-zero quotient at most one direction is lost.
Hence the negative eigenspace on each fixed block has dimension at least \((b-a)/2-1\).
If \(c\) exchanges two blocks, the negative space on the pair is the graph of minus the induced isometry and has dimension \(\dim W_B\ge b-1\).

Assume the number of distinct nonreal exterior conjugates of \(x\) is strictly smaller than \((b-a)/2-1\).
For each \(c\)-orbit of blocks choose a nonzero negative vector supported there and orthogonal to the projections \(gv\) for all those exceptional conjugates.
There is at most one constraint per actual conjugate of \(x\), since its class determines its projection.
The preceding dimension bounds ensure the choice is possible.
Normalize the vector to have norm one on each of its supported blocks, possible on an exchanged pair because its two components have equal norms.
Take \(Q\) to be the sum of squares of the inner products with these vectors.
This is a nonzero nonnegative quadratic form whose trace on each diagonal block is one.
All real exterior conjugates have \(c\)-fixed class, hence \(c\)-fixed projection, and so also give \(Q(gv)=0\).
It follows that all positive log moduli have zero weight.
Some conjugate must have strictly positive weight, because the orbit of \(v\) spans the irreducible \(W\).
By the hypothesis excluding neutral conjugates its log modulus must be strictly negative.
This contradicts (1).
Replacing \(x\) by \(x^{-1}\), with the same active constituent spaces, proves the lower bound for the interior count.
Finally the [real-conjugate estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement) gives \(a=O(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=o(b)\) uniformly by the [polylogarithmic number of blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).

<a id="values"></a>
## Applying the two counts to actual measures

For the value \(A=M(P)\) at small positive \(m\), use the roots of its positive-measure irreducible factors as the small units, each of total log measure at most \(m\).
For a reciprocal non-Salem \(A\), the [off-circle nonreal count](Yin_C_G0901_v141.md#statement) divides equally between the two strict sides.
Apply it with a fixed exponent strictly between \(\eta\) and \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), absorbing the factor two for small \(m\).
For a nonreciprocal \(A\) no conjugate can be neutral, since its complex conjugate would be its inverse.
The preceding bound on each side therefore applies.
The [block-size inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#minimal) for the selected term gives \((1/2-o(1))b\ge\exp(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m))\) for sufficiently small \(m\).
These two cases give the full assertion.
