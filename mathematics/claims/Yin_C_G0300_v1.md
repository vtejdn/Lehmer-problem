### 2. A returned block retains its original mass inside every complete bridge

We first give the finite statement in arbitrary integer endpoint coordinates.
Let a_j in Z^d and let eta_j be the original independent digits.
Let U=sum_j eta_j a_j, and let Y=sum_j eta_j v_j for prescribed real vectors v_j.
For a coordinate set I, write U_I,Y_I for the corresponding partial sums, let J be its complement, and set p_I=Pr(U_I=0)>0.
The empty set has p_empty=1 and zero observable.

For every coordinate set A the law m_A of U_A has its maximum at zero.
Indeed, write eta_j=epsilon_j-epsilon'_j with two independent fair binary families.
If q_A is the binary endpoint law, then

    m_A(x)=sum_z q_A(z)q_A(z-x)<=sum_z q_A(z)^2=m_A(0).

This is the same finite Cauchy--Schwarz/autocorrelation mechanism as in L293; it applies to a gapped coordinate set as well as a consecutive interval.
In particular

    p:=Pr(U=0)=sum_x m_I(x)m_J(-x)<=p_J,
    q_I:=Pr(U_I=0 | U=0)=p_I p_J/p>=p_I.                (3)

All probabilities are positive because the zero digit word is available.
No comparison of different chronological p_N sequences is being used.

**Lemma 1 (complete block minorization).** Let nu_A=Law(Y_A | U_A=0), for A=I,J.
As finite measures on the real observable space,

    Law(Y | U=0) >= q_I (nu_I*nu_J)
                   >= p_I (nu_I*nu_J).                (4)

Here nu_I*nu_J is the law of the sum of independent vectors having the two stated laws.
Both are symmetric about zero.
The inequality means inequality on every measurable set; it does not assert that the whole conditioned word has independent coordinates.

*Proof.* On the subevent U_I=0 of U=0, also U_J=0.
Independence of the original coordinate families implies that their conditional observable laws are independently nu_I and nu_J.
The probability of this subevent is exactly q_I in(3).
Keeping only this subevent proves(4); sign reversal within each set proves symmetry.
This also covers an empty complement.
All the sums are finite.
No limiting interchange is involved.

There is a stronger additive covariance conclusion for DISJOINT coordinate blocks I_1,...,I_k, with any remaining coordinates retained as an additional block:

    Cov(Y | U=0)
       >= sum_(r=1)^k p_(I_r) Cov(Y_(I_r) | U_(I_r)=0).
                                                               (5)

The order is that of positive semidefinite real matrices.
To prove it, condition on the vector of all block endpoints.
Given those endpoints, the internal coordinate families remain independent, even after U=0 is imposed, since that final event is then determined.
The conditional covariance of their sum is the sum of their covariances.
The total covariance is this conditional covariance averaged over endpoint vectors, plus the positive semidefinite covariance of the conditional mean.
For each r keep only endpoint U_(I_r)=0 in its averaged term and apply(3) to I_r and its full complement.
This proves(5).
In particular overlapping blocks cannot be added by(5).
One may average separately valid matrix inequalities, with the averaging factor retained.

For a centered interval I={n-m,...,n+m-1} in a length2n bridge, m<=n, the block endpoint condition is exactly a length2m return after division by beta^(n-m).
Its contribution to(2) is EXACTLY Y_m, not merely a rotated neutral norm: -beta^(-n) beta^(n-m) times its first m coefficients is the definition of Y_m.
Consequently there exists a symmetric probability nu_(n,m), the complementary gapped returned law, such that

    b_n >= p_(2m) (b_m*nu_(n,m)),
    Cov(Z_n) >= p_(2m) Cov(Z_m), n>=m>=1.              (6)

In(6) the measures are on the real embedding space, or equivalently on the underlying additive states; covariance means the neutral image.
It has the complete original block expense p_(2m).
It is not a claim that this expense can be omitted, nor that b_n is stochastically ordered in radius.
Translating a radial event can cause cancellation, which is handled explicitly in Section4.

### 3. An actual positive definite covariance floor and its bounded-scale limitation

The five coefficients of P in increasing order are (1,-1,-1,-1,1).
A length-five original word is a return if and only if it is 0, P, or -P: divisibility by the degree-four monic minimal polynomial leaves a constant quotient, and the coefficient bound forces that constant to be0,+1,-1.
Their ORIGINAL probabilities are respectively 2^(-5),4^(-5),4^(-5).
Hence

    p_5=17/512,
    Law(word | length-five return)
        assigns 16/17 to0 and 1/34 to each of+P,-P.     (7)

For n>=4 place this block at I_k={n-k,...,n-k+4}, with k=1 or4.
Both intervals lie inside{0,...,2n-1}.
Let w_k be its neutral contribution to Z_n when its coefficients equal P.
Directly from(2),

    w_k=-zeta^(-k)sum_(j<k)p_j zeta^j,
    w_1=-zeta^(-1), w_4=1, |w_1|=|w_4|=1.            (8)

Here p_j denotes the displayed coefficient of P only in this finite sum, not a return probability.
For k=4 the identity sum_(j<4)p_j zeta^j=-zeta^4 follows from P(zeta)=0.
Thus the block's return-conditioned covariance is (1/17)w_k w_k^T, viewing a complex number as a real column.
The one-block case of(5), followed by averaging the TWO overlapping-block inequalities, gives the actual all-length lower bound

    Cov(Z_n) >= (w_1 w_1^T+w_4 w_4^T)/1024
              >= ((5-sqrt13)/4096) I_2, n>=4,
    E rho(Y_n)^2>=1/512.                               (9)

Indeed the sum of the two unit-vector outer products has eigenvalues1+/-|Re zeta|, and Re zeta=(1-sqrt13)/4.
Its smaller eigenvalue is(5-sqrt13)/4>0.
The trace of the first matrix in(9) is1/512.
No comparison between the actual covariance and L332's n I/4 upper proxy was presumed.
For n<=2 the complete word has length at most the minimal-polynomial degree and only the zero return is possible, so the stated n>=4 hypothesis has content.

The same block gives a simple uniform small-ball statement.
Any open Euclidean ball of radius less than1/2 contains at most one of the three points y,y+w_4,y-w_4.
Its probability under a translate of the block law in(7) is therefore at most16/17.
By(3)--(4), for every center z and every 0<r<1/2,

    Pr(|Z_n-z|<r | return)<=1-1/512, n>=4.             (10)

The part of the whole bridge outside the event U_(I_4)=0 has only been bounded by its full probability.
In particular(10) does not say that the midpoint eventually leaves each fixed ball.
A tight probability of full support is compatible with(9)--(10).

**Bounded-diameter limitation.** Suppose every block used in the disjoint certificate(5) has coordinate diameter at most L-1, where L>=1 is fixed, and the observable is Z_n.
A block entirely on one side of the midpoint contributes zero on its own return: on the high side it has no coefficient in(2), and on the low side its endpoint identity also vanishes at zeta.
Every contributing block crosses the cut and lies among at most2L coordinates around it.
The blocks are disjoint, so their cardinalities l_r satisfy sum l_r<=2L and l_r<=L.
Since |Y_(I_r)(zeta)|<=l_r, the TRACE of the entire certified lower matrix is at most

    sum_r p_(I_r) tr Cov(Y_(I_r)(zeta) | U_(I_r)=0)
        <=sum_r l_r^2<=2L^2.                          (11)

This is an upper bound on this particular lower certificate, not an upper bound on the actual covariance.
Convex averages of such certificates have the same limitation.
Arbitrarily summing overlapping copies is not justified by(5).
Thus a fixed-diameter local-relation argument cannot produce a linear-in-n lower bound from this disjoint-block mechanism.
A growing relation support, a genuinely coupled comparison, or an independent lower-fiber input is still required.

### 4. A paid exponential tail floor for every sufficiently long original bridge

Retain EXACTLY the fixed-input constants A_D,B_D,c0,C_up from L309's whole-core linear-return and counting proofs.

    Jx=beta^(-1)iota(x), iota(beta)=beta^(-1),

preserves D and rho and reverses actual edges with the digit sign reversed.

    lambda_ann=max{2,sqrt(8 C_up/c0)},
    c_ann=c0 lambda_ann^2/2,
    m(r)=ceil(A_D lambda_ann r+B_D).

    F_r={x in D:r<rho(x)<=lambda_ann r}

contains at least c_ann r^2 distinct states.

    w_x=Pr(W_x)>=4^(-2m(r)).

For -x choose -W_x, so w_(-x)=w_x.

**Theorem 2 (finite original annular floor).** At this fixed actual quartic, for every r>=4 and every integer n>=m(r),

    Pr(rho(Y_n)>r | length2n return)
         >= (c_ann/2) r^2 4^(-2m(r)).                 (12)

There is no hypothesis on the finite or infinite critical mean.

                       Pr_(nu_J)(|y(zeta)+x(zeta)|>r).

### 5. An exact two-integer diagnostic for a diffusive lower moment

Let c_n(x)=4^n Pr(sum_(j<n)eta_j beta^j=x).
These are nonnegative INTEGERS: a word's integer weight is2 raised to its number of zero digits.
Put C_n=c_(2n)(0).
The full original midpoint factorization and reciprocal reversal give

    b_n(x)=c_n(x)c_n(Jx)/C_n,
    C_n=sum_(x in O)c_n(x)c_n(Jx).                      (14)

To verify the second factor, reversal of the n digits preserves the law of beta^(n-1)iota(sum eta_j beta^j).
Applying this map to beta^n x gives Jx.
Hence c_n(beta^n x)=c_n(Jx), and the actual transition identity in L293 yields(14).
Only finitely many x contribute; every such state is in D.
The fixed map J does not depend on n, although both fibers do.
This is an exact reorganization of L293's complete integer-fiber formula, not a new claim about independent midpoint halves.

Write each x uniquely as a0+a1 beta+a2 beta^2+a3 beta^3, with integral coefficients, and define

    s_l(x)=sum_(j=0)^(3-l) a_j a_(j+l), 0<=l<=3,
    A(x)=s_0+s_2+3s_3,
    Bq(x)=s_1+s_2+s_3.

The identities u^2=u+3 and u^3=4u+3, for u in(1), give

    rho(x)^2=A(x)+u Bq(x),
    x_+ x_-=A(x)+u_plus Bq(x),
    u_plus=(1+sqrt13)/2.                              (15)

Indeed x(zeta)x(zeta^(-1))=s_0+s_1 u+s_2(u^2-2)+s_3(u^3-3u).
The same expansion at beta,beta^(-1) proves the other embedding in(15).
The quantity x_+x_- need not be positive, so it is not a second squared Euclidean norm.

Form the complete integer sums

    A_n=sum_x c_n(x)c_n(Jx) A(x),
    B_n=sum_x c_n(x)c_n(Jx) Bq(x),
    T_n=(A_n+u_plus B_n)/C_n.

These sums may involve signed summands; no absolute value or truncation has been introduced.
The strict actual core strips imply |T_n|<BS, while(14)--(15) give exactly

    E rho(Y_n)^2=(A_n+u B_n)/C_n
                =T_n-sqrt13 B_n/C_n,
    |E rho(Y_n)^2+sqrt13 B_n/C_n|<BS.                 (16)

Thus the entire possible linear neutral growth is carried by the signed integer coefficient B_n, up to a fixed paid real-strip error.
In particular

    limsup_(n->infinity) E rho(Y_n)^2/n
       =-sqrt13 liminf_(n->infinity) B_n/(n C_n).        (17)

The bounds in L332 make these ratios finite, but the equality also follows directly with extended limits from the O(1/n) error in(16).
