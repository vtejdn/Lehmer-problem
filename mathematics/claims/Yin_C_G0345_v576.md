# Effective bounds on gaps in minimal ternary relations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finite certificates at each weight

Let \(P\) be the monic minimum of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), let \(k\ge1\), put \(m=\log M(P)\), and let \(s_0\) count its conjugates on the unit circle.
A support is minimal if it supports a nonzero ternary multiple of \(P^k\) and no proper subset does.
For each fixed input \(P,k\), every minimal support of cardinality \(j\) has span \(O_{P,k}(j\log(2j))\), with effective constants.
When \(s_0=0\), every individual gap is bounded independently of \(j\), including at multiple roots.
Thus the complete list up to translation at any fixed weight is effectively computable even for Salem inputs.
The resulting finite forbidden sets through weight \(j\) give a decreasing sequence of computable rational upper bounds converging to the [maximum density of admissible exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement).

When \(s_0=0\), the ordinary generating series by weight of all minimal supports up to translation is rational and computable.
The same is true when one counts distinct patterns after removing both translation and common dilation, with a pattern counted once across all dilations.
In particular their exponential growth rates, defined by the upper limit of the \(j\)-th roots, are computable real algebraic numbers at each such input.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The gap at a cut of a minimal relation

For each conjugate \(\beta\) off the circle set \(\eta_\beta=\min(|\beta|,|\beta|^{-1})\) and put
\[
 C=\sum_{|\beta|\ne1}\log\left(\sum_{i\ge0}(i+1)^{k-1}\eta_\beta^{\,i}\right).
\]
The inner sum is effective, for example by differentiating the geometric series.
Fix a minimal support with span \(W\) and a ternary relation on it.
Every coefficient on the support is nonzero.
At a cut between consecutive selected exponents \(a\) and \(a+g\), divide the relation by \(X^a\) to obtain \(A(X)+X^g B(X)\), where \(A\) uses nonpositive powers and \(B\) nonnegative ones.
Each side is nonempty.
The order \(b\) of \(A\) at \(\alpha\) is less than \(k\), since otherwise the lower block alone would support a multiple.
The order of \(B\) is also \(b\).
With \(D=X\,d/dX\), Leibniz's rule and vanishing of the lower derivatives give the nonzero algebraic integer
\[
                     z=(D^b A)(\alpha)=-\alpha^g(D^b B)(\alpha).
\]
Integrality uses the unit hypothesis also for negative powers.
At expanding conjugates use the first expression, bounded by the sum defining the corresponding term of \(C\).
At contracting conjugates the second is at most \(|\beta|^g\) times the same sum.
At a conjugate on the circle the two expressions give a bound \(\min(j_1,j_2)(1+W)^{k-1}\), where \(j_1,j_2\) count the terms on the two sides.
Taking the norm, using \(\sum_{|\beta|<1}\log|\beta|=-m\), proves
\[
        gm\ \le\ C+s_0\bigl(\log\min(j_1,j_2)+(k-1)\log(1+W)\bigr).
\]
For \(k=1\) each individual gap is at most \((C+s_0\log\min(j_1,j_2))/m\), uniformly in the rest of the span.
For general \(k\) sum over the \(j-1\) gaps and set \(y=W/(j-1)\).
Since \(1+W\le j(1+y)\), this gives
\[
           my\le C+s_0 k\log j+s_0(k-1)\log(1+y).
\]
Above an effectively calculable threshold in \(y\), the last term is at most \(my/2\).
This proves the span bound and gives a terminating enumeration by sign choices and exact polynomial division, discarding supports containing a smaller relation.

<a id="densities"></a>
## Computing upper densities from forbidden finite supports

Through weight \(j\), enumerate all normalized supports starting at zero using the preceding bound and keeping their actual gaps.
Forbid every translate of each listed support in the indicator word of an exponent set, allowing arbitrary bits at the unlisted positions.
This condition is recognized by recording the last \(W_j\) bits, where \(W_j\) is the largest listed span and an empty list requires no history.
One can start with zero padding on the left and reject on the first completed support.
Weight each allowed edge by the new bit.
The maximum asymptotic density \(d_j\) for these constraints is the largest accessible cycle mean, computable and rational by the [finite path argument](Yin_C_G0345_v573.md#period).
In particular a finite directed graph supplies an upper bound at a fixed input and every length up to a bounded additive error in the number of selected positions.

Adding forbidden supports gives \(d_j\downarrow d_\infty\ge D_k(P)\).
Fix any \(N\).
When \(j\ge N\) each allowed interval of length \(N\) satisfies every relation constraint on that interval, because a violation contains a minimal support of size at most \(N\).
Disjoint length-\(N\) intervals then bound the limiting density by \(a_N(P,k)/N\).
Taking the infimum over \(N\) proves \(d_\infty=D_k(P)\).
In particular \(d_j<1/128\) certifies \(0<m\le128d_j/k\) by the [explicit lower density bound](Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#constant).
An arbitrary finite sublist also gives a valid upper bound by the same graph construction.

<a id="series"></a>
## Counting each pattern once for hyperbolic inputs

Suppose \(P\) has no conjugate on the unit circle.
Start from the [finite mask automaton](Yin_C_G0345_v573.md#masks) for \(P^k\), adding an absorbing rejecting state for an inadmissible mask.
To recognize minimal supports, track the state on the original word and the set of all states reached on words obtained so far by changing exactly one selected bit to zero.
At a new bit, advance the existing set and, when the bit is one, also advance the original previous state on a zero for the new deletion.
Accept precisely when the original word rejects, every single-deletion word passes, and the first and last bits are one.
Removing even one selected position must destroy all relations by minimality, and any proper subset omits at least one such position.
This deterministic finite automaton counts each translation class once by reading the unique word between its two occupied endpoints.

For dilations choose an effective integer \(Q\) with \(\rho^{Q+1}>2\), where \(\rho>1\) is the house of \(\alpha\).
A nonzero ternary polynomial cannot have a root of modulus at least two, since the leading monomial then exceeds the sum of the moduli of the lower monomials.
If a normalized pattern \(E\) dilated by \(q\) is minimal for \(P^k\), then \(E\) is a minimal support for \(P_{\alpha^q}^k\), and conversely.
Indeed vanishing of a polynomial on \(qE\) is substitution at \(X^q\), which has nonzero derivative at \(\alpha\) and preserves the vanishing order.
The same applies after deleting any support positions.
Consequently \(q\le Q\) for any such dilation.
Construct the union of the preceding minimal-support languages for \(P_{\alpha^q}^k,\ 1\le q\le Q\), all still hyperbolic.
If an exponent set in this union has common difference gcd \(g\) after translation, its relation at some \(\alpha^q\) gives a ternary relation at \(\alpha^{qg}\), so \(g\le Q\).
Retain gcd one by excluding the words in which all selected positions are congruent modulo some prime at most \(Q\).
Each test uses only finite position residues.
The resulting finite union and filters can be determinized by recording their component states, so distinct words are again counted once.

For either resulting deterministic automaton trim states to paths from the start to an accepting state.
There is no directed cycle consisting entirely of zero bits in this trimmed graph.
Otherwise one could insert arbitrarily many traversals in an accepted word without changing its cardinality, contrary to the fixed-weight span bound, applied to the finite list of powered inputs in the second construction.
With \(U_0,U_1\) the two transition count matrices, \(U_0\) is therefore nilpotent.
The matrix \((I-U_0-zU_1)^{-1}\) exists over rational functions regular at zero.
Summing its entries from the start to the final states counts all paths by their numbers of ones, since paths of each weight have bounded length.
This proves rationality and effectivity, also for an empty accepted language whose series is zero.
A polynomial series gives growth rate zero.
For any other series the reciprocal of the least modulus of a pole after cancellation is its upper exponential growth rate by the power-series radius formula, giving the last assertion.
