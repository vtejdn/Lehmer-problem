# The denominators that produce short signed rows beyond the minimum width

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coordinates from the two minimum-width generators

Let \(G\) be a nonconstant squarefree product of monic reciprocal irreducible minima of nontorsion units, of degree \(m\), such that the quotient of every two distinct roots of \(G\) is nontorsion.
Let \(Q_n\) be the monic polynomial with roots \(x^n\) for all roots \(x\) of \(G\).
It is reciprocal, separable, integral and has logarithmic Mahler measure \(nS\), where \(S=\log M(G)\).
For a polynomial \(A\) of formal width \(w\), write \(A^{*w}(Y)=Y^w A(1/Y)\).
At sign \(\sigma\) and exponent \(n\) let \(\Lambda_{w,\sigma}\) consist of the integral coefficient rows for which
\[
                 G^2\mid A(X^n)+\sigma X A^{*w}(X^n).
\]
Fix the excess width \(k\ge0\) and set \(b=m+k,\ r=k+1\).
At all sufficiently large \(n\), let \(A_+,A_-\) generate the two primitive lines at width \(m\), and put \(H_\pm=\|A_\pm\|_1\).
Let \(E_{\tau,k}\) denote the integral polynomials of formal width \(k\) with \(U^{*k}=\tau U\).

There is a nonzero ordinary integer \(a_n\), determined up to sign, with
\[
 A_+ A_-^{*m}+ A_- A_+^{*m}=a_n Q_n^2.
\]
Every rational row of width \(b\) and sign \(\sigma\) has a unique expression
\[
                    A=A_+U+A_-V,\qquad
       U\in E_{\sigma,k}\otimes\mathbb Q,\quad V\in E_{-\sigma,k}\otimes\mathbb Q .
\]
The integral rows correspond exactly to pairs \(U=B/a_n,\ V=C/a_n\) with integral eigenpolynomials satisfying
\[
                          A_+B+A_-C\equiv0\pmod {|a_n|}.                      \tag{1}
\]
For all rational multipliers, uniformly even when they depend on \(n\), their sizes satisfy
\[
 e^{-o(n)}\max(H_+\|U\|_1,H_-\|V\|_1)
       \le \|A\|_1 \le 2\max(H_+\|U\|_1,H_-\|V\|_1),\qquad
 \log|a_n|=\log(H_+H_-)-2nS+o(n).                                            \tag{2}
\]
The error terms fix \(G,k\).
For \(G=P_1P_2\) as in the [unrestricted gap criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#gap), (1) and (2) give the exponential cost of the shortest genuine row by a congruence on multipliers of degree at most \(k\) for \(0\le k<d/2\), where \(d=\deg P_1\).
At every sufficiently large prime exponent \(n=p\), the modulus \(|a_p|\) is prime to \(p\).

Let \(M_{k,\sigma}=A_+E_{\sigma,k}+A_-E_{-\sigma,k}\) and \(\Gamma_{k,\sigma}=\Lambda_{b,\sigma}/M_{k,\sigma}\).
Under the [volume hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v461.md#statement) for a reciprocal seed and its square put \(S=3h,\ C_0=V_m,\ L=C_0-3h\), where \(h\) is the seed's logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular for a Salem seed \(L=(6d-7)h\).
Then the logarithmic index divided by \(n\) tends to \(kL\) at either sign.
More precisely, list the \(r\) positive Smith invariant factors in divisibility order, padding with ones.
For \(k=2j\), the first \(j+1\) equal one and each of the last \(j\) has limiting normalized logarithm \(2L\).
For \(k=2j+1\), the first \(j+1\) equal one, the next factor has slope \(L\), and the last \(j\) have slope \(2L\).
At \(k=1\), the two cyclic orders \(J_+,J_-\) satisfy \(\gcd(J_+,J_-)\mid4\).
At this first larger width the shortest row has an [explicit two-integer approximation formula](#plane).

## The determinant and the integral parameters

By the [minimum-support result](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement) the two width-\(m\) lines exist and have full support and nonzero values at every powered node eventually.
Use their primitive signs arbitrarily.
Given two pairs of polynomials \((U_i,V_i)\) with \(G^2\mid U_i(X^n)+X V_i(X^n)\), eliminating \(X\) shows that \(Q_n^2\mid U_1V_2-U_2V_1\).
Indeed the determinant after substitution vanishes twice at every root of \(G\), the powered roots are distinct and the power map is unramified there.
This is the [two-row determinant argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) applied to the entire root list.
At width \(m\) it gives the formula for \(a_n\) by degree and monic division.
Its eventual nonvanishing also follows from the leading term proved below.

Use the same determinant with a signed row \(A\) at width \(b\) to define
\[
 B=\frac{AA_-^{*m}+\sigma A^{*b}A_-}{Q_n^2},\qquad
 C=\frac{AA_+^{*m}-\sigma A^{*b}A_+}{Q_n^2}.
\]
The two polynomials have degree at most \(k\), are integral if \(A\) is integral, and have the indicated reversal eigenvalues by reciprocity of \(Q_n\).
Expanding their numerators gives \(a_n A=A_+B+A_-C\).
Conversely the product with an eigenpolynomial multiplies the corresponding signed graph by its own power substitution.
Every pair in (1) thus yields an integral row.
Uniqueness follows by applying the determinant formulas to a linear combination, or by solving the two equations for the row and its reversal using the nonzero determinant.
In particular \(|a_n|\) kills the finite quotient \(\Gamma_{k,\sigma}\).

For a sufficiently large prime exponent \(p\), reduction of \(G\) is squarefree and avoids the endpoints.
The [good-prime kernel formula](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#rank) gives \(A_\pm\bmod p=t_\pm\bar G\), where \(t_\pm\ne0\) by primitivity.
Frobenius permutes the roots of \(\bar G\), so \(\bar Q_p=\bar G\).
The determinant identity reduces to \(a_p\equiv2t_+t_-\bmod p\).

## Growth of the multiplication map in every real direction

Set \(u=m/2\).
On each of the finitely many torsion classes of \(n\), form the two cofactor polynomials \(F_\pm(Y)\) for the signed width-\(m\) matrices, with \(N\) the formal exponent.
Use characters of the actual root group modulo torsion as in the [fixed-support specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height).
Their \(Y^l\) coefficients are the signed minors deleting column \(l\).
After specialization each is a nonzero scalar multiple of the corresponding primitive integral row.
The proof below uses ratios unchanged by scaling either cofactor polynomial.

Choose a generic real weight on the free character lattice.
The absolute weights of the \(u\) sites, one from each inverse pair, are nonzero and distinct.
The excluded hyperplanes are proper by the hypothesis on quotients, also for a site and the inverse of another.
Assign to column \(l\) the distance \(|l-u|\).
In a maximal minor the largest possible weight pairs the largest absolute site weight with the two largest column distances, then does the same with the remaining sites and columns.
The bound is strict when the middle column is retained because the least absolute site weight is positive.
Equality therefore requires deletion of \(u\) and assigns the pair \(l,m-l\) to a unique site at each rank.
Within each pair the two leading terms give a determinant with scalar coefficient \(\pm c\), where \(c\) is the signed site label, as in the [outer-pair computation](Yin_C_G0345_v461.md#support).
Indeed their value and derivative columns use the same site power and the derivative multipliers, after removing the value multipliers, differ by one.
Torsion twists multiply this determinant by a common nonzero phase.
Thus the highest-weight part of each \(F_\pm(Y)\) is a single nonzero character times a scalar times \(Y^u\).

In each \(E_{\tau,k}\) use the integral basis \(Y^l+\tau Y^{k-l}\) for \(0\le l<k/2\), together with \(Y^{k/2}\) at even \(k\) and positive sign.
The union of the two bases spans the whole degree-\(k\) coefficient space over \(\mathbb Q\).
Form the \(r\) coefficient columns \(F_+ e\), \(F_- f\) at width \(b\), using the bases of \(E_{\sigma,k},E_{-\sigma,k}\), respectively.
Let their counts be \(r_+,r_-\).
At the generic weight the exterior product of their initial parts is nonzero because all columns are a scalar character times \(Y^u\) times their respective basis polynomials.
Consequently the maximum weight of the exterior-product coordinates equals \(r_+\) times the maximum weight of \(F_+\) plus \(r_-\) times that of \(F_-\).
In the polynomial \(F_+F_-^{*m}+F_-F_+^{*m}\) the two initial terms add to twice their common scalar character times \(Y^{2u}\).
Its maximum weight is likewise the sum of the two individual maxima.
These equalities extend to all weights by continuity of the maxima over finite supports, with coefficients in the characteristic-zero field of rational functions of \(N\).

At the actual complex embedding, each such maximal support weight is the limit of the logarithm of the largest specialized coordinate divided by \(n\) along its torsion class.
This follows from the [placewise recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch), with polynomial coefficients in \(N\), because the different characters yield bases with nontorsion ratios.
The maximum for the absolute embedding uses the logs of the absolute values of the fixed free generators.
Returning to \(A_\pm\) cancels the scalar factors in the two ratios just computed.
In particular the wedge of the multiplication columns divided individually by \(H_+\) or \(H_-\) has norm \(\exp(o(n))\), and those normalized columns are bounded.
Their least singular value is at least \(\exp(-o(n))\) by taking the product of singular values.
Equivalence of norms in the fixed multiplier spaces proves the first bounds in (2), uniformly in the multiplier vectors.
For the determinant polynomial the same calculation gives logarithmic one-norm \(\log H_++\log H_-+o(n)\).
The one-norm of the fixed-degree \(Q_n^2\) lies between its Mahler measure and \(2^{2m}\) times that measure.
This gives the second equality in (2) and the eventual nonvanishing used above.
There are finitely many torsion classes, so the errors are \(o(n)\) on the full sequence.

## Which invariant factors account for the index

Under the volume hypotheses both boundary generator heights have normalized logarithm tending to \(C_0\).
The full-rank multiplier sublattice \(M_{k,\sigma}\) has covolume of slope \(r C_0\) by the wedge estimate.
The saturated lattice has covolume of slope \(C_0+kS\) by the [fixed-width formula](Yin_C_G0345_v461.md#volume).
Dividing the covolumes gives index slope \(kL\).
Furthermore \(\log|a_n|/n\to 2L\) by (2).

Each multiplier block separately has saturated image in the ambient integral coefficient space.
Indeed for a primitive \(A_\pm\), integrality of \(A_\pm U\) forces rational \(U\) to be integral by the content lemma, and reversal imposes only exact linear equalities.
At every prime, the columns of either block therefore stay independent modulo that prime.
At least the larger block dimension of the Smith factors of \(M_{k,\sigma}\) in its saturation must equal one.
For even \(k=2j\) this leaves at most \(j\) factors, each dividing \(|a_n|\).
Their total slope \(2jL\) and the upper slope \(2L\) per factor give an individual limit \(2L\) for each of the last \(j\).

Multiplication by \(1+Y\) injects \(\Gamma_{k,\sigma}\) into \(\Gamma_{k+1,\sigma}\).
Indeed if the new row belongs to the new multiplier sublattice, uniqueness of the decomposition makes both \((1+Y)U\) and \((1+Y)V\) integral, forcing \(U,V\) integral by monic division.
For finite abelian groups \(D\subset H\), the invariant factors of \(D\) in descending order divide the corresponding factors of \(H\).
At each prime \(\ell\), the \(\ell\)-torsion in \(\ell^{e-1}D\) injects into that of \(\ell^{e-1}H\) for each \(e\ge1\), counting the cyclic factors of order at least \(\ell^e\) in the primary parts.
Thus at \(k=2j+1\) each of the largest \(j\) factors has slope at least \(2L\), and divisibility by \(|a_n|\) gives equality.
The total slope \((2j+1)L\) leaves slope \(L\) for the one remaining possible factor.
This also treats \(j=0\).

## A single modular approximation at the first extra width

At \(k=1\) use the primitive columns \(B_1=(1+\sigma Y)A_+\) and \(B_2=(1-\sigma Y)A_-\), which generate \(M_{1,\sigma}\).
The exact index \(J_\sigma\) is the gcd of their two-by-two minors since the rational plane has saturated lattice \(\Lambda_{m+1,\sigma}\).
Completing the primitive \(B_1\) to an ambient integral basis shows that \(B_2\equiv v_\sigma B_1\bmod J_\sigma\) for one residue \(v_\sigma\), a unit by primitivity of \(B_2\) (with the vacuous convention at modulus one).
Thus the integral rows are precisely \((s B_1+t B_2)/J_\sigma\) with \(s+v_\sigma t\equiv0\bmod J_\sigma\).
Write \(\mu_\sigma\) for the least nonzero value of \(\max(|s|,|t|)\) in this congruence lattice.
Equations (2) and the index slope give, under the volume hypotheses,
\[
       \frac{\log\min_{0\ne A\in\Lambda_{m+1,\sigma}}\|A\|_1}{n}
                    =3h+\frac{\log\mu_\sigma}{n}+o(1).
\]
For a seed and its square here, \(d\ge4\), so all these nonzero rows are eventually genuine by the [numerator rank](Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#rank).
The limiting value of the shortest row is consequently tied to the rational approximation of this explicit residue divided by its modulus.

Let \(D=\gcd(J_+,J_-)\).
The two proportionalities give units \(v,w\) modulo \(D\) with
\((1-Y)A_-\equiv v(1+Y)A_+\) and \((1+Y)A_-\equiv w(1-Y)A_+\).
Eliminating \(A_-\) gives \(A_+\big(v(1+Y)^2-w(1-Y)^2\big)\equiv0\bmod D\).
The primitive polynomial \(A_+\) cancels modulo \(D\), since the integer content of its product with any lift of the other factor equals the latter's content.
Comparing constant and linear coefficients gives \(v=w\) and \(4v=0\bmod D\).
Thus \(D\mid4\), without any volume hypothesis.

A collaborating agent derived the Smith factor slopes and the coprimality bound for the two cyclic quotients.
