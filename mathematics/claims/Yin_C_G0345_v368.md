# Isolated pairs of powered conjugates recover the growth of the power index

<a id="statement"></a>
## An arithmetic characterization of a uniform Mahler gap

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, with roots \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\), and put \(h=\log M(P)\).
For a prime \(p\) preserving the degree of \(\alpha_1^p\), write \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) and \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha_1]:\mathbb Z[\beta_1]]\).
At every rational prime \(\ell\nmid p\,\operatorname{disc}P\), choose a place of the splitting field over \(\ell\), with valuation \(v(\ell)=1\).
For \(i\ne j\) define \(s_{ij}=v(\beta_i-\beta_j)\).
These numbers are nonnegative since all conjugates are units.
Define the length for which exactly \(i,j\) remain together by
\[
 a_{ij}=s_{ij}-\max_{k\notin\{i,j\}}\min(s_{ij},s_{ik},s_{jk}),
 \qquad E_p=\sum_{\ell\nmid p\,\operatorname{disc}P}\log\ell
                  \sum_{i<j}a_{ij},
\]
where an empty maximum is zero.
The inner sum is invariant under relabeling by Galois and hence independent of the chosen place.
The sum is finite by the power-index formula below.
Write \(\lambda_i=\log|\alpha_i|\) for the archimedean conjugates.
For every fixed \(P\), all sufficiently large primes preserve the degree, and
\[
 \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{E_p}{pd}
 =\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log I_p}{pd}
 =\frac1{2d}\sum_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|
 \ \in\ [h/2,(1-1/d)h].                                             \tag{1}
\]
Each limit here is over primes, with \(P\) fixed before the limit.
Consequently an absolute positive lower bound on these isolated-pair rates over all nontorsion units is equivalent to the unrestricted Lehmer gap.

For every prime preserving degree, suppose \(P^e\) divides an integer polynomial with precisely two occupied classes modulo \(p\), whose two rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Write \(H\) for the absolute resultant after removing the individual row monomials.
Then
\[
                         \log H\ge e^2 E_p .                           \tag{2}
\]
Thus an explicit part of the local resultant estimate, computed from the input alone, asymptotically retains the entire logarithmic power index with coefficient \(e^2\).
There is no assertion of equality for the full resultant.

<a id="depth"></a>
## The finite loss from triple collisions

For \(t>0\), partition the indices by \(i\sim_t j\) if \(s_{ij}\ge t\), including \(i\sim_t i\).
The ultrametric inequality gives equivalence relations with finitely many changes, at the positive values of \(s_{ij}\).
The pair \(i,j\) lies in a class of size exactly two for a set of depths of length \(a_{ij}\).
At a good prime as above the original residues \(\bar\alpha_i\) are all distinct.
The [increment estimate for a tree of disks](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#statement) charges at least \(e^2\) times the length of each such two-point segment to \(v_\ell(H)\).
Indeed the two inverse branches have distinct residual coefficients, and two combinations of reduced row polynomials of degrees less than \(e\) cannot each vanish to order \(e\) at their specified nodes unless both rows vanish identically.
This includes a common scaled node.
The segments for different two-point classes have disjoint interiors in the disk tree, so summing proves (2).
For \(e=1\) the same two-combination argument gives the required minimum degree one.

At these primes the [power-index identity](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) gives \(v_\ell(I_p)=\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}\).
Write \(I_p^{\mathrm{good}}\) for the part of \(I_p\) supported at \(\ell\nmid p\,\operatorname{disc}P\).
With the same primes and valuations set
\[
 T_p=\sum_{\ell\nmid p\,\operatorname{disc}P}\log\ell
             \sum_{i<j<k}\min(s_{ij},s_{ik},s_{jk}).
\]
For each pair the omitted maximum in \(a_{ij}\) is at most the sum of its triple depths.
Each unordered triple is counted three times, giving the finite comparison
\[
            0\le\log I_p^{\mathrm{good}}-E_p\le3T_p.                  \tag{3}
\]
This comparison and (2) allow both degree and input to vary.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## Why triple overlaps have sublinear weight for a fixed input

Let \(K\) be the splitting field and \(\Gamma\) the multiplicative group generated by all conjugates.
The quotient of this finitely generated group by its torsion embeds as a lattice in a real vector space.
The finite Galois group acts linearly and preserves a positive definite inner product obtained by averaging any such inner product.
The images of all conjugates have the same norm.
Three distinct points on that sphere cannot lie on one real affine line.
Hence when the three conjugates are pairwise distinct modulo torsion, the units \(u=\alpha_j/\alpha_i\) and \(v=\alpha_k/\alpha_i\) are multiplicatively independent.

For any prime \(p\), the finite places of \(K\) contribute to the normalized logarithm of the ideal gcd of \(u^p-1,v^p-1\) by their minimum valuations.
At a place above a good \(\ell\) this is exactly the triple depth, since \(\alpha_i\) is a unit and the least two of the three pair valuations agree.
More precisely the weight is \(e_w f_w\log\ell/[K:\mathbb Q]\) times that depth for \(v_w(\ell)=1\), where \(e_w,f_w\) are ramification index and residue degree.
After summing all triples, Galois invariance and \(\sum_{w\mid\ell}e_w f_w=[K:\mathbb Q]\) recover the rational-prime normalization in \(T_p\).
By [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), the full normalized finite-place log gcd is \(o_P(p)\) for each of the finitely many independent pairs in this calculation.
Indeed the pairs \((u^p,v^p)\) are independent and distinct as \(p\) varies, and their heights grow linearly with \(p\).

If two of the three original conjugates have a torsion ratio of order \(m>1\), take \(p\) larger than all such orders.
At a good \(\ell\) the two original reductions are distinct, so the reduction of their ratio has order greater than one dividing \(m\).
Its \(p\)-th power cannot be one.
This leaves triple depth zero for every such triple at every good prime.
It follows that \(T_p=o_P(p)\).

The powered conjugates are all distinct for these large \(p\), since equality would require a conjugate ratio of order \(p\).
Also \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\) for sufficiently large \(p\), and the \(p\)-power map on its residue field is injective, so \(p\nmid I_p\).
At each of the finitely many \(\ell\mid\operatorname{disc}P\), fix a residue field of the splitting field.
For sufficiently large primes \(p\), powering by \(p\) permutes this field's multiplicative group and \(p\ne\ell\).
Different original residues therefore give different powered residues.
For equal original residues the quotient of the two power differences is a unit because its reduction is \(p\bar\alpha_i^{\,p-1}\).
Thus these primes do not divide \(I_p\) either, and (3) proves \(\log I_p-E_p=o_P(p)\).

<a id="rate"></a>
## Computing the archimedean rate and its scope

In the product formula for \(I_p\), the original Vandermonde denominator is fixed.
Each numerator difference contributes
\[
       \log|\alpha_i^p-\alpha_j^p|
           =p\max(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)+O_P(\log(2p)).
\]
For unequal radii the error from the ratio of smaller to larger modulus is bounded.
For equal radii and a torsion ratio its \(p\)-th powers stay in a finite set away from one for sufficiently large primes.
For an equal-radius nontorsion ratio use the [unit-circle estimate for a power](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle).
Since \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\), summing the maxima gives \(\frac12\sum_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\).
The sum of the positive \(\lambda_i\) is \(h\).
For the lower bound sum \(\sum_j|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\ge d|\lambda_i|\) over \(i\).
For the upper bound use \(|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\le|\lambda_i|+|\lambda_j|\).
These prove the two constants in (1).
After removing monomial factors, an integer polynomial with content or an extreme coefficient of modulus at least two has multiplicative measure at least two.
Factoring a primitive polynomial with unit extreme coefficients into irreducibles then gives the unrestricted equivalence.

In contrast, at every degree-preserving prime the powered Vandermonde determinant satisfies
\[
        \log I_p\le \tfrac d2\log d+(d-1)p h .
\]
Indeed the norm of its row belonging to \(\beta_i\) is at most \(\sqrt d\max(1,|\beta_i|)^{d-1}\), and the original Vandermonde denominator has absolute value \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}P|}\ge1\).
In the [finite double-root comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), \(p\) is already chosen on the scale \((\log(3d))^4\).
The fixed-field gcd estimate gives no triple-overlap error uniform in that window, and the implicit constants in the archimedean lower estimate also depend on the input.
Even in the iterated limit (1), a lower bound independent of \(h\) remains to be found.
