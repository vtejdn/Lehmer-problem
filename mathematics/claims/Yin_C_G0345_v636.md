# Scalar multiples in rounded Laurent classes at two different expanding radii

<a id="statement"></a>
## Growth with the integer multiplier

Let \(P\) be the monic irreducible polynomial of an algebraic unit \(\alpha\), with no root on the unit circle.
Write \(d=\deg P\), \(m=\log M(P)>0\), and \(D=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\).
For \(x\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) write
\[
 E(x)=\min_{A(\alpha)=x}\int_{\mathbb T}|A/P|^2\,d\mu ,
 \qquad A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}],
\]
using normalized Haar measure and [nearest-integer minimization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#rounding).
Suppose that two conjugates \(\rho,\tau\) have \(\lambda=\log|\rho|>\nu=\log|\tau|>0\).
For every fixed nonzero \(x\),
\[
 \liminf_{\substack{|k|\to\infty\\ k\in\mathbb Z}}
       \frac{E(kx)}{\log\log |k|}
           \ \ge\ \frac1{D^2\log(m/\lambda)}>0 .                      \tag{1}
\]
One may take \(\rho\) of largest modulus.
Two distinct contracting moduli give the same result for the inverse conjugates.
In particular this divergence holds at every hyperbolic reciprocal strict degree record of degree greater than four, where a strict record is a least positive unit measure through its degree strictly below all values at lower degrees.
The lower bound depends on the fixed input.
At reciprocal quadratics the [classification of small scalar multiples](Yin_C_G0345_v633.md#statement) instead gives an infinite sequence with bounded rounded energy.

Here is a finite sparsity estimate underlying (1).
Write \(x_\rho,x_\tau\) for the images of \(x\) at the indicated embeddings.
For any Laurent representation of \(kx\), let \(H\) be the sum of its absolute coefficients and \(s\) its number of terms.
For \(k\ne0\), set \(b=m/\lambda>1,\ r=\lambda/\nu>1,\ L_s=(d\log H+\log 2)(b^{s-1}-1)/(\lambda(b-1))\).
Then
\[
 (r-1)\log |k|
 \ \le\ m L_s+(d-1+r)\log H+\log2+\log|x_\rho|-r\log|x_\tau| .       \tag{2}
\]

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Estimating the leading terms at one expanding embedding

Delete any nonempty subset of terms with zero value at \(\alpha\), and repeat until no such subset remains.
The represented algebraic number is unchanged and nonzero.
Bound all subsequent coefficient masses by the original \(H\) and all term counts by \(s\).
Write \(n_1>\cdots>n_t\) for the remaining exponents.
For the first \(i\) terms divide their Laurent polynomial by \(X^{n_i}\), obtaining an ordinary integral polynomial \(B_i\) of degree \(\ell_i=n_1-n_i\).
Its value at \(\alpha\) is a nonzero algebraic integer, since no selected prefix vanishes.
Its absolute field norm is at least one.
At each conjugate \(\sigma\) the polynomial has modulus at most \(H\max(1,|\sigma|)^{\ell_i}\).
The product over all conjugates other than \(\rho\) therefore gives
\[
                  |B_i(\rho)|\ge H^{-(d-1)}e^{-(m-\lambda)\ell_i}.
\]
The leading \(i\) terms at \(\rho\) have modulus at least \(H^{-(d-1)}e^{n_1\lambda-m\ell_i}\).
Stop if \(i=t\) or if the next gap \(g=n_i-n_{i+1}\) satisfies
\[
                  \lambda g\ge(m-\lambda)\ell_i+d\log H+\log2 .
\]
At such a gap the entire remaining tail has modulus at most \(H e^{\lambda n_{i+1}}\), at most half the lower bound for the prefix.
When the test fails, \(\ell_{i+1}< b\ell_i+(d\log H+\log2)/\lambda\).
Since \(\ell_1=0\), at the stopping position we have \(\ell_i\le L_s\).
The assumption on the two expanding radii gives \(m\ge\lambda+\nu>\lambda\), justifying the geometric sum in \(L_s\).
Thus
\[
 |kx_\rho|\ge \tfrac12 H^{-(d-1)}e^{n_1\lambda-m L_s},
 \qquad |kx_\tau|\le H e^{n_1\nu}.
\]
Eliminating \(n_1\) gives (2), also for negative exponents or complex embeddings.

<a id="energy"></a>
## Converting a sparse representation bound into an energy bound

Choose a numerator \(A=\sum_j a_jX^j\) realizing \(E(kx)\).
The original integer coefficients give
\[
               s\le H\le\sum_j a_j^2
                       \le D^2 E(kx)
\]
by Parseval and the definition of \(D\).
With \(P,x,\rho,\tau\) fixed, (2) gives \(\log|k|\le C b^s(1+\log H)\) for some fixed \(C>0\).
Thus \(E(kx)\to\infty\), and
\(\log\log|k|\le D^2 E(kx)\log b+O_{P,x,\rho,\tau}(1+\log(1+\log H))\).
The error is \(o(E(kx))\) by the coefficient bound, giving (1).
For contracting conjugates replace \(P\) by its monic reciprocal polynomial and \(A(X)\) by \(A(1/X)\).
The circle integral, \(D\) and \(m\) are preserved, and a unit and its inverse generate the same order.
For a reciprocal hyperbolic input the contracting moduli are the inverses of its expanding moduli.
The [two-circle descent](Yin_C_G0345_v587.md#degree) excludes a single expanding modulus at the indicated strict records, so the hypotheses of (1) apply there.
