# Two prescribed classes and a double root suffice for a near-linear degree comparison

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite minimum at primes of fourth-logarithmic size

Fix a monic nontorsion unit minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), put \(h=m(P)=\log M(P)\), and let
\[
                       y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil .
\]
For all sufficiently large \(d\), all but \(O(\log(3d)/\log y)\) primes in \((y,2y]\) preserve the degree when a root of \(P\) is raised to the \(p\)-th power.
There is at least one such prime.
For **every** degree-preserving prime in this interval and any two prescribed residues, take the nonzero integral multiples of \(P^2\) occupying exactly these residues, with degree at most \(p(16d+1)\) and with coprime rows in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Let \(R\) be the product of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P^2\), with multiplicities and ignoring \(x\) and scalar content.
The minimum exists and
\[
                    \frac{h}{3}\ \le\ \min_F\frac{m(R)}p\ \le\ 2h .      \tag{1}
\]
Thus an absolute positive lower bound for this finite minimum on all sufficiently large degree inputs is equivalent to a Lehmer gap.
One may select a single such prime for each input and always use the adjacent classes \(0,1\).
The witness degree is \(O(d(\log(3d))^4)\).

There is also an asymptotically sharp comparison for any fixed \(k\ge2\).
Prescribe \(k\) distinct residues at one of these same primes, take multiplicity \(p\) in place of two and degree at most \(p(dp^3+1)\), and define \(R\) using \(F/P^p\).
Uniformly in the input, prime and residues, with or without the coprime-row requirement, one has
\[
                        \frac{\min_F m(R)}{p^2 h}=\frac1k+O_k(1/p).      \tag{2}
\]
Both comparisons use no restrictions on the signature, coefficient heights or conjugate radii of \(P\).

<a id="rotation"></a>
## Bounding a determinant of rows by rotating the original polynomial

Let \(F(x)=\sum_{j=1}^l G_j(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]\setminus\{0\}\) have distinct nonempty rows \(G_j=x^{r_j}U_j(x^p)\), \(0\le r_j<p\), degree at most \(D\), and let \(E=x\,d/dx\).
Write
\[
             \det(E^i G_j)_{0\le i<l,\,1\le j\le l}
                     =x^{\sum r_j}W(x^p).
\]
The [distinct leading exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#determinant) make \(W\) nonzero.
We claim the coefficient-independent estimate
\[
                           m(W)\le l\,m(F)+O_l(\log(2+D)).                \tag{3}
\]
Choose a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\).
The \(l\) columns indexed by the \(r_j\) in the \(p\)-row matrix \((\zeta^{t r_j})_{0\le t<p}\) are orthogonal with squared norm \(p\).
By Cauchy–Binet the sum of squared absolute values of their \(l\)-row minors equals \(p^l\).
Some minor \(A\) has \(|\det A|\ge1\), since there are at most \(p^l\) choices.
Use its row indices \(t_1,\ldots,t_l\) and set \(F_j(x)=F(\zeta^{t_j}x)\).
The determinant of the columns \((E^i F_j)_i\) is the previous polynomial determinant times \(\det A\), up to the order of the columns.

For \(|x|=1\) let \(S(x)=\sum_{F(z)=0}|x-z|^{-1}\), with multiplicities.
Away from zeros, the root product for derivatives gives \(|F^{(u)}/F|\le S(x)^u\).
Indeed the sum over distinct root indices times \(u!\) is bounded in absolute value by the full \(u\)-th power of \(S\).
Expanding \(E^i\) in the operators \(x^u(d/dx)^u\) therefore gives \(|E^i F/F|\le C_l(1+S)^i\) for \(i<l\), with \(C_l\) depending only on \(l\).
The integral of \(|x-z|^{-1/2}\) over normalized circle measure is bounded by an absolute constant uniformly in \(z\).
For \(1/2\le |z|\le2\), this follows from \(|e^{it}-|z||^2=(1-|z|)^2+4|z|\sin^2(t/2)\) after rotation, and the other radii give a uniform pointwise bound.
Subadditivity of the square root and Jensen's inequality give
\[
              \int\log(1+S)\,d\mu\le2\log(1+C D).
\]
This is the same one-variable integrability used in the [logarithmic derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#resultant).
Factor \(F_j\) out of each column on the circle.
The determinant of the resulting logarithmic derivatives has log absolute value bounded above by
\(\log(l!)+l\log C_l+(l-1)\sum_j\log(1+S(\zeta^{t_j}x))\).
Integration gives (3) since \(m(F_j)=m(F)\), \(|\det A|\ge1\) and power substitution preserves \(m(W)\).
All zeros and poles omitted in the pointwise calculation cause only integrable logarithmic singularities.
In particular the error in (3) involves the logarithm of the degree even for dense rows.

<a id="finite"></a>
## The effective lower and upper estimates

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(P\), let \(\beta=\alpha^p\) have degree \(d\), and let \(Q\) be its minimal polynomial.
If \(P^t\mid F\), set \(c=\min_j\operatorname{ord}_\beta U_j\).
The [multiplicity estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#determinant) gives \(Q^{\,t+(l-1)c-l+1}\mid W\) when \(t\ge\max(1,l-1)\).
Since \(m(Q)=p h\), (3) gives
\[
               (t+(l-1)c-l+1)p h\ \le\ l\,m(F)+O_l(\log(2+D)).         \tag{4}
\]
For the upper bound, take base degree \(b\), degree cap \(D=p(b+1)\) and \(n=k(b+1)\) coefficient positions.
For \(t\) divided derivatives with \(dt<n\), the [integer box construction](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) gives some \(F_0\ne0\) on the allowed classes with \(P^t\mid F_0\) and
\[
       m(F_0)\le\frac{tD h+dt\log(6n)+dt(t-1)\log(1+D)/2}{n-dt}
                    +\log(2n).                                          \tag{5}
\]
The lower and upper estimates are both finite.

For the indicated range of primes and either degree cap in the statement, \(\log(2+D)=O(\log(3d))\).
[Voutier's theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\), so \(p h/\log(2+D)\to\infty\) uniformly.
No fixed-field convergence estimate is used.

For (1), choose \(k=2,\ b=16d,\ t=3\) in (5).
As \(dt/n<3/32\) and \(D/n=p/2\), it gives \(m(F_0)/(ph)\le48/29+o(1)<7/4\).
If there were only one row, (4) would give \(m(F_0)/(p h)\ge3-o(1)\).
For two rows, (4) gives \(2+c\le 2m(F_0)/(p h)+o(1)<4\), hence \(c\le1\).
Dividing the rows by their primitive integral gcd and removing scalar content produces an integral witness of no larger measure and degree, with two coprime rows and at least two copies of \(P\).
For any primitive witness containing \(P^2\), (4) gives \(m(F)/(p h)\ge1/2-o(1)\).
Since \(m(R)=m(F)-2h\), these estimates imply (1) for all sufficiently large \(d\).

For (2), choose \(b=dp^3,\ t=p+1\).
Here \(dt/n=O_k(p^{-2})\), and (5) gives
\[
                   m(F_0)/(p h)\le (p+1)/k+o_k(1).
\]
Indeed the denominator correction to \(t/k\) is \(O_k(1/p)\), and the remaining additive terms before division by \(p h\) are \(O_k(\log(2+D))\).
As in the [row retention argument](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#uniform), (4) first forces \(l=k\), then \((k-1)c\le k-1+o_k(1)\), hence \(c\le1\).
Gcd division therefore leaves all \(p\) required copies.
Conversely (4) with \(t=p\) bounds the measure of any primitive witness from below by \((p-k+1-o_k(1))p h/k\).
Subtracting \(p h\) and dividing by \(p^2 h\) proves (2).
Attainment of the minima follows from the finiteness of bounded-degree primitive integer polynomials \(R\) of bounded measure.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Choosing the prime from a short interval

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If \(p\nmid d\), then \(e=[K:\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)]\) is coprime to \(p\).
The [norm and Bézout argument](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power) gives either \(e=1\) or \(\zeta_p\in K\).
As in the [power-degree selection over a cyclotomic base](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#primes), the distinct prime-conductor fields contained in \(K\) have compositum of degree \(\prod(p-1)\) dividing \(d\), by the cyclotomic degree formula.
The primes dividing \(d\) have product at most \(d\).
Thus the bad primes in \((y,2y]\) have total logarithmic weight at most \(O(\log(3d))\), since \(\log p/\log(p-1)\le2\) for \(p\ge3\).
The [elementary dyadic prime estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units) gives total logarithmic weight at least \(c_0 y\) for large \(y\).
This proves the asserted count and existence.
A candidate is good exactly when the powered conjugates have degree \(d\), which can be tested using the minimal polynomial or its power resultant.

For a fixed choice such as the least good prime in this interval, (1) compares the finite quantity \(\min_F m(R)/p\) directly to \(h\).
The reduction to the unrestricted gap follows by factoring integer polynomials, since scalar content and nonunit extreme coefficients of absolute value at least two give a positive gap and bounded degree has a gap by coefficient finiteness.
An absolute positive lower bound for \(\min_F m(R)/p\) still has to be proved.
The double-root condition prevents interpreting (1) as a universal lower bound for sparse multiples containing only one copy of the input.
