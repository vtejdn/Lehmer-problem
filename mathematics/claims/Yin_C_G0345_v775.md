# Torsion order and the least power degree over the abelian closure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound independent of the common dilation

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), and let \(V>0\) be the area of its Newton polygon.
For any \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\) with \(z\ne0\) nontorsion, \(\xi,\eta\) roots of unity and \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), put
\[
   g=\gcd(|a|,|b|),\quad n=\max(|a|,|b|)/g,\quad
   w=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}\xi,\operatorname{ord}\eta),\quad
   s=[\mathbb Q(z)\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}:\mathbb Q].
\]
Write \(G_j(t)=(\log\log(5t))^3/(\log(2t))^j\) for \(t\ge1\).
Then
\[
      [\mathbb Q(z,\xi,\eta):\mathbb Q(z)]\le22V,\qquad
      \varphi(w)\le22V s,\qquad m(z)\gg_F\varphi(w)\,G_4(n).             \tag{1}
\]
When \(m(z)\le1\) and \(z\) is an algebraic integer the stronger \(m(z)\gg_F\varphi(w)\,G_3(n)\) holds.
Thus if \(m(z)\to0\), necessarily \(n\to\infty\) and
\[
                 \varphi(w)=o_F\big((\log n/\log\log n)^3\big).
\]
These estimates apply to the orders of both chosen translations themselves.
In particular all sections with bounded primitive norm have a uniform positive measure gap even for unbounded translations and arbitrary common dilations.

For \(n\to\infty\) let \(P\) be the rational conjugate product of the translated section with its monomial removed, as in the [individual factor-height theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement).
Every one of its nontorsion irreducible rational factors \(S\) satisfies
\[
                 \frac{\deg S}{\deg P}\gg_F G_4(n).                      \tag{2}
\]
Consequently there are \(O_F(1/G_4(n))\) such factors counted with multiplicity, independently of \(g,w\).
For a monic integral \(S\) with \(m(S)\le1\), \(G_3(n)\) applies to its degree fraction.
Neither estimate gives a constant degree fraction as \(n\to\infty\).

<a id="compression"></a>
## A power in the original field with exactly the same measure

For any nonzero nontorsion algebraic \(z\) put \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\).
For any \(j\ge1\), write \(D_j=[H(z^j):H]\) and \(l=[H(z):H(z^j)]\).
The field \(H(z)\) splits \(X^j-z^j\) over \(H(z^j)\), since \(H\) contains all roots of unity.
The automorphisms multiply \(z\) by a cyclic subgroup of \(\mu_j\) of order \(l\).
In particular \(l\mid j\) and
\[
            \delta=z^l\in H(z^j),\qquad H(\delta)=H(z^j).
\]
The second assertion follows since \(\delta^{j/l}=z^j\).
The ordinary degree \([K:\mathbb Q(\delta)]\) is at most \(l\) by this power equation for \(z\) over \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\), and at least \(l\) after extending scalars to \(H(\delta)\).
Thus \(m(\delta)=m(z)\).
For any finite number field \(L\), \([HL:H]=[L:L\cap H]\) by Galois base change, or by restriction to a finite Galois closure and taking the subgroup product with the normal subgroup fixing its abelian part.
The degrees for \(K\) and \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\) now give
\[
   \mathbb Q(\delta)\cap H=K\cap H,\qquad
   [\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]=sD_j,\qquad m(z)=sD_j h(\delta).         \tag{3}
\]
This extends the [least abelian power calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v310.md#abelian) from \(D_j=1\) to every relative degree.
It selects an actual power in the original field and preserves both its total measure and its abelian subfield.
The [relative class-product compression](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#relative-compression) separately preserves the relative measure while choosing a product of a torsion-equivalence class.

The [Amoroso–Delsinne bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) applied to \(\delta\) gives \(m(z)\gg sG_4(D_j)\).
If \(z\) is integral and \(m(z)\le1\), then \(\delta\) is integral and (3) permits the [cubic abelian-subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#statement) at \(\delta\), giving \(m(z)\gg sG_3(D_j)\).
In particular both bounds hold at the minimum relative degree over *all* powers of \(z\), even without a plane curve.
Along a shrinking sequence that minimum tends to infinity.

<a id="phases"></a>
## How much of the torsion field lies in the root field

Fix one complex embedding and consider \(F(z^aU,z^bV)\) in the two new variables.
Its Newton polygon has area \(V\) and no geometric component is a translate of a one-dimensional subtorus.
Indeed the geometric components of \(F\) are conjugate, and a binomial component would force every conjugate to use the same exponent direction, contradicting positive area.
Translation of the variables preserves that property.
The [Beukers–Smyth bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) gives at most \(22V\) torsion points on this translated curve, irrespective of its coefficient field.
The distinct conjugates of \((\xi,\eta)\) over \(K\) are among them, proving the first assertion in (1).
The cyclotomic field \(L=\mathbb Q(\xi,\eta)\) has degree \(\varphi(w)\); its compositum with \(K\) has degree \([L:L\cap K]\) over \(K\), and \(L\cap K\subseteq H\cap K\).
This gives the second assertion.

Over \(H\), the element \(z^g\) satisfies \(F(\xi T^{a/g},\eta T^{b/g})=0\).
This nonzero Laurent polynomial has width \(O_F(n)\), even when some projected terms cancel.
It cannot vanish identically since the translated one-dimensional torus would then be a geometric component of \(F\).
It follows that \(D_g\ll_F n\).
For \(1\le D_g\ll_F n\) one has \(G_j(D_g)\gg_F G_j(n)\), by eventual monotonicity and positivity on the remaining bounded interval.
Apply (3) with \(j=g\) to obtain the measure assertions.
For \(m(z)\to0\), the primitive minimal polynomial eventually has absolute leading and constant coefficients one, so the integral version applies without a separate assumption along the sequence.

Finally the [exact factor-height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(h(z)\asymp_F 1/(gn)\) uniformly at \(n\to\infty\).
The norm polynomial has degree \(\varphi(w)w_A(a,b)\asymp_F\varphi(w)gn\), where \(A\) is the support of \(F\).
Using \(m(z)=\deg S\,h(z)\) yields (2), also when a root belongs to a coefficient-conjugate section since conjugation preserves the orders.
