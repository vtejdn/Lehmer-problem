# Torsion ratios and individual Mahler measures on every sparse section

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound from all minimal vanishing subsums

Fix \(s\ge2\), distinct integers \(n_1,\ldots,n_s\), and nonzero rational numbers \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\).
Suppose \(z\ne0\) has infinite multiplicative order and \(\sum_{i=1}^s a_i\xi_i z^{n_i}=0\) for roots of unity \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_s\).
Then \(z\) is algebraic since the nonzero Laurent polynomial has algebraic coefficients.
Call a nonempty set \(B\) of indices a block if its terms sum to zero and no nonempty proper subset of them sums to zero.
Use **all** blocks of this particular sum, including overlapping ones, and set
\[
 w_* = \operatorname{lcm}_{B,\ i,j\in B}\operatorname{ord}(\xi_i/\xi_j),
 \qquad W_* = \min_B \frac{\max_{i\in B} n_i-\min_{i\in B} n_i}{\gcd_{i,j\in B}|n_i-n_j|}.
\]
Each block has at least two indices and the exponents are distinct, so \(W_*\) is a positive integer.
Let \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\), \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), \(L=\mathbb Q(\mu_{w_*})\), and \(G_j(t)=(\log\log(5t))^3/(\log(2t))^j\) for \(t\ge1\).
There are constants depending only on \(s\) such that
\[
 [KL:K]\le C_s,\qquad [K\cap L:\mathbb Q]\ge \varphi(w_*)/C_s,
 \qquad m(z)\gg_s \varphi(w_*)G_4(W_*).
\]
Here \(m(z)=[K:\mathbb Q]h(z)\) is the absolute logarithmic Mahler measure.
For integral \(z\) with \(m(z)\le1\) the stronger bound \(m(z)\gg_s\varphi(w_*)G_3(W_*)\) holds.
The constants are uniform in the coefficients, exponents, phases and common dilation of the exponents, with no exceptional set of torsion parameters.
In particular the estimate applies at exceptional parameters of a [generic section theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v863.md#statement) whenever the actual section has the displayed distinct exponents and rational coefficients times roots of unity.
Along a sequence of shrinking measures with bounded term count one must have \(W_*\to\infty\).

<a id="ratios"></a>
## Conjugates of the ratios

Fix an anchor \(b\in B\).
After division by \(-a_b\xi_b z^{n_b}\), the other terms give an equation with right-hand side one and variables \(\xi_i/\xi_b\) for \(i\in B\setminus\{b\}\).
Its coefficients \(-a_i z^{n_i-n_b}/a_b\) lie in \(K^\times\), and no nonempty subsum on the left vanishes by minimality.
For \(d=|B|-1\), the tuple belongs to \(\mu_\infty^d\), a rank-zero group.
When \(d=1\) the equation determines the ratio uniquely.
For \(d\ge2\) the [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt bound](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) gives at most \(N_s=\lceil\exp((6s)^{3s})\rceil\) such solutions, uniformly in these coefficients.
Each distinct embedding over \(K\) of the field generated by the tuple and \(K\) gives a different nondegenerate solution.
Thus adjoining all the ratios in this block costs degree at most \(N_s\), because every pairwise ratio is a quotient of two anchor ratios.
There are at most \(2^s\) blocks, and the compositum degree over \(K\) is bounded by the product of the degrees, giving \(C_s=N_s^{2^s}\).
The subgroup generated by all these roots of unity is cyclic of order \(w_*\), so their field over \(\mathbb Q\) is precisely \(L\).
Since \(L/\mathbb Q\) is Galois, \([KL:K]=[L:K\cap L]\), proving the claimed intersection estimate.

<a id="width"></a>
## Removing the common exponent divisor inside a block

For any block \(B\) let \(g=\gcd_{i,j\in B}|n_i-n_j|\), let \(a=\min_{i\in B}n_i\), and denote its normalized width by \(W_B\).
After dividing by \(z^a\), its relation gives a polynomial over \(H\) annihilating \(z^g\), with exponents \((n_i-a)/g\).
Distinctness gives nonzero constant and leading coefficients and degree exactly \(W_B\), hence \(D_g=[H(z^g):H]\le W_B\).
With \(s_K=[K\cap H:\mathbb Q]\), the [cyclic compression identity](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#compression) produces a power \(\delta\) of \(z\) satisfying
\[
 H(\delta)=H(z^g),\qquad [\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]=s_K D_g,
 \qquad m(\delta)=m(z)=s_K D_g h(\delta).
\]
It preserves integrality when \(z\) is integral.
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) at \(\delta\) gives \(m(z)\gg s_K G_4(D_g)\).
In the stated integral range the [cubic abelian-subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#statement) at the same power gives \(m(z)\gg s_K G_3(D_g)\).
For \(j=3,4\), \(G_j(D)\gg G_j(W)\) throughout \(1\le D\le W\).
Indeed the logarithmic derivative with respect to \(\log t\) is \(3/(\log(5t)\log\log(5t))-j/\log(2t)\), negative for sufficiently large \(t\), and each function is positive and continuous on the remaining compact interval.
Choose a block achieving \(W_*\) and use \(s_K\ge[K\cap L:\mathbb Q]\) from the joint ratio bound.
This choice permits the narrowest block to control the relative degree while every block contributes its phase ratios to the conductor.

<a id="locus"></a>
## Independent phase scalings

Join two indices whenever some block contains both.
The ratios along edges generate all phase ratios within each connected component by multiplication along paths.
Thus for a connected graph \(w_*\) is the order of the group generated by all \(\xi_i/\xi_1\).
The overall scalar is arbitrary.

For fixed \(z\), coefficients and exponents, the phase locus in \(\mu_\infty^s\) is a union of at most \(\operatorname{Bell}(s)N_s^s\) sets of torsion points of torsion cosets.
Here \(\operatorname{Bell}(s)\) counts the partitions of the index set.
Indeed one can partition any vanishing sum into minimal blocks by removing successively a minimal zero subsum.
The remainder still vanishes.
For each partition and each of the at most \(N_s\) normalized nondegenerate tuples on each block, restore a separate free scalar on every block.
The result is the torsion part of a translate of the subtorus of tuples constant on each block, and all its points solve the original equation.
Conversely the removal procedure recovers every solution in this union, even if its additional minimal blocks overlap the partition.

For example take \(z=2\) and \((2-z)+\eta z^2(2-z)=0\) with any nonreal root of unity \(\eta\).
The coefficients in increasing exponent order are \(2,-1,2,-1\), and the phases are \(1,1,\eta,\eta\).
Since \(1,\eta\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), the two displayed pairs are exactly the minimal vanishing blocks.
Their normalized widths are one, \(w_*=1\), and \(m(z)=\log 2\) while the order of the phase ratio between the pairs is unbounded.
Thus even after fixing one global phase, a uniform estimate proportional to the totient of the order of all phase ratios fails in general for disconnected blocks.
