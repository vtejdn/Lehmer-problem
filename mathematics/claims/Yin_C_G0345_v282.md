# Rational Salem-signature models with integral endpoint values and discriminants

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single exterior pair under every prescribed finite torsion test

For arbitrary integers \(q\ge2,\ J\ge1\) and real \(\epsilon>0\), there are monic reciprocal polynomials \(P\in\mathbb Q[Z]\) of arbitrarily large even degree \(d=2n\) with the following properties.

* All roots are simple, exactly two are off the unit circle, and they are positive reciprocals \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\), with \(0<a=\log\lambda<\epsilon\).
* The first \(q-1\) coefficients below the leading coefficient are integers, and the coefficient at depth \(q\) is nonintegral.
* Write \(P(Z)=Z^n S(Z+Z^{-1})\) with \(S\) monic of degree \(n\).
  The numbers \(\operatorname{disc}S,\operatorname{disc}P,P(1),P(-1)\) are nonzero integers and \(P(1)=-1\).
* For every root of unity \(\zeta\) of order at most \(J\), \(P(\zeta)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer.
  Consequently every \(\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)\), \(1\le j\le J\), is a nonzero integer.
* Within such a sequence the measure \(a\) can be held fixed and
  \[
            \frac{|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}}d\longrightarrow e^{2a}.       \tag{1}
  \]
* Exactly one irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q\) is noncyclotomic, and its degree is \(2q\).
  Its primitive integral representative has logarithmic Mahler measure \(\log D+a\), with the even integer \(D\) chosen in the construction below.

Thus the ordinary normalized discriminant upper bound is asymptotically attained with just one exterior pair.
Taking a diagonal sequence allows \(q,J\to\infty\) and \(a\to0\), while the quantity in (1) tends to one.
These are rational reducible models.
Their explicitly located nonintegral coefficient supplies the missing full integrality condition, and their cyclotomic factors supply torsion quotients between distinct roots.
They exhibit the limitation of the listed numerical and finite algebraic tests in a possible proof for integral Salem polynomials.

<a id="construction"></a>
## A small rational perturbation of a trace endpoint

Use the integer polynomials \(C_m\), monic at every positive index, specified by
\[
 C_0=2,\quad C_1=X,\quad C_{m+1}=XC_m-C_{m-1},\qquad
 C_m(2\cos t)=2\cos(mt).
\]
Also write \(U_j(X/2)\) for the monic integer polynomial of degree \(j\) with
\[
 U_0(X/2)=1,\quad U_1(X/2)=X,\qquad
 U_j(\cos t)=\frac{\sin((j+1)t)}{\sin t}.
\]
Its recurrence is the same as for \(C_m\), with these initial values.
Put
\[
 F_q(X)=(X-2)U_{q-1}(X/2),\qquad A(X)=F_q(X)-1/D,\qquad D=2k.
\]
The roots of \(F_q\) are the distinct numbers \(2\) and \(2\cos(\pi j/q)\), \(1\le j<q\).
For every sufficiently large integer \(k\), continuity of its simple roots shows that \(A\) has \(q-1\) simple roots \(y_j\in(-2,2)\) and a simple root \(T>2\).
Since \(F_q'(2)=q\), one has \(T-2\sim1/(Dq)\) as \(k\to\infty\) at fixed \(q\).
Writing \(T=2\cosh a\) therefore gives \(a\sim(Dq)^{-1/2}\).
Choose \(k\) odd and large enough that \(0<a<\epsilon\), and keep \(q,k\) fixed for the rest of the construction.

Choose an even positive integer \(N\) divisible by every integer from \(1\) to \(J\), and set
\[
 h(X)=U_{k-1}(C_N(X)/2),\qquad t=\deg h=N(k-1).
\]
Its roots are simple and lie in \((-2,2)\).
Indeed the \(k-1\) target values \(2\cos(\pi j/k)\) are distinct and in \((-2,2)\), and each is assumed at exactly \(N\) simple points of that interval by \(C_N\).
For a root \(x=z+z^{-1}\) of \(h\), one has \(z^{2kN}=1\).
Choose \(R\) divisible by \(2kN D^4\), with \(R\ge q+t\), and form
\[
               S=A h C_R,\qquad n=q+t+R,\qquad P(Z)=Z^nS(Z+Z^{-1}).       \tag{2}
\]
Every root of \(C_R\) lies in \((-2,2)\) and is simple.
At a root of \(h\) one has \(C_R=2\), so the two factors are coprime.
Every root of \(h C_R\) is an algebraic integer.
It cannot be a root of \(A\), since \(F_q\) would then take the rational nonintegral value \(1/D\) at an algebraic integer.
Thus \(S\) and \(P\) are separable and have the asserted root locations.
The same argument shows that none of the roots of \(A\) is a trace of a root of unity.

Since \(F_q h C_R\) is monic integral of degree \(n\) and \(h C_R\) is monic integral of degree \(n-q\), the coefficient of \(S\) at depth \(q\) is an integer minus \(1/D\), and all earlier coefficients are integers.
The map \(S\mapsto Z^n S(Z+Z^{-1})\) is triangular with integral entries and leading diagonal one at these coefficient depths.
It therefore gives the same first nonintegral depth in \(P\).

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## An irreducible core and the exact arithmetic measure

The reversal of \(D F_q-1\) is Eisenstein at \(2\).
Its leading coefficient \(D F_q(0)-1\) is odd, its other coefficients are divisible by \(2\), and its constant coefficient \(D=2k\) is not divisible by \(4\).
Thus \(A\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(\mathbb Q(T)\) is totally real of degree \(q\).
The polynomial \(Z^2-TZ+1\) is irreducible over this field.
Indeed an embedding taking \(T\) to any \(y_j\in(-2,2)\) takes \(T^2-4\) to a negative number, whereas a square in a totally real field is nonnegative at every real embedding.
The positive outer root therefore has degree \(2q\), with monic rational minimal polynomial
\[
                       B(Z)=Z^q A(Z+Z^{-1}).
\]
Its integral representative \(D B\) is primitive, since all coefficients apart from its central coefficient are divisible by \(D\), while that central coefficient is congruent to \(-1\) modulo \(D\).
The leading coefficient is \(D\), and only \(\lambda\) is exterior.
Consequently
\[
                           \log M(D B)=\log D+a.
\]
The lifted factors associated with \(h\) and \(C_R\) are monic integral with all roots roots of unity, so they are products of cyclotomic polynomials.
Thus the noncyclotomic core is irreducible even when \(q\to\infty\), and its arithmetic Mahler measure includes the displayed leading-coefficient contribution.
The coordinator supplied the odd-\(k\) Eisenstein specialization and this exact arithmetic comparison.

<a id="integer-tests"></a>
## Endpoints, torsion values and denominator absorption

If \(\zeta\) has order at most \(J\) and \(x=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), then \(C_N(x)=C_R(x)=2\) and \(h(x)=k\).
Consequently
\[
                         S(x)=D F_q(x)-1 .                            \tag{3}
\]
This is a nonzero algebraic integer, since equality to zero would make the algebraic integer \(F_q(x)\) equal to \(1/D\).
Multiplication by the unit \(\zeta^n\) proves the asserted property of \(P(\zeta)\), and its norm proves the resultant statement.
At the endpoints \(h(2)=h(-2)=k\) and \(C_R(2)=C_R(-2)=2\), since \(N,R\) are even.
Hence
\[
                       S(2)=-1,\qquad S(-2)=D F_q(-2)-1
\]
are nonzero integers, and the endpoint conclusions for \(P\) follow.

For the discriminant, the elementary cosine-node formula is
\[
                         \operatorname{disc}C_R=2^{R-1}R^R.            \tag{4}
\]
For example, differentiating \(C_R(2\cos\theta)\) at the roots with
\(\theta=(2j-1)\pi/(2R)\) gives derivative magnitude \(R/|\sin\theta|\).
The product of these sines is \(2^{1-R}\), from evaluation of \(Z^R+1\) at \(1\) and pairing the unit roots, giving (4).
The absolute discriminant of the product in (2) is
\[
 |\operatorname{disc}S|
 =|\operatorname{disc}A|\,|\operatorname{disc}h|\,2^{R-1}R^R
   |\operatorname{Res}(A,h)|^2|\operatorname{Res}(A,C_R)|^2\,2^{2t}.      \tag{5}
\]
The last factor follows from \(C_R=2\) on the roots of \(h\).
The discriminant of \(A\), a polynomial of total coefficient degree \(2q-2\), has denominator dividing \(D^{2q-2}\).
The two displayed resultants have denominators dividing \(D^t,D^R\), respectively, since a resultant is homogeneous of the opposite degree in each coefficient set.
Thus all denominators in (5) divide \(D^{2q-2+2t+2R}\).
Because \(D^4\mid R\) and \(R\ge q+t\), the integer \(R^R\) is divisible by this power of \(D\).
Formula (5) is therefore a nonzero integer.
Finally the reciprocal discriminant identity gives
\[
             |\operatorname{disc}P|
                   =|S(2)S(-2)|\,|\operatorname{disc}S|^2,
\]
which proves full discriminant integrality as well.
This identity follows by taking the squared differences within each reciprocal pair and between each two distinct pairs.

<a id="saturation"></a>
## The normalized discriminant along large cosine factors

For the fixed roots \(y_j=2\cos\theta_j\) of \(A\) inside the interval, there are arbitrarily large multiples \(R\) of \(2kN D^4\) with
\[
                         R\theta_j\longrightarrow0\pmod {2\pi}
                               \quad\text{for every }j.
\]
The elementary simultaneous approximation follows by placing sufficiently many multiples of the angle vector in equal boxes of a torus and subtracting two in the same box.
If the resulting integer multiples were bounded, one fixed multiple would be an exact period, whose arbitrarily large multiples also give the conclusion.
Along this subsequence \(C_R(y_j)\to2\), whereas \(C_R(T)=2\cosh(Ra)\).
It follows that
\[
               \log|\operatorname{Res}(A,C_R)|=Ra+O_{q,k}(1).
\]
All other factors in (5) except (4) are constant in \(R\).
Consequently
\[
 \log|\operatorname{disc}S|=R\log R+(R-1)\log2+2Ra+O_{q,k,N}(1).
\]
Divide by \(n=R+q+t\), apply the reciprocal discriminant identity, and subtract \(\log(2n)\).
The limit is \(2a\), proving (1).

The [radial pair bound](Yin_C_G0345_v280.md#radial) loses density when only one pair is exterior.
The present models show that the ordinary discriminant bound can be asymptotically saturated in that case, while its integer floor and any prescribed finite list of torsion norm inequalities all hold.
The [trace endpoint bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative) also hold for these models at the level of the displayed endpoint product.
Their field-theoretic use of a full integral minimal polynomial remains an additional input.
