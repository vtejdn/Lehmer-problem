# The exact cost of taking the real norm of the one exterior complex pair

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two block actions give different real norm costs

Fix \(M>0\) and let \(\alpha\) be an exterior root of a reciprocal irreducible unit polynomial with positive total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(\alpha)\le M\).
Assume that its only exterior roots are the nonreal conjugate pair \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\), and that distinct roots have nontorsion quotients.
Write \(2n=\deg\alpha\), with \(2n\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M\) for the [canonical alternating blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) and the [two alternatives on the actual roots](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement), taking the block size \(b\ge9\).
There are \(u\) blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs, \(n=ub\).
The hypotheses apply to the compressed input itself: if one first takes torsion-class products, the two nonreal exterior roots must still be present after compression.
No strict degree record is required.

Set \(R=|\alpha|>1,\ \beta=\alpha\bar\alpha=R^2,\ K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(E=K\bar K=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\bar\alpha)\), using the chosen complex embedding.
Thus \(M(\alpha)=\beta\) in multiplicative normalization, and \(m(\alpha)=2\log R\).
In the two alternatives the absolute degree and logarithmic measure of this real number as an algebraic unit are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & [\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q] & [E:K] & m(\beta)/m(\alpha)\\ \hline
 \text{independent even inversions}&2n(b-1)&2(b-1)&2b-3\\
 \text{canonical oriented halves}&n(b-1)&b-1&b-1 .
\end{array}                                                               \tag{1}
\]
In both cases \([E:\mathbb Q(\beta)]=2\), \(N_{E/\mathbb Q(\beta)}(\alpha)=\beta\) and \(\alpha+\bar\alpha\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
In particular the norm giving the positive real number is taken after a field extension of the size in (1).
The absolute logarithmic measure of this norm grows by a factor linear in \(b\), even though the norm field index in \(E\) is only two.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The two special labels and their stabilizers

Let \(G\) be the splitting group and let \(c\) be ordinary complex conjugation.
On inverse-pair labels \(c\) exchanges the two labels of \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) and fixes all others, whose roots lie on the circle.
Those two labels belong to the same block: a transposition between different blocks cannot preserve their partition when \(b\ge2\).
On the label action there is an independent \(A_b\) on each block, and the blocks are permuted transitively.
Put \(N=u\binom b2\), the number of unordered pairs of distinct labels lying in a common block.

In the independent inversion case the \(G\)-orbit of \(\beta\) consists of the four possible products of oriented roots on each of the \(N\) pairs.
The alternating action realizes every target pair, and any two required sign choices there can be made by an even inversion in its block, using an unselected third label when necessary.
These \(4N\) products are distinct by the multiplicative independence in this alternative.
The ordered pair \((\alpha,\bar\alpha)\) also admits either order at each target; double transitivity on a block and the same sign correction realize every ordered choice.
The ordered orbit therefore has size \(8N\), equal to \([E:\mathbb Q]\), since its stabilizer is the subgroup fixing both generators of \(E\).

In the oriented case select one of the two canonical inverse halves in every block and denote its representatives by \(a_{B,i}\), \(1\le i\le b\).
Choose the half containing \(\alpha=a_{B_0,1}\) in the distinguished block.
The sign of the action transporting a given half is common to the whole half.
Since \(c\) fixes at least one neutral label in each block and inverts its representative, it preserves each block and swaps its two halves.
In particular \(\bar\alpha=a_{B_0,2}^{-1}\) after numbering the paired label second.
At the chosen embedding the logs of the representative moduli are \(\log R,-\log R,0,\ldots,0\) in \(B_0\) and zero on all other blocks.
Their sum in each block is zero, and every Galois element transports a block sum up to a common sign.
Each oriented block product is consequently a unit all of whose conjugates have modulus one, hence torsion.
The [rank lower bound \(n-u\)](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank) shows that the \(u\) independent block constants give the entire rational relation space in these coordinates.

Here \(\beta=a_{B_0,1}/a_{B_0,2}\).
Its conjugates run over all \(2N\) ratios of distinct representatives in a common block, with the order of the two indices specified.
The supported alternating actions on the [canonical halves](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) realize all such orders, and transport to the other half just inverts a ratio.
Two different ratio vectors cannot agree modulo the relations, since they have coordinate sum zero on each block, whereas a block constant with zero sum is zero.
Thus the \(2N\) values are distinct.
For the ordered pair of actual roots, the first root can belong to either half, and after its selection the second has \(b-1\) choices in the opposite half, excluding the same label.
Transitivity on the \(2u\) halves and their alternating actions give all these choices, for an ordered orbit of size \(2n(b-1)=4N\).

In either alternative the ordered orbit has twice the product orbit size.
Complex conjugation acts nontrivially on \(E\), fixes \(\beta\) and exchanges the two exterior roots.
Hence \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) is its fixed field in \(E\), giving the norm, the sum and the degree assertions.
These statements identify a real embedded field and do not require it to be totally real.

<a id="heights"></a>
## Absolute heights of all rational monomials

Choose one actual representative \(a_{B,i}\) per inverse pair, using the canonical halves in the oriented case.
In the independent case the choices are arbitrary and may include both exterior roots.
Let \(x\) be any unit in the divisible conjugate group, written as
\[
                  x^q=\xi\prod_{B,i} a_{B,i}^{\,p_{B,i}},
  \qquad q\ge1,\quad p_{B,i}\in\mathbb Z,\quad \xi\ \text{a root of unity}.
\]
With \(t_{B,i}=p_{B,i}/q\), its absolute logarithmic Weil height is exactly
\[
 h(x)=\frac{\log R}{2N}
  \begin{cases}
   \displaystyle\sum_B\ \sum_{i<j}\max(|t_{B,i}|,|t_{B,j}|),
           &\text{in the independent case},\\[4pt]
   \displaystyle\sum_B\ \sum_{i<j}|t_{B,i}-t_{B,j}|,
           &\text{in the oriented case}.
  \end{cases}                                                              \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the height of a unit is one half of the mean of the absolute logarithms of its conjugate moduli.
The torsion factor has no effect, and homogeneity gives the indicated rational coefficients regardless of the actual degree of the radical \(x\).
It suffices to average the ordinary integral monomial over uniform \(g\in G\) and divide by \(q\).
The inverse image of the two distinguished labels under \(g\) is uniform over the \(N\) unordered pairs.
Only the two coefficients on that pair enter the logarithm at the chosen embedding.
In the independent case the four sign combinations are equally frequent conditional on the pair and its ordering, by the even sign changes on these labels with a possible third correction.
For real \(t,t'\) the mean of \(|\pm t\pm t'|\) over those signs is \(\max(|t|,|t'|)\).
In the oriented case the exterior logs of the two representatives have opposite signs, and transport changes both signs together or interchanges them.
The absolute value of the combination is therefore always \((\log R)|t-t'|\).
This gives (2).
Changing oriented coefficients by a rational constant on a block preserves its contribution; subtracting the average in each block gives the unique representative with every block sum zero.

For \(\beta\) in the independent case use coefficients \(1,1\) on the special pair, taking both exterior roots as representatives, and zero elsewhere.
Exactly \(2b-3\) unordered pairs meet those two labels.
Its degree \(4N\) in (2) gives \(m(\beta)=2(2b-3)\log R\).
For the oriented case the two coefficients are \(1,-1\) and the remaining coefficients vanish.
The sum of the absolute differences is \(2+2(b-2)=2b-2\).
The degree \(2N\) gives \(m(\beta)=2(b-1)\log R\).
This proves the remaining entries of (1).
The formulas quantify this proposed passage from one exterior complex pair to a positive real norm; they leave the possibility of arbitrarily small positive measures of the original inputs open.
