# The limiting exterior radii and an excluded range of cubic slopes

<a id="statement"></a>
## The distribution for one factor and a lower bound for every factor

Use the [critical section](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) with \(s\to\infty\), \(s\le r=b/a\le Cs^3\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(a\ge2\).
Suppose \(P\) is a nontorsion rational irreducible factor with \(k\le Ka\) exterior roots, where \(K,C>0\) are fixed.
Write \(d=\deg P\), \(D=d/r\), \(\mathbb T=\{z:|z|=1\}\) and \(\langle z\rangle=z/|z|\).
Let \(I=\{u\in\mathbb T:|1+u|<1\}\), give the circle normalized arclength \(du\), and put \(w(u)=-\log|1+u|\) on \(I\setminus\{-1\}\).
For every continuous \(f\) on \(\mathbb T\times[0,\infty)\) of at most polynomial growth in its second argument,
\[
       \frac{1}{sD}\sum_{\substack{P(x)=0\\ |x|>1}}
             f(\langle x^{-a}\rangle,\,as\log |x|)
                           \longrightarrow \int_I f(u,w(u))\,du .              \tag{1}
\]
In particular with \(J_2=\int_I w(u)^2\,du\) one has
\[
        \sum_{P(x)=0}(\log|x|)^2
                       = (2J_2+o(1))\,\frac{d}{a^2rs}.
\]
The same \(J_2\) gives an unconditional exclusion for the exterior count in the entire cubic range.
For all nontorsion irreducible factors in any sequence with \(s\le r\le Cs^3\), without a cap on \(k\),
\[
             \liminf_{s\to\infty}\frac{k}{a}\ \ge\
                           \frac{1}{\sqrt{180 C J_2}} .                         \tag{2}
\]
Consequently fixed constants satisfying \(CK^2<1/(180J_2)\) admit no critical factor at sufficiently large \(s\).

<a id="seed"></a>
## The radii and angles of the trinomial zeros

Let \(\zeta\) run over all zeros of \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\).
All their moduli tend to one uniformly.
For exterior zeros the equation \(|\zeta|^s=|1+\zeta|\le2|\zeta|\) suffices.
For an interior zero put \(w=-s\log|\zeta|\); then \(e^{-w}=|1+\zeta|\ge 1-e^{-w/s}\), which gives \(w=O(\log(2s))\) since \(|\zeta|>1/2\).
For each fixed positive integer \(j<s-1\), Newton's identities give \(\sum \zeta^j=0\) because the first \(s-2\) coefficients after the leading term vanish.
Uniform control of the moduli therefore gives \(\sum\langle\zeta\rangle^j/s\to0\) for each nonzero integer \(j\), using conjugation for negative \(j\).
Trigonometric approximation proves angular convergence to \(du\).

Away from \(u=-1\) and the two points \(|1+u|=1\), the classification of \(\zeta\) as interior eventually agrees with \(\langle\zeta\rangle\in I\), uniformly on compact subsets, and the scaled interior depth differs from \(w(\langle\zeta\rangle)\) by \(o(1)\) there.
This follows from \(-s\log|\zeta|=-\log|1+\zeta|\).
On restricting to bounded tests, the three points can be omitted using angular convergence and arcs of arbitrarily small length.
For polynomial growth use the [Jensen bound near minus one](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles),
\[
       \#\{\zeta:|\zeta|<1,\ -s\log|\zeta|\ge t\}\ll s e^{-t}\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
It gives uniformly vanishing tails for every fixed moment after division by \(s\).
The limiting arclength has the same tail bound up to a constant because \(1+u\) has a simple zero on the circle.
Thus the average of \(f(\langle\zeta\rangle,-s\log|\zeta|)\) over interior zeros, with weight \(1/s\), converges to the right side of (1).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Transfer to a factor and the universal square bound

Write \(k_j\) for the exterior count at the interior zero \(\zeta_j\) and \(w_j=-s\log|\zeta_j|\).
The [unweighted occupation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v543.md#statement) at \(q=1\) gives
\[
         \frac1s\sum_j |k_j/D-1|\ll s^{-1/32}\sqrt{\log(2s)}.
\]
Since \(\max_j w_j=O(\log(2s))\), inserting the weights \(k_j/D\) changes the normalized average of every fixed polynomial-growth test on the seed zeros by \(o(1)\).
The exterior samples in (1) differ from their assigned \((\langle\zeta_j\rangle,w_j)\) by \(O(se^{-c s})\) in the sum of the circle and radial distances, by [localization at the occupied poles](Yin_C_G0345_v543.md#variance).
This preserves the limit for bounded radial arguments by uniform continuity on compact sets.
For unbounded arguments the just-proved weighted tail bound for polynomial majorants, and the same pointwise distance estimate, permit truncation with uniformly small error.
This proves (1).

For \(f(u,w)=w^2\), (1) gives the exterior square sum \((J_2+o(1))D/(a^2s)\).
The reciprocal factor has the same sum over the interior roots and zero contribution on the circle.
The [all-root square bound for a nontorsion unit](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) is \(d\sum_{P(x)=0}(\log|x|)^2\ge1/10\).
It follows that \(d\ge(1-o(1))a\sqrt{rs/(20J_2)}\).
Using the [individual exterior fraction](Yin_C_G0345_v543.md#statement) now gives
\[
                 \frac ka \ge (1-o(1))\sqrt{\frac{s^3}{180J_2 r}}.
\]
For an arbitrary sequence with finite \(\liminf k/a\), take a subsequence tending to the lower limit.
It has a fixed finite cap \(K\), so the preceding estimate applies there and \(r\le Cs^3\) gives (2).
An infinite lower limit satisfies (2) directly.
