# Heights and degrees of products of roots on steep quadratic sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniformity in the powers and the chosen factors

Take the [quadratic sections](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement)
\[
 P_{k,l}(X)=A(X^k)X^{2l}+X^{2k+l}+A^*(X^k),\qquad
 A(U)=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1),\quad A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U).
\]
Let \(k,l\) be positive coprime integers with \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Set \(C=\log(\beta_3\beta_4)\), where \(\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\).
For any fixed \(t\ge1\), choose roots \(r_1,\ldots,r_t\) of the entire section in distinct unordered inverse pairs \(\{r_i,r_i^{-1}\}\).
They may belong to different rational irreducible factors and may lie on the unit circle.
For any nonzero integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_t\), write \(\gamma=\prod_i r_i^{a_i}\) and \(B=\sum_i|a_i|\).
Then, uniformly in all these choices including the sizes of the exponents,
\[
 \frac{l\,h(\gamma)}B\longrightarrow\frac C2,\qquad
 \frac{\log[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]}{\log l}\longrightarrow t,\qquad
 \frac{\log(m(\gamma)/B)}{\log l}\longrightarrow t-1 .                  \tag{1}
\]
Here \(h\) is absolute logarithmic height and \(m(\gamma)=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]h(\gamma)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the minimal polynomial.
For \(t\ge2\) the log measure of every such product tends to infinity.
For all sufficiently large indices the stabilizer of \(\gamma\) in the splitting field consists precisely of the permutations of the roots preserving its exponents after identifying each inverse with the negative of its partner.
One has \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^n)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) simultaneously for every nonzero integer \(n\).

In particular any fixed number of roots from distinct inverse pairs is eventually multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
For an irreducible factor \(S\) with a fixed number \(H\ge2\) of exterior roots, apply (1) to its multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(S)\), up to its rational sign.
Its own log measure satisfies
\[
                  m(M(S))=l^{\,H-1+o(1)},\qquad h(M(S))\sim HC/(2l).
\]
The first limit in (1) controls one monomial even when its exponents grow arbitrarily fast.

<a id="incidence"></a>
## Logarithms supported on infrequent simultaneous visits

The section is monic reciprocal with constant term one, and [all roots are nontorsion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
For every root \(r\) the [individual height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(l\,h(r)\to C/2\) uniformly.
Indeed its projected width is \(2l+4k\), and integration in the vertical coordinate gives \(m(F)=m(A)=C\) because both vertical roots have modulus one over the unit circle, by the [circle-fiber calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#example).

Take a uniformly random complex embedding \(\sigma\) of the splitting field and put \(Y_i=\log|\sigma(r_i^l)|\).
Each \(|Y_i|\le s\log 6\) by the [short-coordinate radius](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#radius), using reciprocity for an interior root.
For \(i\ne j\) we claim uniformly that
\[
                         \Pr(Y_i\ne0,\ Y_j\ne0)=o(1/s).                \tag{2}
\]
Split this event into four sign choices.
For a nonempty sign choice pick one embedding realizing it, and orient each of its two images by inversion if necessary to obtain two exterior roots.
They are distinct because the two original inverse pairs were distinct.
The full joint orbit of this exterior pair has the same fraction of simultaneous exterior visits as the probability of the chosen signs for the original pair.
In fact embeddings of the splitting field are the elements of its Galois group, and precomposition with the chosen automorphism is a bijection.
Inversion commutes with the action.
The [uniform estimate for all distinct exterior pairs](Yin_C_G0345_v796.md#pairs) makes this probability \(o(1/s)\), also when the two roots come from different rational factors.
Summing four terms proves (2).

The product formula for these units gives \(2l\,h(r_i)=\mathbb E|Y_i|\) and \(2l\,h(\gamma)=\mathbb E|\sum_i a_iY_i|\).
The difference between the sum of the absolute values and the absolute value of the sum vanishes when at most one \(Y_i\) is nonzero.
Pointwise it lies between zero and
\[
                   \sum_i |a_iY_i|\,
                   {\bf1}_{\{Y_j\ne0\ \text{for some }j\ne i\}} .
\]
By (2), the radius bound and the union bound, the expected value of the last expression is \(o_t(1)B\), uniformly even if the powers grow.
Division by \(2B\) proves the first assertion of (1).
It also proves eventual multiplicative independence modulo torsion for every fixed number of inverse pairs.
Zero exponents in a prospective relation can simply be deleted.

<a id="stabilizer"></a>
## Identifying the exponents on each inverse pair

Choose one representative from each inverse pair of roots in the splitting field, so that replacing a representative by its inverse changes the sign of its exponent.
To compare two conjugates of \(\gamma\), divide their expressions and collect the signed powers on the union of their supports.
There are at most \(2t\) inverse pairs.
For all sufficiently large indices, simultaneously for support sizes \(1,\ldots,2t\), no nonempty such difference represents a root of unity, by the first part.
An embedding therefore fixes \(\gamma\) exactly when it preserves the signed powers, and an embedding fixing \(\gamma^n\) for any \(n\ne0\) already fixes \(\gamma\).
Galois correspondence proves the power-field assertion.

Put \(J=[\mathbb Q(r_1,\ldots,r_t):\mathbb Q]\) and \(D=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
The field generated by the ordered roots contains \(\gamma\).
Among its embeddings over \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) there are at most \(2^t t!\) possible ordered images, by the signed-support description.
Consequently \(J/(2^t t!)\le D\le J\).
By the [fixed-rank height bound](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U005.md#am16-u005), for each fixed \(\epsilon>0\) the independent roots satisfy \(\max_i h(r_i)\ge c_{t,\epsilon}J^{-1/t-\epsilon}\).
Their individual heights are uniformly \(O(1/l)\), so \(\liminf\log J/\log l\ge(1/t+\epsilon)^{-1}\).
The section has degree \(2l+4k\), giving \(J\le(2l+4k)^t\).
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves the second limit in (1).
The first two limits and \(m(\gamma)=Dh(\gamma)\) prove the third.
For the last application, the set of exterior roots of the monic \(S\) is stable under complex conjugation and contains at most one root of each inverse pair.
Its product is real with absolute value \(M(S)\), so the rational sign has no effect on the degrees or heights.
