# At most two conjugates in each open dyadic disk of radius one half

<a id="statement"></a>
## The disk bound and the two neighborhoods of the rational units

Let \(p=q+1,\ q\ge2,\ L=2q+1\), and let \(0<u<v\) be coprime integers.
Consider the dyadic units \(z\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) that satisfy the graph power equation
\[
        Y(z)^u=z^v,\qquad Y(z)=\frac{pz-q}{p-qz}.
\]
Write \(\nu(2)=1\) for the additive valuation.
After omitting \(1,-1\), each open disk \(\nu(z-c)>1\) contains at most two distinct solutions.
The two disks \(\nu(z-1)>1\) and \(\nu(z+1)>1\) **together** contain at most one reciprocal pair of such solutions.
Every rational dyadic unit belongs to one of these two disks, so there are at most two nontrivial unit solutions in \(\mathbb Q_2\).
These bounds apply to the entire orbit of the reciprocal Salem minimum of any [genuine integral trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), since all its conjugates are units, satisfy the [graph power equation](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion), and differ from \(1,-1\).
In particular no closed radius-\(1/8\) disk contains three of its roots, at any height or degree.

<a id="ordinary"></a>
## A quadratic coefficient controls disks away from both signs

Use \(x=q/p,\ m=u,\ N=v-u\) for even \(q\), and \(x=p/q,\ m=-u,\ N=v+u\) for odd \(q\).
Put \(e=\nu(x)=\nu(pq)\ge1\).
The unit equation is \(z^N=V(x,z)^m\) with \(V(x,z)=(1-x/z)/(1-xz)\). [Dyadic power equation](Yin_C_G0901_v185.md#statement)
Recenter a nonempty disk at one of its solutions \(z_0\), put \(l=\nu(z_0-z_0^{-1})\), and suppose first that \(l\le2\).
On this disk write \(z=z_0\exp w\) with \(\nu(w)>1\), where the exponential and logarithm are inverse isometries between the additive disk and the corresponding principal units.
After division by the equation at \(z_0\), all the solutions give zeros of
\[
       Nw-m\log\frac{V(x,z_0\exp w)}{V(x,z_0)}.
\]
The coefficient of \(w^j\) in the logarithm is \(H_j/j!\), where
\[
       H_j=\sum_{k\ge1} k^{j-1}x^k
                      \bigl(z_0^k-(-1)^j z_0^{-k}\bigr).
\]
This follows by expanding \(\log V(x,z)=\sum_{k\ge1}x^k(z^k-z^{-k})/k\).
The expansions can be interchanged on every closed disk \(\nu(w)\ge t>1\), since the terms in the double expansion gain at least \(ke+(t-1)j\) in valuation for \(j\ge1\).
For even \(j\ge2\), the first summand has valuation \(e+l\) and every later summand has strictly larger valuation, since \(z_0^k-z_0^{-k}\) is an integral multiple of \(z_0-z_0^{-1}\).
For odd \(j\ge3\), replacing the difference by its sum with \(2z_0^{-k}\) gives \(\nu(H_j)\ge e+\min(1,l)\).
Thus the quadratic coefficient has valuation \(\nu(m)+e+l-1\).
On \(\nu(w)\ge t>1\), the coefficient valuation plus \(jt\) for each \(j\ge3\) exceeds the corresponding quadratic value by at least \((j-2)(t-1)\).
For even \(j\) this follows from \(\nu(j!)\le j-1\).
For odd \(j\ge3\), one has \(\nu(j!)=\nu((j-1)!)\le j-2\) and \(l-\min(1,l)\le1\).
If three distinct roots belonged to the open disk, they would lie in a single such closed disk with \(t\) in the value group of a finite extension containing them.
Scaling to its unit disk gives a convergent series whose last coefficient of maximal norm has degree at most two.
The [division argument for zeros in a closed disk](Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#disk) gives a contradiction.

If \(l>2\), the identity \(l=\nu(z_0-1)+\nu(z_0+1)\) and the difference \(2\) of the two signs show that exactly one sign \(\sigma\) satisfies \(\nu(z_0-\sigma)>1\).
The open disk at \(z_0\) then equals the open disk at \(\sigma\).
We count its solutions explicitly.

<a id="signs"></a>
## Exact existence and distance of the reciprocal pair

For \(\sigma=1,-1\) respectively put \(a=L,L^{-1}\), and set \(s=\sigma z,\ Y_\sigma(s)=\sigma Y(\sigma s)\).
On the disk \(\nu(s-1)>1\), the change of variable \(t=(s-1)/(s+1)\) is a bijection onto \(\nu(t)>0\).
In fact \(s=(1+t)/(1-t)\) and \(Y_\sigma(s)=(1+at)/(1-at)\), so \(\nu(Y_\sigma(s)-1)>1\) also.
The original equation becomes \(Y_\sigma(s)^u/s^v=\sigma^{v-u}\), so the negative disk has no solution if the parities differ, since its left side always belongs to this principal-unit group.
For the positive sign, or for the negative sign with matching parities, logarithm is injective on that group.
Expanding the logarithms and dividing by \(2t\) shows that a nonzero \(t\) gives a solution exactly when \(b=t^2\) satisfies
\[
       U_a(b)=\sum_{j\ge0}c_j b^j=0,\qquad
       c_j=\frac{ua^{2j+1}-v}{2j+1},\qquad \nu(b)>0.                  \tag{1}
\]
The logarithm expansions for \(1\pm t\) converge for \(\nu(t)>0\), and their difference gives the logarithm of their quotient.

Put \(K=\nu(u)+\nu(L^2-1)=\nu(u)+e+2\).
Successive numerators in (1) differ by \(ua^{2j+1}(a^2-1)\), of exact valuation \(K\).
If \(\nu(c_0)\le K\), all coefficients have valuation at least \(\nu(c_0)\) and the constant term strictly dominates for \(\nu(b)>0\).
If \(K<\nu(c_0)<\infty\), one has \(\nu(c_1)=K\) and \(\nu(c_j)\ge K\) for \(j\ge2\).
At a zero the valuations of \(c_0\) and \(c_1b\) must then agree, giving \(d=\nu(b)=\nu(ua-v)-K\), a positive integer.
There is exactly one such zero in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\), and it belongs to \(2\mathbb Z_2\).
Indeed the map \(b\mapsto-c_0/c_1-\sum_{j\ge2}(c_j/c_1)b^j\) takes \(2\mathbb Z_2\) to itself and contracts by a factor at most \(1/2\).
In every finite extension any two fixed points of positive valuation also coincide, since the difference quotient for the tail has positive valuation.
When \(c_0=0\), the same linear-term comparison excludes every nonzero \(b\) of positive valuation.

Consequently the exact existence test on each sign disk is
\[
                          K<\nu(ua-v)<\infty.                            \tag{2}
\]
This test already implies the parity condition since \(a\) is odd and \(K>0\).
The two signs cannot both pass, because \(\nu((uL-v)-(u/L-v))=K\).
When (2) holds the pair is
\[
         z_\pm=\sigma\frac{1\pm\sqrt b}{1\mp\sqrt b},\qquad
         \nu(z_\pm-\sigma)=1+d/2,\qquad \nu(z_+-z_-)=2+d/2 .
\]
Both are non-torsion since \(s=\sigma z\ne1\) belongs to the domain of the injective logarithm.
Condition (2) forces \(u,v\) odd by coprimality.
It also forces \(\nu(N)=e+1\), since both values of \(a-\operatorname{sgn}(m)\) have valuation \(e+1\) and \(\nu(v-ua)>K\).
Together with the ordinary-disk argument this proves the claimed bounds, including that for the four odd rational residue classes modulo eight.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Ramification and examples for the graph equation

When the pair exists, its two members each generate \(\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt b)\) by the inverse change of variable.
Write \(B=2^{-d}b\in\mathbb Z_2^\times\).
For odd \(d\) the quadratic extension is ramified by the valuation of \(\sqrt b\).
For even \(d\), the pair is rational if \(B\equiv1\bmod8\), generates the unramified quadratic extension if \(B\equiv5\bmod8\), and generates a ramified quadratic extension if \(B\equiv3,7\bmod8\).
Indeed every odd rational square is one modulo eight.
For \(B\equiv1\bmod4\), solving \(h^2+h=(B-1)/4\) produces \(\sqrt B=1+2h\), and the simple roots of the residual equation occur in \(\mathbb F_2\) or \(\mathbb F_4\) according to the residue of the right side.
For \(B\equiv3\bmod4\), even in an unramified extension a putative \(\sqrt B\) would reduce to one, so would be \(1+2h\) with integral \(h\), whose square is one modulo four.

For example let \(q=4,\ u=1,\ v=9+256h\) for a positive odd integer \(h\), and use the positive sign.
Here \(K=4,\ d=4,\ c_0=-256h,\ c_1/16=(45-16h)/3\equiv7\bmod8\).
After dividing (1) by \(256\), its terms of degree at least two vanish modulo eight since \(\nu(b)=4\) and \(\nu(c_j)\ge4\).
Hence \(B=b/16\equiv7h\bmod8\).
The choices \(h=1,3,7\) give respectively a ramified quadratic pair, an unramified quadratic pair, and two rational roots.
In the rational example \(v=1801\), both nontrivial roots lie in \(1+8\mathbb Z_2\), so this closed disk contains them and the trivial graph solution \(1\).
The choice \(v=137\) with the same \(q,u\) has \(d=3\) and realizes the odd-valuation case.
These are examples for the graph power equation alone.
Their exponents do not establish the exact global pole norm for a Salem minimum, and the disk bound gives no existence result for a sequence at unbounded height.
