# A uniform Mahler gap from the number of occupied powers in a projection of the exponent lattice

<a id="statement"></a>
## Sparse projection with unrestricted coefficient polynomials

Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_h)\) and \(Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)\), with \(h\ge0,\ r\ge1\).
Write a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial in the form
\[
             F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^s B_i(X)Y^{a_i},
             \qquad 1\le s\le k,\quad a_i\in\mathbb Z^r
\]
with distinct \(a_i\) and nonzero Laurent polynomials \(B_i\).
Let \(H\) be their gcd in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\), including integer content and determined up to a signed monomial.
There is a computable number \(\eta_k>0\) depending only on \(k\) such that
\[
        m(F)=m(H)\qquad\text{or}\qquad m(F)\ge m(H)+\eta_k.                  \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure over all the variables of its argument.
There are no bounds on \(h,r\), the widths of the fast exponents \(a_i\), the number of monomials in a row, or their degrees and coefficients.
Integral changes of basis in the exponent lattice preserve the conclusion.
In particular it applies whenever projection onto a collection of lattice coordinates has at most \(k\) occupied values.

In the equality case all irreducible factors of \(F/H\) are cyclotomic polynomials in Laurent monomials, up to Laurent units.
Moreover the sum of the absolute values of the integer coefficients of \(F/H\) is bounded by \(2^{C_k}\), where \(C_k=C(k,1)\) from the [short-interval factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v335.md#statement).
One can take its \(\eta(k,1)\) in (1).
An unrestricted base factor can have any one-variable Mahler measure.
For exactly two occupied fast exponents the optimal positive lower bound in (1) is the logarithm of the plastic constant, as proved below.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## Univariate specializations that preserve all original monomials

Replace \(F\) by \(F/H\); we assume its rows have unit gcd.
A single occupied row gives a Laurent unit.
Suppose \(s\ge2\) and \(m(F)<\eta(k,1)\).
Choose a real vector \(\beta\in\mathbb R^r\) whose coordinates are rationally independent.
For integers \(n\to\infty\) specialize the base variables to powers of \(Z\) with exponents \(p_n=(1,n,\ldots,n^{h-1})\) and the fast variables to powers with integer vector \(q_n\) obtained by rounding \(n^{h+1}\beta\).
When \(h=0\), \(p_n\) is empty.
For every fixed nonzero integer pair \((c,e)\), \(c\cdot p_n+e\cdot q_n\ne0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
If \(e\ne0\) its leading term is \(n^{h+1}e\cdot\beta\); otherwise it is a nonzero polynomial in \(n\).
The shortest nonzero relation therefore tends to infinity in sup norm, with infinite minimum if no relation exists.
The [Boyd–Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(m(F(Z^{p_n},Z^{q_n}))\to m(F)\).
All original support points survive without collisions for large \(n\).

Order the fast rows by \(a_i\cdot\beta\).
The diameter in the specialization of any one row is \(O_F(n^{\max(h-1,0)})\), and distinct row centers are separated by at least \(c_F n^{h+1}\) for large \(n\).
Within the first row the base exponents have a constant order for large \(n\), by the sign of the leading coefficient of each of their finitely many pairwise difference polynomials in \(n\).
Multiply \(F\) by one Laurent monomial so that the first original support point becomes zero in the full lattice.
Now \(P_n=F(Z^{p_n},Z^{q_n})\) is an ordinary polynomial with nonzero constant term, and the first fast row has exponent zero in the fast variables.
Its support is covered by at most \(k\) intervals, each of integer length \(W_n=O_F(n^{\max(h-1,0)})\), with \(W_n=0\) permitted.

For large \(n\), (1) of the [short-interval factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v335.md#statement) applies to \(P_n\) and gives
\[
                P_n=\pm Q_n(Z)\prod_{\nu=1}^{t_n}
                             \Phi_{e_{\nu,n}}(Z^{b_{\nu,n}}).
\]
Each \(Q_n\) is precisely the initial coefficient block through its degree, which is at most \(C_k W_n\).
The gap to the second fast row eventually exceeds this bound, since \(F\) is fixed while \(n\) tends to infinity.
Thus \(Q_n\) consists of actual terms of the first base row, with their original coefficients.
There are finitely many possible subsets of those terms.
After passage to a subsequence, \(Q_n=Q(Z^{p_n})\) for a single fixed nonzero integral base Laurent polynomial \(Q(X)\).
The total cost \(\sum_\nu\varphi(e_{\nu,n})\le C_k\) also allows extraction of a subsequence with a fixed sign, fixed \(t\), and fixed ordered indices \(e_1,\ldots,e_t\).
Indeed \(\varphi(e)\ge\sqrt{e/2}\), as \(e/\varphi(e)^2=\prod_{p^a\parallel e}p^{2-a}/(p-1)^2\le2\).
The remaining positive integers \(b_{\nu,n}\) may vary without any degree bound.

<a id="lifting"></a>
## Lifting a factorization from a subsequence to a finite-rank group

Regard each of the \(h+r\) original exponent coordinates in \((p_n,q_n)\) and each of the \(t\) sequences \(b_{\nu,n}\) as elements of the group of integer sequences on the chosen subsequence.
Expand both sides of the univariate identity as finite formal sums before collecting powers.
The numbers of terms and their integer coefficients are fixed, including those of \(Q\); only the powers at which they occur vary.
There are finitely many equivalence relations on this finite list of indexed terms.
Pass to a further infinite subsequence on which the pattern of equality among all evaluated exponents at a single \(n\) is constant.
On this subsequence two exponents on the list agree as sequences exactly when they agree at any one of its indices.
Cancellations and shared exponents between the two sides thus give an identity of group rings
\[
             F=\pm Q\,T,\qquad T=\prod_{\nu=1}^t\Phi_{e_\nu}([\lambda_\nu])
                                  \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[\Gamma].            \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\Gamma\) is the subgroup generated by all of the finitely many exponent sequences, \(\lambda_\nu=(b_{\nu,n})_n\), and \([\lambda]\) denotes the group monomial of exponent \(\lambda\).
It is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group and hence a free lattice.
Each \(\lambda_\nu\ne0\) since its entries are positive.
The sublattice \(\Delta\) generated by the original coordinates is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z^{h+r}\), since every fixed nonzero original relation is eventually nonzero.
We identify \(F,Q\) with their images in \(\mathbb Z[\Delta]\).

As a module over \(\mathbb Z[\Delta]\), \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\) is the direct sum of one free copy for each coset in \(\Gamma/\Delta\), by collecting the monomials in that coset.
Multiplication by the nonzero \(Q\in\mathbb Z[\Delta]\) preserves each summand and is injective there.
Since \(F\) in (2) belongs to the zero coset, \(T\) also belongs to \(\mathbb Z[\Delta]\).
This works for nonsaturated inclusions of lattices as well.
Consequently \(Q(X)\) divides every coefficient \(B_i(X)\) in the original base Laurent ring, by comparing the coefficients of the distinct \(Y^{a_i}\).
Primitivity forces \(Q\) to be a signed Laurent monomial.

The restriction map from the compact character torus of \(\Gamma\) to that of \(\Delta\) is surjective and carries normalized Haar measure to normalized Haar measure.
For example, in Smith coordinates it is coordinate projection followed by nonzero integer powers.
The logarithmic measure of \(F\) is therefore unchanged under the inclusion.
Every nonzero character on the free lattice torus is uniform on the circle, so \(m(\Phi_{e_\nu}([\lambda_\nu]))=0\).
Equation (2) gives \(m(F)=0\).
The coefficient length of \(F\) is also unchanged under the injective inclusion and is at most
\(\prod_\nu 2^{\varphi(e_\nu)}\le2^{C_k}\), since the roots of each monic cyclotomic factor have modulus one.
This proves the gap and the length estimate in the original lattice.

For the factorization statement in the original polynomial ring, choose a primitive integer fast direction separating the distinct \(a_i\), and complete it to a unimodular fast coordinate system.
In the separating coordinate the coefficients are the individual \(B_i\) times monomials in the other fast variables.
Primitivity excludes any nonunit base-only common factor in this coordinate system.
At zero measure, the [partial-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) and its [torsion-component factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) now give the claimed original-lattice product.
Only positivity of its bound for this finite input width is needed for this last assertion.
Finally multiply by \(H\) and use additivity of logarithmic measure to obtain (1).

<a id="two"></a>
## The optimal bound for two fast exponents

Write \(\theta>1\) for the real root of \(z^3-z-1\).
An integral Laurent polynomial in any number of variables which is not reciprocal up to a signed Laurent monomial has measure at least \(\log\theta\).
Indeed take a sequence of univariate specializations with shortest relation tending to infinity.
For any finite support and all large indices its monomials remain distinct.
If reciprocal specializations persist along a subsequence, pass to one on which the extreme support indices and the sign of reversal are fixed.
For each original support point pass to the same subsequence on which its reflected partner is fixed.
The equality of the two specialized exponents with the specialized sum of the extreme indices must then be the original lattice equality, since every fixed nonzero relation is eventually excluded.
The paired coefficients agree with the indicated common sign.
This gives reciprocity in the original lattice, a contradiction.
The [univariate Smyth bound and its extension to products](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) and the specialization theorem prove the asserted multivariate inequality.

After dividing out the base gcd, two rows give \(F=A(X)+Y^v B(X)\) up to a Laurent monomial, with \(v\ne0\).
If \(m(F)<\log\theta\), it must be reciprocal by the preceding paragraph.
The reversal exchanges the two fast support points, so \(B(X)=\pm X^u A(X^{-1})\) for some integral \(u\).
In particular the two coefficient moduli are equal on the base torus.
For fixed \(X\) outside their zero set, Jensen in the Haar-uniform fast character \(Y^v\) gives the logarithm of this common modulus.
Thus \(m(F)=m(A)\).
Applying the multivariate nonreciprocal bound again shows \(A\) reciprocal, so \(B\) is associated to \(A\) in the integral Laurent ring.
The unit gcd forces both to be Laurent units, and \(m(F)=0\).
Equality at the first positive value is attained by \(A=x^3-x-1,\ B=x^3 A(x^{-1})=1-x^2-x^3\).
These have no common factor since \(A\) is irreducible by the rational root test and does not divide \(B\).
The two nonreal conjugates of \(\theta\) have common modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}<1\), by the cubic discriminant and constant term.
Taking \(F=A+YB\) therefore gives \(m(F)=\log\theta\) exactly.

The coordinator obtained the projected-support gap by lifting the bounded cyclotomic factorization in a group of exponent sequences and descending the base divisor through its lattice cosets.
