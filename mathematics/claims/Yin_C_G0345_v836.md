# Quadratic characters of the two coordinates of the critical octic

<a id="statement"></a>
## Excluding contacts by the slope and the parities of the exponents

Use the [critical octic](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) for a squarefree two-pole section with coprime positive exponents \(k,l\) and \(B(k,l)\ne0\).
At a good odd prime let \(Z=U+U^{-1}\) and \(Y=V+V^{-1}\) in the octic algebra, and let \(\chi_q\) denote the quadratic character on \(\mathbb F_q^\times\).
For the assertions involving \(Y+2\), omit primes dividing \(65\cdot23(30k^2+49l^2)\).
If an octic orbit of degree \(f\) gives any multiple root of the section, then
\[
                    \chi_{p^f}(Z+2)^l=\chi_{p^f}(Y+2)^k.                 \tag{1}
\]
The product of the left sides over the octic orbits divided by the product of the right sides equals the Legendre symbol \((23^l65^k/p)\).
When this is negative, at least one orbit has zero critical valuation and \(p\nmid s_1\) for the [eight Smith integers](Yin_C_G0345_v823.md#ideals).
When the octic is irreducible modulo such a prime, all eight Smith integers are prime to \(p\) and the section is squarefree modulo \(p\).
For even \(k\) the same conclusions use only \((23/p)\), at every good prime, including those omitted for \(Y+2\).
These exclusions apply at every residue order and use only the critical slope modulo \(p\) and the exponent parities.

At every good prime the number of octic Frobenius orbits on which inversion joins the two orientations into one orbit is even.
In particular each such joined orbit has octic degree at most seven and section residue order at most \(p^7+1\).
On the [steep intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#statement), for all but \(O_F(M/\log M)\) directions, the good-prime contacts of this orientation type through \(p\le M^{2/9}\) have total logarithmic weight \(O_F(M\log M)\), with the disk weights used there.

<a id="norms"></a>
## Norms of the critical trace coordinates

Write \(N\) for the norm of the octic algebra.
From \(E(\pm2)=-2300(6l\mp7k)^2\) and leading coefficient \(-4l^2\) one obtains
\[
 N(Z+2)=575(6l+7k)^2/l^2,\qquad N(Z-2)=575(6l-7k)^2/l^2.
\]
Both have squareclass 23 and their product \(N(Z^2-4)\) is a square.
The exceptional form excludes zeros of either factor.

For the second coordinate write \(F=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\) with \(A=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1)\), and \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\).
The polynomial \(R(V)=\operatorname{Res}_U(F,DF)\) has degree sixteen and leading coefficient \(\operatorname{Res}(A,kUA'+2lA)=260k^4\).
Indeed \(A\) is monic of constant term one and discriminant \(13\cdot20\), since its quadratic factors have resultant of absolute value one.
The resultant has degree at most sixteen by homogeneity in the two sets of quartic coefficients, and its leading coefficient follows by selecting the \(V^2\) terms throughout.
The [transverse critical intersection](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#elimination) has sixteen points and no boundary points, so \(R\) divided by this coefficient is the product of the sixteen linear factors \(V-v_c\), counted with their projection multiplicities.
Pairing the orientations gives \(N(Y+2)=R(-1)/(260k^4)\).
At \(V=-1\), write \(t=U-U^{-1}\), so \(F/U^2=2t^2+23\).
On these four zeros, \(DF/U^2=t(4kZ-14l)\), by differentiating \(F/U^2=(t-3)(t-4)V^2+V+(t+3)(t+4)\).
Here \(t^2=-23/2\), \(Z^2=-15/2\), and each sign of \(Z\) occurs twice.
The four \(U\)'s have product one, and the leading coefficient of \(F(U,-1)\) is two.
The quartic resultant formula gives \(R(-1)=184^2(30k^2+49l^2)^2\), hence \(N(Y+2)\) has squareclass 65.
The rational norm identities specialize wherever their denominators and the good-prime reconstruction remain invertible.

<a id="characters"></a>
## The character on an inverse pair

At an octic residue orbit put \(q=p^f\) and choose an orientation \((u,v)\).
The \(q\)-power map fixes both coordinates or inverts both by reconstruction, so they lie in a common cyclic group \(H\), either \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) or the norm-one group in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\).
For \(w\in H\), \(w\ne-1\), the character \(\chi_q(w+w^{-1}+2)\) is the unique quadratic character of \(H\) evaluated at \(w\).
Indeed \((w+1)^2/w=w+w^{-1}+2\), and in the norm-one case \((w+1)^{q-1}=w^{-1}\), giving the value \(w^{-(q+1)/2}\).
In the split case the same identity gives \(w^{-(q-1)/2}\).
A section contact satisfies \(v^k=u^l\), proving (1).
If \(k\) is even then \(l\) is odd and the character of \(u\) must be positive even when \(v=-1\), since the group character is defined there as well.
Multiplying the characters over the orbits computes the character of the corresponding norm, since \(\chi_{p^f}(a)=\chi_p(N_{\mathbb F_{p^f}/\mathbb F_p}a)\).
The same computation for \(Z^2-4\) proves the evenness assertion because its nonsquare orbits are exactly the joined ones.
On a joined orbit a section coordinate is a monomial in \(u,v\) by coprimality, so its order divides \(p^f+1\).
In the [spacing sum](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#spacing), restrict occurrences to joined orbits and replace its bound \(p^8+1\) by \(p^7+1\).
The small-prime contribution remains \(o(M^2)\) over the interval, and the remaining sum through \(Y\) is \(O_F(Y^9+MY)\) by the same spacing and Chebyshev bounds.
Taking \(Y=M^{2/9}\) and applying Markov's inequality gives the stated weight.

<a id="density"></a>
## A positive-density set of primes with a common excluded slope

There is a set of rational primes of natural density at least \(1/16\) such that for every prime in the set, every primitive even \(k\) with \(k\equiv8l\pmod p\) and \(p\nmid l\) gives a section squarefree modulo \(p\).
In particular take \(k=2p+8\), \(l=pk+1\) to obtain simultaneous unbounded primes, short exponents, and slopes \(l/k\).
For each fixed direction with even \(k\), \(k\equiv8l\pmod {17}\), and \(17\nmid l\), the same lower density holds for the primes where its octic is irreducible and \((23/p)=-1\).

To see this, the monic reduction of \(E\) at \((8,1)\) modulo 17 is
\[
 e=Z^8-7Z^7-7Z^6+3Z^5-7Z^4-2Z^3+2Z^2+5Z-6.
\]
Modulo \(e\), successive seventeenth powers starting at \(Z\) give at step four \(7Z^7+4Z^6-5Z^5-5Z^4-8Z^3+3Z^2+3Z+6\), whose difference from \(Z\) has gcd one with \(e\), and at step eight give \(Z\).
These identities certify irreducibility by the finite-field factorization of \(X^{17^j}-X\).
The factors of the excluded polynomial \(G\) in the [octic presentation](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) all have degree less than eight, so 17 is good, and \((23/17)=-1\).
In the compositum of the octic splitting field at \((8,1)\) with \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{23})\), Frobenius at 17 acts as an eight-cycle and changes the sign of \(\sqrt{23}\).
Its centralizer has order at most sixteen, since the Galois group embeds in \(S_8\times C_2\) and the centralizer of an eight-cycle in \(S_8\) has order eight.
The [fixed-field Chebotarev theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) gives its class natural density at least \(1/16\).
Omit the finite primes of bad reduction at \((8,1)\).
Homogeneity gives the same reduced critical algebra at every slope congruent to this one, and the even-exponent obstruction excludes every contact there.
For each fixed direction in the asserted progression, homogeneity at 17 gives the same witness for its own splitting field, so the centralizer argument applies again.
The exclusions give no bound for the prime powers attached to orbits passing the character test.
