# A shared discriminant bound on the reciprocal Newton sums

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single inequality for every coefficient prefix

Let \(q,n\ge2\), \(p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ K=4M+1,\ d=2n\).
Suppose \(H\) realizes the [first nonunit pole on some grid](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#statement).
Let \(\Theta_j\) be the Newton sums of its reciprocal lift, and put \(E_j=(p/q)^j+(q/p)^j,\ \rho=1-1/d\) and \(B=d(\log d+1/2+2/q)\).
Define \(a_j\) to be the distance from zero to the real interval
\[
                 [\Theta_j-E_j,\ \Theta_j-E_j+3].
\]
Every prefix of length \(k\le n\) satisfies the shared bound
\[
                    \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{\rho^j a_j^2}{j}< B.                \tag{1}
\]
It involves only the integer observations and the prescribed rational pole.
With the [pole digit and signature tests](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#digits) it preserves a complete decision over the union of all separating grids.
At depth \(j\), take the remaining bound after subtracting the earlier summands in (1).
It bounds the magnitude of the next moment before deciding which integer in its prescribed residue class can extend the entire prefix.

Only positions \(j\le 8B/(M-3)^2\) can branch, independently of the grid.
If
\[
                      3+2\sqrt{Bd/(d-1)}\le M,                            \tag{2}
\]
there is at most one candidate over all grids for that height and degree, even before the terminal pole and root tests.
In particular this uniqueness range includes \(2\le n\le c M^2/\log M\) for all sufficiently large \(M\), for any fixed \(0<c<1/16\).

<a id="energy"></a>
## Reflecting the exterior conjugate into the disk

Write \(z_i\) for all roots of \(S(z)=z^n H(z+z^{-1}-2)\), put \(t_i=\max(1,|z_i|)\), and set \(b_i=z_i\) for \(|z_i|\le1\), \(b_i=1/\bar z_i\) otherwise.
The [Salem signature](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) gives \(\prod_i t_i=T\) with \(1<T<p/q\).
For \(i\ne l\),
\[
  |1-\rho b_i\bar b_l|\ge\sqrt{\rho}\,|1-b_i\bar b_l|
                      \ge\sqrt{\rho}\,\frac{|z_i-z_l|}{t_i t_l}.
\]
The first inequality follows by subtracting squares, with difference \((1-\rho)(1-\rho|b_i\bar b_l|^2)\).
The second is equality for one exterior and one interior or circle root and otherwise follows from
\(|1-u\bar v|^2-|u-v|^2=(1-|u|^2)(1-|v|^2)\) on the disk.
Let \(P_j=\sum_i b_i^j\).
The convergent series for the logarithm gives the identity
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}\rho^j |P_j|^2/j=-\sum_{i,l}\log|1-\rho b_i\bar b_l|.
\]
The diagonal contributes at most \(d\log(1/(1-\rho))\).
Using the pair estimate and the nonzero integral discriminant of \(S\) on the off-diagonal gives an upper bound for the total of
\[
  d\log d-\tbinom d2\log\rho+2(d-1)\log T-\log|\operatorname{disc} S| < B.
\]
Indeed \(-\log(1-1/d)\le1/(d-1)\) and \(\log T<1/q\).
If \(w=T+T^{-1}-2\) is the positive trace, the [exterior estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#windows) gives \(0<E_j-(T^j+T^{-j})<1\) up to \(j=n\).
Since only \(T\) was reflected, \(P_j=\Theta_j-(T^j+T^{-j})+2T^{-j}\) belongs to the interval used in (1).
All terms of the energy sum are nonnegative, proving the assertion.

<a id="branch"></a>
## The range in which the pole forces every digit

Ignoring the earlier nonnegative terms still confines \(\Theta_j\) to an open interval of length at most \(3+2\sqrt{jB/\rho^j}\).
Bernoulli's inequality gives \(\rho^j\ge(d-j)/d\ge1/2\) for \(j\le n\).
The pole and Newton recurrence give a single arithmetic progression of step \(jM\) once the preceding coefficients have been fixed.
If \(j>8B/(M-3)^2\), the interval length is less than \(3+j(M-3)\le jM\).
Also \(j(d-j)\ge d-1\) throughout \(1\le j\le n\), so every interval length divided by \(j\) is at most \(3+2\sqrt{Bd/(d-1)}\).
This proves (2), including equality because the bound in (1) is strict.
For \(n\le cM^2/\log M\) one has \(B\le (4c+o(1))M^2\) uniformly, proving the asserted uniqueness for \(c<1/16\).
The condition controls the number of candidates; existence still requires the exact pole and open root signature.

For comparison, [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(2/(\log(3d))^3<\log T<1/q\), hence \(d>\exp((2q)^{1/3})/3\).
For sufficiently large \(q\) this necessary degree exceeds every fixed power of \(q\), including the unbranched range just proved.
The contribution of (1) is an effective filter at fixed heights and a constraint on all degrees; (2) alone cannot supply a sequence at unbounded heights.
The regularized discriminant estimate also appears in the [project's phase estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases); here the pole makes each reflected moment accessible from the selected integer prefix.
