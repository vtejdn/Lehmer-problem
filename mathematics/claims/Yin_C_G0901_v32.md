# A quadratic norm-one family unramified over its trace field at every finite prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## Total Mahler measure in the restricted family

Fix \(w=e^{2\pi i/12}\), so \(w+w^{-1}=\sqrt3\) and \(w-w^{-1}=i\).
Use \(m(\gamma)=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]h(\gamma)\) for the total logarithmic Mahler measure, where \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic height.
For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\beta\), put \(L=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})\).
Consider the units \(\beta\) satisfying
\[
    \sqrt3\in F,\qquad L=F(w)=F(i),\qquad [L:F]=2,\qquad
                              N_{L/F}(\beta)=1.                           \tag{1}
\]
In this family \(\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_F[w]\) and the relative discriminant is the unit ideal, so \(L/F\) is unramified at every finite prime, including those above two.
Each generator has a unique expression \(\beta=x+yw\) with \(x,y\in\mathcal O_F\), satisfying
\[
                            x^2+\sqrt3\,xy+y^2=1 .                        \tag{2}
\]
Its trace \(t=2x+\sqrt3\,y\) satisfies \(t^2+y^2=4\).
The trace and generator conditions in (1) are part of the family.

Let \(\lambda_*\) be its infimum of positive total measures and let \(\lambda\) be the unrestricted Lehmer infimum for nonzero integer polynomials of positive logarithmic Mahler measure.
Put \(c=\log\theta_0\), where \(\theta_0>1\) is the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
Then
\[
                 \min\{c/2,\lambda_*/4\}\le\lambda\le\lambda_* .          \tag{3}
\]
In particular the unrestricted infimum vanishes exactly when the infimum in (1) vanishes.
Every nontorsion reciprocal unit \(\alpha\) with \(m(\alpha)<c/2\) supplies a generator satisfying (1) with
\[
                 m(\beta)=\ell\,m(\alpha),\qquad \ell\in\{1,2,4\}.        \tag{4}
\]

For units with a conjugate of modulus one the construction works at every positive measure and retains a conjugate of modulus one.
Writing \(\lambda_\circ\) for the infimum over all nontorsion units with such a conjugate, and \(\lambda_{*,\circ}\) for the infimum with (1) imposed as well, one has
\[
                   \lambda_\circ\le\lambda_{*,\circ}\le4\lambda_\circ.    \tag{5}
\]
In particular Salem numbers of degree at least four tending to one would give generators in this latter family with measures tending to zero.
Their trace fields have exactly one or two pairs of nonreal embeddings.
The field of each output contains \(i\), so its generator has no real conjugate.

<a id="generation"></a>
## Powers of a representative and adjoining a twelfth root of unity

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal unit, so the automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) is defined.
Use the [relative norm construction](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
Specifically choose \(M\) divisible by the orders of all torsion ratios of distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\), set \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^M)\), \(s=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\), and put \(\delta=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)\).
This unit satisfies \(\delta^M=\alpha^{sM}\), and \(\mathbb Q(\delta^j)=K\) for every \(j\ge1\), since \(\alpha^{sMj}\) already generates \(K\).
The power identity gives \(h(\delta)=s h(\alpha)\), so \(m(\delta)=m(\alpha)\) after multiplication by its degree.
The original inversion restricts to an involution \(\tau\) of \(K\) sending \(\delta\) to \(\delta^{-1}\) by functoriality of the norm.
The generator \(\delta\) determines this automorphism uniquely.
It is nontrivial because \(\delta\) is nontorsion.
In particular \(K^\tau=\mathbb Q(\delta+\delta^{-1})\) and \([K:K^\tau]=2\), by the quadratic equation for \(\delta\) over its trace.

Put \(E=\mathbb Q(w)=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt3)\), \(D=K\cap E\), and \(\beta=w\delta\).
Since \(\beta^{12}=\delta^{12}\) generates \(K\), adjoining \(\beta\) also gives \(\delta\) and then \(w=\beta/\delta\).
It follows that \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=KE\), including when \(D\ne\mathbb Q\).
Because \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois, \(KE/K\) is Galois and restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(KE/K)\) with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/D)\).
Indeed the image has fixed field \(E\cap K\), by the fixed field of \(\operatorname{Gal}(KE/K)\) on the compositum.
Multiplication by \(w\) leaves every absolute height unchanged, so
\[
                       m(\beta)=[E:D]\,m(\delta),                         \tag{6}
\]
and \([E:D]\in\{1,2,4\}\).

<a id="involution"></a>
## Compatibility of inversion on the intersection

Let \(c_E\) be complex conjugation on \(E\).
Inversion of \(\beta\) is an automorphism of \(L=KE\) exactly when \(\tau|_D=c_E|_D\).
In fact an inverter must send \(\delta^{12}=\beta^{12}\) to its inverse and therefore preserves \(K=\mathbb Q(\delta^{12})\).
Its restriction to \(K\) agrees with \(\tau\) because the two agree on this generator.
The identity \(w=\beta/\delta\) then forces it to send \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\).

Conversely extend \(\tau\) to the algebraic closure and restrict that extension to \(KE\).
This gives an automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(KE\), since it preserves \(K\) and the Galois field \(E\).
Every subfield of the abelian extension \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\), so \(D\) is preserved.
Under the compatibility condition \(c_E(\sigma|_E)^{-1}\) fixes \(D\).
Lift it to \(\operatorname{Gal}(KE/K)\) by the restriction calculation above, and compose this lift with \(\sigma\).
The result \(c_L\) acts as \(\tau\) on \(K\) and \(c_E\) on \(E\).
These restrictions determine it, and their squares are the identity.
Thus \(c_L\) is a nonidentity involution taking \(\beta\) to its inverse.
Equivalently the minimum of \(\beta\) is reciprocal, since \(\beta\) generates \(L\) and its inverse also generates \(L\).

The three quadratic subfields of \(E\) are \(\mathbb Q(i)\), \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).
On their respective square roots \(c_E\) acts by \(-1,-1,+1\).
The intermediate fields of \(E/\mathbb Q\) are precisely these three, \(\mathbb Q\), and \(E\).
Incompatibility is therefore equivalent to the occurrence of at least one of the following three conditions.

* \(i\in K^\tau\).
* \(\sqrt{-3}\in K^\tau\).
* \(\sqrt3\in K\) and \(\tau(\sqrt3)=-\sqrt3\), equivalently \(K=K^\tau(\sqrt3)\) with \(\sqrt3\notin K^\tau\).

These alternatives may overlap.
In each case \(D\ne\mathbb Q\), so (6) gives \(m(\beta)\le2m(\alpha)\).
The unit \(\beta\) is nonreciprocal in this case, and [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives \(m(\beta)\ge c\).
Consequently incompatibility requires \(m(\alpha)\ge c/2\).

<a id="basis"></a>
## The fixed field, integral basis and gap comparison

When the restrictions agree, \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta+\beta^{-1})\) is the fixed field of \(c_L\).
Indeed \(c_L\) fixes this trace, and the quadratic equation for the generator \(\beta\) gives \([L:F]\le2\), while \(c_L\ne1\).
It fixes \(\sqrt3=w+w^{-1}\) and negates \(i=w-w^{-1}\), so both \(w\) and \(i\) generate \(L\) over \(F\).
For any \(v\in\mathcal O_L\), put
\[
                 y=\frac{v-c_L(v)}{w-w^{-1}},\qquad x=v-yw .
\]
The denominator is the unit \(i\), so \(y\) is integral.
It is fixed by \(c_L\) because numerator and denominator both change sign.
Now \(x\) is integral and \(c_L(x)=x\) by the definition of \(y\).
Thus \(x,y\in\mathcal O_F\).
Since \(w\notin F\), the elements \(1,w\) are linearly independent over \(F\), so this representation is unique.
The same calculation applies to any generator in (1), since \(w\) solves \(X^2-\sqrt3 X+1=0\) over \(F\) and its two images are \(w,w^{-1}\).
The discriminant of this integral basis is \((w-w^{-1})^2=-1\), giving the unit relative discriminant ideal.
The quadratic polynomial is separable in every residue characteristic, and the integral basis identifies every residue algebra with its separable quadratic quotient, giving the finite-prime unramified assertion directly.
Multiplying \(x+yw\) and \(x+yw^{-1}\) gives (2) for the generator.
Their sum is \(t=2x+\sqrt3\,y\), while their difference is \(iy\).
The identity \((\beta+\beta^{-1})^2-(\beta-\beta^{-1})^2=4\) gives \(t^2+y^2=4\) in \(\mathcal O_F\).

At a circle embedding choose the conjugate of \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|=1\) at the outset.
Then \(\bar\alpha=\alpha^{-1}\), so it is reciprocal, and \(\delta^M=\alpha^{sM}\) gives \(|\delta|=1\) in the chosen embedding.
Complex conjugation on \(K=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) now is \(\tau\), hence agrees with \(c_E\) on \(D\).
The twisted generator has \(|\beta|=1\) there, and its trace field is real in that embedding.
This argument imposes no total-reality hypothesis on \(F\).
Equation (6) gives the upper inequality of (5), and the lower inequality follows by inclusion of the two circle classes.
A Salem number of degree at least four has a circle conjugate and total measure equal to the logarithm of its real value greater than one, proving the assertion on such sequences.

For a compatible input let \(b(\delta)\) count the conjugates of \(\delta\) of modulus different from one, and let \(r_2(F)\) count conjugate pairs of nonreal embeddings of \(F\).
Then
\[
                            4r_2(F)=[E:D]\,b(\delta).                    \tag{7}
\]
At any real embedding of \(F\), the two extensions to \(F(i)\) are conjugate, and their values on \(\beta\) have product one, so both have modulus one.
At a nonreal embedding of \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\) the value of \(t\) is nonreal, so neither of its two lifts \(\beta,\beta^{-1}\) can have modulus one.
This counts \(4r_2(F)\) off-circle images of \(\beta\).
Each embedding of \(K\) has \([E:D]\) extensions to \(KE\), and all images of \(w\) have modulus one, which gives the other count in (7).

For a Salem number \(\alpha\) one may take \(\delta=\alpha\) without descent.
Indeed the positive Salem value is the unique conjugate of maximal modulus, so an embedding fixing its \(j\)-th power must fix it for every \(j\ge1\).
Consequently \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and the two off-circle conjugates are exactly \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\).
The circle embedding supplies compatibility as above.
In the embedding with the original positive real \(\alpha\), \(K\) is contained in \(\mathbb R\), so \(D\) lies in the real subfield \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) of \(E\).
Thus \([E:D]\) is two or four and (7) gives \(r_2(F)=[E:D]/2\), as claimed.

For (3), the [reduction for the unrestricted infimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) allows reciprocal nontorsion units.
Equivalently, after removal of zero roots, nontrivial scalar content or a nonunit extreme coefficient costs at least \(\log2\), and a nonreciprocal monic unit costs at least \(c\) by Smyth's theorem.
Measures add under multiplication of polynomials.
An incompatible reciprocal input costs at least \(c/2\).
For every compatible one, (6) and (1) give \(m(\alpha)\ge m(\beta)/4\ge\lambda_*/4\).
This proves the lower bound in (3), while each member of (1) is an unrestricted input for the upper bound.
The comparison proves the equivalence of positive gaps and the transfer (4) at sufficiently small measure.
It does not impose a circle embedding on the general family in (3).
