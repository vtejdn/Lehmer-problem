# Exterior-count obstructions to ratios and products of real circuit roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## The full ratio polynomial and its cost

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with exactly \(e\ge3\) roots outside the unit circle, all real.
Suppose their positive logarithmic moduli form a rational circuit: they are rationally dependent, and every proper subset is rationally independent.
The [real circuit decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) partitions the inverse-pair labels into \(m/e\) blocks of size \(e\).
Each block has one primitive relation modulo roots of unity, with all coefficients in \(\{1,-1\}\); these block relations generate the full relation lattice.

Orient the representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_e\) in each block so that
\[
                              \prod_{i=1}^e x_i\in\{1,-1\}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Take all ordered ratios \(x_i/x_j\), \(i\ne j\), within every block, and let \(Q\) be the monic polynomial with precisely this list of roots.
Then \(Q\) is an integer reciprocal polynomial, its listed roots are distinct and nontorsion, and
\[
                \deg Q=m(e-1),\qquad
                \#\{\gamma:Q(\gamma)=0,\ |\gamma|>1\}
                         =\frac{e(e-1)}2.                            \tag{2}
\]
For the oriented representatives in the unique exterior block put \(t_i=\log|x_i|\).
Then
\[
 \sum_i t_i=0,\qquad
 \log M(P)=\sum_i|t_i|,\qquad
 \log M(Q)=\sum_{i<j}|t_i-t_j|,                                      \tag{3}
\]
and consequently
\[
       \frac e2<
       \frac{\log M(Q)}{\log M(P)}
       <e-1.                                                       \tag{4}
\]
No irreducibility of \(Q\) is asserted in this general statement.

Thus the full ordered-ratio operation leaves the exterior count unchanged for \(e=3\) and increases it for every \(e\ge4\).
Already at \(e=3\), both \(P\) and \(Q\) can be irreducible, so taking a factor does not always turn this operation into a reduction.
An exact example is
\[
 \begin{split}
 P(z)&=z^6-20z^5+118z^4-206z^3+118z^2-20z+1,\\
 Q(z)&=z^6-95z^5+1636z^4-5945z^3+1636z^2-95z+1 .
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{5}
\]
Both have exactly three positive real exterior roots.
The example disproves a direct extension of the [mixed-signature three-to-two ratio construction](Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#ratios-of-roots) to real circuits.
More strongly, every nontorsion algebraic number \(y\) such that
\[
 y^k=\zeta\prod_{\gamma:\,P(\gamma)=0}\gamma^{a_\gamma}
 \quad\hbox{for some }k\ge1,\quad a_\gamma\in\mathbb Z,\quad
 \zeta\hbox{ a root of unity}
                                                                    \tag{5a}
\]
has at least three exterior conjugates.
Thus no finite sequence of products, quotients, conjugation and radical extraction starting from these roots yields a nontorsion number with only one or two exterior conjugates.
This is a statement about their multiplicative group and its radical saturation.
It does not cover sums, arbitrary other units in their splitting field, or a reduction restricted to sufficiently small measure.

A general full-permutation version is proved in the [saturation calculation](#saturation).
For a real circuit of size \(e\ge3\), if the stabilizer of the exterior block induces all permutations and simultaneous inversion on its oriented representatives, the least exterior-conjugate count in the nontorsion radical saturation of all roots is
\[
 \nu(e)=
 \begin{cases}
 3,&e=4,\\
 e,&e=3\ \hbox{or}\ e\ge5.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{5b}
\]
The number of neutral blocks is unrestricted.
If a neutral block exists, the signed-action hypothesis follows already from the stabilizer inducing every unsigned permutation of the exterior block.
These additional action hypotheses are not asserted for every real circuit.
For the exceptional case \(e=4\), the minimum is attained by an explicit reciprocal irreducible polynomial of degree \(3m/2\), with exactly three real exterior roots having rationally independent positive logarithms, and logarithmic Mahler measure at most twice that of \(P\).
If the oriented block product is \(+1\), an unsquared product improves the cost bound to \(\log M(P)\).
Thus the four-label exception supplies a quantitative descent, while its radical saturation still excludes a further descent to one or two exterior conjugates.

<a id="orientation"></a>
## The Galois action and distinctness of the ratios

The hypotheses here are exactly those of the circuit clause of [C345v58](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#statement), rather than a claim about arbitrary rational relations between exterior logarithms.
Its [block argument](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) gives \(e\mid m\), one exterior block containing all \(e\) exterior inverse pairs, and wholly neutral remaining blocks.
It also gives the entire integral relation lattice as the direct sum of the primitive signed generators on these blocks.
The [real projection argument](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) makes each generator's torsion value a sign, which gives (1) after inverting selected representatives.

For any fixed block, the orientation in (1) is unique up to permutation and simultaneous inversion.
Indeed any other orientation multiplies the exponent at each label by a sign.
Its all-ones product is torsion precisely when this sign vector belongs to the rank-one relation lattice in the original orientation.
Its only possibilities are the all-ones vector and its negative.

A Galois automorphism permutes the blocks and takes an oriented list to a permutation of the target list or to the simultaneous inverses of that list.
It therefore permutes the list of ordered ratios.
Every listed value is an algebraic unit, so the Galois-invariant monic product has integer coefficients.
Interchanging the order of a ratio gives its inverse.
Once distinctness is checked, the even list length and its inverse pairing give constant one and reciprocity.

Represent an ordered ratio by the integral exponent vector having one entry \(1\), one entry \(-1\), and all others zero.
Its coordinate sum on each block is zero.
If two listed values were equal, the difference of their exponent vectors would belong to the relation lattice.
In the chosen orientations that lattice consists of vectors constant on each block.
The difference also has sum zero on every block, so every such constant is zero.
The exponent vectors coincide, identifying the same block and the same ordered pair.
The same argument applied to one exponent vector shows that no ratio is torsion.

There are \(m/e\) blocks and \(e(e-1)\) ordered ratios in each.
This proves the degree in (2) without a multiplicity convention or an irreducibility assumption.

<a id="count"></a>
## Exterior count and the two strict measure bounds

In the exterior block every \(t_i\) is nonzero.
The positive numbers \(|t_i|\) are the original exterior logarithms, and any equality between two of them would contradict independence of a proper subset of the circuit.
Thus the \(t_i\) are pairwise distinct.
Equation (1) gives their sum zero.
Every other block has all its representatives on the unit circle.

Within the exterior block, exactly one ordered ratio from each unordered pair has modulus greater than one.
All ratios from other blocks have modulus one.
This gives the exterior count and the second equality in (3); the first measure equality follows by choosing the exterior member of each original inverse pair.

For completeness let \(p\) and \(n\) be the numbers of positive and negative \(t_i\), respectively, and set
\[
              S=\sum_{t_i>0}t_i=-\sum_{t_i<0}t_i>0.
\]
Then \(p+n=e\) and \(\sum_i|t_i|=2S\).
The differences between opposite-sign entries contribute exactly
\[
 \sum_{\substack{t_i>0\\t_j<0}}|t_i-t_j|
                         =nS+pS=eS.
\]
Since \(e\ge3\), at least two entries have the same sign.
Their difference is positive, because the entries are distinct.
The remaining differences therefore make the total strictly greater than \(eS\).

On the other hand, summing \(|t_i-t_j|\le |t_i|+|t_j|\) over all unordered pairs gives
\[
                  \sum_{i<j}|t_i-t_j|
                            \le(e-1)\sum_i|t_i|.
\]
The inequality is strict on every pair of nonzero entries having the same sign, and at least one such pair exists.
This proves (4).
These estimates retain their dependence on \(e\); they give no cost uniform in an unbounded exterior count.

<a id="input"></a>
## An irreducible real-circuit input

Set
\[
 a=10+\sqrt2,\qquad b=10-\sqrt2,\qquad
 f(z)=z^3-az^2+bz-1.
\]
The nonidentity automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) sends \(f\) to
\[
                       f^\sigma(z)=z^3-bz^2+az-1=-z^3f(1/z).
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
 f f^\sigma
 =z^6-(a+b)z^5+(ab+a+b)z^4-(a^2+b^2+2)z^3
          +(ab+a+b)z^2-(a+b)z+1,
\]
which is \(P\) in (5), since
\[
 a+b=20,\quad ab=98,\quad a^2+b^2=204,\quad
                         a^3+b^3=2120.                             \tag{6}
\]

The cubic discriminant is
\[
 \operatorname{disc}(f)
       =a^2b^2-4(a^3+b^3)+18ab-27
       =9604-8480+1764-27=2861>0.
\]
Thus \(f\) has three distinct real roots.
Both \(a,b\) are positive, and \(f(-u)<0\) for every \(u\ge0\), so all three roots are positive.
Their product is one and
\[
                              f(1)=-2\sqrt2<0.
\]
The number of roots greater than one is odd, while their product excludes three such roots.
Consequently they can be ordered
\[
                       0<r_1<r_2<1<r_3,\qquad r_1r_2r_3=1.         \tag{7}
\]

To verify irreducibility, write
\[
 P(z)=z^3S(z+z^{-1}),\qquad S(X)=X^3-20X^2+115X-166 .
\]
Modulo three the cubic is \(X^3+X^2+X+2\), whose values at \(0,1,2\) are \(2,2,1\).
It has no root over \(\mathbb F_3\), so \(S\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
For a root \(T\) of \(S\),
\[
 N_{\mathbb Q(T)/\mathbb Q}(T^2-4)
       =S(2)S(-2)=(-8)(-484)=3872=2^5\,11^2
\]
is not a rational square.
Hence \(T^2-4\) is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(T)\), and \(z^2-Tz+1\) is irreducible over that cubic field.
The tower degree is six, proving that \(P\) is irreducible.

Its exterior roots are
\[
            \alpha=r_1^{-1}>\beta=r_2^{-1}>1,\qquad
                         \mu=r_3=\alpha\beta .
\]
These are positive and distinct.
The logarithmic relation \(\log\mu-\log\alpha-\log\beta=0\) is a circuit: the [pair-log argument for real conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#three) excludes a rational dependence between the logarithms of two distinct positive exterior conjugates.
Its hypotheses apply because irreducibility has just been proved.
In degree six the exterior labels constitute the single circuit block.

<a id="output"></a>
## The exact ratio polynomial is also irreducible

The three roots of \(f\), or their simultaneous inverses, are valid oriented representatives of that block.
Put
\[
                     Q(z)=\prod_{i\ne j}(z-r_i/r_j).
\]
The following resultant identity makes its coefficients directly reproducible:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Res}_x(f(x),f(zx))
       &=(z-1)^3Q(z),\\
 Q(z)&=z^6+(3-ab)z^5+(a^3+b^3-5ab+6)z^4\\
     &\quad +(2(a^3+b^3)-a^2b^2-6ab+7)z^3\\
     &\quad +(a^3+b^3-5ab+6)z^2+(3-ab)z+1 .
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{8}
\]
For the first identity, multiply \(f(zr_i)=\prod_j(zr_i-r_j)\) over \(i\), using \(\prod_i r_i=1\); the three diagonal factors give \((z-1)^3\).
Expansion of the remaining symmetric product gives the second identity.
Substitution of (6) gives
\[
       3-ab=-95,\qquad
       2120-5(98)+6=1636,\qquad
       2(2120)-9604-6(98)+7=-5945,
\]
yielding precisely the polynomial \(Q\) in (5).

Its trace polynomial is
\[
 Q(z)=z^3H(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
                  H(X)=X^3-95X^2+1633X-5755 .
\]
Modulo three this is again \(X^3+X^2+X+2\), so it is irreducible by the same three residue evaluations.
Moreover
\[
                H(2)=-2861,\qquad H(-2)=-9409=-97^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
              N_{\mathbb Q(T)/\mathbb Q}(T^2-4)
                           =2861\,97^2
\]
for a root \(T\) of \(H\).
This is not a rational square, since \(53^2<2861<54^2\).
The same quadratic tower argument proves that \(Q\) is irreducible of degree six.

All its roots are positive real ratios.
They are distinct by the general lattice argument, and precisely three exceed one.
More explicitly, writing
\[
                       A=\log\alpha>B=\log\beta>0,
\]
the logs of the roots \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) are \(-A,-B,A+B\).
The three positive ratio logs are
\[
                         A-B,\qquad 2A+B,\qquad A+2B .
\]
Hence
\[
              \log M(P)=2(A+B),\qquad
              \log M(Q)=4A+2B,\qquad
              \frac32<\frac{\log M(Q)}{\log M(P)}<2.                 \tag{9}
\]
In particular no irreducible factor of this \(Q\) has fewer exterior roots.


<a id="saturation"></a>
## Products and radicals with neutral blocks allowed

Continue with the real circuit hypotheses of the statement, and let \(\mathcal E\) be its exterior block.
Assume in addition that its setwise Galois stabilizer induces all permutations and simultaneous inversion on the oriented representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_e\) of \(\mathcal E\).
As in the orientation argument, every block stabilizer acts on its oriented representatives by a permutation with a common sign.
Thus the additional hypothesis says that its image is the full group \(S_e\times\{1,-1\}\).
It imposes no independence requirement on the action on other blocks.

Let \(\Omega\) be the complete root set of \(P\), and put
\[
 \Gamma=\langle\Omega\rangle,\qquad
 \widetilde\Gamma=
 \{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}:
                y^k\in\mu_\infty\Gamma\text{ for some }k\ge1\},
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]
where \(\mu_\infty\) is the group of roots of unity.
Every member of \(\widetilde\Gamma\) is an algebraic unit, since after clearing torsion a positive power and its inverse are algebraic integers.

Consider a nontorsion monomial \(\gamma\in\Gamma\).
By the complete block relation lattice, its exponent vector is nonconstant on at least one block.
Send that block to \(\mathcal E\) by a Galois automorphism, and write \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_e)\) for the resulting nonconstant exponent vector there.
Every other block is neutral in the chosen embedding.
An automorphism stabilizing \(\mathcal E\) keeps all those other contributions of modulus one, even if it permutes or inverts their coordinates.
The logarithms of the absolute values it realizes for conjugates of \(\gamma\) therefore include
\[
 \left\{\sum_i(\sigma a)_i t_i,\quad
              -\sum_i(\sigma a)_i t_i:\sigma\in S_e\right\},
       \qquad t_i=\log|x_i|.                                       \tag{11}
\]

The rational nullspace of \(t_1,\ldots,t_e\) is precisely the line of the all-ones vector.
Indeed, changing the signs of the original exterior logarithms according to the orientation takes their circuit relation to (1).
Thus (11) is indexed without collisions by
\[
       \{\sigma[a],-\sigma[a]:\sigma\in S_e\},
          \qquad [a]\in\mathbb Z^e/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1).
\]
Every listed log value is nonzero.
Opposite classes give opposite logs, so half the distinct listed magnitudes exceed one.
Distinct magnitudes give distinct conjugates even when the actual conjugates have different phases.
This supplies a lower bound for the exterior count without identifying the full Galois orbit of \(\gamma\).

Let the distinct coordinate values of \(a\) have multiplicities \(n_1,\ldots,n_s\), with \(s\ge2\), and put
\[
                               h=\frac{e!}{n_1!\cdots n_s!}.
\]
The \(S_e\)-orbit of \([a]\) has exactly \(h\) elements: if a permutation of \(a\) equals \(a+c(1,\ldots,1)\), equality of coordinate sums gives \(c=0\).
If the negative orbit is disjoint, (11) gives \(h\) exterior conjugates.
For any nonconstant vector this is at least \(e\), since selecting the positions of a proper nonempty level set already gives \(\binom ej\ge e\).

If the two orbits coincide, the coordinate multiset of \(a\) is symmetric about its mean, and (11) gives \(h/2\) exterior conjugates.
When \(s=2\), symmetry forces equal multiplicities \(e/2,e/2\), so \(e\) is even and the bound is
\[
                                \frac12\binom e{e/2}.              \tag{12}
\]
For \(e=4\) this equals three.
For even \(e\ge6\) it is at least \(e\), since the middle binomial coefficient is at least \(\binom e2\ge2e\).
When \(s\ge3\), one has \(h\ge e(e-1)\).
Indeed among partitions of \(e\) into at least three positive multiplicities the product of their factorials is largest for \((e-2,1,1)\).
Merging classes increases that product, and transferring one unit from a smaller part of size at least two to a largest part also increases it.
Hence \(h/2\ge e\) for \(e\ge3\).

These bounds give the lower bound \(\nu(e)\) in (5b) for every nontorsion \(\gamma\in\Gamma\).
For \(e=3\) or \(e\ge5\), a single original root attains \(e\), by the hypothesis on \(P\).
For \(e=4\), choose two representatives from one block and set \(\gamma=x_1^2x_2^2\).
Squaring removes the sign of the block product, so \(\prod_{i=1}^4x_i^2=1\).
Every conjugate of \(\gamma\) is a product of an unordered pair of squared representatives in one target block, or its inverse.
The inverse is the product of the complementary pair.
Only the exterior target block contributes exterior conjugates.
There all six unordered pairs occur by the assumed stabilizer action, their log values are distinct and nonzero by the circuit condition, and complementary pairs have opposite log values.
The squared representatives there are positive, so there are exactly three exterior conjugates, not additional phase variants of them.
This attains the exceptional bound.

Finally let \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\) be nontorsion.
Clear the torsion in its defining relation to obtain \(y^N=\gamma\) with \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) nontorsion.
Each conjugate of \(\gamma\) is the \(N\)-th power of a conjugate of \(y\), because an embedding extends to a normal closure.
Different conjugates of \(\gamma\) require different preimages, and every exterior conjugate has only exterior preimages.
Hence \(y\) has at least as many exterior conjugates as \(\gamma\).
The already attained minima in \(\Gamma\) are therefore the exact minima in \(\widetilde\Gamma\) as well.

<a id="four-descent"></a>
## The four-label exception gives a quantitative descent

Under the full-action hypothesis with \(e=4\), take \(\gamma=x_1^2x_2^2\) as above, and let \(H\) be its minimal polynomial.
Its Galois orbit consists of all six unordered-pair products of squared representatives in each block.
Transitivity on blocks and the full permutation action on a block supply every such product.
They are distinct: within a block, the difference of their exponent vectors has sum zero and cannot be a nonzero constant vector; across different blocks, either restriction of that difference is nonconstant.
The complete block relation lattice therefore excludes equality in both cases.
Complementary pairs are actual inverses, because the squared block product is one.
Consequently \(H\) is monic integral, irreducible and reciprocal, with
\[
                    \deg H=6(m/4)=3m/2
\]
and exactly three exterior roots.

Only the exterior block contributes to its measure, and its complementary pair logs have opposite signs.
It follows that
\[
 \log M(H)=\sum_{i<j}|t_i+t_j|
          =4\max_i|t_i|\le2\sum_i|t_i|=2\log M(P).                 \tag{13}
\]
For the middle identity, change all signs if needed and relabel so that \(t_1=\max_i|t_i|>0\).
Then each \(t_1+t_j\), \(j>1\), is positive, its complementary pair sum is negative, and their absolute values agree.
The total is \(2\sum_{j>1}(t_1+t_j)=4t_1\), since \(\sum_i t_i=0\).
For the inequality, the largest absolute entry is at most the total of either sign, namely \(\tfrac12\sum_i|t_i|\).
Equality holds precisely when one sign occurs only once; for two entries of each sign the inequality is strict.
This construction changes four exterior roots to three with logarithmic cost at most two, independently of the number of neutral blocks.
The three positive exterior logarithms are \(2(t_1+t_j)\), \(j=2,3,4\), in the orientation just used.
They are rationally independent: writing \(u_j=t_1+t_j\), one recovers \(t_1=(u_2+u_3+u_4)/2\) and \(t_j=u_j-t_1\), whereas the circuit gives rational rank three to the four \(t_i\).

If the oriented block product is \(+1\), the unsquared choice \(\gamma_0=x_1x_2\) gives the sharper conclusion.
All blocks have the same product sign, since Galois transitivity takes a block product to that sign or its inverse, which are equal.
Thus the products of complementary pairs in every block are already inverses without squaring.
The same orbit and distinctness argument gives a monic irreducible reciprocal minimal polynomial \(H_0\) of degree \(3m/2\), with exactly three real exterior roots and rationally independent positive logarithms.
Its measure is
\[
            \log M(H_0)=2\max_i|t_i|\le\log M(P).                  \tag{14}
\]
The equality condition is again that one sign among the \(t_i\) occurs only once.
For block product \(-1\), this inverse identification is not available for the unsquared products; the squared construction retains the unconditional bound (13).

<a id="neutral-inversion"></a>
## When a neutral block supplies inversion

If at least two blocks occur, it suffices that the exterior-block stabilizer induce every unsigned permutation of its labels.
Ordinary complex conjugation fixes every root in the exterior block, since those roots are real, and inverts every root in every neutral block, since those roots have modulus one.
It fixes all inverse-pair labels.

Choose a Galois automorphism taking the exterior block to another block.
Conjugating ordinary complex conjugation by it fixes the roots of that other block and inverts the roots of every remaining block.
In particular the resulting automorphism stabilizes \(\mathcal E\) and simultaneously inverts all its representatives.
Its unsigned permutation there is the identity.
Combining this inversion with any lift of an unsigned permutation removes whichever common sign that lift has.
Thus the stabilizer image is \(S_e\times\{1,-1\}\), as required above.
This does not require independently prescribed actions on neutral blocks.

<a id="full-action"></a>
## The sextic realizes the full action

Return to (5)--(7), and let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).
The cubic \(f\) is irreducible over \(K\): a reducible cubic over a field has a root there, which would give a root of the irreducible degree-six \(P\) in a degree-two field.
Its discriminant \(2861\) is not a square in \(K\).
Indeed, if \((u+v\sqrt2)^2=2861\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb Q\), then \(uv=0\).
The possibility \(v=0\) would make the integer \(2861\) a rational square, excluded by \(53^2<2861<54^2\).
The possibility \(u=0\) would make \(2861/2\) a rational square, impossible because its valuation at two is odd.

The transitive group of this irreducible cubic is therefore \(S_3\): the alternative subgroup \(A_3\) would fix the Vandermonde product of the roots and make the discriminant a square in \(K\).
Let \(F\) be the splitting field of \(f\) over \(K\).
It contains all roots \(r_i\) and their inverses, hence all roots of \(P\).
Conversely the splitting field of \(P\) contains \(K\), since
\[
                   \sqrt2=(r_1+r_2+r_3)-10.
\]
Thus \(F\) is also the splitting field of \(P\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and in particular is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).

An automorphism extending the nonidentity automorphism of \(K\) sends the \(r_i\) to a permutation of their inverses, since \(f^\sigma=-z^3f(1/z)\).
Composing with a suitable element of the \(S_3\) fixing \(K\) gives simultaneous inversion of all three \(r_i\).
The Galois action consequently realizes \(S_3\) and simultaneous inversion.

The single circuit block for \(P\) has the complete relation lattice in the [real circuit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit).
In the orientation \(r_1,r_2,r_3\), its generator is their product one.
The action just established is the full signed permutation action required in the saturation theorem.
The result with \(e=3\) therefore proves the saturation obstruction (5a).
The radical saturation is closed under multiplication, division, Galois conjugation and extraction of further roots, giving the stated obstruction to any finite iteration of those operations.

<a id="limits"></a>
## What this failed descent establishes

The [twice-applied ratio identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v145.md#twice) already identifies the obstruction to iterating the triple construction.
In this example choose the cyclic representatives
\[
                       r_1/r_2,\quad r_2/r_3,\quad r_3/r_1
\]
for \(Q\); their product is one.
Their six ordered ratios are precisely \(r_i^3,r_i^{-3}\), \(1\le i\le3\).
Thus two applications return the original six roots cubed, pay measure \(M(P)^3\), and still have three exterior roots.
The roots stay distinct because they are positive, so this power polynomial remains irreducible of degree six.

The successful mixed three-exterior calculation in [C345v57](Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#ratios-of-roots) uses two equal exterior logarithms.
One of its unordered-pair differences is zero, leaving only two exterior ratios.
For a real circuit, equality of two positive exterior logarithms is forbidden, and every unordered pair contributes an exterior ratio.
This is the precise point where its count reduction fails.

The count formula for general real circuits concerns the full ratio polynomial.
It does not rule out a smaller-exterior irreducible factor for other inputs.
Under the additional full-action hypotheses, the saturation result gives a stronger obstruction to all multiplicative operations and radical extraction.
In particular the sextic allows no such descent to one or two exterior conjugates.
It has fixed degree and fixed measure, however, and gives no obstruction to every proposed reduction along a sequence tending to one.
It also leaves operations producing units outside the radical saturation, for example by addition, untested.
No family of inputs satisfying these action hypotheses with measure tending to one is supplied here.
The [commensurable-log theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement) and its [classical product construction](Yin_C_G0345_v66.md#statement) retain their different hypotheses and positive conclusions.
A real circuit here has logarithmic rank \(e-1\), which is at least two, so the present example is outside their dimension-one hypothesis.
The coordinating agent proposed the full-action saturation extension; its orbit count and the sextic Galois verification were checked during collaborating author work.
No independent review is asserted here.
No conclusion about the unrestricted Lehmer gap follows.
