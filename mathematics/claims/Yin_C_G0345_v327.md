# Mahler measure in separated frequency bands with moving coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A comparison for a fixed number of bands

Fix integers \(m\ge1\) and \(1\le d<n\), and take complex Laurent polynomials \(a_0,\ldots,a_m\) supported in \([-d,d]\).
For \(F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^m a_j(X)Y^j\), use normalized circle measure \(d\nu=dt/(2\pi)\) and put
\[
 I=\iint\log|F(e^{it},e^{iv})|\,d\nu(t)d\nu(v),\qquad
 \mu=\int\log|F(e^{it},e^{i(nt+\phi)})|\,d\nu(t),\qquad D=n/d.
\]
Let \(A(t)\) be the largest absolute value among all real and imaginary parts of the coefficients \(a_j(e^{it})\).
Suppose \(A(t)\ge\kappa>0\) on the circle.
There are constants \(D_m,C_m\), depending only on \(m\), such that for \(D\ge D_m\) and every real \(\phi\)
\[
 |\mu-I|\le {C_m\over D}\{1+I-\log\kappa+m\log2+\log(2D)\}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Increasing the constants gives the same bound with \(\mu\) on the right.
In particular the absolute difference tends to zero if \(D\to\infty\) and \(\mu-\log\kappa\) is bounded above.
The scale in (1) may be taken as \(1/\sqrt2\) whenever one coefficient has modulus at least one at every point.

For integral \(a_j\) and \(\phi=0\), suppose at least one coefficient is a single nonzero Laurent monomial \(cX^u\), at any band index, with \(c\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
There exist \(\epsilon_m,\eta_m>0\) such that, for \(d\le\epsilon_m n\),
\[
             M(F(X,X^n))>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
             \log M(F(X,X^n))\ge\eta_m .                                     \tag{2}
\]
Every other row can have all its coefficients moving, with arbitrary signs, heights and multiplicities.
More generally (2) holds with constants depending also on a fixed lower bound \(\kappa_0>0\) for \(A\), if the greatest common divisor of the rows has Mahler measure one.
The latter condition holds for an irreducible diagonal of positive measure when at least two rows are nonzero and \(n>2d\), as proved below.

<a id="quadrature"></a>
## Relative clipping on a shifted grid

Divide every row by \(\kappa\) for the analytic proof.
Both means lose \(\log\kappa\), so assume \(A\ge1\).
Write \(v_s\) for the \(2m+2\) real coordinate functions of the rows, \(L=\log A\), \(f=1+L\) and \(E=\int f\,d\nu\).
Each \(v_s\) is a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\).
At every \(t\) some coefficient has modulus at least \(A(t)\).
For a polynomial of degree at most \(m\), each coefficient is at most \(2^m\) times its multiplicative Mahler measure by the elementary symmetric formulas.
It follows that \(E\le1+I+m\log2\).

Define \(V(t,v)=|F(e^{it},e^{iv})|/A(t)\), bounded by \(B=\sqrt2(m+1)\).
We compare the two means of \(\log V\), as the removed baseline has the same mean on both sides.
For \(0<u<1\), fix \(v\) and clip this function from below at \(\log u\).
It has variation \(O_m(d\log(B/u))\) in \(t\).
Indeed a change of maximizing coordinate can occur only at an equality \(v_s^2=v_h^2\).
On a region with a fixed maximizer, the squared ratio is \(|F(e^{it},e^{iv})|^2/v_s^2\).
Its clipping equations and the numerator of its derivative are trigonometric polynomials of degrees at most \(4d\).
Ignoring identities, these equations altogether cut the circle into \(O_m(d)\) monotone or constant pieces, since a nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degree \(k\) has at most \(2k\) zeros.
Identical coordinates in modulus allow fixed tie breaking, identically constant ratios have zero variation, and the chosen denominator never vanishes.
Conditioning on \(v=nt+\phi\) modulo \(2\pi\) gives a uniformly shifted grid of \(n\) points in \(t\).
The grid mean differs from the circle mean by at most the variation divided by \(n\), by integration over the \(n\) mesh cells.
Integrating in \(v\) proves the clipped comparison.

At fixed \(t\), the coefficients of \(F(e^{it},e^{iv})/A(t)\) are bounded by \(\sqrt2\) and one has modulus at least one.
The finite-derivative estimate below for this fixed shape gives relative length \(O_m(w^{1/m})\) where \(V\le w\le1\).
Integration in \(dw/w\) up to \(u\) bounds the independent clipping loss by \(O_m(u^{1/m})\).

<a id="charts"></a>
## Counting the exceptional fast periods in real coordinate charts

Use the full periods of the fast variable, of length \(2\pi/n\).
We need both a local and a whole-circle count.
In the local count, take the [critical-scale cover](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#packing) for \(f\) when \(E<\gamma d\), choosing fixed \(r_*>0\) and \(\gamma>0\) sufficiently small.
For each center \(x\), let \(r\) be its radius, \(M=dr\ge1\) and \(J=[x-4r,x+4r]\).
The cover satisfies \(\sum r=O(1)\) and \(\sum M^2=O(dE)\).
Each \(v_s\) has boundary \(\log^+|v_s|\le L\).
The [periodic strip majorant](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#analytic) gives \(\log^+|v_s(z)|\le C M\) on \(|z-x|\le64r\).
Here use strip half-width \(128r\) in that proof and choose \(r_*\le1/512\).
Cauchy's formula on disks of radius \(r\), and \(nr\ge D\), give the same bound with a constant depending on \(m\) for all \(v_s^{(h)}/n^h,\ 0\le h\le m\), on the disk of radius \(32r\).

For every coordinate \(\ell\) that maximizes absolute value somewhere in \(J\), choose such a point \(t_\ell\).
Then \(|v_\ell(t_\ell)|\ge1\).
In its chart put \(h_s=v_s/v_\ell\), and impose lower and upper signed thresholds for every \(h_s\), their magnitudes in \([2,3]\).
The open chart consists of points where these ratios lie strictly between the thresholds and the denominator is nonzero.
It contains every point where \(\ell\) maximizes absolute value.
A boundary inside \(J\) meets a threshold since \(A\ge1\) prevents all coordinates tending to zero while the ratios are bounded.
Inside the chart \(|v_\ell|\le A\le3|v_\ell|\).
For each \(s\) also choose two signed thresholds of magnitudes in \([\delta,2\delta]\) for each \(n^{-k}h_s^{(k)}\), \(1\le k\le m\).
Here \(\delta>0\) is a sufficiently small constant depending only on \(m\).

There are \(O_m(M)\) real threshold points in \(J\) for all the chosen charts.
To see this, write \(n^{-k} h_s^{(k)}=N_{sk}/v_\ell^{k+1}\), starting with \(N_{s0}=v_s\).
The quotient rule gives \(N_{s,k+1}=v_\ell N_{sk}'/n-(k+1)N_{sk}v_\ell'/n\).
Thus each numerator is a fixed polynomial in the normalized derivatives of the coordinates through order \(k\), and has logarithmic upper bound \(O_m(M)\) on the smaller disk.
For each individual threshold \(\tau\) choose one of the two endpoints of its allowed signed range so that \(|N_{sk}-\tau v_\ell^{k+1}|\ge\min(1,\delta)/2\) at \(t_\ell\).
The two endpoint values differ by at least \(\min(1,\delta)|v_\ell(t_\ell)|^{k+1}\).
Jensen's formula centered at \(t_\ell\), on a disk of radius between \(12r\) and \(16r\), counts \(O_m(M)\) zeros within radius \(8r\), which contains \(J\).
This also counts the chart boundaries by taking \(k=0\).
In the whole-circle version take \(J\) to be the circle and choose \(t_\ell\) there.
The same threshold expressions are nonzero trigonometric polynomials of degree at most \((k+1)d\), giving \(O_m(d)\) points globally.

Flag every period meeting a threshold point, and every period meeting a chart where for some \(s\) the first derivative order \(k\ge1\) outside its thresholds occurs.
For the local count use only periods inside \(J\), counting its two edge periods separately.
Off the threshold points the chart membership is constant on each resulting interval.
Indeed a chart cannot be exited through a denominator zero without first crossing one of its ratio thresholds.
On an interval where the specified \(k\) is the first violating order, the preceding normalized derivative is bounded by \(3\) if \(k=1\), or by \(2\delta\) otherwise.
Its derivative has constant sign and magnitude at least \(\delta n\).
Such an interval has length \(O_m(1/n)\), so meets only \(O_m(1)\) periods.
This proves a bound \(C_m M\) for the local number of flagged periods, or \(C_m d\) on the whole circle.
For an unflagged full period, choose a maximizing coordinate at one point.
Its chart persists throughout the period, all its ratios are bounded and their normalized derivatives of orders \(1,\ldots,m\) have magnitude at most \(2\delta\).

<a id="finite-type"></a>
## Small values and a lower bound on each remaining period

In the fast coordinate \(v=nt+\phi\) divide the diagonal polynomial by \(v_\ell\) on an unflagged period.
It becomes \(g(v)=\sum_{j=0}^m w_j(v)e^{ijv}\), with real and imaginary parts of \(w_j\) given by the chart ratios.
At least one coordinate is identically one.
Freeze the coefficients at one point of the period to obtain \(g_0(v)\).
All coefficients vary by \(O(\delta)\) over this period and their derivatives of positive orders through \(m\) are \(O(\delta)\).
Hence the derivatives of \(g-g_0\) through order \(m\) are \(O_m(\delta)\) there by the product rule.
Parseval gives \(\sup|g_0|\ge1\).
The invertible Vandermonde matrix with entries \((ij)^h,\ 0\le h,j\le m\), acting on the phase-weighted coefficients, also gives
\(\max_{0\le h\le m}|g_0^{(h)}(v)|\ge c_m>0\) at every point.
Here the zeroth derivative is included, which covers constant shapes.
All derivatives of the fixed shape through order \(m+1\) are bounded by a constant depending only on \(m\).
Subdivide the period into a bounded number of sufficiently short intervals.
At each midpoint choose an order and a real or imaginary part attaining at least \(c_m/\sqrt2\) in absolute value.
This part of the derivative has constant sign and stays bounded away from zero on the interval, also for \(g\) by choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small.
For order zero there are no sufficiently small values, and for order \(h\ge1\) the [real divided-difference estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v324.md#orders) gives sublevel length \(O_m(w^{1/h})\) at threshold \(w\).
Using \(A/|v_\ell|\le3\), one obtains relative length \(O_m(w^{1/m})\) where the diagonal \(V\le w\le1\).
The same argument with constant coefficients proves the fixed-shape estimate used for independent clipping.
Finally \(\sup|g|\ge1/2\) for small \(\delta\) and \(|v_\ell|\ge1/3\) throughout the chart, so each good period supplies a point where the unnormalized diagonal has modulus at least \(1/6\).

<a id="cost"></a>
## Logarithmic losses on runs and absorption of the error

Write \(G(t)=F(e^{it},e^{i(nt+\phi)})\).
When \(E<\gamma d\), consider the flagged periods meeting a cover arc \([x-r,x+r]\).
Since \(nr=DM\), taking \(D_m\) large ensures that a run of consecutive flags reaches an adjacent unflagged period inside \(J\); even all \(C_mM+6\) periods together have length at most \(r/100\).
Adjoining a good period gives intervals of total length \(O_m(M/n)\); on each such interval of length \(l\) there is a point \(\xi\) with \(|G(\xi)|\ge1/6\).
On the complex disk \(|z-\xi|\le8l\) the slow coordinates have log positive parts \(O_m(M)\), and the fast exponentials cost at most \(8mnl=O_m(M)\) in logarithm.
Thus \(\log^+|G|\le C_mM\) there.
The [disk zero count and logarithmic integral estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v325.md#clusters), applied to \(6G\), bounds \(\int_{\rm run}\log^-|G|\,dt\) by \(O_m(Ml)\).
Since \(\log A\le C_mM\) on the run, this also bounds its relative clipping loss, using \((\log u-\log V)_+\le\log A+\log^-|G|\).
Summation costs \(O_m(\sum M^2/n)=O_m(dE/n)\).
On unflagged periods the integrated sublevel estimate costs \(O_m(u^{1/m}\sum(r+1/n))=O_m(u^{1/m})\).

For \(E\ge\gamma d\), use the whole-circle count, with \(O_m(d)\) flagged periods in total.
Since \(D\) is large a good period exists and each run, with a neighbor, has length \(l=O_m(d/n)\), with the sum of these lengths of the same order.
In a fixed strip \(|\operatorname{Im}z|\le1\), the same strip majorant with half-width two gives \(\log^+|v_s(z)|=O(d+E)=O(E)\), since its Poisson kernels there are bounded.
Enlarge \(D_m\) to put all disks of radius \(8l\) inside this strip.
The fast terms cost \(O_m(d)=O_m(E)\).
The argument at the good neighbor now costs \(O_m(El)\) on each run, again giving \(O_m(dE/n)\) in total.
This treats polynomials of large height without introducing the logarithm of a global coefficient bound.

The two clipping losses and the grid comparison give \(O_m(u^{1/m}+D^{-1}(E+\log(B/u)))\).
With \(u=D^{-m}\), the energy estimate gives (1) after restoring the original scale.
Writing \(J=I-\log\kappa+m\log2\ge0\), the lower bound for \(\mu\) absorbs its \(C_mJ/D\) term when \(C_m/D<1/2\), giving the asserted version in terms of \(\mu\).

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Integral specializations and common factors of the rows

Assume at least two occupied rows.
Their index width is at most \(m\).
If the zero set has a \(Y\)-dependent geometric component which is not a torsion translate of a subtorus, the [partial-degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(I\ge c_m'>0\), independent of \(d\).
Otherwise the [factorization of the horizontal components](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) gives \(F\), up to a unit monomial, as a base Laurent polynomial \(h(X)\) times factors \(\Phi_q(X^aY^b)\) with primitive \((a,b)\), \(b>0\).
Their product has lowest \(Y\)-coefficient a Laurent unit, so \(h\) is the common gcd of the rows up to a unit, including content.
Each factor has \(X\)-width \(|a|\varphi(q)\le2d\), since widths add in products over the integral domain \(\mathbb Q[Y^{\pm1}]\).
For \(n>2d\) we have \(a+nb\ne0\).
Specialization therefore gives cyclotomic products up to Laurent units, with measure one, and \(\mu=\log M(h)\).
If a row is \(cX^u\), its divisor \(h\) is an integer times a Laurent unit, so \(\mu\) is either zero or at least \(\log2\).
With a single occupied monomial row the same statement holds directly.
With a fixed lower bound \(A\ge\kappa_0\) and \(M(h)=1\), the purely torsion case always has \(\mu=0\), including when a single row is present.
In the nontorsion case, apply (1) at \(\kappa=1/\sqrt2\) for a nonzero integral monomial row or at \(\kappa_0\) in the more general case.
Take \(D\) sufficiently large that \(C_m/D\le1/4\) and the additive loss is at most \(c_m'/4\).
This gives \(\mu\ge c_m'/2\), proving the asserted gap.

For the irreducible case let \(h\) be the integral Laurent gcd of all the rows.
The quotient \(F/h\) has positive \(Y\)-width and its \(X\)-width is at most \(2d\) by additivity.
For \(n>2d\) its occupied rows have disjoint specialized exponent intervals and at least two survive.
Its diagonal is a nonunit, so irreducibility of \(F(X,X^n)\) in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\) forces \(h\) to be a Laurent unit.
