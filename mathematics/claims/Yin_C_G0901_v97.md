# Inertia and signed permutation groups of unit factors of consecutive-coefficient four-term polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Three gcds controlling the inverse-pair action

Let \(q\ge1,\ p=q+1,\ L=2q+1\). Let \(0<u<v\) be coprime integers and \(s,\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Consider
\[
      P(X)=q(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+s p(X^u+\epsilon X^v)
\]
and any nontorsion algebraic unit root \(\beta\) of \(P\).
For a root at an arbitrary common dilation first take its \(g\)th power, so the root field and splitting group below refer to this primitive \(\beta\).
Let \(S\) be its monic minimum polynomial and define the finite set
\[
 \mathcal T=\big\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ \ell\mid
        \gcd(q,v-u)\,\gcd(p,v+u)\,\gcd(L,uv)\big\}.
\]
At every finite prime outside \(\mathcal T\), every inertia element in the splitting group of \(S\) moves at most four actual roots.
If \(S\) is reciprocal, write \(2n=\deg S\).
In its action on the \(n\) inverse pairs, each such element is a transposition or the identity.
For any invariant partition of the labels into blocks of size at least two, the normal field of the action on the blocks is unramified at all finite primes outside \(\mathcal T\).

If the three displayed gcds are all one, the group on the labels is \(S_n\).
When \(n\ge3\), the splitting group on the actual roots contains every even sign change on the \(n\) inverse pairs.
In particular one representative from each pair is multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, and the resulting \(n\)-dimensional signed representation is absolutely irreducible.
Thus any reciprocal unit factor with a proper label group in \(S_n\), or with a multiplicative dependence among such representatives for \(n\ge3\), requires at least one of the three gcds to exceed one.
This is uniform in degree and in the exponent sizes.
For \(q\ge5\) reciprocity already follows from the [strict \(13/10\) bound](Yin_C_G0345_v886.md#statement) and [Smyth's nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).

<a id="critical"></a>
## Repeated roots of the reduction

On the torus put \(T=sX^u,\ Y=-\epsilon s X^v\).
The equation \(P=0\) is exactly the [linear fractional relation](Yin_C_G0345_v878.md#quotients)
\[
                   (p+qT)Y=q+pT .
\]
In characteristic \(\ell\nmid pqL\) the function \(R(T)=(q+pT)/(p+qT)\) and its denominator are nonzero at every nonzero root of \(P\): the two linear forms have determinant \(p^2-q^2=L\ne0\).
For \(H=Y/R(T)\), \(P\) has the same root multiplicity there as \(H-1\).
The logarithmic derivative is
\[
 \frac{XH'}H=\frac{v(p+qT)(q+pT)-uLT}{(p+qT)(q+pT)} .                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(\ell\mid u\) or \(\ell\mid v\), the numerator cannot vanish at such a root, since \(\gcd(u,v)=1\).
Otherwise it is a quadratic in \(T\) with nonzero leading and constant coefficients.
The map \(X\mapsto T\) has nonzero derivative at every nonzero \(X\).
For a fixed critical \(T\), the graph determines \(Y\), and the two signed powers determine \(X\) uniquely by an integer Bézout identity for \(u,v\).
At a simple zero of the quadratic the derivative \(H'\) thus vanishes to order one, so the root of \(H-1\) has multiplicity at most two.
At a double zero it has multiplicity at most three.
Indeed a zero of multiplicity \(j\) gives derivative vanishing at least to order \(j-1\), also in positive characteristic.
Consequently the total multiplicity of all multiple nonzero roots is at most four (two double roots or at most one triple root).

If \(\ell\mid q\), the only nonzero roots in the reduction come from \(1+\epsilon X^{v-u}\), which is separable outside \(\mathcal T\).
If \(\ell\mid p\), the same applies to \(1+\epsilon X^{u+v}\).
Finally for \(\ell\mid L\) outside \(\mathcal T\), both exponents are invertible and \(p\equiv-q\bmod\ell\).
The graph equation becomes a nonzero scalar multiple of \((T-1)(Y+1)=0\).
Each signed-power factor is separable on the torus, and the two factors can have at most one common root by the same Bézout identity.
Thus on this branch there is at most one double root.

<a id="inertia"></a>
## From bounded inertia support to the splitting group

The monic unit polynomial \(S\) divides \(P\) integrally and its constant term is a unit.
Its reduction has full degree and no zero root, also when the degree of the parent drops.
In a splitting field with a chosen place over \(\ell\), inertia can only permute roots within a common residue class.
Simple residues therefore fix the actual roots above them individually, and the preceding multiplicity bounds prove the four-root assertion.
For reciprocal \(S\), moving the label of an inverse pair moves both of its roots.
At most two labels can move, proving the transposition assertion.
Moving a block of a nontrivial invariant partition of block size at least two would move at least two whole blocks, hence at least four labels.
This proves the unramified statement on the normal block field.

Now assume \(\mathcal T\) is empty.
The group generated by all inertia groups at finite places in the splitting field is normal, and its fixed field is unramified at every finite rational prime.
The absolute discriminant bound of Minkowski makes that field \(\mathbb Q\). [Minkowski's corollary, Sutherland, Lecture 14, Corollary 14.21](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2018fa/LectureNotes14.pdf#page=8)
Thus the inertia elements generate the full group \(G\).
Its transitive label image is generated by transpositions, and is \(S_n\): the transpositions generate the full symmetric group on each connected component of their edge graph, and the components must form a single orbit under this generated action.

Let \(n\ge3\).
The kernel \(J\) on labels is a subgroup of the sign changes \(\{\pm1\}^n\).
If it contains a nonconstant vector, commuting it with a lift of a transposition swapping two unlike signs yields a flip on exactly those two labels.
Conjugation by the full label group gives every double flip, which generate the even changes.
To rule out the remaining case, suppose \(J\) consists only of constant sign changes.
An inertia element with trivial label image has to be the identity since the simultaneous inversion moves \(2n>4\) roots.
At least one inertia element projects to a transposition, and conjugating it by \(G\) supplies a lift \(h_{ij}\) supported on the four actual roots above every pair of labels \(i,j\).
There is at most one such supported lift for a prescribed transposition: two would differ by a kernel sign change fixing the other \(2n-4>0\) roots.
Its square is trivial for the same reason.
After provisionally orienting the labels, this involution transports the orientation on \(i\) to that on \(j\) with some sign \(\delta_{ij}=\delta_{ji}\).
Conjugating \(h_{jk}\) by \(h_{ij}\) gives the unique supported lift \(h_{ik}\), hence \(\delta_{ik}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{jk}\) for distinct labels.
Orient each label using \(\delta_{1i}\) relative to the first label.
Every supported transposition now acts by pure permutation on the chosen orientations.
These and the identity include all inertia elements, so \(G\) preserves the chosen half of the roots, contradicting irreducibility of \(S\).
This proves the kernel assertion.

The even changes distinguish any two coordinate characters by flipping the first of those coordinates and a third.
Averaging against each character isolates the corresponding coordinate line, and transitivity proves absolute irreducibility of the formal signed module, as in the [sign-character criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs).
Its natural map onto the rational span of the actual root classes is nonzero and injective by irreducibility, giving multiplicative independence.
For example with \(u=1,\ v=pq k,\ \gcd(k,L)=1\), all three gcds are one for every \(q\) and \(k\), since \(pq\) is coprime to \(L\).
Thus a reciprocal unit factor with a smaller label group or a dependence cannot occur anywhere on these infinite exponent progressions, regardless of sign.
The conclusions allow a unit factor with the specified symmetric action and do not settle existence for unbounded \(q\).

<a id="pairs"></a>
## An exact identity on pairs of conjugates

There is also a two-root version of the [single-root divisibilities](Yin_C_G0345_v878.md#statement), for any unit factor under the initial hypotheses, without reciprocity or a gcd restriction on \(q,p,L\) and the exponents.
Put \(E_i=p+qs\beta_i^u,\ Y_i=-\epsilon s\beta_i^v\) at each conjugate \(\beta_i\).
The graph and its inverse give \((p-qY_i)E_i=L\).
Both \(\mathbb Q(\beta^u)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\beta^v)\) equal \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), since the graph is invertible and Bézout recovers \(\beta\) from its two powers.
In particular neither powering identifies distinct conjugates.
Subtracting two graph equations gives
\[
        E_iE_j(\beta_i^v-\beta_j^v)
                       =-\epsilon L(\beta_i^u-\beta_j^u).
\]
For \(i\ne j\) put \(\gamma=\beta_i/\beta_j\), \(U_h=(\gamma^h-1)/(\gamma-1)\).
In \(\mathbb Z[\beta_i,\beta_j]\) both \(U_u\) and \(U_v\) divide \(L\).
Indeed these elements are coprime in that ring: the identity \(U_{h+k}=U_h+\gamma^hU_k\) and invertibility of \(\gamma\) reduce their ideal to \((U_{\gcd(u,v)})=(1)\).
The displayed difference shows \(E_iE_j U_v=-\epsilon L\beta_j^{u-v}U_u\), giving \(U_v\mid L\).
Multiplication by the two complementary factors \(p-qY_i,p-qY_j\) and cancellation of \(L\) likewise gives \(U_u\mid L\).

If \(D=\deg S\) and \(\operatorname{disc}(\beta^h)\) denotes the discriminant of the monic minimum of that power, then
\[
 \frac{|\operatorname{disc}(\beta^v)|}{|\operatorname{disc}(\beta^u)|}
    =\frac{L^{D(D-1)}}{|N_{\mathbb Q(\beta)/\mathbb Q}(p+qs\beta^u)|^{2(D-1)}}.
\]
This follows by squaring the Vandermonde product of the difference identities.
For \(\ell\nmid L\) the two localized power orders are in fact both \(\mathbb Z_\ell[\beta]\).
The norms of \(E=p+qs\beta^u\) and its complementary integral factor \(p-qY\) are \(\ell\)-adic units.
Multiplication by either denominator thus has invertible determinant on the full-rank power order containing it, giving an inverse there.
The two graph formulas yield mutual containment of the power orders, which then contain the unit inverses of both powers and hence \(\beta\) by Bézout.
