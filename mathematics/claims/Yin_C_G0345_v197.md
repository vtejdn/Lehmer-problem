# Short multiples of reciprocal graph polynomials of bounded Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound for the whole polynomial of a connected graph

For a nonempty finite undirected simple graph \(G\) with adjacency matrix \(A\), put
\[
          P_G(X)=X^{|V(G)|}\det((X+X^{-1})I-A)
                 =\det((X^2+1)I-XA).
\]
This monic polynomial has constant term one.
Write \(m(P)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, \(\|P\|_1\) for coefficient length, and \(\lambda(\alpha)\) for the least length of a nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(\alpha\).
There is an absolute \(C>0\) with the following properties whenever \(m(P_G)\le B\).

* If \(G\) is connected, the entire \(P_G\), with multiplicities, divides a nonzero integer polynomial of length at most \(L(B)=\exp(\exp(C(1+B)))\).
* For arbitrary \(G\), the product of all noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(P_G\), with multiplicity, divides one nonzero integer polynomial of length at most \(L(B)\).
  Every individual root of \(P_G\) also satisfies \(\lambda(\alpha)\le L(B)\).

Consequently bounded graph Salem numbers and bounded graph Pisot numbers in the sense of [McKee and Smyth](../audits/literature/MS05b/Yin_U001.md#ms05b-u001) have bounded minimum relation length.
For arbitrary Salem numbers \(\tau\) the conclusion concerns instead the cost of graph realization: for every tree \(T\) realizing \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\) as an eigenvalue, the quotient \(P_T/p_\tau\) by the minimal polynomial satisfies
\[
         m(P_T/p_\tau)\ \ge\ c\log\log(3+\lambda(\tau))-C'-m(\tau)
         \tag{1}
\]
for absolute \(c,C'>0\).
Such trees exist for every \(\tau\).
Thus, along any sequence of bounded Salem numbers with minimum relation length tending to infinity, the measure of the complementary polynomial tends to infinity uniformly over all realizing trees.
No existence of such a sequence is assumed or proved.

<a id="graphs"></a>
## Bounding the number of vertices of degree different from two

Each adjacency eigenvalue \(t\), with multiplicity, contributes the quadratic \(X^2-tX+1\) to \(P_G\).
It contributes \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|t|/2)\) to the logarithmic measure when \(|t|>2\), and zero otherwise.
The largest eigenvalue \(\rho\) of \(A\) is its spectral radius, since \(|v^{\mathsf T}Av|\le |v|^{\mathsf T}A|v|\) for real \(v\), with coordinatewise absolute values.
The maximum vertex degree \(\Delta\) satisfies \(\Delta\le\rho^2\) by applying \(A\) to a coordinate unit vector.
Hence \(\Delta\le(2\cosh B)^2\).

By the [classification stated in McKee and Smyth](../audits/literature/MS05b/Yin_U001.md#ms05b-u001), every graph of spectral radius greater than two has a connected induced subgraph of spectral radius greater than two on at most ten vertices.
There is an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that every such subgraph contributes at least \(\eta\) from its largest eigenvalue: take the minimum of \(\operatorname{arcosh}(\rho(H)/2)\) over the finitely many simple graphs of at most ten vertices with \(\rho(H)>2\).
Select such a subgraph in the current induced graph, remove its vertices and all their current neighbors, and continue until the remaining graph has spectral radius at most two or is empty.
The selected subgraphs have no edges between them in \(G\).
If their number is \(k\), their union as an induced subgraph has at least \(k\) eigenvalues at least \(2\cosh\eta\).
Interlacing puts at least \(k\) eigenvalues of \(G\) above or at that threshold, so \(k\eta\le B\).
The removed vertex set \(S\) has size at most \(10k(1+\Delta)\).

Suppose now that \(G\) is connected.
When \(S\ne\varnothing\), every component of \(G-S\) has an edge to \(S\), so there are at most \(\Delta |S|\) such components.
Each has at most two vertices of degree at least three within the component, by the same source's classification of connected graphs of spectral radius at most two.
At most \(\Delta |S|\) vertices outside \(S\) meet \(S\).
Thus at most \((1+3\Delta)|S|\) vertices have degree at least three in \(G\).
If \(S\) is empty there are at most two directly by the classification.
If \(G\) has at least two vertices and \(b\) of them have degree at least three, the degree sum and \(|E(G)|\ge |V(G)|-1\) show that the number of leaves is at most \(2+\Delta b\).

Keep the vertices of degree different from two, including the isolated vertex in a singleton graph.
For a cycle keep one marked vertex.
Between kept vertices the graph now consists of paths of positive lengths, allowing parallel paths and a path returning to its starting vertex.
Write \(v\) for the number of kept vertices and \(e\) for the number of these paths.
The bounds just proved and \(2e\le\Delta v\) give
\[
                    s:=v+2e\le \exp(C_1(1+B))
                    \tag{2}
\]
for an absolute \(C_1\), independently of the lengths of all the paths.

<a id="multiple"></a>
## A determinant with bounded coefficient length

Orient each of the \(e\) paths, giving it length \(\ell\) and endpoints \(u,w\), possibly equal.
Use one unknown \(c_u\) for each kept vertex and two unknowns \(a,b\) for each path.
For \(X\ne0,\pm1\), the solutions of the adjacency recurrence with parameter \(t=X+X^{-1}\) on a path have the form
\[
                   f(j)=aX^j+bX^{-j}\qquad(0\le j\le\ell).
\]
Impose \(f(0)=c_u\) and \(f(\ell)=c_w\) for each path.
At each kept vertex \(u\) impose \((X+X^{-1})c_u=\sum f(j)\), summing the neighbor value at each incident path end: \(f(1)\) at the start and \(f(\ell-1)\) at the finish.
A returning path contributes both ends, and a path of length one simply uses the opposite endpoint.
This is a square system of size \(s\) over \(\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\).
Denote its determinant by \(Q(X)\).

For each \(X\ne0,\pm1\) the kernel is isomorphic to the \(t\)-eigenspace of \(A\).
Indeed the endpoint and vertex equations give the eigenvector, and two consecutive values uniquely determine \(a,b\) since \(X\ne X^{-1}\), also for length one.
In particular \(Q\) is not identically zero: choose a positive \(X\) with \(X+X^{-1}>\rho\).
At any root \(x\ne0,\pm1\) of \(P_G\), the nullity at \(x\) is the multiplicity \(h\) of \(t=x+x^{-1}\), because \(A\) is real symmetric.
Constant invertible row operations make \(h\) rows of the matrix vanish at \(x\), so its determinant vanishes to order at least \(h\).
The quadratic corresponding to \(t\) has a simple zero at \(x\); hence \(Q\) contains the full multiplicity of \(x\) in \(P_G\).

At \(x=\pm1\), the eigenvectors for \(t=\pm2\) on each path have the form \((a+bj)x^j\), so their values at the kept vertices and two parameters per path determine them.
Each such eigenspace has dimension at most \(s\), including the singleton case.
The quadratic for \(t=\pm2\) is \((X\mp1)^2\), hence \((X^2-1)^{2s}\) covers the possible endpoint multiplicities.
It follows that a monomial shift of \((X^2-1)^{2s}Q(X)\) clearing all negative exponents is a nonzero integer multiple of \(P_G\), since \(P_G\) is monic with nonzero constant.

Each Laurent matrix entry has length at most \(2+2\Delta+3\), independently of the path lengths, by the displayed equations.
The determinant expansion and submultiplicativity therefore bound the length of this multiple by \(2^{2s}s!(5+2\Delta)^s\).
Together with (2) and \(\Delta\le(2\cosh B)^2\) this is at most \(\exp(\exp(C_2(1+B)))\).
This uses both the bound on the number of paths and the fact that each contributes two exponentials, even when its length grows without bound.
The [endpoint gap for a fixed subdivision graph](Yin_C_G0345_v128.md#effective) instead bounds outlying eigenvalues away from two for a given topology.

For a disconnected graph each component with positive measure contains a witness contributing at least \(\eta\); there are at most \(B/\eta\) such components.
The other components have only cyclotomic irreducible factors by Kronecker's theorem.
Multiply the short multiples from the positive-measure components, taking \(1\) for an empty product.
The number of factors can be absorbed by increasing the absolute constant in \(L(B)\).
Roots of unity have relations of length two.
This proves both conclusions for disconnected graphs without asserting a bounded-length multiple for arbitrarily many cyclotomic components.

## Graph Salem numbers, their limits, and the cost of tree realization

For a connected nontrivial Salem graph with largest eigenvalue \(t>2\), let \(w>1\) satisfy \(w+w^{-1}=t\).
In the nonbipartite case \(t\) is the only eigenvalue outside \([-2,2]\), and the graph Salem number is \(\tau=w\).
In the bipartite case the outliers are \(t,-t\), and the graph Salem number is \(\tau=w^2\).
Thus in both conventions \(m(P_G)=\log\tau\).
Disconnected Salem graphs give the same conclusion from the component containing the positive outlier, with any other components contributing zero measure.
The bound already proved gives a relation at \(w\) of length at most \(L(\log\tau)\).
If \(H(w)=0\), the even polynomial \(H(X)H(-X)\) gives a relation at \(w^2\) of length at most \(\|H\|_1^2\).
Hence \(\lambda(\tau)\le L(\log\tau)^2\) in either case.

For positive real \(x_i\to x>1\) with \(\lambda(x_i)\le L\), reverse a relation polynomial of length at most \(L\) at each \(x_i\), obtaining a polynomial at \(1/x_i\) with nonzero constant.
Pass to a subsequence with a fixed number of terms and the same ordered nonzero coefficients; for every exponent take a further subsequence where it is fixed or tends to infinity.
The terms with diverging exponent tend to zero since eventually \(1/x_i\) is bounded above by a constant less than one.
The surviving terms have different exponents and include the constant, giving a nonzero integer polynomial of length at most \(L\) at \(1/x\).
Reversing it gives \(\lambda(x)\le L\).
For graph Pisot numbers \(1<x\le e^B\), take a sequence of graph Salem numbers tending to \(x\) and eventually bounded by \(e^{B+1}\).
The preceding bound then holds uniformly with \(L=L(B+1)^2\).

For an arbitrary Salem number \(\tau\), its trace \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\) is a totally real algebraic integer and therefore an adjacency eigenvalue of some finite tree by [Salez's theorem](../audits/literature/Sal15/Yin_U001.md#sal15-u001).
In every such tree \(T\) the minimal polynomial \(p_\tau\) divides \(P_T\), so \(\lambda(\tau)\le L(m(P_T))\).
Taking two logarithms, adjusting absolute constants for small lengths, and using \(m(P_T)=m(\tau)+m(P_T/p_\tau)\) gives (1).
This pays for all the other eigenvalues in the realization; the individual conjugates of \(\tau\) do not bound that cost.
