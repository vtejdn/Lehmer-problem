# Primitive cyclotomic samples and a Mahler gap at conductors with at most two prime divisors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Sampling one Galois orbit at its interpolation degree

Let \(n\ge2\), let \(\zeta_n\) be a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity, and set
\[
       U_n(P)=\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}
                  \sum_{\substack{1\le u\le n\\(u,n)=1}}|P(\zeta_n^u)|.
\]
Fix \(B\ge0\) and an integer \(H\ge0\).
Suppose \(P\) is a nonzero integer polynomial of degree at most \(\varphi(n)+H\) and \(U_n(P)\le B\).
If \(n\) has at most two distinct prime divisors, there exists a product \(D(X)\) of boundedly many factors \(1-X^l\), \(l\ge1\), such that \(DP\) has boundedly many terms.
Both bounds depend only on \(B,H\).
Thus \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\ge\eta(B,H)>0\), uniformly over the prime divisors, their exponents and the coefficients of \(P\).

For conductors with at most \(r\) distinct prime divisors, write \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), \(s=n/q\), and
\[
                     P(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}X^j P_j(X^s).
\]
At most \(K(B,r)+H+1\) of the polynomials \(P_j\) are nonzero.
Each has degree at most \(\varphi(q)+H\) and satisfies \(U_q(P_j)\le B\).
More generally the bound \(K(B,r)\) on the number of nonzero values \(P_j(\zeta_n^s)\) holds without any degree bound on \(P\).
In particular a bounded-binomial, bounded-term conclusion as above for squarefree conductors with at most \(r\) prime divisors would give it for all conductors with that many divisors.
No restriction on the values at imprimitive roots is assumed.

<a id="support"></a>
## Isolating the primitive values

Write \(D_q(Y)=\prod_{p\mid q}(1-Y^{q/p})\) and \(F(X)=D_q(X^s)P(X)\).
On the \(n\)-th roots of unity the multiplier vanishes at every imprimitive root and has modulus at most \(2^r\).
Indeed an imprimitive root has order dividing \(n/p\) for some \(p\mid n\).
Thus \(n^{-1}\sum_{\xi^n=1}|F(\xi)|\le 2^rB\).
The cyclic coefficient function of the remainder of \(F\) modulo \(X^n-1\) is integral and has Fourier algebra norm equal to this average.
By [Sanders's finite-group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) it is a signed sum of at most \(K(B,r)\) coset indicators.
At \(X=\zeta_n\), the polynomial of any coset with more than one element vanishes by the finite geometric sum.
Only singleton cosets contribute, so \(F(\zeta_n)\) is a signed sum of at most \(K(B,r)\) powers of \(\zeta_n\).

Put \(y=\zeta_n^s\), of order \(q\).
The extension \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q(y)\) has degree \(\varphi(n)/\varphi(q)=s\).
The first \(s\) powers of \(\zeta_n\) form a basis over \(\mathbb Q(y)\).
Group the signed sum by exponent modulo \(s\) and compare it with \(\sum_j\zeta_n^j D_q(y) P_j(y)\).
Since \(D_q(y)\ne0\), at most \(K(B,r)\) of the \(P_j(y)\) can be nonzero.

If \(P_j(y)=0\) but \(P_j\ne0\), the polynomial \(\Phi_q\) divides \(P_j\) and \(\deg P\ge j+s\varphi(q)=j+\varphi(n)\).
The assumed degree bound then forces \(j\le H\).
Every nonzero block also satisfies \(\deg P_j\le\varphi(q)+H\).
For any primitive \(q\)-th root \(v\), all \(s\) solutions of \(x^s=v\) are primitive \(n\)-th roots.
Indeed they are \(n\)-th roots and their orders must contain the full prime power dividing \(n\) at each \(p\mid q\).
Character orthogonality within this fiber gives
\[
          P_j(v)=\frac1s\sum_{x^s=v}x^{-j}P(x).
\]
Averaging its absolute-value bound over primitive \(v\) proves the claimed bound on each \(U_q(P_j)\).

<a id="squarefree"></a>
## Clearing the remaining samples at one or two primes

For \(q=p\) prime, \(\deg D_q=1=q-\varphi(q)\).
For \(q=pt\) with \(p,t\) distinct primes, \(\deg D_q=p+t=q-\varphi(q)+1\).
Thus every nonzero polynomial \(G_j(Y)=D_q(Y)P_j(Y)\) has degree at most \(q+H+1\) and its full \(q\)-grid average is at most \(4B\).
Its cyclic coefficients are again a bounded signed sum of coset indicators.
The ordinary representative \(R_j\) in degrees \(0,\ldots,q-1\) is a bounded sum of finite geometric progressions, each made a binomial or a monomial by multiplication by one \(1-Y^l\).
Also \(G_j-R_j\) is a sum of at most \(2(H+2)\) monomials before collection, since each original term above degree \(q-1\) is subtracted at its residue class and retained at its original position.
Consequently a product \(E_j(Y)\) of boundedly many binomials \(1-Y^l\) makes \(E_jG_j\) an integer polynomial with bounded term count.
This is the single-grid [consecutive-block argument](Yin_C_G0345_v81.md#blocks) with its clearing factors retained.

Let \(I\) be the bounded set of indices of nonzero \(P_j\).
Use the common multiplier
\[
                  D(X)=D_q(X^s)\prod_{j\in I}E_j(X^s).
\]
In the \(j\)-th summand of \(DP\), the product \(E_j D_q P_j\) is sparse and the remaining multipliers have bounded term count because they are products of boundedly many binomials.
The sum over \(I\) therefore gives the asserted bound.
All factors of \(D\) have Mahler measure one, so the [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) applies with the original measure.
The same product argument, using a separate clearing product on each nonzero \(P_j\), proves the general reduction to squarefree conductors.

The interpolation degree is necessary for a conclusion covering all integer polynomials in this sense.
For any \(Q\ne0\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), \(\Phi_n Q\) has zero primitive average and measure \(M(Q)\).
Allowing unbounded excess above \(\varphi(n)\), even on prime conductors alone, realizes the entire unrestricted spectrum.
