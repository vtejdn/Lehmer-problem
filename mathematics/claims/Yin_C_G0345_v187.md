# The eventual minimum number of terms of a rational relation on a power

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number of conjugates modulo roots of unity

Let \(\beta\ne0\) be algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\).
Group its distinct conjugates into equivalence classes by declaring two equivalent when their ratio is a root of unity, and let \(r\) be the number of classes.
For \(z\ne0\) algebraic let \(\nu(z)\) be the minimum number of nonzero terms of a nonzero polynomial in \(\mathbb Q[X]\) vanishing at \(z\).
The coefficients and support span are unrestricted.
Then
\[
             \nu(\beta^n)\le r+1\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
             \nu(\beta^n)=r+1\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if every positive power of \(\beta\) has its original degree \(d\), the eventual minimum is exactly \(d+1\).

More precisely, for each \(2\le k\le r\) there is a list of at most
\[
     C(k)=\sum_{j=2}^{k!}\binom{k!-1}{j-1}
                        \exp\!\big(k(6(j-1))^{3(j-1)}\big)                    \tag{2}
\]
positive integers \(m_i\) such that every \(n\ge1\) with \(\nu(\beta^n)\le k\) divides some \(m_i\).
Every rational relation on \(\beta\) with precisely \(k\) nonzero terms has two successive support exponents differing by at most \(\max_i m_i\), when the list is nonempty.
If the list is empty there is no such relation.
Thus any \(k\) distinct powers of \(\beta\) with all consecutive exponent gaps sufficiently large are rationally independent.
The sizes of the \(m_i\) may depend on \(\beta\).

<a id="order"></a>
## Ordering the determinant terms

Choose \(k\) conjugates \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\) from different classes.
The subgroup \(\Gamma\) of the multiplicative group of a normal closure generated by them is finitely generated.
In its free abelian quotient by torsion their images are distinct.
There is a homomorphism \(\ell:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z\) taking distinct values at these images: in lattice coordinates the integral coefficient vector of the homomorphism need only avoid finitely many proper rational hyperplanes.
For instance, vectors \((1,t,\ldots,t^{b-1})\) in rank \(b\) do this outside the roots of finitely many nonzero polynomials.
Order the chosen rows by \(s_1<\cdots<s_k\) where \(s_i=\ell(\alpha_i)\).

Suppose a rational polynomial with at most \(k\) nonzero terms vanishes at \(\beta^n\).
As in the [sparse determinant for independent conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v184.md#determinant), pad the support by zero coefficients if necessary, and sort its \(k\) distinct nonnegative exponents \(a_1<\cdots<a_k\).
The same nonzero rational coefficient vector annihilates all \(k\) rows \((\alpha_i^{n a_j})_j\).
Consequently the determinant vanishes and gives a signed relation among its terms
\[
          M_\sigma=\prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_i^{n a_{\sigma(i)}},
                        \qquad \sigma\in\mathfrak S_k .
\]
The term indexed by the identity has strictly larger \(\ell\)-value than every other term.
Indeed the rearrangement inequality for the two strictly increasing lists \((s_i)\) and \((a_j)\) has equality only at the identity.

Choose a smallest vanishing subset \(I\) containing this particular term.
A proper vanishing subset inside \(I\) either contains the distinguished term or can be removed to leave it in a smaller vanishing subset.
Both possibilities contradict the choice.
Hence the relation on \(I\) is nondegenerate.
Every ratio \(M_\sigma/M_{\rm id}\) for \(\sigma\in I\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\}\) has strictly negative \(\ell\)-value, even in the presence of multiplicative dependencies between the rows.

<a id="constraints"></a>
## A positive integral constraint on the exponent gaps

For each candidate subset \(I\) containing the identity, division by its term gives an equation with fixed signs, right side one and \(j-1=|I|-1\) variables.
Its tuples belong to the image of
\[
   (e_1,\ldots,e_k)\longmapsto
    \left(\prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_i^{\,e_{\sigma(i)}-e_i}\right)_{
                             \sigma\in I\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\}}.
\]
The diagonal lattice maps to one, so the rank of this group of tuples is at most \(k-1\).
The [finite-rank unit-equation bound](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) gives at most \(\exp(k(6(j-1))^{3(j-1)})\) nondegenerate tuples for this fixed choice.
For \(j=2\) the single coordinate is fixed by the equation.
For every such tuple take one fixed coordinate \(u_\sigma\) indexed by an element of \(I\) different from the identity, and retain \(m_i=-\ell(u_\sigma)\) if it is positive.
There are \(\binom{k!-1}{j-1}\) choices of \(I\) for a given \(j\), proving the bound on the number of retained integers.

For the actual determinant \(e_i=n a_i\), the retained integer satisfies the exact identity
\[
       m_i=n\sum_{l=1}^{k-1} b_{\sigma,l}(a_{l+1}-a_l),\qquad
       b_{\sigma,l}=\sum_{i>l}s_i-\sum_{i:\,\sigma(i)>l}s_i.                 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed expand each \(a_j\) as \(a_1+\sum_{l<j}(a_{l+1}-a_l)\).
The first sum defining \(b_{\sigma,l}\) uses the largest \(k-l\) integers from the ordered list, and the second uses some \(k-l\) of them.
Thus these coefficients are nonnegative integers.
At least one is positive for a nonidentity permutation, by strict rearrangement.
This proves both divisibility by \(n\) and the bound on a consecutive gap when the support has exactly \(k\) terms.
For a rational dependence on a list of \(k\) widely spaced exponents with some zero coefficients, retain the given list itself as the padding.
Equation (3) excludes that dependence as well.
Translation of all exponents gives the same assertion for integer exponents.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The exact eventual number and the case of degree drops

Choose \(T\ge1\) divisible by the orders of all the root-of-unity quotients of pairs of conjugates, using \(T=1\) if there are no nontrivial ones.
For every \(n\ge1\) the distinct conjugates of \(\beta^{nT}\) are exactly the \(r\) values obtained from the classes.
Indeed equivalent conjugates agree after raising to \(T\), and equality of any positive powers of two representatives would make their quotient torsion.
The minimal polynomial of \(\beta^{nT}\) has degree \(r\), so substituting \(X^T\) produces a relation on \(\beta^n\) with at most \(r+1\) terms.
When \(r\ge2\), the finite divisor containment at \(k=r\) allows only finitely many powers with fewer than \(r+1\) terms.
If \(r=1\), a nonzero monomial never vanishes at \(\beta^n\), proving (1) in this case too.
Power stability holds exactly when \(r=d\), by the same conjugate count.

<a id="width"></a>
## A restriction that also keeps the logarithmic measure small

The finite list in (2) gives no bound on its largest entry in terms of \(k\) and the original logarithmic Mahler measure.
One can still exclude every relation of bounded term count and prescribed span relative to the height while preserving the small-measure requirement.
Write \(h(z)\) for absolute logarithmic height, and \(\lambda(z)\) for the minimum coefficient length of an integer relation on \(z\).
If some rational polynomial of support size at most \(k\ge2\) and support span \(D\) vanishes at \(z\ne0\), then
\[
                        \lambda(z)\le k!\exp((k-1)D h(z)).                    \tag{4}
\]
Choose a minimally rationally dependent subset of the given powers, with \(s\le k\) elements.
Translate their least exponent to zero and use the primitive integer relation vector of this subset.
Its rational relation space is one-dimensional, since otherwise some coefficient could be eliminated to give a smaller dependent set.
The [height bound for that primitive vector](Yin_C_G0345_v182.md#coefficients) gives largest absolute coefficient at most \((s-1)!\exp((s-1)D h(z))\).
Summing its \(s\) absolute coefficients proves (4).

Suppose the unrestricted Lehmer gap fails.
For any positive targets \(x_j\), tolerances \(\delta_j>0\), term limits \(k_j\ge2\) integral, and finite positive real \(A_j\), there are power-stable units \(\gamma_j\) of logarithmic measures in \([x_j,x_j+\delta_j]\) with the following property.
Every rational relation on any \(\gamma_j^v\), \(v\ge1\) integral, with at most \(k_j\) nonzero terms has support span \(D\) satisfying \(D h(\gamma_j^v)>A_j\).
Apply the [prescribed minimum-length theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v185.md#prescribed) with length threshold at least \(k_j!\exp((k_j-1)A_j)\), and use (4) at each power.
The same argument preserves the Salem class if the Salem gap fails.
Taking the target intervals to zero controls the measure while the term limits and normalized spans can grow arbitrarily.
Relations of still larger span remain possible.
