# Exterior radii, inversion signs and lower-degree units

<a id="statement"></a>
## Consequences for reciprocal units of bounded Mahler measure

Let \(\gamma\) be a reciprocal algebraic unit of positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\le M\), with no root-of-unity quotient between distinct conjugates.
These hypotheses hold for the class product of a nontorsion reciprocal unit.
Write \(d=2v\) for its degree.
The \(v\) labels are its actual inverse pairs \(\{x,x^{-1}\}\), since the inverse is a conjugate and compression excludes another root differing from it by torsion.
Throughout take \(d\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M\) and use the [minimal block partition](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) into \(u\) blocks of size \(b=v/u\ge9\).
Call an inverse pair neutral when its roots have modulus one, and exterior otherwise, using its unique exterior representative in the latter case.
Fix the given complex embedding and write \(c\) for complex conjugation.

1. Among the exterior roots, take one radius from each \(c\)-orbit, counting a real root once and a nonreal conjugate pair once.
   Every set of at most \(\lfloor(b-1)/2\rfloor\) such radii is multiplicatively independent.
   If there are \(s\) exterior \(c\)-orbits, their log radii have rational rank at least \(s-f\), where \(f\) is the number of \(c\)-orbits of blocks consisting entirely of exterior labels.
   In particular all the exterior radii so indexed are independent if every block has a neutral label.
2. Suppose some block contains three inverse pairs fixed as labels by \(c\), with both a real pair and a neutral pair among the three.
   Then every block admits all even sign changes on its own labels, fixing every other root, inside the commutator group of the splitting group.
   All \(v\) inverse-pair representatives are multiplicatively independent.
   If \(L\) is the splitting field of \(\gamma\), \(K\) the splitting field of \(\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\), and \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), then
   \[
        [L:K]\ \ge\ [FL:FK]\ \ge\ 2^{v-u},\qquad
        [FL:F]\ \ge\ 2^{v-u}(b!/2)^u .                                    \tag{1}
   \]
   The hypothesis holds in particular for totally real trace when one block contains both a real and a neutral inverse pair.
   For a Salem number the second lower bound improves to \(2^{v-1}(b!/2)^u\), and \([FL:FK]\ge2^{v-1}\).
3. If the inverse-pair representatives have rank less than \(v\), the relations canonically determine one choice of roots per block up to simultaneous inversion.
   Every resulting block product is an algebraic unit of degree at most \(2u<d\) and logarithmic measure at most \(m\).
   Either all of these products are torsion and the rank is exactly \(v-u\), with one internal relation per block generating all relations over \(\mathbb Q\), or all are nontorsion and give a lower-degree witness.
   Consequently a reciprocal unit of least degree among **all** algebraic numbers of logarithmic measure in \((0,m]\), under the stated degree and compression hypotheses, can have only ranks \(v\) and \(v-u\).

<a id="radii"></a>
## From real moduli to algebraic relations

Choose the exterior representative at every exterior label and either root at every neutral label.
Write \(E_j\) for the one or two labels supporting exterior complex-conjugation orbit \(j\) and \(r_j>1\) for its radius.
The supports \(E_j\) are disjoint.
For a real root \(x\), \(r_j^2=x^2\), including a negative root.
For a nonreal orbit one has \(r_j^2=x\bar x\), involving two distinct inverse pairs.
Thus any rational relation on the logarithms, after clearing denominators and doubling, gives an exact multiplicative relation on the chosen roots.
On \(E_j\) its coefficient is twice the log coefficient for a singleton and is the log coefficient on each of the two labels for a doubleton.

The [whole-block support conclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank) forces every touched block to be covered, with equal absolute coefficients on that block in the root relation.
Fewer than \(b\) selected labels therefore give independent radii, proving the first assertion.
For the global bound select one label in one block from each \(c\)-orbit of wholly exterior blocks.
The linear map from log relations to their corresponding root coefficients on these \(f\) selected labels is injective.
Vanishing at a selected label forces vanishing on its block, and \(c\)-invariance of the exterior coefficients gives the same on its conjugate block.
Every other block contains a neutral label where the coefficient is zero, so has zero coefficients throughout.
Disjointness of the supports then recovers the zero log relation.
This proves the codimension bound, also when complex conjugation exchanges blocks.
The [earlier commensurable-moduli result](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement) treats rank one quantitatively.
Here the independence range grows with the block size and the global bound applies to every rank.

<a id="inversions"></a>
## A commutator on three fixed inverse pairs

Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
It acts by signed permutations of the inverse labels, and \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) is the elementary abelian subgroup of actual sign changes, since fixing \(x+x^{-1}\) permutes its two roots.
On a fixed inverse label \(c\) fixes each root when the pair is real and inverts each root when it is neutral.
Take a three-cycle on the indicated three fixed labels, fixing all other labels.
It comes from the alternating factor on their block, so choose a lift \(h\in G\).
The square \(h^2\) fixes every root outside the three labels, because any sign change on a fixed pair has square one.
On the three labels its underlying permutation is still a three-cycle.
Conjugating the local sign vector of \(c\) by \(h^2\) just cycles its coordinates, regardless of the signs of the lift.
The complement of these three labels is \(c\)-stable, so the commutator \([h^2,c]\) is trivial there.
On the three fixed labels it flips precisely two since the sign vector is nonconstant.
This local argument allows other inverse labels to be permuted by \(c\).

Conjugation in \(G\) moves this double flip to any two positions in the same block by two-transitivity of \(A_b\), and then to every block by transitivity.
These elements generate a product of \(u\) independent even-sign groups of order \(2^{u(b-1)}=2^{v-u}\), all in \(G'\cap\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\).
If a multiplicative relation had a nonzero coefficient at any position, subtracting its transform under a double flip using that position would produce a nonzero relation supported on at most two labels, impossible by whole-block support.
Hence the rank is \(v\).
The group of \(FL/F\) identifies with \(G'\), and its map onto \(\operatorname{Gal}(FK/F)\) is restriction.
The image contains the product of the alternating factors because they form a perfect subgroup of the trace permutation group.
Its kernel contains the even-sign product just constructed, giving (1).
The [single-block signed group calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#flips) uses a graph hypothesis for one exterior nonreal pair.
Here the three fixed labels suffice, independently of the other complex orbits.

For a Salem number the exterior conjugate is unique, so its torsion-quotient class is a singleton, as are all the conjugate classes.
Complex conjugation inverts all inverse pairs except the real one.
By transitivity its conjugates have every possible single exception.
The product of the two conjugates with exceptions at \(i,j\) flips just those two pairs and belongs to \(G'\), since the two involutions have equal image in the abelianization.
Thus the even-sign subgroup on all \(v\) labels lies in the relative kernel, even across different blocks.
The absolute Salem sign-kernel theorem is classical, and independence of its inverse pairs is already known in the project.
The bound over \(F\) here combines the sign commutators with the alternating factors forced in the trace group at bounded measure. [Salem kernel and original attribution](Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) · [Salem multiplicative independence](Yin_C_G0345_v116.md#records)

<a id="descent"></a>
## Canonical products from the restrictions of global relations

Choose one root \(x_i\) in each inverse pair, and take the rational space \(\mathcal R\) of relations modulo roots of unity on the \(v\) representatives.
Suppose \(\mathcal R\ne0\).
Its projection on the coordinates of each block \(B\) is nonzero by transitivity and signed permutation invariance.
Every nonzero projected vector has full support and all absolute entries equal.
Thus the projection is a line, since two independent projected vectors would have a nonzero linear combination with a prescribed coordinate zero.
Its primitive integral generators are \(\pm(\epsilon_i)_{i\in B}\) with \(\epsilon_i=\pm1\).
These lines are equivariant under the signed action of \(G\), giving a choice of actual roots \(x_i^{\epsilon_i}\) in each block, up to simultaneous inversion, independent of the initial representatives.

Let \(\beta_B=\prod_{i\in B}x_i^{\epsilon_i}\).
All conjugates are among the at most \(2u\) possible products and their inverses, and these are algebraic units.
By the product inequality for the absolute logarithmic height,
\[
        [\mathbb Q(\beta_B):\mathbb Q]\,h(\beta_B)
                 \le 2u\sum_{i\in B}h(x_i)
                 =2ub\,m/d=m .
\]
Transitivity on the blocks implies that either all products are torsion or all are nontorsion.
In the torsion case the \(u\) internal sign vectors are global relations with disjoint supports, and the one-dimensional projection on each block makes them a basis of \(\mathcal R\).
In the nontorsion case the strict degree drop follows from \(d=2ub\) with \(b>1\).
The stated least-degree condition excludes this case and proves the last assertion.
