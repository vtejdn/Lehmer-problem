# The cost of all but the two largest coefficients in a terminal relation

<a id="statement"></a>
## A weighted obstruction in the actual witness window

Let \(\alpha\) range over one of the shrinking-measure sequences in the [full coefficient count](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement), including its near-unit-circle alternative with fixed \(K\).
Use \(m=\log M(\alpha)\) and \(L=\log(1/m)\).
For a full signed ordered integer coefficient tuple \(\mathbf b\) of length \(r\), including its marked coefficient, define
\[
       H_2(\mathbf b)=\prod_{\text{all but the two largest }|b_i|}(1+|b_i|).
\]
Ties can be resolved arbitrarily.
For every fixed \(\lambda\ge0\) there are a constant \(c_\lambda>0\), a weight \(r\to\infty\), and one full tuple in the stated finite coefficient and exponent windows with at least
\[
            H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda(c_\lambda/m)^{r-2}
\]
distinct sorted marked terminal lists.
The constant may also depend on \(K\) in the near-unit-circle case.
When every positive power preserves degree or the input has a real conjugate, the number \(V\) of primitive marked shapes for this tuple satisfies
\[
          \log V-\lambda\log H_2(\mathbf b)
                      \ \ge\ (1-o(1))(r-2)L .
\]
Consequently, a bound \(A^r H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda\) for those primitive counts in the actual windows, with absolute \(A,\lambda\), would exclude every shrinking sequence with a unit-circle conjugate, by passing to power-stable representatives.

<a id="budget"></a>
## Charging small incident coefficients to the leaves

Consider any rooted tree of rational splits used in the [full coefficient reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#reconstruction).
At a vertex \(v\), choose a child with the largest number of descendant original leaves.
Call the other children light and let \(s_e\) be the number of descendant leaves at a light edge \(e\).
At a unit-circle conjugate its cut is a signed sum of \(s_e\) leaves of modulus one, so \(|b_e|\le s_e\).
In the near-unit-circle case the [original-coordinate bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#near) give \(|b_e|\le s_e\exp((N+U)\delta)\le C_K s_e\).
At each vertex the product defining \(H_2\) is at most the product over its light children, since the latter omits precisely its parent and the chosen largest child.
The number of incident edges omitted here refers to descendant leaf counts regardless of the ordering of the coefficients.

Along the path from a single leaf to the root, successive light edges have descendant counts growing at least by a factor of two.
Indeed the parent of a light child contains a different child with at least as many leaves, and the next light subtree contains that parent.
Charge \(\log(1+C_Ks_e)/s_e\) to each of the \(s_e\) leaves below every light edge.
There is at most one charge along each leaf path in each range \(2^a\le s_e<2^{a+1}\).
The sum along that path is at most
\[
           \sum_{a\ge0}2^{-a}\log(1+C_K 2^{a+1})<\infty .
\]
Thus for every such tree with \(j\) original leaves,
\[
                        \prod_v H_2(\mathbf b_v)\le D_K^j
\]
for a constant independent of the input, the tree and every exponent.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Selecting a single full coefficient tuple

Use the same weight \(j\) for the original ternary supports as in the full coefficient count.
For each allowed degree \(k\) and complete coefficient tuple \(\mathbf b\), write \(W(k,\mathbf b)\) for its finite-window terminal count.
Let \(M\) be the maximum of \((W(k,\mathbf b)/H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda)^{1/(k-2)}\) over \(3\le k\le j\) and the allowed tuples.
For a fixed plane tree and its realizable coefficient assignment, the product of the possible list counts at its internal vertices is at most \(M^{j-2}D_K^{\lambda j}\).
The number of plane trees and realizable coefficient labelings remains at most \(C_K^j\) by the [integer triangle count](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#lengths).
Comparing with \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\) yields the claimed lower bound at a maximizing tuple, with a constant depending only on the indicated parameters.
The uniform fixed-weight bound from [the rational terminal count](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement) again gives \(r\to\infty\).
The [dilation cost](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) is independent of the sizes of the coefficients and absorbs \(o((r-2)L)\) in the logarithm.
For an exactly neutral input the [power-field norm](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) supplies the required degree-preserving representative without changing the measure.

<a id="permutations"></a>
## Coefficient sizes in the known factorial construction

Consider the [permutation construction](Yin_C_G0345_v597.md#clusters) at any of its inputs, with \(p=d+2\), \(r=pn+1\), and positive block starts \(L_i\).
Its \(n!\) exhibited shapes obey, for every fixed \(\lambda>0\),
\[
                         n!\ \le\ A_\lambda^r H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda
\]
with an absolute base depending only on \(\lambda\).
This inequality concerns the exhibited permutations and leaves open the number of other terminal shapes for the same tuple.

To see it, write \(H=\max_\beta\log|\beta|>0\) over conjugates and use reciprocity to evaluate at a conjugate of modulus \(e^{-H}\).
After clearing common denominators write \(b\ge1\) for the positive integer output.
The \(i\)-th block before the permuted shift sums to \(b/S\), where \(S=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{qj}\).
At this contracting embedding \(|S|\le n\); the \(p\) powers in block \(i\) all have modulus at most \(e^{-HL_i}\).
Hence at least one of its coefficients has absolute value at least \(be^{HL_i}/(pn)\).
Removing two entries leaves such a coefficient for at least \(n-2\) different blocks.
Since successive starts are separated by at least \(p\), \(L_i\ge(i-1)p\).
Dimitrov's [house inequality](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives \(pH\ge(\log2)/4=:a\).
For \(n\ge3\) this proves
\[
    \log H_2(\mathbf b)\ge a(n-2)(n-3)/2-(n-2)\log(pn).
\]
If \(\log n\le p\), then \(\log(n!)\le pn\le r\).
Otherwise \(p<\log n\) and \(\log(pn)\le2\log n\) for sufficiently large \(n\), so the last lower estimate is at least \(a n^2/4\) eventually, uniformly in \(p\).
This absorbs \(\log(n!)\le n\log n\) after multiplication by any fixed \(\lambda>0\); the remaining finite \(n\) are absorbed in \(A_\lambda^r\).
