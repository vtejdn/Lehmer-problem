# Constituent fields of strict degree records and a logarithmic rank bound

<a id="statement"></a>
## A norm descent and the geometry of the projected conjugates

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), with splitting field \(L\), conjugate set \(\Omega\), and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=m(w)\).
Write \(V_w\) for the rational span of all its conjugates modulo roots of unity.
For any nonzero rational equivariant quotient of \(V_w\) of dimension \(r\), let \(t\) count the distinct images of \([a]\), \(a\in\Omega\), in this quotient.
One can find a nontorsion unit \(z\), a product of original conjugates, of degree exactly \(t\) and total measure at most \(m\).
For \(t>2\) there is an absolute constant \(C>0\) such that
\[
       t\ \le\ C\,m^2 r^2\,2^{r/2}(\log(3t))^{212}.                       \tag{1}
\]
The height input used here gives existence of the absolute constant.

Suppose \(w\) is a strict degree record: it minimizes all positive total measures of integer polynomials up to degree \(d\), and no smaller degree attains that value.
Then every nonzero rational equivariant quotient separates all \(d\) conjugates.
Each nonzero rational submodule of \(V_w\) affords a faithful representation of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
If \(x\) is nontorsion in \(D(w)\), its splitting field contains \(L\).
More precisely, the kernel of the absolute Galois action on the rational multiplicative span of the conjugates of \(x\) has fixed field exactly \(L\).
Thus two strict degree records with different splitting fields have divisible conjugate groups intersecting only in roots of unity.

For a sequence of strict degree records with \(d\to\infty\) and \(m\) bounded above, (1) gives
\[
     r\ \ge\ \frac{2}{\log2}\big(\log d-O(\log\log(3d))\big)
\]
for every nonzero rational constituent, uniformly on the sequence.
If also \(m\to0\), the conjugate arguments of *each* nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) have multiplicative rank modulo torsion at least
\[
                    \left(\frac1{2\log2}-o(1)\right)\log d .              \tag{2}
\]
In particular (2) would be necessary along records approaching one in multiplicative measure, even when the degree of the tested \(x\) differs from \(d\).

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Every fiber product has the degree of the projected orbit

Write \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) and let \(\pi:V_w\to W\) be the quotient map.
The images of the root classes form a single orbit spanning \(W\), and none is zero.
Every projected point \(u\) occurs with multiplicity \(e=d/t\).
Put \(z_u=\prod_{\pi([a])=u}a\).
It is an algebraic unit and \(\pi([z_u])=e u\), so the \(z_u\) are nontorsion, pairwise distinct even modulo torsion, and permuted equivariantly.
Thus \(\deg z_u=t\) and \(t h(z_u)\le te h(w)=m\).
If \(w\) is a strict record, \(t<d\) contradicts its defining degree and measure properties, hence \(t=d\).

If an element of \(G\) is trivial on a nonzero quotient for a strict record, then it fixes all projected conjugates, hence all the actual roots and therefore \(L\).
Every rational submodule is a quotient up to equivariant isomorphism by complete reducibility.
For a nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), its span \(V_x\) is a nonzero submodule, since a positive power of \(x\) is a monomial in conjugates of \(w\) up to torsion.
The absolute Galois action on \(V_x\) factors through \(G\), with trivial kernel in \(G\) by faithfulness.
The splitting field of \(x\) therefore contains \(L\), and the fixed field of the kernel acting on \(V_x\) is exactly \(L\).
This last field depends only on \(x\), proving the intersection result for two records.

<a id="packing"></a>
## A small two-point field from a nearest projected neighbor

Equip \(W\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R\) with an invariant Euclidean inner product; all \(t\) orbit points have the same norm.
For \(t>2\), their dimension \(r\) is at least two.
Choose a pair \(u,v\) at minimum positive distance \(\lambda\).
They are not proportional: distinct points of equal norm on one line are antipodal, and any third point is strictly closer to \(u\) than its antipode.
It follows that \(z_u,z_v\) are multiplicatively independent; projection of an integer relation modulo torsion would give a dependence between \(u\) and \(v\).

Every image of \(v\) under the stabilizer of \(u\) is one of the nearest neighbors of \(u\).
The normalized displacement vectors from \(u\) to these neighbors have norm one and pairwise inner products at most \(1/2\), by the minimum distance.
A set of unit vectors with this separation in \(\mathbb R^r\) has size at most
\[
                              2\pi(r-1)^2\,2^{(r-1)/2}.                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed take a cap defined by \(a\cdot y\ge1/\sqrt2\) for a unit axis \(a\).
The projections of points in this cap onto \(a^\perp\) have pairwise inner product at most zero.
If one projection vanishes, the cap has only one point.
Otherwise normalize the projections to unit vectors.
For their Gram matrix \(B\) of size \(s\), off-diagonal entries lie in \([-1,0]\), and \(\sum_{i,j} B_{ij}\ge0\) gives \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)\le2s\).
Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(B)\ge(\operatorname{tr} B)^2/\operatorname{tr}(B^2)\ge s/2\), whereas its rank is at most \(r-1\).
At most \(2(r-1)\) points therefore occupy any such cap.
Its normalized spherical area is
\[
  \frac{\int_0^{\pi/4}\sin^{r-2}\theta\,d\theta}
       {\int_0^\pi\sin^{r-2}\theta\,d\theta}
       \ \ge\ \frac{2^{-(r-1)/2}}{\pi(r-1)},
\]
using \(x=\sin\theta\) in the numerator; the argument includes \(r=2\).
Averaging the cap occupancy over all axes proves (3).

The degree \(D=[\mathbb Q(z_u,z_v):\mathbb Q]\) is the orbit size of the ordered pair and is at most \(t\) times (3); one also has \(D\le t^2\).
The [rank-two absolute height product estimate](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) now gives
\[
       (m/t)^2 \ \ge\ h(z_u)h(z_v)
                \ \ge\ \frac{c}{D(\log(3D))^{212}}
\]
for an absolute positive \(c\).
These two degree bounds imply (1).

<a id="records"></a>
## Consequences for a sequence of minimizing units

For a strict record \(t=d\) in each constituent.
Taking logs in (1) with \(m\) bounded above proves the rank assertion: if \(r\le 4\log(3d)\), then \(2\log r=O(\log\log(3d))\), and a larger \(r\) already suffices.
The constant in the error can depend on the common bound on \(m\).
The [linear fixed-dimension bound](Yin_C_G0901_v76.md#statement) forces the negative eigenspace dimension of complex conjugation in every constituent to be at least \((1/4-o(1))r\) when \(m\to0\), with the error uniform among constituents.
Every \(V_x\ne0\) contains at least one such constituent, and its own negative eigenspace gives its argument rank by the [rank formula](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection).
This proves (2).
If the unrestricted infimum is zero, such strict records exist along unbounded degrees: bounded-degree coefficient finiteness gives positive minima, which must strictly drop infinitely often; an irreducible factor attains each new minimum, and below \(\log2\) it is a nontorsion unit minimum after monomial and sign normalization.
