# Successive derivatives of an original Salem trace polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## How many derivatives retain a trace above two

Let \(1<\sigma\le2\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2m\), with monic trace minimal polynomial \(S\).
Write \(a=\log\sigma\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), and denote the other roots of \(S\) by \(x_1<\cdots<x_{m-1}\) in \((-2,2)\).
Define
\[
 v_j=(T-x_j)^{-1},\qquad W=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}v_j,\qquad E=uW.
\]
For \(0\le k<m\), let \(y_k\) be the largest root of the divided derivative \(H_k=S^{(k)}/k!\), including \(y_0=T\), and let \(K\) count the orders \(1\le k<m\) for which \(y_k>2\).
They are precisely \(1,\ldots,K\), and all other roots of those derivatives lie in \((-2,2)\).
One has
\[
 |K-E|\le1,\qquad
 E=(m-1)\tanh(a/2)+O\big(\sqrt{m\log(2m)}\big).                     \tag{1}
\]
Every one of these \(K\) roots satisfies
\[
       \frac{k}{W+\max_j v_j}\ \le\ T-y_k
                       \le\ \frac{k}{W+\min_j v_j}.                 \tag{2}
\]
The implied constant is absolute.
In particular, along any sequence of Salem numbers tending to one,
\[
   K\sim\tfrac12m\log\sigma,\qquad
   T-y_k=(1+o(1))\,\frac{2k\sinh a}{m-1}
                       \quad\text{uniformly for }1\le k\le K.        \tag{3}
\]
These derivatives and traces belong to the original input exponent.

Suppose also that \(\sigma\) is smaller than every Salem number of lower degree.
For each \(1\le k\le K\), the primitive irreducible integer polynomial of \(y_k\) has positive leading coefficient dividing \(\binom{m}{k}/\operatorname{cont}(H_k)\) and at least two, after choosing its sign.
In particular the monic polynomial \(H_k/\binom{m}{k}\) fails to be integral at every one of these consecutive orders.
This supplies distinct lower-degree real algebraic traces in \((2,T)\) and identifies the failure of integrality at a degree minimum.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## A sign test at any level between two and the exterior trace

Repeated strict Rolle interlacing gives \(T=y_0>y_1>\cdots>y_{m-1}\), with all roots simple.
Each derivative has at most one root above \(x_{m-1}\), and all its roots are above \(-2\).
Thus for \(2\le b<T\), the sign of \(H_k(b)\) is negative, zero or positive according as \(y_k>b,\ y_k=b\) or \(y_k<b\) for \(k<m\), with \(H_m=1\).

Put \(s=T-b\), \(n=m-1\), \(p_j=s v_j\in(0,1)\), and define the finite coefficient list
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-p_j+p_j z)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}P_l z^l ,
 \qquad \mu=\sum_jp_j=sW .
\]
Use zero outside the support for this list and for every list obtained by deleting a factor.
Expansion of \(S(b+sz)\) gives the exact identity
\[
             s^k H_k(b)=s\prod_j(T-x_j)\,(P_{k-1}-P_k).
\]
The smallest index at which \(P_l\) reaches its maximum is therefore \(K_b=\#\{1\le k<m:y_k>b\}\).
If there is an equality \(y_k=b\), the coefficients at \(k-1,k\) tie for this maximum and \(K_b=k-1\).
In particular \(K=K_2\).
Deleting any factor leaves a unimodal list \(Q_l\), by the same Rolle argument with that neutral root removed.
For the empty product the only nonzero coefficient is \(Q_0=1\).

The coefficients are the probabilities of the number of successes in independent Bernoulli trials.
The one-unit estimate for a mode is classical, and we give the coefficient identities to obtain the location of the derivative roots at a tie.
See [Darroch, 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703287) and [Samuels, 1965](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177699998).

<a id="locations"></a>
## Adjacent coefficients and the location of the largest root

Let \(Q^{(j)}_l\) denote the list omitting \(j\).
It satisfies \(P_l=(1-p_j)Q^{(j)}_l+p_jQ^{(j)}_{l-1}\).
Differentiating the generating product gives \(lP_l=\sum_j p_jQ^{(j)}_{l-1}\), hence
\[
    (\mu-l)P_l=\sum_j p_j(1-p_j)
                             \big(Q^{(j)}_l-Q^{(j)}_{l-1}\big).      \tag{4}
\]
If \(P_l\le P_{l-1}\) at \(1\le l\le n\), then \(Q^{(j)}_l\le Q^{(j)}_{l-1}\) for each \(j\).
Indeed a strict increase at \(l\) in a unimodal deleted list would make its preceding difference nonnegative, giving \(P_l>P_{l-1}\).
Likewise \(P_l\ge P_{l-1}\) implies \(Q^{(j)}_{l-1}\ge Q^{(j)}_{l-2}\).
Applying (4) at \(l=K_b+1\) and at \(l=K_b-1\) using the differences at \(K_b+1\) and \(K_b\), respectively, yields \(K_b-1\le\mu\le K_b+1\).
For \(K_b=0\) the lower bound follows from \(\mu\ge0\), and for \(K_b=n\) the upper bound follows from \(\mu\le n\).
At \(b=2\) this gives the first bound in (1).

Now take \(b=y_k>2\) for \(k\ge1\).
Here \(P=P_k=P_{k-1}>0\) and \(D_j=Q^{(j)}_{k-1}-Q^{(j)}_{k-2}\ge0\).
The tie identity gives \((1-p_j)(Q^{(j)}_k-Q^{(j)}_{k-1})=-p_j D_j\).
Substitution into (4) at \(k\) and direct use at \(k-1\) give
\[
        (k-\mu)P=\sum_j p_j^2D_j,\qquad
        (\mu-k+1)P=\sum_j p_j(1-p_j)D_j.
\]
Their sum shows \(P=\sum_j p_j D_j\), so \(k-\mu\) is a weighted average of the \(p_j\).
Since \(\mu=s W\) and \(p_j=s v_j\), this proves (2).
The bounds hold at \(y_k=2\) as well.

<a id="sampling"></a>
## Sampling the Poisson kernel at the neutral conjugates

For \(x=2\cos\theta\), summing the two geometric series gives
\[
   f_a(\theta)=\frac{u}{T-2\cos\theta}
       =\tanh(a/2)\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}e^{-a|\ell|}e^{i\ell\theta}.
\]
Its circle mean is \(\tanh(a/2)\).
Also \(\sum_{\ell\ne0}|\ell|\,|\widehat f_a(\ell)|^2\) is bounded absolutely and uniformly for \(0<a\le\log2\), since \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell e^{-2a\ell}=e^{-2a}/(1-e^{-2a})^2\).
The absolute Fourier tail past \(N\) is \(O(e^{-aN})\) uniformly in the same range.

Let \(\nu\) be the uniform probability on the \(d-2\) neutral conjugates of \(\sigma\).
The [truncated Fourier energy](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#energy) of \(\nu\) through \(N=d^4\) is \(O(\log(2d)/d)\) with weight \(1/|\ell|\).
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the difference between the \(\nu\)-mean and the circle mean of \(f_a\) by \(O(\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}+e^{-ad^4})\).
The logarithmic Mahler measure of \(\sigma\) equals \(a\), so [Dobrowolski's large-degree estimate](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(a\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for large \(d\).
It absorbs the tail and gives \(a/\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\to\infty\).
For bounded \(d\) the estimate in (1) follows by increasing the absolute constant since \(0<f_a\le1\).
The \(\nu\)-mean of \(f_a\) is \(E/(m-1)\), proving (1).
Thus \(E\sim(m-1)\tanh(a/2)\) as \(d\to\infty\), uniformly over \(1<\sigma\le2\).

Along a sequence approaching one, the degrees tend to infinity by finiteness of bounded-degree monic integer polynomials of bounded Mahler measure.
One has \(K\sim E\sim ma/2\).
Since \(\max_j v_j\le1/u\), both denominators in (2) lie between \(W\) and \(W(1+1/E)\).
Finally \(u/\tanh(a/2)=2\sinh a\), which gives (3).

<a id="records"></a>
## The integer obstruction at every retained order

The divided derivative \(H_k=\sum_{j=k}^m\binom jk s_j X^{j-k}\) is integral when \(S=\sum_{j=0}^m s_jX^j\), \(s_m=1\).
For \(1\le k\le K\), its single root \(y_k>2\) is below \(T\), and its other roots belong to \((-2,2)\).
If \(y_k\) were an algebraic integer, its monic minimal polynomial would divide \(H_k\) over \(\mathbb Q\) and its other roots would all belong to that open interval.
The [lower-degree trace descent](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#records) applies directly to such a factor.
Indeed its monic reciprocal lift has a single exterior root \(\tau\in(1,\sigma)\), the reciprocal interior root and otherwise neutral roots.
The irreducible factor containing \(\tau\) has unit constant coefficient and must contain its inverse, since otherwise its constant coefficient would have absolute value \(\tau>1\).
A quadratic unit would have the integer trace \(y_k\in(2,T)\) with \(T\le5/2\), which is impossible.
Therefore \(\tau\) would be a Salem number of degree at most \(2(m-k)<2m\), contrary to the degree minimum.

The primitive irreducible factor at \(y_k\) must consequently be nonmonic, and its positive leading coefficient divides \(\binom mk/\operatorname{cont}(H_k)\) by primitive integral factorization.
In particular for each \(k\le K,\ k\ge1\) at least one index \(k\le j<m\) satisfies \(\binom mk\nmid \binom jk s_j\).
For prime trace degree \(m\) with \(K\ge1\), the case \(k=1\) excludes \(S\bmod m\in\mathbb F_m[X^m]\).
A degree minimum imposes these divisibility failures even though all the derivatives have integer coefficients and the locations of their distinguished roots satisfy (2).
The bounds leave open whether some other monic integral interlacer supplies descent.
