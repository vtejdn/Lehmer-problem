## 1. Finite-horizon theorem for arbitrary actual-order ideals

All logarithms below are natural.
Let alpha be a nontorsion algebraic unit, P its actual monic irreducible minimal polynomial, d its actual degree, O=Z[alpha], and m=log M(P)>0.
O has its complete integer power basis 1,alpha,...,alpha^(d-1).
A finite quotient means O/I for an ideal I of finite index Q; the zero ring is allowed with Q=1.
Rather than impose a convention on its unit group, a period r>=1 means precisely alpha^r-1 in I.

**Theorem 1.** For every such quotient and period, let b_0,...,b_(d-1) be independent fair binary digits, X=sum_j b_j alpha^j, and Y=X mod I.
For an independent copy (X',Y'),

    Q divides C_r=|Norm_(Q(alpha)/Q)(alpha^r-1)|,             (1)
    H(X|Y)>=d log2-log Q,                                  (2)
    Col(Y)>=1/Q,    Col(X)=2^(-d),                          (3)
    Pr(X=X' | Y=Y')=2^(-d)/Col(Y)<=Q/2^d.                 (4)

Conditional entropy is ordinary Shannon entropy for this finite law.
The bound in(2) can be negative when Q>2^d and is then merely a valid weak lower bound.
The actual conditional probability in(4) is of course at most1, so its effective upper bound is min(1,Q/2^d).

**Proof.** Multiplication by alpha^r-1 is an injective endomorphism of the free abelian group O: the multiplier is nonzero because alpha is nontorsion.
Its determinant in the full power basis is the full field norm, including every original embedding and any multiplicity induced by a powered degree drop.
Integer Smith form gives the finite index C_r.
The inclusion (alpha^r-1)O subset I gives a surjection O/(alpha^r-1)O onto O/I, proving(1) by finite cardinalities.
No maximal-order, good-prime or squarefree hypothesis is involved.

If two binary vectors give the same X, their difference is an integer polynomial of degree<d vanishing at alpha.
Minimality of P forces that polynomial to be zero, so X has exactly2^d equiprobable values.
Since Y is a deterministic function of X, H(X|Y)=H(X)-H(Y); H(X)=d log2 and H(Y)<=log Q prove(2).
Writing k_y for the number of binary vectors in residue y,

    Col(Y)=sum_y(k_y/2^d)^2>=1/Q

by Cauchy--Schwarz, since sum_y k_y=2^d and at most Q residues occur.
Col(X)=2^(-d).
The event X=X' is contained in Y=Y', so division of their probabilities proves(4).
The denominator is positive.
This proves the theorem.

The same statements hold at any starting power alpha^a, a in Z, and after any deterministic translation: multiplication by the actual order unit alpha^a and translation are bijections both in O and in O/I.
This concerns unconditioned binary endpoints; it is not a claim about first-return probabilities of a differently conditioned modular walk.

Its accepted Pri11 and Dim19 statement inputs retain their recorded original-source inspection limits.

**Corollary 2.** If rm/d<=h_0, then

    log Q/d<=Omega(rm/d),
    H(X|Y)>=d[log2-Omega(rm/d)],
    Pr(X=X' | Y=Y')<=exp(-d[log2-Omega(rm/d)]).             (5)

In particular, for r<=C d and Cm<=h_0, Omega(rm/d) can be replaced throughout by Omega(Cm).
Along any hypothetical nontorsion-unit sequence m->0, the conditional entropy divided by d tends to log2 uniformly over these quotients, and the probability that a congruence collision is a true collision is at most exp(-d[log2-o(1)]).
No existence of a near-zero-measure sequence is asserted.

The derivation is immediate from(1)--(4), the fixed cyclic bound and monotonicity of Omega.
Unlike the old fixed-quotient spectral-radius floor, this statement allows the quotient and input to vary; its loss already occurs at the first d digits and charges the actual common period r.

Call a quotient **faithful through d digits** when equality of the two length-d binary evaluations in the quotient is equivalent to equality in O, for every pair of binary vectors.
This is injectivity on the2^d actual endpoints, so faithfulness implies Q>=2^d.

Such a choice exists because Omega(h)->0 as h decreases to zero.
This specifies an absolute positive constant from the retained explicit function; no numerical root finding or new computation has been performed.

**Corollary 3.** Every quotient faithful through d digits has

    r>u_* d/m.                                            (6)

Indeed if r<=u_*d/m then rm/d<=u_*<=h_0, and(5) contradicts log Q/d>=log2.
If rm/d>h_0, inequality(6) already holds because u_*<=h_0.
Thus no unsupported use of Omega outside its stated domain occurs.
This is a necessary long-period condition, not a construction of a short-period faithful quotient and not an unconditional positive gap.

## 3. Simultaneous congruences retain their common period

Let I_1,...,I_k be finite-index ideals, with Q_j=[O:I_j] and alpha^r_j-1 in I_j.
Observing all residues is exactly the map onto the diagonal image O/I, where I=intersection_j I_j and Q=[O:I].
It is not automatically the full product of the individual quotient rings.

The diagonal map is injective into product_j O/I_j, so Q<=product_j Q_j.
For two ideals the exact sequence

    0 -> O/(I_1 intersect I_2) -> O/I_1 direct-sum O/I_2
      -> O/(I_1+I_2) -> 0

gives Q=Q_1 Q_2/[O:I_1+I_2]; the final map is subtraction.
Thus overlapping local information is charged once, and no coprimality assumption is concealed.

Let R=lcm(r_1,...,r_k).
Because alpha^R-1 is an integer-polynomial multiple of every alpha^r_j-1, it belongs to every I_j and hence to I.
Theorem1 and its corollaries therefore apply to the whole observation with Q dividing C_R.
In particular:

- If R<=C d and Cm<=h_0, the entire joint observation has the bound(5) with Omega(Cm), regardless of k.
- If only each r_j<=C d is known, then log Q/d<=k Omega(Cm).
  Faithfulness through d digits consequently requires k Omega(Cm)>=log2.
  This necessary condition concerns the number of congruences as well as their individual costs; it supplies no affordable common R.

These deductions never add local incomes at different exponents and call the sum income at one exponent.
The second conclusion uses only upper bounds on quotient cardinalities; the first uses the actual common exponent R.
When R is large and Rm/d>h_0, no cyclic small-height conclusion is claimed.

## 4. Exact local-factor version, including repeated factors

Fix a rational prime p and factor the full actual polynomial reduction

    Pbar=product_i phi_i^(e_i),    f_i=deg phi_i.

The constant coefficient of P is a unit, so every phi_i has nonzero constant coefficient.
For any chosen submultiset D=product_i phi_i^(a_i), 0<=a_i<=e_i, let I be the kernel of the surjection

    O=Z[t]/(P) -> F_p[t]/(D).

It exists because D divides Pbar.
Its cardinality is Q=p^(sum_i a_i f_i), preserving every selected repeated copy.
This is a quotient of the actual power-basis order even at index primes; a_i is not silently called a local ramification index.

Let o_i be the multiplicative order of t mod phi_i.
The exact period of t in F_p[t]/(phi_i^(a_i)), for a_i>0, is

    r_i=o_i p^(ceil(log_p a_i)),

where ceil(log_p1)=0.
To prove this, write r=p^v s with p not dividing s.
Then t^r-1=(t^s-1)^(p^v) in characteristic p.
An irreducible phi_i divides t^s-1 precisely when o_i divides s; in that case it divides exactly once because the derivative s t^(s-1) is coprime to t^s-1.
Thus phi_i^(a_i) divides t^r-1 precisely when o_i divides s and p^v>=a_i.
CRT over the distinct phi_i makes the full selected period r=lcm_i r_i.
This proof preserves the nilpotent contribution rather than replacing it by the field order alone.

At this common r, the actual full norm satisfies

    (sum_i a_i f_i)log p<=v_p(C_r)log p<=log C_r.           (7)

The first inequality is the quotient-divisibility argument, not a transfer from the full Frobenius/doubling resultant.
Deeper p-adic valuations, when present, can only increase the right side; none is claimed without proof.
Consequently, when rm/d<=h_0,

    [(sum_i a_i f_i)/d]log p<=Omega(rm/d).                 (8)

For selected primary quotients modulo higher powers of p, Theorem1 still applies using their ACTUAL finite cardinality and ACTUAL period; the displayed field-factor formula for r is not asserted there.

## 5. BV19b partition lifting to a specified input, with extension-field guard

Fix L>=0.
Let S_L be the0,1 polynomials of degree at most L, and I_L the distinct monic rational-irreducible factors of nonzero differences from S_L-S_L, exactly as in [BV19b U002](../audits/literature/BV19b/Yin_U002.md#bv19b-collision-partitions).
All these factors are integral by Gauss's lemma.
A prime p is L-exceptional when it divides Res(D_1,D_2) for two distinct D_1,D_2 in I_L.

Suppose additionally that the actual P of alpha belongs to I_L.
This requires an actual nonzero binary-word difference divisible by P in this horizon; it is not supplied merely by uniqueness in the source's Claim1.
Let x in any finite extension F_(p^f) be a root of Pbar, and assume p is not L-exceptional.
Then the FULL collision partition of S_L at x is the partition at alpha.

Here is the extension-field proof, rather than an invocation of an uninspected source extension.
For a nonzero difference F in S_L-S_L, write F as its sign times the product of its integral monic irreducible factors, with multiplicities.
If F(x)=0, one factor D vanishes at x.
If D differs from P, Pbar and Dbar have a common root in the finite extension and hence a common irreducible factor over F_p.
Their integer Sylvester resultant reduces to zero modulo p, contradicting nonexceptionality.
Therefore D=P.
Conversely P dividing F implies F(x)=F(alpha)=0.
Characteristic-zero minimality also gives F(alpha)=0 iff P divides F.
The zero difference is automatic.
This proves every collision equivalence and its restriction to smaller degrees, without any sum-product assertion for extension fields or product rings.

Since P in I_L has degree d<=L, restriction to degree d-1 is available and must be injective on the initial2^d binary words.
Evaluation O -> F_p[x] identifies the target with the ACTUAL generated subfield F_p[x], whose degree f_0 over F_p may be smaller than the ambient f.
It is the quotient attached to the minimal polynomial of x over F_p, a genuine irreducible factor of Pbar.
Let r=ord(x).
The cardinality and period conclusions are therefore

    p^(f_0)>=2^d,    r>u_* d/m.                           (9)

The first inequality must use f_0, not an arbitrary larger ambient field.
No density of split primes, effective Chebotarev bound or uncharged prime selection is involved.
In contrapositive form: whenever an actual root x has ord(x)<=u_*d/m, every horizon L for which P is a factor of a nonzero binary difference makes p L-exceptional.
This is a specific-input transfer obstruction, not a claim about all finite-field witnesses lifting to possibly different beta.
