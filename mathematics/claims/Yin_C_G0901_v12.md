# A coefficient lower bound from all near-circle root multiplicities

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound independent of support and angular separation

Let \(F\) be a nonzero complex polynomial of degree \(D\ge1\), let \(H_{\mathbb T}=\max_{|z|=1}|F(z)|\), and put \(m(F)=\int_0^{2\pi}\log|F(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
For each distinct nonzero root \(z\), choose any integer \(0\le v_z\le\operatorname{ord}_z F\), and write \(\delta_z=1-\min(|z|,|z|^{-1})\).
For every real \(t>0\),
\[
 \log H_{\mathbb T}-m(F)\ \ge\
     \frac{t}{\pi}\sum_z v_z
       \left(\frac{\exp(-1-t)v_z}{D}-\delta_z\right)_+ .                \tag{1}
\]
Here \(x_+=\max(x,0)\).
All angular neighborhoods may overlap.
For an integer polynomial divisible by \(P^a\), with \(P\) primitive, irreducible, nonconstant, and \(P(0)\ne0\), let \(d=\deg P,\ h=m(P)\).
One obtains, with the coefficient one-norm,
\[
 \frac{\log\|F\|_1}{a}
       \ \ge\ \left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)h+
                         \frac{ad}{\pi\mathrm e^2 D}.                    \tag{2}
\]
More generally the same conclusion holds for a primitive squarefree \(P\) of positive degree with nonzero constant term.
For a reciprocal unit of small measure, (2) supplies a positive lower coefficient rate whenever the ratio \(D/(ad)\) is bounded, even for dense polynomials.

<a id="disk"></a>
## A simultaneous estimate on one part of the unit circle

Replace each factor \(X-z\) with \(|z|>1\) by \(|z|(X-1/\bar z)\), retaining its full multiplicity.
The new complex polynomial \(G\) has exactly the same modulus on the unit circle as \(F\) because the two factors have the same modulus there.
Every chosen zero now lies in the closed disk at its original argument, at distance \(\delta_z\) from the circle.
If two images coincide, their available multiplicities add.

For any degree-\(D\) polynomial of circle maximum \(H_{\mathbb T}\), its derivative of order \(r\le D\) on the closed unit disk has modulus at most \(D(D-1)\cdots(D-r+1)H_{\mathbb T}\).
For the first derivative, the polynomial \(G(X)-\lambda X^D\) has all \(D\) zeros strictly in the disk whenever \(|\lambda|>H_{\mathbb T}\), by Rouché's theorem.
Its derivative cannot vanish outside the disk: the logarithmic derivative there is a sum of \(1/(X-z_j)\), and each \(X/(X-z_j)\) has positive real part.
Choosing \(\lambda=G'(X)/(D X^{D-1})\) at an exterior point and then approaching the unit circle gives the first derivative bound.
Iterate and apply the maximum principle.

For \(r\) selected roots \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\) of \(G\), with repetitions up to their multiplicities, and \(w\) on the circle, it follows that
\[
 |G(w)|\le H_{\mathbb T}\binom Dr\prod_{j=1}^r|w-y_j| .               \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the quotient by the displayed root factors at \(w\) is the divided difference of order \(r\) at these nodes and \(w\), since all the earlier interpolation terms vanish.
The divided difference is the integral of \(G^{(r)}\) at the convex combinations of the nodes over the standard simplex of volume \(1/r!\).
For completeness, this identity on a monomial \(X^j\), \(j\ge r\), follows by expanding the derivative at the convex combination and using the simplex monomial integral \(\int\prod t_i^{n_i}=\prod n_i!/(r+\sum n_i)!\).
Each monomial in the nodes of total degree \(j-r\) then has coefficient one, precisely the divided difference obtained from the geometric series for \(\prod_i(1-y_i T)^{-1}\) with \(w\) included among the nodes.
Monomials of lower degree give zero, and continuity permits coincident nodes.

<a id="arcs"></a>
## Charging all the covered arcs at each angle

For each selected \(z\) take the arc centered at \(\arg z\) of angular half-length
\[
                  L_z=(\exp(-1-t)v_z/D-\delta_z)_+ .
\]
Arcs of length zero may be discarded.
At every point on a positive-length arc the chord to the reflected root is at most its radial distance plus the angular displacement, hence at most \(\exp(-1-t)v_z/D\).
Fix an angle and use all the covering arcs at once in (3), with \(v_z\) copies of their root.
For \(r=\sum_{\mathrm{cover}}v_z>0\), the bound \(\binom Dr\le(\mathrm e D/r)^r\) gives
\[
 \frac{|G(e^{i\theta})|}{H_{\mathbb T}}
 \ \le\ e^{-tr}\prod_{\mathrm{cover}}\left(\frac{v_z}{r}\right)^{v_z}
 \ \le\ e^{-tr}.
\]
At a point outside the arcs the bound is \(1\).
Each arc has normalized length \(L_z/\pi\), since \(L_z<1\).
Integration of the resulting logarithmic deficit proves (1), including if some roots on the circle contribute integrable singularities.

For (2), select precisely \(a\) copies of every root of \(P\) and take \(t=1\).
For any nonzero root, \(\delta_z\le |\log|z||\).
If \(b_0,b_d\) are the constant and leading coefficients of \(P\), its distinct roots satisfy
\[
          \sum_{P(z)=0}|\log|z||=2m(P)-\log|b_0 b_d|\le2h .
\]
Use \((x)_+\ge x\) in (1).
Gauss's lemma gives an integral cofactor of \(P^a\), so \(m(F)\ge ah\).
Since \(H_{\mathbb T}\le\|F\|_1\), these inequalities give (2).

<a id="width"></a>
## The degree a small coefficient rate requires

Fix the substitution exponent \(q\ge2\) and a sign in the [unrestricted fixed-exponent criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#lehmer).
A genuine row \(A\) at width \(B\) and multiplicity \(a\) gives a signed graph of degree at most \(qB+1\) and coefficient one-norm exactly \(2\|A\|_1\), since the two residue classes are disjoint.
If \(P\) is the minimum of the qualifying root, (2) implies the explicit degree cap
\[
          \deg P\ \le\
              \pi\mathrm e^2\,\frac{qB+1}{a^2}\log(2\|A\|_1).           \tag{4}
\]
Thus if this fixed-exponent test has \(\log\|A\|_1=o(a)\), the degrees of its qualifying roots are \(o(B/a)\) on the width schedules of that criterion.
More generally, if \(\log\|F\|_1/a\le C h\) for an integer multiple of \(P^a\) with \(h>0\), then \(D/(ad)\ge1/(\pi\mathrm e^2 C h)\).
The estimate allows the degree ratio to grow as \(h\) decreases.

The optimal degree-free constant in a logarithmic multiplicity estimate is exactly determined by Lehmer's infimum.
Specifically let \(\lambda\) be the infimum of positive total logarithmic Mahler measures of nonzero integer polynomials, and take the infimum over integer polynomials \(F\) and positive integers \(a\) such that \(F\ne0\) has a nonzero nontorsion root of multiplicity at least \(a\).
Then
\[
               \lambda=\inf_{F,a}\frac{\log\|F\|_1}{a}.
\]
The lower inequality is immediate from divisibility and Mahler measure.
For the reverse inequality, at any fixed reciprocal nontorsion unit the [full-set coefficient rate](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#statement) realizes its measure in the limit, and these units approach the [unrestricted infimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
In particular an absolute \(C'\) such that \(a\le C'\log\|F\|_1\) for all these pairs would resolve the problem positively.
Inequality (2) proves such a bound for bounded \(D/(a\deg P)\), leaving the unbounded ratios open.
