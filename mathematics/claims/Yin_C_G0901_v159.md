# Relative degrees and radial moments for products of consecutive or separated strict records

<a id="statement"></a>
## What a bound on the second iterate must control

Take any \(k\ge2\) distinct reciprocal strict degree records \(w_i\), in increasing order of their degrees \(d_i\).
Write \(A_i=M(w_i)>1\), \(m_i=\log A_i\), \(D_i=\deg A_i\), and \(d_i=2u_ib_i\) for the canonical blocks of inverse pairs.
Assume the \(m_i\) bounded and the \(d_i\) sufficiently large in terms of that ceiling.
For \(C=\prod_i A_i^{s_i}\) with arbitrary positive integer exponents put \(D=\deg C\).
Then
\[
 D_1\prod_{i=2}^k\max\{b_i,D_i^{\,1/(1+\log(4u_i)/\log b_i)}\}
             \ \le\ D\ \le\ \prod_{i=1}^k D_i.                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\log D=(1-o(1))\sum_i\log D_i\) as the smallest source degree tends to infinity, uniformly in \(k\), without any linear disjointness hypothesis.
At every prefix the absolute logarithmic height of the product is at least that at the preceding prefix.
Consequently its total log measure satisfies
\[
             \log M(C)\ge s_1\log M(A_1)\prod_{i=2}^k b_i.              \tag{2}
\]
The variance of the log modulus of a random conjugate is exactly the sum of the individual variances multiplied by \(s_i^2\), with each variance given by (4) below.

In particular **every** pair of distinct shrinking strict records, including consecutive records, satisfies
\[
        \log M(A_1 A_2)\gg
            \frac{d_2}{m_2^2(\log(8d_2))^{39}} .                       \tag{3}
\]
There is no choice of an especially distant second degree in (3).
For \(k\ge2\) distinct unpowered records write \(m=\sum_i m_i\).
For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), at sufficiently small \(m>0\) uniformly in \(k\),
\[
      \log M\!\left(\prod_i A_i\right)\ge
        \exp\!\left(\eta(k-1)(k/m)^{1/3}\log(k/m)\right).
\]
These are lower bounds for the dependence on relative degree.
They do not supply the upper bound that would exclude the [two-record counterexample criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#statement).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## An independent block orbit at each adjunction

Every record is compressed, since a decrease of degree under torsion-class compression would preserve its record measure.
Its actual class space \(W_i\) is the [pure absolutely irreducible representation](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) on all signed coordinates, or the sum of the blockwise sum-zero spaces with torsion half products.
The sources are unmatched, since matched compressed classes have equal orbit degrees. [Matched fields](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)
Each \(A_i\) is a strict Perron unit and belongs to the source splitting field; the stabilizer of its nonzero class \(v_i\) fixes the actual number.
All its positive powers generate the same field.
Fixing the positive prefix product requires preservation of the maximum modulus of each of its factors, hence its field is the compositum of their actual root fields. [Perron product field](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#fields)

Over the splitting fields of all previous sources, the [relative alternating theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) supplies every supported alternating label permutation on the new source.
In the oriented case fourth powers of lifts of supported three-cycles give their pure actions.
In the independent case the image of the group fixing the previous normal fields is normal; commuting such a lift with a double flip in the full group on a moved and a spare label gives a supported double flip in the relative group.
Conjugation gives all double flips on each block. [Relative action on actual coordinates](Yin_C_G0901_v142.md#average)
Fourth powers of three-cycle lifts are supported on the three pairs and have order three; their remaining even signs can be corrected in this relative group.
Let \(J_i\) be the group generated by the pure alternating block groups and, in the independent case, by all supported even flips on every block.
The relative group contains \(J_i\).

Let \(t\ge1\) be the number of blocks on which \(v_i\) is nonzero and \(n_i=|J_i v_i|\).
On an oriented occupied block some coefficient color has multiplicity strictly between zero and \(b_i\), and its position sets alone give at least \(b_i\) images.
On an independent block any proper nonempty support has at least \(b_i\) possible positions, and a full support has at least \(2^{b_i-1}\) sign choices.
The local actions are independent, so \(n_i\ge b_i^t\).
The full orbit has at most \((4u_i)^t n_i\) images: specify the destination of each occupied block, one permutation parity per occupied block, and one orientation sign or one inversion parity per occupied block in the respective models.
With these data fixed the remaining transports differ by \(J_i\); unoccupied blocks do not affect the vector.
Consequently \(n_i\ge D_i^{1/(1+\log(4u_i)/\log b_i)}\).
The relative degree at each new adjunction is at least \(n_i\), even over the previous splitting fields.
It is at most \(D_i\) over the previous product field.
Multiplying proves (1); the uniform log-asymptotic follows from \(u_i\ll(\log(3d_i))^{212}\) and \(b_i=d_i/(2u_i)\). [Canonical blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement)

<a id="moments"></a>
## Conditional means, exact second moments and absolute-height bounds

Choose a uniformly random embedding by a group element \(g\) in a common normal field and put \(X_i=\log|gA_i|\).
Under the restriction maps to the previous source splitting fields the conditional mean of \(X_i\) is zero.
Indeed the relative subgroup above has no fixed vector on \(W_i\) by the independent block actions on the signed or sum-zero spaces.
Averaging its action on the class and then applying the real linear functional \(\log|\cdot|\) proves the assertion on every fiber.
The partial sums \(S_j=\sum_{i\le j}s_iX_i\) therefore have \(\mathbb E(S_j\mid\text{previous fields})=S_{j-1}\).
Conditional Jensen gives \(\mathbb E|S_j|\ge\mathbb E|S_{j-1}|\); for units the absolute height is half this expectation.
It also gives \(s_i h(A_i)\le2h(C)\) by the triangle inequality at the \(i\)-th step and monotonicity thereafter.
Combining height monotonicity with the degree tower proves (2).

For completeness take one signed representative of each pair, from the oriented halves in the second model.
Use their exterior coefficients \(1,0,-1\), subtracting the mean in each oriented block, to represent \(v_i\) in the standard Euclidean coordinates.
Let \(r_i\) be the vector of the corresponding signed log moduli in the same coordinates; it already has zero sum on each oriented block.
Then \(X_i=\langle gv_i,r_i\rangle\).
If \(t_i=\dim W_i\), group averaging of the square of this expression gives
\[
        V_i=\mathbb E X_i^2=\|v_i\|^2\|r_i\|^2/t_i,\qquad
        \mathbb E S_k^2=\sum_i s_i^2V_i.                                \tag{4}
\]
The first identity follows because the orbit covariance of \(v_i\) is an invariant symmetric operator on an absolutely irreducible orthogonal space, hence scalar, with trace \(\|v_i\|^2\).
The cross terms in the second identity vanish by the conditional means.
Since a conjugate exterior product uses a subset of the source roots, every \(X_i\) lies in \([-m_i,m_i]\), using the unit norm for the lower bound.
Writing \(T=\sum_i s_i m_i=\log C\) gives the explicit two-sided estimate
\[
        \frac{\sum_i s_i^2V_i}{2T}\le h(C)
             \le \frac12\sqrt{\sum_i s_i^2V_i}
             \le \frac12\sqrt{\sum_i s_i^2m_i^2}.
\]
The upper estimate controls the absolute height; multiplication by \(D\) is necessary for the second log measure.

<a id="consecutive"></a>
## Consecutive records and arbitrary block lengths

For two records (2) gives \(\log M(A_1A_2)\ge b_2\log M(A_1)\ge b_2m_1\).
The [two-conjugate effective block bound](Yin_C_G0901_v150.md#statement) gives \(b_2\gg d_2/(m_2^2(\log(8d_2))^{36})\), while [Voutier's degree estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m_1>2/(\log(3d_1))^3\).
This proves (3).
For the many-record estimate, each \(b_i\) satisfies \(\log b_i\ge \eta' f(m_i)\) uniformly at small \(m_i\) for any fixed \(\eta'<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), where \(f(s)=s^{-1/3}\log(1/s)\), by [block-size inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#minimal).
The first record has the largest measure among the selected strictly decreasing record values, so \(m_1\ge m/k\) and \(\sum_{i=2}^k m_i\le(k-1)m/k\).
The function \(f\) is convex and decreasing for small \(s>0\), giving \(\sum_{i=2}^k f(m_i)\ge(k-1)f(m/k)\).
Taking logs in (2), the first factor costs at most \(\log(k/m)\) in this lower estimate because \(\log M(A_1)\ge m_1\).
That loss is \(o((k-1)f(m/k))\) uniformly for \(k\ge2\).
Choosing \(\eta'>\eta\) proves the assertion.
If the full gap fails, consecutive new record minima approach one and every consecutive pair satisfies (3); forcing an arbitrarily preassigned growth function as in v155 can still use a more distant second record.
