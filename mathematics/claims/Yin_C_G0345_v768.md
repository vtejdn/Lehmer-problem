# A positive density of irreducible prime-sum sections and the distribution of their degree constraint

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility and small factors among all short directions

Fix an integer \(B\ge2\) with distinct prime divisors \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\), and \(0<\gamma<(1-\log 2)/2\).
Choose pairs \((\ell,u)\) with equal weight, where \(\ell\in[x,2x]\) is prime and \(U<u\le2U\) is an integer, with \(U=U(x)\to\infty\) and \(2U\le\exp((\log x)^\gamma)\).
For all sufficiently large \(x\), every prime in the interval is coprime to \(B\).
Write \(I_\ell\) for the index of \(\langle p_1,\ldots,p_r\rangle\) in \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\), and put \(g=\gcd(u,I_\ell)\).
Define the fixed splitting fields \(K_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_n,p_1^{1/n},\ldots,p_r^{1/n})\), taking positive real radicals.
The limiting proportion with \(g=a\) exists for every integer \(a\ge1\) and equals
\[
 \pi_B(a)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{am[K_{am}:\mathbb Q]}.
\]
The series is absolutely convergent, the masses sum to one, and
\[
 \pi_B(a)\asymp_r\frac1{a[K_a:\mathbb Q]},\qquad
             \sum_{a>T}\pi_B(a)\asymp_r T^{-(r+1)}
             \quad(T\ge1).
\]

Put \(v=\ell-u\), \(F_\epsilon=X^\ell-BX^v+\epsilon B X^u-\epsilon\), and \(P_\epsilon=F_\epsilon/(X-\epsilon)\) for \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
At least a lower limiting proportion \(\pi_B(1)>0\) of all these pairs have \(P_\epsilon(X^k)\) irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for both signs and all \(k\) coprime to the fixed integer \(R_B\) of the [degree allocation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v765.md#statement).
For \(B=2\) one has explicitly
\[
                  \pi_2(1)=\prod_{p\ {\rm prime}}
                          \left(1-\frac1{p^2(p-1)}\right).
\]
This is the density of a sufficient condition for irreducibility.

Let \(E_x(\eta)\) be the proportion of pairs admitting, for either sign and any \(k\ge1\), a noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(F_\epsilon(X^k)\) of logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(\eta\).
Uniformly for \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[
          E_x(\eta)\ll_{B,\gamma}\eta+\frac{\log(2U)}{U}+o_{B,\gamma}(1).
\]
In particular \(E_x(\eta(x))\to0\) for every \(\eta(x)\to0\).
For each fixed \(0<\eta\le1\), the limiting upper proportion also satisfies \(\limsup_{x\to\infty}E_x(\eta)\ll_{B,\gamma}\eta^{r+1}\).

<a id="distribution"></a>
## The limiting distribution from fixed splitting fields

For each fixed \(n\), away from primes dividing \(nB\), the condition \(n\mid I_\ell\) requires \(\ell\equiv1\bmod n\) and every \(p_i\) to be an \(n\)-th power in \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\), and these conditions suffice by cyclicity.
They hold exactly when \(\ell\) splits completely in \(K_n\), away from the finitely many ramified primes.
Indeed \(\mu_n\) and all the roots of \(X^n-p_i\) then reduce to the prime field, and conversely Frobenius fixes them all when they have distinct reductions there.
The [counting form of Chebotarev's density theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) gives relative proportion \(1/[K_n:\mathbb Q]\) among the primes in \([x,2x]\), by subtracting the two fixed-field asymptotics.
Independently, the proportion of short exponents divisible by \(n\) tends to \(1/n\).
Thus the limiting probability of \(n\mid g\) is \(1/(n[K_n:\mathbb Q])\).

Write \(\mathbb P_x\) for the uniform law on pairs.
For \(n\le2U\), divisibility by \(n\) requires \(n\mid u\) and \(\ell\equiv1\bmod n\).
The [uniform one-progression upper bound](Yin_C_G0345_v765.md#counting) and the prime number theorem give
\[
                \mathbb P_x(n\mid g)\ll
                  \left(\frac1n+\frac1U\right)\frac1{\varphi(n)} .
\]
The mean bound \(\sum_{n\le y}n/\varphi(n)\ll y\) from the same argument gives by dyadic summation
\[
       \sum_{T<n\le2U}\mathbb P_x(n\mid g)
                 \ll \frac1T+\frac{\log(2U)}{U},\qquad T\ge1,
\]
This also bounds \(\mathbb P_x(g>T)\).
For \(g=a\) use the exact indicator \(\sum_{m:\,am\mid g}\mu(m)\).
The tail estimate just proved permits taking the limit first for finitely many \(m\), then extending the sum to infinity.
The limiting series converges absolutely since \([K_n:\mathbb Q]\ge\varphi(n)\).
Tightness from the same estimate shows that the probabilities \(\pi_B(a)\) sum to one.

<a id="fields"></a>
## The degrees of the splitting fields

Let \(E=\mathbb Q(\mu_n)\).
The Galois group of \(K_n/E\) embeds in \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^r\) through its multipliers on the positive radicals.
For its image \(H\), the annihilator \(H^\perp\) under the ordinary character pairing consists of the exponent vectors \(0\le e_i<n\) for which \(\prod_i p_i^{e_i/n}\) is fixed by \(H\), or equivalently belongs to \(E\).
Character orthogonality gives \(|H|\,|H^\perp|=n^r\), also by summing the pairing over all exponent vectors and all elements of \(H\) in either order.
The positive monomial \(b=\prod_i p_i^{e_i/n}\) generates a real field.
If it belongs to \(E\), this field is Galois and abelian over \(\mathbb Q\), so all conjugates of \(b\) are real.
Since \(b^n\) is rational, those conjugates lie among \(b,-b\).
Hence \(b^2\) is rational.
Unique factorization of rational numbers now gives \(n\mid 2e_i\) for each \(i\).
For odd \(n\) only the zero vector occurs.
For even \(n\) the annihilator has one member for each subset of the \(p_i\) whose product has its positive square root in \(E\).
In particular
\[
       n^r\varphi(n)/2^r\ \le\ [K_n:\mathbb Q]\ \le\ n^r\varphi(n).
\]
For \(B=2\) and squarefree \(n\), the field \(E\) is unramified at two, since its roots of unity can be taken of odd order and then have separable reduction at two.
It cannot contain \(\sqrt2\), whose quadratic field is ramified there.
Thus \([K_n:\mathbb Q]=n\varphi(n)\) at every squarefree \(n\), and the multiplicative absolutely convergent series for \(a=1\) gives the product displayed above.

<a id="tail"></a>
## Sharp bounds on the limiting probabilities

Certainly \(\pi_B(a)\le1/(a[K_a:\mathbb Q])\).
To obtain a uniform reverse bound, condition on \(a\mid g\) for fixed \(a\).
For any finite list of primes \(q\le Q\), the limiting fraction of such pairs for which none divides \(u/a\) equals \(\prod_{q\le Q}(1-1/q)\).
Indeed conditioning on \(a\mid g\) restricts the primes and independently restricts \(u\) to multiples of \(a\).
The displayed product is at least \(1/\lfloor Q\rfloor\) for integer \(Q\ge2\), by including all integer factors \(1-1/j\) with \(2\le j\le Q\) in the comparison product.
For any prime \(q>Q\), the limiting conditional probability of \(aq\mid g\) is \([K_a:\mathbb Q]/(q[K_{aq}:\mathbb Q])\).
By the degree bounds it is at most \(2^r/(q^{r+1}(q-1))\), since \(\varphi(aq)/\varphi(a)\) is \(q\) or \(q-1\).
For fixed \(a\), the preceding tightness estimate for the sum of divisibility probabilities justifies the infinite union bound after taking the limit and conditioning.
The sum over \(q>Q\) is \(O_r(Q^{-r-1})\).
Choose an integer \(Q\) depending only on \(r\) that makes this last term less than \(1/(2Q)\).
On the event left after these exclusions, no prime \(q\) has \(aq\mid g\), so \(g=a\).
This proves the uniform lower bound for \(\pi_B(a)\).
Summing it for \(T<a\le2T\), using \(\varphi(a)\le a\), gives the claimed lower tail.
The upper bound follows by summing \(2^r/(a^{r+1}\varphi(a))\) over \(a>T\) using the same mean bound for \(a/\varphi(a)\).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Consequences for every dilation

The exceptional prime totals in the [exact allocation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v765.md#statement) contribute \(o_{B,\gamma}(1)\) of the pairs independently of \(U\), since every prime is paired with the same number of short exponents.
On the remaining totals \(g=1\) suffices for the simultaneous irreducibility in the statement by that theorem.
More generally each positive factor measure under every dilation is at least \((\log B)/(2gR_B)\) there.
Writing \(c_B=(\log B)/(2R_B)>0\), a factor counted by \(E_x(\eta)\) therefore requires \(g\ge c_B/\eta\) on those totals.
For \(\eta<c_B/2\) use the finite tail estimate at \(T=c_B/(2\eta)\) to obtain the uniform inequality.
For any fixed such \(\eta\) the limiting distribution and its upper tail give the sharper exponent \(r+1\).
The complementary thresholds up to one satisfy the same bounds by increasing the constants depending on \(B\).
