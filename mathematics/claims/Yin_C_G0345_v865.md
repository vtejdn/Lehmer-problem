# A coefficient-independent area obstruction for reciprocal section roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative power degree at an isolated inverse intersection

Let \(F\) be a primitive rationally irreducible integral Laurent polynomial in two variables with Newton polygon \(N\) of positive Euclidean area \(A\).
For roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\), a primitive signed pair \((k,l)\), and any positive integer \(g\), take a nonzero nontorsion root \(z\) of \(F(\xi z^{gk},\eta z^{gl})\).
Put \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), \(\beta=z^g\), \(P=(\xi\beta^k,\eta\beta^l)\), \(w=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}\xi,\operatorname{ord}\eta)\) and \(s=[\mathbb Q(z)\cap H:\mathbb Q]\).
Suppose that \(z\) is reciprocal, meaning that it is conjugate over \(\mathbb Q\) to \(z^{-1}\).
Choose an automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) taking \(z\) to \(z^{-1}\), and put \(t=(\xi\sigma(\xi),\eta\sigma(\eta))\).
If \(P\) is an isolated common point in the torus of \(F(X,Y)=0\) and \(F(t_1/X,t_2/Y)=0\) for at least one such choice, then
\[
 D=[H(\beta):H]\le \operatorname{area}(N-N)-2A\le4A.
\]
This isolation condition is automatic for every choice of \(\sigma\) when \(N\) is not centrally symmetric.
With \(G_j(u)=(\log\log(5u))^3/(\log(2u))^j\) and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(z)\), it follows that
\[
 m(z)\gg sG_4(4A),\qquad s\ge\max\{1,\varphi(w)/(22A)\}.
\]
If \(z\) is an algebraic integer and \(m(z)\le1\), the first bound holds with \(G_3\) in place of \(G_4\).
The implied constants are absolute, and the results include the untwisted section and every other torsion twist.

<a id="intersection"></a>
## Reciprocal conjugacy and mixed area

Writing the two coordinates multiplicatively gives \(\sigma P=t/P\), so rationality of \(F\) puts \(P\) on both curves.
Both equations are defined over \(H\), including the torsion constants in the second equation.
All conjugates of \(P\) over \(H\) are isolated when \(P\) is isolated, since each automorphism preserves the two varieties and their intersection dimensions.
Their number is \(D\) because \(H(P)=H(\beta)\) by Bézout's identity for \(k,l\).
Remove the common Laurent gcd of the two equations over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
Every isolated point is outside the zero set of that gcd and remains a common zero of the residual coprime equations.
If one residual equation is a unit, there are no such points.
Otherwise the [plane mixed-area bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#intersection-multiplicities) bounds their number by the mixed area of the residual polygons in the convention \(B(K,L)=|K+L|-|K|-|L|\), where bars denote area.
These polygons are Minkowski summands of \(N\) and \(-N\), respectively, up to translation, since the Newton polygon of a product is the Minkowski sum.
Minkowski additivity of the mixed area and its nonnegativity therefore bound their mixed area by \(B(N,-N)=|N-N|-2A\).

For the numerical estimate put \(K=N-N\), which contains zero in its interior, and \(J(u)=|N\cap(N+u)|\).
By Fubini, \(\int_K J(u)\,du=A^2\).
For \(v\in\partial K\) write \(v=x-y\) with \(x,y\in N\).
At \(u=rv\), \(0\le r\le1\), the set \((1-r)N+rx\) lies in \(N\cap(N+u)\), because subtraction of \(u\) turns it into \((1-r)N+ry\).
The area of the region of radial fraction at most \(r\) is \(r^2|K|\), so integration gives
\[
 A^2\ge A|K|\int_0^1 (1-r)^2 2r\,dr=A|K|/6.
\]
In particular \(B(N,-N)\le4A\).

The geometric factors of \(F\) are distinct conjugates and after monomial normalization their Newton polygons agree up to translation, with a common representative \(N_0\).
Thus \(N\) is a translate of \(cN_0\) for their number \(c\).
Any common component with the scaled inverse would make \(N_0\) a translate of \(-N_0\), since torsion scaling preserves exponents and inversion negates them.
That would make \(N\) centrally symmetric, proving automatic isolation in the stated case.
This supplies a uniform numerical refinement of the [fixed-template inverse-component test](Yin_C_G0345_v777.md#inverse), including isolated points even when different components elsewhere coincide.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Compression, bounded area and shrinking measures

The [cyclic compression formula](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#compression) supplies a positive power \(\delta\) of \(z\) in \(H(\beta)\) with \(H(\delta)=H(\beta)\), ordinary degree \(sD\), and \(m(z)=m(\delta)=sD h(\delta)\).
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) applied to \(\delta\) gives \(m(z)\gg sG_4(D)\).
For integral \(z\) and \(m(z)\le1\), the compressed number is integral and the [cubic abelian-subfield estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#statement) gives \(m(z)\gg sG_3(D)\).
Both \(G_j\) are positive and continuous on \([1,\infty)\), eventually decreasing to zero, so \(G_j(D)\gg G_j(4A)\) uniformly for \(1\le D\le4A\).
The [phase-order estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#phases) gives \(\varphi(w)\le22As\) with no dependence on the coefficients.

Consequently every nontorsion factor measure from a section with a noncentrally symmetric polygon of bounded area has a common positive lower bound depending only on the area bound.
Indeed the reciprocal factors satisfy the preceding estimate, and every nonreciprocal algebraic integer has measure at least \(\log\theta\) by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(X^3-X-1=0\).
A nonintegral algebraic number has measure at least \(\log2\) by the leading coefficient of its primitive integral minimal polynomial.
For isolated reciprocal roots and fixed upper bounds on both area and measure, there are in fact only finitely many different measure values.
The lower bound bounds \(s\), the area bounds \(D\), and the compressed \(\delta\) has bounded degree and height, so Northcott's theorem applies.
In particular for any triangle of area \(1/2\) one obtains \(D\le2\), even for arbitrary torsion phases on the three terms.
Indeed the two edge differences from any vertex form an integral basis, so after dividing out the phase of that term the other two phases are realized by a torsion twist.
This relative degree assertion complements the [sharp factor-measure bound for integer trinomials](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement).

Along any sequence of nontorsion algebraic numbers with \(m=m(z)\to0\), every representation as above by a noncentrally symmetric polygon, and more generally every representation with the stated isolated reciprocal intersection, eventually satisfies
\[
                 \log(2+A)\gg m^{-1/3}\log(1/m).
\]
Indeed the preceding nonintegral and nonreciprocal gaps enforce integrality and reciprocity, and bounded area is excluded.
For \(L=\log(8A)\to\infty\), the cubic estimate gives \(L\gg m^{-1/3}\log L\).
First \(L\gg m^{-1/3}\), whence \(\log L\gg\log(1/m)\), proving the assertion with absolute constants across all varying templates.
