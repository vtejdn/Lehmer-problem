# The Lehmer infimum from one signed row with growing root multiplicity

<a id="statement"></a>
## An explicit width and a finite upper approximation

Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
For every prime \(p\), put
\[
 a=\max(2,\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor),\qquad b_0=\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor,\qquad
 b=b_0+1,\qquad C_p=2^{\lfloor ap/2\rfloor}.
\]
For nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\), put \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\) and
\[
                   G_A(X)=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^p).
\]
Call \(A\) admissible if \(G_A\) has a nonzero root \(\alpha\) of multiplicity at least \(a\), with \(\alpha\) nontorsion and \(A(\alpha^p)\ne0\).
Let \(H_p\) be the least of \(C_p\) and all one-norms of admissible rows of norm at most \(C_p\).
No prior list of polynomials or algebraic numbers enters this finite test.
For the infimum \(\lambda\) of the strictly positive logarithmic Mahler measures of integer polynomials, one has
\[
 T_p:=\frac{\log((2pb+1)H_p^2)}{(a-1)p}\ \ge\ \lambda
 \quad\hbox{at every prime},\qquad
 \lim_{p\to\infty}T_p=\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{2\log H_p}{ap}=\lambda.       \tag{1}
\]
Thus the running minima of \(((2pb+1)H_p^2)^{1/((a-1)p)}\) are computable real algebraic upper approximations decreasing to \(\exp\lambda\).
At all sufficiently large primes each minimizing row supplies a primitive reciprocal unit minimum \(P_p\) with \(\lambda\le m(P_p)\le T_p\).
Its degree is at most \(D_p=\lfloor2b/(a-1)\rfloor\).
Particular minimizing rows or roots need not be chosen consistently across primes.

For \(D\ge1\), let \(\lambda_D\) be the least positive measure of integer polynomials of degree at most \(D\), and define \(T_{p,D}\) by the same recipe, restricting the eligible root degree to at most \(D\).
Uniformly for all integers \(10\le D\le\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\), as \(p\) tends to infinity,
\[
 \lambda_D\ \le\ T_{p,D}\ \le\
       \left(1+\frac{2+o(1)}{a-1}\right)\lambda_D.                     \tag{2}
\]
For the unrestricted test one also has
\[
 \lambda_{D_p}\le T_p\le
       \left(1+\frac{2+o(1)}{a-1}\right)\lambda_{\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor}. \tag{3}
\]
The left inequality in (3) holds at every prime.
These estimates give a relative error \(O(p^{-1/8})\) in (2); convergence to the unrestricted infimum in (1) also uses \(\lambda_D\to\lambda\).

<a id="elimination"></a>
## Exact multiplicity and the finite test

Write \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon A_b^*\), \(G=G_A\), and put
\[
 W=pY(U'V-UV')-UV.
\]
It is a nonzero integral polynomial of degree at most \(2b\) and \(\|W\|_1\le(2pb+1)\|A\|_1^2\).
At the least exponent of \(UV\), the multiplier of its coefficient in \(W\) is \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0U-\operatorname{ord}_0V)-1\ne0\).
With \(Y=X^p\) one has the polynomial identity
\[
 W(X^p)=V(X^p)\,XG'(X)-\bigl(V(X^p)+pX^pV'(X^p)\bigr)G(X).
\]
At a root \(\alpha\ne0\) with \(V(z)\ne0,\ z=\alpha^p\), the first term has exact order \(\operatorname{ord}_\alpha G-1\) and the second has higher order.
The power substitution is unramified there, so \(\operatorname{ord}_z W=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha G-1\).
For an admissible root, \(U(z)\ne0\), \(V(z)\ne0\) and \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\).
Consequently \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
For their primitive integer minima \(Q,P\), respectively, Gauss's lemma gives \(Q^{a-1}\mid W\) in \(\mathbb Z[Y]\), even when \(Q\) is nonmonic.
The [absolute height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) gives \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\) by equality of degrees.
Nontorsion and \(\alpha\ne0\) give \(m(P)>0\).
Nonnegative measure of the integral cofactor proves
\[
       (a-1)p\,m(P)\ \le\ 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2pb+1).
\]
For the fallback \(2\lfloor ap/2\rfloor\ge(a-1)p\) and \(\lambda_D\le\log2\) for \(D\ge1\).
Since \(D_p\ge1\), this proves the asserted pointwise lower estimates including the fallback.

To decide admissibility, factor \(W\) over \(\mathbb Q\), retaining only factors of multiplicity at least \(a-1\) other than \(Y\) and the cyclotomic factors.
For each such primitive \(Q\), in its number field test \(V(z)\ne0\) and
\[
                       (-U(z))^p=z V(z)^p,\qquad z=Y\bmod Q .
\]
These tests produce exactly the eligible roots up to conjugacy by taking \(\alpha=-U(z)/V(z)\), using the exact order identity and the exclusion of zero and torsion.
They require factorization only through degree \(2b\), and each retained factor has degree at most \(D_p\).
The primitive polynomial of \(\alpha\) can be recovered by exact arithmetic in the same field.
The additional bound in (2) is tested on \(\deg Q=\deg P\).

<a id="construction"></a>
## A prescribed sign at every reciprocal unit through degree \(\sqrt p\)

Fix any monic reciprocal irreducible minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), with degree \(d\le\sqrt p\) and \(h=m(P)\).
We construct an admissible row at this very root satisfying, uniformly in these inputs,
\[
                   2\log\|A\|_1\le (a+1+o(1))ph.                        \tag{4}
\]
The construction first uses two independent rows of width \(b_0\).
For sufficiently large \(p\), the [prime power field calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power) gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)\) since \(d<p-1\).
The powered minimum \(Q\) is monic, with \(m(Q)=ph\).

Take \(t=a+1,\ n=2(b_0+1),\ L=p(b_0+1)\).
Apply the [integral jet boxes](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) to the \(n\) exponents of the form \(pi,pi+1\), \(0\le i\le b_0\), all at most \(L\).
For coefficients in \([0,H]\), the \(t\) divided derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,t-1\) at \(\alpha\) are tuples of algebraic integers.
At each conjugate the derivative of order \(j\) has modulus at most \(nH(1+L)^j\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^L\).
Using real and imaginary coordinate intervals of length at most one half bounds the number of tuples by
\((6nH)^{dt}(1+L)^{dt(t-1)/2}\exp(tLh)\), since a nonzero difference of algebraic integers has nonzero integral norm.
Taking \(H\) to be the floor of the exponential of
\[
        B=\frac{tLh+dt\log(6n)+dt(t-1)\log(1+L)/2}{n-dt}
\]
plus one forces a collision among \((H+1)^n\) choices.
Their difference \(F_0=U_0(X^p)+XV_0(X^p)\ne0\) has \(P^t\mid F_0\) and \(\log\|F_0\|_1\le B+\log(2n)\) by its actual coefficient bounds.
Here \(L/n=p/2,\ dt/n=O(p^{-1/4})\) and \(t\,dt/n=O(p^{-1/8})\).
The terms in \(B\) not involving \(h\) are \(O((dt/n)(1+t)\log p)\).
[Voutier's lower bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(1/h=O((\log p)^3)\).
Thus \(2\log\|F_0\|_1\le(t+o(1))ph\) uniformly.

Both rows are nonzero, since a single row would be divisible by \(Q^t\) before substitution and cost at least \(\exp(tph)\).
Take their primitive integral polynomial gcd \(J\), write \(c=\operatorname{ord}_z J\) at \(z=\alpha^p\), and let \(\widetilde U,\widetilde V\) be the quotients.
The polynomial \(W_0=pY(U_0'V_0-U_0V_0')-U_0V_0\) is nonzero by its lowest term.
Writing it with the quotients gives \(W_0=J^2\widetilde W\).
The graph of the quotients vanishes at \(\alpha\) to order at least \(t-c\) if \(c<t\).
The derivative identity gives \(\operatorname{ord}_z\widetilde W\ge\max(0,t-c-1)\), so \(Q^{t+c-1}\mid W_0\), including when \(c\ge t\).
It follows that
\[
 (t+c-1)ph\le 2\log\|F_0\|_1+\log(2pb_0+1)\le(t+o(1))ph .
\]
Hence \(c\le1\), the quotient graph retains \(P^a\), and \(\widetilde U(z),\widetilde V(z)\) are both nonzero by coprimality.
For either nonzero quotient \(E/J\), the elementary symmetric coefficient bound and \(M(J)\ge1\) give \(\|E/J\|_1\le 2^{b_0} M(E/J)\le2^{b_0}\|E\|_1\).
The additional \(b_0\log2\) is \(o(ph)\) uniformly by the same height bound.

Reversing the quotient graph about \(pb_0+1\) preserves divisibility by \(P^a\), since \(P\) is reciprocal.
For \(\delta=\pm1\), put \(A_\delta=\widetilde U+\delta\widetilde V^*_{b_0}\).
Its signed graph of sign \(\delta\) is the quotient graph plus \(\delta\) times that reversal.
At least one \(A_\delta(z)\) is nonzero since their sum is \(2\widetilde U(z)\).
Multiply that row by \(Y+1\) if \(\delta=\varepsilon\), and by \(Y-1\) otherwise.
The product has signed graph at width \(b=b_0+1\) equal to the earlier signed graph times the substituted multiplier, because the multipliers have reversal signs \(+1,-1\) at width one.
The multiplier is nonzero at the nontorsion \(z\), and the resulting row has norm at most four times the larger quotient norm.
This proves (4) at the prescribed sign, with no coprimality requirement on the final signed row.
The derivative and projection steps above extend the [single-sign derivative calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v416.md#derivative) and [width change](Yin_C_G0345_v416.md#projection) by explicitly preserving the required multiplicity.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## Comparison with the bounded-degree measure minimum

At a fixed degree bound \(D\), integer polynomials of measure at most \(\log2\) have coefficients bounded by \(2\binom di\) in degree \(d\le D\), so \(\lambda_D>0\) is attained.
A positive measure below \(\log2\) reduces by integral factorization to that of a primitive irreducible factor of no larger degree and with positive measure no larger than the original.
The factor can be taken monic with unit constant term after excluding monomials, since otherwise the two endpoint bounds already give measure at least \(\log2\).
For \(D\ge10\) the [degree-ten benchmark](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks) gives \(\lambda_D<\log(6/5)\), and [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) excludes a nonreciprocal minimizer at this level.
Therefore some reciprocal unit minimum of degree at most \(D\) attains \(\lambda_D\).

Apply (4) to such a minimizing seed when \(10\le D\le\sqrt p\).
The produced row lies below \(C_p\) uniformly for large \(p\), by the strict gap between \(\log(6/5)\) and \(\log2\).
Also \(\log(2pb+1)/(p\lambda_D)=o(1)\) uniformly by Voutier's bound at this seed.
Including this correction and dividing by \(a-1\) gives (2).
Removing the root-degree condition can only reduce \(H_p\), giving the upper half of (3) by taking \(D=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\).
As \(\lambda_D\downarrow\lambda\), (3) proves \(T_p\to\lambda\).
Since \(a\to\infty\) and \(\log(2pb+1)=o(ap)\), the same limit holds for \(2\log H_p/(ap)\).
The benchmark keeps the winning cost strictly below the fallback eventually, and the elimination result then gives actual primitive minima with \(0<m(P_p)\le T_p\).
The strict global bound \(\lambda<\log(6/5)\) excludes both nonunits and nonreciprocal units for all sufficiently large primes by the same two exclusions.
This proves all the assertions, without supplying a rate at which \(\lambda_D\) approaches \(\lambda\).
