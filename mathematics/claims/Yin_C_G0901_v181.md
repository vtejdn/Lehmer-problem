# Reconstruction of a dense integral trace-pole polynomial from one dyadic residue word

<a id="statement"></a>
## One finite place suffices for uniqueness at a given height and degree

Fix integers \(q,D\ge2\) and write \(p=q+1,\ L=2q+1,\ d=2D\).
A [genuine integral realization at the first nonunit pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) has a monic reciprocal Salem minimum \(F\) of degree \(d\).
There is no realization for \(D=2\), by the [empty degree-two simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#simplex).
For \(D\ge3\), let \(c\) be the even member of \(\{q,p\}\), \(e=v_2(c)\), and choose integer bounds
\[
 U=\left\lceil (\log(3d))^3/(2q)\right\rceil,\qquad
 K=\lceil 2\log_2 L\rceil .
\]
Larger effective integer bounds may also be used.
If \(2^{b-e}>KU\), a monic degree-\(d\) reciprocal residue polynomial modulo \(2^b\) lifts to **at most one** genuine integral realization at \(q\).
For all heights allowing a realization one can take \(b=O(\log\log(9d))\), with an absolute constant.
The data consist of the known \(q,d\) and the residue of **each** coefficient at this one finite place.
Even the first layer modulo two leaves at most \(KU\) candidates at that \(q,d\).

The proof gives a finite reconstruction and rejection test from the residue word, without a sparsity assumption, squarefree reduction, access to a second prime or an archimedean approximation from the input.
At the stated precision a single admissible exponent ratio can survive the ring tests; an exact factor and signature check still decides whether it gives a genuine lift.
In particular fixing an eligible residue word does not guarantee existence.

<a id="identities"></a>
## Two expressions for the same unit over the dyadic order

For an actual exterior root \(1<T<p/q\) write
\[
 T=\beta^u,\qquad Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)=\beta^v,\qquad 1\le u<v,\quad\gcd(u,v)=1,
\]
with the [common positive unit](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion).
The field and degree are the same for \(\beta,T\), and \(\beta\) is the unique exterior conjugate of its own minimum.
Thus \(u<U+1\) by Voutier's degree bound as used in the [full-modulus estimates](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#periods).
Let \(a\) be the order of \(Z\) in \(\mathbb F_2[Z]/(F\bmod2)\).
The [odd index of the short-power order](Yin_C_G0345_v897.md#index) implies that \(\mathbb Z[T]/2\) and \(\mathbb Z[\beta]/2\) agree.
In this ring \(\beta^{N}=1\) for \(N=v-u\) if \(c=q\) and \(N=v+u\) if \(c=p\).
Since \(u\) is prime to \(N\), the order of \(\beta\) also equals \(a\).
Write \(N=ak\); then \(k\ge1\) and \(\gcd(k,u)=1\).
From \(\beta^a=1\bmod 2\mathbb Z[\beta]\) follows \(\beta^{2a}=1\bmod 4\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
The nonzero norm of \(\beta^{2a}-1\) is at least \(4^d\) in absolute value and is smaller than \(2^d\beta^{2a}\), by the individual conjugate moduli.
Consequently \(a\log\beta\ge d\log2/2\).
The [pole norm estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#periods) gives \(N\log\beta\le\log(YT)<d\log L\).
This yields \(k<2\log_2 L\), without assuming the full even modulus is a prime power.

For an arbitrary monic reciprocal residue word of this degree with constant one, use the rings \(R_b=(\mathbb Z/2^b\mathbb Z)[Z]/F\), and write \(z\) for the residue of \(Z\).
All operations below are defined using this word.
The factor \(p-qz\) is invertible since modulo two it equals \(1\) if \(q\) is even and \(z\) otherwise, and an inverse lifts over a nilpotent ideal.
Form
\[
 W=\begin{cases}
 (pz-q)/(z(p-qz)),&c=q,\\
 z(pz-q)/(p-qz),&c=p .
 \end{cases}
\]
An actual realization must have \(z^{ak}=W^u\) in \(R_b\), as the identity holds in the root field and its denominators are dyadic units.

<a id="recovery"></a>
## Exact local order including nonreduced binary reductions

One has \(W=1+cE\), with \(E=(z^2-1)/(z(p-qz))\) when \(c=q\) and \(E=(z^2-1)/(p-qz)\) when \(c=p\).
In both cases \(E\bmod 2=z+z^{-1}\).
Neither this residue nor its product with \(1+z+z^{-1}\) vanishes.
After multiplication by \(z\) or \(z^2\) the relevant numerators are nonzero monic polynomials of degrees two and four, strictly less than \(d\).
This uses the full monic quotient over \(\mathbb F_2\), including its nilpotents.

Taking any monic integral coefficient lift into \(\mathbb Z_2[Z]/F\), use coefficientwise divisibility in its free basis.
Here \(W-1\) has exact valuation \(e\).
When \(e=1\), squaring gives valuation exactly two by the nonvanishing of \(\bar E(1+\bar E)\), since \(c/2\) is odd.
Whenever \(V-1\) has valuation at least two, \(V+1\) is twice a unit, so squaring increases its valuation by exactly one.
Odd powering preserves it for all \(V=1\bmod2\) with \(V\ne1\), since the geometric sum is a unit.
It follows that for \(b\ge e\) the order of \(W\) in \(R_b^\times\) is exactly \(2^{b-e}\).

To perform the test, obtain \(a\) by successive multiplication modulo two, rejecting if the order exceeds \(d(\log(3d))^3\log L\), as the [uniform period cap](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#words) and its proof allow.
Test in \(R_b\) the coprime pairs \(1\le k\le K,\ 1\le u\le U\) against \(z^{ak}=W^u\).
If two pairs pass, eliminating \(z^{ak_1k_2}\) gives \(W^{u_1k_2-u_2k_1}=1\).
The absolute determinant is smaller than \(KU\); divisibility by \(2^{b-e}>KU\) forces it to vanish.
Coprimality makes the positive pairs identical.
At precision modulo two alone the pair count \(KU\) still applies, without this uniqueness step.
If \(q\ge (\log(3d))^3/2\), no actual realization exists by Voutier's inequality.
Otherwise \(e\le\log_2(q+1)\), \(K=O(\log(2q+1))\), and the stated \(U\) and the first eligible \(b\) give the asserted precision.

<a id="real"></a>
## From the recovered exponents to a unique real root

The pair fixes \(v=ak+u\) when \(c=q\) and \(v=ak-u\) when \(c=p\).
Discard any exponent pair violating the original inequalities or coprimality.
For \(1<T<p/q\) put \(x=\log T\), \(s=\tanh(x/2)\).
The exact transformation gives
\[
     \log\frac{pT-q}{p-qT}=2\operatorname{artanh}(L s).
\]
The right side vanishes at zero and has derivative \(L(1-s^2)/(1-L^2s^2)\), strictly increasing for \(0<s<1/L\).
Its quotient by \(x>0\) is therefore strictly increasing.
The equation of slope \(v/u\) on this interval has at most one solution.
A surviving pair consequently yields at most one monic minimum \(F\).
It can be recovered if present by factoring \(Z^v(p-qZ)^u-(pZ-q)^u\) over the rationals, checking the specified degree, pole, full root signature and residue word as in the [bounded-degree census](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#census).
All uniqueness statements refer to the polynomial, independently of which separating grid describes its roots.

For comparison, [one-prime approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#approximation) over any prescribed odd denominator prime gives infinitely many different rational irreducible trace-pole models of the strict signature in the same dyadic residue class of any integral pole-lattice representative, at the same \(q,D\ge3\) and any fixed \(b\).
They have different exact denominators.
Thus congruences, strict root configuration and exact pole alone do not give the asserted dyadic uniqueness after releasing another finite place.

<a id="tests"></a>
## Excluding an entire family of one-prime capacity controls at the prime two

The uniqueness test has a simpler obstruction which can reject even before the precision sufficient to recover a ratio is reached.
For \(q=2\), take
\[
 H=x^3+4x^2+2x-1/3,\qquad
 3F(Z)=3Z^6-6Z^5+3Z^4-Z^3+3Z^2-6Z+3 .
\]
The signs of \(H\) at \(-4,-1,0,1/6\) are \(-,+,-,+\), and \(6^3H(1/6)=25\).
The degree-three integer polynomial \(3H\) has no rational root, by testing \(\pm1,\pm1/3\); the negative trace embeddings make the reciprocal quadratic lift irreducible.
These are rational coefficients integral at two.
In the binary quotient \(F\bmod2=(Z^2+Z+1)(Z^4+Z^3+Z^2+Z+1)\), so \(a=15\).
In the quotient modulo four the ascending coefficients of \(F\) are \((1,2,1,1,1,2,1)\).
Reducing \(z^{15}\) by this monic relation gives ascending coordinates \((3,0,2,2,0,2)\) in the basis through \(z^5\).
Consequently \(z^{15}=1+2(1+\bar E)\bmod4\), where \(\bar E=z+z^{-1}=z^2+z^3+z^5\bmod2\); this notation uses any lift of \(\bar E\) in a term multiplied by two.
The necessary identity \(z^{15k}=W^u\) modulo four forces \(k(1+\bar E)=u\bar E\) modulo two.
Independence of \(1,\bar E\) implies \(k,u\) both even, contradicting their coprimality.
This reasoning excludes every integral realization in the given residue word, without referring to its negative coefficient count or to the separate integral cubic exclusion.

There are such exclusions even under all the [combined capacity tests and exact pole constraints](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement).
Fix any sufficiently large \(q\) divisible by four and any odd denominator prime \(\ell\), and still write \(e=v_2(q)\).
Put \(J=q/2+1,\ m=2J+1,\ l=2D-m\), so \(m,l\) are odd.
In the [degree construction](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) strengthen the congruence for \(p^D\) modulo \(q^{J+1}\) to the same congruence modulo \(q^{J+3}\), keeping the congruence at \(p\).
Its principal-unit proof works with each exponent raised by the required two powers of \(q\) since \(4\mid q\); the chosen residues for \(D\) modulo the primes dividing \(M\) are unchanged.
All extra congruences away from \(M\) remain compatible, giving arbitrarily large \(D\) with the required rotational coprimalities.
For \(P_0=(Z^l-1)(Z^m-1)\), the pole evaluation is now congruent to \(K=L^2\) modulo \(2^{e+1}M^{J+1}\): in the computation at \(q\) both \(m,l\ge J+3\), and \(2^{e+1}\mid q^2\).
The attainable increments at fixed zero prefix have gcd \(M^{J+1}\).
It follows that an integral representative of the exact pole with that prefix can be chosen congruent to \(P_0\bmod 2^{e+1}\), by correcting its evaluation by \(2^{e+1}\) times an integral combination of the free reciprocal coefficient pairs.
Use this as the specified congruence in the [one-prime construction](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place).
The resulting rational irreducible polynomials have one exterior pair, \(J\) zero initial power sums, the exact pole and strictly superunit capacity after each positive power as stated there.
Their sole possible denominator prime \(\ell\) is distinct from the testing prime.

Yet the residue of \(P_0\) modulo \(2^{e+1}\) cannot occur in a genuine integral realization.
Its binary order \(a\) is \(2\operatorname{lcm}(l,m)\): both odd-exponent binomials are separable, their root orders generate the indicated odd least common multiple, and their product has multiplicity two at \(1\) and no higher multiplicity anywhere.
Compute the necessary ring identity \(z^{ak}=W^u\) at \(c=q\) in two quotients of \((\mathbb Z/2^{e+1})[Z]/P_0\).
In the quotient by \((Z-1)^2\) write \(z=1+t,\ t^2=0\).
Then \(z^2-1=2t\), so \(W=1\), and \(z^{ak}=1+ak t\) forces \(2^{e+1}\mid ak\).
As \(v_2(a)=1\), this forces \(k\) even.
In the quotient by \(1+Z+\cdots+Z^{l-1}\) one has \(z^l=1\), hence \(z^{ak}=1\).
Here \(\bar E\ne0\) because multiplication by \(z\) gives the nonzero polynomial \(z^2-1\) of degree less than \(l-1\).
Since \(W=1+q E\), the identity \(W^u=1\bmod 2^{e+1}\) forces \(u\) even.
These requirements contradict coprimality.
In particular at every sufficiently large \(q\equiv4\bmod8\) the same precision, **modulo eight**, excludes this class in arbitrarily large degrees.
This does not assert that every residue class of the pole fails the test.
