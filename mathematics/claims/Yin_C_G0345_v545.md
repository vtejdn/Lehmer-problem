# Primitive cubic-slope sections with long Frobenius cycles at every prescribed prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous cycle bounds on the nontorsion roots

Let \(s\ge3\) be an integer congruent to zero or one modulo three, and let \(Y\ge2\) and \(D\ge1\) be integers.
There are arbitrarily large even integers \(a\) for which \(b=as^3+1\) is coprime to \(a\) and the polynomial
\[
 F=g_s(X^a)+X^b B_s(X^a),\qquad g_s(T)=T^s-T-1,\qquad B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s,
\]
has just one cyclotomic factor, the simple factor \(X+1\).
Set \(H=-F/(X+1)\), a monic polynomial of degree \(a(s^3+s)\).
At each prime \(p\le Y\) the whole section has squarefree reduction, the splitting field of \(H\) is unramified, and every Frobenius cycle on the roots of \(H\) has length greater than \(D\).
Every nonconstant rational factor of \(H\) consequently has degree greater than \(D\).
One may take
\[
                  \log a\ll s^2+Y D^2\log Y
\]
with an absolute constant.
In particular \(Y=D=s\) is possible for arbitrarily large seed degrees.
This excludes factors of bounded degree on these progressions, while the possible [critical factor](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) has degree of order \(as^2\).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## A bound for the degree of a cyclotomic factor

Over the complex numbers the polynomials \(g_s,B_s\) have no common root.
Indeed any common root \(t\) is nonzero and satisfies \(t^s=t+1\) and \(t^{s-1}=-t\), so \(t^2+t+1=0\) and \(t^s=-t^2\).
The former identity gives \(t^3=1\); cubing the latter yields \(1=-1\).
The same proof works in every odd characteristic, including three.
In characteristic two, \(t^2+t+1=0\) gives an element of exact order three, and \(t^s=t^2\) would require \(s\equiv2\pmod3\).

The complex Laurent polynomial \(g_s(T)+ZB_s(T)\) is irreducible by coprimality and linearity in \(Z\).
Its Newton polygon is the rectangle \([0,s]\times[0,1]\), so it has no torsion-binomial component.
The [Beukers–Smyth area bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) gives at most \(22s\) points on this curve with both coordinates roots of unity.
Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), Bézout's identity makes \(x\mapsto(x^a,x^b)\) injective on \(\mathbb C^\times\).
Each cyclotomic factor of \(F\) therefore has degree at most \(22s\).

<a id="construction"></a>
## The exponent and the reductions

Choose \(a\) as the least common multiple of all primes \(p\le Y\), all integers \(p^j-1\) for such primes and \(1\le j\le D\), and the additional integers \(2^j-1\) for \(1\le j\le22s\).
Any positive multiple of this choice works as well.
The sum of the logarithms of the first two lists is \(O(YD^2\log Y)\), by summing \(j\log p\) over \(j\) and using at most \(Y\) primes.
The additional list costs \(O(s^2)\).
The selected \(a\) is even, \(b\) is odd, and \(g_s(1)=B_s(1)=-1\), giving \(F(-1)=0\).

For each selected prime \(p\) the derivative modulo \(p\) equals \(bX^{b-1}B_s(X^a)\), since \(p\mid a\) and \(b\equiv1\pmod p\).
The constant term of \(F\) is \(-1\), and a nonzero common root of \(F,F'\) would give a common root of \(g_s,B_s\), excluded above.
Thus \(F\bmod p\) is squarefree and the rational factor \(X+1\) is simple.
For every \(1\le j\le D\) and every \(x\in\mathbb F_{p^j}^{\times}\), the divisibility \(p^j-1\mid a\) gives \(x^a=1\) and \(x^b=x\), so \(F(x)=-1-x\).
Squarefreeness then excludes every root of \(H\) over these fields.
The same argument at two applies through degree \(22s\), by the additional list.
A monic cyclotomic factor of \(H\) would retain its positive degree at two and have an irreducible constituent of degree at most \(22s\), contrary to this exclusion.

For each \(p\le Y\) choose a finite residue extension splitting \(H\bmod p\).
All its distinct roots lift uniquely to the corresponding unramified local extension by Hensel's lemma.
The local splitting field is therefore unramified, and its Frobenius permutation on the lifts has the same cycle lengths as the residue Frobenius, namely the irreducible factor degrees of \(H\bmod p\).
All these lengths exceed \(D\), and the roots of every nonconstant rational factor form a nonempty union of cycles.
