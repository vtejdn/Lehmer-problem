# Long integral coefficient prefixes on movable interlacing grids

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous feasibility at every sufficiently large degree

Let \(q\ge2,\ k\ge1\) be integers, \(M=q(q+1)\), \(K=4M+1=(2q+1)^2\), \(h=1/M\), and \(n=N+1\) with \(N\ge2Mk^2\).
There is a monic \(H\in\mathbb Q[x]\) of degree \(n\) with \(M^nH(h)=K\), one simple root in \((0,h)\), and \(N\) distinct roots in \((-4,0)\).
Its first \(k\) coefficients below the leading term are integers \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\) satisfying
\[
 1+\sum_{j=1}^k c_jM^j\equiv K\pmod{M^{k+1}}.
\]
There are rational interlacing nodes \(2\cos\theta_i-2\), \(0=\theta_0<\cdots<\theta_N=\pi\), with
\[
 \max_{0\le i\le N}|\theta_i-i\pi/N|<(Mk+1)/N.
\]
On each such constructed grid the [pole-weighted reconstruction](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/trace_grid.md#pole-weights) passes through \(c_k\).
The pole congruence also allows the same prefix to be completed to a monic integral polynomial with that exact pole value if no root constraints are imposed.
Thus checking up to \(\lfloor\sqrt{(n-1)/(2M)}\rfloor\) leading coefficients can succeed on freely chosen grids at every degree with \(n-1\ge2M\).
The integrality of the full polynomial with the indicated roots remains open on this branch.

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Choosing compatible integral coefficients

Put \(\phi_j(x)=2T_j((x+2)/2)\) for \(j\ge1\), where \(T_j(\cos t)=\cos(jt)\).
These polynomials are monic and integral by the three-term recurrence, starting with \(\phi_0=2,\ \phi_1=x+2\).
For the roots \(x_i\) of a monic polynomial, the Newton identities show that \(\sum_i\phi_j(x_i)\) is a known integer minus \(jc_j\) once \(n,c_1,\ldots,c_{j-1}\) are fixed and integral.
Take reference roots \(h\) and \(x_i^0=2\cos\alpha_i-2\), where \(\alpha_i=(i-\tfrac12)\pi/N\), \(1\le i\le N\).
Write \(S_j^0=\phi_j(h)+\sum_i 2\cos(j\alpha_i)\).
Given the earlier choices, the pole congruence selects one class of \(c_j\bmod M\), starting from \(K\equiv1\bmod M\).
The resulting possible values \(T_j\) of the \(j\)-th sum have spacing \(jM\).
Choose a representative giving \(|T_j-S_j^0|\le jM/2\) and repeat through \(k\).
A polynomial with these \(k\) sums has exactly this prefix by the same triangular Newton identities.
An integral completion at the pole alone is obtained by taking \(c_{k+1}=(K-1-\sum_{j=1}^k c_jM^j)/M^{k+1}\) and all later coefficients zero.

<a id="deformation"></a>
## A deformation with prescribed cosine sums

For \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\) put \(f_a(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^k a_\ell\sin(\ell t)\) and \(g_a(t)=t+f_a(t)/N\).
On the cube \(\|a\|_\infty\le M\), the two endpoints are fixed, \(g_a'\ge1-Mk(k+1)/(2N)\ge1/2\), and \(|g_a(t)-t|\le Mk/N\).
Define \(C_j(a)=2\sum_i\cos(jg_a(\alpha_i))\).
For \(1\le j,\ell\le k<N\) the midpoint sine vectors satisfy \(\sum_i\sin(j\alpha_i)\sin(\ell\alpha_i)=N\delta_{j\ell}/2\).
Indeed the cosine sum at the midpoints is zero at every integer frequency from one through \(2N-1\), by geometric summation and taking real parts.
The linear part of \(C_j(a)-C_j(0)\) is therefore \(-ja_j\).
Write
\[
 C_j(a)=C_j(0)-ja_j+jE_j(a).
\]
For any vector \(b\), the directional derivative satisfies
\[
 |dE_j(a)[b]|
 \le \frac{2j}{N^2}\sum_i|f_a(\alpha_i) f_b(\alpha_i)|
 \le \frac{j}{N}\|a\|_2\|b\|_2.
\]
The first inequality follows from the unit Lipschitz bound for sine after subtracting the derivative at zero.
The second uses Cauchy–Schwarz and the same sine orthogonality.
Thus \(E=(E_1,\ldots,E_k)\) is Lipschitz in the maximum norm with constant \(k^2 M/N\le1/2\) on the cube.
Integration of the derivative along the segment from zero gives \(\|E(a)\|_\infty\le k^2 M^2/(2N)\le M/4\).

For \(h(1-4^{-k})\le w\le h\) the equations \(C_j(a)+\phi_j(w)=T_j\) are equivalent to
\[
 a_j=\frac{S_j^0-T_j+\phi_j(w)-\phi_j(h)}{j}+E_j(a).
\]
Throughout \(0\le x\le h\), \(|\phi'_j(x)|\le j^2 2^{j-1}\).
Indeed write \(x+2=z+z^{-1}\) with \(1\le z\le2\); the derivative is \(j\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}z^{j-1-2\ell}\), also at \(z=1\) by continuity.
Consequently \(|\phi_j(w)-\phi_j(h)|/j\le h k2^{k-1}4^{-k}\le h\).
The right side of the fixed-point equations maps the cube into itself since \(M/2+h+M/4<M\).
It contracts distances by at least a factor of two.
Iteration produces a unique solution \(a(w)\) in the cube, continuous in \(w\) by the uniform contraction bound.

<a id="pole"></a>
## Tuning the last root and taking rational nodes

Set \(x_i(w)=2\cos(g_{a(w)}(\alpha_i))-2\).
The \(N\) roots are distinct and strictly between \(-4\) and \(0\).
The continuous homogeneous pole value is
\[
 V(w)=(1-Mw)\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-Mx_i(w)).
\]
It vanishes at \(w=h\).
At the left endpoint \(1-Mw=4^{-k}\).
At least \(\lfloor N/4\rfloor\) midpoints have \(\alpha_i\ge3\pi/4\), and their deformed angles stay above \(\pi/2\) since \(Mk/N\le1/2\).
Their factors are at least \(1+2M\), and all other factors exceed one.
Now \(\lfloor N/4\rfloor\ge k+2\), \(1+2M>4\) and \((1+2M)^2>4M+1\), so \(V(w)>K\) there.
An intermediate \(w\) gives the required exact pole value with the chosen prefix.

The \(N+1\) grid angles \(g_{a(w)}(i\pi/N)\) separate the negative roots and have both endpoints fixed.
Choose rational trace nodes between the same roots, moving the corresponding angles by less than \(1/N\), and keep the two endpoints.
All alternating signs are strict at these nodes.
With the first \(k\) coefficients fixed, the prescribed pole value is a rational affine constraint on the remaining coefficients.
Approximate all remaining coefficients except the constant term by rationals and determine that term from the constraint.
These rational polynomials approximate the constructed real polynomial and retain its finitely many strict signs for sufficiently good approximations.
The positive value at \(h\) gives one final root in \((0,h)\), exhausting all \(n\) roots.
The residue-weight bounds in the fixed-grid reconstruction use real-root interlacing and the pole value, so its first \(k\) congruence steps retain precisely the constructed integers.
