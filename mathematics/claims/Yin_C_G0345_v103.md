# A dyadic Poisson estimate for factors of a central cyclotomic perturbation

<a id="statement"></a>
## Transfer when the complementary degree is small relative to \(d/\log^2 d\)

Let \(A\) be a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials of even degree \(d\ge2\), with arbitrary factors and multiplicities, and take \(a\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).
Let \(G=A+aX^{d/2}=FQ\) with \(F,Q\) monic integer polynomials, allowing constant factors.
Write \(m=\log M\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, \(v=m(Q)\), \(Y=1+m(G)\), \(\Delta=\log(2d)\), and let \(f\) be the degree left in \(Q\) after removal of all cyclotomic factors with their multiplicities.
With absolute implied constants the loss \(m(G)-m(F)=v\ge0\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v&\ll Y\frac{f\Delta}{d}
       +\sqrt{\frac{Yf(v+\Delta)\Delta}{d}},\\
 v&\ll Y\frac{f\Delta}{d}+\Delta\sqrt{\frac{Yf}{d}} .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]

For \(d\to\infty\), \(f=o(d/\Delta^2)\) implies \(v=o(Y)\).
At the full scale \(f\le Kd/\Delta^2\) for fixed \(K\), \(m(G)\to\infty\) still implies \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\), and bounded \(m(F)\) forces bounded \(m(G)\).
If \(m(F)\) stays bounded and \(f=o(d(\log\Delta)^6/\Delta^8)\), then \(Q\) is eventually a product of cyclotomic polynomials, including the empty product, and \(m(F)=m(G)\).
In particular this exact equality holds for \(f\le Kd/\Delta^8\) and any fixed \(K\).
All upper bounds on \(f\) may instead be imposed on \(\deg Q\).

Any class with a uniform positive gap \(\gamma\) for \(m(G)>0\) has a uniform gap for positive \(m(F)\) under \(f\le b(d)=o(d/\Delta^2)\).
This includes \(f\le Kd/\Delta^{2+\delta}\) for fixed \(K,\delta>0\).
The transfer also holds under \(f\le c_\gamma d/\Delta^2\) for sufficiently small \(c_\gamma>0\) depending only on \(\gamma\).
For the [class with binomial exponents on boundedly many multiplicative intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement), bounded \(m(F)\) at \(f\le Kd/\Delta^2\) implies eventual exact equality for every fixed \(K\), and the whole-polynomial gap transfers at that scale.
The estimate for arbitrary \(A\) needs no restriction on its number of cyclotomic factors.
Its gap-transfer conclusions use a whole-polynomial gap for the specified class.

<a id="arcs"></a>
## Root counts from a large Poisson integral on arcs

The coordinator supplied the dyadic arc estimate and its application to the reflected discriminant energy.
Let \(Q_0\) be the noncyclotomic part of \(Q\).
The case \(f=0\) has \(v=0\), so assume \(f>0\).
The [central derivative calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v102.md#simple) makes \(Q_0\) squarefree, and its monic constant-term-unit normalization gives \(\sum_{i=1}^f t_i=2v\), where its roots are \(\alpha_i=|\alpha_i|e^{i\theta_i}\) and \(t_i=|\log|\alpha_i||\).
Put \(w_i=e^{-t_i+i\theta_i}\).
With \(\eta=d^{-3}\), the [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v102.md#energy) gives
\[
       \mathcal E=\sum_{l\ge1}\frac{e^{-\eta l}}l
                       \left|\sum_{i=1}^f w_i^l\right|^2
                      \ll f(v+\Delta).
 \tag{2}
\]
Write \(d\nu=d\theta/(2\pi)\) on the circle, \(L=\log|A|\) there, and \(d\mu=L^-d\nu\).
The [centered root inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v102.md#poisson), using \(w_i=1/\overline{\alpha_i}\) for exterior roots so their angles are preserved, gives
\[
 \mu(\mathbb T)+\log|a|\ll Y,\qquad
 \tfrac12 dt_i\le \log|a|+(P_{e^{-t_i}}*\mu)(\theta_i)\quad(t_i>0).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(P_r(x)=(1-r^2)/(1-2r\cos x+r^2)\) has circle integral one.

Take the dyadic numbers \(t=2^{-j}\), \(j\ge1\), for which \(t\ge C_0 Y/d\), with a sufficiently large absolute \(C_0\ge1\).
These satisfy \(t\ge\eta\).
Let \(N_t=\#\{i:t\le t_i<2t\}\).
For each root in this band (3) gives \((P_{e^{-t_i}}*\mu)(\theta_i)\ge dt/4\).
If \(|u|_{\mathbb T}\le t\), the kernel comparison
\[
          P_{e^{-t}}(x+u)\ \gg\ P_{e^{-t_i}}(x)
\]
holds uniformly in \(x\).
Indeed \(P_{e^{-s}}(x)=\sinh(s)/(\cosh(s)-\cos x)\asymp s/(s^2+|x|_{\mathbb T}^2)\) for \(0<s\le3\), with \(|x|_{\mathbb T}\) denoting distance in radians to \(2\pi\mathbb Z\).
Use \(t\le t_i<2t\) and \(|x+u|_{\mathbb T}\le |x|_{\mathbb T}+t\) in this comparison.
Consequently \(P_{e^{-t}}*\mu\gg dt\) throughout the union \(U_t\) of the arcs \(|x-\theta_i|_{\mathbb T}\le t\) for these roots.
The integral of \(P_{e^{-t}}*\mu\) is \(\mu(\mathbb T)\), so positivity gives \(\nu(U_t)\ll Y/(dt)\).

Define a smoothed count of all reflected roots by
\[
       H_t(x)=\sum_{i=1}^fP_{e^{-(t_i+t)}}(x-\theta_i),\qquad
       B_t=t\|H_t-f\|_2^2 ,
\]
with the norm in \(\nu\).
Every summand is nonnegative and \(\int H_t\,d\nu=f\).
For a root in the band its summand is \(\gg1/t\) on its own arc of radius \(t\), by \(2t\le t_i+t<3t\) and the same kernel formula.
Each counted root therefore contributes at least an absolute constant to \(\int_{U_t}H_t\,d\nu\), even when the arcs overlap.
The bound on \(\nu(U_t)\) and Cauchy–Schwarz now give
\[
      tN_t\ll t\left(f\nu(U_t)+\sqrt{\nu(U_t)}\|H_t-f\|_2\right)
          \ll \frac{fY}{d}+\sqrt{\frac{Y B_t}{d}} .
 \tag{4}
\]

<a id="dyadic-energy"></a>
## Summation across the radial scales

The nonconstant Fourier coefficients of \(H_t\) at frequencies \(\pm l\) have modulus \(e^{-tl}|\sum_i w_i^l|\) for \(l>0\).
By Parseval,
\[
       \sum_t B_t
       =2\sum_{l\ge1} e^{-\eta l}
             \left|\sum_i w_i^l\right|^2
                \sum_t t e^{-(2t-\eta)l}
       \ll \mathcal E .
\]
Since \(t\ge\eta\), the inner sum is at most \(\sum_t t e^{-tl}\le 2/l\), by comparison with the integrals of \(2e^{-xl}\) on the disjoint intervals \([t/2,t]\).
There are \(O(\Delta)\) selected scales.
The indices with \(t_i<1\) outside the selected bands have \(t_i\le 2C_0Y/d\), so they contribute \(O(fY/d)\) to \(\sum_i t_i\).
For \(t_i\ge1\), the kernel at radius \(e^{-t_i}\) is bounded by \((1+e^{-1})/(1-e^{-1})\), so (3) bounds the total contribution of these roots by \(O(fY/d)\) as well.
The bands contribute at most \(2\sum_t tN_t\).
Summing (4) and using \(\sum_t\sqrt{B_t}\le \sqrt{O(\Delta)\sum_t B_t}\) gives
\[
      v\ll Y\frac{f\Delta}{d}
           +\sqrt{\frac{Y\Delta}{d}\,\mathcal E}.
\]
Apply (2), split the square root of \(v+\Delta\), and absorb the \(\sqrt v\) term using \(C\sqrt{vY f\Delta/d}\le v/2+C^2Y f\Delta/(2d)\).
This proves both upper bounds for \(v\) in (1).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Transfer of a whole-polynomial gap

Let \(x=f/d\).
The explicit bound gives \(v/Y\ll x\Delta+\Delta\sqrt{x/Y}\).
For \(x=o(\Delta^{-2})\), this tends to zero since \(Y\ge1\).
For \(x\le K/\Delta^2\) it is \(O(K/\Delta+\sqrt{K/Y})\), which tends to zero if \(Y\to\infty\).
As \(m(F)=Y-1-v\), bounded \(m(F)\) at this full scale excludes every subsequence with unbounded \(Y\).
In particular \(m(F)\le B\) implies \(Y=O_{B,K}(1)\) uniformly for all sufficiently large \(d\).

Suppose the whole polynomials in a class satisfy \(m(G)=0\) or \(m(G)\ge\gamma>0\).
If \(0<m(F)\to0\), then \(m(G)>0\) throughout.
Bounded \(d\) is impossible since integer factors of bounded degree and bounded measure have bounded coefficients and take only finitely many values.
Pass to \(d\to\infty\).
For \(f\le b(d)=o(d/\Delta^2)\), the bound on \(Y\) and \(v=o(Y)\) give \(m(G)=m(F)+v\to0\), a contradiction.
For \(x\le c_\gamma/\Delta^2\), first take \(c_\gamma\le1\) to obtain bounded \(Y\).
Now (1) gives \(\limsup v/\sqrt Y\le C\sqrt{c_\gamma}\) with absolute \(C\).
But \(\liminf v/\sqrt Y\ge\gamma/\sqrt{1+\gamma}\) since \(m(F)\to0\) and \(s/\sqrt{1+s}\) increases on \(s\ge0\).
Choosing \(C\sqrt{c_\gamma}<\gamma/\sqrt{1+\gamma}\) proves the second gap assertion.

<a id="exact"></a>
## When the cofactor must be cyclotomic

For bounded \(m(F)\) and \(x=O(\Delta^{-2})\), bounded \(Y\) gives \(v\ll_{B,K}\Delta\sqrt x\).
If \(Q_0\ne1\), a noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor \(J\) of \(Q\) has degree \(2\le g\le d\), since every monic linear integer factor with unit constant term is cyclotomic.
We have \(v\ge m(J)>0\).
The [Dobrowolski lower bound in Smy08, Section 4.2](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(m(J)\gg(\log\log g/\log g)^3\) for \(g\) sufficiently large, in the [total logarithmic measure normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization).
The function \(\log y/y\) decreases for \(y>e\), so for such \(g\le d\) this gives \(m(J)\gg(\log\Delta/\Delta)^3\) at large \(d\).
For the finitely many smaller degrees, coefficient finiteness and Kronecker give a uniform positive lower bound for \(m(J)\) among the possible noncyclotomic irreducible unit factors \(J\) with \(M(J)\le2\).
The other factors have \(m(J)>\log2\).
Thus the same lower bound holds in these degrees for sufficiently large \(d\).
If \(f=o(d(\log\Delta)^6/\Delta^8)\), the upper bound for \(v\) is \(o((\log\Delta/\Delta)^3)\), forcing \(Q_0=1\) eventually.
For \(f\le Kd/\Delta^8\) the upper bound is \(O_{B,K}(\Delta^{-3})\), giving the stated special case.

Finally let the signed exponents in \(A=\pm\prod_j(1-X^j)^{b_j}\) have support in at most \(h\) intervals \([z,Cz]\) with fixed \(h,C\).
For \(f\le Kd/\Delta^2\) and bounded \(m(F)\), bounded \(Y\) bounds both \(\log|a|\) and \(\|L\|_1=2\mu(\mathbb T)\).
The [interval theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement) then bounds \(S=\sum_j|b_j|\).
The [root bound from this signed product](Yin_C_G0345_v101.md#classes) gives \(|\log|\alpha||\le(2/d)(\log|a|+S\log(4d))=O(\Delta/d)\) for every root of \(G\).
If \(J\) as above occurs, the [house bound of Dimitrov](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives a root \(\alpha\) of \(J\) with \(g\log|\alpha|\ge(\log2)/4\).
However \(g\le f\) gives \(g|\log|\alpha||=O_{B,K,h,C}(1/\Delta)\), a contradiction at large \(d\).
Hence \(m(F)=m(G)\) eventually for bounded \(m(F)\), and the whole-polynomial gap of the interval theorem transfers by the same finite-degree argument.
