# No finite list of integer trace perturbations guarantees Salem descent

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sequence of Salem inputs approaching the golden ratio

There is a sequence of Salem numbers \(\sigma_j\) of increasing degrees \(2m_j\) tending to infinity, with \(\sigma_j\to\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) from below.
Write \(S_j\) for the trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma_j+\sigma_j^{-1}\).
For **every fixed nonzero** \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\), the full lift
\[
               z^{m_j}(S_j+R)(z+z^{-1})                               \tag{1}
\]
has no Salem factor for all sufficiently large \(j\).
In particular no finite list of nonzero fixed integer polynomials guarantees descent on bounded Salem inputs of growing degree.

The same sequence classifies perturbations of bounded degree with unrestricted, varying coefficients.
For every fixed integer \(d\ge0\) and real \(B>1\), eventually the Salem numbers \(\tau\le B\) occurring in (1) as \(R\) ranges over **nonzero** integer polynomials of degree at most \(d\) are exactly those with trace degree at most \(d+1\).
Restricting further to \(\tau<\sigma_j\) gives exactly the strict descents in this class.
For each fixed Salem number of trace degree \(t\), the minimum degree of a nonzero perturbation producing it is therefore eventually \(t-1\).
The zero perturbation gives the input itself.
The index thresholds may depend on \(R\), or on \(d,B\), respectively.

Let \(\|R\|_{\rm coeff}\) denote the maximum absolute coefficient.
For a fixed Salem target \(\tau\), the unique perturbation of degree at most \(t-1\) that produces it satisfies \(\|R\|_{\rm coeff}\asymp_\tau\tau^{m_j}\).
Every perturbation of fixed degree bound \(d\) producing \(\tau\) costs \(\|R\|_{\rm coeff}\gg_{d,\tau}\tau^{m_j}\).
In particular every sequence of nonzero perturbations of bounded degree with \(\log\max(1,\|R_j\|_{\rm coeff})=o(m_j)\) eventually gives no Salem factor under any fixed measure cap.
The [degree-ten Salem number of Lehmer](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0164/readme.md#boyd-descent-search) can be reached on this sequence in minimum trace perturbation degree four, at an exponential coefficient cost.

The coordinator supplied the two-stage construction, the golden-ratio seeds, the limiting bound on the circle, the integral polynomial norm obstruction and the exponential coefficient cost.
The contributor computed the exact common cyclotomic cofactor on the chosen seed sequence and obtained a quantitative target-degree bound and the converse for perturbations with varying coefficients.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Pisot seeds and their Salem constructions

Let \(k\equiv2\bmod6,\ k\ge2\), and put
\[
 f(z)=z^2-z-1,\quad H_k(z)=z^k f(z)+1,\qquad
 F_k(z)=H_k(z)/(z-1)=z^{k+1}-\sum_{a=0}^{k-1}z^a .
\]
For \(|z|=1\), one has \(|f(z)|^2=1+|z-z^{-1}|^2\).
For \(0<u<1\), Rouché's theorem gives exactly \(k+1\) roots of \(z^k f(z)+u\) in the open disk, counted with multiplicity.
Consequently \(H_k\), as \(u\uparrow1\), has at most one root outside the closed disk.
The only possible circle roots are \(1,-1\) by the modulus identity.
At \(1\) the root is simple because \(H_k'(1)=1-k\), and \(H_k(-1)=2\).
The sign just above 1 is negative and \(H_k(\varphi)=1\), so its unique exterior root \(\theta_k\) lies in \((1,\varphi)\).
All other roots of \(F_k\) are inside the open disk.
A proper factorization would give a monic integral factor with positive degree, nonzero integer constant term and only strictly interior roots, which is impossible.
Thus \(F_k\) is the minimal polynomial of a nonreciprocal Pisot number, the nonreciprocity following from its degree at least three and unique exterior root.
For each fixed \(1<s<\varphi\), \(H_k(s)\to-\infty\), proving \(\theta_k\to\varphi\).

Write \(A^*(z)=z^{\deg A}A(1/z)\).
For each fixed \(k\), the [Salem construction](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002) gives, at all sufficiently large \(N\), a single Salem minimal polynomial times cyclotomic factors in \(z^N F_k+F_k^*\).
At \(\theta_k\) this polynomial is negative, since \(F_k(s)<0\) on \(0<s<1\).
At each fixed \(s>\theta_k\) it is eventually positive.
Hence its Salem root \(\sigma_{k,N}\) tends to \(\theta_k\) from above, and can be chosen below \(\varphi\).

<a id="cofactor"></a>
## A progression with the same cyclotomic cofactor for every seed

Define
\[
           G_{k,N}=z^N H_k-H_k^*=(z-1)(z^N F_k+F_k^*).
\]
For each \(k\) we choose a progression of even exponents containing \(N=k+2\).
The rational function \(U=-F_k^*/F_k\) has no circle poles and satisfies \(U(0)=1,\ U'(0)=-1\) by the displayed coefficients of \(F_k\).
At any torsion root \(\zeta\) of \(z^N F_k+F_k^*\), \(U(\zeta)=\zeta^N\).
According as its order \(n\) is odd, divisible by four, or congruent to two modulo four, the conjugates \(\zeta^2,-\zeta,-\zeta^2\), respectively, yield
\[
 U(\zeta^2)=U(\zeta)^2,\qquad U(-\zeta)=U(\zeta),\qquad
 U(-\zeta^2)=U(\zeta)^2 .
\]
Indeed the corresponding exponents \(2,\ 1+n/2,\ 2+n/2\) in their respective cases are units modulo \(n\), and \(N\) is even.
None is a rational-function identity, as their first derivatives at zero differ.
Thus only finitely many torsion points occur for a fixed \(k\) over all even \(N\).
Take \(L_k\) divisible by two and all their orders and restrict \(N\equiv k+2\bmod L_k\).
Every torsion zero of \(G_{k,N}\) on this progression already zeros \(G_{k,k+2}\), and conversely, including \(1\) from the first factor.
For sufficiently large \(N\), the torsion zeros of \(z^N F_k+F_k^*\) are simple because the derivatives of \(z^N\) have modulus \(N\) there while \(|U'|\) is bounded on this finite set.
The root 1 occurs just in \(z-1\), because \(2F_k(1)=2(1-k)\ne0\).

At the base exponent \(G_{k,k+2}=z^{2k+2}f-f^*\).
For \(J=f^*/f\) the identical three conjugation tests apply with the even exponent \(2k+2\).
Direct multiplication gives the following numerators when the indicated differences are written over the products of their original denominators, allowing a common sign.

| Difference | Numerator up to sign |
| --- | --- |
| \(J(z^2)-J(z)^2\) | \(2(z-1)^3(z+1)(z^2+z+1)^2\) |
| \(J(-z)-J(z)\) | \(4z(z-1)(z+1)\) |
| \(J(-z^2)-J(z)^2\) | \(2(z-1)(z+1)^3(z^2-z+1)^2\) |

This restricts the possible orders to \(1,2,3,6\).
Since \(6\mid 2k+2\), the base equation at orders 3 and 6 would give \(f=f^*\), whereas \(f-f^*=2(z^2-1)\) there is nonzero.
Both \(1,-1\) are zeros.
Thus for each \(k\), at all sufficiently large exponents on its progression,
\[
      G_{k,N}=(z^2-1)P_{k,N},\qquad
      P_{k,N}=z^m S_{k,N}(z+z^{-1}),\qquad m=(N+k)/2,                \tag{2}
\]
where \(P_{k,N}\) is the full Salem minimal polynomial of \(\sigma_{k,N}\).

<a id="envelope"></a>
## An integral norm excludes every fixed nonzero perturbation

Fix \(R\ne0\) and take \(m>\deg R\).
If \(z\) is a neutral conjugate of a Salem factor of \(P_{k,N}+z^mR(z+z^{-1})\), then (2) implies on the unit circle
\[
              |(z^2-1)R(z+z^{-1})|\le 2|H_k(z)|.                    \tag{3}
\]
For the whole shifted polynomial, multiplication by \(z^2-1\), of Mahler measure one, bounds its measure by its resulting circle supremum, hence by \(2(1+\sqrt5)+2\|R\|_{[-2,2]}\).
All its Salem factors therefore have a measure bound depending only on \(R\).

There exists a point \(z_0\) on the open upper semicircle with
\[
            |(z_0^2-1)R(z_0+z_0^{-1})|>2(|f(z_0)|-1).
\]
Otherwise conjugation and continuity give the opposite weak inequality on the whole circle.
Writing \(s=|z^2-1|=|z-z^{-1}|\), its right side is \(2s^2/(\sqrt{1+s^2}+1)\).
Thus \(|R(z+z^{-1})|\le 2s/(\sqrt{1+s^2}+1)\) away from the endpoints, forcing \(R(2)=R(-2)=0\).
With \(e=\deg R\), the nonzero integer polynomial \(z^eR(z+z^{-1})\) is divisible in \(\mathbb Z[z]\) by \(z^2-1\).
The quotient has modulus at most \(2/(\sqrt{1+s^2}+1)<1\) off the endpoints.
Its squared circle integral with normalized measure is strictly less than one, contradicting Parseval's identity and its nonzero integer coefficient vector.

For each large \(k\), take a point \(z_k\) at angular distance at most \(\pi/k\) from \(z_0\) where \(z_k^k f(z_0)=-|f(z_0)|\).
Then \(|H_k(z_k)|\to|f(z_0)|-1\).
The strict margin at \(z_0\) consequently makes (3) fail at \(z_k\) by a fixed positive amount.
The angular derivative of \(H_k\) is \(O(k)\) on the circle and that of \((z^2-1)R(z+z^{-1})\) is bounded in terms of \(R\).
Hence (3) fails throughout an upper semicircle arc centered at \(z_k\) of half-width \(a_R/k\) for some fixed \(a_R>0\).

<a id="finite-targets"></a>
## The degree bound before choosing the delay

The [short-arc estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) puts a conjugate argument of any monic irreducible unit polynomial of degree \(D\) and bounded log measure \(M_0\) in every arc of half-width \(h\le1\) when \(h\ge C\sqrt{(1+M_0)\log(2D)/D}\).
A Salem conjugate with argument in the open upper semicircle is neutral.
Thus the forbidden arc and uniform measure bound imply that every possible Salem factor for fixed \(R\) has degree \(D=O_R(k^2\log(2k))\), for all sufficiently large \(k\) and all \(N\) covered by (2) with \(m>\deg R\).
Indeed \(D/\log(2D)\) is increasing for \(D\ge2\), and a sufficiently large constant times \(k^2\log(2k)\) meets the short-arc threshold.
At fixed \(k,R\) the bounds on degree and measure give finitely many possible Salem roots \(\tau\), by the elementary coefficient bounds for monic integer polynomials.

For any fixed such \(\tau>1\), put \(T=\tau+\tau^{-1}\), \(u=\tau^m\).
Vanishing at \(\tau\) in (1), for this seed and delay, implies
\[
       \tau^{-k}H_k(\tau)u^2+(\tau^2-1)R(T)u-H_k^*(\tau)=0 .
\]
Its constant term is nonzero since \(H_k(\tau^{-1})=(\tau^{-1}-1)F_k(\tau^{-1})>0\).
At most two delays work for the pair \(\tau,R\).
Consequently for any finite list of nonzero integer polynomials, all sufficiently large \(k\equiv2\bmod6\) have the following property.
For all sufficiently large delays on that seed's progression, none of the listed perturbations yields any Salem factor.
The delay threshold may depend on \(k\) and the list.

<a id="varying"></a>
## Varying coefficients and prescription of a bounded-degree target

Fix \(d,B\) and restrict to Salem targets \(\tau\le B\) of trace degree at least \(d+2\).
If such a target is produced by a polynomial \(R\) of degree at most \(d\), its \(d+1\) or more distinct neutral traces provide interpolation points for \(R\).
At all neutral points \(z\) away from \(1,-1\), (3) gives
\[
               |R(z+z^{-1})|\le \frac{2(1+\sqrt5)}{|z^2-1|}.
\]
At sufficiently large target degree, use \(d+1\) fixed small arcs in the open upper semicircle, with pairwise separated trace images and positive distance from the endpoints.
The short-arc estimate at \(\log M\le\log B\) gives a neutral conjugate in each arc.
At the remaining bounded target degrees there are finitely many possible targets, each with at least \(d+1\) distinct interior trace nodes, again giving a common positive lower bound on their separation when \(d\ge1\) and their distances from the endpoints.
The \(d+1\) values of \(R\) and the coefficients of the Lagrange numerators are bounded, and all denominator factors stay bounded away from zero.
Interpolation therefore bounds every coefficient of \(R\) in terms of \(d,B\) alone.
This includes \(d=0\), for which a single value suffices.
The resulting finite coefficient list, after exclusion of zero, falls under the preceding simultaneous exclusion.

Conversely, let \(D_\tau\) be the trace minimal polynomial of a target of trace degree \(t\le d+1\).
At an input trace \(S\) of degree \(m>d+1\), choose \(R\) to be the negative remainder of \(S\) modulo \(D_\tau\).
Monic division gives \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(\deg R<t\le d+1\).
It is nonzero by irreducibility of \(S\) and the strict degree inequality.
Then \(D_\tau\mid S+R\), and the lifted polynomial has the Salem minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) as a factor.

Finally enumerate the nonzero integer polynomials.
At stage \(j\ge2\) take the first \(j\) polynomials together with the finite coefficient lists just obtained for every \(0\le d\le j\) at \(B=j\).
Choose a sufficiently large \(k\equiv2\bmod6\), increasing with \(j\), and then a sufficiently large \(N\) on its progression so the complete list produces no Salem factor, \(m>j+1\) exceeds all listed polynomial degrees and the previous input degree, and \(\theta_k<\sigma_{k,N}<\varphi\).
This gives both assertions for every fixed \(R\) and \(d,B\), since the exclusions for cap \(j\) include the desired targets for \(j\ge B\).
For a fixed target of trace degree \(t\ge2\), degree \(t-1\) suffices by division and degree at most \(t-2\) is eventually excluded.

<a id="coefficient-cost"></a>
## The price of prescribing a Salem target

For a fixed target \(\tau\) write \(y=\tau+\tau^{-1}\), and let \(D_\tau\) be its trace minimal polynomial.
Along the constructed sequence \(k,N\to\infty\), equation (2) gives
\[
     \frac{S_j(y)}{\tau^{m_j}}
       =\frac{f(\tau)+\tau^{-k}-\tau^{-(N+k)} f^*(\tau)-\tau^{2-N}}
                    {\tau^2-1}
       \ \longrightarrow\ \frac{f(\tau)}{\tau^2-1}\ne0 .
\]
Here \(f(\tau)\ne0\) since its only root greater than one is the quadratic Pisot number \(\varphi\).
At each of the other trace conjugates \(y'=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) of \(y\), the fixed unit conjugate \(\zeta\) of \(\tau\) differs from \(1,-1\).
The original lift therefore gives
\[
             |S_j(y')|\le 2(1+\sqrt5)/|\zeta^2-1|
\]
uniformly in \(j\).
Every polynomial perturbation \(R\) producing this target satisfies \(R(y)=-S_j(y)\).
For \(\deg R\le d\) the upper bound \(|R(y)|\le\|R\|_{\rm coeff}\sum_{i=0}^d y^i\) proves the required exponential lower bound.
For the unique \(R_j\) of degree less than \(t=\deg D_\tau\), one has \(R_j(y')=-S_j(y')\) at every trace conjugate, since \(R_j\) is the negative remainder.
Lagrange interpolation at the fixed \(t\) nodes gives the coefficientwise limit
\[
      \tau^{-m_j}R_j(X)\ \longrightarrow\
         -\frac{f(\tau)}{\tau^2-1}\,
          \frac{D_\tau(X)}{(X-y)D_\tau'(y)} .
\]
Its degree is \(t-1\), proving the matching upper bound and eventual exact degree.
For fixed \(d,B\), the preceding classification leaves only finitely many possible targets, since their trace degrees and measures are bounded.
If subexponential coefficients of degree at most \(d\) produced any of these infinitely often, some fixed \(\tau>1\) would recur, contradicting the exponential lower bound.
