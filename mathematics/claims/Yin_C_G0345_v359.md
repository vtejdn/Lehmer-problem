# How much reciprocal measure a two-class polynomial can contain

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite inequality for the whole reciprocal part

Let \(p\ge2,\ r\ge1,\ (p,r)=1\), and let
\[
                 F(x)=U(x^p)+x^r V(x^p)
\]
have nonzero integral rows coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
We allow Laurent exponents and ignore scalar content and monomials when taking polynomial gcds.
Define \(G\) as the primitive common gcd of \(F(x)\) and \(F(1/x)\) in the rational Laurent ring, with its lowest exponent normalized to zero and its leading coefficient positive.
It contains all reciprocal factors with their full multiplicity and both members of each inverse pair up to their shared multiplicity.
Write \(m=\log M\).
Whenever the two rows have unequal modulus at some point of the unit circle, one has
\[
                              p\,m(G)\ \le\ 2m(F).                       \tag{1}
\]
More precisely, (1) always holds if
\[
              H(X)=U(X)U(1/X)-V(X)V(1/X)\ne0.                         \tag{2}
\]
If \(H=0\), the coprime rows have \(V(X)=\pm X^a U(1/X)\) for an integer \(a\), which makes \(F\) reciprocal up to sign and monomial.
In particular every nonreciprocal \(F\) with coprime rows satisfies (1), including at composite moduli and with unbounded degree, coefficients or root multiplicities.
The converse implication from reciprocity to \(H=0\) holds when \(p>2\), because reversal must then interchange the two residues.

<a id="grid"></a>
## The entire product over one fiber

Let \(\mu\) denote normalized Haar measure on the circle.
For \(X\) on the unit circle,
\[
       \left|\prod_{x^p=X}F(x)\right|
          =\big|U(X)^p-(-1)^p X^r V(X)^p\big|
          \ \ge\ \big||U(X)|^p-|V(X)|^p\big|.
\]
Here the \(x^r\) take all \(p\) distinct phases in the fiber since \((p,r)=1\).
For \(a\ge b\ge0\), superadditivity of \(t\mapsto t^{p/2}\) on nonnegative reals gives \(a^p-b^p\ge(a^2-b^2)^{p/2}\).
Since the rows are real, \(|H(X)|=\big||U(X)|^2-|V(X)|^2\big|\) on the circle.
For \(H\ne0\), taking logarithms off the finite zero sets and integrating yields
\[
                                m(H)\le2m(F).                            \tag{3}
\]
The logarithms are integrable and the integral of the sum over the fiber is \(p\,m(F)\).
This comparison has no error term from the length of either row.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Transporting reciprocal roots with multiplicity

In the Laurent polynomial ring,
\[
  H(x^p)=U(1/x^p)\,F(x)-x^r V(x^p)\,F(1/x).
\]
Thus \(G\) divides \(H(x^p)\).
For any \(\beta\ne0\) the full fiber \(x^p=\beta\) contains at most one root of \(F\): vanishing of both \(U(\beta),V(\beta)\) is excluded by row coprimality, and \(x^r\) is distinct at all \(p\) points.
Consequently distinct roots of \(G\) have distinct \(p\)-th powers.
Substitution is unramified at nonzero roots, so if \(z\) is a root of \(G\) of multiplicity \(e\), then \(z^p\) is a root of \(H\) of multiplicity at least \(e\).

For \(G(x)=c\prod_j(x-z_j)\) with multiplicities, form \(G_{[p]}(X)=c^p\prod_j(X-z_j^p)\).
This polynomial is integral and primitive.
Indeed, up to sign \(G_{[p]}(x^p)=\prod_{\zeta^p=1}G(\zeta x)\), by multiplication over the roots.
The coefficients of \(G_{[p]}\) are integral by the resultant formula.
At a place above any rational prime, each \(G(\zeta x)\) has nonzero reduction, since \(\zeta\) is a unit and \(G\) is primitive.
Their product also has nonzero reduction.
This includes primes dividing \(p\).
We have \(m(G_{[p]})=p\,m(G)\).
The multiplicity argument gives \(G_{[p]}\mid H\) over the rational Laurent ring, and the quotient is integral by primitivity and Gauss's lemma.
An integral nonzero Laurent polynomial has nonnegative log measure, proving \(p\,m(G)\le m(H)\) and hence (1).

If \(H=0\), writing \(\iota U(X)=U(1/X)\) gives \(U\iota U=V\iota V\).
Coprimality implies \(U\mid\iota V\) and \(V\mid\iota U\).
On applying \(\iota\) these give equality up to Laurent units, so \(V=c_0 X^a\iota U\) for \(c_0\in\mathbb Q^\times\).
The identity forces \(c_0^2=1\).
This proves the asserted symmetry.
Conversely for reciprocal \(F\) at \(p>2\), the involution on exponents preserving \(\{0,r\}\bmod p\) must exchange its members: fixing both would give \(p\mid2r\).
Comparing the exchanged rows then gives the same identity.

<a id="cofactor"></a>
## A uniform lower bound along changing inputs

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial dividing \(F\).
Normalize \(F\) to be primitive and write \(R\) for the product of the noncyclotomic factors of \(F/P\) other than \(x\), including repeated factors.
In the nonreciprocal case,
\[
                     m(R)\ \ge\ (p/2-1)m(P).                            \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(P\mid G\) and \(m(F)=m(P)+m(R)\).
This bound holds simultaneously for all such \(P,p,F\), regardless of whether \(p\) lies in the fixed-input asymptotic range of the [optimal cofactor rate](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement).
It controls the whole common reciprocal part before any restriction on its number of factors.
For a sequence with \(p>2\), any strict violation of (4) by primitive coprime rows therefore forces the exact identity \(V=\pm X^a\iota U\).
The remaining case includes reciprocal inputs multiplied only by cyclotomic factors, for which the two-row phase estimates apply at their [own degree and offset scope](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two).
