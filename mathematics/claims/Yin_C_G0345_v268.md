# Primitive actions on conjugate labels at bounded Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Natural alternating actions and classical subspace actions

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number with logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m(\alpha)\le M\).
Let \(d\) be the absolute degree of the product of the conjugates in its root-of-unity quotient class.
Use the [multiplicative labels on the compressed conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels).
For \(d\) sufficiently large depending only on \(M\), the group \(H\) induced on **every minimal block** of \(b\ge2\) labels satisfies one of the following conditions.

1. \(H=A_b\) or \(S_b\) in its natural permutation action on the \(b\) individual labels.
2. \(H\) is almost simple with classical socle in a subspace action as defined by Liebeck and Shalev in §1.1.
   These actions include single-subspace stabilizers, certain graph-automorphism stabilizers of two subspaces and the orthogonal stabilizers in even-characteristic symplectic groups.

The lower bound on \(d\) is uniform over the algebraic number, the blocks and the permutation groups.
Thus the numerical logarithmic measures of all inputs admitting a minimal block outside both cases form a finite set in every bounded interval.
In particular an abstract alternating section of large degree does not suffice to describe the remaining actions.
For an alternating socle of unbounded natural degree only the action on its individual letters remains, by the classification in the source and the bounded exceptional isomorphisms between alternating and classical simple groups.
Equally, along a sequence with \(0<m(\alpha)\to0\) the two alternatives eventually hold on every minimal block because \(d\to\infty\).

<a id="graphs"></a>
## Sparse invariant graphs

The [ordinary pair](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#ordinary) bounds below the size of every remaining orbit of its pointwise stabilizer in \(H\) by \(c_M d/(\log(3d))^K\) at bounded measure, for absolute \(K\).
Consequently for any \(H\)-invariant simple undirected graph on a block with valency \(v\ge2\), one has
\[
                               v\ge c_M d/(\log(3d))^K .                   \tag{1}
\]
Indeed the first point of the ordinary pair has a neighbor different from both points of the pair, and the pointwise stabilizer preserves its neighborhood.
For a matching of valency one, the neighboring pairs would themselves be blocks of size two, excluded for large \(d\) by the [two-coordinate block-size estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block).
Thus a nonempty graph of valency \(O(b^{1-\epsilon}(\log(2b))^C)\), with fixed \(\epsilon>0,C\), is impossible for sufficiently large \(d\).
The proof applies to every nontrivial block, including the whole label set.

<a id="normal"></a>
## Minimal normal subgroups of a primitive action

On a minimal block \(H\) acts transitively and primitively.
Its [solvable radical is trivial](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#statement).
Every nontrivial normal subgroup of \(H\) is transitive by primitivity, and a minimal normal subgroup has the form \(N=T_1\times\cdots\times T_k\) with the \(T_i\) mutually isomorphic nonabelian finite simple groups.
For the latter assertion choose a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of \(N\) and its conjugates under \(H\).
Distinct conjugates centralize one another since they are minimal normal in \(N\) with trivial pairwise intersections.
Their product is all of \(N\).
If one were abelian the product would be abelian.
Otherwise each is centerless because its center is characteristic and it is minimal normal in \(N\).
Each then intersects the product of the others trivially.
Any normal subgroup in one of these direct factors is normal in \(N\), proving simplicity.
The simple factors are permuted transitively by \(H\).
Since \(N\) preserves each factor by conjugation and \(H=NH_x\) for a point \(x\), the point stabilizer \(H_x\) is transitive on the factors too.

Put \(L=N_x\) and \(P_i=\operatorname{pr}_i L\).
First suppose that the \(P_i\) are proper, which holds simultaneously by this transitivity.
Their direct product \(P\) is normalized by \(H_x\), and \(PH_x\) cannot be \(H\), since its intersection with \(N\) equals \(P\).
Primitivity makes \(H_x\) maximal, hence \(L=P\).
Thus the coset space \(N/L\) is the full Cartesian product of its \(k\) factor coset spaces of a common size \(s\ge2\).
The action of \(N\) is coordinatewise and \(H_x\) permutes the coset coordinates, acting within them by the isomorphisms of factors carrying \(P_i\) to the corresponding \(P_j\).
For \(k\ge2\) this is an [excluded Cartesian action](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#pairs).

Next suppose all projections of \(L\) are surjective.
A subgroup projecting onto each of a direct product of \(k\) nonabelian simple groups is a product of diagonal copies of the simple group, each across a disjoint nonempty set of coordinates, allowing coordinate isomorphisms.
Induct on \(k\), projecting away the last factor.
The kernel in that last factor is normal in it by surjectivity.
A full kernel appends an independent coordinate.
For a trivial kernel the subgroup is the graph of a surjection from the product of the earlier diagonal copies to the last factor.
The images of the earlier factors are commuting normal subgroups in the simple target, so precisely one is the whole target and maps isomorphically.
This joins the last coordinate to one earlier copy.

No diagonal set in \(L\) can be a singleton, since that would give \(T_i\le L\) and transitivity of \(H_x\) on factors would imply \(L=N\).
Writing \(t=|T_1|\), we have \(b=[N:L]\ge t^{k/2}\).
For \(k\ge3\), join two distinct points when an element of a single \(T_i\) takes one to the other.
This gives an \(H\)-invariant undirected graph of valency at most \(k(t-1)\), positive by transitivity of \(N\).
Since \(k\le2\log_2 b\), the valency is \(O(b^{2/3}\log(2b))\), contradicting (1) or the matching exclusion.
For \(k=2\) the subgroup is one diagonal and \(b=t\).
The centralizer in the symmetric group of any transitive group has at most \(b\) elements, since a commuting permutation is determined by the image of one point.
Conjugation by \(H\) on \(N\) then gives
\[
                                |H|\le b\cdot2|\operatorname{Aut}(T_1)|^2
                                      \le 2b^5 .
\]
Here a finite nonabelian simple group has a generating pair, so an automorphism is determined by two images. [Guralnick and Kantor, Theorem I and its corollary](../audits/literature/GK00/Yin_U001.md#gk00-u001)
This contradicts the [block order estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#order), which for \(m\le M\) gives \(\log|H|/\log b\to\infty\) uniformly as \(d\to\infty\).

It remains that \(k=1\) and \(N=T_1\) is simple.
If \(N\) is regular then \(b=|N|\) and as above \(|H|\le b|\operatorname{Aut}(N)|\le b^3\).
Otherwise \(C_H(N)\) is trivial, since a nontrivial centralizer would be normal and transitive and \(N\), commuting with it, would act freely.
Thus conjugation embeds \(H\) between \(N=\operatorname{Inn}(N)\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}(N)\), yielding an almost simple primitive group.

<a id="standard"></a>
## The bounded-base theorem and equal-sized partitions

The [almost-simple base theorem of Liebeck and Shalev](../audits/literature/LS99/Yin_U001.md#almost-simple) gives an absolute bound \(c\) on the size of a base outside the alternating subset and partition actions and the [specified classical subspace actions](../audits/literature/LS99/Yin_U001.md#subspace-action).
An ordered base of at most \(c\) points gives \(|H|\le b^c\), impossible by the block order estimate.
Among subset actions the [ordinary-pair exchange argument](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#pairs) already excludes all \(j\)-subsets with \(2\le j\le n/2\) on an \(n\)-element ground set.
Complementing all subsets identifies the actions for \(j\) and \(n-j\).
A singleton or its complement gives the natural action.

Consider partitions of \(n=as\) letters into \(a\ge2\) parts of size \(s\ge2\).
Join two distinct partitions if they differ by a single transposition of two letters.
The graph is invariant and nonempty, of valency at most \(n(n-1)/2\).
Fix one partition and pair up its parts as far as possible.
Between each pair match up the \(s\) letters of one part with those of the other and transpose any subset of those matched pairs.
Complementary choices give the same unordered pair of parts and all other choices give distinct partitions.
Varying choices in the disjoint pairs gives
\[
                              b\ge 2^{(s-1)\lfloor a/2\rfloor}
                                \ge 2^{n/6}.
\]
Thus the valency is \(O((\log(2b))^2)\), impossible by (1) and the matching case.
Parts of size one or the partition with only one part give a one-point action, excluded by \(b\ge2\).
This leaves exactly the alternatives in the statement as necessary conditions.
When such a condition fails, the degree \(d\) is bounded and the coefficient bound \(2^d e^M\) for the compressed primitive polynomial gives finiteness of its measure values.
