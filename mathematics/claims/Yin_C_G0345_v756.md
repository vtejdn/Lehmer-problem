# Products of Frobenius lifts with the sampled primes revealed

<a id="statement"></a>
## Probabilities on all conjugate embeddings

Let \(\alpha\) be any nonzero algebraic number of infinite order, of degree \(d\), absolute logarithmic height \(h\), and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=dh\).
Let \(\Omega\) be its set of conjugates, \(G\) the Galois group of their splitting field, and \(V\) any transitive quotient of the \(G\)-set \(\Omega\), of size \(s\).
These quotients include the embeddings of each subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) by restricting the original embeddings.
Put
\[
 g(d)=\left(\frac{\log\log(5d)}{\log(2d)}\right)^3,\qquad
 H=1+\frac{m}{g(d)},\qquad
 T=\left\lceil A\frac{\log(2d)}{\log\log(5d)}\right\rceil,\qquad
 X=T^2\log(2T),
\]
where \(A\) is a sufficiently large absolute constant.
Draw rational primes independently in \([X,2X]\) with probabilities proportional to \(\log p\).
At each draw \(p\), independently choose a uniform prime of the splitting field above \(p\) and a uniform lift of residue Frobenius in its decomposition group.
Given an entire list \(\mathbf p=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\), let \(M_{\mathbf p}(x,y)\) be the probability that the ordered product of those independent lifts sends \(x\) to \(y\) in \(V\).

For \(n=2\lceil C\log(2d)\rceil\) with a sufficiently large absolute \(C\),
\[
             \mathbb E_{\mathbf p}\ \max_{x,y\in V}M_{\mathbf p}(x,y)
                             \ \ll\ \min(1,H/s).                       \tag{1}
\]
The maximum is taken after all primes have been revealed, and the same window and sample length work for all the subfields.
One may replace \(H\) throughout by \(m/g(d)\), since the full orbit in the same determinant gives \(m\gg g(d)\).
The group, its permutation rank and ramification are unrestricted.
The full-lift statement permits denominators in \(\alpha\).
If \(\alpha\) is integral and \(m\le1\), (1) also holds if at every \(p\) one fixes an arbitrary Frobenius lift at one prime above it and uses an independent uniformly random conjugate of this chosen element at each occurrence.
One may use either law separately at each \(p\) in this integral case.

For each prime \(p\) let \(S_p\) be the support in \(G\) of the law used there.
On \(V\) join \(x\) to \(gh^{-1}x\) for \(g,h\in S_p\) at the **same** \(p\), allowing every \(p\) in the window.
Let \(c\) be the number of connected components and \(\Delta\) the diameter within one component, counting an isolated vertex as diameter zero.
All components are isomorphic and
\[
                    c\left(1+\frac{\Delta}{\log(2d)}\right)\ll H .      \tag{2}
\]
In particular, on any sequence of quotients with \(s/H\to\infty\), the expected maximal transition probability after revealing the primes tends to zero and the number of reachable embeddings at logarithmic length grows.
The [realized trace fields below](#examples) give two extremes for the same symmetric permutation group.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## A bound uniform in independently chosen source sets

Work first on the full \(\Omega\).
For each \(p\) write \(K_p(x,y)=\mathbb P(gx=y)\), with \(g\) drawn from its indicated law.
These matrices are doubly stochastic because each lift permutes \(\Omega\), and invariant under simultaneous \(G\)-translation of row and column because each law is conjugation invariant.
For any nonempty \(D\subseteq\Omega\) of size \(r\), choose for every \(p\) an \(r\)-element set \(B_p\) on which \(K_p1_D\) takes its \(r\) largest values, breaking ties arbitrarily.
Then \(\langle1_{B_p},K_p1_D\rangle=\mathbb E|D\cap gB_p|\), using unnormalized counting inner products.

The number of primes for which two distinct elements of \(\Omega\) have the same \(p\)-th power is at most \(\log d/\log2\).
Indeed at each such prime every element has at least two conjugates, itself included, in its multiplicative coset of the \(p\)-th roots of unity by transitivity.
Starting at one element, successively double the set of chosen conjugates for distinct such primes by taking two representatives in each current element's coset.
After the earlier primes all ratios lie in the roots of unity of order dividing their product, so two previous distinct elements cannot be in the same coset of the next prime.
Thus \(j\) bad primes require at least \(2^j\) distinct conjugates.
The argument works in the original field regardless of torsion quotients of conjugates.

Omit those primes in the [subset-overlap determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#statement), with target \(D\), chosen source sets \(B_p\), power \(p\), and multiplicity \(T\).
For the moment use full lifts, including at ramified primes.
If \(t\) is the total number of primes in the window and \(W=\sum_{X\le p\le2X}\log p\), then \(t\asymp T^2,\ W\asymp X\) by the [prime interval bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice).
Dividing the determinant inequality by \(rTW\) bounds the weighted mean of \(\mathbb E|D\cap gB_p|/r\) on the retained primes with the full denominator \(W\) by
\[
 \frac{(T^2+t)\log(r(T+t))}{2TW}
       +\frac{(T+t)(T+2Xt)}{TW}\,r h
 \ \ll\ \frac{\log(2d)+\log(2T)}{T\log(2T)}+T^3 r h .               \tag{3}
\]
Restoring the omitted primes costs \(O(\log(2d)\log(2X)/X)\) since their overlaps are at most \(r\).
The first term on the right in (3) can be made arbitrarily small, uniformly for all sufficiently large \(d\), by choosing \(A\) sufficiently large.
The omitted cost tends to zero as \(d\to\infty\).
For the single-lift option, apply this same bound for full lifts using the sets chosen from the desired matrices.
The [lift comparison and different bound](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#interval) cost in normalized weighted mean at most \((\log d+2m)/W\), again tending to zero at \(m\le1\).
These computations use no relation between the different \(B_p\).
In particular taking \(D=B_p=\Omega\) makes the full-window mean overlap one, so (3) and the restored-prime cost give \(T^3m\gg1\) for large \(d\).
For bounded \(d\) the positive total measures are bounded below, since the coefficients of primitive minimal polynomials of bounded degree and measure are bounded by the usual elementary symmetric estimates.
This proves the asserted comparison \(H\asymp m/g(d)\).

Consequently there is an absolute \(c_0>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(d\), every \(1\le r\le R=\lfloor c_0 d/H\rfloor\) satisfies
\[
     \mathbb E_p\ \langle1_{B_p},K_p1_D\rangle\le r/8 .
\]
Indeed \(T^3\ll_A1/g(d)\), so the last term in (3) is arbitrarily small for sufficiently small \(c_0\).
At a fixed prime the entries of \(K_p1_D\) belong to \([0,1]\) and have sum \(r\).
If the largest \(r\) of them sum to \(r b_p\), each remaining entry is at most \(b_p\).
Their total squared sum, including the first \(r\), is therefore at most \(2r b_p\).
We have proved for every such \(D\)
\[
                       \mathbb E_p\|K_p1_D\|_2^2\le r/4.              \tag{4}
\]

<a id="products"></a>
## From escape of small sets to a conditional probability

Let \(Q=\mathbb E_p K_p^{\mathsf T}K_p\), a symmetric stochastic matrix.
Equation (4) gives \(\sum_{x\in D,y\notin D}Q(x,y)\ge3|D|/4\) for every \(|D|\le R\).
Write \(\mathcal E(f)=\langle f,(I-Q)f\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{x,y}Q(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^2\).
For a nonnegative \(b\) supported on at most \(R\) points, integrating the escape inequality over the level sets of \(b^2\) yields
\[
 \sum_{x,y}Q(x,y)|b(x)^2-b(y)^2|\ge (3/2)\|b\|_2^2 .
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the left side by \((2\mathcal E(b))^{1/2}(4\|b\|_2^2)^{1/2}\) since \(Q\) is doubly stochastic.
Thus \(\mathcal E(b)\ge(9/32)\|b\|_2^2\).
For a probability column \(f\) and \(R\ge1\), take \(b=(f-1/R)_+\).
It has support at most \(R\), satisfies \(\|f-b\|_2^2\le1/R\), and truncation contracts every pairwise difference, so \(\mathcal E(f)\ge\mathcal E(b)\).
The inequality \(\|b\|_2^2\ge\|f\|_2^2/2-1/R\) now gives
\[
 \mathbb E_p\|K_p f\|_2^2\le (1-9/64)\|f\|_2^2+9/(32R).
\]
Each new matrix is drawn independently of the current column and keeps it a probability column.
Iteration from a point mass through \(k=\lceil C\log(2d)\rceil\) matrices gives expected squared norm at most \(3/R\) for large enough \(C\), since \(R\le d\).

For a fixed prime list, the transition matrix of its lift product is the product of the corresponding matrices.
It commutes with the transitive permutation action.
Its rows therefore all have a common squared norm, and so do its columns.
The two common norms coincide by summing all its squared entries.
In particular the estimate just proved for one column estimates the common row and column norm of a product from half the sampled primes.
The order of the independent sampling may be reversed in the column iteration.
Split the full product into two halves.
For each revealed list, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds every entry of the product by the common row norm for the first half times the common column norm for the second.
Taking expectations gives at most \(3/R\), proving (1) on \(\Omega\) when \(c_0d/H\ge2\).
If this fails, \(H/d\) is bounded below and the assertion follows from probability at most one.
For the finitely many smaller \(d\) the same conclusion follows by enlarging the absolute constant since \(H\ge1\).
Passing to \(V\) sums the transition probabilities into each fiber of size \(d/s\), with any one original starting point above \(x\).
This gives (1) there with the same prime list and constants.

<a id="diameter"></a>
## Growth caused by different lifts at the same prime

The edge set in (2) is undirected and \(G\)-invariant because the set of differences \(\bigcup_p S_pS_p^{-1}\) is symmetric and conjugation invariant.
Fix all sampled primes and any one possible product of lifts.
The quotient of any other possible product by this one is a product of at most \(n\) elements of the edge set of differences in \(G\).
Indeed replace successive factors by the other choices one at a time; each change is conjugate to a same-prime difference and hence is again in that set.
Therefore the support of each conditional transition from a fixed point lies in a ball of radius \(n\) centered at one possible image.
Transitivity makes the size \(b_n\) of such a ball the same at every center.
The maximal conditional entry is at least \(1/b_n\), and (1) yields \(b_n\gg s/H\).
Inside a component of diameter \(\Delta\), centers spaced \(2n+1\) edges apart along a shortest path realizing the diameter have disjoint radius-\(n\) balls.
There are at least \((\Delta+1)/(2n+1)\) such centers and at least one.
The \(c\) components have the same size, so \(s\ge c\, b_n\max(1,(\Delta+1)/(2n+1))\).
This proves (2).

<a id="examples"></a>
## Two possible behaviors in primitive symmetric trace fields

For every sufficiently large \(s\), take the prime window above with \(d=2s\).
There exist primitive totally real fields of degree \(s\) with full normal closure group \(S_s\) for which all these primes split completely.
There also exist such fields for which every one is unramified with an \(s\)-cycle as Frobenius.
For the latter construction prescribe a separable irreducible degree-\(s\) reduction for a monic polynomial at each test prime.
For complete splitting prescribe coefficients locally sufficiently close to those of \(\prod_{j=1}^s(Z-j)\), whose distinct roots persist over the local field even if some residues agree.
At three additional distinct primes larger than \(s\) prescribe separable reductions with cycle types an \(s\)-cycle, a single \((s-1)\)-cycle and a transposition.
At infinity require \(s\) distinct real roots.
The same [simultaneous approximation and local root construction](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#product) over \(\mathbb Q\) meets these finitely many open conditions.
The group is transitive by the \(s\)-cycle, then doubly transitive by the \((s-1)\)-cycle fixing one point, and contains all transpositions by conjugation.
The root field has no proper nontrivial subfield since the point action of \(S_s\) is primitive.
Each field occurs as the trace field of a Salem number by the [totally real lattice construction](Yin_C_G0345_v741.md#salem).
For every such Salem generator, the full lift upstairs projects to the uniquely specified Frobenius at the restricted prime of the unramified trace closure.
The distribution on the trace embeddings is consequently the uniform conjugacy-class action in that closure.

On the completely split family, every revealed prime list acts identically on the trace embeddings, so (1) requires \(m\gg s g(2s)\) for large \(s\).
In the \(s\)-cycle family each single-prime matrix on the trace embeddings has diagonal zero and all other entries \(1/(s-1)\).
For a list of any length \(n\) its \(x,y\) entry is
\[
 \frac1s+\left(-\frac1{s-1}\right)^n
                 \left(1_{x=y}-\frac1s\right)
\]
by separating the constant vectors and their orthogonal complement.
At logarithmic length its largest entry is asymptotic to \(1/s\), the least possible order for any stochastic matrix on \(s\) points.
Thus the group alone does not prescribe the arithmetic dispersion of these products, and in the second family the transition estimate on the trace quotient using \(H\asymp m/g(d)\) tests only the scale \(m\gg g(2s)\).
Neither construction supplies a sequence of small Salem generators.
