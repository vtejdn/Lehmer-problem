# Phase descent and inversion on torsion-translated plane sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## The stable power field and a reciprocal-factor obstruction

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with support differences of rank two.
Let \(F(x,y)=0\), where
\[
 x=\xi z^a=\xi\beta^p,\qquad y=\eta z^b=\eta\beta^q,\qquad
 g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)>0,\quad \beta=z^g,\quad (p,q)=(a/g,b/g).
\]
Here \(\xi,\eta\) are arbitrary roots of unity and \(z\ne0\) is nontorsion.
Put \(n=\max(|p|,|q|)\), \(H=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), \(D=[H(\beta):H]\) and \(K_*=\bigcap_{j\ge1}\mathbb Q(\beta^j)\).
There are constants \(B=B_F\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\) and \(A_F\ge1\) such that the following statements hold whenever \(D>A_F\), in particular for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in both translations and \(g\).

Both \(x^B,y^B\) belong to \(K_*\).
For every \(j\ge1\), \(\mathbb Q(x^{Bj})=K_*\) if \(p\ne0\), and \(\mathbb Q(y^{Bj})=K_*\) if \(q\ne0\).
For any \(i,l\in\mathbb Z\) with \(ip+lq=1\), the number \(\theta=x^iy^l\) satisfies
\[
        \mathbb Q(\theta^{Bj})=K_*,\qquad h(\theta^B)=B h(\beta).
\]
The assigned phases obey
\[
 \xi^B,\eta^B\in\mathbb Q(\beta),\qquad
             (\xi^q\eta^{-p})^B\in K_* .
\]
In particular the orders of both phases divide \(B|\mu(\mathbb Q(\beta))|\).
They divide \(2B\) whenever \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) has a real embedding, including when \(\mathbb Q(z)\) has one.
Over the abelian base all powers satisfy
\[
 H(\beta^j)=H(\beta^{\gcd(j,B)}),\qquad
 [H(\beta):H(\beta^j)]\le\gcd(j,B).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
For \(n\to\infty\) the normal closure \(N_*/H\) of \(HK_*/H\) satisfies
\[
             \log [N_*:H]\gg_F\log n\,(\log\log n)^{1/3}.      \tag{2}
\]
The normal closure of \(H(z)/H\) contains \(N_*\).

If an automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) sends \(\beta\) to \(\beta^{-1}\), there must be geometric components \(C_i,C_j\) of \(F=0\) with
\[
   (\sigma(\xi)\xi,\ \sigma(\eta)\eta)\, C_i^{-1}=C_j,\qquad
   \sigma(\xi^B)=\xi^{-B},\quad \sigma(\eta^B)=\eta^{-B}.       \tag{3}
\]
Here \(C^{-1}\) denotes simultaneous coordinate inversion and multiplication by a pair denotes coordinatewise translation.
This applies in particular if \(z^{-1}\) is conjugate to \(z\).
If no pair of geometric components is related by a torsion translate of the inverse, every sufficiently long primitive direction excludes all reciprocal nontorsion roots in every translated section and dilation.
In that case there is \(c_F>0\) such that \(m(z)\ge c_F\) over **all** such sections, including the bounded primitive directions.
For absolutely irreducible \(F\) one checks only its own curve against its inverse.

The [untwisted component comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#twists) and its [rational graph specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v763.md#statement) stabilize powers of an untwisted generator.
The [torsion-field degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#phases) bounds the degree needed to adjoin the two assigned phases for arbitrary translations.
The assertions here place specific powers of the phases and powered coordinates in the ordinary fields and impose inversion on every reciprocal translated section.
The [earlier reciprocal-component criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) concerns untwisted sections.

<a id="components"></a>
## A finite list of signed component identifications

Use the notation \(P=(x,y)\) and \(P^\epsilon=(x^\epsilon,y^\epsilon)\) for \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Choose a finite Galois number field \(E\) over which all geometric components \(C_i\) are defined.
They are conjugate, and each has support differences of rank two.
Indeed a component with support on a line is a torus translate of a one-dimensional subtorus, and the conjugates of its binomial equation have the same exponent difference, contradicting the rank of \(F\).
Fix \(d\) bounding the total degrees of all \(C_i\) and \(C_i^{-1}\) after normalizing their Laurent equations to ordinary polynomials divisible by neither coordinate.
Set \(A_F=[E:\mathbb Q]d^2\).

Suppose \(P\in C_i\) and \(tP^\epsilon\in C_j\) with \(t\in\mu_\infty^2\).
If \(tC_i^\epsilon\ne C_j\), their torus intersection has at most \(d^2\) points by Bézout.
Both curves are defined over \(EH\), so the orbit over \(EH\) of \(tP^\epsilon\) has degree at most \(d^2\).
Coordinate inversion and the translations belong to the rational operations over \(H\), and \(\beta=(x/\xi)^i(y/\eta)^l\) for \(ip+lq=1\).
Thus \(EH(tP^\epsilon)=EH(\beta)\), forcing \(D\le A_F\).
For \(D>A_F\) every such pair must satisfy the component identity \(tC_i^\epsilon=C_j\).
The [uniform section height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(h(\beta)\ll_F 1/n\) as \(n\to\infty\), because \(h(\beta)=g h(z)\) and the projected width is comparable to \(gn\).
By the [relative height bound over \(H\)](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) bounded \(D\) would give a positive lower bound for \(h(\beta)\).
This proves the uniform large-direction threshold independently of the orders of \(t,\xi,\eta\).

For fixed \(i,j,\epsilon\), all translations carrying \(C_i^\epsilon\) to \(C_j\), if any, form one coset of the diagonal translation stabilizer of \(C_j\).
In a normalized defining equation, translating scales each coefficient by the monomial of its exponent.
For a stabilizer, the polynomial after translation has the same support and therefore differs only by a constant, since a nonzero shift cannot preserve a finite nonempty support.
Consequently all support differences evaluate to one on that stabilizer.
Two linearly independent differences already cut out a finite subgroup, by taking the adjugate of their exponent matrix.
There are finitely many torsion pairs among all the signed component identifications.
Take \(B\) to be the least common multiple of the orders of their coordinates, including the identity.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Descent from torsion-equivalent conjugates

Let \(M\) be a common multiple of all orders of quotients of conjugates of \(\beta\) by \(\beta\) that are roots of unity.
The group fixing \(\beta^M\) consists exactly of the automorphisms taking \(\beta\) into its class modulo roots of unity.
Every automorphism fixing \(\beta^j\) fixes \(\beta^M\), so \(K_*=\mathbb Q(\beta^M)\).
Furthermore, if \(v^k=\omega\beta^b\) with \(k\ge1,\ b\ne0\) and \(\omega\) torsion, an automorphism fixing \(v\) also preserves the class of \(\beta\).
It follows that \(K_*\subseteq\mathbb Q(v)\).
This is the [minimal field of the class](../audits/literature/FM13/Yin_U002.md#rational-line-degree).

Consider any \(\sigma\) fixing \(\beta^M\), and write \(\sigma\beta=\lambda\beta\) with \(\lambda\) torsion.
If \(P\in C_i\), then \(\sigma P\in\sigma C_i\) and
\[
   \sigma P=\big((\sigma\xi/\xi)\lambda^p,\,
                  (\sigma\eta/\eta)\lambda^q\big)P .
\]
For \(D>A_F\) both coordinates of this translating pair have order dividing \(B\).
Hence \(x^B,y^B\) are fixed by the whole stabilizer of \(\beta^M\) and belong to \(K_*\).
Now \(x^{Bj}\) represents the class of \(\beta^{pBj}\), which for \(p\ne0\) gives the reverse field containment just proved.
The same argument applies to \(y^{Bj}\) and to \(\theta^{Bj}\), because \(\theta=\xi^i\eta^l\beta\).
Dividing \(x^B,y^B\) by the respective powers of \(\beta\) gives \(\xi^B,\eta^B\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
Eliminating \(\beta\) gives \((\xi^q\eta^{-p})^B=(x^B)^q/(y^B)^p\in K_*\).
All roots of unity in a field with a real embedding are signs, giving the stated order bound.

For an automorphism fixing \(H(\beta^j)\) both phases are fixed, and \(\lambda=\sigma\beta/\beta\) has \(\lambda^j=1\).
The component comparison gives \(\lambda^{pB}=\lambda^{qB}=1\), hence \(\lambda^B=1\) by coprimality.
Equality of the two stabilizers gives (1), with the degree bound following from the power equation.
For (2) take \(\alpha=\theta^B\).
All of its ordinary positive powers generate \(K_*\), so \(H(\alpha^{e(\alpha)})=H(\alpha)\) for the actual exponent of the torsion subgroup of the multiplicative group generated by its conjugates, as in [Amoroso and David's Proposition 2.4](../audits/literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field).
Its height is \(B h(\beta)\ll_F1/n\), and \(H(\alpha)=HK_*\).
Writing \(d_*=[N_*:H]\), their bound gives \(\log n-O_F(1)\le C\log(16d_*)/(\log\log(16d_*))^{1/3}\).
First \(\log(16d_*)\gg_F\log n\), and inserting this into the iterated logarithm proves (2).
Finally \(K_*\subseteq\mathbb Q(\beta)\subseteq\mathbb Q(z)\), giving the normal-closure inclusion.

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Reciprocal conjugates and the global gap

If \(\sigma\beta=\beta^{-1}\), then \(\sigma P=((\sigma\xi)\xi,(\sigma\eta)\eta) P^{-1}\).
The signed component comparison gives (3), with \(C_j=\sigma C_i\).
Since \(\xi^B,\eta^B\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), this also describes the action of the reciprocal automorphism of the ordinary root field on these phase powers, independently of any extension of that automorphism.
If \(\sigma z=z^{-1}\), taking \(g\)-th powers gives the required hypothesis on \(\beta\).

When the inverse identifications are all absent, \(D>A_F\) rules out reciprocal \(z\).
For those roots, a nonunit has \(m(z)\ge\log2\) by the leading or constant coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomial, while a nonreciprocal unit has the positive absolute [Smyth lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
The directions with \(D\le A_F\) have bounded \(n\), hence have a uniform positive lower bound by the [all-translation, all-dilation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement).
This proves the all-section gap.

<a id="example"></a>
## A centrally symmetric square with incompatible opposite coefficients

For an absolutely irreducible \(F\), a torsion translate carrying its inverse curve to itself requires central symmetry of the support.
More precisely, equality of the Laurent equations up to a monomial and constant requires the coefficients at \(c\) and \(c_0-c\) to have equal absolute values for every support exponent \(c\).
Indeed the translating factors have modulus one and the common scalar has modulus one by multiplying the coefficient moduli over the finite support permutation.
For the positive sign the stabilizer is given by the support differences, and can likewise be read from the coefficients.

Take \(F=1+X+Y+2XY\).
It is absolutely irreducible because the linear polynomial \((1+2X)Y+(1+X)\) is primitive in \(Y\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[X]\).
Its square support is centrally symmetric, but the coefficients at \(1\) and \(XY\) have moduli one and two.
Thus no torsion translate realizes inversion, while the differences \((1,0),(0,1)\) make its ordinary translation stabilizer trivial.
One may take \(B=1\), and the all-section positive gap holds.

For every positive \(n\) there is \(0<t<1\) satisfying \(1+t-(1+2t)t^n=0\) by opposite signs at zero and one.
This gives the actual nontorsion point \((t,-t^n)\) in direction \((1,n)\).
For fixed sufficiently large \(n\), the preceding conclusions give \(K_*=\mathbb Q(t)\) for this point.
Represent the same point instead with \(\beta=\omega t,\ \xi=\omega^{-1},\ \eta=-\omega^{-n}\), for an arbitrary root of unity \(\omega\), and \(g=1\).
The first coordinate still generates the same stable field by \(B=1\), while the chosen first phase has unbounded order.
For any fixed \(j\) the power \(\beta^j\) lies outside this stable field whenever \(\omega^j\notin\mathbb Q(t)\), which occurs for arbitrarily large orders.
Thus a uniform power of the unmodified generator cannot replace the adjusted representative in general.
