# Dilation counts from the Mahler measure of an evaluation determinant

<a id="statement"></a>
## Zeros of a fixed Laurent polynomial on powers of a unit

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), and set \(m=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Let \(G(X)\) have exactly \(k\ge1\) nonzero terms with distinct integer exponents and arbitrary fixed complex coefficients.
For every positive integer \(Q\) satisfying \(\log M(\alpha^Q)>(k/2)\log k\), the number of integers \(q\) with \(G(\alpha^q)=0\) is at most \((k-1)Q\).
In particular let \(d_*=\min_{n\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^n):\mathbb Q]\).
One can take
\[
 Q=1+\left\lfloor\frac{d}{d_*}\frac{k\log k}{2m}\right\rfloor,
\]
since \(\log M(\alpha^Q)=Qm[\mathbb Q(\alpha^Q):\mathbb Q]/d\) by the degree-height identity.
At a [strict degree record](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm) one has \(d_*=d\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Interpolation in a residue class

Suppose \(k\) hits lie in the same class \(q_j=s+Qt_j\), with \(t_1<\cdots<t_k\).
Order the exponents \(v_1<\cdots<v_k\) of \(G\) and form the integral Laurent polynomial
\[
 D(X)=\det(X^{t_jv_i})_{1\le j,i\le k}.
\]
The ordered diagonal is the unique permutation with largest exponent sum, by the strict rearrangement inequality, so \(D\ne0\).
The coefficient vector multiplied coordinatewise by \(\alpha^{sv_i}\) is a nonzero common kernel vector at \(X=\alpha^Q\), so \(D(\alpha^Q)=0\).
On the complex unit circle Hadamard's determinant inequality gives \(|D(X)|\le k^{k/2}\).
Multiplication by an integral power of \(X\) clears negative exponents without changing the circle modulus or the nonzero root.
The minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^Q\) divides this integral polynomial with a nonzero integral quotient, whose Mahler measure is at least one.
Consequently \(\log M(\alpha^Q)\le(k/2)\log k\), contrary to the choice of \(Q\).
Each residue class contains at most \(k-1\) hits, including when the exponents or the hit times take both signs.

<a id="affine"></a>
## Rational values along affine lines on reciprocal inputs

Suppose \(\alpha^{-1}\) is a conjugate of \(\alpha\).
Fix rational \(a_i\), integer offsets \(b_i\) and integer slopes \(v_i\), allowing repeated slopes, and consider \(F_q=\sum_i a_i\alpha^{b_i+qv_i}\).
Every rational value \(F_q\) gives a zero at \(X=\alpha^q\) of the Laurent polynomial
\[
 G(X)=\sum_i a_i\bigl(\alpha^{b_i}X^{v_i}-\alpha^{-b_i}X^{-v_i}\bigr),
\]
since the embedding taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\) fixes that rational value.
If \(G\ne0\), the preceding bound applies with its actual number \(k\) of surviving terms after collection, independent of the offsets and coefficients.
In particular for ordinary dilations with zero offsets it bounds every asymmetric marked coefficient pattern at a reciprocal strict record, with \(k\le2(r-1)\) at arity \(r\).
For distinct nonzero slopes and nonzero coefficients at such a record of degree greater than one, \(G=0\) requires the slopes to pair under negation, with equal coefficients and opposite offsets in every pair.
Indeed a paired equality gives \(\alpha^{b_i+b_j}=a_j/a_i\), and no nonzero power of \(\alpha\) is rational.
These pair conditions also suffice.
For growing arities on a shrinking-measure sequence of reciprocal strict records the logarithm of this dilation allowance is \(O(\log r+\log(1/m))=o((r-2)\log(1/m))\).
Thus if at least half the guaranteed [terminal rational lists](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement) at the selected arities are asymmetric, their distinct primitive marked shapes still number at least \(\exp((1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m))\).
For even \(r\ge4\) all terminal lists are asymmetric because their exponents are [distinct](Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#rays) and the \(r-1\) unmarked nonzero positions have odd cardinality.

<a id="bounded"></a>
## Varying bounded integral outputs

For any unit as in the statement, fix \(s\ge1\) distinct nonzero integer slopes and fixed nonzero complex coefficients \(a_i\).
Write \(k=s+1\).
The number of integer times at which \(\sum_i a_i\alpha^{qv_i}\) is a nonzero rational integer of absolute value at most \(C\ge1\) is at most \((k-1)Q\) whenever \(\log M(\alpha^Q)> (k/2)\log k+\log C\).
Indeed append a column of the actual integer outputs \(c_j\) to the evaluation matrix for \(k\) hits in one residue class.
At \(X=\alpha^Q\) the resulting determinant vanishes by the prescribed coefficients and the residue scaling.
Its largest exponent term is unique by the same rearrangement argument with the additional slope zero, and its coefficient is a signed nonzero \(c_j\).
On the circle the appended column has norm at most \(C\sqrt k\), and the others have norm \(\sqrt k\), giving the asserted measure threshold.
This includes symmetric patterns and arbitrary fixed outgoing coefficient heights.
If every coefficient and the output are integers of height at most \(Y\), each positive hit satisfies \(q[\mathbb Q(\alpha^q):\mathbb Q]m/d\le \log k+Y\), by applying the circle norm bound to the polynomial for that hit.
At a strict degree record this gives \(q\le(\log k+Y)/m\) and is a stronger allowance for positive times than the determinant count with the same output bound \(C=e^Y\). [Individual height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#polynomial)
Symmetric patterns with unrestricted rational output remain outside the inversion argument, including the [quadratic trace example](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v308.md#patterns).
