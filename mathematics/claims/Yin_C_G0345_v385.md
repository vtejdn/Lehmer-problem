# Ramified primes required by individual factors of the primitive cubic family

<a id="statement"></a>
## A discriminant test using the exponents

Put \(g(T)=T^3-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\) and \(A(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\) for coprime positive \(a,b\).
Work at sufficiently large \(r=b/a\), where the [exterior partition](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) makes \(A\) squarefree and all its nontorsion rational factors \(P_i\) reciprocal.
For a root \(\alpha_i\) of \(P_i\), write \(E_i=\mathbb Q(\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1})\), \(n_i=\deg(P_i)/2\), and let \(\Delta_i\) be the absolute field discriminant of \(E_i\).
The integer \(k_i\) counts the complex-conjugate pairs of exterior roots of \(P_i\).
Define the positive integer \(D\) by the following resultant with a degree-six polynomial:
\[
 D=|\operatorname{Res}_T(R(T),\,T^b B(T)^a-(-g(T))^a)|,\qquad
 R(T)=aT(g'B-gB')-bgB .
\]
It equals the absolute polynomial discriminant of \(-A\).
All ramified rational primes of the \(E_i\) divide \(D\), and
\[
 \sum_i v_\ell(\Delta_i)\le
 \begin{cases}11&\ell=2,\\6&\ell=3,\\3&\ell\ge5.\end{cases}                 \tag{1}
\]
At most three distinct \(E_i\) are ramified at any one prime, counting fields by their rational factors.
If their rational trace groups are alternating in the natural action, no two of those factors can ramify at the same prime.

With \(S_D(Y)=\sum_{\ell\mid D,\ \ell\le Y}\log\ell\) over distinct rational primes, each factor of half-degree \(n=n_i\) with \(k=k_i\) satisfies
\[
 2\log(n^n/n!)+2k\log(\pi/4)
     \ \le\ 8\log2+3\log3+3S_D((16n)^n).                               \tag{2}
\]
The radical of \(D\) satisfies \(\log\operatorname{rad}(D)\ge(1/3-o(1))b\) as \(r\to\infty\).
For any shrinking sequence of individual factors, \(m(P_i)\to0\), and every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the primes of \(D\) of size at most \(\exp(\varepsilon b\log b)\) must supply log weight \(\gg b(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
The same lower order holds after dropping primes below \(o(b(\log\log b/\log b)^3)\).
These statements give necessary conditions on the individual allocation without any assumption about the factorization of the resultant.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## Computing the discriminant from the two exponents

For every root \(x\) of \(A\), the derivative identity in the [finite-inertia calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#inertia) gives
\[
 xB(x^a)A'(x)=R(x^a).
\]
Both \(x\) and \(B(x^a)\) are algebraic integer units, since \(-A\) is monic with constant one and \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) gives \(B(x^a)(x^{a+b}+1-x^a)=1\).
Their products over all roots are rational units.
The [power correspondence](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#projection) identifies the \(x^a\) with the roots of \(T^b B^a-(-g)^a\), whose leading coefficient has absolute value one.
Multiplication over the roots now gives the stated \(D\).
Equivalently, \(R(T)=T^3L(T+T^{-1})\) with
\[
 L(s)=b s^3+(b-a)s^2-(2a+4b)s+a-5b .
\]
If a prime ramifies in \(E_i\), it ramifies in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)\), hence divides the polynomial discriminant of its integral generator and therefore \(D\).
Primes that divide \(a\) give simple reduction of \(A\) by the same derivative calculation.

<a id="local"></a>
## Only three collisions of trace labels are available at a prime

Fix a prime of the joint splitting field over \(\ell\), with inertia group \(I\).
Distinct inverse pairs of nontorsion roots have distinct trace labels within the union of all factors, since \(t=z+z^{-1}\) determines \(\{z,z^{-1}\}\).
If \(m_s\) such labels reduce to one value \(s\), the \(2m_s\) roots above them reduce to at most two residues, the roots of \(X^2-sX+1\).
Different \(s\)'s give disjoint sets of residues.
Thus the total root multiplicity excess above these residues is at least \(2\sum_s(m_s-1)\), also when the residual quadratic has a double root.
The [degree-six calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#inertia) bounds the excess of the whole polynomial by six.
Inertia fixes residual values, so the number of trace labels minus the number of their \(I\)-orbits is at most three.

For a prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(E_i\) above \(\ell\), denote its ramification index and residue degree by \(e,f\).
In the decomposition-group orbit on the embeddings giving \(\mathfrak p\), there are \(ef\) embeddings, in \(f\) inertia orbits of size \(e\).
Indeed the decomposition-group action gives the embeddings of the local field, and passage to the maximal unramified extension leaves a totally ramified degree-\(e\) extension for each of the \(f\) residue embeddings.
Consequently
\[
                  \sum_{i,\mathfrak p\mid\ell} f(e-1)\le3.               \tag{3}
\]
A ramified field uses at least one unit of this sum.
If it uses exactly one, the action on that factor has a single two-point inertia orbit and all other points are fixed.
Some inertia element then induces a transposition on that factor, which an alternating trace group excludes.
This proves the assertions on sharing primes, including in wild characteristic.

By the [local bound for the different and its norm](../audits/literature/Con26g/Yin_U001.md#con26g-u001), the contribution to \(v_\ell(\Delta_i)\) is at most \(f(e-1+e v_\ell(e))\).
Equation (3) gives \(e\le4\), so primes at least five supply no wild term.
At three the only wild index is three, which uses at least two units of (3), has \(f=1\), and adds three.
At two an index four exhausts (3), has \(f=1\), and adds eight.
With only index two wild at two, their total residue degree is at most three and they add at most six.
This proves (1) for the joint collection.

<a id="sizes"></a>
## What prime sizes can support a small factor

The signature of \(E_i\) has \(k_i\) complex places: each exterior conjugate pair gives two nonreal conjugate traces, and unit-circle roots give real traces.
The [Minkowski discriminant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields) gives
\[
 \log\Delta_i\ \ge\ 2\log(n_i^{n_i}/n_i!)+2k_i\log(\pi/4).
\]
For the upper bound the extension \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)/E_i\) has degree two, so \(\Delta_i^2\) divides the absolute discriminant of that root field, which divides \(|\operatorname{disc} P_i|\).
For a monic polynomial of degree \(d=2n_i\), Hadamard's inequality on the Vandermonde rows gives \(|\operatorname{disc} P_i|\le d^d M(P_i)^{2d-2}\).
Here \(M(P_i)\le M(A)\le\sqrt 8\) by the coefficient norm bound in the [trace theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#pairs).
Thus \(\Delta_i\le (16n_i)^{n_i}\).
All its prime divisors are included in \(S_D((16n_i)^{n_i})\).
Applying (1) to the discriminant proves (2).

The nontorsion half-degrees sum to \((3a+b-O(1))/2\) by the [cyclotomic classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic), and \(\sum k_i=a\).
The [factor-degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives \(\min_i n_i\to\infty\).
Stirling's estimate thus gives \(\sum_i\log(n_i^{n_i}/n_i!)=\sum_i n_i-o(b)\) uniformly over the partition.
Summing the Minkowski estimates gives \(\sum_i\log\Delta_i\ge b-o(b)\).
Applying the joint bound (1) gives the lower bound for \(\operatorname{rad}(D)\).

For a shrinking factor put \(\lambda_b=(\log\log b/\log b)^3\).
The [individual degree estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) give \(n_i\gg b\lambda_b\) and \(n_i=o(b)\).
The upper threshold in (2) is at most \(\exp(\varepsilon b\log b)\) eventually for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\).
Since \(k_i/n_i\to0\) by the [uniform allocation estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#degree), the left side of (2) is \((2-o(1))n_i\).
This gives the asserted lower order of log weight.
Primes up to \(y\) have total log weight \(O(y)\), since primes between \(m\) and \(2m\) divide \(\binom{2m}m\le4^m\) and summing over dyadic intervals gives the bound.
Removing primes up to \(o(b\lambda_b)\) preserves that lower order.
