# Square radicals at even seeds and radicals at powers of their own prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exclusions uniform in the delay and sign

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers, let \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(R\) be the largest positive integer \(q\) for which \(r^{1/q}\in K\), taking positive real radicals.
The possible exponents are the divisors of \(R\).

If \(a\) is even and \(2\mid R\), then \(\epsilon=1\), \(n\) is even, and \(a=w^2-1\) for an odd integer \(w\ge3\).
In particular at most \(\lfloor(\sqrt{X+1}-1)/2\rfloor\) even seeds \(a\le X\), \(X\ge3\), can have a square field radical for any choice of delay and sign.
If \(4\mid R\), then in addition \(w\) is the absolute value of an integer norm from \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).

For any prime \(\ell\) and \(u\ge1\) with \(a=\ell^u\ge3\), the condition \(\ell\mid R\) leaves only the following possibilities.
In each row \(v_\ell(R)=1\).
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
 a&\ell& \text{necessary conditions}\\ \hline
 8&2&\epsilon=1,\quad 2\mid n,\quad n\not\equiv4\pmod6,\quad 8\nmid n\\
 9&3&n\equiv1\pmod3 .
\end{array}
\]
The existence of a radical in either row remains open here.
The prime tested in this table is the prime underlying the seed.

<a id="squares"></a>
## The sign, cofactor and square integer

Write \(P\) for the minimal polynomial of \(r\) and \(U=G_a/P\).
The [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives a product of the distinct \(X-c\), \(c=\pm1\), for which \(c^{\,n-1}=\epsilon\).
It gives \(a-1<r<a+1\), \(d\ge n-1\) and \(d\) even.
For any positive field radical \(\beta=r^{1/\ell}\), its minimal polynomial \(S\) is reciprocal of degree \(d\), with a single exterior root \(\beta\).
Indeed \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=K\), so its powered conjugates correspond bijectively to the conjugates of \(r\).
Frobenius on their reductions gives [the coefficient congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius) \(S\equiv P\pmod\ell\).
When \(\ell\mid a\), the polynomial \(G_0=X^{n+1}-\epsilon\) is divisible by \(U\) and \(P\equiv G_0/U\bmod\ell\), by cancellation of the common monic \(U\).
In particular
\[
                  \beta^{n+1}\equiv\epsilon\pmod{\ell\mathcal O_K}.    \tag{1}
\]

Suppose \(a\) even and take \(\ell=2\).
The [two-adic trace test](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#radicals) at indices one and three gives \(4\mid a\) and \(X+1\nmid U\).
Squaring (1) gives \(r^{n+1}\equiv1\bmod4\mathcal O_K\), whereas \(G_a(r)=0\) gives \(r^{n+1}\equiv\epsilon\bmod4\mathcal O_K\).
Thus \(\epsilon=1\), since \(2\notin4\mathcal O_K\).
The absence of \(X+1\) now forces even \(n\), and \(U=X-1\).
Evaluation gives \(P(-1)=a+1\).

Put \(y=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), an algebraic integer.
The reciprocal conjugation \(\beta\mapsto\beta^{-1}\) is a nonidentity automorphism of \(K\) fixing \(y\), and \(\beta^2-y\beta+1=0\).
Thus \(F=\mathbb Q(y)\) has index two in \(K\).
Since \(\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)=1\), the identity \(1+r=\beta y\) gives
\[
          P(-1)=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(1+r)
                =\operatorname{Norm}_{F/\mathbb Q}(y)^2.
\]
This proves the square condition and the count by odd \(w\).
For \(a=2^u\), the consecutive even integers \(w-1,w+1\) are powers of two differing by two.
The smaller must be two because they cannot both be divisible by four.
Thus \(w=3,\ a=8\).

If \(\gamma=r^{1/4}\in K\), let \(z=\gamma+\gamma^{-1}\).
The same degree argument gives \([\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]=d/2\), so the subfield generated by \(y=z^2-2\) is all of \(\mathbb Q(z)\).
For the minimal polynomial \(T\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of \(z\),
\[
       w=\big|\operatorname{Norm}_{F/\mathbb Q}(z^2-2)\big|
         =\big|T(\sqrt2)T(-\sqrt2)\big|.
\]
The last product is \(A^2-2B^2\) for integers \(A,B\).
If three divides it then three divides \(A,B\), because two is a quadratic nonresidue modulo three.
Its absolute value cannot be three.
This excludes fourth radicals at seed eight.

<a id="odd"></a>
## Lifting and the archimedean norm for an odd prime

Suppose \(\ell\) is odd, \(a=\ell^u\), and \(\beta=r^{1/\ell}\in K\).
Set \(Y=\beta^{n+1}\).
For each valuation above \(\ell\), normalize \(v(\ell)=1\).
By (1), \(b=v(Y-\epsilon)\ge1\), and this value is finite since \(Y>1\).
In the expansion of \(Y^\ell-\epsilon=(\epsilon+(Y-\epsilon))^\ell-\epsilon^\ell\) the linear term has valuation \(1+b\).
Each intermediate term of order \(j\ge2\) has valuation \(1+jb\), and the last has valuation \(\ell b>1+b\).
Therefore \(v(Y^\ell-\epsilon)=1+b\).
But \(Y^\ell-\epsilon=a(r^n-\epsilon r)\), so \(b\ge u-1\) at every such valuation and \(Y-\epsilon\in\ell^{u-1}\mathcal O_K\).
Its nonzero norm has absolute value at least \(\ell^{(u-1)d}\).
The bound \(|Y'-\epsilon|\le2\max(1,|Y'|)\) at the embeddings gives
\[
              \ell^{u-1}\le2r^{(n+1)/(d\ell)}
                          <2(\ell^u+1)^{4/(3\ell)},                   \tag{2}
\]
since \(d\ge n-1,\ n\ge7\).

For \(\ell\ge5,\ u\ge2\), the ratio \((\ell^u+1)^{4/(3\ell)}/\ell^{u-1}\) is at most
\((26/25)^{4/15}5^{1-11u/15}\), by splitting off the power of \(\ell\).
This decreases with \(u\), and \(2\cdot26^{4/15}<5\).
For \(\ell=3,\ u\ge3\), the analogous ratio is \((1+3^{-u})^{4/9}3^{1-5u/9}\), also decreasing, and \(2\cdot28^{4/9}<9\).
These ranges contradict (2).

For \(u=1,\ \ell<n\), the [trace valuation](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#traces) would require \(\ell^2\mid a\), and the [diagonal calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#diagonal) gives the same contradiction when \(\ell=n\).
If \(\ell>n\), then \(\ell\ge11\).
The [prime radical gap](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives \(\log\beta\ge0.427800\,d/(n+1)\ge 3(0.427800)/4\).
On the other hand \(\log\beta<\log(\ell+1)/\ell\le\log(12)/11\), since this function decreases for positive \(\ell\).
This rules out \(u=1\).
The remaining odd case is \(a=9,\ \ell=3\); here \(3<n\) and the same trace valuation gives \(v_3(R)\le1\).

<a id="remaining"></a>
## Local restrictions at seeds eight and nine

At seed eight write \(n=2m\), \(\epsilon=1\), and put \(x=r+r^{-1}\).
The trace fields of \(r\) and \(\beta=r^{1/2}\) coincide, because \(x=y^2-2\) and both have degree \(d/2\).
As \(P=G_8/(X-1)\), its outer coefficients are one and all its intermediate coefficients are \(-7\).
Writing \(X^{-m}P(X)=f(X+X^{-1})\) gives the monic minimal polynomial of \(x\).
For \(t_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\),
\[
 f=t_m-7\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}t_j\right),\quad
 f(-2)=(-1)^m9,\quad f'(-2)=(-1)^{m-1}m(9m-7)/2 .
\]
Indeed \(t_j'(-2)=(-1)^{j-1}j^2\) by the recurrence \(t_{j+1}=Xt_j-t_{j-1}\), and \(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{j-1}j^2=(-1)^{m-2}(m-1)m/2\).
If \(3\nmid m\), Hensel's lemma supplies an embedding of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) into \(\mathbb Q_3\) taking \(x\) to a root \(t\equiv-2\bmod9\).
Taylor expansion gives \((t+2)/9\equiv2/(m(9m-7))\equiv 1/m\bmod3\).
Since \(x+2=y^2\) within this field, the displayed unit is a square in \(\mathbb Q_3\), forcing \(m\equiv1\bmod3\).
This excludes \(n\equiv4\bmod6\).

If \(8\mid n\), put \(b=v_2(n)\ge3\).
Modulo three, \(P=(X+1)(X^n-1)/(X-1)\), so a primitive \(2^b\)-th root occurs with odd multiplicity \(3^{v_3(n)}\).
Each square preimage has order \(2^{b+1}\) and lies in a Frobenius orbit twice as long as that of its image.
Indeed \(\operatorname{ord}_{2^b}(3)=2^{b-2}\) for \(b\ge3\), by squaring successively from \(v_2(3^2-1)=3\).
Every orbit in the reduction of \(S\) thus contributes an even multiplicity per target point.
This contradicts the exact equality \(S^{[2]}=P\), where square brackets indicate powering the full root multiset.

At seed nine, reduce the analogous equality \(S^{[3]}=P\) modulo two.
Here \(G_9\) reduces to \((X+1)(X^n+1)\), independently of the sign, and \(U\) removes only roots reducing to one.
If \(3^b\) exactly divides \(n\) with \(b\ge1\), a primitive \(3^b\)-th root in the target has multiplicity \(2^{v_2(n)}\).
Each cube preimage has order \(3^{b+1}\), and its Frobenius orbit is three times longer than the target orbit because \(\operatorname{ord}_{3^b}(2)=2\cdot3^{b-1}\), by cubing successively from \(v_3(2^2-1)=1\).
Constant multiplicity along a source orbit makes its contribution per target point divisible by three, a contradiction.

Finally, if \(3\mid n+1\), set \(j=(n+1)/3\).
The [powered-polynomial congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence) at a cube field radical gives \(P^{[3]}\equiv P\bmod9\).
The roots of \(U\) are fixed by cubing, hence \(G_9^{[3]}\equiv G_9\bmod9\).
Powering a monic polynomial's roots has coefficients given by integral symmetric polynomials in its coefficients.
Since \(G_9\equiv X^{3j}-\epsilon\bmod9\), the powered polynomial reduces to \((X^j-\epsilon)^3\bmod9\), different from \(X^{3j}-\epsilon\) at coefficient \(X^{2j}\).
This excludes the second residue class and completes the table.
