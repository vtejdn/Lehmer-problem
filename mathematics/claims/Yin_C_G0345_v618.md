# Antipodal and prime-power fluctuations of the circle modulus at small Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Necessary tails at every prime

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2h>0\), with no unit-circle root.
Put \(m=m(P)\), \(L(t)=\log|P(e^{it})|\), and let \(d\mu=dt/(2\pi)\) denote normalized circle measure.
Write \(c=\log(11/4)/22\), the constant in the [quadratic pencil bound](Yin_C_G0345_v617.md#quadratic).
For \(q\ge2,\ b\ge0\), set
\[
 a_q=2\operatorname{arsinh}q,\qquad
 J_q(b)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(q\cosh(b/2))-a_q .
\]
For every prime \(p\),
\[
 \int \bigl(|L(pt)-pL(t)|-a_p\bigr)_+\,d\mu
 \ \ge\ J_p\bigl((c-(p+1)m)_+\bigr).                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular, the pointwise balance \(|L(pt)-pL(t)|\le a_p\) implies \(m\ge c/(p+1)\).
On any sequence with \(m\to0\), the lower limit of the integral in (1) is positive for each fixed prime.
For primes tending to infinity with \((p+1)m\to0\), its lower limit is at least \(2\log\cosh(c/2)>0\), although \(a_p\to\infty\).

If \(P(-X)\ne P(X)\), the corresponding antipodal bound is
\[
 \int \bigl(|L(t+\pi)-L(t)|-a_2\bigr)_+\,d\mu
 \ \ge\ J_2\bigl((c-2m)_+\bigr).                                \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(1/(9+4\sqrt5)\le |P(-z)/P(z)|\le 9+4\sqrt5\) throughout the circle implies \(m\ge c/2\).
A separate pencil gives \(m\ge c\) under the stronger upper bound \(|P(-z)/P(z)|\le7\).
An even input can be written \(P(X)=P_0(X^{2^j})\) with \(P_0\) noneven, monic, irreducible, reciprocal and hyperbolic.
Its measure is \(m(P_0)\), so the antipodal conclusions apply to that polynomial.
These restrictions on the modulus allow unbounded fluctuations along a hypothetical sequence with \(m\to0\).

<a id="pencil"></a>
## A quadratic pencil from a coefficient congruence

Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\) be symmetric under \(X\mapsto X^{-1}\), strictly positive on the circle, nonconstant, coprime, and of the same positive bandwidth \(k\).
Assume their coefficients at \(X^k\) have absolute value one and \(U\equiv V\bmod q\) coefficientwise for an integer \(q\ge2\).
Set
\[
 H=UV,\qquad W=\left((U-V)/q\right)^2-2UV,\qquad
 F=H(Y+Y^{-1})+W .
\]
The Laurent polynomials \(H,W\) are integral and symmetric, \(H\) has bandwidth \(2k\) with unit outer coefficients, and \(W\) has bandwidth at most \(2k\).
At a nonzero root of \(U\) the value of \(W\) is \(V^2/q^2\ne0\), and the same calculation interchanging \(U,V\) handles the roots of \(V\).
Thus the monomial normalizations of \(H,W\) share no factor away from zero, and \(W/H\) has a nonzero finite pole.
The quadratic pencil bound applies.

Put \(s=|\log(V/U)|\) on the circle and \(M=m(U)+m(V)\).
Then \(W/H=4\sinh^2(s/2)/q^2-2\).
The two roots of \(YF/H\) have product one and sum \(-W/H\).
Jensen's formula on that fiber gives
\[
 c\le m(F)=M+\int \Psi_q(s)\,d\mu,\qquad
 \Psi_q(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&0\le s\le a_q,\\
 2\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\sinh(s/2)/q\right),&s>a_q .
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{3}
\]
The leading coefficient \(H\) is bounded away from zero on the circle, and all fiber means are bounded below by \(\log H\).
The bounded positive part of \(\log|F|\) thus also gives an integrable negative part on the torus.

The function \(f(x)=\Psi_q(a_q+x)\) is increasing and concave on \(x\ge0\).
Indeed \(f(0)=0\) by continuity, and its derivative for \(x>0\) is \(\cosh(s/2)/\sqrt{\sinh^2(s/2)-q^2}\) at \(s=a_q+x\).
The square of that derivative is \(1+(1+q^2)/(\sinh^2(s/2)-q^2)\), which decreases.
Jensen's inequality in (3) yields \(f(\int(s-a_q)_+\,d\mu)\ge(c-M)_+\).
The inverse function is \(J_q\), giving the stated overshoot bound.
If \(S=\max s>a_q\), (3) also gives the measure estimate \(\mu\{s>a_q\}\ge(c-M)_+/\Psi_q(S)\).

For the associated specialization, the strict crossing condition \(|W|<2H\) holds precisely at \(0<s<a_q\).
Whenever \(|m(V)-m(U)|<a_q\), the continuous nonconstant function \(\log(V/U)\) takes some value in \((-a_q,a_q)\), since its mean belongs to this interval.
It cannot vanish on an open arc, by coprimality and nonconstancy, so there is a strict crossing arc.
A vertical component of \(F=0\) would give a common nonzero root of \(H,W\).
On any nonvertical torsion binomial component one can specialize \(X\) to a root of \(H\), contradicting \(W\ne0\) there.
Consequently there are only finitely many torsion points, by the [plane-curve torsion result](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area).
At consecutive points with \(X^N=\pm1\) on a compact subarc of strict crossing, \(F(X,X^N)\) alternates sign, giving unboundedly many distinct circle roots as \(N\) grows.
For \(N>2k\) its extremal exponents are \(\pm(N+2k)\) with unit coefficients, so its monomial normalization has degree \(2N+4k\) and each sufficiently large specialization has an irreducible unit factor with a nontorsion circle root.
The [scalar specialization limit](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U002.md#luc22-abelian-matrix-approximation) therefore gives neutral infimum at most \(m(F)\) on that crossing class.
Inequality (3) itself holds without the crossing condition.

<a id="applications"></a>
## Frobenius and antipodal substitutions

Choose the sign of \(u=\pm X^{-h}P(X)\) so that \(u>0\) throughout the circle, hence \(\log u(e^{it})=L(t)\).
For a prime \(p\), take \(U=u(X)^p,\ V=u(X^p)\) and \(q=p\).
They are congruent in \(\mathbb F_p[X^{\pm1}]\) by Frobenius and have common bandwidth \(ph\).
If a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\) were also a root of \(P(X^p)\), then \(\alpha^p\) would be a conjugate of \(\alpha\).
An automorphism of the splitting field taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^p\) would have a finite order \(j\), forcing \(\alpha^{p^j}=\alpha\), impossible off the circle.
Thus \(U,V\) are coprime, \(M=(p+1)m\) by invariance of Haar measure under powering, and \(\log(V/U)=L(pt)-pL(t)\).
This proves (1).
For \(p\to\infty\) and \(b\to c\) the identity \(\operatorname{arsinh}x=\log(x+\sqrt{x^2+1})\) gives \(J_p(b)\to2\log\cosh(c/2)\).
The crossing condition above holds if \((p-1)m<a_p\), in particular whenever \((p+1)m<c\).

For noneven \(P\), take \(U=u,\ V=u(-X)\) and \(q=2\).
The congruence is coefficientwise, \(M=2m\), and the two distinct monic irreducibles \(P(X),P(-X)\) certify coprimality.
This gives (2), with \(e^{a_2}=(2+\sqrt5)^2=9+4\sqrt5\).
The mean of the log ratio is zero, so the crossing condition holds.
Each one-sided antipodal overshoot integral contributes half the left side of (2), since the antipodal map reverses the signed difference.
Repeatedly removing an even substitution preserves irreducibility since any factorization before substitution would remain one afterwards.
It preserves reciprocity and absence of circle roots by the reciprocal identity and circle surjectivity, and preserves Mahler measure by the same Haar invariance.

Finally take \(H=u,\ W=(u(-X)-3u)/2\).
These are integral and coprime by the same coefficient congruence and evaluation at a root of \(P\).
With \(r=u(-X)/u>0\), the fiber contribution is zero for \(r\le7\) and equals \(\operatorname{arcosh}((r-3)/4)\) for \(r>7\).
The quadratic pencil bound now gives
\[
 m+\int_{\{r>7\}}\operatorname{arcosh}((r-3)/4)\,d\mu\ge c.
\]
This proves the stronger balance implication.
Also \(r=1\) somewhere, since its logarithm has mean zero, giving a strict crossing arc with \(W/H\) near \(-1\).
Here the extreme exponents of \(F(X,X^N)\) are \(\pm(N+h)\) with unit coefficients for \(N>h\), and the same root and specialization argument applies.
