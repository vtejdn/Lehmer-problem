# The exact cost in Mahler measure of a two-class multiple of a fixed reciprocal unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum for any prescribed pair of exponent classes

Fix the monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit \(\alpha\).
Write \(d=\deg P\) and \(m=\log M\).
Let \(p\) run through the odd primes and let \(b=b_p\) be positive integers with \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(p)\).
For any two distinct prescribed residues modulo \(p\), consider all nonzero integral multiples \(F\) of \(P\) occupying exactly that pair, with \(\deg F\le p(b+2)\).
In \(F/P\) let \(R\) be the product of all primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors other than \(x\), with multiplicities and positive leading coefficients.
Then uniformly over the prescribed pairs,
\[
                     \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\min_F m(R)
                         =\frac{m(P)}2.                                  \tag{1}
\]
The sets of witnesses are nonempty for all sufficiently large \(p\).
The same limit holds if the two rows, expressed with the standard residues in \([0,p-1]\), are required to be coprime over the rational Laurent polynomial ring in \(x^p\).
All coefficient heights and signs are allowed.
The onset and the lower estimate in this theorem can depend on \(P\).

<a id="lower"></a>
## The two signs in the lower bound

For a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(N_\epsilon=|\operatorname{Norm}P(\epsilon\zeta)|\) and let \(B_\epsilon\) be the positive generator of \((P(\epsilon\zeta))\mathbb Z[\zeta]\cap\mathbb Z\).
Write a prime on a positive integer for removal of its full \(p\)-part.
The [valuation deficit](Yin_C_G0345_v356.md#local) applied to the pair of inverse indices where \(v_\ell(P(\epsilon\zeta^j))\) is largest gives the [integer divisor](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction)
\[
      D_\epsilon=(B_\epsilon')^{p-1}/N_\epsilon'
                \ \bigm|\  |\operatorname{Res}(x^p-\epsilon,R)|.
\]
In fact, at \(\ell\ne p\) the evaluations at the distinct primitive nodes form an invertible Vandermonde matrix over the local integers.
Thus \(v_\ell(B_\epsilon)\) is the largest valuation and \(v_\ell(N_\epsilon)\) is the sum over all nodes, as in the cited contraction computation.
Reciprocity makes the maximum occur at least twice, so the missing valuations must be supplied by \(R\).

For fixed \(P\), write \(P(\epsilon X)=X^{d/2}T_\epsilon(X+X^{-1})\) and \(S_p\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
The [fixed-input common-modulus theorem](Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) gives the same logarithmic rate \(m(P)/2\) after division by \(p-1\) for \(H_\epsilon=|\operatorname{Res}(T_\epsilon,S_p)|\) and for \(G_\epsilon=\max_{a\in\mathbb Z}\gcd(T_\epsilon(a),S_p(a))\).
The [trace-ring comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input) gives \(G_\epsilon\mid B_\epsilon\mid H_\epsilon,\ N_\epsilon=H_\epsilon^2\).
Here a common integer root gives a cyclic quotient of that modulus, and the full trace-ring quotient has order \(H_\epsilon\) and exponent \(B_\epsilon\).
Eventually \(p\nmid P(\epsilon)\), hence \(p\nmid N_\epsilon\), by reduction of \(\Phi_p(X)\) to \((X-1)^{p-1}\).
Consequently \(\log D_\epsilon=(m(P)/2+o_P(1))(p-1)^2\) for each sign.
Multiplying the two resultants and using \(|z^{2p}-1|\le2\max(1,|z|)^{2p}\) on the complex roots of \(R\) yields
\[
            \bigl(m(P)+o_P(1)\bigr)p^2
             \ \le\ (\deg R)\log2+2p\,m(R).                             \tag{2}
\]
It applies to every such nonzero multiple.
Here \(\deg R\le p(b+2)=o(p^2)\), giving the lower bound in (1) independently of the residue pair.

<a id="upper"></a>
## An integer multiple with small coefficients

Choose \(1\le r<p\), let \(n=2(b+1)\), and assume \(n>d\).
For each vector of \(n\) integer coefficients in \([0,H]\) evaluate
\[
                 U(\alpha^p)+\alpha^r V(\alpha^p),
              \qquad \deg U,\deg V\le b.
\]
All outputs are algebraic integers in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
For a conjugate \(\alpha_i\) the absolute value of an output is at most
\(A_i=nH\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^{pb+r}\).
In the real coordinates and the real and imaginary parts of one representative from each complex pair, partition the coordinate intervals into intervals of length at most \(1/2\).
Two algebraic integers in the same box have every conjugate of their difference of absolute value less than one.
The norm of a nonzero algebraic integer is a nonzero integer, so each box contains at most one distinct output.
Since \(A_i\ge1\) for \(H\ge1\), each real coordinate meets at most \(6A_i\) intervals, taking a partition of \([-A_i,A_i]\).
Both coordinates of a complex pair contribute, giving at most
\[
                          (6nH)^d\exp((pb+r)m(P))
\]
distinct outputs, using monicity of \(P\).

Take
\[
       H=\left\lfloor\exp\left(
             \frac{(pb+r)m(P)+d\log(6n)}{n-d}\right)\right\rfloor+1.
\]
Then \((H+1)^n>(6nH)^d\exp((pb+r)m(P))\).
Two different vectors therefore have equal output.
Subtract them to obtain a nonzero integral polynomial
\(F_0(x)=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p)\) divisible by \(P\), with coefficients bounded by \(H\).
The different exponent classes ensure the polynomial is nonzero.
The sum of absolute coefficients bounds its Mahler measure, hence
\[
             m(F_0)\ \le\ \frac{(pb+r)m(P)+d\log(6n)}{n-d}
                              +\log(2n).                                \tag{3}
\]
Since \(0<r<p,\ b\to\infty,\ b=o(p)\), this is \((m(P)/2+o_P(1))p\) as an upper bound uniformly in \(r\).

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Retaining the original factor in two coprime rows

For the fixed polynomial, distinct conjugates have distinct \(p\)-th powers for all sufficiently large primes, since each pairwise quotient can have exact order \(p\) for at most one prime.
The minimal polynomial \(Q_p\) of \(\alpha^p\) thus has precisely these \(d\) powered conjugates and \(m(Q_p)=p\,m(P)\).
Any nonzero one-class multiple of \(P\) contains \(Q_p(x^p)\) up to a monomial.
More generally, if a common row factor \(A(x^p)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), it contains \(Q_p(x^p)\).
After clearing rational content by Gauss's lemma, containing this monic integral factor forces log measure at least \(p\,m(P)\).
Since \(m(P)>0\), (3) is strictly smaller for all sufficiently large \(p\).
Both rows of \(F_0\) are therefore nonzero and their common factor does not vanish at \(\alpha\).
Taking their primitive integral polynomial gcd and dividing it out gives an integral multiple still containing \(P\), of no greater degree or measure, with both rows present and coprime over the Laurent ring.
A monomial shift by at most \(p-1\) places the pair at any prescribed residues, preserves Laurent coprimality, and gives degree at most \(pb+r+p-1\le p(b+2)\).
The extra factors have total log measure at most that of the whole multiple, proving the matching upper bound.
For each fixed admissible degree cap the infimum is a minimum: once one witness is given, primitive integer polynomials \(R\) of bounded degree and bounded measure form a finite set by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequence for the unrestricted gap question

For example, choose \(b_p=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\) and the adjacent classes \(0,1\) for every prime.
An absolute positive lower bound on the limiting cofactor rate in (1), as \(P\) ranges over all nontorsion reciprocal unit minimal polynomials, is equivalent to an absolute positive lower bound on \(m(P)\) in that class.
The [nonreciprocal and nonunit reductions](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) then give the usual unrestricted Lehmer gap.
For each \(P\) the prime limit comes first.
To obtain a numerical uniform gap from this formulation one still needs a lower bound on the optimal cofactor rate independent of \(P\).
