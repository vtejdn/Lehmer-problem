# A degree lower bound for the normalized sum of pole residues

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bound in terms of the number of roots

Let \(P\) be a monic complex polynomial of degree \(d\ge1\) with \(|P(0)|=1\), simple roots, and no root on the unit circle.
Write \(r_\rho=|\rho|\), \(m=\sum_{r_\rho>1}\log r_\rho\), and assume \(0<m\le1\).
There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
       T(P)=\sqrt m\sum_{\rho:\,P(\rho)=0}
         \frac{1}{|P'(\rho)|\sqrt{|1-r_\rho^2|}}
               \ \ge\ c\,d^{1/4}.                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, every sequence of hyperbolic algebraic unit minimal polynomials with \(m\to0\) satisfies (1) eventually and has \(T(P)\to\infty\).
This strengthens the [divergence obtained from integral quotient energy](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#statement) by giving the degree dependence.
The test polynomials used below have complex coefficients.
The estimate constrains the residues on hypothetical shrinking sequences and leaves their existence open.

<a id="moments"></a>
## Interpolation and radial weights

Put \(a_\rho=1/(\rho P'(\rho))\), \(x_\rho=|\log r_\rho|/m\), and \(w_\rho=\sqrt m/[|P'(\rho)|\sqrt{|1-r_\rho^2|}]\), so \(T=\sum_\rho w_\rho\).
The product of all the radii equals one, whence
\[
             0<x_\rho\le1,\qquad \sum_\rho x_\rho=2 .
\]
For every complex polynomial \(F\) of degree less than \(d\), its interpolation at the simple roots of \(P\), evaluated at zero, gives
\[
          \sum_\rho a_\rho F(\rho)=-F(0)/P(0).                        \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the polynomial \(P(z)/[(z-\rho)P'(\rho)]\) equals one at \(\rho\) and zero at all the other nodes.
In particular \(\sum_\rho a_\rho=-1/P(0)\) and \(\sum_\rho a_\rho\rho^k=0\) for \(1\le k<d\).

The ratio \(|a_\rho|/w_\rho\) is \(\sqrt{|1-r_\rho^{-2}|/m}\).
Since \(0\le|\log r_\rho|\le m\le1\), the mean value theorem for the exponential gives
\[
          |a_\rho|\le \kappa w_\rho\sqrt{x_\rho},\qquad
          \kappa=\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm e .
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
Using \(F=1\) in (2) shows that \(1\le\kappa T\).
For each \(\eta>0\), the nodes where \(x_\rho\le\eta\) have total \(\sum |a_\rho|\le\kappa T\sqrt\eta\), while at most \(2/\eta\) nodes have \(x_\rho>\eta\).

<a id="circle-zero"></a>
## A bounded polynomial with a prescribed circle zero

For each integer \(N\ge8\) there exists a polynomial \(f_N\) of degree at most \(N-1\) with
\[
       f_N(0)=1,\qquad f_N(1)=0,\qquad
       \max_{|z|\le1}|f_N(z)|\le \exp(C/N)                           \tag{4}
\]
for an absolute \(C\).
The circle case of [Totik's Theorem 4](../audits/literature/Tot12/Yin_U001.md#tot12-u001) also gives (4), and its introduction attributes the circle construction to Halász.
For a direct proof, put \(D_N(z)=N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}z^j\) and \(U_N(t)=1-|D_N(e^{it})|^2\).
Then \(0\le U_N\le1\), with \(U_N(0)=0\), and
\[
     U_N(t)=\frac2{N^2}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)(1-\cos jt).
\]
For \(|t|\le2\pi/N\), the terms \(1\le j\le\lfloor N/4\rfloor\) satisfy \(|jt|\le\pi/2\), so \(1-\cos jt\ge2j^2t^2/\pi^2\).
Summing these terms gives \(U_N(t)\ge c_1N^2t^2\) for an absolute \(c_1>0\), since \(\lfloor N/4\rfloor\ge N/8\).
We can take \(c_1\le1/(4\pi^2)\).
For \(2\pi/N\le |t|\le\pi\), the sine formula for the geometric sum gives \(|D_N(e^{it})|\le\pi/(N|t|)\le1/2\).
There \(-\log U_N\le(4/3)|D_N|^2\).
The circle mean of \(|D_N|^2\) is \(1/N\).
Integrating the first bound on \(|t|\le2\pi/N\) after rescaling by \(N\), and the second on the remaining arc, now gives
\[
                    \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}-\log U_N(t)\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}
                       \le \frac{2C}{N}
\]
with an absolute \(C\), since the logarithmic singularity at zero is integrable.

Use the [scalar Fejér–Riesz factorization](../audits/literature/DR10/Yin_U001.md#dr10-u001) to choose a polynomial \(p_N\) of degree at most \(N-1\) with no zeros in the open disk and with \(|p_N(e^{it})|^2=U_N(t)\).
Here \(U_N\) is a nonzero nonnegative trigonometric polynomial of degree \(N-1\).
In this scalar case the factor can also be obtained by pairing the reciprocal-conjugate roots of \(z^{N-1}(1-D_N(z)D_N(1/z))\), taking the exterior roots and half the multiplicity of each circle root, which has even order by nonnegativity.
The squared modulus of the resulting polynomial on the circle differs from \(U_N\) by a positive constant factor, fixed by evaluation at a point where \(U_N>0\).
Since all the roots of \(p_N\) lie on or outside the unit circle, the single-factor identity \(\int_{\mathbb T}\log|1-az|=0\) for \(|a|\le1\) gives \(\int_{\mathbb T}\log|p_N|=\log|p_N(0)|\).
Thus \(\log|p_N(0)|=\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb T}\log U_N\ge-C/N\).
The boundary conditions \(U_N\le1\) and \(U_N(0)=0\) give (4) for \(f_N=p_N/p_N(0)\) by the maximum modulus principle.

<a id="degree"></a>
## A polynomial vanishing at the roots of larger radial displacement

Let \(C\) be as in (4), set \(B=\exp(C+3)\), and take
\[
                \eta=(2\kappa BT)^{-2},\qquad R=\exp(m\eta).
\]
Since \(\kappa T\ge1\), we have \(0<\eta<1\).
Let \(S=\{\rho:x_\rho>\eta\}\) and \(h=|S|\le 2/\eta\).
All the other roots satisfy \(|\rho|\le R\).
If \(h=0\), (2) at \(F=1\) and (3) already give \(1\le\kappa T\sqrt\eta=1/(2B)\), a contradiction.
Assume \(h\ge1\) and take \(N=\max(8,h)\).

For each \(\rho\in S\) with \(r_\rho\ge R\), use the factor \(g_\rho(z)=f_N(z/\rho)\).
It equals one at zero, vanishes at \(\rho\), and is bounded by \(\exp(C/N)\) on \(|z|=R\).
For each \(\rho\in S\) with \(r_\rho<R\), let \(\beta=R^2/\bar\rho\) and put
\[
          g_\rho(z)=f_N(z/\beta)\,\frac{1-z/\rho}{1-z/\beta}.
\]
Because \(f_N(1)=0\), the denominator cancels, giving a polynomial of degree at most \(N-1\) with \(g_\rho(0)=1\) and \(g_\rho(\rho)=0\).
On every point of \(|z|=R\), the ratio of the two absolute values is exactly \(R/r_\rho\), as follows by squaring and using \(\beta=R^2/\bar\rho\).
Also \(|z/\beta|<1\) there, so \(|g_\rho(z)|\le(R/r_\rho)\exp(C/N)\) on the entire contour.

The product \(F=\prod_{\rho\in S}g_\rho\) has \(F(0)=1\), vanishes at every node of \(S\), and has degree at most \(h(N-1)\).
Each reflected factor costs \(\log(R/r_\rho)\le m\eta+\max(0,-\log r_\rho)\) in the logarithm of its boundary norm.
The sum of the negative radial logarithms is \(m\), so
\[
    \max_{|z|\le R}|F(z)|
       \le \exp(Ch/N+hm\eta+m)\le B
\]
by the maximum modulus principle, \(h\eta\le2\), and \(m\le1\).
If \(\deg F<d\), (2) and (3) would give
\[
        1=\left|\sum_\rho a_\rho F(\rho)\right|
            \le B\sum_{x_\rho\le\eta}|a_\rho|
            \le B\kappa T\sqrt\eta=\tfrac12 .
\]
It follows that \(d\le h(N-1)\le8h^2\le32/\eta^2=32(2\kappa B)^4 T^4\), proving (1).
For the integral sequence in the statement, bounded degree and \(m\le1\) give finitely many possible \(P\), since \(|[X^{d-j}]P|\le\mathrm e\binom dj\) by expansion over subsets of roots.
Thus \(m\to0\) forces \(d\to\infty\) and (1) gives the asserted divergence.
