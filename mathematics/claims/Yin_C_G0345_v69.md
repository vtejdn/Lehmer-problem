# Imaginary quadratic fields and ramified prime divisors for two nonreal exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## A criterion in the trace field

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2m\), with \(m\ge3\) and exactly two exterior roots, a nonreal conjugate pair \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\).
Assume that distinct roots never have root-of-unity quotient and that the translates of the edge between the exterior inverse pairs form a connected graph on all \(m\) pairs.
Use the [signed graph on those pairs](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#statement).
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), \(t=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\), \(B=\mathbb Q(t)\), and write \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\).
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

1. The signed graph is balanced.
2. \(K\) contains an imaginary quadratic field.
3. For a positive squarefree integer \(q\), there is an \(s\in B\) with \(t^2-4=-qs^2\).

When these conditions hold, \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-q})\) is the only quadratic subfield of \(K\), and \(K=BF\).
The minimal polynomial \(f\) of \(\lambda\) over \(F\) is monic integral and self-inversive, with \(P=f\bar f\).
It has one exterior root, one interior root and \(m-2\) roots on the circle.
Over the rationals the splitting group is either \(S_m\) or \(S_m\times C_2\).

<a id="field"></a>
## The orientation character and the two factors

Agent-S obtained the quadratic criterion by using the sign of the unique full relation.
The trace field \(B\) has degree \(m\), since each pair gives a different trace and \(\lambda\) satisfies \(X^2-tX+1=0\).
There are \(m-2\) real trace embeddings, and the two remaining trace images are nonreal conjugates since \(\lambda\) is nonreal of modulus greater than one.
In particular \(B\) admits a real embedding even when \(t\) in the chosen embedding is nonreal.
The quadratic extension \(K/B\) has involution \(\iota\) taking \(\lambda\) to \(\lambda^{-1}\).

Suppose the signed graph is balanced.
By the [full relation-lattice calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations), representatives \(v_i\) can be chosen so that \(\prod_i v_i\) is torsion and its exponent vector generates the full lattice of relations modulo torsion.
Choose the common orientation with \(\lambda\) among these representatives.
The splitting group \(G\) sends the generator to itself or its negative.
Thus every automorphism permutes the \(v_i\) or sends all of them to their inverses in permuted order.
These two possibilities give a character \(\chi:G\to\{\pm1\}\).
Ordinary complex conjugation has character \(-1\) because it inverts the representative of any neutral pair.
The fixed field of \(\ker\chi\) is therefore imaginary quadratic, and it lies in \(K\) because every automorphism fixing \(\lambda\) has character \(+1\).

Conversely let \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-q})\) lie in \(K\).
Since \(B\) has a real embedding, \(F\) is not contained in \(B\), hence \(K=BF\) and \(\iota\) restricts to the nontrivial automorphism of \(F\).
For each trace embedding take the image of \(\lambda\) under the unique extension to \(K\) inducing the given embedding of \(F\).
This gives one representative per inverse pair.
The other extension gives its inverse.
The resulting set contains \(\lambda\), and it contains \(1/\bar\lambda\) since complex conjugation interchanges the two choices over every conjugated trace.
All remaining members have modulus one.
Their full product, including the two off-circle members, thus has modulus one.
Every Galois conjugate of the product is itself or its inverse by the restriction to \(F\).
Kronecker's theorem applied to this algebraic unit gives a torsion relation, which forces balance by the connected graph theorem.
The chosen \(m\) representatives are exactly the conjugates of \(\lambda\) over \(F\), since \([K:F]=m\).
They form the root set of \(f\), and their inverses form that of \(\bar f\).
In particular \(f(0)\) is a unit of \(F\) and \(z^m\overline{f(1/\bar z)}=\overline{f(0)}f(z)\), giving the asserted self-inversiveness in the [complex reciprocal convention](../audits/literature/Sch75/Yin_U001.md#sch75-self-inversive).
The roots in the two sets are disjoint and exhaust \(P\).

The element \(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\) lies in the \((-1)\)-eigenspace of \(\iota\), which is the line \(\sqrt{-q}\, B\) when \(F\subset K\).
Squaring gives the stated squareclass in \(B\).
Conversely \(s\ne0\) in the squareclass identity, and \((\lambda-\lambda^{-1})/s\) generates the required quadratic field.

The action on labels is all of \(S_m\) by the [transposition calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action).
The label permutation and \(\chi\) together determine the action on every root, giving an injection \(G\hookrightarrow S_m\times C_2\) surjecting onto the first factor.
If the kernel over \(S_m\) has order two, the image is the whole product.
Otherwise it is the graph of the sign character, since transpositions are conjugate and generate \(S_m\), and \(\chi\) is nontrivial.
In the graph case \(\chi\) is the only nontrivial quadratic character.
In the product case the stabilizer of \(\lambda\) consists of \(S_{m-1}\times\{1\}\), with the sign coordinate here denoting the orientation.
Since \(m\ge3\) its projection contains a transposition, and the only nontrivial quadratic character trivial on this stabilizer is \(\chi\).
The quadratic subfield correspondence now proves uniqueness in both cases.

<a id="ramification"></a>
## A local cost at the endpoints of the trace interval

For a rational prime \(p\) ramified in \(F\) set
\[
                 a_p(B)=\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p,\ e_{\mathfrak p}\ {\rm odd}}
                                  f_{\mathfrak p},
\]
where \(e_{\mathfrak p}\) and \(f_{\mathfrak p}\) are the ramification index and residue degree in \(B/\mathbb Q\).
For odd \(p\) one has \(p^{a_p(B)}\mid P(1)P(-1)\).
If 2 ramifies in \(F\), then \(2^{a_2(B)}\) divides each of \(P(1)\) and \(P(-1)\).
These are nonzero rational integers.

Indeed for each prime of \(K\) over a counted \(\mathfrak p\), the absolute ramification index is divisible by the index two in \(F\) and equals \(e_{\mathfrak p}\) times the relative index over \(B\).
Thus \(K/B\) has relative index two, a unique prime over \(\mathfrak p\), and trivial residue extension there.
The involution acts trivially on that residue field, so the reduction of the unit \(\lambda\) equals its inverse.
In odd characteristic this gives \(t\equiv2\) or \(-2\) modulo \(\mathfrak p\), and in characteristic two both congruences hold.
Taking norms of \(t-2\) and \(t+2\) proves the divisibility by the residue-degree formula, since the absolute norms are respectively \(|S(2)|=|P(1)|\) and \(|S(-2)|=|P(-1)|\).
For odd \(p\) ramification means \(p\mid q\).
At 2 use the quadratic field discriminant, equal to \(-q\) for \(-q\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(-4q\) otherwise.
In particular the local argument applies to \(q=1\), even though 2 does not divide that squarefree radicand.

<a id="growth"></a>
## Uniform exclusions from the residue degrees

Define
\[
        W(B,F)=\sum_{\substack{p\mid\operatorname{disc}(F)\\p\ {\rm odd}}}
                   a_p(B)\log p+
            \mathbf 1_{\,2\mid\operatorname{disc}(F)}\,2a_2(B)\log 2 ,
\]
using a zero contribution at 2 when it is unramified.
On every sequence of the stated inputs with \(m\to\infty\) and bounded Mahler measure one has \(W(B,F)=o(m)\), allowing \(F\) to vary.
Thus for each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the subclass with \(W(B,F)\ge\epsilon m\) has a uniform positive logarithmic Mahler gap, and its measure tends to infinity with its degree.
If any prime ramified in \(F\) is unramified throughout \(B\), its contribution alone is at least \(m\log2\), giving such a uniform gap.

To prove the assertion, the local divisibilities give \(W(B,F)\le\log|P(1)P(-1)|\).
For any such bounded-measure sequence the absolute height of \(\lambda\) tends to zero, and the conjugates of every polynomial lie in a common compact annulus by reciprocity.
The degrees tend to infinity and the points are nontorsion, so the [one-dimensional small-points theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives weak convergence of the full orbit measures to circle arclength probability.
For \(u=1,-1\), truncate \(\log|z-u|\) from below at \(-A\).
The truncated function is continuous on that annulus, and is an upper bound for the logarithm.
Taking the upper limit of \((2m)^{-1}\log|P(u)|\) and then letting \(A\to\infty\) bounds it by the circle mean of \(\log|z-u|\), which is zero by the elementary logarithmic sine integral.
Each \(P(u)\) is a nonzero integer, so both normalized logarithms tend to zero.
This proves the assertion for bounded measure.
For the indicated subclass it excludes every bounded-measure subsequence whose degrees tend to infinity.
Bounded degree and bounded measure give finitely many monic integer coefficient tuples.

<a id="example"></a>
## A family for every imaginary quadratic field

For any squarefree \(q\ge1\) and integer \(L\ge4\) put \(A=L\sqrt{-q}\) and
\[
                f(z)=z^3+A z^2-Az+1,\qquad
                S(t)=t^3+(qL^2-3)t+2-2qL^2 .
\]
Then \(P=f\bar f=z^3 S(z+z^{-1})\) satisfies the hypotheses with \(m=3\) and has the given quadratic field.
To see irreducibility of the cubic \(f\) over \(F\), any root there would be an integral unit \(u\), hence \(|u|=1\), and \(u=1\) is not a root.
For \(u\ne1\) the positive integer \(|u-1|^2\) is at least one, and \(|A u(u-1)|>2\ge|u^3+1|\).
The two distinct irreducibles \(f,\bar f\) are exchanged by the nontrivial automorphism of \(F\), so their monic rational product is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Direct multiplication gives the displayed trace polynomial.
Its derivative is strictly positive on the real line and \(S(-2)=-4qL^2<0<S(2)=4\).
Thus exactly one trace gives a neutral pair and the two nonreal traces give the two nonreal exterior conjugates.
Transitivity on three labels and ordinary complex conjugation as a transposition yield the full \(S_3\) action and a connected edge graph.
If a positive power identified two distinct roots, its Galois-equivariant fibers would all have the same size at least two.
The fiber at an exterior value could contain only the two exterior roots, hence both, forcing a [matching on the inverse labels](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#matching), impossible on three labels.
Finally \(A=-(\lambda^3+1)/(\lambda(\lambda-1))\) for an exterior root chosen from \(f\), so \(F\subset K\).
The sum of the three roots of \(f\) has modulus \(L\sqrt q\) and is at most \(R+1+R^{-1}\), where \(R\) is the exterior radius.
Thus these examples do not approach measure one as \(L\sqrt q\to\infty\).
