# All radical degrees of a pole at two separated progressions of Salem exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous irreducibility at the divisors of the short exponent

In the setting of the [two-progression theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v748.md#statement), write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), \(a\) for the short exponent, \(J\) for the selected irreducible pole polynomial, and \(E\subseteq F\) for its coefficient field.
Choose the positive real pole \(\lambda>1\) specified there and set \(H=E(\lambda)\), \(j=\deg J\).
At every sufficiently large index, **simultaneously for all positive divisors** \(k\mid a\), the polynomial \(J(Z^k)\) is irreducible over \(K\).
In particular for \(c=\lambda^{1/k}>0\),
\[
                  [K(c):K]=[E(c):E]=kj,\qquad [KH(c):KH]=k .          \tag{1}
\]
The fields \(E(c)\) and \(K\) are linearly disjoint over \(E\).
No reality assumption on the other embeddings of \(H\) is needed.

For a number field \(D\subset\mathbb R\) and \(z>1\) in \(D\), denote by \(e_D(z)\) the largest positive integer \(l\) for which \(z^{1/l}\in D\), with positive real roots throughout.
Then \(\gcd(a,e_{KH}(\lambda))=1\) eventually.
More generally for any Salem number, any \(E\subseteq F\), and any extension \(H=E(\lambda)\subset\mathbb R\) with \(\lambda>1\) and \([KH:K]=[H:E]\), put \(n=[F:E]\).
Whenever \(n>2\), set \(e=e_H(\lambda)\) and \(\mu=\lambda^{1/e}\).
One has
\[
 e_{KH}(\lambda)=e_{FH}(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}
     2e &\text{if }\sqrt{\mu}\in FH,\\
     e &\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                         \tag{2}
\]
At an odd finite prime of \(H\) where \(\mu\) has square nonzero residue, the [local degree test](#primes) can exclude the doubling in (2) from the splitting of the trace extension.

<a id="relative-real"></a>
## The real embeddings that fix the pole field

Put \(D=KH\) and \(M=FH\).
The degree hypothesis makes \(K\) and \(H\) linearly disjoint over \(E\), so \([M:H]=n\) and \(D/M\) is quadratic.
The nontrivial automorphism \(\iota\) of \(K/F\), sending \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\), extends to \(D\) by fixing \(H\).
It generates \(\operatorname{Gal}(D/M)\).
Every embedding of \(M\) fixing \(H\) is real, since the image of the totally real \(F\) and the distinguished image of \(H\) are both real.
Exactly one such embedding restricts to the identity on \(F\), namely the identity on \(M\).
For an embedding with another restriction on \(F\), either extension \(\sigma\) to \(D\) satisfies \(\sigma\iota=\bar\sigma\).
On \(K\) this follows because \(\sigma(\alpha)\) is then a neutral conjugate and its inverse is its complex conjugate.
On \(H\) both sides are the given real embedding.

Let \(u=\lambda^{1/l}\in D\) for some \(l\ge1\).
In the chosen real embedding \(\iota(u)\) is a real \(l\)-th root of \(\lambda\), so \(\iota(u)=\pm u\) and \(u^2\in M\).
All conjugates of \(u^2\) over \(H\) are real by the preceding embedding argument and solve \(X^l=\lambda^2\), so \([H(u^2):H]\le2\).
Since \([M:H]>2\), at least two embeddings of \(M\) fix \(H(u^2)\).
Choose a nonidentity one and extend it to \(\sigma:D\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\).
If \(\iota(u)=-u\), the nonzero number \(\sigma(u)\) is purely imaginary, although \(\sigma(u)^2=u^2>0\).
This contradiction puts \(u\) in \(M\).
Every conjugate of \(u\) over \(H\) is now real and solves \(X^l=\lambda\).
Consequently \(u^2\in H\), and \(u\in H\) when \(l\) is odd.

The admissible positive exponents in a fixed real number field are finite by bounded degree and height and comprise all divisors of their maximum by closure under divisors and least common multiples.
The latter closure uses a Bézout combination of positive real radicals, as in the [trace-field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v746.md#seed).
Thus an admissible odd \(l\) in \(D\) divides \(e\), while an admissible even \(l\) has \(l/2\mid e\) since \(u^2\in H\).
All such radicals are in \(M\), including \(\lambda^{1/e}\in H\).
This proves (2).

<a id="uniform"></a>
## From fixed degrees to every divisor

The poles, monic polynomials and coefficient fields in the two-progression theorem belong to finite lists.
Since \(J\) is irreducible over \(K\), its irreducibility over \(E\) and the degree equation \([KH:K]=[H:E]=j\) hold at the same indices.
The trace degree tends to infinity, so \(n>2\) eventually.
By (2), the prime divisors of every \(e_{KH}(\lambda)\) lie in the fixed finite union of the prime supports of \(2e_H(\lambda)\) over the listed fields and poles.
At each prime \(p\) in this finite set, [fixed-divisor irreducibility](Yin_C_G0345_v748.md#saturation) gives \([K(\lambda^{1/p}):K]=pj\) at all sufficiently large indices with \(p\mid a\).
It follows that \(\lambda^{1/p}\notin KH\) there.
Taking one threshold for the finite union proves \(\gcd(a,e_{KH}(\lambda))=1\).

For any \(k\mid a\) now put \(s=[D(c):D]\le k\).
Every conjugate of \(c\) over \(D\) is a root of \(X^k-\lambda\) and has modulus \(c\).
The absolute value of the real constant term of its monic minimal polynomial therefore equals \(c^s\in D\).
Combining this power with \(c^k=\lambda\) by Bézout gives \(c^{\gcd(k,s)}\in D\).
If \(s<k\), then \(k/\gcd(k,s)>1\) divides both \(a\) and \(e_D(\lambda)\), which is impossible.
Thus \(s=k\).
Since \(D=K(\lambda)\) and \(K(c)\) contains \(D\), the degree over \(K\) is \(kj\).
This equals the degree of \(J(Z^k)\), which vanishes at \(c\), giving irreducibility over \(K\) and hence over \(E\).
The equality of the two degrees over \(E,K\) proves the asserted linear disjointness.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Splitting at primes dividing the pole minus one

Suppose that \(\mathfrak P\) is a prime of \(H\) of odd residue characteristic, with \(\mu\) integral and its residue a nonzero square.
This condition holds if \(v_{\mathfrak P}(\mu-1)>0\), and it holds at every odd \(\mathfrak P\) with \(v_{\mathfrak P}(\lambda-1)>0\) when \(e\) is odd.
Indeed in the latter case \(\mu\) is a unit whose residue has order dividing the odd \(e\), so that residue is a square.
By maximality of \(e\), the real field \(H\) does not contain \(\sqrt{\mu}\).
The quadratic extension \(H(\sqrt\mu)/H\) splits at \(\mathfrak P\), by lifting the two distinct roots of \(X^2-\mu\) in the residue field. [Simple-root lifting](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001)

Write \(d_w=[M_w:H_{\mathfrak P}]\) over all places \(w\mid\mathfrak P\) of \(M\).
If doubling holds, the two places of \(H(\sqrt\mu)\) partition the \(w\)'s into two collections of equal total local degree \(n/2\).
This follows from the local degree sum for \(M/H(\sqrt\mu)\), since the two quadratic completions are both \(H_{\mathfrak P}\).
In particular, odd \(n\) prevents doubling, and for even \(n\) the condition
\[
                    v_2\!\left(\gcd_{w\mid\mathfrak P}d_w\right)
                        =v_2(n)                                       \tag{3}
\]
also prevents it, since this gcd cannot divide \(n/2\).

For an explicit case, suppose the prime of \(E\) below \(\mathfrak P\) is inert and unramified in \(F/E\) and \(f=f(\mathfrak P/E)\) is odd.
The local degrees \(d_w\) are all \(n/\gcd(n,f)\), so doubling is excluded for every \(n>2\).
Indeed \(F\) has a single completion there, unramified of degree \(n\), and base change to \(H_{\mathfrak P}\) decomposes it into \(\gcd(n,f)\) unramified extensions.
To see this, lift a generator of the degree-\(n\) residue extension with its separable minimal polynomial.
After residue base change the \(f\)-th Frobenius power splits its \(n\)-cycle into \(\gcd(n,f)\) cycles of length \(n/\gcd(n,f)\).
Their roots lift uniquely in unramified extensions of that degree and retain their distinct reductions, giving exactly those field factors.
The identification with the local factors of \(FH\) uses the linear disjointness of \(F,H\) over \(E\). [Finite fields and Frobenius](../audits/literature/Hun74/Yin_U006.md#hun74-u006)

<a id="example"></a>
## An even-degree Salem trace field and a pole with nonreal conjugates

Let \(\lambda=\sqrt[3]6\), \(H=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), \(E=\mathbb Q\), and \(\theta=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\).
Set \(F=\mathbb Q(\theta)\).
The polynomial \(Z^3-6\) is Eisenstein at \(2\), so at its unique prime over \(2\) the normalized integral valuation of \(\lambda\) is one.
Thus \(e_H(\lambda)=1\).
At \(5\) the same polynomial has a simple root \(1\bmod5\), giving a degree-one place of \(H\) with \(\lambda\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak P\).

The minimal polynomial of \(\theta\) is \(X^4-4X^2+2\), Eisenstein at \(2\).
Its reduction modulo \(5\) is irreducible.
Indeed \(Y=X^2\) satisfies \(Y^2+Y+2\), with nonsquare discriminant \(3\bmod5\), and the norm of either root from \(\mathbb F_{25}\) is the nonsquare \(2\) of \(\mathbb F_5\).
Neither root is a square in \(\mathbb F_{25}\), so adjoining a square root gives degree four over \(\mathbb F_5\).
Hence \(5\) is inert and unramified in \(F\).
The trace \(1+\theta\) is greater than two and has its three other conjugates in \((-2,2)\), using the four values \(\pm\sqrt{2\pm\sqrt2}\).
The larger root of \(X^2-(1+\theta)X+1\) is a Salem number with trace field \(F\), since the negative discriminant at the other real places gives quadratic irreducibility over \(F\).
Its degree-eight field \(K\) and the cubic \(H\) are linearly disjoint over \(\mathbb Q\) by coprime degrees.
At the degree-one place over \(5\) the preceding test excludes doubling, and (2) gives \(e_{KH}(\sqrt[3]6)=1\).
This calculation concerns the radical group of an actual compositum.
The simultaneous degree conclusion (1) retains the shrinking-sequence hypotheses of the two-progression theorem.
