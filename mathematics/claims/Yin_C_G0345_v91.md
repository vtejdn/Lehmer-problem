# Growth of the Mahler measure of a cyclotomic polynomial plus one monomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform growth with the number of distinct prime divisors

Let \(n\ge2\), put \(r=\omega(n)\), and integrate on the circle with normalized Haar measure.
For each \(\epsilon>0\), as \(r\to\infty\),
\[
             \|\log|\Phi_n|\|_{L^1(\mathbb T)}
                    \gg_\epsilon r^{1/6-\epsilon}.
\]
Write \(m(F)=\log M(F)\) for the full logarithmic Mahler measure.
Uniformly in every integer \(k\ge0\) and even in every complex coefficient \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\),
\[
        m(\Phi_n(X)+aX^k)\ \ge\
                     \tfrac12\|\log|\Phi_n|\|_1-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2).
\]
In particular these measures tend to infinity when \(r\to\infty\).
For \(a\) a nonzero integer there is an absolute uniform positive gap for \(m(\Phi_n+aX^k)>0\) over all \(n,k,a\).
Both central perturbations \(k=\varphi(n)/2,\ a=\pm1\) are covered whenever \(\varphi(n)\) is even.
This settles the two families left by the [critical-degree classification](Yin_C_G0345_v89.md#conductors).

<a id="fourier"></a>
## Fourier coefficients at signed sums of complementary divisors

Put \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\).
The identity \(\Phi_n(X)=\Phi_q(X^{n/q})\) preserves the circle distribution, so it suffices to bound \(L(\theta)=\log|\Phi_q(e^{i\theta})|\).
The Fourier coefficient convention is \(\widehat L(h)=\int e^{-ih\theta}L(\theta)\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
For \(h\ne0\) one has
\[
                  \widehat L(h)=-\frac{c_q(h)}{2|h|},\qquad
                  c_q(h)=\mu(q/g)\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi(q/g)},
                  \qquad g=(q,h).
\]
Indeed each root of the cyclotomic polynomial contributes the corresponding translate of \(\log|1-e^{i\theta}|\), whose nonconstant coefficients are \(-1/(2|h|)\) by expansion at radii below one and \(L^1\) convergence.
For the convergence, away from zero the logarithms converge uniformly, and near zero they are dominated in absolute value by a constant plus \(|\log|\theta||\) for radii between \(1/2\) and one.
The primitive character sum \(c_q(h)\) factors at the primes by the Chinese remainder theorem.
A prime contributes \(p-1\) if \(p\mid h\) and \(-1\) otherwise.
All these coefficients are real.

Choose distinct primes from \(q\) and write \(d_p=q/p\).
For a nonempty subset \(J\) of the chosen primes and signs \(\epsilon_p=\pm1\) let \(h=\sum_{p\in J}\epsilon_p d_p=q S_J\), where \(S_J=\sum_{p\in J}\epsilon_p/p\).
Modulo each \(p\in J\) just its own nonzero summand survives, so \(h\ne0\) and \((q,h)=q/\prod_{p\in J}p\).
Writing \(A=\varphi(q)/q\), this gives
\[
 |\widehat L(h)|=\frac{A}{2|S_J|\prod_{p\in J}(p-1)},\qquad
 \widehat L(d_p)=\frac{Ap}{2(p-1)}\ge A/2.
\]
Every singleton has the same sign, essential for the following bounded Fourier test.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Selecting primes to control the higher odd terms

The odd-product test and its prime-selection argument were developed with agent-S.
For large \(r\), retain the largest \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\) prime divisors, all at least \(r/2\).
Take \(s=\lfloor(r/40)^{1/3}\rfloor\).
In increasing order, take a least remaining prime \(P\) and remove all remaining primes in \([P,4P)\), repeating until the list is exhausted.
If at least \(s\) such groups occur, select their first primes from \(s\) groups, so successive selected primes increase by a factor of at least four.
Otherwise some interval \([P,4P)\) contains at least \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor/s\) retained primes.
Split it into \(12s\) equal subintervals of width \(P/(4s)\).
For all sufficiently large \(r\) the fullest subinterval has at least \(s\) primes, since \(s^3\le r/40\).
Select \(s\) of them.
They then lie in \([P',P'(1+1/(4s))]\) for a common \(P'\ge P\).

In either selection, for every odd \(|J|\) and any choice of signs,
\[
                        |S_J|\ge\frac1{2\min_{p\in J}p}.
\]
For multiplicatively separated primes the sum of all smaller reciprocals is at most one third of the largest reciprocal.
In the short interval the integer \(\sum_{p\in J}\epsilon_p\) is odd, and after multiplication by \(P'\) the total deviation from that integer is at most \(1/4\).
In particular, for odd \(j=|J|\) the mixed coefficients are bounded in absolute value by \(2A/(r/2-1)^{j-1}\).

<a id="test"></a>
## A bounded trigonometric test and a growing integral

For the selected primes set
\[
       G(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\prod_{p}
             \left(1+\frac{i}{\sqrt{s}}\cos(d_p\theta)\right).
\]
Its absolute value is at most \((1+1/s)^{s/2}\le e^{1/2}\).
In the integral of \(LG\) only terms of odd order occur.
The singleton contribution is at least \(A\sqrt{s}/2\).
For a subset of size \(j\), the integral of \(L\) times the product of its cosines is the average of \(\widehat L(h)\) over its \(2^j\) sign choices.
With \(T=r/2-1\) the total absolute contribution of the terms of order at least three is at most
\[
        2AT\sum_{j\ge3}\frac{(\sqrt{s}/T)^j}{j!}
                            =O(A s^{3/2}/r^2).
\]
Thus \(\|L\|_1\gg A\sqrt{s}\gg A r^{1/6}\).

For completeness \(A\gg_\epsilon r^{-\epsilon}\) for every \(\epsilon>0\).
Choose a fixed finite prime product \(W\) with \(\varphi(W)/W<\epsilon/2\).
Such products exist since \(\prod_{p\le y}(1-1/p)^{-1}\ge\sum_{j\le y}1/j\).
All but the prime divisors of \(W\) are among the \(\varphi(W)\) allowed residue classes modulo \(W\), so the number of primes at most \(x\) is at most \(x\varphi(W)/W+O_W(1)\).
The \(j\)-th prime therefore has reciprocal at most \(\epsilon/j\) for large \(j\).
Summing the reciprocals of the \(r\) prime divisors of \(q\) costs at most \(\epsilon\log r+O_\epsilon(1)\).
The additional terms in \(-\log(1-1/p)-1/p\) have bounded sum by comparison with \(\sum_{m\ge2}1/m^2\).
Exponentiation gives the required lower bound for \(A\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Both central signs and all offsets

The angular mean of \(L\) is zero by the factorization into unit-circle roots.
For \(F=\Phi_n\), the [circle-root cancellation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) bounds the integral loss from adding \(aX^k\) by \(\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\) below \(\int\max(\log|F|,\log|a|)\).
For \(|a|\ge1\) this maximum dominates \((\log|F|)^+\), whose integral is \(\frac12\|\log|F|\|_1\).
The lower bound uses all roots of the actual perturbed polynomial, including its nonreal roots off the circle.

For a bounded number of distinct primes, the Möbius expression for \(\Phi_n\) uses at most \(2^r\) numerator and denominator binomials, by substitution from its squarefree radical.
Multiplication by the denominator gives an integer polynomial of bounded term count with the original measure for every nonzero integer \(a\), using the [central-shift clearing identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0457_v2.md#bounded-prime-count-completion) at arbitrary \(k\).
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives a uniform gap in this range.
Choosing the prime-count cutoff so that the preceding growing lower bound exceeds one for larger counts proves the assertion over all conductors.
