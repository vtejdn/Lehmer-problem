# Extracting every noncyclotomic factor from the end of a polynomial supported in short intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## Factorization with a bound independent of the degree

For integers \(k,L\ge1\) there are computable \(C=C(k,L)\ge1\) and \(\eta=\eta(k,L)>0\) with the following property.
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(N\) and nonzero constant coefficient, and suppose its support is covered by at most \(k\) real intervals of length \(W\ge0\).
If its logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)<\eta\), then there are a polynomial \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\), \(s=\deg Q\), and a factorization
\[
 P(X)=\pm Q(X)\prod_{\nu=1}^t\Phi_{e_\nu}(X^{b_\nu}),\qquad
 s\le CW,\quad \sum_{\nu=1}^t\varphi(e_\nu)\le C,\quad b_\nu>L s,            \tag{1}
\]
where \(e_\nu,b_\nu\) are positive integers, \(\Phi_e\) denotes the \(e\)-th cyclotomic polynomial and repeated factors are counted.
The empty product is allowed.
Choose the sign of \(Q\) so that it equals the initial coefficient block
\(\sum_{j=0}^s [X^j]P\,X^j\).
The final block \(\sum_{j=0}^s [X^{N-s+j}]P\,X^j\) also equals \(Q\) up to sign.
Every noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(P\) occurs in \(Q\) with its full multiplicity, and \(m(Q)=m(P)\).
The estimates apply to reducible polynomials, including those with measure zero.
Multiplication by an initial Laurent monomial just shifts the support before this normalization.

<a id="input"></a>
## Separating the actual coefficient blocks

We prove (1) by induction on the number of covering intervals.
For \(k=1\), \(N\le W\); take \(Q=P,C=1,\eta=1/2\).
A polynomial of degree zero is also covered in this way.
Since the exponents are integers, each covering interval can first be replaced by the closed span of the occupied exponents it contains.
Its length is at most \(\lfloor W\rfloor\), and empty intervals may be discarded.
It suffices to work with integer \(W\).

At stage \(k\ge2\), run the [finite rational cover of center positions](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#cover) with requested margin \(J=2L+4\).
For the resulting finite list of local certificates take \(\epsilon>0\) to be the smallest allowed relative covering width, \(D\) the largest degree threshold, and \(g>0\) the minimum of their rational lower gaps \(g_R/2\).
For each certificate let \(w_j\) be the width of its fixed set of fast exponents in coordinate \(j\), and let \(B\) be the maximum of \(\sum_j w_j\) over the finite list.
Take
\[
 \eta=\min\{\eta(k-1,L),g,\tfrac18\,5^{1-D^2},1/2\},\qquad
 C=\left\lceil\max\{1/\epsilon,D,B+C(k-1,L),1\}\right\rceil .                 \tag{2}
\]
All choices are independent of the actual integer coefficients and interval positions.

If \(N\le W/\epsilon\) take \(Q=P\).
If \(N<D\) and \(W\ge1\), the same choice satisfies \(s\le CW\).
For \(N<D\) and \(W=0\), the [bounded-degree norm inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#application) gives \(m(P)=0\) under our hypothesis.
The [integer measure-one characterization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) factors \(P\) up to sign into ordinary cyclotomics with total totient degree \(N\).
Take \(s=0\), \(Q=P(0)\in\{1,-1\}\), and \(b_\nu=1\).
This also explains the zero-width convention in (1).

In the remaining case \(N\ge D,\ W\le\epsilon N\), use the [integer support assignment](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#application).
Select an occupied representative from each interval with endpoint representatives 0 and \(N\), repeat representatives if necessary, and assign each occupied exponent to an interval containing it, assigning the endpoints to their representatives.
Intervals with these two endpoint representatives must be distinct.
The corresponding local model gives a rounded integer vector \(q\), distinct occupied fast exponent vectors \(a_i\), and nonzero slow rows \(B_i(X)\) supported in \([-d,d]\), with
\[
                       P(X)=\sum_i B_i(X) X^{a_i\cdot q}.
\]
At least two rows are occupied.
All terms assigned through the same original interval use the same model row, and different occupied model rows have disjoint ordered exponent intervals.
The width and separation inequalities of the local certificate hold.
In particular the earliest occupied row contains 0 and comes entirely from at most \(k-1\) of the original intervals, since a different row contains \(N\).
Let \(U\) be this entire first block, left in its original position with nonzero constant term.
Its degree is at most \(2d\) and its coefficients are exactly the corresponding coefficients of \(P\).
No restriction on common factors of other rows has been imposed.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Why the complete common factor appears at the first occupied row

Let \(h\) be the gcd of the \(B_i\) in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\) with integer content included.
Divide all rows by \(h\) and recenter by a common Laurent monomial.
Their joint width cannot increase, by additivity of \(X\)-width over the fast Laurent ring.
The primitive diagonal has nonnegative measure \(m(P)-m(h)<g\).
The [torsion alternative in the local certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#certificate) therefore writes its lift, up to a signed Laurent monomial, as
\[
                         \prod_{\nu}\Phi_{e_\nu}(X^{c_\nu}Y^{v_\nu}),
                                     \qquad v_\nu\ne0 .
\]
Fast-coordinate width additivity for the whole product gives the aggregate estimate
\[
           \sum_{\nu}\varphi(e_\nu)\le
           \sum_\nu\varphi(e_\nu)\|v_\nu\|_1=\sum_j w'_j\le B,               \tag{3}
\]
where \(w'_j\) is the actual fast width of the occupied rows.
Repetitions do not affect this argument.
The certificate also gives \(|c_\nu|\le2d\) and \(|v_\nu\cdot q|>Jd\).

Order fast exponents by their scalar product with \(q\).
For each factor the minimal face in this order is a single monomial, with coefficient a Laurent unit in \(X\), since \(v_\nu\cdot q\ne0\) and the two endpoint coefficients of \(\Phi_{e_\nu}\) have modulus one.
Their product is the unique lowest fast monomial of the primitive lift.
Consequently the first slow row before removing the common factor equals \(h\) up to a signed \(X\)-monomial.
The order agrees with the disjoint specialized bands, so \(U\) is precisely this fixed normalization of \(h\).
Restoring it after specialization gives
\[
             P=\pm U\prod_\nu\Phi_{e_\nu}(X^{b_\nu}),
             \qquad b_\nu=|c_\nu+v_\nu\cdot q|>(J-2)d>L\deg U .              \tag{4}
\]
Negative exponents are converted to positive exponents using reciprocal symmetry of the cyclotomic polynomial up to sign and monomial.
The residual monomial shift is zero because \(P,U\), and the positively oriented product all have nonzero constant term.
In particular \(m(U)=m(P)\).
This step identifies an actual coefficient block that retains the whole positive measure.

<a id="induction"></a>
## Stripping large gaps and stopping on the degree scale of an interval

Apply the induction hypothesis to \(U\), since \(m(U)<\eta(k-1,L)\) and it is covered by at most \(k-1\) original intervals of length at most \(W\).
The resulting polynomial \(Q\) is an initial block of \(U\), so \(s\le\deg U\) and it is also an initial block of \(P\).
Its degree bound and the total cost of all indices follow by (2), (3) and the induction bound.
Each newly adjoined exponent in (4) exceeds \(Ls\), as do the exponents obtained inside \(U\).
This proves (1).
Each positively oriented factor in (1) has nonzero terms only at powers spaced by \(b_\nu>s\) and has constant and leading coefficients of modulus one.
The same product has no terms within \(s\) of either extreme except its extreme coefficient.
Thus the leading block of \(P\), read in increasing order of exponent, equals the trailing block up to sign.
Every factor in the product has all its roots on the unit circle and is a product of cyclotomic polynomials over \(\mathbb Z\).
This proves the assertions about multiplicity and measure.

<a id="consequences"></a>
## Low values of the measure and limits of padding by cyclotomic factors

For fixed \(k\) and integer \(W\), the measures below \(\eta(k,1)\) for polynomials covered by these intervals form a finite set.
Every value in it already occurs for a polynomial of degree at most \(C(k,1)W\) in the same class, since its initial block uses a subset of the original support.
For this degree bound and \(m(Q)<\eta\), the elementary symmetric formulas give coefficient bounds \(2^{\deg Q}e^\eta\), which leave only finitely many integer choices.
If an exact enumeration is desired, reduce \(\eta\) to \(\log r\) for a rational \(1<r<e^\eta\).
The class of supports in the specified intervals is decidable by the finite list of occupied exponents, and the multiplicative Mahler measure of each candidate is a real algebraic number that can be compared with \(r\).

In particular, suppose an irreducible noncyclotomic polynomial \(S\) divides \(P\) and \(m(P)<\eta(k,1)\).
Then any cover of the support of \(P\) by \(k\) intervals of length \(W\) must satisfy \(W\ge\deg S/C(k,1)\).
This remains true after multiplying \(S\) by arbitrary cyclotomic factors, or by any other integral factors that keep the total measure below the threshold.
The estimate concerns the absolute width required to retain the original factor even if the degree of the multiple becomes arbitrarily large.

The coordinating researcher derived the inverse factorization from the extreme rows and the induction on the number of occupied intervals.
The strategy researcher supplied the finite cover with an adjustable nonresonance margin.
The ambient-unit researcher checked the descent through the actual first block and the aggregate bound on the cyclotomic indices.
