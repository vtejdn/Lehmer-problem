# A quadratic unit test distinguishes two local factors of the same degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## The same prime pair permits and violates the quadratic unit condition

Fix a positive odd multiple \(h\) of \(39\) prime to five and positive integers \(a_2,a_3\).
There are arbitrarily large pairs of primes \(r<q\) with \(g=q-r=O_{h,a_2,a_3}(\log r)\) and \(6h\mid g\) for which the following holds for both \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
Set
\[
 Q=\frac{X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g)}{X+\varepsilon},
 \qquad d=\frac{8(q-1)}h,\qquad b=\frac{4g}h.
\]
For a polynomial \(F\) define \(N_j(F)=\operatorname{Res}(F,X^g-j)\) for \(j=2,3\) and \(A(F)=\operatorname{Res}(F,X^{g/2}-\sqrt3)\).
At five work in the quadratic unramified extension \(\mathbb Q_5(\sqrt3)\), identifying its residue field with \(\mathbb F_{25}=\mathbb F_5[a]\), \(a^2=3\).
There are two monic reciprocal divisors \(F\mid Q\) in \(\mathbb Z_5[X]\) of degree \(d\), each coprime modulo five to its complement and satisfying
\[
 F(\varepsilon)\equiv 5^{-b}N_2(F)\equiv5^{-b}N_3(F)\equiv1\pmod5.
\]
For one of them \(5^{-b/2}A(F)\) has residue of order three in \(\mathbb F_{25}^\times\), and for the other its residue has order six.
The [totally positive unit condition](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#units) excludes the latter selection from any global monic rational divisor of degree \(d\).
On the same prime pairs both selections combine with reciprocal local factors over \(\mathbb Z_2,\mathbb Z_3\) having the degree \(d\) and the three [prescribed rational unit values](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#precision) through precisions \(a_2,a_3\).
The rational realizability of the first selection remains open.

<a id="parameters"></a>
## The primitive part can be kept fixed

Take the [split-prime construction](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#pairs) with splitting parameter \(Mh\).
Choose \(M\) a sufficiently large multiple of \(144\) and of \(6E\), where \(E\) is a common exponent of the two finite unit groups at the requested precisions.
The integer \(M\) may be enlarged by a multiple to furnish all the equal-label groups used below and at the primes two and three.
Then \(r,q\equiv1\bmod 2Mh\), \(2Mh\mid g\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_q(2),\operatorname{ord}_q(3),\operatorname{ord}_r(5)>2g+2\).
In particular \(r\equiv1\bmod 8\cdot3h\), and \(g/(6h)\) is divisible by \(24\).
The local choices at two and three follow directly from the [fixed-numerator construction](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#precision).
The [necessary global condition](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#statement) follows there from absence of real roots and the totally positive norm-one units in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\).
Their reductions belong to the order-three subgroup, since \(2+\sqrt3\) has order three modulo five.

At five the primitive \(r\)-grid gives \(J=(r-1)/\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_r(5))\) reciprocal Frobenius orbits with \(Mh\mid J\).
Each lifts to a monic reciprocal factor \(V\) with constant term one and coprime complement by [coprime lifting](Yin_C_G0345_v216.md#lifts).
Its four values \(V(\varepsilon),N_2(V),N_3(V),A(V)\) are local units: \(\bar x^g\) on the grid has prime order \(r>5\), and \(\bar x\ne\varepsilon\).
Use their residues as labels in \((\mathbb F_5^\times)^3\times\mathbb F_{25}^\times\).
Partition each label class into groups of \(24\), leaving fewer than \(24\) unused in each class.
The four product labels of each whole group are one.
Exactly \(8J/h\) orbits are required; this number is divisible by \(24\).
Taking \(M\) so that \(M(h-8)\) exceeds \(24\) times the size of the label group leaves room for all the discarded remainders.
Select the required groups and call their product \(V_0\).
It has degree \(8(r-1)/h\) and all four unit residues one.
Use the same \(V_0\) for both exceptional choices at the given sign.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## A formula for the normalized resultant on the exceptional factors

Use either \(H=h\) or \(H=3h\) and set \(B=g/(2H)\), a multiple of \(24\).
Let \(D\) be a subset of the \(H\)-th roots of unity in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), stable under Frobenius and inversion.
For every \(\xi\in D\) take all roots with
\[
                                x^{4B}=-\xi^4                                                     \tag{1}
\]
with their full multiplicities.
Since \(H\) is odd, \(\xi\mapsto-\xi^4\) is injective and its image lies among the roots of \(Z^H+1\).
The latter polynomial is separable since \(5\nmid H\).
Its distinct factors remain pairwise coprime after substitution \(Z=X^{4B}\) by Bezout's identity, including if \(5\mid B\).
The selected product divides \(X^{2g}+1\), is defined over \(\mathbb F_5\), avoids \(1,-1\), and is reciprocal with constant term one by inverse pairing.
It avoids the primitive \(r\)-grid because \(r>4g\).
Lift it uniquely to a monic reciprocal factor \(W\) of \(Q\) over \(\mathbb Z_5\), using the [disjoint grid reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#three) and the preceding lifting argument.
Its degree is \(4B|D|\).

Put \(\eta=a^{H^{-1}\bmod8}\), so \(\eta^H=a\) and \(\eta^4=-1\) since \(a\) has order eight.
Above a fixed \(\xi\), the four possibilities \(w=\bar x^B\) satisfy \(w^4=-\xi^4\).
Their \(H\)-th powers range once over the four roots of \(T^4+1\), and the unique \(w\) with \(w^H=a\) is \(\eta\xi\).
There are exactly \(B\) lifts counted with multiplicity above each \(w\).
For the lifts with \(w^H\ne a\), the product of \(x^{g/2}-\sqrt3\) reduces to \((-4a^3)^B\), by the derivative of \(T^4+1\) at \(a\) and the three factors in the opposite order.

For the lifts above \(w=\eta\xi\), write \(Y=x^g\), so \(\bar Y=3\).
The [root identity at five](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#sign) gives
\[
 (x^r+\varepsilon)(Y-2)(Y-3)=-5\varepsilon(Y^2-1).
\]
Here \(\bar x^r+\varepsilon\ne0\).
Factoring \(Y-3=(x^{g/2}-\sqrt3)(x^{g/2}+\sqrt3)\), we obtain
\[
            \overline{(x^{g/2}-\sqrt3)/5}
                       =\frac{\varepsilon}{a(\bar x^r+\varepsilon)}
\]
because \(\bar Y-2=1,\ \bar Y^2-1=3,\ 2a\ne0\) and \(-3/2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_5\).
The product of the denominators \(\bar x^r+\varepsilon\) over that fiber is \(\varepsilon^B-(-1)^B w^r=1-w^r\).
Indeed the \(r\)-th powers of roots of \(X^B-w\) give the roots of \(X^B-w^r\) with their full multiplicities since \((B,r)=1\).
We have \(w^r=w=\eta\xi\) since \(r\equiv1\bmod8H\).
The remaining constant from the four fibers is \((-4a^3\varepsilon/a)^B=1\), by \(24\mid B\).
Thus \(v_5(A(W))=B|D|\) and
\[
             \overline{5^{-B|D|} A(W)}
                  =\left(\prod_{\xi\in D}(1-\eta\xi)\right)^{-1},
 \qquad
             \overline{W(\varepsilon)}=\prod_{\xi\in D}(1+\xi^4).                                  \tag{2}
\]

<a id="orders"></a>
## Two residue orders for the same rational norm data

Over \(\mathbb F_5\) let \(P(T)=T^4+T^3-T^2+T+1\).
It divides \(\Phi_{13}\), as one verifies by multiplication with the two remaining factors \(T^4+2T^3+T^2+2T+1\) and \(T^4-2T^3-2T+1\).
Its roots \(D_0\) are four distinct primitive thirteenth roots stable under inversion and Frobenius.
Their images \(-\xi^4\) are exactly the roots of \(K(Z)=Z^4+2Z^3+2Z+1\): division of \(K(-T^4)\) by \(P(T)\) has zero remainder and the four images are distinct.
Hence \(\prod_{D_0}(1+\xi^4)=K(1)=1\) in the residue field.
For \(H=h\) use \(D=D_0\).
For \(H=3h\) use \(D=D_0\cup(\mu_9\setminus\{1\})\), where \(\mu_9\) consists of all ninth roots of unity.
This union is disjoint, lies in the \(H\)-th roots and has size twelve.
In the two cases \(4B|D|=8g/h=2b\), so \(F=V_0W\) has the desired degree and \(B|D|=b/2\).
The eight extra roots give \(\prod(1-\eta\xi)=(1-\eta^9)/(1-\eta)=1\).
Raising these roots to the fourth power permutes them, so their other product \(\prod(1+\xi^4)\) is one by evaluation of \((T^9-1)/(T-1)\) at \(-1\).

In (2) the denominator is now \(P(\eta)\), by reciprocity.
Since \(\eta^4=-1\), it equals \(\eta^3-\eta^2+\eta\).
When \(H\equiv1,5\bmod8\), we have \(\eta=\pm a\), so \(P(\eta)\) belongs to \(\{2+a,2-a\}\).
These elements are inverse and have order three.
When \(H\equiv3,7\bmod8\), we have \(\eta=\pm 2a\), and \(P(\eta)\) belongs to \(\{-2+a,-2-a\}\).
These are the negatives of elements of order three and have order six.
Multiplying \(h\) by three interchanges the two cases.
Both values have norm one to \(\mathbb F_5\), since the norm of \(2\pm a\) is \(4-3=1\).
Conjugation \(\sqrt3\mapsto-\sqrt3\) interchanges the two linear factors of \(X^g-3\), so the norm to \(\mathbb Q_5\) of \(A(F)\) equals \(N_3(F)\).
The norm of the normalized residue therefore gives \(5^{-b}N_3(F)\equiv1\bmod5\).

Finally, \(N_2(F)/N_3(F)\) is a five-adic unit with residue one.
Indeed the [oriented-pair computation](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#quartic) uses only the local root equation, the paired roots and Frobenius.
Here \(d\) is divisible by four, half the exceptional degree is \(b=4g/h\), and \(b/2^{v_2(g)}\) is divisible by four.
In equation (2) of that computation the oriented product \(T\) is one and \((-1)^{d/2-b}\varepsilon^b=1\), giving the stated residue.
The resulting value of \(5^{-b}N_2(F)\) completes the two scalar norm tests.
Order six excludes the corresponding local factor, while order three meets the stated necessary quadratic unit test.
