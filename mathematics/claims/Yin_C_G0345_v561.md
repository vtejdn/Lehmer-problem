# A factor gap for reciprocal binomial blocks whose long exponent has fixed prime support

<a id="statement"></a>
## The long exponent and its prime divisors

Let \(n>k>0\), let \(c,\delta\in\{1,-1\}\), and let \((a,b)=(1,2)\) or \((2,1)\).
Consider the integer polynomial
\[
 F(X)=a+b\delta X^k+c X^n(b+a\delta X^k).
\]
For every fixed finite set \(S\) of primes there is \(\eta_S>0\) such that, whenever every prime divisor of \(n\) belongs to \(S\), every integral divisor of \(F\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure has log measure at least \(\eta_S\).
Every irreducible nontorsion unit factor then has degree at least a positive fraction of \(n+k\) depending only on \(S\).
The exponents may have a common divisor.

More generally, let \(P\mid F\) run through irreducible nontorsion unit factors, put \(d=\deg P\), and suppose \(k/n\to0\).
If \(B\) is any fixed squarefree divisor of \(n\) prime to three, put \(R_B=\prod_{q\mid B}q^{v_q(n)}\) and \(L=3^{v_3(n)}\).
Then eventually
\[
                         d\gg_B L R_B .                              \tag{1}
\]
For \(B=1\) the product is one.
In particular, along a sequence of positive-measure factors with measure tending to zero, the largest prime divisor of \(n\) must tend to infinity.

<a id="fibres"></a>
## The distribution among the tame roots at three

Choose a root \(x\) of \(P\) and write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Put \(y=\delta x^k\) and choose \(u=1\) when \(a=1\), \(u=-1\) when \(a=2\).
With \(\beta=x^{un/B}\), \(z=c\beta^B\) and \(f(T)=-(1+2T)/(2+T)\), the parent equation gives \(y=f(z)\) in both orientations.
Here \(f\) is an involution.
The [four-term cut bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \((n+k)h(x)\le3\log 6\), so \(h(y)\to0\).
Every \(y\) is nontorsion, and the sequence eventually avoids each finite set by positivity of the heights of its members that can occur.
The same genericity holds for \(\beta\) because the map \(\psi(T)=f(cT^B)\) is fixed after passing to a subsequence with constant signs and orientation.

Endow \(O(1)\) on the target projective line with its standard adelic metric for \(h\).
Its pullback by the finite degree-\(B\) morphism \(\psi\) is ample with semipositive metric, and its point height is \(h(\psi(\beta))\).
The normalized height of the target line is zero, as is seen by applying the arithmetic projection formula to its squaring map with the standard metric, whose pullback is the metrized \(O(2)\).
The same projection formula gives zero for the height of the source line with the pulled-back metric.
By [Yuan's geometric equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md#yuan-equidistribution), the conjugates of \(\beta\) in \(\mathbf C_3\) converge in distribution on the Berkovich projective line to the normalized curvature measure of this metric.
The pushforward of this probability by \(\psi\) is the Gauss-point mass, since the images are the conjugates of the generic small points \(y\) with equal multiplicities and the standard metric at three has that mass.
The limit is invariant under \(T\mapsto\zeta T\) for \(\zeta^B=1\).
Indeed, as a comparison sequence take the full inverse images under \(\psi\) of the Galois orbits of roots of unity of strictly increasing prime orders.
Each finite set is invariant under these rotations and is a union of small Galois orbits of height zero.
Every choice of one orbit from each set is generic, since its images have increasing orders.
Equidistribution for every such choice implies the same convergence for their cardinality-weighted unions, by choosing an orbit of largest discrepancy for each fixed continuous test function.

Normalize the three-adic valuation by \(v(3)=1\) and the absolute value by \(|3|_3=1/3\).
The inverse image of the target Gauss point under \(f\) is the point of the closed disk \(|z-1|_3\le1/3\).
Indeed \((f(y)-1)/3=-(1+y)/(2+y)\) has good reduction of degree one, and hence fixes the Gauss point.
Under \(T\mapsto cT^B\) the inverse images of this disk point are the disk points \(|T-r|_3\le1/3\) for the \(B\) distinct roots \(r^B=c\).
These roots have distinct reductions, and in the coordinates \(T=r+3V,\ z=1+3W\) the reduced map \(V\mapsto W\) is multiplication by the nonzero scalar \(\overline{cBr^{B-1}}\).
Thus each point maps to the indicated disk point with [local degree one](../audits/literature/Fab13b/Yin_U001.md#reduction), accounting for all \(B\) preimages.
The rotations permute them transitively, so each has mass \(1/B\).
For each \(r\) the open set given by
\[
          |\beta-r|_3<3^{-1/2},\qquad 3^{-3/2}<|c\beta^B-1|_3<3^{-1/2}
\]
contains its disk point.
By weak convergence at least \(1/(2B)\) of the embeddings eventually satisfy both conditions for that \(r\).
Counting embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) repeats each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) equally often.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Ramification and residue degree at the same place

Choose \(r\) of order \(B\) for \(c=1\), and of order \(2B\) for \(c=-1\), so \(r^B=c\).
Choose an embedding furnished by the preceding paragraph.
Let \(\ell\) be the multiplicative order of the residue of \(x\), and let \(e,f_0\) be the ramification index and residue degree of its completion over \(\mathbb Q_3\).
Since \(\bar x^{un/B}=\bar r\) and \(3\nmid 2B\), the order \(\ell/\gcd(\ell,n/B)\) is divisible by each \(q\mid B\).
This forces \(v_q(\ell)\ge v_q(n)\) simultaneously for all these primes.
Consequently \(R_B\mid\ell\), and \(f_0\) is a multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{R_B}(3)\), with \(\operatorname{ord}_1(3)=1\).
The [elementary order-lifting bound](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#orders) gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{R_B}(3)\gg_B R_B\), since the powers \(q^{\max(0,v_q(n)-c_{3,q})}\) dividing this order are pairwise coprime.

As \(z\equiv1\), the order \(\ell\) divides \(2n/L\).
The equality \(x^{2un}=z^2\) and the prime-to-three exponent \(2n/(L\ell)\) give
\[
               v(x^{\ell L}-1)=v(z-1)<3/2 .
\]
Here for a unit congruent to one, raising it to an exponent prime to three or inverting it preserves the valuation of its difference from one.
Initially \(v(x^\ell-1)\ge1/e\).
Cubing a principal unit of depth \(t>0\) gives depth at least \(\min(3t,1+t)\).
It therefore takes at most \(\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\) cubings to reach depth \(1/2\), and a further cubing gives depth at least \(3/2\).
Thus \(v_3(n)\le\lceil\log_3 e\rceil\), and \(e\ge L/3\).
The inequality \(d\ge ef_0\) at this one place proves (1).

If positive log measures tend to zero, take irreducible factors with positive log measures tending to zero.
They are eventually unit factors and \(k/n\to0\), \(d/(n+k)\to0\) by the [classification and degree law at length six](Yin_C_G0345_v555.md#statement).
For \(n\) supported on \(S\) one can pass to a subsequence with the same product \(B\) of its distinct prime divisors other than three.
Then \(L R_B=n\), contradicting (1).
This proves the asserted uniform measure gap, and the cut bound gives the resulting degree gap for every nontorsion unit factor.
If the largest prime factor failed to tend to infinity on a shrinking sequence, a subsequence would be supported on the finite set of primes up to a fixed bound.
