# A degree bound independent of the distance between two exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## The sum of the contact orders minus one

Fix nonzero signed rows \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of formal widths \(b,d\), signs \(\varepsilon,\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\), and a distance \(k\ge1\).
Let \(V(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y)\), \(W(Y)=Y^d B(1/Y)\), \(f=-\varepsilon A/V\), and \(g=-\sigma B/W\).
For a nonzero rational function \(u\) let \(r(u)\) be the number of distinct zeros and poles of the reduced function in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
For each \(n\ge2\) consider all Galois orbits \([c]\) of nontorsion algebraic units at which both graphs
\[
             A(X^n)+\varepsilon X V(X^n),\qquad
             B(X^{n+k})+\sigma X W(X^{n+k})
\]
vanish to order at least two and \(A(c^n)B(c^{n+k})\ne0\).
For each such pair let \(t_{n,c}\) be the smaller order and \(D_c=[\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]\).
Then
\[
           \sum_{n,[c]} (t_{n,c}-1)D_c
              \le r(f)+r(g)
              \le 2\deg f+2\deg g
              \le 2b+2d.                                                 \tag{1}
\]
The sum includes every eligible exponent for the two fixed rows at distance \(k\).
In particular the same upper bound applies to the unweighted sum of their degrees, since \(t_{n,c}\ge2\).

<a id="local"></a>
## The fixed point at a powered node

Assume that at least one pair is counted and put
\[
            F(Y)=Y f(Y)^k,\qquad J(Z)=Z/g(Z)^k,\qquad T=J\circ F .
\]
At \(\beta=c^n\) and \(z=c^{n+k}\) the value equations give \(f(\beta)=g(z)=c\) with nonzero numerators and denominators.
The two graphs give \(f(X^n)=X+O((X-c)^{t_{n,c}})\) and \(g(X^{n+k})=X+O((X-c)^{t_{n,c}})\).
Consequently \(F(X^n)=X^{n+k}+O((X-c)^{t_{n,c}})\).
The map \(g\) is holomorphic at \(z\), so \(f(Y)-g(F(Y))\) vanishes to order at least \(t_{n,c}\) at \(\beta\), using the local coordinate \(Y=X^n\) with nonzero derivative at \(c\).
Since \(T(Y)=Y(f(Y)/g(F(Y)))^k\), the quotient is regular and equals one there, and the derivative of \(v\mapsto v^k\) at one is \(k\ne0\).
It follows that \(T(\beta)=\beta\), \(T'(\beta)=1\), and \(T(Y)-Y\) vanishes to order at least \(t_{n,c}\).

The derivatives of both \(F,J\) are nonzero at the corresponding nodes, since their logarithmic derivatives \(YF'/F\) and \(ZJ'/J\) have values \((n+k)/n\) and \(n/(n+k)\).
If \(\deg T=1\), multiplicativity of degree makes \(F\) a Möbius map.
Reciprocity makes \(F\) commute with inversion.
The [Möbius argument for a genuine double unit](Yin_C_G0345_v433.md#identity) then shows that the specified logarithmic derivative and \(F(c^n)=c^{n+k}\) contradict the strict hyperbolic sine inequality at a real exterior conjugate of the resulting quadratic unit.
Thus \(\deg T>1\).
Each \((n,[c])\) supplies \(D_c\) distinct points \(\beta\), and the sets from distinct pairs are disjoint by [recovery of the exponent and unit from the node](Yin_C_G0345_v433.md#count).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Counting the infinite critical orbits

For a nonconstant rational map \(L\) equal to \(F\) or \(J\), let \(u=f\) or \(g\) respectively.
Let \(E=\{0,\infty\}\), and write \(e_L\) for the number of points \(p\in E\) such that \(L(p)\notin E\).
Off \(E\), the zeros and poles of \(L\) have precisely the support of those of \(u\).
Thus \(dL/L\) has \(r(u)+2-e_L\) simple poles, one at each distinct zero or pole of \(L\).
Every nonzero meromorphic differential on the sphere has divisor of degree \(-2\), so \(dL/L\) has \(r(u)-e_L\) zeros with multiplicity.
At each point with \(L(p)\notin E\), the order of the differential is the local degree of \(L\) minus one.
Consequently \(L\) has at most \(r(u)-e_L\) distinct critical values off \(E\).

Consider two copies of the sphere, applying \(F\) from the first to the second and \(J\) from the second to the first.
Every other step starting in the first sphere is an iterate of \(T\).
By the chain rule a critical point of \(T\) either is critical for \(F\) or has \(F\)-image critical for \(J\).
Its forward orbit therefore passes through a critical value in one of the two spheres.
Two such alternating orbits which meet in the same sphere share their subsequent tail, including their visits to the first sphere.
The critical values outside \(E\) give at most \(r(f)-e_F+r(g)-e_J\) infinite tails.
An orbit starting at an endpoint either stays within the four marked points in the two spheres and is finite, or first leaves from one of the \(e_F+e_J\) marked points whose image is outside \(E\).
The exit point determines the entire subsequent tail.
This proves \(\delta(T)\le r(f)+r(g)\), where \(\delta(T)\) counts distinct infinite critical orbit tails.
The endpoint count includes cancellations for \(k=1\).

By [Epstein's Theorem 1 at multiplier one](../audits/literature/Eps99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-points), the sum over all distinct multiplier-one fixed points of their local orders in \(T-\mathrm{id}\) minus one is at most \(\delta(T)\).
The powered nodes therefore give the first inequality in (1).
The numerator and denominator of a reduced rational function each have degree at most its map degree, giving the remaining inequalities.

For example, if \(c\) and \(c^2\) both occur for the same pair of exponents and \(\mathbb Q(c^2)=\mathbb Q(c)\), their degrees agree and their different positive measures distinguish the two orbits.
Their contribution implies \(2D_c\le r(f)+r(g)\) at every distance.
The bound concerns fixed rows and gives no uniform degree exclusion when both widths grow.
