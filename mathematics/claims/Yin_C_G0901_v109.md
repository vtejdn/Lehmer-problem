# The first two Mahler iterates near one and the exceptional exterior patterns

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded second iterate and a reduction with no increase of total measure

Write \(M\) for multiplicative Mahler measure, \(m=\log M\), and \(M^j\) for iteration on algebraic numbers.
Fix any \(T>0\).
For all sufficiently small positive \(m(w)\), if \(m(M(w))\le T\), there is \(j\in\{1,2\}\) such that
\[
        M^2(w)=M(w)^j,\qquad M^k(w)=M(w)^{j^{\,k-1}}\quad(k\ge1).         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(w\) may be any nonzero algebraic number: a sufficiently small positive measure requires its primitive integer minimal polynomial to be monic up to sign with constant coefficient a unit.
The statement concerns \(m(M(w))=\log M^2(w)\), the total measure of the number \(M(w)\), even if \(w\) itself has very large degree.

In this regime the divisible group \(D(w)\) generated by the conjugates contains a reciprocal unit \(z\), with \(\deg z\le\deg w\) and \(0<m(z)\le m(w)\), in one of the following two classes after choosing a real conjugate and its sign.

* \(z\) is a real Salem number.
* \(z>1\) is a real Perron unit with \(M(z)=z^2\) and the large oriented block action.
  All inverse pairs outside one block are neutral.
  In that block one canonical half consists of \(z\) and \(b-1\) roots of modulus less than one, with torsion product; its inverse half supplies the other \(b-1\) exterior roots.
  All the other half products are torsion as well.

Both classes lie in the [single fixed field over the totally real numbers](Yin_C_G0901_v108.md#few), since their block permutation fields are totally real.
Thus existence of a sequence with \(0<m(w)\to0\) and bounded \(m(M(w))\) is equivalent to existence of such a shrinking sequence in the union of the two displayed classes.
The second iterates in these classes are respectively \(M(z)\) and \(M(z)^2\).
In particular, if this fixed field has a positive gap, every unrestricted shrinking sequence must have \(m(M(w))\to\infty\).

At a sufficiently large reciprocal strict degree record \(a\) of bounded measure, the dichotomy is quantitative without any prior upper bound on the second iterate.
Either \(a\) itself has one of the two displayed patterns up to conjugation and sign, or
\[
                       m(M(a))\,m(a)\gg\frac{\deg a}{(\log(3\deg a))^{212}}
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute implied constant.
The result leaves open both the uniform gaps on the displayed classes and the possibility of unbounded second iterates.

## Following the exterior product down the block chain

By taking the product over a class modulo torsion we may start with the compressed unit \(z_1\), which has \(M(z_1)=M(w)\) and degree at most \(\deg w\).
Use the [chain of compressed half products](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent), with descending terms \(z_1,\ldots,z_s\), leading irreducible summands \(W_i\), and \(V_{z_i}=W_i\oplus V_{z_{i+1}}\) at each nonterminal step.
All terms are reciprocal for sufficiently small \(m(w)\).
Since \(M(w)\) is a signed product of the exterior conjugates of \(w\), it is a nontorsion unit in \(D(w)\).
The [bounded spectrum theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#chain), with ceiling \(T\), implies that its class lies on a nonzero conjugate coordinate line of exactly one \(z_i\), with \(V_{M(w)}=V_{z_i}\).

At a preceding stage \(h<i\), suppose \(M(w)=M(z_h)^{E_h}\) with \(E_h\) a positive integer; initially \(E_1=1\).
The image of the exterior product of \(z_h\) on \(W_h\) vanishes because its class belongs to \(V_{z_i}\).
This stage is necessarily oriented: in the independent case the whole module is \(W_h\).
In a chosen oriented half in every block the exterior product has formal coefficients \(1,0,-1\) according as a representative is exterior, neutral or interior.
Projection onto the blockwise sum-zero spaces subtracts each block's mean, and those spaces embed in the actual module. [Oriented representation](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#oriented)
Vanishing of that projection says that the coefficient is constant within every block.
The [zero-loss norm formula](Yin_C_G0901_v98.md#mass) then gives \(M(z_h)=M(z_{h+1})^{e_h}\) for a positive multiplicity \(e_h\), since compression preserves \(M\).
Induction gives \(M(w)=M(z_i)^E\) for a positive integer \(E\).

The class of \(M(z_i)\) is now a nonzero rational multiple \(t\) of one conjugate coordinate of \(z_i\).
In the independent signed model its formal exterior coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), so \(t=1\) or \(-1\).
In the oriented model subtract \(t\) at the selected coordinate and project to the leading summand.
The result is zero, so the modified coefficient vector is constant on each block.
The difference between the coefficient at the selected coordinate and at any other coordinate of that block is therefore \(t\), giving \(|t|=1\) or \(2\).
This argument allows actual nonzero half products before passing to the leading summand.

The selected conjugate is real: its class is fixed by complex conjugation because the nonzero scalar multiple is represented by a positive real number, and compression separates all the actual conjugate classes.
Choose its inverse when necessary and its sign to obtain \(r>1\).
The real torsion factor can only be a sign, whence \(M(z_i)=r^j\), \(j=|t|\), and \(M(w)=r^{jE}\).
All positive powers of \(r\) have its full degree by the same separation.
Thus \(M(r)=M(z_i)\), \(M(M(w))=M(r)^{jE}=M(w)^j\), and all further iterations follow by the full degrees of the powers.
The product of the exterior conjugates times its rational sign is strictly larger in modulus than each of its distinct conjugates: only the full exterior subset attains that modulus among subsets of the same size. [Exterior product and powers](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field)
Consequently \(r\) is a Perron unit, since \(M(w)\) is a positive power of \(r\) of the same degree.
In particular \(m(M(z_i))=j\,m(z_i)\le2m(w)\).

<a id="minimal"></a>
## Eliminating other nonneutral blocks from a least-degree representative

Inside \(D(w)\) choose a nontorsion unit \(z\) of least degree subject to
\[
                   m(z)\le m(w),\qquad m(M(z))\le2m(w).
\]
The term \(z_i\) just obtained qualifies and has degree at most \(\deg w\).
The minimizing unit is compressed, since compression preserves both measures.
It is reciprocal and its degree tends to infinity uniformly as \(m(w)\to0\), by [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) and bounded-degree finiteness.
Apply the preceding argument to \(z\) at a fixed ceiling, say one, decreasing the original smallness threshold to ensure all conditions.
The resulting coordinate stage must be the first one, because any later one has strictly smaller degree, total measure at most \(m(z)\) and second log measure at most \(2m(z)\).
Choose an exterior real representative of the selected inverse pair \(a\) and put \(r=|a|\).
We have \(M(z)=r^j\) for \(j\in\{1,2\}\).
If \(j=1\), the product of all exterior moduli equals the single factor \(r\), so all other pairs are neutral by reciprocity.
This is the real Salem case at the degrees in question.

The case \(j=2\) requires the oriented model.
Take the half containing \(a\) in its block \(B_*\).
The coefficient of \(a\) is \(1\); since its difference from the common coefficient of the other \(b-1\) positions is \(2\), all those positions have coefficient \(-1\).
All other blocks have constant coefficients.
Write \(R=\log r\), \(I\) for the absolute sum of the \(b-1\) interior logs in the chosen half, and \(J\) for the total exterior log mass of all other blocks.
Then
\[
                 2R=R+I+J,\qquad R-I=J .
\]
Write \(\beta\) for the actual product of this chosen half.
Its modulus is \(e^J\), and the product is real since complex conjugation fixes \(a\), hence preserves its entire half.
If \(J>0\), the two halves over \(B_*\) contribute \(J\) to the exterior log mass of all half products, and the other uniform blocks contribute exactly \(J\).
By transitivity each conjugate half product occurs \(e=2u/\deg\beta\) times in that list.
It follows that \(M(\beta)^e=|\beta|^2\).
The Perron property of exterior products gives
\[
   [\mathbb Q(M(\beta)):\mathbb Q]=[\mathbb Q(M(\beta)^e):\mathbb Q]
          =[\mathbb Q(\beta^2):\mathbb Q]\le\deg\beta .
\]
Absolute height then gives \(m(M(\beta))\le(2/e)m(\beta)\).
The [block norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields) gives \(0<m(\beta)\le m(z)\) and \(\deg\beta\le2u<\deg z\), contradicting the prescribed minimality.
Thus \(J=0\).
Every pair in every other block is neutral, and every signed half product has modulus one, including the half over \(B_*\) because \(R=I\).
All these conjugate units are torsion by Kronecker.
Changing the global sign of the input to make \(a>0\) preserves the pattern and the torsion conclusion, giving the asserted square case.
Ordinary complex conjugation fixes each block: it fixes the label of \(a\), hence its block, and fixes each neutral label outside it.
The block permutation field is totally real and the [fixed-field criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v108.md#few) applies.
In the real Salem case every inverse-pair label is already fixed by complex conjugation.
This proves the reduction and both directions of the shrinking-sequence equivalence.

## Strict records with no ceiling on the second iterate

For a large bounded reciprocal strict record \(a\), the module is the independent signed space or the oriented sum-zero space with torsion half products. [Record module](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules)
If \([M(a)]\) belongs to a coordinate line, the difference-of-coefficients argument above gives \(M(a)=r^j\).
For \(j=2\) the half products being torsion force \(J=0\) directly; \(j=1\) again gives a Salem number.
Otherwise \(M(a)\) is a noncoordinate nontorsion unit in \(D(a)\), so the [quantitative mixed-measure bound](Yin_C_G0901_v103.md#statement) gives (2).
For an integer polynomial of sufficiently small positive logarithmic measure, some nontorsion irreducible factor has positive measure no greater than the original.
In applying the general Lehmer question one may thus study these irreducible inputs, while (1) itself is stated for the measure of a single algebraic number.
