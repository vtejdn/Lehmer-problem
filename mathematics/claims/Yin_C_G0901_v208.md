# Depth and relative degree at primes carrying a Salem translate norm

<a id="cost"></a>
## The relative discriminant pays for simultaneous depths

Let \(K\) be a number field, let \(B\) be an algebraic unit, put \(F=K(B)\), \(n=[F:K]\ge2\) and \(D=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
For a unit \(u\in\mathcal O_K^\times\) set \(z=2-uB\ne0\) and \(H=D^{-1}\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}z|\).
At a prime \(Q\) of \(F\) over \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) over \(p\), use the depth \(v_p(z)=v_Q(z)/e(Q/p)\), the relative degree \(s_Q=[F_Q:K_{\mathfrak p}]\), and \(w_Q=v_Q(z)\log NQ/D\).
Write
\[
 U=\frac{H+\log n}{n}+2(1-1/n)h(B).
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_Q s_Qw_Q\le nU,\qquad
 \sum_{Q\mid p,\ p\in S}\frac{[F_Q:\mathbb Q_p]}{D}\min(v_p(z),t_p)\log p
 \le \sqrt{U\sum_{p\in S}t_p\log p}
\]
for every finite prime set \(S\) and every choice of caps \(t_p\ge0\).
The bounds are uniform in the translating unit \(u\) when \(H\) is fixed.

For each embedding \(\eta:K\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), let \(m_\eta(t)\) count its extensions \(\sigma:F\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) with \(v_p(\sigma z)\ge t\), for \(t>0\).
Write \(\mathbb E_\eta\) for the uniform average over the \([K:\mathbb Q]\) embeddings at this prime.
Two different retained embeddings give \(v_p(\sigma B-\tau B)\ge t\) since \(\eta u\) is a unit.
Let \(\Delta\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}\) be the discriminant of the monic relative minimum of \(B\).
The ordered-pair product of root differences gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty m_\eta(t)(m_\eta(t)-1)\,dt\le v_p(\eta\Delta).
\]
For an archimedean embedding of \(K\), each Vandermonde row on a relative root \(y\) has length at most \(\sqrt n\max(1,|y|)^{n-1}\).
Taking the logarithm of the squared determinant, averaging over the embeddings and using that \(B\) is integral gives
\[
 [K:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Delta|
 \le n\log n+2n(n-1)h(B).
\]
This is the [Vandermonde discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0901_v192.md#packing) applied before averaging over the base field.
The norm formula for \(\Delta\) therefore bounds the sum of the ordered-pair integrals over all primes with weights \(\log p\).
The diagonal contribution \(\sum_p\log p\,\mathbb E_\eta\int m_\eta(t)/n\,dt\) equals \(H\).
Adding it to the pair estimate yields
\[
 \sum_p\log p\,\mathbb E_\eta\int_0^\infty(m_\eta(t)/n)^2\,dt\le U .
\]
Among the counted ordered pairs with the diagonal included, the embeddings inducing a particular \(Q\) at a fixed \(\eta\) occur in a group of size \(s_Q\) and have equal depth.
Their ordered pairs alone give the lower bound \(\sum_Qs_Qw_Q/n\) on the left.
Averaging the first moment up to the caps and applying Cauchy–Schwarz proves the second assertion.

<a id="records"></a>
## One place must be both deep and of small relative degree

On the [shrinking adjacent-record rows](Yin_C_G0901_v200.md#records), take \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), \(u=A^a\) at the second exponent below two and assume the later record is Salem.
Then \(H\to\log 2\), \(n\to\infty\), and \(nh(B)\le d_B h(B)=\log B\to0\), where \(d_B=[\mathbb Q(B):\mathbb Q]\).
In particular \(nU\le H+\log n+2\log B\).
The inequality gives an average relative degree \(O(\log n)\) with weights \(w_Q/H\), and applies even when the earlier record is outside the Salem branch.

Suppose that a fixed finite set \(S\) carries mass at least \(\delta>0\) along a subsequence, and put \(V_S=\sum_{p\in S}\log p\).
At every such row, places with \(s_Q>4nU/\delta\) carry mass at most \(\delta/4\).
At places over \(S\) with \(v_p(z)\le \delta^2/(16UV_S)\), the cap bound discards at most another \(\delta/4\).
Consequently at least \(\delta/2\) of the norm mass survives on places over \(S\) satisfying both
\[
 s_Q\ll_\delta\log n,\qquad v_p(z)\gg_{\delta,S} n/\log n .
\]
Here and in this asymptotic assertion the rows are sufficiently late.
When both records are Salem one may also retain the [single selected place per split trace quartet](Yin_C_G0901_v206.md#trace) at an additional cost \(o(1)\).
The bounds leave open whether a fixed rational prime can carry positive mass for actual adjacent records.

<a id="example"></a>
## Unbounded local depth for two fixed Salem coordinates

Take \(A_0,B_0>1\) from the trace equations \(t^2-t-3=0\) and \(t^2-2t-1=0\) respectively, using the larger trace and the larger root of \(X+X^{-1}=t\) in each case.
Each trace has one conjugate in \((-2,2)\) and one above two, so the discriminant \(t^2-4\) is negative at one real place and gives a quadratic extension of the trace field.
Thus these are quartic Salem numbers with polynomials \(P_A=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1\) and \(P_B=X^4-2X^3+X^2-2X+1\).
Their trace fields are \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})\) and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).
In the totally real compositum \(L_0\) the two elements \(t_A^2-4,t_B^2-4\) and their product each have a negative real conjugate, since either trace field embedding can be chosen independently.
Thus the two square classes are independent and the coordinate field \(L_0(A_0,B_0)\) is biquadratic over \(L_0\).

At \(p=17\) choose the simple roots \(10\) of \(P_A\) and \(5\) of \(P_B\) modulo 17.
They lift to units \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Z_{17}\).
These choices give a common degree-one place since the two traces generate the degree-four compositum and the two square roots have joint degree four over it.
The lift of \(10\) modulo \(17^2\) is \(61\), and \(10^8=-1\bmod17\), \(61^{16}=103\bmod 17^2\).
Therefore \(\alpha\) generates the units modulo \(17^k\) for every \(k\ge1\).
Indeed \(\alpha^{16}-1\) has valuation one, and taking the seventeenth power of any \(1+17^jv\) with unit \(v\) and \(j\ge1\) increases this valuation by exactly one by the binomial expansion.
For every \(k\) choose a positive \(a_k\) with \(\alpha^{a_k}\beta=2\bmod17^k\).
The traces \(T_k=\alpha^{a_k}+\alpha^{-a_k}\) and \(S=\beta+\beta^{-1}\) reduce to \(8,12\bmod17\) respectively.
Both avoid \(2,-2\), and among the four products obtained by inversions exactly one is \(2\bmod17\).
The [trace norm formula](Yin_C_G0901_v206.md#conic) now gives
\[
 v_{17}(4T_k^2-10T_kS+4S^2+9)=v_{17}(2-\alpha^{a_k}\beta)\ge k .
\]
In this example the primes and coordinate fields stay fixed and the large valuations occur at a smooth point of the conic, as its derivative \(8T_k-10S\) is nonzero modulo 17.
The exponents have no archimedean upper cutoff and the Salem numbers are fixed, so the example gives no shrinking adjacent-record rows.
