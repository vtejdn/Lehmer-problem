# Discriminant weights at cubic contacts in a rectangle of primitive directions

<a id="statement"></a>
## All cubic contacts up to a prime cutoff

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) with positive coprime exponents \(k\le K,\ l\le L\), and let \(H(k,l)\) be the [degree-eighteen branch form](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#statement).
Use the fixed exceptional prime set of that result, enlarged to include all primes below 17, and denote it by \(S\).
For squarefree \(P\) let \(w_{p,3}\) be the contribution to \(v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\) of all geometric triple residue roots, counted by the discriminants of their three-root factors over the unramified splitting field.
For every real \(Y\ge2\) one can omit at most \(108\pi(Y)\) primitive directions from the entire rectangle so that at every remaining squarefree section with \(H(k,l)\ne0\),
\[
 \sum_{p\le Y,\ p\notin S} w_{p,3}\log p
 \le 6\omega(|H(k,l)|)\log(2KL)
 \ll_F \log(2KL)\log(2+K+L).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\omega\) counts distinct prime divisors, including \(\omega(1)=0\).
This includes the even resonances of the branch-form estimate.
In any monic integral factorization, the part of the logarithmic complementary resultant from the same disks is at most half the left side of (1).
On a primitive graph \(M\le k<2M,\ l\le(2M)^A\) with fixed \(A>0\), taking \(Y=M\) gives cubic weight \(O_{F,A}(\log^2 M)\) outside \(O_F(M/\log M)\) indices at squarefree sections.
For the full square \(1\le k,l\le M\), taking \(Y=M^2\) gives the same order of weight with \(O_F(M^2/\log M)\) exceptional directions.
In both applications the finitely many primitive zeros of the nonzero binary form may be included among the exceptions.

<a id="values"></a>
## The two critical values of a cubic section

Fix a geometric ramification point \((u,v)\) modulo \(p\notin S\) and its unique unramified lift on the ramification divisor with \(U\)-coordinate \(\beta\) and slope \(s_0\), as in the [branch calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#cubic).
Let \(O\) be the unramified integer ring of the residue point, use brackets for Teichmüller lifts, and normalize \(v_p(p)=1\).
The slope is finite, so \(U\) is an étale parameter and the curve is \(V=f(U)\) with an integral expansion in \(z=U-\beta\) and unit constant term.
Write
\[
 g(U)=Uf'(U)/f(U)=s_0+z^2 e(z),\qquad e\in O[[z]]^\times.
\]
For a direction reducing to this slope write \(s=l/k\), where \(p\nmid k\) and \(v_p(s-s_0)\ge1\).
Formal Weierstrass preparation of \(g(U)-s\) gives a unit times a distinguished quadratic in \(z\).
Its two roots give coordinates \(c_1,c_2\) in the open disk, counted with multiplicity, and \((c_j-\beta)^2 e(c_j-\beta)=s-s_0\) places both in \(v_p(U-\beta)\ge1/2\).

A compatible residue pair satisfies \(u^l=v^k\) and determines a unique residue coordinate \(x\) by Bézout's identity.
In its disk \(X=[x](U/[u])^{1/k}\) and \(X^l=[v](U/[u])^s\), using integral binomial series for exponents in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
The changes between \(X\) and \(U\) preserve valuations of differences, since both expansions have integral coefficients and unit linear terms.
The section equation becomes
\[
 J_s(U)=f(U)/\big([v](U/[u])^s\big)-1,\qquad
 J_s'(U)=(1+J_s(U))(g(U)-s)/U.
\]
A triple contact gives a unit times a distinguished cubic for \(J_s\) by the [local multiplicity calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#cubic).
If \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) are its three root coordinates, the valuation \(d\) of the disk discriminant is \(\sum_i v_p(J_s'(a_i))\), by differentiation of the prepared product at its roots.
The derivative identity and quadratic preparation give
\[
 d=\sum_{i,j} v_p(a_i-c_j)=\sum_{j=1}^2 v_p(J_s(c_j)).              \tag{2}
\]
The second equality evaluates the prepared cubic at the two critical points.
All formal unit factors used here have unit values throughout the open disk.

Define the direction-independent function
\[
 B(U)=\log(f(U)/[v])-g(U)\log(U/[u]).
\]
At \(U=c_j\) both principal-unit arguments differ from 1 by elements of valuation at least \(1/2\), as does \(1+J_s(c_j)\).
On this disk \(1/2>1/(p-1)\), so the logarithm preserves valuations of principal-unit increments and takes binomial powers to scalar multiples.
It follows that \(v_p(J_s(c_j))=v_p(B(c_j))\) in (2).

<a id="centers"></a>
## Three fixed slope centers on a deep branch disk

Write \(B(\beta+z)=\sum h_n z^n\) and \(D(z)=\log((\beta+z)/[u])\).
Then \(h_0\in pO\), \(D(0)\in pO\), and differentiation gives \(B'=-g'D\).
Writing \(g'=2e(0)z+3e'(0)z^2+\cdots\) and \(D=D(0)+z/\beta+\cdots\) gives \(h_1=0,\ h_2\in pO\) and \(3h_3\equiv-2e(0)/\beta\pmod p\), a unit.
For \(j\ge1\) the coefficient of \(z^j\) in \(D\) is \((-1)^{j-1}/(j\beta^j)\).
Multiplication by the integral series \(g'\) and integration therefore give \(v_p(h_n)\ge-v_p(n)-\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\) for \(n\ge1\).
Let \(\pi^2=p\).
For \(4\le n<p\), \(h_n\pi^{n-3}\) is in the maximal ideal, and for \(n\ge p\) its valuation is at least \((n-3)/2-2\log_p n>0\), tending to infinity since \(p\ge17\) and this lower bound is increasing there.
Consequently the terms of positive degree in \(B(\beta+\pi T)\) have valuations at least \(3/2\) on \(v_p(T)\ge0\).
If \(v_p(h_0)=1\), (2) gives \(d=2\) throughout the compatible slope class.

Otherwise \(v_p(h_0)\ge2\), including the possibility \(h_0=0\), since \(O\) is unramified.
The restricted integral series \(B(\beta+\pi T)/\pi^3\) has reduction of degree three, with leading coefficient a unit.
The [restricted-series preparation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v846.md#centers), dividing each successive error polynomial by the reduced monic cubic over the residue field of \(O[\pi]\), gives a monic cubic times a restricted unit whose reduction is a nonzero constant.
For its integral roots \(t_i\), counted with multiplicity, set \(b_i=\beta+\pi t_i\) and \(\lambda_i=g(b_i)\), for \(1\le i\le3\).
Rescaling the three linear factors gives \(v_p(B(c_j))=\sum_i v_p(c_j-b_i)\).
Evaluating the prepared quadratic for \(g(U)-s\) at each \(b_i\) now yields the exact identity
\[
 d=\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{j=1}^2 v_p(b_i-c_j)=\sum_{i=1}^3 v_p(s-\lambda_i).  \tag{3}
\]
These centers depend only on \(p\) and the geometric ramification point.
Squarefreeness ensures that every term in (2) and (3) is finite at a compatible section.

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Exceptional directions and the branch form

For each prime \(p\le Y\) outside \(S\), use the three centers in (3) for every deep branch disk and the single center \(s_0\) for every disk with \(v_p(h_0)=1\).
For each center \(\lambda\) omit directions with \(p\nmid k\) and \(v_p(l/k-\lambda)>\log_p(2KL)\).
Two distinct primitive directions passing the same test would have a nonzero integer determinant \(lk'-l'k\) of absolute value at most \(KL\) and valuation greater than \(\log_p(2KL)\).
Thus each center removes at most one direction, including centers over ramified extensions.
The slope map has exactly 36 geometric ramification points, giving at most 108 exceptions per prime.
At every remaining direction a deep disk costs at most \(3\log(2KL)\) by (3).
For a shallow disk we have \(1\le v_p(s-s_0)\le\log_p(2KL)\), so its cost \(2\log p\) is at most \(2\log(2KL)\), even when the cutoff exceeds the rectangle area.
The [branch classification](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#statement) supplies at most two compatible cubic disks at each prime, all supported on \(p\mid H(k,l)\).
This proves (1) since \(|H(k,l)|\ll_F (K+L)^{18}\) and \(2^{\omega(|H|)}\le |H|\).
A complementary resultant uses only cross-factor differences and hence at most half of the disk discriminant valuation.
The graph and square estimates follow from \(\pi(Y)\ll Y/\log(2Y)\).
