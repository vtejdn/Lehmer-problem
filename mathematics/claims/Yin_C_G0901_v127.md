# Positive averages of Salem power traces in a proper subfield have a large integral minimum

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower degree and the cost of averaging

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2n\), with \(0<m=\log\alpha\le M\) for a fixed \(M\).
Put \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) in its distinguished real embedding, and take any intermediate field \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subsetneq B\).
Write \(u=[E:\mathbb Q]\), \(b=[B:E]\), and \(*\) for the distinguished embedding of \(E\).
For positive integers \(h\) put
\[
       J_h=\frac1b\operatorname{Tr}_{B/E}(\alpha^h+\alpha^{-h})\in E .
\]
Take any finite family of nonnegative rational coefficients \(c_h\) with \(\sum c_h\le1\).
Uniformly in the field, the exponents, the number of terms and their coefficient denominators, if \(Y=\sum c_hJ_h\) belongs to \(\mathcal O_E\), then for large \(n\) either \(Y=0\) or it generates \(E\) and is the trace of a Salem number \(\eta>1\) with
\[
          \deg\eta=2u<2n,\qquad
          \log\eta\ \ge\ (u-1)\bigl(\tfrac12-o_M(1)\bigr)\log b .       \tag{1}
\]
The error tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\), and \(b\to\infty\) uniformly.
In every such subfield of a given Salem trace field, the individual \(J_h\) actually produce infinitely many distinct integral Salem traces as \(h\) varies.
Thus this descent in degree cannot produce a smaller Salem measure along a bounded-measure sequence of growing degrees.
The [comparison over a common subfield](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#comparison) applies also to unrelated constructions; (1) uses the specified averages even for small \(u\).

With arbitrary signs there is instead an exact identity in every degree:
\[
 \left\{\sum_{h\ge1}c_hJ_h:\ c_h\in\mathbb Q,\ \text{finite support},
                                    \ \sum |c_h|\le1\right\}
       =\{Y\in E:\ |\tau(Y)|<2\ \text{at all }\tau\ne *\}.              \tag{2}
\]
At most \(u+1\) nonzero terms suffice to represent any member on the right.
In particular signed averages can represent any already available smaller Salem trace with this marked place in \(E\).
The identity imposes no bound on the exponents needed and gives no existence theorem for a small integral trace in the lower field.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Uniform phase sums inside the individual trace fibers

The \(n\) trace embeddings label the inverse pairs of conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Distinct roots cannot have torsion quotient, because moving one to the unique exterior root would then give another root of the same modulus.
The labels in the [canonical block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) are consequently the actual inverse pairs.
The \(u\) fibers over embeddings of \(E\) are blocks of size \(b\ge2\).
Each such block contains a minimal nontrivial block by descending inclusion.
Its setwise stabilizer acts transitively on its labels, so the unique canonical partition refines the fiber partition.
The cited theorem therefore gives
\[
                  u\ll_M (\log(3n))^{212},\qquad b=n/u .
\]
In the remainder all estimates are uniform over these choices of \(E\).

For one fiber let \(P_\tau\) be the monic polynomial of its \(2b\) actual roots, including both roots above each trace.
Its coefficients are the \(\tau\)-images of integral elements of \(E\): the fibers are the orbits over the marked embedding on trace labels, and adjoining both lifts preserves invariance under every automorphism fixing \(E\).
The product over all fibers of their nonzero absolute discriminants is therefore the absolute value of a nonzero rational integer.
Write a root in any fiber as \(x_j=\exp(t_j+i\theta_j)\) and put \(z_j=\exp(-|t_j|+i\theta_j)\).
Set \(\rho=\exp(-1/(2b))\).
The [reflected discriminant identity](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) applied within each fiber gives
\[
 \sum_\tau\sum_{h\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2h}}h
                \left|\sum_{j\in\tau} z_j^h\right|^2
       \ll b m +u b\log(3b).                                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed for distinct members of a fiber,
\(\log|x_i-x_j|\le t_i^++t_j^++\log(1/\rho)+\log|1-\rho^2\bar z_i z_j|\).
Sum over ordered distinct pairs in that fiber, retain the negative log discriminant on the right of the Fourier estimate and add at most \(2b\log(1/(1-\rho^2))\) from the diagonal.
Summing over fibers drops the sum of negative log discriminants by the integer norm, although an individual discriminant can have modulus below one.
The sum of the positive \(t_j\) over all fibers is exactly \(m\).
This proves (3) without assuming independent arithmetic estimates on each fiber.

For \(h\le b\), positivity in (3) bounds each fiber sum by \(O(\sqrt{h b(m+u\log(3b))})\).
All original roots coincide with their reflected values except for the single exterior root; the correction in its \(h\)-th power is exactly \(2\sinh(hm)\).
Thus
\[
 \left|\tau(J_h)-\mathbf1_{\tau=*}\frac{2\sinh(hm)}b\right|
          \ll\sqrt{\frac{h(m+u\log(3b))}{b}} .                           \tag{4}
\]
At every positive exponent, without a cutoff, the nondistinguished values of \(J_h\) are strictly in \((-2,2)\); a nontorsion circle root cannot have a power equal to either endpoint sign.
At the distinguished embedding one always has \(J_h^*\ge e^{hm}/b-2\), in particular \(J_h^*>-2\).

<a id="positive"></a>
## Unbounded powers, positive mixtures and integrality

Call an exponent small here when \(hm\le2u\log b\).
The [Voutier bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) at the original degree implies \(1/m\ll(\log(3n))^3\) for large \(n\).
Thus every such \(h\) is at most a fixed power of \(\log(3n)\) with a constant depending only on \(M\), and eventually \(h\le b\).
By (4) every nondistinguished embedding value at these exponents is bounded in modulus by
\[
             \varepsilon_n\le b^{-1/2}\,O_M((\log(3n))^{215})
                               =b^{-1/2+o_M(1)} .
\]
For example \(h\ll u(\log(3n))^4\) and \(m+u\log(3b)\ll_M u\log(3n)\), giving the claimed logarithmic exponent.

At each remaining exponent \(J_h^*\ge b^{2u-1}-2\).
If \(Y^*> b^u\) this already large value of the mixture itself suffices for (1).
Otherwise, writing \(w\) for its total coefficient weight on the remaining exponents, positivity and \(\sum c_h\le1\) give
\[
                  wb^{2u-1}-2\le Y^*\le b^u,\qquad w\ll b^{1-u}.
\]
In every nondistinguished embedding one now has \(|\tau(Y)|\le\varepsilon_n+2w\le b^{-1/2+o_M(1)}\).
If the integral \(Y\) is nonzero, its norm has absolute value at least one, giving
\[
               |Y^*|\ge b^{(u-1)(1/2-o_M(1))}.
\]
Since \(Y^*>-2\), this large value is positive.
In both cases the nondistinguished values remain strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\) by the positive mixture property.
Thus \(Y^*>2\) is the unique exceptional trace embedding and \(Y\) generates \(E\), since a value from a proper subfield would recur at multiple embeddings.
The quadratic \(X^2-YX+1\) is irreducible over \(E\) by negativity of its discriminant at a nondistinguished real place.
Its exterior root is a Salem unit and \(\eta\ge Y^*/2\), proving (1).

For actual attainment choose a positive period of \(\alpha\) in the finite unit group of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)}/b\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)}\).
At every positive multiple \(h\) of that period the element \((\alpha^h+\alpha^{-h}-2)/b\) is integral and belongs to \(B\), so \(J_h\in\mathcal O_E\).
The distinguished values tend to infinity along the multiples and all others stay strictly in \((-2,2)\), giving infinitely many different Salem traces of degree \(u\).
This last argument also works at fixed degree without the large-degree bounds.

<a id="signed"></a>
## Why allowing signed coefficients removes the restriction

Take one root on each neutral inverse pair of the original Salem number.
These \(n-1\) complex numbers have no nontrivial monomial equal to a root of unity.
Indeed an automorphism taking a chosen member to \(\alpha\) takes every other member to a neutral root, so absolute values force the chosen exponent to be zero.
Their simultaneous positive integer powers are dense in the full \((n-1)\)-torus: the average of each nonconstant character tends to zero by the finite geometric sum, and trigonometric approximation gives uniform distribution.
On each nondistinguished fiber \(J_h\) is the average of the real parts of these powers times two.
The coordinates involved in different fibers are disjoint.
Consequently the vectors of nondistinguished values are dense in \([-2,2]^{u-1}\), with arbitrarily large indices realizing every open target box.
The distinguished coordinate tends to positive infinity as the index tends to infinity for the fixed input.

In the Minkowski embedding of \(E\) write \(v_h\) for the vector of \(J_h\) with first coordinate distinguished.
Let \(C\) be the real convex hull of \(0\) and all \(\pm v_h\).
For any \(s\in\mathbb R\) and any target in the closed nondistinguished box choose \(h_j\to\infty\) approximating the target there, then \(k_j\) with \(v_{k_j}^*/v_{h_j}^*\to\infty\).
The points
\[
   (1-\lambda_j)v_{h_j}-\lambda_j v_{k_j},\qquad
             \lambda_j=\frac{v_{h_j}^*-s}{v_{h_j}^*+v_{k_j}^*},
\]
are in \(C\) eventually, have first coordinate \(s\) and converge to the target in the other coordinates since \(\lambda_j\to0\).
Thus the closure of \(C\) is the entire closed slab.
Every point of its interior belongs to \(C\): choose a small simplex strictly surrounding it in the slab interior and approximate the vertices from \(C\), preserving strict containment.
Conversely every finite convex combination in \(C\) has all its nondistinguished coordinates strictly between the endpoints.

If the desired point comes from an element of \(E\), use a finite convex representation and discard affine dependencies among its supporting vertices by shifting the coefficients until one vanishes.
The remaining at most \(u+1\) vertices are affinely independent.
In any rational basis of \(E\) these vertices and the desired point have rational coordinates, so their unique barycentric coefficients are rational.
Combining signs gives (2).
