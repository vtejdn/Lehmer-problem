# Sharp simultaneous double-contact cost at every large exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## Powers of one reciprocal unit

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit satisfying \(\mathbb Q(\delta^k)=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) for every positive integer \(k\).
Fix a nonempty finite set \(I\) of distinct positive integers, and write \(P_a\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\delta^a\), \(h=\log M(\delta)>0\), and \(T=\sum_{a\in I}a\).
Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\).
For every sequence of integer widths \(b(n)\to\infty\), let \(H_n\) be the least one-norm of a nonzero integer polynomial \(A\) of degree at most \(b=b(n)\) such that
\[
 \prod_{a\in I}P_a^2\ \bigm|\ A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
 A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y),\qquad A(\delta^{an})\ne0\quad(a\in I).
\]
These rows exist for every sufficiently large integer \(n\), and
\[
                       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_n}{n}=Th .
\]
For each \(\gamma>0\) one fixed width admits such rows with \(\log\|A\|_1/n\le Th+\gamma\) at every sufficiently large integer \(n\).

Write \(B=(\log2)/4\), let \(\lambda\) be the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures, and let \(D(n,b)\) be the [fixed-double count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) for this sign.
For \(r\ge1\) and \(b(n)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\), if \(D(n,b(n))<r\) on any infinite subsequence, then \(\lambda\ge B/[r(r+1)]\).
In particular infinitely many such exclusions on the exponents \(j!-1\) suffice, and the bound for \(r=3\) is \((\log2)/48\).

<a id="matrices"></a>
## A determinant at each shared exterior radius

Any two distinct roots in the combined list of the \(P_a\) have quotient of infinite order.
Indeed, if conjugates \(x,y\) of \(\delta\) satisfy \(x^{an}=y^{a'n}\) for some \(n>0\), equality of the absolute logarithmic heights gives \(an h_{\rm abs}(\delta)=a'n h_{\rm abs}(\delta)\), so \(a=a'\).
The assumption on power fields then gives \(x=y\).
In particular \(m(P_a)=ah\), the \(P_a\) are distinct, and the powers \(c^n\) of distinct roots remain distinct for every \(n\ge1\).

Consider a nonzero two-row graph \(U_0(X^n)-X V_0(X^n)\) with integer coefficients and arbitrary common width.
At a common exterior radius \(R>1\) let \(c\) range over the selected roots with \(|c|=R\), and put \(z_c(n)=(R/c)^n\).
The phases are distinct at every positive integer \(n\).
They lie on the unit circle and are algebraic since \(R^2=c\bar c\).
Suppose a sequence of such graphs at increasing \(n\) has bounded normalized log norm, where the norm is the larger of the two row one-norms.
Use the [coefficient-index construction](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) after reversal and removal of common initial zeros.
Writing \(w_j=(u_j,v_j)^t\), pass to its finite coefficient-index limits \(\gamma_j\) and form \(L(s)=\max_j(\gamma_j-js)\).
At \(s=\log R\), let \(k\) and \(k+d\) be the first and last maximizing indices, so \(d=0\) permits a unique maximizer.

Scale \(w_{k+\ell}\) by \(R^{-\ell n}\) for \(0\le\ell\le d\) and divide by the largest norm of these vectors.
Use the resulting coordinates as the coefficients of \(U,V\), respectively, so the maximum coefficient magnitude of this pair is one.
At \(z=z_c(n)\) its value equation and, at each double node, its derivative equation have the form
\[
 U(z)-cV(z)=O(e^{-\eta n}),\qquad
 n z\big(U'(z)-cV'(z)\big)+cV(z)=O(e^{-\eta n}).                       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\eta>0\) may depend on the subsequence.
Indeed, in the reversed variable \(t=c^{-n}\), the exact derivative equation is \(n\sum_j j(u_j-cv_j)t^j=-c\sum_jv_jt^j\).
Subtract \(nk\) times the exact value equation before scaling.
In the finite truncation every index outside the maximizing interval has strictly smaller limiting slope.
The uniform geometric tail from the coefficient-index construction, also with the factor \(nj\), is exponentially small relative to the largest retained term, whose logarithmic slope at \(s\) is \(L(s)\ge\gamma_0\ge0\).
These estimates give (1) at every selected node after decreasing \(\eta\).

For \(m\ge1\) double nodes, test the \(2m\) equations on pairs of polynomials of degrees at most \(m-1\).
For \(m\ge0\) double nodes together with two further distinct simple nodes, test the \(2m+2\) equations on pairs of degrees at most \(m\).
In each case the system is square.
Write \(D(X,\boldsymbol z)\) for its determinant when the multiplier \(n\) in the derivative rows is replaced by an indeterminate \(X\).
At \(X=0\) the derivative row is exactly \(cV(z)\).
For every positive integer \(a_0\) one therefore has
\[
                             D(0,\boldsymbol z(a_0))\ne0 .                \tag{2}
\]
For the first system the \(m\) derivative rows force \(V\) to vanish at \(m\) distinct phases, and the value rows then do the same for \(U\).
For the second system the values and derivative rows at the \(m\) double nodes force \(U=uL_0,\ V=vL_0\), where \(L_0(Y)\) is the product of \(Y-z\) over the double nodes.
The two simple phases are distinct from these zeros, so the two remaining equations give \(u=c_1v=c_2v\) with \(c_1\ne c_2\).
Thus \(u=v=0\), also for \(m=0\).

<a id="recurrences"></a>
## Subexponential lower bounds for the exact determinants

Expanding in phase monomials and collecting identical bases gives
\[
                D(X,\boldsymbol z(n))=\sum_\beta p_\beta(X)\beta^n
\]
with algebraic polynomials and finitely many distinct algebraic bases of modulus one.
Choose \(q\ge1\) divisible by the orders of all torsion ratios of these bases.
Fix \(a_0\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\).
On \(n=a_0+qt\) group the terms with the same \(\theta=\beta^q\), defining \(Q_\theta(X)=\sum_{\beta^q=\theta}\beta^{a_0}p_\beta(X)\).
The sum of the \(Q_\theta(0)\) is \(D(0,\boldsymbol z(a_0))\ne0\) by (2), including when \(a_0=q\).
Consequently some \(Q_\theta(a_0+qt)\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(t\).
After omitting identically zero coefficients, the exact determinant at \(X=n\) is the nondegenerate recurrence \(\sum_\theta Q_\theta(a_0+qt)\theta^t\).
Indeed a torsion ratio of two remaining bases would already come from a torsion ratio of the original bases, which have equal \(q\)-th powers in that case.
The bases all have complex modulus one.

[Xiao's Lemma 4.1](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) now gives \(\log|D(n,\boldsymbol z(n))|\ge-o(n)\) on this progression, with eventual nonvanishing.
More explicitly, for each \(\xi>0\) it is eventually at least \(-\xi n\).
The finitely many residue classes give the same conclusion through all positive integers.
The entries in the exact system grow at most linearly in \(n\), so its cofactors grow at most polynomially.
The inverse matrix thus has norm at most \(\exp(o(n))\).
This argument uses the polynomial-coefficient version of the recurrence estimate on the exact derivative rows in (1).
The leading derivative equations obtained by removing \(cV(z)\) would discard the term that establishes (2).

If \(d\le m-1\) in the first case or \(d\le m\) in the second, complete the two coefficient vectors by zeros through the stated test degree.
Their concatenation has norm one, while (1) and the inverse bound give norm \(O(\exp(-\eta n+o(n)))\).
This contradiction proves an index jump of at least \(m\) at any radius with \(m\) double nodes, and at least \(m+1\) if two further simple nodes occur there.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Retaining the prescribed factors at every exponent and width

Integrating the index jumps of \(L\) gives \(L(0)-\gamma_0\), the sum of their sizes times their positive breakpoints.
For double contacts at the full list, the exterior roots contribute at least \(\sum_{a\in I}m(P_a)=Th\) to this sum.
Since \(\gamma_0\ge0\) and \(L(0)\) is bounded above by the limiting normalized log norm on a convergent subsequence, this proves the lower bound \(Th\) even for arbitrary widths.

For each \(a\in I\), lower \(P_a\) to a simple factor and keep the remaining factors double.
If \(x\) is a nonreal exterior conjugate of \(\delta\), its powers \(x^a,\bar x^a\) are distinct by the power-field assumption.
Their radius therefore contributes one additional jump beyond the double-node count, giving lower slope at least \((T-a)h+a\log|x|\).
When every exterior conjugate is real, two distinct exterior roots from the list cannot have equal modulus, since their quotient would be \(1\) or \(-1\).
The [real simple-zero estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#simple) at any exterior root of \(P_a\), with the other factors' exterior roots double, gives lower slope strictly greater than \((T-a)h\) in this case as well.
Thus for each \(a\) there is some \(\eta_a>0\) such that every sufficiently late two-row graph with these weakened contacts has normalized log norm at least \((T-a)h+\eta_a\).

For a desired excess \(\gamma>0\), choose a positive excess smaller than both \(\gamma\) and every \(\eta_a\).
The [integral double-jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) with \(t_a=2\) supplies a fixed width \(w\) and nonzero two-row graphs with all double factors and normalized log one-norm at most \(Th\) plus that smaller excess for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Its bound applies to the selected list with \(2\sum_a m(P_a)=2Th\).
If both rows contained the minimum \(Q_{a,n}\) of \(\delta^{an}\), divide it from both rows.
The quotient graph has the weakened contacts just considered, since \(Q_{a,n}(X^n)\) is separable and disjoint from the other orbits.
The quotient row of larger one-norm has log Mahler measure at least its log one-norm minus \(w\log 2\).
Restoring \(Q_{a,n}\) adds \(nah\) to that measure and hence forces original normalized log norm at least \(Th+\eta_a-w\log(2)/n\), a contradiction.
Vanishing of either row at a powered root would force vanishing of both by the graph equation.
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) consequently preserve all double factors and all numerator nonvanishings at the prescribed sign, increasing width by one and norm by a fixed factor.
This gives the claimed fixed-width upper bound with any prescribed positive slack.

For every \(b\ge w+1\), write \(b-(w+1)=2k+e\) with \(e\in\{0,1\}\).
The [reciprocal width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\) preserves the graph factors and the nonzero numerator values, since all selected values are nontorsion units.
Its one-norm is at most two.
Taking the fixed-width slack arbitrarily small proves the upper limit for every \(b(n)\to\infty\).
An attained minimum exists whenever rows exist, because their one-norms are positive integers.

Finally, if \(\lambda<B/[r(r+1)]\), choose a positive irreducible logarithmic measure \(h\) strictly below that threshold.
The [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) and [all-power representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) give a reciprocal unit \(\delta\) as above with measure \(h\).
Taking \(I=\{1,\ldots,r\}\) gives \(2Th<B\).
For \(b(n)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\), the constructed rows eventually satisfy \(2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)<nB\), and their selected factors have the \(r\) distinct positive measures \(ah\).
This proves the asserted implication on every infinite subsequence.

The coordinating agent and a proof-development agent for Sheng Yin obtained the all-exponent result by retaining the exact derivative term in the [bounded-gap argument](Yin_C_G0345_v432.md#rank) and applying Xiao's local growth estimate.
