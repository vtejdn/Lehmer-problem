# A degree-halving reduction for real exterior conjugates in blocks of four

<a id="statement"></a>
## A reciprocal unit with half as many exterior conjugates

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(r\ge1\) exterior roots, all real.
Suppose the [blocks determined by the Galois translates of the exterior labels](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) have size four and their relation lattices have rank two.
A label denotes an inverse pair, and a relation means that the indicated monomial in pair representatives is a root of unity.
In particular \(4\mid m,r\).

If two exterior roots are opposite, \(P(z)=Q(z^2)\) for a monic irreducible reciprocal \(Q\) of degree \(m\) with \(r/2\) positive exterior roots and \(M(Q)=M(P)\).
Otherwise there exists a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial \(Q\) of degree exactly \(m\) with exactly \(r/2\) exterior roots, all positive, such that
\[
                        0<\log M(Q)\le\tfrac12\log M(P).
\]
In this second case, in an integral basis of the rank-two exponent quotient of each block, its four generating label vectors are, up to individual signs and permutation,
\[
                           (a,b),\quad(a,-b),\quad(b,a),\quad(b,-a)
\]
for the same coprime integers \(a>b\ge1\) of opposite parity.
The block stabilizer acts by the eight signed coordinate permutations in this basis.
The polynomial \(Q\) arises from a squared monomial representing a basis vector, and satisfies the stronger bound \(\log M(Q)\le \log M(P)/a\).

For \(r=4\), every input with all exterior roots real either admits one of the degree-halving reductions to a unit with two positive exterior roots, or has [positive-density interlacing powers](#four) in its original degree with four unused exterior traces when \(q=m-4\ge2\).
The small values of \(q\) are described below.

<a id="plane"></a>
## The finite group acting on four rational lines

Use the splitting field and Galois group \(G\) of \(P\).
The block decomposition gives an integral direct sum of the lattices of relations modulo torsion, with saturated summands and a transitive action of the setwise stabilizer \(G_B\) on the four labels of a block \(B\).
Every block relation has value \(1\) or \(-1\), since one can transport the block into the real exterior set.
Each block is either wholly exterior or wholly neutral.
The [opposite-root substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#three) already proves the first alternative, so assume that the real exterior roots have distinct absolute values.

Let \(L=\mathbb Z^4/\mathcal R_B\) for the block relation lattice \(\mathcal R_B\).
It is free of rank two.
The four signed label images generate \(L\), and their rational lines in \(L_{\mathbb Q}\) are distinct.
Indeed a zero image would give a torsion root, and a coincident line would give a relation on two labels.
After transporting the block into the exterior set, proportional logarithms of two real exterior conjugates would force equal absolute values by the [conjugate log-vector argument](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#three).
The image \(J\) of \(G_B\) on \(L\) is finite and is transitive on these four lines.
Averaging a positive definite inner product makes it orthogonal, so its orientation-preserving subgroup is cyclic of order \(n\).
A generating rotation has integer trace \(2\cos\theta\) in \([-2,2]\), which gives \(n\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).
If there is an orientation-reversing element it is a reflection, so the whole group has order \(2n\), otherwise \(n\).
For even \(n\), the rotation through \(\pi\) is \(-I\) and acts trivially on lines.
The orbit of four lines requires the order of the projective image to be divisible by four.
Among the listed possibilities this requires \(n=4\) and a reflection.
Thus \(J\) is the order-eight dihedral group.

Write \(R\) for a quarter-turn and \(S\) for a reflection in \(J\).
Choose a rational nonzero vector \(e\) fixed by \(S\).
Then \(e,Re\) is a rational basis, and \(R^2=-I,\ SRS=R^{-1}\) show that \(J\) consists of all signed coordinate permutations in this basis.
All four label vectors are in one orbit up to sign because \(G_B\) permutes the original roots up to inversion.
Rescale the two basis vectors by the same rational factor, and interchange or negate axes, so one label has coprime integer coordinates \(a>b\ge1\).
Zero coordinates or equal absolute coordinates would give fewer than four lines.
The four elements in the statement, up to signs, are exactly the labels in these coordinates.

Their integer span contains \(2(1,0)\) and \(2(0,1)\) by adding and subtracting sign pairs and using \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
If \(a,b\) have opposite parity their residue classes and their transposes span modulo two, making this lattice all of \(\mathbb Z^2\).
If both are odd it is exactly the lattice of points whose coordinate sum is even.
Use \((1,1),(1,-1)\) as its basis.
The signed coordinate permutations still act as all signed coordinate permutations, while the positive label parameters become \((a+b)/2,(a-b)/2\).
These are coprime by the original coprimality and have opposite parity since their sum is \(a\).
This proves the asserted normal form in an integral basis of the actual quotient \(L\).
Transport that basis to each other block by an element of \(G\).
The possible choices differ by signed coordinate permutations and give the same parameters.

<a id="conjugates"></a>
## Conjugates of a squared coordinate monomial

In a chosen exterior block lift the first basis vector \(e_1\in L\) to an integral exponent vector.
Evaluate its monomial in the chosen roots and square the result, obtaining an algebraic unit \(\gamma\).
This is independent of the lift, since two lifts differ by a block relation of value \(\pm1\).
More generally, taking the square defines an injective map from the full exponent quotient modulo torsion to algebraic units in the splitting field.
It is well-defined by the sign-valued relations of the [real block decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection), and injective since a monomial of square one was already torsion.
It commutes with the Galois action.
Thus the distinct conjugates of \(\gamma\) are indexed by the actual orbit of \(e_1\) in the full exponent quotient.
There are four in each block, represented in its transported basis by \(e_1,-e_1,e_2,-e_2\).
They are distinct in that block by the free rank-two quotient and distinct across blocks by the direct sum.
The orbit has exactly \(4(m/4)=m\) elements, and it is closed under negation in the exponent quotient.
The minimal polynomial \(Q\) of \(\gamma\) is therefore monic integral, irreducible of degree \(m\) and reciprocal, with the two opposite exponent vectors giving mutually inverse conjugates.

On a neutral block all these conjugates have modulus one.
On an exterior block the original roots are real and the squared monomials are positive.
Let \(\ell_1,\ell_2\) be the logarithms of the absolute values of unsquared monomial representatives of its two basis vectors.
They are well-defined since the lifts differ by signs.
They are rationally independent, since any zero integral combination on this real block makes the product a real unit of absolute value one and gives a relation in the quotient.
With \(X=|\ell_1|\), \(Y=|\ell_2|\), exactly two of the four positive conjugates on this block are exterior and their summed logarithmic moduli equal \(2(X+Y)\).
For \(P\), one member of each inverse pair is exterior, so the contribution of the four original pairs is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  &|a\ell_1+b\ell_2|+|a\ell_1-b\ell_2|
     +|b\ell_1+a\ell_2|+|b\ell_1-a\ell_2|\\
  &\hspace{2em}=2\max(aX,bY)+2\max(bX,aY)\ \ge\ 2a(X+Y).
 \end{aligned}
\]
There are \(r/4\) exterior blocks, with the same \(a,b\) but possibly different \(X,Y\).
Summing gives exactly \(r/2\) positive exterior roots of \(Q\) and the stated Mahler measure bound, since \(a\ge2\).

<a id="example"></a>
## Equality for the squared-axis construction

Let \(t_1=(9+\sqrt5)/2,\ t_2=(9-\sqrt5)/2\) and let \(\rho_i>1\) satisfy \(\rho_i+\rho_i^{-1}=t_i\).
The elements \(\Delta_i=t_i^2-4\) of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) have product \(205\) and hence each has rational norm \(205\).
Neither is a square since its norm would then be a rational square.
The product \(205\) is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) either, as neither \(205\) nor \(41\) is a rational square.
Adjoining \(\sqrt{\Delta_1},\sqrt{\Delta_2}\) gives independent inversions of the two units \(\rho_i\), and the conjugation of the base interchanges the two inverse pairs.
The \(\rho_i\) are algebraic units because their traces are integral and their quadratics have constant one.
They are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, as inverting either one alone shows for every torsion-valued monomial.
Thus \(\rho_1^2\rho_2\) has eight distinct conjugates, the monomials with signed permutations of exponents \((2,1)\).
Its minimal polynomial \(P\) is reciprocal and all roots are positive real, with four exterior roots, so every inverse-pair label belongs to the same exterior block.
The quotient has rank two with \(a=2,b=1\), and one squared-axis reduction is the minimal polynomial of \(\rho_1^2\).
Since \(\rho_2>3\) and \(\rho_1<6\), we have \(\log\rho_2<\log\rho_1<2\log\rho_2\).
The maxima in the displayed measure formula consequently give \(\log M(P)=4(\log\rho_1+\log\rho_2)\).
The constructed \(Q\) has \(\log M(Q)=2(\log\rho_1+\log\rho_2)\), attaining equality for this construction.

<a id="four"></a>
## All irreducible reciprocal inputs with four real exterior roots

If an opposite pair occurs, the [exact substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#three) gives a two-exterior polynomial of degree \(m\) with the same measure.
Otherwise no relation on one or two labels is possible by the same conjugate log-vector argument and block projections.
All block sizes divide \(\gcd(m,4)\).
Blocks of size one or two have rank zero.
In size four the local rank is at most two, since a quotient of rank at most one would force a relation on at most two of its four label images.
Local rank two is handled by the preceding squared-axis construction, giving two positive exterior roots of the reduced polynomial and logarithmic measure at most half as large.
In both reductions these positive exterior roots have distinct moduli, since they are distinct positive roots of an irreducible polynomial.
Thus the [two-exterior power result](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#statement) also applies to the reduced unit.

In the remaining cases the representatives are independent or the relation lattice has rank one per block of size four.
The [real block interlacing result](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#powers) then gives preservation of degree at all powers and, for \(q=m-4\ge2\), a positive natural density of powers with one neutral trace in each of the \(q\) complementary intervals of the monic torsion trace polynomial \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\).
This has defect four.
For \(q=1\), \(A=1\) works at every power using the unique neutral trace, and for \(q=0\) it works using a real exterior trace with defect three.
At \(q=0\) the original degree eight also supplies a positive Mahler gap by bounded-degree finiteness.
