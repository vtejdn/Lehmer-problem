# Primitive five-term modular witnesses at the critical width

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive density of degrees with false positives at both signs

Write \(D_j=2^j\), \(\kappa=(\log 2)/8\), and take odd primes \(p=p_j\) satisfying \(p_j/j\to\infty\) and \(p_j=o(D_j)\).
These conditions include the [first prime above \(j^2\)](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#statement).
Fix \(0<\eta<\kappa\) and allow any precision \(p^t\) with \(2\le t\) and \(t\log p\le(\kappa-\eta)p\).
Put
\[
 \alpha=(3+\sqrt5)/2,\qquad P_1=X^2-3X+1,\qquad P_2=X^2-7X+1,\qquad G_0=P_1P_2 .
\]
For \(D_j/4+O(D_j/\log p+p)\) distinct even degrees \(d=2k\) in \([D_j,2D_j)\), the following holds at the [critical width](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#narrow) \(b=5d/2-1\), for all sufficiently large \(j\).
The same degrees work at all the indicated precisions and both signs.
The polynomials \(P_1(X^k),P_2(X^k)\) are the distinct degree-\(d\) minima of a reciprocal unit \(\gamma\) and its square.
For each sign \(\varepsilon\) and precision there is a monic integral row \(A\) with exactly five nonzero coefficients such that
\[
   ((2pb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^p,\qquad
   A\bmod p^t\ \in\ \Lambda_{p,b,\varepsilon}\bmod p^t ,
\]
where \(\Lambda_{p,b,\varepsilon}\) is the full integer lattice of rows \(E\) of degree at most \(b\) satisfying
\[
       G_0(X^k)^2\mid E(X^p)+\varepsilon X^{pb+1}E(X^{-p}) .
\]
At every root \(z\) of \(G_0(X^k)\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), the candidate satisfies \(v_p(A(z^p))=1\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).
In particular it passes both the local primitivity test and the first nonzero numerator digit test for every target.
On the same degrees, for every integer modulus \(Q\) divisible by \(p^2\) with \(\log Q\le(\kappa-\eta)p\), there are primitive five-term rows satisfying the same budget, numerator valuations and membership in the reduction of the exact lattice modulo \(Q\).
This extension permits arbitrary auxiliary prime powers combined by the Chinese remainder theorem.
For this same pair of minima there is no exact passing row with \(A(\gamma^p)A(\gamma^{2p})\ne0\) at either sign, even with unrestricted support.
The two minima have logarithmic measures \(\log\alpha,2\log\alpha\), so a separate small-measure restriction can exclude this family.
The construction leaves open the existence of exact witnesses for other pairs at these degrees.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Counting shifts that work at every residue

All odd \(k\) are available for the [irreducible power substitutions](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#count).
Indeed if \(\alpha=v^\ell\) in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) for an odd prime \(\ell\), then \(v\) is an algebraic integer and its two real embeddings give \(v=\alpha^{1/\ell}\) and \(v^{-1}\).
Their sum is an integer strictly between two and \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}=3\).
The binomial argument therefore gives degree \(2k\) for \(\gamma=\alpha^{1/k}\) and for \(\gamma^2\), yielding the claimed minima and measures.
Fix \(p>23\) avoiding \(\operatorname{disc}(G_0)G_0(1)G_0(-1)\).
For \(p\nmid k\) the inflation \(G_0(X^k)\) also has simple, nonzero residues, so all its \(p\)-adic roots lie in an unramified extension.

For each nonzero residue \(k\bmod p\), take the unique odd representative \(u\) of \(k^{-1}\bmod p\) in \((-p,p)\).
Retain it when \(\xi^u\notin\{1,-1\}\) for every root \(\xi\) of \(\bar G_0\).
Each such root is the reduction of a conjugate of \(\alpha^a\) for \(a=1\) or \(2\).
If its order is \(M\), the rational integer \(2-\alpha^{aM}-\alpha^{-aM}\) is divisible by \(p\), nonzero, and smaller in absolute value than \(\alpha^{2M}\).
Thus \(M>(\log p)/(2\log\alpha)\).
An excluded shift for this root satisfies \(M\mid2u\), accounting for \(O(p/M+1)\) integers in \((-p,p)\).
Across the four roots only \(O(p/\log p)\) of the \(p-1\) inverse classes are lost.
For each retained class, the odd integers \(k\in[D_j/2,D_j)\) form one class modulo \(2p\) and number \(D_j/(4p)+O(1)\).
Summing proves the asserted count and gives a limiting proportion \(1/4\) of all degrees in the block for this construction.

<a id="digits"></a>
## A monic representative of a reduced exact line

At base width four consider the integral rows \(B\) with
\[
       G_0(X)^2\mid B(X^p)+\varepsilon X^u B^*_4(X^p),
           \qquad B_4^*(Y)=Y^4B(1/Y),
\]
in the Laurent polynomial ring, where \(X\) is a unit modulo \(G_0^2\).
The [signed rank calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v465.md#shift) extends to negative \(u\) because the involution \(L(X)\mapsto X^{4p+u}L(1/X)\) is well defined on the same quotient.
Over characteristic zero it exchanges the two length-two local factors at each inverse pair, giving a signed eigenspace of dimension four.
In characteristic \(p\), the derivative at a residue \(\xi\) is \(\varepsilon u\xi^{u-1}\bar B^*_4(\xi^p)\).
The double contact condition is therefore equivalent to \(\bar G_0\mid\bar B\), since Frobenius and inversion permute the four roots.
The graph remainder map has rank four over both fields.
Its integer kernel is a saturated line whose reduction is spanned by \(\bar G_0\).
Moreover all four nonzero Smith factors are \(p\)-units, so the solutions of the remainder equations modulo \(p^t\) are exactly the reductions of that line.

The leading coefficient of a primitive generator is a \(p\)-unit.
Multiply the generator by an integer inverse to its leading coefficient modulo \(p^t\), obtaining an exact row \(\widetilde B\) with reduction \(\bar G_0\).
Center its coefficients modulo \(p^t\) to obtain \(B\).
This makes \(B\) monic and \(\|B\|_1\le5p^t/2\).
Its five coefficients are nonzero since those of \(G_0\) in ascending order are \(1,-10,23,-10,1\).

For any \(p\)-adic root \(x\) of \(G_0\), put \(z=x^p\), \(y=\bar z\), \(\xi=\bar x\), and \(D_Y=Y\,d/dY\).
Combining the value and Euler derivative equations at \(x\) gives
\[
 (u+4p)\widetilde B(z)=p\bigl((D_Y\widetilde B)(z)-
                 \varepsilon x^u z^4(D_Y\widetilde B)(1/z)\bigr).
\]
As \(p\nmid u\), division by \(p\) and reciprocity of \(G_0\) at the paired zeros give
\[
             u\,\widetilde B(z)/p\bmod p
                        =(D_Y\bar G_0)(y)(1+\varepsilon\xi^u)\ne0.
\]
Simplicity gives the first nonzero factor and the choice of \(u\) gives the second at both signs.
Since \(t\ge2\), \(B(z)\) retains valuation one.

For the composite modulus assertion take a primitive exact generator \(B_*\) of the same base line.
Let \(p^t\) be the exact \(p\)-part of \(Q\).
Choose an integer multiplier equal to the inverse leading coefficient modulo \(p^t\) and equal to one modulo \(Q/p^t\).
It is a unit modulo \(Q\).
Center its multiple of \(B_*\) coefficientwise modulo \(Q\).
Every coefficient remains nonzero modulo \(p\), and no prime dividing \(Q\) divides all the centered coefficients, by primitivity of \(B_*\).
Their positive common content \(g\) is therefore coprime to \(Q\).
Dividing by \(g\) gives a primitive row of one-norm at most \(5Q/2\), still a reduction of the exact line because \(g\) is invertible modulo \(Q\).
At \(p\) the numerator keeps valuation one since division by \(g\) multiplies its first nonzero digit by a unit.
This construction imposes monicity in the pure \(p\)-power case treated above.

## Five coefficients at the prescribed width

Set \(v=(ku-1)/p\) and \(w=(k-1-v)/2\).
The integer \(v\) is even, and \(-k<v<k\) for \(k>1\), so \(w\) is an integer between zero and \(k-1\).
Set \(A(Y)=Y^w B(Y^k)\) at width \(b=5k-1\), and define \(\widetilde A\) in the same way using \(\widetilde B\).
The degrees are at most \(4k+w\le b\).
Since \(b-2w=4k+v\) and \(pv+1=ku\), the graph of \(\widetilde A\) is
\[
 X^{pw}\bigl(\widetilde B((X^k)^p)+
             \varepsilon (X^k)^u\widetilde B^*_4((X^k)^p)\bigr).
\]
This is divisible by \(G_0(X^k)^2\) in the Laurent ring and hence in the polynomial ring, since the divisor has constant term one.
Thus \(A\) represents a reduction of the full exact lattice at the final width.
For any inflated root \(z\), \(A(z^p)=z^{pw}B((z^k)^p)\) has valuation one by the preceding calculation.
The multiplication and substitution preserve the five coefficients, monicity and norm.
Uniformly in the block,
\[
   \log\|A\|_1+\tfrac12\log(2pb+1)
         \le(\kappa-\eta)p+O(j+\log p)<\kappa p
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\).
On the other hand a genuine exact row for this pair would satisfy the [powered derivative-product bound](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product)
\[
      3p\log\alpha\ \le\ 2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2pb+1).
\]
The strict budget puts the right side below \(p(\log2)/4\), which is impossible since \(\alpha>2\).
This last exclusion applies at every odd \(k\) and every prime exponent, independently of the congruence construction.

## A negative shift at the first prime above \(17^2\)

At \(j=17\), take \(p=293,\ k=65631,\ d=131262,\ b=328154\).
Here \(u=-1,\ v=-224,\ w=32927\).
At precision \(293^2\), the two monic rows have the following base coefficients, in ascending order.

| Sign | \(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4\) | \(\|A\|_1\) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| \(+\) | \(4103,-25794,-28984,-7628,1\) | 66510 |
| \(-\) | \(-1757,30462,25514,24016,1\) | 81750 |

For either list, integer division of \(X B(X^{293})+\varepsilon B_4^*(X^{293})\) by \(G_0^2\) gives a nonzero remainder with every coefficient divisible by \(293^2\).
Both lists reduce to \(G_0\bmod293\), and direct substitution of the displayed norms in the strict budget gives passing representatives \(Y^{32927} B(Y^{65631})\).
For both lists the eight coefficients of the remainder have positive gcd exactly \(293^2\).
In particular these particular representatives fail the modulus-two test, whereas the composite modulus argument produces representatives chosen for the entire prescribed modulus.
