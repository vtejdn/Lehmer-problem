# The primes three and five in complementary resultants of the quadratic sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small primes in the integer common quotient

Let \(k,l\) be positive coprime integers and let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
For any monic integral factorization \(P=ST\) with nonzero resultant, one has
\[
    v_3\operatorname{Res}(S,T)\in\{0,2\}.
\]
A positive value requires \(v_3(l)=1\) and \(l\equiv3k\pmod4\).
In that case the 3-primary part of \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S,T)\) is \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\).
If all the irreducible factors of \(P\) are reciprocal, then \(5\nmid\operatorname{Res}(S,T)\) for every such factorization, with no slope congruence required.
The reciprocal hypothesis holds eventually along every primitive sequence with \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\).
Together with the [exact residue computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#sieve), this excludes \(2,5,7,11,17\) from every complementary resultant on these steep sequences, with a uniformly bounded contribution at three.

<a id="three"></a>
## Distinct distances to the fourth root of unity

By the [residue support computation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#sieve) and the [lifted-value test](Yin_C_G0345_v813.md#lift), a common root modulo three requires \(3\mid l,\ l\equiv3k\pmod4\) and \(u^2=-1,\ v=-u\) for its residue coordinates \(u=x^k,v=x^l\).
Coprimality and the order condition make both exponents odd; the common residue roots form the single orbit of primitive fourth roots since \(x\) generates the same group as \(u,v\).
Take its lift \(\xi\) of order four in the unramified quadratic ring \(O=\mathbb Z_3[i]\), and set \(L(X)=X^{-(2k+l)}P(X)\), \(D=X\,d/dX\).
Writing \(t=X^k-X^{-k}\) and \(V=X^l\) gives
\[
                  L=(12+t^2)(V+V^{-1})-7t(V-V^{-1})+1.
\]
With \(U=\xi^k\) one has \(U^2=-1,\ V=-U,\ t=2U,\ Dt=0\), and hence
\[
              L(\xi)=-27,\qquad DL(\xi)=-16lU,\qquad
              D^2L(\xi)\equiv-28k^2\not\equiv0\pmod3.
\]
For the last congruence the \(V\)-derivatives vanish modulo three and \(D^2t=2k^2U\).
Thus \(\bar P\) has multiplicity exactly two at this residue root.

If the two factors share the root, each has a simple root in its residue disk.
Hensel's lemma gives two lifts \(r_1,r_2\in O\) with complementary local factors units in the disk.
Since \(v_3(P(\xi))=3\), the valuations of \(\xi-r_1,\xi-r_2\) are one and two in some order.
Their difference has valuation one, and differentiating the product shows that \(v_3(DL(\xi))=1\): the term differentiating the factor of valuation two has valuation one and all other terms have larger valuation.
The exact first derivative therefore forces \(v_3(l)=1\).
Each lift generates the unramified quadratic ring over \(\mathbb Z_3\) since its residue generates \(\mathbb F_9\).
Over \(\mathbb Z_3\) the local quotient at this closed point is \(O/3O\), by evaluation at one lift; the other factor has exactly one root in the matching disk and its other roots give units there.
Its additive group and cardinality give the assertions at three.

<a id="five"></a>
## One rational factor at each endpoint modulo five

Suppose all factors are reciprocal.
The [dyadic squareclass argument](Yin_C_G0345_v810.md#dyadic) gives even \(v_5(S_i(1)S_i(-1))\) for each rational irreducible factor \(S_i\).
Indeed the odd endpoint product, with its degree sign, is square in \(\mathbb Q_2\); its only possible odd prime divisors are 5 and 23, and the odd square residue modulo eight forces an even exponent of five.
This uses reciprocity and the characteristic-two graph splitting at each factor, independent of a growth condition.
There are no roots \(1,-1\) in characteristic zero since \(P(1)=25,\ P(-1)=24+(-1)^l\).

For odd \(l\), every \(S_i(-1)\) is a 5-adic unit.
The nonnegative even valuations of \(S_i(1)\), counted with multiplicity, sum to two, so precisely one factor with multiplicity one meets \(+1\bmod5\).
For even \(l\), coprimality implies \(k\) is odd and at least one of the two residues \(l-2k,\ l+2k\) is nonzero modulo five.
At the corresponding endpoint \(\eta\), the [trace derivative computation](Yin_C_G0345_v810.md#odd-places) gives a simple residue zero \(2\eta\) of the trace polynomial.
Consequently exactly one trace factor meets that endpoint, with multiplicity one, and its value there accounts for the full 5-adic valuation two.
The individual valuations at this endpoint are therefore all even.
The squareclass parity makes them even at the other endpoint as well, where their sum is again two.
At either endpoint only one factor can occur, including multiplicity.
The residue support computation leaves no other common root modulo five, so every complementary resultant is prime to five.
