# Normal closures of intermediate fields of numbers of bounded Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite list up to exponent substitution

Let \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), and fix \(M_0>0,\ A\ge1,\ C\ge0,\ 0<\epsilon\le1\).
Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be any nontorsion algebraic number of absolute degree \(d\), with primitive integer minimal polynomial \(S\) chosen with positive leading coefficient and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(\alpha)=d h(\alpha)\le M_0\).
Suppose there is an intermediate field \(F\subseteq E\subseteq F(\alpha)\) such that
\[
       b=[F(\alpha):E]\le A d^{1-\epsilon},
       \qquad D=[\widetilde E:F]\le A d^C ,                              \tag{1}
\]
where \(\widetilde E\) is the normal closure of \(E/F\).
Then \(S(X)=U(X^t)\), with \(t\ge1\) and \(U\) belonging to an effectively bounded finite set of primitive integer polynomials depending only on \(M_0,A,C,\epsilon\).
The exponent is precisely the gcd of the positive support exponents of \(S\), and \(m(U)=m(S)\).
In particular there is a positive uniform Mahler gap on the class defined by the existence of \(E\) in (1), with no restrictions on the coefficients, signature, support or degree of \(S\).

More quantitatively, assume just the upper bound on \(b\) in (1).
Write \(d_0=d/t\).
For \(d_0\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M_0,A,\epsilon\), one has
\[
 \log D\ \ge\ c\epsilon\log d\,
 \min\!\left\{\sqrt{\log(3d_0)},\
  \sqrt{\frac{\log\log(16d)}{\log(2+\log\log(16d))}}\right\}             \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute \(c>0\).
Consequently along any unrestricted sequence with \(m(\alpha)\to0\), the normal closure degree of **every** intermediate field satisfying the index restriction grows faster than every fixed power of \(d\), uniformly over the choice of that field.
For a reciprocal unit, \(E=F(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) has index at most two.
Thus (2) forces growth in the trace splitting field itself, even when adjoining the individual roots above that field entails many independent quadratic extensions.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Independent conjugates after removing a common exponent

Let \(t\) be the stated gcd and put \(\gamma=\alpha^t\).
Writing \(S(X)=U(X^t)\) gives the primitive irreducible polynomial \(U\) of \(\gamma\), since a factorization of \(U\) would factor \(S\).
Its support gcd is one and its degree is \(d_0\).
The absolute height identity \(h(\gamma)=t h(\alpha)\) gives \(m(\gamma)=m(\alpha)\).
The [power-field calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v254.md#fields) gives \(F(\gamma^j)=F(\gamma)\) for every \(j\ge1\).

Let \(r\) be the multiplicative rank of the group generated by the \(F\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Their \(t\)-th powers give exactly the \(F\)-conjugates of \(\gamma\), by extension of embeddings.
The two groups have the same rank because powering has finite kernel on a finitely generated subgroup of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\).
Let \(N_0/F\) be the normal closure of \(F(\gamma)/F\) and write \(D_0=[N_0:F]\).
The [integral rank argument for power-stable fields](../audits/literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field) gives \(D_0\le3^{r^2}\).
Indeed raise the relative conjugates of \(\gamma\) to the exponent of torsion in the group they generate.
These powers still generate \(N_0\) as a field over \(F\), and the free group they generate has rank \(r\).
Thus the Galois group acts faithfully on that free abelian group, and reduction modulo three is injective on finite subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\).

The one-coordinate case of [Delsinne's height bound](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001), with \([F(\gamma):F]\le D_0\), gives
\[
          \frac{M_0}{d_0}\ \ge\ h(\gamma)\
              \ge\frac{c_0}{D_0(\log(3D_0))^{48}}
\]
for an effective absolute \(c_0>0\).
For large \(d_0\) this forces \(D_0\ge\sqrt{d_0}\), since otherwise the upper bound on \(D_0(\log(3D_0))^{48}\) would be \(o(d_0)\).
Consequently \(r\gg\sqrt{\log(3d_0)}\) with an absolute implied constant for \(d_0\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M_0\).

<a id="intermediate"></a>
## A joint degree controlled by the chosen intermediate field

For \(1\le k\le r\), select \(k\) multiplicatively independent \(F\)-conjugates \(\alpha_i\) of \(\alpha\) from the generating set.
Each \(F\)-embedding sending \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha_i\) sends \(E\) into \(\widetilde E\).
The image of the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(E\) therefore bounds the degree of \(\alpha_i\) over \(\widetilde E\) by \(b\).
Successively adjoining the selected conjugates gives
\[
               J=[F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k):F]\le b^kD.                \tag{3}
\]
Every selected conjugate has absolute logarithmic height \(h(\alpha)\).
Delsinne's product inequality for independent coordinates from the [published Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) gives
\[
 k\log(1/h(\alpha))\le \log J+\log c_2(k)+\kappa_2(k)\log\log(3J).
                                                                            \tag{4}
\]
There is an effective absolute \(C_0\) for which both \(\log c_2(k)\) and \(\kappa_2(k)\) are at most \(\exp(C_0 k^2\log(2k))\).
This follows from the stated constants with \(A_k=2(k+1)^2(k+1)!\), \(\kappa_2(k)=3k A_k^k\) and \(\log c_2(k)=k\log(2k^2)+64k^2 k! A_k^{2k}\).
Estimate (3) pays for only the chosen lifts above the intermediate normal closure.
The argument uses the original height \(h(\alpha)\) even when \(\alpha\) has a large common exponent in its minimal polynomial.

First impose both bounds in (1), and choose a fixed integer \(k>C/\epsilon\).
If \(r\ge k\), then \(J\le A^{k+1}d^{(1-\epsilon)k+C}\).
Since \(h(\alpha)\le M_0/d\), (4) implies
\[
       (\epsilon k-C)\log d
          \le O_{A,C,k,M_0}(1)+O_{A,C,k}(\log\log(3d)).
\]
This bounds \(d\) effectively.
If \(r<k\), the preceding rank estimate bounds \(d_0\) effectively.
In either case the coefficient of \(X^{d_0-i}\) in \(U\) has absolute value at most \(\binom{d_0}{i}\exp(M_0)\), by expanding the product over the roots with the leading coefficient included.
This yields the finite list.
For the gap take \(M_0=1\) and use the minimum of one and the positive logarithmic measures in this finite list, taking one alone if the positive part is empty.
The list can be obtained by enumerating within the degree and coefficient bounds and discarding the constant, reducible and torsion cases.

To prove (2), choose \(k\) as the floor of a sufficiently small absolute multiple of the minimum on its right.
For large \(d_0\le d\), this gives \(1\le k\le r\) and reduces the constant so that \(\exp(C_0k^2\log(2k))\le(\log d)^{1/3}\).
Suppose \(D<d^{\epsilon k/2}\).
Equation (3) and the upper bound on \(b\) then give \(\log J\le k\log A+(1-\epsilon/2)k\log d\), so \(\log\log(3J)\ll_A\log\log(16d)\).
From (4) one obtains
\[
 (\epsilon/2)k\log d
    \le k\log(A M_0)+O_A\big((\log d)^{1/3}(1+\log\log(16d))\big).
\]
This is impossible for large \(d\) depending only on \(A,M_0,\epsilon\).
Rounding \(k\) costs an absolute factor for large \(d_0\), proving (2).
If \(m(\alpha)\to0\), the equality \(m(\alpha)=m(\gamma)\) and bounded-degree coefficient finiteness force \(d_0\to\infty\).
Both entries in the minimum in (2) then tend to infinity.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Prime degree of the reciprocal trace and the sharp eventual gap

Fix a mass bound \(M_0>0\) and let \(q\) vary over primes.
For all sufficiently large \(q\), every monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic integer polynomial of degree \(2q\), logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(M_0\), and solvable trace splitting group is exactly
\[
                         X^{2q}+aX^q+1,\qquad a\in\mathbb Z,\quad |a|\ge3 .
                                                                            \tag{5}
\]
Here the trace splitting group acts on the conjugates of \(\tau=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The reciprocal involution gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\tau)]=2\), so there are \(q\) trace conjugates and the action is transitive of prime degree.
Such an action is primitive.
In a faithful solvable primitive group a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup is elementary abelian and transitive, and is therefore regular.
For degree \(q\) it has order \(q\).
The full permutation group normalizing this regular subgroup has order \(q(q-1)\), since its elements act as \(x\mapsto ax+b\) on \(\mathbb F_q\).
This is the [affine action for solvable primitive groups](Yin_C_G0345_v133.md#blocks) at prime degree.
Thus \(D\le q(q-1)\) for \(E=F(\tau)\), while \(b\le2\).
By (1) the degree \(d_0\) of the compressed polynomial is bounded in terms of \(M_0\).
It divides \(2q\), and it cannot be one since a reciprocal monic polynomial in \(X^{2q}\) of degree \(2q\) with constant one would have only torsion roots.
For large \(q\) it must therefore be two.
Reciprocity, monicity and nontorsion now force (5), since \(Y^2+aY+1\) is cyclotomic or reducible for \(|a|\le2\).

It follows, without an upper measure hypothesis, that the logarithmic measure in this solvable prime-degree trace class is at least \(\log((3+\sqrt5)/2)\) for all sufficiently large \(q\).
Indeed apply (5) at that particular mass bound to all candidates below it.
The lower bound is attained at every sufficiently large prime \(q\).
For \(u=(3+\sqrt5)/2\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), there is no \(q\)-th root of \(u\) in \(K\) for large \(q\), by bounded-degree height finiteness and \(h(u^{1/q})=h(u)/q\).
If \(X^q-u\) had a factor of degree \(e<q\) over \(K\), a root of an irreducible such factor would give \(v\in K\) by its norm with \(v^q=u^e\).
As \(\gcd(e,q)=1\), this would make \(u\) a \(q\)-th power in \(K\).
Thus \(X^{2q}-3X^q+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), since adjoining a chosen root over \(K\) has degree \(q\) and its \(q\)-th power generates \(K\).
Its splitting field is contained in \(K(\zeta_q,u^{1/q})\), which contains the inverses of all roots of \(X^q-u\) and is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Its Galois group is cyclic over the abelian base \(K(\zeta_q)\), so the trace splitting group is solvable.
The roots above \(u,u^{-1}\) give logarithmic measure exactly \(\log u\).

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Trace fields of all powers in a normalized sparse relation

For a nontorsion root \(\beta\) of a rational relation minimal under deleting terms and having support differences of gcd one, the [finite cyclotomic power-field theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v254.md#fields) gives \(F(\beta)=F(\beta^j)\) at every positive \(j\).
The [support calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v254.md#support) gives \(t(\beta)=1\).
Hence for \(E_j=F(\beta^j+\beta^{-j})\) one has \(b\le2\) simultaneously for all \(j\), and \(d_0=d=\deg\beta\).
When the measure is bounded and \(d\) is large, the second entry of the minimum in (2) applies, uniformly in \(j\).
For example, at every prime \(n\) and every \(0<l<n\) the five-term parent \(X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1\) has monic unit factors and support differences of gcd one.
Each nontorsion root satisfies the minimality hypothesis by the [five-term argument](Yin_C_G0345_v254.md#examples).
Its measure is at most \(\log 7\) by multiplicativity and the coefficient length, and its degree tends to infinity uniformly in \(l\) by the [near-linear factor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement).
Thus (2) constrains the relative normal closure of every powered trace of every actual nontorsion factor at large prime \(n\), regardless of the multiplicative order of two modulo \(n\).
The conclusion controls splitting fields and leaves the existence of small-measure factors in this parent open.
