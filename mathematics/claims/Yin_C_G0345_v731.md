# Relative height from powers of Frobenius and the exponent modulo the center

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on normal subfields of a small-measure root field

Fix a positive integer \(b\), and put
\[
                 g_b(x)=\frac{(\log\log(5x))^{b+2}}{(\log(2x))^{2b+1}}
                 \qquad(x\ge1).
\]
Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer of degree \(d\) with \(m=\log M(\alpha)\le1\).
Let \(F\) be a Galois subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), of degree \(s\), and write \(D=d/s\) and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\).
If the exponent of \(G/Z(G)\) divides \(b\), then there is a constant \(c_b>0\), depending only on \(b\), such that
\[
                              m\ge c_b\,s\,g_b(D).                      \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(s\ll_b m/g_b(d)\) and \(s=o(1/g_b(d))\) on every such sequence with \(m\to0\).
The degree and exponent of the center, the sizes of the conjugacy classes of \(G\), and the conductor are unrestricted.
The [abelian-subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#statement) gives the cubic case \(b=1\).
The hypotheses can also detect perfect groups.
For example, at the group level \(A_5^r\) has exponent \(30\), trivial abelianization and conjugacy classes of unbounded size as \(r\) grows.
Thus a subfield with that group supplies no nontrivial abelian subfield of its own, while (1) uses its full degree.
This group comparison asserts no existence of small-measure generators.

For a Galois extension \(F/\mathbb Q\) whose whole group has exponent dividing \(b\), even if \(F\) is infinite, one has
\[
                           D h(\alpha)\ge c'_b g_b(D)                   \tag{2}
\]
for every nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(\alpha\), with \(D=[F(\alpha):F]\) and \(h\) its absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The [central-extension theorem of Amoroso, David and Zannier](../audits/literature/ADZ14/Yin_U001.md#central-local-height-theorem) bounds the height of nontorsion elements lying in the Galois field under its central quotient hypothesis.
Here (1) and (2) give relative estimates for elements of arbitrary degree over the subfield.

<a id="powers"></a>
## The local weights at different powers

Let \(F\) now be any number field, \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(L\) its normal closure, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) and \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\).
Take any finite set \(\mathcal P\) of odd primes and any positive integers \(k_p\).
At a prime \(P\) of \(L\) over \(p\), let \(\Phi_{P,k}\) consist of **all** lifts in the decomposition group of the residue automorphism \(z\mapsto z^{p^k}\).
In particular it is an inertia coset, also when the prime ramifies.
Choose \(P\) uniformly over \(p\), then \(y\) uniformly in \(\Phi_{P,k}\).
Put \(a_{p,k}(Hg)=\mathbb P(Hy=Hg)\) and \(w_{p,k}=\max_{Hg}a_{p,k}(Hg)\).
For \(t=|\mathcal P|\), every positive integer \(T\), and every nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(\alpha\) of relative degree \(D\), one has
\[
 T\sum_{p\in\mathcal P} w_{p,k_p}\log p
 \le \tfrac12(T^2+t)\log(D(T+t))
       +(T+t)\left(T+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{k_p}\right)D h(\alpha).
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
If \(v\) runs over places of \(F\) over \(p\), with residue degrees \(f_v\), then
\[
                        a_{p,k}(H)=s^{-1}\sum_{f_v\mid k} f_v .        \tag{4}
\]
In particular, for the degree-\(s\) trace field of a Salem number \(\alpha\), the relative degree is two and \(2h(\alpha)=\log(\alpha)/s\).
Taking one odd prime and \(T=1\) gives
\[
 \log\alpha\ \ge\ \frac{s\big(w_{p,k}\log p-\log4\big)}{2(1+p^k)}.     \tag{5}
\]
If \(p\ge5\) is fixed, unramified in the normal closure, and its Frobenius order there is \(k\), then \(w_{p,k}=1\).
Thus \(p^k\ge s\log(p/4)/(2\log\alpha)-1\), a restriction at a single prime with its own power cost.

To prove (4), write \(e_v\) for the ramification index at \(v=P\cap F\).
The coset \(\Phi_{P,k}\) meets \(e_v=[I_P:I_P\cap H]\) right cosets of \(H\), uniformly.
It meets \(H\) exactly when its residue map fixes the residue field at \(v\), since the local decomposition group for \(L_P/F_v\) surjects onto the residue extension group.
This condition is \(f_v\mid k\).
The fraction of the \(P\)'s over \(v\) is \(e_v f_v/s\), proving (4).
For any \(Hg\), the conditional probability given \(P\) is \(1/e_v\) whenever that coset is met and zero otherwise.

Use the [relative class-product reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#relative-compression) to obtain a number \(\beta\) of relative degree \(D'\le D\) with \(D'h(\beta)=Dh(\alpha)\), whose distinct relative conjugates \(a_i\) remain distinct after any common positive power.
For each prime choose \(g\) maximizing \(a_{p,k_p}(Hg)\) and extend it to the algebraic closure.
The new nodes \((g^{-1}a_i)^{p^{k_p}}\) are distinct within their set.
Different powers based on distinct primes are distinct integers greater than one, so their positive heights distinguish all the sets from one another and from the original nodes.

Fix \(P\) where \(Hg\) is met, and choose a lift \(y\) in the intersection.
On \(F\) one has \(g^{-1}=y^{-1}\).
For every integral element \(c\) of \(F_v\), choose the unique representative \(z\equiv c\) in \(F_v\) with \(z^{p^{f_v}}=z\), by simple-root lifting, also unique for that residue in \(L_P\).
Then \(y(z)=z^{p^{k_p}}\) and \(v_p(c-z)\ge1/e_v\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).
Applying \(y^{-1}\) and taking powers of integral elements gives \(v_p(c-(y^{-1}c)^{p^{k_p}})\ge1/e_v\).
Scale the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) over \(F\) to a polynomial \(f_0\in F[X]\) of Gauss norm one at \(v\) by the inverse of a largest coefficient.
With \(q=p^{k_p}\), all mixed coefficients in a \(q\)-th power are divisible by \(p\).
Thus the coefficients of \(f_0(X^q)-(g^{-1}f_0(X))^q\) have valuation at least \(1/e_v\).
Evaluation at the \(g^{-1}a_i\), Gauss norm multiplicativity and the [cross-difference estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v728.md#determinant) save \(p^{-D'/e_v}\) relative to the usual products of local maxima.
The confluent determinant takes multiplicity \(T\) at the original nodes and one at the others.
At \(p\) its normalized saving is at least \(TD' w_{p,k_p}\log p\), by the conditional probability above.
The [archimedean row norms and product formula](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#local) cost \(D'(T^2+t)\log(D'(T+t))/2\) and at most \(D'(T+t)(T+\sum_p p^{k_p})D h(\alpha)\) in heights.
Division by \(D'\) proves (3).

<a id="central"></a>
## A prime interval with central residue automorphisms

Suppose first that \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) is as in (1).
At each unramified \(p\) take \(k_p=b\).
All Frobenius elements above \(p\) are conjugate and have the same \(b\)-th power, since this power is central.
Thus \(w_{p,b}=1\).
The [discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v726.md#primes) gives \(\sum_{p\ {\rm ramified\ in}\ F}\log p\le2\log d+4m\).
Take \(T=\lceil A\log(2d)/\log\log(5d)\rceil\), \(X=T^2\log(2T)\), with a sufficiently large absolute \(A\).
The [prime interval estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice) gives \(\asymp T^2\) odd primes in \([X,2X]\).
At most \((2\log d+4)/\log X\) are ramified, leaving \(t\asymp T^2\) for this choice of \(A\).
In (3) the left side is \(\gg T^3\log(2T)\), while the first term on the right is \(O(T^2(\log(2d)+\log(2T)))\).
Enlarging \(A\) makes this expense at most half the left side uniformly in \(d\).
Since every \(p^b\le(2X)^b\), the height coefficient is \(\ll_b T^{4+2b}(\log(2T))^b\).
Subtraction gives \(m/s=D h(\alpha)\gg_b T^{-(2b+1)}(\log(2T))^{1-b}\gg_b g_b(d)\).
Consequently \(s\ll_b 1/g_b(d)\) at \(m\le1\).
For fixed \(b\), this bound is polynomial in \(\log(2d)\), so \(\log(2D)\asymp_b\log(2d)\) and \(g_b(D)\asymp_b g_b(d)\), first for large \(d\), then also in the bounded range by positive constants.
This proves (1).

For (2) first suppose \(F\) is finite Galois of exponent dividing \(b\).
For \(p\nmid b\), tame inertia is cyclic of order \(e_p\le b\) by the [tame character argument](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#consequences).
The residue group has exponent dividing \(b\), so the identity belongs to every \(\Phi_{P,b}\).
Conditionally on every \(P\) it has probability \(1/e_p\), giving \(w_{p,b}\ge1/b\).
Take \(T=\lceil A_b\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\rceil\), use the same interval \([X,2X]\), and omit the finitely many primes dividing \(b\).
Choose \(A_b\) large enough that \(t\asymp T^2\) uniformly and the logarithmic expense of (3) is at most half its left side \(\gg T^3\log(2T)/b\).
The same height coefficient now gives \(D h(\alpha)\gg_b g_b(D)\).
For an infinite \(F\), choose a finite Galois subextension containing all coefficients of the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(F\), possible by normality.
That polynomial remains irreducible over the smaller field, whose Galois group is a quotient of the original group.
Applying the finite estimate proves (2).
