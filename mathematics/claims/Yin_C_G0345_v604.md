# Polygon deficits and coefficient separation in a tree of terminal relations

<a id="statement"></a>
## A coefficient cost with linear dependence on the polygon deficit

Assume the exact unit-circle hypothesis of the [full coefficient count](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement), with total logarithmic measure \(m\to0\), and write \(L=\log(1/m)\).
For the full signed coefficient tuple of a marked list, write \(x_i=|b_i|\), including the marked term, and set \(D=\sum_i x_i-2\max_i x_i\).
Let \(f:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be nondecreasing with \(B_f=\sum_{a\ge0}2^{-a}f(2^{a+1})<\infty\).
For any fixed \(\lambda\ge0\), set
\[
 G(\mathbf b)=\lambda D+\sum_{i\text{ outside the two largest}} f(x_i).
\]
For each sufficiently late input, some tuple with \(r\to\infty\) terms in the same finite windows has at least \(e^{G(\mathbf b)}(c/m)^{r-2}\) distinct sorted marked lists, where \(c>0\) depends only on \(\lambda,B_f\).
Under the power-degree or real-conjugate condition of the full coefficient count, its number \(V\) of primitive shapes satisfies \(\log V-G(\mathbf b)\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)L\).
For instance \(f(s)=s/(3+\log s)^2\) is admissible.
If the two largest lengths are \(A\ge B\) and the remaining lengths sum to \(S\), the identity \(D=S-(A-B)\) pays a linear cost in \(S\) whenever \(A-B\le(1-\eta)S\) for fixed \(\eta>0\).
The same relations satisfy the [coefficient separation estimate](#separation) in their original coordinates.
The result supplies a necessary count for a shrinking sequence and leaves the corresponding uniform upper bound open.

<a id="tree"></a>
## Summing local costs over original leaves

Root the actual split tree at an original leaf, and give every edge the absolute value of its integer coefficient.
At the chosen neutral embedding every edge has this length and every original leaf has length one.
If the outgoing parent length at an internal vertex is \(p\), its children have lengths totaling at least \(p\).
The difference is the sum of all incident lengths minus \(2p\), which is at least the nonnegative polygon deficit at that vertex.
Summing the differences cancels every interior edge once in each direction and leaves exactly \((j-1)-1=j-2\) for a tree with \(j\) leaves.
To bound the cost from \(f\), omit the parent and the child with the most descendant leaves at each vertex.
Each other child with \(s\) leaves has length at most \(s\), and omitting the two largest lengths minimizes the sum that remains.
Charge \(f(s)/s\) to its descendant leaves, as in the [light-edge proof](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#budget).
The charged sizes on any leaf path at least double, so their total charge per leaf is at most \(B_f\).
Consequently \(\sum_v G(\mathbf b_v)\le\lambda(j-2)+jB_f\).
Maximize \((W(k,\mathbf b)e^{-G(\mathbf b)})^{1/(k-2)}\) over the feasible tuples in the finite window, with \(W\) their list count.
The [full-tuple reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#reconstruction) and \(\sum_v(k_v-2)=j-2\) bound the number of original supports by the \((j-2)\)-th power of this maximum times an absolute exponential in \(j\) depending only on \(\lambda,B_f\).
Comparison with the original lower count, exclusion of fixed weights, and division by the [per-shape dilation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) give the assertions just as in the [weighted selection](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#selection).

<a id="separation"></a>
## Distances from the largest coefficient at every neutral conjugate

Let \(h\ge1\) be the number of unit-circle conjugates among the actual \(d\) conjugates and fix an index \(a\) of largest length.
For every marked relation above with \(r\ge4\), including the marked exponent, one has
\[
   \sum_{i\ne a}x_i\exp(-2m|t_i-t_a|/h)\le 2^{1+2(d-h)/h}D.
\]
In particular the total length outside \(a\) within distance \(Kh/m\) of its exponent is at most \(2^{1+2(d-h)/h}e^{2K}D\).
This estimate is uniform when a fixed positive proportion of the conjugates is neutral.
It is an estimate in actual exponent coordinates before division by a list gcd.

For each neutral conjugate \(z\), let \(u_i=-\operatorname{sgn}(b_i b_a)z^{t_i-t_a}\) for \(i\ne a\).
The relation gives \(\sum_{i\ne a} x_i u_i=x_a\), hence \(\sum_{i\ne a}x_i|1-u_i|^2=2D\).
All exponents are distinct by [terminality at weight at least four](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive).
For a nonzero gap \(q\) and either sign \(\epsilon\), the algebraic integer \(1-\epsilon\alpha^q\) is nonzero because \(\alpha\) is a nontorsion unit.
The product of its absolute values over all \(d-h\) nonneutral embeddings is bounded by \(2^{d-h}e^{|q|m}\), since \(|1-\epsilon\gamma^q|\le2\max(1,|\gamma^q|)\) and the unit and its inverse have the same total logarithmic measure.
The integral absolute norm is at least one, even if the powered element lies in a proper subfield, since the norm is taken in the original field.
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality thus bounds the mean of \(|1-\epsilon z^q|^2\) over the neutral embeddings below by \(2^{-2(d-h)/h}e^{-2|q|m/h}\).
Averaging the identity for \(2D\) over those embeddings proves the estimate.
