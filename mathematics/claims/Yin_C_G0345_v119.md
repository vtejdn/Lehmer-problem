# Integer levels between two and the exterior Salem trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## How many levels give a smaller trace

Let \(\sigma\) run through Salem numbers of degree \(2m\to\infty\) with \(0<a=\log\sigma\le A\) for a fixed \(A\).
Write \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(u=T-2\), \(S\) for the monic trace minimal polynomial, \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) for its roots in \((-2,2)\), and
\[
 D=S'(T),\quad v_j=(T-x_j)^{-1},\quad W=\sum_j v_j,\quad E=uW,\quad
 B=\max_{2\le t\le T}|S(t)|.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(m\), the integer levels \(b\) for which \(S(t)=b\) has a root in \((2,T)\) are precisely \(-1,-2,\ldots,-\lfloor B\rfloor\).
Moreover
\[
 W\sim\frac{m}{2\sinh a},\qquad E\sim m\tanh(a/2),\qquad
 B\sim \frac{D}{eW},\qquad \log\lfloor B\rfloor\sim ma.             \tag{1}
\]
Here and below \(e=\exp(1)\).
All but a proportion \(O(E e^{-E}+1/B)\) of these levels give two distinct roots in \((2,T)\).
At different levels their monic integer trace polynomials \(S-b\) are pairwise coprime, so choosing one of these smaller exterior traces from each level gives \(\lfloor B\rfloor\) distinct minimal polynomials.

Put \(P(Z)=Z^m S(Z+Z^{-1})\), \(H_m=\log\max_{|Z|=1}|P(Z)|\).
For every nonzero integer \(b\), every monic irreducible factor \(F\) of \(P(Z)-b Z^m\) satisfies
\[
          \frac{\log M(F)}{\deg F}
                  \ge\frac{(\log|b|-H_m)_+}{2m}.                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular any Salem factor requires \(\log|b|\le H_m\ll_A\sqrt m\log(2m)\).
When \(a\to0\), a factor with \(0<\log M(F)\le a\) requires
\[
           \log|b|\le H_m+2ma\,\exp(-c a^{-1/3})
                    =o(ma\,a^J)\qquad\text{for each fixed }J>0      \tag{3}
\]
with an absolute \(c>0\).
For levels in (1) meeting this positive-measure condition the rightmost trace \(r\in(2,T)\) satisfies
\[
                   T-r\sim |b|/D,\qquad -\log(T-r)\sim ma.          \tag{4}
\]
The statement about \(r\) identifies where the given level meets the trace polynomial.
The irreducible factor that triggers (3) may contain a different root.

<a id="slope"></a>
## Size of the slope and the sum of inverse distances

The coordinator supplied the product profile, slope estimate, counting argument and factor tests.
We work with \(d=2m\) and write \(\nu\) for probability equally distributed over the \(d-2\) neutral phases of \(P\).
In the range \(\sigma\le2\) the [neutral Fourier energy](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#energy) is bounded through \(d^4\), and the [endpoint sampling identity](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#sampling) gives the required sum of inverse distances.
For the whole bounded range \(a\le A\), a cutoff at \(d\) suffices for both functions used here.
Indeed the [reflected energy inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) at smoothing \(e^{-1/d}\) has right side \(O_A(d\log(2d))\).
Up to \(d\) its weight \(e^{-2\ell/d}\) is bounded below, and subtracting the two real reflected terms \(2e^{-\ell a}\) costs \(O(\log(2d))\) in the weighted squared sum by the squared triangle bound.
Thus
\[
         \sum_{1\le|\ell|\le d}\frac{|\widehat\nu(\ell)|^2}{|\ell|}
                         \ll_A\frac{\log(2d)}{d}.                    \tag{5}
\]
Dobrowolski's [large-degree bound for total logarithmic measure](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(a\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\) for the input.

The two circle functions whose empirical averages are \((\log D)/(m-1)\) and \(E/(m-1)\) have Fourier expansions
\[
\begin{split}
 \log(T-2\cos\theta)
       &=a-2\sum_{\ell\ge1}e^{-a\ell}\cos(\ell\theta)/\ell,\\
 \frac{u}{T-2\cos\theta}
       &=\tanh(a/2)\left(1+2\sum_{\ell\ge1}e^{-a\ell}\cos(\ell\theta)\right).
\end{split}
\]
The first uses \(T-2\cos\theta=e^a|1-e^{-a+i\theta}|^2\), and the second is the Poisson kernel times \(\tanh(a/2)\).
Each trace occurs twice in the empirical phase average, giving precisely the stated normalization.
The sums of \(|\ell||\widehat f(\ell)|^2\) for these functions are respectively \(2\sum_{\ell\ge1}e^{-2a\ell}/\ell\ll 1+\log^+(1/a)\) and \(2\tanh^2(a/2)\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell e^{-2a\ell}\ll1\).
The absolute tails after \(d\) are \(O_A(e^{-ad}/(d(1-e^{-a})))\) and \(O(e^{-ad})\), negligible by the lower bound on \(a\).
Cauchy–Schwarz in (5) therefore gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \log D &=(m-1)a+
 O_A\!\left(\sqrt{m\log(2m)(1+\log^+(1/a))}\right),\\
 E &=(m-1)\tanh(a/2)+O_A(\sqrt{m\log(2m)}).
\end{split}                                                         \tag{6}
\]
Since \(\tanh(a/2)\asymp_A a\), the errors are lower order relative to \(ma\), and \(E\to\infty\).
The identity \(u/\tanh(a/2)=2\sinh a\) proves the estimate for \(W\) in (1), including when \(a\) stays away from zero.
When \(a\to0\) one has \(E\sim ma/2\).

<a id="profile"></a>
## Level counting and the two inverse branches

For \(0\le s\le u\) the exact product over the trace roots gives
\[
        -S(T-s)=sD\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(1-sv_j).
\]
All factors in the product are positive, including at \(s=u\), since \(x_j<2\).
In coordinates \(y=Ws\) put \(c_j=v_j/W\), so \(\sum c_j=1\) and \(\max c_j<1/E\).
The normalized profile on \(0\le y\le E\) is
\[
      f_m(y)=-\frac WD S(T-y/W)=y\prod_j(1-yc_j)\le y e^{-y}.
\]
On every fixed bounded \(y\)-interval it converges uniformly to \(y e^{-y}\), since \(\sum c_j^2\le1/E\) and the logarithmic errors in the product are \(O(y^2/E)\) there.
For \(y>0\) the logarithmic derivative \(1/y-\sum_j c_j/(1-yc_j)\) is strictly decreasing.
It is positive near zero and negative at one, for the large inputs with \(E>1\).
Its zero \(y_*\) satisfies \(1/(1+1/E)\le y_*<1\) by \(\sum c_j=1\) and \(\max c_j<1/E\).
The maximizer is interior, \(y_*\to1\), and \(WB/D=f_m(y_*)\to 1/e\).
By (6), \(\log D\sim ma\), while \(\log W=O_A(\log(2m))=o(ma)\).
This proves the count asymptotic in (1), including the integer part.

The original trace takes only negative values inside \((2,T)\).
The strictly increasing portion of \(f_m\) from zero through \(y_*\) attains every normalized level in \((0,WB/D]\), which yields the exact integer list.
The decreasing portion through \(E\) contributes a second distinct interior trace exactly for \(|S(2)|<|b|<B\).
Equality at the lower endpoint would place the second solution at \(2\), and an integer at \(B\) gives a tangency.
Since \(f_m(E)\le Ee^{-E}\), one has \(|S(2)|/B=O(Ee^{-E})\).
This proves the exceptional proportion in the statement.

For instance, along any choice of levels with \(|b|/B\to q\in(0,1)\), the two solutions \(y=W(T-t)\) tend to the smaller and larger positive solutions of \(y e^{-y}=q/e\), respectively.
Indeed the second cannot escape to infinity by the same upper bound \(f_m(y)\le ye^{-y}\), and the strict monotonicity of the limiting function on the two sides of one identifies both limits.
For either solution let \(\gamma>1\) solve \(\gamma+\gamma^{-1}=t\).
Since \((T-t)/u=y/E=o(1)\), the derivative of \(\operatorname{arcosh}(t/2)\) on the intervening interval is \((1+o(1))/(2\sinh a)\), from \(\sqrt{t^2-4}\sim\sqrt{T^2-4}\).
Thus \(m(a-\log\gamma)\) has the same limiting value as \(W(T-t)\).
This gives two positive units strictly smaller than \(\sigma\) at these levels.
Their remaining conjugates determine their total measures.

<a id="factors"></a>
## Necessary conditions on a level with a small factor

Every \(P(Z)-bZ^m\) is monic with constant term one.
For any factor root \(\beta\), \(t=\beta+\beta^{-1}\) satisfies \(S(t)=b\).
For \(z=\beta\) or \(\beta^{-1}\) of modulus \(e^{-|\log|\beta||}\), the maximum modulus principle gives
\[
             |b|=|z|^{-m}|P(z)|
                        \le \exp(H_m+m|\log|\beta||).
\]
All conjugates are units, so the sum of their absolute logarithmic moduli over an irreducible factor of degree \(n\) equals \(2\log M(F)\).
This proves (2).
A neutral conjugate gives the stronger \(\log|b|\le H_m\), in particular for every Salem factor with the usual neutral conjugates.
The [confluent determinant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) at degree \(2m\), input measure \(a\le A\) and confluence order \(\lfloor\sqrt{2m}\rfloor\) gives \(H_m\ll_A\sqrt m\log(2m)\).
For levels with \(|b|/B\to q>0\), (1) and (2) even give absolute height at least \((1-o(1))a/2\) in every factor.

Suppose \(a\to0\) and \(0<\log M(F)\le a\).
The degrees \(n\) tend to infinity because bounded-degree monic integer polynomials with bounded measure form a finite set.
The same external Dobrowolski bound applied to \(F\) gives \(n\ge\exp(ca^{-1/3})\) for some absolute \(c>0\).
Rearranging (2), also when \(\log|b|\le H_m\), gives \(\log|b|\le H_m+2ma/n\).
In this range \(A\) can be fixed at one for all sufficiently late inputs.
The bound on \(H_m\) gives \(H_m/(ma)=o(a^J)\) for every fixed \(J>0\), since \(1/a\ll(\log(2m))^3\), and \(\exp(-ca^{-1/3})=o(a^J)\).
This proves (3).
It implies \(W|b|/D\to0\) by (1).
In particular the proportion of the negative levels in (1) admitting a factor with \(0<\log M(F)\le a\) is at most \(\exp(-(1-o(1))ma)\) when \(a\to0\), by counting the integers permitted by (3).
For the rightmost trace take the solution on the increasing branch, so \(y=W(T-r)\le y_*<1\).
It must tend to zero by local uniform convergence and \(f_m(y)=W|b|/D\to0\).
For \(0\le y\le1\), \(1-y\le\prod_j(1-yc_j)\le1\), so \(f_m(y)/y\to1\) along these solutions.
This yields (4) by \(\log D\sim ma\) and (3).
It makes no assertion that the factor containing the rightmost trace is the factor with small positive measure.

<a id="family"></a>
## Realization along the cubic Pisot limit

For the [Salem numbers approaching the real root \(\theta\) of \(X^3-X-1\)](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family), one has \(\theta<\sigma<4/3\), \(\sigma\to\theta\) and degrees \(2m\to\infty\).
Thus their numbers of integer levels are \(\exp(m\log\theta+o(m))\), the proportion supplying two smaller exterior traces tends to one, and \(W\sim m/(2\sinh(\log\theta))\).
For every sequence of levels with \(|b|/B\to q\in(0,1)\), all factors have absolute height at least \((\log\theta)/2-o(1)\), and the two positive exterior roots have the scaled separations just computed.
On these actual inputs any factor with \(0<\log M(F)\le a\) has degree at least three.
Indeed a linear unit is torsion, and for a quadratic unit of positive measure let \(h>1\) be the larger absolute value of its two roots.
The roots are real, since a complex-conjugate unit pair would have both moduli one.
For norm one, \(h+h^{-1}\) is the absolute integer trace and is at least three.
For norm minus one, \(h-h^{-1}\) is a positive integer and is at least one.
In either case \(h\ge(1+\sqrt5)/2>4/3\).
Consequently (2) restricts all possible positive-measure descents in this family to \(\log|b|\le H_m+2ma/3\).
Of the negative levels giving a smaller real trace, at most \(\exp((2/3+o(1))ma)\) can therefore have any factor of positive measure no larger than the input's.
This realizes the count and its two branches at a positive limiting input measure.
The existence of a Salem sequence tending to one and of a smaller-measure factor at one of the surviving levels is unresolved.
