# The density of powers admitting torsion trace interlacing

<a id="statement"></a>
## Salem powers and torsion nodes

Let \(\sigma\) be any Salem number of degree \(2m\) and put \(k=m-1\).
Every \(\sigma^n\), \(n\ge1\), is a Salem number of the same degree.
For a monic integer polynomial \(A\) of degree \(k\) with distinct torsion trace roots \(y_1<\cdots<y_k\), let \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) be the arcsine probabilities of
\[
                 (-\infty,y_1),\ (y_1,y_2),\ldots,(y_{k-1},y_k).
\]
The arcsine probability here is the law of \(2\cos\theta\) for \(\theta\) uniform on \([0,\pi]\).
A torsion trace is \(\xi+\xi^{-1}\) for a root of unity \(\xi\).
The set of positive \(n\) for which \(A\) strictly interlaces the trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^n\) has natural density
\[
                            k!\prod_{j=1}^k p_j .
\]
For \(A=D_k\), defined by \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\), this density is \(k!/(2k^k)>0\).

More generally, fix \(H\ge0\).
Allow any monic integer polynomial \(A\) with simple torsion trace roots and degree \(g=k-h,\ 0\le h\le H\), requiring one powered trace root in each of the \(g+1\) open intervals cut out by the nodes.
For \(g=0\) there is a single interval, the real line.
This is the [partial interlacing condition](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement), which permits the \(h\) remaining roots to be unused.
The union over all such \(A\) has a natural density \(\Delta_H(\sigma)>0\), and uniformly in \(\sigma\) as \(k\to\infty\),
\[
                          \log \Delta_H(\sigma)=-k+O_H(k^{2/3}).
\]
In particular the first successful \(n\) is finite for every \(\sigma\), while every successful \(n\) satisfies \(n\log\sigma\ge\eta(H)>0\).
We also give [density bounds for reciprocal polynomials](#reciprocal) and a [quotient bound for products of powers](#products).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Independent neutral phases and the exact density

The power construction and the use of independent folded phases to supply interlacing powers were found by agent-S.
Choose one representative \(v_j\) of each of the \(k\) conjugate neutral inverse pairs of \(\sigma\).
These representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Indeed, given a relation \(\prod_jv_j^{a_j}=\zeta\) with \(\zeta\) torsion, send \(v_i\) to \(\sigma\) by a Galois automorphism of the splitting field.
Inversion commutes with the automorphism, so all other selected representatives map to unit-circle conjugates.
Taking absolute values gives \(\sigma^{a_i}=1\), hence \(a_i=0\) for every \(i\).
This is the conjugate-pair argument also used in the [degree-eight construction](Yin_C_G0866_v10.md#coordinate-unit-counterexample).
The powered neutral roots \(v_j^{\pm n}\) are consequently distinct, distinct from \(\pm1\) and distinct from \(\sigma^{\pm n}\).
All \(2m\) powered roots are conjugate by transitivity before powering, so the minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^n\) retains them all and is Salem.

For every nonzero \(a\in\mathbb Z^k\) the averages of \((\prod v_j^{a_j})^n\) over \(1\le n\le N\) tend to zero by the finite geometric sum formula.
By uniform approximation of continuous functions by trigonometric polynomials, \((v_1^n,\ldots,v_k^n)\) is equidistributed on the product circle with Haar probability.
Taking each coordinate to its trace therefore gives the product arcsine law, or equivalently independent uniform folded angles in \([0,\pi]\).
Any finite union of the interval occupancy events used here has boundary of product measure zero, since only fixed node values need be excluded.

The exterior trace \(\sigma^n+\sigma^{-n}\) is above every torsion node, including a possible node at \(2\).
Full interlacing is equivalent to the \(k\) neutral traces occupying the specified \(k\) lower intervals once each.
There are \(k!\) disjoint assignments of the labels, proving the product formula even if a bin has zero mass.
The roots of \(D_k\) have folded angles \((2j-1)\pi/(2k)\), \(1\le j\le k\).
The lower endpoint bin has mass \(1/(2k)\) and the other \(k-1\) required bins have mass \(1/k\), giving the stated positive density.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting all torsion node sets of bounded degree

Let \(N(K)\) count the monic squarefree integer polynomials of degree at most \(K\) with all roots torsion traces.
The conjugates of a primitive order-\(\ell\) trace form one orbit of size \(d_\ell=\varphi(\ell)/2\) for \(\ell\ge3\), since equally traced roots of unity are equal or inverse.
The orders \(1,2\) contribute distinct singleton orbits.
Thus a polynomial amounts to a subset of these orbits, with sum of degrees at most \(K\).
For \(\ell\ge3\), \(d_\ell\ge\sqrt{\ell/8}\).
Indeed \(\varphi(p^a)^2/p^a=p^{a-2}(p-1)^2\ge1\), with the sole exception \(p^a=2\), where the ratio is \(1/2\).
Multiplicativity gives \(\varphi(\ell)^2\ge\ell/2\).
For \(0<t\le1\),
\[
  N(K)\le e^{tK}\prod_{\ell\ge1}(1+e^{-t d_\ell}),\qquad
  \sum_{\ell\ge3} e^{-t d_\ell}
        \le\int_0^\infty e^{-t\sqrt{u/8}}du=16/t^2 .
\]
In particular the number of candidates is finite, and \(t=K^{-1/3}\) gives \(\log N(K)=O(K^{2/3})\) for \(K\ge1\).

For fixed \(g=k-h\ge1\), a partial Salem interlacer needs a neutral trace in each of the \(g\) lower bins.
There are at most \(k!/(k-g)!\) choices of one distinct labeled neutral point per bin, and their bin probabilities have product at most \(g^{-g}\) by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.
Thus the density for this \(A\) is at most \(k!/\big((k-g)!\,g^g\big)\).
For fixed \(H\) and large \(k\) its logarithm is at most \(-k+O_H(\log k)\), by \(\log k!=k\log k-k+O(\log(k+1))\).
The finite union has a density by equidistribution and the boundary observation.
Sum the upper bounds over at most \(N(k)\) polynomials and use the single \(D_k\) for the lower bound.
This proves the stated logarithmic estimate, including \(H=0\).
For a successful power the [bounded-defect gap](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement) applies to its actual minimal polynomial, whose Mahler measure is \(\sigma^n>1\), proving the bound on \(n\log\sigma\).

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## Independent neutral pairs with several exterior pairs

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(r\) inverse pairs off the circle and \(q=m-r\) neutral inverse pairs, counted with multiplicity.
Assume the \(q\) neutral representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Form \(P_n\) from all powered roots with their multiplicities and write \(P_n(z)=z^m Q_n(z+z^{-1})\).
The roots of \(P_n\) are algebraic integers in a Galois-invariant multiset, so \(P_n\) is integral and monic.
The inverse pairing gives reciprocity and hence a monic integral trace polynomial \(Q_n\) of degree \(m\), since a symmetric integral Laurent polynomial is an integer linear combination of \(1,D_1,D_2,\ldots\) in the trace variable.
This uses the full power polynomial even when some exterior traces repeat.
We have \(M(P_n)=M(P)^n\) term by term on the roots.

For \(q\ge2\) take \(g=q-1\) and \(A=D_{q-1}\).
Powers for which every one of its \(q\) open bins receives a neutral trace have natural density
\[
                           \frac{q!}{4(q-1)^q}.
\]
Indeed the two outer bins have arcsine mass \(1/(2(q-1))\) and the other \(q-2\) bins have mass \(1/(q-1)\).
The same geometric sums yield the product law on the \(q\) neutral phases.
Every such power has the required alternation with deficit \(h=r\), irrespective of the exterior traces.
For \(q=1\) the corresponding choice \(A=1\) has success density one.

For fixed \(H\ge r\), write \(\underline\Delta,\overline\Delta\) for the lower and upper natural densities of powers admitting any partial torsion interlacer of \(Q_n\) with \(h\le H\).
Uniformly as \(q\to\infty\) with \(r,H\) fixed,
\[
                   \log\underline\Delta=-q+O_H(q^{2/3}),\qquad
                   \log\overline\Delta=-q+O_H(q^{2/3}).
\]
The event just counted gives the lower bound.
For the upper bound, a powered off-circle root \(\alpha^n\) cannot have trace in \([-2,2]\), since every solution of \(z+z^{-1}=x\) for real \(x\in[-2,2]\) lies on the circle.
Thus all \(b=g-1\) internal gaps of a candidate \(A\) of degree \(g=m-1-h\) must be occupied by neutral traces.
If \(b>q\) success is impossible.
Otherwise for large \(q\) one has \(1\le b=q+O_H(1)\), and the upper density for this \(A\) is at most \(q!/\big((q-b)!\,b^b\big)\) by the same injection count.
Summing over \(N(m)\) candidates proves the bound.
Whenever \(M(P)>1\), every successful exponent satisfies \(n\log M(P)\ge\eta(H)\) by the bounded-defect gap applied to \(P_n\), including when \(P_n\) is reducible.

<a id="products"></a>
## Quotient norms for products of powered Salem polynomials

Agent-S also observed a deterministic restriction when many powered factors are combined.
Take any product of \(s\ge1\) powered minimal polynomials of the Salem number above, with arbitrary positive exponents, and let \(S\) be its monic trace polynomial.
Suppose a monic torsion trace polynomial \(A\) of degree \(sk\) strictly alternates with all \(sk\) neutral traces of the product and one of its exterior traces, in the sense of the stated interval condition.
Put \(S=S_0D\), where \(S_0\) has those \(sk+1\) selected simple roots and \(D\) is the product of \(x-T_a\) over the remaining \(s-1\) exterior traces \(T_a>2\), counted with multiplicity.
Let \(J\) be the polynomial quotient of \(S\) by \(A\), of degree \(s\).
For the [circle norm of this quotient](Yin_C_G0345_v40.md#statement) one has, for every \(b>2\),
\[
 \|J\|_1=\int |J(2\cos\theta)|\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
 \ \ge\ \frac{(b-2)(b+2)^{s-1}}{(2s+1)\rho(b)^s},
 \qquad \rho(b)=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^2-4}}2 ,
\]
where the integral is over a full period.
In particular \(|J(-3)|\ge5^{s-1}\), and \(b=3\) gives the denominator \((2s+1)((3+\sqrt5)/2)^s\) against that same numerator.
Taking \(b=2+s^{-2}\) gives the further bound \(\|J\|_1\ge4^{s-1}/(e s^2(2s+1))\), since \(\log\rho(b)\le1/s\) by \(2\cosh(1/s)\ge2+s^{-2}\).

Indeed the [interlacing partial fractions](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#poles) give \(A/S_0=\sum_j p_j/(x-t_j)\), where \(p_j>0,\ \sum_jp_j=1\), and \(S_0/A=x-a-\sum_y\rho_y/(x-y)\) where \(\rho_y>0\) at the nodes of \(A\).
Multiplication by \(D\) and polynomial division give
\[
               \frac{J(x)}{D(x)}
                 =\frac{S_0(x)}{A(x)}
                         +\sum_y\frac{\rho_yD(y)}{(x-y)D(x)}.
\]
At \(x=-b\) all the added terms are negative, since \(D(y)\) has the sign of \(D(-b)\).
All selected trace roots \(t_j\) are greater than \(-2\), so \(A(-b)/S_0(-b)<0\) has absolute value at most \(1/(b-2)\).
Consequently \(|J(-b)|\ge (b-2)|D(-b)|\ge(b-2)(b+2)^{s-1}\).
Finally the Laurent coefficients of \(J(z+z^{-1})\) are bounded in absolute value by \(\|J\|_1\).
Evaluating its at most \(2s+1\) terms at \(z=-\rho(b)\) yields the required upper bound on \(|J(-b)|\) in terms of the norm.

There is also a bound for arbitrary numbers of unused roots when the product has measure at most two.
For any partial torsion interlacer in the [quotient norm theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v40.md#statement), suppose \(\|J\|_1\le B\).
Then \(2s\le K(B)-1\), where \(K(B)\) is an upper bound on the number of terms in its [cleared polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v40.md#measure).
In fact that construction gives \(W A^\sharp=(1-z^2)V P_{\rm prod}\) for a cyclotomic product \(V\), or \(W=P_{\rm prod}\) for an empty interlacer.
At each positive root \(\sigma^{\pm n}\) from a factor with exponent \(n\), the factors \(A^\sharp,(1-z^2)V\) are nonzero.
Hence \(W\) has at least \(2s\) positive roots counted with multiplicity, including repetitions of the same power.
Descartes' rule of signs bounds their number by one less than the number of nonzero terms of \(W\).
