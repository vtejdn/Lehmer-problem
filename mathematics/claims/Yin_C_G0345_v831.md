# Large primes at both poles for almost every section with \(l=hk+1\)

<a id="statement"></a>
## An explicit family with almost linear prime witnesses

For \(M\ge3\) an integer, take \(h=\lceil(\log M)^6\rceil\) and, for each \(M\le k<2M\), set \(l=hk+1\).
Use the [two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) and, at \(j=3,4\), put \(\beta=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\), \(c=(j+3)(j+4)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
If \(S_i\) is a rational irreducible factor of the section of degree \(d_i\), let \(Q_i(U)=\prod_{S_i(z)=0}(U-z^k)\).
For all but \(O(M/\sqrt{\log M})\) values of \(k\), the following holds for **every** \(S_i\) and both poles simultaneously.
There is an integral ideal \(D_{j,k,i}\mid(Q_i(\beta))\) such that, uniformly,
\[
 \log N_{K/\mathbb Q} D_{j,k,i}
           =\big(\tfrac12\log\beta+o(1)\big)d_i .
\]
Each of its prime ideals \(\mathfrak p\) lies over a split rational prime \(p>M\), and the residue of \(\rho=(-c)\beta^{-h}\) has multiplicative order greater than \(M^{4/5}\).
It is a root of \(\bar S_i\) in \(\mathbb F_p\).
At each fixed pole the ideals can be chosen pairwise coprime across all factors and all the indicated directions.
Thus each factor has a pole prime \(p> M=l^{1-o(1)}\) with a root of order \(>M^{4/5}=l^{4/5-o(1)}\) at each pole.
Each rational prime can underlie at most two of the assigned ideals at one pole.
The assertion concerns divisors of the individual pole resultants and does not presume irreducibility of the sections.

<a id="repetitions"></a>
## The sum of all repeated prime ideal exponents

At each \(k\) the product of the \((Q_i(\beta))\) is generated, up to a unit, by \(\gamma_k=(-c)^k-\beta^{hk+1}\) by the [fiber identity](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#derivative).
All its prime ideals are prime to \(c\).
The ideal \((\gamma_k)\) also equals \((\rho^k-\beta)\), and \(\rho\) is a unit at every prime under consideration.
For each prime ideal in the product over \(k\), choose \(k_0\) maximizing \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\gamma_{k_0}\).
If \(s=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\gamma_k>0\) at \(k\ne k_0\), then \(\rho^k\equiv\rho^{k_0}\equiv\beta\bmod\mathfrak p^s\).
Consequently \(\rho^{|k-k_0|}\equiv1\) and also \(\beta^{|k-k_0|}\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak p^s\).
For each \(\mathfrak p\) and distance \(a\ge1\), there are at most two such indices \(k\).
Although the center depends on \(\mathfrak p\), summing first at fixed distance gives
\[
 \sum_{\mathfrak p}\sum_{k\ne k_0(\mathfrak p)}
       \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\gamma_k\,\log N\mathfrak p
 \le 2\sum_{a=1}^{M-1}\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta^a-1)|\ll M^2.            \tag{1}
\]
The last bound uses the two fixed embeddings and the nontorsion of \(\beta\).
In particular the estimate is independent of \(h\).

At the chosen direction keep only the factor with largest \(\mathfrak p\)-valuation of \(Q_i(\beta)\), retaining precisely its own exponent.
The total log norm lost by this operation is \(O(\log(3+k+l))\) per direction by the [derivative bound for nondominant exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#derivative).
Discard all inert and ramified primes.
Their whole contribution is \(O(k+1)\) per direction since the ideal gcd of \((\gamma_k)\) with its conjugate divides \((c^{2k}-(-1)^l)\) by the [conjugation calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#fiber), and conjugation fixes every nonsplit prime.
Summed over the interval, these discards and (1) cost \(O(M^2+M\log(3+2M(h+1)))\).

<a id="orders"></a>
## The primes of small size or small multiplicative order

Write \(L=\log(3+2M(h+1))\), so \(L\asymp\log M\) for our choice of \(h\).
At a retained split prime, the [fixed-input estimate at one pole](Yin_C_G0345_v820.md#local) bounds its valuation by \(O(pL)\).
Each rational prime has at most two retained occurrences, so the primes \(p\le Y\) cost \(O(Y^2L)\) over the whole interval, by \(\sum_{p\le Y}p\log p\ll Y^2\).

For a prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\nmid c\) let \(g_{\mathfrak p}\) be the order of the subgroup generated by the reductions of \(\beta,-c\).
At any occurrence in \(\gamma_k\), the relations \(\beta\equiv\rho^k\) and \(-c=\rho\beta^h\) show that \(g_{\mathfrak p}\) is the order of \(\bar\rho\).
If also \(\mathfrak p\mid Q_i(\beta)\), reducing the defining product gives a root \(z\) of \(S_i\) whose \(k\)-th power reduces to \(\beta\) at an extension of this place.
The pole equation gives \(z^l\equiv-c\), so \(z\equiv\rho\) since \(l-hk=1\).

The fixed numbers \(\beta,-c\) are multiplicatively independent, because \(\beta\) is a nontorsion unit and \(c>1\) is a rational integer.
At a split prime, [Theorem 3 of Bugeaud and Laurent](../audits/literature/BL96/Yin_U001.md#bl96-u001) applied to their respective positive powers \(l,k\) gives
\[
          \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\gamma_k
                           \ll g_{\mathfrak p}(1+L/\log p)^2.
\]
Indeed \(D=2\), both height majorants can be chosen as a fixed multiple of \(\log p\), and the maximum in the source theorem is \(O(L+\log p)\).
To count primes of order at most \(T\ge2\), use the \((\lceil\sqrt T\rceil+1)^2\) products \(\bar\beta^a(-\bar c)^b\) with \(0\le a,b\le\lceil\sqrt T\rceil\).
Two coincide if \(g_{\mathfrak p}\le T\).
For at least one nonzero difference vector \((a',b')\) in that box of differences, \(\mathfrak p\) divides the numerator ideal of \(\beta^{a'}(-c)^{b'}-1\).
Independence ensures this is nonzero.
Clearing the rational denominator by a power of \(c\) gives an algebraic integer of absolute norm at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt T))\), by the two fixed embeddings, also for negative \(a'\) since \(\beta\) is a unit.
There are \(O(T)\) difference vectors.
Therefore
\[
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\nmid c:\ g_{\mathfrak p}\le T}\log N\mathfrak p
       \ll T^{3/2}.
\]
After (1) each prime ideal appears at most once, so deleting all remaining primes of order at most \(T\) costs \(O(T^{5/2}L^2)\) over the interval.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Uniformity across every factor at the remaining directions

Take \(Y=M,\ T=M^{4/5}\).
All the discarded log norms at both poles together are \(O(M^2(\log M)^2)\).
Consequently at most \(O(M/\sqrt{\log M})\) directions lose more than \(M(\log M)^{5/2}\).
For every factor at every direction in this family, the [uniform degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#degrees) gives
\[
 d_i\gg l(\log\log l/\log l)^3\gg M(\log M)^3(\log\log M)^3,
\]
since \(k\asymp M\), \(h\asymp(\log M)^6\), and the directions are all primitive and steep.
The loss at any remaining factor and pole is consequently \(o(d_i)\).
The [individual pole size](Yin_C_G0345_v802.md#size) gives the stated norm formula before discarding, and hence afterwards.
At each pole only the maximizing direction and one of its factors can retain a given prime ideal.
The split prime and the order witness exist since the retained log norm tends to infinity.
