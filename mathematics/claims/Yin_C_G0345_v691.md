# How cyclotomic degrees restrict radicals of Salem numbers at large integer seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## An effective test for every field radical

Fix a delay \(n\ge7\) and a sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
For each integer \(a\ge3\) let \(r\) denote the Salem root of \(G(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), let \(P\) be its minimal polynomial and put \(L=n-1,\ U=G/P,\ A=(X^L-\epsilon)/U\).
The [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) makes \(A\) independent of \(a\).
For all sufficiently large \(a\), effectively in \(n\), every positive integer \(q\) with \(\beta=r^{1/q}\in\mathbb Q(r)\) obeys
\[
                2q\mid\varphi(j)\quad\text{for every }\Phi_j\mid A(X^q).
\]
More precisely exactly \(\varphi(j)/q\) of the neutral conjugates of \(\beta\) have their nearest lifts at primitive \(j\)-th roots, in the pairing described below.

For a prime \(q=p\), the degree test is equivalent to
\[
 p\mid\varphi(s)/2
 \quad\text{for each }\Phi_s\mid A\text{ with }p\nmid s.
\]
In particular if \(\epsilon=+1\) and \(12\mid n-1\), there is an effective absolute \(C\) such that
\[
                     \log a>C n^3\log^6(3n)
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(X^h)\ \text{is irreducible for every }h\ge1 .
\]
All these substitutions then retain the full Mahler measure \(r\), including at seeds with large square factors in \(a(a-1)(a+1)\).

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Distributing neutral conjugates among torsion orders

Let \(d=\deg P,\ D=d-2=\deg A\).
For every \(q\), the \(D\) nonreal neutral conjugates \(w\) of \(\beta\) map bijectively under \(w\mapsto w^q\) to those of \(r\), since the two elements generate the same field.
The [circle-root pairing](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#angles) assigns each \(w^q\) a distinct root of \(A\) at angular distance at most \(\pi/(aL)\).
Taking its nearest \(q\)-th root to \(w\) gives a label \(\rho\) at angular distance at most \(\pi/(aqL)\).
This step only uses the covering of the circle by the power map and holds for composite \(q\) as well.
No label is real, and conjugate labels occur in pairs.
The possible labels constitute all roots of the monic squarefree cyclotomic product \(A(X^q)\).

For each factor \(\Phi_j\) of this product put \(k_j=\varphi(j)\), and let \(b_j\) count the labels actually chosen at this order.
Every \(b_j\) is even.
At a conjugate bearing such a label, one root factor of \(\Phi_j(w)\) is at most \(\pi/(aqL)\) in modulus and all others are at most two.
All factors at other neutral conjugates are at most two.
The factors at \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{-1}\) contribute at most \(4^{k_j}\beta^{k_j}\) together.
Since \(\beta^q<a+1\le4a/3\), the nonzero integral norm therefore gives
\[
               1\le |N\Phi_j(\beta)|\le C_{n,q}\,a^{k_j/q-b_j}
\]
with an effective constant uniform in the seed and in \(j\).
For example the product estimates above allow \(C_{n,q}=2^{(D+4)qL}\), using \(k_j\le qL\) and \(\pi/(qL)<1\).
If \(b_j>k_j/q\), the exponent is at most \(-1/q\), giving \(a\le C_{n,q}^q\).
The [uniform exponent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) restricts \(q\) to an effective finite set depending on \(n\).
Beyond the maximum of these finitely many cutoffs we have \(b_j\le k_j/q\) for every \(j\).
Now \(\sum b_j=D=\sum k_j/q\), since \(A(X^q)\) has degree \(qD\).
This forces equality term by term and proves the divisibility test.

<a id="primes"></a>
## A prime test on the original orders

If \(p\nmid s\), the preimages of primitive \(s\)-th roots under \(p\)-th powering have orders \(s\) and \(ps\), of degrees \(\varphi(s)\) and \((p-1)\varphi(s)\).
If \(p\mid s\), all preimages have order \(ps\) and degree \(p\varphi(s)\).
The original orders all exceed two, so their totients are even.
These formulas give the prime test.
For \(\epsilon=+1,\ 12\mid L\), the roots of \(A\) include both the primitive third and fourth roots.
For every prime \(p\) at least one of these two orders \(s\) is coprime to \(p\), and \(\varphi(s)=2\).
Thus no prime satisfies the test.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## A uniform cutoff using the two quadratic factors

In the same progression, suppose some prime \(p\) does give a field radical.
Choose \(s\in\{3,4\}\) coprime to \(p\).
Then \(\Phi_s\mid A(X^p)\) and
\[
               1\le |N\Phi_s(\beta)|\le |NA(r)|=1
\]
by multiplicativity, integrality and the [resultant at even \(L\) with positive sign](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#resultant).
If any label has order \(s\), at least two do by conjugation.
The explicit root estimates in the allocation argument, now with \(k_j=2\), give \(1\le 2^{2D+6} a^{2/p-2}\).
As \(p\ge2\), this implies \(\log a\ll d\).

If none does, every label is distinct from each of the two roots of \(\Phi_s\).
They all lie in the grid of \(2pL\)-th roots, and hence each corresponding angular distance is at least \(\pi/(pL)\).
The angular error \(\pi/(apL)\) leaves at least \(2\pi/(3pL)\) at \(w\), giving chord length at least \(1/(pL)\) to either root.
Thus \(|\Phi_s(w)|\ge(pL)^{-2}\) for all neutral \(w\).
For \(\beta\ge2\) the two real evaluations have product of absolute values at least \(\beta^2/16\), using the reverse triangle inequality on each root factor.
The upper norm bound gives \(\beta\le4(pL)^D\).
This bound also covers \(\beta<2\).
Consequently \(\log(a-1)<p\log\beta\ll p d\log(3pL)\).
The [delay bound](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) gives \(p\ll n^2\log^5(3n)\), proving the stated cutoff.
Any field radical index exceeding one has a prime divisor giving a prime field radical.
The [substitution criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now gives irreducibility, and taking all \(h\)-th roots of all conjugates gives Mahler measure \(r\).
