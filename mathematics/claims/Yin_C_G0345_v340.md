# An evaluated two-interval Mahler bound from the occupied exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## A width and a multiplicative threshold

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(N\ge1\) and nonzero constant coefficient.
If its support lies in two intervals of length at most \(2^{-226}N\), then
\[
        M(P)\le 13/10
             \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        P(X)=U(X)(1\pm X^{N-\deg U}),                                      \tag{1}
\]
where \(U\) is the actual low end block.
In particular, all irreducible inputs of positive logarithmic measure in these two intervals satisfy \(M(P)>13/10\).
No coefficient bound or squarefreeness condition is imposed.
This concrete width is a sufficient bound obtained by evaluating the local constants.

There is also an estimate from the actual exponents.
Suppose the end intervals are \([0,W]\) and \([N-W,N]\), \(0\le W<N/2\).
Let \(a=\deg U\ge1,\ n=N-a,\ D=n/a\), and let \(L=\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) is the real zero of \(x^3-x-1\).
If \(D\ge2^{218}\) and the high block is not a signed translate of \(U\), then
\[
       \log M(P)\ge L-
          \frac{2^{217}(1+L)+2^{217}+512\log(2D)}{D}.                       \tag{2}
\]
For \(a=0\) the lower bound in this case is \(L\).
The [sharp limiting threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#statement) identifies \(L\); here the degree-dependent error and an admissible numerical width are evaluated.

## Evaluating the diagonal clipping estimate

Use two fast exponents \(0,1\) with scale \(n\), so that the normalized frequencies are exactly \(0,1\).
In the [finite-type formulas](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#finite-type) put \(s=2,\ p=4,\ B=3,\ \gamma=1\).
For [the diagonal cost calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#analytic-cost) use \(B_0=6\).
Substitution gives the following exact integers; subscripts and letters in the first column refer to the formulas in those two sections.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| \(V\) | 162 |
| \(1/a_{\mathrm{test}}\), the inverse derivative threshold of the finite-type test | 5184 |
| \(1/\eta_{\mathrm{test}}\), the inverse tolerance for moving coefficients | 21233664 |
| \(J\) | 17915904 |
| \(C_{\rm sub}\) | 17832200896512 |
| \(F_0\) | 442679577831968538259095552 |
| \(D_*\) | 3802588618790782910247480340758134784 |
| \(C_*\) | 161599519146588735487247035424825357273507845211859584011556880384 |

For example \(V=2\cdot3^4\), \(1/a_{\mathrm{test}}=32V\), \(T=8\cdot3^3\), \(J=16T/a_{\mathrm{test}}\), and \(C_{\rm sub}=192J/a_{\mathrm{test}}\).
The last three entries are obtained from equation (3) of the diagonal cost calculation, with \(\delta=2^{-18}, H_0=64^4\) and \(L_{\rm loc}=2^{50}\).
In particular \(D_*<2^{122}\) and \(C_*<2^{217}\).

When \(m(P)<L\), the [reciprocity argument](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) gives \(P=U+\sigma X^n U^*\), \(\sigma=\pm1\), where \(U^*=X^aU(X^{-1})\).
For the lift \(U+\sigma YU^*\), the independent logarithmic mean equals \(m(U)\).
After dividing the two rows by their integral Laurent gcd \(h\), the independent and diagonal means \(I,\mu\) are respectively \(m(U)-m(h)\) and \(m(P)-m(h)\).
The joint width of the quotient rows is at most \(a\), so a common Laurent shift places them in \([-a,a]\).
The nonnegative independent mean satisfies \(I\le m(U)\).
If \(D\ge D_*\), the loss from clipping the relative diagonal logarithm below at \(\log u\) is at most \(C_*\{u+(1+I)/D\}\) for \(0<u<1\).

For fixed fast angle, the [explicit variation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v334.md#clipped) for the clipped relative logarithm is at most \(512a\log(2/u)\), since \(32p^2=512\).
Conditioning on that fast angle leaves the \(n\) equally spaced, shifted points of the slow circle.
The [shifted grid estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#quadrature) thus bounds the difference of the two clipped means by \(512\log(2/u)/D\).
For the lower bound on \(\mu\), the clipped independent mean is at least the unmodified independent mean after the common baseline is removed.
Consequently
\[
       m(P)\ge m(U)-C_*\{u+(1+m(U))/D\}-512\log(2/u)/D .
\]
Choose \(u=1/D\).
For \(D\ge2^{218}\), the coefficient \(1-2^{217}/D\) of \(m(U)\) is positive.
If the end blocks are not signed translates, \(m(U)\ge L\) by the same reciprocity argument.
This gives (2).
If \(m(P)\ge L\) the inequality is immediate, and the constant-block assertion follows from reciprocity itself.

## Comparing with the rational cutoff

Take \(W\le 2^{-226}N\).
When \(a\ge1\) this gives \(D\ge2^{226}-1\ge2^{225}\).
The number \(L\) is less than one, so the power-of-two terms divided by \(D\) in (2) total less than \(3/256\).
The function \(\log(2D)/D\) is decreasing here, and \(512\log(2\cdot2^{225})/2^{225}<1/1024\) since \(\log2<1\).
The total loss is less than \(13/1024\).
The inequality \((33/25)^3-33/25-1<0\) gives \(\theta>33/25\).
Hence \(L-\log(13/10)>\log(66/65)\ge1/66>13/1024\).
Thus a polynomial whose end blocks are not signed translates has \(M(P)>13/10\), also when \(a=0\).
A signed translate of a nonconstant \(U\) gives an explicit factorization; if \(U\) is constant and \(M(P)\le13/10\) its absolute value is one and \(m(P)=0\).
This proves (1) and its assertion for irreducibles.
