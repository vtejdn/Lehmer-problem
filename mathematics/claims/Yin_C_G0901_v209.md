# Exact full-degree periods at two nearby times in the trace construction

<a id="times"></a>
## The full degree can return a fixed distance from the target

Use the [strengthened zero-boundary rows](Yin_C_G0901_v203.md#statement) with fixed \(p=3^\nu,\ q=p-1,\ h\) and \(u=2^h\).
The degree is \(d=2tS\), the even-modulus order is \(A=tn\), and the prescribed second order is \(N=A+2u\).
On every sufficiently large such row of the construction there is a one-denominator word \(F_A\) with second order exactly \(A\), already attained modulo three.
It keeps the row's degree, prescribed prefix, even-side word, exact pole and real estimates.
For a word \(F\) write \(k_j(F)=\deg\gcd(F\bmod3,X^j-1)\).
There is an unbounded sequence of the same rows admitting two choices with
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
    & k_A & k_N\\ \hline
 F_A & d & \le 1\\
 F_N & \le 3 & d-o(d).
 \end{array}
\]
Here the second order of \(F_N\) is \(N3^b\) and, for any prescribed \(\omega(d)\to\infty\), one can require \(d/\omega(d)\le3^b=o(d)\).
The two realizations may select different characteristic-zero polynomials.
An unbounded sequence attaining the exact second order \(N\) in degree \(d\) is still unknown.

<a id="divisors"></a>
## Reserving the exact order while filling every central even degree

On the [original period rows](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical) one has
\[
 n=15\cdot7^k11^j(2^a-1)(2^{a+2}-1),\quad
 7^k,11^j\asymp2^a/a^2,\quad t\nmid n ,
\]
where \(a\) is odd, the five displayed factors \(15,7^k,11^j,2^a-1,2^{a+2}-1\) are pairwise coprime, and \(a\to\infty\).
There is an even integer \(E\ll2^a\ll n^{1/4}\log(2n)\) such that every even degree in \([E,n-1]\) occurs for a monic reciprocal integer divisor \(V\) of \(X^n-1\) whose reduction modulo three has exact order \(n\).
We prove this with the entire product of a fixed set of cyclotomic factors of total degree \(E\) present in each divisor.

Begin with \(m=15\) and reserve \(\Phi_{15}\), of degree eight.
Of the other factors omit \(X-1\); the degrees two and four of \(\Phi_3,\Phi_5\) give every even sum through six.
Suppose at modulus \(m\) the factors outside the reserved degree \(E_m\) and \(X-1\) give every even sum from zero to \(m-1-E_m\).
The unrestricted integer divisors of \(X^m-1\) have every degree from zero to \(m\) in the stages below, by the [prime-power induction](Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#practical), which also works when \(7^k\) is adjoined before \(11^j\).
Indeed each new prime \(r\) is at most \(m+1\) when adjoined.
This is the degree interval used in [Thompson, Lemma 4.3](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.866).

Adjoin a new prime power \(r^e\) with \(r\nmid m\) and put \(l_i=\varphi(r^i)\).
The factors with indices \(r^i c,\ c\mid m\), have degrees \(l_i\varphi(c)\).
For \(1\le i<e\) they give all multiples \(l_i z,\ 0\le z\le m\).
At level \(e\) reserve the single factor \(\Phi_{r^e}\).
The remaining factors on that level give every \(l_e z\) with \(z\) even from zero to \(m-1\), since the only odd-degree factor at modulus \(m\) is \(X-1\).
These are all new levels, so previously reserved factors restrict only the starting interval.
At each intermediate stage \(i\) the old interval of even sums ends at \(mr^{i-1}-E_m-1\).
Its translates leave no even gaps if \(l_i\le mr^{i-1}-E_m+1\), and its upper end then becomes \(mr^i-E_m-1\).
At the final level the needed spacing is \(2l_e\le mr^{e-1}-E_m+1\).
The final interval ends at \(mr^e-1-E_m-l_e\), as required to continue with \(E_{mr^e}=E_m+l_e\).

First adjoin \(7^k\).
At the intermediate stages \(6\cdot7^{i-1}\le15\cdot7^{i-1}-7\), and at the top \(12\cdot7^{k-1}\le15\cdot7^{k-1}-7\) for \(k\ge2\).
Next adjoin \(11^j\).
Here \(m=15\cdot7^k\) and \(E_m=8+6\cdot7^{k-1}\), so \(2(11-1)\le m-E_m+1\) for large \(k\), which suffices for every level.
Then adjoin in any order the exact prime powers in \(2^a-1\) and \(2^{a+2}-1\).
Each is less than \(2^{a+2}\).
Their total sum is at most \((2^a-1)+(2^{a+2}-1)\), since the sum of distinct prime-power factors of an odd integer greater than one is at most their product.
Accordingly the reserved degree stays \(O(2^a)\), while \(m\ge15\cdot7^k11^j\asymp 2^{2a}/a^4\).
Thus \(2(r-1)\le m-E_m+1\) throughout, giving the required interval.
Adding the reserved degree back gives the stated full degree range.

The reserved factor \(\Phi_{15}\) reduces to \(\Phi_5^2\) modulo three.
Its roots have order five and multiplicity two, so its reduction has polynomial order fifteen.
Each other reserved \(\Phi_{r^e}\) is separable modulo three and its roots have order \(r^e\).
The resulting divisor \(V\) has order divisible by the least common multiple of these witnesses and divides \(X^n-1\) integrally, giving exact order \(n\) modulo three.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Preserving the full row and comparing the two returns

On the strengthened rows \(2S/n=d/A\to1/\theta\), where \(\theta=u\log p/\log(p/q)>1\).
Hence \(E\le2S\le n-1\) eventually.
For a divisor \(V\) of that degree set \(W=V(X^t)\).
It has degree \(d\), and the coefficients through \(L=(p+3)/2<t\) vanish after the constant term.
It divides \(X^{tn}-1\) integrally and has exact order \(tn\) modulo three: in the quotient with \(Y=X^t\), the elements \(1,X,\ldots,X^{t-1}\) form a free basis over \(\mathbb F_3[Y]/V\).
Thus a power of \(X\) equal to one has exponent divisible by \(t\), and the resulting power of \(Y\) equal to one has exponent divisible by \(n\).
Reduction and integral divisibility sandwich its order modulo \(3^\nu\) at \(tn\).
The [pole correction](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#trace) lifts this word modulo \(p\), retaining the other data of the specified row.

As \(A\) is odd and \(N-A=2u\) is a power of two, \(\gcd(A,N)=1\).
Since \(3\nmid N\), the gcd degree at time \(N\) for \(F_A\) is at most one.
On the [rows with a nearly quadratic delay](Yin_C_G0901_v207.md#repetitions) select their other word \(F_N\).
All its residue root orders divide \(N\) and its squarefree divisor returning at \(N\) has degree \(d-o(d)\).
The only possible common root with \(X^A-1\) is one.
It has multiplicity three in \(X^A-1\), since \(v_3(A)=1\) by the factorization of \(n\).
This gives all four rank bounds.

## How much frequency coverage the synchronized rows allow

For a positive integer \(K<N\) write \(\Omega_K=\{\pm 3^ji\bmod N:j\ge0,\ 1\le i\le K\}\).
On simultaneous zero-boundary rows one can choose \(K\to\infty\) such that in **every** nontrivial subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/N\) the proportion of its nonzero frequencies covered by \(\Omega_K\) tends to zero uniformly.
Also \(|\Omega_K|=o(d)\), so the clique-size condition in the [full-degree Fourier test](Yin_C_G0901_v207.md#frequencies) is automatic there, even at this enlarged cutoff.

Indeed the [synchronization](Yin_C_G0901_v198.md#period-completion) can impose \(l\) different large odd primes in \(N\).
Put \(f_N=|\langle3,-1\rangle\bmod N|\).
Reduction modulo squares at the chosen primes gives \(f_N\le2^{2-l}N\), as in the [orbit bound](Yin_C_G0901_v205.md#large-roots).
For \(R\mid N\) the multiples of \(R\) in \(\Omega_K\) come only from \(i\) that are multiples of \(R\), as \(3\) is a unit.
There are at most \(\lfloor K/R\rfloor f_N\) such residues in the subgroup of size \(w=N/R\).
If \(w>1\), division by \(w-1\) bounds the covered proportion by \(2K f_N/N\).
For any prescribed increasing sequence of cutoffs, choose \(l\) at each stage so that \(K2^{2-l}\to0\), then take an arbitrarily large row at that stage.
The same choices are compatible with the successive prime prescriptions in the [delayed-word construction](Yin_C_G0901_v207.md#repetitions) by imposing further primes before choosing the degree.
Finally, fixing one point of a difference clique injects its other points into \(\Omega_K\), and \(N/d\to\theta\).
This establishes a limitation of the frequency tests on actual rows, without imposing additional vanishing coefficients on the full words.
