# Large modular returns on linear-width blocks and sparse completions

<a id="records"></a>
## Four surviving positions and the cost to the cofactor

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit polynomial of degree \(d\) and \(C\) a monic integral polynomial of degree \(c\) with unit constant term.
Suppose \(F=PC\) is reciprocal up to sign and its reduction modulo two has two or four nonzero terms.
Write \(D=d+c\).
There is an integer \(D/2<N\le D\) for which \(X^N-1\) divides \(\bar F\).
For two terms take \(N=D\).
For four, reciprocity over \(\mathbb F_2\) gives the factorization
\[
                 1+X^i+X^{D-i}+X^D=(1+X^i)(1+X^{D-i}),
                  \qquad 0<i<D/2 .
\]
Take \(N=D-i\).
In either case \(b_p(j)=\deg\gcd(P\bmod p,X^j-1)\) satisfies \(b_2(N)\ge N-c\), since the cofactor can supply at most \(c\) degrees.
The [record ceiling and binomial deficit](Yin_C_G0904_v6.md#deficit) show that \(M(P)\le19/16\) forces
\[
                  c\ge\deg\gcd(\bar C,X^N-1)>N/40>D/80,\qquad c>d/79 .
\]
For an admissible boundary cofactor the sharper necessary condition is \(\sum_h m_h\gcd(h,N)>N/40\), with \(m_h\) the cycle counts. [Boundary overlap](Yin_C_G0904_v6.md#boundary)

In particular every [four-term \((X-1)P\) construction](Yin_C_G0883_v10.md#four) of degree \(d\ge4\) satisfies \(M(P)>\sqrt{3/2}\).
In the four-term residue case the factor \(X-1\) already divides \(1+X^i\) in characteristic two, giving \(b_2(N)=N\) and \(d<2N\).
The [determinant inequality](Yin_C_G0904_v5.md#binomial) gives
\(6Nm\ge5N\log2-d\log(16/(3\sqrt3))>3N\log(3/2)\), with \(m=\log M(P)\).
In the two-term residue case \(N=d+1\) and \(b_2(N)=d\), and the same inequality gives \(6(d+1)m\ge d\log(6\sqrt3)>3(d+1)\log(3/2)\).
Thus the whole four-term class with these two fixed punctures lies above the [known strict-record ceiling from degree ten](Yin_C_G0904_v5.md#record), including its low-coefficient members.

## What a macroscopic return forces in a sparse multiple

Fix a prime \(p\) and let \(F\in\mathbb F_p[X]\) have \(k\ge2\) terms and nonzero constant term.
Suppose the reduction of \(P\) divides \(F\).
For any \(j\ge1\) put \(b=b_p(j)>0\) and choose any integer \(H>2j/b\).
There is a divisor \(g\mid j\) with \(g\le H^{k-1}\), and an element \(\xi\in\overline{\mathbb F}_p\) with \(\xi^g=1\), such that \(L=j/g\) satisfies
\[
  X^L-\xi\mid F,\qquad
  \deg\gcd(\bar P,X^L-\xi)\ge (b-2j/H)/g .                         \tag{1}
\]
All divisibilities and degrees involving \(\xi\) are over the algebraic closure and include multiplicities, even if \(p\mid j\).

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1}\) be the nonconstant exponents.
In \([0,1)^{k-1}\) place the vectors of fractional parts of \(u(e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1})/j\) for \(0\le u\le H^{k-1}\).
Two lie in the same one of the \(H^{k-1}\) half-open cubes of side \(1/H\).
Their indices yield \(1\le q\le H^{k-1}\) such that every \(q e_i\bmod j\) has a signed representative of magnitude \(<j/H\).
Put \(g=\gcd(q,j)\), \(L=j/g\).
For each \(\xi^g=1\) choose \(\omega^L=\xi\).
The substitution \(X=\omega Z^{q/g}\) identifies the algebra \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p[X]/(X^L-\xi)\) with \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p[Z]/(Z^L-1)\).
Indeed \(\gcd(q/g,L)=1\) permutes the powers forming a basis even at inseparable orders.
Under this substitution \(F\) has a Laurent representative supported in \((-L/H,L/H)\), by dividing the signed representatives by \(g\).
If \(X^L-\xi\) does not divide \(F\), this Laurent polynomial is nonzero.
Its exponent span is at most \(2L/H\), so the quotient by it has dimension at most \(2L/H\) on imposing \(Z^L=1\).
The dimension is \(\deg\gcd(F,X^L-\xi)\).

Write \(g=g_0p^s\) with \(p\nmid g_0\).
The \(g_0\) distinct polynomials \(X^L-\xi\) are pairwise coprime and the product of their \(p^s\)-th powers is \(X^j-1\).
For any polynomial \(U\), one has \(\deg\gcd(\bar P,U^{p^s})\le p^s\deg\gcd(\bar P,U)\).
Thus the factors not wholly dividing \(F\) contribute at most \(g_0p^s(2L/H)=2j/H\) to \(b\).
At least one of the remaining factors accounts for at least \((b-2j/H)/g\) degrees of \(\bar P\) before thickening, proving (1).
Kelley's distinct-root estimate for the full multiplicative group of a finite field also relates sparse roots to a full coset. [Kelley, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00208v3#page=3)
The present estimate records the observation time, multiplicities and portion retained by \(P\).

If \(j\le Ad\) and \(b\ge\eta d\), choose \(H=\max(2,\lceil4A/\eta\rceil)\).
Then \(L\ge\eta d/H^{k-1}\) and the gcd in (1) is at least \(\eta L/(2A)\).
In particular for bounded residue support \(k\), positive-fraction linear returns over a prescribed prime list are equivalent to the presence, over one of those primes, of a binomial \(X^L-\xi\) dividing the chosen multiple, with \(L\asymp d\), \(\operatorname{ord}\xi=O(1)\), and a positive fraction of its degree retained by \(\bar P\).
For the converse take \(j=L\operatorname{ord}\xi\).
For \(k=o(\log d)\) the necessary length in (1) is still \(d^{1-o(1)}\).
These tests apply at linear end width and concern the residue support, even when the integral polynomial is dense.
When testing a candidate \(L\), collect the terms of \(F\) at each exponent residue modulo \(L\), weighting \(a_e\) by \(\xi^{\lfloor e/L\rfloor}\).
The binomial divides \(F\) exactly when each sum vanishes, so every occupied class must have at least two terms.

## A whole Salem family avoiding any prescribed prime list at linear width

Given a finite prime set \(S\), choose an odd prime \(\ell\notin S\) and a fixed positive integer \(a>2\ell+2\) divisible by \(\ell^2\) and all the primes in \(S\).
For powers \(g=\ell^v\ge5\) set
\[
 P_g=X^{2g}+1-a(X^{2g-1}+X)
       -\ell\sum_{h=1}^2(X^{g+h}+X^{g-h}).
\]
These are irreducible positive Salem polynomials of degree \(d=2g\), of fixed coefficient height for this \(S\).
They are complete separated closures \(X^{g+1}Q+Q^*\) of reduced width \(r=g-1\), since \(g-1\) is the last occupied position before the center.
Their measures strictly decrease to \(a\).
Nevertheless for all \(p\in S\) and \(j\ge1\) one has \(b_p(j)\le4\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor\).
At \(\ell\) the whole degree returns at \(j=4g=2d\).

For irreducibility write \(Y=X+X^{-1}\) and \(D_h(Y)=X^h+X^{-h}\).
The monic trace polynomial is \(T_g=D_g-aD_{g-1}-\ell(D_1+D_2)\).
Modulo \(\ell\) it is \(Y^g\) by Frobenius.
Since \(g\) is odd, \(D_g(0)=0\), \(D_1(0)+D_2(0)=-2\) and \(\ell^2\mid a\), its constant term is \(2\ell\bmod\ell^2\).
Eisenstein gives irreducibility.
At \(Y=2\cos(t\pi/(g-1))\), \(t=0,\ldots,g-1\), the term \(-aD_{g-1}\) dominates the rest, which has absolute value at most \(2+4\ell\).
The signs alternate starting negative at two.
This gives \(g-1\) roots inside \((-2,2)\) and one above two.
Thus \(Y^2-4\) has a negative real conjugate in the trace field and cannot be a square there, so \(P_g\) is irreducible of degree \(2g\) with exactly one exterior root \(\alpha_g>1\).
At \(X=a\) the outer four terms sum to \(1-a^2<0\) and the other terms are negative, giving \(\alpha_g>a\).
For fixed real \(x>a\) the expression \(x^{-g}P_g(x)\) equals
\[
  x^{g-1}(x-a)+x^{-g}(1-ax)-\ell(x+x^{-1}+x^2+x^{-2}).
\]
It increases strictly with \(g\) to \(+\infty\), so the unique exterior roots strictly decrease to \(a\).

For \(p\in S\), \(X^{-g}\bar P_g\) is the specialization at \(U=X,V=X^g\) of \(V+V^{-1}-\ell(U+U^{-1}+U^2+U^{-2})\).
For any primitive character relation \((u_0,v_0)\), the exponents along a level of that character are \(\pm u_0,\pm v_0,\pm2v_0\).
A nonzero extremal exponent occurs just once unless \(|u_0|=2|v_0|>0\), when the exponents \(\pm v_0\) occur just once.
The corresponding coefficients are nonzero, so the restriction to each level is nonzero.
As in the [two-dimensional torus bound](Yin_C_G0904_v4.md#torus), two points in \(\{0,\ldots,\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor\}^2\) agree under \((s,t)\mapsto s+gt\bmod j\).
For their nonzero difference \((u,v)\), that bound gives \(b_p(j)\le2\max(|u|,2|v|)\le4\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor\).
Modulo \(\ell\), \(P_g=X^{2g}+1\) divides \(X^{4g}-1\), which gives the asserted full return.
The decreasing measures in this example exceed the strict-record ceiling, so the construction isolates what the width, Salem property and decrease alone permit.
Whether strict minimality forces a large return at a fixed finite list remains open.
