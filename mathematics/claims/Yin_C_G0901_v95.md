# A common measure field and exact Mahler iteration at radial rank one

<a id="statement"></a>
## The measure values and their common iteration exponent

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit whose conjugate log moduli span a one-dimensional space over \(\mathbb Q\) at the chosen embedding into \(\mathbb C\).
Let \(D(w)\) consist of all algebraic numbers having a positive power equal, up to a root of unity, to a monomial with integer exponents in the conjugates of \(w\).
Write \(M(x)=\prod_{\sigma}\max(1,|\sigma x|)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of a unit, with one factor per distinct conjugate, and \(F=\mathbb Q(M(w))\subset\mathbb R\) for the field at the chosen embedding.
There is no degree, reciprocity or size condition on \(w\).

There is a unique \(\eta>1\) generating the group of positive real units of \(F\) multiplicatively dependent with \(M(w)\), with the identity included.
For every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\), there is an integer \(e(x)\ge1\) such that
\[
              M(x)=\eta^{e(x)},\qquad
              \mathbb Q(M(x))=\mathbb Q(\eta^n)=F\quad(n\ge1).             \tag{1}
\]
The set of attained indices \(e(x)\) contains every positive integral multiple of each of its members.
There is a common integer \(t\ge1\) such that \(M(\eta)=\eta^t\) and, writing \(M^j\) for iteration,
\[
                    M^j(x)=M(x)^{\,t^{j-1}}\qquad(j\ge1).                 \tag{2}
\]
If \(t=1\), the full measure spectrum is \(\{\eta^n:n\ge1\}\), with minimum \(\eta\).
For \(t>1\), (1) and the multiple closure describe restrictions on the attained indices, and (2) gives the entire orbit after its first step.

<a id="field"></a>
## Commensurability and the fields of the actual exterior products

Every \(x\in D(w)\) is a unit, since all its valuations at finite places vanish after taking a positive power.
For each conjugate of \(x\), conjugating a defining monomial relation shows that its log modulus is a rational linear combination of conjugate log moduli of \(w\).
This applies to every actual embedding of \(x\), including all its possible radical and torsion factors.
Thus \(\log M(x)\) and \(\log M(w)\) belong to the same rational line, and both are positive by Kronecker's theorem when \(x\) is nontorsion.
There are positive integers \(a,b\) with \(M(x)^b=M(w)^a\) as positive real numbers.
This uses radial rank one of the whole original conjugate span. [Fixed-space interpretation](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules)

For any nontorsion algebraic unit \(y\), let \(S\) be the set of its distinct conjugates having modulus strictly greater than one.
The set is stable under ordinary complex conjugation, so \(A=M(y)\) is the product of its elements times a rational sign and is itself a positive algebraic unit.
Among all subsets of the same size, only \(S\) has product of moduli equal to \(A\), since replacing any members by the same number of conjugates of modulus at most one strictly reduces that product.
Every absolute Galois automorphism \(g\) sends \(S\) to a subset of this size and preserves the rational sign.
Hence \(|gA|=A\) forces \(gS=S\), which fixes the actual product and gives \(gA=A\).
This proves the classical Perron property of a nontrivial measure directly for these units, including inputs with torsion quotients among their distinct conjugates. [Fili, Pottmeyer and Zhang, Introduction](https://math.okstate.edu/people/fili/iterated-mahler.pdf)
In particular the stabilizers of \(A^j\) and \(A\) agree for every integer \(j\ge1\), so \(\mathbb Q(A^j)=\mathbb Q(A)\).
Applying this twice to the common power gives
\[
            \mathbb Q(M(x))=\mathbb Q(M(x)^b)
                   =\mathbb Q(M(w)^a)=F .
\]
The equality identifies the embedded field for all \(x\), which may themselves lie outside the splitting field of \(w\).
Bounded measure values in the radical closure of a fixed normal number field form a finite set by Samuels's Lemma 3.1. [Samuels, Lemma 3.1](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-2011-028-x)
Here radial rank one identifies a common field and a single unit line for all values.

<a id="powers"></a>
## The primitive positive unit and attainable indices

The roots of unity in the embedded real field \(F\) are \(1,-1\).
Taking the positive representative of each torsion class therefore identifies the group of units positive at this embedding with the free abelian group \(\mathcal O_F^\times/\{1,-1\}\) of Dirichlet's theorem.
Dividing the coordinates of \(M(w)\) in a lattice basis by their greatest common divisor gives a primitive generator of the lattice on its rational line.
Orient the generator \(\eta\) to be greater than one at the chosen embedding.
Consequently \(M(w)=\eta^h\) with \(h\ge1\), and the common-power identity for \(M(x)>1\) puts it among the positive powers of \(\eta\). [Saturation of a unit line](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#primitive)
For every \(n\ge1\), the element \(M(w)^n=(\eta^n)^h\) already generates \(F\), and \(\eta^n\) lies in \(F\), proving (1).
Also \(M(w)\) is a signed product of original conjugates, so \(\eta\in D(w)\).

To take positive multiples of an attained index, first form the product \(y\) of one class of conjugates of \(x\) modulo torsion.
These products of equal-sized classes form the conjugate orbit of \(y\), are pairwise distinct modulo torsion, and satisfy \(M(y)=M(x)\). [Torsion-class products](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree)
The group \(D(w)\) is invariant under Galois and multiplication, so \(y\in D(w)\).
The distinct conjugates of \(y\) stay distinct under every positive integer power \(n\) and give exactly the conjugates of \(y^n\).
It follows that \(M(y^n)=M(y)^n=M(x)^n\), proving the multiple closure even when the degree of \(x^n\) would drop.

<a id="iteration"></a>
## Iteration and the two complex exterior roots

Applying (1) to \(\eta\in D(w)\) gives \(M(\eta)=\eta^t\) for one integer \(t\ge1\).
For each \(n\ge1\), (1) gives \(\deg(\eta^n)=\deg(\eta)\), so the full list of conjugates of \(\eta^n\) consists of those of \(\eta\) raised to \(n\), without identification.
Hence \(M(\eta^n)=M(\eta)^n=\eta^{tn}\), proving (2) by induction.
When \(t=1\), the input \(\eta\) itself realizes the first index and all its powers realize the remaining indices.

In particular take \(w=\alpha\) under the full [two-exterior-root hypotheses](Yin_C_G0901_v94.md#statement), including the nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots, reciprocity, compression, and the large-degree condition at a fixed upper bound on the total measure.
Its conjugate log moduli are \(0,\pm\log|\alpha|\) wherever those values occur, giving radial rank one.
For \(\beta=M(\alpha)=|\alpha|^2\), equation (2) gives \(t=\log M(\beta)/\log M(\alpha)\).
The [two computed norm costs](Yin_C_G0901_v94.md#heights) therefore give \(t=2b-3\) in the independent even inversion case and \(t=b-1\) in the canonical oriented case, with \(b\) the common block size.
Each value applies to the iterates starting from every nontorsion \(x\in D(\alpha)\).
