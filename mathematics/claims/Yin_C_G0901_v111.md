# Salem measure, unit signatures and ambiguous ideal classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A rank identity and a lower bound for the measure

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2n\ge4\), \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(m=\log\alpha\).
The totally real field \(F\) has a distinguished real place splitting in \(K\), and the other \(n-1\) real places become complex.
Use the full unit groups \(U_F,U_K\), the ordinary ideal class group \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\), and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle\).
Define the following integers:

* \(s\) is the dimension over \(\mathbb F_2\) of the signatures of \(U_F\) at the \(n-1\) nonsplit real places.
* Write \(\mathrm{Am}=\mathrm{Cl}(K)^G\), and let \(\mathrm{Am}_{\rm st}\) be the subgroup of classes possessing a \(G\)-invariant fractional ideal representative.
  Put \(a=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(\mathrm{Am}/\mathrm{Am}_{\rm st})\).
* Put \(q=\log_2[\,N_{K/F}U_K:U_F^2\,]\), which belongs to \(\{0,1,2\}\).

Then \(v=n-s-a-q\) is nonnegative.
It equals the rank of the finite local quadratic norm characters on the units positive at every nonsplit real place.
If \(r_f\) counts all ramified finite primes of \(F\) in \(K\), then \(v\le\max(0,r_f-1)\).
For \(v>0\), let \(\Lambda_F(v+1)\) be the product of the \(v+1\) smallest absolute norms of distinct prime ideals of \(F\), including ties as often as there are such ideals.
For every integer \(T\ge2\),
\[
  (v+1)\log2\ \le\ \log\Lambda_F(v+1)
  \ \le\ \sum_{\mathfrak p\ {\rm ramified}}\log N\mathfrak p
  \ \le\ 2n\frac{\log T}{T-1}+\frac{2(T+1)}{3}m .                       \tag{1}
\]
The upper bound on the ramified sum also holds for \(v=0\).

Consequently every sequence of Salem numbers with \(m\to0\), in arbitrary varying trace fields, must satisfy
\[
                 0\le n-s-a=q+v=o(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}).                    \tag{2}
\]
For every fixed \(\delta>0\) there is a uniform positive gap on the class with \(n-s-a\ge\delta\sqrt{n\log(2n)}\).
If \(n-s-a\ge\delta n\), then \(m\gg_\delta n\) at sufficiently large degrees.
In particular, on a shrinking sequence where \(s=o(n)\), the quotient of ambiguous by strongly ambiguous classes has size \(2^{(1-o(1))n}\).
The finite norm rank and the occurrence of \(a\) in (2) concern the actual quadratic Salem extension.

For every totally real \(F\) of degree \(n\ge2\) with a chosen distinguished real place, each integer \(0\le j\le n-s\) occurs as \(v\) in infinitely many such quadratic Salem extensions \(K/F\).
They can have exactly \(j+1\) finite ramified primes, all of odd residue characteristic and outside any prescribed finite set of places, with every finite place in the prescribed set split.
Thus the two extreme possibilities \(v=0\) and \(a=q=0\) can both occur over each such \(F\).

<a id="norms"></a>
## Local norm symbols and the global norm of a unit

Use the vector space \(U_F/U_F^2\), of dimension \(n\) by Dirichlet's theorem and \(\mu(F)=\{\pm1\}\).
Let \(W\) be its subspace represented by units positive at the \(n-1\) nonsplit real places, so \(\dim W=n-s\).
Write \(K=F(\sqrt{t^2-4})\).
At every finite place use the quadratic Hilbert norm character of this extension.
At a split place it is trivial, and at an unramified nonsplit finite place it is trivial on units by local reciprocity.
At a real place that becomes complex its value on a unit is precisely its sign, while the distinguished real place is split.
The local symbol maps on \(W\) consequently have possible nonzero coordinates only at the \(r_f\) ramified finite places, including all ramified dyadic places.
Hilbert reciprocity puts their image in the even-sum subspace, since the archimedean symbols of elements of \(W\) are trivial. [Milne, Hilbert symbol and product formula](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=124)
Let \(v\) be the dimension of this image.
The Hasse norm theorem for the cyclic extension \(K/F\) identifies its kernel on \(W\) with \((U_F\cap N_{K/F}K^\times)/U_F^2\). [Milne, Chapter VIII, Theorem 3.1](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=243)

For clarity, passage from norms of elements to norms of units is measured by the ordinary ambiguous-class exact sequence
\[
             \mathrm{Am}/\mathrm{Am}_{\rm st}
                    \ \simeq\ (U_F\cap N_{K/F}K^\times)/N_{K/F}U_K .
\]
Indeed if a fractional ideal \(I\) represents an ambiguous class, write \(\sigma I/I=(x)\) and map its class to \(N_{K/F}x\) modulo norms of units.
This is a unit, changing \(x\) by a unit changes its norm by a unit norm, and changing \(I\) by a principal ideal multiplies \(x\) by an element of norm one up to a unit.
Conversely if \(N_{K/F}x\) is a unit, the valuations of \((x)\) at pairs of exchanged primes are opposite and those at fixed primes are zero.
Choosing one ideal exponent in each exchanged pair therefore realizes \((x)=\sigma I/I\).
If \(N_{K/F}x\) is a unit norm, divide \(x\) by a unit to give it norm one, then write the result as \(\sigma z/z\) by Hilbert 90.
The ideal \(I/(z)\) is an invariant representative, proving the kernel assertion. [Lemmermeyer, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1071#page=2)
All the displayed unit quotients modulo \(U_F^2\) have exponent two.

The norm-one group \(U^1\) of units of \(K\) has rank one: \(U_K\) has rank \(n\), and its norm image contains the squares of \(U_F\), of rank \(n-1\).
Its torsion is \(\{\pm1\}\), since \(K\) has a real embedding.
The norm identifies \(N_{K/F}U_K/U_F^2\) with \(U_K/(U_F U^1)\).
The map \(u\mapsto u/\sigma u\) embeds this last quotient into \(U^1/(U^1)^2\), since \(u/\sigma u=e^2\) for \(e\in U^1\) implies \(u/e\in U_F\).
The target has order four, proving \(q\le2\) for the full rings. [Salem unit index](Yin_C_G0485_v1.md#salem-unit-index-prices)
The dimension of the kernel on \(W\) is therefore \(a+q\), which gives \(n=s+v+a+q\) and the claimed rank bound even for \(r_f=0,1\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## The cost of independent finite norm characters

Every ramified finite prime divides the relative discriminant, which divides the quadratic order discriminant \((t^2-4)\).
Thus the product of its distinct prime norms is at most \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(t^2-4)|\). [Relative discriminant](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative)
The norm \(N_{K/F}(\alpha^2-1)=-(t^2-4)\), and \(h(\alpha^2)=m/n\).
Apply the [cyclic norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) to this power in the containing field \(K\) of degree \(2n\).
It gives the upper bound in (1) for the whole ramified sum in every parity of \(n\).
When \(v>0\), the parity constraint supplies at least \(v+1\) distinct ramified primes, proving the lower bounds.

On a positive shrinking sequence \(n\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness.
Choosing \(T=\lceil\sqrt{n\log(2n/m)/m}\rceil\) makes the right side \(O(\sqrt{nm\log(2n/m)})=o(\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\).
Equation (2) follows from \(q\le2\) and (1), also along terms with \(v=0\).
The square-root-scale gap follows by contradiction along a shrinking sequence.
For the linear bound \(v\ge\delta n-2\), choose a fixed \(T=T(\delta)\) so large that \(2\log T/(T-1)<\delta\log2/2\), and apply (1) for large \(n\).
This norm cost uses the rank of the independent finite characters remaining after the real-place signs and the strongly ambiguous subgroup have been accounted for.

## Realizing every finite norm rank over a prescribed trace field

Fix \(F\) and the split real place as in the statement, and define \(W\) by positivity at the other real places before choosing \(K\).
Its unit squareclasses inject into \(F^\times/(F^\times)^2\) because a square root in \(F\) of an integral unit is itself a unit.
Kummer theory identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(F(\sqrt W)/F)\) with \(W^*\), writing the sign action additively.
Fix any \(j\) linearly independent characters in \(W^*\), allowing the empty list.
Choose distinct odd primes \(\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_j\) away from the prescribed set, unramified in this fixed Kummer extension and realizing the respective Frobenius elements. [Chebotarev density](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001)
The quadratic residue characters on the units in \(W\) then equal those prescribed characters.

Take a finite modulus \(\mathfrak m\) supported on the prescribed finite set and all dyadic primes, disjoint from the chosen \(\mathfrak p_i\).
At each of its primes take an exponent greater than twice the valuation of 2, so a local unit congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak m\) is a square by Hensel's lemma.
Append all real places to obtain a narrow ray modulus.
Weak approximation gives \(x_0\in F^\times\) congruent to one at the finite modulus, positive at the distinguished place and negative at every other real place.
With \(J=\prod_{i=1}^j\mathfrak p_i\), choose another prime \(\mathfrak q\) in the narrow ray class of \((x_0)J^{-1}\).
There are infinitely many such primes by the Artin isomorphism for the ray class field and Chebotarev, so they can avoid \(J,\mathfrak m\) and vary. [Ray class fields](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=167)
The ray class identity gives \(J\mathfrak q=(D)\) with \(D/x_0\) totally positive and congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak m\).
In particular \(D\) is integral, has the prescribed signs, and is a local square at every prime in the modulus.

For \(K=F(\sqrt D)\) the \(j+1\) odd primes dividing \(J\mathfrak q\) ramify because their valuations of \(D\) are one.
All other odd primes are unramified, and all primes of the modulus split.
At a ramified odd prime the local quadratic norm character on units is the usual residue square character: the norm reduces to a square in the unchanged residue field, and a unit with square residue is itself a local square by Hensel and hence a norm.
The first \(j\) finite characters on \(W\) thus give the prescribed independent list.
The last equals their sum by reciprocity and adds no rank, so \(v=j\).

The real signature gives a rank-one group of norm-one units as above.
Choose a nontorsion member, squaring and inverting if necessary to make it greater than one at a chosen split real embedding.
Its other split real value is its inverse, and at all complex embeddings it has modulus one.
The unique exterior value among embeddings of \(K\) forces this unit to generate \(K\), because embeddings of a proper subfield would each carry multiplicity greater than one.
Its trace therefore generates \(F\) by the relative quadratic equation, so it is a genuine Salem number with the specified trace field and split place.
Varying \(\mathfrak q\) gives distinct quadratic extensions.
This realizes the full range of the local rank without asserting any small measures in these constructions.
In particular the case \(v=0\) leaves a possible shrinking sequence on which the real signature rank and the non-strongly-ambiguous quotient account for all but at most two dimensions.
