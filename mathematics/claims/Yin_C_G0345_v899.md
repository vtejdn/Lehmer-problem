# Repulsion between distinct realizations of the same trace pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## How many polynomials can occur in a range of degrees?

Fix \(q\ge2\), and put \(p=q+1,\ M=pq,\ L=2q+1,\ K=L^2,\ s=p/q\).
Let \(H_1,\ldots,H_k\) be distinct monic integral trace polynomials realizing the [first nonunit pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#statement) at this \(q\), allowing different grids and repeated degrees.
Thus \(M^{n_i}H_i(1/M)=K\), where \(n_i=\deg H_i\), and \(H_i\) has one root in \((0,1/M)\) and \(n_i-1\) distinct roots in \((-4,0)\).
Let \(S_i(z)=z^{n_i}H_i(z+z^{-1}-2)\), \(P=\prod_i S_i\), and \(d=2\sum_i n_i\).
Then
\[
 K\mid\operatorname{Res}(H_i,H_j)\quad (i\ne j),\qquad
 K^{\,2k^2-k}\mid\operatorname{disc}P .
\]
Writing \(x_+=\max(x,0)\), one has
\[
 (2k^2-k)\log K+
 4\sum_{i<j}\big((1+\min(n_i,n_j))\log M-\log(pKL)\big)_+
 \ \le\ d(\log d+1/2)+2(d-1)k\log s.                           \tag{1}
\]
The left side can also include the nonnegative joint Fourier energy specified [below](#energy).

For every \(N\ge2\), the total number of distinct realized polynomials with \(N\le\deg H\le2N\), across all grids at this height, is
\[
                   O\!\left(1+\frac{\log(2N)}{\log M}\right).       \tag{2}
\]
The implied constant is absolute.
Summing gives \(O(\log(2X)+(\log(2X))^2/\log M)\) realized polynomials of degree at most \(X\) at a fixed height.
In particular the realized degrees have natural density zero at each height, whether or not there are infinitely many.
Even allowing all integer heights \(q\ge2\), the total number of pairs \((q,H)\) with \(\deg H\le X\) is
\[
                    O\!\left(\frac{(\log X)^5}{(\log\log X)^4}\right)
                    \qquad(X\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
Thus the set of trace degrees supporting any first-nonunit realization has natural density zero.

<a id="resultants"></a>
## Common finite-place divisors

The [irreducibility and Salem lift](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) make the \(S_i\) distinct irreducible polynomials.
Indeed the trace field is totally real, and its quadratic lift is nonreal over each negative trace place, so the lift has full degree \(2n_i\).
Consequently \(P\) is squarefree.
When \(n_i\le n_j\), the polynomial \(H_j(x)-x^{n_j-n_i}H_i(x)\) vanishes at \(1/M\).
Primitive division by \(Mx-1\) gives an integral quotient.
Multiplying the resulting identity over the roots of \(H_i\) shows that \(\operatorname{Res}(H_i,H_j)\) is divisible by \(M^{n_i}H_i(1/M)=K\), up to sign.
The two roots of \(z^2-(v+2)z+1\) have product one, and at either of them \(S_j(z)=z^{n_j}H_j(v)\).
Hence \(\operatorname{Res}(S_i,S_j)=\operatorname{Res}(H_i,H_j)^2\).

Also \(1/M=-4\bmod K\), since \(4M+1=K\) and \(M\) is invertible modulo \(K\).
The pole condition gives \(K\mid H_i(-4)\).
Taking the discriminants of the trace quadratics and squaring their mutual resultants yields
\[
 |\operatorname{disc}S_i|
   = |H_i(0)H_i(-4)|\,|\operatorname{disc}H_i|^2 .
\]
In fact a single quadratic has discriminant \(v(v+4)\), and the resultant of the quadratics for \(v,u\) equals \((v-u)^2\).
The discriminant of \(P\) therefore contains one factor \(K\) per polynomial and four per unordered pair, as asserted.

<a id="energy"></a>
## The real conjugates consume part of the discriminant bound

Let \(T_i>1\) be the exterior zero of \(S_i\) and \(w_i=T_i+T_i^{-1}-2\).
The [exterior pinning inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#windows) gives \(0<1/M-w_i<K/M^{n_i+1}\).
Realization requires \(n_i\ge3\), and \(2K<M^3\), so \(w_i>1/(2M)\).
As \(w_i=(T_i-1)^2/T_i\), this implies \(T_i>1+1/(2q)\).
Since \(1/M-w_i=(s-T_i)(1-1/(sT_i))\) and the second factor is at least \(1/p\), both \(T_i\) and \(T_i^{-1}\) lie within \(pK/M^{n_i+1}\) of \(s\) and \(s^{-1}\), respectively.
All exterior roots approach \(s\) from below, and all their inverses approach \(s^{-1}\) from above.
Thus for a pair \(i,j\), each of the two same-side distances is at most \(pK/M^{1+\min(n_i,n_j)}\).

Put \(t=1-1/d\).
For a root \(\alpha=e^{a+i\theta}\) of \(P\), write \(\beta_\alpha=e^{-|a|+i\theta}\) and \(a^+=\max(a,0)\).
The pointwise inequalities in the [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) give, for distinct \(\alpha,\alpha'\),
\[
 |\alpha-\alpha'|\le e^{a^++(a')^+}t^{-1/2}
                  |1-t\overline{\beta_\alpha}\beta_{\alpha'}|.
\]
For two exterior real roots, and also for their two inverses, the last absolute value is at least \(1-T_i^{-1}>1/L\).
The preceding pinning estimate thus improves the logarithm of the displayed upper bound by at least
\[
             A_{ij}=\big((1+\min(n_i,n_j))\log M-\log(pKL)\big)_+ .
\]
Both orders of each same-side pair occur in the logarithm of the absolute discriminant, giving a saving \(4A_{ij}\).
For the regularized factors, expansion of the logarithm over all ordered pairs including the diagonal gives minus the energy
\[
             \mathcal E=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{t^m}{m}
                  \left|\sum_{\alpha:P(\alpha)=0}\beta_\alpha^m\right|^2 .
\]
Each omitted diagonal logarithm is at least \(\log(1-t)=-\log d\).
The radial terms sum to \(2(d-1)\sum_i\log T_i\), and \(-d(d-1)\log t/2\le d/2\).
It follows that
\[
 \log|\operatorname{disc}P|+\mathcal E+4\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}
 \le d(\log d+1/2)+2(d-1)\sum_i\log T_i .
\]
The divisibility already proved and \(T_i<s\) give (1) with the additional energy.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting throughout a degree interval

Suppose \(N\le n_i\le2N\) for all \(i\) and \(k\ge1\).
One has \(d\le4kN\) and \(\log(pKL/M)\le3\log M\), since \(L\le M\) and \(p\le M\).
Dropping the energy and the finite-place term from (1), using \(A_{ij}\ge(N-3)\log M\), and dividing by \(2kN\) gives
\[
 (k-1)(1-3/N)\log M
 \le 2\log(4kN)+1+4k\log s .
\]
The constant \(\gamma=1-4\log(3/2)/\log6\) is positive, since \((3/2)^4<6\).
For every \(q\ge2\), \(4\log s\le(1-\gamma)\log M\).
For \(N\ge6/\gamma\) the last two bounds imply
\[
        (\gamma/2)k\log M \le 2\log(4kN)+1+\log M .
\]
Using \(2\log k\le(\gamma\log6/4)k+C\) for an absolute \(C\) gives (2).
For \(2\le N<6/\gamma\) all trace polynomials have absolutely bounded degree and all roots have absolute value at most four.
The elementary symmetric functions then bound each integral coefficient absolutely, giving an absolute bound for this remaining range.
The count applies to every finite subfamily and hence to the full set.
Dyadic summation gives the asserted cumulative bound, which is \(o(X)\) for fixed \(q\).

<a id="heights"></a>
## Counting all heights at once

Each realization has a Salem root \(T\) of degree \(2n\) and \(\log M(T)=\log T<1/q\), where \(M(T)\) denotes its Mahler measure.
By [Voutier's theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height),
\[
                 q<4\left(\frac{\log(2n)}{\log\log(2n)}\right)^3 .
\]
The function \(x/\log x\) is increasing for \(x>e\); the finitely many smaller degrees give an absolute height bound from the same inequality.
Thus for \(n\le X\), all heights lie below \(Q=O((\log X/\log\log X)^3)\).
We may take \(Q\) to be a fixed sufficiently large constant times this last expression.
The sum of \(1/\log(q(q+1))\) over \(2\le q\le Q\) is \(O(Q/\log Q)\), by splitting at \(\sqrt Q\).
Summing the cumulative estimate over these heights gives \(O(Q\log X+Q(\log X)^2/\log Q)\), as claimed.
This counts pairs even when one only wants to count degrees, so it bounds the union of realized degrees.

In fact the positive trace itself determines the height uniquely.
For \(h_q=1/(q(q+1))\), the pinning estimate puts \(h_q-w<K/M^{n+1}<h_q-h_{q+1}=2/(M(q+2))\).
Here \(n\ge3,\ q+2\le M\) and \(K<2M^2\).
Thus \(h_{q+1}<w<h_q\), and these intervals are disjoint.
