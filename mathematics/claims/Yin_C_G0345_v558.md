# Every intermediate power index is realized in one unramified completion

<a id="statement"></a>
## The marked local factor

Use the [marked factor](Yin_C_G0345_v556.md#statement) of either section with \(s\equiv2\pmod3,\ a=3^k,\ k\ge1,\ j\in\{b,b+2\},\ as+b\) odd and \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,b+2)=1\).
Write \(x\) for a root of the marked rational factor \(P\).
The residual factor \(D=\Phi_{3a}\) occurs simply in the section modulo two and lifts to a degree-\(2a\) unramified factor of \(P\) over \(\mathbb Q_2\).
Let \(K\) denote this completion, \(O\) its integer ring and \(\xi\) the image of \(x\).
For every \(c\mid a\), projection to this one factor gives an additive isomorphism
\[
 \mathbb Z[x]/\mathbb Z[x^c]\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ O/\mathbb Z_2[\xi^c]
 \simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{c-1}(\mathbb Z/2^i\mathbb Z)^{2a/c}.
\]
The local conductor of \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi^c]\) in \(O\) is \(2^{c-1}O\), and the global power index is \(2^{a(c-1)}\).
Thus the full list of indices and conductor layers already occurs in a single local field of the minimum degree \(2a\) forced by the marked residue.
They impose no additional local degree requirement at this place for the cubic offsets \(as^3\pm1\).

<a id="first-digit"></a>
## The first dyadic digit generates the residue extension

Choose a root \(t\) of \(D\) in \(k_K=\mathbb F_{2^{2a}}\), set \(w=t^a\in\mathbb F_4\), and let \(z=t^j\).
Then \(w\) has order three and \(z\) has order \(3a\), so \(z\) generates \(k_K\) over \(\mathbb F_4\) by [irreducibility of the residue factor](Yin_C_G0345_v553.md#statement).
Use \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\) and \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\).
Both vanish modulo two at \(w\), and the derivative of \(F_j(X)=g(X^a)+X^jB(X^a)\) at \(t\) equals \(at^{a-1}h(z)\), where
\[
 h(z)=g'(w)+zB'(w)=
 \begin{cases}1+z,&s\text{ even},\\ w^2+wz,&s\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
This is nonzero since \(z\notin\mathbb F_4\).
In the unramified extension \(K/\mathbb Q_2\) of degree \(2a\), Hensel lifting gives a unique root \(\xi\) reducing to \(t\).
Its residue has degree \(2a\), hence \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi]=O\) by lifting the residue power basis.
The defining factor belongs to \(P\) by uniqueness of the rational factor carrying \(D\).
For the simple-root lifting used here see [The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.153.9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GM).

Let \(\omega\) be the Teichmüller lift of \(w\).
Since \(g(\omega)=2\omega^2\) and \(B(\omega)=2\), Taylor expansion of the section equation in \(Y=\xi^a\) at \(\omega\) gives
\[
 \overline{(\xi^a-\omega)/2}=(w^2+z)/h(z).
\]
In each parity this fractional linear function of \(z\) is invertible over \(\mathbb F_4\): its determinants in characteristic two are \(1+w^2\) and \(w^2+w^3\), respectively.
Consequently its value generates \(k_K\) over \(\mathbb F_4\).

<a id="layers"></a>
## Intermediate powers and the global quotient

For \(c\mid a\), set \(m=a/c\), let \(\eta\) be the Teichmüller lift of \(t^c\), and let \(O_c\) be the integer ring of \(\mathbb Q_2(\eta)\).
The residue \(t^c\) has order \(3a/c\), hence degree \(f=2a/c\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) by the same irreducibility (including \(\Phi_3\) at \(c=a\)).
We have \(O_c=\mathbb Z_2[\eta]\) by lifting its residue basis.
The limit of \((\xi^c)^{2^{fn}}\) as \(n\to\infty\) is \(\eta\), since these powers fix \(\eta\) and their exponents drive the principal-unit factor to one.
The finite \(\mathbb Z_2\)-module \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi^c]\) is closed and therefore contains \(O_c\).
Put \(u_c=(\xi^c-\eta)/2\).
Since \(\eta^m=\omega\) and \(m\) is odd, the residue of \((\xi^a-\omega)/2\) equals \(\overline{m\eta^{m-1}}\,\bar u_c\), with nonzero scalar in the residue field of \(O_c\).
This residue field contains \(\mathbb F_4\), so \(\bar u_c\) generates the full residue extension of degree \(c\) over it.
The elements \(1,u_c,\ldots,u_c^{c-1}\) form an \(O_c\)-basis of \(O\) by their residue basis and the equality of ranks.
It follows that
\[
 \mathbb Z_2[\xi^c]=O_c[2u_c]=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{c-1}2^i O_c u_c^i.
\]
Indeed the integral degree-\(c\) equation for \(2u_c\) reduces every higher power to this span.
The conductor formula follows because \(2^{c-1}O\) is the largest \(O\)-ideal in this lattice, as seen in the last basis coordinate.

At \(c=a\) evaluation maps the completed global additive quotient onto this local quotient since \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi]=O\).
Both groups have order \(2^{a(a-1)}\) by the lattice computation and the [global marked index](Yin_C_G0345_v556.md#statement), so this map is an isomorphism.
It carries the image of \(\mathbb Z_2[x^c]\) onto the image of \(\mathbb Z_2[\xi^c]\) in that quotient.
Passing to the respective quotients and using that the original global quotient at \(a\) is a finite two-group proves the claim for every \(c\mid a\).
