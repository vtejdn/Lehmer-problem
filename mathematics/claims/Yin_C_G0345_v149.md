# Moving power sums and the width of a reciprocal polynomial with small measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A trace test through a fixed multiple of the original degree

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic integer unit, with degree \(d\), logarithmic Mahler measure \(L\), logarithmic house \(b\), and power sums \(p_j=\sum_\alpha\alpha^j\) over its conjugates.
Fix \(B>0\).
As \(L\to0\), uniformly for integers \(1\le j\le Bd\), one has
\[
 \frac{p_j}{d}=
 \frac1d\sum_{|\alpha|>1}(\alpha^j-\bar\alpha^{-j})+O_B(E(L)),
 \qquad
 E(L)=\sqrt L\,\log^2(2/L).                                               \tag{1}
\]
The mean of \(e^{ij\arg\alpha}\) is also \(O_B(E(L))\), and in particular
\[
              \frac{|p_j|}{d}\le O_B(E(L))+
                      \frac{2L}{db}\sinh(jb).                              \tag{2}
\]
These estimates permit all signatures, repeated values of \(\alpha^j\), and all counts of exterior conjugates.
The coordinator suggested retaining the full complex exterior excess in (1).

If a monic cyclotomic product \(Q=\prod_N\Phi_N^{m_N}\) of any degree at least \(k\) matches the first \(k\le d\) coefficients below the leading term of \(P\), it forces the exact left side of (2) to be
\[
            \frac1d\left|\sum_N m_N c_N(j)\right|,\qquad
 c_N(j)=\sum_{\zeta\ {\rm primitive\ of\ order}\ N}\zeta^j,
 \qquad 1\le j\le k.
\]
Thus a fixed positive lower bound for any such normalized sum forces \(jb\ge\log(1/L)+\log\log(1/L)-O(1)\), where the constant may depend on the lower bound.
The same conclusion holds for any independently known power sum \(|p_j|/d\) bounded below at \(j\le Bd\), with \(B\) also allowed in the constant.
By (1) a large sum must be supplied by the signed complex exterior contributions at that specific power.

If the first nonzero coefficient below the leading term occurs at depth \(q\ge\delta d\), write it as \(a_q\).
Then for each fixed \(\delta>0\)
\[
       \frac{2L\sinh(qb)}{qb}\ge |a_q|-o_\delta(1),
 \qquad
       qb\ge \log(|a_q|/L)+\log\log(|a_q|/L)-O_\delta(1).                   \tag{3}
\]
No cyclotomic comparison or reciprocal symmetry is required for (3).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Passing to the minimal polynomial of each power

Write \(\gamma=\alpha^j\) for one conjugate \(\alpha\), and let \(e=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
Each of its distinct conjugates occurs exactly \(d/e\) times among the \(j\)-th powers, since every embedding of the subfield has exactly that many extensions.
In particular \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion algebraic integer unit of absolute logarithmic height \(h(\gamma)=jL/d\le BL\).
Its own logarithmic Mahler measure is \(e h(\gamma)\), which need not be bounded.

For any complex \(\xi\ne0\), put \(r(\xi)=\xi\) for \(|\xi|\le1\) and \(r(\xi)=1/\bar\xi\) otherwise.
The first Fourier term of the [reflected discriminant inequality (3)](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), applied to the separable minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\), gives
\[
 \left|\frac1e\sum_{\xi\sim\gamma}r(\xi)\right|
             \ll \sqrt{h(\gamma)+\log(2e)/e}.
\]
This bound follows there for arbitrary total measure of the minimal polynomial.
The [degree lower bound for positive Mahler measure](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001), weakened to \(e h(\gamma)\gg(\log(2e))^{-3}\) and extended across the finitely many small degrees by the positive minimum at each degree, makes the right side \(O_B(E(L))\).
Indeed for \(e<1/L\) it gives \(1/e\ll_B L\log^3(2/L)\), and for \(e\ge1/L\) monotonicity gives \(\log(2e)/e\ll L\log(2/L)\).
This is uniform even when the power map greatly lowers the original degree.
The mean distance between \(r(\xi)\) and \(\xi/|\xi|\) is at most \(e^{-1}\sum|\log|\xi||=2h(\gamma)\), so the same estimate holds for the phases.
Restoring multiplicities and using \(r(\alpha^j)=r(\alpha)^j\) proves (1).

An exterior conjugate of logarithmic modulus \(u\) contributes magnitude \(2\sinh(ju)\) to the excess.
Convexity bounds this by \(2u\sinh(jb)/b\), and \(\sum_{u>0}u=L\), proving (2).
Newton's identities equate the power sums of two monic polynomials through their matched depth even at different degrees.
For \(y=jb\) the last term in (2) is \((j/d)2L\sinh(y)/y\le B L e^y/y\).
A positive lower bound forces \(y\to\infty\), then \(y\ge\log(1/L)+\log y-O(1)\), giving the claimed two logarithms.
At the first nonzero coefficient Newton gives \(p_q=-q a_q\) exactly.
Dividing (2) by \(q/d\ge\delta\) proves the first inequality in (3).
Since \(|a_q|\ge1\), this gives \(e^{qb}/(qb)\gg_\delta |a_q|/L\), proving the second one also for unbounded coefficients.

<a id="central"></a>
## Lower bounds for the central coefficients and their span

Suppose in addition that \(P\) is reciprocal of degree \(d=2n\) and has the form
\[
      P(z)=z^{2n}+1+z^n J(z),\qquad
      J(z)=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^s a_j(z^j+z^{-j}),\qquad 3s<n,
\]
where \(s\ge1\) is the exact Laurent degree, so \(a_s\ne0\).
For \(s=0\) one has \(P=z^{2n}+a_0 z^n+1\), which is cyclotomic for \(|a_0|\le2\) and has logarithmic measure at least \(\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)\) otherwise by substitution.
Thus the constant case cannot occur at \(L\to0\).
Write \(A^2=a_0^2+2\sum_{j=1}^s a_j^2\).
For every such polynomial the identity
\[
                            \frac{p_d}{d}=\frac{A^2-2}{2}                    \tag{4}
\]
holds.
Indeed \(J(\alpha)^2=(\alpha^n+\alpha^{-n})^2\) at every root.
The nonconstant frequencies of \(J^2\) have absolute value at most \(2s<n-s\), so Newton's identities and reciprocity make their sums vanish.
The constant term of \(J^2\) is \(A^2\), and inverse symmetry gives \(p_{-d}=p_d\).
Since \(P\) is noncyclotomic, \(A^2\ge3\), as the only nonconstant possibility of smaller norm is \(J=\pm(z^s+z^{-s})\), which gives the binomial products of the [zero-prefix classification](Yin_C_G0345_v146.md#classification).
Thus (4) always gives a positive macroscopic trace at the original degree.

Here \(q=n-s>d/3\), so (3) also applies at the shorter power with \(a_q=a_s\).
Along any such sequence with \(L\to0\) one necessarily has
\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{j=-s}^s |[z^j]J|&\gg |a_s|\,qb/L,\\
 (2s+1)L&\ge \log(db)-O(1)\ \ge\ \log\log(1/L)-O(1).                      \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
In particular \(sL\to\infty\), without an upper bound on the house in degree units or on the number of coefficients or exterior roots.
Also (3) alone excludes bounded \(db\) throughout the specified macroscopic zero-prefix range.

To prove (5), let \(\alpha\) be a root with \(|\alpha|=e^b\) and put \(x=db\), \(y=qb\).
By (3), \(y\to\infty\) and \(e^y\gg |a_s|y/L\).
The equation for this root gives \(e^{nb}(1-e^{-2nb})\le |J(\alpha)|\le e^{sb}\sum_{j=-s}^s |[z^j]J|\), proving the coefficient bound.
Equations (2) and (4) give \(A^2\ll 1+Le^x/x\).
Here \(Le^x/x=(Le^y/y)(y/x)e^{x-y}\to\infty\) since \(y/x>1/3\), \(x\ge2y\), and \(Le^y/y\gg1\).
At the same root Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
  e^x(1-e^{-x})^2\le |J(\alpha)|^2
       \le A^2\sum_{j=-s}^s e^{2jb}
       =A^2\frac{\sinh((2s+1)b)}{\sinh b}.
\]
Cancelling \(e^x\) gives \(\sinh((2s+1)b)\gg x b/L\).
Since \(b\le L\) and \(\sinh((2s+1)v)/v\) increases for \(v>0\), it follows that \(\sinh((2s+1)L)\gg x\).
Taking inverse hyperbolic sine and using (3) proves the width bound.
