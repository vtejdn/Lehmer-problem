# A neutral factor controlled by the small values of a hyperbolic polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## The transfer bound

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonconstant, monic and reciprocal of degree \(2h\), with no zeros on the unit circle.
Choose the sign of \(u(X)=\pm X^{-h}P(X)\) so that \(u\) is positive throughout that circle.
Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure and use normalized angular measure \(dt/(2\pi)\).
Define
\[
 B(P)=\int_{u(e^{it})<1/4}\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac{1}{2u(e^{it})}-1\right)\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is a monic irreducible integer polynomial \(Q\) with a nontorsion unit-circle root and
\[
                 0<m(Q)\le m(P)+B(P)+\epsilon.
\]
In particular, a hyperbolic sequence with \(m(P)\to0\) and \(B(P)\to0\) yields a sequence of neutral irreducible units of vanishing total logarithmic measure.
If \(\gamma\) is the infimum of the latter measures over all neutral irreducible nontorsion units, then every such hyperbolic input satisfies \(B(P)\ge\gamma-m(P)\).
A sufficient condition for vanishing loss is \(\int_{u<1/4}\log(1/u)\,dt/(2\pi)\to0\), since \(\operatorname{arcosh}(s)\le\log(2s)\) for \(s\ge1\).

<a id="jensen"></a>
## The quadratic in the second variable

Set \(F(X,Y)=1-u(X)(2-Y-Y^{-1})\).
For fixed \(X=e^{it}\), multiplying by \(Y\) gives a quadratic with leading and constant coefficients \(u\) and root sum \(2-1/u\).
Both roots have modulus one when \(u\ge1/4\).
When \(u<1/4\), the exterior root has logarithmic modulus \(\operatorname{arcosh}(1/(2u)-1)\).
Jensen's formula and integration therefore give \(m(F)=m(P)+B(P)\).
The [two-variable scalar specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U002.md#luc22-abelian-matrix-approximation) gives \(m(F(X,X^N))\to m(F)\) as \(N\to\infty\) for each fixed \(P\).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Nontorsion crossings in the specialization

Since \(m(P)\ge0\), the continuous function \(u\) exceeds \(1/4\) on an open arc.
At successive \(N\)-th roots of unity inside this arc the specialized polynomial has value one, while at the intermediate angles with \(X^N=-1\) it has value \(1-4u<0\).
Consequently its number of distinct unit-circle zeros tends to infinity.
Only finitely many of these zeros can be roots of unity, uniformly in \(N\).
Indeed an infinite set of torsion pairs on \(F=0\) would force a [torsion binomial factor](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area), hence a component parametrized by \((X,Y)=(\xi t^a,\eta t^b)\) with \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\) and nonzero constants \(\xi,\eta\).
If \(b\ne0\), taking \(t^b=1/\eta\) makes \(F=1\).
If \(b=0\), the restriction is either identically one or a nonconstant Laurent polynomial because \(a\ne0\) and \(u\) is nonconstant.
Neither restriction vanishes identically.
Thus for all sufficiently large \(N\) at least one circle root is nontorsion.
After a sign and monomial normalization \(F(X,X^N)\) is an integer polynomial with leading and constant coefficients of absolute value one, since its extreme exponents are \(\pm(N+h)\).
Every irreducible factor is therefore monic up to sign and has unit constant term.
An irreducible factor containing a nontorsion circle root has positive measure by Kronecker's theorem, and additivity with nonnegative factor measures gives the asserted upper bound.
The argument supplies no uniform estimate on \(B(P)\) for unrestricted hyperbolic inputs.
