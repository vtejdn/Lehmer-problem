# Angular distribution and exterior mass for reciprocal five-term multiples

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two scales in the conjugates of an individual factor

Fix nonzero integers \(a,b,c\), and for \(n>l>0\) set \(k=n-l\) and
\[
             P=X^n\{a(X^n+X^{-n})+b(X^l+X^{-l})+c\}.
\]
If \(|a|\ge|b|\), the positive logarithmic measures of its irreducible integral factors have a uniform lower bound depending only on \(a,b,c\).
Assume now that \(|b|>|a|\) and put \(R=|b/a|>1\).
For any sequence of primitive irreducible factors \(S\) of these polynomials of positive logarithmic measure \(m\to0\), normalize \(S\) to be monic once \(m\) is sufficiently small.
Write \(d=\deg S\), \(e=\#\{x:S(x)=0,\ |x|>1\}\), and
\[
    \xi_x=(x/|x|)^k,\qquad
    F(\xi)=\operatorname{arcosh}\max\!\left(1,\frac{|c|}{2|a\xi+b|}\right)
    \quad (|\xi|=1).
\]
Let \(\mu\) be uniform probability measure on the circle.
The pairs \((\xi_x,l\,|\log|x||)\), with mass \(1/d\) at each conjugate, converge weakly to the distribution of \((\xi,F(\xi))\) for \(\xi\) distributed by \(\mu\).
The exact proportion off the circle also satisfies
\[
                  \frac{2e}{d}\longrightarrow
                   \mu\{\xi:2|a\xi+b|<|c|\}.                              \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(|c|>2(|b|-|a|)\) gives a uniform factor gap for any fixed bound on the number of exterior conjugates, and precludes shrinking sequences with \(e=o(d)\).

Fix \(\delta=\tfrac12\log R\) and let \(j\) count the exterior roots with \(k\log|x|>\delta\).
Then \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty,\ dj\gg_{a,b,c}k^2\), and
\[
 \begin{split}
       \frac{l m}{d}
           &=\frac12\int F\,d\mu+\frac{lj}{kd}\log R+o(1),\\
       \frac1d\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,aX^k+b)|
           &=\log|b|-\frac{lj}{kd}\log R+o(1).                             \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
Thus the first moment includes the contribution of exterior roots whose fraction tends to zero.
Equations (1) and (2) are necessary conditions on a putative shrinking sequence, including factors with arbitrarily many exterior conjugates.

For a Salem factor of degree at least four, \(ab>0\) is impossible for every \(c\).
A sequence of Salem factors with measure tending to zero in this family must therefore have \(ab<0,\ |b|>|a|,\ |c|\le2(|b|-|a|)\).
The existence of such a sequence in this remaining regime is unresolved.

<a id="phase"></a>
## Equidistribution at the difference of the exponents

Small positive measure makes \(S\) a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit polynomial up to sign by [the endpoint and nonreciprocal bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v229.md#outer).
Write \(h=m/d\) and \(t=\max_{S(x)=0}\log|x|\).
The same argument applies the [quadratic radial floor](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) to give \(dtm\ge1/20\).
Since \(t\le m\), in particular \(d\to\infty\).
The gap from the leading term of \(P\) to the next term is \(k\), so evaluation at the largest root gives \(kt\le\log(\|P\|_1/|a|)\).
Thus \(kh=O(m^2)\).
The [sparse width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(nh\gg1\) from the five terms and width \(2n\).
Hence \(k/n\to0\), \(l/k\to\infty\), and \(d/l=O(m)\).

If \(|a|\ge|b|\), the leading block in \(P=X^{n+l}(aX^k+b)+cX^n+bX^k+a\) has all roots in the closed disk.
The gap \(l\) minus twice the block degree \(k\) is at least \(n/2\) eventually on such a sequence.
The [separated-block factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) gives a positive lower bound since the norm bounds are fixed and the full width is \(2n\).
This rules out a shrinking sequence in this case.

From now on \(|b|>|a|\).
For every fixed positive integer \(v\), take \(T=\max(d,vk)\) in the [phase estimate at a specified power](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#primitive-phase), so \(T h\le m+vkh\to0\).
The degree-\(d\) relation \(S\) is available at this width.
The estimate bounds \(d^{-1}|\sum_{S(x)=0}\xi_x^v|\) by \(O(\sqrt{Th})\), including power-degree drops.
For negative \(v\) take complex conjugates.
Approximation by trigonometric polynomials gives weak convergence of the phase marginal to \(\mu\).
Also \(d^{-1}\sum_x k|\log|x||=2kh\to0\), so the fraction with \(k|\log|x||>\delta\) tends to zero.

<a id="circle"></a>
## Which branches lie exactly on the circle

Set \(T_x=x^k,\ Y_x=x^l\); after multiplication by \(Y_x\), the equation for \(P\) becomes
\[
             (aT_x+b)Y_x^2+cY_x+(a/T_x+b)=0.                              \tag{3}
\]
For a root with \(k|\log|x||\le\delta\), the leading and constant coefficients in (3) have absolute values bounded below by \(|b|-|a|e^\delta>0\), and all coefficients are bounded above.
Root bounds for (3) in both orientations give \(l|\log|x||=O(1)\), hence \(k|\log|x||=O(k/l)=o(1)\) uniformly on this set.
Thus \(T_x-\xi_x=o(1)\).
At \(T=\xi\) on the unit circle, writing \(a\xi+b=|a\xi+b|e^{i\varphi}\), its two solutions are \(Y=e^{-i\varphi}Z\) with \(Z+Z^{-1}=-c/|a\xi+b|\).
Both satisfy \( |\log|Y||=F(\xi)\).
Compactness gives uniformly over the roots in the stated band
\[
                           l|\log|x||=F(\xi_x)+o(1).                       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed any countersequence has a subsequence along which the phases and the bounded nonzero roots of (3) converge, and the limiting equation gives equality.
Removing the vanishing proportion outside the band now proves weak convergence for every bounded continuous test function.

For (1) it remains to treat exactly circular roots where \(F=0\).
On any closed arc in the open set \(2|a\xi+b|>|c|\), the two solutions \(Y\) of (3) at \(T=\xi\) are separate and have modulus one.
On finitely many neighborhoods covering the arc, each nonzero solution is a holomorphic function of \(T\) with bounded logarithmic derivative on a smaller neighborhood, by the simple-root formula.
Integrating radially from \(\xi_x\) to \(T_x\) shows \(|\log|Y_x||\le C|\log|T_x||\) for band roots with phase on the arc once \(k/l\) is small.
Since these logarithms are \(l|\log|x||\) and \(k|\log|x||\), the inequality forces \(|x|=1\).
On closed arcs in \(2|a\xi+b|<|c|\), (4) precludes \(|x|=1\).
There are at most two boundary points because \(ab\ne0\).
Removing arcs of arbitrarily small total length around them, and applying phase equidistribution and inversion, gives (1).

<a id="mass"></a>
## The large exterior roots and an integral resultant

For the \(j\) exterior roots outside the band, use
\[
        (a x^k+b)x^l=-c-(a x^{-k}+b)x^{-l}.
\]
The second term on the right is \(O(e^{-\delta l/k})\); since \(c\ne0\), uniformly on this set
\[
      x^k=-b/a+O(e^{-\delta l/k}),\qquad
      k\log|x|=\log R+O(e^{-\delta l/k}),\qquad
      \log|a x^k+b|=-l\log|x|+\log|c|+O(e^{-\delta l/k}).
\]
Reciprocity pairs these \(j\) exterior roots with \(j\) interior roots outside the band.
On all other roots, (4) is uniform, and the average there of \(F(\xi_x)\) with denominator \(d\) tends to its circle integral.
The sum of absolute logarithms is \(2m\).
The normalized error from the large roots is \(O((l/k)e^{-\delta l/k})=o(1)\), proving the first formula in (2).

The integer resultant in (2) is nonzero: the unit \(x^k\) cannot equal the rational number \(-b/a\) of modulus greater than one.
For band roots \(\log|a x^k+b|=\log|a\xi_x+b|+o(1)\) uniformly.
The latter continuous function averages to \(\log|b|\) on the circle by the convergent power series for \(\log(1+(a/b)\xi)\).
The \(j\) outlying interior roots give uniformly bounded logarithms because \(|x|<1,\ |b|>|a|\).
Their fraction tends to zero, as does the fraction of the \(j\) exterior roots, on which the preceding estimate supplies the negative term in (2).
This proves the second identity and in particular bounds \(lj/(kd)\) above.

Finally \(1/10\le d\sum_x(\log|x|)^2\) by the quadratic radial floor.
The band roots contribute at most \(O((d/l)^2)=o(1)\) to the right side, and the other roots contribute \(O(dj/k^2)\).
Thus \(dj\gg k^2\), giving \(j\ge1\) eventually; \(m\ge\delta/k\) then forces \(k\to\infty\).
If \(|c|>2(|b|-|a|)\), (1) yields the asserted lower bounds for families with bounded exterior count by contradiction since \(d\to\infty\).
For \(ab>0\) multiply the equation by a sign to have \(a,b>0\).
A positive exterior root would force \(c<-2(a+b)\) by evaluating the two hyperbolic cosines there.
Then there is no circle root, excluding a Salem factor of degree at least four.
This proves the stated necessary conditions on its coefficients.
