# Eventual irreducibility of all power substitutions at each fixed delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## The coefficient obstruction at large integer seeds

Fix an integer \(n\ge7\).
For an integer \(a\ge3\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r\) be the unique exterior root of \(G(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), and let \(P\) be its Salem minimal polynomial.
For all sufficiently large \(a\), depending on \(n\), the only positive integer \(q\) with \(r^{1/q}\in\mathbb Q(r)\) is \(q=1\).
This holds simultaneously for both signs.
Consequently \(P(X^h)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for every integer \(h\ge1\).
It has \(h\) exterior roots of common modulus \(r^{1/h}\) and Mahler measure \(r\).
The other factors of \(G(X^h)\) are cyclotomic.
The proof gives no numerical cutoff for the exceptional seeds.

<a id="approximation"></a>
## Approximating coefficients by the limiting circle roots

Suppose there were infinitely many exceptional seeds at this fixed delay.
The [fixed-delay bound for field radicals](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) allows passage to a sequence \(a\to\infty\) with a common exponent \(q\ge2\) and sign \(\epsilon\).
Write \(\beta=r^{1/q}\) for the resulting Salem numbers, which have the same degree \(d\) as \(r\) by the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Let \(S_a\) denote the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Put \(L=n-1,\ U=G/P,\ A=(X^L-\epsilon)/U\).
By the [torsion count](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots), \(U\) depends only on \(n,\epsilon\) and consists of the linear factors at \(1,-1\) of \(X^L-\epsilon\).
In particular \(D=\deg A=d-2\ge4\) is even.

Pair each neutral conjugate \(z\) of \(r\) with its distinct root of unity \(\zeta\) of \(A\) at angular distance at most \(\pi/(aL)\), using the [circle phase pairing](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#angles).
The \(q\)-th powers of the neutral conjugates \(w\) of \(\beta\) give these \(z\) bijectively.
Choose the nearest \(q\)-th root \(\rho\) of the corresponding \(\zeta\) to \(w\).
Their angular distance is at most \(\pi/(aqL)\), by lifting the shorter arc under the \(q\)-th power map.
This uses only that \(q\) is positive, and also applies when \(q\) shares a factor with \(2L\).
Each \(\zeta\) is nonreal, and the choices respect conjugation.
The set of possible choices is finite, so pass to a subsequence where the monic polynomial \(B(X)=\prod_{\rho}(X-\rho)\) is fixed.
Its \(D\) roots are distinct, in conjugate pairs, and its coefficients are real cyclotomic integers.

The monic polynomial of the \(D\) neutral conjugates \(w\) is \(B+O_{n,q}(a^{-1})\) coefficientwise, by the product formula and the angular bound.
Set \(c=[X^{D-1}]B,\ T=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), and take the integer \(b=-[X^{d-1}]S_a\).
Comparing the coefficient of \(X^{d-1}\) gives \(T=b+c+O_{n,q}(a^{-1})\).
In particular \(b\sim\beta\to\infty\) and \(a\asymp b^q\), since \(a-1<r<a+1\).
Multiplying the neutral polynomial by \(X^2-TX+1\) gives the coefficientwise estimate
\[
    S_a=b\,F+C+O_{n,q}(b^{1-q}),\qquad
    F=-XB,\qquad C=(X^2-cX+1)B .                                     \tag{1}
\]

<a id="subspaces"></a>
## Rational dependencies between coefficient slopes

We use the [number-field Subspace Theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations) at its inspected statement scope.
Suppose \(u_1,\ldots,u_j\) are coefficients of \(F\) such that \(1,u_1,\ldots,u_j\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(v_i\) be the corresponding coefficients of \(C\), and \(y_i\) those of \(S_a\), all at the same powers of \(X\).
By (1),
\[
                 |bu_i+v_i-y_i|=O_{n,q}(b^{1-q}).
\]
If \(j(q-1)>1\), there cannot be such an infinite sequence.
Indeed on the integer vectors \((b,y_1,\ldots,y_j,1)\), the independent linear forms given by the first and last coordinates and the \(j\) errors have product of absolute values \(O(b^{1-j(q-1)})\).
An error can vanish at most once at distinct \(b\), since two such equalities would make \(u_i\) rational.
We may take the \(b\)'s distinct.

For the cited number-field formulation, take a totally real Galois field containing the coefficients, for instance the maximal real subfield of a cyclotomic field containing all the chosen roots.
At each real place corresponding to \(\sigma\), write the forms with coefficients \(\sigma^{-1}u_i,\sigma^{-1}v_i\).
Their unnormalized evaluated absolute values are then the same at every real place on these rational vectors.
The projective height is \(\log b+O(1)\), since the last coordinate is one and each \(y_i=O(b)\).
With \(j+2\) forms, the normalized logarithmic sum over the archimedean places exceeds \((j+2)\) times this height by at least \((j(q-1)-1)\log b-O(1)\).
Thus an infinite subsequence lies in one proper hyperplane.
Its rational points satisfy a nonzero rational linear relation, obtained by expanding the coefficients of a defining equation in a rational basis of the field.
Dividing that relation by \(b\) and taking the limit gives a dependence of \(1,u_1,\ldots,u_j\), unless only the coefficient of the last coordinate survives in the original relation.
That alternative cannot vanish on the vectors, whose last coordinate is one.

At \(q\ge3\), taking \(j=1\) shows that all coefficients of \(B\) are rational and hence integral.
Then \(c,F,C\) are integral, and (1) implies \(S_a=bF+C=(X^2-(b+c)X+1)B\) for sufficiently large \(b\).
This contradicts irreducibility.
At \(q=2\), taking \(j=2\) gives
\[
                  \dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}
                   \bigl(\{1\}\cup\{\text{coefficients of }B\}\bigr)\le2.    \tag{2}
\]
Rational \(B\) gives the same contradiction because the error in (1) still tends to zero.

<a id="square"></a>
## A common rational factor in the square case

Suppose \(q=2\) and the dimension in (2) is exactly two.
Write \(B=B_0+tB_1\) with \(B_0,B_1\in\mathbb Q[X]\), \(B_1\ne0\), and \(t\) irrational.
Exactly one square root of each root of \(A\) was used for \(B\).
Since \(D\) is even, this gives the monic identity \(B(X)B(-X)=A(X^2)\).
The polynomial \(B_1(X)B_1(-X)\) is nonzero, so at a nonzero coefficient of this product the identity gives a quadratic equation over \(\mathbb Q\) satisfied by \(t\).
Thus \(t\) lies in a real quadratic field with nontrivial automorphism \(\sigma\), and all coefficients of \(B,F,C\) belong to that field.

Fix an irrational coefficient \(u_*\) of \(F\), with corresponding intercept \(v_*\) from \(C\) and integer coefficient \(y_*\) from \(S_a\).
For any other coefficient triple \(u_i,v_i,y_i\), write \(u_i=\lambda_i u_*+\mu_i\) with \(\lambda_i,\mu_i\in\mathbb Q\).
By (1), \(y_i-\lambda_i y_*-\mu_i b\to v_i-\lambda_i v_*\).
The left side belongs to a lattice of fixed rational denominator, so the limit is rational.
This holds also for rational \(u_i\), with \(\lambda_i=0\), and for \(u_*\) itself.
Taking quadratic conjugates and subtracting gives the polynomial identity
\[
 C-C^\sigma=kX(B-B^\sigma),\qquad
          k=-\frac{v_*-v_*^\sigma}{u_*-u_*^\sigma}\in\mathbb Q .
\]
The ratio is rational since the anti-invariant subspace of a quadratic field is one-dimensional over \(\mathbb Q\).
Substituting for \(C\) gives
\[
      (X^2-(c+k)X+1)B
               =(X^2-(c^\sigma+k)X+1)B^\sigma.                       \tag{3}
\]
If \(c=c^\sigma\) this would force \(B=B^\sigma\), contradicting the choice of an irrational coefficient.
Otherwise the two monic quadratics in (3) are coprime, since their difference is a nonzero multiple of \(X\) and both constant terms are one.
It follows that \(B=(X^2-(c^\sigma+k)X+1)H\) and \(B^\sigma=(X^2-(c+k)X+1)H\) for the same monic polynomial \(H\).
Conjugating shows \(H^\sigma=H\).
Its roots are roots of unity, so its rational coefficients are algebraic integers and \(H\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Its degree is \(D-2\ge2\).

Both \(F\) and \(C\) are multiples of \(H\).
Taking remainders in (1) modulo this fixed monic polynomial gives an integral polynomial \(S_a\bmod H\) with coefficients tending to zero, because the remainder operation is linear and the degrees are bounded.
Eventually \(H\mid S_a\), again contradicting irreducibility.
This excludes an infinite sequence for \(q=2\) and completes the assertion about field radicals.
The [radical and factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now gives irreducibility at every substitution, and the \(h\)-th roots of \(r\) account for its entire Mahler measure.
