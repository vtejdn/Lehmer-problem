# Two dyadic endpoint values of reciprocal five-term factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Precision from the conductor and the offset

Let \(n>1\) be odd and prime to three, let \(0<k<n\) be odd and coprime to \(n\), and put \(l=n-k\).
For a nonempty set \(\mathcal T\) of divisors \(t>1\) of \(n\), let \(T_l\) be the trace polynomial of the unique monic dyadic divisor of
\[
             P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1
\]
with reduction \(\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}\bar\Phi_{3t}\) modulo two.
Here \(\Phi_j\) is the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial, and \(\Psi_R\) denotes the trace polynomial of \(\Phi_R\) for odd \(R>1\), so \(\Phi_R(X)=X^{\varphi(R)/2}\Psi_R(X+X^{-1})\).
Write \(C_m(X+X^{-1})=X^m+X^{-m}\).
For each \(t\) put \(R=\operatorname{rad}(t),\ H=t/R,\ M=n/H\).
Let \(s_M\) be the unique odd dyadic integer satisfying \(C_M(s_M)=-1/3\), and let \(b_M\in\mathbb Z_2\) be the unique odd \(M\)-th root of three.
Define
\[
                       E_{R,M}=b_M^{\varphi(R)}\Psi_R(-s_M)^2.
\]
If \(j\ge1\) and \((j-1)k<\min_{t\in\mathcal T} H\), then
\[
               T_l(2)\equiv1,\qquad
               T_l(-2)\equiv\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}E_{R,M}\pmod{2^j}.    \tag{1}
\]
Thus a larger ratio \(H/k\) supplies more digits of both values.

For \(R=35\) one has \(v_2(E_{35,M}-1)=4\) for every such \(M\).
Consequently the local divisor cannot be a rational divisor if all radicals are \(35\), the set \(\mathcal T\) has odd cardinality and \(4k<\min H\).
More generally, put \(b=v_2(|\mathcal T|)\).
If all radicals are \(35\), the integers \(M\) agree modulo \(3\cdot2^{4+b}\) and \((4+b)k<\min H\), then
\[
                            v_2(T_l(-2)-1)=4+b .                         \tag{2}
\]
This also excludes a rational divisor.

For example, at \(n=5^a7^d\) with \(a,d\ge1\) the single level \(t=n\) has degree \(48n/35\) in a parent of degree \(2n\).
Its binary divisor has no rational lift for any admissible \(k<n/140\).
If \(d\ge5\), the two levels \(t=n,n/7^4\) together have degree \((48n/35)(1+7^{-4})\).
They have no rational lift for any admissible \(k<n/(175\cdot7^4)\).
These estimates apply to prescribed full conductors of degree comparable to the parent degree.

<a id="branches"></a>
## Factoring a root as a quotient of Laurent series

Use \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z_2[\omega]\) with \(\omega\) a primitive cube root.
Work in the ring of compatible Laurent polynomials in \(Z\) modulo \(2^j\) for all \(j\ge1\), so that substitution of a unit for \(Z\) is defined at every finite precision.
We will use series in which the coefficient of \(Z^i\) is divisible by \(2^{|i|}\).
This condition is preserved by multiplication since \(|a+b|\le |a|+|b|\).

Fix \(u=\omega\) or \(\omega^2\).
There is a unique pair of series \(A_u,B_u\), both one modulo two, with \(A_u\) using only nonnegative powers of \(Z\) and \(B_u\) using only nonpositive powers with constant term one, such that
\[
    u(1-2Z^{-1})A_u^{2M}+(A_uB_u)^M+u^{-1}(1-2Z)B_u^{2M}=0.           \tag{3}
\]
Their coefficients satisfy the divisibility condition above.
To see this start with \(A_u=B_u=1\bmod2\).
Suppose (3) holds modulo \(2^i\) for polynomial representatives satisfying the coefficient bounds.
Divide its left side by \(2^i\) and reduce modulo two.
The resulting Laurent polynomial has powers confined to \([-i,i]\) by the coefficient bounds.
The two partial derivatives of the left side with respect to \(A_u,B_u\) both reduce to one at this pair because \(M\) is odd.
Correct \(A_u\) by \(2^i\) times a lift of the part with nonnegative powers and \(B_u\) by \(2^i\) times a lift of the part with negative powers.
This gives the unique pair at the next precision and preserves the coefficient bounds.
In particular at precision \(2^j\) both series have no powers beyond \(j-1\) in absolute value.

Write \(V_u=A_u/B_u\).
The quantities \(Y_u=uV_u^M\) are the two roots of
\[
                    (1-2Z^{-1})Y^2+Y+(1-2Z)=0 .
\]
Their difference is a unit since their residues are the two distinct primitive cube roots.
Factoring the monic normalization gives
\[
                 (V_{\omega}V_{\omega^2})^M
                     =\frac{1-2Z}{1-2Z^{-1}} .
\]
Taking an odd \(M\)-th root congruent to one modulo two is unique by successive lifting with unit derivative.
In particular the roots of \(1-2Z\) and \(1-2Z^{-1}\) with this normalization exist in their corresponding one-sided series rings, each with constant term one.
An expression as a quotient of a nonnegative series by a nonpositive series, both congruent to one modulo two and with denominator constant term one, is unique.
Indeed the ratio of two candidate numerators equals the ratio of their denominators, so it lies in both one-sided series rings and is constant, and the denominator normalization makes that constant one.
This gives the two separate identities
\[
     A_\omega A_{\omega^2}=(1-2Z)^{1/M},\qquad
     B_\omega B_{\omega^2}=(1-2Z^{-1})^{1/M}.                           \tag{4}
\]

<a id="monic"></a>
## The monic factor over a residue class

For one selected \(t\), take \(\xi\) of order \(3R\) in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), and put \(u=\xi^M\).
Since \(M=R(n/t)\) is prime to three, this \(u\) is a primitive cube root.
The [squarefree fibers of the binary reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v246.md#fiber) above the primitive \(3R\)-th roots have defining polynomials \(X^H-\bar\xi\).
Their product is \(\bar\Phi_{3t}\).
With \((j-1)k<H\), the corresponding monic factor modulo \(2^j\) is
\[
                 G_\xi=X^H B_u(X^k)-\xi A_u(X^k).                      \tag{5}
\]
Indeed all the negative powers multiplied by \(X^H\) land in strictly positive degrees below \(H\), and the positive powers in \(A_u(X^k)\) have degrees strictly below \(H\).
The constant coefficient of \(B_u\) gives the leading term \(X^H\).
The unit constant term of (5) makes \(X\) invertible in the quotient by (5), and \(B_u(X^k)\) there is a unit because it is one modulo two.
Thus \(X^H=\xi V_u(X^k)\) in that quotient.
Substituting \(Z=X^k\) and \(Y=X^n=uV_u(X^k)^M\) into the quadratic above gives \(P_l(X)=0\), since its value is \(X^{2n}-2X^{2n-k}+X^n+1-2X^k\).
Therefore (5) divides \(P_l\), and squarefreeness of the reduction identifies the lift uniquely by monic Hensel factorization.

For \(x=\pm1\) we have \(x^H=x^k=x\).
Write \(\xi=z r\) with \(z\) primitive of order three and \(r\) primitive of order \(R\), so that \(u=z^M\).
At fixed \(z\), multiplication of (5) at \(x\) over \(r\) gives
\[
          (x B_u(x))^{\varphi(R)}
                 \Phi_R\bigl(z V_u(x)/x\bigr)\pmod{2^j}.
\]
This uses \(\prod_r(1-rT)=\Phi_R(T)\).
The full conductor has degree \(2H\varphi(R)\).
Since \(\varphi(R)\) is even, both the powers of \(x\) in these products and the trace normalization \(x^{-H\varphi(R)}\) are one at \(x=\pm1\).

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Specialization at the two signs

At \(Z=1\) the quadratic for \(Y\) has roots \(-\omega,-\omega^2\).
Thus \(Y_u(1)=-u\) by its residue, and \(V_u(1)=-1\) by uniqueness of the root congruent to one of \(V^M=-1\).
The product of the two \(B_u(1)\) is \(-1\) by (4), and its \(\varphi(R)\)-th power is one.
The two remaining factors \(\Phi_R(-z)\) are conjugate cube roots of unity by the [integral evaluation at trace one](Yin_C_G0345_v245.md#reference), so their product is one.
This proves the first congruence in (1).

At \(Z=-1\) the two roots of the quadratic satisfy \(3Y^2+Y+3=0\), are inverse to one another, and are exchanged by the unramified quadratic conjugation.
For one \(z\) put \(r_M=zV_{z^M}(-1)\), so \(r_M^M=Y_{z^M}(-1)\).
The value for the other \(z\) is \(r_M^{-1}\), by the product identity for the two \(V_u\) at \(-1\).
Consequently \(s=r_M+r_M^{-1}\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z_2\), reduces to one modulo two and satisfies \(C_M(s)=-1/3\).
For any \(M\) prime to six one has \(C_M(1)=1\) and \(C'_M(1)=\pm M\).
Indeed one may evaluate the trace and its derivative at \(z+z^{-1}=1\) for a primitive sixth root \(z\), where \(z^M=z^{\pm1}\).
Hensel uniqueness identifies \(s=s_M\).
The product of the remaining cyclotomic evaluations at \(-r_M,-r_M^{-1}\) is \(\Psi_R(-s_M)^2\) by the trace representation.
The two \(B_u(-1)\) have product \(b_M\) by (4).
Multiplication over the selected conductors proves (1).

<a id="mixed"></a>
## A mixed conductor with a nonzero fourth digit

The divided difference of \(C_M\) at \(s_M,1\) is odd since both arguments reduce to one and the derivative is odd there.
Thus \(v_2(s_M-1)=v_2(-1/3-1)=2\).
Put \(Y=-s_M\), so \(Y\equiv3\bmod8\).
The cyclotomic identity \(\Phi_{35}(X)\Phi_5(X)=\Phi_5(X^7)\) gives
\[
          \Psi_{35}(Y)\Psi_5(Y)=\Psi_5(C_7(Y)),\qquad
          \Psi_5(Y)=Y^2+Y-1 .
\]
The identity
\[
                  C_7(Y)-Y=Y(Y^2-1)(Y^2-2)(Y^2-4)
\]
has right side of valuation three because \(Y\equiv3\bmod8\).
The denominator \(\Psi_5(Y)\) and the factor \(C_7(Y)+Y+1\) are odd.
Subtracting \(\Psi_5(Y)\) from \(\Psi_5(C_7(Y))\) now gives \(v_2(\Psi_{35}(Y)-1)=3\).
It follows that \(\Psi_{35}(Y)^2\equiv17\bmod32\).
Since \(\varphi(35)=24\) and \(3^{24}\equiv1\bmod32\), \(b_M^{24}\equiv1\bmod32\) by uniqueness of odd \(M\)-th roots in the finite group of units modulo 32.
This proves \(E_{35,M}\equiv17\bmod32\).
An odd number of these factors has the same residue, whereas [every rational lift of the specified type](Yin_C_G0345_v244.md#traces) must have \(T_l(-2)=1\).

For (2), if \(M\equiv M'\bmod 3\cdot2^{4+b}\), choose the same primitive cube root \(z\) in both constructions of \(r_M,r_{M'}\).
Then the root \(Y_{z^M}(-1)=Y_{z^{M'}}(-1)=y\) is the same, being determined by the quadratic \(3Y^2+Y+3\) and its residue.
The ratio \(r_M/r_{M'}\) reduces to one and its \(MM'\)-th power is \(y^{M'-M}\).
Since \(y^3\equiv1\bmod2\), repeated squaring gives \(y^{M'-M}\equiv1\bmod 2^{5+b}\), also for negative exponent by inversion.
For a unit reducing to one, an odd power preserves the valuation of its difference from one by the geometric sum.
Hence \(r_M\equiv r_{M'}\bmod2^{5+b}\), and the same congruence holds for \(s_M,s_{M'}\).
Applying the same power comparison to \(b_M,b_{M'}\), using \(3\equiv1\bmod2\), proves \(E_{35,M}\equiv E_{35,M'}\bmod2^{5+b}\).
In the product in (1) replace every factor by one such \(E\) at this precision.
As \(v_2(E-1)=4\), raising to an odd power preserves this valuation and each further squaring increases it by one since the unit is one modulo four.
Thus \(v_2(E^{|\mathcal T|}-1)=4+b\), giving (2) at \(j=5+b\).

For the single top conductor, \(H=n/35\), so \(4k<H\) gives the first example.
For the two levels, \(M=35,35\cdot7^4\) agree modulo \(96\) because \(7^4=2401\equiv1\bmod96\).
Here \(b=1\) and \(\min H=n/(35\cdot7^4)\), as needed.
Both displayed degrees follow by summing \(\varphi(3t)=48t/35\).
