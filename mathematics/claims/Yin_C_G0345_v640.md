# Isolation and degree deficits of short integral numerators

<a id="statement"></a>
## Integral numerators near the sharp short-width bound

Let \(P\) be a monic integer polynomial of degree \(d\ge1\) with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Put \(m=\log M(P)>0\) and \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\), and use Haar probability measure for the circle norm.
For fixed \(P\), there is at most one integral polynomial \(S\) satisfying
\[
      S(0)=1,\qquad \deg S<d,\qquad \|S/P\|_2^2<5C/4.                \tag{1}
\]
Every irreducible factor of such an \(S\) equals its reciprocal up to sign.
In particular \(S\) itself has this symmetry.

For every integral \(S\) with \(S(0)=1\) and \(w=\deg S<d\), let \(g=d-w\) and \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sign}([z^w]S)\).
Let \(O\) be the [reflected polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) with \(O(0)=e^m\), only exterior roots, and the same boundary modulus as \(P\).
Set \(J=O^*/O\), with \(O^*(z)=z^d\overline{O(1/\bar z)}\), and let \(J_j=[z^j]J\).
All \(J_j\) are real.
Then \(|J_g|\le1-J_0^2\), and
\[
     \|S/P\|_2^2\ge \frac{2C}{1+\epsilon J_g/(1-J_0^2)}.             \tag{2}
\]

Consider a sequence of such denominators with \(m\to0\) and indivisible integral Laurent numerators \(A\) with \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to1/4\).
The [normalized remainder](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#statement) at any balanced division then satisfies (1) eventually and hence is uniquely determined by \(P\).
For its actual degree deficit, possibly varying with \(P\), one has
\[
               \frac{\epsilon J_g}{1-J_0^2}\longrightarrow1.         \tag{3}
\]
Give each root \(\alpha\) mass \(\bigl|\log|\alpha|\bigr|/(2m)\) at \(\alpha/|\alpha|\), with multiplicity.
Along a subsequence for which these angular probabilities tend to Haar measure, \(g\to\infty\).
If they tend to the uniform measure on \(z^N=\sigma\), where \(N\ge1\) and \(\sigma=\pm1\), every fixed value of \(g\) occurring infinitely often on that subsequence is divisible by \(N\).

<a id="isolation"></a>
## Separation of integral polynomials and reversal of factors

The [orthogonal identity](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#extremizer) shows that the squared weighted distance from any \(S\) with constant one and degree less than \(d\) to the unique complex minimizer is \(\|S/P\|_2^2-C\).
Each candidate in (1) therefore lies at distance less than \(\sqrt C/2\) from that minimizer.
The nonzero difference of two candidates would have integral coefficients and width less than \(d\), hence weighted norm at least \(\sqrt C\) by the [short-width bound](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement).
The triangle inequality proves uniqueness.

For a real polynomial \(H\) of degree \(s\) and nonzero constant, put \(H^\#(z)=z^s H(1/z)\).
This reversal has the same boundary modulus as \(H\).
The short-width inequality applied to \(S^\#\) gives \(C|[z^w]S|^2\le\|S/P\|_2^2<5C/4\), so the leading coefficient of a candidate has modulus one.
Every integral factor then has constant and leading coefficients in \(\{-1,1\}\).
For any irreducible factor \(H\) of \(S\), replace \(H\) by \(H^\#\) in \(S\) and normalize the sign to restore the constant one.
The new polynomial satisfies (1), so uniqueness and cancellation of \(S/H\) give \(H=\pm H^\#\).
The same conclusion for \(S\) follows by multiplying its factors.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Two endpoint evaluations at the actual width

Since \(P\) is real, conjugate roots in the reflection construction give real coefficients for \(O\) after the normalization \(O(0)>0\).
The function \(J\) is analytic in the closed disk and has modulus one on its boundary, with \(b=J_0\) real and \(|b|=e^{-2m}\).
Let \(f=S/O\) and use the circle inner product linear in its first entry.
The vectors \(v=1-bJ\) and \(v_g=z^{-g}(J-b)\) have squared norm \(1-b^2\).
Their inner product is the circle average of \(z^g(\bar J-2b+b^2J)\), equal to \(J_g\) since \(g>0\).
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(|J_g|\le1-b^2\).

The [evaluation identity](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) gives \(\langle f,v\rangle=1/O(0)\).
The average of \(z^g f\) vanishes, whereas \(z^g S/O^*\) is analytic on the exterior of the circle and takes the value \([z^w]S/O(0)\) at infinity.
Since \(\bar J f=S/O^*\) on the circle, this gives \(\langle f,v_g\rangle=[z^w]S/O(0)\).
Thus
\[
  \langle f,v+\epsilon v_g\rangle
       =\frac{1+|[z^w]S|}{O(0)}\ge\frac{2}{O(0)},\qquad
  \|v+\epsilon v_g\|_2^2=2(1-b^2+\epsilon J_g).
\]
The positive inner product ensures that the last squared norm is nonzero.
Since \(1/(O(0)^2(1-b^2))=C\), Cauchy--Schwarz proves (2).

<a id="limits"></a>
## A fixed degree deficit and the limiting angles

For the normalized remainders of the stated sequence, [energy rigidity](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#extremizer) gives integrality eventually and \(\|S/P\|_2^2/C\to1\), since \(mC\to1/4\).
Thus (1) holds eventually, and (2) gives (3) together with \(|J_g|/(1-b^2)\le1\).
To extract the consequence for a fixed \(g=k\), write \(\mu_P\) for the angular probability above and \(\widehat\mu_P(k)=\int z^{-k}\,d\mu_P(z)\).
In the [angular expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#angles), \(J=bFG\) with \(F(0)=G(0)=1\).
For each fixed \(k\ge1\), the coefficient \([z^k](-\log(FG))\) is \(4m\widehat\mu_P(k)+O_k(m^3)\).
All positive-order logarithmic coefficients through degree \(k\) are \(O_k(m)\), so exponentiation gives
\[
   \frac{J_k}{1-b^2}=-b\,\widehat\mu_P(k)+O_k(m),
\]
using \(1-b^2=1-e^{-4m}=4m+O(m^2)\).
If \(g=k\) occurs infinitely often along a subsequence with angular limit \(\mu\), (3) implies \(|\widehat\mu(k)|=1\) since \(|b|\to1\).
Haar measure has zero Fourier coefficients at all positive indices, so every bounded set of deficits is eventually avoided in that case.
For uniform measure on \(z^N=\sigma\), the indices of Fourier coefficients of modulus one are exactly \(N\mathbb Z\), yielding the claimed divisibility.
