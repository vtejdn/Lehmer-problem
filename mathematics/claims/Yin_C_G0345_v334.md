# Computable uniform Mahler gaps for arbitrarily placed short support intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## An algorithm for the constants

There is an algorithm which, on input an integer \(k\ge1\), terminates and returns positive rational numbers \(\epsilon_k,\eta_k\) with the following property.
If \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is irreducible, has degree \(N\ge1\), nonzero constant term and positive logarithmic Mahler measure, and its coefficient support is covered by \(k\) real intervals of length at most \(\epsilon_kN\) each, then
\[
                              m(P)\ge\eta_k.                         \tag{1}
\]
The input does not include the interval positions, coefficient heights or a bound on \(N\).
The algorithm below is a termination result, not a claim that its very large constants have been numerically evaluated.

The [qualitative interval theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v333.md#statement) already proves the existence of such constants.
Here finite rational certificates replace the choice of an unknown real relation space in its compactness argument.
The local analytic bounds are made computable explicitly, rather than treating an unspecified constant in that proof as an enumerable certificate.
Exact rational relations of a real point are used only in the proof that the certificates cover; the algorithm never tests such relations.

We also retain a local certificate for primitive moving rows.
It permits any subset of the distinct model rows to be nonzero, removal of their common integral Laurent factor, and a common recentering of the remaining slow coefficients.
At a certified small width, diagonal measure below \(c_R/2\), with \(c_R\) as in [the partial-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement), forces the primitive multivariable polynomial to be a product of horizontal torsion factors.
Their fast character vectors belong to an explicit finite list, and their specialized exponents have any prescribed fixed margin over the slow width.
The precise data and inequalities are given in [the local certificate](#certificate).

All circle measures are normalized, and \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.

## Rational constants for separated exponential shapes

The following recipe will be used twice.
Its inputs are an integer \(s\ge2\), a rational \(B\ge1\), and a rational \(0<\gamma\le1\).
Put \(p=2s\), \(\alpha=1/(s-1)\), and define
\[
\begin{split}
 V&=s!B^{s^2}\gamma^{-s^2},& a&=(16sV)^{-1},\\
 T&=4sB^{s+1},& E&=32s(B+1)^s,\\
 \eta&=\min(1/16,a/(4E)),& J&=\lceil16T/a\rceil,\\
 C_{\rm sub}&=24psJ/a,& c&=(2C_{\rm sub})^{-(s-1)}.               \tag{2}
\end{split}
\]
These are positive rational numbers, apart from the integer ceiling operation.

Consider \(s\) real frequencies \(\kappa_i\in[-B,B]\), separated by at least \(\gamma\).
For the Vandermonde matrix with entries \((i\kappa_j)^h\), \(0\le h<s\), the determinant has modulus at least \(\gamma^{s(s-1)/2}\).
Each cofactor is at most \((s-1)!B^{(s-1)^2}\), by its permutation expansion.
Its inverse maximum row-sum norm is therefore at most \(V\).
For frozen coefficients of Euclidean norm between \(1/2\) and \(3\sqrt p/2\), some derivative of order at most \(s-1\) has modulus at least \(1/(2sV)\) at every real point.
One real or imaginary component then has modulus at least \(1/(4sV)=4a\).
All next derivatives needed for these tests are bounded by \(T\).

Partition an interval of length \(2\pi\) into \(J\) equal pieces.
Select the frozen derivative component at the midpoint of each piece; its change from that midpoint is at most \(a/4\), using \(2\pi<8\) and (2).
Suppose the coefficients now move on that interval by applying an inverse real orthogonal map to a ratio vector, pairing the resulting real coordinates into complex coordinates and dividing by two, as in the realification below.
One ratio is one, all ratios have modulus at most three, and their derivatives of orders one through \(s-1\) have modulus at most \(2\eta\).
The complex coefficient derivatives have sum of moduli at most \(2s\eta\).
Freezing at one point changes the coefficient vector itself in sum norm by at most \(16s\eta\).
Leibniz's formula consequently changes every derivative of the exponential sum through order \(s-1\) by at most \(E\eta\le a/4\).
Each selected real derivative thus stays separated from zero by at least \(a\).

For a real function with \(|g^{(j)}|\ge a\), its set \(|g|\le u\) has length at most \(4(j+1)(u/a)^{1/j}\).
Indeed a set of length \(l\) supplies \(j+1\) points separated by at least \(l/[2(j+1)]\), with an arbitrarily small limiting relaxation; the divided difference is at least \(a/j!\), whereas its interpolation formula is at most \(u2^j/(j!h^j)\) for separation \(h\).
For order zero the sublevel set is empty when \(u<a\); for larger \(u\) its interval length is an adequate bound.
Summing the pieces gives relative sublevel measure at most \(8sJ a^{-1}u^\alpha\).

For the real-coordinate charts used below, the modulus of the largest original real coordinate is at most \(3\sqrt p\) times the chosen chart denominator.
Replacing a sublevel threshold by \(3\sqrt p\,u\), and using the trivial bound one if this threshold exceeds one, is covered by \(C_{\rm sub}u^\alpha\).
In particular at least half of each such good interval has relative modulus at least \(c\).
The same constants apply to a frozen shape with no moving coefficients, and to fewer occupied coefficients by putting zeros in the unused positions.

## An explicit local analytic cost chain

Fix distinct vectors \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\in\mathbb Z^r\), \(s\ge2\), and let
\[
 W_j=\max_i a_{ij}-\min_i a_{ij},\qquad
 B_0=1+\sum_{j=1}^r W_j+2s.
\]
Suppose integral slow rows \(b_i\), some possibly zero, are supported in \([-d,d]\), where \(d\ge1\), and their gcd including integer content is a unit.
At least two rows are occupied.
Let \(q\in\mathbb Z^r\), let \(N\ge1\) be an integer scale, and suppose the frequencies \(\kappa_i=a_i\cdot q/N\) are in \([-3,3]\) and pairwise separated by \(\gamma\), where \(0<\gamma\le1\).
Set
\[
 F(X,Y)=\sum_i b_i(X)Y^{a_i},\qquad H(X)=F(X,X^q),\qquad I=m(F),
\]
and let \(A(t)\) be the largest modulus among twice the real and imaginary parts of the slow coefficients.
This is positive everywhere on the real circle.

Run (2) with \(B=3\) and this \(\gamma\), and then use only rational operations in
\[
\begin{split}
 \delta&=2^{-18},&K&=2^{24},& Z_0&=2^{36},& L&=2^{48}p,\\
 H_0&=(8ps)^{2s},
 &J_0&=2\{(s+1)L+\delta^{-1}(2/\eta+H_0)\},\\
 Q_0&=4p^2(s+1)J_0+2^{20},
 &F_0&=16Q_0/\eta,\\
 A_0&=L+\delta^{-1}/c,
 &U_0&=4096F_0+s/\delta,\\
 D_*&=2^{15}F_0/\delta,
 &C_*&=2^8sC_{\rm sub}+2^{25}B_0F_0(U_0+A_0).                  \tag{3}
\end{split}
\]
If \(N/d\ge D_*\), the loss from clipping the relative diagonal logarithm below at \(\log u\), \(0<u<1\), is at most
\[
                   C_*\{u^\alpha+(d/N)(1+I)\}.                \tag{4}
\]
We verify the cost chain, keeping the analytic constructions at their [original responsible proof](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#curvature).

The random-phase realification there has swept circle measure \(\tau\) of mass at most \(2d\).
If its occupied slow width is zero, this is the zero measure and the same energy estimates hold with no root terms.
The multivariable coefficient bound, obtained by iterating the elementary bound in each fast coordinate, gives mean random-phase logarithm at most \(I+B_0\).
The cross-resultant argument therefore gives the exact Fourier bound
\[
 \sum_{h\ne0}\frac{|\widehat\tau(h)|^2}{|h|}
                          \le8d(I+B_0).                       \tag{5}
\]
Use the largest overfull radii with threshold \(Kd r\) and baseline \(\delta/d\), as in [the adaptive packing](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#packing).
Their disjoint doubled arcs have \(\sum r_i\le2\).
A plateau tent supported on a doubled arc has Fourier coefficients bounded by
\(\min(2r,2/(rh^2))\), from its mass and derivative variation.
Its homogeneous squared \(H^{1/2}\) norm is at most 64 by summing below and above \(\lceil1/r\rceil\).
Disjoint nonnegative tents have nonpositive cross inner products.
At every nonbaseline radius their pairing with \(\tau-s_X\nu\), where \(s_X\le2d\), is at least \((K/2)dr\).
Weighted Fourier duality in (5), followed by the separate length count for baseline radii, gives
\[
                  \sum_i (dr_i)^2\le2^{12}B_0d(1+I).           \tag{6}
\]
Here the baseline contribution is at most \(2\delta d\), and the other contribution is at most \(2048K^{-2}d(I+B_0)\).

Write \(M=dr\ge\delta\) at one center.
Each reflected root whose lift lies in the disk of radius \(256r\) puts at least \(1/8\) of its Poisson mass in the arc of radius \(512r\).
For logarithmic depth \(a\le256\delta<1\), integrate the bound \(\sinh a/(\cosh a-\cos t)\ge2/(3a)\) on \(|t|\le a\); the boundary-root case has mass one.
Maximality of the radius thus bounds the local zero measure by
\[
                         8\cdot512K M=Z_0M.                   \tag{7}
\]

On the macro disk of radius \(256r\), remove the full real harmonic extension by its nonvanishing holomorphic gauge.
Each normalized real coordinate has modulus at most one.
The Green kernel has integral less than \(4R\) over a real diameter of a disk of radius \(R\), since it is bounded by \(\log(2R/|t-\zeta|)\).
Averaging on the central interval of length \(2r\) costs at most \(512Z_0M\).
Rotate the real vector orthogonally at the resulting point \(t_0\) so every rotated coordinate has modulus at least \(p^{-3/2}\exp(-512Z_0M)\).
The number \(L\) in (3) bounds its negative logarithm divided by \(M\), since \(M\ge\delta\).
All rotated coordinates are bounded by \(\sqrt p\) on the macro disk.

On the smaller disk of radius \(160r\), scaled derivatives of order \(j\le s\) have modulus at most \(p j!\) when \(Nr\ge1\), by Cauchy's estimate on radius \(64r\).
For a ratio \(u_a/u_b\), write its scaled derivative of order \(j\) as \(N_j/u_b^{j+1}\).
The recurrence
\[
 N_{j+1}=u_b N_j'/N-(j+1)N_j u_b'/N
\]
has numerator bound \(p^{j+1}3^j j!\).
One direct verification weights each derivative variable of order \(h\) by \(p h!\): a numerator monomial at order \(j\) has degree \(j+1\) and derivative order \(j\), so differentiation costs at most \(2j+1\), and the second term adds \(j+1\).
The successive bound multiplier is at most \(p(3j+2)\).
Every threshold polynomial through order \(s-1\) is therefore bounded by \(H_0\).

Choose the signed chart thresholds from the endpoints of \([2,3]\), and the signed derivative thresholds from the endpoints of \([\eta,2\eta]\).
At \(t_0\), one endpoint value has modulus at least \((\eta/2)\exp(-sLM)\).
Jensen on radii between \(100r\) and \(120r\), counting zeros inside \(50r\), gives at most \(J_0M\) zeros per equation.
Here \(1/\log2<2\), \(\log(2/\eta)\le2/\eta\), and \(\log H_0\le H_0\) explain the entirely rational choice in (3).
There are at most \(2p^2s\) equations.
Chart membership cannot pass a denominator zero with all ratios bounded, because the vector has no common zero.
Between the threshold points, a first derivative violation has length at most \(6/(\eta N)\); a first violation of higher order has length at most \(4/N\).
Counting threshold intersections, components and endpoints gives at most \(F_0M\) flagged grid intervals of length \(2\pi/N\).
No common period for the real frequencies is used.

The [dyadic parent construction](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#blocks) covers these marks by disjoint blocks with marked fractions in \((1/8,1/4]\), of total length at most \(64F_0M/N\).
The lower bound \(N/d\ge D_*\) makes every block length at most \(r/100\).
Each relevant block has a real good subset of length at least \(3l/8\), on which \(|H|/A\ge c\), by (2).
On its radius-\(16l\) disk, the second Green normalization gives an anchor of logarithm at least \(-A_0M\), using (7) and the diameter estimate with proportion \(3/128\).
Throughout this disk the normalized diagonal has logarithmic modulus at most \(U_0M\): the fast frequencies have modulus at most \(3N\), and \(Nl\le64F_0M\).

Jensen between radii \(7l\) and \(8l\) counts at most \(4(U_0+A_0)M\) zeros inside \(5l\).
After removing disk Blaschke factors in a radius between \(4l\) and \(5l\), Harnack's ratio on the block is at most \(5/3<2\).
Each removed factor costs at most \(l(1+\log12)<4l\) in its negative-logarithm integral.
Thus the block cost is at most \(20(U_0+A_0)Ml\).
Multiplying by the total block length and then by (6) is bounded by the second term defining \(C_*\) in (3).
On good intervals, integration of the sublevel estimate costs at most \((s-1)C_{\rm sub}u^\alpha\) times length.
The cover arcs and their two endpoint grid cells have total length at most 64, since \(\sum r_i\le2\) and \(Nr_i\ge1\).
This is bounded by the first term in \(C_*\), and proves (4).

## An explicit finite set of fast Fourier tests

Choose a primitive integer vector \(\ell\) separating the distinct \(a_i\), and put
\[
                R=\max_i a_i\cdot\ell-\min_i a_i\cdot\ell\ge1.
\]
Such a vector is computable by testing \((1,t,\ldots,t^{r-1})\) at successive integers \(t\); finitely many nonzero difference polynomials exclude only finitely many values.
On each line in this direction, factor out the lowest integer fast frequency; this preserves the modulus and puts all remaining frequencies in \([0,R]\).
Apply (2) with \(B=\max(3,R)\) and \(\gamma=1\), and denote its sublevel constant by \(C_{\rm ind,sub}\).
Set \(C_{\rm ind}=sC_{\rm ind,sub}\).
Averaging along the integer direction \(\ell\) gives independent clipping loss at most \(C_{\rm ind}u^\alpha\), by Haar invariance and the frozen form of the estimate.

For fixed fast angle, the variation of
\(f(t,y)=\log\max(u,|F(e^{it},e^{iy})|/A(t))\)
is at most
\[
                     B_{\rm var}d\log(s/u),\qquad B_{\rm var}=32p^2.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]
Indeed maximizing-coordinate switches have at most \(2p^2d\) roots in total; numerator, denominator and clipping equations and the derivatives of squared quotients add at most \(14pd\).
The numerator and denominator squared have degree at most \(2d\), and their quotient derivative numerator has degree at most \(4d\).
Identically zero equations give constant pieces or ties and do not add roots.
On every remaining piece the clipped quotient is monotone, with range length at most \(\log(s/u)\).
These counts also give (8) for empty rows.

Let \(T_A=1+\sum_i\|a_i\|_1\).
For any positive rational \(\xi\), take
\[
 H=\left\lceil\max\left(256,\left(\frac{64T_A}{u\xi}\right)^2,
                               2+2\max_j W_j\right)\right\rceil,
 \qquad T_H=(2H+1)^r-1.                                      \tag{9}
\]
The product Fejér kernel of order \(H\) approximates every profile \(f(t,\cdot)\) uniformly within \(\xi\).
For completeness, the one-dimensional kernel is bounded by \(16/((H+1)v^2)\) off zero, so its expected circular distance is at most \(32/\sqrt{H+1}\), by splitting at \(8/\sqrt{H+1}\).
The sum of the coordinate Lipschitz constants of the clipped profile is at most \(T_A/u\).
These inequalities imply the claimed error with the larger constant in (9).

If every nonzero integer \(h\in[-H,H]^r\) satisfies \(|h\cdot q|\ge\rho N/2\), integration by parts of each Fejér coefficient, using (8), gives clipped comparison error at most
\[
             2\xi+Q_{\rm clip}\,d/N,
             \qquad Q_{\rm clip}=\frac{2B_{\rm var}T_Hs}{u\rho}. \tag{10}
\]
Here \(\log(s/u)\le s/u\), and the constant Fourier coefficient is the independent fast mean.
Combining (4), (10) and the independent loss proves the finite inequality
\[
 |\mu-I|\le (C_*+C_{\rm ind})u^\alpha+2\xi
                  +(d/N)\{C_*(1+I)+Q_{\rm clip}\}.             \tag{11}
\]
All constants in this inequality have now been specified by finite rational operations and integer ceilings.

## A rational local certificate and its arithmetic conclusion

The certificate starts with a full-column-rank integer matrix \(\mathsf A\in\mathbb Z^{k\times r}\), \(1\le r\le k-1\), whose first row is zero and last row is nonzero.
Write \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\) for its distinct rows and let
\(\mathsf B=(\mathsf A^{\mathsf t}\mathsf A)^{-1}\mathsf A^{\mathsf t}\), a rational left inverse.
The distinct-row count satisfies \(2\le s\le k\).
Choose a rational \(0<\gamma\le1/4\), compute (2)–(3), and choose \(\ell,R,C_{\rm ind}\) as above.
Use the computable rational lower bound
\[
 g_R=\frac{2^{-32769}}{(3R)^{48}}
          < c_R=\frac1{2\exp(16384)(\log(3R))^{48}},            \tag{12}
\]
since \(e<4\) and \(\log(3R)<3R\).
Set
\[
 u=\left(\frac{g_R}{128(1+C_*+C_{\rm ind})}\right)^{s-1},
                  \qquad \xi=g_R/128,                         \tag{13}
\]
and compute \(H,T_H\) from (9).
Only now choose a positive rational \(\rho\le1\), and compute \(Q_{\rm clip}\) from (10).
Let \(J_{\rm mar}\ge4\) be any prescribed integer margin, and put
\[
 e=\min\left\{\frac1{16C_*},\frac{g_R}{128(C_*+Q_{\rm clip})},
       \frac1{2D_*},\frac{\gamma}{16},\frac{\rho}{4J_{\rm mar}},\frac1{100}\right\},
 \qquad \tau=\epsilon_{\rm loc}=e/16.                          \tag{14}
\]
These choices have no circular dependence: the Fourier cutoff precedes \(\rho\) and the final slow-width allowance.

For the ordered compact center polytope
\[
             \Delta_k=\{x\in\mathbb R^k:0=x_1\le\cdots\le x_k=1\},
\]
the open certificate region consists of points satisfying, with \(\beta=\mathsf Bx\),
\[
\begin{split}
 \|x-\mathsf A\mathsf Bx\|_\infty&<\tau,\qquad |a_i\cdot\beta|<2,\\
 |(a_i-a_j)\cdot\beta|&>2\gamma\quad(i\ne j),\\
 |h\cdot\beta|&>\rho\quad(0\ne h\in\mathbb Z^r,\ \|h\|_\infty\le H).
\end{split}                                                   \tag{15}
\]
Repeated rows of \(\mathsf A\) occur only in its projection condition and are merged in the other lines.
This is a finite Boolean combination of strict rational linear inequalities in \(x\).

Let \(A_1=1+\max_i\|a_i\|_1\), and take the integer threshold
\[
 N_0=\left\lceil\max\left(1,\frac{2A_1}{\gamma},
                      \frac{2rH}{\rho},\frac{16(A_1+1)}e\right)\right\rceil.
                                                                    \tag{16}
\]
For any \(x\) satisfying (15) and \(N\ge N_0\), round each coordinate of \(N\beta\) to an integer to obtain \(q\).
Then \(|a_i\cdot q/N|\le3\), the actual frequencies are separated by at least \(\gamma\), and every nonzero Fourier test in (15) has \(|h\cdot q|\ge\rho N/2\).
The ordering of the actual fast frequencies is the ordering of \(a_i\cdot\beta\).

For any nonempty subset of these distinct rows with at least two occupied rows, apply (11) to its primitive quotient, provided one common integer shift puts all its slow coefficients in \([-d,d]\) and \(d/N\le e\).
The constants computed from the full distinct list remain valid when rows are absent.
Equations (13)–(14) imply
\[
                         \mu\ge\frac{15}{16}I-\frac{g_R}{32}.  \tag{17}
\]
Thus no prior bound on \(I\) has been assumed or needed.

Extend the primitive vector \(\ell\) to a unimodular change of the fast Laurent coordinates.
The \(T\)-exponents corresponding to \(\ell\) are distinct, their total width is at most \(R\), and the top occupied coefficient is one slow row times a Laurent monomial in the other fast coordinates.
A \(T\)-independent nonunit factor must therefore be \(X\)-only up to a Laurent unit, by considering this top coefficient in the Laurent UFD.
It would divide every occupied slow row, contradicting the primitive quotient condition, also over the algebraic closure.
Every nontorsion geometric component consequently triggers \(I\ge c_R\) in the [partial-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement).
In that case (17) gives \(\mu>c_R/2\).

Otherwise the [horizontal torsion factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) expresses the primitive polynomial as a Laurent unit times factors
\(\Phi_h(X^bY^v)\), with \(v\ne0\).
The possible vectors belong to the explicit finite list
\[
                     0\ne v\in\prod_{j=1}^r[-W_j,W_j]\cap\mathbb Z^r.
                                                                    \tag{18}
\]
Indeed Newton widths add under multiplication, so
\(\varphi(h)|v_j|\le W_j\) and \(\varphi(h)|b|\le2d\).
The cutoff in (9) contains this list.
Equations (14)–(16) give
\[
                       |v\cdot q|\ge\rho N/2>J_{\rm mar}d.    \tag{19}
\]
Since \(|b|\le2d\), every specialized character exponent \(b+v\cdot q\) is nonzero.
Each factor specializes to a cyclotomic product up to a Laurent unit and sign, and \(\mu=0\).
In particular \(\mu<c_R/2\) forces exactly this torsion alternative.
The primitive single-row case is a Laurent unit and is handled without (11) or the partial-degree theorem.

For rows before common-factor removal, their quotient slow width cannot increase: minima and maxima add in a Laurent product, over the integral coefficient domain.
One common integer shift therefore puts the quotient rows in \([-d,d]\) whenever their total union of exponents has width at most \(2d\).
The common factor contributes its own nonnegative Mahler measure to the diagonal.
This explains the scope for reducible inputs; the certificate does not assign a gap to an unrestricted common factor.

## Enumerating and verifying a finite cover

For fixed \(k\ge2\), enumerate all matrices \(\mathsf A\) allowed above, and all dyadic \(\gamma\in(0,1/4]\), \(\rho\in(0,1]\), in finite stages of increasing height and denominator exponent.
Fix \(J_{\rm mar}=16\), or any other specified integer at least four.
Every region (15), its \(\epsilon_{\rm loc}\), its positive rational gap \(g_R/2\), and its threshold \(N_0\), are computed by (2)–(16).
No entry in this enumeration is an unknown analytic constant.

After each stage test whether the finite union of the regions covers \(\Delta_k\).
This test is decidable in rational arithmetic.
Intersect the complement with \(\Delta_k\), distribute its finitely many Boolean alternatives, and test feasibility of the resulting rational linear systems.
Complements of strict inequalities are nonstrict, so ordinary rational linear feasibility also detects boundary points correctly.
Equivalently one may use Fourier–Motzkin elimination for these finite systems.
Stop at the first stage whose complement is empty.

This search terminates.
For a fixed real point \(x\in\Delta_k\), the additive group generated by its coordinates has a finite \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_r\) with rationally independent entries.
Write \(x=\mathsf A\beta\) in that basis.
The matrix has full column rank, its first row is zero and last row is nonzero, and \(\mathsf Bx=\beta\).
Equal coordinates give equal rows, while distinct rows have distinct real frequencies in \([0,1]\).
Choose a sufficiently small dyadic \(\gamma\), then compute the finite set in (9).
Rational independence supplies a positive dyadic \(\rho\) smaller than the absolute value of every nonzero dot product in that finite set.
The projection error is zero, so (15) holds for this certificate, regardless of how small its positive \(\tau\) becomes.
Thus the enumerated open regions cover every point of \(\Delta_k\).
Compactness supplies a finite subcover, all of whose data eventually appear in the enumeration.
The finite-cover test must therefore halt.

This argument does not determine the relation lattice of an input real point.
Hermite or Smith normal forms may remove duplicate integer subspaces in the enumeration, but they are not required for termination and do not supply a real equality oracle.
Higher-order resonances at a point inside a certificate are allowed: only the finite Fourier and character tests in (15) are used in its proof.

<a id="application"></a>
## Applying the cover to integer supports and closing small degrees

From the finite cover take
\[
 \epsilon=\min\epsilon_{\rm loc},\qquad
 \eta_{\rm large}=\min(g_R/2),\qquad D=\max N_0.
\]
All three numbers are computable and positive.
Suppose \(N\ge D\) and the support of \(P\) lies in \(k\) intervals of length at most \(\epsilon N\).
Choose one actual support point in each nonempty interval, choosing zero and \(N\) in their endpoint intervals, and use repetitions if fewer than \(k\) are needed.
The two endpoint intervals are distinct because \(\epsilon<1\).
After ordering, the normalized representatives form a point \(x\in\Delta_k\).
Every support exponent is within \(\epsilon N\) of an appropriate representative.

Choose a certificate covering \(x\), and form its rounded vector \(q\).
Assign each support term to one representative interval and merge terms whose representatives have the same matrix row.
The endpoints are assigned to their endpoint representatives.
The resulting slow exponents differ from the corresponding \(a_i\cdot q\) by at most
\[
                            (\epsilon+\tau)N+A_1.
\]
With \(d\) the ceiling of this bound, (14) and (16) give \(d/N<e\).
The first and last representatives correspond to different matrix rows, because their projection errors are less than \(\tau<1/100\).
Thus at least two model rows are occupied, and their specialized bands remain disjoint.

Remove the integral gcd \(h\) of these slow rows and recenter the quotient as in the local certificate.
Its diagonal has at least two separated nonzero terms and is a nonunit.
Irreducibility of \(P\) forces \(h\) to be a Laurent unit.
The certificate gives either \(m(P)=0\) or \(m(P)\ge g_R/2\); the former is excluded by hypothesis.
This proves the large-degree assertion.

For the remaining degrees we use an explicit elementary bound:
\[
 \deg P\le D,\quad P\in\mathbb Z[X],\quad m(P)>0
                  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                      m(P)\ge \frac18\,5^{1-D^2}.              \tag{20}
\]
If \(M(P)\ge2\) the bound is immediate.
Otherwise the leading coefficient has modulus one, all roots have modulus less than two, and some root \(\alpha\) has modulus greater than one.
The number \(\beta=\alpha\overline\alpha>1\) is an algebraic integer of degree at most \(D^2\); every conjugate is a product of two roots of \(P\), hence has modulus less than four.
Its minimal polynomial has a nonzero integer value at one, so \(\beta-1\ge5^{1-D^2}\).
Since \(\beta<4\),
\(m(P)\ge\tfrac12\log\beta\ge(\beta-1)/8\), proving (20), also for reducible inputs.
Set
\[
              \epsilon_k=\epsilon,\qquad
              \eta_k=\min\left(\eta_{\rm large},\tfrac18 5^{1-D^2}\right).
\]
For \(k=1\), take \(\epsilon_1=1/2\) and \(\eta_1=1/2\): the support contains both zero and \(N\), so the asserted interval condition is impossible.
This completes the algorithm and its proof.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Computational and mathematical scope

No numerical run of the finite-cover algorithm is claimed.
Its matrix enumeration, rational constants and Boolean linear-feasibility tests are explicit terminating procedures, without a practical complexity bound.
The conclusion is uniform over every placement of a fixed number of intervals; it does not bound the number of intervals needed for arbitrary inputs.
For reducible inputs the local certificate remains useful after removing common slow content, but that factor can retain an unrestricted Mahler measure.
This result neither proves a uniform gap for all integer polynomials nor constructs a sequence whose positive measure tends to zero.
