# How many leading coefficients a Salem descent can preserve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The size of the largest possible decrease

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma=e^a\), of degree \(d\), with \(0<a\le A\) for a fixed \(A\).
Let \(Q\) be any monic real polynomial of degree \(d\) agreeing with \(P\) in the first \(k\) coefficients below the leading one.
Write \(n\) for the largest odd integer at most \(k\).
For any sequence with
\[
                 na-\log(2d)\longrightarrow+\infty,                 \tag{1}
\]
if \(M(Q)\le\sigma\), then \(Q\) has a simple positive real root \(\tau>1\), uniquely largest in modulus, with
\[
 0\le a-\log\tau\le(2+o(1))\,\frac{d}{n}e^{-na},\qquad
 0\le\log M(Q)-\log\tau\le a-\log\tau.                             \tag{2}
\]
The constants are uniform over all remaining coefficients, and repeated roots elsewhere are allowed.
This controls the full polynomial whenever it decreases the measure and in particular bounds the improvement in the full measure itself.

If \(Q\) is integral, all its irreducible factors apart from the minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) are cyclotomic or \(z\) for all sufficiently late inputs with \(d e^{-na}/n=o((\log(2d))^{-3})\) under (1).
If also \(a\to0\), \(k\ge\epsilon d\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\), and the trace splitting group of the minimal polynomial of \(\tau\) is solvable, then \(\tau\) is Salem.
Thus an arbitrary change to the remaining coefficients giving a full descent in this regime has a single noncyclotomic unit factor, and solvability forces that factor to remain Salem.
The factor condition concerns a strict decrease of the full polynomial, and allows arbitrary complementary factors of measure one.

The coefficient scale in (1) is sharp up to an additive constant in \(ka-\log d\).
For every sufficiently large \(k\) there is an irreducible Salem polynomial of degree \(d_k\) with measure \(\sigma_k\to\theta\), the plastic number, and a monic reciprocal product of cyclotomic polynomials of the same degree agreeing in the first and last \(k\) coefficients below the ends.
They satisfy
\[
              k\log\sigma_k-\log d_k
                       \longrightarrow\log((\theta-1)/\theta).       \tag{3}
\]
These examples attain measure one with all \(k\) leading coefficients preserved.
Their source measures have a limit greater than one.

<a id="moments"></a>
## One odd power sum and a bound for the remaining roots

Newton's identities give equality of the two \(n\)-th power sums.
For \(P\) that sum differs from \(\sigma^n\) by at most \(d-1\).
For the roots \(\beta_i\) of \(Q\), counted with multiplicity, set \(u_i=n\log^+|\beta_i|\), with \(u_i=0\) at zero.
We have \(\sum u_i\le na\).
If every \(u_i\le na/2\), convexity on \([0,na/2]\) gives
\[
     \sum|\beta_i|^n\le d+\sum (e^{u_i}-1)\le d+2(e^{na/2}-1).
\]
Under (1) this is strictly smaller than \(\sigma^n-(d-1)\).
Consequently precisely one root has logarithmic modulus above \(a/2\), counted with multiplicity.
It is simple and real by complex conjugation.
The remaining roots have total positive logarithmic modulus less than \(a/2\).
For nonnegative \(v_i\) the inequality \(\sum(e^{v_i}-1)\le e^{\sum v_i}-1\) follows by multiplication.
The sum of the absolute \(n\)-th powers of the remaining roots is therefore at most \(d-2+e^{na/2}\).
The common power sum is larger than this bound, so oddness forces the exceptional real root \(\tau\) to be positive.

Subtract the contributions of the two positive roots.
Since \(\tau\le M(Q)\le\sigma\), we have
\[
       0\le1-(\tau/\sigma)^n\le e^{-na}(2d+e^{na/2})=o(1).
\]
It follows that \(n(a-\log\tau)=o(1)\).
Now the other roots of \(Q\) have total positive logarithmic modulus at most \(a-\log\tau\), so their absolute \(n\)-th powers sum to at most \(d-2+\exp(n(a-\log\tau))=d-1+o(1)\).
Comparing the power sums again gives \(1-(\tau/\sigma)^n\le (2d+o(1))e^{-na}\).
Taking the logarithm proves (2).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Integral factors and the solvable case

An irreducible noncyclotomic factor other than \(z\) has positive logarithmic measure by Kronecker's theorem.
For degrees at most \(d\) it has logarithmic measure \(\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\), by the [Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) and bounded-degree finiteness.
It suffices that the budget in (2) is smaller than this lower bound, as guaranteed by
\[
            d e^{-na}/n=o((\log(2d))^{-3}).                         \tag{4}
\]
In particular (4) follows if \(na\ge(1+\epsilon)\log(2d)\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
Then any such factor missing \(\tau\) contradicts (2), proving the integral factor assertion under (4).

For \(a\to0,\ k\ge\epsilon d\), the input degree bound gives \(a\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\).
Thus (1) and (4) hold, and (2) makes the total logarithmic contribution \(E\) outside \(\tau\) at most \(\exp(-(\epsilon+o(1))da)\) as an upper bound.
The irreducible monic factor \(F\) of \(\tau\) is a unit since \(M(F)<2\) and its constant coefficient is nonzero.
It is reciprocal by [Smyth's nonreciprocal gap](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-u002).
A second real exterior root of \(F\), if present, need not have a nonreal trace.
It too would be exponentially close to \(1\) or \(-1\) in absolute value.
For \(u=\pm1\) choose the nearer endpoint and apply the mean value theorem between \(u\) and the reciprocal root \(1/\gamma\) inside the disk.
Here \(|F(u)|\ge1\), and the polynomial derivative on the unit disk is bounded by \(d^2\|F\|_{\mathbb T}\) by Cauchy's coefficient formula.
The [circle supremum estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant), applied in the actual degree of \(F\) at bounded measure, gives \(\log\|F\|_{\mathbb T}\ll\sqrt d\log(2d)\).
This contradicts \(|u-1/\gamma|\le E\) since \(da\gg d/(\log(2d))^3\).

If \(F\) has any other exterior root, it must therefore have nonreal trace.
For solvable trace splitting group the [surplus bound for a reciprocal unit](Yin_C_G0345_v133.md#statement) bounds \(\log(1/E_F)\) by \(O(d^{2/3}\log(2d)+d^{3/4}(\log(2d))^{3/4})\), where \(E_F=\log M(F)-\log\tau>0\).
Since \(0<E_F\le E\) this also contradicts the preceding exponential upper bound.
The reciprocal quadratic exception has \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\) an integer at least three, impossible as \(a\to0\).
The remaining reciprocal irreducible signature is Salem.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## A reciprocal cyclotomic replacement at the boundary

Write \(f=z^3-z-1\), \(F(w)=w^3 f(1/w)=1-w^2-w^3\), and write \(\theta>1\) for the exterior root of the Pisot polynomial \(f\).
For each \(k\) there are integers \(c_j\), independent of \(k\), with
\[
       F(w)\equiv\prod_{j=1}^k(1-w^j)^{c_j}\pmod {w^{k+1}} .
\]
Indeed one chooses \(c_j\) in succession by the coefficient at \(w^j\), dividing by the earlier factors in the ring of integer power series.
Logarithmic differentiation and Möbius inversion give
\[
       j c_j=\sum_{e\mid j}\mu(j/e)\sum_{f(\alpha)=0}\alpha^e
              =\theta^j+O(j\theta^{j/2}+j).
\]
The error follows from \(|\alpha|<1\) for the two remaining roots and \(e\le j/2\) for proper divisors.
In particular only finitely many \(c_j\) are negative.
For those indices replace \((1-w^j)^{-1}\) by \((1-w^{j h})/(1-w^j)\), \(h=\lfloor k/j\rfloor+1\), preserving the displayed congruence.
The resulting integer polynomial has constant one and only roots of unity.
Reversing it gives a monic product \(U_k\) of cyclotomic polynomials of degree
\[
        D_k=\sum_{j\le k}j c_j+O(k)
              \sim\frac{\theta^{k+1}}{\theta-1}.
\]
Only the finitely many replacements affect the degree estimate.

Choose the smallest \(d_k\equiv4\bmod120\) exceeding \(D_k+k\).
The [positive completion](Yin_C_G0345_v129.md#progression) \(P_k=z^{d_k-3}f+f^*\) is irreducible Salem, with \(\sigma_k>\theta\) tending to \(\theta\).
Its reversed leading coefficient series agrees with \(F\) through order \(k\) since the other block starts at order \(d_k-3>k\).
Put \(h_k=d_k-D_k\), and take \(Q_k=U_k(z)(z^{h_k}+U_k(0))\).
The added factor is monic and cyclotomic up to factorization, with \(h_k>k\); it preserves those first \(k\) coefficients and makes the constant term one.
A monic product of cyclotomic polynomials with constant one is reciprocal since its roots are closed under inversion.
Thus the trailing coefficients agree as well.
Finally \(f(\sigma_k)=-\sigma_k^{-(d_k-3)} f^*(\sigma_k)\) and \(f'(\theta)>0\) give \(\sigma_k-\theta=O(\theta^{-(d_k-3)})\).
This proves (3).
