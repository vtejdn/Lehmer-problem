# Simultaneous visits to two exterior poles and the joint degree of their roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## An obstruction from ordered pairs of conjugates

Let \(q_j(X)=X^2-jX-1\), let \(\beta_j\) be its positive root for \(j=3,4\), and put \(A=q_3q_4\), \(A^*(X)=X^4A(1/X)\).
Take the [reciprocal quadratic curve](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#example)
\[
                   F(U,V)=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U).
\]
Let \(k,l\) be positive coprime integers with \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Choose a noncyclotomic rational irreducible factor \(S\) of \(F(X^k,X^l)\) at every index and write \(d=\deg S\).
Fix small disjoint disks about \(\beta_3,\beta_4\).
Let \(B_j\) be the exterior roots \(u\) of \(S\) for which \(u^k\) is in the disk about \(\beta_j\).
For \(r\in B_3,\ t\in B_4\), let \(\mathcal O(r,t)\) be the absolute Galois orbit of the ordered pair.
Uniformly over all these choices,
\[
       s\,\frac{\#\bigl(\mathcal O(r,t)\cap(B_3\times B_4)\bigr)}
                    {\#\mathcal O(r,t)}\longrightarrow 0 .                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \([\mathbb Q(r,t):\mathbb Q]/s\to\infty\) for any sequence of such pairs.

If the number \(H\) of exterior roots stays bounded, then
\[
                  [\mathbb Q(r,t):\mathbb Q(r)]\longrightarrow\infty .     \tag{2}
\]
If \(H=2\), the multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(S)\) of the monic factor satisfies
\[
                  \frac{[\mathbb Q(M(S)):\mathbb Q]}{d}
                        \longrightarrow\infty .                           \tag{3}
\]
These restrictions leave the case of growing joint degree open.

<a id="blowup"></a>
## A simultaneous approximation height for small points

Let \(K\) be a number field, and fix two algebraic units \(a,b\in K\) such that every infinite conjugate of \(A_0=(a,b)\) lies outside \((S^1)^2\).
Blow up \(A_0\) on \(X_0=(\mathbb P^1)^2\), writing \(\pi:X\to X_0\) for the resulting smooth projective surface and \(E\) for the exceptional divisor.
Use the canonical sup metrics on the two copies of \(\mathcal O(1)\) and let \(\bar L\) be the pullback of their exterior tensor product.
This is semipositive by pullback and geometrically big since \(L^2=2\) and \(L\) is nef.
Its height is \(h(x)+h(y)\) on a point above \((x,y)\).
Its absolute and essential minima are zero by nonnegativity and the Zariski density of the lifted pairs of roots of unity away from the center.

Equip \(E\) at the finite places with the model metric from the exceptional divisor on the blowup of \((\mathbb P^1_{\mathcal O_K})^2\) along the section \((a,b)\).
The section has coordinates in the integral torus at every prime, and the blowup is regular by the usual two coordinate charts.
The effective model divisor gives nonnegative Green functions away from \(E\).
For unit coordinates they have the exact value
\[
       g_{E,v}(x,y)=-\log\max\{|x-a|_v,|y-b|_v\}.                         \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the pulled-back ideal generated by \(x-a,y-b\) is principal along a valuation-ring point of the blowup, generated there by a difference of maximal absolute value.

At an infinite embedding choose a small polydisk about the conjugate center disjoint from \((S^1)^2\), of radius less than one, with affine coordinates on both factors.
Write \(R=\max(|x-a|,|y-b|)\) in those coordinates at this embedding.
Use \(g_{E,v}=-\chi\log R\), with a continuous cutoff \(\chi\) supported in the disk, between zero and one, identically one near the center, and set the function to zero off the disk.
Choose the cutoffs compatibly under complex conjugation.
In the blowup chart \(y-b=(x-a)t\) near \(E\), adding \(\log|x-a|\) gives the continuous function \(-\log\max(1,|t|)\).
The other chart gives the same extension property.
Thus these metrics define an adelic divisor \(\bar E\), with nonnegative local functions and vanishing archimedean contribution on the compact unit torus.

For every generic sequence \(P_i\) on \(X\) with \(h_{\bar L}(P_i)\to0\),
\[
                            h_{\bar E}(P_i)\to0 .                           \tag{5}
\]
To prove this, the [global limit-height formula](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#bs26-u001) first gives the same limit for all such sequences, because \(\bar L\) is semipositive, \(L\) big, and both minima agree.
It allows the arbitrary adelic final entry \(\bar E\).
We determine the limit by constructing a sequence of height zero for both metrics.

<a id="calibration"></a>
## Torsion pairs which avoid the center at every place

Take a growing prime \(p\) and choose a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta_p\ne a\).
In \(K(\zeta_p)\), only finitely many prime ideals divide the nonzero algebraic integer \(\zeta_p-a\).
For each of them let \(n_w\) be the finite multiplicative order of the residue of \(b\).
Choose a prime \(q>p\) different from every \(n_w\) and from each of these residue characteristics.
For any primitive \(q\)-th root \(\zeta_q\), its residue above every such prime has order \(q\).
Indeed a residue equal to one would give \(0=\Phi_q(1)=q\) in the residue field, and the residue order divides \(q\).
Thus \((\zeta_p,\zeta_q)\) reduces to the center at no finite place, so (4) vanishes everywhere there.
At infinity every conjugate lies on the compact unit torus, where the chosen Green function also vanishes.

These torsion pairs form a strict sequence: for any fixed nonzero character \((u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\), the equation \(\zeta_p^u\zeta_q^v=1\) requires \(p\mid u\) and \(q\mid v\), which fails eventually.
Every proper algebraic subgroup of the torus lies in the kernel of a nonzero character, allowing a multiple for a disconnected subgroup.
More generally every strict sequence of pairs with the sum of their coordinate heights tending to zero is generic in the torus.
Indeed the [strict small-point theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) gives Haar measure on \((S^1)^2\) for the absolute orbit measures of every subsequence.
If a proper algebraic set contained one subsequence, the finite union of its conjugates would contain the full orbits along that subsequence.
This is still a proper closed set, of Haar measure zero on \((S^1)^2\) by taking a nonzero Laurent polynomial vanishing on it and applying the one-variable zero bound and Fubini.
The asserted weak limit precludes this.
The lifts of generic pairs are generic on the blowup because every proper closed subset of this projective surface has image of dimension at most one.
The constructed torsion sequence therefore calibrates the common limit at zero, as claimed in (5).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Exponential proximity and full embedding multiplicities

Write \(x=r^k,y=t^k\) for any chosen pair.
All section roots are algebraic units for large indices, by monicity and the unit constant term.
Their [individual section heights](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) give \(h(x)+h(y)=O(1/s)\).
The [radial estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v776.md#statement) gives, uniformly for \(u\in B_j\),
\[
                       |u^k-\beta_j|\le C e^{-cs}                           \tag{6}
\]
with fixed positive \(C,c\), since the parent has unit-circle fibers and simple exterior roots of the top face.
Thus \(r,t\) have distinct moduli greater than one for large indices.
They are conjugate and have the same positive height.
A torsion relation \(r^u t^v\in\mu_\infty\) with \((u,v)\ne(0,0)\) would give \(|u|h(r)=|v|h(t)\), hence \(|u|=|v|\ne0\).
It would force either equal or reciprocal moduli.
Consequently \((x,y)\) has multiplicatively independent coordinates and these pairs form a strict, hence generic, small sequence.

Take a fixed normal number field \(K\) containing \(\beta_3,\beta_4\).
For every point \(A_0\) in the absolute orbit of \((\beta_3,\beta_4)\), apply (5) on its blowup to the lift of \((x,y)\).
All coordinates of these centers are units and all their conjugates are off the circle.
Each of these finitely many heights tends to zero.
Compute their sum over \(K\mathbb Q(r,t)\) with the usual embedding multiplicities.
All the finite local contributions are nonnegative by (4).
For any embedding, the images of the entire set of centers form that same set.
When the embedding of \((r,t)\) is a pair in \(B_3\times B_4\), one image center is therefore exactly \((\beta_3,\beta_4)\).
For large indices the corresponding infinite Green function equals \(-\log R\) at the image pair \((r^k,t^k)\), since (6) puts it in the region where the cutoff equals one.
The sum of the infinite Green functions at that embedding is at least \(cs-\log C\), and is nonnegative at every other embedding.
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(r,t)\) has the same number of extensions to the compositum.
The sum of the global heights therefore bounds from below
\[
        (cs-\log C)
        \frac{\#(\mathcal O(r,t)\cap(B_3\times B_4))}
             {[\mathbb Q(r,t):\mathbb Q]} .
\]
Since the height sum tends to zero and the denominator is the full ordered-pair orbit size, this proves (1).
The estimate is uniform because a failing subsequence with selected pairs would itself satisfy the same strictness and height hypotheses.
The numerator counts at least the original pair.
Hence the joint degree divided by \(s\) tends to infinity.
For bounded \(H\) the [exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#statement) gives \(d/s=H+o(1)\), yielding (2).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Degree of the two-exterior Mahler measure

When \(H=2\), the exterior allocation gives one root at each pole and both roots \(r,t\) are real.
Set \(\delta=M(S)=|rt|=\pm rt\), with a fixed rational sign at each index.
An embedding of the joint root field sends the two roots to distinct conjugates of \(S\).
The modulus of the image of \(\delta\) is strictly less than \(\delta\) unless the image pair is \((r,t)\) or \((t,r)\), since the other roots have modulus at most one.
Any embedding fixing \(\delta\) thus has one of these two ordered pairs as its image.
It follows that \([\mathbb Q(r,t):\mathbb Q(\delta)]\le2\).
Combining this estimate with (2) proves (3).
The same reasoning gives \(\mathbb Q(\delta^n)=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) for every integer \(n\ge1\), because an embedding fixing \(\delta^n\) preserves the maximum modulus, and both possible orders of the exterior roots give the same value \(\delta\).
