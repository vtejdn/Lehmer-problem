# A uniform bound on radical indices at fixed delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## All integer seeds and both signs

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Let \(r\) be the exterior Salem root of \(X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), let \(d=[\mathbb Q(r):\mathbb Q]\), and suppose that \(\beta=r^{1/q}\in\mathbb Q(r)\) for a positive integer \(q\).
Put \(t=\log\beta\).
With absolute effective constants one has
\[
 q\ll d^2\log^5(3d),\qquad
 \log a\ll d^2\log^2(3d)\max(1,t).
\]
In particular, every irreducible factor of \(P(X^h)\), where \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of \(r\) and \(h\ge1\), has logarithmic Mahler measure at least
\[
 \frac{\log(a-1)}{C n^2\log^5(3n)}.
\]
If \(\log a>C' n^2\log^2(3n)\), the same lower bound holds with \(\log^2(3n)\) in the denominator, after adjustment of the absolute constant.
Thus the least factor measure tends to infinity with the seed at every fixed delay, uniformly over the substitutions and the two signs.

Write \(R\) for the greatest such \(q\), and set \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}((a+j)/\operatorname{rad}(a+j))\).
Every failure of \(R\mid H(a)\) satisfies
\[
                         \log a\ll n^2\log^3(3n).
\]
At each fixed delay there are only effectively finitely many seeds with such a failure.

<a id="height"></a>
## A unit-circle conjugate and a linear form in two logarithms

The [root classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(a-1<r<a+1\) and \(n-1\le d\le n+1\).
The [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution) gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=\mathbb Q(r)\) and shows that \(\beta\) is Salem.
Choose one of its nonreal unit-circle conjugates \(w\).
It has degree \(d\) and absolute logarithmic height \(t/d\).
The conjugate \(z=w^q\) of \(r\) satisfies
\[
 a|z^{n-1}-\epsilon|=|z^{n+1}-\epsilon|\le2.
\]
Consequently \(0<|1-w^{2q(n-1)}|\le4/a\), where strict positivity follows since a conjugate of the real number \(\beta>1\) cannot be a root of unity.
Apply the [Baker–Wüstholz estimate on the circle](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) to \(w\) and the exponent \(2q(n-1)\).
Its two modified heights are bounded by \(\max(t,\pi)/d\) and \(\pi/d\), and \(C(2,d)\ll d^4\log(4d)\).
It follows that
\[
             \log(a/8)\le C d^2\log(4d)\max(1,t)\log(4qn).
\]

<a id="inversion"></a>
## Eliminating the seed from the exponent bound

Since \(qt=\log r\le\log(a+1)\) and \(a\ge3\), the preceding inequality gives
\[
 q\le C_1 d^2\log(4d)\max(1,1/t)\log(4qn).
\]
The [explicit height bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(t>2/\log^3(3d)\).
Hence \(q/\log(4qn)\le C_2 d^2\log^4(3d)\).
The left side increases for \(q\ge1\).
At \(q_0=C_3 d^2\log^5(3d)\) the denominator is at most \((C_4+\log C_3)\log(3d)\), since \(n\le d+1\).
Choosing an effective absolute \(C_3\) large enough gives \(q\le q_0\).
Substitution now yields the asserted bound on \(\log a\), and also \(q\ll d^2\log^2(3d)\max(1,1/t)\).

By the [factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) every logarithmic factor measure is at least \(\log r/R\).
Apply the two bounds with \(q=R\).
Above the stated seed threshold, the bound on \(\log a\) gives \(t>1\) and hence \(R\ll d^2\log^2(3d)\).
These estimates give both factor bounds.

<a id="exceptions"></a>
## A finite range for exceptional seeds

A [failure of the divisor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#statement) requires a prime \(p>n\) for which \(r^{1/p}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
By the [torsion localization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#statement), \(r^{1/p}<5p\).
Apply the bounds just proved with \(q=p\).
They give \(\log(5p)\ll\log(3d)\) and then \(\log a\ll d^2\log^3(3d)\), proving the effective finite range.
