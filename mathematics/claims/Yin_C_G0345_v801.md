# Exterior rank and Mahler iteration on steep sections at all growing signatures

<a id="statement"></a>
## The product of the full exterior set

Let \(S\) be any monic rational irreducible factor of the [quadratic section](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) \(P_{k,l}=F(X^k,X^l)\), with \(F=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\), \(A=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1)\) and \(A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U)\).
Assume the positive exponents are coprime, \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Put \(d=\deg S\), let \(H\) count its exterior roots, and let \(R\) be the multiplicative rank of those roots modulo torsion.
Write \(\delta=M(S)\) for their product in absolute value, \(h\) for absolute logarithmic height, \(m(\delta)=[\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]h(\delta)\) and \(D_{\rm ab}=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\delta):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).

For the minimal label-block size \(b\) in the [alternating action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement), uniformly over these factors,
\[
 b\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212},\qquad
             0\le H-R=o(H/b),\qquad \frac{\log D_{\rm ab}}{H}\to\infty.    \tag{1}
\]
In particular all exterior roots of each individual factor are multiplicatively independent whenever \(s\ge c(\log(3d))^{212}\) for a fixed \(c>0\).
If \(\log(d/H)/\log\log(3d)\to\infty\), then
\[
        \log D_{\rm ab}\sim H\log(d/H)
                 \sim \log[\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q].                  \tag{2}
\]
These degree statements also hold for every positive power of \(\delta\), with exactly the same fields.

Whenever \(H\to\infty\), one has
\[
 l\,h(\delta)\to\infty,\qquad
 \frac{\log D_{\rm ab}}{\log d}\to\infty,\qquad
 \frac{\log m(\delta)}{\log d}\to\infty .                               \tag{3}
\]
This includes growing exterior counts \(H=o(k)\) at all steep primitive directions.
Without any hypothesis on \(H\), the log measure of the multiplicative Mahler measure satisfies
\[
                  \liminf \frac{\log m(M(S))}{\log d}\ \ge\ 1.           \tag{4}
\]
Thus even when the original factor has log measure tending to zero, its first Mahler iterate has diverging log measure.
In particular its multiplicative measure in that case cannot be a Pisot or Salem number infinitely often.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Counting exterior labels in the alternating blocks

Every \(S\) is a unit polynomial without torsion roots, and its degree tends to infinity by the [pole allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth), which gives \(d/s\sim H\).
Here \(H\ge1\) by Kronecker's theorem.
The log measure of \(S\) is uniformly bounded by that of the monic full section, which tends to \(C=\log(\beta_3\beta_4)>0\) by the [complete phase average](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#measure), with \(\beta_j\) the positive root of \(U^2-jU-1\).
The [torsion-quotient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) gives singleton torsion-quotient classes in the whole section, as its projected support differences have gcd one by [the primitive support calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v796.md#pairs).
Since the entire section is reciprocal, roots with torsion product must be exact inverses.
Consequently the multiplicative labels for \(S\) are the intersections of its roots with the unordered inverse pairs, each of the same size \(w\in\{1,2\}\).
Every exterior root occupies a different label.

The bounded-measure alternating theorem now applies to the original degree \(d\) with \(d/w\) labels.
There are \(u\) blocks of size \(b\) with \(b\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), and the group induced on them contains \((A_b)^u\) acting independently inside the blocks.
Write \(n_j\) for the number of occupied labels in block \(j\), so \(\sum n_j=H\).
The invariant ordered relation on the roots lying over different labels of the same block has \(dw(b-1)\) entries.
An orbit meeting two exterior roots has exterior-pair fraction \(o(1/s)\), uniformly by [exterior-pair incidence](Yin_C_G0345_v796.md#pairs).
Its other orbits contribute no exterior pairs.
Counting all the exterior entries gives
\[
              \sum_j n_j(n_j-1)=o\bigl(dw(b-1)/s\bigr)=o(Hb).             \tag{5}
\]
This works also for nonreciprocal factors with \(w=1\).
If \(f\) blocks are wholly occupied, then \(fb(b-1)\le\sum_j n_j(n_j-1)\), giving \(f=o(H/b)\).
Delete one exterior root from each such block.
The remaining exterior roots omit at least one label in every block and hence are multiplicatively independent by the [alternating rank theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#rank).
Thus \(H-R\le f\), and \(H/b\ll (\log(3d))^{212}/s\) gives the full-rank consequence by integrality.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The orbit of the occupied labels over the abelian field

The factor splitting group over the maximal abelian field induces its rational splitting group's commutator image on labels.
Indeed the intersection of its finite splitting field with \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is its largest abelian subextension.
The commutator image contains \((A_b)^u\) because this subgroup of the label image is perfect for large \(b\).
In the full splitting field the stabilizer of \(\delta\) is exactly the setwise stabilizer of the exterior roots.
Their product is real, and any other subset of conjugates of the same size has strictly smaller product modulus.
The same test gives the same stabilizer for every positive power, both over the rationals and after abelian base change.

Each \(A_b\) is transitive on subsets of \(n_j\) of its letters, including the empty set and the whole block.
Indeed an odd permutation carrying one proper subset to another can be corrected by a transposition preserving its image subset for \(b\ge3\).
The orbit of the exterior set projects onto the translated occupied label sets, while over \(\mathbb Q\) there are at most \(\binom dH\) subsets of conjugates.
Consequently,
\[
       \prod_j\binom b{n_j}\ \le\ D_{\rm ab}\ \le\
       [\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]\ \le\binom dH .                     \tag{6}
\]
Using \(\binom bn\ge (b/n)^n\) for \(n>0\) and Jensen's inequality for \(-\log\) with weights \(n_j/H\) gives
\[
 \frac{\log D_{\rm ab}}H\ge
 \sum_{n_j>0}\frac{n_j}H\log\frac b{n_j}
       \ge \log\frac{Hb}{\sum_j n_j^2}.
\]
By (5), \(\sum n_j^2=H+o(Hb)=o(Hb)\), proving (1).
The first lower bound also gives \(\log D_{\rm ab}\ge H\log(b/H)\), while the log of the upper bound in (6) is at most \(H\log(ed/H)\).
The size of \(b\) gives (2) by comparison.
If \(H\to\infty\), on indices with \(H\le\sqrt d\) one has \(\log(b/H)\ge(1/2-o(1))\log d\).
On indices with \(H>\sqrt d\), use \(\log D_{\rm ab}/H\to\infty\) instead.
Both give the middle conclusion of (3).

<a id="iterate"></a>
## Moving occupied labels to empty ones

Fix an integer \(q\ge1\).
By (5) the blocks with \(n_j>b/3\) carry only \(o(H)\) exterior labels, since each such label contributes at least \(b/3-1\) to the sum there.
If \(H\to\infty\), choose \(q\) occupied labels in the remaining blocks.
For \(q=1\) this choice is possible eventually for every sequence, even at bounded \(H\).
In each chosen block assign two different empty labels to each chosen label, without reuse, since \(b-n_j\ge2n_j\).
The product of the three-cycles on these disjoint triples lies in \((A_b)^u\).
Choose an automorphism \(g\) of the rational splitting field lifting it.
The square \(g^2\) fixes every root over a label outside the triples, since it squares the permutation of a fiber of size at most two, and sends each chosen exterior root to a distinct empty label.
Therefore \(\delta/g^2(\delta)\) is a monomial with powers \(1,-1\) on exactly \(2q\) distinct inverse pairs.
The [fixed-support height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives
\[
        2l\,h(\delta)\ \ge\ l\,h(\delta/g^2(\delta))\ \longrightarrow qC .
\]
This holds for every fixed \(q\) when \(H\to\infty\), proving \(l\,h(\delta)\to\infty\).
Taking \(q=1\) always gives \(h(\delta)\gg1/l\).
The same fixed-support degree formula with one root yields \(\log d\sim\log l\).
The middle conclusion of (3) now gives its last assertion by \(m(\delta)\ge D_{\rm ab}h(\delta)\).

Along any constant-\(H\) subsequence the [two-pole allocation at bounded counts](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#statement) requires \(H\ge2\), and the fixed-support law gives \(\log m(\delta)/\log d\to H-1\).
An arbitrary sequence either has a constant-\(H\) subsequence or admits a further subsequence with \(H\to\infty\) when testing the liminf.
This proves (4).
If \(m(S)\to0\), then \(\delta\to1\), whereas a Pisot or Salem number \(\delta\) has log measure \(\log\delta\).
