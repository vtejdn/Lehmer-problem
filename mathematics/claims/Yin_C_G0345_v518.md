# Interpolating two rows with prescribed prime support outside the exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## The reduced resultant of the powered nodes bounds the original row ideal

Fix a monic reciprocal minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion unit, of degree \(e\) and logarithmic measure \(h\).
Let \(S\) be a nonempty finite set of primes preserving its degree under powering, put \(N=e|S|,\ s=\sum_{p\in S}p,\ J=\prod_{p\in S}p\), and let \(Q\) be the monic product over the nodes \(z=\alpha_i^p\), where the \(\alpha_i\) are the roots of \(P\).
These nodes are distinct and \(Q(0)=1\) by the [common interpolation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction).
Let \(E\) be the positive generator of \((Q,Q')\cap\mathbb Z\).
There are explicit coprime integer rows \(U,V\) with nonzero reflected difference \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) and \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) for every \(p\in S\).
Their original ideal has positive integer generator \(B\) after contraction to \(\mathbb Z\), and
\[
 B\mid J E^8,\qquad
 \operatorname{rad}(B)=\operatorname{rad}(J\,|\operatorname{disc}Q|).
\]
Writing \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), they satisfy
\[
 \max(\deg U,\deg V)\le4N+1,\qquad
 \log H\le 4hs+4N\log 2+\log(7JN^4).
\]
Thus at widths \(b/|S|\to\infty\) and \(\min S\to\infty\), the assumption of zero Lehmer infimum gives same-seed pairs of vanishing normalized cost with this support prescription, by first fixing arbitrarily small \(h\) and then taking the intervals sufficiently far out.
This upper divisibility applies to the constructed pairs, while the [dyadic lower divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#statement) applies to every qualifying pair in its stated regime.
For every fixed seed with \(4h<\log 2\), take all primes in \([L,2L]\) and let \(L\to\infty\).
All sufficiently large primes preserve the seed degree, the width \(4e|S|+1\) fits the [full-interval width](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width), and the displayed cost is eventually below \(2^s\) by the fixed-seed interpolation estimate above.
The [lower bound after deletions](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#remove) gives \(\log B_{\mathcal A}/s\to\infty\) even after removing all primes through \(L\sqrt{\log L}\) and the additional primes allowed there.
All prime divisors of \(J\) are removed, so \(B\mid JE^8\) gives the same divergence for the logarithm divided by \(s\) of the part of \(E\) outside those deletions.
In particular this explicit construction already excludes a bound \(\log B=O(s)\) on that family at a fixed such seed.

<a id="rows"></a>
## A denominator supported at critical points

Set \(A_z=Q/(Y-z),\ D=Q',\ W=YD\), and form the integer polynomials
\[
 R=\sum_{z=\alpha_i^p}\alpha_i A_z,\qquad T=\sum_{z=\alpha_i^p}(J/p)\alpha_i A_z.
\]
Their coefficients are Galois-invariant algebraic integers.
Consider the rational function
\[
 F=\frac R D+Q\left(\frac{T}{JY D^2}-\frac{R'D-RD'}{D^3}\right).
\]
At a node, \(R(z)=\alpha_iD(z)\) and \(T(z)=(J/p)\alpha_iD(z)\).
Differentiating the correction gives \(F(z)=\alpha_i\) and \(F'(z)=\alpha_i/(pz)\), since the second correction cancels the derivative of \(R/D\).
Use the rows
\[
 K=-JYR D^2-QTD+JYQ(R'D-RD'),\quad
 V=JY^2D^4,\quad U=WK+Q^2(1+Y^{N+1}W).
\]
One has \(-WK/V=F\), and the remaining numerator term vanishes to second order at the nodes, where \(V(z)\ne0\).
The [tangency equations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) therefore give all asserted square divisibilities.
The constant term of \(U\) is one, and the last term has degree \(4N+1\), above both \(\deg(WK)\le4N-2\) and \(\deg V=4N-2\).
The outer coefficient in the reflected product of \(U\) survives.

Set \(A=\prod_z(1+|z|)\le 2^N\exp(hs)\).
Since \(|\alpha_i|\le 1+|z|\), the one-norms of \(Q,D,R,T\) are bounded by \(A,NA,NA,JNA\), respectively.
Differentiation costs at most a factor \(N\) for these four polynomials.
The three terms of \(K\) consequently have norms at most \(JN^3 A^3,JN^2A^3,2JN^3A^3\), respectively.
Submultiplicativity gives the asserted height bound for both rows.

<a id="ideal"></a>
## Contraction in the integer polynomial ring

Since \(Y\) is invertible modulo \(Q\), the ideals \((Q,D)\) and \((Q,W)\) coincide.
Thus \(E^2\in(Q^2,W)=(U,W)\), and taking fourth powers gives \(E^8\in(U,W^4)\).
Now \(JW^4=Y^2V\), so \(JE^8\in(U,V)\), proving rational coprimality and the stated divisibility in the original integer ring.
The Sylvester identity gives \(E\mid |\operatorname{disc}Q|\).
Conversely, if \(\ell\mid\operatorname{disc}Q\), there is a common zero of \(Q,D\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), and the displayed rows both vanish there.
Reducing any identity for \(B\) gives \(\ell\mid B\).
At a prime dividing \(J\), the whole denominator vanishes and the numerator remains nonconstant modulo that prime.
Indeed if \(W\) reduces to zero the numerator reduces to \(Q^2\), and otherwise \(Y^{N+1}WQ^2\) has degree \(3N+1+\deg\overline W\), larger than either remaining term.
A zero of that numerator over the algebraic closure now gives \(\ell\mid B\) also at these primes.
