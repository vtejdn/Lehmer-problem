# Energy outside intervals at the degree divided by the logarithmic measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds on the scale of the degree

Consider hyperbolic algebraic units of degrees \(d\), with minimal polynomials \(P\) and \(m=\log M(P)\to0\).
Assume that all positive powers preserve degree, as happens at [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
Let \(A\) be an integral Laurent polynomial indivisible by \(P\), with circle coefficients \(A/P=\sum_j h_jX^j\) and bounded scaled energy \(m\sum_j |h_j|^2\le C\).
Let \(I\) be covered by \(s\) finite integer intervals with sum of cardinalities \(W\).
If \(m(W/d+s)\to0\), then
\[
             \liminf m\sum_{j\notin I}|h_j|^2\ \ge\ \frac1{2\mathrm e}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
For numerators of width below \(d\) the lower bound is \(1/4\).
If \(m(W/d+s)\) is merely bounded, the lower limit is still positive, with a bound depending only on its upper bound and \(C\).
The unrestricted conclusions include coefficient distances to the nearest integers whenever their scaled energy is bounded, by [integer rounding](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
In particular one moving interval may have length \(o(d/m)\) in (1), or \(O(d/m)\) for the positive lower limit.
This extends the [separation of individual progression vectors](Yin_C_G0345_v665.md#separation) by using up to \(d\) rationally independent translated vectors.

<a id="translated"></a>
## Independent translates at a powered input

Fix any real \(t>0\) and set \(k=\lfloor t/m\rfloor\), \(t_k=km\) and \(q=\lfloor d/k\rfloor\).
We have \(dm\to\infty\) by the [degree-dependent Mahler estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), so \(k,q\to\infty\) and \(N=kq\sim d\).
Indeed an integer unit of bounded degree and bounded measure has bounded polynomial coefficients, and Voutier's bound at growing degrees gives \(dm\to\infty\).
Write \(P_k\) for the minimum of \(\alpha^k\), where \(P(\alpha)=0\), so \(\deg P_k=d\) and \(\log M(P_k)=t_k\).
Let \(B_r\) be the integral sections in the [power decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#sections), for \(0\le r<k\).
The Fourier coefficient sequence of \(B_r/P_k\) is \((h_{kj+r})_{j\in\mathbb Z}\).
For \(0\le l<q\) form the \(N\) scaled vectors given by the circle coefficients of \(\sqrt{t_k}X^l B_r/P_k\).
Their mean squared norm is \(m\sum_j|h_j|^2\le C\).

At \(\beta=\alpha^k\), the [values of the section numerators](Yin_C_G0345_v665.md#separation) satisfy \(B_r(\beta)=u\alpha^{-r}\) with a common \(u\ne0\).
Thus all the \(X^l B_r\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed their values have exponents \(kl-r\) in a single interval of \(N\) consecutive distinct integers, and no nonzero polynomial of degree \(<d\) annihilates \(\alpha\).
In particular their integer coefficient matrix has rank \(N\) over the reals.

<a id="volume"></a>
## A determinant bound for the translated vectors

Write \(G\) for their real Gram matrix.
The [integral pivot estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v667.md#determinant) gives \((\det G)^{1/N}\ge t_k e^{-2t_k}\).
For clarity, a common monomial shift makes every numerator polynomial.
Eliminate the coefficient rows from the lowest exponents to produce distinct increasing pivot positions with leading coefficients \(a_i\), using row permutations and row additions of determinant of absolute value one.
The product of the \(a_i\) is, up to sign, the original nonzero integer minor on the pivot columns.
Orthogonalize in reverse pivot order.
Subtraction of later rows preserves the first coefficient of each row.
Every such polynomial \(T\) has \(\|T/P_k\|_2^2\ge |a_i|^2 e^{-2t_k}\), by reflection to an outer denominator with constant modulus \(e^{t_k}\) at zero and by the coefficient bound for its analytic quotient.
The product of these squared distances proves the estimate.

For short \(A\), translate its support into \([0,d-1]\) and translate the observation intervals with it.
Then every \(B_r\) has support in \([0,d-1]\) by the [section degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#width).
All \(X^l B_r\) have exponents in \([0,d+q-2]\).
At most \(q-1\) pivot positions can be below \(q-1\), and every other pivot row has width less than \(d\), including after reverse orthogonalization.
For those rows the [prescribed lowest coefficient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation) improves the factor \(e^{-2t_k}\) to \(c(t_k)=1/(2\sinh(2t_k))\).
Consequently
\[
 (\det G)^{1/N}\ge
 t_k c(t_k)\left(e^{-2t_k}/c(t_k)\right)^{(q-1)/N}.
\]
Since \(t_k\to t>0\) and \((q-1)/N\le1/k\), this gives a limiting lower bound \(t c(t)\).
For later use let \(\lambda(t)=t e^{-2t}\) in general and \(\lambda(t)=t c(t)\) for short \(A\).

<a id="outside"></a>
## Observation dimension and energy on its complement

For each interval of cardinality \(w\), the quotient indices \(\lfloor j/k\rfloor\) range over an integer interval of cardinality at most \(w/k+2\).
All \(q\) translates of that quotient interval lie in an interval of cardinality at most \(w/k+q+1\).
Their union across the \(s\) original intervals gives a common coordinate set of size \(n\le W/k+s(q+1)\).
Each vector restricted to its complement uses only original coefficients at positions outside \(I\).
Thus the mean squared norm on this complement is at most \(U=m\sum_{j\notin I}|h_j|^2\).

Pass to a subsequence realizing the lower limit of \(U\), and further so that \(n/N\to\rho\) whenever bounded.
If \(\rho<1\), the determinant gives
\[
     \liminf U\ \ge\
       (1-\rho)\lambda(t)
       \left(\frac{\rho\lambda(t)}{C}\right)^{\rho/(1-\rho)},          \tag{2}
\]
where the factor raised to the variable exponent is one at \(\rho=0\).
Indeed the projection of the \(N\) vectors to the observed coordinates has rank at most \(n\).
Choose \(N-n\) orthonormal directions in its kernel in the space of row coefficients, and complete them to an orthonormal basis.
On those \(N-n\) directions the sum of squared norms of the images is at most the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the map to the unobserved coordinates, which is at most \(NU\).
On the remaining \(n\) directions their sum is at most \(NC\).
Hadamard's inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give \((\det G)^{1/N}\le(C/\rho_N)^{\rho_N}(U/(1-\rho_N))^{1-\rho_N}\) with \(\rho_N=n/N<1\), omitting an empty group.
The determinant bound and passage to the limit yield (2).

Write \(D_0=\limsup m(W/d+s)\).
Since \(N\sim d\), \(q\to\infty\) and \(t_k\to t\), the observation dimension satisfies \(\limsup n/N\le D_0/t\) for finite \(D_0\).
When \(D_0=0\), (2) gives \(\liminf U\ge\lambda(t)\) for every fixed \(t>0\).
Taking \(t=1/2\) in the unrestricted case or then letting \(t\downarrow0\) in the short case proves (1) and its short counterpart.
When \(D_0>0\), take \(t>D_0\) and minimize the positive continuous right-hand side of (2) over \([0,D_0/t]\).
This proves the positive lower limit, including arbitrary separations of the intervals.
The argument supplies no bound on the total energy from above on a hypothetical shrinking family.
