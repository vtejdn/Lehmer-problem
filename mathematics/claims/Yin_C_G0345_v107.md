# Binomial exponents with finitely many densely occupied dyadic intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform gap with unrestricted occupancy in the exceptional intervals

Let \(b_n\in\mathbb Z\) have finite support on the positive integers, let
\[
       L(\theta)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\log|1-e^{in\theta}|,\qquad
       S=\sum_n |b_n|,
\]
and use normalized angular measure.
For fixed integers \(h\ge0,\ w\ge1\), suppose at most \(h\) of the intervals \([2^j,2^{j+1})\), \(j\ge0\), contain more than \(w\) occupied indices.
Then \(S\) is bounded in terms of \(h,w,\|L\|_1\).
This allows an arbitrary number of occupied intervals and an arbitrary number of distinct indices in each of the \(h\) exceptional intervals, in any order.

For a nonconstant polynomial \(A=\pm\prod_n(1-X^n)^{b_n}\) in this class, the full logarithmic measures \(m(A+aX^k)\) tend to infinity with \(S\), uniformly in \(k\ge0\) and complex \(|a|\ge1\).
If \(a\) is a nonzero integer, all positive measures in the class have a uniform gap depending only on \(h,w\).
Consequently, in any sequence of integer monomial shifts of cyclotomic products with \(0<m(A+aX^k)\to0\), for every fixed \(w\) the number of dyadic intervals with more than \(w\) occupied binomial exponents tends to infinity.
Equivalently, every fixed order statistic of the interval counts listed in decreasing order tends to infinity, with missing counts filled in by zeros.

<a id="lower"></a>
## A sparse initial segment below unrestricted higher frequencies

For positive integers \(s\), let \(V_s\) denote convolution with the [kernel \(2\mathcal F_{2s}-\mathcal F_s\)](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#filter).
Its multiplier equals one on \(|t|\le s\), vanishes on \(|t|\ge2s\), and lies in \([0,1]\).
Its convolution norm on \(L^1\) and \(L^4\) is at most three, since both Fejér kernels have integral one and are nonnegative.

Suppose the occupied indices strictly below a dyadic integer \(D\ge2\) have occupancy at most \(w\) in every dyadic interval.
Write \(M=\|L\|_1\), \(E=D/2\), \(B=(\sum_{n<D}b_n^2)^{1/2}\) and \(B_0=(\sum_{n\le E}b_n^2)^{1/2}\).
Put \(P=V_E L\).
Exponents \(n\ge D\) have no Fourier coefficients in the open interval \((-D,D)\), since their boundary logarithms have zero mean and are supported on the nonzero multiples of \(n\).
Thus \(P=V_E L_{<D}\), where \(L_{<D}\) denotes the sum over the indicated indices.
The [fourth-moment bound on the sparse support](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#blocks) gives
\[
                 \|P\|_1\le3M,\qquad \|P\|_4\ll \sqrt w\,B .
\]
The [inverse divisor matrix](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#inversion) recovering the coefficients \(b_n\) for \(n\le E\) uses only \(\widehat L(t)\) for \(1\le t\le E\).
These coefficients equal \(\widehat P(t)\).
The row sum estimate on the occupied indices \(n\le E\) and interpolation therefore give
\[
           B_0\ll \sqrt w\,\|P\|_2
                 \ll \sqrt w\,\|P\|_1^{1/3}\|P\|_4^{2/3}
                         \ll_w M^{1/3} B^{2/3}.
\]

For every \(E<n<D\), each proper divisor of \(n\) is at most \(E\).
The exact Fourier coefficient identity gives
\[
 b_n=-2\widehat L(n)-\sum_{\substack{j\mid n\\j<n}}\frac jn b_j,
 \qquad |b_n|\le 2M+B_0\Big(\sum_{r\ge2}r^{-2}\Big)^{1/2}.
\]
There are at most \(w\) occupied indices in this last interval, so \(B\ll_w M+B_0\).
Combining the bounds gives \(B\ll_w M\), since whenever the term proportional to \(M\) is smaller than \(B/2\) one can divide the remaining inequality by \(B^{2/3}\).
For \(M=0\) the divisor identity already gives every coefficient zero by induction.
It follows that the extracted segment has absolute exponent sum at most \(B^2\), by integrality, and boundary norm \(\|L_{<D}\|_1\ll_w B\), by its fourth-moment bound.
Higher indices and their number are unrestricted throughout this extraction.
When \(D=1\) the initial segment is empty.

<a id="upper"></a>
## A run of densely occupied intervals followed by a sparse interval

Now suppose the remaining support has no index below \(D=2^j\), and extract the indices in \([D,E)\), where \(E=2^\ell D\) and \(1\le\ell\le h\).
Assume that the interval \([E,2E)\) contains at most \(w\) remaining indices.
Write \(M=\|L\|_1\) for the current boundary norm and \(K=2E/D\).
The [finite inversion for the circle logarithms](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#filter) gives the cosine coefficient \(-b_t\) at every positive \(t\le2E\) of
\[
                       F(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^K \frac{\mu(j)}j L(j\theta).
\]
This uses only the lack of exponents below \(D\).
For such \(t\), all nonzero divisor terms in \(b_t=-2\sum_{r\mid t}\mu(r)\widehat L(t/r)/r\) have \(r\le K\), since \(\widehat L(t/r)=0\) for \(0<t/r<D\).
In particular every single guard coefficient in \([E,2E)\) has modulus at most \(2M\sum_{j=1}^K1/j\).

The trigonometric polynomial \(V_E F\) has \(L^1\) norm at most \(3M\sum_{j=1}^K1/j\).
The full coefficients from \([D,E)\) survive, and the contributions from \([E,2E)\) have total \(L^1\) norm at most \(2wM\sum_{j=1}^K1/j\), since each multiplier lies in \([0,1]\).
Subtracting the latter terms and doubling gives the integer-coefficient trigonometric polynomial \(2\sum_{D\le n<E} b_n\cos(n\theta)\), up to sign, with bounded \(L^1\) norm depending only on \(h,w,M\).
The [prime-grid coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#coefficients) bounds the sum of the absolute values of its Fourier coefficients, and hence \(\sum_{D\le n<E}|b_n|\), in the same parameters.
The extracted part of \(L\) has \(L^1\) norm at most this exponent sum times \(\|\log|1-e^{i\theta}|\|_1\).

<a id="iteration"></a>
## Alternating extractions and the shifted measure

Group consecutive exceptional dyadic intervals into maximal runs.
Before the first run, remove the entire sparse initial segment by the [estimate below the first dense interval](#lower).
Remove the first run by the [finite inversion](#upper), since the next interval is sparse or empty.
The two operations bound both the removed exponent mass and the boundary norm of the remainder.
Repeat the same steps for the remaining runs in increasing order.
There are at most \(h\) runs, each of length at most \(h\).
The final sparse tail has square norm of its coefficients bounded by the [global occupancy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#statement) applied to the remainder.
When \(h=0\) this last step covers the whole support.
This proves the asserted bound on \(S\).

If \(A\) is a polynomial then it has all its roots on the circle, its leading coefficient has modulus one and \(L=\log|A|\) on the circle.
The [constant-modulus shift bound](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives \(m(A+aX^k)\ge\|L\|_1/2-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\).
It gives divergence for fixed \(h,w\) when \(S\to\infty\).
With bounded measure, \(S\) stays bounded, so denominator clearing gives a nonzero integer polynomial with bounded term count and unchanged measure when \(a\) is an integer, by the [binomial clearing calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v106.md#mahler).
That calculation yields the positive gap.
If the number of intervals above some fixed count \(w\) failed to tend to infinity in a shrinking sequence, a bounded subsequence would violate this gap.
This also proves the order-statistic formulation.
