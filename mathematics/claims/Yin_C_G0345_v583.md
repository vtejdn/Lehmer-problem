# Exact dissociated power counts at multinacci bases and a sharp bound near two

<a id="statement"></a>
## The extremal count on the real line

For a nonzero real \(x\) and \(N\ge1\), let \(a_N(x)\) be the largest size of a subset of \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) on which all binary subset sums of the powers \(x^i\) are distinct.
Equivalently the selected positions support no nonzero polynomial vanishing at \(x\) with coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
Write \(D(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}a_N(x)/N\), which exists by subadditivity since admissibility is preserved on translating each restricted subset to the origin.
This agrees at algebraic units with the [multiplicity-one density](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement).
For \(d\ge2\) an integer let \(\beta_d\in(1,2)\) solve \(\sum_{i=1}^d\beta_d^{-i}=1\), the [multinacci Pisot unit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v29.md#multinacci) also identified in the [source account](../audits/literature/Smi11/Yin_U001.md#smi11-u001).
Then
\[
 \min_{x\in\mathbb R,\,|x|\ge\beta_d}a_N(x)=a_N(\beta_d)
       =N-\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{d}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad D(\beta_d)=1-1/d.
\]
At \(\beta_d\) the complete list of inclusion-minimal ternary relation supports, up to translation, is
\[
 S_\ell=\{0,1,\ldots, d(\ell+1)\}\setminus\{d,2d,\ldots,\ell d\},
 \qquad \ell=0,1,2,\ldots .
\]
Only \(S_0\) is terminal under [splitting at a single intermediate exponent](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#splitting).
The case \(d=2\) recovers the [golden-ratio classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v294.md#supports).
Over all real positive bases one has the sharp limiting envelope
\[
 \limsup_{x\to2^-} (1-D(x))\log\frac1{2-x}=\log2 .
\]

<a id="leading"></a>
## Forced leading terms

Changing the sign of the base multiplies the coefficient at each odd exponent by minus one and preserves every possible support.
It therefore suffices to consider \(x\ge\beta_d\).
The defining equation has a unique solution by strict decrease in the base, and shows that the \(\beta_d\) increase from \(\beta_2=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) to two.
Indeed every fixed \(1<x<2\) has \(\sum_{i=1}^\infty x^{-i}>1\).
The geometric series also gives \((2-\beta_d)\beta_d^d=1\).

Consider a finite ternary relation with leading position \(n\) and normalize its leading sign to plus.
When \(n<d\), even opposing signs at every lower position cannot cancel it since \(\sum_{i=1}^n x^{-i}<1\).
For \(n\ge d\), all \(d-1\) coefficients immediately below the leader must be minus one.
Indeed if the coefficient at distance \(j\le d-1\) were absent or positive, the opposing mass relative to the leader would be strictly less than \(\sum_{i\ge1,\,i\ne j} x^{-i}\), which decreases in \(x\) and is at most \(1/(\beta_d-1)-\beta_d^{-(d-1)}\).
This is at most one, since \((2-\beta_d)\beta_d^d=1\) and \(\beta_d(\beta_d-1)\ge1\).
Consequently deleting the positive multiples of \(d\) in \([0,N)\) gives an admissible mask: every possible leader \(n\ge d\) would require all positions \(n-d+1,\ldots,n\), which include a deleted multiple.
This proves the lower bound at all the stated real bases.

<a id="collapse"></a>
## A reversible reduction of minimal supports

Now take \(x=\beta_d\) and a minimal support with top index \(n\), and put \(h=n-d\).
The signed sum of its forced top \(d\) terms equals \(x^h\).
The coefficient at \(h\) cannot be plus, since the lower opposing mass is strictly less than \(x^h/(x-1)<2x^h\).
If it is minus, the consecutive positions \(h,\ldots,n\) already carry a relation and hence form the whole minimal support.
This consecutive support is itself minimal: any relation on it must have leader \(n\) after using the width lower bound \(d\), and all coefficients down to \(h\) are then required.
If the coefficient at \(h\) is absent, collapse the top \(d\) positions to a single plus at \(h\).
The resulting support is minimal, since a proper relation on it either avoids \(h\) or can expand its coefficient there into the same top cluster with the corresponding signs, giving a proper subrelation on the original support.

Conversely expanding the top position \(h\) of any minimal support to \(h+1,\ldots,h+d\) with the signed cluster prescribed by the defining equation produces another minimal support.
A relation confined to positions below \(h\) would contradict the original minimality.
A relation whose leader lies strictly between \(h\) and \(h+d\) must use position \(h\) by the forced-leading-terms result, or is impossible if its leader is below \(d\).
Position \(h\) has been removed.
Thus the leader must be \(h+d\), and its forced top coefficients collapse to the missing position \(h\).
Any proper subrelation would thereby give a proper subrelation on the original support.
Repeated collapse terminates, as the leader falls by \(d\) each time, and the reverse expansion generates exactly the displayed \(S_\ell\).
For \(\ell\ge1\), the translated consecutive support ending at \(d(\ell+1)\) and \(S_{\ell-1}\) meet only at \(\ell d\); cancelling there gives \(S_\ell\).
The consecutive support cannot split since all smaller supports have weight less than the minimum weight \(d+1\).

<a id="density"></a>
## Counting holes and taking the limit at two

Let \(S\subset[0,N)\) be admissible at \(\beta_d\), and call each missing position a hole.
Between each successive pair of selected positions in the same residue class modulo \(d\) there must be a hole in another residue class.
Otherwise for their difference \(d(\ell+1)\), the translate of \(S_\ell\) starting at the first position lies entirely in \(S\).
For a fixed residue the intervals between successive selected positions have disjoint interiors.
With \(H\) holes in total and \(H_r\) in residue class \(r\), one thus has \(|S\cap(r+d\mathbb Z)|\le1+H-H_r\), also when the left side is zero.
Summing gives \(N-H\le d+(d-1)H\), so \(H\ge\lceil N/d\rceil-1=\lfloor(N-1)/d\rfloor\).
Together with the common admissible mask this gives equality as claimed.

For \(\beta_d\le x<\beta_{d+1}\), the finite inequality gives \(1-D(x)\le1/d\).
Therefore the product in the limsup is at most \((d+1)\log(\beta_{d+1})/d\), by the identity for \(2-\beta_{d+1}\).
At \(x=\beta_d\) the product is exactly \(\log\beta_d\).
These two bounds converge to \(\log2\).
