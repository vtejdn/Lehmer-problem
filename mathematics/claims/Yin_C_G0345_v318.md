# Ramification excludes economical quadratic twists of the prime trace fields

The strategy researcher identified the local-degree obstruction from [C275](Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#cluster), derived the measure bounds and obtained the explicit cutoff below.
The coordinating researcher replaced the initial repeated-root classification by the quadratic greatest-common-divisor identity, and supplied the finite cases and input-measure certificates retained in C317.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exact exterior minimum for the full five-term family

For every prime \(p\ge5\), put
\[
 P_p(z)=z^{2p}-z^{p+1}-z^p-z^{p-1}+1,\qquad
 Q_p(T)=C_p(T)-T-1,\qquad C_p(z+z^{-1})=z^p+z^{-p}.
\]
Let \(F_p\) be the splitting field of \(P_p\).
For an algebraic number \(y\), write \(b(y)\) for the number of exterior conjugates, counted among the distinct roots of its minimal polynomial.
Then
\[
 \min_{\substack{y\ {\rm nontorsion\ algebraic\ unit}\\
                 y^j\in F_p\ {\rm for\ some}\ j\ge1\\
                 M(y)\le M(P_p)}} b(y)
                         =1+2\lfloor p/6\rfloor.                 \tag{1}
\]
The input root attains this minimum.
The statement concerns units in the radical saturation of the entire splitting field, not only products of the input roots.
No reciprocity assumption on the output is needed in (1).

The local result giving the large-prime part is independent of the Galois-group classification.
For any integer \(n\ge2\) such that \(Q_n=C_n-T-1\) is irreducible, let \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\), where \(Q_n(t)=0\).
Let \(D\) be any quadratic number field not contained in \(K\), put \(L=KD\), and suppose
\[
              u\in\mathcal O_L^\times,\quad
              N_{L/K}(u)=1,\quad \mathbb Q(u)=L.                 \tag{2}
\]
Write \(\mu=\log M(u)\) and \(r=\max(n-4,0)\).
At every rational prime \(\ell\) ramified in \(D\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{r(r-1)}4\log\ell
       &\le 2n\log(2n)+2(2n-1)\mu;                              \tag{3}\\
 r(r-1)\log\ell
       &\le 2n\log(2n)+2(2n-1)\mu\qquad(\ell\ne2).               \tag{4}
 \end{split}
\]
In particular,
\[
                           n\ge73\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(u)>13/10. \tag{5}
\]
The lower bound grows linearly in \(n\), uniformly over all quadratic fields \(D\), for this family of trace fields.
It does not require \(D\) to be the trace discriminant field.
The [fixed-block extension](#blocks) below replaces four by \(4\deg B\) under a precise reduction condition.

<a id="gcd"></a>
## A quadratic polynomial controls every repeated reduction

The integral Chebyshev identity is
\[
                   (T^2-4)(C_n')^2=n^2(C_n^2-4).               \tag{6}
\]
It follows by differentiating \(C_n(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(n\theta)\), and the resulting identity on the interval is a polynomial identity.
Fix a rational prime \(\ell\) and reduce all polynomials modulo \(\ell\).
Substitution of \(C_n=Q_n+T+1\) and \(C_n'=Q_n'+1\) in (6) shows
\[
 \gcd(Q_n,Q_n')\ \mid\
 R_n(T)=(T^2-4)-n^2((T+1)^2-4).                                \tag{7}
\]
This divisibility includes multiplicities: the right side belongs to the ideal generated by \(Q_n,Q_n'\).

The polynomial \(R_n\) is never identically zero modulo \(\ell\).
Its leading coefficient is \(1-n^2\); if this vanishes, its constant coefficient \(3n^2-4\) is \(-1\).
Consequently
\[
                         \deg\gcd(Q_n,Q_n')\le2                \tag{8}
\]
at every prime, including two and primes dividing \(n\).

If an irreducible reduction factor has multiplicity \(a\ge2\) in \(Q_n\), it has multiplicity at least \(a-1\) in the greatest common divisor.
Since \(a\le2(a-1)\), the total degree of all factors occurring with multiplicity at least two, with their full multiplicities, is at most four.
Thus the product of the simple reduction factors has degree at least \(n-4\).

These simple factors give unramified local factors of \(K\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\) with total degree at least \(n-4\).
Indeed separate their product from the remaining reduction by coprime Hensel factorization.
Each simple irreducible factor lifts to a monic polynomial with irreducible separable reduction, defining the unramified extension of its residue degree.
Their factors in \(\mathbb Q_\ell[T]/Q_n\) are precisely factors in \(K\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\).
No assumption that \(\mathbb Z[t]\) is maximal at \(\ell\) is needed for these simple factors.

<a id="cluster"></a>
## A ramified quadratic extension clusters primitive units

We use the local clustering argument of [C275](Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#cluster) with the unramified degree just obtained.
More generally, suppose the total degree of the unramified local factors of \(K\) at a prime \(\ell\) ramified in \(D\) is at least an integer \(s\ge0\).
Each such completion \(K_{\mathfrak l}/\mathbb Q_\ell\) is unramified, whereas \(D/\mathbb Q\) is ramified at \(\ell\).
Their compositum over \(K_{\mathfrak l}\) is a ramified quadratic field: its absolute ramification index is divisible by two, and the degree over the unramified field is at most two.
Its nonidentity automorphism is inertia and acts trivially on its residue field.

For \(u\) in (2), this automorphism sends \(u\) to \(u^{-1}\).
Therefore every corresponding local conjugate \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
       v_\ell(\alpha^2-1)\ge\tfrac12,\qquad
       \max\bigl(v_\ell(\alpha-1),v_\ell(\alpha+1)\bigr)\ge\tfrac14, \tag{9}
\]
where \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
There are at least \(2s\) such conjugates.
They are distinct because \(u\) generates the degree-\(2n\) field \(L\).

Assign each of these conjugates to one sign for which the second bound in (9) holds.
One sign receives at least \(s\) conjugates, and every difference within that cluster has valuation at least \(1/4\).
For the monic minimal polynomial \(A_u\in\mathbb Z[X]\), the discriminant is nonzero and is the squared product of all differences.
All other differences are integral.
It follows that
\[
                         v_\ell(\operatorname{disc} A_u)
                                         \ge s(s-1)/4.         \tag{10}
\]
This includes \(\ell=2\).

For odd \(\ell\), exactly one of \(\alpha-1,\alpha+1\) can have positive valuation, so the assigned distance in (9) is at least \(1/2\).
Retain both clusters rather than only the larger one.
If their sizes are \(a,b\), with \(a+b\ge2s\), then
\[
 v_\ell(\operatorname{disc} A_u)
       \ge\binom a2+\binom b2\ge s(s-1).                        \tag{11}
\]
The last bound follows by minimizing the sum at two clusters of size \(s\); extra points cannot decrease it.

For completeness the archimedean bound is the same Vandermonde estimate used in C275.
At a root \(\alpha\), its row has norm at most
\(\sqrt{2n}\max(1,|\alpha|)^{2n-1}\).
Hadamard's inequality, squared, gives
\[
        \log|\operatorname{disc} A_u|
                    \le2n\log(2n)+2(2n-1)\log M(u).             \tag{12}
\]
Take \(s=r\), using the simple local factors from (8), to obtain (3) and (4).
For \(r=0\) the bounds have zero left side and are immediate.

The generation assumption in (2) also implies that \(u+u^{-1}\) generates \(K\).
It lies in \(K\), while \(u\) has degree \(2n\) and is quadratic over this trace field.
Thus the trace field has degree at least \(n\), and equality with \(K\) follows.
In particular the argument is an application to actual primitive units, with no substitution of an unrelated trace field.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## A uniform cutoff without a discriminant computation

Every nontrivial quadratic number field has a finite ramified prime, as follows directly from its fundamental discriminant.
Using \(\log\ell\ge\log2\) in (3), \(M(u)\le13/10\) would imply, for \(n\ge73\),
\[
 \frac{(n-4)(n-5)}4\log2
      \le2n\log(2n)+2(2n-1)\log(13/10).                         \tag{13}
\]
At \(n=73\), its left side exceeds its right side.
Indeed
\[
 \log2>\frac{69}{100},\qquad \log146<5,\qquad
 \log(13/10)<\frac{27}{100}
\]
give the strictly positive difference
\[
       1173\frac{69}{100}-146\cdot5-290\frac{27}{100}
                                            =\frac{107}{100}. \tag{14}
\]
These logarithm bounds have elementary rational certificates.
The first follows from the first three positive terms of
\(2\sum_{j\ge0}3^{-(2j+1)}/(2j+1)=\log2\).
For the second, \(\sum_{k=0}^{12}5^k/k!>146\).
For the third, the alternating bound
\(\log(1+x)\le x-x^2/2+x^3/3\) at \(x=3/10\) is less than \(27/100\).

Divide the difference between the sides of (13) by \(n\), and write \(a=\log(13/10)\).
The resulting function is
\[
 H(n)=\frac{n-9+20/n}{4}\log2-2\log(2n)-(4-2/n)a.
\]
For real \(n\ge73\),
\[
 H'(n)=\frac{1-20/n^2}{4}\log2-\frac2n-\frac{2a}{n^2}>0.
\]
For example, the three terms are respectively greater than \(1/6\), at least \(-1/32\), and greater than \(-1/1000\), using the rational bounds above.
Thus (13) fails for every \(n\ge73\), proving (5).

More generally, the existence of a ramified prime and (3) give the explicit bound
\[
 \mu\ge
 \frac{(n-4)(n-5)\log2/4-2n\log(2n)}{2(2n-1)}
                  =\frac{\log2}{16}\,n-\frac12\log(2n)+O(1)    \tag{15}
\]
as \(n\to\infty\).
The estimate is uniform in \(D\) and in the generating norm-one unit.

<a id="family"></a>
## Combining the local obstruction with the actual ambient classification

Let \(p\ge73\) be prime.
The [five-term input theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) gives irreducibility of \(P_p\) and \(Q_p\).
The [exact signature calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v316.md#five-term) gives
\[
                    R=1,\qquad c=\lfloor p/6\rfloor,\qquad
                    N=p-1-2c>0.
\]
In particular \(0<c<(p-1)/4\), as required by the [quadratic-twist classification](Yin_C_G0345_v316.md#statement).
The [uniform input-measure certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v317.md#uniform) gives \(M(P_p)<13/10\).
This is strictly below the plastic number: \( (13/10)^3-13/10-1<0\).
Consequently [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes every nontorsion unit satisfying the input budget reciprocal.

The classification says that an ambient output under this budget can improve the input count \(2c+1\) only to \(2c\).
For even \(c\), that improvement is already impossible.
For odd \(c\), it would require, after choosing a conjugate, a nontorsion relative norm-one unit generating \(KD\) for an imaginary quadratic field \(D\subset F_p\).
Our bound (5) excludes it for every quadratic \(D\), independently of which fields occur in \(F_p\).

There is no loss through passage to a power in this application.
As recorded in [C316](Yin_C_G0345_v316.md#power), if \(w=y^j\), then
\[
                   b(y)=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q(w)]\,b(w).
\]
An output with the prospective count \(2c\) has the same positive exterior count as its field power, so the two fields coincide and \(y\) itself lies in the classified twist field.
Thus (5) is applied to \(y\), retaining its actual measure budget, rather than to a possibly large-measure power.

For primes \(5\le p<73\), use the [finite exact budget certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v317.md#finite), whose range includes these primes and whose exceptional small-degree actions are treated there.
It excludes the specific trace-discriminant twist where the odd parity branch is possible; a universal statement about every quadratic field is unnecessary for these finite cases.
Together with the large-prime argument, this proves (1).
The input root supplies the upper bound in every case.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Fixed central blocks and a coefficient-rank condition

Let \(B\in\mathbb Z[T]\) have degree \(d\ge1\), and let \(n>d\) be such that
\[
                         Q_{n,B}(T)=C_n(T)-B(T)
\]
is irreducible.
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\) for its degree-\(n\) root field and take \(D,L,u\) as in (2).
Define
\[
 A_B(T)=(T^2-4)B'(T)^2,\qquad C_B(T)=B(T)^2-4,\qquad
 R_{n,B}=A_B-n^2C_B.
\]
If a prime \(\ell\) ramified in \(D\) satisfies
\[
                         R_{n,B}\not\equiv0\pmod\ell,           \tag{16}
\]
then (3), and also (4) for odd \(\ell\), hold with
\[
                              r=\max(n-4d,0).                 \tag{17}
\]
Indeed (6) gives
\(\gcd(Q_{n,B},Q_{n,B}')\mid R_{n,B}\).
The latter has degree at most \(2d\), so the total repeated degree in the reduction is at most \(4d\).
The same unramified-factor and cluster arguments apply without any further coefficient bound.

There is a useful condition independent of \(n\).
Form the two integer coefficient rows of \(A_B,C_B\), padded with zeros through degree \(2d\), and let \(\Delta_B\) be the nonnegative greatest common divisor of their \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors.
Assume \(\Delta_B\ne0\).
At every prime \(\ell\nmid\Delta_B\), these two rows have rank two modulo \(\ell\), proving (16) for every \(n\).
For a fixed \(B\) and a fixed measure bound \(M(u)\le A\), (3) with \(\log\ell\ge\log2\) and (17) excludes such a prime for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Hence all primes ramified in a surviving quadratic \(D\) must divide \(\Delta_B\).
Only finitely many quadratic fields have this ramification support, by the classification of fundamental quadratic discriminants.
If \(\Delta_B=1\), no quadratic field survives for sufficiently large \(n\).

For \(B=T+1\), the constant and quadratic coefficient columns of \(A_B=T^2-4\) and \(C_B=T^2+2T-3\) have determinant \(-1\).
Thus \(\Delta_B=1\), agreeing with (7)–(8).
The auxiliary polynomial can vanish after reduction in examples outside the irreducible setting.
For example \(B=C_d\) gives \(A_B=d^2C_B\) by (6), and \(R_{n,B}\) vanishes modulo every prime with \(n^2\equiv d^2\).
No simple-factor conclusion is claimed at a prime where (16) fails.

<a id="scope"></a>
## What the obstruction settles

Equation (1) resolves the input-budget ambiguity left by the imaginary quadratic alternative for this entire five-term family.
Without a measure budget the odd-parity relative units still attain \(2\lfloor p/6\rfloor\), as [C316](Yin_C_G0345_v316.md#twists) proves.
Their forced large measure is consistent with their positive unit rank.
Neither (1) nor (15) is a lower bound for all algebraic units of the splitting field, since different fields and signatures can supply other units.

The input measures converge to the fixed value in [C306](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit), strictly greater than one.
This remains an obstruction to a universal economical reduction of exterior count, rather than a solution of the unrestricted Lehmer problem.
For a different fixed block, the finite ramification support in (16)–(17) is a necessary condition on bounded-measure quadratic twists; no classification of those remaining fields is asserted.
