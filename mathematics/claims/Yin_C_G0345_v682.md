# Irreducibility under power substitution for two Salem families converging to two

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two signed families

For \(n\ge4\) and \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), put
\[
             G_{n,\epsilon}(X)=X^n(X-2)+\epsilon(2X-1).
\]
This polynomial has precisely one root \(r_{n,\epsilon}\) of modulus greater than one.
It is Salem, with \(3/2<r_{n,+}<2<r_{n,-}<9/4\), and
\[
                      2^n(r_{n,\epsilon}-2)\longrightarrow-3\epsilon .
\]
Write \(P_{n,\epsilon}\) for its minimal polynomial.
The cyclotomic cofactor \(U_{n,\epsilon}=G_{n,\epsilon}/P_{n,\epsilon}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_{n,+}&=(X-1)(X+1)^{[n\ {\rm odd}]}\Phi_6(X)^{[n\equiv2\bmod6]},\\
 U_{n,-}&=(X+1)^{[n\ {\rm even}]}\Phi_6(X)^{[n\equiv5\bmod6]} .
\end{aligned}
\]
For every positive integer \(q\), \(P_{n,\epsilon}(X^q)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with exactly \(q\) exterior roots, all of modulus \(r_{n,\epsilon}^{1/q}\), and Mahler measure \(r_{n,\epsilon}\).
The other factors of \(G_{n,\epsilon}(X^q)\) are cyclotomic.
For the [family below two](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#statement) the argument below also gives an exponent bound directly from the four terms of \(G_{n,+}\).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Root count and cyclotomic factors

For \(|z|=1\) the root equation is \(z^n=\epsilon(2z-1)/(2-z)\).
The rational factor has degree one as a circle map, with phase derivative \(3/|2-z|^2\le3\).
Thus the difference between \(n\arg z\) and the lifted phase of the right side increases strictly by \(2\pi(n-1)\), giving \(n-1\) distinct simple circle roots.
For the positive sign \(G_{n,+}(3/2)<0<G_{n,+}(2)\), and for the negative sign \(G_{n,-}(2)<0<G_{n,-}(9/4)\) already at \(n=4\) and for all larger \(n\).
Reciprocity up to sign then supplies the other off-circle root \(1/r_{n,\epsilon}\).
The exterior root is an algebraic unit, so its minimal polynomial also contains its reciprocal by taking the absolute norm.
That polynomial cannot be quadratic, since its trace in that case would be an integer strictly between 2 and \(9/4+4/9<3\).
It follows that the exterior root is Salem and all remaining irreducible factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Writing \(r=r_{n,\epsilon}\), the identity \(r-2=-\epsilon(2r-1)r^{-n}\) gives \(n|\log(r/2)|\to0\) by the uniform bounds on \(r\), hence the stated limits.

A root of unity \(\zeta\) of \(G_{n,\epsilon}\) satisfies
\[
                   2(\zeta^n-\epsilon\zeta)=\zeta^{n+1}-\epsilon .
\]
If \(\zeta^n=\epsilon\zeta\), the identity implies \(\zeta^2=1\).
Otherwise the algebraic integer \(\zeta^n-\epsilon\zeta\) is nonzero and has modulus at most one at every embedding by this identity.
Its nonzero integral norm forces equality at every embedding, so \(\zeta^{n+1}=-\epsilon\).
Substituting back gives \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=1\).
Conversely each primitive sixth root with \(\zeta^{n+1}=-\epsilon\) satisfies the equation.
Testing \(1,-1\) directly and using the simplicity of the circle roots gives the displayed cofactors.

<a id="primes"></a>
## A bound for prime radicals in the Salem field

Fix one sign and index, write \(P=P_{n,\epsilon}\), \(U=U_{n,\epsilon}\), \(d=\deg P\), and let \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\) in its distinguished real embedding.
If a prime \(p\) satisfies \(\theta=r^{1/p}\in K\), then \(G_{n,\epsilon}(\theta)\ne0\), since \(1<\theta<r\) and \(r\) is the unique exterior root.
The [prime radical norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) applies to \(G_{n,\epsilon}\), whose coefficient absolute values sum to six and whose degree is \(n+1\).
Since \(\log M(r)=\log r\), it gives
\[
                              p\le6r^{(n+1)/(pd)}.                     \tag{1}
\]
The cofactor formulas give \((n+1)/d\le3/2\) on both sides.
For the positive sign this ratio is largest at \(n=5,8\), since \(d=n-1\) at odd indices, \(d=n-2\) at \(n\equiv2\bmod6\), and \(d=n\) at the remaining indices.
For the negative sign the ratio is at most \(5/4\) except at \(n=5\), since \(d=n\) at even indices, \(d=n-1\) at \(n\equiv5\bmod6\), and \(d=n+1\) elsewhere.

For \(p\ge7\) the positive side of (1) would give \(p\le6\cdot2^{3/14}<7\).
On the negative side with \(n\ne5\), \(p\ge7\) would give \(p\le6(9/4)^{5/28}<7\).
These strict numerical comparisons follow from \(8\cdot6^{14}<7^{14}\) and \(9^5 6^{28}<4^5 7^{28}\), respectively.
On the negative side at \(n=5\) even the larger ratio \(3/2\) gives \(6(9/4)^{3/22}<11\), excluding every prime at least eleven.
It remains to test \(2,3,5\) and the one case \(n=5,\epsilon=-1,p=7\).

<a id="congruences"></a>
## The remaining prime exponents

Any such radical \(\theta\in K\) generates \(K\), so its conjugates raised to the \(p\)-th power give the roots of \(P\) with no repetitions.
The [inverse-power congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#congruence) therefore requires \(P^{[p]}\equiv P\bmod p^2\), where \(P^{[p]}\) is the polynomial of \(p\)-th powers of the roots.
In particular \(s_{pj}(P)-s_j(P)\) is divisible by \(p^2\) for every Newton sum \(s_j\).

For \(p=2,3\) on the positive side the [two coefficient and trace tests](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#small) already exclude radicals of its Salem factor.
Indeed a positive radical in \(K\) would be Salem by the [neutral reciprocal pair argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
On the negative side the degree \(d\) is even, so at \(p=2\) the required congruence gives \(P(X)P(-X)\equiv P(X^2)\bmod4\).
Writing \(a_j=[X^{d-j}]P\), this forces
\[
          a_1\equiv2a_2-a_1^2,\qquad
          a_2\equiv2a_4-2a_1a_3+a_2^2\pmod4.
\]
When \(U=1\), \(a_1=-2,a_2=0\) violate the first congruence.
When \(U=\Phi_6\), division gives \((a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(-1,-2,-1,1)\), violating the second.
At even indices \(U=X+1\) gives \((-3,3,-3,3)\) with the last coefficient changed to \(1\) at \(n=4\), violating the second congruence in both cases.

For the odd primes use \(s_j=s_j(G_{n,\epsilon})\).
The reversed monic polynomial is \(1-2z+\epsilon z^n(2-z)\).
Expanding its formal logarithm gives \(s_j=2^j\) for \(1\le j<n\) and
\[
                  s_j=2^j-\epsilon j c_{j-n}\quad(n\le j<2n),
       \qquad c_0=2,\qquad c_b=3\cdot2^{b-1}\quad(b\ge1),
\]
since \(c_b=[z^b](2-z)/(1-2z)\).
For \(p=3\) on the negative side, \(s_3-s_1=6\), and any linear cyclotomic factor has zero contribution to this difference.
When \(U=\Phi_6\), use \(s_6-s_2\), subtracting \(2-(-1)=3\) for the sixth roots.
This gives \(57\) for \(n>6\) and \(75\) at \(n=5\), both nonzero modulo nine.
For \(p=5\) the cofactor is fixed under fifth powers for both signs.
The difference \(s_5-s_1\) equals \(30\) for \(n>5\), \(30-10\epsilon\) for \(n=5\) and \(30-15\epsilon\) for \(n=4\).
Every value is nonzero modulo 25.
At \(n=5,\epsilon=-1,p=7\) the cofactor is also fixed under seventh powers, while \(s_7-s_1=168\) is nonzero modulo 49.

<a id="substitution"></a>
## Exterior roots after dilation

The prime tests exclude every positive prime radical of \(r\) from \(K\).
As in the [real-field binomial argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution), the constant term of any monic factor of \(X^q-r\) of degree \(0<t<q\) would have modulus \(r^{t/q}\) and be real.
It would put \(r^{\gcd(t,q)/q}\) in \(K\) by Bézout's identity, hence put a positive prime radical of \(r\) there.
Thus \(X^q-r\) is irreducible over \(K\), and the degree of \(r^{1/q}\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(qd\).
This proves irreducibility of \(P(X^q)\), and its roots over the unique exterior conjugate \(r\) give exactly the stated count and Mahler measure.
Substituting into \(U\) gives only roots of unity.
