# Simultaneous primes above the delay in the radical index of an integer Salem seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number and size of the large primes

For integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of
\(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(R\) be the largest integer \(l\ge1\) with \(r^{1/l}\in K\), taking positive roots.
For every prime \(p>n\) dividing \(R\),
\[
 \frac Rp<
 \min\!\left\{\frac{\log p}{2}\log^3(3p),\
             4\log p\left(\frac{\log d}{\log\log d}\right)^3\right\}.   \tag{1}
\]
There are at most two distinct such primes.
If there are two, both are less than \(1500\), so all tuples \((a,n,\epsilon,p,q)\) with \(n<p<q\) and \(pq\mid R\) form an effectively finite set.
In particular \(n<1500\) and \(a<1+1500^{1500}\) on that set.

If \(p>n,\ p\ge1500\) divides \(R\), then \(R=pS\), where every prime factor of \(S\) is at most \(n\) and
\[
                  S\mid H(a),\qquad
                  H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}
                            \frac{a+j}{\operatorname{rad}(a+j)}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{rad}\) denotes the product of the distinct prime divisors, and the least common multiple is taken over the three integer quotients.

Along any sequence of these seeds with \((\log r)/R\to0\) and a prime \(p>n\) dividing \(R\) at each term, \(p\to\infty\), and the eventually unique such prime satisfies
\[
       \frac{R/p}{\log p}\ \longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
       \frac Rp=O\!\left(\frac{(\log p)^4}{(\log\log p)^3}\right).      \tag{2}
\]
This applies in particular if an irreducible noncyclotomic factor of a dilation \(G_a(X^h)\) has logarithmic Mahler measure tending to zero along the sequence.

<a id="complement"></a>
## Height of the full field radical

Let \(\theta=r^{1/R}\).
Since \(\theta\in K\) and \(\theta^R=r\), it generates \(K\).
It is an algebraic unit, and powering its \(d\) conjugates gives those of \(r\) bijectively.
It therefore has just one exterior conjugate, \(\theta\), and \(\log M(\theta)=\log\theta=(\log r)/R\).
The [degree and cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(6\le n-1\le d\le n+1\le p\).
The [first radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v702.md#statement) gives \(r^{1/p}<p\), hence
\[
                          (R/p)\log\theta<\log p .
\]
[Voutier's Theorem and Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) apply to this nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\).
They give \(\log\theta>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) and \(\log\theta>2/\log^3(3d)\).
Combining each inequality with the preceding display proves (1).

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Comparing two or three primes

For \(t\ge11\) one has \(\tfrac12\log t\,\log^3(3t)<t^2\).
Indeed the logarithmic derivative of the quotient by \(t^2\), with respect to \(\log t\), is \(1/\log t+3/\log(3t)-2<0\).
At eleven the numerator is less than \(3\cdot4^3/2=96<121\).
If three primes above \(n\) divided \(R\), the smallest one, \(p\ge11\), would satisfy \(R/p>p^2\) by the other two factors, contradicting (1).

Suppose \(n<p<q\) are primes dividing \(R\), so \(q\le R/p\).
Write \(f(t)=\log t/\log\log t\) for \(t\ge6\) and \(T=1500\).
The function \(y/\log y\) decreases to \(y=\mathrm e\) and then increases, so \(f(d)\le\max\{f(6),f(p)\}\).
We have \(f(6)<3.7,\ 7.30<\log T<7.32,\ \log\log T>1.98\), and \(7.32/1.98<3.7\).
For example \(\sqrt{\mathrm e}<5/3<\log6<1.8\) proves the first bound.
The remaining decimal comparisons follow from \(e^{7.30}<1500<e^{7.32}\) and \(e^{1.98}<7.30\), each verifiable by the exponential series through order forty with remainder at most \(x^{41}/[41!(1-8/42)]\) for \(0\le x\le8\).
In particular
\[
                4\log T\max\{f(6),f(T)\}^3
                       <4(7.32)(3.7)^3<1500 .
\]
For \(t\ge T\) both \(4f(6)^3\log t/t\) and \(4(\log t)^4/[t(\log\log t)^3]\) decrease.
For the second expression the logarithmic derivative with respect to \(\log t\) is \((4-3/\log\log t)/\log t-1<0\).
If \(p\ge T\), (1) would give \(q<p\).
If \(p<T\), both \(d\) and \(p\) are in \([6,T]\), so (1) gives \(q<1500\) by the endpoint estimate.
Then \(n<p\) and \(a<1+p^p\) give the stated finite bounds.
For each candidate tuple one can factor the minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(r\) after each of the two prime dilations over \(\mathbb Q\).
The positive real exterior root \(r^{1/l}\) of \(P(X^l)\) at \(l=p,q\) is specified by real root isolation, and lies in \(K\) precisely when its degree is \(d\), since its field contains \(K\).
This decides the two divisibilities by the [description of the field exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).

<a id="shrinking"></a>
## The other prime factors on a shrinking sequence

The [squarefreeness above the delay](Yin_C_G0345_v702.md#statement) gives \(v_p(R)=1\).
If \(p\ge1500\) is above the delay, the two-prime bound makes it the only such prime.
The [valuation bounds through the delay](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#diagonal) place the whole complementary factor \(R/p\) in \(H(a)\) as a divisor.

If \(\log\theta\to0\) and \(p>n\) divides \(R\), Corollary 2 and \(d\le p\) force \(p\to\infty\).
The [prime-power estimate for the seed](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) gives \(p\log(2p)\le C\log r\) with an effective absolute constant \(C\).
Thus \((R/p)/\log p\ge1/(C\log\theta)\to\infty\).
As \(p\to\infty\) the endpoint \(f(p)\) eventually exceeds \(f(6)\), and the second bound in (1) gives the upper bound in (2).
In fact whenever \(p>n\) divides \(R\), even without shrinking, \(\log r\) lies between \(p\log(2p)/C\) and \(p\log p\).
Finally the [factor measure formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) bounds the logarithmic Mahler measure of any noncyclotomic factor of a dilation below by \((\log r)/R\), since the factors contributed by the linear cyclotomic cofactor stay cyclotomic under dilation.
This proves the asserted application.
