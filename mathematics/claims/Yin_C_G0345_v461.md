# Volumes of signed double-contact lattices at a reciprocal unit and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## Successive minima at every fixed width

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d=2s\ge4\).
Choose one root \(x_i\) from each inverse pair and assume that these representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Write \(P_2\) for the minimal polynomial of the square of a root, \(G=P P_2\), \(h=\log M(P)\), and \(m=2d\).
The roots \(x_i^{\pm1},x_i^{\pm2}\) of \(G\) are all distinct by independence.
For a sign \(\varepsilon\) and a formal width \(b\ge m\), let \(\Lambda_{\varepsilon,n,b}\) be the lattice of coefficient vectors of integral polynomials \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) satisfying
\[
             G^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}).
\]
Put \(r=b+1-m\), and let \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_d\) be the decreasing rearrangement of the \(d\) numbers \(|\log|x_i||,\,2|\log|x_i||\).
For the volume formula, assume that \(P\) is the minimum of a Salem number or that the minimal polynomial of \(x_1+x_1^{-1}\) differs from each of \(T^2-46T-128,\ T^2+50T-32,\ T^2+14T+16\).
For each fixed \(b,\varepsilon\), the lattice is saturated of rank \(r\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and its successive Euclidean minima satisfy
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_j(\Lambda_{\varepsilon,n,b})
     = V_b := \sum_{j=1}^d(m-2j+2)v_j-2h+3(b-m)h .                    \tag{1}
\]
For a Salem number \(\delta\) this is \((6d-4+3(b-m))\log\delta\).
The trace condition holds for every independent-pair seed of degree at least six or measure at most two by the [minimum-width theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v460.md#exact).

Call a nonzero row passing if \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\).
When \(r(\log2)/8<V_b\), all passing rows in \(\Lambda_{\varepsilon,n,b}\) eventually span a proper rational subspace.
At \(b=m+1\) all passing rows for each sign lie on at most one primitive lattice line.
There is also a conclusion allowing arbitrary widths and every trace polynomial under the independence assumption.
For each \(1\le q\le8\), any \(q\) independent passing rows at the same sign and width eventually have at least \(m+q+1\) positions in their combined support.
The width and positions may vary with \(n\) in this last assertion.

## The extremal minor after the square substitution

In the splitting field \(K\), replace \(n\) by an indeterminate \(N\) and \(x_i^n\) by independent variables \(z_i\).
For each \(i\) take the two sites \(y=z_i^a,\ c=\varepsilon x_i^a\), \(a=1,2\).
Let \(M_b\) have columns \(l=0,\ldots,b\) with a value and an Euler derivative row at each site,
\[
        y^l+cy^{b-l},\qquad Nl y^l+c(1+N(b-l))y^{b-l}.
\]
At \(N=n,\ z_i=x_i^n\), the rows give the value and derivative tests at the chosen ordinary and squared roots.
The signed graph equals \(\varepsilon\) times its reversal of formal width \(nb+1\), so the same two tests hold at their inverses.
The specialized kernel is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) by taking the remainder of the signed graph modulo \(G^2\).
Let \(C_b\) denote the tuple of maximal minors of the polynomial matrix \(M_b\).

For a weight vector \(u\in\mathbb R^s\) let \(w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_d\) be the rearrangement of \(|u_i|,2|u_i|\).
The maximum weight of a monomial in \(C_b\), regarding coefficients in \(K[N]\) as unweighted, is
\[
                   3b\sum_i u_i+\sum_{j=1}^d(b-2j+2)w_j .             \tag{2}
\]
Indeed a column \(l\) at a site of signed weight \(v\) contributes at most \(bv/2+|l-b/2|\,|v|\).
Each site uses two columns.
The \(m\) largest distances from \(b/2\) are two copies of \(b/2-j+1\), \(1\le j\le d\).
The rearrangement inequality gives (2) as an upper bound.
Take the single minor on column pairs \(j-1,b-j+1\), \(1\le j\le d\).
For generic \(u\) the \(d\) absolute site weights are positive and distinct, so each pair must go to the site of the corresponding rank to attain that bound.
The maximal power choice in each column is unique for this direction.
The \(2\)-by-\(2\) determinant of the leading coefficients of the pair at a site is \(\pm c\), because after removing the value multipliers the two derivative entries differ by one.
The resulting unique leading monomial has coefficient \(\pm\prod c\).
Any other exponent vector before the square substitution mapping to this monomial would have had the same maximal weight, so the coefficient survives substitution.
Continuity of the support functions of these finite sets gives (2) also for ties and zeros, as in the [outer-column argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#support).
In particular the maximum for \(u=-e_i\) is \(-3b+2b+(b-2)=-2\).
Thus the common order in \(z_i\) of the minors is exactly two, for every \(i\).

<a id="divisor"></a>
## The common divisor at larger widths

Assume the hypotheses of (1).
At width \(m\) the gcd of \(C_m\) in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\) is, for each sign,
\[
                         D=\prod_i z_i^2(z_i-1)^4
\]
by the [Salem gcd calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#height) and its [independent-pair extension](Yin_C_G0345_v460.md#exact).
To compare widths put \(k=b-m,\ t=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\).
For even \(k\) take \(g(Y)=Y^t\), and for odd \(k\) take either \(Y^t(1+Y)\) or \(Y^t(1-Y)\).
All satisfy \(Y^k g(1/Y)=\eta g(Y)\) for some sign \(\eta\).
Multiplication by \(g\) gives a constant coefficient matrix \(T_g\) from width \(m\) to width \(b\).
The [multiplier identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#volume) gives at each site the two rows of \(M_bT_g\) as those at width \(m\) and sign \(\varepsilon\eta\), multiplied on the left by
\[
                 \begin{pmatrix}g(y)&0\\ Ny g'(y)&g(y)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This follows by differentiating the product after using the reversal identity for \(g\).
By Cauchy–Binet the gcd of \(C_b\) divides \(D\prod_i g(z_i)^2g(z_i^2)^2\) for each choice.
Away from coordinate variables, the two extra products in the odd case can share only the factors \(z_i+1\), since \(1+z_i^2\) is coprime to both \(1-z_i\) and \(1-z_i^2\).
In particular the order at \(z_i=1\) is at most four, using the plus choice for odd \(k\).

To get the matching lower bound, set \(z_i=e^\theta\) near \(1\) and change the column basis to the polynomials \((Y-1)^j\), \(0\le j\le b\).
This invertible constant change preserves the ideal of maximal minors.
On column \(j\), each of the two value rows at \(e^\theta,e^{2\theta}\) vanishes to order at least \(j\), since both the direct and inverse evaluations do so.
Each derivative row vanishes to order at least \(j-1\) by differentiating those evaluations and their monomial factors.
In a four-column minor of this moving block the column indices are distinct, so its order is at least \(0+1+2+3-2=4\).
Expansion along the four rows gives the same lower bound for every maximal minor.
These expansions are valid over the rational function field in \(N\) and the remaining variables, and \(\theta\) is a uniformizer for \(z_i-1\).

At \(z_i=-1\) the two sites are \(y=-1,1\).
Writing \(D_Y=Y\,d/dY\) and \(c'=cy^b\), their two rows act on \(A\) as
\[
 (1+c')A(y),\qquad N(1-c')D_Y A(y)+c'(1+Nb)A(y).
\]
For both sites \(c'\ne\pm1\) by nontorsion, including the factor \((-1)^b\) at the negative site.
Over \(K(N,\mathbf z_{\ne i})\) these four rows can therefore be replaced by values and Euler derivatives at \(-1,1\).
Keep these new rows fixed and set \(N=0\) in the other rows.
At generic remaining sites the latter pairs are equivalent to simple evaluations at \(y,y^{-1}\).
These nodes are all distinct and disjoint from \(\{-1,1\}\).
The resulting \(m\) Hermite conditions are independent already in degree \(m-1\), because a polynomial there with the prescribed zeros and multiplicities is zero.
Hence the matrix before that specialization has full rank at \(z_i=-1\) over the rational function field, excluding \(z_i+1\).
Together with the axis orders from (2), this proves \(\gcd C_b=D\) up to \(K^\times\).

<a id="volume"></a>
## Projective height and lattice volume

Let \(Q_b=C_b/D\).
Every nonzero entry gives an eventually nonvanishing exponential polynomial under specialization, since distinct exponent vectors in the \(z_i\) give bases with nontorsion ratios.
The [coprime-tuple height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) applies to this finite tuple just as it does to the cofactors of a line.
Specifically, the placewise recurrence estimate gives the maximum weight of the support of \(Q_b\) at each embedding as the logarithmic slope of its largest specialized coordinate.
After clearing scalar denominators, the common finite-place contribution is \(o(n)\) by the almost-unit gcd bound for a coprime pair obtained from the tuple, or is bounded for a nonzero constant coordinate.
Here the input outside the archimedean places costs at most \(\log n\), its height grows linearly in \(n\), and a proper exceptional set is eventually avoided by the same nondegenerate recurrence argument.
Thus the projective logarithmic height divided by \(n\) tends to the average of the support maxima of \(Q_b\).

For \(u_i=\log|\sigma x_i|\) at each embedding \(\sigma\), the ordered absolute site weights are \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) because the embedding permutes the inverse pairs.
We have \(\sum_i|u_i|=h\), and each signed \(u_i\) averages to zero over the embeddings by the unit norm.
The maximum weight of \(D\) is \(2\sum_i u_i+4\sum_i\max(u_i,0)\) and therefore averages to \(2h\).
Support maxima add under multiplication, so (2) gives the projective height slope \(\sum_j(b-2j+2)v_j-2h\).
Since \(\sum_jv_j=3h\), this equals \(V_b\).
For a Salem seed the two nonzero entries of the ordered list are \(2h,h\).

The nonvanishing of an extremal minor gives eventual rank \(m\), and the integral points of the rational kernel form a saturated lattice.
The complementary signed maximal minors give its Plücker coordinates, so their projective height is the logarithm of the maximum absolute coordinate of the primitive integral wedge of a lattice basis.
At fixed \(b\) the Euclidean norm of this wedge, the covolume, differs from that maximum by at most a fixed factor.
The [covolume comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#support) bounds the product of successive minima between the covolume and \((2/\sqrt3)^{r(r-1)/2}\) times it.
This proves (1).
If \(r\) independent rows pass, their Euclidean norms are all less than \(\exp(n(\log2)/8)\), forcing the product of successive minima below \(\exp(nr(\log2)/8)\).
The strict inequality in the statement rules this out for large \(n\).

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Combined support of independent passing rows

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_q\) be independent integral rows at the same \(n,b,\varepsilon\), and suppose their combined support has size at most \(m+q\).
In the matrix with these rows select \(q-1\) independent coefficient columns.
The signed maximal minors after deleting one row of those selected columns give integers \(a_j\), not all zero, whose linear combination \(A=\sum_j a_j A_j\) vanishes at the selected positions.
For \(q=1\) take the empty minor equal to one.
The row \(A\) is nonzero by independence, belongs to the same lattice, and has at most \(m+1\) positions.
The determinant bound \(|a_j|\le\prod_{i\ne j}\|A_i\|_1\) gives \(\|A\|_1\le q\prod_i\|A_i\|_1\).
Consequently, on any sequence of such configurations with \(n\to\infty\), the [arbitrary-position sparse bound](Yin_C_G0345_v460.md#statement) yields
\[
 \liminf\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^q\log\|A_j\|_1
 \ \ge\ \sum_{i<j}\bigl(2\max(2t_i,t_j)+2\max(t_i,2t_j)+4\max(t_i,t_j)\bigr)
 \ \ge\ (3d-6)h ,
 \qquad t_i=|\log|x_i||.
\]
By [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), the last quantity exceeds \(3d/(\log(3d))^3\ge e^3/27>\log 2\), using \(d\ge4\) and the minimum of \(x/(\log x)^3\) for \(x>1\).
For \(q\le8\), passing would make the upper limit at most \(q(\log2)/8\le\log2\).
This proves the union bound independently of the trace condition.
At \(b=m+1\) there are only \(m+2\) available positions, so two independent passing rows are impossible and the line assertion follows by saturation.
The line and its primitive direction may depend on \(n\) and the sign.
