# Integral derivatives at the square endpoint force a Mahler gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound from one integral monic derivative

Suppose \(r>1\) is a compressed reciprocal Perron unit with the [one active oriented block](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal), \(M(r)=r^2\), and \(u\ge2\) blocks of \(b\ge3\) inverse pairs.
Let \(K\) be the field of the half containing \(r\), and \(f\in\mathcal O_K[X]\) its monic half polynomial.
Its constant term is a unit of absolute value one in the chosen real embedding, and its quadratic conjugate over the block field is its monic reciprocal.
Let \(\theta=1.324717\ldots\) be the positive root of \(X^3-X-1\).
For \(1\le j<b\) let \(g_j\) be \(f^{(j)}\) divided by its leading coefficient \(b(b-1)\cdots(b-j+1)\).
Whenever \(g_j\) has a nonzero root and belongs to \(\mathcal O_K[X]\), one has
\[
        M(r)\ge\theta\qquad\text{if }\ u\ge3\ \text{or }\ \gcd(b,j)=1 .
\]
In particular an integral \(f'/b\) suffices at every such endpoint, without a primality assumption on \(b\).
All orders \(1\le j\le b/2\) have a nonzero derivative root.

For \(u=2\) and every fixed \(\delta>0\), the same conclusion holds for all \(\gcd(b,j)\le b^{1/2-\delta}\) once \(b\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(\delta\), again whenever the derivative has a nonzero root.
In particular for each \(\epsilon>0\), if \(M(r)<\theta\), then among the orders \(1\le j\le b/2\) all but \(O_\epsilon(b^{1/2+\epsilon})\) give nonintegral normalized derivatives.
The gap concerns the source \(r\) under the stated integrality conditions.

<a id="critical"></a>
## Measures and locations of the critical points

For a monic complex polynomial \(P\) of degree \(s\ge2\) the monic derivative \(P'/s\) satisfies \(M(P'/s)\le M(P)\).
Here is a linear algebra proof of this derivative inequality.
Let \(A\) be the diagonal matrix of the \(s\) roots with multiplicity and let \(v=s^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\).
The compression \(B\) of \(A\) to \(v^\perp\) has characteristic polynomial \(P'/s\): in an orthonormal basis ending in \(v\) its characteristic determinant is the last diagonal cofactor of \(XI-A\), namely \(P(X)\langle (XI-A)^{-1}v,v\rangle=P'(X)/s\) off the original roots, and hence everywhere as a polynomial.
If \(k\) of its eigenvalues have modulus greater than one, their product is an eigenvalue of \(\bigwedge^k B\), as seen by triangularization.
The norm of this operator is at most that of \(\bigwedge^k A\), since the inclusion and orthogonal projection are contractions also on exterior powers.
For the diagonal \(A\) this last norm is the product of the \(k\) largest root moduli of \(P\), at most \(M(P)\).
The empty product handles \(k=0\).
Also every eigenvalue of \(B\) is a convex combination of the original roots, by taking the inner product of \(A\) on a unit eigenvector of \(B\) with that eigenvector.
At a place where the roots of \(P\) are distinct circle points every derivative root is strictly inside the disk: equality in the convex disk inequality forces the eigenvector to be supported on a single coordinate, impossible in \(v^\perp\).
Higher derivative roots stay strictly inside there by convexity.

The polynomials \(f^\sigma\) over the \(2u\) embeddings of \(K\) partition the rational conjugates of \(r\).
Of these embeddings, \(2u-2\) have only distinct neutral roots, by the [half-field signatures](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#moments).
Thus at each of them every root of \(g_j^\sigma\) is strictly inside the circle.
At the marked real half, every derivative root lies in the convex hull of the closed unit disk and \(r\), by successive applications of the compression argument.
Every point \(z\) in that hull with \(|z|>1\) has positive real part.
Indeed the real functional \(w\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(\bar z w)\) at \(z\) has value \(|z|^2>|z|\), whereas its maximum on the disk is \(|z|\), so \(r\operatorname{Re}z\ge |z|^2\).
Iteration of the derivative measure bound at every embedding gives
\[
       M(N_{K/\mathbb Q}g_j)=\prod_\sigma M(g_j^\sigma)
                \le \prod_\sigma M(f^\sigma)=M(r).
\]

<a id="integral"></a>
## Reciprocity of integral critical factors

Assume \(M(r)<\theta\) and \(g_j\) is integral with a nonzero root.
Remove the zero roots from its rational norm polynomial.
This leaves a monic integer polynomial \(H\) with positive degree and nonzero constant term.
By [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) every irreducible factor of \(H\) is reciprocal up to sign, since the measures of all factors are at least one and \(M(H)<\theta\).
Thus the strictly exterior and strictly interior root counts of \(H\) agree with multiplicity.
Write \(q>0\) for the common number of nonzero derivative roots in each coefficient embedding, counting multiplicity.
There are \(2u q\) roots of \(H\), with at least \((2u-2)q\) strictly interior from the neutral halves.
For \(u\ge3\) this exceeds half.
For \(u=2\) the two real halves must each have all their nonzero derivative roots strictly exterior.
In particular the real parts of those on the marked half are all positive, so their sum cannot be zero.

Write \(S\) for the sum of the \(b\) roots of \(f\).
The sum of the roots of \(g_j\) is \((b-j)S/b\) by its second coefficient.
Hence \(S\ne0\) in the only remaining case \(u=2\), and integrality makes \(\gcd(b,j)S/b\) an algebraic integer by Bézout's identity, since \(S\) is integral.
If \(\gcd(b,j)=1\), the [normalized first power sum test](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#moments) gives
\[
                r\ge 1+b(B_2-1),\qquad B_2=\sqrt{(\sqrt{17}-1)/2}>6/5 .
\]
But this gives \(r^2>(1+3/5)^2>\theta\), a contradiction.
Finally \(f\) has an intermediate nonzero coefficient: otherwise its roots all have the same modulus, contrary to the marked half pattern.
Its coefficient support is symmetric by the monic reciprocal relation over \(K\), so some intermediate exponent is at least \(b/2\).
Consequently every order \(1\le j\le b/2\) retains a nonleading term and has a nonzero derivative root.

<a id="orders"></a>
## The common divisor of the order and the half degree

When \(u=2\) and \(M(r)<\theta\), the [reflected first-power estimate over the coefficient embeddings](Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#energy) gives
\[
                    \max_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C} |\sigma S|
                                      \ll \sqrt{b\log(2b)} .
\]
In that estimate the number of embeddings is four, the sum of their log measures is \(\log M(r)<\log\theta\), the smoothing radius is \(e^{-1/b}\), and the reflected energy summed across embeddings is \(O(b\log(2b))\).
The correction from the exterior roots in the first power sum is bounded absolutely by \(2\log\theta\cdot\theta\) at each embedding.
If \(\gcd(b,j)\le b^{1/2-\delta}\), all conjugates of the nonzero algebraic integer \(\gcd(b,j)S/b\) would have modulus \(O(b^{-\delta}\sqrt{\log(2b)})<1\), contradicting its nonzero integer norm.
The number of \(j\le b\) having \(\gcd(b,j)>b^{1/2-\delta}\) is at most \(b^{1/2+\delta}\tau(b)\), by summing \(b/e\) over the divisors \(e\) of \(b\) in that range.
Here the divisor count \(\tau(b)=O_\gamma(b^\gamma)\) for every \(\gamma>0\): in the product over prime powers \(p^a\Vert b\), one has \(a+1\le p^{a\gamma}\) for \(p\ge2^{1/\gamma}\), and the finitely many smaller primes cost a bounded factor each since \((a+1)p^{-a\gamma}\) is bounded.
Choosing \(\delta,\gamma>0\) with \(\delta+\gamma<\epsilon\) gives the stated count.
