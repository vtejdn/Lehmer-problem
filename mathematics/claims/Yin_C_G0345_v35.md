# An irreducible delay-one inverse for every reciprocal target above measure one

<a id="statement"></a>
## Preserving the exterior-root count and recovering the exterior traces

Let \(P(z)=z^m Q(z+z^{-1})\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(M(P)>1\), where \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure.
Factor \(Q=GH\) over the reals into monic polynomials, placing exactly the trace roots in \((-2,2)\) in \(H\).
Then \(r=\deg G\ge1\) is the number of exterior roots of \(P\).
For every sufficiently large integer \(|C|\) with \(C G(2)<0\) there is a monic irreducible \(f_C\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of the same degree, with no circle roots, exactly \(r\) exterior roots, and
\[
       zf_C+f_C^*=(z+1)P,\qquad f_C^*(z)=z^{2m}f_C(1/z).
\]
Its constant term is \(1+C\) for \(r=1\) and one for \(r\ge2\).
The exterior roots divided by \(|C|^{1/r}\) tend as a multiset to the roots of \(u^r=\operatorname{sgn} C\).
Writing \(L_C\) for their monic polynomial, one has coefficientwise
\[
      L_C+C\longrightarrow
        \operatorname{quo}\big(z^r G(z+z^{-1}),z^{r-1}(z-1)\big),
\]
where \(\operatorname{quo}\) denotes the Euclidean quotient.
This limit determines \(G\) uniquely.
There is a real interior root \(\beta_C\to1\) satisfying \((1-\beta_C)M(f_C)\to |G(2)|\).
The threshold and these limits are for fixed \(P\).

In particular suppose \(r=2\) and \(G=x^2-Sx+U\) has \(G(2)>0\).
This includes two real traces beyond the same endpoint and a conjugate pair of nonreal traces.
Writing \(C=-N\), the resulting unit has two nonreal exterior conjugates \(\eta_N,\bar\eta_N\), and
\[
            2\Re\eta_N\longrightarrow S-1,\qquad
            |\eta_N|^2-N\longrightarrow U-S+3 .
\]
Along a sequence with \(M(P)\to1\), choose \(N\to\infty\) separately for each target to make both errors tend to zero.
For two traces above two the two displayed quantities tend to \((3,3)\), and for two traces below minus two they tend to \((-5,11)\).
For a nonreal pair they satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(2\Re\eta_N,[-5,3])\to0,\qquad
 4\big(|\eta_N|^2-N-2\big)-(2\Re\eta_N-1)^2\to0 .
\]

<a id="construction"></a>
## Integral coefficients and the directions of the circle roots

The traces are simple and different from \(\pm2\) since \(P\) is irreducible and \(M(P)>1\).
Each trace off \([-2,2]\) lifts to exactly one exterior root.
Set \(h=\deg H=m-r\), \(s=\operatorname{sgn}G(2)\), and \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
Following the [polynomial form of the inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex), take
\[
 f_C=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p_C(x),\qquad p_C=C H+E_C\in\mathbb Z[x].
\]
Retain leading coefficient \(C\) and round the lower coefficients of \(C H-s A H'\) to integers.
For a sufficiently large fixed \(A>0\), one has \(E_C(x_j)/Q'(x_j)<0\) at every root \(x_j\) of \(H\).
Indeed \(Q'(x_j)=G(x_j)H'(x_j)\), \(G\) has constant sign \(s\) on the interval, and all rounding errors at these finitely many points are bounded independently of \(C,A\).
The coefficients of \(E_C\) are bounded and \(\deg E_C<h\).
For \(h=0\) use \(p_C=C\).
The correction term is antisymmetric in formal degree \(2m-1\) and has lowest term \(-Cz^{r-1}\), giving the return and the constant.

In the [root deformation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v132.md#exterior) replace \(E_C\) by \(tE_C\), \(0\le t\le1\).
At zero the seed is \(z^hH(x)\) times \(z^rG(x)-C z^{r-1}(z-1)\).
The latter has \(r-1\) roots tending to zero by division by \(-C\), one simple real root \(1+G(2)/C+O_P(C^{-2})\) inside, and \(r\) exterior roots with the asserted scaled limits by setting \(z=|C|^{1/r}u\) and dividing by \(|C|^2\).
The perturbation has degree below \(2m-r\) and bounded coefficients, so the scaled exterior limits persist uniformly for \(0\le t\le1\).
At the circle lift \(\zeta\) of \(x_j\), the moving simple root has derivative
\[
      \frac{z'(0)}{\zeta}
       =\frac{E_C(x_j)}
        {H'(x_j)(\zeta+1)(G(x_j)-C(1-\zeta^{-1}))}.
\]
This follows by differentiating \(H(x)(G(x)-C(1-z^{-1}))=t(1-z^{-1})E_C(x)\).
The real part of \((\zeta+1)(G(x_j)-C(1-\zeta^{-1}))\) is \((1+\Re\zeta)G(x_j)\), so \(E_C(x_j)/Q'(x_j)<0\) gives inward motion.
For \(t>0\) any circle zero of the seed would also be a zero of the return \((z+1)P\), by conjugation and reversal.
The value \(tE_C(x_j)\ne0\) prevents a zero at a circle lift, and \(Q(-2)-2(C H(-2)+tE_C(-2))\ne0\) at \(-1\) for large \(|C|\) since \(H(-2)\ne0\).
This proves the exact count and absence of circle roots.

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## The value at one excludes every proper factorization

Put \(D_0=z^h H(x)z^{r-1}(z-1)\).
The convergence \(f_C/(-C)\to D_0\) on bounded sets shows that the \(2m-r\) bounded roots tend with multiplicity to the roots of \(D_0\).
Only one of them, a simple root \(\beta_C\), tends to one since \(H(2)\ne0\).
Every other bounded root stays a fixed positive distance from one.
Every nonconstant monic integral factor requires an exterior root, since its integer constant is nonzero and all the other roots are strictly inside the disk.
If a factor \(g\) omits \(\beta_C\), its value \(|g(1)|\) grows without bound uniformly over such factors, by the product of its distances from one.
Indeed one distance tends uniformly to infinity and all others are bounded below by a common positive constant, with at most \(2m\) factors.
Yet the nonzero integer \(g(1)\) divides \(f_C(1)=P(1)\).
Thus two nonconstant factors would both have to contain the same simple root, proving irreducibility by Gauss's lemma.
The argument applies to [every large signed integer parameter](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v136.md#inverse).
The root \(\beta_C\) is real by uniqueness, and its equation gives \(C(\beta_C-1)=\beta_C G(x_{\beta_C})/(1+E_C(x_{\beta_C})/(C H(x_{\beta_C})))\to G(2)\), where \(x_b=b+b^{-1}\).
Since \(M(f_C)\sim |C|\) by the exterior scaling, this proves the asserted limit at one.

<a id="recover"></a>
## The polynomial quotient and the two-trace formulas

The monic bounded-root factor \(I_C=f_C/L_C\) tends coefficientwise to \(D_0\).
In \(f_C=P-C D_0-(z-1)z^{m-1}E_C(x)\), the last term and \(I_C-D_0\) both have degree below \(\deg I_C\).
Consequently \(L_C+C=\operatorname{quo}(P,I_C)\) exactly.
Division by a monic polynomial is continuous in its coefficients at fixed degree, and cancellation of \(z^h H(x)\) gives the claimed quotient.
It determines \(G\) since for any nonzero difference \(J\) of two monic candidate polynomials, \(z^rJ(z+z^{-1})\) has degree \(r+\deg J\ge r\).

For \(r=2\) direct division gives the limiting polynomial \(z^2+(1-S)z+U-S+3\).
If \(G(2)>0\), the two simple scaled roots tend to \(\pm i\), so they are conjugate and their sum and product give the two formulas.
For the same-end real cases the exterior moduli of the target tend to one when \(M(P)\to1\), so \(T_1,T_2\to2\) or \(T_1,T_2\to-2\) respectively.
For a nonreal pair \(w,\bar w\), write \(w=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\) with \(|\lambda|>1\).
Since \(M(P)=|\lambda|^2\to1\), one has \(\operatorname{dist}(w,[-2,2])\to0\).
Substituting \(S=2\Re w,\ U=|w|^2\), the expression \(4(U-S+1)-(S-2)^2\) is \(4(\Im w)^2\), giving the last limit after the stated diagonal choice of \(N\).
