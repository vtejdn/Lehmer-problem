# Unique factorization of near-one polynomial Mahler values and the least degree realizing each value

<a id="statement"></a>
## Independent generators and the exact degree cost

Write \(\mathcal M=\{M(P):0\ne P\in\mathbb Z[X]\}\) for the multiplicative monoid of integer-polynomial Mahler values.
A value \(a>1\) is called an atom if it cannot be written as a product of two members of \(\mathcal M\) strictly greater than one.
There is an absolute \(\epsilon>0\) for which the atoms with \(0<\log a<\epsilon\) are multiplicatively independent.
For every such atom there is an algebraic unit \(\rho_a\) of degree \(d_a\) with \(M(\rho_a)=a\).
Every nonzero power of \(\rho_a\) has this same degree, with \(M(\rho_a^j)=a^{|j|}\).
The following assertions use this one cutoff irrespective of the number of factors, with ordinary multiplicities counted.

1. Every \(A\in\mathcal M\) with \(0<\log A<\epsilon\) has a unique expression \(A=\prod_{a\in S}a^{k_a}\) in distinct such atoms and positive integral exponents.
   It is the Mahler measure of a single algebraic number if and only if \(|S|=1\).
2. In any realizing integer polynomial, each positive-measure irreducible factor has measure equal to a power of one atom.
   For each \(a\), the exponents of the factors contributing that atom form a partition of \(k_a\), and **every** collection of such partitions is realizable.
   The possible counts of positive-measure irreducible factors range from \(|S|\) to \(\sum_{a\in S}k_a\).
3. The minimum possible degree of an integer polynomial of measure \(A\) is exactly \(\sum_{a\in S}d_a\).
   Up to an overall rational sign, a degree-minimizing polynomial is precisely a product, one factor for each \(a\), of the minima of units \(\zeta_a\rho_a^{\pm k_a}\), allowing conjugate embeddings, with \(\zeta_a\) a root of unity in the corresponding root field \(\mathbb Q(\rho_a)\).

Consequently the submonoid generated by all individual algebraic-number measures below the cutoff is a free commutative monoid on these atoms, also for large products if one considers only factorizations inside that generated monoid.
The small cutoff can be empty if there is a global gap.
The description does not assert a supply of arbitrarily small positive atoms.

<a id="generators"></a>
## Selecting one measure for each first active constituent

At a sufficiently small absolute cutoff below \(\log2\), any individual input of positive log measure below the cutoff is an algebraic unit by the two extreme coefficients.
Use the [compressed reciprocal chain](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent) of such a unit \(w\).
At its successive terms \(z_i\) the rational class spaces of all conjugates modulo torsion are \(V_{z_i}=\bigoplus_{h\ge i}W_h\), with pairwise inequivalent leading irreducibles \(W_h\) and strictly decreasing attached degrees.
The class of the positive exterior product \(M(w)\) lies in \(V_w\) and is nonzero.
Let \(W_i\) be its first active space, that is, its first nonzero projection in this order.
Then
\[
                                M(w)=M(z_i)^E
\]
for a positive integer \(E\).
Indeed compression preserves the measure, and at each preceding term the projection of its exterior product vanishes on the leading space.
Its exterior coefficients are constant on each oriented block, so passing to the next half product replaces that measure by an integral power of the next measure. [Lossless descent](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#line)
The vanishing projection of \(M(w)\) at the next preceding term implies vanishing there for the current value because the multiplicative exponent accumulated so far is nonzero.
No bound on the measure of the algebraic number \(M(w)\) is needed.

Consider all such chains together, using only finite subfamilies for relations.
Their [actual joint constituent decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules) identifies equal leading spaces as actual subspaces and identifies two attached terms exactly when their coordinate lines match up to conjugation, rational nonzero powers and torsion.
Different actual leading spaces are nonisomorphic, and one space occurring for several terms always has the same attached degree.
Keep the spaces \(W\) occurring as first active spaces of individual exterior products.
For one such \(W\), choose its attached term \(z\) and an embedding defining its root field \(K=\mathbb Q(z)\).
Modulo the roots of unity of \(K\), choose a generator \(\rho\) on the saturated rational line of \(z\) in \(\mathcal O_K^\times\).
The [unit-line saturation and compression](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields) give \(z=\zeta\rho^j\), \(j\ne0\), and every \(\zeta'\rho^l\), \(0\ne l\in\mathbb Z\), \(\zeta'\in\mu(K)\), has full degree \([K:\mathbb Q]\).
Indeed its class stabilizer is that of \(z\), and the number itself lies in \(K\).
Each matched term, after choosing the corresponding conjugate, belongs to the same embedded \(K\) and is another torsion twist of a power of \(\rho\).
Absolute height and full degree show that their total log measures are positive integer multiples of \(\log M(\rho)\).
Thus the positive number \(a_W=M(\rho)>1\) and \(d_W=\deg z\) depend only on \(W\); the choices of sign of the generator, root of unity and embedding have no effect.
Since \(a_W\le M(z_i)\le M(w)\), the value \(a_W\) is itself realized below the cutoff.
Every small individual measure is an integral power of its \(a_W\).

<a id="independence"></a>
## Independence even when the numerical products are large

In the common rational class space, \([a_W]\) is a positive rational multiple of \([M(z)]\) because of the full degree and the height formula.
It has nonzero projection on \(W\), by selection of that first active space and the exact power descent.
All its other components, if any, are attached to strictly smaller degrees since \([M(z)]\in V_z\).
Given any proposed finite rational linear relation among the classes \([a_W]\), choose a participating \(W\) of largest attached degree.
Projection to \(W\) kills every other participating class, including those attached to a different space of the same largest degree.
It leaves a nonzero vector times the coefficient of the selected class, contradicting that this coefficient was nonzero.
The argument concerns actual classes and does not use a measure bound on a putative common product.
In particular the positive real numbers \(a_W\) are multiplicatively independent.

For \(0<\log A<\epsilon\) every positive-measure irreducible factor in any input already lies below the same cutoff.
Factorization of the input and the preceding single-number result therefore give a product in the \(a_W\)'s, and independence gives uniqueness.
No \(a_W\) can decompose in the full monoid \(\mathcal M\): both putative positive factors would be smaller still, and together would have to have the single exponent one.
Conversely a near-one product with exponent sum exceeding one decomposes within \(\mathcal M\).
Thus the \(a_W\)'s are precisely the stated atoms, with no duplication.
Every irreducible factor has contributed to only one atom, while every positive integral power of that atom is already realized by the minimum of \(\rho^l\).
This proves the realization, partition and length assertions and the freeness inside the indicated generated monoid.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Attainment of the least degree and its equality cases

For any irreducible input of small measure \(a^k\) its first active term has attached degree \(d_a\), at most the original degree by successive compression and descent.
If equality holds, the initial torsion class has size one and no descending step occurs, because each actual step strictly lowers degree.
Thus the original root itself, up to the choice of embedding, is on the matched line in \(K=\mathbb Q(\rho_a)\).
The saturated generator property gives precisely \(\zeta\rho_a^{\pm k}\) there; conversely all these units have degree \(d_a\) and measure \(a^k\).
At least one such positive-degree factor is required for each atom in \(S\).
Equality with the sum of their individual degree lower bounds permits just one per atom, no extra zero root or positive-degree cyclotomic factor, and only the overall sign for the content.
Taking one indicated minimum for each full exponent attains equality, completing the proof.

<a id="lehmer"></a>
## Unbounded minimum factor length if the gap fails

If there are unrestricted positive measures tending to one, then there must be distinct atoms tending to one, since an atom of a small product is no larger than that product.
For any prescribed positive integers \(k_j\) and any positive \(\delta_j\to0\), choose \(k_j\) distinct atoms of log measure below \(\min(\epsilon,\delta_j)/(2k_j)\).
Their product has positive log measure below \(\delta_j\) and requires at least \(k_j\) irreducible positive-measure factors in **every** realization, with equality attained.
In the [two-record fast-growth equivalence](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#records), the second record can likewise be taken smaller in measure than the first factor's atom.
The two factors then supply distinct atoms and their product has no single algebraic-number preimage under Mahler measure, even though its assigned growth and angular conditions remain available.
