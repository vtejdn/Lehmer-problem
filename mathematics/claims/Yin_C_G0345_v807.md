# Heights and circle conjugates of the exterior product on steep sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Three height regimes for the first Mahler iterate

Let \(S\) be any rational irreducible factor of the [quadratic section](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) with coprime positive exponents \(k,l\), where \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Write \(2n=\deg S\), let \(H\) count its exterior roots, and set \(\delta=M(S)\), \(\lambda=H^2/n\), and \(a_j=\log((j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2)\) for \(j=3,4\).
For \(u>0\), let \(Z_u\) be a sum of a Poisson number of independent jumps with mean count \(u\), each jump taking the four values \(\pm a_3,\pm a_4\) with probability \(1/4\) each.
Uniformly along arbitrary choices of factors, their absolute logarithmic heights satisfy
\[
 k h(\delta)\sim\begin{cases}
 \lambda(a_3+a_4)/4,&\lambda\to0,\\
 \mathbb E|Z_u|/2,&\lambda\to u\in(0,\infty),\\
 \sqrt{\lambda(a_3^2+a_4^2)/(4\pi)},&\lambda\to\infty.
 \end{cases}
\]
The log measure of the minimal polynomial of \(\delta\) follows by multiplying its height by its [exact degree](Yin_C_G0345_v806.md#products) \(D=2^H\binom nH\).
If \(p_0\) denotes the fraction of its conjugates on the unit circle, then
\[
                    -\log p_0\sim\lambda .
\]
Exactly \(D(1-p_0)/2\) conjugates are exterior.
In particular their fraction is asymptotic to \(\lambda/2\) in the sparse regime, tends to \((1-e^{-u})/2\) in the finite regime and tends to \(1/2\) in the dense regime.

<a id="sampling"></a>
## Sampling inverse pairs and their signs

Choose the exterior representative of each off-circle inverse pair and either representative of each circle pair.
Put \(x_i=k\log|r_i|\) for the chosen representative \(r_i\).
Of these \(n\) values, \(H\) are positive and the others zero.
By the [signed orbit and independence](Yin_C_G0345_v806.md#products), a uniformly chosen conjugate of \(\delta\) has scaled log modulus distributed as
\[
                  T=\sum_{i\in I}\epsilon_i x_i,
\]
where \(I\) is a uniform \(H\)-subset of the \(n\) labels and its signs are independent uniform signs.
The fixed rational sign in the exterior product does not affect this modulus.
As \(\delta\) is a unit and the signs give symmetry, \(k h(\delta)=\mathbb E|T|/2\).
By the [polar expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) and [mass allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth), \(H/n\to0\), and the positive weights separate into two classes of sizes \(b_j\sim H/2\) with \(x_i=a_j+o(1)\) uniformly in each class.
Consequently the first and second empirical moments of the positive weights tend to \(\mu=(a_3+a_4)/2\) and \(v=(a_3^2+a_4^2)/2\).

Let \(K\) count positive weights selected in \(I\).
Then \(\mathbb EK=\lambda\) and \(\mathbb E[K(K-1)]=(H)_2^2/(n)_2=O(\lambda^2)\), using falling factorials.
If \(\lambda\to0\), the difference between \(\mathbb E|T|\) and the expected unsigned weight is \(O(\lambda^2)\), since it vanishes unless \(K\ge2\) and all weights are bounded.
The expected unsigned weight is \(\lambda(\mu+o(1))\).

If \(\lambda\to u>0\) finite, then \(H\to\infty\).
The two selected class counts have joint factorial moments \((b_3)_p(b_4)_q(H)_{p+q}/(n)_{p+q}\to(u/2)^{p+q}\) for fixed nonnegative \(p,q\).
These are the moments of independent Poisson variables of mean \(u/2\), whose joint moment generating function exists near zero, giving convergence in law.
Independent signs and the uniform class approximations give \(T\Rightarrow Z_u\), since the total selected count is tight.
The bounded second moments \(\mathbb ET^2=(H/n)\sum_i x_i^2\sim\lambda v\) imply convergence of the absolute first moments.

If \(\lambda\to\infty\), put \(W=\sum_{i\in I}x_i^2\).
Then \(\mathbb EW\sim\lambda v\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(W)\le (H/n)\sum_i x_i^4=O(\lambda)\) by the nonpositive covariance of distinct inclusion indicators.
Thus \(W/\lambda\to v\) in probability.
Conditionally on \(I\), the characteristic function of \(T/\sqrt\lambda\) is \(\prod_{i\in I}\cos(t x_i/\sqrt\lambda)\).
Its logarithm equals \(-t^2W/(2\lambda)+O_t(W/\lambda^2)\) for large indices by boundedness of the weights.
The conditional characteristic functions converge in probability to \(e^{-t^2v/2}\) and have modulus at most one, giving the centered normal limit of variance \(v\).
The same second moment bound after scaling gives convergence of absolute first moments, proving the height formula.

<a id="circle"></a>
## Exact cancellation on the unit circle

Let \(R\) count real exterior roots and put \(C=(H-R)/2\), the number of complex conjugate pairs among the exterior roots.
For each off-circle label write \(e_i=0,1,-1\) according to its absence or chosen sign.
The squared modulus of a conjugate of \(\delta\) is the product of the off-circle representatives raised to \(e_i+e_{\bar i}\), where \(i\mapsto\bar i\) is complex conjugation on these labels.
Circle labels contribute one.
Multiplicative independence of the representatives forces \(e_i+e_{\bar i}=0\) at every exterior label for modulus one.
Hence real exterior labels must be absent, whereas a nonreal conjugate pair may be absent or enter with opposite signs.
Counting the signed subsets gives
\[
            p_0=\frac{[t^H](1+2t)^{n-H}(1+2t^2)^C}{2^H\binom nH}.
\]
By sign reversal, equal numbers of conjugates lie strictly on either side of the circle.
Let \(p_*=\binom{n-H}H/\binom nH\) be the probability of missing all exterior labels.
Eventually \(n>2H\), and expansion gives
\[
 1\le p_0/p_* =\sum_{b\ge0}\binom Cb 2^{-b}
             \frac{\binom{n-H}{H-2b}}{\binom{n-H}H}
 \le \exp\!\left(\frac C2\Big(\frac H{n-2H}\Big)^2\right).
\]
The upper bound uses the ratio of successive binomial coefficients as their lower index decreases, with out-of-range terms read as zero.
The product \(p_*=\prod_{i=0}^{H-1}(1-H/(n-i))\) gives \(-\log p_*=\lambda(1+O(H/n))\), and the logarithm of the displayed upper bound is \(O(\lambda H/n)\).
This proves the circle estimate even when \(\lambda\) vanishes.
