# Transferring small Mahler measure to units with few exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A relative norm with bounded total measure

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\), write \(D(w)\) for the multiplicative group generated by all conjugates of \(w\), allowing all roots of unity and all roots of products.
Write \(m(w)\) for the total logarithmic Mahler measure of its minimum polynomial.
Let \(k\ge1\).
Let \(\mathcal B_k\) consist of the nontorsion reciprocal algebraic units with at least one conjugate on the unit circle and at most \(k\) exterior conjugates, counting distinct roots of the minimum of modulus greater than one.
If \(D(w)\) meets \(\mathcal B_k\), there is an element \(u\in\mathcal B_k\) in the ordinary group generated by the conjugates of \(w\) such that
\[
                              0<m(u)\le 2k\,m(w).
\]
If \(m(w)\le \epsilon_k=\frac{1}{4(k+1)}4^{-(k+1)}\), the last bound improves to \(k\,m(w)\).
In both cases \(u\) belongs to a root field of a witness in \(D(w)\cap\mathcal B_k\).
There is no restriction on the degree or the measure of the supplied witness or on the radial rank of \(w\).

For \(k=2\) one may use just complex Salem numbers as witnesses and outputs in the small-measure implication, allowing the output to belong to \(D(w)\).
Here a complex Salem number is a nonreal algebraic integer with exactly itself and its complex conjugate outside the unit circle and at least one conjugate on the circle.
A sequence tending to zero among the units whose divisible conjugate groups contain a complex Salem number therefore exists exactly when a sequence of complex Salem numbers has total measures tending to zero.
The remaining units, whose groups contain no complex Salem number, satisfy \(M(w)\ge601/500\) through degree 55, where \(M=\exp m\).

<a id="detection"></a>
## Selecting the field by a nonzero correlation

For a common finite Galois extension write \(f_x(g)=\log|g(x)|\), and take the inner product of real functions using uniform probability on the Galois group.
Take a witness \(a_0\in D(w)\cap\mathcal B_k\).
A positive power of \(a_0\) is a product of conjugates of \(w\) with integer exponents up to torsion.
The same real linear relation for log functions, paired with \(f_{a_0}\), gives a nonzero inner product for some conjugate of \(w\), since \(\langle f_{a_0},f_{a_0}\rangle>0\).
Simultaneous conjugation preserves the inner product, so \(\langle f_w,f_a\rangle\ne0\) for some conjugate \(a\) of \(a_0\).
Enlarge the common field to include all elements used if necessary.

Set \(K=\mathbb Q(a)\), \(B=\mathbb Q(a+a^{-1})\), \(E=K(w)\), and write \(\tau\) for inversion of \(a\) on \(K\).
Reciprocity gives \([K:B]=2\).
Set \(v=N_{E/K}(w)\), \(e=[E:K]\); for each embedding of \(K\), the conditional mean of \(f_w\) over its extensions is the value of \(f_v/e\).
Consequently \(\langle f_v,f_a\rangle=e\langle f_w,f_a\rangle\).
Permuting the embeddings of \(K\) by composition with \(\tau\) negates \(f_a\); hence \(\langle f_{v/\tau v},f_a\rangle=2e\langle f_w,f_a\rangle\ne0\).
The unit \(u=v/\tau v\) is thus nontorsion and satisfies \(\tau u=u^{-1}\).
Both \(v\) and \(\tau v\) are products of conjugates of \(w\), since \(\tau\) extends to the common normal closure.

Write \([K:\mathbb Q]=2n\) and let \(l\le k\) be the number of exterior conjugates of \(a\).
Exactly \(n-l\) of its inverse pairs lie on the circle.
At the corresponding real embeddings of \(B\), the two extensions to \(K\) are complex conjugate.
Every relative norm-one element of \(K\) has modulus one above each such embedding.
Among the other \(l\) inverse pairs of embeddings of \(K\), at most one embedding per pair takes that element to an exterior value.
Thus \(u\) has at most \(l\) exterior conjugates, at least one neutral conjugate and reciprocal minimum, since its inverse is a conjugate.

<a id="cost"></a>
## Counting the extensions of each embedding of the original unit

Let \(A\) be the set of embeddings \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) such that \(|\sigma u|>1\), so \(|A|\le l\).
Then
\[
 [K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)
 =\sum_{\sigma\in A}\big(\log|\sigma v|-\log|\sigma\tau v|\big).
\]
Expand the first logarithm into the sum over embeddings of \(E\) extending \(\sigma\), and bound each summand by its positive part.
For a fixed embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w)\), at most \(|A|\) embeddings of \(E\) participate: their restrictions to \(K\) must lie in \(A\), and the two restrictions determine the embedding of the compositum.
This bounds the first contribution by \(|A|m(w)\).
The other term uses the set \(A\tau\) of the same size and the positive parts for \(w^{-1}\), whose total is also \(m(w)\) by the unit norm.
This proves \([K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)\le2l\,m(w)\).

For the small-measure refinement, the absolute height satisfies \(h(v)\le e h(w)\le m(w)\), because \(e\le\deg w\).
The fraction of neutral inverse pairs in \(K\) is \((n-l)/n\ge1/(k+1)\), as \(n\ge l+1\).
The [small-height relative norm theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#statement), with this last fraction as its lower bound, implies \(v\,\tau v=1\) at the stated threshold.
Now \(u=v/\tau v=v^2\) is nontorsion, and we may take \(v\) itself as the output.
Its exterior embeddings have cardinality at most \(l\).
Only the first sum of the preceding extension count is needed, so \([K:\mathbb Q(v)]m(v)\le l\,m(w)\).

<a id="limits"></a>
## The complex Salem case and escaping every fixed exterior count

If the supplied witness is a complex Salem number, its two exterior values form one nonreal conjugate pair.
The only nonneutral trace places form a single complex place of \(B\); in \(K\) the four corresponding embeddings come in two complex conjugate pairs interchanged by \(\tau\).
For the small-height norm \(v\), the exterior values are consequently a complex conjugate pair of nonreal values, or the same real value repeated twice.
In the first case choose an exterior conjugate as the complex Salem output.
In the second case change sign if needed to obtain a real Salem number \(\beta>1\); the neutral place excludes degree two.
The [quadratic lift](Yin_C_G0345_v72.md#lift) gives the complex Salem number \(\sqrt{-\beta}\) of exactly the same total measure in \(D(w)\).
Every complex Salem number is reciprocal: a unit-circle conjugate of an irreducible real polynomial is also a root of its reciprocal, so their monic normalizations agree and the original monic constant is a unit.
Thus the estimates apply in both directions of the sequence assertion.

For the degree-55 statement, any real Salem number in \(D(w)\) gives a complex one by the same lift.
Hence the remaining units are orthogonal to all real Salem conjugates by the [algebraic criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#algebra).
The [classification below the cutoff](Yin_C_G0901_v56.md#statement) leaves only signs and irreducible power substitutions of its degree-eighteen core.
The core is itself the minimum of a complex Salem number by its [trace signature](Yin_C_G0901_v55.md#certificate), and reversing those substitutions puts an exterior root in \(D(w)\).
This excludes every remaining unit below the cutoff in that degree range.

For any fixed \(k\), a positive lower bound on \(m\) over \(\mathcal B_k\) therefore implies a positive lower bound over all \(w\) with \(D(w)\cap\mathcal B_k\ne\varnothing\).
Explicitly a lower bound \(b_k>0\) on the first class gives \(\min(\epsilon_k,b_k/k)\) on the second.
Conversely each witness is itself an input.
If all the fixed-\(k\) bounds hold and unrestricted Lehmer fails, every sequence with positive measures tending to zero eventually has \(D(w)\cap\mathcal B_k=\varnothing\) for each fixed \(k\).
Whether those bounds or the bound on sequences escaping every fixed \(k\) hold remains open.
