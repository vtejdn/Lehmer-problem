# Three exterior conjugates comprising a real root and a nonreal pair

<a id="statement"></a>
## The measure and the interlacing powers

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with exactly three roots outside the unit circle, one real root \(\mu\) and a nonreal conjugate pair \(\lambda,\overline\lambda\).
Write \(q=m-3\), \(r=\log|\lambda|\), \(s=\log|\mu|\) and \(t=s/r\).
Then either \(t\) is irrational or \(t\in\{1,2\}\).

* If \(t=1\), all three exterior roots have the same positive integer power.
  The measure \(M(P)=|\mu|^3\) is itself a Salem number or a real reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one, of degree at most \(2m/3\).
* If \(t\) is irrational, every set of representatives of all \(m\) inverse pairs is multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
* If \(t=2\), one has \(|\mu|=\lambda\overline\lambda\) and \(M(P)=\mu^2\).
  The minimal polynomial of \(\mu\lambda\) is reciprocal of degree \(2m\), has exactly two exterior roots and has Mahler measure \(M(P)^{3/2}\).
  The inverse-pair labels split into triples that the Galois group permutes.
  For any fixed representatives the full lattice of multiplicative relations modulo roots of unity has one generator per triple, with three coefficients in \(\{1,-1\}\) and product value in \(\{1,-1\}\).
  The three exterior pair labels form one triple, so \(3\mid q\) and the neutral relation lattice has exactly \(q/3\) such generators.

In the irrational and \(t=2\) cases, raising all roots to the same positive power \(n\) gives an irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(P_n\) of degree \(2m\) and measure \(M(P)^n\).
For \(q\ge2\), a set of exponents of positive natural density gives one neutral trace in every interval cut out by the \(q-1\) nodes \(2\cos(j\pi/q)\), \(1\le j<q\).
These powers have partial torsion interlacing of the trace polynomial with exactly three traces unused, and \(n\log M(P)\ge\eta(3)>0\) for every such exponent.
The density of this neutral occupancy condition is \(q!/q^q\) in the irrational case and at least \((24q)^{-2q/3}/2\) in the \(t=2\) case.
For \(q=1\) the empty set of nodes works at every power with the unique neutral trace selected.
For \(q=0\) the real trace of \(\mu^n\) gives the empty-node condition at every power with two traces unused.

<a id="general"></a>
## An independent exterior logarithm and a common exterior power

Two facts apply more generally to a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(e\ge1\) exterior roots.
Its roots are distinct algebraic units and form \(m\) inverse pairs, permuted transitively by the Galois group of the splitting field.
None is torsion since some conjugate has modulus greater than one.

Suppose the logarithm of the modulus of one exterior root is outside the rational span of the logarithms for the other \(e-1\) exterior roots, listed with their individual multiplicities.
Choose arbitrary representatives \(v_i\) of all inverse pairs and suppose \(\prod_i v_i^{a_i}\) is torsion.
For each \(i\), send \(v_i\) to the distinguished exterior root by an automorphism.
Taking logarithms of absolute values in the transformed relation gives an integral linear relation in the exterior logarithms, with coefficient \(a_i\) at the distinguished one.
Each other pair contributes either zero or its own exterior logarithm with the appropriate sign and exponent.
Thus \(a_i=0\) for every \(i\).
In particular the neutral independence hypothesis of the [reciprocal power density theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#reciprocal) holds.

Alternatively, suppose all \(e\) exterior roots have the same \(N\)-th power for some \(N\ge1\), equivalently all their pairwise ratios are torsion.
The power map on the finite set of roots is Galois-equivariant, so every nonempty fiber has the same size by transitivity.
The exterior roots constitute one entire fiber by modulus, and their inverses another, both of size \(e\).
All other fibers consist of neutral roots.
The exterior fiber is closed under complex conjugation, so its product is real.
Multiply it by the sign making it positive and call the resulting algebraic unit \(\beta\).
Then \(\beta=M(P)>1\).
Its conjugates are the fiber products with that same overall sign.
The inverse fiber produces \(\beta^{-1}\), while any other fiber produces a number of modulus one.
Transitivity realizes the inverse fiber, so the orbit is invariant under inversion.
Thus \(\beta\) is Salem or a real reciprocal quadratic unit and its degree is at most the number \(2m/e\) of fibers.
This gives the common-power descent for any \(e\), extending the [two-exterior fiber calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#matching).

<a id="ratios"></a>
## Rational ratios of the two exterior logarithms

For the three exterior roots in the statement, irrational \(t\) makes \(s\) independent of the two equal logarithms \(r\) in the sense just proved.
Now assume \(t\) rational and take positive coprime integers \(a,b\) with \(a/b=2/t\).
The positive real numbers satisfy \((\lambda\overline\lambda)^b=|\mu|^a\).
Since \(\mu\) is real, the right hand side equals \((\operatorname{sgn}(\mu)\mu)^a\), giving an algebraic identity to which Galois automorphisms apply.
Send \(\mu\) to \(\lambda\) and take logarithms of absolute values.
The images of \(\lambda,\overline\lambda\) belong to two distinct inverse pairs, neither in the inverse pair of \(\lambda\) itself.
Among the remaining pairs just one has log modulus \(r\) off the circle and just one has log modulus \(s\), with either sign possible for a representative.
It follows that
\[
             2/t=a/b\ \in\ \{0,\ \pm1,\ \pm t,\ \pm1\pm t\}.
\]
For positive rational \(t\), the equation \(2/t=1\) gives \(t=2\), and \(2/t=t\) would give \(\sqrt2\).
The possibilities \(2/t=t+1,\ t-1\) give \(t=1,\ 2\) respectively, while \(t(1-t)\le1/4\) excludes \(2/t=1-t\).
The remaining right hand sides are nonpositive.

For \(t=1\) we have \(\mu^2=\lambda\overline\lambda\).
For a unit \(\alpha\) in the splitting field let \(L(\alpha)=(\log|g(\alpha)|)_g\), indexed by its Galois group.
Conjugate roots have log vectors of equal Euclidean norm, since the coordinates are permuted.
Now \(L(\mu)\) is the midpoint of \(L(\lambda),L(\overline\lambda)\) with the same norm as both of them, so the two endpoint vectors equal their midpoint.
Both ratios \(\lambda/\mu,\overline\lambda/\mu\) are algebraic units all of whose conjugates have modulus one, hence torsion by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
The common-power descent gives \(M(P)=|\mu|^3\) and the asserted degree bound.
For \(t=2\) the original equality of positive numbers gives \(|\mu|=\lambda\overline\lambda\), whence \(M(P)=\mu^2\).

<a id="triples"></a>
## The full relation lattice when the real logarithm is doubled

Continue with \(t=2\).
The relation \(\mu=\operatorname{sgn}(\mu)\lambda\overline\lambda\) has three distinct inverse-pair labels.
Every Galois translate gives a triple of labels carrying a relation with three coefficients of modulus one and product value \(\pm1\), for the chosen representatives.
To see that intersecting translated triples coincide, fix a shared label \(i\) and choose an automorphism sending its representative to \(\mu\).
At this embedding the coordinate at \(i\) has log magnitude \(2r\), and precisely two other coordinates have nonzero log magnitudes, both \(r\).
For the log sum on a translated triple containing \(i\) to vanish, the two other labels must be exactly those two coordinates.
Thus there is only one translated triple containing \(i\).
The translates cover all labels by transitivity and partition them into blocks of three.

Every log row \((\log|g(v_i)|)_i\) is supported on one such block, the inverse image under \(g\) of the original exterior triple.
For a relation vector \(a\) with torsion product, the row sums against \(a\) vanish.
In a block with labels \(i,j,l\), taking a row with \(i\) distinguished gives \(2|a_i|\le|a_j|+|a_l|\), and each vertex can be distinguished.
Taking a vertex of maximal magnitude forces the three magnitudes to agree.
For nonzero magnitude the signs are determined up to simultaneous negation by one row, because the two terms of magnitude \(r|a_i|\) must both oppose the term of magnitude \(2r|a_i|\).
The already constructed signed relation on this block has precisely those signs up to negation and has primitive coefficients.
Thus the restriction of \(a\) to each block is an integral multiple of its signed generator.
Conversely every combination of those generators has torsion product, in fact \(\pm1\).
Deleting the original exterior block gives the asserted complete neutral relation lattice.

<a id="ratios-of-roots"></a>
## Replacing the doubled case by two exterior roots at the same degree

Orient each block by choosing representatives \(x,y,z\) with \(xyz=\pm1\), which is possible by its signed primitive relation.
By uniqueness of that generator this choice is determined up to permutation and simultaneous inversion.
Take the six ordered ratios of distinct representatives in every block and form the monic polynomial \(Q\) on their union, counting each listed ratio once.
The list is Galois-invariant because simultaneous inversion exchanges the two orders of a ratio, and is closed under inversion.
All its values are algebraic units, so \(Q\) is integral and reciprocal.
These \(2m\) ratios are distinct.
Indeed, in the oriented coordinates each ordered ratio has exponent sum zero on every block.
The difference of two such exponent vectors could be a relation only if it were constant on each triple, by the full lattice calculation, and a constant of sum zero there is zero.
Equality of the vectors identifies the same ordered pair.

In the exterior block choose \((x,y,z)=(\lambda,\overline\lambda,\mu^{-1})\).
Its two exterior ratios are exactly \(\mu\lambda,\mu\overline\lambda\), each of log modulus \(3r\).
Their inverses are the only two interior ratios in the entire list, each of log modulus \(-3r\).
The two remaining ratios in this block and all ratios in the neutral blocks have modulus one.
The minimal polynomial of \(\mu\lambda\) contains its distinct complex conjugate, hence both exterior ratios.
Its constant has absolute value one since \(\mu\lambda\) is an algebraic unit.
The product formula over its roots therefore forces both interior ratios to occur as well.
In particular the orbit contains the inverse of \(\mu\lambda\) and is closed under inversion.
The Galois group is transitive on unordered pairs within the partitioned triples, since each such pair is specified by its single complementary vertex in its block.
The orbit consequently contains both orientations of every such pair, proving that \(Q\) itself is irreducible of degree \(2m\).
Finally \(M(Q)=\exp(6r)=M(P)^{3/2}\).
Thus an input sequence in this doubled case with \(M(P)\to1\) gives a sequence with two exterior roots and measure tending to one at the same degrees.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Equal-bin interlacing for both types of neutral phases

Whenever \(t\ne1\), \(\mu\) is the only root of its modulus.
The fiber of \(\mu^n\) under the \(n\)-th power map on original roots is a singleton, so every fiber is a singleton.
The \(2m\) powered roots are distinct and conjugate, giving the irreducible reciprocal \(P_n\) of degree \(2m\) and \(M(P_n)=M(P)^n\).
In particular its trace degree is still \(m\).
For independent neutral representatives the geometric-sum proof of [power equidistribution](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers) gives independent uniform folded angles on \([0,\pi]\).
For \(q\ge2\) the \(q\) equal bins between the nodes of \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\), with the two exterior intervals included, give exact neutral occupancy density \(q!/q^q\).
The nodes are the traces specified in the statement and \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\) is a monic integer polynomial by its Chebyshev recurrence.
The [other fixed interlacer and union bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#reciprocal) also apply in this independent case with three exterior pairs and fixed \(H\ge3\).

For \(t=2\) and \(q>0\), put \(u=q/3\) and let \(K\subset\mathbb T^q\) be the common kernel of the \(u\) signed triple characters.
Two coordinates per triple are free and the third is their signed monomial, so \(K\) is connected with \(2u\) free circle coordinates.
By the complete relation lattice and the block products \(\pm1\), the even powers of the neutral tuple are Haar-equidistributed on \(K\) and the odd powers on its corresponding translate.
Indeed a character nontrivial on \(K\) is outside the relation lattice, and summing its geometric progression gives zero limiting average on each parity.
Each coordinate projection of \(K\) surjects onto the circle, so all bin boundaries have zero measure on either coset.
The entire neutral occupancy event therefore has a natural density.

Apply the [odd-block bin construction](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#bins) with block size three and \(u\) triples.
Its triple assignment uses exactly the \(3u=q\) bins, with no additional unconstrained coordinates needed.
In units of \(\pi/q\), each assigned triple of bin centers has a signed sum \(\pm1/2\).
Correcting each coordinate by its sign times minus that sum divided by three gives zero signed sum with all entries still at least \(1/3\) from the bin edges.
The signs of the circle arguments can be chosen to give the prescribed block relation.
Vary each of the two free angles of each triple by less than \(\pi/(24q)\) about the constructed point.
The dependent angle varies by less than \(\pi/(12q)\), leaving all coordinates in their respective bins.
The box has Haar measure \((24q)^{-2u}\) on \(K\), so the even powers alone give the stated lower bound on density among all powers.

In both cases the selected neutral traces witness \(q-1\) torsion nodes, with deficit \(m-(q-1)-1=3\).
The [bounded-defect gap](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement) applied to \(P_n\) gives the claimed necessary lower bound on \(n\log M(P)\), including for the empty interlacer when \(q=1\).
When \(q=0\) the real powered trace exists regardless of the sign of \(\mu^n\), and its selection gives deficit two and the corresponding bound with \(\eta(2)\).

<a id="example"></a>
## An irreducible example in the doubled case

Set \(a=1+\sqrt2,\ b=1-\sqrt2\) and \(f(z)=z^3-a z^2+bz-1\).
Its conjugate under \(\sqrt2\mapsto-\sqrt2\) is \(-z^3 f(1/z)\), and their product is
\[
        P(z)=z^6-2z^5+z^4-8z^3+z^2-2z+1
            =z^3 S(z+z^{-1}),\qquad S(x)=x^3-2x^2-2x-4 .
\]
The cubic \(S\) is irreducible by the rational root test.
For a root \(T\), the norm of \(T^2-4\) in \(\mathbb Q(T)\) equals \(S(2)S(-2)=128\), which is not a rational square.
Thus \(z^2-Tz+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), proving irreducibility of \(P\).
The discriminant of \(f\) is \(-100\), so it has one real root \(\mu\), which exceeds one since \(f(1)=-2\sqrt2<0\).
Its nonreal conjugate roots have modulus \(\mu^{-1/2}\) by their product.
Their inverses supply the exterior nonreal pair, proving \(t=2\) and \(M(P)=\mu^2\) in this degree-six example.
