# The noncyclotomic cost of reducing the number of residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quadratic cost for two classes in a five-term family

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, let \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and set
\[
 P_p(x)=x^{2p}-x^{p+1}-x^p-x^{p-1}+1,\qquad L_p=\varphi^p-\varphi^{-p}.
\]
Suppose a nonzero integer multiple \(F\) of \(P_p\) occupies at most two exponent classes modulo \(p\).
In \(F/P_p\) let \(R\) be the product of all primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors other than \(x\), including multiplicities and choosing positive leading coefficients.
The empty product is one.
With \(m=\log M\), one has
\[
    L_p^{\,p-3}\ \bigm|\ |\operatorname{Res}(x^p+1,R)|,\qquad
    \deg R\log 2+p\,m(R)\ \ge\ (p-3)\log L_p .                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular if \(m(R)=o(p)\), then
\[
                  \liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\deg R}{p^2}
                       \ \ge\ \frac{\log\varphi}{\log2}.
\]
The bound allows arbitrary row heights, cyclotomic factors, common row factors and nonreciprocal multiples.
Two-class multiples do exist for every such prime, with monic reciprocal unit witnesses of degree at most \(p(2p+2)+1\).
Their existence supplies no upper bound on \(m(R)\).
The [five-term input](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) itself has multiplicative measure tending to a fixed value strictly between one and the plastic number.

<a id="local"></a>
## Valuations at different conjugates of a root of unity

The following local estimate applies to other polynomials and higher row counts.
Take an odd prime \(p\), a sign \(\epsilon\), a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\), and \(x_j=\epsilon\zeta^j\) for \(1\le j<p\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be nonzero at these points.
Let \(F=PC R\ne0\) occupy at most \(k<p\) classes, where \(R\in\mathbb Z[x]\) has no roots of unity and \(C\) is a nonzero integer times a monomial and a product of cyclotomic polynomials.
For a prime \(\ell\ne p\) fix an extension \(v\) of the \(\ell\)-adic valuation with \(v(\ell)=1\).
Set \(w_j=v(P(x_j))\).
For any progression \(A\) of \(k\) consecutive positions of nonzero step in \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) avoiding zero, put \(b_A=\min_{j\in A}w_j\).
Then simultaneously for all \(1\le j<p\),
\[
                        v(R(x_j))\ \ge\ (b_A-w_j)_+ .                    \tag{2}
\]
Here \(t_+=\max(t,0)\).
One may maximize \(b_A\) over the indicated progressions.
For \(k=2\), it is the second largest of the \(p-1\) valuations with multiplicities, since any two distinct indices form a progression of length two.

To prove (2), first remove all common factors \(x^p-\epsilon\) from \(F\) that divide its rows in the base variable at \(X=\epsilon\).
These factors belong to \(C\) if \(P(\epsilon)\ne0\).
In general \(P\) might vanish at \(\epsilon\), but none of its factors is the minimal polynomial of \(x_j\).
For the proof in that case first move every factor \(x-\epsilon\) of \(P\) into \(C\).
Its values \(x_j-\epsilon\) are \(\ell\)-adic units, so all the \(w_j\) stay the same.
Now division by \(x^p-\epsilon\) changes only \(C\), preserves the set of rows and eventually makes their evaluations at \(\epsilon\) not all zero.
We use \(F,C,P\) for the resulting polynomials.
None of the \(F(x_j)\) vanishes.
Indeed one zero would imply all \(p-1\) conjugate zeros.
The polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\) formed by the row evaluations with coefficients multiplied by \(\epsilon^r\) would be a nonzero multiple of \(1+z+\cdots+z^{p-1}\).
That would require all \(p\) classes.

The values \(C(x_j)\) all have the same valuation \(c_C\).
For a cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_m\) in \(C\), any two \(x_j\) of this order are related by raising to a positive power \(t\) coprime to both their order and \(m\).
Such a lift of the prescribed unit modulo their order exists by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Raising to \(t\) permutes the primitive \(m\)-th roots \(\eta\).
For \(x_j\ne\eta\), the quotients \((x_j^t-\eta^t)/(x_j-\eta)\) are algebraic units.
Indeed the geometric sum gives integrality, and applying an inverse exponent modulo the joint order gives the integral inverse.
This proves valuation equality after multiplication over \(\eta\), and a scalar or monomial contributes a constant valuation as well.

Write the remaining rows as \(U_r\) and let \(c=\min_r v(U_r(\epsilon))\), ignoring zero evaluations.
At all \(j\) the valuation \(v(F(x_j))\) is at least \(c\).
After dividing the coefficient vector by one coefficient of valuation \(c\) and reducing in the residue field, some coefficient survives.
The reductions of \(\zeta^j\) remain distinct since \(\ell\ne p\).
If every value on \(A\) vanished after this normalization, the surviving \(s\le k\) coefficients would solve a nonsingular homogeneous Vandermonde system on the first \(s\) positions of \(A\), as in the [coset zero test](Yin_C_G0345_v355.md#coset).
The bound on the weight of this residual word is the classical [BCH bound](../audits/literature/DL24/Yin_U001.md#dl24-u001).
Thus some \(i\in A\) has \(v(F(x_i))=c\), whence \(c-c_C=w_i+v(R(x_i))\ge b_A\).
Every \(j\) now satisfies \(v(R(x_j))\ge c-c_C-w_j\) and \(v(R(x_j))\ge0\), proving (2).

<a id="lucas"></a>
## The deficit at the Lucas primes

For \(P=P_p,\ \epsilon=-1\), one has \(P_p(-\zeta^j)=3-\zeta^j-\zeta^{-j}\).
Let \(S_p\) be the monic polynomial with roots \(\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j}\), one for each pair \(j,-j\).
From \(\Phi_p(y)=y^{(p-1)/2}S_p(y+y^{-1})\), setting \(y=\varphi^2\) gives
\[
 S_p(3)=\frac{\varphi^p-\varphi^{-p}}{\varphi-\varphi^{-1}}=L_p,
 \qquad \prod_{j=1}^{p-1}P_p(-\zeta^j)=L_p^2.
\]
In particular \(L_p\) is a positive integer and \(L_p\equiv1\bmod p\), by the [trace polynomial reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v355.md#family).
At any prime \(\ell\mid L_p\), exactly two indices \(j,-j\) give positive valuations, since their reductions must solve \(z^2-3z+1=0\), the grid points are distinct and the paired values are equal.
Their common valuation is \(v_\ell(L_p)\) by the product identity.
Use these indices as \(A\) in (2).
At the other \(p-3\) primitive positions, \(v(R(-\zeta^i))\ge v_\ell(L_p)\).
The product of the \(p-1\) evaluations is an integer, and the remaining factor \(R(-1)\) in the resultant with \(x^p+1\) is a nonzero integer.
Taking all \(\ell\mid L_p\) proves the divisibility in (1).
This charges the deficit to the noncyclotomic part even when the discarded cyclotomic degree is unbounded.

For every complex zero \(z\) of \(R\), \(|z^p+1|\le2\max(1,|z|)^p\).
Multiplication over the zeros and inclusion of the \(p\)-th power of the absolute leading coefficient bound the absolute resultant by \(2^{\deg R}\exp(p\,m(R))\).
This gives the second inequality in (1), and \(\log L_p=p\log\varphi+\log(1-\varphi^{-2p})\) gives the limit.

<a id="existence"></a>
## Why two-class witnesses still exist

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(P_p\) and put \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
The identity \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}=\beta+\beta^{-1}-1\) makes \(\alpha\) at most quadratic over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
In degree two the other conjugate over that field would be \(\alpha^{-1}\), forcing \(\beta=\beta^{-1}\) and making \(\alpha\) torsion.
The [irreducible nontorsion input](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) therefore has \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The [integral interpolation construction](Yin_C_G0345_v354.md#construction) already gives a monic reciprocal unit witness of the stated degree before removing its common row factor.
The lower bound (1) quantifies the cost of allowing additional nontorsion roots in this particular family.
