# Simultaneous dual moment constraints for integral interlacing

<a id="statement"></a>
## A residue and positivity test

Fix integers \(q,n\ge2\), put \(M=q(q+1)\), \(h=1/M\), and prescribe a positive integer \(K\equiv1\bmod M\).
For a grid \(-4=t_0<\cdots<t_{n-1}=0\), set \(Q=\prod_i(x-t_i)\), \(D=\prod_i(1-Mt_i)\), and \(B=1-K/D\).
We seek a monic \(H\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(n\) with \(M^nH(h)=K\) and \((-1)^{n-i}H(t_i)>0\) at every node.
If \(B\le0\) no solution exists.
The following tests concern \(B>0\).

When \(Q\in\mathbb Z[x]\), put \(R=(D-K)/M\) and \(c=(q+1)/q\bmod D\).
For every \(1\le j<n\) independently, take the least absolute integer residue
\[
 w_j=\operatorname{center}_D\bigl(R(c^j+c^{-j})\bigr).
\]
Either choice at a tie works.
Let \(\phi_j(x)=2T_j((x+2)/2)\), and define a linear functional \(L\) on \(\mathbb R[x]/(Q)\) by \(L(1)=B\) and \(L(\phi_j)=Mw_j/D\) for these \(j\).
The full integral realization is equivalent to **positive definiteness** of the single rational matrix
\[
               \bigl(L(x^{a+b}\bmod Q)\bigr)_{0\le a,b<n}.                 \tag{1}
\]
This test takes its residue data from the grid polynomial and pole alone, without assuming any unknown coefficient of \(H\).
A successful matrix determines the unique integral polynomial.

For arbitrary real grids the same dual method reconstructs an integer candidate in blocks of at least \(M/2-1\) quotient coefficients, with the last block truncated to the remaining degree.
The observations in a block can be evaluated simultaneously once the previous blocks are fixed.
A degree-independent block length \(s+1\) is also available whenever \(s\ge1\) is an integer and
\[
              6+4\sqrt{s\log(8\lceil\pi s^2\rceil)}\le M.                \tag{2}
\]
In particular for large \(M\) the permitted block length is at least a positive constant times \(M^2/\log M\).
The final strict sign checks decide realization for these general grids using exact real comparisons; rational or real algebraic input supports effective comparisons.

<a id="duality"></a>
## Dual coordinates on the quotient lattice

Use \(P_0=x^n+((K-1)/M)x^{n-1}\) as an integral particular polynomial.
The [pole lattice and interpolation](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#simplex) give \(H=P_0+(Mx-1)G\) with \(\deg G\le n-2\); this parametrization for the stated \(K\) follows likewise by primitive division.
For every such real \(H\), write \(b_i(H)=H(t_i)/((t_i-h)Q'(t_i))\).
The interpolation formula gives \(\sum b_i=B\), and interlacing requires every \(b_i>0\).
Put \(\ell(U)=[x^{n-1}](U\bmod Q)\) and \(A_f=\sum_i f(t_i)b_i(P_0)\).
Lagrange interpolation gives \(\ell(U)=\sum_i U(t_i)/Q'(t_i)\).
Consequently
\[
              \sum_i b_i(H)f(t_i)=A_f+M\ell(fG).                         \tag{3}
\]
For monic \(f_j\) of degree \(j\), the row \(\ell(f_j x^{n-1-i})\), \(1\le i<n\), vanishes for \(i>j\) and has diagonal entry one.
If \(Q\) is integral, taking \(f_j=\phi_j\) makes these rows integral and hence unimodular, as the \(\phi_j\) are monic integral for \(j\ge1\).
Given one representative of each \(A_{\phi_j}+M\mathbb Z\), (3) therefore gives exactly one integral \(G\), before checking signs.
For positive weights the moments have absolute value at most \(2B<M/2\), so only the centered representatives are possible.

<a id="residues"></a>
## Computing every representative directly

Multiplication by \(1-Mx\) on \(\mathbb Z[x]/(Q)\) has determinant \(D\), by evaluation on the distinct roots.
It follows that \(D\) is integral, \(D\equiv1\bmod M\), and \(D/(Mx-1)\) in the quotient ring over \(\mathbb Q\) has integral polynomial coordinates by the adjugate formula.
Here \(D>K>0\).
For integral \(f\) of degree \(j\), \(A_f/M=\ell(fP_0/(Mx-1))\), using the same Lagrange formula in the quotient algebra.
The polynomial \(M^j(f(x)-f(h))\) is divisible in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) by \(Mx-1\), so
\[
 D A_f/M\equiv f(M^{-1})\,D A_1/M \pmod D .
\]
Indeed \(D A_f/M\) is integral; multiply the asserted congruence by the invertible \(M^j\) and use the integral quotient.
Since \(A_1=B\), \(D A_1/M=R\).
The identity \(2+h=(q+1)/q+q/(q+1)\) and the Chebyshev recurrence give \(\phi_j(M^{-1})=c^j+c^{-j}\bmod D\).
This proves the formula for the centered representatives.
With the integral candidate determined by (3), its moment functional is \(L\), so (1) is the product of the transposed node Vandermonde, the diagonal matrix of the \(b_i\), and the node Vandermonde itself (with rows indexed by nodes).
Invertibility of this Vandermonde proves the equivalence, including all strict inequalities.

At \(q=2\) on nodes \((-4,-3,0)\) one has \(D=475\).
For \(K=25\) the three moments \(L(1),L(\phi_1),L(\phi_2)\) are \(18/19,-18/19,-18/19\).
Both nonconstant scalar bounds \(|L(\phi_j)|<2B\) hold, but \(L\) is \(18/19\) times evaluation at \(-3\), giving rank one and excluding the target.
For the comparison value \(K=109\), the moments are \(366/475,318/475,432/475\), and the leading principal minors in (1) are \(366/475,36/25,432/475\).
The recovered polynomial \(x(x+4)(x+2)-1\) has all the prescribed signs and pole value.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Simultaneous tests on arbitrary real nodes

Index the quotient coordinates by \(G=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}g_i x^{n-1-i}\).
Suppose \(g_i\) for \(i<r\) have been fixed and let \(P_r\) be \(P_0\) plus the corresponding pole multiples.
For each index \(j\) in the next block, start with \(\phi_j\) and acquire a monic polynomial
\[
 f_j=\phi_j+\sum_{i=r}^{j-1} e_i\phi_i
\]
by considering \(i=j-1,\ldots,r\) in descending order.
At step \(i\), add \(e_i\) so that the row entry \(\ell(f_jx^{n-1-i})\) is an integer.
The diagonal and vanishing entries proved above make this a translation by \(e_i\) and preserve the entries already rounded.
Nearest-integer rounding gives \(|e_i|\le1/2\), so the oscillation of \(f_j\) on the grid is at most \(4+2(j-r)\).
This is at most \(M\) throughout a block of length \(M/2-1\).

Write \(A_{r,j}=\sum_i b_i(P_r) f_j(t_i)\).
For a solution, \(\sum_{i=r}^j\ell(f_jx^{n-1-i})g_i\) is an integer in the interval
\[
 \frac{B[\min_i f_j(t_i),\max_i f_j(t_i)]-A_{r,j}}{M}.
\]
Its length is less than one since \(B<1\).
All these intervals use the same \(P_r\) and no coordinate of the current block.
A missing integer rejects this grid, while the selected integers give the block by a triangular integral solve.
Once all blocks pass, strict positivity of the weights is necessary and sufficient for the candidate to work.

<a id="longer"></a>
## A uniform choice of longer rows

For larger blocks the two adjacent integers at each rounding step may be chosen with probabilities making \(e_i\) conditionally mean zero, given the previous choices in descending order.
At an integral entry take \(e_i=0\).
Each conditional range has length at most one, and \(|e_i|\le1\).
Write \(d=j-r>0\) and \(Z(\theta)=\sum_{a=0}^{d-1} e_{r+a}\exp(ia\theta)\).
The added trigonometric polynomial is \(2\operatorname{Re}(\exp(ir\theta)Z(\theta))\).
At a fixed angle the real or imaginary part has two-sided tail at most \(2\exp(-2u^2/d)\) at \(u>0\).
Indeed the [one-variable exponential-moment bound](../audits/literature/Hoe63/Yin_U001.md#independent-sum), applied conditionally, bounds each factor by \(\exp(v^2/8)\) at parameter \(v\), since the corresponding real multiplier is at most one in absolute value.
Iteration of conditional expectation and exponential Markov with \(v=4u/d\) give the stated tail, without requiring independent rounding errors.

At \(J=\lceil\pi d^2\rceil\) equally spaced angles, the union bound gives a choice for which both parts are bounded in absolute value by \(\sqrt{d\log(8J)/2}\) at every sample.
Indeed the failure probability is at most \(1/2\).
On the full circle \(|Z'|\le d(d-1)/2\); nearest-sample displacement at most \(\pi/J\) thus adds at most \(1/2\) to the modulus.
Consequently \(\sup|Z|\le\sqrt{d\log(8J)}+1/2\).
The grid oscillation of \(f_j\) is at most \(6+4\sqrt{d\log(8J)}\), since \(\phi_j\) has oscillation at most four.
For \(d=0\) the unaltered row suffices.
Condition (2) applies to every \(d\le s\), and the rows may be selected separately by searching the finite choices for a verified grid oscillation at most \(M\).
For example any sufficiently small fixed \(c_*>0\) makes \(s=\lfloor c_*M^2/\log M\rfloor\) satisfy (2) eventually.
The block conclusion follows from the same intervals and triangular equations.
