# Common factor residues on a finite curve intersection independent of the slope

<a id="statement"></a>
## A necessary congruence modulo the square of a prime

Let \(F=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U)\) be the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement).
For a prime \(p\) define the integral polynomial
\[
        J_p(U,V)=\frac{F(U^p,V^p)-F(U,V)^p}{p}.
\]
Use bars for reduction modulo \(p\).
In the geometric torus over \(\mathbb F_p\) let \(Z_p\) consist of the points on \(\bar F=\bar J_p=0\) for which
\[
    aU\bar F_U+bV\bar F_V=0
       \quad\text{for some }[a:b]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p).
\]
Then \(\# Z_p\le 8p\).
For every coprime positive \(k,l\), every factorization \(P=F(X^k,X^l)=ST\) into monic integral polynomials, and every common geometric root \(x\) of \(\bar S,\bar T\), one has
\[
            (x^k,x^l)\in Z_p,\qquad
            kU\bar F_U+lV\bar F_V=0
            \quad\text{at }(U,V)=(x^k,x^l).
\]
In particular the same finite set works for all slopes, including primes of bad reduction for the critical points of an individual slope.

For each fixed \(p\), define \(\mathcal D_p\) to be the positive primitive directions for which some coprime monic integral factors \(S,T\) with \(P=ST\) have \(p\mid\operatorname{Res}(S,T)\).
Outside a fixed finite set of primes, its upper natural density among primitive directions in \([1,X]^2\), as \(X\to\infty\), satisfies
\[
                         \overline{\operatorname{dens}}(\mathcal D_p)
                                  \ll_F p^{-2/3}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The bound counts directions for each prime separately.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Evaluating at roots of unity of order prime to the residue characteristic

Since \(P(0)=1\), the common residue root is nonzero.
Let \(\widehat x\) be its multiplicative Teichmüller lift in the unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) specified by the residue field.
Both \(S(\widehat x)\) and \(T(\widehat x)\) are divisible by \(p\), so \(F(\widehat x^k,\widehat x^l)\) is divisible by \(p^2\).
For any torus residue pair \((u,v)\) on \(\bar F\) with such lifts, the unramified Frobenius automorphism \(\sigma\) takes each lift to its \(p\)-th power.
Writing \(y=F(\widehat u,\widehat v)\in pO\), one obtains
\[
           J_p(\widehat u,\widehat v)=\frac{\sigma(y)-y^p}{p}
                                     \equiv\sigma(y/p)\pmod p .
\]
Thus the additional equation \(\bar J_p=0\) detects precisely divisibility of this lifted value by \(p^2\).
The logarithmic tangent equation follows from \(\bar P'(x)=0\) by the chain rule, and \(k,l\) are not both zero modulo \(p\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Proper intersection and its multiplicity

On every component of the reduced curve \(\bar F=0\), coordinatewise differentiation in characteristic zero followed by reduction gives
\[
 U(\bar J_p)_U=(U\bar F_U)^p,\qquad
 V(\bar J_p)_V=(V\bar F_V)^p.
\]
Indeed the subtracted terms \(F^{p-1}U F_U,F^{p-1}V F_V\) vanish there.
Both logarithmic partials of \(\bar F\) are generically nonzero on every component, by the [all-characteristic tangency exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#reduction) at the two coordinate directions.
That exclusion also gives reducedness, or this follows from the irreducible quadratic and the two distinct pairs of exceptional graph factors there.
If a component divided \(\bar J_p\), the two gradient rows would be dependent at its smooth generic point.
The ratio of the two logarithmic partials of \(\bar F\) would satisfy \(r^{p-1}=1\) on the component, hence would be a constant in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).
This would make a rational logarithmic direction everywhere tangent, again excluded by the same result.
There is no common boundary component in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), since the corner monomials of \(F\) have unit coefficients.
The total intersection multiplicity is at most \(4(2p)+2(4p)=16p\) by the two bidegrees.

At a smooth point of \(\bar F\) belonging to \(Z_p\), the nonzero tangent vector \((aU,bV)\) also kills \(\bar J_p\), by the displayed derivative identities and \(a^p=a,\ b^p=b\).
The intersection there has multiplicity at least two.
At a singular point of \(\bar F\) the same lower bound holds because its local equation has no linear part and the proper intersection with one other vanishing equation has length at least two.
These multiplicities prove the cardinality bound.

<a id="density"></a>
## Residue orders and the density of directions

For the density assertion it suffices to take \(p\nmid 2\cdot5\cdot575\cdot1039\).
At these primes the torus curve is smooth: its quadratic discriminant \(-4t^4+100t^2-575\), where \(t=U-U^{-1}\), has simple roots in \(U\), since its roots in \(t^2\) are distinct and avoid the branch values \(0,-4\).
At zeros of the quadratic coefficient its linear coefficient is a unit.
Thus each point of \(Z_p\) specifies a unique projective slope modulo \(p\).

At a point \((u,v)\) let \(h\) be the order of the cyclic subgroup generated by \(u,v\).
The direction must satisfy \(v^k=u^l\), a lattice condition of index \(h\), since \((k,l)\mapsto v^k/u^l\) has that subgroup as its image.
The rational tangent condition has index \(p\).
These two conditions are independent by the Chinese remainder theorem since \(p\nmid h\), and so have density \(1/(ph)\) among all integer pairs.
A union bound suffices.
Primitive positive pairs have positive natural density, so restricting to them costs only an absolute factor.

Uniformly in \(p\) the number of torus points of \(\bar F\) with \(h\le Y\) is \(O_F(Y^{3/2})\) for \(Y\ge1\).
Indeed take \(m=\lceil\sqrt Y\rceil\); the \((m+1)^2\) products \(u^i v^j\) with \(0\le i,j\le m\) include two equal values.
Such a point lies on \(U^aV^b=1\) for one of \(O(m^2)\) nonzero integer vectors with \(|a|,|b|\le m\).
Every geometric component of that equation is a torus translate of a subtorus with primitive integral normal, hence has a constant nonzero rational logarithmic tangent direction modulo \(p\).
The tangency exclusion prevents a common component with \(\bar F\).
The bidegree intersection bound gives at most \(4|b|+2|a|\le6m\) points per vector, proving the count even for nonprimitive vectors.
Summing in dyadic ranges gives \(\sum_{(u,v)\in Z_p,\,h\le Y}1/h=O_F(\sqrt Y)\).
Larger orders contribute at most \(8p/Y\) by the intersection bound above.
Taking \(Y=p^{2/3}\) and dividing by \(p\) proves (1).
