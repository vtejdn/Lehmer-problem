# Exterior roots and binary orbits of the five-term polynomial at a moving prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number of exterior roots in each irreducible factor

Let \(q\ge5\) be prime and \(k\) a positive odd integer with \(b=q/k\ge5\).
Put \(l=q-k\), assume \(k<q\), and write
\[
             P=X^{2q}-2X^{2q-k}+X^q-2X^k+1.
\]
The polynomial has exactly \(2(q-k)\) roots on the unit circle and exactly \(k\) exterior roots, all simple, together with the \(k\) inverse interior roots.
The exterior roots \(x_j\), \(0\le j<k\), lie one each over \(|U-2|<1/2\) on the branches \(x=\exp((\log U+2\pi i j)/k)\) of \(U=x^k\).
For an irreducible monic rational factor \(S\), write \(J\) for its set of exterior indices, \(e=|J|\), \(d=\deg S\), and \(m=\log M(S)\).
Every factor has \(e\ge1\), and uniformly in the parameters
\[
     k m=e\log2-2^{-b}\sum_{j\in J}\cos(2\pi qj/k)+O(e b4^{-b}).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(m\ge e\log(3/2)/k\).
There are at most \((k+1)/2\) irreducible factors.
For \(k=1\), \(P\) itself is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number, of degree \(2q\), for every prime \(q\ge5\).

Put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_{3q}(2)\) and \(I=2(q-1)/f\), the total number of binary Frobenius orbits at order \(3q\).
Let \(N(S)\) count those top orbits in \(\bar S\), and put \(\epsilon(S)=1\) if \(\bar S\) contains the order-three orbit and zero otherwise.
Then with absolute implied constants
\[
           \frac{N(S)+2\epsilon(S)/f}{I}\asymp \frac e k.               \tag{2}
\]
In particular, on any sequence with \(I/k\to\infty\), every irreducible rational factor contains an unbounded number of top binary orbits.
There are moving primes realizing this range for every prescribed sequence of odd offsets \(k\to\infty\).
At \(k=1\) the one rational factor contains all the orbits even along primes with \(I\to\infty\).

<a id="exterior"></a>
## One simple zero over each branch near two

In \(U=x^k\), the polynomial equation reads \(x^{2q-k}(U-2)+x^q+1-2U=0\).
Take the branch of the logarithm real on the positive axis in \(|U-2|<1\).
On each branch \(x_j(U)\) above, the equation becomes
\[
                 U-2+x_j(U)^{k-q}+(1-2U)x_j(U)^{k-2q}=0 .
\]
On \(|U-2|=1/2\) let \(u=|U|\ge3/2\).
The terms after \(U-2\) have combined absolute value at most
\[
        u^{1-b}+u^{1-2b}+2u^{2-2b}
           \le (3/2)^{-4}+(3/2)^{-9}+2(3/2)^{-8}<1/2.
\]
Rouché's theorem gives one simple root per branch, and these \(k\) roots and their inverses are disjoint.
On \(x=e^{i\theta}\), the real trace expression is \(2\cos(q\theta)-4\cos((q-k)\theta)+1\).
At the grid points \(\theta=j\pi/(q-k)\), its sign is negative at even \(j\) and positive at odd \(j\).
The \(2(q-k)\) consecutive open arcs therefore each contain a root.
Together with the \(2k\) roots off the circle these exhaust the degree and give simplicity on the circle as well.
Complex conjugation interchanges the branches \(j,-j\bmod k\).
Only branch zero is fixed, and there its unique exterior root is positive real.

Write \(U_j=x_j^k\).
The same estimate on the zero first gives \(U_j-2=O((3/2)^{-b})\).
Thus \(b|U_j-2|\) is bounded absolutely, so \(|U_j|^{-b}\ll 2^{-b}\) and substitution improves the bound to \(U_j-2=O(2^{-b})\).
On the segment from 2 to \(U_j\), the derivative of \(x_j(U)^{k-q}\) has modulus \(|1-b|\,|U|^{-b}\ll b2^{-b}\).
The last term of the equation is \(O(4^{-b})\) at the root.
Consequently
\[
                 U_j=2-2^{1-b}e^{-2\pi iqj/k}+O(b4^{-b}).
\]
Taking \(\log|U_j|\) and summing over \(J\) gives (1).

## Counting rational factors and their binary components

The [cyclotomic order test](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts) applies because \(q\) is odd and prime to three, and \(l\) is even.
Indeed the squarefree binary reduction restricts the possible orders to \(3t\) or \(6t\), \(t=1,q\).
After dividing the parent by \(x^q\), these two cases require \(x^l\) to have order four or six, respectively, whereas it has odd order.
Thus every irreducible factor has positive measure, and at least one exterior root, by Kronecker's theorem for monic integer polynomials of unit constant term.
All factors are real, so each one omitting the positive exterior root must contain a conjugate pair of exterior roots.
This proves the factor count.
At \(k=1\) the unique exterior root and its inverse have all their other conjugates on the circle, proving the Salem assertion.

The binary orders are exactly 3 and \(3q\), with simple roots, so \(d=fN(S)+2\epsilon(S)\).
The [height bound at all four gaps](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) and the [full binary period bound](Yin_C_G0345_v258.md#five) give
\[
             \frac{\log2}{6q}d\le m\le\frac{2\log7}{q}d .
\]
Each exterior root has \(3/2<|x_j|^k<5/2\), giving \(e\log(3/2)< km<e\log(5/2)\).
Combining these inequalities and using \(fI=2(q-1)\) proves (2).
In terms of a shrinking sequence, the [end-gap estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \(m\gg\sqrt{k/q}\), by combining \(d\gg\sqrt{qk}\) with the full period.
Hence \(q/k\to\infty\) on every such sequence, including one initially considered without \(q/k\ge5\).
Formula (1) then requires \(k\to\infty\), \(e/k\sim m/\log2\to0\) and \(N(S)/I\to0\).
The [moving-prime bound](Yin_C_G0345_v258.md#statement) also requires \(I\to\infty\).

For the supply of primes, fix an integer \(h\ge1\).
The [completely split primes](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#prime-pairs) in the indicated finite Galois extension are arbitrarily large, congruent to \(1\bmod 2h\), and have \(\operatorname{ord}_q(2)\mid(q-1)/h\).
Therefore \(f=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(2))\le 2(q-1)/h\), so \(I\ge h\).
For any prescribed odd \(k\) one can take \(h\ge k^2\) and choose such a prime \(q\ge5k\), increasing \(q\) at every step.
This realizes the simultaneous growth of the orbit count and its ratio to \(k\).
Taking \(k=1\) and instead increasing \(h\) gives the asserted irreducible family with unbounded orbit count.
