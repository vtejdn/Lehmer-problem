# Odd trace degrees mark the negative quartic root at even exponents and square offsets

<a id="statement"></a>
## A parity criterion on an infinite set of directions

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers with \(a\) odd, and put \(r=b/a\).
Suppose either \(b\) is even or \(b=6a+c^2\) for an odd positive integer \(c\) coprime to \(a\).
Remove all cyclotomic factors from the monic quartic section
\[
             -g(X^a)-X^b B(X^a),\qquad g(T)=T^4-T-1,\quad B(T)=1-T^3-T^4,
\]
and write the result as \(X^n H(X+X^{-1})\).
For all sufficiently large \(r\), precisely one monic rational irreducible factor of \(H\) has odd degree, and its reciprocal lift contains the unique negative exterior root \(-\sigma\).
In either family the following are equivalent.

1. The number \(\sigma\) is Salem.
2. The unique odd-degree irreducible trace factor has degree less than \(r\).
3. There exists a monic rational divisor of \(H\) of odd degree less than \(r\).

For any prime, the absence of a possible odd degree in this range among products of the modular factors, with their multiplicities, excludes condition 3.
Across these directions a sequence passing condition 3 with \(r\to\infty,\ a\to\infty\) gives \(\sigma\to1\).
Whether such a sequence exists remains open.

More generally, for all primitive positive odd \(a,b\) with \(r\) sufficiently large one can detect the negative factor by signed integral divisors of \(T=|H(-2)|\).
Every such integer arising from a rational trace divisor has absolute value a *unitary divisor* \(h\mid T\), meaning \(\gcd(h,T/h)=1\).
For a monic rational divisor \(f\) and odd \(a,b\), the indicator that its lift contains the negative exterior root is congruent modulo two to
\[
 \deg f + \#\{p\mid f(-2):\ p\equiv3\bmod4,\ v_p(T)\text{ odd}\}.
\]
Thus one can also mark the negative factor on a general odd direction using its degree and its behavior at specified rational primes.

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Rational subset products at the endpoint

For arbitrary high primitive slopes, let \(M_+\) denote multiplication by \(Y+2\) on \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(H)\).
For a monic divisor \(f\mid H\) of degree \(j\) the product of the corresponding \(2+y_i\) is \((-1)^j f(-2)\).
None vanish, since \(X+1\) has been removed.
The sign is negative exactly when the divisor contains the trace of the negative exterior root, for odd \(a\).
Indeed every real trace except that one lies strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), and every nonreal conjugate pair contributes its squared absolute value.
The [independent alternating actions](Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#independence) on the rational blocks show that a subset product of the \(2+y_i\) is rational precisely when it is a union of complete blocks.
A three-cycle supported on a partial block would change just one selected value, and the \(2+y_i\) are distinct and nonzero.
Each rational product is an integer and its complement gives another integer, so its absolute value divides \(T\).
The eigenvalues of \(\wedge^j M_+\) are exactly all such subset products, including irrational ones.
Thus for odd \(a\), the least \(j\) having a negative integral eigenvalue is the degree of the factor containing the negative root.
By the [individual degree and exterior count comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#counts), that root becomes Salem after sign change exactly when this least \(j\) is less than \(r\).

For any \(1\le j\le n\) it suffices to test \(\det(-hI-\wedge^j M_+)=0\) for the positive unitary divisors \(h\) of \(T\), as proved below.
The [powered-root bound](Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#precision) implies \(|x|,|x|^{-1}<4\), so \(|2+x+x^{-1}|<10\).
Vanishing over the integers is equivalent to vanishing modulo \(3^K\) for \(3^K>(T+10^j)^{\binom nj}\), by bounding the product of eigenvalue differences.
This range permits either parity of \(b\) at odd \(a\).

<a id="unitary"></a>
## The prime-power factors of the endpoint value

Take \(a,b\) odd, \(b>6a\), and put \(m=b-6a\).
The parent at \(1\) equals \(2\), and its factor \(X+1\) evaluates to \(2\) there.
The other possible cyclotomic factors are \(\Phi_{10},\Phi_{18},\Phi_{24}\), all with value \(1\) at \(1\), by the [quartic torsion table](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#orders).
Hence \(H(2)=1\).
The derivative of the monic parent at \(-1\) is \(6a-b\).
Dividing by the simple \(X+1\) and the other cyclotomic factors, whose absolute values at \(-1\) are \(5,3,1\), gives
\[
            T=m/(5^{\delta_{10}}3^{\delta_{18}}),
\]
where \(\delta_t=1\) when \(\Phi_t\) is present and zero otherwise.
For odd \(a\) and even \(b\) the only possible cyclotomic factor is \(\Phi_4\), present when \(4\mid b\).
Evaluation at \(-1\) gives \(T=1\) in that case and \(T=2\) for \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), so every divisor is unitary.

For odd \(a,b\), let a prime \(p\) satisfy \(p^2\mid T\).
It suffices to prove \(-2\) is a simple root of \(H\bmod p\), for then exactly one rational irreducible factor can receive any of the \(p\)-part of \(T\).
Primes of valuation one already have that property by integer divisibility.
Here \(p\) is odd, \(p\nmid a\) by primitivity and \(p^2\mid m\).
Put \(X=-Z\) and \(y=Z^a\), so \(y-1\) has a simple zero at \(Z=1\bmod p\).
Up to an overall sign, the parent in these coordinates agrees to order at least \(p^2\) at \(Z=1\) with
\[
                L(y)=y^{10}-y^9-y^6+y^4+y-1 .
\]
Indeed \(Z^b=y^6 Z^m\), and \(Z^m-1\) is divisible by \((Z-1)^{p^2}\) in characteristic \(p\).
The first nonzero coefficient of \(L(1+s)\) over \(\mathbb Z\) is \(20s^3\), from the binomial expansion.
Thus for odd \(p\ne5\) the multiplicity of \(-1\) in the reduced parent is three.
For \(p>5\) the removed cyclotomic product contributes precisely the simple \(X+1\).
At \(p=3\) the only possible extra contribution would be \(\Phi_{18}\), whose reduction is \((X+1)^6\); multiplicity three excludes it.
At \(p=5\), the terms of exponent \(10,9,6\) give
\[
         L(1+s)=s^5(2-(1+s)^4-(1+s))+s^{10}\pmod5 ,
\]
with order seven and leading coefficient \(-1\).
Here \(5\mid b-6a\) implies \(b\equiv a\bmod10\) by oddness and \(5\nmid a\), so \(\Phi_{10}\) occurs and reduces to \((X+1)^4\).
Together with \(X+1\) this accounts for five roots with multiplicity.
In all cases the reciprocal core has multiplicity exactly two at \(-1\bmod p\).
Since \(X+X^{-1}+2=(X+1)^2/X\), the trace has a simple root at \(-2\bmod p\), as required.
This proves unitary divisibility for every rational divisor and its complement.

<a id="parity"></a>
## The signature of each rational divisor at both endpoints

For odd \(a,b\) every monic rational irreducible trace factor \(f\) has \(f(2)>0\), since each real root is below two and the nonreal roots come in conjugate pairs.
The product of these integers is \(H(2)=1\), so every \(f(2)=1\), also for products of factors.
Hence \(f(-2)\equiv1\bmod4\).
Writing \(\chi_4(h)=1\) for \(h\equiv1\bmod4\) and \(-1\) for \(h\equiv3\bmod4\), this says \(\operatorname{sgn}(f(-2))=\chi_4(|f(-2)|)\).
By unitary divisibility each prime dividing \(f(-2)\) occurs there to its full exponent in \(T\).
Combining these two facts with the sign of the endpoint product proves the indicator formula.
Whenever every unitary divisor of \(T\) is \(1\bmod4\), it follows that \(\operatorname{sgn}((-1)^{\deg f}f(-2))=(-1)^{\deg f}\).
In fact the congruence and \(|f(-2)|\equiv1\bmod4\) force \(f(-2)>0\).
Exactly the block containing the negative exterior root has negative endpoint product, so precisely that irreducible trace factor has odd degree.
Every odd-degree divisor must contain it.

For \(m=c^2\), each prime \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) has even valuation in \(T\).
Only removal of \(\Phi_{18}\) could change such an exponent.
If \(3\nmid c\), then \(3\nmid b\) and \(\Phi_{18}\) is absent.
If \(3\mid c\), then \(9\mid m\) and \(3\nmid a\); the order-three calculation at \(-1\bmod3\) above applies directly and excludes \(\Phi_{18}\).
All unitary divisors of \(T\) are therefore \(1\bmod4\), proving the stated parity criterion.

For even \(b\) the degree \(n=(4a+b-2\delta_4)/2\) is always odd, where \(\delta_4=1\) for \(4\mid b\) and zero otherwise.
Since only one exterior root is real and it is negative, \(H(-2)=(-1)^{n+1}T=T\), and the cyclotomic table gives \(H(2)=T\in\{1,2\}\).
Each monic irreducible factor \(f\) still has \(f(2)>0\) and \(f(-2)\equiv f(2)\bmod4\).
If \(T=1\), every \(f(2)=1\) and hence \(f(-2)=1\).
If \(T=2\), all but one factor have \(f(2)=1\) and therefore \(f(-2)=1\), since their possible values at \(-2\) are among \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\).
The remaining factor must take value \(2\) at \(-2\) because \(H(-2)=2\).
Thus every \(f(-2)\) is positive in both cases, and odd degree again marks precisely the negative root.
When \(4\mid b\), every reciprocal irreducible factor has absolute value one at both endpoints.
In this subcase the parity also follows from [Gross and McMullen, Proposition 3.3](../audits/literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#unramified-reciprocal), since all other exterior roots occur in nonreal conjugate pairs.

The modular degree exclusion uses only monicity and reduction, so repeated modular factors may be taken with their true multiplicities.
The [pole limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#real) gives \(a\log\sigma\to\log\beta_4\) along the indicated growing slopes.
