# Exterior roots in six-term sections of growing seed degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound without a restriction on pole occupancy

For integers \(s\ge3\) and relatively prime positive integers \(a,b\) with \(a\ge2\), put \(r=b/a\), \(N=sa+b\), and
\[
 g_s(T)=T^s-T-1,\qquad B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s,\qquad
 F(X)=g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a).
\]
Let \(P\) be any monic nontorsion irreducible rational factor of \(F\), let \(d=\deg P\), and let \(k\) count its roots with \(|x|>1\), including both members of any nonreal conjugate pair.
Write \(m=\log M(P)\) for its complete logarithmic Mahler measure and \(h=am\).
There are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
              m\ge c\sqrt{s/r},\qquad
              \frac{k}{a}\ge c\,\frac{s^{3/2}}{\sqrt r}                 \tag{1}
\]
whenever \(r\ge C s(\log(2s))^8\).
The same bounds hold for every \(r\ge2s\) when \(h\ge\log(2d)\).

Consequently for every fixed \(B>0\), every sequence with \(s\to\infty\) and \(s\le r\le Bs^3\) satisfies \(\liminf k/a>0\).
The earlier [exterior-root count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#balanced) obtained this conclusion with a bound on the relative number of roots assigned to each exterior zero of \(B_s\).
The present argument covers factors concentrated at those zeros and also controls their complete measure by (1) throughout its stated range.
At slopes of order \(s^3\) the measure bound is of order \(1/s\), so a sequence of factors with \(k\) of order \(a\) and \(m\to0\) is still possible under these estimates.

<a id="radial"></a>
## Locating the roots that contribute large logarithmic radii

Throughout the proof take \(r\ge2s\) and \(R=r/s\).
For each exterior root \(x\) of \(P\), set \(v=x^{-a}\), \(L=-\log|v|\), and \(w=sL\).
The reciprocal identity \(F(X)=X^N F(1/X)\) gives
\[
             |g_s(v)|=|v|^r|B_s(v)|\le3e^{-Rw}.
\]
In particular \(|1+v|\le e^{-w}+3e^{-Rw}\).
Fix a sufficiently large absolute \(A_0\).
For \(w\ge A_0\) the last upper bound is at most \(2e^{-w}\), so \(|v|>1/2\) and \(s e^{-w}\ge s(1-|v|)/2\ge w/4\).
Here \(|v|\le1/2\) would give \(1/2\le2|v|^s\), and \(1-e^{-L}\ge L/2\) for \(L\le\log 2\).
It follows that \(|g_s'(v)|\ge s e^{-w}-1\ge s e^{-w}/2\).

There is an interior zero \(\zeta_j\) of \(g_s\) within distance
\[
                     D=12s^{-1}e^{-(R-1)w}
\]
of \(v\).
Indeed \(sD\) is uniformly small after increasing \(A_0\), and throughout that disk \(|g_s''|\le 4s^2 e^{-w}\exp(2sD)\), using \(|v|>1/2\).
On the boundary \(|g_s'(v)|D\ge6e^{-Rw}\) dominates the sum of the constant term and the quadratic Taylor remainder, the latter being at most \(2s^2e^{-w}\exp(2sD)D^2\).
Rouché's theorem gives one zero in the disk, counted with multiplicity.
The whole disk lies inside the unit circle since \(1-|v|\ge w/(2s)\).
Writing \(L_j=-\log|\zeta_j|\), the disk bound gives \(|sL_j-w|\ll sD\), hence \(w/2\le sL_j\le 3w/2\) for \(w\ge A_0\).
In particular
\[
                        |v-\zeta_j|\le 12s^{-1}e^{-rL_j/3},            \tag{2}
\]
since \(R-1\ge R/2\).

For \(A\ge A_0\), assign every root with \(w\ge A\) to its zero \(\zeta_j\) by this disk and let \(k_j\) count these roots.
Two distinct such points \(v,v'\) assigned to the same zero have
\[
         \frac{|v-v'|}{|1-v\overline{v'}|}
                  \le \frac{C_1}{sL_j}e^{-rL_j/3}
                  \le e^{-rL_j/4}.                                     \tag{3}
\]
For the first inequality use (2) and \(1-|v||v'|\ge 1-|v|\gg L_j\).
Choose \(A_0\) so that the second inequality holds for every occupied zero, using \(sL_j\ge A_0/2\).
No separation or assignment is needed for the roots with \(w<A\).

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## The discriminant after raising each root to the same power

Every root \(x\) of \(P\) satisfies \(\mathbb Q(x)=\mathbb Q(x^a)\).
Indeed \(g_s,B_s\) have no common zero, since a common zero would satisfy \(z^2+z+1=0\) and \(z^s=-z^2\), which are incompatible.
Thus \(x^b=-g_s(x^a)/B_s(x^a)\), and Bézout's identity for \(a,b\) recovers \(x\).
The distinct conjugates of \(x^{-a}\) consequently form one degree-\(d\) monic integral polynomial \(Q\).
The polynomial \(P\) has algebraic units as roots because \(F\) has leading and constant coefficients \(-1\).
The product formula therefore gives \(\log M(Q)=am=h=\sum_{|x|>1}L\).
The sparse divisor bound applied to the six terms of \(F\) gives \(m\gg d/N\), hence \(d\ll r h\) because \(r\ge2s\). [Dobrowolski's sparse divisor estimate](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) · [Application to these sections](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#count)

The discriminant of \(Q\) is a nonzero integer.
In its Vandermonde product fold each root \(z\) to \(u=z/\max(1,|z|)^2\) in the closed unit disk.
After extracting the Mahler factors, the product over pairs of \(|1-u_i\overline{u_j}|^2\) is at most \(d^d\) by successive boundary maximization and Hadamard's inequality.
Each pair strictly inside the disk before folding contributes the further factor \(|(u_i-u_j)/(1-u_i\overline{u_j})|^2\le1\).
Strictly exterior pairs give factors at most one as well, and pairs on opposite sides give equality in this factor.
This is the [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#collision).
Retaining the strict losses (3) on the interior pairs counted by each \(k_j\) gives
\[
              \frac r4\sum_jL_j k_j(k_j-1)
                    \le d\log d+2(d-1)h .
\]
Since \(\sum_j L_j k_j\le2h\) by the disk assignment, we obtain uniformly in \(A\)
\[
                    \sum_j L_j k_j^2\ll h(\log(2d)+h).                 \tag{4}
\]

<a id="moments"></a>
## First and second moments for an individual factor

For all interior zeros \(\zeta_j\) of the seed, the identity \(sL_j=-\log|1+\zeta_j|\) and the [Jensen estimate at \(-1\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles) give
\[
           \#\{j:sL_j\ge t\}\ll se^{-t}\qquad (t\ge0).
\]
Integration gives \(\sum_{sL_j\ge t} L_j\ll (1+t)e^{-t}\) and \(\sum_j L_j^3\ll s^{-2}\).
Suppose first that \(h\ge\log(2d)\).
In (4) the right side is \(O(h^2)\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz, the part of \(h\) arising from \(w\ge A\) is at most
\[
  2\sum_j k_j L_j
       \le 2\left(\sum_j L_j k_j^2\right)^{1/2}
             \left(\sum_{sL_j\ge A/2}L_j\right)^{1/2}
       \ll h\sqrt{(1+A)e^{-A/2}} .
\]
Fix \(A\) large enough that this is at most \(h/2\).
The remaining \(k\) roots or fewer contribute at most \(Ak/s\), so \(h\ll k/s\).
For the second moment, the roots with \(w<A\) contribute at most \(Ah/s\), and the others contribute at most
\[
     4\sum_jk_jL_j^2
       \le4\left(\sum_j L_j k_j^2\right)^{1/2}
             \left(\sum_j L_j^3\right)^{1/2}\ll h/s .
\]
Thus \(\sum_{|x|>1}L^2\ll h/s\).

If \(P\) is reciprocal, its interior roots contribute the same sum of squared logarithmic moduli as its exterior roots.
The [all-root square bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) now gives
\[
       \tfrac1{10}\le d\sum_{P(x)=0}\log^2|x|
                  \ll \frac{d h}{a^2s}\ll\frac{r m^2}{s}.
\]
If \(P\) is nonreciprocal, [Smyth's lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) already gives \(m\gg1\).
In either case \(m\gg\sqrt{s/r}\), and \(h\ll k/s\) yields the other assertion of (1).

<a id="small"></a>
## Small projected measure and the full range up to cubic slopes

It remains to handle \(h<\log(2d)\).
The degree is at least two since a rational unit is torsion.
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m>2/(\log(3d))^3\), hence \(a\ll(\log(3d))^4\).
Since \(d\le N=a(s+r)\), one has \(\log(3N)\ll\log(2r)\) by absorbing \(4\log\log(3N)\) into \(\log(3N)\) for large \(N\).
The [radial bound for every root](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#count) gives \(a\log|x|\ll\log(2s)/s\) for \(|x|>1\) when \(r\ge s\).
Consequently
\[
       m\gg\frac1{(\log(2r))^3},\qquad
       \frac{k}{a}\gg\frac{s}{\log(2s)(\log(2r))^3}.
\]
For \(r\ge C s(\log(2s))^8\) one has \(\sqrt{r/s}\gg\log(2s)(\log(2r))^3\).
Indeed \(\sqrt r/(\log(2r))^3\) increases for \(\log(2r)>6\), and at the lower endpoint \(\log(2r)\ll\log(2s)\).
With \(C\) sufficiently large these estimates complete (1) for all such \(s,r\).

For \(s\le r<Cs(\log(2s))^8\), the [unconditional count before localization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#count) gives \(k/a\gg s/(\log(2s))^{11/2}\), which tends to infinity.
For \(Cs(\log(2s))^8\le r\le Bs^3\), (1) gives \(k/a\ge c/\sqrt B\).
This proves the stated consequence across the whole range.
