# A necessary slope for factors with bounded exterior root count

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds for every rational factor

Let \(q_j(U)=U^2-jU-1\), let \(\beta_j\) be its positive root for \(j=3,4\), and put \(A=q_3q_4\), \(A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U)\).
Take positive integers \(l\ge k\) and a monic rational irreducible factor \(S\) of
\[
 P_{k,l}(X)=A(X^k)X^{2l}+X^{2k+l}+A^*(X^k).
\]
Write \(H\) for its number of exterior roots, \(d=\deg S\), \(W=\max(16,2l+4k)\), and \(C=\log(\beta_3\beta_4)\).
Then
\[
 H>\frac{k}{4\log 6}\left(\frac{\log\log W}{\log W}\right)^3 .          \tag{1}
\]
This applies with common dilations and without an asymptotic restriction on \(l/k\).
In particular when \(k\to\infty\) and \(k\le l\le k^B\) for fixed \(B\ge1\), every factor has at least order \(k(\log\log k/\log k)^3\) exterior roots, with a constant depending only on \(B\).

Suppose now \(k,l\) are coprime, \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
If a factor with a fixed number \(H\) of exterior roots exists along this sequence, then
\[
 \liminf \frac{\log s}{k^{1/3}\log k}\ \ge\ \frac{1}{3(2HC)^{1/3}}.     \tag{2}
\]
If \(\log s=o(k^{1/3}\log k)\), the minimum exterior count over all rational irreducible factors tends to infinity, irrespective of the residues of \(k,l\).
This includes the two [congruence examples](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth) as their slopes there are of order \(\sqrt D\) and their short exponents grow at least exponentially in \(D\).
An unbounded exterior count still allows \(H=o(k)\), the range relevant to small measures on this curve.

<a id="radius"></a>
## An absolute radius in the short coordinate

For an exterior zero \(z\) set \(U=z^k,V=z^l\) and \(r=|U|\).
Since \(l\ge k\), one has \(|V|\ge r\).
If \(r\ge6\), triangle inequalities in the two quadratic factors give
\[
 |A(U)|\ge\frac{17\cdot11}{36^2}r^4,\qquad
 |A^*(U)|\le\frac{55\cdot61}{36^2}r^4 .
\]
The curve equation would imply
\[
 \frac{187}{1296}r^4\le |A(U)|\le |U^2/V|+|A^*(U)/V^2|
                        \le r+\frac{3355}{1296}r^2 .
\]
After division by \(r^2\) the left side already exceeds the right side at six and the difference increases thereafter.
Thus \(r<6\).
The section is monic with constant term one, so \(S\) is integral and its log measure is the sum over its exterior roots.
In particular \(k\,m(S)\le H\log 6\).

All roots are nontorsion by the [torsion certificate](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
A rational root of a monic integral polynomial with unit constant term would be \(1\) or \(-1\), so \(d\ge2\).
For \(d\ge16\), the function \(x\mapsto\log\log x/\log x\) decreases up to and beyond \(W\) starting at \(d\).
[Voutier's theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) therefore gives
\[
 m(S)>\frac14\left(\frac{\log\log W}{\log W}\right)^3 .
\]
For \(2\le d\le15\), Corollary 2 of the same paper gives \(m(S)>2/\log^3(45)>1/(4e^3)\), which exceeds or equals the desired lower bound because \((\log t)/t\le1/e\) for \(t>0\).
Combining the two estimates proves (1).

<a id="inversion"></a>
## Inverting the degree bound at bounded signature

For fixed \(H\) the [two exterior pole masses](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#statement) give \(d/s\to H\) and \(k\,m(S)\to HC/2\).
In particular \(d\to\infty\).
Set \(y=\log d\).
Voutier's theorem at the individual degree gives
\[
 \frac{y}{\log y}\ \ge\ (1+o(1))\left(\frac{k}{2HC}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
It follows first that \(y\gg_H k^{1/3}\), hence \(\log y\ge(\log k)/3+O_H(1)\).
Substituting this back gives the indicated lower bound for \(y/(k^{1/3}\log k)\).
Since \(d/s\to H>0\), one has \(\log s=y+O_H(1)\), proving (2).
If the minimum count failed to diverge on a sequence with \(\log s=o(k^{1/3}\log k)\), a subsequence of minimizing factors would have the same fixed integer \(H\), contradicting (2).
