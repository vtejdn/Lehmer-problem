# The least measure through degree fifty-five with radial rank at least two in the entire divisible conjugate group

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum and its primitive core

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(w\), let \(D(w)\) consist of all algebraic numbers whose positive power is a product of integer powers of conjugates of \(w\) up to a root of unity.
For an algebraic unit \(x\) let \(r(x)\) be the dimension over \(\mathbb Q\) spanned by the real numbers \(\log|\sigma(x)|\) as \(\sigma\) ranges over its embeddings.
Use multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(w)\).
Among all nontorsion algebraic units of degree at most \(55\) satisfying
\[
                  r(x)\ge2\qquad(x\in D(w)\text{ nontorsion}),             \tag{1}
\]
the minimum of \(M(w)\) is exactly \(M(P)\) for
\[
    P(X)=X^{18}+X^{17}+X^{16}-X^{14}-X^{13}-X^{12}
                  +X^9-X^6-X^5-X^4+X^2+X+1 .
\]
It satisfies \(1.2193<M(P)<1.2196\).
Every nontorsion member of the divisible conjugate group of a root of \(P\) has radial rank exactly two.
The same minimum holds when only \(r(w)\ge2\) is required.
It also holds for the class of \(w\) for which \(D(w)\) contains no complex Salem number.
For each of these conditions, every such unit through degree \(55\) with \(M(w)<1.2196\) has minimum polynomial \(P(\pm X^k)\) up to leading sign, for some \(k\ge1\) with \(18k\le55\).
Conversely every irreducible substitution of this form in the stated range attains that minimum.

<a id="catalogue"></a>
## Excluding every smaller core

At \(1<M(w)<1.2196\) the minimum polynomial is reciprocal by [Smyth's nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
It has even degree since an odd-degree reciprocal or antireciprocal polynomial has a root at \(-1\) or \(1\).
Write the polynomial, after extracting common exponent factors and allowing \(X\mapsto-X\), as a sign and power substitution of an exponent-primitive irreducible core \(Q_0\).
The measure is unchanged under these substitutions since the product of exterior moduli over the \(k\)-th roots of each root equals its exterior contribution to \(M(Q_0)\).
If \(w^k=\pm z\) is a conjugate of a core root up to sign, then every conjugate of \(w\) gives such a relation and every conjugate of \(z\) occurs by extension of embeddings.
Consequently \(D(w)=D(z)\) and \(r(w)=r(z)\), since the distinct conjugate log moduli are scaled by \(1/k\) with possible repetitions.

The [complete primitive list](../audits/literature/Mos26/readme.md#complete-small-cores) covers measure below \(1.3\) through degree 44.
For \(46,48,50,52,54\) we use its listed [published minimizing polynomial at each degree](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree), as in the [degree-bounded orthogonality search](Yin_C_G0901_v56.md#exhaustion).
The [two exact Graeffe cuts](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#cut) place fourteen cores through degree 44 below the upper cutoff, all 930 remaining cores above, and every listed row at those five higher degrees above.
Of the fourteen, thirteen fall below the lower cutoff \(1.2193\) and only \(P\) lies between the cuts.

For five of those thirteen the [exact trace signatures](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#signatures) give one real exterior root, its inverse, and all remaining roots on the unit circle.
For the other eight there is one conjugate pair of nonreal exterior roots, their inverses, and all remaining roots on the circle.
These two classes are the signed real Salem and the complex Salem cases, respectively.
Both have exactly one positive log modulus and its negative, so the actual core root already has radial rank one and belongs to its own divisible group.
This excludes them under both radial rank hypotheses.
The eight complex Salem cores supply complex Salem witnesses directly, and each signed real Salem core supplies a witness by the [quadratic lift of the positive Salem number](Yin_C_G0345_v72.md#lift) inside the same divisible group.
In contrast, (1) for the candidate excludes every complex Salem number from its group since such a number has radial rank one.
This proves the asserted minimum for the condition of having no complex Salem witness as well.

## Two independent radial logarithms on the degree-eighteen group

The trace polynomial of \(P\), with \(P(X)=X^9Q(X+X^{-1})\), is
\[
          Q(Y)=Y^9+Y^8-8Y^7-8Y^6+19Y^5+19Y^4-12Y^3-12Y^2+1 .
\]
Its trace roots consist of one below \(-2\), six in \((-2,2)\), and one nonreal conjugate pair.
The [squarefree reduction certificates](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#frobenius) give degrees \(9\) modulo two and \(2,7\) modulo thirteen for \(Q\), and \(4,14\) modulo thirteen for \(P\).
In particular \(Q\) is rationally irreducible, and the lift is irreducible since \(t^2-4\) is negative at an interior real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(t)\) for \(Q(t)=0\).
The group on nine trace roots is transitive and contains a permutation of cycle type \((2,7)\) by Frobenius at thirteen.
It is primitive because any nontrivial block system would have three blocks of size three and its stabilizing wreath product has order \(6^4\), coprime to seven.
Complex conjugation transposes exactly the two nonreal traces.
The graph of all conjugates of this transposition has invariant connected components and at least one edge, hence is connected by primitivity.
Its edge transpositions generate \(S_9\).

In the splitting group of \(P\), take a Frobenius \(g\) at thirteen.
Both reductions are squarefree, so the action on the eighteen roots has cycles of lengths \(4,14\) while its trace permutation has order \(14\).
Thus \(g^{14}\) fixes every inverse-pair label and moves just the four roots of the four-cycle, interchanging them in two transpositions.
This flips exactly two inverse pairs.
Lifting all permutations of \(S_9\) and conjugating gives the flip of each pair of labels, so the kernel contains all even flips.

The signed permutation space on the nine oriented inverse pairs is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed the even flips distinguish each two coordinate characters, so averaging against one character projects onto its coordinate, and transitivity then gives irreducibility as in the [signed-action test](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs).
The space maps equivariantly onto the rational span of the actual conjugate classes modulo torsion.
It is an isomorphism since the image is nonzero.
Ordinary complex conjugation fixes the real exterior coordinate, negates each of the six circle coordinates, and acts by a signed swap on the two remaining coordinates.
The swap has square one, so it contributes one more fixed dimension.
Thus the fixed subspace has dimension two, proving by the [rational constituent formula](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules) that every nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) has \(r(x)=2\).
In particular the real exterior modulus and the common modulus of the nonreal exterior pair have multiplicatively independent positive values.
The root of \(P\) attains the asserted minimum, and preservation of measure, radial rank and the divisible group gives the stated classification after substitution.
The completeness and per-degree minima used here reach degree 55 after the reciprocal reduction.
