# Counting distinct Mahler values at a fixed exponent by increasing the contact order

<a id="statement"></a>
## A limit uniform over all exponents, including two

Let \(a\ge2\) be an integer, \(b_0=a^3,\ b=b_0+1\), and \(B=(\log2)/4\).
Fix a sign \(\varepsilon\).
At any integer \(n\ge2\) use the signed graph
\[
 G=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y)
\]
of a nonzero integral row of degree at most \(b\).
Call the row passing if \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^{(a-1)n}\).
For each passing row count the distinct positive logarithmic measures of the primitive irreducible factors of \(G\) having multiplicity at least \(a\), a nonzero nontorsion root \(\alpha\), and \(A(\alpha^n)\ne0\).
Let \(N_{a,n}\) be the maximum such count on one passing row, with default zero.
For a total degree bound \(D\), let \(N_{a,n,D}\) maximize the count of a selection with one factor per value and sum of the selected degrees at most \(D\).
Write \(K_D(B)\) for the corresponding largest cardinality of distinct positive irreducible measures of total sum strictly below \(B\) admitting representatives with sum of degrees at most \(D\), and \(K(B)=\sup_D K_D(B)\).

Then \(N_{a,n}\le K(B)\) pointwise, and, as \(a\to\infty\),
\[
           N_{a,n}\longrightarrow K(B)\quad\hbox{uniformly for }n\ge2.       \tag{1}
\]
A finite limit is attained at all exponents simultaneously for every sufficiently large \(a\).
An infinite limit means that every fixed count is attained or exceeded at every \(n\ge2\) for every sufficiently large \(a\).
In particular the tests with \(n=2\) alone suffice.
At each fixed \(D\), \(N_{a,n,D}=K_D(B)\) for all \(n\ge2\) once \(a\) exceeds a computable threshold depending only on \(D\).

More precisely, start from a nonempty list of distinct positive irreducible measures \(h_i\) of sum \(S<B\) admitting representatives of total degree \(D\le a\).
For \(a\ge2^{300}\), write \(\ell=\log(2a)\).
There exist reciprocal representatives of no greater total degree, fixed independently of \(a,n\), for which at every \(n\ge2\) one row has every required contact and
\[
       \frac{2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)}{(a-1)n}
                 \le S+1000(D+1)\frac{\ell^5}{a}.                       \tag{2}
\]
For representatives already given, (2) holds with those same polynomials whenever powering by \(n\) is injective on their combined root set.
The row is passing as soon as the right side is strictly below \(B\).

<a id="jets"></a>
## A uniform one-norm bound before division

The [small-budget reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) gives monic reciprocal unit minima for the given irreducible measures.
The [all-power norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) preserves each numerical value and does not increase degree.
For these representatives the combined conjugates remain distinct under every \(n\)-th power by the [cross-orbit calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#realization).
In the rest of the construction it suffices to have this injectivity at the chosen \(n\).
Write \(P_i\) for the representatives, selecting roots \(\alpha_i\), and \(Q_i\) for the minima of \(z_i=\alpha_i^n\).
The \(Q_i\) are distinct monic polynomials of the respective original degrees and measures \(n h_i\).
We use \(D\) also as an upper bound if a replacement decreased total degree.

Set \(t=a+\lceil\ell^5\rceil,\ N=2(b_0+1),\ L=n(b_0+1)\).
The [simultaneous integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#seeds) uses all exponents \(nj,nj+1\), \(0\le j\le b_0\).
For the first \(t\) divided derivatives at every original root, multiply the separate bounds on the numbers of possible tuples in the individual fields.
Their archimedean measure term is \(\exp(tLS)\) and their total degree at most \(D\).
Subtracting two equal jet tuples gives a nonzero integral graph \(F_0=U_0(X^n)+X V_0(X^n)\) divisible by every \(P_i^t\), and
\[
 \log\|F_0\|_1\le
 \frac{tLS+Dt\log(6N)+Dt(t-1)\log(1+L)/2}{N-Dt}+\log(2N).
\]
For \(a\ge2^{300}\), \(\ell>200,\ t\le2a,\ u=Dt/N\le1/a\), and \(L/N=n/2\).
The function \(\log^5(2a)/a\) decreases on this range, verifying the bound on \(t\) from its lower endpoint.
We have \(\log(6N)\le5\ell,\ \log(2N)\le4\ell,\ \log(1+L)/n\le2\ell\), the last by \(1+L\le n(a^3+2)\) and \((\log n)/n\le1\).
In twice the displayed bound divided by \(n\), the term involving \(S\) is \(tS/(1-u)\le tS+4S\).
The two remaining terms in the fraction contribute at most \(10\ell/a+8\ell\), using \(1/(1-u)\le2\).
As \(S<B<1\), this proves
\[
                        2\log\|F_0\|_1/n\le tS+20\ell .                 \tag{3}
\]
All original unit minima have degree at least two and satisfy [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), giving \(h_i>2/\log^3(3a)\ge1/(4\ell^3)\).
If only one row of \(F_0\) survived, its polynomial in the powered variable would contain \(\prod_i Q_i^t\), by unramified substitution.
Its log one-norm would be at least \(ntS\), contradicting (3) since \(tS>a/(4\ell^3)>20\ell\).

<a id="division"></a>
## Removing precisely the common factors at the prescribed nodes

Let \(c_i\) be the smaller of the two orders of \(U_0,V_0\) at \(z_i\), and take \(J=\prod_iQ_i^{c_i}\).
The derivative polynomial \(W_0=nY(U'_0V_0-U_0V'_0)-U_0V_0\) is nonzero by its least coefficient.
Dividing the rows by \(J\) extracts \(J^2\) from this polynomial.
At \(z_i\) the quotient differential polynomial has order at least \(\max(0,t-c_i-1)\) by the [local derivative calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention).
Indeed if \(c_i<t\) the quotient graph still vanishes at \(\alpha_i\), and local coprimality then makes both row values there nonzero.
The total order is at least \(t+c_i-1\) also when \(c_i\ge t\).
Comparing the integral product of these powers with \(\|W_0\|_1\le(2nb_0+1)\|F_0\|_1^2\) gives
\[
        \sum_i c_i h_i\le S+20\ell+\frac{\log(2nb_0+1)}n\le24\ell .
\]
Here \(\log(2nb+1)/n\le3\ell\) for the whole range.
Thus \(c_i\le96\ell^4<\lceil\ell^5\rceil\) and \(\deg J\le96D\ell^4\).
The integral quotients retain order at least \(a\) at every \(\alpha_i\), and both quotient rows are nonzero at each \(z_i\).
Any remaining common factor is harmless for these assertions.

For a polynomial \(E\) of degree \(k\ge1\) with a root \(r\), the [two directions of synthetic division](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v215.md#division) give
\[
               \|E/(Y-r)\|_1\le \frac{k}{\max(1,|r|)}\|E\|_1 .
\]
If \(|r|\le1\), solving from the leading end bounds each quotient coefficient by \(\|E\|_1\).
If \(|r|>1\), solving from the constant end uses only inverse powers \(r^{-j}\) with \(j\ge1\), giving the additional factor \(1/|r|\).
Applying these inequalities one root at a time, with multiplicities, costs at most \(b_0^{\deg J}/M(J)\le b_0^{\deg J}\) on either nonzero row.
This charges just the prescribed nodes.

Apply the [two projections and one common slope](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) to the quotient graph.
Reversing formally in degree \(nb_0+1\) preserves all the required reciprocal powers.
The two candidate first rows sum to twice the first quotient row, and the multipliers \(Y\pm1\) used to set the sign at width \(b\) do not vanish at the nontorsion nodes.
For \(s\) prescribed polynomials a slope among \(0,\ldots,s\) avoids vanishing at all of them.
The resulting one-norm is at most \(4(1+s)b_0^{\deg J}\|F_0\|_1\).
Since \(2/n\le1,\ \log b_0\le3\ell,\ s\le D\le a\), (3) gives
\[
 \frac{2\log\|A\|_1+\log(2nb+1)}{n}\le tS+288D\ell^5+26\ell .
\]
Subtracting \((a-1)S\) and dividing by \(a-1\) proves (2), with the stated absolute constant.

<a id="finite"></a>
## Exact finite comparisons and the limiting alternatives

At any \(a,n\), the [powered-factor product and finite field test](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#realization) give total measure less than \(B\) and total selected degree at most \(\lfloor2b/(a-1)\rfloor\) on every passing row.
The test, including distinction of numerical measures by exact real algebraic comparison, is finite.
Thus \(N_{a,n,D}\le K_D(B)\), and \(N_{a,n}\le K_{\lfloor2b/(a-1)\rfloor}(B)\le K(B)\).
Conversely take \(L_a\) to be the least integer with \(2^{L_a}\ge2a\).
For \(a\ge2^{300}\) and a list with total degree \(D\le a\), write \(M_*=\prod_i M(P_i)\).
A sufficient finite condition for its values to occur on one passing row at every \(n\ge2\) is
\[
                         M_*^{4a}\,2^{8000(D+1)L_a^5}<2^a.               \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(\ell\le L_a\), \(2\log2>1\), and taking logarithms in (4) leaves strictly more slack than the error in (2).
All comparisons in (4) use real algebraic numbers.

To compute \(K_D(B)\), enumerate monic irreducible reciprocal unit polynomials of degrees \(d\le D\) with coefficients in position \(i\) bounded by \(2\binom di\) in absolute value.
These bounds contain every positive measure below \(B\) in those degrees by the elementary symmetric coefficient estimate and the small-budget reduction.
Retain \(1<M(P)<2^{1/4}\), merge equal algebraic measures, and assign each value its smallest available degree.
Enumerate finite subsets of total degree at most \(D\), retaining precisely those with \(M_*^4<2\).
This exact comparison excludes equality.
If the largest cardinality is zero then \(N_{a,n,D}=0\) for all \(a,n\).
Otherwise take one maximizing subset, which has positive strict slack, and apply (4) to it.
For an explicit common onset, find an integer \(k\ge300\) with \(2^k\ge D\) and
\[
           M_*^{4\cdot2^k}2^{8000(D+1)(k+2)^5}<2^{2^k}.
\]
The search terminates since \(M_*^4<2\).
For \(2^j\le a<2^{j+1}\) one has \(L_a^5/a\le(j+2)^5/2^j\), decreasing in \(j\ge300\).
Therefore every \(a\ge2^k\) meets (4) for this subset.
The representatives used in (2) have no larger total degree, proving eventual exact equality at each fixed \(D\) uniformly over all exponents.
Every fixed finite list has finite total degree, so the finite or infinite integer supremum gives (1).
As in the [fixed-budget dichotomy](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#consequence), \(K(B)=+\infty\) precisely when the unrestricted Lehmer infimum is zero.
In either the signed test at \(n=2\) or any choice \(n=n(a)\), a count of at least two at even one parameter yields a primitive irreducible factor of multiplicative measure strictly below \(2^{1/8}\).

<a id="ordinary"></a>
## Ordinary integer polynomials and a smaller uniform error

There is a test with no powered-node condition.
Among all nonzero integral \(G\) of degree at most \(2a^3+1\) and \(\|G\|_1^4<2^a\), count the greatest number of different positive measures of irreducible factors of multiplicity at least \(a\), again allowing a default of zero.
One may require \(G\) to satisfy \(X^{\deg G}G(1/X)=\varepsilon G(X)\) for either prescribed sign.
Either maximum is at most \(K(B)\), since \(a\sum_i h_i\le m(G)\le\log\|G\|_1\) for one irreducible factor per value.
Both maxima also tend to \(K(B)\).
In fact, for every \(a\ge2\) and any given list below budget of total degree \(D\le a\), one can keep its original polynomials with the required sign and attain
\[
                         \log\|G\|_1\le aS+30\log(2a).                  \tag{5}
\]

Use every consecutive exponent from zero through \(E=a^3\), with \(N'=E+1\), and set \(t'=\lceil a/2\rceil\).
The same integral jet count yields a nonzero \(F\) divisible by each \(P_i^{t'}\) and
\[
 \log\|F\|_1\le
 \frac{t'ES+Dt'\log(6N')+Dt'(t'-1)\log(1+E)/2}{N'-Dt'}
 +\log(2N').
\]
Here \(Dt'/N'\le1/a\le1/2\).
Consequently the first term involving \(S\) is at most \(t'S+2S\).
The two terms from the boxes are at most \(2\log(6N')/a+\log(1+E)\).
Together with the last logarithm these estimates give \(\log\|F\|_1\le t'S+13\log(2a)\) using \(S<B,\ \log(6N')\le5\ell,\ \log(1+E)\le3\ell,\ \log(2N')\le4\ell\).
Remove the initial power of \(X\), and let \(F^*=X^{\deg F}F(1/X)\) denote the actual reversal of the resulting polynomial.
Then \(G=(X+\varepsilon)FF^*\) has the required sign in its actual degree, has degree at most \(2E+1\), and contains \(P_i^{2t'}\) since each \(P_i\) is reciprocal.
The one-norm bound \(2\|F\|_1^2\), with \(2t'\le a+1\), proves (5).

Thus \(M_*^{4a}2^{120L_a}<2^a\) is an exact sufficient comparison for this ordinary test by \(\log(2a)\le L_a\log2\).
The same finite enumeration gives eventual equality to \(K_D(B)\) at each total degree restriction, with a computable onset, using \((j+2)/2^j\) in place of its fifth-power numerator.
The construction controls the repeated factors themselves and allows \(G(-\alpha)=0\).
For comparison, in the signed graph with \(n=2\) the special nonvanishing requirement is exactly \(G(-\alpha)\ne0\), since \(G(\alpha)=0\) implies \(G(-\alpha)=2A(\alpha^2)\).
Both counts converge to the same spectrum supremum, and its value remains undetermined.

The coordinating agent for Yin proposed keeping \(n\) fixed, increasing the contact order, and dividing only the common factors at selected nodes.
A collaborating agent established the uniform constants, exact finite-degree comparison and ordinary polynomial construction.
