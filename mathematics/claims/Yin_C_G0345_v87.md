# Signed lengths of cyclotomic integers from their first absolute mean

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative lengths at bounded numbers of prime divisors

For \(n\ge2\) and \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z[\zeta_n]\), let \(\ell_n(\alpha)\) be the smallest length of a sum of signed \(n\)-th roots of unity equal to \(\alpha\), with the empty sum for zero.
Let \(U_n(\alpha)\) be the mean of \(|\sigma\alpha|\) over embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)\).
For \(M\ge0\) choose a nondecreasing bound \(S(M)\ge1\) on the number of signed coset indicators in [Sanders's finite-group theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) for integral functions of Fourier algebra norm at most \(\max(1,M)\).
If \(n\) has at most \(r\) distinct prime divisors and \(U_n(\alpha)\le B\), then
\[
                U_n(\alpha)\ \le\ \max_\sigma|\sigma\alpha|
                         \ \le\ \ell_n(\alpha)\ \le\ S(2^r B)^2 .
\]
For squarefree \(n\) the last bound improves to \(S(2^r B)\).
Equivalently, for any integer polynomial \(P\) with bounded primitive average at such conductors there is a signed sum \(R\) of boundedly many monomials, of exponents in \([0,n-1]\), such that \(\Phi_n\mid P-R\).
There is no degree restriction on \(P\) and the conclusion uses the original value \(P(\zeta_n)\).

<a id="squarefree"></a>
## Completing the missing Fourier modes with integral coefficients

Suppose first \(n\) is squarefree and write \(G=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z=\prod_{p\mid n}\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) in Chinese remainder coordinates.
Represent \(\alpha=P(\zeta_n)\) and let \(f:G\to\mathbb Z\) be the coefficients of \(P\bmod(X^n-1)\).
Its normalized Fourier coefficients at primitive characters are the primitive conjugate values divided by \(n\), up to relabeling by inversion.
Thus the sum of the absolute values of these coefficients is \(\varphi(n)U_n(\alpha)/n\).
A character of \(G\) is primitive precisely when it is nontrivial in every prime coordinate.

For each coordinate \(p\) define \(J_p f\) by fixing that coordinate to zero and extending its value constantly in that coordinate.
The integer function \(f'=\prod_{p\mid n}(I-J_p)f\) annihilates the contributions of all characters trivial on at least one prime coordinate.
For a primitive character \(\chi(x)=\prod_p\chi_p(x_p)\), the same operation gives \(\prod_p(\chi_p(x_p)-1)\).
This has Fourier algebra norm at most \(2^r\); its only primitive frequency is \(\chi\) itself with coefficient one.
Linearity now gives \(\|f'\|_A\le2^rB\), while every primitive Fourier coefficient of \(f'\) remains that of \(f\).
Stated in polynomial terms, the cyclic coefficient polynomial of \(f'\) represents \(\alpha\) at \(\zeta_n\).

Sanders's theorem represents \(f'\) as a signed sum of at most \(S(2^rB)\) coset indicators.
The polynomial of a nonsingleton coset evaluates to zero at \(\zeta_n\), by summing a nontrivial character over its subgroup.
The singleton terms give the claimed signed length.
This argument does not bound the other Fourier coefficients of the given coefficient function \(f\).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Returning from the radical to the whole conductor

For general \(n\) write \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), \(s=n/q\) and \(y=\zeta_n^s\).
Decompose \(P(X)=\sum_{j<s} X^jP_j(X^s)\).
The [primitive-component argument](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#support) shows that at most \(S(2^r B)\) of the values \(P_j(y)\) are nonzero, without using a degree bound.
Indeed its masking product bounds the full Fourier norm by \(2^rB\), and only singleton cosets survive evaluation in the power basis over \(\mathbb Q(y)\).
The orthogonality formula in that same argument gives \(U_q(P_j(y))\le B\) for every \(j\).
Since \(q\) is squarefree, each nonzero value is a sum of at most \(S(2^r B)\) signed \(q\)-th roots of unity.
Multiplying these summands by \(\zeta_n^j\) and summing gives an expression for \(\alpha\) in signed \(n\)-th roots of the stated length.
The other two inequalities follow by averaging and the triangle inequality.
Reducing the exponents of the signed expression modulo \(n\) gives the divisibility assertion by minimality of \(\Phi_n\).
