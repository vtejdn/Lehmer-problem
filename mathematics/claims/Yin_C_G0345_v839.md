# Prescribing short residue factors on the quadratic diagonal

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two different behaviors at fixed primes

For the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) take \(l=k^2+1\) and write \(P=X^n Q(X+X^{-1})\), where \(n=(k+1)^2\).
At every positive \(k\equiv360\pmod{380}\), the trace polynomial modulo 19 has the root 11 with multiplicity exactly two.
In particular the good-prime hypothesis of the [single-prime degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v838.md#statement) fails on this progression at the prime that most often sufficed in the [finite diagonal census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0183/readme.md#modular-degrees).

In the other direction, fix any nonempty finite set \(S\) of primes.
There are arbitrarily large diagonal sections on which every \(p\in S\) has squarefree trace reduction and every irreducible factor at \(p\), with one explicit exception, has degree greater than \(c_S\sqrt{\log n}\) for some \(c_S>0\).
The exception is exactly one linear factor \(T+1/12\) for each \(p>3\), and there is no exception at 2 or 3.
In particular the total number of modular factors is \(O_S(n/\sqrt{\log n})\) at each of these primes.
These conclusions describe short cycles and failure of good reduction on specific sections; the least admissible subset sum at the [height cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v837.md#height) on the squarefree sections remains undetermined.

<a id="nineteen"></a>
## The double root at nineteen

Write \(t=U-U^{-1}\), \(c=V+V^{-1}\), \(d=V-V^{-1}\).
The [normalized curve equation](Yin_C_G0345_v815.md#digits) is \(f=(12+t^2)c-7td+1\), with \(Q(X+X^{-1})=f(X^k,X^l)\).
At \(U=1\) it becomes \(12c+1=0\).
In characteristic 19 take any \(v\) with \(v+v^{-1}=11=-1/12\).
Its defining quadratic has nonsquare discriminant \(3\) modulo 19, so Frobenius exchanges its roots and \(v^{20}=1\).
For the stated progression we have \(20\mid k\), \(l\equiv1\pmod{20}\), and \((k,l)\equiv(-1,2)\pmod{19}\).
Thus \(X=v\) maps to \((1,v)\).
For the logarithmic derivative \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\), evaluation at this point gives
\[
 Df=(12l-14k)d,\qquad D^2f=(8k^2+12l^2-28kl)c.
\]
The first expression vanishes modulo 19 and the second equals \(112c\ne0\).
Since \(D\) restricts to \(X\partial_X\) along the section, this gives exactly a double zero there.
The trace map is unramified at \(v\) because \(v\ne\pm1\), proving the exact multiplicity for \(Q\).

<a id="short"></a>
## Simultaneously excluding other short cycles

Fix an integer \(h\ge16\) and take
\[
 k=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\{p^2:p\in S\}\cup\{p^{2j}-1:p\in S,\ 1\le j\le h\}\bigr).
\]
For \(p\in S\) and \(z\in\mathbb F_{p^j}\), \(j\le h\), either lift \(x\) with \(x+x^{-1}=z\) is a nonzero element of \(\mathbb F_{p^{2j}}\).
Hence \(x^k=1\) and \(x^l=x\), giving \(Q(z)=12z+1\) throughout these fields.
In characteristic \(p\), the order of vanishing of \(X^k-1\) at each such \(x\) is at least \(p^2\), since \(p^2\mid k\).
Likewise \(X^l-X=X(X^{k^2}-1)\) vanishes to order at least \(p^2\).
Consequently \(Q(X+X^{-1})-(12(X+X^{-1})+1)\) vanishes to order at least three at each lift.
The local degree of \(X\mapsto X+X^{-1}\) is at most two, so \(Q(T)-(12T+1)\) vanishes to order at least two at \(z\).
This proves the asserted description with multiplicities through degree \(h\), including the ramified traces at 5 and 23.

If \(Q\) had any repeated root, a lift \(x\) would be a repeated root of \(P\) and would lie in the zero set of \(\gcd(P,P')\) modulo \(p\).
The [uniform degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) gives \([\mathbb F_p(x):\mathbb F_p]\le16\), so its trace already occurs among the tested points.
This proves squarefreeness everywhere.
The displayed least common multiple tends to infinity and satisfies \(\log k\le (2+h(h+1))\sum_{p\in S}\log p\) by bounding it by the product.
Thus \(h\ge c_S\sqrt{\log n}\) for all sufficiently large \(h\), and the degree sum gives the claimed factor count.
