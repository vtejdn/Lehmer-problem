# Which contact orders survive an exact coefficient congruence

<a id="statement"></a>
## Precision thresholds for individual factors

Fix distinct monic reciprocal irreducible minima \(P_i\) of nontorsion algebraic units.
Write \(d_i=\deg P_i,\ h_i=\log M(P_i)\), prescribe even orders \(t_i\ge2\), and set \(S=\sum_i t_i h_i/2\).
Take any sequence \(n\to\infty\) on which powering by \(n\) separates the roots in the combined list.
Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\) and put
\[
    G_b(A)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}),\qquad
    K_b=\{A\in\mathbb Z[Y]_{\le b}:\ \prod_iP_i^{t_i}\mid G_b(A)\}.
\]
Let \(k\ge0\), \(w=b-2k\to\infty\), \(b=o(n)\), and let \(L=L(n)\ge1\) be any integer modulus.
For each \(i\) select an integer \(0\le r_i\le t_i\).
Keep at least one deficient index, meaning \(r_i<t_i\), and put
\[
                    \theta=\min_{i:\,r_i<t_i}\frac{h_i}{d_i}.
\]
If \(\limsup\log L/(nw)<\theta\), there exist a genuine row \(A\in K_b\) and an integer row \(B\) congruent coefficientwise to an element of \(K_b\) modulo \(L\), both with log one-norm divided by \(n\) tending to \(S\).
They agree in at least \(k+\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\) coefficients at each end, with at least one nonzero formal endpoint.
Every \(B(c^n)\ne0\) at the selected roots.
For each deficient index the order of \(G_b(B)\) at \(P_i\) is **exactly** \(r_i\); at every other index it is at least \(t_i\).
The same assertion applies with the positive odd orders in the [one- or two-node Salem and quadratic theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#statement) or the [leading odd pair followed by even orders](Yin_C_G0345_v448.md#statement) on the respective input classes.

Conversely, suppose a row \(B\) in such a congruence class agrees in its outer \(k\) coefficients on each side with some \(A\in K_b\), and has order \(r_i<t_i\) at \(P_i\).
At every such exponent
\[
 \log L\ \le\ \log\big(2(\|B\|_1+\|A\|_1)\big)
      +r_i\log(1+nw)+(nw+1)h_i/d_i .                                  \tag{1}
\]
For rows whose log one-norms are \(O(n)\), any deficiency occurring infinitely often therefore has \(\limsup\log L/(nw)\le h_i/d_i\) on those exponents.
Thus at a limiting precision distinct from the finitely many \(h_i/d_i\), every factor below that precision retains its full required order, and the lower orders of all factors above it may be prescribed simultaneously.
For a list of moduli at a given \(n\), use their least common multiple when testing congruence to the same integral kernel.

<a id="bound"></a>
## Dividing out the known coefficient blocks

The difference \(B-A=Y^k C\) has its middle row \(C\) of formal width \(w=b-2k\), and \(G_b(B)-G_b(A)=X^{nk}G_w(C)\).
The difference has order exactly \(r_i\) at a root \(c\) of the deficient factor.
If \(B=B'+L H\) with \(B'\in K_b\), then through order \(t_i-1\) all Hasse derivatives of \(G_b(B)-G_b(A)\) at \(c\) belong to \(L\mathbb Z[c]\).
Because the lower derivatives at orders below \(r_i\) vanish identically, the order-\(r_i\) Hasse derivative of \(G_w(C)\) there differs from the corresponding derivative of the difference by the unit \(c^{-nk}\).
It is a nonzero member of \(L\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(c)}\).
Its norm is divisible by \(L^{d_i}\).
At every conjugate \(c'\), the binomial coefficients in that derivative have size at most \((1+nw)^{r_i}\) and the monomials that survive differentiation have exponents between zero and \(nw+1\).
Taking norms gives (1), since \(\|C\|_1\le\|B\|_1+\|A\|_1\).
For several moduli whose kernel representatives may differ, the equivalence with their least common multiple also holds.
Indeed \(K_b\) is saturated as the kernel of a map to a free integral remainder module.
In an integral basis extending a basis for \(K_b\), coefficientwise congruence to that kernel says precisely that each complementary coordinate is divisible by the modulus.

<a id="construction"></a>
## Creating exactly the requested local orders

An unsigned polynomial column \((U,V)\) is exact when all prescribed orders hold for \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\).
For each fixed \(\eta>0\), the [jet fiber and packing argument](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#attainment) gives two exact columns over \(\mathbb Z[Y]\) independent over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\), of width at most a fixed \(v=v(\eta)\) and one-norms at most \(\exp((S+\eta)n)\) eventually.
Its required lower bound for the full list at fixed width is supplied by the [even-order theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#jets), or by the stated real-node theorem for either odd-order class.
Let \(M\) have those two columns and \(D(Y)=\det M\).
For the powered minimum \(Q_j=\min(c_j^n)\), put \(m_j=\operatorname{ord}_{Q_j}D\).
Then \(m_j\ge t_j\) for every \(j\).
Indeed at \(Y=c_j^n\) take the local inverse branch \(x(Y)\) of \(X\mapsto X^n\) through \(c_j\).
In each column \(U+x(Y)V\) vanishes to order at least \(t_j\), so the determinant has at least that order.
The \(Q_j\) are distinct by separation.

At a deficient index \(i\) take \(s_i=m_i-r_i\ge1\) and \(e=(1,0)^{\mathsf t}\).
Use the [conjugate residue construction](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#residues) in the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(c_i)=\mathbb Q(c_i^n)\) at the middle width \(w\), using modulus \(L\) in place of the binary power.
For clarity, put \(p=\lfloor w/2\rfloor-\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor-2v-3\).
The sum over embeddings of \(np|\log|\sigma c_i||-\log L-\epsilon nw\) is positive eventually for some \(\epsilon>0\), since \(\sum_\sigma|\log|\sigma c_i||=2h_i\) and \(\limsup\log L/(nw)<h_i/d_i\).
The logarithmic unit lattice gives a nonzero algebraic integer with log sizes at most these numbers plus a field-dependent constant.
Match its conjugates at the roots of \(Q_i\), with derivatives through order \(s_i-1\) zero.
Clearing the integral Hermite determinant costs \(\exp(O_v(n))\) and gives an integral polynomial \(E\) nonzero at every one of these roots.
The multiplicities \(m_i\le2v\) take values in a fixed finite set, so the estimates are uniform in them for fixed \(v\).

Use the identity
\[
     M f=W,\qquad f=\operatorname{adj}(M)eE/Q_i^{s_i},\qquad
     W=e(D/Q_i^{s_i})E .
\]
The first entry of \(W\) has exactly the required multiplicity \(r_i\) at \(Q_i\) and at least \(t_j\) at each \(Q_j\) for \(j\ne i\).
The [two-sided coefficient rounding](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#rounding) applies to \(L f\) and \(L W\).
Its pole coefficients are bounded by the corresponding conjugate size times \(\exp(O_v(n))\).
The logarithmic margin \(\epsilon nw\) makes the adjacent omitted coefficients of \(Lf\) tend to zero at the two ends, including contributions from unit-circle poles.
Rounding on \([-p,p]\) gives an integral Laurent column \(N\) for which \(MN-LW\) has one-norm \(O(w)\exp((S+\eta)n)\).
All uses of \(L\) here are as the integer scalar multiplying the identity.

Center this column at width \(w\), allowing a shift by one, and take the signed projection as in the [two-translate argument](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#projection).
The translated \(MN\) is an ordinary exact column, so its signed projection lies in \(K_w\).
At a root \(c_i\), the first nonzero local coefficient of the translated first entry of \(W(X^n)\) is of order \(r_i\), as powering and inversion are unramified.
So is the coefficient of the reflected entry at that order.
Shifting the column by another \(Y\) multiplies these two coefficients by \(c_i^n\) and \(c_i^{-n}\), respectively.
Their ratio changes by \(c_i^{2n}\ne1\), so one translate has nonzero signed coefficient at order \(r_i\).
The exact part vanishes through this order.
All conjugates have the same vanishing order over the rationals, and both reflected terms vanish through order \(t_j-1\) at each other orbit.

Add one such projected perturbation for each deficient index.
At a deficient orbit only its own summand contributes to the coefficient of the prescribed order.
Every perturbation is congruent to an integral member of \(K_w\) modulo \(L\), with both end blocks of length \(\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\) zero.
Multiplication by \(Y^k\) embeds their sum into the formal width \(b\) with the same sign, all orders preserved and the two end blocks enlarged by \(k\).
Taking \(\eta\) to zero along the eventual ranges gives a perturbation \(J\) of upper slope at most \(S\).

At an auxiliary diverging width at most \(b\) the same even- or odd-order theorem supplies a genuine exact signed row of slope \(S\).
Remove paired formal endpoint zeros and pad by \(1+Y\) to width \(b\), as in the two-translate argument; padding contributes \(o(n)\) to the log norm.
The unrestricted-width lower bound keeps the resulting genuine row \(A\) at slope \(S\).
Among a bounded number of positive integers \(u\), take two for which \(A+uJ\) has nonzero numerator at every powered node, using \(A(c_j^n)\ne0\).
Each has all prescribed orders at the deficient indices and the retained lower orders at the others.
Their bounded integer linear combination recovering a nonzero multiple of \(A\) forces one of their norms to be at least a fixed positive multiple of \(\|A\|_1\).
This choice of \(B\) proves the claim.
