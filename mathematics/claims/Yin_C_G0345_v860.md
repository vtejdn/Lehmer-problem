# A Mordell–Weil sieve at seven for the untwisted trace factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Orbits forced into the same rational factor

Let \(Q_{k,l}\) be the monic trace polynomial of the untwisted two-pole section in a positive primitive direction, of degree \(2k+l\).
The degree-nine polynomial \(Q_{2,5}\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), although its reduction at seven has factors of degrees four and five.
For \(Q_{6,37}\), the modular factors of degrees eleven and thirteen must occur in the same rational irreducible factor.
The other two modular degrees are three and twenty-two.
Thus the only possible rational factor degrees are the subset sums of \(3,22,24\), whereas the unweighted test at seven admits every subset sum of \(3,11,13,22\).

For every integer \(h\ge16\), put
\[
 M_h=7\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le h}(7^{2j}-1).
\]
On the diagonal \(l=k^2+1\) with \(k\equiv6\pmod {M_h}\), each rational factor of degree at most \(h\) has degree among the subset sums of \(3,22,24\).
In particular seven individually excludes degrees \(11,13,14,16\) on this infinite progression while the modular degree test admits all four.
For \(k\equiv2\pmod {M_h}\) the only possible positive rational factor degree through \(h\) is nine, although the modular degree test admits four and five.
In each case the same conclusions hold for positive coprime \((k,l)\) in the two corresponding exponent residue classes, without the diagonal restriction.

## The reduction of the rank-one quotient

Use the [second elliptic quotient](Yin_C_G0345_v858.md#models) \(E:Y^2=X(X^2+1550X+597425)\), with \(X=-575Z^2/(Z^2-4)\), and identity \(O\).
At the good prime seven its equation becomes \(Y^2=X(X-1)(X-3)\), with just the four points \(O,(0,0),(1,0),(3,0)\), by checking all seven abscissas.
The [rank and two-isogeny calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v858.md#descent) shows that \(E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)\) has order four, and gives two representatives with independent descent images, the point \((0,0)\) and a point \(G\) with abscissa \(-23(783965/132828)^2\).
Indeed their rational descent classes are \(23\cdot1039\) and \(-23\), and the descent map kills doubles.
Since seven divides the numerator of the displayed fraction and not its denominator, \(G\) reduces to \((0,0)\).
All doubles reduce to \(O\), so the rational reduction image is exactly \(\{O,(0,0)\}\).

The squareclass of \(X-3\) on the special fiber gives a homomorphism with this kernel.
Indeed shifting the abscissa by three moves the corresponding two-torsion point to the origin, so the [line-intersection descent formula](Yin_C_G0345_v858.md#descent) applies, with values \(1\) at \(O\), \(-3\) at \((0,0)\), \(-2\) at \((1,0)\) and \(3(3-1)=6\) at \((3,0)\).
Only the first two are squares modulo seven.
For a closed point of degree \(d\) the value on the sum of its Frobenius conjugates is the norm of the function value modulo squares, using the divisor evaluation of this descent function as in the [elliptic pushforward calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v858.md#factors).
In detail the divisor of \(X-3\) equals \(2[(3,0)]-2[O]\).
Weil reciprocity makes evaluation on degree-zero divisors invariant modulo squares under linear equivalence, with leading unit one at \(O\) in the standard parameter \(X/Y\).
The degree-zero divisor of a closed point minus \(dO\) represents the sum of its conjugates in the elliptic group.

## Computing the orbit characters

Use \(B_j(r)\) determined by \(B_j(s+s^{-1})=s^j+s^{-j}\), with \(B_0=2,B_1=r\) and \(B_{j+1}=rB_j-B_{j-1}\).
Write \(Z=B_k(r)\), and reduce all polynomials in this paragraph modulo seven.
For an irreducible modular trace factor \(f\) such that \(Z^2-3,Z^2-4\) do not vanish at its roots, the character of its elliptic orbit sum is
\[
 \chi(f)=\left(\frac{\operatorname{Res}(f,-4(Z^2-3))\operatorname{Res}(f,Z^2-4)}{7}\right).
\]
Indeed \(X-3=-4(Z^2-3)/(Z^2-4)\), and residues and their inverses have the same quadratic character.
The points on the quotient are the [inverse pairs of signed roots](Yin_C_G0345_v857.md#factors); the coordinates of their elliptic images specialize by the displayed model wherever the denominator is a unit.
A rational factor all of whose modular constituents satisfy these conditions has product of characters \(+1\), with multiplicities, since its elliptic divisor sum is rational and reduces into the subgroup just determined.

The two example polynomials are reductions of
\[
 Q_{k,l}=1+10B_l+B_{l+2k}+B_{|l-2k|}-7B_{l+k}+7B_{|l-k|},
\]
by expanding the [normalized curve equation](Yin_C_G0345_v815.md#digits) at \((s^k,s^l)\).
The table lists all monic irreducible modular factors in descending coefficient order, one digit per coefficient, and the two resultants in the order of the character formula.

| \((k,l)\) | \(\deg f\) | Coefficients in \(\mathbb F_7\) | Resultants | \(\chi(f)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((2,5)\) | 4 | `13535` | 5, 2 | −1 |
| | 5 | `142663` | 2, 3 | −1 |
| \((6,37)\) | 3 | `1011` | 5, 6 | +1 |
| | 11 | `166665606225` | 5, 2 | −1 |
| | 13 | `15052343100136` | 1, 6 | −1 |
| | 22 | `13044651540560203265034` | 6, 5 | +1 |

The table can be verified in \(\mathbb F_7[r]\) by multiplying the entries for each direction and comparing with the recurrence above.
For irreducibility, start at \(v=r\) and replace \(v\) by \(v^7\bmod f\) up to \(d=\deg f\) times; the last residue equals \(r\), and \(\gcd(v-r,f)=1\) at each index \(d/q\) for a prime \(q\mid d\).
The resultants are the determinants of multiplication by the two indicated polynomials in the power basis modulo \(f\).
In each example exactly two entries have negative character, so their membership in any rational factor must agree.
At \((2,5)\) this proves irreducibility; at \((6,37)\) it replaces eleven and thirteen by their sum in the allowed degree list.

## Persistence of the short orbits

Fix one of the base directions, and let both exponents agree with its exponents modulo \(M_h\).
Every \(r\in\mathbb F_{7^j}\) with \(j\le h\) lifts to \(s\in\mathbb F_{7^{2j}}^\times\) satisfying \(s+s^{-1}=r\).
The monomials in the section have their base values at this lift because the multiplicative order divides \(M_h\).
Their logarithmic derivatives agree as well because both exponents retain their residues modulo seven.
Neither base trace polynomial vanishes at \(r=\pm2\) by its nonzero resultant with \(Z^2-4\), since \(B_k(\pm2)^2=4\).
Every short trace root is therefore simple as in the base polynomial, using that the trace map has nonzero differential at its lifts.
The reduced values of \(B_k(r)\) also agree, preserving the resultant characters of all short orbits.
Every constituent of a monic rational factor of degree at most \(h\) is such a short orbit, so the preceding character restriction gives the stated subset sums.
Along the diagonal congruence \(k\equiv k_0\pmod {M_h}\) implies \(k^2+1\equiv k_0^2+1\pmod {M_h}\), and infinitely many positive primitive directions result.
