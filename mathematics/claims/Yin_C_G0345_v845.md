# Horizontal derivative valuations from eight fixed algebraic numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact depth below the valuation of the exponent

For positive coprime integers \(k,l\), let \(P(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement), and put \(D(U)=U^4-4A(U)A^*(U)\).
Fix \(p\mid k\) outside \(S_0=\{2,5,23,1039\}\), write \(e=v_p(k)\), and normalize \(v_p(p)=1\).
Each geometric multiple residue root \(x\) gives \(u=x^k\), a root of \(D\), and \(v=x^l=-u^2/(2A(u))\).
Let \(\alpha\) be the root of \(D\) lifting \(u\), put \(g=\operatorname{ord}(u)\), and define the finite positive integer \(c=v_p(\alpha^g-1)\).
Whenever \(c\le e\), exactly two distinct roots of \(P\) reduce to \(x\), their distance has valuation \(c/2\), and each derivative \(P'\) at these roots has valuation \(c/2\).
If \(c\) is odd they belong to the same rational irreducible factor.
Over all such disks at all \(p\mid k\) outside \(S_0\), the total logarithmic derivative weight is at most \(8\log k\), and the logarithmic complementary resultant in any coprime monic integral factorization receives at most \(4\log k\) from them.
At \(p=3,7,79\) every possible \(u\) has \(c=1\), so \(p\mid k\) implies that \(p\) divides no such complementary resultant.

<a id="coordinate"></a>
## A critical value in the unramified disk

The [horizontal collision equations](Yin_C_G0345_v844.md#collision) give the displayed formulas for \(u,v\).
Conversely \(u^l=v^k\) suffices for a unique residue coordinate \(x=u^a v^b\), where \(ak+bl=1\).
The polynomial \(D=-4U^8+116U^6-799U^4+116U^2-4\) has discriminant of absolute value \(2^{32}5^{12}23^2 1039^2\) by its derivative resultant.
Outside \(S_0\) its roots are simple units, and \(A(u)\ne0\) follows from \(4A(u)A^*(u)=u^4\).
Work in the ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) of the unramified extension with residue field \(\mathbb F_p(u)\), which contains \(v,x\) in its residue field.
Write \(u_0=[u],v_0=[v],x_0=[x]\) for their multiplicative Teichmüller lifts.
Simple-root lifting gives \(\alpha\in\mathcal O\), and the simple reductions of \(D\) and \(U^g-1\) give
\[
 v_p(D(u_0))=v_p(u_0-\alpha)=v_p(\alpha^g-1).
\]
The eight [real roots](Yin_C_G0345_v844.md#collision) have absolute values different from one, which gives finiteness.

On the open residue disk take \(X=x_0(1+s)^{1/l}\), so \(U=u_0(1+s)^{k/l}\) and \(V=v_0(1+s)\).
These binomial series have integral coefficients since their exponents are in \(\mathbb Z_p\), and the coordinate and its inverse preserve distances since both have integral series and unit linear coefficients.
For \(s\in p\mathcal O\), the binomial theorem applied to the \(p^e\)-th power of \((1+s)^{k/(lp^e)}\in1+p\mathcal O\) gives \(U-u_0\in p^{e+1}\mathcal O\), and \(U'=(k/l)U/(1+s)\in p^e\mathcal O\).
Set \(H(s)=F(U,V)\).
Then \(H'(0)\equiv0\) and \(H''(0)\equiv2A(u)v^2\ne0\pmod p\), since the first two derivatives of \(U\) at zero vanish modulo \(p\).
Newton lifting for the integral series \(H'\) gives a unique \(s_*\in p\mathcal O\) with \(H'(s_*)=0\).
Put \(v_0(1+s_h)=-u_0^2/(2A(u_0))\), so \(s_h\in p\mathcal O\).
The identity \(H'=F_U U'+v_0 F_V\) at \(s_*\) gives \(2A(u_0)v_0(s_*-s_h)\in p^e\mathcal O\) by replacing \(U\) by \(u_0\) in \(F_V\).
Freezing \(U=u_0\) in \(H(s_*)\) costs a multiple of \(p^{e+1}\), and completing the square in \(V\) then leaves a quadratic error divisible by \(p^{2e}\).
Thus
\[
 H(s_*)\equiv-\frac{D(u_0)}{4A(u_0)}\pmod {p^{e+1}},
\]
which determines its valuation when \(c\le e\).

<a id="distances"></a>
## The two roots and their contribution

Expand \(H(s_*+w)=H(s_*)+w^2 B(w)\) with integral coefficients and \(B(0)\) a unit.
The change \(t=w\sqrt{B(w)/B(0)}\) has integral coefficients by the binomial series at odd \(p\), with linear coefficient one and integral compositional inverse.
It preserves distances on the open disk over every finite extension of the base field.
The section roots there correspond to the two square roots of \(-H(s_*)/B(0)\), with mutual distance of valuation \(c/2\) for \(c\le e\).
All roots of \(P\) are units by monicity and its unit constant term, so roots in other disks contribute zero to the valuation of the derivative product at either root.
For odd \(c\) the square roots are conjugate over the unramified base field, whose value group is \(\mathbb Z\), and the coordinate changes over that field carry the conjugacy back to the section roots.
Each of the at most eight disks at a given prime contributes \(c\log p\) to the [geometric derivative weight](Yin_C_G0345_v833.md#weights), and a separated pair contributes \((c/2)\log p\) to a complementary resultant.
Summing \(e\log p\) over \(p\mid k\) gives \(\log k\).

<a id="three-primes"></a>
## The horizontal primes 3, 7 and 79

The following polynomial identities test the order of every fixed critical coordinate to precision \(p^2\).
In \((\mathbb Z/p^2)[U]/(D)\) write \(U^{p^f-1}=1+pL(U)\).
The entries give the coefficients of \(L\) at degrees \(0,2,4,6\), with all odd coefficients zero.

| \(p\) | \(f\) | Coefficients of \(L\) modulo \(p\) | \(\gcd(D,L)\bmod p\), monic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 4 | 1, 2, 2, 0 | 1 |
| 7 | 2 | 1, 2, 1, 5 | 1 |
| 79 | 2 | 78, 8, 70, 57 | 1 |

These identities follow by binary powering with the substitution \(U^8=29U^6-(799/4)U^4+29U^2-1\) modulo \(p^2\), followed by Euclidean division modulo \(p\).
Reducing the identity at \(u\) gives \(g\mid p^f-1\), and evaluating at \(\alpha\) gives \(v_p(\alpha^{p^f-1}-1)=1\) by the indicated gcd.
As \((p^f-1)/g\) is prime to \(p\), the geometric sum for that quotient is a unit, proving \(c=1\).
In particular the [exceptional repeated trace factors for two signed powers](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#frobenius-primes) at these primes yield no common residue root between distinct rational factors of the section.

<a id="steep"></a>
## Consequence for a small factor on steep sections

Let \(k\to\infty\), \(l/k\to\infty\), and suppose an irreducible factor of degree \(d=o(l)\) occurs.
For every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1/17\), the [large-prime lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#statement) of \((1-o(1))d\log(l/d)\) for the logarithmic complementary resultant above \(l^{1/17-\varepsilon}\) persists after discarding all horizontal disks with \(c\le v_p(k)\).
Indeed the cutoff eventually exceeds every prime of \(S_0\), and \(4\log k=o(d\log(l/d))\) by the [degree estimate used in the cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#factor).
The horizontal disks remaining in that lower bound require \(\alpha^g\equiv1\pmod{p^{v_p(k)+1}}\) for one of eight fixed algebraic numbers, as well as the residue compatibility \(u^l=v^k\).
The congruence leaves the critical valuation above this precision undetermined and does not imply that the roots split between rational factors.
