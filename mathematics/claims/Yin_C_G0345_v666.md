# Lifting powered numerators through the conductor of a power order

<a id="statement"></a>
## The energy of one prescribed progression

Let \(\alpha\) be a hyperbolic algebraic unit with minimal polynomial \(P\), and suppose that \(\beta=\alpha^k\) has the same degree, for an integer \(k\ge1\).
Write \(P_k\) for its minimal polynomial and set \(O=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(S=\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Both units and their inverses belong to their respective orders.
For \(z\in S\) write \(E_k(z)\) for the minimum of \(\|B/P_k\|_2^2\) over integral Laurent representatives \(B(\beta)=z\), with normalized circle measure.
The minimum in a fixed class exists by [coefficient rounding](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
Write \(\mathcal E(P_k)=\inf_{z\ne0}E_k(z)\), and let
\[
     \mathfrak f=\{y\in O:yO\subset S\},\qquad c=P_k'(\beta)/P'(\alpha).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak f=cO\).
For a specified residue \(r\) modulo \(k\), the infimum \(L_r\) of the squared energy of the \(r\)-th progression of \(A/P\), over integral Laurent \(A\) indivisible by \(P\), satisfies
\[
     L_r=\inf_{0\ne y\in\mathfrak f} E_k(y),\qquad
     \mathcal E(P_k)\ \le\ L_r\ \le\ 2\mathcal E(P_k).                 \tag{1}
\]
The upper factor \(2\) is sharp already for \(k=2\).
The energies on all progressions together obey the exact formula
\[
      \mathcal E(P)=\inf_{0\ne y\in\mathfrak f}
                      \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} E_k(\alpha^{-r}y).              \tag{2}
\]
The ratio \(\mathcal E(P)/\mathcal E(P_2)\) is unbounded on reciprocal quadratics.

<a id="compatibility"></a>
## Which lists of powered numerators lift

Put \(D(X)=P_k(X^k)/P(X)\), a monic integral polynomial, and prescribe integral Laurent polynomials \(B_0,\ldots,B_{k-1}\).
They are the [progression numerators](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#sections) of an integral Laurent \(A\) exactly when the elements \(\alpha^r B_r(\beta)\) all have the same value \(y\).
In fact, set \(F(X)=\sum_r X^r B_r(X^k)\).
For a primitive \(k\)-th root \(\omega\), the values \(F(\alpha\omega^j)\) form the finite Fourier transform of \((\alpha^r B_r(\beta))_r\).
Only \(\alpha\) among these preimages is a root of \(P\), by degree preservation.
Thus the equality of all entries is equivalent to the vanishing of \(F\) at all the other preimages.
Extending each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) to a splitting field gives the same vanishing over each conjugate of \(\beta\).
These are precisely the simple roots of \(D\), so monic division after clearing Laurent powers gives \(A=F/D\) integral.
Conversely, \(F=AD\) implies the stated equality by Fourier inversion.
Differentiation at \(\alpha\) gives \(D(\alpha)=k\alpha^{k-1}c\), hence
\[
              y=\alpha^{k-1}c A(\alpha).
\]
In particular \(P\nmid A\) is equivalent to \(y\ne0\).

The eligible \(y\) are exactly those satisfying \(\alpha^{-r}y\in S\) for all \(0\le r<k\).
The \(S\)-span of \(1,\alpha^{-1},\ldots,\alpha^{1-k}\) is \(O\), by grouping powers modulo \(k\).
Consequently this membership test defines \(\mathfrak f\), while the displayed expression for \(y\) ranges over \(cO\) because \(\alpha\) is a unit.
This proves the conductor formula.
For arbitrary \(y\) in the conductor, the representatives of the \(k\) individual classes may be chosen independently and then lifted by \(F/D\).
The [sum of the progression energies](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#statement) proves (2).
Multiplication by \(\alpha^{-r}\) permutes \(\mathfrak f\), giving the expression for \(L_r\) in (1).

<a id="two"></a>
## A difference of shifts realizes the factor two

The order \(S\) has finite index in \(O\) because their rational spans coincide.
For \(n=[O:S]\) the ideal \(nO\) lies in \(\mathfrak f\), so \(S/\mathfrak f\) is finite.
Choose \(h\ge1\) with \(\beta^h\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak f\), using invertibility of \(\beta\) in this quotient, or choose \(h=1\) if \(\mathfrak f=S\).
Given any powered numerator \(B\) with \(B(\beta)\ne0\), take
\[
                      B_N(Y)=(1-Y^{Nh})B(Y).
\]
Its value is a nonzero element of \(\mathfrak f\) because \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
Its squared energy tends to \(2\|B/P_k\|_2^2\) as \(N\to\infty\).
Indeed the inner product of the coefficient sequence of \(B/P_k\) with its translate tends to zero, first for finitely supported sequences by disjointness, and then for square-summable sequences by approximation.
To realize \(B_N\) in row \(r\), set \(y=\alpha^r B_N(\beta)\), represent the other \(\alpha^{-s}y\) in the power basis of \(S\), and use the quotient \(F/D\) above.
Taking infima gives the upper bound (1).
Indivisibility in every row gives the lower bound.

<a id="full"></a>
## A constructive upper bound for the whole numerator

Take any nonzero \(y\in\mathfrak f\) and integral Laurent representatives \(C_r(\beta)=\alpha^{-r}y\).
One available choice is the tuple of sections of \(D\) itself.
For every powered trial \(B\) indivisible by \(P_k\), the tuple \(C_r B\) lifts by the compatibility criterion, with nonzero common value \(yB(\beta)\).
Define \(W=\sum_r |C_r|^2\) on the circle.
This gives an explicit original numerator with squared energy
\[
             \int_{\mathbb T} W |B/P_k|^2,\qquad
             \mathcal E(P)\le \|W\|_\infty\,\mathcal E(P_k).          \tag{3}
\]
The mean of \(W\) is at least \(2k-1\).
In fact two monomial rows would make \(\alpha^{r-s+k\ell}\) rational for distinct \(r,s\) and an integer \(\ell\).
The exponent is nonzero, and a rational algebraic unit is \(1\) or \(-1\).
Thus at most one row is a monomial, and every other integral row has squared coefficient norm at least two.

For reciprocal \(P\) the mean of \(W\) is at least \(3k-2\).
Let \(\sigma\) be the field automorphism taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
All the rows have the same product \(C_r(\beta)\sigma(C_r(\beta))=y\sigma(y)\).
A row of squared coefficient norm one has product one.
A row of squared norm two has the form \(\pm Y^p(1+\epsilon Y^q)\) with \(q>0\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and product \(2+\epsilon(\beta^q+\beta^{-q})\).
It cannot have product one since then \(\beta^q\) would be a root of unity.
If two such binomials have the same product, the identity \(u+u^{-1}=v+v^{-1}\) implies \(u=v\) or \(u=v^{-1}\), applied to \(u=\epsilon\beta^q\) and the corresponding second signed power.
Nontorsion forces the same \(q\) and sign, so their ratio is a signed power of \(\beta\), again impossible for distinct rows.
At most one row has squared norm below three, proving the claim.
The bounds on the mean concern fixed integral multipliers in (3), where the actual weighted energy can depend on the trial \(B\).

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## Sharpness at one row and divergence for the whole input

Let \(P=X^2-aX+1\) for an integer \(a\ge5\), take the larger root \(\alpha\) and set \(k=2\).
Then \(P_2=Y^2-tY+1\) with \(t=a^2-2\), and \(c=(\alpha^2-\alpha^{-2})/(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})=a\).
Since \(\beta=a\alpha-1\), the order \(S=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z a\alpha\) has quotient \(S/aO\cong\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\), with \(\beta\) mapping to \(-1\).
Thus a numerator in the nonzero conductor classes cannot be a signed monomial.
The [quadratic energy calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#quadratic) gives \(\mathcal E(P_2)=t/(t^2-4)^{3/2}\) and bounds the energy of every nonzero numerator other than a signed monomial below by \(2(t-2)/(t^2-4)^{3/2}\).
It follows that \(L_r/\mathcal E(P_2)\ge 2-4/t\), proving sharpness in (1) as \(a\to\infty\).

The same exact calculation at \(a\) and \(t\), using \(t^2-4=a^2(a^2-4)\), gives
\[
                       \frac{\mathcal E(P)}{\mathcal E(P_2)}
                            =\frac{a^4}{a^2-2}.
\]
For comparison, \(D=X^2+aX+1\) gives row multipliers \(1+Y,a\) and \(\|W\|_\infty=a^2+4\) in (3).
The divergence occurs at large Mahler measure.
Whether compatible powered constructions give a useful upper bound on shrinking degree records remains open.
