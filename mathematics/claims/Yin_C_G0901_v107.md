# Universal normal fields indexed by the ranks of nonabelian chief factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exact level at which a small-measure input occurs

A chief factor of a finite group \(G\) is a section \(N/H\) between normal subgroups with no \(G\)-normal subgroup strictly between them.
A nonabelian chief factor is a direct product \(S^j\) of copies of a nonabelian simple group; call \(j\) its rank.
For \(h\ge0\), let \(U_h\) be the compositum of all finite Galois extensions of \(\mathbb Q\) for which every nonabelian chief factor has rank at most \(h\).
These are specified normal fields independent of any Mahler measure cutoff.
\(U_0\) is the solvable closure of \(\mathbb Q\), and already \(U_1\) contains every finite Galois extension with symmetric or alternating group, in every degree.
Each \(U_h\) contains every finite solvable extension of itself (allowing any finite extension with solvable normal closure).

Fix \(M>0\) and let \(y\) be a nonzero algebraic number with \(0<m(y)\le M\), in total logarithmic normalization.
Write \(d\) for the degree after taking the product of each class of conjugates differing by torsion, and suppose \(d\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(M\).
Let \(P_y\) be the rational field of the action on compressed multiplicative labels, with \(u\) blocks of size \(b\).
For every \(h\ge0\) simultaneously,
\[
                           y\in U_h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u\le h.   \tag{1}
\]
Immediately before this first level the relative label group is exactly
\[
        \operatorname{Gal}(U_{u-1}P_y/U_{u-1})=(A_b)^u                    \tag{2}
\]
under restriction to \(P_y\).
For every \(h<u\) this full product survives over \(U_h\) after all towers consisting of solvable steps and single adjunctions of degree less than \(b\); the first sufficient degree bound for such towers reaching \(y\) is \(b\).

Let \(T=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) be the field of all totally real algebraic numbers in the chosen closure.
With the same degree threshold, membership in the solvable closure of \(T U_h\) is exactly
\[
                y\in (T U_h)^{\rm sol}
                    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                   u\le h\quad\text{or}\quad P_y\subseteq T .             \tag{3}
\]
When both conditions fail the same product survives and the same exact step bound \(b\) applies over \(T U_h\).
For a reciprocal compressed unit the second condition in (3) says that its trace \(y+y^{-1}\) is totally real.
In particular if there are gaps on every \((T U_h)^{\rm sol}\) but unrestricted Lehmer fails, every positive shrinking sequence outside finitely many terms at each fixed level must have nonreal label fields and \(u\to\infty\).
Along every positive shrinking sequence one also has
\[
                  u\ll m(y)^2\bigl(\log(3d)\bigr)^{212}.                 \tag{4}
\]
The field criteria do not decide whether a gap holds on \(U_1\) or on these larger bases.

<a id="formation"></a>
## What can occur in an infinite compositum with a chief-rank bound

The ranks are independent of the chief series, by Jordan–Hölder for normal series with the conjugation action of the whole group.
The condition defining \(U_h\) passes to quotients by taking a chief series through the kernel.
It is closed under finite subdirect products.
In fact, for a subgroup projecting onto each of several finite groups, project away the last coordinate.
The kernel identifies with a normal subgroup of the last group, on which the full subdirect product induces precisely conjugation by that whole last group.
A chief series in the last group through this normal subgroup gives chief factors of the very same ranks inside the kernel.
Factors above the kernel come from the projection, so induction proves closure.
An extension by a solvable normal kernel preserves the condition as well, since all chief factors inside this kernel are abelian.

Every finite normal subfield of \(U_h\) lies in a finite compositum of generating fields, and its group is a quotient of a finite subdirect product.
Thus its nonabelian chief factors satisfy the prescribed rank bound.
Conversely every finite Galois extension with that bound appears by definition.
The solvable closure \(U_h^{\rm sol}\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\); the kernel over \(U_h\) has only solvable finite quotients.
In any finite rational Galois quotient its image is a solvable normal subgroup and the quotient by that subgroup comes from \(U_h\).
The closure rules give the required chief-rank bound for that quotient, hence the corresponding normal field already lies in \(U_h\).
It follows that \(U_h^{\rm sol}=U_h\).

<a id="level"></a>
## The nonabelian chief factor of greatest rank in the label field

In \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q)\) the [canonical independent alternating groups](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) form the unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup \(D=(A_b)^u\).
It is itself a chief factor of rank \(u\).
The action on the \(u\) blocks has image \(J\le S_u\), and its kernel modulo \(D\) is contained in the blockwise sign group \((C_2)^u\).
Thus every nonabelian chief factor above \(D\) is a chief factor of \(J\).
For any such factor \(S^j\), let \(p\) be any prime dividing \(|S|\).
Then \(j\le v_p(u!)<u\), since \(|S|^j\) divides \(|J|\) and Legendre's formula bounds \(v_p(u!)\le u-1\).
Consequently \(G\) has maximum nonabelian chief-factor rank exactly \(u\), and \(P_y^D\subseteq U_{u-1}\).
The actual rational splitting field \(L_y\) over \(P_y\) has solvable group, including the original torsion and radical fibers. [Solvable kernels](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal)
It has the same maximum nonabelian chief-factor rank, by taking a chief series through that solvable kernel.
As \(U_h\) is normal, \(y\in U_h\) exactly when \(L_y\subseteq U_h\).
This proves (1).
Over \(U_{u-1}\), the restriction group on \(P_y\) lies in \(D\) because \(P_y^D\) is already present.
It is nontrivial and normal in \(G\) since \(P_y\not\subseteq U_{u-1}\) and \(U_{u-1}\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
The minimal normal subgroup property proves (2).
For \(h<u\) the normal restriction group over \(U_h\) likewise contains \(D\).

Across towers over \(U_h\), use \(U_{u-1}\) as an intermediate normal base to isolate the group in (2).
A finite tower of the indicated steps has a normal closure with no section \(A_b\): each degree-\(<b\) step has a normal closure inside a symmetric group of order less than \(|A_b|\), and solvable steps have no such section.
The absence of this simple section is preserved by subgroups, quotients, products, extensions and base change.
Hence even over \(U_{u-1}\) the towers leave \(D\) intact, since every nontrivial quotient of \(D\) has a factor \(A_b\).
Restriction over \(U_h\) gives the asserted lower threshold; normality of the union of the allowed towers excludes \(y\) itself.
The upper bound \(b\) already holds over \(\mathbb Q\) by the [label-stabilizer tower for the actual input](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#degree).
Finally the [joint-degree bound for two labels in a block](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) gives \(b\gg d/(m(y)^2(\log(3d))^{212})\).
There are at most \(d\) labels, proving (4).

<a id="real"></a>
## What changes upon adjoining the totally real numbers

If \(u\le h\) then (1) suffices.
If \(P_y\subset T\), the solvable extension \(L_y/P_y\) suffices over the combined base.
Otherwise suppose \(h<u\) and \(P_y\) is not totally real.
Let \(G_{\mathbb Q}\) be the absolute Galois group in the chosen algebraic closure and let \(\pi:G_{\mathbb Q}\to G\) be restriction.
Let \(N\) fix \(U_{u-1}\), and let \(C\) be the closed normal subgroup generated by ordinary complex conjugation \(c\).
Its fixed field is \(T\), since an algebraic number is totally real precisely when all conjugates of \(c\) fix it.
Here \(\pi(N)=D\) by (2), while \(\pi(c)\ne1\) by normality of \(P_y\) and its nonreal embedding.
The centralizer of \(D\) in \(G\) is trivial: it is normal, and any nontrivial normal subgroup would contain the centerless nonabelian \(D\).
There is thus \(n\in N\) with \([\pi(n),\pi(c)]\ne1\).
The commutator \([n,c]\) lies in \(N\cap C\), and \(\pi(N\cap C)\) is normal in \(G\), contained in \(D\), and nontrivial.
It equals \(D\).
The fixed field of \(N\cap C\) is \(T U_{u-1}\), so the whole product is isolated even over that field.
The preceding section's tower argument applies there and then over \(T U_h\), proving (3) and the exact threshold. [Totally real base comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v91.md#commutator)
On a shrinking sequence the compressed degree must tend to infinity by coefficient finiteness.
If every fixed combined base has a positive gap, only finitely many terms of the sequence can meet the criterion for each \(h\); this gives the two stated restrictions.

For the four-term family with consecutive coefficient magnitudes, every reciprocal unit on the [three-coprime branch](Yin_C_G0901_v97.md#statement) belongs to \(U_1\), also at every common dilation.
Indeed the rational splitting group maps onto the symmetric group of inverse pairs with an elementary abelian inversion kernel.
Every nonabelian chief factor of a symmetric group has rank one, and taking radicals or torsion twists over the normal base adds only solvable Galois kernels.
Thus any counterexample sequence supplied by this branch would already give failure in the one fixed field \(U_1\).
