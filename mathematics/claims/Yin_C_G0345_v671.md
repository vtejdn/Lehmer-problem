# Simultaneous tail bounds on nested windows and the energy of the rounded middle

<a id="statement"></a>
## A construction from the radii of the conjugates

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\).
For each root \(\rho\) write \(a_\rho=|\log|\rho||\), and put \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
Write \(\sum_-\) and \(\sum_+\) for sums over all interior and exterior roots, respectively, counting conjugates separately.
Both sums of \(a_\rho\) equal \(m\).
For an integral Laurent numerator \(A\), let \(h_j\) be the circle coefficients of \(A/P\), \(E(A)=\sum_j|h_j|^2\), and \(T_H(A)=\sum_{j\notin H}|h_j|^2\) for an interval \(H\) of integers.

Choose integers \(L,R\ge0\), \(W>L+R\), and set \(J=[0,W)\cap\mathbb Z\), \(I=[L,W-R)\cap\mathbb Z\).
Given \(\gamma\ge0\), put
\[
 S=\sum_-\max(\gamma W/2,a_\rho L)+
   \sum_+\max(\gamma W/2,a_\rho R),\qquad
 Z=\exp(2(S-mW)/d),
 \qquad
 B(P)=2d e^{-2m/d}
 \left(\frac{\prod_{\rho}(1-e^{-2a_\rho})^{-1}}{|\operatorname{disc}P|}\right)^{1/d}.
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is an explicit integer \(q=q(\epsilon)\) from the [scalar estimate](#scalar).
One can construct an integral \(A\) indivisible by \(P\) such that
\[
 T_I(A)+e^{\gamma W}T_J(A)\le q^2 B(P)Z,\qquad
 E(A)\le (1/12+\epsilon)|I|+q^2 B(P)Z.                         \tag{1}
\]
Its support lies in \(I+\{0,\ldots,d\}\).
Within this support restriction the infimum of the first expression in (1) is between \(c_P Z\) and \(B(P)Z\), with \(c_P>0\) independent of the window choices and \(\gamma\).
The lower bound also applies to every admissible Laurent numerator with \(T_I(A)<\|P\|_2^{-2}\), where the polynomial norm is the Euclidean norm of its coefficient vector.

For example take the [optimal fixed-input outer exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v670.md#statement) \(\gamma=2m/d\).
Write
\[
  u_*=\frac{m}{d\max_- a_\rho},\qquad v_*=\frac{m}{d\max_+ a_\rho}.
\]
Then \(u_*+v_*\le1\), since each maximum is at least its side's mean.
Choose \(L=\lfloor u_*W\rfloor\), \(R=\lfloor v_*W\rfloor\), reducing a positive one of the two by one if necessary to leave \(I\) nonempty.
These choices give \(S=mW\).
In particular the same \(A\) has \(T_J(A)\ll_{P,\epsilon}e^{-2mW/d}\) and
\[
       E(A)\le(1/12+\epsilon)(1-u_*-v_*)W+O_{P,\epsilon}(1).      \tag{2}
\]
If \(L/W\) or \(R/W\) exceeds its displayed edge fraction by a fixed positive amount, the corresponding summand raises \(S-mW\) by a positive multiple of \(W\).
Thus the same outer rate and an inner tail below the support cutoff cannot coexist there as \(W\to\infty\).
These fixed-polynomial conclusions give explicit constants on the constructive side, with no uniform bound asserted for \(B(P)\) as \(P\) varies.

<a id="weights"></a>
## The two windows on one order lattice

Represent the nonzero value of a numerator supported in \(I+\{0,\ldots,d\}\) by writing \(A=X^LD+PQ\), where \(\deg D<d\) and \(Q\) is supported in \(I\).
This follows by monic division of \(X^{-L}A\), and \(D\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is nonzero.
Conversely every nonzero integral \(D\) of degree \(<d\) is allowed with \(Q=0\).
All such \(Q\) preserve both tails.
If \(T_I(A)<\|P\|_2^{-2}\), convolution with \(P\) bounds each integer coefficient of \(A\) outside \(I+\{0,\ldots,d\}\) by \(\|P\|_2\sqrt{T_I(A)}<1\), giving the claimed automatic support.

Write \(y_\rho=D(\rho)/P'(\rho)\) and \(\lambda_\rho=\rho\) on the interior and \(\rho^{-1}\) on the exterior.
For the proper quotient \(D/P\), the [partial fractions](Yin_C_G0345_v670.md#lattice) give geometric sequences with nodes \(\lambda_\rho\) for the two tails.
The numerator \(X^LD\) translates those sequences by \(L\).
In the left tails of \(I,J\), respectively, the initial scalars multiplying \(y_\rho(\lambda_\rho^k)_{k\ge0}\) are \(1,\rho^L\).
In the right tails of \(I,J\) they are \(-\rho^{-(W-R-L)-1},-\rho^{-(W-L)-1}\).
The quadratic form \(F=T_I+e^{\gamma W}T_J\) is the squared norm of the four sequences taken together, with the two outer sequences multiplied by \(e^{\gamma W/2}\).

Let \(b_\rho\) be the larger absolute value of the two weighted scalars on its side.
Thus \(b_\rho=\exp(\max(0,\gamma W/2-a_\rho L))\) inside, and
\(b_\rho=\exp(-a_\rho(W-L+1)+\max(a_\rho R,\gamma W/2))\) outside.
Summing the logarithms and using \(\sum_- a_\rho=\sum_+a_\rho=m\) gives \(\prod_\rho b_\rho=e^{S-mW-m}\).
On either side the map with columns \((\lambda_\rho^k)_{k\ge0}\) has a positive least singular value, since its first square block is Vandermonde on distinct nodes.
Taking the smaller squared singular value \(c>0\) of the two maps, we get
\[
 F\ge c\sum_\rho |b_\rho y_\rho|^2
   \ge cd\,\big(e^{S-mW-m}/|\operatorname{disc}P|\big)^{2/d}.
\]
The second inequality uses \(|N(D(\alpha))|\ge1\) and \(\prod_\rho|P'(\rho)|=|\operatorname{disc}P|\).

For the upper bound let \(H\) be the real positive definite matrix of \(F\) in the \(d\) monomial coefficients of \(D\).
The map from these coefficients to \((y_\rho)\) has squared absolute determinant \(1/|\operatorname{disc}P|\) by the Vandermonde formula.
The four-sequence map from \((y_\rho)\) has each column of squared norm at most \(2b_\rho^2/(1-e^{-2a_\rho})\).
Hadamard's inequality on its complex Gram matrix and the determinant of the evaluation map give
\[
 (\det H)^{1/d}
 \le 2e^{-2m/d}Z\,
 \left(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{-1}\prod_\rho(1-e^{-2a_\rho})^{-1}\right)^{1/d}.
\]
The real matrix \(H\) equals this composite map's Hermitian Gram matrix because its columns are real sequences, including the sums of conjugate terms.
The [Euclidean shortest-vector bound](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#packing) gives a nonzero integer \(D\) with \(F\le d(\det H)^{1/d}\).
Indeed Minkowski's theorem on the ball gives this factor since the radius-\(\sqrt d\) ball contains the side-two cube.
Choose a shortest vector in this finite-dimensional lattice, for instance by enumerating integer vectors in the ellipsoid up to any known nonzero candidate's value.
This gives \(F\le B(P)Z\) before any central rounding.

<a id="scalar"></a>
## One bounded multiplier for an arbitrary finite list of phases

For integers \(N\ge2\) and \(s\ge1\), every finite list of real numbers \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\) admits an integer \(n\) with
\[
  1\le n\le 2^{s-1}(N-1),\qquad
  \sum_{i=1}^k\operatorname{dist}(nt_i,\mathbb Z)^2
  \le k\,\frac{N}{N-1}\left(\frac1{12}+\frac1{12(2^s-1)}\right). \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(q(\epsilon)=2^{s-1}(N-1)\) works in (1) for any pair making the factor on \(k\) at most \(1/12+\epsilon\).

Set \(g(t)=\{t\}^2-\{t\}+1/6\), using the fractional part, and \(f(t)=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mathbb Z)^2\).
Substitution on the two half intervals gives \(f(t)=1/12+g(2t)/2-g(t)\).
The identity
\[
  g(x-y)=2\int_0^1(\{u+x\}-1/2)(\{u+y\}-1/2)\,du
\]
follows by translating \(u\) and splitting the unit interval at \(1-\{x-y\}\).
For independent uniform integers \(A_1,A_2\) on \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\), putting \(V=A_1-A_2\) now gives \(\mathbb E g(Vt)\ge0\).
Indeed independence changes the expected product in the integral into a square.
Also \(g\le1/6\).
Give \(j=0,\ldots,s-1\) probabilities proportional to \(2^{-j}\), independently of \(V\).
In the unnormalized dyadic sum of \(\mathbb E f(2^j Vt)-1/12\), cancellation leaves just \(2^{-s}\mathbb E g(2^s Vt)-\mathbb E g(Vt)\le2^{-s}/6\).
Division by \(2(1-2^{-s})\), summation over all \(t_i\), and conditioning on \(V\ne0\) give (3), since \(f(0)=0\) and \(\Pr(V\ne0)=(N-1)/N\).
Choose \(n=2^j|V|\) for which the sum of squared distances is at most its conditional mean.
The constant \(1/12\) cannot be reduced for arbitrary phase lists with a bounded multiplier independent of the list.
Indeed for \(t_i=i/p\), \(0\le i<p\), with a prime \(p\) larger than all allowed multipliers, each multiplier permutes the residues, whose mean \(f\) is \((1-p^{-2})/12\) for odd \(p\).

Apply (3) to the \(|I|\) quotient coefficients of \(X^L D/P\) in \(I\) and multiply the numerator by the resulting \(n\).
Subtract \(P\) times the polynomial of nearest integers on \(I\).
The new numerator represents the nonzero value \(n\alpha^LD(\alpha)\), and its value of \(F\) is at most \(q^2\) times the original one.
The rounded middle costs at most \((1/12+\epsilon)|I|\), and adding \(T_I\le F\) proves (1).
