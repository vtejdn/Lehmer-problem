# The trace polynomial of an odd squarefree cyclotomic polynomial has no integral critical value

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simple fibers and the two endpoint multiplicities

Let \(q\ge3\) be odd and squarefree, let \(2d=\varphi(q)\), and let \(\Psi_q\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be the monic polynomial determined by
\[
            A_q(z)=z^{-d}\Phi_q(z)=\Psi_q(z+z^{-1}).
\]
At every root of unity \(\xi\) of order \(m>2\), one has \(A'_q(\xi)\ne0\).
In particular a torsion root of order greater than two is simple in every fiber \(A_q(z)=a\), for any \(a\in\mathbb C\).
Neither a root of \(\Psi'_q\) nor its image under \(\Psi_q\) can be an algebraic integer.
Consequently \(\Psi_q(T)-a\) is squarefree over \(\mathbb C\) for every algebraic integer \(a\).
In its polynomial lift \(\Phi_q(z)-az^d\), each of \(z=1,-1\) is double precisely when \(a=\Psi_q(2),\Psi_q(-2)\), respectively, and all other roots are simple.

For members of the [critical-degree family](Yin_C_G0345_v89.md#statement) with at least two distinct prime divisors of \(q\), one has \(\Psi_q(2)=\Psi_q(-2)=1\).
Hence \(\Phi_q(z)+z^d\) is squarefree, and \(\Phi_q(z)-z^d\) is \((z^2-1)^2\) times a squarefree polynomial coprime to \(z^2-1\).
These multiplicities include all irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\).

<a id="torsion-derivative"></a>
## The logarithmic derivative at a primitive root

Put \(D=z\,d/dz\) and \(h(w)=(w+1)/(2(w-1))\).
For \(z\) away from the zeros and poles in its Möbius product, \(\Phi_q(z)=\prod_{j\mid q}(z^j-1)^{\mu(q/j)}\) gives
\[
                \frac{D A_q(z)}{A_q(z)}
                 =\sum_{j\mid q}\mu(q/j)j\,h(z^j).
\]
Indeed \(\sum_{j\mid q}\mu(q/j)j=2d\), so the halves subtracted from the individual logarithmic derivatives account exactly for the centering monomial.
At \(m=q\), each primitive \(q\)-th root is already a simple zero of \(\Phi_q\).
For \(m>2\), \(m\ne q\), fix \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/m)\), so \(A_q(\zeta)\ne0\).
Write \(s=\gcd(m,q)\), \(t=q/s\), and let \(\sigma_p(\zeta)=\zeta^p\) for \(p\mid t\).
Then
\[
       \frac{D A_q(\zeta)}{A_q(\zeta)}
         =\prod_{p\mid t}(p\sigma_p-1)\, B_s,\qquad
       B_s=\sum_{\substack{e\mid s\\e<m}}\mu(s/e)e\,h(\zeta^e).
       \tag{1}
\]
To see this, write each \(j\mid q\) as \(eb\) with \(e\mid s\), \(b\mid t\).
For \(e<m\), the operator in (1) expands as the sum over \(b\mid t\) with coefficients \(\mu(t/b)b\) acting by \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^b\).
The only remaining terms occur when \(e=s=m\), in which case \(t>1\).
At \(z=\zeta\exp(u)\) the term \(mb\,h(z^{mb})\) is \(u^{-1}+O(u)\), with zero constant term.
The poles cancel since \(\sum_{b\mid t}\mu(t/b)=0\), so these terms contribute zero in the limit.
Each \(p\sigma_p-1\) is invertible on \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), because a vector in its kernel would be fixed by multiplication by \(p^r\), where \(r\) is the order of \(\sigma_p\).
It remains to show \(B_s\ne0\).

<a id="cotangent"></a>
## The sign of the remaining cotangent sum

Write \(F(x)=1-x\cot x=\sum_{k\ge1}c_k x^{2k}\) for \(|x|<\pi\).
The differential equation \(xF'=x^2-F+F^2\) gives \(c_1=1/3\) and
\[
                 (2k+1)c_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}c_j c_{k-j}\qquad(k\ge2).
\]
All coefficients are strictly positive.
For \(s=1\), \(B_s=h(\zeta)\ne0\) because \(\zeta\ne-1\).
For \(1<s<m\), put \(x=\pi/m\).
Since \(h(\zeta^e)=-i\cot(ex)/2\), it suffices to check the real sum
\[
 \sum_{e\mid s}\mu(s/e)e\cot(ex)
   =-\sum_{k\ge1}c_k x^{2k-1}
           \prod_{p\mid s}(p^{2k}-1)<0 .
\]
Here every \(ex<\pi\), the constant term \(1/x\) cancels, and the product comes from the squarefree divisor sum.

If \(s=m\), then \(m\) is odd and squarefree.
Set \(S=\sum_{e\mid m,\ e<m}\mu(m/e)e\cot(\pi e/m)\).
Substituting \(\ell=m/e\) and expanding \(F(\pi/\ell)\) only for \(\ell>1\) gives
\[
     \frac{\pi S}{m}
        =-1+\sum_{k\ge1}c_k\pi^{2k}
                    \left(1-\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2k})\right).
\]
The initial term is \(\sum_{\ell\mid m,\ \ell>1}\mu(\ell)=-1\).
By \(1-\prod_i(1-u_i)\le\sum_i u_i\) for \(0\le u_i\le1\), the positive sum is at most \(\sum_{p\mid m}F(\pi/p)\).
Positivity of the coefficients makes \(F(x)/x^2\) increasing on \((0,\pi)\).
As \(p\ge3\), this gives \(F(\pi/p)\le 9F(\pi/3)/p^2=(9-\pi\sqrt3)/p^2\).
The telescoping sum \(\sum_{j\ge3,\ j\ {\rm odd}}1/(j^2-1)=1/4\) now yields
\[
              \sum_{p\mid m}F(\pi/p)
                   \le (9-\pi\sqrt3)/4<1.
\]
Thus \(S<0\) also in this case.
Equation (1) proves nonvanishing at \(\zeta\), and Galois conjugation gives the same conclusion at every primitive root of order \(m\).

<a id="integral-values"></a>
## Critical points, integral values and ramification of the trace map

The roots of \(\Psi_q\) are the \(d\) distinct traces of inverse pairs of primitive \(q\)-th roots, all in \((-2,2)\).
Rolle's theorem therefore places all \(d-1\) roots of \(\Psi'_q\) strictly inside \((-2,2)\) as well.
If such a critical point \(c\) were an algebraic integer, all its conjugates would lie in that interval.
A root \(z\) of \(z^2-cz+1=0\) would then be a nonzero algebraic integer all of whose conjugates have modulus one.
By [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization), \(z\) is a root of unity.
The strict interval excludes \(z=\pm1\), while \(A_q'(z)=(1-z^{-2})\Psi_q'(c)=0\) contradicts the torsion derivative result.
Thus every critical point is nonintegral.
If \(\Psi_q(c)\) were an algebraic integer, the monic equation \(\Psi_q(T)-\Psi_q(c)=0\) and transitivity of integrality would force \(c\) to be integral too.
This proves squarefreeness of the trace fibers over all algebraic integers.
For a rational critical value \(a\), its reduced denominator is greater than one and divides \(d^d\).
Indeed \(dc\) is integral since \(\Psi_q'\) has integer coefficients and leading coefficient \(d\), and hence \(d^d a=d^d\Psi_q(c)\) is a rational algebraic integer.

The substitution \(T=z+z^{-1}\) has nonzero derivative away from \(z=\pm1\), and \(T-2=(z-1)^2/z,\ T+2=(z+1)^2/z\).
This gives exactly the endpoint multiplicities stated above, since \(\Psi_q'(\pm2)\ne0\) and the polynomial lift has constant coefficient one.
For \(q\) with two or more prime factors the [endpoint evaluations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0457_v2.md#central-minus-one-trace-reduction) give the stated signs.
For a prime \(q\), the same general result uses \(\Psi_q(2)=q\) and \(\Psi_q(-2)=(-1)^d\), by summing the geometric series \(\Phi_q\) at \(1,-1\).
