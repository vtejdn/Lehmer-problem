# Pisot inverses at every grid size with an explicit bound on their excess

<a id="statement"></a>
## A prescribed Salem factor at each even degree

Let \(P_0\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma\), let \(m=\deg P_0\ge4\), and set \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) and \(h=m/2-1\).
Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_h\in(-2,2)\) be the neutral traces of \(P_0\), and write \(P_0(z)=z^{m/2}Q_0(z+z^{-1})\) and \(H_0(x)=Q_0(x)/(x-T)\).
Define
\[
 B_h=\sqrt{2h\log(4h)},\qquad
 K=3B_h\sum_{i=1}^h\frac{1}{(2+x_i)|H_0'(x_i)|},\qquad
 K_*=6B_h h(T+2)m^{m/2}\sigma^{m-1}.
\]
Then \(K\le K_*\).
For every integer \(q>\max(2,K)\) and every even \(d\ge m\) there is a Pisot number \(\theta\) whose monic minimal polynomial \(f\) has degree \(d\), constant term \(+q\), and full return
\[
 \frac{zf(z)+f^*(z)}{z+1}=
 \begin{cases}
   P_0(z),&d=m,\\
   P_0(z)(z^{2k}+1),&d=m+2k,\quad k\ge1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad f^*(z)=z^d f(1/z).
\]
Put \(\tau_0=0,\ \tau_1=T,\ \tau_k=T-1\) for \(k\ge2\).
The inverse can be chosen with
\[
 0<\theta-q-(T-2)
 \le\frac{T-2}{q-1}+\frac{4K+\tau_k}{q-2}.                         \tag{1}
\]
Thus one sufficiently large constant \(q\) works simultaneously at all these degrees.
For each positive grid size the full return \(P\) satisfies \(P(1)=2P_0(1)\) and \(P(-1)=2P_0(-1)\).
At the square constant the error in (1) is \(O_{P_0}(d^{-2})\).
The [all-even-degree limit](Yin_C_G0345_v370.md#statement) gave an input-dependent onset and a larger upper error.
The [lower estimate below](#lower) applies to every inverse of this same prescribed grid return.

<a id="grid"></a>
## An integer polynomial with prescribed residues at the new traces

For \(k\ge1\) let \(B=C_k\), where \(C_k(2\cos t)=2\cos(kt)\).
The recurrence with \(C_0=2,\ C_1=x\) shows that \(B\) is integral and monic of degree \(k\), and \(z^k B(z+z^{-1})=z^{2k}+1\).
Its roots \(y_j=2\cos t_j\), \(t_j=(2j-1)\pi/(2k)\), \(1\le j\le k\), are simple and belong to \((-2,2)\).
All grid points in the \(z\)-plane are roots of unity, whereas every root of the irreducible \(P_0\) is nontorsion.
Consequently \(Q_0B\) has simple roots, exactly the old exterior trace and the old and new neutral traces.

Use \(V_{-1}=0,\ V_0=1,\ V_{n+1}=xV_n-V_{n-1}\), so \(V_n(2\cos t)=\sin((n+1)t)/\sin t\).
The polynomial \(D_k=V_{k-1}+V_{k-2}\) is integral and monic of degree \(k-1\), including \(D_1=1\).
Differentiation gives \(B'=kV_{k-1}\), and \(\cos(kt_j)=0\) gives \(V_{k-2}(y_j)=\cos(t_j)V_{k-1}(y_j)\).
It follows that
\[
             \frac{D_k(y_j)}{B'(y_j)}=\frac{1+y_j/2}{k}.             \tag{2}
\]

Apply the [rounding estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#square-interpolation) to \(P_0\) with auxiliary constant \(q_0=q+1\).
It gives an integer polynomial \(R_0\) of degree \(h\) with leading coefficient \(1-q_0\) and residues \(b_i=-R_0(x_i)/H_0'(x_i)>0\) satisfying
\[
               \sum_i\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}\le K,\qquad
               \sum_i b_i\le 4K .
\]
Indeed its strict hypothesis is \(K<q_0+T/2\), which follows from \(K<q\).
For \(Q=Q_0B,\ H=H_0B\), set
\[
                       R=BR_0+Q_0D_k .
\]
The leading coefficients sum to \(1-q_0+1=1-q\), so \(R\) has the required degree \(h+k\) and fixes the constant \(q\) of the enlarged inverse.
At an old root \(x_i\), the factors \(B(x_i)\ne0\) in \(R(x_i)\) and \(H'(x_i)\) cancel, leaving exactly \(b_i\).
At a new root \(y=y_j\), the identity \(Q_0(y)=(y-T)H_0(y)\) gives
\[
       b_y=-\frac{R(y)}{H'(y)}
           =\frac{(T-y)(1+y/2)}{k}>0,\qquad
       \sum_{j=1}^k\frac{b_{y_j}}{2+y_j}=\frac T2 .                  \tag{3}
\]
The second equality uses \(\sum_j y_j=0\) and holds also for \(k=1\).
When \(k\ge2\), the sum of \(e^{2it_j}\) vanishes by the geometric series, giving \(\sum_j y_j^2=2k\).
Expansion of (3) yields \(\sum_j b_{y_j}=T-1\) in this case.
For \(k=1\) the sole node is zero, with residue \(T\).

The total weighted residue is at most \(K+T/2<q+T/2\).
All residues are strictly positive, so the [full-return inverse criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) gives a Pisot minimal polynomial of degree \(d\) and constant \(q\).
Explicitly it is \(f(z)=P(z)+(z-1)z^{d/2-1}R(z+z^{-1})\).
The same criterion gives \(\theta>q\) and the identity
\[
       \theta-q=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}(T-2)
                +\sum_{x}\frac{b_x}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x},           \tag{4}
\]
with \(x\) running over all neutral traces.
Use \(x<2\) and the bound \(4K+\tau_k\) on the total residue to obtain (1).
For \(d=m\) use the rounding estimate directly at \(q\) and (4), with the same bounds on the old residues.
Every positive grid has \(z^{2k}+1=2\) at \(z=1,-1\).

<a id="threshold"></a>
## An effective threshold for moving inputs

Put \(A(z)=(z+1)P_0(z)\), an integral monic polynomial with \(m+1\) distinct roots and Mahler measure \(\sigma\).
For any root \(\xi\), the absolute determinant of the Vandermonde matrix of all the roots is \(|A'(\xi)|\) times the absolute Vandermonde determinant of the other \(m\) roots.
The first determinant is at least one in absolute value, since its square in absolute value is the absolute nonzero integer discriminant.
For each other root \(v\) the row of powers from zero through \(m-1\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt m\max(1,|v|)^{m-1}\).
Hadamard's inequality therefore gives
\[
                         |A'(\xi)|^{-1}\le m^{m/2}\sigma^{m-1}.
\]
At a neutral root \(\xi=e^{it_i}\) with trace \(x_i\), the [derivative identity](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#square-padding) bounds \(1/((2+x_i)|H_0'(x_i)|)\) by \(2(T+2)/|A'(\xi)|\).
Summing over the \(h\) upper-half-circle representatives proves \(K\le K_*\).
Thus all even \(d\ge m\) with \(d^2>K_*\) satisfy the square-constant conclusion.
For varying Salem inputs and \(d\to\infty\), \(K_*=o(d^2)\) with \(d\ge m\) even suffices for an excess \(T-2+o(1)\), since \(T\le K_*\).
In particular, when \(\sigma\) stays bounded and \(m\log m\le(4-\varepsilon)\log d\) for some fixed \(0<\varepsilon<4\), one has \(K_*=o(d^2)\).
Indeed \(\log K_*\le(m/2)\log m+O(m+\log m)\) uniformly for bounded \(\sigma\), with \(m=o(\log d)\) under this condition.
These bounds give no unconditional sequence of exterior Salem roots tending to one.

<a id="lower"></a>
## A lower bound for every inverse of the prescribed grid

Suppose \(k\ge1\) and a monic Pisot minimal polynomial with \(q\ge2\) has the full return \(P=P_0(z)(z^{2k}+1)\) above.
The [integer parametrization and necessity of the strict residue signs](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) extend to this simple reducible return.
Indeed antisymmetry of \(f-P\) supplies an integral \(R\) of the stated degree, independently of the factorization of \(P\).
All neutral residues are nonzero because \(f\) has no unit root.
Also \(f(-1)>0\) by the root signs and the even degree.
Along the linear path from the quadratic factor times the neutral factor to \(f\), the [root-displacement calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) then excludes all circle crossings for positive path parameter.
Each negative initial residue would send a pair outside in addition to the positive exterior root, contradicting the Pisot hypothesis.
Thus all the residues in (4) are positive.

At every grid node \(y_j\), \(b_{y_j}=|R(y_j)|/(|H_0(y_j)|\,|B'(y_j)|)\).
Since \(B\) is monic integral and \(R\) is integral,
\(\prod_j|R(y_j)|=|\operatorname{Res}(B,R)|\ge1\).
The other factors satisfy
\[
 \prod_j|H_0(y_j)|=\prod_{i=1}^h|B(x_i)|\le2^h,\qquad
 \prod_j|B'(y_j)|=2^{k-1}k^k .
\]
For the last identity use \(|B'(y_j)|=k/\sin t_j\) and
\(\prod_j 2\sin t_j=\sqrt{|B(2)B(-2)|}=2\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives \(\sum_j b_{y_j}\ge 2^{-1+(1-h)/k}\).
In (4) each denominator at a grid root is at most \(\theta+\theta^{-1}+2\), so every such inverse satisfies
\[
 \theta-q-(T-2)\ \ge\
     \frac{T-2}{\theta-1}
       +\frac{2^{-1+(1-h)/k}}{\theta+\theta^{-1}+2}.                 \tag{5}
\]
For fixed \(P_0\), as \(k,q\to\infty\) through any such inverses, the [two-point estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) gives \(\theta=q+O_{P_0}(1)\).
Hence \(\liminf q(\theta-q-(T-2))\ge T-2+1/2\).
Together with (1), this proves the order \(d^{-2}\) for the least excess above \(T-2\) at the prescribed single-grid return and \(q=d^2\).
Other full returns at the same degree can have different neutral traces and need not satisfy this grid bound.
