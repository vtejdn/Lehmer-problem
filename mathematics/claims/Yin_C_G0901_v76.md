# Linear restrictions on the local fixed dimensions of every constituent at small total measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Complex conjugation and decomposition groups at growing rank

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(L\) its splitting field, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), \(c\) usual complex conjugation, and \(V\) the rational span of the multiplicative classes of all its conjugates modulo roots of unity.
Write \(m(w)=(\deg w)h(w)\).
For each nonzero irreducible rational constituent \(W\subset V\), put \(r=\dim W\), \(j=\dim W^{-c}\).
If \(p=1-4j/r>0\), then
\[
                     m(w)\ \ge\ p\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/p}).          \tag{1}
\]
In particular along any positive shrinking-measure sequence every constituent satisfies \(j/r\ge1/4-o(1)\), uniformly in the choice of constituent.
The same bound holds for the argument rank divided by the multiplicative conjugate rank of every nontorsion element in the entire divisible conjugate group.
This linear obstruction applies with both ranks unbounded.

Fix a rational prime \(\ell\), and at each input choose a place of \(L\) above \(\ell\), with decomposition group \(D\subset G\).
For every fixed integer \(A\ge1\), along such a sequence one has
\[
       \frac{\dim W^H}{r}\ \le\ \frac12+o(1)
       \qquad(H\subset D,\ [D:H]\le A).                                  \tag{2}
\]
The error tends to zero uniformly over the constituents, places and indicated subgroups, without a ramification restriction.
At an unramified prime, for every fixed \(B\ge1\), no more than \(1/2+o(1)\) of the eigenvalues of Frobenius on any constituent can have order at most \(B\), counting multiplicities.
Each violation by a fixed positive margin yields an effective positive lower bound on the original total measure.
These restrictions do not control constituents outside the specified ranges.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Small orbits from a shortest lattice vector

Intersect \(W\) with the lattice of multiplicative classes generated by the original conjugates, obtaining a full \(G\)-invariant lattice in \(W\).
Give \(V\) a \(G\)-invariant positive definite rational inner product and choose a shortest nonzero lattice vector \(v\) in \(W\).
For any \(u\in Gv\) and \(s\in G\), either \(su=u\) or \(\|u-su\|\ge\|v\|\), since their difference is a nonzero lattice vector.
For a subgroup \(H\subset G\) let \(P_H=|H|^{-1}\sum_{s\in H}s\) on \(W\), the orthogonal projection onto \(W^H\).
Writing \(m_H(u)=|H\cdot u|\) gives
\[
  2\|(1-P_H)u\|^2
    =\frac1{|H|}\sum_{s\in H}\|u-su\|^2
    \ \ge\ (1-1/m_H(u))\|v\|^2.
\]
Averaging \(g^{-1}P_H g\) over \(g\in G\) gives \((\dim W^H/r)I\).
Indeed the average is central and its scalar on each complex irreducible summand is the dimension of that summand's \(H\)-fixed subspace divided by its dimension.
Those scalars are rational, so the rational eigenspaces and irreducibility of \(W\) leave just one, as in the [conjugation projection calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#overlap).
Averaging the last inequality at \(u=gv\) consequently gives
\[
                    \mathbb E_g\frac1{m_H(gv)}
                            \ \ge\ 2\dim W^H/r-1 .                         \tag{3}
\]
The shortest vector removes the dimension-dependent overlap cost of the earlier projection estimate.

The [selected product of original conjugates](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection), choosing the sign of \(v\) to use at most half the roots, gives a nontorsion unit \(z\in L\) satisfying \(2h(z)\le m(w)\).
The stabilizer of \(v\) fixes \(z\) as an actual number, even when root-of-unity factors occur among the conjugates.
For all \(g\), \(m_H(gz)\le m_H(gv)\).
With \(H=\langle c\rangle\) the latter orbit size is one or two, and (3) gives \(\Pr(cgv=gv)\ge1-4j/r\).
At all such embeddings \(gz\) is real.
Applying the [real-conjugate height estimate](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) proves (1).
For a nontorsion \(x\) in the divisible conjugate group, \(V_x\) is a nonzero rational submodule of \(V\); its argument-rank fraction is the weighted average of these negative-dimension fractions by the [argument rank formula](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection).
This gives the stated uniformity for such elements.

<a id="local"></a>
## A positive fraction of bounded local degrees costs positive height

Fix an embedding of \(L\) into an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) inducing the chosen place.
For \(u\in L\), its degree over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) in that embedding equals \(|D\cdot u|\), by restriction of the local absolute Galois group.
For any \(H\subset D\) of index at most \(A\), \(|D\cdot u|\le A |H\cdot u|\) by the coset decomposition, with no normality assumption.
Suppose \(2\dim W^H/r-1\ge\lambda>0\).
Choose an integer \(C\ge2/\lambda\).
The reciprocal orbit size is at most one and is at most \(1/(C+1)\) when \(m_H(gv)>C\), so (3) implies \(\Pr(m_H(gv)\le C)\ge\lambda/2\).
For the same embeddings the local degree of \(gz\) is at most \(B_0=AC\).

Here is an explicit height bound for a nontorsion unit \(z\) for which at least a fraction \(\delta>0\) of embeddings has local degree at most \(B_0\).
Let \(T=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le f\le B_0}(\ell^f-1)\), take any integer \(a\ge0\), and put \(N=T\ell^a\).
Normalize the \(\ell\)-adic valuation by \(\nu(\ell)=1\).
At each such embedding, residue degree and ramification index are at most \(B_0\), and the residue of \(z^T\) is one, so \(\nu(z^T-1)\ge1/B_0\).
For \(\nu(y)\ge1/B_0\), the binomial formula gives
\(\nu((1+y)^\ell-1)\ge\min(1+\nu(y),\ell\nu(y))\ge\nu(y)+1/B_0\).
Thus \(\nu(z^N-1)\ge(a+1)/B_0\) at the selected embeddings.
At other finite places \(z^N-1\) is integral and at infinity its mean log modulus is at most \(\log2+Nh(z)\).
Use the product formula for the nonzero \(z^N-1\), counting all embeddings at \(\ell\) with their local multiplicities, to obtain
\[
             Nh(z)\ge\frac{\delta(a+1)}{B_0}\log\ell-\log2 .
\]
Set \(\delta=\lambda/2\) and choose \(a\) giving a positive right side.
Together with \(m(w)\ge2h(z)\), this is effective and uniform for fixed \(\ell,A,\lambda\), proving (2).

If \(\ell\) is unramified, \(D\) is cyclic with a Frobenius generator \(s\).
Taking \(H=\langle s^{B!}\rangle\) gives index at most \(B!\).
All eigenvalues of order at most \(B\) are fixed by \(s^{B!}\) and finite-order operators are diagonalizable in characteristic zero.
This proves the final spectral restriction.
