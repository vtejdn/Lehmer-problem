# An all-degree lower bound of 1.3 at conjugate argument rank at most one

<a id="statement"></a>
## The gap and the remaining finite interval

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(M(w)\) be its multiplicative Mahler measure, and let \(k\) be the rank modulo roots of unity of the multiplicative group generated by the arguments \(a/|a|\) of its conjugates.
If \(k\le1\), then \(M(w)>13/10\) in every degree.
The minimum on this class exists and is at most the plastic number \(\theta\), the positive root of \(X^3-X-1\).
Any value strictly below \(\theta\) in this class is realized by a reciprocal unit with distinct conjugates modulo torsion and degree at most 42.
The finite list below \(13/10\) suffices for the lower bound.
The exact minimum in \((13/10,\theta]\) remains undetermined here.

<a id="reflection"></a>
## The conjugate reflections and their primitive lattice normals

Write \(m=\log M(w)\) and suppose \(m<\log\theta\).
The [nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives reciprocity.
Apply the [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) and use \(w\) henceforth for the compressed unit.
The compression preserves \(m,k\), and inversion permutes the new roots since it permutes the old classes and inverts their products.
Write \(d=[\mathbb Q(w):\mathbb Q]\), \(h=m/d\), and let \(\Lambda\) be the free abelian group spanned by the classes of these roots modulo torsion.
Its rational span \(V\) has dimension \(b\).
The splitting group \(G\) acts faithfully on \(V\), since fixing a class fixes its unique actual conjugate.
Use a \(G\)-invariant inner product and let \(c\) be complex conjugation.
Its negative eigenspace has dimension \(k\), by the [argument-rank identity](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection).

If a nonzero rational constituent has trivial \(c\)-action, every conjugate of \(c\) fixes that constituent pointwise.
The [selected-product argument](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection) then gives a totally real nontorsion unit of height at most \(m/2\).
The [real-conjugate bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) implies \(m\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2)>\log\theta\).
Consequently \(k=1\) and \(V\) is rationally irreducible, since only one constituent in a rational decomposition can carry the single negative dimension.
Rank \(b=1\) is impossible: \(c=-I\) would give \([a\bar a]=0\) for every conjugate \(a\), hence \(a\bar a=1\) by positivity, contrary to Kronecker's theorem.

The conjugates \(s\) of \(c\) act as orthogonal reflections.
Their normal lines span \(V\), since the span is rational, nonzero and invariant.
On each choose both primitive vectors in its intersection with \(\Lambda\).
They have a common length, which we normalize to \(\sqrt2\).
For normal vectors \(v,u\), the difference \(v-s_u v=(v,u)u\) belongs to \(\Lambda\), so \((v,u)\in\mathbb Z\).
For distinct lines the inner product is thus \(0\) or \(\pm1\).
Their set of signed normals is a finite simply laced root system, stable under all its root reflections.
The [finite reflection-group classification](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#finite-classification) gives components of types \(A,D,E\), since the simple-root inner products allow only unlabelled bonds.
All components are isomorphic because \(G\) is transitive on the normal lines.
The reflection group of the roots is contained in \(G\), which in turn preserves the whole root system.

Let \(C\) be the number of lines.
Write \(p\) for the fraction of these lines perpendicular to one fixed line.
The mean of their orthogonal projectors is \(I/b\), as in the [averaging argument](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#overlap).
Indeed the average commutes with every reflection and therefore acts by a scalar on its one-dimensional negative space.
Transitivity makes these scalars equal, the lines span, and the average has trace one.
For a fixed projector \(P\), its trace overlap with a random one equals \(1\) at its own line, \(0\) on perpendicular lines and \(1/4\) otherwise.
It follows that
\[
                     p=1-4/b+3/C.                                        \tag{1}
\]
A conjugate on the unit circle lies on the negative line of \(c\) in \(V\), since its complex conjugate is its inverse.
The classes in the orbit have equal length, and compression makes them distinct.
Thus the compressed unit has at most one inverse pair on the unit circle.
If that pair exists the whole orbit consists of the two orientations of every normal line, since all lines are conjugate and the orbit is reciprocal.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## A unit of controlled height on one normal line

For \(x=[w]\) define the positive integer \(K=\max_v |(x,v)|\) over all primitive normals.
These pairings are integers by the same difference formula, and one is nonzero since the normals span.
Lift any primitive normal to an actual unit \(\beta\) in the splitting field by an integer monomial in the conjugates.
Height is independent of a torsion factor.
At a maximizing normal \(v\), \([w/s_v(w)]=(x,v)v\).
Conjugacy and height invariance therefore give \(h(\beta)\le 2h/K\) for every normal.

Suppose \(p>0\) and use the normal to \(c\).
For each conjugate reflection \(s\) with line perpendicular to it let \(\eta_s=s(\beta)/\beta\), a root of unity since \(s\) fixes the normal.
Every such \(s\) inverts torsion, since restrictions to roots of unity are the same for all complex conjugations.
For two distinct such \(s,t\), their product has order two or three by the angle between their lines and the faithfulness of the action.
It fixes torsion and sends \(\beta\) to \((\eta_s/\eta_t)\beta\).
The ratio of any two phases therefore has order dividing two or three.
The phases lie in one translate of \(\mu_j\) for a single \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\): ratios to a fixed phase cannot include both an element of order two and one of order three, since their quotient would have order six.
Take a root of unity \(\lambda\) with \(\lambda^2=\eta_t\) for a fixed \(t\).
In a Galois field also containing \(\lambda\), every conjugate of ordinary complex conjugation that restricts to one of these \(s\) inverts \(\lambda\) and fixes \(z=(\lambda\beta)^j\).
Uniform conjugation in the larger group projects to uniform conjugation in \(G\).
Thus \(z\) has at least a proportion \(p\) of real conjugates, is nontorsion, and has height at most \(2jm/(dK)\).
With \(F(p)=p\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/p})\), the increasing function twice the real-conjugate height bound, this gives
\[
                          dK\le 4j m/F(p).                                \tag{2}
\]
When all perpendicular lines are mutually orthogonal one may use \(j\le2\).
For the original compressed unit, each normal orthogonal to \(x\) also gives a real conjugation: fixing \([w]\) fixes \(w\) itself.
If their fraction is \(p_x>0\), then
\[
                          m\ge dF(p_x)/2.                                 \tag{3}
\]

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Bounds by rank and by the root orbit

We use the [classical coordinate models](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#finite-classification).
The \(A_b\) lines are given by coordinate differences in the sum-zero space, with \(C=b(b+1)/2\).
The \(D_b\) lines are the coordinate sums and differences, with \(C=b(b-1)\).
A root-system automorphism is a product of a Weyl group element and an automorphism of the simple diagram, by moving its chamber back to the chosen one.
In \(A_b\) the full group therefore enlarges the coordinate-permutation group by at most two.
In \(D_5\) the full group is the group of all signed permutations: these realize the even sign changes and the nontrivial diagram symmetry.

* For \(b\ge7\), (1) gives \(p\ge3/7\), so (2) with \(j\le3\) gives \(dK<41\).
* For \(b=6\) every possible system has \(C\le36\).
  The classical types and the equal-component products give this directly.
  To check the remaining \(E_6\) bound, use the even-coordinate-sum integer lattice in \(\mathbb R^8\) and its translate by \((1/2,\ldots,1/2)\), taking their union.
  This is an integral lattice, so its vectors of norm squared two preserve it by reflections.
  In the subspace \(x_6=x_7=x_8\) there are 40 such integer vectors and 32 half-integer ones, by choosing two signed positions among the first five or choosing half-signs of the required parity with the last three equal.
  The six roots \(e_1-e_2,e_2-e_3,e_3-e_4,e_4-e_5,e_4+e_5,-(e_1+\cdots+e_8)/2\) have the \(E_6\) simple Gram matrix, so their reflection orbits contain its roots in these 72 vectors.
  Thus \(p\ge5/12\) and \(dK<44\).
* A product of \(b\ge2\) components \(A_1\) has \(p=1-1/b\ge1/2\), so \(dK<28\).
  Two \(A_2\) components at rank four also give \(p=1/2\).
  A single \(A_2\) has at most twelve automorphisms, so \(d\le12\).
  These cases exhaust the remaining equal-component products in ranks at most five.
* For \(A_3\), let \(H=S_3\) permute the first three coordinates.
  Its averaging operator on the sum-zero space has nonzero image, since it fixes \((1,1,1,-3)\).
  Some conjugate class therefore has nonzero average, as the orbit spans.
  The product over the distinct members of its \(H\)-orbit is nontorsion and has height at most \(6m/d\).
  Three of the six conjugate reflections belong to \(H\) and fix that product exactly.
  Its real-conjugate height bound gives \(d\le12m/F(1/2)<28\).
* At rank five the worst value of \(p\) is \(7/20\), in type \(D_5\), so \(dK<71\).
  For \(K\ge2\) this gives \(d\le35\).
  In \(A_5\) with \(K=1\) all coordinate differences are integers of absolute value at most one.
  The coordinates take two values, giving a permutation orbit of size at most \(\binom63=20\), and \(d\le40\).
  In \(D_5\) all coordinate sums and differences for distinct indices are integers of absolute value at most one.
  The coordinates are either all integers or all strict half-integers.
  In the integer case only one entry is nonzero, of absolute value one.
  Otherwise every coordinate has absolute value \(1/2\).
  The signed-permutation orbit size is at most 32.
* In \(A_4\), \(p=3/10\) gives \(dK<115\), so \(K\ge3\) gives \(d\le38\).
  For \(K=1\) the coordinates have at most two levels and \(d\le2\binom52=20\).
  For \(K=2\) there are at most three levels.
  The possible multinomial orbit sizes on five coordinates are \(1,5,10,20,30\).
  Adjoining the diagram involution at most doubles each size.
  The only possibility above 40 is 60, which violates \(dK<115\).
* In \(D_4\), \(p=1/4\).
  For the line \(e_1-e_2\) the three perpendicular lines are \(e_1+e_2,e_3-e_4,e_3+e_4\), all mutually orthogonal.
  The unit \(z\) from the normal-line construction has \(j\le2\) and height \(H\le4m/(dK)\).
  A conjugate of \(z\) is real exactly when its normal line is perpendicular to \(e_1-e_2\): perpendicular conjugations give real images by construction, and every other conjugation moves the oriented class.
  The linear functional on \(V\) given by the logarithm of the ordinary modulus is \(c\)-invariant and has coordinate vector \((A,A,B,C')\) for some real \(A,B,C'\).
  The absolute log moduli of \(z\) are obtained by evaluating this functional at the uniformly occurring signed normals and multiplying by \(j\).
  Perpendicular lines contribute \(S=2|A|+2\max(|B|,|C'|)\) to the sum of absolute evaluations over lines.
  Its own line contributes zero, and the eight remaining lines contribute
  \(4(\max(|A|,|B|)+\max(|A|,|C'|))\ge2S\).
  Consequently the real conjugates account for at most one third of the mean absolute log modulus \(2H\) of this unit.
  At a real conjugate \(u\), the normalized factor \(|1-u^2|/\max(1,|u|^2)\) equals \(1-\exp(-2|\log|u||)\le2|\log|u||\).
  Its mean over the real conjugates is at most \(16H/3\), since their proportion is \(1/4\).
  The factor is at most two at the other archimedean conjugates and at most one at the finite places.
  The product formula and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, as in [the real-place proof](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#proof), give
  \(e^{-2H}\le2^{3/4}(16H/3)^{1/4}\).
  Thus \(1-8H\le e^{-8H}\le128H/3\), or \(H\ge3/152\).
  We obtain \(dK\le608m/3<58\), giving \(d\le28\) for \(K\ge2\).
  When \(K\le2\), at least three of the twelve normal lines are perpendicular to \(x\).
  Indeed, after sorting absolute values, any integral coordinate vector has either a lone entry of absolute value two or \(s\) entries of absolute value one with \(1\le s\le4\).
  A lone two leaves six perpendicular lines; the latter cases leave \(\binom{s}{2}+2\binom{4-s}{2}\ge3\).
  For half-integral coordinates at least three have absolute value \(1/2\), since the sum of the two largest absolute values is at most two.
  The three equal absolute values give at least three perpendicular lines.
  Formula (3) now gives \(d\le2m/F(1/4)<37\).

For the numerical inequalities one may use \(\log\theta<0.282\), \(\log(13/10)<0.263\) and \(\operatorname{arsinh}y\ge y-y^3/6\) for \(y\ge0\).
The logarithm comparisons follow from \(\theta<1.325\) by its cubic and the exponential series.
At \(p=3/7,5/12,7/20,3/10\) the values \(2^{-1/p}\) exceed \(0.198,0.189,0.1379,0.099\) respectively by integer powers.
At \(p=1/4,1/2\) they are exactly \(1/16,1/4\).
The compressed reciprocal degree is even, since an odd-degree reciprocal polynomial has a root at \(-1\).
The bounds above give \(d\le42\) in every case.

<a id="catalogue"></a>
## The finite exclusion and the upper endpoint

If \(M(w)<13/10\), its compressed minimum polynomial is reciprocal of degree at most 42 and cannot be a substitution \(S(X^n)\) with \(n>1\), since this would give distinct conjugates with a root-of-unity ratio.
Up to \(X\mapsto-X\) it therefore occurs in the [complete primitive catalogue](../audits/literature/Mos26/readme.md#complete-small-cores).
The [rational Sturm check on all 944 rows](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0189/readme.md#circle) gives at least three inverse pairs on the unit circle on each row.
The sign substitution preserves their number, contradicting the single-pair upper bound.
Equality at \(13/10\) is impossible: the measure of an algebraic unit is, up to sign, the product of its exterior conjugates, hence is an algebraic integer.

The plastic unit has a real exterior conjugate and one nonreal conjugate pair, so its argument rank is at most one and its measure is \(\theta\).
Its argument rank is exactly one: a torsion quotient of the nonreal pair would make a positive power of the unit have degree less than three, hence rational by the prime degree, which is impossible for a nontorsion unit.
This gives the stated upper endpoint.
The coefficient bounds for fixed-degree monic polynomials of bounded measure give only finitely many compressed polynomials through degree 42.
Together with the attained upper endpoint this proves existence of the minimum.
Determining it would require excluding or realizing the remaining interval up to \(\theta\); the degree-44 catalogue used here has the strict cutoff \(13/10\).
