## 1. Exact all-root contact and a dimension-free radial error

Let P be an actual monic irreducible nontorsion integer unit of degree d, with all original roots alpha_i=r_i z_i, |z_i|=1, and TOTAL m=log M(P)>0.
Choose an integer N>d, put T=N-1, and let R be L318's rank-d real rational orthogonal projection onto the complement of the coefficient vectors of P,tP,...,t^(N-d-1)P.
Set

    v_N(z)=(1,z,...,z^T)^t,
    k_N(z)=v_N(z)^*R v_N(z)/N, |z|=1,
    a=d/N, u=T*m/d.
                                                               (1)

Here star is conjugate transpose, and all circle integrals are Haar probability.
R is extended complex linearly.
Positivity and contraction give 0<=k_N<=1, and coefficient orthogonality gives integral k_N=trace(R)/N=a.
This is the old L321 kernel, with its normalization explicitly retained.
It is a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most T, with rational coefficients in the Laurent expansion.
Its being real on the circle does not mean all root values of that Laurent polynomial are nonnegative.

At EVERY original complex root, not only at a circle root,

    (I-R)v_N(alpha_i)=0.                              (2)

Indeed the transpose of each real relation column paired with v_N(alpha_i) is alpha_i^j P(alpha_i)=0.
Orthogonal projection onto those real columns extended to C^N therefore annihilates v_N(alpha_i).
This uses the bilinear polynomial evaluation followed by the complex-linear projection; no invalid equality of v(alpha)^t and v(alpha)^* is assumed.

Write ell_i=|log r_i|.
If r_i<=1, compare v_N(z_i) with v_N(alpha_i).
If r_i>=1, compare it with r_i^(-T)v_N(alpha_i).
Every coordinate difference has modulus at most min(1,T ell_i), because 1-exp(-x)<=min(1,x).
Thus (2) and contraction of I-R imply

    1-k_N(z_i)=||(I-R)v_N(z_i)||_2^2/N
                 <= min(1,T ell_i)^2.               (3)

This is an actual full-vector estimate; it pays all coefficients without an l1 norm.
Define

    D_N(P)=(1/d)sum_i [1-k_N(z_i)].

The unit identities sum log^+r_i=sum log^-r_i=m yield

    0<=D_N(P)<=min{1, 2T*m/d, 2T^2*m^2/d, b/d},    (4)

where b is the number of original roots off the unit circle.
For the linear bound use min(1,x)^2<=x and sum ell_i=2m.
For the quadratic bound, the positive and negative logarithms separately have total m, so sum ell_i^2<=2m^2.
The b/d bound uses the exact zero deficit at every circle root.
In particular a genuine Salem polynomial has b=2.
Equation (4) is a positive all-root estimate on the required near-one scale: at T<=Cd the radial error is at most2Cm, independent of d.
It is NOT a comparison of the angular node law with Haar, and does not make the positive surplus disappear.

For completeness the original L237 arithmetic has not been replaced.
A common integer q makes every coefficient column q R e_j integral with polynomial F_j=q z^j-P U_j.
At every alpha_i, F_j(alpha_i)=q alpha_i^j and abs Res(P,F_j)=q^d; the average coefficient energy is q^2d/N.
No individual content is divided out here.
The kernel in(1) is exactly sum_j |F_j(z)|^2/(N q^2); q cancels only from this normalized analytic object after both full norm and energy are retained.
The equations above do not assert that k_N is itself an ordinary integer auxiliary with integer root values.

## 2. Regularized logarithms retain a compulsory moving-test bias

For any delta>0 put

    L_delta(x)=log(delta+x), 0<=x<=1,
    c_delta(a)=log[(1+delta)/(a+delta)],
    A_delta=log[(1+delta)/delta].

Concavity gives the chord lower bound L_delta(x)>=L_delta(1)-A_delta(1-x), whereas Jensen gives integral L_delta(k_N)<=log(delta+a).
Hence the actual empirical angular law of ALL original roots satisfies

    (1/d)sum_i log(delta+k_N(z_i))
           -integral log(delta+k_N)
       >= c_delta(a)-A_delta D_N(P).                (5)

Every quantity is finite, including zeros of k_N, because delta is positive.
For N/d bounded below by1+eta with eta>0 and T/d bounded above, the right side stays positively bounded on any hypothetical sequence of positive TOTAL m tending to zero.
For genuine Salem inputs the stronger remainder2A_delta/d applies, regardless of the off-circle radii.
These are conditional sequence assertions, not existence of a near-one sequence.

The same proof works for ANY increasing concave function Phi:[0,1]->R with finite endpoint values: putting A=Phi(1)-Phi(0),

    empirical Phi(k_N)-integral Phi(k_N)
            >=Phi(1)-Phi(a)-A D_N(P).               (6)

This includes fractional powers x^s,0<s<1, for which A=1.
No differentiability, small-value regularization or external probabilistic assertion is required.
It explains precisely why a bounded circle range and removal of a raw coefficient scale do not by themselves yield a small joint conjugate error.
It does NOT refute a theorem using genuinely stronger arithmetic of nontorsion integer inputs; such a theorem would exclude the hypothetical sequence rather than contradict(5).

There is a useful exact cancellation distinction.
A positive measure value in(5) is a discrepancy for a P-dependent moving test.
It is not an extra independent norm income.
In particular the log of this aggregate square must not be identified with the average log of the individual F_j: Jensen gives an inequality, not equality.

## 3. An automatic positive lower floor for the actual dense kernel

Unlike an arbitrary nonnegative trigonometric polynomial, this actual residual kernel is strictly positive everywhere on T as soon as P has an exterior root.
More quantitatively, if alpha is ANY original root of modulus r>1, then

    k_N(z)>= (1/N) tanh(N log r/2) tanh(log r/2),
                                      for every |z|=1.           (7)

Proof.
Equation(2), self-adjointness of R, and Cauchy--Schwarz give

    ||R v_N(z)||_2^2
       >= |v_N(alpha)^*v_N(z)|^2/||v_N(alpha)||_2^2.

The numerator is the squared modulus of sum_(j=0)^(N-1)(conj(alpha)z)^j.
Since r>1, its geometric-series expression and the two triangle inequalities give a lower bound (r^N-1)^2/(r+1)^2.
The denominator is (r^(2N)-1)/(r^2-1).
Dividing by N and cancelling positive factors gives

    (1/N) [(r^N-1)/(r^N+1)] [(r-1)/(r+1)],

which is(7).
This calculation keeps the whole coefficient window N and uses the original root only as a test vector in the EXACT residual range.
No deletion of its other conjugates, no replacement polynomial, and no logarithmic arithmetic income from this one root is made.

For a degree-only floor use the ALREADY ACCEPTED Dim19 arXiv v1 Theorem1 house statement, exactly as recorded in [Smy08-U001](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) and [L80](../ledger/Yin_L_G0080_v1.md#degree-only-primitive-escape).
It states house(P)>=2^(1/(4d)) on the present monic irreducible nontorsion d>1 domain.
Its original proof remains an external accepted input and has NOT been newly audited; neither the 2026 accepted edition nor a stronger Mahler bound is substituted.
Put c=log2/4.
Choosing an actual root attaining the house and using monotonicity in(7) gives

    k_N(z)>= [tanh(Nc/(2d)) tanh(c/(2d))]/N
             >= c^2/(16Nd) > 1/(1024Nd)=:beta.       (8)

Here N>d and0<c<1, while tanh x>=x/2 for0<=x<=1: sinh x>=x and cosh x<=cosh1<2 prove that elementary bound.
Thus the two tanh factors are at least c/4 and c/(4d), respectively.
Finally c>1/8 follows from log2>1/2.
All constants in beta are rational and depend only on ORIGINAL d and full N.
There is no coefficient height, denominator, root separation, discriminant or unknown same-P Mahler value in beta.

This is a genuinely automatic positive input bound on the all-column kernel.
It concerns k_N, not |P| itself; circle zeros of P cause no problem because k_N=1 at their phases.
It is compatible with the coefficient-field ceiling of C103, because neither R nor k_N is a same-symbol Gram of |P|^2 with an improved constant coefficient.
It is compatible with C113, whose regularization is for the different symbol |P|^2.
The theorem gives no bounded angular-discrepancy coefficient norm.

## 4. Fully paid finite logarithmic transfer and its obstruction

First, positivity in(8) makes the UNREGULARIZED log k_N continuous.
The elementary bound log(1+x)<=x gives, for every delta>=0,

    0<=log(k_N+delta)-log k_N<=delta/beta.            (9)

Consequently delta<=eta*beta gives uniform eta approximation with an explicitly degree-paid regularization.
This avoids the coefficient/discriminant dependence of a generic small-value estimate, but does not make delta fixed independently of degree.

The finite positive logarithmic series already used in [C137](Yin_C_G0137_v1.md#finite-logarithmic-auxiliary-argument) and [C140](Yin_C_G0140_v1.md#argument) gives a particularly transparent exact construction.
For J>=1 define

    H_J(z)=-sum_(j=1)^J (1-k_N(z))^j/j.

It is a rational real Laurent polynomial of degree at most L=J(N-1), and

    0<=H_J-log k_N
       <=(1-beta)^(J+1)/[(J+1)beta].                (10)

Indeed the omitted terms of -log(1-y), for0<=y<=1-beta, are nonnegative and bounded by y^(J+1)/[(J+1)(1-y)].
This is direct substitution of the old finite logarithm mechanism, not a new logarithm-series theorem.
For0<eta<=1/2, the explicit choice

    J=ceil(beta^(-1) log(2/eta))

has error at most eta, since (J+1)beta>=log(2/eta)>1 and (1-beta)^(J+1)<=exp(-(J+1)beta)<=eta/2.
The resulting sufficient cutoff is O(Nd log(1/eta)); its FULL Fourier degree is O(N^2d log(1/eta)), hence cubic in d at N comparable to d.
No optimality or necessity of this particular cutoff is claimed.

Possible leading cancellations must be checked before calling a frequency cost actual.
The [L321 exact identity](Yin_C_G0592_v2.md#residual-laurent-square-identity) gives N k_N=N-P(z)P(z^-1) B_(N-d)(z), where B_(N-d) is the nonzero symmetric Laurent polynomial formed from the inverse relation Gram.
Its constant coefficient is the positive trace of that inverse, so its Laurent degree b0 is at least0.
The two extreme coefficients of P(z)P(z^-1) are nonzero and its Laurent degree is d.
Products add Laurent degrees, whence

    ell_N=degree_Laurent(k_N)=d+b0, d<=ell_N<=N-1,
    degree_Laurent(H_J)=J ell_N.                   (10a)

The last equality follows from the nonzero leading coefficient of the scalar polynomial -sum_(j=1)^J(1-x)^j/j; lower powers cannot cancel the highest Laurent degree.
This retains radical substitutions and all possible zero extreme inverse-Gram diagonal sums without presuming ell_N=N-1.

This prescribed sufficient cutoff does not fit the small-height hypothesis of L229, even on a hypothetical near-one input.
The same house input gives m>=c/d.
Hence its ACTUAL Fourier height parameter obeys

    (J ell_N)*m/d >=J*m
                   >=1024c N log(2/eta)>1,         (10b)

for d>=2,N>d,eta<=1/2, whereas L229 requires at most h0=c exp(-20).
This is a precise failure of THIS degree-only constructive majorant followed by THAT angular estimate; it is not a lower bound on the best possible logarithmic approximation or a claim that no larger-frequency theorem can work.
The declared upper budget J(N-1) is cubic at linear windows; the lower statement(10b) uses the separately proved ACTUAL degree and must not be inferred merely from that upper budget.

There is a distinct obstruction even when delta is fixed and a finite logarithmic approximation has a degree-linear frequency budget.
Suppose G is ANY real Laurent polynomial of degree at most L>=1 with

    sup_T |G-log(delta+k_N)|<=eta.

Write W_*(G)=sum_(0<|h|<=L)|Ghat(h)|.
By the accepted [L229 angular estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0229_v1.md#uniform-powered-angular-coherence), if0<L*m/d<=h0 then

    epsilon(L*m/d) W_*(G)
      >= c_delta(d/N)-A_delta D_N(P)-2eta.          (11)

To prove it, replace both empirical and Haar terms in(5) by G, costing at most2eta, expand the finite Fourier series, cancel its constant coefficient, and bound each original angular moment by epsilon(L*m/d).
This applies to arbitrary coefficient signs and irrational coefficients, not only to a particular positive series.
It is an exact inequality for supplied G; a negative right side is merely vacuous.

For example take N=2d,delta=1 and g=log(4/3)>0.
Whenever m<=g/(16log2) and eta<=g/8, (4) gives A_delta D_N<=4m log2<=g/4, and (11) gives

    W_*(G)>=g/[2 epsilon(L*m/d)].                  (12)

Thus along any HYPOTHETICAL positive-total-m sequence tending to zero, every uniformly accurate such G with L/d bounded must have divergent nonconstant coefficient l1 norm.
This is not merely the old linear kappa_R lower bound: it applies to every finite Fourier approximation of the fixed regularized logarithm, after its range and small values have already been controlled.
It does not produce a sequence or refute an arithmetic theorem proving that sequence impossible.
It rules out the shortcut of asserting an automatic bounded finite-log coefficient budget from the projection identity or its bounded circle range alone.

For comparison, at fixed delta one can certainly form a bounded-length logarithm series in k_N after an affine rescaling of delta+k_N.
Its uniform scalar error can be made small with J independent of d.
However its Fourier degree is J ell_N, between Jd and J(N-1), and its bounded circle range only yields a generic coefficient l1 bound of square-root order in that degree, by Parseval and coefficient Cauchy--Schwarz.
The same familiar square-root dimension price therefore remains; fixed scalar approximation length is not fixed Fourier coefficient complexity.
No improved error is inferred by changing its name.

## 5. Positive multiscale averaging retains the same node constraint

Take ANY finite collection of actual windows N_j>d, and real weights omega_j>=0 summing to1.
Omit zero weights.
Construct EACH actual residual k_(N_j) separately, and put

    kbar=sum_j omega_j k_(N_j),
    abar=sum_j omega_j d/N_j,
    Cbar=sum_j omega_j (N_j-1)/d.
                                                               (13)

Then0<=kbar<=1, integral kbar=abar<1, kbar=1 at every original circle root, and linearity of the deficit bound gives

    (1/d)sum_i [1-kbar(z_i)]<=min(2 Cbar*m,b/d).     (14)

Consequently (5)--(6) hold with kbar,abar and the right side of(14).
This permits arbitrarily many windows and very unequal weights; it does not assume that averaging means orthogonality or independent conjugate sampling.
Every window is formed from the SAME original P.
Common positive scalar normalization of each original auxiliary family gives its own actual q_j, energy and norms; (13) is an analytic average after those normalizations, not a single polynomial of uncharged degree.

In particular fixing abar<=a0<1 and Cbar<=C0 keeps a positive logarithmic node discrepancy as total m tends hypothetically to zero.
If all windows are bounded by N_j-1<=Cd, the full Fourier degree of kbar is at most Cd.
If only Cbar is bounded, no such maximum-frequency bound follows automatically.
A precise truncation is possible: put B=4C0/(1-a0), discard terms with (N_j-1)/d>B, and let tau be the discarded total weight.
Then

    tau<=C0/B=(1-a0)/4,
    retained total weight=1-tau>0,
    a_ret<=a0/(1-tau),
    1-a_ret>=3(1-a0)/4,
    C_ret<=C0/(1-tau)<=4C0/3.                       (15)

The first inequality is the direct sum of omega_j (N_j-1)/d over the discarded terms.
The remaining assertions follow after dividing retained weights by1-tau; for the gap use1-a_ret>=(1-tau-a0)/(1-tau)>=(1-a0)-tau.
The retained mixture uses only actual windows of Fourier degree at most Bd, and still obeys (14) with its own exact mean window.
The case tau=0 is included.
Existence of the starting mixture forces C0>=1, so the nonempty short-window assertion has no hidden inconsistent small-budget case.

Thus rare very long windows cannot make the contact discrepancy disappear while retaining both a fixed mean-window budget and a fixed averaged energy surplus.
This result is about positive mixing of the normalized actual kernels.
It does not cover arbitrary signed weights, logarithms of a different arithmetic residue class, unbounded mean window, or a comparison which pays and uses the discarded part by another mechanism.
