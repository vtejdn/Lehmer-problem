# A uniform gap for the full interlacing inverse image of a Salem trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary real poles and arbitrary Salem inputs

Let \(S\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Salem trace and \(m=\deg S\ge2\).
Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(A\) monic of degree \(s\ge1\), \(B\) of degree \(s-1\) and positive leading coefficient \(k\).
Suppose \(g=A/B\ne X\) has the [interlacing form](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#statement)
\[
          g(X)=X/k+c-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\frac{w_i}{X-b_i},
          \qquad w_i>0,\quad b_1<\cdots<b_{s-1}\text{ real}.
\]
For \(x=z+z^{-1}\) and the full monic reciprocal inverse polynomial
\[
                  P_g(z)=z^{sm}B(x)^m S(g(x)),
\]
there is an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
                   \log M(P_g)=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad \log M(P_g)\ge\eta.
\]
There are only finitely many choices of \(S,A,B\) giving measure zero in logarithmic normalization.
The allowable degree, coefficients and pole locations of the map are otherwise unrestricted.
The full measure includes all cofactors, as required by the [individual-factor field criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#fields).

For a fixed source let \(D\) be the span of its real trace conjugates and put
\[
                   N_D=\max\{1,\lfloor2/(D-2)\rfloor\}.
\]
The [integer span bound](Yin_C_G0345_v120.md#span) ensures \(D>2\).
For all sufficiently small nonnegative full measures at this source, the number of poles is strictly less than \(N_D\).
An explicit sufficient inequality for this conclusion is given below in the monic residue-mass-one case.

<a id="moments"></a>
## Positive integer averages of squared Chebyshev polynomials

Write \(L=\log M(P_g)\), \(p=s-1\), and take \(t>2\cosh L\).
Every real inverse trace belongs to \((-t,t)\), and so do the interlacing poles.
Every source value is strictly between \(g(-t)\) and \(g(t)\).
For \(k=1\), the [endpoint comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v120.md#mass) therefore gives
\[
       \sum_i\frac{2t w_i}{t^2-b_i^2}<2t-D .                         \tag{1}
\]
At all sufficiently small positive full measures the same result gives \(k=1\) and, in the nonlinear case, \(\sum_i w_i=1\).
At \(L=0\) the endpoint width before setting \(k=1\) is at most \(2t/k\), and letting \(t\downarrow2\) excludes \(k\ge2\).
Then \(W=\sum_iw_i\) is an integer by monic division, positive when \(p>0\).
The width bound \(2t-2W/t\) excludes \(W\ge2\) as \(t\downarrow2\) since \(D>2\).
The affine case has no poles.

Now take \(k=W=1\) and put \(\mu=\sum_i w_i\delta_{b_i}\).
Its mass is one and all its power moments are integers, by expanding \(X+c-A/B=\sum_i w_i/(X-b_i)\) at infinity.
Let \(V_j(X)=U_j(X/2)\), where \(U_j\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
The recurrence \(V_0=1,\ V_1=X,\ V_{j+1}=XV_j-V_{j-1}\) makes \(V_j\) monic integral of degree \(j\).
If \(j<p\), this polynomial cannot vanish at every one of the \(p\) distinct poles, so positivity and integrality give
\[
                        \int V_j(X)^2\,d\mu(X)\ge1 .                 \tag{2}
\]
This uses the actual real poles, including any outside \([-2,2]\).

<a id="fejer"></a>
## A finite upper bound on the number of poles

Fix \(N\ge1\) and \(2\cosh L<t\le3\).
Use the positive weights \(a_j=(2/N)(1-j/(N+1))\), \(1\le j\le N\), which sum to one.
The identity
\[
  1+2\sum_{j=1}^N(1-j/(N+1))\cos(2j\theta)
       =\frac{1}{N+1}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{N}e^{2ij\theta}\right|^2
\]
implies \(\sum_{j=1}^N a_j\sin^2(j\theta)\le (N+1)/(2N)\).
For \(|X|<t\), write \(X/t=\cos\theta\), \(0<\theta<\pi\).
Since \(V_{j-1}(2\cos\theta)=\sin(j\theta)/\sin\theta\), one obtains
\[
    \sum_{j=1}^N a_j V_{j-1}(2X/t)^2
           \le \frac{N+1}{2N}\frac{1}{1-X^2/t^2}.
\]
To return to integral polynomials, \(|X-2X/t|\le t-2\).
Writing \(V_i(X)=\sum_l v_{il}X^l\), one has \(\sum_l|v_{il}|3^l\le4^i\) by the recurrence and induction.
Consequently \(|V_i|\le4^i,\ |V_i'|\le i4^i\) on \([-3,3]\), so replacing \(2X/t\) by \(X\) in the squared polynomial costs at most \(2N16^N(t-2)\) for every \(i<N\).
If \(p\ge N\), integrate against \(\mu\) and use (2) on the left.
Equation (1) bounds the integral of \(1/(1-X^2/t^2)\) by \((t/2)(2t-D)\).
It follows that
\[
     p\ge N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      1\le 2N16^N(t-2)+\frac{N+1}{2N}\frac{t}{2}(2t-D).            \tag{3}
\]

For \(N=N_D\), one has \(N>(4-D)/(D-2)\).
The right side of (3) at \(t=2\) equals \((N+1)(4-D)/(2N)<1\).
By continuity this strict upper bound persists for all \(t>2\) sufficiently close to \(2\), with an interval depending only on \(D\).
Whenever \(2\cosh L\) is small enough to allow such a \(t>2\cosh L\), (3) forces \(p<N_D\).

<a id="gap"></a>
## Finiteness at small full measure

By [finiteness of Salem sources](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#finite), \(L\le1\) allows only finitely many \(S\) for these nonidentity maps.
Use the preceding pole bounds on this list and the small-measure condition forcing \(k=1\) and \(W=1\) at nonlinear maps.
There is a fixed neighborhood of zero, including zero, on which \(S\) belongs to this finite list, \(k=1\), and \(s\) is bounded, also for the affine maps.
The coefficients of the full monic polynomial \(P_g\) are bounded there by its degree and measure.
Hence only finitely many such polynomials occur, and the positive values among their logarithmic measures are separated from zero.
This proves the stated uniform positive gap.

For finiteness of the presentations themselves, the monic \(B\) also has bounded degree and bounded real roots by interlacing, hence only finitely many possibilities.
Choose a root \(y\) of one of the finitely many \(S\).
The monic \(A-yB\) has all its roots in a bounded interval and its degree is bounded.
Its coefficients and those of \(yB\) are bounded, so the integer polynomial \(A\) also belongs to a finite list.
This includes all presentations with \(L=0\).
The explicit [Chebyshev descents](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#chebyshev) and the constructions with single selected Salem factors are compatible with this result, which only bounds the positive full measure away from zero.
