# Pisot inverses at the square of their degree and the Salem trace infimum

<a id="statement"></a>
## The prescribed square constant

For even \(d\ge4\), let \(\eta_d\) be the infimum of \(\theta-d^2\) over the Pisot numbers \(\theta\) whose monic minimal polynomial has degree exactly \(d\) and constant term \(+d^2\).
Use \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\).
Then
\[
 \liminf_{\substack{d\to\infty\\2\mid d}}\eta_d
       =\inf_{\sigma\ {\rm Salem}}(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2).                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus Salem numbers tending to one exist if and only if there are such even-degree Pisot inputs with \(\theta-d^2\to0\).

More precisely, for each fixed Salem minimal polynomial \(P_0\) of degree \(d_0\), put \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) for its exterior root \(\sigma\).
There are arbitrarily large multiples \(J\) of four for which \(d=d_0+J\) and a degree-\(d\) Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) satisfy
\[
 f(0)=d^2,\qquad
 \frac{zf+f^*}{z+1}=P_0(z)(z^J+1),\qquad
 0<\theta-d^2-(T-2)
     =O_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{d^{3/2}}\right),             \tag{2}
\]
where \(f^*(z)=z^d f(1/z)\).
The entire return has just one noncyclotomic factor, the specified \(P_0\).
The argument also gives an [unpadded family of changing Salem minimal polynomials](#square-return) at the prescribed square constant.

<a id="square-interpolation"></a>
## A finite test on the neutral trace roots

Take a monic reciprocal polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of degree \(d=2m\ge4\) with simple roots, exactly one real pair \(\sigma>1,\sigma^{-1}\) off the unit circle, and no roots at \(1,-1\).
Write \(P(z)=z^m Q(z+z^{-1})\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(h=m-1\), and \(H(x)=Q(x)/(x-T)\).
The \(h\) remaining trace roots \(x_i\) of \(H\) are distinct in \((-2,2)\).
Put
\[
 B_h=\sqrt{2h\log(4h)},\qquad
 \Gamma(P)=\sum_{i=1}^h\frac1{(2+x_i)|H'(x_i)|}.
\]
For any prescribed integer \(q>2\) satisfying \(3B_h\Gamma(P)<q+T/2\), there is a monic Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) of degree \(d\), constant \(q\), and entire return \(P\), with
\[
 0<\theta-q-(T-2)
       \le \frac{T-2}{q-1}+\frac{12B_h\Gamma(P)}{q-2}.                   \tag{3}
\]
For bounded exterior roots as \(d\to\infty\), the condition \(B_h\Gamma(P)=o(d^2)\) therefore suffices for an error \(o(1)\) at \(q=d^2\).

The [integral inverse parametrization](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) uses
\[
 f=P+(z-1)z^h R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad b_i=-R(x_i)/H'(x_i).
\]
An integer polynomial \(R\) of degree \(h\) and leading coefficient \(1-q\) gives an integer monic \(f\) of degree \(d\) and constant \(q\).
The added term is antisymmetric in formal degree \(d-1\), so \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).

Choose a real polynomial \(S\) of degree at most \(h-1\) interpolating the values \(-2B_h\operatorname{sgn}H'(x_i)\), and set \(R_0=(1-q)H+S\).
Expand \(R_0\) in the monic integer basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_h\), where \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\) for \(j\ge1\).
Keep the leading coefficient and round each of the \(h\) other coefficients independently to its floor or ceiling with probabilities preserving its mean.
Integral coefficients stay fixed.
At one node the error \(Z\) is a sum of \(h\) independent mean-zero terms, each supported on an interval of length at most two because \(|C_j(x_i)|\le2\).
The concentration estimate below is [Hoeffding's bounded-sum inequality](../audits/literature/Hoe63/Yin_U001.md#independent-sum) in this setting.
For any one term the second derivative of the logarithm of its moment-generating function is its variance under exponential tilting, at most one by measuring squared distances from the midpoint of the interval.
The log moment and its first derivative both vanish at zero.
Integration and independence give \(\log\mathbb E e^{tZ}\le ht^2/2\) for every real \(t\).
By Markov's inequality at \(t=\pm B_h/h\), \(\Pr(|Z|\ge B_h)\le2e^{-B_h^2/(2h)}=1/(2h)\).
Some integer rounding therefore has error less than \(B_h\) at every node, yielding
\[
      0<\frac{B_h}{|H'(x_i)|}\le b_i\le\frac{3B_h}{|H'(x_i)|},
      \qquad \sum_i\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}\le3B_h\Gamma(P)<q+T/2 .           \tag{4}
\]

<a id="simple-return"></a>
## Strict stability for a full return with cyclotomic factors

Here \(P\) may be reducible, so we check the simple-root argument behind the [inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) in this setting.
When \(R=(1-q)H\) the starting polynomial is
\[
 F_0(z)=p_q(z)z^hH(z+z^{-1}),\qquad p_q(z)=z^2-(q+T-1)z+q .
\]
Its quadratic factor has one simple root in \((0,1)\) and one in \((q,\infty)\), by its signs at \(0,1,q\).
The other roots are the simple neutral pairs of \(P\).
Move from \(F_0\) to \(f\) along \(F_u=F_0+u(f-F_0)\), \(0\le u\le1\).
For a neutral root \(\zeta=e^{it_i}\), \(0<t_i<\pi\), the initial log-radius derivative is \(-\Re((f-F_0)(\zeta)/(\zeta F_0'(\zeta)))\).
The ratio inside the real part is
\[
 \frac{\sin(t_i/2)R(x_i)}{\sin(t_i)H'(x_i)}
                    \frac1{e^{-it_i/2}p_q(\zeta)} .
\]
The first factor is negative by (4), and the real part of \(e^{-it_i/2}p_q(\zeta)\) is \(\cos(t_i/2)(x_i-T)<0\).
Both conjugates move strictly inward.

For \(u>0\) a unit root would have to be a zero of \((z+1)P\), since the entire return identity holds along the path.
At an original neutral root the correction has the nonzero value \(u(\zeta-1)\zeta^hR(x_i)\).
At \(-1\), \(F_0(-1)>0\).
Partial fractions give \(R/H=1-q-\sum_i b_i/(x-x_i)\) and hence
\[
       f(-1)=(-1)^m[Q(-2)+2R(-2)]
            =(-1)^h H(-2)\left(2q+T-2\sum_i\frac{b_i}{2+x_i}\right)>0.
\]
Thus the linear path is positive at \(-1\) throughout.
Also \(f(1)=P(1)<0\) since there is one exterior real root and all neutral roots occur in conjugate pairs.
The resulting monic \(f\) has one exterior root, real and greater than one, and every other root lies strictly inside the unit circle.
Any proper monic factor omitting the exterior root would have a nonzero integer constant term of absolute value less than one, so \(f\) is irreducible of degree \(d\).
The product of the moduli shows \(\theta>q\).

With \(x_\theta=\theta+\theta^{-1}>2\), substitution in \(f(\theta)=0\) gives \(\theta(x_\theta-T)+(\theta-1)R(x_\theta)/H(x_\theta)=0\).
Thus the [exact excess identity](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) extends to this full return,
\[
       \theta-q-(T-2)=\frac{T-2}{\theta-1}
                     +\sum_i\frac{b_i}{x_\theta-x_i}.
\]
Using (4), \(2+x_i<4\) and \(x_\theta-x_i>q-2\) proves (3).
These estimates use the specified integer \(q\), whereas the [trace-power inverse](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#seed) selects a constant from a degree-dependent interval.

<a id="square-padding"></a>
## A fixed Salem factor at arbitrarily large square constants

Let \(P_0\) be as in (2) and let \(\mathcal U\) be its finite set of neutral roots, both members of every conjugate pair included.
Choose strictly increasing integers \(n_a\) with \(n_{a+1}-n_a\to\infty\) such that the vectors \((\zeta^{4n_a})_{\zeta\in\mathcal U}\) converge in the compact product of circles.
This follows by taking and then thinning a convergent subsequence.
The positive multiples \(J=4(n_{a+1}-n_a)\) tend to infinity and \(\zeta^J\to1\) simultaneously on \(\mathcal U\).
We may take a strictly increasing subsequence of \(J\).

Set \(P=P_0(z)(z^J+1)\).
The new grid \(\mathcal G_J=\{z:z^J=-1\}\) has \(J\) simple neutral points and contains neither \(1\) nor \(-1\).
The roots of \(P_0\) are nontorsion, so all roots of \(P\) are simple.
It is monic reciprocal with the same exterior pair and with degree \(d_0+J\).
The evaluations at \(1,-1,i\) all equal twice those of \(P_0\), preserving in particular the endpoint ratio \(P(-1)/|P(1)|\).

For a neutral trace \(x=2\cos t\), \(0<t<\pi,\ z=e^{it}\), differentiating \(P(z)=z^mQ(z+z^{-1})\) gives
\[
 \frac1{(2+x)|H'(x)|}
       =\frac{2\sin(t/2)(T-x)}{|(z+1)P'(z)|}.                           \tag{5}
\]
The numerator is at most \(2(T+2)\).
At an old neutral point \(P'(\zeta)=P_0'(\zeta)(1+\zeta^J)\), so its contribution is bounded in terms of \(P_0\) along the chosen sequence.
At \(z\in\mathcal G_J\) we have \(|P'(z)|=J|P_0(z)|\).
The function \((z+1)P_0(z)\) has simple unit-circle zeros precisely at the fixed set \(\mathcal Z=\{-1\}\cup\mathcal U\).
Compactness away from these zeros and their simplicity give a constant depending on \(P_0\) such that
\[
   \frac1{|(z+1)P_0(z)|}
       \le C_{P_0}\left(1+\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal Z}\frac1{|z-\xi|}\right)
       \qquad (|z|=1,\ z\notin\mathcal Z).
\]
For every fixed \(\xi\in\mathcal Z\), eventually \(|1+\xi^J|\ge1\).
On the grid, \(J|z-\xi|\ge|z^J-\xi^J|=|1+\xi^J|\), so the nearest grid point is at distance at least \(1/J\).
Apart from the two nearest points, the \(k\)-th nearest grid point in circular order of distance has chord distance \(\gg k/J\), by the angular spacing \(2\pi/J\) and the chord bound on a semicircle.
Hence \(\sum_{z\in\mathcal G_J}|z-\xi|^{-1}=O(J\log J)\).
Summing (5) over the old and new neutral traces, or majorizing with the full grid, gives \(\Gamma(P)=O_{P_0}(\log J)\).
At \(q=d^2\), (3) now yields (2).
Since every root of \(z^J+1\) is a root of unity, \(P_0\) is the entire noncyclotomic part of the return.

<a id="infimum"></a>
## Both directions of the comparison

For every fixed Salem number \(\sigma\), (2) gives an unbounded sequence of even \(d\) with \(\liminf_d\eta_d\le\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\).
Taking the infimum over \(\sigma\) proves the upper bound in (1), without any uniformity of the constants in \(P_0\).
If the Salem infimum is zero, one can choose a different Salem factor at each step and then choose its padding length large enough that its error in (2) also tends to zero.

Conversely the [positive delay-one return](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) of any input counted by \(\eta_d\) has exactly one exterior root \(\sigma>1\) and, by reciprocity, exactly one interior root \(\sigma^{-1}\).
The full monic polynomial \(zf+f^*\) has unit constant, so its irreducible factor containing the interior root must also contain the exterior root.
Every other root of that factor is neutral, and the remaining factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
The factor carrying \(\sigma\) is therefore Salem, except possibly for a reciprocal quadratic with integer trace \(T\ge3\).
Removing \(z+1\) leaves that factor in the return.
The [two-point estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) gives \(T-2\le(1-1/\theta)(\theta-d^2)\).
In the quadratic case \(T-2\ge1\), while the Salem infimum on the right of (1) is less than one, for instance by the family below.
This proves the lower bound for every input and (1).
In particular any shrinking-excess sequence supplies actual Salem factors once \(T-2<1\).

<a id="square-return"></a>
## A changing family without cyclotomic padding

Let \(J\) run through the sufficiently large positive multiples of \(12600\), put \(d=J+2,\ q=d^2\), and take the [Salem minimal polynomials](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#plateau)
\[
      P_J(z)=\frac{z^Jg(z)+g^*(z)}{z+1},\qquad
      g(z)=z^3-z-1,\qquad g^*(z)=z^3g(1/z).
\]
If \(T_J=\sigma_J+\sigma_J^{-1}\) for their exterior roots, then each \(P_J\) is the entire return of a degree-\(d\) Pisot minimal polynomial of positive constant \(d^2\) with
\[
                0<\theta_J-d^2-(T_J-2)
                         =O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{d^{3/2}}\right).
\]
The outputs have distinct minimal degrees and satisfy \(P_J(1)=-1,\ P_J(-1)=J+1\).
The excess tends to \(\tau+\tau^{-1}-2\), approximately \(0.079596\), where \(\tau^3=\tau+1,\ \tau>1\).
The construction imposes no specified zero of the Schur defect at \(w=1\) or \(w=-1\).

<a id="square-salem-family"></a>
### Derivative control at the two ends of the trace interval

The [root count and irreducibility calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v102.md#salem) gives the exact degree \(J+2\), the displayed endpoint values, and \(\sigma_J\to\tau\).
Since \(g\) has no zero on the unit circle, \(c_0=\min_{|z|=1}|g(z)|>0\).
At a neutral root \(\zeta\), differentiate \((z+1)P_J=z^Jg+g^*\) to obtain
\[
       |(\zeta+1)P'_J(\zeta)|
        \ge Jc_0-\max_{|z|=1}\bigl(|g'(z)|+|(g^*)'(z)|\bigr).
\]
The numerator of (5) is bounded uniformly for this family since \(T_J\to\tau+\tau^{-1}\).
Every neutral trace therefore contributes \(O(1/J)\), even at the two ends of the interval.
It follows that \(\Gamma(P_J)=O(1)\), so the strict endpoint test and the excess bound follow from (3).
This family has a positive limiting trace excess.
Equation (1) leaves open whether the Salem infimum is zero.
