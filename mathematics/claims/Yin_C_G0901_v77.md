# A linear height bound for the probability of a specified Frobenius lift

<a id="statement"></a>
## Frobenius probabilities at the scale of the compressed degree

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=d h(w)\), rational splitting field \(L\), and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
Let \(n\) be the number of distinct conjugate classes of \(w\) modulo roots of unity.
For a prime \(p\) and \(q=p^f\), \(f\ge1\), choose uniformly a prime \(P\) of \(L\) above \(p\), then uniformly an element \(X\) of its decomposition group inducing the \(q\)-power map on the residue field.
This second choice is from the entire specified coset of inertia.
For an absolute effective \(C\), every \(g\in G\) satisfies
\[
                 \Pr(X=g)\log p\ \le\ C(q+1)\frac{m}{n}.                \tag{1}
\]
For example \(C=10^{15}\) suffices, and for all sufficiently large \(n\) an effective threshold allows \(C=23\).
Consequently on a positive shrinking-measure sequence,
\[
       \sup_{p,\ f\ge1,\ g\in G}\frac{n\log p}{p^f+1}\Pr(X=g)
                               \longrightarrow0.                        \tag{2}
\]
At an unramified prime the law is uniform on the conjugacy class \(\mathcal C\) of the \(f\)-th power of arithmetic Frobenius.
In particular \(|\mathcal C|/n\to\infty\) on any shrinking sequence with \(p,f\) fixed and with \(p\) unramified in its splitting fields.
At first-degree records and whenever the distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotients one has \(n=d\).

There is a finite version for partial specifications at a possibly ramified prime.
Let \(F\subseteq L\) be any number field, \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\), and choose \(R\) conjugates of \(w\) in \(F\) with pairwise distinct multiplicative classes modulo torsion.
Let \(r\ge1\) be the affine dimension of those classes over \(\mathbb Q\).
For any integer \(1\le s\le r\), put
\[
        D_s=\binom{r+1}{s}+(R-r-1)\binom r{s-1}.
\]
For every right coset \(Hg\),
\[
       \Pr(X\in Hg)\log p
           \ \le\ \frac{\log D_s}{D_s-1}+2s(q+1)\frac{m}{d}.             \tag{3}
\]
In particular, if \(F\) is generated by the chosen tuple, \(X\in Hg\) says exactly that \(X^{-1}\) and \(g^{-1}\) agree on all of its entries.
The [single-coordinate Frobenius estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#cosets) bounds individual coset probabilities from a relative degree and absolute height.
Here a collection of different products of actual conjugates gives the scale \(m/n\) for an entire specified lift, even when its conjugacy class or the inertia order grows.

<a id="sums"></a>
## Counting products of a fixed number of conjugates

Work in the rational span \(V\) of all the conjugate classes modulo torsion and choose a \(G\)-invariant positive definite inner product by averaging.
All distinct orbit points have the same positive norm, so each is a vertex of the convex hull of any subset containing it.
Indeed its scalar product with itself is strictly larger than with any other point of the same norm.
We may compute the affine dimension over the reals, which agrees with the rational affine dimension in rational coordinates on \(V\).

For the chosen set in \(F\), take an initial affinely independent simplex on \(r+1\) of its points.
The sums of \(s\) distinct vertices of this simplex are all different by affine independence.
Add the remaining points in any order.
Each next point \(v\) is outside the previous full-dimensional hull in its affine span, so it strictly violates one of the facet inequalities of that hull.
Choose \(r\) affinely independent vertices on that facet.
The averages of \(v\) with any \(s-1\) distinct vertices from these \(r\) are pairwise distinct and strictly violate that same inequality.
All averages counted at earlier stages belong to the previous hull, so there are at least \(\binom r{s-1}\) new ones.
Multiplying all averages by \(s\) proves that at least \(D_s\) distinct sums are available, including the case \(s=1\).
In particular \(D_s\ge R\binom r{s-1}/s\) and \(D_s\ge2\).
Choose exactly \(D_s\) actual products \(u_j\) of \(s\) of the specified conjugates representing different sums.
They are units in \(F\) and \(h(u_j)\le s\,m/d\).

<a id="local"></a>
## One determinant at the matching Frobenius primes

Fix \(g\in G\) and take nodes \(z_j=u_j/(g^{-1}u_j)^q\) in \(L\).
The rational endomorphism \(I-qg^{-1}\) of \(V\) is invertible, since every eigenvalue of \(g^{-1}\) is a root of unity and \(q>1\).
Consequently the \(z_j\) have distinct classes and in particular are distinct algebraic integers.
They are units of heights at most \(s(q+1)m/d\).

Let \(P\) be a prime over \(p\) where the prescribed Frobenius coset meets \(Hg\), and put \(v=P\cap F\).
Choose a lift \(x\) in the intersection.
For every \(u_j\) one has \(x^{-1}u_j=g^{-1}u_j\).
In \(F_v\) the residue of \(u_j\) has a unique Teichmüller representative \(\zeta_j\), a root of unity of order prime to \(p\).
Indeed \(u_j\) is a unit and the nonzero residue lifts uniquely as a root of \(Z^{|\kappa(v)|-1}-1\) by simple-root lifting.
In the normalization \(v_p(p)=1\), \(v_p(u_j-\zeta_j)\ge1/e_v\), where \(e_v=e(F_v/\mathbb Q_p)\).
The automorphism \(x\) preserves the valuation in \(L_P\), and \(x(\zeta_j)=\zeta_j^q\) by the residue action and uniqueness.
It follows that \((x^{-1}u_j)^q\) and \(u_j\) both differ from \(\zeta_j\) by elements of valuation at least \(1/e_v\).
The denominator of \(z_j\) is a local unit, so \(v_p(z_j-1)\ge1/e_v\) for every node at this same prime.
This is the [Teichmüller comparison over the original completion](Yin_C_G0345_v728.md#determinant), valid also when the compositum of the two conjugate fields has larger ramification.

Write \(I_P\) for inertia at \(P\).
At a hit, the local Frobenius coset and \(Hg\) can be written \(I_P x\) and \(Hx\).
The fraction of lifts in their intersection is \(|I_P\cap H|/|I_P|=1/e_v\), by multiplicativity of ramification indices in \(L_P/F_v/\mathbb Q_p\).
At a miss this probability is zero.
All primes \(P\) of the normal field \(L\) above \(p\) have the same local-degree weight, and those weights sum to one at \(p\).
Thus the average of \(1/e_v\) at hits, with zero at misses, is precisely \(\Pr(X\in Hg)\), as in the [inertia-coset count](Yin_C_G0345_v728.md#weights).

Put \(D=D_s\), \(B=D(D-1)/2\), and \(V_D=\prod_{j<l}(z_j-z_l)\ne0\).
At every hit all \(B\) factors have \(v_p\ge1/e_v\), and at every other finite place the determinant is integral.
Its total finite-place negative logarithm with local-degree weights is at least \(B\Pr(X\in Hg)\log p\).
At an archimedean embedding divide each Vandermonde row at \(z_j\) by \(\max(1,|z_j|)^{D-1}\).
The normalized entries have absolute value at most one, giving the Hadamard bound \(D^{D/2}\).
The mean archimedean logarithm of the removed factors equals \((D-1)\sum_j h(z_j)\), because the nodes are units.
The product formula and division by \(B\) give (3).

<a id="scale"></a>
## From four and eleven factors to the compressed degree

Take \(F=L\) and one representative from each of the \(n\) distinct classes.
Their sum is zero because each class has the same multiplicity and the product of all original conjugates is a rational unit.
Hence their affine span is the whole \(V\), of dimension \(b\ge1\), and \(n\ge b+1\).
The finite group induced on the integral span of the classes injects into \(\mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb F_3)\) by reduction modulo three, and its orbit on the classes has size \(n\).
Thus \(n\le3^{b^2}\).
The [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree) is a unit of degree \(n\) with this same positive \(m\), so \(n\to\infty\) on any shrinking sequence, and first attainment at degree \(d\) requires \(n=d\).

Already \(s=4\) in (3) gives the fixed-\(p,f\) vanishing \(n\max_g\Pr(X=g)\to0\) without using a general lower bound for \(m\).
Indeed \(D_4\ge n\binom b3/4\gg n(\log n)^{3/2}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and \(D_4\le n^4\).
Then \(n\log D_4/(D_4-1)\to0\), while \(n m/d\le m\).

For \(b\ge20\) choose \(s=11\).
Now
\[
 D_{11}\ \ge\ \frac{n}{11}\frac{(b/2)^{10}}{10!},
 \qquad
 \frac{n\log D_{11}}{D_{11}-1}
 \ \le\ \frac{C_0}{(\log n)^4},\qquad
 C_0=242\cdot2^{10}\cdot10!\,(\log3)^5 .
\]
Here \(D_{11}\le n^{11}\), \(D_{11}-1\ge D_{11}/2\), and \(b^{10}\ge(\log n/\log3)^5\).
The [explicit degree bound of Voutier, Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) applied to the compressed unit gives \(m>2/(\log(3n))^3\).
As \(n\ge3\) in this case, \(n\log D_{11}/(D_{11}-1)\le4C_0m/\log n\).
For sufficiently large \(n\), \(b\ge20\) and this last error is at most \(m\).
Inserting it in (3) gives \(n\Pr(X=g)\log p\le(1+22(q+1))m\le23(q+1)m\).

For an explicit all-degree constant one has \(4C_0<2\cdot10^{14}\) even using \(\log3<2\).
If \(b<20\), then \(2\le n\le3^{361}\) and one can take \(s=1\), \(D_1=n\).
Here \(n\log n/(n-1)\le2\log n\), and Voutier bounds this by \(m(\log n)(\log(3n))^3<800^4m\).
These bounds imply (1) with the displayed effective constant and give (2).

<a id="ramified"></a>
## Conjugacy classes, ramification and a gap criterion

Fix one prime \(P_0\) of \(L\) above \(p\), and let \(\Phi\) be the full coset of lifts of the \(q\)-power residue map there.
Its size is the ramification index \(e(L_{P_0}/\mathbb Q_p)\).
The random variable \(X\) has the same law as a uniformly chosen element of \(\Phi\) conjugated by a uniformly chosen element of \(G\).
Indeed every prime is reached with the same multiplicity and conjugation transports its full inertia coset.
For the conjugacy class \(\mathcal C(g)\) of any \(g\), it follows that
\[
 \Pr(X=g)=\frac{|\Phi\cap\mathcal C(g)|}{|\Phi|\,|\mathcal C(g)|}.
\]
At unramified \(p\) this proves the class-size consequence of (2).
More generally, if along a family there exist \(p,f,g\), allowed to change with the input, for which
\[
    \frac{|\Phi\cap\mathcal C(g)|}{|\Phi|\,|\mathcal C(g)|}
                   \frac{n\log p}{p^f+1}\ \ge\ \eta>0 ,
\]
then \(m\ge\eta/C\) on that family.
For a bounded \(p,f\) at an unramified place this permits a class size growing linearly with the compressed degree.
It complements the [bounded powered-lift class criterion](Yin_C_G0580_v1.md#powered-frobenius-statement) by using the normal-field atom and its exact inertia weight.
The argument supplies no upper bound on the size of the Frobenius class at a bounded prime for an unrestricted sequence.
