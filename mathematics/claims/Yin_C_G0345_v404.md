# The rotations of one two-class multiple give an integral determinant over the seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound from a single multiple

Let \(n\ge2\) and suppose \(P(X)=P_0(X^n)\) is a monic irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\).
Write \(h=m(P)=m(P_0)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure.
Take positive integers \(p,r\) with \((p,n)=(r,n)=1\), and assume that raising a root of \(P\) to the \(p\)-th power preserves its degree.
Write \(Q_0\) for the monic minimal polynomial of the \(p\)-th power of a root of \(P_0\).
Let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have nonzero constant terms, let \(F(x)=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p)\), and suppose \(P^t\mid F\) for an integer \(t\ge1\).
Use normalized circle measure to define \(I(F)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\).
Then there is an explicit nonzero integral polynomial \(E\) over the seed variable such that, with \(N=n(n-1)/2\),
\[
                 2I(F)\ \ge\ t p h+\frac{m(E)}{N}
                          \ \ge\ t p h+\log|U(0)V(0)|.                 \tag{1}
\]
If \(U,V\) are coprime in \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), the first inequality is strict.
The determinant uses the two displayed summands of a single polynomial, which occupy distinct classes modulo \(p\) whenever \(p\nmid r\).
The coefficient of \(ph\) in (1) retains the entire imposed multiplicity \(t\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Dividing the orbit determinant by the Fourier determinant

Take a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\), and put \(u_j=U(\zeta^{jp}Y)\), \(v_j=\zeta^{jr}V(\zeta^{jp}Y)\) for \(0\le j<n\).
Define
\[
 D(Y)=\frac{\det[\,u_j^{\,n-1-k}v_j^k\,]_{0\le j,k<n}}
                  {\det[\,\zeta^{jk}\,]_{0\le j,k<n}}
     =\prod_{i<j}\frac{u_i v_j-u_j v_i}{\zeta^j-\zeta^i}.             \tag{2}
\]
The second expression follows by the homogeneous Vandermonde identity.
For each \(k\), reduce \(U(Z^pY)^{n-1-k} Z^{rk} V(Z^pY)^k\) modulo \(Z^n-1\) and take its coefficients in the basis \(1,Z,\ldots,Z^{n-1}\).
The evaluations at \(Z=\zeta^j\) multiply this column of integral polynomials by the Fourier matrix in the denominator.
Consequently \(D\in\mathbb Z[Y]\).
At \(Y=0\) the permutation \(j\mapsto rj\bmod n\) gives \(D(0)=\pm(U(0)V(0))^N\).

Replacing \(Y\) by \(\zeta^pY\) cycles the rows and multiplies column \(k\) by \(\zeta^{-rk}\).
The resulting constant factor is one since \(D(0)\ne0\).
Thus \(D(Y)=D_0(Y^n)\) for a nonzero \(D_0\in\mathbb Z[Y]\).
Every rotated polynomial \(F(\zeta^j x)=u_j(x^p)+x^r v_j(x^p)\) is divisible by \(P(x)^t\) over \(\mathbb C\), since \(P(\zeta^j x)=P(x)\).
Eliminating \(x^r\) from two such rotations shows that \(P(x)^t\) divides \((u_iv_j-u_jv_i)(x^p)\).
Each determinant factor is nonzero already at \(Y=0\).
The \(p\)-th powers of the roots of \(P\) are distinct, and \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified there.
It follows that their monic polynomial \(Q\) occurs to power at least \(t\) in every determinant factor.
The powered roots of \(P_0\) are distinct as well, since otherwise two of their complete sets of \(n\)-th roots after powering would coincide.
Indeed \((p,n)=1\) permutes every such set onto the corresponding full set of roots of the powered seed.
Thus \(Q(Y)=Q_0(Y^n)\) and \(m(Q_0)=ph\).
This is the [powered-substitution identity](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers).
Equation (2) gives \(Q^{tN}\mid D\), and monic division over the seed variable now gives
\[
                  D_0(T)=Q_0(T)^{tN}E(T),\qquad E\in\mathbb Z[T].
\]
For example, the remainder of the division by \(Q_0^{tN}\), after substituting \(Y^n\), would be a multiple of \(Q^{tN}\) of strictly smaller degree and hence vanishes.
Since \(Q_0\) has unit constant term, \(|E(0)|=|U(0)V(0)|^N\).

<a id="circle"></a>
## The circle estimate and strictness

At each point of the unit circle row \(j\) of the numerator matrix in (2) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt n\max(|u_j|,|v_j|)^{n-1}\).
Hadamard's bound gives the product of these norms as an upper bound for the determinant.
The Fourier determinant has absolute value \(n^{n/2}\), exactly canceling all the factors \(\sqrt n\).
Integrating logarithms and using invariance under rotations and power substitution gives
\[
 tNph+m(E)=m(D)=m(D_0)\le n(n-1)I(F).
\]
Jensen's inequality \(m(E)\ge\log|E(0)|\) proves the remaining assertion of (1).

Suppose equality holds in the integrated matrix bound.
Then the pointwise upper bound is an equality almost everywhere, since its logarithmic deficit is nonnegative with finite integral.
Away from the finitely many zeros of the entries and determinant, this forces all \(|u_j|=|v_j|\) and makes the rows pairwise orthogonal.
Writing \(w_j=v_j/u_j\), the geometric sum for the inner product of rows 0 and 1 shows that \(w_1/w_0\) is a nontrivial \(n\)-th root of unity almost everywhere.
Its \(n\)-th power is identically one as a rational function, hence the function itself is constant, with value \(\zeta^r\) at zero.
It follows that \(V(Y)/U(Y)\) is invariant under \(Y\mapsto\zeta^pY\).
If the two rows are coprime, unique factorization and unchanged degrees imply \(U(\zeta^pY)\) and \(V(\zeta^pY)\) are respectively the same constant times \(U(Y)\) and \(V(Y)\).
Their constant terms force that constant to be one.
Both polynomials therefore involve only powers divisible by \(n\).
At a root \(\gamma\) of \(P\), the two equations \(F(\gamma)=F(\zeta\gamma)=0\) then force \(U(\gamma^p)=V(\gamma^p)=0\), since \(\zeta^r\ne1\).
This contradicts coprimality and proves strictness.

<a id="fourth"></a>
## A leading constant for each actual cofactor

For the [representatives with \(\deg P_0\le\log(3d)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#statement), let \(d=\deg P\) and take any prime in their fourth-logarithmic window.
For either sign consider any primitive integral \(F=A(x^p)\pm xA^*(x^p)\) of span \(D_F=4pd+1\) with \(\deg A=4d\), \(A(0)\ne0\), \(\gcd(A,A^*)=1\) and \(P^2\mid F\).
Here \(A^*(Y)=Y^{4d}A(1/Y)\).
Both constant terms are nonzero, and \(I(F)=m(A)\) by reciprocity on the circle.
Let \(R\) be the noncyclotomic part of \(F/P^2\) with multiplicities.
With \(C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(z)\), the [single-graph circle estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) gives \((p-4)I(F)\le p\,m(F)+2\log(CD_F)\).
Combining this with (1) and \(m(R)=m(F)-2h\) yields
\[
 m(R)\ \ge\ (p-6)h+
       (1-4/p)\frac{m(E)}{2N}-\frac{2\log(CD_F)}p .                  \tag{3}
\]
The [uniform height lower bound at this window](Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) gives \(\log(2D_F)=o(ph)\).
Consequently each such cofactor satisfies \(m(R)/(ph)\ge1-o(1)\), uniformly in the representative, prime, sign and choice of the graph as \(d\to\infty\).
In particular this improves the lower bound \(h/3\) on \(m(R)/p\) for each of the two short rows of the representative theorem.
The two-row upper bound is still \(O(h)\) on that scale.
Neither strictness nor (3) supplies a lower bound on \(h\) independent of the seed.
