# Counting Salem numbers in intervals of their half-degree powers

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound uniform in the degree and the exterior root

Let \(d=2h\ge4\), \(B\ge1\), and let \(I\subset[1,B^h]\) be an interval of length \(w\).
There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that the number of Salem numbers \(\beta\) of degree exactly \(d\) with \(\beta^h\in I\) is at most
\[
 (1+w)\exp\!\left(C\min\{d,\sqrt{d\log(2dB)}\}\right).               \tag{1}
\]
In particular the number with \(1<\beta\le B\) is at most
\[
 B^{d/2}\exp\!\left(C\min\{d,\sqrt{d\log(2dB)}\}\right).             \tag{2}
\]
For a degree bound \(D\ge4\), the total through degree \(D\) is at most \(D B^{D/2}\exp(C\min\{D,\sqrt{D\log(2DB)}\})\).
For fixed \(B>1\), the factor multiplying \(B^{d/2}\) is subexponential in \(d\).
The full count in (2) is subexponential along \(d\to\infty\) with \(\log B\to0\), and the count in a unit-length window in (1) is subexponential whenever \(\log B=o(d)\).

<a id="energy"></a>
## Energy of the neutral power sums

Let \(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_{d-2}\) denote all conjugates on the unit circle, and put \(u_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-2}\zeta_\ell^j\).
These sums are real by conjugate pairing.
Reflecting the exterior root of the Salem minimal polynomial across the circle replaces it by \(\beta^{-1}\), so the power sum of the reflected list is \(u_j+2\beta^{-j}\).
Use the [discriminant estimate for reflected power sums](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), equation (3), at \(\rho=e^{-1/d}\).
The hypothesis of a monic irreducible unit polynomial holds and its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\log\beta\le\log B\).
It gives
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2j}}{j}|u_j+2\beta^{-j}|^2
                  \ll d\log(2dB).
\]
For \(j\le h\) the weight \(\rho^{2j}\) is at least \(e^{-1}\), and \(\sum_{j\le h}4\beta^{-2j}/j\ll\log(2d)\).
The squared triangle inequality therefore gives the uniform budget
\[
                     \sum_{j=1}^{h}\frac{|u_j|^2}{j}
                                      \ll d\log(2dB).                 \tag{3}
\]

<a id="encoding"></a>
## Recovery from Newton sums in a fixed window

Split \(I\) into at most \(1+8w\) subintervals of length at most \(1/8\), with a consistent endpoint convention.
For each nonempty subinterval fix its left endpoint \(t_0\).
Write \(t=\beta^h\) and \(g_j(t)=t^{j/h}+t^{-j/h}\).
For \(1\le j\le h\), \(0\le g'_j(t)\le1\) on \(t\ge1\), so \(|g_j(t)-g_j(t_0)|\le1/8\) throughout this subinterval.

Let \(s_j=g_j(t)+u_j\) be the full power sums of the minimal polynomial, whose descending coefficients are \(1,a_1,\ldots,a_d\).
Suppose that \(a_1,\ldots,a_{j-1}\) and \(s_1,\ldots,s_{j-1}\) have already been recovered.
Newton's identity
\[
            s_j+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i s_{j-i}=-j a_j                  \tag{4}
\]
fixes the residue class of \(s_j\) modulo \(j\).
In that class let \(b_j\) be the integer nearest to \(g_j(t_0)\), choosing the smaller integer if tied.
Define \(z_j=(s_j-b_j)/j\in\mathbb Z\).
From \(z_j\) and the preceding data one recovers \(s_j\), and then (4) recovers \(a_j\).
Starting with \(j=1\) therefore recovers \(a_1,\ldots,a_h\) from the bin and the signed integer vector \(z\).
Reciprocity determines the other coefficients and the polynomial has a unique exterior root.
This makes the encoding injective on the Salem numbers in each bin.

Because \(|b_j-g_j(t_0)|\le j/2\), each nonzero \(z_j\) satisfies
\[
       |u_j|\ge j|z_j|-j/2-1/8\ge \tfrac38 j|z_j|.
\]
Thus (3) gives an absolute \(C_0\) with
\[
       \sum_{j=1}^h j|z_j|\le\sum_{j=1}^h jz_j^2
                         \le C_0 d\log(2dB)=E .                      \tag{5}
\]
The first inequality uses the integrality of the code.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the signed integer vectors

For every \(v>0\) the number of signed vectors of weight \(\sum_j j|z_j|\le E\) is bounded by
\[
 e^{vE}\prod_{j\ge1}\frac{1+e^{-vj}}{1-e^{-vj}}
       \ \le\ e^{vE}\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-vj})^{-2}.
\]
The logarithm of the last product, excluding \(e^{vE}\), is
\[
  2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac1{\ell(e^{v\ell}-1)}
        \le\frac2v\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell^{-2}\ll v^{-1}.
\]
Taking \(v=E^{-1/2}\) bounds the count in a bin by \(\exp(O(\sqrt E))\).

A bound independent of \(B\) also holds per bin.
At the \(j\)-th step the real sum \(g_j(t)\) varies by at most \(1/8\), and \(|u_j|\le d-2\).
The admissible \(s_j\) therefore lie in an interval of length \(2(d-2)+1/8\) in one residue class modulo \(j\), giving at most \(1+(2(d-2)+1/8)/j\le3d/j\) choices.
Multiplication over \(j\le h\) bounds the number by \((3d)^h/h!\le(6\mathrm e)^h\).
Taking the smaller of these two bounds and counting the bins proves (1), with the constant factor from the bins absorbed into the absolute \(C\).
For \(I=[1,B^h]\) its length is \(B^h-1\), which proves (2) also at \(B=1\).
The stated cumulative bound follows by summing over even degrees and using monotonicity of both exponents.
