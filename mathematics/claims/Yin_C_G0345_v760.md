# Prime exponent sums giving one noncyclotomic factor at a rational pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility for every direction with the selected sum

Fix distinct rational primes \(p_1,p_2,p_3\).
There is a set of primes \(\mathcal P\) with \(\#\{\ell\in\mathcal P:\ell\le x\}\gg_{p_1,p_2,p_3} x/(\log x)^2\) for all sufficiently large \(x\) with the following property.
Let \(B=A/D>1\) be in lowest terms, where \(A,D\) are positive integers and each \(p_i\) divides \(A\).
For every \(\ell\in\mathcal P\), every pair \(u,v\ge1\) with \(u+v=\ell\), and each sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\), the factorization over \(\mathbb Q\) is
\[
 F(X)=D X^\ell-A X^v+\epsilon A X^u-\epsilon D
     =(X-\epsilon)P(X).
\]
The primitive integer polynomial \(P\) is irreducible of degree \(\ell-1\), has leading coefficient \(D\), and satisfies
\[
             \log(A/4)\le m(P)=m(F)\le\log(2(A+D)),
\]
where \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
The same prime set works simultaneously for every displayed choice of \(B,u,v,\epsilon\).
For \(D>1\), every primitive irreducible factor of \(P(X^k)\), at every \(k\ge1\), has logarithmic measure at least \(\log 2\).

Suppose \(D=1\), and write \(L=2(B+1)\) and \(H=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,L^2)\).
Whenever \(P\) has a positive real root \(\beta>1\), write \(r\) for the largest \(j\) with \(\beta^{1/j}\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), using the positive root.
Then \(r\mid H\), and for every \(k\ge1\)
\[
 \min_{Q\ \mathrm{irreducible},\ Q\mid P(X^k)}m(Q)
   =\frac{m(P)}{\gcd(k,r)}
   \ge\frac{\log(B/4)}{H}.
\]
Here and below irreducible factors are normalized as primitive integer polynomials with positive leading coefficient.
In particular \(P(X^k)\) is irreducible whenever \(\gcd(k,H)=1\).
For each fixed integer \(B\) the bound excludes shrinking Salem factors uniformly in the direction and dilation, since a Salem factor would supply a positive exterior root of \(P\) by powering.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Three residue orders determine the degree

Take for \(\mathcal P\) the primes \(\ell>\max(p_1,p_2,p_3)\) for which the residues of the three primes generate \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\).
The counting bound is [Gupta and Murty's Lemma 3](../audits/literature/GM84/Yin_U001.md#gm84-u001).
All these \(\ell\) are odd, so \(u,v\) have opposite parity and \(F(\epsilon)=0\).
Since the four exponents are distinct and \(\gcd(A,D)=1\), \(F\) and hence \(P\) are primitive.
For \(p\in\{p_1,p_2,p_3\}\) normalize by \(D^{-1}\) modulo \(p\).
Then the reduction of \(P\) is \(\Phi_\ell(X)\) for sign plus, and \(\Phi_{2\ell}(X)\) for sign minus, since \((X^\ell+1)/(X+1)=\Phi_{2\ell}(X)\).
In characteristic two these two reductions agree.
They are separable, since \(p\ne\ell\) and \(X^\ell-\epsilon\) has simple roots.
Their irreducible factors all have degree \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(p)\): Frobenius sends each primitive root of unity to its \(p\)-th power, and for odd \(p\) the orders of \(p\) modulo \(2\ell\) and \(\ell\) agree.
For \(p=2\) use the reduction of \(\Phi_\ell\).

The leading coefficient of every primitive rational factor of \(P\) divides \(D\), by Gauss's lemma.
Its degree is therefore preserved modulo each \(p_i\), and is a multiple of all three multiplicative orders.
Their least common multiple is \(\ell-1\) because the three residues generate the cyclic group \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\).
As \(\deg P=\ell-1\), there is exactly one irreducible factor, of multiplicity one.
The linear factor has zero measure and the sum of the absolute coefficients of \(F\) equals \(2(A+D)\).
The [four-term coefficient-height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(A\le4\exp(m(F))\).
Since \(A\ge 2\cdot3\cdot5\), \(P\) is noncyclotomic for both signs.
If \(D>1\), each root of \(P\) is nonintegral by its primitive irreducibility and leading coefficient.
Each root of \(P(X^k)\) has a \(k\)-th power of this kind, hence is nonintegral as well.
The leading coefficient of its primitive minimal polynomial is at least two, proving the arbitrary-dilation bound.

<a id="dilations"></a>
## The bound on positive field radicals

For \(D=1\) the constant coefficient of \(P\) is one, so its roots are algebraic units.
Let \(d=\ell-1\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
If the positive \(q^a\)-th root of \(\beta\) lies in \(K\), put \(\gamma=\beta^{1/q}\) for the prime \(q\).
The nontorsion unit \(\gamma\) has absolute logarithmic height \(h(\beta)/q\), strictly smaller than that of any root of \(P\).
It also differs from the root of unity \(\epsilon\).
Thus \(F(\gamma)\ne0\).
The [prime-power norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) with degree \(\ell\) and coefficient length \(L\) yields
\[
 q^a\le L\exp\!\left(\frac{\ell m(P)}{q(\ell-1)}\right)\le L^2,
\]
using \(\ell/(\ell-1)\le2\), \(q\ge2\) and \(m(P)\le\log L\).
Consequently every maximal prime power in \(r\) divides \(H\).
The [positive radical formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives the stated minimum and irreducibility criterion for each dilation.
All noncyclotomic factors of \(F(X^k)\) divide \(P(X^k)\), since \(X^k-\epsilon\) has only roots of unity.

<a id="winding"></a>
## Exterior conjugates and the limiting measure

For each fixed rational \(B=A/D>1\), put \(s=v/u\).
When \(s>(B+1)/(B-1)\), the [unit-circle argument and positive-root count](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#winding) give exactly \(u\) exterior roots of \(F\), including a positive real root.
All \(u\) belong to \(P\).
When \(D=1\) and \(u=1\), the positive root is Salem with degree \(\ell-1\), since \(\ell>5\) and all but the exterior root and its reciprocal are on the unit circle.
When \(u\ge2\), no dilation \(F(X^k)\) has a Salem factor in this range.
Indeed positive powers of a Salem number are Salem: the exterior conjugate remains unique, and each image of a neutral conjugate still has modulus one.

As \(s\to\infty\) through these directions, the sharper estimate
\[
                       m(P)=\log A+O_B(e^{-c_Bs})
\]
holds for some \(c_B>0\), uniformly in both exponents and both signs.
For an exterior root \(z\) put \(t=z^u\).
Its equation gives \(|t|^s=|(Bt-1)/(t-B)|\).
Choose \(a=(1+B)/2\).
On \(1\le|t|\le a\) the rational function has neither a zero nor a pole and has modulus one on the unit circle.
Its logarithmic modulus has bounded derivative with respect to \(\log|t|\), uniformly in the argument.
Thus \(\log|(Bt-1)/(t-B)|\le C_B\log|t|\) on this annulus.
For \(s>C_B\) an exterior root must have \(|t|>a\), whence
\[
 |t-B|=\frac{|Bt-1|}{|t|^s}
        \le (B+1)a^{1-s}.
\]
In particular \(\log|z|=u^{-1}(\log B+O_B(a^{-s}))\).
Summing over the \(u\) exterior roots and adding \(\log D\) gives the estimate.
For example, \(B=30\) yields simultaneously both signs and every \(1\le u<\ell/2\) at each selected exponent sum, including directions with \(u\to\infty\) and \(v/u\to\infty\).
The argument supplies no prime set of this type from the single prime divisor of \(B=2\).
