# Reading individual exterior roots from the real and complex values of a binomial resultant

<a id="statement"></a>
## The logarithm at each embedding

Fix a positive odd multiple \(h\) of \(39\) prime to five, either sign \(\varepsilon\), and one of the [prime pairs for numerator eight](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#pairs).
Write \(g=q-r\), \(\rho=8/h\), \(s=\rho g\), \(b=s/2\), \(d=\rho(q-1)\), and assume \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) with a sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
Let \(S\) be a monic rational degree-\(d\) divisor of
\[
 Q=\frac{X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g)}{X+\varepsilon}.
\]
All assertions about \(S\) are conditional on such divisibility.
For \(j=2,3\) let \(m\) be any divisor of \(g\), \(t=g/m\), \(\theta=j^{1/m}>0\), and
\[
  A_{j,m}=\operatorname{Res}(S,X^t-\theta)\in\mathbb Z[\theta].
\]
For each \(\theta_\nu=\theta e^{2\pi i\nu/m}\), let \(\sigma_\nu\) denote the embedding sending \(\theta\) to \(\theta_\nu\); the binomial \(Z^m-j\) is Eisenstein.
Let \(a_{j,\nu}\) count the roots of \(S\) in the exterior disks at the centers \(c\) with \(c^t=\theta_\nu\).
The [integer exterior share](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#pairs) gives \(\sum_\nu a_{j,\nu}=s\).
Set
\[
 E_m=\sqrt{d(g/m)\log(2m)\log(2dg)}+s\log(2m)+1 .
\]
Uniformly in \(j,m,\nu\), with absolute implied constants,
\[
 \log|\sigma_\nu A_{j,m}|
       =\left(\frac d m-\frac r g\,a_{j,\nu}\right)\log j+O(E_m).          \tag{1}
\]
For dyadic \(m\) the [totally positive norm-one unit](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#units) \(U_{j,m}=5^{-b/m} A_{j,m}\) therefore satisfies
\[
 \log|\sigma_\nu U_{j,m}|
       =\frac r g\left(\frac s m-a_{j,\nu}\right)\log j+O(E_m).           \tag{2}
\]
Nearest-integer rounding of \(s/m-g\log|\sigma_\nu U_{j,m}|/(r\log j)\) recovers each individual count.
For \(m=2\), write \(U_{j,2}=e_j^{n_j}\) with \(e_2=3+2\sqrt2\), \(e_3=2+\sqrt3\).
Then
\[
 n_j\log e_j
    =\frac r{2g}(a_{j,1}-a_{j,0})\log j+O(E_2),\qquad 2\mid n_3.          \tag{3}
\]
In particular an unequal division between the two sets of exterior disks fixes the sign of the Pell exponent and forces its magnitude to be at least \(c r/g\) for an absolute \(c>0\).

At \(m=g\), the signs of the logarithms of the \(g\) conjugate evaluations \(S(j^{1/g}e^{2\pi i\nu/g})\) recover the full set of exterior disks of \(S\) in block \(j\).
The logarithm is negative precisely at the selected centers.
For the absolute logarithmic Weil height this yields
\[
 h_{\rm Weil}\!\left(S(j^{1/g})\right)
       =\rho(1-\rho)\frac r g\log j+O(E_g).                              \tag{4}
\]
If \(D_j\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is the remainder of \(S\) modulo \(X^g-j\) of degree less than \(g\), then
\[
                  \log\|D_j\|_1=\frac d g\log j+O(E_g+\log(2g)).          \tag{5}
\]
These restrictions concern archimedean values of the specific global factor and do not establish or exclude a rational divisor of degree \(d\).

<a id="circle"></a>
## An angular test with controlled energy

For \(\alpha=\theta_\nu\), set \(F(z)=\log|z^t-\alpha|\) for \(|z|\le1\) and \(F(z)=\log|z^{-t}-\bar\alpha|\) for \(|z|\ge1\).
The two values agree on the unit circle; \(F\) is globally Lipschitz with constant \(O(g)\).
Indeed the argument of each logarithm has modulus at least \(|\alpha|-1\gg 1/m\), the derivative of the power map is bounded by \(t\) on its disk, and inversion on the exterior gives derivative at most one in modulus.
The circle mean of \(F\) is \(\log|\alpha|\), from the uniformly convergent series for \(\log(1-z^t/\alpha)\).
Its Dirichlet energy with the normalization \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb C}|\nabla F|^2\) equals
\[
          t\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\alpha|^{-2k}}{k}
                = -t\log(1-j^{-2/m})\ll (g/m)\log(2m).                   \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the interior harmonic term \(-\operatorname{Re}(z^{tk}\alpha^{-k}/k)\) contributes \(tk|\alpha|^{-2k}/(2k^2)\) on the disk by polar integration.
The terms of distinct angular frequencies are orthogonal; the exterior has the same energy by inversion and conjugation.

The [exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) and the [cyclotomic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion) give a squarefree \(Q\) after the only torsion root is removed, so \(S\) is squarefree.
It is monic integral and \(m(S)=O(1)\) since it divides a monic polynomial of bounded coefficient length.
Use its uniform root measure \(\mu\), circle probability \(\lambda\), and the measure \(\mu_\epsilon\) obtained by replacing each root with uniform measure on a circle about it of radius \(\epsilon=(2dg)^{-1}\).
The [discriminant regularization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test) applies to this squarefree product as well: its discriminant is a nonzero integer, which is the only multiplicity or irreducibility input to that estimate.
It gives \(I(\mu_\epsilon-\lambda)\le d^{-1}(\log(1/\epsilon)+2m(S))+2\epsilon\) for the positive logarithmic energy of a signed difference of mass zero.
The [Dirichlet pairing inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v172.md#disk) bounds the change in the average of \(F\) by the square root of this energy times the square root of (6).
The regularization changes each value of \(F\) by \(O(g\epsilon)\).
Multiplication by \(d\) yields
\[
             \sum_{S(x)=0}F(x)=\frac d m\log j
                 +O\!\left(\sqrt{d(g/m)\log(2m)\log(2dg)}+1\right).       \tag{7}
\]

<a id="disks"></a>
## The cost of a selected exterior root

For each of the \(2s\) exterior roots \(x\) of \(S\), let \(c\) be its disk center, with \(c^g=i\) for \(i=2\) or \(3\).
The [disk estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#disks) gives \(|x-c|\le\Delta=\exp(-c_0r/(2g+1)^2)\).
For \(1\le k\le g\), telescoping powers gives \(|x^k-c^k|\ll g\Delta\): the intervening powers are bounded since \((|c|+\Delta)^g\le3\exp(g\Delta)\).
Hence \(\log|x|=(\log i)/g+O(\Delta)\).
Write \(Y=x^g\), \(W=x^t\) and \(\alpha=\theta_\nu\).
The clipped value \(F(x)=\log|x^{-t}-\bar\alpha|\) is \(O(\log(2m))\) in absolute value by the same lower bound \(|\alpha|-1\) and an absolute upper bound.

If \(c^t\ne\alpha\), the distance \(|c^t-\alpha|\) is \(\gg1/m\).
On the same circle this is the root-of-unity spacing; on distinct circles it follows from \(3^{1/m}-2^{1/m}\ge\log(3/2)/m\).
Consequently \(|\log|W-\alpha||=O(\log(2m))\) there.
If \(c^t=\alpha\), then \(i=j\) and \(Y\) is close to \(j\).
Let \(j'\) be the other block prime.
The root equation gives
\[
                 x^r(Y-j)=-\varepsilon(1-2Y)(1-3Y)/(Y-j').
\]
The right side has modulus bounded above and below by positive absolute constants because \(Y=j+O(g\Delta)\).
Thus \(\log|Y-j|=-(r/g)\log j+O(1)\), absorbing \(O(r\Delta)\).
Moreover
\[
             \frac{Y-j}{W-\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}W^k\alpha^{m-1-k}
                           =m\alpha^{m-1}+O(m^2 g\Delta).
\]
Here \(|W-\alpha|\ll g\Delta\) and the powers through \(m\) of \(\max(|W|,|\alpha|)\) stay bounded.
The last display has modulus comparable to \(m\), so \(\log|W-\alpha|=-(r/g)\log j+O(\log(2m))\) at each selected center above \(\alpha\).
All roots of modulus at most one already contribute their exact \(\log|x^t-\alpha|\) to (7).
Correcting the exterior terms proves (1).

## Rounding, height and coefficient length

Since \(d=\rho(r+g-1)\) and \(b=s/2\), changing the main term in (1) to that for the normalized unit in (2) costs \(O(s/m)\).
The error is small enough even to resolve an individual center with mean occupancy as low as \(1/g\).
In fact \(E_m\ll\sqrt{rg\log(2rg)}+g\log(2g)\) since \(\log(2m)/m\) is bounded, \(s\le g\) and \(d\le 2r\).
For \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\), \(g^2 E_m/r\) is arbitrarily small on increasing the absolute \(C\), uniformly in \(g\); this follows from monotonicity of \(\log(2rg)/r\) in this range.
This proves the stated rounding.

For \(m=2\) the two real embeddings of a totally positive norm-one unit are reciprocals.
The description of \(e_3\) is [already established](Yin_C_G0345_v225.md#units).
For \(j=2\), multiply any totally positive norm-one unit of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) by a power of \(e_2\) to put its positive real value \(v\) in \([1,e_2)\).
The coefficient \((v+v^{-1})/2\) is an integer between one and three, with three excluded.
If it were two the norm equation would require an integer \(y\) with \(2y^2=3\); hence \(v=1\).
Subtracting (2) for the two embeddings now gives (3).
At the ideal \((\sqrt3)\) the integral resultant \(A_{3,2}\) reduces to \(\prod_{S(x)=0}x^{g/2}=1\) by the even degree and constant term one of \(S\).
Also \(5^{b/2}\equiv1\bmod(\sqrt3)\) since \(b/2=2g/h\) is even.
The generator \(2+\sqrt3\) maps to minus one in the residue field \(\mathbb F_3\), forcing even \(n_3\).

At \(m=g\) each \(a_{j,\nu}\) is either zero or one.
The positive main term when \(a_{j,\nu}=0\) has size \((d/g)\log j\ge (r/g^2)\log j\) since \(s\ge1\).
For \(a_{j,\nu}=1\) the negative main term has absolute value \(\gg r/g\) since \(\rho=8/h\le8/39\).
The preceding bound for \(E_g\) gives the claimed strict signs.
With \(t=1\) and even degree \(d\), the resultant is \(S(\theta_\nu)\).
As \(S(\theta)\) is integral, its absolute logarithmic height is \(g^{-1}\sum_\nu\log^+|S(\theta_\nu)|\), even if it generates a proper subfield.
Averaging (1) over the \(g-s\) positive embeddings gives (4).
Finally write \(D_j=\sum_{k=0}^{g-1} c_k X^k\).
Its values at all \(g\) embeddings equal the values of \(S\) there.
The upper bound \(\max_\nu|D_j(\theta_\nu)|\le j\|D_j\|_1\) and finite Fourier inversion \(c_k\theta^k=g^{-1}\sum_\nu D_j(\theta_\nu)e^{-2\pi i k\nu/g}\) bound the logarithm of \(\|D_j\|_1\) within \(O(\log(2g))\) of the largest logarithm of an evaluation.
There is an unselected center and (1) at \(m=g\) gives (5).
