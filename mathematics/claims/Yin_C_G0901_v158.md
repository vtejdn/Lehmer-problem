# Free Mahler generators outside a finite exceptional monoid at every fixed measure ceiling

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded inputs including nonunits

Fix a multiplicative Mahler ceiling \(H>1\).
There is a degree cutoff \(D=D(H)\) with the following description.
Let \(\mathcal B\) be the monoid generated by positive integers at most \(H\) and the measures at most \(H\) of primitive irreducible integer polynomials of degree less than \(D\).
It is finitely generated.
There is a set \(\mathcal A_H\) of values \(1<a\le H\), realized individually by algebraic numbers of degrees \(d_a\ge D\), such that every integer-polynomial measure \(1\le A\le H\) has a unique numerical expression
\[
                             A=b\prod_{a\in S}a^{k_a},
             \qquad b\in\mathcal B,\quad S\subset\mathcal A_H\text{ finite},
                         \quad k_a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
The exponents and the numerical \(b\) are unique; factorizations within \(\mathcal B\) need not be unique.
In fact the high generators are multiplicatively independent modulo the entire group of fractions of \(\mathcal B\), without a ceiling on the proposed relation.
Each is an atom even in the full monoid of integer-polynomial measures.
Each irreducible input of measure at most \(H\) contributes either to \(\mathcal B\) alone or an integer times a positive power of just one high generator.

For \(b\in\mathcal B\) within the ceiling write \(d_0(b)\) for the minimum degree realizing \(b\) using integer content and only irreducible factors of degree less than \(D\); put \(d_0(1)=0\).
The minimum degree of any integer polynomial of measure \(A\) is exactly
\[
                               d_0(b)+\sum_{a\in S} d_a .
\]
This finite-exception description extends the [near-one unique factorization](Yin_C_G0901_v156.md#statement) without a reciprocity, unit or monicity restriction.
There exist unconditionally infinitely many multiplicatively independent atoms of the full monoid accumulating at a value strictly between one and the plastic number.
This last assertion does not give accumulation at one.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## The alternating spaces for arbitrary compressed conjugates

For a nontorsion algebraic number \(w\) first take the product \(z\) over its torsion-quotient conjugate class.
This preserves total measure also at finite places and does not increase degree. [Compression and labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels)
Distinct conjugates of \(z\) have distinct classes in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}/\mu_\infty\) in additive notation.
Each multiplicative label \(\{v,-v\}\) has one or two actual conjugates, uniformly.
At degree \(d\ge D\) use the [large block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) with fixed ceiling \(\log H\) to partition labels into \(u\) blocks of size \(l\), taking \(D\) large enough for all the group comparisons below and \(l\ge9\), \(2u<d\).

With one root per label use ordinary coordinates on the \(d\) roots.
The direct sum \(W\) of the blockwise sum-zero spaces is absolutely irreducible under the full group: the [independent alternating modules](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules) are irreducible on each block, inequivalent under their product and transitively permuted.
The map to the actual multiplicative classes is nonzero on their sum, since a difference within a block is nontorsion, hence injective there.
Its other summand is the image of the block constants, of dimension at most \(u<\dim W\); the two images have zero intersection by irreducibility.

With two roots per label orient each by one signed class.
The actual permutation action on the roots is identical to the action on their signed classes by compression, even when the paired roots have product a nontrivial root of unity.
The [permutation proof for signed lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) therefore applies: a nonuniform kernel sign on a block gives all supported double flips; otherwise a three-cycle has a unique supported lift of order three, and the two orbits of these lifts above a block give two inverse selections.
These statements use the permutations on positive and negative labels only.
In the first case the full signed coordinate space \(W\) maps isomorphically onto the actual span, by irreducibility from the distinct sign characters and transitive labels.
In the second case use the oriented selections and the supported pure alternating permutations.
As in the ordinary case the sum-zero space \(W\) embeds and the remaining summand is generated by half products, of dimension at most \(u\).
Opposite actual halves have opposite classes, so this last assertion does not require their products to be exact inverses.
In all three models the action on \(W\) detects the full root permutation action: on a sum-zero block of size at least three no permutation acts as minus the identity, and only the identity permutation acts as the identity. [Faithfulness](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#chain)

For a primitive minimum of leading coefficient \(a_0>0\), \([M(z)]\) is the sum of \([a_0]\) and the classes of the exterior roots.
The rational factor is fixed by the group and makes no contribution on \(W\).
The latter projection is given by the ordinary \(0,1\) exterior indicators, or by the signed \(0,\pm1\) coefficients, subtracting block means in a sum-zero model.
These projections are actual equivariant projections: the other class spaces just described, with the fixed rational line added, cannot contain a copy of \(W\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## Descent with the positive integer content accounted for

If the projection on \(W\) vanishes in a sum-zero model, the exterior indicator is constant on each ordinary block, or the signed exterior coefficient is constant on each oriented block.
Thus on each member of the partition into actual blocks or actual halves, all moduli are at most one or all are at least one.
Write \(\beta\) for the product on one such member.
The partition is transitive, so every distinct conjugate of \(\beta\) occurs the same number \(s\) of times among its products.
In particular \(\deg\beta\le2u<d\).
For archimedean moduli this partition gives exact equality
\[
       \prod_{z'}\max(1,|z'|)=
                              \left(\prod_{\beta'}\max(1,|\beta'|)\right)^s .
\]
For each rational prime \(p\) the left product with \(p\)-adic moduli is at least the corresponding right product, by submultiplicativity on each member of the partition.
The \(p\)-adic Gauss norm of a primitive integer minimum is one, and multiplicativity on its linear factors gives \(\prod_{z'}\max(1,|z'|_p)=|a_0|_p^{-1}\), and likewise for the leading coefficient \(b_0>0\) of the minimum of \(\beta\).
Thus \(b_0^s\mid a_0\) and
\[
                       M(z)=(a_0/b_0^s)\,M(\beta)^s .
\]
This also handles a torsion \(\beta\).
In the independent signed model vanishing of the full vector means no exterior roots and hence simply \(M(z)=a_0\).
Compress and repeat after each descent with nontorsion \(\beta\), whose measure is no larger.
The process ends by strictly decreasing degrees.
Its output for any individual source is either an integer times a power of one terminal low-degree measure, an integer alone, or an integer times a power of \(M(z)\) for a compressed high-degree term with nonzero projection.

<a id="independence"></a>
## Saturation and independence relative to the finite part

Group the compressed high-degree terms with nonzero projection by matching of conjugate class lines.
Two matched terms have, in matching embeddings, the same class stabilizer and hence the same embedded root field \(K\).
Scaling their conjugate classes identifies the same label partition and leading module, and their numerical log measures are proportional by height and the equality of degrees.
Choose a primitive generator \(\rho\) of the saturation of the line in \(K^\times/\mu(K)\).
This group on the line is cyclic also for nonunits: the orders of every element on the line vanish outside the finitely many prime ideals supporting a chosen representative, so it is a line in a finitely generated group of \(S\)-units modulo torsion. Equivalently, in the nonunit case any nonzero order at one prime ideal embeds that line subgroup into \(\mathbb Z\).
For every root-of-unity twist in \(K\) of every nonzero power of \(\rho\), its class stabilizer fixes the original compressed root, and its own field is contained in \(K\); thus it has full degree \(d_a=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
The height identity gives \(M(z)=M(\rho)^j\) for some integer \(j\ge1\) and \(M(\rho^k)=M(\rho)^{|k|}\).
These are the high generators \(a=M(\rho)\), independent of the choice of matched representative, and \([a]\) has nonzero projection on \(W\).

Enlarge \(D(H)\) to cover the [relative alternating theorem over all unmatched inputs](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) at this same ceiling.
In any purported relation among finitely many high generators modulo the low value group choose a participating term \(z\) of largest attached degree.
All other participating terms are unmatched and have degree at most its degree.
Each low input has smaller compressed degree and cannot match \(z\), since matching identifies the point stabilizers of compressed classes.
The normal subgroup fixing their splitting fields can therefore still effect the entire product of alternating label groups of \(z\).
It fixes all the other numerical measures and their classes, and all rational integers.
Its action on \(W\) has no fixed space: its image is normal, nontrivial there by the label permutations and faithfulness, and \(W\) is irreducible.
Consequently it cannot fix the class of the selected high generator, because its projection to \(W\) is nonzero.
This rules out the relation.
Bounded-degree coefficient finiteness makes the low input set finite, including nonunits at this fixed ceiling.
The descent and independence now give the stated unique numerical decomposition on factoring the original polynomial.
Each high generator is an atom of the full monoid since every factor in a possible decomposition has measure bounded by that generator and so is covered by the same theorem; its uniquely determined low part is one.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Recovering the exact degree and actual high atoms below the plastic number

For each high-producing source its degree is at least the compressed degree \(d_a\) of the term reached.
It can be replaced, as an integer-polynomial input, by the integer multiplier from the descent and the primitive minimum of \(\rho^k\) when its high part is \(a^k\).
For each low-producing source the corresponding terminal compressed root \(y\) has degree less than \(D\), and all its positive powers have full degree; hence its descended integer times \(M(y)^s\) is realized by that integer times the minimum of \(y^s\).
This replacement uses no more degree than the source, also when the output is a constant alone.
Together the low-producing sources and all descended integer multipliers realize the unique numerical low part \(b\) at degree at most the sum of the degrees of the low-producing sources.
Thus that sum is at least \(d_0(b)\); the high-producing sources separately cost at least \(\sum d_a\).
Conversely multiply a least-degree low realization by the minimum of \(\rho_a^{k_a}\) for each high generator, attaining the formula.

For an unconditional supply choose any ceiling strictly between the limit \(L\) of the [prime five-term family](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#statement) and the plastic number.
At sufficiently large primes its compressed sources have one independent signed block, unbounded distinct degrees and a nonzero exterior projection. [Actual orbit](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#orbit)
They therefore supply distinct high generators \(a_p=M(\rho_p)\).
Their powers \(a_p^{k_p}=M(P_p)\) tend to \(L>1\), and the saturation indices \(k_p\) all divide one fixed integer by the [bounded-length root estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#fields).
Passing to a constant-index subsequence gives accumulation of these independent full-monoid atoms at \(L^{1/k}\) for some \(k\ge1\), strictly between one and the plastic number.
