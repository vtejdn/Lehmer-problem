# A uniform Salem gap for two Chebyshev traces at separation two

<a id="statement"></a>
## Both signs of the lower harmonic

Define \(D_j(u+u^{-1})=u^j+u^{-j}\), including \(D_0=2\).
There exists \(\epsilon>0\) such that every Salem number \(\sigma\) whose monic trace minimal polynomial \(S\) of degree \(k+1\), \(k\ge2\), is strictly interlaced by \(2D_k+D_{k-2}\) satisfies \(\sigma\ge1+\epsilon\).
The same positive constant can be chosen for both \(2D_k+D_{k-2}\) and \(2D_k-D_{k-2}\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## The two possible prefixes

Suppose \(\sigma_i\to1\) on a sequence with \(C=2D_k+D_{k-2}\) a strict interlacer, and write \(m=k+1\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) and \(P(u)=u^mS(u+u^{-1})\).
The [separation-two profile classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v143.md#families) gives, on a subsequence, coefficientwise convergence \(P\to1-u\) or \(P\to1\).
The [exclusion of the linear prefix](Yin_C_G0345_v49.md#statement) eliminates the first alternative.
We treat the second one below.
The same profile classification already gives a uniform positive gap for the negative sign, so taking the smaller of the two gaps proves the joint assertion.

At the roots \(x_1<\cdots<x_m=T\) of \(S\) let \(c_j=C(x_j)/S'(x_j)>0\), with total weight two, and put \(t=c_m\).
Let \(\lambda_i\) give half the weight \(c_j\) to each circle lift of \(x_j\) for \(j<m\).
By the [residue generating identity](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) the integers \(g_{i,r}\) are given by
\[
 G_i(u)=2+\sum_{r>0}g_{i,r}u^r=\frac{(1-u^2)C^\sharp(u)}{P(u)},\qquad
 g_{i,r}=2\int u^r\,d\lambda_i+t(\sigma^r+\sigma^{-r}).
\]
Here \(C^\sharp=2+u^2+u^{2k-2}(1+2u^2)\), and \(G_i\to G=(1-u^2)(2+u^2)\).
Write \(d\theta\) for normalized arclength and \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
The classification gives the weak limit of \(\lambda_i+t\delta_1\) with doubled density
\[
                   B_0=(4-x^2)(1+x^2)=|1-z^2|^2(1+x^2).
\]
It has no atom at one, so \(t\to0\) and \(2\lambda_i\to B_0\,d\theta\).

<a id="first"></a>
## The polynomial determined by the first varying coefficient

Let \(n\) be the least positive index with \([u^n]P\ne0\).
It tends to infinity, and \(n\le m\) by reciprocity because \(P=1+u^{2m}\) would have no exterior root.
Bounded-degree finiteness gives \(k\to\infty\).
Hence \(2k-2-n\to\infty\), and the moment numerators \(G_iP\) and \(G\) agree through \(n+J\) for any fixed \(J\) eventually.
It follows that \(G_i-G\) starts at degree \(n\) with coefficient \(-2p_i\), where \(p_i=[u^n]P\).
As \(g_{i,n-1}=0\) eventually, the moment formula gives \(t\sigma^n\le4\sigma\), and its application at \(n\) bounds \(p_i\).
Extract \(p_i=p\ne0\), a limit \(t\sigma^n\to c\), and a weak limit of the pushforwards of \(2\lambda_i\) by \(z=u^{-1},\,v=u^n\).
The projection of \(v\) times this joint measure to the first circle has density \(U(z)\,d\theta\), with \(|U|\le B_0\).
For fixed integer \(j\), the coefficient with convention \(\widehat U(j)=\int z^{-j}U(z)\,d\theta\) satisfies
\[
                        \lim_i g_{i,n+j}=\widehat U(j)+c .
\]
When \(j<0\) the left side is zero, so decay of the Fourier coefficients of \(U\) gives \(c=0\).
The coefficients \(\widehat U(j)\) are therefore integer limits tending to zero at either end.
They vanish for \(j<0\), so \(U\) is an integer polynomial with \(U(0)=-2p\).
The bound by \(B_0\) forces double zeros at \(1,-1\), giving \((1-z^2)^2\mid U\).

Write \(P=1+u^nF_i\).
Subtracting \(G\) from \(G_iP\) shows at offsets \(n+j\), \(j\ge0\) fixed, that \(F_i\) stabilizes coefficientwise to \(F\in\mathbb Z[[z]]\) with \(U=-GF\).
Indeed \((G_i-G)/u^n\to U\), and the equation for successive coefficients of \(F_i\) has constant multiplier \(G_i(0)=2\).
In the dyadic field containing a root \(\beta\) of \(2+z^2\), one has \(|\beta|_2=2^{-1/2}\), so the integral coefficient series \(F\) converges at \(\beta\).
This gives \(U(\beta)=0\).
The monic polynomial \(2+z^2\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and coprime to \(1-z^2\), hence
\[
 U=(1-z^2)^2(2+z^2)V,\qquad V\in\mathbb Z[z],\qquad V(0)=-p .
\]
On the unit circle \(|2+z^2|^2=1+2x^2\), so
\[
                  |V|^2\le\frac{(1+x^2)^2}{1+2x^2}
                     =\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac34+\frac{1}{4(1+2x^2)} .
\]
The circle mean of the right side is strictly below two since \(\int x^2\,d\theta=2\) and the last term is strictly less than \(1/4\) almost everywhere.
Parseval's identity and the nonzero constant \(-p\) force \(V=-p\) with \(|p|=1\).

<a id="conditional"></a>
## The two possible values of the moving power

Use the same joint limit in the coordinates \(z=u^{-1}\) and \(w=p u^{n+2}=p z^{-2}v\), denoting the resulting measure by \(\Phi\).
For each integer \(j\) write \(B_j(z)\,d\theta\) for the projection of \(w^j d\Phi\).
All \(B_j\) are integer Laurent polynomials.
For clarity, the [moving moment argument](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#rows) applies here with the following bounds.
The \(m-1\) nodes \(y_1<\cdots<y_{m-1}\) of \(C\) all lie in \((-2,2)\) since \(C(2\cos(l\pi/k))\) has sign \((-1)^l\) for \(0\le l\le k\).
Telescoping the factors using \(y_{j-1}<x_j\) gives
\[
          t=2\prod_{j<m}\frac{T-y_j}{T-x_j}
              \ge\frac{2(T-y_{m-1})}{T-x_1}\ge\frac{2(T-2)}{T+2}.
\]
Thus \(t\sigma^{j(n+2)}=O_j((T-2)^{1-j})\) for \(j\ge1\), since \(t\sigma^n=O(1)\).
For any fixed offset \(r\), the coefficient of \((1-z)^{2j}B_j\) at \(r\) is the limit of \(p^j\sum_{l=0}^{2j}(-1)^l\binom{2j}{l}g_{i,j(n+2)+r-l}\).
Multiplication by the indicated difference kills the exterior contribution in the limit, because it supplies \((1-\sigma^{-1})^{2j}\) on the growing term and \((1-\sigma)^{2j}\) on the decreasing term, with \(T-2=(\sigma-1)^2/\sigma\).
Since \(|B_j|\le B_0\), these integer limiting coefficients tend to zero at both ends.
The resulting Laurent polynomial vanishes to order at least \(2j\) at one, so integral division gives \(B_j\in\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\).
Conjugation gives negative \(j\).

Every \(B_j\) has double zeros at \(1,-1\) by the bound \(|B_j|\le B_0\).
Consequently every \(D_j'=B_j/|1-z^2|^2\) is an integer Laurent polynomial on the circle, using \(|1-z^2|^2=-z^{-2}(1-z^2)^2\).
These are the projected moments of the finite positive measure \(\Psi=\Phi/|1-z^2|^2\), defined away from the two marginal null points.
Put \(s=z^2\).
Since \(B_1=p z^{-2}U\), its marginal and first moment after this division are
\[
                         D_0'=3+s+s^{-1},\qquad D_1'=2+s .
\]
Weight \(\Psi\) by \(|1-w|^2\) to form \(\Lambda\).
The \(j\)-th projected moment \(L_j=2D_j'-D_{j-1}'-D_{j+1}'\) is integral Laurent and has modulus bounded by \(L_0=2+s+s^{-1}=|1+s|^2\).
Its double zeros at \(z=\pm i\) give integral Laurent divisibility by \(L_0\), and Parseval makes \(L_j/L_0\) zero or a signed monomial.
On a common full-measure set in the first circle these quotients are the moments of the conditional probabilities for \(w\) under \(\Lambda\).
As in the [conditional graph argument](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#graphs), the indices with nonzero moment form a subgroup because a unit-modulus moment makes that power constant on almost every fiber.
The subgroup cannot be zero, since the resulting Haar fibers would have infinite integral of \(|1-w|^{-2}\), whereas recovery away from \(w=1\) gives part of the finite measure \(\Psi\).
Thus the conditional probabilities are uniform on the \(d\) roots of \(w^d=W(z)\) for some \(d\ge1\) and signed monomial \(W\), by comparison of all their moments.
Here \(W\ne1\) almost everywhere because \(\Lambda\) gives no mass to \(w=1\).

Dividing \(1-w\) by \(|1-w|^2\) and averaging over these roots gives
\[
           \frac{1}{1-\overline W}
             =\frac{D_0'-D_1'}{L_0}
             =\frac{1+s^{-1}}{2+s+s^{-1}}
             =\frac{1}{1+s}.
\]
The average follows from the finite geometric series, and the mass of \(\Psi\) at \(w=1\) makes no contribution.
Hence \(W=-s^{-1}\).
Orthogonality on the same \(d\) roots gives mean \(d/|1-W|^2\) for \(|1-w|^{-2}\).
The projected density of \(\Psi\) off \(w=1\) is therefore \(d L_0/|1-W|^2=d\).
Since its whole marginal \(3+s+s^{-1}\) has minimum one, \(d=1\).
Thus \(\Phi\), like \(\Psi\), is supported on \(w=1\) and \(w=-z^{-2}\).

<a id="trace"></a>
## Returning the two relations to the Salem trace roots

In the original variable the graph relations give \(u^{n+2}=p\) or \(u^n=-p\).
Take \(Q_i(u)=(u^{n+2}-p)(u^n+p)\) and let \(A_i(x)=Q_i(u)Q_i(u^{-1})\), an integer polynomial in \(x=u+u^{-1}\) by symmetric Laurent expansion.
As in the [positive integer trace test](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#trace),
\[
                         J_i=\sum_j c_j(2-x_j)^4 A_i(x_j)^2
\]
is a positive integer.
Indeed the weighted sum against any integer polynomial \(F\) is the coefficient of degree \(m-1\) in the integer remainder of \(CF\) modulo the monic \(S\), by Lagrange interpolation.
All summands of \(J_i\) are nonnegative, and the exterior term is strictly positive since the two binomials have only circle zeros.
On the circle \(|Q_i(u)|=|w-1|\,|w+z^{-2}|\).
The neutral contribution tends to zero because the fixed continuous test \((2-x)^4|w-1|^4|w+z^{-2}|^4\) vanishes on both graphs.
At the exterior trace, \(Q_i(\sigma^{-1})\) stays bounded and the contribution is \(O(t(T-2)^4\sigma^{4n+4})=O(T-2)\) by \(t\sigma^n=O(1)\) and the lower bound for \(t\).
Thus \(J_i\to0\), contradicting \(J_i\ge1\) and excluding the remaining prefix.
