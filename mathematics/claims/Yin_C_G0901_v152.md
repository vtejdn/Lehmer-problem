# An exponentially growing second logarithmic measure for an explicit five-term family

<a id="statement"></a>
## A subplastic first measure, a Gaussian law and a gap in the full splitting-field compositum

For every odd prime \(p\ge5\) put
\[
       P_p(X)=X^{2p}-X^{p+1}-X^p-X^{p-1}+1,\qquad A_p=M(P_p).
\]
Write \(m=\log M\) in total normalization, let \(J_p\) count the exterior roots of \(P_p\), and set \(D_p=2^{J_p}\binom p{J_p}\).
The [prime five-term family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) has \(J_p=p/3+O(1)\) and \(A_p\to L\) for a fixed \(1<L<\theta\), where \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0,\ \theta>1\).
Let
\[
       I_2=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}
                       \operatorname{arcosh}^2(1/2+\cos t)\,dt>0 .
\]
For all sufficiently large primes the actual value \(A_p\) has degree exactly \(D_p\).
For a uniformly chosen conjugate \(A_p'\), the real random variables \(\sqrt p\,\log|A_p'|\) tend in distribution to the centered Gaussian of variance \(I_2/3\), and
\[
             m(A_p)\ \sim\ \sqrt{\frac{I_2}{6\pi}}\frac{D_p}{\sqrt p},
       \qquad m(A_p)\asymp \frac1p\left(\frac{3}{\sqrt[3]2}\right)^p .   \tag{1}
\]
In particular these integer polynomials give rapid second-iterate growth with their first measures eventually below the plastic number.

Let \(L_p\) now denote the rational splitting field of \(P_p\).
The solvable closure of \(\prod_{p\ge5}L_p\), the compositum of all its finite solvable Galois extensions, has a uniform positive log Mahler gap for all nontorsion nonzero elements.
There is also a second-iterate statement at any fixed first log measure ceiling \(T>0\).
Take any finite or infinite set of primes sufficiently large in terms of \(T\) and put \(C=\prod_{p\ {\rm selected}}L_p\).
For every integer polynomial \(Q\ne0\) with nonzero roots in \(C^{\rm sol}\), \(m(Q)\le T\), and at least one root outside \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\), mark each source \(p\) to which such a root matches, meaning that some conjugates have nonzero powers equal up to torsion.
Every root outside \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\) matches exactly one source.
Let \(D=\deg M(Q)\).
For each marked \(p\) one has
\[
                     m(M(Q))\ \ge\ \frac{D}{D_p R_5}\,m(A_p),
                 \qquad \prod_{p\ {\rm marked}}D_p\mid D,                \tag{2}
\]
where \(R_5\) is the fixed [radical-denominator bound at coefficient length five](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement).
The arbitrary remaining factors may have all their roots in the rational solvable closure.
In particular no sequence of such polynomials using sources of unbounded largest marked prime can have uniformly bounded second measure.
The construction gives a positive accumulation value \(L\), not a sequence of first measures tending to one.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The orbit of the exterior product

The polynomial \(P_p\) is irreducible of degree \(2p\), with one positive real exterior root \(r_p\) and neutral roots for all sufficiently large \(p\). [Prime family and root count](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input)
One representative per inverse pair is multiplicatively independent modulo torsion by the [prime-degree argument](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#prime), since there is both a real exterior pair and a neutral pair.
This proves compression and makes the \(p\) labels precisely the inverse pairs.
The transitive label action in prime degree has just one canonical block.
At bounded total measure and sufficiently large degree it contains \(A_p^{\rm alt}\), here denoting the alternating permutation group to distinguish it from the number \(A_p\). [Canonical label action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement)
The oriented alternative at one block and \(m(r_p)<\log\varphi\), \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), would require an imaginary quadratic subfield of the real embedded field \(\mathbb Q(r_p)\).
Thus all even pair inversions are available independently of the label action. [Actual root alternatives](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement)

Choose the exterior orientation on each nonneutral pair and either orientation on a neutral one, writing the chosen members \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\).
The exterior product itself is positive, by the positive real root and complex-conjugate exterior pairs.
Its class has coefficient one at its \(J_p\) exterior positions and zero elsewhere.
The alternating group is transitive on sets of \(J_p\) labels.
Any sign prescription on those labels is attained by an even inversion after using an unoccupied position to adjust parity, since \(J_p<p\).
Their independent classes ensure distinctness for every signed subset.
Conversely every conjugate of the actual exterior product is one of the corresponding actual products, proving \(\deg A_p=D_p\).
In particular, with \(\ell_i=\log|x_i|\ge0\), the logarithm of a random conjugate modulus has exactly the law of
\[
                        X_p=\sum_{i\in S}\epsilon_i\ell_i ,
\]
where \(S\) is a uniform \(J_p\)-subset and its signs are independent fair signs.

<a id="limit"></a>
## The size of the radial displacements and the limiting second measure

For any root \(z\) of \(P_p\), \(z^p+z^{-p}=1+z+z^{-1}\).
By reciprocity and the monic coefficient bound, \(1/2\le|z|\le2\).
Hence \(2\sinh(p|\log|z||)\le1+|z|+|z|^{-1}\) is bounded uniformly.
Put \(G(\theta)=\operatorname{arcosh}(\max(1,1/2+\cos\theta))\).
Uniformly over every root as \(p\to\infty\),
\[
                             p|\log|z||=G(\arg z)+o(1).
\]
Indeed pass to any convergent subsequence of \(\arg z,\ p\arg z\bmod 2\pi\) and \(p\log|z|\), with limits \(\theta,\psi,y\).
The real and imaginary parts of the equation give \(\cosh y\cos\psi=1/2+\cos\theta\) and \(\sinh y\sin\psi=0\).
If \(y\ne0\) then \(\cos\psi=1\), since the right side is at least \(-1/2\), and \(|y|=G(\theta)\).
If \(y=0\), the right side is at most one, giving the same identity.
Compactness proves uniformity.

The phase distributions of the full minima tend to Haar probability on the circle, since the absolute heights are \(m(r_p)/(2p)\to0\) and the degrees increase. [Small-point equidistribution](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures)
Each inverse pair contributes the same value of the even function \(G^2\) at either orientation.
Thus \(p\sum_i\ell_i^2\to I_2\), while \(\max_i\ell_i=O(1/p)\).
For the uniform subset \(S\), \(p\sum_{i\in S}\ell_i^2\) has expectation tending to \(I_2/3\).
Its variance is at most \(p^2\sum_i\ell_i^4=O(1/p)\), since distinct inclusion indicators have nonpositive covariance.
Condition on \(S\).
The characteristic function of \(\sqrt p X_p\) is then \(\prod_{i\in S}\cos(t\sqrt p\,\ell_i)\).
For fixed \(t\) its logarithm is \(-t^2p\sum_{i\in S}\ell_i^2/2+O_t(p^2\sum_i\ell_i^4)\) for all large \(p\).
The product converges in probability to \(\exp(-t^2 I_2/6)\), and its bounded expectation converges to the same limit.
This proves the Gaussian convergence.
The uniformly bounded second moments imply convergence of the positive-part means.
The positive-part mean of the limiting Gaussian is \(\sqrt{I_2/(6\pi)}\), and \(m(A_p)=D_p\,\mathbb E[(X_p)_+]\), since \(A_p\) is an algebraic unit.
Stirling's formula with \(J_p=p/3+O(1)\) gives the second order-of-magnitude expression in (1).

<a id="fields"></a>
## Matching in a solvable extension and the bounded coefficient length

For sufficiently large primes the sources \(r_p\) are pairwise unmatched by their different compressed degrees: a match identifies the stabilizers of two nonzero proportional classes, and these fix the corresponding actual roots by compression.
Let \(\rho_p>1\) be the positive primitive unit on the line of \(r_p\) in its actual real field; write \(r_p=\rho_p^{k_p}\).
Its positive powers generate that field by [line saturation](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields).
Since \(P_p\) has length five and \(\rho_p\) is a \(k_p\)-th root in the same field, the radical-denominator theorem gives \(k_p\mid R_5\).
Write \(B_p=M(\rho_p)\); then \(B_p^{k_p}=A_p\).
As a positive exterior product \(B_p\) is strictly Perron and all its positive powers generate the same field, giving \(\deg B_p=D_p\) and \(m(B_p)=m(A_p)/k_p\). [Exterior product field](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field)

Apply the [bounded-measure compositum theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v106.md#statement) with a fixed source ceiling of one, target ceiling \(T\) and rational base.
Each source block field is rational as there is only one block; its label splitting field is nontrivial.
Every positive bounded-measure number in \(C^{\rm sol}\) either belongs to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\) or matches a unique source.
In the latter case its measure is \(j\,m(\rho_p)\) for a positive integer \(j\), by compression on the matched line.

For the gap assertion including **all** primes, take target ceiling one, absorb the finitely many small source splitting fields into one finite rational normal base \(K_0\), and apply the same theorem to the remaining large sources.
The positive measures at most one in \(K_0^{\rm sol}\) have a uniform lower bound.
Indeed it suffices to consider units below \(\log2\).
If their measures tended to zero, their torsion-class products would have the same measures and unbounded degrees by bounded-degree finiteness.
At level zero of the [relative block-field criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v108.md#statement) the rational label field of each such large compressed input must lie in \(K_0\).
This is impossible since its Galois group contains a supported alternating group on a block of size tending to infinity, while \(K_0\) is fixed.
For any remaining matching input its positive measure is at least \(m(r_p)/R_5\), uniformly positive since \(m(r_p)\to\log L>0\).
These bounds establish the asserted gap in the whole field.

## Why arbitrary factors from the rational solvable closure cannot cancel the second measure

Fix the ceiling \(T\) and the large-prime family in (2).
For a contributing source \(p\), the last part of the [unmatched-source theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v106.md#statement) gives the supported alternating label action on \(p\) over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\) and all the other source splitting fields.
Indeed use \(r_p\) as target, omit it from the source list, and take \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\) as external base.
All block fields are rational, and the target label field cannot be in the solvable base as its group contains the nonsolvable alternating group.
The image \(N_p\) on \(L_p\) of the group fixing those other fields and the solvable base is normal in the rational splitting group.
For a lift in \(N_p\) of a three-cycle \((1\,2\,3)\), its commutator with the available double inversion at \(1,4\) is a double inversion on \(1,2\), because conjugation merely permutes the entries of a pure sign change.
This commutator is in \(N_p\) by normality.
Conjugation now gives every double inversion in \(N_p\).
Together with its alternating label action this realizes the entire signed-subset orbit on \(A_p\) while fixing all other fields in question.
The individual orbits of the \(B_p\) are equivariantly identical to these by taking the full-degree \(k_p\)-th power.

Factor \(Q\) with multiplicities and put \(A=M(Q)\).
The absolute content, powers of \(X\) and factors with roots in \(\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\) contribute a positive algebraic integer \(B_0\in\mathbb Q^{\rm sol}\).
Indeed each individual nontrivial measure is the signed exterior product times the absolute leading coefficient of its primitive minimum; it is integral, since the leading coefficient times an elementary product of a subset of roots is integral at every finite place by the local Gauss norm formula.
It is strictly dominant among its distinct conjugates, since a different subset of the same size cannot attain the product of the exterior moduli.
The same property holds for products of such positive values, including a rational \(B_0\).
Every positive-measure factor outside the solvable base has measure \(B_p^j\) for its matched source \(p\) by the bounded first-measure comparison, as its own measure is at most \(T\).
Consequently \(A=B_0\prod_p B_p^{j_p}\) for integers \(j_p\ge1\) over exactly the marked set.

An automorphism fixing \(A\) must attain the maximum modulus for each factor separately, hence fixes every factor as an actual value.
It follows that the embedded field of \(A\) is the compositum of the individual value fields.
All marked signed-subset orbits are realized independently at each conjugate of \(B_0\), using the respective \(N_p\); each such action fixes the other marked fields and \(B_0\).
Thus \(D=(\deg B_0)\prod_p D_p\), and under uniform embeddings the log moduli are independent contributions \(\log|B_0'|+\sum_p (j_p/k_p)X_p\), where \(B_0'\) is uniform on its conjugates.
The \(X_p\) have mean zero by their signs, and the first term has nonnegative mean because the absolute norm of \(B_0\) is a nonzero integer.
Conditional Jensen for the increasing convex function \(s\mapsto s_+\), conditioning on any one \(X_p\), therefore gives
\[
                  m(A)\ \ge\ \frac{D}{D_p}\frac{j_p}{k_p}m(A_p).
\]
This proves (2) with the stated degree divisibility.
The [earlier splitting-field exterior-count obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v310.md#statement) controls the number of exterior roots of a bounded radical.
Here the second total measure itself grows exponentially, including for bounded-first-measure polynomials with additional solvable factors.
