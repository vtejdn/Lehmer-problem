# The pinned exterior root separates the binary period from rational multiples of the degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## What the real pole forces on the binary residue word

Let \(q\ge2\), \(p=q+1\), \(s=p/q\), \(L=2q+1\), and \(M=pq\), with \(q\) an integer.
For a [genuine integral first-nonunit trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) let \(F\) be the reciprocal Salem minimum of degree \(d=2D\).
Let \(T\) be its exterior real root and \(A\) the multiplicative order of \(Z\) in \(\mathbb F_2[Z]/(\bar F)\), allowing repeated factors.
For each fixed \(0<\eta\le 1/4\) there is an effective \(c_\eta>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(d\) in terms of \(\eta\),
\[
 |bA-nd|>d^{1-\eta}
 \quad\left(n\in\mathbb Z,\quad
       1\le b\le\exp\{c_\eta\log d/\log^2(2q+1)\},\quad b\in\mathbb Z\right).       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(d/\gcd(A,d)\) exceeds the displayed denominator cutoff.
The cutoff is at least \(\exp(c'_\eta\log d/(\log\log(9d))^2)\) uniformly over realizable heights.
At each fixed height it grows at least as a fixed positive power of \(d\).
One can read \(A\) directly from the complete binary factorization: its odd part is the least common multiple of the residue root orders, and its 2-part is the smallest power of two no smaller than the largest factor multiplicity.
Indeed \(Z^{2^a m}-1=(Z^m-1)^{2^a}\) for odd \(m\), and the latter base polynomial has simple roots.

There are one-prime countermodels passing arbitrarily prescribed finite-precision dyadic power congruences, the exact pole, the powered capacity tests and the long zero prefix, with all roots in distinct residue classes modulo two.
For the particular families below \(A=hd-O_{q,B}(1)\) for a positive integer \(h\), where \(B\) is the prescribed precision.
Consequently (1) excludes their binary words for true solutions in all sufficiently large degrees at each such height and precision.
The estimate constrains a residue period; it does not imply that four conjugates occupy a disk of radius \(1/8\).

<a id="real"></a>
## The archimedean center for the long exponent

In the graph coordinates put \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\).
The [coprime power representation](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) gives \(T=\beta^u,\ Y=\beta^v\), \(1\le u<v\), with \(\beta>1\).
Let \(\epsilon=1\) for even \(q\), \(-1\) otherwise.
The [binary period bound and congruence](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities) give \(N=v-\epsilon u=kA\), where \(1\le k<2\log_2 L\); the [degree and exponent bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#census) give \(q,u\ll(\log(3d))^3\) and \(N\ll d(\log(3d))^3\log\log(9d)\).
All implied constants here and below are absolute and effective except where a dependence is indicated.

Set \(r=q/p\) and \(\rho=1-1/d\).
For \(j\ge1\) write \(P_j=2T^{-j}+\sum_z z^j\), summing over the \(d-2\) neutral roots.
The [reflected energy inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v896.md#energy) gives
\[
                 \sum_{j\ge1}\rho^j |P_j|^2/j\ll d\log(2d).
\]
At all sufficiently large degrees \(d\ge2p\) by the height bound, and \(r^2/\rho\le r\).
The Taylor expansion of \(\log|1-rz|\), followed by Cauchy–Schwarz using \(\sum(r^2/\rho)^j/j\le\log p\), yields
\[
 \left|\sum_{z}\log|1-rz|\right|\ll
        \sqrt{d\log(2d)\log p}+\log p .
\]
Here the replacement of neutral power sums by \(P_j\) costs at most \(2\sum_j r^j/j=2\log p\).
By the pole norm \(N_{\mathbb Q(T)/\mathbb Q}(p-qT)=L^2\),
\[
 \log Y=(d-2)\log p+\sum_z\log|1-rz|
                +\log(pT-q)+\log(p-q/T)-2\log L .
\]
Both factors in the last two positive-real logarithms are between one and three since \(1<T<s\).
Thus \(\log Y=d\log p+O(\sqrt{d\log(2d)\log L})\).

The trace difference \(h-w\), for \(h=1/M\) and \(w=T+T^{-1}-2\), is less than \(L^2/M^{D+1}\) by the [pinning bound](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#windows).
It equals \((s-T)(1-1/(sT))\ge(s-T)/p\).
Consequently \(N|\log s-\log T|=o(1)\) uniformly as the degree grows.
Using \(N\log T=u(\log Y-\epsilon\log T)\) proves
\[
     \left|\frac{kA}{d}\log s-u\log p\right|
        \ll u\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)\log L}{d}} .                         \tag{2}
\]

<a id="forms"></a>
## Repulsion of rational slopes

If (1) fails at the specified \(b,n\), then \( |n|k\ll b(\log(3d))^{C_0}\) for an absolute \(C_0\), by the upper bounds for \(N/d,k\).
Apply the [Baker–Wüstholz theorem](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#wus02-u001) to
\(\Lambda=nk\log s-bu\log p\).
The rationals \(s,p>1\) are multiplicatively independent, since \(q,p\) are coprime and \(q>1\), and \(bu>0\).
If \(n=0\) one can use the direct value \(|\Lambda|=bu\log p\); otherwise use the two real logarithms over \(\mathbb Q\).
Both modified heights are at most \(C\log L\).
The resulting bound, uniform also at \(b=1\), is
\[
           \log|\Lambda|\ge-C_1\log^2 L\,(\log b+\log\log(9d)).
\]
For \(b\le d^{c_\eta/\log^2L}\) choose \(c_\eta\) sufficiently small so the first term costs at most \((\eta/4)\log d\).
Since \(q\ll(\log(3d))^3\), the second term costs \(O((\log\log(9d))^3)=o(\log d)\).
Eventually \(|\Lambda|\ge d^{-\eta/2}\).

On the other hand (2) and \(|bA-nd|\le d^{1-\eta}\) give
\[
 |\Lambda|\ll k(\log s)d^{-\eta}
       + bu\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)\log L}{d}} .
\]
Reduce \(c_\eta\) if necessary to ensure \(b\le d^{1/8}\).
The right side is \(o(d^{-\eta/2})\) for the stated \(\eta\), proving (1).
Taking \(b=d/\gcd(A,d)\) and \(n=A/\gcd(A,d)\) gives the denominator consequence.

## Squarefree binary data satisfying an arbitrarily deep prescribed graph congruence

Fix any sufficiently large \(q\) divisible by four, an odd denominator prime \(\ell\) and any prescribed \(B\ge1\).
Let \(u_0=2^B\) and \(J=q/2+1\).
Choose an odd prime \(h>q+1\), of size comparable to \(u_0q\log p\), for instance between \(u_0q\log p/3\) and \(2u_0q\log p/3\) by Bertrand's theorem after adjusting the endpoints by rounding.
Choose a distinct odd prime \(t>q+B+h\).
Require \(D\) to satisfy the [degree congruences and rotation conditions](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences), raising the \(q\)-side modulus to \(q^{J+1+B}\) in the congruence for \(p^D\).
The same principal-unit proof works there; the prescribed residues for \(D\) modulo primes of \(M\) do not change.
Impose also \(D=0\bmod t\) and, modulo \(h\), choose \(2D/t+2-h\ne0\).
Both new primes exceed \(q+1\), so these requirements are compatible with all the existing restrictions by Chinese remaindering.
Write \(l=2D/t+2-h\); for arbitrarily large such \(D\) it is positive odd, larger than one, and prime to \(h\).

Put \(B_j(W)=1+W+\cdots+W^{j-1}\), and use the monic reciprocal template
\[
                      U(Z)=B_l(Z^t) B_h(Z^t).
\]
It has degree \(d=t(l+h-2)=2D\), and its first \(J\) coefficients below the leader vanish.
Its homogeneous pole evaluation \(q^d U(p/q)\) is congruent to \(L^2\) modulo \(2^BM^{J+1}\).
Indeed the first nonconstant exponent offset on either end is at least \(t\), so modulo \(q^{J+1+B}\) or \(p^{J+1}\) the evaluation retains only the respective pure end power.
These two powers give \(L^2\) at the required primes by the degree choices, and \(2^B\mid q^B\).
Since the image of the integral zero-prefix lattice of differences under the pole evaluation is \(M^{J+1}\mathbb Z\), an integral correction by \(2^B\) times a lattice element produces an exact-pole integral representative in the class of \(U\bmod2^B\).
The [single-prime construction with residue choice](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) gives \(\mathbb Z[1/\ell]\)-polynomials in this class with the stated strict root signature, exterior root as close to \(s\) from below as desired at growing admitted degrees, zero initial power sums, and all powered capacity tests.
More explicitly its auxiliary \(B_LQ_t\) model approaches \(s\) at each fixed height as the degree grows, and its local approximations can be taken in arbitrarily small open neighborhoods of that model. [Archimedean control](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#real)

Since \(t,l,h\) are odd, \(U\bmod2\) is squarefree: its factors have simple individual roots, and a shared root would give a common nonidentity \(l\)-th and \(h\)-th root on taking its \(t\)-th power.
Thus every two distinct dyadic roots of the lifted rational polynomial have different residues in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\).
The order of the class of \(Z\) is \(A=tlh\), since primitive \(tl\)-th and \(th\)-th roots both occur and each root has order dividing \(tlh\).
Already over \(\mathbb Z\), \(U\mid Z^A-1\) by the same simple-root description.
In the monic quotient modulo \(2^B\), the expression \(W=(pZ-q)/(Z(p-qZ))\) is defined and congruent to one modulo two.
Therefore \(W^{u_0}=1=Z^A\bmod2^B\); repeated squaring suffices.
Taking \(v_0=A+u_0\) gives coprime positive exponents with the required local relation \(Y^{u_0}=Z^{v_0}\bmod2^B\).
For arbitrarily large admitted degrees the size upper bounds \(u_0<(\log(3d))^3/(2q)\), \(k=1<2\log_2 L\) allow these choices.
This is a finite-precision congruence, not a characteristic-zero power identity.
At fixed \(q,B,\ell\) one has \(A=hd-th(h-2)\), so (1) excludes these binary words regardless of the chosen exterior angles or possible exponent choices at a genuine solution.
The precision is chosen before the degree and can increase with the height; no uniform assertion for a precision chosen after seeing a particular degree is needed.
