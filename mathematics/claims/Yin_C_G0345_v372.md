# Critical points after inseparable reduction on a power disk

<a id="statement"></a>
## A disk estimate in mixed characteristic

Let \(p\ne\ell\) be primes and normalize \(v(\ell)=1\) on a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\).
Let \(U_0,V_0\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero coprime polynomials over \(\mathbb Q\) with nonzero constant terms, and let \(k\in\mathbb Z\) satisfy \(p\nmid k\).
Suppose \(U_0(x^p)+x^kV_0(x^p)\) vanishes to order at least \(e\ge1\) at distinct units \(\alpha_i\).
Multiplication of this expression by a monomial allows arbitrary Laurent rows in two different residues modulo \(p\).
The \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) are distinct since the ratio of the two nonvanishing row values determines \(\alpha_i^k\) within the fiber.
Work over a finite extension containing all the data and write \(\mathcal O\) for its valuation ring.
First remove the common \(\ell\)-content from the two rows, which preserves their vanishing orders.

Consider a closed disk \(X=c+\tau Y\) with \(v(c)=0,\ v(\tau)=s>0,\ v(Y)\ge0\), containing \(m\) of the specified \(\beta_i\).
Write \(g\) for the minimum valuation of all coefficients of \(U_0(c+\tau Y),V_0(c+\tau Y)\).
Divide both by a scalar of valuation \(g\), denote the resulting integral polynomials by \(a,b\), and their reductions by \(A,B\).
Set \(n=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\), with \(\deg 0=-\infty\).
At least one reduction is nonzero, and \(sn\le g\) by the binomial expansion of the original rows.
When the reduced ratio is nonconstant, let \(\ell^t\) be its inseparable degree after cancelling common factors.
Then
\[
       2g\ \ge\ s\big((e-1)m+1\big)-t,\qquad 0\le t\le\log_\ell n.        \tag{1}
\]
Points with the same \(Y\)-residue count separately in \(m\).
For constant reduced ratio, including a zero row, suppose the nodes in use are partitioned by their original residues into groups of sizes \(m_C\), each at a single scaled residue \(y_C\), with distinct \(y_C\) for distinct groups.
For a nonempty collection of these groups,
\[
                 n\ \ge\ e\left(\sum_C m_C-\max_C m_C\right).               \tag{2}
\]
One may discard other nodes before applying (2).

<a id="derivative"></a>
## Divisibility of the critical polynomial

Differentiate with respect to \(Y\) and set
\[
                K=pX(a'b-ab')-\tau k ab,\qquad X=c+\tau Y .
\]
At a node \(Y_i=(\beta_i-c)/\tau\) use the \(p\)-th root branch taking \(X=\beta_i\) to \(\alpha_i\).
Its \(k\)-th power \(f_i\) obeys \(pXf_i'=\tau k f_i\).
The order of \(a+f_i b\) at \(Y_i\) is at least \(e\), since the branch has nonzero linear coefficient in characteristic zero.
Substitution in the formula for \(K\) gives vanishing to order at least \(e-1\), as in the [critical-point identity](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#jets).
The coefficient of \(Y^j\) of either scaled row has valuation at least \(js-g\).
In \(c(a'b-ab')\), each coefficient at degree \(j\) involves row indices summing to \(j+1\).
The factor \(\tau\) in the remaining terms compensates for one lower index sum.
Thus
\[
                         v([Y^j]K)\ge(j+1)s-2g.                           \tag{3}
\]

For a nonconstant \(A/B\), write \(A/B=\psi(Y^{\ell^t})\) where \(\psi'\ne0\).
The kernel of the derivative on the rational function field in characteristic \(\ell\) consists of rational functions in \(Y^\ell\), by expansion in the basis \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{\ell-1}\).
Iterating gives this factorization and \(\ell^t\le\deg(A/B)\le n\).
After a finite unramified extension choose an integral \(y_0\) whose residue avoids the zeros of \(B\), the poles of \(\psi(\bar y_0^{\ell^t})\) and the zeros of \(\psi'(\bar y_0^{\ell^t})\).
In the variable \(Z=Y-y_0\), the first nonconstant term of the reduced quotient is then of degree \(\ell^t\), by the Frobenius identity.
Let \(X_0=c+\tau y_0\) and let \(u(Z)\) be the formal \(p\)-th root of \(1+\tau Z/X_0\) with constant one.
It exists by recursive division by \(p\), and \(u\) reduces to one.
Since \(b(y_0)\) is a unit, \(L=u^{-k}a(y_0+Z)/b(y_0+Z)\) is integral and has a unit coefficient at degree \(\ell^t\).
The coefficient of \(L'\) one degree lower therefore has valuation exactly \(t\).
Differentiation gives
\[
                     L'= \frac{u^{-k}}{pX b(y_0+Z)^2}K(y_0+Z).
\]
The formal multiplier is integral with integral inverse.
If every coefficient of \(K\) had valuation greater than \(t\), the same would hold after integral translation and multiplication by this series, a contradiction.
Thus \(K\ne0\) and its minimum coefficient valuation \(\nu\) satisfies \(\nu\le t\).
After dividing by a scalar of valuation \(\nu\), its reduction has degree at most \((2g+\nu)/s-1\) by (3).
The degree of this reduction counts the roots in the closed unit disk with multiplicity, by the [normalized linear-factor proof](Yin_C_G0345_v367.md#pencil).
The \(m\) characteristic-zero nodes supply at least \((e-1)m\) roots, proving (1).

<a id="groups"></a>
## Coalescing nodes on one inverse branch

Fix one residue \(C\) among the original \(\alpha_i\) above the disk.
Choose the \(p\)-th root \(z_C\) of \(c\) with this residue after a finite extension.
The branch \(z_C(1+\tau Y/c)^{1/p}\) belongs to the ring of integral restricted power series in \(Y\), whose coefficients tend to zero.
Indeed the formal \(p\)-th root of \(1+T\) with constant one has integral coefficients by recursion, and \(T=\tau Y/c\) has positive depth.
Its integral powers, including negative powers, have the same property and constant reduction.
At each node of group \(C\) the branch equals the specified root, since distinct \(p\)-th roots of a unit have distinct reductions.
The integral restricted series \(a+z_C^k(1+\tau Y/c)^{k/p} b\) therefore has a zero of order at least \(e\) at each node of this group.
Division by \(Y-y\) at an integral root \(y\) preserves integrality and convergence on the closed disk.
Indeed one divides the difference of each monomial and its value at \(y\) by \(Y-y\), and the coefficient tails still tend to zero.
Dividing successively at all nodes and reducing gives
\[
                    (Y-y_C)^{e m_C}\mid A+\lambda_C B,
                  \qquad \lambda_C=\bar z_C^{\,k}.
\]
Distinct groups have distinct \(\lambda_C\), since \(p\nmid k\).
If the reduced ratio is constant, write \((A,B)=J(a_0,b_0)\) for constants \(a_0,b_0\) not both zero.
All but at most one group have \(a_0+\lambda_C b_0\ne0\), so their factors at distinct \(y_C\) divide \(J\) simultaneously.
Its degree \(n\) gives (2).

<a id="resultant"></a>
## Summing contributions at distinct original residues

For disks as above with distinct center residues let \(g_j,s_j\) be their common minimum row valuation and depth.
The [resultant inequality for disjoint residue classes](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#resultant) gives
\[
               v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)\bigr)
                              \ge\sum_j g_j^2/s_j .
\]
This applies both to (1) and to (2), using \(g_j\ge s_j n_j\) for the latter.
Restoring removed common content increases the valuation by its valuation times the sum of the row degrees.
For a nested family, the same row factors can contribute to several \(g_j\), so this particular summation requires the stated separation of center residues.
