# Rational Cayley parameters in Salem fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform count for two powers and an explicit denominator bound

Write \(C(z)=(z-1)/(z+1)\).
For every genuine real Salem number \(\tau>1\), the number of tuples
\[
   (u,v,\epsilon,\eta),\qquad 0<u<v,\quad \epsilon,\eta\in\{1,-1\},
       \qquad C(\eta\tau^v)/C(\epsilon\tau^u)\in\mathbb Q
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]
is at most the absolute constant \(B=4\cdot4140\exp(12\cdot42^{21})\).
Both exponents and the rational coefficient are unrestricted.
In particular the bound applies to the signed two-power graph of the consecutive-coefficient family on a given Salem line.
These are bounds on the number of parameters, not on their magnitudes.

Now let \(\alpha>1\) be any genuine real Salem number of degree \(2n\), let \(m=\log\alpha\), \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), and write \(w=t-2,\ z=\sqrt w>0\) at the distinguished real embedding.
For coprime positive integers \(r,s\), put
\[
             y_{r,s}=\frac{r+sz}{r-sz},\qquad E_{r,s}=r^2-s^2w .
\]
The numbers \(y_{r,s}\) have one exterior real conjugate in modulus, one real interior conjugate, and unit-circle values at all other embeddings.
At most \(B+1\) positive rational slopes \(r/s\) give algebraic units, uniformly in \(\alpha\).
Each unit so obtained is a signed Salem number up to conjugation.

For \(0<m\le1/2\) and every integer \(T\ge6\), write
\[
           H_T=14n\left(\frac{\log T}{T-1}+\frac{T+1}{6n}m\right).
\]
For all the slopes simultaneously, in total logarithmic Mahler measure,
\[
    m(y_{r,s})\ge(n-1)\log(r^2+s^2)-H_T .                                \tag{2}
\]
An integral value requires
\[
         |r^2-s^2w|\le \frac{\exp(H_T)}{(r^2+s^2)^{n-1}}.                 \tag{3}
\]
In particular at bounded \(m\le1/2\) and all sufficiently large \(n\) there is no integral value at any positive integer slope.
The allowed rational slopes in (3) must approximate the pole in the distinguished real embedding even when the finite-place cancellations occur at ramified or dyadic primes.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Counting rational ratios on all signed pairs of powers

Fix a neutral conjugate \(\mu\) of \(\tau\).
The two numbers \(\tau,\mu\) are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, since an integral relation first has zero exponent on the only one of modulus greater than one, and \(\mu\) is nontorsion.
Set \(A=\epsilon\tau^u,\ B_1=\eta\tau^v,\ A'=\epsilon\mu^u,\ B'_1=\eta\mu^v\).
Rationality in (1) gives equality of the ratios at these two embeddings.
Using \(C(B_1)/C(A)=(AB_1-1+B_1-A)/(AB_1-1-B_1+A)\) gives the eight-term relation
\[
              (B_1-A)(A'B'_1-1)=(B'_1-A')(AB_1-1).                       \tag{4}
\]
All terms of its expansion are nonzero signed monomials.

Fix the two signs and partition the eight labeled terms into minimal vanishing subsums.
There are at most 4140 partitions of eight labels, and at most four parts in any such partition since singletons cannot vanish.
Within a part of size \(h\ge2\), divide by one specified term and absorb the signs into the coefficients.
The resulting equation in \(h-1\le7\) variables is nondegenerate, and the tuples of term ratios belong to the image of \(\mathbb Z^2\) under a multiplicative homomorphism since their exponents are integral linear forms in \(u,v\).
Thus each part has at most \(\exp(3\cdot42^{21})\) tuples by the [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound), using rank at most two in the group of tuples.

For any fixed collection of these ratio tuples the preimage in \(\mathbb Z^2\) is empty or a coset of a subgroup.
If two different qualifying pairs had the same ratios, they would yield a nonzero direction \((p,q)\) along which (4) held at every translate \((u+kp,v+kq)\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\).
In (4) now replace \(\tau^{kp},\tau^{kq}\) by \(X^p,X^q\) and \(\mu^{kp},\mu^{kq}\) by \(Y^p,Y^q\).
The resulting Laurent polynomial must vanish identically.
Indeed its values at \((\tau^k,\mu^k)\) vanish for every \(k\), and distinct Laurent monomials have distinct bases \(\tau^i\mu^j\) by independence.
The Vandermonde matrix on these bases forces all grouped coefficients to vanish.
Reversing the clearing of denominators therefore makes
\[
       \frac{C(\eta\tau^v X^q)}{C(\epsilon\tau^u X^p)}
       =\frac{C(\eta\mu^v Y^q)}{C(\epsilon\mu^u Y^p)}
\]
an identity of rational functions of independent variables, so both sides are constant.
None of the denominators is identically zero, including when an exponent direction vanishes, because the original \(u,v\) are positive and the bases are nontorsion.
If just one of \(p,q\) vanishes, the first function is nonconstant.
Otherwise its limit at positive infinity is \(1\) or \(-1\).
Constant \(1\) forces \(\eta\tau^v X^q=\epsilon\tau^u X^p\); constant \(-1\) forces the product of these two arguments to be one.
Evaluating the two conditions in absolute value at \(X=1\) contradicts \(0<u<v\).
Hence each collection of ratio tuples contributes at most one pair, and counting partitions, parts and signs proves (1).

In the graph \(Y=(p_0 X-q_0)/(p_0-q_0 X)\) with \(p_0=q_0+1\), the Cayley ratio is \(2q_0+1\).
Thus the signed exponent pair determines \(q_0\). [Signed graph](Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#norm)
For a fixed embedded real Salem field take \(\tau\) to be its primitive positive norm-one generator.
All real Salem numbers generating this same field are positive powers of \(\tau\). [Full-field Salem generator](Yin_C_G0485_v1.md#salem-relative-regulator-identity)
If such an input is \(\tau^g\) and the short and long exponents are coprime, their scaled exponents in (1) have greatest common divisor \(g\), so recording different powers in that field requires no additional counting factor.
At a fixed measure ceiling and sufficiently large trace degree, the same bound holds collectively for the positive Salem inputs over one specified embedded totally real trace field: the [common-base comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#base) puts all their quadratic extensions in the same field.
This counts primitive exponent presentations, before any common dilation.

<a id="field"></a>
## The quadratic field of the Cayley pencil

The \(n-1\) conjugates \(w_i\) other than the positive \(w\) lie in \((-4,0)\).
Consequently \(w\) is not a square in \(F\), and \(K'=F(z)\) has two real embeddings over the distinguished place and only complex embeddings elsewhere.
The denominator and numerator defining \(y_{r,s}\) do not vanish.
The other real value is the inverse of the chosen one, whose modulus is strictly greater than one; the nonreal values have modulus one since their \(z\) is purely imaginary.
The unique exterior embedding forces \(y_{r,s}\) to generate \(K'\), since each embedding of any proper subfield would occur more than once among the embeddings of \(K'\).
The trace field is \(F\), since \(y_{r,s}+y_{r,s}^{-1}\in F\) and \(K'\) has degree at most two over the field of that trace.

The norm of \(y_{r,s}\) over \(F\) is one.
The relative norm-one units of \(K'/F\) have rank one by Dirichlet, as their norm image on the full unit group contains \(U_F^2\), and their torsion consists of the two signs.
A positive generator \(\xi>1\) modulo torsion has the same Salem signature and generates \(K'\) by the unique exterior embedding. [Relative unit argument](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units)
If \(y_{r,s}\) is a unit then \(y_{r,s}=\pm\xi^k,\ k\ge1\).
All the values \(C(y_{r,s})=sz/r\) lie on one rational line.
Two distinct slopes cannot yield the two signs at the same exponent, since \(C(-\xi^k)=1/C(\xi^k)\) and a rational ratio between these two values would give \(C(\xi^k)^2\in\mathbb Q\).
This would imply \(\deg\xi^k\le2\), whereas the unique exterior value gives \(\mathbb Q(\xi^k)=K'\) of degree \(2n\ge4\).
For either fixed sign and exponent the value of \(C(y_{r,s})\) recovers the slope.
Each pair of distinct unit slopes therefore gives a tuple in (1) for \(\xi\).
If there are any, fixing one of them yields the asserted bound \(B+1\).

When \(\alpha\) is primitive modulo torsion on its line in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(K'\) differs from that original field.
Indeed the two quadratic squareclasses are \(t-2\) and \((t-2)(t+2)\).
Equality would make \(t+2\) a square in \(F\), and then \((\alpha+1)/\sqrt{t+2}\) in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) would square to \(\alpha\), contrary to primitivity.

## The primitive denominator and the total measure at every rational slope

Write \(E=E_{r,s}\).
The trace of \(y_{r,s}\) is \(-2+4r^2/E\), with denominator ideal \((E)/\mathfrak g\) in lowest terms, where \(\mathfrak g=\gcd((E),(4r^2))\).
At any finite place the two local roots have product one.
If their trace has a pole, the larger root in modulus has exactly its modulus by the ultrametric inequality, and otherwise both roots have modulus one.
Counting the local embeddings with their degrees therefore gives \(\log N((E)/\mathfrak g)\) for the total finite-place contribution to \(m(y_{r,s})\).
Adding the only positive archimedean log yields the exact identity
\[
       M(y_{r,s})=\frac{(r+s\sqrt w)^2\prod_{i>1}(r^2+s^2|w_i|)}
                           {N\mathfrak g}.
\]
This uses the absolute degree \(2n\) just proved and allows nonintegral inputs.
The integral cases are exactly those with \((E)=\mathfrak g\).

The ideal \(\mathfrak g\) divides \(\gcd((E),(r^2))\,\gcd((E),(4))\) by the minimum-of-valuations inequality.
The first factor divides \((w)\) since \(r,s\) are coprime.
If \(r,s\) are odd, \(E\equiv3-t\equiv-(t+1)\pmod4\).
If \(r\) is even then \(E\equiv-w\pmod4\), and if \(s\) is even then \(E\equiv1\pmod4\).
These congruences hold in the full ring, including ramified dyadic places, and imply
\[
     N\mathfrak g\le |N_F w|\max(|N_F w|,|N_F(t+1)|).
\]
The two factors are nonzero.
For the consecutive-power norm budget at \(\alpha\) of height \(m/(2n)\), let \(L_j=(2n)^{-1}\log|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}\Phi_j(\alpha)|\).
Here \(\log|N_F w|=2nL_1\) by the relative norm of \(\alpha-1\), and \(\log|N_F(t+1)|=nL_3\) by \(t+1=\Phi_3(\alpha)/\alpha\).
The weights of the [simultaneous cyclotomic norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) at 1 and 3 are respectively 1 and at least \(1/12\) when \(T\ge6\). [Weight estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#auxiliary)
Thus \(\log N\mathfrak g\le H_T\).

For \(m\le1/2\) one has \(0<w=2\cosh m-2<1\), and \(\prod_{i>1}|w_i|=|N_Fw|/w>1\).
Convexity of \(x\mapsto\log(r^2+s^2e^x)\) gives \(\prod_{i>1}(r^2+s^2|w_i|)\ge(r^2+s^2)^{n-1}\).
The exact formula now implies (2), and integrality gives (3) by \(|N_FE|=N\mathfrak g\).
Choosing \(T\) of order \(\sqrt{n\log(2n)}\) makes \(H_T=O(\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\) uniformly for \(m\le1/2\).
For \(s=1\) the left side of (3) is at least \(1-(2\cosh(1/2)-2)>0\), while the right side tends to zero uniformly in \(r\ge1\).
This proves the integer-slope exclusion.

The [signature and ambiguous-class identity](Yin_C_G0901_v111.md#realization) permits \(n-s_{\rm sig}-a\le2\) over each prescribed totally real field by taking the finite norm rank zero, where \(s_{\rm sig}\) and \(a\) denote the ranks in that result.
Thus those two ranks alone do not force a uniform deficit across all quadratic Salem extensions.
The rational-parameter restrictions above apply to individual Salem inputs without assumptions on those ranks, but they leave the possibility of a shrinking sequence through differing trace fields open.
