# Separating the powered residues of conjugates in the odd-block pencil

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower bound for each primitive direction

Use the [odd-block pencil](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#statement) with base scale \(L\), \(K=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\), block length \(w=\lfloor L/(100K^3)\rfloor\), \(r=Kw\), base sum \(s\), and actual exponent sum \(\Sigma=gs\).
Require the monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) to have nontorsion quotients between distinct conjugates.
Write \(e=\deg P\), \(h=\log M(P)\), and suppose the two coprime rows satisfy the pencil test with common graph multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\).
There is a computable \(j=j(L)\le K\) tending to infinity such that with width \(b=rj\), for every \(g=3^a\le j\), \(a\ge0\), the primitive integral representative \(A\) of every nonzero rational pencil direction satisfies
\[
                        m(A)\gg kgwh .                                      \tag{1}
\]
The constant is absolute, and the assertion holds for all sufficiently large \(L\).
This includes every fixed such dilation and an effective unbounded choice, the largest power of three at most \(j\).
For double contact and any schedule in this range the vanishing-cost equivalence of the [odd-block test](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#rate) persists, since its converse construction already uses seeds satisfying the quotient condition and its width requirement for each fixed seed is eventually met.

<a id="calibration"></a>
## Excluding small relations in residue fields

For each candidate \(2\le j\le K\) use the [width calibration and retained periods](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#width), imposing its two numerical tests for every \(g=3^a\le j\) on the candidate seed list.
For seeds satisfying the quotient condition add the following finite test.
In a splitting field of each such \(P\), take all pairs of distinct conjugates \(\alpha,\beta\) with \(\beta\ne\alpha^{-1}\).
For every allowed \(g\) and every integer pair \((a,b')\ne(0,0)\) of sup norm at most \(j^2\), take the rational primes dividing the absolute norm of \(\alpha^{ga}-\beta^{gb'}\).
Require \(\ell^e<2L\) for each prime on this list, using the degree of its seed.
The differences are algebraic integers because the roots are units.
Their nonvanishing follows from equality of the positive absolute logarithmic heights of the conjugates, since a power equality forces \(|a|=|b'|\), and then a torsion quotient of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) or of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta^{-1}\).
Reciprocity puts \(\beta^{-1}\) among the conjugates, so both alternatives are excluded by the choice of pair.
All tests are effective by finite enumeration, arithmetic in number fields and the root-of-unity test for algebraic numbers.
Take the largest passing \(j\), or one if none passes.
Every fixed candidate passes eventually by the [fixed-seed primitive norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) and finiteness of the added prime lists, so \(j(L)\to\infty\).
Graph tangencies give \(e\le j\), \(h\le\log2\) and the calibrated inequality \(Kh\ge ej\log2\), as in the [original width argument](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#width).

<a id="separation"></a>
## Exclusive primitive roots at one output order

Fix a retained period \(n\) and a prime \(\ell\nmid gn\).
Use all distinct residue roots \(x\) of \(P\) of exact order \(gn\) in an algebraic closure of the residue field, including every member of each Frobenius orbit.
Choose a single prime of the splitting field above \(\ell\) and identify its residue field with a subfield of that closure, so every such root lifts to a conjugate of \(P\).
Since \(x\) belongs to an extension of degree at most \(e\), its order satisfies \(gn\le\ell^e-1\), whereas \(n\ge2L\).
Therefore \(\ell\) divides none of the norms excluded for this seed.
For distinct \(x,y\) with \(y\ne x^{-1}\), the chosen lifts are distinct and noninverse, so
\[
 (x^g)^a=(y^g)^{b'}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (a,b')=(0,0)\quad\text{if }\max(|a|,|b'|)\le j^2.
\]
Both \(x^g\) and \(y^g\) generate the same cyclic group of order \(n\).
Their full relation lattice in \(\mathbb Z^2\) has index \(n\), since the homomorphism \((a,b')\mapsto(x^g)^a(y^g)^{-b'}\) is onto that group.

Let \(I\) be the coprime first exponents from the smaller block in the [representations of this period](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#blocks).
The [coprime count](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#width) gives \(|I|\ge c_0w\), and these exponents lie in an interval of length at most \(2w\) strictly between zero and \(n/2\), since the other block lies entirely above the first.
For each \(x\) the set \(B_x=\{(x^g)^p:p\in I\}\) has \(|I|\) primitive roots, and \(B_x\cap B_{x^{-1}}\) is empty because no two first exponents sum to \(n\).
For other pairs the intersection size is bounded by the number of relation lattice points in the square containing \(I\times I\).
If \(\lambda\) is the Euclidean length of a shortest nonzero lattice vector, then \(j^2<\lambda\ll\sqrt n\), the upper bound following by pigeonholing integer points of \([0,\lceil\sqrt n\rceil]^2\) modulo the lattice.
A shortest vector is primitive in the lattice, so it extends to a lattice basis and its parallel lattice lines have spacing \(n/\lambda\).
The square meets \(O(1+w\lambda/n)\) lines and contains \(O(1+w/\lambda)\) points on each, giving
\[
                   |B_x\cap B_y|\ll 1+w/j^2+w^2/n .
\]
There are at most \(e\le j\le K\) residue roots and \(w/n\ll K^{-3}\).
Thus the union of all intersections for one \(x\) costs \(O(j+w/j+jw^2/n)=o(w)\), uniformly in the seed, prime, dilation and period.
Each \(x\) has at least \(c_0w/2\) output roots belonging exclusively to its own image set.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Resultant transfer and its ceiling

Write \(W=uU+vV=cA\) with coprime integers \(u,v\) and positive content \(c\).
Use the notation \(D_{gn}\), \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_n,A)|\), \(T=\sum_n\varphi(n)\), and the unramified lifts \(\xi_x\) of the residue roots as in the [component argument](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#mass).
For \(t_x=v_\ell(P(\xi_x))\), every exclusive output contributes at least \((kt_x-v_\ell(c))_+\) by the opposite-pair inclusion, since a primitive output lifts uniquely to a root of unity of order \(n\).
For fixed \(\ell\) the roots counted over the different input orders are distinct, at most \(e\) in all, and their valuations sum to \(\sum_n v_\ell(D_{gn})\) by the component norm identity with every Frobenius conjugate counted separately.
Summing exclusive contributions at each order and then over primes gives
\[
 \sum_n\log R_n\ \ge\ (c_0w/2)\left(k\sum_n\log D_{gn}-e\log c\right).
\]
Here \(\log c\le 2\Sigma\log2+\log2\), whereas \(\sum_n\log D_{gn}\gg hgT\), \(T\gg Ks\), and \(Kh/e\gg j\).
Thus the content expense is at most an \(O(1/j)\) fraction of the positive term.
The [circle estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#circle) bounds the left side divided by \(T\) by \(m(A)+o(w/K)\), proving (1).

For arbitrary nonnegative output weights the [slot bound](Yin_C_G0345_v533.md#statement) also bounds the normalized primitive-norm credit when all allowed projected roots are credited with perfect separation.
Indeed each individual input root has at most \(m_{nt}\) images at output order \(t\), so its contribution before content and circle errors is at most \(k m_{nt}\) times its input valuation.
Summing these valuations over residue roots and primes and applying that bound without the global degree divisor gives at most
\[
                         kh+\frac{ker\log2}{\Sigma}
\]
for the credit divided by the total weighted output totient and by \(\Sigma\).
For the calibrated blocks \(er/\Sigma\le K/(gL)\), so at fixed multiplicity this credit tends to zero along every sequence with \(h\to0\).
This limits the normalized lower bound available from the primitive norms even under complete separation and leaves open other restrictions on the rows.
