# The relative unit lattice has large area and bounded division on the small real Chebyshev unit

2026-11-24T19:31 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Area, index and a remaining direction for small measures

Take the [Chebyshev pair and its radical closure](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#statement) with \(q\) sufficiently large, \(M=q(q+1)\), and even \(n\ge4\) such that \(4^{n-1}\le2M\).
Let \(U\) be the relative norm-one units of \(K/B\), \(H=\langle T,Y\rangle\), and \(I=[U:\{\pm1\}H]\).
Write \(D_H=|\det(\lambda(T),\lambda(Y))|\) for the area of the logarithm lattice of \(H\) in the [two embedding coordinates](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#fields), and \(D_U=D_H/I\).
With absolute constants,
\[
             D_U\gg\frac{n}{(\log(6n))^{212}},\qquad
             I\ll(\log M)^2(\log(6n))^{212}.                              \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(I/(n(\log M)^2)\) is bounded for all parameters and tends to zero along any subsequence with \(n\to\infty\).
This rules out the [sufficient index growth for a small unit](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#index).

For every nontorsion \(\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\) with \(\gamma^r\in\mu_\infty\langle T\rangle\) for some \(r\ge1\), one has, uniformly for large \(q\),
\[
                            m(\gamma)>\frac{\log M}{110}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(m\) is total logarithmic Mahler measure.
The index of the subgroup generated by \(T\) in its saturation inside \(U/\{\pm1\}\) is at most 109 for all sufficiently large \(q\).

For any \(u\in U\) multiplicatively independent of \(T\),
\[
             m(u)\gg\frac{n}{\log M\,(\log(6n))^{212}},
             \qquad I\,m(u)\ge\frac{2D_H}{3m(T)}
                         \sim\frac{n-1}{3}\log M.                         \tag{3}
\]
In particular a sequence in the full radical closure with positive measures tending to zero would require \(I/(n\log M)\to\infty\).
This necessary growth differs from the sufficient growth excluded by (1).

<a id="area"></a>
## A height product for two independent relative units

Write \(\ell(x,y)=|x|+2|y|\), and let \(l_1\le l_2\) be the successive minima of the lattice \(\lambda(U)\) for this norm.
The two real logarithms record the [complete sum over the embeddings of \(K\)](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#fields), including multiplicities.
In particular \(h(u)=\ell(\lambda(u))/(2n)\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height, even when \(u\) generates a proper subfield.
Choose independent lattice vectors attaining \(l_1,l_2\) and lifts \(u_1,u_2\in U\).
The lifts are multiplicatively independent.
By [Amoroso and David, Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001), with joint field degree \(D=[\mathbb Q(u_1,u_2):\mathbb Q]\le 2n\),
\[
        h(u_1) h(u_2)\ge\frac{c(2)}{D(\log(3D))^{212}}
                      \ge\frac{c(2)}{2n(\log(6n))^{212}} .
\]
The logarithmic exponent is \(2(3(3!)^2-2)=212\).
This use is at the inspected theorem-statement scope.
The unit ball of \(\ell\) has area one, so Minkowski's second theorem gives
\[
                     2D_U\le l_1l_2\le4D_U .
\]
Substituting the two heights yields the first bound in (1).
The [uniform asymptotic for the generated area](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#index) gives \(D_H\sim\tfrac12(n-1)(\log M)^2\), which proves the second bound.
For example, if the least positive measure in the full radical closure is \(\mu\), its [attainment in \(U\) and degree accounting](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#statement) give \(\mu\le l_1\le3\mu\).
Thus \(l_2\gg n/(\mu(\log(6n))^{212})\).
The height product allows one short direction if the independent direction is sufficiently long.

<a id="division"></a>
## Roots in the direction of \(T\)

First let \(u\in U\) be nontorsion, with \(u^r=\pm T^a\) for integers \(r>0,\ a\ne0\).
Put \(c=a/r\), so \(\lambda(u)=c\lambda(T)\).
By the [field degree formula](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#fields),
\[
                   |c|=\frac{[K:\mathbb Q(u)]m(u)}{m(T)}
                       \le\frac{3m(u)}{m(T)} .
\]
Suppose \(m(u)\le(\log M)/110\).
Since [\(m(T)\sim\log M\) and \(\log T=O(M^{-1/2})\)](Yin_C_G0902_v1.md#conjugates), this makes \(|c|<1\) for large \(q\) and \(|\log|u||=O(M^{-1/2})\).
The two real numbers \(u,u^{-1}\) are conjugate because \(\iota(u)=u^{-1}\).
Take the one \(\rho\) of modulus below one and put \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}\rho\).
Then \(0<|\epsilon-\rho|=1-\exp(-|\log|u||)=O(M^{-1/2})\).

The monic minimum \(P\) of \(u\) has integer coefficients and degree \(D\le2n\), with \(P(\rho)=0\) and \(P(\epsilon)\) a nonzero integer.
Each coefficient has modulus at most \(\|P\|_{\mathbb T}=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\) by integration on the circle.
Consequently on the unit disk \(|P'|\le D^2\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\).
Integrating on the real segment \([\rho,\epsilon]\) in either order gives
\[
                \log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\ge \tfrac12\log M-O(\log(2D)).
\]

In the [finite circle estimate for monic separable integer polynomials](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#statement) take \(j=\lceil D/24\rceil\), \(N=D+j\).
The estimate bounds \(\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\) by
\[
        \frac{N}{2j}\log N+\frac{N-1}{j}m(u)
                     +\frac1j\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\log{N-1\choose i}.
\]
Here \(N/j\le25\) and \(N\le2D\).
For \(1\le i\le j\), the binomial logarithm is at most \(i\log(\mathrm e N/i)\le j\log(25\mathrm e)\).
Thus
\[
       \log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}
       \le \tfrac{25}{2}\log(2D)+25m(u)+(D/24+1)\log(25\mathrm e).
\]
The degree condition gives \(D\le(\log M)/\log2+3\).
If the proposed inequality \(m(u)\le(\log M)/110\) held for arbitrarily large \(q\), the upper limit of \(\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}/\log M\) would therefore be at most
\[
                      \frac{25}{110}+\frac{\log(25\mathrm e)}{24\log2}
                         <0.49.
\]
The preceding lower bound gives a lower limit of at least \(1/2\).
This contradiction proves (2) in \(U\).
For an external radical \(\gamma\) of \(T\) up to torsion, the [relative norm descent](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#radicals) gives a nontorsion \(u\in U\) with a power in \(\langle T\rangle\) and \(m(u)\le m(\gamma)\).
This proves the whole assertion.

The intersection of the lattice \(\lambda(U)\) with the line through \(\lambda(T)\) is infinite cyclic.
For a generator \(u\) modulo torsion and its index \(j_T\), one has \(T=\pm u^{j_T}\) and hence \(\mathbb Q(u)=K\).
Therefore \(m(T)=j_Tm(u)\), and \(j_T<110m(T)/\log M<111\) eventually.
It is odd since \(T\) is [not a square modulo signs in \(U\)](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#parity).
Thus \(j_T\le109\).

<a id="independent"></a>
## Units independent of \(T\)

For \(u\) independent of \(T\) apply the same rank-two height product to \((u,T)\), whose joint field is \(K\).
Since \(h(T)=m(T)/(2n)\) and \([\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\ge2n/3\), it gives the first bound in (3).
Write \(u^I=\pm T^aY^b\), necessarily with \(b\ne0\).
Taking determinants with \(\lambda(T)\) yields
\[
       D_H\le |b|D_H= I|\det(\lambda(T),\lambda(u))|
              \le\tfrac I2\,m(T)\,\ell(\lambda(u))
              \le\tfrac{3I}{2}\,m(T)m(u),
\]
where \(2|\det(v,w)|\le\ell(v)\ell(w)\) by the triangle inequality.
This gives the second estimate in (3).
Any small positive measure in the radical closure descends to a positive measure at most as large in \(U\).
By (2) such a descended unit must be independent of \(T\) for large \(q\).
Then (3) gives the stated stronger necessary index growth, but does not exclude a single shrinking sequence in this independent direction.
