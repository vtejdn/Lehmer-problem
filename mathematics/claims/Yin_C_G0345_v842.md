# Short factors at two on a Frobenius subsequence of the quadratic diagonal

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed prime giving only the height cutoff

For the [two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) take \(q=2^a,\ k=q,\ l=q^2+1\) with \(a\ge1\), and let \(Q\) be the monic trace polynomial of degree \(n=(q+1)^2\).
The reduction of \(Q\) modulo two is squarefree and every irreducible factor has degree at most \(2a(q+1)\).
Except for factors of total degree \(O(q^{3/2})\), their degrees are multiples of \(a\).
For the [auxiliary-prime minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v838.md#statement) this gives
\[
               B\le b(2)<B+2a(q+1),\qquad b(2)/B\longrightarrow1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(a\), with \(B\asymp n(\log\log n/\log n)^3\).
The modular test at two therefore gives \(b(2)=o(n)\) on this entire subsequence irrespective of the rational minimum.
The integer-point signed-divisor tests used in the [diagonal census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0183/readme.md#modular-degrees) do not discard any additional modular divisor at two.

## Factorization over the Frobenius field

Write \(y=x^q,\ z=x^{q^2}\).
The [two graphs in characteristic two](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#reduction) show that the full section reduces to the product of
\[
 G(X)=(1+X^{2q})X^{q^2+1}+X^{2q}+X^q+1
\]
and its reciprocal.
On the first graph \(xz=w(y)\) with \(w(Y)=(Y^2+Y+1)/(Y^2+1)\).
The two graphs in the torus are disjoint since the two equations \(tV+t+1=(t+1)V+t=0\) would imply \(V=1\), an impossibility.
The roots of \(G\) are nonzero and simple since \(G'=(1+X^{2q})X^{q^2}\) and \(G(1)=1\).
Inversion interchanges the graphs, so \(x\mapsto x+x^{-1}\) gives a Frobenius-equivariant bijection from the roots of \(G\) to all roots of \(\bar Q\).

For \(t\in\mathbb F_q\) define
\[
 D_t(X,Y)=X^2Y^2+X^2+Y^2+X+Y+1+tXY,\qquad
 I(X,Y)=D_0(X,Y)/(XY).
\]
In characteristic two,
\[
 I(X,Y)+I(Y,Z)=\frac{X+Z}{XYZ}\bigl((Y^2+1)XZ+Y^2+Y+1\bigr).
\]
In particular \(I(x,y)^q=I(y,z)=I(x,y)\) on \(G\).
In the polynomial ring one has the exact identity
\[
            \prod_{t\in\mathbb F_q}D_t(X,X^q)=(X^{q^2-1}-1)G(X).
\]
Indeed the product equals \((X^{q+1})^q(I^q+I)\) under the substitution, and \((X^{q+1})^q=yz\).
The specialized \(D_t\)'s are monic of degree \(2(q+1)\), have constant coefficient one and are pairwise coprime because their differences are nonzero multiples of \(X^{q+1}\).
Dividing each by its monic gcd with \(X^{q^2-1}-1\) therefore gives monic factors \(G_t\) with product \(G\).
This also holds at points common to the two polynomials on the right, with one copy removed from the relevant \(D_t\).
The two-power Frobenius permutes these factors by \(t\mapsto t^2\).
Each irreducible degree over \(\mathbb F_2\) is at most \(2a(q+1)\), and is divisible by the degree of its label \(t\).
The labels of degree less than \(a\) belong to the union of all fields of orders \(2^j,\ j\le a/2\), whose total size is \(O(\sqrt q)\).
The degree bound per label gives the claimed exception.

Taking successive distinct irreducible factors until their total degree first reaches \(B\) overshoots it by less than the largest allowed degree, and the resulting sum is eventually at most \(n/2\).
Also \(a(q+1)=o(B)\), proving the limit.
At any integer \(s\) with \(Q(s)\) odd, every modular divisor has value one modulo two, which is the residue of a signed divisor of \(Q(s)\).
For nonzero even \(Q(s)\) both residues are permitted.

## Exact degrees at the labels zero and one

Put \(S_a=\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_q}(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(u^3)}\).
Then \(\deg G_0=q+1+S_a\), where \(S_a=0\) for odd \(a\) and \(S_a=(-1)^{a/2+1}2^{a/2+1}\) for even \(a\).
Every root of \(G_0\) has degree exactly three over \(\mathbb F_q\).
For odd \(a\), \(G_1\) has degree \(2(q+1)\) and all its roots have degree six over \(\mathbb F_q\).
For even \(a\), \(G_1=1\).

To count the roots removed from \(D_0(X,X^q)\), take \(x\in\mathbb F_{q^2}^{\times}\), \(s=x+x^q\) and \(v=xx^q\).
The pencil equation becomes \(v^2+s^2+s+1=0\).
When \(s=0\) the only solution is \(x=1\).
For \(s\ne0\), \(v=s+\sqrt{s}+1\), and the polynomial \(T^2+sT+v\) supplies two conjugate roots of the required trace and norm exactly when \(\operatorname{Tr}(v/s^2)=1\).
Indeed the image of \(u\mapsto u^2+u\) on \(\mathbb F_q\) is the trace-zero subspace by dimension.
Since the trace is unchanged by squaring, \(\operatorname{Tr}(v/s^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(s^{-3})\).
The number removed is \(1+q-S_a\), giving the degree.
The cube map is bijective when \(a\) is odd.
For even \(a\), square the character sum and group the pairs by their difference \(v\).
The inner sum of \((-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(v u^2+v^2u)}\) over \(u\) is zero unless \(v^{q/2}=v^2\), equivalently \(v^4=v\).
For each of the four such \(v\), \(\operatorname{Tr}(v^3)=0\), so \(S_a^2=4q\).
The nonzero cubes have three preimages each, giving \(S_a\equiv1\pmod3\) and fixing the sign.
For \(x\) on \(G_0\), factorization of the numerator of \(Xw(X)-Yw(Y)\) gives \((X-Y)D_0(X,Y)\).
Hence \(xw(x)=yw(y)=xyz\in\mathbb F_q^\times\).
This implies \(x^{q^3-1}=1\), whereas \(x\in\mathbb F_q\) would give \(D_0(x,x)=x^4+1=0\) and \(x=1\), excluded from \(G\).

For \(t=1\) the specialized equation is \(T^2+T+1=0\) with \(T=(X+1)^{q+1}\).
Its \(2(q+1)\) roots are simple.
For even \(a\), both primitive cube roots lie in \(\mathbb F_q\), and the norm map from \(\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\) supplies \(q+1\) preimages each.
They are all removed in forming \(G_1\).
For odd \(a\), neither cube root lies in \(\mathbb F_q\), so no root is removed.
Each \(x+1\) then has order dividing \(3(q+1)\), which divides \(q^6-1\).
Such an \(x\) cannot lie in \(\mathbb F_{q^3}\), which contains no primitive cube root, or in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), where \(T\) would belong to \(\mathbb F_q\).
Its degree over \(\mathbb F_q\) is consequently six.

## Obstruction to a fixed rational invariant at nineteen and other odd primes

Fix an odd prime \(p\ne5\) and now set \(q=p^a\).
For every fixed nonconstant rational function \(J(X,Y)\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), at most \(O_{J,p}(q)\) distinct torus roots \(x\) of \(P_{q,q^2+1}\bmod p\) have \(J(x,x^q)\) defined and in \(\mathbb F_q\).
The same bound holds for \(J(x,x^q)^q=\psi(J(x,x^q))\) with a fixed nonconstant rational function \(\psi\), with the constant also depending on \(\psi\).
In particular the fixed rational labeling that covers all roots at two cannot cover a positive fraction at nineteen by the same mechanism.

Write \(F(Y,V)/Y^2=A_0V^2+V+B_0\), where \(A_0=(t-3)(t-4),\ B_0=(t+3)(t+4)\) and \(t=Y-Y^{-1}\).
This quadratic is [irreducible over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Y)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#reduction), and remains so after adjoining an independent variable \(X\) and setting \(V=XZ\).
For its roots \(w_1,w_2\) in \(V\), their ratio has infinite multiplicative order since \((w_1+w_2)^2/(w_1w_2)=1/(A_0B_0)\) is nonconstant.
Let \(J^{(q)}\) apply the \(q\)-power map to coefficients only.
If \(J^{(q)}(Y,Z)=\psi(J(X,Y))\) identically on this quadratic, it holds at both \(Z=w_i/X\).
Over the algebraic closure of the coefficient field \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Y)\), the rational function \(J^{(q)}(Y,Z)\) in \(Z\) would then be invariant under multiplication by \(w_1/w_2\).
Its zeros and poles can occur only at zero and infinity, since all other orbits under this multiplication are infinite.
A nonzero monomial can be invariant only with exponent zero.
Thus \(J\) is independent of its second variable, leaving \(J^{(q)}(Y)=\psi(J(X))\), impossible for independent \(X,Y\).
The nonconstant function of one variable used in this last expression is the same specialization of \(J\) to its first argument.

After clearing denominators, the resultant in \(Z\) of the two equations is therefore a nonzero polynomial in \(X,Y\).
The denominators are nonzero at the generic point since \(X,Y\) are independent and \(w_i/X\) is transcendental over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p(Y)\).
Only finitely many coefficient twists \(J^{(q)}\) occur.
For any resulting polynomial \(E\), the distinct monomials of \(E(X,X^q)\) have distinct degrees once \(q>\deg_X E\).
This univariate polynomial is nonzero of degree \(O_{J,\psi,p}(q)\) and vanishes on all the stated roots, proving the bound.
The [degree-sixteen derivative bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) ensures that there are \(2(q+1)^2-O(1)\) distinct geometric section roots in total.
The invariant obstruction gives no lower bound on a rational factor degree at nineteen.
