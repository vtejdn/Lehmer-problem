# A lower bound for positive logarithmic Mahler measure in terms of the number of monomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## An inverse polynomial bound independent of the degree

There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that every nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(P\), in any finite number of variables, satisfies
\[
        m(P)=0\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
        m(P)\ \ge\ \frac{c}{k^4\log(2k)},                              \tag{1}
\]
where \(k\ge1\) counts its distinct nonzero monomials and \(m=\log M\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
There is no restriction on the exponents, coefficient sizes, irreducible factors or cyclotomic multiplicities.
The constant is not optimized.
The bound tends to zero as \(k\) increases.
[Dobrowolski's Theorem 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#sparse-measure) gives an earlier explicit support bound allowing arbitrary cyclotomic factors; Proposition 1 in that source treats the support of a monic irreducible polynomial itself.

The modular construction uses the [specialization in a large cyclic group](Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#statement).
Its essential feature here is that the reverse error is linear in the ratio of the vertical degree to the group order even when the vertical degree grows.
The arithmetic estimate uses the [relative height theorem of Amoroso and Delsinne](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base).
The two estimates are compared at a prime whose logarithm is of order \(k\log(2k)\).

<a id="fiber"></a>
## Summing relative heights over a horizontal component

For \(D\ge1\) put
\[
              b(D)=\frac{(\log\log(5D))^3}{(\log(2D))^4}.
\]
For every finite abelian extension \(K/\mathbb Q\), nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(\beta\), and \(\delta=[K(\beta):K]\), the stated unconditional relative height theorem gives \(\delta h(\beta)\ge c_1 b(\delta)\) with an absolute \(c_1>0\).
For the rational base take the fixed quadratic field \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) in that theorem and its abelian extension \(K K_0/K_0\).
The resulting relative degree \(\delta'=[K K_0(\beta):K K_0]\) lies in \([\delta/2,\delta]\), since \([K K_0:K]\le2\).
The fixed field constant and the comparable logarithmic expressions give the displayed bound with no special convention for the discriminant of \(\mathbb Q\).
It follows that any integral two-variable Laurent polynomial \(F\) of positive width \(R\) in \(Y\), having a geometric component depending on \(Y\) which is not a torsion coset, satisfies
\[
                         m_2(F)\ge c_2 b(R).                          \tag{2}
\]
Indeed use the [torsion-grid and finite-place arguments](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#grid) and the [exclusion of torsion projections](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal).
For all sufficiently large prime orders the primitive base grid avoids the projections excluded there and the zeros of both end coefficients.
At a fixed primitive base point take \(K\) to be its cyclotomic field and take a nontorsion root \(\beta\) on the selected component.
Its full orbit over this finite \(K\) supplies \(\delta\le R\) distinct roots in that fiber with equal absolute heights.
The sum of their heights is at least \(c_1 b(\delta)\).
The conjugate-grid mean of the archimedean logarithmic fiber measures dominates this sum by the finite-place Gauss norm and product formula in the cited argument, also for nonmonic \(F\).
Its upper limit is at most \(m_2(F)\).
Finally \(\min_{1\le t\le R} b(t)\gg b(R)\): \(b\) is positive and continuous on \([1,\infty)\), eventually decreasing to zero, as follows by logarithmic differentiation.
This proves (2).

If there is no such component, the same fixed factorization writes \(F\), up to a Laurent unit, as an integral base polynomial \(G(X)\) times factors \(\Phi_e(X^aY^j)\) with \(j>0\).
In particular its top \(Y\)-coefficient is \(G\) up to a signed monomial.

<a id="compression"></a>
## Putting all exponents in a short interval of residues

First let \(P\) be univariate with \(k\ge2\).
Multiplying by a monomial and then dividing all exponent differences by their greatest common divisor preserve \(k\) and \(m(P)\), by invariance of circle measure under power maps.
We may therefore write the exponents as \(0,e_2,\ldots,e_k\) with greatest common divisor one.
For an integer \(B\ge5\), choose a prime \(p\) with \(B^{k-1}<p\le 2B^{k-1}\) by Bertrand's postulate.
Consider the \(p\) vectors of least nonnegative residues
\[
       (te_2,\ldots,te_k)\pmod p,\qquad 0\le t<p .
\]
Partition \([0,p)^{k-1}\) into \(B^{k-1}\) boxes of side \(p/B\).
Taking the difference of two parameter values in one box yields an invertible \(t\bmod p\) with all the residues \(t e_i\) represented by integers \(y_i\) of absolute value less than \(p/B\), with \(y_1=0\).
They cannot all be zero since \(p\) does not divide the greatest common divisor of the exponents.
Let \(N\) represent \(t^{-1}\bmod p\) and write \(e_i=p x_i+Ny_i\), \(x_i\in\mathbb Z\).
Place each original coefficient at \((x_i,y_i)\) in a Laurent polynomial \(F\), shifting the minimum \(Y\)-exponent to zero.
Then \(F(z^p,z^N)\) is \(P(z)\) up to a monomial and has exactly the same \(k\) coefficients.
There are at least two occupied rows and
\[
                    1\le R=\max y_i-\min y_i<2p/B<p.                \tag{3}
\]
No estimate on the \(X\)-exponents is needed.
Apply the lower side of the [cyclic specialization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#statement) at \(H=p\).
It gives, with an absolute constant,
\[
                 m(P)\ \ge\ m_2(F)- C(R/p)k\log(2p)
                        \ \ge\ m_2(F)-2Ck\log(2p)/B .                \tag{4}
\]

<a id="induction"></a>
## Paying for the specialization or decreasing the support exactly

Fix a sufficiently large absolute \(D_0\ge2\) and take \(B=\lceil(D_0 k)^8\rceil\).
If there is a nontorsion horizontal component, (2) gives \(m_2(F)\gg b(p)\), since \(R<p\).
In this choice of prime \(\log(2p)\asymp k\log(D_0 k)\) with absolute comparison constants.
In particular \(b(p)\gg [k\log(D_0 k)]^{-4}\), using only boundedness below of \(\log\log(5p)\).
The error term in (4), divided by this last bound, is
\[
                 \ll D_0^{-8} k^{-2}\bigl(\log(D_0 k)\bigr)^5.
\]
This tends to zero as \(D_0\to\infty\), uniformly for \(k\ge2\), by bounding \((\log k)^5/k^2\) on that range.
Choose \(D_0\) once to absorb the error into half the lower bound in (2).
Now \(\log\log(5p)\gg\log(2k)\), and \(\log(D_0 k)\ll_{D_0}\log(2k)\).
Retaining the numerator gives \(b(p)\gg_{D_0}1/[k^4\log(2k)]\) and proves (1) in this case.

In the other case take the top \(Y\)-coefficient \(A_R(X)\).
Every horizontal cyclotomic factor has \(Y\)-width at most \(R\) since widths add in a Laurent polynomial domain.
Its primitive character has \(0<j\le R<p\), so \(pa+Nj\ne0\) as \(\gcd(p,N)=1\).
On specialization the factor \(\Phi_e(z^{pa+Nj})\) therefore has measure zero.
The top coefficient is \(G\) up to a Laurent unit and has strictly fewer than \(k\) monomials because another \(Y\)-row is occupied.
Thus
\[
                             m(A_R)=m(G)=m(P)
\]
exactly, including any integral content or cyclotomic multiplicities.
Apply induction on \(k\), noting that the bound in (1) decreases with \(k\).
A single integral monomial with positive measure has measure at least \(\log2\), giving the base case after reducing the constant.
This proves the full univariate assertion.

<a id="variables"></a>
## Any finite number of variables

For completeness let \(P\) have \(h\ge2\) variables and substitute \((z,z^N,\ldots,z^{N^{h-1}})\).
For each fixed nonzero integer frequency vector its specialized frequency is a nonzero polynomial in \(N\), so eventually does not vanish.
The resulting Haar pushforwards converge to full Haar measure against every continuous function by Fourier approximation.
The specialized polynomial \(P_N\) has exactly the same \(k\) nonzero integral coefficients for all sufficiently large \(N\).
For \(k\ge2\) its supremum is at least one, and the [measurable-set exponential polynomial inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) gives \(\Pr(|P_N|\le v)\le C v^{1/(k-1)}\), uniformly in such \(N\) for \(0<v<1\).
Consequently truncating \(\log|P_N|\) below at \(-T\) changes its integral by at most \(C(k-1)e^{-T/(k-1)}\).
The clipped logarithm of \(P\) is continuous on the torus including its zeros.
Passing to the limit first for clipped logarithms and then letting \(T\to\infty\) by monotone convergence from above proves \(m(P_N)\to m(P)\).
If \(m(P)>0\), the specialized measures are eventually positive and obey (1) with the same \(k\).
The monomial case is immediate.
