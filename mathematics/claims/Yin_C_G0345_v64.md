# A Mahler gap outside the Salem case for reciprocal Perron units with five conjugate moduli

<a id="statement"></a>
## The measure and the reduction in exterior conjugates

Let \(\theta>1\) be a real algebraic unit with reciprocal minimal polynomial \(P\), strictly larger than the modulus of each other conjugate.
Suppose every conjugate has modulus in \(\{\theta^{-1},\theta^{-1/2},1,\theta^{1/2},\theta\}\), and put \(d=\deg P\).
If \(\theta\) is neither Salem nor a reciprocal quadratic unit, there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
            \log M(\theta)\ \ge\
                 c\,\frac{d^{1/4}}{(\log(3d))^{106}} .
\]
In particular the logarithmic Mahler measure tends to infinity with \(d\) in this case, and there is a uniform positive gap.
The existence of a positive Mahler gap on the full class in the hypothesis is therefore equivalent to the existence of a gap for Salem numbers.

There is also a construction in the group generated by the actual conjugates.
Their images modulo roots of unity form isomorphic orthogonal components of one of the types below.
Write \(t\) for the number of components.

* In type \(A_1\) the original unit is Salem or reciprocal quadratic.
* In type \(A_{N-1}\), \(N\ge3\), there is a unit of degree at most \(2Nt\) with precisely two nonreal exterior conjugates and measure \(\theta^N=M(\theta)^{N/(N-1)}\).
* In type \(D_n\), \(n\ge5\), there is a unit of degree at most \(2nt\) with precisely two nonreal exterior conjugates and measure \(\theta^2=M(\theta)^{2/(2n-3)}\).
* In type \(D_4\) a unit of degree at most \(24t\) has between two and six exterior conjugates, all of modulus \(\theta\), and measure at most \(M(\theta)^{6/5}\).
* In types \(E_6,E_7,E_8\) the original unit has at most 57 exterior conjugates.

Thus the construction always supplies a nontorsion unit with at most 57 exterior conjugates and logarithmic measure at most \(3\log M(\theta)/2\).

<a id="lift"></a>
## Root reflections and exact multiplicative identities

Let \(\Gamma\) be the multiplicative group generated by the conjugates, let \(\Lambda=\Gamma/\Gamma_{\rm tors}\), and denote by \(a\) the image of \(\theta\) in its real span.
The Galois orbit \(R\) of \(a\) has \(d\) elements.
Indeed, if the quotient of two distinct conjugates were torsion, sending one to \(\theta\) would give another conjugate of the same modulus.
For \(b\in R\) write \(x_b\) for its unique actual conjugate.
Reciprocity gives \(x_{-b}=x_b^{-1}\).
Choose a Galois-invariant Euclidean inner product on the span by averaging and scale it so that each root has squared length two.

Ordinary complex conjugation \(c\) satisfies \(x_b c(x_b)=\theta^{k_b}\) as actual algebraic numbers, with \(k_b\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}\) by the prescribed moduli.
Hence \(c(b)=-b+k_ba\) and \(c(a)=a\).
Taking scalar products with \(a\) gives \(k_b=(a,b)\).
Thus \(-c\) acts as the root reflection \(s_a\), and its conjugates give the reflections at all of \(R\).
They preserve \(R\) because negation preserves it.
This is a finite spanning root system, reduced since every root has squared length two.
All scalar products are integral by transitivity.
The [normalized simple-root and classification theorems](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#normalized-root-conventions) now give \(A,D,E\) components, since every bond joining simple roots has scalar product \(-1\).
All components are isomorphic by transitivity on \(R\).

Agent-S observed that the reflection identities also lift all integral root coordinates without a torsion factor.
For \(u=g a\) put \(c_u=g c g^{-1}\).
Applying \(g\) to the complex-conjugation identity at \(g^{-1}b\) gives \(x_b c_u(x_b)=x_u^{(u,b)}\).
As the actual conjugate \(c_u(x_b)^{-1}\) has class \(s_u b\), uniqueness gives
\[
                     x_{s_u b}=x_b x_u^{-(u,b)} .
\]
Map each simple root to its actual conjugate and extend multiplicatively on their free integral span.
The simple roots are linearly independent, and each reflection in a simple root preserves their integral span.
Every root is a product of simple reflections applied to a simple root by the [simple-root theorem](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#normalized-root-conventions).
The displayed identity shows along this word that the resulting monomial is exactly \(x_b\).
Hence \(\Gamma\) is generated by the simple-root values, whose independence modulo torsion makes this mapping an isomorphism from the root lattice onto \(\Gamma\).
It is Galois-equivariant since it is so on all roots.
For every lattice vector \(w\) its corresponding unit therefore satisfies
\[
                         \log|x_w|=\tfrac12(a,w)\log\theta .
\]

<a id="coordinates"></a>
## Units in the classical coordinate lattices

Use the [normalized classical models](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#finite-classification), with \(a=e_1-e_2\) in its own component.
Other components are orthogonal to \(a\), so their vectors always give units of modulus one in the chosen embedding.
In \(A_1\) only \(a\) is exterior and its inverse is the only interior conjugate, giving the first assertion.

In \(A_{N-1}\) the roots are \(e_i-e_j\), \(i\ne j\).
Exactly \(2(N-2)\) have pairing one with \(a\), and \(a\) alone has pairing two, giving \(M(\theta)=\theta^{N-1}\).
Use the integral root-lattice vector \(w_i=Ne_i-\sum_j e_j=\sum_{j\ne i}(e_i-e_j)\).
The collection \(\{\pm w_i\}\) is intrinsic, since its members divided by \(N\) are the shortest nonzero vectors in the dual root lattice.
Indeed the dual consists of real vectors of coordinate sum zero whose coordinate differences are integers.
If \(k\) coordinates of a nonzero such vector \(v\) attain the minimum, then \(N\|v\|^2=\sum_{i<j}(v_i-v_j)^2\ge k(N-k)\ge N-1\).
Equality requires a single exceptional coordinate differing by one from all the others, which gives precisely the stated vectors.
The orbit of \(w_1\) thus has size at most \(2Nt\).
Only \(w_1\) and \(-w_2\) among all these vectors have positive pairing with \(a\), each equal to \(N\).
Complex conjugation acts as \(-s_a\) and exchanges these distinct vectors, so both actual conjugates occur and are nonreal.

In \(D_n\) the roots are the vectors \(\pm e_i\pm e_j\) for \(i<j\).
There are \(4(n-2)\) roots of pairing one with \(a\) and one of pairing two, giving \(M(\theta)=\theta^{2n-3}\).
Take \(w=2e_1=(e_1+e_2)+(e_1-e_2)\).
All vectors of squared length four in this integral lattice have the form \(\pm2e_i\) or have four nonzero coordinates each equal to \(\pm1\).
For the former, \(2(n-1)\) roots have pairing two with the vector, while for the latter precisely six do.
When \(n\ge5\) this distinguishes \(\{\pm2e_i\}\) intrinsically and bounds the degree by \(2nt\).
Exactly \(2e_1\) and \(-2e_2\) have positive pairing, each equal to two, and they are exchanged by complex conjugation.
For \(n=4\) retain both kinds, at most 24 vectors in each component.
The four additional positive pairings arise for four-coordinate vectors with signs \(+,-\) in the first two coordinates.
Every positive pairing is still two, proving the \(D_4\) assertions without fixing a coordinate frame under triality.

<a id="exceptional"></a>
## The maximum count in the exceptional components

One can embed the three exceptional root systems in the norm-two vectors of the lattice in \(\mathbb R^8\) consisting of the integer or half-integer coordinate vectors of even coordinate sum, with all coordinates of the same kind.
This additive lattice is integral, as follows by writing it as the union of the even-sum integer lattice and its translate by \((1/2,\ldots,1/2)\).
Reflection in any norm-two lattice vector therefore preserves it.
Explicit simple vectors for the embedding are
\[
 \tfrac12(e_1+e_8-e_2-e_3-e_4-e_5-e_6-e_7),\quad
 e_1+e_2,\quad e_2-e_1,\quad e_3-e_2,\quad\ldots,\quad e_7-e_6.
\]
Their first six, seven and eight vectors have the respective \(E_6,E_7,E_8\) simple Gram diagrams, by direct scalar products and the [classification table](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#finite-classification).
Every root is obtained by simple reflections, so all images have norm two in the displayed lattice.
Within each connected component all roots are Weyl-conjugate, since simple roots joined by a single edge are conjugate by their two simple reflections and every root is conjugate to a simple root.
We can thus perform the count at \(a=e_1-e_2\), the negative of the third simple root.
The integer norm-two vectors are the signed two-coordinate vectors, of which 24 have pairing one with \(a\).
The half-integer norm-two vectors have all coordinates equal to \(\pm1/2\) with an even number of minus signs.
Exactly 32 have pairing one, since the first two signs must be \(+,-\) and the remaining six have prescribed parity.
Only \(a\) has pairing two, so there are at most 57 exterior roots in the original orbit.

<a id="gap"></a>
## Two adjacent roots and their joint field degree

In each non-\(A_1\) case choose roots \(a,b\) with \((a,b)=-1\).
These vectors are linearly independent, so their actual units are multiplicatively independent.
Let \(k\) count roots pairing to \(-1\) with \(a\).
For any image of \(a\), only \(k\) images of \(b\) can accompany it, because Galois automorphisms preserve scalar products.
Thus \(D=[\mathbb Q(x_a,x_b):\mathbb Q]\le dk\), by counting the orbit of the ordered pair of actual conjugates.

In \(A_{N-1}\) one has \(k=2(N-2)\) and \(d\ge N(N-1)\).
In \(D_n\) one has \(k=4(n-2)\) and \(d\ge2n(n-1)\).
In the exceptional components \(k\le56\) by the previous count and negation.
Hence \(D\le56d^{3/2}\) in all cases, independently of the number of components.
The fixed two-coordinate instance of [Amoroso and David's Theorem 1.6](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
             h(x_a)h(x_b)\ \ge\ \frac{c_2}{D(\log(3D))^{212}},
                \qquad c_2>0 ,
\]
since \(\kappa(2)=3(3!)^2-2=106\).
The two absolute logarithmic heights both equal \(\log M(\theta)/d\).
Taking square roots and using \(D\le56d^{3/2}\) gives the claimed estimate after an absolute adjustment to the constant.
It tends to infinity with \(d\); at bounded degree there are only finitely many monic integer polynomials of measure at most two by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
In \(A_1\), a reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one has trace at least three, and all Salem numbers satisfy the original modulus hypothesis.
These facts give the stated equivalence.
