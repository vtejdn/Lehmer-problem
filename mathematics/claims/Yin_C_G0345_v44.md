# A lower bound for the fraction of empty intervals in a partial interlacing

<a id="statement"></a>
## The distribution of available interval lengths

Let \(P_i\) be monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomials of degree \(2m_i\) with \(M(P_i)>1\) tending to one, and let the real symmetric Laurent polynomials \(f_i=A_i(z+z^{-1})\) have degrees \([-k_i,k_i]\), bounded integer coefficients and bounded term counts.
Assume \(k_i\to\infty\) and \(k_i/m_i\to r\) for \(0<r<\infty\).
Use any subsequence on which the coefficients, limiting frequency ratios and eventual character relations stabilize as in the [common phase limit](Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#phases).
Write \(G,\mu,F,\phi_t\) for its compact torus, Haar probability, Laurent polynomial and limiting time flow, where time corresponds to angle multiplied by \(k_i\).
Assume \(f_i\) has at least one circle zero.
Let \(E_i\) be the number of open arcs between successive distinct circle zeros of \(f_i\) containing no circle root of \(P_i\).
At a typical phase \(X\in G\), let \(\ell(X)\) be the distance in time between the two real zeros of \(F(X\phi_t)\) bracketing zero, assigning infinity if either side has no zero.
Then
\[
         \liminf_i \frac{E_i}{2k_i}
             \ \ge\ \int_G \left(\frac{\pi}{\ell(X)}-\frac1r\right)_+\,d\mu(X),
              \qquad \frac1\infty=0.
\]
In particular \(E_i=o(k_i)\) requires every finite bracketing length to be at least \(\pi r\) almost surely.
This permits arbitrarily many unused roots of the target.

<a id="count"></a>
## Comparing interval counts with full Galois orbits

The [stability of distinct zeros](Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#regular) holds on every fixed compact time window at almost every starting phase.
In particular if a phase has a zero-free forward or backward half-line, along any approaching sequence of actual angular origins its rescaled bracketing lengths tend to infinity, by testing successively larger compact intervals on that side.
If both neighbors are finite the actual lengths converge to \(\ell(X)\).
Boundary points can be assigned value zero, since \(F(X)\ne0\) almost surely.
Thus for \(h\) continuous with compact support in \((0,\infty)\), both \(h(\ell_i)\) and \(h(\ell_i)/\ell_i\), extended by zero outside that support, have the stability needed for the uniform and target phase limits.
Here \(\ell_i=k_i|I|\) in an arc \(I\).
These [two phase limits](Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#phases) require only bounded \(k_i/m_i\) and the stated height and frequency assumptions.

Take \(0\le h\le1\).
The sum of \(h(\ell_i)\) over all arcs, divided by \(2k_i\), equals \(\pi\) times the uniform angular expectation of \(h(\ell_i)/\ell_i\).
For the occupied arcs the same normalized sum is at most \(m_i/k_i\) times the target angular expectation of \(h(\ell_i)\), since each occupied arc supplies at least one distinct root.
Any other conjugates counted by their arguments can only increase that expectation.
The unnormalized sum over empty arcs is at most \(E_i\).
Passing to the limits gives the lower bound by \(\int h(\ell)(\pi/\ell-1/r)\,d\mu\).
Taking the supremum over the compactly supported tests proves the positive-part formulation by approximation from below on \((0,\pi r)\).

<a id="collisions"></a>
## An obstruction from two transverse factors

Suppose \(F\) has two factors on \(G\) represented by real-valued trigonometric polynomials \(F_1,F_2\), possibly after multiplication by nonvanishing local phases, so zeros of either are zeros of \(F\).
Assume there is an \(X_0\in G\) where they both vanish, their real gradients in local coordinates on \(G\) are linearly independent, and both derivatives along \(\phi_t\) are nonzero.
Then the integral in the bound is strictly positive for every \(r>0\).
Indeed the two zeros continue as smooth hitting times \(t_1(X),t_2(X)\) near zero by the implicit function theorem.
Their gradients are the gradients of the two factors divided by the negatives of their respective time derivatives.
Thus for points near \(X_0\) the two times can be distinct and arbitrarily close.
Translate one such point by the flow to place the two zeros on opposite sides of zero, at distance apart strictly less than \(\pi r\).
This strict bracketing persists on an open set, and any additional zeros only shorten the bracketing interval at regular origins.
An open set has positive Haar measure, proving the obstruction.

For example suppose a Salem trace polynomial admits a strict interlacer containing both \(D_{a_i}(x)\) and \(D_{b_i}(x)\) as a product of factors, with \(D_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\).
The factors must have disjoint simple roots.
If both degrees have positive limiting proportions of \(m_i\), and the ratio \(a_i/b_i\) is not eventually constant on any subsequence, these conditions exclude a Salem sequence tending to one.
Indeed take \(k_i=a_i+b_i\), using the product alone as the template.
No nontrivial fixed integer relation between the exponents \(a_i,b_i\) holds infinitely often, so the two coordinate phases have joint Haar limit on \(\mathbb T^2\).
The normalized directions \(a_i/k_i,b_i/k_i\) are both positive after extraction.
Here \(F=(z+z^{-1})(w+w^{-1})\) has the transverse collision at \(z=w=i\).
One may take \(a_i,b_i\) as the two independent character coordinates even though the actual product expansion displays their sums and differences: the product-angle coordinates are a finite quotient of this torus, and Haar measure and the realized bracketing lengths push forward to the same values.
Equivalently the phase and stability proof works directly in the two displayed coordinates.
Strict interlacing of the whole interlacer makes each internal gap of its selected product contain a target trace, which supplies a circle root in each lifted internal arc; the two seam arcs cost at most two.
This contradicts the positive fraction of empty arcs.
