# Individual factors of integer polynomials of length at most five

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform gap for moving exponents

There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that every irreducible integral factor \(A\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measure of a polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(0<\|P\|_1\le5\) satisfies \(m(A)\ge c\).
Here \(\|P\|_1\) is the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients.
The bound allows arbitrary exponents, positive-measure cofactors and arbitrarily many exterior conjugates of \(A\).

More generally, fix \(L\ge2\), an integer \(k\ge0\), and \(\delta>0\).
Remove any common monomial from a nonzero integral polynomial \(P\) and let \(D\) denote the resulting degree.
Suppose either orientation of \(P\), obtained by optionally replacing it with \(X^DP(1/X)\), admits the representation
\[
 P=X^u B+H,\qquad
 B=b\prod_{j=1}^{q}(X^{v_j}-\epsilon_j),\qquad
 g=u-\deg H\ge\delta D>0,                                      \tag{1}
\]
where \(H\ne0\), \(\|H\|_1\le L\), \(b\) is a nonzero integer, \(0\le q\le k\), \(v_j\ge1\), and \(\epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}\).
Then \(m(A)\ge c(L,k,\delta)>0\) for each positive-measure irreducible factor of the original polynomial.
The exponents in \(H\) and all of \(u,v_1,\ldots,v_q\) may vary independently within (1).

<a id="split"></a>
## The separating gap and the norm of the endpoint

A nonunit constant irreducible factor, or an irreducible factor with an endpoint coefficient of absolute value at least two, has measure at least \(\log 2\) by the root formula and reciprocity of Mahler measure.
The monomial \(X\) has zero measure.
For a monic irreducible nonreciprocal unit the [theorem of Smyth](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives a positive absolute gap.
We may therefore take \(A\) monic, reciprocal and nontorsion with unit constant term, so it divides both orientations of \(P\).
Write \(d=\deg A\), fix a root \(\alpha\), and let \(\gamma_i\), \(1\le i\le d\), run through all of its conjugates.
For a unit \(\xi\), its absolute logarithmic height \(h(\xi)\) is the mean of the positive parts of the logarithmic conjugate moduli.
In particular \(m(A)=d h(\alpha)\) and \(h(\alpha^v)=v h(\alpha)\).

In (1) put \(B_0=B/b\) and \(C=\max(2^k,L,2)\).
For \(|\gamma_i|>1\), evaluation of (1) gives
\[
       |B_0(\gamma_i)|\le L e^{-x_i},\qquad
       x_i=g\log^+|\gamma_i|.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For the other conjugates \(|B_0(\gamma_i)|\le 2^k\) and \(x_i=0\).
Every factor of \(B_0\) has only roots of unity, so its value at \(\alpha\) is a nonzero algebraic integer, also for the empty product.
Taking its absolute norm gives \(1\le C^d\exp(-\sum_i x_i)\), hence
\[
                         g h(\alpha)\le\log C.                       \tag{3}
\]

<a id="phases"></a>
## Concentration of the arguments of a power

Let \(v\ge1\) and \(y=v/g\).
For \(0<y<y_0(k,L)\), every arc of angular half-width \(0<w\le\pi\) contains a fraction at most
\[
                           O_{k,L}(w+y^{1/16})                       \tag{4}
\]
of the arguments of the \(d\) numbers \(\gamma_i^v\).
To prove this, write \(\beta=\alpha^v\), \(e=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), \(h=h(\beta)\le y\log C\) by (3), and denote the arguments of its \(e\) conjugates by \(\theta_j\).
Each occurs with the same embedding multiplicity \(d/e\) in the original list.
The [quadratic logarithmic bound for nontorsion units](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(e\sum_j(\log|\beta_j|)^2\ge1/10\).
As \(\sum_j|\log|\beta_j||=2eh\), this implies \(e\gg h^{-2/3}\gg_{k,L} y^{-2/3}\).
In particular \(\log(2e)/e\ll_{k,L} y^{1/3}\), using \(\log(2e)\ll\sqrt e\).

The [reflected discriminant estimate (3) and its phase comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), applied to the separable minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), give for \(1\le \ell\le e\)
\[
 \left|\frac1e\sum_j e^{i\ell\theta_j}\right|
 \ll \sqrt{\ell\bigl(h+\log(2e)/e\bigr)}+2\ell h.
\]
Indeed the weight \(\exp(-2\ell/e)\) is bounded below on this range, and the total logarithmic measure in that estimate is \(eh\).
Set \(N=\lfloor y^{-1/16}\rfloor\) and decrease \(y_0\) until \(2\le N\le e\).
All the displayed phase means for \(\ell\le N\) are \(O_{k,L}(y^{1/16})\), since \((1/3-1/16)/2>1/16\).
For an integer \(1\le n\le N\), the normalized Fejér kernel \(\left|n^{-1}\sum_{a=0}^{n-1}e^{ia\theta}\right|^2\) has constant coefficient \(1/n\) and nonnegative Fourier coefficients summing to one.
It is at least \((\cos 1)^2\) for \(|\theta|\le1/n\), by taking real parts of the summands.
The mean of any translate on the conjugate arguments is at most \(1/n+O_{k,L}(y^{1/16})\).
For \(w\le1\) take \(n=\min(N,\lfloor 1/w\rfloor)\), which gives \(w\le1/n\) and \(1/n\ll w+y^{1/16}\).
For \(w>1\) the trivial bound suffices, proving (4).

<a id="moment"></a>
## A uniform second moment at the separating gap

We show \(d^{-1}\sum_i x_i^2\ll_{k,L}1\).
If \(q=0\), (2) bounds every \(x_i\) by \(\log L\).
For \(q\ge1\), at every exterior conjugate at least one index \(j\) realizes
\[
          |\gamma_i^{v_j}-\epsilon_j|\le C^{1/q}e^{-x_i/q}.        \tag{5}
\]
For such a pair put \(y_j=v_j/g\).
The left side is at least \(e^{y_jx_i}-1\ge y_jx_i\), so when \(x_i\ge1\),
\[
                         x_i\le \log C+q\log^+(1/y_j).              \tag{6}
\]
The angular distance of \(\gamma_i^{v_j}\) from the ray of \(\epsilon_j\) is \(O_{k,L}(e^{-x_i/q})\), since radial projection onto the circle moves any point by at most its distance to \(\epsilon_j\), and arc distance is at most \(\pi/2\) times chord distance.

For \(s\ge1\), consider the fraction of indices with \(x_i>s\) satisfying (5) at a specified \(j\).
When \(y_j\ge y_0\), it vanishes for \(s>\log C+q\log^+(1/y_0)\) by (6).
When \(0<y_j<y_0\), (4) bounds it by \(O_{k,L}(e^{-s/q}+y_j^{1/16})\), and (6) cuts it off at \(\log C+q\log(1/y_j)\).
Summing over at most \(k\) indices and integrating with weight \(2s\,ds\) proves the second-moment bound.
Indeed \(\int_1^\infty s e^{-s/q}\,ds\) is bounded in terms of \(k\), and \(y^{1/16}(1+\log(1/y))^2\) is bounded for \(0<y<1\).
The interval \(0\le s\le1\) contributes at most one.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree of the individual factor and the length-five case

By reciprocity, \(\sum_i(\log|\gamma_i|)^2=2g^{-2}\sum_i x_i^2\ll_{k,L}d/g^2\).
Applying the [quadratic logarithmic bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) to \(\alpha\) itself now gives \(1/10\le d\sum_i(\log|\gamma_i|)^2\ll_{k,L}d^2/g^2\).
Thus \(d\gg_{k,L}g\ge\delta D\).
The polynomial in (1) has at most \(L+2^k\) nonzero monomials, irrespective of the scalar \(b\).
[Dobrowolski's divisor estimate, Lemma 1](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) applied to \(A\mid P\) gives \(M(A)\ge1+0.31 d/(s!D)\) with \(s=|\operatorname{supp}P|\).
The degree proportion just proved supplies the claimed positive bound on \(\log M(A)\).

If \(\|P\|_1\le5\), a monomial has only the already treated prime and monomial factors.
Otherwise remove its common monomial and take a largest gap between consecutive nonzero exponents.
There are at most four gaps partitioning the degree \(D\), whence \(g\ge D/4\).
The two sides have positive integral lengths summing to at most five, so one has length at most two.
Orient this side upwards.
After shifting its first exponent to zero its polynomial is either a nonzero integer or \(\pm(X^v\pm1)\) with \(v\ge1\).
This is (1) with \(L=5\), \(k=1\), \(\delta=1/4\).
