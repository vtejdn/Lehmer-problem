# Which cubic factors split over solvable extensions of the totally real numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The group over the enlarged coefficient field

In a fixed algebraic closure in \(\mathbb C\), let \(T=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) be the field of all algebraic numbers every rational conjugate of which is real.
Let \(B\) be the union of the finite solvable Galois extensions of \(T\) in that closure.
The field \(T\) is also described as the fixed field of the real involutions in the [introduction of Fried, Haran and Völklein](https://www.math.uci.edu/~mfried/paplist-cov/QTotallyReal.pdf#page=3).
Both fields are Galois over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(B\) contains \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), since a finite abelian extension stays abelian after base change to \(T\).
It also contains the splitting fields of all Salem polynomials: their inverse-pair traces are totally real, and the roots above each trace satisfy a quadratic equation over \(T\).
Thus the single field \(B\) permits all these inputs at once.

For coprime positive integers \(a,b\) consider
\[
       A_{a,b}(X)=X^{3a}-X^a-1+X^b-X^{2a+b}-X^{3a+b}.
\]
For \(\max(a,b)\) sufficiently large, let \(P_i(X)=X^{n_i}H_i(X+X^{-1})\) be its distinct monic nontorsion rational irreducible factors, \(M_i\) their splitting fields and \(M\) the compositum.
Set \(W_i=V_i\rtimes A_{n_i}\), where \(V_i\subset\mathbb F_2^{n_i}\) consists of the vectors of even coordinate sum acting by inverse-pair exchanges.
Put \(J=\{i:H_i\text{ has a nonreal root}\}\).
Then in the joint action on the roots,
\[
             {\rm Gal}(MB/B)=\prod_{i\in J}W_i .                    \tag{1}
\]
The other coordinates in this product act trivially.
In particular each \(P_i\) with \(i\in J\) stays irreducible over \(B\); all roots of every other \(P_i\) belong to \(B\).
There is the exact intersection
\[
 M\cap B=E\prod_{i\notin J} M_i,\qquad
 E=\mathbb Q\bigl(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}H_i},
                     \sqrt{H_i(2)H_i(-2)}:\text{all }i\bigr),          \tag{2}
\]
where the right side denotes a compositum.
When \(b/a\) is sufficiently large, every index belongs to \(J\) and \(M\cap B=E\).
In this range the nontorsion factorization, and hence the allocation of exterior roots to each factor, is identical over \(\mathbb Q\), over the compositum of all Salem splitting fields and over \(B\).

<a id="conjugation"></a>
## The normal subgroup forced by a nonreal trace

The [joint cubic group](Yin_C_G0345_v384.md#statement) identifies the rational commutator subgroup in \({\rm Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\) with \(\prod_i W_i\) on the actual factors, with all \(n_i\ge7\) for sufficiently large directions.
Each individual \(W_i\) is normal in the rational group, since it is the parity kernel for a single factor inside its signed permutation group.
Let \(C={\rm Gal}(M/(M\cap T))\), the group after base change to \(T\).
It is normal in the rational group and contains the restriction \(c\) of complex conjugation, which fixes \(T\) pointwise.
For \(i\in J\) the action \(c_i\) on the trace labels moves at least one letter.
Its commutators with the alternating permutations on those labels include a nonidentity permutation: a permutation commuting with all three-cycles preserves every three-element support and hence fixes each letter.
Their lifts give commutators in \(C\cap W_i\).
Thus this normal subgroup of \(W_i\) maps onto the simple group \(A_{n_i}\).

For distinct \(j,k\), choose distinct \(l,m\) outside the pair and use the three-cycle \((j\ k\ m)\), which fixes \(l\).
Commuting any lift of that permutation with the even flip \(e_j+e_l\) gives \(e_j+e_k\).
Taking the lift in \(C\cap W_i\) shows that this subgroup contains the generators of \(V_i\), and consequently \(W_i\subseteq C\).
The same calculation gives \([A_{n_i},V_i]=V_i\), so \(W_i\) is perfect, since \(A_{n_i}\) is perfect.
It also shows that the normal closure of the natural \(A_{n_i}\) in \(W_i\) equals \(W_i\).

Passing from \(T\) to \(B\) quotients out only solvable information: \((MT)\cap B\) is finite Galois and solvable over \(T\), being contained in a finite compositum of solvable Galois extensions.
Each perfect \(W_i\subset C\) just identified maps trivially to that quotient.
Consequently the group over \(B\) contains their direct product; it lies in the rational commutator subgroup since \(B\supseteq\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
If \(i\notin J\), all roots of \(H_i\) belong to \(T\), and their quadratic lifts show \(M_i\subset B\).
This proves (1), including transitivity on the \(2n_i\) roots for each surviving coordinate by even flips and alternating permutations.
The [rational parity fields](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v187.md#quotient) identify \(E\) as the fixed field of \(\prod_i W_i\) in \(M\).
Over \(E\), fixing all \(M_i\) for \(i\notin J\) leaves exactly \(\prod_{i\in J}W_i\), which proves (2).

For large \(b/a\), the [exterior partition](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) puts a nonreal exterior root \(z\) in every nontorsion factor.
Its trace has imaginary part \((|z|-|z|^{-1})\sin(\arg z)\ne0\), so all indices survive.
In particular every root field \(\mathbb Q(z)\) of such a factor is linearly disjoint from \(B\) over \(\mathbb Q\), since its defining polynomial keeps its full degree there.
This applies to the root field of each factor; the larger splitting field has the explicit intersection (2).

<a id="towers"></a>
## The exact adjunction degree for an element of the splitting field

For \(y\in MB\setminus B\), call a factor index active for \(y\) if \(W_i\) in (1) moves \(y\).
Allow finite towers from \(B\) with arbitrary solvable adjunctions and with single algebraic adjunctions of degree at most \(q\) over the current field.
Here a solvable adjunction is one whose finite normal closure over the current field has solvable Galois group.
Then the least possible \(q\) to reach \(y\) is exactly
\[
                          \max_{i\ {\rm active\ for}\ y} n_i .    \tag{3}
\]
This distinguishes the contribution of each surviving field to an individual element of the compositum, even when different labels have the same degree.

For the lower bound fix an active index \(i\) and take \(q<n_i\).
A finite group is said here to have an \(A_{n_i}\) section if a subgroup has \(A_{n_i}\) as a quotient.
Absence of this section is closed under subgroups, quotients and extensions: for a map from a subgroup onto the simple group, the intersection with a normal kernel has image either trivial or the full simple group.
This also proves closure under finite direct products.
Solvable groups have no such section, and neither does \(S_q\) since \(q!<n_i!/2\).
Inductively the normal closure over \(B\) of any permitted tower has no such section.
Indeed after taking the normal closure of the preceding field, the next relative normal closure is contained in the compositum of the conjugate splitting fields of that step after base change.
Its new group embeds in a product of subgroups of the corresponding solvable groups or of \(S_q\).
Intersect this normal closure with \(MB\).
The resulting quotient of (1) still has no such section and so kills the simple natural \(A_{n_i}\).
It kills \(W_i\) by the [normal generation above](#conjugation).
It therefore cannot contain \(y\) in its fixed-kernel field.
For the upper bound, adjoin all trace roots for each active index, one by one, with degrees at most \(n_i\), then take the quadratic lifts.
The resulting compositum is the fixed field of the inactive coordinate groups in (1) and contains \(y\).

For a root of \(P_i\) at large slope the value in (3) is \(n_i\); the single trace adjoining step followed by one quadratic already attains it.
The same value holds for the multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(P_i)\), since its [signed-subset orbit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v183.md#orbits) under \(W_i\) is nontrivial and the other groups fix it.
For a product of these measures over any nonempty set of distinct factors, all the indicated groups are active because they act on their own factors independently and the measures are nonzero.
More explicitly, if \(k_i\) counts conjugate pairs of exterior roots in \(P_i\), then (1) and the same orbit give
\[
       [B(M(P_i)):B]=2^{2k_i}\binom{n_i}{2k_i}.
\]
This recovers the degree in the [small-exterior-share criterion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v183.md#criterion) even over the enlarged base.
For a varying selected factor at \(b/a\to\infty\), write \(q_i\) for the least possible largest nonsolvable adjunction degree needed to obtain its root or its Mahler measure.
By (3) and the [factor-degree asymptotic](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#degree),
\[
            \frac{2q_i}{b}=\frac{k_i}{a}(1+o(1))
\]
uniformly in the factor.
Thus a sequence of nontorsion cubic factors with measures tending to one exists exactly when such towers over \(B\) reach selected primitive roots at large slopes with \(q_i=o(b)\), by the [primitive reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v191.md#criterion).
The distribution of the integers \(k_i\) across the rational factors remains open.
