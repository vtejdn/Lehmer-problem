<a id="good-prime-short-order-capacity"></a>
## 1. Actual roots and a uniform degree-capacity bound

Let P be a monic irreducible integer polynomial with constant coefficient plus or minus one, degree d>=1, and a nontorsion root alpha.
Let p be a good prime: p does not divide disc(P).
Its reduction is squarefree and has d distinct nonzero roots in an algebraic closure of F_p.
For every positive integer o let w_(p,o) count those roots whose multiplicative order is exactly o.
This is L74's root count, so each irreducible factor contributes its degree; there is no further residue-degree multiplier.
There are only finitely many nonzero counts, and

    sum_o w_(p,o)=d,
    w_(p,o)=0 if p divides o,
    0<=w_(p,o)<=phi(o).                                      (1)

For p not dividing o, the algebraic closure has exactly phi(o) elements of multiplicative order o: the o-th roots form a cyclic group of order o, since t^o-1 has no repeated roots.
Squarefreeness of P ensures that these roots cannot be counted repeatedly.
This proves the last bound in (1); the same assertion with polynomial multiplicities is not valid for an arbitrary bad-prime reduction.

For X>=1, or with all orders included, define

    F_p(P;X)=sum_(o<=X) w_(p,o)/o,
    F_p(P;infinity)=sum_o w_(p,o)/o.

For any integer R>=0, split the coefficient 1/o at 1/(R+1).
Terms with o>R have nonpositive excess.
Using (1) gives

    F_p(P;X)
      <=d/(R+1)+sum_(1<=o<=R) phi(o)(1/o-1/(R+1))
      <=d/(R+1)+R/2.                                       (2)

Indeed phi(o)<=o, and sum_(o=1)^R [1-o/(R+1)]=R/2.
The same proof holds for F_p(P;infinity).
Put x=ceil(sqrt(2d)) and R=x-1.
Since 0<=x-sqrt(2d)<1 and x>=1,

    d/x+(x-1)/2
       =sqrt(2d)-1/2+(x-sqrt(2d))^2/(2x)
       <=sqrt(2d).

Consequently

    F_p(P;X)<=F_p(P;infinity)<=sqrt(2d).                    (3)

This is a capacity upper bound, not an assertion that a given P has many short-order roots.
It holds without any Mahler-measure hypothesis and without assuming that powers of alpha preserve degree.

<a id="frobenius-inversion-capacity"></a>
## 2. The finite Frobenius and inversion constraints

For p not dividing o>=2 let f_p(o)=ord_o(p).
Frobenius acts on the primitive o-th roots in orbits of exactly f_p(o) elements.
Hence every actual root set satisfies

    w_(p,o) in f_p(o) Z_(>=0),
    w_(p,o)<=phi(o).                                       (4)

Order one is a separate orbit of size one.
These constraints already give a finite integer-capacity problem for any fixed cutoff X.

For the reciprocal core relevant here, assume in addition that P(t)=t^(2s)P(t^(-1)), d=2s, and P(0)=1.
Then a good-prime reduction has no root of order one or two.
The identities over the integers

    P'(1)=sP(1),             P'(-1)=-sP(-1)

follow by differentiating reciprocity and evaluating before reduction.
Thus a root at either sign modulo p is also a derivative root, contrary to squarefreeness.
This reasoning includes p=2, where the two signs coincide.

For o>=3 with p not dividing o define

    h_p(o)=|<p,-1> in (Z/oZ)^*|
      =f_p(o)   if -1 belongs to <p>,
      =2f_p(o)  otherwise.                                (5)

This number is even.
The joint Frobenius/inversion action on the primitive o-th roots has exactly phi(o)/h_p(o) orbits, each of size h_p(o).
To see the size, identify such a root with an exponent a coprime to o.
Multiplication by an element of <p,-1> fixes a only for the identity element.
The root set of the reduction of P is invariant under both operations.
Therefore

    w_(p,o)=h_p(o)z_o,
    z_o in Z_(>=0),       z_o<=phi(o)/h_p(o).                (6)

Define the finite integer capacity

    C_(p,d)(X)=max sum_(3<=o<=X, p does not divide o) h_p(o)z_o/o,

    subject to sum_o h_p(o)z_o<=d,
               z_o integer, 0<=z_o<=phi(o)/h_p(o).          (7)

The zero choice is allowed.
For every actual reciprocal P in this domain,

    F_p(P;X)<=C_(p,d)(X)<=sqrt(2d).                         (8)

The capacity in (7) exactly describes the available joint-orbit selections with degree at most d.
Namely, choose z_o of the disjoint joint orbits at each order and multiply their linear factors.
Frobenius invariance puts the resulting monic polynomial in F_p[t]; inversion invariance makes it reciprocal with constant coefficient one, since no chosen root is its own inverse.
All roots are distinct and nonzero.
Its degree is sum h_p(o)z_o<=d.
The empty choice gives the constant polynomial one.

This finite-field realization neither provides an integer lift with small Mahler measure nor an irreducible integer lift.
It also does not assert realization at degree exactly d when the chosen total is smaller.
Those distinctions are essential: capacity is not coverage.

<a id="necessary-prime-range-for-density"></a>
## 3. A necessary prime budget for the proposed density screen

Let S be a finite set of good primes for P, and put

    Theta_S=sum_(p in S) log p,
    T_P(X,S)=sum_(p in S) log(p) F_p(P;X).

By (3), for every X,

    T_P(X,S)<=sqrt(2d) Theta_S.                            (9)

The proposed S8 sufficient screen asks for fixed eta,C>0 and some integer X<=Cd with

    T_P(X,S)>=2(log 2+eta)d.

Every successful instance necessarily satisfies

    Theta_S>=sqrt(2)(log 2+eta)sqrt(d).                    (10)

In the reciprocal domain, a sharper necessary test replaces (9) by sum_(p in S) log(p) C_(p,d)(X).
In particular, a fixed S, or a varying S with Theta_S=o(sqrt(d)), cannot satisfy this density screen along unbounded degrees.
This is a statement about that sufficient screen, not a necessary condition for every possible arithmetic gap certificate.

If S is contained in the primes at most Y, then also

    |S| log Y>=sqrt(2)(log 2+eta)sqrt(d).                  (11)

There is an elementary range-only consequence.
Write theta(Y)=sum_(p<=Y) log p.
For integer k>=1, every prime in (2^(k-1),2^k] divides binomial(2^k,2^(k-1)); this includes k=1.
Thus

    theta(2^k)-theta(2^(k-1))<=2^k log 2,
    theta(2^k)<2^(k+1) log 2.

For Y>=2 choose k>=1 with 2^(k-1)<Y<=2^k.
It follows that theta(Y)<4Y log 2.
Since Theta_S<=theta(Y), (10) implies

    Y>[sqrt(2)(log 2+eta)/(4 log 2)] sqrt(d).              (12)

No prime number theorem or asserted density of good primes is used.
Bad primes can only reduce Theta_S in this bound.
Neither (10) nor (12) is sufficient for actual short-order filling.

## 4. The existing actual controls do not fill the local capacity

Their reductions at p=2 are squarefree reciprocal pairs of irreducible factors of degrees 5,5 and 11,11.
The exact root orders are respectively N=31 and N=2047.
The first input is the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial; the second is the actual degree-22 core

    P_22=(t^24-t^17-t^12-t^7+1)/(t^2-t+1).

Consequently their first-layer short-order contribution at p=2 is exactly

    T_P(X,{2})=0                    if X<N,
               d log(2)/N          if X>=N.               (13)

This is a direct reuse of the retained factors and exact order certificates, not a new factorization or numerical experiment.
In these examples f_p(o) is odd and -1 is not in <p>, so (5) combines the two inverse Frobenius orbits into a joint orbit of size d.

These controls show that the retained small-measure assumptions do not automatically fill short orders at this fixed prime.
They do not refute a sufficiently-near-one theorem, do not provide an infinite small-measure family with this reduction pattern, and do not exclude successful certificates using other primes.
No claim about such a family is needed for the universal capacity upper bound (9).

<a id="automatic-lift-average-capacity"></a>
## 5. Automatic higher layers have the same degree-scale average cost

Retain a finite set S of good primes and use exactly L74's automatic valuation floor

    A_S(n)=sum_(p in S) sum_(o divides n)
                    w_(p,o)(1+v_p(n)) log p.              (14)

It is a lower bound for the full log cyclic norm, not in general its actual local valuation.
Because every contributing order o is prime to p,

    1_(o divides n)(1+v_p(n))
       =sum_(j>=0) 1_(o p^j divides n).

For a positive integer X the finite average is therefore exactly

    (1/X)sum_(1<=n<=X) A_S(n)
      =sum_(p in S,o) w_(p,o)log p
                    sum_(j>=0) floor(X/(o p^j))/X.

Only finitely many summands on the right are nonzero.
Using the upper bound floor(u)<=u and then a convergent geometric series gives

    (1/X)sum_(n<=X) A_S(n)
      <=sum_(p in S) [p/(p-1)] log(p) F_p(P;infinity)
      <=2sqrt(2d) Theta_S.                                (15)

This bound is independent of X.
In the first inequality one may replace F_p(P;infinity) by F_p(P;X), because orders o>X have no nonzero finite summand and may be omitted before the upper bound.
In particular the finite reciprocal capacity (7) can sharpen that form of (15).

Thus even an averaging argument retaining every automatic higher layer and requiring mean A_S(n)>=(log 2+eta)d must pay

    Theta_S>=(log 2+eta)sqrt(d)/(2sqrt(2)).                 (16)

This does not claim that automatic higher layers are equal to actual initial or subsequent valuation depths.
It does not bound an individual A_S(n) by the right side of (15), and it does not bound the peak of the true cyclic norm at a rare exponent.
In particular no contradiction arises if a linear-cost individual exponent has a useful certificate while the average over many exponents is small.

## 6. Exact reused input and irreducibility

Use the actual Lehmer polynomial

    P(t)=t^10+t^9-t^7-t^6-t^5-t^4-t^3+t+1,
    d0=10,                  0<m0=log M(P)<log 2.             (A1)

Its irreducible Salem status and the stronger bound M(P)<13/10 are retained in [L55](Yin_C_G0226_v1.md#lehmer-joint-prime-control).
The lower bound used below is exactly the accepted statement-level consequence of Dim19 in [L80](Yin_C_G0267_v1.md#degree-only-primitive-escape): every noncyclotomic monic irreducible polynomial of degree e>1 has logarithmic Mahler measure at least log(2)/(4e).
Its source proof is not newly reviewed here.

Fix a root alpha of P and K=Q(alpha).
For every rational prime q>40, alpha is not a q-th power in K.
Indeed, if alpha=b^q with b in K, then b and b^(-1) are algebraic integers: use the monic equations x^q-alpha and x^q-alpha^(-1) over the algebraic integers.
Thus b is an algebraic unit.
Also Q(b)=K, since alpha=b^q and b belongs to K.
The power rule for absolute logarithmic height gives

    m(b)=m0/q<log(2)/40,

contrary to L80 in degree ten.
The power rule here follows directly by multiplying each place's log-positive absolute value by q; both numbers have degree ten and are units.

For completeness, the needed prime-binomial irreducibility argument is elementary.
If x^q-alpha has a monic proper factor over K of degree 1<=r<q, let c be the product of its r roots, including their multiplicities.
Then c belongs to K, c is nonzero, and c^q=alpha^r.
Choose integers u,v with ur+vq=1.
It follows that

    (c^u alpha^v)^q=alpha,

a contradiction.
Thus x^q-alpha is irreducible over K.
If beta^q=alpha, then K is contained in Q(beta), and the field tower gives

    F_q(t):=P(t^q) irreducible over Q,
    deg F_q=10q=:d,             M(F_q)=M(P).                (A2)

The Mahler identity follows by grouping the q roots above each root of P: their moduli are |alpha_i|^(1/q), and their total Mahler factor is max(1,|alpha_i|).
In particular F_q is a genuine monic reciprocal unit minimal polynomial, not a reducible presentation.

## 7. A retained good prime and a positive peak at a linear exponent

The existing J11 result (`mathematics/computations/outputs/Yin_J_G0011/result.json`) records, for the Lehmer input at p=3:

    squarefree=true,
    (factor degree, root order)=(2,4),(8,82),

These are existing exact data, not a new factorization or order run.
Their retained verification scope is given in [J11](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0011/readme.md).

Since q>40, q is nonzero modulo 3.
The reduction F_q mod 3 is squarefree: at any root rho, rho is nonzero and

    F_q'(rho)=q rho^(q-1) P'(rho^q) !=0.

Here P mod 3 is squarefree by (A3).
Thus 3 is a good prime for F_q.
Every reduced root rho satisfies (rho^q)^h=1, so its order divides hq.
At the single exponent

    n_q=hq=(82/5)d,                                        (A4)

all d roots return.
Also 3 does not divide n_q.
The automatic good-prime floor of [L74](Yin_C_G0256_v1.md#good-prime-aggregation-and-global-tail) therefore gives exactly

    [A_{3}(n_q)-d log 2]/n_q
       =5 log(3/2)/82>0.                                  (A5)

This is an actual positive norm certificate with a constant independent of q.

## 8. The first-layer order average is nevertheless sublinear

For F_q mod 3, let w_o count its individual roots of multiplicative order o in the algebraic closure of F_3.
Define the first-layer statistic, for any real X>=1, by

    T_3(X)=log 3 * sum_{o<=X} w_o/o.                        (A6)

The roots are distinct.
The capacity proof (1)--(3) applies and gives, uniformly for every X>=1,

    T_3(X)/d <=sqrt(2/d) log 3 ->0 as q->infinity.           (A7)

Even the average of the complete automatic floor vanishes after normalization by d.
For any integer Y>=1, its divisibility-event expansion from L74 and nonnegative summation give

    (1/Y) sum_{1<=n<=Y} A_3(n)
       <=log 3 * sum_o (w_o/o) sum_{j>=0}3^(-j)
       =(3/2)T_3(infinity)<= (3/2)sqrt(2d)log 3.            (A8)

Thus a linear lower bound for this average or for T_3 is not necessary for a positive single-exponent certificate at n=O(d).
This assertion concerns the selected singleton prime set {3}; it does not exclude a different selector using additional primes.

The scope boundary is essential.
This family has the same fixed Mahler measure as P, so it does not approach measure one.
It also has an actual q-th-power dimension drop:

    [Q(beta):Q]=10q,            [Q(beta^q):Q]=10.            (A9)

It is therefore not in the all-positive-power degree-preserving unresolved core.
For q>1 it has q expanding conjugates, rather than the single expanding conjugate of a Salem polynomial.
No counterexample to a selector restricted to that core, or to any unrestricted Mahler-gap assertion, is claimed.

<a id="reciprocal-endpoint-and-powered-bad-primes"></a>
## 9. What the good-prime condition itself discards

Let G be any monic irreducible reciprocal unit polynomial of even degree 2D, with no root of unity.
Differentiating G(t)=t^(2D)G(t^(-1)) gives the integer identities

    G'(1)=D G(1),              G'(-1)=-D G(-1).             (A10)

If a rational prime p divides G(1)G(-1), then G and G' have a common root modulo p, at 1 or -1.
Hence p divides disc G.
This includes p=2.
Therefore all prime mass in

    C_1=|G(1)|,               C_2=|G(1)G(-1)|             (A11)

is excluded by the condition p does not divide disc G.
In particular, every good-prime floor has A_S(1)=A_S(2)=0.
This does not assert that later return mass is similarly excluded.

Now let alpha be a root of G, fix an integer a>=1, and suppose its a-th power preserves degree.
Its minimal polynomial G^[a] is still monic reciprocal.
Applying (A10) to G^[a] shows that every prime dividing |Norm(alpha^a-1)| divides disc G^[a].
If that prime was good for G, the exact index identity in [L59](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) identifies a newly bad monogenic-order prime:

    disc G^[a]=disc G * [Z[alpha]:Z[alpha^a]]^2.             (A12)

The number field has not changed, so no new field ramification is being inferred.
This distinction agrees with [L70](Yin_C_G0251_v1.md#local-conductor-period-bound).
Powering also costs m(alpha^a)=a m(alpha), and cannot be used without that cost to produce a bounded-measure counterfamily.
Equations (A10)--(A12) identify a limitation of the good-prime selector; they do not rule out its coverage over a longer window.

## 10. Reuse, positive-mechanism test, and stopping boundary

L74 already supplies w_(p,o), its degree normalization and the exact automatic divisibility-event expansion.
L152 classifies primitive inversion orbits and distinguishes actual mass from existence-only floors.
J10 already uses two special inverse-Frobenius orbit patterns.
Those statements are reused only at their fixed accepted scopes.
Neither the inspected L74 nor L152 states the short-order degree-capacity bound or the necessary prime-range estimates above.
The finite-field facts here are proved directly, without a novelty claim.

This test has not found a positive mechanism deriving sufficient short-order filling from degree and Mahler measure alone.
The upper capacity (7), the existence of a sufficiently large prime range, and the actual density lower bound are separate assertions.
A subsequent source or construction must supply the last assertion with its full input uniformity, or provide another costed gain in the direct L74 certificate.

The result stops the specified short-order density screen on fixed or too-slowly-growing good-prime sets.
Its automatic-layer extension stops the analogous average argument on those sets.
It does not stop isolated useful exponents, deeper actual valuation certificates, ramified primes, actual primitive excess, or the unrestricted G0001 objective.
In particular a sufficient density screen must not become a necessary gate for the entire direct cyclic-norm route.
