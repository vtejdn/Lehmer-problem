# Factor measures at polynomial Salem seeds with repeated roots in the three neighboring values

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive density of parameters at every polynomial

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be nonconstant with positive leading coefficient, and let \(\mathcal A\) be either the positive integers or the rational primes.
At each \(t\in\mathcal A\) with \(a=F(t)\ge3\), consider all the noncyclotomic irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\) of
\[
 G_{a,n,\epsilon}(X^h),\qquad
 G_{a,n,\epsilon}(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1),\quad n\ge7,\quad \epsilon=\pm1,\quad h\ge1 .
\]
The delay \(n\) and dilation \(h\) are integers.
Write \(V=(F-1)F(F+1)\).
For prime parameters remove the full power of \(T\) dividing \(V\), and for integer parameters retain \(V\) as it stands.
Factor the resulting polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\) and list its distinct irreducible factors of multiplicity at least two.
Denote their multiplicities by \(m_1+1,\ldots,m_k+1\).

For each \(0<\eta<1\), the set of \(t\in\mathcal A\) at which **every** factor above has logarithmic Mahler measure greater than \((\log t)^{1-\eta}\) has lower relative density at least \(\mathbb P(Y\le\eta)>0\).
The random variable \(Y\) is zero for \(k=0\).
For \(k\ge1\), take independent Poisson processes \(\Pi_i\) on \((0,1]\), each of intensity \(dy/y\), and set
\[
  w_m(y)=y\min\{m,\lfloor1/y\rfloor\},\qquad
  Y=\sum_{i=1}^k\ \sum_{y\in\Pi_i} w_{m_i}(y).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
For \(0<x\le1/2\) the lower-tail probability is
\[
           \mathbb P(Y\le x)=
           \frac{e^{-k\gamma}x^k}{k!\prod_{i=1}^k m_i},         \tag{2}
\]
where \(\gamma\) is Euler's constant.
When every \(m_i=1\), (2) holds through \(x=1\).
In particular \(F(T)=T^2\) on integer parameters and \(F(T)=(T-1)^2\) on prime parameters both give lower density at least \(e^{-\gamma}\eta\) for every \(0<\eta<1\).

<a id="divisor"></a>
## Definition of the largest divisor satisfying the two arithmetic bounds

For \(a\ge3\) let \(H(a)\) be the least common multiple of \((a+j)/\operatorname{rad}(a+j)\) for \(j=-1,0,1\), with \(\operatorname{rad}(b)\) the product of the distinct prime factors of \(b\).
For a fixed real \(B>0\), define \(D_B(a)\) to be the largest divisor of \(H(a)\) such that every prime power \(\ell^e\) dividing it, with \(e\ge1\), satisfies \(\ell^e\log(2\ell)\le B\log(a+1)\).
It exists because one can take independently at each prime the greatest allowed exponent, including zero.
For either sampling set, with \(t\) chosen uniformly from its elements up to \(X\) satisfying \(t\ge3,\ F(t)\ge3\), one has convergence in distribution
\[
               \frac{\log D_B(F(t))}{\log\log t}\ \Longrightarrow\ Y
                      \qquad (X\to\infty) .                      \tag{3}
\]
For \(k\ge1\) and every fixed \(A>0\), the proportion of parameters with \(D_B(F(t))>(\log t)^A\) therefore tends to \(\mathbb P(Y>A)>0\).
This counts the parameters that pass both stated arithmetic bounds at arbitrarily large fixed logarithmic exponents.
To turn a divisor counted by these bounds into a field radical one still needs a root in the Salem field.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Frequencies of prime divisors of the distinct factors

Write the polynomial after the possible removal of \(T\) as \(c\prod_i H_i^{e_i}\) with primitive, distinct, irreducible integer factors and \(0\ne c\in\mathbb Z\).
Use the same indices \(1,\ldots,k\) as in (1) for its repeated factors and put \(K=\prod_i H_i\), including the simple factors.
Omit finitely many primes dividing \(2c\), the leading coefficients, or the discriminant of \(K\), and in the prime sample also those dividing \(K(0)\ne0\).
We can further omit the ramified primes of the splitting field of \(K\).
For each remaining prime \(\ell\), the roots of the \(H_i\bmod\ell\) are simple and their sets are disjoint.
Let \(\nu_i(\ell)\) count the roots of \(H_i\bmod\ell\).
The chance of lying in one of its classes for a uniform residue is \(P_{\ell i}=\nu_i(\ell)/\ell\) in the integer case and \(P_{\ell i}=\nu_i(\ell)/(\ell-1)\) in the prime case.
All its roots are units in the latter case.

At a prime above \(\ell\) the roots of \(H_i\) are integral, reduce distinctly, and Frobenius acts on their reductions by raising to \(\ell\).
Consequently \(\nu_i(\ell)\) equals the number of fixed roots of Frobenius.
The Galois action on the roots of the irreducible \(H_i\) is transitive, so the mean number of fixed roots over the group is one by counting pairs of a point and an element of its stabilizer.
The [counting form of Chebotarev's theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) applied to this fixed splitting field therefore gives \(\sum_{\ell\le z}\nu_i(\ell)\sim\operatorname{li}(z)\), with the indicated finite omissions.
Set \(L=\log X\).
For fixed \(0<u<v\), partial summation yields
\[
               \sum_{L^u<\ell\le L^v} P_{\ell i}\ \longrightarrow\
                              \log(v/u).                          \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the endpoint terms for the sum with weight \(1/\ell\) vanish, and the integrated main term is \(\int_{L^u}^{L^v} ds/(s\log s)=\log(v/u)\) up to \(o(1)\).
Replacing \(1/\ell\) by \(1/(\ell-1)\) changes the sum by \(O_F(L^{-u})\).

<a id="counts"></a>
## Independent Poisson counts on fixed ranges of prime size

Choose any finite partition of \((\delta,1]\) into intervals \(I=(u,v]\), with \(0<\delta<1\).
For each pair \(i\le k,I\), count the good primes \(\ell\) with \(y_\ell=\log\ell/\log L\in I\) and \(\ell\mid H_i(t)\).
As \(X\to\infty\), these counts jointly approach independent Poisson variables of means \(\log(v/u)\).

To see this, expand any mixed factorial moment of total order \(q\ge1\).
Within each pair the factorial counts use distinct primes, and a shared prime across two different pairs contributes zero by the interval partition and the disjoint root sets.
For an ordered tuple of distinct primes the Chinese remainder theorem gives main term \(\prod P_{\ell i}\).
There are at most \((\deg K)^q\) admissible residue classes modulo their product, which is at most \(L^q\).
Uniformly in the tuple, normalized counting of the integer classes costs \(O_{F,q}(1/X)\) in absolute error.
For the prime sample the [Siegel–Walfisz estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#ked07-u001), including modulus one for the denominator, gives \(O_{F,q,A}((\log X)^{1-A})\) in absolute normalized error for every fixed \(A>0\).
Here one may average first over the entire sample through \(X\), since deleting the finitely many small parameters in (3) has no effect on its eventual distribution.
There are \(O_{F,q}(L^q)\) tuples, so the total error is \(o(1)\) on taking \(A>q+2\).
In the product of the individual sums from (4), tuples with a repeated prime cost \(o(1)\), since every such collision has two weights \(O_F(1/\ell)\) at \(\ell>L^\delta\), with \(\sum_{\ell>L^\delta}1/\ell^2=o(1)\) and the other total weights bounded by (4).
The limiting factorial moments are precisely the products of the stated Poisson means raised to the corresponding orders.
The first moments give tightness, higher moments give uniform integrability at each fixed order, and the independent Poisson vector is determined by its moments because its exponential moments are finite in a neighborhood of zero.
This proves the joint convergence.

For \(0<\delta<1\), this also proves convergence of the sums of \(w_{m_i}(y_\ell)\mathbf 1_{\ell\mid H_i(t)}\) over \(\delta<y_\ell\le1\) to the corresponding sums in (1).
The bounded weights are linear on each of finitely many intervals, with possible jumps only at the reciprocals of integers through \(\max_i m_i\).
Step-function approximations away from these finitely many points have arbitrarily small uniform error, and counts in small neighborhoods of the jumps have arbitrarily small limiting mean by (4) and the single-prime class estimate.
For \(y_\ell\le\delta\) the mean of the omitted weighted sum is \(O_F(\delta)+o(1)\), by \(w_m(y)\le my\), the same class estimates and \(\sum_{\ell\le z}(\log\ell)/\ell\ll\log(2z)\), which follows by partial summation from the prime number theorem.
The Poisson sum below \(\delta\) has expectation at most \(\delta\sum_i m_i\).
Thus the full weighted prime-divisor sum converges to (1) by truncation and Markov's inequality.
For \(k=0\) both sums here are empty.

<a id="cap"></a>
## Recovering the logarithm of the arithmetic divisor

Discard \(t<\sqrt X\), which has vanishing relative proportion in either sample.
Uniformly in the remaining parameters \(B\log(F(t)+1)\) lies between \(c_1L\) and \(c_2L\) for some fixed positive constants, so every prime in \(D_B(F(t))\) is at most \(c_2L\) for sufficiently large \(X\).
For prime parameters the removed power of \(T\) contributes no valuation in this range, because \(t>c_2L\).

At each fixed prime, \(v_\ell(K(t))\) is tight as \(X\to\infty\).
Indeed the [local root-count estimate for a squarefree polynomial](Yin_C_G0345_v732.md#local) bounds the number of its roots modulo \(\ell^j\) by a constant depending only on \(K,\ell\), for all \(j\).
The valuations of \(c\prod_i H_i(t)^{e_i}\) are therefore tight at each fixed prime too.
At the good primes, the same simple-root count bounds the upper relative density of having some \(Z<\ell\le c_2L\) with \(\ell^2\mid K(t)\) by \(O_F(1/Z)\).
For integers, sum \(O_F(X/\ell^2+1)\) over these primes.
For prime parameters the reduced classes modulo \(\ell^2\) cost \(O_F(1/\ell(\ell-1))\) in main term and \(O_F((\log X)^{-3})\) in normalized error per prime, exactly as in the [square-divisor truncation](Yin_C_G0345_v732.md#density).

Take \(Z\) above every omitted prime.
The total contribution of primes up to \(Z\) to \(\log D_B(F(t))/\log L\) tends to zero in probability for fixed \(Z\), by tightness.
Their contributions to the weighted prime-divisor sum vanish as well.
Away from the square-divisor event just bounded, each remaining contributing good prime divides exactly one \(H_i(t)\), to order one.
Such an odd prime divides only one of the three neighbors of \(F(t)\), so \(v_\ell(H(F(t)))=e_i-1\) at this prime.
It contributes one copy of \(\log\ell/\log L\) for each \(1\le j\le e_i-1\) satisfying \(\ell^j\log(2\ell)\le B\log(F(t)+1)\).
In the simple factor case there are no copies.

Replacing the last condition by \(\ell^j\le L\) can change the contribution only if
\[
  c_1 L/\log(2CL)\le \ell^j\le CL,\qquad C=\max(2,c_2).
\]
For each of the finitely many \(j\le\max_i(e_i-1)\) this restricts \(y_\ell\) to a shrinking neighborhood of \(1/j\).
The expected number of divisors \(\ell\mid H_i(t)\) in such a neighborhood tends to zero, by enclosing it in a fixed neighborhood, using the single-prime class estimate and (4), and then shrinking the fixed neighborhood.
The upper edge at \(1\) is covered by allowing \(v>1\) in (4).
After replacement the contribution at each remaining prime is precisely \(w_{m_i}(y_\ell)\) if \(i\le k\), and zero otherwise, taking the weight to be zero for \(y_\ell>1\).
Letting \(Z\to\infty\) proves that the normalized logarithm and the weighted prime-divisor sum differ by a quantity tending to zero in probability.
Finally \(\log\log t/\log L\to1\) uniformly on \([\sqrt X,X]\), proving (3).

<a id="mass"></a>
## The lower tail and support of the limiting law

Assume \(k\ge1\).
The expectation of (1) is finite because \(w_{m_i}(y)\le m_i y\).
The sum has no atoms.
Indeed, in any one process restrict to \((b,d)\) with \(0<b<d<1/m_i\).
Given a nonzero number of points in that interval, their locations have a joint density when labeled in increasing order, and the sum of their weights \(m_i y\) has no atoms and is independent of the contribution outside the interval.
An atom of the full law has mass at most the probability \(b/d\) of no point in this interval, which tends to zero as \(b\downarrow0\).
On \((1/2,1)\), all the weights equal \(y\), and the Poisson count there can exceed any prescribed integer with positive probability, proving the assertion about the upper tail.

For \(0<x\le1/2\), the points with \(w_m(y)\le x\) are exactly those with \(y\le x/m\), up to endpoints of zero intensity.
Indeed \(j=\lfloor1/y\rfloor\) gives \(jy>j/(j+1)\ge1/2\) for \(y>1/m\).
The probability that no larger point occurs in any of the processes is \(\prod_i(x/m_i)\).
Independently of this event, change variable \(z=m_i y/x\) on each remaining interval.
These changes preserve \(dy/y\), and the sum divided by \(x\) becomes the sum of the point locations on \((0,1]\) for intensity \(k\,dz/z\), by superposition of the independent processes.
Its probability of being at most one is [\(e^{-k\gamma}/\Gamma(k+1)\)](../audits/literature/ABT99/Yin_U001.md#abt99-u001), proving (2).
For \(m_i=1\) all the weights are \(y\), so the same calculation works up to \(x=1\).

<a id="measures"></a>
## Passage from divisors to factor measures

Let \(r\) be the exterior Salem root at \(a,n,\epsilon\) and \(R\) the largest positive integer with \(r^{1/R}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
By the [all-delay exceptions estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v703.md#seeds), at most \(\exp(O_F(\sqrt{\log X}))\) parameters \(t\le X\) fail \(R\mid H(F(t))\) at any choice of delay or sign, since \(F(t)\ll_F X^{\deg F}\) and every fiber has at most \(\deg F\) parameters.
This exceptional set has density zero in either sample.
The [prime-power estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) then gives \(R\mid D_B(F(t))\) simultaneously for all \(n,\epsilon\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(B\), using \(a-1<r<a+1\).
The [seed cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) and [least factor measure formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) give a common lower bound \(\log(F(t)-1)/D_B(F(t))\) for every factor in the statement.
For \(0<x<\eta\), the parameters with \(D_B(F(t))\le(\log t)^x\) therefore satisfy the desired strict bound for all large \(t\), since \(\log(F(t)-1)\sim(\deg F)\log t\).
Equation (3), continuity at \(x>0\) and passage \(x\uparrow\eta\) give the asserted lower density.
In each stated quadratic example there is exactly one repeated factor after the specified choice at \(T=0\), of multiplicity two.
Both examples follow from (2) in its range up to one.
