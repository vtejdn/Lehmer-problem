# Exact Pisot parameter intervals and a gap for bounded integral slopes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Feasibility from finitely many circle crossings

Let \(C\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) be monic cyclotomic of degree \(k\ge2\), allowing a product with repeated factors, with \(C(0)=-1\).
Fix \(U\in\mathbb Z[Z]\), \(U(0)=0\), \(\deg U<k\), and put
\[
 f_q=(Z-q)C+U,\qquad U^\dagger=Z^{k+1}U(1/Z),\qquad
 H=Zf_q+f_q^*=(Z^2-1)C+ZU+U^\dagger .
\]
Here \(f_q^*=Z^{k+1}f_q(1/Z)\), and \(H\) is independent of the real parameter \(q\).
Call \(q>1\) feasible when \(f_q\) has one root greater than one and every other root strictly inside the unit disk.
For integer \(q\ge2\), feasibility makes \(f_q\) an irreducible Pisot minimal polynomial.

Feasibility requires \(\gcd(C,U)=1\), \(U(1)<0\), and \(H\) to have one simple positive exterior root, its inverse, and \(k\) simple unit-circle roots.
If these conditions hold, let \(\mathcal Z\) be the set of those unit-circle roots.
For \(\zeta\in\mathcal Z\) with \(C(\zeta)\ne0\), define the real numbers
\[
               t_\zeta=\zeta+\frac{U(\zeta)}{C(\zeta)},\qquad
               b_\zeta=\frac{H'(\zeta)}{C(\zeta)}\ne0 .
\]
At a root with \(C(\zeta)=0\), require instead
\[
                              H'(\zeta)/U(\zeta)>0 .                 \tag{1}
\]
If any of these fixed inequalities fails, there is no feasible parameter.
Otherwise the complete feasible set is the single open interval
\[
 \left(\max\!\left(\{1\}\cup\{t_\zeta:b_\zeta<0\}\right),
                 \ \min\{t_\zeta:b_\zeta>0\}\right),                 \tag{2}
\]
with an empty upper minimum interpreted as infinity and an interval of nonpositive length interpreted as empty.
Every nonempty interval has finite upper endpoint.
If its endpoints are \(a<b\), the number of feasible integers is exactly
\(\max(0,\lceil b\rceil-\lfloor a\rfloor-1)\).
An endpoint arising from some \(t_\zeta\) is excluded because \(f_q\) then has a unit-circle root, and the endpoint one is excluded by the stipulated parameter range.

An equivalent criterion for real \(q>1\) is \(U(1)<0\) and strict disk stability of the degree-\(k\) polynomial
\[
                   A_q=f_q^*-qf_q=(q^2-1)C-qU+U^\dagger.             \tag{3}
\]
Degree exactly \(k\) is part of this criterion.
If \(u_1=[Z]U\), feasibility implies \(u_1>0\), and the [integer branch index](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) of every feasible integer parameter is exactly \(u_1\).

There is also an obstruction when the degree and return vary.
Suppose \(k\to\infty\), \(q\ge ck\) for a fixed \(c>0\), and
\[
                         U=qV+W,
\]
where \(V,W\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) have degree less than \(k\), constant zero, and coefficient heights bounded by a fixed \(M\).
If each \(f_q\) is a Pisot minimal polynomial with exterior root \(\theta\), then
\[
                       \liminf(\theta-q)\ge e^{-1/c}.               \tag{7}
\]
If \(q/k\to\infty\), the sharper formula is
\[
             \theta-q=-[Z^{k-1}]V+O_M(k/q),
                      \qquad [Z^{k-1}]V\le-1
                            \quad\text{eventually}.                 \tag{8}
\]
In particular every limiting excess is a positive integer.
The polynomials \(C,V,W\) may all vary with \(k\), and \(U\) itself may have height of order \(q\).

<a id="count"></a>
## The winding count gives the full number of exterior roots

A common root of \(C,U\) would be a fixed unit-circle root of \(f_q\), so the coprimality condition is necessary.
At a feasible parameter, \(f_q(1)<0\), since its exterior root is positive and every interior factor has positive value at one after conjugate pairing.
This is \(U(1)<0\).

For a parameter at which \(f_q\) has no unit-circle root, set
\(\mathcal W(Z)=Zf_q(Z)/f_q^*(Z)\).
It maps the circle into itself.
If \(I\) roots of \(f_q\) are inside, counted with multiplicity, its winding is
\[
                                1+I-(k+1-I)=2I-k .
\]
Possible common factors of numerator and denominator cancel equally in this difference.
The preimages of minus one are exactly the unit-circle roots of \(H\).
At a simple such root the derivative of the argument is
\[
                              \Psi'=\frac{H'(\zeta)}{f_q(\zeta)}.    \tag{4}
\]
This follows by differentiating \(1+\mathcal W=H/f_q^*\) and using
\(f_q^*(\zeta)=-\zeta f_q(\zeta)\).

At a feasible parameter \(I=k\), so the winding is \(k\).
The sign \(H(1)=2U(1)<0\) supplies a real root above one and its reciprocal.
The remaining degree allows at most \(k\) circle roots counted with multiplicity.
The [signed crossing count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v34.md#crossings) therefore forces exactly \(k\), all simple and all upward.
This proves the necessary root profile of \(H\).

More generally, suppose \(H\) has \(r\) exterior roots and all of its circle roots are simple.
Reciprocity gives \(k+2-2r\) circle roots.
If \(\ell\) of their crossings are downward, the winding is \(k+2-2r-2\ell\), hence
\[
                           \#\{\text{exterior roots of }f_q\}
                                        =r+\ell .                  \tag{5}
\]
In the profile of the statement, \(r=1\).
Thus feasibility is exactly positivity in (4) at every circle root, together with \(U(1)<0\).
The latter condition makes the unique exterior root positive, by the intermediate value theorem.

<a id="interval"></a>
## Converting the crossings to an interval

If \(C(\zeta)\ne0\), the anti-reciprocity of \(C\) and reciprocity of \(H\), at a circle root of \(H\), show that \(t_\zeta\) and \(b_\zeta\) are real.
Explicitly \(\overline{C(\zeta)}=-\zeta^{-k}C(\zeta)\), and
\[
 t_\zeta-\overline{t_\zeta}=\frac{H(\zeta)}{\zeta C(\zeta)}=0,
 \qquad
 \overline{H'(\zeta)}=-\zeta^{-k}H'(\zeta).
\]
Also \(f_q(\zeta)=(t_\zeta-q)C(\zeta)\).
The positivity condition is therefore \(q<t_\zeta\) when \(b_\zeta>0\) and \(q>t_\zeta\) when \(b_\zeta<0\).
If \(C(\zeta)=0\), coprimality gives \(f_q(\zeta)=U(\zeta)\ne0\), and (4) becomes the fixed real inequality (1).
All possible circle zeros of \(f_q\) have now been accounted for, since any such zero also annihilates \(H\).
This proves (2), including sufficiency and the excluded endpoints.
An unbounded nonempty interval would contain arbitrarily large integer parameters, contradicting the [fixed-\(C,U\) height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#statement).
This proves finiteness of the upper endpoint.

For integer \(q\), a monic factor of \(f_q\) not containing its sole exterior root would have all roots strictly inside and nonzero integer constant coefficient.
Its constant coefficient would have absolute value less than one, a contradiction.
This proves the asserted irreducibility.
The interval specializes the [inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) to a fixed affine line and also handles the other degree parity and arbitrary cyclotomic factors of the return.

<a id="schur"></a>
## Stability and the branch coefficient

On the unit circle \(|f_q|=|f_q^*|\).
If there are no circle zeros, Rouché's theorem gives \(f_q-qf_q^*\) the same interior root count as \(f_q^*\), because \(q>1\).
Since
\[
                     f_q-qf_q^*=Z A_q^*,
\]
where the star on \(A_q\) uses degree \(k\), \(f_q\) has exactly one exterior root if and only if \(A_q\) has degree \(k\) and all its roots strictly inside.
For the reverse implication, stability first excludes circle zeros of \(f_q\), since any such zero would also annihilate \(A_q\).
The condition \(U(1)<0\) then selects the positive exterior root.
This proves (3).

The constant coefficient of \(A_q\) is \(1-q^2\), and its leading coefficient is \(q^2-1+u_1\).
At a feasible parameter \(A_q(1)=(1-q)U(1)>0\).
A real stable polynomial has the same sign at one as its leading coefficient, so that leading coefficient is positive.
The product of its root moduli is less than one, giving \(q^2-1+u_1>q^2-1\), hence \(u_1>0\).
Writing \(c_j=[Z^j]C\), anti-reciprocity gives \(c_1=-c_{k-1}\).
The coefficients of \(f_q\) at degrees one and \(k\) are
\(-1-qc_1+u_1\) and \(c_{k-1}-q\), respectively.
Expansion at zero now gives
\[
                        [Z](f_q/f_q^*)-q^2+1=u_1 .
\]
In particular the [first-branch gap](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) applies when \(u_1=1\), regardless of the other perturbation coefficients.

<a id="quartic"></a>
## An explicit unbounded-height family with its whole parameter interval

For any integer \(A\ge2\), take \(C=Z^3-1\) and \(U=-AZ^2+(A-1)Z\).
Then
\[
 f_q=Z^4-qZ^3-AZ^2+(A-2)Z+q,\qquad
 H=(Z+1)(Z^4-Z^3-Z^2-Z+1).
\]
The trace polynomial of the quartic factor is \(X^2-X-3\).
Its two roots are \(T=(1+\sqrt{13})/2>2\) and \(x=(1-\sqrt{13})/2\in(-2,2)\), giving the required profile.
At \(-1\), the threshold is \(A-3/2\) and \(H'/C=-3/2<0\).
At each of the other circle roots, with trace \(x\), direct substitution gives
\[
 t_\zeta=1+\frac{3+\sqrt{13}}2(A-1),\qquad
 \frac{H'(\zeta)}{C(\zeta)}
                   =\frac{x+2}{x+1}(2x-1)>0 .
\]
Consequently the exact feasible interval is
\[
             \max(1,A-3/2)<q<1+\frac{3+\sqrt{13}}2(A-1).            \tag{6}
\]
For example \(A=2\) gives precisely the integer parameters \(2,3,4\).
Every integer parameter in (6) gives an irreducible Pisot polynomial, with branch \(A-1\).

Put \(g=(3+\sqrt{13})/2\).
If \(A\to\infty\) through these inputs and \(q/A\to c\), necessarily \(1\le c\le g\).
The exterior root equation gives
\[
 \theta-q=\frac A\theta-\frac{A-2}{\theta^2}-\frac q{\theta^3},
                 \qquad 0<\theta-q\le A/q .
\]
It follows that \(\theta-q\to1/c\).
Every \(c\in[1,g]\) is realized by choosing integer parameters in (6) with that ratio.
At the upper endpoint scale the limit is \(1/g=T-2>0\).
Thus arbitrarily large perturbation height and branch index coexist with a fixed positive return excess in this family.

<a id="slopes"></a>
## Bounded integral slopes force a positive excess as the degree grows

Assume the hypotheses for (7), and put \(\delta=\theta-q\).
The [exterior evaluation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#height), with coefficient height at most \(M(q+1)\), gives
\[
              0<\delta<
                \frac{M(q+1)}{q(1-1/q)^{k+1}}=O_{M,c}(1).
\]
Consequently \(\theta=q+O_{M,c}(1)\).
Write \(v=[Z^{k-1}]V\), an integer with \(|v|\le M\).
The geometric coefficient bounds give
\[
 \frac{U(\theta)}{\theta^{k-1}}
       =qv+O_M(q/\theta)+O_M(1)=qv+O_M(1).                         \tag{9}
\]
The value \(C(\theta)\) is positive, since its real factors and its conjugate pairs are positive at \(\theta>1\).
The root equation \(\delta C(\theta)=-U(\theta)\) therefore excludes \(v\ge1\) eventually.

If \(v=0\), the coefficient bound gains one degree in \(V\), giving
\[
              |U(\theta)|=O_M(\theta^{k-1}),\qquad
              \delta=O_{M,c}(1/q)
\]
by \(|C(\theta)|\ge(\theta-1)^k\).
Along any such subsequence this contradicts the [uniform lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#return)
\[
           \delta>
           2^{-10}\left(\frac{\log\log(k+2)}{\log(k+2)}\right)^6,
\]
since \(q\ge ck\).
Thus \(v\le-1\) eventually.
Now (9) and \(|C(\theta)|\le(\theta+1)^k\) give
\[
             \delta\ge
                \frac q\theta(1+1/\theta)^{-k}(1-O_M(1/q)).
\]
Here \(q/\theta\to1\) and \(k/\theta\le1/c+o(1)\).
This proves (7).

If \(q/k\to\infty\), the root product for \(C\) further gives
\[
        C(\theta)/\theta^k=1+O(k/q).
\]
Indeed the absolute deviation of \(\prod_{C(\zeta)=0}(1-\zeta/\theta)\) from one is at most \((1+1/\theta)^k-1\), including repeated factors.
Equation (9), divided by \(\theta\), and \(\theta=q+O_M(1)\) give
\(U(\theta)/\theta^k=v+O_M(1/q)\).
The root equation proves (8).
Thus bounded integral coefficients in both the slope and the constant part of this affine perturbation prevent excess tending to zero at the large-\(q\) scales of the controlled inverse construction.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Coverage of the fixed inverse problem

If a monic return \(H\) of degree \(k+2\) is fixed, any degree-\(k+1\) inverse \(f\) with constant \(q\) has
\([Z^k]f+q=[Z^{k+1}]H\).
Whenever a cyclotomic \(C\) as above has \([Z^{k-1}]C=[Z^{k+1}]H\), the difference \(U=f-(Z-q)C\) has degree less than \(k\) and constant zero.
Thus allowing \(U\) to vary recovers every inverse for that return under this matching condition.
The interval criterion solves feasibility on each fixed affine line.
Producing changing returns with exterior roots tending to one remains the outstanding construction problem.
The coordinator derived the signed crossing interval and exterior count, and the contributor supplied the stability identity, branch calculation and explicit quartic family.
The coordinator also proposed the bounded-slope obstruction for changing degrees, and the contributor checked its uniform estimates and the integer limiting excess.
