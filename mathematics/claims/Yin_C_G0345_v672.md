# A logarithmic factor beyond inverse measure in terminal witness arity

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number of terms required by the witness multiplicity

Let \(\alpha\) range over nontorsion algebraic units of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\to0\).
Suppose that some conjugate \(\gamma\) satisfies \(H=|\log|\gamma||\ge\eta m\), where \(\eta>0\) is fixed.
For a fixed ordered tuple of \(r\) nonzero rational integers \(\mathbf b\), let \(H_2\) be the product of \(1+|b_i|\) outside the two largest magnitudes.
Count strictly increasing lists \(t_1<\cdots<t_r\) modulo translation satisfying \(\sum_i b_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) with no nonempty proper zero subsum.
If \(r\to\infty\) and the count is at least \(H_2^2(c/m)^{r-2}\) for fixed \(c>0\), then
\[
                         r\gg_{c,\eta}\frac{\log(1/m)}m .                    \tag{1}
\]
The same conclusion holds for [marked terminal witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement) of that multiplicity in either the neutral or the near-neutral setting, in particular at Salem numbers.
Indeed, choosing the full position of the mark loses at most a factor \(r\), which can be absorbed by decreasing \(c\), and the [terminal exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) are distinct for \(r\ge4\).
The result strengthens the [previous arity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v668.md#statement).
An upper bound on arity that contradicts (1) remains open.

<a id="forest"></a>
## The cost of merges with strong cancellation

Write \(L=\log(1/m)\) and let \(N\) denote the count at a fixed full order.
Use the [binary forest](Yin_C_G0345_v668.md#forest) in the embedding \(\gamma\), stopping with two groups.
Each of its \(r-2\) merges joins terms \(u,v\) whose moduli are within a factor two.
Call a merge exceptional if \(|u+v|<m\max(|u|,|v|)\).
At an ordinary merge there are at most \(1+2\log(2)/H\ll_\eta 1/m\) relative exponents once the two child coefficients are known.
At an exceptional merge the moduli have ratio between \(1-m\) and \(1/(1-m)\), so the interval for the integer exponent difference has length at most \(2|\log(1-m)|/H\ll_\eta 1\).
The relative translation of the final two groups is unique.

Put \(p=3/2\).
In the [signed multiplicity estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v662.md#multiplicities) one may use the probability weights \((1+|b|)^{-p}/Z_p\), since \(Z_p=\sum_{b\ne0}(1+|b|)^{-p}\) is finite.
The same multinomial argument after omitting the two largest entries pays \(C^r H_2^p\) for \(r!/\prod_b n_b!\), where \(n_b\) counts occurrences of \(b\).
For any \(0\le j\le r-2\), the number of lists admitting a comparable forest with at least \(j\) exceptional nodes is at most
\[
                       C_\eta^r H_2^p m^{-(r-2)}m^j .                       \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the choices of forest cost at most \(2^{2r}(2r-3)!!\), its designated \(j\) nodes cost at most \(2^r\), and permuting the labels of every equal signed coefficient preserves the existence of such a forest.
This permits division by \(\prod_b n_b!\) as in the forest proof.
Every counted list admits a comparable forest.
Comparing (2) at \(j=0\) with the assumed lower bound gives \(\log H_2\ll_{c,\eta}r\).
In particular the sum \(S\) of the coefficient magnitudes outside the largest two satisfies \(\log(2S)\ll_{c,\eta}r\).
For \(j=\lceil K r/L\rceil\) with sufficiently large fixed \(K=K(c,\eta)\), (2) bounds the proportion admitting that many exceptional nodes by \(e^{-r}\) for large inputs.

Cut the two child edges at each exceptional merge of a forest on one of the remaining lists.
This gives at most \(2+2j\) components.
On every uncut edge the logarithms of the child and parent moduli differ by at most \(L+\log 3\), using comparability and the lower bound defining an ordinary merge.
Thus the values \(t_i\log|\gamma|+\log|b_i|\) at leaves of any one component lie in an interval of length at most \(2r(L+\log 3)\).
Outside the two largest coefficients their logarithmic magnitudes lie in \([0,\log(2S)]\).
Allowing the two omitted positions singleton intervals, every such list is covered by at most \(4+2j\) integer intervals of widths at most \(C_{c,\eta}r(L+1)/m\).
Their positions may depend on the entire list.

<a id="locations"></a>
## Locating all the short intervals using two reference relations

We give a simultaneous restriction on the positions at a fixed tuple, independent of its two largest coefficients.
For any nondegenerate zero sum of integer multiples of powers of \(\alpha\) with total absolute coefficient mass \(U\), every adjacent exponent gap \(g\) satisfies
\[
                                mg\le d\log U.                              \tag{3}
\]
Translate the largest exponent on the left of a positive gap to zero and take the partial sum on that side.
It is a nonzero algebraic integer because \(\alpha\) is a unit.
At conjugates of modulus at least one its modulus is at most \(U\).
At a contracting conjugate \(\rho\) the opposite partial sum bounds its modulus by \(U|\rho|^g\).
The product of the contracting moduli is \(e^{-m}\), so the integral norm proves (3).
Repeated exponents cause no difficulty in this estimate.

Call the two largest coefficient labels \(a,b\), in their fixed left-to-right order, and normalize the exponent of \(a\) to zero.
For each reference list write \(a+b\alpha^{g_i}=C_i(\alpha)\), where \(g_i\) is the exponent of \(b\) and \(C_i\) is the integral Laurent polynomial given by the negated surviving terms.
Its coefficient mass is at most \(S\).
We show that all positions of all normalized lists lie in a common union of \(O(r^6)\) intervals of widths
\[
                             O\big(r^2d\log(2S)/m\big).                     \tag{4}
\]

If all \(g_i\) coincide, fix one reference list.
Subtract the surviving sum of any other list from that of the reference, and partition the resulting labeled terms into minimal zero subsums.
Each block containing a term of the other list meets the reference, since a zero subsum of surviving terms alone is forbidden.
Each block has at most \(2r\) terms and mass at most \(2S\).
By (3) every other surviving position is within \(2rd\log(2S)/m\) of a reference position, and the two largest positions are fixed.

Otherwise fix two reference lists with \(g_0\ne g_1\).
Set
\[
 F=X^{g_1}-X^{g_0},\qquad
 D_a=X^{g_1}C_0-X^{g_0}C_1,\qquad D_b=C_1-C_0 .
\]
At \(\alpha\) we have \(F\ne0\), \(D_a=Fa\) and \(D_b=Fb\).
Multiply any target row by \(F(\alpha)\) and expand it into monomials, using \(D_a,D_b\) at the two distinguished labels and \(b_iF\) elsewhere.
Keep the original label of each monomial.
The expanded row has at most \(6r\) terms and total mass at most \(6S\).
Partition it into minimal zero sums.
Each such block has span at most \(D=6rd\log(6S)/m\) by (3).

Form a graph on the original labels by joining two labels occurring in a common block.
It is connected, since the blocks in any component sum to \(F(\alpha)\) times the corresponding original subsum and every original term is nonzero.
If a path step goes from \(i\) to \(k\), then \(t_k-t_i\) differs by at most \(D\) from \(s_i-s_k\), with shifts from their respective expansion polynomials.
Take a simple path from the label \(a\) to any target label.
At each intermediate ordinary label the incoming and outgoing shifts are in \(\{g_0,g_1\}\), so their difference is \(0\) or \(\pm(g_1-g_0)\).
The two distinguished labels together have at most four incidences on the path, and any ordinary endpoint uses a single shift.
It follows that the target exponent is within \(rD\) of a number
\[
                         n(g_1-g_0)+s_1+\cdots+s_5 ,
\]
where \(|n|\le r\) and each \(s_i\) is zero or a signed element of
\(\{g_0,g_1\}\cup\operatorname{supp}D_a\cup\operatorname{supp}D_b\).
This set of available shifts has size \(O(r)\), so there are \(O(r^6)\) centers.
This proves (4) in both cases.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the occupied intervals at the degree scale

The [previous arity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v668.md#weight) gives \(r/L\to\infty\).
Its comparison uses only the multiplicity, nondegeneracy, the off-circle radius and \(r\to\infty\), so it applies to the stated full-order count.
It suffices to treat \(r\le m^{-2}\).
Take \(v=2(4+2j)\asymp_{c,\eta}r/L\) and partition the integers into consecutive cells of cardinality \(q=\lfloor d/(2v)\rfloor\).
[Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(\log(3d)>(2/m)^{1/3}\), so \(q\gg d/v\) and the short intervals from the forest have width less than \(q\) for all sufficiently late inputs.
Thus every list in the proportion at least \(1-e^{-r}\) occupies at most \(v\) cells.

After normalizing at \(a\), all possible cells intersect the common union (4).
Using \(q\gg d/v\), \(\log(2S)\ll r\) and \(v\ll r\), their number is at most
\[
                O_{c,\eta}\big(r^6(1+r^2v\log(2S)/m)\big)
                      \ll_{c,\eta} r^{10}/m .
\]
Selecting at most \(v\) cells from these choices costs
\(\exp(O_{c,\eta}(v(L+\log r)))=\exp(O_{c,\eta}(r))\) in our range.
For each chosen union the sum of its widths is at most \(v(q-1)<d\).
The [fixed-union count below the degree](Yin_C_G0345_v662.md#statement) bounds the translation classes it contains by \((Cv)^rH_2^2\).
Consequently
\[
        (1-e^{-r})H_2^2(c/m)^{r-2}
                           \le e^{O_{c,\eta}(r)}v^r H_2^2 .
\]
Taking \(r\)-th roots and using \(L/r\to0\) gives \(v\gg_{c,\eta}1/m\), which proves (1).
