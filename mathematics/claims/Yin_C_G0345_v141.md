# A strict Mahler descent with two additional exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree-sixteen Salem input and its degree-fourteen output

Put
\[
 H=X^7+X^6-7X^5-6X^4+14X^3+8X^2-8X-1,\qquad S=(X-2)H-1.
\]
The lift \(P=z^8 S(z+z^{-1})\) is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma\).
At the integer level \(-1\) its full shifted polynomial is
\[
 P+z^8=(z-1)^2 F,\qquad
 F=z^{14}+z^{13}-z^9-z^8-z^7-z^6-z^5+z+1 .
\]
The polynomial \(F\) is irreducible and reciprocal.
It has three strictly exterior roots, a positive real root \(\tau\) and a nonreal conjugate pair.
The trace of \(\tau\) is the rightmost root of \(S+1\), and it is strictly smaller than the exterior trace of \(S\).
The following rational bounds separate the house contribution of the positive root, the complete Mahler measure and the input:
\[
      11/10<\tau<9/8<5/4<M(F)=M(P+z^8)<4/3<\sigma=M(P).
\]
In particular both the complete shifted polynomial and the distinguished factor give strict descent with positive leakage \(\log M(F)-\log\tau\).
For scale, \(\tau\approx1.11548,\ M(F)\approx1.30786,\ \sigma\approx1.36650\).
The contributor found this example by searching trace polynomials in the Chebyshev basis and testing integral cofactors.
The proof below verifies this one input by rational arithmetic, without a completeness claim for the search.

<a id="trace"></a>
## The two trace fields

For \(S=X^8-X^7-9X^6+8X^5+26X^4-20X^3-24X^2+15X+1\), apply the finite-field irreducibility test at \(p=2\).
For \(H\) use \(p=3\).
The table records polynomial congruences in \(\mathbb F_p[X]\) modulo the indicated reduction \(g\), with \(D=\deg g\).
In both rows \(X^{p^D}\equiv X\), and \(U V\equiv1\).

| \(p,\ D\) | \(g\) | \(U=X^{p^{D/\ell}}-X\bmod g\), \(\ell\) the unique prime dividing \(D\) | \(V\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(2,8\) | \(X^8+X^7+X^6+X+1\) | \(X^4+X^3+X^2+1\) | \(X^5+X+1\) |
| \(3,7\) | \(X^7+X^6-X^5-X^3-X^2+X-1\) | \(X^3-X\) | \(X^4+X^3+X-1\) |

The congruences follow by successive \(p\)-th powers and polynomial remainder.
The first congruence puts every irreducible factor degree among the divisors of \(D\), with no repetitions, and the inverse of \(U\) excludes all proper divisors of \(D\).
Both monic trace polynomials are consequently irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).

At the ordered nodes
\[
 -2,\ -11/6,\ -3/2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 5/3,\ 2,\ 25/12,\ 3
\]
the signs of \(S\) are \(+,-,+,-,+,-,+,-,-,+\).
Degree eight accounts for seven distinct roots in \((-2,2)\) and one in \((25/12,3)\).
The lift has degree sixteen and is irreducible because its quadratic discriminant is negative at a neutral real embedding of the trace field, so is not a square in that field.
It has the Salem signature and \(\sigma>4/3\), since \(4/3+3/4=25/12\).

The signs of \(H\) at \(-3/2,-1,0,1,3/2\) are \(-,+,-,+,-\).
Thus at least four distinct traces give eight unit roots of \(F=z^7H(z+z^{-1})\).
Here too a real embedding sends the discriminant \(X^2-4\) to a negative number, proving that the lift is irreducible of degree fourteen.
Evaluation gives \(F(11/10)<0<F(9/8)\), yielding a positive exterior root \(\tau\) and its reciprocal.

## A certified nonreal quartet and the complete product

Let \(c=(-1012+386i)/1000\) and \(r=1/1000\).
Direct rational evaluation gives \(|\Re F(c)|<16/10000,\ |\Im F(c)|<5/10000,\ \Im F'(c)<-4\).
For example these tests use the coefficients displayed in the statement, with each multiplication by \(c\) sending the real and imaginary parts \((a,b)\) to \((-1012a-386b,386a-1012b)/1000\).
Since \(|c|+r<11/10\), the Taylor remainder on \(|z-c|=r\) after the linear term satisfies
\[
  |F(z)-F(c)-F'(c)(z-c)|
      \le 279(11/10)^{12}r^2<1/1000 .
\]
Here \(279=\sum_j |[z^j]F|\binom{j}{2}\), and the bound follows by integrating the second derivative along the segment.
The sum of this error and \(|F(c)|\) is less than \(4r\), whereas \(|F'(c)(z-c)|>4r\).
Rouché's theorem gives one root \(\rho\) in the disk.
It satisfies \(\Re\rho<-1.011\), \(\Im\rho>0.385\).
Consequently \(\rho,\overline\rho,\rho^{-1},\overline\rho^{-1}\) are distinct, two exterior and two interior.
Together with the eight neutral roots and the positive real inverse pair already located, these account for the full degree.
The only trace root of \(S+1=(X-2)H\) above two is therefore \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\).

The other two exterior conjugates give \(M(F)=\tau|\rho|^2\).
The rectangle containing the disk gives the rational inequalities
\[
    \tfrac{11}{10}\bigl(1+ (385/1000)^2\bigr)
       <M(F)<
    \tfrac98\bigl((1013/1000)^2+(387/1000)^2\bigr).
\]
The lower and upper bounds lie strictly above \(5/4\) and strictly below \(4/3\), respectively.
The remaining lift factor \((z-1)^2\) has measure one, proving the full comparison.
