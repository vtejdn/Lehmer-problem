# The first two measures of reducible integer polynomials near one

<a id="statement"></a>
## The low range, the escape estimate and what lies strictly between the endpoints

Let \(P\ne0\) be any integer polynomial, \(A=M(P)>1\) and \(m=\log A\).
Fix \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
Whenever \(m\) is sufficiently small in terms of \(\eta\), one has either
\[
                   A\ \le\ M(A)\ \le\ A^2,                              \tag{1}
\]
or
\[
                  \log M(A)\ \ge\
                        \exp\!\big(\eta\,m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).         \tag{2}
\]
There is no common bound on the number, degrees or multiplicities of the irreducible factors.
In (1), equality at the lower endpoint makes \(A\) a real Salem number.
Every value strictly above the lower endpoint in (1) is an even power of a positive reciprocal Perron unit in its own field.
If \(A<M(A)<A^2\), one can obtain a real Salem number \(\sigma\) in the divisible group of the roots of a *single* irreducible factor of \(P\), with
\[
                       1<\sigma<A,\qquad \deg\sigma<\deg A .             \tag{3}
\]
The strict interior requires at least two positive-measure irreducible factors counted with multiplicity.

Consequently failure of a uniform gap for integer polynomials along a sequence with bounded second iterates is equivalent to failure on the union of real Salem numbers and the real reciprocal Perron units with the one exceptional oriented block and torsion half products of the [single-input reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#statement).
Here a bounded second iterate means that \(\log M(M(P))\) has a common finite upper bound along the sequence.
The result does not exclude (2) along an unrestricted shrinking sequence.

<a id="factors"></a>
## Collecting the factors that contribute to the leading constituent

Factor \(P\) as in the [inverse theorem for reducible inputs](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules), with positive factors counted with multiplicity.
Write \(A_h=M(w_h)\) for their individual measures, so \(A=\prod_h A_h\).
They are positive Perron units.
An automorphism fixing \(A\) must fix each \(A_h\): all conjugate moduli are bounded by their individual values there, and equality in the product forces equality separately. [Perron exterior product](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field)
Thus every \(\mathbb Q(A_h)\) is contained in the actual field \(\mathbb Q(A)\), and \(A\) and all \(A_h\) are compressed.

Use the [actual combined direct sum](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules) of pairwise inequivalent leading constituents for the factor chains, identifying any repeated constituent as an actual subspace.
Choose the constituent \(W\) of \([A]\) attached to a term \(z\) of largest compressed degree among its active constituents.
If \(A\) does not match \(z\), (2) follows from the [quantitative unmatched estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#quantitative) and its degree inversion.
In a match let \(\rho>1\) be the positive primitive unit on this real line.
Then \(A=\rho^k\), \(k\ge1\), and \(\rho\) is reciprocal and Perron; every positive power of \(\rho\) generates the field of \(A\).
Its degree \(D\) equals that of \(z\), and \(m(\rho)\le m\). [Saturation at a match](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#match)

Let \(S\) consist of those \(h\) for which the projection of \([A_h]\) to \(W\) is nonzero.
At least one such index is present, since \([A]=\sum_h[A_h]\) has nonzero projection.
In each such factor's chain the term giving \(W\) has degree \(D\), by matching of the leading terms with the same constituent.
It is the first active term for \([A_h]\) in that chain.
Indeed an earlier active term would have strictly larger degree, and the orbit of its nonzero leading projection has size at least that degree by the [minimum orbit bound on each chain space](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#rank).
This would give \(\deg A_h>D\), contrary to the field inclusion.

At every preceding stage the projection of \([A_h]\) therefore vanishes, and the [lossless exterior-product descent](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#line) makes \(A_h\) a positive integral power of the measure of the term giving \(W\).
This use of the descent only needs vanishing of these projections and imposes no measure ceiling on \(A_h\) as an algebraic number.
That term is compressed and matched to \(z\); in the common real embedding it is a signed conjugate of a nonzero power of \(\rho\).
Hence each \(A_h\) for \(h\in S\) is a positive integral power of \(M(\rho)\).
For some integer \(e\ge1\) this gives
\[
                    A=\rho^k=M(\rho)^e B,\qquad
                    B=\prod_{h\notin S} A_h\ge1.                         \tag{4}
\]
The empty product is one and the class of \(B\) has zero projection to \(W\).

<a id="interval"></a>
## The two possible projected coordinate differences

The signed or oriented coordinate model of \(z\) also works in the coordinates of \(\rho\): the original actual roots are, up to common sign, the same nonzero integral powers of the conjugates of \(\rho\), and both have full degree \(D\).
Choose the exterior coordinate \(\rho\) itself.
In these coordinates the projection of \([M(\rho)]\) to \(W\) comes from the usual exterior coefficients \(1,0,-1\).
Equation (4) projects this vector to \(k/e\) times the coordinate of \(\rho\).
In the independent signed case the coefficient is one.
In the oriented case subtracting \((k/e)\) at that coordinate leaves constant coefficients on each block, since the blockwise sum-zero spaces embed in the true module.
Thus \(k/e\) is the difference between the entry \(1\) at \(\rho\) and another entry in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) on its block.
It is positive and therefore one or two. [Oriented projection](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#oriented)

If \(k=e\), taking logs in (4) and using \(M(\rho)\ge\rho\) forces \(B=1\) and \(M(\rho)=\rho\).
The degree is large and \(\rho\) is reciprocal, so \(\rho\) and \(A\) are Salem.
If \(k=2e\), put \(\Theta=\rho^2/M(\rho)\ge1\), so \(B=\Theta^e\).
This gives \(M(\rho)\le\rho^2\), and full degree of powers gives \(A\le M(A)=M(\rho)^k\le A^2\).
Equality \(M(A)=A\) itself implies \(A\) is Salem, since it is reciprocal of large degree here.
In the strict interior \(B>1\), and both \(S\) and its complement have a positive-measure factor.

<a id="interior"></a>
## A smaller polynomial value at every strict interior step

Suppose \(A<M(A)<A^2\), so \(k=2e\) and \(\Theta>1\).
The model is oriented, its selected half contains \(\rho>1\), and each of its other \(b-1\) roots lies strictly inside the circle by the coefficient difference of two.
The other blocks have constant exterior coefficients.
Write \(R=\log\rho\), \(I>0\) for the absolute sum of the \(b-1\) interior logs in the selected half, and \(J\ge0\) for the log mass of the exterior roots of all the other blocks.
Then \(m(\rho)=R+I+J<2R\).
The product \(\beta\) of the selected half is real, because conjugation fixes \(\rho\) and therefore its half.
Put \(r_1=|\beta|=\exp(R-I)\).
Its log modulus is strictly larger than \(J\), and \(1<r_1<\rho\).
The opposite half has reciprocal modulus, and each other half has absolute log modulus at most \(J\).
It follows that \(r_1\) is Perron, all \(2u\) half products are distinct as algebraic numbers, and the degree of \(r_1\) is \(2u<D=2ub\).
Indeed the maximal modulus occurs in just one half and transitivity gives every actual half product the same multiplicity.
Their total exterior log mass is \(R-I+J\), since the remaining blocks have constant exterior sign.
With signs harmless in measures, this proves
\[
                   \Theta=\frac{r_1^2}{M(r_1)},\qquad
                   1\le\frac{\log M(r_1)}{\log r_1}<2 .
\]
The same degree also proves that \(r_1\) generates its field under all positive powers, by its strict Perron property.
Both inverse halves occur; up to the rational sign used to make \(r_1>0\), their products are actual reciprocal conjugates.

It follows from \(B=\Theta^e\) that \(A_1=r_1^{2e}=M(r_1)^e B\) is again a Mahler value of an integer polynomial.
For example take \(e\) copies of the minimal polynomial of \(r_1\) and the factors previously supplying \(B\).
It satisfies
\[
              1<A_1<A,\qquad \deg A_1<\deg A,\qquad
                         A_1\le M(A_1)<A_1^2 .
\]
If \(J=0\), \(M(r_1)=r_1\) and this reciprocal unit has large degree in the small-measure regime by coefficient finiteness, so it is Salem.
Otherwise (1) is again strict at both ends.
The thresholds above are uniform: every new value is closer to one, and its second log measure is at most twice its first log measure, excluding the growing alternative.
Repeat on the new polynomial while the inequalities are strict.
At each repetition the selected primitive line unit belongs to the group of its target because a positive power of it equals the target.
The next half product and its powers then belong to this same divisible conjugate group.
In particular all subsequent values remain in \(D(\rho)\) for the first \(\rho\), hence in the group of one original factor whose chain provided its line.
Degrees strictly decrease, so the process reaches the lower endpoint and gives (3).

Finally, if \(M(A)=A^2\) in the matched alternative, \(k=2e\) and \(M(\rho)=\rho^2\).
On a sequence approaching one these \(\rho\)'s have bounded second log measure (their powers have full degree) and \(0<m(\rho)\le m\).
The [single-input minimal-degree reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal) supplies the stated two classes with measure no greater than the original.
Both classes themselves yield integer minimal polynomials of bounded second iterate along any shrinking sequence.
Together with the lower endpoint and the strict interior descent this gives both directions of the claimed gap equivalence.
