# At most nine large primes for excess at cubic contacts

<a id="statement"></a>
## A factorwise alternative along steep sections

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)=SM\) be the two-pole section along a primitive sequence with \(k\to\infty\), \(l/k\to\infty\), and take a rational irreducible factor \(S\) of degree \(d=o(l)\).
Write \(N=3+k+l\), \(L=\log N\), \(A=d\log(l/d)\), and use the [degree-18 branch form](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#statement) \(H(k,l)\).
For the finite set of excluded projective directions, eventual avoidance follows from \(l/k\to\infty\).
A contact between a root of \(S\) and a root of \(M\) in the same residue disk contributes its logarithmic distance \(v_p(z-w)\log p\) to the logarithm of the absolute complementary resultant.
Define the ordinary contribution \(W\) as the sum of these terms in all multiplicity-two disks outside the fixed finite exceptional set of v822.
At primes outside the [degree-56 form](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) this contribution admits the [eight-integer upper bound and trace square test](Yin_C_G0345_v823.md#statement), since all factors are eventually reciprocal by the [factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#factors).
At the remaining ordinary disks the [quadratic local test](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local) still applies.

There exists a set \(\mathcal P\) of at most nine rational primes, depending on the section, with
\[
 \begin{gathered}
   p^2\mid H(k,l),\qquad p\ge d/L-1,\qquad
   g_p>d/L,\qquad g_p\mid p-1\quad\text{or}\quad g_p\mid p+1,\\
   W+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P} E_p\ \ge\ (1-o(1))A .
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here \(g_p\) is the common multiplicative order of the two mutually inverse triple residue roots, and \(E_p\) is their actual excess as defined below.
In each geometric triple-root disk at such a prime, only one of the three unordered root pairs can supply excess.
It can do so only when an explicit normalized quadratic splits over the unramified residue-point field and its two roots belong to different rational factors.
In particular, if \(W=o(A)\), at least one of these primes supplies at least \((1/9-o(1))A\) in excess.
If \(\mathcal P\) is empty, the ordinary disks must supply \((1-o(1))A\).

<a id="pair"></a>
## The unique close pair inside a triple disk

Work outside the fixed excluded primes and use the depressed cubic \(X^3+aX+b\) and \(r=v_p(H(k,l))\) from the [cubic disk](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#cubic).
Only its resonant case, with \(r=2m\), \(v_p(a)=2m\), \(v_p(b)=3m\), requires extra terms.
Divide the coordinate by \(p^m\), giving the monic polynomial \(Y^3+A_0Y+B_0\) with \(A_0=a/p^{2m}\), \(B_0=b/p^{3m}\) units in the unramified integer ring \(O\).
All three roots are integral in the scaled coordinate, so every original pair has valuation at least \(m\).
Any repeated residue is exactly double, since the residue characteristic exceeds three and \(A_0\) is a unit.
There is at most one such residue and it belongs to the residue field of \(O\) by uniqueness, as does the remaining simple residue.
Hensel lifting the simple root factors off a monic quadratic over \(O\), whose nonzero discriminant is denoted by \(\delta\).
Its two roots have difference of valuation \(v_p(\delta)/2\) in the scaled coordinate, while both differences from the simple root have valuation zero.
Thus each original shared pair contributes a baseline \(m\log p\), and the formerly double pair alone contributes the excess \(\tfrac12 v_p(\delta)\log p\) when separated by \(S,M\).
Without a repeated scaled residue the excess is zero.
Separation requires \(\delta\) to be a square in \(\operatorname{Frac}(O)\), equivalently an even valuation and square leading residue, since an irreducible quadratic over that field has both roots in one rational factor.
Let \(E_p\) be the sum of these actual excess terms in the two geometric disks at \(p\), including zero terms when separation fails.
In a partition of three roots there are at most two shared pairs, so the baseline in both disks is at most \(4m\log p=2r\log p\).
Together with the nonresonant bound at most \(3r\log p\) per prime of [v822](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#statement), the total baseline and nonresonant contributions are \(O(\log N)\), because \(\log|H(k,l)|\le18\log N+O(1)\).
The finite excluded primes together contribute \(O(L^3)\) by the [fixed-prime bound](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#statement).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Large orders on the branch locus

The two branch points above a rational branch value are inverses and form a Frobenius-stable pair by the [branch calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#map).
If either yields a section root \(x\), so does the other, and their residue coordinates are \(x,x^{-1}\) by the integral torus parametrization.
Frobenius fixes or interchanges them, so their common multiplicative order \(g_p\) divides \(p-1\) or \(p+1\).
The derivative bound of the [residue-order argument](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#valuation) gives \(v_p(P'(z))\ll_F g_p L^4\) for each of the at most six roots at these points.
For \(p\ge N^{1/2}\) that same argument gives the stronger \(v_p(P'(z))\ll_F g_p\).
Indeed the critical approximation incurs an additive \(O_F(L/\log p)\), and the independent-input estimate is \(O_F(g_p(1+L/\log p)^4)\).
In rank one the height term is \(O_F(g_p L/\log p)\) and the exponent term is \(O_F(\log(NL)/\log p)\), while torsion values are bounded.
For each root, the derivative valuation bounds the sum of all its contact valuations, since all roots are integral and distinct.

Summing over primes of \(H\) with \(p<N^{1/2}\), we get total cubic contribution \(O_F(N^{1/2}L^5)\) by \(g_p\le p+1\) and \(\sum_{p\mid H}\log p=O(L)\).
At the remaining primes with \(g_p\le d/L\), the stronger bound gives a total \(O_F(d)\).
Both errors are \(o(A)\), since the [single-root degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) implies \(d=l^{1+o(1)}\), and \(l/d\to\infty\).
Let \(\mathcal P\) consist of the remaining resonant primes with positive actual excess.
Then \(p\ge g_p-1>d/L-1\), so \(\log p\ge(1-o(1))\log N\) uniformly on this set.
Each such prime contributes at least \(2\log p\) to \(\log|H(k,l)|\).
Ten distinct such primes would contribute \((20-o(1))\log N\), contradicting the degree-18 upper bound.
The same counting argument gives at most \(\lfloor D/h\rfloor\) such primes for any nonzero value of a fixed homogeneous binary form of degree \(D\) at \((k,l)\), when each has valuation at least the positive integer \(h\) and logarithm at least \((1-o(1))\log N\).
Finally the [factor resultant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#resultant) gives total logarithmic contribution at least \(A-O(d)\), proving the inequality after the discarded contributions above.
