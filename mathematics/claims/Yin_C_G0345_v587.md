# A lower-measure Salem or quadratic unit from two reciprocal circles

<a id="statement"></a>
## Descent at the square of the outer radius

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) whose every positive integral power has degree \(d\).
Suppose that the absolute values of its conjugates are precisely \(R\) and \(R^{-1}\), where \(R>1\).
Then \(b=R^2\) is a Salem number or a real reciprocal quadratic unit, and
\[
       [\mathbb Q(b):\mathbb Q]\le d,\qquad
       0<\log M(b)=2\log R=\frac4d\log M(\alpha).
\]
In particular a unit attaining the least positive measure among all units of degree at most \(d\), strictly below all smaller-degree values, cannot have these two reciprocal radii when \(d>4\).
On any shrinking sequence of such reciprocal record minima, the difference between the largest and smallest of the numbers \(\lvert\log|\beta|\rvert\) over conjugates \(\beta\) is therefore strictly positive eventually.

Dubickas and Smyth's Lemma 8.1 finds a power \(R^{2n}\) that is extended Salem or reciprocal quadratic and bounds its degree by \(\deg(\alpha^n)\) in the reciprocal case. [Published lemma and choice of power](../audits/literature/DS01/Yin_U001.md#power)
Here preservation of the degree of every power allows the specific choice \(n=1\), which determines the cost in Mahler measure.

<a id="stabilizer"></a>
## Images of the product of a conjugate and its complex conjugate

Let \(E\) and \(I\) be the sets of exterior and interior conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Inversion interchanges them, so \(|E|=|I|=d/2\).
Two different conjugates cannot have a root of unity as quotient, since they would coincide after a common positive power, lowering its degree.
If a scalar \(t\) multiplies either one of these finite nonempty sets onto itself, some positive power of the permutation is the identity, so \(t\) is a root of unity and hence \(t=1\).

For \(x\in E\) the product \(x\bar x=b\) is an algebraic unit, and \(b/E=E\) as sets since complex conjugation preserves \(E\).
Similarly \(b^{-1}/I=I\).
For any automorphism \(\sigma\) of the normal closure, every \(u\in E\) pairs with \(b/u\in E\) to represent \(\sigma(b)\) as the product of their images.
Consequently \(|\sigma(b)|\) belongs to \(\{R^2,1,R^{-2}\}\).
If its modulus is \(R^2\), both images of each pair are exterior, whence \(\sigma(E)=E\) and \(\sigma(b)/E=E\).
Comparison with \(b/E=E\) shows that \(\sigma(b)/b\) stabilizes \(E\), so \(\sigma(b)=b\).
If its modulus is \(R^{-2}\), then \(\sigma(E)=I\) and \(\sigma(b)/I=I\).
Comparison with \(b^{-1}/I=I\) now gives \(\sigma(b)=b^{-1}\).
The rational norm of the unit \(b\) has absolute value one, so \(b^{-1}\) occurs.
These are its only conjugates off the unit circle.
This proves the assertion about the type of \(b\) and its measure.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Counting the degree using ordered pairs

Among all ordered pairs \((u,v)\) of conjugates of \(\alpha\), exactly \(d/2\) have product \(b\).
These are \((u,b/u)\) for \(u\in E\), including any diagonal pairs.
Conjugation gives the same multiplicity for every conjugate of \(b\).
If \(D=[\mathbb Q(b):\mathbb Q]\), its \(D-2\) conjugates on the unit circle use \((D-2)d/2\) distinct ordered pairs.
Every one of those pairs joins \(E\) to \(I\) in one of the two orders, giving \(d^2/2\) available pairs.
The \(d\) ordered inverse pairs have product one, which cannot be a conjugate of \(b>1\).
It follows that \((D-2)d/2\le d^2/2-d\), or \(D\le d\).
Since \(M(\alpha)=R^{d/2}\), the claimed ratio follows.

At a strict minimum as in the statement, the [relative norm into a power field](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent) would give a nontorsion unit of degree less than \(d\) and measure at most \(M(\alpha)\) if any power lost degree.
Thus the degree hypothesis holds there.
For \(d>4\) the constructed unit has degree at most \(d\) and strictly smaller positive log measure, contradicting minimality even when \(D=d\).
Bounded degree permits only finitely many units of bounded measure, so degrees on any shrinking sequence tend to infinity.
Equality of the absolute logarithmic radii in the reciprocal case would put all conjugates on precisely the two circles in the statement.
The [radial dilation bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v308.md#spread) consequently applies at each sufficiently late reciprocal record, with its quantitative dependence on the spread still present.
