# Square field radicals in translated intervals of Salem delays

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds at one seed and uniformly in the seed

For integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_{a,n,\epsilon}(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Let \(E_a(M,N)\) count the integers \(M<n\le M+N\) at which \(r^{1/2}\in\mathbb Q(r)\) for at least one sign, where \(M\ge6,\ N\ge1\) are integers.
At every other delay in the interval, both signs give a minimal polynomial of \(r\) irreducible under every substitution \(X\mapsto X^{2^i}\), by the [positive field radical formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).

If \(a\equiv3\bmod4\), put \(A=a+1\), let \(\rho(A)\) be the number of solutions to \(x^2=1\bmod A\), and let \(K\) count the odd delays in the interval.
For positive absolute computable constants \(C_1,C_2\),
\[
 E_a(M,N)\le
 \min\!\left\{\rho(A)\!\left(1+\sqrt{N/(2A)}\right),
              C_1 K^{3/5}(\log(2K))^{C_2}\right\}                    \tag{1}
\]
when \(K\ge1\), and \(E_a(M,N)=0\) when \(K=0\).
In particular \(E_a(M,N)=o(N)\) uniformly in the seed in this class and the starting delay.
For each fixed \(c>0\), uniformly over \(A\le N^c\) and all \(M\), the first bound gives
\[
                  E_a(M,N)\le (2/\sqrt3+o_c(1))\sqrt N.               \tag{2}
\]
The constant \(2/\sqrt3\) is attained asymptotically by the integer square test alone at \(a=23,\ M=6\).

For every seed outside the class in (1), \(E_a(M,N)=0\) unless the seed and its only possible sign and delay parity occur in one of these rows:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \text{seed}&\epsilon&n& t\\ \hline
 a=w^2-1,\ w\ge3\ \text{odd} &+1&\text{even}&n\\
 a=2w^2-1,\ w\ge3\ \text{odd}&-1&\text{odd}&n+1 .
 \end{array}                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Define \(h_\ell=v_2(\ell-1)\) for primes \(\ell\equiv1\bmod4\), and \(h_\ell=1+v_2(\ell+1)\) for \(\ell\equiv3\bmod4\).
In the first row set
\[
 b=\min\big(\{h_\ell-1:\ell\mid a-1\}\cup\{h_\ell:\ell\mid a+1\}\big),
\]
and in the second set \(b=\min_{\ell\mid a}(h_\ell-1)\), with only prime divisors used in both definitions.
Then a square field radical in either row requires \(2^b\nmid t\).
Consequently throughout every translated interval
\[
                  E_a(M,N)\le (1/2-2^{-b})N+2 .                       \tag{4}
\]
Each displayed minimum is over nonempty finite sets of odd primes and \(b\ge1\).
The rows give necessary conditions, and an independent pointwise test may remove additional seeds or delays.
For example, [perfect-power coprimality](Yin_C_G0345_v729.md#statement) excludes any square seed in the second row.

For every \(L\ge2,\ H\ge1\) integral, write \(T(L,H;M,N)=\sum_{L<a\le L+H}E_a(M,N)\).
Let \(\psi(J)=J^{-2/5}(\log(2J))^{C_2}\), with \(C_2\) as in (1).
The estimate
\[
       \frac{T(L,H;M,N)}{HN}\ll
                H^{-1/2}+\min\{\psi(H),\psi(N)\}                       \tag{5}
\]
has an absolute implied constant for every \(H,N\), independently of both translations \(L,M\).
In particular, at any single delay at most \(O(H^{3/5}(\log(2H))^{C_2})\) of any \(H\) consecutive seeds admit a square field radical for either sign.
Thus the exceptional proportion tends to zero uniformly as \(H\to\infty\), even with the delay chosen as a function of both seed endpoints.

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## The square progression at the odd plus sign

The [parity and norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v718.md#two) requires \(P(-1)\) to be an integer square for the minimal polynomial \(P\) of \(r\) whenever a square field radical exists.
It also shows that an odd seed allows only odd \(n\), with sign minus when \(a\equiv1\bmod4\) and plus when \(a\equiv3\bmod4\).
At the former seed \(P(-1)=2(a+1)\), forcing \(a=2w^2-1\) with \(w\) odd.
At an even seed the [pointwise square test](Yin_C_G0345_v716.md#squares) gives exactly the necessary seed, sign and parity in the first row of (3).

At \(a\equiv3\bmod4\) write an odd delay as \(n=2k+1\).
The evaluation from the odd plus-sign case is
\[
             P(-1)=((a+1)n+1-a)/2=1+A k .
\]
Thus possible delays correspond to square values in one arithmetic progression of length \(K\) with nonzero difference \(A\).
The [uniform square-count theorem](../audits/literature/BZ02/Yin_U001.md#bz02-u001) gives the second bound in (1) for every translate and every \(A\).

The positive square root \(x\) of \(1+A k\) must belong to a root class modulo \(A\).
As \(M<n\le M+N\), its containing interval has length at most \(\sqrt{AN/2}\) by \(\sqrt{u+v}-\sqrt u\le\sqrt v\).
Each of the \(\rho(A)\) classes gives at most \(1+\sqrt{N/(2A)}\) choices.
For fixed \(A\) and \(M=6\), counting in these same residue classes up to \(\sqrt{1+A\lfloor(M+N-1)/2\rfloor}\) shows that the necessary square condition holds at \((\rho(A)/\sqrt{2A})\sqrt N+O_A(1)\) delays.

The function \(\rho\) is multiplicative by the Chinese remainder theorem.
For an odd prime power \(\ell^j\), the two factors \(x-1,x+1\) cannot both be divisible by \(\ell\), giving exactly two solutions.
At \(2^2\) there are two solutions, and at \(2^j,\ j\ge3\), one of \(x-1,x+1\) must be divisible by \(2^{j-1}\), since their common divisor for odd \(x\) is two.
The two classes modulo \(2^{j-1}\) each give two roots modulo \(2^j\).
As \(4\mid A\), its dyadic contribution to \(\rho(A)/\sqrt A\) is at most \(\sqrt2\), attained at eight.
At odd primes only \(\ell=3\) can give a factor \(2/\sqrt{\ell^j}>1\), maximized at \(j=1\).
Hence \(\rho(A)/\sqrt A\le\sqrt{8/3}\), with equality at \(A=24\).
For each \(\delta>0\), \(\rho(A)\ll_\delta A^\delta\) by charging \(2\le\ell^\delta\) at all sufficiently large odd primes and allowing a constant for all the others and the dyadic factor.
Taking \(\delta=1/(4c)\) bounds the additive term in (1) by \(O_c(N^{1/4})\) when \(A\le N^c\).
This proves (2) and the stated attainment for the necessary test.

<a id="two-orders"></a>
## Orbit obstructions within the remaining parity

For each odd prime \(\ell\) and \(s\ge h_\ell\),
\[
                 \operatorname{ord}_{2^{s+1}}(\ell)
                    =2\operatorname{ord}_{2^s}(\ell).                  \tag{6}
\]
For \(\ell\equiv1\bmod4\), an odd power leaves \(v_2(\ell-1)\) unchanged and each squaring raises the valuation of the difference from one by exactly one, since the sum with one has valuation one.
Thus \(v_2(\ell^j-1)=v_2(\ell-1)+v_2(j)\) for positive \(j\).
For \(\ell\equiv3\bmod4\), odd \(j\) give valuation one, while for even \(j\) the same calculation starting from \(\ell^2\) gives \(v_2(\ell^j-1)=v_2(\ell+1)+v_2(j)\).
These formulas yield (6) including its first allowed \(s\).

In the first row of (3), reduction modulo \(\ell\mid a-1\) gives \((X-1)(X^n+1)\).
A primitive root of order \(2^s\), \(s=1+v_2(n)\), belongs to that binomial and has multiplicity \(\ell^{v_\ell(n)}\).
Whenever \(v_2(n)\ge h_\ell-1\), it has order at least four and odd multiplicity.
Reduction modulo \(\ell\mid a+1\) gives \((X+1)(X^n-1)\).
Here choose \(s=v_2(n)\) when \(v_2(n)\ge h_\ell\); the resulting order is again at least four and the multiplicity is \(\ell^{v_\ell(n)}\).
In the second row, reduction modulo \(\ell\mid a\) gives \(X^{n+1}+1\), with the same first choice for \(s=1+v_2(n+1)\) and multiplicity \(\ell^{v_\ell(n+1)}\).
In every case the order is larger than two under the stated divisibility condition, so removing the linear cyclotomic factors at \(1,-1\) leaves the target multiplicity intact by the [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots).
By (6) the [Frobenius orbit criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v717.md#statement) at dilation two forces the minimal polynomial of \(r\) to remain irreducible after squaring its variable.
The [field radical criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) therefore excludes the square field radical.
Taking the minimum of the available thresholds excludes all multiples of \(2^b\) in the respective even argument \(t\).
In any interval of \(N\) consecutive integers there are at most \(N/2+1\) even integers and at least \(N/2^b-1\) multiples of \(2^b\), all even.
This gives (4).

<a id="rectangles"></a>
## Counting along either coordinate

The numbers \(w\) in each row of (3) whose seed lies in \((L,L+H]\) form a subset of an interval of length at most \(\sqrt H\), by the square-root difference bound used above.
Thus these two rows together contribute \(O((1+\sqrt H)N)\) to \(T\).
The remaining possible pairs are confined to \(a=4u-1,\ n=2k+1\), at the plus sign, and require \(1+4uk\) to be a square.
For fixed \(u\) these values as \(k\) varies form an arithmetic progression of length at most \(\lceil N/2\rceil\), and for fixed \(k\) they form an arithmetic progression in \(u\) of length at most \(\lceil H/4\rceil\).
Both differences are nonzero because \(a\ge3\) and \(n\ge7\).
Applying the [square-count estimate](../audits/literature/BZ02/Yin_U001.md#bz02-u001) in each direction and summing bounds that contribution by the smaller of \(O(HN\psi(N))\) and \(O(NH\psi(H))\).
This gives (5), including the asserted single-delay estimate on putting \(N=1\) and using the bound by \(\psi(H)\).
The [density theorem in moving rectangles](Yin_C_G0345_v720.md#statement) concerns all radical primes, with the seed interval beginning at three.
For the prime two, inequality (5) allows both intervals to start at arbitrary positions and the delay interval to consist of a single point.
Whether a positive proportion of delays in either of those rows actually has a square field radical remains undecided by these estimates.
