# Windows for full-conductor Fourier coefficients at Salem periods

<a id="statement"></a>
## The period and the Fourier coefficient

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2s+2\) with monic minimal polynomial \(P\), and write \(m=\log\alpha\).
On \(G_n=\mathbb Z[\alpha]/(\alpha^n-1)\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), let \(\mu_n\) be the distribution of \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\alpha^j\) for independent fair binary digits.
Set
\[
             a=2s(d-1),\qquad \delta_d=\frac{1}{s(a-1)}.
\]
For every fixed rational \(\lambda>1\) and every sufficiently large integer \(N\), there are a period \(n\) and a character \(\chi\) such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 n\equiv1\pmod N,\qquad N+1\le n\le1+N\left\lceil N^{(\lambda-1)/s}\right\rceil^s,\\
 |\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\ge
   \exp\!\left(-C_{P,\lambda} n^{1-2(1-1/\lambda)\delta_d}\right).
 \end{gathered}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The character has exact full conductor, meaning that the \(x\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) with \(\chi(xy)=1\) for every \(y\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) form the ideal \((\alpha^n-1)\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
The input threshold and the amplitude constant in (1) can be computed from \(P,\lambda\).
For \(\lambda=2\), the upper endpoint is at most \(1+2^sN^2\) and the bound is \(\exp(-C_P n^{1-\delta_d})\).

If \(m\le1\), every sufficiently large \(N\) also admits a period \(n\) and a character \(\chi\) of exact full conductor satisfying
\[
  N+1\le n\le1+\left\lceil4\pi N^{B_d}\right\rceil^s,\qquad
  |\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\ge\exp\!\left(-C_P n^{1-2\delta_d}\right),
  \qquad B_d=\kappa d^3\log d.                                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\kappa\ge1\) is the absolute constant in the [accepted logarithmic-form bound](../audits/literature/Dim16/Yin_U001.md#dim16-reported-bound).
This use of the reported bound carries its original proof and numerical-constant inspection limits.
For (2) the timing interval uses that uncalibrated constant and does not impose a congruence on \(n\).
A separate finite upper endpoint computable from \(P,N\) works for the same full saving at every Salem input.
These conclusions locate the [previous infinite sequence of full-conductor amplitudes](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#statement) at prescribed scales and leave the unrestricted Mahler gap open.

<a id="returns"></a>
## Returns at a prescribed scale

Choose one \(z_i\) from each pair of neutral conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For any positive integers \(M,H\), place the first \(H^s+1\) nonnegative multiples of the vector of arguments of \((z_i^M)_{i=1}^s\), in turns, in the \(H^s\) half-open cubes of side \(1/H\) in the torus.
Two points share a cube, so some \(1\le k\le H^s\) satisfies
\[
                       \max_i|z_i^{Mk}-1|\le2\pi/H.
\]
For (1) take \(M=N\), \(H=\lceil N^{(\lambda-1)/s}\rceil\) and \(n=1+Nk\).
With \(c_s=1+2^s\) we have \(n\le c_s N^\lambda\), whence
\[
        \max_i|z_i^{n-1}-1|\le 2\pi c_s^{\theta} n^{-\theta},
        \qquad \theta=(1-1/\lambda)/s.
\]

For the full saving take \(M=1\).
For \(N\ge2\), the number
\[
        g_P(N)=\min_{1\le j<N}\max_{1\le i\le s}|z_i^j-1|
\]
is a positive computable algebraic number, since every \(z_i\) is algebraic and nontorsion.
Choose any integer \(H>2\pi/g_P(N)\), for example by certified upper approximation.
The pigeonhole index \(k\) then satisfies \(N\le k\le H^s\).
At \(n=k+1\) its error is at most \(2\pi(n-1)^{-1/s}\), giving the promised finite upper endpoint \(1+H^s\).

For \(m\le1\), one neutral conjugate \(z_1\) already gives \(|z_1^j-1|\ge j^{-B_d}\) for every \(j\ge2\).
Indeed \(z_1\) is nontorsion of degree \(d\ge4\) and its logarithmic Weil height is \(m/d\le1/d\), as required by the displayed bound in the cited source.
For \(H=\lceil4\pi N^{B_d}\rceil\) we have \(2\pi/H\le N^{-B_d}/2\).
This excludes \(2\le k<N\), and it excludes \(k=1\) once \(N^{-B_d}/2<|z_1-1|\).
Thus (2) again has return exponent \(\theta=1/s\).

<a id="amplitude"></a>
## From the return estimate to the full-conductor amplitude

Suppose \(0<\theta\le1/s\), \(K_0>0\) and \(\max_i|z_i^{n-1}-1|\le K_0 n^{-\theta}\).
Put \(\gamma=\theta/(a-1)\) and \(\varepsilon=A n^{-\gamma}\), with \(A\ge1\) fixed below.
For sufficiently large \(n\), the neutral denominators \(|z_i^n-1|\) exceed half the minimum of \(|z_i-1|\).
Choose a unit \(u\) of the digit order positive at both real embeddings with each neutral image \(O_P(\varepsilon)\) and both real images \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s})\).
The [trace-dual construction](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#conductor) with \(b=u/P'(\alpha)\) gives the exact full conductor, and the absolute Fourier coefficient is the product of the \(n\) absolute cosines of \(\pi\operatorname{Tr}(b\alpha^j/(\alpha^n-1))\).
The lower bounds on the neutral denominators make \(|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^n-1)|\gg_P\alpha^n\) at these periods, so the character is eventually nontrivial.

Use the [two endpoint approximants](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#ends) through \(J=\lceil s\log(1/\varepsilon)/m\rceil+J_0\), with \(J_0\) a sufficiently large fixed integer.
The positive unit and the nontrivial inversion of \(\alpha\) give the same irrationality and the same norm lower bound \(c_P\varepsilon^a\) for the distance of each approximant to the half-integers.
The return estimate enters the endpoint argument only through replacing \(1/(z_i^n-1)\) by \(1/(z_i-1)\) and \(z_i^n/(z_i^n-1)\) by \(z_i/(z_i-1)\).
Both replacements have error \(O_P(K_0 n^{-\theta})\) by the neutral denominator bounds.
After multiplication by the neutral amplitudes, the total endpoint error is \(O_P(K_0\varepsilon n^{-\theta}+\varepsilon^{-2s}\alpha^{-n})\).
Since \(\varepsilon n^{-\theta}/\varepsilon^a=A^{1-a}\), choose \(A\) large enough that the first error is at most \(c_P\varepsilon^a/4\), and then \(n\) large enough for the second error to obey the same bound.
The endpoints contribute \(O_{P,K_0,\theta}((\log n)^2)\) to the negative logarithm.
The [middle geometric tails](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#middle) contribute \(O_P(1+n\varepsilon^2)\).
As \(0<2\gamma\le2/5\), these bounds give \(-\log|\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\le C_{P,K_0,\theta}n^{1-2\gamma}\).
The two choices of \(\theta\) prove (1) and (2).

<a id="constants"></a>
## Computing the constants for the digit order

The [weighted logarithmic unit theorem](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002) applies to the full order \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) of signature \((2,s)\).
The lattice of logarithms of squared units spans the hyperplane of coordinate sum zero, using twice the logarithm of the absolute value at each chosen complex embedding.
Enumerate digit-order elements in the power basis, recognize units by norm \(\pm1\), and square them for positivity.
Test tuples of \(s+1\) resulting logarithm vectors for nonzero determinant after dropping the last coordinate.
Dovetail the tuples and increasing precision in certified real intervals so an inconclusive test cannot block the enumeration.
A full-rank tuple is eventually certified by the lattice theorem.
If its vectors are \(v_i\), take a rational upper bound \(L>\sum_i\|v_i\|_\infty\).
In this real basis round the coordinates of
\[
            \log(1/\varepsilon)\,(s,s,-2,\ldots,-2)
\]
to integers.
The logarithm of the resulting unit differs from the target by at most \(L\) in each coordinate, giving computable constants \(e^L\) in both unit bounds.
For effective approximation one may use integers at distance less than one in each basis coefficient, avoiding any exact rounding tie.

For the remaining constants use strict rational bounds on the distinct roots, the positive distances \(|z_i-1|\), all \(|P'(\beta)|\), and \(\alpha-1\), together with \(L\) and the finite sums and geometric series in the endpoint and middle estimates.
In the [endpoint norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#ends) a denominator for \(f=u/((\alpha-1)P'(\alpha))\) is the positive absolute norm of \((\alpha-1)P'(\alpha)\), since its multiplication matrix on the digit order is integral and its adjugate clears its inverse.
The conjugate upper bounds for the nonzero algebraic integer in that estimate then give a computable positive \(c_P\).
The choices of \(J_0,A\) use strict inequalities between these bounds, and the final threshold only needs geometric decay against fixed powers and the elementary domination of \((\log n)^2\) by \(n^{1-2\gamma}\).
Each admits an upper threshold by elementary monotone estimates.
This supplies the claimed algorithms without a numerical evaluation of unit generators or of the constants.

For each fixed \(d\) and \(m\le1\), the amplitude constants and thresholds can also be bounded uniformly over \(P\), computably in \(d\) in (1) for fixed rational \(\lambda\).
Indeed the absolute value of the coefficient at index \(j\) from the top is at most \(3\binom dj\), by the elementary symmetric functions and \(e^m\le e<3\).
Enumerate these finitely many monic polynomials and test the Salem condition by factorization and real algebraic root isolation, using \(\alpha\le3\) as the outer bound.
The maximum of the computable per-input bounds suffices, with the index-one guard for (2) imposed separately and bounded using \(B_d\ge1\).
This supplies neither a useful numerical growth rate for these constants as \(d\) increases nor a uniform positive lower bound on \(m\).
