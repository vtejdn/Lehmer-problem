# The least coefficient length and sparse tests beyond critical exponent spans

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite bound for the proportion of small evaluations

For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(\alpha\), let \(\lambda(\alpha)\) be the least sum of absolute coefficients of a nonzero integer polynomial vanishing at \(\alpha\).
Write \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\), and let \(h(\alpha)\) be the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Fix distinct exponents \(0=a_1<\cdots<a_s=W\), with \(s\ge2\), and a complex coefficient column \(c\) of Euclidean norm one.
For each embedding \(\sigma\) of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), set \(v_\sigma=(1,(\sigma\alpha)^{a_2},\ldots,(\sigma\alpha)^W)\).
Write \(p_\epsilon\) for the proportion of the \(d\) embeddings with \(|v_\sigma c|\le\epsilon\|v_\sigma\|_2\).
A common shift of the exponents preserves this proportion.
Put
\[
 u=W h(\alpha),\quad
 A_s(u)=\log(s!)+2s u+\tfrac{s}{2}\log s,\quad
 B_s(u)=\log s+2s\log(s!)+2s^2 u .
\]
For \(0\le\epsilon<1/(\mathrm e s)\),
\[
 p_\epsilon\ \le\ \min\!\left\{1,\max\!\left\{
 16\left(\frac{B_s(u)}{\log\lambda(\alpha)}\right)^{1/2^{s-2}},
 \ 2\left(\frac{A_s(u)}{\log(1/(s\epsilon))-1}\right)^{1/s}
 \right\}\right\}.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The second term in the maximum is zero at \(\epsilon=0\).
In particular, an exact hyperplane containing at least \(\eta d\) evaluation rows requires
\[
 B_s(u)\ \ge\ (\eta/16)^{2^{s-2}}\log\lambda(\alpha).                \tag{2}
\]
The result holds for arbitrary complex coefficients in the measured hyperplane.

If the unrestricted Lehmer gap fails, there is a sequence of power-stable units \(\gamma_n\) with \(m(\gamma_n)\to0^+\) for which the following holds.
For any choice of positive integers \(q_n\), put \(\alpha_n=\gamma_n^{q_n}\).
For any bounded number of terms and \(u_n=W_n h(\alpha_n)=o(\log\lambda(\gamma_n))\), the proportions \(p_{\epsilon_n}\) tend to zero whenever \(\log(1/\epsilon_n)/(1+u_n)\to\infty\), including exact zeros.
The scale \(\log\lambda(\gamma_n)\) tends to infinity.
The same reduction applies within the Salem numbers if there is a shrinking sequence of Salem numbers.
Thus proving that every shrinking sequence admits a test on a subsequence with fixed bounded term count, bounded \(Wh\), fixed positive density and thresholds tending to zero would already imply the Lehmer gap.

<a id="length"></a>
## Coefficient length after taking a field norm

Apply the [exact relation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#statement) with density \(0<\eta\le1\) and height budget \(u\).
Its [minor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#minors) permits the threshold
\[
 \epsilon_*=\tfrac1s\exp\bigl(-1-A_s(u)(2/\eta)^s\bigr).
\]
If \(p_{\epsilon_*}\ge\eta\), the [subspace partition](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#blocks) supplies a field \(E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and coefficients \(b_i\in E\) with one \(b_i=1\), satisfying
\[
 \sum_i b_i\alpha^{a_i}=0,\qquad h(b_i)\le2\bigl(\log(s!)+s u\bigr).
\]
The dimension thresholds used there start at \(\tau_{s-1}=\eta/2\) and satisfy \(\tau_{i-1}=\tau_i^2/8\).
Consequently \(\tau_1=8(\eta/16)^{2^{s-2}}\), and \(M=[E:\mathbb Q]\le\lceil2/\tau_1\rceil\le(16/\eta)^{2^{s-2}}\).

Take the product \(G(X)=\prod_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\sum_i\tau(b_i)X^{a_i}\) in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
Every factor is nonzero since the original exponents are distinct and one coefficient is one.
The identity embedding makes \(G(\alpha)=0\).
For a finite place \(v\) of \(E\) above \(p\), use absolute values extending \(|p|_p=p^{-1}\), and put \(n_v=[E_v:\mathbb Q_p]\).
The largest \(p\)-adic coefficient norm of \(G\) is at most \(\prod_{v\mid p}\max_i|b_i|_v^{\,n_v}\) by expansion of the product over the \(p\)-adic embeddings.
Each local maximum is at least one.
Thus the least positive integer clearing all denominators of \(G\) is at most the product of these bounds over the rational primes.
At an infinite place the sum of the absolute coefficients of a product is at most the product of the sums, giving the factor \(s^M\prod_{\tau}\max_i|\tau b_i|\).
Combining the two bounds shows that the logarithm of the length of this integer annihilator is at most
\[
 M\log s+M\sum_i h(b_i)\ \le\ M B_s(u).
\]
Here each local maximum is bounded by the product of the corresponding \(\max(1,|b_i|_v)\), and the archimedean product counts complex embeddings separately.
Consequently \(\log\lambda(\alpha)\le(16/\eta)^{2^{s-2}}B_s(u)\), even if some terms in \(G\) cancel.

<a id="density"></a>
## Inverting the threshold and passing to powers

If \(p_\epsilon>0\), take \(\eta=p_\epsilon\).
When \(\epsilon\le\epsilon_*\), the length bound gives the first term in (1).
Otherwise \(\log(1/(s\epsilon))-1 < A_s(u)(2/\eta)^s\), giving the second term.
At zero threshold only the first alternative occurs.
A one-term normalized evaluation has relative magnitude one.

Under failure of the Lehmer gap the [coefficient-length escape theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#statement) supplies the stated power-stable units with \(\lambda(\gamma_n)\to\infty\), preserving a Salem input class when applicable.
Substitution of \(X^q\) in any annihilator of \(\gamma^q\) preserves its coefficient length and gives an annihilator of \(\gamma\).
Thus (1) applies to \(\alpha_n=\gamma_n^{q_n}\) with \(\lambda(\alpha_n)\ge\lambda(\gamma_n)\) at every chosen power.
Under the two scale assumptions both terms in (1) tend to zero, uniformly over the finitely many admissible term counts.
This conclusion puts no bound on \(q_n m(\gamma_n)\).
