# Cyclotomic compression of rational powers and torsion twists at Mahler measure records

<a id="statement"></a>
## Removing a power substitution from the exact minimal polynomial

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(L=m(\alpha)>0\).
Here \(m(x)=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]h(x)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive minimal polynomial, and \(h(x)\) is the absolute logarithmic height.
Assume that distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) have nontorsion quotient and that the class of \(\alpha\) is primitive in the free abelian group \(\mathcal O_K^\times/\mu(K)\).
The second condition means that it belongs to an integral basis of that group.
Both hypotheses hold for the [least-degree records of positive Mahler measure](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#minimum) on a sequence approaching zero, as recalled below.

Take an algebraic number \(\beta\) satisfying
\[
            \beta^b=\zeta\alpha^a,\qquad b\ge1,\quad
            a\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\quad \gcd(a,b)=1,
\]
with an arbitrary root of unity \(\zeta\).
Put \(H=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(K_c=K\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), \(E=K\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) and \(w_K=|\mu(K)|\).
There are positive integers \(j,s,h\) with \(b=sh\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
 K\subset H,\qquad [H:K]=bj,\qquad m(\beta)=|a|jL,\qquad
 s=[HK_c:K_c],\qquad h\mid w_K,\\
 \gamma=\beta^s,\qquad \gamma^h=\zeta\alpha^a,\qquad
 \mathbb Q(\gamma)=H\cap K_c,\qquad [\mathbb Q(\gamma):K]=hj,\\
 P_\beta(X)=P_\gamma(X^s),\qquad m(\gamma)=m(\beta),              \tag{1}
\end{gathered}
\]
where \(P_x\) denotes the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic integer \(x\).
Thus the normalized support \(\{i/\deg P_\beta:[X^i]P_\beta\ne0\}\) is exactly the normalized support of \(P_\gamma\).
If \(\zeta\in K\) there is the more direct identity \(P_\beta(X)=P_{\zeta\alpha^a}(X^b)\), with \(j=1\).
In particular, extracting an arbitrary root of a record unit gives the irreducible polynomial \(P_\alpha(X^b)\).

If \(K\) has a real embedding, \(h\) is either one or two.
In general a bound on the measure ratio \(m(\beta)/L\) and on \([E:\mathbb Q]\) leaves [finitely many reduced equations](#finite) \(\gamma^h=\zeta\alpha^a\), uniformly over all inputs obeying these bounds.
At equal measure, for a positive real representative and \(E=\mathbb Q\), the only external twists left after (1) can occur if \(2\alpha\) or \(3\alpha\) is a square in \(K\).
The [quadratic classification](#quadratic) gives all of them and their polynomials.

<a id="norm"></a>
## An exact integer measure ratio for every external twist

The distinct conjugates of every nonzero integral power \(\alpha^v\) number exactly \(d\), since a collision would give a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Hence \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^v)=K\).
If \(t\) is the order of \(\zeta\), then \(\beta^{bt}=\alpha^{at}\), proving \(K\subset H\).
Both \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{-1}\) are integral since nonzero positive powers of them are algebraic integer units.
Let \(e=[H:K]\) and \(u=N_{H/K}(\beta)\).
Taking norms after raising to \(t\) gives \(u^{bt}=\alpha^{ate}\).
In the free unit group modulo torsion this gives \(b[u]=ae[\alpha]\).
A basis coordinate taking \([\alpha]\) to one therefore shows \(b\mid e\), so \(e=bj\).
The height equation \(bh(\beta)=|a|h(\alpha)\), unchanged by torsion, gives \(m(\beta)=|a|jL\).
This extends the [relative norm calculation for an untwisted root](../ledger/Yin_L_G0450_v1.md#unit-ray-saturation).
If \(\zeta\in K\), then \(e\le b\) from the original binomial and \(e=b\).
Moreover \(\mathbb Q(\zeta\alpha^a)=K\), since its \(t\)-th power is \(\alpha^{at}\).
Consequently \(P_{\zeta\alpha^a}(X^b)\) is monic of degree \(bd=\deg\beta\) and vanishes at \(\beta\), proving the direct identity.

For the record application, choose the least degree attaining each new minimum positive measure among degrees up to that degree.
Near zero these inputs are units by the integer bounds at both ends.
A torsion quotient of conjugates would give a [smaller-degree polynomial of the same measure](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).
If \([\alpha]\) were divisible by \(k>1\), some unit \(v\in K\) would satisfy \(\alpha=\eta v^k\) with \(\eta\in\mu(K)\), giving \(0<m(v)\le d h(\alpha)/k=L/k\) in degree at most \(d\).
These two contradictions give precisely the stated hypotheses.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Descent through the abelian compositum

The extension \(K_c/K\) is Galois and abelian, since it is the compositum with a Galois abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\), and restriction embeds its Galois group in \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}/\mathbb Q)\).
All roots of unity belong to \(K_c\).
Over \(K_c\) the field \(HK_c=K_c(\beta)\) splits the separable binomial \(T^b-\zeta\alpha^a\), so it is finite Galois.
The map \(\rho\mapsto\rho(\beta)/\beta\) embeds its group into \(\mu_b\) as a subgroup because these roots of unity are fixed by every \(\rho\).
With \(s=[HK_c:K_c]\), the image is the subgroup \(\mu_s\) and \(s\mid b\).
It follows that \(\gamma=\beta^s\) belongs to \(H\cap K_c\) and satisfies the equation in (1) with \(h=b/s\).
Its \(ht\)-th power is \(\alpha^{at}\), hence \(K\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
The inclusions \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\subset H\cap K_c\subset K_c\) and \(\beta^s=\gamma\) give
\[
 s=[K_c(\beta):K_c]\le[H:H\cap K_c]\le[H:\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\le s.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=H\cap K_c\) and its relative degree over \(K\) is \(bj/s=hj\).
Comparing degrees of the monic annihilator \(P_\gamma(X^s)\) and \(P_\beta\) yields the polynomial identity and its normalized support assertion.
Since \(\gamma=\beta^s\) gains a factor \(s\) in height and loses exactly \(s\) in degree, it has the same logarithmic Mahler measure as \(\beta\).

To control the remaining denominator, choose \(\xi\in\mu_\infty\) with \(\xi^h=\zeta\).
From \((\gamma/\xi)^h=\alpha^a\) and \(\gcd(a,h)=1\), Bézout's identity gives a unit \(v\in K_c\) with \(v^h=\alpha\).
For \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_c/K)\) write \(z_\sigma=\sigma(v)/v\in\mu_h\), and choose an integer \(c_\sigma\) representing its action as an exponent on \(\mu_h\).
For \(h=1\) the divisibility claim is immediate, so assume \(h>1\).
Since any two \(\sigma,\tau\) commute,
\[
 z_\tau^{c_\sigma} z_\sigma
     =\sigma\tau(v)/v=\tau\sigma(v)/v=z_\sigma^{c_\tau}z_\tau.
\]
Hence \(v^{c_\sigma-1}/z_\sigma\) is fixed by every \(\tau\) and belongs to \(K\).
Its \(h\)-th power is \(\alpha^{c_\sigma-1}\), so it is a unit in \(K\).
Primitivity of \([\alpha]\) in the free unit group implies \(h\mid c_\sigma-1\), by applying the same basis coordinate as in the norm argument.
Thus every automorphism of \(K_c/K\) is trivial on \(\mu_h\), which means \(\mu_h\subset K\) and \(h\mid w_K\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## Which bounds give finitely many reduced equations?

From \(\zeta=\gamma^h/\alpha^a\) one has \(K(\zeta)\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
If \(m_0=\operatorname{ord}\zeta\), restriction in the finite Galois cyclotomic extension gives
\[
 \varphi(m_0)
    =[K(\zeta):K]\,[K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]
    \le hj[E:\mathbb Q].                                          \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the group of \(K(\zeta)/K\) restricted to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\) has fixed field \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
Also \(\mathbb Q(\mu_h)\subset E\), so \(\varphi(h)\le [E:\mathbb Q]\).
If \(m(\beta)/L\le C\) and \([E:\mathbb Q]\le B\), then \(|a|j\le C\) and these two inequalities bound both \(h\) and \(m_0\) in terms of \(B,C\).
One can use \(\varphi(n)^2/n\ge1/2\), since in its prime-factor product only the contribution for \(2\) of exponent one is below one.
There are therefore only finitely many triples \((a,h,\zeta)\) for the reduced equation, with bounds uniform even as \(K,\alpha\) vary.
Each equation has at most \(h\) solutions for a fixed \(\alpha\).
The possible values of \(\gamma\) may vary with \(\alpha\).
For every fixed \(K\), its own value of \([E:\mathbb Q]\) supplies the needed bound.
In particular \(E=\mathbb Q\) gives \(h\le2\) and \(\varphi(m_0)\le2j\) uniformly across all such fields.

A real embedding by itself implies \(\mu(K)=\{1,-1\}\), hence \(h\le2\).
An order bound for external twists cannot follow just from this signature condition, the two algebraic hypotheses on \(\alpha\), and bounded relative measure cost.
For instance, for each odd prime \(p\ge5\) let \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1})\), with \(\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}\), and take \(x=\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\).
It is a positive nontorsion unit at this embedding, since \(x=\zeta_p^{-1}(1+\zeta_p^2)\), the latter factor has norm \(\Phi_p(-1)=1\), and \(x\ne1\).
Distinct conjugates of \(x\) are real and never negatives of each other, since negating their common trace would negate the two inverse roots of the corresponding quadratic, turning primitive \(p\)-th roots into roots of order \(2p\).
Choose the primitive generator \([\alpha]\) in the direction of \([x]\) on its rational line in the free unit lattice.
Taking a representative positive at the given embedding gives \(x=\alpha^k\) for some \(k\ge1\), since \(\mu(K)=\{\pm1\}\) and both sides are positive there.
Then \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and a torsion quotient between distinct conjugates would give the same collision for \(x\) because \(x\) also generates \(K\).
Taking \(\beta=\zeta_p\alpha\) gives \(\mathbb Q(\beta)=K(\zeta_p)\) of degree two over \(K\), by the power inclusion above.
Here \(a=b=h=1\), \(j=2\), and the order \(p\) grows without bound.
These fields illustrate the algebraic signature bound and carry no assertion that the seeds are degree records or that their measures shrink.

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## All equal-measure exceptions for a real representative with trivial abelian subfield

Assume \(E=\mathbb Q\) and work at a real embedding with \(\alpha>0\).
A seed with a real embedding can be given this sign by multiplying by \(-1\), which only changes signs of polynomial coefficients and a root-of-unity factor in the equation.
Suppose \(m(\beta)=L\), so \(|a|=j=1\).
If \(\zeta\in K\), then \(\zeta=\pm1\) and the direct substitution in (1) preserves the normalized support of \(P_\alpha\), up to reflection \(u\mapsto1-u\) when \(a=-1\).

If \(\zeta\notin K\), then \(h\ne1\), since \([\mathbb Q(\gamma):K]=h\) and \(\zeta\in\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Thus \(h=2\) and \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K(\zeta)\) is quadratic over \(K\).
Equation (2) and \(E=\mathbb Q\) give \(\varphi(m_0)=2\), hence \(m_0\in\{3,4,6\}\).
Ordinary complex conjugation fixes this embedded \(K\) and acts nontrivially on \(\zeta\).
The equation \(\gamma^2=\zeta\alpha^a\) gives \(|\gamma|^2=\alpha^a\) and \(\bar\gamma=\zeta^{-1}\gamma\).
Therefore \(T=\gamma+\bar\gamma\in K\) satisfies
\[
       T^2=(2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\alpha^a,\qquad
       \operatorname{minpol}_{K}(\gamma;Z)= Z^2-TZ+\alpha^a.      \tag{3}
\]
The scalar in parentheses is respectively \(1,2,3\) for the three possible orders.
Order three would make \(\alpha^a\) a square in \(K\), contradicting primitivity.
For order four one needs \(2\alpha^a\) to be a square, and for order six one needs \(3\alpha^a\) to be a square.
These tests are unchanged by replacing \(a=1\) with \(a=-1\).
At most one can hold since otherwise \(K\) contains \(\sqrt{2/3}\), contrary to \(E=\mathbb Q\).

Conversely let \(p_0=2\) or \(3\) and \(T^2=p_0\alpha^a\) in \(K\), and take either primitive \(\zeta\) of order four or six respectively.
Then \(\gamma=T/(1+\zeta^{-1})\) satisfies \(\gamma^2=\zeta\alpha^a\).
It lies in \(K(\zeta)\), the power inclusion gives \(K\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), and the equation recovers \(\zeta\) from \(\gamma\).
Thus its field is exactly \(K(\zeta)\), of degree \(2d\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(m(\gamma)=L\).
The two choices of \(T\) supply both signs.
Its absolute minimal polynomial is the product of the quadratics in (3) over the \(d\) embeddings of \(K\), since this monic polynomial has the full degree.
Conversely every further substitution \(P_\gamma(X^s)\), \(s\ge1\), is irreducible and has the same measure.
Indeed a root \(\beta\) with \(\beta^s=\gamma\) satisfies \(\beta^{2s}=\zeta\alpha^a\) with \(|a|=1\).
The norm divisibility gives \([\mathbb Q(\beta):K]\ge2s\), and the tower through \(\gamma\) gives the matching upper bound.
In particular if, for each of the rational primes \(2,3\), at least one prime of \(K\) above it has odd ramification index, neither square test holds.
The valuation of \(p_0\alpha^a\) at that prime is its odd ramification index, since \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Under those conditions every equal-measure radical or torsion transform has the same normalized support as the input up to reflection.
The unrestricted Lehmer problem allows fields outside these conditions and transformations beyond a single multiplicative dependence.

<a id="examples"></a>
## Both external quadratic cases occur for primitive real seeds

For \(p=2\) and \(p=3\) take the root \(\pi\in(0,1)\) of \(f_p(X)=X^4+pX-p\), and let \(K=\mathbb Q(\pi)\).
The polynomial is Eisenstein at \(p\).
Its Galois group on the four roots is \(S_4\).
Indeed, if the four roots are \(r_1,\ldots,r_4\), the three quantities \(r_i r_j+r_k r_l\) indexed by partitions into two pairs have monic polynomial \(Y^3+4pY-p^2\) by the elementary symmetric formulas.
It is strictly increasing over the reals and has its unique real root strictly between zero and one for both primes, so it is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Thus the group order is divisible by both four and three.
The derivative of \(f_p\) has just one real zero and \(f_p(0)<0\), so the quartic has two real roots and one nonreal pair.
Complex conjugation acts as a transposition, excluding \(A_4\), the unique index-two subgroup of \(S_4\).
This proves the group assertion.
The root stabilizer \(S_3\) is maximal and not normal, so the quartic field \(K\) has no proper intermediate subfield and is not itself Galois.
In particular \(E=\mathbb Q\).

The number \(x=1-\pi=\pi^4/p\) generates \(K\) and is a unit since \(f_p(1)=1\).
It lies in \((0,1)\), and \(px=\pi^4\) is a square in \(K\).
As above choose a primitive unit on its ray, positive at the chosen embedding, so \(x=\alpha^k\) with \(k\ge1\).
If \(k\) were even, the square \(p\alpha^k\) would imply \(\sqrt p\in K\), which the subfield computation excludes.
Thus \(k\) is odd and \(p\alpha\) is a square in \(K\).
The field generated by \(\alpha\) contains \(x\), hence equals \(K\).
No two distinct conjugates of \(x\) have torsion quotient, since the doubly transitive Galois action would then make all conjugate moduli equal, and their product has modulus one whereas \(0<x<1\).
Taking \(k\)-th powers gives the same exclusion for \(\alpha\).
The construction in (3) therefore realizes the order-four case for \(p=2\) and the order-six case for \(p=3\).
It establishes occurrence under the algebraic hypotheses without asserting a new degree record.
