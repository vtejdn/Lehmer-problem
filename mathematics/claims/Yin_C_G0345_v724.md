# A joint restriction on prime powers shared by an integer Salem seed and a field radical index

<a id="statement"></a>
## Full valuations from several primes in one norm

Let \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) be integers and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Write \(r>1\) for the exterior Salem root of \(X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), \(d=[\mathbb Q(r):\mathbb Q]\), and \(R\) for the largest positive integer with \(r^{1/R}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
Fix \(c\in\{0,-1,1\}\) and put \(X_0=a-c\).
Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_t\), \(t\ge1\), be distinct odd primes dividing \(X_0\), with \(q_i=p_i^{e_i}\mid R\), \(e_i\ge1\).
Set \(q=\prod_iq_i\), \(u_i=v_{p_i}(X_0)\), \(N=n+1\) at \(c=0\) and \(N=n\) otherwise.
Then
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^t(1-\tau_i/d)u_i\log p_i
 \ \le\ \log(q C)+\frac{N}{d}
            \left(1-\prod_{i=1}^t(1-1/q_i)\right)\log r,              \tag{1}
\]
where \(C=4^{2^{t-1}}q^{2^{t-1}-1}\).
One may take \(C=2\) for \(t=1\), and \(C=2\min(q_1,q_2)\) for \(t=2\).
At \(c=0\) or \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\) take every \(\tau_i=0\).
At either remaining \(c=\pm1\) take \(\tau_i=1\), except that \(\tau_i=2\) when \(p_i=3\) and \(3\nmid n\).
The estimate applies pointwise, irrespective of the other prime factors of \(R\) or \(X_0\), and requires no lower bound on \(n\) depending on the seed.

For any fixed \(T\ge1\), all sufficiently large seeds, both signs and every \(n\ge7\) have the following property.
If \(X_0=a-c\) is odd and has at most \(T\) distinct prime factors, then
\[
          \prod_{\substack{p\mid X_0\\p\nmid R}}p^{v_p(X_0)}
                        \ \ge\ X_0^{\,1/(T+2)} .                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(\operatorname{rad}(X_0)\nmid R\).
The seed cutoff is effective in \(T\) and the primes may vary with the seed.
For each fixed \(0<\delta<1\), (2) also holds for all sufficiently large \(a\) whenever
\[
        1\le T\le(1-\delta)\frac{\log\log a}{\log2},
        \qquad n\ge 1+8(T+2),
\]
with \(X_0\) odd and at most \(T\) distinct prime factors as before.
The proof retains complete powers \(p^{v_p(X_0)}\) even when \(e_i=1\).

<a id="local"></a>
## Valuations of one signed binomial at a divisor radical

Put \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\) and \(G_s(Z)=Z^{n+1}-sZ^n+\epsilon(sZ-1)\).
The [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(P_a=G_a/U\) for the minimal polynomial of \(r\), with \(U\) the product of \(Z-v\) over \(v=\pm1\) satisfying \(v^{n-1}=\epsilon\).
Let \(P_c=G_c/U\).
Both polynomials in the pencil \(G_a-G_c=-(a-c)(Z^n-\epsilon Z)\) and \(G_c\) are divisible by \(U\), with \(U\mid Z^n-\epsilon Z\).
Thus \(P_c(r)\in X_0\mathcal O_K\).
Each positive radical \(\beta_i=r^{1/q_i}\) generates \(K\), and its minimum reduces to \(P_c\bmod p_i\) by the [prime-power Frobenius congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius).
In particular \(P_c(\beta_i)\in p_i\mathcal O_K\).
Put \(\eta=\epsilon\) for \(c=0\) and \(\eta=-\epsilon c\) for \(c=\pm1\).
We prove
\[
 v_{p_i}\!\left(\left|\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta_i^N-\eta)\right|\right)
                         \ \ge\ (d-\tau_i)u_i-d e_i.                   \tag{3}
\]
All these norms are nonzero since the positive real value \(\beta_i^N\) exceeds one.

Fix \(p=p_i,\ e=e_i,\ u=u_i\).
For local computations take a valuation of a splitting field over \(p\), normalized by \(v(p)=1\), and apply it after each of the \(d\) embeddings \(\sigma\) of \(K\).
Write \(z=\sigma\beta_i,\ s=\sigma r=z^{p^e}\).
The sum of these valuations of a nonzero element over the embeddings gives the ordinary \(p\)-valuation of its absolute norm.
Whenever \(v(z^N-\eta)>1/(p-1)\), the odd-prime binomial lifting identity gives
\[
                     v(z^N-\eta)=v(s^N-\eta)-e .
\]
Indeed in each raising to the \(p\)-th power the linear term has strictly least valuation, and \(\eta^p=\eta\).

At \(c=0\), \(P_c\) divides \(Z^{n+1}-\eta\).
At \(c=\pm1\), \(G_c=(Z-c)(Z^n-\eta)\); when \(c^{n-1}=\epsilon\), the factor \(Z-c\) is in \(U\), so \(P_c\mid Z^n-\eta\).
In all these cases \(v(z^N-\eta)\ge1\) and \(v(s^N-\eta)\ge u\) by the two evaluations of \(P_c\).
Summing proves (3) with \(\tau_i=0\).

Now \(c=\pm1,\ c^{n-1}\ne\epsilon\), so \(c^n=\eta\).
The evaluation of \(G_c\) gives
\[
 v(z-c)+v(z^n-\eta)\ge1,\qquad
                   (s-c)(s^n-\eta)=(a-c)(s^n-\epsilon s).
\]
Since \(Z-c\mid Z^n-\eta\), one has \(v(z^n-\eta)\ge v(z-c)\), hence \(v(z^n-\eta)\ge1/2\).
For \(p\ge5\) the lifting identity applies.
It also applies at \(p=3\) if \(3\mid n\): a value \(v(z^n-\eta)\le1/2\) would force \(v(z-c)\ge1/2\); cubing \(z/c\) then yields valuation at least \(\min(1+1/2,3/2)>1/2\) for its difference from one.
Any subsequent positive power preserves that strict lower bound.
Thus in these cases \(v(z^n-\eta)\ge u-e-v(s-c)\).
As \(U(c)\) is a unit at odd \(p\) and \(|G_a(c)|=2(a-c)\), summing \(v(s-c)\) gives precisely \(v_p(|P_a(c)|)=u\).
This proves (3) with \(\tau_i=1\).

At \(p=3,\ 3\nmid n\), the point \(c\) has multiplicity exactly two in \(\overline P_c\): it is a simple root of \(Z^n-\eta\), the other linear factor \(Z-c\) adds one, and \(U(c)\ne0\bmod3\).
Exactly two of the \(d\) embeddings therefore have \(z\equiv c\) at the fixed valuation, counting the roots of the reduced minimum with multiplicity.
At every other embedding \(s-c\) is a unit by Frobenius, and \(v(z^n-\eta)\ge1\) because \(z-c\) is a unit.
These \(d-2\) embeddings give \(v(z^n-\eta)\ge u-e\); the two discarded contributions are nonnegative.
This implies (3) also in this case.

<a id="union"></a>
## The degree saved by taking a common polynomial

Let \(\gamma=r^{1/q}\in K\); this follows because the prime powers are coprime divisors of \(R\) and the [positive field exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) are its divisors.
In \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) take the monic least common multiple
\[
              F(Z)=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le i\le t}(Z^{q/q_i}-1).
\]
Each binomial is a squarefree product of cyclotomic polynomials.
Inside the cyclic group \(\mu_q\), the intersection belonging to a nonempty subset \(I\) is \(\mu_{q/\prod_{i\in I}q_i}\).
Thus inclusion-exclusion gives exactly \(D=\deg F=q(1-\prod_i(1-1/q_i))\), together with the rational product expression for \(F\) having the binomials \(Z^{q/\prod_{i\in I}q_i}-1\) in its numerator for odd \(|I|\) and its denominator for even \(|I|>0\).
Every original binomial divides \(F\) over the integers.
Since \(q/q_i\) is odd, substituting \(Z=\eta\gamma^N\) makes that binomial \(\eta(\beta_i^N-\eta)\).
The quotient is integral at every embedding and \(F(\eta\gamma^N)\ne0\) because its exterior argument has modulus greater than one.
Consequently (3) supplies, for each distinct \(p_i\), its own lower valuation on the norm of this single element.

On \(|Z|=1+1/q\) all numerator binomials have absolute value below four, and all denominator binomials have absolute value at least \(1/q\).
There are \(2^{t-1}\) numerator factors and \(2^{t-1}-1\) denominator factors.
By the maximum modulus principle \(F\) is bounded by \(C\) on the closed unit disk; the reciprocal polynomial then gives \(|F(z)|\le C\max(1,|z|)^D\) everywhere.
For one binomial the unit-circle bound is two.
For two coprime prime powers the least common multiple is \((Z^{q_1}-1)(Z^{q_2}-1)/(Z-1)\); keeping the shorter geometric sum gives the bound \(2\min(q_1,q_2)\).
There is just one exterior conjugate of \(\gamma\), so
\[
        |\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}F(\eta\gamma^N)|
                              \le C^d r^{ND/q}.
\]
Multiplying the prime contributions from (3), taking logarithms and moving \(\sum_i e_i\log p_i=\log q\) to the right proves (1).
The earlier [adjacent-integer valuation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v715.md#statement) combines full valuations for a common powering multiplier.
Here each prime uses its own divisor radical and the common polynomial counts overlapping cyclotomic roots just once.

## The part of an odd integer missed by the radical index

Let \(X_0\) be odd, \(X_0\ge2\), and choose the list to consist of all odd primes \(p_i\mid\gcd(R,X_0)\), with \(q_i=p_i\).
If the list is empty all of \(X_0\) is missed.
Otherwise put \(\rho=\prod_i(1-1/p_i)\) and write \(V=\prod_{p\mid X_0,\,p\nmid R}p^{v_p(X_0)}\).
In increasing order the primes satisfy \(p_i\ge i+2\), giving \(\rho\ge2/(t+2)\ge2/(T+2)\).
The selected full powers divide \(X_0\), and \(\tau_i\le2\), so the total possible loss on the left of (1) is at most \(2\log X_0/d\).
Also \(N/d\le1+2/(n-1)\), \(d\ge n-1\), and \(\log r\le\log(a+1)\le\log X_0+O(1/a)\).
Thus with an absolute constant,
\[
       \frac{\log V}{\log X_0}\ge
          \rho-\frac4{n-1}
               -\frac{2^{t-1}\log(4q)+O(1/a)}{\log X_0}.              \tag{4}
\]
The [prime-power gap](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives \(q_i\le C_0\log(a+1)\) with an absolute effective \(C_0\).
Hence \(\log(4q)=O(1+t\log\log(a+3))\), uniformly in the primes.
For fixed \(T\), the last term in (4) tends effectively to zero.
For \(T\le(1-\delta)\log\log a/\log2\), it is \(o(1/(T+2))\) uniformly, since \(2^T\le(\log a)^{1-\delta}\).
If \(n\ge1+8(T+2)\) the second subtracted term is at most \(1/(2(T+2))\).
This gives (2) in both stated ranges.
For fixed \(T\) there are only finitely many smaller delays, each with an [effective cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v694.md#statement) beyond which \(R=1\) at both signs.
Taking their maximum completes the all-delay assertion.

For instance at \(c=0\), a common index divisible by 15 would bound the sum of the logarithmic shares of three and five by
\((n+1)(7/15)/d+O(1/\log a)\) after division by \(\log a\).
Separate applications to the two divisor radicals give \((n+1)(8/15)/d+O(1/\log a)\).
For an explicit difference, let \(a_L=2^{\lfloor L/(2\log2)\rfloor}3^{\lfloor8L/(25\log3)\rfloor}5^{\lfloor9L/(50\log5)\rfloor}\) for integers \(L\to\infty\).
The limiting individual shares \(8/25\) and \(9/50\) are below \(1/3\) and \(1/5\), but their sum is \(1/2\).
For all \(n\ge30\), \((n+1)(7/15)/d\le(31/29)(7/15)<1/2\), so (1) excludes \(15\mid R\) at every such delay once \(L\) is sufficiently large.
The finitely many smaller delays also admit eventual exclusion by the fixed-delay cutoff.
This comparison isolates the gain over summing the individual central binomial norm bounds.
