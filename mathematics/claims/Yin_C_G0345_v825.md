# Prime size and residue order in a complementary resultant

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two simultaneous lower bounds on the residue contacts

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be a squarefree [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) in a positive primitive direction, set \(N=3+k+l\), and omit the finitely many directions excluded by the [critical-point approximation](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#statement).
Fix one extension of \(v_p\) to the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_p\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).
For a root \(z\) let \(g(z)\) be the multiplicative order of its reduction.
At every rational prime,
\[
 v_p(P'(z))\ll_F g(z)(\log N)^4,\qquad
 v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\ll_F p^8(\log N)^4.                     \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P=SM\) with monic integral factors.
For \(Y,H\ge2\), the following parts of the sum \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,M)|=\sum_{p,z,w}v_p(z-w)\log p\), over roots \(z\) of \(S\) and \(w\) of \(M\), satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{p\le Y,z,w}v_p(z-w)\log p&\ll_F Y^9\log(2Y)(\log N)^4,\\
 \sum_{p,z,w:\,g(z)\le H}v_p(z-w)\log p&\ll_F H^3(\log N)^4.       \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
On a sequence \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\), if \(S\) is irreducible and \(d=\deg S=o(l)\), then for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/9\) the contacts with **both** \(p>l^{1/9-\epsilon}\) and \(g(z)>l^{1/3-\epsilon}\) contribute at least \((1-o(1))d\log(l/d)\).
The order restriction includes primes of arbitrary size and the two bounds include primes of bad reduction.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Frobenius on a residue point and its inverse

Only terms with \(z,w\) in the same residue class can contribute.
At any multiple residue \(x\) of \(P\), the point \((x^k,x^l)\) lies on the finite torus intersection of \(F=0\) with \((kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V)F=0\).
This intersection has at most sixteen geometric points in every characteristic by the [proper intersection bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#ramification).
Frobenius and simultaneous inversion preserve the intersection, since \(F(U^{-1},V^{-1})=U^{-4}V^{-2}F(U,V)\) and differentiation of this equality preserves tangency on the curve.
The two coordinates generate both the same residue field and the same multiplicative subgroup as \(x\), by Bézout's identity for \(k,l\).
Let \(f\) be the Frobenius orbit length of this point.
If \(f\le8\), the order of \(x\) divides \(p^f-1\).
If \(f>8\), the inverse point must belong to the same orbit, as two such orbits would exceed sixteen points.
Inversion commutes with Frobenius, and cannot fix the point since coordinates of order at most two lie in the prime field.
It therefore acts on the orbit by the half-turn, so \(f\) is even and \(x^{p^{f/2}}=x^{-1}\).
In both cases
\[
                            g(z)\le p^8+1.                              \tag{3}
\]

<a id="valuation"></a>
## Dependence on the actual residue order

For \(p\le17\) the [uniform bound of v819](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#statement) gives (1), after increasing the constant for these finitely many primes.
For \(p>17\) put \(a=v_p(P'(z))\).
The [seventeen projections](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#distance) either give \(a\ll_F\log N/\log p\) or give a critical point \(c=(u,v)\) with the same reductions as \((z^k,z^l)\) and
\[
                a\le 16v_p(v^ku^{-l}-1)+O_F(\log N/\log p).
\]
Here \(v^ku^{-l}\ne1\) by squarefreeness, \(K=\mathbb Q(u,v)\) has degree at most sixteen, and \(h(u),h(v)\ll_F\log N\) by the [critical height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#logarithms).
The reductions of \(u,v\) generate a group of order \(g=g(z)\).

If \(u,v\) are multiplicatively independent, apply [Theorem 3 of Bugeaud and Laurent](../audits/literature/BL96/Yin_U001.md#bl96-u001) to \(v^k-u^l\), whose valuation is the one in the last display.
Their residual exponent is precisely \(g\), and the ratio of global degree to residue degree lies between one and sixteen.
Choose both logarithmic height majorants equal to a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(\log N+\log p\).
The exponent parameter \(b'\) is \(O_F(N)\), the squared maximum in that theorem is \(O_F((\log N+\log p)^2)\), and its denominator includes \((\log p)^4\).
This gives \(v_p(v^ku^{-l}-1)\ll_F g(1+\log N/\log p)^4\).

For dependent inputs the subgroup of \(K^\times\) they generate has rank at most one.
The order \(m\) of its torsion subgroup is bounded absolutely because \([K:\mathbb Q]\le16\).
A nontrivial root of unity in such a field has bounded valuation of its difference from one, for example by its bounded degree and height.
This handles rank zero and any torsion value of \(v^ku^{-l}\).
In rank one choose a generator \(\gamma\) modulo torsion within the subgroup, and write \(u=\zeta_1\gamma^i,\ v=\zeta_2\gamma^j\).
At least one of \(i,j\) is nonzero, so \(h(\gamma)\ll_F\log N\).
Northcott finiteness in degree at most sixteen gives an absolute positive lower bound for \(h(\gamma)\), hence \(|i|+|j|\ll_F\log N\).
The reduction order of \(\gamma\) divides \(g\).
If \(n=jk-il\ne0\) and \(v_p(v^ku^{-l}-1)>0\), raising to \(gm\) gives
\[
 v_p(v^ku^{-l}-1)\le v_p(\gamma^{gmn}-1)
                    =v_p(\gamma^g-1)+v_p(mn).
\]
Indeed \(v_p(\gamma^g-1)\ge1/e>1/(p-1)\) for local ramification index \(e\le16\), so the binomial lifting formula applies also to negative exponents by inversion.
The local height inequality gives \(v_p(\gamma^g-1)\log p\le16(g h(\gamma)+\log 2)\), and \(|mn|\ll_F N\log N\).
This proves the required bound in rank one.

The derivative valuation vanishes outside nonsingleton residue classes.
Combining the root estimate with (3) and summing over the [at most 32 roots in these classes](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#ramification) proves (1).
The first bound of (2) follows by summing over primes through \(Y\).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Weights at bounded residue order over all primes

For each positive integer \(g\), put \(A_g=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_g,P)|\).
This is a positive integer by the [torsion exclusion for the whole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
Each of the \(\varphi(g)\) complex factors is bounded by the sum of the absolute coefficients of the fixed \(F\), giving \(\log A_g\ll_F\varphi(g)\) independently of the exponents.
Whenever a root of \(P\) has residue order \(g\) at \(p\), its reduction is a primitive \(g\)-th root and \(p\nmid g\), so \(p\mid A_g\).
At one such prime, for the roots of \(S\) reducing to order \(g\), the total of their contacts with the roots of \(M\) is at most the sum of their derivative valuations.
At most 32 roots can contribute.
By (1), the order-\(g\) contribution over all primes is thus \(O_F(g(\log N)^4\log A_g)\).
Sum over \(g\le H\) and use \(\sum_{g\le H}g\varphi(g)\ll H^3\) for the second estimate of (2).
Finally \(d=l^{1+o(1)}\) and the full log resultant is at least \(d(\log(l/d)+O_F(1))\) by the [individual degree and resultant estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v819.md#factor).
At the two stated cutoffs, the sum of the two discarded upper bounds is \(o(d)\), so their union leaves the claimed simultaneous contribution.
