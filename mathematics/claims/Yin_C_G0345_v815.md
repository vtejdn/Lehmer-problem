# Higher gluing digits at thirteen and nineteen

<a id="statement"></a>
## The second slope digit

Consider the [two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) for positive coprime integers \(k,l\), and put \(s=l/k\) in \(\mathbb Z_p\) on the surviving rows of the [critical residue calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0184/readme.md#sieve).
Retain that row's order congruence and write \(s=s_0+pb\).
For any decomposition into monic reciprocal integral factors \(P=ST\), the following polynomial in \(b\) controls \(v_p(\operatorname{Res}(S,T))\).

| \(p\) | \(s_0\) | \(D(b)\) modulo \(p\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | 0 | \(b^2-10\) |
| 19 | \(1,-1\) | \((b+s_0)^2-5\) |

A nonsquare value forces the resultant valuation to vanish.
A nonzero square gives valuation at most two.
Outside the surviving rows the valuation vanishes even without reciprocity.
At each of the two zeros of \(D\) in each row, primitive integer directions permit two separate local trace roots with arbitrarily large valuation of their difference.
The latter conclusion concerns the local trace polynomial and leaves the placement of those roots in rational factors open.

<a id="digits"></a>
## Expanding at the torsion point

Put \(t=U-U^{-1}\) and \(f=F/(U^2V)=(12+t^2)(V+V^{-1})-7t(V-V^{-1})+1\).
Use the Teichmüller lifts \((u,v)\) of one surviving pair, the operators \(L_s=U\partial_U+sV\partial_V\) and the values \(B=V\partial_V f\), \(N=L_{s_0}f\), \(A=L_{s_0}^2 f/2\) there.
In both cases \(v_p(f(u,v))=2\), \(B\) is a unit, and the three residues \((c,h,a)=(f/p^2,N/(pB),A/B^2)\) are respectively \((2,0,11)\) and \((4,s_0,11)\).

At thirteen choose \((u,v)\equiv(5,4)\), so \(u^2=-1\), \(v+v^{-1}=1\), and \(j=u(v-v^{-1})\) has square three.
The product \((9-14j)(9+14j)=-3\cdot13^2\) gives \(f=9-14j\) and \(c=2\).
Differentiation gives \(N=0\), \(A=-4-7j\equiv11\) and \(B=8(v-v^{-1})-14u\equiv-1\).

At nineteen take \(v\) of order twenty and \(u=v^2\) or \(v^8\) according as \(s_0=1\) or \(-1\).
Write \(y=v+v^{-1}\), the root of \(y^4-5y^2+5=0\) congruent to \(-8\) modulo nineteen, hence to 106 modulo 361 by substitution and simple lifting.
Reduction using \(v^2=yv-1\) gives
\[
 f=-6y^3+35y+1,\quad A=(-27y^3+91y)/2,\quad
 B=(14-12y^2)(v-y/2),\quad N=s_0(22-14y^2)(v-y/2).
\]
The characteristic polynomial of multiplication by \(-6y^3+35y+1\) in the quartic quotient is \(X^4-4X^3-1619X^2+3246X+19^2\cdot1741\), as follows by expansion in \(1,y,y^2,y^3\).
Its constant and linear coefficients give \(c=-1741/3246\equiv4\), since \(f\equiv0\) is a simple root modulo nineteen.
The formulas give \(B^2\equiv8\), \(A\equiv12\), and \(N/(19B)\equiv s_0\).

## Separating local trace roots

Let \(\xi\) be the Teichmüller lift of the unique section coordinate above \((u,v)\), determined by Bézout's identity for \(k,l\).
Take \(X=\xi\exp(pw/(kB))\) and divide \(f(X^k,X^l)\) by \(p^2\) to obtain \(G(w,b)\).
This restricted power series is integral on the closed unit bidisk, with reduction
\[
                         c+(h+b)w+aw^2.
\]
Indeed the terms of order \(n\ge3\) in the exponential expansions have valuation at least \(n-2-v_p(n!)>0\), tending to infinity.
At nineteen unramified conjugation takes \((u,v,B,\xi)\) to \((u^{-1},v^{-1},-B,\xi^{-1})\), so simultaneous inversion invariance puts \(G\) over \(\mathbb Z_{19}\).
The coordinate \((X+X^{-1}-\xi-\xi^{-1})/p\) has integral coefficients over the prime field's valuation ring in both cases and unit linear coefficient \((\xi-\xi^{-1})/(kB)\), with higher coefficients divisible by \(p\).
It therefore preserves distances in the \(w\)-disk and commutes with prime-field descent.

The two roots per original residue disk are exhausted here, counted with multiplicities over an algebraic closure.
Indeed the unscaled local expansion at \(X=\xi\) has constant valuation two, linear coefficient divisible by \(p\), and unit quadratic coefficient by \(A\not\equiv0\) and \(kB\) a unit.
Its Newton polygon places both roots at distance \(p^{-1}\) from \(\xi\).
Reduction of \(G\) gives the stated discriminants.
For a nonsquare discriminant the two scaled trace roots are conjugate over \(\mathbb Q_p\), so a reciprocal factor, expressed in the trace coordinate, contains both or neither.
For nonzero square discriminant their unscaled difference has valuation one, and the only other possible common section disk is its inverse, giving the bound two.

At each zero of \(D\), the double residue root \(w_0=-(h+b)/(2a)\) is nonzero because \(c\ne0\).
The system \(G=G_w=0\) lifts uniquely to \((w_*,b_*)\in\mathbb Z_p^2\) by Hensel's lemma since \(G_b\equiv w_0\) and \(G_{ww}\equiv2a\) are units there.
For every \(m\ge1\) choose \(b\equiv b_*+p^{2m}d\pmod {p^{2m+1}}\) with \(d\equiv-a/w_0\pmod p\).
Taylor expansion gives \(p^{-2m}G(w_*+p^m z,b)\equiv a(z^2-1)\pmod p\), so the two simple lifts give unscaled trace difference of valuation exactly \(m+1\).
At thirteen take \(k=3\) and require \(l\equiv2\pmod4\), \(l\equiv1\pmod3\).
At nineteen take \(k=2\) or \(8\) according to the row and \(l\equiv1\pmod {20}\).
The Chinese remainder theorem matches the required \(p\)-adic digits independently in each case.
