# Endpoint coefficient estimates and sharp costs for a growing number of prescribed powers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds from one exterior conjugate

Fix a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with minimal polynomial \(P\), and a conjugate \(c\) with \(a=\log|c|>0\).
Take distinct positive integers \(p_1<\cdots<p_r\), put \(\Sigma=\sum_i p_i\), and suppose \(|c|^{-p_1}\le1/2\).
Let \((U,V)\) be any nonzero pair of integer polynomials satisfying \(P^2\mid U(X^{p_i})+XV(X^{p_i})\) for all \(i\), and write \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
Let \(d\) and \(m\) be the largest and smallest occupied indices across both polynomials, and take coefficients outside their supports to be zero.
For either polynomial \(W\), and \(0\le j<r\),
\[
 \max\{|W_{d-j}|,|W_{m+j}|\}
 \le H\exp\!\left(-a\sum_{i=1}^{r-j}p_i+O_c(r+\log(2\Sigma))\right).               \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\log H\ge a\Sigma-O_c(r+\log(2\Sigma))\), with no bound on the degrees and including pairs with a common factor or identically vanishing reflected difference.

Suppose now that \(P\) has just one exterior conjugate (in particular this applies to every Salem seed), and write \(h=\log M(P)=a\).
Fix \(C>1\), and allow any nonempty exponent sets in \([L,CL]\) with \(r=o(L)\) as \(L\to\infty\).
For a constant \(A=A(P,C)\) there are nonzero coprime integer rows of degrees at most \(A r^2+1\) with these double contacts, nonzero \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) for \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\), and
\[
               h\Sigma\le\log H\le h\Sigma+O_{P,C}(L+r^2).             \tag{2}
\]
For \(r\to\infty\), the minimum normalized cost at this width therefore converges to \(h\) for every such prescribed schedule.
This includes all primes of a dyadic interval and the [explicit blocks of odd integers](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#statement) at this quadratic width.
The latter test uses a different, smaller calibrated width for its individual support bound.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Cancellation of node differences in the derivative data

Reverse both polynomials at degree \(d\), obtaining \(u(Y)=Y^dU(Y^{-1})\) and \(v(Y)=Y^dV(Y^{-1})\).
Write \(z_i=c^{-p_i}\), \(A_0=u+cv\), and \(T=\prod_i(Y-z_i)\).
The value and derivative of the reversed graph at \(c\) give \(A_0(z_i)=0\) and \(p_i z_i A_0'(z_i)=cv(z_i)\), by differentiating \(u(X^{-p_i})+Xv(X^{-p_i})\).
This expression equals the original graph times a monomial in \(X\).
The moduli of the nodes are distinct.
Thus \(A_0=T B\) with a polynomial \(B\), and interpolation of \(v\) yields polynomials \(I,D\) with
\[
 v=TD+I,\qquad I(Y)=c^{-1}\sum_i p_i z_i B(z_i)\,T(Y)/(Y-z_i).
\]
Indeed the displayed interpolant has the required values because \(A_0'(z_i)=T'(z_i)B(z_i)\).
On the unit circle \(2^{-r}\le|T|\le(3/2)^r\).
Consequently \(\sup_{|Y|\le1}|B(Y)|\le (1+|c|)2^r H\), and the same argument applied to \((v-I)/T\) gives \(\sup_{|Y|\le1}|D(Y)|\le H\exp(O_c(r+\log(2\Sigma)))\).
These bounds control each Taylor coefficient by the circle integral.
For \(k\le j\), every term of the coefficient of \(Y^k\) in \(T\) contains \(r-k\) distinct small nodes, so its modulus is at most \(\exp(-a\sum_{i\le r-j}p_i)\).
The coefficient of \(Y^j\) in \(z_iT/(Y-z_i)\) has the same property, since every product there uses \(z_i\) together with \(r-1-j\) different nodes.
Each coefficient involves at most \(2^r\) products, and each convolution at most \(r\) Taylor coefficients.
The representations of \(A_0,v\) and \(u=A_0-cv\) give the upper-end estimate in (1).
For the lower end first remove the common monomial \(Y^m\) from the original rows, which preserves double contacts because the seed is a unit.
Use now the unreversed polynomials and the conjugate \(c^{-1}\) supplied by reciprocity.
The nodes are again \(z_i=c^{-p_i}\), while the derivative equation becomes \(p_i z_i (u+c^{-1}v)'(z_i)=-c^{-1}v(z_i)\) for the shifted polynomials \(u,v\).
Only the constant multiplier of the interpolant changes, so the same bounds follow.
At least one leading coefficient is a nonzero integer, proving the lower bound on \(H\).

<a id="growing"></a>
## Packing at growing width and preserving all prescribed contacts

Write \(e=\deg P\), fix \(0<\delta<h/4\), put \(w=\lceil A r^2\rceil\), and use the coefficient sup bound \(B_0=\lfloor\exp(h\Sigma+\delta L)\rfloor\).
The unique exterior conjugate is real, and no two distinct conjugates differ by a root-of-unity multiplier.
Indeed an automorphism of the splitting field taking the first conjugate to the exterior one would give a second distinct conjugate of the same exterior modulus.
Thus powering preserves degree for every positive integer, and the powered minima are distinct across exponents since their measures are \(p_i h\).

Record both integral jet values at the seed for each exponent, as in the [embedding-box argument](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#many), for all integer coefficient lists in \([0,B_0]^{2(w+1)}\).
At a conjugate \(x\) each of the two jet coordinates is bounded by \(C_P(1+CL)(w+1)^2 B_0\max(1,|x|)^{w p_i}\), by the monomial sum and its first Euler derivative.
The field norm separates distinct algebraic integers in boxes of diameter less than one at every embedding.
Thus the log number of possible images is at most \(2er\log B_0+2wh\Sigma+O_{P,C}(r\log(2Lw))\).
Differences from one member of the largest fiber are distinct homogeneous solutions, with log count at least
\[
 2(w+1)\log B_0-2er\log B_0-2wh\Sigma-O_{P,C}(r\log(2Lw)).
\]
Two distinct such solutions have sup distance at least \(\exp(h\Sigma-O_{P,C}(r+\log(2Lw)))\), by (1) and the factor \(w+1\) from sup norm to one-norm.
In dimension at most \(w+1\), packing disjoint balls in the induced sup norm therefore bounds their log count by \((w+1)(\delta L+O_{P,C}(r+\log(2Lw)))\).
Choose \(A\) so large that \(A\delta>4e(Ch+\delta)\).
The lower count exceeds the upper one eventually, since \(\Sigma\le CLr\) and \(r+\log(2Lw)=o(L)\).
Their real span has dimension greater than \(w+1\), hence contains a nonzero vector \((0,V)\) by projection to the first row.
The quadratic expression \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) therefore takes a nonzero value on a fiber difference or a sum of two differences, by polarization.
The resulting pair has \(\log H\le h\Sigma+\delta L+O(\log(2w))\).

Each powered minimum contributes its square to the reflected difference, and a powered minimum dividing both rows would [contribute at least its cube](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#genuine).
The proof there applies to arbitrary degree-preserving positive powers by the same substitutions and reciprocity.
The resulting inequality \(2\log H\ge 2h\Sigma+hL\) contradicts the last upper bound.
A zero row would force its partner to contain every powered minimum squared, with measure at least \(2h\Sigma\), which also contradicts that bound.
Dividing out the primitive polynomial gcd removes no prescribed contact and leaves the reflected difference nonzero.
The row one-norms increase by at most \(2^w\), since an integral factor's one-norm is at most \(2^w\) times its multiplicative Mahler measure and the integral gcd has measure at least one.
The [tangency lower bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives the other direction in (2).
Since \(\Sigma\ge rL\), the excess in (2) is \(o(\Sigma)\) for \(r\to\infty\) and \(r=o(L)\).
The separation proof uses the full sum of exponents for a single conjugate; for a seed with several exterior conjugates it gives the maximum of the resulting bounds.
