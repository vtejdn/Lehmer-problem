# Capacity after adjoining a disk and degree bounds from an endpoint block

<a id="statement"></a>
## The difference between the two top degrees

Let \(B,C\) be nonzero integer polynomials of coefficient \(\ell^1\) norm at most \(L\), where \(L\ge2\), and let \(N\ge0\) be an integer.
Set
\[
     f=X^NB+C,\qquad b=\deg B,\qquad n=N+b,\qquad
     g=N-\deg C,\qquad D=g+b=n-\deg C .
\]
Assume \(D>0\), so the leading term of \(X^NB\) survives and \(\deg f=n\).
Choose \(H\ge1\) at least as large as every root modulus of \(B\), and count the complex roots \(\xi\) of \(B\) with their multiplicities.
If
\[
                   g+\sum_{B(\xi)=0}\frac{H}{H+|\xi|}\ \ge\ 0,        \tag{1}
\]
then every monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor \(A\) of \(f\) satisfies
\[
             \deg A\ \ge\
             \frac{D}{40960\{\log(2L)+D\log H\}} .                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular, when all roots of \(B\) lie in the closed unit disk, \(g\ge-b/2\) suffices for the degree lower bound \(D/(40960\log(2L))\).
This covers \(g=0\) whenever \(b>0\), as well as strictly separated blocks with any \(g\ge1\).
The estimate for \(g=0\) uses the difference \(D=b\) between the two top degrees, and permits the blocks to meet at exponent \(N\).
If \(D\ge\delta n\) and \(D\log H\le J\), then for fixed \(L,\delta>0,J\) every positive-measure irreducible integral factor has a uniform positive logarithmic Mahler gap under (1).
In particular this holds for all unit-disk endpoint blocks with \(g\ge0\) and \(g+b\ge\delta n\).

The [geometric estimate below](#disk) applies to any union of \(s\) radial segments of capacity at least one and every regular full compact set containing both those segments and the closed unit disk.
The agent working on curve specializations derived this estimate for Yin, and the coordinating agent proposed applying it to the second and fourth power images of a single containing set.
The degree argument uses the arithmetic capacity input and the [dyadic reduction of v170](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#dyadic).

<a id="disk"></a>
## A disk inside a regular compact set

Write \(\mathbb D_1=\{|z|\le1\}\).
Let \(K\) be a union of \(s\ge1\) radial segments from zero to nonzero endpoints, with logarithmic capacity \(\operatorname{cap}K\ge1\).
If a regular full compact set \(E\) contains \(\mathbb D_1\cup K\), then
\[
                        \log\operatorname{cap} E\ge\frac1{1024s}.     \tag{3}
\]
Here full means that its complement in the Riemann sphere is connected.
Both the number and positions of the components of its boundary may vary.

Write \(c_F=\log\operatorname{cap}F\), and denote by \(\mu_F\) and \(G_F\) its equilibrium probability measure and Green function with pole at infinity.
We extend \(G_F\) by zero on \(F\), so that for the regular sets in this argument
\[
                    G_F(z)=\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu_F(w)-c_F .
\]
Let \(\mu_0=d\theta/(2\pi)\) denote the equilibrium probability measure on the unit circle.
For finite-energy real measures set \(I(\nu,\eta)=-\iint\log|z-w|\,d\nu(z)\,d\eta(w)\).
This form satisfies Cauchy–Schwarz on compactly supported signed measures of mass zero and finite energy.
Indeed its quadratic form there is the Dirichlet integral \((2\pi)^{-1}\int|\nabla U_\nu|^2\) of \(U_\nu(z)=-\int\log|z-w|\,d\nu(w)\), by the distributional Laplace equation and integration by parts, or by smoothing both measures in the identity.
Since the logarithmic potential of \(\mu_E\) with the positive-log convention equals \(c_E\) throughout \(E\), we have
\[
 I(\mu_K-\mu_E,\mu_K-\mu_E)=c_E-c_K,\qquad
 I(\mu_0-\mu_E,\mu_0-\mu_E)=c_E .
\]
Expanding the cross term and using \(c_K\ge0\) now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int G_K\,d\mu_0
     &=c_E-c_K-I(\mu_K-\mu_E,\mu_0-\mu_E)\\
     &\le c_E-c_K+\sqrt{(c_E-c_K)c_E}\le 2c_E .                     \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

To bound the same integral from below, suppose \(c_E<1/(1024s)\) and put \(R=1+1/(16s)\), so \(\log R\ge1/(32s)\).
The quantity \(\log R-G_E(Re^{i\theta})\) is nonnegative because \(E\) contains the unit disk.
By the circle mean of the logarithmic kernel its average over \(\theta\) is
\[
          c_E-\int \log^+(|w|/R)\,d\mu_E(w)\le c_E .
\]
The angles at which \(G_E(Re^{i\theta})<(\log R)/2\) thus have normalized measure at most \(2c_E/\log R<1/16\).
Also discard the angular intervals of radius \(1/(4s)\) about the directions of the segments of \(K\).
Their total length is at most \(1/2\), and the two exclusions together leave more than half of the circle.
At each retained angle the disk centered at \(e^{i\theta}\) of radius \(1/(8s)\) misses every segment, since \(\sin(1/(4s))\ge1/(8s)\).
The Green function \(G_K\) is positive and harmonic on this disk.
The point \(Re^{i\theta}\) lies at half its radius, where \(G_K\ge G_E\ge(\log R)/2\) by domain monotonicity.
The disk Harnack inequality yields \(G_K(e^{i\theta})\ge(\log R)/6\ge1/(192s)\).
Integration gives \(\int G_K\,d\mu_0\ge1/(384s)>2c_E\), contradicting (4) and proving (3).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Unions and powers of a containing set

For regular compact radial sets \(E_1,\ldots,E_t\) that each contain \(\mathbb D_1\) and whose union \(V\) is regular, one has
\[
                   \log\operatorname{cap}V
                        \le\sum_i\log\operatorname{cap}E_i .          \tag{5}
\]
Here a radial set contains the segments from zero to all its points.
On the exterior of \(V\), write \(G_0=\log|z|\).
The bounded harmonic function \(\sum_i(G_0-G_{E_i})-(G_0-G_V)\) has nonnegative boundary values, since \(G_V=0\) there, at least one \(G_{E_i}\) vanishes, and every \(G_{E_i}\le G_0\).
The function extends harmonically at infinity, where the minimum principle gives (5).
This is the common-disk comparison used for slits in [C855 v2](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#the-common-disk-makes-the-logarithmic-comparison-valid).

Write \(E^{[q]}=\{z^q:z\in E\}\).
The full inverse image of \(E^{[q]}\) under \(z\mapsto z^q\) is \(\bigcup_{\zeta^q=1}\zeta E\).
Pulling the exterior Green function back and dividing by \(q\) gives the Green function of this inverse image by its boundary values and logarithmic growth.
It follows from (5) that
\[
        \log\operatorname{cap} E^{[q]}
            =q\log\operatorname{cap}\Bigl(\bigcup_{\zeta^q=1}\zeta E\Bigr)
            \le q^2\log\operatorname{cap}E .                          \tag{6}
\]
We will use (5) and (6) on closed radial regions with positive, Lipschitz polar radius.
Their rotations, power images and finite unions still have positive Lipschitz radius, obtained by taking a finite maximum of rotated or rescaled radius functions.
These regions have connected exteriors and regular boundaries.

## A single level curve for the dyadic core

Let \(a=\deg A\), and let \(k\) be the largest power of two for which \(A(X)=A_0(X^k)\).
Then \(A_0\) is a monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit polynomial of degree \(a/k>1\), and the orbit polynomial of the squares of its roots is irreducible.
The [dyadic argument and the published arithmetic input](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#dyadic) give \(\operatorname{cap}K\ge1\) for the radial segments ending at \(\beta^2,\beta^4\) over all roots \(\beta\) of \(A_0\).
Their count is at most \(2a/k\).

Let \(Q\) be the monic polynomial whose multiset of zeros consists of all \(\xi^k\) for \(B(\xi)=0\), including multiplicities.
Its degree is \(b\).
After division by its leading coefficient, \(\prod_{\rho^k=1}B(\rho X)\) equals \(Q(X^k)\), and the modulus of that leading coefficient is at least one.
It follows that \(\|Q\|_1\le L^k\).
If \(A_0(\beta)=0\), take \(\alpha^k=\beta\), so \(f(\rho\alpha)=0\) for every \(\rho^k=1\).
When \(|\alpha|\ge1\), these equations give \(|B(\rho\alpha)|\le L|\alpha|^{-g}\), and multiplication yields
\[
                           |\beta^gQ(\beta)|\le L^k .                  \tag{7}
\]

Put \(R_0=H^k\), \(T=2L^kR_0^D\), and form the compact set
\[
            E_0=\{|z|\le R_0\}\ \cup\
                  \{|z|\ge R_0:\ |z^g Q(z)|\le T\}.
\]
For \(r=|z|>R_0\), logarithmic radial differentiation gives
\[
       \partial_{\log r}\log|z^gQ(z)|
          =g+\sum_{B(\xi)=0}\operatorname{Re}\frac{z}{z-\xi^k}
          \ \ge\ g+\sum_{B(\xi)=0}\frac{r}{r+|\xi|^k}.
\]
The last bound follows by minimizing over the argument of \(z/\xi^k\) for each root separately.
It is nonnegative by (1), since \(r>H^k\) and \((|\xi|/H)^k\le |\xi|/H\).
It is strictly positive if there is a nonzero root, and when every root equals zero the derivative is \(D>0\), also covering constant \(B\).
On \(|z|=R_0\), the coefficient bound gives \(|z^gQ(z)|\le L^kR_0^D<T\).
Since \(D>0\), the level \(T\) is attained at exactly one radius greater than \(R_0\) in every direction.
The resulting polar radius is smooth by the strict radial derivative at that level.
Every \(\beta\) is in \(E_0\), by the inner disk or (7), and all the radial segments to those \(\beta\) lie in \(E_0\).

On its exterior, \(D^{-1}\log(|z^gQ(z)|/T)\) is harmonic, vanishes on the boundary and has leading growth \(\log|z|\).
Consequently
\[
            \log\operatorname{cap}E_0=\frac{\log T}{D}
             =\frac{\log2+k\log L}{D}+k\log H .                      \tag{8}
\]
These formulas allow negative \(g\), since the entire construction uses \(z^gQ(z)\) only outside the inner disk.

<a id="conclusion"></a>
## The degree bound for each factor

The union \(U=E_0^{[2]}\cup E_0^{[4]}\) contains the closed unit disk and every segment of \(K\).
Applying (5) and (6) to these two power images gives
\[
                   \log\operatorname{cap}U\le20\log\operatorname{cap}E_0 .
\]
On the other hand (3) gives \(\log\operatorname{cap}U\ge k/(2048a)\).
Combining this with (8) and \((\log2)/k\le\log2\) proves (2).
If \(D\ge\delta n\) and \(D\log H\le J\), the resulting positive degree proportion depends only on \(L,\delta,J\).
The polynomial \(f\) has at most \(2L\) nonzero coefficients, even when the blocks overlap, so [Dobrowolski's divisor estimate](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) gives a uniform positive logarithmic measure for these factors.
The root formula for a polynomial and its reciprocal gives \(m(A)\ge\log2\) whenever its absolute leading or constant coefficient is at least two.
An irreducible factor with zero constant coefficient is associated to \(X\) and has measure zero.
