# Exact factorization of the rational norm in the bounded-second-iterate construction

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which factors supply the exterior measure

Let \(F\) be a monic half polynomial of degree \(b\ge3\) over the quadratic extension \(K/E\) in the [single norm construction](Yin_C_G0901_v135.md#statement), with real exterior root \(R>1\) at the marked embedding.
The same assertions apply to any monic integral \(F\) with constant \(\pm1\), monic reciprocal \(F^\tau\), precisely one simple exterior root \(R>1\) and all other roots strictly inside at the marked place, and only neutral roots at every nonsplit place.
Here \(E\) is totally real of degree at least two, and \(K/E\) splits at just the marked real place.
Set \(H=N_{K/\mathbb Q}F\), \(A=M(H)=R^2\), and let \(\sigma>1\) be the primitive positive norm-one unit of \(K/E\) in the marked embedding; it is a real Salem number.

Taking the coefficient norm gives a bijection from the distinct monic \(K\)-irreducible factors of \(F\) to the distinct monic rational irreducible factors of \(H\), with the same multiplicities.
For every root \(y\) of a marked factor \(g\), \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(y)\) and \(\deg_{\mathbb Q}y=2[E:\mathbb Q]\deg g\).
Each \(g^\tau\) is its own factor's monic reciprocal.
Let \(g_0\) denote the factor containing \(R\).
For \(g\ne g_0\), its rational norm has measure \(M(Ng)=\sigma^{j_g}=1/|g(0)|\) for a positive integer \(j_g\) at the marked embedding.
Writing \(J\) for the sum of these exponents with multiplicity gives
\[
           |g_0(0)|=\sigma^J,\qquad M(R)=R^2/\sigma^J,\qquad
                          A/\sqrt{M(A)}=\sigma^J .
\]
The three cases are exact without a smallness cutoff:

* \(F\) is irreducible if and only if \(M(A)=A^2\). Then \(H\) is the minimum of \(R\) and \(M(R)=R^2\).
* \(g_0\) is linear if and only if \(M(A)=A\). Then \(R=\sigma^J\) is itself Salem.
* Otherwise \(F\) is reducible with \(\deg g_0>1\), \(A<M(A)<A^2\), and the directly extracted Salem power \(\sigma^J\) lies strictly between one and \(R\).

In particular the marked root of every reducible input over this pair of fields is at least \(\sigma\), uniformly over all half degrees and all choices of the integral seed.
Over a family with bounded \([E:\mathbb Q]\) this gives a uniform gap for the reducible inputs, by bounded-degree finiteness for Salem numbers in a bounded measure interval.
The irreducible square endpoints are still present in the construction.

<a id="factors"></a>
## Relative factors and their separate rational conjugate sets

The [archimedean root count](Yin_C_G0901_v131.md#delays) gives precisely two marked real coefficient polynomials, \(F\) and its monic reciprocal, with all other coefficient copies neutral.
The product of the \(b-1\) marked interior moduli of \(F\) is \(1/R\).
Since there are at least two, every one is strictly greater than \(1/R\).
Thus \(R\) occurs only once among all roots of \(H\), and all others have smaller modulus.
Consequently its powers retain its full degree.

Factor \(F\) over \(K\); these monic factors have integral coefficients and unit constants by integrality and \(F(0)=\pm1\).
At a nonsplit place all the roots of every factor \(g\) have modulus one, and \(\tau\) acts on coefficients there by complex conjugation.
Hence \(g(0)\tau g(0)=1\) by injectivity of this embedding.
The [relative unit group](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) now gives \(g(0)=\pm\sigma^j\).
Any factor omitting \(R\) is strictly interior at the marked place, so its constant has modulus \(\sigma^{-j_g}<1\).
The product of all constants gives \(|g_0(0)|=\sigma^J\).
The operation on monic factors \(g\mapsto(g^\tau)^*/\tau(g(0))\) permutes them with multiplicities by the reciprocal identity for \(F\) and preserves the absolute value of each marked constant by its relative norm one.
It fixes \(g_0\), trivially for irreducible \(F\), and otherwise because \(g_0\) is the unique factor whose marked constant has modulus greater than one.
It permutes the factors omitting \(R\) among themselves.
Thus for every such \(g\), all roots at its opposite real embedding are strictly exterior.

For an irreducible \(g\) over \(K\), \(Ng\) is a power of the rational minimum of any root: all conjugate polynomials and all of their roots form one rational Galois orbit before repetitions are removed.
For \(g_0\) the occurrence of \(R\) exactly once forces this power to have exponent one.
For every other \(g\), each of its marked interior roots occurs in just that one coefficient embedding in \(Ng\), as the opposite embedding is exterior and all complex embeddings are neutral.
The individual factor is separable, so the norm exponent is again one.
The equality of degrees \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]=2[E:\mathbb Q]\deg g=[K(y):\mathbb Q]\) gives the stated field inclusion.
No norm of another factor contains \(R\).
If the norms of two factors omitting \(R\) coincide, their complete sets of interior roots coincide with their respective marked root sets, hence the factors are identical.
Finally \(Ng\) has a neutral conjugate, so it is reciprocal up to its constant.
For \(g\ne g_0\) the inverses of all its marked roots must therefore be the roots at the only exterior coefficient embedding, which proves the individual monic reciprocal identity as well.

<a id="measures"></a>
## The defect and the three cases

For a factor omitting \(R\) only the opposite real conjugates contribute to its rational norm measure, with product \(|\tau g(0)|=1/|g(0)|=\sigma^{j_g}\).
For \(g_0\) the product of the moduli of its marked interior roots is \(\sigma^J/R\), including the empty product when \(g_0\) is linear.
The opposite copy is reciprocal, so the complete measure of its norm is \(R^2/\sigma^J\).
Since that norm is the minimum of \(R\) and \(R^2\) has full degree, \(M(A)=M(R)^2\).
These equations yield the displayed defect.
The exponent \(J\) vanishes exactly when \(F=g_0\) is irreducible, since \(g_0\) occurs once.
It has \(\sigma^J=R\) exactly when the product of interior moduli in \(g_0\) is one, equivalently there are no such roots.
In that case \(R=\sigma^J\) with \(J>0\), while reducibility with \(\deg g_0>1\) gives \(1<\sigma^J<R\).
This proves all three alternatives.
