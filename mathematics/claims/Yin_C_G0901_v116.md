# All small unit minima whose measure is the square of a real exterior conjugate through degree 45

<a id="statement"></a>
## The 27 square-root polynomials

Let \(x\) be any nontorsion algebraic unit of degree at most 45 and multiplicative Mahler measure \(M(x)\le13/10\).
Then \(M(x)=|a|^2\) for some real exterior conjugate \(a\) of \(x\) if and only if the monic minimum of \(x\) is \(Q(X^2)\), where \(Q\) is the minimum of a positive Salem number \(\theta<13/10\) of degree at most 22.
There are exactly 27 such minima, one for each [listed Salem row](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0192/readme.md#lifts) after orienting its exterior root positively.
They have degrees \(16,20,24,28,32,36,40,44\) with respective counts \(1,5,1,4,1,7,4,4\).
Every root of each of these polynomials qualifies as an input, giving 872 distinct algebraic units.
Its only exterior conjugates are \(\sqrt\theta,-\sqrt\theta\), so its measure is \(\theta\) and neither exterior conjugate is strictly dominant in modulus.
Its first measure is fixed by further Mahler iteration since \(M(\theta)=\theta\).

In particular there is no equality in the given degree and measure range if the input is compressed, if its minimum is exponent-primitive, or if the specified real exterior conjugate is required to be a Perron root up to sign.
Here compressed means that distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient, and exponent-primitive means the minimum cannot be written as \(H(X^g)\) with \(g>1\).

The least-degree example has polynomial \(X^{16}-X^{10}-X^8-X^6+1\).
The least attainable measure is Lehmer's number, and the polynomial of the inputs attaining it in this classification is
\[
            X^{20}+X^{18}-X^{14}-X^{12}-X^{10}-X^8-X^6+X^2+1 .
\]
These examples involve a real exterior conjugate of the original input itself.

<a id="cores"></a>
## Exact exclusion on all exponent-primitive rows

The product of the exterior conjugates of a unit, times a rational sign, is its positive multiplicative measure.
The measure is an algebraic integer and cannot equal the nonintegral rational \(13/10\).
Below \(13/10\), Smyth's nonreciprocal bound gives a reciprocal minimum; its degree is even since an odd reciprocal polynomial has the root \(-1\). [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions)
The complete list of exponent-primitive irreducible noncyclotomic polynomials below this cutoff through degree 44 is available up to \(X\mapsto-X\), which preserves the measure and the absolute values of the real exterior roots. [Catalogue coverage](../audits/literature/Mos26/readme.md#complete-small-cores)

The [exact coefficient test](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0192/readme.md#comparison) exhausts all 944 catalogue rows in this range.
The trace Sturm signatures leave 534 rows without real exterior roots and 46 signed Salem rows, where the sole exterior modulus is already \(M(x)>1\).
For each of the other 364 rows, iterated integer root squaring encloses \(\sqrt{M(x)}\) between rational endpoints.
Sturm counts in both signed trace intervals separate those endpoints from every possible \(|a|\) after the monotone change \(r\mapsto r+r^{-1}\), \(r>1\).
There are 304 real exterior roots with square below the measure and 123 with square above it over all 944 rows, and none with equality.
This tests all real exterior conjugates without assuming that the chosen one is the root of largest modulus.

## Substitutions, torsion classes and attainment

Write the monic minimum as \(P(X)=Q(X^g)\), where \(g\) is the greatest common divisor of the positive exponents with nonzero coefficient.
The monic \(Q\) is exponent-primitive, irreducible by irreducibility of \(P\), and has the same measure since the \(g\) preimages of each root contribute its original factor to the measure.
If \(P(a)=0\), \(a\) is real exterior, and \(M(P)=|a|^2\), then \(a^g\) is a real exterior root of \(Q\) and
\[
                      |a|^g\le M(Q)=|a|^2 .
\]
Thus \(g\le2\), and the coefficient comparison excludes \(g=1\).
For \(g=2\), \(a^2>1\) itself equals \(M(Q)\) and is the sole exterior root of \(Q\).
Reciprocity gives its inverse as the sole interior root, and all others are neutral.
A real reciprocal quadratic unit greater than one has integer trace at least three and exceeds \(13/10\), so \(a^2\) is a Salem number and the degree of its minimum is at most 22.

Conversely each positive Salem number \(\theta<13/10\) of degree at most 22 has an exponent-primitive minimum \(Q\), since any nontrivial substitution would give more than one exterior root.
These are exactly the [27 oriented rows](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0192/readme.md#lifts).
For each, \(\sqrt\theta\notin\mathbb Q(\theta)\).
Indeed, if its positive real square root belonged to that field, it would generate the field since its square does, and its conjugates would square bijectively onto those of \(\theta\).
It would be another positive Salem number of degree at most 22, with measure \(\sqrt\theta<\sqrt{13/10}<8/7\).
Every positive Salem row of those degrees exceeds \(8/7\) by the exact evaluations, a contradiction.
Thus \(Q(X^2)\) has a root of degree \(2\deg Q\) and is irreducible.
Its reciprocal pairs, measure and real exterior roots now follow from the roots of the substitution.
The rational comparison at \(59/50\) isolates the Lehmer row from the other 26, proving the asserted least measure; the degree-eight row gives the unique least-degree example.

In the orbit of a Salem number no two distinct roots have torsion quotient, because an automorphism sending the first to the unique exterior root would give a second conjugate with that same modulus.
It follows that the torsion classes of the roots of \(Q(X^2)\) consist exactly of the two square roots of each conjugate \(\theta_i\).
Their products are \(-\theta_i\), so the compressed minimum is \(Q(-X)\) and has just one real exterior root up to conjugation.
The exterior-square equality on the uncompressed input therefore comes from the two actual roots in its exterior torsion class.
The reduction by exponents exhausts arbitrary original minima, including those arising from torsion twists.
