# The exterior roots of a critical factor occupy almost all seed poles in proportion to its degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## The occupation numbers and the exterior fraction

Fix \(C,K>0\) and let \(s\to\infty\) through the [critical six-term sections](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement).
Write \(r=b/a\), \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and let \(P\) be a monic nontorsion irreducible factor of \(g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) having \(k\le Ka\) exterior roots, where \(s\le r\le Cs^3\).
Keep the coprimality and positivity conditions of the cited statement.
Put \(d=\deg P\), \(m=\log M(P)\), \(D=d/r\), and \(h=am\).
Write \(\zeta_j\) for the interior zeros of \(g\), \(L_j=-\log|\zeta_j|\), and \(t_s\) for their number.
Each exterior root \(x\) is assigned to the unique \(\zeta_j\) near \(x^{-a}\).

For any \(q\mid a\), \(q>0\), take the \(q\) analytic branches of the \(q\)-th root on each localization disk and let \(n_{j,l}\) count the exterior roots whose \(x^{-a/q}\) lies on branch \(l\), \(1\le l\le q\).
Set
\[
       e_q=s^{-1/16}+\frac{q\log(2d)}{D}.
\]
Uniformly over these divisors and all critical factors,
\[
 \frac1q\sum_{j,l}L_j\left(\frac{q n_{j,l}}D-1\right)^2\ll e_q,\qquad
 \frac1{qt_s}\sum_{j,l}\left|\frac{q n_{j,l}}D-1\right|
                  \ll\sqrt{e_q\log(2s)} .                                      \tag{1}
\]
All implied constants depend at most on \(C,K\).
In particular the relative occupation tends to one on all but a vanishing fraction of the branches whenever \(q\log(2d)\log(2s)=o(D)\).
At the original poles \(q=1\), this yields the explicit exterior fraction
\[
            \frac{k}{d}=\frac{t_s}{r}
                  \left(1+O\bigl(s^{-1/32}\sqrt{\log(2s)}\bigr)\right)
                       \sim \frac{s}{3r}.                                      \tag{2}
\]
These are necessary conditions on each individual factor in a hypothetical sequence.

<a id="means"></a>
## Quantitative comparison of the two logarithmic averages

Write \(\mu=\int_{|z|=1}\log^+|1+z|\,dz\) with normalized Haar measure.
We need
\[
                 \sum_j L_j=\mu+O(s^{-1/16}),\qquad h/D=\mu+O(s^{-1/16}).       \tag{3}
\]
The [critical scales](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#scales) give \(r\asymp s^3,\ d\asymp as^2,\ h\asymp a/s,\ k\asymp a\) and \(\log a\gg s^{1/3}\log s\).
In particular \(D\asymp a/s\) and \(\log(2d)/D\) decays faster than every fixed inverse power of \(s\).

Put \(\delta=s^{-1/16},\ H=\lfloor s^{1/4}\rfloor\) and \(f_\delta(z,w)=\log\max(\delta,|w-z-1|)\) on the two-torus.
This test has supremum norm \(O(\log(2s))\) and Lipschitz constant \(O(\delta^{-1})\) in angular coordinates.
Convolving in each variable with the normalized Fejér kernel of order \(H\) gives a polynomial of frequencies \(|i|,|j|\le H\) within \(O(\delta^{-1}\log(2H)/H)\) uniformly, with each Fourier coefficient \(O(\log(2s))\).
Indeed the kernel on \([-\pi,\pi]\) is bounded by a constant times \(\min(H,1/(Ht^2))\), so its first absolute moment is \(O(\log(2H)/H)\).

For \(z\) uniform on the circle, the graph \((z,z^s)\) has exactly zero mean on every nontrivial character of this polynomial once \(H<s\).
For the circle conjugates of \(y=x^a\) weighted by \(1/d\), the corresponding mean at frequency \(i+sj\ne0\) is \(O(\sqrt H/s+s^{-2})\), uniformly in the same box.
To see this apply the [compact discrepancy estimate](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) to the minimal polynomial of \(y^{i+sj}\), with smoothing radius the reciprocal of its degree and the fixed compact phase tests of the [circle-root calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#circle).
The power has height \(O(Hs/r)=O(H/s^2)\) and degree at least \(d/(H(s+1))\), and omission of the off-circle terms of the bounded test costs \(O(s^{-2})\).
The circle conjugates have total mass \(1-O(s^{-2})\).
Thus both averages of \(f_\delta\) differ from its Haar torus integral by at most
\[
 O\bigl(\delta^{-1}\log(2H)/H+ H^2\log(2s)(\sqrt H/s+s^{-2})\bigr)
                      =O(s^{-1/16}).
\]
For the single Haar graph and for the full torus, removing the logarithmic truncation costs \(O(\delta)\), since \(|w-z-1|\le v\) requires \(\big||1+z|-1\big|\le v\), a set of circle length \(O(v)\) for small \(v\).
For the discrete circle-root average the [truncation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#tails) gives \(O(\delta+s^{-1/2}\log(2s))\).
The torus integral of the untruncated logarithm is \(\mu>0\) by Jensen's formula.
The single Haar graph integral equals \(m(g)=\sum_j L_j\) because \(g\) is a monic unit polynomial.
For the second mean the [integral norm computation](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#norm) gives the circle-root average as \((r-s)h/d+O((a+k\log(2s))/d)\).
Since \(s/r=O(s^{-2})\) and \(h/D\) is bounded, these estimates prove (3).

<a id="variance"></a>
## Both sides of the projected discriminant

For \(z=x^{-a}\) in the disk of \(\zeta_j\), [localization](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#collisions) gives \(|z-\zeta_j|\ll\exp(-rL_j+O(rs^{-4}))\ll e^{-rL_j}\), using \(|g(z)|\le3|z|^r\), the uniform derivative lower bound there and \(-\log|z|=L_j+O(s^{-4})\).
Also \(L_j\gg s^{-2}\), \(1/2<|\zeta_j|<1\), and \(rL_j\gg s\).
On each inverse branch over the disk, the derivative of \(z^{1/q}\) has magnitude \(O(1/q)\), uniformly even for growing \(q\).
Two points \(u,u'\) from that branch are within \(O(e^{-rL_j}/q)\), while \(1-|u||u'|\gg L_j/q\).
Consequently
\[
       \log\left|\frac{u-u'}{1-u\overline{u'}}\right|
                  \le -rL_j+O(\log(2s)).
\]
The reciprocal factor \(P\) has the same projected interior and reflected exterior clusters, since \(u\mapsto1/\bar u\) permutes the projected conjugates.
The projected minimum of \(x^{-a/q}\) has degree \(d\) by [power-field preservation](Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#statement) and log measure \(h/q\).
In its [reflected discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v401.md#collision), each unordered pair has squared distance and each interior pair has a separate exterior partner.
The nonzero integral discriminant therefore gives
\[
       2r(1-O(\log(2s)/s))\sum_{j,l}L_j n_{j,l}(n_{j,l}-1)
                         \le d\log d+2(d-1)h/q .                              \tag{4}
\]
The loss at one occurrence of each root is controlled by
\[
          H_1:=\sum_{j,l} L_j n_{j,l}=h+O(k e^{-c s}),
\]
because \(h\) is the sum of \(-\log|x^{-a}|\) over the exterior roots and every localization error is exponentially small.
Multiplying (4) by \(q/(2rD^2)\) and adding \(qH_1/D^2\) yields
\[
      \frac{q}{D^2}\sum_{j,l}L_j n_{j,l}^2
              \le \frac hD+O\!\left(\frac{\log(2s)}s+
                                         \frac{q\log(2d)}D\right).
\]
Here \(H_1\asymp D\), and the reciprocal of the factor \(1-O(\log(2s)/s)\) is bounded for large \(s\).
Expanding the square in (1) adds \(\sum_j L_j-2H_1/D\).
Equation (3) cancels the two leading contributions and gives the first bound.

<a id="unweighted"></a>
## Controlling the poles nearest the unit circle

For \(0<u\le u_0\) small, \(sL_j\le u\) puts \(\zeta_j\) within \(O(u)\) of a primitive cube root \(\omega\), by the [radial separation argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles).
Since \(|g(\omega)|\ge1\), Jensen's disk formula on a circle of twice that radius gives \(O(1+su)\) zeros in either disk.
Indeed throughout the larger disk the maximum of \(|g|\) is at most \((1+O(u))^s+O(1)\), and each zero in the smaller disk contributes at least \(\log 2\).
As \(sL_j\gg1/s\), dyadic summation from \(1/s\) to \(u_0\) gives \(\sum_j(sL_j)^{-1}\ll s\log(2s)\), including the \(O(s)\) larger terms with \(sL_j>u_0\).
Thus \(\sum_j L_j^{-1}\ll s^2\log(2s)\).
The [exact pole count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v230.md#poles) gives \(t_s=s/3+O(1)\).
Cauchy--Schwarz in the first bound of (1), with the second factor \((q\sum_j L_j^{-1})^{1/2}\), gives the second bound.
At \(q=1\), summing the signed occupation errors gives (2).
