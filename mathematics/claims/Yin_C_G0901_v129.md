# Square classes of Salem traces at the conjugates of one cyclotomic trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## Large multiquadratic degree with small finite ramification support

Fix \(M>0\).
Let \(\alpha>1\) be a genuine Salem number of degree \(2n\), with \(m=\log\alpha\le M\), and let \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\) be the distinct conjugates of \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), with \(t_1=t\).
Write \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\), let \(S\) be its normal closure, and put \(T=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{tr}}\), the field of all totally real algebraic numbers.
At sufficiently large \(n\) in terms of \(M\), use the canonical partition of the traces into \(u\) blocks of size \(b=n/u\), with \(u\ll_M(\log(3n))^{212}\).
Write \(E\subset F\) for the field fixed in \(S\) by the stabilizer of the block through \(t\).
It contains every proper subfield of \(F\). [Trace field lattice](Yin_C_G0345_v277.md#lattice)

Take any prime \(b/(2\log(2b))\le p\le 2b/\log(2b)\); there is such a prime for all sufficiently large \(b\) by Bertrand's postulate.
Put \(k=(p-1)/2\), \(\lambda_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\) for \(1\le j\le k\), \(C=\mathbb Q(\lambda_1)\), and \(D=SC\).
For some absolute \(c>0\), the \(nk\) nonzero algebraic integers
\[
                              t_i-\lambda_j
\]
have square-class rank at least \(cnp\) in \(T^\times/T^{\times2}\).
In particular
\[
      [T(\sqrt{t_i-\lambda_j}:i,j):T]\ \ge\ 2^{cnp},
                   \qquad np\gg_M n^2/(\log(3n))^{213}.                   \tag{1}
\]
Already the single row \(t-\lambda_j\) has rank at least \(cp\) in \(T^\times/((EC)^\times T^{\times2})\).
Thus multiplying its products by scalars from the compositum of \(C\) and all proper subfields of \(F\) still leaves that many independent sign conditions.

Every radicand has absolute logarithmic Weil height at most \(\log(2+2\cosh M)\).
Over the finite base \(D\) the multiquadratic extension by the same radicals is unramified at all odd primes away from the single positive rational integer
\[
      R_p=|N_{F/\mathbb Q}\Psi_p(t)|,\qquad
      \Psi_p(Y)=\prod_{j=1}^k(Y-\lambda_j).
\]
Uniformly over the stated primes and bounded measures, \(\log R_p=o(n)\).
More precisely, for every integer \(L\ge2\),
\[
       \frac{\log R_p}{n}\le\frac{\log L}{L-1}
                                  +\frac{L+1}{3}\,\frac{pm}{2n}.          \tag{2}
\]
The ramification statement is relative to \(D\) and allows dyadic ramification.

There is also an exact rank formula at the smaller conductors.
For a sufficiently small absolute \(c_0>0\), every odd prime
\[
                p\ \le\ c_0\sqrt{b/(M+u\log(2b))}
\]
has the following property at sufficiently large \(n\).
Over any \(D\subseteq D'\subseteq T\) the rank of all \(t_i-\lambda_j\) equals
\[
        (n-u)k+
        \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\big\langle
             [\textstyle\prod_{i\in B}(t_i-\lambda_j)]:
                     B\text{ a block},\ 1\le j\le k
                                    \big\rangle_{D'^\times/D'^{\times2}} . \tag{3}
\]
The corresponding single row has rank exactly \(k\) modulo \((EC)^\times T^{\times2}\).
The conditions above are necessary consequences for real Salem numbers and allow arbitrarily small positive \(m\).

<a id="occupancy"></a>
## How many of the phase intervals are occupied

Every conjugate class of \(\alpha\) modulo roots of unity is a singleton, since any purported equality of classes between distinct conjugates can be transported to the unique exterior root.
Thus its multiplicative labels are the inverse pairs, equivalently the traces.
The [canonical alternating actions](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) give the stated blocks and a subgroup \(H=(A_b)^u\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}(S/\mathbb Q)\) acting independently on them.
Each block gives \(2b\) roots of the Salem minimum.
Represent each neutral trace by its angle in \((0,\pi)\), and the exterior trace by angle zero.
Partition \([0,\pi]\) at the \(k\) cuts \(2\pi j/p\).
None of the traces equals a cut value, since equality would give a root of unity as a Salem conjugate.
Write \(N_{Ba}\) for the number of traces of block \(B\) in interval \(a\), and \(l_B\) for the number of its occupied intervals.

For each full block of \(2b\) roots \(z\), let \(\beta_z=z/\max(1,|z|)^2\), take \(\rho=\exp(-1/(2b))\) and set
\[
  \mathcal E_B=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2j}}j
                           \left|\sum_{z\in B}\beta_z^j\right|^2 .
\]
Here the inner sum uses both roots of every label in the block.
The [relative discriminant energy](Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#energy) gives
\[
                    \sum_B\mathcal E_B\ll b m+ub\log(2b).                 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the proof for a monic factor bounds its energy by \(2(2b-1)m_B-\log|\Delta_B|+O(b\log(2b))\), where \(\Delta_B\) is its polynomial discriminant.
The discriminants have nonzero integral product by invariance of the partition, and \(\sum_B m_B=m\).
Write \(S_B(j)=\sum_{z\in B}e^{ij\arg z}\) over the same full block.
The replacement of \(\beta_z^j\) by the phase power changes only the two positive real roots, in just one block, by a total of at most two at each \(j\).
For \(1\le h\le b\), (4) consequently gives
\[
            \sum_B\sum_{j=1}^h|S_B(j)|^2
                \ll h\bigl(1+bm+ub\log(2b)\bigr).                        \tag{5}
\]

At the conductors of (1) take \(h=\lfloor p/4\rfloor\).
The Fejér kernel \(h^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}e^{ijx}|^2\) is nonnegative everywhere and at least \(c_1h\) for \(|x|\le2\pi/p\), by its sine quotient.
Two representatives from one interval have angle difference at most \(2\pi/p\).
Choose one root with the indicated nonnegative angle per label.
The sum of the kernel over all ordered pairs from the full block bounds its sum over these selected pairs in common intervals.
Its Fourier expansion and (5) therefore give
\[
       \sum_{B,a}N_{Ba}^2
            \ll ub^2/p+1+bm+ub\log(2b)
            \ll n\log(2b).
\]
The last constant is absolute once \(\log(2b)\ge M\).
Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\sum_{B,a}N_{Ba}=n\) yield
\[
               \sum_B(l_B-1)\gg n/\log(2b),\qquad
               \max_B(l_B-1)\gg p .                                      \tag{6}
\]
Here subtracting \(u=n/b\) costs a vanishing fraction.

At the conductors in (3) all \(k+1\) intervals are occupied in every block.
To check this use the [disk count for each block](Yin_C_G0345_v539.md#statement) on its \(2b\) roots.
Place a disk of radius \(r=1/(10p)\) on the unit circle at the angular midpoint of each interval, including the last interval of length \(\pi/p\).
Every such disk avoids the real axis, because the center has imaginary part at least \(\sin(\pi/(2p))\ge1/p\).
Any root in the disk is neutral and has angle in its interval, since the chord distance to either boundary point is at least \(2\sin(\pi/(4p))\ge1/p\).
The lower bound for its fraction of block roots is
\[
           r/(4\pi)-1/(2br)-
                           \sqrt{(2m+u(\log(2b)+4))/(4b)} .
\]
It is positive throughout the smaller conductor range after choosing \(c_0\) sufficiently small.

<a id="classes"></a>
## Subtracting signs using even permutations

For label \(i\) form \(s_i\in\mathbb F_2^k\) with coordinate one if \(t_i-\lambda_j<0\), zero otherwise, in the displayed real embedding.
As the angle increases, one further coordinate changes at each cut.
The differences among the \(s_i\) in a block therefore span a space of dimension \(l_B-1\): successive occupied intervals differ on nonempty, disjoint strings of coordinates.
The same rule applies to angle zero, since \(t_1>2\).
For both conductor ranges \(b>k+4\) at large \(n\), so after excluding any two labels from a block, two other labels still have the same vector by the pigeonhole principle.

The supported alternating group \(H\) fixes every block field and acts on \(D\) fixing \(C\).
Indeed \(S\cap C\) is abelian over \(\mathbb Q\); perfection of the factors of \(H\) gives \(H\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(S/S\cap C)\), whose action extends fixing \(C\).
For a family of coefficients \(a_i\in\mathbb F_2^k\), a product \(\prod_{i,j}(t_i-\lambda_j)^{a_{ij}}\) square in a totally real field must be positive at every embedding.
Hence \(\sum_i a_i\cdot s_{g i}=0\) for each \(g\in H\).
Fix two distinct source labels \(i,i'\) in a block and any two distinct destination labels \(x,x'\) there.
Choose \(g\in H\) sending \(i,i'\) to \(x,x'\), by double transitivity.
Compare with the even permutation obtained by composing \(g\) with the transposition of \(x,x'\) and a disjoint transposition of two labels having identical sign vectors.
Subtraction gives
\[
                           (a_i-a_{i'})\cdot(s_x-s_{x'})=0 .
\]
Thus, on each block the first coefficient row is unrestricted and each of its \(b-1\) differences from the remaining rows lies in a subspace of codimension \(l_B-1\).
The square-class rank is at least \((b-1)\sum_B(l_B-1)\), proving (1) by (6) and elementary multiquadratic theory.
When \(l_B=k+1\) for all blocks, every square relation must have constant coefficient rows on each block.
On that space the map to square classes is exactly the map given by the \(uk\) block products in (3).
Rank-nullity proves (3) over every indicated field, regardless of which block products become squares.

For a single row, let \(B_*\) maximize \(l_B\), and choose a rational Galois automorphism taking the distinguished label into \(B_*\).
It permutes the \(\lambda_j\), and transports \(EC\) to the block field of \(B_*\) times \(C\).
Suppose a row product times some nonzero scalar of \(EC\) is square in \(T\).
After transport its signs must be positive at all the embeddings given by \(A_b\) on \(B_*\).
The scalar is fixed there, so the transported coefficient vector is orthogonal to all sign differences in \(B_*\).
The transport permutes the coefficient coordinates by a single fixed permutation.
This bounds the original space of relations by \(k-(l_{B_*}-1)\) and proves both single-row assertions.

<a id="ramification"></a>
## The integer supporting the odd ramification

All conjugates of every \(t_i-\lambda_j\) have absolute value at most \(2+2\cosh M\), giving the stated height bound by integrality.
Their product over the entire rectangle, up to sign, is \(R_p\), even when \(C\) meets \(S\) nontrivially.
Each radicand therefore divides \(R_p\) in \(\mathcal O_D\).
At any prime of odd residue characteristic not dividing \(R_p\), adjoining the square root of a unit is split or unramified, by the separable reduction of its quadratic polynomial.
The full compositum stays unramified there.

The identity \(\alpha^k\Psi_p(t)=\Phi_p(\alpha)\) and relative degree two give
\[
       R_p^2=|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}\Phi_p(\alpha)|
                \le |N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^p-1)| ,
\]
since the omitted factor \(\alpha-1\) has nonzero integral norm.
The [complete cyclic norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) applied in the containing field to \(\alpha^p\), of absolute height \(pm/(2n)\), proves (2).
Here \(p/n\ll1/\log(2b)\) and \(b\to\infty\) uniformly for fixed \(M\).
Letting \(n\) tend to infinity and then \(L\) to infinity proves \(\log R_p=o(n)\).
The rank in (1) pertains to conjugate differences at one conductor; the [earlier independent signatures](Yin_C_G0901_v105.md#statement) use the individual cyclotomic norms or power traces in the original trace field.
