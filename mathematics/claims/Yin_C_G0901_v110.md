# Trace units and Frobenius cycles for the positive consecutive-coefficient sign

<a id="statement"></a>
## Necessary conditions at short exponent one

Let \(q\ge3\), \(p=q+1\), \(L=p+q\), \(M=pq\), and let \(v>1\) be an integer.
Suppose
\[
             F(X)=X^v(qX-p)+pX-q
\]
has a nontorsion algebraic-unit root at a positive common dilation, meaning that \(F(z^g)=0\) for such a unit \(z\) and some integer \(g\ge1\).
By the [positive-sign pole theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#sign), the primitive polynomial has a unique exterior root \(1<\alpha<p/q\), itself a reciprocal unit, and \(v>L\).
Write \(S\) for its minimal polynomial of degree \(2n\), \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(w=t-2\), \(B=\mathbb Q(w)\), and \(\delta=1-Mw\).
Then
\[
        M^n |S(1)|<\alpha^v<(p/q)^v,\qquad
        n<\frac{v\log(1+1/q)}{\log(q(q+1))}.                         \tag{1}
\]
For odd \(v\), \(\delta\) is a square in the trace unit group.
For even \(v\), \(S(-1)=1\) and \(\delta\) and \(t+2\) represent the same class in \(\mathcal O_B^\times/(\mathcal O_B^\times)^2\).
For each fixed trace \(w\) with the required conjugate locations, the pole norm is one at **at most one** positive real value of \(M<1/w\).

If \(v=\ell^a\) for an odd prime \(\ell\nmid L\), set
\[
  h=\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(L^2),\qquad
  h_a=h\prod_{r\mid h,\ r\ {\rm prime}} r^{v_r(a)}.
\]
Then \(h_a\mid n\).
More precisely, a Frobenius at \(\ell\) is defined without ramification in the splitting field of \(S\), and every cycle length \(f\) on inverse-pair labels satisfies \(f/\gcd(f,a)=h\).
Together with (1) this excludes every common dilation whenever \(h_a\ge v\log(1+1/q)/\log M\).
For instance \(q=3,\ v=223\) allows no nontorsion unit root.
These tests allow long indices outside the excluded cases, and existence for unbounded \(q\) remains open.

<a id="trace"></a>
## The degree and the uniqueness of the coefficient parameter

Let \(w=w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_n\) be the distinct trace conjugates.
Here \(0<w<1/M\), and \(-4<w_i<0\) for \(i>1\).
The signed pole norm gives \(N_{B/\mathbb Q}(1-Mw)=1\), and the [quadratic trace lift](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) gives \(|S(1)|=|N_{B/\mathbb Q}(w)|\).
Thus
\[
    \delta^{-1}=\prod_{i>1}(1+M|w_i|)
       \ \ge\ M^{n-1}\frac{|S(1)|}{w}
       \ >\ M^n|S(1)|.
\]
The equation at the exterior root yields \(\alpha^v\delta=(p\alpha-q)(p-q/\alpha)>1\) because both factors exceed \(p-q=1\).
This proves (1), since \(|S(1)|\) is a nonzero integer.

For a fixed \(w\) with this single positive conjugate and all the others negative, consider
\[
             f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n\log(1-xw_i),\qquad 0\le x<1/w .
\]
It starts at zero, tends to \(-\infty\) at the right endpoint, and has second derivative \(-\sum_i w_i^2/(1-xw_i)^2<0\).
Consequently it has a positive zero exactly when \(f'(0)=-\operatorname{Tr}_{B/\mathbb Q}(w)>0\), and that zero is unique.
In particular the same exterior root cannot work for two different positive coefficient parameters \(q\), even if their long indices differ.

<a id="squares"></a>
## Parity in the full trace unit group

Put \(Y=\alpha^v\), and write \(\iota\) for the nontrivial automorphism over \(B\).
The algebraic unit \(A=(p-q\alpha)^{-1}=(Y+1)/(\alpha+1)\) satisfies
\[
          \iota(A)=\alpha^{1-v}A,qquad \delta^{-1}=A^2\alpha^{1-v}.
\]
For odd \(v\) the unit \(A\alpha^{(1-v)/2}\) is fixed by \(\iota\) and has square \(\delta^{-1}\).
Its distinguished value exceeds one and each of its other conjugates has modulus less than one.
For even \(v\), use the trace identity
\[
      (\alpha^{v/2}+\alpha^{-v/2})^2=Y+Y^{-1}+2
                       =\frac{t+2}{\delta}.
\]
Here the last equality follows by expanding \((Y+1)^2/Y\) with \(Y=(p\alpha-q)/(p-q\alpha)\).
The even-index pole theorem gives \(S(-1)=1\), hence \(t+2\) is a unit because its norm is \(S(-1)\).
The square root on the left belongs to \(\mathcal O_B\) and its square is a unit, proving the assertion in the full trace unit group.
Applied to a candidate satisfying the signed norm and the real trace signature, failure of both applicable square tests rules out short exponent one at every index, even though the [two-power criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) permits other short exponents.

<a id="cycles"></a>
## The long prime power acts as a scalar after reduction

Use the projective coordinate \(Z=(X-1)/(X+1)\), with \(X=1,-1\) corresponding to zero and infinity.
On the graph \(Y=(pX-q)/(p-qX)\), the Cayley coordinate of \(Y\) is \(LZ\).
In characteristic \(\ell\), the Cayley coordinate of \(X^{\ell^a}\) is \(Z^{\ell^a}\), since \(\ell\) is odd.
Both identities hold as maps of projective lines, the graph map being invertible because \(p^2-q^2=L\ne0\bmod\ell\).
Thus the homogenized parent reduces, up to an invertible change of variables and a nonzero scalar, to
\[
                 Z_0^{\ell^a} Z_1-L Z_0 Z_1^{\ell^a}.
\]
The two coordinate axes are simple zeros and the remaining affine nonzero solutions satisfy \(Z^{\ell^a-1}=L\), all distinct.
This argument also works when \(\ell\mid pq\), since the projective degree is retained.
The odd-index integer polynomial \(F\) contains both linear factors \(X-1,X+1\); its monic unit factor \(S\) is disjoint from them before reduction and the homogenized factorization stays nonzero modulo \(\ell\) by primitivity.
Hence \(S\) reduces with distinct roots disjoint from both axes in the Cayley coordinate.
Hensel lifting its roots in the maximal unramified local extension shows that the splitting field is unramified at \(\ell\), with arithmetic Frobenius acting on roots by the residue power map.

Inversion in \(X\) changes the sign of \(Z\), so \(Z^2\) labels the distinct inverse pairs injectively after reduction.
The \(a\)-th power of Frobenius sends each \(Z^2\) to \(L^2 Z^2\); all its cycles have length exactly \(h\).
On any original Frobenius cycle of length \(f\), its \(a\)-th power has cycles of length \(f/\gcd(f,a)\).
For a prime \(r\mid h\) this forces \(v_r(f)=v_r(h)+v_r(a)\), hence \(h_a\mid f\).
Summing the cycle lengths gives \(h_a\mid n\).

For \(q=3\), the strict upper bound in (1) is less than \(v/8\), since \((4/3)^8<12\).
The numbers \(223\) and \(37\) are prime, and \(7^{37}\equiv1\pmod{223}\) with \(7\not\equiv1\), so the order of both \(7\) and \(7^2\) is \(37\).
For \(v=223\) the cycle condition requires \(37\mid n\), contradicting \(n<223/8\).
The [coefficient-prime norm condition](Yin_C_G0345_v878.md#norm) at this prime index gives \(37\le2n\), which by itself is consistent with (1).
One can also recover the stronger divisibility for this particular example by factoring the reduced parent at the coefficient prime \(7\): after removal of the two endpoints its roots have order \(223\), and inversion pairs have orbit length \(37\) there as well.
