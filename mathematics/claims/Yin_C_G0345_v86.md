# Primitive sampling below the full cyclotomic degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fixed fractional margin and two varying prime divisors

Fix \(r\ge1,\ B\ge0\), an integer \(H\ge0\), and \(0<\epsilon<1\).
Let \(n\ge2\) have at most \(r\) distinct prime divisors and \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero, with [primitive absolute average](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#statement) \(U_n(P)\le B\).
If
\[
                           \deg P\le (1-\epsilon)\varphi(n)+H ,
\]
then a product of boundedly many binomials \(1-X^l\) makes \(P\) an integer polynomial with bounded term count after multiplication.
The bounds depend only on \(B,r,H,\epsilon\).
There is consequently a uniform positive gap for \(\log M(P)>0\).

Alternatively fix any finite set of primes \(\mathcal S\), and suppose at most two distinct prime divisors of \(n\) lie outside \(\mathcal S\).
The same assertions then hold all the way up to \(\deg P\le\varphi(n)+H\), with bounds depending only on \(\mathcal S,B,H\).
Every prime-power exponent is unrestricted in both statements.

In particular consider \(1<M(P_i)\to1\) on inputs with bounded \(U_{n_i}(P_i)\), bounded numbers of distinct prime divisors, and \(\deg P_i\le\varphi(n_i)+H\).
Then \(\deg P_i/\varphi(n_i)\to1\) and the third-largest distinct prime divisor of \(n_i\) tends to infinity, taking that divisor as one if there are fewer than three.
The [exact mask degree below](#mask) also specifies an additive margin for each squarefree conductor before a fractional estimate is taken.

<a id="mask"></a>
## Removing the imprimitive roots with a bounded conductor factor

Write \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n)=c m\), where \(c\) belongs to a fixed finite set of positive integers and \(m\) is a product of distinct primes relatively prime to \(c\).
Put \(s=n/q\).
When \(m=1\), use the empty product and empty sum.
Define
\[
 Z(X)=\frac{(X^m)^c-1}{\Phi_c(X^m)}
                      \prod_{l\mid m,\ l\ {\rm prime}}\Phi_c(X^{m/l}) .
\]
This is an integer polynomial with all its roots on the unit circle.
The factor \((Y^c-1)/\Phi_c(Y)\) is an ordinary fixed polynomial in \(Y\), including the value one for \(c=1\).
The supremum of \(|Z|\) on the circle is bounded in terms of the finite set and \(r\).
For a \(q\)-th root whose order omits any divisor prime of \(c\), the first factor vanishes.
If its order contains \(c\) but omits a prime \(l\mid m\), raising to \(m/l\) leaves exact order \(c\), so the corresponding factor vanishes.
This covers all imprimitive points.

Writing \(\omega(m)\) for the number of prime divisors of \(m\), the excess \(E\) of \(\deg Z\) over the number \(q-\varphi(q)\) of imprimitive points is exactly
\[
 E=\varphi(c)m\left\{\sum_{l\mid m,\ l\ {\rm prime}}l^{-1}-1
                +\prod_{l\mid m,\ l\ {\rm prime}}(1-l^{-1})\right\}.
\]
It is zero for \(\omega(m)\le1\) and is \(\varphi(c)\) for \(\omega(m)=2\).
In general it is nonnegative and at most \(\varphi(c)m\sum_{l<t,\ l,t\mid m\ {\rm prime}}(lt)^{-1}\).
Indeed in independent Bernoulli trials with success probabilities \(1/l\), the expression in braces is the expectation of \(J-{\bf1}_{J>0}\) for the number \(J\) of successes, and \(0\le J-{\bf1}_{J>0}\le\binom J2\).
For example, at three distinct primes \(q=uvw\) with \(c=1\) the exact excess is \(u+v+w-1\).

<a id="clear"></a>
## From the degree of the mask to the original polynomial

More precisely suppose \(\deg P\le\varphi(n)+H\) and
\[
                 \left\lfloor\deg P/s\right\rfloor
                              \le\varphi(q)-E+J_0
\]
for a fixed integer \(J_0\ge0\).
The [primitive-component calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#support) gives \(P=\sum_{j<s}X^j P_j(X^s)\) with boundedly many nonzero \(P_j\) and \(U_q(P_j)\le B\).
For such a \(P_j\), \(\deg (ZP_j)\le q+J_0\) and the absolute average on all \(q\)-th roots is bounded, since the mask vanishes on every omitted sample.
By the [cyclic coefficient argument](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#squarefree), multiplying \(ZP_j\) by boundedly many binomials leaves boundedly many terms.
In that argument only the degree bound, the full-grid average and the cyclic coefficient decomposition are used after forming the masked polynomial, so the conclusion applies unchanged to this \(Z\).

Complete \(Z\) itself to a binomial product.
The first factor divides \((X^m)^c-1\) with quotient \(\Phi_c(X^m)\), and each further factor divides \((X^{m/l})^c-1\) with quotient \(((Y^c-1)/\Phi_c(Y))|_{Y=X^{m/l}}\).
The product of these quotients has boundedly many terms because \(c\) ranges over a fixed finite set and there are at most \(r+1\) factors.
Thus for each \(P_j\) a product of boundedly many binomials makes \(P_j\) sparse, allowing harmless overall signs.
Taking the product of all the block multipliers after substitution \(X^s\) gives the claim for \(P\) itself, since the number of nonzero blocks is bounded and the extra binomials have bounded term count.
The [bounded-term Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) now applies without changing \(M(P)\).

For the fractional margin, choose \(L\ge2\) so that \(\binom r2 L^{-2}\le\epsilon 2^{-r}\).
Take \(c\) to be the product of the primes dividing \(q\) that are at most \(L\).
It ranges over a fixed finite set, and the mask estimate gives \(E\le\epsilon\varphi(q)\), since \(\prod_{l\mid m}(1-1/l)\ge2^{-r}\) with the product over primes.
As \(\varphi(n)=s\varphi(q)\), the bound on \(\deg P\) gives the required inequality with \(J_0=H\).
For the second assertion take \(c\) to be the product of the prime divisors in \(\mathcal S\).
Then \(E\le\varphi(c)\) and \(J_0=H+\max_c\varphi(c)\) suffices.

On a sequence as in the statement, the degrees must tend to infinity since integer polynomials of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure have bounded coefficients.
Thus \(\varphi(n_i)\to\infty\), and failure of the asserted degree ratio would give a fixed fractional margin on a subsequence.
If the third-largest prime divisor stayed bounded on a subsequence, all but at most two prime divisors would lie in one fixed finite set there.
Each case contradicts the corresponding gap.
