# Counting different Mahler measures on one row at a fixed total budget

<a id="statement"></a>
## An integer sequence which stabilizes or tends to infinity

Use \(a=\max(2,\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor),\ b_0=\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor,\ b=b_0+1\) at each prime \(p\), and fix either sign \(\varepsilon\).
Put \(B=(\log2)/4\).
Enumerate nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree at most \(b\) and one-norm \(H\) satisfying the strict integer inequality
\[
                   \big((2pb+1)H^2\big)^4<2^{(a-1)p}.                  \tag{1}
\]
For each such row, form the graph \(A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^p)\), with \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\).
Count the **distinct positive measure values** \(m(P)\) of primitive irreducible factors \(P\) occurring at least \(a\) times, with a nonzero nontorsion root \(\alpha\) and \(A(\alpha^p)\ne0\).
Repeated values count once even if the factors have different degrees.
Let \(N_p\) be the greatest count on one row, with zero included as a default.

Let \(K(B)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\cup\{+\infty\}\) be the supremum of cardinalities of finite sets of distinct positive logarithmic measures of primitive irreducible integer polynomials with sum strictly below \(B\).
Then \(N_p\) is computable, \(N_p\le K(B)\) at every prime, and
\[
                        \lim_{p\to\infty}N_p=K(B).                       \tag{2}
\]
If \(K(B)\) is finite, equality holds at every sufficiently large prime.
If it is infinite, every fixed count is reached or exceeded at every sufficiently large prime.
Thus the unrestricted Lehmer gap is positive exactly when \(N_p\) is bounded on some infinite set of primes.
A zero gap is equivalent to \(N_p\to\infty\).
In the latter case one maximizing row supplies \(N_p\) different measures with sum below \(B\), and in particular an actual positive measure below \(B/N_p\).
The bound \(B/N_p\) applies at every prime with \(N_p>0\), independently of the limiting case.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Counting spectral values rather than polynomials

For any row and any collection of different qualifying factors, the [powered-value product](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) gives
\[
        (a-1)p\sum_i m(P_i)\le 2\log H+\log(2pb+1).
\]
Using one factor per distinct measure proves that the sum is strictly below \(B\) by (1).
Every root used is recovered in the field of its \(p\)-th power by the [exact elimination test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination).
This requires factoring a polynomial of degree at most \(2b\).
The numbers \(M(P_i)=\exp m(P_i)\) are positive real algebraic numbers, so equality of two measures can be tested by exact algebraic comparison.
All row coefficient bounds are finite and effective.
These facts prove computability, the pointwise upper bound on \(N_p\) and the assertion about its least recovered measure.

Every positive measure below \(B\) in the definition of \(K(B)\) belongs to a monic reciprocal unit minimum after choosing positive leading coefficient.
A nonunit end costs at least \(\log2\), and the positive-measure polynomial cannot have a zero root by irreducibility.
[Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) bounds a nonreciprocal unit below by the logarithm of the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
That root exceeds \(6/5>2^{1/4}\), as direct substitution shows.
Thus all the measures relevant to this budget have reciprocal representatives.

<a id="supply"></a>
## Putting a fixed list of unequal measures on the same signed row

Fix distinct positive values \(h_i=m(P_i)\), \(1\le i\le s\), with \(S=\sum_i h_i<B\), and choose the above reciprocal irreducible representatives.
For every sufficiently large prime, the powered minima \(Q_i\) of their chosen roots have the same degrees as the original minima and measure \(ph_i\) by the [power-field calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#power).
In particular the \(Q_i\) are pairwise distinct even if several \(P_i\) have equal degree.
Set \(h_{\min}=\min_i h_i>0\), choose a fixed integer \(K>S/h_{\min}+1\), and put \(t=a+K\).
We shall keep order \(a\) at every listed factor.

Use the [simultaneous divided-derivative count](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#seeds) with total degree \(D=\sum_i\deg P_i\), \(n=2(b_0+1)\) coefficient positions and largest possible exponent bounded by \(L=p(b_0+1)\).
In the count, the product of the archimedean contributions is now \(\exp(tLS)\), since the measures need not be equal.
A nonzero integral two-class graph \(F_0=U_0(X^p)+XV_0(X^p)\) containing every \(P_i^t\) hence satisfies
\[
 \log\|F_0\|_1\le
       \frac{tLS+Dt\log(6n)+Dt(t-1)\log(1+L)/2}{n-Dt}+\log(2n).
\]
Here \(D,S,K\) are fixed while \(a,b_0\) grow.
Since \(L/n=p/2\) and \(t^2/n=O(p^{-5/8})\), this yields
\(2\log\|F_0\|_1\le p(tS+o(1))\).
If only one row occurred it would contain \(\prod Q_i^t\) before substitution, contradicting this cost for large \(p\).

Write \(J\) for the primitive polynomial gcd of the two rows and \(c_i\) for its order at a root of \(Q_i\).
The [divisibility after extracting the gcd](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) applies to the entire list, whether or not \(c_i<t\).
It gives \(\prod Q_i^{t+c_i-1}\mid pY(U'_0V_0-U_0V'_0)-U_0V_0\).
Comparing the measure of this product with the coefficient bound for the derivative polynomial yields
\[
                            \sum_i c_i h_i\le S+o(1).
\]
Hence \(c_i\le K\) for every \(i\) eventually.
Dividing by \(J\) leaves order at least \(a\) everywhere and coprimality gives nonzero row values at each powered node.
As in the [simultaneous sign choice](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention), take the two signed projections of the divided rows, apply \(Y+1\) or \(Y-1\) to give both the prescribed sign at width \(b\), and use a slope in \(0,\ldots,s\) to avoid vanishing at every chosen node.
Division and projection increase the one-norm by at most the factor \(4(1+s)2^{b_0}\).
Since \(b_0=o(p)\), the resulting row satisfies
\[
               \limsup_{p\to\infty}
                 \frac{2\log H+\log(2pb+1)}{(a-1)p}\le S<B .
\]
It therefore satisfies (1) at every sufficiently large prime and contributes all \(s\) different measures.
Each fixed finite set of values below the total budget has this property.
An integer supremum, when finite, is attained as a cardinality, so the pointwise bound and this construction prove (2), also in the infinite case.

<a id="consequence"></a>
## A two-value test against the degree-ten example

If the unrestricted infimum \(\lambda\) is positive then \(K(B)\le B/\lambda\).
If \(\lambda=0\), for every \(s\) there are \(s\) distinct positive irreducible measures each below \(B/(s+1)\), by factoring integer polynomials of arbitrarily small positive measure and successively decreasing the threshold.
Their sum is below \(B\), so \(K(B)=+\infty\).
This proves both gap equivalences without estimating how rapidly new values appear.

Let \(\rho\) be the exterior root of the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#minus-delay-progression) and let \(h_L=\log\rho\).
One has \(11/10<\rho<59/50\).
Indeed its displayed integer polynomial has opposite signs, negative and positive respectively, at these rationals and has only one real root greater than one.
Also \((11/10)^8>2\) and \((59/50)^4<2\).
It follows that \(B/2<h_L<B\).
The fixed one-element set \(\{h_L\}\) therefore gives \(N_p\ge1\) eventually.

A **single** instance \(N_p\ge2\), or an explicit qualifying row with two different measures, would give a primitive irreducible factor with \(M(P)<2^{1/8}<\rho\).
Conversely, a proof that \(N_p\le1\) on infinitely many primes would imply \(K(B)=1\).
Every other positive irreducible measure \(h\) would then satisfy \(h+h_L\ge B\), and \(h_L>B/2\) gives the explicit unrestricted bound \(\lambda\ge B-h_L>0\), including scalar contents and products by factorization.
No prime with two such different values or infinite prime exclusion is established here.
