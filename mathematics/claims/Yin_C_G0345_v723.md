# Simultaneous resultant bounds at the primes of a cyclotomic conductor

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coordinate subgroups and the measure of a generator

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer whose positive powers all generate \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and put \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(m=\log M(\alpha)\).
For any \(N\ge2\), take \(E=\mathbb Q(\mu_N)\), \(F=K\cap E\), \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(h=[E:F]\) and \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\).
In the Chinese remainder decomposition of \(G=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\), let \(I_p\le\Gamma\) be the restriction to \(F\) of the full \(p\)-power coordinate group, acting trivially in every other coordinate.
Define
\[
       e=\max_{p\mid N}|I_p|,\qquad
       C_N=\prod_{p\mid N}(1-1/p)p^{1/(p-1)} .
\]
Along any sequence with \(hm\to0\), allowing the conductor and all its prime factors to vary,
\[
                  \limsup\frac{e\log(1/(hm))}{\log d}\le1 .          \tag{1}
\]
If \(e\ge\eta\log(2d)\) for some \(\eta>0\), then \(hm\) has an effective positive lower bound depending only on \(\eta\).
If \(\log h=o(\log(1/m))\) along a sequence with \(m\to0\), (1) holds with \(\log(1/m)\) in the numerator.
For general nonzero nontorsion algebraic integers the same conclusions hold in the fields of the [torsion-quotient class products](Yin_C_G0345_v714.md#scope), with the original total logarithmic measure.

The following finite bound uses all the coordinate subgroups together.
Take any subgroup \(B\le\Gamma\) of size \(b\), let \(e_p=|B\cap I_p|\), and choose integers \(J_p\ge1\) with \(J=\prod_{p\mid N}J_p\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{p\mid N}(e_pJ_p-1)\log p
 \ \le\ \log\!\left(\frac bs Jd C_N\right)
  +\left(2-\frac{2}{bJhd}\right)bhm
                    \prod_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{J_p}-1}{p-1}.           \tag{2}
\]
For example, suppose \(I_p=\Gamma\) for every \(p\mid N\).
Taking all \(J_p=1\) and \(B=\Gamma\) gives
\[
                   (s-1)\log\operatorname{rad}(N)
                          \le\log(d C_N)+2shm .                      \tag{3}
\]
At a conductor with at least two prime factors such a field \(F\) meets each individual prime-power component of \(E\) only in \(\mathbb Q\).
The bounds (1) and (3) then use \(e=s\) even though all those individual intersections have degree one.

<a id="grid"></a>
## Frobenius congruences for primitive twists

Put \(L=KE\), choose a primitive \(N\)-th root \(\xi\), and identify \(\Gamma=G/H\), where \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) by restriction.
For \(p\mid N\) take \(g_p\in G\) to be one in the \(p\)-power coordinate and \(p\) in every other coordinate.
For \(0\le j_p<J_p\) write \(u=\prod p^{j_p}\) and \(g=\prod g_p^{j_p}\).
At this level use the twists \(\xi^{xg}\alpha^u\), one for each \(xH\in B\).
Changing \(x\) within its coset applies an automorphism over \(K\) and leaves the minimal polynomial unchanged.
Every twist generates \(L\) because its \(N\)-th power generates \(K\) and \(\xi^{xg}\) is primitive.
Within a level distinct cosets give distinct minimal polynomials, since any embedding identifying two twists would fix \(\alpha^{uN}\), and hence \(K\).
Each polynomial has degree \(hd\) and logarithmic measure \(hum\).
Distinct levels have distinct \(u\) and hence distinct measures.
This is the field and measure argument used for [one prime tower](Yin_C_G0345_v722.md#orbits).

Fix \(p\mid N\), keep all the indices other than \(j_p\) fixed, and reduce at a prime over \(p\) in a common normal closure.
For a fixed \(j_p\), cosets of \(H\) in the same coset of \(B\cap I_p\) within \(B\) have representatives differing by an element supported in the \(p\)-power coordinate.
The quotient of the corresponding torsion factors has \(p\)-power order and reduces to one at every conjugate.
Their reduced minimal polynomials therefore agree with all multiplicities.
Increasing \(j_p\) by one multiplies \(u\) by \(p\), and the new torsion factor differs from the \(p\)-th power of the old one only by a \(p\)-power root of unity, by the definition of \(g_p\).
Thus its conjugate root multiset after reduction is the Frobenius image of the previous reduced multiset, which equals that multiset because its polynomial has coefficients in \(\mathbb F_p\).
For each \(p\) we have partitioned the \(bJ\) polynomials into classes of size \(e_pJ_p\) with equal coefficients modulo \(p\) within each class.
Further coincidences can only increase resultant divisibility.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Adding the contributions at different primes

Let \(D=bJhd\), the degree of the product of all the twist polynomials.
For each ordered pair of distinct members of a congruence class at \(p\), its nonzero integer resultant is divisible by \(p^{hd}\) by evaluating the coefficient difference at the \(hd\) roots of the first member.
For a given pair the contributions at distinct primes multiply in the same integer.
There are \(bJ(e_pJ_p-1)\) such ordered pairs at \(p\).
The [product discriminant and Vandermonde estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v707.md#resultants) therefore gives, after taking logarithms and dividing by \(D\),
\[
 \sum_{p\mid N}(e_pJ_p-1)\log p+\frac{1}{D}\sum_T\log|\operatorname{disc}T|
 \le \log D+\left(2-\frac2D\right)\sum_T\log M(T).
\]
Here each \(T\) generates \(L\), and hence its root discriminant is at least the root discriminant of \(L\) and in turn that of \(E\), by the power-basis index and the discriminant tower formula.
The latter is \(N/\prod_{p\mid N}p^{1/(p-1)}=\varphi(N)/C_N\) by the [cyclotomic discriminant formula](../audits/literature/Bel17/Yin_U002.md#bel17-u002).
Subtracting its logarithm gives \(\log((b/s)Jd C_N)\), since \(\varphi(N)=sh\).
Summing \(hum\) over the levels and twists gives the product of the finite geometric sums in (2).

<a id="limits"></a>
## Varying conductors

Choose \(p\) with \(|I_p|=e\), take \(B=I_p\), and set the other \(J_q\) equal to one.
For \(hm\le1\) put \(Y=\log(1/(hm))\), \(J_p=1+\lfloor Y/\log p\rfloor\).
Thus \(p^{J_p-1}hm\le1\) and \(J=J_p\le1+Y/\log2\).
Discarding the nonnegative terms for \(q\ne p\) in (2) gives
\[
              (e-1)Y\le\log d+\log C_N+\log J+4e .                  \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(J\log p>Y\), \(e/s\le1\), and \(2hm(p^J-1)/(p-1)\le 2p/(p-1)\le4\).

To control \(C_N\) uniformly, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) and every sufficiently large prime \(q\) one has \(\log q/(q-1)\le\varepsilon\log(q-1)\).
The finitely many remaining prime contributions are bounded independently of their exponents.
Hence \(C_N\le C(\varepsilon)\varphi(N)^\varepsilon\).
When \(hm\to0\), one has \(m\to0\) and \(d\to\infty\) by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), with \(m\ge\log2\) at degree one.
The same corollary gives \(h\le 1/m\le(\log(3d))^3/2\) eventually and \(s\le d\).
Consequently \(\log C_N=o(\log d)\).
For \(e\to\infty\), (4) now gives (1) because \(Y\to\infty\) and \(\log J+4e=o(eY)\).
For bounded \(e\) the same corollary gives \(eY=O_e(\log\log(3d))\).
Taking subsequences proves (1) for varying \(e\).

For effectivity assume \(hm\le1\).
Each factor in \(C_N\) is at most \(q-1\), and hence \(C_N\le\varphi(N)=sh\).
Using the preceding degree bound for \(h\) at \(d\ge2\), and \(h\le1/\log2\) at \(d=1\), gives \(\log(dC_N)\le5\log(3d)\le10\log(2d)\).
When \(e\ge\eta\log(2d)\) and \(e\ge2\), use \(J\le1+2Y\le4\exp(Y/2)\).
Inequality (4) yields \((e-3/2)Y\le e(4+10/\eta)+\log4\) and thus \(Y\le16+40/\eta+2\log4\).
When \(e=1\), the hypothesis bounds \(d\le\exp(1/\eta)\), so the degree-one bound or Voutier's corollary gives an effective positive bound for \(m\).
These cover all cases, since \(hm>1\) is immediate.

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## Quotients coupling two prime coordinates

For \(I_p=\Gamma\) at every conductor prime, the subgroup of \(G\) acting trivially in any one coordinate still maps onto \(\Gamma\) through any other coordinate.
Its fixed field in \(F\) is therefore \(\mathbb Q\), proving the individual intersection assertion following (3).
For a concrete family of fields let \(p,q\) be distinct odd primes both equal to one modulo \(t\), with \(t\ge2\).
Map each of the cyclic groups \((\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times,(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\) onto a cyclic group of order \(t\), and take \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_{pq})\) to be the fixed field of the kernel of the product map.
Both coordinate images have order \(t=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
In fact this \(F\) meets each of the full \(p\)- and \(q\)-primary cyclotomic towers only in \(\mathbb Q\).
For example \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{pq})\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^j})=\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\) for \(j\ge1\), by computing degrees in the compositum \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^j q})\).
For any power-stable generator with \(K\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_{pq})=F\), (3) bounds \((t-1)\log(pq)\) by \(\log(dC_{pq})+2thm\).
Here \(C_{pq}\le4\) and \(h=(p-1)(q-1)/t\).
The estimates constrain small measure conditional on such intersection data and do not assert the existence of a shrinking sequence.
