# The least span of a norm-one exception in a scalar quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## A criterion for reciprocal units

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit of degree \(d\), write \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) and \(m=\log M(\alpha)\), and let \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(W\) and nonzero constant term.
Put \(H=\max_{|z|=1}|G(z)|\).
For an integer \(q>H\), consider the conditions
\[
 G(\alpha)\in qR,\qquad
 \frac{G(\alpha)G(\alpha^{-1})}{q^2}=1.                               \tag{1}
\]
Such \(q,G\) exist exactly when \(\alpha\) has no conjugate of modulus one.
For each such hyperbolic \(\alpha\), the least possible \(W\) is \(d\).
One can attain this minimum with all sufficiently large integer moduli, with \(q/H\to1\) and with the multiplicative order of \(\alpha\bmod qR\) tending to infinity.
The minimum of \(Wm/d\) for this fixed \(\alpha\) is therefore \(m\).
If (1) is weakened to require only that \(G(\alpha)/q\) be integral and that its displayed product be one, the possible spans still satisfy \(W\ge d/2\).

Consequently, for each \(0<c\le c_0=0.427800\) the following two statements are equivalent.

1. For every nontorsion reciprocal unit, every integral \(G\) with nonzero constant term and every integer \(q>H\), the conditions \(G(\alpha)\in qR\) and \(Wm<cd\) imply \(G(\alpha)=0\).
2. Every hyperbolic nontorsion reciprocal unit has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(c\).

Here \(c_0\) has the numerical scope of the [height transfer for reciprocal words](Yin_C_G0345_v697.md#statement).
The equivalence uses integral words of arbitrary coefficient size.

<a id="center"></a>
## A shortest word for each hyperbolic unit

Reciprocity and exclusion of the roots \(1,-1\) give \(d=2D\) and the monic minimal polynomial has the form \(P(X)=X^D T(X+X^{-1})\), with \(T\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Indeed the symmetric Laurent polynomial \(X^{-D}P(X)\) is an integral combination of \(1\) and \(X^j+X^{-j}\), each an integer polynomial in \(X+X^{-1}\).
If \(\alpha\) has a unit-circle conjugate \(z\), applying that embedding to (1) gives \(q^2=|G(z)|^2\le H^2\).
Thus hyperbolicity is necessary also for the weaker integrality condition.

For a hyperbolic unit, \(T\) has a constant nonzero sign \(s\) on \([-2,2]\).
For any integer \(q>\max_{[-2,2]}|T|\), set
\[
                      G(X)=qX^D-sP(X).
\]
The leading and constant coefficients are both \(-s\), and \(G(\alpha)=q\alpha^D\).
On the unit circle, \(X^{-D}G(X)=q-|T(X+X^{-1})|>0\), giving \(H=q-\min_{[-2,2]}|T|<q\) and \(q/H\to1\).
For fixed \(j\ge1\) the vector of power-basis coordinates of \(\alpha^j-1\) is a nonzero integer vector.
All sufficiently large \(q\) fail to divide that vector, so the order modulo \(qR\) eventually exceeds any fixed bound.

For any \(G\) of degree below \(d\), its coefficients are its coordinates in the power basis of \(R\) after padding by zeros.
The Fourier coefficient bound \(|[X^i]G|\le H<q\) makes divisibility of every coordinate by \(q\) impossible for a nonzero \(G\).
This proves minimality and also shows that every nonzero modular false positive in the full scalar quotient has \(W\ge d\), regardless of sign or signature.
For the weaker integrality condition put \(G^*(X)=X^W G(1/X)\).
The product equation gives \(P\mid GG^*-q^2X^W\), an integral polynomial of degree at most \(2W\) which is nonzero since \(|G(z)|<q\) on the unit circle.
This proves the weaker lower bound.

For the equivalence, (1) at \(W=d\) supplies a false positive whenever a hyperbolic input has \(m<c\).
Conversely, on a hyperbolic input with \(m\ge c\) the full scalar degree bound already yields \(Wm\ge cd\) for every false positive.
On an input with a unit-circle conjugate the [reciprocal-word theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v697.md#statement) excludes false positives at \(q>H,\ Wm<c_0d\).
This gives the stated equivalence without presuming a hyperbolic gap.

<a id="family"></a>
## An alternating example with its exact modular order

There are examples with every coefficient of the word in \(\{-1,1\}\).
For \(k\ge2\), put \(n=3^k,\ D=(n-1)/2,\ q=n+3\), and let
\[
                G_n(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-X)^j,\qquad
                P_n(X)=G_n(X)-qX^D.
\]
The polynomial \(P_n\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(2D=n-1\) with constant one.
In characteristic three the identity \((1+X)G_n=1+X^n=(1+X)^n\) gives \(P_n\equiv(1+X)^{n-1}\).
Also \(P_n(-1)=n-q(-1)^D\) has 3-adic valuation one, since \(9\mid n\) and \(q\equiv3\bmod9\).
Thus \(P_n(X-1)\) is Eisenstein at three.
Since \(P_n(1)=1-q<0\) and \(P_n\) is monic, there is a real root \(\alpha>1\).
There are no roots on the unit circle since \(H(G_n)=n<q\), with equality in the circle maximum at \(-1\).
Every such \(\alpha\) is a hyperbolic reciprocal unit of degree \(d=W=n-1\), and \(G_n(\alpha)=q\alpha^D\) gives (1) with \(q/H=(n+3)/n\to1\).

Modulo \(qR\) one has \(\alpha^n=-1\) by the geometric identity.
If \(1\le j<n-1\), the nonzero coefficients of \(X^j-1\) preclude modular vanishing in the power basis.
At \(j=n-1\), subtracting the monic \(P_n\) leaves a coefficient of magnitude one modulo \(q\) at \(X\).
As \(-1\ne1\bmod qR\) and every proper divisor of \(2n\) is at most \(n\), the multiplicative order is exactly \(2n\).

<a id="supports"></a>
## Minimal signed supports within one period

The same quotient has at least \(2^{(n-3)/2}\) different signed supports of size \(n\), all containing \(0,1\) and of span strictly less than the order \(2n\).
The signs in increasing order alternate from plus to plus, the circle maximum of each word is \(n\), and each word is nonzero at the positive real \(\alpha\).
In the quotient each support is inclusion-minimal among supports admitting any vanishing choice of signs, and its only vanishing sign choices are the given one and its negative.

On the original block \(0,\ldots,n-1\), a nonzero ternary polynomial vanishing modulo \(qR\) has degree \(n-1\), by the power basis.
Its leading coefficient is \(\pm1\), and subtracting the corresponding \(\pm P_n\) gives a remainder with every coefficient divisible by \(q\).
As \(P_n\equiv G_n\bmod q\), comparison of ternary coefficients for \(q>2\) shows that the polynomial is exactly \(\pm G_n\).
Now keep \(0,1\) and the singleton \(n-1\), and independently add \(n\) to both positions in any of the pairs \(2i,2i+1\) for \(1\le i<D\).
Reverse the two signs on each moved pair, using \(\alpha^n=-1\bmod qR\).
In sorted order the unmoved pairs have signs \((+,-)\), the singleton has sign plus, and the moved pairs each have signs \((-,+)\).
The chosen coefficients at all positions \(j\) are \((-1)^j\), giving circle maximum \(n\) at \(-1\).
The odd alternating sum is positive at \(\alpha>1\), as one can keep the initial term and group each negative term with the larger positive term following it.
All \(n\) residues modulo \(n\) remain distinct, so pulling back any signed subrelation to the original block proves minimality and sign uniqueness.
The choices are recovered from the exponents, the smallest two positions fix translation and common dilation, and the largest position is at most \(2n-2\).
This supplies a count inside one period in addition to the [short-order quadratic construction](Yin_C_G0345_v690.md#statement).
The assertion (1) about the relative norm is for the original consecutive word.

<a id="measure"></a>
## House and logarithmic measure of the examples

For every root \(z\) of \(P_n\) with \(|z|\ge1\), the equation \(1+z^n=qz^D(1+z)\) gives \(|z|^n\le(2q+1)|z|^{D+1}\).
Thus the house, the largest modulus of a conjugate, is at most \((2q+1)^{1/D}\) and tends to one.
The total logarithmic measure satisfies
\[
                    \log M(P_n)=\log q+O\bigl((\log n)/n\bigr).
\]
To see this, put \(U(z)=z^{-D}G_n(z)/q\) on the unit circle with normalized arc measure.
The function is real, \(|U|\le n/q<1\), its mean is \((-1)^D/q\), and orthogonality gives \(\int U^2=n/q^2\).
The set \(|U|>1/2\) therefore has measure at most \(4n/q^2\), and there \(|\log(1-U)|=O(\log n)\) since \(1-U\ge3/q\).
On the complement, \(\log(1-U)=-U+O(U^2)\), and the omitted integral of \(U\) has absolute value at most \(4n/q^2\).
Integrating \(\log|P_n|=\log q+\log(1-U)\) proves the formula by Jensen's formula.
The absolute logarithmic heights tend to zero, whereas the total logarithmic measures of this particular family grow like \(\log n\).
