# A logarithmic action threshold for Salem collision packets

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sufficient condition for a strict collision deficit

Fix a Salem number \(1<\alpha<2\) of degree \(d=2s+2\) with minimal polynomial \(P\), and an integer \(k\ge1\).
Let \(p_{n,k}\) be the collision probability for the sum of \(k\) independent copies of \(\sum_{j<n}\xi_j\alpha^j\), with independent fair bits indexed from zero, and put \(h_{k,2}=\lim_n-n^{-1}\log p_{n,k}\).
Consider a sequence of [localized packets for \(P\)](Yin_C_G0798_v2.md#multiple-neutral-packet-definition) with neutral amplitude \(\Delta\to0\), core length \(\ell=O(\log(1/\Delta))\) and a common geometric tail bound.
For the localized part \(H\) of each packet let \(E=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}V(H_i)\), where \(V(x)=-\log\cos^2(\pi x)\).
Then
\[
     \liminf_{\Delta\to0}\frac{kE}{\log(1/\Delta)}<2
             \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h_{k,2}<\log\alpha .             \tag{1}
\]
The liminf is along the given packets, whose individual boundary margins may tend to zero.

Every candidate \(\xi_h\) in the [real-frequency construction](Yin_C_G0345_v676.md#strip) at a sufficiently large scale gives such a packet, with
\[
 \Delta\asymp_\alpha 2^h N^{-2},\qquad
 E=-2\log|\widehat\nu(\xi_h)|+O_\alpha(1),\qquad N=\lceil\log_\alpha T\rceil .  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\nu\) is the real law of \(\sum_{j\ge0}\epsilon_j\alpha^{-j}\) for independent fair signs, using the Fourier kernel \(e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\).
In particular, a sequence of those candidates with \(T\to\infty\) and \(|\widehat\nu(\xi_h)|\ge\Delta^{(1-\varepsilon)/k}\) for a fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\) gives a strict deficit at that input.
The available [real-frequency lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v676.md#statement) has an exponent exceeding the sufficient range here for every \(1<\alpha<2\).

<a id="spacing"></a>
## Action at spacing comparable to the inverse squared amplitude

Pass to a subsequence with \(kE\le(2-\varepsilon_0)\log(1/\Delta)\) for some \(0<\varepsilon_0<2\).
Write \(r=\alpha^{-1}\), and choose \(Q=\lceil\Delta^{-2}\rceil\).
With \(Q>2\ell+d\) for large packets, use the steering and placement construction of [MP4](Yin_C_G0798_v2.md#multiple-neutral-packet-argument) at this spacing.
Its number of spacing choices at each stage is at least \(b_Q=\lfloor Q/L\rfloor\), where \(L\) depends only on \(P\).
For \(M\) placements, assign every observed site to the nearest translated core.
The sum \(y_i\) of the packet sequences differs there from the assigned translated \(H\) by at most
\[
 u=K_P\Delta+\frac{2h_* r^{\lfloor(Q-\ell)/2\rfloor}}{1-r^Q}
        =O_P(\Delta),
\]
where \(K_P\) is the steering bound and \(h_*\) the common tail bound.
All constants in the estimates below may depend on \(P,k,h_*\) and the bound on \(\ell/\log(1/\Delta)\).

Put \(\rho=\inf_i(1/2-|H_i|)\).
Since each nonnegative action term is at most \(E\), the inequality \(\cos(\pi H_i)\le\pi(1/2-|H_i|)\) yields
\[
             \rho\ge \pi^{-1}e^{-E/2}
                    \ge \pi^{-1}\Delta^{(2-\varepsilon_0)/(2k)}.
\]
In particular \(u/\rho\) tends to zero as a positive power of \(\Delta\), and the summed observations stay inside the canonical interval with margin at least \(\rho/2\).
Enlarge each translated core by a fixed number \(q\) of sites at each end, large enough that \(h_*r^q\le1/8\).
At assigned sites in this enlarged core \(|V'|\ll1/\rho\) throughout the perturbation, so the total excess over the assigned localized terms is \(O((\ell+2q)u/\rho)=o(1)\) per packet.
At every other assigned site \(|H_i|\le1/8\) in the coordinates of its packet.
There \(|V'(H_i)|\ll |H_i|\) and \(V''\) is bounded on the segment to the perturbed observation.
The sum of \(|H_i|\) on these two tails is bounded uniformly by the geometric series.
Consequently the total tail excess over all placements is \(O(Mu+n u^2)\).
The common length satisfies \(n<2QM\) and \(Qu^2=O(1)\).
Since an assigned subset of a packet has localized action at most \(E\), the total action is at most \(M(E+C)\) with \(C\) independent of the packet on this subsequence and of \(M\).

<a id="collision"></a>
## Disjoint placements and the entropy limit

Fix a sufficiently far packet so that \(\log b_Q-k(E+C)>0\), which is possible since \(\log b_Q=2\log(1/\Delta)+O_P(1)\).
The placement construction gives a common length \((M-1)Q+\ell+d\le n<2QM\) and at least \(b_Q^{M-1}/(MQ)\) distinct observed integral forcing words for every \(M\ge2\).
The margin just proved permits the [MP5 boxes](Yin_C_G0798_v2.md#multiple-neutral-packet-argument) with margin parameter \(\min\{\rho/4,1/16\}\).
They have disjoint interiors, Haar volume \(c(P,\rho)\alpha^{-n}n^{-d}\) apiece, and action at most \(M(E+C)+1\) throughout.
The factor \(c(P,\rho)>0\) is fixed before \(M\) tends to infinity.
Integrating over these boxes in the [torus collision identity](Yin_C_G0345_v581.md#integral) gives
\[
  \log(\alpha^np_{n,k})
       \ge M\bigl(\log b_Q-k(E+C)\bigr)-O_{P,k,Q,\rho}(\log M).
\]
Division by \(n<2QM\) for large \(M\) and the existence of the entropy limit give (1).

<a id="trace"></a>
## Packets from the real Fourier candidates

Use the unit \(u_N\), integer \(J\), element \(b=u_N/P'(\alpha)\), exponent \(L_h\) and \(\xi_h=2^{h-1}b_\alpha\alpha^{L_h}\) of the [transition-interval construction](Yin_C_G0345_v676.md#strip).
Set \(\lambda=2^hb\) and let \(Y_t\) be the centered representative of \(\lambda_\alpha\alpha^t\) for every integer \(t\).
The real embeddings of \(\lambda\alpha^t\) have opposite signs for every \(t\), so its distinguished value is irrational and centering never encounters a half-integer.
Since the original real sequence satisfies the homogeneous recurrence, \(P(S)Y\) is integral, where \((SY)_t=Y_{t+1}\).
To the left of \(-J\) we have \(Y_t=\lambda_\alpha\alpha^t\).
For \(t>J\), integer traces and the estimates for the contracting and neutral embeddings yield
\[
      Y_t=-\lambda_r r^t-2\Re\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_{z_i}z_i^t .
\]
Indeed their sum is \(O_\alpha(N^{1+2s}\alpha^{-t}+N^{-1})\) uniformly for all \(t>J\), including beyond \(L_h\).
Here \(z_i\) ranges over one member of each neutral pair.
Both exterior sequences are homogeneous, so \(P(S)Y\) is supported in \([-J-d,J]\).
The forcing is nonzero, because a bounded bilateral homogeneous solution with distinct Salem characteristic roots that tends to zero at negative infinity must vanish identically, whereas the left tail is nonzero.

Use \([-J-d,J]\) as core and shift its first index to zero.
Let \(H=Y\) up through the last core site and remove the outgoing neutral sum thereafter, leaving \(H_t=-\lambda_r r^t\) in the original right-tail coordinates.
The wave coefficients have moduli \(|\lambda_{z_i}|\), since the shift and the choice of outgoing phase origin only multiply them by unit complex factors.
The two exterior real tails satisfy the packet bound with a common \(h_*\) by \(\alpha^J\ge N^d\) and \(|\lambda_\alpha|+|\lambda_r|\ll_\alpha N^{1+2s}\).
The finite core avoids the centering boundary, so each packet has a positive margin.
The norm of \(u_N\) has magnitude one.
Its two real absolute values are \(O_\alpha(N^{2s})\) and its neutral absolute values \(O_\alpha(N^{-2})\).
Bounding every factor of the norm except one neutral conjugate pair gives \(|(u_N)_{z_i}|\gg_\alpha N^{-2}\) for each \(i\).
Thus the packet amplitude \(2(\sum_i|\lambda_{z_i}|^2)^{1/2}\) obeys (2), and \(\ell=2J+d+1=O_\alpha(\log(1/\Delta))\).

On \([-J,J]\), the localized packet action equals the negative logarithm of the squared cosine product of the real frequency on that interval.
The extra \(d\) core sites and the two localized tails have total action \(O_\alpha(1)\) by the real geometric bounds.
The product for \(\widehat\nu(\xi_h)\) includes this entire interval, and the negative logarithm of its squared product off the interval is also \(O_\alpha(1)\) by the two tail estimates of the real-frequency construction.
This proves (2) uniformly over all its candidates.
A lower bound \(c_\alpha N^{-p}\) along such candidates with \(p<1/k\) suffices for (1), since \(\Delta\ll_\alpha N^{-1}\).
If \(h=o(\log N)\), the corresponding sufficient range is \(p<2/k\), since \(\log(1/\Delta)=(2+o(1))\log N\).
