# Distinct two-class multiples of one polynomial and the cost of doubling a root

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound for two different row ratios

Fix a prime \(p>4\) and \(1\le r<p\).
Write \(F(x)=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p)\), where the two nonzero integral Laurent rows are coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Let \(D\) be the exponent span of \(F\), let \(m=\log M\), and put
\[
 I(F)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max\{|U(X)|,|V(X)|\}\,d\mu(X),
 \qquad C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-X|)^{1/2}\,d\mu(X)
\]
for normalized Haar measure \(\mu\).
In particular \(I(F)\ge m(U)\ge0\).
For two such graphs \(F_i=U_i(x^p)+x^rV_i(x^p)\) in the same coordinates, suppose \(U_1V_2-U_2V_1\ne0\).
If a primitive integer minimal polynomial \(P\) of a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number divides both diagonals with multiplicity at least \(e\ge1\), then
\[
 \begin{split}
    ep\,m(P)&\le I(F_1)+I(F_2)+\log 2,\\
    m(F_1)+m(F_2)&\ge(1-4/p)\{ep\,m(P)-\log 2\}
                          -(2/p)\log(C^2D_1D_2).                         \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
The determinant condition means the row ratios differ in \(\mathbb Q(X)\).
For bounded \(m(P)\), fixed \(e\), and \(\log(2D_1D_2)=o(p^2)\), the sum of the two measures divided by \(p\) is at least \(e\,m(P)-o(1)\), including when the input varies.
Thus any saving below \(e\,m(P)/2\) in the first rate raises the required second rate by the same amount up to \(o(1)\).

There is an explicit second graph with the same two residues and coprime rows that doubles every original nonzero root multiplicity.
For any initial graph \(E\) it has span at most \(pD_E\) and measure at most \(p I(E)+\log(2p)\).
For a family of genuine Salem inputs tending to \(2\), one primitive monic graph has zero extra measure, whereas the optimal rate \(m(F)/p\) among projectively distinct graphs is \(\log2\) in the degree windows specified [below](#salem).
The rate is attained asymptotically even if the competing graph must be monic and contain the Salem factor twice.

<a id="row"></a>
## Comparing the row integral with the measure on the diagonal

Write \(\iota U(X)=U(1/X)\) and \(H=U\iota U-V\iota V\).
If \(H\ne0\), the product over the \(p\) points \(x^p=X\) has modulus \(|U^p-(-1)^pX^r V^p|\), since \(r\) is invertible modulo \(p\).
For \(a=\max(|U|,|V|)\), \(b=\min(|U|,|V|)\) on the unit circle, this modulus is at least
\[
                  a^p-b^p\ \ge\ a^{p-2}(a^2-b^2)=a^{p-2}|H|.
\]
Logarithmic integration and \(m(H)\ge0\) give \(p\,m(F)\ge(p-2)I(F)\) without a degree term.

If \(H=0\), the [coprime row classification](Yin_C_G0345_v359.md#powers) gives \(V=\sigma X^a\iota U\) for some \(a\in\mathbb Z,\ \sigma=\pm1\).
Write \(U=X^u f\) with \(f(0)\ne0,\ n=\deg f,\ g=f^*=X^nf(1/X)\).
After removing an \(x\)-monomial the two summands become \(f(x^p)+\sigma x^{S_0}g(x^p)\), where \(S_0=r+p(a-2u-n)\ne0\).
When \(S_0<0\), multiply by \(\sigma x^{-S_0}\) and exchange \(f,g\).
We therefore have \(S=|S_0|>0,\ (S,p)=1,\ D=pn+S\), and \(I(F)=m(f)=m(g)\).
The polynomials \(f,g\) are coprime and free of unit-circle zeros, since a circle zero of either also belongs to the other by conjugation and reversal.

For clarity, the [one-branch estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two) holds here for any nonzero integral \(f(0)\), including \(n=0\).
Set \(u_0=-\sigma f/g,\ K=pX(f'g-fg')-Sfg\), and
\(L=\int_{\mathbb T}\log(2/|u_0^p-X^S|)\,d\mu\).
The phase \(u_0^p/X^S\) has angular speed \(|K/(fg)|\).
The constant coefficient \(K(0)=-Sf(0)g(0)\) is a nonzero integer.
Each phase value has at most \(D\) preimages because it gives a phase twist of the two-term-in-rows diagonal of span \(D\), with \((p,S)=1\).
The [circle change of variable and logarithmic Jensen estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#speed) with exponent \(1/2\) consequently give
\[
   \tfrac12 L\le\log(CD)+2I(F)-m(K),\qquad
   p\{m(F)-I(F)\}=\log2-L.
\]
The second identity is the product over \(x^p=X\).
Since \(m(K)\ge0\), in either case we have the convenient common bound
\[
                         (p-4)I(F)\le p\,m(F)+2\log(CD).                \tag{2}
\]

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Retaining the whole powered factor in the determinant

Let \(\Delta=U_1V_2-U_2V_1\).
Then \(\Delta(x^p)=F_1(x)V_2(x^p)-F_2(x)V_1(x^p)\) is divisible by \(P^e\) in the rational Laurent ring.
In any fiber \(x^p=\beta\ne0\) containing a root of \(F_1\), both rows at \(\beta\) are nonzero and their ratio determines \(x^r\).
Thus at most one point in that fiber is a root, and the \(p\)-th powers of all conjugate roots of \(P\) are distinct.
Write \(P(x)=c\prod_j(x-\alpha_j)\) with positive leading coefficient and \(Q(X)=c^p\prod_j(X-\alpha_j^p)\).
The [integral primitive powering argument](Yin_C_G0345_v359.md#powers) gives \(Q\) primitive even for nonmonic \(P\), and \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\).
The distinct powered roots form one Galois orbit, so \(Q\) is their minimal polynomial.
Substitution \(X=x^p\) is unramified at each \(\alpha_j\ne0\), hence \(Q^e\) divides the nonzero \(\Delta\).
By Gauss's lemma the Laurent quotient is integral and \(ep\,m(P)\le m(\Delta)\).
On the circle,
\[
 |\Delta|\le 2\max(|U_1|,|V_1|)\max(|U_2|,|V_2|).
\]
Integration gives the first bound (1), and (2) gives the second.

If \(\Delta=0\), the coprimality of both row pairs implies that their proportionality factor is a unit of \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\), hence a rational scalar times a Laurent monomial.
If both pairs also have primitive integer coefficient content, that scalar is \(\pm1\).
This describes the excluded representations explicitly.

<a id="doubling"></a>
## A second primitive graph with exactly double multiplicities

Put \(X=x^p,\ a=x^rV(X),\ b=W(X)=-U(X)\), so \(E=a-b\).
Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 T(E)&=a^p+(p-1)b^p-pab^{p-1}\\
     &= X^rV(X)^p+(p-1)W(X)^p -p x^r V(X)W(X)^{p-1}.
 \end{split}
\]
The first row is nonzero because the quotient \(W^p/V^p\) cannot be a nonzero constant times \(X^r\), as seen by taking the order at zero.
At a nonzero root of \(V\) its value is \((p-1)W^p\ne0\); at a nonzero root of \(W\) its value is \(X^rV^p\ne0\).
This exhausts the possible common roots with the second row and proves Laurent coprimality.
At any nonzero root of \(E\), \(a=b\ne0\).
Expanding \(a=b+(a-b)\) shows that \(T(E)/(a-b)^2\) takes the nonzero value \(\binom p2 b^{p-2}\) there.
In particular each such multiplicity is doubled exactly, so \(T(E)\) is projectively distinct from \(E\) when \(E\) has a nonzero root.
All exponents belong to the \(p\)-fold sum of the original support interval.
The span is at most \(pD_E\), and the pointwise triangle inequality gives \(m(T(E))\le p I(E)+\log(2p)\).

If \(|W|=|V|\) on the unit circle we also have a two-sided comparison independent of the degrees,
\[
 \log\frac{p-1}{2}
 \ \le\ m(T(E))-(p-2)I(E)-2m(E)
 \ \le\ \log\frac{p(p-1)}2.                                               \tag{3}
\]
Indeed put \(t=a/b\), of modulus one, and
\[
 B_p(t)=\frac{t^p-pt+p-1}{(t-1)^2}
           =\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}(p-1-j)t^j.
\]
On the unit circle,
\[
         2\operatorname{Re} B_p(t)
                =(p-1)+\left|\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}t^j\right|^2.
\]
Consequently \((p-1)/2\le |B_p|\le p(p-1)/2\).
Integrating \(T(E)=b^{p-2}(a-b)^2 B_p(a/b)\) and \(m(b)=I(E)\) proves (3).
For a balanced coprime graph carrying a nontorsion root, the seed \(f\) in (2) is nonconstant, since a constant yields a scalar times a signed binomial.
Coprimality with its reversal and the [nonreciprocal seed bound](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) give \(I(E)=m(f)\ge\log\theta\), where \(\theta^3=\theta+1,\ \theta>1\).
Thus this explicit doubling costs at least \((p-2)\log\theta+\log((p-1)/2)\) in total log measure.
For hypothetical shrinking unit inputs, the [two-class coefficient construction with a double root](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) gives witnesses of rate tending to zero in its prime range.
Formula (3) excludes this particular balanced-graph doubling as their construction.

<a id="salem"></a>
## A Salem family with one exceptional representation

For \(d=6p\) the [irreducible Salem family](Yin_C_G0022_v1.md#salem-first-window-precise-statement) has
\[
 S_d=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1,\qquad
 E=(x-1)S_d=x^{6p+1}-2x^{6p}+2x-1.
\]
Its real exterior root \(\lambda_d\) lies below \(2\), tends to \(2\), and \(h_p=m(S_d)=\log\lambda_d\).
The support differences of \(S_d\) generate \(\mathbb Z\).
Its conjugates retain full degree after powering, by the fiber argument for \(E\).
The relation \(2-\lambda_d=(2\lambda_d-1)/\lambda_d^{6p}\) exhibits the exponentially small integer distance that enters the [monic leading-row bound](Yin_C_G0345_v360.md#house) at \(s=1\).

Here \(r=1,\ W=2X^6+1,\ V=X^6+2\).
The rows are coprime since \(2V-W=3\), and \(|V|=|W|\ge1\) on the circle, giving \(I(E)=m(V)=\log2\).
The only extra factor of \(E\) is \(x-1\), so its extra log measure is zero.
For every other primitive-row graph \(F\) with these coordinates and \(S_d\mid F\), whose row ratio differs from that of \(E\), the determinant gives
\[
               I(F)\ge p h_p-2\log2,\qquad
 m(F)\ge (1-4/p)(p h_p-2\log2)-(2/p)\log(CD_F).                        \tag{4}
\]
Hence for any such sequence with \(\log(2D_F)=o(p^2)\) the lower limit of \(m(F)/p\) is at least \(\log2\).
With primitive integer content, the only representations sharing the excluded row ratio are the signed base-monomial translates of \(E\).

The polynomial \(T(E)\) above is monic of degree \(p(6p+1)\), since the leading term comes only from \(X V^p\).
Its integral rows are coprime, and it contains \(S_d\) exactly twice.
Its measure satisfies \(m(T(E))\le p\log2+\log(2p)\).
Thus if a span cap \(D_p\ge p(6p+1)\) satisfies \(\log(2D_p)=o(p^2)\), the minimum of \(m(F)/p\) over projectively distinct competitors tends to \(\log2\).
The same conclusion holds for monic competitors divisible by \(S_d^2\), and for their extra log measure after subtracting \(2h_p\).
Minima are attained after a Laurent monomial translation to ordinary polynomials by the bounded-degree coefficient bounds for bounded Mahler measure.
The input measures here tend to \(\log2\), leaving the small-measure case open.
