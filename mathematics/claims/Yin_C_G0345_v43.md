# Sparse interlacers have an exact polynomial with no roots inside the disk

<a id="statement"></a>
## An arithmetic restriction on the two end bands

Let \(S_i\) be the trace minimal polynomials of Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\).
For each \(i\) suppose \(A_i\) is a strict integer interlacer, of degree \(k_i=\deg S_i-1\), whose positive leading coefficients \(c_i\) are bounded and whose reciprocal lifts \(A_i^\sharp=z^{k_i}A_i(z+z^{-1})\) have bounded term counts.
Eventually there are \(B_i\in\mathbb Z[z]\), \(B_i(0)=c_i\), \(\ell_i=\deg B_i=o(k_i)\), with
\[
      A_i^\sharp(z)=B_i(z)+z^{2k_i}B_i(1/z),\qquad
      M(B_i)=c_i .
\]
The polynomial \(B_i\) is exactly the part of \(A_i^\sharp\) below its middle coefficient.
It has no roots in the open unit disk.
Consequently every root of \(A_i^\sharp\) is on the unit circle, including the pair over any initially unrestricted last root of \(A_i\).

If the \(A_i\) are primitive irreducible with \(c_i=2\), then eventually \(B_i\) is irreducible, has leading coefficient \(\pm1\) and all its roots are strictly outside the unit circle.
Its roots are conjugate algebraic integers of absolute norm two.
In particular any other bounded-term polynomial with constant two and fixed degree less than \(k_i\) cannot occur as the lower part along such a sequence unless it has these same arithmetic properties.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Comparing the two Mahler measures at separated exponent scales

The [frequency concentration theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v42.md#statement) leaves only the two end bands and gives the displayed identity with \(\ell_i=o(k_i)\).
The distinct interlacing traces give \(c_i\le M(A_i^\sharp)\le c_i\sigma_i\), since only the last trace can exceed two and its lift above one would be less than \(\sigma_i\).
The [sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds the coefficients of both \(A_i^\sharp\) and \(B_i\).
We prove \(\log M(A_i^\sharp)-\log M(B_i)\to0\).

In each subsequence extract a fixed list of coefficients for \(B_i\) and a fixed \(c_i=c\).
Extract further so that each integer relation among the positive exponents of \(B_i\) either holds eventually or fails eventually.
The eventual relation lattice is saturated.
Choose a basis of its free quotient of rank \(r\), so \(B_i(z)=B(z^{v_{1,i}},\ldots,z^{v_{r,i}})\) for a fixed nonzero Laurent polynomial \(B\).
Each \(v_{j,i}\) is a fixed integer combination of exponents at most \(\ell_i\), hence \(o(k_i)\), and no fixed nonzero relation among the \(v_{j,i}\) can hold infinitely often.
The same is true after appending \(2k_i\), since its nonzero fixed multiples dominate the other coordinates.
Thus the shortest relation lengths tend to infinity for both specializations.
By [Lawton's theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002), the first measure tends to that of \(B(X)+w B(X^{-1})\) on \(\mathbb T^{r+1}\) and the second to that of \(B(X)\) on \(\mathbb T^r\).
Here the first measure refers to \(A_i^\sharp\).
For each \(X\) outside the null zero set of \(B\), the coefficients of the affine polynomial in \(w\) have equal absolute value because \(B\) is real on coefficients.
Jensen's formula in \(w\) therefore gives logarithmic mean \(\log|B(X)|\).
The two limiting measures agree, including for \(r=0\), when \(B_i=c\) and the calculation is direct.
This proves the assertion on the full sequence by extraction.

The constant coefficient of \(B_i\) is \(c_i\), so \(M(B_i)\ge c_i\) and \(M(B_i)-c_i\to0\).
The [bounded-term gap above an endpoint coefficient](Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#statement), taken over the finitely many \(c_i\), gives \(M(B_i)=c_i\) eventually.
Reversal and the root-product formula identify this equality with absence of roots of \(B_i\) in the open disk.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Unit-circle roots of the lift and irreducibility of an expanding lower part

Write \(B_i^*(z)=z^{\ell_i}B_i(1/z)\).
Common unit-circle zeros cancel from \(B_i^*/B_i\) with equal multiplicities, by conjugation.
The quotient is holomorphic on the closed disk after cancellation and has modulus one on its boundary.
The maximum modulus principle gives \(\big|z^{2k_i-\ell_i}B_i^*/B_i\big|<1\) in the open disk.
This proves that \(A_i^\sharp\) has no root inside, and reciprocity excludes exterior roots.

In the primitive irreducible leading-two case \(A_i^\sharp\) is primitive, since a prime dividing its coefficients would make \(A_i(z+z^{-1})\) identically zero in the Laurent ring modulo that prime, and substitution here is injective by the leading term.
It is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for all sufficiently large \(i\).
Indeed all trace roots are real and a neutral trace \(y\in(-2,2)\) exists for \(k_i\ge2\) sufficiently large by strict interlacing.
The real field \(\mathbb Q(y)\) has \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]=k_i\), and its polynomial \(z^2-yz+1\) has negative discriminant at this embedding.
Its roots consequently have degree \(2k_i\).
If \(B_i\) had a unit-circle root then it would also be a root of \(A_i^\sharp\), impossible since \(\ell_i<2k_i\) and \(A_i^\sharp\) is irreducible.
The lower part cannot be the constant two, since the whole lift would then be divisible by two.
It is primitive because its coefficients together with their reversals give the entire primitive lift.
Any nonconstant integer factor has constant term of modulus strictly larger than its positive absolute leading coefficient, by the product of its exterior moduli.
Thus each such factor requires absolute constant at least two.
The primitive polynomial of absolute constant two has only one such factor, and its leading coefficient has modulus strictly less than two.
This proves the remaining assertions.
