# Short additive relations and zonotopes for integer polynomials of small Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A logarithmic bound on the dimension of the exponents

Let \(P(X)=\sum_{i=1}^k c_iX^{a_i}\) have distinct integer exponents, nonzero integer coefficients and \(k\ge2\).
Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, \(M=\exp m\), and \(\theta\) for the real root greater than one of \(T^3-T-1\).
Fix \(0<\delta<1\), and put
\[
 \eta=\left(\frac{\delta}{48k^2}\right)^{4(k-1)},\qquad
 N=\left\lceil(1+k^2/\eta)/\delta\right\rceil .
\]
Let \(W\) be the rational span of the integer relations
\[
 \sum_i v_i=0,\qquad \sum_i v_i a_i=0,\qquad \sum_i |v_i|\le2N .          \tag{1}
\]
Their positive and negative parts each have sum at most \(N\).
Set \(t=\dim_{\mathbb Q}W\) and \(r=k-1-t\).
If \(m(P)+\delta<\log\theta\), then \(k\ge2^r\).
If also \(m(P)>0\), then \(k\ge2^r+1\).
In particular positive-measure inputs in this range have at least
\[
                   k-1-\lfloor\log_2(k-1)\rfloor                         \tag{2}
\]
linearly independent relations of the form (1).
One can choose \(r\) of the differences \(a_i-a_k\) as integer parameters and express every difference rationally in them with a common positive denominator and integer numerators all bounded in absolute value by \((\sqrt2 N)^t\).
Explicit bounds and a Laurent-polynomial specialization are given below.

The lifted polynomial defined below has a Newton polytope of dimension \(r\).
If any face polynomial, including the entire polynomial, fails signed reciprocity up to a monomial, then
\[
                              M(P)\ge\theta e^{-\delta}.                  \tag{3}
\]
A face whose convex hull fails central symmetry suffices.
Below this bound the polytope is a zonotope, a Minkowski sum of line segments.
If \(k<3\cdot2^{r-1}\), it is a parallelotope.
For \(r\ge2\) and \(k=2^r+1\), a positive-measure input below (3) satisfies the further bound \(m(P)\ge C-\delta\), where \(C>1/4\) is the [two-pair constant](Yin_C_G0901_v69.md#statement).

For example \(\delta=1/64\) gives (2) whenever \(1<M(P)\le13/10\).
Indeed \((13/10)e^{1/64}<(13/10)(64/63)=416/315<\theta\), the last comparison following from the sign of the cubic.
If \(1<M(P)\le3049/2500\) and \(r\ge2\), then \(k\ge2^r+2\), since \((3049/2500)(64/63)<5/4<e^C\).
The relation length is uniform in the exponents and coefficient heights at fixed \(k,\delta\).
With varying term count one uses the actual \(N(k,\delta)\) for each input.
The conclusions concern the support of the whole polynomial.
An irreducible unit minimum is one such input.
A cofactor of logarithmic Mahler measure zero preserves the measure, and the support in (1) is that of the chosen product.

<a id="lift"></a>
## The quotient by the short balanced relations

In the lattice \(E=\{v\in\mathbb Z^k:\sum v_i=0\}\) form \(\Gamma=E/(W\cap E)\).
It is free of rank \(r\) by saturation.
Write \(s_i\) for the image of \(e_i-e_k\), so \(s_k=0\), and choose a basis of \(\Gamma\).
The \(s_i\) generate \(\Gamma\), and \(A(s_i)=a_i-a_k\) defines a homomorphism \(A:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z\), since \(W\) lies in the kernel of evaluation at \(a\).
The \(s_i\) are distinct because the \(a_i\) are distinct, and in particular \(r\ge1\).
The Laurent polynomial \(F=\sum c_i Z^{s_i}\) has support \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}\) of full affine dimension \(r\) and specialization \(F_A=X^{-a_k}P\).

If a sum of \(N\) differences in \(S-S\) maps to zero under \(A\), lift its pairs of indices to the corresponding differences \(e_i-e_j\) and sum them.
The resulting vector satisfies (1) and so has zero image in \(\Gamma\).
Therefore \(\ker A\cap N(S-S)=\{0\}\).
The [finite constant-coefficient comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v61.md#transfer) gives \(m(P)\ge m(F)-\delta\) for this \(F\) and this map.
This construction uses all the short relations in the actual exponents at once.
The earlier [bounded-pattern reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v754.md#statement) concerns a selected exact subsum at an algebraic root and gives a centrally symmetric summand for its support polytope.

<a id="faces"></a>
## The plastic-number lower bound for an asymmetric face

For a face \(B\) of \(\operatorname{Newt}(F)=\operatorname{conv}(S)\), write \(F_B\) for the terms with exponents on \(B\).
Then \(m(F_B)\le m(F)\).
Indeed a proper face is exposed by a primitive integer functional on the exponent lattice.
In lattice coordinates having this functional as their last exponent, the face polynomial up to a monomial is the leading coefficient in the last variable.
Clearing a Laurent monomial and using Jensen's formula in that variable gives the bound upon integration in the other variables.
The leading coefficient is nonzero almost everywhere, and the logarithms of nonzero Laurent polynomials are integrable.

For any nonzero integer Laurent polynomial \(G\) in \(r\) variables with \(m(G)<\log\theta\), there are a sign \(\epsilon\) and a lattice vector \(u\) with \(G(Z)=\epsilon Z^u G(Z^{-1})\).
To check this, specialize the variables by \((1,T,\ldots,T^{r-1})\) in the exponent map, for integers \(T\to\infty\).
Every nonzero fixed relation vector evaluates to a nonzero polynomial in \(T\), so the least norm of an integer relation tends to infinity, with the injective one-variable case understood directly.
The [Boyd–Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#specializations-and-remaining-source-reports) gives convergence of the measures to \(m(G)\).
For large \(T\) the one-variable polynomial has measure less than \(\log\theta\) and is signed reciprocal up to a Laurent monomial.
Indeed its integer content and the nonzero extreme coefficients after clearing monomials have absolute value one since the multiplicative measure is less than two.
Each irreducible factor is reciprocal up to sign by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) or is cyclotomic, and the product has the stated symmetry.

On the fixed finite support the order induced by the specializing functional stabilizes, giving extremal exponents \(s_-,s_+\).
For large \(T\) the exponent map is injective on the union of the support and its reflection in \((s_-+s_+)/2\), since all nonzero differences in this finite set eventually have nonzero image.
The one-variable reflection thus lifts to that very reflection with the coefficient sign determined by the extremes.
This proves the assertion for \(G\), including constant or single-term polynomials.
Taking \(G=F_B\) proves (3) and signed reciprocity of every face below its threshold.
Every such face has centrally symmetric support, and each vertex coefficient has absolute value one by the face bound.

<a id="zonotope"></a>
## From two-dimensional faces to the number of vertices

We give the geometric argument also when the polytope is itself two-dimensional.
Let \(Q\) be a polytope all of whose two-dimensional faces are centrally symmetric, using the whole polytope among its faces.
In dimensions zero and one it is already a point or a segment.
Otherwise choose an edge direction \(e\) of unit length and project along it by \(\pi\).
The chosen edge is the full fiber over a vertex of \(\pi Q\): an exposing functional for the edge descends under \(\pi\).
Write \(l>0\) for its length.
Above a projected edge incident to that vertex the exposed face in \(Q\) has dimension two, since it contains the fiber segment and projects onto an edge.
By central symmetry the fiber at the other endpoint is a parallel segment of the same length.
Connectivity of the graph of \(\pi Q\) propagates the length \(l\) to every projected vertex.
For each other projected point take a convex combination of projected vertices.
Combining the lower and upper endpoints of their fibers with these weights gives two points of its fiber at distance \(l\).
Thus every fiber has length at least \(l\), and fiberwise subtraction gives
\[
                    Q=Q'+[0,le],\qquad Q'=Q\cap(Q-le).
\]

The fibers of \(Q'\) over projected vertices are singletons, so \(Q'\) has no edge parallel to \(e\).
For any exposed edge of \(Q'\), the face of \(Q\) in the same direction is its translate or its sum with \([0,le]\).
The latter is a parallelogram when the directions are independent, so the original edge direction always occurs in \(Q\).
Every two-dimensional face \(T\) of \(Q'\) is centrally symmetric.
In fact the corresponding face of \(Q\) is a translate of \(T\) or \(T+[0,le]\).
When the latter is two-dimensional, central symmetry of the sum implies central symmetry of \(T\) by cancellation of the segment in the support functions of the sum and its reflection.
When it is three-dimensional it is a prism over \(T\), and a translate of \(T\) is a face of this prism and hence of \(Q\).
Iteration removes at least one edge direction at each step and introduces none, proving the segment-sum description.

For a zonotope of dimension \(r\), combine parallel generators and select \(r\) linearly independent directions among the remaining segments.
A generic linear functional chooses exactly one endpoint of every segment, and its maximum on the zonotope is their sum.
Two different chambers of the central hyperplanes perpendicular to the segments give distinct vertices, because a functional from one chamber is strictly decreased by reversing any of its selected endpoints.
The \(r\) basis hyperplanes give \(2^r\) chambers, hence at least \(2^r\) vertices.
A further direction has \(j\ge2\) nonzero coordinates in the selected basis.
Its hyperplane meets the interior of every basis orthant where these \(j\) signed summands have both signs.
By varying their magnitudes the sum can then be zero.
This splits \((2^j-2)2^{r-j}\ge2^{r-1}\) of the original orthants.
Thus fewer than \(3\cdot2^{r-1}\) vertices forces the zonotope to be generated by just the \(r\) independent directions, hence to be a parallelotope.

<a id="corners"></a>
## Equality and the case of a single additional term

If \(k=2^r\), every term is a vertex of a parallelotope.
Write the vertices as \(v_0+\sum_i d_i g_i\), \(d_i\in\{0,1\}\), with independent integer edge vectors \(g_i\).
The coefficient \(c_d\) at each vertex is \(1\) or \(-1\).
On every square of indices the products of the two pairs of opposite corner coefficients agree by signed reciprocity of that two-dimensional face.
Consequently the ratio along an edge in coordinate \(i\) is unchanged when any other binary index is changed.
There are signs \(\epsilon_i\) with \(c_d=c_0\prod_i\epsilon_i^{d_i}\), also directly for \(r=1\).
The lift is therefore \(c_0 Z^{v_0}\prod_i(1+\epsilon_i Z^{g_i})\).
Each \(A(g_i)\ne0\) since the corresponding edge endpoints give distinct original exponents.
The one-variable product has measure zero, proving the strict dimension bound at positive measure.

Suppose \(k=2^r+1\) and \(r\ge2\).
The vertex bound still forces a parallelotope.
Its single extra support point must be its center by signed reciprocity of the whole lift.
Every square of corner indices still has the same opposite-product identity, including for \(r=2\) when the whole face also contains the central term.
Call its nonzero integer coefficient \(c\).
After the Haar-preserving substitution \(Z\mapsto Z^2\) and removal of the central monomial, the lift becomes
\[
                 c+c_0\prod_{i=1}^r(Z^{-g_i}+\epsilon_i Z^{g_i}).
\]
The \(Z^{g_i}\) are independent Haar variables because the \(g_i\) have full rank.
Each factor has, up to a constant of modulus one, the distribution of \(2\cos\theta_i\) for independent uniform angles.
Let \(T\) be the product of the factors with \(i\ge3\), including all constant phases, so that \(\mathbb E\log|T|=0\) and \(T\ne0\) almost surely.
The product \(4\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2\) has the law of \(2\cos U+2\cos V\) by the degree-two torus map \((\theta_1,\theta_2)\mapsto(\theta_1+\theta_2,\theta_1-\theta_2)\).
Conditioning on \(T\) gives \(\log|T|+U_2(c/T)\), where \(U_2\) is the logarithmic potential of that sum.
The [complex comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v69.md#potential) and its [global supporting line](Yin_C_G0901_v69.md#support) give \(U_2(z)\ge C+p\log|z|\) for \(z\ne0\) with \(p>0\).
Integration yields \(m(F)\ge C+p\log|c|\ge C\).
The one-variable transfer now gives the asserted bound.

<a id="parameters"></a>
## An explicit representation in fewer exponent differences

Select \(t\) linearly independent original rows (1) spanning \(W\).
Delete column \(k\), so that the row equations act on \(a_i-a_k\) for \(i<k\).
Deletion preserves their independence because all original row sums vanish.
Choose \(t\) pivot columns with a nonzero minor, of absolute determinant \(\Delta\), leaving \(r=k-1-t\) free columns.
For \(t=0\) take \(\Delta=1\).
The free differences \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\) are actual \(a_i-a_k\) at these last columns.
In each pivot equation solve by Cramer's rule, expanding its right side in these free differences.
Each numerator coefficient up to sign is a minor with one pivot column replaced by a free column.
Every row before restriction has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt2 N\) because its positive and negative masses each are at most \(N\).
Hadamard's bound gives \(1\le\Delta\le(\sqrt2 N)^t\) and the same upper bound for the absolute values of all the numerator coefficients.
Using \(\Delta\) itself on the free coordinates supplies integers \(u_{ij}\) in that box, including zeros for the reference exponent, with
\[
           \Delta(a_i-a_k)=\sum_{j=1}^r u_{ij}y_j,\qquad
           X^{-\Delta a_k}P(X^\Delta)
             =\sum_{i=1}^k c_i\prod_{j=1}^r(X^{y_j})^{u_{ij}}.
\]
The coefficients of \(P\) are retained exactly, and at fixed \(k,\delta\) only finitely many such exponent matrices occur.
The case of unbounded term counts satisfying these support constraints remains open.
