# The largest conjugate displacement forces measure in every two-row double-root witness

<a id="house"></a>
## The finite bound

Let \(p\ge2\) be an integer, and let a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(F\) occupy exactly two residue classes modulo \(p\), with rows coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Its row integral \(I\) is the Haar probability mean on the unit circle of \(\log\max(|U|,|V|)\) for its two signed rows.
Multiplication of either row by a monomial and exchange of the rows preserve \(I\).
If \(a\ne0\) is a zero of \(F\) of multiplicity at least two, then
\[
                     I\ \ge\ p\,\big|\log|a|\big|-\log(8p).              \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for \(P^2\mid F\) and a monic nontorsion unit minimal polynomial \(P\), set \(h=m(P)=\log M(P)\) and \(b=\max_{P(a)=0}|\log|a||\).
Then \(I\ge pb-\log(8p)\) for every choice of the two occupied classes.
For prime \(p>4\), a primitive \(F\), its ordinary exponent span \(D\), and the noncyclotomic cofactor \(R\) of \(F/P^2\), this gives
\[
 \frac{m(R)}p\ \ge\
 (1-4/p)b-\frac{(1-4/p)\log(8p)+2h}{p}
                 -\frac{2\log(CD)}{p^2},
 \qquad C=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-z|)^{1/2}\,d\sigma(z).                  \tag{2}
\]
The estimate on \(I\) has no span, coefficient-height or degree-preservation hypothesis.

<a id="jet"></a>
## Removing the second lowest possible exponent with the double-root condition

Multiply \(F\) by the monomial sending its lowest exponent to zero.
In this orientation there is a unique \(1\le r<p\) for which
\[
           F(x)=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p),\qquad U,V\in\mathbb Z[X],\quad U(0)\ne0 .
\]
Here \(r\) specifies the other residue after translation, even if its first occupied exponent exceeds \(r\).
The rows have no common unit-circle zero by coprimality, so \(f(\zeta)=\log\max(|U(\zeta)|,|V(\zeta)|)\) is continuous on the circle.
For \(|z|<1\) put
\[
 W(z)=\exp\int_{\mathbb T}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z} f(\zeta)\,d\sigma(\zeta),
 \qquad u=U/W,\qquad v=V/W.
\]
The real part of the exponent is the Poisson integral of \(f\), harmonic on the disk with continuous boundary values \(f\).
By the subharmonic maximum principle it majorizes both \(\log|U|\) and \(\log|V|\).
Thus \(W\) is analytic and zero-free, \(W(0)=e^I\), and the Taylor coefficients \(u_j,v_j\) of \(u,v\) have modulus at most one, by Cauchy's inequality on radii tending to one.
In particular \(I\ge\log|U(0)|\ge0\).

Suppose first \(0<\rho=|a|<1\) and put \(q=\rho^p\).
The function \(H(x)=u(x^p)+x^rv(x^p)=F(x)/W(x^p)\) satisfies \(H(a)=H'(a)=0\).
The operator \(1-(x/r)d/dx\) preserves the constant and kills the coefficient of \(x^r\).
Applying it at \(a\) to the Taylor series gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 |U(0)| e^{-I}=|u_0|
 &\le\sum_{j\ge1}\left(\frac{jp}{r}-1+\frac{jp}{r}\rho^r\right)q^j\\
 &=q\left(\frac{p(1+\rho^r)}{r(1-q)^2}-\frac1{1-q}\right).             \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
Absolute convergence at \(a\) justifies the differentiation and summation.
If \(q\le1/2\), the last expression is at most \(8pq/r\), so (1) follows from \(|U(0)|\ge1\) and \(r\ge1\).
If \(q>1/2\), then \(p\log(1/\rho)<\log2\), and \(I\ge0\) suffices.

If \(|a|>1\), let \(D\) be the degree after the lowest-exponent translation and apply the disk calculation to \(x^D F(1/x)\) at \(1/a\).
Its lowest coefficient is the nonzero integral leading coefficient of \(F\).
Its two row magnitudes at \(\zeta\) are those of the original rows at \(\zeta^{-1}\), in some order, since the other factors in reversing the rows are monomials.
Haar invariance thus preserves \(I\), regardless of the residue of the highest exponent.
The case \(|a|=1\) follows from \(I\ge0\), proving (1).

## Cofactors and units with one conjugate on one side of the circle

For a primitive integral witness, \(m(F)=2h+m(R)\), since cyclotomic and monomial factors have measure zero.
The [two-row comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) gives \(m(F)\ge(1-4/p)I-(2/p)\log(CD)\), proving (2).
Equivalently, for the stripped rows of maximum degree \(k>0\), their absolute homogeneous resultant \(B\) and the energy \(\mathcal D\) in the [Dirichlet identity](Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#identity) obey
\[
                         \log B+\mathcal D\ \ge\ 2k\{pb-\log(8p)\}.
\]

For a unit the sums of the positive logarithms and of the absolute negative logarithms of the conjugate moduli both equal \(h\).
In particular \(b\le h\), with equality if either the open disk or its open exterior contains just one conjugate, as for Salem numbers.
Throughout the [fourth-logarithmic prime window and degree cap](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#finite), its uniform estimate \(ph/\log(2+D)\to\infty\) and \(D\ge p-1\) when a double root is present give
\[
                     \frac{m(R)}{ph}\ \ge\ \frac{b}{h}-o(1)
\]
as \(d=\deg P\to\infty\), uniformly in the admissible prime, residues and capped witness.
Indeed a polynomial of span below \(p\) using two classes has only two terms and simple nonzero roots.
Consequently the lower limit is at least one when \(b/h\to1\), including for every sequence of Salem inputs in this window.
Since \(b\) can shrink along with \(h\), an absolute gap would require an additional lower estimate.
