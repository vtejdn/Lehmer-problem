# Cyclotomic comparison seeds attaining the Lehmer return

<a id="statement"></a>
<a id="benchmark"></a>
## An exact family at every positive integer constant

Put
\[
 P(z)=z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1
\]
and define the monic cyclotomic products
\[
 \begin{split}
 B(z)&=(z-1)(z+1)^2\Phi_3(z)\Phi_5(z),\\
 C(z)&=(z-1)(z+1)^2\Phi_3(z)\Phi_4(z)^2.
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
For every integer \(q\ge2\), the polynomial
\[
                         f_q=P-(q-1)B                              \tag{2}
\]
is the minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta_q\), with \(f_q(0)=q\).
It has the representation
\[
 \begin{split}
 f_q(z)&=(z-q)C(z)+U_q(z),\\
 U_q(z)&=-qz^8-qz^7+z^6+z^5+z^4-z^3+(q-2)z^2+(q-1)z.
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\deg C=9\), \(C(0)=-1\), \(\deg U_q=8\), \(U_q(0)=0\), and the coefficient height of \(U_q\) is exactly \(q\).
The positive delay-one return is independent of \(q\):
\[
                  zf_q(z)+z^{10}f_q(1/z)=(z+1)P(z).                 \tag{4}
\]
Its sole noncyclotomic factor is the degree-ten Lehmer minimal polynomial \(P\).
The input excess satisfies
\[
                       \theta_q-q=1-\frac2q+O(q^{-2}) .             \tag{5}
\]
Thus this family realizes the fixed Lehmer output at arbitrarily large constants but does not make the Pisot excess approach zero or the output's trace excess.

For example, \(q=2\) gives the height-two perturbation
\[
 \begin{split}
 f_2(z)&=z^{10}-3z^8-5z^7-4z^6-2z^5+2z^3+4z^2+4z+2,\\
 U_2(z)&=-2z^8-2z^7+z^6+z^5+z^4-z^3+z.
 \end{split}
\]

## Exact residue signs for the benchmark

The [accepted Lehmer control](Yin_C_G0226_v1.md#lehmer-joint-prime-control) supplies the irreducible Salem identity of \(P\), at its recorded source and inspection scope.
The trace expression is
\[
 P(z)=z^5Q(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
 Q(x)=x^5+x^4-5x^3-5x^2+4x+3.
\]
Write \(T>2\) for its exterior trace root and \(x_1<\cdots<x_4\) for its four other roots.
Let
\[
 \alpha=\frac{-1-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{-1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 A(x)=(x+2)(x+1)(x^2+x-1).
\]
Direct polynomial evaluation gives
\[
 Q(-2)=Q(-1)=Q(2)=-1,\qquad
 Q(\alpha)=\alpha+2>0,\qquad Q(\gamma)=\gamma+2>0 .
\]
The last two identities follow from the remainder \(Q(x)\equiv x+2\pmod{x^2+x-1}\).
The intermediate value theorem gives a root in each of
\[
            (-2,\alpha),\quad(\alpha,-1),\quad(-1,\gamma),
                         \quad(\gamma,2),\quad(2,\infty).
\]
These five disjoint intervals exhaust the degree of \(Q\), so their roots are simple and are respectively \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,T\).

The identity
\[
                      B(z)=(z-1)z^4A(z+z^{-1})
\]
puts (2) in the [integral inverse parametrization](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex), with
\[
                   R_q(x)=-(q-1)A(x),\qquad
                   H(x)=\frac{Q(x)}{x-T}.
\]
At the four neutral roots the signs of \(R_q\) are respectively \(+,-,+,-\), whereas those of \(H'\) are \(-,+,-,+\).
Consequently every residue \(-R_q(x_j)/H'(x_j)\) is strictly positive.
Also \(B(-1)=0\) and \(P(-1)=1\), so \(f_q(-1)=1>0\).
By the same inverse criterion, this endpoint sign is exactly its remaining strict simplex inequality.
The criterion therefore proves that \(f_q\) is a Pisot minimal polynomial for every integer \(q\ge2\), with no numerical root test.

Since \(B\) has degree nine and constant coefficient minus one, \(z^9B(1/z)=-B(z)\).
The reciprocal identity of \(P\) then gives (4) directly from (2).
Expanding (1) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 B(z)&=z^9+3z^8+4z^7+3z^6+z^5-z^4-3z^3-4z^2-3z-1,\\
 C(z)&=z^9+2z^8+3z^7+3z^6+z^5-z^4-3z^3-3z^2-2z-1.
 \end{split}
\]
Subtracting \((z-q)C\) from (2) proves (3) and its exact height assertion.

For (5), polynomial division gives
\[
                 \frac{P(z)}{B(z)}=z-2+\frac2z+O(z^{-2})
                       \qquad(z\longrightarrow+\infty).
\]
The Pisot norm identity implies \(\theta_q>q\), so \(\theta_q\to\infty\).
At its exterior root, (2) gives \(P(\theta_q)/B(\theta_q)=q-1\); the denominator cannot vanish for \(\theta_q>1\).
Substitution first yields \(\theta_q=q+1+O(q^{-1})\), then the stated expansion (5).

<a id="general-encoding"></a>
## Every small Salem target admits this comparison form

Let \(P\) now be any monic Salem minimal polynomial of degree \(d=2m\ge4\), with exterior root \(1<\sigma\le2\).
There exists a monic cyclotomic product \(C\), depending only on \(P\), of degree \(k=d-1\) and constant coefficient minus one, whose factors have orders only in \(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), with the following property.
Every monic integral degree-\(d\) solution \(f\) with constant coefficient \(q\) of
\[
                     zf+f^*=(z+1)P,\qquad f^*(z)=z^df(1/z),
\]
has a representation
\[
             f=(z-q)C+U,\qquad U\in\mathbb Z[z],\quad
                             U(0)=0,\quad\deg U<k.                 \tag{6}
\]
In particular, for every real \(L\ge16d\), the [controlled inverse theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v31.md#statement) supplies a Pisot solution in this comparison class with
\[
 3L<q<10L,\qquad
 0<\theta-q-(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2)<\frac{36d}{q-2}.                 \tag{7}
\]
No height restriction on \(U\) is asserted here.

To prove the encoding, write \(a\) for the coefficient of \(z^{d-1}\) in \(P\) and put \(c=a+1\).
Its trace roots are \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\in(2,5/2]\) and \(m-1\) numbers in \((-2,2)\).
Their sum is \(-a\), giving
\[
                    -k-\tfrac32<a<k-3.
\]
Since \(a\) is an integer, in particular \(-k\le c\le k-2\).
Put \(s=(k-1)/2\).
Every integer in \([-2s,2s]\) is a sum of \(s\) integers from \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\).
Choose such a sum equal to \(c+1\), and realize its summands as the linear coefficients of the monic quadratic cyclotomic products
\[
 (z-1)^2,\qquad \Phi_6(z),\qquad\Phi_4(z),\qquad
                       \Phi_3(z),\qquad(z+1)^2 .
\]
Let \(C\) be their product multiplied by \(z-1\).
Then \(\deg C=1+2s=k\), \(C(0)=-1\), and its coefficient of \(z^{k-1}\) is \(-1+(c+1)=c\).

Comparing coefficients of \(z^d\) in the return identity gives \(f_{d-1}+q=c\).
Thus the leading two coefficients of \(f\) and \((z-q)C\) agree, as do their constants.
Their difference is exactly a polynomial \(U\) satisfying (6).
Applying the controlled inverse theorem proves (7).
The general encoding and coefficient observation were supplied by the coordinating agent.

The [height obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v289.md#statement) therefore concerns restricted coefficient growth of the perturbation.
Once that restriction is removed, this comparison form already covers every Salem target up to two, including the controlled inverses whose excess approaches the trace excess.
The explicit benchmark (2) has a different excess limit, as (5) records.
Neither result constructs Salem numbers tending to one or resolves the unrestricted Lehmer problem.
