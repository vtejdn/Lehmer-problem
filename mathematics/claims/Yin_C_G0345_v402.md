# Recovering the least rational factor degree from two prime reductions

<a id="statement"></a>
## Common divisor degrees at two primes

Let \(H\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be monic, separable and nonconstant, with rational irreducible factor degrees \(d_1,\ldots,d_t\), counted once per factor.
Write \(m=\min_i d_i\) and suppose the Galois group \(G\) on the roots contains \(D=\prod_{i=1}^t A_{d_i}\), acting independently on the rational factors.
Call \(p\) good if \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H\), let \(S(p)\) be the set of positive degrees of monic divisors of \(H\bmod p\), and put \(u(p)=\min S(p)\).
Thus \(u(p)\) is the least degree of an irreducible factor of the reduction, and \(S(p)\) consists of sums of such degrees using the available multiplicities.
Fix any two reduced residue classes, with potentially different moduli, and let \(p,q\) range over good primes in their respective classes.
If \(m\ge12(t+2)\), then
\[
 m=\max_{p\ne q}\min\bigl(S(p)\cap S(q)\bigr).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for any proposed bound \(k\), the absence of a positive rational divisor degree below \(k\) is equivalent to the existence of two such primes having no common positive divisor degree below \(k\).
The one-prime estimate in either prescribed class is
\[
       \lfloor m/2\rfloor\ \le\ \max_p u(p)\ \le\ m.           \tag{2}
\]
For each extremum the attaining primes can be taken arbitrarily large.

<a id="classes"></a>
## Single-prime optima in a prescribed class

Let \(L\) be the splitting field of \(H\), and set \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_M)\) for the modulus \(M\) of one class \(v\bmod M\).
The elements of \({\rm Gal}(LK/\mathbb Q)\) acting by \(\zeta_M\mapsto\zeta_M^v\) restrict on \(L\) to a nonempty coset of \({\rm Gal}(L/(L\cap K))\).
Each alternating group here is perfect since \(d_i\ge5\), so \(D\subseteq[G,G]\).
The latter group acts trivially on the abelian extension \(L\cap K\), and each fixed \(D\)-coset in the cyclotomic fiber therefore permits all tuples of permutations of its prescribed block signs independently.
By [Chebotarev's theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001), every cycle pattern so obtained occurs at infinitely many good primes of the prescribed class, after omitting the finitely many ramified primes and divisors of \(\operatorname{disc}H\).
The cycles on the roots give the degrees of the modular irreducibles, as in the [quartic residue-class argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v220.md#classes).

Let \(\Sigma_v\) be the set of block sign vectors in that fiber and define
\[
 L_i(e)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_i,&e=(-1)^{d_i-1},\\
 \lfloor d_i/2\rfloor,&e\ne(-1)^{d_i-1}.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \max_p u(p)=\max_{\epsilon\in\Sigma_v}\min_i L_i(\epsilon_i).
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
When the prescribed sign agrees with a full cycle, take one cycle of length \(d_i\).
Otherwise at least two cycles occur, so the smallest length is at most \(\lfloor d_i/2\rfloor\).
Two cycles of lengths \(\lfloor d_i/2\rfloor,\lceil d_i/2\rceil\) have the required sign and attain that bound.
Independence inside each sign vector proves (3) and hence (2).

<a id="pair"></a>
## Separating the small divisor degrees

A smallest rational factor of \(H\) is monic integral by Gauss's lemma, so it reduces to a degree-\(m\) divisor at every prime.
This gives the upper bound in (1).
For the reverse bound choose any one feasible sign vector for the class of \(p\).
On each block use a full cycle when its sign is feasible and cycles of lengths \(1,d_i-1\) otherwise.
A sum of cycle lengths below \(m\) is then either a sum of at most \(t\) ones, or a single length \(m-1\) from a block of size \(m\).
Consequently
\[
       S(p)\cap[1,m-1]\ \subseteq\ [1,t]\cup\{m-1\}.           \tag{4}
\]

Choose an integer \(j\) strictly between \(m/3\) and \((m-1)/2\) and unequal to each \(d_i-m+1\).
The open interval has length \((m-3)/6>t+1\), so deleting at most \(t\) forbidden integers leaves a choice.
Use any feasible sign vector for the class of \(q\), taking a full cycle when feasible and cycles of lengths \(j,d_i-j\) otherwise.
All cycles now exceed \(t\).
Below \(m\), a positive sum of their lengths can only be \(j,2j\), or one \(d_i-j\), since \(3j>m\), every \(d_i-j>m/2\), and adding \(j\) to any such length reaches \(m\).
The first two possibilities are less than \(m-1\), and the last avoids \(m-1\) by the exclusions on \(j\).
Together with (4) this rules out all common positive degrees below \(m\).
The two cyclotomic fibers can be chosen separately, and the infinitude in each supplies distinct primes.
This imposes no upper bound on the least witnessing primes.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Independent alternating actions for the reciprocal seeds

Fix \(s\ge3\), \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\) and \(F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\).
For coprime positive \(a,b\) put \(r=b/a\), remove the cyclotomic factors from the monic associate of \(F(X^a,X^b)\), and write the result as \(X^nH(X+X^{-1})\).
The following conclusions hold for all sufficiently large \(r\) at fixed \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\), including every quartic direction.
For fixed \(s\equiv2\bmod3\) they hold for large \(r\) with odd \(a\), or with \(r>C_s(\log(3(a+b)))^{424}\) for a sufficiently large constant \(C_s\).

In these ranges the individual nontorsion factors are reciprocal and their inverse-pair traces carry \(A_{d_i}\) or \(S_{d_i}\), by the [trace group theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#statement) and the [dyadic block bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v213.md#slopes).
The [exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) gives simple section roots, so each trace occurs just once in \(H\) and every rational trace factor lifts to a single irreducible reciprocal factor of degree \(2d_i\).
We verify that the [quartic independence argument](Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#independence) extends to all these fixed seeds.
Write \(N=\max(a,b)\) and \(E=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
The coefficients of \(1,U,V\) in \(F\) are nonzero, so the support differences generate \(\mathbb Z^2\).
The polynomials \(g,B\) are coprime, and \(g\) has only simple nonzero roots by the same exterior allocation result.
For every positive \(k\) and nonzero \(c,e\), the curve \(F(cU^k,eV^k)=0\) is absolutely irreducible by Eisenstein in \(V\) at a simple zero of \(g(cU^k)\) and primitivity over \(\mathbb C[U]\).
Its total degree is \((s+1)k\).

The [torsion-quotient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) excludes a root-of-unity quotient of distinct nontorsion section roots for large \(N\), and yields \(E(x^\ell)=E(x)\) for every such root \(x\) and positive \(\ell\).
Suppose two unequal, noninverse nontorsion roots \(x,z\) are multiplicatively dependent.
After replacing \(z\) by its inverse if needed, which preserves membership in this reciprocal section, write \(x=\xi w^u,\ z=\eta w^v\) for roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) and coprime positive \(u,v\).
Indeed take a primitive relation \(x^v/z^u\) torsion, choose \(i,j\) with \(iu+jv=1\), and put \(w=x^i z^j\).
Then \(E(x)=E(x^v)=E(z^u)=E(z)=E(w)\), since \(w\in E(x,z)\) and \(x\in E(w)\).
Its degree over \(E\) is at most \((s+1)N\).
The [primitive height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives \(h(x),h(z)\ll_s N^{-1}\).
The [relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) at \(w\) and \(x\) gives \(u,v\ll_s(\log(2N))^4\) and \([E(x):E]\gg_s N/(\log(2N))^4\), using \(h(x)=u h(w)\), \(h(z)=v h(w)\).
If \(u\ne v\), the two irreducible curves \(F(\xi^a U^u,\xi^b V^u)=0\) and \(F(\eta^a U^v,\eta^b V^v)=0\) have different degrees and contain \((w^a,w^b)\).
These coordinates generate \(E(w)\) over \(E\), so Bézout bounds its degree by \((s+1)^2uv\), contradicting the lower bound at large \(N\).
If \(u=v\), then \(u=v=1\), contrary to the exclusion of torsion quotients.

The [two-coordinate height product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) now gives \([\mathbb Q(x,z):\mathbb Q]\gg_s N^2/(\log(2N))^{212}\) for unequal noninverse roots, using the upper bound \((s+1)^2N^2\) on their joint degree.
As in the [quartic subdirect product argument](Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#independence), the commutator of the group on all traces maps onto every \(A_{d_i}\).
A proper subdirect product would identify two simple factors of the same degree diagonally.
All \(d_i\) tend uniformly to infinity, so the automorphism in this diagonal is a relabeling, and its normalizer in the two symmetric groups is diagonal as well.
Two corresponding traces on different rational factors would then have the same stabilizer, giving two roots above them of joint degree at most \(4d_i\ll_s N\).
The preceding lower bound excludes this possibility, proving \(D\subseteq G\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## A one-prime criterion for a uniform lower bound on measure

Let \(\mu_{a,b}\) be the least logarithmic Mahler measure among nontorsion rational irreducible factors of \(F(X^a,X^b)\), and \(I_s=m(B)>0\).
At large primitive slopes every such factor accounts for at least one exterior root and there are \(a\) roots above each of the fixed exterior poles of \(B\), so \(1\le t\ll_s a\).
The [individual height estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(m\gg_s a\sqrt r\).
In particular \(m\ge12(t+2)\) eventually, so (1) applies throughout the stated ranges of seed degrees and directions.
On the unit circle \(|g|=|B|\), and thus the limiting height integral is \(I_s\) by Jensen's formula.
At a root \(x\) above a degree-\(d_i\) trace factor the height estimate gives
\[
  b h(x)=I_s+O_s(\delta),\qquad
  \delta=r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(4m)}{2m}},
  \qquad
  \mu_{a,b}=\frac{2m}{b}\bigl(I_s+O_s(\delta)\bigr).            \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(\log(2d)/d\) decreases for \(d\ge2\), so the same height error bounds every reciprocal degree \(d=2d_i\).
Multiplication by \(2d_i\) and minimization give a lower bound using \(d_i\ge m\) and \(I_s-O_s(\delta)>0\), and the degree-\(m\) trace block gives the matching upper bound.

It follows from (2) and (5) that, for an arbitrary set of primitive directions in these ranges, a uniform positive lower bound on \(\mu_{a,b}\) for sufficiently large \(r\) is equivalent to the following condition.
There is a constant \(c>0\) such that for every direction in the set with sufficiently large \(r\) there exists a good prime \(p\) for which every irreducible factor of \(H\bmod p\) has degree at least \(cb\).
The prime may even be required to lie in a reduced residue class prescribed separately for each direction.
Equivalently, along any sequence in these ranges with \(r\to\infty\), one has \(\mu_{a,b}\to0\) exactly when \((\max_p u(p))/b\to0\).
For \(s\not\equiv2\bmod3\), the [bounded-slope and common-dilation reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#criterion) makes the high-slope criterion equivalent to a positive gap for all nontorsion factors in the entire fixed-seed family over all positive exponent pairs.
It treats every factor, including one with several exterior roots, and leaves open whether a suitable \(c\) exists.
