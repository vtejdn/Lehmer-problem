# Solvable normal subgroups of the permutation group on conjugate labels

<a id="statement"></a>
## The radical of the splitting group for bounded measure and large compressed degree

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number of logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Group its rational conjugates by root-of-unity quotient and let \(\gamma\) be the product in the class of \(\alpha\).
Put \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
On the conjugates of \(\gamma\), form the [labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) identifying \(x,y\) if \(x/y\) or \(xy\) is torsion.
Denote the faithful permutation group on these labels by \(G_0\).
Write \(R(K)\) for the largest solvable normal subgroup of a finite group \(K\).

For fixed \(M>0\), assume \(0<m\le M\) and \(d\) sufficiently large depending only on \(M\).
Then **every block** of at least two labels has a setwise stabilizer whose induced permutation group \(H\) satisfies \(R(H)=1\).
In particular \(R(G_0)=1\).
The radical of the full splitting group \(G_\gamma\) is precisely the kernel of its action on the labels, an elementary abelian two-group, possibly trivial.
For the splitting group of the original number one has the canonical identification
\[
                              G_\alpha/R(G_\alpha)\ \simeq\ G_0 .          \tag{1}
\]
This identifies the quotient with a specific Galois action even when there are large torsion-quotient classes before compression.

Consequently the positive logarithmic measures of all inputs admitting a block with nontrivial solvable radical in its induced action form a finite set in every bounded interval.
This includes a group that is itself nonsolvable but has an affine primitive action on such a block, with arbitrary dimension and field characteristic.
The [alternating-section constraint](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#statement) allows nonsolvable groups of this kind by their sections alone.

<a id="affine"></a>
## A regular elementary abelian group on a block

Suppose first that the group induced on a block \(B\) of \(b\ge2\) labels contains a regular normal elementary abelian subgroup \(V\).
After choosing an origin the labels are the additive group \(V\), and all permutations induced on \(B\) are affine.
Indeed a permutation normalizing the translations is determined by its value at the origin and its conjugation action on the translations.
Write \(w\in\{1,2\}\) for the number of conjugates per label and \(u=d/(wb)\) for the number of translates of \(B\).
The [rank bound inside every block](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) makes the rank of these multiplicative vectors at least four for \(d\) large depending on \(M\).

In odd characteristic any ordered pair \(a,b_0\) determines the third point \(2b_0-a\), distinct from both and fixed by its pointwise stabilizer.
This contradicts the [ordinary-pair bound](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#ordinary) for \(d\) large.

Now suppose that \(V\) has exponent two.
For each point \(v\in V\) let \(L_v\) be the one-dimensional rational subspace corresponding to its multiplicative label in the torsion-free root group tensored with \(\mathbb Q\).
The \(L_v\) are pairwise distinct.
Each translation on \(V\) is realized by a Galois automorphism acting linearly on this rational vector space, so ranks of sets of lines are unchanged by translation.

Fix the origin \(o=0\), quotient the rational space by \(L_o\), and consider the set of distinct projected points from \(V\setminus\{o\}\).
All are nonzero and the set has rank at least three.
The [real ordinary-line argument](Yin_C_G0345_v266.md#ordinary) supplies two distinct projected points whose line contains no third projected point.
Take representatives \(a,b_0\) of these two points and put \(z=a+b_0\) in \(V\).
The labels \(o,a,b_0,z\) form an affine plane of four distinct points.
The translations by its four elements act transitively on its triples, since each triple is the complement of one point.
The triple \(L_o,L_a,L_{b_0}\) has rank three by the choice of distinct projected points.
Thus **every** triple in this plane has rank three.
In particular \(L_z\) projects to a point distinct from both chosen ones.
It lies outside their ordinary line, proving that all four original multiplicative representatives are independent.

The fourth affine point is determined by the first three as their affine sum in characteristic two.
Every possible image within a translated block is therefore specified at the level of labels by three ordered points.
The joint field degree \(J\) of four selected actual roots over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) satisfies
\[
                       J\le u b^3 w^4\le 8d^3 .
\]
The [four-coordinate height inequality](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) at common height \(m/d\) gives
\[
                       m^4\gg \frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{K_4}}
\]
with \(K_4\) absolute.
This excludes the binary affine case as well for fixed \(M\) and large \(d\).
Only the translations realizing ranks and the affine form of the action were needed, so the conclusion includes binary affine groups that are not triply transitive.

<a id="normal"></a>
## Orbits of an abelian normal subgroup and the two kernels

Take any block \(B\) and let \(H\) be its induced faithful action.
If \(R(H)\ne1\), the last nontrivial term of the derived series of \(R(H)\) is a characteristic abelian subgroup of \(R(H)\), normal in \(H\).
For any prime \(p\) dividing its order, its subgroup of elements killed by \(p\) is elementary abelian, nontrivial and normal in \(H\).
Choose an orbit \(B'\) of this subgroup having at least two points, which exists by faithfulness.
Normality makes \(B'\) a block in \(B\), hence also a block of the full label action.
The image of the elementary abelian group on \(B'\) is transitive and faithful, so it is regular, since an element fixing one point fixes every point by commutativity.
This image is normal in the induced action of the setwise stabilizer of \(B'\).
Indeed a full-group element preserving \(B'\) must preserve \(B\) and hence normalize the elementary abelian subgroup on \(B\).
The affine exclusion applied to \(B'\) yields the contradiction, uniformly in the choice of \(B\) and \(B'\).

The kernel of \(G_\gamma\to G_0\) embeds in the product of the permutation groups on the \(w\)-element fibers.
It is therefore elementary abelian of exponent at most two.
Since \(R(G_0)=1\), it equals \(R(G_\gamma)\).
There is a natural surjection \(G_\alpha\to G_\gamma\) by the class-product construction inside the original splitting field.
Its kernel is solvable.
In fact if \(f\) is the common original class size, choose \(N\) divisible by all within-class torsion quotient orders.
Every original root \(x\) in a class with product \(y\) satisfies \(x^{fN}=y^N\).
Over the splitting field of \(\gamma\), adjoining the \(fN\)-th roots of unity is abelian.
Adjoining the \(fN\)-th roots of the finitely many \(y^N\) there has abelian Galois group, by the action on one radical per \(y\) and the presence of the roots of unity.
The splitting field of \(\alpha\) is a subextension of this solvable extension, proving the kernel assertion.
The composite \(G_\alpha\to G_0\) has solvable kernel and trivial radical in its image, proving (1).

Finally \(m(\gamma)=m(\alpha)\).
The preceding bound on \(d\) when the block radical is nontrivial, and the coefficient bound \(2^d e^M\) for the primitive polynomial of \(\gamma\), prove the finite-spectrum assertion.
