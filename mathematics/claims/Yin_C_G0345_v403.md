# Irreducible power substitutions realize the fourth-logarithmic bounds for two coprime rows

<a id="statement"></a>
## Representatives with the original positive measure

Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Consider monic irreducible polynomials
\[
 P(X)=P_0(X^n),\qquad n\text{ an odd prime},\qquad
 d=\deg P=ne,\qquad e=\deg P_0\le\log(3d),                            \tag{1}
\]
where \(P_0\) is the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit.
Every such \(P_0\) has representatives (1) of arbitrarily large degree, all of measure \(h=m(P_0)>0\).
Put \(y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil\).
For every sufficiently large \(d\) in (1), **every** prime \(y<p\le2y\) preserves the degree of a root \(\gamma\) of \(P\) on powering.
Write \(Q=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\gamma^p)\), let \(\mu\) be normalized circle measure, and set
\[
 J(Q)=\int_{\mathbb T}(\log|Q|-m(Q))_+\,d\mu,\qquad
 A_j(Q)=\frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_j)|}{\varphi(j)} .
\]
Let \(C_D(Q)\) be the [least possible maximum coefficient one-norm](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v228.md#resultant) of a family of integral polynomial multiples of raw degrees at most \(D\) with monic gcd \(Q\), and use \(D=\infty\) for unrestricted degrees.
Uniformly over (1) and the whole prime window,
\[
 \begin{split}
  J(Q)&\ll e,\\
  ph=m(Q)&\le\sup_{j\ge1}A_j(Q)
     \le\log C_\infty(Q)\le\log C_{2d}(Q)
     \le\log\|Q\|_1\le ph+e\log2 .
 \end{split}                                                          \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(J(Q)=o(ph)\), and every quantity between \(ph\) and \(ph+e\log2\) in (2) is \(ph(1+o(1))\) as \(d\to\infty\).

For each of the two signs \(\sigma=\pm1\) there are two primitive coprime-row diagonals
\[
 F_i(x)=A_i(x^p)+\sigma x A_i^*(x^p),\qquad
 \deg A_i=4d,\quad A_i(0)\ne0,\quad A_i^*(X)=X^{4d}A_i(1/X),
 \quad \gcd(A_i,A_i^*)=1,\qquad i=1,2.
\]
Each has span \(4pd+1\) and is divisible by \(P^2\).
For the noncyclotomic part \(R_i\) of \(F_i/P^2\), counted with multiplicities, one has
\[
                   h/3\ \le\ m(R_i)/p\ \ll h.                         \tag{3}
\]
Both rows have a nonzero value at \(\gamma^p\), a nonzero individual derivative remainder and nonproportional reduced traces, and their two value and Euler derivative vectors have nonzero determinant.
All the normalized norm and trace costs in the [simultaneous two-row estimates](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v227.md#rows) are \(O(ph)\) on this same pair.
The constants are absolute.

<a id="powers"></a>
## Irreducible substitutions before and after powering

Fix a root \(\alpha\) of \(P_0\), with \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The [irreducible power-substitution lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) gives irreducibility of \(P_0(X^n)\) for all sufficiently large primes \(n\) and preservation of \(m(P_0)\).
Indeed only finitely many primes \(n\) have \(\alpha\in K^n\), by bounded degree and coefficient bounds for possible unit roots in \(K\).
A proper factor of \(X^n-\alpha\) over \(K\) would supply a product \(v\in K\) of \(t\) roots with \(0<t<n,\ v^n=\alpha^t\), giving \(\alpha\in K^n\) by Bézout.
The degree tower gives degree \(ne\), and taking all \(n\)-th roots of every conjugate preserves the sum of the positive logarithmic moduli.
The substituted polynomial remains monic and reciprocal with constant coefficient one.
Taking arbitrarily large such \(n\) eventually gives (1), as in the [degree enlargement for exact-order criteria](Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#transfer).

For \(p>e(e-1)+1\) the powers of distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\) remain distinct.
Otherwise their quotient has order \(p\), so its \(p-1\) conjugates lie among the quotients of the \(e(e-1)\) ordered pairs of distinct roots of \(P_0\).
Consequently \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)\).
For any irreducible \(P_0(X^n)\), any root \(\gamma\) with \(\gamma^n=\alpha\), and any such \(p\) with \(\gcd(p,n)=1\), the field \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^p)\) therefore contains \(\alpha\) because it contains \((\gamma^p)^n=\alpha^p\).
From \(\gamma^n=\alpha\) and \(\gamma^p\) it contains \(\gamma\) by Bézout, so powering preserves the full degree.
If \(P_{0,p}\) is the degree-\(e\) minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^p\), this proves the exact identity
\[
                    Q(X)=P_{0,p}(X^n),\qquad
                    m(Q)=m(P_{0,p})=ph.                                \tag{4}
\]
The monic polynomial on the right vanishes at \(\gamma^p\) and has the full degree \(ne\).
In (1) all primes of the stated window satisfy \(p>e(e-1)+1\) and \(p<n=d/e\) for sufficiently large \(d\), uniformly in \(e\le\log(3d)\).

An arbitrarily high tower at a fixed odd prime is also possible for each seed.
Choose one odd \(\ell\) with \(\alpha\notin K^\ell\).
If \(X^{\ell^k}-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\), the norm to \(K\) of any root \(\eta\) is \(\alpha\), since \(\ell^k\) is odd.
An \(\ell\)-th root of \(\eta\) in \(K(\eta)\) would then give an \(\ell\)-th root of \(\alpha\) by taking the norm.
The prime-degree binomial argument above proves the next irreducible step, as in the [norm induction for composite substitutions](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions).
Thus \(P_0(X^{\ell^k})\) has degree \(e\ell^k\) and measure \(h\) for every \(k\).
Equation (4) holds when \(p>e(e-1)+1\) and \(p\ne\ell\), in particular at every window prime for large \(k\) at fixed seed and \(\ell\).

<a id="angular"></a>
## Angular variation, coefficient sums and all cyclotomic resultants

For a monic degree-\(e\) polynomial \(G\), the centered circle logarithm has \(L^1\) norm \(O(e)\), uniformly in the roots.
In fact each root contributes a centered term of the form \(\log|1-z e^{-it}|\) with \(|z|\le1\), after reflecting an exterior root.
Its squared \(L^2\) norm is at most \(\tfrac12\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}\) by the [single-root Fourier expansion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#energy).
Summation and invariance of \(\mu\) under \(X\mapsto X^n\) give \(J(Q)=J(P_{0,p})\ll e\).
The same substitution preserves the coefficient sum in absolute value, and
\[
 \|Q\|_1=\|P_{0,p}\|_1\le\prod_{\rho:P_{0,p}(\rho)=0}(1+|\rho|)
                           \le 2^e\exp(ph).
\]
The singleton \(\{Q\}\) is allowed in degree \(2d\).

In fact the exact cost of a dilation satisfies \(C_D(G(X^n))=C_{\lfloor D/n\rfloor}(G)\) for every monic nonconstant integral \(G\), every \(n\ge1\) and \(D\ge n\deg G\).
For the upper bound substitute \(X^n\) in an optimal family for \(G\), preserving the gcd by a Bézout identity for its quotients.
Conversely write each member \(H_i\) of any eligible family for \(G(X^n)\) as \(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}X^rH_{ir}(X^n)\).
Decomposing its quotient by \(G(X^n)\) in the same way shows that every \(H_{ir}\) is divisible by \(G\).
If the nonzero \(H_{ir}\) had a larger common monic divisor \(GW\), then \(G(X^n)W(X^n)\) would divide every \(H_i\), contradicting the original gcd.
The nonzero \(H_{ir}\) have degrees at most \(\lfloor D/n\rfloor\) and norms at most \(\|H_i\|_1\), giving the reverse bound.
Thus (4) preserves the optimal coefficient budget at any one fixed prime when the degree cap scales by \(n\).
The dilation permits larger primes through its higher degree.

For any monic polynomial \(H\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with no root of unity we have \(m(H)\le\sup_j A_j(H)\).
To see this, take positive integers \(t_k\to\infty\) for which all unit-circle roots \(z\) of \(H\) satisfy \(z^{t_k}\to1\).
Such a sequence exists by taking widely separated differences along a convergent subsequence of the simultaneous powers in their compact torus.
If there are no such roots take any \(t_k\to\infty\).
With \(M_k=t_k+1\) the circle roots have \(z^{M_k}\to z\ne1\).
For each root \(\rho\) inside or outside the circle, \(\log|\rho^{M_k}-1|/M_k\to\log^+|\rho|\).
The same limit is zero at the indicated circle roots.
Consequently
\[
 m(H)=\lim_k \frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(H,X^{M_k}-1)|}{M_k}
     =\lim_k\sum_{j\mid M_k}\frac{\varphi(j)}{M_k}A_j(H).
\]
Each weighted average is at most the stated supremum.
The [cyclotomic resultant lower bound on the coefficient norm](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v228.md#resultant) gives the remaining inequality \(\sup_j A_j(H)\le\log C_\infty(H)\), since for each \(\Phi_j\) some member of every gcd family is coprime to \(\Phi_j\).
This proves (2), including all cyclotomic orders at once.

<a id="equivalence"></a>
## The two-row criterion and the unrestricted shrinking question

For sufficiently large \(d\), [Voutier's lower bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\).
Thus in the window \(ph/\log(3d)\gg(\log\log d)^3\), uniformly, and \(e+\log(2dp)=o(ph)\).
This proves the relative bounds following (2).
The [small integral multiple construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v227.md#rows) at \(Q\) now gives both rows with \(m(R_i)\ll ph+\log(2dp)+\log\|Q\|_1\ll ph\).
It gives, on both final rows, reduced Chebyshev coefficient logarithms and squarefree first-trace lift measures \(O(ph)\).
It also bounds by \(O(ph)\) the logarithm of the absolute norm of each nonzero individual derivative remainder divided by \(d/2\), and the logarithms of the absolute norms of the nonzero values and the joint determinant divided by \(d\).
Alternatively the [angular perturbation bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#rows) gives the same cofactor upper bound using \(J(Q)\ll e\).
The two occupied classes \(0,1\bmod p\), span \(4pd+1\) and coprimality put each final diagonal within the [two-class cofactor comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement), giving its lower bound \(m(R_i)/p\ge h/3\).

If integral polynomials have positive measures tending to zero, they give a sequence of monic irreducible nontorsion reciprocal units of positive measures \(h_j\to0\).
Indeed integer content or a leading coefficient of absolute value at least two costs at least \(\log2\), as does a nonunit constant coefficient of a monic factor after deleting powers of \(X\).
Factorization gives a positive irreducible measure bounded by the whole measure, and [Smyth's nonreciprocal theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives a fixed positive lower bound for every nonreciprocal nontorsion unit.
Choose for each reciprocal seed a representative (1) with \(d_j\to\infty\).
Its measure is exactly \(h_j\), while (2) gives \(J(Q)/p\to0,\ (\log C_{2d_j}(Q))/p\to0\) and \((\sup_k A_k(Q))/p\to0\) uniformly at **all** its window primes.
At each prime and either sign both cofactor measures and every normalized norm and trace cost above are \(o(p)\).
Conversely, a sequence of representatives with \(d_j\to\infty\), a window prime for each representative and a pair of the stated diagonals for which \(\max_i m(R_i)/p\to0\) gives positive measures \(h_j\to0\) by the lower bound in (3).

Equivalently, there is an unrestricted positive Lehmer gap if and only if there is \(c>0\) such that for every sufficiently large representative (1) some window prime gives \(\max_i m(R_i)/p\ge c\) for every pair of diagonals of the indicated shape and positive sign satisfying the coprimality, double-root and nonvanishing conditions.
The forward implication follows from the same lower bound for each eligible diagonal, and the reverse implication uses the simultaneous upper bound at every window prime on representatives of each hypothetical shrinking sequence.
This equivalence leaves the positive lower bound open.
It preserves the measures of the original irreducible seeds and changes their degrees and powered minimal polynomials, so a bound on the angular variation or on the coefficients at the original degrees is a separate question.
