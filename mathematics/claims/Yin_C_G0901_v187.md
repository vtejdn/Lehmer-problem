# An integral trace-pole family with one additional pair of nonreal exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## How much of the root signature forces a dyadic collision?

Write \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ M=pq,\ h=1/M,\ K=(2q+1)^2=4M+1\).
For every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four there are arbitrarily large \(d=2D\) and monic reciprocal irreducible \(F\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) of degree \(d\) with
\[
 q^d F(s)=K,\qquad
 \frac{c}{D p^d}\le s-T\le\frac{C q^2}{D p^d}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The constants are absolute and \(T\in(1,s)\) is the only **real** root outside the unit circle.
There is one pair of nonreal complex conjugates outside the circle, their two inverses inside it, and \(D-3\) conjugate pairs on the circle.
All \(d\) dyadic roots have distinct residues and form a single unramified orbit.
Both \(T\) and \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\) are algebraic units.
The Mahler measure of \(F\) is comparable to \(MD\), even though \(\log(1/(s-T))=d\log p+O(\log D+\log(2q))\).
The extra nonreal pair accounts for this cost.
There is also an explicit degree-eight integral family for every \(q\ge2\) with the same root signature and pairwise distinct dyadic residues, for which \(p^8(s-T)/q\to2/5\).

<a id="round"></a>
## Rounding on the exact pole and controlling the real intervals

Use the [pole slice and real polynomial](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#real) with \(J=q/2+1,\ L=2D-2J-1\) and its [binary irreducible integral representative](Yin_C_G0901_v184.md#inert).
In the admissible unbounded degrees take \(D\) sufficiently large in terms of \(q\), in particular \(D\ge 10q\).
Write \(F_*=Z^D H_*(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\) and denote the integral representative's trace polynomial by \(H_1\).
The reciprocal coefficients of \(F_*\) strictly below the leading term begin with \(J\) zeros, shared with the representative.
Moreover \(\|F_*\|_1\le4+L\le 2D+4\) by the displayed formula \(F_*=(Z^L-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-tZ^{J+1}(1+\cdots+Z^{L-1})\), \(0<t<1\).
Both traces have \(M^D H(h)=K\).
Thus \(H_*-H_1=(MX-1)G_*\), with real \(G_*\) of degree at most \(D-J-2\), by the [triangular trace change](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place).

Put \(\phi_j(X)=Z^j+Z^{-j}\) when \(X=Z+Z^{-1}-2\), \(j\ge1\).
Together with \(1\) these monic integral polynomials form a basis in each degree by triangularity.
Round the coordinates of \(G_*\) in this basis to the nearest even integers to form \(G_0\).
Then \(E=G_0-G_*\) has basis coefficients of absolute value at most one.
Its Laurent pullback has coefficient one-norm at most \(2D\), and \(|E(x)|\le2D\) throughout \([-4,0]\).
Take \(m=D-3,\ A=16D\) and form
\[
 H=H_1+(MX-1)(G_0+A\phi_m)=H_*+(MX-1)(E+A\phi_m),\qquad
 F=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2).
\]
The increment at \(H_1\) has even coefficients and vanishes at \(h\).
Consequently \(F\) is integral, monic, reciprocal, has the exact pole, and reduces to the prescribed irreducible polynomial of degree \(2D\) modulo two.
Irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\), distinct residues and the single unramified orbit follow.

At \(x_j=2\cos(j\pi/m)-2,\ 0\le j\le m\), one has \(\phi_m(x_j)=2(-1)^j\).
On \([-4,0]\) one has \(|H_*|\le\|F_*\|_1\) and \(Mx-1\le-1\).
Thus \(H(x_j)\) has sign \((-1)^{j+1}\), and \(H\) has at least one real root in each of the \(m\) intervening intervals.
Since \(H(0)<0<H(h)\), it has a root \(w\in(0,h)\).
There must also be a pair of nonreal roots.
Indeed the zero first and second Newton sums of \(F_*\) give \(\sum_{H_*(x)=0}x^2=6D\): expand \((Z+Z^{-1}-2)^2\) and sum over reciprocal pairs.
The increment to \(H_*\) changes only coefficients of degree at most \(D-2\), adding \(MA\) to that of \(X^{D-2}\).
Newton's second identity therefore gives \(\sum_{H(x)=0}x^2=6D-2MA<0\).
These \(m+1\) real roots and the nonreal pair exhaust the degree and are simple.
Under the reciprocal lift the \(m\) intervals give pairs on the circle, \(w\) gives \(T,T^{-1}\), and the nonreal traces \(r,\bar r\) give the four nonreal roots off the circle.

<a id="pin"></a>
## Location and measure of the integral roots

Let \(R>1\) denote the common modulus of the nonreal exterior pair.
The Laurent coefficient norm of \(MX-1\) is \(4M+1\); hence
\[
 T R^2=M(F)\le\|F\|_1\le 2D+4+(4M+1)(2D+2A).
\]
The inequality for Mahler measure follows by averaging \(\log|F|\) on the unit circle.
Conversely \(2\Re(r^2)\le 6D-2MA\) because all other trace roots are real.
It follows that \(|r|^2\ge MA-3D\) and \(R\ge |r+2|-1\), giving \(R^2\asymp MD\), \(|h-r|^2\asymp MD\) and the asserted measure estimate.
The latter upper bound uses \(|r|\le R+R^{-1}+2\).

Label the neutral traces \(y_j\in(x_j,x_{j-1})\), \(1\le j\le m\).
The monic polynomial on the \(m-1\) interior nodes is \(U_{m-1}(1+X/2)\), where \(U\) denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
Its value at \(h\) is \((s^m-s^{-m})/(s-s^{-1})\asymp q s^m\), since \(h=s+s^{-1}-2\) and \(D\ge10q\).
Interlacing therefore gives
\[
 h\, U_{m-1}(1+h/2)\le\prod_{j=1}^m(h-y_j)\le(h+4)\,U_{m-1}(1+h/2).
\]
In \(M^D H(h)=K\) isolate \(h-w\) and use \(|h-r|^2\asymp MD\) and \(K\asymp M\).
The bounds on the product range from \(c s^m/q\) to \(C q s^m\).
Since \(M^D s^m=p^d s^{-3}\) and \(h-w=(s-T)(1-1/(sT))\) with \(1-1/(sT)\asymp1/q\), this proves (1).

For both this construction and the degree-eight family below, put \(\delta=1-Mw\).
The norm of \(\delta\) in the trace field is \(M^D H(h)=K\), so \(K/\delta\) is integral.
The identity \(Y+Y^{-1}-2=Kw/\delta\) shows that \(Y\) and its inverse are integral, as is already the case for \(T,T^{-1}\).
In the unbounded-degree construction these units are multiplicatively independent.
At the positive real embedding \(Y>T>1\).
At a nonreal exterior conjugate \(z\) of modulus \(R\), the image \(Y(z)\) satisfies
\[
 1<|Y(z)|\le\frac{sR+1}{R-s}<R
\]
for all sufficiently large \(D\).
The lower bound follows from \(|pz-q|^2-|p-qz|^2=(p^2-q^2)(|z|^2-1)\), and the upper bound from \(R\gg\sqrt{MD}\).
A common multiplicative relation would give the same ratio of the two logarithmic absolute values at both embeddings, which these inequalities exclude.

## An explicit example in fixed degree with the exact leading pinning constant

For every \(q\ge2\), take
\[
 H(X)=X^3(X+4)+5(MX-1)(X+1),\qquad F(Z)=Z^4 H(Z+Z^{-1}-2).
\]
The pole is exact since \(H(h)=K/M^4\).
There is a unique positive root by the coefficient signs, and \(H(0)<0<H(h)\) places it in \((0,h)\).
Both terms of \(H\) are nonpositive on \([-1,0]\) and cannot vanish together.
They are nonnegative below \(-4\) with the second strictly positive there.
On \([-4,-2]\) the magnitude of the first term is at most \(27\) and the second is at least \(5(2M+1)>27\).
On \([-2,-1]\), \(4X^2(X+3)\le16\) and \(5M(2X+1)-5\le-5M-5\), so \(H\) decreases strictly from \(10M-11\) to \(-3\).
This accounts for one positive trace, one neutral trace and two nonreal traces.
Modulo two \(H=X^4+X+1\) has no linear factor and is prime to \(X^2+X+1\), proving its irreducibility.
The negative real embedding sends the discriminant \(w(w+4)\) to a negative number, proving the trace lift is also irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The binary trace roots are simple and nonzero, and \(Z^2+xZ+1\) has two different roots for each one, giving distinct residues for \(F\).
Finally
\[
 h-w=\frac{w^3(w+4)}{5M(w+1)}.
\]
Since \(0<w<h\), this first gives \(h-w=O(M^{-4})\) and then \(M^4(h-w)\to4/5\).
Thus \(w/h\to1\) and \(q(T-1)\to1\) by \(w=(T-1)^2/T\).
It follows that \(q(1-1/(sT))\to2\), proving the stated \(2/5\) limit.
