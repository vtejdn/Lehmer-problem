### 2. The exact complete-constraint midpoint matrix

Set N=2n, and reverse the chronological digits by xi_j=eta_(N-1-j).
Let V_N be the4-by-N integer matrix of power-basis columns of1,beta,...,beta^(N-1).
The event of return is V_N xi=0.
For N>=4 it has row rank four.
Define Pi_N to be the orthogonal projection in R^N onto ker V_N; for N<4 this kernel is zero.
Thus for N>=4,

    Pi_N=I-V_N^T(V_N V_N^T)^(-1)V_N.                      (2)

Write u_j=(Re zeta^j,Im zeta^j)^T and let A_n be the2-by-N matrix with column u_j for0<=j<n and column zero for n<=j<N.
Under return, the high and low halves sum to zero at EVERY embedding, so

    Z_n=-zeta^(-n) sum_(j<n)xi_j zeta^j.

The minus rotation is orthogonal in R^2; denote its matrix by O_n.
The exact optimized midpoint proxy is

    Gamma_n=O_n A_n Pi_N A_n^T O_n^T.
    E_(2n) exp(v dot (Re Z_n,Im Z_n))
          <=exp(v^T Gamma_n v/2), v in R^2,
    Cov_(2n)(Re Z_n,Im Z_n)<=Gamma_n.                     (3)

Indeed L256 applies to the scalar coefficient vector A_n^T O_n^T v on the SAME full endpoint condition.
It minimizes its squared norm modulo the complete row space of V_N.
Simultaneous sign reversal makes the conditional mean zero, and comparison of second derivatives at zero gives the covariance inequality.
All laws are finite, so that derivative step is legitimate.
When N<=4, no nonzero word of degree<N is divisible by the degree-four minimal polynomial, and both the actual midpoint and Gamma_n are zero.
Thus the early endpoints cause no inverse or probability exception.

Equation(3) is an UPPER proxy, not a covariance identity.
It is unchanged by replacing the integer power-basis rows with their invertible full real embedding matrix.
Accordingly the same projection is onto the common kernel of the four row vectors

No projected real equation replaces actual integer return: the four equations are simply an invertible real expression of V_N xi=0.

### 3. Full proxy asymptotics with all nonneutral costs

The following proof applies to any fixed real beta>1 with a reciprocal degree-four minimal polynomial and one genuine complex unit-circle pair, including the later control.
Define

    a=1/|1-zeta^2|, s=1/(1-beta^(-1)),
    C_l=sum_(j<l)u_j u_j^T.

The finite geometric sum gives

    C_l=(l/2)I+E_l, ||E_l||<=a.                           (5)

For example the traceless part is one half of the real matrix associated with sum_(j<l)zeta^(2j), whose modulus is at most2/|1-zeta^2|.
This is a fixed phase cost, not an equidistribution assumption or a degree-uniform constant.

There is an especially simple ALL-n upper bound.
The full endpoint row space contains the two neutral rows.
Subtracting one half of the complete neutral row from the prefix observable leaves coefficients +u_j/2 on its first half and-u_j/2 on its second half.
Its Gram matrix is C_(2n)/4.
The optimum in(3) is no larger, hence

    0<=Gamma_n<=s_n^2 I,
    s_n^2=(n+a)/4, for EVERY n>=1.                       (6)

This already includes both real constraints, because optimizing over more constraints can only reduce the residual norm.
No assertion that they are irrelevant is made.

For a matching asymptotic of the PROXY, first optimize only over the neutral rows R_N=(u_0,...,u_(N-1)).
For n>a, C_(2n) is positive definite.
The residual matrix before O_n is

    G_n^0=C_n-C_n C_(2n)^(-1)C_n
         =C_(2n)/4-Delta_n C_(2n)^(-1)Delta_n,
    Delta_n=C_n-C_(2n)/2.                                    (7)

Here Delta_n is a2-by-2 matrix, distinct from the scalar midpoint overlap of L329.
Since ||Delta_n||<=3a/2 and C_(2n)>=(n-a)I, for n>=2a,

    (n/4-5a/2)I <=G_n^0<=(n/4+a/4)I.                    (8)

The identity in(7) is valid for noncommuting symmetric matrices: expand the square with the middle inverse.
The lower error is at most a/4+(9a^2/4)/(n-a)<=5a/2.

Now include the expanding and contracting equations.
Normalize their two rows as

    h_j=beta^(j-(N-1)), k_j=beta^(-j),
    B_N=(h_j;k_j)_(j=0)^(N-1),
    e_N=N beta^(-(N-1)).

Each row has l1 norm at most s and squared norm at least1.
Their cross inner product is exactly e_N.
Also each column of R_N B_N^T has Euclidean norm at most s, so ||R_N B_N^T||<=sqrt(2)s.
The residual real-row Gram after eliminating the neutral rows is

    T_N=B_N B_N^T-B_N R_N^T C_(2n)^(-1)R_N B_N^T
          >=[1-e_N-2s^2/(n-a)]I.

Choose a completely specified fixed-input onset n0 so that, for all n>=n0,

    n>=2, n>=2a, n-a>=8s^2, e_(2n)<=1/4.                 (9)

Thus T_N>=I/2 and ||T_N^(-1)||<=2.

Let

    W_N=A_n B_N^T-C_n C_(2n)^(-1)R_N B_N^T.

Here ||A_n B_N^T||<=sqrt(2)s and, when n>=2a, ||C_n C_(2n)^(-1)||<=(n/2+a)/(n-a)<=2.

This follows either from the orthogonal decomposition of the row space or by direct completion of the least-squares quadratic form; T_N is positive here.

### 4. Complete exponential moments and a necessary subdiffusive condition

The all-n bound(6) has consequences for the actual original returned word.

    E_(2n) exp(t rho_n^2) <=(1-2t s_n^2)^(-1).

To prove the first estimate, take a standard two-dimensional Gaussian vector g independent of the finite word.

No Gaussian model for the actual bridge has been assumed.
The second estimate also follows directly by summing the two covariance bounds in(3); differentiating the first estimate at zero gives the same result.

For reference, coordinate Chernoff bounds give the fully explicit tail and fourth-moment cost

    P_(2n){rho_n^2>=u}<=min{1,4exp[-u/(n+a)]}, u>0,
    M_4(n)<=8(n+a)^2.                                     (12)

The tail follows because at least one coordinate has squared magnitude at least u/2.

**Theorem 1 (a new necessary moment condition for localization).** At the actual primitive quartic, if mu<infinity, then for every fixed p>0 and every0<=lambda<2,

    M_p(n)/n^(p/2) ->0,
    E_(2n) exp(lambda rho_n^2/n) ->1.                      (13)

*Proof.* L329 supplies total-variation convergence of b_n to its actual probability pi, hence tightness.
For each epsilon>0 choose a finite F capturing all but epsilon of pi and, for sufficiently large n, all but2epsilon of b_n.
The neutral radius is bounded on F.
Consequently rho_n^2/n tends to zero in probability.
This step alone would NOT justify passage to moments.

For every fixed 0<lambda'<2, (11) with t=lambda'/n gives, for all sufficiently large n,

    E_(2n) exp(lambda' rho_n^2/n)
       <=[1-(lambda'/2)(1+a/n)]^(-1),                    (14)

whose right side is bounded.
If0<lambda<lambda'<2, the larger exponential gives uniform integrability of exp(lambda rho_n^2/n): on a value above M its expectation is at most a fixed constant times M^(-(lambda'/lambda-1)).
Convergence in probability to1 therefore gives the second limit in(13).
The case lambda=0 is exact.

These statements require no integrability of the limiting pi's unscaled neutral moments: pi may have an infinite pth moment, while(13) still holds.

The converse to(13) is not asserted.
Unbounded neutral escape on a scale smaller than sqrt(n) is compatible with all its limits.
