# Cyclotomic multiplicities on a diagonal in coordinates of the support lattice

<a id="statement"></a>
## An area estimate without assumptions on the noncyclotomic part

Let \(H\) be an irreducible rational Laurent polynomial in \(X,Y\) whose support differences generate a rank-two lattice \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\).
Write \(I=[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda]\) and \(V=\operatorname{area}(\operatorname{Newt}H)\) in the original coordinates.
For a primitive integral row \(w=(p,b)\) let \(g>0\) generate the subgroup \(w\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z\).
For any nonzero Laurent polynomial in one variable let \(c\) be its number of roots of unity counted with multiplicities, ignoring monomial factors.
Then the specialization \(J=H(x^p,x^b)\) is nonzero and satisfies
\[
                     c(J)\le 10\,\frac gI\,V,\qquad g\mid I.          \tag{1}
\]
The estimate allows several noncyclotomic factors in \(J\), or none.
It controls the cyclotomic factors of a rank-two curve on one fixed one-dimensional subtorus.

For a rational Laurent polynomial \(F\), remove its irreducible factors that are cyclotomic in a Laurent monomial, and denote the remainder by \(F_0\), including remaining multiplicities.
Suppose \(p>0\), \(\gcd(p,b)=1\), and the difference \(h\) of the extreme \(Y\)-exponents of \(F\) is less than \(p\).
If \(d_0\) is the exponent span of \(F_0(x^p,x^b)\), then
\[
                   c(F_0(x^p,x^b))\le 10d_0h/p.                      \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for \(10h<p\) an irreducible factor of \(F\) with positive fast width and a purely cyclotomic diagonal must itself be cyclotomic in a Laurent monomial.

<a id="coordinates"></a>
## What the parametrization does in the support lattice

After shifting one support point to zero, choose an integer basis \(v_1,v_2\) of \(\Lambda\).
In the corresponding monomials \(U,V_1\) write \(H=G(U,V_1)\), up to a Laurent unit.
The support differences of \(G\) generate \(\mathbb Z^2\), its Newton area is \(V/I\), and \(G\) remains irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed the monomial change is injective, preserves the number of terms, and takes a nonunit over \(\mathbb Q\) to a nonunit.
This is the [exponent-lattice reduction of Beukers and Smyth](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#support-lattice).

Put \(m_i=wv_i\), so \(\gcd(m_1,m_2)=g\), and \(a_i=m_i/g\).
On the diagonal, the parameter of the primitive curve \((t^{a_1},t^{a_2})\) is \(t=x^g\).
If \(J_1(t)=G(t^{a_1},t^{a_2})\), every torsion root of \(J_1\) has exactly \(g\) preimages under \(x\mapsto x^g\), all torsion and with the original multiplicity.
Non-torsion roots have no torsion preimages.
Thus \(c(J)=g c(J_1)\) when \(J_1\ne0\).
Since \(I\mathbb Z^2\subseteq\Lambda\) and \(w\mathbb Z^2=\mathbb Z\), one has \(g\mid I\).
The kernel of substitution on the Laurent ring for the primitive row \((a_1,a_2)\) is generated by a single primitive-character binomial, as is seen by completing that row to a unimodular matrix.
The irreducible full-rank \(G\) cannot be divisible by this binomial, proving nonvanishing.

<a id="intersections"></a>
## Three intersections for any irreducible full-lattice polynomial

For each \(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}\) the sign translate \(G(\epsilon_1 U,\epsilon_2 V_1)\) has the same support as \(G\).
Two distinct sign translates cannot be associates.
A monomial translation of the finite support onto itself must be zero, and the ratio of the two signs would otherwise be a nontrivial character constant on the support and trivial on all its differences.
The latter generate \(\mathbb Z^2\).

None of the four translates divides \(G(\pm U^2,\pm V_1^2)\).
If one did, invariance of the latter under both sign changes would force all four distinct irreducible translates to divide it.
For any direction in which \(G\) has width \(W>0\), their product has width \(4W\), while the target has width \(2W\).
This is the [full-lattice sign argument](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#support-lattice).
It gives proper intersections over \(\mathbb C\) as well, because a common geometric component of two rational polynomials gives a nonunit rational gcd, as in the [diagonal intersection proof](Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#torsion).

Use just the three transforms on the parameter \(t\) given by \(-t,t^2,-t^2\).
The first induces the sign vector \(((-1)^{a_1},(-1)^{a_2})\), which is nontrivial since the two \(a_i\) are relatively prime.
Both squaring transforms give instances of the proper intersections just established.
For a root of unity of order divisible by four, odd, or twice an odd integer, respectively, the indicated transform is a rational Galois conjugate of the root.
A zero of order \(e\) of \(J_1\) therefore contributes local intersection number at least \(e\) for the corresponding transform, by restricting the intersection to the primitive parameter curve as in the [local length argument](Yin_C_G0345_v349.md#torsion).
Distinct parameter values give distinct points.
The [intersection bound with multiplicities](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#intersection-multiplicities) gives totals at most \(2V/I,4V/I,4V/I\) in the three order classes of \(J_1\).
Summing and lifting through \(x^g\) proves (1).

<a id="strip"></a>
## Separating the cyclotomic factors of a product in a strip

Write \(F_0\) up to a Laurent unit as the product of rational irreducibles \(H_j\) with multiplicities \(e_j\).
Let \(s_j,d_j\) denote their fast widths and their diagonal spans.
Since widths add and \(s_j\le h<p\), all exponents of each \(H_j\) remain distinct on the diagonal.
Indeed equality of diagonal exponents forces equality first of their fast indices modulo \(p\), then of both coordinates.
Thus a nonunit rational factor still has positive diagonal span.
For a rank-one factor \(H_j=f(X^uY^v)\) up to a monomial, \(f\) is irreducible and noncyclotomic, and \(pu+bv\ne0\) by injectivity.
Its diagonal has no torsion roots.
For each rank-two factor the map on exponent vectors \((i,j)\mapsto(pi+bj,j)\) has determinant \(p\) and puts its Newton polygon inside a \(d_j\)-by-\(s_j\) rectangle.
Using (1) and \(g_j/I_j\le1\) gives \(c(H_j(x^p,x^b))\le10d_js_j/p\).
After summing with the multiplicities, \(s_j\le h\) and \(\sum e_jd_j=d_0\) prove (2).
In the purely cyclotomic case for a single irreducible positive-width factor, (2) with \(10h<p\) excludes all possibilities apart from an original Laurent-monomial cyclotomic.

Consequently the [factor isolation and exact neutral-root loss](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#statement) hold under \(10h<p\) in place of \(24h<p\), with \(d\le D/(1-10s/p)\) for the factor of width \(s\) carrying the original degree \(D\).
The proof of uniqueness uses only narrow injectivity and the single noncyclotomic factor on the diagonal, and the new estimate rules out each remaining nontoric factor at its own width.
The gap and loss conclusions then follow from the same factorization and grid identities.
Likewise the [finite multiplier test](Yin_C_G0345_v348.md#carrier) works for \(10H<p\), with \(d_{\max}=\lfloor Dp/(p-10H)\rfloor\) for a chosen width cap \(H\).
This widens the decision range for individual inputs and leaves the existence of such representations along a shrinking sequence open.

<a id="example"></a>
## Why the induced power matters

For \(m\ge2\) let \(H_m=1+X^m+Y\), which is primitive and irreducible by linearity in \(Y\).
Here \(I=m\) and \(V=m/2\).
On the primitive direction \((p,1)\) with \(p>0\) we have \(g=1\), so (1) gives a uniform bound of five.
In fact \(1+x^{pm}+x\) has a torsion root only when \(x\) is a primitive cube root and \(pm\equiv2\bmod3\), by \(|1+x|=1\).
Each such zero is simple because \(pm>1\).
On \((1,2m)\), however, the induced power is \(g=m\) and \(J=1+x^m+x^{2m}\) has exactly \(2m\) torsion roots.
Thus the area of the normalized lattice alone does not bound the cyclotomic degree on the original parameter.
