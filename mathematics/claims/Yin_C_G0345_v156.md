# A uniform Mahler gap for integral Laurent polynomials monic in a bounded-degree variable

<a id="statement"></a>
## The trace quadratic and the general partial-degree bound

Write \(m(F)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure over all variables of a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
Set
\[
             C_*=2\exp(16384),\qquad c_R=\frac1{C_*(\log(3R))^{48}}\quad(R\ge1).
\]
Every symmetric integral Laurent polynomial \(J(X)\) satisfies
\[
          m(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X))=0
             \quad\text{or}\quad m(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X))\ge c_2.                    \tag{1}
\]
The zero cases are the integer constants of absolute value at most two and \(J=\pm(X^s+X^{-s})\), \(s\ge1\).
The lower bound is independent of the span, the number and sizes of the coefficients, and their signs.

More generally let \(F\) have integral coefficients, any finite number of base variables \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_h)\), and width \(R\ge1\) in \(Y\), the difference of its largest and smallest \(Y\)-exponents.
After a Laurent monomial normalization suppose the coefficient of \(Y^R\) is one.
Then \(m(F)=0\) or \(m(F)\ge c_R\).
The same lower bound applies without monicity if the zero set in the algebraic torus has a \(Y\)-dependent geometric irreducible component which is not a translate of a subtorus by a torsion point.
When every \(Y\)-dependent geometric component is such a translate, the entire measure comes from an integral Laurent factor in the base variables alone.
Thus an unrestricted base-only factor retains its original measure in this last case.

For the monic separable central polynomials
\[
          P(z)=z^{2n}+1+z^n J(z),\quad d=2n,\quad 1\le s<n/3
\]
with \(J\) symmetric integral of span \(s=\max\{|j|:[X^j]J\ne0\}\), there are absolute \(c,\lambda>0\) such that, for sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
       P\ \text{noncyclotomic},\qquad
       s\le c\,\frac{d^{1/3}}{(\log(2d))^{2/3}}
                         \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log M(P)\ge\lambda .      \tag{2}
\]
This applies to every exterior signature.
In particular every shrinking sequence of these inputs must have \(s>c\,d^{1/3}/(\log(2d))^{2/3}\) eventually.
The coordinator proposed the trace-quadratic gap question following the [central measure comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#statement).
The contributor supplied the torsion specialization, relative height argument and its partial-degree extension.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Torsion specialization directly in the trace quadratic

The [relative height product of Delsinne](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) in dimension one gives, for every nonzero algebraic \(\beta\) which is not a root of unity,
\[
       h(\beta)>
           \frac1{C_*\,D_{\rm ab}(\log(3D_{\rm ab}))^{48}},
       \qquad D_{\rm ab}=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\beta):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}].       \tag{3}
\]
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The source has \(A_1=2(1+1)^2(1+1)!=16\), logarithmic exponent \(3A_1=48\), and admissible constant \(2\exp(64A_1^2)=C_*\).
Its independence condition on one coordinate holds exactly off torsion in the multiplicative group.

For real \(t\) let \(g(t)=\operatorname{arcosh}(|t|/2)\) if \(|t|>2\) and zero otherwise.
The two roots of \(Y^2+tY+1\) have opposite logarithmic moduli with absolute value \(g(t)\).
Jensen's formula gives \(m(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X))=\int g(J(e^{i\theta}))\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
When this is positive there is an open arc with \(|J|>2\).
For each sufficiently large prime \(p\) the primitive \(p\)-th roots meet the arc.
Fix any primitive \(\zeta_p\), and let \(\beta\) be either root of \(Y^2+J(\zeta_p)Y+1\).
It is an algebraic integer unit and \(D_{\rm ab}\le2\).
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) extends to the same number of embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p,\beta)\).
At any embedding above the indicated arc either choice of the root lies off the circle, so \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
For a unit the mean absolute archimedean logarithm is twice its height by the product formula.
It follows exactly, including when the quadratic already splits over the cyclotomic field, that
\[
           \frac1{p-1}\sum_{u=1}^{p-1}g(J(\zeta_p^u))=2h(\beta)>c_2 .
\]
For this fixed \(J\) the means converge to its circle integral by continuity, proving (1).
The order at which the grid meets the arc may depend on \(J\); the resulting lower bound does not.
The [zero-measure classification](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#quadratic) follows from \(|J|\le2\) on the circle and Parseval, with nonconstant equality at a coefficient norm of four excluded by continuity.

<a id="grid"></a>
## Summing the heights of the entire specialized fiber

First multiply by a power of \(Y\) to leave the occupied powers between zero and \(R\).
Write \(A_R(X)\), \(A_0(X)\) for its nonzero end coefficients.
For each grid take distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_h\) and let \(x=(\zeta_{p_1},\ldots,\zeta_{p_h})\).
Its rational Galois orbit is the product of the primitive grids by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Letting their smallest prime tend to infinity gives convergence to torus Haar measure on all continuous tests by ordinary rectangular Riemann sums.
Any fixed proper rational algebraic subset of the base torus misses these entire orbits eventually.
Indeed some rational nonzero Laurent polynomial vanishing on that subset does not vanish identically on the compact torus, by uniqueness of Fourier coefficients.
The grid then meets an open set on which this polynomial is nonzero, and rational Galois invariance rules out a zero anywhere on that orbit.
Thus one may require \(A_R(x)A_0(x)\ne0\) throughout.
For \(h=0\) use the one-point orbit.

At a base point of the orbit let \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_R\) be all roots of \(F(x,Y)\) with multiplicities, and let \(K\) be a finite Galois field containing them and the grid coordinates.
The mean of \(m_Y(F(x',Y))\) over the orbit points \(x'\) is at least \(\sum_i h(\beta_i)\).
To see this, average the archimedean Jensen identity
\(\log|\sigma A_R(x)|+\sum_i\log^+|\sigma\beta_i|\)
over the embeddings of \(K\).
At each nonarchimedean place \(v\), with usual local degree weight \(n_v\), all coefficients are integral, and the Gauss norm gives
\[
         |A_R(x)|_v\prod_i\max(1,|\beta_i|_v)
                       =\max_j|A_j(x)|_v\le1.
\]
The equality follows from multiplicativity of the polynomial coefficient maximum at a nonarchimedean place, by reducing normalized factors in its residue field.
The product formula turns the archimedean average of \(\log|\sigma A_R(x)|\) into \(-[K:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\sum_{v\nmid\infty}n_v\log|A_R(x)|_v\).
This pays for all finite-place terms of all the heights.
For monic \(F\) the equality of the archimedean mean and the sum of heights follows directly since the roots are integral.

If one root \(\beta\) is nontorsion, its full degree \(D_{\rm ab}\) orbit over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) consists of distinct roots of this same fiber because its coefficients are in \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
All these conjugates have height \(h(\beta)\).
In particular \(D_{\rm ab}\le R\), and the fiber mean is at least \(D_{\rm ab}h(\beta)>c_R\) by (3).
This multiplication by the whole relative orbit removes the linear degree factor from the denominator.

To pass a lower bound on the grid means to \(m(F)\), integrate the continuous function \(\log\max(|F(X,Y)|,e^{-N})\) on the \(Y\)-circle.
Its base grid mean dominates that of \(m_Y\), and tends to its full torus integral at each fixed \(N\).
Now let \(N\to\infty\) by monotone convergence after subtraction from a constant majorant.
The logarithmic integral of a nonzero Laurent polynomial is finite, also seen by induction from Jensen's lower bound \(\log|A_R(X)|\) on each nondegenerate fiber and boundedness of the positive part.
Thus \(m(F)\) is at least the limsup of the untruncated grid means; no lower equidistribution estimate for logarithmic singularities is needed.

For monic \(F\), the fiber measure is the nonnegative continuous sum of the positive logarithms of its root moduli, by continuity of the root multiset, taking \(\log^+|0|=0\).
If the full measure is positive, some open set of fibers has an exterior root, so every sufficiently fine orbit has at least one nontorsion root above it.
One can choose any point over the orbit and transport that root by an embedding.
The preceding estimate proves the monic theorem.

<a id="horizontal"></a>
## Nonmonic factors and base-only content

Suppose a geometric irreducible component \(Z\) of \(F=0\) depends on \(Y\) and is not a torsion translate of a subtorus.
It is a hypersurface of dimension \(h\), defined by an irreducible Laurent factor over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
The [finiteness of maximal torsion subvarieties](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) puts all torsion points of \(Z\) in finitely many proper translates contained in \(Z\).
Each has dimension at most \(h-1\), and the closure of its projection to the base also has dimension at most \(h-1\).
The union of these closures and their finitely many rational Galois conjugates is a proper rational algebraic subset.
Exclude it from the grids, together with the zeros of both end coefficients of a defining Laurent factor of \(Z\) and of their finitely many conjugates.
For \(h=0\) a nontorsion component is itself a nonzero nontorsion point, so the exclusion of projected torsion is empty.
Over every remaining grid point there is a root on \(Z\), since its specialized polynomial has positive degree and nonzero constant term.
This root cannot be zero or torsion, by the excluded projections and the fact that all the base coordinates are torsion.
Applying the full relative orbit and grid inequality proves the nonmonic gap.

If every \(Y\)-dependent component is a torsion translate, take the factorization in the integral Laurent polynomial ring with integer content extracted.
A codimension-one torsion translate has an equation \(X^{v}Y^b=\xi\) for a primitive integer character \((v,b)\) and a root of unity \(\xi\); for a \(Y\)-dependent component orient this character with \(b>0\).
Indeed the characters constant on a connected subtorus of codimension one form a saturated rank-one sublattice, and the value at a translating torsion point is torsion.
Over \(\mathbb Q\) the conjugates of this component range over the primitive roots of the order of \(\xi\), giving the single factor \(\Phi_{\operatorname{ord}\xi}(X^vY^b)\) up to a Laurent unit.
It is primitive integral and irreducible by changing Laurent lattice coordinates to make the primitive character one coordinate.
Collecting the remaining factors therefore expresses \(F\) as an integral Laurent polynomial in the base times these cyclotomic factors and a monomial, by Gauss's lemma.
Each of the displayed factors has measure zero by integration in \(Y\).
This gives the asserted reduction, including arbitrary multiplicities and integer content.

<a id="central"></a>
## The resulting obstruction on central blocks

For the stated central polynomials the [finite central comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#quadratic) gives, with \(L=\log M(P)\le1,\ D_d=\log(2d)/\sqrt d,\ Q=\log^+(\max_{\mathbb T}|J|/2)\),
\[
  |m(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X))-L|
       \ll s(1+Q)D_d+\sqrt{sL/\sqrt d}+sL/\sqrt d .
\]
The [peak bound](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#peak) gives \(Q\ll 1+\sqrt{sL}+s^2D_d\) when \(sD_d\) is sufficiently small, with absolute constants after fixing the measure bound one.
For \(s\le cD_d^{-2/3}\) and \(0<c\le1\), this condition holds for all sufficiently large \(d\), and substitution bounds the right side by
\[
                        O(c^{3/2}\sqrt L+c^3)+o(1).
\]
Here \(sD_d\le cD_d^{1/3}\to0\), and \(s/\sqrt d\to0\) uniformly on this range.
For a noncyclotomic \(P\) the [central zero-case argument](Yin_C_G0345_v154.md#quadratic) guarantees positive two-variable measure.
Choose \(c\) sufficiently small that the displayed bound, using \(L\le1\), is less than \(c_2/2\) at all sufficiently large degrees.
Then \(L\ge c_2/2\) by (1), proving (2); inputs with \(L>1\) already satisfy it.
