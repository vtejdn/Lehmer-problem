# A uniform positive Pisot excess for every six-term trace lift on branch two

<a id="statement"></a>
## The bound

Let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\), with positive constant \(q\ge2\) and branch index \(j=[z]\bigl(f/f^*\bigr)-q^2+1=2\).
Write \(C\) for its [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace), \(h=\deg C\), and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
There are absolute constants \(c>0,\ Q<\infty\) such that
\[
              \#\operatorname{supp}\Pi\le7
                   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
              \theta-q\ge c,\qquad q\le Q .
\]
The lift has even support because it is anti-reciprocal with zero midpoint, so the hypothesis allows at most six terms.
In particular it covers both parities and all trace numerators
\[
       C(x)=2U_h(x/2)+aU_{h-d}(x/2)+bU_{h-e}(x/2),
       \qquad 1\le d<e\le h,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb Z ,
\]
where \(U_n\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
The bounds are qualitative.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Two possible limits of the offsets

The [four-term theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v15.md#statement) covers zero coefficients and every smaller support.
On branch two the [derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(2=(\theta-q)(q+\theta^{-1}-H_q'(0))\) with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\).
It therefore suffices to exclude a six-term sequence with \(q\to\infty\).
Let \(B^\sharp\) be the actual monic reciprocal trace denominator and write \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\).
The [signed Herglotz measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) of \(J_i\) is symmetric with mass one and negative mass tending to zero, so its positive Taylor coefficients \(j_l\) are bounded doubled moments, with \(j_0=1\).
The [delay-one construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) gives \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, where \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure.

By the [six-term reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six), \(e=o(h)\).
Put \(g=\gcd(d,e)\) and extract one of the following two cases.

| Reduced upper offset | Scale \(D\) and vector \(\mathbf d\) | Lower polynomial \(A\), with \(\Pi/2=A(z^{\mathbf d})/2\) through \(h\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(e/g\to\infty\) | \(D=e,\ \mathbf d=(d,e)\) | \(A(x,y)=2+a x+b y,\quad a,b=\pm1\). |
| Fixed pair \(d/g=m,\ e/g=n\) | \(D=g,\ \mathbf d=(g)\) | \(A(w)=2+a w^m+b w^n,\quad \lvert a\rvert\le3,\ \lvert b\rvert=1\), with no open-disk zero. |

The coefficients can be fixed after extraction in both cases.
In the second case write \(A=EF\) over the integers with the irreducible factors normalized to positive constant term.
The unique factor \(F\) of constant two has multiplicity one.
Every other factor has unit leading coefficient, constant one and all roots on the circle by the product of their moduli, hence is cyclotomic up to sign.
If \(F\) had a circle root \(\zeta\), then \(\bar\zeta=\zeta^{-1}\) would also be a root and irreducibility would make \(F\) proportional to its reversal.
Reversing twice gives proportionality constant \(\pm1\), impossible for constant two and unit leading coefficient.
Thus \(F\) is strictly exterior and \(E\) contains every circle root, with multiplicities.

<a id="rows"></a>
## Integer Fourier coefficients and the continuous part of a mixed row

Take a limiting probability \(\mu\) of \(w=\bar v^{\mathbf d}\) on its one- or two-dimensional base torus.
In the two-variable case each fixed nonzero \(r\in\mathbb Z^2\) has \(r\cdot\mathbf d\ne0\) eventually, because the shortest nonzero exact relation has maximum coordinate at least \(e/g\).
Extract the eventual sign for every \(r\) and order the lattice by these signs, using the usual order in one variable.
With \(\widehat\mu(r)=\int w^{-r}\,d\mu\), put \(J(r)=2\widehat\mu(r)\) for \(r>0\), \(J(0)=1\) and \(J(r)=0\) for \(r<0\).
Then the finite convolution \(B=(A/2)J\) is integral, since its positive entries are limits of \([z^{r\cdot\mathbf d}]B^\sharp\) within the prefix.
The marginal cannot be Haar, since then \(B=A/2\) has a fractional coefficient.

For any positive integer scales \(t\) with \(D/t,t/h\to0\), let \(\Omega\) be any further joint limit of \((w,v^t)\), and call its last coordinate \(u\).
For \(k>0\), the projection \(\nu_k\) of \(A(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) onto the base has integer Fourier coefficients \(\lim[z^{kt+r\cdot\mathbf d}]B^\sharp\), since all convolution indices are strictly positive and \(o(h)\).
The finite positive measure \(\Lambda=|A|^2\Omega\) has integer coefficients on these layers by multiplication by \(A^*\), where the star is conjugate reversal.
Its zero layer has integer coefficients \(A^* B+A B^*\) by \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\), and conjugation gives the other layers.

Apply Sanders's [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) on the discrete lattices.
The inverse transform of a coset indicator is a character times Haar measure on the annihilator of the subgroup.
The resulting finite decomposition consists of trigonometric polynomial densities on torsion translates of connected subtori, after splitting disconnected subgroups.
The full-dimensional density of each \(\nu_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial \(H_k\), from the singleton cosets.
After combining terms on identical subtori, distinct component measures are mutually singular, so the densities of the positive measure \(\Lambda\) on these components are nonnegative.

A proper component projecting onto the full base has equation \(u^j=\zeta w^p\), with \(j>0\), \(p\) an integer vector and \(\zeta\) torsion.
Group these components by slope \(p/j\), and let \(f_s\ge0\) be the projected density of their mass in \(\Omega\) for each slope \(s\).
Division by \(|A|^2\) is valid over Haar-almost every base point, and these densities are integrable since \(\Omega\) is a probability.
The full-torus component of \(\Lambda\) has only finitely many harmonics in \(u\), also after division by a base function.
For large multiples \(k\) of the finitely many \(j\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\), it follows that \(H_k=\sum_s w^{ks} A f_s\).
Shifting to slope \(s\) and using Riemann–Lebesgue decay at the diverging slope differences shows that every coefficient of \(A f_s\) is a limit of integers.
Its own Fourier coefficients tend to zero, so \(P_s=A f_s\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.

In two variables, reality of \(f_s\) gives \(P_s A^*=P_s^*A\).
The primitive linear polynomial \(A\) is irreducible and its triangular support is not a translate of its negative, so \(A\mid P_s\) in the integer Laurent ring.
In one variable \(P_s/A\) is rational and equal to its conjugate reciprocal on the circle.
A pole in \(\mathbb C^\times\) would be a zero of \(A\) whose reciprocal conjugate is also a zero, hence a circle pole by the root restriction.
Integrability rules out such a pole, so the quotient is Laurent and its coefficients are integers by division using the unit leading coefficient.
Thus in both cases a nonzero \(f_s\) would have positive integer mean at most one.
It would then account for the whole marginal, and all nonconstant Fourier coefficients would have modulus strictly less than one by absolute continuity and strict triangle inequality.
Their integrality would force the excluded Haar marginal.
All graph contributions therefore vanish.

Only the full-torus term can contribute to \(H_k\), and its positive rows vanish above a finite index.
At a last nonzero row write \(H_k=A f c_k\), with \(f\) the base marginal density relative to Haar and \(c_k\) the conditional probability moment.
Where \(f>0\), the \(k\)-th power pushforward has only the constant and first harmonics, which gives \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by positivity.
Also \(M(f)<1\) by Jensen's inequality and the exclusion of Haar.
One has \(M(A)=2\) by the root moduli in one variable and by successive Jensen integrals in two variables, using \(|2+b y|\ge1\).
A nonzero integer Laurent polynomial has Mahler measure at least one by successive one-variable Jensen inequalities.
Hence \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(A)M(f)/2<1\).
This excludes every positive continuous row.

<a id="period"></a>
## The multiplier supplied by the singular part

The same coset decomposition applied to \(|A|^2\mu\) confines the singular part of \(\mu\) to a finite union of proper torsion translates, together with the torus zeros of \(A\).
Those zeros are torsion points in both cases, the sole two-variable zero being \((-a,-b)\).
Each translate or point admits a nonzero integer character \(r\) that equals one on it.
Take the product \(U_i=\prod_r(1-z^{|r\cdot\mathbf d|})\) over one such choice for each component, allowing the empty product.
All exponents are positive eventually and \(O(D)\).
Each \(\nu_1\) is dominated by \(\|A\|_\infty\mu\) and has zero full-dimensional density, so the product of the corresponding character differences kills it.
Its shifted Fourier coefficients give \([z^t]U_i B^\sharp=0\) eventually by compactness and integrality at every intermediate scale.
The same holds after multiplication by an additional binomial of degree \(O(D)\).
Hence all these coefficients vanish simultaneously on \(LD\le t\le h/L\) eventually for some fixed \(L\), by diagonal extraction of a violating sequence otherwise.

<a id="moving"></a>
## The initial coefficients for an unbounded primitive pair

Set \(p=2(e-d),\ W=1-z^p,\ S_i=A(z^d,z^e)/2\).
We need bounded absolute coefficient sum of \(WB^\sharp\) through \(C e\) for each fixed \(C\), and of \(J_i\) through \(p\).
Through \(C e\), the coefficients of \(1/S_i\) have absolute sum at most \(2(1+\lfloor C\rfloor)\).
Indeed expansion in \(b z^e/2\) over \(1+a z^d/2\) costs at most \(2^{-m}(1-1/2)^{-m-1}=2\) at each power \(m\le C\).

For bounded \(e/d\), the [two-offset interval bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) gives the required sparsity for \(WB^\sharp\).
It gives the same for \(B^\sharp\) if \((e-d)/d\) stays bounded away from zero, so \(J_i=B^\sharp/S_i\) on the prefix gives the second bound.
If \(e-d=o(d)\), then \(j_l=[z^l]B^\sharp\) through \(p<d\).
When the first positive nonzero index \(u\) is \(o(d)\), the [first-index alternatives](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#separation) give \(e-d=u\) or \(2u\), with prefix \(1\pm z^u\) or \(1/(1\pm z^u)\) on a fixed fraction of \(d\).
There are at most five nonzero coefficients through \(p\).
Otherwise passage to a subsequence leaves only the constant there.

For \(e/d\to\infty\), rotate the first coordinate to give first multiplier \(S_1(w)=1-w/2\).
Its marginal transform \(J\) satisfies \(S_1J\in\mathbb Z[[w]]\), by the indices \(ld\) before \(e\).
The [nine projected profiles](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix) have positive continuous mass and atoms only at sixth roots, by the [profile table](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v44.md#profiles).
In the joint limit with \(u=v^e\), the projection \(\chi\) weighted by \(2S_1u\) satisfies \(\widehat\chi(l)+(b/2)[w^l]J\in\mathbb Z\) for every integer \(l\), by the coefficient at \(e+ld\) and the rotation sign.
The Taylor term is zero for \(l<0\) and one before multiplication by \(b/2\) at zero.
Along positive and negative multiples of six the atomic coefficients of \(\chi\) are identical and its other coefficients tend to zero.
The two integer limits thus differ by \(b\) times the marginal's atomic mass, which is strictly less than one, so there are no atoms.
The [single-offset mixed-row theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) at \(d/t,t/e\to0\) uses only indices \(kt+ld<e\) for fixed \(k,l\), and its surviving atomic contribution now vanishes.
It gives \([z^t]B^\sharp=0\) throughout \(L_1d\le t\le e/L_1\) for a fixed \(L_1\).
The [low-index Bessel estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#scales) gives bounded coefficient sum for \(B^\sharp\) through \(L_1d\), also directly when \(d\) stays bounded.
Hence \(J_i=B^\sharp/(1+a z^d/2)\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum through \(e/L_1\).
On blocks of that diameter the signed character Gram has bounded operator norm by its absolute row sums, and the positive-part correction on any \(K\) characters has norm \(o(1)K\).
Bessel's inequality for the bounded partial multiplier between successive trace offsets bounds the number of nonzero integer denominator coefficients per block by testing fixed sufficiently large \(K\).
This bounds the coefficient sum of \(B^\sharp\), hence also of \(WB^\sharp\), on every interval of length \(e\).
The bound for \(1/S_i\) gives the required initial sum for \(J_i\) through \(p\).

<a id="return"></a>
## Recovering the actual denominator

In the unbounded-pair case truncate \(U_iWB^\sharp\) through \(H=\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_0\).
By the vanishing and the local bounds it has degree \(O(e)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
The series \(Q_0/S_i\) agrees with \(U_i W J_i\) through \(H\).
Its positive coefficients have a measure of bounded variation, since \(1/S_i\) has positive real part and constant one, and shifting twice its symmetric representing probability by any exponent of \(Q_0\) costs only the bounded initial sum through \(O(e)\) and the constant correction.

For the other series put \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\), so \(m_{\pm l}=j_l\) at positive \(l\).
The Fourier sequence \(m_l-m_{l-p}\) represents the coefficients of \(WJ_i\) at nonnegative indices after corrections of bounded total size through \(p\).
At every negative index \(-r\), \(r>0\), it equals \(-[z^{r+p}]WJ_i\).
The initial coefficients of \(WJ_i=(WB^\sharp)/S_i\) through \(O(e)\) have bounded absolute sum.
Thus each moving shift in \(U_i\) requires only bounded corrections from negative indices, giving a bounded-variation representation at positive indices for \(U_iWJ_i\).
Each singleton correction is represented by a monomial density.
The [two-cutoff estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#cutoff) applied to
\[
 S_i U_i W B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2=S_i(\Pi/2)(U_i W J_i-Q_0/S_i)
\]
gives \(\|S_i U_i W B^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\) by the common prefix of positive fractional length, degree \(O(h)\) on the left and bounded polynomial multipliers.
Writing \(X=a z^d,\ Y=b z^e\) on the circle gives
\[
       |W|^2\le4|X-Y|^2=4(4-|X+Y|^2)\le32|S_i|.
\]
Thus \(U_i W^3 B^\sharp\) has bounded \(L^1\) norm.

In the fixed-pair case \(B^\sharp\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum through each \(C g\).
For \(g\to\infty\) let \(D_0\) be the first fractional offset of \(A(z^g)/2\).
The \(j_l\) are integral below \(D_0\) by division of the integral prefix.
At \(D_0+rt'\) with \(t'=o(g)\) positive and \(r\) fixed, each earlier multiplier gives an integer at an index below \(D_0\), each later offset is absent, and the coefficient at \(D_0\) is an integer plus a signed half.
The [fractional-seam congruences](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#seam) force a [Haar marginal](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) for \(v^{t'}\), hence a zero moment interval through \(\kappa g\) for some \(\kappa>0\) by integrality and extraction.
The [block Bessel estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) gives the local coefficient bound, while bounded \(g\) follows directly from the moment bounds.
Truncate \(U_i B^\sharp\) through \(H\) to obtain \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(g)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
Put \(E_i=E(z^g),\ F_i=F(z^g)/2\).
Then \(Q_0/F_i\) agrees with \(E_iU_iJ_i\) through \(H\).
Its coefficients are absolutely summable uniformly because \(F\) is fixed and strictly exterior.
The coefficients of \(J_i\) through every \(C g\) have bounded absolute sum by the local bound for \(B^\sharp\) and the first \(1+\lfloor C\rfloor\) fixed coefficients of \(2/A\).
The shifts \(O(g)\) in \(E_iU_i\) therefore have bounded corrections in the symmetric moment sequence.
Applying the same cutoffs to
\[
 E_i F_i U_i B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2=F_i(\Pi/2)(E_iU_iJ_i-Q_0/F_i)
\]
and using the positive circle minimum of \(F_i\) bounds \(\|E_iU_i B^\sharp\|_1\).

In both cases the resulting integer polynomial has [bounded support](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
Its extra factors are cyclotomic up to sign, so its Mahler measure is \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, contrary to the [fixed-support gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001).
This proves both uniform bounds.
