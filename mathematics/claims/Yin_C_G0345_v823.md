# A trace-field square test and eight integers controlling complementary resultants

<a id="statement"></a>
## The good part of a reciprocal complementary resultant

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be a squarefree two-pole section with positive coprime exponents and \(B=B(k,l)\ne0\) the [exceptional binary form](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement).
Set \(D=\mathbb Z[1/B]\) and let \(A=D[Z]/(E)\) be its critical octic algebra, using the equations and units of the same reconstruction.
The eight invariant factors \(s_1\mid\cdots\mid s_8\) below are positive integers prime to \(B\) and depend only on \((k,l)\).
In \(A[t]/(t^2-(Z^2-4))\) compute the critical value \(W=V^k/U^l\) and form
\[
       T=W+W^{-1}-2,\qquad H=(W-W^{-1})/t,\qquad I=TA+HA.
\]
Both generators belong to \(A\).
Take \(s_j\) as the Smith invariant factors over \(D\) of \(A/I\), including unit factors and choosing each representative prime to \(B\).
Thus they can be calculated from the eight-row matrix juxtaposing multiplication by \(T\) and by \(H\) in the power basis of \(A\).
For a positive integer \(n\) define \(e(n)=\prod_{p:\,2\mid v_p(n)}p^{v_p(n)}\), including only prime divisors with even exponent.
For every factorization \(P=SM\) into monic reciprocal integer factors,
\[
          |\operatorname {Res}(S,M)|_{(B)}\ \mid\ \prod_{j=1}^8 e(s_j),                 \tag{1}
\]
where the subscript discards all primes dividing \(B\).
The right side is a square computed before factoring \(P\).
For each good prime the [trace square test](#descent) below can further remove individual orbit contributions in (1), including at nonsplit inverse pairs.

<a id="ideals"></a>
## Valuations of the eight integers

The leading coefficient of \(E\), its discriminant and the resultant excluding the denominators are units over \(D\), so the [octic reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#elimination) gives a finite étale algebra with the indicated power basis.
Inversion takes \((t,W)\) to \((-t,W^{-1})\), fixing \(T,H\), and \(2t\) is a unit, so the fixed algebra is precisely \(A\).
The nonvanishing of \(W-1\) at every characteristic-zero critical point follows from [squarefreeness of the section](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#statement).
Thus \(T\) is invertible over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(A/I\) is finite.
Fix \(p\nmid B\) and an octic residue orbit of degree \(f\), with unramified integer ring \(O\).
For a lift of either orientation in an unramified extension of \(O\), set \(b=v_p(W-1)\), the same for the inverse orientation.
When \(b>0\) the two generators have valuations \(2b,b\), since \(W+1\) and \(t\) are units.
When \(b=0\), the generator \(T=(W-1)^2/W\) is a unit even if \(W\equiv-1\).
Hence \(IO=p^bO\), and the eight integers \(v_p(s_j)\) are exactly the sorted list of the numbers \(b\), each repeated \(f\) times.
The critical points above this octic orbit have total residue degree \(2f\).
By the [quadratic disk calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local), if \(b\) is positive even they can supply at most \(fb\) to the resultant valuation, and otherwise supply zero.
Summing these exponents proves (1).

<a id="descent"></a>
## Testing squares before adjoining either orientation

Fix one octic residue orbit with \(b>0\), use its ring \(O\) and choose one orientation of its critical lift \((u_c,v_c)\).
Write \(t_c=u_c-u_c^{-1}\) and choose integers \(a,h\) with \(ka+lh=1\).
Use torus coordinates \(x=U^aV^h,\ y=V^k/U^l\), the Laurent equation \(G(x,y)=F(x^ky^{-h},x^ly^a)\) and critical coordinates \((x_c,w_c)\) as in the [local double-root model](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local).
At the lift put
\[
 C=-G_{xx}/(2G_y),\qquad
 C_0=\frac{(1+w_c^{-2})C}{t_c(1-x_c^{-2})^2},\qquad
 \delta=-\frac{w_c-w_c^{-1}}{t_c C_0}.
\]
Then \(C_0\in O^\times\) and \(\delta\in O\) has valuation \(b\).
The two local trace roots are separately defined over \(\operatorname{Frac}(O)\) exactly when \(b\) is even and the residue of \(p^{-b}\delta\) is a square in the degree-\(f\) residue field.
An orbit failing this test contributes zero to the resultant of reciprocal factors.
For every passing orbit its contribution is either zero or exactly \(fb\), according as the two trace roots lie in the same factor or in opposite factors.

Indeed \(\bar x_c\ne\pm1\) since \(\bar y_c=1\), \(\bar u_c=\bar x_c^k\) and \(\bar t_c\ne0\).
On the lifted curve in this orientation take the local functions \(q=x+x^{-1}\) and \(s=(y-y^{-1})/t_c\).
Simultaneous inversion of the point and the orientation fixes both functions, and \(q-q_c\) is a local parameter by its unit derivative in \(x\), where \(q_c=x_c+x_c^{-1}\).
The expansion in the complete local disk has the form
\[
               s=H_c+C_0(q-q_c)^2\bigl(1+(q-q_c)g(q-q_c)\bigr),
                \qquad H_c=(w_c-w_c^{-1})/t_c.
\]
The coefficient formula follows by composing the expansion \(y=w_c+C(x-x_c)^2+\cdots\) with these coordinate changes.
The formal implicit function and inverse series have integral coefficients because their linear derivatives are units.
Changing orientation gives the same expansion by invariance of \(q,s\) and of the curve under inversion.
Thus all coefficients lie in \(O\), also when Frobenius to the power \(f\) exchanges orientations in the unramified quadratic extension.
In this disk the equation \(s=0\) means \(y=1\) since the residue characteristic is odd and \(\bar y_c=1\).
An invertible square-root change of the parameter over \(O\) now gives the equation \(z^2=\delta\), proving the square test and showing that the difference of the two split trace roots has valuation \(b/2\).
Their common residue \(\bar q_c\) lies in the degree-\(f\) field since Frobenius to the power \(f\) fixes or inverts \(\bar x_c\), and conversely \(\bar u_c+\bar u_c^{-1}=\bar x_c^k+\bar x_c^{-k}\) is an integral polynomial in \(\bar q_c\) generating that field.
Hence exactly \(f\) conjugate trace disks occur.
Each reciprocal factor groups the two orientations of each trace root, so conjugate trace roots over \(\operatorname{Frac}(O)\) cannot be separated by these factors.
If they are separated, both original orientation disks for each geometric trace residue contribute \(b/2\), since \(x\mapsto x+x^{-1}\) has unit derivative there.
This gives \(fb\) in all, including when the orientations form one residue orbit of degree \(2f\).
At a nonsplit inverse pair a nonsquare unit in the trace residue field becomes a square after adjoining the orientation, which explains why the trace-field test is required for the stated local splitting criterion.
