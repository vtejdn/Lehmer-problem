# A two-point test for the Lehmer gap on adjacent degree records

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two first measures can be specified in advance

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure and \(\ell=\inf\{m(P)>0:P\in\mathbb Z[X]\setminus\{0\}\}\).
Use the consecutive strict decreases in the minimum positive measure through each degree, choosing one minimizing root \(w_i\) at each record degree \(d_i\).
Discard the initial segment through any fixed degree large enough that the remaining records have measure below the plastic number.
If there are only finitely many records there are only finitely many remaining rows; the rows below can also be empty.
Put \(A_i=M(w_i)\) and \(m_i=\log A_i\).
In row \(i\), for every integer \(1\le a\le\lceil(\log(3d_i))^3\rceil\), take the squarefree product of the monic minima of \(w_i^a\) and \(w_{i+1}\).
Its first measure is \(C_{i,a}=A_i^a A_{i+1}\); its second log measure is \(m(C_{i,a})\).
The degrees of its two irreducible factors are the adjacent record degrees \(d_i,d_{i+1}\).

Let \(U\) be the set of \(t>0\) at which \(m(C_{i,a})\) is unbounded in every neighborhood of \(\log C_{i,a}=t\), meaning that for all \(\varepsilon,B>0\) there is an allowed pair with \(|\log C_{i,a}-t|<\varepsilon\) and \(m(C_{i,a})>B\).
Then
\[
 U=\begin{cases}
 \varnothing &\text{if there are finitely many strict records},\\
 \{q\ell:q=2,3,\ldots\} &\text{if there are infinitely many and }\ell>0,\\
 (0,\infty) &\text{if }\ell=0 .
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
In particular the unrestricted Lehmer gap holds if and only if for **at least one** of the two first measures \(2,3\), the second measure on these adjacent-degree inputs is bounded in some neighborhood of that first measure.
Any prescribed two numbers greater than one with irrational log ratio work.
The test imposes no dependence of the first-measure neighborhoods on the degrees.
The required boundedness at either point remains to be established.

<a id="rows"></a>
## The degrees, row spacing and second-measure bound

Under any fixed ceiling coefficient finiteness gives a minimum at each degree.
Once below a fixed [subplastic example](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#statement), a strict improvement is a monic irreducible unit minimum by factorization and the two endpoint coefficients, and is reciprocal by [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
The values of infinitely many improvements decrease to the unrestricted infimum by exhaustion of degrees; if there are finitely many, the last value attains that positive infimum. [Record reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#records)
Each record has singleton conjugate classes modulo roots of unity, since [class compression](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) otherwise preserves its measure at a strictly smaller degree.
Consequently taking any positive power leaves all the conjugates distinct, preserves the degree and raises the measure to the same power.
This proves the polynomial realization and squarefreeness since the degrees differ.

The [relative degree and height bound](Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#statement), for all sufficiently large rows and any positive \(a\), gives
\[
   m(C_{i,a})\ \ge\ b_{i+1}a\,m(A_i)\ \ge\ b_{i+1}a\,m_i,\qquad
   b_{i+1}\gg d_{i+1}/(\log(3d_{i+1}))^{212}.                        \tag{2}
\]
Here \(b_{i+1}\) is the size of the canonical block on inverse pairs at the later source, and the constant is uniform under the fixed measure ceiling.
The second inequality uses the real conjugate \(A_i>1\) in its own measure.
The first logs in row \(i\) are the arithmetic progression \(a m_i+m_{i+1}\).
The smallest tends to \(2\ell\) for infinitely many rows; their largest tends to infinity even if \(\ell=0\), because [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives
\[
        m_i(\log(3d_i))^3\ \ge\ \tfrac14(\log\log d_i)^3
\]
for all sufficiently large \(d_i\).

<a id="limits"></a>
## All exceptional points and the fixed two-point criterion

If the list is finite, each row is finite.
If it is infinite and \(\ell>0\), on any bounded first-log interval the exponent \(a\) is uniformly bounded because \(m_i\ge\ell\).
Any limit obtained with unbounded row indices therefore equals \((a+1)\ell\) on a constant-exponent subsequence.
Conversely every fixed \(a\ge1\) occurs in all sufficiently large rows; their first logs decrease to \((a+1)\ell\) and their second logs tend to infinity by (2).
This proves the middle line of (1).

If \(\ell=0\), fix any compact interval \([s,t]\subset(0,\infty)\).
For every sufficiently large row and **every** \(x\in[s,t]\) take \(a=\lfloor(x-m_{i+1})/m_i\rfloor\).
The endpoints of the row proved above make \(a\) admissible.
Then
\[
  x-m_i<\log C_{i,a}\le x,\qquad
  m(C_{i,a})\ge b_{i+1}(s-m_i-m_{i+1}) .
\]
Across the entire interval the first approximation error tends uniformly to zero and the second log tends uniformly to infinity.
In particular the entire positive axis belongs to \(U\).
When \(\ell>0\) the lattice in (1) cannot contain both \(\log 2\) and \(\log 3\), since \(2^p=3^q\) for positive integers is impossible.
This proves the test.

<a id="growth"></a>
## Approaching one with arbitrary prescribed growth using only neighboring degrees

Suppose the unrestricted gap fails.
Given any positive locally bounded \(G\) on \((0,\infty)\), choose target logs \(x_j\downarrow0\).
Apply the uniform estimate at each fixed \(x_j\), taking a later row than before so that its first log lies in \([x_j/2,x_j]\) and its second log exceeds both \(j\) and \(\sup_{[x_j/2,x_j]}G\).
Thus adjacent record degrees suffice even for the [prescribed growth problem](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#statement) if one is allowed to power the earlier root.
For \(x_j\le T\), the required exponent in the selected row is at most \(4T(\log d_i/\log\log d_i)^3\) by the same degree estimate.

Keeping both roots unpowered instead gives only \(m_i+m_{i+1}\downarrow0\).
These first logs form a locally finite subset of \((0,\infty)\), so there exists a positive locally bounded function on that interval majorizing every such second log, for example one plus the supremum of the finitely many second logs with first log at least the argument (include zero in this supremum).
This explains why the uniformity in the exponent in (2) adds information even though every unpowered adjacent pair already obeys a divergent degree lower bound.
