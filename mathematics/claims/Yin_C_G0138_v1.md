## 1. A bounded harmonic terminal cost does not provide the old sparse charge

The following direct counting argument makes the failure quantitative, without importing a weighted Hardy--Ramanujan theorem.
For every even integer y>=2,

    sum_(n<=y, n odd)phi(n)>=y^2/200.                 (1)

Indeed there are y/2 odd integers in[1,y].
If an ordered pair of them is not coprime, it is divisible in both coordinates by some odd integer d>=3.
There are at most y/d odd multiples of d for d<=y.
The union bound therefore gives at least

    y^2[1/4-sum_(d>=3,d odd)d^(-2)]

coprime ordered odd pairs.
The series is bounded above by

    1/9+1/25+1/49+1/14 <49/200,

using the integral comparison for the odd tail starting at7.
Hence the number is at least y^2/200.
That number equals sum_(n<=y,n odd)phi(n): for each odd n>1 exactly half of its phi(n) smaller coprime positive integers are odd, by m<->n-m, and the two ordered orientations together contribute phi(n); the pair(1,1) supplies phi(1)=1.
This proves(1).

There is also the uniform upper bound

    sum_(n<=y)phi(n)r_(1/2)(n)<=2y^2, y>=1.           (2)

Use phi(n)<=n and sum_(p|n)p^(-1/2), then sum the multiples p*m.
With k=floor(y/p), the contribution at p is p^(1/2)k(k+1)/2, at most y^2/(2p^(3/2))+y/(2sqrt(p)).
The bounds sum_(p>=2)p^(-3/2)<2 and sum_(p<=y)p^(-1/2)<=2sqrt(y) give y^2+y^(3/2)<=2y^2.
Both series estimates follow by enlarging to the integers and elementary integrals.

Combining(1)--(2) and discarding the labels with r_(1/2)(n)>800 yields

    sum_(n<=y,n odd,r_(1/2)(n)<=800)phi(n)
       >=y^2/400, every even y>=2.                   (3)

Thus even a fixed POINTWISE terminal harmonic bound does not give F(y)=o(y^2), let alone C124's summable logarithmic saving.
All these n<=y satisfy the canonical stopping smoothness condition at window Y=y^2, since each exact prime power is at most y.
This is a concrete obstruction to the proposed sparse-count substitution, not merely lack of a source.

It can be placed inside a finite array satisfying the full capacity/linear-window/positive-density constraints, without becoming a counterexample to capture.
Take y=2^j, j>=3, X=y^2.
Include every odd n<=y/2 with r_(1/2)(n)<=800, with weight phi(n), and the two additional labels c=2y and X, each with its full totient weight.
All odd labels stop at the root at window X.
Both powers of2 have terminal remainder1.
Their total mass W satisfies X/2<=W<=X: the odd part is at most (y/2)(y/2+1)/2, phi(c)=y and phi(X)=X/2.
For j>=3 the displayed upper sum is at most X.
Hence X<=2W and the repeated triple(X,X,X) alone gives delta_X>=1/8.

The first child on c's path strips the minimal power2y>sqrt(X)=y.
Every chosen odd n is NONMATCHING at that root, and lcm(n,c)=2yn<=X.
By(3) with y/2, their original compatible mass is at least X/1600.
Therefore the nonmatching mass divided by X does not tend to zero as the window grows, despite the pointwise harmonic terminal bound800.
The whole array is readily captured at time X with mass at least X/2>=W/2.
This example refutes only the old sparse nonmatching step in this new domain; it does not refute harmonic-terminal capture, the general capacity conjecture or any actual-polynomial theorem.

## 5. Full proof: one original weighted link and exact G restoration

First assume the pointwise bound R>0.

Let A_c contain all original labels stopping at v(c) or its ancestors; its original mass M_c is at most W.

The four choices of deepest coordinate give

    T4_X<=4 sum_(eligible c) w_c L_c.                  (10)

Ties and repeated coordinates only cause overcounting.
At least one choice exists for each compatible tuple; no noncompatible tuple is required on the left.

For each a in A_c set u(a)=a/gcd(a,G).
By C119 its stopping core divides G, so u(a) divides its original terminal remainder.
Hence r_(1/2)(u(a))<=R POINTWISE.
Merge the original weights by u and divide them by G:

    z_u=(1/G)sum_(a in A_c:u(a)=u)w_a,
    sum_u z_u=M_c/G, z_u<=phi(u).                     (11)

The capacity is C121's exact fiber identity, not phi evaluated on a formal new polynomial.
Zero buckets are removed.
All these residual orders enter the merged law, including those above Y; their mass is not discarded.

Since G divides c, a contributing four-tuple has original lcm divisible by G and

    G*lcm(u(a),u(b),u(e),u(c))=lcm(a,b,e,c)<=X.

Dropping the fixed u(c) gives a valid residual three-coordinate tuple at Y.
Merging is exact for finite ordered sums with repetitions, so

    L_c <= G^3 T3_Y(z).                               (12)

For any integer n<=Y, all original a in a residual divisor bucket u|n divide Gn.
Thus the residual capture H_Y(z) obeys

    G H_Y(z)<=H_X,
    Gn<=G floor(X/G)<=X.                              (13)

These are actual original times and weights.

Apply C133 to THIS merged stock and its own law.

    D_c<=D:=exp(8 C_s R^2/delta).

The C133 triple upper bound, followed by(11)--(13), gives

    L_c <= beta_c M_c^3
                  +4 D_c (XH)^s M_c^(3-2s)
         <= (delta/8)W^3+4D(XH)^s W^(3-2s).           (14)

    delta4_X<=delta/2+16D Z^s.                         (15)
