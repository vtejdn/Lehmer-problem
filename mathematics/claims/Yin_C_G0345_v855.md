# Distinct critical values modulo roots of unity and independent translated covers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Geometric monodromy outside finitely many primitive directions

Let \(C\) be the smooth projective model of the [two-pole curve](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
For all but finitely many primitive signed directions \((k,l)\), put \(T=V^k/U^l\) and \(n=2|l|+4|k|\).
The cover \(T:C\to\mathbb P^1\) has geometric monodromy \(S_n\).
It has sixteen branch values in \(\mathbb C^\times\), each with a single ramified point of index two, and the quotient of any two distinct such values has infinite multiplicative order.
Every one of these values is itself nontorsion by [squarefreeness of the torsion translates](Yin_C_G0345_v852.md#statement).

For every finite list of distinct roots of unity \(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_b\), take the covers \(T/\zeta_i:C\to\mathbb P^1\) over the same parameter line.
The compositum of their Galois closures over \(\mathbb C(t)\) has group \(S_n^b\).
Pullback of this compositum by \(t=z^m\) preserves that full group over \(\mathbb C(z)\) for every positive integer \(m\).
The exceptional set of directions is independent of the list and its length.

<a id="heights"></a>
## Comparing two points of the same critical fiber

Away from the axes and the finite projective branch values of the [logarithmic slope map](Yin_C_G0345_v822.md#map), the torus critical fiber consists of sixteen distinct points.
Indeed the boundary has only zero or infinite slope by the [boundary parameters](Yin_C_G0345_v854.md#critical), and the slope map has degree sixteen.
A simple point of this fiber gives ramification index two for \(T\), since its logarithmic differential is \(k\,dV/V-l\,dU/U\) and the curve is smooth and immersed in the torus.
Conversely these are all critical points with finite nonzero value.
The boundary divisor of \(T\) gives degree \(n\) for either sign of either exponent, since the two \(U\)-zeros and two \(U\)-poles have unit \(V\), and the four \(V\)-zeros and four \(V\)-poles have unit \(U\), with every boundary order simple.

Write \(N=\max(|k|,|l|)\) and choose distinct torus critical points \(P_i=(u_i,v_i)\), \(i=1,2\).
Each point has field degree at most sixteen and its two coordinates have absolute logarithmic height \(O_F(\log(2N))\) by the [projected resultant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v851.md#elimination).
Thus their compositum \(K\) has degree at most 256.
Put \(a=u_1/u_2,\ b=v_1/v_2\).
If \(T(P_1)/T(P_2)\) is a root of unity, so is \(a^l/b^k\).
Choose integers \(i,j\) with \(ik+jl=1\) and set \(\gamma=a^i b^j\).
Then \(a/\gamma^k\) and \(b/\gamma^l\) are roots of unity, giving
\[
              N h(\gamma)=\max\{h(a),h(b)\}\ll_F\log(2N).
\]
If \(\gamma\) is nontorsion, Northcott finiteness and Kronecker's theorem give \(h(\gamma)\ge c_{256}>0\), since \(\gamma\in K^\times\).
The inequality then allows only bounded \(N\).

<a id="translations"></a>
## Torsion ratios of both coordinates

When \(\gamma\) is torsion, both \(a,b\) are roots of unity of degree at most 256.
There are only finitely many such ordered pairs, and \((a,b)\ne(1,1)\) because the critical points are distinct.
For each pair the point \(P_2\) lies on both \(F(U,V)=0\) and \(F(aU,bV)=0\).
These irreducible torus curves are distinct.
In fact equality would make the Laurent polynomials associates, and equality of their finite supports forces the monomial in the associate relation to be constant.
The coefficient at \((0,0)\) makes that constant one, the coefficient at \((1,0)\) then forces \(a=1\), and the coefficient at \((2,1)\) forces \(b=1\).
The intersection is therefore finite.
Every geometric point of it on the curve has a unique logarithmic tangent direction by smoothness.
The finitely many pairs give only finitely many such directions, and each rational direction has at most two primitive signed representatives.
Together with the nontorsion case this excludes all critical-value coincidences up to roots of unity outside one fixed finite set.

<a id="group"></a>
## Branch transpositions in one or several covers

The connected cover \(C\to\mathbb P^1\) has transitive monodromy.
Let \(H\) be the normal subgroup generated by the inertia at all finite nonzero branch values.
Each inertia generator there is a transposition by the separation just proved.
The action on the orbits of \(H\) corresponds to a connected intermediate cover branched only over zero and infinity.
A connected complex cover of degree \(q\) with this property is \(z\mapsto z^q\) up to coordinates respecting the two points.
Indeed the punctured target has fundamental group \(\mathbb Z\) and a unique connected cover of each finite degree.
In particular, the zero and pole multiplicities of \(T\) would all be divisible by \(q\).
They include \(|k|\) and \(|l|\), so \(q=1\).
The subgroup \(H\) is thus transitive.
A transitive group generated by transpositions is the full symmetric group, as its generating edges form a connected graph.
This proves both that the group is \(S_n\) and that the finite nonzero inertia generate it normally.

The finite nonzero branch loci of the scaled covers are pairwise disjoint.
An overlap would give a quotient of critical values equal to \(\zeta_i/\zeta_j\), which is a nontrivial root of unity when \(i\ne j\).
The compositum group projects onto every factor \(S_n\).
A small loop around a branch value unique to one factor acts as a transposition there and as the identity elsewhere.
Conjugating inside the compositum group gives every transposition supported on this factor, by surjectivity of the projection.
This holds for every factor, yielding the full direct product generated normally by these inertia elements.
Any subfield of the Galois compositum lying in the cyclic Kummer extension \(\mathbb C(z)/\mathbb C(t)\), \(t=z^m\), is Galois and unramified at every finite nonzero branch value.
In its quotient group all those inertia groups vanish, so the intersection equals \(\mathbb C(t)\).
Galois base change proves the asserted persistence of the full product.
