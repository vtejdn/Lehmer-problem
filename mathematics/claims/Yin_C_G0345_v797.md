# Ternary polynomials whose arithmetic-progression sections vanish to prescribed orders

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous restrictions at one exponent span

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with monic minimal polynomial \(P\), degree \(d\), logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), and polynomial root discriminant \(\rho=|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}\).
For a positive integer \(N\), choose a mask \(S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\), a finite nonempty set \(A\) of positive steps and an integer \(r_j\ge1\) for each \(j\in A\).
Suppose \(jd r_j\le N\) for every \(j\).
For a polynomial \(f(X)=\sum_{i<N} a_i X^i\) and \(0\le t<j\), write
\[
           f_{j,t}(X)=\sum_{0\le jv+t<N}a_{jv+t}X^v .
\]
There is a family of binary polynomials supported on \(S\) in which every difference \(Q\) satisfies
\[
                 P^{\,r_j}\mid Q_{j,t}\qquad (j\in A,\ 0\le t<j).       \tag{1}
\]
For one absolute constant \(C\) its cardinality satisfies
\[
 \log|\mathcal F|\ \ge\ |S|\log2-Nm\sum_{j\in A}r_j
       -\frac d2\sum_{j\in A} j r_j^2\log\!\left(\frac{CN}{jr_j\rho}\right).
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Every nonzero difference is a single ternary polynomial of degree less than \(N\).
Condition (1) is equivalent to divisibility by \(\prod_{j\in A}P(X^j)^{r_j}\).
The largest binary family satisfying (1) on the full interval has size at most
\[
                   2^{(N-d\sum_{j\in A}j r_j)_+}.                       \tag{3}
\]

In particular, fix \(\delta,c_0>0\), suppose \(|S|\ge\delta N\) and \(\rho\ge c_0d\), and put \(N=\lceil d/m\rceil\) as \(m\to0\).
For each of the following choices, (1) holds in a family of at least \(\exp(\delta N\log2/2)\) words for all sufficiently small \(m\), with \(c>0\) sufficiently small in terms of \(\delta,c_0\).

* Take all steps \(1\le j\le J\), each with multiplicity \(r\), where \(rJ\le c/\sqrt{m\log(1/m)}\).
* Take all primes \(j\le c/\sqrt m\), each with multiplicity one.
* Take all powers of two \(j\le c/m\), including one, each with multiplicity one.

The last two menus may also be imposed together after decreasing \(c\).
For the dyadic menu the sections at its largest step have length at most \(O_{\delta,c_0}(d)\) when that step is the greatest power of two at most \(c/m\) for a fixed admissible \(c\).
For all three choices the original span times \(h(\alpha)=m/d\) is at most \(1+o(1)\).
The [grid construction](Yin_C_G0345_v782.md#distance) yields signed relations in several independent coordinates and can use exponent span \(L^J-1\) in a single variable.
Here the progressions occupy the same interval from the start.

<a id="jets"></a>
## The entropy cost of one progression with repeated zeros

First let \(g\) be a random polynomial with independent fair bits on an arbitrary mask in \([0,L)\), zeros elsewhere, and \(L\ge rd\).
Write \(Y\in\mathbb Z^{rd}\) for the coefficient vector of its remainder modulo \(P^r\) in increasing powers.
The [power-basis cube smoothing argument](Yin_C_G0345_v786.md#discriminant) gives the following extension to all \(r\ge1\):
\[
 H(Y)\le r(L-1)m+\frac{dr^2}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{C_1 L}{r\rho}\right). \tag{4}
\]
To verify the extension, add independent uniform noise on the centered unit cube in the \(rd\) remainder coefficients.
Its translates have disjoint interiors, so the differential entropy after smoothing equals \(H(Y)\).
For every root \(\beta\) of \(P\) and \(0\le a<r\), take the coordinate \(\beta^a g^{(a)}(\beta)/a!\), applied linearly to the smoothed remainder polynomial.
Before adding the noise this equals the same expression for \(g\), since its difference from its remainder is divisible by \(P^r\).
The coordinate of the monomial \(X^i\) is \(\binom ia\beta^i\), with a zero binomial for \(i<a\).

The complex determinant of all these coordinate rows on \(1,\ldots,X^{rd-1}\) has absolute value \(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{r^2/2}\).
Indeed start with the ordinary power evaluation determinant at the points \(\beta+u\epsilon\), \(u=0,\ldots,r-1\), for every \(\beta\).
Replace successive rows within each root group by their divided differences of orders \(0,\ldots,r-1\) at the initial consecutive points.
This divides the determinant by the product of all within-group differences, and their limit rows are the derivatives divided by \(a!\).
The remaining pairwise differences give \(\prod_{\beta<\gamma}|\beta-\gamma|^{r^2}\) in any chosen root ordering.
Multiplying back the row weights \(\beta^a\) leaves its absolute value unchanged since \(|P(0)|=1\).
For a real coordinate system keep real roots and the real and imaginary parts at one root of each conjugate pair.
The absolute real Jacobian is \(2^{-rr_2}|\operatorname{disc}P|^{r^2/2}\), with \(r_2\) the number of nonreal pairs.

At each real component belonging to \(\beta,a\), the variance is at most
\[
    (L/4+rd/12)\binom{L-1}{a}^2\max(1,|\beta|)^{2(L-1)} .
\]
This sums the variances of the independent digit and noise contributions, with exponents less than \(L\) in both.
The Gaussian entropy bound at each component, subadditivity and the Jacobian therefore give
\[
 H(Y)\le r(L-1)m+\frac{dr}{2}\log(C_2 L)
              +d\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}\log\binom{L-1}{a}
              -\frac{r^2}{2}\log|\operatorname{disc}P| .
\]
For \(1\le a<r\), \(\log\binom{L-1}{a}\le a\log(eL/r)+a\log(r/a)\), and \(a\log(r/a)\le r/e\).
Summing the \(a\)'s gives \(r(r-1)/2\).
Combining with the term \(dr\log(C_2L)/2\) leaves an extra \(O(dr^2)\), since \((\log r)/r\) is bounded.
This proves (4), including \(r=1\).

<a id="sections"></a>
## Residue classes and the exact rank of the joint conditions

Choose independent fair digits on \(S\) and form the record of all remainders \(f_{j,t}\bmod P^{r_j}\).
For a fixed step pad all sections to length \(L_j=\lceil N/j\rceil\) with deterministic zeros.
This length is at least \(r_jd\), \(j(L_j-1)\le N\), and \(L_j\le2N/j\).
Subadditivity and (4) applied to every residue class bound the entropy of the whole record by the two costs in (2).
Given the record, the random word is uniform on its fiber, so the largest log fiber size is at least \(|S|\log2\) minus this entropy.

The direct sum \(\mathbb Q[X]=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{j-1}X^t\mathbb Q[X^j]\) shows that divisibility by \(P(X^j)^{r_j}\) is equivalent to divisibility of each section by \(P^{r_j}\).
For distinct steps \(i,j\), a common complex root \(z\) of \(P(X^i)\) and \(P(X^j)\) would have \(i h(z)=j h(z)=h(\alpha)>0\), an impossibility.
Thus the polynomials for different steps are coprime, and the joint difference space in degrees below \(N\) is exactly the space of multiples of the product.
Monicity also gives divisibility over \(\mathbb Z[X]\) for every integral input.
The dimension of that rational space is \((N-d\sum_j j r_j)_+\).
Projection onto a set of that many pivot coordinates is injective on an affine fiber, proving (3).

<a id="menus"></a>
## Choosing progressions at span \(d/m\)

Write \(B=\log(1/m)\) and assume \(\rho\ge c_0d\).
For all steps through \(J\) with constant multiplicity \(r\), the cost in (2), divided by \(N\), is at most
\[
         rJm+C_{c_0}m(rJ)^2(1+B).
\]
Here one may increase the constant in the logarithmic upper bound to make it positive.
Under the first stated choice this is less than \(\delta\log2/2\) by taking \(c\) small and then \(m\) small.
Also \(jdr/N\le mrJ\to0\).

For primes up to \(T=c/\sqrt m\) we use the elementary upper bound \(\#\{p\le T\}\ll T/\log T\) for large \(T\).
Indeed the product of the primes in \((n,2n]\) divides \(\binom{2n}{n}\le4^n\); summing these dyadic counts down to \(\sqrt T\) and treating the smaller primes trivially gives the estimate, with rounding of endpoints absorbed in the constant.
Consequently the normalized logarithmic cost in (2) is \(O_{c_0}(mT^2(1+B)/\log T)=O_{c_0}(c^2)\) for small \(m\) at each fixed \(c\), and the measure cost is at most \(mT\).
All steps satisfy \(jd\le N\).

For the dyadic menu let \(T\) be its largest step.
Then \(c/(2m)<T\le c/m\).
For \(j=T/2^b\) the logarithm in (2) is bounded by \(\log(C_3/(mc_0 T))+b\log2\) with an absolute \(C_3\).
The sums of \(2^{-b}\) and \(b2^{-b}\) are bounded, so the normalized logarithmic cost is \(O(c(1+\log^+(C_3/(c_0c))))\).
The measure cost is \(O(m(1+B))\), and \(jd\le N\) for \(c<1\).
Both costs tend below the required budget with \(c\) chosen first.
The two menus together obey the sum of the two estimates by taking all their records together and using subadditivity.
Finally \(Nh(\alpha)\le1+m/d\) and each section at step \(T\) has length at most \(N/T+1=O_c(d)\).
