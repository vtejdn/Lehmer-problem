# Uniform Pisot inverses at every sufficiently large prescribed constant

<a id="statement"></a>
## A nearly linear threshold uniform in the Salem degree

Let \(P=z^mQ(z+z^{-1})\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(1<\sigma\le2\), with \(d=2m\ge4\).
Put \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(E=T-2\), \(h=m-1\), and
\[
 \kappa_h=288\sqrt{\frac{\log(8h)}{\pi}},\qquad
 B_h=h(2\kappa_h+1).
\]
For every prescribed integer \(q\ge\lceil B_h\rceil\), there is a monic Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of degree \(d\), constant \(q\) and exterior root \(\theta\), such that
\[
 zf+f^*=(z+1)P,\qquad
 0<\theta-q-E<\frac{4B_h+1}{q-2},\qquad
 f^*(z)=z^df(1/z).                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus the threshold is \(O(d\sqrt{\log d})\), and the error is \(O(d\sqrt{\log d}/q)\), with absolute constants independent of \(P\) and of the prescribed \(q\).

In particular let \(q(d)\) be any prescribed integer function on the even degrees with
\[
             \frac{q(d)}{d\sqrt{\log d}}\longrightarrow\infty.
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
After omitting finitely many initial terms, every supplied sequence of Salem numbers tending to one has same-degree exact inverses with constant \(q(d)\) and \(\theta-q(d)\to0\).
Conversely, Pisot minimal polynomials of even degrees tending to infinity, constants \(q(d)\), and excesses tending to zero give Salem numbers tending to one.
The prescribed choice \(q(d)=d^2\) therefore suffices, with forward error \(O(\sqrt{\log d}/d)\).

<a id="dual"></a>
## The endpoint-weighted residue lattice and its dual

Write \(x_1<\cdots<x_h\) for the neutral trace roots of \(Q\), and put \(H=Q/(x-T)\).
The [exact inverse simplex](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) parametrizes all integral solutions by
\[
 f=P+(z-1)z^{m-1}R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
 R=(1-q)x^h+r(x),\quad r\in\mathbb Z[x],\quad\deg r<h.
\]
Define
\[
 b_j=-\frac{R(x_j)}{H'(x_j)},\qquad
 c_j=\frac{b_j}{2+x_j}.
\]
The necessary and sufficient Pisot conditions are \(c_j>0\) for every \(j\) and \(\sum_jc_j<q+T/2\).
For fixed \(q\), these vectors form a translate of the full lattice
\[
 \Lambda=\left\{\left(-\frac{r(x_j)}{(2+x_j)H'(x_j)}\right)_{j=1}^h:
                 r\in\mathbb Z[x],\ \deg r<h\right\}\subset\mathbb R^h.
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Its Euclidean dual consists exactly of the vectors
\[
 y_j=(2+x_j)\frac{A(x_j)-A(T)}{x_j-T},\qquad
 A\in\mathbb Z[x],\quad A(0)=0,\quad\deg A\le h.                         \tag{4}
\]

To verify (4), let \(c\) denote the lattice vector in (3) associated with \(r\).
Interpolation gives
\[
 r(x)=-\sum_{j=1}^h(2+x_j)c_j\frac{H(x)}{x-x_j}.
\]
Consequently the coefficient rows on the right are a basis of \(\Lambda^*\).
Write \(Q(x)=\sum_{l=0}^m a_lx^l\), with \(a_m=1\), and set
\[
 A_i(X)=\sum_{l=i+2}^m a_lX^{l-i-1}\qquad(0\le i<h).
\]
Polynomial division by \(x-T\) shows that
\[
 [x^i]\frac{H(x)}{x-x_j}
       =\frac{A_i(x_j)-A_i(T)}{x_j-T}.
\]
The \(A_i\) have successive degrees \(h,h-1,\ldots,1\), leading coefficient one and constant zero.
They therefore form an integral basis of the polynomials appearing in (4); the common minus sign in the coefficient rows does not affect their integral span.

<a id="norm"></a>
## An arithmetic lower bound for every dual vector

Every nonzero \(y\in\Lambda^*\) satisfies
\[
                         \|y\|_1>\frac h{72}.                           \tag{5}
\]
Indeed let \(A\ne0\) represent \(y\) as in (4) and put \(\alpha=A(T)\).
The irreducible trace polynomial \(Q\) has degree \(m\), so \(\alpha\) is an irrational algebraic integer: otherwise the polynomial \(A-\alpha\) of degree less than \(m\) would vanish at \(T\), forcing \(A\) to be constant and hence zero.

The nine points \(0,\alpha,\ldots,8\alpha\) on \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) are distinct, and their nine circular gaps sum to one.
Two endpoints of a gap of size at most \(1/9\) give integers \(1\le s\le8\) and \(r\) with \(|s\alpha-r|\le1/9\).
This also treats the gap that crosses zero, by subtracting the appropriate integer.
The nonzero algebraic integer
\[
                         \gamma=(T+2)(s\alpha-r)
\]
has absolute norm at least one in \(\mathbb Q(T)\).
Its distinguished conjugate has modulus at most \((T+2)/9\le1/2\).
At an internal conjugate, (4) gives
\[
 \gamma_j=(2+x_j)(s\alpha-r)+s(x_j-T)y_j,
 \qquad
 |\gamma_j|\le\frac12+36|y_j|,
\]
because \(-2<x_j<2<T\le5/2\).
The field norm counts all \(m\) embeddings, including multiplicities if \(\gamma\) lies in a proper subfield.
It follows that
\[
 1\le |N_{\mathbb Q(T)/\mathbb Q}\gamma|
     \le\frac12\prod_{j=1}^h\left(\frac12+36|y_j|\right)
     \le\frac12\left(\frac12+\frac{36\|y\|_1}{h}\right)^h.
\]
If \(\|y\|_1\le h/72\), the last expression is at most \(1/2\), proving (5).
No lower bound for the individual trace-root separations or for \(2+x_j\) is required.

<a id="covering"></a>
## Covering all translates by a positive box

Use the [cube covering bound from Banaszczyk's Gaussian transference lemmas](../audits/literature/Ban95/Yin_U001.md#cube-covering): for every full lattice \(L\subset\mathbb R^h\),
\[
 \mu_\infty(L)\lambda_{1,1}(L^*)
               \le4h\sqrt{\frac{\log(8h)}\pi}.                         \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\mu_\infty\) is the covering radius for the maximum norm, and \(\lambda_{1,1}\) is the least nonzero sum norm in the dual.
The explicit specialization uses Lemma 1.5 on p.222 with \(U=aB_\infty\), \(V=4hB_1\), \(a=\sqrt{\log(8h)/\pi}\).
Lemma 2.10 on p.226 gives \(\beta(U)\le1/4\).
The \(p=1\) moment estimate in Lemma 2.6 on p.225, followed by Markov's inequality, gives \(\alpha(V)\le\beta(V)\le1/4\), so \(\beta(U)+2\alpha(V)\le3/4<1\).
The norms in (6) follow by scaling the covering radius and the first minimum in that lemma.

Equations (5) and (6) imply \(\mu_\infty(\Lambda)<\kappa_h\).
Every translate of \(\Lambda\), including the translate associated with any integer \(q\), therefore contains a vector within maximum distance \(\kappa_h\) of \((\kappa_h+1)\mathbf1\).
Choose such a vector; it has
\[
             1\le c_j\le2\kappa_h+1,\qquad
             \sum_jc_j\le B_h.                                      \tag{7}
\]
For \(q\ge\lceil B_h\rceil\), (7) is strictly inside the Pisot simplex because \(T/2>0\).
Its integral polynomial \(f\) is consequently a Pisot minimal polynomial of exactly degree \(d\), with the required constant and return identity.

The [exact root equation](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) is
\[
 \theta-q-E=\frac{E}{\theta-1}
       +\sum_j\frac{b_j}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_j}.
\]
Here \(\theta>q\), \(0<E\le1/2\), and \(\sum_jb_j=\sum_j(2+x_j)c_j<4B_h\).
All denominators in the sum exceed \(q-2\), while \(\theta-1>q-1\).
These inequalities prove (1).

<a id="prescribed"></a>
## Prescribing the constant along changing degrees

Under (2), the threshold in (1) holds for all sufficiently large even degrees, and its upper error tends to zero uniformly in the supplied Salem polynomial.
The degrees of a Salem sequence tending to one must tend to infinity: in any bounded degree, the conjugate bound \(|z|\le2\) bounds every integral coefficient of its minimal polynomial and leaves only finitely many possibilities.
Since \(E\to0\), equation (1) proves the forward assertion.

Conversely, the [positive delay-one return](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) and the [two-point inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) give an exterior reciprocal unit with trace excess less than the Pisot excess.
A reciprocal quadratic exterior factor would have integral trace at least three and excess at least one.
Thus a sequence with Pisot excess tending to zero eventually has a Salem exterior factor, whose root tends to one.
This converse may lower the degree; the forward construction preserves it exactly.

The [selected-constant construction](Yin_C_G0345_v31.md#statement) already gives error \(O(d/q)\) at selected constants in controlled intervals.
The present dual-norm argument supplies every sufficiently large prescribed constant, replacing the [exponential uniform threshold](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v33.md#degree-uniform) and losing only a factor \(\sqrt{\log d}\) against the selected-constant error.
It does not establish error \(O(d/q)\) uniformly at every prescribed constant, nor an obstruction to that stronger estimate.
It produces inverses of supplied Salem polynomials and does not construct a new Salem sequence or prove a positive uniform Mahler gap.
