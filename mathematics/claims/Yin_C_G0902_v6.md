# Exceptional coefficient parameters for small measures in the Chebyshev radical closure

2026-11-24T20:10 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Counting the parameters that admit a small unit

Fix an even integer \(n\ge4\) and let \(q\ge1\) range over integers with \(4^{n-1}\le2M\), where \(p=q+1\) and \(M=pq\).
Use the [Chebyshev units](Yin_C_G0902_v1.md#minimum) \(T,Y\), the trace field \(B=\mathbb Q(T+T^{-1})\), and the real field \(K=\mathbb Q(T)\) of degree \(2n\).
Let \(\mathcal R_{q,n}\) consist of those nonzero algebraic numbers some positive power of which belongs to \(\mu_\infty\langle T,Y\rangle\).
Write \(m(\gamma)\) for the total logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\).
For every fixed \(C>0\),
\[
 \#\{q:\ 4^{n-1}\le2q(q+1),\
           0<m(\gamma)\le C\text{ for some }\gamma\in\mathcal R_{q,n}\}
 \ \le\ \exp\!\big(O_C(n^{3/2}\log(2n))\big).                   \tag{1}
\]
The count is over the whole unbounded parameter interval.
In particular the minimum positive measure tends to infinity with \(q\) for each fixed \(n\).
More generally if \(N=N(X)\ge4\) and \(N^{3/2}\log(2N)=o(\log X)\), at most \(X^{o(1)}\) integers \(q\in[X,2X]\) admit measure in \((0,C]\) for any admissible even \(n\le N\).
This follows by summing (1) over at most \(N\) degrees.

Write \(U=\ker(N_{K/B}:\mathcal O_K^\times\to\mathcal O_B^\times)\).
For all sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly in \(n\), a nontorsion \(u\in U\) generates \(K\) unless \(3\mid n\), \(v_2(n-2)\) is even, and
\[
                     \log M\le\tfrac43(\log2)n^2+O(n).           \tag{2}
\]
Here \(v_2(a)\) is the exponent of \(2\) in a positive integer \(a\).
The exceptional field index can only be three.
The count (1) covers both indices, including degrees and heights permitted by (2).
It leaves open whether positive measures can tend to zero along exceptional parameters with \(n\to\infty\).

## The dyadic different and the degree of a relative unit

Put \(d=n-3\), \(B_j(x)=U_j((x+2)/2)\), \(C_d=B_d+B_{d-1}\), and
\[
 H(x)=x^{n-1}(x+4)+2(Mx-1)C_d(x),\qquad w=T+T^{-1}-2.
\]
Here \(U_j\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
The [trace computation](Yin_C_G0902_v1.md#conjugates) gives \(H(w)=0\), \(0<w<1/M\), and \(H\) is Eisenstein at two.
There is one place of \(B\) over two, with completion \(B_2/\mathbb Q_2\) totally ramified of degree \(n\) and uniformizer \(w\).
Normalize \(v_{B_2}(w)=1\), so \(v_{B_2}(2)=n\).
For \(a_j\in\mathbb Q_2\), the nonzero summands in \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_jw^j\) have valuations distinct modulo \(n\).
The valuation of the sum is their minimum, so integrality of the sum forces every \(a_j\in\mathbb Z_2\).
Thus \(\mathcal O_{B_2}=\mathbb Z_2[w]\), and the different is generated by \(H'(w)\).

Since \(M\) is even,
\[
 H'(x)/2\equiv (n/2)x^{n-1}+C_d'(x)\pmod2 .
\]
Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(B_{j+1}=xB_j+B_{j-1}\), with \(B_{-1}=0,\ B_0=1\).
Induction in this recurrence gives \(B_{2h+1}=xB_h^2\) and \(B_{2h}=B_h^2+B_{h-1}^2\).
All even-indexed polynomials have constant term one by the original recurrence.
Consequently \(\operatorname{ord}_x B_h=2^{v_2(h+1)}-1\), by descent in the odd-index identity.
Taking \(d=2h+1\), we obtain \(C_d'=B_h^2\) in characteristic two.
The least odd coefficient of \(H'/2\), in order of increasing exponent, therefore has exponent \(j=2^{v_2(n-2)}-2\le n-4\).
At \(w\) the lower terms have valuations at least \(n\), and the higher terms have valuations greater than \(j\).
The different exponent is
\[
                         v_{B_2}(H'(w))=n+2^{v_2(n-2)}-2.       \tag{3}
\]

Use the [relative-unit degree calculation](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#fields), valid for all sufficiently large \(q\) with an absolute cutoff.
For \(F=\mathbb Q(u)\), \(u\in U\) nontorsion, it gives \([K:F]\in\{1,3\}\), and index three requires \(3\mid n\).
In the latter case the involution \(\iota\) of \(K/B\) restricts nontrivially to \(F\), since \(\iota u=u^{-1}\ne u\).
Its fixed field in \(F\) is \(E=\mathbb Q(u+u^{-1})\subset B\), since \(u\) satisfies the corresponding quadratic over \(E\).
It follows that \([B:E]=3\).
The unique dyadic place restricts to \(E\), where the local degrees are again the full global degrees since \(B_2/\mathbb Q_2\) is totally ramified of degree \(n\).
The upper step \(B_2/E_2\) is totally and tamely ramified of degree three, with different exponent two.
Transitivity of the different gives \(v_{B_2}(H'(w))\equiv2\pmod3\).
As \(3\mid n\), (3) gives \(2^{v_2(n-2)}\equiv1\pmod3\), as asserted.

## Cancellation between the two relative norms

Suppose \([K:F]=3\), put \(D=[F:\mathbb Q]=2n/3\), and use the specified embeddings \(F\subset K\subset\mathbb R\).
The conjugates of \(T\) consist of the real pair \(T,T^{-1}\), the \(2(n-3)\) values of modulus one above the real trace roots in \((-4,0)\), and the four values \(\zeta,\bar\zeta,\zeta^{-1},\bar\zeta^{-1}\) above the nonreal trace pair, where \(r=|\zeta|>1\).
At each of the \(2(n-3)\) neutral embeddings of \(K\), complex conjugation acts on values as \(\iota\), so every relative norm-one unit has modulus one there.
The [index-three signature](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#fields) gives precisely one exterior and one interior conjugate of \(u\), both real and reciprocal.
All remaining conjugates of \(u\) are nonreal because a real conjugate of modulus one would equal \(1\) or \(-1\).
Thus \(F\) has exactly two real embeddings, interchanged by the restriction of \(\iota\).
At the specified real embedding of \(F\), \(|u|\ne1\).
Exactly one of its three extensions to \(K\) is real, since the other real embedding of \(K\) takes \(u\) to its distinct inverse.
The remaining two extensions are a complex conjugate pair and cannot be neutral, as they must still take \(u\) to the same real value.
Their images of \(T\) are therefore \(\zeta^s,\bar\zeta^s\) for some common \(s\in\{1,-1\}\).
Application of \(\iota\) gives the inverse triple at the other real embedding of \(F\), exhausting all six embeddings of \(K\) at which \(T\) has modulus different from one.

Put \(Z(x)=(px-q)/(p-qx)\), so \(Y=Z(T)\), \(Z(x^{-1})=Z(x)^{-1}\), and \(|Z(x)|=1\) on the unit circle.
The specified values of the relative norms are
\[
 N_{K/F}T=T r^{2s},\qquad N_{K/F}Y=Y|Z(\zeta)|^{2s}.
\]
At the other real embedding both values invert, and above every nonreal embedding of \(F\) all three factors in each norm have modulus one.
The unit
\[
 W=\frac{(N_{K/F}T)^{s(n-1)}}{N_{K/F}Y}\in\mathcal O_F^\times
\]
is positive, with inverse at the other real embedding and modulus one at all nonreal embeddings.

Uniformly for \(4^{n-1}\le2M\) and large \(q\), the [trace moduli](Yin_C_G0902_v1.md#conjugates) and the [positive value of \(Y\)](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#index) give
\[
 \log T=M^{-1/2}+O(M^{-3/2}),\quad
 2\log r=\log(2M)+O(n^2/M),\quad
 \log Y=(n-1)\log M+\log(2(2n-5))+O(n^2/M).
\]
The identity \(Z(\zeta)+p/q=(p+q)/(q(p-q\zeta))\) gives \(Z(\zeta)=-p/q+O(M^{-1})\).
Also \(\log(p/q)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/(2\sqrt M))=M^{-1/2}+O(M^{-3/2})\), using \(p-q=1\).
Thus \(\log|Z(\zeta)|=M^{-1/2}+O(M^{-1})\), and substitution yields
\[
 \log W=\log R_n+\frac{s(n-3)}{\sqrt M}+O(n^3/M),
       \qquad R_n=\frac{2^{n-2}}{2n-5}.                         \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(|\log W-\log R_n|\ll n/\sqrt M\), since \(n^2/\sqrt M\) is bounded in the admissible range.
For sufficiently large \(q\), \(W>1\) uniformly as \(n=O(\log M)\), \(n\ge6\), and \(R_n\ge R_6=16/7\).
Write \(A=2^{n-2}\), \(b=2n-5\).
The rational number \(A/b\) is not integral since \(b\ge7\) is odd, so \(bW-A\) is nonzero.
Its nonzero integer norm has a specified factor \(|bW-A|\ll A n/\sqrt M\), and the other \(D-1\) factors have absolute values at most \(A+b\).
Hence \(1\ll (An/\sqrt M)(A+b)^{D-1}\).
Taking logarithms and inserting \(D=2n/3\) proves (2), since \(\log(A+b)=(n-2)\log2+O(n2^{-n})\).

## Counting possible fields and parameters of full field degree

Fix \(C>0\).
The [radical norm descent](Yin_C_G0902_v2.md#radicals) takes each \(\gamma\) with \(0<m(\gamma)\le C\) to a nontorsion \(u\in U\) with \(m(u)\le C\), for every \(q\) above the same absolute cutoff after enlargement if necessary.
The monic irreducible polynomial \(P\) of \(u\) has degree \(e\le2n\).
For \(e\to\infty\), the [squarefree coefficient bound](Yin_C_G0901_v10.md#circle) gives
\[
 \log\|P\|_1\ll e\sqrt{a\log(2/a)},\qquad a=(\log(2e)+m(u))/e.
\]
For \(m(u)\le C\) the right side is \(O_C(\sqrt e\log(2e))\).
For the bounded remaining degrees the elementary bound \(\|P\|_1\le2^e\exp(C)\) suffices.
Counting the possible integer coefficients, then allowing all \(e\le2n\) and at most \(e\) choices of an actual root, gives at most \(\exp(O_C(n^{3/2}\log(2n)))\) possible embedded fields \(F=\mathbb Q(u)\subset\mathbb R\).

When \(F=K\) is one of these fixed fields, let \(S\) contain its archimedean places and all primes over divisors of \(2(2n-5)\).
The monic minimal polynomial \(X^n H(X+X^{-1}-2)\) gives \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-T)=H(0)=-2(2n-5)\).
Thus both \(T\) and \(1-T\) are \(S\)-units.
There are at most \(2n\) archimedean places and at most \(2n\) primes above each rational prime, so the tuple group \((\mathcal O_{K,S}^\times)^2\) has rank \(O(n\log(2n))\) by the \(S\)-unit theorem.
The equation \(T+(1-T)=1\) has two nonzero terms, hence is nondegenerate.
The [Amoroso–Viada bound, Theorem 6.2](../audits/literature/AV09/Yin_U001.md#retained-primary-statement) gives at most \(16^{64(3+\operatorname{rank}((\mathcal O_{K,S}^\times)^2))}=\exp(O(n\log(2n)))\) such tuples.
For each possible \(T\), the value \(w=T+T^{-1}-2>0\) gives
\[
                          M=1/w-w^{n-2}(w+4)/(2C_d(w)).
\]
Here \(C_d(w)>0\), and \(M=q(q+1)\) determines the positive integer \(q\) uniquely.

## Packing the cubic norms in a fixed field

In the index-three case fix an embedded candidate field \(F\).
By (4), all its possible values \(\log W\), as \(q\) and \(s\) vary, lie in an interval about \(\log R_n\) of length \(O(n2^{-n})\).
For two distinct such units put \(v=W_1/W_2\) and \(\delta=|\log v|>0\).
At the second real embedding the ratio is \(v^{-1}\), and at the \(D-2\) other embeddings it has modulus one.
The nonzero integer norm of \(1-v\) consequently gives
\[
                 1\le4\sinh^2(\delta/2)\,2^{D-2}.
\]
It follows that \(\delta\gg2^{-D/2}=2^{-n/3}\) with an absolute constant, using the estimate for \(\sinh(\delta/2)\) on \(0\le\delta\le1\) and the trivial bound when \(\delta>1\).
Hence the number of possible units \(W\) is \(O(1)\) in each field and is at most one for sufficiently large \(n\).

## Counting preimages of a fixed cubic norm

For this paragraph replace \(q\) by a positive real variable \(t\), \(p=t+1\), and \(M=t(t+1)\).
Write \(H_t\) for the same trace polynomial and \(P_t(x)=x^nH_t(x+x^{-1}-2)\).
The \(2n\) coefficients below the monic leading term are polynomials in \(t\) of degree at most two.
For each sign \(s\) define polynomials \(A_s(t,x),B_s(t,x)\) by
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 s&A_s&B_s\\ \hline
  1&x^{n-1}(p-tx)&px-t\\
 -1&p-tx&x^{n-1}(px-t).
 \end{array}
\]
Then \(A_s/B_s=x^{s(n-1)}/Z(x)\) and their degrees in \(t,x\) are at most \(1,n\) respectively.
For the roots \(x_1,\ldots,x_{2n}\) of \(P_t\), take the symmetric product
\[
 \mathscr D_s(t,z)
   =\prod_{\substack{J\subset\{1,\ldots,2n\}\\ |J|=3}}
       \left(z\prod_{i\in J}B_s(t,x_i)-\prod_{i\in J}A_s(t,x_i)\right).
\]
Put \(N=\binom{2n}{3}\).
Before symmetric reduction the direct \(t\)-degree is at most \(3N\) and the total root degree is at most \(3nN\).
Expressing the symmetric coefficients by elementary symmetric functions preserves this last bound with weight \(j\) on the \(j\)-th function.
Substituting the coefficients of \(P_t\) proves \(\mathscr D_s\in\mathbb Q[t,z]\) with \(t\)-degree at most \((3+6n)N\).
Its leading coefficient in \(z\) is nonzero, since in the admissible range no root is zero, \(t/p\), or \(p/t\).
Indeed the latter two numbers yield trace \(1/M\) and \(H_t(1/M)>0\).

On the connected interval of real \(t\) with \(4^{n-1}\le2M\) and \(t\) above the absolute cutoff, the same [alternating signs and negative second Newton sum](Yin_C_G0902_v1.md#conjugates) give \(n-2\) distinct real roots of \(H_t\) and a distinct nonreal pair.
No trace is \(0\) or \(-4\), so the \(2n\) roots of \(P_t\) are simple as well.
The unique \(T>1\) over \(w>0\), and the unique exterior \(\zeta\) over the trace with positive imaginary part, are real analytic on the interior by simplicity and uniqueness, and continuous at any included endpoint.
Using these roots in the formula of [the cubic norm calculation](#cubic) defines a positive real analytic function
\[
 W_s(t)=\frac{(T|\zeta|^{2s})^{s(n-1)}}{Z(T)|Z(\zeta)|^{2s}} .
\]
It realizes the factor indexed by \(T,\zeta^s,\bar\zeta^s\), whence \(\mathscr D_s(t,W_s(t))=0\).
Formula (4) also holds here, since its input estimates use the trace root equation, the real sign and Newton bounds, and the two displayed rational function identities, all valid for real \(t\).
For fixed \(n\) the coefficient \(s(n-3)\ne0\) in (4) shows \(W_s(t)\) is nonconstant as \(t\to\infty\).

For a fixed candidate \(W_0\in F\) remove the highest power of \(z-W_0\) dividing \(\mathscr D_s(t,z)\) in \(\mathbb C[t,z]\), leaving \(D_s(t,z)\).
Then \(D_s(t,W_0)\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \((3+6n)N\).
The identity \(D_s(t,W_s(t))=0\) holds wherever \(W_s(t)\ne W_0\).
These points are dense on the interval by nonconstancy and real analyticity, so the identity extends by continuity to every admissible point of the interval.
Each \(t\) with \(W_s(t)=W_0\) is thus a zero of \(D_s(t,W_0)\), even if some other triple has constant value \(W_0\).
Both signs together give at most \(2(3+6n)N=O(n^4)\) integer parameters for this \(W_0\).
Multiplication by the field count proves (1) for all \(q\) above the absolute cutoff.
The finitely many smaller positive integers contribute at most that absolute cutoff to the count for every \(n\), independently of their radical measures.
