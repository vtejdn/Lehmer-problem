# Sharp cost of prescribed even contact orders at every separating exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary fixed even multiplicities

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_s\) be distinct monic reciprocal irreducible minima of nontorsion units, with \(h_i=\log M(P_i)\).
Prescribe positive integers \(u_i\) and put \(S=\sum_i u_i h_i\).
An exponent \(n\ge2\) is admissible if \(c\mapsto c^n\) separates all roots of the combined list.
For either sign \(\varepsilon\) and every integer width sequence \(b=b(n)\to\infty\), let \(H_n\) be the least one-norm of an integer row \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) such that
\[
 \prod_i P_i^{2u_i}\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
 A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y),\qquad A(c^n)\ne0\quad(P_i(c)=0).
\]
Such rows exist at every sufficiently large admissible exponent and \(\log H_n/n\to S\) there.
For each \(\gamma>0\) one fixed width suffices for upper slope \(S+\gamma\) along this entire tail.
At an inadmissible exponent the two original roots over the same node cannot both equal \(-\varepsilon A/A_b^*\) there.
The multiplicities and the polynomials are fixed as \(n\) varies.

<a id="jets"></a>
## Separating the numerator and denominator derivatives at one radius

Consider a nonzero integral graph \(U_0(X^n)-XV_0(X^n)\) of arbitrary width.
Reverse both rows at their common width, and remove a common initial monomial.
Up to a factor which is a unit at every nonzero point, the graph has terms \(u_j x^{-nj}-v_j x^{1-nj}\).
Use the [coefficient-index limits and envelope](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) along a sequence of admissible exponents with bounded normalized log row norm.
At any exterior radius \(R\), write \(k,k+d\) for the extreme maximizing indices and \(z_c=(R/c)^n\) at a root \(c\) of that modulus.
After multiplication by \(x^{nk}\), the exponents in the two columns of offset \(j\) are \(-nj\) and \(1-nj\).
Scale the coefficient pairs in the block by \(R^{-nj}\) and divide by their largest scaled norm as in the [double contact equations](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#matrices).
Call the resulting coefficients \(U_j,V_j\), with maximum coordinate magnitude one.

For a node \(c\) requiring order \(2v\), and any polynomial \(Q\) of degree at most \(2v-1\), apply \(Q(x\,d/dx)\) to the graph after the multiplication by \(x^{nk}\).
At the node this yields
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{d}\bigl(Q(-nj)U_j-cQ(1-nj)V_j\bigr)z_c^j
      =O(e^{-\eta n}) .                                                 \tag{1}
\]
The normalizing scalar for the whole block is common to every equation at this radius up to a factor of modulus one.
All terms outside the block have a strictly smaller slope in the finite truncation.
The tail estimate remains valid for every fixed derivative order \(l\), since \(\max_{j>J}(1+nj)^l R^{-nj}\le(1+n(J+1))^l R^{-(J+1)n}\) for fixed \(J,l\) and sufficiently large \(n\).
A smaller \(\eta>0\) absorbs polynomial factors on the subsequence.

Take the unique polynomials \(Q_{e,l}\) of degree below \(2v\), for \(e=0,1\) and \(0\le l<v\), whose expansion at \(e\) is \((s-e)^l\bmod(s-e)^v\) and which vanish to order \(v\) at \(1-e\).
They exist by the Chinese remainder theorem in \(\mathbb Q[s]\) and form a basis.
In (1) replace \(n\) in the arguments of \(Q_{e,l}\) by a formal variable \(X\) and divide that row by \(X^l\).
Both resulting coefficient polynomials are regular at \(X=0\).
On columns of type \(e\) their \(Q\)-part there is \((-j)^l\), and on columns of the other type it is zero.
Thus the specialized rows prescribe, separately in each coordinate, zeros through Euler derivative order \(v-1\) at \(z_c\).
As \(z_c\ne0\), these are equivalent to ordinary vanishing of order \(v\).
The actual equations at \(X=n\) retain exponentially small errors after the row divisions.

At a radius assign half-orders \(v_c\ge0\), where \(v_c=0\) requires no even equation.
Put \(m=\sum_{|c|=R}v_c\).
For \(m>0\) the \(2m\) equations on polynomial pairs of degrees at most \(m-1\) give a square matrix.
At \(X=0\), evaluating phases at any positive exponent separating the roots makes both coordinate polynomials divisible by \(\prod_{v_c>0}(Y-z_c)^{v_c}\), so the matrix is invertible.
Its entries are polynomials in \(X\) and the algebraic phases with fixed algebraic coefficients.
By the [grouped determinant argument](Yin_C_G0345_v436.md#transfer), on every admissible residue class the exact determinant at \(X=n\) has modulus at least \(\exp(-o(n))\).
Indeed grouping the finitely many phase bases after powering by a common modulus leaves a nonzero polynomial coefficient, as is seen by evaluating at \(X=0\) at a positive representative of that class.
The cofactors grow only polynomially.
If \(d<m\), padding the normalized block to degree \(m-1\) would make a vector of norm one exponentially small by (1) and this inverse estimate.
Thus the index jump at \(R\) is at least \(m\).
Integrating these jumps at all exterior radii for the required even orders gives the lower slope \(\sum_i u_i h_i=S\) at any widths.

<a id="odd"></a>
## The extra equation when one contact has odd order

Lower the required order at one factor \(P_i\) from \(2u_i\) to \(2u_i-1\).
Its even base order is now \(2(u_i-1)\), while all other factors keep their previous even orders.
The lower slope from these base equations is \(S-h_i\).
Choose one exterior root \(c_*\) of \(P_i\), and write \(v=u_i-1\).
We show that its one remaining equation forces a strictly larger slope, uniformly in width for this fixed list.

Suppose there were a sequence realizing slope at most \(S-h_i+o(1)\).
As in the [equality case and transfer across radii](Yin_C_G0345_v436.md#transfer), every jump equals the required sum \(m\) of half-orders at its radius, with no other positive jumps.
The first coefficient pair \(w_0\) is a nonzero integer pair of size \(\exp(o(n))\).
Visit the distinct base radii in decreasing order down to \(|c_*|\), inserting that final radius if it has no positive base order.
At \(R\) normalize \(w_{k+j}\) by multiplication by \(gR^{-nj}\), with \(k\) the sum of preceding jumps and \(g=\prod_{R'>R}R'^{-nm_{R'}}\).
The last normalized pair at a radius is exactly the first at the next.
The extreme coefficients at \(R\) have normalized slope zero and coefficients outside the block have strictly negative slopes, yielding (1) with exponentially small absolute errors also in this normalization.

To transfer through the block, fix its constant pair \(y_0\), solve the \(2m\) even equations on the remaining \(m\) pairs by Cramer's rule, and set \(L_R(Y)=\prod_{v_c>0}(Y-z_c)^{v_c}\).
At formal \(X=0\) the solution is \(y_0L_R(Y)/L_R(0)\), since each coordinate has degree at most \(m\) and the indicated zeros with multiplicities.
Thus the determinant on the unknown coordinates is nonzero at every separating phase representative.
The same recurrence bound gives a subexponential inverse on the admissible exponents.
Passing through a finite number of these blocks keeps the error exponentially small.
For an empty block take \(L_R=1\) and the identity transfer.

At \(c_*\) apply the additional equation of degree \(2v\) in the Euler derivative using \(Q(s)=s^v(s-1)^v\).
In its formal row divide by \(X^v\).
At \(X=0\) the \(Q\)-parts of the numerator and denominator columns are \(j^v\) and \((-j)^v\), respectively, including the constant row \(v=0\).
The specialized functional on the two polynomial coordinates is therefore evaluation at \(z_{c_*}\) of \((Y\,d/dY)^v(U-(-1)^v c_* V)\).
The formal block solution makes this a nonzero scalar times \((1,-(-1)^v c_*)y_0\).
Indeed \(L_R\) has exact order \(v\) at \(z_{c_*}\), since the positive representative separates the roots, and \(z_{c_*}\ne0\).

After composing the transfers and clearing their polynomial determinants, the resulting algebraic polynomial row \(p(X,\boldsymbol z)\) satisfies \(|p(n,\boldsymbol z(n))w_0|\le e^{-\eta' n}\) for some \(\eta'>0\).
At \(X=0\) and every admissible positive representative \(a_0\) it equals a nonzero scalar times \((1,-(-1)^v c_*)\), because earlier transfers at zero are scalar multiples of the identity.
The [small-coefficient power-sum bound](Yin_C_G0345_v436.md#small-coefficients) and [rational descent](Yin_C_G0345_v436.md#descent) now apply.
Explicitly, group the phase monomial bases on one infinite admissible class \(n=a_0+qt\) so their \(q\)-th powers have pairwise nontorsion quotients.
The coefficients of the sum after multiplication by \(w_0\) have height \(o(t)\), so every grouped polynomial row evaluated at \(n\) annihilates \(w_0\) eventually.
Their sum and each of its Galois conjugates annihilate the same rational pair, hence the corresponding polynomial determinant vanishes identically.
At zero this would make \((-1)^v c_*\) rational, impossible for an algebraic unit of modulus greater than one.
The weakened list therefore costs at least \(S-h_i+\eta_i\) in normalized log norm eventually, for some \(\eta_i>0\).

## Genuine signed rows and a conditional bound at any fixed even order

Apply the [integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) at the specified orders.
It gives a two-row graph at a fixed sufficiently large width \(w\) with normalized log norm at most \(S\) plus an arbitrarily small positive excess, since the weighted measure in its numerator is \(2S\).
Choose that excess below every strict gain \(\eta_i\) and below the requested slack.
If the two rows shared the minimum of \(c^n\) for \(P_i(c)=0\), removing it would lower the order by exactly one at that orbit and preserve all other required contacts, by separability and admissibility.
The powered minimum contributes \(nh_i\) in log measure.
The quotient row of largest norm has log measure at least its log norm minus \(w\log2\), contradicting the upper slope after restoration.
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) and [width padding](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) preserve all required orders and simultaneous nonvanishing at bounded norm cost.
Letting the fixed slack decrease to zero proves the limit.

For a fixed even \(a\ge2\), require multiplicity \(a\) and use the budget \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^{(a-1)n}\).
At any prescribed \(b(n)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\), a finite list of distinct positive irreducible measures of sum below \((1-1/a)B\), where \(B=(\log2)/4\), occurs together at every sufficiently large integer and either sign.
Indeed the [all-power representatives](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) have no collisions across distinct measures, and the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) makes them reciprocal units.
For \(u_i=a/2\), the slack is exactly \(2S<(a-1)B\).
Consequently, if fewer than \(r\) distinct values occur on every passing row along any infinite subsequence at this order, then the unrestricted infimum satisfies
\[
                \lambda\ \ge\ (1-1/a)\,\frac{B}{r(r+1)/2}.
\]
Otherwise the [degree-preserving positive multiples](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#multiples) of one sufficiently small irreducible measure would yield \(r\) distinct values below the total budget.
