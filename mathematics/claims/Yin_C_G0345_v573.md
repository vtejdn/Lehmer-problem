# Exact periodic densities of dissociated powers away from the unit circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## The maximizing period and the finite interval error

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit with no conjugate of modulus one and let \(k\ge1\).
Write \(a_N\) for the largest size of a subset of \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) supporting no nonzero polynomial divisible by \(F=P^k\) with coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
Its [limiting density](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement) \(D_k(P)=\lim a_N/N\) is rational and computable, and a computable periodic set of nonnegative exponents attains it.
There are also computable integers \(p\ge1\) and \(N_0\ge0\) such that
\[
                     a_{N+p}=a_N+pD_k(P)\qquad(N\ge N_0).
\]
Thus the deviations from the limiting line are eventually periodic at each such input, for arbitrary root multiplicities.

<a id="carries"></a>
## A finite graph for returning carries

Drungilas, Jankauskas and Šiurys give a finite remainder graph for bounded-digit multiples of monic polynomials without roots on the unit circle, including repeated roots. [Theorem 18 and the remainder bounds](../audits/literature/DJS18/Yin_U001.md#djs18-u001)
We give the bounds needed here to identify every returning digit word.
Use the coefficient lattice \(\mathbb Z[X]/(F)\), the integer matrix \(A\) of multiplication by \(X\), and the vector \(v\) of the class of one.
Reading a ternary digit \(e\) sends \(z\) to \(Az+ev\), with digits read from highest exponent to lowest.
Thus a word starting at zero ends at zero precisely when its polynomial is zero modulo \(F\).
Let \(V_s,V_u\) be the real invariant generalized eigenspaces for eigenvalues of modulus less than and greater than one, respectively, grouping complex conjugates together.
In this direct sum write \(A_s,A_u,v_s,v_u\) for the restrictions and projections.
On a path from zero back to zero every vertex satisfies
\[
 \|z_s\|\le\sum_{j\ge0}\|A_s^jv_s\|,
 \qquad
 \|z_u\|\le\sum_{j\ge1}\|A_u^{-j}v_u\|.
\]
The first bound expands forward from the initial zero and the second solves backward from the terminal zero.
Both sums have effective finite upper bounds in the ambient Euclidean norm.
For example, in algebraic bases for the two spaces take powers until their induced matrix norms for \(A_s\) and \(A_u^{-1}\) are each less than one, then sum geometric bounds on blocks and multiply by an upper bound on the basis change norm.
The powers tend to zero even for nontrivial Jordan blocks.
Real algebraic root isolation, linear algebra and strict rational upper estimates suffice for these operations.
A zero-dimensional summand contributes zero.
Enumerate all lattice points in an integer coordinate box containing the resulting ball and retain their ternary transitions within the box.
Trim to vertices reachable from zero and able to reach zero.
The graph includes every zero-to-zero path and every path it records uses exact integer remainders.
For polynomials with unit-circle roots finiteness of this returning set can fail, as the [fixed quartic Salem example](Yin_C_G0371_v1.md#statement) shows.

<a id="masks"></a>
## Recognizing whole sets of allowed exponents

Read a binary mask in descending exponent order.
A zero bit allows only digit zero and a one bit allows all three digits.
Keep a subset \(T\) of the nonzero vertices of the trimmed graph, starting from the empty subset.
These are the carries obtainable by a word on the bits already read that has used a nonzero digit and has stayed in the graph.
While the prefix is admissible no such word has returned to zero, since appending zeros to a zero carry preserves it.
To read a bit, follow its allowed transitions from \(T\) and also from zero, which is always available via all previous digits zero.
Reject a transition reaching zero from \(T\).
A nonzero digit from zero cannot land at zero since \(v\ne0\).
Otherwise store precisely the nonzero destinations of these transitions in the trimmed graph.
Any word witnessing a relation travels entirely in that graph, and a first nontrivial return is detected at its last digit.
Conversely any rejected transition gives a supported relation on the prefix, which extends to the full mask by zero digits.
This proves by induction that the nonrejected paths from the empty subset are exactly the admissible masks.

<a id="period"></a>
## Maximizing the mean and computing the transient

Keep the reachable subset states and weight each allowed edge by its bit.
Then \(a_N\) is the maximum weight of a length \(N\) path from the empty subset with free terminal state.
Let \(\delta=h/b\) be the largest mean weight of a directed cycle, with integers \(b>0,h\ge0\).
It is computable by enumerating simple cycles, and there is at least a zero loop at the empty subset.
Rescale every edge weight \(w\) to \(w'=bw-h\), so all cycles have nonpositive weight.
Removing cycles shows that the weight of any path from any reachable vertex is at most the largest weight \(B\ge0\) among simple paths, including empty paths.
A fixed path to a maximizing cycle followed by repetitions and partial repetitions gives paths of every length after that initial path, whose rescaled weights stay bounded below.
Including prefixes of the initial path gives a computable \(C\ge0\) with \(ba_N-hN\ge-C\) for every \(N\).
Consequently \(D_k(P)=\delta\).
If \(u\) is the label of the maximizing cycle, a fixed prefix followed by \(u^j\) is admissible for every \(j\).
Its suffix \(u^j\) is admissible by padding any relation with zero digits.
Reading these words in increasing exponent order gives the periodic exponent mask repeating the reversed word, at the required density.

For the exact transient, enumerate reachable pairs of vertex and integer accumulated rescaled weight within \([-C-B,B]\), starting at the empty subset with weight zero.
Drop a transition leaving this interval.
Every optimal path to a fixed length stays within the interval at every prefix, since its total is at least \(-C\) and its remaining suffix contributes at most \(B\).
The largest weight present after \(N\) steps is therefore \(ba_N-hN\).
The set of reachable pairs evolves deterministically among finitely many subsets, so one repeated subset gives computable \(N_0,p\) with this largest weight periodic thereafter.
This proves the stated recurrence.
