# Matching exterior roots of two polynomials from their leading coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cost of removing an exterior root

Let \(P,Q\) be complex monic polynomials of degree \(d\), agreeing in the first \(k\) coefficients below the leading one, \(1\le k\le d\).
Put \(L=\log M(P)+\log M(Q)\).
Suppose \(\lambda\) is a root of \(P\), with \(b=\log|\lambda|\in(0,A]\), and every root of \(Q\) has distance at least \(\Delta\) from \(\lambda\), where \(0<\Delta\le\min(1,b)\).
Then
\[
                    L\gg_A \frac{k b^2}{\log(2d/\Delta)}.             \tag{1}
\]
Coefficients, multiplicities, root signs and the number of exterior conjugates are unrestricted.
For a Salem input with \(a=\log\sigma\to0\), preserving a fixed positive fraction of the coefficients while removing all roots from a disk of inverse-polynomial radius around \(\sigma\) thus forces
\[
                \log M(Q)\gg \frac{d a^2}{\log(2d)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Here the constant may depend on the preserved fraction and the exponent in the radius.
The [input degree bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(a\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\).

In fact for two polynomials of bounded logarithmic measure the exterior roots can be matched with their multiplicities.
At a specified \(b\in(0,A]\), put \(p\) equal to the number of distinct roots of either polynomial with modulus greater than \(e^{b/2}\), so \(p\le 2L/b\).
Along any sequence with \(L\le A\), \(p\ge1\), and
\[
            \frac{k b}{p\log(2d)}\longrightarrow\infty,              \tag{3}
\]
there are disjoint clusters around the roots of modulus at least \(e^b\).
Each such cluster contains equally many roots of \(P\) and \(Q\) counting multiplicity, including any of their roots just below the threshold that belong to that cluster.
Any two roots in one cluster are at distance at most \(\exp(-c k b/p)\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
This matches the two multisets locally even when several conjugates of either input approach one another.
The claim applies to the complete zero sets of the polynomials under comparison.

<a id="rational"></a>
## A rational function of consecutive power sums

Newton's identities give equality of the power sums of \(P,Q\) at every order from 1 to \(k\).
Write \(\Gamma\) for the \(p\) distinct roots of modulus greater than \(e^{b/2}\) appearing in their union, and \(c_\gamma\) for multiplicity in \(P\) minus multiplicity in \(Q\).
Keep a common root in this set even when its signed multiplicity is zero.
The equal power sums give
\[
          |s_j|=\left|\sum_{\Gamma}c_\gamma\gamma^j\right|
                         \le 2d\,e^{jb/2},\qquad 1\le j\le k .         \tag{4}
\]
The bound follows by summing the complementary roots of the two polynomials.
Each distinct \(\gamma\) contributes more than \(b/2\) to \(L\) on at least one side.

For \(p\le k\), put \(n=k-p+1\) and define
\[
 D(z)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}(z-\gamma)=\sum_{j=0}^p u_jz^{p-j},
 \qquad
 H_n(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{c_\gamma\gamma^n}{z-\gamma}.
\]
The polynomial \(B_n=D H_n\) has degree at most \(p-1\).
In its coefficient of \(z^{p-1-j}\), \(0\le j<p\), the expansion at infinity gives precisely \(\sum_{h=0}^j u_{j-h}s_{n+h}\).
Every \(|u_j|\le 2^p e^L\) by the elementary symmetric functions.
For \(|z|\le e^A+1\), (4) now gives
\[
             |B_n(z)|\le 2d\, C_A^p e^{L+nb/2},                     \tag{5}
\]
for a constant \(C_A>1\) depending only on \(A\).
The \(p^2\) summands, the powers \(e^{hb/2}\) and the bounded powers of \(|z|\) are absorbed in \(C_A^p\).

<a id="counts"></a>
## Exact signed multiplicities on a union of small disks

Let \(0<\delta\le\min(1,b)/(32p)\).
Take disks of a common radius \(\rho\in[\delta,2\delta]\) around the points of \(\Gamma\), choosing the radius outside the finite set of tangencies.
Consider a connected component containing a center \(\lambda\) with \(e^b\le|\lambda|\le e^A\).
Its diameter is at most \(4p\delta\), and its boundary has total length at most \(4\pi p\delta\), including any inner boundary curves.
At every boundary point \(z\), all centers are at distance at least \(\delta\), so \(|D(z)|\ge\delta^p\).
The whole component lies in \(e^{3b/4}<|z|\le e^A+1\), using \(e^b-e^{3b/4}\ge b/4\).
In particular it contains no omitted root from the complement of \(\Gamma\).

The integral of \(z^{-n}H_n(z)\) around the positively oriented boundary, divided by \(2\pi i\), equals the signed multiplicity \(J\) of all roots in the component.
Multiplication by \(z^{-n}\) restores the integer residue \(c_\gamma\) at each center.
Formula (5) and the boundary bounds give
\[
             |J|\le 2d\, (C_A/\delta)^p e^{L-nb/4}.                 \tag{6}
\]
This estimate is independent of all mutual spacings inside the component.

To prove (1), if \(p>k/2\) then \(L\ge kb/4\), which suffices since \(b\le A\).
Otherwise take \(\delta=\Delta/(32p)\), giving \(n\ge k/2\).
The component through the marked root has diameter less than \(\Delta\) and contains only \(P\)-roots.
Therefore \(J\ge1\).
Taking logarithms in (6) yields
\[
      kb/8\le \log(2d)+L+p\log(32C_Ap/\Delta)
                \ll_A (L/b)\log(2d/\Delta),
\]
using \(b\le L\), \(b\le A\), and \(p\le2d\).
This proves (1), including the case of repeated roots in just one input.

For (3) take \(\delta=\exp(-kb/(32p))\).
Condition (3) ensures \(p=o(k)\), \(\delta\le\min(1,b)/(32p)\) eventually, and \(p\delta\le\exp(-kb/(40p))\) eventually.
Indeed \(k\le d\) and (3) imply \(b\gg1/d\), so \(\log(1/b)=O(\log(2d))\) when \(b<1\).
With \(n=(1-o(1))k\), the right side of (6) tends to zero uniformly over the components in question.
Every signed multiplicity is an integer, so it vanishes.
Since \(L\le A\) bounds every root modulus by \(e^A\), this covers every component meeting a center of modulus at least \(e^b\).
Pair the two root multisets within each such component in any order to obtain the stated matching.
