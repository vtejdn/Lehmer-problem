# The reflection group of a reciprocal unit with commensurable exterior log moduli

<a id="statement"></a>
## From the original conjugates to a positive algebraic unit

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial with \(M(P)>1\).
Suppose the logarithms of the moduli of its exterior roots span a one-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb Q\).
If necessary first use the [products of torsion classes](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) to replace \(P\) by a polynomial having the same measure and no two distinct roots with torsion quotient.
All elements in a class have the same modulus, and the common class size multiplies every log modulus, so the hypothesis is preserved.
In the rest of the proof \(P\) denotes this compressed polynomial, \(d\) its degree, and \(\Omega\) its set of roots.

There is a unique \(r>0\) for which the additive group generated by the log moduli is \(r\mathbb Z\).
Write \(w(x)=\log|x|/r\), and \(W=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\max(0,w(x))\).
The positive real algebraic unit \(\kappa=e^{2r}\) belongs to the group generated by \(\Omega\), and
\[
        M(P)=\kappa^{W/2},\qquad h(\kappa)\le2h(x)\quad(x\in\Omega),
\]
where \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The signed conjugates of \(\kappa\), meaning all Galois images of \(\kappa\) and their inverses, give a finite simply laced root system spanning the real vector space generated by the original units modulo torsion.
Its irreducible components are isomorphic and the original roots are weights with integral pairings.
The number \(\kappa\) strictly exceeds the moduli of its other conjugates, whose moduli belong to \(\{1,\kappa^{1/2},\kappa^{-1/2},\kappa^{-1}\}\).
Either its minimal polynomial is reciprocal or its degree is at most six.
The height and measure statements concern different quantities when the degrees vary.

<a id="reflection"></a>
## Complex conjugation on the full group of original units

Let \(G\) be the Galois group of the splitting field and \(c\) its complex conjugation for the chosen embedding.
Write \(\Gamma=\langle\Omega\rangle\) multiplicatively, and let \(V=(\Gamma/\Gamma_{\rm tors})\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R\), writing \([x]\) for the vector of a unit.
The integers \(w(x)\) for \(x\in\Omega\) have greatest common divisor one.
For every root, \(x c(x)=e^{2rw(x)}\), so Bézout's identity expresses \(\kappa\) as an integral monomial in these products.
It follows that \(\kappa\) is an algebraic unit and \(w(\kappa)=2\).
Taking a root with positive weight in \(x c(x)=\kappa^{w(x)}\) proves the height bound by subadditivity and Galois invariance of height.
The formula for \(M(P)\) follows by summing the positive logs.

The group \(H\) generated on \(\Gamma\) by \(G\) and the inversion map is finite and acts on \(\Omega\).
Give \(V\) an \(H\)-invariant Euclidean inner product by averaging a rational positive definite form and scale it so that \(a=[\kappa]\) has squared norm two.
The integer-valued homomorphism \(w\) on \(\Gamma\) extends linearly to \(V\).
Multiplying the identities for the original generators shows that \(s(x)=c(x)^{-1}=x\kappa^{-w(x)}\) on the entire group.
On \(V\) this is the orthogonal involution \(v\mapsto v-w(v)a\), whose fixed hyperplane is perpendicular to \(a\), since it sends \(a\) to \(-a\).
Consequently \(w(v)=(a,v)\).

The finite set \(\Phi=H a\) spans \(V\).
Indeed its span and its orthogonal complement are rational for the averaged form.
A nonzero orthogonal complement would contain a nonzero lattice vector \([u]\), after clearing denominators.
Then \(w(g[u])=0\) for every \(g\in G\), making every conjugate of \(u\) have modulus one, which contradicts [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
The set \(\Phi\) contains both signs of each vector and is preserved by each reflection \(s_{ha}=hsh^{-1}\).
Its scalar products with all original lattice vectors are integral, by transport of \(w\).
As all roots have squared length two, different nonopposite roots have inner products in \(\{0,\pm1\}\).
This gives a reduced simply laced crystallographic root system, and its root reflections also preserve the original root orbit modulo torsion.
The [finite diagram classification and models](../audits/literature/Hec18/Yin_U001.md#finite-classification) give types \(A,D,E\), because between different simple roots the only possible nonzero inner product is \(-1\).
The components are permuted transitively since \(H\) is transitive on \(\Phi\).

<a id="lifts"></a>
## Distinct unit lifts, the four other moduli and reciprocity

Each vector \(b\in\Phi\) has a unique lift in \(H\kappa\).
Indeed if \(h_1 a=h_2 a=b\), the two orthogonal reflections \(h_i s h_i^{-1}\) agree on \(V\).
They send any actual root to actual roots with the same vector, which coincide since torsion quotients between distinct original roots have been removed.
Their exact formulas on \(\Omega\) therefore give
\[
        (h_1\kappa)^{(b,[x])}=(h_2\kappa)^{(b,[x])}\qquad(x\in\Omega).
\]
The pairings on the right and left range over the same integers as the original \(w(x)\), since \(h_1^{-1}\) permutes \(\Omega\).
Their greatest common divisor is one, proving equality of the lifts.
Opposite vectors have inverse lifts, and \(H\kappa\) is exactly the union of the conjugates and inverse conjugates of \(\kappa\).
Write \(x_b\) for its element over \(b\).
The real log of its modulus is \(r(a,b)\), so the only possible moduli are the five stated levels, and the unique lift at the largest modulus is \(\kappa\).

A root orthogonal to \(a\) gives a conjugate of \(\kappa\) or its inverse on the unit circle.
This conjugate and its complex conjugate are inverses, proving reciprocity of the minimal polynomial.
If there is no orthogonal root, there can be just one component, and by transitivity no pair of roots is orthogonal.
A simple system of rank at least three would then have three roots of squared length two with all three pairwise products \(-1\).
Their sum would have squared length zero, contradicting their linear independence.
Thus the rank is at most two, giving at most six signed conjugates from the \(A_1,A_2\) models.
In the opposite direction, neither these five modulus levels nor the height comparison alone bounds the degree of the original polynomial by an absolute constant.
