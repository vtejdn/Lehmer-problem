# A fourth-power divisor of the ordinary row resultant at reciprocal contacts

<a id="statement"></a>
## The division values at all sums of two exponents

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(e\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(h=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Let \(S\) be a nonempty finite set of positive integers and \(L_S=\operatorname{lcm}_{p\in S}p\).
Let nonzero coprime rows \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) have \(\deg U+\deg V\ge2\).
Assume \(P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) for every \(p\in S\).
Write \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\ne0\) for their ordinary integer resultant using their actual degrees.
Let \(\mathcal D(S)\) consist of all positive divisors of the integers in \(S+S\), including sums with the same exponent twice.
Then the following is divisibility in the ring of integers of \(K\):
\[
        \left(\prod_{k\in\mathcal D(S)}\Phi_k(\alpha)\right)^4
             \ \bigm|\ R\,\big[L_S^2(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\big]^4 .            \tag{1}
\]
No auxiliary modulus or assumptions of good reduction enter (1).
The bound does not involve a row width or its leading coefficients.
For \(S=\{p\}\), for instance,
\[
                 \log |R|\ge (8/e)ph-O_P(\log(2p)).                        \tag{2}
\]
This proves exponential growth of the ordinary resultant with the exponent for every fixed seed, even at prime exponents where its [valuation at the exponent prime itself](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v244.md#sharp) can be as small as \(e\).

There are computable growing sets of comparable primes on which every common pair for a fixed seed has \(\log|R|/\sum_{p\in S}p\to\infty\).
One can also choose shrinking relative prime intervals and common pairs of asymptotically sharp coefficient cost for any fixed all-power reciprocal seed, with this same divergence for **every** pair on the chosen sets.
In particular bounding \(|R|\) by any fixed power of the row norm removes all asymptotically optimal pairs for that seed on those sets.

<a id="local"></a>
## Two double zeros and a quotient of length four times the collision valuation

Fix \(p,q\in S\), allowing \(p=q\), and a finite prime of \(K\).
Write \(O\) for its discrete valuation ring and \(v\) for the integral valuation.
Set \(c=\alpha,\ z=c^p,\ w=c^{-q},\ d=z-w\ne0\).
Both roots \(c,c^{-1}\) of the reciprocal polynomial are used.
In the variable \(t=Y-z\) form the finite \(O\)-module and ring
\[
                              A=O[t]/(t^2,(t+d)^2).
\]
It is generated over \(O\) by \(1,t\) and has length \(4v(d)\).
Indeed multiplication by \(d^2+2dt\) on \(O[t]/t^2\) has triangular matrix of determinant \(d^4\), and \(A\) is its cokernel.
The claim includes \(v(d)=0\), when \(A=0\).

The value and first derivative at \(c\) imply that
\[
          pU(Y)+\left(pc+\tfrac c z(Y-z)\right)V(Y)
\]
is divisible by \((Y-z)^2\) in \(O[Y]\).
Its value vanishes at \(z\), and its derivative there is zero by differentiating \(U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\).
At \(c^{-1}\) the analogous expression with \(q,c^{-1},w\) is divisible by \((Y-w)^2\).
Thus the two expressions vanish in \(A\).
Their coefficient matrix on \((U,V)\) has determinant \(D_0+D_1 t\), with
\[
                 D_0=pq(c^{-1}-c)+p c^{-1}d/w,\qquad D_1\in O .
\]
The adjugate and \(t^2=0\) show that \(D_0^2\) annihilates the ideal generated by the images of \(U,V\) in \(A\).

If \(v(d)>v(q)+v(c^{-1}-c)\), the first term of \(D_0\) has strictly smaller valuation than the second, so \(v(D_0)=v(pq)+v(c^{-1}-c)\).
The submodule of \(A\) killed by \(D_0^2\) has length at most \(4v(D_0)\), by the elementary divisors of a two-generator torsion module over a discrete valuation ring.
Hence \(A/(U,V)\) has length at least \(4v(d)-4v(pq(c^{-1}-c))\).
When the displayed condition on \(v(d)\) fails, this lower bound is nonpositive and remains valid.

The target of the ordinary Sylvester map consists of polynomials of degree less than \(\deg U+\deg V\).
It maps onto \(A/(U,V)\), since it contains \(1,Y\) and these span \(A\) over \(O\).
The image of the Sylvester map vanishes there.
Its cokernel over \(O\) has length \(v(R)\) because its determinant is the nonzero ordinary resultant.
This proves
\[
               4v(\alpha^{p+q}-1)\le v(R)+4v\big(pq(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\big)  \tag{3}
\]
at every finite prime, since \(w\) is a unit.
In particular the estimate keeps the primes above \(p,q,2\), the ramified primes and the primes where the two reciprocal labels coincide after reduction.

<a id="aggregate"></a>
## Combining all exponent sums without counting the same ideal twice

Put \(I_n=(\alpha^n-1)\).
The Euclidean algorithm in the exponents gives \(I_n+I_m=I_{\gcd(n,m)}\), using the identity \(\alpha^n-1-\alpha^{n-m}(\alpha^m-1)=\alpha^{n-m}-1\) for \(n>m\).
For any finite set \(B\) of positive exponents one consequently has the exact identity of ideals
\[
         \bigcap_{n\in B} I_n =
             \left(\prod_{k:\,\exists n\in B,\ k\mid n}\Phi_k(\alpha)\right).
\]
Indeed at each finite prime the maximum of the valuations \(v(I_n)\) is the alternating inclusion-exclusion sum of the minima over all nonempty subsets.
Such a minimum is the valuation of \(I_{\gcd(n:\,n\text{ in the subset})}\).
Expanding \(\alpha^j-1\) into cyclotomic factors now counts each represented divisor exactly once by inclusion-exclusion.
This argument allows different cyclotomic values to share prime ideals.
Take \(B=S+S\) and use (3), since each \(pq\) divides \(L_S^2\).
The result is (1).

At the archimedean places the standard [logarithmic-form estimate for nontorsion rotations](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) gives
\[
       \log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^n-1)|=nh+O_P(\log(2n)).
\]
At a conjugate of modulus greater than one factor out its \(n\)-th power, and at a conjugate of modulus less than one the remaining logarithm is bounded.
For a conjugate \(z\) of modulus one use the nonzero form \(n\log z-2j\log(-1)\) with imaginary part in \([-\pi,\pi]\), where \(j=O(n)\).
The cited bound is polynomial in \(n\) after exponentiation, and \(|e^{it}-1|\ge 2|t|/\pi\) on this interval.
Taking norms in (3) gives (2).
Möbius inversion also gives
\[
     \log |N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Phi_k(\alpha)|
         =\varphi(k)h+O_P(2^{\omega(k)}\log(2k)).                           \tag{4}
\]
Every one of these cyclotomic norms is a nonzero integer in absolute value.

<a id="explicit"></a>
## An explicit choice of prime sets with large resultant

For large integers \(L\) greedily choose the least remaining prime of \([L,2L]\) that preserves distinct unordered pair sums, counting doubles.
If \(r\) primes have been selected, at most \(O(r^3+r^2+r)\) of all primes can be the next forbidden value, by the equations \(x+a=b+c,\ 2x=b+c\) or \(x=a\) with old entries.
Two sums both involving one copy of the new prime cannot first collide unless their old summands agree.
Thus the final cardinality is \(\gg (L/\log L)^{1/3}\) by the [prime count at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz).
There are \(r(r+1)/2\) distinct sums in \([2L,4L]\).
For a positive integer \(k\) with \(j=\omega(k)\) distinct prime factors, the \(i\)-th one is at least \(i+1\), giving \(\varphi(k)\ge k/(j+1)\ge k/(1+\log_2 k)\).
Also \(2^{\omega(k)}=O(\sqrt{k})\), since \(2\le\sqrt{\ell}\) at every prime \(\ell\ge5\).
For this fixed seed, (4) therefore gives \(\log|N\Phi_k(\alpha)|\gg h L/\log L\) uniformly for all the pair sums once \(L\) is sufficiently large.

In (1) retain these distinct indices from the product and use nonnegativity of the other logarithmic norms.
Writing \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\le2rL\) and \(\log L_S\le r\log(2L)\) gives
\[
                    \frac{\log|R|}{\Sigma}
                            \gg_P \frac{r}{\log L}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
This necessity has actual witnesses: for a fixed all-power reciprocal seed the [common Hermite interpolation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) produces coprime rows of width at most \(2er+2\) and \(\log H\le(2h+o(1))\Sigma\).
The degree condition for the Sylvester argument holds for these and for every common candidate with nonzero reflected determinant, by the [degree sum of the powered factors](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies).

<a id="sharp"></a>
## Large resultants also occur at sharp coefficient cost

For a fixed all-power seed take \(r=j\ge2\) and choose distinct scales in \((1,1+r^{-4})\) separated for the seed, with all their unordered pair sums distinct.
Only finitely many equality conditions on the new scale need be avoided at each step, including equality of a doubled scale with a previously chosen sum.
Choose actual primes \(q_i/L\) approaching these scales as in the [sharp prime-packet construction](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#packets).
For fixed stage and large \(L\) their unordered pair sums are distinct and the primes lie in the same relative interval.
At each stage take \(L\) large enough to get a common pair with \(\log H/\Sigma\le h+1/j\), and increase \(L\) beyond the preceding packet.
This is a diagonal existence statement and can also be searched for by exact operations with a rational multiplicative slack tending to one as in the cited construction.

For completeness, write \(B=S+S\), of size \(k=r(r+1)/2\), and use at each finite place
\(\max_{n\in B}v(I_n)\ge\sum_{n\in B}v(I_n)-\sum_{n<m}\min(v(I_n),v(I_m))\).
For two distinct sums here \(\gcd(n,m)\le|n-m|\le2r^{-4}L\).
The elementary archimedean bound \(\log |N(\alpha^g-1)|\le gh+e\log2\) bounds every shared ideal.
Taking norms in (1) yields
\[
  e\log |R|\ \ge\
  4h(r+1)\Sigma-O_P\!\left(k\log(4L)+k^2(1+r^{-4}L)+r\log(2L)\right).
\]
Here \(\sum_{n\in B}n=(r+1)\Sigma\), since the doubles are included once each.
Take \(L\) sufficiently large at each stage so that the error is \(o(r\Sigma)\) as \(r\to\infty\), which is possible since \(k^2r^{-4}L=O(L)\) and \(\Sigma\ge rL\).
Every common pair on these sets then has \(\log |R|/\Sigma\to\infty\).
The exhibited pairs also have \(\log H/\Sigma\to h\) by the tangency lower bound.
The result applies in particular to the degree-ten Lehmer seed at every strict threshold above its measure through \(6/5\).
The divisor (1) still depends on the seed, so these estimates leave the uniform positive gap open.
