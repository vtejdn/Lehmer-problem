# A coefficient obstruction to keeping a perturbed cyclotomic trace totally real

2026-11-24T21:55 UTC.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Perturbations of almost full degree

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of degree \(D\), with unit constant coefficient and all its exterior roots real.
Suppose its first \(K\) coefficients below the leading one agree with those of a monic polynomial \(C\) of degree \(D\) whose roots are all on the unit circle.
The comparison polynomial can have arbitrary complex coefficients and repeated roots.
If \(P\) has any noncyclotomic factor, then
\[
                  K\ll
     \frac{\sqrt D\,(\log(2D))^{9/2}}{(\log\log(3D))^3} .                \tag{1}
\]
All implied constants are absolute unless a dependence is indicated.

In particular take a monic integral polynomial \(Q_0\) of degree \(n\) with roots in \([-2,2]\) and a nonzero \(H\in\mathbb Z[T]\) of degree \(h<n\).
If \(Q=Q_0+H\) has only real roots and at least one root outside \([-2,2]\), then \(K=n-h-1\) satisfies (1) with \(D=2n\).
Indeed \(C=X^n Q_0(X+X^{-1})\) has all roots on the circle, whereas \(P=X^n Q(X+X^{-1})\) has positive measure and only real exterior roots.
The difference \(X^n H(X+X^{-1})\) has degree \(n+h\).
For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\), a correction with \(n-h\ge n^{1/2+\epsilon}\) therefore gives either a trace polynomial entirely in \([-2,2]\) or a nonreal trace.
In the first case Kronecker's theorem makes the reciprocal lift a product of cyclotomic polynomials.
This conclusion permits arbitrarily many real outliers and arbitrary integer coefficients in the correction.

If the output has only real roots and has a root outside \([-2,2]\), and neither endpoint is a root of \(Q_0\), the integral grid of \(C\) gives the stronger large-degree condition
\[
                n-h-1\ll
             \frac{\sqrt n\,(\log(2n))^{3/2}}{\log\log(3n)}.             \tag{2}
\]
The [Salem prefix theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement) treats a single irreducible Salem polynomial and gives a sharper answer in its own range.
The [perturbation-span theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v111.md#statement) excludes large-degree bounded-measure Salem factors at bounded perturbation span even with an uncontrolled cofactor.
Here the obstruction concerns total reality of the entire trace polynomial, including outputs with many distinct or repeated noncyclotomic factors.
It allows an individual Salem factor accompanied by other factors with nonreal traces.

<a id="rays"></a>
## Concentration of conjugates on a fixed ray

Let \(\alpha\) be an algebraic unit of degree \(d\ge2\) and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), with conjugates \(z_j\).
On any given ray from the origin the number of these conjugates satisfies
\[
                           r\ll\sqrt{d\,(m+\log(2d))}.                  \tag{3}
\]
The bound is uniform in the ray and makes no hypothesis on the other conjugates.
Put \(a_j=z_j\) when \(|z_j|\le1\) and \(a_j=1/\bar z_j\) otherwise.
These points preserve the original phases and lie in the closed unit disk.
The [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v36.md#arch), applied to the full set of \(d\) conjugates, gives
\[
      \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k}{k}
                \left|\sum_j a_j^k\right|^2\ll d(m+\log(2d)),
                    \qquad \rho=1-1/d.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Its hypotheses here are the nonzero integer discriminant of the monic minimum and \(\sum\log^+|z_j|=m\).
The companion identity \(\sum|\log|z_j||=2m\) follows from the unit norm.
The [earlier triangular arc estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) uses the same energy with a different passage from the reflected points to the rays.

For any integer \(1\le T\le d\) use the harmonic polynomial on the disk
\[
        H_T(w)=1+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}(1-k/T)w^k .
\]
Its boundary values are \(T^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}w^j|^2\), so it is nonnegative throughout the disk.
At real \(w\in[\mathrm e^{-1/T},1]\) it is at least \(\mathrm e^{-1}T\) by its nonnegative coefficient weights.
At most \(2mT\) of the conjugates have \(|\log|z_j||>1/T\).
All remaining points on the ray of angle \(\xi\) have \(\mathrm e^{-i\xi}a_j\in[\mathrm e^{-1/T},1]\).
By (4) and Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
 \sum_j H_T(\mathrm e^{-i\xi}a_j)
       \le d+O\!\left(\sqrt{d(m+\log(2d))}
                \sqrt{\sum_{k<T}k\rho^{-k}(1-k/T)^2}\right)
       \le d+O\!\left(T\sqrt{d(m+\log(2d))}\right).
\]
Here \(\rho^{-k}\le4\) for \(k\le d\).
Dividing by the ray contribution and restoring the discarded points bounds \(r\) by a constant times \(d/T+\sqrt{d(m+\log(2d))}+mT\).
For \(m\ge d/4\) the trivial \(r\le d\) gives (3).
Otherwise take \(T=\max(1,\lfloor\sqrt{d/(1+m)}\rfloor)\), proving (3) for the remaining range.

<a id="house"></a>
## A house lower bound for real exterior conjugates

Suppose \(\alpha\) is nontorsion and every exterior conjugate is real.
Write \(b=\log\max_j|z_j|>0\).
Only the conjugates on the two real rays contribute to \(m\), so by (3)
\[
             m\ll b\sqrt{d(m+\log(2d))},\qquad
             b\gg
             \frac{(\log\log(3d))^3}{\sqrt d\,(\log(2d))^{7/2}}.         \tag{5}
\]
For the second estimate, if \(m\le\log(2d)\), use the [Dobrowolski lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) at large degree in the first estimate.
If \(m>\log(2d)\), the first estimate gives the stronger \(b\gg\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\).
Finitely many small degrees are covered by decreasing the constant, since units of bounded degree and bounded house form a finite set and nontorsion units have house greater than one.
More generally if the exterior conjugates lie on \(q\) rays, the first bound for \(m\) gains a factor \(q\) on the right, by (3).
No condition on interior conjugates is needed in these statements.

For comparison, at \(m\le H\) and with all conjugates in \(\mathbb R\cup\mathbb T\), (3) bounds the number on the real line by \(O_H(\sqrt{d\log(2d)})\).
Writing \(M_2=(d\sum_j\log^2|z_j|)^{1/2}\), Cauchy–Schwarz on the exterior real conjugates gives
\[
       M_2\gg_H \frac{d^{1/4}(\log\log d)^3}{(\log d)^{13/4}}
                      \qquad(d\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
This improves the degree exponent in the [bounded-total-measure result for real and circle conjugates](Yin_C_G0865_v1.md).

<a id="prefix"></a>
## An even power sum bounds every real exterior root

Under the hypotheses of (1), matching the coefficients implies equality of the power sums at every positive integer exponent through \(K\), by Newton's identities.
For \(K\ge2\) take the even exponent \(k=2\lfloor K/2\rfloor\).
The comparison power sum has absolute value at most \(D\).
All exterior terms in the power sum for \(P\) are positive real at this exponent, and each other term has real part at least \(-1\).
Thus every exterior root \(\gamma\) has \(|\gamma|^k\le2D\).
The inequality includes all multiplicities and uses no individual factor matching.

Each noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(P\) is the minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit, since \(P\) is monic with unit constant term.
Its exterior roots are among the real exterior roots of \(P\).
Apply (5) to this factor, of degree \(d\le D\), and compare with \(b\le\log(2D)/k\).
The function \(\sqrt d(\log(2d))^{7/2}/(\log\log(3d))^3\) increases for all sufficiently large \(d\), as seen by logarithmic differentiation, and is bounded on the remaining finite range.
This gives (1), also covering \(K<2\) after a constant enlargement.

## A stronger estimate when the comparison omits the endpoints

Suppose now that \(P\) is reciprocal with all exterior roots real and that \(C=\prod_{j\ge1}\Phi_j^{e_j}\) is a cyclotomic product with the same coefficient agreement.
Write \(E=e_1+e_2\).
For \(K\ge2\) put \(L=1+\lfloor K/2\rfloor\).
Then
\[
                 2L^2m(P)\le LE+\sum_{j>2}e_j j.                       \tag{6}
\]
Use the symmetric Laurent polynomial
\[
 W_L(z)=\frac1L\left(\sum_{v=0}^{L-1}z^{2v}\right)
                      \left(\sum_{v=0}^{L-1}z^{-2v}\right)
       =1+\sum_{v=1}^{L-1}(1-v/L)(z^{2v}+z^{-2v}).
\]
It is nonnegative on the circle.
For real \(z=\pm\mathrm e^u\), the inequality \(\cosh x\ge1+x^2/2\) gives
\[
 W_L(z)\ge L+4u^2\sum_{v=1}^{L-1}(1-v/L)v^2
          =L+\frac{L(L^2-1)}3u^2\ge L^2|u| .
\]
The last step uses \(L\ge2\) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
Each reciprocal pair off the circle consequently contributes at least \(2L^2\log^+|\gamma|\) when \(\gamma\) denotes its exterior member.
Both polynomials have inverse-stable root multisets, so matching the positive exponents through \(K\) matches also their negative power sums there.
It follows that \(\sum_{P(z)=0}W_L(z)=\sum_{C(z)=0}W_L(z)\), with roots counted with multiplicity.

At \(z=\pm1\) the value is \(L\).
For a primitive \(j\)-th root with \(j>2\) let \(g=(j,2)\) and \(j'=j/g\), so that \(z^2\ne1\).
If \(L\le j'\), summing \(W_L\) over the full \(j\)-th root grid gives \(j\) by orthogonality, an upper bound for the primitive subset.
If \(L>j'\), each nonzero phase \(2\pi b/j'\) occurs at most \(g\) times on the primitive subset and contributes at most \(1/(L\sin^2(\pi b/j'))\).
Using \(\sin(\pi b/j')\ge2\min(b,j'-b)/j'\) gives
\(\sum_{b=1}^{j'-1}\sin^{-2}(\pi b/j')\le (j')^2\sum_{v\ge1}1/(2v^2)<(j')^2\).
Hence the primitive sum is again at most \(g(j')^2/L\le j\), proving (6).

For every occupied \(j\), \(\varphi(j)\le D\) and \(j\le2D^2\) by the [elementary totient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
The standard Mertens product bound yields \(j/\varphi(j)\ll\log\log(3D)\): take the primes through \(\max(3,\log j)\) in that product and bound the remaining log factors by \(O(1)\), since there are at most \(\log j/\log2\) prime divisors and each remaining contribution is \(O(1/\log j)\).
Thus \(\sum_{j>2}e_jj\ll D\log\log(3D)\).
When \(E=0\) and \(P\) has a noncyclotomic factor, that factor has degree at most \(D\) and gives \(m(P)\gg(\log\log(3D)/\log(2D))^3\) by Dobrowolski and bounded-degree finiteness.
Combining with (6) gives \(K\ll\sqrt D\,(\log(2D))^{3/2}/\log\log(3D)\).
For the trace comparison \(Q_0\) in (2), Kronecker's theorem makes \(C\) cyclotomic, and absence of trace roots at \(\pm2\) is exactly \(E=0\).
This proves (2).
