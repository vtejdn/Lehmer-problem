# Mahler measure and the density of dissociated powers modulo polynomial powers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform density bounds

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit \(\alpha\), let \(m=\log M(P)\) with natural logarithm, and let \(k\ge1\) be an integer.
Write \(a_N(P,k)\) for the largest size of a subset of \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) on which all binary subset sums of the powers of \(X\) are distinct in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(P^k)\).
Equivalently, this subset supports no nonzero ternary multiple of \(P^k\).
The limit \(D_k(P)=\lim_N a_N(P,k)/N\) exists, and there is an absolute constant \(c>0\) for which
\[
 c\min\{1,km\}\le D_k(P)\le\min\{1,km/\log 2\}.
\]
In particular, Lehmer's positive-gap assertion for nontorsion units is equivalent to a uniform positive lower bound on \(D_1(P)\) for those units.
The limit follows by subadditivity, since \(X\) is a unit in the quotient and restrictions to adjacent intervals remain admissible after translation.
The [jet collision count](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#statement) at weight \(\lfloor a_N/2\rfloor\) gives the upper bound, also when \(m=0\): the logarithm of the central binomial coefficient is at least \(a_N\log 2-\log(a_N+1)\), and the remaining errors for fixed \(P,k\) are \(O_{P,k}(\log(N+1))\).
It remains to consider \(m>0\).

<a id="matrix"></a>
## Entropy from the contracting conjugates with multiplicity

Let \(g(X)=\prod_{P(\beta)=0,\ |\beta|<1}(X-\beta)^k\), a real polynomial of degree \(s>0\), and let \(T\) be multiplication by \(X\) on \(\mathbb R[X]/(g)\).
Since \(\alpha\) is a unit, \(|\det T|=\exp(-km)\).
The minimal polynomial of \(T\) is \(g\), so over \(\mathbb C\) it has one Jordan block of size \(k\) at each contracting conjugate.
In particular, \(P^k(T)=0\), \(T\) is invertible, and its spectral radius is strictly below one.
Let \(L\) read the coefficient of \(X^{s-1}\) in the representative of degree less than \(s\).
If this representative of a nonzero \(y\) has degree \(j\), then \(L(T^{s-1-j}y)\ne0\).
Thus \(Q(y)=\sum_{i\ge0}|L(T^iy)|^2\) is positive definite, with convergence by exponential decay of the powers of a stable matrix.
Choose coordinates making \(Q\) Euclidean and call the matrix of \(T\) in these coordinates \(A\).
The identity \(Q(y)-Q(Ty)=|L(y)|^2\) makes \(I-A^t A\) positive semidefinite of rank one.
Consequently the singular values of \(A\) are one with multiplicity \(s-1\) and \(b=\exp(-km)\) with multiplicity one.

We apply the Gaussian inequalities in [BV20, Lemma 9 and the proof of Proposition 13](../audits/literature/BV20/Yin_U001.md#bv20-gaussian-scale-entropy) to this matrix.
Use entropy in bits in this paragraph and the next, writing \(H\) for discrete entropy and \(h\) for differential entropy.
For independent standard Gaussian \(G_s\) and invertible \(B\), put \(J(Y;B)=h(Y+BG_s)-h(BG_s)\) and \(J(Y;B\mid C)=J(Y;B)-J(Y;C)\).
For independent fair binary digits \(\xi_i\) and any vector \(x\), set \(Y_N=\sum_{i<N}\xi_i A^ix\) and \(Y=\sum_{i\ge0}\xi_i A^ix\), with the indices starting at zero.
These sums are uniformly bounded for fixed \(A,x\).
By \(Y\overset{d}=Y_N+A^NY'\) with independent copy \(Y'\), convolution subadditivity at scale \(A^N\) and change of variables imply \(J(Y;A^N\mid I)\le J(Y_N;A^N)\le H(Y_N)\).
In the telescoping sum for the left side, its \(i\)-th increment \(J(Y;A^i\mid A^{i-1})\), \(1\le i\le N\), is at least \(J(\xi_0 x;A\mid I)\).
Indeed the covariance ordering follows from \(A^{i-1}(I-AA^t)(A^{i-1})^t\succeq0\), the increment increases under independent convolution, and the single digit at position \(i-1\) gives the stated increment by change of variables.

Choose \(x=t u\), where \(u\) is a unit left singular vector for \(b\).
Orthogonal coordinates diagonalizing \(AA^t\) give \(J(\xi_0x;A\mid I)=h(b^{-1}t\xi_0+G)-h(t\xi_0+G)\), where \(G\) is a standard real Gaussian independent of \(\xi_0\).
The supremum over \(t>0\) is the function \(\Phi(b^{-1})\) of BV20, since replacing a binary digit by a fair sign only rescales \(t\) and translates the mixtures.
By [the qualitative Gaussian bound](../audits/literature/BV20/Yin_U001.md#bv20-u001), \(\gamma=\Phi(2)>0\) and \(\Phi\) is increasing.
Let \(B_N\) be the set of all binary polynomial residues of length \(N\) modulo \(P^k\).
For each \(x\), the map \([F]\mapsto F(A)x\) is well defined and sends a uniformly sampled binary word's residue to \(Y_N\), because \(P^k(A)=0\).
It follows that \(\log_2|B_N|\ge H(Y_N)\ge N J(\xi_0x;A\mid I)\).
Taking the supremum shows \(|B_N|\ge 2^{N\gamma}\) whenever \(km\ge1\), since then \(b^{-1}\ge e>2\).

<a id="shattering"></a>
## From distinct residues to dissociated exponents

Select the lexicographically first binary word for each residue in \(B_N\), with positions ordered by increasing exponent and \(0<1\), and call the resulting family \(\mathcal F\).
A set \(S\) is shattered if the restrictions of \(\mathcal F\) to \(S\) contain all binary patterns.
Suppose distinct subsets \(U,V\subseteq S\) have equal residue sums, cancelling their intersection so that they are disjoint.
Order them so the indicator of \(U\) is lexicographically larger.
A representative restricting to the indicator of \(U\) on \(S\) can have \(U\) replaced by \(V\), with every digit outside \(S\) unchanged, giving a smaller word for its residue.
Thus every shattered set is admissible.

The shattering inequality associated with Pajor bounds the cardinality of a binary family by its number of shattered sets, and implies the dimension bound of Sauer and Shelah. [Pajor](https://www.editions-hermann.fr/livre/sous-espaces-ln1-des-espaces-de-banach-alain-pajor), [Sauer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(72)90019-2), [Shelah](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1972.41.247)
Induct on the number of positions, starting with zero, and let \(F_0,F_1\) be the two sections at the last position.
Sets shattered by \(F_0\cup F_1\) are shattered in the original family, and each set shattered by \(F_0\cap F_1\) is shattered there after adjoining the last position.
These two collections are disjoint, so induction gives the bound \(|F_0\cup F_1|+|F_0\cap F_1|=|\mathcal F|\).
Choose a fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\) so that \(H_2(\delta)=-\delta\log_2\delta-(1-\delta)\log_2(1-\delta)<\gamma\).
The binomial theorem with weights \(\delta,1-\delta\) gives \(\sum_{j\le\delta N}\binom Nj\le 2^{NH_2(\delta)}\), because every subset counted has weight at least \(2^{-NH_2(\delta)}\).
For \(km\ge1\) there must therefore be a shattered set of size greater than \(\delta N\), and \(D_k(P)\ge\delta\).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Transfer to small Mahler measure

For \(q\ge1\), let \(Q_q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^q\) and let \(e_q=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^q)]\).
Every conjugate of \(\alpha^q\) occurs \(e_q\) times among the powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\), so \(\log M(Q_q)=qm/e_q\).
By [independence of the powered jets](Yin_C_G0345_v567.md#period), a mask of size \(a_N(Q_q,k)\) can be copied into each of the \(e_q\) residue classes \(0,\ldots,e_q-1\) modulo \(q\) to form an admissible mask of size \(e_q a_N(Q_q,k)\) in \([0,qN)\).
Taking limits gives \(D_k(P)\ge(e_q/q)D_k(Q_q)\).
If \(0<km<1\), let \(q\) be the first power of two with \(kqm/e_q\ge1\), which exists since \(e_q\le\deg P\).
The tower law gives \(e_{2r}/e_r=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^r):\mathbb Q(\alpha^{2r})]\in\{1,2\}\), so the first crossing satisfies \(1\le kqm/e_q<2\).
Applying the previous bound to \(Q_q\) gives \(D_k(P)\ge \delta e_q/q>\delta km/2\).
The assertion follows with \(c=\delta/2\).
