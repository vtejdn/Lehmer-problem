# A family of reciprocal Perron units with square Mahler measure and an obstruction to delayed relative constructions

<a id="statement"></a>
## An actual square endpoint with an unbounded number of neutral conjugates

Put \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), \(d=2+\sqrt3\), \(c=3+2\sqrt3\).
Let \(a>a'\) be the roots of \(Y^2-cY+d\) in the indicated real embedding, put \(K=E(a)\), and let \(\tau\) interchange \(a,a'\).
For every integer \(b\ge3\), set
\[
 f_b(X)=X^{b-1}(X-a)-(1-a'X),\qquad
                 S_b(X)=N_{K/\mathbb Q} f_b(X),
\]
where the polynomial norm multiplies its four coefficient conjugates.
Then \(S_b\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is monic, reciprocal and irreducible of degree \(4b\).
It has a strictly dominant real root \(1<r_b<a\).
Precisely \(2b\) of its conjugates lie on the unit circle and exactly \(b\) are exterior.
Their product of moduli is
\[
                            M(r_b)=r_b^2 .
\]
The root is a compressed Perron unit, and \(r_b\to a=(3+2\sqrt3+\sqrt{13+8\sqrt3})/2\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(b\), its canonical label action has exactly two blocks of size \(b\) in the oriented alternative.
One half of the first block consists of \(r_b\) and \(b-1\) interior roots, while the second block is entirely neutral, and every half product is a sign.
This realizes the [oriented square pattern](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal) in arbitrarily large degrees.
Its measures converge to \(a^2>1\).

A second result gives a lower bound in a general relative version of the delayed polynomial construction.
Let \(E_0\) be totally real of degree \(u\ge2\), and let \(K_0/E_0\) be quadratic, split at one real place and complex at the others, with involution \(\tau_0\).
Take a monic \(P\in\mathcal O_{K_0}[X]\) of positive degree and nonzero constant term.
At the chosen split embedding suppose that its only exterior root is a simple real \(\beta>1\), with every other root strictly inside the circle.
Write \(Q=(P^{\tau_0})^*\), where \(R^*(X)=X^{\deg R}R(1/X)\).
Assume \(|P|>|Q|\) on the unit circle at that embedding, and that every root of \(P\) is in the closed unit disk at each complex embedding of \(K_0\).
Consider \(X^kP\pm Q\), \(k\ge1\).
If every root is neutral at one complex coefficient embedding of \(P\), then \(Q\) is a scalar multiple of \(P\), and the delayed polynomial factors over \(K_0\).
Otherwise its distinguished unique exterior root of modulus \(R\) satisfies
\[
       \beta\ \ge\ B_u:=\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{1+4^u}-1}{2}},\qquad
                    R\ \ge\ \beta-\frac{\beta+1}{k}.                    \tag{1}
\]
These estimates are independent of the seed degree and coefficients.
In this nondegenerate class \(R\to1\) is excluded on each of the following ranges, with a uniform positive gap in the stated range:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
        u&2&3&4&\ge5\\ \hline
        k\text{ at least}&10&4&3&2 .
\end{array}
\]
In particular unbounded-degree coefficient fields can contribute to a near-one construction of this type only at delay one eventually.
The classical [delayed rational Pisot construction](Yin_C_G0345_v30.md#statement) uses the reciprocal of the same polynomial, while (1) allows different coefficients at the two real embeddings.
The conditions on \(P\) and \(Q\) restrict this exclusion to the specified construction.
For a square endpoint with \(u\ge2\) in the pattern of v109, a related integrality test applies without assuming any construction.
If a normalized power sum \(b^{-1}\sum_i a_i^j\) of the marked half is a nonzero algebraic integer, then \(r^j\ge1+b(B_u-1)\), where \(j\ge1\) and \(r>1\) is its distinguished exterior root.

<a id="field"></a>
## A quartic coefficient field and its primitive norm-one unit

The two values of the quadratic discriminant are \(13+8\sqrt3>0\) and \(13-8\sqrt3<0\).
Consequently \(K/E\) is quadratic with two real embeddings and one complex pair.
Since \(c>d+1\) and \(d>1\), one has \(a>1>a'>0\).
Under the other embedding of \(E\) the two conjugate values of \(a\) have common modulus \(\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}<1\).
Also \(a\) is an algebraic unit since \(d\) is a unit.
Put \(\alpha=a/a'>1\).
Its other real value is \(\alpha^{-1}\) and its two complex values have modulus one.
Its trace to \(E\) equals \(c^2/d-2=4+3\sqrt3\), whose conjugate \(4-3\sqrt3\) is strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\).
In particular \(\alpha\) is a quartic Salem number, generates \(K\), and is smaller than ten.

The relative norm-one units have rank one and torsion \(\{\pm1\}\). [Full unit kernel](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units)
Choose a positive generator \(\sigma>1\) modulo torsion, so \(\alpha=\sigma^h\) for \(h\ge1\).
The trace \(s=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) lies in \(\mathcal O_E=\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\), exceeds two at the chosen place and has its other value strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\).
The strict inequality follows also from the corresponding complex embeddings of \(\alpha\) and powering.
Writing \(s=m+j\sqrt3\) gives \(j\ge1\).
If \(j=1\), then \(m\ge1\), and if \(j\ge2\), then \(s>-2+4\sqrt3\).
Hence \(s\ge1+\sqrt3>9/4+4/9\), giving \(\sigma>9/4\) and \(\sigma^3>10\).

Nor is \(\alpha\) a square in \(K\), because \(\alpha=a^2/d\) would then imply \(\sqrt d\in K\).
The element \(d\) is nonsquare in \(E\): writing \((x+y\sqrt3)^2=2+\sqrt3\) with rational \(x,y\) forces \(x^2\in\{1/2,3/2\}\).
The extension \(E(\sqrt d)\) is totally real since \(d\) is totally positive, and thus cannot equal \(K\).
This excludes \(h=2\), and the cubic bound excludes every larger \(h\).
Therefore the relative norm-one group is \(\{\pm\alpha^j:j\in\mathbb Z\}\), with \(\alpha>a\).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Locations, relative irreducibility and the four actual conjugate polynomials

At the chosen embedding and \(|X|=1\),
\[
              |X-a|^2-|1-a'X|^2=(a-a')(c-2\operatorname{Re}X)>0 .
\]
Rouché's theorem gives \(f_b\) exactly \(b-1\) interior roots, counted with multiplicity, and no circle roots. [Rouché's theorem](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk)
Since \(f_b(1)=a'-a<0\) and \(f_b(a)=d-1>0\), its sole exterior root is real and belongs to \((1,a)\).
For every fixed \(1<x<a\) one has \(f_b(x)<0\) once \(b\) is large, proving \(r_b\to a\).
The other real polynomial is \(f_b^\tau=-f_b^*\).
The moduli of all \(b-1\) interior roots of \(f_b\) have product \(1/r_b\).
Since \(b-1\ge2\) and each modulus is less than one, each is strictly greater than \(1/r_b\).
Thus \(f_b\) and \(f_b^\tau\) are disjoint, and every exterior root of \(f_b^\tau\) has modulus strictly below \(r_b\).

At a complex place, write \(\gamma\) for the image of \(a\), so the image of \(a'\) equals \(\bar\gamma\) and \(|\gamma|<1\).
The equation is \(X^{b-1}(X-\gamma)/(1-\bar\gamma X)=1\).
The left-hand side has modulus strictly less than one in the open disk by the disk automorphism inequality.
The denominator does not vanish there, and reflection with inversion and conjugation preserves the root set.
Thus all roots at each complex coefficient embedding lie on the unit circle.

If \(f_b\) factored over \(K\), a proper monic factor could omit \(r_b\), since \(r_b\) occurs once.
Its coefficients are integral and its constant \(h\) is a unit since \(f_b(0)=-1\).
At the chosen real embedding it satisfies \(1/r_b\le |h|<1\), by the product of all interior moduli.
At a complex place every root of that factor has modulus one, so the value of \(h\tau(h)\) there is \(|h|^2=1\).
Injectivity gives \(N_{K/E}h=1\).
Yet any such unit of modulus less than one at our real embedding has modulus at most \(1/\alpha\), whereas \(1/r_b>1/\alpha\).
This proves relative irreducibility.

The complex coefficient polynomials \(f_b^\iota,f_b^{\iota\tau}\) are disjoint as well: an absolute automorphism undoing \(\iota\) on \(K\) takes this pair to the two disjoint real polynomials.
A complex coefficient polynomial cannot intersect a real one because only the former has circle roots.
All four polynomials are separable, and each is an irreducible conjugate over its coefficient field.
The rational orbit of \(r_b\) contains the roots of all four by extension of embeddings, giving \(4b\) actual conjugates.
This proves the irreducibility of \(S_b\) and identifies its three modulus classes relative to the circle.
The opposite half products have absolute value one since their polynomials have constant term \(-1\).
The exterior product of moduli is \(r_b\) times the product of the reciprocals of the interior moduli of \(f_b\), namely \(r_b^2\).
Strict dominance also proves compression: transport either member of a supposed torsion-equivalent pair to \(r_b\), producing a second conjugate of the same modulus.

The conjugate factors themselves mark two inverse halves of roots over each embedding of \(E\).
Their four root sets are permuted by the rational splitting group, which preserves the partition into the two blocks of inverse pairs.
The stabilizer of a block is transitive on its labels because the full label action is transitive.
For prime \(b\) a block of size \(b\) cannot contain a proper nontrivial block: its stabilizer would give a partition of those \(b\) labels into equal smaller sizes.
Thus these are minimal nontrivial blocks, and at large degrees their partition is the unique [canonical partition](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition), since \(m(r_b)<2\log a\) is uniformly bounded.
Even flips at two labels in a block cannot preserve or exchange its two marked halves when \(b\ge3\).
The [actual-root dichotomy](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) is therefore oriented.
The supported pure alternating subgroup on a block fixes both marked halves, since it is perfect and its action on the pair of halves is abelian.
It acts transitively on their labels, so its two orbits are exactly the marked halves, identifying them with the [canonical halves](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles).

<a id="seed"></a>
## An integer norm obstruction at the neutral coefficient places

Return to the general seed and set \(h=P(0)\), \(d_0=h\tau_0(h)\in\mathcal O_{E_0}\).
At a complex place over \(E_0\) one has \(\iota\tau_0=\bar\iota\), so the corresponding conjugate of \(d_0\) is \(|\iota h|^2\in(0,1]\).
If every root there has modulus one, the monic conjugate polynomial \(\iota P\) agrees up to its leading coefficient with its reflected conjugate reciprocal, since their roots with multiplicity coincide.
It follows by injectivity at this embedding that \(Q=\tau_0(h)P\) in \(K_0[X]\).
The delayed polynomial is then \(P(X)(X^k\pm\tau_0(h))\).
Otherwise at each complex embedding some root is strictly inside, and all \(u-1\) other values of \(d_0\) belong to \((0,1)\).
In particular \(d_0\ne0,1\).
The nonzero integers \(N_{E_0/\mathbb Q}d_0\) and \(N_{E_0/\mathbb Q}(1-d_0)\) give
\[
          1\le
             |d_0(1-d_0)|\prod_{\iota\ne {\rm id}}
                              \iota(d_0)(1-\iota(d_0))
             \le |d_0(1-d_0)|\,4^{1-u}.
\]
Here the product uses the embeddings of the whole field \(E_0\), including their proper multiplicities if \(d_0\) lies in a subfield.
At the split embedding \(M(P)=\beta\) and \(|h|\le\beta\).
Taking the circle mean of the strict boundary inequality gives \(M(Q)\le\beta\).
Since the leading coefficient of \(Q\) is \(\tau_0(h)\), one has \(|\tau_0(h)|\le\beta\) as well.
Therefore \(4^{u-1}\le |d_0(1-d_0)|\le\beta^2(1+\beta^2)\), which gives the first bound in (1).
The argument permits nonunit seed constants.

<a id="delay"></a>
## The exterior pole and the delay

Rouché on the circle gives \(X^kP\pm Q\) exactly one exterior root \(r\) and no neutral roots at the chosen split embedding, since the degree is \(k+\deg P\) and \(X^kP\) has just one exterior root.
All coefficients are real there, so \(r\) is real.
On the exterior of the disk the rational function
\[
                 \frac{X-\beta}{\beta X-1}\frac{Q(X)}{P(X)}
\]
has a removable singularity at \(\beta\), no other poles, and is bounded at infinity.
Its modulus is at most one on the boundary because the first quotient has modulus one there.
The exterior maximum principle gives the same bound throughout.
If \(P(r)=0\), then \(r=\beta\) and (1) already holds.
Otherwise \(r^kP(r)=\mp Q(r)\) gives
\[
                       R^k |r-\beta|\le|\beta r-1|,\qquad R=|r|>1 .
\]
A negative exterior root would imply \(R^k\le(\beta R+1)/(R+\beta)<R\), contrary to \(k\ge1\).
Thus \(r=R>1\).
When \(R<\beta\),
\[
     1+k(R-1)\le R^k\le
         \frac{\beta R-1}{\beta-R}
             =1+(R-1)\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-R}.
\]
This gives the second bound in (1), which holds immediately also when \(R\ge\beta\).
At nonsplit embeddings the delayed polynomial has only unit-circle roots: any circle factors of \(\iota P\) occur also in its reflected conjugate reciprocal with the same multiplicities.
After cancellation \(X^k(\iota P)/(\overline{\iota P})^*\) is a finite Blaschke product times a factor of modulus one, with modulus strictly below one in the disk.
The resulting equation with value \(1\) or \(-1\) has no interior roots and reflection gives no exterior roots.
At the opposite real embedding the monic reciprocal of the delayed polynomial gives the inverse root multiset, since its constant is \(\pm1\).
In particular the positive exterior modulus \(R\) alone already bounds the Mahler measure of its own minimum below by \(R\), regardless of any factorization among the other roots.

The lower bound on \(R\) is increasing in both \(k\ge2\) and \(\beta\).
It is strictly above one whenever \(B_u>(k+1)/(k-1)\).
The defining values give \(B_2>11/9,\ B_3>5/3,\ B_4>2,\ B_5>3\) and \(B_u\) increases with \(u\).
These inequalities yield the stated four uniform exclusions.

## An intrinsic integrality test on an oriented half

In the [square endpoint pattern](Yin_C_G0901_v109.md#minimal) write \(a_1=r,a_2,\ldots,a_b\) for the marked half and \(f\) for its monic polynomial over the field \(K_0\) of this half.
Write \(E_0\) for the field of its block, with \(u=[E_0:\mathbb Q]\ge2\).
Complex conjugation fixes the marked block because it contains the label of \(r\), and fixes every label outside that block because it is neutral.
The marked halves are fixed separately because one contains the real root \(r\), whereas on every other block ordinary conjugation takes each neutral root to its inverse and exchanges its halves.
The [fields of the block and the half](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields) therefore have exactly the signatures used in (1).
The coordinates are compressed, so the powers of two distinct representatives never coincide.

For \(j\ge1\) put \(h_j=b^{-1}\sum_i a_i^j\in K_0\).
At a nonsplit embedding \(|\iota h_j|<1\) by the strict triangle inequality on these distinct circle points.
At the marked real place, \(|h_j|\le1+(r^j-1)/b\) since the other roots are interior.
For the opposite half write \(t_i=-\log|a_i|>0\) for \(i\ge2\), so \(\sum_{i\ge2}t_i=\log r\) by the unit-modulus half product.
The inequality \((e^x-1)+(e^y-1)\le e^{x+y}-1\) for \(x,y\ge0\) gives \(\sum_{i\ge2}|a_i|^{-j}\le b-2+r^j\).
Including \(r^{-j}\) thus bounds \(|\tau_0 h_j|\) by the same \(1+(r^j-1)/b\).
If \(h_j\ne0\) is integral, the two norms of \(d_0=h_j\tau_0h_j\) and \(1-d_0\) used above apply unchanged.
They force \(1+(r^j-1)/b\ge B_u\), as claimed.

For example, suppose \(b\) is prime and \(f'/b\in\mathcal O_{K_0}[X]\).
Writing \(c_i=[X^{b-i}]f\), this integrality gives \(b\mid(b-i)c_i\), hence \(c_i\in b\mathcal O_{K_0}\) for \(1\le i<b\).
Newton's identities give integral \(h_j\) for \(1\le j<b\).
They must vanish throughout the initial range \(r^j<1+b(B_u-1)\), \(1\le j<b\), and then the same identities force each corresponding \(c_j=0\).
The reciprocal coefficient relation forces the opposite coefficients to vanish as well.
This is a restriction on the actual relative polynomial, independently of the delayed construction.
