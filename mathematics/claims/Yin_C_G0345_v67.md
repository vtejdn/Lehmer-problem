# The field of squared conjugate moduli and a Mahler bound at fixed radial rank

<a id="statement"></a>
## A field degree controlled by complex conjugation

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(d\) and measure \(M(P)>1\).
Apply the [exact torsion-class reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) first if needed, and let \(P,d\) refer to the resulting polynomial and degree.
Let \(G\) be the Galois group of its splitting field, let \(c\in G\) be ordinary complex conjugation, and put \(B=[G:C_G(c)]\).
Write \(k\) for the rational rank of the log moduli of its exterior conjugates.
For the real field \(F=\mathbb Q(xc(x):P(x)=0)\) one has
\[
                     B\ \le\ [F:\mathbb Q]\ \le\ 3^{k^2} B .
\]
Any \(k\) multiplicatively independent products \(xc(x)\) generate this field.
For every fixed \(k\ge2\) there exists \(C_k>0\), independent of \(P\), with
\[
          \log M(P)\ \ge\
           C_k\,\frac{d}{B^{1/k}(\log(3B))^{\kappa(k)}},
           \qquad \kappa(k)=(k+1)((k+1)!)^k-k.
\]
In particular \(B\le A d^{k-\delta}\) for fixed \(A>0,\ 0<\delta<k\) gives a positive gap uniform over these polynomials and logarithmic measure tending to infinity with the compressed degree.
Neither this inequality on \(B\) nor a bound on \(k\) is assumed for the unrestricted Lehmer problem.

<a id="field"></a>
## The stabilizer of a basis of positive products

Write \(\Lambda\) for the group generated by the original roots modulo torsion, additively, and \(V=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).
The action of \(G\) on \(V\) is faithful, since any automorphism acting trivially sends each root to one with torsion quotient and therefore fixes every root after the stated compression.
Let \(U=(1+c)V\) be the fixed subspace of \(c\).
The actual products \(xc(x)\) are positive reals and generate a free abelian group of rank \(k\), by applying the real logarithm and using reciprocity to include all the negative logs.
They map injectively modulo torsion and their images span \(U\).
In particular one can choose \(k\) independent such products \(u_1,\ldots,u_k\).

Average a rational positive definite form over \(G\).
The involution \(c\) acts as minus the identity on \(U^\perp\), so the set stabilizer of \(U\) in \(G\) is exactly the centralizer of \(c\), by faithfulness.
Any \(g\in G\) fixing the chosen \(u_i\) acts trivially on \(U\), hence centralizes \(c\).
It sends every \(xc(x)\) to \(g(x)c(g(x))\), another positive real product having the same class in \(U\).
Their quotient is a positive real root of unity, so these products are actually equal.
Conversely a centralizing element with trivial action on \(U\) fixes all the positive products by the same argument.
Thus the kernel of the centralizer action on \(U\) fixes precisely the field generated by our tuple, in the Galois correspondence, and the tuple generates \(F\).

The centralizer permutes these products and acts on their rank \(k\) lattice through a finite subgroup of \({\rm GL}_k(\mathbb Z)\).
Reduction modulo three is injective on every such finite subgroup.
Indeed any nonidentity kernel element of finite order would have a power \(I+3^\ell A\) of prime order \(p\), with \(\ell\ge1\) and some entry of \(A\) prime to three.
If \(p\ne3\) the first term \(p3^\ell A\) of its \(p\)-th power after the identity survives modulo \(3^{\ell+1}\).
For \(p=3\) the first term \(3^{\ell+1}A\) survives modulo \(3^{\ell+2}\) by the binomial expansion.
This contradiction proves the asserted bound on the image order by the total of \(3^{k^2}\) matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\).
The degree of \(F\) is the index of the kernel in \(G\), giving both degree bounds.

<a id="height"></a>
## Independent heights in this same field

The [height-product theorem of Amoroso and David](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
       \prod_{i=1}^k h(u_i)
       \ \ge\ c(k)\,[F:\mathbb Q]^{-1}
                   \{\log(3[F:\mathbb Q])\}^{-k\kappa(k)} .
\]
Every selected product has height at most \(2\log M(P)/d\), by subadditivity and conjugacy of the two factors.
Taking \(k\)-th roots and substituting the degree bound proves the assertion with an absolute-in-\(P\) constant at each fixed \(k\).
Under the polynomial bound on \(B\), the factor \(d^{\delta/k}\) dominates the logarithmic denominator as \(d\) grows.
At bounded degrees finiteness of monic integer polynomials of measure at most two gives the uniform positive gap.

<a id="real"></a>
## A gap for real exterior roots in nonsingleton blocks

Suppose the exterior roots are all real.
Partition the inverse-pair labels into the [blocks of identical membership](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) in the Galois translates of the set \(E\) of exterior labels.
Let \(b\) be the common block size, \(\rho\) the rank modulo torsion of the representatives of one block and \(j\) the number of blocks in \(E\).
Then \(k=j\rho\) and \(B\le \binom{d/(2b)}{j}\).
Indeed the full rational relation space splits as a sum over the blocks, and each relation among the real exterior logs is a multiplicative relation modulo signs on the corresponding actual real roots.
Thus the log rank on \(E\) is the sum of the \(j\) identical block ranks.
Conjugation fixes each real pair pointwise and interchanges the endpoints of each neutral pair.
Its conjugates are therefore specified exactly by the set of labels on which they fix the endpoints, namely a translate of \(E\).
These translates are unions of \(j\) whole blocks, giving the count.

If \(b>1\), then \(\rho\ge2\).
Otherwise two roots on different labels in one block would have proportional nonzero classes modulo torsion.
Their identical positive absolute heights force the proportionality to be \(+1\) or \(-1\), yielding torsion quotients between distinct roots after choosing an inverse if necessary.
Consequently at every fixed radial rank \(k\) such nonsingleton-block cases satisfy
\[
            \log M(P)\ \ge\ c_k\,
                    \frac{d^{\,1-1/\rho}}{(\log(3d))^{\kappa(k)}} .
\]
In particular this gives an unconditional uniform gap for every fixed \(k\) in this class, and applies to the three-real-exterior circuit where \(j=1,\rho=2\).
For singleton blocks the original representatives are independent modulo torsion and the bound on \(B\) from the number of blocks may have degree exponent exactly \(k\).
