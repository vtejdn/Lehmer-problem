# Multiplicative independence across critical six-term sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## A support bound independent of the exponents and the number of sections

Fix positive constants \(C,K\) and take any finite nonempty family of distinct irreducible factors satisfying the [critical hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement), allowing the parameter triple \((s,a,b)\) to vary between factors.
All seed degrees are assumed sufficiently large in terms of \(C,K\) alone.
For factor \(i\) of degree \(d_i\), the \(d_i/2\) inverse pairs of roots have the [minimal block partition](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) into \(u_i\) blocks of size \(q_i=d_i/(2u_i)\).
Choose one root per inverse pair.
Any set of chosen roots omitting at least one pair from every block of every factor is multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
In particular the rank generated by the whole family is at least \(\sum_i(d_i/2-u_i)\).
Every nontrivial relation uses at least \(\max_i q_i\) distinct inverse pairs, with the maximum taken only over factors having a nonzero coefficient after collecting inverses and repetitions.
More precisely, in each occurring factor attaining that maximum, the absolute coefficients are constant on every block, including the zero coefficients.

The estimate \(q_i\gg d_i/(\log(3d_i))^{212}\) makes the support threshold grow with the degrees even if the absolute exponents are unbounded.
The family may have arbitrarily many members with unrelated degrees.
For each fixed \(n\), the joint phases of \(n\) roots from distinct inverse pairs converge to Haar measure on \((S^1)^n\) when the least seed degree tends to infinity, averaging over embeddings of their compositum.
This extends the [single-section phase conclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v532.md#statement) to roots from different critical sections.

<a id="largest"></a>
## Isolating the factors of largest block size

By [rational-power separation](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#statement), two distinct chosen roots are multiplicatively independent in pairs, including roots from different factors.
The torsion-quotient class of any root among its conjugates is a singleton, so its class product is the root itself.
Reciprocity and the same separation identify the multiplicative labels exactly with the inverse pairs.
The [critical degree and measure bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#scales) ensure uniformly that all compressed degrees are large and all logarithmic measures bounded.
Thus the [single-factor block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) applies with \(q_i\ge9\) and \(u_i<q_i\).

Consider only the factors occurring in a given nontrivial relation and put \(Q=\max_i q_i\).
Let \(T\) be the compositum of the label splitting fields of factors with \(q_i=Q\).
Within this subfamily the [independent alternating factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#statement) applies, since \(Q>\max_{q_i=Q}u_i\).
Hence \(\operatorname{Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\) contains all copies of \(A_Q\) acting independently on the individual blocks of these factors.
Let \(S\) be the compositum of the label splitting fields of the remaining factors, taking \(S=\mathbb Q\) for an empty family.
Each single label group embeds in \(S_{q_i}\wr S_{u_i}\).
Every nonabelian simple composition factor of a subgroup of this wreath product has order at most \(\max(q_i!,u_i!)\), by first taking the kernel on blocks and then filtering that kernel by its coordinate projections.
The same coordinate filtration on the product shows that \(\operatorname{Gal}(S/\mathbb Q)\) has no composition factor isomorphic to \(A_Q\), since \((Q-1)!<Q!/2\).
Its quotient \(\operatorname{Gal}(S\cap T/\mathbb Q)\) therefore has none either.
The product of the independent copies of \(A_Q\) is normal in \(\operatorname{Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\), being the kernel over the auxiliary field in the cited theorem.
Its image in the intersection quotient is normal and a quotient of that direct product, so it is trivial.
Consequently each single-block three-cycle extends to an automorphism of \(ST\) fixing \(S\) and every label outside its three letters.

<a id="relations"></a>
## Applying a three-cycle to a multiplicative relation

Lift such an automorphism to the full root splitting compositum and square the lift.
Its square fixes both roots over every fixed label, since each such fiber consists of an inverse pair.
On the selected three labels the square still acts as a three-cycle.
Divide the transported relation by the original one to obtain a relation modulo torsion supported on at most three labels of one factor.
The [single-factor independence theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) makes its coefficient vector zero because the block has more than three letters.
Thus the original coefficients have equal absolute values on these three labels, regardless of the signs introduced by the lift.
Every triple in every maximal block can be used, giving the coefficient assertion and the claimed support bound.
A relation in a set omitting one label per block would vanish on every occurring maximal factor, a contradiction.
Choosing precisely one omission per block proves the rank bound.

For the phase assertion, each fixed nonzero integer character on the \(n\) chosen roots gives a nontorsion monomial for all sufficiently large least seed degrees by the support bound.
Its absolute logarithmic height tends to zero by subadditivity and the critical height estimates.
The [one-variable phase estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#orthogonality) gives a vanishing first phase mean for that monomial, also when averaged in the larger compositum since every embedding has equally many extensions.
The character criterion for Haar convergence proves the assertion.
