# Counting narrow-width witnesses through the degrees before substitution

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite union of progressions from a bounded search

Write \(w(d)=5d/2-1\) for even \(d\).
A base at a prime \(p>3\) consists of a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit \(x\) whose minimum and square minimum have the same degree \(e\), and two integer rows \(A_\pm\) of formal width \(w(e)\).
Require both signed graphs to contain both minima to order at least two, with nonvanishing numerators at the powered nodes, as in the [joint degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#statement).
Write \(H_\pm=\|A_\pm\|_1\) and choose either of the following tests.

* In the individual test use \(V=\max(H_+,H_-)^8\).
* In the transverse test require \(A_+A_-^{*w(e)}+A_-A_+^{*w(e)}\ne0\) and use \(V=(H_+H_-)^4\), with the [common formal-width reversal](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement).

The coefficient cutoff is \(V<2^p\).
For each admitted base let \(B_x\) be twice the product of the odd primes \(\ell\) for which \(x\in\mathbb Q(x)^\ell\).
Include its degrees
\[
              d=ek,\qquad (k,B_x)=1,\qquad k\equiv1\pmod p .             \tag{1}
\]
Each is witnessed at precisely \(w(d)\), with the same two coefficient lists up to their common substitution and translation.
In particular the normalized span of the *union* of the supports is unchanged.
Here the normalized span of a set with at least two integer positions is its maximum difference divided by the gcd of its differences.
The graph conditions rule out monomial rows, whose nonzero roots in these graphs are roots of unity.

For fixed \(p\) and any fixed finite bound \(E\) on \(e\), the degrees (1) from all admitted bases form an effectively computable finite union of residue classes.
Both its density and its count on any interval can be computed from the base search without factoring any graph at a lifted degree.
To include the width cost, keep a presentation of \(d\) precisely when
\[
                      (2p w(d)+1)^4 V<2^p.                                \tag{2}
\]
The resulting sets for each base are its residue classes intersected with an explicit initial interval.
These descriptions concern the degrees arising from the specified presentations.
They give lower bounds for tests allowing arbitrary placements.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Exact preservation at a fixed prime

By bounded-degree finiteness the integer \(B_x\) is computable.
Indeed an \(\ell\)th root of \(x\) in \(\mathbb Q(x)\) is a nontorsion unit of degree at most \(e\) and absolute logarithmic height \(h_{\rm W}(x)/\ell\).
Enumerate monic unit polynomials of degree at most \(e\) with coefficient norm at most \(2^e\exp(eh_{\rm W}(x))\), rounding the upper bound upwards.
Every possible root occurs in that box by the elementary coefficient bound from its Mahler measure.
The finite positive heights among the roots have a computable positive lower bound, giving a finite upper bound on \(\ell\).
Factor \(T^\ell-x\) over \(\mathbb Q(x)\) at each remaining prime.

For all \(k\) in (1), the [odd binomial induction](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#count) gives irreducibility of \(T^k-x\) over \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
It uses only the exclusion of prime powers in that field.
For \(y^k=x\), the two minima are \(P_1(T^k),P_2(T^k)\) where \(P_1,P_2\) are the minima of \(x,x^2\).
Indeed \(\mathbb Q(y^2)\) contains \(x^2\), hence \(x\) because \(\mathbb Q(x^2)=\mathbb Q(x)\), and then \(y\) since \(k\) is odd.
Their degrees equal \(ek\), their positive logarithmic Mahler measures equal those of \(P_1,P_2\), and reciprocity is preserved.
The proof permits coincidences among higher powers of conjugates of the base.

Set \(u=(k-1)/p\), \(v=(k-1-u)/2\).
Both \(k-1\) and \(u\) are even, and \(v\ge0\).
At width \(kw(e)+u\) the substituted rows \(A_\pm(Y^k)\) have signed graphs equal to the base graphs evaluated at \(X^k\), by \(pu+1=k\).
Since \(w(ek)=kw(e)+u+2v\), multiplying both rows by \(Y^v\) gives
\[
 \widetilde A_\pm(Y)=Y^v A_\pm(Y^k)
\]
at the required width, multiplying each substituted graph only by \(X^{pv}\).
The numerator remains nonzero and both multiplicities are preserved at the lifted unit and its square.
The two-row determinant is multiplied by \(Y^{2v+u}\) after the same substitution, so transversality is preserved.
The occupied positions on both signs undergo exactly the same map \(i\mapsto ki+v\).
Consequently neither one-norm nor the normalized span of their union changes.

For a finite degree bound \(E\), the coefficient cutoff bounds both \(H_\pm\), so each pair of rows and each genuine common minimum can be found by finite integer enumeration and polynomial factorization in degrees depending only on \(p,E\).
For one base the conditions \(d=ek\) and (1) specify classes modulo \(e\,\operatorname{lcm}(B_x,p)\).
The union can be formed over a common multiple of these periods, counting each residue only once.
The cutoff (2) is monotone in \(d\) for each base and is directly testable.

There is also a fixed-prime consequence for the full coefficient-only degree test at \(w(d)\).
One may impose any fixed upper bound on the normalized span of the union of the supports, or omit the bound.
If even one degree \(e\) passes this test, use its two rows and unit as a base.
Write \(B'=B_x/\gcd(B_x,p)\).
In every \([D,2D)\) its displayed lifts number
\[
             \frac{D}{ep}\frac{\varphi(B')}{B'}+O_{B_x}(1) .              \tag{3}
\]
This follows by inclusion-exclusion on the primes of \(B'\), since \(k\equiv1\bmod p\) already excludes division by \(p\).
Thus at a fixed \(p\) positive lower dyadic density is equivalent to the existence of a passing degree anywhere for these width-independent cutoffs.
For (2) the width alone bounds \(d\) at a fixed \(p\).

<a id="gap"></a>
## Slowly increasing base and joint span bounds in the gap criterion

Let \(D_j=2^j,\ p_j>3\) be computable primes with \(p_j\to\infty,\ p_j=o(D_j)\).
Choose arbitrary computable integer bounds \(E_j,C_j\to\infty\).
At block \(j\) perform the finite base calculation at \(p_j\) with
\[
                    e\le E_j,\qquad w(e)\le C_j.
\]
Let \(M_j\) be its union count in \([D_j,2D_j)\), for either the individual or the transverse test.
All the resulting pairs have joint normalized span at most \(C_j\).
For the coefficient cutoff alone, and also with (2) whenever \(p_j/j\to\infty\), one has
\[
                \liminf_j \frac{p_j M_j}{D_j}=0
                 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda\ge\frac{\log2}{120},   \tag{4}
\]
where \(\lambda\) denotes the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.
The same implication applies to the larger count of all genuine pairs counted by degree at the chosen budget with joint normalized span at most \(C_j\).
An additional support cap tending to infinity can also be imposed, by bounding the number of occupied base positions before constructing the union.

Indeed, if a positive measure lies below the asserted gap, the [irreducible reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) and [all-power representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) give a fixed reciprocal all-power base unit with positive logarithmic measure \(h<(\log2)/120\).
At its fixed degree \(e\) the [narrow-width supply](Yin_C_G0345_v466.md#narrow) yields genuine rows for both signs at every sufficiently large prime with \(\log H_\pm/p\le(15-10/e)h+o(1)\).
For the transverse version the [paired minima estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#budgets) gives genuine transverse rows with \(\log(H_+H_-)/p\le2(15-10/e)h+o(1)\).
Thus \(\log V/p\) lies below \(\log2\) by a fixed positive margin at every sufficiently large \(p\).
Both base bounds and any diverging support cap eventually admit these rows, since their union fits in \(w(e)+1\) positions.
When (2) is required its extra cost \(4\log(2p_jw(d)+1)/p_j\) tends uniformly to zero on the block under its stated prime hypothesis.
The count (3) for this one fixed base, with \(B'=B_x\) at every sufficiently large prime, gives \(\liminf_j p_j M_j/D_j\ge\varphi(B_x)/(e B_x)>0\).
The coefficients may vary with \(j\); the unit, degree and excluded primes stay fixed.

In particular, an empty base census along any sequence of primes tending to infinity, with both base bounds tending to infinity, suffices for the same gap.
This last test needs no dyadic intervals or relation between primes and block indices.
It may charge the width at the searched base itself, since \(4\log(2pw(e)+1)/p\to0\) for the fixed seed in the proof.
An upper bound establishing the required exclusion for increasing base bounds remains open.

A collaborating agent developed the progression formula, the effective base census and the joint normalized span restriction.
