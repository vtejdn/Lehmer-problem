# A uniform Salem gap for two Chebyshev traces at separation three

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two signs

Let \(D_j(u+u^{-1})=u^j+u^{-j}\) for \(j\ge0\).
There exists \(\epsilon>0\) such that every Salem number \(\sigma\) whose monic trace minimal polynomial \(S\) of degree \(m=k+1\), \(k\ge3\), is strictly interlaced by \(C=2D_k+\delta D_{k-3}\), \(\delta\in\{-1,1\}\), satisfies \(\sigma\ge1+\epsilon\).
Strict interlacing places the \(k\) roots of \(C\) in the respective open gaps between the ordered roots of \(S\).

<a id="phase"></a>
## The possible cyclotomic quotients

Suppose that \(\sigma_i\to1\) for a fixed sign.
Put \(T=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\), \(P=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\), \(B=2+\delta z^3\), and \(B^*=2z^3+\delta\).
The degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness for monic polynomials with bounded roots.
The positive residues \(c_x=C(x)/S'(x)\) sum to two, and \(t=c_T\) denotes the exterior residue.
By the [fixed lower polynomial argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v142.md#finite), after extraction the trace measures converge to a mass-two measure on \([-2,2]\), whose symmetric lift to the circle we denote by \(\mu\).
The integer moment series \(G_i=(1-z^2)(B+z^{2k-3}B^*)/P\) stabilizes coefficientwise to \(G=BE/F\), and \(P\) stabilizes to \(P_0=(1-z^2)F/E\).
The polynomials \(E,F\) are coprime squarefree cyclotomic products normalized at zero, \(1+z\mid E\), and every root of \(EF\) annihilates \(B+\eta z^r B^*\), where \(r=-3-\deg E+\deg F\) and \(\eta\) is the sign under reversal of \(E/F\).
The limiting doubled continuous density is \(D=G(z)+G(1/z)\) away from poles, with respect to Haar probability \(dm\), and must be nonnegative.

There is also a bound on the derivative at the compulsory zero.
In the trace variable, \(\int(x+2)^{-1}d\mu\le2B(-1)\) follows by partial fractions at \(-2\), since \(|C(-2)|=2B(-1)\) and \(|S(-2)|\ge1\).
The [symmetric integral kernel](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#profiles) gives
\[
 \frac{G(-v)}{1-v}
 =(1+v)\int\frac{d\mu(x)}{(1-v)^2+v(x+2)}
 \longrightarrow 2\int\frac{d\mu(x)}{x+2}.
\]
Dominated convergence applies for \(1/2<v<1\).
Dividing \(G'(-1)\) by \(B(-1)>0\) gives \(0<E'(-1)/F(-1)\le4\).

The [torsion phase identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v142.md#binomials) restricts the possible factors to
\[
 1-z,\quad 1+z,\quad \Phi_6=z^2-z+1,\quad
 \Phi_3=z^2+z+1,\quad z^6+\delta z^3+1.
\]
The last is \(\Phi_9\) or \(\Phi_{18}\).
Write \(a,1,c,d,e\) for their respective exponents in \(E/F\), each variable in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
Then \(\eta=1\) if \(a=0\) and \(-1\) otherwise, and
\[
                     r=-4-a-2(c+d)-6e,\qquad \eta=(-1)^r .
\]
For a root \(\xi\) of any occurring factor, the phase identity requires \(\xi^{r-3}=-\eta\) if \(\delta\xi^3=\pm1\), and \(\xi^{r-3}=\eta\) if \(\delta\xi^3\) is a primitive third root.
It follows that \(c\ne0\) requires \(3\mid r\), and \(d\ne0\) requires both \(3\mid r\) and \(a\ne0\).

For \(\delta=-1\), a primitive eighteenth root cannot have \(\xi^{r-3}=\eta\).
If \(r\) is even the exponent is odd and cannot be divisible by 18, while if \(r\) is odd the exponent is even and cannot be 9 modulo 18.
For \(\delta=1\), a primitive ninth root with \(\xi^{r-3}=\eta\) requires \(a=0\), \(r\equiv3\bmod9\), and hence \(d=0\).
Now \(r=-4-2c-6e\) is even and lies between \(-12\) and \(4\), so \(r=-6\) and \(c+3e=1\), impossible for \(e=\pm1\).
Thus \(e=0\) for both signs.
For \(a=0\) the pairs \((c,d)\) are \((0,0),(1,0)\).
For \(a=1\) they are \((0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,1)\), since a nonzero pair requires \(c+d\equiv-1\bmod3\).
For \(a=-1\) they are \((0,0),(1,-1),(-1,1)\), since then \(c+d\equiv0\bmod3\).
The triple \((1,1,1)\) gives \(E=1-z^6,\ F=1\) and violates the derivative bound.

<a id="density"></a>
## Signs of the eight remaining densities

Put \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(X=z^3+z^{-3}=x^3-3x\), and \(H=-x^3+x^2+2x+1\).
In the table a rational number is a value of \(x\) where \(D<0\).
The other entries give the actual \(D\) or an excessive atom of \(\mu\).

| \((a,c,d)\) | \(r\) | \(\delta=1\) | \(\delta=-1\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((0,0,0)\) | \(-4\) | \(-15/8\) | \((x+2)H\) |
| \((0,1,0)\) | \(-6\) | \(1/2\) | \((X+2)(3-X)\) |
| \((1,0,0)\) | \(-5\) | \(-15/8\) | \(15/8\) |
| \((1,0,-1)\) | \(-3\) | \(0\) | \(3/2\) |
| \((1,-1,0)\) | \(-3\) | \(-3/2\) | \(0\) |
| \((-1,0,0)\) | \(-3\) | \(-3/2\) | \(0\) |
| \((-1,1,-1)\) | \(-3\) | \(0\) | \(X+2\) |
| \((-1,-1,1)\) | \(-3\) | \(0\) | Mass three at one |

For explicit checks, in the first three rows the densities for either sign are respectively
\[
 (x+2)\{2+\delta(x^3-x^2-2x+1)\},\quad
 (X+2)\{2+\delta(X-1)\},\quad
 (4-x^2)\{2+\delta(x^3-2x)\}.
\]
In the last five rows \(D\) equals \(\delta\) times, in order,
\[
 (4-x^2)(x-1),\quad (4-x^2)(x+1),\quad
 -(x-1)(x+1)(x+2),\quad -(x-1)^2(x+2),\quad -(x+1)^2(x+2).
\]
These identities follow from \(D=(E/F)(B+\eta z^r B^*)\).
For the final minus row \(\lim_{v\uparrow1}(1-v)G(v)/2=3\), beyond the entire mass.
This proves the positive-sign assertion.
For the remaining minus rows write \(s=z^3\).
Their quotients \(E/F\) are \(1+z,\ 1+s,\ (1+s)/(1-s)\), respectively.

<a id="moving"></a>
## The first varying coefficient and its Fourier polynomial

Write \(P_0=N/J\) in lowest terms, both constants one.
In the three rows \(J=1,\Phi_6,\Phi_6\), respectively, and \(N=1-z,\ 1-z,\ (1-z)(1-s)\).
The first differing index \(n\) of \(JP\) from \(N\) tends to infinity and satisfies \(n\le m+\deg J/2\).
Indeed \(JP\) is reciprocal, and agreement to its central coefficient would force \(JP=N+z^{2m+\deg J}N(1/z)\) for large \(m\).
This product of \(N\) and a signed binomial has only circle roots, contradicting the exterior root.
Write \(p_i=[z^n](JP-N)\ne0\).
For any fixed \(j\), eventually \(n+j<2k-3\).
Consequently \(G_i JP=J(1-z^2)(B+z^{2k-3}B^*)\) and \(GN=J(1-z^2)B\) imply agreement of moment coefficients below \(n\) and a difference \(-2p_i\) at \(n\).
The positive neutral circle measure \(\lambda_i\) lifts all residues except \(t\).
For \(h\ge1\) the coefficient of \(G_i\) is \(2\int u^h\,d\lambda_i(u)+t(\sigma_i^h+\sigma_i^{-h})\).
At \(h=n-1\) the corresponding coefficient of \(G\) stays bounded by four, so \(t\sigma_i^n=O(1)\).

In the last row \(D=|1+s|^2\), and all atoms occur at \(s=1\).
The [prediction pairing for a rational prefix](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v143.md#prediction) applies with \(h(z)=1+s,\ A(z)=1-s\).
Indeed \(h,A\) have constant one and all zeros on the circle, so there is a fixed real polynomial \(q\) divisible by \(A\), \(q(0)=1\), with \(\int|qh|^2dm<2\).
The two vectors \(q(u)\) and \(u^nq(u^{-1})\) in \(L^2(\lambda_i)\) have squared norms tending to \(\int|qh|^2dm/2<1\).
Their baseline cross moment vanishes since \(q\) kills the atoms and \(D\) has finite Fourier support.
The actual top lag changes their pairing by \(-p_i\) up to the discarded exterior term, which is \(o(1)\) since \(q(1)=0\) and \(t\sigma_i^n=O(1)\).
This contradicts Cauchy–Schwarz.

The first two remaining rows have \(F=1\), \(E=1+z\) or \(1+s\) and \(N=1-z\).
Here \(G\) is a polynomial and \(\mu=D\,dm/2\) has no atom, so \(t\to0\) and \(\lambda_i\to\mu\).
The coefficient formula at \(n\) bounds \(p_i\).
Pass to constant \(p_i=p\), a limit of \(t\sigma_i^n\), and a weak limit of twice \(\lambda_i\) under \((z,w)=(u^{-1},u^n)\).
The projection of \(w\) against this limit has a density bounded by \(D\).
Its Fourier coefficients at \(j\) are the limits of \([z^{n+j}]G_i\) minus \(\lim t\sigma_i^n\), using the convention \(\widehat f(j)=\int z^{-j}f(z)dm\).
For fixed \(j<0\) the moments eventually equal zero.
Riemann–Lebesgue as \(j\to-\infty\) forces \(\lim t\sigma_i^n=0\).
All the Fourier coefficients are therefore integer limits and vanish at infinity.
The density is an integer polynomial \(U\) with \(U(0)=-2p,\ |U|\le D\), by Fourier uniqueness and continuity.
This is the [first varying moment construction](Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#moving) with the denominator cleared.

In the coefficient identity above, division of the difference by \(z^n\) gives in the limit
\[
                         G A=-N U,\qquad A\in\mathbb Z[[z]].
\]
Indeed \(A\) is the limit of \((JP-N)/z^n\), whose coefficients stabilize successively since the other series stabilize and \(G_i(0)=2\).
The upper band lies beyond every degree used in this comparison.
A root \(\beta\) of \(B=2-z^3\) in an algebraic closure of the dyadic field satisfies \(|\beta|_2=2^{-1/3}<1\).
The identity converges there and \(N(\beta)\ne0\), so the irreducible \(B\) divides \(U\) in \(\mathbb Z[z]\).

<a id="cubic"></a>
## The cubic density at the first varying coefficient

When \(E=1+z\), the double zero in \(D=(x+2)H\) at \(z=-1\) also gives \((1+z)^2\mid U\).
Thus \(U=(1+z)^2(2-s)V\) with \(V\in\mathbb Z[z]\), \(V(0)=-p\ne0\) and \(|V|\le H/|2-s|\).
The Laurent expansion is \(H=3-(z+z^{-1})+(z^2+z^{-2})-(z^3+z^{-3})\).
The coefficients of \(H^2\) at \(0,3,6\) are \(15,-8,1\).
Using \(|2-s|^{-2}=\frac13\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}2^{-|j|}s^j\), Parseval gives
\[
                  \sum_j |[z^j]V|^2\le(15-8+1/2)/3=5/2 .
\]
At \(z=i\) the bound is \(1/\sqrt5\).
At \(\zeta=e^{3\pi i/5}\), \(x=(1-\sqrt5)/2\), \(H=x+1=(3-\sqrt5)/2\), and \(|2-s|\ge1\).
A constant \(V=\pm1\) is impossible at \(i\).
Thus \(V\) is, up to sign, \(1\pm z^b\) for some \(b>0\).
It must vanish at both \(i\) and \(\zeta\), since each bound is smaller than the smallest nonzero chord of the respective fourth or tenth roots of unity, namely \(\sqrt2\) or \((\sqrt5-1)/2\).
The plus sign requires even \(b\) at \(i\) and odd \(b\) at \(\zeta\).
The minus sign requires \(20\mid b\).
At \(z=\exp(i(3\pi/5+\pi/b))\) the latter binomial has modulus two, while \(-1\le x\le0\) since \(b\ge20\).
On this interval \(H\le1\) by \(H-1=-x(x-2)(x+1)\le0\).
This contradicts \(|V|\le H/|2-s|\).

<a id="graphs"></a>
## The two possible phases for the last density

For \(E=1+s\), \(D=(X+2)(3-X)\) forces \((1+s)^2\mid U\), by the double zero at each of the three roots of \(1+s\).
Thus \(U=(1+s)^2(2-s)V\), \(V(0)=-p\).
The Haar image under \(z\mapsto s=z^3\) is Haar, so the [adjacent-sign integral](Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#moving) gives
\[
      \int |V|^2\,dm\le\int\frac{(3-X)^2}{5-2X}\,dm
                         =\frac74+\frac1{12}=\frac{11}{6}<2 .
\]
Hence \(V=-p=\pm1\).
Use \(w=-p u^n,\ z=u^{-1}\) in the doubled limit measure, now denoted by \(\Phi\), and let \(B_j\,dm\) be the projections of \(w^j d\Phi\).
Then
\[
            B_0=(X+2)(3-X),\qquad B_1=(X+2)s(2-s).
\]

For the higher projections, the roots \(y_1<\cdots<y_k\) of \(C\) lie in \((-2,2)\) by the alternating signs of \(C(2\cos(l\pi/k))=(-1)^l(4-2\cos(3l\pi/k))\) for \(0\le l\le k\).
Interlacing in the residue product then gives \(t\ge 2(T-y_k)/(T-x_1)\ge2(T-2)/(T+2)\), where \(x_1\) is the least trace root.
Consequently \(t\sigma_i^{jn}=O_j((\sigma_i-1)^{-2(j-1)})\) for each \(j\ge1\).
For fixed integer \(r\), the Fourier coefficient at \(r\) of \((1-z)^{2j}B_j\) is the limit of the integer sum of trace moments at \(jn+r-l\), weighted by \((-p)^j(-1)^l\binom{2j}{l}\), \(0\le l\le2j\).
The removed exterior terms are \(t\sigma_i^{jn+r}(1-\sigma_i^{-1})^{2j}\) and \(t\sigma_i^{-jn-r}(1-\sigma_i)^{2j}\) up to the common sign, both tending to zero.
As \(|B_j|\le B_0\), these limiting integers vanish for \(|r|\) large by Riemann–Lebesgue.
The resulting integer Laurent polynomial is divisible by \((1-z)^{2j}\) because its modulus is bounded by \(|1-z|^{2j}B_0\).
Hence each \(B_j\) is an integer Laurent polynomial in \(z\), including negative \(j\) by conjugation.

Multiply \(\Phi\) by \(|1-w|^2\) to obtain \(\Lambda\).
Its projected moments \(L_j=2B_j-B_{j-1}-B_{j+1}\) have marginal
\[
                      K=L_0=(X+2)(2-X)^2=|(1+s)(1-s)^2|^2.
\]
The bound \(|L_j|\le K\) forces vanishing at every root of \(1+s\) to order at least two and at every root of \(1-s\) to order at least four.
Thus \(L_j/K\in\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\), since \(K\) is a monomial unit times \((1+s)^2(1-s)^4\).
Its modulus is at most one, so it is zero or a signed monomial in \(z\) by Parseval.
Condition \(\Lambda\) on \(z\) with marginal \(K\,dm\).
The indices of nonzero quotients form a subgroup, because a conditional moment of modulus one makes that power of \(w\) constant on the fiber, and such powers are closed under multiplication and inversion.
These conclusions hold simultaneously outside one null set.
If the subgroup is trivial all the conditional laws are Haar, incompatible with \(\int |1-w|^{-2}d\Lambda\le\Phi(\mathbb T^2)<\infty\).
Otherwise the subgroup is \(b\mathbb Z,\ b\ge1\), and the \(b\)-th conditional moment is \(W=\pm z^l\).
The conditional law is uniform on the roots of \(w^b=W\) by Fourier uniqueness, and \(W\ne1\) almost everywhere since \(\Lambda\{w=1\}=0\).

The fiber average of \((1-\overline w)^{-1}\) in this law equals \(1/(1-\overline W)\) by the geometric sum.
Recovering \(B_0-B_1\) from \(\Lambda\) away from \(w=1\) gives
\[
       \frac1{1-\overline W}=\frac{B_0-B_1}{K}
           =\frac{3-X-s(2-s)}{(2-X)^2}=\frac{s}{s-1}.
\]
Hence \(W=s\), including its phase in the original \(z\) coordinate.
The conditional average of \(|1-w|^{-2}\) is \(b/|1-s|^2\) by orthogonality in the squared geometric sum.
The density of the part of \(\Phi\) at \(w=1\) is therefore \((X+2)(3-X-b(2-X))\).
Its nonnegativity as \(X\downarrow-2\) gives \(b\le5/4\), so \(b=1\).
Thus the support of \(\Phi\) lies on \(w=1\) or \(w=s\), by the same [conditional moment argument](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#graphs) with all quotients taken in the original Laurent ring.

<a id="trace"></a>
## A positive integer that tends to zero

The graphs give \(u^n=-p\) or \(u^{n+3}=-p\) in the limiting test.
Put \(Q_i(u)=(u^n+p)(u^{n+3}+p)\) and let \(A_i\in\mathbb Z[x]\) satisfy \(A_i(u+u^{-1})=Q_i(u)Q_i(u^{-1})\), using the integral basis \(1,D_1,D_2,\ldots\) of symmetric integral Laurent polynomials.
The sum \(\sum_x c_x(2-x)^4 A_i(x)^2\) is a positive integer by the [remainder and trace identity](Yin_C_G0345_v47.md#trace).
Indeed it is the coefficient of degree \(m-1\) in the remainder of \(C(2-x)^4 A_i^2\) modulo \(S\), and the exterior summand is positive.
On the neutral circle the fixed continuous test \((2-x)^4|(1-w)(1-w/s)|^4\) vanishes on both graphs, so the neutral contribution tends to zero.
The exterior contribution is \(O(t(T-2)^4\sigma_i^{4n+6})=O((\sigma_i-1)^2)\) by the \(j=4\) bound above.
This contradicts positivity of the integer.
Both signs are now excluded along sequences tending to one, which proves the uniform gap.
