# Subfields and small elements of a reciprocal unit field

<a id="statement"></a>
## The field lattice and the bounded spectrum

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit such that distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient.
Put \(A=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\), \(d=[A:\mathbb Q]\), and write \(m(y)=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]h(y)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Fix \(M>0\) and assume \(m(x)\le M\) and \(d\) sufficiently large depending on \(M\).
The \(d/2\) inverse pairs have a [unique minimal block partition](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) into \(u\) blocks of size \(b=d/(2u)\ge9\), where \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\).
Let \(E\) be the field fixed in the splitting field by the setwise stabilizer of the block through the label of \(x\).
Thus \(E\subset F\) and \([E:\mathbb Q]=u\).
In the [independent inversion case](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) set \(J=E\).
In the other case let \(J=K\) be the [field of the oriented half containing \(x\)](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields), of degree \(2u\) with \(K\cap F=E\).

* The subfields of \(A\) are exactly \(A,F\) and the subfields of \(J\), including \(J\) itself.
  The proper subfields of \(F\) are exactly the subfields of \(E\).
  One has \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(F)=1\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(A)=\{1,\iota\}\), where \(\iota(x)=x^{-1}\).
* There is a unit \(\rho\) in \(A\) with \(x=\eta\rho^k\) for \(\eta\in\mu(A)\), \(k\ge1\), such that every \(\zeta\rho^j\) with \(\zeta\in\mu(A)\), \(j\ne0\), generates \(A\) and has measure \(|j|m(x)/k\).
  Every nonzero \(y\in A\) outside \(J\) and outside this line satisfies, with an absolute constant \(c>0\),
  \[
               m(y)\ \ge\ \frac{c\,d}{2m(x)(\log(3d))^{212}}.                 \tag{1}
  \]
  In particular, for any fixed \(N>0\), at sufficiently large \(d\) depending only on \(M,N\), the elements with \(0<m(y)\le N\) consist exactly of those in \(J\) and those on the indicated line satisfying that bound.
* Suppose \(u=1\), or every block contains a pair on the unit circle, or every block contains a pair of real roots.
  With \(\theta>1\) the real root of \(X^3-X-1\), at sufficiently large \(d\) depending only on \(M\) the positive measures below \(\log\theta\) in \(A\) are **exactly** the positive integer multiples of \(m(x)/k\) below \(\log\theta\).
  In particular the conclusion applies when fewer than \(b\) inverse pairs have roots off the unit circle.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Blocks of roots and their intermediate fields

Write \(G\) for the Galois group of the rational splitting field of \(x\).
In a transitive \(G\)-set, the blocks \(C\) containing a point correspond to the subgroups containing its stabilizer, by taking the subgroup orbit of that point or the setwise stabilizer of the block.
In particular the latter is transitive on \(C\), and inclusion of such blocks reverses inclusion of their fixed fields.
For the label action on inverse pairs, any block of size at least two through the base label contains a minimal such block through that label by finite descent.
Uniqueness of the minimal partition gives precisely the block defining \(E\).
This proves the assertion about the subfields of \(F\).

Now use the action on individual roots, and let \(C\) be a nonsingleton block containing \(x\).
If \(C\) contains both roots of any inverse pair, transitivity of its stabilizer shows that it contains the inverse of every one of its roots.
In particular it contains the pair through \(x\), and the corresponding field lies in \(F\).
In the independent inversion case choose any \(y\in C\) with label different from that of \(x\), whenever such a root exists.
Flip the pair of \(x\) and a spare pair in its block, avoiding the label of \(y\).
This fixes \(y\), so preserves \(C\), and puts \(x^{-1}\) in \(C\).
The only other nonsingleton possibility is the pair itself.

In the orientation case assume \(C\) contains no full inverse pair and choose \(y\in C\) distinct from \(x\).
The [supported alternating group](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) acts simultaneously and naturally on the two halves over each label block and fixes every root over the other blocks.
If \(y\) lies over another block, the entire alternating group on the block through \(x\) preserves \(C\) by fixing \(y\), and its orbit of \(x\) is the whole selected half.
If \(y\) belongs to the same half as \(x\), the alternating subgroup fixing \(x\) carries \(y\) to every other root of this half.
If \(y\) lies in the opposite half of the same block, their labels are different by assumption.
The stabilizer of \(x\) supplies all roots of that opposite half except at the label of \(x\), and the stabilizer of \(y\) supplies those of the half of \(x\) except at the label of \(y\).
These include a full inverse pair at each spare label, a contradiction.
Thus \(C\) contains the entire selected half through \(x\), and its fixed field lies in \(K\).
Conversely \(K\subset A\), and \(\iota\) interchanges the two halves above the base block, inducing the nontrivial \(K/E\) automorphism by the [quadratic field description](Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#quadratic).
In particular \(K\cap F=E\).
This proves the lattice assertion, including the single-block case.

The stabilizer of the base label fixes no other label, since a three-cycle can move any specified other label while fixing the base label.
Likewise the root stabilizer fixes only \(x,x^{-1}\), because the fourth power of any lift of such a three-cycle fixes all roots outside the three moved pairs and still induces the three-cycle.
Indeed the sign changes over fixed labels vanish upon taking an even power.
An automorphism of a simple extension sends its generator to one of the conjugates fixed by its stabilizer.
The inverse map on \(x\) is genuinely realized since \(x\) is reciprocal and \(\mathbb Q(x^{-1})=A\).
This proves the two automorphism assertions and gives a group-theoretic proof of the [previous automorphism bound for the root field](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#subfields).

## Simultaneous height bounds for arbitrary elements of the field

Let \(\Gamma\subset A^\times\) consist of the elements some positive power of which belongs to \(\mu(A)\langle x\rangle\).
Every element of \(\Gamma\) is a unit by its finite valuations, and \(\Gamma/\mu(A)\) is infinite cyclic by [Dirichlet's unit theorem](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U001.md#con26-u001).
Indeed it is the intersection of the rational line through the class of \(x\) with the free unit lattice modulo torsion.
Choose a lift \(\rho\) of a generator, reversing it if necessary so that \(x=\eta\rho^k\) with \(k>0\).
For \(y\in\Gamma\setminus\mu(A)\) write \(y^r=\xi x^s\) with \(r>0,\ s\ne0,\ \xi\) torsion.
Every automorphism of the splitting field fixing \(y\) sends \(x\) to a conjugate whose quotient by \(x\) is torsion, hence fixes \(x\).
It follows that \(\mathbb Q(y)=A\).
Height invariance under torsion and scaling by \(|j|\) under a \(j\)-th power now give the asserted measure of \(\zeta\rho^j\).

For \(y\in A^\times\setminus(J\cup\Gamma)\), the lattice classification gives \([\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\ge d/2\).
The pair \((x,y)\) is multiplicatively independent and has joint degree \(d\).
The [Amoroso–David product estimate in dimension two](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
       \frac{2m(x)m(y)}{d^2}\ \ge\ h(x)h(y)\
          \ge\frac{c}{d(\log(3d))^{212}} .
\]
This proves (1) and the description for each fixed \(N\).
Every torsion element belongs to \(J\), since its cyclotomic field is abelian whereas \(A,F\) have degrees greater than two and the automorphism groups just computed.
Thus the statement also covers elements of zero measure outside the displayed line.
The earlier [least-degree result](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#minimum) addresses selected witnesses, and the [result for products of conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#statement) starts in a divisible group in the splitting field.
Here an arbitrary field element of bounded measure belongs to the line or to the specified subfield.

## Real embeddings, neutral pairs and the Smyth threshold

If each block contains an inverse pair on the unit circle, complex conjugation fixes that pair label and hence stabilizes its block.
It fixes every embedding of \(E\), so \(E\) is totally real.
In the orientation case it swaps the two halves in every block, since it takes each root of that neutral pair to its inverse.
On the embeddings of \(K\) the interchange of the two halves over each embedding of \(E\) is composition with the nontrivial \(\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)\).
Thus complex conjugation acts in that way at every embedding, and \(K/E\) is totally imaginary quadratic over a totally real field.
If each block instead contains a real inverse pair, complex conjugation fixes both roots of this pair and hence both halves of its block.
In this case \(E\), and also \(K\) when present, are totally real.
When \(u=1\), \(J\) is rational or quadratic, so again it is totally real or CM.

A nontorsion unit \(y\) in a totally real or CM field has \(h(y)\ge(\log\varphi)/2\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Indeed, for the identity or canonical complex conjugation \(\kappa\), the condition \(y\kappa(y)=1\) would put all conjugates on the unit circle, contradicting Kronecker's theorem.
Otherwise the bound follows from [Schinzel's Theorem 2 for a linear polynomial](../audits/literature/Sch73/Yin_U001.md#linear-height).
Such a unit has degree at least two, so \(m(y)\ge\log\varphi>\log\theta\).
A nonunit or nonintegral algebraic number of positive measure has \(m(y)\ge\log2\), by its primitive polynomial's leading or constant coefficient.
Consequently \(J\) contributes no positive measure below \(\log\theta\) in all three stated cases.
The bounded-spectrum result with \(N=\log\theta\) gives the asserted progression.
Every term in it comes from a reciprocal unit by [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).

## Relative units and a single exterior orbit

A nontorsion unit generating \(A\) is reciprocal exactly when it has norm one to \(F\), since the only possible nonidentity automorphism sending it to its inverse is \(\iota\).
A proper-field norm-one unit in \(F\) satisfies \(y^2=1\).
In the orientation case the norm-one units in \(K\) are exactly those with \(N_{K/E}(y)=1\), by the restriction of \(\iota\).
Thus all nontorsion relative units generate \(A\) except precisely the nontorsion relative units of \(K/E\) when this field occurs.
No nontorsion reciprocal element generates \(F\), since its automorphism group is trivial.

Let \(s\) be the number of real places of \(F\) splitting in \(A\) and \(c\) its number of complex places.
The relative norm-one unit group has rank \(s+c\).
Indeed its rank is the difference of the two Dirichlet unit ranks, since the norm image contains the squares of the units of \(F\).
If \(r\) is the number of real places of \(F\), then \(A\) has \(2s\) real places and \(r-s+2c\) complex places, giving that difference.
Each split real place has \(|x+x^{-1}|>2\) at its trace embedding and supplies one real exterior root.
Each complex place of \(F\) supplies one conjugate pair of nonreal exterior roots, because neither root over a nonreal trace can have modulus one.
The remaining real places supply only roots on the unit circle.
Hence \(s+c\) counts exactly the orbits of exterior roots under ordinary complex conjugation.

If there is a single such orbit, the norm-one group modulo its torsion is infinite cyclic.
Since \(x\) is one of its nontorsion elements, every other nontorsion relative unit lies in \(\Gamma\) and generates \(A\).
All reciprocal unit generators are therefore precisely \(\xi\epsilon^j\) for \(j\ne0\), with \(\epsilon\) a generator modulo relative torsion and \(\xi\) a relative torsion unit.
This description of generators holds at every measure.
The bounded description for arbitrary elements still applies since there are at most two nonneutral labels.
For real Salem numbers the fixed-field assertion that all Salem numbers are positive powers of the smallest one is classical. [Smyth, Proposition 3](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U001.md#smy15-u001)
The present conclusions include a single nonreal exterior orbit without the earlier [trace-graph assumption](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#fields).
Neither generator description supplies a uniform lower bound for its positive measure as the fields vary.
