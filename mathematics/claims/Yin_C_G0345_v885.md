# A coefficient test for a common power and an integral binomial construction

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two units at each height and an obstruction at arbitrary degrees

Write \(p=q+1,\ M=pq\) for integers \(q\ge2\).
At each such \(q\) and each positive multiple \(m\) of \(3M\), the construction below gives an irreducible integral trace polynomial of degree \(m+1\) and positive algebraic units \(Y>T>1\) satisfying
\[
 (p-qT)Y=pT-q,\qquad N_{\mathbb Q(w)/\mathbb Q}(1-Mw)=1,\qquad w=T+T^{-1}-2.
\]
The auxiliary polynomial has nonreal roots and recovers the necessary [large complex roots](Yin_C_G0345_v884.md#zeros) at every fixed nonzero index as \(m\to\infty\).
For every \(q\ge17\) the two units are multiplicatively independent.
The obstruction holds no matter how rapidly the trace degree grows with \(q\).

More generally, suppose actual four-term sections of consecutive coefficient magnitudes admit nontorsion unit roots at unbounded \(q\).
Let \(\beta\) be the primitive unit after the common dilation, \(0<u<v\) the coprime exponents, and \(T=\pm\beta^u\).
Let \(H\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(w=T+T^{-1}-2\), of degree \(n\).
On the branch \(M^nH(1/M)=1\), write \(H=x^n+(Mx-1)G\).
Eventually \(G\) has degree \(n-2\).
If \(A,B\) are its leading and next coefficients, then
\[
 MA\sim2n,\qquad \frac{2B}{A}=MA-3+o(1).
\]
In particular \(B/A\) cannot be an integer at arbitrarily large \(q\), even for auxiliaries with nonreal roots and traces of arbitrary signature.
These statements use the actual minimal trace polynomial; lower-degree factors of a reducible trial require their own test.

<a id="construction"></a>
## An irreducible integral trace from shifted powers

Start with \(((x+2)^m-x^m)/M\).
Round each nonconstant coefficient to the nearest even integer and round the constant to the nearest integer congruent to two modulo four, taking the smaller integer in a tie.
Call the resulting polynomial \(G\) and put \(H=x^{m+1}+(Mx-1)G\).
The constant of \(G\) is positive since \(m\ge3M\ge18\).
Its first three coefficients
\[
 A=2m/M,\qquad B=2m(m-1)/M,\qquad C=8\binom m3/M
\]
are already even integers because \(3M\mid m\).
After monic normalization, the sum of the squares of the roots of \(G\) is
\[
 (B/A)^2-2C/A=(m-1)(5-m)/3<0.
\]
Thus \(G\) has nonreal roots.
For each fixed \(j\ne0\), one of its roots is \(2m/(2\pi i j)+O_j(1)\), uniformly along the allowed \(q,m\) as \(m\to\infty\).
Indeed on any compact disk in \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\), multiplying the rounded polynomial at \(x=2mz\) by \(M/x^m\) gives
\[
 (1+1/(mz))^m-1+O(M/m^4)=e^{1/z}-1+O(1/m).
\]
The rounding errors are bounded by two and start at degree at most \(m-4\), so a geometric series gives the first error.
On a disk about \(1/(2\pi i j)\) containing no other limit zero and avoiding zero, [Rouché's theorem](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk) supplies a root.
The limit zero is simple, so dividing the limiting function by its linear zero factor on the disk gives error \(O_j(1/m)\) in \(z\), as asserted.

The monic \(H\) is Eisenstein at two, since all its lower coefficients are even and its constant is two modulo four.
We have \(H(0)<0<H(1/M)=M^{-(m+1)}\).
Choose any root \(0<w<1/M\) and let \(T>1\) solve \(T+T^{-1}=2+w\).
Both \(T\) and its inverse are integral.
Since \(M^{m+1}H(1/M)=1\), the algebraic integer \(1-Mw=(p-qT)(p-qT^{-1})\) is a unit; hence each factor is a unit.
Consequently \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\) is a unit as well, since \(pT-q=T(p-qT^{-1})\).
The inequality \(w<1/M\) gives \(1<T<p/q\), and \(pT-q-T(p-qT)=q(T^2-1)>0\) gives \(Y>T\).

<a id="independence"></a>
## A norm excludes a common base for every large height

If \(Y,T\) were multiplicatively dependent then their positivity and \(Y>T>1\) would give coprime integers \(0<u<v\) and an algebraic unit \(\beta>1\) in their multiplicative group with \(T=\beta^u,\ Y=\beta^v\).
Indeed \(T^v=Y^u\) with coprime exponents, and a Bézout combination gives the common base.
Substitution would give \(q(1-\beta^{u+v})-p(\beta^u-\beta^v)=0\).
For every embedding the [strict radial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#radius) then gives \(r+r^{-1}\le2p/q\) for \(r=|T_i|\), using reciprocity for an interior root.
Hence every conjugate \(w_i\) of \(w\) satisfies
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}w_i|\le |r-r^{-1}|\le2\sqrt{(p/q)^2-1}.
\]

Put \(a=MA=2m\) and \(s_2=\sum_i w_i^2\).
The second coefficient after the leading one of \(H\) is \(MB-A\), so \(s_2=a^2-2(MB-A)=a(2+2/M)\).
Since \(\sum_iw_i=-a\), this yields \(\sum_i(w_i+2)^2=4+4m/M\).
The trace polynomial is real, so the sum of the absolute squares exceeds this last sum by precisely \(2\sum_i(\operatorname{Im}w_i)^2\).
The average of \(|w_i+2|^2\) would therefore be at most
\[
 \frac{4+4m/M}{m+1}+8\big((1+1/q)^2-1\big)
 \le \frac8M+\frac{16}{q}+\frac8{q^2}<1
 \qquad(q\ge17).
\]
The upper bound decreases with \(q\) and is strictly below one at 17.
But \(H(-2)\) is a nonzero integer by irreducibility in degree greater than one.
The product of \(|w_i+2|^2\) is at least one, contradicting the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
This argument excludes common powers for every distinguished positive root selected above regardless of how many real or complex places the trace field has.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## The first two auxiliary coefficients of an actual solution

For actual unit roots as \(q\to\infty\), the [short-power height and equidistribution](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#height) give Haar measure on the unit circle for the conjugates of \(T\).
The allowed sign only rotates Haar measure by zero or half a turn.
The radial bound puts all these conjugates in a fixed compact annulus away from zero, so the continuous first two powers of \(T+T^{-1}-2\) can be averaged in the limit.
Every embedding of the trace field extends with equal multiplicity to the field of \(T\).
For the roots \(w_i\) of \(H\), the Haar averages of \(2\cos\theta-2\) and \((2\cos\theta-2)^2\) consequently give
\[
 a/n=-\sum_iw_i/n\longrightarrow2,\qquad s_2/n\longrightarrow6.
\]
The trace degree grows since the field of \(T\) has the [primitive degree](Yin_C_G0345_v879.md#field) and is at most quadratic over its trace field.
When the norm is one, evaluation at \(1/M\) and Gauss's lemma give \(G\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
Its degree is \(n-2\) eventually because \(a/n\to2\), and \(MA=a\).
The identity \(s_2=a^2-2(MB-A)\) now gives \(2B/A=a-s_2/a+2/M=a-3+o(1)\).
Since \(M\) is even, \(a-3\) is odd, excluding integral \(B/A\) for all sufficiently large \(q\).
