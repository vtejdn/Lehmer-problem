# Nonreal conjugates carry the growing second Mahler measure of every near-one polynomial value

<a id="statement"></a>
## A mass alternative for arbitrary integer polynomials

Write \(A=M(P)>1\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure of any nonzero integer polynomial, \(m=\log A\), \(D=\deg A\) and \(\mu=\log M(A)\).
As \(m\to0\) on the branch \(M(A)>A^2\), uniformly in all inputs,
\[
                      \sum_{\sigma A\ {\rm real}}
                                  |\log|\sigma A||=o(\mu).                 \tag{1}
\]
Each conjugate is counted once.
The nonreal exterior conjugates therefore supply \((1-o(1))\mu\) in positive log mass; the nonreal interior conjugates separately supply \((1-o(1))\mu\) in absolute negative log mass.
For each fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) both contributions on this branch are at least
\[
                     \exp\!\big(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big)
\]
for sufficiently small \(m>0\).
This applies to reducible polynomials without a limit on the number, multiplicities or degrees of their factors.

In particular, for a sequence of integer polynomials with positive measures tending to one, either **one** of these nonreal log masses staying bounded implies \(A\le M(A)\le A^2\) eventually.
Conversely that interval implies both masses tend to zero.
Thus failure of a gap on sequences with either one bounded is equivalent to failure of the [single delay-one norm gap](Yin_C_G0901_v135.md#statement).
The nonreal mass is that of the first measure as an algebraic number, rather than a sum over the roots of the original polynomial.

<a id="conditional"></a>
## Averaging while fixing the other active spaces

Use the [factor-chain decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules).
Among the active leading spaces in the class of \(A\) take \(W\) attached to a term \(z\) of largest compressed degree \(d=2ub\); distinct active spaces have unmatched representatives.
Write \(v\) for the projection of \([A]\) on \(W\), \(v'\) for the remaining class, \(\lambda\) for log modulus, and \(L=\lambda(g[A])\), \(L_1=\lambda(gv)\), \(L'=\lambda(gv')\) at a uniform element \(g\) of a finite common normal group.
Then \(L=L_1+L'\) and \(\mathbb E|L|=2\mu/D\).
Let \(\mathcal R\) be the event that \(gA\) is real.
Equivariance shows this requires \(cgv=gv\) on every active space, for ordinary complex conjugation \(c\).
All attached degrees grow uniformly as \(m\to0\) by bounded-degree finiteness.

Over the other active splitting fields, the [relative alternating theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) supplies the independent supported alternating actions on the labels of \(z\).
The [normal subgroup calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v142.md#average) gives the pure supported three-cycles in the oriented case and the even flips as well in the independent signed case.
The subgroup fixing those fields has no fixed space on \(W\), since its local signed or sum-zero representations have no invariant vectors.
Averaging on its cosets first thus gives \(L'\) as the conditional mean of \(L\); in particular
\[
                    \mathbb E|L'|\le\mathbb E|L|,\qquad
                    \mathbb E|L_1|\le2\mathbb E|L|.                     \tag{2}
\]
On every such coset the probability of \(cgv=gv\) is \(o(1)\), uniformly.
For a single projected coordinate, the destination label must be fixed with positive sign and thus real, with at most \(O(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=o(b)\) choices in any block.
For two exceptions the same conclusion follows by conditioning on one position as in the [two-coordinate count](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#two).
With at least three exceptions the [sparse](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#sparse) and [dense](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#dense) estimates apply conditionally, since their bounds use only those supported actions, uniformly in every initial transport of the blocks.
It follows that \(\mathbb E(|L'|{\bf1}_{\mathcal R})=o(\mathbb E|L|)\).

For noncoordinate \(v\), the same [permutation and absolute first-moment proof](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#reduction) on \(W\) gives \(\mathbb E(|L_1|{\bf1}_{\mathcal R})=o(\mathbb E|L_1|)\).
This use needs only the pure leading representation on \(W\), also when \(z\) has further block-product terms: its projected coordinates sum to zero on each oriented block, the signed group is unchanged, and its actual real-label bound is that for \(z\).
The maximum-log projection estimate holds for the linear functional on its orbit exactly as in that proof.
By (2) this proves (1) whenever \(v\) is noncoordinate.

Suppose the label field of some other active term \(z_j\) is not contained in the splitting field of \(z\).
Over the latter field the image on the labels of \(z_j\) is a nontrivial normal subgroup, hence contains its [unique minimal product of alternating groups](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
Normality again gives the supported lifts and even flips in the respective models.
On those cosets \(L_1\) is fixed, while the probability that the projection to \(W_j\) is \(c\)-fixed is \(o(1)\) by the same three cases at \(z_j\).
Thus \(\mathbb E(|L_1|{\bf1}_{\mathcal R})=o(\mathbb E|L|)\) here as well, whether or not \(v\) is a coordinate.

<a id="nested"></a>
## Degree when all remaining label fields are contained in the first splitting field

It remains to consider a coordinate \(v\) and all other active label fields inside \(L_z\), the splitting field of \(z\).
Write \(P_z\) for its label field, and \(P_j,L_j,d_j=2u_jb_j\) for the label field, splitting field and degree at a remaining attached term.
The abelian sign kernel of \(L_z/P_z\) has trivial image on \(P_j\), since the label group there has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup.
Thus \(P_j\subseteq P_z\).
Equality is excluded by the relative alternating theorem for \(z\) over the unmatched field \(L_j\), including a tied degree.
The nontrivial kernel on restriction from \(P_z\) to \(P_j\) contains \((A_b)^u\).
All these \(P_j\) therefore lie in one field of degree at most \(2^u u!\).
The only nonabelian composition factors of this quotient come from a subgroup of \(S_u\), by the [canonical wreath action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).
In particular \(|A_{b_j}|\le u!\), which implies \(b_j\le u\) at the large attached degrees.
Together with \(u_j\ll(\log(3d_j))^{212}\) and \(d_j\le d\), this bounds every \(d_j\) by a fixed power of \(\log(3d)\).

The number of remaining active spaces is also at most a fixed power of \(\log(3d)\).
Indeed assign each one to an input factor whose chain contains its representative or a matched term of the same degree and space.
Each assigned factor has log measure at least that of its term, hence at least \(2/(\log(3d))^3\) by [Voutier's bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
The sum of input factor log measures with multiplicity is \(m\).
Within each chain the selected degrees at most \(d\) halve or better at every successive step by the [block-product degree bound](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent); thus there are at most \(O(\log(3d))\) choices there.
Above the field of degree \(2^u u!\) just bounded, all the \(L_j\) can be adjoined using at most \(\sum_j d_j/2\) square roots, by the inverse-pair quadratics over their label fields.
The coordinate \(v\) has \(d\) images; its image and the action on all \(L_j\) determine the conjugate of the full class of \(A\).
This class has exactly \(D\) images by the strict Perron property.
Consequently
\[
                     \log(3D)\ll(\log(3d))^{C_0}                       \tag{3}
\]
for an absolute \(C_0\), also when there is no other active term.

<a id="integral"></a>
## An integral unit representing the remainder after one root power

The signed label of the coordinate \(v\) is fixed by \(c\), since \(A\) is real.
Choose the corresponding actual real conjugate of \(z\), taking sign and inverse to make it greater than one.
Take its primitive positive unit \(r\) on that line in its real root field.
Compression gives a common field \(\mathbb Q(r)\) of degree \(d\) and preserves degrees of all nonzero powers. [Primitive lines](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#match)
Also \(\mathbb Q(r)\subseteq\mathbb Q(A)\): fixing \(A\) fixes its projected coordinate, whose \(d\) signed images biject with the \(d\) actual conjugates of the selected root.

For any irreducible input factor whose measure \(A_h\) projects nontrivially to \(W\), this term is the first active one in that factor's chain.
Indeed an earlier active degree \(d'\) would have \(d\ll(\log(3d'))^{212}\) by the block-product degree bound, but \(d'\le\deg A_h\le D\) by the [leading orbit bound](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#rank) and the [Perron field inclusion](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#factors).
This contradicts (3) for large \(d\).
Lossless descent through the preceding zero projections makes each such \(A_h\) a positive integral power of \(M(r)\), since the matched compressed term is a signed conjugate of a power of \(r\), with full degree. [Factor descent](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#factors)
For their aggregate exponent \(e\ge1\) this gives \(e\log M(r)\le m\) and \(v=e[M(r)]_W\).
The exterior coefficients of the conjugates of \(r\) are \(1,0,-1\) on the signed pairs.
In the independent model the selected exterior position contributes one, and in the oriented model the difference from the other entries on that block is one or two when projected to its single coordinate; it is positive since the selected entry is one and the difference is nonzero. [Coordinate comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval)
Consequently
\[
                   v=je[r]_W,\quad j\in\{1,2\},\qquad
                   C=A/r^{je}\in\mathbb Q(A)
\]
with \(C\) an algebraic unit and \([C]_W=0\).
The chosen real positive \(r\) here need not be a Mahler value.
In the independent model \([r]_W=[r]\); in the oriented model the projection subtracts the mean of the \(b\) classes in that half.
Each of those classes has the same averaged absolute log as \(r\).
Thus \(\mathbb E|L_1|\le 8m/d\).

Suppose (1) fails along a sequence, with real absolute log mass at least a fixed positive fraction of \(\mu\).
The earlier conditional bound on the \(L'\) part and \(\mathbb E|L_1|\le8m/d\) give
\[
                \mu=O(mq),\qquad q=D/d .
\]
Since \(M(A)>A^2\), the value is unmatched to the largest term by the [interval at a match](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval).
The [unmatched estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#quantitative) gives \(\mu\gg d/(m(\log(3d))^{212})\).
It follows that \(q\ge d^{1-o(1)}\), and the absolute logarithmic height of \(C\) satisfies
\[
                   h(C)\le h(A)+jeh(r)\le \mu/D+2m/d=O(m/d).           \tag{4}
\]

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Angular distribution over the smaller block field

Let \(T\) denote the field of the oriented half of \(r\) in the oriented case, of degree \(2u\), or the field of the pair block in the independent case, of degree \(u\).
It lies in \(\mathbb Q(r)\), and \([\mathbb Q(r):T]=b\) or \(2b\), respectively, by transitivity and the [block and half stabilizers](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields).
The class of \(C\) belongs to the sum of the other active spaces of \(A\) and the lower spaces of the chain through \(z\), by its definition and cancellation of \(W\).
All are attached to unmatched terms of degree at most \(d\).
Hence \(C\) belongs to the solvably and radically enlarged base in the [relative alternating theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement): a positive power of \(C\) is, up to torsion, a monomial in roots from those terms.
This base also contains \(T\) since \([T:\mathbb Q]\le2u<b\) for large \(d\).
Over this normal base the available alternating label permutations give all \(b\) positions in the chosen block.
In the independent case their normal lifts supply supported double flips by the commutator calculation above, giving both roots at each label.
Thus \([T(C,r):T(C)]=[\mathbb Q(r):T]\), the opposite inequality being automatic.
Since \(\mathbb Q(A)=\mathbb Q(r,C)\), it follows that \([T(C):T]=q\).
At each fixed embedding of \(\mathbb Q(r)\) the multiset of \(C\) values over it is exactly the full set of \(q\) conjugates above that embedding of \(T\).
Indeed the minimum of \(C\) over \(T\) retains its degree over \(\mathbb Q(r)\).

In each of these \(T\)-fibers the fraction of real \(C\) values tends uniformly to zero.
Here are the energy details, allowing the varying degrees and the possible proper inclusion \(\mathbb Q(C)\subseteq T(C)\).
Take the monic minimum of \(C\) over \(T\), of degree \(q\), with coefficients algebraic integers, and put \(t_0=[T:\mathbb Q]\le2u\).
Its conjugate polynomials have simple roots, and the product of their absolute discriminants is the absolute value of a nonzero rational integer.
Their total log measure with multiplicity is \(t_0 q h(C)\).
For the roots \(y\) in a fiber reflect them to \(\beta_y=y/\max(1,|y|)^2\), and use smoothing radius \(\rho=\exp(-1/q)\).
The [relative reflected energy calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#energy), summing before dropping the discriminants, bounds the sum of all fiber energies by
\[
                  O\!\left(q\left(t_0 qh(C)+t_0\log(3q)\right)\right).
\]
Consequently for each fixed positive integer \(h\), the absolute normalized Fourier coefficient \(q^{-1}|\sum e^{ih\arg y}|\) is
\[
       O_h\!\left(\sqrt{t_0h(C)+t_0\log(3q)/q}+t_0h(C)\right)=o(1)
\]
in every fiber.
Indeed use the single \(h\)-term for the reflected powers, and \(|e^{ih\arg y}-\beta_y^h|\le h|\log|y||\), whose sum over all fibers is bounded by \(2h t_0 q h(C)\) since \(C\) is a unit.
The limit follows from (4), \(t_0\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\), \(q\ge d^{1-o(1)}\) and \(\log q/q\) decreasing for large \(q\).
Applying a fixed Fejér kernel at both \(0\) and \(\pi\) now bounds the fraction of real roots by \(2/H+o_H(1)\) for kernels of order \(H\): the kernel is nonnegative, has constant term one and value \(H\) at its center.
Let \(H\) tend to infinity after taking the sequence limit.

Every real embedding of \(A\) restricts to a real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(r)\) and gives a real \(C\).
The preceding uniform fiber estimate shows that the probability of \(\mathcal R\) conditional on any conjugate of \(r\) is \(o(1)\).
The value \(L_1\) is fixed in each such fiber, since its projected coordinate is determined by that conjugate.
Thus \(\mathbb E(|L_1|{\bf1}_{\mathcal R})=o(\mathbb E|L_1|)=o(\mathbb E|L|)\), contrary to the supposed failure of (1).
This proves the uniform assertion.
Finally the [large second-iterate estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) at an intermediate exponent between \(\eta\) and \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) absorbs the factor \(1-o(1)\) on each strict side.
The interval versus exponential alternative and the bounded-second-iterate equivalence in the statement follow.
