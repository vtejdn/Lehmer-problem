# Coefficient rates with a forbidden cyclotomic factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## An exact rate for Lehmer's polynomial

Let
\[
 L(X)=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1 .
\]
For each positive integer \(a\), let \(H_a\) be the least coefficient one-norm of \(L^aC\), where \(C\) is any finite product of monic cyclotomic polynomials other than \(\Phi_4=X^2+1\), including the empty product.
Then
\[
                         \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{\log H_a}{a}=\log 3.
\]
The same rate holds if arbitrary nonzero integer cofactors \(C\) coprime to \(\Phi_4\) are allowed.
The upper bound already holds with total degree at most \(100\lfloor a/6\rfloor+10(a\bmod6)\).
The same conclusion holds if any additional orders outside \(\{9,10,15,21\}\) are forbidden.
In particular the rate strictly exceeds \(m(L)\), the total logarithmic Mahler measure of this fixed seed.
Indeed \(m(L)<\tfrac12\log\int_{\mathbb T}|L|^2=\log3\), with normalized arc measure, by strict Jensen since the boundary modulus is nonconstant and nine coefficients have modulus one.

<a id="derivative"></a>
## The cost of a prescribed cyclotomic multiplicity

Fix a nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) with no root of unity, and let \(F=P^a\prod_n\Phi_n^{c_n}\), with nonnegative integer multiplicities of finite support.
Write \(D=\deg F\), let \(\varphi\) denote Euler's totient and set
\[
 A_m(U)=\frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_m,U)|}{\varphi(m)}
 \quad(\Phi_m\nmid U),\qquad
 \delta_m=\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}\Phi_m|}{\varphi(m)} .
\]
At each positive integer \(m\), with \(c=c_m\), one has
\[
 a A_m(P)+c\delta_m+\sum_{n\ne m}c_n A_m(\Phi_n)
 \ \le\ \log\|F\|_1+\log\binom Dc .                                      \tag{1}
\]
The same inequality without the sum holds when the cofactor of \(P^a\) is arbitrary integral and \(c\) is its exact \(\Phi_m\)-multiplicity.
In fact at a primitive \(m\)-th root \(\zeta\) the divided derivative \(F^{(c)}(\zeta)/c!\) equals
\[
          P(\zeta)^a\Phi_m'(\zeta)^c
              \left(F/(P^a\Phi_m^c)\right)(\zeta).
\]
Its modulus is at most \(\binom Dc\|F\|_1\) by termwise differentiation.
Multiplying over all primitive \(\zeta\) gives (1).
The final cofactor has nonzero integral resultant with \(\Phi_m\), and all the displayed discriminant and cofactor costs are nonnegative.
At \(c=0\) this recovers the [cyclotomic resultant bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v228.md#resultant) at unrestricted degree.
In particular, whenever \(c\log(\mathrm e D/c)=o(a)\), interpreting the cost as zero at \(c=0\), one has \(\liminf \log\|F\|_1/a\ge A_m(P)\) for fixed \(P,m\).
Conversely a sequence with \(\log\|F\|_1/a\le A_m(P)-\epsilon\) must pay \(\log\binom Dc\ge a\epsilon\).

<a id="deletion"></a>
## Removing all the capped factors

Fix any set \(S\) of forbidden orders, bounds \(b_m\ge0\) for \(m\in S\), and a total degree budget \(D\ge a\deg P\).
Let \(H_0\) and \(H_b\) be the minimum one-norms at this budget with \(c_m=0\) and with \(c_m\le b_m\), respectively, for all \(m\in S\).
Either the cyclotomic-only cofactor class or the arbitrary integral class can be used, with the same class in both minima.
The minima exist since \(P^a\) qualifies and the possible norms are positive integers.
For an optimizer of the capped problem put \(E=\prod_{m\in S}\Phi_m^{c_m}\), \(g=\deg E\).
Then
\[
             0\le\log H_0-\log H_b\le\log\binom Dg .                       \tag{2}
\]
Indeed divide \(F\) by the monic \(E\) with all \(g\) roots of modulus one.
The quotient has degree at most \(D-g\).
In the Taylor expansion at zero of \(1/E\), each linear inverse factor has coefficients of modulus one.
The sum of coefficient moduli up to degree \(D-g\) is at most the number of nonnegative \(g\)-tuples of sum at most \(D-g\), which is \(\binom Dg\).
Truncating the convolution with \(F\) proves \(\|F/E\|_1\le\binom Dg\|F\|_1\), including \(g=0\).
This is the unit-circle case of the [common divisor estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v2.md#division).
The quotient satisfies all avoidance conditions and the same degree budget.
For example, putting \(G=\min(D-a\deg P,\sum_{m\in S}\varphi(m)b_m)\), the two normalized log minima differ by \(o(1)\) whenever \(G\log(\mathrm eD/G)=o(a)\).
Here the binomial estimate and monotonicity of \(x\log(\mathrm eD/x)\) on \([0,D]\) permit a bound using the caps alone.
This transfers the avoidance rate to such caps on the same degree schedules.

<a id="lehmer"></a>
## A cyclotomic cofactor attaining the rate at order four

For the fixed \(L\), \(|L(i)|=3\), and its value at \(-i\) has the same modulus.
Equation (1) with \(c_4=0\) gives \(\log\|L^aC\|_1\ge a\log3\) in either cofactor class.
For the matching upper bound take
\[
             R=L^6\,\Phi_9^2\Phi_{10}^2\Phi_{15}\Phi_{21}.
\]
It has degree \(100\).
With \(x=z+z^{-1}\) write \(z^{-50}R(z)=W(x)\), where
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S(x)&=x^5+x^4-5x^3-5x^2+4x+3,\\
 C_9(x)&=x^3-3x+1,\qquad C_{10}(x)=x^2-x-1,\\
 C_{15}(x)&=x^4-x^3-4x^2+4x+1,\\
 C_{21}(x)&=x^6-x^5-6x^4+6x^3+8x^2-8x+1,\\
 W(x)&=S(x)^6 C_9(x)^2 C_{10}(x)^2 C_{15}(x)C_{21}(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
These identities follow by expansion with \(z+z^{-1}=x\).
In particular \(W(0)=729,\ W'(0)=0\).
We verify \(|W|\le729\) on \([-2,2]\) by positive polynomial coefficients on sixteen rational subintervals.
For \(f=729+W\) or \(f=(729-W)/x^2\), put \(d=\deg f\) and for \(j=-8,\ldots,7\) set
\[
 T_j(t)=[4(1+t)]^d f\!\left(\frac{j+(j+1)t}{4(1+t)}\right).
\]
The following integers, in increasing order of \(j\), are the floors of the smallest normalized coefficients \([t^i]T_j/(4^d\binom di)\), \(0\le i\le d\).

| \(f\) | \(d\) | Interval minima, rounded down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(729+W\) | 50 | 727, 729, 680, 346, 414, 727, 692, 695, 528, 425, 418, 728, 730, 507, 713, 4 |
| \((729-W)/x^2\) | 48 | 176, 237, 323, 497, 1022, 1298, 2916, 12206, 14318, 4129, 1292, 728, 176, 183, 61, 13 |

The [integer verifier](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0186/readme.md#lehmer-phi4) obtains these values directly from the factors of \(R\).
For \(f=\sum_{l=0}^d f_l x^l\), start \(U=f_d\), \(V=1\).
For \(l=d-1,\ldots,0\) replace \(V\) by \(4(1+t)V\), then \(U\) by \((j+(j+1)t)U+f_lV\).
The final \(U=T_j\) gives the listed minima by integer division.
Every coefficient is strictly positive, so both polynomials are positive on \([j/4,(j+1)/4]\) by \(t\in[0,\infty)\) and the endpoint limit.
It follows that \(\max_{\mathbb T}|R|=729=3^6\), with equality at \(z=i\).

For \(a=6j+r,\ 0\le r\le5\), use \(R^jL^r\).
Its cofactor avoids \(\Phi_4\) and its degree is \(100j+10r\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval its coefficient one-norm is at most
\(\sqrt{100j+10r+1}\,729^j\|L\|_{\mathbb T}^r\).
This proves the stated limit for all positive multiplicities.
The same limit holds under any degree budget admitting these witnesses.
Under such a budget, (2) also gives this limit for capped orders replacing avoidance at order four whenever the stated deletion cost is \(o(a)\).
The equality \(\log3\) concerns this seed and conductor.
The infimum over all noncyclotomic seeds is a separate question.
