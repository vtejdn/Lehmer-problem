# A common arclength component in both radial signs of a record's exterior product

<a id="statement"></a>
## The angular limits in both signed block models

Use the [record exterior product and angular probability measures](Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#statement), with \(d=2ub\to\infty\) at a fixed source measure ceiling and at least two exceptions.
For every continuous function \(f\) on the circle,
\[
                        \int f\,d\nu_+ -\int f\,d\nu_-\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Every subsequential limit of either probability measure has a bounded density and dominates one half of normalized arclength.
In the independent signed model both measures converge to normalized arclength, even with only two nonneutral inverse pairs.
In the oriented model this convergence also holds when the number of exceptions tends to infinity or when the exceptions carry a fraction tending to one of the total absolute root log mass of the source.
Thus on escaping strict records every fixed open arc of positive length carries a positive fraction of the growing log mass on each strict side.

When the exception count tends to infinity the uniform angle is asymptotically independent of the normalized radial log \(Z=\log|gA|/\mathbb E|\log|gA||\) for a uniform conjugation \(g\), in the sense that every subsequential joint weak limit is the product of its radial marginal and arclength.
These conclusions apply also to compressed reciprocal inputs of bounded measure and growing degree in the independent signed model, or in the oriented model with torsion half products; these are the precise pure representations used in the argument.
For the [explicit five-term prime family](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#statement), the pair consisting of \(\sqrt p\log|gA_p|\) and the argument of \(gA_p\) consequently tends to a centered Gaussian of variance \(I_2/3\) independent of a uniform angle, with \(I_2\) as in that family.

<a id="bounded"></a>
## The log mass on a single exceptional destination

It suffices first to take a subsequence with exception count \(2\le e\le E\) bounded.
In the independent model take zero baselines, and in the oriented model subtract the unique most frequent exterior coefficient \(1,0,-1\) on each half.
The oriented half products are torsion, so this does not change the real log.
Write \(L=\log|gA|=\sum_{i\in S} t_i\ell_i\) with \(|S|=e\), \(1\le |t_i|\le2\), \(\ell_i=\log|ga_i|\); the \(a_i\) are the corresponding actual half representatives.
The possibly nontrivial block baseline factors in the phase are held fixed when permuting within a half.
Let \(S\) also mark these inverse-pair labels as destinations in the initial distinguished embedding.
Write \(E_i\) for the event that the image label of \(i\) lies in \(S\), \(h_1=\mathbb E|\ell_i|\), and
\[
      f_S=\frac{\sum_{\text{labels in }S} |\log|a_i||}
                   {\sum_{\text{all labels}} |\log|a_i||}.
\]
Use either inverse representative when computing a label's absolute log.
In each oriented half the log sum is zero.
If the baseline is nonzero, all entries outside \(S\) on that half have the same strict sign of the log; if it is zero they are neutral.
Therefore \(f_S\ge1/2\).
The independent model gives \(f_S=1\).
Signed transitivity on the actual roots gives
\[
               \mathbb E (t_i\ell_i)_\pm {\bf1}_{E_i}
                            = f_S |t_i|h_1/2 ,
\]
with \(x_+=\max(x,0)\) and \(x_-=\max(-x,0)\).

For \(i\ne j\), conditional on the signed image of \(j\), the probability of \(E_i\) is \(O(e/(b-1))\), by the supported label permutations, first fixing the transport of its block if needed.
In particular \(\mathbb E(|\ell_j|{\bf1}_{E_i})=O(eh_1/b)\).
For any nonnegative continuous test function \(\phi\) bounded by one, sum on the disjoint events where exactly one mark lands in \(S\).
Replacing \(L_\pm\) there by \((t_i\ell_i)_\pm\), and then inserting the missing overlaps to recover all of \(E_i\), costs altogether \(O_E(h_1/b)\).
This uses the 1-Lipschitz property and the preceding cross estimate, also with \(i,j\) interchanged.
Consequently
\[
   \mathbb E\big[L_\pm\phi(\arg gA)\big]\ \ge\
      \sum_{i\in S}\mathbb E\big[(t_i\ell_i)_\pm{\bf1}_{E_i}
                                               \phi(\arg gA)\big]-O_E(h_1/b).
\]

Holding the signed image of a given \(i\) fixed, move a different marked position among the baseline positions while keeping the others fixed.
The [conditional phase estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#fourier) gives conditional mean of \(\phi(\arg gA)\) equal to its arclength mean plus \(o(1)\), uniformly over the data then fixed.
Indeed only \(O(E)\) positions have been removed from a whole equidistributed half in the oriented case; in the independent case a spare zero label permits both signs, giving a full block up to \(O(E)\) deleted positions.
The varying coefficient difference has absolute value one or two, so all its fixed nonzero Fourier orders tend to zero uniformly; trigonometric approximation handles \(\phi\).
This estimate is valid on \(E_i\) as well, since the label of \(i\) is held fixed.
Now \(\mathbb E L_\pm=\mathbb E|L|/2\le h_1\sum|t_i|/2\), and the [first moment lower bound](Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#weights) absorbs the errors.
This gives the domination by \(f_S\) times arclength up to a vanishing error in each such test.
Taking \(\phi\) and \(1-\phi\) gives convergence to arclength if \(f_S\to1\).

The same conditional averaging, with no indicator \(E_i\), gives \(\mathbb E[L e^{ih\arg gA}]=o(\mathbb E|L|)\) at each fixed \(h\ne0\) by summing \(t_i\ell_i\) separately.
At \(h=0\) the identity is exact by the unit norm.
Since \(L=L_+-L_-\), every pair of simultaneous limits is identical.
For unbounded \(e\) one can pass further to \(e\to\infty\) or to bounded \(e\); the [growing-exception Haar limit](Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#fourier) and the bounded-density result there complete the first assertions.

<a id="joint"></a>
## Joint angular and radial limits

When \(e\to\infty\), the two randomizations used in the [weighted Fourier proof](Yin_C_G0901_v149.md#fourier) work also for any fixed bounded Lipschitz function \(F(Z)\).
In the range \(e\le\sqrt b\), replacing \(Z\) by the normalized log with one moving coordinate removed costs \(O(e^{-1/2})\) in expected absolute difference, and hence at most the Lipschitz constant times this in the test.
All fixed nonzero phase harmonics vanish after the conditional average.
In the range \(e>\sqrt b\), use \(K=\lceil C_h\log(3d)\rceil\) compensated swaps.
Replacing \(Z\) by its mean on the swap orbit costs \(O(K/\sqrt e)=o(1)\); the mean modulus of the conditional phase harmonic is at most \(\gamma_h^K\) with \(\gamma_h<1\).
The remaining radial test is bounded.
Thus \(\mathbb E[F(Z)e^{ih\arg gA}]\to0\) for every fixed \(h\ne0\).
The variables \(Z\) are tight since \(\mathbb E|Z|=1\).
Testing a subsequential joint limit against bounded Lipschitz functions in the radial variable and trigonometric polynomials in the angular variable proves the product assertion.
The five-term family has the [pure independent single block](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#orbit), bounded source measure, and \(e=J_p\to\infty\).
Its [radial central limit and first-moment asymptotic](Yin_C_G0901_v152.md#limit) give the stated result after restoring the \(\sqrt p\) normalization.
