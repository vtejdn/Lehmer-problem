# Reduction, discriminants and complementary resultants of the two-pole sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Local multiplicities and the degree of a rational factor

Let \(k,l>0\) be coprime and let \(P(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\), where \(A=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1)\), \(A^*=U^4 A(1/U)\) and \(F=A V^2+U^2 V+A^*\).
For every prime \(p\), with bars denoting reduction modulo \(p\),
\[
                        \deg\gcd(\bar P,\bar P')\le16.                   \tag{1}
\]
In particular at most 32 roots counted with multiplicity lie in nonsingleton residue classes.
On every squarefree section the rational splitting group is generated by finite inertia subgroups each supported on at most 32 roots.

Suppose \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Every monic rational irreducible factor \(S\) is then reciprocal, uniformly for large indices.
Its degree \(d\) tends to infinity, and its rational Galois action on the \(n=d/2\) trace values \(z+z^{-1}\) contains the natural \(A_n\).
In particular the individual trace field \(\mathbb Q(z+z^{-1})\) has no intermediate field strictly between it and \(\mathbb Q\).

Put \(W=2l+4k\), \(C=\log(\beta_3\beta_4)\) with \(\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\), and
\[
     L=C+\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}\log(4|A(e^{it})|^2-1)\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
\]
Uniformly over the individual factors,
\[
   \frac{|\operatorname{disc}(S)\operatorname{Res}(S,P/S)|^{1/d}}l
            \longrightarrow e^L.                                       \tag{2}
\]
For the full section, writing \(M(P)\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure,
\[
 \frac{|\operatorname{disc} P|^{1/W}}{W M(P)^2}\longrightarrow
   \exp\left\{\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}
          \log\left(1-\frac1{4|A(e^{it})|^2}\right)\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right\}
       \in [\sqrt{575/576},1).                                           \tag{3}
\]
For a proper factor with \(d=o(l)\), the finite additive group \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S,P/S)\) is generated by at most 16 elements and its exponent is at least
\[
                              \left((e^L-o(1))\,l/d\right)^{d/16}.        \tag{4}
\]
Thus a small-degree factor requires a large resultant with its complement even though each prime sees only boundedly many common residue factors.

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Coprimality with the logarithmic derivative in every characteristic

On the torus put \(t=U-U^{-1}\).
Then \(F/U^2=aV^2+V+b\), with \(a=(t-3)(t-4)\), \(b=(t+3)(t+4)\).
Write \(D=kU\partial_U+lV\partial_V\).
We check \(F\) and \(DF\) have no common geometric component over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).

In odd characteristic other than five, the quadratic discriminant after division by \(U^2\) is
\[
           1-4(t^2-9)(t^2-16)=-4t^4+100t^2-575 .
\]
As a quadratic in \(t^2\) it has distinct roots since its discriminant is \(800\).
At least one root is different from both zero and \(-4\), since the values at zero and \(-4\) are \(-575\) and \(-1039\), which are coprime integers.
Such a root gives simple zeros as a function of \(U\): the squaring map is unramified at nonzero \(t\), and \(U-U^{-1}\) has finite branch values only at \(t^2=-4\).
The quadratic therefore stays irreducible over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p(U)\), and its middle Laurent coefficient is a unit, excluding vertical factors.
If \(F\mid DF\), their bidegree bounds would force \(DF\) to be a scalar multiple of \(F\), including the zero scalar.
Its monomial weights would all agree modulo \(p\).
The bottom row has terms in degrees zero and four with nonzero coefficients, forcing \(4k=0\bmod p\), and the central term then forces \(l=0\bmod p\).
This contradicts coprimality.

In characteristic two the Laurent equation factors, up to the unit \(U^2\), as
\[
                     (t V+t+1)((t+1)V+t).
\]
These are distinct primitive graphs over \(U\).
Their rational \(V\)-coordinates are reciprocals and have a simple zero or pole over \(t=1\), since this nonzero value is unramified for \(U-U^{-1}\) in characteristic two.
In characteristic five the factorization is
\[
         ((t+2)V-(t-1)/2)((t+1)V+(t-2)/2).
\]
Again the factors are distinct primitive graphs.
The first has a simple pole at \(t=-2\) and the second a simple zero at \(t=2\), both away from the branch values \(t^2=-4\).
On a graph \(V=R(U)\) tangency everywhere would require \(kU R'/R=l\) in its function field.
If \(k\ne0\bmod p\), the indicated simple zero or pole at a nonzero \(U\) makes the left side have a pole.
If \(k=0\bmod p\), then \(l\ne0\bmod p\).
Since the factors have multiplicity one, neither component divides \(DF\).
This finishes the check at all primes.

<a id="ramification"></a>
## Intersection lengths and inertia

In \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) the divisor of the bihomogenized \(F\) has bidegree \((4,2)\) and no boundary component, by its four nonzero corner coefficients.
Bihomogenizing \(DF\) to the same bidegree therefore gives proper intersection of total length \(4\cdot2+2\cdot4=16\), even if the derivative acquires boundary factors.
The section is a smooth subtorus with parameter recovered from its two coordinates by Bézout in \(k,l\).
Distinct residue roots give distinct torus points.
At a nonzero root \(x\), quotient the local intersection algebra of \(F,DF\) further by the equation of this subtorus.
Its length becomes the minimum of the orders at \(x\) of \(\bar P\) and \(X\bar P'\), by the [local tangency computation](Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#tangencies).
This quotient length is a lower bound for the original intersection length.
The section has unit constant coefficient in every characteristic, so summing gives (1).
For multiplicity \(e>1\) each contribution is at least \(e-1\), including when \(p\mid e\), and \(e\le2(e-1)\).
In a splitting field of a squarefree section the roots all reduce to nonzero values at a chosen prime, with exactly the multiplicities of \(\bar P\).
Every inertia element fixes all singleton residue classes pointwise, so it can move at most 32 roots.
The [finite inertia generation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields) applies since the fixed field of the normal subgroup generated by these groups is unramified at every finite prime and Minkowski's discriminant bound forces it to be rational.

<a id="factors"></a>
## Trace actions on the steep sections

Squarefreeness holds for large \(s\) by the [circle and polar root counts](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
Every factor and its reciprocal have \((1+o(1))d/s\) exterior roots by the [uniform pole counts](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth), with \(d\to\infty\).
Thus \(S\) has at least one root on the unit circle for all sufficiently large indices.
Its complex conjugate is its inverse, so the irreducibility of \(S\) gives reciprocity.
The [plane-curve torsion-quotient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) gives singleton classes because the support differences generate the full lattice, as in the [exterior incidence proof](Yin_C_G0345_v796.md#pairs).
The [section measure](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#measure) bounds \(m(S)\) uniformly.
The [alternating block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) therefore gives a partition of the inverse pairs into blocks of size \(b\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), with independent alternating actions within each block.
The inertia bound implies that no inertia element can permute two such blocks once \(b>32\).
By generation the whole group fixes them setwise, and transitivity on inverse pairs leaves a single block.
This proves the natural alternating assertion and hence primitivity on the trace embeddings.

<a id="derivative"></a>
## The average derivative at a single irreducible factor

For a section root \(z\) let \(U=z^k,V=z^l\).
Logarithmic differentiation gives \(zP'(z)=kUF_U+lVF_V\).
At a circle root \(|VF_V|=\sqrt{4|A(U)|^2-1}\ge\sqrt{575}\).
Indeed the circle equation makes the real part of \(A(U)V/U^2\) equal to \(-1/2\), and \(VF_V=U^2V(2A(U)V/U^2+1)\) at the root.
The first term is \(O(k)\) on the unit torus.
Thus
\[
     \log|zP'(z)|=\log l+\tfrac12\log(4|A(U)|^2-1)+O(1/s).
\]
For an exterior root the [polar estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#lifts) gives \(U=\beta_j+O(e^{-cs})\) and \(|V|\gg e^{cs}\).
Here \(UF_U=U A'(U)V^2+O(|V|+1)\) and \(VF_V=-U^2V-2A^*(U)\) at a zero.
The simple zero \(\beta_j\) of \(A\) gives
\[
        \log|zP'(z)|=\log k+2l\log|z|+O(1)
\]
uniformly at all exterior roots.
For an interior root invert \(z\): reciprocity gives \(zP'(z)=-z^W z^{-1}P'(z^{-1})\).
The corresponding formula is \(\log|zP'(z)|=\log k+O(1)\), since \(W-2l=4k\) and \(|z|^{-k}\) is bounded.

The fraction of off-circle roots in \(S\) is \(O(1/s)\), so replacing \(\log k\) by \(\log l\) there costs \(O((\log s)/s)\) in the average.
The exterior terms \(2l\log|z|\) sum to \(2l\,m(S)\), and \(2l\,m(S)/d\to C\) by the [individual height law](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#incidence).
Also \(h(z^k)\to0\); Bilu's [equidistribution theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) applies along any chosen sequence of these nontorsion units.
The images of the conjugates under powering count each conjugate of \(z^k\) with equal multiplicity.
Extend the continuous circle function \(\tfrac12\log(4|A|^2-1)\) boundedly and continuously to the complex sphere.
The \(O(1/s)\) off-circle fraction does not affect its limiting average.
This proves that the mean of \(\log|zP'(z)|-\log l\) over the conjugates tends to \(L\), uniformly by choice of sequence.
The product of the moduli of these units \(z\) is one, and the product of \(|P'(z)|\) is \(|\operatorname{disc}(S)\operatorname{Res}(S,P/S)|\).
This gives (2).
Averaging (2) in logarithmic form with weights \(d/W\) yields the same derivative limit for all roots together.
Now \(W/l\to2\), \(m(P)\to C\), and the circle mean of \(\log|A|\) equals \(C\).
These identities give (3), including the interval since \(|A|\ge12\) everywhere on the circle.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## An integer quotient at a proper factor

For \(T=P/S\), the finite additive group \(G=\mathbb Z[X]/(S,T)\) is the cokernel of multiplication by \(T\) on the free degree-\(d\) order \(\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\).
Its order is \(|\operatorname{Res}(S,T)|\) by the determinant formula.
At every prime \(p\), the dimension of \(G/pG\) is \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar T)\), at most 16 by (1) since each common divisor divides \(\bar P'\).
The finite abelian group theorem gives at most 16 generators and exponent at least \(|G|^{1/16}\).
Hadamard's bound on the Vandermonde determinant gives \(|\operatorname{disc}(S)|\le d^d\exp(2(d-1)m(S))\).
Indeed the row at \(z\) in the power basis has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt d\max(1,|z|)^{d-1}\).
When \(d=o(l)\), the individual height law gives \(m(S)\to0\).
Inserting this bound into (2) proves (4).
