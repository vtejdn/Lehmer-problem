# Full cyclic divisibility of both rows at the sum of two odd exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## The common reciprocal polynomial on the sum and difference periods

Let \(p<q\) be odd positive integers, \(s=p+q\), \(r=q-p\), and \(G_j(X)=U(X^j)+XV(X^j)\) for \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\).
Suppose a monic reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with constant term one and no root of unity divides both \(G_p,G_q\).
It may be a product with arbitrary multiplicities.
For \(d\ge1\) write \(A_d=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^d-1)\) and \(x=X\bmod(X^d-1)\).
In the indicated rings one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (U(x^p),V(x^p))&\ \subseteq\ (F(x)) &&\text{in } A_s,\\
 (U(x^p),V(x^p))&\ \subseteq\ (F(x),F'(x)) &&\text{in } A_r .
\end{aligned}                                                         \tag{1}
\]
These are inclusions of integral ideals on the entire periods, including the two residues \(X=\pm1\).
They also hold after projection to any divisor of the respective period, and in every individual cyclotomic factor ring of that period.

If \(p,q\) are coprime, substitution \(Y\mapsto x^p\) identifies the original \(Y\)-coordinate ring with \(A_s\), and likewise with \(A_r\).
When the group is finite, put \(J_d(U,V)=|\mathbb Z[Y]/(Y^d-1,U,V)|\).
In particular finiteness holds if the two rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).
Under the same coprimality assumption on \(p,q\), suppose both row quotients are finite.
If \(F=P^k\) for a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) and a positive integer \(k\), put \(C_d(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(X^d-1,P)|\).
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_s(P)^k &\mid J_s(U,V),\\
 C_r(P)^{k-1}\,|\mathbb Z[X]/(X^r-1,P,kP')| &\mid J_r(U,V).
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{2}
\]
For general \(F\) the entire \(|\operatorname{Res}(X^s-1,F)|\) divides \(J_s(U,V)\), even when different factors of this integer share prime divisors.

<a id="aligned"></a>
## Dividing the difference of the two graphs before taking a period

For \(W\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) let \(W[u,v]=(W(u)-W(v))/(u-v)\), using its integral polynomial expression in \(u,v\), so \(W[u,u]=W'(u)\).
In \(\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) set
\[
 D_+(X)=U[X^p,X^q]+X V[X^p,X^q],\qquad
 D_-(X)=U[X^p,X^{-q}]+X V[X^p,X^{-q}].
\]
One has \(G_p-G_q=(X^p-X^q)D_+\).
Since \(F\) has no root in common with \(X^p-X^q\), divisibility of the graphs gives \(F\mid D_+\) over \(\mathbb Q[X]\), and monic division gives the same over \(\mathbb Z[X]\).
This retains all multiplicities of \(F\).
Modulo \(X^r-1\) it yields \(U'(x^p)+xV'(x^p)\in(F(x))\).
Differentiating \(G_p=F B\) gives
\[
 V(X^p)+pX^{p-1}\big(U'(X^p)+X V'(X^p)\big)=F'B+FB'.
\]
It follows that \(V(x^p)\), and then \(U(x^p)=G_p(x)-xV(x^p)\), belong to \((F(x),F'(x))\) in \(A_r\).

<a id="opposite"></a>
## Removing the two fixed points of inversion without a denominator

Reciprocity gives \(F\mid G_q(X^{-1})\) in the integral Laurent ring.
The identity
\[
 G_p(X)-G_q(X^{-1})=(X^p-X^{-q})D_-(X)+(X-X^{-1})V(X^{-q})
\]
can be divided by \(X-X^{-1}\) since \(s\) is even.
Put \(L=X^{1-q}(X^s-1)/(X^2-1)\), which is integral Laurent.
The resulting quotient \(E=L D_-+V(X^{-q})\) is divisible by \(F\): cancellation is valid over the rationals as \(F(\pm1)\ne0\), and clearing Laurent powers and using monic division gives integral divisibility.

The integral polynomial expressions for divided differences give \(D_--D_+\in(X^2-1)\) in the Laurent ring, since \(X^q=X^{-q}\) modulo \(X^2-1\).
Consequently \(L(D_--D_+)\in(X^s-1)\).
Both \(E\) and \(L D_+\) belong to \((F)\).
In \(A_s\) this proves \(V(x^p)=V(x^{-q})=E-LD_+\in(F(x))\), and \(G_p\) gives the same assertion for \(U\).
No cancellation by \(x-x^{-1}\) in \(A_s\) was used, so the conclusion also holds at primes dividing \(2sF(1)F(-1)\).
For example both \(U(1),V(1)\) are divisible by \(F(1)\), and \(U(-1),V(-1)\) by \(F(-1)\).

<a id="groups"></a>
## Exact quotient orders and a finite control

If \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), then \(\gcd(p,s)=\gcd(p,r)=1\).
Taking an inverse of \(p\) modulo the period gives the inverse substitution on each cyclic ring, including at period one.
By (1) the \(Y\)-row quotient at \(s\) maps onto \(A_s/(F)\).
Multiplication by \(F\) on the free lattice \(A_s\) has determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(X^s-1,F)\), since over \(\mathbb C\) its eigenvalues are \(F(\zeta)\) at all \(s\)-th roots of unity.
This determinant is nonzero, proving the sum-period divisibility.
The same reasoning works at every \(d\mid s\) or in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_d)\), with \(\Phi_d\) the \(d\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.
The full assertion for \(F\) follows directly from one ring surjection on the entire period.

At the difference period the target for \(P^k\) is \(A_r/P^{k-1}(P,kP')A_r\).
Multiplication by \(P^{k-1}\) is injective in \(A_r\), as \(P\) is nonzero at every root of unity.
Inside \(P^{k-1}A_r\) the ideal has index \(|A_r/(P,kP')|\), while \(P^{k-1}A_r\) has index \(C_r(P)^{k-1}\) in \(A_r\).
This gives the second divisibility of (2).
For the original \(J_d\), tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\) gives zero when the rows are rationally coprime, so the quotient of the rank-\(d\) lattice is finite even when all row coefficients have a common integer divisor.
Its order can be computed as the gcd of the rank-\(d\) minors of the juxtaposed multiplication matrices of \(U,V\) on \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(Y^d-1)\), by Smith normal form.

For instance take \(P=X^2-3X+1\), \(p=5,\ q=7,\ k=2\).
If \(a>1\) is the larger root, then \(a^3+a^{-3}=18\), \(a^6+a^{-6}=322\) and \(a^{12}+a^{-12}=103682\) by the power recurrence.
Thus \(103680^2\mid J_{12}(U,V)\) for any rationally coprime pair of rows satisfying these double contacts.
With no restriction on the coefficient norm, the [common interpolation construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) supplies such pairs since both powers preserve the quadratic degree.
Here \(5\mid P(-1)\) and \(5\mid103680\), so the exact period retains a prime which the [auxiliary-modulus argument](Yin_C_G0345_v492.md#opposite) excluded.

<a id="size"></a>
## Growth visible at the two exponent periods

For a fixed \(P\) and \(h=\log M(P)>0\), the [norm estimate at unit-circle conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) gives \(\log C_d(P)=dh+O_P(\log(2d))\).
At a repeated contact in the [common-row test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common), the same seed at both distinct odd primes therefore forces
\[
     \log J_{p+q}(U,V)\ge 2(p+q)h-O_P(\log(2(p+q))),\qquad
     \log J_{q-p}(U,V)\ge(q-p)h-O_P(\log(2(q-p))).
\]
For a fixed all-power reciprocal subcritical seed the [bounded-partner construction](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) gives actual rows at cost arbitrarily close to the seed measure despite the sum-period index.
The dependence on \(P\) in the asymptotic estimate does not enter the integral periodic divisibilities.
