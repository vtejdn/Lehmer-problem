# The first denominator coefficient for a six-term trace lift with independent frequencies

<a id="statement"></a>
## An upper bound on the first nonconstant denominator index

Consider the [positive-constant inverse problem](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) on branch \(j=2\), with constant \(q\), trace numerator of degree \(h\), and reciprocal monic integer denominator \(B^\sharp(z)=\sum_{n=0}^N b_nz^n\) of degree \(N=2h+2\).
Suppose the anti-reciprocal trace lift has six terms, with lower half \(A(z)=2+a z^d+b z^e\), \(0<d<e<N/2\).
There are absolute \(C,Q,R\) such that whenever \(q\ge Q\) and \(e/\gcd(d,e)\ge R\), some \(1\le n\le Ce\) has \(b_n\ne0\).
Thus along any escaping sequence with unbounded reduced frequency denominator tending to infinity the first positive denominator index is \(O(e)\), including when the offsets approach each other or their ratio approaches a rational number.
The proof gives no numerical value for \(C\).

<a id="middle"></a>
## A denominator with a long initial gap

If the assertion fails, take inputs with \(q_i,e_i/\gcd(d_i,e_i)\to\infty\) and first positive nonzero denominator index \(t_i\) satisfying \(t_i/e_i\to\infty\).
The [six-term restrictions](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#six) give \(e_i=o(N_i)\) and, after extraction, fixed \(a,b\in\{-1,1\}\).
Suppress the subscripts when convenient and write \(F_i(z)=1+(az^d+bz^e)/2\).
For the [signed measure](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_i\) with Herglotz series \(J_i=2/K_i\), the negative mass \(1/[2(q_i-1)]\) tends to zero and the total mass is one.
The coefficients of \(B^\sharp\) below \(N-e\) are exactly those of \(F_iJ_i\), since the remaining coefficients of the numerator start at \(N-e\).
Thus throughout \(e<n<N-e\), \(b_n\) is the \(n\)-th moment of the measure \(2F_i(v^{-1})\,d\omega_i(v)\), whose variation is uniformly bounded.

In fact \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
The alternative \(B^\sharp=1+z^N\) is impossible since \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) by the [delay-one identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), so reciprocity gives \(t_i\le N_i/2\).
Suppose \(t_i\ge\eta N_i\) for some fixed \(0<\eta<1/2\) on a subsequence.
Set \(s=\lfloor\eta N_i/8\rfloor\) and convolve the integer interval indicator \(1_{[3s,N_i-3s]}\) with the uniform probability on the integers \([-s,s]\).
This multiplier is one on \([4s,N_i-4s]\), supported on \([2s,N_i-2s]\), and its circle kernel has bounded \(L^1\) norm by Cauchy–Schwarz on the two interval sums.
For large \(i\) its support lies strictly between \(e_i\) and \(N_i-e_i\).
Multiplying the moment coefficients above by the cutoff gives exactly \(B^\sharp-1-z^{N_i}\), by the initial gap and its reflection.
Hence \(\|B^\sharp\|_1\) is bounded, so the [integer Fourier norm argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) bounds its number of terms.
The [fixed-support gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) contradicts \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one on this branch as \(q_i\to\infty\).

<a id="marginal"></a>
## The two frequency coordinates have an explicit limiting law

Push the positive part of \(\omega_i\) forward by \(v\mapsto(x,y,u)=(-a v^d,-b v^e,v^{t_i})\) and take a weak limit \(\Omega\), a probability.
The removed negative mass makes an \(o(1)\) difference uniformly on individual characters.
Its first two coordinates have density \(\Re(1/F)\) against Haar probability on the two-torus, where \(F(x,y)=1-(x+y)/2\).

To prove this, through degree \(t_i-1\) the series \(J_i\) equals \(1/F_i\) since \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
For fixed integers \(l,r\) the index \(m=ld+re\) satisfies \(|m|=O(e)=o(t_i)\).
Except at \((l,r)=(0,0)\), \(m\) is eventually nonzero because the primitive frequency relation has maximum coefficient \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
For \(m>0\) the moment is one half the coefficient of \(z^m\) in \(1/F_i\), up to the signs introduced by the coordinates.
In the geometric expansion the contributing pairs \(p,s\ge0\) satisfy \(pd+se=m\) and have unsigned weight \(\binom{p+s}{s}2^{-p-s}\).
There are boundedly many choices of \(s\), as \(m=O(e)\).
A contribution with \(p\to\infty\) tends to zero, while the only possible bounded pair eventually is \((p,s)=(l,r)\), again by divergence of the primitive relation.
For \(m<0\) apply symmetry to \(-m\).
The signs cancel for the surviving pair.
These are precisely the moments of the asserted density, with half the displayed binomial weight in the positive and negative quadrants away from the origin and zero for mixed strict signs.

Indeed the nonnegative functions \(\Re(1/[1-\lambda(x+y)/2])\), \(0<\lambda<1\), have integral one and the corresponding damped binomial coefficients.
Their pointwise limit away from \((1,1)\) still has integral one.
For \(x\ne1\) integration first in \(y\) gives \(\Re(1/[1-x/2])\) by the geometric series, and this last function has mean one.
Consequently the densities converge in \(L^1\), proving the moment identification, including the absence of singular mass.

<a id="harmonics"></a>
## The two coordinate circles force incompatible second harmonics

Reflect the first two coordinates by complex conjugation, leaving \(u\) unchanged.
The explicit marginal is invariant under this reflection.
Write \(c_k(x,y)\) for the \(k\)-th moment in \(u\) of the conditional probabilities and \(\sigma=2\Re(1/F)\).
For every \(k\ge1\) the function \(H_k=F\sigma c_k\) has integer Fourier coefficients.
Indeed its coefficient at \((l,r)\), using negative exponents for the Fourier transform, is the limit of \((-a)^l(-b)^r b_{kt_i+ld+re}\), by the interior moment identity above.
All three original indices used to form this coefficient are strictly positive and below \(N_i-e_i\), since \(e_i=o(t_i)\) and \(t_i=o(N_i)\).
In particular \(H_1\ne0\), since its constant coefficient is the limit of nonzero integers \(b_{t_i}\).

Away from \((1,1)\), \(|H_k|\le D=|F|\sigma=2\Re F/|F|\le2\).
In particular \(\int D^2<4\), since \(F\) is nonreal on an open set.
By Parseval each \(H_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial with squared coefficient sum less than four, and the pointwise bound extends over the punctured torus by continuity.
Along \(x=y\to1\) the bound tends to zero, so \(H_k(1,1)=0\).
Every nonzero \(H_k\) is therefore a signed monomial times \(1-x^p y^s\) with \((p,s)\ne(0,0)\).

Where \(x^p y^s=-1\) this polynomial has modulus two, which forces \(\Im(x+y)=0\).
The latter locus is the union of the circles \(xy=1\) and \(x/y=-1\).
Each connected component of the former locus is a full translated one-dimensional subtorus and must equal one of these two circles, since two circles with distinct primitive character directions meet at finitely many points.
A direction parallel to \((1,1)\) is impossible since its components would have \(xy\) equal to roots of \(-1\), none equal to one.
In direction \((1,-1)\) all solutions of \((x/y)^p=-1\) must have \(x/y=-1\), forcing \(|p|=1\).
It follows that every nonzero \(H_k\) is of the form \(\epsilon x^A y^B(x-y)\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\).

At each point of the punctured torus, \(F\sigma\ne0\).
The continuous representatives \(c_k=H_k/(F\sigma)\), together with \(c_0=1\) and their conjugates, have all Toeplitz moment matrices positive semidefinite, first almost everywhere by disintegration and then everywhere by continuity.
Thus a value \(|c_k|=1\) forces \(c_{2k}=c_k^2\) there by the equality case for a circle probability, or the three-by-three moment matrix.
On \(x=1,\ y\ne1\) we have \(\sigma=2,\ F\sigma=1-y\), and on \(y=1,\ x\ne1\) we have \(F\sigma=1-x\).
Accordingly a nonzero row as above has \(c_k(1,y)=\epsilon y^B\) and \(c_k(x,1)=-\epsilon x^A\).
Both have modulus one, so \(H_{2k}\ne0\) and its moments on those axes must be \(y^{2B}\) and \(x^{2A}\).
No signed monomial times \(x-y\) gives these two moments, because its two constant signs on the axes are opposite.
This rules out every nonzero row, contradicting \(H_1\ne0\) and proving the statement.
