# Individual factors on every monomial specialization of a Newton polygon

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two freely varying exponents

Fix a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial \(F(X,Y)\) whose support spans an affine plane.
For \(v=(p,q)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\) let \(F_v(Z)=F(Z^p,Z^q)\) and \(N=\max(|p|,|q|)\).
Only nonzero specializations are considered, and monomial factors are disregarded.
For each edge of the convex hull of the support, write the terms on that edge as a monomial times a polynomial \(B(T)\) in its primitive character.
Removing powers of \(T\) leaves \(B(0)\ne0\).
Reversing the edge replaces \(B\) with its reciprocal.

* If all roots of all edge polynomials have modulus one, there is \(c_F>0\) such that **every** positive-measure integral divisor of **every** \(F_v\) has logarithmic measure at least \(c_F\).
  Every monic irreducible noncyclotomic unit factor has degree at least \(c_FN\).
* If those edge roots are all roots of unity, the same degree lower bound holds for **every nonconstant nontorsion irreducible factor**, without monicity or a unit condition.
  Here nontorsion means that a nonzero root has infinite multiplicative order.
  Every such degree is also \(O_F(N)\), so the number of these factors counted with multiplicities is bounded in terms of \(F\).
* For an arbitrary \(F\), if a sequence of irreducible factors of its specializations has positive logarithmic measure tending to zero, then after passage to a subsequence it comes from a fixed reciprocal irreducible component with two-dimensional support.
  Any shrinking sequence of reducible divisors also supplies such a sequence of irreducible factors by additivity.
  Directions converge to a normal of an edge on which the oriented edge polynomial has a root outside the unit disk.
  The absolute exponent of the primitive edge character tends to infinity and is \(o(N)\).
  If the factors are Salem minimal polynomials with positive root \(\tau\) and degree \(a\), there is a fixed real root \(b>1\) of that oriented edge polynomial and positive integers \(l=o(N)\), \(l\to\infty\), such that
  \[
                          |\tau^l-b|\le C_F e^{-c_F a}.                 \tag{1}
  \]
  In particular a fixed two-variable polynomial whose edge polynomials have no positive real roots other than possibly one has a uniform gap for its Salem factors, even when some edge roots lie off the unit circle.

There is no primitivity restriction on \((p,q)\).
The constants concern the fixed \(F\), including the coefficients and exponents of its faces.

<a id="fixed"></a>
## Specializations on one rational line and isolated common zeros

We use the following elementary observation.
If \(\alpha\) is nontorsion and a fixed number field \(K\subset\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) contains \(\alpha^j\) for some integer \(j\ne0\), set \(r=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\) and \(\eta=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)\).
Then \(\eta^j=(\alpha^j)^r\), so \(h(\eta)=r h(\alpha)>0\).
Northcott finiteness gives a positive lower bound for the height of nontorsion elements of \(K\).
The [height normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) therefore gives a uniform lower bound for the measure of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
When \(\alpha^j\) itself belongs to a fixed finite set of nontorsion numbers, taking \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\) also gives \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\gg |j|\), since \(h(\alpha)=h(\alpha^j)/|j|\).

For the multiples \(v=jv_0\) of a fixed primitive vector, \(F_v=F_{v_0}(Z^j)\).
A fixed nonzero \(F_{v_0}\) has finitely many roots, so the observation handles every nontorsion factor on this line with degree \(\gg_{F,v_0}N\).
If \(F_{v_0}\) is zero the entire line is excluded.
In particular this disposes of all directions, finitely many up to multiples, in which two original exponents project to the same integer.
For the measure bound, factors with a nonunit endpoint or a nonunit integer constant already have measure at least \(\log2\).
[Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) excludes nonreciprocal monic nontorsion units from a shrinking sequence.

Factor \(F\) over the rational Laurent ring and pass to a fixed irreducible factor \(G\) carrying a putative shrinking sequence of reciprocal units \(\alpha\).
If \(G\) is not associated to \(G(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\), their common zero set in the two-dimensional torus is finite.
Indeed coprime rational plane polynomials remain without common component over the algebraic closure, since the product of the Galois conjugates of a common component would give a common rational factor after normalization.
The point \((\alpha^p,\alpha^q)\) lies in this finite set by the reciprocal minimal polynomial.
Its coordinate field contains \(\alpha^{\gcd(p,q)}\) and is one of finitely many fields, so the observation excludes this case.
If \(G\) is supported on a line, specialization gives a fixed polynomial of one character evaluated at a power of \(\alpha\), or a constant.
The same observation again excludes a shrinking sequence.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Separating the terms on the highest face

Consider a sequence of remaining exponent vectors with \(N\to\infty\), after fixing a limit \(v/N\to u\) on the boundary of the unit square in the maximum norm.
For the moment use the original \(F\), with distinct projected exponents.
Their span \(D\) satisfies \(D\asymp_F N\), because the width of a fixed two-dimensional polygon is positive and continuous in each unit direction.
The face maximizing the scalar product with \(u\) is a vertex or an edge.
Every exponent outside it is separated in the limiting scalar product by a positive constant from those on it.
After a monomial shift write the specialization as \(Z^s B_v+C_v\), with \(B_v\) the normalized face contribution, \(C_v\ne0\), and \(g=s-\deg C_v\).
Then \(g\gg_{F,u}N\), the face width \(b_v=\deg B_v=o(N)\), and the coefficient lengths of both blocks are bounded in terms of \(F\).
At a vertex \(B_v\) is a nonzero integer.
At an edge it is \(B(Z^l)\) for \(l=|e\cdot v|\), where \(e\) is its primitive lattice tangent and the orientation of \(B\) is fixed on a subsequence.
The collision lines have been removed, hence \(l\ge1\).

When the oriented \(B\) has all its zeros in the closed disk, the same holds for \(B_v\).
The [degree and measure theorem for separated blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) applies because \(g-2b_v\gg_{F,u}D\).
It gives degree \(\gg_{F,u}N\) for every monic irreducible nontorsion unit factor and a uniform positive measure bound on this subsequence for every positive-measure factor.
The vertex case has the same conclusion.
If the uniform statements in the first part failed, a sequence of failing ratios or measures would have such a convergent subsequence, or would come from one of the finitely many collision lines already treated.
This proves the first part, including reducible divisors by additivity and nonnegativity of integral Mahler measure.

For the degree assertion without monicity, suppose the edge roots are torsion.
For a nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(F_v\), the algebraic number \(B_v(\alpha)\) is nonzero at both vertex and edge faces.
At an archimedean place with \(|\alpha|>1\) the defining equation gives \(|B_v(\alpha)|\le \|C_v\|_1|\alpha|^{-g}\).
At \(|\alpha|\le1\) use \(\|B_v\|_1\).
The same inequalities at finite places have coefficient bound one by integrality and the ultrametric triangle inequality.
The product formula in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) now gives \(g h(\alpha)\le\log\max(2,\|C_v\|_1,\|B_v\|_1)\).
An irreducible primitive nonunit factor has logarithmic measure at least \(\log2\), by applying the root formula at the two endpoints.
As this measure equals its degree times \(h(\alpha)\), its degree is \(\gg_{F,u}N\).
Compactness as above gives the uniform lower bound, while additivity of degrees in the original specialization bounds the number of factors with multiplicities.

<a id="escape"></a>
## Necessary edge behavior on a shrinking sequence

For the third part apply the face argument to the fixed reciprocal component \(G\).
It forces an edge whose oriented polynomial has a zero outside the disk.
For its primitive tangent \(e\) take a complementary lattice vector \(e'\) with determinant one in absolute value.
Writing the exponents in this lattice basis gives \(G_v(Z)=H(Z^{e\cdot v},Z^{e'\cdot v})\) up to a monomial for a fixed Laurent polynomial \(H\).
If \(|e\cdot v|\) is bounded, fix its signed value \(s\) and the sign of \(e'\cdot v\) on a subsequence.
Then \(H(X^s,Y^{\pm1})\) is fixed, and the [fixed specialization factor gap](Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#statement) excludes that subsequence.
Consequently \(l\to\infty\), while \(l=o(N)\) follows from \(e\cdot u=0\).

For a Salem factor put \(t=\log\tau=m(A)>0\).
At its positive root, \(y=\tau^l>1\) satisfies \(|B(y)|\le \|C_v\|_1e^{-gt}\).
The [sparse-divisor bound](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) at \(A\mid G_v\), whose support size is fixed and whose degree is \(O_G(N)\), gives \(t\gg_G a/N\) for small \(t\).
Thus \(|B(y)|\ll_G e^{-c a}\) with \(c>0\) on the subsequence.
The degrees tend to infinity by Northcott finiteness.
The fixed nonconstant \(B\) keeps \(y\) bounded, and its factorization places \(y\) within \(O_G(e^{-c' a})\) of one of its roots.
After fixing that root and passing to the limit it is real and at least one.
The [house bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives \(y-1\ge\tau-1\ge(\log2)/(4a)\), excluding one and proving (1).
Here the faces approached are among finitely many edge normals, so the exponential constant can be reduced to one depending only on \(F\).
An edge of a factor has its initial polynomial as a divisor of the initial polynomial of the product in the same normal direction: the highest terms of two factors multiply without vanishing in the Laurent ring.
Consequently an oriented positive real edge root of \(G\) is also a root on the corresponding edge of \(F\).
This proves the asserted Salem exclusion for \(F\).
