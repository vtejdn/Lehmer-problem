# A prime trace family with all finite relative ramification at one dyadic place

The ambient-unit researcher developed this construction for [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
It tests whether a large group of inverse-pair sign changes forces ramification at a positive fraction of places in the original quadratic extension of the trace field.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The field, the place and its local degree

For each prime \(p\ge5\), put
\[
 P_p(z)=z^{2p}-z^{p+1}-2z^p-z^{p-1}+1,\qquad
 Q_p(T)=C_p(T)-T-2,\qquad C_p(z+z^{-1})=z^p+z^{-p}.
\]
For any root \(x\) of \(P_p\), put \(t=x+x^{-1}\), \(K=\mathbb Q(t)\) and \(A=\mathbb Q(x)\).
The polynomials \(Q_p,P_p\) are irreducible of degrees \(p,2p\).
The reciprocal unit \(x\) has exactly one real exterior conjugate, \(c=\lfloor p/4\rfloor\) pairs of nonreal exterior conjugates and \(N=p-1-2\lfloor p/4\rfloor\) neutral inverse pairs.
Its measures satisfy \(M(P_p)\le6\) and have a fixed limit strictly between one and two.

The quadratic extension \(A/K\) ramifies at exactly one finite place \(\mathfrak p\), above two.
It has absolute residue degree one and absolute ramification index in \(K\)
\[
                     e_{\mathfrak p}=1+2^{v_2(p^2-1)-2}.                \tag{1}
\]
At the prime of \(A\) above it the relative ramification index is two and the exponent of the relative different is two.
The relative discriminant ideal is \(\mathfrak p^2\), of norm four.
In the notation of the [original-field local degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v275.md#statement), \(\delta_\ell(Y)=0\) for \(\ell\ne2\) and
\[
 \delta_2(Y)=
 \begin{cases}e_{\mathfrak p}/p,&Y\ge e_{\mathfrak p},\\
               0,&Y<e_{\mathfrak p}.
 \end{cases}                                                         \tag{2}
\]

For \(p>23\) with \(p\equiv3\bmod4\), the Galois group on the inverse pairs contains \(A_p\), and the kernel of the action on those pairs consists of **all even sign changes**.
For each fixed \(s\ge2\), infinitely many such primes have \(e_{\mathfrak p}=2^{s-1}+1\).
In particular the family at \(p\equiv3\bmod8\) has positive finite relative ramification with \(e_{\mathfrak p}=3\) and \(\delta_2(3)=3/p\).
This coexists with a sign kernel of rank \(p-1\) and uniformly bounded original Mahler measure.
The relative ramification here occurs in the original root field; the fraction in (2) measures exactly the local mass in that field.

<a id="signature"></a>
## Irreducibility and all exterior and neutral roots

Modulo \(p\), \(Q_p(T)=T^p-T-2\) by the Chebyshev Frobenius identity, as in the [prime trace argument](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input).
On its roots Frobenius acts by adding 2, so each has degree \(p\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
Hence \(Q_p\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).

The odd polynomial \(C_p(T)-T\) increases from zero to infinity on \(T\ge2\), since at \(T=2\cosh u>2\) the derivative of \(C_p\) is \(p\sinh(pu)/\sinh u>1\).
By oddness there is no solution of \(C_p(T)-T=2\) below \(-2\), and above two there is exactly one.
For \(T=2\cos\theta,\ 0<\theta<\pi\), the trace equation is
\[
                      D(\theta)=\cos(p\theta)-1-\cos\theta=0.
\]
It requires \(\theta\ge\pi/2\).
At a peak \(\theta_k=2\pi k/p\) the value of \(D\) is positive exactly when \(\theta_k>\pi/2\); all valleys have negative value.
Each such peak gives one crossing on the rising half-wave by strict increase.
At a crossing on the descending half-wave its distance beyond the threshold is at least the distance from the peak to \(\pi/2\), which is at least \(\pi/(2p)\).
There \(0\le\cos(p\theta)=1+\cos\theta<1\), so
\[
 \sin^2(p\theta)\ge1-\cos(p\theta)=-\cos\theta
     \ge\sin(\pi/(2p))\ge1/p>1/p^2 .
\]
Consequently \(p\sin(p\theta)>\sin\theta\) and \(D'(\theta)<0\) at every descending crossing.
There is exactly one such crossing between the peak and its valley.
If an earlier peak's descending half-wave reaches \(\pi/2\), the phase difference from that peak is already at least \(\pi/2\), so \(\cos(p\theta)\le0\) there and it cannot equal \(1+\cos\theta>0\) before \(\pi\).
The endpoint \(\pi\) is a valley.
Thus each of the \((p-1)/2-\lfloor p/4\rfloor\) eligible peaks gives exactly two distinct neutral trace roots, and there are no others.
In particular \(t^2-4\) is negative in a real embedding of \(K\), so is not a square in \(K\).
The quadratic \(X^2-tX+1\) is irreducible over \(K\), proving the stated degree and irreducibility of \(P_p\).
The remaining counts follow from the single trace above two and complex conjugation pairing the other nonneutral traces.

For \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) one has \(0<c=(p-3)/4<(p-1)/4\).
Since both real and neutral inverse pairs occur, the [small-support classification](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#classification) and [even-sign theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v315.md#signs) apply for \(p>23\).
The product \(\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\) over inverse-pair representatives changes by the product of the coordinate signs.
Its square is \(Q_p(2)Q_p(-2)=4\); the product is itself rational.
Hence every signed permutation in the Galois group has even total sign change, giving the asserted equality of kernels.

<a id="local"></a>
## Dyadic multiplicity and ramification in the field

Both \(Q_p(2)\) and \(Q_p(-2)\) are \(-2\), so each of \(t-2,t+2\) generates an integral ideal of norm two.
Each is a single prime ideal with exponent one and residue degree one.
Any prime dividing either lies over two and contains their difference four, so the two ideals equal the same \(\mathfrak p\).
The [relative discriminant divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#relative) shows that no finite prime outside \(\mathfrak p\) ramifies in \(A/K\).

Modulo two, \(Q_p=C_p-T\).
The identity
\[
 C_p(z+z^{-1})-(z+z^{-1})
              =z^{-p}(z^{p-1}-1)(z^{p+1}-1)
\]
shows that the multiplicity at \(z=1\) is \(2^{v_2(p-1)}+2^{v_2(p+1)}\).
At this point \(T=z+z^{-1}=(z-1)^2/z\) in characteristic two.
Thus \(T=0\) has multiplicity \((2^{v_2(p-1)}+2^{v_2(p+1)})/2\) in \(\overline Q_p\).

To relate this polynomial multiplicity to the number field, each of the \(p\) embeddings \(K\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) supplies one distinct integral root of \(Q_p\), and their reductions with multiplicity give all roots of \(\overline Q_p\).
The embeddings for which \(t\) reduces to zero come precisely from places over two that contain \(t-2\).
We proved that there is just the single place \(\mathfrak p\), so the number of these embeddings is \([K_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb Q_2]=e_{\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak p}=e_{\mathfrak p}\).
One of \(v_2(p-1),v_2(p+1)\) equals one and their sum is \(v_2(p^2-1)\).
This proves (1) without any assumption on the index of \(\mathbb Z[t]\).

Write \(s=t+2\), a uniformizer of \(K_{\mathfrak p}\), and \(Y=x+1\).
Its equation \(Y^2-sY+s=0\) is Eisenstein, so the unique extension of the given place is totally ramified quadratic, and \(Y\) is a uniformizer there.
For coefficients \(a,b\in K_{\mathfrak p}\) the valuations of the two nonzero terms \(a,bY\) have different parities in this quadratic extension.
Thus \(a+bY\) is integral exactly when both coefficients are in \(\mathcal O_{K_{\mathfrak p}}\).
This proves that \(1,Y\) is a relative integral basis.

Put \(\Delta=2Y-s=Y-Y'\), where \(Y'\) is the conjugate over \(K_{\mathfrak p}\).
The relative traces of \(1/\Delta,Y/\Delta,Y^2/\Delta\) are \(0,1,s\), respectively, so the relative trace pairing of \(1,Y\) with \(1/\Delta,Y/\Delta\) has unit determinant.
The inverse different is therefore \(\Delta^{-1}\mathcal O_{A_{\mathfrak P}}\).
The valuation of \(2Y\) is \(2e_{\mathfrak p}+1\), whereas that of \(s\) is two.
Hence \(\Delta\) has valuation two, proving the different exponent and, since the residue degree over \(K_{\mathfrak p}\) is one, the relative discriminant exponent.
These local facts give (2).

<a id="parameters"></a>
## Bounded and varying local indices at bounded measure

For each fixed \(s\ge2\) the residue class \(p\equiv2^s-1\bmod 2^{s+1}\) is prime to its modulus.
There are infinitely many such primes by [the fixed-progression theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#fixed-progression-pnt).
Then \(v_2(p+1)=s\) and \(v_2(p-1)=1\), which gives the asserted local index and the even sign kernel for \(p>23\).
One can also choose increasing \(s\) and successively choose primes in those progressions larger than \(s\,2^s\) and the preceding prime.
Then both the index and the degree grow, while the entire ramified fraction \(e_{\mathfrak p}/p\) tends to zero.

Coefficient length gives \(M(P_p)\le6\).
The [fixed-polynomial specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) for \(y+y^{-1}-x-x^{-1}-2\) along \((1,p)\) gives, by Jensen's formula in \(y\),
\[
                \log M(P_p)\ \longrightarrow\
          I_2=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\cos v)\,dv.
\]
The integrand is positive inside the interval and bounded by \(\operatorname{arcosh}2\).
In particular \(0<I_2\le\tfrac12\operatorname{arcosh}2<\log2\), since \(2<\cosh(2\log2)=17/8\).
The finite ramification example has a positive limiting total logarithmic measure.
It excludes an inference of positive-density ramification from the presence of the even sign kernel and finite relative ramification alone, and leaves the small-measure range open.
