# Powers admitting sparse polynomial relations with unrestricted rational coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finitely many exceptional powers

Let \(\beta\ne0\) be algebraic and suppose that \(k\ge2\) of its conjugates over \(\mathbb Q\) are multiplicatively independent.
Define \(E_k(\beta)\) to be the set of positive integers \(n\) for which some nonzero rational polynomial with at most \(k\) nonzero terms vanishes at \(\beta^n\).
The exponents and coefficient heights of these polynomials are unrestricted.
There are positive integers \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\), depending on \(\beta,k\), such that
\[
 E_k(\beta)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\{n\ge1:n\mid g_i\},\qquad
 s\le B(k):=\sum_{j=2}^{k!}\binom{k!}{j}
             \exp\!\left(k(6(j-1))^{3(j-1)}\right).                 \tag{1}
\]
The list may be empty.
In particular, for every sufficiently large \(n\), any \(k\) distinct nonnegative powers of \(\beta^n\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Factoring out a common power gives the same conclusion for distinct integer exponents.
For \(k=1\) there is no exceptional power for any nonzero \(\beta\).

If \(\beta\) is nontorsion and power-stable, meaning \(\deg_{\mathbb Q}\beta^n=d=\deg_{\mathbb Q}\beta\) for every \(n\ge1\), then the rank \(r\) of the group generated by its conjugates modulo torsion satisfies
\[
                        d\le |\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_3)|<3^{r^2}.   \tag{2}
\]
Consequently (1) applies to any such \(\beta\) with \(d>3^{(k-1)^2}\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Removing the coefficients by a determinant

Fix multiplicatively independent conjugates \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k\) in a normal closure of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
Suppose \(n\in E_k(\beta)\).
If its relation has fewer than \(k\) terms, adjoin additional distinct nonnegative exponents with zero coefficients to obtain exponents \(a_1<\cdots<a_k\) and a nonzero rational coefficient vector.
Every conjugate satisfies the same rational relation.
Thus the matrix \((\alpha_i^{n a_j})_{1\le i,j\le k}\) has that vector in its kernel.
Expanding its determinant gives
\[
             \sum_{\sigma\in S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)M_\sigma=0,
             \qquad M_\sigma=\prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_i^{n a_{\sigma(i)}} .
\]
Choose a minimal nonempty vanishing subset \(I\) of these signed terms, so \(j=|I|\ge2\).
For each candidate subset fix two distinct permutations \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1\) in it.
Dividing by the term indexed by \(\sigma_0\) gives a unit equation with \(j-1\) variables \(M_\sigma/M_{\sigma_0}\), coefficients \(-\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)/\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_0)\), and right side one.
Minimality of \(I\) excludes every vanishing subsum on the left.

For this \(I\) all possible tuples belong to the image of the homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z^k\) sending \((e_1,\ldots,e_k)\) to
\[
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_i^{e_{\sigma(i)}-e_{\sigma_0(i)}}\right)_
                                    {\sigma\in I\setminus\{\sigma_0\}} .
\]
The constant exponent vectors belong to the kernel, so the tuple-group rank is at most \(k-1\).
The [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem, Theorem 1.1](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess-exact-counting-bound) gives at most \(\exp(k(6(j-1))^{3(j-1)})\) nondegenerate tuples for this fixed group and these fixed signs.
The original rational coefficients have disappeared.

<a id="divisors"></a>
## Recovering an integer divisible by the power

In each of the finitely many tuples read the coordinate corresponding to \(\sigma_1\).
It has a unique expression \(\prod_i\alpha_i^{w_i}\) with integer exponents, by the definition of the image and multiplicative independence of the chosen conjugates.
Discard all tuples with zero exponent vector.
For a tuple actually coming from the above determinant the coordinates are
\[
                 w_i=n(a_{\sigma_1(i)}-a_{\sigma_0(i)}).
\]
They are not all zero since the permutations are distinct and the \(a_j\) are distinct.
Record the positive gcd of the absolute values of the nonzero \(w_i\) for each remaining tuple.
The gcd produced by the actual tuple is divisible by \(n\).
Summing the count over the \(\binom{k!}{j}\) possible \(I\) of each size proves (1).
Divisibility by a recorded gcd is necessary only, since a unit-equation subsum need not produce a rational polynomial relation.

The [bounded coefficient-length result](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#prime) controls its exceptional gcds by evaluating a short integer relation at an actual power.
In (1) only the number of gcds is bounded uniformly.
Neither their magnitudes nor the least prime outside their divisor sets are bounded here in terms of \(k\) and the Mahler measure.
For power-stable \(\beta\) the logarithmic measure of \(\beta^n\) is \(n\,m(\beta)\).
The finite containment therefore gives no sequence simultaneously preserving small positive total measure and forcing the minimum support of all rational annihilators to tend to infinity.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Power-stable degree forces independent conjugates

Let \(K\) be a normal closure and \(\Gamma\subset K^\times\) the finitely generated subgroup generated by the conjugates of \(\beta\).
The Galois group preserves \(\Gamma\) and its torsion subgroup, so it acts on the free lattice \(\Gamma/\Gamma_{\mathrm{tors}}\) through a finite subgroup \(H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\).
Power-stability puts all \(d\) conjugates in distinct classes of this lattice.
Indeed, if the ratio of two distinct conjugates were a root of unity, raising to its order would identify those conjugates, and the corresponding power would have degree strictly less than \(d\).
The \(d\) classes form one orbit, hence \(d\le |H|\).

The [congruence-kernel argument](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#field) proves that reduction modulo three injects every finite subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\) into \(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_3)\).
The latter has fewer than \(3^{r^2}\) elements, since \(r\ge1\) and the zero matrix is excluded.
This proves (2).
When \(d>3^{(k-1)^2}\) one has \(r\ge k\).
The conjugate classes generate the lattice, so some \(k\) of them are independent over \(\mathbb Q\) after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\).
Their representatives are multiplicatively independent as required in (1).
