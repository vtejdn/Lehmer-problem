# Linear prediction from the past and the exact endpoint of a Salem collision bridge

<a id="statement"></a>
## The optimized quadratic bound

Let \(\alpha>1\) be Salem of degree \(d\ge4\), put \(m=\log\alpha\) and \(s=d-2\), and let \(\pi\) be the \(s\)-dimensional neutral embedding with coordinates \(\sqrt2\operatorname{Re}\sigma_l,\sqrt2\operatorname{Im}\sigma_l\), one pair for each neutral conjugate pair.
Start the [binary difference carry](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#statement) at zero, with \(C_{t+1}=\alpha^{-1}(C_t+\delta_t)\) and independent digits of probabilities \(1/4,1/2,1/4\) on \(-1,0,1\).
Fix integers \(k,L\ge1\), put \(N=kd,\ T=LN\), and condition on the exact return \(C_T=0\).
Let \(\delta\in\mathbb R^T\) be the digit column, \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\) the full digit past before block \(i\), and \(K=L^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^L\mathbb E\operatorname{Cov}(\pi(C_{iN})\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\) under this bridge.

For \(0\le t<T\), form column \(t\) of the real matrix \(A\) by stacking \(\alpha^t,\alpha^{-t}\) and the column vector \(\pi(\alpha^t)\).
The endpoint equations are \(A\delta=0\).
Let \(J_i\) select the first \((i-1)N\) digits and \(X_i\) be the \(s\)-by-\(T\) matrix supported on block \(i\), with within-block column \(v_j=\pi(\alpha^{j-N})\) for \(0\le j<N\).
The neutral carry at the end of block \(i\) equals \(X_i\delta\) plus a known linear function of \(J_i\delta\).

For block \(i\) allow any deterministic matrices \(B_i,D_i\) of compatible sizes.
Subtract a prediction based linearly on \(J_i\delta\) and add any combination of the endpoint equations, giving a residual coefficient matrix
\[
                       R_i=X_i-B_i J_i-D_i A.
\]
The [endpoint-conditioned quadratic estimate](Yin_C_G0419_v1.md#original-endpoint-conditioned-exponential-moments) assigns the upper bound \(R_iR_i^{\mathsf T}\) to the residual second moment.
Write \(U_i\) for the least such bound in the positive semidefinite order and \(U=L^{-1}\sum_i U_i\).
The coefficients may vary with the input, horizon, cut and output coordinate, and the minimization permits all real linear combinations.
Affine predictions give the same optimum since every digit is centered on the bridge.

Put \(c=(d e^{m(d-1)})^{-2/s}\).
Then \(K\preceq U\), and for every \(L\ge2\),
\[
          (\det U)^{1/s}\ \ge\
             (1-1/L)\,k c\left(\frac{c}{Ld}\right)^{1/(L-1)}.          \tag{1}
\]
For \(L=1\) the only cut is the terminal cut and \(K=U=0\).
Consequently, along every Salem sequence with \(m\to0\), \(k\sim1/(2m)\), and \(\log(4T)=o(L)\),
\[
                            \liminf m(\det U)^{1/s}\ \ge\ \tfrac12.     \tag{2}
\]
The [entropy bound on the actual covariance](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#statement) has threshold \(\pi/(8\mathrm e)\) for \(m(\det(K+\tau I_s))^{1/s}\) at \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\), with the positive dither variance \(\tau\) defined there.
Even at that choice of \(k\), optimizing this class of quadratic upper bounds cannot bring \(m(\det(U+\tau I_s))^{1/s}\) below that threshold in the limit.
The same limitation applies to the geometric mean of the squared-error proxies for the individual complex embeddings.
Indeed, if \(U_{ll}\) denotes the two-by-two principal block for conjugate pair \(l\), its complex squared-error proxy is \(\operatorname{tr}U_{ll}/2\) in the scaled real coordinates.
Hadamard's determinant bound followed by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on each diagonal pair gives
\[
                  \left(\prod_{l=1}^{s/2}\frac{\operatorname{tr}U_{ll}}2\right)^{2/s}
                         \ge (\det U)^{1/s}.
\]

<a id="prediction"></a>
## Least bound from the endpoint equations and the digit past

For any deterministic row \(r\), the zero-endpoint estimate gives \(\mathbb E[(r\delta)^2]\le rr^{\mathsf T}\) by its quadratic term and sign symmetry.
It applies using the integral power-basis columns for \(\alpha^t\), since their zero-sum event is \(A\delta=0\).
Thus \(\mathbb E[R_i\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}R_i^{\mathsf T}]\preceq R_iR_i^{\mathsf T}\).
Because \(A\delta=0\), the error here is the error of predicting \(X_i\delta\) from the past.
Optimal conditional mean prediction in each direction implies \(\mathbb E\operatorname{Cov}(\pi(C_{iN})\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\preceq R_iR_i^{\mathsf T}\).
Let \(H_i\) be the orthogonal projector onto \(\ker A\cap\ker J_i\) in digit space.
The component \(X_iH_i\) occurs in every residual, and all other components of its rows lie in the perpendicular subspace.
They can be removed simultaneously by choosing \(B_i,D_i\), since that subspace is the sum of the row spaces of \(J_i,A\).
It follows that \(U_i=X_iH_iX_i^{\mathsf T}\), proving existence of the least bound and \(K\preceq U\).
At the terminal cut the future consists of just the current block, whose neutral sum is determined by the past and the endpoint, so \(U_L=0\).

The same matrices give the actual conditional covariances for an auxiliary real Gaussian digit vector \(Z\) obtained by projecting a standard Gaussian onto \(\ker A\).
This vector is supported on \(AZ=0\) and satisfies \(\mathbb E[(rZ)^2]\le rr^{\mathsf T}\).
Within \(\ker A\), its components on \(\ker A\cap\ker J_i\) and on the orthogonal complement of that subspace in \(\ker A\) are independent standard Gaussian vectors on their respective subspaces.
The second component is determined by \(J_iZ\), because \(J_i\) is injective on that complement, while the first component vanishes under \(J_i\).
Hence the conditional covariance of \(X_iZ\) given \(J_iZ\) is exactly \(U_i\).
Any improvement for the binary bridge must use information beyond these real linear equations and the deterministic quadratic domination alone.

<a id="local"></a>
## Volume after removing the two real rows in every degree block

In the remaining digits at a cut, grant the predictors the two real evaluations separately on each consecutive group of \(d\) digits.
This enlarges the row space available for removing coefficients, since the global real rows restricted to this suffix are linear combinations of the separate real rows.
The resulting least Gram matrix is a lower bound for \(U_i\).
These additional evaluations need not be known on the binary bridge and are used solely for this comparison.

In a single group write \(E\) for the two real rows at exponents \(0,\ldots,d-1\), \(F\) for the scaled real neutral rows at those exponents, and \(\Pi_E\) for orthogonal projection onto \(\ker E\).
Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\).
The [full evaluation determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram) has squared absolute value \(|\operatorname{disc}P|\), since the replacement of conjugate rows by the scaled real coordinates preserves row volume.
The full evaluation matrix is nonsingular, and orthogonally removing the real rows from the neutral rows gives
\[
        \det(F\Pi_E F^{\mathsf T})
          =\frac{|\operatorname{disc}P|}{\det(EE^{\mathsf T})}
          \ge (d e^{m(d-1)})^{-2}=c^s.                               \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the discriminant is a nonzero integer and the product of the two real row norms is at most \(d e^{m(d-1)}\).

For one \(N\)-digit block put \(V=[v_0,\ldots,v_{N-1}]\), \(\Pi=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi_E,\ldots,\Pi_E)\) with \(k\) copies, \(M=V\Pi\), and \(W=MM^{\mathsf T}\).
Translating the exponents multiplies each real row by a nonzero scalar and rotates each neutral pair orthogonally.
Thus \(W\) is the sum of \(k\) orthogonal conjugates of \(F\Pi_EF^{\mathsf T}\).
Log-determinant concavity applied to their average and (3) give \((\det W)^{1/s}\ge kc\).
Moreover \(\operatorname{tr}W/s\le N\) since each of the \(N\) original columns of \(V\) has squared norm \(s\) and the digit-space projection is a contraction.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## The neutral endpoint constraint across the remaining blocks

Set \(h=L-i+1\) and let \(O\) be the orthogonal map rotating the neutral pairs by the corresponding \(\sigma_l(\alpha)^N\).
After removing the past columns and the separate real rows, the residual target has rows \([M,0,\ldots,0]\).
The residual global neutral endpoint constraints have row matrix \([M,OM,\ldots,O^{h-1}M]\) after one common invertible row rotation, because each succeeding block translates all exponents by \(N\).
These constraints have positive definite Gram matrix
\[
          S_h=\sum_{r=0}^{h-1} O^r W(O^r)^{\mathsf T},
          \qquad S_1=W,\qquad S_h=W+OS_{h-1}O^{\mathsf T}\quad(h\ge2).
\]
Removing their row space from the residual target leaves the Gram matrix
\[
                     P_h=W-W S_h^{-1}W\preceq U_i.
\]
Here \(P_1=0\), and when \(h\ge2\) both \(W\) and \(S_h-W\) are positive definite.
The identity \(P_h=W S_h^{-1}(S_h-W)\) gives
\[
                       \det P_h=\det W\,\frac{\det S_{h-1}}{\det S_h}.
\]
Thus the loss telescopes over the cuts even when the rotated Gram matrices have unequal eigenspaces.

For \(L\ge2\), discard \(P_1=0\) and apply log-determinant concavity to the other \(L-1\) matrices.
Monotonicity of determinant in positive semidefinite order then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
      (\det U)^{1/s}
       &\ge (1-1/L)\left(\prod_{h=2}^L\det P_h\right)^{1/[s(L-1)]}\\
       &=(1-1/L)(\det W)^{1/s}
                  \left(\frac{\det W}{\det S_L}\right)^{1/[s(L-1)]}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The trace bound yields \((\det S_L)^{1/s}\le\operatorname{tr}S_L/s\le LN\).
Inserting \((\det W)^{1/s}\ge kc\) and \(N=kd\) proves (1).

Along a shrinking Salem sequence the degrees tend to infinity by finiteness at bounded degree and bounded measure.
Consequently \(c\to1\), since \(-\log c=2(\log d+m(d-1))/(d-2)\).
The horizon assumption gives \(L\to\infty\) and \(\log(Ld)=o(L)\), so the last factor in (1) tends to one.
Multiplication by \(m\) proves (2).
The determinant bound constrains the upper estimate obtainable by the specified projection argument and leaves open a smaller covariance for the actual conditioned binary digits.
