# Primitive exponent patterns forced by small Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Patterns after removing translations and all integer dilations

Let \(P\) be the monic minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\), with natural log Mahler measure \(m\).
Call a finite set of nonnegative exponents minimal if it supports a nonzero ternary multiple of \(P\) and none of its proper subsets does.
Normalize its exponents by subtracting the least one and dividing the resulting integers by their gcd.
Let \(B_j(P,N)\) count the distinct normalized patterns arising from minimal sets of size \(j\) in \([0,N)\).
Write \(T=\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil\) and \(N=\lfloor T\log2/(4m)\rfloor\).
If \(0<m\le(\log2)/16\), there is a weight \(3\le j\le T+1\) for which
\[
 B_j(P,N)\ \ge\ \frac{2m}{\log j}\left(\frac{\log2}{64m}\right)^{j-1}.
\]
Along any sequence with \(m\to0\), the weight supplied by the argument tends to infinity and satisfies
\[
                 \log B_j(P,N)\ \ge\ (1-o(1))(j-1)\log(1/m).
\]
These statements allow all conjugate configurations.
In particular a uniform exponential bound on these normalized pattern counts, even just at the indicated lengths and for units realizing record measures in bounded degree, would prove the unrestricted Lehmer gap.

<a id="dilations"></a>
## Paying for all powered minima of one pattern

Fix a normalized \(j\)-element pattern \(E\) and write \(e_q=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^q)]\).
Let \(J_E\) be the positive integers \(q\) for which a signed polynomial with support exactly \(qE\) vanishes at \(\alpha\).
Then
\[
                  m\sum_{q\in J_E} q/e_q\ \le\ 2^{j-2}\log j .
\]
Indeed each such \(q\) gives a sign choice on \(E\), with the constant normalized to one, for which the corresponding polynomial vanishes at \(\alpha^q\).
There are at most \(2^{j-1}\) choices and each has multiplicative Mahler measure at most \(\sqrt j\), by Jensen's inequality on the unit circle and orthogonality of the monomials.
The monic minima of \(\alpha^q\) at distinct positive \(q\) are distinct, because their houses are \(\rho^q\) where \(\rho>1\) is the house of \(\alpha\).
For any one sign choice the product of the assigned distinct minima divides its polynomial.
Each minimum has log measure \((q/e_q)m\), by summing over the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Summing across sign choices proves the bound, since the other factors have nonnegative log measure.
In particular \(|J_E|\le 2^{j-2}\log(j)/m\) because \(e_q\le q\).
If all these powers preserve the field, the sum on the left is at least \(m |J_E|(|J_E|+1)/2\).

## A witness at degree-dependent finite length

Every set of \(T+1\) exponents in \([0,N)\) supports a nonzero ternary multiple of \(P\).
To verify this apply the [integral fixed-weight count](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#statement) with multiplicity one, size \(T+1\), and subset weight \(w=\lfloor(T+1)/2\rfloor\).
The central binomial coefficient has logarithm at least \((T+1)\log2-\log(T+2)\).
The cost of the real exponential growth is at most \(Nm\le T\log2/4\).
The remaining error fits because
\[
 \log(T+2)+d\log(8w)\ \le\ (d+1)\log(8(T+2))
             \ \le\ 9(d+1)\log(2d)\ <\ 3T\log2/4.
\]
Here \(d\ge2\), \(8(T+2)\le(2d)^9\) follows from \(T+2\le67d\log(2d)\), and \(d+1\le3d/2\).
The finite count therefore supplies the claimed relation on each set.

Let \(A_j\) count the minimal sets of size \(j\) and span less than \(N\) up to translation alone.
Since \(N\ge 4T\), the [random deletion bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v290.md#shapes) supplies a \(j\le T+1\) with \(A_j\ge(N/(4T))^{j-1}\).
The floor in \(N\) gives \(N/(4T)\ge\log2/(32m)\).
For a given normalized pattern there is just one translation class per dilation and no more than \(|J_E|\) dilations.
Thus \(A_j\le B_j(P,N)\,2^{j-2}\log(j)/m\), proving the displayed bound.

The [unit-equation estimate for fixed weight](../audits/literature/ESS02/Yin_U001.md#ess02-u001) bounds \(A_j\) uniformly in the unit and length at each fixed \(j\), as applied in the random deletion argument.
Hence this selected weight tends to infinity whenever \(m\) tends to zero.
In the logarithm of the displayed pattern bound, the loss from the factor \(m\) divided by \((j-1)\log(1/m)\) is \(1/(j-1)\).
The constant in the base contributes \(O(1/\log(1/m))\), and \(\log\log j/(j-1)\to0\).
This proves the asymptotic assertion.
If the unrestricted gap fails, positive unit measures tend to zero.
Minimizing positive measure up to the degree of each such unit gives a sequence of record-unit minima with measures tending to zero, by finiteness at bounded degree and bounded measure.
The pattern bound applies to these inputs and rules out a uniform exponential bound there.
