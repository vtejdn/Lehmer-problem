# A uniform positive Pisot excess for every eight-term trace lift on branch two

<a id="statement"></a>
## The full eight-term class

Let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\) with positive constant coefficient \(q\ge2\) on branch \(j=[z](f/f^*)-q^2+1=2\).
Let \(C\) be its [trace numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace), \(h=\deg C\) and \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
There exist absolute constants \(c>0\) and \(Q<\infty\) such that
\[
       \#\operatorname{supp}\Pi\le9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                       \theta-q\ge c,\qquad q\le Q .
\]
The lift is anti-reciprocal with zero midpoint, so a lift in this class has at most eight terms.
The conclusion is qualitative and covers both degree parities, all offsets and all integer coefficients.

<a id="limit"></a>
## The limiting polynomial

On this branch \(q\to\infty\) and \(\theta-q\to0\) are equivalent along sequences by the [derivative identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
The [six-term theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#statement) leaves just eight terms to consider.
Suppose there is an escaping sequence with eight terms.
The [three-offset and lattice reduction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) gives, after extraction, a rank-two lattice \(\Gamma\) generated by three vectors \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) and a fixed Laurent polynomial
\[
      A=2+\sum_{j=1}^3 a_j X^{v_j},\qquad a_j\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},
                      \qquad M(A)=2 .
\]
Here \(M\) is the multiplicative Mahler measure.
The three vectors evaluate to the actual positive lower offsets, so there is an integral linear functional strictly positive on all three.
The [affine-line exclusion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#eight) forbids collinearity of the three nonconstant exponent points.
The [minimum-modulus classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v101.md#independent) also forbids collinearity of any two of them with zero.
In particular the cone they generate has two extreme rays through distinct vectors, and the third vector is in its interior.

<a id="slices"></a>
## What a circle slice at an extreme ray forces

The following calculation concerns only \(M(A)=2\) and the geometry just obtained.
Use integral coordinates on \(\Gamma\) which send the two extreme vectors to \((p,0)\) and \((0,q_0)\), where \(p,q_0>0\).
For instance choose a primitive integral functional vanishing on each ray and positive on the other, and use the two functionals as coordinates in the appropriate order.
This gives an injective finite-index map into \(\mathbb Z^2\).
It preserves the torus measure integral because the dual map onto the original two-torus preserves Haar probability.
Thus in these variables
\[
           A(x,y)=2+a x^p+b y^{q_0}+c_0 x^r y^s,\qquad r,s>0 .
\]
If \(r=p\) or \(s=q_0\), the four points form a convex quadrilateral with a pair of parallel sides.
The [parallel-side exclusion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v99.md#parallel) rules out every such limit of actual inputs.
Suppose henceforth that \(r\ne p,\ s\ne q_0\).

For \(|x|=1\), the polynomial in \(y\) has constant coefficient \(2+a x^p\) and fixed positive degree with leading coefficient of fixed positive modulus, either \(|b|\) or \(|c_0|\).
Its log measure is continuous in \(x\) by the root product formula and is at least \(\log|2+a x^p|\).
The mean of the latter lower bound is \(\log\max(2,|a|)\), while the mean of the log measure is \(\log2\).
Hence \(|a|\le2\), and equality between the slice log measure and its pointwise lower bound holds at every \(x\) with nonzero constant coefficient, by continuity there.
If \(|a|=2\), approaching a zero of \(2+a x^p\) would force the log measure to tend to minus infinity, contradicting the lower bound given by its leading coefficient.
Thus \(|a|=1\), and interchanging the variables gives \(|b|=1\).

Choose any \(x\) with \(a x^p=-1\).
At this slice \(1+b y^{q_0}+c_0 x^r y^s\) has measure one, constant one and leading coefficient of modulus at least one.
The root product formula at both endpoints forces every root to have modulus one and the leading coefficient to have modulus one.
For a polynomial with all roots on the circle the conjugate reversal is a scalar multiple of the polynomial, because the roots with multiplicities agree.
The two positive powers are distinct and the unique interior exponent of this trinomial must therefore be the midpoint.
Consequently \(s/q_0\) is \(1/2\) or \(2\).
By interchanging the variables once more, \(r/p\) is \(1/2\) or \(2\).

If \(s=2q_0\), the polynomial in \(Y=y^{q_0}\) is \(1+bY+c_0x^rY^2\).
Its middle coefficient is real and nonzero, so conjugate reversal forces \(c_0 x^r=1\).
This holds at every solution of \(x^p=-1/a\), implying \(p\mid r\) by taking the ratios of these \(p\) roots.
In particular \(r/p=2\).
Likewise \(r=2p\) forces \(s=2q_0\).
When both ratios are \(1/2\), the interior-ray vector is the midpoint of the two extreme vectors, contrary to the affine-line exclusion.
When both ratios are \(2\), the same slice condition gives \(c_0=1\), since \(x^{2p}=1\) there.

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## A diagonal specialization in the last case

In the remaining case the Haar-distributed characters \(X=a x^p,\ Y=b y^{q_0}\) transform \(A\) into
\[
                           F(X,Y)=2+X+Y+X^2Y^2.
\]
If \(M(F)=2\), then for every phase \(X,Y\) the diagonal polynomial \(F(Xz,Yz)\) must have measure exactly two.
Its constant is two and its leading coefficient has modulus one, so its log measure is continuous in the phases and is bounded below by \(\log2\).
Its Haar average is \(\log M(F)\) by invariance under the simultaneous shift and Fubini, as in the [endpoint translation identity](Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#endpoint).
In particular \(2+2z+z^4\) would have no root in the open unit disk.

To see the contradiction, its reversal \(P(z)=2z^4+2z^3+1\) would then have all roots in the closed disk.
For a real polynomial \(P\) with all roots there and \(|\operatorname{lc}P|>|P(0)|\), the polynomial \(((\operatorname{lc}P)P-P(0)P^*)/z\) also has all roots in the closed disk.
Indeed \(|1-\bar\alpha z|\le|z-\alpha|\) for each root \(|\alpha|\le1\) and every \(|z|>1\), so \(|P^*(z)|\le|P(z)|\) there, and the numerator has zero constant term.
Applying this calculation twice gives first \(3z^3+4z^2-2\) and then \(5z^2+12z+8\).
The last quadratic has root product \(8/5>1\), which contradicts the disk condition.
This exhausts the possible exponent configurations and proves both bounds.
