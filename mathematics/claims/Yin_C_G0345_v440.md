# Two qualifying values excluded through prime 251 and three through 457

<a id="statement"></a>
## The next ordered degree pairs and triple

For monic reciprocal irreducible noncyclotomic integer polynomials of degrees \(56,58,68,70\), respectively, the multiplicative Mahler measures strictly exceed \(1.101,\ 1.0965,\ 1.082,\ 1.0775\).
In the [signed prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) at either sign there is at most one distinct positive qualifying value at each prime through 251.
At the next prime 257, the remaining ordered degree pairs for two distinct qualifying values are \((68,60),(70,58),(72,56)\), in increasing order of measure.
There are at most two distinct qualifying values at each prime through 457.
At the next prime 461, three distinct values require the ordered degrees \((86,72,56)\), with the degree-86 multiplicative measure below \(1.02\).
All these conclusions apply as well to the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at the same widths \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\).

<a id="census"></a>
## Separate cutoffs for the four degrees

At the four stated cutoffs a nontrivial power substitution would contradict the published small-degree bound after descent.
An auxiliary vanishing at a signed trace of a power would force power-field degree at most sixteen, contrary to the existing degree-56 power-field bound for every sub-Lehmer input.
These reductions and the rational ellipse certificates justify the [four sets of intervals](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v12/readme.md#bounds) for the first half of the Newton sums.
The first two enumerations join the coefficient suffixes to their prefixes using the certified range for each Newton congruence.
The other two use direct Newton recursion, with all integer sizes checked before enumeration.
Their [respective counts](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v12/readme.md#census) sum to 12,075,226,464 representatives and 35,251 final classifications.
The [separate verifier](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v12/readme.md#verification) checks every claimed cyclotomic divisor on the retained coefficient vectors by an integer Ramanujan sum.
It confirms 35,250 such divisors.
On the remaining degree-56 polynomial the coefficient of \(X^6\) after eight Graeffe squarings is \(37360284721810959382\), above the allowed \(1618136021964206191\), as also obtained by full integer convolution.
These cases exhaust the four possible irreducible searches at their respective cutoffs.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Products at 251 and 257

The [earlier prime restrictions](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#primes) exclude two distinct values through 241.
At \(p=251,\ b=126\) they leave exactly the ordered possibilities \((68,56),(68,58),(70,56)\).
Write \(T_j\) for the lower cutoff in degree \(j\) from the statement.
For a passing row the [strict derivative budget](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) gives
\[
       M(P_1)M(P_2)< C(p,b)=2^{1/4}
                \left(\frac{pb+1}{2pb+1}\right)^{1/p} .
\]
Each of the three cutoff products is greater than \(C(251,126)\) by the [exact integer comparisons](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v12/readme.md#prime).
This closes 251.

At \(257\), the inequalities \(128^8<257^7<129^8\) give \(b=129\).
The [prime-width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement) bounds the sum of the two degrees by 129.
If either measure is at least Lehmer's measure \(\rho\), the other logarithmic measure is smaller than
\[
       0.69315/4-0.162356-0.6926/257<1/108
\]
by \((2pb+1)/(pb+1)>1.999\) and the [certified logarithm bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite).
This contradicts [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), since \(3\cdot129<(65/24)^6<e^6\).
Both degrees are therefore at least 56 by the [sub-Lehmer degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree), and the smaller measure is less than \(2^{1/8}\), so its degree is at least 68 by the [1.091 threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree).
The six even ordered degree pairs under the sum bound consist of the three from 251 and the three in the present statement.
The smallest cutoff product among the former three is \(z=474531/400000\).
At \(p=257,\ b=129\) the integer comparison \(474531^{4p}(2pb+1)^4>400000^{4p}2^p(pb+1)^4\) gives \(z>C(p,b)\).
Thus only the latter three possibilities remain.

<a id="triple"></a>
## A degree budget of 215 for three distinct values

The [earlier three-value bound](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#primes) excludes all primes through 443.
The primes from 449 through 461 are \(449,457,461\).
Their widths are \(210,213,215\), respectively, by \((b-1)^8<p^7<b^8\).
The three degrees sum to at most \(b\) by the same prime-width criterion.
Let \(d_1,d_2,d_3\) be ordered by increasing measure.
The [sorted-measure argument](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#sums) gives \(d_1\ge86,d_2\ge68\).
Also \(d_3\ge56\) in this range: otherwise its logarithmic measure is at least \(\log\rho\) whereas the other two exceed \(4/\log^3(3\cdot215)>4/343\) in total by Voutier, contrary to the same comparison with \(B=(\log2)/4\).
Hence \(d_1\le91\), so Voutier gives \(M(P_1)>e^{1/108}>109/108\) because \(3\cdot91<e^6\).

The even possibilities for \((d_2,d_3)\) with \(d_2+d_3\le215-86\) are
\[
       (68,56),(68,58),(70,56),(68,60),(70,58),(72,56).
\]
In the first five cases use the four new degree cutoffs and \(T_{60}=1091/1000\) from the degree-68 threshold.
Their smallest cutoff product is \(T_{68}T_{60}=590231/500000\).
Since \(\big((109/108)(590231/500000)\big)^4>2\), even this least product makes the full product exceed \(2^{1/4}\), contradicting the budget.
The last pair requires total degree at least \(86+72+56=214\).
It is excluded at 449 and 457 and forces \(d_1=86\) at 461.
In this last case \(M(P_2)>53/50\) by the [1.06 degree threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree), and \(M(P_3)>1101/1000\).
The comparison \(((51/50)(53/50)(1101/1000))^4>2\) proves \(M(P_1)<51/50\).
Throughout the range \(p<3^8\), both prescribed orders are two.
All exclusions here concern finitely many primes.

The coordinating agent and computation contributors for Sheng Yin obtained the cutoff products and remaining degrees while testing the [unrestricted Lehmer problem](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
