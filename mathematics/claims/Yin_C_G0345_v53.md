# The first distant coefficients of a sparse leading-two interlacer are even except at zero or two positions

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction on the first group of coefficients

Let \(S_i\) be the trace minimal polynomials of Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\), and suppose they have strict integer interlacers \(A_i\) of leading coefficient two whose reciprocal lifts have a bounded number of terms.
Let \(B_i\) be the polynomial in the lower band of \(A_i^\sharp\) furnished by the [two-band theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v43.md#statement).
Suppose \(B_i\) is nonconstant and its smallest positive exponent \(h_i\) with nonzero coefficient tends to infinity.
After extraction write
\[
              B_i=2+z^{h_i}\big(D(z)+O(z^{l_i})\big),\qquad l_i\to\infty,
\]
where \(D\) is a fixed integer polynomial with \(D(0)\ne0\), and \(O(z^{l_i})\) denotes vanishing through degree \(l_i-1\).
Such an extraction exists by the bounded coefficients and term count.
On every such subsequence the number of odd coefficients of \(D\) is either zero or two.

More precisely, on a further subsequence put \(H=1-(-z)^a\) for the integer \(a\ge1\) given by the limiting circle measure below.
The reciprocal Salem polynomials converge coefficientwise to \(P_0=(1-z^2)/H\).
There is a Laurent polynomial \(V\) equal to zero or to a signed monomial such that
\[
     D\equiv HV\pmod2,\qquad
     z^{-h_i}(P_i-P_0)\longrightarrow
                \frac{(1-z^2)(D-HV)}{2H}
\]
coefficientwise as Laurent series with a common lower bound on their exponents.
Thus when \(D\) has odd coefficients at indices \(d_1<d_2\), the marginal index is \(a=d_2-d_1\) and \(V=\pm z^{d_1}\).

In particular suppose the entire lower polynomial is \(B_i=2+z^{h_i}D(z)\) with fixed \(D\).
Then the only possible \(D\) are \(\epsilon+\eta z^a\) with \(a\ge1\) and \(\epsilon,\eta\in\{-1,1\}\).
This last assertion gives a necessary condition over all fixed polynomials in this position.

<a id="initial"></a>
## The base measure and the position of the first change

Write \(k_i=\deg A_i\), \(T_i=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\), and use the positive trace weights, exterior weight \(t_i\), neutral circle lift \(\lambda_i\), and moment series \(G_i\) of the [growing-separation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v48.md#marginal).
By the [lower-band theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v43.md#statement), eventually \(h_i\le\deg B_i=o(k_i)\), every interlacer node lies in \([-2,2]\), and \(A_i^\sharp=B_i+z^{2k_i}B_i(1/z)\).
All coefficients of \(A_i^\sharp\) are bounded by the sparse height bound used in that theorem.
In particular \(|A_i(-2)|\) is uniformly bounded by the coefficient sum of the lift.
The identity \(G_i P_i=(1-z^2)A_i^\sharp\) makes every positive coefficient of \(G_i\) below \(h_i\) even.
The marginal proof in C345v48 uses only these parities, the bounded inverse endpoint integral \(-A_i(-2)/S_i(-2)\), the total residue mass two and positivity of integer traces.
It therefore gives on a further subsequence
\[
       \lambda_i\longrightarrow |H|^2\,dm,\qquad t_i\to0,\qquad
       G_i\longrightarrow G=2H,\qquad P_i\longrightarrow P_0 .
\]
Here \(m\) is circle Haar probability and all series limits are coefficientwise.

Let \(n_i\) be the first index at which \(P_i\) differs from \(P_0\).
It exists eventually since \(P_i\) is a polynomial of diverging degree and \(P_0\) is fixed.
If \(h_i-n_i\to\infty\) the [earlier-change proof](Yin_C_G0345_v48.md#early) applies unchanged: the numerator agrees with \(2(1-z^2)\) in every fixed window around \(n_i\).
Its lower estimate \(t_i\ge2(T_i-2)/(T_i+2)\) uses only strict interlacing, leading coefficient two and the greatest node at most two.
The saturated first moving moment then forces a torsion relation and contradicts a positive integer trace.
After further extraction we may therefore assume \(n_i\ge h_i-K\) with \(K\) fixed.
As in [the moving moment calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v48.md#moments), the common unchanged coefficient at \(h_i-K-1\) gives \(t_i\sigma_i^{h_i}=O(1)\).

<a id="joint"></a>
## Dividing the two-circle measure by the square of its marginal factor

Take a weak limit \(\Phi\) of the pushforwards of \(2\lambda_i\) under \((z,w)=(u^{-1},u^{h_i})\).
Its first marginal is \(2|H|^2\,dm\).
The exponent pair on the base variable \(u^{-1}\) is \((1,-h_i)\), which has no eventual nonzero integer relation.
Since \(t_i\gg T_i-2\) and \(t_i\sigma_i^{h_i}=O(1)\), every \(t_i\sigma_i^{j h_i}\) is bounded by a fixed inverse power of \(\sigma_i-1\) for each fixed \(j\).
The [positive moving mass theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v51.md#statement) gives a nonzero absolutely continuous part of \(\Phi\) in two dimensions.
The [finite-difference proof](Yin_C_G0345_v51.md#integrality) also gives, for every integer \(j\), an integer Laurent polynomial \(U_j(z)\) as the density of the projection of \(w^j\,d\Phi\).
We have \(|U_j|\le2|H|^2\).
Each simple zero of \(H\) forces a double zero of \(U_j\), so \(H^2\) divides \(U_j\) in the integer Laurent ring.
On the circle \(H^2/|H|^2=-(-1)^a z^a\).

Set \(\Psi=\Phi/|H(z)|^2\), ignoring the zero set which has zero first marginal for \(\Phi\).
The resulting positive measure has mass two and first marginal \(2\,dm\).
It has a nonzero absolutely continuous part and integer Fourier coefficients, the latter because every projected density \(U_j/|H|^2\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
Apply the [classification of positive measures of mass two](Yin_C_G0345_v11.md#classification) to \(\Psi\).
For clarity, its nonnegative integer Laurent density has mean one or two.
At mean two the full density is two or \(2+\epsilon(\chi+\chi^{-1})\) for a single nonconstant character \(\chi\).
Thus its first \(w\)-moment has density zero or one signed monomial in \(z\).
At mean one the density is identically one and its complement is a probability with integer Fourier coefficients.
Each nonzero coefficient of that probability is a sign, with the corresponding character constant almost surely.
Two distinct such characters of second exponent one would have quotient a nonconstant character of the first coordinate alone, contradicting the uniform first marginal of the complementary probability.
Again the first \(w\)-moment is zero or a single signed monomial.
Since \(H^2/|H|^2\) is a signed monomial, \(U_1=H^2V\) with \(V\) of the asserted form.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Returning to the actual coefficients

The [moving coefficient identity](Yin_C_G0345_v48.md#parity) uses only the lower bound \(h_i-K\) on the first changes of numerator, moment series and denominator.
Here the numerator change at \(h_i+j\) stabilizes to \([z^j](1-z^2)D\) for each fixed integer \(j\).
The coefficients of \(G_i-G\) there stabilize to \([z^j]U_1\): its neutral moment limit gives this coefficient, and \(t_i\sigma_i^{h_i}\to0\) by the same negative-offset Riemann–Lebesgue argument in [C345v48](Yin_C_G0345_v48.md#moments).
Formal multiplication and division by \(G(0)=2\) thus give a common Laurent lower bound and the integral limit \(F\) of \(z^{-h_i}(P_i-P_0)\), with
\[
        (1-z^2)D=P_0U_1+2HF=(1-z^2)HV+2HF .
\]
Reducing modulo two and canceling \(1-z^2\) gives the claimed parity and the formula for \(F\).

For the exact single group \(B_i=2+z^{h_i}D\), the two-band theorem gives \(M(B_i)=2\).
By [Lawton's specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002), the limit of \(\log M(2+z^{h_i}D(z))\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(2+wD(z)\) on two circles.
The vectors \((1,h_i)\) have shortest nonzero relation tending to infinity, and the two-variable polynomial is fixed.
Integration first in \(w\) gives \(\int\log\max(2,|D(z)|)\,dm=\log2\).
Thus \(|D|\le2\) everywhere and the sum of squares of its coefficients is at most four.
If all coefficients are even, \(A_i^\sharp\), and hence \(A_i\), is divisible by two, by injectivity of trace substitution modulo two.
The normalized interlacer \(A_i/2\) is monic and its lift has only circle roots, so its nodes are torsion traces by Kronecker.
The [torsion interlacing gap](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#statement) excludes this case.
The two odd coefficients must therefore be signs, and no nonzero even coefficient fits the squared bound.
Since \(D(0)\ne0\), the two indices are \(0\) and \(a\).
