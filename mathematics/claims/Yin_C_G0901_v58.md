# Irreducible signed actions and Salem detection for reciprocal units

<a id="statement"></a>
## A dichotomy at bounded total measure

Let \(w\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(2n\), and orient each reciprocal pair of conjugates by choosing one member \(w_j\), \(1\le j\le n\).
Let \(V_w\) be the real vector space with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\), on which a Galois automorphism acts by \(e_j\mapsto\epsilon e_k\) when \(w_j\mapsto w_k^\epsilon\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Changing orientations only changes the basis.
Suppose this signed permutation representation is irreducible over \(\mathbb R\).
Write \(m\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure, and use [Salem detection](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement) to mean nonzero inner product of the logarithm of the conjugate moduli of \(w\) with that of some conjugate of a primitive positive Salem number.

For every fixed \(M>0\), all such inputs with \(m(w)\le M\) and \(n\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M\) satisfy precisely one of the following alternatives.

* The unit is a signed conjugate of a nonzero integer power of a primitive Salem number.
* It is orthogonal to every conjugate of every primitive Salem number.

There are also conclusions without a measure or degree bound.
If \(w\) is detected, the positive primitive Salem number \(\alpha\) detecting it is unique, \(\deg\alpha=2n\), and \(m(w)\) is a positive integer multiple of \(\log\alpha\).
The divisible groups generated by all conjugates of \(w\) and of \(\alpha\), allowing torsion factors, coincide.
For odd \(n\ge5\) detection already implies the signed-power conclusion without an upper bound on \(m(w)\).
At any degree, if the number of real exterior roots of the minimum of \(w\) plus the number of nonreal conjugate pairs of exterior roots differs from one, only the orthogonal alternative is possible.
Exterior roots here have modulus greater than one.

One sufficient condition for the irreducibility hypothesis is that the kernel of the action on the \(n\) unoriented pairs has pairwise distinct coordinate characters on \(V_w\).
In particular, the conclusion applies when the kernel contains all even coordinate flips and \(n\ge3\).
It also applies if the permutation action on the trace conjugates of \(w+w^{-1}\) is primitive, all these traces are real, and there are both real off-circle pairs and nonreal pairs on the circle.

<a id="span"></a>
## A detected logarithm spans exactly one Salem representation

Use a common finite Galois field \(N/\mathbb Q\) containing all relevant conjugates, with group \(G\).
For an algebraic unit \(v\) in this field put \(f_v(g)=\log|g(v)|\), and act on real functions of \(g\) by \(R_s f(g)=f(gs)\).
This action preserves the uniform inner product and satisfies \(R_s f_v=f_{s(v)}\).
The linear map \(e_j\mapsto f_{w_j}\) is thus equivariant.
Its image \(W\) is nonzero because \(w\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit and hence has a conjugate off the circle.
Its kernel must be zero by irreducibility, so \(W\) is irreducible of dimension \(n\).
Passing from the splitting field of \(w\) to \(N\) does not affect irreducibility, since the action factors through a surjective restriction map.

Suppose the primitive Salem number \(\alpha>1\) is detected.
By the [algebraic form of the projection criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#algebra), a positive power of \(\alpha\) is a product of integer powers of conjugates of \(w\), up to a root of unity.
Conjugating and taking logarithms of moduli puts all the functions \(f_\gamma\) of conjugates of \(\alpha\) in \(W\).
Their span is a nonzero invariant subspace, so equals \(W\).
Choosing one orientation \(\gamma_j\) per inverse pair of \(\alpha\), the functions \(f_{\gamma_j}\) are nonzero and have disjoint supports because only the real pair of a Salem number lies off the circle.
They form an orthogonal basis of this span, and its dimension is \(\deg\alpha/2\).
This gives the degree equality.
Distinct positive primitive Salem generators have orthogonal spans by the [joint norm projection for their conjugates](Yin_C_G0901_v51.md#orthogonality), which proves uniqueness.

Expand \(f_w=\sum_j q_j f_{\gamma_j}\) in this basis.
The [conditional norm identity](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#coefficient) makes each \(q_j\) rational, including zero coordinates.
With a common positive denominator \(a\), the unit \(w^a/\prod_j\gamma_j^{a q_j}\) has modulus one at every embedding and is torsion by Kronecker's theorem.
Together with the reverse membership above, this gives equality of the two divisible groups.
The [integer orbit multiplier](Yin_C_G0901_v51.md#integral) applied to this expression and the equal input degrees gives \(\sum_j|q_j|\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).
The disjoint supports yield the exact measure
\[
                         m(w)=\left(\sum_j|q_j|\right)\log\alpha .          \tag{1}
\]
Indeed the mean absolute logarithm of an oriented Salem conjugate is \(2\log\alpha/(2n)\), and for a unit the total measure is half its degree times its mean absolute logarithm.
In particular \(m(w)\le M\) implies \(\log\alpha\le M\).

<a id="supports"></a>
## Recovering a single signed coordinate

The stabilizer \(G_w\) of \(w\) fixes its coefficient vector \(q\) for the signed action on the Salem basis.
Its orbit therefore has size at most \(2n\).
Let \(k\ge1\) be the number of nonzero coordinates.
The [orbit bounds for the Salem action](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#degrees) give \(2^k n/k\le2n\) when \(k<n\), because the supports cover the \(n\) labels and all \(2^k\) signs occur over each support.
For \(k=n\), at least \(2^{n-1}\) signs occur.
Hence for \(n\ge5\) the full support is impossible, and the remaining possibilities are \(k=1,2\).

For \(k=2\) and \(n\ge5\), at least four signed vectors lie over each support.
The number of supports is thus at most \(n/2\).
They cover all \(n\) labels by transitivity, so equality must hold and the supports form a partition into pairs.
This excludes \(k=2\) for odd \(n\).
For even \(n\) the stabilizer of the pair containing a given trace label contains the point stabilizer with index two, by transitivity on the labels and invariance of the partition.
The corresponding Salem trace field has a quadratic subextension over the fixed field of the pair stabilizer, hence admits a nonidentity rational automorphism.
This is the [field consequence of the matching](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#record), where only the support and the equality of degrees are used in this step.
For \(\log\alpha\le M\) and sufficiently large \(n\), the [uniqueness of bounded Salem traces over one base](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#base) gives a trivial rational automorphism group for this same trace field.
Thus only \(k=1\) remains in the bounded-measure assertion.

In that case orient the surviving coordinate as \(\gamma\), with \(f_w=q f_\gamma\).
By (1) and integrality of the coefficient absolute sum, \(q\) is a nonzero integer.
The stabilizer of \(q f_\gamma\) fixes the oriented root \(\gamma\), since another reciprocal label has disjoint support and inversion negates its nonzero function.
Therefore \(G_w\subseteq G_\gamma\).
Both have index \(2n\), so \(\mathbb Q(w)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
The quotient \(w/\gamma^q\) is a unit in this field all of whose conjugates have modulus one, hence a root of unity.
The field has a real embedding, so the root of unity is \(1\) or \(-1\).
This proves the signed-power conclusion.
Conversely such a power has nonzero inner product with the corresponding Salem conjugate, giving the asserted exclusivity.

<a id="signs"></a>
## Complex conjugation and tests for irreducibility

Let \(c\in G\) be ordinary complex conjugation.
A real inverse pair contributes a single fixed vector to \(V_w^c\).
A nonreal unit-circle pair is inverted by \(c\), and contributes none.
A nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots yields two distinct inverse labels exchanged by \(c\).
On that two-dimensional space \(c\) is a signed transposition whose two signs multiply to one, so it has a one-dimensional fixed space.
These are all the possibilities because no conjugate of a nontorsion unit is \(1\) or \(-1\).
It follows that \(\dim V_w^c\) equals the count of real exterior roots plus nonreal conjugate pairs of exterior roots.
A Salem signed representation has one real exterior root and no nonreal exterior roots, hence fixed-space dimension one.
Detection gave an isomorphism of \(G\)-modules with this Salem representation in the proof above, proving the stated obstruction in every degree.

Let \(J\) be the kernel of the permutation action on the inverse-pair labels.
It acts on each coordinate line by a sign character \(\chi_j\).
If these characters are pairwise distinct, the operator \(|J|^{-1}\sum_{h\in J}\chi_j(h)h\) is the projection onto the \(j\)-th coordinate, by character orthogonality.
Any invariant subspace is therefore a sum of coordinate lines, and transitivity on the labels forces it to be zero or all of \(V_w\).
When \(n\ge3\), even flips distinguish any two labels by flipping one of them and a third.
Finally, equality of the characters defines a permutation-invariant equivalence relation on the labels, since \(J\) is normal and conjugation transports the coordinate lines.
In a primitive permutation action the equivalence classes are all singletons or comprise one full class.
For totally real traces, \(c\) fixes every label, so \(c\in J\).
It has opposite signs on any real off-circle pair and any nonreal circle pair, excluding the full-class possibility when both occur.
Thus all the coordinate characters in that case are distinct.
In particular a primitive permutation action on such mixed real traces with at least two real exterior roots forces orthogonality in all degrees.
The general orthogonal branch of the unrestricted Lehmer infimum remains open.
