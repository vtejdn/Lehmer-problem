# Sublinear Pisot branches from multiquadratic subfields of Salem trace fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A trace-one subfield retaining almost all the quadratic generators

For \(k\ge0\) put \(s_k=\max(0,k-2-\lceil3\sqrt{k}\rceil)\).
Every totally real multiquadratic field \(F\) of degree \(2^k\) has a subfield \(F'\) of degree at least \(2^{s_k}\) whose maximal-order codifferent contains a totally positive element of trace one.
For a totally real field \(B\), that codifferent is \(\mathcal O_B^\vee=\{y\in B:\operatorname{Tr}_{B/\mathbb Q}(y\mathcal O_B)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}\).

In particular let \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) for a Salem number \(\sigma\) of degree \(d=2m\), let \(S\) be the minimal polynomial of \(T\), and suppose \(F\subseteq K=\mathbb Q(T)\).
Then \(t(S)\), the least trace of a totally positive element of the codifferent of \(\mathbb Z[T]\), satisfies \(t(S)/m\le 2^{-s_k}\).
Along any sequence \(\sigma\to1\) with such subfields of ranks \(k\to\infty\), there exist monic Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) of degree \(d\) with positive integer constant \(q\), exterior root \(\theta\), and branch \(j=[z](f/f^*)-q^2+1\) satisfying
\[
     zf+f^*=(z+1)z^mS(z+z^{-1}),\qquad \theta-q\to0,\qquad
     \frac{j}{m}\le(4+o(1))2^{-s_k},\qquad
     \frac{q\log\sigma}{m}\le(1+o(1))2^{-s_k}.
\]
Here \(f^*(z)=z^d f(1/z)\), and \(w_T\) denotes the residue at \(x=T\) of \((1-z^2)f/(zf+f^*)-(q-z)\), considered as a rational function of \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
If positive integers \(b\) make \(b w_T\) algebraic integers along the selected sequence, then \(b\ge (97/280-o(1))2^{s_k}\).
These sequence assertions are conditional on the given Salem numbers and the subfield inclusions.

<a id="characters"></a>
## A trace-one functional on squarefree character products

Represent \(F\) by its \(k\)-dimensional space of positive rational squareclasses, identifying every class with its positive squarefree integer \(d_0\).
The parity of the exponent of two and the residue modulo four of the odd part give two binary characters on this space, since odd squares equal one modulo four.
Their simultaneous kernel \(V\) has dimension \(h\ge k-2\) and consists of odd \(d_0\equiv1\bmod4\).
Let \(\mathcal P\) be the finite set of primes occurring in \(V\).
For \(p\in\mathcal P\) put \(p^*=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p\) and \(E_p=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p^*})\).
For each \(d_0\in V\) the product of the \(p^*\) over \(p\mid d_0\) equals \(d_0\), so the compositum \(E\) of the \(E_p\) contains the real multiquadratic field represented by \(V\).

The signed prime squareclasses are linearly independent by their distinct prime valuations.
Thus \(E\) has all independent changes of radical sign as its Galois group, and its embeddings run over all tuples of embeddings of the \(E_p\).
Each \(E_p\) has discriminant \(p^*\) and integer basis \(1,\omega_p=(1+\sqrt{p^*})/2\).
The products of these bases form an integer basis of \(E\).
Indeed for linearly disjoint fields \(B,D\) with coprime discriminants, an integral basis \((a_i)\) of \(B\) is a \(D\)-basis of \(BD\), so write \(x\in\mathcal O_{BD}\) as \(\sum_i a_i x_i\) with \(x_i\in D\).
Then \(x_i=\operatorname{Tr}_{BD/D}(x a_i^\vee)\) for the trace-dual basis \(a_i^\vee\), and \(\operatorname{disc}(B)a_i^\vee\in\mathcal O_B\) by the adjugate of the integral trace matrix.
It follows that \(\operatorname{disc}(B)x\in\mathcal O_B\mathcal O_D\), and interchanging the fields gives the same inclusion with \(\operatorname{disc}(D)\).
Bézout proves equality of the product order with \(\mathcal O_{BD}\).
Induction applies here because the trace matrix in a product basis is a tensor product, so its determinant has prime divisors only among the discriminants already used.

The element \(y_p=(1+1/\sqrt{p^*})/2\) pairs to one under the quadratic trace with each of \(1,\omega_p\).
Consequently \(Y=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}y_p\) has trace one and belongs to \(\mathcal O_E^\vee\), by the product basis and factorization of the trace on simple tensors.
This construction allows imaginary \(E_p\).
For a subspace \(W\subseteq V\) of dimension \(s\) let \(F_W=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{d_0}:d_0\in W)\).
The element \(y_W=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F_W}Y\) belongs to \(\mathcal O_{F_W}^\vee\) by transitivity of the trace, and has rational trace one.
Expanding \(Y\) and summing over the sign changes fixing \(F_W\) kills exactly the characters outside \(W\).
At each real embedding of \(F_W\) this gives
\[
       y_W=2^{-s}\left(1+\sum_{1<d_0\in W}
                               \frac{\epsilon_{d_0}}{\sqrt{d_0}}\right),
               \qquad \epsilon_{d_0}\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Indeed each surviving product of inverse radicals is real and has absolute value \(d_0^{-1/2}\), while the subgroup sum has size \(2^{|\mathcal P|-s}\) against the denominator \(2^{|\mathcal P|}\).
Thus \(\sum_{1<d_0\in W}d_0^{-1/2}<1\) suffices for total positivity, with no restriction on the overlap of the prime supports.

<a id="subspace"></a>
## Choosing a large subspace on which the weights sum to less than one

Identify \(V\) with its vectors of prime-exponent parities in \(\mathbb F_2^{\mathcal P}\).
There are \(h\) coordinate primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_h\) whose joint projection is an isomorphism on \(V\), by row reduction.
Each \(d_0\) contains the full product of the selected primes occurring in its projected vector.
Summing over all such vectors gives
\[
    \sum_{d_0\in V}d_0^{-1/2}
      \le\prod_{j=1}^{h}(1+p_j^{-1/2})
      \le \exp(2\sqrt h).
\]
For the last bound order the primes increasingly, use \(p_j\ge j\), \(\log(1+x)\le x\), and \(\sum_{j=1}^h j^{-1/2}\le2\sqrt h\).
Take \(c=\min(h,\lceil3\sqrt h\rceil)\).
For \(c=h\) take \(W=0\).
Otherwise a uniformly chosen codimension-\(c\) subspace contains a given nonzero vector with probability \((2^{h-c}-1)/(2^h-1)<2^{-c}\), by symmetry and counting its nonzero vectors.
The expected sum of the nontrivial weights in \(W\) is therefore below \(2^{-c}\exp(2\sqrt h)<1\), since \(3\log2>2\).
Some \(W\) satisfies the strict positivity criterion and has dimension \(h-c\ge s_k\).
Taking \(F'=F_W\) proves the field assertion.
The construction is testable on prescribed radicands by finding such a subspace in the finite binary vector space.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## The Salem inverse and the range of the relative height bound

Extend the trace-one \(y_W\) from \(F'\) to \(K\) as an element of \(K\).
For \(a\in\mathcal O_K\), the relative trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F'}a\) lies in \(\mathcal O_{F'}\), so \(y_W\in\mathcal O_K^\vee\subseteq\mathbb Z[T]^\vee\).
It remains totally positive and its rational trace from \(K\) is \([K:F']\le m2^{-s_k}\).
This proves the bound on the codifferent minimum.
The [factor-four branch construction](Yin_C_G0345_v34.md#statement) gives \(q\sim t(S)/\log\sigma\), \(\theta-q\to0\) and \(j\le(4+o(1))t(S)\) along the specified sequence.
For any indicated integral scaling the [normalized trace bound](Yin_C_G0345_v37.md#statement) gives \(\liminf bj/m\ge97/70\).
Combining the two inequalities gives the asserted lower bound on \(b\).

The [rank-one relative height bound of Amoroso–Zannier in the form recorded by Amoroso–Masser](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002) already restricts the possible growth of \(2^k\).
Indeed \(F/\mathbb Q\) is abelian and \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\sigma)\), so the relative degree is \(D=[F(\sigma):F]=2m/2^k\).
For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) the bound \(h(\sigma)\ge c_\varepsilon D^{-1-\varepsilon}\) and \(h(\sigma)=\log\sigma/(2m)\) give
\[
         \log\sigma\ge c_\varepsilon (2^k)^{1+\varepsilon}(2m)^{-\varepsilon}.
\]
If \(2^k\ge m^\delta\) infinitely often for some fixed \(\delta>0\), then choosing \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\) makes the right side grow as a positive power of \(m\).
Since degrees grow along \(\sigma\to1\), every such sequence must satisfy \(2^k=m^{o(1)}\).
The sublinear branch conclusion applies also to ranks tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly within this restriction.
