# The full Mahler cost of prescribing a separated Salem trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum-degree perturbation and the roots of its complementary factor

Let \(P(z)=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma=e^a\), with \(m\to\infty\) and \(a\le A\).
Let \(\tau=e^b\) be any Salem target with \(b\le B\), whose trace minimal polynomial \(D\) has degree \(t\le m\).
Write \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), \(y=\tau+\tau^{-1}\ne T\), and put
\[
              L=mb+\log|y-T|,\qquad H=\sqrt m\log(2m).
\]
Constants below may depend on the fixed bounds \(A,B\).
A perturbation \(R\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(r<m\) produces the target when \(D\mid S+R\), equivalently when \(\tau\) is a root of the monic reciprocal lift
\[
                    Q_R(z)=P(z)+z^mR(z+z^{-1}).
\]
Let \(R_0\) be the negative remainder of \(S\) after monic division by \(D\).

Suppose \(L>0\) and
\[
                      H+t\log(2t)=o(L).                              \tag{1}
\]
Then \(R_0\) has degree exactly \(t-1\) and is the unique perturbation of degree at most \(t-1\) giving the target.
Its leading coefficient satisfies
\[
                \log|[X^{t-1}]R_0|=L-(t-1)b+O_{A,B}(H).             \tag{2}
\]
The entire lifted perturbation has logarithmic measure \(\log M(Q_{R_0})=(1+o(1))L\).
After division by one copy of the target minimal polynomial, the complement still has logarithmic measure \((1+o(1))L\) and at least \(m-t\) roots of modulus exceeding \(\exp((1-o(1))L/m)\), counted with multiplicity.
Along any subsequence \(L/m\to l\), the empirical root measures of both \(Q_{R_0}\) and its complement converge to the equal mixture of the uniform circle laws at radii \(e^l,e^{-l}\).
For \(l=0\) these circles coincide.

There is a lower bound with no minimum-degree requirement.
If \(L>0,\ H=o(L)\), \(R\) produces the target, and
\[
                 r\bigl(1+\log^+(m/L)\bigr)=o(L),                   \tag{3}
\]
then \(Q_R\) has at least \(m-r\) roots of modulus exceeding \(\exp((1-o(1))L/m)\).
Consequently \(\log M(Q_R)\ge(1-o(1))L\), and at most one of the counted exterior roots is accounted for by one copy of the target.
There is no bound on the coefficients of \(R\) in this assertion.
For a fixed target and any bounded Salem input sequence converging to a limit different from the target, \(L/m\to b\), so (3) allows every \(r=o(m)\).
In particular the [golden-ratio-limit sequence and its unique quartic for Lehmer's number](Yin_C_G0345_v132.md#coefficient-cost) have complementary roots with the two limiting uniform circle laws at Lehmer's number and its inverse.
The estimates also apply to varying targets approaching one under the indicated inequalities, which depend on the separation of source and target.

The coordinator supplied the exterior evaluation, integral interpolation, inner-circle comparison and full-cost argument.
The contributor sharpened the degree range by averaging the logarithmic loss on the comparison circle, computed the leading-coefficient correction and determined the limiting distribution of the complementary roots.

## Evaluation at the target conjugates

Write the neutral source traces as \(x_i=2\cos\theta_i\), one from each inverse pair.
Factoring at \(y=2\cosh b\) gives
\[
 \log|S(y)|=\log|y-T|+(m-1)b
            +2\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\log|1-e^{-b+i\theta_i}|.
\]
Let \(\nu\) be the uniform measure on all \(2m-2\) source neutral angles, including both signs.
By the [neutral Fourier estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v119.md#slope), \(\sum_{1\le j\le 2m}|\widehat\nu(j)|^2/j\ll_A\log(2m)/m\).
The [degree-dependent Mahler lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) for the target and bounded-degree finiteness give \(b\gg(\log(2m))^{-3}\), uniformly for \(t\le m\).
Expanding the logarithms, Cauchy–Schwarz up to \(2m\) bounds their sum in absolute value by
\[
       O_A\!\left(\sqrt{m\log(2m)}
                    \sqrt{\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-2bj}/j}\right).
\]
The remaining tail is \(O(m e^{-2mb}/(2m(1-e^{-b})))=o(1)\).
The resulting bound is \(O_A(H)\), whence \(\log|S(y)|=L+O_{A,B}(H)\).
The [circle supremum bound](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) for the separable monic \(P\) at bounded measure gives \(\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\ll_A H\), by taking confluence order \(\lfloor\sqrt{2m}\rfloor\).
Thus \(|S(y_i)|\le e^{O_A(H)}\) at each neutral conjugate \(y_i\) of the target trace, including the case of zero evaluation.

<a id="interpolation"></a>
## Inverse derivatives and the integral remainder

List the \(t\) conjugates of \(y\), writing \(y_1=y\).
Use the basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{t-1}\) with monic \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\) for \(j\ge1\).
Its evaluation determinant at these nodes has the ordinary Vandermonde absolute value \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}D|}\ge1\).
Deleting a node and the last column gives the Vandermonde determinant at the remaining nodes, so the absolute ratio is \(1/|D'(y_i)|\).
Every remaining neutral row has norm at most \(2\sqrt t\), and the single exterior row when present has norm at most \(2\sqrt t\,e^{tb}\).
Hadamard's inequality therefore gives \(1/|D'(y_i)|\le \exp(O_B(t\log(2t)))\) at every node.
At the exterior node, the [slope formula applied to the target itself](Yin_C_G0345_v119.md#slope) gives
\[
                         \log D'(y)=(t-1)b+O_B(\sqrt t\log(2t)).
\]
For bounded target degrees the same bound holds after enlarging the constant by bounded-degree finiteness at \(b\le B\).

Lagrange interpolation expresses the leading coefficient of \(R_0\) as \(-\sum_i S(y_i)/D'(y_i)\).
The exterior term has log magnitude \(L-(t-1)b+O_{A,B}(H)\) by the preceding evaluation and \(t\le m\).
The sum of the absolute values of all remaining terms is at most \(\exp(O_{A,B}(H+t\log(2t)))\).
Under (1) its ratio to the exterior magnitude tends to zero, proving (2), including nonvanishing.
Every \(R\) producing the target is congruent to \(R_0\bmod D\) and the negative remainder is integral by monicity.
This proves the minimum and uniqueness assertions.
On \([-2,2]\) each Lagrange numerator \(\prod_{j\ne i}(X-y_j)\) has modulus at most \((2+2\cosh B)^{t-1}\).
Consequently
\[
              \log\max(1,\|R_0\|_{[-2,2]})\le L+o(L).               \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(\log M(Q_{R_0})\le L+o(L)\) by the unit-circle supremum and \(H=o(L)\).

<a id="circle"></a>
## A comparison circle for a perturbation of arbitrary height

Let \(R\) produce the target and have exact degree \(r<m\).
It is nonzero since the distinct Salem traces have distinct minimal polynomials.
Set \(U(z)=z^r R(z+z^{-1})\), an ordinary polynomial of degree \(2r\) with nonzero constant term, also when \(r=0\).
At \(z=\tau\) one has \(|U(\tau)|=\tau^r|S(y)|\).
From the product formula for its complex roots \(w_i\), with multiplicity,
\[
              |U(\tau)|\le M(U)(1+\tau)^{2r}.
\]
Thus \(\log M(U)\ge L-O_{A,B}(H+r)\).
Under the conditions of (3), \(L\to\infty,\ L/m\le B+O_{A,B}(1/m)\) and \(r=o(m)\).

For \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), take the interval of positive \(s\) between \((1-2\varepsilon)L/m\) and \((1-\varepsilon)L/m\), of length \(h=\varepsilon L/m\).
For \(z\) of modulus \(e^{-s}\), the reverse triangle inequality gives, uniformly in each root,
\[
 \frac{|z-w_i|}{\max(1,|w_i|)}
       \ge c_B\min\{1,|s+\log|w_i||\}
\]
for sufficiently large \(m\).
Indeed the exact radial difference divided by \(\max(e^{-s},|w_i|)\) is \(1-\exp(-|s+\log|w_i||)\), while \(\max(e^{-s},|w_i|)/\max(1,|w_i|)\ge e^{-s}\).
On an interval of length \(h\), integration over the level sets of length at most \(\min(h,2e^{-u})\) for \(u>0\) bounds the mean of \(\log^-|s-v|=\max(0,-\log|s-v|)\) by \(1+\log^+(2/h)\) for every \(v\).
Summing this inequality over the \(2r\) roots gives a single radius throughout whose circle
\[
   \log |U(z)|\ \ge\ \log M(U)
            -O_B\!\left(r\left[1+\log^+\!\frac{m}{\varepsilon L}\right]\right).
\]
This average has no dependence on the separation between different root moduli.
Under (3) choose \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) slowly enough that \(H+r(1+\log^+(m/(\varepsilon L)))=o(\varepsilon L)\).
For example one may take the cube root of \((H+r(1+\log^+(m/L)))/L\) for all sufficiently large inputs.
On the selected inner circle,
\[
      \log|z^{m-r}U|\ \ge\ L-(m-r)s-o(\varepsilon L)
                              \ge (1-o(1))\varepsilon L .
\]
The maximum principle bounds \(|P|\) there by \(\exp(O_A(H))\).
Rouché therefore gives \(Q_R\) at least \(m-r\) roots strictly inside \(e^{-s}\) from the factor \(z^{m-r}\).
By reciprocity at least \(m-r\) roots lie strictly outside \(e^s\).
Since \(s=(1-o(1))L/m\) and \(r=o(m)\), this also proves the full lower measure bound.

<a id="distribution"></a>
## Complementary measure and the limiting radii and arguments

Under (1) the minimum degree \(r=t-1\) satisfies (3).
Indeed \(H=o(L)\) implies \(m/L<\sqrt m\) eventually.
If \(t\ge m^{1/4}\), then \(\log^+(m/L)\le2\log(2t)\).
For \(t<m^{1/4}\), the product \(t\log(2m)\) is already \(o(H)\).
The lower estimate on \(\log M(Q_{R_0})\) and (4) give \(\log M(Q_{R_0})=L+o(L)\).
Removing one copy of the target costs \(b\le B\) and at most one of the counted exterior roots.
Thus the remaining logarithmic measure is \(L+o(L)\) and at least \(m-t\) roots are still exterior at the indicated radius.
All such roots belong to noncyclotomic factors of the complement.

Write the roots of \(Q_{R_0}\) as \(z_j=e^{v_j+i\theta_j}\) and put \(l_m=L/m\).
Reciprocity gives a matching interior root \(1/\bar z_j\) for every exterior root, with the same angle.
Consequently all but \(2r=o(m)\) roots have \(|v_j|\ge(1-o(1))l_m\), and \(\sum_j |v_j|=2\log M(Q_{R_0})=2L+o(L)\).
The sum of the deficits \((l_m-|v_j|)_+\) is \(o(L)\), so the same holds for the sum of the excesses.
In particular \(\sum_j||v_j|-l_m|=o(L)\).

There is also a fixed house bound.
For fixed \(s>A+B+4\), on \(|z|=e^s\) the original product satisfies \(|P(z)|\ge(e^s-e^A)^{2m}\).
On this circle \(|z^{m-r}U(z)|\le e^{(m-r)s}M(U)(1+e^s)^{2r}\) and \(\log M(U)\le\log\|R_0\|_{[-2,2]}\le L+o(L)\).
Since \(L/m\le B+o(1)\) and \(r=o(m)\), the lower bound for \(P\) dominates uniformly, so all roots of \(Q_{R_0}\) lie inside this fixed radius by Rouché.
Reciprocity gives the matching inner bound.

For every fixed integer \(j\ge1\), the powers of order \(j\) have the same sum in \(Q_{R_0}\) and \(P\) eventually, by Newton's identities since the changed coefficients start at depth \(m-r\to\infty\).
Dividing the source sum by \(2m\) gives a quantity tending to zero by its neutral Fourier estimate and the bounded exterior pair.
For the perturbed roots, pairing across the unit circle rewrites that normalized sum as the mean of \(\cosh(j|v_i|)e^{ij\theta_i}\), with the same expression valid at unit roots.
The uniform house bound and the mean radial deviation \(o(L/m)=o(1)\) permit replacing each weight by \(\cosh(jl_m)\) with total normalized error \(o(1)\).
This positive common weight is at least one.
Thus the aggregate angular measures tend to the uniform circle law by the trigonometric polynomial criterion.
The inner and outer roots have identical lists of angles with multiplicity, and at most \(2r=o(m)\) roots can lie exactly on the circle.
Their separate normalized angular distributions therefore both tend to the uniform law.
Combined with radial concentration this proves the two-circle weak limit whenever \(l_m\to l\).
Removing the \(2t=o(m)\) target roots preserves that probability limit.
