# Degrees of sparse rational factors and the exact rational lift of a conductor antichain

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degrees comparable to the support width up to logarithms

Let \(P=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}c_iX^{a_i}\in\mathbb Z[X]\), with every \(c_i\ne0\), \(0=a_0<\cdots<a_{s-1}=D\), and \(L=\sum_i|c_i|\).
Let \(S\) be the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) dividing \(P\), with degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Define \(\lambda(x)=(\log\log y/\log y)^3\) for \(y=\max(16,x)\).
If no proper nonempty initial sum of \(P\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), then
\[
 \frac{c}{s^2}\ \le\ \frac{Dm}{d}\ \le\ (s-1)\log L,\qquad
 d\ \gg\ \frac{D\lambda(D)}{(s-1)\log L}.                                \tag{1}
\]
The constants are absolute.
The hypothesis on initial sums holds for **every** nontorsion unit factor when \(s\le5\), and for every such factor with \(0<m<\log\theta\) when \(s\le7\), where \(\theta\) is the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
No restriction on the exponent gcd or orientation of \(P\) is needed.
The five-term threshold for all nontorsion unit factors cannot be extended to six: \((1+X^u)(X^3-X-1)\) for \(u>3\) has six terms, bounded length and a fixed nontorsion unit factor.
For arbitrary \(s\) the successive zeros of the initial sums divide the support into consecutive annihilating blocks.
Each such block satisfies (1) with its own term count, length and width between its extreme terms.

There is also an elementary degree estimate retaining the end gaps \(b_-=a_1,\ b_+=D-a_{s-2}\).
Under the same no-vanishing hypothesis,
\[
 d\ge\frac{\sqrt{D\,b}}{\sqrt{20(s-1)}\log L},\qquad
 b=\min(b_-,b_+).
                                                                         \tag{2}
\]
For reciprocal \(S\) one may take \(b=\max(b_-,b_+)\).
Here the [two-separated-power estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v247.md#two) is replaced by estimates at the actual cuts of the support.

In the five-term parent \(P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1\) with odd \(n\) prime to three, every genuine noncyclotomic factor reaches a [binary order](#orders) \(3t\) with \(t\gg n\lambda(n)/\tau(n)\), even for incomplete levels and without a coprimality assumption on \(l\).
For the antichain in the [two-prime specialization](#lift), the only rational lift at any \(0<l<n\), for all sufficiently large \(A\), occurs at odd \(A\) and \(l=n/5\) and is given by the explicit cyclotomic product there.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## The classical gap argument and the bounds for unit factors

Lenstra, Proposition 2.3 and its proof on printed pp. 270–271, use the product formula at the two sides of a gap to bound the height of a root where the two sums are nonzero.
Amoroso and David sum that estimate over surviving consecutive cuts in the proof of their Proposition 3.3, printed pp. 26–27, before their separate Galois-group argument.
These are classical height estimates.
The earlier [project discussion of Lenstra's gap](../ledger/Yin_L_G0450_v6.md#moving-map-stronger-source-boundary) records a near-linear degree estimate for a particular four-term family over a coefficient number field.
Here we use the integral-unit version for all the relevant cuts, then apply it to actual divisor degrees and residual orders in a moving parent. [Lenstra, Proposition 2.3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1999a/art.pdf#page=4) and [Amoroso and David, Proposition 3.3](https://www.rivmat.unipr.it/wwwprotetto/2022-13-1/Riv_Parma_13-1-2022_02.pdf#page=8)

At a cut let \(C=\sum_{i\le j}c_iX^{a_i}\) and \(G=a_{j+1}-a_j\).
If \(C(\alpha)\ne0\) its norm over all \(d\) conjugates is a nonzero rational integer.
At an exterior conjugate \(x\), \(|C(x)|\le L|x|^{a_j}\).
At an interior conjugate, use \(C(x)=-\sum_{i>j}c_ix^{a_i}\) to give \(|C(x)|\le L|x|^{a_{j+1}}\), and the bound \(L\) suffices on the circle.
The exterior and interior sums of the log moduli are \(m\) and \(-m\), respectively, because \(\alpha\) is a unit.
Hence
\[
                     \log|\operatorname{Res}(S,C)|+Gm\le d\log L .
\]
Every surviving cut gives \(Gm\le d\log L\), and summing gives the upper bound of (1).
The lower bound uses the [width estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) on the nonzero multiple \(P\), since its effective support is at most \(s\).
If some initial sums vanish, include the empty and total sums and subtract consecutive vanishing initial sums.
After monomial normalization each resulting block annihilates \(\alpha\) and has no vanishing proper initial sum.
No positive-width gap between two different blocks is charged to their individual widths.
In particular the block widths are mutually comparable in terms of \(s,L\), because \(d/m\) is the same on every block.

A vanishing cut makes both sides zero, whereas a nonzero monomial never vanishes at a unit and an integer binomial would force a positive power of the unit to be a rational unit, hence a root of unity.
When \(s\le5\), one side has at most two terms at every cut.
For \(s\le7\), one side has at most three terms, so a common root gives \(m\ge\log\theta\) by the [individual-factor trinomial gap](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement).
The six-term example in the statement has zero for the partial sum after its first three terms.
Its cubic is irreducible by the rational-root test, has unit constant term and has a real root greater than one.

Voutier's theorem gives \(m>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) for nontorsion algebraic numbers of degree \(d\ge2\), with \(m=d h(\alpha)\).
For \(d\ge16\) the displayed expression decreases in \(d\), and \(d\le D\).
For \(d<16\) the [quadratic bound for nontorsion units](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(m\ge1/\sqrt{40d}\).
Together these give \(m\gg\lambda(D)\) with an absolute constant, and (1) follows. [Voutier, main theorem](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa74/aa7417.pdf#page=1)

At an exterior conjugate the leading-term inequality gives \(b_+\log|x|\le\log L\), and at an interior one the reversed inequality gives \(-b_-\log|x|\le\log L\).
For reciprocal \(S\) its inverse conjugates allow the larger end gap on both sides.
The sum of the squared log moduli is at most \(2m\log L/b\), while the original-degree quadratic bound gives at least \(1/(10d)\).
Combining \(m\ge b/(20d\log L)\) with the upper bound of (1) proves (2).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Which conductors every genuine noncyclotomic factor must reach

Let \(n>1\) be odd and prime to three, take **any** integer \(0<l<n\), and put
\[
                    P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1 .
\]
Every noncyclotomic monic rational irreducible factor \(S\) has degree \(d\gg n\lambda(n)\), uniformly in \(l\), by (1) with \(s=5,\ L=7\); monicity and constant one make all its roots algebraic units.
Its nonempty set of represented binary orders consists of numbers \(3t\) with \(t\mid n\), by the [squarefree binary factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors), which is independent of \(l\).
Write \(t_*\) for the largest represented \(t\).
Then
\[
             t_*\gg\frac{n\lambda(n)}{\tau(n)},                         \tag{3}
\]
where \(\tau(n)\) counts the positive divisors of \(n\).
Indeed the degree is at most \(\sum_{t\mid n,\ t\le t_*}\varphi(3t)\le 2t_*\tau(n)\), allowing arbitrary partial Frobenius orbits within each conductor.
In particular \(t_*=n^{1-o(1)}\) as \(n\to\infty\), by the elementary divisor bound \(\tau(n)=n^{o(1)}\).
For that bound, at fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and all large primes \(p\) one has \(a+1\le2^a\le p^{\varepsilon a}\); at each of the finitely many other primes the supremum of \((a+1)p^{-\varepsilon a}\) is finite.

When \(n=5^A7^b\), the stronger simultaneous restriction is
\[
              \frac n{t_*}\ll\frac{(\log n)^3}{(\log\log n)^2}
                           \qquad(n\to\infty).                           \tag{4}
\]
Write \(y=n/t_*\).
Every represented \(t\) has complementary divisor \(5^i7^j=n/t\ge y\).
Consequently
\[
      \frac{d}{2n}\le\sum_{\substack{i,j\ge0\\5^i7^j\ge y}}5^{-i}7^{-j}
                       \ll \frac{1+\log y}{y}.
\]
For each \(i\le\log_5 y\) the sum over \(j\) is a geometric tail costing \(O(1/y)\) after multiplication by \(5^{-i}\); the remaining \(i\)'s together cost \(O(1/y)\).
Thus \(y/(1+\log y)\ll1/\lambda(n)\), whence \(y\ll\lambda(n)^{-1}\log(2/\lambda(n))\) by monotonicity for \(y\ge1\).
This gives (4).
It puts the exponents of both five and seven within \(O(\log\log n)\) of their maxima at one and the same represented level.

The whole squarefree reduction has period \(3n\), since order \(3n\) occurs and every order divides \(3n\).
In particular \(\bar S\mid X^{3n}-1\).
At this period the [binary period inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement) with one doubling gives
\[
                      m(S)\ge \frac{d\log2}{6n}.
\]
The gap inequality also gives \(m(S)\le 2(\log7)d/n\) by (1) at width \(2n\).
For these actual nontorsion factors a shrinking sequence would therefore require both \(d=o(n)\) and \(d\gg n\lambda(n)\).
Neither condition requires complete levels in the reduction.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Exact classification of the prescribed rational lift without offset restrictions

For \(A\ge1\), put \(b_a=\lfloor(A-a)\log_7 5\rfloor,\ n=5^A7^{b_0}\), and \(t_a=5^a7^{b_a}\) for even \(0\le a\le A\).
Let \(B_A=\prod_{a\ {\rm even}}\bar\Phi_{3t_a}\), with that same index range.
For all sufficiently large \(A\) and all \(0<l<n\), a monic rational divisor of \(P_l\) reduces to \(B_A\) **if and only if** \(A\) is odd and \(l=n/5\).
In that case the unique divisor is \(\prod_{a\ {\rm even}}\Phi_{6t_a}\), a product of cyclotomic polynomials.

Indeed the [conductor count](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#conductors) gives \(\deg B_A\ll A\sqrt n=o(n\lambda(n))\).
Monic reduction preserves the degree of any rational divisor of the monic parent, and (1) therefore prevents it from containing any noncyclotomic irreducible constituent, at every \(l\).
This also excludes all noncyclotomic rational divisors whose reductions are supported on any subset of this antichain.
Here the [previous all-admissible-offset exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v247.md#statement) used a two-scale estimate and trace congruences, and assumed \(k=n-l\) odd and coprime to \(n\).

For arbitrary \(l\), a cyclotomic factor must have order \(3t\) or \(6t\), \(t\mid n\), by the [order reduction at two](Yin_C_G0345_v243.md#lifts): the odd part must give a represented residual order and higher two-parts would repeat it.
For a primitive \(\zeta\) of order \(3t\), its \(n\)-th power has trace \(-1\), and \(P_l(\zeta)=0\) would require \(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}=0\), impossible at odd order.
For an order \(6t\), the \(n\)-th power has order six, so its trace is one and the equation requires \(\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}=1\).
This is equivalent to \(\zeta^l\) having order six, hence to \(\gcd(6t,l)=t\).
As \(t\mid n\) is prime to six, this holds exactly when \(t\mid l\) and \(\gcd(l,6)=1\).
Thus \(\Phi_{6t}\) supplies precisely the full residual level \(\bar\Phi_{3t}\) under these conditions, and all such factors occur simply by the squarefree reduction.

For large \(A\) the levels \(t_a\) are distinct, and their least common multiple uses the largest \(b_a\) at \(a=0\) and the largest even index.
If \(A\) is even its value is \(n\).
If \(A\) is odd its value is \(n/5\), with final index \(A-1\) having \(b_{A-1}=0\) since \(5<7\).
For even \(A\) the positivity constraint \(l<n\) precludes the common multiple.
For odd \(A\) only the first of \(n/5,2n/5,3n/5,4n/5\) is coprime to six.
The explicit cyclotomic product divides \(P_{n/5}\), proving existence as well as uniqueness in the asserted case.
It has measure zero.
