# Every strict terminal cut forces a nonreal exterior pair in the positive-residue-sum domain

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statement and exact scope

Let \(n\ge2\), \(p=2n+1\), and retain the integer trace polynomials
\[
E_{-1}=0,\quad E_0=1,\quad E_1=t,\quad E_{j+1}=tE_j-E_{j-1},
\qquad B_j=E_j+E_{j-1},\qquad b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p).
\]
Let \(C\in\mathbb Z[t]\) have degree \(n-1\) and leading coefficient two, and put
\[
Q=tB_n+C,\qquad P(z)=z^{n+1}Q(z+z^{-1}).
\tag{1}
\]
For any integer \(1\le\kappa<n\), assume
\[
r_j=\frac{Q(b_j)}{B_n'(b_j)}>0\quad(j\le n-\kappa),\qquad
r_j<0\quad(j>n-\kappa),\qquad
\frac{Q(-2)}{B_n(-2)}<0.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(P\) is monic reciprocal integral of degree \(2n+2\), and the following conclusions hold.

1. \(Q\) has no real root above \(b_1\), and every root in \((-2,b_n)\) is not the trace of a root of unity; at least one such root exists.
2. \(M(P)>1\), every cyclotomic factor of \(P\) has order \(3\le m<p\), and exactly two roots of \(P\) are exterior to the unit circle, counted with multiplicity.
   They are a nonreal conjugate pair, and there are exactly two interior roots and \(2n-2\) unit-circle roots.
3. If \(F\) is a monic reciprocal Salem polynomial with its sole exterior root positive and real, \(H\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials, \(k\ge1\), and \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), then \(P\ne H(z)F(\varepsilon z^k)\).
4. Suppose nonzero complex polynomials \(U,V\), all of whose roots lie on the unit circle, satisfy \(U(0)/V(0)=1\) and
   \[
   P-U/V=O(z^{L+1}),\qquad 2\le L\le n+1,
   \qquad H_0=\deg U+\deg V,\qquad q=\lfloor L/2\rfloor.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   1<M(P)\le(8q+H_0+3)^{2/q}.
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Consequently actual inputs with \(L\to\infty\) and \(\log(L+H_0)=o(L)\) would refute a uniform positive Mahler gap.

The integer existence of any such sequence remains open.
The signs and endpoint are simultaneous hypotheses, not conclusions of integrality or of the prefix.
In the original coordinates \(C=B_{n-1}h\bmod B_n\), \(h=\sum a_iE_i\), the leading-coefficient condition in (1) is exactly \(a_0=2\).

<a id="boundary-pivot"></a>
## Upper-root exclusion at an arbitrary sign boundary

The monic polynomials \(Q\) and \(B_n\) have degrees \(n+1\) and \(n\), respectively, and their partial fractions give
\[
\frac{Q(t)}{B_n(t)}=t+\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{r_j}{t-b_j},
\qquad \sum_jr_j=2.
\tag{4}
\]
The last identity follows from the coefficient of \(t^{-1}\), since \(C\) has leading coefficient two.
Put \(s=n-\kappa\) and \(c=b_s\).
For \(t>b_1>0\),
\[
\frac{Q(t)}{B_n(t)}
=t+\frac2{t-c}
 +\sum_{j=1}^n r_j
 \left(\frac1{t-b_j}-\frac1{t-c}\right)>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(b\mapsto(t-b)^{-1}\) is increasing on the pole interval.
For \(j\le s\) the bracket is nonnegative and \(r_j>0\); for \(j>s\) the bracket is negative and \(r_j<0\).
Both initial terms in (5) are positive, and \(B_n\) has no zero in this range.
This argument does not depend on the size of either sign block.

<a id="nontorsion-endpoint"></a>
## Endpoint, conjugacy and strict positivity

On \((-2,b_n)\), the ratio in (4) is continuous, is negative at \(-2\), and tends to positive infinity at \(b_n\) from the left because \(r_n<0\).
It therefore has a zero in that interval.
If any such zero were \(2\cos(2\pi a/m)\), with \(0<a<m/2\) and \(\gcd(a,m)=1\), then its position and \(b_n=-2\cos(\pi/p)\) would imply
\[
0<m-2a<m/p,
\]
so \(m>p\).
Its primitive lift is a root of the monic integer \(P\); cyclotomic irreducibility forces \(\Phi_m\mid P\), and hence also the trace root \(2\cos(2\pi/m)>b_1\).
This contradicts (5).
Thus every endpoint-interval root is nontorsion.
If \(M(P)=1\), the accepted integral measure-one characterization would make every root of the monic constant-one polynomial \(P\) a root of unity, a contradiction.

The same upper-root argument excludes cyclotomic orders \(m>p\) and order one.
The endpoint excludes order two, and \(Q(b_1)\ne0\) excludes order \(p\).
Therefore every actual cyclotomic factor has \(3\le m<p\), including when \(p\) is composite.

<a id="nonreal-pair"></a>
## Complete root count and exclusion of real residual roots

The \(n-2\) intervals between consecutive same-sign poles each contain a trace root.
Together with a root in \((-2,b_n)\), select one root in each of these \(n-1\) disjoint intervals, and remove exactly one copy from \(Q\).
The remaining monic factor is a real quadratic with roots \(v_1,v_2\), retaining multiplicity.
All selected roots lift to unit-circle pairs.

If the residual roots are real, (5) puts both below \(b_1<2\).
Let \(A_0\) be the product of the selected monic linear factors.
Then \(A_0(-2)\) has sign \((-1)^{n-1}\), while \(B_n(-2)=(-1)^n\), so the strict endpoint gives
\[
(-2-v_1)(-2-v_2)>0.
\]
Both roots are below \(-2\), or both are above \(-2\).
In the second case every trace root lies in \((-2,2)\), giving \(M(P)=1\), already excluded.

Match each selected root to the right endpoint pole of its interval.
The matched poles are distinct and omit precisely \(b_s\), where \(s=n-\kappa\); this is the [v9 disjoint matching](Yin_C_G0881_v9.md#interval-root-accounting).
Since \(\deg C=n-1\), the sum of all trace roots of \(Q\) equals the sum of all poles of \(B_n\).
Every matched root \(u\) is strictly less than its pole \(b\), hence
\[
v_1+v_2=b_s+\sum_{(u,b)}(b-u)>b_s>-2.
\tag{6}
\]
This contradicts two roots below \(-2\), whose sum would be below \(-4\).
The residual roots are therefore nonreal conjugates.
Each reciprocal quadratic \(z^2-v_i z+1\) has one interior and one exterior root and no unit-circle root, since a unit-circle lift has real trace.
Their exterior roots are conjugate, so \(M(P)=\rho^2>1\) for their common modulus \(\rho\).
This proves all multiplicity and complete-measure assertions.

<a id="seed-and-prefix"></a>
## Salem sources and variable neutral prefixes

For a proposed product \(H(z)F(\varepsilon z^k)\), the number of exterior roots is exactly \(k\); cyclotomic multiplication adds none.
Thus the count above forces \(k=2\).
If \(\varepsilon=1\), the positive exterior root \(\sqrt\lambda\) gives a trace above two, contradicting (5).
If \(\varepsilon=-1\), take an endpoint-interval root \(u\) and a unit-circle lift \(\zeta\).
Its nontorsion property excludes \(H(\zeta)=0\), so \(F(-\zeta^2)=0\).
The same factor vanishes at \(-\zeta\), whose trace is
\[
-u>-b_n=2\cos(\pi/p)>b_1,
\]
again contradicting (5).
This uses the actual polynomial product, without cancellation or factor extraction.

The hypotheses of [v9](Yin_C_G0881_v9.md#statement) hold at the given arbitrary cut, with its shift parameter zero.
Its upper bound and the strict positivity just proved give (3).
The logarithmic upper bound tends to zero under the stated growth condition, but no integer input is obtained by that implication alone.
