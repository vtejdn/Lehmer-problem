# Large rational primes in the complementary resultant of a small factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound on the discriminant at every prime

Let \(P(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) be the [two-pole quadratic section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement), let \(k,l\) be positive and coprime and put \(N=3+k+l\).
Outside finitely many primitive directions, if \(P\) is squarefree then at every rational prime \(p\)
\[
                      v_p(\operatorname{disc}P)\ll_F p^{16}(\log N)^3.    \tag{1}
\]
The bound includes primes dividing the discriminant or leading coefficient of the [critical octic](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement).
For a monic integral factorization \(P=ST\) it gives
\[
       \sum_{p\le Y}v_p(\operatorname{Res}(S,T))\log p
                      \ll_F Y^{17}\log(2Y)(\log N)^3,\qquad Y\ge2.      \tag{2}
\]

Suppose \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\), and choose any irreducible factor \(S\) with \(d=\deg S=o(l)\) and \(T=P/S\).
For every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1/17\),
\[
    \sum_{p>l^{1/17-\varepsilon}}
        v_p(\operatorname{Res}(S,T))\log p
       \ \ge\ (1-o(1))d\log(l/d).                                       \tag{3}
\]
The primes at or below the cutoff contribute \(o(d\log(l/d))\), uniformly along such sequences.
In particular the largest rational prime divisor of this resultant is at least \(l^{1/17-o(1)}\).
These assertions have no exceptional sequence of primitive slopes in the stated limit.
For a finite set of primes, the contribution is \(O_{F,\mathcal P}((\log N)^3)\) even at the exceptional residue classes of the [two local slope tests](Yin_C_G0345_v815.md#statement).

<a id="projections"></a>
## Integral identities for seventeen projections

Use an indeterminate \(s\) and the Laurent coordinate algebra
\[
  \mathcal A=\mathbb Q(s)[U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}]/(F,H_s),
       \qquad H_s=U F_U+sV F_V.
\]
It is finite and reduced of dimension sixteen by the [generic critical intersection](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#critical).
For each integer \(j=0,\ldots,16\) take the characteristic polynomial \(\Phi_j(T)\) of multiplication by \(U+jV\) on \(\mathcal A\).
Its roots with multiplicity are precisely the sixteen values \(u_c+jv_c\) over the critical points.
Cayley–Hamilton gives an identity
\[
                  \Phi_j(U+jV)=A_j F+C_j H_s
\]
in the Laurent ring, with \(A_j,C_j\) Laurent polynomials over \(\mathbb Q(s)\).
After specializing \(s=l/k\), the projection polynomial is still the product over the sixteen specialized points outside finitely many directions.
Indeed a basis of the generic algebra and its multiplication tables extend after clearing finitely many denominators to a free algebra over an open subset of the slope line.
Equivalently the [explicit étale octic and its quadratic algebra](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#elimination) furnish a basis there.
Removing finitely many rational slopes excludes just finitely many primitive positive directions.

There are nonzero integers \(a_j=a_j(k,l)\) of size at most \(N^{O_F(1)}\) such that the specialized identity reads
\[
 a_j\prod_c(U+jV-u_c-jv_c)
            = A_{j,k,l}F+C_{j,k,l}(kU F_U+lV F_V),                      \tag{4}
\]
where both multipliers are integral Laurent polynomials.
In fact denominators arise only from fixed rational functions of \(s\) and the extra factor \(1/k\) in \(H_s\).
At \(s=l/k\), their denominators divide products of fixed homogeneous integer polynomial values in \(k,l\) and fixed powers of \(k\), after removal of their zero slopes.
Enlarging \(a_j\) by these products proves the asserted polynomial bound.

<a id="distance"></a>
## Approximating a critical point at an arbitrary prime

Fix the valuation on \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) normalized by \(v_p(p)=1\).
Every root \(z\) of \(P\) is a unit there because \(P\) is monic with unit constant term.
Set \(U=z^k,V=z^l\) and \(e=v_p(zP'(z))\).
With \(A_0=\max_j\log|a_j|/\log p\), equation (4) gives for each \(j\) a critical point \(c_j\) satisfying
\[
                      v_p(U+jV-u_{c_j}-jv_{c_j})\ge(e-A_0)/16.
\]
This follows from the valuation of the product even if some factors have negative valuation or one is zero.
The same point occurs for two distinct indices since there are seventeen indices and sixteen points.
Subtract the two linear forms and divide by their nonzero index difference of absolute value at most sixteen.
It follows that both \(v_p(V-v_c)\) and \(v_p(U-u_c)\) are at least
\[
                           \eta=(e-A_0)/16-\log(16)/\log p .             \tag{5}
\]
If \(\eta>0\), both coordinates of this critical point are \(p\)-adic units.
Powers of congruent units retain at least their original precision, so \(V^k=U^l\) implies
\[
                             v_p(v_c^k u_c^{-l}-1)\ge\eta .
\]
The expression is nonzero: a critical point on the primitive subtorus \(V^k=U^l\) would give a repeated root in characteristic zero, as in the [subtorus coordinates](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local).
When \(\eta\le0\), (5) already bounds \(e\) by \(O_F(\log N/\log p)\).

<a id="logarithms"></a>
## Two algebraic logarithms with moving inputs

Each field \(K=\mathbb Q(u_c,v_c)\) has degree at most sixteen and both absolute logarithmic heights are \(O_F(\log N)\).
Indeed the octic has integral coefficients of size \(O_F(N^2)\) and nonzero integral leading coefficient, so every root has height \(O_F(\log N)\) by the polynomial Mahler bound.
The formulas adjoining \(t^2=Z^2-4\) and reconstructing both critical coordinates are rational operations of bounded degree in these numbers and in \(k,l\), with nonzero denominators.
The usual height inequalities give the same bound for the coordinates.

Apply the [explicit consequence of Yu's Theorem 1](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-input-logarithmic-bound) with two local units \(v_c,u_c\) and exponents \(k,-l\).
For odd \(p\), if \(p^f\equiv3\bmod4\) the field condition holds with \(f\) the original residue degree.
Otherwise the residue field contains a square root of \(-1\), and Hensel lifting puts \(i\) in the local field.
Adjoining \(i\) globally and choosing this place preserves the completion and its residue degree \(f\le16\).
At two one may adjoin a third root of unity of order three, doubling the global degree at most, with the increase in \(2^f\) absorbed into an absolute constant.
All global degrees in the estimate are at most 32.
In the explicit bound the product of the two factors \(\max(h(\alpha),\log p)\) divided by \((f\log p)^2\) is \(O_F((\log N)^2)\), and the exponent parameter costs \(O(\log N)\).
After passing from the integral prime-ideal valuation to the normalized valuation we obtain
\[
           v_p(v_c^ku_c^{-l}-1)\ll_F p^{16}(\log N)^3.                  \tag{6}
\]
This uses the nonvanishing just proved and requires no multiplicative independence.

Combining (5) and (6) gives the same upper bound for \(v_p(P'(z))\) at each root.
Only roots lying in nonsingleton residue classes contribute to the discriminant valuation, because \(P'(z)\) is the product of its differences from the other roots.
At most 32 roots lie in those classes by the [uniform modular multiplicity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#ramification).
Summing proves (1), including the primes at which the critical scheme has bad reduction.

<a id="factor"></a>
## Contribution required at the original factor degree

The square of the complementary resultant divides \(\operatorname{disc}P\), so (1) summed over primes gives (2), using at most \(Y\) terms.
On the steep sequences the [individual degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives \(d=l^{1+o(1)}\), while \(N\asymp l\).
Hence at \(Y=l^{1/17-\varepsilon}\) the right side of (2) is \(O_F(l^{1-17\varepsilon}(\log l)^4)=o(d)\).
For \(d=o(l)\), the [derivative average and discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#resultant) give \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(S,T)|\ge d\log((e^L-o(1))l/d)\) with fixed \(L\).
Since \(\log(l/d)\to\infty\), subtracting the small-prime part proves (3).
