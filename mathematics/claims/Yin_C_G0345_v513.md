# Many distinct large prime divisors in a single row ideal

<a id="statement"></a>
## Removing prime powers after the rows are chosen

For each integer \(L\ge2\) let \(S\) consist of all primes in \([L,2L]\), \(r=|S|\), \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\), and put
\[
 \mathcal N=\{p+q:p,q\in S,\ p<q\},\qquad W=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\varphi(n).
\]
The [dyadic totient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#statement) gives \(W\asymp L^2\) and \(W/\Sigma\asymp\log L\).
There is a computable integer \(j=j(L)\to\infty\) such that the following holds at width \(b=rj\) for all sufficiently large \(L\).
Let \(U,V\) pass the [common test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) on \(S\), and suppose one reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P\) occurs with multiplicity at least \(k\ge2\) in every graph.
Write \(e=\deg P,\ h=\log M(P),\ H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), and let \(B>0\) generate \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\).

For \(n\in\mathcal N\) let \(D_n\) be the part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_n)|\) prime to \(n\).
For any finite set \(\mathcal A\) of rational primes, let \(\mathcal W_{\mathcal A}\) be the set of primes outside \(\mathcal A\) dividing at least one \(D_n\), and let \(B_{\mathcal A}\) be the part of \(B\) supported on \(\mathcal W_{\mathcal A}\), with full multiplicities.
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
        |\mathcal W_{\mathcal A}|&\ge {W\over 4Le}-|\mathcal A|,\\
        e\log B_{\mathcal A}&\ge
                 k(1-1/j) h\,\big(W-4Le|\mathcal A|\big).               \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
Every \(\ell\in\mathcal W_{\mathcal A}\) gives a nonzero common root of \(U,V\) of exact multiplicative order \(n\in\mathcal N\) and residue degree at most \(e\).
For any representation \(n=p+q\) in the definition of \(\mathcal N\), the two powered minima attached to \(p,q\) and the same two rows have such a common root at \(\ell\) when \(\ell\mid D_n\).

In particular one may first remove all primes at most \(L\sqrt{\log L}\), including the entire set \(S\), and then remove **any** further \(\lfloor W/(16Le)\rfloor\) prime divisors chosen after inspecting \(P,U,V\) and their valuations.
Among the remaining witnesses there are \(\gg L/e\) distinct rational primes on this single pair of rows, each giving a root of order between \(2L\) and \(4L\).
Uniformly over these rows and deletions, \(\log B_{\mathcal A}/\Sigma\) and \(\log B_{\mathcal A}/\log H\) tend to infinity.
For this prescribed sequence of full intervals and widths the existence of same-seed rows with multiplicity at least two at every exponent and \(\log H/\Sigma\to0\) is equivalent to vanishing of the unrestricted Lehmer infimum.
The seed may vary with \(L\).

<a id="layers"></a>
## Accumulating distinct orders in one integer

For a moment take any fixed \(L\) with \(S\) odd and any such common seed and rows.
In every \(n=p+q\) period both \(U(X^p)\) and \(V(X^p)\) belong to the ideal generated by \(P(X)^k\), by the [integral period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement).
For each rational prime \(\ell\), consider all \(n\in\mathcal N\) prime to \(\ell\).
Their reduced cyclotomic polynomials are squarefree and have disjoint root sets, because their roots have the indicated exact orders.
Factor each such cyclotomic polynomial over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) by lifting its distinct monic residual irreducible factors.
For a lifted root \(\xi_i\) in its unramified field let \(f_i\) be the residue degree and \(t_i=v_\ell(P(\xi_i))\), with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Keep the components with \(t_i>0\).
Their distinct residual irreducibles all divide the same \(P\bmod\ell\), including when that polynomial has multiple roots.
Consequently \(\sum_i f_i\le e\), across *all* the periods at this prime.

In the component belonging to \(n=p+q\), evaluating an integral Bézout identity for \(B\) at \(Y=\xi_i^p\) shows that \(B\) is a multiple of \(P(\xi_i)^k\) in its valuation ring.
Thus \(v_\ell(B)\ge kt_i\) on every component.
The norm calculation in the [unramified components](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#exponent) gives
\[
      e\,v_\ell(B)\ge k\sum_i f_i t_i
                 =k\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}v_\ell(D_n).                    \tag{2}
\]
This comparison uses a single integer \(B\) across the different periods, without requiring a map from one period ring onto the others.
In particular \(\ell\) divides at most \(e\) of the integers \(D_n\).
Each occurrence gives the common root \(\bar\xi_i^p\) of exact order \(n\), since \(\gcd(p,n)=1\).
The \(p\)-powered minimum contains the powers of the reduced roots of \(P\), and the \(q\)-powered minimum contains \((\bar\xi_i^{-1})^q=\bar\xi_i^p\) by reciprocity.
The [tangency equations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) preserve the powered degrees.

<a id="width"></a>
## A degree range uniform over the whole interval

For fixed \(P\) the [primitive cyclotomic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) gives \(\log D_n=(1+o_P(1))\varphi(n)h\) as \(n\to\infty\).
This applies to the full range \(2L\le n\le4L\), before any restriction on residue characteristics.
Let \(m_L=\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor\).
For each integer \(j\ge2\) with \(j^4\le m_L\), use the [finite seed list](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width) of all reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P\) of degree \(e\le j\) and \(M(P)\le2\), with the coefficient bound \(2\binom{e}{i}\) at index \(i\).
Require
\[
        M(P)^{m_L}\ge2^{ej},\qquad
        D_n^j\ge M(P)^{(j-1)\varphi(n)}\quad(n\in\mathcal N)
\]
for every seed on the list.
All tests use integers and real algebraic numbers and are decidable.
Choose the largest passing \(j\) or \(j=1\) if none passes.
Each fixed \(j\) eventually passes, by positivity of the finitely many \(h\) and the fixed-seed estimate.
Thus \(j(L)\to\infty\).
For every actual common seed the [weighted degree and height bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) give \(re\le b\) and \(\Sigma h\le\log H\le\Sigma\log2\).
So \(e\le j,\ M(P)\le2\), and every \(D_n>1\) with \(\log D_n\ge(1-1/j)\varphi(n)h\).

<a id="remove"></a>
## The number of characteristics and the remaining mass

As every \(D_n>1\) and a prime covers at most \(e\) distinct \(n\), the entire support has at least \(|\mathcal N|/e\ge W/(4Le)\) primes.
Deleting \(\mathcal A\) gives the count in (1).
At most \(e|\mathcal A|\) orders have any divisor of \(D_n\) in \(\mathcal A\), and their total totient is at most \(4Le|\mathcal A|\).
Apply (2) at each remaining characteristic to the product over the unaffected orders.
The second inequality in (1) follows, even if the deleted set depends on the rows.

By the [prime counting estimate at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz), all primes up to \(L\sqrt{\log L}\) together number \(O(L/\sqrt{\log L})=o(W/(Le))\) since \(e\le j\le m_L^{1/4}\).
With the additional deletions in the statement the total deleted totient is at most \((1/4+o(1))W\), and the remaining count is \(\gg W/(Le)\).
At each actual seed \(h\log L/e\gg j\) by the first width test.
Combining (1) with \(W/\Sigma\asymp\log L\) gives \(\log B_{\mathcal A}/\Sigma\gg kj\), uniformly.
The inequality \(\log H\le\Sigma\log2\) gives the other divergence.

Finally, if the unrestricted infimum is zero, take reciprocal nontorsion seeds of arbitrarily small positive \(h\) using the [reciprocal reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
For each fixed such seed and all sufficiently large \(L\), the [common Hermite interpolation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) gives rows with this same repeated seed across all of \(S\), width at most \(2er+2\) and \(\log H/\Sigma\le 2h+o_P(1)\) by the [uniform interpolation cost](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap).
These widths fit as \(j\to\infty\), and for small \(h\) the rows meet the cutoff.
Taking successively smaller \(h\) proves existence along the full sequence of intervals.
Conversely the lower bound \(\Sigma h\le\log H\) at every such row forces the unrestricted infimum to vanish on a vanishing-cost sequence.
This establishes the equivalence, while leaving open whether these low-cost rows exist.

The coordinating research agent for Sheng Yin derived the simultaneous prime count and the lower bound after arbitrary deletions by combining the disjoint residual orders with the totient sum over the interval.
