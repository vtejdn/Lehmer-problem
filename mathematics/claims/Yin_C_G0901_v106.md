# Bounded measures over splitting fields of unrestricted relative degrees

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact reduction to the block permutation fields

Fix \(M,N>0\), a possibly infinite normal algebraic extension \(K/\mathbb Q\), and a possibly infinite family of pairwise unmatched nonzero algebraic numbers \(x_i\) of total logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m(x_i)\le M\).
Matching means that nonzero powers of two conjugates agree up to torsion.
Let \(L_i\) be the rational splitting field of \(x_i\), and \(d_i\) the degree of the product over the torsion class of \(x_i\).
Assume each \(d_i\) exceeds a threshold depending only on \(M,N\).
In its [canonical label action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement), write \(P_i\) for the label splitting field, \(G_i=\operatorname{Gal}(P_i/\mathbb Q)\), \(u_i\) for the number of blocks, \(b_i\) for their size, \(D_i=(A_{b_i})^{u_i}\) for the independently supported alternating groups, and \(B_i\subseteq P_i\) for the normal field of the permutation action on the \(u_i\) blocks.
All composita are taken in one algebraic closure.
Put
\[
             A=K\prod_i B_i,\qquad C=K\prod_i L_i,\qquad
             I=\{i:P_i\not\subseteq A\}.
\]
For a normal base \(U/\mathbb Q\) write \(U^{\rm sol}\) for the compositum of its finite solvable Galois extensions.
For every algebraic \(y\) with \(0<m(y)\le N\),
\[
  y\in C^{\rm sol}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  y\in A^{\rm sol}\ \ \text{or}\ \ y\text{ matches some }x_i,\ i\in I.       \tag{1}
\]
These alternatives are disjoint, and a match is unique.
For \(i\in I\) let \(k_i\) be the saturation index of the compressed \(x_i\) on its multiplicative line in its own root field, modulo torsion.
Writing \(\Sigma_N(U)\) for the positive total measures at most \(N\) of elements of \(U^{\rm sol}\), one has
\[
       \Sigma_N(C)=\Sigma_N(A)\ \cup\
       \bigcup_{i\in I}\left((0,N]\cap\frac{m(x_i)}{k_i}\mathbb Z_{>0}\right).
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
Overlaps of numerical progressions are allowed.
There is no comparison between the smallest \(b_i\) and the largest \(u_i\), and there need not be any bound on the block counts.
The base \(A\) accounts for inputs for which larger or unrelated fields have already supplied the label field.
This extends the [compositum theorem under a separation of block sizes and counts](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#statement) to arbitrary relative degrees and an external normal field.

There is also an elementwise degree obstruction.
For any unmatched \(y\) of sufficiently large compressed degree in terms of \(M,N\), with \(0<m(y)\le N\), let \(P_y,D_y,b_y\) be its corresponding objects.
If \(P_y\subseteq A\), a solvable extension of \(A\) suffices to reach \(y\).
Otherwise the restriction group on \(P_y\) over \(C\) contains \(D_y\), even after arbitrary towers of solvable steps and single adjunctions of degree at most \(b_y-1\).
The first sufficient bound on the degrees of the single adjunctions, allowing solvable steps, is exactly \(b_y\).
The cases below give degree and signature conditions and an infinite external field for which this obstruction applies.

<a id="independence"></a>
## The alternating factors that remain over all block fields

Set \(P=K\prod_i P_i\), \(Z=K\prod_i P_i^{D_i}\), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(P/\mathbb Q)\), and \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(P/Z)\).
The kernel of each block action modulo \(D_i\) embeds in \((C_2)^{u_i}\) by the individual parities, so \(Z/A\) is abelian, also for an infinite family.
The projection of \(H\) to \(G_i\) lies in \(D_i\) and is normal in \(G_i\).
The unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of \(G_i\) is \(D_i\), whose centralizer in \(G_i\) is trivial. [Canonical alternating factors](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition)
Indeed the centralizer is normal, and it cannot contain the nonabelian centerless \(D_i\).
Thus the projection is trivial or all of \(D_i\).
It is trivial precisely for \(P_i\subseteq Z\), which is equivalent to \(P_i\subseteq A\).
For the nontrivial implication the normal subgroup \(\operatorname{Gal}(A P_i/A)\) of \(G_i\) would be abelian, since \(Z/A\) is abelian, and hence trivial.

We claim that restriction gives a topological isomorphism
\[
                       H \ \simeq\ \prod_{i\in I}D_i .                      \tag{3}
\]
For two surviving coordinates the image \(H_{ij}\) is a subdirect subgroup of \(D_i\times D_j\), normal under the projection of \(G\).
Its kernel on the second coordinate, viewed in \(D_i\), is normal in \(G_i\) by surjectivity of \(G\to G_i\).
This kernel is trivial or all of \(D_i\), and the latter gives the full pair product.
If the pair product is proper, both kernels are therefore trivial, so \(H_{ij}\) is the graph of an isomorphism of the two alternating products.
For \(g\in G\) fixing \(P_i\), the commutator of its two-coordinate image with any member of \(H_{ij}\) lies in \(H_{ij}\) and has trivial \(i\)-coordinate.
Graph injectivity makes that commutator trivial, forcing the \(j\)-coordinate of \(g\) to centralize \(D_j\) and hence to be trivial.
By symmetry the kernels on \(P_i\) and \(P_j\) coincide, giving \(P_i=P_j\).
The [identification of labels and matched roots in equal label fields](Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#letters) then forces \(x_i,x_j\) to match, contrary to hypothesis.
Here the solvable-kernel hypothesis of the two-field comparison holds by [solvability over the common label field](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal).
The height and degree thresholds apply to both compressed inputs.

Consequently every pair of individual simple factors has full pair projection, including two factors of the same \(D_i\).
On any finite set of simple factors this forces the whole direct product.
Induct on their number, projecting off the last one.
A trivial kernel in the last simple group would make the subgroup the graph of a surjection from the preceding product onto this group.
Exactly one factor could have nontrivial image, since its factor images are commuting normal subgroups of a nonabelian simple group.
That would contradict the corresponding full pair projection, whereas a nontrivial kernel gives the entire last factor.
For infinitely many factors, surjectivity on all finite projections gives density and compactness gives closedness.
Restriction is injective because the coordinates together with \(K\subseteq Z\) generate \(P\).
This proves (3), allowing inputs whose label field already lies in \(A\).

<a id="output"></a>
## Detection of an arbitrary bounded-measure element

For \(y\) let \(T_y\) be the fixed field of the largest solvable normal subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_y/\mathbb Q)\).
Its Galois group has trivial solvable radical.
The [kernels from actual roots to compressed labels](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal) are solvable, so \(T_y\subseteq P_y\) and \([T_y:\mathbb Q]\le d_y!\) in all degrees, where \(d_y\) is the compressed degree.
For \(d_y\) sufficiently large in terms of \(N\) the [radical-free label action](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#statement) gives \(T_y=P_y\).
For every normal \(U/\mathbb Q\) one has \(y\in U^{\rm sol}\) exactly when \(T_y\subseteq U\).
Indeed \(U^{\rm sol}\) is rationally normal, and the restrictions to \(T_y\) of its Galois group over \(U\), when \(y\in U^{\rm sol}\), give a solvable normal subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(T_y/\mathbb Q)\).
The converse uses that \(L_y/T_y\) is solvable.
The group of \(C/P\) embeds as a closed subgroup of the product of the solvable input kernels, so it is prosolvable.
The group of \(C^{\rm sol}/P\) is also prosolvable, as an extension of two prosolvable groups.
Thus \(y\in C^{\rm sol}\) implies \(T_y\subseteq P\).

Restrict \(G\) to \(\operatorname{Gal}(T_y/\mathbb Q)\).
If every supported \(D_i\) in (3) has trivial image, then \(H\) has trivial image by continuity.
This gives \(T_y\subseteq Z\) and then \(T_y\subseteq A\) because \(Z/A\) is abelian and \(T_y\) has radical-free group.
Choose a common threshold \(d_0\) for the output block theorem and the two-field comparison at the bound \(\max(M,N)\).
Require each \(b_i>d_0\), which holds uniformly for large enough \(d_i\) by [the block-size bound](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block).
For \(d_y\le d_0\), every \(D_i\) has trivial image because each simple factor has order \(b_i!/2>d_y!\).
This includes small outputs without a canonical large alternating action.

Suppose instead some supported \(D_i\) survives in the quotient.
Then \(d_y>d_0\), and the support subgroup \(D_i\) is minimal normal in \(G\) because its simple factors are permuted transitively under conjugation.
Its restriction to \(P_y\) maps it isomorphically onto the unique minimal normal subgroup \(D_y\) of \(G_y=\operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q)\).
The kernel on \(P_i\) centralizes the supported \(D_i\) in \(H\), so it fixes \(P_y\) since \(D_y\) has trivial centralizer in \(G_y\).
The resulting surjection \(G_i\to G_y\) is faithful on \(D_i\), and any nontrivial normal kernel in \(G_i\) would contain \(D_i\).
It follows that \(P_y=P_i\), and the same two-field comparison gives matching.
Conversely a match gives equality of the compressed root fields at matched conjugates and of their label fields, since a stabilizer fixing either actual compressed root fixes the other's class and hence that root. [Matched-field argument](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#quotients)
It makes \(y\) reachable by radicals over \(L_i\), and for \(i\in I\) it excludes \(T_y=P_i\) from \(A\).
Matching is transitive on the Galois orbits of the multiplicative lines, giving uniqueness.
This proves (1), and [saturation in the matched root field](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#progressions) gives (2), including the realization of every listed multiple.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## The first sufficient adjunction degree for an unmatched target

Suppose \(d_y\) is sufficiently large at the common measure bound and \(y\) is unmatched to every input.
The case \(P_y\subseteq A\) follows from \(L_y/P_y\) solvable.
Otherwise \(\operatorname{Gal}(A P_y/A)\) restricts to a nontrivial normal subgroup of \(G_y\) and contains \(D_y\).
As \(D_y\) fixes \(B_y\), adjoining \(B_y\) to \(A\) still leaves this subgroup and \(P_y\not\subseteq A B_y\).
Apply (3) to the family enlarged by \(y\), using the common ceiling \(\max(M,N)\) for its inputs and choosing the original thresholds accordingly.
In that proof (3) itself only uses the two-input threshold at this common ceiling.
With \(Z'=ZP_y^{D_y}\) and \(U=Z'\prod_iP_i\), it gives
\[
                       \operatorname{Gal}(U P_y/U)=D_y
\]
under restriction, since the supported target factor fixes every original coordinate.
The group of \(UC/U\) is prosolvable.
Normality and the fact that each nontrivial quotient of \(D_y\) retains a simple factor \(A_{b_y}\) give \(\operatorname{Gal}(U C P_y/U C)=D_y\).

Let \(C_*\) be the field of all the indicated finite towers over \(C\) with single adjunction degree at most \(b_y-1\), allowing arbitrary solvable steps at any stage.
It is normal over \(\mathbb Q\) because the starting field is, and composita of towers are contained in towers.
Every finite Galois subextension of \(U C_*/U C\) has no section isomorphic to \(A_{b_y}\).
Indeed the Galois closure of a single degree-bounded step embeds into \(S_{b_y-1}\), whose order is too small, and solvable steps have only solvable sections.
This absence of a simple *section* is preserved by subgroups, quotients, extensions and finite products, so it persists upon taking normal closures up a tower and after base change. [Tower argument](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#independence)
The intersection with \(U C P_y\) over \(U C\) is therefore trivial, since it would give a quotient of \(D_y\).
Restriction over \(C_*\) still contains \(D_y\), and normality of \(C_*\) excludes \(y\) itself.
Conversely the block stabilizer chain fixes the \(u_y<b_y\) blocks one at a time and then each block's labels, with indices at most \(b_y\), reaching \(P_y\) from \(\mathbb Q\).
A solvable extension reaches the actual \(y\).
Base change to \(C\) proves exactness. [Degree of tower steps](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#degree)

<a id="factors"></a>
## Which block groups can supply a target of smaller degree

For \(K=\mathbb Q\), a large bounded-measure unmatched \(y\) in \(C^{\rm sol}\) forces \(A_{b_y}\) to be a Jordan–Hölder composition factor of at least one of the permutation groups \(\operatorname{Gal}(B_i/\mathbb Q)\).
Indeed \(P_y\subseteq A=\prod_iB_i\) by (1) and the radical-free test.
The finite normal field \(P_y\) is contained in a finite subcompositum, and its Galois group has composition factor \(A_{b_y}\).
For a finite subdirect product, every composition factor is a composition factor of a projected group.
To see this, project away the last coordinate.
The kernel, viewed in the last group, is normal there by surjectivity onto that coordinate.
The assertion follows by induction and the composition series for a normal subgroup and quotient.
Thus the named simple group occurs as an actual composition factor of a block group.
In particular \(u_i\ge b_y\) for some \(i\), by \(b_y!/2>(b_y-1)!\).
Using \(u_i\ll_M(\log(3d_i))^{212}\) and \(b_y\gg_N d_y/(\log(3d_y))^{212}\) gives the degree requirement
\[
             \log(3d_i)\gg_M b_y^{1/212}
                 \gg_{M,N}\frac{d_y^{1/212}}{\log(3d_y)} .
\]
If each block permutation group is \(A_{u_i}\) or \(S_{u_i}\), the condition even forces \(u_i=b_y\) for some \(i\), by simplicity and the distinct orders in the large alternating degrees.

For an external field depending on the target, fix \(b=b_y\) and take \(K_b\) to be the compositum of **all** finite normal extensions of \(\mathbb Q\) whose Galois group has no composition factor \(A_b\).
This includes all splitting fields with group \(A_m\) or \(S_m\) for \(m\ge5\), \(m\ne b\), with no bound on \(m\).
If none of the given block permutation groups has composition factor \(A_b\), then every finite normal subextension of \(K_b\prod_i B_i\) is a quotient of a finite subdirect product of groups without that composition factor.
Hence \(P_y\) is outside this compositum, and the full obstruction and exact threshold above apply with external base \(K_b\).
In particular the fields with \(m>b\) may themselves have \(A_b\) as a subgroup.
The composition-factor argument applies to these rational normal fields before isolating \(D_y\).
The later bounded-degree towers use the stronger section condition on their own steps over the enlarged base.

<a id="real"></a>
## A signature condition for fields of arbitrarily many reciprocal inputs

Assume now that the compressed inputs are reciprocal units and that each has fewer than \(b_i\) nonreal conjugate *pairs of exterior roots*.
On inverse-pair labels ordinary complex conjugation fixes all real or circle labels and makes one transposition for each such nonreal exterior pair.
Moving a block to a different block under this involution would require \(b_i\) transpositions between their labels.
Thus complex conjugation fixes all blocks, is trivial on \(B_i\), and normality makes \(B_i\) totally real.
In particular the condition holds for an input with just one nonreal exterior pair.

Set \(K=\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\), the field of all totally real algebraic numbers in the chosen algebraic closure.
Then \(A=K\) for this family.
For every bounded-measure target of sufficiently large compressed degree, (1) now says that membership in \(C^{\rm sol}\) is equivalent to its label field being totally real or to matching an input.
For a reciprocal compressed target the first test is precisely total reality of its trace field since \(P_y\) is the rational normal closure of that field. [Label field of a reciprocal unit](Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#statement)
An unmatched target with nonreal label field still requires degree \(b_y\) in the tower sense even after adjoining the entire family, with arbitrarily many input degrees on either side of its own.
This removes the ordering by target degree in the [totally real base comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v91.md#statement) for families satisfying the stated signature condition.
For smaller targets the exact alternative (1) continues to use \(T_y\subseteq\mathbb Q^{\rm tr}\) for the solvable contribution.
