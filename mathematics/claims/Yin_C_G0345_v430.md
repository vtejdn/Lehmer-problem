# Genuine double contacts for powers separated by their exterior moduli

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact simultaneous cost without separating the powers by torsion phases

Let \(\delta>1\) be a Salem number, or a real quadratic reciprocal unit greater than one, with \(h=\log\delta\).
Fix distinct positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\) and put \(T=\sum_i a_i\).
Let \(P_i\) be the minimum of \(\delta^{a_i}\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
For either sign and every sequence \(b=b(n)\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\), the least one-norm of an integral row \(A\) such that
\[
  \prod_i P_i^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
  A(\delta^{a_i n})\ne0\quad(1\le i\le r),\qquad A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y),
\]
exists for all sufficiently large integers \(n\) and satisfies \(\log\|A\|_1/n\to Th\).
For each \(\gamma>0\) one fixed width suffices for the upper slope \(Th+\gamma\).
Thus \(2Th<(\log2)/4\) realizes these particular \(r\) values on one passing row of the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at every sufficiently large integer, also at widths tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\).
In particular the three original minima for \(a=1,2,3\) have slope \(6h\) at even as well as odd exponents.
The result concerns a single fixed input before the exponent increases.

More generally let \(\delta\) be any reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit whose positive powers all generate its field, and put \(h=\log M(\delta)\).
Assume that among the distinct exterior roots of all \(P_i\), two roots of the same modulus must be complex conjugates.
Then all the preceding assertions hold with this \(h\).
The hypothesis holds for every list of powers of such a unit when all its exterior conjugates are real, or when its exterior conjugates consist of just one nonreal conjugate pair.

<a id="radii"></a>
## A double contact forces a change of maximizing coefficient index

Consider nonzero integral two-row graphs \(U(X^n)-XV(X^n)\), allowing one zero row and allowing the widths to vary freely.
Fix algebraic units \(c_i\) in one complex embedding with pairwise distinct moduli greater than one and put \(s_i=\log|c_i|\).
Require double zeros at all \(c_i\).
The least possible limiting lower slope of the maximum row one-norm \(H\) is at least \(S=\sum_i s_i\).
If one additional fixed algebraic unit \(c_*\) with modulus greater than one, distinct from all the indicated moduli, is a zero even to order one, the limiting lower slope is **strictly greater** than \(S\).
Neither assertion requires nonvanishing numerator values.
The strict bound also applies to an empty double list.

To prove the assertions, suppose there is a sequence of such graphs at \(n\to\infty\) with \(\log H/n\le S+o(1)\).
Reverse the two rows using their common formal width and divide out common powers of the new variable at zero.
Write the resulting coefficient vectors \(w_j=(u_j,v_j)^t\in\mathbb Z^2\), with \(w_0\ne0\) and maximum coordinate norm \(|w_j|\).
These operations preserve all contact orders at the selected nonzero points when expressed in the variable \(Y=X^{-n}\).
For \(t=c_i^{-n}\) put \(L_c=(1,-c)\).
The two equations at a double contact are
\[
       \sum_j L_{c_i}w_j t^j=0,\qquad
       n\sum_j jL_{c_i}w_j t^j=-c_i\sum_j v_j t^j .                    \tag{1}
\]
They follow by differentiating the graph before using its value equation and hold even if both row values vanish.
The first equation alone holds at the simple contact with \(t=c_*^{-n}\).

Take a fixed integer \(J\) so large that \((J+1)\min s>S+2\), with the minimum over all nodes in the argument.
At each such node the terms with \(j>J\), including a factor \(nj\) if present, have sum \(O(e^{-n})\) by the norm bound and the geometric decay of \(|t|^j\).
In fact \(\max_{j>J}j|t|^j\le(J+1)|t|^{J+1}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Pass to a subsequence on which \(\gamma_j=\lim \log|w_j|/n\) exists for each \(0\le j\le J\), with value \(-\infty\) for an eventually absent coefficient.
A nonzero integral coefficient has nonnegative logarithm, so \(0\le\gamma_0\le S\) and each finite \(\gamma_j\le S\).
Consider the piecewise linear function
\[
                L(s)=\max_{0\le j\le J}(\gamma_j-js),\qquad s\ge0.
\]
This is the [Archimedean Newton polygon construction](../audits/literature/AKNR18/Yin_U001.md#tropical) applied to limiting logarithms of vector norms.
Its value at each node is at least zero.
If a unique index \(k\) maximized at a double node, all other terms in (1) would be exponentially smaller than \(|w_k t^k|\), even with the polynomial factors in \(n\).
The first equation bounds \(L_{c_i}w_k t^k\) by those smaller terms.
Subtracting \(nk\) times the value equation in the second then bounds \(v_k t^k\) and hence \(u_k t^k\) by such terms, a contradiction.
Each double node therefore forces a breakpoint of \(L\).

As \(s\) decreases from infinity, the maximizing index starts at zero and increases by positive integers at the breakpoints \(s>0\).
Integrating the slopes, \(L(0)-\gamma_0\) equals the sum of these breakpoints weighted by their index jumps.
The required breakpoints already cost \(S\).
It follows that equality holds: \(\gamma_0=0,\ L(0)=S\), every required jump has size one and there are no other positive breakpoints.
In particular \(\log H/n\) cannot stay below \(S\) by a fixed positive amount.
At the \(i\)-th breakpoint in decreasing order the two maximizing indices are \(i-1,i\), and \(\gamma_i=\sum_{\ell\le i}s_\ell\).

<a id="simple"></a>
## The obstruction from one simple zero at a further radius

Order the double nodes by decreasing modulus, put \(t_i=c_i^{-n}\), and set \(T_j'=\prod_{i=1}^j t_i\) with \(T_0'=1\).
At the \(i\)-th breakpoint multiply (1) by \(T_{i-1}'/t_i^{\,i-1}\).
The leading vector terms are \(T_{i-1}'w_{i-1}\) and \(T_i'w_i\).
Every other term has size \(O(e^{-\eta n})\) for some \(\eta>0\) along the chosen subsequence.
Indeed the exponent of the normalizing scalar is \(-L(s_i)\le0\), and every other finite index is strictly below the maximum.
A smaller \(\eta>0\) absorbs both the tail and polynomial factors in \(n\).
Subtract \(n(i-1)\) times the value equation from the derivative sum and solve for the two coordinates of the second vector.
The resulting relations are
\[
 T_i'w_i=M_{c_i}(n)T_{i-1}'w_{i-1}+O(e^{-\eta n}),\qquad
 M_c(Z)=-I+Z
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-c\\ c^{-1}&-1\end{pmatrix}.                         \tag{2}
\]
For example the second coordinate is the negative preceding second coordinate plus \(n/c_i\) times \(L_{c_i}\) of the preceding vector, up to the stated error.
Every \(M_c(Z)\) has determinant one, because its displayed linear coefficient has trace and determinant zero.
Since the list is fixed, iteration changes only the choice of positive \(\eta\).

At \(s_*=\log|c_*|\) the unique maximizing index is \(j=\#\{i:s_i>s_*\}\).
Normalize its single value equation by \(T_j'/t_*^j\), where \(t_*=c_*^{-n}\).
The same strict separation of indices now gives
\[
        p(n)w_0=O(e^{-\eta n}),\qquad
        p(Z)=L_{c_*}M_{c_j}(Z)\cdots M_{c_1}(Z).                        \tag{3}
\]
For \(j=0\) the empty product is the identity.
All entries of \(p\) are polynomials over the integers of the fixed number field containing the nodes, since the \(c_i\) are units.
The coordinates of \(w_0\) are rational integers of size \(\exp(o(n))\).
Every conjugate of the algebraic integer \(p(n)w_0\) is therefore at most \(\exp(o(n))\) in absolute value.
Its integer norm and (3) force \(p(n)w_0=0\) for all large \(n\) on the subsequence.

For each embedding \(\tau\) of that number field, \(p(n)\) and \(\tau p(n)\) now both annihilate the same nonzero rational vector.
Their determinant, a polynomial in \(n\), vanishes on infinitely many integers and hence identically.
At \(Z=0\) one has \(p(0)=(-1)^j(1,-c_*)\), so \(c_*=\tau c_*\) for every embedding.
This would make \(c_*\) a rational algebraic unit of modulus greater than one.
The contradiction rules out any sequence with limiting upper slope at most \(S\) when the additional simple zero is present.
Equivalently there is some \(\eta_0>0\), depending on the fixed nodes, for which every sufficiently large exponent costs at least \(S+\eta_0\) in normalized log norm.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Keeping every powered numerator nonzero

For the stated Salem or quadratic input set \(c_i=\delta^{a_i}\).
Their distinct radii are exactly their measures in multiplicative notation.
Every positive power of \(\delta\) generates \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\), since there is only one exterior conjugate to lie above its powered exterior value.
Thus the minima \(Q_i\) of \(\delta^{na_i}\) have logarithmic measures \(na_i h\) and are pairwise distinct.
The double-node bound gives limiting lower slope at least \(Th\) for any graph, including signed rows.

The [integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) at width \(w=b-1\) supplies a nonzero two-row graph containing \(\prod P_i^2\) with \(\log H/n\le Th+o(1)\).
When \(w\) is sufficiently large and fixed, it instead supplies any prescribed positive excess on the right at large \(n\).
If both rows contained \(Q_i\), divide it out of both.
The quotient graph retains all other double zeros and at least a simple zero at \(c_i\), since \(Q_i(X^n)\) is separable and disjoint from the other selected orbits.
The strict bound just proved gives a quotient row of normalized log norm at least \((T-a_i)h+\eta_i\) for some fixed \(\eta_i>0\).
Its log Mahler measure is within \(w\log2=o(n)\) of its log one-norm.
Restoring \(Q_i\) forces original slope at least \(Th+\eta_i-o(1)\), contradicting interpolation, taking the fixed excess below all \(\eta_i\) in the fixed-width case.
If one row vanished at a powered node, the graph equation would force the same powered minimum into both.
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) therefore preserve all the genuine contacts at either sign, at one more unit of width and bounded norm cost.
This gives both upper bounds.
With strict budget slack, the [reciprocal width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) extends fixed-width rows to the stated subexponential widths.

<a id="conjugates"></a>
## Two nonreal conjugate contacts at one radius

At a radius \(e^s>1\) occupied by two distinct double nodes \(c,d\) of the same modulus, the coefficient-index jump of \(L\) is at least two.
For this assertion take any sequence with bounded normalized log norm and choose the finite cutoff \(J\) using that bound in place of \(S\).
One maximizing index is impossible by (1).
If the jump were one, only two adjacent indices \(k,k+1\) would maximize.
For \(z=c^{-n},w=d^{-n}\), the equations (1) reduce, after removing \(nk\) times the value equation, to four equations on \(w_k,w_{k+1}\).
Divide by \(z^k\) or \(w^k\) in each pair and scale the vector \(w_{k+1}\) by \(r_n=|z|=|w|\).
Writing \(Z=z/r_n,\ W=w/r_n\), the two rows of the resulting matrix at \((c,Z)\) are
\[
       (1,-c,Z,-cZ),\qquad (0,c,nZ,(1-n)cZ),
\]
and the other two replace \(c,Z\) by \(d,W\).
Expansion gives determinant
\[
                     cd(W-Z)^2-n^2 WZ(c-d)^2 .
\]
Since \(|Z|=|W|=1\) and \(c\ne d\) are fixed, its magnitude is at least \(n^2|c-d|^2-4|cd|\).
The inverse matrix therefore has at most polynomial growth in \(n\), uniformly in both phases.
After dividing the scaled unknown vector by \(\max(|w_k|,r_n|w_{k+1}|)\), its maximum norm is one and all discarded terms in the four equations are exponentially small, by separation from the other indices.
The inverse bound gives a contradiction.

At a radius occupied by a simple nonreal node \(c\), even the value equation alone forces a breakpoint.
Indeed \(|u-cv|\ge C_c\max(|u|,|v|)\) for real \(u,v\), by compactness of the real unit square boundary and \(\Im c\ne0\).
One maximizing real coefficient vector could not be canceled by the other exponentially smaller terms.

Under the stated modulus hypothesis each real exterior root of a double factor therefore contributes a jump of at least one, and each nonreal conjugate pair contributes at least two at its radius.
The sum of their logarithmic radii with these multiplicities is \(Th\).
This gives the full lower bound.
If all exterior roots are real, apply the simple-zero argument (2)–(3) after dividing out \(Q_i\), using all exterior roots of the other factors as double nodes and any one exterior root of \(P_i\) as the simple node.
If a nonreal exterior conjugate \(x\) occurs, every \(x^{a_i}\) remains nonreal, since equality with the same power of \(\bar x\) would contradict the full power degrees.
After division by \(Q_i\), the remaining double nodes contribute \((T-a_i)h\), and the simple nonreal node \(x^{a_i}\) contributes a further jump of at least one at its separate radius.
Its contribution \(a_i\log|x|>0\) supplies the strict gain required in the interpolation argument.
The same contradiction, projections and padding prove the extended result.

For real exterior conjugates \(x,y\), equality \(|x|^a=|y|^b\) implies \(x^a=\pm y^b\).
Their equal positive absolute heights then force \(a=b\), while full power degrees exclude a torsion quotient between distinct \(x,y\).
For a single nonreal exterior pair the radii at the different levels are distinct positive powers of one number greater than one.
These observations prove both stated sufficient hypotheses.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin obtained the coefficient-index argument while studying the [square pair](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) and the [overlap for three consecutive powers](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v275.md#counts).
If more exterior roots or different powered orbits share one modulus, the proved index jumps can account for less than the full measure and the general attainment question remains open.
