# Finite exponent windows and primitive patterns for general algebraic units

<a id="statement"></a>
## The signature and degree bounds

Let \(\alpha\) range over a sequence of nontorsion algebraic units with \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\), write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(d_*=\min_{q\ge1}\deg(\alpha^q)\), and \(L=\log(1/m)\).
Let \(k\) be the larger of the numbers of expanding and contracting embeddings of \(K\), counting each complex embedding individually.
Choose integers \(1\le B\le d_*/4\) with \(Bm\to0\).
There is a weight \(r\to\infty\) and one fixed ordered tuple of \(r-1\) nonzero integral coefficients of degrees at most \(B\) giving at least \((c_0/m)^{r-2}\) sorted marked lists under the [full degree-bounded terminality test](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement), for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Their marked exponents have absolute values \(O(d^4\log(2d))\).
For this witness let \(V_r\) count the lists after division by the positive gcd of the exponents, counting the all-zero list separately if present.
Whenever \(\log(2k)=o(rL)\),
\[
                      \log V_r\ge (1-o(1))(r-2)L.
\]
In particular this holds if \(k\le m^{-C}\) for a fixed \(C\), or if \(\log(2k)=o(L^{5/4}/(\log(2+L))^{1/4})\) by the [general witness weight estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v589.md#statement).
The polynomial exponent window alone gives the same primitive conclusion when \(\log d=o(rL)\).

<a id="window"></a>
## A polynomial exponent window without a record assumption

Use the [original support interval and marked tree reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#count), with its \(j,N\) and \(Y=\log j+Nm/d=O(\log(2d))\).
For every edge written as \(z=c\alpha^n\) in the original coordinates, the [cut into original leaves](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#edges) gives a nonzero algebraic integer \(z\) with \(h(z)\le Y\), where \(h\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Thus \(c\) is integral, and its degree is at most \(B\) by the splitting rule.
Choose an embedding \(\sigma\) fixing \(c\) such that \(\alpha,\sigma(\alpha)\) are multiplicatively independent, using the [embedding incidence bound](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings) and \(B\le d_*/4\).
Then \(\eta=\alpha/\sigma(\alpha)\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(D\le d^2\).
Dimitrov's [disc estimate at constant term one](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-house) gives \(h(\eta)=h(\eta^{-1})\ge\log(2)/(4D^2)\).
To apply it, multiply the minimal polynomial by its constant term \(\pm1\) to normalize it, and take the inverse of a conjugate in the indicated disc.
As \(\eta^n=z/\sigma(z)\), we get
\[
 |n|\le U:=8d^4Y/\log2,\qquad h(c)\le Y+Um/d.
\]
Normalized local exponents are differences of two edge exponents and therefore have magnitude at most \(2U\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Relative norms in fields with neutral extensions

Suppose \(d>2k\).
There is a neutral embedding, so the minimal polynomial has a root whose complex conjugate equals its inverse.
Irreducibility gives an involution \(\iota\) of \(K\) taking \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\).
For any subfield \(F\subset K\) put \(E=F\,\iota(F)\).
If \(2k[E:\mathbb Q]<d\), the unit \(N_{K/F}(\alpha)\) is a root of unity.
Indeed, every embedding \(\tau:E\to\mathbb C\) has more than \(2k\) extensions, one of them neutral.
On this extension composition with \(\iota\) is complex conjugation, by checking the generator \(\alpha\).
Hence \(\tau(\iota x)=\overline{\tau(x)}\) for every \(x\in E\).
The field \(E\) is stable under \(\iota\), and norm naturality gives \(\iota(u)=u^{-1}\) for \(u=N_{K/E}(\alpha)\).
Every conjugate of this integral unit consequently has modulus one, so Kronecker's theorem makes it torsion.
Transitivity of norms gives the assertion for \(F\).

When \(d>2kB^2\), apply this result to every edge field \(F=\mathbb Q(c)\), since \([E:\mathbb Q]\le B^2\).
Taking the norm of \(z=c\alpha^n\) now gives \(h(c)\le h(z)\) and \(|n|m/d\le2Y\), exactly as in the [norm calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm).
Use the preceding polynomial windows in every case, imposing these additional bounds whenever \(d>2kB^2\).
All realizations constructed by splitting the original supports satisfy the respective bounds, so the [finite-window reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#count) gives the claimed fixed tuple and raw count at a maximizing arity \(r\).
The bound \(2BH_r\) from the [general embedding count](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings) forces \(r\to\infty\).
Any nonzero primitive list has at most \(\lfloor2U\rfloor\) positive dilations in the window, proving the assertion for \(\log d=o(rL)\).

<a id="signature"></a>
## Counting dilations with few nonneutral embeddings

Assume \(\log(2k)=o(rL)\).
For \(d\le 2kB^2\) the polynomial window suffices because \(\log d\le\log(2k)+2L\) eventually.
For the remaining inputs the stronger norm window gives heights at most \(Y\) for the output and every outgoing coefficient.
Retain the most frequent equality and sign pattern of the sorted lists as in the [group argument](Yin_C_G0345_v593.md#groups), with logarithmic loss \(O(r)\).
The least size \(g\) of a nonzero-exponent group satisfies \(g=o(r)\) by that argument, since the mixed embedding estimate on arbitrary fixed grouped coefficients uses only \(B\le d_*/4\).

If \(2kB^2<d\le2kB^{2r}\), use the [height estimate for a smallest group](Yin_C_G0345_v593.md#height).
It bounds the logarithm of the number of dilations per ray by
\[
 O\big(L+\log(2k)+\log(2r)+\log(2+Y)\big)+g\log B.
\]
Indeed the proof at the extremal embedding for the sign of this group uses only the height bounds, the degree bound \(B^g\) for its nonzero coefficient and a log modulus of magnitude at least \(m/k\).
Here \(\log(2+Y)=O(\log(2+\log(2k))+\log(2r)+\log(2+L))\), and \(\log B\le L\) eventually, so the cost is \(o(rL)\).

Finally suppose \(d>2kB^{2r}\).
Fix an embedding \(\sigma\) where \(H=\log|\sigma(\alpha)|\) is largest, so \(H\ge m/k\).
For a primitive list, group its coefficients as \(b_w\) at distinct exponents \(w\), putting \(b_0=0\) if the constant group is absent.
At \(\sigma\), choose the side of nonzero exponents with greater total coefficient modulus, breaking ties either way, and call its mass \(S\).
For each realized positive dilation with output \(c\), take \(F=\mathbb Q(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1},c)\), so \([F\,\iota(F):\mathbb Q]\le B^{2r}\).
The restriction of \(\sigma\) to this compositum has a neutral extension, by the fiber argument above.
Evaluating there gives \(|\sigma(c-b_0)|\le2S\).
Moreover \(\sigma\iota\) and complex conjugation after \(\sigma\) agree on this compositum.
Thus \(\sigma\iota\) preserves the moduli of the embedded coefficients and of \(c-b_0\) relative to \(\sigma\), while its value on \(\alpha\) is \(\sigma(\alpha)^{-1}\).
Evaluate at \(\sigma\) if the positive side was chosen, and at \(\sigma\iota\) if the negative side was chosen, reversing exponents in the latter case.
The chosen sum now has absolute value at most \(3S\) and its expanding base has log modulus \(H\).
Both the evaluating embedding and the chosen side are fixed over all dilations of this primitive list, even though \(c\) can vary.
The [separated complex evaluation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#matrix) gives at most \(r\lceil k\log(64(r+1)^2)/m\rceil\) hits, of logarithmic size \(o(rL)\).
Dividing the retained count by the respective dilation bound proves the assertion.
