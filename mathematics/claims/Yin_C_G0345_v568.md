# The dyadic Gauss fibre for unequal trinomial spacings

<a id="statement"></a>
## A local restriction on six-term factors

Let \(0<k<l<n\), let \(a,c,\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), and consider the reciprocal or antireciprocal polynomial
\[
 F(X)=1+aX^k+cX^l+\epsilon X^n(c+aX^{l-k}+X^l).
\]
For \(l\ne2k\), the odd Kummer covers of the long power \(x^n\) at the dyadic Gauss valuation have a single branch with transcendental residue, both on the torus of the two short powers and on each reduced slope.
On the reduced slope this holds even after multiplying the long power by an arbitrary monomial in the slope parameter.
Equal spacing is the unique reduced slope giving constant residue for some such twist.

There is an arithmetic consequence for moving spacings.
Suppose \(x\) runs through nontorsion unit roots of such parents with \(l\ne2k\) and \(l h(x)\to0\), for the absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h\).
Write \(N=2^{v_2(n)}\).
For each fixed \(B,C\ge1\), the fraction of embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) in \(\mathbb C_2\) satisfying both
\[
         \operatorname{ord}(\overline{x^n})\le B,
         \qquad e(\mathbb Q_2(x)/\mathbb Q_2)\le C N
\]
tends to zero, with \(x\) interpreted under the embedding.
This includes shrinking positive-measure factors with \(l=o(n)\), since the [six-term upper height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#statement) gives \((n+l)h(x)\ll1\) there.
If the odd part of the long exponent is bounded on such a shrinking sequence, then the residue order of the long power tends to infinity in probability.
Indeed the lower bound on normalized height in the same six-term estimate gives degree \(d\ll(n+l)m\) for factor log measure \(m\to0\), hence every dyadic ramification index is at most \(d=o(N)\).

<a id="fibre"></a>
## Simple zeros after reduction

With independent variables \(Y,Z\), the long power is represented by
\[
 G=-\epsilon(1+aY+cZ)/(c+aZ/Y+Z).
\]
At the dyadic Gauss valuation its residue is \((1+Y+Z)/(1+Z/Y+Z)\) over \(K=\overline{\mathbb F}_2\).
The numerator line has multiplicity one and the denominator restricts on it to \((Y^2+Y+1)/Y\), a nonzero rational function.
Consequently the residue of \(G\), even after multiplication by a Laurent monomial, has a simple zero along that line in the torus.

On substituting \(Y=T^r,Z=T^s\) for coprime \(0<r<s\), the reduced numerator and denominator are \(P=1+T^r+T^s\) and \(P^*=1+T^{s-r}+T^s\).
The polynomial \(P\) is squarefree in characteristic two.
If exactly one exponent is odd its derivative is a monomial, and if both are odd a nonzero derivative root satisfies \(T^{s-r}=1\), where \(P=1\).
The same argument applies to \(P^*\).
For \(s\ne2r\), any common root \(t\) satisfies \(t^{s-2r}=1\) by subtraction and \((t^r)^2+t^r+1=0\).
Since \(\gcd(r,s-2r)=1\), raising to the \(r\)-th power preserves the order of \(t\), so \(t\) has order three.
Conversely, both primitive cube roots are common exactly when \(3\mid s-2r\).
As \(s\ge3\), cancellation leaves a simple nonzero numerator root, which survives every monomial twist.
For \(s=2r\), coprimality gives \((r,s)=(1,2)\) and the ratio reduces to one, as in the [equally spaced case](Yin_C_G0345_v564.md#cover).

In either unequal-spacing calculation let \(H\) be the indicated ratio with a monomial twist, and take odd \(b\ge1\).
A simple zero makes \(U^b-\overline H\) Eisenstein at its divisor, hence irreducible over the residue function field, and oddness gives separability.
Over the completed Gauss field the monic polynomial \(U^b-H\) therefore generates a field whose residue degree is \(b\), its full degree.
There is a single point above the Gauss point, and the residue of \(U\) is transcendental over \(K\) because its \(b\)-th power is nonconstant.
Thus no open residue disk about a constant unit contains that point.
This identifies the obstruction to selecting fixed constant residue branches by positive limiting mass; individual conjugates in such disks can still occur.

<a id="density"></a>
## Ramification when the long power has small residue order

Fix a positive integer \(M\).
We first show that \(v_2(x^{Mn}-1)\to0\) in probability over embeddings, with \(v_2(2)=1\).
From any subsequence choose one with fixed signs and either fixed coprime \((r,s)=(k/g,l/g)\), \(g=\gcd(k,l)\), or with \(s\to\infty\).
In the fixed case, \(T=x^g\) has positive height tending to zero, so its conjugate orbits equidistribute at the Gauss point of the projective line by [Yuan's theorem](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md#yuan-equidistribution).
Here genericity follows since every fixed finite set contains only finitely many terms.

In the moving case the points \((Y,Z)=(x^k,x^l)\) have both heights tending to zero and form a strict sequence on the two-dimensional torus.
Indeed any character relation \(Y^uZ^v=1\) forces \(ur+vs=0\) because \(x\) is nontorsion, and a fixed nontrivial relation allows at most one coprime positive slope.
[Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives complex Haar equidistribution along every subsequence, so the points are Zariski generic in the projective plane under \((Y,Z)\mapsto[1:Y:Z]\).
Otherwise a subsequence would lie on a proper rational hypersurface (take the product of conjugate equations if necessary), whose complex locus cannot contain the full unit torus supporting Haar measure.
For the standard maximum metric on \(\mathcal O(1)\) the point heights tend to zero, the plane has canonical height zero under coordinatewise squaring, and its standard model has one special-fibre component of multiplicity and degree one.
Yuan's Theorem 3.1 and the model measure formula in Section 3.3 therefore give dyadic convergence to the two-variable Gauss point ([published paper](https://math.berkeley.edu/~yxy/preprints/big.pdf), pp. 637, 642–643, 647).

In both cases \(G\) is defined near the relevant Gauss point and \(|G^M-1|_2=1\) there by its nonconstant residue.
For any \(\delta>0\) a neighborhood thus has \(|G^M-1|_2>2^{-\delta}\) with denominator nonzero.
The proportion of conjugates in that neighborhood tends to one, and each base embedding has the same number of extensions to \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
There the parent equation gives \(G=x^n\), proving convergence in probability on the original sequence by the subsequence criterion.

Take \(M=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor B\rfloor)\) and \(n=Nt\) with \(t\) odd.
At a place satisfying the residue-order condition, \(x^{Mt}\) reduces to one since squaring is injective in the residue field.
Thus \(w=v_2(x^{Mt}-1)\ge 1/e\).
Squaring a unit whose difference from one has valuation \(w\) doubles that valuation while \(w<1\) and preserves the lower bound one thereafter, by the binomial formula.
Consequently \(v_2(x^{Mn}-1)\ge\min(1,N/e)\), at least \(1/C\) if also \(e\le CN\).
Convergence in probability proves the assertion.
