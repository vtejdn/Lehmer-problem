# A simultaneous residue-order bound for conjugates of a small unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## One height budget for all finite primes and congruence depths

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(w)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), \(m=d h(w)\) its total logarithmic Mahler measure, and \(L\) its rational splitting field.
Write \(n\) for the number of conjugate classes modulo torsion, \(b\) for the dimension of their rational span, and \(\Gamma\subset\mathcal O_L^\times\) for the group generated by the actual conjugates.
For a prime \(p\), choose \(P\mid p\) in \(L\), write \(e_p=e(L_P/\mathbb Q_p)\), and let \(B_{p,j}\) be the size of the image of \(\Gamma\) in \((\mathcal O_L/P^j)^\times\) for \(j\ge1\).
These numbers are independent of the choice of \(P\).
For \(1\le s\le b\), set
\[
 D=\binom{b+1}{s}+(n-b-1)\binom b{s-1},\qquad
 H_s=\frac{\log D}{D-1}+2s\,\frac{m}{d},\qquad x_+=\max(x,0).
\]
Then
\[
   \sum_p\frac{\log p}{e_p}\sum_{j\ge1}
          \frac{(D/B_{p,j}-1)_+}{D-1}\ \le\ H_s .                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(B_{p,1}\) is the least common multiple of the multiplicative orders of all the distinct roots of the original minimum modulo \(p\).

There is also a bound using only the original root field, even if some conjugates have large local degrees or orders.
For each prime tested, choose positive integers \(E_p,T_p\), with the choices allowed to vary independently, and put
\[
 \gamma_p=\frac1d\sum_{\substack{v\mid p\ {\rm in}\ K,\ e_v\le E_p\\
                         \operatorname{ord}_v(w)\mid T_p}} e_v f_v ,
       \qquad \beta_p=(1-s(1-\gamma_p))_+ .
\]
Here \(e_v,f_v\) are the ramification index and residue degree at \(v\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_v(w)\) is the order of the residue of the unit \(w\).
For every such collection,
\[
    \sum_p\frac{\log p}{E_p}
       \frac{(D\beta_p^2/T_p-1)_+}{D-1}\ \le\ H_s .                     \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\), one may take \(s=11\), \(D\gg n(\log n)^5\), and obtain
\[
\begin{split}
    n\sum_{\substack{p,j\ge1\\ B_{p,j}\le D/2}}
               \frac{\log p}{e_pB_{p,j}}&\le46m,\\
    n\sum_{\substack{p:\ \gamma_p\ge21/22\\T_p\le D/8}}
               \frac{\log p}{E_pT_p}&\le184m .                          \tag{3}
\end{split}
\]
The lower threshold on \(n\) and the implied constant are absolute and effective.
Thus both weighted sums times \(n\) must tend to zero on every positive shrinking-measure sequence, including for varying sets of tested primes.
The [single Frobenius probability bound](Yin_C_G0901_v77.md#statement) tests one prime power at a time.
Here different primes and residue exponents are charged against the same total measure.

<a id="depths"></a>
## Counting all collisions in one Vandermonde determinant

The [conjugate-product count](Yin_C_G0901_v77.md#sums) and its [dimension calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v77.md#scale) provide \(D\ge2\) integral units \(u_1,\ldots,u_D\) with pairwise different classes.
Each is a product of \(s\) actual conjugates of \(w\), and \(h(u_i)\le s m/d\).
The class vectors have affine dimension \(b\) because their sum is zero and their linear span has dimension \(b\).
Thus the count applies also when several original roots had the same class.
Set \(V=\prod_{i<k}(u_i-u_k)\ne0\) and \(B=D(D-1)/2\).
Hadamard's inequality on the Vandermonde rows divided by \(\max(1,|\sigma u_i|)^{D-1}\) bounds the archimedean mean of \(\log|V|\), divided by \(B\), by \(H_s\).
Indeed the normalized determinant is bounded by \(D^{D/2}\), and the removed factors have mean logarithm \((D-1)\sum_i h(u_i)\).
By the product formula this pays the sum of \(-\log|V|_P\) at all finite places with their local-degree weights, divided by \(B\).
All these costs are nonnegative by integrality.

At level \(P^j\), the \(D\) nodes occupy at most \(B_{p,j}\) residue classes.
For occupancies \(c_i\) in at most \(B_{p,j}\) classes, Cauchy's inequality gives \(\sum_i c_i(c_i-1)/2\ge D(D/B_{p,j}-1)/2\), and the collision count is nonnegative.
The ideal valuation \(\operatorname{ord}_P(V)\) is exactly the sum of these unordered collision counts over all \(j\ge1\).
The group \(\Gamma\) is Galois stable, so its residue image size is the same at all \(P\mid p\).
Thus each local count gives the lower bound in (1) with the factor \(\log p/e_p\), since \(|p|_P=p^{-1}\).
The local-degree weights above \(p\) sum to one.
This proves (1), also for a wildly ramified prime.
Only finitely many summands can be positive for this input, since the fixed nonzero algebraic integer \(V\) has a finite ideal factorization.

Modulo \(P\), the local residue multiplicative group is cyclic.
Its subgroup generated by the conjugate residues has cardinality equal to the least common multiple of their individual orders.
The reduction of the original monic minimum is their product of linear factors in the splitting residue field, with multiplicity, so its distinct residue roots already give that least common multiple.
For an irreducible factor \(f\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) its root order is the least \(T>0\) for which \(f\mid X^T-1\).
This computes \(B_{p,1}\) regardless of repeated factors in the original reduction.

<a id="original"></a>
## Allowing some original conjugates to have uncontrolled reduction

Fix \(p\) and average over a uniformly chosen \(P\mid p\) in \(L\), as prescribed by the equal local-degree weights for a normal field.
For any particular conjugate \(a\) the place induced on \(\mathbb Q(a)\) corresponds by transport to a place \(v\) of \(K\).
Its probability is \(e_v f_v/d\) by the local degree formula and prime transitivity in \(L/\mathbb Q(a)\).
Thus \(a\) meets the two local tests defining \(\gamma_p\) with probability \(\gamma_p\).
Call a node good at \(P\) if every one of its \(s\) specified conjugate factors meets both tests.
The union bound gives probability at least \(\beta_p\) for each node, so if \(M_P\) nodes are good then \(\mathbb E_P M_P\ge D\beta_p\).
The factors need no statistical independence.

At a good factor its residue has order dividing \(T_p\).
Its unique prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift in the completion of \(\mathbb Q(a)\) at \(v\) therefore also has order dividing \(T_p\).
Simple-root lifting in that completion shows that \(a\) and its lift differ by valuation at least \(1/e_v\ge1/E_p\) in the normalization \(\nu_p(p)=1\).
For a good node the product of these lifts differs from the node by valuation at least \(1/E_p\), since all factors are units.
There are at most \(T_p\) such products of lifts, and the same residue gives exactly the same lift in \(L_P\).
This [local comparison in the original completion](Yin_C_G0345_v728.md#determinant) makes the cost independent of the ramification of the entire splitting field.

At least \((M_P^2/T_p-M_P)/2\) unordered pairs of good nodes have the same lift.
Each such pair contributes at least \(\log p/E_p\) to \(-\log|V|_P\).
After averaging, \(\mathbb E M_P^2\ge(\mathbb E M_P)^2\) and \(M_P\le D\), giving the lower bound \((D^2\beta_p^2/T_p-D)_+/2\) for the mean count.
Divide by \(B\), sum over the tested primes and use the archimedean estimate for the same \(V\) to obtain (2).

For a test directly on the original polynomial, take \(E_p=1\).
Every irreducible factor \(f\) occurring with multiplicity one in its reduction contributes an unramified completion of residue degree \(\deg f\).
Indeed the coprime factor lifts over \(\mathbb Z_p\) and its separable irreducible reduction generates an unramified extension.
Summing \(\deg f/d\) over such factors dividing \(X^{T_p}-1\) gives a lower bound for \(\gamma_p\) that uses only \(\mathbb F_p[X]\).
It is valid also if other factors in the reduction are repeated.
For squarefree reduction the test gives \(\gamma_p\) exactly.
The [single-prime cyclic-norm estimate](Yin_C_G0245_v1.md#cyclic-norm-residue-density) uses a common order at the selected prime.
In (2) the orders tested at different primes may all differ, with no global least common multiple in the height cost.

## The linear total-measure bound and polylogarithmic ranges

For sufficiently large \(n\), \(b\ge\sqrt{\log n/\log3}\ge20\).
At \(s=11\) the [product count and Voutier estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v77.md#scale) give \(D\gg n(\log n)^5\) and \(n\log D/(D-1)\le m\).
Thus \(nH_{11}\le23m\) since \(n\le d\).
If \(B_{p,j}\le D/2\), the coefficient in (1) is at least \(1/(2B_{p,j})\).
If \(\gamma_p\ge21/22\), then \(\beta_p\ge1/2\), so for \(T_p\le D/8\) the coefficient in (2) is at least \(1/(8T_p)\).
This proves (3).
Torsion-class compression preserves \(m\) at degree \(n\), so bounded-degree finiteness forces \(n\to\infty\) on a shrinking sequence.

More generally, for any fixed \(A>0\) choose an integer \(s\ge11\) with \((s-1)/2>A\).
Then \(D\gg_s n(\log n)^{(s-1)/2}\) and \(D\le n^s\) for sufficiently large \(n\), by the same count.
Thus \(n\log D/(D-1)\ll_s(\log n)^{1-(s-1)/2}\), eventually at most \(m\) by the [explicit bound \(m>2/(\log(3n))^3\)](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
The first sum in (3) with \(B_{p,j}\le n(\log n)^A\) is at most \(2(1+2s)m\).
The second with \(T_p\le n(\log n)^A\) and \(\gamma_p\ge1-1/(2s)\) is at most \(8(1+2s)m\).
Both refer to the sums including their prefactor \(n\).

## Many growing primes with one uncontrolled factor in every reduction

For every sufficiently large even \(d=2r\) put \(X=d(\log d)^3\) and choose \(\lceil(\log d)^{5/2}\rceil\) distinct primes in \([X,2X]\).
There are enough primes by the [prime number theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#fixed-progression-pnt).
Put \(t=\lfloor r/22\rfloor\).
Consider all degree-\(d\) nontorsion units with distinct conjugate classes whose minimum has squarefree reduction at every chosen \(p\), with \(d-2t\) nonzero linear factors and a remaining factor irreducible of degree \(2t\) with root order \(p^t+1\).
Every such unit satisfies
\[
                         m(w)\gg\sqrt{\log d}.                            \tag{4}
\]
In fact \(n=d\), and the test \(E_p=1,\ T_p=p-1\) has \(\gamma_p\ge1-t/r\ge21/22\).
For large \(d\), \(2X<D/8\) in (3), giving
\[
       184m\ \ge\ d\sum_{p\ {\rm chosen}}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
                          \ \gg\ \sqrt{\log d}.
\]
Each individual term after multiplication by \(d\) is only \(O((\log d)^{-2})\).
At each chosen prime the full residue group has \(B_{p,1}\ge p^t+1>D\), since \(t\to\infty\) and \(D\le d^{11}\).
Thus the all-conjugate collision term in (1) at \(s=11\) vanishes there, and the bounded-order fraction in the original field gives the aggregate lower bound.

These congruence classes contain Salem units.
Over \(\mathbb F_p\), an element of order \(p^t+1\) in \(\mathbb F_{p^{2t}}^\times\) has degree \(2t\), since every proper divisor of \(2t\) is at most \(t\).
The \(t\)-th Frobenius power inverts it, so its minimum is reciprocal and comes from a trace factor of degree \(t\).
For the other \(r-t\) trace values choose distinct pairs of mutually inverse elements of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) different from \(1,-1\), possible since \(p>2r+2\).
Their traces are distinct and give the required linear factors in the reciprocal lift.
Independently, modulo two prescribe the trace of an element of order \(2^r+1\), whose reciprocal lift is irreducible of degree \(2r\) by the same degree and inversion argument.
The Chinese remainder theorem prescribes the nonleading coefficients of a monic integer trace polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(r\) at these finitely many primes.

Choose \(r-1\) fixed distinct points \(u_i\in(-2,2)\) and start from \((Y-R)\prod_i(Y-u_i)\) for large \(R>0\).
Round its lower coefficients to the prescribed classes with errors bounded independently of \(R\) for the fixed prime list.
Sign changes across fixed disjoint intervals around the \(u_i\) persist, since there the initial endpoint values grow linearly in \(R\) and the errors stay bounded.
An additional sign change persists between \(R/2\) and \(3R/2\), with initial endpoint magnitudes of order \(R^r\) and errors \(O(R^{r-1})\).
This is the [real trace approximation with prescribed residues](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#families).
Then \(Y\) ranges over one trace greater than two and \(r-1\) interior traces.
The polynomial \(Z^r Q(Z+Z^{-1})\) is irreducible by its prescribed mod-two reduction, and its positive exterior root is a Salem number.
Its conjugates have distinct classes modulo torsion, since transporting a pair with torsion quotient to the unique strictly dominant root would give a second root of the same modulus.
This proves nonemptiness of the family at arbitrarily large degrees.
The construction imposes local behavior at selected primes and supplies no such prime sets for all putative shrinking sequences.
