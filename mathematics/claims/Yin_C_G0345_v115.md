# Monic Taylor quotients at the endpoint of a Salem trace polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Smaller exterior traces and the number of nonreal conjugates

Let \(1<\sigma\le2\) be a Salem number of degree \(2m\), let \(S\) be its monic trace minimal polynomial, and set \(a=\log\sigma,\ T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1},\ u=T-2\).
Write \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\) for its neutral traces in \((-2,2)\).
Put \(h_j=S^{(j)}(2)/j!\), define \(K=\#\{1\le j<m:h_j<0\}\), and form the integer Taylor quotients
\[
 Q_k(X)=\frac{S(X)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}h_j(X-2)^j}{(X-2)^k},
                   \qquad 1\le k<m.
\]
For every \(1\le k\le K\), the monic polynomial \(Q_k\) has exactly one real root \(r_k>2\), counted with multiplicity.
It satisfies \(T>r_1>\cdots>r_K>2\).
The monic irreducible factors of the respective \(Q_k\) containing these roots are pairwise distinct.

Consider any sequence of Salem numbers tending to one and fix \(0<\epsilon<1\).
Here \(K\sim ma/2\).
Uniformly for \(1\le k\le(1-\epsilon)K\), one has
\[
                        0<\frac{T-r_k}{u}\ll e^{-c_\epsilon K}.       \tag{1}
\]
There is an absolute constant \(A>0\) such that for all sufficiently late inputs, every order
\[
               A\sqrt m\log(2m)\le k\le(1-\epsilon)K
\]
has at most \(O_\epsilon(ma)\) real roots of \(Q_k\), counted with multiplicity, and hence at least \(m-O_\epsilon(ma)\) nonreal roots.
For each fixed \(\delta>0\), the proportion of orders \(1\le k\le K\) for which \(Q_k\) has at least \((1-\delta)m\) nonreal roots tends to one.

If all conjugates of \(r_k\) other than itself belong to \([-2,2]\), it gives a smaller Salem number of degree at most \(2(m-k)\), and in the indicated range the degree is \(O_\epsilon(ma)\).
At a lower-degree Salem minimum each of the \(K\) distinct distinguished factors must therefore itself have a conjugate which is nonreal or strictly below \(-2\).
The total nonreal-root estimate allows roots belonging to other irreducible factors.
At the first order there is an exact critical-value test for the entire quotient to have real roots in the required interval, and it gives an explicit descent from degree eight to six.

<a id="roots"></a>
## The positive root of the divided Taylor tail

In the notation of the [derivative sign test](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#coefficients) put \(p_i=u/(T-x_i)\), and let \(P_j\) be the probability of \(j\) successes in independent Bernoulli trials with these parameters, extended by zero outside \(0\le j<m\).
Write \(E=\sum_i p_i\) and \(D=\prod_i(T-x_i)=S'(T)>0\).
The exact Taylor identities are
\[
           u^j h_j=uD(P_{j-1}-P_j),\qquad
           S(2+uz)=uD(z-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P_jz^j .
\]
The signs of \(h_j\) are strictly negative through \(K\), possibly zero at \(K+1\), and then positive.
In particular \(h_0=S(2)<0\), and \(K\) is the smaller mode when two adjacent probabilities tie.
The bounds \(|K-E|\le1\) and \(E\sim ma/2\) along the given sequence follow from the [finite count and neutral sampling](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#statement).

The polynomial \(Q_k(2+uz)\) has one sign change in its coefficients for \(1\le k\le K\) and is negative at zero.
At \(z=1\) it equals \(u^{1-k}DP_{k-1}>0\), by telescoping.
Descartes' rule, or monotonicity after division by the power of \(z\) at the sign change, gives exactly one positive root counted with multiplicity, and it lies in \((0,1)\).
For \(k<K\) one has \(Q_{k+1}(r_k)=-h_k/(r_k-2)>0\), giving \(r_{k+1}<r_k\).
No distinguished factor can recur since it would give two different roots above two in one quotient.

For an estimate on \(r_k\), divide \(Q_k(2+uz)\) by \(u^{1-k}D\) and sum the remaining consecutive differences.
The resulting polynomial is
\[
             P_{k-1}-(1-z)\sum_{j=k}^{m-1}P_jz^{j-k}.                \tag{2}
\]
If \(k\le(1-\epsilon)K\), then \(k\le(1-\epsilon/2)E\) for large inputs.
For \(s=\epsilon/2\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-sJ}\) for the success count \(J\) gives
\[
  \Pr\{J<k\}\le
    \exp\big(s(1-s)E-(1-e^{-s})E\big)\le e^{-s^2 E/2}=:\eta .
\]
Here \(\mathbb E(e^{-sJ})=\prod_i(1-(1-e^{-s})p_i)\), and \(1-e^{-s}\ge s-s^2/2\).
The [large-degree measure bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(a\gg(\log\log(2m)/\log(2m))^3\) for large \(m\), so \(m\eta\to0\).
For \(1-2P_{k-1}\le z\le1\) the sum in (2) lies between \((1-2P_{k-1})^m(1-\eta)\) and one.
Equation (2) changes sign on this interval for large \(m\).
Consequently
\[
                \frac{T-r_k}{u}=P_{k-1}\bigl(1+O(m\eta)\bigr),
\]
which proves (1) uniformly.

<a id="dominance"></a>
## A bound for the Taylor section on complex arguments

For fixed \(\epsilon\) in the same range and sufficiently large inputs put \(q=1-\epsilon/4\).
Multiplying \(P_j\) by \(q^j\) and normalizing gives another Bernoulli sum, with individual probabilities \(qp_i/(1-p_i+qp_i)\).
Its mean is at least \(q E\).
Its smallest mode differs from its mean by at most one by the [deleted-factor coefficient proof](Yin_C_G0345_v112.md#locations).
That proof applies to these tilted factors as well, since any list of distinct probabilities \(p'_i\in(0,1)\) is realized at \(b=2,T'=3\) by real nodes \(3-1/p'_i<2\).
The Rolle argument only uses their distinctness and their position below \(b\).
For every \(j\le k\le(1-\epsilon)K\) the tilted probabilities are therefore nondecreasing up to \(j\), since \(qE-1>(1-\epsilon)K\) eventually.
Thus \(P_{j-1}\le qP_j\) for \(1\le j\le k\).

It follows that
\[
 \frac{|h_{j-1}|}{|h_j|}\le B_\epsilon u\quad(1\le j\le k),
 \qquad |h_{k-1}|\ge(1-q)|S(2)|u^{1-k},\qquad
 B_\epsilon=\frac{q}{1-q}.
\]
Indeed \(P_j-P_{j-1}\ge(1-q)P_j\), \(P_{k-1}\ge P_0\), and \(|S(2)|=uDP_0\), with the same lower bound immediate for \(k=1\).
For every complex \(z\) satisfying \(|z|\ge4B_\epsilon u\), the section consequently satisfies
\[
  \left|\sum_{j<k}h_j z^j\right|
       \ge\tfrac23|h_{k-1}|\,|z|^{k-1}
       \ge\tfrac23|S(2)|\left(\frac{|z|}{B_\epsilon u}\right)^{k-1}. \tag{3}
\]
The first inequality subtracts a geometric series of ratio at most \(1/4\), and the second iterates the consecutive coefficient bound.

<a id="real-count"></a>
## Almost all roots of the monic quotient are nonreal at most orders

By the [unit-circle maximum estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#determinant) applied to \(Z^m S(Z+Z^{-1})\) with confluence order \(\lfloor\sqrt{2m}\rfloor\), one has
\[
                    \log\sup_{-2\le X\le2}|S(X)|\ll\sqrt m\log(2m).
\]
Its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(a\le\log2\).
Since \(S(2)\) is a nonzero integer, (3) is at least \(\tfrac23 4^{k-1}\) on \([-2,2-v]\) for \(v=4B_\epsilon u\).
Thus \(S\) cannot equal its Taylor section anywhere in this interval once \(k\ge A\sqrt m\log(2m)\) for an absolute \(A\).
Here \(v<4\) eventually for each fixed \(\epsilon\).

Below \(-2\), the difference \(F_k=(X-2)^k Q_k\) has at most \(k\) real roots with multiplicity, since its \(k\)-th derivative \(S^{(k)}\) has none there.
On \([2-v,\infty)\), \(F_k\) has the \(k\) roots at two in addition to all real roots of \(Q_k\) there, counted with multiplicity.
Rolle's theorem with multiplicities therefore bounds their number for \(Q_k\) by the number of roots of \(S^{(k)}\) in \([2-v,\infty)\).
The latter count is at most the number of roots of \(S\) in the same interval by repeated strict interlacing.
The [neutral endpoint count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#arcs) gives \(O_\epsilon(ma)\) such roots of \(S\), including \(T\).
In fact the neutral angular window has size \(O_\epsilon(\sqrt u)=O_\epsilon(a)\) and \(a/\sqrt{\log(2m)/m}\to\infty\) by the large-degree measure bound.
This proves the total real-root estimate since \(k\le K=O(ma)\).
Subtracting from \(m-k\) gives the count of nonreal roots.
For each fixed \(\epsilon\) the discarded lower range is \(o(K)\), again by the lower bound for \(a\).
Since \(a\to0\), the retained range has at least \((1-\delta)m\) nonreal roots for all sufficiently late inputs, and then letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves the assertion on the proportion of orders.

<a id="descent"></a>
## A critical-value test and an explicit descent

If all other conjugates of \(r_k\) are in \([-2,2]\), the [real trace descent](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#records) applied to its monic irreducible factor gives a smaller Salem number.
The quadratic alternative would have the integer trace \(r_k\in(2,T)\subset(2,5/2)\).
The degree of the new Salem number is at most twice the degree of the factor, and every root of that factor counts toward the real-root bound when the descent applies.
This proves the assertions on the degrees and the obstruction in the distinguished factor at a degree minimum.

At \(k=1\le K\) put \(b=S(2)<0\).
The number of nonreal roots of \(Q_1\) equals twice the number of local minima \(c\) of \(S\) with \(S(c)>b\).
Indeed all local maxima have positive value, all local minima have negative value, and the real roots of \(S(X)=b\) are counted on its consecutive monotone branches.
Each minimum above \(b\) loses two intersections, and equality retains a double root because every critical point is simple.
Removing the root at two does not change the nonreal count.
For even \(m\), \(S>0\) on the ray to the left of its smallest root, so there is no solution below \(-2\).
For odd \(m\), \(S\) is increasing there and the unique solution on that ray is below \(-2\) exactly when \(S(-2)>b\).
Consequently the whole first quotient gives the required real trace configuration exactly when every minimum is at most \(b\) and either \(m\) is even or \(S(-2)\le b\).
A separate factor could still produce descent if this whole-polynomial test fails.

For example take
\[
 S=X^4-3X^3-X^2+7X-3,\qquad Q_1=X^3-X^2-3X+1 .
\]
Here \(S=(X-2)Q_1-1\), \(S'(2)=-1\), and \(S\) has signs \(+,-,+,-,+\) at \(-2,-1,1,2,5/2\).
The cubic has signs \(-,+,-,-,+\) at the same five points.
Thus each polynomial has one root in \((2,5/2)\) and all remaining roots in \((-2,2)\).
The upper cubic root lies below the upper quartic root since \(S\) is negative there.
The cubic is irreducible by the rational-root test.
The quartic reduces modulo two to \(X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1\), which has neither a linear factor nor the only irreducible quadratic \(X^2+X+1\) as a factor.
Each trace polynomial therefore defines a Salem number of twice its degree, since at a neutral embedding the discriminant \(x^2-4\) of the lifting quadratic is negative in the real trace field.
This proves the claimed descent.
