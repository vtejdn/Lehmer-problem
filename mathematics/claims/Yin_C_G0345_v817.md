# Integral indices and complementary resultants under power projection

<a id="statement"></a>
## Allocation of the index at a divisor of the short exponent

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be a [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) with positive coprime exponents, and let \(h\mid k\).
Suppose the roots of \(P\) are simple and have distinct \(h\)-th powers, as holds eventually on every steep primitive sequence by the [torsion quotient result](Yin_C_G0345_v796.md#statement).
Take a monic integral factorization \(P=ST\) and write \(S_h(Y)=\prod_{S(z)=0}(Y-z^h)\), with the corresponding convention for \(T_h\).
In \(B_S=\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\) write \(x\) for the class of \(X\) and \(I_h(S)=[B_S:\mathbb Z[x^h]]\).
Put \(t=U-U^{-1}\), \(B(U)=(t^2-9)(t^2-16)\), and define \(D_1=1,D_2=-1,D_{r+2}=-D_{r+1}-B D_r\).
Then
\[
 I_h(S)^2\,\left|\frac{\operatorname{Res}(S_h,T_h)}{\operatorname{Res}(S,T)}\right|
       =\prod_{S(z)=0}|D_h(B(z^k))|.                                   \tag{1}
\]
The resultant quotient is an integer.
For even \(k\), squaring preserves the entire integral order of every factor and the absolute complementary resultant exactly.
For this even-\(k\) case and roots \(z,w\) of distinct rational irreducible factors, both \(z+w\) and, by reciprocity of the section, \(z+w^{-1}\) are algebraic units whenever those factors are reciprocal.
Thus all complementary gluing required by the [degree-dependent lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) survives squaring even when the individual resultant grows faster than exponentially in the small factor degree.

<a id="proof"></a>
## Rotating a root in its short-coordinate fiber

For a root \(z\) write \(U=z^k,V=z^l,a=(t-3)(t-4),b=(t+3)(t+4)\), so \(aV^2+V+b=0\) and \(ab=B\).
For each nonidentity \(h\)-th root \(\zeta\), put \(\eta=\zeta^l\).
Evaluation at \(\zeta z\) gives
\[
       P(\zeta z)=U^2V(\eta-1)\big(1+(\eta+1)aV\big).
\]
Put \(y=aV\) and \(y'=-1-y\), the roots of \(Y^2+Y+B\).
The product over \(\eta\ne1\) of \(1+(\eta+1)y=\eta y-y'\) equals \((y^h-y'^h)/(y-y')=D_h(B)\) up to sign, with the divided difference interpreted polynomially at coincident roots.
The map \(\zeta\mapsto\eta\) permutes the nonidentity roots and \(\prod_{\eta\ne1}|1-\eta|=h\).
The product of \(|U^2V|\) over \(S\) equals one since every root is an algebraic unit and \(S(0)=\pm1\).
Consequently the product of \(|P(\zeta z)|\) over these rotations and all roots of \(S\) is \(h^{\deg S}\) times the right side of (1).

In the same product, the diagonal differences contribute \(h^{\deg S}\).
For each unordered pair of distinct \(S\)-roots the combined absolute contribution is \(|(z^h-w^h)/(z-w)|^2\).
Their product is \(I_h(S)^2\) by the Vandermonde change of power basis.
Each cross pair with \(w\) a root of \(T\) contributes the same divided difference in absolute value without squaring.
Their product before absolute values is a rational algebraic integer by Galois invariance, and equals the resultant quotient.
This proves (1).
At \(h=2\) its right side is one, so both integral factors on the left are one.
Every cross sum is then an algebraic unit since all are integral and their product has absolute value one.
For even \(k\) the order equality also follows directly because \(X^{2k+l}\) agrees in \(B_S\) with a polynomial in \(X^2\), the exponent is odd, and \(x^2\) has integral inverse in \(\mathbb Z[x^2]\).
