# The energy before a large power sum, including every power or radical of a Salem number

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sharp lower bound and an obstruction at every index

For a monic polynomial \(P\) of degree \(D\) write \(p_j=\sum_{P(\alpha)=0}\alpha^j\), \(L=\log M(P)\), \(K=e^{2L}-1\), and \(E(q)=\sum_{1\le j<q}|p_j|^2\).
Fix \(\eta>0\), and consider positive integer indices with \(|p_q|\ge\eta D\).
The following estimates concern \(D\to\infty\).

* For every monic separable integral unit polynomial with \(0<L\le A\) bounded, at every \(q=o(D/\log(2D))\) one has
  \[
                            \frac{K E(q)}{|p_q|^2}\ge 1-o(1).                    \tag{1}
  \]
  The constant one is attained asymptotically on actual bounded Salem families.
  Equality in the limit determines the entire preceding sequence in squared norm by the exterior roots, as specified below.
* For irreducible nontorsion units with \(L\to0\) and \(r\) exterior conjugates satisfying
  \[
                     r=o\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{D\log(1/L)}}{\log(2D)}\right),         \tag{2}
  \]
  (1) holds at **every** such \(q\), with no upper bound on the index.
  In particular it holds throughout \(r=O(\sqrt D/\log(2D))\).
  Without any restriction on \(r\), (1) still holds for \(q\le B D\), \(B\) fixed, whenever \(LX/\log(2+X)\to0\) with \(X=q\log(2D)/D\).
  In particular every index through \(BD\) qualifies if \(L=o(\log\log(3D)/\log(2D))\).
* If a positive power of any conjugate of the unit is a positive power of a genuine Salem number, (1) holds at **every** such \(q\) along \(L\to0\), with **no restriction** on the number of exterior conjugates or on the two powers.
  This includes arbitrary complex radicals and root-of-unity multiples of radicals.
  Consequently none of these orbits can furnish a macroscopic power sum with \(E(q)=O(D^2)\) at shrinking total measure.

For a Salem number \(\sigma=e^a\) itself, of degree \(d\to\infty\), \(0<a\le A\), the exact asymptotic along every integer-step progression with \(0<t=ha\le A'\) bounded is
\[
             \frac{\sum_{1\le j<q}|p_{hj}|^2}{|p_{hq}|^2}
                       =\frac{1+o(1)}{e^{2t}-1}
        \qquad (h,q\ge1,\quad |p_{hq}|\ge\eta d).                               \tag{3}
\]
The step \(h\) and the time \(q\) may both vary.
In particular the ratio is asymptotic to \(1/(2ha)\) when \(ha\to0\).
Allowing unrestricted \(h\), an actual macroscopic power sum with preceding sampled energy \(O(d^2)\) requires \(ha\) bounded away from zero and \(q=O(\log d)\).
Conversely such a power sum with bounded preceding energy always exists on a suitable progression at this logarithmic scale.

<a id="filter"></a>
## The endpoint functional and its equality condition

Let
\[
 F(w)=\prod_{|\alpha|>1}\frac{1-\alpha w}{1-w/\bar\alpha}
       =1+\sum_{\ell\ge1}f_\ell w^\ell ,
\]
and put \(v_j=\sum_\alpha r(\alpha)^j\), where \(r(\alpha)=\alpha\) on the closed disk and \(r(\alpha)=1/\bar\alpha\) outside.
The [full Blaschke identity](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#filter) gives \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}|f_\ell|^2=K\) and
\[
      p_q+\sum_{\ell=1}^{q-1} f_\ell p_{q-\ell}
             =-q f_q+v_q+\sum_{\ell=1}^{q-1} f_\ell v_{q-\ell}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Also \(q|f_q|\le r e^L\), since the normalized finite Blaschke product has boundary derivative modulus of mean \(r\), as proved in the [derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#wiener).
For the short indices of the first assertion, the [reflected discriminant inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives \(\sum_{j\le q}|v_j|^2\ll_A qD\log(2D)=o(D^2)\).
This application uses only separability, monicity, integrality and the total measure.
Bounding \(q|f_q|\le q\sqrt K=o(D)\) here makes the right side of (4) \(o(|p_q|)\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on the left proves (1).

For the equality condition put \(x_\ell=p_{q-\ell}\) for \(1\le\ell<q\) and zero for all larger \(\ell\).
Whenever the right side of (4) is \(o(|p_q|)\), expansion of the square gives
\[
 \frac K{|p_q|^2}\sum_{\ell\ge1}
         \left|x_\ell+\frac{p_q}{K}\bar f_\ell\right|^2
                    = \frac{K E(q)}{|p_q|^2}-1+o(1).                          \tag{5}
\]
Thus limiting equality is equivalent to vanishing of this normalized squared distance in the short-index assertion.
There is an exact two-sided counterpart for the pure exterior sequence \(s_j=\sum_{|\alpha|>1}\alpha^j\), defined for every integer \(j\).
Indeed \(\sum_{\ell\ge0} f_\ell s_{q-\ell}=0\) with \(f_0=1\), by evaluating \(F\) at each \(1/\alpha\), and the negative-index tail is square summable for each fixed polynomial.
The same squared-distance identity therefore holds without an error for \(x_\ell=s_{q-\ell}\) and \(p_q\) replaced by \(s_q\ne0\), now using the full squared sum of \(x\).
For a single positive exterior root \(e^a\), one has \(-\bar f_\ell/K=e^{-a\ell}\).

<a id="all-times"></a>
## Removing the restriction on the index

Consider now the second assertion, with \(r\ge1\) and \(L\to0\).
It suffices to check subsequences on which \(K E(q)/|p_q|^2\) is bounded.
Pass further so that \(|p_q|/(D(1+\sqrt{Kq}))\) either tends to infinity or stays bounded.
In the first case \(|v_j|\le D\), the derivative bound after (4), and Cauchy–Schwarz make its right side \(o(|p_q|)\), proving (1) and (5).

In the second case each preceding pure exterior sum has modulus
\[
 |s_j|\le |p_j|+D\ll D(1+K^{-1/2}+\sqrt q),\qquad 1\le j<q.                    \tag{6}
\]
Let \(b\) be the logarithmic house, so \(b\ge L/r\).
If \(q>2r+2\), divide the exterior roots by one of largest modulus and apply the [last \(r\) consecutive powers inequality (3)](Yin_C_G0345_v143.md#powers) to the block \(q-r,\ldots,q-1\).
For some \(j\) in it one obtains \(|s_j|\ge\exp((q-r)b-Cr\log(2qr))\), without a separation hypothesis on phases.
Combining with (6) gives
\[
                         q\ll \frac{r^2}{L}\log(2D).                            \tag{7}
\]
Indeed the [weak Dobrowolski bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(L^{-1}\ll\log^3(2D)\), so first \(q\ll (r^2/L)\log(2Dq)\).
Using \(r\le D\) and monotonicity of \(q/\log(2Dq)\) first gives \(q\le D^3\) for large \(D\), and then (7).
The range \(q\le2r+2\) satisfies (7) directly.

For \(1\le q\le D^3\), write \(X=q\log(2D)/D\).
The reflected energy obeys
\[
                 D^{-2}\sum_{j\le q}|v_j|^2
                             \ll 1+\frac{X}{\log(2+X)}.                         \tag{8}
\]
To see this put \(x=1/q\), \(H(x)=\sum_{j\ge1} e^{-xj}|v_j|^2/j\).
For \(q\le D\) monotonicity and the discriminant proof at smoothing \(1/D\) give \(H(x)\ll D\log(2D)\).
For \(D<q\le D^3\) use the very same [discriminant comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#energy) with \(\rho=e^{-1/(2q)}\).
Its costs are \(2(D-1)L+D(D-1)/(2q)+D\log(1/(1-e^{-1/q}))\ll D\log(2D)\).
The [integrated positive Poisson kernel inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#energy) then gives
\[
       x\log U\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-xj}|v_j|^2\ll H(x)+Ux D^2
\]
for \(2\le U\le q\).
For \(q\ge2\) take \(U=\max(2,X)\), permitted for large \(D\); the case \(q=1\) is immediate.
This proves (8).

Set \(Z=r^2\log^2(2D)/D=o(\log(1/L))\) by (2).
Equation (7) gives \(qL/D\ll Z/\log(2D)=o(1)\) and \(X\ll Z/L\).
In particular \(L X/\log(2+X)\to0\): when \(Z\le\sqrt L\) use the numerator alone, and otherwise \(\log(2+Z/L)\gg\log(1/L)\) and \(x/\log(2+x)\) is increasing.
The conjugates of any one \(q\)-th power occur equally often in the full list, and its absolute height is \(qL/D\to0\).
Applying the [compressed-degree reflected estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v149.md#powers) at this height gives \(v_q/D=o(1)\).
Specifically the normalized bound there is \(O(\sqrt{h+\log(2e)/e})\) for power degree \(e\) and height \(h\to0\); the nontorsion degree lower bound gives \(e\to\infty\).
Finally (2) gives \(r=o(D)\), and (8) gives \(K\sum_{j\le q}|v_j/D|^2=o(1)\).
All three terms on the right of (4) are now \(o(|p_q|)\).
This proves (1), and (5) also holds on every subsequence attaining equality in (1).
For the assertion without a count bound at \(q\le BD\), use (8) directly, \(qL/D\le BL\to0\) for the endpoint reflected moment, and \(q|f_q|\le BD\sqrt K=o(D)\).
These again make the right side of (4) negligible under the specified condition on \(X\).

## The exact history along a Salem power progression

For a bounded Salem input let \(w_k=p_k-e^{ak}\).
It is the neutral power sum plus \(e^{-ak}\), of modulus at most \(d-1\).
For \(j\le d\), its difference from the full reflected power sum at \(j\) is just \(-e^{-aj}\).
The discriminant bound therefore gives \(\sum_{k\le N}|w_k|^2\ll_A N d\log(2d)\) for \(1\le N\le d\).
The positive measure bound gives \(a^{-1}\ll_A\log^3(2d)\).
If \(tq\le3\log d\), then \(hq\ll_A\log^4(2d)\) and the sum of \(|w_{hj}|^2\) over all \(1\le j\le q\) is \(o(d^2)\).
In particular \(e^{tq}\ge(\eta-o(1))d\) at the given macroscopic index.
The exact geometric sum before that index is \((e^{2tq}-e^{2t})/(e^{2t}-1)\).
Since \(t\le A'\), its square root is \(\gg_{\eta,A'}d\) and \(|p_{hq}|/e^{tq}\to1\).
The triangle inequality in squared-summable sequences proves (3).
If \(tq>3\log d\), the trivial sum \(q d^2\) for the squared errors divided by \(e^{2tq}/(e^{2t}-1)\) is \(O_{A'}((tq)d^2e^{-2tq})=o(1)\).
The endpoint error is also negligible there, completing the uniform proof.
Taking \(h=1\) and \(q=\lceil\log d/a\rceil+1\), for example on the [actual Salem family tending to the plastic number](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family), realizes (1) with ratio tending to one in its short-index range.

For unrestricted \(h\), if a macroscopic endpoint has preceding energy at most \(B d^2\), a subsequence with \(ha\to0\) is impossible by (3).
For \(q\ge2\) the penultimate bound \(|p_{h(q-1)}|\le\sqrt B\,d\) gives \(e^{ha(q-1)}\le(1+\sqrt B)d\), so \(q=O(\log d)\) as claimed.
Conversely choose any fixed \(c>0\), \(h=\lceil c/a\rceil\) and \(q=\lceil\log d/(ha)\rceil\).
Then \(c\le ha\le c+A\), \(d\le e^{haq}\le e^{c+A}d\), and \(hq\ll_{A,c}\log^4(2d)\).
The error estimate above gives \(|p_{hq}|\ge(1-o(1))d\), \(E\) on this progression is \(O_{A,c}(d^2)\), and \(q\asymp_{A,c}\log d\).
This construction uses a power of positive logarithmic measure even if the original Salem measure shrinks.

## Compressing the actual minimal polynomial of a complex Salem radical

Choose the embedding so that \(\alpha^k=\sigma^h>1\) with positive integers \(h,k\).
By [degree preservation under Salem powers](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers), \(U=\mathbb Q(\sigma^h)=\mathbb Q(\sigma)\subseteq\mathbb R\) has degree \(d\) and is contained in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Put \(\beta=|\alpha|>1,\ m_0=[U(\beta):U]\) and \(m=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):U]\).
Over \(U\) the conjugates of \(\beta\) differ from it by \(k\)-th roots of unity, and their product is real.
Thus \(\beta^{m_0}\in U\) by taking the absolute value of this norm in the chosen real embedding.
The minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) over \(U\) is \(X^{m_0}-\beta^{m_0}\) by its degree.
In particular \(\beta^j\in U\) for a positive integer \(j\) only if \(m_0\mid j\), by applying all the \(m_0\) embeddings.
This specializes the [one-label radical norm](Yin_C_G0345_v116.md#norm) using the real embedding.

The positive unit \(\gamma=\beta^{m_0}\) is itself a Salem generator of \(U\).
Indeed \(\gamma^k=\sigma^{h m_0}\) generates \(U\), so \(\gamma\) has one positive exterior conjugate, one interior conjugate and \(d-2\) neutral conjugates.
The real-coefficient minimal polynomial shares a nonreal neutral zero with its reciprocal, by complex conjugation and inversion.
Irreducibility over the rationals then gives the inverse root \(\gamma^{-1}\), and the monic unit polynomial is reciprocal.
Here \(d\ge4\) since \(\sigma\) is a genuine Salem number.

Let \(H(X)=\sum_{i=0}^m c_i X^{m-i}\), \(c_0=1\), be the relative minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(U\).
All its roots have modulus \(\beta\) in the chosen embedding and are closed under ordinary conjugation.
Hence \(|c_m|=\beta^m\in U\), and comparison under \(z\mapsto\beta^2/z\) gives \(c_{m-i}=c_m c_i/\beta^{2i}\).
Thus \(m_0\mid m\) and \(c_i\ne0\) implies \(m_0\mid2i\).
Set \(M=m_0/\gcd(m_0,2)\).
We have \(H(X)=G(X^M)\) over \(U\).
The norm polynomial of \(H\) from \(U\) to \(\mathbb Q\) is the actual minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\), because \(U\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and all \(dm\) embeddings are present.
This polynomial is therefore \(P_\lambda(X^M)\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\lambda=\alpha^M\): the coefficient polynomial is rational monic and irreducible since its substitution is irreducible.

Put \(t=m/M\).
The compressed degree is \(D'=dt\), with exactly \(t\) exterior roots all of logarithmic modulus \(u=M\log\beta\), lying over the positive exterior embedding of \(U\).
Indeed the \(d\) base embeddings send \(\sigma^h\) to one exterior, one interior and \(d-2\) neutral values and every relative root has the modulus prescribed by its \(k\)-th power.
The full and compressed measures coincide under substitution, with \(L=tu\), and \(u\ge\frac12\log\gamma\).
If \(L\to0\) then \(\log\gamma\to0\), \(d\to\infty\), and Dobrowolski for the genuine Salem generator gives \(u\gg\log^{-3}(2d)\).
Consequently \(t\ll\log^3(2d)\), which meets (2) at degree \(D'\).
The original degree \(md\) and the original exterior count \(m\) can be much larger.

The power sum of \(P_\lambda(X^M)\) vanishes unless \(M\mid j\), and at \(j=Mi\) it is \(M\) times the \(i\)-th power sum of \(P_\lambda\), by summing a full set of \(M\)-th roots over each compressed conjugate.
Thus the normalized endpoint and the normalized preceding energy at each nonzero index agree exactly with the compressed quantities, including the factors of the degree and its square.
The unrestricted-index result at \(D'\) gives (1) for the actual radical orbit.
