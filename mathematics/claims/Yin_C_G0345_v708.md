# Recovering simultaneous large prime radicals from finite fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree restriction and a finite search independent of the seed interval

Let \(G_a=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), with integer seed \(a\ge3\), delay \(n\ge7\), and sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Write \(r>1\) for its exterior Salem root, \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(P_a=G_a/U\) for its minimal polynomial.
Fix primes \(n<p<q\).
If both \(r^{1/p}\) and \(r^{1/q}\) belong to \(K\), then the positive root \(\gamma=r^{1/(pq)}\) belongs to \(K\) and satisfies
\[
                    d\ge56,\qquad n\ge55,\qquad p\ge59,\qquad
                    1<\gamma<59^{1/61}<1.070.                         \tag{1}
\]
The degree restriction uses the published finite calculation of Mossinghoff, Rhin and Wu at the scope recorded in the [source inspection](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree).

For each \(n,\epsilon,p,q\), all simultaneous radicals can be determined without searching an interval of integer seeds.
For each possible residue \(a\bmod p\), enumerate monic degree-\(d\) polynomials \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) whose \(q\)-th root powers, counted with multiplicity, give the roots of \(\overline P_a\).
Each such \(T\) admits at most six candidates for the integral minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\).
A single integer-polynomial test decides whether each candidate gives a simultaneous radical and recovers its seed.

When the multiplicative order \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)>d\), there is exactly one \(T\) for each residue of \(a\).
There are then at most \(6p\) candidates at fixed \(n,\epsilon,p,q\), regardless of the size of the seed bound.
For \(p=59,q=61\), this applies to every remaining delay \(55\le n\le58\) and both signs.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Small primes and the published degree bound

Bézout's identity applied to the coprime indices gives \(\gamma\in K\) by taking a product of integral powers of the two field radicals.
Since \(\gamma^{pq}=r\), their fields agree, the exterior root has a unique preimage among its conjugates, and \(\gamma\) is Salem of degree \(d\) and multiplicative Mahler measure \(\gamma\).
By the [strict first-radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v702.md#statement), \(\gamma<p^{1/q}\).
For \(p\ge17\) the decreasing function \(\log t/(t+2)\) on \(t\ge17\) bounds this above by \(17^{1/19}<7/6\).
For \(p=13\) or \(p=11,q\ge17\), it is at most \(13^{1/17}<7/6\).

It remains to check \(p=11,q=13\), where \(n\le10\).
Write \(\beta=r^{1/11}\), \(L=n-1\le9\), \(A=(X^L-\epsilon)/U\), and \(D=d-2\le L\).
In the [matched-lift estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#norm), \(\beta^{22-D}<L^{D+2}\), whence \(\beta<9^{11/13}<7\).
In the other case take \(\beta\ge2\), the smaller values already satisfying the desired bound.
At a neutral conjugate \(w\) of \(\beta\), the angular comparison there gives
\[
               11|w^L-\epsilon|\ \ge\ 22\sin(\pi/11)-\pi/3>5.
\]
Indeed \(\sin t\ge t-t^3/6\) in this range, so the last expression is at least \(5\pi/3-\pi^3/363>5\), using \(31/10<\pi<22/7\).
For \(U\ne1\) the [four cofactor estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v702.md#prime) give \(|U(w)|\le2\), \(A(1/\beta)\ge2/3\), and \(|N A(\beta)|=1\).
Thus \(\beta<(22/5)(3/2)^{2/D}\le(22/5)\sqrt{3/2}<7\) since \(D\ge4\).
For \(U=1\) they instead give \(A(1/\beta)>1\), \(|N A(\beta)|\le4\), and \(\beta<(11/5)4^{1/D}<7\).
This proves \(\gamma<7^{1/13}<7/6\) also for the last pair.
The rational-power comparisons follow by raising to the displayed integer powers.

The degree-ten Lehmer polynomial has value \(-13481789/60466176\) at \(7/6\) and has a unique exterior root, so its measure exceeds \(7/6\).
The [published sub-Lehmer degree bound](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) now gives \(d\ge56\).
Since \(d\le n+1\), it follows that \(n\ge55,\ p\ge59\).
Using \(q\ge p+2\) and the same decreasing function gives \(\gamma<59^{1/61}<107/100\), proving (1).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Enumerating the reduced polynomials

Write \(S\) for the monic irreducible minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\), and \(V^{[j]}\) for the monic polynomial obtained by raising the roots of a monic polynomial \(V\) to their \(j\)-th powers with multiplicity.
Since \(S^{[pq]}=P_a\) and \(p\)-th powering over the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_p\) preserves every Frobenius-invariant multiset, \(\overline S^{[q]}=\overline P_a\).
The polynomial \(\overline P_a\) depends only on \(n,\epsilon,a\bmod p\) because the [linear cofactor](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) is monic and independent of \(a\).
All its roots are nonzero.
Every \(T=\overline S\) with the required image divides \(\overline P_a(X^q)\), including all multiplicities since \(q\ne p\).
Thus one can generate the \(T\) by factoring this last polynomial over \(\mathbb F_p\), enumerating its monic degree-\(d\) divisors and retaining exactly those with \(T^{[q]}=\overline P_a\).

Suppose \(\operatorname{ord}_q(p)>d\).
A root of any possible \(T\) has degree \(f\le d\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and raising to the \(q\)-th power is a bijection on \(\mathbb F_{p^f}^{\times}\) because \(q\nmid p^f-1\).
Moreover its image generates the same finite field, since the inverse map there is itself an integral power.
Hence each root \(z\) of \(\overline P_a\) of degree \(f\) has exactly one admissible preimage, namely \(z^{\,q^{-1}\bmod (p^f-1)}\), and retains its multiplicity.
These choices commute with Frobenius and define one monic \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
At the first possible prime pair \(59,61\), the powers of \(59\) modulo \(61\) at exponents \(12,20,30\) are \(9,47,60\), respectively.
Since \(60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5\), the order is \(60>d\) whenever \(n<59\).

<a id="decoding"></a>
## Integral lifts and the seed test

Let \(s_j\) be the integral power sums of \(S\), and write \(1,b_1,\ldots,b_d\) for its descending coefficients.
For \(1\le j\le d/2\), the Salem signature and \(d\le n+1\le p<q\) give
\[
                |s_j|\le d-2+\gamma^j+\gamma^{-j}
                        <d-1+\sqrt p=:D_p .
\]
Indeed \(\gamma^q=r^{1/p}<p\).
With \(T\) known and the earlier sums and coefficients recovered, Newton's identity specifies \(s_j\) in one residue class modulo \(jp\), because
\[
                    s_j=-j b_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1} b_i s_{j-i}
\]
and \(b_j\bmod p\) is fixed.
The interval \((-D_p,D_p)\) has length less than \(3p\), since \(d\le p\) and \(2(\sqrt p-1)<p\).
It contains at most three admissible values at \(j=1\), at most two at \(j=2\), and at most one at every later index.
This enumerates at most six viable initial halves, and reciprocity recovers their remaining coefficients.
All operations can use integer comparisons by squaring after separating the integer and square-root parts of the bound.

For each recovered polynomial \(S_0\), compute \(V=U S_0^{[pq]}\) over \(\mathbb Z\), for example by a resultant.
Its coefficient at \(X^n\) fixes \(a=-[X^n]V\).
Test \(a\ge3\), its residue, and the identity \(V=G_a\).
If this test passes, \(S_0^{[pq]}=P_a\) is irreducible of degree \(d\).
Each rational factor of \(S_0\) would contribute a powered factor of the same degree, so \(S_0\) is itself irreducible.
The unique exterior root of \(P_a\) has a unique preimage among the roots of \(S_0\), counting multiplicity.
Its preimage is real by conjugation and positive since \(pq\) is odd.
The fields generated by this root and its \(pq\)-th power therefore agree by the degree comparison, proving it is the required simultaneous field radical.
Conversely any genuine simultaneous radical has the residue, power sums and powered polynomial used above, so it passes the test.
The algorithm can be applied to the [effective ranges for the prime pair](Yin_C_G0345_v705.md#cutoff).
