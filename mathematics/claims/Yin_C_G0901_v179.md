# What happens to denominators under the fractional linear map at the trace pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## Integrality and multiplicative dependence on the same rational orbit

Let \(q\ge2\), \(p=q+1\), \(L=p+q\), \(d=2D\), with \(q,D\) integers and \(D\ge2\).
Let \(F\in\mathbb Q[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(d\).
Suppose its only roots off the unit circle are \(T,T^{-1}\), with \(1<T<p/q\), and \(q^dF(p/q)=L^2\).
Put \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\), \(E=p-qT\), and let \(A\) be the positive leading coefficient of the primitive integer minimum of \(T\), so \(A=1\) precisely for an integral \(T\).
Write \(A_q,A_p,A_o\) for the parts of \(A\) supported on the primes of \(q\), of \(p\), and on all remaining primes, respectively.

For every pair of integers \(a,b\) one has
\[
 m(T^aY^b)\ \ge\ |a\log T+b\log Y|
       +|a+b|\log A_q+|a-b|\log A_p+|a|\log A_o ,                    \tag{1}
\]
where \(m\) denotes the total logarithmic Mahler measure of the individual number.
In particular \(A=1\) if and only if \(T,Y\) are multiplicatively dependent.
In that case \(T=\beta^u,\ Y=\beta^v\) for a unit \(\beta>1\) and coprime integers \(1\le u<v\).
Thus the rational single-prime examples at this pole cannot satisfy the true power relation at the distinguished real embedding.

There is a test for arbitrary products, even when their net contribution in (1) at a prime vanishes.
For a prime \(r\) put \(\alpha=v_r(A)\), \(e=v_r(pq)\) and \(l=v_r(L)\).
If \(r\mid pq\) and \(\alpha>de/2\), or if \(r\nmid pq\) and \(\alpha>(d-2)l\), no nonidentity element of the group generated by \(T,Y\) is an \(r\)-adic unit at **all** places over \(r\).
The same is true for all nontorsion radicals of its elements, including root-of-unity twists.
In particular their primitive measures are at least two.
For a fixed degree of any of the [joint one-prime models](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement), the minimum total log measure over all nontrivial integer monomials in \(T,Y\) tends to infinity as the prescribed denominator exponent tends to infinity.
This last divergence concerns integer monomials; for their radicals only the nonunit conclusion is asserted.

<a id="height"></a>
## Measuring one finite place across the conjugates

Work in \(K=\mathbb Q(T)\), of degree \(d\).
For each \(r\) sum over its \(d\) embeddings into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_r\), and write \(v(r)=1\) for the extended valuation.
The list of conjugates is invariant under inversion, which also inverts \(Y\).
For the subset \(I=\{\sigma:v(\sigma T)<0\}\) we have
\[
  \sum_{I}-v(\sigma T)=v_r(A).
\]
This follows by multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean Gauss norm of the primitive integer minimum, whose Gauss norm at \(r\) is one.
The \(r\)-part of \(d h(T^aY^b)\) is \(\log r\) times the sum of the negative parts of the valuations of the monomial.
Since the embeddings in \(I\) and their inverses are disjoint, this sum is at least \(\big|\sum_I v(\sigma(T^aY^b))\big|\).

The norm of \(E\) is \(L^2\), as is that of \(E'=pT-q\) since \(E'=T(p-q/T)\) and \(N(T)=1\).
For \(r\mid q\), one has \(v(E)=0\) on every embedding outside \(I\), and \(v(E')=v(T)\) on \(I\).
As \(r\nmid L\), \(\sum_I v(E)=0\) and therefore \(\sum_I v(Y)=-\alpha\).
For \(r\mid p\), \(v(E')=0\) outside \(I\) and \(v(E)=v(T)\) on \(I\), giving \(\sum_I v(Y)=\alpha\).
For \(r\nmid pq\), both \(E,E'\) have valuation \(v(T)\) on \(I\), so \(\sum_I v(Y)=0\).
These sums yield the three finite terms in (1).

At infinity the real pair contributes \(\lvert a\log T+b\log Y\rvert\) to \(d h(T^aY^b)\).
All other images have modulus one for both coordinates since the fractional linear map preserves that circle.
If the distinguished monomial is not one, one of the real pair is its unique image of modulus greater than one among the \(d\) embeddings.
A proper subfield would repeat that image, so the monomial has degree \(d\) and \(d h\) equals its total log measure.
If it is one both heights vanish and the same lower bound holds.
Since \(Y>T>1\) (the numerator of \(Y-T\) is \(q(T^2-1)>0\)), a nontrivial relation at the real place must have both exponents nonzero and \(|a|>|b|\).
In (1) all three absolute exponent multipliers are then positive, forcing \(A=1\).
Conversely, for integral \(T\) the trace \(w=T+T^{-1}-2\) is an algebraic integer with one positive embedding and all others in \((-4,0)\).
The relative quadratic norm of \(E\) is \(\delta=1-pqw\) and its trace-field norm is \(L^2\).
The product of the other conjugates gives \(L^2/\delta\) integral.
The [integral graph-coordinate criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) gives the stated common powers, with positive sign here since \(Y>1\).

<a id="remaining"></a>
## When cancellation of the finite contribution is possible

If \(r\mid q\) and \(A\) contains \(r^\alpha\), \(\alpha>0\), only exponent pairs with \(a=-b\) could give a unit at every \(r\)-adic embedding.
Put \(e=v_r(q)\), and for \(\sigma\in I\) write \(s=-v(\sigma T)>0\).
There \(v(Y/T)=-v(E)\), where \(v(E)=e-s<0\) if \(s>e\), is zero if \(s<e\), and is nonnegative if \(s=e\).
The valuations \(v(E)\) sum to zero on \(I\).
The contributions of \(I\) and its inverses to the finite height of \(Y/T\), divided by \(\log r\), therefore total
\[
           \sum_I|v(E)|=2\sum_I(s-e)_+\ \ge\ 2\alpha-de ,
\]
using \(|I|\le d/2\).
For \(r\mid p\) only \(a=b\) can cancel in (1); now \(v(YT)=v(E')\) on \(I\), the sum there is zero, and the same proof gives at least \(2\alpha-de\) for \(YT\).

If \(r\nmid pq\), \(Y\) is a local unit whenever \(T\) is not one, and \(a=0\) is the only possibility left by (1) if \(\alpha>0\).
Over the embeddings with \(v(T)=0\) one has \(v(E),v(E')\ge0\).
Their first valuations sum to \(2l+\alpha\), since the embeddings in \(I\) contribute \(-\alpha\) to the norm of \(E\) and the embeddings with positive valuation of \(T\) contribute zero.
The identity \(qE'+pE=L\) gives \(\min(v(E),v(E'))\le l\) at each of these unit embeddings.
The negative parts for \(Y=E'/E\) on them therefore sum to at least \(2l+\alpha-dl=\alpha-(d-2)l\).
This proves the thresholds for every exponent pair, and taking a root or multiplying by torsion preserves a nonzero local valuation.

For \(\alpha\) above the threshold, every nonzero exponent pair thus costs at least
\(\min(\alpha,2\alpha-de)\log r\) when \(r\mid pq\), or \((\alpha-(d-2)l)\log r\) when \(r\nmid pq\).
Each bound tends to infinity with \(\alpha\) at fixed \(p,q,d,r\).
The single-prime models have unbounded available denominator exponent at each of their admissible fixed degrees; this establishes the claimed uniform divergence.
