# Sparse inputs with a saturated one-sided Fourier projection

<a id="statement"></a>
## Support dispersion and signed differences

Use Haar probability \(\tau\) on the unit circle.
Let \(W\) range over analytic inner functions, with \(t=|W(0)|=e^{-m}\) and \(m\downarrow0\).
Let \(Q=\sum_{j\in S}q_jX^j\) be an integral Laurent polynomial with support \(S\) in the strictly negative integers and \(n=|S|\).
Write \(P_+\) for projection onto the nonnegative Fourier modes.
Suppose for some fixed \(0<x<1/2\) that
\[
 mn\to x,\qquad m\sum_{j\in S}(q_j^2-1)\to0,\qquad
 \sqrt m\|P_+(WQ)\|_2\to\sqrt2(1-e^{-x}).                           \tag{1}
\]
Put \(d\gamma=|W-W(0)|^2\,d\tau/(1-t^2)\), a probability with moments \(\widehat\gamma(g)=\int z^{-g}\,d\gamma\).
Suppose for every positive integer sequence \(h\) with \(mh\to0\) that every weak limit of the images of \(\gamma\) under \(z\mapsto z^h\) is at most \(2\tau\) as a measure.

For any fixed \(C>0\), every arithmetic progression of at most \(C/m\) terms and positive step \(h\) with \(mh\to0\) contains \(o(n)\) points of \(S\), uniformly in its initial position.
In particular the span of \(S\), multiplied by \(m\), tends to infinity.

Write \(e_j=\operatorname{sign}(q_j)\) on \(S\), and zero elsewhere.
For \(g>0\) let \(c(g)=\sum_j e_j e_{j+g}\), the difference between the numbers of equally and oppositely signed pairs at that lag.
Set
\[
 b=\left(\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}\right)^2,\qquad
 0<\beta<b,\qquad \alpha=\tfrac12 x\beta^2(b-\beta).
\]
Let \(\mathcal G\) consist of the positive lags for which
\[
 |\widehat\gamma(g)|\ge\beta,\qquad
             c(g)\operatorname{Re}\widehat\gamma(g)\ge\alpha n.       \tag{2}
\]
After removing any fixed finite number of progressions of the sizes and steps above from \(\mathcal G\), the remaining set \(G\) satisfies
\[
 \liminf\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{g\in G}c(g)\operatorname{Re}\widehat\gamma(g)
        \ge\frac{b-\beta}{4},\qquad
 \frac{b-\beta}{4}\le\liminf\frac{|G|}{n}\le
         \limsup\frac{|G|}{n}\le\frac{1}{2x\beta^2}.                 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently a positive proportion of the signed pairs occurs at linearly many distinct lags, each with \(|c(g)|\ge\alpha n\).
For every fixed \(C\) one may remove all \(g\le C/m\).
The sum \(\sum_{g\in G}c(g)^2\) has lower limit after division by \(n^3\) at least \(\alpha^2(b-\beta)/4\).
The ordinary additive energy of \(S\), which counts ordered equal differences, dominates this sum.

Both ends of every [balanced quotient](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#division) at the [unrestricted energy value](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement) satisfy these conditions with \(x=(\log2)/2\).
Then \(b=(1-1/\sqrt2)^2/x^2>2/\pi\), so \(\beta\) may be chosen above \(2/\pi\).
The one-ended hypotheses admit an [exact model](#model) for every \(x\) above.

<a id="angular"></a>
## Sliding blocks of Fourier coefficients

Fix a positive integer \(B\) and any admissible step \(h\) with \(mh\to0\).
The Gram matrix of \(1,z^h,\ldots,z^{(B-1)h}\) in \(L^2(\gamma)\) has operator norm at most \(2+o(1)\).
Indeed, on any subsequence take a further weak limit of the pushed probabilities.
The entries then converge to the Gram matrix of the first \(B\) monomials for a measure dominated by \(2\tau\), whose quadratic form is at most twice the sum of the squared coefficients.
The squared sum of the inner products of these monomials with any unit vector is therefore at most \(2+o(1)\).
Testing against \(z^{-a}\) gives, uniformly in all integers \(a\),
\[
                 \sum_{j=0}^{B-1}|\widehat\gamma(a+jh)|^2\le2+o(1).
\]
Tile any progression \(I\) of at most \(C/m\) terms of step \(h\) by such blocks, extending its final block if needed.
Since \(mn\to x\), the upper limit of \(n^{-1}\sum_{g\in I}|\widehat\gamma(g)|^2\) is at most \(2C/(xB)\).
Letting \(B\to\infty\) and then using Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
       \sum_{g\in I}|\widehat\gamma(g)|^2=o(n),\qquad
       \sum_{g\in I}|\widehat\gamma(g)|=o(n).                        \tag{4}
\]
The same holds for a fixed finite union with independently varying initial positions and admissible steps.
At a single subcritical lag \(h\), the angular hypothesis also gives \(\limsup|\widehat\gamma(h)|\le2/\pi\).
Indeed, for any weak limit \(\lambda\le2\tau\), project its first moment onto its own phase and bound the real part by integrating twice the positive part of the rotated cosine against Haar measure.

<a id="deletion"></a>
## Deleting coefficients in an arithmetic progression

For \(S'\subset S\), write \(Q'\) for the restriction to \(S'\) and \(k=|S'|\).
The [one-row contraction estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#compression) gives, uniformly in the subset,
\[
             \sqrt m\|P_+(WQ')\|_2\le\sqrt2(1-t^k)+o(1).             \tag{5}
\]
To see this, compress multiplication by \(W\) to the occupied positions of \(Q'\) in increasing order.
Write \(z_i^2\) for the squared norm of the first \(i\) inputs minus that of the outputs at the same positions.
Each prefix is a triangular contraction, so \(0\le z_i^2\le\|Q\|_2^2\).
With \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\), the hypotheses give \(s^2\|Q\|_2^2\to2x<1\).
Thus \(sz_{i-1}\le t|q_j|\) for each occupied input for all sufficiently small \(m\).
The cited row inequality, squared as in the [integer defect estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#defect), gives \(z_i\le t z_{i-1}+s|q_j|\).
Starting with \(z_0=0\), iteration bounds \(z_k\) by \(s(1-t^k)/(1-t)+s\sum_{j\in S}(|q_j|-1)\).
The sum is \(o(1/m)\) by integrality, and \(\sqrt m\,s/(1-t)\to\sqrt2\).
The occupied outputs are all at negative positions, so \(\|P_+(WQ')\|_2\le z_k\), proving (5).

Now let \(I\) be a progression as in the statement and let \(Q_I\) contain its selected coefficients.
Since \(\sum_{j\in S}(|q_j|-1)=o(n)\), the polynomial obtained by changing these coefficients to their signs differs from \(Q_I\) by \(o(n)\) in supremum norm.
For the resulting signed polynomial its squared \(L^2(\gamma)\) norm is at most \(n\sum_{g\in I-I}|\widehat\gamma(g)|=o(n^2)\), since every multiplicity is at most \(n\) and \(I-I\) has at most \(2C/m\) terms of step \(h\).
Consequently \(\|Q_I\|_{L^2(\gamma)}=o(n)\).
The constant multiplier \(W(0)\) contributes only negative frequencies, so
\[
 \sqrt m\|P_+(WQ_I)\|_2\le\sqrt{m(1-t^2)}\,\|Q_I\|_{L^2(\gamma)}
       =o(1).
\]
On deleting \(I\), the limiting projected norm in (1) is unchanged.
Apply (5) to the remaining \(k=n-|S\cap I|\) inputs.
It forces \(mk\to x\), since \(mk\le mn\to x\) and \(u\mapsto1-e^{-u}\) is strictly increasing.
This proves the support assertion, including uniformity by taking a sequence of offending positions.
If the span times \(m\) stayed bounded on a subsequence, all the inputs would lie in one such interval of step one.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Positive contributions from many distinct lags

For \(T=\sum_{j\in S}e_j X^j\), replacing \(Q\) by \(T\) changes its \(L^2(\gamma)\) norm by at most \(o(n)\).
Using (1) and \(\|P_+(WQ)\|_2\le\sqrt{1-t^2}\|Q\|_{L^2(\gamma)}\) therefore gives
\[
  \liminf \frac{1}{n^2}\int |T|^2\,d\gamma\ge b,\qquad
  \int |T|^2\,d\gamma=n+2\sum_{g>0}c(g)\operatorname{Re}\widehat\gamma(g).
\]
By unimodularity of \(W\), its positive Fourier coefficients give
\(\widehat\gamma(g)=-\overline{W(0)}[X^g]W/(1-t^2)\) for \(g>0\).
Parseval thus gives
\[
       \sum_{g>0}|\widehat\gamma(g)|^2
            =\frac{t^2}{1-t^2}\sim\frac{n}{2x}.
\]
In particular the number of lags with \(|\widehat\gamma(g)|\ge\beta\) is at most \((1/(2x\beta^2)+o(1))n\).
The lags below that threshold contribute at most \(\beta n(n-1)/2\) to the sum of products, since \(\sum_{g>0}|c(g)|\le n(n-1)/2\).
Among the remaining lags, those failing the second condition of (2) contribute at most \((\alpha/(2x\beta^2)+o(1))n^2\).
An excluded finite union of progressions contributes at most \(n\sum_{g\in I}|\widehat\gamma(g)|=o(n^2)\) by (4), also after restricting to \(\mathcal G\).
Subtracting these upper bounds proves the first inequality of (3) by the choice of \(\alpha\).
Each positive contribution is at most \(n\), giving the lower bound on the count.
Every retained \(|c(g)|\ge\alpha n\), which proves the signed energy bound.

<a id="balanced"></a>
## The two ends of an unrestricted extremizer

Suppose \(P\) is monic and integral with constant coefficient of modulus one and no root on the unit circle, \(m=\log M(P)>0\), and the integral Laurent numerator \(A\) is indivisible by \(P\).
Suppose \(m\to0\) and \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to K=5/2+\log2-2\sqrt2\).
Use the [balanced division and its factors](Yin_C_G0345_v638.md#projections) and the [complementary projection](Yin_C_G0345_v657.md#decomposition) \(D\), with inner functions \(U,V\).
The functions \(DQ_-\) and \(DQ_+\) have strictly negative and nonnegative Fourier support respectively.
Indeed \(DQ_-=Q_--\bar U P_-(UQ_-)\), and \(DQ_+=Q_+-V P_+(\bar VQ_+)\).
Their norms are \(\|P_+(UQ_-)\|_2\) and \(\|P_-(\bar VQ_+)\|_2\).
Under the antiunitary map \(f(z)\mapsto z^{-1}\overline{f(z)}\), the second becomes \(\|P_+(V\widetilde Q_+)\|_2\) with \(\widetilde Q_+(z)=z^{-1}\overline{Q_+(z)}\) supported at strictly negative indices.

The [balanced equality conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement) give the first two hypotheses of (1) for each end with \(x=(\log2)/2\).
The [norm reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v651.md#reconstruction) gives \(\sqrt m\|DQ\|_2\to2(1-e^{-x})\).
By (5), each separate squared norm has upper limit after scaling by \(m\) at most \(2(1-e^{-x})^2\).
Their squared sum has limit \(4(1-e^{-x})^2\) by orthogonality, forcing both separate limits and the last hypothesis of (1).
In (5) only the counts and integer excess were used.
For each admissible step \(h\), the [radial equidistribution and moment comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v657.md#angles) identify every weak limit of each pushed \(\gamma\) with the corresponding limit of the pushed interior or exterior radial probabilities.
Indeed the comparison applies to \(kh\) for every fixed positive integer \(k\), and the conjugate moments follow as well.
Each radial probability is at most twice their mean, whose pushforward tends to Haar by that same result.
This proves the required domination.
Finally \(x<0.35\) and \(1-1/\sqrt2>0.29\) give \(b>(0.29/0.35)^2>2/3>2/\pi\).

<a id="model"></a>
## A single factor attaining the projection bound

Take positive integers \(H,n\) with \(mH\to\infty\) and \(mn\to x\), and put
\[
    W(z)=\frac{t-z^H}{1-tz^H},\qquad Q(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n z^{-iH}.
\]
The inner function has value \(t\) at zero and coefficient \(-(1-t^2)t^{j-1}\) at each \(jH\), \(j\ge1\).
Thus the projected product has coefficients \(-(1+t)(1-t^n)t^j\) at \(jH\) for \(j\ge0\), and no others.
Its norm is \((1+t)(1-t^n)/\sqrt{1-t^2}\), giving (1).
All nonzero Fourier indices of \(\gamma\) are multiples of \(H\).
For \(mh\to0\), every fixed \(kh\) with \(k\ge1\) lies strictly between zero and \(H\) eventually, so every such pushed probability converges to Haar.
This example attains the one-ended assumptions with a single arithmetic progression with step \(H\) satisfying \(mH\to\infty\).
