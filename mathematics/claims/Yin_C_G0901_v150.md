# An explicit gap for cubic square endpoints and the minimum in degree twelve

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound in all degrees and a sharp minimum for quadratic bases

Let \(R>1\) be the modulus of the marked real exterior root of an irreducible half polynomial in the [relative square-endpoint construction](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#statement).
Write \(b\ge3\) for its degree over \(K\), where \(K/E\) is quadratic over a totally real field of degree \(u\ge2\), split at just the marked real place.
The rational degree of that root is \(d=2ub\), its measure is \(R^2\), and we write \(m=2\log R\).
With \(n=\lceil\sqrt{2(b-1)d}\rceil\), one has
\[
                m\ \ge\ \frac{d}{2n\big(29920\log(4n)\big)^{18}} .       \tag{1}
\]
In particular the minimum for cubic halves exists over all bases and satisfies
\[
        \exp(3\cdot 10^{-7})<\min M(R)<2.372 .
\]
All cubic candidates with \(M(R)\le e\), where \(e=\exp(1)\), have \(d<\exp(600)\).
These are explicit uniform bounds with arbitrary coefficient trace, norm and field discriminant.
The upper bound comes from the [degree-eighteen example](Yin_C_G0901_v146.md#attainment).

In degree twelve the exact minimum for all cubic halves is \(R_0^2\), where \(1.564<R_0<1.565\) and \(R_0\) is the positive exterior root of the cubic over the marked field specified by
\[
              t=-1,\quad p=1-\sqrt2,\quad
              a=\frac{-1+\sqrt{4\sqrt2-3}}2,\quad a'=t-a,\qquad
              X^3-aX^2+a'X-1 .
\]
In fact this is the only degree-twelve example of measure at most \(\varphi^2\), up to conjugation and the rational sign before normalizing the constant to \(-1\), with \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
The [rational-trace classification](Yin_C_G0901_v146.md#rational) treats rational \(t=a+a'\) in all degrees; the degree-twelve assertion here also treats every irrational \(t\) in a quadratic base.
The all-degree cubic minimum is still unknown.

There is a block bound for unrestricted Lehmer inputs from the same estimate.
For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number of total log measure \(m\), form its [compressed conjugates and multiplicative labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels).
Let \(d\) now denote the compressed degree, \(w\in\{1,2\}\) the number of actual compressed roots per label, and \(b\ge2\) the size of **any** block on these labels, including the entire set.
Its number \(u=d/(wb)\) of translates satisfies
\[
                        u<16m^2(29920\log(8d))^{36}.                     \tag{2}
\]
This improves the logarithmic exponent in the [previous two-root block estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) from 212 to 36 and specifies the constant.
It imposes no bound on the number of terms of the minimum polynomial, and it leaves a potentially shrinking sequence with growing block sizes open.

<a id="curve"></a>
## A plane curve through two conjugates

Suppose \(x,y\) are multiplicatively independent conjugates of degree \(d\), total log measure \(m\), and joint field degree \(J\).
For \(n=\lceil\sqrt{2J}\rceil\), the \(\binom{n+2}{2}>J\) values of \(x^iy^j\) with \(i+j\le n\) satisfy a nontrivial rational linear relation.
Factor its polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\) and retain an irreducible factor vanishing at \((x,y)\).
This defines a proper \(\mathbb Q\)-irreducible plane curve \(V\) in the torus, of dimension one and degree of definition at most \(n\), since its projective closure is defined by the homogenized equation.
The point belongs to no proper torsion coset of \(\mathbb G_m^2\): such a coset imposes \(x^i y^j=\zeta\) for integers \(i,j\) not both zero and a root of unity \(\zeta\), contrary to independence.
In particular it lies in \(V^*\) in the [height theorem of Amoroso and Viada](../audits/literature/AV12/Yin_U001.md#av12-u001).
In that theorem the ambient dimension is two and the curve dimension is one, giving \(935\cdot2^5=29920\) and exponent \((1+1)(2+1)^2=18\).
The coordinate height bound now yields
\[
       \frac{2m}{d}=h(x)+h(y)\ \ge\ h(1:x:y)
                 \ \ge\ \frac{1}{n(29920\log(4n))^{18}}.                \tag{3}
\]
The right side decreases with \(n\), so any upper bound on \(J\) may be used in the ceiling.

For (2), choose \(x,y\) on different labels in the same block.
These roots are independent by the label construction; compression preserves total log measure even for nonunits.
An automorphism fixing \(x\) preserves its block and takes \(y\) to one of at most \(w(b-1)\) actual roots on the other labels.
Thus \(J\le d w(b-1)\).
Set \(v=w(b-1)d\), so \(2\le v\le d^2\) and \(\lceil\sqrt{2v}\rceil\le2\sqrt v\) (for \(v=2\) this is immediate, and for \(v\ge3\) use \(\sqrt{2v}+1\le2\sqrt v\)).
Formula (3) gives \(m\ge\sqrt{d/(w(b-1))}/[4(29920\log(8d))^{18}]\).
Here \(d/(w(b-1))=ub/(b-1)>u\), proving (2).

For an irreducible half polynomial, take its marked exterior root \(x\) and any one of the other marked roots \(y\).
The interior moduli have product \(1/R\), and there are at least two of them, so \(1/R<|y|<1\).
A multiplicative dependence of the conjugates \(x,y\) would, by equality of their positive heights, make \(xy\) or \(x/y\) a root of unity, contrary to these moduli.
The [norm and field-degree identity](Yin_C_G0901_v143.md#factors) gives \(K\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\) and \(d=2ub\).
Over \(\mathbb Q(x)\) the other root satisfies the half polynomial divided by \(X-x\), of degree \(b-1\).
Hence \(J\le(b-1)d\), proving (1).
For each fixed \(b\), the right side of (1) grows at least as a positive constant depending on \(b\) times \(\sqrt d/(\log(3d))^{18}\).
Thus bounded measure gives an effective finite degree range, and the bounded coefficients of monic minima in that range give only finitely many possible marked roots.

<a id="gap"></a>
## An absolute numerical cutoff for cubic halves

For \(b=3\), \(n=\lceil2\sqrt d\rceil\le3\sqrt d\), so (1) gives
\[
             m\ge\frac{\sqrt d}{6(29920\log(12\sqrt d))^{18}} .
\]
At \(d=\exp(600)\) the right side is greater than one: \(29920(300+\log12)<10^7\) and \(\log6+126\log10<300\).
For larger \(d\) it is increasing, since for \(s=\log\sqrt d\ge300\) its log derivative in \(s\) is \(1-18/(s+\log12)>0\).
It follows that \(m\le1\) forces \(12\le d<\exp(600)\).
In this range [Voutier's inequality](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives
\[
       m>\frac14\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^3
            \ge\frac14\left(\frac{\log600}{600}\right)^3>3\cdot10^{-7}.
\]
Indeed the minimum of \((\log s)/s\) on \([\log12,600]\) is at \(600\) by the derivative and comparison of the endpoints.
The asserted bound also holds when \(m>1\).
The known degree-eighteen example puts the minimum below \(e\), where finiteness just proved ensures it is attained.

<a id="box"></a>
## The finite box from both coefficient embeddings in degree twelve

For \(d=12,\ b=3\) one has \([E:\mathbb Q]=2\).
Normalize the half polynomial to \(F(X)=X^3-aX^2+a'X-1\) by the sign substitution in the statement, put \(p=aa'\), and suppose \(R\le\varphi\) and \(t=a+a'\) is irrational.
The marked exterior root of this normalized half can be \(R\) or \(-R\).
Use primes for the other embedding of \(E\), and set \(D=t^2-4p\).
The [cubic deltoid criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v146.md#cubic) gives
\[
      0<D<4,\quad D'<0,\quad
      (p')^2+(18+12t')p'-4(t')^3-27<0,\quad 0<p'<9.
\]
Here (2) of that criterion gives \(|a-a'|<2(R-R^{-1})\le2\).
At the other embedding the cubic has three distinct unit roots with sum \(\gamma\), so \(p'=|\gamma|^2<9\), and \(D'<0\) ensures \(p'>0\).
The negative discriminant, viewed as a quadratic inequality in \(p'\) with discriminant \(16(t'+3)^3\), gives \(t'>-3\), and \(t'=2\operatorname{Re}\gamma<6\).

At the mark let the two interior radii be \(e^{-s_1},e^{-s_2}\); their logs satisfy \(s_1+s_2=\log R\) with both positive.
Their contribution to the real sum \(t\) of the roots and their inverses has absolute value at most \(2(\cosh s_1+\cosh s_2)<2+R+R^{-1}\).
For a positive exterior root this yields \(-2<t<2+2(R+R^{-1})\).
For a negative exterior root the other two roots must be real of opposite signs by their negative product.
Their trace contribution is at least \(2-(R+R^{-1})\), giving \(t>2-2(R+R^{-1})\); also \(F(-1)>0\) then gives \(t<-2\).
Since \(R+R^{-1}\le\sqrt5\), either case gives \(-5/2<t<13/2\) and \(-1<p<169/16\).

Write \(D_0\) for the positive fundamental discriminant of \(E\), and
\[
       2t=T+k\sqrt{D_0},\qquad 2p=P+l\sqrt{D_0}.
\]
The full integral ring requires \(T\equiv kD_0,\ P\equiv lD_0\pmod2\).
The preceding bounds on both embeddings force
\[
 -5\le T\le12,\quad 0\le P\le19,\quad
 0<|k|\sqrt{D_0}<19/2,\quad |l|\sqrt{D_0}<11 .
\]
In particular \(5\le D_0\le90,\ |k|,|l|\le4\).

One additional necessary sign test uses the rational number \(q=13/8>\varphi\).
For \(t>-2\) one has \(F(1)<0\), \(a-a'=\sqrt D\), and \(F(q)>0\).
For \(t<-2\) one has \(F(1)>0\), \(a-a'=-\sqrt D\), and \(F(-q)<0\).
Substituting \(a=(t+a-a')/2\) into these tests gives respectively
\[
       \sqrt D <\frac5{21}\left(\frac{337}{52}-t\right),\qquad
       \sqrt D <\frac{21}{5}\left(t+\frac{129}{52}\right).
\]
The [exact quadratic-field enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0193/readme.md#quadratic-base) checks the finite box, testing signs in the full ring with integer arithmetic.
The field discriminants and the local inequalities leave 40 tuples before this last test and zero afterwards.
Thus no irrational \(t\) can occur at \(R\le\varphi\) in degree twelve.
The [exact rational-trace attainment](Yin_C_G0901_v146.md#attainment) now gives the stated minimum and uniqueness.
