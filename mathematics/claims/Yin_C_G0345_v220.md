# A congruence between the exceptional degrees at two and five

<a id="statement"></a>
## Resultants for every reciprocal rational divisor

Let \(g\ge2\) be even, let \(r>6g\) and \(q=r+g\) be primes, and fix \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
Write \(Y=X^g\) and set
\[
    A(Y)=(Y-2)(Y-3),\qquad
    P=X^r A(Y)+\varepsilon(1-2Y)(1-3Y),\qquad Q=P/(X+\varepsilon).
\]
Let \(S\) be any monic reciprocal rational divisor of \(Q\), and let \(d=\deg S\).
It is integral, and its roots occur in inverse pairs without \(1,-1\) by the [cyclotomic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion).
In the range \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) every monic rational divisor of \(Q\) is reciprocal by the [individual factor result](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#degrees).

At \(\ell=2,3\) let \(\delta_\ell\) count, with multiplicity, the roots of \(\bar S\) satisfying \(X^g=-1\).
At five let \(2u\) count those satisfying \(X^{2g}=-1\).
These are precisely the exceptional roots complementary to the two prime grids in the [three reductions](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#three).
Put \(v=v_2(g)\ge1\) and \(\kappa=v_2(S(-\varepsilon))\).
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
      |\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-2)|&=3^{\delta_3}5^u,\\
      |\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-3)|&=2^{3\delta_2-\kappa}5^u,\\
      \kappa&\in\{0,1\},\qquad
      u\equiv\delta_2\equiv 2^v\kappa\pmod {2^{v+1}} .               \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
In particular a divisor with \(v_2(u)=v\) must contain every copy of the residue root \(1\) at \(2\); these copies form a single local factor of degree \(2^v\).
The signed ratio of the two binomial resultants has sign \((-1)^{\delta_3/2^v+\kappa}\), with \(2^v\mid\delta_3\).
Writing \(g_0=g/2^v\), one has the stronger congruence
\[
        r g_0^{-1}\frac{u}{2^v}
          \equiv 2\frac{\delta_3}{2^v}+3\kappa+3\delta_3-3\delta_2-d
                    \pmod4,                                          \tag{2}
\]
where \(g_0^{-1}\) is taken modulo four.
When \(\delta_2=\delta_3=0\), this recovers the [quartic obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#statement).
Formula (2) also tests divisors supported in the exceptional parts at two and three.

<a id="valuations"></a>
## Exact valuations at the exceptional roots

At each characteristic take all the roots \(x\) of \(S\) in one algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), counting multiplicity, and use \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
In local expressions write \(Y=x^g\).
The two [grid identities](Yin_C_G0345_v215.md#six) at a root of \(P\) are
\[
  (x^q+\varepsilon)A(Y)=-6\varepsilon(Y-1)(Y^2+1),\qquad
  (x^r+\varepsilon)A(Y)=-5\varepsilon(Y^2-1).                         \tag{3}
\]
All these roots are units.
Evaluating \(P\) over the roots of \(X^g-2\) and \(X^g-3\) gives absolute resultants \(15^g\) and \(40^g\), respectively.
Thus the first resultant for \(S\) can only have prime factors \(3,5\) and the second only \(2,5\).

At \(3\) only the exceptional roots have \(Y\equiv2=-1\).
There \(Y-3,Y-1,Y^2+1\) are units, as is \(x^q+\varepsilon\), since a root of \(X^g=-1\) avoids \(X^q=-\varepsilon\) for even \(g\) and \((2g,q)=1\).
The first identity gives \(v_3(Y-2)=1\) on every such root.

At \(2\) only the exceptional roots have \(Y\equiv3=1\).
For each of them \(Y-2\) is a unit, \(v_2(Y^2+1)>0\), and \(v_2(Y-1)\ge v_2(x^q+\varepsilon)\).
For the latter inequality the denominator is a unit unless \(\bar x=1\), since \(\bar x^g=1\) and \((g,q)=1\).
At \(\bar x=1\), the odd integer \(q\) gives \(v_2(x^q+\varepsilon)=v_2(x+\varepsilon)\), and \(X+\varepsilon\) divides \(X^g-1\) over \(\mathbb Z\).
The first identity in (3) now gives \(v_2(Y-3)>1\), forcing \(v_2(Y-1)=1\) and \(v_2(Y^2+1)=1\).
Substitution gives the exact value \(v_2(Y-3)=3-v_2(x+\varepsilon)\) at every exceptional root.
On all the other roots \(v_2(x+\varepsilon)=0\).
Summing proves the exponent \(3\delta_2-\kappa\).
Direct differentiation gives \(Q(-\varepsilon)=P'(-\varepsilon)=2(r-5g)\), of valuation one.
Since \(S\mid Q\), \(\kappa\) is zero or one, and it is one exactly when \(\bar S(1)=0\) at two.

At \(5\) the exceptional roots separate into \(Y=2\) and \(Y=3\) in the residue field.
Inversion exchanges these sets with multiplicities, so each contains \(u\) roots.
For each of them \(x^r+\varepsilon\) is a unit: a residue root of \(X^{2g}=-1\) has order dividing \(4g\), and cannot satisfy \(X^r=-\varepsilon\) since \((r,4g)=1\) and \(X\ne\pm1\).
As \(Y^2-1\) is a unit, the second identity in (3) gives \(v_5(Y-j)=1\) at every root reducing to \(Y=j\), \(j=2,3\).
This completes the two resultant formulas without prescribing any local factor of \(S\).

<a id="two"></a>
## Multiplicities in the exceptional classes at two

Write \(g=2^v g_0\) with \(g_0\) odd.
The reduction of \(Q\) at two is the product of \((X^{g_0}-1)^{2^v}\) and the disjoint primitive \(q\)-grid.
For an exceptional root \(x\) put \(z=x^{g_0}-1\).
Its valuation is positive, and the preceding calculation gives \(v_2((1+z)^{2^v}-1)=1\).
If \(v_2(w)<1\), then \((1+w)^2-1=w(2+w)\) has valuation exactly \(2v_2(w)\); if \(v_2(w)\ge1\), the valuation is at least two.
Applying this backwards through the \(v\) squarings shows \(v_2(z)=2^{-v}\).
Consequently the ramification index of \(\mathbb Q_2(x)\) is at least \(2^v\).
For a local irreducible factor its residue roots are Frobenius conjugates of equal multiplicity, each of multiplicity at least that ramification index by the [local degree and residue count](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#embeddings).
But the full parent \(Q\) has multiplicity exactly \(2^v\) at each exceptional residue.
Thus any local factor taking that residue takes all its multiplicity, and \(\bar S\) selects a set of distinct roots of \(X^{g_0}-1\) with exactly \(2^v\) copies of each.
At residue \(1\) these \(2^v\) copies account for a single local factor, since its residue orbit has size one.
The selected set is closed under inversion and only \(1\) is self-inverse in characteristic two.
Counting singleton and paired residues yields
\[
                   2^v\mid\delta_2,\qquad
                   \delta_2/2^v\equiv\kappa\pmod2 .                   \tag{4}
\]

<a id="sign"></a>
## The sign from the exceptional roots at three

Put \(N_j=\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-j)\) and \(R=N_2/N_3\).
The [real inverse-pair calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#sign) shows that \(R\) has the same sign as \(S(\varepsilon)\).
That calculation uses only reciprocity, the absence of \(1,-1\), and \(x^r A(x^g)=-\varepsilon A^*(x^g)\).
Also \(Q(\varepsilon)=2\), and \(S(\varepsilon)\equiv S(-\varepsilon)\bmod2\), so \(|S(\varepsilon)|=2^\kappa\).

Evaluate \(S(\varepsilon)\) at three.
For the roots in the primitive \(q\)-grid the product of \(\varepsilon-\bar x\) is one in \(\mathbb F_3\) by the [odd-order inverse-pair argument](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#sign).
Indeed writing \(\bar x=-\varepsilon z\) and \(z=w^2\) in the odd-order group of \(q\)-th roots, the factors \(w\) in \(1+z=w(w+w^{-1})\) cancel by inversion.
The remaining product is a nonzero square in \(\mathbb F_3\), since Frobenius permutes the inverse pairs; the additional powers of \(\varepsilon\) have even total exponent.

Each exceptional residue \(x_0\) has order with exact dyadic part \(2^{v+1}\) because \(x_0^g=-1\) in characteristic three.
Its Frobenius orbit has even length \(f\), since its order is a multiple of four.
If its inverse forms a different orbit, combine the two, whose multiplicities agree by reciprocity.
For one occurrence, their total contribution to \(S(\varepsilon)\bmod3\) is
\[
  \operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb F_{3^f}/\mathbb F_3}
     \big((\varepsilon-x_0)(\varepsilon-x_0^{-1})\big)
     = \operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb F_{3^f}/\mathbb F_3}(x_0)^{-1}.
\]
The sign factor \((-\varepsilon)^f\) is one, and the norm of the squared factor \((\varepsilon-x_0)^2\) is one.
The right side is minus one precisely when \(v_2(3^f-1)=v+1\), by the finite-field norm exponent \((3^f-1)/2\).
For even \(f\), successive squaring starting at \(3^2-1=8\), followed by the geometric sum for an odd multiplier, gives \(v_2(3^f-1)=2+v_2(f)\).
Consequently \(2^v\mid2f\), and this pair of orbits contributes \((-1)^{2f/2^v}\).

If inversion preserves one orbit, it acts on the orbit by the \(a\)-th Frobenius power with \(f=2a\): it has order two and is nontrivial there.
Then \(3^a\equiv-1\bmod 2^{v+1}\).
No power of \(3\) is \(-1\bmod8\); thus \(v=1\), and \(a\) is odd.
Write \(z=\varepsilon x_0\), still of order with dyadic part four.
The identities \(z^{3^a}=z^{-1}\) and \((1-z)^{3^a-1}=-z^{-1}\) give the orbit contribution
\[
   \operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb F_{3^f}/\mathbb F_3}(\varepsilon-x_0)
       =(-z^{-1})^{(3^a+1)/2}=-1=(-1)^{f/2^v}.
\]
Indeed \(v_2(3^a+1)=2\) for odd \(a\), the odd part of the order of \(-z^{-1}\) divides \(3^a+1\), and its dyadic part is four.
In both orbit cases the total degree is divisible by \(2^v\).
Multiplying with the actual multiplicities proves \(2^v\mid\delta_3\) and \(S(\varepsilon)\equiv(-1)^{\delta_3/2^v}\bmod3\).
Since \(2^\kappa\equiv(-1)^\kappa\bmod3\), the sign of \(R\) is \((-1)^{\delta_3/2^v+\kappa}\).

<a id="character"></a>
## Comparing the two values modulo five

By the [oriented residue product at five](Yin_C_G0345_v219.md#quartic), \(2^v\mid u\) and
\[
     R \equiv (-1)^{d/2-u}\varepsilon^u
                 2^{r g_0^{-1}u/2^v}\pmod5 .
\]
Here \(g_0=g/2^v\) and its inverse is taken modulo four.
This part of the cited proof applies to arbitrary exceptional allocations: the regular pairs contribute \(-1\), and for each exceptional pair its member with \(\bar x^g=2\) determines the pair's unit contribution.
The product of those \(u\) residues equals \(2^{-g_0^{-1}u/2^v}\).
Only the prime-grid reductions, the root identity and reciprocity enter that computation, so they also suffice in the present range \(r>6g\).
Since \(v\ge1\), \(u\) is even and the first two signs reduce to \((-1)^{d/2}\).

By the rational norm formulas and the sign calculation above,
\[
     R=(-1)^{\delta_3/2^v+\kappa} 3^{\delta_3}2^{-3\delta_2+\kappa}.
\]
Equate exponents of the generator \(2\) of \(\mathbb F_5^\times\), using \(3=2^3\) and \(-1=2^2\) there.
This gives (2).
In particular both \(\delta_2,\delta_3\) and \(d\) are even, so reduction modulo two gives \(u/2^v\equiv\kappa\bmod2\).
Together with (4) this proves the coupled congruences of (1).
