# The entire divisible conjugate group of a large strict record has one irreducible module

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact conjugate ranks and the least possible exterior count

Fix \(M>0\).
Let \(w\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of total logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(M\) that is a strict degree record among positive integer-polynomial measures.
Write \(d=2n=\deg w\), and consider all sufficiently large \(d\) in terms of \(M\).
The canonical blocks of its inverse pairs have size \(b\), number \(u=n/b\), and \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\).
The rational span \(V_w\) of its conjugates modulo torsion is **absolutely irreducible**.
Its dimension is \(n\) in the independent inversion case and \(n-u\) in the oriented case.
In particular every nontorsion \(x\) in the divisible conjugate group \(D(w)\) satisfies
\[
                 V_x=V_w,\qquad D(x)=D(w).
\]
Both the radial and the argument ranks of \(x\) equal the respective ranks of \(w\).

Uniformly over all these \(x\), their conjugate argument rank is at least
\[
                d/4-O_M(\sqrt{d\log d}).
\]
On a shrinking-measure sequence the error is \(o(\sqrt{d\log d})\).
When the conjugates of complex conjugation commute on \(V_w\) along such a shrinking sequence the argument rank is \((1-o(1))d/2\).
These are linear estimates in the degree of the original record.

Let \(l(w)\) be the number of exterior conjugates of \(w\).
The minimum exterior count for reciprocal nontorsion \(x\in D(w)\) is exactly \(l(w)\).
For \(x\) that is not a torsion twist of a rational power of a single conjugate of \(w\), one has
\[
                        l(x)\ \ge\ (b-1)l(w)/16                           \tag{1}
\]
whenever \(x\) is reciprocal.
This diverging factor is uniform in the signature, the radical denominator and the original exterior count.
In particular for every \(k\ge1\), with no change to the degree threshold as \(k\) varies, the group meets the [reciprocal class with a neutral conjugate and at most \(k\) exterior conjugates](Yin_C_G0901_v59.md#statement) exactly when \(w\) itself belongs to that class, provided \(w\) has a neutral conjugate.
Without that provision the minimum exterior count assertion still holds.

<a id="modules"></a>
## The two representations on block coordinates

The torsion-class compression of a strict record cannot lower its degree since it preserves measure, hence distinct roots have distinct classes.
Use the [canonical alternating actions](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) and the [two alternatives for lifts to the roots](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement).
In the first case the full signed coordinate space of dimension \(n\) maps isomorphically onto \(V_w\), by independence.
The normal sign group contains even flips on each individual block.
Its characters on the coordinate lines are pairwise distinct: flip one of two given labels and an unused label of its block different from both.
Averaging against any of these characters isolates the corresponding coordinate even over \(\mathbb C\).
Transitivity on labels gives irreducibility over \(\mathbb C\).

In the second case orient each block by one of its two canonical halves.
The Galois action permutes the blocks, with a common orientation sign on every transported block.
Each block sum is zero modulo torsion at a strict record by the [oriented norm descent](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields); these \(u\) relations give the full kernel.
The coordinate quotient is thus the direct sum of the coordinate-sum-zero spaces on the individual blocks, with the same permutation and sign action.
The [supported lifts of three-cycles](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) realize pure independent \(A_b\)-actions on these spaces, one factor per block.
The sum-zero representation of \(A_b\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb C\) for \(b\ge5\): double transitivity makes its full permutation-module commutant two-dimensional (matrices constant on the diagonal and off it), one dimension already belonging to the constant summand.
The \(u\) sum-zero representations are pairwise inequivalent for the product of the alternating factors since different factors act nontrivially on them.
Every invariant subspace for the full group is consequently a sum of some of these summands, and transitivity on blocks again proves absolute irreducibility.
Any nonzero \(V_x\subseteq V_w\) must therefore be all of it; equality of rational class spaces is exactly equality of the groups after adjoining torsion and all roots.

<a id="signature"></a>
## The dimensions at complex conjugation

Write \(a\) for the number of real exterior conjugates, \(s\) for the number of nonreal conjugate pairs of exterior conjugates, and \(q=n-a-2s\) for the number of neutral inverse pairs.
On the original signed coordinate space, the negative eigenspace of complex conjugation has dimension \(q+s\), since each neutral pair contributes one and each exchanged pair of labels contributes one.
Passing to either quotient loses at most \(u\) dimensions.
The [argument rank formula](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#selection) and the equality of spans now give, for every indicated \(x\),
\[
          k(x)\ge q+s-u=d/4+q/2-a/2-u .
\]
The [real-place estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#signature) applied to the \(2a\) real roots gives \(a=O_M(\sqrt{d\log d})\), improving to little-oh on shrinking sequences.
The polylogarithmic upper bound on \(u\) is \(o(\sqrt{d\log d})\).
This proves both linear lower bounds.
In the commuting case [the fixed-dimension fraction tends to zero](Yin_C_G0901_v82.md#small); together with \(\dim V_w=n-O(u)\) and the equality of spans this gives the last rank assertion.

Put \(R=\dim V_w^c\), the [radial rank](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules).
The [independence of exterior radii on alternating blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#radii) gives \(R\ge a+s-u\) and independence of every set of at most \(\lfloor(b-1)/2\rfloor\) of the \(a+s\) exterior radii, one per complex conjugation orbit.
If \(a+s\ge2u\) this gives \(R\ge(a+s)/2\).
Otherwise for large \(d\) one has \(2u\le\lfloor(b-1)/2\rfloor\), since \(u\) is polylogarithmic and \(b=n/u\).
Then \(R=a+s\).
In either case \(R\ge l(w)/4\), since \(l(w)=a+2s\).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Noncoordinate elements have many exterior roots

Represent the nonzero class of \(x\) in the appropriate model.
For oriented blocks always use the unique representative of coordinate sum zero in each block.
In a block a coordinate line means the line of the image of one original basis vector; in the sum-zero model its vectors have one exceptional coordinate and all others equal.
The class of a noncoordinate \(x\) either occupies at least two blocks or occupies a single block away from those lines.
We claim its orbit contains at least \(u b(b-1)/2\) vectors.

For \(t\ge2\) occupied blocks there are at least \(u/t\) choices of support sets by transitivity and coverage.
In the sum-zero model each nonzero block vector has at least \(b\) images under its independent alternating factor.
This follows either by the coordinate multiplicity count below or by the fact that a nonzero orbit spans the \((b-1)\)-dimensional irreducible summand and has sum zero.
In the sign model, for a support of size \(1\le j<b\) in one block there are at least \(2^j\binom bj\) possibilities and for full support at least \(2^{b-1}\).
Indeed the label group independently moves supports within the blocks, and the independent even flips give the indicated signs after choosing lifts.
Thus both models give at least \(b^t\) vectors over each such support set.
The product \((u/t)b^t\) suffices for the claim when \(t\ge2\).

In a single active block of the sign model away from the coordinate lines one has \(j\ge2\); the same count is at least \(\binom b2\) for large \(b\), also at \(j=b-1,b\).
In one sum-zero block away from the coordinate lines, no value occurs \(b-1\) times.
If some value occurs between two and \(b-2\) times, its possible position sets already number at least \(\binom b2\) by subset transitivity of \(A_b\).
If all values are different the orbit has size \(b!/2\).
Multiplying by \(u\) possible blocks proves the claim.
Each different class gives a distinct actual conjugate, so \(\deg x\) is at least this orbit size.
For a reciprocal \(x\) the [degree and exterior-count inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v64.md#degree) gives \(2 l(x)\dim V_w\ge(\deg x)R\).
Since \(\dim V_w\le ub\), the preceding estimates give (1).
Eventually \(b-1>16\).

On one coordinate line, \([x]\) is a nonzero rational multiple of the class of an original conjugate.
The orbit of this class has size \(d\), and all fibers from the actual conjugates of \(x\) onto it have the same size \(h\).
Thus \(\deg x=hd\) and \(l(x)=h l(w)\) by scaling the logarithms and using reciprocity of \(w\).
Neutral conjugates persist on this line exactly when \(w\) has them.
Since \(w\) attains the minimum, the assertions about exterior counts and the class with a neutral conjugate follow under the stated conditions.
