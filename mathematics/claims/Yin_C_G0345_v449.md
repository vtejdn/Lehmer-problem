# Degree at least 112 at measure 1.02 and the first remaining triple at prime 499

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree thresholds and three qualifying values

Let \(P\) be a nonzero integer polynomial with \(1<M(P)\le T\).
The following are lower bounds on its degree, including equality in the measure cutoff.
For an algebraic unit \(c\) of measure in the same interval, the corresponding bound holds for \([\mathbb Q(c^k):\mathbb Q]\) at every positive integer \(k\).

| \(T\) | 1.042 | 1.04 | 1.036 | 1.027 | 1.022 | 1.02 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Minimum required degree | 88 | 90 | 92 | 100 | 102 | 112 |

In the [signed prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) and the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) with \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\), at most two distinct positive values qualify at either sign for each prime through 491.
At the next prime \(499\), three distinct values require the ordered degrees \((86,72,72)\) in increasing order of measure, and the degree-86 measure lies strictly between \(1.042\) and \(1.057\).
For \(r\ge9\) primitive irreducible integer factors of positive logarithmic measures summing to less than \(B=(\log 2)/4\), their total degree is at least \(112(r-8)+682\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Enumerating the next thirteen even degrees

The [degree-86 result](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree) applies throughout the table.
By its small-measure reduction it suffices to consider a monic reciprocal irreducible noncyclotomic factor of even degree between 86 and 110.
A nontrivial power substitution would preserve the same positive measure in degree at most 55.
For each remaining degree, the [trace bounds](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v13/readme.md#bounds) apply to all 120 traces, including those computed after reflecting the first half of the coefficients.
The [complete enumerations](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v13/readme.md#census) test \(1.042,1.04,1.036\) in degrees \(86,88,90\), then \(1.027\) in degrees 92 through 98, \(1.022\) in degree 100, and \(1.02\) in degrees 102 through 110.
There are 37,450,064 sign-normalized representatives and every one of the 818 final polynomials has an exact cyclotomic factor.
The [separate checks](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v13/readme.md#verification) count each first-pair slice and verify every final divisor.
Since the degree-specific cutoffs decrease with degree, these exclusions imply each entry for the arbitrary polynomial.
For a unit \(c\), its norm down to \(\mathbb Q(c^k)\) has measure strictly above one and at most \(M(c)\) by [relative norm descent](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree).
Its degree is at most that of the power field, giving the second assertion.

<a id="sums"></a>
## Degree sums in a fixed measure budget

Let \(r\ge3\) and order the positive logarithmic measures from largest to smallest.
The \(j\)-th is less than \(B/j\).
Write
\[
 (L_1,L_2,\ldots,L_8)=(56,68,86,86,92,92,100,102),
 \qquad L_j=112\quad(j\ge9).
\]
Then the total degree \(D\) is at least \(\sum_{j=1}^r L_j\).
To prove this it suffices to take \(D\le112r\).
The measures are below \(\log 2\), so their irreducibles are nontorsion units of degree at least two.
Each of the \(r-1\) smaller measures exceeds \(2/\log^3(336r)\) by [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
The function \((x-1)/\log^3(336x)\) increases for \(x\ge3\), since \(\log(336x)>3\).
Using \(1008<(65/24)^7<e^7\), the largest measure is less than \(B-4/343<\log\rho\) by the [bounds for Lehmer's measure](Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite).
Its degree is at least 56 by the [published degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree).
The [earlier sorted bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#sums) give degree at least 68 at position two and 86 at positions three and four, whenever present.
At each of positions \(5,6,7,8,9\), the corresponding values \(T=1.036,1.036,1.027,1.022,1.02\) satisfy \(T^{4j}>2\) by integer powers.
For \(j>9\) the ninth-position cutoff still applies.
The new degree table proves all remaining terms of the sum.
Its first eight terms total 682.

<a id="primes"></a>
## The strict budget at the remaining seven primes

The [previous exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v440.md#triple) covers the primes through 457.
The primes from 461 through 499 are \(461,463,467,479,487,491,499\), with respective widths \(215,215,217,222,225,227,230\) by \((b-1)^8<p^7<b^8\).
Take three distinct qualifying minima with degrees \(i,j,k\) ordered by increasing measure.
The [width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement) gives \(i+j+k\le b\) for both tests, as \(b\le(p+1)/2\) here and both prescribed orders are two.
The proof of the [degree-sum bound](#sums) gives \(i\ge86,\ j\ge68,\ k\ge56\), since the total degree is at most \(230\le112\cdot3\).
All three degrees are even by reciprocity and irreducibility.

For such degrees use the following strict lower bound \(f(d)\) on multiplicative measure.
At \(56,58,68,70\) use \(1.101,1.0965,1.082,1.0775\) from the [degree-specific cutoffs](Yin_C_G0345_v440.md#statement).
At the other even degrees below 68 use [1.091](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree), and at the remaining even degrees below 86 use [1.06](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree).
From 86 use the thirteen cutoffs of the present census.
Every candidate degree at these primes is within the listed range, since the other two degrees are at least 56 and 68 for \(i\), at least 86 and 56 for \(j\), and at least 86 and 68 for \(k\).

The product of the three measures exceeds \(z=f(i)\max(f(i),f(j))\max(f(i),f(j),f(k))\).
The [strict derivative budget](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) requires the product to be smaller than \(2^{1/4}((pb+1)/(2pb+1))^{1/p}\).
For each \(p,b\), loop over even \(86\le i\le b-124,\ 68\le j\le b-i-56,\ 56\le k\le b-i-j\).
Retain the triple only if
\[
      z^{4p}(2pb+1)^4<2^p(pb+1)^4 .
\]
The [rational comparisons](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v13/verify.py) retain zero triples at each of the first six primes and only \((86,72,72)\) at 499.
For this triple each degree-72 measure exceeds \(1.06\), while \((1.057\cdot1.06^2)^{4p}(2pb+1)^4>2^p(pb+1)^4\) at \(p=499,b=230\).
Thus its smallest measure must be less than \(1.057\), and the degree-86 census puts it above \(1.042\).
The computation leaves existence at this prime open.

The coordinating agent and computation contributor for Sheng Yin obtained the degree and prime bounds in the [unrestricted Lehmer investigation](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
