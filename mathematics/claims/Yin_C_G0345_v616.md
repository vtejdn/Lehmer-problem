# Relative discriminants and the real embeddings of a subfield of a reciprocal unit field

<a id="statement"></a>
## A mixed-signature estimate

Let \(\alpha\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with monic minimal polynomial \(P\), write \(m=m(P)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure, and let \(\iota\) act on \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) by \(\iota\alpha=\alpha^{-1}\).
Suppose \(F\subset K\) is stable under \(\iota\) and \(\tau=\iota|_F\ne1\).
Write \(e=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(r>0\) for the number of real embeddings of \(F\), \(s=e-r\) for the number of nonreal embeddings counted separately, \(b=[K:F]\), and \(\mathcal D\) for the absolute norm of the relative discriminant ideal of \(F/F^\tau\).
Let \(q\) be the monic minimum of \(\alpha\) over \(F\), \(a=q(0)\), and \(j\) the first index at which the coefficients of \(q\) and \(q^\tau\), read downwards from their common leading coefficient, differ.
Then \(1\le j\le b\), and
\[
 \mathcal D\ \le\
 \bigl[4\sinh(m/r)\bigr]^r
 \left(\frac{2b}{\sqrt j}\right)^s e^{2m}.                         \tag{1}
\]
This includes torsion relative constants, which are necessarily \(\pm1\) since \(F\) has a real embedding.
If \(a\) is nontorsion there is also a bound independent of \(b\),
\[
 m\ \ge\ \frac r2\,\operatorname{arsinh}\!
       \left(\frac{\mathcal D^{1/r}}{2^{\,1+s/r}}\right).           \tag{2}
\]
In particular, a sequence in this class with \(m\to0\) must satisfy \(r(2b)^{-s/r}\to0\).
Along the subsequence with nontorsion relative constant it must also satisfy \(r\,2^{-s/r}\to0\).
Thus \(s\log(2b)/r\le\log r+C\) with a fixed \(C\) excludes vanishing measure, even when the relative degrees are unbounded.
For example this holds if \(s\) is bounded and \(b\) grows at most polynomially in \(r\).
For \(s=0\), (2) recovers the nontorsion case of the [totally real discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v612.md#statement).

<a id="coefficient"></a>
## The first different coefficient at an arbitrary complex embedding

For monic complex polynomials \(f,g\) of degree \(b\) put \(U(z)=z^bf(1/z)\), \(V(z)=z^bg(1/z)\).
Suppose \(U/V=1+wz^j+O(z^{j+1})\) with \(w\ne0\), so \(w=[z^j](U-V)\).
Then
\[
                   |w|\le \frac{2b}{\sqrt j}\exp(m(f)+m(g)).        \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(U'/U-V'/V\) is a signed sum of \(2b\) terms \(\lambda/(1-\lambda z)\) over the roots of \(f,g\), with multiplicity.
Form the product \(B\) of the factors \((p-z)/(1-\bar p z)\) for \(p=1/\lambda\) over all these roots with \(|\lambda|>1\).
It satisfies \(|B|\le1\) in the open disk and \(|B(0)|=\exp(-m(f)-m(g))\).
After multiplication by \(B\), every summand has the form \(H(z)/(1-tz)\) with \(|t|\le1\) and \(H\) holomorphic and bounded by one in the disk.
For \(|\lambda|\le1\) take \(t=\lambda\) and \(H=\lambda B\).
For \(|\lambda|>1\), cancel its designated factor using \(\lambda(p-z)=1-\lambda z\), and take \(t=\bar p\) and \(H\) the product of the remaining factors.
Writing \(H(z)=\sum h_k z^k\), integration of \(|H|^2\) on circles of radii tending to one gives \(\sum |h_k|^2\le1\).
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the coefficient of \(z^{j-1}\) in \(H/(1-tz)\) by \(\sqrt j\).
The same coefficient of \(B(U'/U-V'/V)\) equals \(B(0)jw\), since the logarithmic derivative starts at order \(j-1\).
Summing proves (3).

<a id="reflection"></a>
## The coefficient at a reflected pair

Suppose the roots of \(g\) are \(1/\bar\lambda\) for the nonzero roots \(\lambda\) of \(f\), with all multiplicities, and put \(u=m(f)\), \(v=m(g)\).
Then
\[
 |w|\le 2\sinh u+2\sinh v
       =4\sinh((u+v)/2)\cosh((u-v)/2).                             \tag{4}
\]
To see this, factor \(U/V=\prod_\lambda(1-\lambda z)/(1-z/\bar\lambda)\).
Circle roots cancel.
Each exterior root contributes a constant times a Blaschke factor with zero \(1/\lambda\), and each interior root contributes a constant times an inverse Blaschke factor with denominator zero \(\bar\lambda\).
Thus \(U/V=(B_1/A_1)/(B_2/A_2)\) where \(B_1,B_2\) are finite Blaschke products normalized to take the positive values \(A_1=e^{-u},A_2=e^{-v}\) at zero.
Every positive coefficient index \(k\) of \(B_i\) has coefficient of modulus at most \(1-A_i^2\).
Indeed averaging \(B_i(\zeta z)\) over the \(k\)-th roots of unity gives a disk map in the variable \(z^k\), to which Schwarz–Pick at zero applies, including the constant case by inspection.
The first different coefficient of the normalized pair is \(w\), giving (4).
At a unit-modulus relative constant \(u=v\) this recovers the [torsion-constant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#gap).

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## Product over the embeddings

The tower identity \(\prod_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}q^\sigma=P\) gives \(\sum_\sigma m_\sigma=m\), where \(m_\sigma=m(q^\sigma)\).
The coefficients of \(q\) generate \(F\), since over their field the same polynomial still has degree \([K:F]\) and \(\alpha\) generates \(K\).
In particular \(q\ne q^\tau\).
The polynomial \(q^\tau\) is the monic reciprocal of \(q\) by its irreducibility and its root \(\alpha^{-1}\), so \(\tau a=a^{-1}\).
For any integral \(c\in F\) moved by \(\tau\), the absolute norm of \(c-\tau c\) is at least \(\mathcal D\), by discriminant divisibility for the quadratic order with basis \(1,c\) over \(\mathcal O_{F^\tau}\). [Relative order argument](Yin_C_G0345_v612.md#field)

Let \(c\) be the \(j\)-th coefficient of \(q\) from the top and \(w=c-\tau c\).
At a real embedding \(\sigma\) the roots of \(q^{\sigma\tau}\) are the conjugate reciprocals of those of \(q^\sigma\), since the latter polynomial has real coefficients.
Apply (4) with \(u=m_\sigma,v=m_{\sigma\tau}\), bounding its last hyperbolic cosine by \(e^{u+v}\).
At each nonreal embedding use (3) with the same \(u,v\).
The product of all exponential factors is \(e^{2m}\) because right composition with \(\tau\) permutes the embeddings.
The mean of \((u+v)/2\) over the real embeddings is at most \(m/r\), since that permutation also preserves the real embeddings.
The nonvanishing of every \(\sigma w\) makes \(u+v>0\) there, and the increasing concave function \(\log\sinh x\) on \(x>0\) yields (1).

If \(a\) is nontorsion, then \(a-\tau a=a-a^{-1}\ne0\).
Write \(x_\sigma=|\log|\sigma a||\) and \(X=\sum_{\sigma\ {\rm real}}x_\sigma\).
The unit norm and \(\log^+|\sigma a|\le m_\sigma\) give \(\sum_\sigma x_\sigma\le2m\).
At a real place \(|\sigma(a-a^{-1})|=2\sinh x_\sigma\), and at a nonreal place it is at most \(2\cosh x_\sigma\le 2 e^{x_\sigma}\).
The \(x_\sigma\) at real places are positive, since a real embedded value \(1\) or \(-1\) would give \(a=1\) or \(-1\).
Concavity now gives \(\mathcal D\le2^e\sinh(X/r)^r e^{2m-X}\).
Since \((\log\sinh x)'=\coth x\ge1\), the last factor is absorbed by increasing \(X\) to \(2m\) inside the hyperbolic sine, proving (2).

For \(m\to0\), (1) and \(\mathcal D\ge1\) imply \(r(2b)^{-s/r}\le4r\sinh(m/r)e^{2m/r}=O(m)\), uniformly for \(r\ge1\).
Similarly (2) gives \(r\,2^{-s/r}\le2r\sinh(2m/r)=O(m)\) when \(a\) is nontorsion.
These estimates leave open the cases with sufficiently many nonreal embeddings or sufficiently large relative degree.

<a id="orbit"></a>
## A bound on an orbit of ordered pairs of embeddings

Here retain a stable \(F\) but allow \(r=0\).
In the splitting field of \(P\), let \(\Omega\) be a Galois orbit of ordered pairs \((\sigma,\eta)\) of distinct embeddings of \(F\).
Define \(\psi(\sigma)=\bar\sigma\circ\tau\), where \(\bar\sigma\) is ordinary complex conjugation after \(\sigma\).
Suppose \(\Omega\) contains \(t>0\) pairs \((\sigma,\psi(\sigma))\), and put \(o=|\Omega|/e\).
All pairs in \(\Omega\) have the same first unequal coefficient index \(j\) of their two monic polynomials \(q^\sigma,q^\eta\), by Galois transitivity and generation of \(F\) by the coefficients.
Then
\[
 1\ \le\
 [4\sinh(m/t)]^t
 \left(\frac{2b}{\sqrt j}\right)^{|\Omega|-t}e^{2om}.               \tag{5}
\]
Each difference is a nonzero algebraic integer and their product over \(\Omega\) is Galois invariant.
At the indicated \(t\) pairs the second polynomial's roots are the conjugate reciprocals of the first polynomial's roots, so (4) applies with \(u=m_\sigma,v=m_{\psi(\sigma)}\).
The sum of \((u+v)/2\) over those pairs is at most \(m\) by injectivity of \(\psi\).
Use (3) on the other pairs.
Each embedding occurs \(o\) times in each coordinate by transitivity, giving the exponential factor in (5) after the same concavity estimate as in (1).
In the orbit \((\sigma,\sigma\tau)\), the reflected pairs are exactly those at real embeddings, and the quadratic relative order supplies the stronger left side in (1).
The [reflected-resultant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v280.md#orbit) bounds a mean discriminant deficit of the factors.
Inequality (5) instead bounds their total measure directly when the indicated orbit size and relative degree permit it.
