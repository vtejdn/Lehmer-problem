# Orders represented by the roots of a factor when the period primes vary

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quantitative restriction independent of the prime divisors of the period

Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic irreducible and noncyclotomic, with degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Suppose \(\bar S\mid X^B-1\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), with multiplicities, and \(m\le Kd/B\), where \(p\) is prime, \(B\ge2\), and \(K>0\).
Choose an irreducible factor of \(\bar S\) at random, with probability equal to its degree times its multiplicity \(\mu\), divided by \(d\).
If its roots have order \(t\), let \(b=t p^{\lceil\log_p\mu\rceil}\), the least period containing the whole factor with that multiplicity.
For every set \(\mathcal P\) of \(r\ge1\) primes dividing \(B\), at least half of the degree belongs to factors satisfying
\[
          \sum_{q\in\mathcal P}v_q(B/b)\log q
                      \le C r\{\log(2+K)+\log(2r)\} .                  \tag{1}
\]
The constant \(C\) is absolute, including when \(p\) and the primes in \(\mathcal P\) vary.

Let \(r=\omega(B)\), the number of distinct prime divisors of \(B\), let \(B_0=B/p^{v_p(B)}\), and put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_{B_0}(p)\), with \(f=1\) for \(B_0=1\).
With \(A=C r(\log(2+K)+\log(2r))\), one has
\[
                  \frac dB\ \ge\ \frac{f}{p B_0}e^{-A}.               \tag{2}
\]
When \(p\nmid B\) the factor \(p\) in the denominator may be omitted.
In particular the constants in the near-maximal order requirement depend on the *number* of period primes rather than their sizes.
This gives an alternative to the [fixed-prime estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v256.md#statement) when the primes themselves vary.

For \(P_l=X^{2n}-2X^{n+l}+X^n-2X^{n-l}+1\) with any \(0<l<n\), put \(B=3n\), \(B_0=B/2^{v_2(B)}\) and \(I=\varphi(B_0)/\operatorname{ord}_{B_0}(2)\).
Thus \(I\) counts Frobenius orbits of primitive \(B_0\)-th roots in characteristic two.
There is an absolute \(C_1\) such that every noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor satisfies
\[
              \min\{m(S),\,d/n\}
                \ \ge\ \exp\{-C_1\log(2I)\log(2+\log(2I))\}.           \tag{3}
\]
Hence a shrinking sequence of positive factor measures requires \(I\to\infty\), even when the odd prime divisors of \(n\) vary and its two-adic valuation is unrestricted.

<a id="tail"></a>
## Removing one prime power from the period

For every \(J\mid B\) let \(\delta(J)=\Pr(b\mid J)\).
The [effective period and resultant calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v257.md#multiplicities) gives
\[
                  (i+1)\delta(J)\log p
                        \le\log2+\frac{p^i J m}{d}\qquad(i\ge0).
\]
Indeed the gcd with \(X^J-1\) counts at least the full degree of the selected factors, including their multiplicities.
Fix \(q\mid B\), set \(Y_q=v_q(B/b)\log q\), and take \(u>0\).
Put \(j=\lceil u/\log q\rceil\).
If \(j>v_q(B)\) then the event \(Y_q\ge u\) is empty.
Otherwise it is exactly \(b\mid J\) for \(J=B/q^j\).
Choosing \(i=\lfloor u/(2\log p)\rfloor\) gives \(p^i J/B\le e^{-u/2}\) and \((i+1)\log p>u/2\).
It follows that
\[
            \Pr(Y_q\ge u)\le
                \min\{1,\,2(\log2+K e^{-u/2})/u\}.                    \tag{4}
\]
This applies to the characteristic prime as well as to all other primes.

For \(u\ge u_0=2\log(2+K)\), (4) is at most \(4/u\).
Put \(T=u_0+32r\).
The union bound gives probability at most \(1/8\) that any \(Y_q>T\) for \(q\in\mathcal P\).
Integrating the tail, \(\mathbb E\min(Y_q,T)\le u_0+4\log(T/u_0)\ll\log(2+K)+\log(2r)\).
By Markov's inequality the sum of these truncations is at most four times the sum of these expectation bounds except on a set of probability at most \(1/4\).
Outside the union of the two exceptional sets this bounds the untruncated sum, proving (1).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Comparing an attained order with the full period

Apply (1) to all primes of \(B\) and choose one attained effective period \(b\) with \(B/b\le e^A\).
Write \(b=t p^h\) and \(a=v_p(B)\).
The multiplicity of the chosen orbit satisfies \(\mu\ge p^{h}/p\), with \(\mu=1\) if \(p\nmid B\).
Its Frobenius orbit has size \(f_t=\operatorname{ord}_t(p)\), with \(f_1=1\).
Reduction of the units modulo \(B_0\) onto the units modulo \(t\mid B_0\) is surjective and its kernel has order \(\varphi(B_0)/\varphi(t)\le B_0/t\).
Consequently \(f\le (B_0/t) f_t\).
Since \(d\ge\mu f_t\), this yields
\[
                     d\ge \frac{p^a f}{p(B/b)} .
\]
For \(p\nmid B\) use \(a=h=0,\mu=1\) to omit the factor \(p\).
This proves (2).

<a id="five"></a>
## A bounded number of top Frobenius orbits suffices

In characteristic two [the reduction of the five-term parent](Yin_C_G0345_v257.md#families) divides \(X^{3n}-1\) for every \(n\), with multiplicities.
All its monic noncyclotomic irreducible rational factors are nontorsion unit polynomials.
The [five-term cut bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) gives \(m(S)\le 6(\log7)d/B\), and the full period with one doubling gives \(m(S)\ge(\log2)d/(2B)\).
Let \(r_0=\omega(B_0)\), where \(B_0\) is odd and divisible by three.
The order of two modulo \(B_0\) divides the least common multiple of the \(r_0\) even totients of its distinct prime powers.
Their least common multiple is at most their product divided by \(2^{r_0-1}\), so \(I\ge2^{r_0-1}\) and \(r\le r_0+1\ll\log(2I)\).
Also \(f/B_0=\varphi(B_0)/(I B_0)\ge 2^{-r_0}/I\).
Substituting these bounds in (2) proves (3), with absolute constants.
In particular a middle exponent with many distinct odd prime divisors already has a growing number of top orbits; when the number of prime divisors stays bounded, their Frobenius orders become decisive.
