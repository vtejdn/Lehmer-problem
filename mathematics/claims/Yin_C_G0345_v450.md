# The sharp precision constant for short representatives of the signed contact lattice

<a id="statement"></a>
## The threshold for omitting both factors

Use the reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\delta\), the minima \(P_i\) of \(\delta,\delta^2\), their common degree \(d\), their logarithmic measures \(h_i\), \(S=h_1+h_2\), and \(h_*=\min_i h_i\) as in the [dyadic precision theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v445.md#statement).
Every power of \(\delta\) generates its field.
Write \(F=P_1^2P_2^2\), fix either sign \(\varepsilon\), and set \(G_b(B)=B(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}B(X^{-n})\) and \(K_b=\{B\in\mathbb Z[Y]_{\le b}:F\mid G_b(B)\}\).
The assertion below works along **all** integers \(n\to\infty\) and needs no squarefree reduction hypothesis.
For integral widths \(b\to\infty\), \(b=o(n)\), and integers \(q\ge1\) satisfying
\[
                     \limsup\frac{q\log 2}{nb}<\frac{h_*}{d},
\]
there are rows \(B\) with \(B\bmod 2^q\in K_b\bmod 2^q\), \(G_b(B)(c)B(c^n)\ne0\) at every root \(c\) of \(F\), and \(\log\|B\|_1/n\to S\).
Both endpoint blocks of \(\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\) coefficients of \(B\) can agree exactly with those of a genuine row \(A\in K_b\) of slope \(S\) having at least one nonzero formal endpoint.
In particular they have the same limiting exterior coefficient faces, and the strict height budget in the dyadic theorem holds if \(8S<\log 2\).
The [resultant upper bound](Yin_C_G0345_v445.md#upper) gives the opposite weak inequality on the limiting precision for any such rows, so the constant above is sharp.
The construction here does not require coprimality of \(B\) and its reversal.
The dyadic theorem gives that stronger property in its stated smaller range.

<a id="columns"></a>
## Two independent short polynomial columns

Let a column \(v=(U,V)^{\mathsf t}\) of integer polynomials have graph \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\).
Call it exact if \(F\) divides this graph, and use the larger row one-norm as its norm.
Polynomial multiples of exact columns remain exact.
For each fixed \(\eta>0\) we first construct, at all sufficiently large \(n\), a \(2\)-by-\(2\) polynomial matrix \(M\) with exact columns independent over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\), degrees at most a fixed \(w=w(\eta)\), and norm at most \(\exp((S+\eta)n)\) in each column.

To see the independence, fix \(0<\tau<\eta\), choose \(w\) large, put \(L=2(w+1)\), and take all pairs of degree at most \(w\) with coefficients in \([0,T]\), \(T=\lfloor\exp((S+\tau)n)\rfloor\).
Record the value and the first Euler derivative of the graph at one root of each \(P_i\) in its own degree-\(d\) field.
Every entry belongs to the ring of integers.
At an embedding taking the root to \(c\), its absolute value is bounded by \(O_w(T(1+n)\max(1,|c|)^{nw+1})\).
The fixed embedding lattices have bounded fundamental domains.
Counting their points in these archimedean boxes, with complex embeddings counted twice, bounds the number of joint images by
\[
                         O_w((1+n)^{4d}T^{4d}\exp(2nwS)).
\]
The shortest sides already tend to infinity, so enlarging by the fixed domains costs a constant factor.
Subtracting one vector from the largest fiber gives distinct exact integer columns with logarithmic count at least
\[
             n\{L(S+\tau)-4d(S+\tau)-2wS\}-O_w(\log(2+n)).            \tag{1}
\]

For a rational polynomial line containing such a nonzero column, take a primitive integral polynomial pair spanning it with coprime entries over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\) and joint content one.
Its integral polynomial multiples exhaust the integral columns on that line by Gauss's lemma.
Among these multiples exactness means that the multiplier vanishes to the residual required orders at the two powered orbits.
Indeed substitution by \(X^n\) is unramified at each root and the powers separate the orbits.
Thus all the exact columns on the line are precisely the integral polynomial multiples of a single integral column \(v_0\), obtained by inserting the required powers of the two monic powered minima.
If the line meets the counted set nontrivially, \(\deg v_0\le w\).
By the [even-order lower bound for arbitrary two-row graphs](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#jets), uniformly for these columns \(\log\|v_0\|\ge n(S-o(1))\).
For the row of largest norm its Mahler measure is at least \(2^{-w}\|v_0\|\).
An integral multiplier giving a column of norm at most \((w+1)T\) consequently has one-norm at most \(2^{2w}(w+1)T/\|v_0\|\).
There are at most \(\exp(n(w+1)(\tau+o(1)))\) such multipliers, uniformly in the line for fixed \(w\).
The coefficient of \(n\) in (1) exceeds \((w+1)\tau\) for all sufficiently large fixed \(w\).
Hence the counted columns span two dimensions over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\), and their norms are eventually below the claimed bound.

<a id="residues"></a>
## Choosing the sizes of the conjugate residues

Write \(D(Y)=\det M(Y)\ne0\), choose one \(i\), and put \(c=\delta^i\), \(\beta=c^n\), \(K=\mathbb Q(c)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
The minimum \(Q(Y)\) of \(\beta\) divides \(D\), since evaluation of either column at \(\beta\) lies in the line \(U+cV=0\).
The other powered minimum also divides \(D\) and is distinct.
Let \(m\ge1\) be the full multiplicity of \(Q\) in \(D\), bounded by \(2w\).
Let \(e=(1,0)^{\mathsf t}\).
We will use an integral polynomial \(E\) and the identity
\[
        M f=W,\qquad f=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(M)e E}{Q^m},
                 \qquad W=e(D/Q^m)E .                                 \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(W\) is integral and its first entry is nonzero at \(\beta\) if \(E(\beta)\ne0\).
Its first entry vanishes at the other powered orbit.

Put \(t=\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\) and \(p=\lfloor b/2\rfloor-t-2w-3\).
The precision assumption and \(\sum_\sigma|\log|\sigma c||=2h_i\) give a constant \(\kappa>0\) for which the numbers
\[
           u_\sigma=np|\log|\sigma c||-q\log 2-\kappa nb
\]
have positive sum for all sufficiently large \(n\), counting both embeddings of each complex pair.
The [logarithmic unit lattice](../audits/literature/Con26/Yin_U002.md#con26-u002) supplies a unit \(a\in\mathcal O_K^\times\) with \(\log|\sigma a|\le u_\sigma+O_K(1)\).
Indeed subtract the common mean from the \(u_\sigma\), using weight two at a complex place, and approximate the resulting vector in the sum-zero hyperplane by a lattice point within bounded distance.

Prescribe the value \(\sigma a\) at \(\sigma\beta\) and zero derivatives of orders \(1,\ldots,m-1\) there.
Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial of degree less than \(dm\) with rational coefficients by Galois invariance.
One integer \(s\) with \(1\le s\le\exp(O_w(n))\) clears its denominators uniformly in \(a\).
In fact the confluent evaluation matrix in a fixed normal closure has integral entries of size \(\exp(O_w(n))\) at every embedding, and its nonzero determinant has absolute field norm at most \(\exp(O_w(n))\).
Multiplication by the absolute norm clears its inverse on integral right sides by the adjugate formula.
Use \(E\) for the resulting integral polynomial with values \(s\sigma a\).
Distinct conjugates \(\beta',\beta''\) satisfy \(|\beta'-\beta''|\ge\exp(-O(n))\) by the same integral norm bound.

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Rounding a two-sided expansion

Expand each coordinate of \(f\) in (2) as a polynomial part plus partial fractions \(C_{\beta',l}(Y-\beta')^{-l}\).
At \(\beta'=\sigma\beta\) their coefficients satisfy
\[
                        |C_{\beta',l}|\le |\sigma a|\exp(O_w(n)).
\]
Indeed they are Taylor coefficients of order below \(m\) after multiplying by \((Y-\beta')^m\).
The prescribed derivatives of \(E\) leave only its value in these coefficients, while the matrix coefficients, powers of the roots and inverses of root differences cost at most \(\exp(O_w(n))\).
The degree of the polynomial part is bounded in terms of \(w,d\).

At poles with \(|\beta'|\ge1\) expand in nonnegative powers of \(Y/\beta'\).
At poles with \(|\beta'|<1\) expand in negative powers using \(\beta'/Y\).
The resulting two-sided real sequence of vector coefficients satisfies \(Mf=W\) coefficientwise, since multiplication by a polynomial is a finite convolution and each chosen series satisfies its own rational identity.
Conjugate poles use the same choice, and at unit-circle poles the series identity is purely formal.
For indices \(j\) within \(2w+2\) of either \(p\) or \(-p\) the coefficients of \(2^q f\) have magnitude bounded by
\[
    \sum_{\sigma}2^q|\sigma a|\exp(O_w(n))(1+b)^{O_w(1)}
                           \exp(-np|\log|\sigma c||)=o(1).
\]
This follows directly from the binomial coefficients of each pole power on the side where it contributes, including \(|\sigma c|=1\).
The fixed-degree polynomial part has no coefficients in these ranges once \(b\) is sufficiently large.
The factor \(\exp(-\kappa nb)\) absorbs the displayed losses at fixed \(w\).

Truncate the coefficients of \(2^q f\) to \([-p,p]\) and round each retained real coordinate to a nearest integer, giving a Laurent polynomial column \(N\).
Then
\[
                               J=MN-2^q W
\]
is integral and supported on \([-p,p+w]\) for large \(b\).
In this interval its coefficients are bounded by \(O(\|M\|_1)\), with \(\|M\|_1\) the sum of the four row-polynomial norms.
Indeed (2) expresses each coefficient as a finite convolution of \(M\) with the rounding error and, at the two boundaries, the omitted adjacent coefficients just bounded by \(o(1)\).
There are \(O(b)\) positions.
Translation by \(Y^{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}\), with an optional extra shift by one, puts \(J\) strictly inside the width \(b\), leaving both endpoint blocks of length \(t\) zero.
Both translations of \(MN\) are ordinary exact polynomial columns since each translated multiplier has only nonnegative exponents.
Thus the translated \(J\) is coefficientwise congruent modulo \(2^q\) to an integral exact column.

<a id="projection"></a>
## Signed rows and their ends

For an integral column \((U,V)^{\mathsf t}\) supported in \([0,b]\) take the row \(U+\varepsilon Y^bV(1/Y)\).
Its signed graph is the sum of the column graph and \(\varepsilon\) times its reversal at \(nb+1\).
Since \(F\) is reciprocal, projection preserves exactness and congruence to an exact integral row.
For \(J\) corresponding to \(i\), its signed graph vanishes at the other orbit, because both entries of \(W\) vanish there, including at the inverse nodes.
At \(c\) the unsubtracted exact term vanishes.
The two translates of \(W\) contribute a nonzero first term at \(c\) and its reflected term, with the ratio of the multipliers changing by \(c^{2n}\ne1\) between the translates.
Hence at least one projected translate misses \(P_i\).
Choose such a translate for each \(i\), and add the two projected rows to obtain a row \(J'\) whose graph misses both factors.
It is congruent to an integral exact row, both endpoint blocks vanish, and for fixed \(\eta\) its norm is at most \(O(b)\exp((S+\eta)n)\).
Taking \(\eta\) to zero by a diagonal choice along the eventual ranges gives \(\log\max(1,\|J'\|_1)/n\le S+o(1)\).

Choose a genuine exact signed comparison row of slope \(S\) at width \(\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor\) by the [sharp even-order construction](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#statement).
Remove its common initial monomial with its formal reversal, shortening the width by twice that order, then pad to \(b\) with a power of \(1+Y\).
These operations preserve genuine signed divisibility and at least one nonzero endpoint, and padding costs \(\exp(o(n))\).
The even-order lower bound still gives slope \(S\) for the padded row \(A\).
In \(B=A+uJ'\) at most \(2d\) positive integers \(u\) can cancel any numerator value at the chosen roots, since \(A(c^n)\ne0\).
Among \(1,\ldots,2d+2\) take two surviving choices.
For distinct choices \(u,v\), the identity \(v(A+uJ')-u(A+vJ')=(v-u)A\) ensures that at least one resulting norm is bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(\|A\|_1\).
For that choice \(B\) has slope \(S\), the required endpoint equality and both missing graph factors.
The [finite exterior truncation](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) transfers the exterior coefficient faces.
The upper bound in the statement follows for arbitrary \(n\) by taking the norm at either missing factor exactly as in the cited resultant proof.
No reduction or lifting condition on \(F\bmod 2\) enters the construction.
