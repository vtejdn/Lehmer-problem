# A short denominator row bounds the resultant in terms of the power index

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two nonconstant rows for every prescribed difference

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\).
Let \(p\) be any prime such that \(\beta=\alpha^p\) also has degree \(d\), write \(Q\) for its monic minimal polynomial, and put
\[
 I=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]],\qquad h=\log M(P),\qquad
 J_e=p^{d\binom e2} I^{e^2}\quad(e\ge1).
\]
Choose a positive integer multiplicity \(e\), two residues \(0\le a<b<p\), and an integer \(1\le j\le d\).
There is a primitive polynomial
\[
                  F=x^a U(x^p)-x^b V(x^p),\qquad P^e\mid F ,
\]
in which \(P\) has multiplicity exactly \(e\) and the two integral rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\) with nonzero constant terms.
For some \(1\le m\le j\) their degrees are \(\deg V=m\), \(\deg U=ed+m\), and \(\deg F=p(ed+m)+a<p(ed+j+1)\).
Thus \(N=\deg U+\deg V=ed+2m\) is the sum of the stripped row degrees.
For \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\),
\[
 \frac{\log H}{N}\ \le\ \log(2j)+\frac{\log J_e}{j}
             +\frac{m}{d}\,p h+\frac{m}{ed}\log(3m/2).                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular a small normalized index \(\log I/(pd)\), together with bounded \(h\), allows a small normalized resultant \(\log H/(Np)\) for double roots in the fourth-logarithmic prime window, even if the exponent of the order quotient is large.

<a id="denominator"></a>
## A short polynomial in the kernel of the order congruence

Set \(r=b-a\) and use the rings \(A_e=\mathbb Z[x]/(P^e)\), \(B_e=\mathbb Z[X]/(Q^e)\), embedded by \(X=x^p\).
Their additive index \([A_e:B_e]\) equals \(J_e\), by the [index on successive multiplicity layers](Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#layers).
In particular this holds for \(e=1\).
Map each integral polynomial \(W\) of degree below \(j\) additively to the class of \(x^rW(x^p)\) in \(A_e/B_e\).
For \(T=\lfloor J_e^{1/j}\rfloor\), there are \((T+1)^j>J_e\) coefficient vectors with entries in \(\{0,\ldots,T\}\).
Two have the same class, so their difference gives a nonzero \(W\) in the kernel with \(\|W\|_1\le jT\), where \(\|\cdot\|_1\) denotes the sum of the absolute coefficients.
It is coprime to \(Q\), since \(\deg W<j\le d\) and \(Q\) is irreducible.

Write \(W=X^t W_0\) with \(W_0(0)\ne0\), and put \(V=(X+1)W_0\).
Both \(X\) and \(X^{-1}\) act on \(B_e\), since \(Q(0)=\pm1\), so the congruence \(x^rV(x^p)\in B_e\) holds in \(A_e\).
As \(\beta\) is nontorsion, \(Q(-1)\ne0\).
Thus \(V(0)\ne0\), \(\gcd(V,Q)=1\), \(1\le m=\deg V\le j\), and
\[
                       \|V\|_1\le 2j J_e^{1/j}.                        \tag{2}
\]
Let \(U_0\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be the representative of degree below \(ed\) of \(x^rV(x^p)\) in \(B_e\), obtained by monic division by \(Q^e\).
All rows \(U=U_0+Q^e C\) with \(C\in\mathbb Z[X]\) satisfy \(U(x^p)-x^rV(x^p)\equiv0\bmod P^e\).

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Controlling the numerator at the zeros of the denominator

Work first in \(\mathbb Q[X]/(V)\), where \(Q^e\) is invertible.
Take \(C_*\in\mathbb Q[X]\) of degree below \(m\) representing \(-U_0/Q^e\) there, and round its coefficients to nearest integers to get \(C_0\).
For each distinct complex zero \(z\) of \(V\), at most one integer \(v\) makes \(C_0(z)+v=C_*(z)\).
There are at most \(m\) such zeros, so some \(v\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) avoids them all.
For \(S=C_0+v-C_*\) this gives \(\|S\|_1\le3m/2\), \(\deg S<m\), and \(S(z)\ne0\) at every zero of \(V\).

Set \(U=U_0+Q^e(C_0+v+wV)\).
At most one \(w\) gives \(U(0)=0\), since \(Q(0)^e V(0)\ne0\).
Changing \(w\) by one changes the unshifted witness by \(Q(x^p)^e V(x^p)\), which contains exactly \(e\) copies of \(P\) since the simple root \(\beta\) of \(Q\) pulls back to a simple root at \(\alpha\) and \(V(\beta)\ne0\).
Thus at most one \(w\) gives divisibility by \(P^{e+1}\).
Choose \(w\in\{1,2,3\}\) outside both exceptions.
Since \(wV\) has degree \(m\), \(U\) has degree \(ed+m\).
At every zero \(z\) of \(V\),
\[
                        U(z)=Q(z)^e S(z)\ne0 .
\]
Thus the rows are coprime even when \(V\) has repeated roots.
Write \(c=|\operatorname{lc}V|\).
Multiplying over those \(m\) roots with multiplicity, using \(|S(z)|\le(3m/2)\max(1,|z|)^{m-1}\), gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Res}(V,U)|
 \ \le\ c\,M(V)^{m-1}(3m/2)^m|\operatorname{Res}(V,Q)|^e.             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the leading coefficient for the left side contributes \(c^{ed+m}\), while \(|\operatorname{Res}(V,Q)|^e\) contributes \(c^{ed}\) and \(\prod_z\max(1,|z|)=M(V)/c\).
By evaluating \(V\) at the \(d\) conjugates of \(\beta\) one also has
\[
              |\operatorname{Res}(V,Q)|\le\|V\|_1^d M(Q)^m
                                            =\|V\|_1^d\exp(mph),
\]
since the conjugates of \(\beta\) are precisely the \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
The inequalities \(c\le M(V)\le\|V\|_1\) follow from the root formula and the circle integral for Mahler measure.
Thus (3) is at most \(\|V\|_1^{ed+m}(3m/2)^m\exp(emph)\).
Multiplication of the original two-row expression by \(x^a\) gives the promised classes and degree.
Its positive scalar content divides both rows because the two classes are disjoint.
Removing it preserves the \(P^e\) divisor by monic division, preserves both row degrees and divides the resultant by that content to the power \(N\).
Division of the log bound by \(N\ge ed\), with \((ed+m)/N\le1\), now gives (1) by (2).

<a id="window"></a>
## How much index a uniform double-root resultant would require

Fix \(e=2\).
For \(P,p,a,b\) as above let \(\rho\) be the infimum of \(\log H/(Np)\) over all primitive coprime two-row multiples of \(P^2\) on these prescribed residues of ordinary degree at most \(p(16d+1)\), with \(H,N\) computed after stripping the individual row monomials.
The value \(N\) is positive because a two-term polynomial has only simple nonzero roots in characteristic zero.
All the witnesses of (1) are admissible, since their degrees are less than \(p(3d+1)\).
Write
\[
 x=\frac{\log p}{p}+\frac{4\log I}{pd},\qquad
 \Phi(x,h)=
 \begin{cases}2\sqrt{xh},&0\le x\le h,\\ x+h,&x\ge h.\end{cases}
\]
Then for every such input, prime and pair of classes,
\[
              \rho\le\Phi(x,h)+\frac hd+
                     \frac{\log(2d)+\tfrac12\log(3d/2)}p .             \tag{4}
\]
Indeed (1), \(m\le j\le d\) and \(\log J_2=d p x\) give the last logarithmic error plus \(dx/j+jh/d\).
The infimum of \(x/t+th\) over \(0<t\le1\) is \(\Phi(x,h)\).
Here \(x,h>0\), and rounding its minimizer \(t=\min(1,\sqrt{x/h})\) up to \(j=\lceil dt\rceil\) adds at most \(h/d\).

Let \(d\to\infty\) with \(h\le h_0<\infty\), and choose any degree-preserving prime in the [fourth-logarithmic window](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) for each input.
The residues can vary freely with the input and the prime.
If \(\log I=o(pd)\), (4) gives \(\rho\to0\), attained asymptotically by witnesses with two nonconstant rows.
Conversely if \(\liminf\rho\ge c_0>0\), then
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log I}{pd}\ \ge\
  \begin{cases}
        c_0^2/(16h_0),&0<c_0\le2h_0,\\
        (c_0-h_0)/4,&c_0>2h_0 .
  \end{cases}                                                         \tag{5}
\]
In fact \(\log d=o(p)\) in this window, \(\log p=o(p)\), and \(\Phi(x,h)\) is continuous and nondecreasing in both arguments.
Taking a subsequence realizing the lower limit of \(x\) in (4) and inverting \(\Phi(x,h_0)\) proves (5), with infinite lower limit immediate.
The same implications hold for any degree-preserving sequence with \(\log d=o(p)\).
The [constant-row construction](Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#statement) uses the exponent of the additive quotient, whereas (4) and (5) depend on its order \(I\).
The bounds concern the full resultant of a selectable witness and leave its archimedean cofactor measure to separate estimates.
