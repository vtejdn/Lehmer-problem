# Density one for irreducibility at every dilation in moving rectangles of integer Salem seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous irreducibility for both signs

For \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\), let \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}\) be the minimal polynomial of the exterior Salem root \(r_{a,n,\epsilon}\) of \(X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\), where \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Call a pair \((a,n)\) exceptional if \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}(X^k)\) is reducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for some sign and some positive integer \(k\).
Write \(B(A;M,N)\) for the number of exceptional pairs with \(3\le a\le A,\ M<n\le M+N\), for integers \(A\ge3,\ M\ge6,\ N\ge1\).
Then
\[
 \lim_{\min(A,N)\to\infty}\ \sup_{M\ge6}
                   \frac{B(A;M,N)}{(A-2)N}=0,\qquad
 \lim_{A\to\infty}\ \sup_{N\ge1}
                   \frac{B(A;6,N)}{(A-2)N}=0.                        \tag{1}
\]
There is no relative growth restriction on the sides of the rectangle.
At each nonexceptional pair and either sign the unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor of the original polynomial after any substitution \(X\mapsto X^k\) has logarithmic Mahler measure exactly \(\log r_{a,n,\epsilon}\).
The second limit includes arbitrarily short initial intervals of delays.
In the first limit arbitrary translates are allowed when the interval length tends to infinity.

<a id="sieve"></a>
## A fixed radical prime in two varying integer parameters

Fix a prime \(p\) and a sign \(\epsilon\).
Let \(R\) be the largest positive integer \(q\) with \(r_{a,n,\epsilon}^{1/q}\in\mathbb Q(r_{a,n,\epsilon})\).
We first show that pairs with \(p\mid R\) have density zero in the moving rectangles of the first limit.
Fix \(b\ge2\) and write \(m=p^b\).
Use the auxiliary primes \(\lambda\equiv1+m\bmod mp\).
At each such prime choose a root \(z\in\mathbb F_\lambda^\times\) of order \(m\).
Every \(p\)-th root of \(z\) has order \(mp\), so its Frobenius orbit has size \(p\), since \(1+m\) has order \(p\) modulo \(mp\).
Thus a simple root at \(z\) would exclude \(p\mid R\) by the [factor degree criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v717.md#statement) applied to \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}(X^p)\) and the [field radical formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).

Fix a residue \(u\bmod m\) for \(n\), omitting \(u\equiv1\bmod p^{b-1}\).
Then \(z^{u-1}\ne\epsilon\), because equality would give \(p^b\mid2(u-1)\).
In particular for this \(z,u,\epsilon\) there is exactly one residue
\[
                a_\lambda=\frac{z^{u+1}-\epsilon}{z^u-\epsilon z}
                                \pmod\lambda                           \tag{2}
\]
giving \(G_a(z)=0\) whenever \(n\equiv u\bmod m\).
It differs from \(z\) since \(G_z(z)=\epsilon(z^2-1)\ne0\) at \(m\ge4\).
At \(a=a_\lambda\) the derivative at \(z\) is
\[
                 n z^{u-1}(z-a_\lambda)+z^u+\epsilon a_\lambda.
\]
It vanishes at exactly one residue of \(n\bmod\lambda\).
Away from that residue the root is simple in \(G_a\) and in \(P_{a,n,\epsilon}\), since the [removed factors](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) have roots only at \(1,-1\).
Thus, conditional on \(n\equiv u\bmod m\), exactly \(\lambda-1\) out of the \(\lambda^2\) pairs of residues modulo \(\lambda\) give this particular simple-root obstruction.

For any finite set \(\Lambda\) of the designated auxiliary primes, these pairs are independent in the uniform residue counts by the Chinese remainder theorem, since the primes are distinct and prime to \(m\).
At fixed \(m,\Lambda\) the two integer intervals approach uniformity on their residue classes as \(A,N\to\infty\), uniformly in \(M\): the counts of any one residue in two intervals of the same length differ by at most one.
After allowing the omitted classes of \(u\), the upper limiting fraction with \(p\mid R\) is therefore at most
\[
              p^{1-b}+
                 \prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
                     \left(1-\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^2}\right).       \tag{3}
\]
At this fixed \(p,b\), the class \(1+m\bmod mp\) is reduced, and the [divergence of the sum of prime reciprocals in a fixed progression](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#sut21-u001) makes the product tend to zero along increasing finite sets.
Letting \(b\) grow after this step proves the assertion for the fixed radical prime.
This order of limits uses the source only at fixed modulus.

<a id="tail"></a>
## Large radical primes and short initial intervals

Set \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{i=-1,0,1}((a+i)/\operatorname{rad}(a+i))\).
The [all-delay exceptional-seed count](Yin_C_G0345_v703.md#seeds) allows us to remove at most \(\exp(C\sqrt{\log(A+1)})\) seeds up to \(A\), after which \(R\mid H(a)\) for every delay and both signs.
For any \(Y\ge2\), a remaining prime \(p>Y\) dividing \(R\) must have \(p^2\mid a+i\) for some \(i=-1,0,1\).
Thus \(p\le\sqrt{A+1}\), and the number of eligible seeds for these primes is at most
\[
             3A\sum_{j>Y}j^{-2}+3\sqrt{A+1}.
\]
Both estimates concern the seed alone and therefore hold in every translated interval of delays with the same relative error.
At each of the finitely many \(p\le Y\), the fixed-prime result at both signs gives zero upper limiting fraction.
Taking \(Y\to\infty\) gives the first limit, since the [positive-unit substitution formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) identifies exceptional pairs with those having \(R>1\) at some sign.
The same formula and the removed linear factors give the assertion on the unique noncyclotomic factor and its measure.

If the second limit failed, some sequence \(A\to\infty\) with corresponding initial interval lengths would have exceptional fraction bounded below by a positive constant.
Unbounded lengths have a subsequence tending to infinity, already excluded by the first limit.
Bounded lengths involve only finitely many fixed delays, each with only finitely many exceptional seeds by the [fixed-delay cutoff](Yin_C_G0345_v694.md#statement).
Their exceptional fraction tends to zero as well.
