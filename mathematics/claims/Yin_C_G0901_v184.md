# A pinned exterior root with no two conjugates in one open dyadic unit disk

<a id="statement"></a>
## Single-prime controls with pairwise distinct residues

Fix an odd prime \(\ell\).
For every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four, put \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ M=pq,\ K=(2q+1)^2,\ J=q/2+1\).
There are arbitrarily large degrees \(d=2D\) and monic reciprocal irreducible \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) having the [joint trace-pole and capacity properties](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement) and satisfying, with absolute constants,
\[
                    c q\,p^{-2D}<s-T<C q\,p^{-2D}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Here \(T>1\) is the unique exterior root, the first \(J\) Newton sums vanish, \(q^{2D}F(s)=K\), and every powered segment-capacity test holds.
Every pair of distinct roots in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) has dyadic distance **one**.
Thus every open unit disk, including every closed disk of radius \(1/8\), contains at most one conjugate.
The actual primitive integer minimum has measure \(\ell^aT\) for the prescribed sufficiently large denominator exponents of the joint construction.

There are two choices of residue behavior.
One may require the binary reduction to be irreducible, so all \(2D\) roots form a single unramified Frobenius orbit.
Alternatively one may impose a squarefree cyclotomic residue polynomial of order \(5D/2\) with all its root orders divisible by five.
In this second construction, for any precision \(b\ge1\) **fixed before choosing the degree**, one can realize the entire cyclotomic residue polynomial modulo \(2^b\).
This yields a finite positive power relation at that precision without asserting an exact multiplicative relation in the number field or the full set of necessary exponent estimates.
The alternative [clustered models](Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#control) can likewise be given (1).
Hence the sharp real pin and these archimedean and local integrality conditions permit both behaviors when integrality at one odd prime is released.

<a id="pin"></a>
## Pinning rate at the real pole before and after approximation

Use the [real model](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#real) \(F_t=B_L Q_t\) with \(L=2D-2J-1\), \(B_L=1+\cdots+Z^{L-1}\), and \(Q_t=(Z-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-tZ^{J+1}\).
For \(U=\log s\) its outer logarithm \(u\) solves \(f_J(u)=t\), where \(f_J(v)=4\sinh(v/2)\sinh((J+1/2)v)\).
The exact parameter giving the specified pole satisfies
\[
 f_J(U)-t=\frac{K}{q^{2D}s^{J+1}B_L(s)}
         =\frac{K(s-1)s^J}{p^{2D}(1-s^{-L})}.
\]
For fixed \(q\) and sufficiently large admissible \(D\) we have \((1-1/q)U<u<U\) and \(1-s^{-L}\ge1/2\).
Throughout that interval \(f'_J(v)\) lies between absolute positive constants for all sufficiently large \(q\), because \((J+1/2)v\) tends uniformly to \(1/2\) and \((J+1/2)\sinh(v/2)\) stays bounded.
Also \(s^J\) is bounded above and below by positive constants and \(K(s-1)\asymp q\).
The mean value theorem gives \(s-e^u\asymp q p^{-2D}\) with absolute constants.
For each fixed \(q,D\) one may shrink the open real neighborhood in the [simultaneous approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#place) so that \(s-T\) differs by less than half of \(s-e^u\).
All strict inequalities used in the joint construction persist.
The same choice can be made when imposing the clustered local data, since their approximation permits every sufficiently small such real neighborhood.

<a id="word"></a>
## A cyclotomic binary word and an arbitrary prescribed finite precision

Write \(e=v_2(q)\).
In the [degree congruences](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#congruences) require \(p^D\equiv1+2q\bmod q^{J+1+\lceil b/e\rceil}\), leaving \((1-p)^D\equiv1-2p\bmod p^{J+1}\) unchanged.
The principal-unit argument at the prime divisors of \(q\) works at the higher precision with unchanged reduction \(D\equiv2\) at each such prime, and the Chinese remainder conditions ensuring the rotations remain valid.
In fact \(D\equiv2\bmod q\) by expanding the first congruence modulo \(q^2\), so \(m=D/2\) is odd.
Take \(D\) large enough that \(m>J+1+\lceil b/e\rceil\).

Put \(C(Z)=\Phi_5(Z^m)=1+Z^m+Z^{2m}+Z^{3m}+Z^{4m}\).
It is integral and reciprocal with the required zero prefix.
Its evaluation \(q^{2D}C(s)\) is congruent to \(K\) modulo \(2^bM^{J+1}\).
At the \(q\)-part only the leading term \(p^{2D}\) survives modulo \(q^{J+1+\lceil b/e\rceil}\), and at the \(p\)-part only the constant contribution \(q^{2D}\) survives modulo \(p^{J+1}\).
Equation (1) of the degree congruences, with the increased precision, gives the same \(K\) in each case.
For monic reciprocal degree \(2D\) with zero prefix of length \(J\) the integral increments of the pole functional have gcd \(M^{J+1}\).
Consequently adding \(2^b\) times an integral combination of the free coefficient pairs and center corrects the pole exactly and leaves \(C\bmod2^b\) unchanged.
Use the resulting integral pole-and-prefix representative as the prescribed congruence modulo \(2^b\) in the joint construction, with the smaller real neighborhood above.

In characteristic two, \(Z^{5m}-1\) is separable because \(m\) is odd, and \(C\) is the quotient by \(Z^m-1\).
Thus all residual roots are distinct, each \(m\)-th power has order five, and a primitive \(5m\)-th root occurs.
The order of \(z\) modulo \(C\) in characteristic two is exactly \(5m\).
The local field extensions are unramified by simple-root lifting and no two conjugates have positive dyadic difference valuation.
The linear period \(5m\) also passes the test of dividing a binomial with the [polynomial degree cap](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#words); this assertion concerns that test alone.

For clarity, in the quotient modulo \(2^b\) by the resulting \(F\), put \(x=q/p\) and \(W=(1-x/z)/(1-xz)\).
The element \(z\) is a unit, \(z^{5m}=1\) by the congruence to \(C\), and \(W\equiv1\bmod2^e\).
Taking \(u_0=2^{\max(1,b-e)}\) and \(v_0=5m+u_0\) gives coprime positive exponents and \(z^{v_0-u_0}=W^{u_0}\) in this quotient, by repeated squaring.
This exhibits one soluble finite-place equation.
In particular the exponents witnessing this local congruence do not imply that the corresponding distinguished real values are powers of a common unit. [Global integrality and dependence](Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement)

## One unramified orbit instead of a cyclotomic residue word

Take the strengthened admissible degrees just obtained at \(b=1\), and an integral representative for which the first \(J+1\) binary coefficients below the leading one vanish.
Such a representative was obtained by lifting \(C\bmod2\) above.
For fixed \(q\) and hence fixed \(J\), Gao's prescribed-coefficient estimate gives a monic self-reciprocal irreducible \(S\) of degree \(2D\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) with those same \(J+1\) zero coefficients for every sufficiently large \(D\).
Specifically Theorem 2(b), inequality (16), gives a lower count \(2^{D-J-1}/(2D)-(2J+4)2^{D/2}/D\) when \(J+1\le\lceil D/2\rceil-1\), which is positive eventually. [Gao, AofA 2022, p. 9:4](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2022.9)

The integral triangular trace change \(F=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\) carries equality of these first \(J+1\) reciprocal coefficients to equality of the corresponding trace coefficients, also in characteristic two.
Thus the trace of \(S\) differs from the binary trace of the integral representative by a polynomial of degree at most \(D-J-2\).
In the exact pole-and-prefix slice one may add \((MX-1)G\) for an arbitrary integral \(G\) of that degree.
Since \(M\) is even, such an addition realizes precisely the required difference modulo two.
Impose the lifted representative as the congruence in the same joint construction.
Now the entire degree-\(2D\) reduction is irreducible and separable, and its roots lift to a single orbit in the unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\) of degree \(2D\).
This choice does not prescribe the earlier cyclotomic period.
