# The perturbation span required to represent a Salem polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound uniform in both the parent and the cofactor

Fix \(M>0\).
Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\lambda\) of degree \(d\), with \(m=\log\lambda\le M\).
Suppose \(P\) divides the nonzero polynomial \(A+X^k B\), where \(A\) is a nonconstant product of monic cyclotomic polynomials, \(k\ge0\), and \(B\) is any nonzero real polynomial with \(B(0)\ne0\).
Here divisibility is in \(\mathbb R[X]\), so the same hypothesis includes integer coefficients.
Write \(e=\deg B\), the difference between the highest and lowest exponents of the perturbation.
For sufficiently large \(d\), with constants depending only on \(M\),
\[
 e\gg_M \frac{d\min(m^2,1)}{\log(2d)}
 \gg_M \frac{d(\log\log d)^6}{(\log d)^7}.
\]
The conclusion holds at arbitrary offsets, with arbitrary coefficients and without a bound on the degree of \(A\) or the cofactor.
Consequently an infinite bounded-measure sequence of distinct Salem numbers cannot be represented using perturbations of bounded span.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Eliminating the offset

For a real polynomial \(U\) with \(U(0)\ne0\), define the polynomial \(C_U(t)\) by \(C_U(z+z^{-1})=U(z)U(z^{-1})\).
The right side is a symmetric Laurent polynomial and hence a polynomial in \(t\), by the recurrence for \(z^j+z^{-j}\).
If \(U\) has degree \(n\), nonzero leading coefficient and nonzero constant coefficient, \(C_U\) has degree \(n\), by the coefficient of \(z^n\).
For each root \(\rho\) the identity \((z-\rho)(z^{-1}-\rho)=-\rho(t-\rho-\rho^{-1})\) describes the corresponding root of \(C_U\), with multiplicity.
In particular all \(D=\deg A\) roots of \(C_A\) lie in \([-2,2]\).

At every root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), both \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\) satisfy the divisibility equation, so \(C_A(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})=C_B(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
These common values are nonzero because \(A\) has only roots of unity.
There are \(d/2\) distinct traces, consisting of \(s=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}>2\) and \(d/2-1\) real traces in \((-2,2)\).
If \(C_A=C_B\), every root of \(B\) has trace in \([-2,2]\), and therefore lies on the circle.
Reality forces equal multiplicities at either member of every nonreal inverse pair.
The trace multiplicities then recover the individual root multiplicities of \(A\) and \(B\), including the singleton fibers at \(1\) and \(-1\).
Consequently \(B=cA\) for a real scalar \(c\) with \(c^2=1\).
The nonzero polynomial \(A(1+cX^k)\) cannot vanish at \(\lambda\).
Thus \(C_A-C_B\) is a nonzero polynomial with \(d/2\) distinct roots, so \(\max(D,e)\ge d/2\).

<a id="packing"></a>
## Angular crowding at scale \(1/d\)

Write \(\theta_j=\arg\alpha_j\), and \(S_\ell=\sum_j e^{i\ell\theta_j}\).
For \(\beta_j=e^{-|\log|\alpha_j||+i\theta_j}\) the [reflected discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives
\[
 \sum_{1\le\ell\le d}\frac{1}{\ell}
          \left|\sum_j\beta_j^\ell\right|^2\ll_M d\log(2d).
\]
Only the two positive real conjugates differ from their radial projections, so replacing the reflected sum by \(S_\ell\) costs at most two at each positive frequency.
Using \(\ell\le d\) and summing squares gives \(\sum_{1\le\ell\le d}|S_\ell|^2\ll_M d^2\log(2d)\).
Put \(F_d(x)=d^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}e^{ijx}|^2\).
Its Fourier coefficients are \((1-|\ell|/d)_+\), and \(F_d(x)\gg d\) for circular distance \(|x|\le1/d\), by the finite geometric sum.
It follows that the number of ordered pairs of conjugate phases at circular distance at most \(1/d\), including the diagonal, satisfies
\[
 \#\{(i,j):|\theta_i-\theta_j|_{\mathbb T}\le1/d\}
 \ll d^{-1}\sum_{i,j}F_d(\theta_i-\theta_j)
 \ll_M d\log(2d).
\]
Partition the circle into equal bins of width \(2\pi/\lceil2\pi d\rceil\).
If \(n_j\) phases fall in the \(j\)-th bin then \(\sum_j n_j^2\ll_M d\log(2d)\).
Any union of \(l\) such bins contains at most \(O_M(\sqrt{l d\log(2d)})\) phases.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## A neutral trace away from the perturbation roots

Put \(u=\min(s-2,1)\), so \(u\asymp_M\min(m^2,1)\) and \(s-2\ll_M u\).
Take the positive angular interval \(I\subset(0,\pi)\) on which \(t=2\cos\theta\) ranges over \([2-u/2,2-u/4]\).
Its length and the absolute derivative \(|dt/d\theta|\) on it are both comparable to \(\sqrt u\).
The [Dobrowolski lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\), so \(\sqrt u\gg_M\sqrt{\log(2d)/d}\) with ratio tending to infinity.
The [short-arc count](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives \(\gg_M d\sqrt u\) phases in \(I\), all belonging to neutral conjugates since the exterior and interior real conjugates have phase zero.

For each complex root \(\gamma\) of \(C_B\) exclude the part of \(I\) where \(|t-\operatorname{Re}\gamma|<\sqrt u/d\).
The derivative bound gives an excluded angular interval of length \(O(1/d)\), intersecting at most a fixed number of the bins above.
Thus the union for all \(e\) roots contains \(O_M(\sqrt{e d\log(2d)})\) phases.
If \(e<c_M du/\log(2d)\) for a sufficiently small \(c_M>0\), a neutral trace \(t\) survives with \(|t-\gamma|\ge\sqrt u/d\) for every root of \(C_B\).
This assumption also implies \(e<d/2\), whence \(D\ge d/2\).

For every real root \(\xi\in[-2,2]\) of \(C_A\), one has \(|s-\xi|/|t-\xi|\ge1+u/4\).
Indeed for \(\xi<t\) the ratio is \(1+(s-t)/(t-\xi)\), with \(s-t\ge u\) and \(t-\xi\le4\).
For \(\xi>t\) the ratio is at least \((s-2)/(2-t)\ge2\), and equality \(t=\xi\) was excluded by the nonzero common value.
On the other hand \(|s-\gamma|/|t-\gamma|\le1+|s-t|d/\sqrt u\le1+O_M(d)\) for every perturbation root.
Factoring the two trace polynomials and using their common values at \(s\) and \(t\) gives
\[
 D\log(1+u/4)
 \le\log\frac{|C_A(s)|}{|C_A(t)|}
 =\log\frac{|C_B(s)|}{|C_B(t)|}
 \ll_M e\log(2d).
\]
This contradicts the assumed upper bound on \(e\) upon further decreasing \(c_M\).
The two stated inequalities follow, and bounded measure bounds coefficients in each fixed degree, giving the sequence consequence.
