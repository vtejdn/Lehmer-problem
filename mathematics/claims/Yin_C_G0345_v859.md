# Mahler measures on generic torsion translates of a fixed plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform degree fraction on cofinite torsion fibers

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}]\) be primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with Newton polygon of area \(A>0\).
Write \(c\) for its number of geometric components, \(I\) for the [character lattice index](#lattice) of any one of them, and \(H=\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)\).
Fix any nonzero signed exponent pair \((a,b)=g(k,l)\), where \(g>0\), \(\gcd(|k|,|l|)=1\), and put \(n=\max(|k|,|l|)\).
Let \(W\) be the width of the Newton polygon in the functional \((u,v)\mapsto au+bv\).
There is a finite set \(E_{F,a,b}\subset\mu_\infty\) with the following property.
For all roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) with \(t=\eta^k/\xi^l\notin E_{F,a,b}\), every nonzero root \(z\) of \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\) satisfies
\[
                         [H(z):H]\ge W/(cI).
\]
Writing \(w=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}\xi,\operatorname{ord}\eta)\), each such nontorsion root satisfies
\[
 [\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\ge\frac{W\varphi(w)}{22A cI},\qquad
 m(z)\ge\frac{\varphi(w)}{22A cI}\bigl(m_2(F)-\epsilon_F(n)\bigr),
 \qquad \epsilon_F(n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here \(m(z)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive minimal polynomial and \(m_2(F)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure on the two-torus.
In particular \(m(z)\ge b_F\varphi(w)\) for a positive constant \(b_F\) independent of both exponents, both twists and the root.
The exceptional invariants may depend on the full exponent pair, including the common dilation, so the estimates leave open the behavior of the untwisted fiber as the direction varies.
The [two-pole result](Yin_C_G0345_v856.md#measure) and the [cubic graph example](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#torsion-twists) give full cyclotomic degree in their respective primitive directions.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## A component bound for all isogeny pullbacks

Fix a geometric component \(C\) over \(B=\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and its function field \(K=B(C)\).
The components are distinct and conjugate over \(\mathbb Q\), with identical support after multiplying their equations by suitable monomials to normalize the exponents.
Hence each has positive Newton area, since their product has positive area and the Newton polygon of a product is the Minkowski sum.
The classes of \(U,V\) generate a free rank-two lattice \(L\) in \(K^*/B^*\), since a nontrivial constant character would force \(C\) to be a torus coset with a binomial equation.
Let \(\Lambda\) be the saturation of \(L\) in this group, consisting of the classes some positive power of which belongs to \(L\).
On the smooth projective normalization the divisors of representatives of \(\Lambda\) are supported on the finite union of the supports of \(\operatorname{div}U\) and \(\operatorname{div}V\).
The divisor map injects \(K^*/B^*\) into the divisor group, and the integral divisors in the rational span of the two character divisors form a rank-two lattice.
Thus \(I=[\Lambda:L]\) is finite and independent of the choice of conjugate component.

For \(d\ge1\) choose \(u^d=U,v^d=V\) over \(K\).
The Galois group \(D\) of \(K(u,v)/K\) embeds into \(\mu_d^2\) by its action on the two radicals.
All geometric components of \([d]^{-1}C\) dominate \(C\) because the torus isogeny is finite étale, and they correspond to the orbits of the absolute Galois group of \(K\) on the \(d^2\) simultaneous radical choices.
Hence their number is \([\mu_d^2:D]\), the order of the character group of \(\mu_d^2/D\).
A character represented by \((r,s)\bmod d\) is trivial on \(D\) precisely when \(u^rv^s\in K\).
Sending it to the class of \(u^rv^s\) in \(\Lambda/L\) is well defined, since changing representatives multiplies it by a monomial in \(U,V\).
If the image vanishes, then \(u^rv^s\) is a constant times \(U^pV^q\) for some integers \(p,q\), so taking \(d\)-th powers and using independence modulo constants gives \((r,s)=d(p,q)\).
This injection bounds the component count by \(I\) for every \(d\).

<a id="covers"></a>
## Passing to covers with irreducible multiplication pullbacks

Choose integers \(i,j\) with \(ik+jl=1\) and use torus coordinates \(X=U^iV^j,T=U^{-l}V^k\), with inverse \(U=X^kT^{-j},V=X^lT^i\).
The width in \((k,l)\) of one component is \(W/(gc)\), by the conjugacy and product formula for Newton polygons.
It is the degree of the projection to \(T\), since after clearing the lowest power of \(X\) the component equation has this degree and nonzero constant coefficient over \(B(T)\).
For \(m\ge1\) form the inverse image of \(C\) under \((y,s)\mapsto (X,T)=(y^g,s^m)\).
Its components are permuted transitively by \(\mu_g\times\mu_m\), since this group acts transitively on the generic geometric fiber over \(C\) and every component dominates \(C\).
Each has the same degree over \(s\), since these translations induce automorphisms of the base, and their total degree is \(W/c\) by substitution in the generic equation and separability.
The scalar pullback with \(d=\operatorname{lcm}(g,m)\) surjects finitely onto this inverse image, by raising coordinates to \(d/g,d/m\) respectively.
Scalar multiplication commutes with the integral coordinate change, so the number \(q_m\) of components is at most \(I\).

Choose \(m_0\) attaining the largest \(q_m\) for this component and these fixed exponents.
Pulling its \(q_{m_0}\) components back by any \(s=r^h\) produces \(q_{hm_0}\) components in total, each original component receiving at least one and distinct original components having distinct generic preimages.
Maximality therefore makes each individual pullback geometrically irreducible for every \(h\ge1\).
This verifies condition (PB) for the projection to \(s\) on each component at \(m_0\), of degree \(W/(cq_{m_0})\).
One may use their normalizations over the torus, removing finitely many base values where the affine equations fail to give the complete smooth fibers, including boundary points, intersections and branch values.
Choose a number field \(M\) defining the finitely many components and their covers and put \(M^c=M(\mu_\infty)\).
By [Zannier's torsion fiber theorem](../audits/literature/Zan10/Yin_U001.md#zan10-u001), outside finitely many torsion values of \(s\) every specialized point on each such component has degree \(W/(cq_{m_0})\) over \(M^c\).
Its coordinates are \((y,s)\), with \(s\in H\), so \([H(y):H]\ge[M^c(y):M^c]\ge W/(cI)\).
Include in \(E_{F,a,b}\) the \(m_0\)-th powers of all excluded torsion values, taking the union over all original geometric components with their own choices of \(m_0\).
For any remaining \(t\) and any section root \(z\), its point on \(F=0\) has \(X=\xi^i\eta^j z^g,T=t\).
Choose torsion \(s\) with \(s^{m_0}=t\) for the component containing that point and torsion \(\rho\) with \(\rho^g=\xi^i\eta^j\).
Then \(y=\rho z\) is a point coordinate on the pullback and generates \(H(z)\) over \(H\), proving the degree estimate also for axis directions and arbitrary dilations.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Absolute degree and height

For a nontorsion root the [torsion-order bound](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) gives \([\mathbb Q(z,\xi,\eta):\mathbb Q(z)]\le22A\).
Over \(\mathbb Q(\xi,\eta)\subset H\) the degree of \(z\) is at least its degree over \(H\), and the twist field has rational degree \(\varphi(w)\).
The tower formula gives the asserted rational degree.
The [uniform section-height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives \(Wh(z)=m_2(F)+o_F(1)\) for increasing \(n\) and \(m_2(F)>0\).
Multiplication by the degree bound gives the measure estimate since \(m(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\).
For sufficiently large \(n\) the parenthesis is at least \(m_2(F)/2\), and for bounded \(n\) the same [torsion-order theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) gives a positive lower bound for \(m(z)/\varphi(w)\) independent of the dilation.
