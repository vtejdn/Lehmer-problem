# Power fields and normal closures of individual factors of sparse relations

<a id="statement"></a>
## A minimal vanishing sum at an individual root

Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be algebraic and nontorsion, and let a nonzero rational Laurent polynomial vanish at \(\alpha\).
Among its distinct monomials choose any nonempty subset whose sum vanishes at \(\alpha\) and which contains no smaller nonempty vanishing subset.
Write its exponents as \(a_0<\cdots<a_k\), let \(g=\gcd_{i>0}(a_i-a_0)\), put \(\beta=\alpha^g\) and let \(W=(a_k-a_0)/g\).
Clear the denominators and divide the coefficients of the subset by their common divisor to obtain primitive integer coefficients \(c_0,\ldots,c_k\).
Write \(H=k(\log\max_i|c_i|+\log k)\) and \(T=W/H\).
Here \(k\ge1,\ W\ge1\) and \(H>0\).

For every \(j\ge1\), with \(\zeta_j\) a primitive \(j\)-th root of unity, one has
\[
             \mathbb Q(\beta,\zeta_j)=\mathbb Q(\beta^j,\zeta_j).                 \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) is an abelian extension of \(A_j=\mathbb Q(\beta^j)\).
More precisely \(K=A_j(K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j))\), and
\[
       [K:A_j]=[K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j):A_j\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)]
                   \ \mid\ \varphi(j).
\]
In particular squaring preserves \(K\), and the degree loss at a prime exponent \(j=p\) divides \(p-1\).

Put \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) and let \(D\) be the degree of the normal closure of \(F(\beta)/F\).
Every power \(\beta^j\) has this same relative field and normal closure.
There is an effective absolute \(c>0\) such that for \(T\ge1/c\)
\[
 D\ \ge\ T^{c(\log\log T)^{1/3}},\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(\beta^j):\mathbb Q]\ \gg\
     \frac{T(\log\log T)^3}{(\log T)^4}\quad(j\ge1).                         \tag{2}
\]
The second bound even holds for \([F(\beta):F]\), with an absolute implied constant.
The relative normal closure of \(F(\alpha^j)/F\) contains that of \(F(\beta)/F\) for every \(j\), since \(F(\beta)=F(\beta^j)\subseteq F(\alpha^j)\).
The multiplicative rank of the group generated by the \(F\)-conjugates of \(\beta\) is \(\gg(\log T)^{1/2}(\log\log T)^{1/6}\) in the same range after decreasing \(c\).

If the primitive coefficient length of the original relation is bounded by \(L\), its minimal subsets all satisfy
\[
                   m(\alpha)\ \asymp_L\ \frac{[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]}{W}.
                                                                            \tag{3}
\]
Here \(m(\alpha)=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]h(\alpha)\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
Thus a sequence of these actual factors has \(m(\alpha)\to0\) if and only if the normalized root degrees are \(o(W)\) for one choice of a minimal subset in each relation, equivalently for every such choice.
In that case the normalized widths tend to infinity, and (2) applies with \(T\gg_L W\).

<a id="support"></a>
## Detecting the intrinsic exponent from vanishing subsets

For a nonzero algebraic \(z\), define \(t(z)\) to be the gcd of the positive support exponents of its monic rational minimal polynomial \(S_z\).
Its constant term is nonzero, and \(S_z(X)=U(X^{t(z)})\) for \(U\in\mathbb Q[X]\).
The polynomial \(U\) vanishes at \(z^{t(z)}\), and a rational factorization would factor \(S_z\) on substitution.
Hence \(U\) is the monic irreducible polynomial of \(z^{t(z)}\).
In particular \(t(z^{t(z)})=1\).
The integer \(t(z)\) equals the gcd of the exponent-difference gcds of **all** rational relations at \(z\) minimal under deleting terms.

Indeed set \(t=t(z)\).
For each \(t\)-th root of unity \(\xi\), the number \(\xi z\) is a root of \(S_z\), so it is also a root of every rational Laurent relation at \(z\).
Evaluating a relation at all \(\xi z\) and summing with weights \(\xi^{-r}\) gives \(t\) times the subsum at \(z\) in residue class \(r\bmod t\).
Every such subsum vanishes.
Minimality under deleting terms therefore puts every exponent in one residue class, so \(t\) divides its difference gcd.
The support of \(S_z\) itself gives a minimal relation, since in a partition into two nonempty vanishing subsets the part without the leading term would annihilate \(z\) in a smaller degree.
This attains the gcd \(t\) and proves the characterization.

For the selected subset at \(\alpha\), write \(b_i=(a_i-a_0)/g\).
The normalized relation \(\sum c_i\beta^{b_i}=0\) is still minimal since evaluation differs on all terms by the same nonzero scalar.
Its difference gcd is one, so the preceding result gives \(t(\beta)=1\).
This is a test at a single actual factor even when its given sparse multiple is reducible.

## A conductor bound for each power

Fix \(j\ge1\), put \(C_j=\mathbb Q(\zeta_j)\) and \(E=C_j(\beta^j)\cap K\).
The proof of [Amoroso–David, Lemma 3.1](../audits/literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field) works over this finite cyclotomic base.
If \(\delta=[K:E]\), every \(E\)-conjugate of \(\beta\) is a multiple of \(\beta\) by a \(j\)-th root of unity.
Their product \(\operatorname{Norm}_{K/E}(\beta)=\eta\beta^\delta\) has \(\eta\in\mu_j\cap K\subseteq E\), and therefore \(\beta^\delta\in E\).
Taking the product of \(X^\delta-\sigma(\beta^\delta)\) over the rational embeddings \(\sigma\) of \(E\) gives a monic rational polynomial annihilating \(\beta\) of degree \(\delta[E:\mathbb Q]=\deg S_\beta\).
Thus \(S_\beta\in\mathbb Q[X^\delta]\), and \(t(\beta)=1\) forces \(\delta=1\).
This proves (1).
The extension \(A_j C_j/A_j\) is abelian Galois.
Its subgroup fixing \(K\) acts by restriction on \(C_j\) with fixed field \(K\cap C_j\).
Galois correspondence then gives \(K=A_j(K\cap C_j)\).
Since \(K\cap C_j\) is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\), the formula for \([K:A_j]\) follows by restriction, and this degree divides \([C_j:\mathbb Q]\).

There is also an exact formula for arbitrary powers of the original uncompressed root over \(F\).
Compute \(t=t(\alpha)\) from its minimal polynomial and set \(b=[F(\alpha):F(\alpha^t)]\).
As \(t(\alpha^t)=1\), the preceding norm argument applies to \(\alpha^t\) itself, giving \(F(\alpha^t)=F(\alpha^{tj})\subseteq F(\alpha^j)\).
The extension \(F(\alpha)/F(\alpha^t)\) is the splitting field of \(X^t-\alpha^t\) and its Galois group acts on \(\alpha\) by multiplication by the elements of a subgroup of \(\mu_t\) of order \(b\).
All roots of unity lie in the base, so this subgroup is cyclic, \(b\mid t\), and
\[
       [F(\alpha):F(\alpha^j)]=\gcd(b,j),\qquad
       F(\alpha^j)=F(\alpha^{\gcd(b,j)}).                                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the automorphisms fixing \(\alpha^j\) correspond exactly to roots of unity in that subgroup whose \(j\)-th power is one.
As \(t\mid g\), the field \(F(\alpha^t)=F(\beta)\) is independent of the chosen minimal subset and equals the intersection of all \(F(\alpha^j)\).

The integer \(b\) in (4) is computable from the finite splitting field \(N\) of \(S_\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)\) and \(G'\) be its commutator subgroup.
The field \(N\cap F=N^{G'}\) is the maximal abelian subextension, so restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(NF/F)\) with \(G'\).
The \(F\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\) form the orbit \(G'\alpha\).
Counting the elements \(x\) of this orbit with \(x^t=\alpha^t\) gives precisely \(b\), the degree over \(F(\alpha^t)\).

## Height, relative degree and normalized factor measure

The normalized relation has actual width \(W\), coefficient height \(\log\max_i|c_i|\) and no nonempty proper vanishing prefix in decreasing exponent order.
If \(H=0\), it would be a binomial with coefficient ratio \(1\) or \(-1\), which would make \(\beta\) torsion.
By (1) all powers preserve \(F(\beta)\), including the power by the actual exponent of torsion in its conjugate group.
The [full Proposition 3.3 of Amoroso and David](../audits/literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#lacunary) therefore gives the first inequality of (2) and the upper bound \(h(\beta)\le1/T\).
If \(r\) is the rank of the relative conjugate group, the [faithful integral representation after powering](../audits/literature/AD22/Yin_U001.md#power-field) gives \(D\le3^{r^2}\).
Combining the two estimates gives the claimed rank bound.

For \(B=[F(\beta):F]\), the [relative height bound over the rational abelian extension](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) gives
\[
        B(\log(2B))^4\ \gg\ T(\log\log(5B))^3 .
\]
If \(B<T\) and \(T\) is large, first use \(\log\log(5B)\ge\log\log 5>0\) to obtain \(B\gg T/(\log(2T))^4\), and then \(\log\log(5B)\gg\log\log T\).
Substitution gives the required estimate for \(B\), and when \(B\ge T\) the conclusion holds directly.
Since \(F(\beta^j)=F(\beta)\) for each \(j\), the absolute degree of each power is at least \(B\).
In particular every such power has degree at most \(W\) and at least the second bound of (2), including powers that identify some rational conjugates.
In the [eventual sparsity formula](Yin_C_G0345_v187.md#statement), the number of torsion-quotient classes of the rational conjugates of \(\beta\) is the degree of \(\beta^u\) for any \(u\) divisible by the orders of all torsion quotients between these conjugates.
That number also satisfies the second bound of (2), with no uniform threshold in the eventual sparsity formula being used.

For (3), all primitive subset coefficients have length at most \(L\), whence \(H\ll_L1\).
For a unit \(\beta\) the [width lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(W h(\beta)\gg_L1\).
For a nonunit the leading or constant coefficient of its primitive irreducible integer polynomial has modulus at least two, so \(m(\beta)\ge\log2\), and \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\le W\) gives the same lower bound on \(W h(\beta)\).
Together with \(W h(\beta)\le H\) this gives \(m(\beta)\asymp_L[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]/W\).
The [bounded denominator for measures of radicals](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) gives \(m(\beta)/R_L\le m(\alpha)\le m(\beta)\) for this actual root of \(X^g-\beta\).
This proves (3), uniformly in the chosen subset, in all exponents and in the original cofactor.
If the measures tend to zero, (3) and \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\ge1\) force \(W\to\infty\).

## Automatic cases and sharp distinctions

For a nontorsion algebraic unit, a vanishing rational binomial would force a nonzero power of the unit to be a rational unit, which is impossible.
Consequently every relation on such a unit with at most five terms is already minimal, since splitting its support into two vanishing sums would give one of at most two terms.
For a relation of at most seven terms, a split gives a vanishing sum of at most three terms and hence \(m(\alpha)\ge\log\theta\) by the [sharp trinomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(X^3-X-1=0\).
Thus \(0<m(\alpha)<\log\theta\) makes the entire relation minimal in this range, for arbitrary coefficients and offset.
This argument checks arbitrary subsets, including interleaved ones.

The requirement is sharp at six terms for units even if every proper ordered prefix survives.
Let \(u=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), take odd primes \(p\), and let \(\alpha=u^{1/p}>1\) be real.
Then \(\alpha\) is a unit root of
\[
                (1+X)(X^{2p}-3X^p+1),
\]
whose support has difference gcd one.
In increasing order the five proper prefix values are \(1,\ 1+\alpha,\ \alpha-u^2,\ (1+\alpha)(1-3u),\ -\alpha u^2\), all nonzero.
The two interleaved triples both vanish.
As \(u\in F\), for all sufficiently large \(p\) one has \(\alpha\notin F\) by the [abelian height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) at degree one and \(h(\alpha)=h(u)/p\).
Then the relative normal closure of \(F(\alpha)/F\) has degree exactly \(p\), since its Galois group acts by a subgroup of \(\mu_p\) and the extension is nontrivial.
The normalized width of either minimal triple is two.
Using the full primitive support instead would give width \(2p+1\), fixed coefficient height and a false superpolynomial prediction.

The absolute degree loss in (1) is attained at every prime exponent \(j=p\).
For \(\beta=2\zeta_p\), the minimal polynomial is \(2^{p-1}\Phi_p(X/2)\) by scaling, has support gcd one and is itself a minimal relation.
Here \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) and \(\beta^p=2^p\), giving \([K:A_p]=p-1\).
Preservation of all absolute power fields in the [fixed-curve theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#fields) uses its additional geometric hypotheses.
