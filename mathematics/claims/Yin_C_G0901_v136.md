# An inverse test for a near-one Mahler value and the exact cost of its next iterate

<a id="statement"></a>
## A Salem number extracted directly from the two lowest iterates

Let \(A>1\) be a real algebraic number sufficiently close to one with \(A\le M(A)\le A^2\), and write \(m=\log A\).
Then \(A=M(P)\) for a nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) if and only if either \(A\) is a real Salem number or both of the following conditions hold:

* \(R=\sqrt A\) is an algebraic unit in \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
* \(B=A/\sqrt{M(A)}\) is one or a real Salem number.

If \(A<M(A)<A^2\) and \(A\) is such a value, \(B\) is Salem and the product of the minimal polynomials of \(R\) and \(B\) has measure exactly \(A\).
Two is the minimum number of positive-measure irreducible factors counted with multiplicity in any realization of this \(A\).
Let \(D=\deg A\), \(d=\deg B\), and \(Y=M(A)\).
The embedded fields satisfy
\[
             \mathbb Q(B)\subsetneq\mathbb Q(Y)=\mathbb Q(A),
                 \qquad d\ll(\log(3D))^{212}.
\]
In every realization of \(A\), the measures of its positive-measure irreducible factors generate exactly these two fields.
The product of the factor measures generating \(\mathbb Q(B)\) is \(B\), and each is a positive power of the same primitive Salem number.
The product of those generating \(\mathbb Q(A)\) is \(\sqrt Y\), and each is a positive power of one common Perron unit.
In particular, the two individual measures in a two-factor realization are unique up to order.

On the strict interior branch the third logarithmic iterate satisfies
\[
       \left|\log M(Y)-2(D/d)\log B\right|\le 2\log Y .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(636\sqrt[3]2)\) and sufficiently small \(m>0\) on this branch,
\[
      \log M(Y)\ \ge\ \exp\!\left(\exp\!\left(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\right)\right).
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
Consequently for integer polynomials approaching measure one with bounded third iterates the actual values satisfy \(M(A)=A\) or \(M(A)=A^2\) eventually, and all subsequent iterates are the corresponding successive powers.
These conditions leave the possible existence of shrinking Salem or square values and of large second iterates open.

<a id="invariant"></a>
## A quotient preserved exactly at every strict descent

For a polynomial realization in the low interval use the [factor decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#factors) and [projected coefficient differences](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval).
The lower endpoint makes \(A\) Salem.
At every other point there is a positive reciprocal Perron unit \(\rho\) and an integer \(e\ge1\) with \(A=\rho^{2e}\), with all its positive powers of degree \(D\).
Put \(Y=M(\rho)^{2e}=M(A)\).
The factor decomposition there partitions the positive-measure irreducible factors into those supplying positive powers of \(M(\rho)\), with product \(M(\rho)^e=\sqrt Y\), and the remainder with product \(B=A/\sqrt Y\).
Thus \(B\) is itself an integer-polynomial Mahler value, possibly one, and \(R=\rho^e\in\mathbb Q(A)\).
At the square endpoint \(B=1\).

For the strict interior put \(\Delta(X)=X^2/M(X)\) for a positive algebraic number \(X\).
One step of the [interior descent](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interior) gives a smaller polynomial value \(A_1=r_1^{2e}\) from the real signed half product \(r_1>1\), with \(\deg A_1=2u<D\).
The selected half is stabilized by the stabilizer of \(\rho\), so \(r_1\in\mathbb Q(\rho)=\mathbb Q(A)\). [Fields of the half products](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields)
Every positive power of \(r_1\) still generates \(\mathbb Q(r_1)\).
The descent identity \(r_1^2/M(r_1)=\rho^2/M(\rho)\) consequently gives
\[
               \Delta(A_1)=\big(r_1^2/M(r_1)\big)^{2e}
                            =\Delta(A)=B^2 .
\]
As long as the inequalities are strict this calculation repeats, regardless of the new exponent \(e\), with strict nested field inclusions at each step.
The degrees descend to a lower endpoint \(A_*=M(A_*)\) which is a real Salem number.
Thus \(A_*=\Delta(A_*)=\Delta(A)=B^2\), and \(\mathbb Q(B^2)\subseteq\mathbb Q(A_1)\).
The product \(B>1\) of the remaining factor measures is a strict Perron unit: any of its conjugates attaining the full modulus must fix all its individual positive Perron factors. [Exterior product](Yin_C_G0901_v95.md#field)
Its square has the same degree, so its conjugate moduli already give a single exterior root, one interior root and neutral conjugates.
A neutral conjugate has its inverse as its complex conjugate, giving reciprocity.
This proves that \(B\) itself is Salem and \(\mathbb Q(B)=\mathbb Q(B^2)\); the bound \(d\le2u\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\) follows from the [matched block size](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement).

For sufficiency, a real Salem input has its own value as measure.
In the other case \(\mathbb Q(R)=\mathbb Q(A)\) since \(R^2=A\) and \(R\) belongs to that field.
Thus \(M(R)^2=M(A)\) for the unit \(R\), by counting its embeddings.
Multiplying its minimum by the minimum of \(B\) when \(B>1\) gives measure \(M(R)B=A\).
The necessity argument required two nonempty groups of factors in the strict interior, proving minimality there.

<a id="fields"></a>
## Recognizing both groups of factors by their actual fields

In the strict interior \(Y=A^2/B^2\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(A)\).
On the leading irreducible constituent \(W\) of the matched coordinate of \(\rho\), the class of \(B\) projects to zero by the factor decomposition.
Here classes are taken modulo roots of unity in the rational span of the conjugates of the original factors.
The projection of \([Y]\) to \(W\) is therefore twice that of \([A]\).
An automorphism fixing \(Y\) must fix that projected coordinate.
Its orbit has exactly \(D\) vectors, with different signs and labels all distinct, by the [signed coordinate orbit](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#rank).
The projection is consequently a bijection on the orbit of the \(D\) conjugate classes of \(A\); the powers of the compressed \(\rho\) have distinct classes.
It follows that the same automorphism fixes \(A\) itself.
Hence \(\mathbb Q(Y)=\mathbb Q(A)\).
Every positive power of the Perron unit \(M(\rho)\) generates its field, which thus equals \(\mathbb Q(Y)\).
This identifies all factors in the first group.

For any positive factor measure \(C\) in the remaining product \(B\), attaining the Perron maximum in that product implies that an automorphism fixing \(B\) fixes \(C\).
Thus \(C\in K=\mathbb Q(B)\), and all its conjugates have modulus at most \(C\le B<2^{1/4}\), reducing the smallness cutoff if necessary.
Let \(\tau\) be the relative involution of this Salem field and \(v=C\tau C\) its norm to the totally real trace field.
Every conjugate of \(v\) has modulus strictly between zero and \(\sqrt2\).
Every value of the algebraic integer \(1-v^2\) therefore lies strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\), so its norm forces \(v^2=1\).
At any complex place of \(K\), \(v\) is positive, giving \(v=1\).
By the [norm-one unit group](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units), \(C>1\) is a positive power of its primitive Salem generator, and every such power generates \(K\).
Since \(K\) is proper in \(\mathbb Q(A)\), the two field descriptions are disjoint and exhaustive for each realization.

<a id="growth"></a>
## The third iterate and the small Salem degree

Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height, so \(h(x)=\log M(x)/\deg x\).
The identity \(Y=A^2/B^2\) and the height triangle inequality in both directions give \(|h(Y)-2h(B)|\le 2h(A)\).
Here \(B\) is Salem, and \(A,Y\) have the same degree \(D\), proving (1).
In particular \(\log M(Y)\gg D/((\log(3D))^{212}(\log\log(3D))^3)\) for sufficiently large \(D\): use \(d\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\), the [Voutier bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) \(\log B>2/(\log(3d))^3\), and \(2\log Y\le4m\).

Since \(B^2=A^2/Y<A\), \(\log B<m/2\); in particular \(d\) tends to infinity uniformly by bounded-degree finiteness.
With \(t=\log d\) and \(a=(2m)^{-1/3}\), the other Voutier inequality \(\log B>\frac14(\log t/t)^3\) gives \(t>a\log t>a\log a\) for sufficiently small \(m\).
Thus
\[
       \log d\ \ge\ \left(\frac1{3\sqrt[3]2}-o(1)\right)
                           m^{-1/3}\log(1/m).
\]
On the other hand \(\log d\le212\log\log(3D)+O(1)\).
The preceding lower bound for \(\log M(Y)\) then gives
\(\log\log(\log M(Y))\ge \log\log D-o(1)\) as \(D\to\infty\), proving (2).
Finally a bounded third iterate bounds the second because \(Y\) is a positive algebraic unit with \(M(Y)\ge Y\).
The [low interval or growing second iterate](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) puts that sequence in the low interval eventually.
The strict interior contradicts (2).
At either endpoint \(M(A)=A^j\), \(j=1,2\), and full degree of all powers of the Perron unit \(A\) gives \(M(A^k)=M(A)^k\) for every \(k\ge1\).
