# The full coefficient cutoff through row degree seven

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exclusion of all genuine pairs at sufficiently large primes

For every sufficiently large odd prime \(p\), the [prime-indexed coefficient minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) equals its initial candidate \(2^p\) simultaneously at every row-degree bound \(1\le b\le7\).
No genuine pair at those widths has cost \(H\le2^p\), including equality.
Moreover, every reciprocal sextic seed is excluded at every width through nine at this cutoff for sufficiently large odd primes.
These statements have no numerical prime threshold.

<a id="seeds"></a>
## Exhausting all factors that could contribute

Suppose that \(P\) is a repeated monic reciprocal irreducible factor of a qualifying graph, and write \(e=\deg P\), \(h=\log M(P)>0\).
By [field recovery and reflected elimination](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower), the powered minimum has degree \(e\), its square divides the nonzero reflected polynomial of degree at most \(2b\), and \(ph\le\log H\).
Hence \(e\le b\) and \(M(P)\le2\).
A nontorsion reciprocal irreducible has even degree, since inversion permutes its roots without fixed points.
At \(b=7\) only degrees two, four and six need consideration.
The [small-degree classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short) rules out degree two and leaves two quartics up to a variable sign.
The [row-value norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#salem) excludes both through width eight for sufficiently large \(p\).

For \(e=6\), the [exact sextic census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0178/readme.md#census) gives eleven sign classes with one exterior root and six with two.
In the first group \(M(P)>7/5\), in the second \(M(P)>17/10\).
Writing \(g\) for the exterior count, the sum \(D(P)\) of all pairwise absolute differences of log moduli is at least \(2(6-g)h\) by reciprocity.
Apply the [norm inequality at multiplicity two](Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#statement), with the rows padded to width nine.
The lower limit of \(p^{-1}\log H\) is at least \(13h/6\) in the first group and \(9h/6\) in the second.
Both exceed \(\log2\), since \(7^{13}>2^6 5^{13}\) and \(17^9>2^6 10^9\).
Changing the variable sign preserves every log modulus and the bound.
Taking the maximum of the onsets over the finite set of seeds proves both assertions.

<a id="eight"></a>
## An octic where the norm estimate falls below the cutoff

At \(b=8\) consider \(P=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1=X^4T(X+X^{-1})\) with \(T(Z)=Z^4-4Z^2-Z+1\).
The reduction \(Z^4+Z+1\) modulo two has no linear factor and is coprime to \(Z^2+Z+1\), so \(T\) is irreducible over the rationals.
Its signs at \(-2,-1,0,1,2\) are respectively \(+,-,+,-,-\), and it tends to \(+\infty\) on the positive ray.
It has three distinct roots in \((-2,2)\) and a fourth above two.
Its root field is totally real, and the discriminant \(Z^2-4\) of the quadratic lift is negative at an interior embedding, so the lift is irreducible.
Thus \(P\) has exactly one exterior root \(\rho>1\).
Since \(P(1)=-1\) and \(7^8P(9/7)=552451>0\), one has \(\rho<9/7\).
At width eight the [Salem bound](Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#salem) for this seed is \((11/4)\log\rho<\log2\), since \(9^{11}<16\cdot7^{11}\).
The norm estimate alone therefore leaves this octic eligible for further analysis.
These inequalities do not establish the existence of a qualifying pair.
