# Exterior counts after adjoining units from all conjugate root fields

The coordinating researcher proposed the prime-degree unit-module decomposition and the transfer to original root products.
The author-side development below supplies the finite-generation argument, the exact single-field conclusion and their application to the explicit prime family.
It concerns [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) and extends the root-generated obstruction without identifying the enlarged group with all units of the splitting field.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The enlarged group and its exterior minimum

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2p\), where \(p\) is an odd prime, with nontorsion roots.
Let \(R\) count its real exterior roots, \(C\) its complex-conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots, and \(N\) its neutral inverse pairs.
Assume
\[
             p=R+N+2C,\qquad R>0,\quad N>0,
             \qquad e=R+2C,\quad \kappa=p-C-1.                 \tag{1}
\]
Here exterior means modulus greater than one, and neutral means modulus one.
Choose a root \(\alpha\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and let \(F\subset\mathbb C\) be the splitting field with group \(G\) and complex conjugation \(\tau\).
Write \(\Gamma\) for the group generated by the roots of \(P\), and define
\[
           \Lambda_K=\left\langle\mathcal O_{gK}^{\times}:
                                      g\in G\right\rangle
                     \ \subseteq\ \mathcal O_F^{\times}.       \tag{2}
\]
The brackets mean finite products of units and their inverses.
For any such group \(A\), put
\[
       \widetilde A=\{y:y^n\in\mu_\infty A
                                      \text{ for some }n\ge1\}.
\]
Every element of this saturation is an algebraic unit.
Let \(b(y)\) count the distinct exterior conjugates of an algebraic number, and put \(\mu=\log M(P)\).
Define the original and enlarged reciprocal minima by
\[
 c_* =\min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma\ \mathrm{nontorsion}\\
                              y\ \mathrm{reciprocal}}}b(y),
 \qquad
 c_K =\min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde{\Lambda_K}\ \mathrm{nontorsion}\\
                              y\ \mathrm{reciprocal}}}b(y).     \tag{3}
\]
Both minima are finite, since the input root is eligible.

Every reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde{\Lambda_K}\) satisfies
\[
                            b(y)\ge R+C.                       \tag{4}
\]
There is also the following alternative.
Either \(b(y)\ge\kappa\), or there is an ordinary signed subset product of original roots,
\[
          \beta=\prod_{i=1}^{p}x_i^{a_i},\qquad
                        a_i\in\{0,1,-1\},                     \tag{5}
\]
which is reciprocal and nontorsion and satisfies
\[
              b(\beta)\le b(y),\qquad
       0<\log M(\beta)\le b(\beta)\mu\le b(y)\mu.              \tag{6}
\]
Here \(x_i\) is one representative from each inverse pair of \(P\).
The proof gives (5)–(6) whenever the cyclic unit module of \(y\) has a signed constituent, even if \(b(y)\ge\kappa\).
In particular every output with \(b(y)<\kappa\) transfers in this way.

Consequently
\[
                   R+C\le c_K\le c_*\le e,\qquad
                   N\ge C+1\ \Longrightarrow\ c_K=c_* .      \tag{7}
\]
For radicals of units of the single field \(K\), the stronger exact minimum is
\[
       \min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde{\mathcal O_K^{\times}}\ \mathrm{nontorsion}\\
                              y\ \mathrm{reciprocal}}}b(y)=e.  \tag{8}
\]
No height bound on the supplied units is assumed in these statements.

<a id="finite"></a>
## A finite expression supplies a finite permutation module

Use additive notation for the rational unit space
\[
             \mathcal U_F=(\mathcal O_F^{\times}/\mu(F))
                                      \otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q.
\]
For \(y\) as in the statement, clear both its radical exponent and its torsion multiplier to obtain \(w=y^n\in\Lambda_K\).
Its class \(v=[w]\) is nonzero.
Let \(W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{gv:g\in G\}\).
This is a finite-dimensional cyclic \(G\)-module, regardless of how many units exist in the fields in (2).

By definition, \(w\) is a finite product of units from finitely many conjugate root fields.
Transporting each factor back to \(K\), choose units \(u_1,\ldots,u_t\in\mathcal O_K^{\times}\) such that \(w\) is a product of integral powers of conjugates of these units.
Put \(G_\alpha=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)\).
Since \(G_\alpha\) fixes each \(u_i\), the rule
\[
        \mathbb Q[G/G_\alpha]\longrightarrow\mathcal U_F,
                  \qquad [gG_\alpha]\longmapsto[g u_i]          \tag{9}
\]
is well-defined and equivariant.
The sum of these finitely many maps has image containing \(W\).
Every invariant subspace of a finite-dimensional rational representation of a finite group has an invariant complement: average any projection over the group.
Thus the irreducible constituents of \(W\) occur among those of the permutation module \(\mathbb Q[G/G_\alpha]\).
This argument needs neither a choice of fundamental units nor a uniform bound on unit rank or on the number \(t\).

<a id="representations"></a>
## The only two nontrivial constituents

The \(2p\) cosets of \(G_\alpha\) correspond to the individual roots of \(P\).
Inversion of roots commutes with the \(G\)-action.
The sums and differences of the two basis vectors in each inverse pair therefore give
\[
       \mathbb Q[G/G_\alpha]
             =\mathbf 1\ \oplus\ A\ \oplus\ B,\qquad
                   \dim A=p-1,\quad\dim B=p.                  \tag{10}
\]
Here \(A\) is the augmentation subspace of the unsigned permutation representation on the \(p\) pair labels, and \(B\) is their signed permutation representation.

The transitive label group contains a \(p\)-cycle.
On its cyclic subgroup, the augmentation space is the irreducible rational representation defined by \(\Phi_p\); hence \(A\) is irreducible for \(G\).
The [mixed-signature prime-cycle argument](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#prime) proves that \(B\) is irreducible under \(R,N>0\).
To recall the decisive point, an order-\(p\) signed lift gives the rational regular representation of that cycle, so any nontrivial decomposition of \(B\) would include a line.
Transitivity forces a vector on such a line to have equal nonzero absolute values in all coordinates.
Complex conjugation fixes the real exterior coordinate and negates the neutral coordinate, making an invariant line impossible.
The original root classes give a nonzero quotient of \(B\), which is therefore an isomorphism
\[
                B\ \simeq\ V:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
                                  \{[x_i]:1\le i\le p\}.       \tag{11}
\]
In particular the chosen root representatives are independent modulo torsion.

There is no trivial constituent in \(\mathcal U_F\).
Indeed, on a class represented by a unit \(u\), the averaging numerator is
\[
                \sum_{g\in G}g[u]=[N_{F/\mathbb Q}(u)]=0,      \tag{12}
\]
since the rational norm of a unit is \(\pm1\).
The same holds after rational scaling, so any invariant vector is zero.
By (9)–(12), there are nonnegative integers \(a_W,b_W\), not both zero, with
\[
                         W\simeq A^{a_W}\oplus B^{b_W}.        \tag{13}
\]
The two constituents are nonisomorphic because their dimensions differ.

On the unsigned labels, \(\tau\) fixes \(R+N\) labels and exchanges the two labels associated with each nonreal exterior conjugate pair.
Thus its fixed dimension on the unsigned representation is \(p-C\), and
\[
               \dim A^\tau=p-C-1=\kappa.
\]
On \(B\), a real exterior label contributes a positive eigenvalue, a neutral label a negative eigenvalue, and each exchanged pair contributes one of each.
Consequently
\[
      \dim B^\tau=R+C,\qquad
      \dim W^\tau=a_W\kappa+b_W(R+C),\qquad
                         \kappa=R+C+N-1\ge R+C.               \tag{14}
\]

<a id="rank"></a>
## The archimedean dimension counts exterior conjugates

For every rational unit class \(z\in\mathcal U_F\), the real logarithm \(\ell(z)=\log|z|\) is well-defined by rational scaling of representatives.
Its kernel is exactly the negative eigenspace of \(\tau\):
\[
                         \ell(z)=0
                    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau z=-z.  \tag{15}
\]
To see this, clear denominators and represent the class by a unit \(u\).
The product \(u\overline u=|u|^2\) is positive real, and it is torsion precisely when it is one.
This proves both implications in (15).
In particular \(\ell\) is injective on the rational space \(W^\tau\), and
\(\ell(z)=\ell((z+\tau z)/2)\).

The projections \((gv+\tau gv)/2\) span \(W^\tau\).
Their distinct nonzero pairs modulo simultaneous sign therefore number at least \(\dim W^\tau\).
Injectivity in (15) makes the corresponding absolute logarithms distinct and positive.
Since \(y\) is reciprocal, so is \(w=y^n\), and its actual conjugates are closed under inversion.
Each such absolute logarithm therefore occurs with a positive sign at an exterior conjugate of \(w\).
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w)\) extends through \(y^n=w\); different exterior power values have different exterior preimages.
It follows that
\[
                     b(y)\ge b(w)\ge\dim W^\tau.              \tag{16}
\]
Combining (14) and (16) proves (4).
If \(b_W=0\), it also proves \(b(y)\ge\kappa\).
The argument is the [root-class dimension calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#rank) applied to the actual finite unit module (13); (15) checks that no root-generation hypothesis is needed for this extension.

<a id="transfer"></a>
## A signed constituent returns the output to the original roots

Suppose \(b_W>0\).
Semisimplicity and (11) give a nonzero equivariant map \(\pi:W\to V\).
Since \(v\) generates \(W\), one has \(\pi(v)\ne0\).
Let \(J\) be the stabilizer of \(\{v,-v\}\), and let \(\lambda:J\to\{1,-1\}\) give the sign of its action.
The character is nontrivial because an automorphism sends the actual \(w\) to \(w^{-1}\).
Equivariance gives
\[
                          j\pi(v)=\lambda(j)\pi(v)
                                      \quad(j\in J).           \tag{17}
\]
Thus \((J,\lambda)\) is admissible for the original root space \(V\), in the sense of [C304](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#statement).

Put
\[
       c(J,\lambda)=\#\{gJ:g^{-1}\tau g\notin\lambda^{-1}(-1)\}.
                                                                    \tag{18}
\]
The cosets index the distinct antipodal pairs of orbit classes of \(v\).
By (15), such a pair is neutral precisely when \(g^{-1}\tau g\in\lambda^{-1}(-1)\).
Every other pair supplies an exterior conjugate of \(w\), since both signs occur in its actual conjugate orbit.
Different class pairs cannot supply the same actual conjugate.
Consequently
\[
                            c(J,\lambda)\le b(w)\le b(y).      \tag{19}
\]
The [single-coordinate signed averaging construction](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#average), applied to (17), now produces a reciprocal nontorsion product (5) with \(b(\beta)\le c(J,\lambda)\).
At every conjugate, its signed subset exponent vector gives
\[
                   |\log|g\beta||\le
                         \sum_{i=1}^{p}|\log|x_i||=\mu.
\]
Summing over the actual exterior conjugates proves (6), exactly as in the [signed-product measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#measure).
This estimate does not compare the heights of the supplied units with those of the roots, or assert that the projection \(\pi\) preserves height.

Since \(\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda_K\), one has \(c_K\le c_*\le e\).
If \(N\ge C+1\), then \(\kappa\ge e\).
For any eligible enlarged output, the signed case gives \(b(y)\ge c_*\), while the unsigned-only case gives \(b(y)\ge\kappa\ge e\ge c_*\).
This proves equality in (7), including the boundary \(\kappa=e\).
The unconditional transfer threshold remains the strict inequality \(b(y)<\kappa\).

<a id="single-field"></a>
## Every reciprocal nontorsion element in one root field has the original signature

Put \(E_0=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
Inversion defines an automorphism \(\iota\) of \(K\) of order two, with fixed field \(E_0\) of degree \(p\).
The quadratic extension \(K/E_0\) has \(R\) real places splitting into two real places, \(N\) real places becoming complex, and \(C\) complex places of \(E_0\).

There is no quadratic subfield \(L\) of \(K\).
Indeed \(K\) has a real embedding because \(R>0\), so any such \(L\) would be a real quadratic field.
Since \([E_0:\mathbb Q]=p\) is odd, \(L\cap E_0=\mathbb Q\), whence \(K=LE_0\).
Adjoining a real quadratic field splits every real place of \(E_0\) into two real places.
This contradicts \(N>0\): at a neutral trace value \(t\in(-2,2)\), the polynomial \(X^2-tX+1\) gives a complex extension of that real place.

Now let \(w\in K\) be any reciprocal nontorsion element.
Its degree is even, because its conjugates pair under inversion and no conjugate is \(1\) or \(-1\).
That degree divides \(2p\), so the absence of a quadratic subfield forces
\[
                            \mathbb Q(w)=K.                    \tag{20}
\]
The group \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(K)\) acts freely on all \(2p\) embeddings, and also on the \(2R\) real embeddings.
Its order divides both integers, hence divides \(2\), since \(0<R<p\).
It already contains \(\iota\), so it is exactly \(\{1,\iota\}\).
The inversion automorphism associated with the generator \(w\) must therefore be \(\iota\), and
\[
                     \mathbb Q(w+w^{-1})=E_0.                  \tag{21}
\]

At every real split place of \(E_0\), the images of \(w\) are real reciprocals distinct from \(\pm1\), contributing one exterior root.
At every real nonsplit place, conjugation agrees with \(\iota\), so both images have modulus one.
At a complex place of \(E_0\), the image of \(w+w^{-1}\) is nonreal by (21).
The corresponding images of \(w\) therefore cannot have modulus one or be real.
The four embeddings over that complex-conjugate pair of places give two exterior and two interior roots.
By (20) all these images are distinct conjugates, so
\[
                               b(w)=R+2C=e.                    \tag{22}
\]
This field-theoretic conclusion did not need integrality or a height bound on \(w\).
For reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde{\mathcal O_K^\times}\), choose a positive power \(w=y^n\in\mathcal O_K^\times\).
It is reciprocal and nontorsion, and exterior lifting gives \(b(y)\ge b(w)=e\).
The original \(\alpha\) attains equality, proving (8).

<a id="family"></a>
## The explicit subplastic prime family resists this enlargement

For odd primes \(p\ge5\), the [irreducible family and signature count](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) give
\[
          P_p(z)=z^{2p}-z^{p+1}-z^p-z^{p-1}+1,
          \qquad R_p=1,\qquad e_p=p/3+O(1).
\]
Thus \(C_p=(e_p-1)/2=p/6+O(1)\) and \(N_p=p-e_p=2p/3+O(1)\).
In particular \(N_p\ge C_p+1\) for all sufficiently large primes.
For those primes, even after adjoining every unit of every conjugate root field and then taking radicals,
\[
                  c_{K_p}=c_{*,p},\qquad
             (e_p+1)/2\le c_{K_p}\le e_p.                      \tag{23}
\]
For radicals of units in any one root field, the exact reciprocal minimum is \(e_p\).
By the [measure limit](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit),
\[
 \log M(P_p)\longrightarrow
      I=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}
                  \operatorname{arcosh}(\tfrac12+\cos t)\,dt,
                    \qquad 0<I<\log\theta,                    \tag{24}
\]
where \(\theta>1\) is the root of \(X^3-X-1\).
For every fixed exterior cap \(B\) and every cutoff \(A>\exp I\), these inputs eventually have \(M(P_p)<A\), while no reciprocal nontorsion enlarged output has \(b(y)\le B\).
The cutoff \(A\) may be chosen strictly below \(\theta\).

Every radical considered here is a unit: integrality of a positive power and of its inverse implies integrality of the number and its inverse.
Therefore [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes an enlarged nontorsion output reciprocal whenever its own measure is strictly below \(\theta\).
Under that output hypothesis, (4), (7), (8) and (23) apply without separately assuming reciprocity.
This is a condition on the output's measure; the small measure of the input alone does not impose it on every unit of \(\Lambda_K\).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Comparison and remaining scope

The root-generated [prime obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) allows rational powers of root monomials.
The present result allows arbitrary units from all conjugate root fields and arbitrary finite products of them before taking radicals.
The new restriction is the two-constituent decomposition (13), which gives the unconditional rank bound and the cost-controlled return to roots below \(\kappa\).
When \(N\ge C+1\), it proves equality of the two exterior minima, rather than merely a common lower bound.

The [all-real atom theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v303.md#statement) computes the original root-group minimum for real exterior signatures.
The [signature-unrestricted signed-product theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#statement) computes and realizes that minimum for arbitrary signatures.
Neither result by itself places general root-field units in the original root module.
The [ambient field-unit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v307.md#statement) permits all units of \(F\), a potentially larger supply than (2), and gives an explicit separation from root-generated units in a different family.
No equality \(\widetilde{\Lambda_K}=\widetilde{\mathcal O_F^\times}\), or bound for all units of \(F\), is asserted here.

The older [small-element and subfield bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#statement) and [bounded-spectrum field classification](Yin_C_G0345_v277.md#statement) use measure bounds and sufficiently large compressed degree.
The single-field statement (20)–(22) instead uses prime half-degree and mixed real/neutral places, holds at every such degree, and fixes the exterior count of every reciprocal nontorsion element in \(K\).
The [bounded-measure divisible-root-group restrictions](Yin_C_G0345_v276.md#statement) concern powers of the supplied roots, not arbitrary units from their fields.
These comparisons do not claim height control on new field units.

The example has the fixed positive limit (24), not a sequence of measures tending to one.
It rules out a bounded-exterior compression principle on every cutoff larger than that limit even after the enlargement (2).
A principle confined to a smaller neighborhood of one, a different supply from the rest of the splitting field, or a direct lower bound for inputs resisting compression remains possible.
No universal Mahler gap or shrinking input sequence follows from the present theorem.
