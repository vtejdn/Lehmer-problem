# Unique completion from joint residue orders at the quadratic span scale

<a id="statement"></a>
## Comparing two completions directly

Use the [ordered reciprocal-unit relations](Yin_C_G0345_v645.md#statement) of weight \(r\ge5\) and integer span \(W\ge1\).
Two erased coefficients are divisible by \(Q\), two unspecified surviving coefficients \(c,e\) occupy positions \(i<j\), and the other \(r-4\) positions are fixed up to a common translation.
Normalize the smallest fixed surviving position to zero.
Select any nonempty finite collection of residue roots of the reciprocal minimal polynomial at primes \(p\mid Q\) with \(p\nmid ce\), and let \(M\) be the least common multiple of their multiplicative orders.

For two completions write \(u=j-i\), \(v=j'-i'\).
Then
\[
                M\mid (u-v)(u+v).                                    \tag{1}
\]
If \(u=v\), one also has
\[
       \frac{M}{\gcd(M,2u)}\mid i-i'.                                \tag{2}
\]
If all selected primes satisfy \(c^2\not\equiv e^2\bmod p\), the divisor in (2) can be replaced by \(M\).
These restrictions also hold between candidate pairs that pass the reciprocal residue evaluations at all the selected roots.

In particular \(M>2W(W-1)\) and \(M>W^2-1\) together give at most one completion for each fixed choice of the other surviving positions.
Thus \(M>2W^2\) suffices.
If \(M\) is odd, or if \(c^2\not\equiv e^2\bmod p\) at every selected prime, \(M\ge W^2\) suffices.
The odd case includes every selection of roots in characteristic two.
Under these uniqueness hypotheses there are at most \(\binom W{r-5}\) full relations up to translation with the prescribed order.
For the weaker ordinary condition \(M>W\), a [quartic Salem example](#example) gives two distinct ordered candidates passing every residue test with all other surviving terms fixed.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The square of the gap and the common translation

At a selected root \(\beta\) with order \(s\), the two values of \(z=c\beta^i+e\beta^j\) and the corresponding values at \(\beta^{-1}\) agree for the two candidates, since the other surviving positions are fixed.
Multiplying the reciprocal evaluations gives
\[
  c^2+e^2+ce(\beta^u+\beta^{-u})
       =c^2+e^2+ce(\beta^v+\beta^{-v}).
\]
As \(ce\ne0\) in the residue field, \(\beta^v\) is one of the roots \(\beta^{u},\beta^{-u}\) of the same monic quadratic.
Consequently \(s\mid u-v\) or \(s\mid u+v\), including when the quadratic has a double root.
Taking the least common multiple proves (1) without selecting local orientations.

Assume \(u=v\).
The two equalities at \(\beta,\beta^{-1}\) now give
\[
 (\beta^i-\beta^{i'})(c+e\beta^u)=0,\qquad
 (\beta^{-i}-\beta^{-i'})(c+e\beta^{-u})=0.
\]
If either coefficient in parentheses on the right of a product is nonzero, then \(s\mid i-i'\).
If both vanish, then \(\beta^{2u}=1\) and \(c^2=e^2\) in the residue field.
Thus \(s\mid 2u(i-i')\) in every case, giving (2).
If the coefficients have distinct squares at every selected prime, all orders divide \(i-i'\).

One has \(1\le u,v\le W\), hence \(|u^2-v^2|\le W^2-1\).
If \(M>W^2-1\), (1) forces \(u=v\).
The fixed total order puts \(i,i'\) on the same side of the fixed zero, so \(|i-i'|\le W-1\).
Now \(M\mid 2u(i-i')\) gives \(i=i'\) if \(M>2W(W-1)\).
For odd \(M\) one has \(M\mid u(i-i')\), whose right side has magnitude at most \(W(W-1)<W^2\).
When all coefficient squares differ, \(M\mid i-i'\) already suffices.
Thus \(i,j\) are fixed in each stated case.
For an actual characteristic-zero relation, the [reciprocal recovery of the deleted exponents](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v313.md#recovery) gives at most one full completion, since a two-term sum at distinct positions of a reciprocal nontorsion unit cannot vanish.
The other \(r-5\) fixed surviving positions form a subset of \([1,W]\) with labels determined by the order, proving the count.

<a id="example"></a>
## Two different ordinary orientations at all residue roots

Consider \(P(X)=X^4-11X^3+3X^2-11X+1=X^2 H(X+X^{-1})\), with \(H(Y)=Y^2-11Y+1\).
Its trace roots \((11\pm\sqrt{117})/2\) generate a real quadratic field, with one root in \((0,2)\) and the other in \((2,\infty)\).
The square root of \(Y^2-4\) cannot belong to this field at a trace root \(Y\), since its square would be negative at one real embedding.
The larger real root of the lift is therefore a degree-four Salem number with minimal polynomial \(P\).

Modulo \(11\) one has \(P=(X^2-2)(X^2-6)\).
The elements \(2,6=2^{-1}\) have order ten, since \(2^2=4\ne1\) and \(2^5=-1\).
Every residue root of \(P\) thus has order twenty.
Use \(Q=11,\ W=18\), fixed surviving terms \(7+7X^6\), unspecified coefficients \(c=1,\ e=-2\), and deleted terms \(11X+11X^7\).
Both pairs
\[
                          (i,j)=(2,8),\qquad (4,18)
\]
give the same order of all six labels and fit the specified window.
After reduction modulo \(11\), the full polynomials are \(7+7Y^3+Y-2Y^4\) and \(7+7Y^3+Y^2-2Y^9\), respectively, at \(Y=X^2\).
At \(Y=2\) the residues of \(Y^3,Y^4,Y^9\) are \(8,5,6\), and at \(Y=6\) they are \(7,9,2\).
Direct substitution gives zero for both polynomials at each value.
Since \(2\ne6\bmod11\), both reductions are divisible by \((X^2-2)(X^2-6)\).
Here \(M=20>W\) and \(c^2\ne e^2\bmod11\), yet the distinct positive gaps \(6,14\) give opposite orientations modulo twenty.
The example establishes ambiguity of the complete modular data in the stated window.
Characteristic-zero vanishing imposes the additional exact trace and power conditions.
