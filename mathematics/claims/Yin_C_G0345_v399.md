# Exterior counts and an integer determinant for quartic Salem factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree test when both exponents vary

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers, \(r=b/a\), \(g(T)=T^4-T-1\), and \(B(T)=1-T^3-T^4\).
Remove the [complete cyclotomic part](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#orders) of \(-g(X^a)-X^b B(X^a)\), and write the monic remainder as \(X^n H(X+X^{-1})\).
Write \(C_s(z+z^{-1})=z^s+z^{-s}\), and let \(M\) be the matrix of multiplication by \(D(C_a(Y))\) on the integral power basis of \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(H)\), where
\[
                         D(u)=1+4u+4u^2-u^3-u^4 .
\]
For \(0\le j\le n\) and \(\epsilon=\pm1\) define
\[
 k_j^\epsilon=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\ker(\wedge^j M-\epsilon I),
 \qquad E_{j,\epsilon}=\det(\epsilon I-\wedge^j M).
\]
All assertions below about recovering factors or their exterior counts hold uniformly for sufficiently large \(r\).
If the irreducible trace factors have degrees \(n_i\) and signs \(\epsilon_i\) as defined below, then
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^n(k_j^++k_j^-)T^j=\prod_i(1+T^{n_i}),\qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^n(k_j^+-k_j^-)T^j=\prod_i(1+\epsilon_i T^{n_i}).             \tag{1}
\]
Thus the first identity gives the degree multiset with multiplicities by successively removing the factors belonging to the smallest remaining positive degree.
The corresponding reciprocal factors have degrees \(2n_i\).
For odd \(a\) and even \(b\), let \(-\sigma<-1\) be the unique negative exterior root.
Then \(\sigma\) is a Salem number if and only if
\[
            E_{j,-1}=0\quad\text{for some integer }1\le j\le n,\ j<r.    \tag{2}
\]
For either sign, the vanishing can be tested modulo \(3^K\) at any precision satisfying
\[
                   3^K>(1+321^{2j})^{\binom nj}.                         \tag{3}
\]
This test allows repeated factors in the reduction modulo three.
On a sequence with \(r/a^2\to\infty\), every factor's number of exterior roots is eventually the nearest integer to \(2n_i/r\).
In particular every possible sequence in (2) giving Salem numbers tending to one eventually permits this recovery for *all* its rational factors.

<a id="independence"></a>
## Independent actions on distinct rational factors

The [root allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) gives simplicity, and the [quartic trace theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#statement) gives reciprocity and the alternating or symmetric group on each irreducible trace factor.
Write \(N=\max(a,b)\).
The [primitive height and degree estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) give \(h(x)\ll1/N\) for every nontorsion root and make the smallest \(n_i\) tend to infinity.
We verify the quartic input to the [argument across distinct cubic factors](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v177.md#independence).
The graph \(F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\) has support differences generating \(\mathbb Z^2\), and \(|\operatorname{Res}(g,B)|=1\) because \(g+B=-T(T^2+1)\), \(g(0)=-1\) and \(g(i)g(-i)=1\).
In particular it is absolutely irreducible.
For every large \(N\) the [torsion-quotient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) excludes distinct nontorsion roots with a root-of-unity quotient.
With \(L=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) this gives \(L(x^s)=L(x)\) for every positive \(s\), since distinct conjugates remain distinct on taking powers.

Suppose two nontorsion roots \(x,z\), unequal even up to inversion, are multiplicatively dependent.
After possible inversion write \(x=\xi w^u,\ z=\eta w^v\) for torsion \(\xi,\eta\) and coprime positive \(u,v\).
Indeed choose \(i,j\) with \(iu+jv=1\) and set \(w=x^i z^j\) after taking the primitive relation modulo torsion.
The equality \(L(x)=L(x^v)=L(z^u)=L(z)\) shows \(L(w)=L(x)\), of degree at most \(5N\) over \(L\).
The [relative abelian height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) at \(w\) and \(x\) gives \(u,v\ll(\log(2N))^4\) and \([L(x):L]\gg N/(\log(2N))^4\).
For nonzero \(c,e\) and positive \(q\), the curve \(F(cU^q,eV^q)=0\) is absolutely irreducible.
The zeros of \(g\) are nonzero and simple, since \(g=g'=0\) would give \(T=-4/3\) and \(4T^3=1\).
Eisenstein at a simple zero of \(g(cU^q)\) disjoint from \(B(cU^q)\), followed by primitivity over \(\mathbb C[U]\), proves the assertion.
If \(u\ne v\), the two curves scaled by \((\xi^a,\xi^b)\) and \((\eta^a,\eta^b)\) with exponents \(u,v\) have distinct \(V\)-degrees and total degrees \(5u,5v\).
Their intersection contains \((w^a,w^b)\), which generates \(L(w)\) over \(L\) by coprimality.
Bézout gives \([L(x):L]\le25uv\), contradicting the lower bound.
The alternative \(u=v=1\) is excluded by the torsion-quotient theorem.
The [two-coordinate height product bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) at these independent roots, whose joint degree is at most \(25N^2\), now gives \([\mathbb Q(x,z):\mathbb Q]\gg N^2/(\log(2N))^{212}\).

Let \(G\) be the Galois group of the compositum of the trace splitting fields over \(\mathbb Q\).
Its commutator projects onto each \(A_{n_i}\) and lies in their product.
If it were proper, the [subdirect product argument](Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#normal) would identify two same-degree simple groups diagonally, up to an automorphism.
All \(n_i\ge9\) eventually, so this automorphism is a [relabeling](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition).
The normalizer of the resulting diagonal \(A_{n_i}\) in \(S_{n_i}^2\) is diagonal because its centralizer in a single symmetric group is trivial.
Since \(G\) normalizes its commutator, the corresponding two traces would have the same stabilizer and generate the same degree-\(n_i\) field.
Two roots above them would have joint degree at most \(4n_i\), contradicting the joint lower bound.
Thus \(G\) contains the independent alternating groups on all blocks.

<a id="units"></a>
## Signed units in the trace algebra

Label the trace roots by inverse pairs \(\{x,x^{-1}\}\) and put \(t=x^a\).
The corresponding eigenvalue of \(M\) is \(\lambda=D(t+t^{-1})=B(t)B(t^{-1})\), by direct expansion.
The resultant of the parent with \(B(X^a)\) has absolute value one by evaluation at the roots of \(B(X^a)\) and the quartic resultant above.
Hence \(B(x^a)\), and therefore \(\lambda\), are algebraic integer units.
Their product over a complete block of traces is a rational unit \(\epsilon_i\).
For a circle pair the eigenvalue is \(|B(t)|^2>0\).
Nonreal traces come in conjugate pairs whose two eigenvalues have positive product.
For a real exterior root use \(t^4 B(t^{-1})=g(t)=-x^bB(t)\) to get \(\lambda=-x^{b-4a}B(t)^2\).
The [real-root count](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#real) gives no positive exterior roots and exactly one negative exterior root when \(a\) is odd at these slopes.
Consequently for even \(b\) its factor alone has sign \(-1\), and for odd \(b\) every sign is \(+1\).

Within any one trace block the values \(t+t^{-1}\) are distinct.
Indeed \(x^a\) determines \(x^b=-g(t)/B(t)\), and hence \(x\) by coprimality, with inversion preserving the pairing.
If two eigenvalues within a block coincided, double transitivity would make them all equal, impossible for the nonconstant quartic \(D\) on \(n_i>4\) distinct values.
If a subset of labels has a rational product of eigenvalues, it is fixed as a product by the independent alternating actions.
For a partial block choose three labels, one from its smaller side and two from its other side, with the two sides given by membership in the subset.
A three-cycle on these labels changes a single member and preserves every other block, which contradicts the within-block distinction because all eigenvalues are nonzero.
Thus the subsets with rational products are precisely unions of complete blocks, and the product on such a union is the product of its signs.
The separable trace algebra has an eigenbasis of labels over its splitting field, even if different blocks have common eigenvalues.
The exterior power has a basis indexed by subsets of size \(j\).
Counting the two signed eigenspaces proves (1), since dimensions are preserved by extending scalars.

<a id="precision"></a>
## An integral test including ramified primes

For \(r\ge1\), \(|t|\ge4\) would give \(|t|^r=|g(t)/B(t)|<2\), using \((|t|^4+|t|+1)/(|t|^4-|t|^3-1)<2\).
Inversion gives \(1/4<|t|<4\), and so \(|\lambda|\le(1+4^3+4^4)^2=321^2\).
Multiplying the \(\binom nj\) eigenvalue differences bounds \(|E_{j,\epsilon}|\) by the right side of (3).
The integral determinant therefore vanishes exactly when it vanishes modulo that precision.
Any smaller modulus detecting a nonzero determinant already excludes that signed rational divisor degree.
The estimate controls exactness and gives no practical complexity bound.

<a id="counts"></a>
## Recovering exterior counts from rational degrees

Write \(\beta>1\) for the root of \(\beta^3(\beta-1)=1\).
The [only exterior pole](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic) is \(-\beta\).
For each irreducible reciprocal factor put \(d_i=2n_i\), let \(s_i\ge1\) count its exterior roots, and let \(m_i\) denote its logarithmic measure.
The [pole allocation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) gives \(am_i=s_i(\log\beta+O(e^{-cr}))\) and \(\sum_i s_i=a\).
On the circle \(|g|=|B|\), so Jensen's formula gives \(\int\log\max(|g|,|B|)=m(B)=\log\beta\).
The [individual height estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(bm_i/d_i=\log\beta+O(r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\log(2d_i)/d_i})\).
It follows uniformly that \(d_i/r=s_i(1+o(1))\) and
\[
 \left|s_i-\frac{d_i}{r}\right|\ll
 \frac{a}{\sqrt r}+\sqrt{\frac{a\log(2(4a+b))}{r}} .                  \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(d_i\asymp r s_i\), \(s_i\le a\), and the exponential error is absorbed in \(r^{-1/2}\).
Both terms tend to zero if \(r/a^2\to\infty\), giving simultaneous recovery by rounding.
For the second term one may use \(r\ge a^2\) eventually, hence \(a\le\sqrt r\) and \(\log(2(4a+b))=O(\log(2r))\).

For even \(b\), the least \(j\) with \(E_{j,-1}=0\) is the degree of the trace factor containing the negative root, since any negative subset is its union with positive blocks.
Its exterior count is odd by complex conjugation.
When the count is one, \(j/r=(1+o(1))/2<1\); when it is at least three, \(j/r\ge(3/2)(1-o(1))>1\).
Since \(d_i\to\infty\), a reciprocal factor with exactly this one exterior root has circle conjugates and yields a Salem number after changing sign.
This proves (2).
If (2) holds then \(m_i=\log\sigma\sim(\log\beta)/a\), and the [primitive degree and height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) forces \(a\ll(\log N/\log\log N)^3\).
Thus \(r/a^2\to\infty\) along any such sequence with \(N\to\infty\), and \(\sigma\to1\) precisely when \(a\to\infty\).
More generally the [reduction of arbitrary directions to primitive ones](Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#criterion) and \(n\sim b/2\) show that shrinking in the whole quartic family is equivalent to a sequence \(r\to\infty\) with \(E_{j,\epsilon}=0\) for some \(1\le j=o(n)\) and either sign.
Indeed each detected degree contains a complete irreducible trace block, and one such block itself gives a detected degree.
The existence of such vanishing determinants for growing first exponent is open.
