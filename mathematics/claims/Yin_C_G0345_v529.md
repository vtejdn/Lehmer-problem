# Sharp coefficient cost at every growing width per exponent for a single exterior conjugate

<a id="statement"></a>
## All prescribed comparable powers at the calibrated widths

Fix a monic reciprocal minimum \(P\) of a nontorsion unit with exactly one conjugate outside the unit circle, and put \(h=\log M(P)>0\).
Fix \(C>1\).
For any sets \(S\subset[L,CL]\) of distinct positive integers with \(r=|S|\to\infty\), and any integer widths \(b\) with \(b/r\to\infty\), put \(\Sigma=\sum_{p\in S}p\).
There exist nonzero coprime integer polynomials \(U,V\) of degrees at most \(b\) such that \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\), and \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) for every \(p\in S\).
The minimum over all such rows satisfies
\[
        \min\frac{\log\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)}{\Sigma}\longrightarrow h.        \tag{1}
\]
All prescribed slots survive in the same pair.
This applies at the original [full-prime width](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#width) and [odd-block width](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#width).
It also applies to the [dilations of those odd blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v524.md#width), since the ratio of their extreme exponents stays bounded and the width per slot tends to infinity.
If the finite test includes the fallback \(2^\Sigma\) and imposes that cutoff on its rows, the minimum normalized cost for this fixed seed tends to \(\min(h,\log2)\).
Let \(\lambda_1\) be the infimum of the positive logarithmic measures of all monic reciprocal unit minima with exactly one exterior conjugate.
If the seed may be any member of this class, the finite normalized minimum with fallback converges to \(\min(\lambda_1,\log2)\) on every such schedule.

<a id="counts"></a>
## Short homogeneous solutions and a nonzero reflected difference

Write \(e=\deg P\), \(q=\min(\lfloor b/r\rfloor,\lfloor\sqrt L\rfloor,r)\), \(w=rq\), and \(t=2+\lceil r/q^{1/3}\rceil\) for sufficiently large \(L\).
These parameters satisfy
\[
 q\to\infty,\quad t=o(r),\quad w\le b,\quad
 r^2=o(wt),\quad w+r+\log(2Lw)=o(tL),\quad w=o(\Sigma).
\]
Indeed \(tq/r\ge q^{2/3}\) and \(w/(tL)\le q^{4/3}/L\).
The powered minimum \(Q_p\) of the \(p\)-th power of a root of \(P\) has degree \(e\) and logarithmic measure \(ph\), and the \(Q_p\) are distinct by the [single-exterior argument](Yin_C_G0345_v525.md#growing).

Use integral coefficient lists in \([0,B]\) through degree \(w\) for each of two polynomials, with the upper endpoint rounded down.
For requested multiplicities \(m_i\) at exponents \(p_i\), record the first \(m_i\) Hasse jet values at a fixed root of \(P\), multiplying the jet of index \(j\) by the \(j\)-th power of that unit.
For a monomial \(X^d\) the resulting value is \(\binom dj\alpha^d\).
Each coordinate is integral in the same degree-\(e\) field.
The [embedding-box count](Yin_C_G0345_v487.md#many) bounds the log number of possible images by
\[
   e\sum_i m_i\log B+wh\sum_i m_i p_i
         +O_{P,C}\!\left(\sum_i m_i^2\log(2Lw)\right).                    \tag{2}
\]
In fact at an embedding \(x\) the coordinate of index \(j\) has modulus at most \(2(w+1)B(CLw+1)^j\max(1,|x|)^{wp_i+1}\).
A box of diameter less than one at each embedding contains at most one algebraic integer by the norm.
Thus (2) follows by multiplying over the real dimensions of these boxes.
Differences from one member of a largest fiber have coefficient sup norm at most \(B\), satisfy the homogeneous multiplicity conditions, and have log cardinality at least \(2(w+1)\log B\) minus (2).

First use multiplicity two at every slot and take \(\log B=h\Sigma+htL\) up to rounding error.
The [endpoint bound for all homogeneous solutions](Yin_C_G0345_v525.md#endpoints) gives sup distance at least \(\exp(h\Sigma-O_{P,C}(r+\log(2Lw)))\) between any distinct differences.
If their real span had dimension at most \(w+1\), disjoint balls of half that radius in its induced sup norm would bound their log count by \((w+1)(htL+O_{P,C}(r+\log(2Lw)))\).
The lower count from (2) exceeds this bound for large \(L\).
After subtracting the packing bound the leading term is \(whtL\), while the losses are \(O_{P,C}(r\Sigma+rtL+w(r+\log(2Lw)))=o(wtL)\).
As in the [quadratic-width construction](Yin_C_G0345_v525.md#growing), projection of this larger span onto the first polynomial has a nonzero kernel, and polarization then gives a difference or a sum of two differences with nonzero reflected difference.
Denote this pair by \(Z_0\).
Its one-norm cost has logarithm at most \(h\Sigma+o(\Sigma)\).

<a id="protect"></a>
## Preserving one designated slot without a quadratic width

For each designated \(p\in S\), use multiplicity \(t\) at that exponent and two at every other exponent.
Take \(\log B=h\Sigma+3tph/4\) up to rounding.
There is a nonzero integer pair in the difference set.
Indeed in (2) we have \(\sum m_i=2r+t-2\) and \(\sum m_i p_i=2\Sigma+(t-2)p\).
The log domain size minus (2) is bounded below by \(\tfrac12 wtph-o(wtL)\).
The expense \(O_{P,C}((r+t)(\Sigma+tL))=o(wtL)\) follows from \(r^2=o(wt)\).
The remaining expense \(O_{P,C}((r+t^2)\log(2Lw))=o(wtL)\) follows from \(t=O(r)\) and \(w\ge r\).
All estimates hold uniformly in the designated slot.

Let \(\nu\) be the largest common power of \(Q_p\) dividing the two entries of this pair, allowing one entry to be zero.
Divide by \(Q_p^\nu\) in the integral polynomial ring.
At every other slot the homogeneous double contact persists, since the powered minima are distinct.
If \(\nu\ge t-1\), the larger one-norm of the divided pair is at most
\[
     2^w(w+1)B\exp(-\nu ph).
\]
For each nonzero entry this follows by the coefficient bound in terms of Mahler measure, multiplicativity of measure, and the original one-norm bound \((w+1)B\).
But the [endpoint lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v525.md#statement) on the remaining \(r-1\) slots requires the log norm to be at least \(h(\Sigma-p)-O_{P,C}(r+\log(2\Sigma))\).
Together the two bounds would imply
\[
       (t/4-2)ph\le O_{P,C}(w+r+\log(2Lw)),
\]
which is impossible since \(t\to\infty\) and the right side is \(o(tL)\).
Thus \(\nu\le t-2\).
The factor \(Q_p(X^p)\) has a simple zero at each root of \(P\) by separability, the nonzero root, and the nonzero derivative of \(X^p\).
The divided pair therefore still satisfies the required double contact at \(p\), and its entries have no common zero at a root of \(Q_p\).
Its second entry is nonzero there by the value equation.
Call it \(Z_p\), denoting the entire divided pair.
Every \(Z_p\) satisfies all homogeneous double contacts and has log one-norm cost at most \(h\Sigma+3tph/4+O(w+\log(2w))=h\Sigma+o(\Sigma)\) uniformly in \(p\).

<a id="combine"></a>
## One integral pair preserving every slot

Take a linear combination of \(Z_0\) and all the \(Z_p\), with integer scalar parameters.
At a chosen node of each \(Q_p\) the second entry is a nonzero linear polynomial in the parameters, as its coefficient from \(Z_p\) is nonzero.
Some fixed Laurent coefficient of the reflected difference is a nonzero quadratic polynomial in the parameters, by \(Z_0\).
Their product over the \(r\) node tests and this one quadratic is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most \(r+2\) over the splitting field.
It is nonzero at a point of \(\{0,\ldots,r+2\}^{r+1}\) by iterating the univariate root bound.
The selected pair thus has nonzero reflected difference and its entries are both nonzero at every prescribed node, using the value equations and Galois conjugation.
The polynomial degrees are at most \(w\) and combining the pairs adds \(O(\log(2r))\) to the largest log norm.
Remove the primitive polynomial gcd of its two entries.
The contacts survive because this gcd misses all the nodes and each graph was divisible by \(P^2\).
The reflected difference stays nonzero and the increase in log one-norm is at most \(w\log2=o(\Sigma)\) by the same factor bound.
This proves the upper bound in (1), and the [tangency measure inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives the lower bound.
The proof uses the full mass of the sole exterior conjugate in the endpoint estimate for the remaining homogeneous slots.
For the minimum allowing any seed in this class, the tangency inequality and the fallback give the pointwise lower bound \(\min(\lambda_1,\log2)\).
Taking the upper limit first for each fixed seed and then the infimum over these seeds proves equality without a uniform threshold in their degrees.
