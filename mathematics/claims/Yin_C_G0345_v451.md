# Genuine double contacts on every support of minimum cardinality

<a id="statement"></a>
## A genuine signed row on each prescribed set of positions

Let \(G\) be a nonconstant squarefree product of monic reciprocal irreducible minima of nontorsion algebraic integer units.
Assume distinct roots of \(G\) have nontorsion quotient and write \(m=\deg G\).
Let \(K\) be its splitting field and \(D=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
There are constants \(I_G\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\) and \(c_G>0\) with the following property.
For either sign \(\varepsilon\), at every sufficiently large integer \(n\), take \(m\le b\le\exp(c_G n)\) and any \(m+1\) positions \(J\subset\{0,\ldots,b\}\) distinct modulo \(n/\gcd(n,I_G)\).
There is a unique primitive integral row \(A\), up to sign, supported on \(J\) and satisfying
\[
                G(X)^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}).
\]
It uses every position in \(J\), and \(A(c^n)\ne0\) at every root \(c\) of \(G\).
The reversed numerator is therefore also nonzero there.
Every choice of \(J\) is allowed at all widths \(b<n/\gcd(n,I_G)\), hence for every sublinear width sequence eventually.
For every sufficiently large prime exponent \(p\) this includes all widths \(m\le b<p\).
In particular the least formal width of a genuine row for this fixed list is exactly \(m\) at every sufficiently large exponent, before a norm cutoff is imposed.
At fixed \(J,b,\varepsilon\) the primitive row has the [explicit limiting height](#height) on each of finitely many residue classes, allowing arbitrary multiplicative relations among the roots subject to the stated pairwise hypothesis.

<a id="extra"></a>
## Detecting a spurious contact by one more row

The [independent columns theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#statement) gives the unique rational line, all its entries nonzero.
It suffices to rule out numerator vanishing at every prescribed node.
Choose one root from each inverse pair, including a specified root \(c\) as the representative of its pair.
Write \(x\) for a representative, \(t=x^n\), \(D_Y=Y\,d/dY\), and \(X_0\) for a formal variable to be specialized to \(n\).
At the ordinary nodes \(x\ne c\) the rows of the value and Euler derivative system are
\[
  A(t)+\varepsilon x t^b A(t^{-1}),\qquad
  X_0(D_YA)(t)+\varepsilon x t^b\big[(1+bX_0)A(t^{-1})-X_0(D_YA)(t^{-1})\big].
\]
These formulas follow by differentiating the signed graph before evaluation.
If the graph vanishes at \(c\) and \(A(c^n)=0\), then also \(A(c^{-n})=0\).
At this target, with \(t=c^n\), use the three rows
\[
          A(t),\qquad A(t^{-1}),\qquad
          (D_YA)(t)-\varepsilon c t^b(D_YA)(t^{-1}).
\]
With the two values zero, the last row is the actual derivative condition divided by \(n\).
These \(m+1\) equations are square on the chosen columns \(J\).

First set \(X_0=0\), keeping all powers \(x^n\) as characters.
At each ordinary node the two rows then separate into evaluations at \(x^n,x^{-n}\), up to a nonzero root multiplier and a monomial character.
Apart from a common nonzero factor, the resulting determinant is the difference of two determinants.
Each has the \(m\) evaluation rows at all original powered roots and one extra Euler-derivative row.
In the first the derivative is at \(c^n\), in the second at \(c^{-n}\) with the additional multiplier \(\varepsilon c(c^b)^n\).

Take a real linear functional injective on the lattice generated by the roots modulo torsion, by choosing rationally independent values on a basis.
At the maximum of this functional on the bases of either determinant expansion, the increasing column indices must be assigned to the roots in increasing functional order.
The twice-used root receives two distinct consecutive slots in this ordering.
The two permutations at that root sum, after removal of the common base, to a signed nonzero difference of column indices, since the two row multipliers are \(1,j\).
All other roots have single slots, so the maximizing base is unique up to these two permutations, which give the same actual base.
Multiplication of the second determinant by \((c^b)^n\) translates all its functional weights by the same amount.
If the two maximal weights differ, the larger survives all grouping by torsion ratios.
If they are equal, the corresponding bases differ by a root of unity.
On every residue class killing this torsion, cancellation would require \(c\) times a root of unity to be a nonzero rational number, the signed ratio of the two positive column differences.
An algebraic integer unit which becomes rational after multiplication by a root of unity must itself be torsion.
Thus the combined expression at \(X_0=0\) has at least one surviving group on every class, uniformly in the choice of the column indices.

<a id="moving"></a>
## Keeping a nonzero coefficient when the support moves

In the determinant before specializing \(X_0\), group bases that differ by torsion, absorbing into the coefficients the corresponding roots of unity raised to the actual exponent \(n\).
The number of terms and their degrees in \(X_0\) are bounded solely in terms of \(m\).
Consider the coefficient polynomial \(p(X_0)\) of the surviving maximal group at zero.
Its coefficients are algebraic integers in \(K\), and, up to multiplication by a unit, its constant term is either a nonzero difference of two column indices or \(d_1-c\xi d_2\).
Here \(d_1,d_2\) are signed nonzero differences of indices in \(J\), and \(\xi\) belongs to the finite group of roots of unity in \(K\).
This follows from the two surviving derivative permutations at each maximum, since all scalar factors from splitting the ordinary rows are units common to the two determinants.
It also covers groups whose additional terms at \(X_0\ne0\) have zero constant coefficient.

For each target and each \(\xi\), the group \(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Zc\xi\) has rank two and finite index in its saturation inside \(\mathcal O_K\).
Take \(I_G\) to be a common multiple of these finitely many indices.
If \(d_1-c\xi d_2\in n\mathcal O_K\), then its quotient by \(n\) belongs to that saturation.
After multiplication by \(I_G\), uniqueness of rational coordinates on \(1,c\xi\) yields \(n\mid I_Gd_1\) and \(n\mid I_Gd_2\).
For a single rational difference, membership in \(n\mathcal O_K\) already implies divisibility by \(n\).
Neither case is possible when all indices are distinct modulo \(n/\gcd(n,I_G)\).
Thus \(p(0)\notin n\mathcal O_K\), and \(p(n)\equiv p(0)\bmod n\mathcal O_K\) supplies a nonzero grouped coefficient at the actual exponent.

After evaluation at \(X_0=n\) all the nonzero grouped coefficients have absolute logarithmic heights \(O_G(\log(2n)+\log(2+b))\).
The surviving bases are units in \(K\) with pairwise nontorsion ratios.
The [almost-unit noncancellation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#heights) now excludes a zero determinant at all sufficiently large \(n\), even when the bases vary with the columns.
In detail, on a minimal vanishing subsystem each normalized coordinate would have height at least \(n\eta_K-O_G(\log n+\log(2+b))\), for the positive lower bound on nontorsion unit height in the fixed field.
Its outside-archimedean-place sum would be \(O_G(\log n+\log(2+b))\), coming entirely from the coefficient ratio.
Choose \(c_G\) sufficiently small for the height to be at least \(n\eta_K/2\) and the outside sum to be at most a fixed admissible tolerance times this lower bound, for the finitely many possible subsystem dimensions.
Decrease \(c_G\) if necessary so that the original column independence theorem applies on the same range.
Each coordinate separately satisfies [Xiao's almost-unit hypothesis](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations), while its height grows without bound.
This contradicts finiteness for a nondegenerate unit equation, including the immediate one-coordinate case.
The finite list of target roots and the two signs allow a common eventual cutoff.

<a id="height"></a>
## The height of the primitive row at fixed positions

Fix \(b,J,\varepsilon\), and fix the finite group of torsion elements in the multiplicative group generated by all roots of \(G\).
Write \(T\) for its order and take ordinary units \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\) lifting a basis of the quotient by torsion.
At residue \(n\equiv a\bmod T\), replace each \(x^n\) in the \(m\) ordinary value and derivative rows by \(\xi_x^a Z^{v_x}\), where \(x=\xi_x\prod g_i^{v_{x,i}}\).
Take their signed maximal minors on the \(m+1\) columns.
In \(K[X_0,Z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,Z_r^{\pm1}]\) divide the tuple by the common polynomial gcd.
Clear scalar denominators and multiply by a monomial so the entries are integral polynomials with no common polynomial factor, including a coordinate variable.
Write \(\mathcal S_a\) for the union of their \(Z\)-exponent supports, treating the remaining coefficients as polynomials in \(X_0\).
At every residue class these minors are generically nonzero by column independence and eventual specialization.
Then
\[
    \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\ n\equiv a\bmod T}}
        \frac{\log\|A\|_1}{n}
      =\frac1D\sum_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
         \max_{v\in\mathcal S_a}\sum_{i=1}^r v_i\log|\sigma(g_i)|.
\]

Indeed distinct exponent vectors give bases with nontorsion ratios, so each nonzero polynomial specializes to an eventually nonzero recurrence at \((n,g_1^n,\ldots,g_r^n)\).
The [placewise recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives the displayed maximum as the limiting normalized log maximum at that embedding.
It applies also to a common factor removed before specialization.
For the finite places, the [projective height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#height) applies to these coprime entries.
One nonconstant entry and an integral linear combination avoiding all its irreducible factors give two coprime polynomials whose common local valuations dominate those of the tuple.
A nonzero constant entry or constant combination bounds the common valuations directly.
Otherwise use [Xiao's outside-place gcd bound](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) with the archimedean set of places at \((n,g_1^n,\ldots,g_r^n)\).
Its height is of order \(n\) and the entire outside-place contribution to the almost-unit condition is at most \(\log n\).
For any fixed positive tolerance in that bound the exceptional proper subvariety meets only finitely many points on our progression, since a nonzero polynomial containing it specializes to an eventually nonzero recurrence.
Letting the tolerance tend to zero makes the normalized sum of common finite valuations \(o(n)\).
Consequently the projective height of the specialized tuple has the asserted slope.
It is the height of the rational primitive kernel generator because the row conditions define the rational graph remainder kernel.
For that generator the projective height is the log maximum coefficient, within \(\log(m+1)\) of the log one-norm.
Translating the exponent support by a single vector changes the sum by zero by the product formula for the units \(g_i\).
This gives a finite polynomial calculation from the specified multiplicative presentation for each fixed support.
It also decides the eventual strict height test on each residue class for the fixed row line.
Indeed if \(L_a\) is the displayed rate, choose one maximizing exponent at each embedding.
Then \(\exp(2D L_a)\) is a product of squared absolute values of conjugate algebraic units, hence is an algebraic unit.
The identity \(L_a=(\log2)/8\) would give \(\exp(8D L_a)=2^D\), a rational integer greater than one, contradicting that unit property.
Thus \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) eventually holds throughout the class for \(L_a<(\log2)/8\), and eventually fails throughout it for \(L_a>(\log2)/8\).
The signs of these comparisons are decidable by comparing products of positive real algebraic numbers, as in the [one-seed progression test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v257.md#cutoff).
The computation uses the fixed root group data and gives no numerical onset.

For \(c>1\) quadratic reciprocal with integer trace at least three congruent to three modulo 1009, the [quadratic unit and square calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/versions/v4/readme.md#costs) evaluates this height at both signs.
At the minimum width four the rate is \(8\log c\).
At width five, omitting position \(0,1,2,3,4,5\), respectively, gives the six rates \(8,10,11,11,10,8\) times \(\log c\).
Every fixed width at least four admits a five-position support of rate \(8\log c\) by central translation, while the growing-width unrestricted-support rate is \(3\log c\).
