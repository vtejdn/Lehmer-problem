# Counting terminal exponent arrays in affine subspaces

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound in the dimension of the exponent family

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit and let \(c_1,\ldots,c_r\) be fixed nonzero complex coefficients, with \(r\ge2\).
Count integer arrays \((t_1,\ldots,t_{r-1},0)\) satisfying \(\sum_i c_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) with no nonempty proper zero subsum.
Fix the marked exponent at zero, let \(Q\) be any positive integer with \(\log M(\alpha^Q)>(r/2)\log r\), and put \(B=\binom r2\).
Every rational affine subspace of dimension \(h\) in the remaining \(r-1\) exponent coordinates contains at most \((QB)^h\) distinct solution arrays.
The bound is uniform in the subspace, its intercept and the coefficients.
For the shrinking sequences and fixed tuples specified [below](#deleted), it gives the stated weighted lower bound for primitive shapes after deleting two coefficients.

<a id="flat"></a>
## Proof of the affine bound

If the subspace contains an integer point, its integer points form a translate of a rank-\(h\) lattice.
Partition by the coefficients of a lattice basis modulo \(Q\), producing at most \(Q^h\) classes.
In each class write the full exponent array as \(s+Qu\), with a single fixed integral representative \(s\) and with both marked coordinates zero.
For any finite selection of rows in the class the [common rational-function lift](Yin_C_G0345_v592.md#lifting) gives common nonzero rational functions \(b_i(X)\) for which each row satisfies \(\sum_i b_i(X)X^{u_i}=0\) without a proper identically zero subsum.
Using an arbitrary integral representative in the lift just multiplies each monomial column by a fixed Laurent monomial and adjusts the corresponding kernel coordinate.

On the current affine space of possible rows, group indices whose exponent coordinates differ by a constant throughout the space.
Discard branches with no selected rows, so every such constant is integral.
Within a group absorb these differences into its coefficient, keeping one representative exponent.
With positive dimension there must be at least two groups, since all differences constant together with the fixed zero exponent would determine the entire row.
Each group coefficient is nonzero by nondegeneracy at any selected row in the branch.
At a solution two distinct group terms must tie for largest degree at infinity.
For each candidate pair the degree tie prescribes one difference of representative exponents, a nonconstant affine functional on this space by construction.
The corresponding level set has dimension one less, and there are at most \(B\) choices.
Regroup on each such level set, using the same lifted rows and coefficients.
At dimension zero the subspace contains at most one row, giving at most \(B^h\) rows per class by induction.
The conclusion for arbitrary solution sets follows by applying this bound to every finite selection.

<a id="deleted"></a>
## Primitive shapes after erasing two terms

Take a power-degree-preserving sequence covered by the [weighted witness count](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement), fix \(\lambda\ge0\), and choose its actual signed tuple, including marked coefficient.
Delete the two labeled entries of largest absolute magnitude, resolving ties once for the tuple.
Within each remaining exponent list translate one predetermined surviving coordinate to zero and divide by the positive greatest common divisor of the remaining differences.
First keep the surviving labels in their original coordinate order.
In the sorted marked terminal lists the ordinary coordinates already have fixed order, and the marked coordinate can occupy at most \(r\) positions, so forgetting that label costs at most a factor \(r\).
For sufficiently large \(r\) the survivors contain distinct exponent coordinates since the ordinary exponents are distinct.

All full arrays realizing one such primitive labeled shape lie in a single rational affine subspace of dimension at most three in the original marked coordinates.
Indeed, before imposing the zero mark the survivors have the form \(a+qv_i\) for a fixed integral vector \(v\), with the two erased coordinates free.
There are four parameters and simultaneous translation freely changes the marked coordinate, giving dimension at most three after imposing the mark.
Choose \(Q=1+\lfloor r\log r/(2m)\rfloor\), where \(m=\log M(\alpha)\); degree preservation gives \(\log M(\alpha^Q)=Qm\).
The first bound shows that at most \((QB)^3\) full arrays yield each surviving primitive labeled shape, without requiring reciprocity or recovering a dilation.
For the number \(D\) of surviving shapes, even when each list is sorted, translated to have minimum zero and stripped of the surviving labels, this proves
\[
  D\ \ge\ \frac{H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda(c_\lambda/m)^{r-2}}{r(QB)^3},
  \qquad \log D-\lambda\log H_2(\mathbf b)\ge (1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m).
\]
Indeed \(\log(QB)=O(\log(1/m)+\log r)\), whose ratio to \((r-2)\log(1/m)\) tends to zero as both \(r\) and \(\log(1/m)\) grow.
This controls concentration of the witnesses on simple exponent families even after projecting away the two most expensive coefficients.
It leaves open the number of different surviving shapes passing the [reciprocal trace and divisibility tests](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v313.md#recovery) in the actual finite windows.
