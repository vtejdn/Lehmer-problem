# The cost of monicity for multiples on two exponent classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A house obstruction and a reciprocal unit construction

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit, let \(d=\deg P\), and write \(m=\log M\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Choose a root \(\alpha\) of maximum modulus \(\rho>1\).
Let \(p\ge3\) be an odd integer and \(1\le s\le(p-1)/2\).
For a monic integral multiple \(F\) of \(P\), set \(m(R)=m(F/P)\), equivalently the total log measure of its extra noncyclotomic irreducible factors counted with multiplicity.
If \(F\) has \(k\) nonzero terms occupying two residues modulo \(p\) at cyclic distance \(s\), then
\[
 \begin{split}
 m(R)\ \ge\ &(p-s)\log\rho+\log\delta_s
                  -(k-2)\log2-\log k-m(P),\\
 \delta_s={}&\min\{1,\operatorname{dist}(\alpha^s,\mathbb Z)\}
       \ \ge\ (2+\rho^s)^{-(d-1)}\exp[-s\,m(P)]>0.                    \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
If the \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) are distinct, then for each \(1\le r<p\) there is a monic reciprocal multiple \(J\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(J(0)=1\), occupying precisely classes \(0,r\), whose two rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X]\) and for which
\[
   \deg J\le p(d+2)+r,\qquad
   m(J/P)\le (p-1)m(P)+(d+1)\log 2+\log(d+1).                         \tag{2}
\]
Any prescribed pair at distance \(s\) is an additive translate of \(0,s\) modulo \(p\).
A monomial shift by at most \(p-1\) gives a monic witness on this pair with the same measure and coprime rows over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
The ordinary reciprocal polynomial with constant one in (2) is constructed on \(0,r\).

For fixed \(P\), take primes \(p\to\infty\), integers \(b\to\infty\) with \(b=o(p)\), and prescribed pairs at cyclic distances \(s=o(p)\).
Let \(A_p\) be the minimum of \(m(F/P)\) over monic multiples on each prescribed pair with degree at most \(p(b+2)\).
The same assertions hold when coprime Laurent rows are required.
Then
\[
        \max\{\log\rho,\ m(P)/2\}
         \ \le\ \liminf A_p/p\ \le\ \limsup A_p/p\ \le\ m(P).           \tag{3}
\]
For a Salem minimal polynomial, or a reciprocal quadratic unit with just one exterior conjugate, \(\log\rho=m(P)\).
The limit in (3) is then \(m(P)\), twice the [minimum for unrestricted integer leading coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement).
For classes \(0,s\) the same limit holds with a monic reciprocal witness of constant one and coprime polynomial rows.

<a id="house"></a>
## The two possible leading residues

Multiply \(F\) by a monomial so that the occupied residues are \(0,s\).
This preserves monicity, the coefficient height \(H\), the number \(k\) of terms, the zeros in \(\mathbb C^\times\) and \(m(R)\).
If the leading exponent is \(np\), every other exponent is at most \(np-p+s\).
Evaluating at \(\alpha\) gives \(kH\ge\rho^{p-s}\) by the triangle inequality.
If the leading exponent is \(np+s\), let \(A\in\mathbb Z\) be the coefficient of \(x^{np}\), taking \(A=0\) when this term is absent.
The two top positions at \(\alpha\) contribute \(\alpha^{np}(\alpha^s+A)\).
Every remaining exponent is at most \(np-p+s\), giving \(kH\ge|\alpha^s+A|\rho^{p-s}\).
The number \(\alpha^s\) cannot be a rational integer since it is a nontorsion algebraic unit.
Both cases therefore give \(kH\ge\delta_s\rho^{p-s}\).
The [sparse coefficient bound of Dobrowolski and Smyth](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(H\le 2^{k-2}M(F)\), proving the first inequality in (1).

If \(\delta_s<1\), take \(a\in\mathbb Z\) nearest to \(\alpha^s\), so \(|a|\le1+\rho^s\).
The absolute field norm of \(\alpha^s-a\) is a positive integer.
For each of the other \(d-1\) conjugates \(\alpha_i\), use
\(
 |\alpha_i^s-a|\le(2+\rho^s)\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^s.
\)
The product of the \(\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^s\) over these other embeddings is at most \(\exp[s\,m(P)]\).
This proves the second inequality of (1), which also holds for \(\delta_s=1\).

<a id="interpolation"></a>
## Interpolation with integral coefficients and a uniform norm bound

Write the conjugates as \(\alpha_i\) and put \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\).
Under the distinctness hypothesis \(Q(X)=\prod_i(X-\beta_i)\) is monic integral of degree \(d\).
Inversion permutes its roots without fixed points, so \(Q(0)=1\) and \(X^dQ(1/X)=Q(X)\).
Define
\[
 V(X)=Q'(X),\qquad
 U(X)=-\sum_{i=1}^d\alpha_i^r\prod_{j\ne i}(X-\beta_j),\qquad
 F_0(x)=U(x^p)+x^r V(x^p).
\]
The coefficients of \(U\) are algebraic integers fixed by the Galois group of the splitting field, hence rational integers.
For every \(i\), \(V(\beta_i)\ne0\) and \(U(\beta_i)=-\alpha_i^r V(\beta_i)\ne0\).
Thus \(P\mid F_0\), and both row degrees are at most \(d-1\).

For the sum of absolute coefficients \(\|\cdot\|_1\), each term in \(U\) has norm bounded by
\[
 |\alpha_i|^r\prod_{j\ne i}(1+|\beta_j|)
 \ \le\ 2^{d-1}\prod_j\max(1,|\alpha_j|)^p
 = 2^{d-1}\exp[p\,m(P)].
\]
Here \(r<p\) allows the omitted \(i\)-th factor to absorb \(|\alpha_i|^r\).
The same upper bound holds for each of the \(d\) summands in \(Q'\).
It follows that
\[
        \|F_0\|_1\le d\,2^d\exp[p\,m(P)],
        \qquad \|Q\|_1\le 2^d\exp[p\,m(P)].                            \tag{4}
\]

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Unit endpoints and the common factor of the rows

Put \(K=d+2,\ T=pK+r\), and for \(t\in\{1,2\}\) set \(H_t(x)=x^{tp}F_0(x)\).
For \(c\in\{1,-1\}\) form
\[
        J_{t,c}(x)=Q(x^p)(1+x^{2p+r})
                    +c\{H_t(x)+x^T H_t(1/x)\}.
\]
Both \(H_t\) and its reversal about \(T\) have all exponents strictly between \(0\) and \(T\), since \(1\le t\le2\) and \(t+d-1\le K-1\).
Reciprocity of \(P\) implies \(P\mid x^TH_t(1/x)\).
Thus \(J_{t,c}\) is reciprocal and divisible by \(P\), with leading and constant coefficients one.
Reversal about \(T\) exchanges residues \(0,r\).

At \(\beta=\alpha^p\), the value of the zero row is
\[
               c\{\beta^t U(\beta)+\beta^{K-t}V(\beta^{-1})\}.
\]
Both summands are nonzero.
Vanishing for both \(t=1,2\) would imply \(\beta^2=1\), so choose \(t\) for which the displayed value is nonzero.
At \(X=1\) the same row is \(Q(1)+c\{U(1)+V(1)\}\).
Since \(Q(1)\ne0\), at least one choice of sign \(c\) makes this nonzero as well.

Let \(A(X)\) be the monic polynomial gcd of the two rows.
It is integral since it divides the monic row of residue \(r\), and \(A(0)=\pm1\) since it divides the row of residue zero with constant one.
The two rows are reversals of each other about \(K\), so their common nonzero roots and multiplicities are invariant under inversion.
Thus \(A^*=\pm A\).
The sign is positive since \(A(1)\ne0\), and monicity then gives \(A(0)=1\).
Dividing by \(A(x^p)\) produces a monic reciprocal integral polynomial \(J\) of constant one and coprime nonzero rows, with \(P\mid J\) because \(A(\beta)\ne0\).
From (4),
\(
 M(J)\le M(J_{t,c})\le\|J_{t,c}\|_1
        \le 2^{d+1}(d+1)\exp[p\,m(P)].
\)
This proves (2).

<a id="limits"></a>
## Fixed and varying inputs

For fixed \(P\) the powers \(\alpha_i^p\) are distinct at all sufficiently large primes, since each quotient of two distinct conjugates can have order \(p\) for at most one prime.
For a varying input it suffices that \(p\) be prime with \(p-1>d(d-1)\).
Indeed a quotient of two distinct conjugates has degree at most \(d(d-1)\) by counting its Galois images, whereas a primitive \(p\)-th root has degree \(p-1\).
A Salem input satisfies the distinctness condition for every \(p\ge1\).
The unique exterior root is the only conjugate with the same \(p\)-th power, so its field degree over that of its \(p\)-th power is one.

Under the degree cap \(p(b+2)\), two classes have at most \(2(b+3)\) occupied exponents.
For fixed \(P\), (1) gives \(-\log\delta_s=O_P(s+1)\), hence \(m(R)/p\ge\log\rho-o(1)\) under the hypotheses of (3).
The [fixed-input minimum for integral rows](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) gives the other lower bound \(m(P)/2\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\), the shifted construction from (2) fits the cap and supplies the upper bound and nonemptiness.
Minima are attained because bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound the integer coefficients.
The same upper and lower bounds apply when imposing the specified reciprocal and coprimality conditions on \(0,s\).

The finite bounds also delimit uniformity when \(P\) varies.
Suppose \(p\to\infty\), \(m(P)\) stays bounded, the powered conjugates are distinct, \(b\ge d+3\), \(b=o(p)\), and \(ds=o(p)\).
For the same prescribed-pair minima, (1) and (2) give
\[
                        \log\rho-o(1)\le A_p/p\le m(P)+o(1).
\]
Indeed \(-\log\delta_s\le s\,m(P)+(d-1)\log(2+\rho^s)=O(ds+d)\) uniformly for bounded \(m(P)\).
For a sequence of Salem inputs with \(m(P)\to0\), one can compare the costs on the smaller relative scale as well.
If \(s\,m(P)\to0\), the exterior conjugate is \(\rho=\exp m(P)>1\), and eventually \(\delta_s=\rho^s-1\ge s\,m(P)\).
If also \(s=o(p)\), \(b\ge d+3\), and \(b+|\log(s\,m(P))|=o(p\,m(P))\), the two bounds give \(A_p/(p\,m(P))\to1\).
These conditions leave open an input-independent positive lower bound for \(m(P)\).
