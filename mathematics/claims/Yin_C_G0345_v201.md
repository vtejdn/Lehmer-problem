# Local degree bounds for prime-binomial factors at prime-power delays

<a id="statement"></a>
## A gap for bounded cofactors of the delay

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), with \(k\ge2\), positive exponents \(u_i,n\), and a sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Write
\[
 A=\prod_i(X^{u_i}-p_i),\quad D=\sum_i u_i,\quad A^*=X^DA(1/X),
 \quad P=X^n A+\varepsilon A^*,\quad g=\gcd_i u_i.
\]
Choose a prime \(\ell\) such that every seed \(p_i\ne\ell\) is congruent to \(1\) or \(-1\) modulo \(\ell\); the choice \(\ell=2\) always works.
Put \(\Delta=u_j\) if \(p_j=\ell\), and \(\Delta=0\) otherwise.
Write \(N=n+\Delta=mM\), where \(M=\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) and \(\ell\nmid m\), and put \(B=D+\Delta\).
Let \(b=1\) if \(\ell=2\); for odd \(\ell\) take the unique \(b\in\{1,-1\}\) congruent to \(-\varepsilon\prod_{p_i\ne\ell}(-p_i)\) modulo \(\ell\).
Set
\[
 H=bA+\varepsilon X^\Delta A^*,\qquad
 h=v_\ell(\operatorname{content} H),\qquad L=M/\ell^{h-1}.
\]
Here \(1\le h\le\log_\ell(2\prod_i(1+p_i))\); when \(\ell\) is one of the seeds, \(h=1\) for both signs.

If \(B<M/\ell^h\), the irreducible rational factors of \(P\) of degree less than \(L-B\) occupy total degree at most \(mB+D\), counted with multiplicity.
For every fixed bound \(m\le m_0\) and \(n\ge C(D+1)^9\), with \(C\) depending only on the primes and \(m_0\), **each noncyclotomic factor has degree at least \(L-B\)**.
For such a factor \(S\), as \(n\to\infty\) in this range,
\[
   m(S)\ \ge\ \left(\frac{1}{m\ell^{h-1}}-o(1)\right)\sum_i\log p_i,
\]
where \(m(S)\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure and the error is uniform.
In particular these factors have a uniform positive gap, with no bound on \(g\).
When \(m=h=1\) there is just one noncyclotomic factor, and its measure differs from \(\sum_i\log p_i\) by the exponentially small error in the [prime-binomial allocation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement).

<a id="residue"></a>
## Roots reducing to a root of unity

Modulo \(\ell\), for every \(p_i\ne\ell\) one has \(1-p_iX^{u_i}\equiv-p_i(X^{u_i}-p_i)\).
For a seed equal to \(\ell\), its factors in \(A,A^*\) reduce to \(X^\Delta,1\), respectively.
Consequently \(H\equiv0\pmod\ell\).
It is nonzero: at any zero of \(A\), the nonzero factor \(X^\Delta A^*\) does not vanish since the roots of \(A\) are strictly exterior and those of \(A^*\) strictly interior.
Its coefficient norm is at most \(2\prod_i(1+p_i)\), proving the bounds on \(h\).
If \(\Delta>0\), the constant coefficient \(bA(0)\) is divisible by \(\ell\) exactly once.

Work in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) with \(v(\ell)=1\) and \(v(0)=+\infty\).
The monic polynomial \(F=X^\Delta P=X^N A+\varepsilon X^\Delta A^*\) has integral roots and reduces to
\[
             \overline F=(X^m-b)^M\overline A,
\]
since \(b^M=b\) (also when \(\ell=2\), by our choice of \(b\)).
The \(m\) roots \(\zeta\) of \(X^m=b\) reduce to distinct residue classes because the reduction is separable.
Reducing the linear factors of \(F\) shows that at most \(D\) roots counted with multiplicity reduce outside those classes.
For \(\alpha\) in the class of \(\zeta\), write \(\alpha=\zeta(1+y)\); then \(v(y)>0\) and
\[
                        v(\alpha^m-b)=v(y).
\]
Indeed \(((1+y)^m-1)/y\) has unit constant coefficient \(m\) and is a unit at such \(y\), including \(y=0\) by polynomial evaluation.

<a id="local"></a>
## Counting roots close to each lift

Assume \(B<M/\ell^h\).
For each lift consider the coefficients \(c_i\) of
\[
 F(\zeta(1+Y))=H(\zeta(1+Y))+
              bA(\zeta(1+Y))\big((1+Y)^N-1\big).
\]
The first polynomial has degree at most \(B\) and its minimum coefficient valuation is exactly \(h\), since the substitution and its inverse preserve integrality after dividing by \(\ell^h\).
For \(1\le r\le N\), the identity \(\binom Nr=(N/r)\binom{N-1}{r-1}\) gives \(v\binom Nr\ge v_\ell(M)-v_\ell(r)\).
Thus the second summand has coefficient valuations at least \(h+1\) at indices \(i<M/\ell^h\), and there is a \(j\le B\) with \(v(c_j)=h\).

We claim that for \(t>1/(L-B)\) the minimum of \(v(c_i)+it\) occurs only at indices \(i\le B\).
For \(B<i<L\) all the contributing positive binomial indices are less than \(M/\ell^{h-1}\), so \(v(c_i)\ge h\).
For \(i\ge L\), partition the indices into \(M/\ell^e\le i<M/\ell^{e-1}\) for \(1\le e\le h-1\), and \(i\ge M\) for \(e=0\).
The binomial bound and integrality give \(v(c_i)\ge e\) in each range.
Since \(i\ge\ell^{h-1-e}L\ge(h-e)L\), we have \((i-B)t>h-e\).
In both cases \(v(c_i)+it>h+Bt\ge v(c_j)+jt\), as claimed.

For a polynomial over this valued field the least index minimizing the weighted coefficient valuation is additive under multiplication.
Indeed, the product of the two least minimizing terms uniquely gives minimum valuation at the sum of their indices, and every earlier coefficient has strictly greater weighted valuation.
For a linear factor \(Y-y\), the least minimizing index is one if \(v(y)>t\), and zero otherwise.
Factoring over the algebraic closure now bounds by \(B\) the number of roots with \(v(y)>t\).
Choosing \(t\) just above \(1/(L-B)\) gives the same bound for \(v(y)>1/(L-B)\), since there are only finitely many roots.

Over the \(m\) residue classes, all but at most \(mB+D\) roots of \(F\) therefore satisfy
\[
                  0<v(\alpha^m-b)\le 1/(L-B).
\]
If such an \(\alpha\) has rational degree \(d\), the nonzero number \(\alpha^m-b\) has a rational polynomial equation of degree at most \(d\).
At least two nonzero terms minimize valuation in that equation, so \(v(\alpha^m-b)\) is the quotient of a nonzero integer by an integer of absolute value at most \(d\).
Its positivity gives \(v(\alpha^m-b)\ge1/d\), proving \(d\ge L-B\).
Every root of a smaller rational factor is thus among the exceptions, as asserted.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Ruling out small noncyclotomic factors

For \(n\ge C(D+1)^9\), the [integer allocation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) assigns each noncyclotomic factor of degree \(d\) a positive integer share \(s\), with \(|d/n-s/g|<1/(3(D+1)^2)\).
In particular \(d\ge n/(2g)\).
For \(m\le m_0\) we may enlarge \(C\) so that \(B<M/\ell^h\) and \(n/(2g)>mB+D\), using \(B\le2D\), \(g\le D\) and the fixed bound on \(h\).
The local count then gives \(d\ge L-B\) for every noncyclotomic factor.
Dividing by \(n\) gives \(d/n\ge1/(m\ell^{h-1})-B/n\), with \(B/n\to0\) uniformly.
The allocation error \(|d/n-s/g|\) tends to zero uniformly: it is bounded by \(1/(3(D+1)^2)\) when \(D\) grows, and the more precise estimate in the same argument tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\) for bounded \(D\).
The cited theorem gives \(m(S)=(s/g)\sum_i\log p_i+O((D+1)\exp(-cn/(D+1)^2))\), proving the measure bound.
For the finitely many inputs below any fixed delay bound a noncyclotomic factor has positive measure by the same theorem's Kronecker argument.
When \(m=h=1\), the bound \(d\ge M-B=n-D\) excludes two such factors since \(2(n-D)>n+D\); their shares sum to \(g\), so the unique factor has share \(g\).

For example, let \(p_1=2,p_2=3\), and take \(u_1=u_2=g\) with arbitrary \(g\) coprime to six.
For either \(\ell=2\) or \(\ell=3\), choose \(n=\ell^a-g\ge C(2g+1)^9\).
The other seed is congruent to \(\pm1\pmod\ell\), and \(\gcd(n,g)=1\).
Both signs have \(h=m=1\), hence just one noncyclotomic factor, although \(g\) can grow without bound.
