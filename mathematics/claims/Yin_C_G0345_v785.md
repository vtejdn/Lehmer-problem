# Pole orbits and powered face polynomials for Salem numbers on a plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## A necessary condition at a limiting ray

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be primitive and absolutely irreducible, with two-dimensional Newton polygon.
Let \(C\) be the smooth projective normalization of its curve and \(H\) its finite group of translations in the algebraic torus preserving the curve.
Write \(s\) for the exponent of \(H\), with \(s=1\) for the trivial group.
The group is the annihilator of the lattice generated by the differences of the exponent vectors of \(F\), so \(s\) can be computed from the support alone.

Suppose infinitely many distinct Salem numbers \(\tau\) satisfy \(F(\xi\tau^a,\eta\tau^b)=0\) with arbitrary roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) and \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\).
After passing to a subsequence, the exponent directions approach a normal to opposite primitive edges, both of logarithmic measure \(m(F)>0\).
Use unimodular torus coordinates \(U,V\), with \(U\) tangent to those edges and \(V\) complementary.
Inverting the coordinates separately as needed, one can arrange
\[
 U(P_i)=\zeta\tau_i^{k_i},\qquad V(P_i)=\theta\tau_i^{l_i},
 \qquad l_i/k_i\to\infty,\qquad k_i,l_i>0 ,
\]
with fixed roots of unity \(\zeta,\theta\) satisfying \(\zeta^s,\theta^s\in\{1,-1\}\).
There is \(R>1\) with \(\tau_i^{k_i}\to R\).
Write \(F=\sum_{j=0}^{D}A_j(U)V^j\) in these coordinates up to a monomial, with \(A_0A_D\ne0\).

* For each nonzero root \(\lambda\) of \(A_D\), the number \(\lambda^s\) is conjugate over \(\mathbb Q\) to \(r=\zeta^sR^s\).
  For each nonzero root \(\lambda\) of \(A_0\), its \(s\)-th power is conjugate to \(r^{-1}\).
  In particular the two powered root sets are each the complete root set of one irreducible rational polynomial, and the two sets are reciprocal.
* Consider the poles of \(V\) with \(U\)-value finite and nonzero on \(C\).
  They form a single orbit under the group generated by \(H\) and absolute Galois conjugation, and the analogous zeros form another such orbit.
  A torsion translate of coordinate inversion carries the pole orbit to the zero orbit.
  All these marked poles and zeros have the same absolute order of \(V\) and the same height \(h(R)\) of their \(U\)-value.
* There is an integer \(1\le f\le |H|\) common to both sides such that for every rational Galois orbit \(\mathcal O\) of marked poles or marked zeros,
  \[
            f\,\frac{|\mathcal O|\,|\operatorname{ord}_Q V|\,h(U(Q))}
                       {m(F)} \ \in\ \{1,\ldots,f\}\quad(Q\in\mathcal O).
  \]
  The fractions before multiplication by \(f\) sum to one on each side.
  For untwisted sections, or when \(H\) is trivial, \(f=1\) and all marked poles form one rational Galois orbit containing a real point, as do all marked zeros.

If no eligible ray and orientation passes these conditions, the set of Salem numbers on the translated sections is finite.
For a sequence of Salem numbers tending to one, \(k_i\) must also tend to infinity.
The [single pole polynomial for two exponent progressions](Yin_C_G0345_v748.md#statement) arises from a critical sparse test on a shrinking Salem sequence.
Here the fixed curve can have several sheets, and distinct Salem numbers suffice.

<a id="directions"></a>
## From an infinite set to a short and a long character

Each fixed powered pair can give only finitely many Salem numbers by the [degree bound over a fixed pair](Yin_C_G0345_v781.md#roots) with one exterior conjugate.
Pass to distinct pairs and omit the finitely many singular points, so each pair defines its unique lift to \(C\) over the field of the pair.
The [uniform height bound for section roots](Yin_C_G0345_v774.md#salem) and Northcott's theorem imply \(d_i=[\mathbb Q(\tau_i):\mathbb Q]\to\infty\).
Only two conjugates of a Salem number lie off the circle, so their proportion tends to zero.
The [archimedean face criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v774.md#statement) forces every limiting direction to expose opposite primitive edges of measure \(m(F)\), including when the exponent directions have bounded primitive norm.
There are finitely many edge normals, each rational.
Along a convergent subsequence choose \(U,V\) as in the [unit-circle fiber theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v776.md#statement).
All fibers over \(|U|=1\) have \(|V|=1\), and neither extreme face polynomial has a root on the circle.
In particular \(k_i=0\) is impossible for a nontorsion unit.
It follows that the primitive norms tend to infinity and \(|l_i/k_i|\to\infty\).
Fix the signs by a further subsequence and the two coordinate inversions.
The same fiber theorem bounds \(k_i\log\tau_i\) above and away from zero.
This gives \(R>1\) on a subsequence and \(l_i\log\tau_i\to\infty\).
The [phase descent theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v777.md#statement) bounds the original phase orders because the Salem field has a real embedding.
We may therefore fix both original phases, and hence the phases \(\zeta,\theta\) of the two characters.

The width in the new exponent direction is \(D l_i+O_F(k_i)\), and \(D=\deg U\) since the irreducible equation has \(V\)-degree \(D\) after its lowest monomial is removed.
The [individual section height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) gives
\[
         l_i h(\tau_i)=l_i\log(\tau_i)/d_i\longrightarrow m(F)/D,
         \qquad h(U(P_i))=k_i h(\tau_i)\longrightarrow0 .
\]

<a id="branches"></a>
## Translating the unique exterior embedding

Put \(L_0=\mathbb Q(\zeta,\theta)\), \(L_i=L_0\mathbb Q(\tau_i)\).
Restriction to \(L_0\) identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_i/\mathbb Q(\tau_i))\) with a subgroup of the fixed cyclotomic Galois group.
Pass to a subsequence with this subgroup equal to \(\Gamma\) and write \(f=|\Gamma|\).
For \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), the translate
\[
        h_\gamma=(\gamma\zeta/\zeta,\gamma\theta/\theta)
\]
sends \(P_i\) to its image under the corresponding embedding extending \(\tau_i\mapsto\tau_i\).
Both points belong to the original curve defined over \(\mathbb Q\) for infinitely many distinct \(P_i\), so irreducibility forces \(h_\gamma\in H\).
In fact a translation preserving the curve multiplies its Laurent equation by a scalar, since the finite support cannot be fixed by a nontrivial translation of exponent vectors.
Coefficient comparison proves the stated annihilator description of \(H\).
Each \(h_\gamma\) determines \(\gamma\) since \(\zeta,\theta\) generate \(L_0\), so \(f\le|H|\).
The elements \(\zeta^s,\theta^s\) are fixed by \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_i/\mathbb Q(\tau_i))\) and belong to the real field \(\mathbb Q(\tau_i)\), giving the asserted signs.

In the distinguished exterior embedding, compactness allows \(P_i\to Q_+\) on \(C(\mathbb C)\), with \(V(Q_+)=\infty\) and \(U(Q_+)=\zeta R\).
Every lift of the exterior embedding therefore tends to the corresponding \(h_\gamma Q_+\), using the extension of a curve automorphism to its smooth projective normalization.
For each \(i\) also choose one embedding of \(L_i\) extending \(\tau_i\mapsto\tau_i^{-1}\).
Its restriction \(\rho\) to \(L_0\) can be fixed on a subsequence.
The image of \(P_i\) is obtained by the same map at every index,
\[
         I(u,v)=((\rho\zeta)\zeta/u,\;(\rho\theta)\theta/v).
\]
The infinitely many pairs prove that \(I\) preserves the curve and extends to the normalization.
The chosen images tend to \(Q_-=I Q_+\), with \(U(Q_-)=(\rho\zeta)/R\).
All embeddings over \(\tau_i\mapsto\tau_i^{-1}\) are obtained by composing this chosen embedding with the \(f\) automorphisms over \(\mathbb Q(\tau_i)\).
Their limits are \(\rho(h_\gamma) Q_-\), where \(\rho(h_\gamma)\in H\) since \(H\) is Galois stable.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Summing the individual height contributions over a rational orbit

Every marked pole \(Q\) lies above a nonzero root of \(A_D\), and every such root occurs because the boundary intersection in the \(V=\infty\) chart has equation \(A_D(U)=0\) and the normalization is surjective.
The same statements hold for zeros and \(A_0\).
Thus no conjugate of \(U(Q)\) is on the unit circle.
In particular \(h(U(Q))>0\).
Choose disjoint sufficiently small neighborhoods of all marked points, taking complex conjugate neighborhoods in conjugate pairs and requiring \(|V|>1\) at the poles and \(|V|<1\) at the zeros.
Fix a finite Galois number field defining all marked points and sum the [individual boundary formula](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement) over \(Q\) in one rational Galois orbit \(\mathcal O\).
The neighborhood family for each \(Q\) uses at each embedding the chosen neighborhood of the image of \(Q\).
The union for \(\mathcal O\) is thus the same fixed union at every embedding of the base field.
Each embedding of \(L_i\) has the same number of extensions to the common compositum, so the normalized sum for this union can be computed already over \(L_i\).
Its limit is \(|\mathcal O|\,|\operatorname{ord}_Q V|\,h(U(Q))/D>0\).

On the pole side only the \(f\) lifts of the exterior embedding can visit the union, and each contributes exactly \(l_i\log\tau_i\) when visiting.
All their limits belong to the finite marked set, so for large \(i\) the count is exactly
\[
                 n_{\mathcal O}=|\{\gamma\in\Gamma:h_\gamma Q_+\in\mathcal O\}|.
\]
At the zeros the same argument counts \(\rho(h_\gamma)Q_-\) using lifts of the unique interior embedding and \(|\log|V||\).
Dividing by \([L_i:\mathbb Q]=fd_i\) and using the height limit gives
\[
           n_{\mathcal O}/f
              =|\mathcal O|\,|\operatorname{ord}_Q V|\,h(U(Q))/m(F).
\]
Every orbit is therefore visited, and their counts on each side sum to \(f\).
This proves the claimed transitivity under translations and conjugation, since the whole marked set on each side is stable under both actions.
Translations scale \(U,V\) by roots of unity, Galois conjugation preserves orders and height, and \(I\) inverts both coordinates up to roots of unity.
The orders and heights consequently agree on both sides.
The \(U\)-values at the visited poles have \(s\)-th power \(\zeta^sR^s\), and those at the visited zeros have \(s\)-th power \((\rho\zeta)^sR^{-s}=r^{-1}\).
This proves the statements for every edge root, including algebraicity of \(R\).
With trivial \(H\) both phases are signs, and untwisted phases are already real.
In both cases \(f=1\) and the limits of the real lifted points on the two sides are real.

For example a top polynomial with roots \(5,3+4i,3-4i\) fails the powered test for every \(s\), although all three moduli equal five.
A common minimal polynomial with \(5^s\) would give \(((3+4i)/5)^s=1\), which is impossible because this nonreal quadratic number has trace \(6/5\) and is not an algebraic integer.
This rules out that ray when these are among its top roots.
Other limiting rays of a given curve require their own tests.
