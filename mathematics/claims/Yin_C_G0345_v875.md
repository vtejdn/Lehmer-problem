# Exterior roots and individual factor heights as a rational pole approaches the circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## Location, height and the proportion of nonneutral conjugates

Let \(0<u<v\) be coprime, \(g\ge1\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(a,b\) be coprime nonzero integers.
Write \(B_0=b/a\), \(B=|B_0|=1+\delta>1\), \(H=|b|\), \(k=v/u\), \(W=u+v\), and
\[
 P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^W)+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v).
\]
Consider any sequence with \(\delta\to0^+\) such that each \(P(X^g)\) has a nontorsion algebraic-unit root \(z\).
Put \(\beta=z^g\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), and let \(J\) count the exterior conjugates of \(\beta\).
The number of exterior roots of the entire primitive polynomial \(P\), counted with multiplicity, is eventually exactly \(u\).
For every such exterior root \(x\), uniformly over the roots,
\[
 |x^u+B_0|\ll\delta e^{-ck\delta},\qquad
 u\log|x|=\log B+O(\delta e^{-ck\delta})
\]
with absolute constants, including \(c>0\).
Its total exterior logarithmic sum is \(\log B+O(\delta e^{-ck\delta})\).
Every individual primitive nontorsion unit factor is eventually reciprocal, with \(J\) interior conjugates, and satisfies
\[
 m(\beta)=Dh(\beta)=\frac Ju\log B\,(1+O(e^{-ck\delta})),
 \qquad v h(\beta)=\log H+o(1),
 \qquad \frac JD\sim\frac{u\log H}{v\log B}.
\]
Here the height error tends to zero in absolute value along every such sequence, for every choice of its unit factor.
The proportion off the unit circle is exactly \(2J/D\) eventually, hence asymptotic to \(2u\log H/(v\log B)\), and tends to zero.
The same proportion holds among the conjugates of any chosen lift \(z\), since the embeddings above each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) have equal multiplicity and taking powers preserves comparison of the modulus with one.
The coefficient difference \(|b|-|a|\) may vary arbitrarily.

<a id="pole"></a>
## Exterior localization and exact count

Coprimality and \(\delta=(|b|-|a|)/|a|\) imply \(H\to\infty\).
The [slope, primitive degree and measure estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement) give \(k\gg\log H/\delta^2\), \(u/D\ll\delta\), and \(Dh(\beta)\le3\delta\).
In particular \(k\delta\to\infty\) and \(u h(\beta)\ll\delta^2\).
For an exterior root, set \(T=x^u\), \(r=|T|>1\).
The [graph modulus identity](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#radius) gives
\[
 |T+B_0|^2=(B^2-1)\frac{r^2-1}{r^{2k}-1}\le (B^2-1)/k,
\]
since \(y^k-1\ge k(y-1)\) for \(y\ge1\) and real \(k\ge1\).
Thus \(|r-B|\le|T+B_0|=o(\delta)\) uniformly, since \(k\delta\to\infty\).
Eventually \(1+\delta/2\le r\le1+2\delta\), so \(r^2-1\ll\delta\) and \(r^{2k}-1\gg\exp(2ck\delta)\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
Substitution in the identity followed by taking square roots proves the location estimate and hence the estimate for \(\log r\).

On \(|T|=1\) the graph map \(R(T)=-\epsilon(1+B_0T)/(T+B_0)\) has degree one and its argument derivative with respect to \(\arg T\) equals \((B^2-1)/|T+B_0|^2\), by logarithmic differentiation.
This derivative is at most \((B+1)/(B-1)\), which is eventually smaller than \(k\).
The argument of \(x^v/R(x^u)\) on the unit circle therefore has strictly positive derivative and degree \(v-u\).
Its fiber over one has exactly \(v-u\) points, all simple zeros of \(P\), since the quotient has nonzero derivative there and the cleared factors are nonzero.
The remaining \(2u\) roots form interior-exterior pairs with multiplicities by reciprocity.
Summing the radial estimate over all \(u\) exterior roots gives the exterior sum.

Every rational factor has at most \(u\) conjugates on either open side of the circle, since the entire polynomial does.
For a nontorsion unit factor this leaves at least \(D-2u>0\) circle conjugates eventually.
Complex conjugation sends any such conjugate to its inverse, so the irreducible factor equals its reciprocal up to normalization, and has equally many interior and exterior conjugates.
Summing only its \(J\) positive logarithms gives the stated formula for \(m(\beta)\), since \(\log B\sim\delta\).
In particular the fraction off the circle is \(O(\delta)\).

<a id="height"></a>
## Absolute error in the individual height

Write \(\mathbb E\) for the normalized sum over the complex embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), let \(x\) be the corresponding conjugate, and put \(T=x^u\), \(Y=x^v=R(T)\).
Every exterior \(T\) has modulus \(1+O(\delta)\) by the location estimate, and interior values have reciprocal moduli of exterior values by reciprocity of \(P\).
Thus \(|T|=1+O(\delta)\) uniformly.
The nontorsion numbers \(\beta^u\) have heights \(u h(\beta)\to0\), and avoid every fixed finite subset eventually because each fixed nontorsion point has positive height.
Their conjugates equidistribute on the circle by [Bilu's theorem in dimension one](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures), also with the embedding weights used here, since every embedding of the power field extends equally often.
Pass to a subsequence with the sign \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(B_0)\) and \(\epsilon\) fixed, which suffices since there are only four choices.
On any closed arc away from \(-s\), and in a sufficiently thin neighborhood of that arc, \(R(T)\) tends uniformly to \(-\epsilon s\), since \(B_0\to s\) and \((1+sT)/(T+s)=s\).

The height of \(Y\) is the projective height of \([a+bT:aT+b]\) at the algebraic point \(T=\beta^u\), since the sign of their ratio does not change height.
Both coordinates are algebraic integers, so the sum of their finite-place log sup norms is nonpositive.
Subtracting \(\log H\) from the archimedean contribution leaves the mean of
\[
 F(T)=\log\max\left(|a+bT|/H,\ |aT+b|/H\right).
\]
Uniformly this is bounded above by \(\log(1+|T|)\).
For any fixed truncation level \(-M\), \(\max(F(T),-M)\) converges uniformly on a compact annulus around the circle to \(\max(\log|T+s|,-M)\), since the normalized absolute values converge uniformly there and the truncated logarithm is continuous even at zero.
Equidistribution and then \(M\to\infty\) give
\[
 \limsup (v h(\beta)-\log H)\le
 \int_0^{2\pi}\log|e^{i\theta}+s|\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=0,
\]
using the circle integral for a linear factor of unit modulus.

For the reverse inequality the section equation and coprimality put \(1+\epsilon\beta^W\) in \(b\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\).
It is nonzero because \(\beta\) is nontorsion, so its absolute norm is at least \(H^D\).
Define the archimedean correction
\[
 C(x)=\log|1+\epsilon T Y|-W\log^+|x|\le\log2.
\]
On the circle \(C(x)=\log|1+\epsilon TY|\), whose limit away from the exceptional direction is \(\log|1-sT|\), with the variable \(T\) on the circle.
For precision, truncate both logarithms from below at \(-M\).
Outside an arc of fixed radius around \(-s\) the truncated quantities for circle conjugates differ uniformly by \(o(1)\), including near a zero of \(1-sT\).
The off-circle conjugates have proportion \(O(\delta)\) and all truncated quantities lie between \(-M\) and a fixed upper bound on the annulus.
The exceptional arc has limiting mass equal to its normalized length, by equidistribution of \(T\) (use the radial projection for its argument).
Letting that length tend to zero gives the Haar integral of the truncated limiting function for the truncated mean.
Removing truncation in the upper bound and using the same linear-factor circle integral gives \(\limsup\mathbb E C(x)\le0\).
Since \(\beta\) is a unit, the normalized norm inequality reads \(\log H\le Wh(\beta)+\mathbb E C(x)\).
Together with \(uh(\beta)\to0\) and the upper bound this gives \(vh(\beta)-\log H\to0\).
Finally combine this estimate with \(Dh(\beta)=(J/u)\log B(1+O(e^{-ck\delta}))\) and \(\log H\to\infty\) to get the claimed individual degree proportion.
