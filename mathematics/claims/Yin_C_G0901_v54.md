# A finite sign criterion for Salem trace fields over all totally real numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Which positive Salem numbers belong to the compositum

Work in a fixed algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb C\), with its usual complex conjugation.
Let \(T=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{tr}}\) be the field of totally real algebraic numbers and let \(L/\mathbb Q\) be finite Galois with group \(G\).
Let \(c\in G\) be the restriction of complex conjugation, \(H\) the normal subgroup generated by its conjugates, \(S=L^H\), and \(P=G/H=\operatorname{Gal}(S/\mathbb Q)\).
Then \(S=L\cap T\).

For an embedded totally real number field \(B\) of degree \(n\ge2\), the following are equivalent.

1. The field \(LT\) contains a positive Salem number \(\alpha>1\) whose trace field in this embedding is \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
2. The inclusion \(B\subseteq S\) holds and there is a character \(\chi:H\to\{1,-1\}\) with
   \[
      \{gH\in P:\chi(g^{-1}cg)=1\}=\operatorname{Gal}(S/B).              \tag{1}
   \]
3. Every positive Salem number whose embedded trace field is \(B\) belongs to \(LT\).

Every such \(B\) occurs as an embedded Salem trace field in the algebraic closure, so the third condition has content for all the indicated \(B\).
For an eligible \(B\), infinitely many distinct Salem quadratic extensions of \(B\) at its given real embedding lie in \(LT\).
The character in (1) is unique when it exists.
There are only finitely many eligible \(B\) for fixed \(L\).
Putting \(b=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H/([H,H]H^2)\), any eligible \(B\) satisfies
\[
              n\le[S:\mathbb Q],\qquad
              \begin{cases} n\le b,&n\text{ even},\\ n-1\le b,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}                  \tag{2}
\]
In fact for the rational splitting field \(K_\alpha\) of any Salem number of degree \(2n\ge4\),
\[
                 [TK_\alpha:T]=
                  \begin{cases}2^n,&n\text{ even},\\2^{n-1},&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
                  \qquad i\in TK_\alpha\ \Longleftrightarrow\ n\text{ even}.             \tag{3}
\]

<a id="signs"></a>
## Square classes determined by real signs

An element \(x\in L\) is totally real precisely when \(c\) fixes \(g(x)\) for every \(g\in G\).
This is equivalent to \(H\) fixing \(x\), as in the [description of the maximal totally real subfield](Yin_C_G0901_v35.md#blocks).
Restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(LT/T)\) with \(H\).
Indeed \(LT/T\) is finite Galois, and the image of restriction to \(L\) has fixed field \(L\cap T=S\).

For \(a,d\in T^\times\), one has \(a/d\in T^{\times2}\) if and only if \(a,d\) have equal signs at every embedding of a common number field in \(T\) containing them.
A square of a nonzero totally real number is positive at every embedding.
Conversely if \(a/d\) is totally positive, every conjugate of a square root is real, since its square is a conjugate of \(a/d\).
This puts that square root in \(T\).
In particular two nonsquares define the same quadratic extension of \(T\) exactly when their signs agree at all such embeddings.

For any nontrivial \(\chi:H\to\{1,-1\}\), its finite quadratic field over \(S\) in \(L\) has the form \(S(\sqrt a)\) for some \(a\in S^\times\).
Here \(\chi(h)=h(\sqrt a)/\sqrt a\), and the corresponding field over \(T\) is \(T(\sqrt a)\) by the restriction isomorphism.
The sign of \(g(a)\), which is real since \(S\subset T\), is the sign in (1).
Indeed \(c(g\sqrt a)/(g\sqrt a)=\chi(g^{-1}cg)\), and the square root of the real nonzero number \(g(a)\) is real or purely imaginary according to its sign.
This value is independent of the lift of \(gH\), both because \(g(a)\) depends only on \(g|_S\) and because a character is invariant under inner conjugation in \(H\).

<a id="criterion"></a>
## Recovering the embedded marked field

Suppose that \(\alpha>1\) as in condition 1 belongs to \(LT\).
With \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) and \(d=t^2-4\), the unique embedding of \(B\) where \(d>0\) is its given inclusion, since \(t>2\) whereas its other conjugates lie in \((-2,2)\).
As \(n\ge2\), \(d\) has a negative conjugate and is a nonsquare in \(T\).
The equation for \(\alpha\) gives \(T(\alpha)=T(\sqrt d)\).
This quadratic subfield of \(LT/T\) corresponds to a nontrivial \(\chi\) and to \(T(\sqrt a)\) as above.
The two sign patterns coincide by the square-class criterion.

More explicitly, in the absolute Galois group of \(\mathbb Q\), the positive locus of \(d\) is the subgroup fixing \(B\) pointwise.
Since \(a\in S\) and \(a>0\) at the identity, the positive locus of \(a\) contains the subgroup fixing \(S\) pointwise.
It follows that \(B\subseteq S\).
Restricting the equality of the sign patterns to the finite quotient \(P\) gives exactly (1).

Conversely assume condition 2.
The specified subgroup is proper since \(n\ge2\), so \(\chi\) is nontrivial.
Choose \(d\in B\) positive in the given embedding and negative in every other embedding.
Such elements exist by weak approximation, and the [construction over a marked totally real field](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#extensions) gives a Salem generator in \(B(\sqrt d)\) at that place by the rank-one relative unit group.
For the existence assertion one can take the \(d\) constructed there, including its optional finite valuation.
For any \(d\) with these signs, (1) and the equality of real sign patterns imply \(T(\sqrt d)=T(\sqrt a)\subset LT\).
In particular, for every positive Salem number with embedded trace field \(B\), its own radicand \((\alpha+\alpha^{-1})^2-4\) has these signs and the number itself belongs to that quadratic field.
This proves condition 3, and existence over \(B\) gives the implication from 3 to 1.

<a id="rank"></a>
## Finite linear algebra and the exact splitting degree

In the elementary abelian quotient \(V=H/([H,H]H^2)\) put \(v(gH)=[g^{-1}cg]\).
The vector is well-defined because conjugation by \(H\) acts trivially on \(V\), and these vectors span \(V\) since \(H\) is generated by conjugates of \(c\).
Thus (1) is a finite linear system asking for a linear form taking value zero on the specified subgroup and one on its complement, evaluated at the listed vectors.
Any solution is unique.
The functions on \(P\) obtained from linear forms on \(V\) form a vector space of dimension \(b\), stable under right translations on \(P\) by conjugating the form.
If \(B\) is eligible, this space contains the indicators of the complements of each of the \(n\) right cosets of \(\operatorname{Gal}(S/B)\).
Viewed on these \(n\) disjoint cosets, the vectors have a zero at their own coset and ones elsewhere.
A sum indexed by a subset \(J\) has entry \(|J|-\mathbf1_{\{j\in J\}}\bmod2\) at coset \(j\).
Only the empty sum vanishes when \(n\) is even, while the full sum is the sole additional relation when \(n\) is odd.
Their dimensions are therefore as in (2).

For (3) let \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\) be the distinct trace conjugates of \(\alpha\), and \(d_j=t_j^2-4\).
All \(t_j\) belong to \(T\), and \(TK_\alpha\) is obtained from \(T\) by adjoining their square roots \(\sqrt{d_j}\), since the roots of the Salem minimum are \((t_j\pm\sqrt{d_j})/2\).
Under any embedding of the trace splitting field exactly one of the \(d_j\) is positive and all others are negative.
Transitivity realizes all \(n\) positions for this positive entry.
By the square-class criterion, a product of a subset of the \(d_j\) is a square in \(T\) exactly when its binary sign vector is zero.
These are the same \(n\) vectors just counted, so their square classes span a space of dimension \(n\) or \(n-1\) according to parity.
The elementary multiquadratic degree formula gives (3)'s degree.
In that formula the quadratic subextensions correspond to the nonzero classes in this span, by changing signs independently on a basis of square roots.
The square class of \(-1\) has all entries negative.
A subset gives all negative signs exactly when it is the full subset and \(n\) is even, by the same entry formula.
This proves the assertion about \(i\).
The quadratic field \(T(\alpha)\), and consequently the compositum of all its rational conjugates \(TK_\alpha\), depend here only on the embedded marked trace field \(B\).

## Consequences for Salem detection over varying finite extensions

For a fixed finite Galois \(L\), positive Salem numbers in \(LT\) have degree at most \(2[S:\mathbb Q]\), and there are finitely many at any bounded total measure by the coefficient bounds for their monic minima.
If the set is nonempty its positive logarithmic measures have a positive minimum.
For a unit \(w\in LT\), nonzero projection onto a conjugate of a primitive Salem number \(\alpha\) in the [logarithmic inner product](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement) implies \(K_\alpha\) lies in the splitting field of \(w\) by the [norm criterion for membership](Yin_C_G0901_v53.md#algebra).
The latter splitting field lies in \(LT\), since both \(L\) and \(T\) are normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Thus every detected \(\alpha\) has one of the marked trace fields in (1).

More generally let \(w_j\in L_jT\) be nontorsion units with positive total logarithmic measures tending to zero, with finite Galois \(L_j/\mathbb Q\), and define \(H_j\) as above.
If infinitely many \(w_j\) have nonzero Salem projections, the [small-measure projection bound](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement) supplies along those indices primitive \(\alpha_j\) with \(0<\log\alpha_j\le m(w_j)\) eventually.
Their trace degrees \(n_j\) tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Equations (2) and (3) require
\[
     \dim_{\mathbb F_2} H_j/([H_j,H_j]H_j^2)\ge n_j-1,\qquad
                           [L_jT:T]\ge2^{n_j-1}.
\]
In particular bounded dimension of these quotients along a sequence forces eventual orthogonality to every primitive Salem conjugate.
This is a conditional restriction on shrinking sequences.

One may replace \(L\) throughout by \(L(i)\) to include all CM fields as constants after compositing with \(T\).
A CM field is quadratic over its totally real subfield and can be written using the square root of a totally negative element, so it lies in \(T(i)\) by the square-class criterion.
Finite abelian fields are totally real or CM, since complex conjugation is central and its fixed field is totally real.
The sole quadratic subextension of \(T(i)\) over \(T\) has the everywhere negative square class.
Since a Salem radicand has both signs, \(T(i)\) contains no positive Salem number of degree at least four.
