# Sparse rational substitutions and strict descent of Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree cost for strict descent and a height floor for arbitrary outputs

Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be a nontorsion algebraic number of degree \(d\), and let \(\beta=R(\alpha)\) be defined and nonzero for a nonconstant reduced rational function \(R=N/D\in\mathbb Q(X)\) of degree \(e\).
Assume \(R(X)\ne\pm X^j\) identically for every \(j\in\mathbb Z\).
Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_s\) be the nonzero rational coefficients of the distinct monomials of \(N(X)-YD(X)\).
Put
\[
     \rho=\frac{(\sum_i|c_i|)^2}{\sum_i|c_i|^2}\le s,\qquad
     H=e h(\alpha)+h(\beta),
\]
where \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Write \(L_x=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]h(x)\) for the total logarithmic Mahler measure of \(x\).
Consider any sequence of strict positive descents \(0<L_\beta<L_\alpha\to0\).
For all sufficiently large indices, the degree and the coefficients satisfy the following bounds.

* For \(\rho\le s_0\), the degree satisfies \(e\ge c_{s_0}d/L_\alpha\).
  In particular, a bounded number of graph monomials excludes a strict descent by rational substitutions of degree \(o(d/L_\alpha)\).
* For \(e\le Kd\) with fixed \(K\), the effective number of graph coefficients satisfies
  \[
       \rho\gg_K \frac{L_\alpha^{-1/2}}{(1+\log(1/L_\alpha))^2}.
  \]

These conclusions allow arbitrary degrees for the output and require no minimality of the input among algebraic numbers of its degree.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that, whenever \(d>2e\),
\[
        e h(\alpha)+h(\beta)+\frac ed\log\frac{3d}{e}
                                  \ \ge\ \frac{c}{\rho^2}.            \tag{1}
\]
The input and output may have any signature, and nonintegral coordinates are allowed.
The constants are independent of the coefficients, exponents and fields.

In particular, for bounded \(\rho\le s_0\) and input total logarithmic Mahler measure \(L_\alpha\le B\), every sequence with \(e=o(d)\) has \(\liminf h(\beta)\ge c/s_0^2\).
If \(h(\beta)\le c/(2s_0^2)\), the degree satisfies \(e\ge c_{B,s_0}d\).
If instead both total logarithmic Mahler measures are at most \(B\), then for some \(c_B>0\),
\[
                        e\ge \frac{c_B d}{\rho^2\log(2\rho)}.        \tag{2}
\]
The [unrestricted degree bound for bounded total measures](Yin_C_G0345_v77.md#statement) gives its own nearly linear comparison independently of sparsity.
Here (1) controls absolute output height even when total output measure is unbounded, and (2) gives a linear degree bound at bounded effective support.
The [fixed-function height floor](Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#fixed-rational-function-height-gap) allows dependence on the whole rational function.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## A phase test for a sparse Laurent relation on units

The coordinator proposed using the phase Gram matrix of the graph equation and detecting torsion among ratios of its terms.
We give the general two-variable test before using the two rows of a graph.
Let \(\alpha,\beta\) now be algebraic units and suppose a nonzero rational Laurent polynomial with distinct exponent pairs \((u_i,v_i)\) and coefficients \(c_i\ne0\) vanishes at \((\alpha,\beta)\).
Use \(s,\rho\) as above, let \(E,F\) be the respective coordinate spans of the pairs, and put \(H=E h(\alpha)+F h(\beta)\).
Suppose \(0<H\le h_0\), and use the increasing function \(\varepsilon\) on that interval from the [first phase estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0229_v1.md#uniform-powered-angular-coherence).
Here \(h_0=(\log 2)e^{-20}/4\), and \(\varepsilon(H)\ll H^{1/4}\sqrt{1+\log(1/H)}\).
For \(w_i=\alpha^{u_i}\beta^{v_i}\), if
\[
                    (\rho-1)\varepsilon(H)+2\rho H\le\tfrac12,       \tag{3}
\]
then some quotient \(w_i/w_j\), \(i\ne j\), is a root of unity of order \(q\) satisfying \(\varphi(q)\le2(\rho-1)\).
In particular \(q\le 8(\rho-1)^2\), since \(q\le2\varphi(q)^2\) by comparing prime-power factors.
For an odd prime power \(p^a\), the factor \(p^{a-2}(p-1)^2\) of \(\varphi(q)^2/q\) is at least one, while a power of two contributes at least \(1/2\).
For a graph equation on units with \(\alpha\) nontorsion, strict inequality in (3), using \(H=e h(\alpha)+h(\beta)\), forces \(\beta=\pm\alpha^j\) for some \(|j|\le e\).
In that case \(R\) is a signed monomial identically or \(d\le2e\).

To prove the test, average with equal weights over all complex embeddings \(\sigma\) of the joint field.
Write \(\chi_i=\sigma w_i/|\sigma w_i|\) and divide the vanishing relation at this embedding by \(M_\sigma=\max_i|\sigma w_i|\).
The logarithms of its term moduli lie in an interval of length at most \(T_\sigma=E|\log|\sigma\alpha||+F|\log|\sigma\beta||\).
It follows that \(|1-|\sigma w_i|/M_\sigma|\le\min(1,T_\sigma)\).
For any nonzero algebraic coordinates the means of the two absolute radial logarithms are at most \(2h(\alpha)\) and \(2h(\beta)\), since \(h(x^{-1})=h(x)\) and each archimedean positive part is included in the [height formula](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization).
Thus the squared radial error obeys
\[
       \mathbb E_\sigma\left|\sum_i c_i\chi_i\right|^2
           \le \|c\|_1^2\,\mathbb E_\sigma\min(1,T_\sigma)^2
           \le 2H\|c\|_1^2.                                         \tag{4}
\]
Every pair ratio has height at most \(H\) by subadditivity and power homogeneity.
Its phase average over the joint field equals its own conjugate phase average, since each embedding of its generated field occurs with equal multiplicity.
For a nontorsion ratio of units, the first phase estimate at frequency one bounds this modulus by \(\varepsilon(H)\), as used in the [one-variable Gram comparison](Yin_C_G0092_v1.md#angular-short-annihilator-effective-support).
For a torsion ratio of order \(q_{ij}\), the mean is exactly \(\mu(q_{ij})/\varphi(q_{ij})\).
Indeed the sum of the primitive \(q\)-th roots is \(\mu(q)\) by divisor inversion of the sum over all \(q\)-th roots.
Expanding the square, retaining its diagonal and allowing either sign on every off-diagonal contribution yields
\[
  1\le 2\rho H+(\rho-1)\varepsilon(H)
     +\frac{1}{\|c\|_2^2}\!
        \sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\w_i/w_j\ {\rm torsion}}}
              \frac{|c_i c_j|\,|\mu(q_{ij})|}{\varphi(q_{ij})}.       \tag{5}
\]
The sum of all ordered off-diagonal coefficient weights is \((\rho-1)\|c\|_2^2\).
Hence (3) forces a torsion order with the stated totient bound.

<a id="matching"></a>
## Torsion pairs form a matching in a rational graph

The two rows of the graph monomials are \(\alpha^u\) and \(\beta\alpha^v\).
Quotients on a single row at distinct indices are nontorsion because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
If any cross-row quotient is torsion, write \(\beta=\zeta\alpha^j\), \(j=u-v\).
A second torsion difference must give the same \(j\), since their difference would give a torsion power of \(\alpha\).
For each \(u\) there is then at most one matched \(v\) and conversely.
Every ordered ratio on the matching is \(\zeta\) or its inverse.
Writing \(q=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\) and using \(2|c_i c_j|\le |c_i|^2+|c_j|^2\) on disjoint edges bounds the entire last term of (5) by \(1/\varphi(q)\).
The same statement holds for the phase expansion for nonunits, irrespective of which bound is used on the nontorsion correlations.
Thus under strict (3) there must be a matching with \(1/\varphi(q)>1/2\), which forces \(q=1\) or \(2\).
If \(\beta=\pm\alpha^j\), the equation \(N(X)\mp X^jD(X)=0\) at \(\alpha\), after clearing a possible negative power, gives a polynomial of degree at most \(e+|j|\le2e\).
It vanishes identically exactly when \(R(X)=\pm X^j\) with the chosen sign.
Consequently if \(R\) is not a signed monomial and \(d>2e\), the torsion part of the normalized Gram expansion has absolute value at most \(1/2\).

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## A first phase estimate that uses the degree of a ratio

Let \(\gamma\ne0\) have degree \(n\), primitive integer minimal polynomial with leading coefficient \(a>0\), and conjugates \(\gamma_l=e^{t_l+i\theta_l}\).
There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that
\[
     \left|\frac1n\sum_l e^{i\theta_l}\right|
          \le C\sqrt{h(\gamma)+\frac{\log(3n)}n}+2h(\gamma).         \tag{6}
\]
To check this for arbitrary leading and constant coefficients, set \(v_l=e^{-|t_l|+i\theta_l}\) and \(r=e^{-1/n}\).
The [disk reflection and logarithmic expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) use
\[
 |\gamma_i-\gamma_j|\le e^{t_i^++t_j^+}|1-\overline v_i v_j|,
       \qquad |1-z|\le r^{-1}|1-r^2z|\quad (|z|\le1).
\]
The nonzero integer discriminant gives
\(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|\gamma_i-\gamma_j|\ge-(2n-2)\log a\).
This extra term in the energy upper bound combines with \(2(n-1)\sum_l t_l^+\) to give exactly \(2(n-1)n h(\gamma)\) by the primitive height formula.
Including the diagonal terms and expanding as in the cited disk estimate gives
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{r^{2k}}k\left|\sum_l v_l^k\right|^2
   \le 2(n-1)n h(\gamma)+n(n-1)\log(1/r)+n\log(1/(1-r^2)).
\]
The \(k=1\) term therefore gives the square-root contribution in (6) after division by \(n\).
Replacing \(v_l\) with its phase costs at most \(\sum_l|t_l|/n\le2h(\gamma)\).

For a nontorsion algebraic unit, (6) also gives a height-only bound
\[
      \left|\mathbb E\,\operatorname{ph}(\gamma)\right|
          \ll \sqrt u\,(1+\log(1/u))^2,\qquad u=h(\gamma)\to0,
          \qquad \operatorname{ph}(z)=z/|z|.                         \tag{7}
\]
Indeed [Dobrowolski's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(nu\gg 1/(\log(3n))^3\) for large \(n\).
The same lower bound with a smaller absolute constant covers the remaining degrees, because bounded degree and bounded total measure give finitely many primitive minimal polynomials, and a nontorsion unit has positive measure.
If \(n<1/u\), the bound implies \(n\gg 1/(u(1+\log(1/u))^3)\), and \(n\ge1/u\) also implies this conclusion.
Monotonicity of \(\log(3n)/n\) now gives \(\log(3n)/n\ll u(1+\log(1/u))^4\), proving (7).
Its right side is increasing in \(u\) for all sufficiently small \(u>0\).
Consequently (5) holds on sufficiently small positive \(H\) also with \(C\sqrt H(1+\log(1/H))^2\) in place of \(\varepsilon(H)\) for an absolute \(C\).

<a id="descent"></a>
## Strictly smaller positive measure rules out every signed power

Suppose \(0<L_\beta<L_\alpha<\log2\).
The leading and constant terms of each primitive minimal polynomial have absolute value at most its multiplicative Mahler measure, so both coordinates are algebraic units.
For \(j\ne0\), \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)]\le |j|\) by the defining power equation, also for negative \(j\) by inversion.
Thus \(L_{\pm\alpha^j}=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^j):\mathbb Q]\,|j|h(\alpha)\ge L_\alpha\).
The remaining signed powers \(j=0\) have zero measure.
Hence no signed-power value is a strict positive descent, irrespective of \(e/d\).
The torsion matching therefore contributes at most \(1/2\) in (5).

For every fixed \(s_0\), choose \(\delta_{s_0}>0\) small enough that \((s_0-1)\varepsilon(H)+2s_0H<1/2\) for \(0<H\le\delta_{s_0}\le h_0\).
Under \(\rho\le s_0\), a strict descent must have \(H>\delta_{s_0}\), since otherwise the graph conclusion of (3) would give a signed-power value.
As \(h(\beta)\le L_\beta<L_\alpha\), once \(L_\alpha<\delta_{s_0}/2\) this forces \(eL_\alpha/d>\delta_{s_0}/2\).
This proves the first strict-descent assertion, including when \(e\) exceeds the input degree.

For \(e\le Kd\), one has \(H<(K+1)L_\alpha\).
For small \(L_\alpha\), (5) and the matching bound give \(1/2\le(\rho-1)\varepsilon(H)+2\rho H\).
The first phase estimate already gives \(\rho\gg_K L_\alpha^{-1/4}/\sqrt{1+\log(1/L_\alpha)}\).
Using (7) in the same inequality gives the stronger second assertion of the statement.
This concerns the coefficients of the actual reduced graph equation and is uniform over all rational coefficient heights and all choices of numerator and denominator supports.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree and height costs of the graph ratios

Assume \(d>2e\) and put \(U=d/(2e)>1\) and \(\ell=(e/d)\log(3d/e)\).
Every ratio of distinct graph monomials is the value at \(\alpha\) of \(S(X)=X^jR(X)^\delta\) with \(|j|\le e\) and \(\delta\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
Its reduced rational-function degree is at most \(2e\), by multiplying rational functions of degrees at most \(|j|\) and \(e\).
If \(S\) is constant and its value is nontorsion, it is a nonzero rational other than \(\pm1\), so \(H\ge h(S(\alpha))\ge\log2\), which gives (1) after adjusting the absolute constant.
Otherwise each nontorsion ratio \(\gamma\) arises from a nonconstant \(S=A_1/B_1\).
The nonzero polynomial \(A_1-\gamma B_1\) over \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) has \(\alpha\) as a root, hence \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\le2e\).
Thus \(n=\deg\gamma\ge U\), and \(\log(3n)/n\le\log(3U)/U\le2\ell\) by monotonicity on \([1,\infty)\).
Equation (6) and \(h(\gamma)\le H\) now bound every nontorsion phase correlation by \(C'\sqrt{H+\ell}+2H\).

The radial bound (4) is already valid for nonunits.
Expanding the phase Gram matrix with this new estimate and the matching bound gives
\[
       \tfrac12\le 2\rho H+(\rho-1)\bigl(C'\sqrt{H+\ell}+2H\bigr).
\]
For \(H+\ell\le1\), the right side is at most \((4+C')\rho\sqrt{H+\ell}\).
For \(H+\ell>1\), the claimed lower bound follows from \(\rho\ge1\).
This proves (1).

For the absolute height consequences, use \(e h(\alpha)\le Be/d\) and \(t\log(3/t)\to0\) as \(t=e/d\to0\).
The same observation gives a positive \(c_{B,s_0}\) whenever \(h(\beta)\le c/(2s_0^2)\), and the case \(d\le2e\) already gives \(e/d\ge1/2\).
For (2), applying the degree argument also to \(R\) itself gives \(\deg\beta\ge d/e\), including torsion \(\beta\).
Hence \(H\le 2Bt\) if both total measures are at most \(B\).
For \(t<1/2\), (1) gives \(t(2B+\log(3/t))\ge c/\rho^2\).
This left side increases with \(t\) there, and substituting \(t=c_B/(\rho^2\log(2\rho))\) bounds it above by \(O_B(c_B(1+|\log c_B|))/\rho^2\), uniformly for \(\rho\ge1\).
Choosing \(c_B\) small gives (2), also when \(t\ge1/2\) by further decreasing \(c_B\).
These inequalities concern the degree and support of actual rational substitutions and do not give a uniform lower bound for the total measure of every input.
