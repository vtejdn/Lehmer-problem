3. **The conclusion is stronger than a rational-field ceiling in its stated domain.**
   C103 already forces rational original-width Grams to be trivial even with no support restriction.

   The result determines a necessary cost of such certificates, not their nonexistence.
   C103's field nonattainment and cofinal construction retain their separate roles.

4. **The new support result is not C113's Sidon criterion.**
   Arbitrary additive collisions in the support are allowed.
   Actual circle roots force the residual quadratic factorization, and original irreducibility prices the coefficient relations.
   The source of rigidity is different from independent phase assignments in the collision-free model.
   If \(Z\) is empty, no support obstruction follows from A--C, though the exact outer optimizer still has full support by D.

5. **A support-count consequence has a limited meaning.**
   If the original support has \(r(P)\) elements, a nontrivial feasible Gram has positive diagonal at its \(d+1-r(P)\) zero positions and at both endpoints, hence at least \(d+3-r(P)\) positions when this observation is informative.
   Positivity at every original NONzero interior position is not asserted for arbitrary nonoptimal Grams.
   Only the unique exact optimizer is proved to activate all \(d+1\) positions.
   A large number of active coordinates does not give a lower bound on Gram rank or a large objective gain.

6. **Arithmetic constants are fully paid and can be very small.**
   Equations(10)--(11) involve original degree and height, not an auxiliary normalized degree or an unpriced coefficient field.
   They do not bound the denominator of a trace-field construction or assert a uniform lower limit of \(\beta_j,D_h\).
   The elementary norm floor is reused to price a newly identified coordinate requirement, not promoted to a new unrestricted norm-gap principle.

7. **The actual near-one question remains.**
   Along a hypothetical Salem sequence \(\lambda\downarrow1\), any available objective gain can itself tend to zero.
   Neither the required activation nor(9) yields a degree-uniform positive \(\gamma\); the original coefficient-energy budget can grow.
   The work supplies a new necessary structure for original-input primal constructions and an explicit obstruction to support-only simplification.
   It does not prove a uniform Mahler gap, supply every unrestricted input by Salem reduction, construct a no-loss density estimate, or establish a positive certificate beyond the already known input-dependent C103 family.

The useful next discriminator is whether a candidate original-input construction can actually pay simultaneous activation of its missing coefficients while controlling signed correlations or a correction symbol.
Simply choosing the known outer optimizer, restricting the dual to integers, or asking for more numerical precision does not address that bridge.
