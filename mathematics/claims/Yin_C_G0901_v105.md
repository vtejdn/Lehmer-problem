# Independent signatures and simultaneous prime-ideal costs in Salem trace fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two sets of independent signatures in the original trace field

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number, \(d=2n=\deg\alpha\), \(m=\log\alpha\le1\), \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) and \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\subset\mathbb R\).
For \(y\ne0\) in the totally real field \(F\), let its signature be its \(n\) signs, written as a vector over \(\mathbb F_2\) with a negative sign represented by one.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the conclusions below hold with
\[
          K=\left\lfloor c\left(\frac{n}{\log(2n)}\right)^{1/4}\right\rfloor,
               \qquad \mathcal I=\{K+1,\ldots,2K\}.
\]
For \(r\ge3\) let \(\Psi_r\) be the monic polynomial of \(2\cos(2\pi/r)\), so \(\Phi_r(X)=X^{\varphi(r)/2}\Psi_r(X+X^{-1})\).
Each of the following two sets of \(K\) algebraic integers has linearly independent signatures in \(F\):
\[
           y_r=\Psi_r(t)\quad(r\in\mathcal I),\qquad
           z_r=\alpha^r+\alpha^{-r}\quad(r\in\mathcal I).
\]
The ideals \((y_r)\) are pairwise coprime.
Every \(z_r\) generates \(F\), and its absolute logarithmic Weil height satisfies, uniformly for the indicated \(r\) and \(m\le1\),
\[
            h(z_r)=H_*+o(1),\qquad
            H_*=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\cos x)\,dx.                  \tag{1}
\]
For every integer \(T\ge16K\) set
\[
             B_T=n\frac{\log T}{T-1}+\frac{T+1}{6}m.
\]
The two norm budgets are
\[
       \sum_{r\in\mathcal I}\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}y_r|\le8K B_T,\qquad
       \sum_{r\in\mathcal I}\log|N_{F/\mathbb Q}z_r|\le32K B_T.          \tag{2}
\]
Neither the field nor its real signature classes are fixed in these bounds.

Let \(s(F)\) be the dimension of the signatures of \(\mathcal O_F^\times\), and let \(\pi_F(Y)\) count finite prime ideals of absolute norm at most \(Y\).
For every \(Y>1\),
\[
                 s(F)+\pi_F(Y)\ \ge\ K-\frac{8K B_T}{\log Y}.              \tag{3}
\]
In the second set at least \(K-32K B_T/\log Y\) independent signatures are individually represented by integral generators of \(F\) satisfying (1), with all prime ideal divisors of their principal ideals of norm at most \(Y\).
For every fixed \(\gamma>0\), these two lower bounds are \((1-o(1))K\) along any sequence \(m\to0\) for \(Y=\exp(\gamma\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\), with a suitable \(T\).
In particular, fix \(\gamma,\delta>0\).
There is a positive uniform Salem gap on any family of totally real trace fields satisfying
\[
      s(F)+\pi_F\big(\exp(\gamma\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\big)
                           \ \le\ (1-\delta)K                              \tag{4}
\]
for all sufficiently large degrees in the family.
The same gap implication holds if the left side is replaced by the signature rank of the integral generators supported on those small primes and of height at most \(H_*+\delta\).
Support here refers to prime divisors of the principal ideal.
Both criteria allow either parity of \(n\).

<a id="signs"></a>
## A triangular matrix at the first cyclotomic trace roots

Use the phases of the \(2n\) actual conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Only the two positive real conjugates have phase zero.
For bounded \(m\) the [uniform short-arc estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) ensures that each arc of half-width at least \(C\sqrt{\log(2n)/n}\) and at most one contains a conjugate phase, for an absolute \(C\) when \(m\le1\).
For every \(r\in\mathcal I\) choose a neutral phase \(\theta_r\) in \((2\pi/r,2\pi/(r-1))\).
All these intervals are in \((0,\pi)\) for large \(n\), and their lengths are at least \(\pi/(2K^2)\), so taking \(c\) small enough puts an arc of the required width strictly inside each.
The chosen phases lie at distinct trace embeddings.
As \(\theta\) increases from zero, \(\Psi_j(2\cos\theta)\) is positive until its first simple zero at \(2\pi/j\).
Its next zero in \((0,\pi)\), if present, has phase at least \(4\pi/j\).
Thus the row at \(\theta_r\) has positive signs for all \(j<r\) in \(\mathcal I\) and a negative sign at \(j=r\).
This gives an invertible triangular signature minor.
For the second set take phases in \((\pi/(2r),\pi/(2(r-1)))\), whose lengths are at least \(\pi/(8K^2)\).
After decreasing the same \(c\), a neutral phase exists in each.
The signs of \(2\cos(j\theta)\) before and just after its first zero give a second triangular minor by the same argument.
All conjugate evaluations of both sets are nonzero since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.

Suppose a prime ideal of residue characteristic \(p\) divides two of the \(y_r\), and lift it to \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The residue of the unit \(\alpha\) is then a common root of the two reduced cyclotomic polynomials.
Writing \(r=p^a r_0\) with \(p\nmid r_0\), each root of the reduction of \(\Phi_r\) has exact order \(r_0\), by separability of \(X^{r_0}-1\) and the identity \(\Phi_{p^a r_0}(X)=\Phi_{r_0}(X^{p^a})/\Phi_{r_0}(X^{p^{a-1}})\) for \(a\ge1\).
Consequently distinct such indices would have integer ratio a power of \(p\) when ordered largest first.
This contradicts \(1<r'/r<2\) in \(\mathcal I\), proving coprimality even at ramified primes.

<a id="generators"></a>
## Fields and heights of the power traces

An automorphism fixing \(z_r\) must take \(\alpha^r\) to one of the two roots \(\alpha^{\pm r}\) of its reciprocal quadratic over \(\mathbb Q(z_r)\).
The only conjugates of \(\alpha\) having the corresponding two moduli are \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\).
Thus it fixes \(t\), proving \(\mathbb Q(z_r)=F\) because \(z_r\in\mathbb Z[t]\).

Let \(\nu_d\) be the normalized phase measure on all conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For \(1\le h\le d\), the same [reflected discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives
\[
   \left|\int e^{ih\theta}\,d\nu_d(\theta)\right|
           \ll\sqrt{\frac{h\log(2d)}{d}}+\frac{h}{d}.
\]
Indeed its sum of squared reflected moments with weights \(e^{-2h/d}/h\) is \(O(d\log(2d))\) before normalization, and the cost of replacing a reflected sum by a phase sum is at most \(2hm\).
For each fixed nonzero integer \(l\), this bound tends to zero uniformly at \(|l|r\), \(r\in\mathcal I\).
Uniform approximation of the continuous periodic function \(f(\theta)=\log^+|2\cos\theta|\), extended by zero at the zeros of cosine, by trigonometric polynomials now gives
\[
                 \int f(r\theta)\,d\nu_d(\theta)
                    =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\,d\theta+o(1)
                    =H_*+o(1)
\]
uniformly.
The \(n-1\) interior trace embeddings have their correct weights because their two conjugate phases have the same \(f\)-value.
At the distinguished embedding one replaces \(\log2\) by \(\log(2\cosh(rm))\), with cost at most \(rm/n\) in the mean.
All \(z_r\) are integral, so this corrected mean is their absolute height, proving (1).

<a id="ideals"></a>
## Simultaneous norm budget and the trace-field criterion

For every \(a\ge1\) write \(L_a=(2n)^{-1}\log|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}\Phi_a(\alpha)|\ge0\).
At the unit \(\alpha\) this is the finite-place cyclotomic quantity of the [consecutive-power norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement).
There \(h(\alpha)=m/(2n)\), and its positive weights give
\[
           \sum_{a\le T/2} \frac{L_a}{a}
                       \le4\left(\frac{\log T}{T-1}+\frac{T+1}{6n}m\right).
\]
The weight bound follows by keeping the first \(\lfloor T/(2a)\rfloor\) terms of \(E_a(T)\). [Weights](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#auxiliary)
The norm identity for the cyclotomic trace polynomial gives \(\log|N_F y_r|=nL_r\), since the unit power has absolute norm one and the extension to \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) is quadratic.
As \(r\le2K\), this gives the first bound in (2).
Likewise \(z_r=\alpha^{-r}(1+\alpha^{2r})\), and the cyclotomic indices \(a\) in \(1+X^{2r}\) divide \(4r\) and do not divide \(2r\).
Each such index is at most \(8K\), and for a fixed \(a\) there are at most \(2K\gcd(4,a)/a\le8K/a\) possible multiples \(r\) of \(a/\gcd(4,a)\) up to \(2K\).
Applying the same bound to their sum proves the second inequality of (2).

Among the first \(K\) integers \(y_r\) at most \(s(F)\) are units by independence of their signatures.
Each remaining principal ideal has a prime divisor, and the choices for distinct \(r\) are distinct.
The number of these choices with norm greater than \(Y\) is at most \(8K B_T/\log Y\) by (2), proving (3).
Applied to the \(z_r\), (2) bounds the number having even one prime divisor of norm greater than \(Y\) by \(32K B_T/\log Y\), yielding the stated small-height generator bound.

On a positive shrinking-measure sequence the degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
Taking \(T=\lceil\sqrt{n\log(2n/m)/m}\rceil\) eventually ensures \(T\ge16K\) and \(B_T\ll\sqrt{nm\log(2n/m)}=o(\sqrt{n\log(2n)})\).
This proves both asymptotic rank bounds and rules out a shrinking sequence under either field criterion, which gives the positive gap.
The [endpoint norm condition](Yin_C_G0901_v45.md#statement) and [unramified Artin sign test](Yin_C_G0901_v60.md#statement) constrain a single prescribed signature or ideal class.
Here the separated cyclotomic conductors yield many independent signatures and disjoint prime ideal divisors for the nonunit values.

As a boundary example take odd primes \(p\to\infty\), \(t=\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\) and \(n=(p-1)/2\).
The same triangular signature minor exists for \(K\) as above, since the grid of positive neutral phases has spacing \(2\pi/p\).
Every \(y_r\) is a unit: a prime divisor would force the prime-to-residue-characteristic parts of \(p\) and \(r\) to coincide, impossible for \(1<r<p\) except if \(p/r\) or \(r/p\) were a prime power integer.
Thus the independent signatures can all be supplied by units at the cyclotomic boundary.
The estimates impose the field criteria and leave open the Salem gap for arbitrary varying trace fields.
