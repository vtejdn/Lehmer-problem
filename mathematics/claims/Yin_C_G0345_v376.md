# A constant row for a repeated unit and the index after powering

<a id="statement"></a>
## An upper bound for the full row resultant

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\).
Fix a prime \(p\) for which \(\beta=\alpha^p\) has degree \(d\), and write \(Q\) for its monic minimal polynomial.
Put \(O=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(O'=\mathbb Z[\beta]\), \(I=[O:O']\) and let \(s\) be the exponent of the finite additive group \(O/O'\), with \(s=1\) if \(I=1\).
For every \(1\le r<p\) there is a primitive polynomial divisible by \(P^2\) of the form
\[
               F(x)=U(x^p)-b x^r,\qquad
      \deg U\le2d-1,\qquad p\mid b,\qquad b\mid p s^3,\qquad b>0.          \tag{1}
\]
Its rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
After stripping the monomial from \(U\), write \(N\) for its degree and \(H\) for the absolute row resultant.
Then \(N>0\) and
\[
                       \frac{\log H}{N}=\log b\le\log p+3\log s.           \tag{2}
\]
Common monomial shifts give either prescribed pair of classes of difference \(r\), preserving the two stripped rows.
For classes \(0\le i<j<p\) one may take \(r=j-i\) and shift by \(x^i\), keeping degree below \(2pd\).
In particular these witnesses meet the [finite comparison's degree cap](Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement).
When \(I=1\), their entire normalized logarithmic resultant is exactly \(\log p\), and every auxiliary prime dividing the discriminant contributes zero.

<a id="layers"></a>
## The index on a polynomial quotient with multiplicity

For \(e\ge1\) put \(O_e=\mathbb Z[x]/(P(x)^e)\) and embed \(O'_e=\mathbb Z[X]/(Q(X)^e)\) by \(X\mapsto x^p\).
Indeed \(Q(x^p)=P(x)L(x)\) with \(L\in\mathbb Z[x]\), and
\[
                   u=L(\alpha)=p\alpha^{p-1}Q'(\beta)/P'(\alpha)\ne0 .
\]
If \(P(x)^e\mid G(x^p)\), the order of vanishing of \(G\) at \(\beta\) is at least \(e\), because \(x^p-\beta\) has a simple zero at \(\alpha\).
Monic division by \(Q^e\) then gives the indicated injection.
The filtrations by powers of \(Q\) and \(P\) have free abelian successive quotients naturally isomorphic to \(O'\) and \(O\), respectively, by division by the corresponding power and reduction.
On layer \(j\), \(0\le j<e\), the map is the inclusion \(O'\subset O\) followed by multiplication by \(u^j\).
With bases formed by powers \(X^iQ^j\) and \(x^iP^j\), \(0\le i<d\), the matrix is block triangular.
The absolute determinant of its \(j\)-th diagonal block is \(I\,|\operatorname N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(u)|^j\).
Taking norms of the derivative identity gives \(|\operatorname N(u)|=p^d I^2\), since \(|\operatorname N(\alpha)|=1\) and the derivative norms are the two absolute discriminants, whose ratio is \(I^2\) by the [power-basis determinant](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index).
Multiplication of the block determinants gives
\[
                    [O_e:O'_e]=p^{d\binom e2}I^{\,e^2}.                  \tag{3}
\]

<a id="denominator"></a>
## Bounding the denominator of an inverse with multiplicity two

First, \(sP'(\alpha)/Q'(\beta)\in O\).
To see this, for every \(C\in\mathbb Z[z]\) represent \(s C(\alpha)\in O'\) by an integral polynomial of degree below \(d\) at \(\beta\).
Its coefficient of degree \(d-1\) is \(\operatorname{Tr}(s C(\alpha)/Q'(\beta))\), by the leading coefficient in Lagrange interpolation at the \(d\) conjugates of \(\beta\).
In particular these traces are integers.
Write \(\alpha_i,\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\) for the corresponding conjugates and form
\[
        J(z)=\sum_i \frac{s}{Q'(\beta_i)}\,\frac{P(z)}{z-\alpha_i}.
\]
Each coefficient of the quotient in the summand is an integral polynomial in \(\alpha_i\), by synthetic division of the monic \(P\).
Thus \(J\) has integral coefficients by the trace observation, and \(J(\alpha)=sP'(\alpha)/Q'(\beta)\).
Since \(\alpha\) is a unit in \(O\), this proves \(ps/u\in O\).

For any \(D(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]\), take \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree below \(d\) with \(G(\beta)=sD(\alpha)\).
Write \(sD(x)-G(x^p)=P(x)W(x)\) with \(W\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
Now \(ps^2W(\alpha)/u\in sO\subset O'\), so some \(B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree below \(d\) represents this element at \(\beta\).
Consequently \(ps^2W(x)-L(x)B(x^p)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\) and is divisible by \(P\).
It follows that
\[
           ps^3 D(x)\equiv ps^2G(x^p)+Q(x^p)B(x^p)\pmod {P(x)^2}.      \tag{4}
\]
The right side is a polynomial in \(x^p\) of base degree at most \(2d-1\).
In particular \(ps^3\) annihilates \(O_2/O'_2\).
For \(I=1\), (3) gives order \(p^d\), and (4) gives exponent dividing \(p\), hence
\[
                         O_2/O'_2\simeq(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^d .
\]

<a id="witness"></a>
## A full resultant supported on the powering prime when the orders coincide

Use (4) with \(D=x^r\), then divide the resulting witness by its positive content.
This gives (1) except for the asserted divisibility of \(b\) by \(p\).
Because \(P^2\mid F\), its derivative is divisible by \(P\), also after reduction modulo \(p\).
In that characteristic \(F'=-br x^{r-1}\).
The reduction of \(P\) has degree \(d\ge1\) and unit constant, so cannot divide this monomial unless \(p\mid b\).
The polynomial \(U\) must be nonzero and cannot itself be a monomial, since in characteristic zero a binomial with distinct exponents has only simple nonzero roots.
After stripping its monomial the remaining degree is thus positive, and its resultant against a constant of absolute value \(b\) is \(b^N\).
This proves (2).
If \(s=1\), it also shows that \(p\) is the least possible positive denominator for representing \(x^r\) in the subring \(O'_2\).

The [reciprocal unit construction at prescribed primes](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#lift) has \(I=1\) for every selected prime of its actual fourth-logarithmic window.
At each such prime, for every prescribed pair of residues there is therefore a witness within the degree cap with \(H=p^N\).
Every auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne p\) has zero valuation in that witness.
In particular, for these inputs and primes, the [input-dependent sums from rational expressions](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v160.md#input-bound) vanish for every collection of admissible disks and expressions, since their common witness has zero valuation there.
This obstructs any uniform order-\(p\) lower bound for \(\log H/N\) over all witnesses at those prescribed primes.
The measures of the polynomial rows can still be large, and selecting a different prime in the window remains possible.
