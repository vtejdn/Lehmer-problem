# The plastic number is the minimum at conjugate argument rank at most one

<a id="statement"></a>
## An exact all-degree minimum

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit and write \(k(w)\) for the multiplicative rank modulo roots of unity of the arguments \(a/|a|\) of all its algebraic conjugates \(a\) in a fixed complex embedding.
Then
\[
       \min_{k(w)\le1} M(w)=\min_{k(w)=1} M(w)=\theta,\qquad
              \theta^3-\theta-1=0,\quad \theta>1,
\]
where \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure.
The [plastic unit](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#catalogue) itself has rank one and measure \(\theta\).
The result also gives the same lower bound for arbitrary nontorsion nonzero algebraic integers of argument rank at most one, since nonunits have measure at least two by their nonzero integer norm.
The unrestricted problem includes units of higher argument rank.

Suppose for contradiction that \(k(w)\le1\) and \(m=\log M(w)<\log\theta<0.282\).
The [rank-one reduction](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#reflection) preserves \(m,k\) and gives a compressed reciprocal unit with distinct conjugates modulo torsion and at most one inverse pair on the unit circle.
Its degree \(d\) is even and at most 42 by the [degree bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#degrees).
Let \(x\) be its multiplicative class, \(\Omega=Gx\) its orbit in the rational span of the conjugates, \(G\) its faithful splitting group and \(h=m/d\).
The conjugates of complex conjugation \(c\) act as the reflections in the lines of a simply laced root system, whose isomorphic components span the class space.
The Weyl group \(W\) generated by these reflections is normal in \(G\), and \(G\) preserves the set of roots.
Normalize the primitive lattice normals \(v\) to have squared length two and put \(K=\max_v|(x,v)|\), a positive integer.
Let \(p\) denote the fraction of reflection lines perpendicular to one chosen reflection line and \(p_x\) the fraction perpendicular to \(x\).
With \(F(u)=u\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/u})\) for \(u>0\), the [normal-line unit](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#sparse) gives \(dK\le4j m/F(p)\) when \(p>0\), with \(j\le3\), or \(j\le2\) if all perpendicular lines commute, and \(j=1\) if there is just one.
Also \(m\ge d F(p_x)/2\) for \(p_x>0\), because a reflection fixing a compressed class fixes the actual root.
These estimates use the [real-conjugate height bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates).
We sharpen the degree conclusion to \(d\le12\) before using a finite polynomial computation.
For the numerical comparisons below, \(\operatorname{arsinh}y\ge y-y^3/6\) for \(y\ge0\) gives
\[
 F(1/3)>0.041,\quad F(1/2)>0.123,\quad F(2/3)>0.230,\quad
 F(3/4)>0.289,\quad F(3/5)>0.185,\quad F(3/10)>0.0295,\quad F(2/5)>0.070 .
\]
These rational inequalities can each be checked by taking integer powers in the lower bound with \(y=2^{-1/u}\).

<a id="products"></a>
## Several components

Suppose there are \(t\ge2\) isomorphic components of rank \(s\), and let \(q\) count the nonzero component projections of \(x\).
The projections in any active component have Weyl orbit of size at least \(s+1\): a nonzero orbit spans the irreducible reflection space and has sum zero.
Irreducibility here follows since orthogonal invariant summands would partition the spanning reflection normals into orthogonal subsets, contradicting connectivity of the diagram.
The Weyl group acts independently in its components and the possible active sets cover all \(t\) components by transitivity.
Therefore
\[
 d\ge\lceil t/q\rceil(s+1)^q,\qquad p\ge1-1/t,\qquad p_x\ge1-q/t.       \tag{1}
\]

If \(s\ge2\) and \(q=1\), then \(d\ge6,\ p_x\ge1/2\), whence \(m\ge3F(1/2)>\log\theta\).
For \(t\ge3\) one has \(dK\le12m/F(2/3)<15\).
With \(q=2\) the orbit size is at least 18, and with \(q\ge3\) at least 27.
Thus only \(t=q=2\) remains.
Here \(dK<28\), giving \(s\le4\).
For \(s=2\) the component is \(A_2\), and its nonzero orbits under \(S_3\) have size three or six by permuting the coordinates.
The \(W\)-orbit size is divisible by nine, and so is \(d\), since all \(W\)-orbits in \(\Omega\) have equal size by normality.
Thus \(d\ge18\) by reciprocity.
For \(s=3,4\) one already has \(d\ge16\).
In each case \(K=1\).
In \(A_s,\ 2\le s\le4\), every coordinate difference is an integer of absolute value at most one.
Each active component vector consequently has two coordinate values, and at least one third of the transpositions exchange equal values: for \(s+1=3,4,5\) the minimum fractions are \(1/3,1/3,2/5\).
In \(D_4\) with \(K=1\), the active vector is either a coordinate axis or has all absolute coordinates \(1/2\), by the integral pair sums and differences.
At least six of the twelve lines are perpendicular to either vector.
In all cases \(p_x\ge1/3\) and \(m\ge8F(1/3)>\log\theta\).

For \(s=1\), \(p=1-1/t\) and one may take \(j\le2\).
If \(t\ge4\), then \(dK<8\), which excludes \(q\ge3\) by (1).
For \(q=1,2\), (1) itself gives \(d\ge8\).
If \(t=3\), \(dK\le8m/F(2/3)<10\).
For \(t=2\) the full root automorphism group consists of at most eight signed permutations.
Every surviving product has degree at most nine.

<a id="coordinate"></a>
## A unit selected by one coordinate in type \(A_{n-1}\)

Use the coordinate-sum-zero model with Weyl group \(S_n\), so the full group acts by coordinate permutations and possibly simultaneous negation.
Indeed the only possible extra diagram automorphism reverses the simple chain, as realized by global negation followed by the coordinate reversal.
The case \(n=3\) has \(d\le12\), and rank one was already excluded.
For \(n\ge4\), fix a coordinate \(i\), a nonzero coordinate value \(t\) occurring in \(\Omega\), and let \(z\) be the product of the actual roots whose classes have \(i\)th coordinate \(t\).
Write \(s_t\) for the number selected and \(e_i^0=e_i-\mathbf1/n\).
Their sum as class vectors has coordinate \(s_t t\) at \(i\) and constant value on the other coordinates, since their permutations preserve the selected set.
Thus \([z]=s_tnt\,e_i^0/(n-1)\ne0\) and \(h(z)\le s_t h\).
All \(\binom{n-1}2\) transpositions avoiding \(i\) fix the product exactly.
The fraction of real conjugates of \(z\) is at least \(P=(n-2)/n\), so \(h(z)\ge F(P)/2\).
Because \(\Omega=-\Omega\), some positive level has \(s_t\le d/2\), or \(s_t\le d/4\) if there are at least two distinct nonzero absolute values.

For two uses below we compute the height ratio more closely.
Represent the functional \([u]\mapsto\log|u|\) by a real sum-zero vector \(\ell\).
For every algebraic unit \(u\) its height is one half the mean absolute logarithm of its conjugate moduli.
Hence \(2h=\mathbb E_{y\in\Omega}|\ell\cdot y|\) and \(2h(z)=s_t n|t|\,\mathbb E_i|\ell_i|/(n-1)\).
To bound the first mean from below for fixed \(\sum|\ell_i|\), average \(\ell\) over permutations within its positive coordinates and within the remaining coordinates.
This preserves its one-norm and zero sum, and convexity with \(S_n\)-invariance only decreases the first mean.
We may thus calculate the lower ratio on two-level functionals with entries \(n-j\) repeated \(j\) times and \(-j\) repeated \(n-j\) times, up to scaling and overall sign.

<a id="type-a"></a>
## The remaining coordinate orbits

For \(n\ge8\), \(m\ge F(P)\ge F(3/4)>\log\theta\).
For \(5\le n\le7\), two different nonzero absolute levels would give \(m\ge2F(P)\ge2F(3/5)>\log\theta\).
In the remaining case the coordinates are \(r\) copies of \(t>0\), \(r\) copies of \(-t\) and \(n-2r\) zeros.
The permutation orbit already contains its negative and its size is
\[
                        d=\frac{n!}{r!^2(n-2r)!}.
\]
For \(n=5\) the possibilities are \(d=20,30\).
For \(n=6\) only \(r=1,3\), with \(d=30,20\), fit \(d\le42\).
For \(n=7\) only \(r=1,\ d=42\), fits.
In each of the cases \(r=1,\ n\ge5\), \(p_x=(n-2)(n-3)/(n(n-1))\ge3/10\), so \(m\ge10F(3/10)>\log\theta\).
For \(n=6,\ r=3\), \(p_x=2/5\) also gives \(m\ge10F(2/5)>\log\theta\).

For \(n=5,\ r=2\), normalize the orbit coordinates temporarily to \(1,1,0,-1,-1\), scaling both height computations by the same factor.
The twelve terms at the level \(+1\) give \([z]=15 e_i^0\) in this normalization.
For the two-level functionals with \(j=1,2\), the ratios \(\mathbb E|\ell\cdot y|/\mathbb E_i|\ell_i|\) are respectively \(5/2,5/3\).
Indeed \(\ell\cdot y=5\sum_{i\le j}y_i\); for \(j=2\) the mean absolute pair sum over the ten pairs is \(4/5\), and for \(j=1\) the mean absolute entry is \(4/5\).
The coordinate bound gives \(h(z)\le9h\).
It follows that \(m=30h\ge(30/9)F(3/5)/2>\log\theta\).

In type \(A_3\), the [three-letter subgroup estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#degrees) gives \(d<28\).
For coordinate multiplicities \(3+1\), reciprocity joins the two opposite four-element orbits, and \(d=8,\ p_x=1/2\) contradict (3) of that source.
For \(2+2\) the coordinates are \(t,t,-t,-t\) and \(d=6\).
For \(2+1+1\), the permutation orbit with its own negative has the form \(t,0,0,-t\) when it coincides with that negative, giving \(d=12\).
Otherwise \(d=24\) and some nonzero singleton value occurs with fraction \(1/8\) in the full orbit.
Indeed if the doubled value is \(a\ne0\), the two distinct singletons \(b,c\) satisfy \(b+c=-2a\); neither equals \(-a\), they cannot be opposites, and at least one is nonzero.
The coordinate product then gives \(m\ge4F(1/2)>\log\theta\).

If all four coordinates are distinct the permutation orbit alone has size 24, hence must contain its own negative.
Write the coordinates \(t,u,-u,-t\), \(t>u>0\).
Here \(K=2t\) and \(t-u\) is a positive integer.
To bound \(K\) use the normal-line unit from the proof of the [sparse height bound](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#sparse): the line \(e_1-e_2\) has just one perpendicular line \(e_3-e_4\), so its torsion twist has height \(H\le2h/K\) and precisely \(R=1/6\) of its conjugates real.
Indeed only the perpendicular reflection fixes its oriented class, and the twist makes that reflection give real images.
Writing the modulus functional as \((A,A,B,C)\), these real images contribute \(|B-C|\) to the sum of absolute evaluations over the six lines, while the other lines contribute \(2(|A-B|+|A-C|)\ge2|B-C|\).
Thus real places contribute at most one third of the total absolute log modulus.
As in the [radial estimate in type \(D_4\)](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#degrees), the product formula applied to \(1-z^2\) for this normal-line unit gives
\[
 e^{-2H}\le2^{1-R}\big(4H/(3R)\big)^R .
\]
At the real places the normalized factor is at most twice the absolute log modulus and the mean absolute log modulus over all embeddings is \(2H\).
At other archimedean places the factor is at most two, and integrality gives one at finite places as an upper bound.
Taking the \(1/R\) power and using \(1-2H/R\le e^{-2H/R}\) gives \(H\ge R/(2+2^{1/R+1}/3)=1/268\).
Hence \(24K\le536m<152\); the integer \(K\le6\) gives \(u/t\le2/3\).
For the level \(+u\) the six selected terms sum to \(8u e_i^0\).
For two-level functionals with \(j=1,2\) the ratios \(\mathbb E|\ell\cdot y|/\mathbb E_i|\ell_i|\) are \(4(t+u)/3\) and \(4t/3\), respectively, since the six pair sums are \(0,0,\pm(t+u),\pm(t-u)\).
Therefore \(h(z)\le6(u/t)h\le4h\), where \(z\) is the product at level \(+u\).
It follows that \(m\ge3F(1/2)>\log\theta\).
This leaves only degrees at most twelve in type \(A\).

<a id="type-d"></a>
## Even signed permutations

For \(D_b,\ b\ge4\), the Weyl group permutes coordinates and makes even sign changes.
If exactly \(q<b\) coordinates are nonzero, all \(2^q\) sign patterns occur by using a zero slot to correct parity, so the orbit size is at least \(2^q\binom bq\).
When all coordinates are nonzero the size is at least \(2^{b-1}\), and at least \(b2^{b-1}\) if two absolute values differ, by permuting the distinct absolute-value patterns.
For a single active coordinate its orbit has size at least \(2b\), and a fraction \((b-2)/b\) of the root lines are perpendicular to it.
For full support with all absolute values equal, exactly half the lines are perpendicular to the vector.
In both of these cases \(m\ge4F(1/2)>\log\theta\), using \(d\ge8\).

For \(b\ge6\) every other case has more than 42 orbit points.
Indeed \(2^q\binom bq\) for \(2\le q\le b-1\) is bounded below by its smaller endpoint value, since its successive ratios \(2(b-q)/(q+1)\) decrease.
At \(b=5\) the only additional support size that could fit is \(q=2\), giving at least 40 points.
With a zero coordinate all entries are integers, since the pair sums and differences in the normalization of the primitive normals are integral.
Thus \(K\ge2\) for this support, contradicting the [rank-five bound](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#degrees) \(dK<71\).

For \(D_4\) the same source gives \(dK<58\).
Outside an axis or full support with equal absolute values, all orbits have size at least 24.
Hence \(K\le2\) in these cases.
For three active coordinates integrality gives \(K\ge2\) and \(d\ge32\), a contradiction.
For full support with unequal absolute values one also has \(K\ge2\): the integral pair sums and differences make the coordinates all integers or all half-integers, and the sum of the two largest absolute values is then at least two.
Here again \(d\ge32\).
For two active coordinates the bound \(K\le2\) forces them both to have absolute value one.
The Weyl orbit consists of the 24 signed roots, and the full root automorphism group preserves this set.
The original compressed unit itself therefore has the uniformly occurring oriented normal classes.
It has precisely the three perpendicular lines out of twelve as its real conjugations, by compression.
The [type \(D_4\) radial argument](Yin_C_G0901_v74.md#degrees) applies directly to this very unit: on the normals, the real fraction is \(1/4\) and the share of absolute log modulus at the real embeddings is at most \(1/3\).
It gives \(h\ge3/152\), so \(m\ge24(3/152)>\log\theta\).

## The exceptional diagrams

In the remaining single-component types \(E_6,E_7,E_8\) choose a simple basis whose chamber contains \(x\) in its closure.
At least one simple-root label \((x,\alpha_i)\) is positive.
The stabilizer of \(x\) in the Weyl group is generated by the simple reflections for its zero labels: chambers incident to \(x\) can be joined through their walls containing \(x\), as seen by taking a small sphere about \(x\) that avoids the other mirrors and a generic path of directions on the sphere.
The chambers for a finite real reflection group are permuted simply transitively by the Weyl group, and the incident-wall reflections in the chosen chamber generate the adjacent incident chambers by transport.
Thus the stabilizer is contained in the stabilizer of the fundamental weight with \(i\)th label one and other labels zero.
The orbit size of \(x\) is a multiple of the orbit size of that weight, and \(d\) in turn is a multiple of the Weyl orbit size by normality.

Here are explicit finite integer counts requiring at most 43 distinct vectors for each node.
Number the diagram vertices \(0,\ldots,b-1\), with edges \((0,1),(0,2),(2,3),(0,4)\) and \((j-1,j)\) for \(5\le j<b\).
The arm lengths are \(1,2,b-4\).
In node order, the fundamental-weight orbit sizes truncated at 43 are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 6&43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 27,\ 43,\ 27\\
 7&43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43\\
 8&43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43,\ 43 .
\end{array}
\]
For reproducibility, the following pseudocode computes each entry from the displayed edges and the simple reflection formula.
The labels after reflection at \(j\) are \((y,\alpha_i)-(y,\alpha_j)(\alpha_j,\alpha_i)\).
A completed queue gives the entire orbit because the simple reflections generate the Weyl group.

~~~text
start = coordinate vector with one at the chosen node and zero elsewhere
seen = {start}
queue = [start]
while queue is nonempty and size(seen) < 43:
    remove the first vector y from queue
    for each node j in increasing order:
        copy y to z
        set z[j] to -y[j]
        for each neighbor i of j:
            add y[j] to z[i]
        if z is absent from seen:
            insert z into seen and append z to queue
            if size(seen) == 43:
                break
return size(seen)
~~~

In \(E_7,E_8\) every possible positive label therefore forces degree at least 43.
In \(E_6\), a degree below 43 would have to be a multiple of 27.
There is no positive even multiple of 27 through 42.
This excludes all three types.

<a id="finish"></a>
## The low-degree exhaustion

All possibilities left by the reflection argument have \(d\le12\), with at most one inverse pair on the unit circle.
The [exact small-degree enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0190/readme.md#exhaustion) covers all monic reciprocal integer polynomials of positive even degree through 12 without a cyclotomic factor at \(M\le53/40\), and \(\theta<53/40\).
Every surviving noncyclotomic irreducible possibility in this range has at least three inverse pairs on the unit circle.
This contradicts the necessary signature of the compressed input and completes the proof.
