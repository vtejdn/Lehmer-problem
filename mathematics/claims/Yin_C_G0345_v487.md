# Sharp common-row costs at several distinct primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## One pair at every prime in a prescribed finite set of scales

Let \(\lambda\) denote the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.
At a nonempty set of distinct primes \(S\) and an integer width \(b\) use the [common coefficient minimum](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) \(M(S,b)\).
One coprime integral pair \(U,V\) of degree at most \(b\) must have \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\).
At every \(q\in S\) the graph \(U(X^q)+XV(X^q)\) must have a repeated reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum.
The cost is \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), with fallback \(2^{\sum_{q\in S}q}\).

Fix \(r\ge1\) and positive distinct scales \(t_1,\ldots,t_r\).
Call the scales separated for a given unit if \(t_i\log|x|\ne t_j\log|y|\) for \(i\ne j\) and any exterior conjugates \(x,y\) of the unit.
Suppose the scales are separated for every reciprocal nontorsion unit whose positive powers all preserve its degree.
There are computable such scales in any prescribed nonempty open intervals with rational positive endpoints.
Take any real parameters \(L\to\infty\) and primes \(q_i/L\to t_i\).
For every width bound \(b(L)\to\infty\) the common minimum satisfies
\[
             \lim_{L\to\infty}
             \frac{\log M(\{q_1,\ldots,q_r\},b(L))}{\sum_i q_i}=\lambda .       \tag{1}
\]
For a fixed reciprocal unit \(\alpha\) of positive measure \(h\) with all powers preserving degree, it is enough to have scale separation for its own exterior conjugates.
For every positive excess one fixed width then admits a coprime pair with nonzero reflected determinant, the minimum of \(\alpha\) repeated at **every** exponent, and \(\log H/\sum q_i\le h+\text{excess}\) eventually.
When \(h<\log2\) the pair also meets the coefficient cutoff in \(M\) on choosing a sufficiently small excess.
This last construction holds at arbitrary positive integer exponents with the given limits.
It removes the factor two in the previous common-prime upper estimate for these prime sets.

There is also an effective construction with \(r_j\to\infty\) prescribed and \(\eta_j\downarrow0\) prescribed positive rationals.
It returns disjoint sets \(S_j\) of \(r_j\) primes within \([L_j,(1+\eta_j)L_j]\), \(L_j\to\infty\), one qualifying pair for each set, and a single reciprocal minimum for all primes of that set.
The normalized log costs and the recovered measures both converge to \(\lambda\).
The search chooses \(L_j\).
The statement gives no uniform time bound or upper bound for the first such interval.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## A lower bound on all integral pairs before imposing coprimality

Fix the seed and the separated scales, put \(T=\sum_i t_i\), and write \(S_0=hT\).
At any fixed width \(w\), every nonzero integral pair satisfying the repeated-root equations at this seed for all \(q_i\) has
\[
                         \log H\ge (S_0-o(1))L .                             \tag{2}
\]
This includes pairs with a common factor and pairs with identically zero reflected determinant.

Here are the details adapting the [exterior-root matrices](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#matrices).
It suffices to take an arbitrary sequence along which the normalized log norm is bounded.
Reverse both coefficient lists in the common width and strip any common initial zeros in the reversed coordinate.
Equivalently take their actual common degree as the reversal width, which is fixed after passage to a subsequence.
For nonzero coefficient vectors \(w_j\) in these lists, pass to fixed support and limiting slopes \(\gamma_j=L^{-1}\log\|w_j\|_\infty\) in the sense of limits.
They are nonnegative, and \(w_0\ne0\).
Write \(E(s)=\max_j(\gamma_j-js)\).
For one exponent slot \(i\) and one exterior radius \(R\) of the seed, consider the breakpoint candidate \(s=t_i\log R\).
Let \(k,k+m'\) be the first and last indices attaining this maximum.
The finitely many terms outside that interval are exponentially smaller than the maximum after scaling by the actual \(R^{-q_i j}\), since \(q_i/L\to t_i\).
Polynomial factors in \(q_i\) preserve exponential decay.
Use the terms on this interval as coefficients of two polynomials after multiplication by \(R^{-q_i(j-k)}\), and divide them by the largest scaled coefficient modulus.
The resulting coefficient norm is one.

If \(c\) is an exterior conjugate at this radius, the two exact graph equations give the value and Euler derivative equations for these polynomials at \(z_c=(R/c)^{q_i}\), with errors \(O(\exp(-\eta L))\) for some \(\eta>0\) on this subsequence.
In the sign convention \(U-XV\), obtained by changing the sign of \(V\), the rows are \(U(z_c)-cV(z_c)\) and \(q_i z_c(U'-cV')(z_c)+cV(z_c)\).
The common reversal and the shift by \(k\) add only a multiple of the value equation in the derivative.
If there are \(m\) conjugates at radius \(R\), the [single-exponent determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#recurrences) shows that the inverse of this square system on two lists of length \(m\) has norm \(\exp(o(q_i))\).
Indeed the powered points remain distinct by degree preservation, and at zero derivative parameter the rows separately test the two ordinary Vandermonde matrices.
The cited estimate deals with the phases at the actual integer \(q_i\).
Only equations from slot \(i\) are used in this matrix.
If \(m'<m\), padding by zeros and applying the inverse would force the normalized coefficient vector to tend to zero.
Thus \(m'\ge m\).
By separation the different slots count disjoint positive breakpoints.
For the convex piecewise linear function \(E\), the sum of its positive breakpoints weighted by their index jumps equals \(E(0)-\gamma_0\), by summing its linear segments.
It is therefore at least \(\sum_i t_i\sum_{|c|>1}\log|c|=S_0\).
As \(\gamma_0\ge0\), (2) follows.
In particular it holds uniformly over all nonzero pairs in the homogeneous lattice at this fixed width along the chosen sequence.

<a id="many"></a>
## More than one row space of short solutions

Write \(e=\deg\alpha\), fix \(\Delta>0\), and choose \(w\) sufficiently large but fixed, as specified below.
Use \(2(w+1)\) integer coefficients in \([0,B]\), with \(B=\lfloor\exp((S_0+\Delta)L)\rfloor\).
At each \(q_i\), record the value and first Euler derivative of the graph at \(\alpha\).
These are algebraic integers in its degree-\(e\) field.
The [integer boxes at all embeddings](Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) give for the total number of images the logarithmic upper bound
\[
                  2re\log B+2w h\sum_i q_i+o(L).                           \tag{3}
\]
In detail, the modulus at conjugate \(c\) of either coordinate is at most \(C_{w,\alpha}(1+\max_i q_i)B\max(1,|c|)^{w q_i}\).
Use one real coordinate at each real embedding and two at one member of each complex pair.
Subdivide the resulting boxes into cells of diameter less than one in each embedding.
Two distinct integral values in one cell would have a nonzero integral norm of absolute value less than one.
Multiplying the coordinate bounds for the two jets and all slots gives (3), since the fixed polynomial factors cost \(o(L)\).

Differences from a fixed member of the largest fiber give distinct integral homogeneous solutions with coefficient sup norm at most \(B\).
Their logarithmic cardinality is at least
\[
    \big[2(w+1)(S_0+\Delta)-2re(S_0+\Delta)-2wS_0-o(1)\big]L .             \tag{4}
\]
By (2) any two distinct such points have sup distance at least \(\exp((S_0-o(1))L)\), since at fixed width the two norms are comparable.
In any fixed or varying real subspace of dimension at most \(w+1\), balls of half this distance at these points are disjoint for the induced sup norm.
Comparison with the ball of radius \(B\) plus the half distance bounds their log count above by \(((w+1)\Delta+o(1))L\).
Choose \(w\) with \((w+1)\Delta>2re(S_0+\Delta)\).
Then (4) exceeds that bound, so the solutions span a space of dimension at least \(w+2\).

The reflected determinant \(D=UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) cannot vanish identically throughout this real space.
Indeed projection to the first polynomial has a nonzero kernel by dimension, and a nonzero pair \((0,V)\) has \(D=-VV^\iota\ne0\).
Take a basis from the fiber differences.
As \(D\) is quadratic in the entries, some basis vector or sum of two has \(D\ne0\) by polarization.
The selected integral pair has
\[
                            \log H\le (S_0+\Delta)L+O_w(1).                 \tag{5}
\]

<a id="genuine"></a>
## Detecting an extra common factor from its cost in the determinant

Put \(Q_i=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{q_i})\).
All have degree \(e\), measure \(q_i h\) and are pairwise distinct for large \(L\).
The two graph equations at a conjugate \(c\) and its inverse imply \(Q_i^2\mid D\) in the integral Laurent ring.
This divisibility also holds when numerator and denominator both vanish, by the [reciprocal elimination identity](Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) or directly by eliminating the two reflected graphs before applying the unramified substitution \(Y=X^{q_i}\).

If \(Q_i\) divides both rows, its reciprocity gives at least three copies in \(D\).
If its common multiplicity is one, division by \(Q_i\) leaves at least a simple graph zero, at both reciprocal points, for the divided pair.
Its reflected determinant has at least one copy by the same identity and restoring the two factors gives three.
Common multiplicity at least two already gives four.
Thus a common powered minimum forces
\[
                         2\log H\ge m(D)\ge 2h\sum_i q_i+h\min_i q_i .
\]
The first inequality follows from \(\big||U|^2-|V|^2\big|\le H^2\) on the unit circle.
Choose \(2\Delta<h\min_i t_i\) as well as taking \(\Delta\) small enough for the desired excess.
This contradicts (5) for large \(L\).
Both rows are nonzero: if one were zero its partner would contain the square of each \(Q_i\) and would exceed (5), reducing \(\Delta\) below \(S_0\) if needed.
Divide by the primitive polynomial gcd and by joint scalar content.
No required root is lost, since the gcd is coprime to every \(Q_i\).
The determinant stays nonzero, and each new row has one-norm at most \(2^w H\) by monic-factor coefficient bounds, Gauss's lemma and nonnegativity of the integral gcd measure.
This proves the asserted common-pair upper bound for the fixed seed.

<a id="packets"></a>
## Universal scales and computable prime intervals of shrinking relative length

For the universal choice it suffices that no ratio of two selected scales equals \(\log u/\log v\) for real algebraic \(u,v>1\).
These ratios have a computable enumeration with arbitrarily accurate rational approximations.
Choose the scales successively in the supplied open intervals.
For a scale under construction and each previously chosen positive computable scale, enumerate its products with all these ratios.
Starting with a closed interval of positive length inside the prescribed open interval, at each step choose a nested closed rational interval of positive length and at most half the previous length separated from the next enumerated real.
Rational approximations and two disjoint candidate subintervals find such an interval effectively.
The limiting scale is computable and avoids each forbidden value, including a previously chosen scale itself.
This construction works since conjugate moduli are positive real algebraic numbers.
Alternatively for a fixed seed only finitely many ratios must be avoided.

The [common-prime tangency bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(\log M/\sum_iq_i\ge\lambda\) even when the qualifying seed may differ by prime.
For the reverse inequality take an all-power reciprocal seed with measure arbitrarily close to \(\lambda\), using the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) and the [relative norm](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm).
Its common pair has cost eventually below the fallback since \(\lambda<\log2\).
Taking an arbitrarily small excess proves (1).
The argument bounds the actual minimum without requiring any effective threshold for the exterior determinant estimate.

For the effective choice at stage \(j\), enumerate a growing finite box of monic reciprocal unit minima and retain those of positive measure preserving degree under all positive powers, a finite test by the relative norm calculation.
Include the degree-ten Lehmer minimum initially.
Choose a seed of least measure \(h_j\) in the box by comparing the real algebraic multiplicative measures, arranging the boxes to exhaust all eligible polynomials.
Then \(h_j\to\lambda\).
Choose \(r_j\) distinct separated scales in \((1,1+\eta_j)\) and find primes approaching those scales times an increasing integer \(L\).
For example, approximate each computable scale to within \(1/L\), round its product with \(L\) to an integer with bounded error, and take the first prime at least that integer.
The [prime count with modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) gives the required ratios by subtracting counts at the endpoints of every fixed relative interval.
Search over increasing \(L\) beyond the last packet, and over pairs of width at most \(L\) with \(H\le 2^{\sum_iq_i}\) and
\[
                     H^j\le (M(\alpha_j)^j\,2)^{\sum_iq_i}.
\]
Try only packets having distinct primes in the specified interval and check the exact double factors, coprimality and nonzero reflected determinant.
The bound is finite and decidable by real algebraic comparisons.
For this fixed stage take the excess strictly below both \(\log(2)/j\) and \(\log2-h_j\), which is positive by the initial Lehmer seed.
The fixed-seed construction then guarantees termination.
The [tangency bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) for the actual common seed places each returned cost between \(h_j\) and \(h_j+\log(2)/j\) after normalization, proving convergence.
Neither (1) nor this search decides the sign of \(\lambda\).

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin obtained the sharp rate by separating exterior radii across prime slots and using the resulting lower bound for differences in a fiber of the simultaneous jet map.
