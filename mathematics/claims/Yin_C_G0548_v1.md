### 2. One support anchor gives actual cyclic capture times

Let

\[
 Q_k=\prod_{\ell\le k,\ \ell\text{ prime}}\ell,
 \qquad n_j=Q_k a_j\quad(1\le j\le k-1).             \tag{4}
\]

These are distinct positive integers, each at most Q_k T.
Then **for each selected order o there exists at least one index j with N_o dividing n_j**.
This is an existence statement for a possibly different j at each o, not divisibility for every support difference.

To prove it, choose a primitive N_o-th root zeta.
The last factor Phi_(op^r_o) in (2) divides H and hence F, so F(zeta)=0.
Partition the displayed k nonzero terms c_j zeta^(a_j) into minimal nonempty vanishing subsums.
Such a partition exists by successively removing a minimal vanishing subset.
No component is a singleton.
Take specifically the component I containing the fixed term with exponent a_0=0, and choose any j in I other than 0.
L44's rational minimal-relation lemma says that the order of the ratio zeta^(a_j-a_0) divides Q_(|I|), and hence Q_k.
Therefore zeta^(Q_k a_j)=1, or N_o divides n_j.
Coefficients are not expanded into repeated unit terms: their magnitudes do not enter k or Q_k.
All cancellations were performed before (3), and possible equality of the evaluated roots is allowed by the reused lemma.

Assign each o to one such j, for example the smallest.
The assigned sets O_j are pairwise disjoint and cover O; empty sets are allowed.
Write W_j=sum_(o in O_j)w_o.
The full selected contribution at that actual time is

\[
 A_{O_j}(n_j)=(\log p)\sum_{o\in O_j}
        \sum_{i:\,\operatorname{ord}(\bar\alpha_i)=o}
                        v(\alpha_i^{n_j}-1),
 \qquad v(p)=1.
\]

Since n_j is a multiple of N_o for every o assigned to j, prime-to-p multiplication preserves the captured depth and every extra p-power adds its exact nonnegative increment.
Thus

\[
 A_{O_j}(n_j)\ge(\log p)\sum_{o\in O_j}V_o
                 \ge2W_j\log p.                     \tag{5}
\]

In particular one j has W_j>=W/(k-1), proving the genuine one-time conclusion

\[
 n_j\le Q_kT,\qquad
 A_p(n_j)\ge A_{O_j}(n_j)
                 \ge\frac{2W\log p}{k-1}.           \tag{6}
\]

Here A_p is the full p-primary contribution to log C_(n_j).
All d original conjugates are retained; no smaller powered field or residue-degree normalization is used.
Different times have not been added and renamed as one income.
The new premise responsible for (6) is the actual sparse rational multiple F of H, beyond individual order efficiency.

There is also an exact complete-factor comparison.
Put

\[
 G_F(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(t^{n_j}-1).
\]

For each o, the chosen factor t^(n_j)-1 contains every Phi_(op^h), 0<=h<=r_o.
Since those factors are distinct over Q across all o, H divides G_F in Z[t].
Consequently J_F=G_F/H is monic integral and cyclotomic, with every repeated factor retained, and

\[
 \deg G_F=Q_k\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_j\le(k-1)Q_kT,
 \quad\deg J_F=\deg G_F-\deg H,
 \quad\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}C_{n_j}
       =|\operatorname{Res}(P,H)|\,
        |\operatorname{Res}(P,J_F)|.                \tag{7}
\]

Both resultants on the right are nonzero integers.
Equation (7) retains the entire new complement; it does not assert J_F=F/H or remove the latter's degree from T.
Its lower integer norm bound gives an upper bound on Res(P,H), not permission to drop a cofactor from an equality.

### 3. Full actual height cost and a sufficient sparse-completion bridge

Reuse the accepted [L209 full powered-height modulus](Yin_C_G0504_v1.md#height-uniform-cyclic-cost-modulus), with its original Pri11 and Dim19 statement/applicability boundaries and no new source-proof audit:

\[
 \begin{split}
 c&=\log2/4,\qquad h_0=c e^{-20},\\
 b(h)&=\max\{h,\sqrt{h/c}\log\sqrt{c/h}\},\qquad
 \Omega(h)=19b(h)^{1/6},\quad0<h\le h_0,\\
 \frac{\log C_n}{d}&\le\Omega(nm/d)\quad(nm/d\le h_0).
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{8}
\]

Omega is increasing on this interval and tends to zero at zero.
Put h=Q_k Tm/d.
If h<=h_0, (5), the disjoint assignments and (8) give

\[
 \frac{(\log p)\sum_o V_o}{d}
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{A_{O_j}(n_j)}d
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{\log C_{n_j}}d
 \le\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Omega(n_jm/d)
 \le(k-1)\Omega(h).
                                                               \tag{9}
\]

In particular

\[
 \boxed{\quad 2\Gamma\log p\le(k-1)\Omega(Q_k Tm/d).\quad}       \tag{10}
\]

All exceptional initial depths are retained by the first term in (9), not bounded above by the floor 2W.
The different n_j in (9) supply a legitimate upper bound for their disjoint assigned captures.
The separate one-time claim remains (6); (9) is not called one cyclic norm.

The full late-depth version holds as well.
For every integer R>=0 for which p^R h<=h_0, use the same assignments at the actual times p^R n_j.
Each previously captured root gains exactly R in valuation, because its depth at n_j is already at least two.
Therefore

\[
 \frac{(\log p)\sum_o V_o}{d}+R\Gamma\log p
       \le\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Omega(p^Rn_jm/d)
       \le(k-1)\Omega(p^Rh).                                  \tag{11}
\]

The full time and degree multiplier p^R remains inside the height argument.
Equivalently the complete factor comparison (7) may be powered by substitution t -> t^(p^R); both deg H and the entire complementary degree are multiplied by p^R.
No late depth or large time is free.

Here is a precise sufficient uniform bridge.
Fix an integer K>=2, constants C>0 and 0<gamma_0<=1.
Suppose a class of actual inputs has, for each P, some prime p, a nonempty selected O with Gamma>=gamma_0, and a rational multiple F of its actual H having k<=K nonzero terms and T<=C d.
The prime p and its residue degrees may vary.
Choose any 0<h_*<=h_0 such that

\[
 (K-1)\Omega(h_*)<2\gamma_0\log2.
\]

Such a positive h_* exists because Omega(h) tends to zero.
Then every input in that class satisfies

\[
 m>\frac{h_*}{Q_K C}.                                          \tag{12}
\]

Indeed a contrary m would give h<=Q_K C m<=h_*, and (10), log p>=log2, k-1<=K-1 and monotonicity would contradict the choice of h_*.
This conclusion keeps the original d and the entire T, and is uniform even in p.
It is a conditional class theorem, not a claim of these sparse-completion and mass hypotheses on arbitrary near-one inputs.
Unlike a bounded-circle-maximum criterion, it permits arbitrarily large coefficients of F.
Unlike merely cheap individual orders, its support gives the actual finite synchronization in (6).

<a id="near-one-sparse-term-growth"></a>
### 4. A height-independent logarithmic term obstruction

Suppose hypothetically that actual inputs satisfy m -> 0, while Gamma>=gamma_0>0 and there is a multiple F as above of degree T<=C d, with C fixed.
Neither p nor the coefficients of F need be bounded.
Then k must grow.
In fact the following nonoptimized quantitative conclusion holds:

\[
 \liminf_{m\to0}\frac{k}{\log(1/m)}\ge\frac1{\log16}.           \tag{13}
\]

The liminf is along the stated hypothetical sequence and choices of F.
It asserts no existence of a near-one sequence or of the selected positive mass.
We give the elementary estimates to make the dependence explicit rather than importing a prime-number asymptotic.

First Q_k<=16^k.
For an integer n>=1, every prime strictly between n and 2n divides the central binomial coefficient binom(2n,n); including the endpoint 2n causes no problem, since it is prime only for n=1 and then also divides that coefficient.
Hence the product of primes in (n,2n] is at most binom(2n,n)<=4^n.
For the smallest dyadic integer 2^r>=k, multiply these bounds on (1,2],(2,4],...,(2^(r-1),2^r].
This yields

\[
 Q_k\le4^{2^r-1}\le16^k.                                      \tag{14}
\]

This deliberately coarse estimate only uses binomial coefficients.
Its constant is not an optimization target.

Next, the elementary bound already used in L221 gives, on 0<h<=h_0,

\[
 \Omega(h)\le C_0 h^{1/12}(1+\log(1/h))^{1/6},\qquad
 C_0=19\max\{1,(2\sqrt c)^{-1}\}^{1/6}.                        \tag{15}
\]

To see it, b(h)<=max{1,(2sqrt(c))^(-1)} sqrt(h)(1+log(1/h)); this follows from c<1, h<=1 and the definition of b.
Taking the sixth root proves (15).

Put L=log(1/m).
For any fixed 0<eta<1/log16, suppose along a subsequence that k<=eta L.
Then, with delta=1-eta log16>0, (14) and T/d<=C imply

\[
 h=Q_kTm/d\le C e^{-\delta L}.
\]

For sufficiently large L the last quantity is at most h_0.
Apply monotonicity before (15), evaluating the latter at C exp(-delta L).
The right side of (10) is then at most a constant, depending only on C and eta, times

\[
 L^{7/6}e^{-\delta L/12}\longrightarrow0.                      \tag{16}
\]

Its left side is at least 2gamma_0 log2>0, a contradiction.
No such subsequence exists for any eta<1/log16, which proves (13).
When k/log(1/m) already exceeds that range no small-h assertion was needed; this avoids using (8) outside its domain.

For fixed p and B, any positive fraction Gamma>=gamma_0 of L221's efficient orders has deg H<=p^2 B d.
Taking F=H in (13) therefore shows that H itself must have at least a constant times log(1/m) nonzero coefficients.
More generally **every** rational or integral multiple of H with degree at most C d has that term obstruction, independent of how large its remaining coefficients or its complementary factor are.
This is stronger information about sparsity than L221's diverging circle maximum alone: a fixed number of coefficients could otherwise become arbitrarily large.
It remains a necessary restriction; it neither bounds Gamma away from zero nor excludes increasingly many support terms.

### 5. Actual controls, completion costs and remaining scope

\[
 \max_{1\le n\le C'd}A_2(n)/d\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{17}
\]

\[
 H\mid t^{L_O}-1,\qquad
 \deg((t^{L_O}-1)/H)=L_O-\deg H.                               \tag{18}
\]

This binomial has two terms and circle maximum two, with its complete cyclotomic complement retained.

In the L217 family, (17) forces L_O/d -> infinity: if L_O<=C d on an infinite subsequence, the actual time L_O already carries all captured depth and hence A_2(L_O)>=2d log2, contradicting (17).
Thus (18) does not supply a free balanced auxiliary.
