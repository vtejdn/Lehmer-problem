# Sharp cost for consecutive powers on exponents with bounded gaps

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sharp subsequence for every fixed reciprocal input

Fix a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit \(\delta\) whose positive powers all generate its field, and a finite nonempty set \(I\) of distinct positive integers.
Let \(P_a\) be the minimum of \(\delta^a\), \(h=\log M(\delta)\), and \(T=\sum_{a\in I}a\).
For every fixed integer \(q\ge1\) there is an unbounded set \(\mathcal N\) of positive exponents \(n\equiv-1\bmod q\) with bounded gaps in that progression.
For either graph sign and every formal width sequence \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\), the least log one-norm of a row with genuine double contacts at all these original minima, divided by \(n\), tends to \(Th\) along \(\mathcal N\).
Genuine contact means \(P_a^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n)\) and \(A(\delta^{an})\ne0\) for every \(a\in I\).
Each fixed positive excess over \(Th\) is attainable throughout the same tail at one fixed width.
There is no restriction on equal moduli of exterior roots.

In particular take \(I=\{1,\ldots,r\}\), \(T=r(r+1)/2\), \(B=(\log2)/4\), and fix one graph sign and a width sequence tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\).
If there are arbitrarily long consecutive blocks in the progression \(n\equiv-1\bmod q\) on which the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) has fewer than \(r\) distinct values, then the unrestricted infimum satisfies \(\lambda\ge B/(2T)\).
The bound for \(r=3\) under this block exclusion is \((\log2)/48\).
The set of good exponents may depend on the input as well as on \(I,q\).

<a id="rank"></a>
## A full-rank limit at aligned phases

Consider all exterior roots \(c\) of the minima \(P_a\).
These roots are distinct across levels since all power degrees are retained and the measures are \(ah\).
In the [coefficient-index argument](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) let \(R>1\) be one of the exterior radii.
The inverse nodes \(t_c=c^{-n}=R^{-n}Z_c\) on that radius have phases \(Z_c=(R/c)^n\).
At the aligned phases \(Z_c=c/R\) they are distinct.

At a breakpoint with first and last maximizing indices \(k,k+j\), scale the coefficient vectors at offsets \(\ell=0,\ldots,j\) by \(R^{-\ell n}\) and normalize all of them by their largest norm after this scaling.
Denote the resulting pair of coefficient polynomials by \(U,V\), of degrees at most \(j\).
At least one normalized coefficient has magnitude one.
After dividing out \(t_c^k\), the value equation gives \(U(Z_c)-cV(Z_c)\) exponentially small.
For a double node subtract \(nk\) times the value equation from the derivative sum and divide by \(n\).
In the limit the derivative equation gives \(Z_c(U'(Z_c)-cV'(Z_c))=0\).
Indeed the remaining term \(cV(Z_c)/n\) tends to zero and all indices outside the block are exponentially smaller, including the tail at free widths as in the preceding coefficient-index argument.
This also applies to a unique maximizing index by taking \(j=0\).

Suppose \(m\ge2\) of the distinct nodes at this radius are double and \(j\le m-1\).
At exact alignment the limiting value equations make \(U(Y)-RYV(Y)\) vanish at their \(m\) distinct phases \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\).
It must therefore equal \(uL(Y)\), where \(L(Y)=\prod_{i=1}^m(Y-z_i)\) and \(u\) is a scalar, possibly zero.
Differentiating this identity and using the derivative equations at the \(z_i\) gives \(V(z_i)=-u L'(z_i)/R\).
Both sides have degree at most \(m-1\), whence \(V=-uL'/R\) and \(U=u(L-YL')\).
Its degree-\(m\) coefficient \(u(1-m)\) must vanish, giving \(U=V=0\).
This contradicts normalization.
When \(m=1\), the [one-node argument](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii) uses the two unscaled equations to exclude \(j=0\) at every phase.
Thus a radius with \(m\) double nodes forces an index jump of at least \(m\) near alignment.

Suppose instead that the radius has \(m\) double nodes and at least two further distinct simple nodes.
If \(j\le m\), the polynomial \(U-RYV\) vanishes at \(m+2\) distinct points and has degree at most \(m+1\), so \(U=RYV\).
Then \(\deg V\le m-1\) and the derivative conditions force \(V\) to vanish at all \(m\) double nodes.
For \(m=0\) the degree constraint already forces \(V=0\).
Thus \(U=V=0\), again impossible.
Such a radius forces a jump of at least \(m+1\).

These conclusions hold throughout a sufficiently small closed neighborhood of the aligned phase vector.
Indeed the two homogeneous linear systems at alignment are injective respectively on polynomial pairs of degrees at most \(m-1\) and \(m\).
Their matrix entries are continuous in the phases and at least one maximal minor is nonzero at alignment.
A neighborhood can be chosen on which that minor stays bounded away from zero.
Along any hypothetical violating sequence the normalized finite coefficient vector and the phases have a convergent subsequence there, yielding a nonzero element of the limiting kernel.
Use one such neighborhood for all the finitely many radii, the full list and each list obtained by lowering one powered minimum to a simple factor.

<a id="exponents"></a>
## Returning to the same neighborhood on an arithmetic progression

In the finite product of unit circles indexed by the exterior roots put \(g=(|c|/c)_c\).
The aligned vector is \(g^{-1}\).
Choose a relatively open neighborhood \(O\) of \(g^{-1}\) whose closure lies in the preceding good neighborhood.
For \(n=qj-1\), the condition \(g^n\in O\) is a return of \((g^q)^j\) to a neighborhood of the identity in the closure \(K\) of the cyclic subgroup generated by \(g^q\).
The nonnegative powers are dense in \(K\): compactness gives positive powers returning arbitrarily close to the identity and hence approximating the negative powers.
Every translate of that neighborhood by the inverse action can consequently be reached in a finite forward time from each point in \(K\).
By compactness, finitely many translates cover \(K\), so there is a uniform finite upper bound on the waiting time from every \(j\).
This proves bounded gaps for \(\mathcal N=\{n\ge2:n\equiv-1\bmod q,\ g^n\in O\}\).

For any nonzero two-row graphs with the required double factors along these exponents, the weighted index jumps sum to at least
\[
              \sum_{a\in I}\ \sum_{\substack{P_a(c)=0\\ |c|>1}}\log|c|=Th .
\]
Hence their normalized log one-norm has limiting lower bound \(Th\).
This follows by taking any sequence of bounded slope, passing to its coefficient-index limits and integrating the jumps as in the [all-phase proof](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii).
If the input has a nonreal exterior conjugate \(x\), every power \(x^a\) remains nonreal by the power-degree condition.
After lowering the factor \(P_a\) from double to simple, the radius \(|x^a|\) consequently has at least the two simple nodes \(x^a,\bar x^a\), in addition to any remaining double nodes there.
The second rank calculation gives a lower bound of \((T-a)h+\log|x^a|\) for the quotient graph's normalized log norm.
If all exterior conjugates are real, the [real-node strict inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#simple) and its [application to all real exterior roots](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#conjugates) already give a strictly positive excess over \((T-a)h\) at every exponent.

<a id="consequences"></a>
## Retaining the factors and detecting gaps in the count

Apply the [integral double-jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) at one fewer unit of width to obtain a two-row graph with upper slope \(Th+o(1)\), or with any prescribed positive excess at a sufficiently large fixed width.
If both rows contain the minimum of \(\delta^{an}\), dividing it out leaves at least a simple factor at \(P_a\) and double factors at the other levels.
The preceding strict estimate, the coefficient-to-measure loss of at most \(b\log2=o(n)\) on the quotient row, and the restored powered minimum of log measure \(nah\) contradict the upper bound.
At fixed width first choose the excess below the minimum of the finitely many strict gains.
Thus all powered numerator values can be retained by the [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment).
This proves the least cost and fixed-width assertions.

If \(\lambda<B/(2T)\), some positive irreducible measure \(h<B/(2T)\) has a reciprocal unit [all-power representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm), by the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper).
At sufficiently large \(n\in\mathcal N\) the constructed row passes since \(2Th<B\), choosing the fixed excess inside this slack.
The [width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) preserves all contacts under the indicated subexponential padding.
The row realizes \(r\) distinct values \(ah\), one for each \(a=1,\ldots,r\).
Since \(\mathcal N\) has bounded gaps in the progression, arbitrarily long blocks of smaller counts contradict this conclusion.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin obtained the aligned phase calculation while investigating the shared exterior radii left open by the [all-exponent argument](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#conjugates).
The argument does not give a uniform upper bound for the gaps as the unit varies.
