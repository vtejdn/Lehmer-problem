# Factor degrees on fixed horizontal covers of the two-pole curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The exterior allocations and an irreducible cubic cover

Let \(P_{k,l}(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) for the [two-pole quadratic](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement), and fix any positive integer \(k\).
Set \(g=\gcd(k,3)\) and let \(l\) tend to infinity through positive integers.
For all sufficiently large \(l\), each rational irreducible factor \(S\) occupies exactly \(b\) exterior disks above each of \(\beta_3,\beta_4\), for some \(b\in\{g,2g,\ldots,k\}\).
Here the disks have small fixed radius around the distinct \(k\)-th roots of each positive pole.
If \(g=3\), exactly \(b/3\) of the disks occupied above \(\beta_4\) have centers satisfying \(X^{k/3}=\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Uniformly over the factors for this fixed \(k\),
\[
 \frac{\deg S}{2l}=\frac{b}{k}+o_k(1),\qquad
 m(S)=\frac{b}{k}\log(\beta_3\beta_4)+o_k(1).
\]
There are at most \(k/g\) factors counted with multiplicity.
In particular \(P_{3,l}\) itself is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for all sufficiently large \(l\); this includes every sufficiently large \(l\) prime to three.
It has six exterior conjugates, of which two are real, and \(2l\) circle conjugates.
The full list of top-face orbit mass equations, including conjugation of the exterior disks, allows every displayed value of \(b\).
For \(k/g>1\) these equations allow a split with exterior allocations \(g,k-g\) above each pole.
This last assertion concerns the equations alone; realization of a split by actual factors is open here.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Rational orbits of the top face

Write \(q_j(T)=T^2-jT-1\), with positive root \(\beta_j\), and put \(H_j=\mathbb Q(\beta_j)\).
Every positive real radical of \(\beta_j\) in \(H_j\) is a unit of norm minus one for an odd exponent; an even exponent is impossible at the other real embedding.
Such a unit greater than one has integral trace at least one and is at least \(\phi\).
Since \(\beta_3<\phi^3=\beta_4\), the only possible exponents for the two poles are respectively one and the divisors of three.
The same argument gives only exponent one for \(\phi\).
For a positive real radical \(c=\beta_j^{1/k}\) of degree \(d\) over \(H_j\), all conjugates have modulus \(c\); the absolute value of the real constant coefficient gives \(c^d\in H_j\).
Bézout's identity gives \(c^{\gcd(k,d)}\in H_j\).
Thus \(q_3(X^k)\) is irreducible, and so is \(q_4(X^k)\) when \(3\nmid k\), since the radical generates a field containing \(H_j\).

Suppose \(k=3h\).
The positive root of \(X^h=\phi\) similarly has degree \(h\) over \(H_4\), giving an orbit with \(h\) exterior and \(h\) interior roots.
The remaining roots form one orbit with \(2h\) exterior roots.
Indeed, put \(E=H_4(\zeta_3)\) and take \(\alpha^h=\phi\zeta_3\) with \(\zeta_3\) a primitive cube root of unity.
Let \(d=[E(\alpha):E]\), and let \(a\in E\) be the constant coefficient of the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(E\).
All its roots have modulus \(\phi^{1/h}\), so \(a\bar a=\phi^{2d/h}\) belongs to \(H_4\) and is totally positive, as \(E\) is totally imaginary quadratic over the totally real \(H_4\).
The absence of positive radicals of \(\phi\) in \(H_4\) of exponent greater than one implies \(h\mid 2d\), by Bézout's identity.
Total positivity and the negative conjugate of \(\phi\) force \(2d/h\) even.
Thus \(d=h\), and \(\alpha\) generates a field containing \(E\) because \(\alpha^{3h}=\beta_4\) and \(\alpha^h/\phi=\zeta_3\).
Its rational degree is \(4h\), exhausting the remaining roots.

## Applying all the orbit equations

Apply the [fixed-curve face theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#statement) to the absolutely irreducible [horizontal cover](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#pullback) \(F(X^k,V)\), of \(V\)-degree two with both endpoint coefficients one.
The face is squarefree, and all exterior roots in any rational face orbit have the same modulus.
For an orbit with \(t\) exterior centers and \(c\) occupied disks, its mass equation therefore gives \(\deg S/(2l)=c/t+o_k(1)\).
All factors are noncyclotomic by the [torsion exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion).
There are finitely many values of all the fractions, so the fractions for different orbits are exactly equal for large \(l\).
The orbit above \(\beta_3\) has \(t=k\).
Above \(\beta_4\) the exterior orbit sizes are \(k\) if \(g=1\), and \(h,2h\) if \(g=3\).
This gives the stated allocations and factor count since each disk contains a single root of the full polynomial.
The [full signature and localization](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) account for every exterior root, give the measure limit by summing exterior logarithms, and give the signature on the cubic cover.

For sharpness of the allocation equations, the exterior centers above a positive pole consist of conjugate pairs and one or two real centers, according as \(k\) is odd or even.
A conjugation-stable subset of this set can have any size from zero through \(k\), by taking pairs and real centers.
For \(k=3h\), the orbit of \(X^h=\phi\) has the same property with \(h\) replacing \(k\), and the other exterior orbit consists of \(h\) nonreal pairs.
For \(b=3a\) choose \(a\) centers of the first orbit and \(a\) pairs of the second.
The complements realize the complementary mass equations simultaneously.
Taking \(b=g\) proves the splitting assertion, and exhibits the limitation of diagonalizing the fixed-cover mass argument to unbounded \(k\).
