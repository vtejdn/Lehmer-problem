# Linear growth of full interlacing inverse measure with the source degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform lower bound and its consequence for small factors

Let \(S\) be any monic irreducible integer polynomial of degree \(m\ge2\), and let \(g=A/B\) range over the [integral interlacing rational maps](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#statement) except integer translations.
Write \(s=\deg A=\deg B+1\), \(k\ge1\) for the leading coefficient of \(B\), \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(P_g(z)=z^{sm}B(x)^m S(g(x))\), and \(L=\log M(P_g)\).
Put \(\delta=10^{-4}\), \(\rho=9/10\), and \(C_m=\sum_{e=0}^{m-1}\lfloor e/4\rfloor\).
Then for all \(m\),
\[
 L\ \ge\
 \frac{|\log\rho|\, C_m/m-\log(8\sqrt m)}
      {1+\log(16)/\delta}.                                           \tag{1}
\]
In particular whenever \(m\to\infty\),
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log M(P_g)}{m}
 \ \ge\ \frac{\log(10/9)}{8(1+10^4\log16)}>4.7\cdot10^{-7}.        \tag{2}
\]
The bound is independent of the original coefficients, the number and location of poles, the source signature and the full output degree \(2sm\).
The [absolute gap at every irrational source](Yin_C_G0345_v122.md#statement) also applies at bounded \(m\).

For any monic integer factorization \(P_g=FQ\) along such a sequence, if \(\log M(F)=o(m)\), then \(\liminf m^{-1}\log M(Q)\) satisfies the same lower bound (2).
In particular extracting factors of bounded positive measure from full inverses of increasing source degree leaves at least linear logarithmic measure in the complementary polynomial.
There are examples with arbitrarily large source degree and \(\log M(P_g)\le m\,\operatorname{arcosh}2\).

<a id="good"></a>
## A common short interval for the low-cost fibers

Use the partial fractions \(g(x)=x/k+c-\sum_i w_i/(x-b_i)\) with distinct real poles and positive weights.
For each root \(y\) of \(S\) write \(L_y\) for the sum of logarithmic Mahler measures of the reciprocal quadratics \(z^2-uz+1\) over the fiber \(A(u)=yB(u)\), with multiplicities.
These quantities are nonnegative and sum to \(L\).
The [real-part fiber bound](Yin_C_G0345_v122.md#fibers) applies separately at cost \(L_y\).
Indeed each reciprocal pair contributes the logarithm of the larger root modulus, so the sum of the imaginary parts in absolute value over this fiber is at most \(2\sinh L_y\).
For every \(t>2e^{L_y}\), all roots of \(A-(\Re y)B\) and all poles lie in \((-t,t)\) by that argument.
Consequently
\[
 g(-t)<\Re y<g(t),\qquad |\Re y-c|<t/k,\qquad k|\Im y|\le2\sinh L_y.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow2e^{L_y}\) yields \(|y-c|\le3e^{L_y}\).

Call a fiber low-cost if \(L_y\le\delta\).
If none occurs, \(L>m\delta\), which implies (1) by \(C_m\le m^2/8\) and \(|\log\rho|<8\log16\).
Otherwise put \(t_0=2e^\delta+\delta<2.001\).
The real parts of all low-cost source roots lie in the common interval \([g(-t_0),g(t_0)]\).
Its width is at most \(2t_0/k-2W/t_0\), where \(W=\sum_i w_i\).
For \(k\ge2\) this is at most \(t_0\).
For \(k=1\) the map is nonlinear and \(W\) is a positive integer by monic division at infinity.
The width is then at most \(2t_0-2/t_0\).
Both bounds are less than \(13/4\).
Its midpoint \(c_0\) satisfies \(|c_0-c|\le t_0\), since the interval is contained in \([c-t_0/k,c+t_0/k]\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Charging the exceptional conjugates in the discriminant

Write \(v=y-c_0,\ q=13/16,\ C(v)=v^4-4q^2v^2+2q^4\).
At the low-cost source roots, \(|\Re v|\le2q\), \(|\Im v|\le2\sinh\delta<0.00021\), and \(|v|\le2\).
Evaluation at \(v=2q\cos\theta\) gives \(|C|\le2q^4\) on the real interval.
The derivative bound \(|C'|\le48\) on \(|v|\le2\) gives \(|C(v)|\le2q^4+48(0.00021)<\rho\) throughout the low-cost set.
For \(e=4j+h,\ 0\le h<4\), the monic column polynomial \(C(v)^j v^h\) therefore has modulus at most \(8\rho^j\) on that set.

At every other root the preceding bound on \(|y-c|\) gives \(\max(1,|v|)\le 8e^{L_y}\).
There \(|C(v)|\le7(8e^{L_y})^4\) by the coefficients.
For \(0\le e<m\) it follows that
\[
       |C(v)^j v^h|\le 7^j(8e^{L_y})^e
                        \le \rho^j(16e^{L_y})^m ,
\]
since \(7/\rho<2^4\) and \(4j\le e\).
There are at most \(L/\delta\) exceptional roots and the sum of their \(L_y\)'s is at most \(L\).

Change the Vandermonde columns at the original conjugates to \(C(v)^j v^h\) in increasing order of \(e\).
These are monic polynomials in \(y\), so the determinant is unchanged.
Divide each column by \(\rho^j\) and each exceptional row by \((16e^{L_y})^m\).
Every resulting entry has modulus at most eight.
The unscaled determinant has modulus at least one because its square up to sign is the nonzero integer discriminant of \(S\).
Hadamard's bound on the scaled columns gives
\[
 0\le \log|\det|\le
 m\log(8\sqrt m)+C_m\log\rho+
 m\bigl(1+\log(16)/\delta\bigr)L .
\]
This is (1), and \(C_m/m^2\to1/8\) proves (2).
The factor assertion follows by additivity of logarithmic Mahler measure.

<a id="example"></a>
## A family of linear upper bounds

For odd primes \(p\ge5\) use the minimal polynomial of the totally real integer \(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\) for the source.
Its degree is \((p-1)/2\) and all its conjugates are in \([-2,2]\).
Use the allowed map \(g(X)=X/2\), with \(A=X,\ B=2\).
The single inverse trace in each fiber is \(2y\in[-4,4]\).
Its reciprocal quadratic has logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(\operatorname{arcosh}2\), proving the claimed linear upper bound.
