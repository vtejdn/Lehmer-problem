# The cost of limiting multiplicities in a cyclotomic completion

<a id="statement"></a>
## Squarefree products and arbitrary multiplicity caps

Prescribe the first \(k\ge1\) integer coefficients below the leading coefficient of a monic polynomial.
For the reversed coefficient series with constant one write
\[
              1+a_1w+\cdots+a_kw^k
                    \equiv\prod_{j\le k}(1-w^j)^{c_j}\pmod {w^{k+1}},
             \qquad B=1+\max_{j\le k}|c_j|.
\]
Let \(D_H\) be the smallest degree at least \(k\) of a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials with this prefix, using each irreducible \(\Phi_n\) at most \(H\) times, for any integer \(H\ge1\).
There are absolute constants in the bounds
\[
 c\max(k,B,B^2/H)\le D_H
       \le C(k+1)^6\log(2+B)\, B(1+B/H).                            \tag{1}
\]
Above the right side, with a sufficiently large constant, every specified integer degree is attainable by a reciprocal product obeying the same cap.
In particular any finite integral prefix admits a squarefree reciprocal cyclotomic completion at every sufficiently large degree.

Now let the source be a Salem minimal polynomial of degree \(d\to\infty\) with \(a=\log\sigma\le A\).
For fixed \(A,T>0\) and a sufficiently large \(C_{A,T}\), uniformly in \(H\), in the range
\[
    \tfrac12\log d+C_{A,T}\log\log(3d)\le ak\le T\log d,
\]
we have
\[
              \log D_H= ak+(ak-\log H)_++O_{A,T}(\log\log(3d)).      \tag{2}
\]
Let \(K_H\) be the longest prefix attainable at the original degree \(d\) and set \(h=\min(d,H)\).
For all caps,
\[
               aK_H\le\tfrac12(\log d+\log h)+O_A(\log\log(3d)).     \tag{3}
\]
If \(h\ge(\log(3d))^{C_A}\) with sufficiently large fixed \(C_A\), equality holds in (3) up to \(O_A(\log\log(3d))\) in both directions.
For \(H=\lfloor d^\nu\rfloor,\ 0<\nu\le1\) fixed, this gives \(aK_H/\log d\to(1+\nu)/2\).
At squarefree cap the universal upper bound is half the leading term for [unrestricted multiplicity](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement).
There are actual Salem inputs converging to the plastic number where the two-sided bound holds simultaneously for **every** cap, including \(H=1\).
The general lower bound at degree \(d\) for small caps is not asserted by (3).

<a id="lower"></a>
## What a large Euler exponent forces

The integer exponents \(c_j\) are determined successively by the coefficient at \(w^j\).
For a product using \(b_n\le H\) copies of \(\Phi_n\), they satisfy
\[
                   c_l=\sum_{l\mid n}\mu(n/l)b_n
\]
as in the [cyclotomic prefix degree proof](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#degree).
Hence \(S_l=\sum_{l\mid n}b_n\ge|c_l|\), regardless of cancellations.

We use the elementary bound \(\#\{t\ge1:\varphi(t)\le x\}\le C_0x\) for \(x\ge1\).
Indeed \(g(t)=(t/\varphi(t))^2=\prod_{p\mid t}(1+h_p)\) has \(0\le h_p=(1-1/p)^{-2}-1\ll1/p\).
Expanding over squarefree divisors yields \(\sum_{t\le y}g(t)\le y\prod_p(1+h_p/p)\ll y\).
For \(2^j x<t\le2^{j+1}x\) with \(\varphi(t)\le x\), one has \(g(t)>2^{2j}\).
Summing the resulting \(O(2^{-j}x)\) counts over \(j\ge0\), together with the integers up to \(x\), proves the bound.

Since \(\varphi(lt)\ge\varphi(l)\varphi(t)\), at most \(C_0 H x\) of the \(S_l\) copies come from \(\varphi(t)\le x\), counted with their multiplicities.
Taking \(x=S_l/(2C_0H)\) when it is at least one, and using \(\varphi(t)\ge1\) in the other case, proves
\[
                   D_H\gg\varphi(l)|c_l|\max(1,|c_l|/H).             \tag{4}
\]
The cap-independent lower bound \(D_H\ge k\) deals with \(B=1\), and taking the largest \(|c_l|\) proves the lower side of (1).

<a id="labels"></a>
## A construction with distinct prime divisors in the conductors

For each \(j\), split \(|c_j|\) into batches of size at most \(H\), one sign throughout that index.
The number of nonempty batches is \(G\le k(1+B/H)\).
Choose a prime \(p_0>4(k+1)\), and a different prime \(p>4(k+1)\) for each batch, distinct from \(p_0\).
They can be taken with \(p_0\ll (k+1)\log(2k+2)\) and \(p\ll(k+G+1)\log(2k+2G+2)\).
For completeness \({2m\choose m}\ge4^m/(2m+1)\), whereas the power of each prime dividing it is at most \(2m\) by the factorial valuation formula.
Thus \(\pi(2m)\log(2m)\ge\log{2m\choose m}\gg m\) for large \(m\), which supplies the primes even after discarding every integer up to \(4(k+1)\).

For one positive copy of \(c_j\) with batch prime \(p\), use \(\Phi_{p_0p}(w^j)\).
For one negative copy use \(\Phi_p(w^j)\).
The formulas
\[
   \Phi_{p_0p}(u)=\frac{(1-u^{p_0p})(1-u)}{(1-u^{p_0})(1-u^p)},
                \qquad \Phi_p(u)=\frac{1-u^p}{1-u}
\]
give precisely \(1-w^j\) and \((1-w^j)^{-1}\) respectively through order \(k\).
For \(m=p\) or \(p_0p\) the factorization of \(\Phi_m(w^j)\) is \(\prod_{t\mid j}\Phi_{mt}(w)\) since \((m,j)=1\), by classifying the orders of its roots.
Each factor occurs once within this polynomial.
Every conductor in a batch has its own prime \(p\) and no other batch prime, since \(j\le k\).
Thus the product \(U\) is reciprocal with constant one, and all multiplicities are at most \(H\).
There are at most \(kB\) individual copies, each of degree at most \(kp_0\max p\).
The prime estimates give \(\deg U\ll(k+1)^6\log(2+B)B(1+B/H)\), also for the empty product.

The exact-degree assertion requires a way to add degree without duplicating a factor already at its cap.
Let \(m_0\) be the smallest power of two strictly exceeding \(k\).
For any desired additional degree \(s\ge 3m_0\), if \(s\) is not divisible by \(m_0\), take the unique \(h_0\in(k,k+m_0]\) congruent to \(s\bmod m_0\), and insert \(w^{h_0}+1\).
When \(s\) is divisible by \(m_0\) omit this step and set \(h_0=0\).
Write \((s-h_0)/m_0\) in binary and for each set bit \(i\) insert \(w^{2^i m_0}+1\).
The latter are distinct primitive cyclotomic polynomials of orders \(2^{i+1}m_0\).
In the first step \(m_0\nmid h_0\), so every order contributed by \(w^{h_0}+1\) has two-part at most \(m_0\).
Its odd prime divisors are at most \(h_0<2m_0\le4(k+1)\).
Thus all padding is squarefree and disjoint from all batch factors.
Every added term starts above order \(k\), and its reversal is itself.
Padding also provides a comparator of degree at least \(k\) in the definition of \(D_H\) if \(U\) has smaller degree.
This concludes (1).

<a id="salem"></a>
## The cap at the degree of a Salem input

For \(ak\le T\log d\) the [input degree bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) gives \(k\ll_{A,T}(\log(2d))^4\).
The [Salem moment estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#degree) gives, uniformly at each \(1\le l\le k\),
\[
       |l c_l-e^{al}|\ll_A k(1+\sqrt{k d\log(2d)}+e^{ak/2}).
\]
Alternatively the full power sums satisfy \(|p_j-e^{aj}|\le 1+C_A\sqrt{jd\log(2d)}\) and the bound follows by Möbius inversion, since every proper divisor is at most \(k/2\).
Thus with \(\ell=\log(3d)\) and some absolute \(u\),
\[
       B\ll_{A,T}\ell^u(e^{ak}+\sqrt d).
\]
For \(ak\ge\tfrac12\log d+C_{A,T}\log\ell\), taking \(l=k\) also gives \(B\ge e^{ak}/(2k)\), since both error terms above are \(o(e^{ak})\) once the constant is large.
Taking logarithms in (1) proves (2), including uniformity across the switch \(\log H=ak\).

At degree \(d\) replacing \(H\) by \(h=\min(H,d)\) changes no feasible polynomial.
Put \(t=(\log d+\log h)/2\).
At \(k_+=\lceil(t+C\log\ell)/a\rceil\), the estimate \(B\ge e^{ak_+}/(2k_+)\) and (4) make \(D_h>d\) for sufficiently large fixed \(C\) depending on \(A\).
Here \(t\le\log d,\ a\le A\), so a single fixed \(T\) applies.
This proves (3) by monotonicity of prefix feasibility.
For the opposite bound take \(k_-=\lfloor(t-C\log\ell)/a\rfloor\).
The degree of the unpadded construction there is bounded by
\[
               C_A\ell^v\bigl(\sqrt d+e^{ak_-}
                                       +(d+e^{2ak_-})/h\bigr)
\]
for an absolute \(v\), by the preceding upper bound on \(B\) and the construction.
Since \(e^{ak_-}\le\sqrt{dh}/\ell^C\le d/\ell^C\), this is \(o(d)\) when \(C>v+2\) and \(h\ge\ell^{v+2}\).
Exact padding gives degree \(d\) even when some constructed multiplicities already equal \(h\).
This proves the two-sided assertion.

<a id="plastic"></a>
## Squarefree completion at the original degree of an irreducible Salem input

Take \(P_N=z^N f+f^*\) with \(f=z^3-z-1,\ \theta>1\) its real root and \(N\equiv1\bmod120\) sufficiently large.
These are [irreducible Salem inputs](Yin_C_G0345_v129.md#progression) of degree \(d=N+3\), with exterior root \(\sigma_N\to\theta\).
For all \(k<N\) their reversed polynomials agree with \(f^*(w)=1-w^2-w^3\) through order \(k\).
The corresponding powers are \(\theta^j+O(1)\), since the other two roots of \(f\) are inside the disk.
Equation (4) of the [integral prefix construction](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets) gives \(j c_j=\theta^j+O(j\theta^{j/2}+j)\).
Consequently \(e^{k\log\theta}/(2k)\le B\ll e^{k\log\theta}\) for large \(k\).
In the construction at \(k_-=\lfloor(t-C\log\ell)/\log\theta\rfloor\) the \(\sqrt d\) term is absent.
The upper estimate from (1) and exact padding give feasibility for every \(1\le h\le d\); (4) excludes \(k_+=\lceil(t+C\log\ell)/\log\theta\rceil\).
For both indices \(k_\pm<N\) eventually and \(\log\sigma_N-\log\theta=O(\theta^{-N})\) by the construction's root equation.
This proves \(a K_H=t+O(\log\log(3d))\) simultaneously for all caps on these original inputs.
