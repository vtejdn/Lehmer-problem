# A resultant bound below the square root of two on the full odd grid

<a id="statement"></a>
## Upper bounds for the logarithmic mean

Let \(\eta\) be a probability measure on \([0,\infty)\) with \(\int x\,d\eta\le2\) and put \(E(\eta)=\iint\log|x-y|\,d\eta(x)d\eta(y)\).
If \(E(\eta)\ge0\), then
\[
                         \int\log x\,d\eta\le\log4-1.
\]
This is sharp under the two stated constraints.
Adding \(\int\log|x-1|\,d\eta\ge0\) lowers the upper bound to \(\log4-1-1/110\).
If instead \(\int\log|f_i|\,d\eta\ge0\) for the ten [integer polynomials below](#certificate), the mean bound alone gives
\[
                         \int\log x\,d\eta\le\log(1413/1000).           \tag{1}
\]

Apply (1) to any [strict integral odd-grid sequence](Yin_C_G0893_v1.md#statement) with \(p=2n+1\to\infty\) and \(1<M(P)\to1\).
All five normalized residue profiles then satisfy (1), and the whole resultant \(\Delta=|\operatorname{Res}(B_n,Q)|\) satisfies
\[
                        \limsup\Delta^{1/n}\le1413/1000<\sqrt2 .
\]
The same constant bounds the upper limit of the lifted measures of the [ordinary divisors and their products](Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#statement) and the [modular divisors and their products](Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#statement) under their respective degree and divisibility conditions.
For prime conductors \(p\), the resultants are eventually odd if the subgroup generated by \(2\) has index at most two in \((\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\).

<a id="equilibrium"></a>
## The extremizer under the mean and energy conditions

Put \(a=3-2\sqrt2,\ b=3+2\sqrt2\), and let \(\mu\) have density \(\sqrt{(b-x)(x-a)}/(2\pi x)\) on \([a,b]\), zero elsewhere.
This is the free Poisson law of rate two and scale one in Remark 2.6(1) of [Szpojankowski and Wesołowski](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v17/17-10.pdf).
Its Cauchy transform and logarithmic potential \(U(z)=\int\log|z-x|\,d\mu(x)\) can be computed explicitly.
Choose the square root \(S(z)=\sqrt{(z-a)(z-b)}\) satisfying \(S(z)/z\to1\) at infinity.
The function \(G(z)=(z-1-S(z))/(2z)\) extends through zero since \(S(0)=-1\).
Its lower boundary value on \((a,b)\) minus its upper boundary value is \(2\pi i\) times the stated density.
The Cauchy integral formula around the segment consequently gives \(G(z)=\int(z-x)^{-1}d\mu(x)\), since \(G\) tends to zero at infinity and is bounded at both ends.
The expansion \(G(z)=1/z+2/z^2+O(z^{-3})\) gives mass one and mean two.

Write \(z=3+\sqrt2(w+w^{-1})\) with \(|w|>1\).
Then \(S=\sqrt2(w-w^{-1})\) and \(G=1/(1+\sqrt2w)\).
Integrating \(G\,dz\), taking its real part and matching \(U(z)-\log|z|\to0\) gives
\[
         U(z)=\log2+2\log|w|-\log|1+\sqrt2w|+\Re(\sqrt2/w).
\]
The formula extends continuously to the segment, as does the logarithmic integral since its density is bounded.
For \(|w|=1\) we have \(|1+\sqrt2w|^2=x\) and \(2\Re(\sqrt2/w)=x-3\), so \(2U(x)+\log x-x=\ell:=2\log2-3\) on \([a,b]\).
Off the segment on \(x>0\), the derivative of the left side is \(-S(x)/x\), positive to the left and negative to the right.
It follows that \(2U(x)+\log x-x\le\ell\) throughout the positive axis.
At \(z=0\) we have \(w=-\sqrt2\), giving \(U(0)=2\log2-1\).
Integrating the equality on the segment and using \(\int\log x\,d\mu=U(0)\) yields \(E(\mu)=0\).

Set \(\nu=\eta-\mu\).
If \(\int\log x\,d\eta=-\infty\), the upper bounds already hold.
Otherwise all logarithmic integrals in the next identity are finite when \(E(\eta)\ge0\), since the positive parts are integrable by the first moments and the cross-potential \(U\) is continuous with logarithmic growth.
Bilinearity gives
\[
 E(\eta)+\int(\log x-x)\,d\eta
     = E(\nu)+\int(2U+\log x-x)\,d\eta-E(\mu)
     \le E(\nu)+\ell .
\]
The first moment bound and \(E(\eta)\ge0\) therefore give \(\int\log x\,d\eta\le\log4-1+E(\nu)\).

For clarity, let \(\widehat\nu(t)=\int e^{itx}\,d\nu(x)\).
For \(s>0\), integration of the elementary Laplace transform of \(\sin(rt)\) gives
\[
 \log\sqrt{s^2+r^2}-\log s=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}(1-\cos(rt))\,dt/t .
\]
Integration against \(d\nu(x)d\nu(y)\) at \(r=x-y\) is justified at zero by \(|1-\cos(rt)|\le2|r|t\) and the first moments, and at infinity by the exponential.
The zero total mass of \(\nu\) gives the regularized energy \(-\int_0^\infty e^{-st}|\widehat\nu(t)|^2\,dt/t\).
Let \(s\downarrow0\) separately in each positive-measure pairing on the left, using monotonicity and the integrable majorant at \(s=1\).
Thus
\[
                   -E(\nu)=\int_0^\infty|\widehat\nu(t)|^2\,dt/t\ge0.   \tag{2}
\]
This proves the first bound, and \(\eta=\mu\) attains it.

<a id="separation"></a>
## The effect of the first integer test

On the support of \(\mu\), the potential formula gives \(U(1)=\log2-1<0\).
For \(\epsilon>0,\ R>0\), put
\[
                f(x)=\log|x-1-i\epsilon|-\log|x+iR|+\log R .
\]
For any \(\eta\) satisfying the additional integer test we have \(\int f\,d\eta\ge-1/R\), since \(\log(1+t^2)\le t\) on \(t\ge0\).
Also \(\int f\,d\mu\le U(1+i\epsilon)\).
The density of \(\mu\) is bounded by \(\sqrt2/\pi\), because \(-1+6/x-1/x^2\le8\).
Using \(\int_{\mathbb R}\frac12\log(1+\epsilon^2/t^2)\,dt=\pi\epsilon\), obtained by integrating in \(\epsilon\), gives \(U(1+i\epsilon)\le\log2-1+\sqrt2\epsilon\).

Subtract the regularized logarithm identities used for (2), now centered at \(1\) and at zero with smoothing parameters \(\epsilon\) and \(R\).
The mass-zero terms drop after integration against \(\nu\).
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\[
 \left|\int f\,d\nu\right|^2
 \le -E(\nu)\int_0^\infty |e^{-it-\epsilon t}-e^{-Rt}|^2\,dt/t
 =-E(\nu)\log\frac{(R+\epsilon)^2+1}{4R\epsilon}.
\]
For the integral evaluation, expand the square, pair the two differences of real exponentials with \(\int_0^\infty(e^{-ut}-e^{-vt})dt/t=\log(v/u)\), and use the displayed regularized logarithm for the cosine term.
At \(R=40,\ \epsilon=1/32\) the logarithm is below 6, whereas \(\int f\,d\nu\ge1-\log2-1/40-\sqrt2/32>47/200\).
Here \(\log2<0.695,\ \sqrt2<1.42\), and the logarithm argument is below \(321<e^6\).
Consequently \(-E(\nu)>(47/200)^2/6>1/110\), as claimed.

## A rational certificate using ten integer polynomials

In the order shown, take
\[
\begin{split}
(f_i)={}&(x-1,x-2,x-3,x^2-3x+1,x^2-4x+2,\\
       &x^3-5x^2+6x-1,x^3-6x^2+9x-3,x^3-6x^2+8x-2,
                 x^3-7x^2+13x-5,x^2-4x+1),\\
(w_i)={}&(667,245,25,189,63,53,31,5,4,15).
\end{split}
\]
Put \(D=1000,\ s=1201,\ K=1413/1000\).
We verify the scalar bound \(H(x)=x\exp(s(2-x)/D)\prod_i|f_i(x)|^{w_i/D}\le K\) for every \(x\ge0\).
Off its zeros write \(g=D(\log H)'=D/x-s+\sum_i w_i f_i'/f_i\) and \(F=\prod_i f_i\).
The following integers \(r\) specify brackets \(a_r=r/10^6,\ b_r=(r+1)/10^6\) in increasing order.
\[
\begin{gathered}
172652,\ 248943,\ 285380,\ 326699,\ 451714,\ 511610,\ 531364,\ 651623,\\
1428834,\ 1474868,\ 1613842,\ 1733261,\ 2287685,\ 2341642,\ 2911240,\\
3082053,\ 3322988,\ 3631377,\ 3783459,\ 4053157,\ 4185125,\ 4260232 .
\end{gathered}
\]
For every pair of consecutive indices \(r,r'\), \(F(b_r)F(a_{r'})<0\).
This accounts for all 21 zeros by degree, so they are simple, positive and distinct.
Between them the partial fractions make \(g\) strictly decreasing.
Each bracket satisfies \(g(a_r)>0>g(b_r)\), so these 22 brackets enclose every local maximum, including the ones before the first and after the last zero.
The function vanishes at zero and at the polynomial roots, and tends to zero at infinity.

The following exact comparisons give both the verification and the precision of the brackets.
Write \(f_i(x)=\sum_k c_{ik}x^k\), and for each bracket put \(a=a_r,\ b=b_r,\ v=s(a-2)/D\).
Define
\[
    M_i=|f_i(a)|+(b-a)\sum_{k\ge1} k|c_{ik}|b^{k-1},
     \qquad T=\sum_{j=0}^{19}\frac{v^j}{j!}.
\]
For each of the listed integers, substitution gives \(T>0\) and
\[
                        b^D\prod_i M_i^{w_i}<(K T)^D .
\]
These are rational comparisons: equivalently form the list \((b,D),(M_i,w_i),(1/(KT),D)\), multiply the powered numerators and denominators separately, and compare the resulting integers.
The mean value inequality bounds \(|f_i|\) by \(M_i\) on the bracket.
The positive twentieth-order Taylor remainder gives \(T\le e^v\le e^{s(x-2)/D}\) there.
This proves the scalar bound, whose logarithm integrates to (1) using the ten polynomial tests and \(\int x\,d\eta\le2\).
All positive logarithmic parts are integrable by the first moment, and the polynomial tests give finite negative parts.

## Transfer to arithmetic inputs

The [normalized residue law](Yin_C_G0893_v1.md#decision-normalized-residue-tests-reviewed) supplies every integer polynomial test, the energy condition from \(x_1-x_2\), and \(\limsup n^{-1}\log\Delta\le\int\log x\,d\eta\) along each extracted profile and weak law.
This proves the whole resultant bound along every subsequence.
For an ordinary divisor \(F\mid h\) of degree \(o(\sqrt{p/\log p})\), the [deleted-grid estimate](Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer) bounds the upper limit of \(\log M(F^*)\) by the same logarithmic mean after extraction.
Its subproduct argument applies with \(K\) in place of \(149/100\) to the product with multiplicity of all primitive irreducibles of degree at most \(p^{1/2-\delta}\) for fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\).
For a modular factor \(h\equiv FG\bmod B_n\), the nonzero integral resultant of \(G\) gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(B_n,F)|\le\Delta\).
The [two-sided cyclic estimate and subproduct argument](Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#decision-modular-trace-factor-reviewed) consequently give the same bound when \(d^3\log(2d)\log(2p)=o(p)\), \(d=\deg F\ge1\), or for a specified product of factors of individual degree at most \(p^{1/3-\delta}\) with \(0<\delta<1/3\) and modular divisibility of the full product.
Integral constant divisors obey the same conclusion by their exact sampled norm.
These transfer statements retain all original hypotheses on the chamber, including the strict endpoint and whole measures tending to one.

## Prime divisors at prime conductors

The integer determinant of multiplication by \(h\) on the power basis of \(\mathbb Z[t]/(B_n)\) has absolute value \(\prod_j|h(b_j)|=\Delta\).
Indeed evaluation diagonalizes multiplication on the distinct complex roots, while \(Q\equiv B_{n-1}h\bmod B_n\) and consecutive \(B_i\) have absolute resultant one by their monic three-term recurrence down to \(B_0=1\).
Fix a prime \(\ell\).
On reducing modulo \(\ell\), the nullity of this matrix is \(r=\deg\gcd(\bar h,\bar B_n)\).
Indeed the kernel in \(\mathbb F_\ell[t]/(\bar B_n)\) consists of the multiples of \(\bar B_n/\gcd(\bar h,\bar B_n)\).
Thus \(\ell^r\mid\Delta\), since at least \(r\) diagonal entries of its integer Smith normal form are multiples of \(\ell\).
In particular \(\limsup r/n\le\log(1413/1000)/\log\ell\) for each fixed prime \(\ell\) along any of the shrinking sequences above.

Suppose \(p\) is prime and \(p\ne\ell\).
The roots of \(\bar B_n\) in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_\ell\) are the \(n\) distinct values \(w+w^{-1}\) indexed by nonidentity \(p\)-th roots of unity modulo inversion, using \(z^n B_n(z+z^{-1})=(z^p-1)/(z-1)\).
Frobenius sends such a trace to \(w^\ell+w^{-\ell}\), so each irreducible factor over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) has degree \(n/k\), where \(k\) is the index of the subgroup generated by \(\ell\) in \((\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\).
If \(\ell\mid\Delta\), the reduced matrix is singular and therefore \(r\ge n/k\), giving \(\Delta^{1/n}\ge\ell^{1/k}\).
It follows that for fixed \(\ell,k_0\) with \(\ell^{1/k_0}>1413/1000\), all sufficiently large prime conductors on the sequence with \(k\le k_0\) have \(\ell\nmid\Delta\).
In particular \(\ell=2,\ k_0=2\) is allowed because \(1413^2<2\cdot1000^2\).
