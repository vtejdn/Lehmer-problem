# A quadratic coefficient bound for divisors at unrestricted exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## Width, degree and effective support

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), let \(m=m(\alpha)>0\) be its logarithmic Mahler measure, and let \(F\) be any nonzero rational Laurent polynomial vanishing at \(\alpha\).
After a monomial shift write \(F=\sum_{j=1}^k c_j X^{a_j}\), with nonzero rational coefficients and \(0=a_1<\cdots<a_k=T\).
Put \(\rho=(\sum|c_j|)^2/\sum|c_j|^2\).
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
                 T\,\frac{m}{d}\ \ge\ \frac{c}{\rho^2}.              \tag{1}
\]
The width, the coefficients and the cofactor are unrestricted.
Since \(\rho\le k\), (1) replaces the factorial dependence on the number of terms in the small-measure range of the [sparse divisor estimate](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) by \(k^2\) up to absolute constants, for each irreducible factor.
It also improves the dependence on effective support in the [phase Gram estimate](Yin_C_G0092_v1.md#angular-short-annihilator-effective-support).

More generally, let \(F_1,\ldots,F_u\) be complex polynomials of degrees at most \(T\), not all zero, each vanishing at every conjugate of \(\alpha\).
Let \(W(\theta)=\sum_\nu |F_\nu(e^{i\theta})|^2=\sum_{n=-T}^T b_n e^{in\theta}\) and \(A=\sum|b_n|/b_0\).
Then
\[
                          T\,m/d\ \ge\ c/A^2 .                         \tag{2}
\]
Thus correlations can cancel between different shifts and different annihilating polynomials.
For an integer polynomial of width \(T\) the sum of the degrees of its nontorsion factors, counted with multiplicity and excluding monomials, is at most \(C T\rho^2\) times its total logarithmic measure.
In particular a primitive irreducible polynomial has either zero measure or logarithmic measure at least \(c/\rho^2\).
The statement for a general reducible input retains the proportion of its degree contributed by nontorsion factors.

<a id="primitive-phase"></a>
## A phase estimate at a specified power

Write \(z_i=\alpha_i/|\alpha_i|\) for the phases of all conjugates and \(h=m/d\).
Put \(H=Th\) and suppose \(H\le1\), as (1) and (2) hold with \(c\le1\) for \(H>1\).
We show for \(1\le r\le T\) that
\[
        \left|d^{-1}\sum_i z_i^r\right|
                 \le C\sqrt H .                                      \tag{3}
\]
Let \(\delta=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^r):\mathbb Q]\).
The \(\delta\) distinct conjugates of \(\alpha^r\) each occur \(d/\delta\) times, are units, and have absolute height \(s=rh\) and total logarithmic measure \(\delta s\).
Apply the [discriminant energy inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases), equation (3) at its first Fourier coefficient, with degree \(\delta\) and regularization parameter \(e^{-1/\delta}\).
It bounds the mean of the reflected points \(e^{-|\log|w||}w/|w|\) over those conjugates \(w\) in absolute value by \(C\sqrt{s+\log(2\delta)/\delta}\).
Replacing these points by their phases costs at most \(2s\) in the mean, by the sum of absolute radial logarithms.
Those phases with their original repetitions give the left side of (3).

To control the diagonal error even when the power drops degree, take \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha^r)\), \(e=[K:E]\), and \(\gamma=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/E}\alpha\).
Then \(\gamma^r=(\alpha^r)^e\), so \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion unit with height \(e h\) and degree \(b\le\delta\).
Its measure is \(b e h\le m\), as in the [ordinary norm descent](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#statement).
The [quadratic logarithmic floor](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(b(2m(\gamma))^2\ge1/10\), since the sum of absolute logarithmic moduli of its conjugates is \(2m(\gamma)\).
Consequently \(1/\sqrt{\delta}\le C m\).
The existence of a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(T\) vanishing at all \(d\) conjugates implies \(T\ge d\), including in the vector statement.
Therefore
\[
          \frac{\log(2\delta)}{\delta}\le C/\sqrt\delta
                        \le C m\le C H .
\]
Combining this with \(s\le H\) proves (3) with absolute constants.
Negative indices give its complex conjugate.

<a id="radial"></a>
## Radial evaluation of simultaneous annihilators

Use the Euclidean norm on the vector \(F=(F_\nu)\) and put \(S=\sup_{|z|=1}\|F(z)\|\).
For \(|z|\le1\), \(\|F'(z)\|\le e T S\).
Indeed testing on scalar products with unit vectors gives the polynomial growth bound \(S(1+1/T)^T\) throughout the disk of radius \(1+1/T\), by the maximum principle inside and polynomial reversal outside the unit circle.
Cauchy's derivative estimate with radius \(1/T\) then gives the claim.
For a root \(\alpha_i=r_i z_i\) inside the unit disk, integrate this derivative along the segment to \(z_i\).
For an exterior root, apply the same reasoning to the reversed vector \(z^T F(1/z)\), which vanishes at \(1/\alpha_i\) and has the same boundary supremum.
In both cases
\[
        \|F(z_i)\|\le S\min(1,eT|\log r_i|).
\]
This includes an actual circle root with both sides zero.
Squaring, using \(\min(1,y)^2\le y\) and averaging gives
\[
       d^{-1}\sum_i W(\arg z_i)\le 2e H S^2\le 2e H A b_0 .
\]
The last inequality follows from the Fourier expansion of \(W\).

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The constant Fourier coefficient

The phase estimate (3) bounds the same mean below by \(b_0-C\sqrt H\sum_{n\ne0}|b_n|\).
Here \(b_0>0\) by the assumption on the polynomials.
Combining both bounds gives \(1\le C(A-1)\sqrt H+2e A H\), hence \(H\ge c/A^2\).
For a single rational Laurent relation, remove its monomial first.
Its Fourier coefficients are the autocorrelations of the coefficient vector, so \(b_0=\sum |c_j|^2\) and \(\sum|b_n|\le(\sum|c_j|)^2\).
This proves (1) and the stronger autocorrelation form (2) even when many equal differences cancel.

For an irreducible primitive nonunit factor with a nonzero root, the root formula in the two orientations gives logarithmic measure at least \(\log 2\), so its degree is at most \(C T\rho^2\) times its measure as well.
Sum the individual estimates over the nontorsion irreducible factors of the given polynomial with repetitions, using its own fixed \(T,\rho\) throughout.
Integer content can only increase its measure.
The irreducible bound follows on taking the input to be the factor itself and removing any Laurent monomial.
