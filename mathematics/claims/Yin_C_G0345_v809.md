# Distinct fractional ideals from evaluations on a reversed exterior block

<a id="statement"></a>
## Dyadic evaluations at a least-degree witness

Let \(x\) have the [reversed real-root signature](Yin_C_G0345_v804.md#one) at a least-degree witness of positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Thus every algebraic number of positive logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(m\) has degree at least \(d=2ub\), where \(b\) is the block size and \(u\) is the number of blocks.
Assume \(b\ge3\), write \(r\) for the exceptional real exterior root, and put \(R=|r|=e^{m/2}\).
Take the [oriented block field](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) \(K\) at the majority orientation of the exceptional block, its block field \(E\), and the nonidentity \(\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)\).
Let \(f\in\mathcal O_K[X]\) be the monic polynomial with the majority roots multiplied by \(\operatorname{sgn}(r)\).
For each integer \(Q\ge10R\), set \(q_j=2^jQ\) and
\[
                \epsilon_j=f(q_j)/(\tau f)(q_j)\qquad(j\ge0).
\]
For every \(s\ge1\) the \(2^s\) products \(\prod_{j\in I}(\epsilon_j)\), indexed by subsets \(I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,s-1\}\), are distinct fractional ideals of \(K\).
Their prime divisors all lie above primes of \(E\) that split in \(K\).

<a id="expansion"></a>
## Positive coefficients at the real place

At the chosen embedding the roots of \(f\) are \(R^{-1}\) and \(e^{t_i+i\theta_i}\) for \(1\le i<b\), where \(t_i>0\) and \(\sum_i t_i=t=\log R\) by the torsion block product.
The roots of \(\tau f\) are their inverses, since multiplication by a rational sign commutes with inversion.
For real \(q>R\) both evaluations are positive, as the two polynomials are real monic and all their roots have modulus at most \(R\).
Writing \(L(q)=\log(f(q)/(\tau f)(q))\) and expanding \(\log|1-z/q|\) in its absolutely convergent series gives
\[
 L(q)=2\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{q^{-k}}{k}
     \left(\sinh(kt)-\sum_{i=1}^{b-1}\cos(k\theta_i)\sinh(kt_i)\right).
\]
Every coefficient of \(q^{-k}\) is strictly positive, since \(\sinh(a+b)>\sinh(a)+\sinh(b)\) for positive \(a,b\) and there are at least two positive \(t_i\).
The bracket is at most \(2\sinh(kt)\), and \(\sinh(kt)\le ktR^k\) by integration of \(\cosh\) on \([0,kt]\).
Consequently
\[
                 0<L(q)\le 4t\,R/(q-R).
\]
For every \(j\), summing each power over later dyadic arguments multiplies its value at \(q_j\) by \(1/(2^k-1)\).
This equals one for \(k=1\) and is strictly smaller for \(k\ge2\).
Positivity and convergence therefore give \(\sum_{h>j}L(q_h)<L(q_j)\), and \(\sum_{j\ge0}L(q_j)<2L(Q)\le8t/9<m\).

<a id="ideals"></a>
## A collision gives a smaller-degree unit

Suppose two different subsets give the same fractional ideal, and divide their element products to obtain a unit \(w\in\mathcal O_K^\times\).
At the chosen real embedding the first index where the subsets differ determines the sign of \(\log|w|\), because the sum of all later logarithms is smaller than its term.
Thus \(0<|\log|w||<m\).
The other real embedding has reciprocal value since \(\tau w=w^{-1}\).
At each of the remaining places the two embeddings of \(K\) over the same real embedding of \(E\) are complex conjugate, so \(\tau w=w^{-1}\) gives modulus one there.
Hence the sum of positive logarithms over embeddings of \(K\) is \(|\log|w||\); it equals \([K:\mathbb Q(w)]m(w)\) because \(w\) is an algebraic unit.
This contradicts least degree, since \(0<m(w)<m\) and \([\mathbb Q(w):\mathbb Q]\le 2u<d\).
Finally each \((\epsilon_j)\) is inverted by \(\tau\), so its valuation vanishes at every prime fixed by \(\tau\), including all primes above inert or ramified primes of \(E\).
The resulting obstruction requires distinct ideals and leaves open their possible split-prime support as \(s\) grows.
