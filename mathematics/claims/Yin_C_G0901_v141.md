# Nonreal conjugates off the circle in actual Mahler values near one

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quantitative alternative without a reciprocity hypothesis

For \(x\ne0\) let \(k(x)\) be the number of its distinct conjugates which are both nonreal and off the unit circle.
Equivalently these are the embeddings at which \(x+x^{-1}\) is nonreal, counted as embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
Let \(A=M(P)>1\) for any nonzero integer polynomial and put \(m=\log A\).
For each \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), at sufficiently small positive \(m\) depending only on \(\eta\), either \(A\) is a real Salem number or
\[
                     k(A)\ \ge\ \exp\!\big(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).
\]
For reciprocal \(A\), precisely half of these conjugates are exterior.
Thus bounding the number of nonreal exterior conjugates alone forces any reciprocal Mahler value sufficiently close to one to be Salem, regardless of the number of its real exterior roots.
The first estimate also covers nonreciprocal values and places no ceiling on the second iterate.

There is an underlying restriction on a family of nontorsion units \(w_i\) whose positive total log measures are all at most \(m\).
If a nontorsion \(x\) in their divisible conjugate group has \(k(x)\) below the displayed threshold, its class modulo torsion is proportional to a single coordinate of a terminal compressed term \(z\) from an individual input.
The measure and degree of \(x\) may be arbitrarily large.
This statement allows a nonzero number of nonreal trace embeddings, beyond the [exact classification at zero](Yin_C_G0901_v140.md#statement).

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## Detecting lines moved by conjugation with quadratic polynomials

Use the [constituent decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules) for a finite subfamily supporting \(x\).
For a nonzero projection \(v\) of \([x]\) to an active leading space \(W\) write \(\Omega=Gv\) in a common finite Galois group \(G\), let \(N=|\Omega|\) and \(t=\dim W\), and use \(c\) for complex conjugation.
Call an orbit vector moved if its line is moved by \(c\); equivalently \(cv'\notin\{v',-v'\}\).
The number of moved vectors in \(\Omega\) is at most \(k(x)\): the projection map on conjugates is surjective, and a real or neutral conjugate has class fixed or negated by \(c\).

If even one vector in \(\Omega\) is moved, their number is at least \(2N/(t(t+1))\).
Choose a scalar component of the quadratic map \(w\mapsto w\wedge cw\) on \(W\) which is nonzero on that vector.
Its evaluations on \(\Omega\) are supported on moved vectors.
The restrictions of all homogeneous quadratic forms to \(\Omega\) span a \(G\)-invariant subspace of \(\mathbb R^N\) of dimension \(h\le t(t+1)/2\).
The Euclidean orthogonal projection to this subspace has constant diagonal \(h/N\) by transitivity.
Any vector \(f\) in the subspace satisfies \(|f_i|\le\sqrt{h/N}\|f\|_2\), by projecting the \(i\)-th basis vector.
Summing over the support of our nonzero evaluation vector gives the assertion.

Write \(2ub=\deg z\) for the compressed term attached to \(W\), so \(t\le ub\).
If none of \(\Omega\) is moved, the [character argument for the orbit of a class](Yin_C_G0901_v140.md#action) shows that \(W\) is independent signed and \(v\) a coordinate.
Indeed that argument uses only the lines of the orbit being fixed by every conjugate of \(c\), the faithfulness of the leading space, and the distinct [sign characters](Yin_C_G0901_v140.md#characters) on its signed coordinates.
Thus any noncoordinate orbit with \(N\ge\binom b3\) gives \(k(x)\gg b/u^2\).

<a id="small"></a>
## Small noncoordinate orbits still have many nonreal trace embeddings

Suppose \(N<\binom b3\) and \(v\) is noncoordinate.
In the independent signed model mark the nonzero coordinates.
In the oriented model use the representative of sum zero on each block and mark the entries different from its most frequent entry, ignoring constant blocks.
There are exactly two marked labels in total, and the set is equivariant under the signed permutations in the respective model.
Here are the small-orbit details, using the [supported alternating and even-flip actions](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#orbit).
In the signed case a support of size between \(3\) and \(b-3\) in any block has at least \(\binom b3\) images; a larger support gives at least \(2^{b-4}>\binom b3\) images by even flips at large \(b\).
In the oriented case a block with no color of multiplicity at least \(b-2\) has a union of colors of size between \(3\) and \(b-3\): take one color in that range or accumulate colors of size at most two.
Its possible position sets again give \(\binom b3\) images under the ordinary alternating action.
Thus a nonzero block has at most two marks and the oriented majority is unique.
Three or more total marks in several blocks would give at least \(b\binom b2\) or \(b^3\) images, using the independent actions on two or three blocks.
Zero or one total mark cannot give a noncoordinate vector.

If \(c\) moves any pair label in the attached term, transport an occupied block to a block containing a moved label \(i\).
Arrange one mark at \(i\) and vary the second mark within its block.
The supported label actions realize all these choices, including when both marks are in the same block.
Avoid \(i\) and \(c(i)\) when applicable, leaving at least \(b-2\) choices.
The resulting marked sets are moved, since they contain \(i\) and omit \(c(i)\).
Different sets give different vectors, and a sign change of the whole vector preserves its marks.
Thus \(k(x)\ge b-2\).

If instead \(c\) fixes every pair label of the term, its roots are all real or neutral.
The [real-place and orientation argument](Yin_C_G0901_v140.md#action) forces the independent signed case.
There is a block with a real pair, and the total number of real pair labels over all blocks is \(O(\sqrt{\deg z\log(3\deg z)})=o(b)\) uniformly at the fixed measure ceiling.
Transport a block with a mark to the indicated block, place one mark on a real label, and let the other range over neutral labels of its own block.
These give at least \(b-o(b)\) vectors with opposite signs of \(c\) on their two nonzero positions, so all are moved.
This completes the lower bound \(k(x)\gg b/u^2\) on every noncoordinate projection.

<a id="terminal"></a>
## Removing simultaneous coordinate projections

Uniformly for all attached terms of all inputs, \(b/u^2\) divided by \(\exp(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m))\) tends to infinity.
Indeed \(u\ll(\log(3\deg z))^{212}\); the [degree inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#minimal) using \(0<\log M(z)\le m\) works unchanged after subtracting another \(2\log u=O(\log\log(3\deg z))\) from \(\log b\).
Its limiting lower constant \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) is unchanged.
Assume \(k(x)\) below the threshold, so all active projections are coordinates.
The signed projected coordinates have [full labeled orbit](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#rank), even in an oriented leading space with possible lower summands.
Thus the number of pair labels moved by \(c\) at any attached term is at most \(k(x)=o(b)\), since each moved label gives moved coordinate lines.
In an oriented term no whole blocks can be exchanged.
On any block with positive sign the \(b-o(b)\) fixed labels would be real, contradicting the same \(o(b)\) bound on real labels.
Consequently all block signs there are negative; this makes the [actual half products torsion](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#oriented) and that term terminal.
Independent signed terms are already terminal.

If several active terms remain, take one of largest degree, denoted by \(z\), and any other \(z'\).
They are unmatched representatives.
Choose an initial conjugation sending the selected label of \(z'\) to a nonneutral pair, which exists by reciprocity and nontorsion.
If the label is moved by \(c\), its coordinate is already moved.
If it is fixed, the signed coordinate is real with positive sign of \(c\), since the two actual roots of that pair are real.
This sign assertion holds for the projected oriented coordinate as well because the projection is equivariant.
Now vary the selected label of \(z\) within its current block while fixing the splitting fields of all other active terms.
The [largest-degree relative alternating action](Yin_C_G0901_v87.md#statement) allows all \(b\) label positions, with arbitrary induced signs harmless here.
When the chosen label of \(z'\) is moved, all \(b\) resulting classes of \(x\) are moved.
When it is real, all but \(o(b)\) positions of \(z\) either are moved or have negative sign from a neutral pair, since only \(o(b)\) can be real.
In both cases the full vector cannot be fixed or negated by \(c\) on those positions.
The distinct label positions give distinct conjugate classes through the first projection.
This contradicts \(k(x)=o(b)\).
Thus the only active term is terminal and the given class is on its coordinate line.

<a id="values"></a>
## The consequence for integer polynomials

For \(A=M(P)\) at small positive \(m=\log A\), take as inputs the positive-measure irreducible factors after discarding zeros, using their roots with individual total log measures at most \(m\).
The value \(A\) is a real positive unit in their combined group.
If \(k(A)\) is below the threshold it matches the one terminal coordinate just found.
The oriented alternative above is impossible: on every block \(c\) has negative orientation sign and cannot fix any signed coordinate vector, whereas it fixes \([A]\).
The negatives of projected coordinates are distinct from all positive coordinates in an oriented block of size \(b\ge3\).
In the independent signed case the [factor decomposition at a coordinate match](Yin_C_G0901_v140.md#values) gives \(M(A)=A\) and makes \(A\) a real Salem number.
This proves the asserted dichotomy, including the case of unbounded second iterates.
For a reciprocal \(A\), inversion pairs nonreal interior and exterior conjugates bijectively, giving the factor of two.
