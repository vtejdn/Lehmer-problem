## Argument

### Complete trace identities and common periods

For odd n, the complete divisor product B_n obeys the exact symmetric Laurent identity

    B_n(z+z^(-1))=sum_(j=-(n-1)/2)^((n-1)/2)z^j.

Indeed the nontrivial nth roots are simple in characteristic 2.
Inversion pairs have distinct traces: if z+z^(-1)=w+w^(-1), then z,w are roots of the same quadratic, so w is z or z^(-1).
Their complete trace product is therefore monic of degree (n-1)/2.
Every symmetric Laurent polynomial has a unique expression in X=z+z^(-1), by triangular elimination of its highest pair of exponents.
The geometric sum has the displayed complete trace roots and leading degree (n-1)/2, giving the identity; the substitution F_2[X] into F_2[z,z^(-1)] is injective.
It follows that

    B_1=1, B_3=X+1, B_(n+2)=XB_n+B_(n-2).

For the companion matrix A=[[X,1],[1,0]] one has

    A^2=XA+I,
    A^64-I=X^32A^32,
    A^5-I=phi[phi A+(X+1)I].

The middle identity is obtained by five successive squarings of A^2-I=XA.
Cayley reduction gives A^5=(X^4+X^2+1)A+X^3I, and X^3+1=(X+1)phi, proving the last identity.
Raising it to the 128th power shows phi^128 divides A^640-I entrywise.
Also A^64=I modulo X^32, so A^640=I modulo X^32.
The factors are coprime, and therefore

    A^640=I modulo BOTH M and M_pre.

One matrix step advances the odd B index by 2, giving

    B_(n+1280)=B_n modulo BOTH M and M_pre

for every positive odd n.
Only a common period is needed; no minimal-period assertion is made.

For every odd n divisible by 5, B_n contains exactly one phi factor.
It is precisely the complete primitive-order-5 trace group, and the complete trace roots are simple.
If B_n=R+M_pre Q after whole-polynomial reduction, then phi divides R and
