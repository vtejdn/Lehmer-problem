# How small can all the integral coefficients of a band polynomial be on the circle?

<a id="statement"></a>
## An integral bound and an obstruction with resultant one

Let \(F(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^M A_j(X)Y^j\ne0\), where the \(A_j\) are integral Laurent polynomials supported in \([-d,d]\), \(d\ge1\).
Suppose that the nonzero \(A_j\) have no common nonunit factor in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Use normalized circle measure \(d\nu=dt/(2\pi)\), and set
\[
 B(t)=\max_j |A_j(e^{it})|,\qquad I=m_2(F),\qquad
 L_\pm=\int\log^\pm B(t)\,d\nu(t).
\]
Here \(\log^+ x=\max(0,\log x)\) and \(\log^- x=\max(0,-\log x)\).
The common-factor hypothesis implies \(B>0\) everywhere.
Then
\[
 L_-\ll_M\sqrt{d\bigl(I+\log(2d)\bigr)},\qquad
 L_+\le L_-+I+M\log2.                                             \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(L_-=o(d)\) if \(M\) is fixed and \(I=o(d)\).
For a row with common integral Laurent content \(H(X)\), the result applies to the quotient row after the common factor is divided out and the powers of \(X\) are recentered.
The full and diagonal Mahler measures each contain the same additive term \(m(H)\) whenever the diagonal specialization is nonzero.

Even for \(M=1\), there is no constant \(C\) such that \(L_-\le C(1+I)\) for every such row.
For each \(k\ge1\) we construct monic coprime cyclotomic products \(A,B_0\) with \(|\operatorname{Res}(A,B_0)|=1\) for which \(F=AY+B_0\) satisfies
\[
 I\ll\sqrt{k},\qquad
 L_-\ge \tfrac14\binom{2k-2}{k-1}-O(\sqrt{k}).                    \tag{2}
\]
The diagonal discrepancy with all integral coefficients varying remains open even though the size estimate \(L_-\le C(1+I)\) already fails.

<a id="section"></a>
## A squarefree integer linear combination

Multiply the entire row by a common power of \(X\) so all its exponents lie in \([0,s]\) with both \(0\) and \(s\) occupied somewhere, where \(s\le2d\).
This preserves all circle moduli.
The resulting ordinary polynomials have no common complex zero, since zero is excluded by the normalization and the minimal polynomial of a common nonzero zero would divide every occupied coefficient.
If \(s=0\), all their values are integers and \(L_-=0\).
A row with only one occupied coefficient satisfying the hypothesis reduces to this case.

Suppose \(s\ge1\).
Pick a polynomial \(P_0\) of degree \(s\) in the complex linear span of the row.
Some polynomial \(Q\) in that span is nonzero at every root of \(P_0\), since each root rules out only a proper hyperplane of coefficients.
The rational function \(P_0/Q\) is nonconstant, and its derivative numerator \(P_0'Q-P_0Q'\) is a nonzero polynomial in characteristic zero.
If \(P_0+\lambda Q\) has a repeated root \(\alpha\), coprimality gives \(Q(\alpha)\ne0\), the derivative numerator vanishes at \(\alpha\), and \(\lambda=-P_0(\alpha)/Q(\alpha)\).
Only finitely many choices of \(\lambda\) are excluded by this condition or by a drop in degree.
Thus the product of the leading coefficient and the degree-\(s\) discriminant of \(\sum_j c_j A_j\) is a nonzero polynomial in the parameters \(c_j\), of degree at most \(2s-1\).
For \(s=1\) take the discriminant to be one.
A polynomial of total degree at most \(2s-1\) vanishing on the full grid \(\{0,\ldots,2s-1\}^{M+1}\) vanishes identically, by univariate interpolation in each coordinate.
We may therefore choose integers \(c_j\) from this grid so that \(P=\sum_j c_j A_j\) has degree \(s\) and is squarefree.
With \(H=2s(M+1)\) this gives \(|P(e^{it})|\le H B(t)\).

For every \(t\), expansion into linear factors in \(Y\) gives \(B(t)\le2^M\exp m_Y(F(e^{it},Y))\), including fibers where the degree drops.
Jensen's formula and integration give
\[
 0\le m(P)\le I+M\log2+\log H,\qquad
 \int\log B\,d\nu\le I+M\log2.                                    \tag{3}
\]
Also \(I\ge0\), by applying the fiberwise Jensen lower bound for the leading occupied coefficient and its univariate integral Mahler measure.

<a id="energy"></a>
## From the discriminant to a circle logarithm

Write \(P=a\prod_{i=1}^s(X-\alpha_i)\), \(h_i=\max(1,|\alpha_i|)\), and \(w_i=\alpha_i/h_i^2\).
For any \(t\), the equality \(|e^{it}-\alpha_i|=h_i|1-w_i e^{-it}|\) identifies \(\log|P(e^{it})|-m(P)\) with \(\sum_i\log|1-w_i e^{-it}|\) almost everywhere.
We use the [reflected-root discriminant identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#energy) for this entire squarefree polynomial.
Indeed
\[
 |1-w_i\overline{w_j}|\ge |\alpha_i-\alpha_j|/(h_i h_j).
\]
For opposite sides of the unit circle this is equality.
For two interior roots it follows by subtracting the squared distance from the squared left side, obtaining \((1-|w_i|^2)(1-|w_j|^2)\ge0\).
For two exterior roots the normalized distance is \(|w_i-w_j|\), so the same calculation applies.
For \(0<q<1\) and \(|v|\le1\), the identity \(|1-qv|^2=q|1-v|^2+(1-q)(1-q|v|^2)\) bounds the loss from shrinking each off-diagonal product.
Expansion of the logarithmic kernel therefore gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{q^\ell}{\ell}\left|\sum_i w_i^\ell\right|^2
 &=-\sum_{i,j}\log|1-q w_i\overline{w_j}|\\
 &\le s\log(1/(1-q))-\tfrac12s(s-1)\log q
      +2(s-1)m(P)-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|.                     \tag{4}
\end{split}
\]
The coefficient of \(m(P)\) includes the contribution of \(|a|^{2s-2}\) in the discriminant, since the sum over distinct indices counts each unordered pair twice.
The last term is nonpositive by squarefreeness and integrality.
Take \(q=1-1/(2s)\) and \(\rho=\sqrt q\).
The right side is \(O(s(m(P)+\log(2s)))\).
Parseval applied to \(\sum_i\log|1-\rho w_i e^{-it}|\) gives squared \(L^2\)-norm \(\tfrac12\sum_{\ell\ge1}q^\ell|\sum_i w_i^\ell|^2/\ell^2\), bounded by half the left side of (4).

Removing the smoothing costs \(O(1)\) in \(L^1\) for the whole polynomial.
For \(|w|\le1\), the two logarithms \(\log|1-w e^{-it}|\) and \(\log|1-\rho w e^{-it}|\) have mean zero.
The positive part of their difference is at most \(\log(2/(1+\rho))\), since the maximum of \(|(1-z)/(1-\rho z)|\) on the closed unit disk is \(2/(1+\rho)\), attained at \(-1\).
This maximum follows by taking the squared modulus on the circle and comparing the resulting ratio as a function of \(\cos t\).
Twice the positive-part integral is the \(L^1\) distance, so the sum over roots costs \(O(s(1-\rho))=O(1)\).
Consequently
\[
 \int\big|\log|P(e^{it})|-m(P)\big|\,d\nu
       \ll 1+\sqrt{s(m(P)+\log(2s))}.
\]
Use \(m(P)\ge0\) and \(B\ge|P|/H\) to bound \(L_-\) by this last quantity plus \(\log H\).
Substituting (3), using \(s\le2d\), and absorbing \(\log(2d)\ll\sqrt{d\log(2d)}\) proves the first part of (1).
Its second part follows by adding \(\int\log B\,d\nu\) to \(L_-\).

<a id="example"></a>
## A coprime pair with exponentially large negative logarithm

Choose \(2k\) distinct primes and partition them into sets \(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\) of size \(k\).
Let \(N\) be their product and put
\[
 U=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(X^{N/p}-1),\qquad
 V=\prod_{p\in\mathcal Q}(X^{N/p}-1),\qquad
 G=\gcd(U,V),\quad A=U/G,\quad B_0=V/G
\]
with \(G\) monic.
For a divisor \(t\mid N\) let \(i,j\) be the numbers of primes missing from \(t\) in \(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\), respectively.
The multiplicities of \(\Phi_t\) in \(A,B_0\) are \((i-j)_+,(j-i)_+\).
Thus a divisor index from one product and an index from the other differ in at least two prime coordinates.
At a prime \(\ell\) the roots of the reduction of \(\Phi_t\) have exact order equal to the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(t\).
For \(\ell\nmid t\) this follows from separability of \(X^t-1\) and its cyclotomic factorization.
For \(\ell\mid t\), writing \(t=\ell t'\) with \(\ell\nmid t'\), the identity \(\Phi_{\ell t'}(X)=\Phi_{t'}(X^\ell)/\Phi_{t'}(X)\) gives the same roots as \(\Phi_{t'}\) on reduction.
Deleting a single prime coordinate cannot make the two divisor indices agree.
The two monic polynomials have no common root modulo any prime, so their integer resultant has absolute value one.

Set \(u(t)=\log|A(e^{it})|\), \(v(t)=\log|B_0(e^{it})|\).
Both functions have mean zero and \(I=m_2(AY+B_0)=\int\max(u,v)\,d\nu=\tfrac12\|u-v\|_1\).
Their difference is a signed sum of \(2k\) functions \(h_a(t)=\log|1-e^{iat}|\), with \(a=N/p\) over the distinct primes \(p\).
The cosine series for the logarithm gives
\[
 \langle h_a,h_b\rangle=\frac{\pi^2}{12}\frac{\gcd(a,b)^2}{ab}.
\]
Indeed only the common multiples of \(a,b\) contribute to the inner product, and the logarithm series converges in \(L^2\) by its square-summable coefficients, or by radial smoothing.
Distinct prime indices \(p,q\) therefore contribute absolute inner product \(\pi^2/(12pq)\).
The diagonal terms sum to \(O(k)\), and \((\sum_{p\mid N}1/p)^2\le2k\sum_{m\ge2}m^{-2}=O(k)\).
This gives \(\|u-v\|_1\le\|u-v\|_2=O(\sqrt{k})\).

Use the normalized Fourier coefficient \(\widehat u(1)=\int u(t)e^{-it}\,d\nu(t)\).
The identity \(\log|\Phi_t(e^{ix})|=\sum_{b\mid t}\mu(t/b)\log|1-e^{ibx}|\) shows that its frequency-one coefficient is \(-\mu(t)/2\), where \(\mu\) is the Möbius function.
Since \(\mu(t)=(-1)^{i+j}\), we obtain
\[
 -2\widehat u(1)=\sum_{i,j=0}^k(-1)^{i+j}\binom{k}{i}\binom{k}{j}(i-j)_+
   =\sum_{l=1}^k(-1)^l l\binom{2k}{k+l}
   =-\binom{2k-2}{k-1}.
\]
The middle identity groups the terms by \(l=i-j\) and uses Vandermonde's identity.
For the last one use \(l\binom{2k}{k+l}=k[\binom{2k-1}{k+l-1}-\binom{2k-1}{k+l}]\) and the alternating binomial tail identity \(\sum_{j=h}^m(-1)^j\binom mj=(-1)^h\binom{m-1}{h-1}\), taking out-of-range binomial coefficients to be zero.
Since \(u\) has mean zero, its negative-part integral equals \(\|u\|_1/2\ge|\widehat u(1)|/2\).
Passing from \(u\) to \(\max(u,v)\) costs at most \(\|u-v\|_1\) in the negative-part integral.
This proves (2).
The total \(X\)-width grows in this construction, and it remains open here whether bounded \(I\) alone permits divergent \(L_-\).
