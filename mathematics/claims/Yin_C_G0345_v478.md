# Half-mass powers of the Littlewood Salem numbers below a multinacci limit

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least exponent for the actual Salem factor

Fix integers \(m\ge3,\ n\ge1\), put \(N=2mn+1\), and use \(f_m=X^{m-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}X^j\) and \(f_m^*=X^{m-1}f_m(1/X)\).
The reciprocal Littlewood polynomial
\[
 A_{m,n}=X^{2mn}+f_m^*\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}X^{mj}
\]
is the lower sequence displayed by [Borwein, Dobrowolski and Mossinghoff](../audits/literature/BDM07/Yin_U001.md#lower-littlewood-sections).
It has a unique exterior root \(\sigma_{m,n}\), which is a Salem number.
For fixed \(m\) these roots strictly increase to the unique exterior root \(\beta_m\) of \(f_m\).
In particular the complete multiplicative Mahler measures of both \(A_{m,n}\) and the minimal polynomial of \(\sigma_{m,n}\) are \(\sigma_{m,n}\).

Define
\[
 \mathcal D_{m,N}=\{d>1:d\mid N,\ d\nmid m-1,\ 2d\nmid m-2\}.
\]
For the [half-branch-mass condition on a power of a Salem input](Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#statement), an exponent \(k\ge1\) is admissible for \(\sigma_{m,n}\) exactly when some \(d\in\mathcal D_{m,N}\) with \(d\ge5\) divides \(k\).
Thus its least admissible exponent is \(\min\{d\in\mathcal D_{m,N}:d\ge5\}\).
If \(N\) is prime, then \(A_{m,n}\) is irreducible of degree \(N-1\), and the admissible exponents are precisely the multiples of \(N\).
For \(m=3\) this gives infinitely many distinct Salem inputs of unbounded degree and full measure below the golden ratio, with least admissible exponent equal to the degree plus one.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Counting all exterior roots

The identity \(X f_m-f_m^*=X^m-1\) gives
\[
 F_N=X^N f_m-f_m^*=(X^m-1)A_{m,n}.
\]
The polynomial \(F_N\) is anti-reciprocal, so its monic quotient \(A_{m,n}\) is reciprocal of degree \(N-1\) and constant term one.
Each length-\(m\) block of \(A_{m,n}\) is \(f_m^*\), with one further leading coefficient \(1\).
The [multinacci root count](Yin_C_G0021_v1.md#multinacci-local-roots-irreducibility-and-measure) gives exactly one root \(\beta_m\in(1,2)\) of \(f_m\) outside the closed unit disk and all remaining roots in the open disk.
On the unit circle \(|X^N f_m|=|f_m^*|>0\).
By Rouché's theorem \(X^N f_m-\lambda f_m^*\) has exactly one exterior root, counted with multiplicity, for \(0\le\lambda<1\).
The leading coefficient stays one, so continuity gives at most one exterior root at \(\lambda=1\).
Now \(F_N(1)=0,\ F_N'(1)=-N(m-2)+2(m-1)<0\), whereas \(F_N(\beta_m)=-f_m^*(\beta_m)>0\).
Hence \(F_N\), and thus \(A_{m,n}\), has one simple exterior root \(\sigma_{m,n}\in(1,\beta_m)\).
Reciprocity gives exactly one interior root, its inverse.
Every monic irreducible factor has unit constant term, so the factor containing \(\sigma_{m,n}\) also contains its inverse.
If these were its only roots, their sum would be an integer strictly between \(2\) and \(5/2\).
It therefore has a unit-circle conjugate, as required for a Salem number.
Kronecker's theorem makes every remaining factor cyclotomic.

At \(x=\sigma_{m,n}\) we have \(f_m(x)<0\), hence \(F_{N+2m}(x)=F_N(x)+(x^{N+2m}-x^N)f_m(x)<0\).
The positive value at \(\beta_m\) then places the next exterior root strictly above the previous one.
For each \(1<y<\beta_m\), \(F_N(y)\to-\infty\) as \(n\to\infty\), which proves the asserted limit.

<a id="factors"></a>
## All cyclotomic cofactors and all residue orders

Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\overline A_{m,n}=(X^N-1)/(X-1)\) is squarefree since \(N\) is odd.
It has exactly the nonidentity \(N\)-th roots of unity.
If \(\Phi_{2^a d}\) divides \(A_{m,n}\), with \(d\) odd and \(a\ge0\), its reduction is \(\overline\Phi_d^{\,\varphi(2^a)}\), whose roots have exact order \(d\).
Thus \(d>1,\ d\mid N\), and squarefreeness forces \(a=0\) or \(1\).
For either possible conductor a primitive complex root \(\xi\) satisfies \(\xi^m\ne1\), since \(\gcd(N,m)=1\).
For conductor \(d\) we have \(\xi^N=1\), so the vanishing condition is \((f_m-f_m^*)(\xi)=2(\xi^{m-1}-1)=0\), equivalent to \(d\mid m-1\).
For conductor \(2d\) we have \(\xi^N=-1\), so the condition is \((f_m+f_m^*)(\xi)=-2\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\xi^j=0\), equivalent to \(2d\mid m-2\).
The two conditions cannot hold at the same \(d>1\) since \(\gcd(m-1,m-2)=1\).
Every such factor occurs to exponent one by squarefreeness.
Consequently the monic Salem minimal polynomial is exactly
\[
 P_{m,n}=A_{m,n}\bigg/
 \left(\prod_{\substack{1<d\mid N\\d\mid m-1}}\Phi_d
       \prod_{\substack{1<d\mid N\\2d\mid m-2}}\Phi_{2d}\right).
\]
For each \(d\in\mathcal D_{m,N}\) its reduction retains all \(\varphi(d)\) primitive roots of order \(d\), each once.
There are no other residue roots.
In the [residue-order criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#statement) the weights are therefore \(w_d=\varphi(d)/2\) on this set and zero elsewhere, in particular \(w_1=0\).
Among odd \(d>1\) only \(d=3\) has weight one, so an admissible power must meet a surviving order \(d\ge5\), and each such divisor suffices.
The set of these orders contains \(N\) since \(N>m\).

<a id="primes"></a>
## Prime sections below the golden ratio

When \(N\) is prime, both products are empty and \(\mathcal D_{m,N}=\{N\}\).
For each fixed \(m\) there are infinitely many primes \(N\equiv1\bmod 2m\).
Indeed, given any finite list, choose a large positive multiple \(t\) of \(2m\) and of every listed prime.
Any prime divisor \(\ell\) of \(\Phi_{2m}(t)>1\) is coprime to \(t\) since the constant term is one.
In particular \(\ell\nmid2m\), so squarefreeness of \(X^{2m}-1\) modulo \(\ell\) gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(t)=2m\) and \(\ell\equiv1\bmod 2m\).
This supplies a prime outside the list.
For \(m=3\) the smallest prime section is \(N=7\), and \(\beta_3=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
Along the prime sections the minimal log measure of an admissible power is \(N\log\sigma_{3,n}\), and its ratio to \(N\) tends to \(\log\beta_3>0\).
This obstructs uniform control of the powered measure by a bounded exponent even in a bounded interval of actual Salem measures.
