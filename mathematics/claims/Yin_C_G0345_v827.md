# An irreducibility test from the critical integers and the full trace discriminant

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cost of every rational splitting

Consider the [two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) in positive primitive directions, with \(W=\deg P=2l+4k\).
For squarefree \(P\) write \(P(X)=X^{W/2}Q(X+X^{-1})\), and let \(\Delta=|\operatorname{disc}Q|\).
Put \(b(n)=\prod_p p^{2\lfloor v_p(n)/2\rfloor}\), the largest square divisor of \(n\).
With the [exceptional form](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) \(B(k,l)\ne0\), take the [eight integers](Yin_C_G0345_v823.md#statement) \(s_j\) of the full section, and \(e(s_j)\) as defined there.
Form the integer
\[
 U=\gcd\left(b(\Delta),\ b\left(\prod_{p\mid B}p^{v_p(\Delta)}\right)
                      \prod_{j=1}^8 e(s_j)\right).
\]
On every sequence with \(k\to\infty\) and \(l/k\to\infty\), all sufficiently late reducible sections satisfy
\[
                 \log U\gg l\frac{(\log\log l)^4}{(\log l)^3}.                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular little-oh growth relative to the displayed scale certifies eventual irreducibility on the sequence.
Both \(b(\Delta)\) alone and the integer obtained by applying the [individual good-orbit square test](Yin_C_G0345_v823.md#descent) in the definition of \(U\) give valid versions of this criterion.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## An upper divisor and a lower bound uniform up to half the section

For a monic reciprocal splitting \(P=SM\) into palindromic even-degree factors, let \(Q_S,Q_M\) be their monic trace polynomials, so \(Q=Q_SQ_M\).
The two preimages of a trace root have product one, giving \(R=|\operatorname{Res}(S,M)|=|\operatorname{Res}(Q_S,Q_M)|^2\).
Thus \(R\) is a square dividing \(\Delta\) by the discriminant product formula.
Combining this upper divisor with the eight-integer bound at primes outside \(B\) gives \(R\mid U\).

On a reducible steep sequence take \(S\) a rational irreducible factor of smallest degree \(d\le W/2=l+2k\).
All factors are eventually reciprocal with no roots at the endpoints, and the [mean derivative and Hadamard estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#derivative), with the [individual height law](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement), give uniformly
\[
     d^{-1}\log R\ge\log(l/d)+L-Cd/l+o(1).
\]
Indeed \(|\operatorname{disc} S|\le d^d\exp(2(d-1)m(S))\), while \(m(S)=(C/2+o(1))d/l\), with \(C,L\) as in the mean derivative estimate.
On the unit circle \(|A|=|(t-3)(t-4)|\ge12\) since \(t=U-U^{-1}\) is purely imaginary there.
Hence \(L-C\ge\tfrac12\log575\), and \(d/l\le1+o(1)\) shows that eventually \(\log R\ge d(2+\log(l/d))\).
The latter function of \(d\) is increasing up to \(l+2k\) eventually.
The [uniform degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v811.md#degrees) is \(d\ge c l(\log\log l/\log l)^3\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
At this lower endpoint the logarithm \(\log(l/d)\) is asymptotic to \(3\log\log l\), proving (1), including when \(d\) is comparable to \(l\).

<a id="finite"></a>
## A test on one section

Let \(l\ge2k\), assume squarefreeness, and use an upper bound \(U_0=U\) or \(U_0=b(\Delta)\), the latter requiring no exceptional-form check.
For each even integer \(2\le d\le W/2\) and each inverse root pair \(\{z,z^{-1}\}\), choose \(|z|\ge1\) and compute
\[
              J_d(z)=|P'(z)P'(z^{-1})|\,|z|^{-2(d-1)} .
\]
If for every such \(d\) the product of the \(d/2\) smallest pair values exceeds \(d^d U_0\), then \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed inversion permutes the irreducible factors up to monic normalization.
A [simple circle root](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) forces its factor to be fixed by inversion, since its inverse is its complex conjugate.
There are no roots at \(\pm1\), so on a reducible section the inversion orbits give at least two blocks, each with monic palindromic product of even degree.
The smallest block gives a reciprocal factor \(S\) of even degree \(d\le W/2\).
The identity \(R\,|\operatorname{disc}S|=\prod_{S(z)=0}|P'(z)|\) and the same Hadamard bound give \(d^d R\ge\prod_{\{z,z^{-1}\}\subset S} J_d(z)\).
This contradicts the strict test since \(R\le U_0\).

For example, at \((k,l)=(1,5)\) the trace polynomial is
\[
 Q(Y)=Y^7-7Y^6+3Y^5+49Y^4-35Y^3-91Y^2+40Y+29.
\]
Its absolute discriminant is \(19\cdot3137\cdot343830539219\), a product of distinct primes (trial division through the respective square roots suffices), so \(U_0=1\).
Direct substitution gives opposite endpoint signs in each interval \([j,j+1]/10^4\) for
\[
 j\in\{-19384,-14042,-4222,8544,18308,36078,44714\}.
\]
These intervals contain all seven trace roots, in order.
For a trace root \(y=z+z^{-1}\) the pair derivative product is \(|4-y^2|\,|Q'(y)|^2\).
Bounding the latter derivative by the product of the six interval separations gives lower bounds \(22000,25000,27000,20000,10000,2000000,36000000\) for the seven pair derivative products, respectively.
The first five pairs have modulus one, and the exterior moduli in the last two pairs are below \(3.31,4.24\), respectively.
For \(d=2,4\) all pair values exceed \(100\), whereas for \(d=6\) they all exceed \(10\) and five exceed \(10000\).
The three tests therefore pass, proving irreducibility of this degree-fourteen section.
