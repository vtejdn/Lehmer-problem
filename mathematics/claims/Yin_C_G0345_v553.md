# Rational factors selected by a common dyadic residue at two cubic offsets

<a id="statement"></a>
## The paired factors

Put \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\) and \(F_j(X)=g(X^a)+X^jB(X^a)\), where \(s\ge3\) and \(a,j\) are positive integers.
Fix \(b\ge1\) with \(as+b\) odd and set \(H_j=-F_j/(X+1)\) for \(j=b,b+2\).
These are monic integer polynomials, and their common integral quotient is
\[
 \mathbb Z[X]/(H_b,H_{b+2})\simeq
 \begin{cases}
  0,&s\equiv0,1\pmod3,\\
  \mathbb F_2[X]/(X^{2a}+X^a+1),&s\equiv2\pmod3.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Their absolute resultant is respectively \(1\) or \(2^{2a}\).

When \(s\equiv2\pmod3\) and \(a=3^t\), \(t\ge0\), each \(H_j\) has exactly one monic rational irreducible factor \(P_j\) whose reduction modulo two is divisible by \(X^{2a}+X^a+1\).
Each \(P_j\) occurs with multiplicity one in its parent, and \(|\operatorname{Res}(P_b,P_{b+2})|=2^{2a}\).
Every other pair of rational irreducible factors from opposite parents has absolute resultant one.
If \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,b+2)=1\) and \(2a>22s\), both selected factors are noncyclotomic.
In particular this selects two nontorsion factors of degrees at least \(2a\) on every cubic pair \(b=as^3-1,\ b+2=as^3+1\) satisfying these conditions on \(s,a\).
These specified factors can be tested against the [criterion for small degree share](Yin_C_G0345_v548.md#statement); their normalized degrees may still stay bounded away from zero.

<a id="quotient"></a>
## The integral intersection at offsets two apart

The coefficients of \(F_j\) are symmetric about degree \((as+j)/2\) because \(B(T)=T^s g(1/T)\).
Thus odd \(as+j\) gives \(F_j(-1)=0\).
Its constant and leading coefficients are both \(-1\), and \(F_j(1)=-2\), so \(H_j(0)=H_j(1)=1\).
In \(\mathbb Z[X]/(H_b)\) both \(X\) and \(X-1\) are units, by division of \(H_b\) by each linear polynomial.
The identity
\[
 H_{b+2}-H_b=-X^b(X-1)B(X^a)
\]
therefore gives \((H_b,H_{b+2})=(H_b,B(X^a))\).
Since \(B(1)=-1\) and \(B(-1)=1\), division of \(B(X^a)\) by \(X+1\) makes \(X+1\) invertible modulo \(B(X^a)\).
Multiplying \(H_b\) by \(-(X+1)\) now gives \((H_b,B(X^a))=(g(X^a),B(X^a))\).

For the [seed identity](Yin_C_G0345_v549.md#change), write \(C(T)=T^2+T+1\).
The equation \(TB+(1+T)g=-C\) puts \(C(X^a)\) in the last ideal.
Modulo \(C(X^a)\) the element \(T=X^a\) has \(T^3=1\), so is invertible and the two seed generators generate the same ideal as \(g\) alone.
There \(g=T^s+T^2\) equals \(-T,-1,2T^2\) according as \(s\equiv0,1,2\pmod3\).
This proves (1).
Multiplication by \(H_{b+2}\) on the free integral power basis modulo the monic \(H_b\) has cokernel (1) and determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(H_b,H_{b+2})\).
Its absolute determinant equals the indicated finite cardinality, with the zero ring contributing cardinality one.

<a id="selection"></a>
## One common residue factor exhausts the resultant

For \(a=3^t\) the polynomial \(X^{2a}+X^a+1=\Phi_{3^{t+1}}(X)\) has as roots in characteristic two exactly the elements of order \(3^{t+1}\).
The residue Frobenius orbit length of each is the order of two modulo \(3^{t+1}\), which equals \(2\cdot3^t\).
Indeed an admissible exponent is even, say \(2m\); writing \(m=3^e u\) with \(3\nmid u\), one has \(v_3(4^m-1)=1+e\).
The exponent \(u\) preserves \(v_3(4-1)=1\) by the geometric sum, and each subsequent cubing adds one because \(z^2+z+1\equiv3\pmod9\) for \(z\equiv1\pmod3\).
This gives order two also for \(t=0\).
The orbit exhausts the degree, giving an irreducible polynomial \(D\) of degree \(2a\) and the field of order \(2^{2a}\) in (1).

Both parents reduce to zero modulo \((2,D)\), so some monic irreducible rational factor on each side reduces to a multiple of \(D\) by Gauss's lemma and irreducibility over \(\mathbb F_2\).
For any such pair \(P,Q\) the finite integral quotient \(\mathbb Z[X]/(P,Q)\) maps onto that field.
Consequently \(2^{2a}\) divides \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)|\) by the same determinant computation; finiteness follows because the parent resultant is nonzero.
Multiplicativity over all pairs, including multiplicities, exhausts the parent resultant with this pair, proving the selection, simplicity and unit resultants asserted above.
Finally, for each primitive exponent pair the [torsion-point estimate for the seed curve](Yin_C_G0345_v545.md#torsion) gives at most \(22s\) distinct torsion roots: the irreducible curve has Newton area \(s\) and \(x\mapsto(x^a,x^j)\) is injective by coprimality.
That argument over characteristic zero covers every residue class of \(s\).
Since the reduction divisibility gives \(\deg P,\deg Q\ge2a\), neither can be cyclotomic at \(2a>22s\).
On the two cubic offsets the exponents are each coprime to \(a\), and \(a(s^3+s)\) is even, giving the required odd parent degrees.
