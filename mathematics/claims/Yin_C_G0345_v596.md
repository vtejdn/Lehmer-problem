# Primitive terminal patterns at strict records having a neutral conjugate

<a id="statement"></a>
## Fixed rational coefficients with no restriction on the other conjugates

Let nontorsion algebraic units \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\) realize [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement) for positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\to0\).
Assume each has at least one conjugate on the unit circle.
Write \(L=\log(1/m)\), \(T=\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil\), and \(N=\lfloor T\log(2)/(4m)\rfloor\).
For each sufficiently late input there exist \(3\le r\le j\le T+1\) and one ordered tuple of nonzero rational integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1}\) with the following property.
There are at least \(V\) distinct sorted integer exponent lists after division by their positive gcd, marked at the exponent zero, where
\[
 r\gg (L/\log(2+L))^{1/4},\qquad
 \frac{\log V}{r-2}\ge L-O\!\left(1+L^{3/4}(\log(2+L))^{1/4}\right).
\]
Each list occurs before division with the same ordered outgoing coefficients, a nonzero rational integer output and [full rational terminality](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
The coefficients, outputs and exponents before division meet the [finite witness bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#statement) for this \(j,N\).
An absolute exponential upper bound for primitive terminal patterns with fixed outgoing rational coefficients would therefore exclude every shrinking sequence of strict records with a neutral conjugate, regardless of the numbers of expanding and contracting conjugates.
The arity and tuple are selected by a count over complete splitting trees and can differ from the ones maximizing the raw number of lists.

<a id="edge"></a>
## What the second-largest child controls

Choose \(j\) as in the [finite support estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale), giving at least \((c/m)^{j-1}\) translation classes of minimal ternary supports in the original interval for an absolute \(c>0\).
For each support fix signs and completely split its relation using all rational coefficients and arbitrary integer exponents.
Root its [tree](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees) at the original leaf of least exponent, and order the outgoing edges at every internal vertex by exponent.
An edge leading to \(u\) original leaves represents, up to sign, the sum of those signed powers in the original coordinates, as in the [cut identity](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#edges).
Its rational coefficient is integral since the cut sum and the inverse of the edge power are algebraic integers.
Evaluating at the neutral conjugate gives the stronger estimate \(|a|\le u\), since every power at that conjugate, including the one removed from the cut sum, has modulus one.

At an internal vertex of degree \(s\ge4\), let \(b_v\ge1\) denote the second-largest number of original leaves below any of its \(s-1\) children.
Terminality forces the outgoing exponents to be distinct, nonzero and different from the marked exponent zero by the [two-term test](Yin_C_G0345_v585.md#rays).
Fix a primitive list \((w_i)\) and its outgoing coefficients, which are known once the child subtrees have been constructed.
The neutral conjugate and its complex conjugate are reciprocal images, so the minimal polynomial is reciprocal.
If the Laurent polynomial \(F(X)=\sum_i a_iX^{w_i}\) is asymmetric under inversion, the [evaluation determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v590.md#affine) gives \(O(s^2\log(2s)/m)\) positive dilations with rational output.
If it is symmetric, every coefficient is repeated on the opposite exponent, and every pair has a child with at most \(b_v\) leaves.
Hence \(\max_i|a_i|\le b_v\), even if the largest child contains nearly all original leaves.
At every positive realization \(q\) the integral output \(c_q=F(\alpha^q)\) has \(|c_q|\le(s-1)b_v\) at the neutral conjugate.
Clearing negative powers in \(F(X)-c_q\) yields a nonzero integral polynomial of Mahler measure at most \(2(s-1)b_v\) by its norm on the unit circle.
It contains the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^q\), whose logarithmic measure is \(qm\) by [preservation of degree at strict records](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm).
Thus at the vertex every shape has at most \(D_v=C m^{-1}s^3 b_v\) positive dilations for an absolute \(C\), in either symmetry case, whenever the child sizes and fixed coefficients are compatible with the tree.

<a id="tree"></a>
## Summing the losses over the tree

For every internal vertex, including degree three, define \(b_v\) by the same child-size rule.
For \(1\le t\le j\), consider the subtree of vertices below the root leaf whose descendant leaf count is at least \(t\), with the edge toward the root as the parent direction.
This is empty or is connected with a unique vertex nearest the root.
Its terminal vertices have disjoint original leaf sets, each of size at least \(t\), so their number is at most \(j/t\).
The vertices with \(b_v\ge t\) each have at least two children in this subtree and hence are at most \(j/t\) in number.
Integration of this count against \(dt/t\) from one to infinity gives \(\sum_v\log b_v\le j\).
Also \(\sum_v(s_v-2)=j-2\), and consequently \(\sum_v s_v\le3(j-2)\).

Put \(R=\lfloor a(L/\log(2+L))^{1/4}\rfloor\), with a sufficiently small absolute \(a>0\).
For \(3\le s\le R\) the [uniform raw count](Yin_C_G0345_v589.md#statement) at degree bound one gives at most \(\exp((s-2)L/2)\) arrays per outgoing coefficient tuple, since \(s^5\log(2s)/(s-2)\le 3s^4\log(2s)\).
For \(s>R\) let \(V_s\) be the largest number of primitive marked lists for one ordered integer coefficient tuple in the height, output and exponent windows for \(j,N\), with full rational terminality.
These are finite maxima, and the [finite reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#count) puts every vertex realization inside those windows.
Set \(X=\max\big(\{L/2\}\cup\{\log\max(1,V_s)/(s-2):R<s\le j\}\big)\).
At each large vertex, for fixed child subtrees and the given plane tree, the number of choices is at most \(V_s D_v\), since the preceding bounds apply to all compatible completions, even though the output coefficient may depend on the dilation.
The number of large vertices is at most \((j-2)/(R-2)\).
Thus for a fixed tree and fixed leaf signs the total number of reconstructed supports is at most
\[
 \exp\big((j-2)X+(j-2)L/(R-2)+O(j)\big).
\]
Here the terms \(\log C+3\log s_v+\log b_v\) in the dilation cost sum to \(O(j)\) by the two tree estimates, uniformly in the degrees and coefficients.
The number of plane trees and sign assignments is at most \(32^j\) by the same reconstruction.
Comparison with the initial \((c/m)^{j-1}\) supports gives \(X\ge L-L/(R-2)-O(1)\).
Eventually this exceeds \(L/2\), so the maximum is attained at some \(r>R\) and a single coefficient tuple realizing \(V_r\).
This yields the stated estimates.
