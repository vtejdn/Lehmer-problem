# A height cost for unrestricted coefficients on powers of a progression

<a id="statement"></a>
## Growth beyond the critical width

Fix integers \(D\ge2,\ T\ge1\) and \(0<\delta<1\).
Take \(n\ge0,\ b,h_0\ge1\) integers, and write \(n/b=x/y\) in lowest terms with \(y>0\).
Put \(H=\max(x,y)\).
Consider a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial supported, up to translation, on
\[
                           h_0(n+bj)^D,\qquad 0\le j\le T .
\]
Let \(W\) be the span of the actual exponents.
For every reciprocal nontorsion algebraic-unit root \(\alpha\) of this polynomial, with absolute height \(h(\alpha)\), one has
\[
                    H^{\,1-\delta}\ \ll_{D,T,\delta}\ 1+W h(\alpha).          \tag{1}
\]
There is no restriction on the coefficients or the common dilation.
In particular at large \(H\) each such irreducible factor of degree \(d\) has logarithmic Mahler measure \(\gg_{D,T,\delta} d H^{1-\delta}/W\).

There is also a relative small-value test on the original embeddings for complex coefficients.
Fix a fraction \(0<\eta\le1\).
There are \(K,M\) depending only on \(T,\eta\) such that the following holds for any reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with \(\min_{j\ge1}\deg(\alpha^j)>M\).
Use any nonzero complex polynomial on the indicated support, shifted to have minimum exponent zero and normalized to coefficient norm one.
Put \(d=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\) and write \(v_\sigma\) for the unscaled monomial row at \(\sigma\alpha\).
If at least \(\eta d\) embeddings satisfy
\[
       |Q(\sigma\alpha)|\le \|v_\sigma\|_2\exp[-K(1+W h(\alpha))],
\]
then (1) holds with a constant also depending on \(\eta\).
Along a sequence of shrinking positive total measures, the minimum power degree condition holds eventually.
For such inputs satisfying this density test or the integer factor condition, \(H\to\infty\).
Consequently even \(1+Wh(\alpha)=o(H^{1-\delta})\) is excluded, allowing widths larger than the critical scale.
The [fixed-coefficient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v757.md#statement) gives an unconditional factor gap at every width for bounded integer coefficients.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Replacing the coefficient vector by a relation of controlled height

In the integer case choose a minimally rationally dependent subset of the powers of \(\alpha\) on the actual support.
There is such a subset because the original polynomial vanishes.
After a common shift, the powers corresponding to all proper subsets are rationally independent.
If the chosen size is \(p\), their conjugate evaluation matrix has rank \(p-1\) over the algebraic closure.
Indeed writing field elements in a rational basis and applying the square embedding matrix of that basis preserves matrix rank, by separability.
Its one-dimensional kernel has a rational generator with all coordinates nonzero and one coordinate normalized to one.
Cramer's rule on \(p-1\) independent rows gives the remaining entries as ratios of minors.
The [absolute minor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#minors) in the form \(h(\Delta)\le\log(k!)+kWh(\alpha)\) for size \(k\) bounds their heights by \(O_T(1+Wh(\alpha))\).
Here and below one can shift the selected exponents by their minimum before computing a minor.
A two-element relation would make a nonzero power of \(\alpha\) rational and equal to its inverse by reciprocity, contrary to nontorsion.

For the complex test use the [critical subspace argument](Yin_C_G0345_v740.md#blocks) with its current value \(C=Wh(\alpha)\).
For each actual term count \(r\le T+1\), its threshold is \(r^{-1}\exp(-1-(\log(r!)+2rC+(r/2)\log r)(2/\eta)^r)\).
The common choice \(\exp(-K(1+C))\) can be made smaller than all these thresholds and smaller than one.
The bound \(M\) on the subfield degree in that argument depends on term count and density alone.
It supplies an exact relation with coefficients in a field \(E\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) of degree at most \(M\), of heights \(O_T(1+Wh(\alpha))\).
Choose an inclusion-minimal nonempty vanishing subsum of that relation.
A two-element subsum would put a nonzero power in \(E\), excluded by the power degree hypothesis.
A monomial is impossible in both cases.
Thus in either case there are \(p\ge3\) distinct original positions, a minimally vanishing subsum with bounded coefficient heights as above, and a bounded-degree coefficient field \(E\), with \(E=\mathbb Q\) for the integer case.
Only the selected subsum is used from now on.

<a id="direction"></a>
## Size and common divisor of the specialization direction

Put \(w_j=(j,j^2,\ldots,j^D)\).
Fix a selected index \(j_0\) and use the lattice generated by all selected differences \(w_j-w_{j_0}\), of rank \(q=\min(D,p-1)\) by the Vandermonde determinant.
Choose a basis once for each possible subset.
In the resulting coordinates, the support spans the full \(q\)-space and both its coordinate widths and its degree after removing a monomial are bounded solely in terms of \(D,T\).
Factoring out \(h_0\gcd(n,b)^D y\) from the projected exponent differences gives the homomorphism on this lattice defined by dot product with
\[
                      U_k=\binom Dk x^{D-k}y^{k-1},\qquad 1\le k\le D.
\]
Let \(v\) be its values on the chosen basis, divided by their positive gcd \(c\).
The torus point at which the lifted polynomial vanishes is \((\xi^{v_1},\ldots,\xi^{v_q})\) with \(\xi=\alpha^{h_0\gcd(n,b)^D y c}\) a nontorsion unit.
The original actual exponents remain distinct.

The integer \(c\) is bounded by a constant depending only on \(D,T\).
It divides the values of the two integer homogeneous forms
\[
            L_j(X,Y)=\frac{(X+jY)^D-(X+j_0Y)^D}{Y}
\]
for two different selected indices \(j\ne j_0\).
The two forms have no common projective zero over the complex numbers.
At \(Y=0\) their values are nonzero multiples of \(X^{D-1}\).
At \(Y\ne0\), equality of the three \(D\)-th powers would force the three distinct collinear numbers \(X/Y+j\) to have the same modulus, impossible on a circle.
Their dehomogenizations on each of the two coordinate charts are therefore coprime.
Bézout identities in each one-variable rational polynomial ring yield, by clearing denominators and homogenizing, integral combinations giving fixed nonzero multiples of \(X^k\) and \(Y^{k'}\), respectively, for suitable \(k,k'\).
Since \(\gcd(x,y)=1\), their common divisor at \((x,y)\) is bounded.
Also at least one \(|L_j(x,y)|\gg_{D,T}H^{D-1}\) on \(x,y\ge0\), by homogeneity and strict positivity of the absolute divided difference on \(\max(x,y)=1\), including the endpoint \(y=0\).
Thus \(\|v\|_\infty\gg_{D,T}H^{D-1}\).

A bounded nonzero character orthogonal to \(v\) gives one of finitely many nonzero integer homogeneous polynomials of degree \(D-1\) vanishing at \((x,y)\).
Indeed its lattice vector in \(\mathbb Z^D\) is nonzero and the \(D\) distinct monomials in the displayed \(U_k\) are linearly independent as polynomials.
Each such form has finitely many projective roots, so in this alternative the reduced height \(H\) is bounded in terms of the character bound and the fixed support.

<a id="intersection"></a>
## Codimension and exclusion of translated subtori

Denote the lifted minimal subsum in the intrinsic lattice by \(G\), and extend the root-field involution \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) to the algebraic closure.
The point lies on both \(G=0\) and \(G^\iota(Z^{-1})=0\), with all coefficients in a field of degree at most \(M^2\).
A common irreducible factor \(f\) with Newton polytope of dimension at least two cannot occur.
In fact \(G\) has the two factors \(f\) and \((f^{\iota^{-1}})(Z^{-1})\), whose polytopes are negatives of each other up to translation.
If they are associates the first polytope is centrally symmetric, and if not, the product divides \(G\) and gives a centrally symmetric summand.
Either case contradicts the [strictly convex projection](Yin_C_G0345_v757.md#projection) of the chosen support onto its first two moment coordinates.
For clarity, the lattice identification maps the support to a translate of the selected \(w_j\)'s.
The projection to \((j,j^2)\) stays strictly convex under a small generic perturbation, which can be chosen to keep dimension two on the proposed summand.
An irreducible factor of dimension one is a binomial up to a monomial.
If a binomial factor of \(G\) vanished at the point, grouping its monomials by cosets of the binomial direction would make each nonempty group vanish there.
Minimality would put the entire chosen support on one line, contradicting \(q\ge2\).
Thus every geometric component through the point has codimension at least two.

Nor is the point on any positive-dimensional translated subtorus contained in such a component.
Otherwise grouping the terms of \(G\) by character restriction to that subtorus gives separate zero sums at the point, by linear independence of distinct characters.
Minimality puts all support differences in the annihilator of the subtorus, impossible since they span rank \(q\).
These two facts verify the hypotheses on the point in the [quantitative intersection theorem of Amoroso, Sombra and Zannier](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U001.md#intersection).
The degrees of the equations after Laurent shifts are bounded in terms of \(D,T\), their coefficient field degree is bounded independently of \(H,W\), and their logarithmic heights are \(O_{D,T}(1+Wh(\alpha))\) by the coefficient estimates.
The theorem gives a nonzero orthogonal character of uniformly bounded size or
\[
         \|v\|_\infty^{(1-\delta)/(q-1)}\ll 1+Wh(\alpha).
\]
The first alternative bounds \(H\) as proved above.
In the second, \(q\le D\) and the lower bound for \(\|v\|_\infty\) proves (1).

Finally at bounded \(H\) the exponents always project, after removing a common monomial and dilation, to one of finitely many bounded integer lists.
The exact subsum at that dilation is a nonzero one-variable polynomial of bounded degree over a field of bounded degree, even for varying coefficients.
It bounds the degree of a positive power of \(\alpha\) uniformly.
This excludes bounded \(H\) on a shrinking positive-measure sequence by the [uniform escape of all powered degrees](Yin_C_G0345_v739.md#volume).
