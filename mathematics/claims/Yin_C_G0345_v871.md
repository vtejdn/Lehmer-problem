# Conjugates of reciprocal four-term sections with a short middle gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## Height and the proportion of conjugates on the circle

Consider a sequence of coprime nonzero integers \(a,b\), integers \(0<u<v\), signs \(\epsilon=\pm1\) and integers \(g\ge1\).
In each member choose any nontorsion algebraic unit \(z\) such that
\[
 a(1+\epsilon\beta^W)+b\beta^u(1+\epsilon\beta^t)=0,\qquad
 \beta=z^g,\quad W=u+v,\quad t=v-u .
\]
Write \(h\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(B=|b/a|\).
If \(t h(\beta)\to0\), then eventually \(|a|=1\) and \(B\) is an integer at least two.
With embeddings counted uniformly one has
\[
 \Pr_\sigma(|\sigma z|=1)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(1/B)+o(1),
 \qquad Wh(\beta)=\log B+o(1).
\]
These absolute errors tend to zero even when the coefficients are unbounded.
In particular, for each \(\eta>0\) there is a constant \(c_\eta>0\) such that a unit as above with at least \(1/3+\eta\) of its conjugates on the circle satisfies \(t g h(z)\ge c_\eta\).
Writing \(m(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\), this implies \(v-u\ge c_\eta[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]/(g m(z))\).

<a id="divisibility"></a>
## The two integer divisibilities

Write \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
The equation and coprimality imply \(1+\epsilon\beta^t\in a\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\) and \(1+\epsilon\beta^W\in b\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\), since \(\beta\) is a unit.
For \(n=t\) or \(W\) and its corresponding divisor \(c=a\) or \(b\), the nonzero norm and the archimedean triangle inequality give \(nh(\beta)\ge\log(|c|/2)\).
If \(2\mid c\), then \(\beta^n=-\epsilon+2s\) for an algebraic integer \(s\), so \(\beta^{2n}-1\in4\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\).
The same norm estimate for this nonzero difference gives \(2nh(\beta)\ge\log2\).
Together these estimates give \(nh(\beta)\ge c_0=\min(\log(3/2),(\log2)/2)>0\) whenever \(|c|\ge2\).
Consequently \(|a|=1\) eventually.
If also \(|b|=1\), the equation factors as \(a(1+(b/a)\beta^u)(1+\epsilon(b/a)\beta^v)=0\), which forces torsion.
We thus have \(Wh(\beta)\ge\max(c_0,\log(B/2))\).
It follows that \(\kappa=t/W\to0\) and \(\kappa\log(2B)\to0\).

<a id="radial"></a>
## Pointwise equations at a conjugate

For \(x=\sigma\beta\), put \(Y=(W/2)|\log|x||\), \(\phi=W\arg(x)/2\), \(\psi=t\arg(x)/2\), \(\theta=2\psi\bmod 2\pi\), and \(\rho(s)=|1+\epsilon e^{is}|/2\).
Dividing by a consistent value of \(x^{W/2}\) compares the hyperbolic cosines of \((W/2)\log x\) and \((t/2)\log x\) when \(\epsilon=1\), and their hyperbolic sines when \(\epsilon=-1\), with real coefficient ratio of absolute value \(B\).
Taking absolute squares gives
\[
 \sinh^2Y+\rho(2\phi)^2=B^2\{\sinh^2(\kappa Y)+\rho(\theta)^2\}.
\]
Equating real and imaginary parts in their respective absolute values gives
\[
 \rho(2\phi)\cosh Y=B\rho(\theta)\cosh(\kappa Y),\qquad
 \sqrt{1-\rho(2\phi)^2}\sinh Y
 =B\sqrt{1-\rho(\theta)^2}\sinh(\kappa Y).
\]
The triangle inequality yields \(e^Y-e^{-Y}\le B(e^{\kappa Y}+e^{-\kappa Y})\), hence \(Y\le\log(2B+1)/(1-\kappa)\).
In particular \(\kappa Y=o(1)\) uniformly in the conjugates.
Since \(h(\beta^t)\to0\) and each \(\beta^t\) is nontorsion, no algebraic point repeats infinitely often.
[Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures) gives Haar equidistribution of \(\theta\).
Passing to embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(z)\) from either power field preserves the uniform weights, and \(|\sigma z|=1\) is equivalent to \(Y=0\).

<a id="bounded"></a>
## Fixed coefficient ratio

First fix \(B\) and \(\epsilon\) along a subsequence.
When \(Y>0\), convexity gives \(\sinh(\kappa Y)\le\kappa\sinh Y\), so \(\rho(2\phi)^2\ge1-B^2\kappa^2\).
Consequently \(B\rho(\theta)\ge1-o(1)\) on every such conjugate, and \(\cosh Y=B\rho(\theta)+o(1)\) there.
On the circle the first component equation gives \(B\rho(\theta)\le1\).
Haar measure gives zero mass to the level \(B\rho=1\), so the fraction on the circle tends to the measure of \(B\rho\le1\), namely \(2\arcsin(1/B)/\pi\).
Uniformly at all conjugates \(Y=\operatorname{arcosh}(\max(1,B\rho(\theta)))+o(1)\).
The product formula for the unit gives \(\mathbb E Y=Wh(\beta)\).
The Haar integral of the last radial profile equals \(\log B\).
Indeed it is zero at \(B=1\), and integration of the nonnegative derivative of the integrand by Tonelli reduces the derivative integral at \(B>1\) to
\[
 \frac2\pi\int_0^{\arccos(1/B)}\frac{\cos s}{\sqrt{B^2\cos^2s-1}}\,ds=\frac1B ,
\]
using the substitution \(y=B\sin s/\sqrt{B^2-1}\).
The sign only translates the angular variable.

<a id="unbounded"></a>
## Growing coefficient ratio

Now suppose \(B\to\infty\), fixing the sign by a subsequence.
For any fixed \(\delta>0\) the squared identity shows uniformly on \(\rho(\theta)\ge\delta\) that \(\sinh Y/B=\rho(\theta)+o(1)\).
Thus \(Y\to\infty\) in probability, the neutral fraction tends to zero, and \(Y-\log B\) converges in distribution to \(\log(2\rho(\theta))\) for uniform \(\theta\).
These conclusions follow by excluding \(\rho<\delta\) and then letting \(\delta\downarrow0\).
The uniform radial bound gives \(Y-\log B\le\log2+o(1)\).
Truncation from below and angular equidistribution therefore give
\[
 \limsup\mathbb E(Y-\log B)\le
 \int_0^{2\pi}\log|1+\epsilon e^{i\theta}|\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=0
\]
by the circle integral for a linear factor.
For the opposite inequality the original equation and \(|a|=1\) give
\[
 |N(1+\epsilon\beta^W)|=B^D|N(1+\epsilon\beta^t)|\ge B^D .
\]
At each conjugate \(\log|1+\epsilon x^W|\le W\log^+|x|+\log(1+e^{-2Y})\).
The last term is bounded and tends to zero in probability.
Averaging proves \(\log B\le Wh(\beta)+o(1)\).
Every integer sequence of ratios has a constant or divergent subsequence after extraction, which proves the two assertions along the full sequence by the subsequence criterion.
Since \(2\arcsin(1/B)/\pi\le1/3\), the positive lower bound for \(t g h(z)\) follows by contradiction.
