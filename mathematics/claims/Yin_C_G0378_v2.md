## 1. Actual quotient directions and complete normalization

For any actual order unit u in O*, monic division gives its unique integral representative B_u of degree below d.

The exact accepted [L341 lattice interface](../ledger/Yin_L_G0341_v1.md#residual-lattice-fixed-scope) gives

    F_u=q B_u-P U_u, U_u in Z[t], deg F_u<=T,
    F_u(alpha_i)=q u_i,
    Res(P,F_u)=q^d Norm_K(u)=plus or minus q^d.       (1)

Every original conjugate alpha_i and signed full norm is present.
Inverses remain in O because multiplication by an order unit is an integral unimodular map; equivalently its adjugate produces the integral inverse.
The inverse is reduced in the SAME original quotient and window.
Its coefficients, content, energy and correlations need not equal those of u and are not replaced by a real relaxation.

For a finite collection of these actual auxiliaries and nonnegative real weights w_j summing to 1, omit zero-weight labels and define

    e_j=||F_j||_2^2/q^2>0,
    h_(lj)=q^(-2) sum_(i=0)^(T-l) F_(j,i)F_(j,i+l),
    E_0=sum_j w_j e_j>0, H_l=sum_j w_j h_(lj),
    G(z)=q^(-2) sum_j w_j |F_j(z)|^2
        =E_0+2 sum_(l=1)^T H_l Re(z^l), |z|=1.      (2)

The exact complete norm income is I=log q.

Then

    sum_j e_j v_j=1,
    eta=I-log(q^2 E_0)/2=log S/2,
    kappa=2 sum_(l=1)^T |C_l|.                      (3)

In particular the true target eta>=log2/2 is exactly S>=2, with no norm subsidy.

## 2. Inverse pairing forces a pointwise lower value on every original circle conjugate

Say that mass theta, 0<=theta<=1, is paired under inversion if the weighted unit labels can be decomposed into finitely many pairs (u,u^(-1)), with each member of pair a having weight p_a/2 and sum_a p_a=theta, and arbitrary remaining unit labels of total weight 1-theta.
Repeated units or labels are allowed; this is a decomposition of weights, not a unit-group classification.
The two auxiliaries in each pair are the actual residuals in (1).
Full inversion symmetry means theta=1.

**Proposition 1.** Suppose P has k>0 original conjugates on the unit circle, and let

    zeta_l=(1/k) sum_(|alpha_i|=1) Re(alpha_i^l),
    delta_circ=max_(1<=l<=T)|zeta_l|.              (4)

Then any bundle with paired mass theta satisfies

    G(alpha_i)>=theta for every |alpha_i|=1,
    E_0+2 sum_l H_l zeta_l>=theta,
    kappa delta_circ>=theta S-1.                 (5)

These statements hold with either signed norm +1 or -1, at every window and every positive original Mahler measure.
If theta S>1, exact complementary cancellation H_l=0 for all l is impossible.
In particular a fully inversion-symmetric unit bundle cannot have exact complementarity and ANY positive true surplus eta>0.

*Proof.* At an original circle conjugate z the evaluation map O->C is a field embedding.
By (1), F_u(z)/q=u(z) and F_(u^-1)(z)/q=u(z)^(-1), both nonzero.
For a=|u(z)|>0,

    (a^2+a^(-2))/2-1=(a-a^(-1))^2/2>=0.          (6)

Each paired mass p_a therefore contributes at least p_a to G(z).
The remaining terms are nonnegative squares.
This proves the first inequality.
Average (2) over the k actual circle conjugates and divide by E_0.
The result is 1+2 sum C_l zeta_l>=theta S.
The absolute-value bound 2 sum C_l zeta_l<=kappa delta_circ proves (5), without dividing by a possibly zero delta_circ.
If H=0, its middle inequality gives E_0>=theta, whereas theta S>1 means E_0<theta.
For theta=1, positive eta in (3) is exactly E_0<1.
This is the stated contradiction.
No estimate of the expanding or contracting conjugates was discarded from the income: their complete norm was already used in (1)--(3).
The pointwise assertion simply supplies additional restrictions at existing circle embeddings.

**Salem finite-frequency consequence.** Now suppose P is an actual Salem minimal polynomial of degree d=2s>=4: its roots are beta,beta^(-1), beta>1, and d-2 nonreal circle conjugates.
Write m=log beta for its TOTAL logarithmic Mahler measure and u=Tm/d.
The accepted [L229 angular estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0229_v1.md#uniform-powered-angular-coherence) bounds the full original angular average at every 1<=l<=T by epsilon(u), on 0<u<=h_0 in that source's exact normalization.
The two real roots have angle zero.
Thus

    zeta_l=(d a_l-2)/(d-2),
    delta_circ<=min{1,(d epsilon(u)+2)/(d-2)},
    a_l=(1/d) sum_(i=1)^d (alpha_i/|alpha_i|)^l.    (7)

The conjugate pairing makes these averages real, but the same bound follows from their complex moduli.
Therefore paired mass theta with the true target S>=2 forces

    kappa min{1,(d epsilon(u)+2)/(d-2)}>=2theta-1.  (8)

Along a hypothetical Salem sequence with m->0, T/d bounded, d->infinity and theta>=theta_0>1/2, kappa must diverge.
This is a necessary profile, not existence of that sequence.
The condition d->infinity is automatic for an actual sequence of distinct integer minimal polynomials of bounded total measure tending to zero, by the already accepted fixed-degree finiteness; it is displayed rather than silently used.
The same conclusion holds when theta S-1 has any uniform positive lower bound.
Full inversion symmetry gives kappa delta_circ>=1 at S>=2.
The angular function and powered-degree multiplicities are exactly the accepted L229 input, without a new interchange of limits.

This changes the candidate: indiscriminate inversion symmetrization is not a free way to cancel signed Fourier coefficients.
It imposes a positive lower value at EVERY original circle conjugate, and substantial balanced mass forces a correlation cost on hypothetical small-measure Salem inputs.
Asymmetric weights, unpaired units, nonunits, input classes with fewer circle roots, and other changes of auxiliary remain outside this obstruction.

## 3. The full norm sign and the trace norm of c-1 give a stronger joint restriction

Continue with a genuine Salem input.
The accepted [L66 trace-field interface](../ledger/Yin_L_G0066_v1.md#reciprocal-trace-domain) gives sigma(alpha)=alpha^(-1), F=Q(alpha+alpha^(-1)), [K:F]=2 and [F:Q]=s.
In this Salem scope F is totally real: one trace conjugate beta+beta^(-1)>2 and the others 2cos(theta_j) lie in (-2,2).
For a unit u define c=u sigma(u) in F.
It is an algebraic integer unit; it need not be declared an element of the smaller order Z[alpha+alpha^(-1)].
Its complete trace-field norm is

    Norm_F(c)=Norm_K(u).                           (9)

At every circle trace embedding c_j=|u(e^(i theta_j))|^2>0.
Consequently Norm_K(u)=+1 forces positivity also at the exterior trace embedding c_0=u(beta)u(beta^(-1)); Norm_K(u)=-1 forces c_0<0.
This is exactly where the retained FULL signed norm affects the analytic argument.

Assume in the rest of this section that the entire bundle is decomposed into inverse pairs of total masses p_a, sum p_a=1, and EVERY unit has full signed norm +1.
For pair a put c_a=u_a sigma(u_a), and set

    b_a=0 if c_a=1,
    b_a=|Norm_F(c_a-1)|^(2/s) if c_a!=1,
    B=sum_a p_a b_a,
    Theta=sum_(a:c_a!=1) p_a, so B>=Theta.          (10)

The last inequality is integer arithmetic: each nonzero Norm_F(c_a-1) is a nonzero integer, retaining all s embeddings and any degree multiplicities.
These powers are exact real quantities, not silently rational coefficients.

Define the actual damped moments

    Gamma_l=[beta^(-l)+sum_(j=1)^(s-1) cos(l theta_j)]/s,
    delta_damp=max_(1<=l<=T)|Gamma_l|,
    W=[s-1+beta^(-T)]/s, u=Tm/d.                  (11)

**Proposition 2.** Under precisely these hypotheses, at every m>0 and every actual window,

    E_0+2 sum_l H_l Gamma_l>=W+(1/2)e^(-2u) B,
    kappa delta_damp>=S[W+(1/2)e^(-2u) B]-1.      (12)

When 0<u<=h_0, delta_damp<=min{1,epsilon(u)+2u}.
At S>=2 this gives the explicit necessary condition

    kappa min{1,epsilon(u)+2u}
        >=2W+e^(-2u) B-1
        >=1-4u+e^(-2u) Theta.                     (13)

Thus a fixed positive mass of units with c_a!=1 adds a genuine arithmetic penalty to inverse averaging; it is not extra norm income.
A norm -1 unit cannot be inserted into (12)--(13) under the old proof, because c_0 and (c_0-1)^2/c_0 are negative.
Proposition 1 remains valid for those units.

*Proof.* For c=c_a!=1 all its s conjugates are positive.
No conjugate can equal 1, since an embedding is injective and that would imply c=1.
Hence

    y=c+c^(-1)-2=(c-1)^2/c

is a totally positive algebraic integer.
By (9),

    Norm_F(y)=Norm_F(c-1)^2/Norm_F(c)
             =Norm_F(c-1)^2.                     (14)

Use positive weights lambda_0=beta^(-T) at the exterior trace embedding and lambda_j=1 at the s-1 circle embeddings.
The elementary arithmetic/geometric mean inequality, applied to the s positive numbers lambda_j y_j, gives

    (1/s)sum_j lambda_j y_j
        >=[product_j lambda_j y_j]^(1/s)
        =e^(-Tm/s)|Norm_F(c-1)|^(2/s)
        =e^(-2u)b_a.                             (15)

Since the two members u,u^(-1) have trace values c,c^(-1), their average weighted trace is W+(1/(2s))sum lambda_j y_j.
For c=1 it is exactly W.
Averaging with the ACTUAL pair masses p_a therefore gives the lower bound W+(1/2)e^(-2u)B.

For the upper bound, apply the following calculation to EACH actual integer F in the bundle.
At a circle trace embedding F(alpha)F(alpha^(-1))/q^2=|F(alpha)|^2/q^2.
At the exterior embedding,

    beta^(-T) F(beta)F(beta^(-1))
        =F^*(beta^(-1))F(beta^(-1))
        <=[F^*(beta^(-1))^2+F(beta^(-1))^2]/2,     (16)

where F^*(t)=t^T F(t^(-1)) is reversed in the SAME full degree window, with endpoint zeros retained.
This is the scalar inequality 2xy<=x^2+y^2 for real x,y.
The existing [L318 polynomial Poisson majorant](Yin_C_G0600_v1.md#polynomial-poisson-majorant), applied at r=beta^(-1) to F and F^*, bounds each square in (16) by

    ||F||_2^2+2 sum_l A_l(F) beta^(-l),

because reversal preserves every complete autocorrelation A_l(F).
The circle values use the exact Fourier expansion.
Dividing the weighted trace sum by s q^2 thus gives an upper bound e_F+2sum h_(l,F)Gamma_l.
Summing with the same real weights as in (2) gives the first inequality (12).
Dividing by E_0 and using absolute values only AFTER aggregation gives the second.

For the angular bound, Gamma_l differs from the full original angular average by (beta^(-l)-1)/s.
Therefore

    |Gamma_l|<=epsilon(u)+(1-beta^(-l))/s
             <=epsilon(u)+lm/s<=epsilon(u)+2u,

and its definition also gives the trivial bound 1.
Finally W=1-(1-e^(-Tm))/s>=1-Tm/s=1-2u.
Use S>=2, B>=Theta and positivity of the bracket in (12) to obtain (13).
This proves every asserted case; it does not assert that either Cauchy or Poisson equality can be attained by an actual inverse pair.

The new quantity in (14) is the complete norm of c-1, not a selected exterior value, a trace-degree version of the original income, or the determinant of a changed original polynomial.
For powers u^k, c becomes c^k and (14) retains the corresponding full integer Norm_F(c^k-1)^2, provided c^k!=1.
This increases a necessary joint coefficient/correlation expense.
It creates no additional income in (3), where every original auxiliary still has full absolute norm q^d.
No asymptotic rate or cheap infinite power family is asserted.
