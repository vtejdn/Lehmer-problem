# Conditional covariance of projected binary difference digits on an exact return

<a id="statement"></a>
## Prediction from the full digit past

Use the [finite collision bridge](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#statement) of an algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Fix integers \(k,L\ge1\), put \(N=kd\), \(T=LN\), and write \(B_i\in\{-1,0,1\}^N\) for block \(i\) of the word.
The original digits are independent, with probabilities \(1/4,1/2,1/4\) on \(-1,0,1\).
The law \(Q_T\) conditions on \(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\delta_t\alpha^t=0\), and \(\mathcal F_{i-1}\) reveals all digits before block \(i\).
Write \(\langle Z_i\rangle=L^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^L\mathbb E_{Q_T} Z_i\), allowing matrix-valued \(Z_i\).
All \(L\) cuts, including the terminal cut, enter the average.

For a fixed \(1\le s\le N\), let any matrix \(A_i\in\mathbb R^{s\times N}\) with \(A_iA_i^{\mathsf T}=I_s\) depend on the revealed past.
Write \(\mu_i=\mathbb E_{Q_T}[A_iB_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\) and \(\Sigma_i=\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_T}(A_iB_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\).
Thus \(\Sigma_i\) measures the prediction error after allowing arbitrary functions of the past.
Use the trace norm \(\|D\|_1=\sum_j|\lambda_j(D)|\) for real symmetric \(D\), and put
\[
     h=\frac d s\left(km+\frac{\log(4T)}L\right),\qquad
     b(t)=\sup_{0<c\le1}\mathbb E\left|\cos\!\left(\sqrt{tc/2}\,g\right)\right|^{2/c}
          \quad(t>0),
\]
where \(g\) is standard real Gaussian.
One has \(\langle\|\mu_i\|^2\rangle/s\le h\), and for every \(0<t<1\),
\[
 \frac1s\left\langle\left\|\Sigma_i-\tfrac12 I_s\right\|_1\right\rangle
       \le \frac{2h+\log b(t)-\tfrac12\log(1-t)}t .                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular the left side is \(O(\sqrt h)\) as \(h\to0\), uniformly in the adaptive matrices and all other parameters.

Let \(n_\eta(\Sigma_i)\) count the eigenvalues of \(\Sigma_i\) at most \(\eta\), including multiplicity.
For every \(\eta\ge0,\ t>0\) with \(-\log b(t)>t\eta\),
\[
        \frac{\langle n_\eta(\Sigma_i)\rangle}{s}
                     \le \frac{h}{-\log b(t)-t\eta}.                    \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\lim_{t\to\infty}b(t)=1/2\).

<a id="salem"></a>
## Salem block innovations at two scales

For a Salem number \(\alpha>1\), take \(s=d-2\), \(m=\log\alpha\), and let \(V\in\mathbb R^{s\times N}\) have columns \(v_j=\pi(\alpha^{j-N})\), where \(0\le j<N\) and \(\pi\) uses \(\sqrt2\) times the real and imaginary coordinates of one conjugate from each neutral pair.
The [neutral carry recursion and Vandermonde calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v688.md#gram) give \(W=VV^{\mathsf T}>0\), since each consecutive \(d\)-column group has full neutral row rank.
The carry \(C_{iN}\) has neutral projection \(VB_i\) plus a rotation of its previous projection, which is known from the past.
Taking \(A_i=W^{-1/2}V\) gives the actual conditional covariance of \(W^{-1/2}\pi(C_{iN})\) in (1) and (2).
The estimate on \(\mu_i\) is for the whitened new block contribution.

Along any Salem sequence with \(m\to0\) the degrees tend to infinity by finiteness at bounded measure and bounded degree, so \(d/s\to1\).
If \(\log(4T)=o(L)\) and \(km\to0\), (1) gives convergence to the unrestricted covariance \(I_s/2\) in mean trace norm divided by \(s\).
At \(k\sim1/(2m)\) with the same horizon condition, (2) gives, for any \(\eta\to0\) with \(\eta\ge0\),
\[
             \limsup \frac{\langle n_\eta(\Sigma_i)\rangle}{s}
                                 \le\frac{1}{2\log 2}.                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed first fix \(t\) with \(b(t)<1\), pass to the limit in (2), and then let \(t\to\infty\).
At that critical scale even the fixed cutoff \(\eta=1/1000\) satisfies \(\limsup\langle n_\eta(\Sigma_i)\rangle/s<4/5\).
This controls the conditional spectra at individual pasts in the stated average, extending the [bound for deterministic linear prediction](Yin_C_G0345_v696.md#statement) to the errors of the optimal conditional means.
These spectral bounds allow exceptional small eigenvalues and leave the determinant of the actual innovation covariance undetermined.

<a id="laplace"></a>
## Uniform Laplace bounds for projected product digits

Let \(B\) have the unrestricted \(N\)-digit product law \(P_N\) and \(A\) be an \(r\)-by-\(N\) real matrix with \(AA^{\mathsf T}=I_r\), \(r\ge1\).
For any \(z\in\mathbb R^r\) the Gaussian characteristic function gives
\[
               \mathbb E_{P_N}e^{-t\|AB-z\|^2}\le b(t)^r.              \tag{4}
\]
To see this, write every nonzero column \(a_j=\sqrt{c_j}u_j\) with \(\|u_j\|=1\).
Then \(0<c_j\le1\), \(\sum c_j=r\) and \(\sum c_j u_ju_j^{\mathsf T}=I_r\).
Let \(G\) be standard Gaussian in \(\mathbb R^r\).
Using the digit characteristic function \(\cos^2(\theta/2)\), the left side of (4) is the expectation over \(G\) of
\[
     e^{-i\sqrt{2t}\langle G,z\rangle}
        \prod_j\cos^2\!\left(\sqrt{tc_j/2}\,\langle u_j,G\rangle\right).
\]
The product is nonnegative, so taking absolute values removes the phase.
Apply the [Gaussian form of the geometric Brascamp–Lieb inequality](../audits/literature/BCCT08/Yin_U001.md#gaussian-frame) to the functions
\(x\mapsto|\cos(\sqrt{tc_j/2}\,x)|^{2/c_j}\) with weights \(c_j\).
Each resulting one-dimensional expectation is at most \(b(t)\), proving (4).

For \(0<t<1\), the positive quadratic moment satisfies
\[
             \mathbb E_{P_N}e^{t\|AB\|^2}\le(1-t)^{-r/2}.              \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(\mathbb E e^{u\delta}=\cosh^2(u/2)\le e^{u^2/4}\).
Replacing the phase in the Gaussian identity by a real exponential, applying this bound to every independent digit and using \(AA^{\mathsf T}=I_r\) gives \(\mathbb E_G e^{t\|G\|^2/2}=(1-t)^{-r/2}\).

<a id="scalar"></a>
## Small and large parameters in the one-dimensional bound

Taking \(c=1\) gives \(b(t)\ge (1+e^{-t})/2\ge e^{-t/2}\).
On \(|x|\le\pi/2\), \(|\cos x|\le e^{-x^2/2}\), by integration of \(\tan x\ge x\) on the positive half interval.
Applying this bound on the principal interval and the bound one elsewhere yields
\[
 b(t)\le(1+t)^{-1/2}+2e^{-\pi^2/(4t)}.
\]
Indeed the Gaussian tail outside that interval begins at \(|g|=\pi/\sqrt{2tc}\), and \(c\le1\).
Consequently \(\log b(t)=-t/2+O(t^2)\) as \(t\downarrow0\).

For \(1/4\le c\le1\), the exponent \(2/c\ge2\) bounds the expectation by \((1+e^{-tc})/2\le(1+e^{-t/4})/2\).
For smaller \(c\), use \(|\cos x|^{2/c}\le\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\exp(-(x-\pi n)^2/c)\) by shifting to a nearest multiple of \(\pi\).
Termwise Gaussian integration bounds the expectation by
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt{1+t}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi^2n^2/[c(1+t)]}
      \le \frac1{\sqrt{1+t}}+\sqrt{\frac c\pi}
      \le \frac1{\sqrt{1+t}}+\frac1{2\sqrt\pi}.
\]
The first inequality after the sum bounds its positive-index terms by the integral of a decreasing Gaussian over the positive half-line.
Combining the two ranges with the lower bound proves \(b(t)\to1/2\).
At \(t=16\) both upper bounds are below \(21/40\).
Since \(\log(40/21)-16/1000>5/8\), (2) at \(h\to1/2\) gives the stated fixed cutoff.
The logarithm comparison also follows by bounding \(\log(40/21)\) below by \(2(x+x^3/3)\) at \(x=19/61\).

<a id="conditional"></a>
## Conditional entropy cost of the prediction error

At a realized past of positive bridge probability let \(D_i=D(Q_T(B_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\Vert P_N)\).
The [endpoint cost and entropy chain rule](Yin_C_G0345_v683.md#cost) give \(\langle D_i\rangle\le sh\).
For any real function \(f\) of that block, Jensen's inequality for the density of the conditioned block law relative to \(P_N\) gives
\(\mathbb E_{Q_T}[f\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le D_i+\log\mathbb E_{P_N}e^f\).
The past and its matrices are fixed during each use of this inequality.
Taking \(f=\langle v,A_i B_i\rangle\) and using the unrestricted linear moment bound \(e^{\|v\|^2/4}\) gives \(D_i\ge\|\mu_i\|^2\) by choosing \(v=2\mu_i\).

Project the rows of \(A_i\) onto any \(r\) orthonormal directions of the output, using an \(r\)-by-\(s\) matrix \(P\) with \(PP^{\mathsf T}=I_r\).
At the fixed past this gives another row isometry \(P A_i\), including when the directions are chosen from the conditional eigenspaces.
Using (4) with \(z=P\mu_i\) gives
\[
               D_i\ge-r\log b(t)-t\,\operatorname{tr}(P\Sigma_iP^{\mathsf T}).
                                                                         \tag{6}
\]
Using (5) at zero center, the conditional second moment dominates the conditional covariance, so
\[
               D_i\ge t\,\operatorname{tr}(P\Sigma_iP^{\mathsf T})
                                    +\tfrac r2\log(1-t),\quad 0<t<1.    \tag{7}
\]
For \(r=0\) both inequalities reduce to \(D_i\ge0\).

Take \(P\) in (6) to select all eigenvalues below \(1/2\), and in (7) those above \(1/2\).
The total deficit below \(1/2\) is bounded by \(D_i/t+r(1/2+\log b(t)/t)\) using the first rank, and the total excess by \(D_i/t+r(-1/2-\log(1-t)/(2t))\) using the second.
Both coefficients of \(r\) are nonnegative, so replacing each rank by \(s\) and averaging proves (1).
The scalar expansion and \(t=\sqrt h\) for sufficiently small positive \(h\) give the stated rate.
Taking \(P\) in (6) to select the eigenvalues at most \(\eta\) gives (2).

<a id="affine"></a>
## Exact affine supports of neutral carries

For the Salem map \(A=W^{-1/2}V\), write \(a_k=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}\).
Under \(P_N\), for every affine plane \(H\subset\mathbb R^s\) of codimension \(r\),
\[
                              P_N(AB\in H)\le a_k^r.                    \tag{8}
\]
The same holds for any fixed \(s\)-by-\(N\) matrix whose consecutive \(k\) groups of \(d\) columns each have rank \(s\).
Consequently the actual Salem bridge also satisfies
\[
              \frac{\langle\dim\ker\Sigma_i\rangle}{s}
                    \le\frac{h}{\log(1/a_k)}.                           \tag{9}
\]
In particular at \(k\sim1/(2m)\) and \(\log(4T)=o(L)\) this fraction is \(O(1/\log k)\).
This addresses exact rank loss at the critical scale in addition to the fixed positive-width bound (2).

For \(r>0\) project onto the \(r\) normal directions of \(H\), so the desired event fixes the value of a sum of projected columns times independent digits.
The subgroup generated by those finitely many real vectors is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group, hence isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z^\ell\) for some \(\ell\).
A target outside this subgroup has probability zero.
For a target in it, Fourier inversion on \(\mathbb T^\ell\) expresses its mass as the integral of its character times the product of the nonnegative digit factors \(\cos^2(\pi\langle v,\theta\rangle)\), where \(v\in\mathbb Z^\ell\) denotes a projected column in the chosen group basis.
In each of the \(k\) groups retain \(r\) real-linearly independent projected columns and bound the remaining factors by one after removing the character by absolute values.
The retained columns in each group have no nonzero integral relation.
Their \(r\) phases therefore have joint Haar law on \(\mathbb T^r\), since all nonconstant integral characters have integral zero.
Hölder's inequality with exponent \(k\) across the groups bounds the integral by the product of the \(k\)-th roots of their \(k\)-th moments.
Each moment is \(a_k^r\) by the one-dimensional cosine integral in the [degree-block calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v679.md#blocks).
This proves (8), including the immediate case \(r=0\).

Given a past, the conditioned block output lies in the affine translate by \(\mu_i\) of the range of \(\Sigma_i\), since every direction in its kernel has zero conditional variance.
Thus (8) and relative entropy on this support give \(D_i\ge(\dim\ker\Sigma_i)\log(1/a_k)\).
Averaging proves (9), and \(a_k\sim(\pi k)^{-1/2}\) gives the rate.
All plane choices may depend on the full prior word at their cut.
Whether all actual prediction errors can meet the determinant threshold for a shrinking Salem sequence remains open.
