<a id="elementary-unit-rank-upper-bound"></a>
## 1. The needed unit-rank upper bound without Dirichlet's theorem

Let alpha be any nontorsion algebraic unit, K=Q(alpha), d=[K:Q], O_K its maximal order and E=O_K^* its unit group.
Count every complex embedding separately, so there are d embeddings sigma_1,...,sigma_d.
The homomorphism

    ell:E -> R^d,  ell(u)=(log|sigma_i(u)|)_(i=1)^d

has image in the hyperplane sum_i y_i=0, since |Norm(u)|=1.
Its kernel is finite and its image meets every compact set in only finitely many points.
Here is the elementary finiteness argument.
If ell(u) stays in a fixed compact set, all conjugates of u have a common upper bound R>=1: embeddings of K cover every conjugate of u.
Its minimal polynomial is monic integral of degree e<=d, and its kth coefficient has absolute value at most binomial(e,k) R^k.
Only finitely many such integer polynomials, and hence finitely many such u, exist.
The same argument with every conjugate bounded by one treats the kernel.

For completeness, a subgroup G of a finite-dimensional real vector space meeting compact sets in finite sets has rank at most the dimension of its real span.
Choose a real basis g_1,...,g_s of that span from G.
The lattice L=sum_i Z g_i has finite index in G: subtracting the integer parts of the coordinates of any g puts it in the compact parallelepiped sum_i [0,1]g_i, which contains only finitely many elements of G.
Thus G is finitely generated with rank s.
Applying this to ell(E), and then lifting generators across its finite kernel, proves

    rank E=rank ell(E)<=d-1.                         (1)

Only this upper bound is required; no lower bound, fundamental units, regulator estimate or full Dirichlet unit theorem is imported.

<a id="two-variable-unit-hit-count"></a>
## 2. Uniformly bounded unit hits along one geometric progression

Fix any x in E.
For each integer j for which 1+x alpha^j is a unit, the tuple

    (u,v)=(alpha^j,1+x alpha^j)

belongs to Gamma=<alpha> x E and solves

    (-x)u+v=1.                                      (2)

Both coefficients are fixed and nonzero.
Every solution in question is nondegenerate: each one-term subsum is nonzero and the full sum is one.
Nontorsion makes different j give different u and hence different tuples.
The rank is that of the direct-product tuple group, not the scalar group <alpha>:

    rank Gamma=1+rank E<=d.

ESS02-U001 with n=2 therefore gives, uniformly in x,

    #{j in Z:1+x alpha^j in E}
        <=exp(12^6(d+1)).                            (3)

If 1+x alpha^j=0, it is not counted and is already outside E.
Define explicit positive constants

    B_d=ceil(exp(12^6(d+1))),
    L_d=B_d+1,            epsilon_d=2^(-B_d-2).       (4)

Among j=1,...,L_d there is consequently a j=j(x) for which 1+x alpha^j is zero or is not a unit.
Neither finding that j by an algorithm nor enumerating the unit group is part of this argument.
The existence and uniform bound on j suffice for the probability estimate below.

<a id="actual-unit-sojourn-tail"></a>
## 3. An exact block bound for the true killed unit subchain

Let O=Z[alpha], and let D be the set of states occurring on a finite path from zero back to zero for the original walk

    X_(k+1)=alpha X_k+eta_k,
    Pr(eta=0)=1/2, Pr(eta=1)=Pr(eta=-1)=1/4.

Use U=D intersect E.
In particular zero is not in U.
On nonnegative functions on U define the genuinely killed kernel

    (Qf)(x)=sum_(a in {-1,0,1}) w_a
               1_U(alpha x+a) f(alpha x+a), x in U. (5)

For an actual starting x in U, take the j from Section 2.
Prescribe j-1 zero digits and then the digit +1.
The ambient state after k<j of these steps is alpha^k x, still a maximal-order unit, and its state after step j is 1+x alpha^j, outside E.
If a preceding state has already left D, the killed subchain has stopped even earlier.
Thus the entire cylinder of paths with this prescribed prefix is dead by time j, whether or not its earlier unit states lie in D.
The cylinder's probability under the unchanged law is exactly

    (1/2)^(j-1)(1/4)=2^(-j-1)>=epsilon_d.             (6)

All later digits up to time L_d are unrestricted, so they introduce no further probability cost.
In particular this is an actual path event; it does not choose a different state representative after each transition.
For every x in U,

    (Q^L_d 1)(x)<=1-epsilon_d.                       (7)

Write L=L_d and epsilon=epsilon_d.
Positivity and substochasticity give, for every integer n>=0,

    (Q^n 1)(x)<=(1-epsilon)^floor(n/L).              (8)

Indeed iterate (7) at multiples of L; for n=kL+s with 0<=s<L use Q^s 1<=1 before applying Q^(kL).
All sums are over actual digit paths.
The constant function is bounded and the rows are substochastic, so these finite powers require no infinite-dimensional spectral theory.

Equivalently, for the original walk started at x in U, define tau_U=min{k>=1:X_k notin U}.
Then Pr_x(tau_U>n)=(Q^n1)(x).
It follows that tau_U is finite almost surely and, uniformly in x,

    E_x tau_U<=L/epsilon.                            (9)

This identity and bound follow by summing (8) over n>=0.
More generally, for every real z>=1 with (1-epsilon) z^L<1, the complete weighted exit-time law satisfies

    E_x z^(tau_U)
      <=epsilon z^L/[1-(1-epsilon)z^L].             (10)

To check the full tail, Tonelli applied to z^k=1+(z-1)sum_(n=0)^(k-1)z^n gives E z^tau=1+(z-1)sum_(n>=0)z^n Pr(tau>n).
Insert (8) and sum first over n=kL+s.
The resulting geometric series is (10); z=1 holds by the already proved almost-sure exit.
No finite truncation or omitted long unit stay is used.

<a id="unit-sojourn-rate-and-return-boundary"></a>
## 4. Exact cost and the distinction from a full return bound

Set

    gamma_d=-log(1-epsilon_d)/L_d.

Equations (8)--(10) imply

    Pr_x(tau_U>n)
       <=(1-epsilon_d)^(-1) exp(-gamma_d n),
    E_x exp(gamma_d tau_U/2)
       <=1+(1-epsilon_d)^(-1/2).                    (11)

The second bound substitutes z^L=(1-epsilon_d)^(-1/2) in (10).
The elementary integral bounds for -log(1-epsilon) give

    epsilon_d/L_d <= gamma_d
       <=epsilon_d/[(1-epsilon_d)L_d],
    L_d/epsilon_d=(B_d+1)2^(B_d+2).                 (12)

Thus the supplied positive rate is uniform over inputs of fixed degree, but tends to zero as d grows, with B_d exponential in d and epsilon_d exponentially small in B_d.
These are the costs of this sufficient recipe, not necessary bounds on the best escape rate or shortest possible escape block.
No practical search or optimization ran.

This is a genuine improvement over merely powering a one-step cell upper bound for unit states.
On the actual Lehmer control, L155 forces the unit-cell diagonal of any such upper matrix to be at least one; powers cannot contract it.
Here the whole block is computed in the true killed unit chain before its row survival is bounded by one number, and (7) proves strict contraction.
Intermediate-state compatibility is preserved throughout the block.

The restriction to U is essential.
Killing at D's complement removes no path from zero back to zero, by the definition of D.
Killing upon leaving U can remove such a path: exit may itself be a return to zero, or the walk may pass through nonunit states and later return to units or to zero.
In particular Q^L is not the same as the full killed-D operator T^L restricted only at its two endpoints to U; the latter can include paths that leave U and reenter.
Formula (7) is not a full-domain inequality T^L v<=rho v for a positive norm-only weight.

The new estimate controls every consecutive unit-state sojourn, with its complete law and explicit cost.
A return argument still needs to control visits to nonunits, repeated entries, and their joint weights.
It cannot multiply the unit-sojourn estimates as if they exhausted all time.
No new bound for the unrestricted first-return generating function, degree-uniform entropy floor, sufficiently-near-one exclusion, or resolution of G0001 is inferred.

## 5. Finite-prime and bounded-norm extensions

Let S be any fixed finite set of nonzero prime ideals of O_K, with cardinality s.
Write E_S for the S-unit group: the elements of K^* whose prime-ideal valuations outside S vanish.
The homomorphism

    E_S -> Z^s,  x -> (ord_p(x))_(p in S)

has kernel E.
Its image is a subgroup of Z^s, hence is finitely generated of rank at most s.
This elementary subgroup fact follows by induction on s, projecting to the first coordinate and using that every subgroup of Z is cyclic.
Lifting image generators and using the unit-group conclusion in Section 1 proves

    rank E_S<=d-1+s,
    rank(<alpha> x E_S)<=d+s.                       (13)

The same nondegenerate equation (2), now with v in E_S and any fixed x in E_S, bounds the number of integer j with 1+x alpha^j in E_S by exp(12^6(d+s+1)).
Set

    B=B_(d,s)=ceil(exp(12^6(d+s+1))),
    L=B+1,              epsilon=2^(-B-2).           (14)

The genuinely killed chain on D intersect E_S obeys every bound (7)--(12) with these substituted constants.
The proof is unchanged: zero digits preserve E_S, and among B+1 positive exponents one endpoint is zero or outside E_S.
This conclusion permits unbounded valuations at primes in S.
It is not a finite-state approximation.

There is also an explicit bounded-norm domain.
For a real H>=1 put

    S_H={p a nonzero prime ideal:Norm(p)<=H},
    s_H=|S_H|<=d pi(floor H)<=d floor H,             (15)

where pi(k) counts rational primes at most k.
To verify finiteness and the bound, a prime ideal above p has norm p^f, so its underlying rational prime is at most H; there are at most d prime ideals above each p, since sum_(p_ideal|p) e_(p_ideal) f_(p_ideal)=d.
These are exactly the retained maximal-order conventions in L74.
For a nonzero algebraic integer x with |Norm(x)|<=H, every ideal prime factor of (x) has norm at most H, because their positive norm powers multiply to |Norm(x)|.
Thus x is an S_H-unit.

The actual chain killed on leaving {x in D:0<|Norm(x)|<=H} therefore satisfies (7)--(12) with s=s_H.
One can use the weaker fully degree/H bound obtained by replacing s_H by d floor H in (14).
Along the specified zero prefix, |Norm(alpha^k x)|=|Norm(x)|, and the chosen zero-or-non-S_H-unit endpoint is outside the band.
Earlier departure from D only kills the path sooner.
No boundedness of the number of states in this norm band is asserted.

## 6. Include zero: a complete return certificate on each fixed domain

Keep a fixed S and B=B_(d,s), and now let V_S=D intersect ({0} union E_S).
Let Qhat be the true kernel killed on leaving V_S, including its transitions at zero.
For x in E_S, among j=1,...,B+2 at most B endpoints 1+x alpha^j are S-units and at most one endpoint is zero.
The latter follows from nontorsion, since the powers alpha^j are distinct.
Hence one of these endpoints is nonzero and outside E_S.
The zero-prefix-then-plus-one cylinder reaches it with probability at least 2^(-B-3), in at most B+2 steps.

For the starting state zero, prescribe +1 first, with probability 1/4, and then use that cylinder from the unit 1.
If 1 is outside D, the initial step already kills the path; otherwise the stated continuation is valid.
Consequently the following constants work uniformly for every starting state in V_S:

    M=B+3,             delta=2^(-B-5),
    (Qhat^M 1)(x)<=1-delta.                         (16)

For a nonzero starting state the stronger escape event above fits within M, and its extra final digits are free.
For zero, the total specified prefix has length at most M and probability at least (1/4)2^(-B-3)=delta.
Any earlier exit from D is again harmless for this upper survival bound.
Unlike Q in Section 3, Qhat does not kill merely on visiting zero.

Let p_n^S=(Qhat^n)_(0,0) and let f_n^S be the actual first-return probability restricted to paths staying in V_S.
The exact uniform survival and complete return bound is

    p_n^S <= (Qhat^n1)(0)
           <=(1-delta)^floor(n/M),      n>=0.       (17)

This includes all intermediate visits to zero for p_n^S and all lengths; there is no omitted path within V_S.
Put U_S(z)=sum_(n>=0)p_n^S z^n and F_S(z)=sum_(n>=1)f_n^S z^n.
Every path counted by p_n^S decomposes uniquely into first-return segments still in V_S.
Coefficientwise, and then by nonnegative summation, U_S(z)=sum_(k>=0)F_S(z)^k.
From (17), for every real 1<=z<exp(gamma_(d,s)), where gamma_(d,s)=-log(1-delta)/M,

    U_S(z)<= [sum_(t=0)^(M-1) z^t]/[1-(1-delta)z^M]<infinity,
    F_S(z)=1-1/U_S(z)
        <=1-[1-(1-delta)z^M]/[sum_(t=0)^(M-1)z^t]<1. (18)

The renewal identity is applied to the complete restricted law, not to a representative-state approximation.
It gives a positive exponential return certificate on this fixed-prime domain.
Its rate and cost retain the full dependence on d and s:

    delta/M<=gamma_(d,s)<=delta/[(1-delta)M],
    M/delta=(B+3)2^(B+5).                           (19)

This sufficient rate tends to zero as d+s grows.
No necessary lower bound on an optimal block length is claimed.
The same entire argument applies to the domain {x in D:x=0 or 0<|Norm(x)|<=H}, with s=s_H in (16)--(19), or the weaker s=d floor H.
Its zero step enters 1, whose norm is at most H; its subsequent zero digits preserve norm, and the chosen nonzero non-S_H-unit endpoint has norm greater than H.

Every individual finite returning word is contained in some fixed finite-prime domain: collect the finitely many prime ideals dividing its finitely many nonzero states.
The set S may depend on the word, length and input.
Passing to all such S does not preserve the positive z-window in (18), because the supplied gamma_(d,s) degenerates as s increases.
No finite-S coverage of all returning paths, nor a uniform positive rate for unrestricted returns, follows.
The next section shows actual omitted return weight already at S empty.

## 7. Actual nonunit reentries in the complete first-return kernel

Retain T for the original kernel killed only outside D, U=D intersect E, and W=D minus (U union {0}).
Every nontrivial first return starts with 0 -> +1 or -1, and its last step starts at +alpha^(-1) or -alpha^(-1).
All these states are units when they occur.
A nonzero state can reach zero in one step only if alpha x is +1 or -1.
Thus there is no direct 0 -> W or W -> 0 transition.

Define nonnegative path series

    E(z)=sum_(ell>=0) z^(ell+2) T_UW T_WW^ell T_WU,
    R(z)=z T_UU+E(z).                                (20)

These are first formal power series with matrix coefficients.
R records the next unit visit before a visit to zero, either a direct step or an excursion having only W interior states.
For real z>=0 they are the corresponding nonnegative path sums, with infinity allowed.
No inverse of an infinite matrix or analytic convergence is assumed.
Unique decomposition at successive unit visits gives the unrestricted first-return series F of L154 as

    F(z)=z/2+(z T_0U)[sum_(k>=0)R(z)^k](z T_U0).      (21)

This holds coefficientwise, and then by nonnegative summation.
At each fixed total length there are only finitely many digit words, and every R segment has positive length.
Every term avoids zero in its interior; conversely every nontrivial first return has a unique initial unit, final unit and intervening R-segment decomposition.
Equation (21) retains all reentries, but alone gives no entropy estimate.

<a id="actual-lehmer-nonunit-reentry"></a>
For a concrete actual correction use the retained Lehmer input in L155,

    P=t^10+t^9-t^7-t^6-t^5-t^4-t^3+t+1,
    Q_2=(t-1)^2P=t^12-t^11-t^10+t^8+t^4-t^2-t+1.     (22)

The descending coefficients of Q_2 form a genuine first-return word of length 13.
An earlier return would require both a nonzero prefix and a nonzero suffix of length at least 11, because the leading and final digits are one and each vanishing nonzero polynomial is divisible by P.
Their total length would be at least 22, a contradiction.
The first five digits are (1,-1,-1,0,1), giving actual states

    u=X_2=alpha-1,
    y=X_3=alpha^2-alpha-1,       X_4=alpha y,
    v=X_5=alpha^4-alpha^3-alpha^2+1
         =(alpha-1)(alpha^3-alpha-1).                (23)

They all belong to D and are nonzero by that first-return word.
For g=t^2-t-1 and h=t^3-t-1, exact polynomial reduction gives

    P mod g=59t+37,
    P mod h=2t^2+3t+2.                              (24)

The first reduction can also be checked from t^n=F_n t+F_(n-1) modulo g, with F_0=0,F_1=1.
Resultant interchange has sign (-1)^(10*2)=1, and the roots of g have sum 1 and product -1.
Consequently

    Norm(g(alpha))=Res(P,g)=Res(g,P)
        =37^2+37*59-59^2=71.                        (25)

Multiplication by 2t^2+3t+2 on Q[t]/(h), in the basis (1,t,t^2), has matrix

    [ 2  2  3 ]
    [ 3  4  5 ]
    [ 2  3  4 ].

Its determinant is 2-4+3=1.
Since (-1)^(10*3)=1, this proves

    Norm(h(alpha))=Res(P,h)=Res(h,P)=1.              (26)

These are symbolic reductions and a displayed determinant, not an executed resultant or numerical-root calculation.
Also Norm(alpha)=1 and Norm(alpha-1)=P(1)=-1, so

    |Norm(u)|=|Norm(v)|=1,
    Norm(y)=Norm(alpha y)=71.                       (27)

Thus the three-step digit segment (-1,0,+1) goes from u in U through two W states to v in U.
Given u, its probability is (1/4)(1/2)(1/4)=1/32, and therefore

    E(z)_(u,v)>=z^3/32                              (28)

coefficientwise and for all real z>=0.
This is actual mass absent from the direct killed-unit kernel z T_UU.
The zero step preserves the nonunit principal ideal (alpha y)=(y), and the following +1 step restores a unit.
The always valid coprimality (x)+(alpha x +/- 1)=O_K supplies no monotone ideal-size barrier to this reentry.

Let F_U count exactly first returns whose nonzero interior states are all in U, together with the one-digit zero return.
This is F_S from Section 6 at S empty.
Its exact expression replaces R in (21) by z T_UU.
The two distinct words Q_2 and -Q_2 have length 13 and eight nonzero digits, each with probability 2^(-21).
Both are genuine first returns entering W, so neither contributes to F_U.
Hence

    F(z)>=F_U(z)+2^(-20)z^13.                       (29)

This is coefficientwise and is an inequality of nonnegative sums for real z>=0, including infinity; no subtraction of infinite series is used.
The same words leave any norm band with 1<=H<71 and reenter, but no such assertion for every larger band or finite S is made.

The sojourn bound cannot replace R by z T_UU in (21), and the restricted subcriticality in (18) cannot be transferred without the missing paths.
Equations (28)--(29) exhibit that difference on an actual fixed noncyclotomic input.
Their finite lower bounds do not bound the entire weighted reentry tail, compare sufficient mass with the escape margin, refute a valid full-path certificate or exclude a claim restricted to sufficiently near-one inputs.
All repeated unit/nonunit alternations and their weights remain an obligation for an unrestricted return argument.
No uniform Mahler gap is established.
