# Counting exterior conjugates from a single large power sum after a small prefix

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior counts at every radial threshold

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic integer unit of degree \(d\), logarithmic Mahler measure \(L\) and logarithmic house \(b\).
Write \(p_j\) for its power sums and \(r(t)\) for the number of conjugates of logarithmic modulus strictly greater than \(t\), for \(t\ge0\).
Fix \(\delta,\eta>0,\ C\ge0\), and suppose that at an integer \(\delta d\le q\le d\),
\[
                 |p_q|\ge\eta d,\qquad \sum_{1\le j<q}|p_j|^2\le C d^2.          \tag{1}
\]
Along any sequence of these inputs with \(L\to0\), uniformly for all \(t\ge0\),
\[
                          L(1+dt)(1+r(t))\gg_{\delta,\eta,C}1.                    \tag{2}
\]
If the stronger prefix bound \(\sum_{1\le j<q}|p_j|\le C'd\) holds for fixed \(C'\), then
\[
                    L(1+dt)\left(1+\frac{r(t)^2}{d}\right)
                                              \gg_{\delta,\eta,C'}1.              \tag{3}
\]
In particular (3) holds when the first nonzero coefficient below the leading term occurs at \(q\ge\delta d\), since \(p_q=-q a_q\) and all preceding sums are zero.
Neither reciprocal symmetry nor a bound on the number of conjugates of the same modulus is required.

At \(t=b\), (2) gives \(db\gg 1/L\) in the full range (1).
Under the stronger hypothesis, at least \(c\sqrt{d/L}\) conjugates have logarithmic modulus greater than \(1/d\), and at least \(\lfloor\sqrt d\rfloor\) have logarithmic modulus at least \(c/(dL)\), for a fixed \(c>0\) depending only on the indicated constants.
More generally, if \(b_1\ge b_2\ge\cdots\) are the exterior logarithmic moduli padded by zeros, then (3) gives
\[
                d b_j\ \ge\ \frac{c}{L(1+j^2/d)}-1
                           \qquad(1\le j\le d).                                \tag{4}
\]
The corresponding bound under (1) is \(d b_j\ge c/(L(1+j))-1\).
These conclusions refine the [first coefficient obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v149.md#statement) and the [earlier count using selected exterior factors](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#radii).
The coordinator suggested allowing the square-summed prefix in (1), testing the effect of moving powers and including the extra damping of nonzero moments.

<a id="matrix"></a>
## A positive moment matrix after changing the reference radius

Fix \(t\ge0\).
For each conjugate \(\alpha=e^{u+i\theta}\), scale it by \(e^{-t}\) and reflect the resulting point into the closed disk if it is outside, preserving its angle.
This gives \(w_\alpha=e^{-|u-t|+i\theta}\).
Let \(\mu_t\) be the average of the associated Poisson probability measures on the circle, using point masses for \(|w_\alpha|=1\).
Their positive moments are \(w_\alpha^j\).
On indices \(0\le k,l<q\), put \(M_{kl}=d\int z^{k-l}\,d\mu_t\).
This Hermitian matrix is positive semidefinite.
Define another Hermitian matrix \(E\) with diagonal \(d\) and lower entries \(e^{-t(k-l)}p_{k-l}\) for \(k>l\), and write \(r=r(t)\).
The Hilbert–Schmidt norm satisfies \(\|E\|_{\rm HS}^2\le(1+2C)d^2q\) under (1).
The absolute-sum prefix also gives \(\|E\|_{\rm op}\le(1+2C')d\) by row and column sums.

For each outlier \(u>t\), set \(v=u-t\) and form the positive Poisson moment matrix \(H_\alpha\) with entries \(e^{-v|k-l|+i(k-l)\theta}\), of trace \(q\).
Define vectors \(a_k=e^{kv+ik\theta}\), \(b_k=e^{-kv+ik\theta}\) for this point.
The exact formula is
\[
                  M=E+2H-K,\qquad H=\sum_{u>t}H_\alpha,\qquad
                  K=\sum_{u>t}(ab^*+ba^*).                                    \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(2\cosh((k-l)v)=e^{v|k-l|}+e^{-v|k-l|}\), which changes each exterior radial exponential in \(E\) to the reflected one and leaves the diagonal unchanged.
Write \(K=K_+-K_-\) for its positive and negative parts.
On the subspace perpendicular to all the vectors \(a+b\), \(K\) is nonpositive by the identity \(2(ab^*+ba^*)=(a+b)(a+b)^*-(a-b)(a-b)^*\).
Hence the projection \(\Pi\) onto its positive eigenspace has rank at most \(r\).
Because \(K\le E+2H\), \({\rm tr}\,K=2rq\), and \({\rm tr}\,H=rq\),
\[
 {\rm tr}\,K_-={\rm tr}(\Pi K)-2rq
       \le {\rm tr}(\Pi E)
       \le \min(\sqrt r\,\|E\|_{\rm HS},\,r\|E\|_{\rm op}).                    \tag{6}
\]
This bound does not require \(E\) to be positive.
From \(0\le M\le E_++2H+K_-\), eigenvalue monotonicity and the Hilbert–Schmidt triangle inequality give
\[
                  \|M\|_{\rm HS}\le \|E\|_{\rm HS}+2rq+{\rm tr}\,K_- .
\]
Since \(q\asymp_\delta d\) and \(r\le d\), these estimates imply
\[
 \frac{\|M\|_{\rm HS}^2}{d^2q}\ll_{\delta,C}1+r
 \quad\hbox{under (1)},\qquad
 \frac{\|M\|_{\rm HS}^2}{d^2q}\ll_{\delta,C'}1+r^2/d
 \quad\hbox{under the absolute-sum bound}.                                    \tag{7}
\]
In the latter case (1)'s squared-prefix bound holds with \(C=(C')^2\).
For \(t>0\), even under just (1), row sums and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(\|E\|_{\rm op}\le d(1+2\sqrt{C/(e^{2t}-1)})\).
Keeping both options in (6) therefore replaces \(1+r\) in (7) by
\[
      1+\frac{r^2}{d}+
        \min\left(r,\frac{r^2}{d}\left(1+\frac1{e^{2t}-1}\right)\right).       \tag{8}
\]
The constants in this refinement again depend only on \(\delta,C\).

<a id="poisson"></a>
## From the matrix to the reflected power sums

Put \(W=\|M\|_{\rm HS}^2/(d^2q)\).
For \(F_q(\theta)=q^{-1}|\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}e^{ik\theta}|^2\), expanding the square gives
\[
                   W=\iint F_q(\theta-\psi)\,d\mu_t(\theta)d\mu_t(\psi).
\]
Divide the circle into \(\lceil 2\pi q\rceil\) equal intervals, and write \(m_i\) for their masses.
Their lengths are comparable to \(1/q\) and are at most \(1/q\).
Since \(F_q\ge c q\) on \(|\theta|\le1/q\), positivity gives \(\sum_i m_i^2\ll W/q\).
Let \(x=1/d\) and \(P_y(\theta)=(1-e^{-2y})/|1-e^{-y+i\theta}|^2\).
For intervals separated by \(h\) steps in cyclic distance, \(P_{2x}(\theta-\psi)\ll_\delta d/(1+h^2)\), using \(q\asymp_\delta d\).
For each fixed step, the sum of products of the two interval masses is at most \(\sum_i m_i^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.
Summing the kernel bound yields
\[
                 \iint P_{2x}(\theta-\psi)\,d\mu_t(\theta)d\mu_t(\psi)
                         \ll_\delta W.                                         \tag{9}
\]

We compare (9) with \(t=0\) inside the Poisson measures while retaining the right side at the chosen \(t\).
By the Poisson convolution identity the left side is the average over all pairs of conjugates of \(P_y(\theta_i-\theta_j)\), with \(y=2x+|u_i-t|+|u_j-t|\).
For \(t=0\) the corresponding \(y'\) differs by at most \(2t\), and both parameters are at least \(2x\).
For \(y,y'>0\) the ratio of the two kernels has maximum over their common angle at zero or \(\pi\), by the fractional linear dependence on the cosine.
Its upper bound is consequently
\(\tanh(\max(y,y')/2)/\tanh(\min(y,y')/2)\le \max(y,y')/\min(y,y')\), since \(\tanh v/v\) decreases.
Thus the pairwise comparison costs at most \(1+t/x=1+dt\).
Writing \(v_j=\sum_\alpha e^{-j|u|+ij\theta}\) for the original reflected power sums, the left side at \(t=0\) is \(1+2\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-2xj}|v_j/d|^2\).
In particular
\[
                     \sum_{j=1}^d |v_j/d|^2\ll_\delta(1+dt)W .                 \tag{10}
\]
Comparing after forming this positive pair sum incurs only one factor of \(1+dt\).

<a id="consequences"></a>
## The first large power and the consequences for a central block

Apply the [normalized Blaschke identity (5)](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#filter) with all exterior roots.
Its coefficients satisfy \(f_0=1\) and \(\sum_{l\ge1}|f_l|^2=e^{2L}-1=O(L)\).
After separating the zeroth coefficient its identity gives
\[
 p_q=-q f_q+v_q+\sum_{1\le l<q} f_l(v_{q-l}-p_{q-l}).
\]
The [moving-power estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v149.md#powers) gives \(|v_q|/d=o(1)\) uniformly, including when the \(q\)-th powers repeat.
Dividing by \(d\), Cauchy–Schwarz with (1) and (10) bounds the right side in modulus by \(o(1)+O_C(\sqrt L)+O_\delta(\sqrt{L(1+dt)W})\).
As \(|p_q|/d\ge\eta\), this forces \(L(1+dt)W\gg_{\delta,\eta,C}1\) for every \(t\ge0\).
Substitution of (7) gives (2) and (3), and (8) gives the corresponding refined bound under (1).
At \(t=b\) there are no outliers.
At \(t=b_j\) there are at most \(j-1\) strict outliers even in case of ties, proving (4); taking \(t=1/d\) gives the stated strict count.
Taking \(j=\lfloor\sqrt d\rfloor\) in (4) yields the claimed lower count at the larger radial scale.

For the reciprocal central polynomials \(P=z^{2n}+1+z^nJ(z)\) of [the central span estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v149.md#central), with exact Laurent span parameter \(s<n/3\), the first nonzero depth \(q=n-s\) exceeds \(d/3\), where \(d=2n\).
That estimate already gives \((2s+1)L\ge\log(db)-O(1)\).
The conclusion at \(t=b\) strengthens this to
\[
                             (2s+1)L\ge\log(1/L)-O(1).                         \tag{11}
\]

<a id="compressed"></a>
## Power maps with substantial degree compression

Suppose the first nonzero coefficient occurs at \(q\ge\delta d\), let \(p\mid q\) be a positive integer that may vary, and put \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^p):\mathbb Q]\).
If \(e\le C_0d/p\) for a fixed \(C_0\), then \(e\ge q/p\) and
\[
                         r(1/(pe))\gg_{\delta,C_0}\sqrt{pd/L}.                 \tag{12}
\]
In particular \(1/(pe)\) is comparable to \(1/d\) with constants depending only on \(\delta,C_0\).
Indeed each conjugate of \(\alpha^p\) occurs \(d/e\) times in the full list of powers.
Its power sums through \(q/p-1\) vanish and the next, divided by \(e\), is \(p_q/d\) of modulus at least \(\delta\).
If \(e<q/p\), Newton's identities would force its monic minimal polynomial to be \(X^e\), contrary to its nonzero constant term.
Thus \(q/p\ge(\delta/C_0)e\) and its own logarithmic measure \(L'=peL/d\) lies between \(\delta L\) and \(C_0L\).
Applying (3) to this minimal polynomial at radial threshold \(1/e\) gives at least \(c\sqrt{e/L'}\) distinct exterior conjugates of \(\alpha^p\) there.
Restoring the multiplicities gives \(r(1/(pe))\gg(d/e)\sqrt{d/(pL)}\), which implies (12) by the assumed upper bound on \(e\).
