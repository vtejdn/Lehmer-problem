# Exact factor degrees at almost every prime exponent sum for a fixed integer pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior roots determine the degree of every factor

Fix an integer \(B\ge2\) and \(0<\gamma<(1-\log 2)/2\), with natural logarithms.
There is a set \(\mathcal P\) of relative natural density one among the primes such that the following holds for every sufficiently large \(\ell\in\mathcal P\).
For every integer \(1\le u\le\exp((\log\ell)^\gamma)\), put \(v=\ell-u\), and for each \(\epsilon=\pm1\) put
\[
 F=X^\ell-BX^v+\epsilon B X^u-\epsilon,\qquad
 P=F/(X-\epsilon).
\]
Let \(M_\ell\) be the order of the subgroup of \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\) generated by the primes dividing \(B\).
Write \(I_\ell=(\ell-1)/M_\ell\) and \(g=\gcd(u,I_\ell)\).
For each monic irreducible factor \(S\) of \(P\) write \(d=\deg S\) and let \(j\) count its roots of modulus greater than one.
Then
\[
              u d=j(\ell-1),\qquad
              (d,j)=t\left(\frac{\ell-1}{g},\frac{u}{g}\right)
              \quad\text{for some integer }t\ge1.
\]
The integers \(t\) over all factors, counted with multiplicity, sum to \(g\).
In particular \(P\) has no cyclotomic factor, it is irreducible whenever \(g=1\), and every factor satisfies
\[
 m(S)=\frac{t}{g}\bigl(\log B+O_B(e^{-c_B v/u})\bigr)
\]
for a constant \(c_B>0\).
A Salem factor of \(F(X^k)\) for any \(k\ge1\) requires \(u\mid I_\ell\), hence \(u\mid\ell-1\).
These conclusions hold in particular for the single-prime pole \(B=2\).
There is also an integer \(R_B\) depending only on \(B\) such that every noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(T\) of \(F(X^k)\) satisfies
\[
             m(T)\ge\frac{\log B}{2g\gcd(k,R_B)}.
\]
If \(g=1\), then \(P(X^k)\) is irreducible for all \(k\) coprime to \(R_B\).

For \(x\to\infty\) and real \(U=U(x)\to\infty\) with \(2U\le\exp((\log x)^\gamma)\), consider the pairs consisting of a prime \(\ell\in[x,2x]\) and an integer \(U<u\le2U\).
The proportion of such pairs admitting a Salem factor of \(F(X^k)\) for some sign and some \(k\) tends to zero.
If the short exponent \(u\) ranges only over primes in the same interval, the proportion of pairs for which \(P\) is irreducible in **both** signs tends to one.
The original prime set and the bounds on the exceptional proportions depend only on \(B,\gamma\).

<a id="size"></a>
## A uniform error in the integer relation

Throughout this range \(s=v/u\to\infty\) uniformly.
The [winding and exterior-root estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#winding) use only the polynomial and the slope in their root-count and annulus arguments.
Thus \(F\) has exactly \(u\) exterior roots with multiplicity, and each such \(z\) satisfies \(u\log|z|=\log B+O_B(e^{-c_Bs})\).
Here the prime subgroup condition of that earlier irreducibility result is unnecessary.
Since \(S\) is monic, this gives
\[
                     u\,m(S)=j\log B+O_B(j e^{-c_B s}).
\]

Apply the [individual factor-height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#statement), equation (1), to \(F=X^v A+C\), with \(A=X^u-B\) and \(C=\epsilon(BX^u-1)\).
The polynomials are coprime since \(B^2\ne1\), the first is monic, the second has constant coefficient \(\pm1\), and their absolute coefficient sums are \(B+1\).
On the circle their moduli are equal, and Jensen's formula gives the integral of \(\log|X^u-B|\) as \(\log B\).
Consequently
\[
           |v m(S)-d\log B|\ll_B (u+1)\sqrt{d\{u+1+\log(2d)\}}.
\]
This holds for every factor, including any putative cyclotomic factor.
Combining the two estimates, and using \(\ell-1=v+u-1\), yields
\[
 \big|u d-j(\ell-1)\big|
 \ll_B u(u+1)\sqrt{d\{u+1+\log(2d)\}}+vj e^{-c_Bs}+j(u-1).
\]
Since \(j\le u\), \(d\le\ell\), and \(u\le\exp((\log\ell)^\gamma)\), the right side is at most
\(\sqrt\ell\,\exp(O_{B,\gamma}((\log\ell)^\gamma))\).
Indeed \(s\ge(\ell-u)/\exp((\log\ell)^\gamma)\), which makes the term containing \(e^{-c_Bs}\) tend uniformly to zero.

<a id="order"></a>
## Frobenius orders force equality

Choose a single prime \(p\mid B\) and a real \(\delta\) with \(\gamma<\delta<(1-\log2)/2\).
The [fixed-rank order estimate of Pappalardi](../audits/literature/Pap96/Yin_U001.md#pap96-u001) implies
\[
        \operatorname{ord}_\ell(p)>\sqrt\ell\exp((\log\ell)^\delta)
\]
outside a set of primes of relative density zero.
Indeed in the rank-one theorem \(p\) is a fixed nonsquare prime, and one can take \(\psi(\ell)=(\log\log\ell)^{1/4}\).
For \(L=\log\log\ell\), the logarithm of the gain over \(\sqrt\ell\) is then at least \(\exp(((1-\log2)/2)L-L^{3/4})\), eventually larger than \(\exp(\delta L)\).
Choose these primes for \(\mathcal P\), discarding also the finitely many prime divisors of \(2B\).

Since \(\ell\) is odd, \(F(\epsilon)=0\).
For every prime \(q\mid B\) the reduction of \(P\) consists of a primitive \(\ell\)-th root grid in sign plus or characteristic two, and a primitive \(2\ell\)-th root grid in odd characteristic and sign minus.
Frobenius orbits in each grid have length \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)\), and monic factors preserve their degrees in each reduction. [Cyclotomic reductions for both signs](Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#reduction)
It follows that \(M_\ell=\operatorname{lcm}_{q\mid B}\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)\) divides both \(d\) and \(\ell-1\).
It therefore divides the integer \(ud-j(\ell-1)\), whose absolute value is smaller than \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(p)\) for every sufficiently large \(\ell\in\mathcal P\).
The integer must vanish.

Now \(d>0\) forces \(j>0\), and \(d/M_\ell=jI_\ell/u\) is an integer, so \(u/g\) divides \(j\).
The formula for \(t\) follows.
Summing \(j\) with multiplicity gives \(u\), proving the factor count and the stated measure formula.
If \(\alpha\) is Salem and is a zero of \(F(X^k)\), then \(\alpha^k\) is Salem and is a zero of \(P\).
Its factor has exactly one exterior conjugate, forcing \(u/g=1\).
The argument proves the necessary condition for every dilation without an assumption on the number of its factors.

The [bounded-denominator theorem for arbitrary complex radicals](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) applies with \(R_B=R_{2(B+1)}\), since every root \(\beta\) of \(P\) has the relation \(F(\beta)=0\) of this coefficient length.
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(T\), its \(k\)-th power is a root of some \(S\) as above, and \(m(T)/m(S)\) is a positive integer multiple of \(1/\gcd(k,R_B)\).
The measure estimate gives \(m(S)\ge\log B/(2g)\) uniformly for all sufficiently large selected totals.
If \(g=1\), the sole factor is \(P\), and the same theorem gives irreducibility of \(P(X^k)\) when \(\gcd(k,R_B)=1\).
In particular the conclusion for pairs with a prime short exponent below also holds simultaneously for these dilations.

On the [prime sums with three generating numerator primes](Yin_C_G0345_v760.md#statement), the same argument gives the complementary all-direction bound \(m(T)\ge\log(B/4)/\gcd(k,R_B)\) for a fixed integer \(B\) with those three divisors.
Here one uses \(m(P)\ge\log(B/4)\) and the irreducibility proved there at every positive direction, independently of the slope or signature.

<a id="counting"></a>
## The proportions of exceptional directions

We give the progression estimate needed in this range.
For an integer \(n\le\exp((\log x)^\gamma)\), the number of primes \(\ell\in[x,2x]\) with \(n\mid\ell-1\) is
\[
                             \ll \frac{x}{\varphi(n)\log x}.              \tag{1}
\]
To see this put \(z=x^{1/20}\), take the squarefree \(a\le z\) coprime to \(n\), and let \(G\) be the sum of \(1/\varphi(a)\) over this set.
In the interval \(x\le nm+1\le2x\), the number of integers \(m\) with \(a\mid nm+1\) is \(x/(na)+O(1)\) for each \(a\) coprime to \(n\).
As in the [upper-bound sieve for two forms](Yin_C_G0345_v222.md#sieve), majorize the indicator of a prime value by \((\sum_{a\mid nm+1}\lambda_a)^2\) for weights on the chosen squarefree integers with \(\lambda_1=1\).
The main quadratic form after counting these divisibilities is
\[
 \sum_{a,b}\frac{\lambda_a\lambda_b}{[a,b]}
       =\sum_d\varphi(d)\left(\sum_{d\mid a}\frac{\lambda_a}{a}\right)^2,
\]
where \([a,b]\) denotes the least common multiple.
Prescribe the inner sums as \(\mu(d)/(\varphi(d)G)\) for each chosen \(d\).
Möbius inversion on this divisor-closed set gives \(\lambda_1=1\) and \(|\lambda_a|\le a\), and the quadratic form equals \(1/G\).
The total counting remainder is \(O(z^4)\).
Grouping \(\sum_{m\le z}1/m\) by the radical of \(m\) bounds it above by the sum of \(1/\varphi(a)\) over all squarefree \(a\le z\).
Separating the part of \(a\) supported on primes dividing \(n\) bounds this latter sum by \(G\prod_{q\mid n}(1+1/(q-1))=Gn/\varphi(n)\).
Thus \(G\gg(\varphi(n)/n)\log x\), and \(z^4\) is absorbed in (1) uniformly for \(n=x^{o(1)}\) in the specified range.

The primes outside \(\mathcal P\) account for \(o(1)\) of all pairs, both with integer and with prime short exponents, by relative density one and the prime number theorem.
At each remaining prime a Salem factor requires \(u\mid\ell-1\).
Since \(u/\varphi(u)=\sum_{a\mid u}\mu^2(a)/\varphi(a)\) and \(\sum_a\mu^2(a)/(a\varphi(a))=\prod_q(1+1/(q(q-1)))<\infty\), one has \(\sum_{U<u\le2U}1/\varphi(u)\ll1\).
Summing (1) gives \(O(x/\log x)\) candidate pairs, among \(\asymp Ux/\log x\) pairs in total.
For a prime \(u\), reducibility of \(P\) at a good \(\ell\) requires \(u\mid I_\ell\), hence again \(u\mid\ell-1\).
Here \(1/\varphi(u)=1/(u-1)\ll1/U\).
Summing (1) over precisely the short primes and dividing by the number of pairs gives \(O(1/U)\), in addition to the same \(o(1)\) from the exceptional totals.
