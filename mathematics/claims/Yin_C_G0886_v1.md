# A canonical Coxeter cover for all fixed-cusp words over E8 blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statement and scope

Let \(r\ge1\), let \(H_r=E_8^{\oplus r}\) with its standard positive integral pairing, and put \(L_r=H_r\oplus U\), with \((e,e)=(f,f)=0\) and \((e,f)=1\).
For every \(v\in H_r\), use the integral transvections
\[
T_{e,v}(x)=x-(v,x)e+(e,x)v-\tfrac12(v,v)(e,x)e
\]
and \(T_{f,v}\) obtained by exchanging \(e,f\).
Extend every element of \(O(H_r)\) to fix \(e,f\).
Let \(\Gamma_r\) be the group generated by these positive isometries and both entire transvection groups.

Consider any specified finite word in these generators, with value \(A\in\Gamma_r\).
Each positive-isometry factor permutes the \(r\) orthogonal E8 components.
Let \(\pi\in S_r\) be the ordered product of those permutations, and put \(d=\operatorname{ord}(\pi)\), with \(d=1\) for the identity.
The definition of \(\pi\) uses this specified word; no presentation-independent permutation homomorphism on \(\Gamma_r\) is asserted.

Let \(\lambda_{\rm L}>1\) be the Lehmer spectral threshold in the accepted McM02 canonical Coxeter theorem.
Then the complete characteristic polynomial \(P_A(z)=\det(zI-A)\) satisfies
\[
M(P_A)=\rho(A),\qquad
M(P_A)=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad
M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}^{\,1/d}.                         \tag{1}
\]
Consequently every subfamily admitting such word presentations with \(d\le D\), for one fixed positive integer \(D\), has the uniform complete-logarithmic gap
\[
m(P_A)=0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
m(P_A)\ge \frac{\log\lambda_{\rm L}}{D}.                   \tag{2}
\]
There is no bound on \(r\), word length, parameter coordinates or transvection norms in (2).

For \(r=3\), every permutation has order at most three, so every \(A\in\Gamma_3\) satisfies \(M(P_A)=1\) or \(M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}^{1/3}\).
If a sequence in these groups has \(1<M(P_{A_j})\to1\), the permutation orders in every choice of the stated word presentations must tend to infinity.
Unbounded permutation order is only a necessary condition; no shrinking family is constructed.

<a id="argument"></a>
## Argument and source interfaces

The proof constructs an actual finite-generator canonical Coxeter representation covering the specified generators, rather than identifying them with the entire root-reflection group of the ambient Lorentzian lattice.
The accepted [McM02 theorem and normalization](../audits/literature/McM02/Yin_U002.md#canonical-coxeter-gap-input) apply to that explicit representation.
The [degree-18 diagram model](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0158/coxeter_model.md#two-transvection-model) is its two-block calibration, not the proof of the unbounded-block statement.

The standard finite E8 root facts used below are that its full integral isometry group is the Weyl group generated by the simple-root reflections, and that this group is transitive on the norm-two roots.
The [WvL21 statement and root-orbit unit](../audits/literature/WvL21/Yin_U001.md#full-isometry-and-transitivity) checks the full isometry group and transitivity, and the [JKZ08 simple-generator unit](../audits/literature/JKZ08/Yin_U001.md#simple-generators-and-gram) checks the eight generators and the exact Cartan-basis correspondence.
Accept these established external statements at their recorded statement, convention and applicability depth; their cited foundational root-system proofs are not newly audited here.
No classification of the full orthogonal group of \(L_r\) is required.

<a id="positive-blocks"></a>
### The positive components and their isometries

Use the E8 simple basis \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_8\) whose Gram \(K\) has diagonal two and off-diagonal minus one on the chain
\[
1-2-3-4-5-6-7
\]
and the additional edge \(3-8\), with zero on other distinct pairs.
The leading principal minors are \(2,3,4,5,6,7,8,1\), so this is positive, even and unimodular.
In this basis put
\[
\theta=2\alpha_1+4\alpha_2+6\alpha_3+5\alpha_4
       +4\alpha_5+3\alpha_6+2\alpha_7+3\alpha_8 .
\]
Direct multiplication gives \(K\theta=(0,0,0,0,0,0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\), and hence \((\theta,\theta)=2\).
These exact finite data agree with the [independently checked E8 block](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v6.md#coxeter-two-transvection-model-review).

Every nonzero vector of an E8 block has even positive norm, at least two.
A norm-two vector of \(H_r\) therefore lies in exactly one block.
Within each block, the graph on all norm-two vectors joined by nonzero pairing is connected: it contains the connected simple-root basis, and every other nonzero root pairs nontrivially with some basis vector.
There are no such edges between different blocks.
An integral isometry of \(H_r\) preserves this root graph and thus permutes its \(r\) connected components.
Each component spans its full integral E8 block, since it contains the simple basis.
After the block permutation is removed, the isometry acts separately on the blocks.
The finite E8 input above therefore gives
\[
O(H_r)=W(E_8)^r\rtimes S_r.                              \tag{3}
\]

<a id="canonical-star"></a>
### An explicit canonical star and its entire integral quotient

Let \(F_r\) be the free integer module on \(9r+1\) symbols
\[
a_{j,i}\ (1\le j\le r,\ 1\le i\le8),\qquad
b_j\ (1\le j\le r),\qquad c.
\]
Define a surjection \(q:F_r\longrightarrow L_r\) by
\[
q(a_{j,i})=\alpha_{j,i},\qquad
q(b_j)=e-\theta_j,\qquad
q(c)=-e-f,
\]
where the subscript \(j\) places the root in the \(j\)-th block.
Write \(\vartheta_j=2a_{j,1}+4a_{j,2}+6a_{j,3}+5a_{j,4}+4a_{j,5}+3a_{j,6}+2a_{j,7}+3a_{j,8}\) in \(F_r\), so \(q(\vartheta_j)=\theta_j\).
Surjectivity is integral: \(e=q(b_1+\vartheta_1)\) and \(f=q(-c-b_1-\vartheta_1)\), besides all positive basis vectors.

Pull back the form on \(L_r\) to \(F_r\).
Every displayed basis vector has norm two.
Within each block the \(a_{j,i}\) have the E8 Cartan pairings; \(b_j\) pairs to minus one only with \(a_{j,7}\) and with \(c\).
Different \(b_j\)'s are orthogonal, and all other undisplayed pairings are zero.
Thus this Gram matrix is exactly the canonical geometric form of the finite-generator simply-laced Coxeter graph obtained by attaching each of \(r\) affine E8 blocks to the common vertex \(c\).
Only exponents two and three occur, so no normalization at an infinite Coxeter exponent is involved.

Since the target form is nondegenerate, the rational radical of this pullback is \(\ker(q\otimes\mathbb Q)\).
Its full integer radical is the primitive free subgroup
\[
N=\bigoplus_{j=2}^r
\mathbb Z\bigl(b_j+\vartheta_j-b_1-\vartheta_1\bigr).       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed these vectors vanish under \(q\), and eliminating \(b_2,\ldots,b_r\) by them leaves the \(8r\) positive vectors, \(b_1,c\) as an integral quotient basis.
Consequently \(F_r/N\cong L_r\), with no torsion or unverified overlattice.
The canonical form has signature \((8r+1,1,r-1)\).

Let \(\mathcal W_r\) denote the canonical matrix group generated by the basis reflections on \(F_r\otimes\mathbb R\).
Each reflection fixes the radical pointwise and descends through \(q\) to the norm-two reflection in the corresponding displayed root of \(L_r\).
For any \(w\in\mathcal W_r\), writing \(\bar w\) for the quotient action, the complete polynomials satisfy
\[
\chi_w(z)=(z-1)^{r-1}\chi_{\bar w}(z).                    \tag{5}
\]
This identity follows from the invariant radical and quotient, including all algebraic multiplicities.
In particular, the complete measures and spectral radii of these two actions agree, since the only additional eigenvalues are ones.

<a id="generator-coverage"></a>
### Coverage of both entire transvection groups

The positive simple-root reflections already occur among the generators of \(\mathcal W_r\).
In the quotient, first applying the positive reflection in \(\theta_j\) and then the reflection in \(q(b_j)=e-\theta_j\) gives
\[
s_{e-\theta_j}s_{\theta_j}=T_{e,\theta_j}.                \tag{6}
\]
To check this on the complete lattice, put \(s=(e,x)\) and \(t=(\theta_j,x)\).
The first reflection sends \(x\) to \(x-t\theta_j\), whose pairing with \(e-\theta_j\) is \(s+t\).
The second reflection therefore gives \(x+s\theta_j-(t+s)e\), precisely the [retained transvection formula](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#integer-generators) with half-norm one.

The finite E8 Weyl group is transitive on roots, so conjugating (6) by positive Weyl words gives \(T_{e,\alpha_{j,i}}\) for every simple basis vector.
For arbitrary positive vectors, the [addition and inverse identities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#short-word-reduction) give
\[
T_{e,u}T_{e,v}=T_{e,u+v},\qquad T_{e,v}^{-1}=T_{e,-v}.
\]
Every integral vector of \(H_r\) is an integral sum of these simple basis vectors, so the entire group \(T_{e,H_r}\) is in the quotient image of \(\mathcal W_r\), with no coordinate or norm bound.

The reflection in \(q(c)=-(e+f)\) sends \(e\) to \(-f\), sends \(f\) to \(-e\), and fixes \(H_r\).
Its conjugation sends \(T_{e,v}\) to \(T_{f,-v}\).
Hence the entire other cusp group \(T_{f,H_r}\) is covered too.
This proves coverage by the explicit canonical subgroup, not a generation theorem for every isometry of \(L_r\).

<a id="permutations-and-gap"></a>
### Permutations, powers and complete Mahler measure

Every block permutation \(\pi\) lifts to the diagram automorphism \(\widetilde\pi\) of \(F_r\) that permutes the corresponding \(a\)-blocks and \(b_j\)'s and fixes \(c\).
This lift preserves the canonical Gram matrix, normalizes \(\mathcal W_r\), and descends to the stated block permutation on \(L_r\), fixing \(e,f\).

By (3) and the generator coverage, the specified word for \(A\) has a canonical-space lift of the form
\[
\widetilde A=\widetilde\pi w,\qquad w\in\mathcal W_r,
\qquad q\widetilde A=Aq.
\]
The product permutation is exactly the ordered product specified in the statement.
Moving diagram automorphisms across Coxeter factors uses their normalization of \(\mathcal W_r\); it does not require any commuting isometries.
Since \(\widetilde\pi^d=I\), one has \(\widetilde A^d\in\mathcal W_r\).
Its quotient is \(A^d\), so (5) gives
\[
\chi_{\widetilde A^d}(z)=(z-1)^{r-1}\chi_{A^d}(z).        \tag{7}
\]

An isometry of \(L_r\) has at most one expanding eigenvalue counted with multiplicity, by the [negative-index argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#negative-index-bound).
Its spectrum is reciprocal and \(\rho(A)\ge1\); hence its complete monic measure is exactly \(\rho(A)\), including the neutral case.
When \(\rho(A)>1\), (7) and the accepted canonical Coxeter theorem imply
\[
\rho(A)^d=\rho(A^d)=\rho(\widetilde A^d)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}.
\]
This proves (1) and (2).
Equivalently \(m(P_{A^d})=d\,m(P_A)\); taking the power is used with its full cost, not substituted as an unchanged-polynomial realization.

For \(r=3\), permutations have orders one, two or three, proving the stated concrete group bound.
For any sequence with \(1<M(P_{A_j})\to1\), (1) gives
\[
d\ge\frac{\log\lambda_{\rm L}}{\log M(P_A)},
\]
so \(d\) must tend to infinity for every such chosen presentation.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Scope of the coverage result

The root-reflection group of \(II_{25,1}\) in the [Leech chamber description](../audits/literature/Bor96/Yin_U001.md#canonical-normalization-boundary) can have actual simple-root pairings below minus two.
That does not refute the present canonical cover: the proof specifies a particular finite star with only zero and minus-one off-diagonal entries and proves that it covers the requested generators over \(E_8^r\).
It neither identifies that subgroup with the entire ambient reflection group nor changes the Leech chamber's normalization.

The result does not apply automatically to positive Leech summands, other positive lattices, arbitrary cusps, or general indefinite-lattice isometries.
It imposes no absolute bound when the product permutation order is unbounded.
Unbounded word length or vector norms with bounded product permutation order cannot yield positive measures tending to one in this domain.
Increasing permutation order is not evidence of small radius, nonneutrality or new irreducible cores.
The unrestricted [G0001 contract](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) remains unresolved.
