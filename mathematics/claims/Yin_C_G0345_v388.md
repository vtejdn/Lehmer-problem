# Irreducibility under every power substitution in every primitive cubic direction

<a id="statement"></a>
## Complete classification of the power substitutions

Put \(g=T^3-T-1,\ B=1-T^2-T^3,\ A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\).
Let \(a,b\) be any coprime positive integers and \(P\) the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(A_{a,b}\).
For every integer \(q\ge1\), \(P(X^q)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), has degree \(q\deg P\), and has the same logarithmic Mahler measure as \(P\).
Consequently all nontorsion factors of every positive specialization with exponents \(qa,qb\) come from these substitutions with their original multiplicities.
The set of positive factor measures over the whole family is exactly the set over primitive directions.

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
The [dyadic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v380.md#statement) excludes both \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\) from \(K^2\).
We prove that \(\alpha\notin K^p\) for every odd prime \(p\), including the entire [finite region previously left open](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#statement).

<a id="small"></a>
## Third, fifth and seventh powers

Suppose \(\alpha=\gamma^p\) with \(\gamma\in K\).
The element \(\gamma\) is a nontorsion algebraic integer unit generating \(K\).
Let \(x\) denote the residue of a conjugate of \(\alpha\) at a fixed place above \(p\) in the splitting field, \(m_x\) its multiplicity in the reduction of \(-A_{a,b}\), and \(t=x^a\).
The ramification index \(e\) of the local root field is at most \(m_x\), since its local minimal polynomial reduces to a power of the minimal polynomial of \(x\), with exponent at least \(e\).
For \(F(U,V)=g(U)+VB(U)\), Frobenius gives \(v_p(F(\gamma^a,\gamma^b))\ge1/e\), using the corresponding embedding, \(v_p(p)=1\) and \(v_p(0)=+\infty\).
If \(m_x<p\), raising to the \(p\)-th power and dividing by \(p\) still leaves positive valuation.
Substitution of \(V=-g(U)/B(U)\) in the first Frobenius correction therefore gives \(H_p(t)=0\bmod p\), where
\[
 H_p(T)=\bigl(g(T^p) B(T)^p+(-g(T))^p B(T^p)\bigr)/p .
\]
The [graph substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#frobenius) first gives the identity at \(U=\overline{\gamma^a}\) and Frobenius gives it at \(t=U^p\).

For \(p=3,5,7\), the [small-prime calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0171/readme.md#residues) shows that all admissible zeros of \(H_p\) are reductions of the torsion graph coordinates, and every other residue would have \(m_x<p\).
Every torsion graph residue in the parent admits a characteristic-zero torsion root of this same parent, by uniqueness of its prime-to-\(p\) lift.
Thus at most \(m_x-1\) conjugates of \(\alpha\) can reduce to any remaining \(x\).
Their sum is at most six by the [derivative bound](Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#inertia).
At \(p=3\) and slope \(b/a=0\bmod3\) with \(3\nmid a\), the computed upper bound is zero.

If \(\alpha\) were nonreciprocal, \(\gamma\) would be nonreciprocal and the reciprocal parent would contain the distinct minimal polynomials of \(\alpha\) and its inverse.
Smyth's theorem would give \(M(A_{a,b})\ge M(\gamma)^{2p}\ge\theta^{2p}>\sqrt8\), with \(\theta>1.3\) the plastic number, contradicting the parent coefficient norm.
For reciprocal \(\alpha\), let \(\iota\) invert it in \(K\).
Then \(\delta=\gamma\iota(\gamma)\) is a \(p\)-th root of unity fixed by \(\iota\), and \(\eta=\gamma\delta^{(p-1)/2}\) generates \(K\), has \(\eta^p=\alpha\) and satisfies \(\iota(\eta)=\eta^{-1}\).
The [exact reciprocal polynomial enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0171/readme.md#graeffe) yields \(M(\eta)>27/20\) in degree at most six.
Consequently \(M(A_{a,b})\ge M(\eta)^p>(27/20)^p\).
This exceeds \(\sqrt6\) already for \(p=3\) and \(\sqrt8\) for \(p\ge5\).
The parent has coefficient norm at most \(\sqrt6\) except at \((a,b)=(1,3)\), where \(p=3\) was excluded by its zero slope.

<a id="bounds"></a>
## Bounding the remaining primes and the shorter exponent

For \(p\ge11\) let \(v_i=\log|\alpha_i|\) over all \(d\) embeddings and \(m=\log M(P)\le\log\sqrt8<1.04\).
The positive \(v_i\) sum to \(m\), and the absolute values of the negative \(v_i\) sum to \(m\).
Apply the [all-root square bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) to \(\gamma\), whose conjugate logarithms are \(v_i/p\).
It gives
\[
     p^2\le10d\sum_i v_i^2\le20d\,m^2,\qquad d\le6p+12.                 \tag{1}
\]
The degree estimate is the [simple-residue count](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#frobenius).
The function \(p^2/(6p+12)\) increases with positive \(p\), and at \(p=137\) it exceeds \(20(1.04)^2\).
Thus the prime \(p\) is at most 131.

Put \(G=\min(a,b)\).
The two endpoint coefficients are units, and every other exponent has distance at least \(G\) from its nearest endpoint whenever it is compared with that endpoint.
In particular the first gap and the last gap between occupied exponents are both at least \(G\).
The coefficient one-norm is at most six, so comparison with the leading coefficient at an exterior root and reversal at an interior root give \(\lvert v_i\rvert\le(\log6)/G\).
It follows from (1) that
\[
       pG\le20(d/p)m\log6
       \le20(6+12/11)(1.04)(1.8)<270 .
\]
The [exponent difference bound](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#finite) gives \(\lvert b-3a\rvert\le2858\) for every putative odd power.

<a id="remaining"></a>
## Counting the residue orbits at primes at least eleven

The derivative bound \(\sum_x(m_x-1)\le6\) gives \(m_x\le7<p\) at every residue, including the case \(p\mid a\) of simple reduction.
Thus every conjugate of \(\alpha\) has an admissible coordinate \(t=x^a\) with \(H_p(t)=0\).
On the graph \(x^b=f(t)=-g(t)/B(t)\), hence \(t^b=f(t)^a\).
Both coordinates are nonzero and \(B(t)\ne0\), and the pair determines \(x\) by the integral Bézout identity for \(a,b\).

If \(t\) has order \(j=1,2,3,5,8,12,18,30\), its prime-to-\(p\) root-of-unity lift \(\tau\) has \(f(\tau)\) a root of unity of order prime to \(p\) by the [torsion table](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic).
For the lift \(\xi\) of \(x\) the identities \(\xi^a=\tau,\ \xi^b=f(\tau)\) follow by uniqueness, producing a torsion occurrence over \(x\).
All other eligible coordinates occur in Frobenius orbits of the finite-field factors of \(H_p\) after the exclusions of \(TgB\) and those eight cyclotomic polynomials.
Let \(D\) be the sum of the degrees of the distinct remaining factors satisfying \(T^b=f(T)^a\) in their residue fields.
There are at most \(D\) residues \(x\) outside the torsion list.
Counting one occurrence at each of these residues and at most \(m_x-1\) further nontorsion occurrences at every residue gives
\[
                            d\le6+D .
\]
For all prime and exponent ranges proved above, the [exact orbit calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0172/readme.md#orbits) gives \(D=0\) at 11 and 13 and \(D\le4\) at every other prime.
Thus \(d\le10\); for \(p\ge17\) this contradicts \(p^2\le20d(1.04)^2\).
At \(p=13\), \(d\le6\) contradicts the same inequality.
At \(p=11,\ d\le6\), (1) is impossible with \(m\le\log\sqrt6<0.9\).
In the only larger-norm direction \((1,3)\), the [exterior root count](Yin_C_G0345_v386.md#count) excludes real exterior roots, and reciprocity of the parent excludes real interior roots.
Each nonzero logarithm therefore occurs with its conjugate, giving \(\sum_i v_i^2\le m^2\).
Then \(11^2\le10d(1.04)^2\) is also impossible.

<a id="dilations"></a>
## Passage to arbitrary exponents

The preceding exclusions and the two dyadic sign exclusions apply to every nontorsion factor individually.
The [binomial norm induction](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions) gives \([K(\alpha^{1/q}):K]=q\) for any \(q\ge1\) and any root.
The degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is therefore \(dq\), and the \(q\) roots above each conjugate contribute its original logarithmic measure.
Distinct factors lift disjointly, multiplicities are preserved because the power map has nonzero derivative at each root, and every lift of a torsion root has finite order.
Taking the common divisor of the two positive exponents proves the assertion about the full set of measures.
