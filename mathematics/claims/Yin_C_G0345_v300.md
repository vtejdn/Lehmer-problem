# Bounded rational relations between small-height algebraic numbers

The coordinating agent supplied the uniform torsion-collision argument and its application to additive transformations of real circuit roots.
The agent-s global strategy contributor checked the phase argument, the retained source interface, the earlier sparse-power results and the complete output application, and authored this fixed account.
Both contributed to the proof.

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform torsion collision

Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height.
For every integer \(B\ge2\), there are constants \(\eta_B>0\) and \(K_B\ge1\), depending only on \(B\), with the following property.
Suppose \(2\le s\le B\), \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\), and
\[
                 \sum_{i=1}^s c_i u_i=0,
             \qquad c_i\in\mathbb Q^{*},\qquad
                 \max_i h(u_i/u_1)<\eta_B.                         \tag{1}
\]
Then some distinct \(i,j\) satisfy
\[
                 u_i/u_j\in\mu_\infty,\qquad
                 \operatorname{ord}(u_i/u_j)\le K_B.              \tag{2}
\]
There is no bound on the rational coefficients, their denominators, field degrees or the individual heights \(h(u_i)\).
Only the number of terms and their relative heights occur in the hypotheses.
The constants are existential; this argument gives no evaluated numerical values.

The constants can moreover be chosen so that any family of at most \(B\) nonzero algebraic numbers satisfying the relative-height bound and having no pair with quotient a root of unity of order at most \(K_B\) is rationally linearly independent.
In particular, distinct positive real algebraic numbers satisfying this bound are rationally linearly independent.
A sufficient condition for that positive-real conclusion is
\[
                         \max_i h(u_i)<\eta_B/2.                   \tag{3}
\]
Positivity is needed only at the selected real embedding: a positive real root of unity is one.

<a id="phase"></a>
## Proof from one-dimensional equidistribution

The external input is Bilu's Theorem 1.1, with its precise height, strictness and orbit-measure conventions in [Bil97-U001](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures).
Only its one-dimensional statement is used.
The source unit's statement-level acceptance and original-proof inspection limits are unchanged.

Suppose the assertion fails for a fixed \(B\).
For every positive integer \(n\), choose a relation as in (1) with all relative heights less than \(1/n\), but with no pair quotient a root of unity of order at most \(n\).
Pass to a subsequence on which \(s\) is fixed.
Put
\[
 z_{i,n}=u_{i,n}/u_{1,n},\qquad
 a_{i,n}=c_{i,n}/\max_j|c_{j,n}|.
\]
Thus \(z_{1,n}=1\), \(|a_{i,n}|\le1\), at least one \(|a_{i,n}|=1\), and
\[
                         \sum_i a_{i,n}z_{i,n}=0.                 \tag{4}
\]

Choose a number field \(F_n\) containing all the \(z_{i,n}\), and give its embeddings into \(\mathbb C\) equal probability.
For any nonzero algebraic \(z\in F_n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma\bigl|\log|\sigma z|\bigr|
 \le h(z)+h(z^{-1})=2h(z).                                        \tag{5}
\]
Indeed the positive and negative archimedean parts are bounded separately by these two heights, including their finite-place contributions.
Consequently \(|\sigma z_{i,n}|\to1\) in probability for every \(i\).
With \(\operatorname{ph}(z)=z/|z|\), (4) gives
\[
 L_n(\sigma):=\sum_i a_{i,n}\operatorname{ph}(\sigma z_{i,n})
   =\sum_i a_{i,n}\operatorname{ph}(\sigma z_{i,n})
                                  (1-|\sigma z_{i,n}|).
\]
The right side tends to zero in probability, since there are at most \(B\) summands.
On the left, \(|L_n|\le B\), so
\[
                              \mathbb E|L_n|^2\longrightarrow0.    \tag{6}
\]
This step uses bounded phase sums, and does not assume convergence of any positive radial moment.

For \(i\ne j\), the sequence \(w_n=z_{i,n}/z_{j,n}\) has height tending to zero.
It is strict in \(\mathbb G_m\): every proper algebraic subgroup is a finite group \(\mu_N\), and our choice excludes membership in \(\mu_N\) once \(n\ge N\).
The common-field embedding average pushes forward to the uniform measure on the conjugates of \(w_n\), because every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(w_n)\) has the same number of extensions to \(F_n\).
The bounded continuous function \(\operatorname{ph}\) has integral zero on the unit circle.
Bilu's theorem therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   \operatorname{ph}(\sigma z_{i,n})
   \overline{\operatorname{ph}(\sigma z_{j,n})}\right]
       =\mathbb E\operatorname{ph}(\sigma w_n)\longrightarrow0.    \tag{7}
\]
The diagonal entries of this Gram matrix are one.
Since there are a fixed number of entries and \(|a_{i,n}|\le1\),
\[
 \mathbb E|L_n|^2
     =\sum_i |a_{i,n}|^2+o(1)\ge1+o(1),                            \tag{8}
\]
contradicting (6).
Rationality of the coefficients is used in (4) at every embedding and in keeping the same normalized coefficients in (7)–(8).
No bounded-height hypothesis on those coefficients is needed.
This proves (1)–(2).

For the independence formulation, apply the result to the support of any purported rational relation.
If its support omits \(u_1\), choosing a new reference can double the relative-height bound.
To avoid that reference issue, fix the constants initially obtained above as \(\eta_B^{\,0},K_B\), and use
\[
                              \eta_B=\eta_B^{\,0}/2               \tag{9}
\]
throughout the statement.
For a support omitting \(u_1\), every ratio within that support then has height less than \(2\eta_B=\eta_B^{\,0}\).
The collision conclusion still follows.
The theorem itself remains valid with this smaller constant.
For positive distinct numbers such a collision is impossible, and (3) follows from subadditivity of height.

<a id="sparse-output"></a>
## A bounded additive encoding forces multiplicative saturation

Let \(\Gamma\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}\) be a multiplicative group, and let
\[
          \sum_{i=1}^s c_i a_i y^{k_i}=0,\qquad
     2\le s\le B,\quad c_i\in\mathbb Q^{*},\quad
     a_i\in\Gamma,\quad k_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad y\ne0.                \tag{10}
\]
Assume
\[
 \max_i h\!\left(\frac{a_i y^{k_i}}{a_1y^{k_1}}\right)<\eta_B,
 \qquad
 k_i=k_j,\ i\ne j\ \Longrightarrow\ a_i/a_j\notin\mu_\infty.       \tag{11}
\]
Applying (1) to the actual terms yields indices with \(k_i\ne k_j\) and
\[
                y^{k_i-k_j}=\zeta\,a_j/a_i,
                   \qquad \operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\le K_B.      \tag{12}
\]
In particular
\[
 y\in\widetilde\Gamma:=
 \{v\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:
       v^r\in\mu_\infty\Gamma\text{ for some integer }r\ge1\}.      \tag{13}
\]
The same-power assumption can be weakened to excluding only torsion quotients of order at most \(K_B\).
It cannot be omitted: a relation among coefficients at one power can impose no condition on \(y\).

For the applications below, \(\Gamma\cap\mu_\infty\subseteq\{1,-1\}\).
At each fixed \(k\), group the \(a_i\)'s by their quotient being \(1\) or \(-1\), absorb those signs into their rational coefficients, sum the coefficients and delete zero sums.
Choose a representative from the original list for every surviving group.
The resulting list has no same-power torsion quotient and has no more terms than the original.
This is an exact collection of values, not a claim that formal Laurent monomials were distinct before evaluation.
A faithful encoding here means that
\[
                         \sum_i c_i a_i Y^{k_i}                   \tag{14}
\]
is not the zero Laurent polynomial after the input values \(a_i\) have been substituted.
Thus the collected list is nonempty; if it vanishes at a nonzero \(y\), it has at least two terms.
Whenever the relative-height hypothesis holds for that surviving list, (12) applies.
An empty collected expression, or an identically zero evaluated polynomial, is not a constraint on an output.

<a id="height-budget"></a>
## Heights for varying Laurent monomials in conjugates

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible integer polynomial of degree \(d\), whose roots are algebraic units, and put \(\mu=\log M(P)\).
Each conjugate \(\gamma\) has height \(\mu/d\).
For
\[
       a_i=\prod_{\gamma:P(\gamma)=0}\gamma^{v_{i,\gamma}},
        \qquad \sum_\gamma|v_{i,\gamma}|\le L,\qquad |k_i|\le K,
\]
height subadditivity gives
\[
 h(a_i)\le L\mu/d,\qquad
 h\!\left(\frac{a_i y^{k_i}}{a_jy^{k_j}}\right)
          \le 2\bigl(L\mu/d+K h(y)\bigr).                         \tag{15}
\]
Choosing surviving representatives during collection preserves these bounds.
Consequently
\[
                      2\bigl(L\mu/d+K h(y)\bigr)<\eta_B           \tag{16}
\]
is sufficient for (11).
For sequences, it is enough that
\[
                       L\mu/d+K h(y)\longrightarrow0.            \tag{17}
\]
The number of input roots, their field degree, the rational coefficients, the exponent vectors and the powers \(k_i\) may all vary.
There is no separate bound on exponents beyond this height budget, and no assumption that the input roots are multiplicatively independent.
For a nonzero algebraic output with minimal-polynomial Mahler measure \(M_y\) and degree \(d_y\), the exact identity \(h(y)=\log M_y/d_y\) applies.
Thus vanishing total output measure implies \(h(y)\to0\), but alone does not pay for arbitrarily growing \(K\).

<a id="real-circuits"></a>
## Exterior-count obstruction for full-permutation real circuits

Assume now the full-permutation real-circuit hypotheses of [C345v299](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#saturation).
Explicitly, \(P\) is monic irreducible reciprocal of degree \(2m\), its \(e\ge3\) exterior roots are all real, and their positive logarithmic moduli form a rational circuit.
Orient the representatives on each circuit block so their product is \(1\) or \(-1\).
The stabilizer of the exterior block must induce every permutation and simultaneous inversion on its \(e\) oriented representatives.
When a neutral block exists, [C345v299's conjugation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#neutral-inversion) shows that every unsigned permutation suffices.
This is an additional action hypothesis, not a property asserted for all real circuits.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the group generated by all roots of \(P\).
The [real projection argument](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) gives
\[
                          \Gamma\cap\mu_\infty\subseteq\{1,-1\}.  \tag{18}
\]
Thus the same-power collection above uses rational coefficients throughout.
If a faithful relation (10), with \(a_i\in\Gamma\), satisfies the small-height hypothesis after collection, every nontorsion output \(y\) has at least
\[
 \nu(e)=
 \begin{cases}
 3,&e=4,\\
 e,&e=3\text{ or }e\ge5
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{19}
\]
exterior conjugates.
Indeed (12) puts it in \(\widetilde\Gamma\), and the [saturation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#saturation) gives exactly (19).
That theorem also implies that \(y\) is an algebraic unit.

In particular consider any hypothetical sequence of such inputs, with the full action required separately at each term and with the number of terms in their faithful encodings bounded by one fixed \(B\).
If (17) holds, no sequence of nontorsion outputs with only one or two exterior conjugates can be produced eventually.
This applies in particular when both input and output total measures tend to zero and the chosen exponent bounds satisfy (17).
Neither a sequence of shrinking real-circuit inputs nor a source of their required Galois actions is supplied here.

For \(e=4\), the lower count three is sharp within these sparse transformations, conditionally on any such input.
The [four-to-three construction](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#four-descent) supplies
\[
                           y=x_1^2x_2^2
\]
for two oriented roots of one block, with exactly three exterior conjugates and \(\log M_y\le2\log M(P)\).
It has the faithful two-term encoding \(Y-x_1^2x_2^2=0\), \(L=4\), \(K=1\), and \(h(y)\le4\mu/d\).
Thus along any hypothetical shrinking sequence of these inputs its height budget tends to zero.
This is sharpness of the permitted exterior count, without an existence assertion for that shrinking sequence.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Trace equations and the remaining boundary

A direct example is
\[
                         y+y^{-1}=F(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_d),    \tag{20}
\]
where \(F\) is a rational Laurent polynomial with at most \(r\) terms and rational coefficients.
After evaluating the input roots and collecting signs, there are at most \(r+2\) terms in (20).
Its evaluated Laurent polynomial in \(Y\) is nonzero because the coefficients of \(Y\) and \(Y^{-1}\) are one.
The possible output exponents are \(-1,0,1\).
Under the height budget, (12) therefore gives a torsion monomial expression for either \(y\) or \(y^2\).
For nontorsion \(y\), the collision between the \(Y\) and \(Y^{-1}\) terms would force \(y^2\) torsion and is impossible; any collision must involve an evaluated term of \(F\).
In that case \(y\) itself is a root of unity times an input monomial.

More generally let \(F=A/B\), with rational Laurent polynomials \(A,B\) evaluated at the input roots and \(B(\gamma)\ne0\).
Clearing that actual nonzero denominator gives
\[
              B(\gamma)y+B(\gamma)y^{-1}-A(\gamma)=0.             \tag{21}
\]
The evaluated polynomial is faithful.
If \(A,B\) have a bounded total number of monomials, (21) has a bounded number of terms before collection and again has output exponents in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
Equation (12) now gives \(y\) or \(y^2\) as a torsion monomial; a cross-collision between the two denominator lists need not force torsion because their individual monomials may differ.
The general exterior-count conclusion still applies.
Growing Laurent exponents and unbounded rational coefficients are permitted subject to (17).

The [earlier sparse-power Gram bound](Yin_C_G0092_v1.md#statement) already gives explicit, coefficient-unrestricted independence of any fixed number of powers of a single nontorsion unit in a sufficiently small span-height window.
The [seven-power account](Yin_C_G0093_v1.md#statement) adds a coefficient-lattice classification at its stated scope.
The present proof uses the same basic phase orthogonality principle, but allows arbitrary algebraic terms, their fields and their pairwise multiplicative dependencies, and concludes a uniformly bounded-order torsion collision.
It does not replace those stronger quantitative one-generator statements.

The [fixed rational-function theorem](Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#fixed-rational-function-height-gap) has a height floor depending on one fixed nonmonomial function and assumes independent inputs.
Here coefficients, supports and functions may vary, and the inputs may have circuit relations; the uniform parameter is the term count and the conclusion is multiplicative saturation of a sufficiently small output.
The [finite-support shift theorem](Yin_C_G0848_v1.md#phase-support-and-exceptional-shifts) instead retains a fixed coefficient-energy budget and an explicit support-dependent exceptional-shift set.
None of these comparisons is used as a proof premise for (1).

This obstruction covers actual bounded-term additive algebraic encodings, extending the multiplicative operations excluded in C345v299.
It leaves dense relations, a growing number of terms, and transformations that fail the height budget untreated.
It supplies neither a universal positive Mahler gap nor a reduction of all possible real circuits to the full-permutation case.
