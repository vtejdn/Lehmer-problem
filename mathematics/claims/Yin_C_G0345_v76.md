# Degree of a rational function with a small integral value at a complex Salem number

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform degree bounds for varying inputs and rational functions

Let \(\lambda\) be a complex Salem number of degree \(d=2m\ge6\), so the exterior roots of its monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(P\) are \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\), and the only other roots off the unit circle are their inverses.
Assume distinct roots have nontorsion quotient.
Write \(t\) for the number of [connected components of the Galois orbit of the exterior edge](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#statement) on inverse-pair labels and \(b=m/t\) for their common size.
Let \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\) be a nonconstant rational function different from every \(cX^j\), \(c\in\mathbb Q^\times,\ j\in\mathbb Z\).
Its degree \(k\) is the maximum of the numerator and denominator degrees in lowest terms.
Suppose \(R\) is defined at \(\lambda\), and \(\alpha=R(\lambda)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer.
Use \(L_y=\log M(y)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive minimal polynomial of \(y\).

For each fixed \(H>0\), whenever \(L_\lambda,L_\alpha\le H\),
\[
 k\ \gg_H\ \frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{48}},\qquad
 k\ \gg_H\ \frac{d}{\sqrt t\,(\log(3d))^{106}}
       \ \gg_H\ \frac{d}{(\log(3d))^{212}} .
\]
At \(t=1\) one has the stronger bound \(k\gg_H d(\log\log d/\log d)^3\).
The rational function may be chosen separately for each input, with arbitrary rational coefficients.
If \(\alpha=0\), one has \(k\ge d\) directly.

When \(h(\alpha)\le1/768\), the absolute logarithmic height threshold from the [relative norm criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#norm), every nontorsion output generates \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).
In this range the sharper quantity controlled from below is \(kL_\lambda+L_\alpha\), and the [relative-degree inequality](#clearing) records the actual cost at the two measures.
A torsion output always has \(k\ge d/(2t)\) when \(b\ge3\).
For a [primitive relative unit in one unbalanced block](#primitive), the degree bound improves to \(d-O_H((\log d/\log\log d)^3)\).

<a id="cyclic"></a>
## The relative generator and the degrees of its powers

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), \(B=\mathbb Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\), and \(\iota\) for the nonidentity element of \({\rm Gal}(K/B)\).
Among the \(m\) trace embeddings, \(m-2\ge m/3\) are neutral, so the relative norm criterion applies with \(\delta=1/3\).
For \(h(\alpha)\le1/768\), it gives \(\alpha\iota(\alpha)=1\) and integrality makes \(\alpha\) a unit.
By the [cyclic relative unit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#cyclic), the entire group of norm-one integral units is generated modulo the roots of unity in \(K\) by one \(\rho\).
Orient it to have positive log modulus in the chosen embedding.
There are roots of unity \(\zeta,\eta\in K\) and integers \(u\ge1,\ v\in\mathbb Z\) such that
\[
                      \lambda=\zeta\rho^u,\qquad \alpha=\eta\rho^v .
\]
The description of \(\lambda\) uses only its norm-one identity.

For two distinct embeddings of \(K\), a torsion quotient between their images of \(\rho\) would give a torsion quotient between the corresponding images of \(\lambda\).
The images of \(\rho\) are therefore pairwise distinct even modulo torsion, and \(\mathbb Q(\rho)=K\).
Their moduli are the \(u\)-th roots of those of the corresponding images of \(\lambda\).
The involution \(\iota\) acts as inversion on \(\rho\) and \(\lambda\).
Thus \(\rho\) has the same two exterior embeddings and inverse-pair labels, equivariantly for the Galois group of the common normal closure.
It is itself a compressed complex Salem number with the same \(d,t,b\).
When \(v\ne0\), the same embedding argument makes \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=K\).
Height invariance under a torsion factor and homogeneity under powers now give the exact relations
\[
                      L_\lambda=u L_\rho,\qquad L_\alpha=|v|L_\rho.
\]
The second equality holds also for \(v=0\), when both sides are zero.

<a id="clearing"></a>
## The polynomial over the field of roots of unity

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\mu(K))\) be the cyclotomic field generated by the roots of unity in \(K\), \(f=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and \(D=[K:F]=d/f\).
Take \(R=A/B_0\) in lowest terms.
Over \(F\), clear \(R(\zeta X^u)=\eta X^v\) by multiplying by \(B_0(\zeta X^u)\) and, if \(v<0\), by \(X^{-v}\).
This gives a polynomial of degree at most \(ku+|v|\) vanishing at \(\rho\).
It is nonzero because a nonmonomial rational function has a zero or pole in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,\times}\), and substitution of \(\zeta X^u\) preserves the existence of such a zero or pole.
Since \(F(\rho)=K\), it follows that
\[
               k L_\lambda+L_\alpha\ \ge\ D L_\rho=dD h(\rho).
\]
In particular there is no subfield-index factor on \(L_\alpha\), even when the output is torsion.

The [relative height theorem of Delsinne](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) at rank one gives \(h(\rho)>c/(D_{\rm ab}(\log(3D_{\rm ab}))^{48})\), where \(D_{\rm ab}=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\rho):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).
Indeed its \(A_1=2(1+1)^2(1+1)!=16\) gives exponent \(3A_1=48\), and \(\rho\) is nontorsion.
As \(F\subset\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), \(D_{\rm ab}\le D\le d\).
Consequently \(kL_\lambda+L_\alpha\gg d/(\log(3d))^{48}\).

To remove the small-height assumption in the bound for \(k\), let \(e=[K:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\).
The nonzero polynomial \(A(X)-\alpha B_0(X)\) has degree at most \(k\) and vanishes at \(\lambda\), so \(e\le k\).
If \(h(\alpha)>1/768\), then
\[
                k\ge e=\frac{d\,h(\alpha)}{L_\alpha}>
                       \frac{d}{768H}.
\]
For smaller height the preceding estimate applies and \(kL_\lambda+L_\alpha\le2kH\).
For a zero value the nonzero numerator is divisible by \(P\), proving the separate bound.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Roots of unity in the trace blocks

Suppose \(b\ge3\).
The relative involution on \(\mu(K)\) is inversion, as seen at any neutral embedding where it agrees with ordinary conjugation.
Thus the maximal real subfield \(F^+\) of \(F\) is contained in \(B\), with \(f\le2[F^+:\mathbb Q]\).
The embeddings of \(B\) correspond to the inverse-pair labels.
The Galois group of its normal closure contains the product \(S_b^t\) of the independent full symmetric groups on the components by the [exterior-edge action](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action).
Restriction of the embeddings of \(B\) to \(F^+\) is constant on the orbits of the commutator subgroup, since \(F^+/\mathbb Q\) is abelian.
This commutator subgroup contains \(A_b^t\), which is transitive within each component.
All embeddings of \(F^+\) extend to \(B\), so \([F^+:\mathbb Q]\le t\) and \(f\le2t\).
In particular any torsion \(\alpha\) has \(k\ge [K:\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\ge d/(2t)\).

At small height the clearing inequality and the [two-height bound for the block count](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) applied to \(\rho\) give
\[
           kL_\lambda+L_\alpha\ \ge\ \frac{d L_\rho}{2t}
                \ \gg\ \frac{d}{\sqrt t\,(\log(3d))^{106}} .
\]
The same block bound on \(\lambda\) gives \(t\ll H^2(\log(3d))^{212}\), so \(b=d/(2t)\) tends to infinity with \(d\) under the assumed measure bound.
The condition \(b\ge3\) therefore discards only bounded degrees depending on \(H\) from the displayed uniform estimates on \(k\), covered by \(k\ge1\) after adjusting constants.
At high height the linear lower bound already suffices.
If \(t=1\), then \(f\le2\), and [Voutier's absolute degree bound](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives \(L_\rho>\tfrac14(\log\log d/\log d)^3\).
This proves the claimed strengthening for a single block.

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Primitive inputs and the degree of interpolation

Suppose \(\lambda\) generates the norm-one integral units of \(K/B\) modulo torsion.
This holds in particular for a compressed input minimizing the positive measure among complex Salem inputs of degree at most \(d\), since the primitive generator above has degree \(d\) and measure \(M(\lambda)^{1/u}\).
Use \(\rho=\lambda\) in the small-height case, so \(\alpha=\eta\lambda^v\).
The clearing polynomial now has coefficients already in \(\mathbb Q(\eta)\), giving
\[
            k\ \ge\ \frac d{[\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]}
                    -\frac{L_\alpha}{L_\lambda}.
\]
In an unbalanced single block, [the individual root field](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#fields) has only the roots of unity \(\pm1\) and its only proper nontrivial subfield is the trace field of degree \(d/2\).
Thus a nonrational \(\alpha\) has \(\deg\alpha\ge d/2\) and \(h(\alpha)\le2H/d\), so it satisfies the small-height criterion for all sufficiently large \(d\).
A rational value gives \(k\ge d\) from \(A-\alpha B_0\) directly.
Using Voutier on \(\lambda\), all bounded-measure integral outputs at such primitive inputs consequently satisfy
\[
                  k\ \ge\ d-O_H\!\left(
                     (\log d/\log\log d)^3\right).
\]
For each input, the nonmonomial polynomial \(R(X)=X+P(X)\) of degree \(d\) gives \(R(\lambda)=\lambda\).
Thus rational functions with a monomial value at the input are included, and linear degree always suffices for this example.
