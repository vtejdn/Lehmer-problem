# Sharp signed-row costs at integer exponents and a two-value criterion

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least norm and the conditional gap

Fix distinct monic reciprocal irreducible unit minima \(P_i\) of positive logarithmic Mahler measures \(h_i\), positive integral multiplicities \(t_i\), and \(F=\prod_iP_i^{t_i}\) with \(W=\sum_i t_i h_i>0\).
On an infinite set of integers \(n\ge2\), assume powering by \(n\) is injective on the combined set of distinct roots.
Choose one root per inverse pair.
For each \(n\) suppose there is a sign \(\sigma_n\) such that every multiplicatively dependent pair \(x,y\) among the chosen roots has a primitive relation \(x^u y^v=\eta\) modulo torsion with \(\eta^n\ne\sigma_n^{u+v}\).
Here \(u,v\) are coprime nonzero integers and \(\eta\) is a root of unity.

For either sign \(\varepsilon\), and widths \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(n)\), the least one-norm \(H_n\) of an integral polynomial \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) such that
\[
   F\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\quad
   A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y),\quad A(\alpha^n)\ne0\quad(F(\alpha)=0)
\]
exists eventually and satisfies \(\log H_n/n\to W/2\).
Any fixed positive excess over \(W/2\) in the upper bound on \(\log H_n/n\) is attainable eventually at one fixed width.

Write \(B=(\log2)/4\) and \(\lambda=\inf\{\log M(P)>0:0\ne P\in\mathbb Z[X]\}\).
In the [fixed-double-factor test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement), one positive irreducible value \(h\) with \(2(2^r-1)h<B\) realizes the \(r\) values \(h,2h,\ldots,2^{r-1}h\) on one passing row at every sufficiently large integer \(n\), for either prescribed graph sign.
For \(r=1\) the list consists of \(h\) alone.
Any two different positive irreducible values of sum below \(B/2\) occur together at every sufficiently large odd \(n\).
Both conclusions hold at some fixed width, and also along any prescribed \(b(n)\to\infty\) with \(\log b(n)=o(n)\).
In particular an infinite subsequence of the latter widths with fewer than two values for one sign implies \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/24\).
The subsequence may consist of powers of two.
It also suffices that, for each reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\delta\) whose positive powers all generate its field, arbitrarily large exponents exclude the particular pair of minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\) on passing rows at a prescribed sign and widths tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\).
Here the sign, width sequence and infinite set of exclusions may depend on \(\delta\).

## Valuations at prime divisors of the exponent

Let \(L\) be a number field of degree \(d\), \(x\) a nontorsion unit, and \(m=2n\).
The logarithmic norm of the part of \(x^m-1\) supported over primes dividing \(m\) or in a fixed finite set is \(o_{L,x}(n)\).
At a fixed prime over \(\ell\), let \(a\) be the residue order of \(x\).
Only multiples of \(a\) contribute, with prime-to-\(\ell\) powers of \(x^a\) preserving the valuation of its difference from one.
For the normalization \(v(\ell)=1\), an \(\ell\)-th power raises a current valuation \(v>0\) by at least \(\min(1,(\ell-1)v)\), and by exactly one once \(v>1/(\ell-1)\), by binomial expansion.
Thus each fixed prime, including primes at two and ramified primes, costs \(O_{L,x,\ell}(\log(2m))\).
At a remaining contributing prime \(\mathfrak l\) over \(\ell\mid m\), the unramified odd case gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^m-1)=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^a-1)+v_\ell(m),
 \qquad a\le\min(\ell^d,m/\ell).
\]
Indeed \(a\) divides both the residue group order and the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(m\).
The first term times \(\log N\mathfrak l\) is at most \(\log|N_{L/\mathbb Q}(x^a-1)|=O_{L,x}(1+a)\), while all lifts together cost at most \(d\log m\).
Among \(\ell\mid m\), those below or equal to \(m^{1/(2d)}\) give \(a\le\sqrt m\) at at most \(d\log_2m\) prime ideals.
The others include at most \(2d\) rational primes and have \(a\le m^{1-1/(2d)}\).
These contributions are \(o(n)\), as asserted, also for the divisor \(x^n-\sigma_n\).

## The two norm bounds and nonvanishing

In a fixed Galois splitting field \(L\) of \(F\), discard primes above \(2nF(1)F(-1)\) and the ramified primes.
At every other \(\ell\) put \(v_\ell(B_n)=\max_{z^n=\sigma_n}v_\ell(F(z))\), taking all local nodes with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Give \(B_n\) valuation zero at discarded primes.
All nodes lie in an unramified extension with distinct residues, so these are nonnegative integers.
For \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(F,X^n-\sigma_n)|\) the [fixed signed estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) gives \(\log R_n=nW+O_F(\log(2n))\).
Positive maxima occur at two inverse nodes of equal valuation and distinct residue because \(F\) is reciprocal and \(F(1)F(-1)\) is a unit at the retained primes.
The resultant product therefore gives \(2\log B_n\le\log R_n\).

For each selected \(x\) and prime \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\) put \(w_x=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^n-\sigma_n)\) with multiplicity \(t_x\).
Summed with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\), the total of the \(t_xw_x\) is \(\tfrac12\log R_n\) since inverse roots give the same ideal.
At a retained prime a positive \(w_x\) comes from a single node \(z\), whence \(e_{\mathfrak l}v_\ell(B_n)\ge\max_x t_xw_x\) by evaluating \(F(z)\).
The sum of the left side with the given prime weights is \(\log B_n\).
The discarded contribution is \(o(n)\) by the preceding valuation bound.
The loss in replacing the sum by the maximum is bounded by the sum of pairwise minima.
For independent \(x,y\), [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) on the distinct pairs of independent units \(\sigma_n x^n,\sigma_n y^n\), of heights \(O_F(n)\), makes the normalized common valuation sum \(o_F(n)\).
For a dependent pair the ideal generated by the two signed differences contains \(\eta^n-\sigma_n^{u+v}\), a nonzero member of a finite set by hypothesis.
Indeed \(x^n,y^n\) are congruent to \(\sigma_n\) in that quotient and are units.
The weighted minimum sum is then bounded by a constant, since all multiplicities are fixed.
This proves \(\log B_n=nW/2+o(n)\), including the analogous result on each fixed nonempty subproduct.

For a nonzero graph \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\) divisible by \(F\), remove the largest common power \((Y-\sigma_n)^j\) from the rows, preserving all factors of \(F\) after substitution.
At a maximizing inverse pair the quotient rows evaluated at \(\sigma_n\) satisfy two homogeneous linear congruences modulo \(\ell^{v_\ell(B_n)}\) with determinant a unit.
Thus \(B_n\) divides both integers, one nonzero, which are divided derivatives bounded by \(\binom bj\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
This gives the needed lower bound for all two-row graphs of width \(o(n)\).

The [integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) gives a nonzero graph at width \(b-1\) of log one-norm at most \(nW/2+o(n)\), or at sufficiently large fixed width any prescribed excess in the slope.
Its embedding counts depend on the actual monomial exponents and the \(t_i\) prescribed jets, so they apply at every integer \(n\), including value alone for \(t_i=1\).
Injectivity makes the powered minima \(Q_i\) distinct with log measures \(nh_i\).
If both rows contained \(Q_i\), dividing it out would preserve \(F/P_i\) in the resulting graph, since \(Q_i(X^n)\) is separable and contains no other selected minimum.
The larger quotient row has log one-norm at least \(n(W-h_i)/2-o(n)\) by the lower bound or by integrality for an empty list.
Its log measure is at least this minus \(b\log2\), by the coefficient bound in terms of Mahler measure.
Restoring \(Q_i\) would cost at least \(n(W+h_i)/2-o(n)\), contradicting the construction, taking the fixed-width excess less than \(\min_i h_i/2\) when needed.
Vanishing of either row would force vanishing of both at the same node by the graph equation.
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) retain all multiplicities and nonzero values, increasing width by one and one-norm by at most a constant depending only on the list.
This proves the least-cost assertion.

## Realizing small values at the required exponents

Take an [all-power representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) \(\delta\) of the small value \(h\).
All minima of the specified powers \(\delta^{2^j}\) are reciprocal by the [nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
Their distinct absolute heights prevent inter-orbit collisions after powering, and all powers retain their degree.
Within the orbit of \(\delta\), dependent conjugates have quotient or product torsion by their equal heights, and a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates contradicts the retained degrees.
Thus dependence across dyadic levels matches an even power of the same conjugate after choosing orientation, with phase one and odd exponent sum.
The choice \(\sigma_n=-1\) works for all \(n\).
Taking all \(t_i=2\) gives \(W=2(2^r-1)h<B\).
Choosing a fixed positive excess smaller than \((B-W)/2\) gives the strict budget \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) for all large \(n\).
The [standard width multiplier](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) costs at most two and gives the same conclusion at the stated growing widths.
For \(r=2\), any \(\lambda<B/6\) supplies a positive irreducible \(h<B/6\) by factorization, so an infinite exclusion gives the asserted conditional gap.
For that same \(h\) the construction uses the fixed pair of minima of \(\delta,\delta^2\) at every sufficiently large \(n\), at either sign and on any one such width sequence.
Thus even an input-dependent subsequence excluding just this pair contradicts \(h<B/6\).
If \(P\) is the minimum of \(\delta\), the minimum \(Q\) of \(\delta^2\) is determined exactly by \(Q(X^2)=P(X)P(-X)\), since \(P\) has even degree and squaring preserves it.
In particular \(Q\equiv P\bmod 2\), by the Frobenius identity.

For arbitrary unequal small values take their all-power representatives.
A torsion quotient across orbits would give conjugate powered roots with the same degree and height, contradicting the distinct original measures.
If dependent, their [matched conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#two) have a common primitive slope \(u/v>0\) and conjugate phases \(y^v/x^u=\eta\) after orientation.
When the phase order is odd, negate the whole orbit with odd exponent in the relation, giving phases of even order without changing degrees, measures or injectivity.
Then \(\eta^n\ne1\) for all odd \(n\), so \(\sigma_n=1\) suffices in the dependent case, and independent orbits already meet the hypothesis.
The two multiplicities give \(W=2(h_1+h_2)<B\), so the same strict slack and padding prove the odd-exponent assertion.
