# A cyclotomic Mahler gap with two bounded-degree factors of arbitrary height

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniformity in both coefficient vectors

Fix \(H\ge0\).
For \(n\ge2\), \(k\ge0\) integers and \(T,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degrees at most \(H\), allow either factor to be zero and put
\[
                              P(X)=T(X)\Phi_n(X)+X^k B(X).
\]
Every nonzero \(P\) satisfies \(m(P)=0\) or \(m(P)\ge\eta_H>0\), where \(m=\log M\) denotes the logarithmic Mahler measure.
When \(T,B\ne0\), \(m(P)\to\infty\) as \(\omega(n)\to\infty\), uniformly in \(k,T,B\).
This includes both signs and permits both coefficient vectors, all their roots and the relative shift to vary.

<a id="radii"></a>
## Separating two summands on one of two interior circles

Write \(d=\varphi(n)\) and let \(U,V\) be nonzero integer polynomials of degree at most \(H\).
Choose radii \(\rho_1\in[1/4,1/3]\), \(\rho_2\in[2/3,3/4]\) at distance at least \(1/(100(H+1))\) from every root modulus of \(U,V\).
The excluded intervals have total length less than \(1/12\).
On both circles \(|z|=\rho_i\) one has
\[
                     |U(z)|\asymp_H M(U),\qquad |V(z)|\asymp_H M(V).
\]
Indeed for a root \(\alpha\) of modulus at most two, the radial distance bound controls \(|z-\alpha|/\max(1,|\alpha|)\) from below, and for larger \(\alpha\) that ratio is at least \(1/2\).
The upper bound follows from \(|z|\le3/4\).
Moreover \(|\Phi_n(z)|\asymp1\) for \(|z|\le3/4\), uniformly in \(n\), by the Möbius product and the convergence to a positive value of \(\prod_{j\ge1}(1-(3/4)^j)/(1+(3/4)^j)\).

Place the single factor \(\Phi_n\) in either of the two summands of \(U+z^jV\), and denote the result by \(F\).
On the \(i\)-th circle the sizes of the two summands are respectively comparable to \(M(U)\) and \(\rho_i^j M(V)\), with both comparability constants depending only on \(H\).
The ratios \(t_i=\rho_i^j M(V)/M(U)\) obey \(t_2/t_1\ge2^j\).
Choose \(K_H>1\) so that \(t_i<K_H^{-1}\) or \(t_i>K_H\) makes the larger summand at least twice the other on the entire \(i\)-th circle.
For all \(j\) sufficiently large in terms of \(H\), the two ratios cannot both belong to \([K_H^{-1},K_H]\).
On a circle with one summand dominant, its modulus is \(\gg_H M(U)\) in either case, so \(|F|\gg_H M(U)\) there.
Monotonicity of the angular logarithmic mean with radius gives
\[
                                M(U)\ll_H M(F).                       \tag{1}
\]
This uses the interior bounds also when the higher summand contains \(\Phi_n\).

<a id="offset"></a>
## Exposing the coefficients at one end

Suppose \(T,B\ne0,\ r=\omega(n)\to\infty\) and \(M(P)\) stays bounded along a sequence.
Then \(d\to\infty\).
Write \(U^*(X)=X^{\deg U}U(1/X)\) for reversal of a nonzero polynomial, which preserves its measure even in the presence of zero roots.

If \(k>2d\), (1) gives bounded \(M(T)\).
For large \(d\), the degree of \(P\) is \(k+\deg B\), and its reversal is
\[
                        B^*+X^{k+\deg B-d-\deg T}\,T^*\Phi_n.
\]
The exponent tends to infinity, so (1) also gives bounded \(M(B)\).
Integer polynomials of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure have bounded coefficients by expansion in their roots.
Consequently, if this case occurs infinitely often we can take a subsequence with both \(T,B\) fixed, in contradiction with the [fixed-polynomial divergence](Yin_C_G0345_v95.md#statement).

We may therefore take \(k\le2d\).
If \(k\le d/3\), then for all large \(d\) the degree is \(d+\deg T\) and reversal writes \(P^*=T^*\Phi_n+X^{d+\deg T-k-\deg B}B^*\).
The resulting offset is at least \(2d/3-H\).
If \(k>d/3\) retain the original orientation.
In either orientation the degree \(D\) is \(O_H(d)\), the new offset tends to infinity, and the low summand is \(T\Phi_n\) in the resulting notation.
By (1) the new \(M(T)\) stays bounded.
Pass to a subsequence with this low factor fixed.

For each fixed integer \(a\), the Fourier coefficient \(\widehat{\log|P|}(a)\) on the unit circle stays bounded.
Indeed after stripping the fixed zero multiplicity of \(T\) at zero, each fixed Taylor prefix of \(P\) is eventually that of the stripped \(T\Phi_n\), with coefficients bounded because only divisors at most the prefix length occur in the Möbius product.
The constant coefficient is a fixed nonzero integer.
The [root reflection calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v95.md#moments) applies because the formal logarithm controls the inverse power sums, and replacing \(\alpha^{-a}\) by \(\bar\alpha^a\) at roots inside the unit circle for \(a>0\) costs \(O_a(m(P)e^{a m(P)})\).
The mean \(m(P)\) and the conjugate coefficients at negative indices are bounded as well.
This step needs no coefficient information from the higher summand.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Uniform localized growth for moving root angles

Give the circle Haar probability \(\nu\).
For each nonzero root of \(B\) take its argument \(\gamma\), retaining multiplicities, and form
\[
                         W(\theta)=\prod_\gamma |e^{i\theta}-e^{i\gamma}|^4 .
\]
If \(B\) has no nonzero root, take \(W=1\).
These weights are nonnegative, have Fourier degree at most \(2H\), and satisfy \(\|W\|_\infty\le16^H\).
Their means are at least one by Jensen's inequality, since each factor has zero mean logarithm.
By the preceding bounds on the finitely many Fourier coefficients and \(|\widehat W(a)|\le16^H\),
\[
                                   \int W\log|P|\,d\nu=O(1).           \tag{2}
\]

In contrast, writing \(L_n=\log|\Phi_n|\) and \(L_n^+=\max(0,L_n)\), we have uniformly for these weights
\[
                                  \int W L_n^+\,d\nu\to\infty.         \tag{3}
\]
To check uniformity, take the [localized Fourier test](Yin_C_G0345_v95.md#local) at the middle ranks of the primes in \(\operatorname{rad}(n)\).
Its bounded test function \(G\) satisfies \(\int L_nG\,d\nu\gg A\sqrt{s}\to\infty\), where \(A=\varphi(\operatorname{rad}(n))/\operatorname{rad}(n)\) and \(s=\lfloor(r/200)^{1/3}\rfloor\).
For \(0<|l|\le2H\), the shifted Fourier coefficients in that test are bounded by \(O_H(2^{-r/2})\) apiece, and the sum of the absolute coefficients of the test is at most \(e^{\sqrt{s}}\).
Thus the nonconstant terms of \(W\) contribute \(o_H(1)\) to \(\int W L_nG\,d\nu\), while the constant term is at least one.
As \(\|G\|_\infty\le e^{1/2}\), this proves uniform divergence of \(\int W|L_n|\,d\nu\).
Also \(\int W L_n\,d\nu=O_H(1)\), since every Fourier coefficient of \(L_n\) has absolute value at most \(1/2\).
Adding the two estimates proves (3).

<a id="poles"></a>
## Controlling the loss around projections of roots of arbitrary modulus

For a root \(\alpha=R e^{i\gamma}\), \(R>0\), and \(z=e^{i\theta}\ne\alpha\), differentiation gives
\[
  \left|\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}\log|z-\alpha|\right|
        \le\frac{R}{|z-\alpha|^2}
        =\frac{R}{(1-R)^2+4R\sin^2((\theta-\gamma)/2)}
        \le\frac1{4\sin^2((\theta-\gamma)/2)}
\]
away from the projected angle.
A zero root contributes zero boundary logarithm.
Let \(E\) consist of the angular \(\delta\)-neighborhoods of the projected nonzero roots of \(B\), where \(\delta=C_H/\sqrt d\) with \(C_H\) sufficiently large.
On the complement the entire second derivative of \(\log|B|\) has absolute value at most \(d/16\), independent of its height and the moduli of its roots.
The off-circle roots of the fixed \(T\) produce a bounded second derivative, at most \(d/16\) in absolute value for all sufficiently large \(d\).

On an arc of length \(\ell\) between consecutive distinct circle roots of \(T\Phi_n\), their contributions to \(-(\log|T\Phi_n|)''\) sum to \(S\ge\max(d/4,4/\ell^2)\) by the [circle-root estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#curvature).
Consequently \(u=L_n+\log|T|-\log|B|\) satisfies \(-u''\ge S-d/8\ge S/2\ge2/\ell^2\) outside \(E\) on that arc.
Deletion of \(E\) leaves at most \(2H+1\) pieces per original arc.
On each of at most two monotone branches per piece, the portion with \(|u|\le t\) has length at most \(\ell\sqrt{2t}\) by integration of this curvature bound.
Summing over the arcs gives \(\nu\{\theta\notin E: |u|\le t\}\ll_H\sqrt t\).
The [restricted cancellation integral](Yin_C_G0345_v95.md#poles) therefore gives
\[
             \int_{\theta\notin E} -\log(1-e^{-|u|})\,d\nu=O_H(1).
\]
The reverse triangle inequality bounds \(\log|P|\) below there by \(\max\{L_n+\log|T|,\log|B|\}+\log(1-e^{-|u|})\).
The maximum is at least \(L_n^+-(\log|T|)^--(\log|B|)^-\), where \(h^-=\max(0,-h)\).
For every nonzero integer polynomial \(F\), \(\int(\log|F|)^-d\nu\le(\deg F)\log2\) by normalizing each root factor to have measure one and supremum at most two.
Together with \(\sup W\le16^H\), this yields
\[
                \int_{\theta\notin E} W\log|P|\,d\nu
                     \ge \int_{\theta\notin E} W L_n^+\,d\nu-O_H(1).
\]
On \(E\), if nonempty, at least one factor of \(W\) is at most \(\delta^4\), so \(W\ll_H\delta^4\).
The positive part \(L_n^+\) is at most \(d\log2\), while \(\int(\log|P|)^-d\nu\le D\log2\).
The omitted contributions to the lower bound therefore cost \(O_H((d+D)\delta^4)=o(1)\).
It follows from (3) that \(\int W\log|P|\,d\nu\to\infty\), contradicting (2).
This proves uniform divergence for nonzero factors.

<a id="gap"></a>
## A positive gap over all conductors

For bounded \(r\), the same [Möbius denominator clearing](Yin_C_G0345_v95.md#gap) gives a nonzero integer polynomial of the same measure with term count bounded in terms of \(r,H\), since each of \(T,B\) has at most \(H+1\) terms.
The [bounded-term Mahler theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) separates its positive measures from zero independently of coefficient size.
Choose the cutoff in \(r\) so that uniform divergence gives \(m(P)\ge1\) above it for nonzero \(T,B\).
When one factor is zero, the measure equals that of the other polynomial of degree at most \(H\), so its positive measures also have a gap by bounded-degree finiteness below \(M=2\).
This proves the assertion.
