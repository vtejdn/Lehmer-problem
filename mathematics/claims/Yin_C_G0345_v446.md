# How many coefficients a signed graph needs for prescribed double factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Independence of every small set of coefficient columns

Let \(G\) be a nonconstant squarefree product of monic reciprocal irreducible polynomials of nontorsion algebraic units.
Assume that the quotient of any two distinct roots of \(G\) is not a root of unity, and write \(m=\deg G\).
For \(n\ge2,\ b\ge0,\ \varepsilon=\pm1\), consider the rational linear map on rows of degree at most \(b\) given by
\[
       A\longmapsto A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n})
                         \pmod {G(X)^2}.
\]
At every sufficiently large \(n\) on any sequence with \(\log(2+b)=o(n)\), every set of at most \(m\) coefficient columns is independent.
In fact the same eventual assertion holds throughout \(0\le b\le\exp(c_G n)\) for some \(c_G>0\).
It holds for both signs and makes no coefficient-height assumption.
If \(b<m\) the kernel is zero, and if \(b\ge m\) its smallest nonzero support has exactly \(m+1\) positions.
Every prescribed set of \(m+1\) positions supports an integral solution, unique up to rational scaling.
These last solutions concern divisibility alone, with no claim on their heights or values at the powered roots.

For the [two-value criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) take \(G=PQ\), where \(P,Q\) are the respective minima of a reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\delta\) and its square, and every positive power generates \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
No two distinct conjugates of either unit have torsion quotient, since some power would identify them and lower the degree.
Conjugates from the two different lists have unequal absolute logarithmic Weil heights.
Thus any passing graph with the two prescribed double factors needs at least \(2\deg P+1\) nonzero coefficients throughout the stated width range.
This condition permits dense rows at large widths.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## A surviving term of a double-contact minor

Take a splitting field \(K\) of \(G\).
The subgroup generated by the roots modulo torsion is free abelian of finite rank.
Choose a real linear functional on its real span that separates the images of all roots and is nonzero on each, by avoiding finitely many hyperplanes.
Represent each inverse pair by its root \(\alpha\) of positive functional value, and order these representatives by increasing value.
For a chosen set of \(1\le s\le m\) distinct columns use both the value and the Euler derivative at the first \(\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) representatives and, if \(s\) is odd, the value alone at the next.
The determinant of these \(s\) equations on the \(s\) columns is a sum of terms \(a u^n\), with \(u\) a product of powers of the roots.
The graph column at \(\alpha\) has terms \(\alpha^{nj}\) and \(\varepsilon\alpha\,\alpha^{n(b-j)}\).
The corresponding derivative terms have the additional factors \(nj\) and \(n(b-j)+1\).

At a maximum of the functional on the bases of the formal expansion, a column of level \(l_j=\max(j,b-j)\) must use that exponent since all selected weights are positive.
For \(j=b/2\) both choices of term have the same exponent and can be combined.
Strict rearrangement between unequal weights and unequal levels assigns the increasing list of levels to the increasing list of node slots, using two slots for each chosen double node and one for the optional last node.
Any other allocation has strictly smaller functional value.
These comparisons refer to the formal allocation before any zero coefficients are discarded.
All maximal allocations give precisely the same multiplicative base, since they differ only by permutations within a node or between copies of the same level.

At a node \(\alpha\), write a maximal value entry as \(u_\alpha\alpha^{nl}\) and its derivative as \(u_\alpha(nl+e_\alpha)\alpha^{nl}\).
For \(j>b/2\) the pair \((u_\alpha,e_\alpha)\) is \((1,0)\), for \(j<b/2\) it is \((\varepsilon\alpha,1)\), and for \(j=b/2\) it is \((1+\varepsilon\alpha,\varepsilon\alpha/(1+\varepsilon\alpha))\).
All the multipliers are nonzero and the shifts are bounded in the chosen complex embedding by a constant depending only on \(G\).
If two unequal levels \(l<l'\) occupy a double node, their local determinant after removing the common base is
\[
       u_\alpha u'_\alpha\, n(l'-l)
                  \left(1+\frac{e'_\alpha-e_\alpha}{n(l'-l)}\right).
\]
After division by \(n(l'-l)\) the error in replacing the last parenthesis by one is \(O_G(1/n)\), uniformly in the levels.
Equal levels at one node occur only for the reflected columns \(j,b-j\), whose local determinant after removing the base is exactly \(\pm\varepsilon\alpha\).
A single slot contributes just its multiplier.

Divide the whole maximal coefficient by the product of \(n(l'-l)\) over the unequal-level double nodes, which have the same assigned levels in every maximal allocation.
The only remaining choices after the local determinants are taken are swaps of reflected columns of equal level assigned across consecutive nodes \(\alpha,\beta\).
No such column belongs to two swaps, and each swap reverses the permutation sign.
After dropping the error terms the sum over each pair of choices factors as the determinant of the two multipliers \(1,\varepsilon\alpha\) at \(\alpha\) and \(1,\varepsilon\beta\) at \(\beta\), giving \(\pm\varepsilon(\beta-\alpha)\).
This factorization still holds when two different swaps meet the same node, since its normalized leading contribution is the product of the two individual multipliers.
The resulting product of these factors, the within-node equal-level determinants and the remaining individual multipliers is nonzero.
There are finitely many possible such products and a bounded number of uniformly bounded error terms, depending only on \(G\).
Hence the actual maximal coefficient is nonzero for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly in the columns and the width.
Its base cannot be torsion-equivalent to a base of strictly smaller functional value.

<a id="heights"></a>
## The other terms cannot cancel at slowly growing widths

Group all terms whose bases differ by a root of unity, absorbing the \(n\)-th powers of those roots of unity into the coefficients, and discard zero groups.
The number of terms before or after grouping is bounded by a constant depending only on \(G\).
Each original coefficient is a product of a bounded number of factors taken from a fixed set of algebraic numbers and the integers \(nj,\ n(b-j)+1\).
The standard product and sum height inequalities give
\[
           h(a)\le C_G\bigl(1+\log n+\log(2+b)\bigr)=H
\]
for every nonzero grouped coefficient, with \(h\) the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The maximal group survives by the preceding calculation.
All remaining bases are algebraic units in \(K\) with pairwise nontorsion ratios.
Northcott finiteness in this fixed number field gives some \(\eta_K>0\) such that the height of every one of these ratios is at least \(\eta_K\).

If the determinant vanishes, choose a minimal vanishing subsum of nonzero grouped terms and divide by the negative of one term, obtaining \(y_1+\cdots+y_p=1\).
There is no vanishing nonempty proper subsum.
Each coordinate is a signed quotient of two grouped coefficients times the \(n\)-th power of a nontorsion unit.
Its height is at least \(n\eta_K-2H\).
For \(S\) the archimedean places of \(K\), the outside-\(S\) sum of the local heights of the affine tuple and its coordinatewise inverse is at most \(4pH\).
Indeed the unit powers contribute zero there and this sum is bounded by the sum of the individual heights of the coefficient quotients and their inverses.
When \(H=o(n)\), the tuple has height tending to infinity and its outside-\(S\) sum is \(o(1)\) times its height.
The same conclusions about unbounded height and the required almost-unit inequality hold for all \(b\le\exp(c_G n)\) if \(c_G>0\) is sufficiently small.
More explicitly, fix \(\delta_0>0\) smaller than \(1/(p(p+1))\) for every possible \(p\).
Choose \(c_G\) so that for all large \(n\), \(2H\le n\eta_K/2\) and \(4pH\le\delta_0 n\eta_K/2\) throughout this width range.
Each coordinate has outside-\(S\) sum at most \(4pH\le\delta_0 n\eta_K/2\le\delta_0 h(y_i)\).
For \(p\ge2\), [Xiao's almost-unit equation](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations) therefore restricts these nondegenerate tuples of individual almost units to a fixed finite set.
For \(p=1\) the only tuple is \(y_1=1\).
Neither case allows the stated height growth along an infinite sequence of vanishing determinants.
This proves the asserted eventual independence without an effective bound here for its last exceptional integer.

The signed remainder belongs to the sign eigenspace of the involution \(H(X)\mapsto X^{nb+1}H(1/X)\) on \(\mathbb Q[X]/(G^2)\).
It interchanges each pair of two-dimensional local factors at inverse roots, so this eigenspace has dimension \(m\).
Thus \(m+1\) columns have a rational dependence.
By the independence of every \(m\) of them this dependence is unique up to scaling and uses every column.
Clearing denominators gives the integral assertion.

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## Effective arithmetic progressions at fixed width

For a specified \(G,b,\varepsilon\) one can compute \(a,M\ge1\) such that every \(k\ge a\) with \(k\equiv a\bmod M\) has the asserted independence at \(n=2^k\).
Use \(D=\deg(G^2)\), \(v=2+\lfloor\log_2 D\rfloor\), and search \(a\ge v\) by exact rational rank tests of the coefficient columns in the remainder modulo \(G^2\).
The eventual independence already proved guarantees termination.
Keep one nonzero integer minor for each tested set of positive size.
To select a prime \(p\equiv3\bmod8\) avoiding their product, take an odd integer \(t\) divisible by all its odd prime divisors and factor \(2t^2+1\).
Every prime factor \(q\) of this integer satisfies \((-2/q)=1\), so \(q\equiv1,3\bmod8\), and at least one is \(3\bmod8\) since \(2t^2+1\equiv3\bmod8\).

Compute the order \(L\) of \(X\) in the finite unit group of \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(G^2)\), and put \(L_{\rm odd}=L/2^{v_2(L)}\).
Modulo each distinct irreducible factor of degree \(f\le D\), the order divides \(p^f-1\), and lifting through nilpotents adds only a power of \(p\).
For odd \(f\) one has \(v_2(p^f-1)=1\), while for even \(f\) it is \(v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1)+v_2(f)-1=2+v_2(f)\).
In particular \(v_2(L)\le v\).
Take \(M\) to be the multiplicative order of \(2\) modulo \(L_{\rm odd}\), with \(M=1\) if \(L_{\rm odd}=1\).
At the indicated \(k\), \(2^k\equiv2^a\bmod L\), so all remainder columns agree modulo \(p\) with those at the certified exponent.
Their minors remain nonzero there.
The procedure supplies a certified infinite progression for each specified width, while the height argument above allows varying widths and all large integers.
