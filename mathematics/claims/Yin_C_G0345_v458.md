# Centered supports of bounded width for sparse double contacts at a Salem number and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite set of supports for each exponential height bound

Fix a Salem number \(\delta\) of degree \(d=2s\ge4\), put \(h=\log\delta\) and \(m=2d\), and let \(G\) be the product of the minimal polynomials of \(\delta\) and \(\delta^2\).
For an integer \(n\ge2\), a formal width \(b\ge0\) and a sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), write
\[
    F_{n,b,\varepsilon}(A)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}),
       \qquad A\in\mathbb Z[Y],\quad \deg A\le b.
\]
Consider nonzero \(A\) having at most \(m+1\) nonzero coefficients and satisfying \(G^2\mid F_{n,b,\varepsilon}(A)\).
Neither \(b\) nor the positions of those coefficients are restricted.

For each fixed \(C\ge0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\) depending only on \(\delta,C\), every such \(A\) with \(\|A\|_1\le\exp(Cn)\) has the form
\[
        b=2u+w,\qquad A(Y)=Y^u B(Y),\qquad
        0\le w\le W=(2m+1)q,\qquad u\ge0,
\]
where \(q=\lfloor dC/h\rfloor+1\).
Here \(\deg B\le w\), \(B\) has exactly \(m+1\) nonzero coefficients, and \(G^2\mid F_{n,w,\varepsilon}(B)\).
At each of the prescribed roots \(\alpha\) one has \(B(\alpha^n)\ne0\).
The row \(B\) is an integer multiple of the primitive generator for its sign, width and support.

In particular along any sequence \(n\to\infty\) of the indicated nonzero divisible rows, of either sign and any height,
\[
        \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A\|_1}{n}\ \ge\ (3d-6)h.
\]
If the rows have at most \(m\) nonzero coefficients, their normalized log norms tend to \(+\infty\).
Every nonzero row at either sign satisfying both divisibility and the strict test \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) therefore eventually needs at least \(2d+2\) nonzero coefficients, uniformly over all widths for this seed.

For each fixed sign, let \(H_n\) be the least one-norm among the indicated nonzero divisible rows with at most \(m+1\) nonzero coefficients over all widths.
For all sufficiently large \(n\), this minimum exists and can be taken over a finite set of fixed widths and supports independent of \(n\).
The limit of \(\log H_n/n\) equals the smallest of the fixed-support rates over all widths and all \(m+1\)-position supports for this sign, and some fixed support attains that rate.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## Splitting the graph and its derivative at the same gaps

Let \(J=\operatorname{supp} A\), \(H=\|A\|_1\), and \(I=J\cup(b-J)\).
Split the ordered set \(I\) wherever consecutive positions differ by more than \(q\).
Each resulting block has span at most \((|I|-1)q\le W\), and reflection \(j\mapsto b-j\) permutes the blocks.
If \(a_i\) is the first position in block \(i\), write the graph as \(\sum_i X^{na_i}T_i(X)\) by assigning a monomial of degree \(nj\) or \(nj+1\) to the block containing \(j\).
These two degree classes are disjoint since \(n\ge2\).
Each \(T_i\) is ordinary of degree at most \(nW+1\), and \(\sum_i\|T_i\|_1=2H\).

At either target \(\gamma=\delta,\delta^2\), the degree is \(d\) and the logarithmic Mahler measure is \(h\) or \(2h\), respectively, by the [Salem conjugate calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#divisors).
The [unit gap inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) for an integral polynomial of one-norm \(L\) vanishing at \(\gamma\) implies that a cut of length \(g\) has zero partial sum at \(\gamma\) whenever \(g h>d\log L\).
Indeed it bounds \(g\log M(\gamma)\) by \(d\log L\) whenever the partial sum is nonzero.
We measure a cut between its last occupied exponent on the left and its first on the right.
If either side has no terms, both sides already vanish at \(\gamma\).
All interblock cuts of the graph have length at least \(nq-1\).
Since \(qh>dC\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
                 (nq-1)h>d\bigl(Cn+\log(2(nW+1))\bigr).
\]
Apply the gap inequality at every cut and take successive differences, including the zero empty and total sums.
It follows that every \(T_i(\gamma)\) vanishes at both targets.

Write \(D=X\,d/dX\).
At either target, the equation \(D F_{n,b,\varepsilon}(A)(\gamma)=0\) now gives
\[
             \sum_i \gamma^{na_i}(D T_i)(\gamma)=0,
\]
since each discarded term is \(na_i\gamma^{na_i}T_i(\gamma)=0\).
The polynomial \(\sum_i X^{na_i}D T_i\) has one-norm at most \(2H(nW+1)\), independently of the positions \(a_i\), and omitting terms can only widen the gaps at the old cuts.
For the zero polynomial the block terms vanish identically because their degree intervals are disjoint.
Otherwise the same gap inequality and subtraction give \((D T_i)(\gamma)=0\) at both targets for every block.
The targets are nonzero and their minimal polynomials are distinct and separable, so \(G^2\) divides each \(T_i\).

<a id="mirror"></a>
## Excluding a pair of reflected blocks

Consider two distinct reflected blocks with extreme positions \([a,a+w]\) and \([a',a'+w]\), where \(a'=b-a-w\).
Define \(U,V\) by taking the coefficients of \(A\) in these intervals in increasing order, using exponent \(j-a\) or \(j-a'\), respectively.
Either row can be empty, but \(I=J\cup(b-J)\) ensures at least one is nonzero.
With \(B^*_w(Y)=Y^wB(1/Y)\), their normalized graph blocks are
\[
      U(X^n)+\varepsilon X V^*_w(X^n),\qquad
      V(X^n)+\varepsilon X U^*_w(X^n).
\]
Thus their sum and difference are signed graphs at width \(w\) on \(U+V\) and \(U-V\), with signs \(\varepsilon\) and \(-\varepsilon\) respectively.
Each has a coefficient row of norm at most \(H\) and support size at most \(|J|\).

First suppose \(U,V\) are both nonzero.
Their total number of coefficients is at most \(m+1\), so each has at most \(m\) coefficients.
We show that for fixed \(w\) and these fixed support sets, even the first normalized block cannot have \(G\) as a double factor at large \(n\).
Choose one representative \(x_i\) of each inverse pair of conjugates of \(\delta\).
By the [Salem conjugate calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#divisors) these \(s\) units are independent modulo torsion, and the \(m\) roots of \(G\) are the \(x_i^e\) with \(e=\pm1,\pm2\).
Form the value and Euler derivative matrix of the first normalized block at all these roots, with unknowns the coefficients on the two supports.
At the row for \(\alpha=x_i^e\) let \(y=z_i^e\), where \(z_i,N\) stand for \(x_i^n,n\).
A column of \(U\) at \(j\) has entries \(y^j,Nj y^j\), and a column of \(V\) at \(k\) has entries \(\varepsilon\alpha y^{w-k},\varepsilon\alpha(1+N(w-k))y^{w-k}\).

At \(N=0\), subtract the derivative row from the value row and divide the derivative row by \(\varepsilon\alpha\).
This gives separate evaluations of \(U(y)\) and \(y^wV(y^{-1})\) at \(m\) nodes \(y=z_i^e\).
Choose positive real \(z_i\) so that all \(m\) nodes are distinct.
Evaluation at these nodes, or at their inverses, is injective on any space spanned by at most \(m\) distinct monomials, by the [positive-zero bound](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#jets).
The full matrix thus has a maximal column minor nonzero as a Laurent polynomial in the \(z_i\) with polynomial coefficients in \(N\).
After grouping its Laurent monomials, evaluation at \((n,x_1^n,\ldots,x_s^n)\) gives a nonzero exponential polynomial whose distinct bases have nontorsion ratios.
The [eventual nonvanishing of such recurrences](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) makes the specialized matrix injective at all sufficiently large \(n\).
This is incompatible with the two nonzero rows \(U,V\).

If one row is empty, name the other \(U\) by exchanging the blocks if necessary.
The two original blocks give \(G^2\mid U(X^n)\) and \(G^2\mid F_{n,w,\varepsilon}(U)\).
The [fixed-width column independence](Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#statement) requires at least \(m+1\) nonzero coefficients of \(U\) eventually.
At exactly \(m+1\) the [fixed-support genuine-contact theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement) gives \(U(\delta^n)\ne0\), contradicting the first divisibility.
These results apply because the roots of \(G\) have nontorsion quotients and every fixed set of distinct positions is eventually distinct modulo the growing modulus in that theorem.
Only finitely many widths \(w\le W\), pairs of supports in those widths, and signs occur, so one onset excludes all distinct reflected blocks.

There is at most one block fixed by reflection because its extreme interval contains \(b/2\) and the extreme intervals are disjoint.
There must consequently be exactly one block.
Writing its first position as \(u\) gives \(b=2u+w\) and \(A(Y)=Y^u B(Y)\).
The original graph equals \(X^{nu}F_{n,w,\varepsilon}(B)\).
The same column independence and genuine-contact results now give full support size \(m+1\), a unique primitive generator up to sign, and nonvanishing at all prescribed powered roots.
They apply simultaneously to the finitely many possible centered supports.

<a id="rate"></a>
## Height along arbitrary support sequences

If the lower limit of \(\log\|A\|_1/n\) is finite, take a subsequence realizing it and bounded above by a constant \(C\).
The centered reduction puts this subsequence into finitely many widths, supports and signs, so pass to one fixed choice.
The norm is at least that of its primitive generator, whose full-sequence rate is at least \((3d-6)h\) by the [fixed-support height theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#statement).
An infinite lower limit already satisfies this inequality.
For support size at most \(m\), the centered reduction rules out every fixed exponential bound eventually, giving divergence to \(+\infty\).
The strict test requires \(\log\|A\|_1/n<(\log 2)/8\), strictly below \((3d-6)h\) by the same theorem, which gives the asserted uniform support exclusion by contradiction.

For a prescribed sign, the fixed support \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\) at width \(m\) provides a primitive row of finite height rate for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Choose \(C\) strictly above that rate.
The positive integral norms have a least value \(H_n\), which is bounded above by the norm of this fixed competitor and thus by \(\exp(Cn)\) eventually.
Every minimizer then centers into one of finitely many supports at its original sign with no change in norm.
Conversely every such centered support at width at most \(W\) is admissible for the unrestricted minimum when its primitive row exists.
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(H_n\) is therefore the minimum of the primitive norms on the finite list of \(m+1\)-position supports of widths at most \(W\).
Each normalized logarithm has a limit by the fixed-support theorem, hence the minimum has the smallest of these limits.
Every other fixed support is itself a competitor eventually, so its rate is at least that limit.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed splitting the folded support into bounded blocks and pairing the normalized rows on reflected blocks.
A collaborating agent derived the uniform cutoff for values and Euler derivatives and the exclusion of all distinct reflected blocks by specialization at \(N=0\).
