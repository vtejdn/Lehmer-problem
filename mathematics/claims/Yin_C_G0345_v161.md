# A Mahler gap for exponents in a short progression of residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## Specialization without a bound on the base-variable exponents

Let \(F(X,Y)\) be a complex Laurent polynomial with \(k\) nonzero terms and width \(R\ge1\) in \(Y\), the difference of the largest and smallest \(Y\)-exponents.
Let \(H>R\) be an integer and \(N\) any integer coprime to \(H\).
For every real \(\phi\) put \(P_\phi(z)=F(z^H,e^{i\phi}z^N)\).
The logarithmic one- and two-variable Mahler measures satisfy, for an absolute \(C\),
\[
  m(P_\phi)-m_2(F)\le\frac{R\log2}{H},\qquad
  m_2(F)-m(P_\phi)\le C\frac{R k\log(2H)}{H}.                    \tag{1}
\]
For \(k=2\) the two measures agree exactly.
The orders and magnitudes of the \(X\)-exponents, the sizes of the coefficients and the number of repeated roots in a fiber are unrestricted.

In particular, let \(P\) be an irreducible noncyclotomic integral Laurent polynomial with \(k\) nonzero terms.
Suppose their exponents modulo \(H\) belong to \(\{c+jN:0\le j\le R\}\) with at least two occupied classes, \(1\le R<H\) and \((N,H)=1\).
With \(c_R=[2\exp(16384)(\log(3R))^{48}]^{-1}\) one has
\[
                 m(P)\ge c_R-C R k\log(2H)/H.                       \tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(R\), \(H\ge C_R k\log(2H)\) with sufficiently large \(C_R\) therefore gives a positive uniform lower bound.
Individual exponents in the same residue class may occur arbitrarily far apart or interlace the exponents in every other class.
The coordinator proposed averaging over all \(H\) preimages of a base point and supplied the local derivative estimate which avoids an exponential loss in the fiber width.
The contributor chose the regularization scale and derived the finite bounds, the arithmetic consequence and the examples.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Exact averaging over the preimages

Multiplication of \(F\) by a Laurent monomial only adds a factor of modulus one on the integration circles.
Normalize the occupied powers of \(Y\) to range from zero through \(R\).
Two occupied monomials colliding on specialization would satisfy \(H(i-i')+N(j-j')=0\), forcing \(H\mid j-j'\) and hence \(j=j'\), \(i=i'\).
Thus \(P_\phi\) is nonzero with exactly the original \(k\) coefficient moduli.
For a binomial the quotient of its monomials is a nonconstant character both before and after specialization.
Its Haar distribution is uniform on the circle, giving the same logarithm of the greater coefficient modulus in both cases.

For almost every base point \(X\) on the circle the fiber has its full degree \(R\) and nonzero end coefficients.
Let \(y_i=y_i(X)\) be all its roots with multiplicity and \(v_i=y_i\) if \(|y_i|\le1\), \(v_i=1/\bar y_i\) otherwise.
These can be chosen measurably, for instance by ordering the finite root multiset lexicographically in real and imaginary parts.
The logarithmic fiber measure \(b(X)=m_Y(F(X,Y))\) satisfies on \(|Y|=1\)
\[
                  |F(X,Y)|=e^{b(X)}\prod_{i=1}^R |1-\bar v_iY|.
\]
This is the [reflected fiber identity](Yin_C_G0345_v159.md#series) from Jensen's formula; \(b\) is integrable with mean \(m_2(F)\), including when the original coefficients have a common nonconstant factor in \(X\).
For \(0<r\le1\), define \(m_{\phi,r}\) as the integral in \(z\) of \(b(z^H)+\sum_i\log|1-r\bar v_i(z^H)e^{i\phi}z^N|\).
At \(r=1\) it is \(m(P_\phi)\).
For a fixed \(X=z^H\), multiplication of \(z\) by every \(H\)-th root of unity makes \(Y=e^{i\phi}z^N\) range over precisely the \(H\) solutions of \(Y^H=e^{iH\phi}X^N\).
Averaging their equally weighted logarithms gives the exact identity
\[
     m_{\phi,r}-m_2(F)=\frac1H\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_{i=1}^R
           \log|1-r^H\bar v_i(X)^H e^{iH\phi}X^N|\,d\mu(X).        \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\mu\) is Haar probability.
For \(r=1\), the finite averaging of logarithms is valid away from the finitely many specialized zeros and exceptional fibers, and their original sum is integrable.
In particular (3) at \(r=1\) gives the upper inequality of (1).
For \(r<1\), it gives \(|m_{\phi,r}-m_2(F)|\le (R/H)\log(1/(1-r^H))\) since all folded roots are in the closed disk.

<a id="layers"></a>
## Small values with a linear cost in the fiber width

Let \(B\) be the sum of the absolute coefficients of \(F\).
Noncollision and Parseval give \(\|P_\phi\|_\infty\ge B/\sqrt k\).
For a chosen folded root define \(D=|1-\bar v_i(z^H)Y|\) at the original \(Y=e^{i\phi}z^N\).
When \(D<1/(4R)\), the corresponding actual root \(y_i\) has distance at most \(2D\) from \(Y\).
In the closed disk this distance equals \(D\); outside it equals \(|y_i|D\), and \(|y_i|^{-1}=|v_i|\ge1-D\).
Throughout the segment from \(Y\) to \(y_i\),
\[
        |\partial_Y F(z^H,y)|\le R B(1+2D)^{R-1}\le C R B .
\]
Since the fiber vanishes at \(y_i\), this proves \(|P_\phi|/\|P_\phi\|_\infty\le C R\sqrt k D\) in the stated small range.
For \(k\ge3\) put \(h=1/(k-1)\).
The [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) on an interval of length \(2\pi\) for the \(k\) distinct imaginary frequencies of \(P_\phi\) gives, for absolute constants \(C_0,C_1\),
\[
                  \Pr(D<v)\le C_1(C_0 R\sqrt k\,v)^h\qquad(v>0).
\]
For larger \(v\ge1/(4R)\) this follows by the probability bound one after increasing \(C_0\).
Write \(\epsilon=1-r\).
For each factor the regularized logarithm minus the original is at most \(\log(1+\epsilon/D)\).
Integrating its layer probabilities against \(\epsilon/(v(v+\epsilon))\) bounds its mean by \(C k(C_0 R\sqrt k\,\epsilon)^h\).
Indeed the substitution \(v=\epsilon x\) leaves \(\int_0^\infty x^{h-1}/(1+x)\,dx\le 1/h+1/(1-h)\), and \(0<h\le1/2\).
This proof also verifies integrability of the individual logarithmic singularities.
Take \(\epsilon=(C_0R\sqrt k)^{-1}H^{-(k-1)}\), increasing \(C_0\) to at least one.
The total regularization excess over the \(R\) roots is then \(O(Rk/H)\).
Since \(1-r^H\ge\epsilon\) and \(H>R\),
\[
        \log(1/\epsilon)=\log(C_0R\sqrt k)+(k-1)\log H
                           \ll k\log(2H).
\]
Together with (3) this proves the lower comparison in (1), uniformly in the phase.

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Detecting a horizontal component from residue classes

For the congruence criterion, subtract \(c\) from each exponent after multiplying \(P\) by a Laurent monomial.
Each occupied exponent has a unique expression \(Hi+Nj\) with \(0\le j\le R\), by the assumed congruence and the distinct residues of \(0,N,\ldots,RN\).
Placing its coefficient at \(X^iY^j\) constructs an integral \(F\) of positive \(Y\)-width at most \(R\).
If \(F\) has a \(Y\)-dependent geometric component that is not a torsion translate, the [partial-degree height gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(m_2(F)\ge c_R\), also if \(P\) is reducible.
Otherwise [the horizontal factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal) expresses \(F\) up to Laurent monomials and sign as a base-only integral factor times cyclotomic factors in monomials.
At least one such factor has the form \(\Phi_s(X^aY^b)\) with \(b>0\).
Widths in \(Y\) add on products over the Laurent domain, so \(b\le b\varphi(s)\le R<H\).
Under the integer specialization it becomes \(\Phi_s(z^{aH+bN})\), which is nonconstant since \((H,N)=1\) would otherwise imply \(H\mid b\).
Up to a Laurent unit it is a nonconstant product of cyclotomic polynomials, incompatible with the stipulated irreducible noncyclotomic \(P\).
This proves (2).
The restriction to two occupied classes matters: a base-only \(A(z^H)\) always has exactly \(m(A)\).

<a id="examples"></a>
## Reciprocal polynomials with two interlaced sets of exponents

Let \(M,H\ge3\) be integers, \(A_M(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^M-2X\), and \(A_M^*(X)=X^M A_M(1/X)\).
The monic integral reciprocal unit polynomial
\[
                   A_M(z^H)+z A_M^*(z^H)
\]
has degree \(MH+1\) and exactly \(2(M+1)\) nonzero coefficients, with occupied exponents \(0,1,H,H+1,\ldots,MH,MH+1\).
In particular its next-to-leading coefficient is nonzero.
Its lift \(A_M(X)+Y A_M^*(X)\) has, after removal of any common base factor, the component \(Y=-A_M(X)/A_M^*(X)\).
This rational function is not a scalar monomial: numerator and denominator have nonzero constant terms, the same degree and distinct first coefficients, with equal constants.
A torsion coset that is the graph of a rational function of \(X\) would have equation \(Y=\xi X^a\) by its degree one over the base.
Hence (1) and the positive partial-degree gap give \(m(P)\ge c_1-O(M\log(2H)/H)\), without a factorization hypothesis on this example.
In fact \(|A_M|=|A_M^*|\) on the unit circle, so Jensen in \(Y\) identifies the comparison measure exactly as \(m(A_M)\).
Taking \(M=\lfloor \sqrt H\rfloor\) for large \(H\) gives increasing numbers of interlaced nonzero coefficients of absolute value one and a uniform gap in this family.
