# Sharp dissociated densities from returns of the exterior arguments

<a id="statement"></a>
## The alternatives for a loss of density

Let \(P\) run along a sequence of minimal polynomials of nontorsion units with \(m=\log M(P)\to0\).
Write \(D=D_1(P)\) for the limiting maximum density of exponents supporting distinct binary subset sums.
Suppose there are exactly \(s\) exterior conjugates \(\beta_i\), with \(s\) fixed, and that
\[
                   \max_i\left|\frac{\log|\beta_i|}{m}-\frac1s\right|\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Put \(\theta_i=\arg(\beta_i)/(2\pi)\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), and write \(\|x\|\) for distance to the nearest integer.
If \(D\log2/m\) stays below \(1-\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\), then on a subsequence at least one of the following holds, after labeling the conjugates.

* Two distinct exterior arguments satisfy \(\|\theta_i-\theta_j\|=O(m)\).
* Some fixed nonzero \(h\in\mathbb Z^s\) and constants \(0<c<C\) satisfy \(cm\le\|\sum_i h_i\theta_i\|\le Cm\).

An identically integral combination of arguments does not supply the second alternative.
In the absence of either alternative on every subsequence one has \(D\log2/m\to1\).
Periodic masks realize densities with this same asymptotic, and the min-entropy rate and the Shannon entropy rate of fair binary sums, in nats, are both asymptotic to \(m\).
When \(s=1\) the sharp density conclusion always holds.
When the only exterior roots are one nonreal conjugate pair the radial hypothesis is automatic.
In that case a fixed density loss forces the argument of one of them to approach a rational angle within \(O(m)\) in turns on a subsequence.
At a limiting angle other than \(0\) and \(1/2\) the distance is also bounded below by a positive multiple of \(m\).

<a id="block"></a>
## Reading a periodic mask at several embeddings

Let \(q\) be a positive integer and choose \(s\) distinct residues \(J\subseteq[0,q)\).
Form the matrix \(V=(\beta_i^j)_{1\le i\le s,\,j\in J}\) with rows indexed by the \(s\) embeddings and columns by \(J\).
If \(V\) is invertible and
\[
           \|B\|_\infty<1/2,\qquad B=V^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\beta_1^{-q},\ldots,\beta_s^{-q})V ,
\]
then every finite subset of the periodic mask with residues \(J\) is admissible.
Here the matrix norm is maximum absolute row sum.
Indeed a ternary relation on the mask gives, after evaluation at these embeddings, division by the last nonzero block and change of coordinates, an identity
\[
                         c_0+\sum_{b\ge1}B^b c_b=0
\]
with a finite tail and ternary coefficient vectors, where \(c_0\ne0\).
Specifically, divide row \(i\) by \(\beta_i^{q t}\) for the last occupied block index \(t\), then multiply by \(V^{-1}\).
The tail has norm at most \(\|B\|_\infty/(1-\|B\|_\infty)<1\), whereas \(\|c_0\|_\infty=1\).

Suppose \(s\ge2\) and \(\Delta=\min_{i\ne j}\|\theta_i-\theta_j\|\) satisfies \(\Delta/m\to\infty\).
Choose integer lengths \(L\) with \(Lm\to0\) and \(L\Delta\to\infty\), for instance by rounding \((m\Delta)^{-1/2}\).
For the matrix \(W=(\beta_i^j)_{1\le i\le s,\,0\le j<L}\) one has \(WW^*/L\to I\).
Indeed the radial factors in each product are \(1+o(1)\) uniformly in the columns, and the geometric sum for distinct rows divided by \(L\) has modulus at most \(1/(2L\Delta)\) after the radial factors are removed.
Cauchy–Binet now gives a set of \(s\) columns with determinant bounded below in absolute value by a positive constant depending only on \(s\), since the sum of the squared absolute minors is \((1+o(1))L^s\).
Their entries are bounded, so the resulting \(V\) and \(V^{-1}\) have norms bounded in terms of \(s\).
All selected residues are \(o(1/m)\).

<a id="return"></a>
## A missed return in a macroscopic interval

Fix \(0<a<b\) and \(0<\tau<1/2\).
For sufficiently large \(H\), if no integer \(q\in[aH,bH]\) satisfies \(\max_i\|q\theta_i\|<\tau\), then there is an integer vector \(h\ne0\) of bounded norm such that
\[
                c/H\le\|h\cdot\theta\|\le C/H.
\]
All constants and the norm bound depend only on \(a,b,\tau,s\).

To prove this take on each circle the triangular function \(f(x)=(1-\|x\|/\tau)_+\).
It is the normalized convolution of the indicator of \([-\tau/2,\tau/2]\) with its reflection, so its Fourier coefficients are nonnegative and absolutely summable, with sum one and constant coefficient \(\tau\).
The product over the \(s\) coordinates has constant coefficient \(c_0=\tau^s\) and coefficient sum one.
Truncate to a fixed box of frequencies with discarded coefficient sum less than \(c_0/4\).
If \(\|h\cdot\theta\|<c/H\), choose \(c>0\) small enough that all exponentials on the indicated interval have nonnegative real part.
If \(\|h\cdot\theta\|>C/H\), their average has modulus at most \(1/(2|\mathbb Z\cap[aH,bH]|\,\|h\cdot\theta\|)<c_0/4\) for large enough \(C,H\).
In the absence of an intermediate nonzero frequency the real average of the product function is therefore positive.
A point with positive product gives the required simultaneous return.

<a id="saturation"></a>
## The density and entropy rates

Put \(H=\log2/m\).
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and look for a return with \(q\in[s(1+\varepsilon)H,s(1+2\varepsilon)H]\).
For such \(q\), radial balance gives
\[
       |\beta_i|^{-q}=\exp(-qm/s)+o(1)
\]
uniformly in the embeddings and the interval.
If \(\max_i\|q\theta_i\|<\tau\), then \(B\) is within \(O_s(\tau)+o(1)\) in matrix norm of \(\exp(-qm/s)I\) for the columns chosen above.
Choose \(\tau\) small enough that this error is eventually less than \(1/2-2^{-(1+\varepsilon)}\).
The block argument gives \(D\ge s/q\ge m/((1+2\varepsilon)\log2)\).
For a persistent loss \(\delta\), choose \(\varepsilon\) with \(1/(1+2\varepsilon)>1-\delta\).
Unless the pair alternative holds on a subsequence, \(\Delta/m\to\infty\); hence the desired return is impossible and the frequency lemma gives the second alternative, passing to a subsequence to fix its bounded vector.
The [upper density bound](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement) proves the sharp limit when both obstructions are absent.
Letting \(\varepsilon\) decrease by a diagonal choice gives periodic masks with that limiting ratio.
For \(s=1\), the sole exterior root is real and exponents spaced by \(\lceil\log2/m\rceil\) already suffice, by domination of all earlier terms.
For one complex pair, the two arguments are negatives of each other.
The first alternative puts one angle within \(O(m)\) of \(0\) or \(1/2\).
In the second, \(h_1-h_2\ne0\), so the angle lies at distance comparable to \(m\) from a rational with this fixed denominator, after taking a subsequence.

Finally let \(X_N=\sum_{i<N}\xi_i\alpha^i\) for independent fair binary digits.
Conditioning on all digits off an admissible mask of size \(a_N\) gives \(\max_x\mathbb P(X_N=x)\le2^{-a_N}\).
The min-entropy \(-\log\max_x\mathbb P(X_N=x)\) is at most the Shannon entropy.
The logarithm of the number of possible values is at most \(Nm+O_P(\log(N+1))\), by the [embedding boxes for ordinary algebraic integers](Yin_C_G0345_v560.md#count) applied with digit weight bounded by \(N\) and derivative order zero.
Dividing by \(N\) bounds both rates between \(D\log2\) and \(m\).
The rates exist by subadditivity for independent blocks, using multiplication by \(\alpha^N\) on the second block and the product lower bound for its most likely joint outcome with the first.
This proves the entropy assertion.
