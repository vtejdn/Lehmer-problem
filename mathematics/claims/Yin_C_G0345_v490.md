# Bounded gaps between degrees realizing each measure below a strict threshold

<a id="statement"></a>
## Passing degrees in moving intervals

Use the [strict transverse test](Yin_C_G0345_v480.md#statement) with real algebraic \(1<Q\le6/5\), primes \(p>3\), and integer width slopes \(2\le s_p\le p\) and joint normalized span caps \(1\le C_p\le p\) tending to infinity.
The common width is \(b=s_p d-1\).
Let \(h\) be any positive irreducible logarithmic Mahler measure strictly below \(\log Q\).
Choose a reciprocal nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(e\) and measure \(h\) such that every positive power generates its field, as in the [reduction for this threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v480.md#spectrum).
Let \(B\) be twice the product of the odd primes \(\ell\) for which \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)^\ell\).

For all sufficiently large primes the number of degrees in every real interval \([X,X+L)\), \(X,L\ge1\), realizing this particular \(h\) under the coefficient budget \(2H_+H_-<Q^{6p}\) is at least
\[
                 \frac{\varphi(B)}{eB}L-O_{\alpha,Q}(L/p+1).                 \tag{1}
\]
In fact (1) holds with the stronger budget \(2\max(H_+,H_-)^2<Q^{6p}\), with transversality retained.
Thus this one measure occurs with bounded gaps uniformly over the sufficiently large primes.
If the left side of either budget is multiplied by \(2pb+1\), the same estimate holds along sequences with \(p\to\infty\) and \(\log(2p(X+L))/p\to0\).
These statements also allow a bound tending to infinity on the number of occupied positions.

Consequently, if on any sequence of such intervals with \(p,L\to\infty\) the proportion of degrees passing the original test at any measure tends to zero, then the unrestricted Mahler infimum \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\lambda\ge\log Q\).
The converse follows from the [determinant lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement), since then no degree passes.

<a id="determinants"></a>
## Phase determinants at a shared exterior radius

Write \(P_a=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha^a)\) for \(a=1,2\), and \(G=P_1P_2\).
Distinct roots of \(G\) have nontorsion quotient by the [power-field argument](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#matrices).
Fix an exterior radius \(R>1\) of \(G\), list the \(n\) roots \(x_i\) on that circle, and put \(\xi_i=R/x_i\).
These are algebraic numbers of modulus one since \(R^2=x_i\bar x_i\).
Take two sets \(J_0,J_1\) of \(n\) consecutive integers each.
For each \(i\) use a value row and a derivative row, with columns indexed by \((a,j)\), \(a=0,1,\ j\in J_a\).
Their entries are respectively
\[
           \xi_i^{pj+au},\qquad (pj+au)\xi_i^{pj+au}.                     \tag{2}
\]
For any fixed finite collection of such matrices, as \(p\to\infty\), all but a bounded number of integers \(u\) in \(0<|u|<p\) have every determinant nonzero.
Uniformly on the retained displacements the inverses have norm at most \(\exp(o(p))\).

For the zero count first fix any nonzero integer \(u_0\).
Replace \(p\) in the derivative multipliers by an indeterminate \(N\), keeping the phases \(\xi_i^p\).
At \(N=0\) the derivative rows involve only the second set of columns and have multiplier \(u_0\).
Both consecutive-power Vandermonde blocks are invertible at every positive integer phase exponent since the \(\xi_i^p\) are pairwise distinct.
The additional phase factors \(\xi_i^{u_0}\) never vanish.
The determinant with \(N=p\) is an exponential polynomial in \(p\) with polynomial coefficients.
On each progression killing the torsion quotients of its bases, the nonvanishing at \(N=0\) and at a positive phase exponent on that progression ensures that some grouped coefficient polynomial is nonzero.
The [fixed-progression recurrence argument](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#recurrences) gives nonvanishing there for all sufficiently large \(p\).

With \(p\) fixed, the determinants as functions of \(u\) are linear combinations of \(u^j t^u\) from fixed finite lists of \(j,t\), independent of \(p\).
Choose a finite place where all these algebraic bases are units.
By the [finite-dimensional disk zero bound](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#exceptions) there is a common bound on the number of zeros on each of finitely many residue classes in \(u\), provided the restricted power series is nonzero there.
Choosing one fixed nonzero \(u_0\) in each class supplies that condition for every large \(p\) by the preceding argument.
Taking the union over the matrices proves the claimed zero count.

To bound a nonzero determinant from below, expand it into terms \(z_i\) of the form \(c p^a u^b t^p v^u\), with nonzero fixed algebraic \(c,t,v\), \(|t|=|v|=1\) at the chosen complex embedding, and nonnegative integers \(a,b\).
Work in a fixed number field with a finite set \(S\) of places containing the archimedean places and the nonunit places of the fixed constants.
Each term has height \(O(p)\), its absolute log modulus at the chosen place is \(O(\log(2p))\), and its sum of absolute log moduli outside \(S\) is \(O(\log(2p))\).
These estimates remain valid after fixed nonzero rescalings.

Suppose along a sequence that a nonzero sum of \(l\) such terms has modulus below \(\exp(-\delta p)\) for a fixed \(\delta>0\).
For \(l=1\) this contradicts the local modulus bound.
For \(l\ge2\) use the coordinate forms at all places of \(S\), replacing the first coordinate at the chosen place by the sum.
The product formula and the outside-\(S\) bound give a sum of local proximities at least \(l h([z])+\delta' p-O(\log(2p))\) for some \(\delta'>0\) allowing for place normalization.
Since \(h([z])=O(p)\), the [number-field Subspace Theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations) confines the points to finitely many proper hyperplanes.
On a subsequence one fixed relation eliminates a coordinate from the sum.
Discard zero coefficients and absorb the remaining fixed coefficients into the terms.
The sum stays nonzero and has fewer terms, contradicting induction.
Thus every nonzero determinant has modulus at least \(\exp(-o(p))\) uniformly for \(0<|u|<p\).
Its cofactors grow at most polynomially, proving the inverse bound.

<a id="heights"></a>
## A lower bound for every row with the double contacts

Fix a width \(w\).
Consider any nonzero integral two-row expression \(U(X^{-p})+X^{-u}V(X^{-p})\), with \(\deg U,\deg V\le w\), having double zeros at all roots of \(G\).
The same form applies to a signed graph \(A(X^p)+\sigma X^u A^{*w}(X^p)\) after multiplication by \(X^{-pw-u}\) and reversal of the rows.
Write \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
Outside a bounded number of displacements per prime,
\[
                         \log H\ \ge\ (3h-o(1))p                           \tag{3}
\]
uniformly over all such rows.

To prove (3), pass to any sequence with bounded \(\log H/p\), take subsequences with fixed occupied indices, convergent \(u/p\) and convergent coefficient slopes \(p^{-1}\log|c_j|\).
For each occupied monomial let \(\gamma\ge0\) be its limiting coefficient slope and \(t\) its limiting position \(j\) or \(j+u/p\).
Subtract the smallest limiting position from all \(t\) and form \(L(s)=\max(\gamma-ts)\), \(s\ge0\).
At \(s=\log R\) the difference between the largest and smallest maximizing positions must be at least the number \(n\) of double nodes at this radius.
Indeed, if their difference were smaller than \(n\), all active indices in each of the two sets of integer indices would fit within \(n\) consecutive integers.
Choose such blocks for both sets, including an arbitrary block for a set with no active index.
Only finitely many choices are needed at the fixed width, allowing zero coefficients beyond its endpoints.
Use (2) on the two blocks, weighting each actual coefficient by \(R^{-pj-au}\) and dividing by the largest weighted modulus among both whole rows.
The resulting vector has maximum modulus one for large \(p\), since the omitted terms have strictly smaller limiting slopes at \(\log R\).
Its matrix image has norm \(O(\exp(-\eta p))\) for some \(\eta>0\) by the exact value and Euler derivative equations, since each omitted coefficient has this exponential loss after normalization and each derivative multiplier is \(O_w(p)\).
If there are no omitted terms the image is zero.
The inverse bound contradicts either conclusion after excluding the bounded set of bad displacements for these finitely many matrices.

The drop in maximizing position at each prescribed \(\log R\) therefore contributes at least \(n\log R\) to \(L(0)-L(+\infty)\), by integration of the successive slopes of this piecewise linear function.
Here \(L(+\infty)\ge0\) since a coefficient in the smallest position is a nonzero integer at each prime.
Summing over the exterior radii gives \(L(0)\ge\log M(G)=3h\).
Since \(\liminf \log H/p\ge L(0)\) on the selected subsequence, this proves (3).

<a id="genuine"></a>
## Making a short transverse pair genuine at both targets

Put \(m=2e,\ S=3h,\ w=m+r-1\) with \(r\) a fixed positive integer to be chosen.
At every sufficiently large good prime and every \(0<|u|<p\) the [signed kernel pairing](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#pairing) supplies a transverse pair before imposing genuineness.
Indeed the good-prime proof works for negative \(u\) in the unit Laurent quotient, and the pairing eliminates the common \(X^u\).
The [centered minor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#height) applies verbatim with these \(m,r,w\), since it pairs the \(m\) largest distances among the \(w+1\) integer columns and uses only \(w+1\ge m\).
If \(v_j\) are the decreasing absolute logs for one representative of each inverse pair of \(G\), then \(\sum_jv_j=S\).
Writing \(U_w=\sum_{j=1}^{m/2}(w-2j+2)v_j=(r-1)S+U_m\), the [complementary minima estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#budgets) gives
\[
                \log(H_+H_-)/p \le 2U_w/r+O_{\alpha,w}(\log(2p)/p).
\]
For any prescribed \(\tau>0\) choose \(r\) large enough that the right side is eventually below \(2S+\tau\).
By (3) each of the two selected rows then satisfies \(\log H_\pm/p\le S+\tau+o(1)\) at every retained displacement.

Write \(Q_a=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{ap})\) for \(a=1,2\).
These are distinct reciprocal polynomials of measures \(aph\).
Take \(\tau<h\).
For each \(a\) at least one of the two rows has nonzero numerator at \(\alpha^{ap}\).
Otherwise \(Q_a\) divides both signed rows as polynomials in \(Y\), and hence also their reversals.
Their two graphs after division by \(Q_a(X^p)\) retain a simple contact at every root of \(P_a\) and a double contact at every root of the other minimum.
Eliminating \(X^u\) shows that their determinant is divisible by \(Q_a Q_{3-a}^2\), since the power map is unramified and the two powered orbits are disjoint.
Restoring \(Q_a^2\) forces \(Q_a^3Q_{3-a}^2\) into the nonzero original determinant, contradicting its log Mahler upper bound \(\log(2H_+H_-) < (6+a)ph\) for large \(p\).

At common width \(w+1\) form the two plus rows \(U=(1+Y)A_+\) and \(V=(1-Y)A_-\).
Their graphs retain the double factors because \(1+Y\) is reciprocal and \(1-Y\) is antireciprocal at width one.
The determinant \(UV^*-VU^*\) is the old transverse determinant times \(-(1-Y^2)\).
At each target at least one of \(U,V\) has nonzero numerator since the powered nodes are nontorsion.
At most two values of \(t\) make \(U+tV\) vanish at either selected node.
Choose distinct \(t_1,t_2\) from \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) avoiding them.
The two rows \(U+t_iV\) have nonzero ordinary determinant with their reversals by linearity and \(t_1\ne t_2\).
At width \(w+2\) multiply the first of these by \(1+Y\) and the second by \(1-Y\), producing the required opposite signs.
The two-row determinant remains nonzero, both numerators stay genuine, and each one-norm costs at most a fixed multiple of the former maximum.
Choose \(\tau>0\) smaller than both \(h\) and \(3(\log Q-h)\).
The resulting bound on each row has a fixed strict margin below \(3\log Q\) after division by \(p\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Raising the degree at the prescribed width

Pad both rows at the base degree to \(c=s_p e-1\) by the common reciprocal width multiplier \(Y^{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}(1+Y)^{t\bmod2}\), where \(t=c-(w+2)\ge0\) eventually.
This multiplies each norm by at most two and leaves the occupied indices in an interval of fixed length after common translation.
For each positive \(k\) coprime to \(Bp\), let \(u\) be the unique odd integer in \((-p,p)\) congruent to \(k^{-1}\bmod p\).
If this \(u\) was retained, the [odd radical substitution and common translation](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#intervals) lift both rows at \(c\) to the prescribed width \(s_p e k-1=kc+k-1\).
The two minima are \(P_a(X^k)\), of degree \(ek\), and the initial measure is \(h\).
The construction preserves both norms, both numerator nonvanishings and the nonzero determinant.
The growing cap eventually exceeds the fixed normalized span since the common substitution and translation preserve relative positions up to scaling.

At most a bounded number of classes \(k\bmod p\) were lost, including zero.
Inclusion-exclusion for \((k,B)=1\) followed by deletion of these classes gives (1) as in the [moving-interval count](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#intervals).
For the charged test \(\log(2pb+1)=o(p)\) uniformly on the stated intervals since \(s_p\le p\).
The fixed strict margin therefore also covers the charge.
