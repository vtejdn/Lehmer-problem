# Optimal exterior counts from real circuit roots and their cut orbits

This fixed account concerns [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The coordinating agent supplied the projected-orbit obstruction, the exact cut-orbit minimum and the primitive cut-code bound.
The agent-s global strategy contributor checked their arithmetic interfaces, constructed the extremizing partial products and wrote this account.
An auxiliary author-side checker verified the finite-group quotient argument for the two-exterior case.
These are development checks; independent whole-proof review remains pending under the open decision below.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact minimum in the radical saturation

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with \(e\ge3\) exterior roots, all real.
Suppose their positive logarithmic moduli form a rational circuit.
The [real circuit decomposition](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) partitions the inverse-pair labels into \(n=m/e\) parent blocks of size \(e\), one exterior and the others neutral.
Assume first that \(n\ge2\).

Orient representatives \(x_i\) on each parent so that their product is the same sign \(\omega\in\{1,-1\}\).
Let \(E\) be the exterior parent, and let \(H\le S_E\) be the unsigned action of its setwise stabilizer in the splitting-field Galois group.
It is transitive.
Let \(\Gamma\) be the multiplicative group generated by all roots of \(P\), and put
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma=
 \{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:y^N\in\mu_\infty\Gamma
                           \text{ for some }N\ge1\}.
\]
For a nonempty proper subset \(A\subset E\), its unoriented cut is \(\{A,E\setminus A\}\).
Define the finite, exactly computable invariant
\[
 \kappa(H)=
 \min_{\varnothing\ne A\ne E}
                   |H\cdot\{A,E\setminus A\}|.                    \tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
 \min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma\\y\text{ nontorsion}}}
       \#\{\text{exterior conjugates of }y\}=\kappa(H).             \tag{2}
\]
No reciprocity or reality is assumed for \(y\) in this minimum.

The minimum is attained by an ordinary squared partial product
\[
                              y_A=\left(\prod_{i\in A}x_i\right)^2
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
for some minimizing subset \(A\).
Its minimal polynomial is monic integral, irreducible and reciprocal, of degree \(2n\kappa(H)\).
It has exactly \(\kappa(H)\) exterior roots, all real and positive.
For
\[
 t_i=\log|x_i|\quad(i\in E),\qquad
 s_A=\sum_{i\in A}t_i,\qquad \mu=\log M(P)=\sum_{i\in E}|t_i|,
\]
choose one side \(A_r\) of every cut in \(H\cdot\{A,A^c\}\).
The exact measure and its bound are
\[
 \log M(y_A)=2\sum_{r=1}^{\kappa(H)}|s_{A_r}|
                     \le\kappa(H)\mu.                            \tag{4}
\]
Here \(M(y_A)\) means the Mahler measure of its actual minimal polynomial.
The choice of side does not change (4).
If \(\omega=1\), unsquared partial products also attain the same degree and exterior count, with
\[
 \log M\left(\prod_{i\in A}x_i\right)
       =\sum_{r=1}^{\kappa(H)}|s_{A_r}|
       \le\tfrac12\kappa(H)\mu;                                   \tag{5}
\]
their exterior roots are real, without a positivity assertion.

Thus every radical output is subject to a finite permutation-theoretic minimum, and that minimum is attained by a product of an actual subset of the input roots.
The theorem is about the optimal exterior count, not a minimum of Mahler measure within that count.

<a id="setup"></a>
## Parent orientations, the logarithm map and pure inversion

The [orientation argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v301.md#parents) gives a common sign \(\omega\) and shows that every Galois element transports a parent by an ordinary permutation followed by one common inversion sign.
The complete relation lattice modulo torsion consists of the exponent vectors constant on each parent.
For the exterior parent,
\[
       \sum_{i\in E}t_i=0,\qquad
       \ker_{\mathbb Q}\left(a\longmapsto\sum_i a_i t_i\right)
                              =\mathbb Q\mathbf1_E.               \tag{6}
\]
Consequently the map
\[
 V=\{v\in\mathbb Q^E:\sum_i v_i=0\}
       \longrightarrow\mathbb R,\qquad
                         \ell(v)=\sum_i v_i t_i                   \tag{7}
\]
is injective.
The centered projection of an exponent vector \(a\in\mathbb Q^E\) is
\[
                         \pi(a)=a-\frac{\sum_i a_i}{e}\mathbf1_E.
\]
Subtracting this constant does not change its logarithmic value.

There is an actual Galois automorphism stabilizing \(E\) and inverting every representative in \(E\), without permuting its labels.
Let \(\tau\) be complex conjugation in the chosen embedding.
It fixes the real representatives on \(E\) and inverts every representative on a neutral parent.
Choose a neutral parent \(B\) and \(g\) taking \(B\) to \(E\).
Then \(g\tau g^{-1}\) acts on every root in \(E\) by inversion.
This uses only \(n\ge2\), not a full symmetric action of the stabilizer.
It is the [neutral-parent inversion mechanism](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#neutral-inversion) at precisely its stated scope.

<a id="lower"></a>
## Every radical output supplies a small symmetric projected orbit

Take a nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\), and let \(b(y)\) be its number of exterior conjugates.
Raise it to a positive power to remove both the radical denominator and its root-of-unity multiplier:
\[
                              w=y^N\in\Gamma.
\]
Every conjugate of \(w\) has an expression as a monomial in the oriented root representatives.
Its exponent vector is unique modulo the complete relation lattice.
Project its restriction to \(E\) by \(\pi\); the resulting vector is therefore well defined.
Let \(S\subset V\) be the set of distinct nonzero vectors so obtained from all conjugates of \(w\).

The set \(S\) is nonempty.
Otherwise every conjugate exponent vector would be constant on \(E\).
Sending each parent to \(E\) would then make the original exponent vector constant on every parent, forcing \(w\) to be torsion.
For a projected vector \(v\), its logarithm is exactly
\[
                               \log|w'|=\ell(v),                 \tag{8}
\]
because the other parents are neutral.
Injectivity in (7) identifies distinct projected vectors with distinct logarithmic values.

Pure inversion on \(E\) makes \(S=-S\).
Write \(|S|=2j\).
Exactly \(j\) vectors have positive logarithmic value, and these yield \(j\) distinct exterior conjugates of \(w\).
Every conjugate of \(w\) lifts to a conjugate of \(y\), and positive logarithms remain positive under the power map.
Thus
\[
                                  j\le b(y).                     \tag{9}
\]
This counts distinct actual conjugates, without assuming distinct moduli for all conjugates of \(y\).

The unsigned group \(H\) preserves \(S\).
A lift of \(h\in H\) acts on its projected vectors by \(h\) or by \(-h\), and the symmetry of \(S\) removes that common sign.
Therefore for every \(v\in S\),
\[
                       |H\cdot\{v,-v\}|\le j\le b(y).            \tag{10}
\]
Define
\[
          \lambda(H)=\min_{0\ne v\in V}|H\cdot\{v,-v\}|.
                                                                    \tag{11}
\]
The positive integer minimum exists, and (10) proves \(b(y)\ge\lambda(H)\).

There is also a useful quotient constraint for any such output.
Set \(W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}S\) and let \(K\triangleleft H\) be the kernel of its action on \(W\).
Then
\[
                         H/K\hookrightarrow C_2\wr S_j,
                           \qquad j\le b(y).                     \tag{12}
\]
Indeed its action on the \(j\) antipodal pairs is faithful together with their individual signs, since \(S\) spans \(W\).
The subgroup \(K\) is intransitive on \(E\): if it were transitive, every vector fixed by \(K\) would be constant, contradicting \(0\ne W\subset V\).
For primitive \(H\), the normal subgroup \(K\) must therefore be trivial.
The actual output then forces an embedding of \(H\) in a signed permutation group on at most \(b(y)\) coordinates.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## A minimum vector orbit always comes from a subset

For every nonempty proper \(A\subset E\), put
\[
                            v_A=\mathbf1_A-\frac{|A|}{e}\mathbf1_E.
\]
It is nonzero.
Its antipodal-pair stabilizer is precisely the stabilizer of the cut \(\{A,A^c\}\).
In fact \(h v_A=v_A\) means \(hA=A\), while \(h v_A=-v_A\) means \(hA=A^c\); the latter can occur only for \(|A|=e/2\).
Hence
\[
                     |H\cdot\{v_A,-v_A\}|
                              =|H\cdot\{A,A^c\}|,                \tag{13}
\]
and \(\lambda(H)\le\kappa(H)\).

Conversely take any \(0\ne v\in V\), and let
\[
                              J=\{h\in H:hv\in\{v,-v\}\}.
\]
If \(J\) is intransitive on \(E\), choose a nonempty proper \(J\)-orbit \(A\).
Then \(J\) fixes \(v_A\), so
\[
                   |H\cdot\{v_A,-v_A\}|
                          \le[H:J]=|H\cdot\{v,-v\}|.             \tag{14}
\]
If \(J\) is transitive, the absolute values of all coordinates of \(v\) are equal.
Their common value \(c\) is positive; otherwise \(v=0\).
The zero coordinate sum forces equally many \(c\)'s and \(-c\)'s.
For the subset \(A\) of positive coordinates,
\[
                                    v=2c\,v_A.
\]
Its antipodal pair has exactly the same stabilizer as that of \(v_A\).
Thus in every case a subset has no larger orbit.
Taking the minimum proves
\[
                                 \lambda(H)=\kappa(H).            \tag{15}
\]

For explicit computation one can enumerate the \(2^{e-1}-1\) nontrivial unoriented cuts.
For a fixed subset \(A\), its cut orbit has size \(|H\cdot A|\), except when \(|A|=e/2\) and \(A^c\in H\cdot A\), in which case the size is \(|H\cdot A|/2\).
No enumeration has been run or is needed for the theorem.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Squared partial products attain the exact minimum

Fix any nonempty proper subset \(A\subset E\), and write \(r=|H\cdot\{A,A^c\}|\).
Consider \(y_A=p_A^2\), where \(p_A=\prod_{i\in A}x_i\).
Every conjugate of \(y_A\) is supported on one parent and is a square of a partial product or its inverse.
On \(E\), the stabilizer gives exactly the signed orbit of these products: each unsigned permutation is realized with one common sign, and pure inversion provides the opposite sign.

The exponent relation lattice tests all coincidences.
Within one parent, two such squares with the same sign coincide only when the subsets coincide.
Opposite signs coincide precisely when their subsets are complementary: the exponent sum is then the constant vector \(2\mathbf1_E\), whose value is \(\omega^2=1\).
No squared product is equal to its own inverse, since a nonzero multiple of a proper-subset indicator is not constant.
Across different parents, any proposed equality has a nonconstant restriction on each involved parent and is impossible even modulo torsion.

There are therefore exactly \(2r\) distinct values over each parent, one inverse pair for each cut.
Transitivity on parents gives \(2nr\) values in the single Galois orbit of \(y_A\).
Their orbit polynomial is monic integral and irreducible because the values are algebraic units.
It is reciprocal because pure inversion takes \(y_A\) to \(y_A^{-1}\).
Only the exterior parent contributes off-circle values.
For a representative \(A_r\) of a cut, their logarithms are \(2s_{A_r}\) and \(-2s_{A_r}\), with \(s_{A_r}\ne0\) by (6).
Each pair contributes exactly one positive real exterior root.
This proves the degree, exterior count and exact formula in (4).

Since the positive and negative \(t_i\)'s each have total absolute mass \(\mu/2\),
\[
                     |s_A|\le\mu/2                              \tag{16}
\]
for every subset.
Summing (16) proves the bound in (4).
For \(\omega=1\), the same coincidence and inversion proof works without squares and gives (5).

Choose \(A\) attaining (1).
Equations (9)–(15) give the lower bound for every radical output, and this construction attains it.
This proves (2).
It also proves that an exterior-count extremizer can always be chosen reciprocal and with all exterior roots positive, even though the minimum allowed arbitrary algebraic outputs.

<a id="bipartition"></a>
## Exactly when one or two exterior conjugates are possible

For any transitive \(H\) at issue here,
\[
        \kappa(H)\le2
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \kappa(H)=1
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        H\text{ preserves a partition into two nonempty parts}.  \tag{17}
\]
A fixed cut is exactly such a partition, so the last equivalence is immediate.
For the first implication, either use (12) for a minimizing output or use the action on a minimizing symmetric subset orbit of size at most four.
The quotient \(H/K\) is a subgroup of \(C_2\wr S_j\) with \(j\le2\), hence is a finite \(2\)-group.
As proved above \(K\) is intransitive.
Choose one of its orbits and let \(J<H\) be its setwise stabilizer.
Then \(K\le J\), and \(J/K\) is a proper subgroup of \(H/K\).
It lies in a maximal subgroup of index two.
Pulling back gives an index-two subgroup \(L<H\) containing \(J\), and in particular containing the stabilizer in \(H\) of a label in that \(K\)-orbit.
The equivariant map from labels to the two cosets \(H/L\) gives the desired bipartition.
For completeness, let \(Q\) be a finite \(p\)-group and \(U<Q\).
The action of \(U\) on \(Q/U\) by left multiplication has a positive number of fixed points divisible by \(p\), since its nontrivial orbit lengths and \([Q:U]\) are divisible by \(p\).
Its fixed points are exactly the cosets represented by the normalizer \(N_Q(U)\), so \([N_Q(U):U]\ge p\).
A maximal subgroup is therefore normal, and its quotient has no proper nontrivial subgroup; taking a subgroup of order \(p\) in that quotient shows that its order is \(p\).

Combining (2), (17) and the [two-part construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v301.md#two-parts) gives the exact arithmetic criterion:
there is a nontorsion element of \(\widetilde\Gamma\) with at most two exterior conjugates if and only if \(H\) has an invariant bipartition.
When it does, there is already a Salem number of degree \(2n\) in this saturation with
\[
                    0<\log M_{\mathrm{Salem}}\le\mu.              \tag{18}
\]
When \(\omega=1\), the bound improves to \(\mu/2\).
Thus allowing two exterior conjugates supplies no additional inputs beyond those admitting a one-exterior partial-product output.

There is also a precise \(n=1\) variant.
If \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\) is required to have a reciprocal minimal polynomial, its conjugate power orbit is inverse-closed.
That property replaces neutral-parent inversion in the symmetry argument for \(S\), so (9)–(12) and the necessity of a bipartition still hold.
Conversely an invariant bipartition gives the [reciprocal quadratic output](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v301.md#two-parts), of measure at most that of \(P\).
Thus the same existence criterion holds for reciprocal outputs when \(n=1\).
The exact optimization (2) and its general attainment proof are asserted here for \(n\ge2\); this paragraph does not extend them to arbitrary nonreciprocal outputs with one parent.

<a id="primitive"></a>
## A logarithmic lower bound for primitive parent actions

Suppose \(H\) is primitive on \(E\), with \(e\ge3\), and take an orbit of \(r\) nontrivial unoriented cuts.
Orient the cuts arbitrarily and assign to every label its \(r\)-bit string of side memberships.
The action of \(H\) on these strings consists of permuting coordinates and optionally reversing individual bits.

Equality of strings is an \(H\)-invariant equivalence relation on labels.
It is not universal, because every cut is nontrivial.
Primitivity makes it discrete, so all label strings are distinct.
The relation that two strings are equal or bitwise complementary is also invariant.
Each equivalence class has size at most two by the preceding injectivity.
Since \(e>2\), this second equivalence cannot be universal, and primitivity again makes it discrete.
Thus the \(e\) distinct label strings contain no complementary pair.
There are only \(2^{r-1}\) pairs of complementary \(r\)-bit strings, so
\[
                       e\le2^{r-1},\qquad
               r\ge1+\lceil\log_2 e\rceil.                        \tag{19}
\]
Applying this to a minimizing cut orbit and then to (2) proves
\[
                 b(y)\ge\kappa(H)\ge1+\lceil\log_2 e\rceil
                  \quad(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\text{ nontorsion}).   \tag{20}
\]
The same lower bound holds for reciprocal radical outputs in the \(n=1\) case, by the projected-orbit lower bound and (15).

In particular primitive parent actions on at least five labels forbid every radical output with at most three exterior conjugates.
On four labels the bound is three, and the [primitive four-label construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v301.md#four-labels) attains it, with measure at most \(2\mu\), improved to \(\mu\) when \(\omega=1\).
On three labels, singleton cuts give \(\kappa(H)\le3\), so the exact minimum is three.

The group-theoretic inequality (19) is sharp for every odd integer \(r\ge3\).
Let
\[
       E=\{a\in\mathbb F_2^r:\sum_i a_i=0\},
                 \qquad H=E\rtimes S_r
\]
act affinely on the \(2^{r-1}\) even-weight words.
This action is primitive.
Indeed a block containing zero for a group containing all translations is an additive subgroup; invariance under the stabilizer \(S_r\) makes it an invariant subspace.
The even-weight \(S_r\)-module is irreducible when \(r\) is odd: every nonzero vector is nonconstant, since the all-ones vector has odd weight, and subtracting a transposition image with unequal selected coordinates gives a two-coordinate vector.
All such vectors are conjugate and span the even-weight space.
Therefore there are no proper nonzero invariant subspaces.
The \(r\) coordinate cuts are distinct and form one \(H\)-orbit.
They give \(\kappa(H)\le r\), while (19) gives the reverse inequality.
This example establishes sharpness for abstract transitive groups; no polynomial or shrinking-measure realization of these actions is claimed.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Consequence for bounded additive encodings

Apply the [small-height relation theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#sparse-output) to a faithful equation
\[
                          \sum_{i=1}^s c_i a_i y^{k_i}=0,
                \qquad s\le B,\quad c_i\in\mathbb Q^*,\quad
                       a_i\in\Gamma,\quad k_i\in\mathbb Z.        \tag{21}
\]
Every torsion element of \(\Gamma\) is a sign, by the [real projection argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection).
Thus the same-power collection and faithfulness convention in C345v300 apply with rational coefficients.
Under its sufficiently small relative-height hypothesis, that theorem puts every nonzero output in \(\widetilde\Gamma\).
Here no full symmetric parent action is required.
For nontorsion outputs, (2), (17) and (20) therefore apply verbatim.

For example, let \(a_i\) be Laurent monomials in the roots with exponent \(\ell^1\)-norm at most \(L\), and let \(|k_i|\le K\).
The sufficient sequence condition remains exactly
\[
                          L\mu/(2m)+K h(y)\longrightarrow0        \tag{22}
\]
for one fixed term cap \(B\), as in the [height budget](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#height-budget).
Along any hypothetical sequence satisfying (22), a nontorsion output with at most two exterior conjugates eventually forces an invariant bipartition of the input parent action.
That bipartition independently supplies a Salem witness with the cost (18).
For primitive actions with \(e\ge5\), even three exterior conjugates are excluded under (22).
The argument concerns the evaluated, faithful equation; an identity after input substitution does not count as an output constraint.

## Comparison, checks and the next boundary

The [full-permutation calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#saturation) gives the explicit minimum \(e\), except for three when \(e=4\), under its full symmetric block hypothesis.
The current cut minimum removes that action hypothesis and optimizes over every actual radical output.
For an invariant partition into at least three parts, the [imprimitive partial-product descent](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v301.md#statement) gives a sharper measure bound than (4) for the orbit of a part.
For a partition into two parts the bounds coincide.
Here the new necessity direction identifies exactly which inputs admit any output with at most two exterior conjugates.
The primitive cut-code estimate handles every primitive parent action, without classifying finite simple groups.

The [small-height relation result](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v300.md#real-circuits) previously combined additive encodings only with the full symmetric saturation theorem.
Section [Sparse encodings](#sparse) gives its actual extension to every parent group in the present circuit class.
The earlier [whole-block descent](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#descent) uses a nontorsion whole-block product; these whole-parent products are signs, and the operative objects are proper-subset cuts instead.
A scoped search of the retained C345 statements found no earlier cut-orbit optimization or primitive cut-code bound.
This is a comparison within the project, not a claim of exhaustive literature novelty.
The finite-group and coding arguments needed here are proved above, and no uninspected classification theorem is an input.

Author-side checking covered the common inversion sign, the pure inversion supplied by a neutral parent, exponent ambiguity modulo all parent constants, distinct logarithms versus distinct roots, clearing torsion before powering, and the exact orbit degree of squared partial products.
It also covered the difference between a vector pair and a projective line, the balanced-complement exception in (13), both subgroup cases in the reduction to subsets, the reference height after same-power collection, and the restricted reciprocal scope at \(n=1\).
The independent whole-proof review remains to be arranged by the coordinating agent.

The theorem constructs outputs from a supplied real circuit; it does not supply an input sequence whose measure tends to one.
For primitive groups with large \(e\), even the minimum exterior count may grow, and the general measure cost in (4) grows with it.
Independent exterior logarithms, noncircuit relation spaces, nonreal exterior signatures, dense additive moves and sparse moves outside (22) remain outside this obstruction.
The exact finite invariant provides a next executable classification problem for any supplied parent action, without resolving unrestricted G0001.
