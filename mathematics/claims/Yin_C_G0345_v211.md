# Individual prime-block gaps from the multiplicative order of the residue characteristic

<a id="statement"></a>
## A gap for effective delays with bounded order ratio

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\), and a prime \(\ell\) such that every \(p_i\ne\ell\) is congruent to \(1\) or \(-1\) modulo \(\ell\).
For positive integers \(u_i,n\) and either \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), put
\[
 A=\prod_i(X^{u_i}-p_i),\quad D=\sum_i u_i,\quad A^*=X^D A(1/X),
 \quad P=X^nA+\varepsilon A^*,\quad g=\gcd_i u_i .
\]
Set \(\Delta=u_j\) if \(p_j=\ell\), and \(\Delta=0\) otherwise.
Write \(N=n+\Delta=mM\), where \(M=\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) and \(\ell\nmid m\).
Let \(o=\operatorname{ord}_m(\ell)\), the multiplicative order of \(\ell\bmod m\), with \(o=1\) for \(m=1\).
Write \(s=\max(1,\omega(m))\), where \(\omega\) counts distinct prime divisors.
There are positive constants \(C_0,C_1,c_1\), depending only on the fixed primes \(p_i,\ell\), such that for \(n\ge C_0(D+1)^9\) every noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(S\) of \(P\) satisfies
\[
            \min\!\left(\frac{\deg S}{n},m(S)\right)
                  \ \ge\ c_1\,\frac{o}{m}\exp(-C_1s^2).               \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m(S)\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
In particular, bounding \(m/o\) suffices for a positive uniform individual-factor gap.
This applies when the prime divisors of \(m\) belong to any fixed finite set, with all exponents in \(N\), the block gcd \(g\), and the individual block exponents unrestricted within the delay range.
It includes \(N=\ell^a u p^b\) for a fixed prime \(p\ne\ell\) and bounded \(u\), even when both \(a\) and \(b\) grow.

## Ramification at a fixed fraction of the embeddings

Fix a root \(\alpha\) of \(S\) and put \(d=\deg S\), \(B=D+\Delta\).
The [allocation among exterior roots](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) gives an integer \(r\in[1,g]\) with \(|d/n-r/g|<1/(3(D+1)^2)\), hence \(d/n\asymp r/g\) and \(d\ge n/(2g)\).
The same result estimates \(m(S)=(r/g)\sum_i\log p_i+O((D+1)\exp(-cn/(D+1)^2))\).
Since \(g\le D\), increasing \(C_0\) makes the error less than \((2g)^{-1}\sum_i\log p_i\), so \(m(S)\asymp d/n\).
In particular \(\alpha\) is a nontorsion unit and its absolute logarithmic height satisfies \(h(\alpha)=m(S)/d\le C_2/n\).

Use the [reduction at \(\ell\)](Yin_C_G0345_v201.md#residue), with \(b=1\) for \(\ell=2\), and otherwise the sign congruent to \(-\varepsilon\prod_{p_i\ne\ell}(-p_i)\bmod\ell\).
Set \(H=bA+\varepsilon X^\Delta A^*\) and \(h=v_\ell(\operatorname{content} H)\).
The cited reduction gives \(1\le h\le\log_\ell(2\prod_i(1+p_i))\) and
\[
       F=X^\Delta P=X^NA+\varepsilon X^\Delta A^*,\qquad
                 \bar F=(X^m-b)^M\bar A .                             \tag{2}
\]
Use \(v(\ell)=1\) on \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\); bars denote reduction.
We show that at least \(3d/4\) of the conjugates \(x\) of \(\alpha\) in this closure satisfy
\[
           \bar x^m=\bar b,\qquad c_0M\le e_x\le 2M ,                  \tag{3}
\]
where \(e_x\) is the ramification index of \(K_x=\mathbb Q_\ell(x)\) and \(c_0>0\) depends only on the fixed primes.

For any such local field, put \(f_x=[k_x:\mathbb F_\ell]\), where \(k_x\) is its residue field.
The local degree is \(e_x f_x\le d\), and its valuation group is \(e_x^{-1}\mathbb Z\).
The multiplicity of \(\bar x\) in the reduction of any monic integral multiple of the local minimal polynomial of \(x\) is at least \(e_x\).
Indeed, the \(e_x f_x\) local conjugates reduce to conjugates of \(\bar x\), since local embeddings preserve the valuation.
These residues occur with equal multiplicity by Frobenius invariance of the reduced local minimal polynomial.
Their number is at most \(f_x\), so each occurs at least \(e_x\) times.
We apply this count to \(F\).

First suppose \(m\le64g(B+1)\).
Here \(m\ll (D+1)^2\) and \(h\) is bounded, so taking \(C_0\) sufficiently large gives \(M\ge2\ell^h(B+1)\) and \(mB+D\le d/4\), using \(M\ge n/m\) and \(d\ge n/(2g)\).
The [local valuation count](Yin_C_G0345_v201.md#local) bounds by \(mB+D\) the number of roots of \(F\), counted with multiplicity, outside
\[
                 0<v(x^m-b)\le (M/\ell^{h-1}-B)^{-1}.
\]
Every remaining conjugate has \(\bar x^m=\bar b\) and \(e_x\ge M/\ell^{h-1}-B\ge M/(2\ell^{h-1})\), because the valuation is positive in \(K_x\).
By (2) its residue has multiplicity at most \(M+D\le2M\), giving the other half of (3).

Now suppose \(m>64g(B+1)\).
The nonzero polynomials \(\bar A\) and \(\overline{H/\ell^h}\) have at most \(D+B\) distinct zeros in all.
Discard the residue classes at those zeros and every class outside \(X^m=\bar b\).
By (2), the total number of discarded roots of \(F\) is at most \((D+B)M+D\), since each exceptional class on \(X^m=\bar b\) costs \(M\) roots there, and the entire additional contribution of \(\bar A\) is \(D\).
This bound is at most \(3(B+1)M<d/4\), using \(mM=n+\Delta\le2n\) and \(d\ge n/(2g)\).
Each surviving conjugate has residue multiplicity \(M\) in (2), hence \(e_x\le M\).

For such an \(x\), choose the root \(\zeta\) of \(X^m=b\) with residue \(\bar x\), and put \(x=\zeta(1+y)\).
The [simple residue classes](Yin_C_G0345_v201.md#residue) give \(v(x^m-b)=v(y)>0\), finite because \(x\) is nontorsion.
Also \(v(A(x))=0\) and \(v(H(x))=h\).
Since \(\zeta^N=b\), the identity \(0=F(x)=H(x)+bA(x)((1+y)^N-1)\) implies \(v((1+y)^N-1)=h\).
We claim \(v(y)\le\ell^{h-1}/M\).
Indeed, suppose the opposite and write \(a=v_\ell(M)\).
For the term of index \(1\le i\le N\) in the binomial expansion, put \(k=a-v_\ell(i)\).
The identity \(i\binom Ni=N\binom{N-1}{i-1}\) gives coefficient valuation at least \(\max(0,k)\).
For \(k\ge h\) the term therefore has valuation greater than \(h\).
If \(0\le k<h\), then \(i v(y)>\ell^{h-1-k}\ge h-k\); if \(k<0\), then \(i v(y)>\ell^{h-1-k}\ge h\).
Thus every term would have valuation greater than \(h\), a contradiction.
We conclude \(e_x\ge M/\ell^{h-1}\) from \(v(x^m-b)=v(y)\).
This completes (3) in both ranges.

## Residue orders and a common exponent

For a conjugate satisfying (3), let \(t\) be the multiplicative order of \(\bar x\), and put \(t'=\gcd(t,m)\).
Then \(t\mid 2m\), \(t\mid2t'\), and \(t\mid\ell^{f_x}-1\).
Reduction from units modulo \(m\) to units modulo \(t'\) has kernel of order at most \(m/t'\), since at most that many classes reduce to one.
Applying this to the subgroup generated by \(\ell\) gives
\[
         \frac{o t'}{m}\le\operatorname{ord}_{t'}(\ell)
                    \le f_x\le \frac{d}{c_0 M},\qquad
            \frac{m}{t'}\ge Z:=\frac{c_0 M o}{d}.                      \tag{4}
\]
For \(Z\le1\) this already gives \(d\ge c_0 M o\ge c_0 n o/m\), as required.
Henceforth \(Z>1\), which forces \(m>1\).
Each integer \(m/t'\ge Z\) has some prime-power divisor \(p^{v_p(m/t')}\ge Z^{1/s}\), since at most \(s\) primes divide \(m\).
For each prime \(p\mid m\) with \(p^{v_p(m)}\ge Z^{1/s}\), let \(a_p\) be the smallest nonnegative integer with \(p^{a_p}\ge Z^{1/s}\).
For every conjugate satisfying (3) at least one such \(p\) has \(t'\mid m/p^{a_p}\), and then \(t\mid2m/p^{a_p}\).
Consequently for at least \(3d/(4s)\) conjugates satisfying (3) there is a single common integer \(J=2m/p^{a_p}\) with \(\bar x^J=1\).
For these conjugates \(v(x^J-1)\ge1/e_x\ge1/(2M)\).

For \(z\) of positive valuation the binomial expansion gives
\[
                  v((1+z)^\ell-1)\ge\min(1+v(z),\ell v(z)).
\]
Starting from \(v(z)\ge1/(2M)\), at most \(\lceil\log_\ell(2M)\rceil\) iterations suffice to reach \(1/(\ell-1)\), since the lower bound multiplies by \(\ell\) below that threshold.
Thereafter each iteration raises the lower bound by one.
Take \(E=\ell^{\lceil\log_\ell(2M)\rceil+4s}\le2M\ell^{4s+1}\).
The selected conjugates have \(v(x^{JE}-1)\ge4s\); all other conjugates have nonnegative valuation because \(\alpha\) is integral.
The rational integer \(\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^{JE}-1)\) is nonzero because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion, and its \(\ell\)-adic valuation is at least \(3d\).
At the complex embeddings, the triangle inequality bounds the same absolute norm by \(2^d\exp(JE\,m(S))\).
Combining the bounds gives
\[
       3\log\ell-\log2\le JE\, h(\alpha)
          \le \frac{4mM\ell^{4s+1}}{p^{a_p}}\frac{C_2}{n}
          \le \frac{8C_2\ell^{4s+1}}{p^{a_p}} .                        \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(Z^{1/s}\le p^{a_p}\le C_3\ell^{4s+1}\), proving \(Z\le\exp(C_4s^2)\) for a constant depending only on the fixed primes.
By (4), \(d/n\ge c_0(o/m)\exp(-C_4s^2)\), since \(mM\ge n\).
The comparison \(m(S)\asymp d/n\) proves (1).

## Bounded order ratio and fixed sets of prime divisors

Suppose first that \(m/o\le K\).
If \(r\ge1\) different odd primes divide \(m\), the order \(o\) divides the least common multiple of \(\varphi(p^w)\) over the exact prime powers \(p^w\Vert m\).
Each of the \(r\) odd-prime contributions is even, so their least common multiple, with the possible dyadic contribution included, is at most \(\varphi(m)/2^{r-1}\).
It follows that \(2^{r-1}\le m/o\), and hence \(s\le2+\log_2 K\), also when \(r=0\).
Equation (1) gives a positive lower bound depending only on \(K\) and the fixed primes.

For an explicit family, suppose all prime divisors of \(m\) lie in a fixed finite set \(\mathcal P\) avoiding \(\ell\).
For each odd \(p\in\mathcal P\) put \(b_p=v_p(\ell^{p-1}-1)\); if \(2\in\mathcal P\) put \(b_2=v_2(\ell^2-1)\).
For any \(p^w\Vert m\) we have \(p^{\max(0,w-b_p)}\mid o\).
Indeed take \(z=\ell^{p-1}\) for odd \(p\) and \(z=\ell^2\) for \(p=2\).
Then \(v_p(z-1)=b_p\ge1\), with \(b_2\ge3\), and \(z^o\equiv1\bmod p^w\).
For any unit \(u\equiv1\bmod p\), raising to an exponent coprime to \(p\) preserves \(v_p(u-1)\) by the geometric sum.
Raising to the \(p\)-th power increases this valuation by exactly one for odd \(p\), since \(1+u+\cdots+u^{p-1}\equiv p\bmod p^2\).
The same holds for \(p=2\) when \(u\equiv1\bmod4\).
Applying these facts to \(z\) gives \(w\le b_p+v_p(o)\), as asserted.
The resulting prime powers for distinct \(p\) are coprime, so their product divides \(o\).
Therefore \(m/o\le\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{b_p}\), proving the fixed-prime-set gap.

## Primitive delays with small orders at both eligible primes

Take the seeds \(2,3\) with \(u_1=u_2=g\ge2\).
The only eligible residue characteristics are \(2\) and \(3\), since any other eligible prime would divide both \(2^2-1\) and \(3^2-1\).
For both choices the effective delay is \(N=n+g\).
There are arbitrarily large prime values of \(N\), coprime to \(6g\), for which
\[
         \operatorname{ord}_N(2)\le (N-1)/g,\qquad
         \operatorname{ord}_N(3)\le (N-1)/g.                           \tag{6}
\]
In particular these examples have \(\gcd(n,g)=1\), and taking \(N\) large enough ensures the required ninth-power delay bound for each \(g\).

To obtain the primes, take an integral primitive element \(\theta\ne0\) of the number field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_g,2^{1/g},3^{1/g})\) and let \(f\) be its monic minimal polynomial.
Clearing the denominators in the three power-basis expressions puts \(\zeta_g,2^{1/g},3^{1/g}\) in \(\mathbb Z[\theta,1/T]\) for one integer \(T\ge1\).
Infinitely many primes divide the nonzero values of \(f\).
Indeed, if a finite list exhausted them and \(V\) were its product, then \(f(f(0)Vt)/f(0)\) would be an integer congruent to one modulo each prime on the list.
Here \(f(0)\ne0\), and for large integer \(t\) this quotient has absolute value greater than one, giving another prime divisor.
Choose such a prime \(N\nmid6gT\); a root of \(f\bmod N\) defines a homomorphism \(\mathbb Z[\theta,1/T]\to\mathbb F_N\).
The image of \(\zeta_g\) still has order \(g\), since it satisfies \(\Phi_g\) and \(X^g-1\) is separable in characteristic \(N\).
Thus \(g\mid N-1\), and the images of \(2^{1/g},3^{1/g}\) give \(g\)-th roots of \(2,3\) in \(\mathbb F_N^\times\).
Raising either element \(2,3\) to \((N-1)/g\) now gives one, proving (6).
Since \(m=N\) in both characteristics, the ratios \(m/\operatorname{ord}_m(\ell)\) both exceed \(g\).
The bounded-ratio criterion therefore leaves primitive examples with just one prime divisor of \(m\); their actual factor measures are undetermined by (6).
