# The full Galois group of the degree-2450 five-term polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducibility and independent inversions

For \(n=1225,\ k=37,\ l=n-k=1188\), the polynomial
\[
        P=X^{2450}-2X^{2413}+X^{1225}-2X^{37}+1
\]
is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The Galois group on its roots is the full group \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{1225}\rtimes S_{1225}\) permuting the 1225 inverse pairs and allowing each pair to be inverted independently.
In particular this instance has no proper nonconstant monic rational divisor.
The [degree-848 dyadic selection](Yin_C_G0345_v252.md#statement) is one of the rational lifts excluded by this factorization.
For any root \(\alpha\) and every positive integer \(j\), \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).

Write \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Y,\ C_{i+1}=YC_i-C_{i-1}\), and set \(W=C_{1225}-2C_{1188}+1\).
The identity \(C_i(z+z^{-1})=z^i+z^{-i}\) gives \(P=X^{1225}W(X+X^{-1})\), with \(W(2)=-1,\ W(-2)=-5\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## A two-prime irreducibility certificate

The finite-field factor degrees of \(W\) are as follows, with multiplicity one in every row.

| Prime | Degrees |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | \(1,3,62,226,434,499\) |
| 31 | \(10,282,933\) |
| 37 | \(1,17,22,81,187,337,580\) |
| 61 | \(11,57,257,900\) |

Every monic rational factor is integral and preserves its degree at both 31 and 61.
A proper rational factorization must have a factor of degree at most 612.
At 31 the only possible positive degrees in that range are \(10,282,292\).
At 61 they are \(11,57,68,257,268,314,325\).
The lists are disjoint, proving trace irreducibility.
For any root \(\beta\) of \(W\), the norm of \(\beta^2-4\) from \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) equals \(W(2)W(-2)=5\).
Thus \(\beta^2-4\) is not a square there, and \(X^2-\beta X+1\) is irreducible over that field.
Either of its roots generates a degree-2450 extension of \(\mathbb Q\), proving the asserted rational factorization of \(P\).

<a id="certificate"></a>
## Reproducing the finite-field arithmetic

For each row \(p\) and each positive divisor \(d\) of at least one of its listed degrees, the certificate checks
\[
      \deg\gcd(\bar W,Y^{p^d}-Y)
                     =\sum_{\substack{j\ {\rm listed}\\ j\mid d}}j .
\]
There are \(12,12,21,31\) gcd comparisons in ascending prime order.
Indeed \(Y^{p^d}-Y\) contains each monic irreducible of degree dividing \(d\) exactly once.
Induction on divisors determines the count in every listed degree, and their sum already exhausts \(\deg W\), proving the table and squarefreeness.
The following complete arithmetic check uses polynomial residues and gcds only.
It was run with Python-FLINT 0.9.0 and the rows were also checked by finite-field factorization.

```python
from flint import nmod_poly

rows = {
    5: [1, 3, 62, 226, 434, 499],
    31: [10, 282, 933],
    37: [1, 17, 22, 81, 187, 337, 580],
    61: [11, 57, 257, 900],
}
for p, degrees in rows.items():
    y = nmod_poly([0, 1], p)
    previous, current = nmod_poly([2], p), y
    for i in range(2, 1226):
        previous, current = current, y * current - previous
        if i == 1188:
            inner = current
    trace = current - 2 * inner + 1
    assert trace.degree() == sum(degrees)
    residue = y
    for d in range(1, max(degrees) + 1):
        residue = pow(residue, p, trace)
        if any(j % d == 0 for j in degrees):
            assert trace.gcd(residue - y).degree() == sum(
                j for j in degrees if d % j == 0
            )
```

## Permutations of the trace roots

Let \(K\) be the splitting field of \(W\).
At each prime in the table the reduced polynomial has distinct roots.
They lift by Hensel's lemma to roots in a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), and its Frobenius restricts on the roots of \(W\) to a permutation with the displayed cycle lengths.
This restriction is an element of the rational Galois group, since it permutes all the roots and fixes \(\mathbb Q\).

Trace irreducibility makes the group transitive.
A nontrivial invariant partition into equal blocks would have both its block size and its number of blocks at most 245, since both are proper factors of \(1225=5^2 7^2\).
All permutations preserving that partition form a group whose order divides a product of factorials of integers at most 245, by the action on the blocks and the permutations inside them.
The permutation at 37 has order divisible by the prime 337.
It rules out such a partition, so the group is primitive.
At 5, only the cycle of length three has length divisible by three.
Raising the permutation to the least common multiple of all its other cycle lengths therefore gives a single three-cycle.

For completeness, the supports of all conjugates of this three-cycle form a hypergraph on the trace roots.
The connected components are permuted by the group, and primitivity forces the hypergraph to be connected since it has an edge.
The cycles on the edges of a connected hypergraph of triples generate the alternating group on its vertices.
To see this, start with one triple and adjoin edges meeting the constructed set \(U\), for which the generated group contains \(A_U\).
If an edge meets \(U\) in two vertices, conjugation by \(A_U\) and taking inverses supplies every three-cycle containing its new vertex and two elements of \(U\), since \(A_U\) is transitive on unordered pairs for \(|U|\ge3\).
These cycles and \(A_U\) generate the full alternating group on the enlarged set.
If an edge \(\{a,b,c\}\) meets \(U\) only at \(a\), conjugation first supplies \((u\,b\,c)\) for some other \(u\in U\).
The product \((a\,b\,c)(u\,b\,c)^{-1}\) is a three-cycle on \(\{a,b,u\}\), so one may adjoin \(b\) and then \(c\) by the two-vertex step.
Thus the trace group contains \(A_{1225}\).
The Frobenius at 61 has four cycles on an odd number of vertices, hence is odd.
This proves \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)=S_{1225}\).

## An endpoint prime giving every independent inversion

More generally let \(W\) be monic integral and irreducible, let \(K\) denote its splitting field with roots \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m\), and suppose an odd prime \(p\) has squarefree reduction of \(W\) and \(v_p(W(2)W(-2))=1\).
Then the splitting group of \(X^mW(X+X^{-1})\) above \(K\) is the full group of \(m\) independent inversions.
Indeed \(K\) is unramified at \(p\) by the same simple-root lifting.
At any prime over \(p\), the integral numbers \(\delta_i=\beta_i^2-4\) have valuations adding to one.
Precisely one has valuation one, and all others have valuation zero.
Transitivity on the \(\beta_i\) supplies a prime giving this pattern at any prescribed index \(i\).
After adjoining square roots of any subset of the other \(\delta_j\), the extension is still unramified at the chosen prime.
Locally, a square root of a unit at odd residue characteristic lies in an unramified extension by lifting a nonzero residue square root.
The valuation of \(\delta_i\) stays odd there.
Adjoining its square root therefore doubles the degree, in any order, giving total degree \(2^m\).
The two roots of each \(X^2-\beta_i X+1\) are \((\beta_i\pm\sqrt{\delta_i})/2\).
Consequently the action on the inverse pairs realizes the full preimage of the trace group inside the signed permutation group, by its order and its surjection onto the trace group.

Here \(p=5\) meets both assumptions, so the two group calculations give the stated semidirect product.
The real root of \(P\) beyond one exists by \(P(1)=-1\) and monicity.
Irreducibility then makes every root nontorsion.
If positive powers of roots from different inverse pairs were equal, an automorphism inverting only the first pair would imply that the first root was torsion.
Within one inverse pair the same equality is also impossible.
Thus the \(2450\) conjugates of a powered root stay distinct, proving the root-field conclusion for every positive power.
