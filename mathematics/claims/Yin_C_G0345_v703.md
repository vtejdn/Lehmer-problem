# Counting Salem polynomials in a coefficient congruence class and exceptional integer seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## The count with prescribed coefficient residues

Let \(d=2h\ge4\), \(B\ge1\), and \(M\ge1\) be an integer modulus.
Fix any residues \(c_1,\ldots,c_h\bmod M\).
Count distinct Salem numbers \(1<\beta\le B\) of degree \(d\) whose monic minimal polynomial has coefficient \(c_j\bmod M\) at \(X^{d-j}\) for \(1\le j\le h\).
Their number is at most
\[
               \exp\!\left(C\frac{B}{M}\sqrt{d\log(2dB)}\right)       \tag{1}
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\).
In particular there is at most one such number in each class when \(M>C' B\sqrt{d\log(2dB)}\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C'\).

At \(M=1\), (1) counts the full interval \(1<\beta\le B\).
For fixed \(B>1\) this gives \(\exp(O_B(\sqrt{d\log d}))\) at degree \(d\), and a subexponential bound in \(D\) through degree \(D\) by summation.
Consecutive power sums locate the real conjugate contribution across the full interval, extending the [count in a half-degree power window](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#statement).

<a id="seeds"></a>
## A uniform bound on exceptional seeds over all delays

For integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and a sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r=r_{a,n,\epsilon}>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Let \(R_{a,n,\epsilon}\) be the largest positive integer \(l\) such that \(r^{1/l}\in\mathbb Q(r)\) and set
\[
              H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}
                          \frac{a+j}{\operatorname{rad}(a+j)},
\]
where \(\operatorname{rad}\) is the product of distinct prime factors.
There is an absolute constant \(C_1\) such that for all \(X\ge3\),
\[
 \#\{(a,n,\epsilon,p):3\le a\le X,\ n\ge7,\ p>n\text{ prime},
                              \ r^{1/p}\in\mathbb Q(r)\}
                  \le \exp\!\big(C_1\sqrt{\log(X+1)}\big).             \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the same bound holds for the number of distinct \(a\le X\), \(a\ge3\), for which \(R_{a,n,\epsilon}\nmid H(a)\) at some delay and sign.
This improves the [previous all-delay exceptional count](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#statement) and also bounds the number of choices of delay, sign and large prime attached to the seeds.

<a id="encoding"></a>
## Recovering a Salem polynomial from errors in consecutive power sums

Write \(1,a_1,\ldots,a_d\) for the descending coefficients of the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Its full \(j\)-th power sum is the integer \(s_j=\beta^j+\beta^{-j}+u_j\), where \(u_j\) is the sum over all \(d-2\) neutral conjugates.
The [discriminant estimate for these sums](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#energy) gives
\[
                     \sum_{j=1}^h |u_j|^2/j\ll d\log(2dB).            \tag{3}
\]
Newton's identity at \(j\le h\) reads
\[
                      s_j=-j a_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i s_{j-i}.
\]
Once the earlier sums and coefficients are known, \(a_j\equiv c_j\bmod M\) specifies \(s_j\) modulo \(jM\).

Predict \(s_1\) by \(y_1=0\).
For \(j\ge2\), let \(x_{j-1}\) be the nearest point of the interval \([1,B^{j-1}]\) to \(s_{j-1}\), and put \(y_j=x_{j-1}^{j/(j-1)}\).
In its known residue class modulo \(jM\), let \(b_j\) be the integer nearest to \(y_j\), choosing the lesser integer at a tie.
Store the signed integer \(z_j=(s_j-b_j)/(jM)\).
Decoding in increasing order recovers \(s_j=b_j+jMz_j\) and then \(a_j\) from Newton's identity.
Reciprocity determines all remaining coefficients from this first half, and the polynomial has a unique root outside the unit circle.
The code \(z_1,\ldots,z_h\) is therefore injective for fixed \(d,B,M\) and residue data.

Since \(s_j\) itself belongs to the known class, \(|b_j-y_j|\le|s_j-y_j|\), and hence
\[
                       jM|z_j|\le2|s_j-y_j|.
\]
At \(j=1\), \(|s_1|\le B+1+|u_1|\).
At \(j\ge2\), projection onto \([1,B^{j-1}]\) does not increase the distance to \(\beta^{j-1}\), which belongs to that interval.
The derivative of \(x^{j/(j-1)}\) there is at most \(jB/(j-1)\le2B\).
It follows that
\[
                    |s_j-y_j|\le 1+|u_j|+2B(1+|u_{j-1}|).
\]
Squaring, summing with weight \(1/j\), and reindexing the shifted terms using \(1/j\le1/(j-1)\) give
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^h j|z_j|
    \le\sum_{j=1}^h jz_j^2
    \le \frac4{M^2}\sum_{j=1}^h\frac{|s_j-y_j|^2}{j}
    \ll\frac{B^2}{M^2}\,d\log(2dB).
\]
Here \(B\ge1\), the harmonic sum is \(O(\log(2d))\), and (3) bounds the neutral terms, including \(u_1\).
For any \(E>0\), the [signed partition estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#count) gives at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt E))\) integer vectors with \(\sum_j j|z_j|\le E\).
This proves (1), including the uniqueness consequence by making its right side smaller than two.

<a id="application"></a>
## Counting large prime field radicals with a fixed residue polynomial

For a tuple in (2), put \(Z=\log(X+1)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), \(\beta=r^{1/p}\) and let \(S\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Then \(\beta\) is a Salem number generating \(K\).
The [cofactor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(a-1<r<a+1\) and the minimal polynomial \(P_a=G_a/U\), with \(U\) the product of the factors at \(1,-1\) satisfying \(x^{n-1}=\epsilon\).
Thus \(d=\deg S=\deg P_a\le n+1\le p\).
Fix \(n,p,\epsilon\) and \(a\bmod p\).
These data determine \(P_a\bmod p\) because \(U\) is monic and depends only on \(n,\epsilon\).
The \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\beta\) are the conjugates of \(r\), each once by equality of fields.
Therefore \(S\equiv P_a\bmod p\) by Frobenius on the multiset of reduced roots, as in the [prime-power reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#frobenius).
In particular all the residues required in (1) are known for \(M=p\).

By the [large-prime localization](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#statement), \(\beta\le B=\min(5p,e^{Z/p})\), where the second bound uses \(r<X+1\).
The [degree and measure estimate for such radicals](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#count) gives \(p\ll Z\log^3(2Z)\), using only \(d\le p\), \(p\log\beta=\log r<Z\) and the lower logarithmic Mahler measure estimate in that argument.
For \(p\log p\le2Z\), one has \(B/p\le5\) and \(d\log(2dB)\ll p\log p\ll Z\).
For \(p\log p>2Z\), one has \(B\le\sqrt p\), so
\[
           \frac Bp\sqrt{d\log(2dB)}
                      \ll\sqrt{\log(2p)}\ll\sqrt Z .
\]
The congruence count (1) therefore gives \(\exp(O(\sqrt Z))\) choices of \(\beta\) for the fixed data in either range.
Once \(\beta\) is specified the seed is unique, since
\[
                a=\frac{r^{n+1}-\epsilon}{r^n-\epsilon r},
                \qquad r=\beta^p>1 .
\]
The denominator is positive.
The choices of sign, prime \(p\ll Z\log^3(2Z)\), delay \(7\le n<p\), and residue \(a\bmod p\) together contribute only a polynomial factor in \(1+Z\), absorbed by \(\exp(O(\sqrt Z))\).
This proves (2).
The [valuation bounds through the delay](Yin_C_G0345_v686.md#diagonal) imply that every failure of \(R_{a,n,\epsilon}\mid H(a)\) entails a prime field radical with \(p>n\), proving the assertion for distinct exceptional seeds.
