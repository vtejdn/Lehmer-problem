# Steep sections avoiding prescribed small primes and residue orders

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous exclusion

Write \(P_{k,l}=F(X^k,X^l)=X^{2k+l}Q_{k,l}(X+X^{-1})\) for the [primitive two-pole sections](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) and \(\Delta_{k,l}=|\operatorname{disc}Q_{k,l}|\).
There are primitive directions with \(k\to\infty\), \(l/k\to\infty\) and an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
 v_p(\Delta_{k,l})=v_p(\Delta_{1,5})\quad(p\le c\log l).
\]
The [template discriminant](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#finite) is squarefree, so every prime divisor of the largest square dividing \(\Delta_{k,l}\), and hence of the certificate \(U\) where defined, exceeds \(c\log l\).
Moreover, on these same sections every contact between complementary rational factors has residue order exceeding \(c\sqrt{\log l}\), after decreasing \(c\) if necessary.
These are exact exclusions along a constructed sequence, complementing the [bounds on the mass of contacts](Yin_C_G0345_v825.md#statement) on all steep sequences.

<a id="period"></a>
## A finite period for repeated trace roots

Fix \(Y\ge5\) and set \(D=\operatorname{lcm}_{p\le Y,\ 1\le j\le16}p^4(p^j-1)\), taking rational primes \(p\).
For any primitive positive direction \((k,l)\equiv(1,5)\pmod D\), a multiple geometric root \(t=x+x^{-1}\) of either reduced trace polynomial gives a multiple root \(x\) of its section, including when \(x=x^{-1}\).
The [uniform gcd bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) puts \(x\) in \(\mathbb F_{p^j}\) for some \(j\le16\), since all its Frobenius conjugates belong to that gcd.
Here and throughout this paragraph \(p\le Y\).
For any lift \(t_0\) of \(t\) to the unramified degree-\(j\) ring of integers \(O\) over \(\mathbb Z_p\), the two trace polynomials agree at \(t_0+u\) in \(R=(O/p^2)[u]/(u^3)\).
Indeed the matrix \(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t_0+u&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has eigenvalues \(x,x^{-1}\) modulo \(I=(p,u)\), so \(A^{p(p^j-1)}\equiv1\pmod I\), including a possible Jordan block.
Raising a matrix congruent to the identity modulo \(I^a\) to the \(p\)-th power gives the identity modulo \(I^{a+1}\) for \(a\ge1\).
Since \(I^4=0\), the order of \(A\) divides \(D\).
Writing \(C_n=\operatorname{tr}(A^n)\), direct expansion gives the trace value
\[
 10C_l+C_{2k+l}+C_{2k-l}-7C_{k+l}+7C_{k-l}+1.
\]
This proves equality of the constant and first two Taylor coefficients modulo \(p^2\), with no division by two.
It follows in both directions that the sets of multiple geometric trace roots coincide.

At primes outside the template discriminant both trace reductions are therefore separable.
In particular their separability modulo two implies separability in characteristic zero.
At each prime dividing the template discriminant, its valuation one bounds the degree of the gcd of the reduced template and its derivative by one, by the nullity of the reduced resultant matrix and its integral determinant.
These primes are odd, so the unique multiple root is rational and double.
The Taylor comparison gives the same multiplicity for the new section.
For either polynomial, lift the factorization at its unique double root by Hensel's lemma and translate by an integral lift \(t_0\) of that root.
The quadratic factor becomes \(u^2+au+b\) with \(p\mid a,b\), while the complementary factor has unit value at zero and unit discriminant.
Thus the full discriminant has valuation one exactly when the value at \(t_0\) has valuation one, by \(a^2-4b\) and the unit cross-resultant.
The constant-term comparison transfers this condition from the template.

<a id="order"></a>
## Removing small orders at arbitrarily large primes

Set \(H=\lfloor\sqrt Y\rfloor\), and take \(M\) to be \(D\) times \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,H)\) times the product of \(|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_g,P_{1,5})|\) for \(1\le g\le H\).
All these resultants are nonzero because the [irreducible template](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#finite) has roots off the circle.
Each logarithm is at most \(\varphi(g)\log\|P_{1,5}\|_1\), by evaluation at complex primitive roots of unity with the coefficient sum norm.
Chebyshev's estimate \(\sum_{p\le Y}\log p=O(Y)\) gives \(\log D=O(Y)\) directly from its defining product bound, so \(\log M=O(Y+H^2)=O(Y)\).
Take \(k=1+M\) and \(l=5+kM\).
Since \(5\mid M\), these directions are primitive, with \(k,l/k\to\infty\) and \(\log l=O(Y)\).

For \(Y\) greater than every prime divisor of the nonzero template section discriminant, consider a residue root \(x\) of order \(g\le H\) at \(p>Y\).
Since \(g\mid M\), evaluating at \(x\) gives the same value for both sections, so \(p\) divides the \(g\)-th template resultant and hence \(M\).
The logarithmic section derivatives at \(x\) also agree, because their coefficients depend on the exponents modulo \(p\) and their monomials on the exponents modulo \(g\).
The template is separable at \(p>Y\), so this root is simple and cannot be shared by complementary factors.
At \(p\le Y\), every complementary rational splitting on sufficiently late steep sections is reciprocal and its absolute resultant divides the largest square dividing the trace discriminant by the [trace product formula](Yin_C_G0345_v827.md#resultant).
The previous exclusion therefore rules out all such contacts there as well.
