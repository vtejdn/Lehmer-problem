# Resolving the prescribed Salem exponent at a deep prime

<a id="window"></a>
## Earlier powers are separated before the required depth

Suppose that the [shrinking adjacent strict records](Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement) are both Salem, with earlier value \(A\), later value \(B\), earlier degree \(D=[\mathbb Q(A):\mathbb Q]\), and \(m=\log A\).
Write \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), \(F=K(B)\), \(n=[F:K]\), and take \(a=\lfloor(\log2-\log B)/m\rfloor-1\).
Set \(z=2-A^aB\) and use \(v_p(p)=1\).
If a fixed finite prime set carries positive normalized norm mass along a subsequence, the [simultaneous depth and degree bound](Yin_C_G0901_v208.md#records) retains positive mass there at places \(Q\) with \(t=v_p(z)\gg n/\log n\) and \([F_Q:K_{\mathfrak p}]\ll\log n\), where \(\mathfrak p\) lies under \(Q\).
All following assertions about these places are uniform on the retained set.
The [relative adjunction estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) gives \(n\ge d_B/(\log(3d_B))^{O(1)}\) up to an absolute constant, where \(d_B=[\mathbb Q(B):\mathbb Q]>D\).
In particular \(t/D^{1-\gamma}\to\infty\) for every fixed \(\gamma>0\).

For every pair of different integers \(i,j\) in the whole archimedean window \(0\le i,j\le(\log2)/m\),
\[
                  v_p(A^i-A^j)\log p\ll\sqrt{D\log(2D)} .
\]
Consequently the congruence to \(2/B\) already distinguishes the prescribed \(a\) from all other integers in that window at depth \(C\sqrt{D\log(2D)}/\log p\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
The equality \(v_p(2/B-A^a)=t\) must then persist to the greater depth \(t\).
Indeed \(A\) is a unit, and for \(k=|i-j|>0\) the nonzero integer norm and the [cyclic norm inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) give, for every integer \(\ell\ge2\),
\[
 [K_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb Q_p]\,v_p(A^k-1)\log p
 \le \log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(A^k-1)|
 \le \frac{D\log\ell}{\ell-1}+\frac{\ell+1}{3}km .
\]
Take \(\ell=\lceil\sqrt{D\log(2D)}\rceil\) and use \(km\le\log2\).

<a id="order"></a>
## The multiplicative order at the precision of the translate

Reduce units modulo \(\mathfrak q^{v_Q(z)}\) in \(\mathcal O_{F,Q}\), where \(v_Q\) is the integer valuation and \(\mathfrak q\) the maximal ideal of the local ring.
The two units \(Y=A^a\) and \(2/B\) have the same reduction there, since \(p\ne2\) at a place dividing \(z\).
For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1\) their common order \(o_Q\) exceeds \(D^{1-\epsilon}\) on all sufficiently late retained places.
In fact the argument for the window with \(k=aj\), \(1\le j\le D^{1-\epsilon}\), and \(\ell=\lceil D^{\epsilon/2}\rceil\) gives
\[
             v_p(Y^j-1)\log p\ll_\epsilon D^{1-\epsilon/2}\log(2D)=o(t).
\]
Here \(am<\log2\) is what bounds the height cost uniformly over the tested orders.

There is an exponential order requirement when the residue field of \(K_{\mathfrak p}\) has at most \(D^{1-\epsilon}\) elements.
In that case
\[
                           v_p(o_Q)=(1+o(1))t .
\]
To see this, let \(e,f\) be the absolute ramification index and residue degree of \(K_{\mathfrak p}\).
Set \(k=(p^f-1)p^{\lceil\log_p(2e)\rceil}\).
The reduction of \(Y^{p^f-1}\) is one, and its difference from one has valuation at least \(1/e\).
For \(v_p(x)>0\), the binomial formula gives \(v_p((1+x)^p-1)\ge\min(pv_p(x),1+v_p(x))\).
Iteration up to the chosen exponent yields \(v_0=v_p(Y^k-1)>1/(p-1)\).
The quantity is finite since \(Y>1\) at the real embedding.
Apply the cyclic norm bound to \(Y^k=A^{ak}\) with \(\ell=\lceil D^{\epsilon/2}\rceil\) and divide by \(ef\log p\).
Since \(k\le2pe p^f\), \(am\le\log2\), \(p^f\le D^{1-\epsilon}\) and the prime set is fixed, this gives \(v_0\ll_{S,\epsilon} D^{1-\epsilon/2}\log(2D)=o(t)\) even for growing ramification.
For every positive integer \(b\) one now has \(v_p(Y^{kb}-1)=v_0+v_p(b)\).
Indeed a power prime to \(p\) preserves this valuation and the linear term in the \(p\)-th power has strictly smaller valuation than all the other terms above the stated threshold.
Using \(b=o_Q\) gives \(v_p(o_Q)\ge t-v_0\).
Conversely \(o_Q\mid k p^{\lceil t-v_0\rceil}\) once \(t>v_0\).
As \(v_p(k)=O(\log(2D))=o(t)\), the assertion follows.
The archimedean exponent budget and these local order requirements leave open the mass at a single eligible exponent above a fixed prime.

<a id="targets"></a>
## Separated translates at powers of a common integer

On the same rows, fix an integer \(T\ge2\), put \(b=\log B\), and use \(a_j=\lfloor(j\log T-b)/m\rfloor-1\) and \(z_j=T^j-A^{a_j}B\) for \(j\ge1\).
For every fixed distinct \(j,k\), the common finite ideal divisors of \(z_j,z_k\) carry \(o(1)\) normalized log norm.
This statement concerns the same prime ideals in \(F\), and allows different places over one rational prime to carry the two masses.

More precisely set \(h_0=h(A)+h(B)\).
At a prime dividing either translate, \(T\) is a unit.
In the quotient by the ideal gcd of the two translates, raising the two congruences to powers \(k,j\) yields
\[
                A^{ka_j-ja_k}B^{k-j}=1 .
\]
Call the unit on the left \(w\).
It is nontorsion since \(k\ne j\), the distinguished values are positive, and \(B^{|k-j|}\in K\) would contradict \(n>1\): a positive power of a strictly dominant \(B\) generates \(\mathbb Q(B)\). [Field of a power](Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits)
Since \(0<b<m\), one has \(|a_j-j\log T/m|<3\), hence \(|ka_j-ja_k|<3(j+k)\) and \(h(w)\le 3(j+k)h_0\).
The common normalized log norm is bounded by the cyclic norm estimate for \(w-1\).
Uniformly for \(j,k\le J\), \(j\ne k\), it is \(O(\sqrt{Jh_0\log(1/(Jh_0))})\) when \(Jh_0\to0\).
If \(J^3 h_0\log(1/h_0)\to0\), giving each finite prime to a translate of largest valuation there discards \(o(1)\) normalized log norm in each translate, since each loss is at most the sum of its other \(J-1\) pairwise overlaps.
At fixed \(j\), the total normalized norm of \(z_j\) tends to \(j\log T\) by the [second-exponent calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v165.md#statement).
No uniform fixed-target asymptotic in growing \(j\) is required for the loss bound.
