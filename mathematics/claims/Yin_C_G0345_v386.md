# A sharp winding threshold for the number of exterior pairs

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior pairs for all primitive slopes greater than one

For coprime positive integers \(a,b\) with \(r=b/a>1\), put
\[
 g(T)=T^3-T-1,\qquad B(T)=1-T^2-T^3,\qquad
 A(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a),\qquad f=-g/B.
\]
Let \(K(a,b)\) count complex-conjugate pairs of roots of \(A\) in \(|X|>1\), with multiplicities.
All exterior roots are nonreal.
Let \(c\) be the unique positive root of
\[
                 23c^4-322c^3-81c^2-44c-8=0,
                 \qquad c=14.256558\ldots .
\]
For \(r>c\), \(A\) has exactly \(b-a\) roots on the unit circle, all simple, and \(K(a,b)=a\).
The number \(c\) is irrational, so equality \(r=c\) cannot occur.

For \(1<r<c\), take the continuous lift \(\phi(\theta)=\arg f(e^{i\theta})\) with \(\phi(0)=\pi\), and put
\[
 F(\theta)=\frac{b\theta-a\phi(\theta)}{2\pi},\qquad
 W(\theta)=V(2\cos\theta),\qquad
 V(s)=\frac{1-2s-s^2}{5+4s-s^2-s^3}.
\]
There are exactly two solutions \(0<\theta_1<\theta_2<\pi\) of \(W(\theta)=r\).
Then
\[
 K(a,b)=a-\#\bigl(\mathbb Z\cap[F(\theta_2),F(\theta_1)]\bigr).       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, writing \(u_+=\max(u,0)\), one has
\[
 \left|\frac{K(a,b)}a-1+
     \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(W(\theta)-r)_+\,d\theta\right|
     \le \frac1a .                                                     \tag{2}
\]
Every fixed limiting slope in \((1,c)\) has a positive limiting deficit proportion \(1-K(a,b)/a\) along primitive directions of growing denominator.
Thus \(c\) is the infimum of all slopes above which every primitive direction has \(K(a,b)=a\).
These counts concern the total across all rational factors.

<a id="speed"></a>
## The maximum angular speed

On the unit circle \(B(t)=t^3g(t^{-1})\) and \(g,B\) have no zeros there.
The [powered circle equation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#circle) gives \(|f|=1\) and \(\phi(\theta+2\pi)=\phi(\theta)+2\pi\).
Differentiating the phase gives \(\phi'(\theta)=t f'(t)/f(t)\) at \(t=e^{i\theta}\).
Using \(s=t+t^{-1}\), expansion gives \(\phi'=V(s)\), with denominator \(D(s)=5+4s-s^2-s^3=|g(t)|^2>0\).
In particular \(V(-2)=1\) and \(V(2)=-7\).
Its derivative is \(-H(s)/D(s)^2\), where
\[
 H(s)=s^4+4s^3+3s^2+8s+14,\qquad
 H'(s)=4s^2(s+3)+6s+8 .
\]
On \([-2,-1]\) the first term of \(H'\) is at least four and \(6s+8\ge-4\), with no simultaneous equality.
On \([-1,2]\) the first term is nonnegative and \(6s+8\ge2\).
Since \(H(-2)=-6\) and \(H(2)=90\), the unique zero \(s_*\in(-2,2)\) is simple and \(V\) has a unique, nondegenerate maximum there.
The upper-half maximum of \(W\) occurs at \(\theta_*=\arccos(s_*/2)\).

At \(v=V(s_*)\), the cubic
\[
 v s^3+(v-1)s^2-(4v+2)s+1-5v
\]
has a repeated root at \(s_*\).
Its discriminant in \(s\) expands to \(-23v^4+322v^3+81v^2+44v+8\).
The maximum exceeds \(V(-2)=1\), and the sign changes in the quartic for \(c\) give exactly one positive root by Descartes' rule and the signs at \(14,15\).
Thus the maximum is \(c\in(14,15)\).
A positive rational root in lowest terms would have numerator dividing eight, which excludes this interval.

<a id="count"></a>
## Oriented crossings and double roots at the turning levels

The [power correspondence](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#projection) sends a root \(x\) of \(A\) to \(t=x^a\) satisfying \(t^b=f(t)^a\).
It is bijective, preserves multiplicity, conjugation and the location relative to the circle, and sends circle roots to the integer levels of \(F\) modulo one turn.
There is no real \(t\) with \(|t|>1\) satisfying the powered equation.
For \(t>1\) the quantities \(-B(t)-g(t)=t^2+t\) and \(-B(t)+g(t)=(t-1)(2t^2+3t+2)\) are positive, giving \(|f(t)|<1\).
For \(t=-u\), \(u>1\), one has
\[
  f(-u)=\frac{u^3-u+1}{u^3-u^2+1},\qquad
  u-f(-u)=\frac{(u-1)^3(u+1)}{u^3-u^2+1}>0 .
\]
The numerator of \(f(-u)\) exceeds its positive denominator, so \(1<f(-u)<u<u^r\).
These inequalities exclude real exterior roots of \(A\).
Reciprocity of \(A\), of degree \(3a+b\), therefore gives \(4K(a,b)=3a+b-N_\circ\), where \(N_\circ\) counts circle roots with multiplicity.

The lift \(F\) gains \(b-a\) in one turn and \(F'=a(r-W)/(2\pi)\).
Complex conjugation and the winding of \(\phi\) give \(\phi(2\pi-\theta)=4\pi-\phi(\theta)\), so \(F(2\pi-\theta)=b-2a-F(\theta)\).
At \(\theta=0,\pi\) the values are \(-a/2,\ b/2-a\), respectively, and the slope is positive at both.
An integer level there is a single positive crossing on the circle.
In the original variable \(A(1)=-2\), and \(x=-1\) occurs exactly for opposite parity, corresponding to \(t=1\) for even \(a\) and \(t=-1\) for even \(b\).

The sum of the signed crossings at the integer levels over one turn is \(b-a\).
Indeed one can begin at a noninteger level and follow \(\lfloor F\rfloor\) around the circle, with a net change of one for each upward crossing, minus one for each downward crossing and zero at a tangency.
If \(r>c\), the derivative is positive everywhere, giving \(N_\circ=b-a\) simple roots.
For \(1<r<c\), \(F\) decreases on \((\theta_1,\theta_2)\) and on its reflected interval in the other half-circle, and increases off the closures of these intervals.
Both critical points in each half-circle are nondegenerate since \(W'\ne0\) there.
At an integer turning level the root has multiplicity two, as \(e^{2\pi iF}-1\) has a zero of order two in the local circle parameter and differs from the powered equation by a nonvanishing factor.
A downward crossing contributes two more to the multiplicity count than to the signed count, and so does a turning point at an integer level.
For each decreasing interval these contributions are indexed by the integers in its **closed** range, once per integer.
Reflection preserves their count because \(b-2a\) is an integer.
Consequently
\[
 N_\circ=(b-a)+4\,\#\bigl(\mathbb Z\cap[F(\theta_2),F(\theta_1)]\bigr),
\]
which proves (1), including either or both turning levels when integral.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## The deficit near the threshold and its sharpness

The number of integers in a closed interval differs from its length by at most one.
The interval in (1) has length
\[
  F(\theta_1)-F(\theta_2)=
  \frac{a}{2\pi}\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}(W(\theta)-r)\,d\theta
  =\frac{a}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(W(\theta)-r)_+\,d\theta .
\]
This proves the uniform bound (2).
For \(\delta=c-r\downarrow0\) and \(\lambda=-W''(\theta_*)>0\), it gives
\[
 a-K(a,b)=\frac{2}{3\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\,a\delta^{3/2}
                         +O(a\delta^2+1).
\]
Indeed on the upper half-circle \(W(\theta_*+u)=c-\lambda u^2/2+O(|u|^3)\), and its positive excess over \(r\) is supported on \(|u|=O(\sqrt\delta)\).
There the integral of the positive part differs by \(O(\delta^2)\) from that of \((\delta-\lambda u^2/2)_+\), which is \((4/3)\sqrt{2/\lambda}\,\delta^{3/2}\).
The reflected maximum contributes equally.
The displayed leading term is an asymptotic equivalent when \(a\delta^{3/2}\to\infty\).

For any \(r_0\in(1,c)\), continuity in \(r\) of the integral in (2) follows from the pointwise Lipschitz bound for the positive part.
At \(r_0\) the integrand is positive on a neighborhood of \(\theta_*\), giving a positive limiting deficit proportion whenever \(a\to\infty,\ b/a\to r_0\).
Such primitive sequences exist by taking prime denominators \(a\) and one of the consecutive integers \(\lfloor ar_0\rfloor,\lfloor ar_0\rfloor+1\) coprime to \(a\).
Thus each proposed lower threshold fails along a primitive sequence with limiting slope strictly between it and \(c\).
