# Height cost for positive-constant Pisot seeds near a cyclotomic product

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree threshold for bounded-height perturbations

Let \(C\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) be a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials of degree \(k\ge2\), with \(C(0)=-1\).
Multiplicities and the number of factors are unrestricted.
Let \(q\ge2\) be an integer and \(U\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) satisfy \(U(0)=0\), \(\deg U<k\).
Suppose
\[
                              f(Z)=(Z-q)C(Z)+U(Z)                     \tag{1}
\]
is the minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\).
Its constant coefficient is \(q\).
Put \(\delta=\theta-q\), \(D=k+2\), and let \(H\ge1\) be the maximum absolute coefficient of \(U\).
Then
\[
 b_D=2^{-10}\left(\frac{\log\log D}{\log D}\right)^6,\qquad
 \delta>b_D,\qquad
                         H>b_D q(1-1/q)^{k+1}.                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular, on any such sequence with \(k\to\infty\) and \(H=O(k^a)\), for fixed \(0\le a<1\),
\[
                         \limsup\frac{q\log k}{k}\le\frac1{1-a}.       \tag{3}
\]
Bounded coefficient height gives the constant one on the right.
For every fixed \(c>0\), inputs with \(q\ge ck\) instead satisfy
\[
                  H\gg_c q
                         \left(\frac{\log\log(k+2)}{\log(k+2)}\right)^6.
                                                                         \tag{4}
\]
The perturbation may be dense, and its coefficients and the coefficients of \(C\) may vary with \(k,q\).

For an exact nonempty example, take \(q=2\), \(C=Z^3-1\), and \(U=-2Z^2+Z\).
The resulting \(f=Z^4-2Z^3-2Z^2+2\) is Eisenstein at two.
Its derivative is \(2Z(2Z+1)(Z-2)\).
The critical values show that it has just two real roots, lying respectively in \((4/5,1)\) and \((5/2,3)\).
Their product exceeds two, so the other conjugate pair has squared modulus less than one by the constant coefficient.
Thus it is a Pisot polynomial in the stated class.

<a id="return"></a>
## Cancellation of the large integer constant in the return

All roots of \(C\) are on the unit circle, and its constant coefficient is minus one.
Thus its reversal in degree \(k\) is \(-C\).
Write \(U^\dagger(Z)=Z^{k+1}U(1/Z)\), a polynomial because \(\deg U<k\).
The exact positive delay-one return is
\[
             Zf+f^*=(Z^2-1)C+ZU+U^\dagger,\qquad
                          f^*(Z)=Z^{k+1}f(1/Z).                      \tag{5}
\]
It is a monic reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(D\), with constant coefficient one.
The \(q\)-terms cancel exactly.

The [positive-constant Pisot return](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#two-points) has a single exterior root \(\sigma>1\), and
\[
                    0<E=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2
                             \le(1-1/\theta)\delta<\delta .
\]
The root \(\sigma\) is either a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit.
In the latter case its integer trace is at least three, so \(E\ge1>b_D\).
Otherwise its degree \(r\) is even, \(4\le r\le D\).
The [inspected Voutier bound](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs) gives
\[
                  \log\sigma>\frac14
                             \left(\frac{\log\log r}{\log r}\right)^3,
                  \qquad E\ge(\log\sigma)^2 .
\]
For \(4\le r\le D\), the function \(\log\log x/\log x\) is bounded below by half its value at \(D\).
Indeed it increases up to \(e^e\), then decreases, its maximum is \(1/e\), and its value at four exceeds \(1/(2e)\).
Consequently \(E>b_D\), proving the first bound in (2) at every supported degree.
In particular the noncyclotomic output needed for this lower bound follows from the Pisot hypothesis.

<a id="height"></a>
## Evaluation at the exterior Pisot root

The norm of the interior conjugates gives \(q<\theta\).
Also \(U\ne0\), because otherwise (1) would have unit-circle roots and be reducible.
For \(\theta>1\), the root factorization of \(C\) and the coefficient bound on \(U\) give
\[
 |C(\theta)|\ge(\theta-1)^k,\qquad
 |U(\theta)|\le H\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\theta^j
                           <\frac{H\theta^k}{\theta-1}.
\]
At the root of (1), \(\delta C(\theta)=-U(\theta)\).
It follows that
\[
       \delta<\frac{H}{\theta(1-1/\theta)^{k+1}}
                   <\frac{H}{q(1-1/q)^{k+1}},                        \tag{6}
\]
where the last inequality uses strict increase of
\(t(1-1/t)^{k+1}\) on \(t>1\).
Combining with the lower bound already proved yields the second inequality in (2).
This estimate requires no lower bound on \(q/k\).

For (3), use \(\log(1-1/q)\ge-1/(q-1)\).
If \(H=O(k^a)\), (2) implies
\[
          \log q-\frac{k+1}{q-1}
                       \le a\log k+6\log\log k+O(1).                \tag{7}
\]
For any fixed \(\eta>0\), suppose along a subsequence that
\(q\ge(1+\eta)k/((1-a)\log k)\).
The left side of (7) is at least
\[
       \left(1-\frac{1-a}{1+\eta}\right)\log k
                                          -\log\log k-O(1).
\]
Its coefficient of \(\log k\) exceeds \(a\), contradicting (7).
This proves (3).
For \(q\ge ck\), the factor \((1-1/q)^{k+1}\) stays bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(c\) as \(k\to\infty\).
The finitely many smaller \(k\) can be absorbed into that constant, proving (4).

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequence for the selected large-norm inverses

The [controlled inverse construction](Yin_C_G0345_v31.md#statement) supplies same-degree Pisot seeds for any hypothetical Salem sequence approaching one, with selected constants \(q\) larger than the degree by an arbitrarily slowly growing factor.
At those scales, any representation of the form (1) necessarily has
\[
                         H\gg q
                              \left(\frac{\log\log(k+2)}{\log(k+2)}\right)^6.
\]
Thus bounded-height lower-degree perturbations of any cyclotomic product cannot supply those particular inverses.
The restriction concerns the coefficients of \(U\) in (1), with its leading-degree and constant conditions.
A general Pisot seed can have larger perturbation coefficients, and the bounds here allow \(q\) below the threshold in (3).
The unrestricted positive-constant excess problem remains open.
The coordinator supplied the estimate using all factors \(|\theta-\zeta|\ge\theta-1\), which removes a preliminary linear lower bound on \(q/k\).
