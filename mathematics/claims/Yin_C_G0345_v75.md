# Small elements in a reciprocal unit field and the relative norm-one group

<a id="statement"></a>
## Small height forces relative norm one

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with \(M(P)>1\), let \(\lambda\) be a root, and put \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), \(B=\mathbb Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\).
Write \(\iota\) for the nonidentity element of \({\rm Gal}(K/B)\).
Let \(g\) be the number of inverse pairs on the unit circle and assume \(g/m\ge\delta>0\).
For any \(\gamma\in K^\times\),
\[
          h(\gamma)\le\frac{\delta}{4}\,4^{-1/\delta}
               \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
          \gamma\,\iota(\gamma)=1 ,
\]
where \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
If \(\gamma\) is an algebraic integer this conclusion makes it a unit.
In particular, suppose there are \(a\) real exterior roots and \(s\) nonreal pairs of complex-conjugate exterior roots.
There are at most \(a+s\) multiplicatively independent algebraic integers in \(K\) satisfying this height bound and different from zero.
For a complex Salem input all such integers belong, up to roots of unity, to one cyclic group of relative units, even when the inverse-pair action is imprimitive.

<a id="norm"></a>
## A lower height bound from positive real embeddings

Let \(B\) first be any number field of degree \(m\), and let \(u\in B^\times\) be different from one.
Suppose at least \(g\ge\delta m>0\) embeddings have \(u\) positive real.
For any \(x>0\),
\[
          |1-x|\le\max(1,x)|\log x| ,
\]
because \(1-e^{-y}\le y\) for \(y\ge0\).
At every other archimedean embedding one has \(|1-x|\le 2\max(1,|x|)\) and at each finite place one has the same upper bound with factor one.
The product formula for \(1-u\) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on any \(g\) specified positive embeddings therefore give
\[
          1\ \le\ e^{m h(u)}2^{m-g}
                  \left(\frac{2m h(u)}{g}\right)^g .
\]
Here the sum of the absolute logs over the selected embeddings is at most \(m h(u)+m h(u^{-1})=2m h(u)\), including the finite places in the two height budgets if necessary.
These statements count the two embeddings of any complex place separately.
If \(h(u)\le(\delta/2)4^{-1/\delta}\), the last power is at most \(4^{-m}\).
Since \(0<\delta\le1\), the entire right side is then at most \((e^{1/8}/2)^m<1\).
This contradiction proves the lower bound for \(u\ne1\).

Returning to the reciprocal field, the \(m\) different traces show that \([K:B]=2\) and \(\iota(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\).
Each neutral inverse pair gives one distinct real embedding of \(B\) where the two extensions to \(K\) are complex conjugates.
There are thus \(g\) embeddings with \(u=\gamma\iota(\gamma)>0\).
Since \(h(u)\le2h(\gamma)\), the preceding bound forces \(u=1\) at the stated threshold.
In particular \(\iota(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\) is integral when \(\gamma\) is.

<a id="cyclic"></a>
## Independent integral units at the remaining places

Let \(U=\{\gamma\in\mathcal O_K^\times:\gamma\iota(\gamma)=1\}\).
Over a neutral real trace embedding its elements all have modulus one.
Each real exterior root gives a real trace place splitting into two real embeddings, where their logs are opposite.
Each nonreal conjugate exterior pair gives one complex trace place, with two extensions over a chosen embedding there.
Their logs are opposite, and the conjugate trace embedding just repeats the same logs.
Thus the entire logarithmic absolute-value vector of a unit in \(U\) is determined by \(a+s\) real coordinates, one per such place.
The image in \(\mathbb R^{a+s}\) is discrete with finite kernel.
Indeed, any fixed bound on these coordinates bounds the conjugate moduli in every embedding.
There are only finitely many algebraic integers of degree at most \(2m\) satisfying this bound, by their monic minimal-polynomial coefficients.
A unit in the kernel has all powers there, and finiteness makes it a root of unity.
Conversely every root of unity in \(K\) belongs to \(U\), because one neutral trace embedding makes its images under \(\iota\) and inversion identical.
A discrete subgroup of \(\mathbb R^{a+s}\) has rank at most \(a+s\): choose a maximal real-linearly independent set of its vectors and reduce the coefficients of any group element modulo the lattice they generate.
A bounded fundamental parallelotope contains finitely many image points, so this lattice has finite index in the group.
The rank bound follows, as does the stated restriction on independent small integers.

For a complex Salem number \(m\ge3\), \(a=0,\ s=1,\ g=m-2\); in particular \(\delta=1/3\) works uniformly over all degrees.
The discrete image is a nonzero subgroup of the line since \(\lambda\) has a conjugate outside the circle.
Choose \(\rho\in U\) whose log generates this image; then every relative norm-one integral unit is a root-of-unity multiple of a power of \(\rho\).
This gives the cyclic conclusion for small algebraic integers throughout the varying fields.
It extends the [relative generator in the unbalanced single-block case](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#fields) to arbitrary complex Salem fields, and supplies a height criterion without an initial relative norm assumption.
No uniform lower bound for the measure of \(\rho\) follows from discreteness in the individual fields.
