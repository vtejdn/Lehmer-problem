## 1. An exact finite logarithmic upper certificate

Let P be an ACTUAL monic irreducible nontorsion integer-unit polynomial, of original degree d, roots alpha_i, and total m=log M(P)>0.
Put r_i=|alpha_i|, z_i=alpha_i/r_i.
All original roots are retained.
Let F in Z[t] have degree T>=1, coefficient l1 norm S, and Res(P,F) nonzero.
Set h=T*m/d.
Choose rational delta>0 and rational B>=S^2+delta^2.
For an integer J>=1 define the real Laurent polynomials on the circle

    H(z)=|F(z)|^2+delta^2,
    Y(z)=1-H(z)/B,
    Phi_J(z)=(1/2)log B-(1/2)sum_(j=1)^J Y(z)^j/j.
    c_J(F;delta,B)=constant coefficient of Phi_J.       (1)

The coefficient c_J is a completely finite coefficient calculation: its nonlogarithmic part is rational, and its only displayed transcendental constant is log B/2.
It does not use M(P), an outer optimizer or a root of P.
For R=1-delta^2/B one has0<=Y(z)<=R<1 and the scalar logarithm series gives

    (1/2)log H(z)<=Phi_J(z),
    0<=Phi_J(z)-(1/2)log H(z)
          <=R^(J+1)/[2(J+1)(1-R)].                     (2)

The first inequality is the UPPER direction of positive regularization followed by a finite log majorant.
It is not a lower-capacity or a pointwise-maximum inference from C_F6.

There is an exact finite matrix realization.
Write Y(z)=sum_(|k|<=T)y_k z^k and form the Hermitian matrix K=(y_(i-j)) on indices -JT,...,JT.
For every vector x,

    x* K x=integral_T Y(z)|sum_i x_i z^i|^2,
    0<=K<=R I.

For j<=J every length-j bandwidth-T path starting and ending at0 remains in this index interval.
Thus (K^j)_(0,0) is exactly the constant coefficient of Y^j, with no boundary approximation, and

    c_J=(1/2)log B-(1/2)sum_(j=1)^J (K^j)_(0,0)/j.   (3)

The actual matrix size is2JT+1.
No efficient-size, capacity or determinant normalization claim is made.
This is the finite compression mechanism of C_F8 used in an upper logarithmic cost calculation, with its required no-wrap calculation supplied here.

Use precisely L229's accepted function, with c=log2/4,

    h0=c exp(-20),
    b(u)=max{u,sqrt(u/c)log sqrt(c/u)},
    epsilon(u)=min{1,15sqrt(b(u))+4u}, 0<u<=h0,

and its actual finite-frequency bound at EVERY frequency up to JT.
These functions are increasing on this exact domain and epsilon(u) tends to0.
Whenever0<Jh<=h0,

    log|Res(P,F)|/d
       <=c_J(F;delta,B)+(1+S/delta)h
                                      +2^J epsilon(Jh). (4)

This is the proposed reusable finite inequality.
Its complete proof follows; it does not assume that the source discrepancy estimate applies to an infinite Fourier series.

## 2. Original radial expense and every finite Fourier coefficient

Normalize the actual values by V_i=F(alpha_i)/max(1,r_i)^T.
L229's elementary coefficient normalization gives

    |V_i-F(z_i)|<=S min(1,T|log r_i|),
    sum_i |log r_i|=2m,
    log|Res(P,F)|/d=h+(1/d)sum_i log|V_i|.             (5)

Nonzero Res ensures every V_i is nonzero.
The function psi_delta(x)=(1/2)log(x^2+delta^2), x>=0, has derivative x/(x^2+delta^2)<=1/(2delta).
Therefore

    log|V_i|<=psi_delta(|V_i|)
       <=psi_delta(|F(z_i)|)+|V_i-F(z_i)|/(2delta).

Averaging and using(5) pays the entire radial error S*h/delta.
By(2), the remaining term is at most the average of Phi_J(z_i).

The Laurent coefficient l1 norm of Y is at most1+(S^2+delta^2)/B<=2.
Its j-th power has coefficient l1 norm at most2^j.
The sum of the absolute NONCONSTANT coefficients of Phi_J is therefore at most

    (1/2)sum_(j=1)^J 2^j/j<=2^J-1<2^J.              (6)

Collecting equal exponents after any substitution only lowers this bound.
Every nonconstant frequency has absolute value at most JT.
L229 gives absolute angular mean at most epsilon(Jh), with original-field power multiplicities retained.
Consequently

    (1/d)sum_i Phi_J(z_i)<=c_J+2^J epsilon(Jh).

Together with(5) and the paid radial error this proves(4).
No root separation, pointwise smallness of F, integer value of a filter, or assumption that alpha^n preserves degree was introduced.
Formula(4) only becomes useful when its ACTUAL finite c_J and errors fit the arithmetic income.
Replacing c_J by log M(F) without its finite majorant and frequency costs would be unjustified.

If F(alpha)/q is a nonzero algebraic integer for an integer q>=2, its full original norm gives log|Res(P,F)|/d>=log q.

on the same domain.

## 4. Actual modular income, a fully finite error cutoff and the zero branch

    B_P(t)=(A_N(t)-P(t))/2

At an original root alpha,

    A_N(alpha)=2B_P(alpha),
    Res(P,A_N)=2^d Norm_(Q(alpha)/Q)(B_P(alpha)).       (14)

If P differs from A_N, irreducibility and equality of their monic degrees imply B_P(alpha) nonzero.
Thus its signed norm is a nonzero integer, and the absolute full norm in(14) is at least2^d.
This is genuine income in the ORIGINAL field, not a quotient/powered field, a numerical observation or an integral containing unknown m(P).

## 5. An actual reciprocal irreducible polynomial in every even degree

This supplies nonempty-domain controls, not near-one examples or separation from every older arithmetic certificate.
Let d=2h>=8 and N=d-3.
Both A_N and(t-1)^d are reciprocal monic polynomials with constant1.
For each1<=j<=h-1 choose c_j in{0,...,13} by CRT so that

    c_j agrees with the t^j coefficient of A_N modulo2,
    c_j agrees with the t^j coefficient of(t-1)^d modulo7.

Let S0=sum_(j=1)^(h-1)c_j and set

    c_h=-16-2S0,
    P_d(t)=t^d+1+c_h t^h
                         +sum_(j=1)^(h-1)c_j(t^j+t^(d-j)). (17)

This is an explicit coefficient recipe using finitely many rational-integer operations.
Its original endpoints and reciprocity are exact.
The central coefficient of A_N is0 for all even d>=8, and c_h is even, so the whole polynomial is congruent to A_N modulo2.
At7 every noncentral coefficient has the prescribed residue, while P_d(1)=-14 is0 modulo7.
Since(t-1)^d also vanishes at1, the central residue is forced to match as well.
Therefore

    P_d mod7=(t-1)^d,
    P_d(1)=-14.

The monic translate P_d(t+1) has every nonleading coefficient divisible by7 and constant term-14 not divisible by49.
Eisenstein at7 proves its irreducibility, hence that of P_d.
Explicitly, any rational monic factors of a monic integer polynomial have integer coefficients: their coefficients are rational symmetric functions of algebraic integers, hence rational algebraic integers.
A nontrivial such factorization of P_d(t+1), reduced modulo7, has both monic positive-degree factors equal to powers of t, since their product is t^d.
Both constant terms would then be divisible by7, forcing49 to divide-14, a contradiction.
Translation preserves rational factorizations.
Thus no polynomial-factorization computation is substituted for the argument.
Since P_d(1)<0 and P_d tends to positive infinity, it has a real root greater than1 and is nontorsion.
It is therefore an actual irreducible reciprocal integer unit of original degree d.

The coefficient and l1 costs are fully bounded:

    max_j |coeff_j P_d|<=16+26(h-1)=13d-10,
    ||P_d||_1=18+4S0<=18+52(h-1)=26d-34.              (18)

Also A_N(1)=-2 differs from P_d(1)=-14, so this family lies in the nonzero branch of(14).
Reduction at2 can be bad; all conclusions use original-order divisibility rather than a good-prime root-count floor.
The extra Eisenstein condition may itself place these particular controls in older sufficient classes.
