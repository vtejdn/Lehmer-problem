# Open volume for four-term multiples obtained by two fixed punctures

<a id="four"></a>
## A rotation and one twist for the four occupied positions

Let \(P\) be monic, reciprocal, integral, irreducible and noncyclotomic of degree \(2g\), with \(g\ge2\), and suppose \(P\notin\mathbb Z[x^h]\) for \(h>1\).
If \(F=(x-1)P\) has four nonzero terms, there is an orientable hyperbolic bundle with two fixed boundary circles and an absolute upper bound for its volume, whose whole open polynomial is \(F\) and whose capped polynomial is \(P\).
The bound is independent of the genus, exponents, coefficient magnitudes and number of nonzero coefficients of \(P\).
This gives a volume bound in terms of open support alone on the entire stated four-term class.

Put \(N=2g+1\).
Antireciprocity gives \(F=x^N-1+q(x^{N-i}-x^i)\), where \(1\le i<N/2\) and \(q\ne0\) is integral.
If \(b=\gcd(N,i)>1\), then \(x^b-1\) divides \(F\), contradicting the irreducibility and noncyclotomicity of \(P\).
Choose \(u\) with \(ui\equiv1\bmod N\).
Take the double cover of the sphere branched at the \(N\) vertices on the unit circle and at infinity.
It is a closed surface of genus \(g\).
Rotation by \(2\pi/N\) about the origin has a lift \(R\) of order \(N\), since either lift has \(N\)-th power a deck transformation and \(N\) is odd.
The two lifts differ by the commuting deck involution.
Let \(e\) be the oriented lift of a polygon edge, a simple closed curve.
The \(N\) classes \(R^je\) meet only for neighboring indices, with algebraic intersection of absolute value one.
Indeed the corresponding arcs share just one branch point, where their lifted tangent lines are transverse, and all nonneighbors have disjoint lifts.
Their skew circulant intersection matrix has kernel precisely the constant vectors, since its kernel equations are \(v_{j+1}=v_{j-1}\) with odd period.
Thus the classes span the \(N-1\) dimensional rational homology, with their sum as their only relation up to scalar.
The action of \(R^u\) on this quotient has characteristic polynomial \((x^N-1)/(x-1)\).

Compose \(A=R^u\) with a power of the Dehn twist about \(e\).
On homology the twist is \(v\mapsto v+c\langle v,e\rangle e\), where the exponent can give either sign for \(c\).
The rank-one determinant identity multiplies the characteristic polynomial of \(A\) by \(1-c\langle(xI-A)^{-1}Ae,e\rangle\).
In the identity
\[
       (x^N-1)(xI-A)^{-1}A=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{N-j}A^j
\]
only \(j=i,N-i\) give nonzero pairings with \(e\), with opposite unit signs.
Hence a choice \(c=\pm q\) realizes the required \(P\).
Write \(f\) for this closed mapping class.
The [closed homological criterion](Yin_C_G0883_v2.md#mapping-tori) makes \(f\) pseudo-Anosov.

<a id="punctures"></a>
## Marking the two lifts of the origin

First suppose \(u\not\equiv\pm1\bmod N\).
Mark the origin downstairs and both its lifts upstairs, which \(R\) fixes individually since it has odd order.
The elementary twist on the cover projects to a half twist on the polygon edge, as one sees in the annular double cover of a two-branch-point disk.
Let \(b\) be the rotation followed in the corresponding order by the power of the half twist on the sphere.
With just the \(N+1\) branch points marked, \(b\) is pseudo-Anosov.
Indeed a periodic class would lift to a periodic class after forgetting the branch points upstairs, since a suitable power lifts an isotopy to the identity or the deck involution.
An essential reducing multicurve would also lift to an essential one.
Both disks cut off by each of its components contain at least two branch points, and the double cover of each such disk is connected with nonpositive Euler characteristic and has no disk component.
The classification of surface mapping classes and the closed criterion for \(f\) now give the assertion.

After also marking the origin, \(b\) remains nonperiodic and every component of any reducing multicurve must become peripheral or inessential on forgetting the origin.
Each such component encloses the origin with just one old marked point.
There can be only one component, since disjoint nonisotopic disks of this kind can neither overlap nor contain each other.
Its enclosed old mark must therefore be fixed.
No finite branch point is fixed, because the rotation moves all vertices through \(u\) positions and the only permutation correction can exchange two neighbors.
The possible disk thus contains the origin and infinity, and its complementary disk would contain all \(N\) polygon vertices.

Identify the twice-punctured sphere with \(\mathbb C^*\).
The total winding of the \(N\) vertices along an isotopy from the identity to a representative of \(b\), fixing the two ends, has a well-defined residue modulo \(N\).
It is the winding of the product of the \(N\) point coordinates, which is a loop even for a permutation.
Two such isotopies differ by a loop of annulus homeomorphisms and an isotopy relative to the marked set.
For a loop of homeomorphisms the winding of one point is independent of the point by continuity, so the total change is a multiple of \(N\).
The rotation contributes \(u\) and the half twists contribute zero because their supporting disk avoids both ends.
If the complementary disk containing all vertices were invariant up to isotopy, the class could be supported in that disk.
Indeed on its complement, a disk with two individually marked points, a homeomorphism fixing the boundary acts there up to isotopy by a power of the boundary twist.
This follows from the pure two-strand braid group, generated by the full twist, and that boundary twist can be moved into the vertex disk.
A disk-supported representative gives winding zero, contradicting \(\gcd(u,N)=1\).
The marked sphere class is consequently pseudo-Anosov.
Lift its invariant foliations and fill the branch points upstairs.
At each of those points the branch cover doubles the prong number to at least two.
The resulting two-punctured surface is pseudo-Anosov with capped class \(f\) and both punctures fixed.

Triangulate the polygon sphere by fixed local patterns in the sectors from each polygon edge to the two poles, then lift the triangulation through the branched cover and truncate the two lifts of the origin.
This takes \(O(N)\) triangles, is invariant under \(R^u\), and gives the lift of a single polygon edge a bounded-size neighborhood.
For instance truncate the two pole vertices by a belt with one edge per incident sector, and subdivide the equatorial sectors a bounded number of times near each edge and branch point.
The moving boundary circles may rotate simplicially.
The rotation then costs no extra layer and one drilled horizontal curve implements the twist with bounded cost at any power.
The [cyclic-cover volume estimate with retained cusps](Yin_C_G0883_v9.md#layers) removes the initial \(O(N)\) collar and gives the claimed absolute bound.

<a id="neighbor"></a>
## A one-sector rotation with fixed marks in a disk

For \(u\equiv\pm1\bmod N\), isotope the rotation on the capped surface to fix a small disk about one lift of the origin pointwise.
Use the [annular fan](Yin_C_G0883_v7.md#fan) between a three-edge disk and its surrounding sector circle.
The rotation shifts the outer circle just one sector in either direction and takes boundedly many flips with the inner circle fixed.
Put two marks inside the fixed disk.
Choose the lift of a small disk about three consecutive branch points at the fan seam.
It is a one-holed torus, triangulated in a bounded number of equatorial pieces using a thin neighborhood of two consecutive polygon edges.
Join it by a band to a small disk about just one mark through the corresponding equatorial piece, a sector on the seam and the adjacent fan triangles.
This band crosses boundedly many triangles, by the [seam access calculation](Yin_C_G0883_v7.md#interfaces).
The boundary \(c_0\) of the enlarged torus stays separating on capping and separates the two marks.

The orbit of \(c_0\) under the marked lift fills the twice-marked surface.
Every test curve still essential on closing meets some iterate by the closed pseudo-Anosov property, with a finite segment sufficient as in the [marked filling argument](Yin_C_G0883_v9.md#filling).
Every other essential test curve bounds a disk containing both marks, so intersects \(c_0\), since it cannot contain this closed-essential curve or meet both sides while disjoint from it.
The same filling-orbit twist theorem makes the marked composite with a sufficiently large power of the twist about \(c_0\) pseudo-Anosov.
This twist preserves closed homology and fixes the two marks.
It contributes one more drilled horizontal curve in boundedly many triangles independently of its exponent.
The original edge twist has the same bounded complexity as above.
Thus the cyclic-cover argument gives the same absolute open volume bound and the closed criterion still gives hyperbolicity on capping.
In both constructions the [boundary homology module](Yin_C_G0883_v8.md#cycles) of two fixed marks has polynomial \(x-1\), giving the exact whole polynomials.

## Dense Salem polynomials and the scope for records

For every odd \(N\ge5\) and every integer \(m\ge3\), take \(i=1,\ q=-m\).
The interior coefficients of \(P\) are all \(1-m\).
There is exactly one root outside the unit circle.
Indeed \(F=x^{N-1}(x-m)-(1-mx)\), and reducing the multiplier of the second term from one to any number in \((0,1)\) gives \(N-1\) roots in the unit disk by Rouché's theorem.
Passing to the limit gives at most one exterior root counting multiplicity, and \(F(2)<0\) gives a real root greater than two.
A root of unity \(\zeta\ne1\) in \(F\) would give \(\zeta^N-1=m(\zeta^{N-1}-\zeta)\).
The latter difference cannot vanish, since then \(\zeta^{N-2}=\zeta^N=1\) and \(N\) is odd.
Its nonzero integral norm has absolute value at least one, whereas the norm of \(\zeta^N-1\) has absolute value at most \(2^{[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]}\).
This contradicts \(m\ge3\), and \(P(1)=N-m(N-2)\ne0\).
Each noncyclotomic irreducible factor of this monic unit polynomial requires at least one exterior root by Kronecker's theorem, so \(P\) is irreducible Salem and its consecutive occupied positions exclude a proper power substitution.
Thus the construction realizes dense polynomials of unbounded degree at open support four, with \(M(P)>2\).

For a general reciprocal strict record, any closed realization of volume \(V\) already supplies an admissible multiple with at most \(2\exp(bV)\) terms for an absolute \(b\).
The [cleared cellular determinant](Yin_C_G0883_v3.md#columns) gives \(P\) times two geometric sums \((x^a-1)/(x-1)\), \((x^{a'}-1)/(x-1)\) and finitely many binomials \(x^{c_j}-1\), all exponents taken positive after monomial normalization.
Multiplication by one more \(x-1\) costs at most a factor two in support and leaves exactly the boundary cofactor for the periods \(a,a',c_1,\ldots,c_s\).
This transfers a volume bound to existence of an admissible multiple, without estimating the volume of its open realization.
Along any proposed record sequence with measures tending to one the [bounded-support Mahler gap](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#sparse-measure) forces the admissible support to grow.
The direct four-term construction supplies a case of the converse volume estimate, while its extension to growing open support and dense closed support remains open.
