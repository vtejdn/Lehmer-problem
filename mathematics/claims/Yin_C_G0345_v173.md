# Trace-field degrees on all hyperbolic filling slopes of a fixed one-cusped manifold

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree growth without a Lehmer hypothesis

Fix an orientable finite-volume one-cusped hyperbolic three-manifold \(M\) with a meridian–longitude basis on its torus cusp.
For a primitive slope \((p,q)\) with hyperbolic Dehn filling \(M_{p/q}\), let \(K(M_{p/q})\) be the trace field of its geometric representation, in the convention of Garoufalidis and Jeon.
There exist \(0<c_M\le C_M\) such that
\[
              c_M\max(|p|,|q|)
              \ \le\ [K(M_{p/q}):\mathbb Q]\
              \le\ C_M\max(|p|,|q|)
\]
over all these slopes.
For the corresponding fixed \(A\)-polynomial, every nontorsion irreducible factor of each nonzero filling polynomial likewise has degree comparable to \(\max(|p|,|q|)\).
The constants depend on \(M\) and the chosen peripheral basis.

[Garoufalidis and Jeon, Theorems 1.2 and 2.5](../audits/literature/GJ24/Yin_U001.md#gj24-u001) obtained these growth estimates under a Lehmer hypothesis and treated restricted sectors unconditionally.
Here the [degree theorem for torsion edges](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) supplies the needed polynomial lower bound directly.
This application uses the geometric inputs of that article.
It does not determine the growth of the least positive Mahler measure for unrestricted integral polynomials.
Fili, Hoffman and Petersen have separately announced unconditional bounds, including a linear lower bound on most slopes and an exponent \(1-\epsilon\) on all slopes. [Lecture abstract](https://sites.rutgers.edu/dltg/lectures/)

<a id="polygon"></a>
## The peripheral polynomial and its faces

Use the integral primitive \(A(m,l)\) in the dilation coordinates of Garoufalidis and Jeon, §2.
Their Theorem 2.2 gives vertex coefficients of absolute value one and cyclotomic edge polynomials.
For every sufficiently long filling the geometric point is \((m,l)=(t_0^{-q},t_0^p)\) on this curve, tends to \((1,1)\), and has \(|t_0|\ne1\).
Indeed for integers \(r,s\) with \(ps-qr=1\) the core curve \(\mu^r\lambda^s\) has dilation \(m^r l^s=t_0\); the hyperbolic filling core is loxodromic.
The orientation of that core or choice of its reciprocal multiplier does not affect its degree.

These facts also ensure that the support of \(A\) spans an affine plane.
Otherwise its equation in the torus has the form \(H(m^a l^b)=0\) for a nonzero one-variable Laurent polynomial and a nonzero primitive direction \((a,b)\), up to a monomial.
The point-support case cannot have any geometric torus zero.
On all sufficiently long geometric fillings \(m^a l^b\) tends to one and belongs to the fixed finite root set of \(H\), hence equals one eventually.
It follows that \(t_0^{-qa+pb}=1\), which with \(|t_0|\ne1\) allows only the single unoriented primitive slope proportional to \((a,b)\).
There are infinitely many other hyperbolic filling slopes by the hyperbolic Dehn surgery theorem, a contradiction.

Apply the two-dimensional degree theorem to \(F=A\) and the vector \((-q,p)\).
A common Laurent monomial factor of a specialization can be removed before taking its nonzero roots.
Directions in which distinct terms collide form finitely many rational lines, hence give only finitely many primitive slopes.
Thus the specialization is nonzero for all sufficiently large slopes, and every nontorsion factor has both stated degree bounds on the whole range of nonzero specializations.

<a id="field"></a>
## Passing from the core dilation to the trace field

For large slopes, \(t_0\) is a root of the nonzero specialization and is nontorsion by loxodromy.
Its minimal polynomial therefore has degree comparable to \(\max(|p|,|q|)\).
Theorem 2.4 of the [same published source](../audits/literature/GJ24/Yin_U001.md#gj24-u001) compares this specific geometric degree with \([K(M_{p/q}):\mathbb Q]\) in both directions, with constants depending only on the fixed manifold.
For the lower-bound direction one can also see the bounded index explicitly.
The core has eigenvalues \(u,u^{-1}\) in a lift, so its dilation \(t_0\) is \(u^2\) or \(u^{-2}\).
Then \(t_0+t_0^{-1}=(\operatorname{tr}(\mu^r\lambda^s))^2-2\in K(M_{p/q})\).
Thus \([\mathbb Q(t_0):\mathbb Q]\le2[K(M_{p/q}):\mathbb Q]\).
The comparison requires no equality of the peripheral field with the full trace field.
Enlarging the constants to include the finitely many smaller hyperbolic fillings finishes the proof.
