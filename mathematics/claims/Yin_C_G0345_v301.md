# Partial-product descent for imprimitive real circuits

<a id="statement"></a>
## Partial products reduce both degree and exterior count

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with exactly \(e\ge3\) exterior roots, all real.
Suppose their positive logarithmic moduli form a rational circuit: they are dependent over \(\mathbb Q\), and every proper subset is independent.
The [real circuit theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) partitions the inverse-pair labels into \(n=m/e\) parent blocks of size \(e\), one exterior and all others neutral.
Orient representatives \(x_i\) in each parent so that
\[
                          \prod_{i\in B}x_i=\omega,\qquad
                              \omega\in\{1,-1\}.                   \tag{1}
\]
The sign is the same for all parents.
In these orientations the complete relation lattice modulo torsion consists of the vectors constant on each parent.

Let \(G\) be the splitting-field Galois group, let \(\mathcal E\) denote the exterior parent, and let \(H\) be the unsigned permutation group induced by \(G_{\mathcal E}\) on its \(e\) labels.
Assume \(H\) preserves a nontrivial partition into \(k\) parts \(A\) of equal size \(b=e/k\), where \(1<b<e\).
Write
\[
 t_i=\log|x_i|\quad(i\in\mathcal E),\qquad
 s_A=\sum_{i\in A}t_i,\qquad
                       p_A=\prod_{i\in A}x_i.                     \tag{2}
\]
Transport the partition to every parent by Galois action.

If \(k\ge3\), the signed partial products \(p_A,p_A^{-1}\) in all parents are the distinct roots of a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial \(P'\), satisfying
\[
 \deg P'=2nk=\frac{2m}{b}<2m,\qquad
 \#\{\text{exterior roots of }P'\}=k,\qquad
 0<\log M(P')=\sum_A|s_A|\le\sum_i|t_i|=\log M(P).                 \tag{3}
\]
All its exterior roots are real, and their positive logarithms again form a rational circuit.
Its parent blocks correspond exactly to the original parents, with one label per part, and the exterior-parent stabilizer induces the action of \(H\) on those parts.

If \(k=2\), squared partial products give a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial \(P'\) of degree \(2n=2m/e\), with a unique exterior root, which is positive, and
\[
                       0<\log M(P')=2|s_A|\le\log M(P).           \tag{4}
\]
It is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number when \(n\ge2\), and of a positive real reciprocal quadratic unit when \(n=1\).
If \(\omega=1\), unsquared partial products, followed if necessary by multiplication by \(-1\), give the same degree and signature with the stronger bound
\[
                         \log M(P')=|s_A|
                                       \le\tfrac12\log M(P).       \tag{5}
\]

Choosing a maximal proper block for the imprimitive action \(H\) makes its action on the parts primitive.
Thus one application produces either a Salem or quadratic unit, or a real circuit with primitive unsigned exterior-parent action, without increasing Mahler measure.
This applies with both \(e\) and the number of neutral parents unbounded.

For \(e=4\) no action hypothesis is needed to obtain a smaller-degree alternative.
An imprimitive \(H\) gives (4); a primitive \(H\) gives an irreducible reciprocal polynomial of degree \(3m/2\), with exactly three real exterior roots whose positive logarithms are rationally independent, and logarithmic measure at most \(2\log M(P)\).
When \(\omega=1\), the latter cost improves to \(\log M(P)\).
This primitive case uses the pair-product calculation in [C345v299](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#four-descent), with the orbit justification supplied below without assuming a full signed symmetric action.

<a id="parents"></a>
## Orientations and the transported partition

The [real circuit decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#circuit) supplies exactly one primitive relation per parent, with coefficients of absolute value one, and no further relations except their integer combinations.
Its [projection argument](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) makes the value of each relation a sign.
Inverting representatives according to those coefficients gives (1).
This orientation is determined up to simultaneous inversion within a parent.

A Galois automorphism permutes the parents and sends the representatives in one parent to a permutation of the target representatives with a common inversion sign.
Indeed its image of the all-ones relation must be the target's primitive relation or its negative.
The product sign in (1) is uniform: transporting a parent takes its rational sign to the target product or its inverse, and the inverse of a sign is itself.

The action of \(G\) is transitive on the original roots and on the inverse-pair labels.
The setwise stabilizer of a parent is transitive on that parent's labels.
For if an automorphism sends a label in the parent to another label there, its image parent intersects the original parent and therefore equals it.
In particular \(H\) is transitive.
An invariant partition of a transitive set has equal part sizes.

The partition in the statement transports unambiguously to other parents.
Two automorphisms taking \(\mathcal E\) to the same parent differ by an element of \(G_{\mathcal E}\), whose unsigned action preserves the original partition.
The resulting partitions are consequently \(G\)-equivariant, including under automorphisms with a common negative sign on a parent.

The rational nullspace of the \(t_i\) in (2) is exactly the line of the all-ones vector.
The original positive exterior logarithms have a one-dimensional nullspace by the circuit hypothesis, and the chosen orientation transforms its primitive signed relation into (1).
In particular
\[
 \sum_i t_i=0,\qquad
 \sum_{A}s_A=0,\qquad s_A\ne0
       \quad\hbox{for every proper nonempty part }A.                \tag{6}
\]
The nonvanishing follows because the indicator vector of a proper part is not constant.

<a id="many-parts"></a>
## Three or more parts give one irreducible orbit

Assume \(k\ge3\).
A signed partial product is represented by the exponent vector \(\epsilon\mathbf1_A\), where \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Two such vectors from different parents cannot differ by a relation, since either nonzero restriction is nonconstant on its parent.
Within one parent, if the parts are different, a third part has coefficient zero in their difference.
Any constant relation vector would therefore be zero, which is incompatible with the nonzero entries on the selected parts.
For equal parts and opposite signs, the difference is \(2\mathbf1_A\), again nonconstant.
Thus all signed partial products are distinct even modulo torsion.

They form one Galois orbit.
Choose a label \(i\) of a source part \(A\), a label \(j\) of any target part \(A'\), and a sign \(\epsilon\).
Transitivity on the original roots supplies \(g\in G\) with
\[
                              g(x_i)=x_j^\epsilon .
\]
The transported partition makes \(g(A)=A'\).
The common sign on the parent then gives \(g(p_A)=p_{A'}^\epsilon\).
This proves transitivity on the entire listed set, rather than only on its unsigned supports.

Every value is an algebraic unit, and the list is closed under inversion.
Its orbit polynomial is therefore monic integral, irreducible and reciprocal.
There are \(n\) parents, \(k\) parts in each, and two distinct signed values per part, giving degree \(2nk\).

Only values belonging to the exterior parent can be off the circle.
There the representatives are real, so all signed partial products are real.
For each part the two logarithms are \(s_A,-s_A\), and \(s_A\ne0\) by (6).
Thus there is one exterior root per part, proving the exterior count and the exact measure in (3).
The bound there is the triangle inequality on each part.

More generally, if rational coefficients \(c_A\) satisfy \(\sum_A c_A s_A=0\), their lift to the original labels is constant with value \(c_A\) on each part.
The original circuit forces that lifted vector to be constant on the whole parent, so all \(c_A\) agree.
Hence the \(s_A\) have precisely the all-ones rational relation.
Changing their signs to positive exterior logarithms gives a circuit on all \(k\) entries.

The full relation lattice of the partial products can also be read directly.
Choose \(p_A\) as the representative of each new inverse pair.
Any relation on these representatives lifts to an exponent vector constant on each original part.
It is a relation precisely when it is constant on every original parent.
Thus the new relation lattice consists exactly of the vectors constant on the \(k\) parts of each parent.
Their product is \(\omega\), so every such vector is indeed a relation modulo torsion.
The original parents are exactly the new circuit parents.

Finally the induced stabilizer action is the action of \(H\) on parts.
The splitting field of \(P'\) is a Galois subfield of the splitting field of \(P\), and restriction of Galois groups is surjective.
Distinctness of the new labels in different parents means that an automorphism stabilizes the new exterior parent precisely when its lift stabilizes the old one.
Its unsigned action on the new labels is exactly its old action on the parts.
This proves the claimed group interface.

<a id="two-parts"></a>
## Two parts give a single positive exterior conjugate

Suppose the partition has two complementary parts \(A,A^c\).
Their products satisfy
\[
                       p_Ap_{A^c}=\omega,\qquad
                       p_A^2p_{A^c}^2=1.                          \tag{7}
\]
Take the squared products in every parent.
There are just two possible values per parent, which are mutually inverse.

These \(2n\) values are distinct.
On one parent, equality to the inverse would make a nonzero multiple of \(\mathbf1_A\) a constant relation vector.
Across different parents the same relation test has a nonconstant restriction.
The signed-root transitivity used above sends any squared partial product to any listed one, so the list is one orbit.
It gives a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2n\).

At the exterior parent both values are positive and have logarithms \(2s_A,-2s_A\).
All other parents give circle roots.
This proves the single exterior root and the exact measure in (4).
Since the two part sums are opposite,
\[
 2|s_A|=|s_A|+|s_{A^c}|
            \le\sum_{i\in A}|t_i|+\sum_{i\in A^c}|t_i|
            =\log M(P).
\]
In particular the new unit is nontorsion.

If \(\omega=1\), the unsquared values already satisfy \(p_{A^c}=p_A^{-1}\).
The same orbit and distinctness proof gives degree \(2n\) and unique real exterior value of modulus \(\exp(|s_A|)\).
Multiply that value and all its conjugates by its exterior sign if needed, obtaining a positive exterior root.
Multiplication by a rational sign preserves degree, irreducibility, integrality and measure, and preserves reciprocal pairing because \((-\xi)^{-1}=-\xi^{-1}\).
This gives (5).

For \(n\ge2\), the reciprocal polynomial has degree at least four and unit-circle conjugates besides its unique positive exterior root and its inverse.
That is a Salem polynomial.
For \(n=1\), its two positive roots are reciprocal and its minimal polynomial is \(z^2-az+1\), where the integer \(a>2\) is at least three.
Consequently
\[
                  M(P')\ge\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
                  M(P)\ge\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}.                       \tag{8}
\]
Thus the quadratic alternative cannot persist along an input sequence whose measures tend to one.

<a id="primitive-quotient"></a>
## A maximal proper block gives a primitive quotient

A block for a transitive permutation group is a nonempty subset whose translates are either equal or disjoint.
Choose a proper block \(A\) for \(H\) of largest possible size.
When \(H\) is imprimitive, this size exceeds one, and its translates give a nontrivial invariant partition as in the statement.

The induced action on those parts is primitive.
Otherwise a nontrivial block in the part action is a proper union of at least two parts.
Its union as a subset of the original labels is an \(H\)-block, because translates of that union inherit equality or disjointness from the part action.
It is a larger proper block than \(A\), a contradiction.

For \(k\ge3\), the stabilizer interface proved above transfers precisely this primitive quotient action to the descended real circuit.
For \(k=2\), the construction has already reached the Salem or quadratic alternative.
There is no repeated multiplicative cost: every input in this imprimitive class has such a one-step output satisfying \(\log M(P')\le\log M(P)\), independently of \(e,k,n\).

In particular an input of least degree among all algebraic numbers with logarithmic measure in \((0,\log M(P)]\) cannot have this imprimitive exterior-parent action.
The output has strictly smaller degree, positive measure, and no larger measure.

<a id="four-labels"></a>
## Four exterior labels: the primitive alternative

Now let \(e=4\), with no further assumption on \(H\).
If \(H\) is imprimitive, its only possible nontrivial partition has two parts of size two.
The preceding two-part construction applies.

Suppose instead that \(H\) is primitive.
There are three perfect matchings on four labels.
The action of \(H\) on these matchings is transitive: a nontransitive action on a three-element set has a fixed point, and a fixed perfect matching would be an invariant partition into two pairs, contrary to primitivity.

Consider the squared pair products \(x_i^2x_j^2\), \(i<j\), in every parent.
There are six distinct values per parent, also distinct across parents, by the relation-lattice argument used in [the four-label calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#four-descent).
Complementary pairs give inverse values, so each perfect matching labels one inverse pair of products.
A common negative orientation sign exchanges a pair with its complement; it does not change its matching.

Take a Galois orbit of one of these products from the exterior parent.
Its intersection with that parent is the orbit under the parent stabilizer.
On the three matchings this action is transitive, so it has the same nonzero number of values above each matching.
That number is one or two.

The one-value possibility is impossible for an algebraic unit.
To see this, write \(t_1,\ldots,t_4\) for the exterior oriented logs.
The three forms
\[
               t_1+t_2,\qquad t_1+t_3,\qquad t_1+t_4                \tag{9}
\]
are rationally independent.
Indeed, their span contains \(t_1\), obtained as half their sum, and then every other \(t_i\), while the circuit gives rational rank three to the four \(t_i\).
If the orbit took one value above each matching, its exterior-parent contributions to the logarithm of its absolute norm would be one signed choice of twice each form in (9).
Their sum cannot be zero.
Every conjugate in another parent has modulus one and contributes zero.
This contradicts the absolute norm one of an algebraic unit.

The orbit therefore contains both values above each matching, hence all six values in the exterior parent.
Transitivity on parents gives all six values in every parent.
Thus their polynomial is irreducible of degree \(6n=3m/2\), and it is reciprocal by complementary pairing.
Exactly three of its roots are exterior.
The proof has not assumed independent inversion or a full signed \(S_4\) action.

The [exact pair-product computation](Yin_C_G0345_v299.md#four-descent) now applies to this complete orbit:
\[
                  \log M(P')=4\max_i|t_i|\le2\log M(P).            \tag{10}
\]
Its three positive exterior logarithms are rationally independent, by (9) after choosing their signs.
If \(\omega=1\), repeat the orbit argument for unsquared pair products.
Complementary pairs are still actual inverses, the same norm contradiction forces all six values per parent, and
\[
                  \log M(P')=2\max_i|t_i|\le\log M(P).             \tag{11}
\]
The exterior roots remain real, though they need not all be positive in this unsquared case.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Comparison and the remaining primitive class

The descent uses an invariant partition inside a real circuit parent and the common inversion sign forced by its relation.
Imprimitivity of the inverse-pair action alone is insufficient: the [independent-flip example](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v20.md#nonreal-block-example) has trivial proper-subfield norms and no smaller-degree monomial witness.
Here the circuit relation forbids independent signs within a parent and prevents every proper-part product from being torsion.

The [whole-block product descent](Yin_C_G0345_v271.md#descent), under its bounded-measure and sufficiently-large-degree hypotheses, gives a lower-degree witness when its canonical whole-block product is nontorsion.
The whole products used here are signs, and hence give no such witness.
The present partial products instead use a preserved refinement of a circuit parent, with exact exterior counts and the quotient action recorded.
The [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) is also different: a circuit of size at least three has no two-label relation, whereas that compression uses a torsion quotient of two roots.
The [commensurable-log theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement) and its [product construction](Yin_C_G0345_v66.md#statement) have logarithmic rank one; the input here has rank \(e-1\ge2\).

For a hypothetical sequence in the imprimitive real-circuit class with \(M(P)\to1\), maximal-block descent gives another sequence with measure tending to one.
After excluding the quadratic case by (8), the outputs are Salem numbers or real circuits with primitive unsigned exterior-parent action.
The exterior count of the latter may still grow.
For four exterior roots, the primitive branch instead gives three real exterior roots with independent positive logarithms and measure tending to one.
In each case the formulas count every exterior root of the output polynomial and give its exact Mahler measure.

No input sequence tending to one is constructed here.
Nor are arbitrary reciprocal units reduced to this circuit class: independent exterior logarithms, more general relation spaces and nonreal exterior signatures remain outside the general descent statement.
Thus the result removes an imprimitive obstruction within this unrestricted-degree class, without resolving G0001.

The coordinating agent proposed the imprimitive partial-product descent.
The exact signed orbit, quotient interface, two-part signs and primitive four-label norm argument were developed and checked in collaborating author work.
No independent review is asserted here.
