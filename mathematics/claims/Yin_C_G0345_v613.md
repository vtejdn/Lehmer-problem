# Integral denominator costs and an explicit excluded coefficient prefix

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound allowing rational numerators

Keep the hyperbolic reciprocal polynomial \(P\), the modulus \(u=|P|\), and \(s_n(P)\) from the [finite loss obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#statement).
Take nonzero \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(|B(0)|=1\), remove any initial monomial from \(A\), and put
\[
 \ell=\int D\!\left(\frac{|A|^2}{u|B|^2}\right)d\mu,\qquad
 T=m(P)+2m(B)+\ell .
\]
Values at the finitely many zeros of \(B\) do not affect the integral.
Then for every \(n\ge1\)
\[
  2m(B)+\ell\ \ge\ \frac{2}{n}\log^+s_n(P),\qquad
  \sum_{j<n}\big|[X^j] A^2/(P B^2)\big|^2\ \le\ 16e^{2nT}.
\]
In particular the infimum of \(2m(B)+\ell\) over these rational choices is zero exactly when an integer polynomial numerator already attains zero loss with \(B=1\).
The same statement holds if the choices are confined to coprime transfer-admissible numerators and monic cyclotomic denominators, with \(B=1\) allowed.
For the explicit length-504 prefix [given below](#example), every such \(A,B\) satisfies \(T\ge\log(17/16)/1008\).
This last inequality excludes simultaneous vanishing of the input measure and the denominator-adjusted loss throughout a nonempty infinite class of hyperbolic irreducible inputs.
It does not give a positive lower bound on \(m(P)\) alone.

<a id="boundary"></a>
## Boundary poles and integral Taylor coefficients

The [analytic multiplication bound](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#compression) remains valid for rational functions \(f\) analytic in the open disk with possible poles on its boundary.
Apply it to \(f(rz)\) and take \(r\uparrow1\), which gives coefficientwise convergence at each fixed depth.
For each boundary pole \(\zeta\) and \(r\ge1/2\), the identity \(|re^{it}-\zeta|^2=(1-r)^2+r|e^{it}-\zeta|^2\) supplies an integrable dominating logarithm for its contribution to \(\log^+|f(re^{it})|\).
Numerators and the other denominator factors cause no unbounded positive contribution on this annulus after canceling common factors.
Dominated convergence proves the required limit of the circle integral.

Form the finite Blaschke product \(I\) from the interior zeros of \(B\) with multiplicity, normalized by \(I(0)>0\).
Jensen's formula and \(|B(0)|=1\) give \(I(0)=e^{-m(B)}\), including when \(B\) has circle zeros.
As in the [integer prefix estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v609.md#poles), \(L_n(I)\) is a contraction and \(\|L_n(I)^{-1}\|\le e^{nm(B)}\) by its determinant.
Let \(G\) be the exterior spectral factor with \(|G|^2=4u\) used to define \(s_n(P)\).
Applying the boundary-pole estimate to \(IA/(BG)\) gives \(\|L_n(A/(BG))\|\le e^{n(m(B)+\ell/2)}\).
The Taylor series of \(A/B\) has integral coefficients and nonzero constant, so its prefix is a candidate in \(s_n(P)\).
This proves the first inequality.
If there is no pointwise integer certificate then some \(s_n(P)>1\), whereas any such certificate gives zero penalty with \(B=1\) and is [automatically admissible](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#coprimality).

For the second inequality clear the interior zeros of \(P B^2\) with a Blaschke product whose value at zero is \(e^{-m(P)-2m(B)}\).
Apply the same estimate to the resulting analytic function times \(A^2/(4P B^2)\), whose positive logarithm integrates to at most \(\ell\).
Inverting the truncated Blaschke matrix and taking the squared first-column norm gives the asserted bound.
The coefficients of \(H=A^2/(P B^2)\) are integral, with \(h_0=A(0)^2\), because \(P(0)=1\).
Moreover \(P H=(A/B)^2\) holds in \(\mathbb Z[[X]]\).
Thus the arithmetic square-prefix tests apply to this full denominator range.
For the [signed cyclotomic specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#denominator), \(m(B)=0\) and \(T\) is exactly the measure of the curve before specialization.

<a id="example"></a>
## A prefix forcing at least seventeen units of squared coefficient sum

Take
\[
 S=\{0,1,3,7,12,20,30,44,65,80,96,122,147,181,203,251\},\qquad
 R(Y)=\sum_{j\in S}Y^j.
\]
All \(120\) positive differences of the sixteen members of \(S\) are distinct.
This finite check is reproduced by testing that the set of \(b-a\) over \(a,b\in S,\ a<b\) has cardinality \(120\).
Prescribe \(P(X)\equiv1+X R(X^2)\bmod(2,X^{504})\).
Suppose the first 504 coefficients of \(H\) had squared sum at most sixteen.
If \(|A(0)|=2\), then \(H=4\bmod X^{504}\).
The integral series \(V=A/B\) satisfies \(V^2=4P\bmod X^{504}\), so reducing modulo two forces all its coefficients of indices \(0,\ldots,251\) to be even.
Consequently \(P\) is an integer square modulo \(X^{252}\), contradicting its odd linear coefficient.
The energy bound excludes all larger constant magnitudes.

For \(|A(0)|=1\) write the binary prefix of \(H\) as \(H_0(X^2)+X H_1(X^2)\), with each polynomial of degree below 252 and \(H_0(0)=1\).
Since \(P H=V^2\), vanishing of the odd terms gives \(H_1=R H_0\bmod Y^{252}\).
The constant of \(H_0\) contributes all sixteen positions \(S\).
Every other monomial \(Y^j\) of \(H_0\) contributes a translate \(S+j\) meeting \(S\) at most once by uniqueness of positive differences.
Writing \(\mathrm{wt}\) for the number of nonzero binary coefficients, one obtains
\[
 \mathrm{wt}(H_1)\ge 16-(\mathrm{wt}(H_0)-1).
\]
All original positions in \(S\) lie below the truncation point.
At least seventeen odd coefficients are therefore required in \(H\), contradicting the squared sum bound.
The sum is an integer and is at least seventeen in every case, which gives the stated lower bound on \(T\).

<a id="realize"></a>
## Irreducible hyperbolic representatives

For each \(h\ge504\), a reciprocal irreducible polynomial of degree \(2h\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) is the minimal polynomial of an element of order \(3^h+1\) in \(\mathbb F_{3^{2h}}^*\).
The multiplicative order of 3 modulo \(3^h+1\) is exactly \(2h\): below \(h\) this follows from size, and \(3^{h+j}\equiv-3^j\) for \(0\le j<h\) excludes the remaining smaller positive exponents.
The inverse is a Frobenius conjugate and the roots occur in distinct inverse pairs, so the polynomial is monic reciprocal with constant one.
Lift its coefficients to a monic reciprocal integer polynomial with constant one, prescribing the displayed binary prefix simultaneously by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Altering the central coefficient by a sufficiently large positive multiple of six makes \(X^{-h}P(X)\) strictly positive on the circle by domination of all other Laurent coefficients.
This preserves both residue conditions, and irreducibility follows by reduction modulo three.
The construction realizes the excluded prefix in every stated degree, without a small-measure assertion for its representatives.
