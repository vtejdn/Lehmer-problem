# A uniform conductor and degree bound for cyclotomic coefficient balancing

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite family of allowed cyclotomic factors

There is an absolute \(C\) with the following property.
For every nonzero real polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\ge1\), every integer \(M\ge2\), and every integer \(a\ge1\), there is a monic product \(H\) of cyclotomic polynomials of conductors at most \(M\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
    \deg H &\le C(ad M^3+M^2),\\
    \log\|P^aH\|_1
      &\le a\,m(P)+ C a d\,M^{-1/64}
                         +C M^2+C\log(2+adM).
 \end{split}                                                           \tag{1}
\]
No bounds on the coefficients or roots of \(P\) enter the constant.
This quantifies the [fixed-polynomial cyclotomic coefficient rate](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#statement), allowing the input polynomial to vary.
In particular for a sequence of nontorsion unit minima of degrees \(d\to\infty\) and measures tending to zero one may take \(M=\lceil d^{128}\rceil\), \(a=\lceil d^{257}\rceil\).
Then \(a^{-1}\log\|P^aH\|_1\to0\), with only the given noncyclotomic irreducible factor and a degree bound polynomial in \(d\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Uniform estimates with errors in the finitely many integral tests

Let \(\mu\) be an even probability measure on the unit circle with
\[
                   \int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge -M^{-2}
                         \quad (1\le n\le M).
\]
Write \(f(z)=\log|1-z|\), \(A(k)=\int f(z^k)\,d\mu(z)\), and put \(\xi=M^{-1}\).
For \(k j\le M\) all the logarithms involved are integrable.
By factoring \(X^k-1\) and \((X^{kj}-1)/(X^k-1)\) we have \(A(k)\ge-\xi\) and \(A(kj)-A(k)\ge-\xi\), since each product uses at most \(M\) distinct factors.

Use the [triangular log average](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#power) \(F_L(z)=\sum_{j=1}^L p_j f(z^j)\), where the weights are positive, proportional to \(L+1-j\) and sum to one.
It satisfies \(\sup F_L\ll\log(2L)/L\).
For \(0<s\le1\) write \(b_s(z)=\log|1-e^{-s}z|\).
Whenever \(kL\le M\) and \(L\ge2\), we claim
\[
       -\xi-s/2\ \le\ \int b_s(z^k)\,d\mu(z)
               \ \le\ \xi+C\bigl(\log(2L)/L+sL\bigr).                  \tag{2}
\]
The lower bound follows from \(b_s\ge f-s/2\).
For the upper bound use
\[
              F_L(z)-f(z)+b_s(z)\le C\log(2L)/L+sL .
\]
If \(|1-z|\ge1/L\), then \(b_s-f\le\log(1+s/|1-z|)\le sL\).
Otherwise \(F_L-f\le\log L\) by \(|1-z^j|\le j|1-z|\), while \(b_s\le\log(1/L+s)\).
These inequalities are used away from the null set where some of the finitely many logarithms at \(z^k\) is singular.
Integrating and using \(\int(F_L(z^k)-f(z^k))\,d\mu\ge-\xi\) proves (2).

For \(0<\delta<1/8\) and \(k/\delta\le M\), choose \(L=\lfloor1/(2\delta)\rfloor\).
On \(|1-z^k|<\delta\) we have \(F_L(z^k)\le-\log2\).
Its integral is at least \(-\xi\), so
\[
               \mu\{|1-z^k|<\delta\}\ll\delta\log(2/\delta)+\xi .
\]
Let \(\mu_j\) be the image under the \(j\)-th power and measure angular distances in \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).
At any center and radius \(x\le1/2\) with \(x\ge C_0 j/M\), this gives \(\mu_j(B(\theta,x))\ll x^{1/3}\) with absolute constants.
Indeed the [Dirichlet arc argument](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#arcs) chooses \(k\le 2/\sqrt{x}\) taking the entire arc within chord distance \(C_1\sqrt x\) of 1.
The preceding inequality applies with power \(jk\) and \(\delta\) a sufficiently large multiple of \(\sqrt x\), since \(jk/\delta\ll j/x\).
For large radii the trivial mass bound suffices, and \(\xi\ll x\) in the indicated range.
The [Fejér energy estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#fourier) therefore gives
\[
                     \sum_{n\le N,\ n\ge1}|\widehat\mu(jn)|^2
                            \ll N^{2/3}\qquad (jN\le c_0 M)              \tag{3}
\]
for an absolute \(c_0>0\), using the kernel \(K_{2N}\).
Only the ball of radius \(1/N\) and larger dyadic shells occur in that proof.
Here \(\widehat\mu(n)=\int z^n\,d\mu(z)\).

<a id="inversion"></a>
## Recovering finitely many ordinary Fourier coefficients

Take \(R=\lfloor M^{1/4}\rfloor\), \(Y=\lfloor R^{1/2}\rfloor\), and \(L=\lfloor R^{1/4}\rfloor\), working first with sufficiently large \(M\).
For every \(1\le k\le R\), absolute convergence of the exponentially damped logarithm series gives
\[
   -e^{-1/R}\operatorname{Re}\widehat\mu(k)
     =\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\mu_{\rm ar}(j)}{j}
             \int b_{j/R}(z^{kj})\,d\mu(z),
\]
where \(\mu_{\rm ar}\) is the arithmetic Möbius function and for \(j>R\) the same definition of \(b_s\) is used.
Indeed at total frequency \(km\) the inner divisor sum is \(\sum_{j\mid m}\mu_{\rm ar}(j)\).
For \(j\le Y\), the bound (2) applies at power \(kj\), since \(kjL\le M\).
This part of the sum has modulus \(O(R^{-1/4}(1+\log R)^2+\xi(1+\log R))\).
For \(j>Y\), expand the series again.
Its modulus is bounded by
\[
                     \sum_{m>Y}\frac{\tau(m)}m
                                 e^{-m/R}|\widehat\mu(km)| ,
\]
with \(\tau\) the divisor-counting function.
For dyadic blocks up to \(R^2\), (3) and the [divisor square-sum bound](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#fourier) bound this by \(O(Y^{-1/6}(1+\log(2Y))^{3/2})\).
Indeed \(2kR^2\le c_0 M\) for all sufficiently large \(M\).
The remaining tail is \(O(R e^{-R/2})\) by \(\tau(m)\le m\).
Evenness makes \(\widehat\mu(k)\) real, so uniformly for \(k\le R\),
\[
                    |\widehat\mu(k)|\ll M^{-1/48}(1+\log M)^2 .
\]

For a reflected root \(w\) of \(P\) in the closed disk, replace its boundary logarithm \(\log|1-w\bar z|\) by \(\log|1-rw\bar z|\) with \(r=\exp(-1/\sqrt R)\).
This majorizes the original up to an additive \(O(1/\sqrt R)\), because \(|(1-u)/(1-ru)|\le 2/(1+r)\) for \(|u|\le1\).
The smoothed logarithm has mean zero against arc length.
Its integral against \(\mu\) is bounded above by \(O(M^{-1/48}(1+\log M)^3+e^{-\sqrt R/2})\), by the Fourier bound through \(R\) and absolute summation of the remaining series with weights \(r^k/k\).
The reflection constants and leading coefficient contribute exactly \(m(P)\).
Summing over all roots proves
\[
                     \int\log|P|\,d\mu\le m(P)+C d M^{-1/64}.           \tag{4}
\]
Larger absolute constants cover bounded \(M\), using the elementary pointwise upper bound \(m(P)+d\log2\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Controlling the integer cofactor as well as the circle norm

Let \(E=C d M^{-1/64}>0\) be the error in (4), with \(C\) chosen as there.
There is no even probability measure having \(\int \log|P|\ge m(P)+2E\) and all the first \(M\) cyclotomic integrals at least \(-M^{-2}\).
Truncate these \(M+1\) functions from below at the same level \(-J\).
For some finite \(J\) there is still no measure meeting these requirements for the truncated functions.
Otherwise the [compactness and monotone truncation argument](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#dual) gives a measure contradicting (4).
The functions are even, so it suffices to work throughout with even measures.
Separate their compact convex set of truncated moment vectors strictly from the orthant with the indicated lower endpoints.
The separating normal is nonnegative and has positive first component by the Haar moments.
Normalize that component to one and let \(w_1,\ldots,w_M\ge0\) be the others.
Taking symmetrized point masses and then dropping the truncations gives
\[
    \sup_{z\in\mathbb T}\left(\log|P(z)|+
                        \sum_{n:w_n>0}w_n\log|\Phi_n(z)|\right)
                  \le m(P)+2E-M^{-2}\sum_n w_n .
\]
The supremum is at least \(m(P)\) by its Haar mean, hence \(\sum_n w_n\le2M^2E\).
Now take \(H=\prod_{w_n>0}\Phi_n^{\lceil a w_n\rceil}\).
Since \(\deg\Phi_n\le M\), its degree is at most \(2aM^3E+M^2\).
Each \(\log\|\Phi_n\|_{\mathbb T}\le\varphi(n)\log2\) by its roots on the circle.
Rounding costs at most \(M^2\log2\) in the log supremum.
The [coefficient conversion](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#integer) costs at most half the log of \(1+\deg(P^a H)\).
These bounds yield (1) with a common absolute constant.
