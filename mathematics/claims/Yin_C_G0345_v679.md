# The size and residue classes of the binary collision carry set

<a id="statement"></a>
## Lower bounds for the actual returning carries

Let \(\alpha\) be a hyperbolic algebraic unit of degree \(d\), put \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and write \(m=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Two independent fair binary words have difference digits \(\delta_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\) with probabilities \(1/4,1/2,1/4\).
Define \(S\subset R\) to consist of the states visited on finite paths from zero back to zero under \(c_{j+1}=\alpha^{-1}(c_j+\delta_j)\).
For \(q\ge2\) let \(S_q\) be its image in \(R/qR\).
For every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 |S|^{1/d}&\ge \frac{e^{-km}}{a_k},
 &a_k&=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k},\\
 |S_q|^{1/d}&\ge \frac{e^{-km}}{a_{k,q}},
 &a_{k,q}&=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\cos^{2k}(\pi j/q).
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(|S_2|\ge(2e^{-m})^d\).
Along any sequence with \(m\to0\),
\[
 \liminf \sqrt m\,|S|^{1/d}\ge\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\mathrm e}},
 \qquad \frac{|S_q|^{1/d}}q\longrightarrow1
               \quad\text{whenever }mq^2\longrightarrow0.             \tag{2}
\]
The second assertion controls the logarithm of the occupied fraction per degree.
All these bounds apply without an assumption on degrees of powers of \(\alpha\).

For each input there is a single \(c\in S\) and a family \(F\subset\{-1,0,1\}^d\) of admissible difference words from \(c\) of total probability at least \(e^{-dm}\).
In the basis \(1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}\) their vectors belong to \(\alpha^d S-c\).
As \(m\to0\), they satisfy \(\liminf d^{-1}\log|F|\ge\tfrac32\log2\).
There is a set of at least \(d(1/4-\sqrt{m/2})\) basis coordinates on which their projections contain every assignment of the three possible digits.
Thus the modulus-two bound counts distinct carry residues, while the ternary assertion concerns coordinate projections of representatives from one translated set.

<a id="survival"></a>
## The mass of paths staying in the return set

The [two-sided carry bounds](Yin_C_G0212_v1.md#carry-state-bound) place \(S\) in a bounded region of the Minkowski lattice, so it is finite.
Write \(p_n\) for the probability of an \(n\)-step return to zero.
This is the collision probability of two independent binary sums.
Cauchy–Schwarz and the [norm-grid support bound](Yin_C_G0281_v1.md#norm-grid-support-bound) give \(p_n\ge(4n)^{-d}e^{-(n-1)m}\).
For fixed \(N\), let \(M_N\) be the largest probability over \(c\in S\) that an \(N\)-step path from \(c\) stays throughout in \(S\).
Every return from zero stays in \(S\), so iteration of the Markov property gives \(p_{\ell N}\le M_N^\ell\).
Taking \(\ell\)-th roots and letting \(\ell\) tend to infinity proves \(M_N\ge e^{-Nm}\).

An unrestricted path from \(c\in S\) whose endpoint is also in \(S\) stays in \(S\) at every intermediate time.
Indeed one can prepend a path from zero to \(c\) and append a path taking its endpoint to zero.
Thus survival from \(c\) is exactly the event that \(\alpha^{-N}(c+\sum_{i<N}\delta_i\alpha^i)\in S\).
For \(N=d\) choose a state attaining \(M_d\) and let \(F\) consist of all its surviving words.
The degree makes the \(d\) digit coordinates unique as elements of \(R\), proving the claimed containment.
Parity of a difference digit is uniform, and reduction of the power basis identifies \(R/2R\) with \((\mathbb Z/2)^d\).
The survival mass therefore gives at least \(2^d e^{-dm}\) distinct parity vectors of words in \(F\), and the affine endpoint map preserves their distinctness modulo two.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Atom bounds in the order and its finite quotients

At \(N=kd\), use the [degree-block Fourier moment](Yin_C_G0365_v1.md#uniform-small-unweighted-return-tail) for the unrestricted displacement.
The following argument also gives its finite-quotient form.
Take \(G=R\) or \(R/qR\) as an additive group, write its dual in the power basis, and give the dual Haar probability.
For a \(d\)-digit block starting at exponent zero the characteristic function in these coordinates is \(\Phi(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^d\cos^2(\pi\theta_i)\).
For the successive \(d\)-digit blocks each characteristic function is the composition of \(\Phi\) with the dual of multiplication by a power of \(\alpha^d\).
These are Haar-preserving automorphisms because \(\alpha\) is a unit of \(R\).
Fourier inversion and Hölder's inequality with \(k\) equal exponents bound every atom of the complete \(kd\)-digit displacement by \(\int\Phi^k\).
For \(k=1\) the bound follows directly by integration.
This integral is \(a_k^d\) on the continuous torus and \(a_{k,q}^d\) on the finite dual, by the constant coefficient of \(\cos^{2k}\) and by direct summation, respectively.

Choose a state attaining \(M_{kd}\) in the actual order.
Every path counted by survival ends in \(S\), and in the quotient its endpoint belongs to \(S_q\).
The affine change from displacement to endpoint preserves the maximum atom in either group.
Bounding the mass of those endpoints by their number times the maximum atom proves (1).
In the finite group the probability estimate comes from paths surviving in the actual core before reduction.

Taking \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\) for sufficiently small \(m\) and using \(a_k\sim1/\sqrt{\pi k}\) proves the first part of (2).
For the second, symmetry and \(\cos x\le e^{-x^2/2}\) for \(0\le x\le\pi/2\) give
\[
 1\le q a_{k,q}\le1+2\sum_{j\ge1}\exp(-\pi^2 k j^2/q^2).
\]
If \(mq^2\to0\), take \(k=\lceil q/\sqrt m\rceil\), so \(k/q^2\to\infty\) and \(km\to0\).
The sum tends to zero, and \(|S_q|\le q^d\) completes the limit.
The proof counts actual lattice points in the bounded Minkowski region.
A contradiction to shrinking measure would also require an incompatible upper estimate for those points.

<a id="words"></a>
## Signed words and their coordinate projections

Write \(p=(1/2,1/4,1/4)\) in the order \(0,-1,+1\).
For each fixed \(u>0\), Hölder's inequality applied to the product measure of \(F\) gives
\[
 e^{-dm}\le |F|^{u/(1+u)}
             \left(\sum_{a}p_a^{1+u}\right)^{d/(1+u)}.
\]
Taking logarithms and then the lower limit as \(m\to0\) bounds \(d^{-1}\log|F|\) from below in the limit by \(-u^{-1}\log\sum_a p_a^{1+u}\).
Letting \(u\downarrow0\) gives the asserted entropy \(\tfrac32\log2\).

Say that a coordinate set is shattered when the projection of \(F\) onto it contains every ternary word.
Let \(v\) be the maximum cardinality of a shattered set.
In each fiber in one coordinate replace the symbols present by an initial segment of \(0,-1,+1\) of the same cardinality.
This increases or preserves the product mass and cannot create a newly shattered set.
For a set excluding that coordinate the projection is unchanged.
For a set including it, each pattern on its other coordinates must occur with the last symbol in a compressed fiber, and that entire fiber was already full before compression.
Iterating the compressions produces a family closed under replacing any symbol by a smaller one, since every nontrivial compression decreases the sum of the symbol ranks over all words.
Every word of this family has at most \(v\) copies of the last symbol, since those coordinates are shattered by closure.
Its product mass is consequently at most \(\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(d,1/4)\le v\}\).
If \(v/d<1/4\), the binomial lower-tail bound gives
\[
           e^{-dm}\le\exp[-2d(1/4-v/d)^2],
\]
which proves the stated dimension bound.
The other coordinates of a realizing word can depend on the prescribed ternary assignment.
