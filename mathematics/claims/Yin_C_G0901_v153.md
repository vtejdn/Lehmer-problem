# Exponentially many different angular directions carry the log mass of a rapidly growing Mahler iterate

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform bounds for individual lines of arbitrary direction

Let \(A=M(P)>1\) for a nonzero integer polynomial, \(m=\log A\), \(\mu=\log M(A)\), and \(\mathcal H(m)=m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\).
For every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(6\sqrt[3]4)\), on the branch \(M(A)>A^2\) and for sufficiently small \(m\) in terms of \(\eta\), every real line \(\Lambda\) through the origin in \(\mathbb C\) satisfies
\[
              \sum_{\sigma A\in\Lambda}|\log|\sigma A||
                            \ \le\ e^{-\eta\mathcal H(m)}\mu.             \tag{1}
\]
The line may depend on \(P\); all conjugates are counted once.
Thus capturing half the exterior log mass, or half the absolute interior log mass, requires at least \(\frac12e^{\eta\mathcal H(m)}\) distinct directions modulo \(\pi\).
On the other branch \(A\le M(A)\le A^2\), the positive real root \(A\) alone supplies at least half of the exterior log mass, and its reciprocal supplies at least half of the absolute interior log mass.
These two branches exhaust all sufficiently small positive \(m\).
The estimate concerns exact lines in the complex plane, without an arc-width assertion for arbitrary reducible inputs.

<a id="affine"></a>
## A bound for a specified value of the anti-invariant projection

For an active leading space \(W\) attached to a small compressed term of degree \(d=2ub\), write \(v\ne0\) for the projected class and \(c\) for complex conjugation.
Fix any target \(w\in W\).
On a coset supplying the independent local alternating actions and, in the signed case, the supported even flips, consider
\[
                            (1-c)gv=w.                                   \tag{2}
\]
The fraction satisfying (2) is at most \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}\) for all \(v\); when \(v\) has at least three exceptions in the [signed or oriented models](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#reduction), it is at most \(b^{-3/2+o(1)}\).
The error is uniform over targets and cosets at a fixed source measure ceiling.

For one signed coordinate \(E_i\), a zero target requires a real label and there are at most \(a=O(\sqrt{d\log(3d)})=b^{1/2+o(1)}\) of these in total.
A nonzero target fixes at most two possible destination labels within a specified destination block.
Indeed the unprojected vector is the signed coordinate minus its image under \(c\), marking a singleton or the pair of labels exchanged by \(c\).
Subtracting the block mean in the oriented model does not obscure those labels: the nonzero sparse combination has at most two nonzero entries per block and hence is uniquely determined by its sum-zero projection for \(b>4\).
The overall sign does not change the support.
With exactly two exceptional coordinates condition on the signed image of one, then apply the one-coordinate bound to the other at the translated and rescaled target, with at least \(b-1\) available labels.
The [stabilizer and sign correction](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#two) ensure this uniformity also across different blocks.

For at least three exceptions take two independent draws \(g,gh\) on the same coset \(g_0H\), with \(g\) uniform there and \(h\) uniform and independent in its subgroup \(H\).
The square of the largest atom in (2) is at most the probability of equality of the two draws' projections, namely the probability that \(cg(v-hv)=g(v-hv)\).
In applying this argument one may take cosets of a subgroup with independent local actions, and average the uniform bounds if a larger subgroup is given.
Let \(L=\lceil(\log b)^2\rceil\).
When every block of \(v\) has fewer than \(L\) exceptions, their total \(e\) is \(b^{o(1)}\).
Conditional on block transports of \(h\), the probability that at least \(j\) of its transported marks overlap the original marks is at most \(\binom ej(e/(b-e))^j\), by exposing a chosen ordered sublist of positions.
With exactly \(j\) overlaps, the difference has between \(2(e-j)\) and \(2e\) exceptions, since a position marked just once still differs from its block baseline.
In the oriented model the sum of the two transported baseline contributions is understood with the minus sign of the difference; it is the unique majority outside at most \(2e\) positions.
For a difference with \(k\le2e\) exceptions the [sparse involution count](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#sparse) bounds the probability of being fixed by \(c\) by \(b^{-(1/2-o(1))k}\), including \(k=0,1,2\) by the same fixed-point and transposition count.
Summing over \(0\le j\le e\) bounds the collision probability by \(b^{-(1-o(1))e}\).

If some source block of \(v\) has at least \(L\) exceptions, fix its destination under \(h\).
On that block alone \(hv\) has more than any fixed power of \(b\) equiprobable arrangements.
For signed vectors there are at least \(2^{L-1}\) by even flips.
For oriented vectors a union of coefficient colors has size between \(L/2\) and \(b/2\) by the [dense color argument](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#dense), giving at least \(\binom b{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\) images.
Among them at most \(O(b^{12})\) can make the difference on that block have at most five exceptions: choose their positions and values from at most \(2b\) possible transported coefficients per position.
In the oriented case fix also its baseline difference, for which at most \(2b^2\) choices arise from a single ordinary position.
Thus with probability \(1-O(b^{-4})\) the difference has at least six exceptions in total.
Then the sparse bound for a difference with every block below \(L\), or the dense bound \(O(b^{-3+o(1)})\) when one is above that cutoff, gives collision probability \(O(b^{-3+o(1)})\) after averaging.
This proves both claimed bounds for (2).

For a noncoordinate \(v\), the fraction of absolute log mass of \(\lambda(gv)\), \(\lambda=\log|\cdot|\), at (2) is at most \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}\) on averaging over the whole group.
With three or more exceptions this follows by multiplying the probability bound by \(\dim W\le ub\), using the transitive maximum-log inequality.
With two exceptions bound each coordinate's absolute-log contribution by conditioning on that coordinate and using the one-coordinate probability for the other.
The lower first moment in the [two-position computation](Yin_C_G0901_v144.md#two) absorbs their sum without dependence on their scalars.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Separating the fields that can move independently

Use the [largest active space and conditional averages](Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#conditional) of the class of \(A\), with the notation there: \(L=\log|gA|=L_1+L'\), where \(L_1=\lambda(gv)\) comes from the largest space and \(L'\) from all other components.
The inequalities \(\mathbb E|L'|\le\mathbb E|L|\) and \(\mathbb E|L_1|\le2\mathbb E|L|\) hold.
If the line \(\Lambda\) contains any conjugates of \(A\), all their ratios to one selected such conjugate are real.
Thus \((1-c)g[A]\) and each of its constituent projections have a prescribed common value on this event.
By the relative alternating action fixing the other active fields, the contribution of \(|L'|\) to \(\mathbb E(|L|{\bf1}_{gA\in\Lambda})\) in the triangle bound is at most \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}\mathbb E|L|\).
The weighted bound just proved gives the same estimate for \(|L_1|\) if \(v\) is noncoordinate.
If some other active label field is not contained in the largest splitting field, one may instead fix that largest field and apply the conditional bound on the other space, since the image there contains its minimal normal alternating subgroup as in the cited conditional calculation.
This bounds the \(|L_1|\) contribution by \(2b_j^{-1/2+o(1)}\mathbb E|L|\) using that term's block size.
The [block-size inversion](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#minimal) gives the claimed exponential decay in all these cases, taking an intermediate exponent to absorb absolute factors including the conversion from \(\mathbb E|L|\) to \(\mu\).

<a id="fiber"></a>
## A quantitative point bound on the remaining angular fibers

In the remaining case \(v\) is a coordinate and all other active label fields are inside the largest splitting field.
Put \(D=\deg A\), \(q=D/d\), and \(\delta=\mathbb E(|L|{\bf1}_{gA\in\Lambda})/\mathbb E|L|\).
The field and height arguments of the [nested-field](Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#nested) and [integral remainder](Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#integral) calculations depend only on that algebraic case.
They give an actual real primitive unit \(r\), integers \(j,e\) with \(j\le2,\ e\log M(r)\le m\), and a unit \(C=A/r^{je}\) in \(\mathbb Q(A)\), with
\[
              \mathbb E|L_1|\le8m/d,\qquad
              h(C)\le\mu/D+2m/d,\qquad
              \mu\gg d/(m(\log(3d))^{212}).
\]
The last bound uses the fast branch.
The conditional estimate on \(L'\) shows \(\delta\le b^{-1/2+o(1)}+4mq/\mu\).
If \(\delta\ge d^{-1/5}\), then \(q\ge d^{4/5-o(1)}\) and \(h(C)\ll m/(\delta d)\le O(d^{-4/5})\).

The [complete relative fibers](Yin_C_G0901_v148.md#fibers) for \(C\) above any fixed image of \(r\) have degree \(q\) over a block field of degree \(t_0\le2u\).
For normalized phase sums \(S_k\) on each such fiber the same reflected energy calculation gives, uniformly for \(1\le k\le q\),
\[
       |S_k|\ll\sqrt{k(t_0h(C)+t_0\log(3q)/q)}+k t_0 h(C).
\]
Indeed the single energy term costs a factor \(e^{k/q}\le e\), and replacing each reflected power by its phase power costs at most \(k|\log|y||\) per root.
The argument of \(A\) on the fiber differs from the argument of \(C\) by a constant rotation.
Apply the order \(H=\lfloor d^{1/4}\rfloor\) Fejér kernel at either of the two prescribed angles for \(C\).
It bounds their normalized count by \(2/H+O(\max_{1\le k<H}|S_k|)\).
Here \(t_0=d^{o(1)}\) and \(\log(3q)/q\le d^{-4/5+o(1)}\), by monotonicity on \(q\ge d^{4/5-o(1)}\).
The resulting conditional probability is \(d^{-1/4+o(1)}\) or less, uniformly over angles and fibers.
Since \(L_1\) is fixed on each fiber, (2) of the conditional calculation in v148 bounds \(\delta\) by \(b^{-1/2+o(1)}+2d^{-1/4+o(1)}\), contradicting \(\delta\ge d^{-1/5}\).

If there is no other active term then \(D=d\) by the signed-coordinate orbit and strict Perron property, so \(q=1\).
The earlier estimate for \(\delta\) directly gives \(\delta\le d^{-1/2+o(1)}\) in that case.
Otherwise a smaller nested label field has \(b_j\le u\ll(\log(3d))^{212}\), by the same nested-field calculation.
Its own block-size inversion makes \(d^{-1/5}\) smaller than \(\exp(-C_1\mathcal H(m))\) for every fixed \(C_1>0\) at sufficiently small \(m\).
This proves (1) also in the nested case.
Finally \(A\) is reciprocal in the [low interval](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval) and its reciprocal is a conjugate, giving the two lower contributions there.
