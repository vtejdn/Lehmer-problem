# Finite inertia and the full alternating action on the cubic trace roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## Root fields along primitive directions

Put \(g(T)=T^3-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\) and \(A(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) for coprime positive \(a,b\).
Let \(\alpha\) be a root, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
At every finite prime each inertia automorphism of the splitting field of \(A\) moves at most twelve distinct roots in the whole polynomial.
Every intermediate field \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subseteq K\) satisfies \([K:E]\le6\).
If \(d>12\), the only subfield of \(K\) Galois over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(\mathbb Q\), and \(K\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}=\mathbb Q\).

For all sufficiently large \(N=\max(a,b)\), each nontorsion irreducible factor is reciprocal, and the natural Galois action on its \(n=d/2\) inverse pairs contains \(A_n\).
Equivalently, the normal closure of the trace field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) has group \(A_n\) or \(S_n\) on its \(n\) embeddings.
Its group after base change to \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is exactly \(A_n\).
Writing \(L\) for the splitting field of that individual factor, one has
\[
 \frac{n!}{2}\le [L\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}:\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\le[L:\mathbb Q]\le 2^n n!.
\]
These conclusions apply to every part of the [exterior-pair partition](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition).

<a id="inertia"></a>
## A degree-six bound on all collisions at one prime

Work first over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
A root \(x\) of \(\overline A\) is nonzero and its coordinate \(t=x^a\) satisfies \(x^b=-g(t)/B(t)\), with nonzero numerator and denominator by \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\).
Since \((a,b)=1\), the two powers recover \(x\), so distinct roots have distinct \(t\)-coordinates.
In polynomial identities below write \(t=X^a\).
The logarithmic derivative gives
\[
 XB(t) A'(X)=(bB(t)+a tB'(t))A(X)+R(t),\qquad
 R(T)=aT(g'B-gB')-bgB .
\]
If \(\ell\mid a\), then \(A'(x)=b x^{b-1} B(t)\ne0\) at every root.
Otherwise the map \(X\mapsto X^a\) has nonzero derivative at \(x\).
Here \(R\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most six over the residue field.
If \(\ell\nmid b\) its constant coefficient is \(b\), and if \(\ell\mid b\) the polynomial \(g'B-gB'\) has constant coefficient \(-1\).
At a root of multiplicity \(m_x\), both terms on the two sides involving \(A,A'\) vanish to order at least \(m_x-1\), even when \(\ell\mid m_x\).
It follows that \(\operatorname{ord}_{t=x^a}R\ge m_x-1\), and summing over the distinct coordinates gives \(\sum_x(m_x-1)\le6\).

The roots of the monic polynomial \(-A\) are integral.
At a prime of its splitting field over \(\ell\), inertia fixes residues, so it can move roots only in clusters above residues of multiplicity at least two.
These clusters contain at most \(\sum_{m_x\ge2}m_x\le2\sum_x(m_x-1)\le12\) roots, counting even with multiplicity.
This also covers wild inertia and any repeated roots in characteristic zero.

<a id="subfields"></a>
## Intermediate fields and finite inertia

The conjugates of all finite inertia groups generate the rational Galois group of the splitting field.
Indeed the fixed field of the subgroup they generate is unramified at every finite prime, hence has absolute discriminant one.
Minkowski's discriminant bound excludes this in degree \(h>1\).
For the last implication, in signature \((r,s)\) the integer lattice has covolume \(2^{-s}\sqrt{|\Delta|}\), and the body \(\sum_{i=1}^r |x_i|+2\sum_{j=1}^s|z_j|\le t\) has volume \(2^r(\pi/2)^s t^h/h!\).
If \(|\Delta|=1\), then \(h^h/h!\ge2^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}>(4/\pi)^s\) for \(h>1\).
With \(t<h\) sufficiently close to \(h\), Minkowski's convex body theorem supplies a nonzero integer of absolute norm at most \((t/h)^h<1\), which is impossible.

For \(E\subseteq K\), restriction of the \(d\) embeddings of \(K\) partitions them into blocks of size \(e=[K:E]\), one per embedding of \(E\).
If \(E\ne\mathbb Q\), some finite inertia element moves a block, because the action on those blocks is transitive and the inertia groups generate.
It moves at least two blocks, hence at least \(2e\) embeddings of \(K\).
Each embedding is distinguished by its image of \(\alpha\), so \(2e\le12\).
If \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois and nontrivial, its nonidentity automorphisms act without fixed points on its embeddings.
An inertia element acting nontrivially there consequently moves all \(d\) embeddings of \(K\), forcing \(d\le12\).
Every finite subfield of \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is Galois, giving the asserted intersection.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## The single block on inverse pairs

For all primitive directions with \(N\to\infty\), one has \(\limsup m(A)\le\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) is the real zero of \(g\).
Indeed, each fixed nonzero character \((j,k)\) integrates to zero on \(z\mapsto(z^a,z^b)\) eventually, since \(ja+kb=0\) would make \((j,k)\) a multiple of \((b,-a)\).
By trigonometric approximation the integrals of continuous functions converge to their means on the two-dimensional torus.
Apply this to \(\log\max(\delta,|g(U)+VB(U)|)\) and then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).
Integration first over \(V\) gives the mean of \(\log\max(|g(U)|,|B(U)|)\), and \(|B(U)|=|g(U)|\) on the circle.
The resulting mean is \(m(g)=\log\theta\). [Cubic measure](Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#seed)
The reciprocal polynomial \(A\) would contain both a nonreciprocal factor and its distinct reversal, at total measure cost at least \(2\log\theta\) by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
This proves eventual reciprocity uniformly.
Every nontorsion factor degree tends to infinity uniformly by the [primitive-direction degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).

The graph \(g(U)+VB(U)=0\) is absolutely irreducible since \(g,B\) are coprime, and its support differences contain \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\).
Their projections therefore have gcd one.
The [torsion-quotient criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) shows that distinct nontorsion roots have nontorsion quotient for sufficiently large \(N\).
In a reciprocal factor the multiplicative labels of the [alternating block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) are thus exactly the inverse pairs.
The theorem applies with a fixed measure bound since \(M(A)\le\|A\|_2\le\sqrt8\), including coincident exponents.
Every block in its partition has size \(s\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), hence \(s>3\) eventually.
If a block of this partition were proper, its preimage in the root set of the factor would be a proper block of size \(2s\).
Its setwise stabilizer contains the stabilizer of a chosen root in the block, so its fixed field \(E\) satisfies \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subset K\) and \([K:E]=2s\).
The bound of six rules this out.
The alternating block theorem now puts the natural \(A_n\) inside the permutation image on the one block of all inverse pairs.

Restriction of the rational Galois group to the trace roots identifies that permutation image with the group of their splitting field.
Its maximal abelian quotient is trivial or the sign quotient when \(n\ge5\).
The intersection of a finite Galois extension with \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is its maximal abelian subextension, so the trace group becomes \(A_n\) over that base.
The upper bound for \([L:\mathbb Q]\) counts all permutations of pairs and all independent exchanges within pairs.
Thus the conclusion specifies the full trace action for each factor, while the number of rational factors and their assigned proportions of exterior pairs remain undetermined.
