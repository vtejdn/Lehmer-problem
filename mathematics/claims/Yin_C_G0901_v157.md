# Exact Mahler iteration for a product of two real Salem numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The trace-field indices determine the dynamics

Let \(x,y>1\) be real Salem numbers and put \(F_x=\mathbb Q(x+x^{-1})\), \(F_y=\mathbb Q(y+y^{-1})\) in their marked real embeddings.
If their root fields coincide then \(xy\) is a power of the same primitive Salem generator and is fixed by \(M\).
Otherwise put \(F=F_xF_y\), \(k_x=[F:F_x]\), \(k_y=[F:F_y]\).
For all positive integers \(s,t\) one has
\[
        M(x^s y^t)=\frac{x^{2k_xs}y^{2k_yt}}{\min(x^s,y^t)^2}.
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
This includes equal trace fields with different quadratic root fields.
For incomparable trace fields the iterates always diverge to infinity.
For equal trace fields the first measure is the square of the larger factor and is fixed thereafter.
For \(F_y\subsetneq F_x\) put \(k=[F_x:F_y]\), \(U=s\log x\), \(V=t\log y\).
If \(U\le V\), the next value is the fixed power \(y^{2kt}\).
If \(U>V\), the orbit diverges when \(k=2\) and stabilizes after finitely many steps when \(k>2\).
The case \(F_x\subsetneq F_y\) follows by exchanging the letters.

At sufficiently small positive \(\log(xy)\), distinct root fields always give \(M(xy)>(xy)^2\) and hence the [exponential lower bound for its second log measure as a polynomial input](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement), using the product of the two minima.
In the same regime equal trace fields and proper inclusions of index two are impossible for distinct root fields.
Thus near one the product of two distinct-field Salem values has an unbounded forward orbit exactly when the marked trace fields are incomparable.
By [local unique factorization](Yin_C_G0901_v156.md#statement) its first value \(xy\) cannot be realized by a single irreducible input, since its two factors supply distinct Salem atoms.
These assertions do not require that any Salem values actually approach one.

<a id="compositum"></a>
## Four lifts over the common totally real base

All positive powers of a real Salem number generate its root field, by uniqueness of the largest-modulus conjugate.
Over its totally real trace field the extension is quadratic, split just at the marked real place, with the involution taking the unit to its inverse. [Salem fields](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units)
Write \(K_x=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(K_y=\mathbb Q(y)\).
If these fields coincide, their maximal totally real trace fields agree by the [proper subfield property](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#subfields), and both units are positive powers of their primitive norm-one generator.

Otherwise \(K_xF\) and \(K_yF\) are quadratic over \(F\), since the two root fields have nonreal embeddings and \(F\) is totally real.
They are distinct: equality would make their split real places in \(F\) identical.
Those sets consist respectively of the embeddings extending the marked embedding of \(F_x\) or \(F_y\).
In the absolute Galois group their inverse images are precisely the two pointwise stabilizers of the marked fields \(F_x,F_y\).
Equality of the sets therefore gives \(F_x=F_y=F\); the equality of the quadratic extensions would now contradict \(K_x\ne K_y\).
Hence \([K_xK_y:F]=4\), with the two root choices independent at every embedding of \(F\).
Also \(\mathbb Q(x^s y^t)=K_xK_y\): fixing the positive Perron product requires maximal modulus in each factor and fixes both positive powers. [Product field](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#fields)

The identity embedding of \(F\) is the only one marked for both factors.
There are \(k_x-1\) others marked for \(x\) alone, and \(k_y-1\) marked only for \(y\).
The sum of the four positive-part logs over each of the latter two kinds is \(2U\) or \(2V\), respectively, since the unmarked unit there is neutral.
At the identity the four logs are \(\pm U\pm V\), so their positive parts sum to \(2\max(U,V)\).
The remaining fibers contribute zero.
Summing gives \(2k_xU+2k_yV-2\min(U,V)\), proving (1).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Iteration, finite stopping and the near-one exclusion

As long as both exponents remain positive, (1) gives the same update on the two log contributions.
For incomparable trace fields both \(k_x,k_y\ge2\); even the factor from which the minimum is subtracted has its log contribution at least doubled.
For equal trace fields both indices are one, and (1) leaves only the square of the larger factor.
For \(F_y\subsetneq F_x\), the indices are \(1,k\).
If \(U\le V\) the \(x\)-factor disappears, whereas if \(U>V\) the new contributions are \(2U,\,2(k-1)V\).
For index two their ratio stays unchanged forever in the latter case.
For \(k>2\) after at most \(\lceil\log(U/V)/\log(k-1)\rceil\) such updates the second contribution catches the first, and the next iterate leaves just a power of \(y\).
All these one-factor terminal powers are Salem and fixed.

For the small-value assertion write \(n=[F:\mathbb Q]\).
When the root fields differ the two units are multiplicatively independent, by full degrees of powers.
If \(k_x,k_y\le2\), their individual degrees \(2n/k_x,2n/k_y\) and joint degree \(4n\) give
\[
        (\log x)(\log y)\gg n/(\log(12n))^{212}
\]
by the [two-unit height comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#comparison).
On a putative sequence \(xy\to1\), \(n\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness, giving a contradiction.
But \(M(xy)\le(xy)^2\) in (1) would require both indices at most two, as is seen by putting the smaller of the two logarithms in the subtracted term.
This proves the fast alternative and the two stated exclusions of trace indices.
Finally, a small primitive Salem generator is an atom in the local factorization: its terminal signed representation has its own value on the original coordinate, and it generates its [primitive norm-one unit line](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units).
Taking primitive powers in the two different fields therefore supplies two different atoms in \(xy\), proving the single-input exclusion.
