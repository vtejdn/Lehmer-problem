# Degrees of rational maps between algebraic numbers of bounded total measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A growing lower bound on the degree of any nonmonomial relation

Let \(x_j\) be nonzero algebraic numbers of degrees \(d_j\to\infty\), and let \(y_j=R_j(x_j)\) be finite and nonzero, for nonconstant \(R_j\in\mathbb Q(X)\) of degrees \(t_j\).
Exclude the maps \(R_j(X)=\pm X^k\) for nonzero integers \(k\).
If the two total logarithmic Mahler measures \(m(x_j),m(y_j)\) are bounded, then
\[
                 \liminf_{j\to\infty}
                   \frac{t_j\log\log d_j}{\log d_j}\ \ge\ 1 .            \tag{1}
\]
Integrality, a fixed rational map and equality of the two root fields are unnecessary.
For example, (1) applies if \(x_j,y_j\) are two conjugates of a nontorsion unit of bounded measure, \(y_j\in\mathbb Q(x_j)\), and \(y_j\) is not any signed nonzero integer power of \(x_j\).
It applies as well to any proposed construction transforming one sequence of bounded positive measures into a second one by rational functions, with the stated exclusions.

<a id="height"></a>
## The height of an interpolating map

Drop the subscript \(j\).
Take relatively prime integer polynomials \(A,B\) for \(R=A/B\) whose combined coefficient list is primitive, and put \(H\) equal to the largest absolute coefficient.
Here relatively prime means without a common nonconstant rational polynomial factor.
The homogeneous pair \(A_h,B_h\) of degree \(t=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\) has nonzero integer resultant, since the leading coefficients do not both vanish.
Let \(h\) denote absolute logarithmic Weil height; use \(m(x)=d\,h(x)\) and the corresponding identity for \(y\).
If \(d\ge2t+1\), put \(l=2t+1\).
Then
\[
                  \log H\le\tfrac l2\log l+l(t h(x)+h(y)).               \tag{2}
\]
Choose \(l\) embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) with distinct images of \(x\), and denote the pairs of images by \(x_i,y_i\).
The \(l\) linear equations \(C(x_i)-y_i D(x_i)=0\) on the \(2t+2\) coefficients of two polynomials of degrees at most \(t\) have a one-dimensional kernel.
Indeed \(CB-DA\) vanishes at all \(l>2t\) distinct points and is identically zero, and coprimality with maximal degree \(t\) leaves only scalar multiples of \((A,B)\).
Thus the signed maximal minors of the matrix give its coefficient vector projectively.
At every archimedean place of a normal closure each minor is bounded by \(l^{l/2}\) times the product of \(\max(1,|x_i|)^t\max(1,|y_i|)\), by Hadamard's inequality.
At finite places omit \(l^{l/2}\) by the ultrametric inequality.
Taking the projective height of the vector of minors gives (2), as the primitive rational coefficient vector has height \(\log H\).
The polynomial \(A(X)-yB(X)\) over \(\mathbb Q(y)\) is nonzero by nonconstancy, so \([\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q(y)]\le t\).
In particular, under a common total-measure bound \(M\),
\[
                      h(x)\le M/d,\qquad h(y)\le tM/d.                   \tag{3}
\]

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Comparison with a prime power at a prime of good reduction

For any odd prime \(p\nmid\operatorname{Res}(A_h,B_h)\) with \(2pt<d\), one has
\[
                \log p\le\log(2(t+1)H)+p\bigl(h(y)+t h(x)\bigr).         \tag{4}
\]
To prove this, first compare the projective points \(R(x)^p\) and \(R(x^p)\), with powers on homogeneous coordinates.
At every place above \(p\) choose local integral homogeneous coordinates \(X_0,X_1\) for \(x\) with at least one a unit.
The homogeneous forms have no common projective zero in the residue field or its algebraic closure.
Both the pair \((A_h(X_0,X_1),B_h(X_0,X_1))\) and the pair evaluated at \((X_0^p,X_1^p)\) thus have maximum absolute value one.
By Fermat's congruence for the rational integer coefficients and divisibility of the mixed multinomial coefficients,
the first pair raised coordinatewise to \(p\) agrees with the second modulo the ideal generated by the rational integer \(p\), even at ramified places.

The two projective points are distinct.
If they agreed, \(A(X)^p B(X^p)-B(X)^p A(X^p)\) would vanish at \(x\); its degree is at most \(2pt<d\).
An identical vanishing would give \(R(X^p)=R(X)^p\).
The latter identity forces all zeros and poles to lie at zero or infinity, because their finite support on \(\mathbb C^\times\) would equal its full \(p\)-th power preimage, of \(p\) times its cardinality.
Thus \(R=cX^k\), and \(c^{p-1}=1\) implies \(c=\pm1\), which was excluded.

For distinct projective points, take the determinant of their two coordinate pairs.
At finite places its modulus divided by the two maximum norms is at most one, and at places over \(p\) it is at most \(|p|\) by the preceding congruence.
At infinite places the corresponding quotient is at most two.
The product formula on the nonzero determinant gives
\[
                    \log p\le\log2+h(R(x)^p)+h(R(x^p)).
\]
Here and in this calculation \(h(\infty)=0\) for projective height.
The direct evaluation bound on the integral homogeneous forms is \(h(R(x^p))\le\log((t+1)H)+pt\,h(x)\), by bounding their \(t+1\) terms at each archimedean place and using their integral coefficients at the others.
This proves (4).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Choosing a prime below the degree threshold

It suffices to consider subsequences with \(t\le2\log d\); on the other indices the quotient in (1) diverges.
On these subsequences (2) and (3) give \(\log H=O(\log d\,\log\log d)\) for large \(d\).
Hadamard's inequality on the \(2t\) rows of the Sylvester determinant gives
\[
             \log|\operatorname{Res}(A_h,B_h)|
                            \le 2t\log(\sqrt{t+1}\,H).
\]
The shifts and any zero padding of the two homogeneous coefficient lists preserve this row bound.
The [prime number theorem at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) gives a sum of \(\log p\) of order \(d/t\) in \((d/(8t),\,d/(4t)]\); removing proper prime powers costs \(O(\sqrt{d/t}\log^2 d)\).
For large \(d\) this prime sum exceeds the entire log resultant bound, so some odd prime in the interval has good reduction.
At this prime the last term in (4) is at most \(M/2\) by (3).
Consequently \(\log H\ge\log d-O_M(\log(2t))\).
Combining with (2) gives
\[
                 \log d\le (t+\tfrac12)\log(2t+1)
                                      +O_M(\log(2t)+t^2/d).
\]
This first forces \(t\to\infty\).
Since \(\log(2t+1)\le\log\log d+O(1)\) throughout the subsequence, division gives (1).
