# A determinant bound for the two Laurent tails of an integral quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## The finite determinant test

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be a monic irreducible polynomial of degree \(d\) with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\sum_{P(\rho)=0}\log^+|\rho|>0\), \(K(m)=\max_{\ell\ge1,\,\ell\in\mathbb Z}\ell e^{-2\ell m}\), and let \(\mathcal E(P)\) be the infimum of \(\|A/P\|_2^2\) over integral Laurent numerators \(A\) with \(P\nmid A\).
The circle norm uses probability Haar measure.
Fix \(0\le q<d\), put \(k=q+1\), and write \(t_j(a)\) for the Laurent coefficients on the circle of \(Q_a/P\), where \(Q_a(X)=\sum_{i=0}^q a_iX^i\).
For real \(a\) define the real symmetric matrix \(H_q\) and the middle coefficient map \(T_q\) by
\[
 a^{\mathsf T}H_qa=\sum_{j<0}|t_j(a)|^2+\sum_{j\ge q}|t_j(a)|^2,\qquad
 T_qa=(t_0(a),\ldots,t_{q-1}(a)).
\]
Then \(H_q\) is positive definite.
Put \(D_q=(\det H_q)^{1/k}\), and let \(V_k\) be the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb R^k\).
With \((x)_+=\max(x,0)\), one has
\[
 \sqrt{\det H_q}\ \ge\
 V_k\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^q}\bigl(K(m)/4-\|y\|^2\bigr)_+^{k/2}\,dy.       \tag{1}
\]
An integral over zero coordinates evaluates the integrand at zero.
In particular, on putting \(s=K(m)/(k+q)\),
\[
 D_q\ge s\min(1,s)^{q/k}
 \ \ge\ \frac14\min\!\left(\frac{K(m)}k,\frac{K(m)^2}{k^2}\right).    \tag{2}
\]
There is also a finite upper bound on the energy,
\[
 K(m)\le\mathcal E(P)\le
 \min\left\{\frac q4+\gamma_k D_q,\ (k+q)\max\bigl(D_q,D_q^{\,k/(k+q)}\bigr)\right\}, \tag{3}
\]
where \(\gamma_k\) is the Hermite constant for squared Euclidean length.
One integral numerator attains energy at most the right side.

For a sequence with \(m\to0\) and \(km\to c\in(0,\infty)\), the test gives
\[
 \liminf D_q\ \ge\ \frac1{4\mathrm e c}\min\!\left(1,\frac1{4\mathrm e c}\right).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Thus a determinant upper bound strictly below this threshold along a proposed sequence excludes that sequence.
If \(km\to0\), then \(D_q\to\infty\).
If \(km\to\infty\) while \(m\to0\), then \(\liminf (km)^2D_q\ge 1/(16\mathrm e^2)\).

<a id="packing"></a>
## Packing after reducing the middle coefficients modulo one

For real \(a\), all \(t_j(a)\) are real by conjugation.
If both tails vanish, \(Q_a/P\) agrees on the circle with a polynomial supported in \(0,\ldots,q-1\), with the zero polynomial understood for \(q=0\).
Multiplication by \(P\) gives a polynomial identity, and \(\deg Q_a<d\) forces \(a=0\).
This proves positive definiteness.

Equip \(G=\mathbb R^k\times(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^q\) with the product Euclidean distance, using shortest representatives in each circle, and with Lebesgue measure times Haar probability measure.
Consider the discrete subgroup
\[
 \Lambda=\{(H_q^{1/2}a,T_qa\bmod\mathbb Z^q):a\in\mathbb Z^k\}.
\]
A fundamental parallelepiped for \(H_q^{1/2}\mathbb Z^k\) times the full torus is a fundamental domain for \(\Lambda\), since each first coordinate can be reduced uniquely using a half-open parallelepiped.
Its volume is \(\sqrt{\det H_q}\).
Let \(E_q\) be the minimum squared distance to zero among the nonzero elements of \(\Lambda\).
The minimum exists since bounded first coordinates admit only finitely many \(a\).
For fixed nonzero \(a\), choose the nearest integer vector \(b\) to \(T_qa\) and put \(B(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}b_jX^j\).
By Parseval, the squared distance for this \(a\) equals
\[
 \left\|\frac{Q_a-PB}{P}\right\|_2^2
 =a^{\mathsf T}H_qa+\operatorname{dist}(T_qa,\mathbb Z^q)^2.
\]
The nonzero \(Q_a\) has degree below \(d\) and \(P(0)\ne0\), so \(P\nmid Q_a\) even after inverting \(X\).
Thus \(Q_a-PB\) is admissible, giving \(E_q\ge\mathcal E(P)\ge K(m)\) by the [integral quotient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient).

The open ball of radius \(\sqrt{E_q}/2\) in \(G\) projects injectively to \(G/\Lambda\) by the triangle inequality and the definition of \(E_q\).
Its volume is therefore at most \(\sqrt{\det H_q}\).
Taking the representative \(y\in[-1/2,1/2)^q\), its fiber in \(\mathbb R^k\) has volume \(V_k(E_q/4-\|y\|^2)_+^{k/2}\), proving (1) even with \(E_q\) in place of \(K(m)\).

Write \(u=E_q/(k+q)\).
In the same ball, up to boundary of measure zero, take an interval of length \(\sqrt u\) centered at zero in each real coordinate and of length \(\min(1,\sqrt u)\) in each torus coordinate.
The sum of squared coordinates is at most \((k+q)u/4=E_q/4\).
Squaring the volume comparison and taking the \(k\)-th root gives \(D_q\ge u\min(1,u)^{q/k}\).
This function is increasing in \(u\), giving the first bound in (2).
For \(t=K(m)/k\) one has \(s\ge t/2\) and \(q/k<1\), hence \(s\min(1,s)^{q/k}\ge(t/2)\min(1,t/2)\ge\min(t,t^2)/4\).
Inverting the same comparison at \(u\) gives \(E_q\le(k+q)\max(D_q,D_q^{k/(k+q)})\).
A shortest nonzero vector of the Euclidean lattice \(H_q^{1/2}\mathbb Z^k\) has squared length at most \(\gamma_kD_q\), while its middle coordinates have total squared distance at most \(q/4\).
These two bounds for the attained minimum \(E_q\) prove (3).
For (4), \(mK(m)\to1/(2\mathrm e)\) by the [small-measure asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#quotient), and \(k\to\infty\) gives \(q/k\to1\).
The two remaining limiting assertions follow directly from the last bound in (2).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Evaluation at the roots in the full coefficient dimension

Let \(I\) and \(O\) consist of the roots inside and outside the circle, respectively, and set \(\lambda_\rho=\rho\) on \(I\) and \(\lambda_\rho=\rho^{-1}\) on \(O\).
For \(J=I,O\) use the unnormalized geometric kernel
\[
 (K_J)_{\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{1-\overline{\lambda_\rho}\lambda_\sigma},
 \qquad \rho,\sigma\in J.
\]
When \(q=d-1\), the determinant in (1) has the root expression
\[
 \det H_{d-1}
 =\frac{e^{-2dm}}{|\operatorname{disc}P|}\det K_I\det K_O.           \tag{5}
\]
In fact the same two kernel matrices give \(H_q\) in every allowed dimension.
Order the roots with \(I\) first, put \(V_{\rho j}=\rho^j\) for \(0\le j\le q\), and let \(W\) be diagonal with entries \(1/P'(\rho)\) on \(I\) and \(-\rho^{-q-1}/P'(\rho)\) on \(O\).
The [two Laurent tail expansions](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails) give the coefficients at \(-h-1\) and \(q+h\) as the sums over their respective sides of \((WVa)_\rho\lambda_\rho^h\), for \(h\ge0\).
These expansions also apply when the leading coefficient of \(Q_a\) is zero, since convolving \(1/P\) with the \(q+1\) specified coordinates permits trailing zeros.
Summing the products of conjugate geometric series gives
\[
 H_q=V^*W^*(K_I\oplus K_O)WV .
\]
Indeed the matrix on the right has real entries by simultaneous conjugation of the row and column roots, and its quadratic form on real coefficients is the two-tail energy.

For \(k=d\), the evaluation matrix is square with \(|\det V|^2=|\operatorname{disc}P|\) by the Vandermonde formula.
Monicity gives \(\prod_\rho |P'(\rho)|=|\operatorname{disc}P|\), and the exterior weights give \(\prod_{\rho\in O}|\rho|^{-2(q+1)}=e^{-2dm}\).
Taking determinants yields (5), so the full-dimensional test (1) or (2) can be evaluated using only the roots.
