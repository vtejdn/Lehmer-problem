# Archimedean radial distributions of individual plane-curve factors and a finiteness test for Salem numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## The scaled logarithmic radii of an individual orbit

Let \(F=\sum_{c\in A}f_c X^{c_1}Y^{c_2}\) be a primitive integral Laurent polynomial, irreducible over \(\mathbf Q\), with support differences of rank two.
Put \(b_+(u)=\max_{c\in A}c\cdot u\), \(b_-(u)=\min_{c\in A}c\cdot u\) and \(w(u)=b_+(u)-b_-(u)\).
Write \(F_\pm\) for the sums over the two corresponding exposed faces at \(u\).
For \(\|u\|_\infty=1\), the restriction of the Ronkin function of \(F\) to the line \(tu\) is
\[
             R_u(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}
                 \log|F(e^{tu_1}X,e^{tu_2}Y)|\,dX\,dY ,
\]
where the integral uses Haar probability.
Let \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\) with arbitrary roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\), nonzero nontorsion \(z\), \(N=\max(|a|,|b|)>0\), and \(g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)\).
If \(N/g\to\infty\) and \((a,b)/N\to u\), then along every such sequence
\[
 \frac1{[\mathbf Q(z):\mathbf Q]}
       \sum_{\sigma:\mathbf Q(z)\hookrightarrow\mathbf C}
                   \delta_{N\log|\sigma z|}
             \ \Longrightarrow\ \nu_u=\frac{R_u''}{w(u)}.               \tag{1}
\]
The second derivative is distributional, and \(\nu_u\) is a probability measure on \(\mathbf R\).
The same limit holds when the powered pairs \((\xi z^a,\eta z^b)\) are distinct and their primitive direction is constant.
In particular the formula applies even when the coefficient fields of the translations have unbounded degree.

Put \(s_+=\max(s,0)\), \(s_-=\max(-s,0)\), and let \(m\) denote logarithmic Mahler measure.
The limiting law has finite one-sided first moments
\[
       w(u)\int s_+\,d\nu_u(s)=m(F)-m(F_+),\qquad
       w(u)\int s_-\,d\nu_u(s)=m(F)-m(F_-).                            \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\nu_u=\delta_0\) exactly when both faces have measure \(m(F)\).
Formula (2) concerns the limiting law; a vanishing fraction of the finite orbit may carry logarithmic height at unbounded scaled radius, as in the [critical graph analysis](Yin_C_G0345_v759.md#statement).

For an edge, call its polynomial primitive if the edge coefficients have greatest common divisor one.
If a sequence of distinct powered pairs from algebraic units \(z\) has a proportion of conjugates off the unit circle tending to zero, each accumulation direction must expose **two parallel edges** whose polynomials are primitive and both have logarithmic measure \(m(F)>0\).
If the Newton polygon has no such pair, there is a constant \(\epsilon_F>0\) such that every nontorsion algebraic unit from every section above has at least \(\epsilon_F[\mathbf Q(z):\mathbf Q]\) conjugates off the unit circle.
For every fixed \(B\) the full set of such units with at most \(B\) off-circle conjugates is then finite.
In particular only finitely many Salem numbers occur across all directions, dilations and torsion translations.
This criterion covers polygons without parallel edges and polygons for which all edge polynomials have Mahler measure zero.
The [earlier edge theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) gives measure gaps and restrictions on shrinking Salem factors in untwisted sections.

<a id="continuity"></a>
## Uniform logarithmic integration with moving coefficients

Consider a trigonometric polynomial with \(k\le |A|\) distinct integer frequencies and maximum coefficient modulus at least \(M>0\).
Its supremum on the circle is at least \(M\) by Fourier inversion.
Applying the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) on \([0,2\pi]\) to the set where the modulus is below \(\varepsilon<M\) bounds that set's normalized length by \(C(\varepsilon/M)^{1/(|A|-1)}\).
For a single term the set is empty.
The layer-cake identity therefore bounds the error in replacing \(\log|P|\) by \(\log\max(|P|,\varepsilon)\) in its integral by \(C_A(\varepsilon/M)^{1/(|A|-1)}\).
For Laurent polynomials on \(\mathbb T^2\) with support in \(A\), choose one integer direction separating \(A\) and average the circle estimate over all translated circles in that direction using Haar invariance.
On each such circle the modulus of every coefficient is unchanged.

Let \(u_n=(a_n,b_n)/N_n\to u\) with \(N_n/g_n\to\infty\).
For fixed \(t\), let \(J_n(t)\) be the circle mean of
\(\log|F(e^{tu_{n,1}}Z^{a_n},e^{tu_{n,2}}Z^{b_n})|\).
The support projects injectively for large \(n\).
The pushforward of circle Haar probability under \(Z\mapsto(Z^{a_n},Z^{b_n})\) converges to Haar probability on \(\mathbb T^2\), since each fixed nontrivial character eventually has zero integral.
The truncated logarithms for the coefficients \(f_c e^{t c\cdot u_n}\) converge uniformly to the limiting truncated logarithm on \(\mathbb T^2\).
All coefficients are bounded and a largest coefficient stays bounded away from zero, uniformly for \(t\) in any fixed compact interval.
The two sublevel estimates thus give \(J_n(t)\to R_u(t)\) at every \(t\) and a uniform bound for \(|J_n|\) on each such interval.

For \(t\to+\infty\), factor out \(e^{t b_+(u)}\) inside the Ronkin integral.
The remaining coefficients are bounded, converge to those of \(F_+\), and one coefficient on that face has fixed nonzero modulus.
The same torus estimate gives \(R_u(t)-b_+(u)t\to m(F_+)\).
At \(-\infty\) the corresponding intercept is \(m(F_-)\).
All these assertions allow ties in the limiting projections.

<a id="jensen"></a>
## The whole-section measure and its moments

In the untwisted section write \(e_{\min},e_{\max}\) for the extreme exponents, \(c_{\rm top}\) for the top coefficient, and \(\rho_j\) for the nonzero roots with multiplicities.
Their number is \(W_n=e_{\max}-e_{\min}=N_n w(u_n)\).
Jensen's formula at radius \(e^{t/N_n}\) gives
\[
 J_n(t)=\log|c_{\rm top}|+\frac{e_{\min}}{N_n}t+
        \frac1{N_n}\sum_j\max(t,N_n\log|\rho_j|).
\]
These functions are convex, and the distributional second derivative is \(N_n^{-1}\sum_j\delta_{N_n\log|\rho_j|}\).
The local bounds and pointwise convergence permit testing against the second derivative of any smooth compactly supported function.
Thus the normalized whole-section laws converge vaguely to the positive measure \(R_u''/w(u)\).
The finite linear asymptotes give the extreme slopes \(b_-(u),b_+(u)\) of this convex function, so its second derivative has total mass \(w(u)>0\).
This gives weak probability convergence, also on the compact extended real line with zero limiting mass at either endpoint.

For \(s\ge0\), \(b_+(u)-R'_{u,+}(s)=R_u''((s,\infty))\).
Integrating over \(s\ge0\) and using the positive-side intercept gives \(R_u(0)-m(F_+)=\int x_+\,dR_u''(x)\).
Applying the same calculation to \(R_u(-s)\) gives the other part of (2).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Passing from the whole section to one orbit

Pass to a subsequence on which a fixed signed coordinate attains \(N\).
Let \(y_*\) denote that coordinate on the normalized curve and \(l=\pm1\) its exponent sign.
Then \(l\log|\sigma y_*|=N\log|\sigma z|\) in any common complex embedding.
For a continuous test \(\psi\) on the compact extended real line, equip the trivial divisor with Green function \(\psi(l\log|y_*|)\) at all infinite places of the field of the component and zero elsewhere.
This function extends continuously through zeros and poles via the map to \(\mathbf P^1\), and is invariant under local complex conjugation wherever required.
Compute the normalized global point height over a common number field containing the point, its coefficients, the phases and \(z\).
Its embeddings repeat those of each subfield equally often, so this height is the absolute average of the test over the embeddings of \(\mathbf Q(z)\).

The [directional semipositive divisor and common-limit argument](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#common) now give the same limit for this test for every root selection from long sections in the direction \(u\).
One can transport the points by conjugation to the same geometric component, preserving their absolute distributions.
If convergence to the common value failed uniformly within the sections, selecting a violating root on each exceptional section would contradict that argument.
The roots of an untwisted rational section fall into full rational Galois orbits with their factor multiplicities.
Their torsion part accounts for \(O_F(g_n)\) out of \(W_n\) terms, by the [torsion fiber count](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#calibration).
As the test is bounded, the whole-section average just computed determines the common orbit limit in (1).

For distinct pairs in a fixed primitive direction \((p,q)\), the character \(x^q y^{-p}\) has torsion values on those pairs.
The argument for [fixed-direction dependent points](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#fixed) compares such a generic sequence to points on growing untwisted primitive sections approaching \((p,q)/\|(p,q)\|_\infty\).
Both sequences have heights tending to zero for the same semipositive character pullback.
Applying its global convergence theorem to the above archimedean test gives (1) also on the fixed direction.
At a tie for the maximal coordinate the auxiliary directions may be chosen on the side of the selected coordinate, or the test may be rescaled by its exponent divided by \(N_n\), which tends to the selected sign.

<a id="salem"></a>
## Excluding factors with too few conjugates off the circle

Suppose that the fraction of conjugates off the unit circle tends to zero.
Every bounded test on the scaled radii then has average tending to its value at zero, since it has that value identically at the unit-circle conjugates.
For distinct pairs, along any accumulation direction one can extract either long primitive directions or a fixed primitive direction.
Consequently (1) forces \(\nu_u=\delta_0\), and (2) forces both face measures to equal \(m(F)>0\).
This implication uses the finite orbit's hypothesis directly and requires no convergence of its mass at the atom zero.
If \(z\) is a unit, the [finite-place face criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) further requires each of the two extreme faces to have relatively prime coefficients.
A singleton primitive face has coefficient of absolute value one and measure zero.
Thus both faces must be edges, and they are parallel since they support the polygon on opposite sides in the same normal direction.

If the displayed test on opposite edges fails and there were no uniform positive fraction off the unit circle, choose a sequence of nontorsion unit roots with this fraction tending to zero.
After a subsequence its powered pairs are distinct or one pair repeats.
The preceding argument excludes the first case.
In the second, choose a nontorsion coordinate of the repeated pair, say \(x\).
Such a coordinate exists because \(z\) is nontorsion and one exponent is nonzero.
It is an algebraic unit and has some conjugate off the circle by Kronecker's theorem.
At each occurrence \(x=\xi z^a\) with \(a\ne0\).
In a field containing these elements the off-circle events agree at every embedding; counting embeddings therefore gives exactly the fixed positive off-circle fraction of \(x\) for every such \(z\).
The argument for the other coordinate is identical.

Finally all root heights are bounded by \(\log\|F\|_1\), also in short directions.
Each translated section is nonzero because an identically zero section would put a torus coset component in \(F=0\), contrary to [rank two and rational irreducibility](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#geometry).
Remove its extreme vanishing coefficients and a monomial, and split it as \(c\prod_j(Z-\rho_j)\) over a number field.
At a finite place its Gauss norm is at most one, and at an infinite place its logarithmic Mahler measure is at most \(\log\|F\|_1\), including when projected terms collide.
The sum of these local logarithmic measures with normalized place weights equals \(\sum_j h(\rho_j)\), since the contributions of \(c\) sum to zero.
If there are at most \(B\) off-circle conjugates, the uniform fraction gives degree at most \(B/\epsilon_F\), so Northcott's theorem yields finiteness.
A Salem number has exactly two such conjugates.

For example \(F=X+X^{-1}+Y+Y^{-1}-1\) is reciprocal and has infinitely many zeros on the unit torus, since \(2\cos s+2\cos t=1\) has an arc of solutions.
After multiplication by \(Y\) it is quadratic in \(Y\) with discriminant \((X+X^{-1}-3)(X+X^{-1}+1)\).
The first factor has two simple zeros and shares neither with the second, so the discriminant is not a square and the Laurent polynomial is irreducible.
All four edge polynomials are monomials times \(1+T\) for a primitive character \(T\).
They have measure zero, so the finiteness assertion applies to this curve in every translated section.
