# A reciprocal plane curve with finitely many dependent unit points despite primitive opposite edges

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finiteness of points and the gap across translated sections

Consider the image of the following rational functions in the two-dimensional torus, with its irreducible primitive integral equation \(F(X,Y)=0\):
\[
    X(t)=\frac{25t^2-1}{t^2-25},\qquad
    Y(t)=\frac{(2t-1)(3t-1)(t-6)}{(t-2)(t-3)(6t-1)}.
\]
There are only finitely many points of this curve whose two coordinates are multiplicatively dependent algebraic units.
There is consequently a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
           m(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\ge c
\]
for every nonzero nontorsion solution of \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\), where \(\xi,\eta\) are arbitrary roots of unity and \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\).
Both pairs of opposite edges satisfy the [primitive-edge and oriented-root conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v778.md#statement).
The finiteness assertion concerns the powered coordinate pairs, and the lower bound applies to every root realizing any of those pairs and to nonunit roots as well.

<a id="edges"></a>
## The four face polynomials

The rational maps \(X,Y\) from the projective line have degrees two and three.
Over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) the index of the field generated by \(X,Y\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\) divides both degrees, so this index is one.
The same argument over \(\mathbb Q\) proves that the projective line is the normalization over \(\mathbb Q\).
Replacing \(t\) by \(1/t\) inverts both coordinates.

Write \(A(t),B(t)\) for the displayed numerator and denominator of \(Y(t)\).
The resultant in \(t\) of \((X-25)t^2+1-25X\) and \(YB(t)-A(t)\), divided by \(48\), gives the following four faces of \(F\).

| Face | Polynomial |
| --- | --- |
| Coefficient of \(Y^2\) | \(-(X+14)(7X+33)(899X-11)\) |
| Constant in \(Y\) | \((11X-899)(14X+1)(33X+7)\) |
| Coefficient of \(X^3\) | \(-(29Y+21)(217Y-242)\) |
| Constant in \(X\) | \((21Y+29)(242Y-217)\) |

For example the first face follows by evaluating \((X-25)t^2+1-25X\) at the three zeros of \(B(t)\), with the leading coefficient of \(B\) squared.
For the last two faces take the resultants of \(t^2-25\) and \(1-25t^2\), respectively, against \(YB-A\).
To check the scalar normalization directly, substitution of \(t^2=(25X-1)/(X-25)\) in the product \((YB-A)(t)(YB-A)(-t)\), followed by multiplication by \((X-25)^3\), gives the four displayed faces times \(48\), with remaining coefficients \([X^2Y]=[XY]=48\cdot501539\).
The resulting quotient is integral and each displayed face is primitive.
Its bidegree \((3,2)\) already equals that of the birational image, which therefore accounts for the whole resultant up to scalar.
The four corner coefficients are nonzero of absolute value \(6293\) or \(5082\), so its Newton polygon is the full rectangle.
Each upper face has an exterior root and an interior root, and the opposite face has the reciprocal roots.

<a id="units"></a>
## Opposite signs at poles on each eligible axis

The torsion points form a finite set by the [torus-curve intersection bound](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area), since the irreducible curve has two-dimensional Newton polygon.
Suppose there were infinitely many distinct rank-one unit pairs.
Write a sequence of these pairs as \((\xi z^a,\eta z^b)\) with \(z\) a nontorsion algebraic unit, by choosing a generator of the subgroup generated by the two coordinates modulo torsion.
After passing to a subsequence their sup-normalized directions converge.
The [finite-place criterion for distinct unit points](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) requires the coefficients on each of the two exposed faces to be relatively prime.
Every nonaxial direction exposes a corner on each side, and no corner has coefficient of absolute value one.
Thus one exponent, that of the short coordinate \(S\), divided by the maximum of both absolute exponents tends to zero.
The [coordinate height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#statement) gives \(h(S)\to0\), including on a fixed axis.
The distinct pairs lift to a generic sequence on the normalization after omitting the finitely many singular points.

Write \(T\) for the other coordinate.
For \(S=X,T=Y\) consider the poles \(t=2\) and \(t=1/6\) of \(T\), at which \(S\) takes the values \(-33/7\) and \(11/899\), respectively.
For \(S=Y,T=X\) consider the poles \(t=5\) and \(t=-5\) of \(T\), at which \(S\) takes the values \(-21/29\) and \(242/217\).
On each axis the two poles are rational, their short-coordinate values have positive height, and one modulus lies on each side of one.
Choose a small fixed complex neighborhood of each pole on which \(|T|>1\) and the indicated strict inequality for \(|S|\) holds.
Since both coordinates are algebraic units, the [allocation of height to individual boundary points](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement) supplies positive limiting contributions at both poles, and hence embeddings visiting both neighborhoods at every sufficiently large index.
For a section with short and long exponents \(k,l\), each embedding satisfies \(\log|S|=(k/l)\log|T|\) since both phases are roots of unity and \(l\ne0\) on this subsequence.
On \(|T|>1\) the sign of \(\log|S|\) is therefore fixed for each section, including when \(k=0\).
This contradicts visitation of both neighborhoods and proves finiteness.

Every nonunit \(z\) contributes at least \(\log 2\) to its logarithmic Mahler measure by its integral primitive minimal polynomial and the same test on its reciprocal.
For a remaining fixed rank-one pair, a Bézout product of its coordinates is a fixed nontorsion unit \(\beta\) and every realizing \(z\) satisfies \(z^{\pm\gcd(|a|,|b|)}=\omega\beta\) with a root of unity \(\omega\).
The [externally twisted radical lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v767.md#external), as applied in the [finite-pair gap argument](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#finite), bounds these measures below by a positive constant for this pair.
Taking the minimum over the finite set and \(\log 2\) gives the assertion.
