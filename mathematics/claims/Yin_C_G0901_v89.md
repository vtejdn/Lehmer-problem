# Transfer of arbitrarily small measure from bounded radial rank in the divisible group to the original exterior places

<a id="statement"></a>
## A representative with no increase in total measure

Fix an integer \(R\ge1\).
There exists \(\varepsilon_R>0\) with the following property.
Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with total logarithmic measure \(0<m(w)<\varepsilon_R\).
Assume \(D(w)\), the divisible group generated by its conjugates with all torsion factors allowed, contains some nontorsion \(x\) whose conjugate log moduli span a rational space of dimension at most \(R\).
The degree and total measure of this witness \(x\) are unrestricted.
Then \(D(w)\) contains a reciprocal unit \(z\) with
\[
       \deg z\le\deg w,\qquad 0<m(z)\le m(w)
\]
whose exterior conjugates consist of at most \(R\) places, counting a real exterior root once and each pair of conjugate nonreal exterior roots once.
It has a neutral conjugate.
Its exterior log moduli, one from each such place, are rationally independent.
All nontorsion elements of \(D(z)\) have the same radial rank as \(z\).
The transfer also holds with growing \(R\): for every fixed \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\), it suffices that \(m(w)\) be sufficiently small in terms of \(\eta\) and
\[
                R\le\exp\big(\eta\,m(w)^{-1/3}\log(1/m(w))\big).
\]

Consequently the existence of a positive Lehmer gap for inputs whose divisible conjugate group contains a nonzero radial-rank-at-most-\(R\) direction is equivalent to a positive gap for reciprocal units with a neutral conjugate and at most \(R\) exterior places.
For \(R=1\) it is equivalent to the complex Salem gap.
In this case a complex Salem number of no greater total measure can be taken inside the original \(D(w)\), allowing a quadratic lift if the representative is real Salem up to sign.
In a failure of unrestricted Lehmer with gaps at every fixed exterior count, even the smallest radial rank in the whole divisible group of each input must tend to infinity along every positive shrinking-measure sequence.
This conclusion has no degree-record hypothesis.

<a id="minimal"></a>
## Minimal degree with a small-rank direction still available

Use the [rational constituent characterization](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#statement): the least radial rank in a divisible conjugate group equals the smallest fixed dimension of \(c\) on a nonzero rational irreducible constituent of its conjugate span.
Here \(c\) is ordinary complex conjugation.
Inside \(D(w)\) choose \(z\) of least degree among nontorsion units with \(m(z)\le m(w)\) for which \(D(z)\) still has a nontorsion element of radial rank at most \(R\).
This set includes \(w\).
Compression by products of torsion classes preserves the measure and the entire rational span and yields smaller degree if any distinct classes coalesced; see the [compression argument](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree).
Thus \(z\) is already compressed.
Take \(\varepsilon_R\) below [Smyth's nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) in logarithmic units and below a fixed bound, say one.
Bounded-degree coefficient finiteness allows it to be decreased so that any such \(z\) has degree beyond the threshold of the [alternating block construction](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement), with the common block size \(b\) arbitrarily large in terms of \(R\).
Indeed the number \(u\) of blocks is bounded by a fixed power of \(\log(3\deg z)\) at measure at most one, and \(bu=\deg z/2\).
In particular we can require \(b/2-1>R\) and \(\deg z>4R\).
The unit \(z\) is reciprocal, with all these strict conditions.

For the growing-\(R\) version put \(m=m(w)\) and \(t=\log\deg z\).
Bounded-degree finiteness first gives \(t\to\infty\) uniformly as \(m\to0\).
The [Voutier height estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) for \(z\) gives \(t>A\log t\), where \(A=(4m)^{-1/3}\), since \(m(z)\le m\).
For small \(m\) this gives first \(t>A\) and then \(t>A\log A\).
The block estimate at the fixed bound one gives \(\log b\ge t-212\log(t+\log3)-O(1)\).
The right side is increasing for large \(t\), and at \(t=A\log A\) it equals \((1/(3\sqrt[3]4)+o(1))m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\).
Consequently \(b/R\to\infty\) uniformly in the stated range, so both \(b/2-1>R\) and \(\deg z>4R\) hold there too.

In the independent inversion alternative of the [sign theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement), its conjugate module is precisely the absolutely irreducible signed module on all inverse pairs.
The proof uses distinct sign characters and transitivity, as in the [module calculation](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#modules); it needs no record condition in this alternative.
The radial rank of any nontorsion element of \(D(z)\) is therefore its full \(c\)-fixed dimension.
This is the number of exterior places by the action on real, neutral and exchanged inverse pairs.
It is at most \(R\) by the choice of \(z\).

<a id="oriented"></a>
## Eliminating the part generated by oriented block products

In the other alternative there is a canonical choice of one of two inverse halves on each block, up to interchanging those halves, so the group permutes the blocks with a uniform sign within each.
Work first in the formal signed coordinate space.
It splits as \(U\oplus B_0\), where \(U\) is the direct sum of the coordinate-sum-zero spaces and \(B_0\) consists of vectors constant on each block.
The pure alternating factors on the blocks make \(U\) absolutely irreducible under the full group, as proved from the [supported three-cycle lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) in the module calculation.
The natural map to \(V_z\) is nonzero on \(U\), since differences of two oriented roots in one block have nontorsion quotient after compression.
It is consequently injective there.
Write \(B\) for the image of \(B_0\).
As \(\dim B\le u< u(b-1)=\dim U\), its intersection with the irreducible image of \(U\) is zero.
These two images span \(V_z\); identify \(U\) with its image.

The space \(B\) is the rational span of the conjugates of the product \(\beta\) of the roots in one oriented half.
The [block norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields) gives \(\deg\beta\le2u<\deg z\) and \(m(\beta)\le m(z)\).
If \(B\) had an irreducible constituent with \(c\)-fixed dimension at most \(R\), the constituent characterization would give exactly the required small-rank direction in \(D(\beta)\).
In this case \(\beta\) is nontorsion, belongs to \(D(w)\), and contradicts the choice of \(z\).
The constituent already known to exist must therefore be isomorphic to \(U\), and \(\dim U^c\le R\).

If \(c\) exchanged two whole blocks, their two sum-zero spaces would contribute \(b-1>R\) fixed dimensions: the fixed vectors in the exchanged sum are the graphs of the induced isomorphism, since \(c^2=1\).
Thus \(c\) fixes every block.
If its uniform sign on a fixed block were positive, its action there on the labels would be an involutive permutation with at least \(b/2\) cycles.
The sum-zero space would have at least \(b/2-1>R\) fixed dimensions.
So the uniform sign on every block is negative.
In particular \(c\) acts as \(-1\) on \(B\).
Since \(B\) is Galois stable, every conjugate of any representing block product has squared modulus of zero multiplicative class, hence equal to one.
Kronecker's theorem makes every such product torsion and \(B=0\).

On a block with negative sign, each fixed label is a neutral inverse pair, and each two-cycle of labels gives one nonreal conjugate pair of exterior roots.
There are no real exterior conjugates.
The fixed dimension on its sum-zero space is the number of these two-cycles: negating the involutive permutation leaves one fixed direction per two-cycle, and the removed constant line has sign minus.
Summation proves that the number of exterior places is exactly \(\dim U^c\le R\) in this alternative too.

<a id="gaps"></a>
## The sequence equivalences

In either alternative \(V_z\) is irreducible, so every nontorsion element of \(D(z)\) has radial rank \(\dim V_z^c\).
The exterior log moduli span the entire radial space by reciprocity, and conjugate nonreal exterior roots give equal logarithms.
Having exactly as many exterior places as the dimension proves the asserted independence.
At most \(2R\) exterior roots and the reciprocal interior roots account for at most \(4R\) conjugates; the larger degree guarantees a neutral pair.

Conversely any reciprocal unit with at most \(R\) exterior places already has radial rank at most \(R\).
The construction without measure increase now gives the gap equivalence.
For one place, the output either has a single real exterior root up to choosing its conjugate and sign, or has exactly one nonreal exterior pair.
These are respectively the real Salem and complex Salem cases; in the former the [quadratic complex Salem lift](Yin_C_G0345_v72.md#lift) stays in \(D(w)\) and preserves total measure.
Finally, if radial ranks bounded by some \(R\) occurred in the groups of infinitely many members of a shrinking sequence, the constructed outputs along that subsequence would have at most \(2R\) exterior roots, a neutral pair and measures tending to zero.
This contradicts fixed exterior-count gaps when those are assumed.
