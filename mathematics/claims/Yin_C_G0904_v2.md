# Finite-time energy from modular polynomial relations and Salem relative norms

<a id="statement"></a>
## A modular condition for a positive lower bound on the two observables

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\ge2\).
Write \(m=\log M(P)\), let \(B\) be multiplication by \(\alpha\) in the power basis of \(O=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and identify \(u\in O\) with its integer coefficient vector.
For \(u\ne0\) put \(E_t(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\|B^iu\|_2^2\) and \(\kappa_t=\min_{u\ne0}E_t(u)\).
Use the [periodic character radius \(r_j\)](Yin_C_G0904_v1.md#periodic) and the original times
\[
 Y=\lceil d(\log(2d))^4\rceil,\qquad T=\lceil d^2(\log(2d))^4\rceil .
\]

Fix \(A>0\), an integer \(L\ge1\), and \(0<\eta\le1\).
Suppose there exist a rational prime \(p\), an integer \(1\le s\le Ad\), and a monic \(H\in\mathbb F_p[Z]\) of degree at most \(L\) such that
\[
             \deg\gcd\big(\overline P(X),H(X^s)\big)\ \ge\ \eta d .       \tag{1}
\]
Then there is \(c=c(A,L,\eta)>0\) such that, uniformly on these inputs for sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
 \min_{u\in O\setminus\{0\}}\frac{d\log E_T(u)}{2(T-1)}\ \ge c,\qquad
 \operatorname{median}_{Y\le j\le2Y}\frac{d\log r_j}{j}\ \ge c .
\]
Both the prime and the coefficients of \(H\) may vary, and the gcd is taken with the full multiplicities in the specified residue ring.
The [bounded-prime collision estimate](Yin_C_G0245_v1.md#statement) treats dense powered collisions at unramified primes using a binomial.
For unbounded primes the argument below constructs a polynomial of bounded degree with small coefficients from the quotient by \(H\).
An example satisfying (1) consists of the [irreducible Salem polynomials](Yin_C_G0901_v195.md#integer) \(F_d=X^d+1-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}X^i\), \(d=r-1\) with \(r\ge5\) prime.
Modulo two, \(F_d=(X^{d+1}-1)/(X-1)\), so \(s=d+1\), \(H=Z-1\) give gcd degree \(d\).
It remains open whether all inputs of hypothetical shrinking measure admit (1) with fixed \(A,L,\eta\).

<a id="krylov"></a>
## Determinant certificates for every nonzero integer vector

Let \(K_u=[u,Bu,\ldots,B^{d-1}u]\).
Its columns give the multiplication matrix of \(u\) on \(O\), so \(\det K_u=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(u)\) is a nonzero integer.
Let \(F\in\mathbb C[X]\) be nonconstant of degree \(q\), let \(b\ge1\) be an integer, and write \(\|F\|_\infty=\max_{|z|=1}|F(z)|\).
If \(bq+d\le t\), then
\[
 E_t(u)\ \ge\ d|\det K_u|^{2/d}
       \left(\frac{|\det F(B)|^{1/d}}{\|F\|_\infty}\right)^{2b}.        \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the columns of \(F(B)^bK_u\) are the \(d\) consecutive outputs of the filter with coefficients of \(F^b\) applied to \(B^i u\) for \(0\le i<t\).
Extend that vector sequence by zero to the integer line.
Each output equals \(\sum_h [X^h]F^b\,B^{i+h}u\), \(0\le i<d\).
Plancherel's identity for sequences gives an upper bound \(\|F\|_\infty^{2b}E_t(u)\) for the sum of squared norms of these \(d\) outputs, by bounding the Fourier multiplier \(F(e^{-i\theta})^b\).
Hadamard's inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean bound the squared determinant by the \(d\)-th power of their mean squared norm, giving (2).
The maximum modulus principle applied inside the disk and to the reversed polynomial also gives
\[
        |\det F(B)|^{1/d}\le \|F\|_\infty \exp(qm/d).                   \tag{3}
\]

A sharper bound from two blocks of times will also be useful.
For \(t\ge2d\), put \(n=t-d\) and choose \(|z|=1\) with \(|\det(I+zB^n)|\ge e^{nm}\).
Such a choice exists because the mean of the logarithm around the circle is \(nm\) by Jensen's formula.
The two blocks \(0,\ldots,d-1\) and \(n,\ldots,n+d-1\) are disjoint, so the columns of \((I+zB^n)K_u\) have sum of squared norms at most \(2E_t(u)\).
The same determinant bound gives
\[
        E_t(u)\ \ge\ \tfrac d2\,|\det K_u|^{2/d}\exp(2(t-d)m/d).       \tag{4}
\]

<a id="modular"></a>
## Constructing a certificate from the residue polynomial

In \(\mathbb F_p[X]/\overline P\), the nullity of multiplication by \(G\) is \(\deg\gcd(\overline P,G)\), including repeated factors.
Indeed its kernel is generated by the residue class of \(\overline P/\gcd(\overline P,G)\).
If \(H\) divides \(\overline Q\) for an integral polynomial \(Q\), (1) therefore gives nullity at least \(\eta d\) for \(Q(B^s)\bmod p\).
When its integer determinant is nonzero, the Smith normal form gives
\[
                  |\det Q(B^s)|^{1/d}\ge p^\eta .                      \tag{5}
\]

Take \(R=\lceil 2L/\eta\rceil\), \(\theta=L/(R+1)<\eta/2\) and \(p_0=(2(R+1))^{2/\eta}\).
For \(p\ge p_0\), reduce all degree-at-most-\(R\) polynomials with coefficients \(0,\ldots,\lfloor p^\theta\rfloor\) modulo \(p,H\).
There are more than \(p^L\) such polynomials and at most \(p^L\) residues.
A difference of two with the same residue gives a nonzero \(Q\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) of degree at most \(R\) with \(H\mid\overline Q\) and \(\|Q\|_\infty\le(R+1)p^\theta\).
Since \(\theta<1\), the difference remains nonzero modulo \(p\), and (1) requires \(H\) to be nonconstant.
If \(D=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^s):\mathbb Q]>R\), then \(Q(\alpha^s)\ne0\).
For \(F(X)=Q(X^s)\), (5) gives
\[
       \frac{|\det F(B)|^{1/d}}{\|F\|_\infty}
         \ \ge\ \frac{p^{\eta-\theta}}{R+1}\ \ge\ 2,\qquad \deg F\le RAd .
\]
In this branch (3) even gives \(m\ge ((\eta-\theta)\log p-\log(R+1))/(RA)\).
For a Salem root \(\alpha>1\), no other conjugate has its modulus, so \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^s)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for all positive \(s\).
Thus on bounded-measure Salem polynomials with \(d>R\), all primes witnessing (1) with the fixed parameters are bounded in terms of those parameters and the measure ceiling.
The same conclusion holds on sufficiently late strict degree records because [compression preserves the degrees of all powers](Yin_C_G0901_v163.md#rows).

For \(p<p_0\), discard the power of \(Z\) in \(H\), which does not affect the gcd in (1) because \(\overline P\) has nonzero constant term.
Let \(\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,L)\), let \(a\ge0\) be least with \(p^a\ge L\), and put \(h=p^a(p^\ell-1)\).
The remaining \(H\) divides \(Z^h-1\) modulo \(p\) since every root lies in \(\mathbb F_{p^\ell}^{\times}\) and has multiplicity at most \(p^a\).
Put \(b_0=\lceil2/\eta\rceil\).
The factors \(Z^h-1,\Phi_p(Z^h),\ldots,\Phi_{p^{b_0}}(Z^h)\) all vanish on that same \(H\) with its multiplicities modulo \(p\), since \(\Phi_{p^i}(W)\equiv(W-1)^{p^{i-1}(p-1)}\pmod p\) for \(i\ge1\).
Each factor has nonzero determinant on \(B^s\) because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
Applying (5) to each and multiplying gives \(|\det F(B)|^{1/d}\ge p^{(1+b_0)\eta}\) for \(F=X^{shp^{b_0}}-1\).
Its circle norm is two, so its determinant ratio is at least two.
Its degree is at most \(Adhp^{b_0}\), with \(hp^{b_0}\) bounded in terms of the fixed \(p_0,L,\eta\).

Consequently in both polynomial constructions \(\deg F\le Kd\) with \(K\) fixed by \(A,L,\eta\).
For \(b=\lfloor(T-d)/\deg F\rfloor\), (2) gives \(d\log E_T(u)/(2(T-1))\ge\log(2)/(2K)\) for all sufficiently large \(d\) and every \(u\ne0\).
Equation (3) also gives \(m\ge\log(2)/K\).
The remaining case is \(p\ge p_0\) with \(D\le R\).
There is a positive lower bound \(\mu_R\) for the logarithmic measure of non-torsion algebraic units of degree at most \(R\), by finiteness at bounded degree and measure and Kronecker's theorem.
The absolute height identity \(m(\alpha^s)/D=sm/d\) gives \(m\ge\mu_R/(AR)\), and (4) gives the asserted heat bound.
The [uniform relative approximation of the median](Yin_C_G0904_v1.md#finite-time-dynamics) now gives the second bound in every case.

## A quantitative return test on the full record polynomial

For every such irreducible unit polynomial, any \(p,j\ge1\) with \(p\) prime, and \(\beta=d^{-1}\deg\gcd(\bar P,X^j-1)\), one has
\[
 \beta(a+1)\log p\ \le\ \log2+(j/d)m p^a \qquad(a=0,1,\ldots).
\]
This is the preceding Smith-form argument applied to \(X^j-1\) and the \(a\) factors \(\Phi_{p^i}(X^j)\), followed by the archimedean bound for \(X^{jp^a}-1\).
It extends the [unramified count of distinct roots](Yin_C_G0245_v1.md#statement) to the gcd degree with multiplicity at every prime.
If \(0<x=jm/d<1\), choose \(a=\lfloor\log(1/x)/\log p\rfloor\) to get \(\beta\le(1+\log2)/\log(1/x)\).
In particular a rank fraction \(\beta\ge\eta>0\) forces \(m\ge(d/j)\exp(-(1+\log2)/\eta)\), uniformly in \(p,d,j\), with the case \(x\ge1\) included.
When \(P\) is also reciprocal of even degree at least four and does not belong to any \(\mathbb Z[X^k]\) for \(k>1\), the [integral cyclic homology realization](Yin_C_G0883_v3.md#cyclic-homology) gives \(d\beta=b_1(M_{f^j};\mathbb F_p)-1\) for all \(p,j\) in one closed hyperbolic bundle.
Thus on a hypothetical shrinking sequence even the largest such rank fraction over all primes and all \(1\le j\le J_d\) tends to zero whenever \(J_d m/d\to0\).

On bounded-measure strict records with growing degree, the criterion (1) has a simpler coverage question.
By the large-prime estimate and power-degree preservation above, the witness prime is bounded once \(A,L,\eta\) and the measure ceiling are fixed.
After removing the power of \(Z\) from \(H\), the argument for \(Z^h-1\) gives an \(h\) bounded only in the same parameters with \(H\) dividing this binomial up to that removed power.
Taking \(j=hs\), the single cyclic realization then has \(b_1(M_{f^j};\mathbb F_p)-1\ge\eta d\) at some \(j=O(d)\).
Conversely any such rank fraction yields (1) with \(H(Z)=Z-1\) and \(s=j\).
This reformulation permits one bounded list of primes for the record family; the existence of the required return on all candidate records is open.

## The cost of restricting the relative norm on a Salem torus

Now let \(\alpha=\lambda>1\) be a Salem number, \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), \(k=\mathbb Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]=2e\), and \(m=\log\lambda\).
Write \(\tau\) for the involution interchanging \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\).
For every subfield \(L\subseteq k\) of degree \(f=[L:\mathbb Q]\), put
\[
 \kappa_t(L)=\min\{E_t(u):0\ne u\in\mathbb Z[\lambda],\
                              u\tau(u)\in L\}.
\]
The unrestricted minimum is \(\kappa_t(k)=\kappa_t\).
For \(t\ge d\) and \(\ell=\lceil(t-1)/2\rceil\) one has
\[
 \frac{\lambda^{(t-d)/f}}{d}
 \ \le\ \kappa_t(L)\
 \le\ 4t d^{d+2}\lambda^{2(d-1)+2\ell/f}.                             \tag{6}
\]
At the original \(T=\lceil d^2(\log(2d))^4\rceil\), consequently,
\[
 \log\kappa_T(L)=(1+o(1))\,\frac{Tm}{f},\qquad
 \frac{\log\kappa_T(L)}{\log\kappa_T}=(1+o(1))[k:L] .                 \tag{7}
\]
The relative errors tend to zero uniformly over the Salem numbers and all their trace subfields as \(d\to\infty\).

To see the lower bound, write \(y=u\tau(u)\), and denote its distinguished real value by \(y_0=u(\lambda)u(\lambda^{-1})\), using the power-basis representatives.
Evaluation of \(u\) at \(\lambda^{-1}\) costs at most \(\sqrt d\) times its coefficient norm.
Evaluation of \(B^{t-1}u\) at \(\lambda\) costs at most \(\sqrt d\,\lambda^{d-1}\) times its coefficient norm and gives \(\lambda^{t-1}u(\lambda)\).
Hence \(|y_0|\le d\lambda^{d-t}E_t(u)\).
At each of the \(e-1\) remaining real places of \(k\), the pair over that place is \(\omega,\overline\omega=\omega^{-1}\) on the unit circle.
Evaluation at \(\omega\) on all \(t\) iterates gives \(\sigma(y)=|u(\omega)|^2\le d E_t(u)/t\).
If \(s=[k:\mathbb Q(y)]\), the distinguished value of \(y\) occurs \(s\) times and its nonzero absolute norm is an integer.
Multiplication of the embedding bounds gives
\[
 1\le |N_{k/\mathbb Q}y|
 \le (d\lambda^{d-t}E_t(u))^s(dE_t(u)/t)^{e-s},
 \qquad
 E_t(u)\ge d^{-1}t^{1-s/e}\lambda^{(t-d)s/e}.                         \tag{8}
\]
For \(y\in L\) one has \(s\ge e/f\), giving the lower half of (6).

For the upper bound put \(I=[\mathcal O_K:\mathbb Z[\lambda]]\), and let \(D_L,D_K\) denote the field discriminants.
The lattice \(I\mathcal O_L\) is contained in \(\mathbb Z[\lambda]\) and has covolume \(I^f\sqrt{|D_L|}\) in the \(f\) real embeddings of \(L\).
Set \(R=2I|D_L|^{1/(2f)}\lambda^{\ell/f}\).
By Minkowski's convex body theorem there is \(0\ne a\in I\mathcal O_L\) with \(|a|\le R\lambda^{-\ell}\) at the distinguished place of \(L\) and \(|\sigma a|\le R\) at every other place.
Indeed this box has volume \((2R)^f\lambda^{-\ell}>2^f I^f\sqrt{|D_L|}\).
Set \(u=\lambda^{-\lfloor(t-1)/2\rfloor}a\).
It belongs to the power order since \(\lambda\) is a unit there, and \(u\tau(u)=a^2\in L\).
Every embedding of every \(\lambda^ju\), \(0\le j<t\), has modulus at most \(R\), since the two off-circle embeddings restrict to the distinguished place of \(L\).
For the Vandermonde matrix \(V_{ih}=\lambda_i^h\), \(0\le h<d\), conversion back to the power basis gives \(E_t(u)\le t d\|V^{-1}\|_2^2 R^2\).
The [cofactor estimate](Yin_C_G0904_v1.md#deterministic) yields \(\|V^{-1}\|_2^2\le d^{d+1}\lambda^{2(d-1)}/|\det V|^2\).
Here \(|\det V|^2=I^2|D_K|\), and the discriminant tower gives \(|D_L|^{1/f}\le |D_K|\).
Substitution proves the upper half of (6).
After taking logarithms the two bounds differ from \(tm/f\) by at most \(O(d\log d+dm+\log t)\).
At \(t=T\) this error is \(o(Tm/f)\) uniformly, since \(f\le d/2\) and the [degree bound](Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) gives \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\).
Applying the result also at \(L=k\) gives (7).

<a id="norm-one"></a>
## Relative norm one and the primitive trace-field generator

In (7) with \(L=\mathbb Q\), the asymptotic \(\log\kappa_T(\mathbb Q)\sim Tm\) persists if \(u\) is restricted further to the units of relative norm one or to the monomials \(\pm\lambda^j\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\).
Indeed these sets are nested inside the rational-relative-norm class, and the centered monomial \(u=\lambda^{-\lfloor(T-1)/2\rfloor}\) gives the matching upper bound \(E_T(u)\le Td\|V^{-1}\|_2^2\lambda^{2\lceil(T-1)/2\rceil}\).
Thus the normalized minimum on any of these classes is asymptotic to \(dm/2\), whereas the unrestricted normalized minimum is asymptotic to \(m\).
The distinction concerns the relative norm of the unit; a unit with nonrational relative norm may still occur in the unrestricted search.

For any fixed \(0<\delta<1\) and all sufficiently large \(d\), every \(u\) with \(\log E_T(u)\le(2-\delta)\log\kappa_T\) satisfies \(\mathbb Q(u\tau(u))=k\), by (7) applied to \(L=\mathbb Q(u\tau(u))\).
For an actual minimizer, (8) gives an additional description of this generator.
Put \(y=u\tau(u)\).
The bound on \(y_0\) and \(\log\kappa_T=(1+o(1))Tm/e\) imply \(-\log|y_0|\ge(1-o(1))Tm\).
Conversely the integer norm and the \(e-1\) bounds \(\log\sigma y\le\log(d\kappa_T/T)\) give \(-\log|y_0|\le(1+o(1))Tm\).
The sum of these other logarithms is at least \(-\log|y_0|\), while each is at most \((1+o(1))Tm/e\).
It follows that, for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), all but \(o(e)\) of those places satisfy \(|\log\sigma y/(Tm/e)-1|\le\varepsilon\).
Conditions using only units of relative norm one or elements with relative norm in a proper subfield therefore omit the integer vectors attaining the heat minimum at this time.
