# Constructing units on the consecutive-coefficient curve from a trace polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## The power relation in a field with one exterior pair

Let \(q\ge2\) be an integer, \(p=q+1\), \(M=pq\) and \(L=p+q\).
Suppose that a monic irreducible integer polynomial \(H\) of degree \(n\ge2\) has one root \(w\) in \((0,2/q)\) and all its other roots in \((-4,0)\).
In \(B=\mathbb Q(w)\) put \(\delta=1-Mw\).
Define at the distinguished real place
\[
 T+T^{-1}=2+w,\qquad T>1,\qquad Y=\frac{pT-q}{p-qT}.
\]
The following conditions are equivalent.

1. The element \(L^2/\delta\) is an algebraic integer.
2. The element \(p-qT\) divides \(L\) in the full ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(T)\).
3. The graph coordinate \(Y\) is an algebraic unit.
4. There exist a real algebraic unit \(\beta>1\), coprime integers \(0<u<v\) and a sign \(e\in\{-1,1\}\) such that \(T=\beta^u\) and \(Y=e\beta^v\).

A real reciprocal quadratic unit \(T>1\) has integral trace \(T+T^{-1}\ge3\), so the strict radial condition \(T+T^{-1}<2p/q\le3\) gives no further solutions in degree two.
The last condition constructs an actual root of the four-term polynomial
\[
 q(1-e X^{u+v})-p(X^u-e X^v).
\]
In particular, the signed norm identity \(M^nH(1/M)=1\) suffices.
It forces \(w<1/M\) and \(e=+1\).
For the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial \(S\) of the Salem number \(T\), the integer on the left is \(q^{2n}S(p/q)\).
The short exponent equals one precisely when \(|Y|\) belongs to the group generated by \(T\), where \(|Y|\) refers to the distinguished real embedding.

For every \(q\ge6\) there are explicit irreducible examples satisfying this same signed norm identity, the exact graph equation, integrality of both coordinates and the totally positive Pisot condition on \(\delta^{-1}\), with one additional trace root below \(-4\).
Their degrees can increase without bound as \(q\) grows.
The [first two trace moments](#moments) also exclude the required trace signature for the Chebyshev correction at every degree, and for every single shifted primitive cyclotomic trace correction with an even prefactor.
In every example \(T,Y\) are multiplicatively independent even modulo roots of unity.
Thus these pairs fail the power relation at all exponents and dilations.
Existence at unbounded \(q\) with all nondistinguished trace roots in \((-4,0)\) and the required integrality remains open.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## Integrality and the two powers

The quadratic \(X^2-(2+w)X+1\) defines a unit \(T\) and is irreducible over \(B\), since its discriminant \(w(w+4)\) is negative at a real embedding of \(B\).
The field \(K=\mathbb Q(T)\) has an involution \(\iota\) over \(B\) with \(\iota(T)=T^{-1}\).
The distinguished trace gives a real inverse pair, and each other trace gives a nonreal pair on the unit circle.
Thus \(T\) is a Salem number.
The rational numbers \(p/q,q/p\) cannot equal the unit \(T\), so \(\delta=(p-qT)(p-qT^{-1})\ne0\).
A direct calculation gives
\[
 \iota(Y)=Y^{-1},\qquad Y+Y^{-1}-2=\frac{L^2w}{1-Mw}.
\]
Since \(Y\) satisfies the monic reciprocal quadratic of trace \(Y+Y^{-1}\), being a unit is equivalent to integrality of this trace.
As \(1=\delta+Mw\), the element \(L^2w/\delta\) is integral exactly when \(L^2/\delta\) is integral.
This proves the trace-field test in the full ring of integers, including at primes dividing \(L\).
For the test in \(K\), write \(A=p-qT\) and \(C=pT-q\).
The identity \(pA+qC=L\) gives \(A\mid L\) if \(Y=C/A\) is integral.
Conversely \(A\mid L\) implies \(A\mid qC\), and \(A\) is coprime to \(q\) since \(A-q(1-T)=1\).
Thus \(A\mid C\), proving integrality of \(Y\), and \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\) then gives its unit property.

One also has \(|Y|>T\).
For \(1<T<p/q\) this follows from \(pT-q-T(p-qT)=q(T^2-1)>0\).
If \(p/q<T\), the required inequality is \(T+T^{-1}<2p/q\), which follows from \(w<2/q\).
When \(Y\) is a unit, \(T\) and \(|Y|\) are positive relative norm-one units in the distinguished embedding.
At every other trace place their moduli equal one because inversion acts by complex conjugation there.
The distinguished logarithms form a discrete subgroup of the real line.
Indeed a bounded interval of logarithms bounds the moduli at every embedding, so only finitely many monic minimal polynomials of degree at most \(2n\) and bounded coefficients can occur.
The logarithm is injective on positive real numbers.
This is the one-real-exterior-pair case of the [bound on independent relative units](Yin_C_G0345_v75.md#cyclic).
The subgroup generated by the logarithms of \(T,|Y|\) therefore has a positive generator \(\log\beta\) with \(\beta\) a unit in their multiplicative group.
Writing the two positive logarithms as multiples gives coprime \(u,v\), and \(|Y|>T\) gives \(v>u\).
Substitution into \((p-qT)Y=pT-q\) gives the stated section with original sign \(\epsilon=-e\).
Conversely, powers of an algebraic unit are units.
The test for \(u=1\) follows from the generator of this same subgroup.

The trace polynomial gives \(S(X)=X^nH(X+X^{-1}-2)\), monic and irreducible of degree \(2n\) by the quadratic extension proved above.
Since \(p/q+q/p-2=1/M\), one has
\[
 q^{2n}S(p/q)=M^nH(1/M)=N_{B/\mathbb Q}(\delta).
\]
If this equals one, \(\delta\) is a unit.
All nondistinguished conjugates of \(\delta\) exceed one, so its distinguished value lies strictly between zero and one, giving \(w<1/M\) and \(T<p/q\).
In particular \(\delta^{-1}\) is a totally positive Pisot unit.

<a id="family"></a>
## An explicit family with one additional exterior pair

Take even \(n\ge4\) and \(q\) with \(2M\ge4^{n-1}\).
This permits \(n=4\) for every \(q\ge6\) and unbounded \(n\) as \(q\) grows.
Using the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, put
\[
 B_{n-2}(x)=U_{n-2}((x+2)/2),\qquad H(x)=x^n+2(Mx-1)B_{n-2}(x).
\]
The polynomial \(B_j\) is monic and integral by the recurrence \(B_0=1,\ B_1=x+2,\ B_{j+1}=(x+2)B_j-B_{j-1}\).
Since \(B_{n-2}(0)=n-1\) is odd, \(H\) is Eisenstein at two.
It satisfies \(M^nH(1/M)=1\) and \(H(0)=-2(n-1)<0\), giving a root in \((0,1/M)\).

At \(\theta_j=(j+1/2)\pi/(n-1)\), \(j=0,\ldots,n-2\), the points \(x_j=2\cos\theta_j-2\) decrease through \((-4,0)\).
The recurrence gives \(B_{n-2}(x_j)=(-1)^j/\sin\theta_j\).
Because \(0<|x_j|<4\), we have \(|x_j|^n<4^{n-1}|x_j|\le2M|x_j|\).
The signs of \(H(x_j)\) therefore alternate, giving \(n-2\) roots between these points.
Also \(B_{n-2}(-4)=n-1\) and \(H(-4)=4^n-2(4M+1)(n-1)<0\).
As \(n\) is even, there is a root below \(-4\), exhausting the degree.
The quadratic lift is irreducible of degree \(2n\) since there is still a trace in \((-4,0)\).
Its roots comprise a positive and a negative exterior root, their reciprocals and \(n-2\) pairs on the unit circle.
The norm calculation above applies because all traces other than \(w\) are negative.
The algebraic integers \(p-qT\) and \(p-qT^{-1}\) have unit product \(\delta\), so \(p-qT\) is a unit.
As before \(Y\) is a unit, \(Y>T>1\), and \(\delta^{-1}\) is a totally positive Pisot unit of degree \(n\), since it generates \(\mathbb Q(w)\).

The trace below \(-4\) supplies an embedding with \(T=-r,\ r>1\).
At this embedding
\[
 1<|Y|=\frac{pr+q}{p+qr}<r .
\]
The first inequality follows from \(p>q,\ r>1\), and the second from \(q(r^2-1)>0\).
Thus \(\log|Y|/\log|T|\) is below one here and above one in the distinguished embedding.
A nontrivial multiplicative relation modulo torsion would give the same ratio at both embeddings, proving independence.
The exceptional root of \(H\) is less than \(-2M+4(n-2)\), since the sum of all its roots is \(-2M\).
These points realize the pole norm at increasing degrees while isolating the conjugate that defeats the power relation.

<a id="moments"></a>
## A trace obstruction at every degree

Let \(q\ge2,\ n\ge3\), and let \(c\ge2\) be even.
Suppose \(B_*(x)\) is monic of degree \(n-2\) with coefficient \(b\) at \(x^{n-3}\), where \(b\ge2(n-2)-1\).
Then
\[
 H_*(x)=x^n+c(Mx-1)B_*(x)
\]
cannot have one positive root and all its other roots in \((-4,0)\).
In particular, increasing \(n\) at fixed \(q\) cannot transfer the preceding Chebyshev family into the required interval, even outside the range \(2M\ge4^{n-1}\).

Write \(a=cM\), so \(a\ge12\) and \(4\mid a\).
The two coefficients after the leading one in \(H_*\) are \(a,ab-c\).
If the desired real roots \(w_i\) existed, their sum \(-a\) would exceed \(-4(n-1)\).
Hence \(n\ge a/4+2=:n_0\).
The second power sum and Cauchy's inequality give
\[
 \frac{a^2}{n}\le\sum_i w_i^2=a^2-2ab+2c
 \le a^2-4a(n-2)+2a+2c .
\]
The function \(f(t)=a^2/t+4a(t-2)-a^2-2a-2c\) is strictly increasing for \(t\ge n_0\), since \(f'(t)>4a-16>0\).
At \(n_0\) it equals \(2a(a-8)/(a+8)-2c>0\): indeed \((a-8)/(a+8)\ge1/5\) and \(c=a/M\le a/6\).
This contradicts \(f(n)\le0\).

This obstruction also covers a single shifted primitive cyclotomic trace polynomial for \(B_*\).
For an order \(m\ge3\) let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity, and use the roots \(\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j}-2\), one for each primitive pair \(j,-j\bmod m\).
If their number is \(n-2\), their sum is \(\mu(m)-2(n-2)\), because the sum of all primitive \(m\)-th roots is the Moebius function \(\mu(m)\) by divisor inversion.
Thus \(b=2(n-2)-\mu(m)\) meets the obstruction for every such order.
