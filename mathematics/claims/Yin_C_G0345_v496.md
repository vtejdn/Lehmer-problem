# An integral bound with a single power of the row resultant for two distinct seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## The corrected divisor at all rational primes

Let \(P,Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be distinct monic minimal polynomials of algebraic units \(a,b\), of degrees \(e,f\), and let \(p,q\) be positive integers.
Suppose nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), satisfy
\[
             P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad
             Q(X)^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q).
\]
Let \(A_p,B_q\) be the monic minimal polynomials of \(a^p,b^q\), put \(C=\operatorname{Res}(A_p,B_q)\), and let \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) be the ordinary row resultant.
By [field recovery at the two nodes](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#nodes), \(A_p,B_q\) have degrees \(e,f\), their roots are disjoint and the power-order indices \(i_p=[\mathbb Z[a]:\mathbb Z[a^p]],\ j_q=[\mathbb Z[b]:\mathbb Z[b^q]]\) are finite.
Write
\[
        \Delta_p=p^e i_p^4,\qquad \Delta_q=q^f j_q^4,\qquad
        \rho=|\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)|,\qquad d=2\min(e,f).
\]
Then in \(\mathbb Z\) one has
\[
                    C^4\ \mid\ R\,(\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3)^d .             \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(4v_\ell(C)\le v_\ell(R)\) at each prime \(\ell\nmid pq\,i_pj_q\rho\), as in the [local bound for the two seeds](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#statement).
At every other prime (1) gives the explicit loss \(d\,v_\ell(\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3)\), independently of the row degrees and coefficients.

<a id="index"></a>
## The integral index of powering modulo a squared minimum

Consider the substitution
\[
        \iota_p:C_p=\mathbb Z[Y]/(A_p^2)\longrightarrow
        D_p=\mathbb Z[X]/(P^2),\qquad Y\longmapsto X^p .
\]
Since \(A_p(a^p)=0\) and \(P\) is monic, \(A_p(X^p)=P(X)H_p(X)\) with \(H_p\in\mathbb Z[X]\), so the map is well defined.
Quotienting source and target by the ideals generated by \(A_p\) and \(P\) gives the order inclusion \(\mathbb Z[a^p]\subset\mathbb Z[a]\) of index \(i_p\).
Multiplication by \(A_p\) identifies the source order with the ideal \((A_p)/(A_p^2)\), and multiplication by \(P\) gives the corresponding target identification.
Under these identifications the map on the ideals sends \(s\in\mathbb Z[a^p]\) to \(H_p(a)s\in\mathbb Z[a]\).
Differentiation at the simple root \(a\) gives
\[
                    H_p(a)=\frac{p a^{p-1}A_p'(a^p)}{P'(a)}\ne0 .
\]
Thus \(\iota_p\) is injective on the ideals and quotients, hence on the full source.

Take the absolute norm in \(K=\mathbb Q(a)=\mathbb Q(a^p)\).
The norm of \(a\) has absolute value one, and the absolute norms of \(A_p'(a^p)\) and \(P'(a)\) are the absolute discriminants of their power bases, as follows by multiplying the differences from the other conjugates.
The discriminants are the determinants of their trace pairings, so their absolute ratio is the square of the change-of-basis determinant \(i_p\).
It follows that \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(H_p(a))|=p^e i_p^2\).
Multiplying the lattice \(\mathbb Z[a]\) by \(H_p(a)\) has this index, and \(H_p(a)\mathbb Z[a^p]\) has the further index \(i_p\) in \(H_p(a)\mathbb Z[a]\).
The index on the ideals is consequently \(p^e i_p^3\).
The exact sequences of each ideal and its quotient multiply their two indices to give \([D_p:\iota_p(C_p)]=p^e i_p^4=\Delta_p\).
The analogous map \(\iota_q:C_q=\mathbb Z[Y]/(B_q^2)\to D_q=\mathbb Z[Z]/(Q^2)\) given by \(Y\mapsto Z^q\) has index \(\Delta_q\).

<a id="tensor"></a>
## An annihilator for the two rows

Regard the four rings as modules over \(\mathbb Z[Y]\) by these substitutions, and put
\[
     M=C_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z[Y]}C_q=\mathbb Z[Y]/(A_p^2,B_q^2),\qquad
     T=D_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z[Y]}D_q .
\]
Let \(\iota:M\to T\) be the induced ring map.
The index computation gives a \(\mathbb Z[Y]\)-linear map \(s_p:D_p\to C_p\) with \(s_p\iota_p=\Delta_p\,\mathrm{id}_{C_p}\).
Indeed \(\Delta_p\) annihilates the finite additive cokernel, so \(\Delta_p x\) has a unique preimage for every \(x\in D_p\), and taking this preimage commutes with the action of \(Y\).
Construct \(s_q\) in the same way.
The composite \((s_p\otimes s_q)\iota\) is multiplication by \(\Delta_p\Delta_q\), so this integer kills \(\ker\iota\).

Write \(x,z\) for the two seed coordinates in \(T\).
Here \(Y=x^p=z^q\), \(P(x)^2=Q(z)^2=0\), and the graph equations give \(U(Y)=-xV(Y)=-zV(Y)\).
Consequently \((x-z)V(Y)=(x-z)U(Y)=0\).
The Sylvester Bézout identity writes \(\rho\) as an integral polynomial combination of \(P\) and \(Q\).
In \(T/(x-z)\) the two polynomials evaluated at the common coordinate have square zero.
The cube of their Bézout combination therefore vanishes, since every term contains two copies of one polynomial.
Thus \(\rho^3\) belongs to \((x-z)T\) and kills both rows in \(T\).
It follows that the row ideal \(J=(U,V)M\) is annihilated by \(k=\Delta_p\Delta_q\rho^3\).

<a id="length"></a>
## The loss in the Sylvester cokernel

It suffices to treat rows whose combined coefficients have gcd one.
Dividing by their common content preserves the monic graph divisibilities, and restoring a positive content \(c\) multiplies \(R\) by \(c^{\deg U+\deg V}\).
Fix a rational prime \(\ell\), and write \(M_\ell=M\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\), \(J_\ell=J\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\).
As in the [length computation for two squared minima](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#full-length), \(M_\ell\) has length \(4v_\ell(C)\) by the determinant of multiplication by \(B_q^2\) on \(\mathbb Z_\ell[Y]/(A_p^2)\).
It is an additive quotient of free modules of ranks \(2e\) and \(2f\) by its two presentations, so it is a quotient of \(\mathbb Z_\ell^d\).
The preimage of \(J_\ell\) in this free module is free of rank at most \(d\), as \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) is a discrete valuation ring.
Hence \(J_\ell\) has at most \(d\) generators.
Since \(k J_\ell=0\), its length is at most \(d\,v_\ell(k)\).

Set \(N=M_\ell/J_\ell\).
Modulo \(\ell\), this has the presentation \(\mathbb F_\ell[Y]/(g)\) for \(g=\gcd(\overline A_p^{\,2},\overline B_q^{\,2},\overline U,\overline V)\).
At least one row has nonzero reduction by normalization, so \(\deg g\le\max(\deg U,\deg V)\le\deg U+\deg V\).
The powers of \(Y\) of degrees below \(\deg U+\deg V\) therefore span \(N/\ell N\), and Nakayama's lemma gives the same span over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) in \(N\).
This is a surjection from the ordinary Sylvester target, and the image of the Sylvester map dies in \(N\) since \(U,V\) vanish there.
Consequently \(\operatorname{length}(N)\le v_\ell(R)\).
Adding the bounds for \(N\) and \(J_\ell\) gives \(4v_\ell(C)\le v_\ell(R)+d\,v_\ell(k)\) for every \(\ell\), proving (1).
