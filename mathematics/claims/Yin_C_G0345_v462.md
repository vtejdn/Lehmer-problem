# Short genuine double contacts at widths close to the sum of the target degrees

<a id="statement"></a>
## The numerator rank and a bound for the shortest genuine row

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with \(\mathbb Q(\delta^a)=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) for every positive integer \(a\).
Write \(d=2s=[\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]\), \(P_a=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\delta^a)\) for the monic minimal polynomial, and \(h=\log M(\delta)>0\).
Fix a nonempty finite set \(I\) of positive integers, \(k=|I|\), \(T=\sum_{a\in I}a\), \(G=\prod_{a\in I}P_a\), and \(m=kd\).
The powers preserve degree, \(\log M(P_a)=ah\), and distinct roots of \(G\) have nontorsion quotient by the [power-field and height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#matrices).
For \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), \(r\ge1\) and fixed formal width \(b=m+r-1\), let \(\Lambda_n\) consist of the integer coefficient vectors of polynomials \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) such that
\[
             G(X)^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}).
\]
Call a row genuine if \(A(\delta^{an})\ne0\) for every \(a\in I\).
Write \(H_n\) for the least one-norm of a genuine integral row.

For each sign and every sufficiently large \(n\), the rational span of \(\Lambda_n\) has dimension \(r\).
For each \(a\in I\), the subspace cut out in this span by \(A(\delta^{an})=0\) has codimension \(q=\min(r,s)\).
Thus when \(r\le s\), **every nonzero rational row is genuine**.
At \(r>s\), any \(r-s+1\) independent rows span a space containing genuine rows.

Choose one \(x_i\) from each inverse pair of roots of \(P_1\).
Let \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_{m/2}\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \(a|\log|x_i||\) for \(1\le i\le s,\ a\in I\), and put
\[
               U_w=\sum_{j=1}^{m/2}(w-2j+2)v_j,\qquad
               \sum_{j=1}^{m/2}v_j=Th.
\]
There are genuine rows for every sufficiently large \(n\), with
\[
 Th\ \le\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_n}{n}
 \ \le\ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_n}{n}
 \ \le\ \frac{U_{m+q-1}}q\ \le\ \frac{(m+q-1)Th}{q}.                 \tag{1}
\]
At \(r=s\), the width is \((k+\tfrac12)d-1\) and the last bound is \((2k+1-2/d)Th\).
For \(I=\{1,2\}\) at this width the bound improves to \((15-10/d)h\).

Under the [exact volume hypotheses](Yin_C_G0345_v461.md#statement) for \(I=\{1,2\}\), including every Salem seed, the upper bound in (1) improves to
\[
                  3h+\frac{C_{\min}-3h}{q},\qquad C_{\min}=U_m-2h.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In this case \(d\ge4\), inverse-pair representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, and either the seed is Salem or the minimal polynomial of \(x_1+x_1^{-1}\) differs from each of \(Z^2-46Z-128,\ Z^2+50Z-32,\ Z^2+14Z+16\).
For a Salem seed \(C_{\min}=(6d-4)h\).
The resulting [degree-dependent tests](#gap) give a conditional unrestricted gap and an equivalence for vanishing measure at widths asymptotic to the target degree sum.

## First variation of the numerator in the signed kernel

Let \(K\) be the splitting field of \(G\).
Choose unit lifts \(g_1,\ldots,g_e\) of a basis of the group generated by its roots modulo torsion.
Fix a residue class \(n\equiv p\bmod L\), where \(L\) is the order of the torsion subgroup.
For a root \(x=\xi_x\prod g_j^{w_{x,j}}\), use \(y_x=\xi_x^p Z^{w_x}\) in the Laurent function field \(F=K(Z_1,\ldots,Z_e)\).
The \(y_x\) are distinct because \(w_x\ne w_{x'}\) at distinct roots, and \(y_{1/x}=1/y_x\).
For each inverse pair take a representative \(x\), and abbreviate \(y=y_x,\ c=\varepsilon x,\ D_Y=Y\,d/dY\).
With an indeterminate \(N\) in place of the exponent \(n\), the two rows at this site impose
\[
 \begin{split}
 A(y)+cy^b A(1/y)&=0,\\
 N(D_Y A)(y)+cy^b\{(1+bN)A(1/y)-N(D_Y A)(1/y)\}&=0.
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
At the actual powers these are the value and Euler derivative of the signed graph.
Its reciprocity up to sign gives the equivalent double vanishing at the inverse.

At \(N=0\) the two conditions impose separate evaluations at \(y,1/y\).
The first \(m\) columns are invertible by the Vandermonde determinant at the \(m\) distinct nodes.
The kernel then consists of \(H(Y)B(Y)\) for
\[
                   H(Y)=\prod_{x:G(x)=0}(Y-y_x),\qquad \deg B<r .
\]
This monic polynomial satisfies \(H(Y)=Y^mH(1/Y)\).
For \(0\le l<r\) hold the coefficients at degrees \(m,\ldots,b\) equal to those of \(H(Y)Y^l\), and solve (3) for the other \(m\) coefficients.
This gives a basis \(A_l(N,Y)\) over \(F(N)\), regular at \(N=0\), with \(A_l(0,Y)=H(Y)Y^l\).
Indeed the matrix of the held coefficients is triangular with diagonal one.

On this lifted kernel the two equations give
\[
  (1+bN) A(y)=N\bigl((D_YA)(y)-cy^b(D_YA)(1/y)\bigr).
\]
At a zero of \(H\), reciprocity gives \((D_YH)(1/y)=-y^{-m}(D_YH)(y)\).
Consequently the numerator at each site, divided by \(N\) and then evaluated at zero, has entries
\[
       (D_YH)(y)\bigl(y^l+c y^{r-1-l}\bigr),\qquad 0\le l<r.          \tag{4}
\]
The common row factor is nonzero since \(H\) has simple roots.

<a id="minor"></a>
## A nonzero numerator minor on each torsion class

Fix \(a\in I\) and take one representative from each inverse pair of roots of \(P_a\).
Choose a real linear functional on the exponent lattice avoiding the finitely many hyperplanes on which a target weight vanishes or two target absolute weights agree.
These hyperplanes are proper since \(w_x\ne0\) and \(w_x\ne\pm w_{x'}\) for different inverse pairs.
Orient the representatives to have positive weights and order any \(q\) of them by strictly decreasing weights \(u_1>\cdots>u_q>0\).
Such reorientation is available in (3) from the start.
Write \(y_i,c_i\) for these oriented nodes and labels.

To obtain a \(q\)-column minor of (4), select the outermost \(\lfloor q/2\rfloor\) pairs of complementary columns \(l,r-1-l\).
If \(q\) is odd, take also the smallest unused column.
Let \(L_l=\max(l,r-1-l)\).
In each selected column the highest exponent of \(y_i\) is \(L_l\) since \(u_i>0\).
For a complementary pair these exponents coincide and the corresponding two leading coefficients in row \(i\) are \(c_i,1\), in increasing column order.
For a central single column the coefficient is \(1+c_i\ne0\), and any other single column has nonzero leading coefficient as well.

The largest possible weight in the determinant pairs the largest \(L_l\)'s with the largest \(u_i\)'s.
Strictness between different column pairs forces their assigned row pairs, leaving only the swaps inside each pair.
Any extra single column has smaller \(L_l\) than the selected pairs and receives the last row.
All maximizing terms therefore have the same Laurent monomial, including the same product of torsion factors from the \(y_i\).
Its coefficient, up to the common torsion factor and sign, is the product of \(c_i-c_j\) for consecutive assigned rows and the leading coefficient of the single column when present.
It is nonzero because the oriented \(x_i\) are distinct.
This also covers \(q=1\).
The chosen minor of (4) is nonzero over \(F\), and the corresponding numerator minor of the lifted basis has a nonzero coefficient of \(N^q\).

Each of the pivot determinant and the numerator of this rational minor, after clearing denominators, is a nonzero polynomial in \(N,Z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,Z_e^{\pm1}\).
Every required denominator is likewise nonzero as a Laurent polynomial.
On substituting \((N,Z)=(n,g_1^n,\ldots,g_e^n)\), each such polynomial gives a nonzero polynomial-coefficient recurrence with pairwise nontorsion base ratios after collecting like exponent vectors.
The [nondegenerate recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives eventual nonvanishing, as in the [fixed-support specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height).
Only finitely many targets, signs and residue classes occur.

The specialized signed kernel is the extension to \(K\) of the rational remainder kernel modulo \(G^2\), so has dimension \(r\) over its field of definition.
Within it the rational condition \(A(\delta^{an})=0\) is equivalently the vanishing of the remainder of \(A(X^n)\) modulo \(P_a\).
After scalar extension to \(K\) this imposes all conjugate evaluations, since \(P_a\) is separable.
The value equation in (3) makes the two evaluations at each inverse pair proportional on the kernel.
Hence at most \(s\) scalar equations remain, and the minor proves rank \(q\).
Dimensions are preserved by scalar extension, giving the claimed rational codimension.

## From the integral minors to a genuine short vector

At the actual exponent \(n\), take the maximal minors of the \(m\)-row matrix (3) in the coefficient basis \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^b\).
Every entry and minor is an algebraic integer since the sites and labels are units.
The complementary signed minors are projective coordinates of the rational kernel.
The logarithm of the covolume of its saturated integer lattice differs by \(O_b(1)\) from their absolute logarithmic projective height, by the [primitive wedge calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v256.md#support).
The same calculation bounds the product of the Euclidean successive minima \(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_r\) above and below by fixed positive multiples of that covolume.

At an embedding \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), put \(u_x=\log|\sigma(x)|\) for each oriented site.
Up to a factor \(O_{b,G}(1+n)\), either entry of column \(l\) at that site has absolute value at most
\[
           \exp\bigl(n(bu_x/2+|l-b/2|\,|u_x|)\bigr).
\]
Each site occurs twice in a determinant and each selected column once.
The \(m\) largest distances from \(b/2\) consist of two copies of \(b/2-j+1\) for \(1\le j\le m/2\).
Pairing these distances in decreasing order with the repeated absolute site weights gives the upper bound \(n(b\sum_xu_x+U_b)+O_{b,G}(\log(2+n))\) for the log maximum of the minors.
The absolute site weights at every embedding have the same multiset since conjugation permutes the inverse pairs of each \(P_a\).
The linear part \(b\sum_xu_x\) averages to zero by the unit product formula, and every finite-place contribution to the projective height is nonpositive by integrality.
It follows that
\[
               \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\prod_{i=1}^r\lambda_i
                 \le U_b .                                             \tag{5}
\]

The [lower bound from the exterior double contacts](Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#attainment) gives \(\liminf \log\lambda_1/n\ge Th\).
Its argument applies to every nonzero two-row graph with these factors, including rows with a zero numerator, and the one-norm and Euclidean norm are comparable at fixed \(b\).
Set \(j=r-q+1\).
Since \(\prod_i\lambda_i\ge\lambda_1^{r-q}\lambda_j^q\), (5) gives \(\limsup \log\lambda_j/n\le (U_b-(r-q)Th)/q=U_{m+q-1}/q\).
Choose \(j\) independent integral rows of lengths at most \(\lambda_j\).
Their rational span has dimension greater than the dimension \(r-q\) of each forbidden subspace.
For each \(a\) at least one of these rows has nonzero numerator.
Label the rows \(A_1,\ldots,A_j\) and consider \(\sum_{i=1}^j t^{i-1}A_i\).
Each target rules out at most \(j-1\) integers \(t\), by a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(j-1\) over \(K\).
Some \(t\in\{0,\ldots,k(j-1)\}\) therefore gives a genuine row of one-norm bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\lambda_j\).
This proves (1), including \(j=1\) when a shortest Euclidean row itself suffices.

For \(I=\{1,2\}\), the total weight is \(3h\) and \(v_1\le2h\), so \(U_w\le 3wh-2\sum_{l\ge2}v_l\le(3w-2)h\).
At \(w=5d/2-1\), division by \(s\) gives \((15-10/d)h\).
When the exact [signed lattice volume formula](Yin_C_G0345_v461.md#volume) applies, the left side of (5) equals \(C_{\min}+3(r-1)h\).
The same successive-minimum inequality gives (2).

## A degree-dependent width test for Lehmer's problem

Write \(\kappa=(\log2)/8\) and let \(\lambda\) be the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.
At fixed \(b\), any slope bound strictly below \(\kappa\) gives \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) at every sufficiently large \(n\).
For any seed as above with \(h\le\kappa/((2k+1)T)\), (1) at \(r=s\) gives this strict inequality and \(k\) genuine double factors of distinct positive measures \(ah,\ a\in I\), for each sign.
Thus suppose that for every such power-field-preserving reciprocal seed some sign admits arbitrarily large \(n\) at which no passing row of width \((k+\tfrac12)d-1\) realizes all the specified factors genuinely.
Then \(\lambda\ge\kappa/((2k+1)T)\).
Indeed a smaller positive measure would supply an irreducible factor of positive measure below this bound by factorization.
At these small heights the [reciprocal-unit reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) and the [all-power norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) give a seed of that same irreducible measure, contradicting the construction for both signs.
For \(I=\{1,\ldots,k\}\) the threshold is \((\log2)/(4k(k+1)(2k+1))\).
In particular the pair \(\delta,\delta^2\) gives the conditional gap \(\lambda\ge(\log2)/120\) from exclusions at the single width \(5d/2-1\) for each seed.
The exclusion remains a condition to be established.

For the same pair and \(0<h\le\kappa/15\), a sufficient width for passing is \(2d+r-1\), where
\[
                  r=1+\left\lfloor\frac{3h(2d-1)}{\kappa-3h}\right\rfloor.
\]
Indeed \(3h(2d-1)/(\kappa-3h)\le(2d-1)/4<s\).
The defining strict inequality for \(r\) gives \(3(2d+r-1)h/r<\kappa\), so (1) applies.

There is also a zero-gap equivalence at widths asymptotic to the least width \(2d\) of a genuine row for this pair in the [unrestricted-norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement).
If \(\lambda=0\), choose all-power reciprocal seeds of positive measures \(h_j\to0\) as above, with degrees \(d_j\to\infty\).
The last assertion follows because monic integer polynomials of bounded degree and bounded measure form a finite set, by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
Set \(r_j=\max(1,\lceil d_j\sqrt{h_j}\rceil)\).
Eventually \(r_j\le d_j/2\) and \(b_j=2d_j+r_j-1=2d_j+o(d_j)\).
For the two targets let \(H_{j,n}\) be the shortest genuine one-norm at this fixed width for seed \(j\).
By (1),
\[
     0\le \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_{j,n}}n
          \le\frac{3b_j h_j}{r_j}\longrightarrow0,
\]
for each sign, since \(r_j\ge d_j\sqrt{h_j}\).
Conversely, suppose there exist all-power reciprocal seeds of degrees \(d_j\to\infty\), widths \(b_j\ge2d_j\) with \(b_j/d_j\to2\), and signs, possibly depending on \(j\), such that \(\lim_{j\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log H_{j,n}=0\).
The lower bound \(3h_j\) in (1) forces \(h_j\to0\) and hence \(\lambda=0\).
In both directions the limit over exponents is taken separately at each fixed seed and width.

The coordinating agent for Sheng Yin proposed the first variation, the complementary-column minor and the unrestricted degree-dependent width test.
A collaborating agent developed the rank proof and the successive-minimum bounds for a finite set of powers.
