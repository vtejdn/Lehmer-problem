# Mahler measure in divisible conjugate groups with even inverse-pair flips

<a id="statement"></a>
## Mixed radicals and the primitive measure on a coordinate line

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit of degree \(2n\), \(n\ge3\), and \(L\) its rational splitting field.
Assume \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) contains, for every even subset of inverse pairs, the permutation interchanging the two roots in each selected pair and fixing every other root.
Orient the pairs by choosing one root \(\alpha_i\) from each.
Write \([z]\) for the multiplicative class modulo roots of unity, in additive notation, and \(m(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure.
In \(K_i=\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)\) choose a primitive unit on the line of \(\alpha_i\) modulo torsion, oriented so that \([\alpha_i]=e[\rho_i]\), where \(e\) is the same positive integer for all \(i\).
Set \(\lambda=m(\alpha)/e\), and let \(k\) count the inverse pairs off the unit circle.

Every nontorsion element \(w\) in the divisible group generated by all conjugates of \(\alpha\), including all torsion factors, has unique rational coordinates \([w]=\sum_i q_i[\rho_i]\).
Write \(s=|\{i:q_i\ne0\}|\), \(d=\deg w\) and \(\ell=\min(s,k)\).
Then
\[
           J=\frac{d}{2n}\sum_i|q_i|\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
           m(w)\ge J\lambda/\ell .                                       \tag{1}
\]
For \(n\ge5\) one can replace \(\ell\) in the denominator by \(B_\ell=\min(\ell,\sqrt{3\ell})\).
On a single coordinate the value is exactly \(J\lambda\), and the least such value is \(\lambda\).
If \(w\) first attains its measure at its degree and minimizes all positive integer-polynomial measures through that degree, then \(d>2n\) forces \(J<\ell\), and \(J<B_\ell\) when \(n\ge5\).

Suppose further that the action on the \(n\) labels contains \(A_n\), with \(n\ge8\), and write \(t=|\mu(L)|\).
Then \(t\mid24\), and every mixed element \((s\ge2)\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
     m(w)/\lambda\ \ge (n-1)/(2t), &2\le s<n,\\
     m(w)/\lambda\ \ge 2^{n-1}/(8tk), &s=n .
 \end{array}                                                            \tag{2}
\]
For \(s=2\) the lower bound improves to \((2n-k-1)/(2t)\).
In particular for \(n\ge50\) the least positive measure in the entire divisible group is \(\lambda\), and only the coordinate lines can attain it.
Every strict degree record in that group in this range has degree \(2n\) and measure \(\lambda\).
As \(n\to\infty\) the mixed measures tend to infinity uniformly over these source units.

For the [degree-eighteen radial-rank-two minimum](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#statement), put \(M_0=\exp(m(\alpha))\) for a root of its displayed polynomial.
In its entire divisible conjugate group every mixed element satisfies \(m(w)\ge(7/2)\log M_0>\log2\).
The complete list of multiplicative measures strictly between one and two there, at arbitrary degrees, is \(M_0,M_0^2,M_0^3\).

<a id="norms"></a>
## Coordinate norms give an integer after arbitrary radical extraction

The even flips act on the rational space on the oriented labels by pairwise distinct sign characters, since one can flip \(i\) and a third label while fixing \(j\).
Averaging against a character projects to its coordinate, and transitivity on labels makes the space irreducible.
Its map onto the conjugate classes of \(\alpha\) modulo torsion is nonzero and equivariant, hence injective, as in the [signed-action argument](Yin_C_G0901_v58.md#signs).
It follows that the oriented classes are independent and that all \(2n\) signed classes of the actual roots are distinct.
Thus every positive power of each \(\alpha_i\) generates \(K_i\).
The [saturated line in the original field](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#primitive) gives the primitive \(\rho_i\), with the index \(e\) independent of \(i\) by conjugation, and \(\mathbb Q(\rho_i)=K_i\).
Its measure is \(\lambda\), and the classes \([\rho_i]\) have the signed action specified by the original roots even if the chosen units acquire torsion factors.

Take a finite normal closure \(N\) containing \(L,w\) and all conjugates of \(w\).
On the span of the coordinate classes the action of \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)\) factors through \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
The subgroup fixing \(K_i\) fixes \([\rho_i]\), and averaging over it kills every other coordinate.
Indeed a flip at \(j\ne i\) and at a third label different from \(i,j\) fixes \(\alpha_i\) and negates \([\rho_j]\), so the sum of its orbit images cancels.
Put \(E_i=K_i(w)\).
Taking the norm of the class of \(w\) to \(K_i\), first from \(N\) and then using transitivity through \(E_i\), gives
\[
                [N_{E_i/K_i} w]=[E_i:K_i]q_i[\rho_i].
\]
The norm is a unit in \(K_i\), and primitivity on that field's line gives \([E_i:K_i]q_i\in\mathbb Z\).
This is the [coordinate orbit norm](Yin_C_G0901_v51.md#integral) with the primitive line in place of a rank-one relative unit group.
Reorient each supported coordinate to make its coefficient positive.
The stabilizer of \(w\) preserves this weighted oriented support by uniqueness of the coordinates.
Its orbit \(O\) through the oriented root \(\alpha_i^{\pm1}\) has size \([E_i:\mathbb Q(w)]\), by Galois restriction.
Therefore \(d|O|=2n[E_i:K_i]\).
Summing the positive integers \(|q_i|[E_i:K_i]\) over one representative of each orbit gives exactly \(J\).

<a id="height"></a>
## Sign averages at the actual degree

Put \(a_i=|\log|\rho_i||\).
As \([\alpha_i]=e[\rho_i]\), one has \(\sum_i a_i=\lambda\), with \(k\) nonzero entries.
Average \(|\log|g w||\) over \(g\in\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)\).
For any fixed action on the labels, one may first average over the uniform even flips, retaining any initial signs of a coset representative.
Write \(S=\sum_i \epsilon_i u_i\) for this signed sum, with \(|u_i|=|q_i|a_{\pi(i)}\) and at most \(\ell\) nonzero terms.
The signs on any two distinct labels are independent and uniform, including for \(n=3\).
Thus \(|u_i|=|\mathbb E(\epsilon_i S)|\le\mathbb E|S|\) and \(\mathbb E|S|\ge\sum_i|u_i|/\ell\).
Each label visits every target with equal probability, so averaging the sum gives \(\lambda\sum_i|q_i|/n\).
Multiplication by \(d/2\) gives (1) by the unit product formula, with equality on an axis.
For \(n\ge5\) the even flips are four-wise independent.
Hence \(\mathbb E S^2=\sum_i u_i^2\) and \(\mathbb E S^4\le3(\sum_i u_i^2)^2\).
Hölder's inequality gives \(\mathbb E|S|\ge(\mathbb E S^2)^{3/2}/(\mathbb E S^4)^{1/2}\) when the sum is nonzero, and Cauchy's inequality then yields \(\mathbb E|S|\ge\sum_i|u_i|/\sqrt{3\ell}\), also valid when all its terms vanish.
At a strict degree record of degree greater than \(2n\), the positive-measure competitor \(\rho_i\) of degree \(2n\) implies \(m(w)<\lambda\), proving the asserted strict conditions on \(J\).

<a id="denominators"></a>
## Bounding the rational coordinates in the field fixed by their stabilizer

Let \(H\) be the stabilizer of the coefficient vector \(q\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), \(F=L^H\), \(b=[F:\mathbb Q]=|Gq|\), with \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).
The stabilizer of \(w\) in the larger normal closure restricts into \(H\), so \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(w)\).
Set \(v=N_{\mathbb Q(w)/F}w\) and \(Q_i=[\mathbb Q(w):F]q_i\).
All the relative conjugates in this norm have the same class, hence \([v]=\sum_i Q_i[\rho_i]\).
Any automorphism fixing \(v\) fixes this vector and belongs on \(L\) to \(H\), so \(\mathbb Q(v)=F\).
This [norm descent for torsion-equivalent conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) gives \(m(v)=m(w)\), since \(h(v)=[\mathbb Q(w):F]h(w)\).
In particular at any first degree of attainment one has \(d=b\).

For distinct indices \(i,j\) let \(f_{ij}\) flip their two pairs and fix all remaining roots.
For a supported \(i\) and distinct \(j,l\) away from it, \((1-f_{ij})(1-f_{il})[v]=4Q_i[\rho_i]\).
This class is represented by a unit in \(L\), using the actual quotients of conjugates of \(v\).
If \(s<n\), choose \(j\) with \(Q_j=0\), and already \((1-f_{ij})[v]=2Q_i[\rho_i]\).
Every element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_i)\) fixes either isolated class, so it changes its representative by a root of unity in \(L\).
Raising the representative to \(t=|\mu(L)|\) puts it in \(K_i\).
Primitivity of \(\rho_i\) now proves
\[
       2tQ_i\in\mathbb Z\quad(s<n),\qquad 4tQ_i\in\mathbb Z\quad(s=n).
\]
For \(s=1\) the field \(F\) is \(K_i\) because the signed coordinate determines the oriented root, and \(Q_i\) is already integral.

If the trace permutation group contains \(A_n\), it acts transitively on the \(s\)-sets for every \(s\).
For \(s<n\) the even flips give all \(2^s\) supported signs, hence \(b\ge2^s\binom ns\).
On \(2\le s\le n-1\) the minimum of this bound is \(2n(n-1)\), since consecutive ratios \(2(n-s)/(s+1)\) decrease and the last endpoint \(2^{n-1}n\) is at least the first.
The expectation in the height proof applied to \(v\) is at least \(|Q_i|\lambda/n\) for any one fixed supported \(i\).
Thus \(m(w)=m(v)\ge b\lambda/(4tn)\) in this case.
For full support \(b\ge2^{n-1}\), and at every permutation the largest \(|Q_i|a_{\pi(i)}\) is at least \(\max_i a_i/(4t)\ge\lambda/(4tk)\).
These estimates prove (2).

For support two, the mean of \(|\epsilon x+\epsilon' y|\) over two independent signs is \(\max(|x|,|y|)\).
All pairs of positions occur uniformly, each nonzero \(|Q_i|\) is at least \(1/(2t)\), and \(b\ge4\binom n2\).
Therefore \(m(v)\ge t^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\max(a_i,a_j)\).
Edges between neutral and nonneutral labels give \((n-k)\lambda\).
Among nonneutral labels \(\max(a_i,a_j)\ge(a_i+a_j)/2\) gives another \((k-1)\lambda/2\), proving the improvement stated for \(s=2\).
The [integral-monomial bounds for individual flips](Yin_C_G0345_v62.md#orbit) and the [two-exterior-root integral bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v74.md#unbalanced) concern integer coefficients before radical extraction.
Here the denominators are controlled after descent at the original total measure.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Torsion in a splitting field with alternating action on the pair labels

Let \(V\) be the kernel on pair labels.
It lies between the full even flip space and the full binary coordinate space.
All double flips are conjugate since \(A_n\) is transitive on unordered pairs.
Their common image in the abelianization of \(G\) is trivial, since the three double flips on the edges of a triangle multiply to one and each has order two.
Thus the image of \(V\) there has order at most two.
If the trace group is \(A_n\), its perfection for \(n\ge5\) shows that this image is the entire abelianization.
If it is \(S_n\), a lift of a transposition suffices in addition, and its square is an even flip because the sign vector of the square is the sum of a sign vector and its permutation.
Consequently every abelian quotient of \(G\) has exponent at most two.
In particular \((\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z)^\times\), the group of the cyclotomic field generated by \(\mu(L)\), has exponent at most two.
For an odd prime divisor \(p\) of \(t\) this forces \(p-1\le2\), and the modulus nine is excluded by its order-six unit group.
The modulus sixteen is also excluded since three has order four modulo sixteen.
This proves \(t\mid24\).

For \(n\ge50\), (2) is strictly greater than one in both cases, using \(k\le n\).
Since every axis measure is a positive integer multiple of \(\lambda\) and \(\rho_i\) attains it, the least measure follows.
Every mixed vector then has orbit size greater than \(2n\), and an axis representative has degree at least \(2n\) by its orbit.
A strict record must match the competitor \(\rho_i\) in degree and measure.
Finally two distinct coordinate units \(\rho_i,\rho_j\) are multiplicatively independent and each has height \(\lambda/(2n)\).
Their joint field has degree at most \(4n^2\).
The [rank-two height-product estimate](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) therefore yields \(\lambda\gg(\log(12n^2))^{-106}\) with an absolute implied constant.
The bounds (2) grow at least linearly in \(n\) as multiples of \(\lambda\) for sufficiently large \(n\), proving the uniform divergence.

## All measures below two in the degree-eighteen example

For the [certified degree-eighteen input](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#group) the trace group is \(S_9\), with every double flip, and \(k=3\).
The trace minimum \(Q\) there has \(Q(2)=Q(-2)=1\), so the product of its nine conjugate radicands \(t_i^2-4\) is one.
Choose square roots of these radicands in \(L\).
Their product is \(1\) or \(-1\), hence fixed, so every element of \(G\) has an even number of sign changes relative to these oriented square roots.
These orientations identify a square root with the difference of the two reciprocal roots of its pair.
The kernel \(V\) is consequently exactly the even flips.
It dies in \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) by the triangle argument, so the only quadratic subfield belongs to the sign of \(S_9\).
The [Frobenius at thirteen](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#frobenius) has trace cycles of lengths two and seven, hence acts nontrivially in this quadratic field.
Thirteen splits in both \(\mathbb Q(i)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) since \(13\equiv1\bmod12\).
Thus neither is contained in \(L\), and \(t\mid24\) gives \(t=2\).

The primitive line unit \(\rho_i\) has degree eighteen and lies in the divisible group of this input.
Its radial rank is two by the [rank and finite minimum](Yin_C_G0901_v63.md#statement), so its measure is at least \(m(\alpha)\).
As \(\lambda=m(\alpha)/e\), necessarily \(e=1\).
For \(s=2\) the strengthened bound is \((18-3-1)/4=7/2\) times \(\lambda\).
For \(3\le s\le8\), the orbit bound \(2^s\binom9s\) is at least \(672\) by its two endpoints, so the proper-support estimate \(b\lambda/(4tn)\) exceeds \((7/2)\lambda\).
For \(s=9\) the full-support bound is \(256\lambda/(8\cdot2\cdot3)=(16/3)\lambda\).
The rational lower bound \(M_0>12193/10000\) gives \(M_0^7>4\) by integer powers, so every mixed measure exceeds \(\log2\).
On an axis all positive measures are positive integer multiples of \(\log M_0\), and every such multiple is realized by an ordinary positive power.
The [certified upper and lower cuts](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0188/readme.md#cut) give \(M_0^3<(3049/2500)^3<2\) and \(M_0^4>(12193/10000)^4>2\), completing the list.
