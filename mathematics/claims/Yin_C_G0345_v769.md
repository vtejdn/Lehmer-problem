# Individual factor heights on all long monomial sections of a fixed plane curve

<a id="statement"></a>
## The projected width, including arbitrary torsion translations

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) in the Laurent ring, with support differences of rank two.
For nonzero \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\), put \(g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)\), \(N=\max(|a|,|b|)\), and
\[
 W=\max_{(i,j)\in\operatorname{supp}F}(ai+bj)
       -\min_{(i,j)\in\operatorname{supp}F}(ai+bj).
\]
Let \(\xi,\eta\) be any roots of unity and \(z\) any nonzero nontorsion algebraic number with \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\).
As \(N/g\to\infty\),
\[
                         W h(z)=m(F)+o_F(1),\qquad m(F)>0.                 \tag{1}
\]
The error is uniform in the direction, common dilation, orders of both translations and choice of root.
The height is the absolute logarithmic Weil height, and \(m(F)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure on the two-torus.

For the untwisted specialization the logarithmic measure of each primitive rational nontorsion irreducible factor divided by the total measure is asymptotic to its degree divided by \(W\).
More generally let \(K=\mathbb Q(\xi,\eta)\), \(k=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and take the product over the \(k\) embeddings of \(K\) of the translated specialization, removing its lowest monomial.
This rational polynomial \(P\) is primitive integral for all sufficiently large \(N/g\), and uniformly
\[
          \frac{m(P)}k\longrightarrow m(F),\qquad
          \frac{m(S)}{m(P)}
                =(1+o_F(1))\frac{\deg S}{kW}                              \tag{2}
\]
for each primitive rational nontorsion irreducible factor \(S\).
In particular a sequence of such factors has \(m(S)\to0\) exactly when \(\deg S=o(W)\), even if \(k\) tends to infinity.
The [earlier plane-curve bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) compare untwisted heights to \(1/N\) up to constants.
The [Möbius graph formula](Yin_C_G0345_v764.md#statement) treats all factors at slopes tending to infinity on that family.

<a id="geometry"></a>
## Distinct curve points and a uniform upper bound

Put \(x=\xi z^a,\ y=\eta z^b\).
For nontorsion \(z\), the relation lattice of \((x,y)\) modulo roots of unity is precisely the line generated by \((b/g,-a/g)\).
Consequently as \(N/g\to\infty\) every sequence of such pairs eventually avoids every fixed finite set.
The same holds for lifts to a smooth projective normalization of any one geometric component.
The components are conjugate, and each has rank-two support differences.
Indeed, if one were a translate of a one-dimensional subtorus, all its coefficient conjugates would have the same binomial exponent difference up to sign, giving rank at most one for \(F\).
A component has no nontrivial monomial constant on it.

The projection on the finite support of \(F\) is injective for all sufficiently large \(N/g\), since an exponent difference causing a collision is a nonzero integral multiple of \((b/g,-a/g)\).
The rank-two assumption gives \(W\asymp_F N\) by compactness on the unit sup-norm sphere.
No nonempty proper subsum of \(F\) vanishes at the moving points for sufficiently large \(N/g\).
Indeed such a subsum is rationally coprime to \(F\), since a divisible subsum with a contained Newton polygon would have to be a scalar multiple of \(F\).
A shared absolute component would give a rational common divisor by conjugation.
Their intersection in the torus is therefore finite, and there are only finitely many subsums.

For any gap \(i<j\) between consecutive projected exponents, take the initial subsum up to \(i\), after shifting the minimum exponent to zero.
It is nonzero at \(z\).
At \(|z|_v\ge1\) bound it by \(L_v|z|_v^i\), and at \(|z|_v<1\) use the opposite subsum to bound it by \(L_v|z|_v^j\), with \(L_v=\|F\|_1\) at infinity and \(L_v=1\) otherwise.
The two translations have modulus one at every place, irrespective of their field.
The product formula gives \((j-i)h(z)\le\log\|F\|_1\).
A largest gap is at least \(W/(|\operatorname{supp}F|-1)\), hence \(Nh(z)\ll_F1\).

<a id="metric"></a>
## A real direction on the normalization

Pass to a subsequence with \((b/N,-a/N)\to (s,t)\) and a fixed choice of the signed coordinate attaining \(N\).
By conjugating the algebraic points and the translations, work on one fixed geometric component over a number field, enlarging the field so the zeros and poles of the coordinate functions \(x,y\) on its smooth projective normalization \(C\) are rational.
Use the same letters for the functions and their values on the curve.
There is a semipositive adelic real divisor \(\overline D\) on \(C\) whose height on the open torus curve is
\[
        h_{\overline D}(P)
          =\sum_v d_v\,\max(0,s\log |x(P)|_v+t\log |y(P)|_v),             \tag{3}
\]
where \(d_v\) are the usual normalized place weights in a field containing \(P\).
Its underlying divisor is
\[
        D=\sum_Q\max(0,-s\,\operatorname{ord}_Q x-t\,\operatorname{ord}_Q y)[Q].
\]
It has positive degree.
The integer divisor vectors of \(x\) and \(y\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb R\), since otherwise an integral combination would define a constant monomial.
Their indicated real combination is nonzero, of degree zero, and therefore has a negative coefficient somewhere.

For a rational direction the displayed construction is a positive real multiple of the canonical sup-norm divisor at infinity on \(\mathbb P^1\) pulled back by a nonconstant \(x^u y^v\), with \(u,v\) integral and a common positive scaling.
It is semipositive and has nonnegative height on the full projective curve.
For an irrational direction, approximate \((s,t)\) by rational pairs \((s_n,t_n)\).
At each \(Q\) in the boundary at least one of the two orders is nonzero.
Hence \(s\operatorname{ord}_Q x+t\operatorname{ord}_Q y\ne0\), and its sign is constant in a common neighborhood of \((s,t)\) for all boundary points.
On that neighborhood the coefficients of the underlying divisors vary linearly.

Here are the local convergence details for these varying divisors.
In a parameter \(u\) at \(Q\), write \(x=u^{i}f,\ y=u^{j}h\) with \(f,h\) analytic units.
Near \(Q\) the sign of the diverging term \((s_n i+t_n j)\log |u|\) fixes which branch of the maximum occurs, uniformly for large \(n\), since the logarithms of the units are bounded there.
After removing the indicated logarithmic divisor singularity the remaining term is zero or \(s_n\log|f|+t_n\log|h|\); it converges uniformly.
Away from these neighborhoods both coordinate logarithms are bounded on the compact complement and the maxima converge uniformly there as well.
These arguments apply also to the continuous absolute values on the Berkovich curve.
Outside finitely many places take a smooth proper model with disjoint boundary sections and both principal divisors supported on their horizontal closures.
The coordinate units off those sections and the local unit factors at each section have norm one on their residue domains.
Thus the displayed metrics agree there with the model metrics of the real combinations of the horizontal sections.
At the finitely many remaining places the preceding uniform convergence, after subtraction of fixed Green functions for the boundary with the varying divisor coefficients, gives the continuous adelic limit.
The rational approximants are scaled pullbacks of semipositive metrics.
Equivalently, at the good places their model divisors are relatively nef, and at the remaining places their semipositive Green functions converge locally uniformly in a local trivialization with the varying boundary coefficients removed.
For approximation on the fixed real divisor \(D\), keep the component of \((s,t)\) of modulus one fixed and bracket the other component by two converging rational numbers within the same sign interval.
The positive barycentric weights giving \((s,t)\) from the two resulting rational pairs give exactly \(D\) from their underlying divisors, by the linearity on this interval.
Their weighted Green functions therefore define a metric on \(D\) and converge uniformly relative to any fixed metric there by the local calculation above.
At a finite place the two rational pullbacks admit relatively nef model metrics, by resolving their maps to the projective line on a common dominating model and pulling back its standard model of \(\mathcal O(1)\).
Their positive real combination stays relatively nef on that model.
At infinity the corresponding positive combinations remain plurisubharmonic in local trivializations.
This is uniform semipositive approximation on the fixed divisor as required by [the local metric definition](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#adelic-divisors).
The convergence also gives nonnegative height at every fixed point, including the boundary, from the nonnegative heights of the rational approximants.

<a id="common"></a>
## Why all choices of a factor have the same height limit

For the point \(P\) specified by \(z\) the right side of (3) equals \(|sa+tb|h(z)\), by the product formula and the torsion translations.
Since \((b/N,-a/N)\to(s,t)\), one has \(|sa+tb|/N\to0\).
The uniform upper bound for \(Nh(z)\) therefore gives \(h_{\overline D}(P)\to0\).
These points are generic on \(C\), since they avoid finite sets.
Their heights and nonnegativity on all of \(C\) give both essential and absolute minimum zero.
On a curve the positive degree of \(D\) means it is big.

For this semipositive real divisor, [the global height-convergence result of Ballaÿ and Sombra](../audits/literature/BS26/Yin_U001.md#equidistribution-height) gives a common limit of \(h_{\overline M}(P)\) for every generic \(\overline D\)-small sequence and every fixed adelic divisor \(\overline M\).
In the rational-direction case [the height-convergence lemma of Chambert-Loir and Thuillier](../audits/literature/CLT09/Yin_U001.md#height-convergence) already applies to the integral pullback after rescaling.
Here one can take \(\overline M\) to be the ordinary sup-metric pullback by the coordinate whose exponent attains \(N\).
Its height is exactly \(Nh(z)\).
This supplies one common limit for all selections of nontorsion roots, including selections from differently translated sections of these directions.
All points may be placed on the same component by coefficient conjugation, which preserves absolute heights.
Uniformity along the directional subsequence follows by selecting a violating root wherever the convergence would fail and applying the same theorem to the resulting generic sequence.

<a id="calibration"></a>
## Determining the constant from whole untwisted sections

The torus curve \(F=0\) contains only finitely many torsion points by [Bilu's torsion-subvariety theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#torus-bogomolov-complement), since no component is a torsion coset.
For any fixed \(\xi,\eta\) and \((a,b)\), each such pair has at most \(g\) preimages under \(z\mapsto(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)\).
Every nonzero root of a specialization has multiplicity at most \(|\operatorname{supp}F|-1\) by the [Vandermonde argument](Yin_C_G0345_v764.md#mass).
The torsion degree per section is therefore \(O_F(g)=o(W)\).
In particular nontorsion roots exist even in the untwisted sections for all sufficiently large \(N/g\).

Write \(P_0\) for the untwisted section with lowest monomial removed.
It has degree \(W\) and is primitive by eventual injectivity of the support projection.
The [Boyd–Lawton limit](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(m(P_0)\to m(F)\) since the shortest relation has length \(N/g\).
For a primitive univariate integer polynomial the total measure is the sum of the degrees of its irreducible factors times their root heights, including multiplicities.
The nontorsion roots of \(P_0\) have total degree \(W-O_F(g)\).
The uniform common limit for \(Nh(z)\), and \(W/N\asymp_F1\), consequently give \(Wh(z)=m(F)+o(1)\) on the entire directional subsequence, for translated and untwisted roots alike.
The [untwisted lower height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) on the nontorsion roots makes \(m(F)>0\).
Compactness of the normalized directions, and the finite choices of maximal coordinate and component, prove the uniform assertion (1).

For the product in (2), support injectivity and primitivity of \(F\) give each translated polynomial Gauss norm one at every finite place.
Roots of unity preserve each individual coefficient modulus.
Gauss norm multiplicativity and conjugation give an integer norm polynomial with content one and degree \(kW\), after the common monomial removal.
At most \(O_F(kg)\) of that degree is torsion, even when different factors or embeddings repeat.
Applying (1) to its remaining roots and summing degrees times heights proves (2) with a relative error independent of \(k\).
This proves the individual factor comparison without an irreducibility or a transitivity assumption on the section.
