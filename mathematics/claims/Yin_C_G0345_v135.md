# Radial separation for fixed bivariate specializations and the cost of a second exterior conjugate

<a id="statement"></a>
## A subexponential bound at the unit circle

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be fixed and nonzero.
There is a constant \(C_F>0\) such that for all sufficiently large integers \(n\) and every nonzero root \(z\) of \(F(X,X^n)\) off the unit circle,
\[
             \big|\log |z|\big|\ \ge\ \exp\bigl(-C_F\log^3(2n)\bigr).          \tag{1}
\]
No squarefreeness, reciprocity or unit hypothesis is imposed on the specialization.
The constant depends on the entire fixed polynomial.

In particular, fix a nonreciprocal Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) with exterior root \(\theta>1\).
For either sign let \(\sigma_N\) be the Salem number in the sufficiently large delay construction \(z^N f\pm f^*\), and let \(S_N\) be the trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma_N+\sigma_N^{-1}\).
At every sufficiently large \(N\), \(S_N+1\) has a rightmost trace root in \((2,\sigma_N+\sigma_N^{-1})\).
Let \(\tau_N>1\) be its exterior lift.
If \(\tau_N\) has any other conjugate outside the unit circle, its total Mahler measure strictly exceeds \(\sigma_N\).
Otherwise \(M(\tau_N)=\tau_N<\sigma_N\).
In the second case \(\tau_N\) is Pisot or Salem, including the reciprocal quadratic Pisot case.
Thus on a fixed seed positive leakage, meaning \(\log M(\tau_N)-\log\tau_N>0\), cannot eventually fit the exponentially small saving \(\log\sigma_N-\log\tau_N\).
The [explicit degree-eight input](#example) realizes a nonquadratic Pisot descent with zero leakage.

The coordinator proposed testing positive leakage and the possibility of an exponential radial displacement for fixed Pisot seeds.
The contributor supplied the bivariate separation argument, its application to the distinguished root, and the degree-eight example.

<a id="preparation"></a>
## Reciprocal pairs and a bounded-degree eliminant

Multiplying Laurent polynomials by monomials has no effect on the nonzero roots.
For any fixed finite support, the exponents \(i+nj\) of its distinct monomials are distinct once \(n\) exceeds the largest difference of their \(X\)-exponents.
In particular our specializations are then nonzero.
Include the inverse polynomial \(F(X^{-1},Y^{-1})\) in the product with \(F\), clear Laurent monomials, and take the product of the distinct irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb Z[X,Y]\), up to nonzero scalars.
This leaves the same nonzero zero set as the product for every sufficiently large \(n\).
Discard powers of \(X,Y\) and all factors only in \(X\).
The latter contribute just finitely many fixed nonzero roots, whose nonzero absolute logarithmic moduli have a positive lower bound.
For a pair \(z,z'=1/\overline z\) of distinct reciprocal-conjugate roots sufficiently close to the circle, neither can come from such a factor.
The inverse product and the real coefficients guarantee both roots, including when inverse bivariate factors are associates.
It is enough to work with the remaining squarefree \(U\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]\).
If it is constant there is nothing left to prove.
Otherwise \(U\) is primitive in \(Y\) over \(\mathbb Q[X]\), so \(U(\alpha,Y)\) is never the zero polynomial at any algebraic \(\alpha\).
Factors depending only on \(Y\) are allowed.

Let \(D_n=X\partial_X+nY\partial_Y\), \(Q_n(X)=U(X,X^n)\).
The polynomials \(U,D_nU\) have no common irreducible factor for large \(n\).
Indeed squarefreeness would force a common factor \(V\) to divide \(D_nV\) by the product rule.
The operator preserves the exponent support except for the weight-zero term, and does not raise total degree, so \(D_nV=cV\) for a scalar \(c\).
For \(n\) sufficiently large the weights on the fixed support of \(V\) are distinct, and this equality forces a single monomial, already removed.
Thus \(A_n(X)=\operatorname{Res}_Y(U,D_nU)\) is nonzero.
There are polynomials \(B_n,E_n\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]\) with \(A_n=B_nU+E_nD_nU\), by the adjugate of the Sylvester matrix.
The degrees of \(A_n,B_n,E_n\) are bounded and their coefficient magnitudes are \(O(n^K)\) for a fixed \(K\), since the matrix size is fixed and its entries have bounded degree with coefficients \(O(n)\).

<a id="algebraic"></a>
## Approximating a close reciprocal pair by algebraic numbers of bounded degree

In what follows, constants and bounded degrees may depend on \(U\).
Let \(\epsilon=|z-z'|\).
It suffices to consider \(\epsilon<n^{-A}\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(A\), since larger separations already give (1) near the circle.
We can increase \(A\) to meet the finitely many polynomial precision requirements below.
Then \(\big||z|-1\big|\le\epsilon\), \(|z^n|\) is bounded above and away from zero, and on the segment from \(z\) to \(z'\) one has \(|Q_n''|\ll n^2\).
The derivative bound follows by differentiating the fixed number of monomials of degree \(O(n)\) at modulus within \(1/n\) of one.
Since both endpoints are zeros, Taylor's formula gives \(|Q_n'(z)|\ll n^2\epsilon\).
As \(D_nU(z,z^n)=zQ_n'(z)\), the resultant identity implies \(|A_n(z)|\ll n^K\epsilon\), on increasing \(K\).
An integer constant resultant would have modulus at least one, excluding such a pair.
Otherwise, by the product over its roots and its nonzero integer leading coefficient, \(A_n\) has a root \(\alpha\) with
\[
           |z-\alpha|\le n^K\epsilon^{a}
\]
for some fixed \(a>0\).
Its degree is bounded and its absolute logarithmic Weil height is \(O(\log(2n))\), because its primitive minimal polynomial divides the bounded-degree integer \(A_n\) of polynomial coefficient size and Mahler measure.

We have \(|U(\alpha,z^n)|\ll|z-\alpha|\).
The nonzero leading coefficient of \(U(\alpha,Y)\), even if its degree drops on specialization, has bounded degree and height \(O(\log(2n))\) by the elementary height inequalities.
Its absolute value is at least \(n^{-K}\) at the selected embedding by the product formula.
The same bound excludes the constant-polynomial case at this precision.
Factoring in \(Y\) now gives a root \(\beta\) with \(|z^n-\beta|\le n^K\epsilon^{a'}\) for some fixed \(a'>0\).
The degree and height of \(\beta\) are respectively bounded and \(O(\log(2n))\).
Indeed it is a zero of \(\operatorname{Res}_X(A_n,U)\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), which has bounded degree and polynomial coefficient size and is nonzero since \(U(\alpha',Y)\) is nonzero at every root \(\alpha'\) of \(A_n\).
In particular the joint field degree is bounded.
Reducing \(a'\) and increasing \(K\) as needed gives
\[
                  |\alpha^n-\beta|\le n^K\epsilon^{a'}.                        \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are nonzero and their moduli are bounded above and away from zero, since \(\alpha\) is within \(o(1/n)\) of \(z\), and \(\beta\) is near \(z^n\).

<a id="logarithms"></a>
## The logarithmic bound and exact multiple roots

If \(\alpha^n\ne\beta\), form a logarithm of \(\alpha^n/\beta\) with imaginary part in \([-\pi,\pi]\), written as \(n\log\alpha-\log\beta-2l\log(-1)\) using principal logarithms of the bases.
Here \(l=O(n)\) and the form is nonzero.
Unit arguments equal to 1 can be omitted and coincident arguments combined.
The [Baker–Wüstholz estimate](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#wus02-u001) applies in bounded joint degree.
The two variable modified heights are \(O(\log(2n))\), the height attached to \(-1\) is bounded, and the coefficient form has modified height \(O(\log(2n))\).
Thus the absolute value of the form is at least \(\exp(-C\log^3(2n))\).
At the precision of (2) the principal logarithm has modulus at most twice \(|\alpha^n/\beta-1|\), so (2) gives the claimed lower bound on \(\epsilon\).

If \(\alpha^n=\beta\), then \(Q_n(\alpha)=0\).
The multiplicity \(j\) of this root is bounded by one less than the fixed number of monomials of \(U\).
In fact the first \(s\) Euler derivatives \((X\partial_X)^l\), \(0\le l<s\), of a sum of \(s\) distinct monomials cannot all vanish at a nonzero point, by the Vandermonde determinant in their exponents.
The first nonzero ordinary derivative \(Q_n^{(j)}(\alpha)\) has bounded degree and height \(O(\log(2n))\).
For each monomial \(cX^{i+nh}\) its derivative at \(\alpha\) is \(c(i+nh)_{\!j}\alpha^{i-j}\beta^h\), with \((i+nh)_{\!j}\) the falling factorial.
The product formula therefore gives \(|Q_n^{(j)}(\alpha)|\ge n^{-K}\).
On a disk of radius \(1/n\) about \(\alpha\), \(|Q_n^{(j+1)}|\ll n^{j+1}\) by the fixed number of terms, the bounded \(j\) and \(\big||\alpha|-1\big|=o(1/n)\).
Taylor expansion to order \(j\) then shows that a second distinct zero in the disk has distance at least \(n^{-K'}\) from \(\alpha\).
Both \(z,z'\) are within \(n^K\epsilon^{a''}\) of \(\alpha\) for some fixed \(a''>0\), and at least one differs from it.
This excludes the exact case at sufficiently small polynomial scales.
Finally \(\epsilon=2|\sinh(\log|z|)|\) is comparable with \(\big|\log|z|\big|\) near the circle, completing (1).

<a id="fixed-seed"></a>
## A fixed Pisot seed and the saving at level minus one

Let \(q_N=z^N f+\eta f^*\) with \(\eta=\pm1\) and \(f^*(z)=z^{\deg f} f(1/z)\).
By the [signed Salem construction](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002), for all sufficiently large \(N\), \(q_N=C_N P_N\) with \(C_N\) monic cyclotomic and \(P_N\) the Salem minimal polynomial of \(\sigma_N\).
The \(C_N\) belong to a finite set for this fixed seed.
To see this, \(R=-\eta f^*/f\) has no circle poles, and a torsion zero \(\zeta\) satisfies \(\zeta^N=R(\zeta)\).
According as its order \(l\) is odd, divisible by four or congruent to two modulo four, its conjugates \(\zeta^2,-\zeta,-\zeta^2\) give respectively
\[
 R(\zeta^2)=R(\zeta)^2,\quad R(-\zeta)=(-1)^N R(\zeta),\quad
 R(-\zeta^2)=(-1)^N R(\zeta)^2 .
\]
The corresponding exponents \(2,\ 1+l/2,\ 2+l/2\) in their respective cases are units modulo \(l\).
None of these equations is a rational-function identity because the right side has a pole at \(\theta\), while \(R\) has none at \(\theta^2,-\theta,-\theta^2\).
Indeed \(f\) and \(f^*\) are coprime by irreducibility and nonreciprocity, and \(f\) has only one exterior root.
Hence there are finitely many possible torsion points.
All are simple at sufficiently large \(N\) since \(|(z^N)'|=N\) there while \(R'\) is bounded on the finite set.

If \(2m_N=\deg P_N\), then \(2m_N=N+\deg f-\deg C_N=N+O(1)\).
The full lift of \(S_N+1\) is \(P_N+z^{m_N}\).
Multiplication by \(C_N\) gives \(q_N+C_N z^{m_N}\).
Since \(N-2m_N=\deg C_N-\deg f\), this is a specialization at \((z,z^{m_N})\) of
\[
             X^{\deg C_N-\deg f}Y^2 f(X)+C_N(X)Y+\eta f^*(X).
\]
Only finitely many bivariate Laurent polynomials occur.
Consequently (1) gives a common radial lower bound \(\exp(-C\log^3(2N))\) for every off-circle root of the shifted lift.

Fix \(1<\lambda<\theta\).
The monic \(f\) is negative at \(\lambda\) and positive at every real \(s>\theta\).
At these fixed points \(z^N f\) dominates the remaining term of \(q_N\), giving \(\sigma_N\to\theta\) from the unique exterior root.
At \(z=\lambda\), \(q_N+C_N z^{m_N}<0\) eventually, since \(m_N=N/2+O(1)\) and the finite family of \(C_N\)'s is bounded there.
At \(\sigma_N\) it equals \(C_N(\sigma_N)\sigma_N^{m_N}>0\).
Thus the rightmost lift \(\tau_N\) in the smaller trace window exists and \(\lambda<\tau_N<\sigma_N\).
Dividing its equation by \(\tau_N^N\) gives \(|f(\tau_N)|\ll \lambda^{-N/2}\), and likewise \(|f(\sigma_N)|\ll\lambda^{-N}\).
Since \(\tau_N\to\theta\) by the arbitrary choice of \(\lambda<\theta\), and \(f'(\theta)\ne0\), it follows that \(0<\log\sigma_N-\log\tau_N\ll\lambda^{-N/2}\).
Any other exterior conjugate of \(\tau_N\) therefore costs more than this saving for all sufficiently large \(N\).
With no additional exterior conjugate a neutral root entails a reciprocal minimal polynomial, hence the Salem signature, and in the absence of neutral roots the number is Pisot.

## A strict descent to the plastic number

Let \(S(X)=X^4-5X^2-X+4\).
Modulo three it becomes \(p=X^4+X^2-X+1\).
The successive remainders of \(X^9-X\) modulo \(p\), of \(p\) modulo the result, and of the latter two in turn are \(-X^3+X^2-1,-X^2+X,-1\).
Thus \(p\) has no factor of degree one or two over \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(S\) is irreducible.
At \(-2,-3/2,-1,0,1,2,3\) its signs are \(+,-,+,+,-,-,+\).
Hence three roots are in \((-2,2)\) and just one is above two.
The trace lift is irreducible of degree eight since the discriminant of the lifting quadratic is negative at a neutral embedding of the totally real trace field.
Its exterior root \(\sigma\) is Salem.

At level minus one,
\[
 S+1=(X-1)(X^3+X^2-4X-5),\qquad
 z^3(X^3+X^2-4X-5)\big|_{X=z+z^{-1}}=(z^3-z-1)(z^3+z^2-1).
\]
The real root \(\alpha>1\) of \(z^3-z-1\) is the plastic Pisot number.
The cubic is irreducible by the rational-root test, its discriminant is \(-23\), and its other roots form a conjugate pair of modulus \(\alpha^{-1/2}\).
Its inverse factor has positive real root \(\alpha^{-1}<1\).
Thus \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) is the sole trace root of \(S+1\) above two and \(S=-1\) there, placing it below the exterior trace of \(S\).
The distinguished lift has \(M(\alpha)=\alpha<\sigma\).
The reciprocal mate in the full shifted lift has its own positive measure, so the comparison proved here concerns the distinguished factor.
