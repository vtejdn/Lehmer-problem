# An ambient unit radical retains the nonreal exterior count in small-support prime degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds for an entire splitting field and an exact minimum for root products

Let \(P\) be an irreducible reciprocal monic integer polynomial of degree \(2p\), with \(p>23\) prime and nontorsion roots.
Let \(F\) be its splitting field and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\).
On its \(p\) inverse pairs write \(R,N,c\) for the numbers of real exterior roots, neutral inverse pairs and complex-conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots, respectively.
Thus \(p=R+N+2c\), with \(e=R+2c\) exterior roots.
Suppose
\[
                          0<c<(p-1)/4 .
\]
Let \(y\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit with a positive power \(w=y^j\in F\).
Assume \(M(y)<(3+\sqrt5)/2\) or that \(y\) has a conjugate of modulus one.
Then **\(y\) has at least \(2c\) nonreal exterior conjugates**.
If its total exterior count is less than
\[
                        B=\min(4c,(p+1)/2),                       \tag{1}
\]
the inverse pairs of conjugates of \(w\) form exactly the original \(p\)-point permutation action of \(G\).
In particular \([\mathbb Q(w):\mathbb Q]=2p\) and \(w\) has exactly \(2c\) nonreal exterior conjugates.
These conclusions hold for every such positive power in \(F\).

If \(R,N>0\), the Galois group contains every even sign change of the input inverse pairs.
Suppose also that \(e<B\).
In the radical of the group generated by the input roots, **the minimum exterior count among the reciprocal nontorsion outputs is exactly \(e\)**, without a bound on the output measure.
If such an output has fewer than \(B\) exterior conjugates, some positive power of it is an integral nonzero power of a single input conjugate.
Roots of unity are allowed in the definition of this radical saturation.

For the [five-term prime family](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement), these results hold unconditionally for all sufficiently large primes.
The minimum number of exterior conjugates over all nontorsion radicals of splitting-field units of measure at most the input lies in
\[
                       \{e_p-1,e_p\},\qquad e_p=p/3+O(1).
\]
For the root-generated group the minimum is \(e_p\).
The same statements hold along any infinite irreducible subsequence of the [adjacent seven-term prime family](Yin_C_G0345_v312.md#statement), with \(e_p=(\delta+o(1))p\) and \(\delta=2\arcsin((\sqrt2-1)/2)/\pi\).
The existence of that irreducible subsequence is open.

<a id="classification"></a>
## The trace permutation group and its transitive subgroups

Let \(H\le S_p\) be the image of \(G\) on the original inverse pairs.
It is transitive and its complex conjugation \(t\) interchanges \(c\) disjoint pairs of labels.
In any transitive group of prime degree \(p>23\), the presence of a nonidentity involution fixing more than \((p+1)/2\) points forces the group to contain \(A_p\).
We give the details because the same fact is needed inside point stabilizer images later.

Such a group is primitive and contains a full cycle by Cauchy's theorem.
By [Jones's classification in its stated point actions](../audits/literature/Jo13/Yin_U001.md#full-cycle-classification), the remaining possibilities are affine or projective semilinear, with the exceptional degrees already excluded.
A nonidentity affine involution in odd prime degree fixes one point.
For the projective action let \(p=(q^h-1)/(q-1)\) with \(h\ge2\).
A nonidentity projective linear element fixes the projective points of its eigenspaces over \(\mathbb F_q\).
Their dimensions sum to at most \(h\), and none can be \(h\).
Consequently there are at most \((q^{h-1}-1)/(q-1)+1\) fixed points, by combining dimensions to maximize the sum of the projective point counts.
This is \(1+(p-1)/q\le(p+1)/2\).

If the field automorphism of the involution is nonidentity, it has order two and \(q=t_0^2\) with fixed field of size \(t_0\).
Choose a maximal linearly independent collection of fixed projective lines, with representatives \(v_i\) taken to \(a_i v_i\) by a semilinear representative.
All fixed lines lie in their span.
In the coordinates of a fixed line, normalize its first nonzero coordinate to one, at position \(i\).
Each later coordinate satisfies \(\sigma(x_j)=(a_i/a_j)x_j\), giving at most \(t_0\) choices, since the ratio of any two nonzero solutions is in the fixed field.
There are therefore at most \(1+t_0+\cdots+t_0^{h-1}\le(p+1)/2\) fixed lines, using \(2t_0^j\le t_0^{2j}\) for \(j\ge1\).
The empty fixed set satisfies the same bound.
This proves the assertion for every transitive subgroup on these labels.
In particular \(H\supseteq A_p\), since \(p-2c>(p+1)/2\).

<a id="output"></a>
## Comparing the two inverse-pair actions

Let \(\tau\in G\) be complex conjugation and \(D\) the elementary abelian kernel of the signed action's projection \(\pi:G\to H\).
Let \(X\) be the inverse pairs of actual conjugates of \(w\).
The set is transitive under \(G\) and its pairs have two distinct members.
Every label moved by \(\tau\) supplies one nonreal exterior conjugate of \(w\).
Indeed real roots are fixed pointwise by conjugation, and on the unit circle conjugation is inversion.
Conversely each nonreal exterior root produces a moved inverse pair.
Each nonreal exterior conjugate of \(w\) lifts to a distinct nonreal exterior conjugate of \(y\) by extending embeddings.

The [ambient representation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v310.md#representation) shows that a subgroup of order \(p\) in \(G\) cannot fix all conjugates of \(w\) under either hypothesis on \(y\).
It cannot act trivially on \(X\), since the remaining within-pair sign group has exponent two.
In particular the kernel \(K\) on \(X\) has order prime to \(p\), and \(\pi(K)=1\).
Indeed a nontrivial normal subgroup of the faithful primitive group \(H\) is transitive, hence has order divisible by \(p\).
Thus an element with nontrivial original unsigned action, including \(\tau\), acts nontrivially on \(X\).
The arguments below only need the nontrivial \(p\)-action; this will also give the result about root products without the height or neutral assumption.

Fix a point of \(X\) with stabilizer \(J\) and put \(L=\pi(J)\le H\).
There is an equivariant map \(X=G/J\to H/L\).
Its fibers are the orbits of \(D\), all of size \(s=[D:D\cap J]\).
Each fiber is a torsor under an elementary abelian 2-group, the regular image of \(D\) on it.
Every element stabilizing a fiber acts affinely on that torsor by normalizing the translations.

<a id="stabilizers"></a>
## When the conjugation support can be small

Suppose first that \(L\) is intransitive on the original labels.
If \(L\) fixes a point, the map to its \(H\)-orbit gives an equivariant surjection \(X\to\{1,\ldots,p\}\).
If its fiber size is one, \(X\) is the original action.
Otherwise \(\tau\) moves at least \(4c\) points of \(X\), since every point over a moved label is moved.
If \(L\) has no singleton orbit, choose a smallest orbit of size \(2\le k\le p/2\).
The action on \(X\) maps onto the \(k\)-subsets, since \(H\supseteq A_p\) is transitive on them.
For any 2-cycle \((a,b)\) of \(t\), all \(k\)-subsets containing exactly one of \(a,b\) move.
There are \(2\binom{p-2}{k-1}\ge2(p-2)\) of these.

If \(L\) is transitive and does not contain \(A_p\), it cannot contain any conjugate of \(t\), by the fixed-point argument above.
Thus \(t\) fixes no coset in \(H/L\).
The coset action is faithful since its normal kernel, if nontrivial, would contain \(A_p\).
Here one uses simplicity of \(A_p\) and its trivial centralizer in \(S_p\): a normal subgroup intersecting \(A_p\) trivially centralizes it.
In particular \(p\) divides the order of this permutation image and there are at least \(p\) cosets, all moved.

It remains that \(L\supseteq A_p\), giving at most two fibers.
An order-\(p\) element preserves each fiber and acts nontrivially on at least one.
If \(s=2^a\), the affine group of this size has order \(2^a|\operatorname{GL}_a(2)|\).
Its odd prime divisors divide some \(2^i-1\), \(1\le i\le a\), by counting ordered bases.
It follows that \(s\ge p+1\).
If \(\tau\) exchanges two fibers, it moves every point of them.
Otherwise it must act nontrivially on at least one fiber.
The fixed set of a nonidentity affine map on \(\mathbb F_2^a\) is empty or a proper affine subspace, so at least \(s/2\) points move.
In every case other than the natural \(p\)-set, at least \(B\) points move.
In the natural action exactly \(2c\) move, and \(2c<B\).
This proves the ambient assertions, including the count specifically outside the circle and off the real line.

<a id="signs"></a>
## Even sign changes in mixed signature

On the \(p-2c\) labels fixed by \(t\), the sign of \(\tau\) is positive on a real inverse pair and negative on a neutral one.
When both signs occur, choose a 3-cycle of these fixed labels containing two of one sign and one of the other.
There are at least four fixed labels, so one further fixed label is untouched.
The 3-cycle commutes with \(t\); any signed lift \(g\) therefore has \(g\tau g^{-1}\tau^{-1}\in D\).
On labels fixed by \(t\), conjugation by \(g\) just permutes the signs of \(\tau\), independently of the signs of the lift.
The displayed commutator has both an unchanged and a changed sign among these labels, so \(D\) contains a nonconstant binary vector.
The action on \(D\) permutes coordinates and contains \(A_p\).
For any nonconstant binary vector in \(D\), adding its image under a 3-cycle with two equal entries and one unequal entry gives a vector of weight two.
Its conjugates give all weight-two vectors, which span the full even sign subgroup.

<a id="products"></a>
## Recognition of the only economical root products

Choose one representative \(x_i\) of each input inverse pair.
They are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion by [the mixed prime-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement).
For an output in the specified radical, after raising to a suitable power one has \(w=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\) with integer vector \(a\ne0\).
The conjugate exponent vectors span all of \(\mathbb Q^p\).
Indeed the even sign subgroup has \(p\) distinct coordinate characters and their coordinate eigenspaces are lines; its rational character projections therefore isolate every nonzero component of \(a\), and transitivity reaches every coordinate.
An order-\(p\) subgroup acts nontrivially on this span since it permutes all \(p\) lines.
Consequently it acts nontrivially on the inverse pairs of \(w\), even without any hypothesis on measure or neutral places.
The stabilizer proof applies.

When the exterior count of \(y\) is below \(B\), the inverse-pair action for \(w\) is the original one.
In particular the entire even sign subgroup fixes every output pair.
Every even sign change must take \(a\) to \(a\) or \(-a\), by independence.
Two distinct coordinates give different characters of that subgroup, so \(a\) has exactly one nonzero coordinate.
Thus \(w=x_i^k\), \(k\ne0\).
Independence also ensures that raising the input conjugates to \(k\) does not identify any two.
The number of exterior conjugates of \(x_i^k\) is \(e\), including when \(k<0\) by reciprocity.
Every exterior conjugate lifts to \(y\), giving the lower bound \(e\) in this case.
The other case gives at least \(B>e\), and the input itself realizes \(e\).

<a id="families"></a>
## The two prime families

The [five-term signature](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#input) gives \(R=1\), \(c=p/6+O(1)\) and \(N=p-1-2c>0\) for large \(p\).
Its measure is eventually strictly subplastic.
Thus Smyth's [nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) ensures that any nontorsion output of no greater measure is reciprocal.
The measure hypothesis holds and \(e=2c+1<B\).
All such ambient outputs have at least \(e-1\) nonreal exterior conjugates, and the input has \(e\) exterior conjugates.
For the [lower limiting benchmark](Yin_C_G0345_v312.md#statement), the same argument uses \(R=1\), \(c=(\delta/2+o(1))p\), and \(0<\delta<1/2\), the last inequality following from \((\sqrt2-1)/2<\sin(\pi/4)\).
The argument here does not decide whether the ambient minimum is \(e-1\) or \(e\), or whether positive measures accumulate at one.

The main researcher developed the small-support trace classification, stabilizer alternatives, and even-sign rigidity for root products.
The passage from the logarithmic representation to the permutation action on actual output inverse pairs was also obtained in parallel by the whole-field researcher.
