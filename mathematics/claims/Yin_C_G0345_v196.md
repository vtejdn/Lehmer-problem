# A lower bound for Mahler measure from sums of the exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## The size of an iterated sumset

Let \(P\) be a nonzero integral Laurent polynomial in finitely many variables with support \(S\subset\mathbb Z^h\), and write \(k=|S|\).
For a positive integer \(n\), let \(nS=\{s_1+\cdots+s_n:s_i\in S\}\), allowing repetitions.
There are absolute constants \(A\ge2\) and \(c>0\) such that, with \(n=\lceil(Ak)^{20}\rceil\) and \(D=|nS|\), the logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
          m(P)=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad
          m(P)\ \ge\ c\left(\frac{\log(2+\log(2D))}{\log(2D)}\right)^3.       \tag{1}
\]
The bound applies to the whole polynomial with arbitrary coefficients and arbitrary cyclotomic factors.
Recording the number of occurrences of each element of \(S\) in an \(n\)-term sum gives \(D\le(n+1)^k\), which recovers the [inverse cubic bound in \(k\)](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#statement) up to an absolute constant.
When \(D\) grows only polynomially in \(k\), (1) improves the lower bound to a constant times \((\log\log(5k)/\log(2k))^3\), with the constant depending on the polynomial growth bound.

For example, this stronger estimate holds whenever \(S\) lies in a generalized arithmetic progression of fixed rank whose side lengths are polynomial in \(k\).
The steps and the spacing between individual exponents are unrestricted.
It also holds if \(|3S-2S|\le Kk\) with fixed \(K\).

<a id="collision"></a>
## A short interval of residues from a collision probability

First let \(S\subset\mathbb Z\) and \(k\ge2\).
Choose, by Bertrand's postulate, a prime \(2D<p<4D\) and retain that same prime and \(n\) throughout the proof.
At an intermediate step we will have a finite set \(E\subset\mathbb Z\) containing zero, with \(\gcd(E)=1\), \(2\le |E|=s\le k\), and \(|nE|\le D\).
Reduction modulo \(p\) may identify distinct elements of \(E\).
For \(t\in\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) put
\[
               \phi(t)=\frac1s\sum_{e\in E}\exp(2\pi i te/p).
\]
Let \(\nu\) be the distribution modulo \(p\) of the sum of \(n\) independent uniform choices from \(E\).
It is supported on at most \(D\) residues.
Cauchy–Schwarz and finite Fourier orthogonality give
\[
        \frac1D\le\sum_a\nu(a)^2
                 =\frac1p\sum_t|\phi(t)|^{2n}.
\]
The term at zero is one before division by \(p\).
As \(p>2D\), some nonzero \(t\) satisfies \(|\phi(t)|^{2n}\ge1/(2D)\).
For \(\omega=\phi(t)/|\phi(t)|\) this implies
\[
 \sum_{e\in E}\big|\exp(2\pi i te/p)-\omega\big|^2
      =2s(1-|\phi(t)|)\le s\log(2D)/n .
\]
The summand at \(e=0\) bounds \(|1-\omega|^2\), so every phase is within \(2\sqrt{k\log(2D)/n}\) of one.
Let \(y_e\) be the least absolute residue of \(te\bmod p\).
Since \(|\exp(2\pi i y/p)-1|\ge4|y|/p\) for \(|y|\le p/2\), their width \(R\) satisfies
\[
            0<R=\max_e y_e-\min_e y_e
                \le p\sqrt{k\log(2D)/n}.                              \tag{2}
\]
Positivity follows from \(y_0=0\), invertibility of \(t\bmod p\) and \(\gcd(E)=1\).

<a id="descent"></a>
## Keeping the sumset bound when a row is selected

Normalize the input by removing its smallest exponent and dividing all exponent differences by their gcd.
Translation and dilation of the support in this normalization preserve logarithmic measure and the cardinality of the \(n\)-fold sumset.
At any stage with at least two terms, suppose the normalized polynomial is \(Q(z)=\sum_{e\in E}a_e z^e\) with the conditions just used.
Take an integer \(N\) inverse to \(t\bmod p\), write \(e=p x_e+N y_e\), and set
\[
                F(X,Y)=\sum_{e\in E}a_e X^{x_e}Y^{y_e}.
\]
Then \(F(z^p,z^N)=Q(z)\), with exactly \(s\) terms, and \(F\) has \(Y\)-width \(R\).
Put \(L=\log(2D)\), so \(L\le C_0 k\log(Ak)\) with an absolute \(C_0\).
By (2) we have \(R<p\) for sufficiently large absolute \(A\).
The [cyclic specialization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v161.md#statement) gives
\[
          m(Q)\ge m_2(F)-C_1 kL\sqrt{kL/n}.                            \tag{3}
\]

If \(F\) has a geometric component depending on \(Y\) which is not a torsion translate of a subtorus, the [prime-conductor height estimate on horizontal components](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#support) gives
\[
             m_2(F)\ge c_1
                  \left(\frac{\log(2+L)}{L}\right)^3 .
\]
Indeed \(\log\log(5R)\asymp\log(2+\log(2R))\) for \(R\ge1\), the function \(\log(2+u)/u\) decreases for \(u>0\), and \(\log(2R)\le\log(8D)\ll L\).
To absorb the error in (3), multiply it by \(L^3\).
The result is bounded by
\[
          C_2 k^{3/2}L^{9/2}/\sqrt n
               \le C_3 A^{-10} k^{-4}(\log(Ak))^{9/2}.
\]
The supremum of the right side over \(k\ge2\) tends to zero as \(A\) increases.
Fix \(A\) large enough to absorb at most half the lower bound and to ensure \(R<p\) above.
This proves (1) in this case.

If all the components depending on \(Y\) are torsion translates, the [top coefficient argument](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#induction) gives \(m(Q)=m(A_{\max})\), where \(A_{\max}(X)\) is the top \(Y\)-coefficient of \(F\).
It applies after shifting the lowest \(Y\)-exponent to zero since \(0<R<p\) and \(\gcd(N,p)=1\).
Each horizontal cyclotomic factor specializes to a cyclotomic polynomial in a nonzero power of \(z\), of measure zero.
On this top row \(y_e\) is fixed, so the \(X\)-exponents \(x_e=(e-Ny_e)/p\) are distinct and are an affine image of a subset of \(E\).
For the \(n\)-fold sumset this map amounts to subtracting \(nNy_e\) and dividing by \(p\), so the new sumset has at most \(D\) elements.
Normalizing the new support again preserves this cardinality, while \(R>0\) ensures that at least one term has been discarded.
Hence each necessary repetition satisfies the same hypotheses at the same \(n,D,p\), with exact equality of measures at every descent.
The process ends in the horizontal case already proved or at a nonzero integer monomial, whose positive measure is at least \(\log 2\).
Decreasing \(c\) covers all monomials including the original case \(k=D=1\).

<a id="variables"></a>
## Several variables and structured sets of exponents

For general \(h\), substitute \((z,z^b,\ldots,z^{b^{h-1}})\) and let the positive integer \(b\) tend to infinity.
For every sufficiently large \(b\) the linear map on frequencies is injective on the fixed finite sets \(S\) and \(nS\), since each nonzero difference gives a nonzero polynomial in \(b\).
Thus the one-variable polynomial has exactly the same \(k,n,D\).
The [one-variable specialization limit](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#variables) gives convergence of its logarithmic measure to \(m(P)\).
If \(m(P)>0\), the specialized measures are eventually positive and (1) passes to the limit.

If the support belongs to \(a+\{\sum_{j=1}^r u_j g_j:0\le u_j\le L_j,\ u_j\in\mathbb Z\}\), then
\[
                           D\le\prod_{j=1}^r(1+nL_j).
\]
For fixed \(r,b_0\) and \(L_j\le (2k)^{b_0}\) this gives \(\log(2D)\ll_{r,b_0}\log(2k)\), independently of the steps \(g_j\) and of any coincidences in the progression.

For the other application put \(V=S-S\) and \(K=|3S-2S|/k\).
Choose a maximal collection \(X\subset 2V\) for which the translates \(x+S\) are pairwise disjoint.
These translates lie in \(2V+S=3S-2S\), so \(|X|\le K\).
Maximality gives \(2V\subset X+V\), since every \(y+S\) for \(y\in2V\) meets some \(x+S\).
By induction \(nV\subset (n-1)X+V\).
Any chosen translate of \(S\) by the negative of one of its elements is contained in \(V\).
Also \(|V|\le Kk\) by translating \(V\) into \(3S-2S\).
Consequently \(D\le Kk(n+1)^K\), giving the stated fixed-\(K\) result.

The [quadratic divisor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) instead gives a bound proportional to the degree contributed by nontorsion factors divided by the width of the original polynomial.
For fixed noncyclotomic monic irreducible \(Q\) with \(Q(0)\ne0\), the polynomials \((z^j-1)Q(z)\), \(j>\deg Q\), have \(2|\operatorname{supp}Q|\) terms and that degree fraction tends to zero.
Any nonzero simultaneous family of multiples of the full input has width at least \(j+\deg Q\), and its normalized Fourier coefficient sum is at least one.
Thus restricting the divisor estimate to those full-input multiples retains the width loss even for a fixed number of terms.
Estimate (1) applies directly to the total measure throughout this example and to univariate or multivariate inputs of arbitrary cyclotomic multiplicity.
