# A uniform strict improvement over the two-ended integral energy constant

<a id="statement"></a>
## The unrestricted numerator bound

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic of positive degree with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\log M(P)>0\), use Haar probability measure on the circle, and put \(K=5/2+\log 2-2\sqrt2\).
There are absolute constants \(\eta>0,\ m_0>0\) such that, for \(0<m<m_0\) and every integral Laurent polynomial \(A\) indivisible by \(P\),
\[
                   m\|A/P\|_2^2\ \ge\ K+\eta .                       \tag{1}
\]
This applies to reducible polynomials, repeated roots and any numerator width.
It excludes the limiting scenario of the [earlier two-ended bound](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement), without any extra condition on the roots or coefficients.
The proof below does not evaluate \(\eta\) or \(m_0\).
Constructing a numerator with sufficiently small energy on arbitrary hypothetical shrinking inputs remains a separate step in the Mahler problem.

<a id="state"></a>
## The actual future output of one inner multiplier

Assume towards a contradiction that \(m\to0\) and \(m\|A/P\|_2^2\to K\).
Take a balanced division \(X^{-j}A/P=Q+H,\ H=R/P\), and write \(Q_-,Q_+\) for the strictly negative and nonnegative Fourier parts of \(Q\).
The [balanced support and coefficient limits](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement) give \(mn_\pm\to x=(\log2)/2\) and \(m\sum(|q|^2-1)\to0\) over all occupied coefficients of \(Q\).
In particular \(m\|Q_\pm\|_2^2\to x\).

Let \(W\) be the analytic inner function \(U=P/O\) on the negative end.
On the positive end reflect exponents by the isometry \(f(z)\mapsto z^{-1}f(1/z)\) and use the analytic multiplier \(W(z)=\bar V(1/z)\) in boundary notation, that is, \(W(z)=\overline{V(\bar z)}\) on the circle, where \(V=O^*/P\).
Thus the two cases reduce to an analytic inner function \(W\) and an input \(B=\sum_{j=1}^n b_j X^{e_j}\), with \(e_1<\cdots<e_n<0\), all \(b_j\) nonzero integers.
In particular \(|W(0)|=t=e^{-m}\) and \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\).
In the reflected case \(V\) has real coefficients when \(O(0)>0\), so its boundary probability \(|V-V(0)|^2d\tau/s^2\) has the same signed-period tests under the reflection.
Here and below \(P_+\) denotes projection to the nonnegative Fourier modes.

For \(B_j=\sum_{i\le j}b_iX^{e_i}\), let \(L_j\) be the norm of the modes strictly above \(e_j\) of \(WB_j\), with \(L_0=0\).
This is the entire future output after that impulse.
Before the \(j\)-th impulse the translated tail \(Y=P_+(X^{-e_j}WB_{j-1})\) has norm at most \(L_{j-1}\), since advancing the cut only discards modes.
It belongs to \(H^2\ominus WH^2\): for every \(f\in H^2\), its inner product with \(Wf\) equals that of \(X^{-e_j}B_{j-1}\) with \(f\), which is zero by strict negativity of the input exponents.
Therefore \(Y\perp W\) and \(|Y(0)|\le s\|Y\|_2\), by projecting the constant function onto this subspace.
Writing \(l=\|Y\|_2\) and removing the constant output after adding the impulse gives the exact identity
\[
 L_j^2=l^2+|b_j|^2-|Y(0)+W(0)b_j|^2 .
\]
For both ends \(s l\le s\|B\|_2\to\sqrt{2x}<1\) as an upper bound in the limit, so \(sl<t|b_j|\) for every step of all sufficiently late inputs.
The reverse triangle inequality and \(s^2+t^2=1\) now yield
\[
       L_j\le t l+s|b_j|\le tL_{j-1}+s|b_j| .                       \tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(S_j=s\sum_{i\le j}t^{j-i}|b_i|\), then \(0\le S_j-L_j\) and
\[
              t^{\,n-j}(S_j-L_j)\le S_n-L_n
                   \le S_n-\|P_+WB\|_2 .                            \tag{3}
\]
The last inequality holds because \(e_n<0\).

<a id="saturation"></a>
## Why both ends must saturate every initial fraction

The integer excess and \(mn\to x\) give
\[
             \sqrt m\, S_n\ \to\ \sqrt2(1-e^{-x})=:b .
\]
Indeed \(\sum(|b_i|-1)\le\sum(b_i^2-1)=o(1/m)\) and \(\sqrt m\,s/(1-t)\to\sqrt2\).
Use the [reconstruction at the two-ended threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v651.md#reconstruction), where \(D\) projects onto the short space in the unreflected quotient variables.
It gives \(\sqrt m\|DQ\|_2\to 2(1-e^{-x})=\sqrt2 b\).
The [two projected outputs](Yin_C_G0345_v657.md#decomposition) are orthogonal.
Indeed the negative input contributes \(DQ_-=\bar U P_+(UQ_-)=Q_- -\bar U P_-(UQ_-)\), which has only negative modes, while \(DQ_+=V P_-(\bar V Q_+)=Q_+-V P_+(\bar VQ_+)\) has only nonnegative modes.
Their respective norms are \(\|P_+ WB\|_2\) in the two cases above.
Each has scaled limit superior at most \(b\) by (3).
The sum of their scaled squared norms tends to \(2b^2\), so each scaled norm tends to \(b\).
Consequently \(\sqrt m(S_n-\|P_+WB\|_2)\to0\) separately on each side.
As \(t^n\to e^{-x}>0\), (3) gives \(\sqrt m(S_j-L_j)\to0\) uniformly over \(0\le j\le n\).

Fix \(0<\delta<x\) and let \(k=\lfloor\delta/m\rfloor\).
For all sufficiently small \(m\), \(1\le k\le n\) on each side, and the same calculation as for \(S_n\) gives
\[
          \sqrt m\,L_k\ \longrightarrow\ \sqrt2(1-e^{-\delta}),
       \qquad m\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|\ \longrightarrow\ \delta .         \tag{4}
\]
These estimates retain the entire tail at its own cut \(e_k\), including all unoccupied output positions.

<a id="contradiction"></a>
## Two independently chosen signed binomials

Let \(d\gamma=|W-W(0)|^2d\tau/s^2\) on the end under consideration.
Since \(W(0)B_k\) contains no terms above \(e_k\),
\[
              L_k\le\|(W-W(0))B_k\|_2
                    =s\left(\int|B_k|^2d\gamma\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Put \(M_\gamma=\sup_{g\ge1}|\widehat\gamma(g)|\le1\).
The distinct positions imply
\[
 \frac{\int|B_k|^2d\gamma}{(\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|)^2}
 \le M_\gamma+(1-M_\gamma)
        \frac{\sum_{i\le k}b_i^2}{(\sum_{i\le k}|b_i|)^2}.
\]
The last fraction tends to zero for each fixed \(\delta\), since its numerator is \(O(1/m)\).
By (4) it follows that \(\liminf M_\gamma\ge ((1-e^{-\delta})/\delta)^2\).
First take this limit for fixed \(\delta\), then let \(\delta\downarrow0\).
Thus \(M_\gamma\to1\) on both ends.
The probabilities have real Fourier coefficients by conjugation symmetry, so for each input one can select a positive lag and a sign on each side with \(\int|X^{g_\pm}-\epsilon_\pm|^2d\gamma_\pm\to0\).
This violates the [two-binomial obstruction at independently moving lags](Yin_C_G0345_v657.md#orders).
It uses the lower bound for the same nonzero remainder class after multiplication by both binomials, and therefore needs no upper bound on the lags.

No sequence can attain \(K\).
If (1) failed, for each \(j\ge1\) one could choose \(0<m<1/j\) and an admissible numerator with \(m\|A/P\|_2^2<K+1/j\).
The previous unrestricted lower bound gives limit inferior at least \(K\), yielding the excluded sequence.
This proves (1).
