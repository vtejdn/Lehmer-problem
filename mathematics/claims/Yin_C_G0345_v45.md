# A uniform Salem gap for the interlacer formed by two adjacent Chebyshev traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## The family with coefficients two and one

Write \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
There exists \(\epsilon>0\) such that every Salem number \(\sigma\) whose monic trace minimal polynomial of degree \(k+1\) is strictly interlaced by \(C=2D_k+D_{k-1}\), \(k\ge2\), satisfies \(\sigma\ge1+\epsilon\).
The bound covers all degrees and all values of the trace polynomial at \(-2\).
Strict interlacing means that the \(k\) roots of \(C\) lie in the \(k\) respective open gaps between consecutive trace roots.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Integer moments and the endpoint at minus two

Suppose to the contrary that such \(\sigma\) tend to one.
Write \(m=k+1\), \(S\) for the monic trace polynomial, \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) for its largest root and \(P=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\).
The remaining roots of \(S\) lie in \((-2,2)\).
We have \(m\to\infty\) since the roots of the monic integer \(P\) stay bounded and bounded degree would allow only finitely many such polynomials.
At the roots \(x\) of \(S\), the weights \(c_x=C(x)/S'(x)\) are positive, sum to two and satisfy \(C(X)/S(X)=\sum_x c_x/(X-x)\).
Put \(\mu_i=\sum_x c_x\delta_x\) for each member of the sequence.
Because \(C(-2)=2(-1)^k\), we have
\[
             \int\frac{d\mu_i(x)}{x+2}=-\frac{C(-2)}{S(-2)}
                        =\frac{2}{|S(-2)|}\le2.                       \tag{1}
\]
Extract a weak limit \(\mu\) of mass two on \([-2,2]\).
Truncating the integrand in (1) and then removing the truncation gives \(\int (x+2)^{-1}d\mu\le2\), with an infinite value assigned to the integrand at \(-2\).

Lift \(\mu\) symmetrically to the circle by \(x=w+w^{-1}\), retaining the notation \(\mu\) for this lifted measure.
The coefficient identity in the formal power series at zero is
\[
 G_i(z)=2+\sum_{j\ge1} g_{i,j}z^j
   =\frac{(1-z^2)C^\sharp(z)}{P(z)},\qquad
 g_{i,j}=\sum_x c_x D_j(x),\qquad
 C^\sharp(z)=2+z+z^{2k-1}(1+2z).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(z^{-1}C(z+z^{-1})/S(z+z^{-1})=\sum_x c_x/(1-xz+z^2)\), and the geometric series gives (2).
The \(g_{i,j}\) are integers by \(P(0)=1\), and they converge at every fixed \(j\) to \(g_j=\int (w^j+w^{-j})d\mu(w)\).
Each fixed coefficient therefore stabilizes.
In particular \(G=2+\sum_{j\ge1} g_jz^j\) has positive real part in the disk by its integral kernel \((1+wz)/(1-wz)\).
Equation (2) gives a coefficientwise limit
\[
                       P_0=(1-z^2)(2+z)/G\ \in\mathbb Z[[z]],
                       \qquad P_0(0)=1.                               \tag{3}
\]
The Fourier–Stieltjes transform of \(2\mu\) on \(\mathbb Z\) is integer valued, with values \(g_{|j|}\) off zero and four at zero.
By [Sanders's Corollary 12.4](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) it is an integer combination of indicators of arithmetic progressions and singletons.
Its generating series on the positive integers is rational with only simple roots of unity as possible poles.
Thus \(G=A/B\) in lowest terms, where \(B(0)=1\) is a squarefree signed cyclotomic product and \(A\in\mathbb Z[z]\).

<a id="phase"></a>
## The possible numerator and its circle phase

Here \(A(0)=2\) and \(A'(0)\) is odd by (3), since \(G'(0)=1-2P_0'(0)\).
In particular \(A\) is primitive, and in its primitive irreducible factorization exactly one factor has even constant term.
This factor has a root of dyadic absolute value less than one.
Indeed if all its roots had modulus at least one, the product of its leading coefficient modulus with their moduli would give both the modulus of the even constant and the dyadic Gauss norm, whereas the latter equals one by primitivity.
The integral series \(P_0\) converges at this root.
Evaluation in \(A P_0=(1-z^2)(2+z)B\) forces the root to be \(-2\), as the zeros of \(B(1-z^2)\) have dyadic modulus one.
The even-constant factor is consequently \(2+z\) up to sign.
All remaining factors have constant of absolute value one and no complex zero inside the disk, by positivity of \(\Re G\).
Their reversals are monic up to sign and have all roots in the closed unit disk, hence are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Write \(G=(2+z)E/F\), with \(E,F\) coprime signed cyclotomic products normalized to have constant one.

Put \(R=E/F\), \(r=-1-\deg E+\deg F\) and choose \(\eta=\pm1\) so that \(R(1/z)=\eta z^{r+1}R(z)\).
On the circle away from its poles,
\[
               2\Re G(z)=R(z)H_r(z),\qquad
               H_r(z)=2+z+\eta z^r(1+2z).                              \tag{4}
\]
Every pole and boundary zero of \(G\) is simple.
Indeed both \(G\) and \(1/G\) have positive real part, so a pole of either at a boundary point has order at most one, by the disk bound \(O((1-|z|)^{-1})\) for a function of positive real part along the radius there.
This bound follows by applying Schwarz's lemma after mapping the right half-plane to the disk and sending the function's value at zero to zero.
At a pole of \(G\), (4) would have a simple pole along the circle if \(H_r\ne0\), contrary to nonnegativity on both sides.
At a boundary zero with \(H_r\ne0\), (4) would have a simple real zero and change sign.
Thus every root of \(E,F\) lies among the zeros of \(H_r\), with multiplicity at most one in each product.
Also every simple unit-circle zero of \(H_r\) is torsion, since at a nontorsion point \(R\) is finite and nonzero and the same change of sign would occur.

For \(r\ge2\) the equation \(H_r(z)=0\) on the circle is \(z^r b(z)=-\eta\), with \(b=(1+2z)/(2+z)\).
The circle phase of \(b\) has winding one and derivative \(3/|2+z|^2\) with respect to the argument.
Thus all \(r+1\) zeros of \(H_r\) are simple circle zeros and must be torsion.
But \(H_r\) is primitive with leading coefficient of absolute value two, which is impossible for a polynomial all of whose roots are torsion.
For \(r=-s\le-4\) the corresponding phase \(z^s/b(z)\) has derivative at least \(s-3>0\) and winds \(s-1\) times.
The monic \(z^s H_{-s}\) of degree \(s+1\) has exactly \(s-1\) simple circle roots, all torsion.
Its remaining two roots are real and mutually reciprocal by real coefficients and signed reciprocity.
After division by the monic cyclotomics their monic quadratic therefore has integral trace of absolute value at least three.
Its exterior root must have modulus at least \(L=(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
However every root with modulus \(t\ge L\) would satisfy \(t^s(t-2)\le1+2t\) by the equation \(z^s(2+z)=-\eta(1+2z)\).
This fails there, since \(t^{s-1}(t-2)\ge L^3(L-2)>3\ge2+1/t\).
It follows that \(-3\le r\le1\).

<a id="profiles"></a>
## The five possible phase exponents

The integrability in (1) forces \(G(-1)=0\).
Indeed for \(0<u<1\), symmetry gives the real value \(G(-u)=\int(1-u^2)/((1-u)^2+u(x+2))\,d\mu(x)\), which tends to zero by (1).
Thus \(1+z\) divides \(E\), \(H_r(-1)=0\) and \(\eta=(-1)^r\).
In order of increasing \(r=-3,-2,-1,0,1\), multiplication of \(H_r\) by \(z^{\max(0,-r)}\) gives, up to a nonzero constant,
\[
 (z-1)(z+1)^3,\quad (z+1)(z^2+z+1),\quad
 (z-1)(z+1),\quad z+1,\quad (z-1)(z+1).
\]
Squarefreeness, coprimality, \(z+1\mid E\) and \(\deg E-\deg F=-1-r\) leave respectively
\[
                    R=1-z^2,\qquad 1+z,\qquad (1+z)/(1-z)
\]
in the first three cases, and no possibility in the last two.
The third has \(G'(0)=5\), whereas \(|g_1|\le4\) since \(\mu\) has mass two.
The second has \(2\Re G=(x+2)(x+1)\) on the circle, negative for \(-2<x<-1\).
The first is therefore forced and (3) gives \(P_0=1\).
The lifted limit has density
\[
           d\mu=|h(z)|^2\,dm_{\mathbb T}(z)/2,\qquad
           h(z)=(1-z)(1+z)^2,                                        \tag{5}
\]
for normalized Haar measure \(m_{\mathbb T}\), since the density is the real part of \(G=(1-z^2)(2+z)\) by its Fourier coefficients.

<a id="departure"></a>
## The first nonzero coefficient beyond the constant

Let \(n\) be the first positive degree with \([z^n]P=p_n\ne0\).
Then \(n\to\infty\) by \(P_0=1\), and \(n\le m\) by reciprocity, since \(P=1+z^{2m}\) could not have an exterior root.
In particular \(n<2k-1\) eventually.
Equation (2) now gives \(g_{i,j}=g_j\) for \(j<n\), and \(g_{i,n}=g_n-2p_n\).
Write \(t_i=c_T\) for the exterior trace weight and \(\lambda_i\) for the symmetric positive circle lift of the weights at the interior traces alone.
For \(j\ge1\), \(g_{i,j}=2\int z^j\,d\lambda_i+t_i(\sigma^j+\sigma^{-j})\).
Since \(g_{i,n-1}=g_{n-1}=0\) eventually and \(\lambda_i\) has mass at most two, \(t_i\sigma^n\le4\sigma\).

Choose a fixed real polynomial \(q\) with \(q(0)=1,\ q(1)=0\) and \(\int |q|^2\,d\mu<1\).
For an explicit choice take \(N=16\) and
\[
 q=(1-z)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-j/N)z^{2j}\right)^2,\qquad
 hq=(1-V)^2,\qquad V=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N z^{2j}.
\]
The nonconstant part \(-2V+V^2\) has coefficient \(\ell^2\) norm at most \(3/\sqrt N\), since convolution with \(V\) averages shifts.
Parseval and (5) give \(\int |q|^2\,d\mu\le(1+9/N)/2<1\).

In \(L^2(\lambda_i)\) pair \(q(z)\) with \(z^nq(z^{-1})\).
Their squared norms agree and tend to \(\int|q|^2d\mu\), since the full measures converge weakly in the trace variable and the discarded exterior contribution to this fixed test tends to zero by \(q(1)=0\).
More explicitly, the even Laurent polynomial \(q(z)q(z^{-1})\) has the form of a fixed polynomial in \(x\), whose value at \(T\) tends to zero.
The pairing, or its conjugate, is the integral of \(z^n q(z^{-1})^2\), with real coefficients and lags ranging from \(n-2\deg q\) to \(n\).
All those limiting moments vanish eventually because \(G\) is a fixed polynomial.
In the actual trace moments the single change at the greatest lag contributes \(-p_n\) to the pairing after division by two, since \(q(0)=1\).
The exterior contribution removed from that calculation is
\[
       \tfrac12 t_i\bigl(\sigma^n q(\sigma^{-1})^2+
                                  \sigma^{-n}q(\sigma)^2\bigr)=o(1)
\]
by \(t_i\sigma^n\le4\sigma\) and \(q(1)=0\).
Thus the absolute pairing is at least \(1-o(1)\), since \(p_n\) is a nonzero integer.
This contradicts the Cauchy–Schwarz bound given by the common squared norm, and proves the uniform gap.
