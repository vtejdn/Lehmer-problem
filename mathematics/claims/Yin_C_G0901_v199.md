# Heights of integral polynomial traces and the Leech lattice reflection quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statement

Fix \(B>1\), let \(1<\lambda\le B\) be a Salem number of degree \(2e\), and put \(t=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\).
Write \(T_m\) for the Chebyshev polynomial with \(T_m(\cos\theta)=\cos(m\theta)\).
Take \(f\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(m\ge1\) and write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) and, for every \(B\), a constant \(c_B>0\) such that either root of \(\nu+\nu^{-1}=f(t)\) satisfies
\[
 m\le c_B\sqrt{e/\log(2e)},\quad f\ne\pm2T_m(X/2)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(\nu)\ge c/m .
\]
The two signed Chebyshev polynomials give only the roots \(\pm\lambda^{\pm m}\), each multiplicatively dependent with \(\lambda\).
In particular, a multiplicatively independent companion with integral polynomial trace and \(h(\nu)\ll(\log(12e))^{-212}\) requires \(m\gg(\log(12e))^{212}\) as \(e\to\infty\), since this last scale is eventually smaller than \(c_B\sqrt{e/\log(2e)}\).

The lattice construction below also gives elements of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice of signature \((25,1)\) whose spectral radius is a Salem number of degree four and whose positive powers all lie outside its root-reflection subgroup.

<a id="trace-proof"></a>
## Uniform escape on a trace interval

The supremum \(A=\max_{\theta}|f(2\cos\theta)|\) is at least \(5/2\) for every integral \(f\) in the displayed implication.
Writing \(C_i(X)=2T_i(X/2)\) for \(i\ge1\) gives the integral unitriangular basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_m\).
At the nodes \(\theta_j=j\pi/m\), \(0\le j\le m\), give each interior node weight \(1/m\) and each endpoint weight \(1/(2m)\), and write \(E\) for this average.
Discrete cosine orthogonality gives \(E[(-1)^j f(2\cos\theta_j)]=2a_m\) for the leading coefficient \(a_m\), so \(|a_m|\ge2\) gives \(A\ge4\).
For \(|a_m|=1\), change the sign of \(f\) if needed so that \(R=f-C_m\) has degree less than \(m\).
The values \(s_j=(-1)^jR(2\cos\theta_j)\) have mean zero and satisfy \(s_j\le A-2\), whence \(E|R|\le2(A-2)\).
Each coefficient of \(R\) in the same basis equals \(E[R(2\cos\theta_j)\cos(i\theta_j)]\), including the constant coefficient at \(i=0\).
At least one is a nonzero integer, proving the bound for \(A\).

The polynomial \(z^m f(z+z^{-1})\) has degree \(2m\), is bounded by \(A\) on the closed unit disk, and by \(A|z|^{2m}\) outside it by the maximum principle applied to its reversal.
Cauchy's derivative estimate on disks of radius \(1/(2m)\) centered on the unit circle therefore gives \(|(d/d\theta)f(2\cos\theta)|\le(1+2\exp(1))m A\).
There is an arc of angular radius \(\eta=c_0/m\) about a point of maximum modulus on which \(|f(2\cos\theta)|\ge9/4\), for an absolute \(c_0>0\).

To count conjugates there, let \(z_1,\ldots,z_N\) be the \(N=2e-2\) unit-circle conjugates of \(\lambda\) and put \(a_k=N^{-1}\sum_j z_j^k\).
The nonzero integral discriminant of the Salem polynomial gives \(\sum_{i\ne j}\log|z_i-z_j|\ge-O_B(N)\), because the remaining factors involve \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) and each has absolute value at most \(2B+2\).
Put \(\rho=1-1/N\).
The Fourier expansion of \(-\log|1-\rho e^{i\theta}|\), the diagonal contribution \(N\log N\), and \(|1-\rho z|^2\ge \rho|1-z|^2\) for \(|z|=1\) give
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k}{k}|a_k|^2\ll_B\frac{\log(2N)}N .
\]
Use the triangular function \(\varphi(\theta)=(1-\operatorname{dist}(\theta,\theta_*)/\eta)_+\) centered at the selected point, with distance in radians modulo \(2\pi\).
Its Haar mean is \(\eta/(2\pi)\), and direct integration gives \(|\widehat\varphi(k)|\ll\min(\eta,1/(\eta k^2))\), hence \(\sum_{k\ge1}k|\widehat\varphi(k)|^2\ll1\).
For \(k\le N\), Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\rho^{-N}\le4\) bound the Fourier error in the conjugate average by \(O_B(\sqrt{\log(2N)/N})\).
For \(k>N\), the sum of absolute coefficients is \(O(1/(N\eta))\).
If \(\eta\ge C_B\sqrt{\log(2N)/N}\) with sufficiently large \(C_B\), the conjugate average of \(\varphi\) is at least \(\eta/(4\pi)\).
Thus a proportion \(\gg 1/m\) of the \(z_j\) give \(|f(z_j+z_j^{-1})|\ge9/4\).
For \(k=\mathbb Q(t)\), each interior trace occurs twice among the unit-circle conjugates and each corresponding \(k\)-embedding contributes at least \(\operatorname{arcosh}(9/8)\) to the sum
\[
  2e h(\nu)=\sum_{\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb R}
      \operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(\max(1,|f(\sigma t)|/2)\bigr).
\]
Indeed the two roots at a trace embedding are reciprocal units with equal absolute log modulus, and the height of a unit is half the average of its absolute log moduli.
Averaging over both roots is valid also when the roots lie in \(k\), since \(h(\nu)=h(\nu^{-1})\).
This proves the asserted height bound.

## A Salem isometry with no positive power in the root-reflection group

Realizing a Salem number as the spectral radius of a Lorentzian lattice isometry leads to a separate question about its image in the quotient by reflections.
In the even unimodular lattice \(II_{25,1}=\Lambda\oplus U\), write \(\Lambda\) for the Leech lattice and use the norm \(x^2-2ab\) on triples \((x,a,b)\).
The quotient of its automorphism group by root reflections and \(\{\pm1\}\) is the affine automorphism group of \(\Lambda\), with the stabilizer of the distinguished isotropic vector giving a splitting. [Borcherds, Corollary 6.3](../audits/literature/Bor85/readme.md)
Translation by \(v\in\Lambda\) in this stabilizer acts by \(T_v(x,a,b)=(x+av,a,b+(x,v)+av^2/2)\).
Indeed the formula preserves the norm and sends the isotropic point \((x,1,x^2/2)\) to the point indexed by \(x+v\).
The reflection \(s_0\) in the root \((0,1,-1)\) interchanges \(a,b\), and \(s_w=T_ws_0T_{-w}\) is another root reflection.
Choose nonzero perpendicular \(v,w\in\Lambda\), possible since \(v^\perp\cap\Lambda\) has rank \(23\), and multiply \(w\) by a positive integer as needed.
Put \(A=v^2/2,\ D=w^2/2\) and \(g=s_0s_wT_v\).
On the rational four-space with \(x=c v+d w\) the formulas give
\[
 T_v(c,d,a,b)=(c+a,d,a,b+2Ac+Aa),\qquad
 T_w(c,d,a,b)=(c,d+a,a,b+2Dd+Da).
\]
Taking the determinant of \(XI-s_0T_ws_0T_{-w}T_v\) on that space gives \(X^2 Q(X+X^{-1})\), where
\[
 Q(Y)=(Y-2)^2-D(A+D-4)(Y-2)-4AD .
\]
The complementary \(22\)-space is fixed.
Here \(Q(2)=-4AD<0\) and \(Q(1)=D^2-(3A+4)D+1>0\) for large \(D\).
So \(Q\) has one root in \((1,2)\) and one above two, and is irreducible because its roots cannot both be rational integers.
Over the resulting real quadratic field the equation \(X+X^{-1}=Y\) is irreducible, since at the embedding with \(1<Y<2\) its discriminant is negative.
Thus \(g\) has a genuine degree-four Salem spectral radius.
Its image in the reflection quotient is the translation by the nonzero \(v\), of infinite order, so no positive power of this isometry belongs to the root-reflection group.
The large values of \(D\) in this example give large spectral radii.
Using a Coxeter spectral gap near one would require a separate realization or a theorem controlling the reflection quotient and the power for those particular small units.
