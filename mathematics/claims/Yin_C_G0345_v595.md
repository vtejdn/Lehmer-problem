# Fixing every coefficient in terminal relations at a unit-circle conjugate

<a id="statement"></a>
## Counts with the distinguished coefficient prescribed

Let \(\alpha\) range over a sequence of nontorsion algebraic units of degree \(d\) with \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\).
Suppose that some conjugate of every input lies on the unit circle.
There are an absolute constant \(c>0\) and, for each sufficiently late input, a weight \(r\to\infty\) and one ordered tuple of \(r\) nonzero rational integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1},b\) for which at least \((c/m)^{r-2}\) different sorted integer lists solve
\[
                    \sum_{i=1}^{r-1}a_i\alpha^{t_i}=b .
\]
The mark is at zero and the appended term is \(-b\).
Every relation is terminal for all intermediate rational multiples of arbitrary integer powers in the sense of the [rational splitting rule](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
The same \(b\) works throughout the count.
Put \(T=\lceil64d\log(2d)\rceil\).
All these coefficients have absolute value at most \(T+1\), and all the marked exponents have magnitude \(O(d\log(2d)/m)\).

The same count and exponent window hold if the least absolute logarithmic conjugate radius \(\delta=\min_\beta|\log|\beta||\) satisfies
\[
                   \delta\le K m/(d^2\log^2(2d))
\]
for a fixed \(K\ge0\), with \(c\) now depending only on \(K\) and coefficient magnitudes \(O_K(T+1)\).
For either hypothesis, if all positive powers preserve the degree of the input, dividing the marked lists by their positive exponent gcd leaves \(V\) distinct lists with
\[
                    \log V\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m).
\]
The same primitive conclusion at these inputs holds without a power-degree assumption if there is a real conjugate.
There is no bound on the number of expanding embeddings in these statements.
Every sequence of shrinking measures with a unit-circle conjugate has a sequence of representatives of exactly those measures with a unit-circle conjugate and with every power preserving degree.

<a id="lengths"></a>
## Counting the possible integer lengths on a tree

Root the [tree of rational splits](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees) of a minimal ternary relation at a leaf, ordering the children by their exponent relative to their parent.
Every original leaf has coefficient of absolute value one.
In the original coordinates each nonzero edge value is \(z_e=c_e\alpha^{n_e}\).
It is a sum up to sign of original leaves on either side of its cut, so \(c_e\) is a nonzero rational algebraic integer and hence an integer. [Cut identity](Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#edges)
Evaluate this tree at a unit-circle embedding.
The modulus of the value of each edge is exactly \(|c_e|\).
Given the plane tree, fix a binary bracketing of the children at every internal vertex, using auxiliary edges for intermediate sums in that bracketing.
This leaves a binary tree on the \(j-1\) nonroot leaves, with its output incident to the distinguished root leaf.
Put the ceiling of the modulus on each edge, also allowing zero on the auxiliary edges.
For two children labeled \(x,y\), the parent label \(z\) satisfies \(|x-y|\le z\le x+y\), since all three triangle inequalities persist on taking ceilings.
For nonnegative integers \(x,y\) one has
\[
          \sum_{z=|x-y|}^{x+y}(2z+1)=(2x+1)(2y+1).
\]
Induction from the leaves shows that the sum of \(2z+1\) over all admissible labelings, where \(z\) denotes the final output label, is the product of the leaf weights \(2x+1\).
In particular at most \(3^{j-1}\) assignments to the tree edges are possible when the leaf labels are one.
This counts all original edge magnitudes simultaneously, including those along a long chain of splits.
Each original edge sign can be specified at one end in at most two ways, with the opposite sign fixed at its other end.
There are at most \(2j-3\) original edges.
The cut identity also gives \(|c_e|\le j\).

<a id="near"></a>
## An embedding whose logarithmic radius is small

Use the [finite interval of ternary witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale) with \(j\le T+1\), \(N=\lfloor T\log(2)/(4m)\rfloor\) and original exponents in \([0,N)\).
Put \(h=m/d,\ Y=\log j+Nh,\ U=2Y/h\).
Every cut value has absolute logarithmic height at most \(Y\).
For rational \(c_e\), the norm of \(z_e\) to \(\mathbb Q\) equals \(\pm c_e^d\) because \(\alpha\) is a unit.
The product bound for the heights of the conjugate factors gives \(h(c_e)\le h(z_e)\).
Thus \(|n_e|h=h(z_e/c_e)\le2Y\) for all actual edges, including the original leaves.
In particular \(N\le U\) and \(U=O(d\log(2d)/m)\), without a record assumption.

At the embedding attaining \(\delta\), the original leaf lengths are at most \(\exp(N\delta)\).
The modulus \(w_e\) of any original or auxiliary partial sum is at most \(j\exp(N\delta)\), by expansion into the descendant leaves.
For each actual edge,
\[
     \big||c_e|-w_e\big|\le w_e(\exp(U\delta)-1)
                 \le j\exp(N\delta)(\exp(U\delta)-1).
\]
The hypothesis implies \(U\delta=O_K(1/(d\log(2d)))\), so the last expression is \(O_K(1)\).
Every rounded length at an actual edge therefore leaves only \(O_K(1)\) choices of its signed integer coefficient, and each original leaf has \(O_K(1)\) possible rounded lengths of size \(O_K(1)\).
The binary triangle count with these leaf weights again bounds the number of complete coefficient labelings of each plane tree by \(C_K^j\) for a constant \(C_K\).
The edge magnitude bound follows from the displayed inequality.
At a unit-circle embedding the preceding exact count applies directly.

<a id="reconstruction"></a>
## Counting exponent lists once the edge labels are known

For fixed \(\alpha,j,U\), let \(W_k\) be the maximum number of sorted terminal lists in \([-2U,2U]\) with all \(k\) signed coefficients prescribed, respecting the coefficient bound above.
Set the count to zero if the class is empty.
A vertex of degree \(k\) with its full edge labeling fixed contributes at most \(W_k\) possibilities.
Translation to incoming exponent zero subtracts two original edge exponents and keeps all coefficients fixed.
The completed local lists position all child subtrees relative to the incoming edge, and root exponent zero reconstructs the original support up to translation.
The plane tree count from [the terminal reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees) is at most \(4^{2j}\).
The exponential bound on all its coefficient and sign labelings gives for an absolute constant \(C\), or one depending only on \(K\) under the small-radius hypothesis,
\[
    A_j\le C^j\Big(\max_{3\le k\le j} W_k^{1/(k-2)}\Big)^{j-2},
\]
since \(\sum_v(\deg v-2)=j-2\).
Here \(A_j\) counts minimal ternary supports in the finite window modulo translation.
For the weight supplied by the [finite witness bound](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale), \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\).
For \(m\) sufficiently small these inequalities give \(W_r\ge(c/m)^{r-2}\) at a maximizing weight \(r\), for example with \(c=\log(2)/(32 C^3)\) and \(C\ge1\).
For every fixed \(k\), the [bound with free rational output](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement) bounds \(W_k\) independently of the input, since the minimum degree of a positive power tends to infinity. [Power-field descent](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent)
Hence \(r\to\infty\).

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Removing dilations and choosing a representative of the measure

Eventually \(r\ge4\), so two coincident exponents would give a proper zero subsum or a two-term sum equal to a rational multiple of the same power.
Both are excluded by terminality.
On a fixed primitive marked list the polynomial including its constant term \(-b\) therefore has exactly \(r\) terms.
When all powers preserve degree, the [fixed-polynomial determinant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v590.md#statement) allows at most \((r-1)(1+\lfloor r\log r/(2m)\rfloor)\) positive dilations.
If a conjugate is positive real, its positive powers are distinct positive numbers, and a real Laurent polynomial with \(r\) terms has at most \(r-1\) distinct positive zeros by Descartes' rule of signs after a monomial shift.
For a negative real conjugate apply the same count separately to the even and odd powers, replacing \(X\) by \(-X\) for the odd ones.
A real conjugate of a nontorsion unit cannot have modulus one.
Thus at most \(2(r-1)\) dilations occur in the real case.
Either logarithmic loss is \(o((r-2)\log(1/m))\), proving the primitive estimate.

For the final assertion choose \(Q\ge1\) minimizing \(d_*=\deg(\alpha^Q)\), put \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha^Q)\), \(e=d/d_*\), and \(\eta=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/F}(\alpha)\).
The [relative norm identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent) gives \(\eta^Q=\alpha^{Qe}\) and \(h(\eta)=e h(\alpha)\).
For every \(n\ge1\), \(\alpha^{Qen}\in\mathbb Q(\eta^n)\subseteq F\), and the left element already has degree at least \(d_*\) by minimality.
Thus \(\mathbb Q(\eta^n)=F\) for all \(n\), and \(\log M(\eta)=d_* e h(\alpha)=m\).
At the restriction of a unit-circle embedding the identity for \(\eta^Q\) gives modulus one as well.
The representative has its own terminal relations.
