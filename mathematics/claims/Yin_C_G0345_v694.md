# A computable seed cutoff for irreducibility at every fixed delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## Effective exclusion of all positive field radicals

There is an algorithm which, given an integer \(n\ge7\), returns an integer \(C(n)\) with the following property.
For each integer \(a>C(n)\) and each \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(r\) be the unique exterior root of \(X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\) and \(P\) its minimal polynomial.
Then \(r^{1/q}\notin\mathbb Q(r)\) for every integer \(q\ge2\).
Consequently \(P(X^m)\) is irreducible of Mahler measure \(r\) for every positive \(m\).
One can also compute the complete finite list of exceptional seeds, signs and exponents at each fixed delay.
The construction below gives a finite computation, with no estimate of its practical running time.
It makes effective the [eventual irreducibility theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v692.md#statement).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The generic reciprocal factors form one orbit

Fix \(n,\epsilon\), set \(L=n-1\), and use the indeterminate \(T\) for the seed.
Let \(U\) be the product of \(X-\eta\) over \(\eta=\pm1\) satisfying \(\eta^L=\epsilon\).
Set
\[
 A(X)=\frac{X^L-\epsilon}{U(X)},\qquad
 P_T(X)=\frac{X^{n+1}-\epsilon}{U(X)}-T X A(X),\qquad
 2h=\deg P_T.
\]
At each integral \(T=a\ge3\), this specializes to the Salem minimal polynomial by the [root classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots).
The two coefficients of the linear pencil in \(T\) are coprime over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), since a common nonzero root before division by \(U\) satisfies \(X^2=1\), and \(X^L-\epsilon\) has simple roots.
Zero is also excluded by the constant term.
The pencil is therefore irreducible in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[X,T]\), and monicity in \(X\) gives irreducibility over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\).
It is reciprocal, since the numerator before division by \(U\) is reciprocal up to sign and the roots of \(U\) are self-inverse.
The \(2h\) generic roots form \(h\) distinct inverse pairs.

Fix any integer \(q\ge2\).
For each inverse pair \(\{z,z^{-1}\}\), choose a \(q\)-th root \(w\) of \(z\) and take both \(w,w^{-1}\).
The products of \(X-w\) over the resulting sets of \(2h\) roots are \(N=q^h\) distinct monic reciprocal factors of \(P_T(X^q)\).
They are distinct because powering recovers the original pairs and the selected root above each \(z\).
We show that the Galois group over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\) acts transitively on these factors.

Put \(s=T^{-1/q}\) and \(u=s^q\).
At \(u=0\) the equation \(uP_{1/u}(X)=0\) has simple finite roots at zero and at every root \(\zeta\) of \(A\).
Each continues to a convergent power series in \(u\) with coefficients in the field of its constant term, by the simple-root recursion and the analytic implicit function theorem.
The series at zero starts with \(u\), as follows by comparing linear terms before division by \(U\).
Its inverse is the remaining root, with leading term \(u^{-1}\).
The \(q\)-th roots over a neutral series have power series in \(u\) with chosen constants \(\rho^q=\zeta\) and coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(\rho)\).
The \(q\)-th roots over the exterior series are \(\omega s^{-1}\) times a series in \(u\) with rational coefficients and constant term one, for \(\omega^q=1\).
The roots over the contracting series are their inverses.
These expansions realize the whole splitting field of \(P_T(X^q)\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\) inside \(\overline{\mathbb Q}((s))\).

For a primitive \(q\)-th root of unity \(\omega\), the automorphism \(s\mapsto\omega s\) permutes these roots and restricts to an automorphism of the splitting field fixing \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\).
It fixes all lifts over every neutral pair and rotates the choices over the exterior and contracting pair in a single \(q\)-cycle.
Irreducibility of \(P_T\) gives, in this splitting field, an automorphism carrying the exterior root to a member of any prescribed pair.
Conjugating the rotation by that automorphism gives a \(q\)-cycle on the choices for the prescribed pair alone.
These cycles allow all choices to be made independently, proving transitivity for every \(q\), including composite \(q\).

<a id="parameter"></a>
## An integral coordinate and its real expansions

For each of these factors \(S\), write \(c_i(S)=[X^i]S\), and choose an integer \(b\) such that the values \(Y_S=\sum_{i=0}^{2h} b^i c_i(S)\) are distinct as algebraic functions.
Each pair of different coefficient vectors excludes only finitely many \(b\).
The polynomial
\[
                    H(T,Z)=\prod_S (Z-Y_S)
\]
is invariant over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), since all automorphisms preserve the collection of inverse-pair choices.
Its coefficients are integral over \(\mathbb Z[T]\), since the roots of the monic \(P_T(X^q)\) are integral.
As \(\mathbb Z[T]\) is integrally closed, \(H\in\mathbb Z[T,Z]\).
The transitivity just proved shows that each \(Y_S\) has degree \(N\) even over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)\).

These data can be found by rational and cyclotomic arithmetic.
On every generic branch all \(c_i(S)\) have pole order at most one in \(s\), since only one selected root has a pole and that pole is simple.
Thus the coefficient of \(Z^{N-j}\) in the product defining \(H\) has degree at most \(\lfloor j/q\rfloor\) in \(T\).
Truncating the finitely many Laurent expansions to obtain the nonpositive powers of \(s\) in the product computes \(H\) for a proposed \(b\).
Testing successive integers \(b\) until its discriminant in \(Z\) is nonzero finds a separating coordinate.

On the positive real ray, select the positive exterior lift and its inverse.
For each of the other inverse pairs choose any lift \(\rho\) at infinity and its inverse over the opposite root of \(A\).
These are all the branches relevant to a positive field radical.
Indeed, if \(\beta=r^{1/q}\in\mathbb Q(r)\), it generates the same field and is a Salem number by the [field radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Its conjugates power bijectively onto those of \(r\), and its monic integral minimal polynomial consists of exactly one of these reciprocal selections.
For real \(T\) sufficiently large the continuation of each neutral series stays on the unit circle, since its inverse and its complex conjugate have the same constant term and represent the same simple branch.
The same observation holds for its chosen \(q\)-th root.
Consequently the Laurent series of \(Y_S\) on each selected branch has real coefficients in the field
\[
 K=\mathbb Q(\mu_m)^+,\qquad
 m=\begin{cases}qL&\epsilon=1,\\2qL&\epsilon=-1,\end{cases}
 \qquad g=[K:\mathbb Q]=\varphi(m)/2 .
\]
Here \(\mu_m\) denotes the \(m\)-th roots of unity and the superscript denotes the maximal real subfield.
Indeed all neutral lift constants lie in \(\mu_m\), each inverse pair of their series is interchanged by complex conjugation, and the chosen exterior series and its inverse have rational coefficients.

<a id="dimension"></a>
## More independent functions than expansion conditions

For all the stated parameters one has \(N>qg\), or equivalently \(q^{h-1}>g\).
For odd \(L\ge7\) either sign gives \(h=(L+1)/2\) and \(g\le qL/2\), using \(\varphi(2qL)\le qL\) for the negative sign.
Here \(q^{(L-3)/2}>L/2\), starting at \(L=7,\ q=2\) and increasing by at least a factor of two on each increment of two in \(L\).
For even \(L\ge6\) with positive sign, \(h=L/2\) and \(g\le qL/4\) since \(qL\) is even.
In this case \(q^{L/2-2}>L/4\), starting at \(L=6,\ q=2\).
For even \(L\ge6\) with negative sign, \(h=L/2+1\) and \(g\le qL/2\).
Now \(q^{L/2-1}>L/2\), again starting at \(L=6,\ q=2\).
The last two inequalities persist on increasing \(q\) or increasing \(L\) by two.

Fix one of the relevant real branches and write \(Y=Y_S\).
Take \(M=gN+1\) and the \((M+1)N\) monomials \(T^iY^j\) with \(0\le i\le M,\ 0\le j<N\).
They are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\) because \(Y\) has degree \(N\) over \(\mathbb Q(T)\).
Each has pole order at most \(qM+N-1\) in \(s\).
For a rational linear combination to have all coefficients of \(s^{-qM-N+1},\ldots,s^0\) vanish gives at most \(g(qM+N)\) rational homogeneous equations.
There are more unknowns, since \(N-qg\ge1\) and \(M=gN+1\).
Compute a nonzero null vector from the truncated series and clear denominators.
This gives \(f(T,Z)\in\mathbb Z[T,Z]\) of \(Z\)-degree less than \(N\) with \(f(T,Y)=O(s)\) on the chosen branch.
Independence shows that \(f(T,Y)\) is nonzero, and its limit zero makes it nonconstant.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Computing the exclusions

For this branch compute the three integer polynomials
\[
       D_j(T)=\operatorname{Res}_Z\bigl(H(T,Z), f(T,Z)-j\bigr),
                    \qquad j\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
They are all nonzero since \(H\) is the minimal polynomial of \(Y\) and \(f(T,Y)\) is nonconstant.
Choose an integer larger than three and larger than the moduli of all roots of these three polynomials and of the nonzero \(X\)-discriminant of \(P_T(X^q)\).
Ordinary polynomial root bounds suffice.
On the positive ray above this integer the chosen branch continues continuously from infinity, since \(P_T(X^q)\) has simple roots everywhere on the ray.
The branch stays real in its coefficients by uniqueness of continuation.
The real function \(f(T,Y)\) tends to zero and cannot meet \(0,1,-1\) anywhere there, by the resultants.
It therefore has \(0<|f(T,Y)|<1\) on the entire ray.
At an integral seed with an integral selected factor, both \(Y\) and \(f(T,Y)\) would be integers.
This excludes the branch above its computed cutoff.

There are only \(q^{h-1}\) real choices with positive exterior lift, so taking the maximum gives a computable cutoff for the given \(n,\epsilon,q\).
All the reciprocal selections with the positive exterior root are covered on the final ray by continuation of these choices with distinct roots.
By the [effective exponent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v689.md#statement) only an effectively bounded finite set of \(q\)'s can occur at each fixed \(n\), uniformly in the seed and sign.
Taking the maximum over that set and both signs gives \(C(n)\).
For each remaining integer seed \(3\le a\le C(n)\) and each such exponent, rational polynomial factorization of \(P_a(X^q)\) and real root isolation decide whether the factor containing the positive exterior \(q\)-th root has degree \(2h\).
This is equivalent to membership in \(\mathbb Q(r)\), since the field generated by that root contains \(\mathbb Q(r)\).
The [factor formula for power substitutions](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives the claimed irreducibility and measure.
