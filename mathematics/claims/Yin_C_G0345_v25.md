# Positive Pisot excess when the lower trace exponents have bounded ratios to their gcd

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound over every positive branch

Let \(\theta\) be a Pisot number whose monic minimal polynomial has positive constant \(q\ge2\), and form its [trace lift](Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(\Pi\) on branch \(j\ge1\) with trace degree \(h\).
Let \(d_1<\cdots<d_s\) be the positive exponents of \(\Pi\) at most \(h\).
When \(s>0\) put \(D=d_s/\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_s)\), and put \(D=0\) for empty lower support.
For each fixed \(K\ge1\) there is a constant \(c_K>0\) such that \(D\le K\) implies \(\theta-q\ge c_K\), independently of the branch and both degree parities.
Thus \(D\to\infty\) is necessary on every vanishing-excess sequence.
On positive-constant inputs with \(D\le K\), the [branch identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) gives \(q\le j/c_K+2\).

More generally, fix \(K_0,N,\eta>0\) with \(K_0,N\) integers and \(0<\eta\le1\).
Let \(S\) be a nonintegral real polynomial of degree at most \(N\), with \(S(0)=1\) and no root in the open unit disk.
If the full lift has at most \(K_0\) terms and \(\Pi/j\) agrees through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) with \(S'(z^g)\), \(1\le g\le h,\ S'(0)=1,\ \deg S'\le N\), the excess has a uniform positive lower bound whenever the padded coefficient vector of \(S'\) is sufficiently close to that of \(S\).
Both constants may depend on \(S,K_0,N,\eta\).

<a id="nonintegral"></a>
## Nonintegral coefficients in a limiting prefix

For the second assertion, suppose a sequence with excess tending to zero has \(S_i\to S\) in the prescribed coefficient space.
Write \(B=(\Pi/j)J_i\) for the [actual integer denominator and its inverse series](Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality), so \(B(0)=1\) and \(1<M(B)\to1\).
The [prefix argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v108.md#profile) gives \(g=o(h)\) and bounded coefficients of \(B\).
For any joint weak limit at the prefix and an intermediate frequency, the weighted positive rows of the limiting measure have integer Fourier coefficients.
Each grouped graph component with nonnegative projected density \(f\) gives \(2Sf\) integral Laurent, and its base Herglotz germ \(J_0\) satisfies \(SJ_0\) integral.
Reality and integrability make \(f\) Laurent polynomial.
Its largest coefficient in \(2f\), when \(f\) is nonconstant, has modulus at least one by integrality of the outer coefficient of \(2Sf\).
Its mean is at most two, so averaging over roots of unity forces \(2f=2\pm(w^k+w^{-k})\) and exhausts the marginal.
This gives \(J_0=1\pm w^k\), contradicting nonintegrality of \(S\).
The [density lemma](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v104.md#harmonic) excludes a positive constant as well.
The [positive-row argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v104.md#rows) then leaves only torsion atoms in those rows, yielding an integral binomial \(U=1-z^{Tg}\) for which \(UB\) vanishes on \(Lg\le n\le h/L\) after extraction.

The [return to \(B\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v108.md#return) takes \(V=PU S(z^g)\) with \(P=1\) or a signed binomial supplied by the local integral moments below \(g\).
In the common prefix \(PUB\) differs coefficientwise from \(VJ_i\) by \(o(1)\) uniformly.
The resulting two-sided moment identity bounds the Gram matrix of characters weighted by \(V\) on each fixed-size subset of an interval of length comparable to \(h\).
The coefficients of \(VB\) belong to a fixed finite real set with nonzero moduli bounded away from zero.
Bessel's inequality and the all-location bound at scale \(g\) on the finitely many transition intervals therefore bound the number of nonzero coefficients on the full degree.
But \(M(VB)=M(B)>1\) tends to its constant coefficient one, contradicting the [endpoint argument for fixed real coefficient tuples](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations).
This proves the second assertion.

<a id="integral"></a>
## Integral limits in the full reflected lift

Suppose now that \(D\le K\) on a vanishing-excess sequence.
The [bounded reduced exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v22.md#bounded-offsets) give, after extraction, \(g=\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_s)=o(h)\) and lower polynomials \(S_i\) converging in degree at most \(K\) to a nonconstant real polynomial \(S\) with \(S(0)=1\) and no interior root.
The empty case is excluded by the same result.
The actual lift equals \(S_i(z^g)-z^{2h+2}S_i(z^{-g})\) and has at most \(2(K+1)\) terms.
Its prefix through \(h\) is \(S_i(z^g)\), so the second assertion excludes every nonintegral \(S\).

For integral \(S\), its leading coefficient has modulus one and every root has modulus one by the product of their moduli.
Thus \(w^nS(1/w)=\sigma S(w)\) for \(n=\deg S\), \(\sigma=\pm1\).
Put \(k=ng,\ p=2h+2-k\).
In coefficient absolute sum \(\Pi/j\) differs from \((1-\sigma z^p)S(z^g)\) by \(o(1)\), even when the approximating polynomials have larger degree than \(S\).
The [folding calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#fold) expresses all coefficients of the integer remainder \(I=B\bmod(z^p-\sigma)\), up to uniform \(o(1)\), as the moments of the single weighted signed measure \(2S(v^{-g})\,d\omega_i\).
Its [projection to the \(p\) grid points](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#grid) changes these moments by \(o(1)\) throughout one period, using the vanishing of \(\Pi\) on the support of \(\omega_i\).
Rounding the integer function on the doubled cyclic group and clearing the finitely many progression denominators gives a bounded-term cyclotomic multiple of \(I\).
Writing \(P_k\) for the initial truncation of \(B\) through \(k\), the actual division identity is
\[
                         B=I+(z^p-\sigma)z^kP_k(1/z).
\]
The [local estimate including the truncation edge](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v107.md#lower) gives a bounded-term multiple of \(P_k\) by one or a signed binomial.
Clearing both multipliers now gives an integer polynomial of bounded term count with measure \(M(B)>1\) tending to one, contradicting the fixed-support gap.
This excludes the integral limit and proves the uniform bound by compactness.
Finally the branch identity gives \(H_q'(0)=q+\theta^{-1}-j/(\theta-q)\) with \(|H_q'(0)|\le2\) by the positive-real-part estimate, giving \(q\le j/c_K+2\).
