# A single noncyclotomic factor from a moving conjugate pair

<a id="statement"></a>
## The angular and radial conditions

Let \(A\) be a monic integer polynomial of degree \(d\), let \(A^*(z)=z^dA(1/z)\), and put \(P(z)=z^N A(z)+\sigma A^*(z)\) for an integer \(N\ge1\) and \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\).
Suppose the roots of \(A\) exterior to the unit circle, counted with multiplicity, are one nonreal conjugate pair \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\).
Let \(R=|\alpha|\) and let \(\eta\in(0,\pi/2]\) be the angular distance from \(\alpha\) to the nearer real half-axis.
If
\[
                  N\sin\eta\ge2,
                  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
                  N\ge2\ \text{ and }\ |\Re\alpha|\le1,                 \tag{1}
\]
then \(P\) is either a product of cyclotomic polynomials or has exactly one noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor.
In the latter case this factor is reciprocal, occurs once, and contains the entire exterior conjugate pair of \(P\).
There is no restriction on the support, degree, coefficient height, constant coefficient or reciprocal gcd of \(A\).

For a uniform positive-measure conclusion, assume also \(R\ge R_0>1\).
If \(1<r<R_0\) and
\[
                      r^N>\left(\frac{rR_0-1}{R_0-r}\right)^2,         \tag{2}
\]
then \(P\) has exactly two roots outside \(|z|=r\), and none in \(1<|z|\le r\).
Under (1) and (2) its unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(S\) has \(m(S)=m(P)>2\log r\), where \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
For \(\eta\ge\eta_0>0\) and \(R\ge R_0>1\), one may choose \(r>1\) and an integer threshold for all \(N\) depending only on \(\eta_0,R_0\).
If \(A\) has an irreducible nonreciprocal factor of positive measure, one can take \(R_0=\sqrt{\theta}\), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
Indeed \(m(A)=2\log R\) and the nonreciprocal factor contributes at least \(\log\theta\) by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).

<a id="counts"></a>
## Exterior roots with multiplicity and common factors

For the counting argument let \(E\) denote any number of exterior roots of a real monic \(A\), counted with multiplicity.
Remove from \(A\) the monic product \(C\) of all its circle roots with their multiplicities and write \(A=CB\).
Complex conjugation permutes those roots, hence \(C\) is real and \(C^*=\kappa C\) for \(|\kappa|=1\), with \(\kappa=1\) when \(C=1\).
Thus \(P=C(z^NB+\sigma\kappa B^*)\), using the actual degrees in each reversal.
For \(0\le t<1\) the polynomial \(z^N B+t\sigma\kappa B^*\) has precisely \(N+\deg B-E\) roots inside the unit circle by Rouché's theorem, since \(|B|=|B^*|>0\) on it.
It is monic of degree \(N+\deg B\) and has exactly \(E\) exterior roots.
Every collection of disjoint closed disks around the exterior roots at \(t=1\), chosen wholly outside the unit disk with root-free boundaries, contains the same multiplicities for \(t\) sufficiently near one.
Consequently \(P\) has at most \(E\) exterior roots even when \(A,A^*\) have common factors or \(P\) has multiple circle roots.

For \(1<r<R_0\) suppose all \(E\) exterior roots have modulus at least \(R_0\).
For \(|z|=r\), an individual root \(\beta\) contributes \(|1-\bar\beta z|/|z-\beta|\) to \(|A^*/A|\), using conjugation of the multiset of roots.
This ratio is at most one for \(|\beta|\le1\), since the denominator squared minus the numerator squared equals \((r^2-1)(1-|\beta|^2)\).
For \(|\beta|=T\ge R_0\) its square is \(1+(r^2-1)(T^2-1)/|z-\beta|^2\), maximized on the same ray as \(\beta\).
The resulting ratio \((rT-1)/(T-r)\) decreases with \(T\).
Hence (2), with power \(E\) in place of 2, gives \(|z^NA|>|A^*|\) on the contour.
Rouché gives exactly \(E\) exterior roots beyond \(r\), so the preceding unit-circle count rules out the entire intervening annulus, including its outer boundary.
Every positive-measure monic factor then has measure greater than \(\log r\).
When \(E>0\), any integer \(N_0>E(R_0+1)/(R_0-1)\) allows a single \(r>1\) for every \(N\ge N_0\), since
\[
          \lim_{r\downarrow1}\frac{\log((rR_0-1)/(R_0-r))}{\log r}
                         =\frac{R_0+1}{R_0-1}.
\]
This is a sufficient strict contour condition, including when the real threshold is an integer.
It extends the [exterior contour for coprime unit seeds](Yin_C_G0345_v1.md#height-free-exterior-contour) to this count without removing off-circle common factors.

<a id="real"></a>
## Excluding real exterior roots for the two signs

Fix real \(|x|=e^t>1\), write \(R=e^u\), and let \(c=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\Re(\alpha)/R\).
Both exterior roots together contribute to \(|A^*(x)/A(x)|\) the positive ratio
\[
       \frac{1-2cR e^t+R^2e^{2t}}{e^{2t}-2cR e^t+R^2}
       =\frac{\cosh(u+t)-c}{\cosh(u-t)-c}.
\]
There is no real exterior zero of \(A\) and the other root ratios are at most one.
For \(h(v)=\log(\cosh v-c)\) the identity
\[
          (\cosh v-c)^2-(1-c^2)\sinh^2 v=(c\cosh v-1)^2
\]
gives \(|h'(v)|\le(1-c^2)^{-1/2}=1/\sin\eta\).
The bound is strict except possibly at isolated points, since \(|c|<1\).
Integration on \([u-t,u+t]\) gives \(h(u+t)-h(u-t)<2t/\sin\eta\).
This excludes a real exterior zero of \(P\) for both signs also at \(N\sin\eta=2\).

For the strip condition put \(a=\Re\alpha\).
The denominator of the pair ratio is \(x^2-2ax+R^2>0\), and
\[
     x^2(x^2-2ax+R^2)-(1-2ax+R^2x^2)
                          =(x^2-1)(x^2+1-2ax)>0
\]
for every \(|x|>1\) if \(|a|\le1\), including \(a=\pm1\).
Thus \(|A^*(x)/A(x)|<|x|^2\) throughout these two rays and \(N=2\) is allowed.
In either case of (1) any exterior root of \(P\) must be nonreal and its conjugate is also exterior.
The count \(E=2\) forces these two roots to be simple and excludes any others.
Every monic integral factor of \(P\) has nonzero constant coefficient, and one without exterior roots has only roots of unity by Kronecker's theorem.
Both exterior roots belong to the same rational irreducible factor by complex conjugation.
Its multiplicity is one and its normalized reciprocal must be the same factor because \(P^*=\sigma P\).
With (2) the conjugate roots each contribute more than \(\log r\).

## Dense seeds and a plane curve

For \(k\ge1\) take
\[
           A_k(z)=z^k(z^2+2k+4)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z^j .
\]
On \(|z|=1\) the first summand has modulus at least \(2k+3>k\).
Rouché gives \(k\) interior roots and no circle roots.
At any exterior root \(\alpha\) one has \(|\alpha^2+2k+4|\le k\), whence \(\Re(\alpha^2)\le-k-4\) and \(|\alpha|\ge\sqrt{k+4}\).
The two exterior roots are therefore conjugate, and their angular distance to the real axis exceeds \(\pi/4\).
The uniqueness alternative applies for every \(N\ge3\), while the degree, number of terms and coefficient height all grow with \(k\).
For \(N\ge6\) one can use \(r=5/4\) and \(R_0=11/5<\sqrt5\), since \((5/4)^6>(35/19)^2\).
Every one of these returns has a unique noncyclotomic factor of logarithmic measure greater than \(2\log(5/4)\).

For \(M\ge2\) an integer, the reciprocal plane curve
\[
                 Q_M(X,Y)=Y(X+M)+\sigma(MX+1)=0
\]
remains irreducible over \(\mathbb C\) after every torus isogeny.
In fact \(Q_M(X^h,Y^h)\) for \(h\ge1\) is Eisenstein in \(Y\) at any simple root of \(MX^h+1\), where \(X^h+M\ne0\).
Its two coefficients are coprime, so this gives Laurent irreducibility.
For any nonsingular integral monomial substitution its adjugate gives a further substitution whose composite is a scalar power map, up to simultaneous inversion.
Either substitution preserves nonunits since distinct support exponents remain distinct.
The specialization \(Q_M(z^2,z^N)\) has seed \(A=z^2+M\) and \(\Re\alpha=0\).
For every \(N\ge2\) it has at most one noncyclotomic irreducible factor, with multiplicity one.
On primitive directions \((2,N)\) with odd \(N\to\infty\), the shortest nonzero integral orthogonal relation has sup norm \(N\).
Here all positive factors even have an absolute gap at every \(N\ge2\), by the [whole-polynomial gap for at most four terms](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#statement) and uniqueness.
For \(N=2,\sigma=-1\) the return equals \(z^4-1\), so the cyclotomic alternative occurs.
This calculation concerns the specified directions of that curve.

## Bounded delays and moving coefficients

The [Pisot construction](Yin_C_G0345_v30.md#statement) treats one positive real exterior root of a minimal polynomial.
The [fixed two-variable specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v167.md#statement) and the [factor allocation for two fixed blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v193.md#degrees) use fixed coefficient data.
The [radial-segment bound](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) and [endpoint-block bound](Yin_C_G0345_v172.md#statement) impose coefficient \(\ell^1\) bounds.
The [varying-seed whole-measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v3.md#statement) does not allocate the exterior conjugates to one irreducible factor.
Condition (1) controls that allocation at finite \(N\).
The angular argument alone gives no uniform positive lower bound without a radial estimate such as (2).
For varying seeds covered by a fixed sector and Smyth's hypothesis, any remaining shrinking sequence of positive factors has bounded delays by (1) and (2).
Delay one can represent every monic irreducible reciprocal target \(S\) with exactly two exterior roots forming a nonreal conjugate pair as \((z+1)S=zA+A^*\), using irreducible seeds whose two exterior roots become arbitrarily large and approach the imaginary directions, by the [delay-one inverse](Yin_C_G0345_v35.md#statement).
The bounds here leave the individual Mahler gap for such targets open.
