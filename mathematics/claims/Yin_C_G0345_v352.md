# Controlling cyclic phase loss by angular derivatives and the logarithm of the degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## An upper bound for the loss on unit-circle roots

Let \(0<b<p\) be relatively prime integers.
Let \(G\) be an integral Laurent polynomial in \(X,Y\), with \(k\) occupied powers of \(Y\) and fast width \(h<p\), where fast width is the difference of the extreme \(Y\)-exponents.
Let \(B\) be an integral Laurent factor of \(G\), primitive and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), occurring once and having fast width \(1\le s\le h\).
Assume its \(Y\)-fibers are self-inversive on the unit circle away from a finite set, as holds for the [reciprocal factor containing the original input](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#loss).
Let \(d_G,d_B\) be the exponent spans of the corresponding diagonals \(G(x^p,x^b),B(x^p,x^b)\).
Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, on the full torus of its argument.
For normalized arc measure \(\mu\), put
\[
 L(B)=\int_{\mathbb T}\ \sum_{\substack{B(X,u)=0\\|u|=1}}
                     \log\frac{2}{|u^p-X^b|}\,d\mu(X).
\]
Ignore the exceptional fibers of measure zero and count roots with their multiplicities.
There is an absolute constant \(C\) for which
\[
 L(B)\le 2h\,m(B)+2s\,m(G)+Cs\{k+\log(2p d_G)\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
The \(k\) on the right counts the occupied rows of \(G\), and \(d_G\) includes the full chosen multiplier when \(G\) contains padding.
We first prove this bound, then apply it to the original irreducible polynomial and to two residue classes.

<a id="speed"></a>
## Measuring the speed of the neutral phases

Multiply \(G,B\) separately by Laurent monomials so their least base and fast exponents are zero.
These shifts preserve all measures, fiber roots and degree spans in (1).
Write \(G=\sum_j a_j(X)Y^j\) over its occupied rows.
Because \(0\le j\le h<p\), each weight \(pi+bj\) occurs at most once among the monomials \(X^iY^j\) in \(G\), and likewise in \(B\).
Thus every phase twist \(B(x^p,w x^b)\), \(|w|=1\), has span \(d_B\) and \(1\le d_B\le d_G\), the last inequality by additivity of spans in a product.
The degrees of the \(a_j\) are at most \(d_G/p+h\), since the difference of the weights of any two occupied monomials has absolute value at most \(d_G\).

Define \(D=pX\partial_X+bY\partial_Y\) and \(U=DG\).
The polynomial \(B\) does not divide \(U\).
Indeed \(G=B V\) in the Laurent ring and \(B\nmid V\), so divisibility would imply \(B\mid DB\).
The support of \(DB\) lies in that of \(B\), and \(DB\ne0\) because the weights in \(B\) are distinct.
Additivity of widths in both coordinates would make the quotient \(DB/B\) a monomial, whose shift must be zero because a finite support cannot contain a nontrivial translate of itself.
A constant quotient would require all weights of \(B\) to coincide, which they do not.
Gauss's lemma therefore gives a nonzero resultant over \(\mathbb Q(X)\).
Use the formal \(Y\)-degree \(h\) for \(U\): if its actual degree drops, multiply the ordinary resultant by the missing power of the leading coefficient of \(B\).
The resulting polynomial \(\mathcal R(X)\) is integral and nonzero.

Off the zeros of the extreme coefficients, the discriminant of \(B\), the row polynomials and \(\mathcal R\), the \(s\) fiber roots of \(B\) are simple and locally analytic in the circle parameter \(X=e^{it}\).
The discriminant is nonzero by separability.
Self-inversivity and simplicity imply that a unit-circle root stays on the unit circle along its regular arc.
For a neutral branch \(u_i(t)\), put \(\phi_i(t)=\arg(u_i(t)^p/X^b)\).
At a regular root the factor \(V\) is nonzero, since otherwise \(U\) would vanish there.
Implicit differentiation along that branch gives the angular speed
\[
 J_i=|\phi_i'(t)|
    =\left|\frac{pXG_X+bYG_Y}{u_i G_Y}(X,u_i)\right|>0 .             \tag{2}
\]
Here the signed derivative is the negative of the ratio inside the absolute value.

For each \(v\in\mathbb T\) the total number of preimages of \(v\) on the regular neutral branches is at most \(d_B\).
Choose \(w^p=v\).
Every pair \((X,u)\) with \(u^p=vX^b\) has the unique representation \((x^p,w x^b)\): the \(p\) choices of \(x\) for \(X\) give distinct second coordinates.
The desired pairs give roots of the nonzero twisted diagonal of span \(d_B\).
Cut the regular branches into intervals with continuous angle lifts.
Ordinary change of variable, counting visits to each angle modulo \(2\pi\), consequently gives for nonnegative integrable \(H\)
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}\sum_{\rm neutral} H(e^{i\phi_i})J_i\,d\mu
                         \le d_B \int_{\mathbb T}H\,d\mu.             \tag{3}
\]
One may exhaust each open interval by compact subintervals and pass to the limit, so the estimate requires no endpoint derivative bound.

For \(0<\alpha<1\), set \(C_\alpha=\int_{\mathbb T}(2/|1-v|)^\alpha\,d\mu(v)<\infty\).
Let \(M\) denote the mean number of neutral roots, \(0\le M\le s\).
If \(M=0\), then \(L(B)=0\).
Otherwise Jensen's inequality for the logarithm, applied on the branches with total mass normalized to one, yields from (3)
\[
 \alpha L(B)\le M\log(C_\alpha d_B/M)
                   -\int_{\mathbb T}\sum_{\rm neutral}\log J_i\,d\mu . \tag{4}
\]
The following estimates justify the logarithmic integrability in (4).
Alternatively one can first integrate on compact regular arcs omitting the finitely many diagonal zeros and then exhaust them.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## Paying for small angular derivatives with an integer resultant

Write \(m_X(G)\) and \(m_X(B)\) for the one-variable fiber means in \(Y\).
At a regular \(X\), the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) with \(k\) purely imaginary frequencies gives
\[
                  \max_j |a_j(X)|\le e^{C_0 k}\exp(m_X(G)) .           \tag{5}
\]
Indeed if \(T=\sup_{|Y|=1}|G(X,Y)|\) and \(k\ge2\), the fraction with \(|G|<T e^{-t}\) is at most \(\min(1,Ae^{-t/(k-1)})\) for an absolute \(A\ge1\).
Integrating this estimate in \(t\) bounds \(\log T-m_X(G)\) by \((k-1)(1+\log A)\), while each coefficient has modulus at most \(T\).
The one-term case gives the coefficient identity directly.

Put \(Z(X)=1+\max_j|Xa_j'(X)/a_j(X)|\).
For an absolute \(C_1\),
\[
                  \int_{\mathbb T}\log Z\,d\mu\le C_1\log(2p d_G).     \tag{6}
\]
To verify this, factor each row into its complex roots with multiplicity.
The integral of \(|e^{it}-z|^{-1/2}\) is bounded uniformly in \(z\): rotate \(z\) to the positive real axis and use \(|e^{it}-|z||\ge|\sin t|\).
The triangle inequality for the square root and the logarithmic derivative formula give \(\int Z^{1/2}\,d\mu\le 1+C_2 k(d_G/p+h)\).
Jensen's inequality now gives (6), since \(k\le h+1\le p,\ h<p,\ d_G\ge1\).

Let \(A_B\) be the leading \(Y\)-coefficient of \(B\) and \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\) all its roots at a regular fiber.
The resultant and Jensen's fiber formula give the exact identity
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^s \frac{|U(X,u_i)|}{\max(1,|u_i|)^h}
                         =|\mathcal R(X)|\,\exp(-h\,m_X(B)).            \tag{7}
\]
In particular the logarithm of the right side is integrable, and \(m(\mathcal R)\ge0\) since \(\mathcal R\) is nonzero and integral.
For any root, the logarithm of its normalized factor on the left is at most
\(m_X(G)+C_3 k+\log[p(Z+h)]\) by (5).
For a neutral root, (5) likewise gives
\(\log|u_iG_Y(X,u_i)|\le m_X(G)+C_3 k+\log h\).
To bound \(-\sum_{\rm neutral}\log J_i\), sum the latter bounds, subtract the logarithm of (7), and add back the normalized \(U\)-terms for all off-circle roots.
The coefficients of \(m_X(G)\) then sum to exactly \(s\) in every fiber, even if this local mean is negative.
Integrating, using (6) and \(\log(Z+h)\le\log Z+\log(1+h)\), gives
\[
 -\int\sum_{\rm neutral}\log J_i\,d\mu
       \le h\,m(B)+s\,m(G)+C_4s\{k+\log(2p d_G)\}.                    \tag{8}
\]
All individual logarithmic sums here are integrable: their positive parts are bounded by the displayed integrable majorants with positive parts taken, and (7) controls the negative parts of the normalized \(U\)-terms.
For the denominators on neutral branches the same resultant identity applies with \(G_Y\) in place of \(U\), with formal degree \(h-1\), since \(B\) is a simple factor of \(G\).
Thus the logarithms of the speeds are integrable.
In (4) take \(\alpha=1/2\) and use \(M\le s,\ d_B\le d_G,\ -M\log M\le1/e\).
This proves (1).

## An exponential degree requirement for original irreducible inputs

In the [irreducible fixed-count setting](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#statement), take \(B=G\) to be the centered lift of the original \(P\), with diagonal span \(d=\deg P\).
Combining (1) with the [discriminant lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#statement), including \(m(\mathcal D)\ge0\), yields
\[
   m(P)\ge(1-6h/p)m(G)-C_5h\{k+\log(2p d)\}/p .                      \tag{9}
\]
The projected-support alternative gives \(m(G)\ge\eta_k>0\) when \(0<m(P)<\min(\log 2,\log\theta)\).
At fixed \(k\) one has \(h=O_k(p^{1-1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor})\).
Consequently any such sequence with \(p\to\infty\) and \(m(P)\to0\) satisfies
\[
                 \log(2d)\ \ge\ c_k\,p/h
                    \ \ge\ c'_k\,p^{1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor}              \tag{10}
\]
eventually, for some \(c_k,c'_k>0\).
Indeed the coefficient of \(m(G)\) in (9) tends to one, while \(h\log(2p)/p\to0\).
This degree obstruction allows arbitrarily many terms, exterior roots and unbounded row heights.

For a supplied padded representation in the [factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#statement), one may keep \(G\) as the complete primitive lift with its original \(k\) rows and use its simple factor \(B\) carrying the core \(P\).
In that case \(m(G)=m(B)\ge\eta_k\).
Combining (1) with the [factor comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#loss) shows that \(0<m(P)\to0\) at fixed \(k\) requires \(s\log(2d_G)\ge c_k p\) eventually.
This estimate concerns the degree of the chosen reduced multiple.
Using \(G=B\) instead gives a bound in terms of its own span \(d_B\) and its actual row count at most \(s+1\), at cost \(O(s^2+s\log(2pd_B))\) in (1).
Since \(d_B\le D/(1-24s/p)\) for original core degree \(D\), even unrestricted padding is excluded on a shrinking sequence for which \(s^2+s\log(2D)=o(p)\).
For bounded \(s\), including the two- and three-class cases, this excludes \(\log(2D)=o(p)\).

## The offset and seed degree for every coprime two-row graph

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(n\ge1,\ f(0)=\pm1\), let \(g=f^*\), and assume \(\gcd(f,g)=1\).
Let \(p\ge2,\ S\ge1\) be coprime and \(\sigma=\pm1\), and put \(J(x)=f(x^p)+\sigma x^Sg(x^p)\), \(d=pn+S\).
This includes both signs and arbitrary positive offsets in the [two-row normal form](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#rows).
The single root \(u=-\sigma f/g\) of its graph has modulus one throughout the circle.
In fact \(g\) has no unit-circle zero because \(f,g\) have real coefficients and are coprime.
Set
\[
 K=pX(f'g-fg')-Sfg .
\]
The speed of the phase \(u^p/X^S\) is \(|K/(fg)|\), and \(K(0)=-Sf(0)g(0)\ne0\).
For each unit target the preimage bound used in (3) is \(d\), because each phase twist of \(J\) has span \(d\).
Thus the one-branch estimate (4) gives
\[
 \alpha L\le \log(C_\alpha d)+2m(f)-m(K),\qquad
 p\{m(J)-m(f)\}=\log2-L,                                              \tag{11}
\]
where \(L=\int\log(2/|u^p-X^S|)\,d\mu\).
The equality follows by conditioning on \(x^p=X\) and taking the product over the \(p\) choices of \(x^S\).
Jensen's formula at zero gives \(m(K)\ge\log(S|\operatorname{lc} f|)\).
The [nonreciprocal seed gap](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) gives \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\).
Rearranging (11), for each fixed \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(\alpha p>2\) we obtain
\[
 \log(1+pn/S)
       \ \ge\ (\alpha p-2)\log\theta-\alpha p\,m(J)
                  +\alpha\log2+\log|\operatorname{lc}f|-\log C_\alpha .
\]
In particular, along any sequence with \(p\to\infty,\ m(J)\to0\),
\[
                    \liminf \frac{\log(1+pn/S)}p\ge\log\theta .         \tag{12}
\]
For this last conclusion first hold \(\alpha\) fixed, then let it increase to one.
Thus even exponential growth of \(S\) does not offset the required growth of \(n/S\).
Unlike a criterion based on common sparse multiples, (12) imposes no a priori bound on the support complexity of the seed.
