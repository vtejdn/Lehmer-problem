# Integer points in the simplex of interlacing traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact realization at the first nonunit pole value

Fix integers \(q,n\ge2\) and put \(M=q(q+1),\ h=1/M,\ K=(2q+1)^2=4M+1\).
Identify each monic real polynomial of degree \(n\) with its \(n\) coefficients below the leading term.
Let \(\mathcal A\) be the affine hyperplane defined by \(M^nH(h)=K\), and let \(\Lambda=\mathcal A\cap\mathbb Z^n\).
For an ordered grid \(\tau=(-4=t_0<\cdots<t_{n-1}=0)\), let
\[
 \mathcal S_\tau=\{H\in\mathcal A:\ (-1)^{n-i}H(t_i)>0\quad(0\le i<n)\}.
\]
Call \(\mathcal S_\tau\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\) **integral realization** on this grid.
For a rational affine observation map \(\pi\) on coefficients, call \(\pi(\mathcal S_\tau)\cap\pi(\Lambda)\ne\varnothing\) **realization of the observed data**.
One can also prescribe the common value \(y\) and test both sets in the fiber \(\pi^{-1}(y)\).
Examples of \(\pi\) include a coefficient prefix, values at rational points, or their joint vector.

* The pole lattice is nonempty for every \(n\).
  For \(n=2\) the sign region is empty, and for \(n\ge3\) it is a relatively open simplex of dimension \(n-1\) containing rational polynomials on every real grid.
  On each fixed grid the intersection with the lattice has at most one point.
* A point in the intersection is automatically irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
  It gives a Salem unit \(T\) with \(1<T<1+1/q\), \(M(T)=T\), where \(M(T)\) denotes Mahler measure, and the common-power units in the [consecutive-coefficient construction](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#statement).
  Every monic integral polynomial with the pole value, distinct roots, one root in \((0,h)\) and all other roots in \((-4,0)\) occurs in this description even if only rational grids are used.
  Thus integral realization for unbounded \(q\) would make the unrestricted positive Mahler gap zero.

<a id="simplex"></a>
## The lattice and the simplex in the same hyperplane

A point of the pole lattice is \(P_0=x^{n-1}(x+4)\).
For any other point the difference vanishes at \(h\) and has degree at most \(n-1\).
Since \(Mx-1\) is primitive, Gauss's lemma gives
\[
 \Lambda=P_0+(Mx-1)\mathbb Z[x]_{\le n-2}.
\]
In particular the lattice has full rank \(n-1\) in the hyperplane.
For the grid put \(Q(x)=\prod_i(x-t_i),\ Q_i=Q/(x-t_i)\), and \(\delta=K/\prod_i(1-Mt_i)\).
The positive-pole [interpolation identity](Yin_C_G0345_v888.md#moments) gives the affine coordinates
\[
 H=\delta Q+(x-h)\sum_i b_iQ_i,\qquad
 b_i=\frac{H(t_i)}{(t_i-h)Q'(t_i)}>0,\qquad \sum_i b_i=1-\delta .
\]
Conversely these weights give exactly the required monic polynomials, by evaluating at \(h\), at the \(t_i\), and at the leading coefficient.
The evaluation formula for \(b_i\) gives injectivity.
When \(n=2\) the denominator in \(\delta\) equals \(K\), and the required positive sum is zero.
Every interior node for \(n\ge3\) contributes a factor strictly greater than one, so \(0<\delta<1\).
The weights then give an open simplex in the hyperplane.
Rational polynomials are dense there because its equation and \(P_0\) are rational.
The same density argument applies in any observation fiber meeting \(\Lambda\), by rational elimination.
Thus the real polynomial realizing common rational data with an integral point can itself be taken rational.
The [weighted coefficient reconstruction](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0185/trace_grid.md#pole-weights) gives at most one integral point per grid: each coefficient lies in an interval of length \(4(1-\delta)<4\), and its residue modulo \(M\ge6\) is fixed by the preceding coefficients and the pole.

<a id="return"></a>
## Automatic irreducibility and return to Mahler measure

Strict alternation puts one root in every negative grid interval, and \(H(0)<0<H(h)\) puts the last in \((0,h)\).
Conversely choose rational separating nodes between consecutive negative roots and take the two endpoints.
Suppose an integral \(H\) in this region is reducible, and take a nonconstant monic integral factor \(J\) complementary to the irreducible factor of the positive root.
All roots of \(J\) are negative.
The positive integer \(K_J=M^{\deg J}J(h)\) divides \(K\), by multiplicativity on monic integral factors.
For \(\deg J\ge2\),
\[
 K_J=\prod_{J(v)=0}(1-Mv)>M^{\deg J}|J(0)|\ge M^2>4M+1 .
\]
For a linear factor \(J=x+a\) one has \(a=1,2,3\).
Then \(1+aM\mid 4M+1\) would imply \(1+aM\mid4-a\), which is impossible.
This excludes all complementary factors.

For the positive root \(w\), the integral element \(1-Mw\) has norm \(K\).
The product of its other conjugates gives \(K/(1-Mw)\) integral, as required by the [inverse construction](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion).
Set \(T+T^{-1}=2+w,\ T>1\).
There is one exterior conjugate \(T\) and the negative trace places give unit-circle pairs, so \(T\) is Salem and its log measure is \(\log T\).
The identity \((q+1)/q+q/(q+1)-2=h\) and monotonicity on \(T>1\) give \(\log T<\log(1+1/q)\).
Here \(T<(q+1)/q\) also makes the second graph coordinate positive.
No existence of an integral point is assumed beyond the individual grids for which this implication is used.
