# Polynomially large Fourier coefficients on the odd part of Salem period quotients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A polynomial lower bound on a dense set of periods

Fix a Salem number \(\alpha\) with minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), and put \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(I_n=(\alpha^n-1)R\) and \(G_n=R/I_n\).
For the fair binary digit law \(\mu_n\) and the energy of a character use the [period definitions](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#statement).
The conductor ideal of a character \(\chi\) is \(\mathfrak a_\chi=\{x\in R:\chi(xy)=1\ \text{for all }y\in R\}\).
There exist \(C_P,B_P,\sigma_P>0\) such that for all but \(O_P(N^{1-\sigma_P})\) integers \(N\le n<2N\), there is a nontrivial character satisfying
\[
           |\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\ge n^{-C_P},\qquad
           \sum_{j<n}\operatorname{dist}(t_j,\mathbb Z)^2=O_P(\log n),
           \qquad \mathfrak a_\chi/I_n=G_n[2^\infty].                 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(G_n[2^\infty]\) is the subgroup of elements of order a power of two.
Thus the conductor quotient \(R/\mathfrak a_\chi\) has the full odd part of \(|G_n|\) as its order.
For every sufficiently large \(N\), every interval of \(\lceil(\log N)^{B_P}\rceil\) consecutive periods in \([N,2N)\) contains a period satisfying (1).
When \(P(1)\) is odd, a positive density of all periods have full conductor and the polynomial bound in (1), and the same short-interval existence holds with full conductor, after increasing \(B_P\) if necessary.
The [quasipolynomial bound with full conductor](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#statement) also applies where \(G_n\) has even order.
All assertions concern a fixed Salem input.

<a id="odd"></a>
## The size of the omitted primary subgroup

Take \(g\) to be the order of the unit \(\alpha\) in the finite group \((R/4R)^\times\).
There is a constant \(C_0=C_0(P)\ge1\) such that
\[
                          |G_n[2^\infty]|\le C_0\,2^{d v_2(n)}.        \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(I_{gn}\subseteq I_n\), and \(|G_{gn}|=|N((\alpha^g)^n-1)|\).
Writing \(n=2^a b\) with \(b\) odd, the geometric sum for the odd power of \(\alpha^g\) is one modulo two and has odd norm.
For each succeeding square the additional factor is \(1+v\) with \(v\equiv1\bmod4R\).
Then \((1+v)/2\) is an order element congruent to one modulo two, with odd norm.
Each square increases the norm valuation by \(d\), proving (2) with \(C_0=2^{v_2(N(\alpha^g-1))}\).
In particular \(|G_n[2^\infty]|\le C_0(2N)^d\) throughout the dyadic interval.

Put \(h_0=\lceil\log_2(C_0(2N)^d)\rceil\), \(H=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor\) and \(K=2^{h_0+H}\).
For all \(h_0\le h<h_0+H\), multiplication by \(2^h\) annihilates the two-primary subgroup and is invertible on the odd subgroup.
If \(\chi_*\) has full conductor then the conductor ideal of \(2^h\chi_*\) is the preimage of this two-primary subgroup in \(R\), by applying the full conductor identity to \(2^h x\).
It suffices to select one of these \(H\) characters with the asserted amplitude.

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Separation of the final sine at the endpoints

Use the [simultaneously contracting neutral unit](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#unit) and its full-conductor trace character \(\chi_*\), writing its phases as \(t_{n,j}\).
In that construction one can increase \(A\) arbitrarily and then increase the constant in \(J=\lceil C_1\log N\rceil\).
Choose them so that all middle phases \(Kt_{n,j}\) have modulus at most \(1/4\) and their squared sum is \(O_P(1)\).
Indeed \(K\ll_P N^{d+1}\), the neutral denominators have inverse bounded by a fixed power of \(N\), the neutral numerator is \(O_P(N^{-A-1})\) and the two real tails decay geometrically beyond \(J\).
This choice yields the same middle estimates for every \(2^h\) considered above.
The odd part of \(|G_n|\) grows at least exponentially in \(n\) up to a fixed polynomial factor by (2) and the norm estimate in that trace construction.

At the ends apply the [cotangent representation](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#affine) to \(Kt_{n,j}\).
The affine forms and their slopes are multiplied by \(K\), and the errors remain exponentially small.
Every maximal slope is at least \(N^{-D_P}\) and at most \(O_P(N^{-2})\) after increasing \(A\) as just described, with \(D_P\) allowed to depend on this choice.
Test distance of the forms to integers.
The integrated [slab and box estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#avoidance) is unchanged with integer targets in place of half-integers: their spacing is one, the forms have variation \(o(1)\) away from the singular coordinate strips, and the derivative in the direction of maximal slope has the same lower bound.
More explicitly, for a block of \(M\le N\) periods and \(v=M^{-\nu}\), outside a proportion \(O_P(Jv+v^{-4s}M^{-\xi})\), each of the \(2J\) endpoints satisfies
\[
                       \operatorname{dist}(Kt_{n,j},\mathbb Z)
                                    \ge \tfrac12 v N^{-D_P}.           \tag{3}
\]
Here \(s=(d-2)/2\), \(\xi>0\) is the [box discrepancy exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v674.md#discrepancy), and \(0<\nu<\min(1/4,\xi/(4s+2))\).
As before this gives the exceptional count with \(M=N\) and the short-block assertion for \(M=\lceil(\log N)^{B_P}\rceil\) with \(B_P>2/\nu\).

<a id="telescope"></a>
## Averaging over successive doublings

Fix one of the remaining periods.
For every endpoint let \(x=t_{n,j}\).
The final sine \(\sin(\pi Kx)\) is nonzero by (3), so successive doubling gives
\[
 \sum_{h=h_0}^{h_0+H-1}-\log|\cos(\pi2^h x)|
  =H\log 2+\log|\sin(\pi2^{h_0}x)|-\log|\sin(\pi Kx)|
  \le H\log2+O_P(\log N).
\]
The last inequality uses (3), \(M\le N\), and \(|\sin(\pi y)|\ge2\operatorname{dist}(y,\mathbb Z)\).
Sum over the \(2J\) endpoints and divide by \(H\).
At some common \(h\), their total negative logarithm is \(O_P(J)=O_P(\log N)\), since \(H\asymp\log N\).
The middle costs \(O_P(1)\) at every \(h\) and every remaining \(n\).
The Fourier product therefore gives (1) for \(2^h\chi_*\).
Its squared centered energy is bounded by \(2J/4+O_P(1)\).
Its conductor has nontrivial odd order for sufficiently large \(n\) by the norm estimate above.

Finally, if \(P(1)\) is odd, all periods \(n\equiv1\bmod g\) have odd \(|G_n|\), since \(\alpha^n-1\equiv\alpha-1\bmod2R\) and \(|N(\alpha-1)|=|P(1)|\).
Removing the dyadic power-saving exceptional sets from this progression leaves a set of density \(1/g\).
For intervals of polylogarithmic length, run the box estimate on this progression inside each interval.
Its step angles are those of \(z_i^g\), whose multiplicative independence and logarithmic form bounds follow from the same properties at \(z_i\) with the fixed multiplier \(g\).
The interval then contains a consecutive block of \(\gg_P(\log N)^{B_P}\) indices along the progression, and increasing \(B_P\) makes its exceptional proportion tend to zero.
Here the two-primary subgroup is trivial and (1) gives exact full conductor.
If \(P(1)\) is even then \(G_1\) has even order and is a quotient of every \(G_n\), so the odd-part assertion alone leaves full conductor unresolved at the polynomial amplitude scale.
