# A quantitative two-residue Mahler comparison from sparsity of common multiples

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cyclic comparison

Write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure and \(\#A\) for the number of nonzero coefficients of a polynomial \(A\).
Let \(f\in\mathbb R[z]\) have nonzero constant term, put \(g(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z)\), and let \(D\ge2\) and \(S\ge1\) be relatively prime integers.
For \(|w|=1\), set
\[
 P_w(x)=f(x^D)+w x^S g(x^D).
\]
Choose any nonzero \(H\in\mathbb C[z]\), and put \(A=Hf,\ B=Hg,\ N=\#A+\#B,\ k=\min(\#A,\#B)\).
Let \(c\ge1\) be the absolute constant in the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001).
Then
\[
 -\frac{(N-2)\log D+\log(\pi k/2)+c(N-1)}{D}
 \ \le\ m(P_w)-m(f)\ \le\ \frac{\log 2}{D}.                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular the difference tends uniformly to zero when \(D\to\infty\) and \(N\log D=o(D)\).
The estimate holds for arbitrary effective offsets \(S/D\), degrees, coefficient sizes, and numbers of exterior roots.
Choosing \(D=d/\gcd(d,s)\) and \(S=s/\gcd(d,s)\) gives the same measures for an unreduced pair \(d,s\) by power substitution.
Both the cost and the zeros of the common multiplier disappear from the difference in (1).

<a id="grid"></a>
## Clipping the closest of the cyclic phases

Use normalized arc measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb T\).
Let \(Q_w(x)=A(x^D)+w x^S B(x^D)=H(x^D)P_w(x)\).
The supports of the two summands occupy distinct residues modulo \(D\), so \(Q_w\) has exactly \(N\) terms and its largest coefficient modulus \(L>0\) is independent of \(w\).
On the circle \(|A|=|B|\) since \(f\) is real and \(g=f^*\).
Thus \(\sup_{\mathbb T}|A|=\sup_{\mathbb T}|B|\le k L\).
Off the finite zero set of \(A(x^D)\), write
\[
 V_w(x)=\frac{|Q_w(x)|}{|A(x^D)|}=|1+w u(x)|,\qquad
 u(x)=x^S B(x^D)/A(x^D),\qquad |u(x)|=1 .
\]
The logarithm of \(V_w\) is integrable as a difference of logarithms of absolute values of nonzero polynomials, and its integral is \(m(P_w)-m(f)\).
As \(x\) runs through the rotations \(\zeta x\) for \(\zeta^D=1\), the values \(u(\zeta x)=\zeta^S u(x)\) run through a complete grid of \(D\) phases.
The product of the \(D\) corresponding values \(V_w(\zeta x)\) is \(|1-(-wu(x))^D|\le2\).
Integrating the logarithm and using rotation invariance gives the upper bound in (1).

Fix \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), and take \(t\in[0,\pi/D]\) to be the angular distance from \(-1\) to the closest grid point \(w\zeta^S u(x)\).
The smallest factor is \(2\sin(t/2)\) and the product of all \(D\) factors is \(2\sin(Dt/2)\).
Omitting one smallest factor therefore gives a product of at least \(2D/\pi\), since \(\sin(Dt/2)\ge Dt/\pi\) and \(\sin(t/2)\le t/2\).
At \(t=0\) the quotient has the continuous value \(D\).
Clipping every factor from below at \(\varepsilon\) makes the product at least \(2D\varepsilon/\pi\).
Rotation invariance now gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(V_w,\varepsilon)\,d\mu
       \ \ge\ D^{-1}\log(2D\varepsilon/\pi).                         \tag{2}
\]

<a id="tail"></a>
## Recovering the logarithm with a bound independent of coefficient height

By its Fourier coefficient of modulus \(L\), \(\sup_{\mathbb T}|Q_w|\ge L\).
Apply the Turán–Nazarov inequality on \([0,2\pi]\) to the \(N\) terms of \(Q_w(e^{it})\) with purely imaginary distinct frequencies.
For \(v>0\) this gives \(\mu\{|Q_w|<vL\}\le c v^{1/(N-1)}\), also when the set has zero measure.
The bound on \(|A|\) and integration over logarithmic levels give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_{\mathbb T}\log^+(\varepsilon/V_w)\,d\mu
 &\le \int_0^\infty \mu\{|Q_w|<\varepsilon k L e^{-t}\}\,dt\\
 &\le c(N-1)(\varepsilon k)^{1/(N-1)} .
\end{aligned}                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Subtract (3) from (2) and take \(\varepsilon=k^{-1}D^{-(N-1)}\).
This proves the lower bound in (1), including \(N=2\).
The only input for (3) beyond the phase identities is the stated source inequality with an absolute constant independent of the frequencies and coefficients.

<a id="escape"></a>
## The term count required for a shrinking two-row sequence

For each nonconstant integral seed with \(f(0)=\pm1,\ \gcd(f,f^*)=1\), put
\[
 \tau(f)=\min_{0\ne H\in\mathbb C[z]} \big(\#(Hf)+\#(Hf^*)\big).
\]
This minimum exists because the set of counts is a nonempty set of positive integers.
Such an \(f\) is nonreciprocal, so \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\), where \(\theta>1\) is the real root of \(z^3-z-1\), by the [whole-polynomial reciprocity threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity).
If \(D_i\to\infty\) and \(m(P_{w_i})\to0\), then
\[
               \liminf_{i\to\infty}
               \frac{\tau(f_i)\log D_i}{D_i}\ \ge\ \log\theta .        \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, on any subsequence where the ratio is bounded, choose minimizing multipliers in (1).
Then \(N_i=O(D_i/\log D_i)\), so both \(c(N_i-1)/D_i\) and \(\log(\pi k_i/2)/D_i\) tend to zero.
The lower bound in (1) gives (4).
This applies in particular to the [coprime two-residue normal form](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#rows) with either sign.
An arbitrary polynomial need not admit that normal form, and (4) leaves seeds with sufficiently large \(\tau(f)\) unresolved.

<a id="multinacci"></a>
## Multinacci seeds and the earlier phase estimate

For \(f_n=z^n-z^{n-1}-\cdots-z-1\), \(n\ge2\), take \(H=1-z\).
The two products are \(A=(2-z)z^n-1\) and \(B=1-2z+z^{n+1}\), with three terms each.
The [multinacci seed calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v29.md#multinacci) gives \(m(f_n)=\log\theta_n\), where \(\theta_n\to2\) is the Pisot root.
Equation (1) yields
\[
              m(P_w)=\log\theta_n+O(\log D/D)
\]
uniformly in \(n,S,w\).
In particular the joint limit is \(\log 2\) as \(n,D\to\infty\), including \(S/D\to\infty\).

For signs \(w=\sigma=\pm1\), the qualitative convergence for these seeds is already a consequence of the [earlier sparse-multiple phase estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#sparse-phases).
In that estimate take the seed \(f(x^D)\), the reciprocal multiplier \(H(x^D)\) up to sign, phase count \(j=D\), integer offset \(-S\), and sign \(-\sigma\).
Its phase polynomial is \(f(x^D)^D-(-\sigma)^D x^{SD}g(x^D)^D\), whose measure is \(D m(P_\sigma)\) by the [cyclic norm identity](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#norm) and power substitution.
For this use of the earlier result the seed measure and the term count are bounded and the multiplier has measure one.
The grid calculation above gives an explicit dependence on a growing joint term count, permits arbitrary common multipliers and removes the seed-measure bound.
For the actual multinacci polynomials it gives a decay rate for the [boundary deficit allowed by disk compactness alone](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#scope).
