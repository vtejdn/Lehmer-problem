# Full-conductor Salem characters with small energy and large Fourier amplitude

<a id="statement"></a>
## Every period for energy and almost every period for amplitude

Fix a Salem number \(\alpha>1\) of degree \(d=2s+2\), its minimal polynomial \(P\), its order \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) and \(G_n=R/(\alpha^n-1)R\).
Let \(\mu_n\) be the law on \(G_n\) of \(\sum_{j<n}\xi_j\alpha^j\), with independent fair binary digits indexed from zero.
For a character \(\chi(\alpha^j)=e^{2\pi i t_j}\), let its energy be \(\sum_{j<n}\operatorname{dist}(t_j,\mathbb Z)^2\).
Full conductor means that the \(x\in R\) with \(\chi(xy)=1\) for all \(y\in R\) form precisely \((\alpha^n-1)R\).

At every sufficiently large \(n\) there is a nontrivial character of full conductor and energy \(O_P(\log n)\).
There are constants \(C_P,B_P,\sigma_P>0\) such that, for all but \(O_P(N^{1-\sigma_P})\) integers \(N\le n<2N\), such a character also satisfies
\[
                       |\widehat\mu_n(\chi)|\ \ge\
                           \exp[-C_P(\log n)^2].                        \tag{1}
\]
For every sufficiently large integer \(N\), each interval of \(\lceil(\log N)^{B_P}\rceil\) consecutive periods contained in \([N,2N)\) contains a period satisfying both estimates.
These conclusions complement the [bounds with prescribed congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v634.md#statement).
For fixed \(P\) the largest nontrivial amplitude still [tends to zero](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#statement).
The estimates give no uniform control of \(C_P,B_P,\sigma_P\) as the Salem number varies.

<a id="unit"></a>
## One unit for all periods in a dyadic interval

Write \(r=\alpha^{-1}\) and choose \(z_1,\ldots,z_s\) from the neutral conjugate pairs.
By the [logarithmic form bound for powers](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle), there is a fixed \(A>1\) such that \(|z_i^n-1|\ge N^{-A+1}\) for \(N\le n<2N\) sufficiently large, for every \(i\).
Increase \(A\) if necessary to absorb constants.
Use the [unit construction in the digit order](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#units) at \(\varepsilon=N^{-A-1}\).
It gives a unit \(u\in R^\times\) positive at the two real embeddings, of modulus \(O_P(\varepsilon)\) at each neutral embedding and \(O_P(\varepsilon^{-s})\) at the real embeddings.
Put \(b=u/P'(\alpha)\), and use the same \(b\) for the whole dyadic interval.
The [trace-dual identity](Yin_C_G0345_v626.md#conductor) shows that
\[
            t_{n,j}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}
                 \frac{b\alpha^j}{\alpha^n-1}
\]
defines a character of exact full conductor.
It is nontrivial for large \(N\), since the two real factors and the neutral lower estimates give \(|N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q}(\alpha^n-1)|\gg_P\alpha^n N^{-2sA}\).

Take \(J=\lceil C_1\log N\rceil\), with \(C_1\) sufficiently large depending only on \(P,A\).
The neutral contribution to every \(t_{n,j}\), \(0\le j<n\), is \(O_P(N^{-2})\).
For \(J\le j<n-J\) the real terms are \(O_P(N^{(A+1)s}(\alpha^{-j}+\alpha^{j-n}))\).
By increasing \(C_1\), the middle has absolute values less than \(1/4\) and sum of squares \(O_P(1)\), by summing the geometric tails.
The two ends contribute at most \(2J/4\) to the centered energy.
In the Fourier product the total negative logarithm from the middle is \(O_P(1)\), using \(-\log|\cos(\pi x)|=O(x^2)\) for \(|x|\le1/4\).

<a id="affine"></a>
## Endpoint values are affine functions of cotangents

Let \(z_i^n=e^{2\pi i\theta_i}\), and put \(y_i=\cot(\pi\theta_i)\).
Up to errors \(O_P(e^{-c_P N})\) from the real embeddings, the \(2J\) endpoint values are the following affine forms in \(y\).
For \(0\le j<J\), the real term is \(-b_r r^j\) and the \(i\)-th neutral pair contributes \(-\Re w_i+(\Im w_i)y_i\), with \(w=b\alpha^j\) and \(w_i\) its image at \(z_i\).
For \(j=n-l\), \(1\le l\le J\), the real term is \(b_\alpha\alpha^{-l}\) and the neutral pair contributes \(\Re w_i+(\Im w_i)y_i\), with \(w=b\alpha^{-l}\).
These expressions use \(1/(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)=-1/2-(i/2)\cot(\pi\theta)\).
All coefficients of the forms are independent of the chosen period in \([N,2N)\).

For each form let \(a=\max_i|\Im w_i|\).
There exists \(D_P>0\) such that
\[
                           N^{-D_P}\le a\ll_P N^{-A-1}                 \tag{2}
\]
uniformly at these endpoints.
Indeed inversion \(\iota(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}\) is an involution of the Salem field and acts as complex conjugation under each neutral embedding.
The two real values of \(P'\) at the largest and the smallest root have opposite signs, so \(w\) and \(\iota(w)\) have opposite signs in the distinguished real embedding, even for negative exponents.
Hence \(w-\iota(w)\ne0\), and every \(\Im w_i\ne0\).
A fixed positive integer clears the denominator of \(b\) for every selected unit.
The conjugates of the resulting integral multiple of \(w-\iota(w)\) have modulus at most a fixed power of \(N\), since \(|j|\) or \(l\) is \(O_P(\log N)\) in the corresponding formula.
Its nonzero integer norm gives the lower bound in (2) at each neutral embedding.
The upper bound uses the chosen neutral absolute values of \(u\).

<a id="discrepancy"></a>
## A uniform box estimate in the period variable

The \(z_i\) are multiplicatively independent.
If their product with integer exponents is one, take a splitting-field automorphism carrying \(z_i\) to \(\alpha\).
Only the inverse of \(z_i\) can map to \(r\), and it was omitted from the list.
Taking absolute values in the transformed relation forces the exponent of \(z_i\) to be zero.
For every nonzero integer vector \(h\), the [complex logarithmic form bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) thus gives
\[
                        |1-\textstyle\prod_i z_i^{h_i}|
                             \gg_P (2+\|h\|_\infty)^{-D}
\]
for a fixed \(D\).
Use the fixed logarithms of the \(z_i\) and subtract the nearest multiple of \(2\log(-1)\) so that the imaginary part lies in \([-\pi,\pi]\).
The resulting nonzero form has coefficients \(O_s(1+\|h\|_\infty)\), and chord length is at least \(2/\pi\) times the absolute reduced angle on this interval.

In any \(M\) consecutive periods, the empirical proportion of the angle vectors in an axis-parallel torus box \(Q\) is at most
\[
                               |Q|+O_P(M^{-\xi})                       \tag{3}
\]
for some \(\xi>0\), uniformly in the box and the starting period, with \(|Q|\) its normalized volume.
Here are details to include the shrinking boxes needed below.
For the product of the one-dimensional Fejér kernels of order \(H\), the mass outside distance \(H^{-1/2}\) from zero in any coordinate is \(O_s(H^{-1/2})\), by the elementary bound \(K_H(x)\ll 1/(H\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb Z)^2)\).
Convolving the indicator of the enlarged box with this product and dividing by \(1-O_s(H^{-1/2})\) majorizes its original indicator, where each edge is enlarged by \(H^{-1/2}\) at each end.
The mean of the majorant is at most \(|Q|+O_s(H^{-1/2})\).
Its nonconstant Fourier coefficients are bounded absolutely by two for large \(H\), with \(O_s(H^s)\) frequencies.
The finite geometric sum for each frequency has normalized modulus \(O_P(H^D/M)\).
Taking \(H=\lfloor M^{1/(2(D+s+1))}\rfloor\) yields (3).

<a id="avoidance"></a>
## Counting the exceptional periods

Take any block of \(M\) periods in \([N,2N)\), \(M\le N\), and put \(v=M^{-\nu}\) for a fixed \(0<\nu<\min(1/4,\xi/(4s+2))\).
Consider first the angles having some coordinate within \(v\) of an integer.
By (3) these occupy proportion \(O_P(v+M^{-\xi})\).
On the remaining region all \(|y_i|\ll v^{-1}\).
Each endpoint form varies by at most \(O_s(a/v)=o(1)\) from its constant term, by (2), so at most two half-integer targets within distance \(O_s(av)\) need be considered per form for large \(N,M\).

Partition the angle torus into cubes of side \(1/\lceil v^{-4}\rceil\).
On every cube meeting the remaining region all coordinates stay at distance at least \(v/2\) from the ends of the circle, and the variation of any one endpoint form across it is \(O_s(av^2)\) by differentiation of the cotangents.
Take the union of cubes meeting a point of the remaining region where the form is within \(av\) of a half-integer.
This union has volume \(O_s(v)\).
Indeed it lies within distance \(O_s(av)\) of at most two half-integers in value, and for fixed other coordinates the derivative in a coordinate of maximal slope has fixed sign and modulus at least \(\pi a\) on \((0,1)\).
Integrating the resulting interval length proves the volume bound.
The union over all \(2J\) forms has volume \(O_P(Jv)\).
Applying (3) to its at most \(O_s(v^{-4s})\) cubes bounds the total exceptional proportion, including the singular region, by
\[
                          O_P(Jv+v^{-4s}M^{-\xi}) .                    \tag{4}
\]
Outside it all endpoint distances to half-integers for the actual traces are at least \(av/2\), since \(M\le N\), (2) holds and the real errors are exponentially small.
Each endpoint therefore contributes \(O_P(\log N)\) to the negative logarithm of the absolute cosine, using \(|\cos(\pi x)|\ge2\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb Z+1/2)\).
Together with the middle estimate this proves (1) on every remaining period.
For \(M=N\), (4) is \(O_P(N^{-\nu/2})\).
For \(M=\lceil(\log N)^{B_P}\rceil\) with \(B_P>2/\nu\) it tends to zero, proving the assertion for every such interval.
