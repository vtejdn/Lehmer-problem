# A common factor of two delayed polynomial equations

<a id="statement"></a>
## The compression estimate

Let \(f=P/Q\) and \(g=R/S\) be nonzero rational functions over \(\mathbb Q\) in reduced form, with integral numerators and denominators of degrees at most \(W\ge1\) and sums of absolute coefficients at most \(L\ge2\).
Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) where both functions have defined nonzero values.
Suppose that
\[
                 f(\alpha)=\alpha^n,\qquad g(\alpha)=\alpha^m,
                 \qquad n,m\in\mathbb Z,\quad n\ge1,\quad |m|\le n .
\]
The two functions, the unit and its field may all vary between inputs.
One has the following alternative, with absolute constants.

* There are integers \(a,b,c\), with \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), such that \(f^a g^b=X^c\) identically and \(an+bm=c\).
* With \(W_0=\max(\deg P,\deg Q)\) and \(h\) the absolute logarithmic Weil height,
  \[
       d\le C(W+\sqrt{nW}),\qquad (n-W_0)h(\alpha)\le\log L .          \tag{1}
  \]
  In particular \(n\le C'W(1+\log L)^2(\log(2d))^6\) and
  \[
       d\le C''W(1+\log L)(\log(2d))^3 .                              \tag{2}
  \]
  The constants also cover small \(d,n\).

For fixed \(W,L\), outside the identity case there are only finitely many possible units and exponent pairs.
For bounded \(L\), every sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and \(W=o(d/(\log(2d))^3)\) must eventually satisfy a rational-function identity in the first alternative.
In particular, for functions multiplicatively independent modulo \(\mathbb Q^\times X^{\mathbb Z}\) the inequalities apply to every such common factor.
This supplies a restriction on two moving relations distinct from the [eventual description of spacings for one fixed unit](Yin_C_G0345_v162.md#statement).

<a id="compression"></a>
## A short combination of the two exponents

Take \(H=\max(1,\lceil\sqrt{n/W}\rceil)\).
Among the fractional parts of \(jm/n\) for \(0\le j\le H\), two lie in the same one of \(H\) intervals of length \(1/H\) partitioning \([0,1)\).
Subtracting, find \(1\le |b|\le H\) and \(a\in\mathbb Z\) such that \(|c|=|an+bm|\le n/H\), with \(|a|\le H+1\) since \(|m|\le n\).
Write \(f^a g^b=U/V\) by multiplying powers of the four given polynomials, using the inverse ratios for negative exponents.
Both degrees are at most \(W(|a|+|b|)\), even without canceling common factors, and \(U(\alpha)V(\alpha)\ne0\).
The numerator obtained by clearing the Laurent equation \(U/V=X^c\) is either identically zero or a nonzero rational polynomial of degree at most
\[
            (2H+1)W+n/H .
\]
In the first case this is the asserted identity with \(b\ne0\).
In the second case that polynomial vanishes at \(\alpha\) by the two given equations, so its degree is at least \(d\).
If \(n\ge W\), the displayed bound is at most \(C(W+\sqrt{nW})\), and for \(n<W\) one uses \(H=1\) and the same bound.

<a id="height"></a>
## The degree and delay cannot both grow with fixed blocks

At a finite place of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) the unit \(\alpha\) and the integral coefficients give \(\max(|P(\alpha)|,|Q(\alpha)|)\le1\).
At an archimedean embedding the same maximum is at most \(L\max(1,|\alpha|)^{W_0}\).
Using the homogeneous coordinates \([P(\alpha):Q(\alpha)]\) for the absolute height and the product formula yields \(nh(\alpha)=h(f(\alpha))\le W_0h(\alpha)+\log L\).
This part of (1) holds also in the identity case.

The [classical degree-dependent measure estimate](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) gives \(d\,h(\alpha)\ge c/(\log(2d))^3\), using its weaker consequence with bounded numerator and decreasing \(c>0\) for the finitely many small degrees by the coefficient bound and Kronecker's theorem.
For \(n\le2W\) compression already gives \(d\ll W\), which proves the asserted estimates in that range.
For \(n>2W\) one has \(d\ll\sqrt{nW}\) and
\[
      n\le C d\log L\,(\log(2d))^3
          \le C'\sqrt{nW}\log L\,(\log(2d))^3 .
\]
Division by \(\sqrt n\) and substitution back into (1) prove the bounds.
When \(W,L\) are fixed, (2) bounds \(d\) since \(d/(\log(2d))^3\to\infty\).
It then bounds \(n\), hence also \(|m|\).
There are finitely many possible rational functions with these degree and integral coefficient bounds, and in the nonidentity case each of the finitely many compressed nonzero polynomials supplies finitely many roots.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## When a second spacing can persist

Suppose the two functions have no nontrivial multiplicative relation modulo constants and integral powers of \(X\).
Then \(f^a g^b/X^c\) cannot be identically one for any \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\).
More generally, even when a relation modulo this group exists, it must have scalar exactly one to furnish the first alternative with \(c=an+bm\).
This is an equality of the rational functions themselves; equality of their measures or of the two heights at a chosen root does not supply it.
