# Exact local factor degrees and irreducible families at the primes dividing a tetranomial coefficient

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two families with no nontorsion unit root

Let \(0<u<v\), \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), \(|a|,|b|\ge2\), and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Consider
\[
          P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+bX^u(1+\epsilon X^{v-u}).
\]
At an odd prime \(\ell\) dividing a coefficient use \(N=v-u\) for \(\ell\mid a\), and \(N=v+u\) for \(\ell\mid b\).
Write \(r\) for the valuation of that coefficient, \(s=v_\ell(N)\), and assume \(s>r\ge1\).
Put \(L=\ell^{s-r+1}\) and \(h=N/\ell^s\).

Suppose first that \(r=h=1\), so \(N=\ell^s\) with \(s\ge2\), and put \(\xi=-\epsilon\).
In either of the following families the primitive polynomial \(P/(X-\xi)\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\):

* \(\ell\mid b\), with unrestricted \(u,v\) subject to these conditions;
* \(\ell\mid a\) and \((v-u)\log\ell>2u\log(|a|+|b|)\).

Thus the only root of \(P\) that is an algebraic unit is \(\xi\).
At every positive common dilation \(P(X^g)\) a nontorsion algebraic unit is still impossible, so every positive-measure integral divisor has total logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log2\).
For the consecutive magnitudes \(|a|=q,\ |b|=q+1\), concrete unbounded coefficient families are \(q=\ell-1,\ u+v=\ell^s\) and \(q=\ell,\ v-u=\ell^s\) with the indicated inequality in the latter.
In both families \(s\) may grow arbitrarily fast, so these exclusions reach the large ratios \(v/u\) permitted by the archimedean estimates.

More generally remove the actual roots \(1,-1\) where they occur.
For every nonexceptional residue root of \(X^h=-\epsilon\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\), its Frobenius orbit of length \(f\) gives exactly \(r\) distinct \(\mathbb Q_\ell\)-irreducible factors.
Their respective residue degrees are \(f\), and their ramification indices are
\[
              L,\quad (\ell-1)L,\quad (\ell-1)\ell L,\quad
                      \ldots,\quad(\ell-1)\ell^{r-2}L,                     \tag{1}
\]
where the list after \(L\) is empty when \(r=1\).
At a residue root \(1\) or \(-1\), which is called exceptional, the same statement holds with \(f=1\) and first index \(L-1\).
This describes all factors with \(\ell\)-adic unit roots after the removal.
For any nontorsion monic irreducible unit factor of degree \(D\) this proves
\[
             D\ge L-1,\qquad D+c_\ell\equiv0\pmod L,                     \tag{2}
\]
where \(c_\ell\) counts the exceptional first factors it contains locally and is at most two.
If \(\epsilon=1\) and \(u,v\) are odd then \(c_\ell=0\) simultaneously at all indicated odd primes.
The divisibilities in (2) then multiply across distinct primes.
An explicit two-prime exclusion below allows arbitrarily large cofactors at each endpoint separately.
The [earlier local degree estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#statement) treat both coefficient choices and give a lower bound also at two.
Here the exact widths in the odd-prime range \(s>r\) give congruences and irreducibility of whole rational factors.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Every negative slope at a unit residue

Normalize valuations by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Modulo \(\ell\) the equation for nonzero roots is \(X^N=-\epsilon\), equivalently \(X^h=-\epsilon\) since \(\ell\) is odd.
For a residue root take its prime-to-\(\ell\) root-of-unity lift \(\zeta\) in the finite unramified field of its residue.
It satisfies \(\zeta^N=-\epsilon\).
Write \(X=\zeta(1+T)\).
After division by an \(\ell\)-adic unit coefficient and, in the case \(\ell\mid a\), by the unit series \(X^u\), the local equation takes the form
\[
               (1+T)^N-1+\ell^r E(T)=0,\qquad
                         E(T)\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q_\ell(\zeta)}[[T]] .
\]
Indeed for \(\ell\mid b\) the perturbation is a unit multiple of \(X^u(1+\epsilon X^{v-u})\), while for \(\ell\mid a\) it is a unit multiple of \(X^{-u}(1+\epsilon X^{v+u})\).
At \(X=\zeta\) the parenthesized factor is respectively \(1-\zeta^{-2u}\) or \(1-\zeta^{2u}\).
These values are units unless \(\zeta^{2u}=1\): reduction is injective on roots of unity of order prime to \(\ell\).
Since also \(\zeta^{2N}=1\) and \(\gcd(u,N)=1\), only \(\zeta=\pm1\) can be exceptional.
At such a \(\zeta\), \(E(0)=0\) exactly, and \(E'(0)\) is a unit since the other endpoint exponent is congruent to \(2u\) or \(-2u\) modulo \(\ell\).
After division by \(T\) the new constant thus has valuation \(r\), because \(s>r\).
This also proves simplicity of each actual exceptional root. [Translated centers at both coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#centers)

Let \(e=1\) at an exceptional center, \(e=0\) elsewhere, and divide the displayed series by \(T^e\).
Its constant has valuation \(r\).
For \(1\le j\le\ell^s\) one has
\[
                 v_\ell\binom Nj=s-v_\ell(j).
\]
In fact \(j\binom Nj=N\binom{N-1}{j-1}\), and in degrees below \(\ell^s\) the latter binomial coefficients are congruent to the coefficients of \((1+T)^{-1}\) modulo \(\ell\).
The perturbation has all coefficients divisible by \(\ell^r\).
Consequently the consecutive vertices on negative-slope edges are
\[
      (0,r),\qquad
      \bigl(\ell^{s-r+k}-e,\ r-k\bigr)\quad(1\le k\le r).
\]
Each indicated endpoint has valuation strictly below \(r\), except for the initial constant.
At intermediate indices before the next endpoint, the binomial coefficient has valuation at least the ordinate of the preceding endpoint, and the perturbation respects that bound.
The successive horizontal lengths increase, and all coefficients after the last endpoint are integral, so no other negative-slope edges occur.
Multiplication by the integral series \((1+T)^u\) or its inverse has no effect on these edges.
Indeed for positive valuation parameter \(t\), the minimum of \(v_\ell(c_j)+jt\) for either unit series is zero and these weighted minima are additive on products.
For polynomials this last fact follows by taking the smallest index attaining the minimum in each factor; the same argument holds for convergent integral series at \(t>0\).

Each edge has vertical drop one.
Its horizontal length \(w\) counts the roots in this residue class with \(v_\ell(X/\zeta-1)=1/w\), including multiplicity. [Newton polygon root count](Yin_C_G0345_v238.md#ramification)
The field of any such root over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) contains \(\zeta\), by Hensel lifting the prime-to-\(\ell\) residue root of unity inside that field.
Over the unramified field \(\mathbb Q_\ell(\zeta)\) its ramification index is a multiple of \(w\).
Its conjugates over this field remain in the same residue class with that valuation, of which there are exactly \(w\).
Thus it has degree and ramification index exactly \(w\) there.
The \(f\) conjugate residue classes give a single irreducible factor of degree \(fw\) over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), with residue degree \(f\).
Taking the differences of the consecutive abscissae proves (1), its exceptional version, and hence (2) by summing the degrees of the selected local factors.
For \(\epsilon=1\) and \(N\) even neither sign center solves \(X^N=-1\) at an odd prime.

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## From the local factors to rational irreducibility

For \(r=h=1\) the only unit center is \(\xi=-\epsilon\), and after removing \(X-\xi\) its unique local factor has degree \(\ell^s-1\).
When \(\ell\mid b\) the leading and constant coefficients are \(\ell\)-adic units, so this accounts for the whole quotient.
It is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), hence over \(\mathbb Q\).

When \(\ell\mid a\), the original polynomial at zero has Newton polygon with vertices \((0,1),(u,0),(v,0),(u+v,1)\).
The two nonhorizontal edges each have width \(u\) and height one.
Each therefore contributes a single irreducible local factor of degree \(u\), by the value-group argument, one with roots of positive valuation and the other of negative valuation.
After removal of \(X-\xi\) the three local factors have degrees \(u,\ell^s-1,u\).
In any proper rational factorization, one rational irreducible factor would omit the unique local factor at the unit center.
It would have degree \(d'\le2u\) and a root \(\kappa\) nonunit at \(\ell\).
Thus at least one of its primitive integer leading and constant coefficients is divisible by \(\ell\), so \(d'h(\kappa)\ge\log\ell\) for absolute logarithmic height \(h(\kappa)\).

Solving the tetranomial for \(\kappa^v\) gives
\[
           \kappa^v=-\epsilon\,\frac{a+b\kappa^u}{b+a\kappa^u}.
\]
The denominator cannot vanish since \(a^2\ne b^2\).
At finite places the two linear forms in projective coordinates increase the maximum norm by at most a factor one, and at archimedean places by at most \(|a|+|b|\).
Therefore \((v-u)h(\kappa)\le\log(|a|+|b|)\).
Together with \(d'\le2u\) this contradicts the inequality in the second family.
The rational quotient is primitive by Gauss's lemma and has leading coefficient of absolute value \(|a|\ge2\).
An irreducible polynomial of this form has no algebraic-integer roots.
Taking a positive power of a nontorsion unit preserves that property, proving the claims at common dilations.
An integral divisor with multiplicative measure strictly between one and two would have a monic unit factor of positive logarithmic measure after omitting powers of \(X\).

## Two simultaneous congruences with large remaining cofactors

For consecutive magnitudes \(q,q+1\) and \(q\to\infty\), suppose a primitive nontorsion unit factor exists, and let \(J\) denote its number of exterior roots and \(D\) its degree.
Uniformly along any such sequence, the [individual near-pole degree formula](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement) gives \(J\le u\), \(v/u\to\infty\), and
\[
          \frac{N}{D}=(1+o(1))\frac{u}{J}
                \frac{\log(q+1)}{\log(1+1/q)}
              =(1+o(1))\frac uJ q\log q
\]
for both endpoints \(N=v\pm u\).
Thus (2) gives \(N/L\ge(1-1/L)(1-o(1))(u/J)q\log q\), even when \(\ell,r,s\) vary with the input.
When \(r=1\) the left side is precisely the cofactor prime to \(\ell\).
The general degree congruence also distinguishes cases using divisibility at several odd coefficient primes.

For example, set \(u=3\), \(\epsilon=1\), and choose any distinct primes \(\ell_1,\ell_2\ge5\).
Choose \(q\) with \(\ell_1\parallel q\), \(\ell_2\parallel q+1\), possible at arbitrarily large \(q\) by the Chinese remainder theorem modulo their squares.
Take arbitrary \(s_1,s_2\ge2\) and write \(Q=\ell_1^{s_1}\ell_2^{s_2}\).
There exists \(Q\le v<19Q\) with
\[
          v\equiv1\pmod6,\qquad v_{\ell_1}(v-3)=s_1,\qquad
                                      v_{\ell_2}(v+3)=s_2 .
\]
Indeed take a solution of the lower divisibilities and \(v\equiv1\pmod6\) in \([Q,7Q)\) by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Among it and its translates by \(6Q,12Q\), each unwanted extra prime power excludes at most one, since \(\ell_i\ge5\).
The exponents are coprime and both odd, so (2) at the two endpoints forces \(Q\mid D\) and \(D>v/19\).
This contradicts the preceding degree formula at all sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly in the primes, their exponents, the signs of the two coefficients and common dilations.
The respective remaining cofactors \((v-3)/\ell_1^{s_1}\) and \((v+3)/\ell_2^{s_2}\) can both be arbitrarily large compared with \(q\), by choosing the powers after \(q\).
This exclusion thus uses their simultaneous divisibility even with unrestricted other prime factors, in addition to the single-prime [period bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#statement).
These families supply no sequence of positive measures tending to zero.
