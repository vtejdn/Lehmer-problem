# A computable growing width with uniform resultant estimates for the unrestricted prime-pair test

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative accuracy for every seed admitted by the test

Fix the multiplicative threshold \(Q=(1091/1000)^{1/3}\).
There is a computable positive integer width \(b(p)\to\infty\) on the primes with the following property.
For each sufficiently large prime \(p\), simultaneously for every prime \(p<q\le2p\), consider all ordered pairs of reciprocal nontorsion unit minima \(P_1,P_2\) of degrees at most \(b(p)\) and measures \(M(P_i)\le Q^3\).
The two powered lists at exponents \(p,q\) have full degree and are disjoint.
To each pair attach an explicitly computable positive integer \(W\) and a positive real number \(\Gamma\), defined below.
They satisfy
\[
             (1-1/b(p))\Gamma\ \le\ \log W\
                           \le (1+1/b(p))\Gamma .                       \tag{1}
\]
Every row \((U,V)\) passing the [original two-prime test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width and \(H<Q^{p+q}\) has \(W\mid|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\) for **every** choice of one of its qualifying factors at each prime.
Consequently every such row satisfies
\[
       (\deg U+\deg V)\log H\ \ge\ (1-1/b(p))\Gamma                      \tag{2}
\]
for each of those choices, including when the same factor occurs twice.
The existence of arbitrarily large \(p\) with unbounded numbers of rejected partners at this particular computable width is equivalent to \(\lambda\ge\log Q\), where \(\lambda\) is the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.

<a id="divisors"></a>
## The two integers and their fixed-seed limits

For roots \(a_i\) of \(P_1\) and \(c_j\) of \(P_2\), put \(x_i=\log|a_i|\), \(y_j=\log|c_j|\), and \(F=\sum_{ij}\max(p x_i,q y_j)\).
When \(P_1\ne P_2\), take \(W\) to be the [fourth power of the powered resultant divided by its common divisor with the correction](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) and put \(\Gamma=4F\).
When \(P_1=P_2=P\), take the numerator \(|B|^4|D|^2\) of the [single-seed divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#statement) and divide it by its gcd with the positive integer correction there.
Put \(\Gamma=4F-2(q-p)\log M(P)\) in this case.
Both integers divide the ordinary row resultant under their respective contact hypotheses.

For each fixed pair of seeds all sufficiently large distinct primes have full and disjoint powered lists by the [fixed-seed index estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#gcd).
For different seeds that result gives \(\log W=\Gamma+o_{P_1,P_2}(p+q)\) on the whole dyadic interval.
For one seed the [estimate of the gcd loss](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#rate) and the estimates of the logarithms of its numerator factors give the same equality.
The lower bound \(F\ge\max\{(\deg P_2)p\log M(P_1),(\deg P_1)q\log M(P_2)\}\) shows that \(\Gamma/(p+q)\) is bounded below by a positive constant for each fixed pair.
For equal seeds use \(F\ge(\deg P)q\log M(P)\) before subtracting the diagonal term.
Thus the ratio \(\log W/\Gamma\) tends to one uniformly in \(p<q\le 2p\) for each fixed pair.

<a id="width"></a>
## Effective pointwise selection without an effective threshold for the unit equation

At a prime \(p\), test integers \(j=2,\ldots,p\).
List the monic reciprocal irreducible integer polynomials of degree at most \(j\) with unit constant coefficient and \(1<M(P)\le Q^3\).
The coefficient of degree \(k\) of any candidate of degree \(d\) has absolute value at most \(\binom{d}{k}Q^3\) by expansion in its roots.
The list is therefore finite and can be obtained by integer polynomial factorization and comparison of positive real algebraic numbers.
For every ordered pair in the list and every prime \(p<q\le2p\), require the two full degrees and disjointness, and test
\[
                   \exp(\Gamma)^{j-1}\le W^j\le \exp(\Gamma)^{j+1}.       \tag{3}
\]
All quantities in (3) are real algebraic numbers computable exactly.
In particular \(\exp F=\prod_{i,j}\max(|a_i|^p,|c_j|^q)\), including when some absolute values agree.
The single-seed diagonal factors are products over the same labeled roots and are invariant under their simultaneous permutation.
The indices are obtained from the power-basis determinant and the two integer resultants from polynomial arithmetic.
Define \(b(p)\) as the greatest passing \(j\), defaulting to one if no \(j\) passes.

At each fixed \(j\) the list is finite, so the fixed-seed limits and positivity give (3) for all sufficiently large primes \(p\).
This proves \(b(p)\to\infty\); the algorithm tests current prime pairs and needs no numerical onset from the unit-equation theorem.
At the selected \(b(p)\ge2\) the required comparisons (1) hold by construction.
No monotonicity or practical running time is asserted.

For a passing row the [tangency inequalities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) give \(p\log M(P_1)+q\log M(P_2)\le\log H\) and \(\deg P_1+\deg P_2\le b(p)\), with degrees added also when \(P_1=P_2\).
Since \(q\le2p\) and both measures are positive in logarithmic form, each multiplicative measure lies below \(Q^3\).
Thus every available choice occurs in the tested list and its integer \(W\) divides the actual ordinary resultant.
Hadamard's inequality for the Sylvester matrix gives \(\log|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\le(\deg U+\deg V)\log H\), proving (2).
The [bounded-partner criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) applies to any width tending to infinity, so applies to this one with the asserted unchanged gap criterion.

<a id="limitation"></a>
## The comparison with the full width budget for a hypothetical small seed

Suppose a reciprocal unit minimum \(P\) has \(0<h=\log M(P)<\log Q\), and put \(e=\deg P\).
For every sufficiently large distinct prime pair under consideration, use \(P\) at both slots.
The elementary inequality \(\max(u,v)\le\max(0,u)+\max(0,v)\) gives \(F\le e(p+q)h\), whence \(\Gamma\le4e(p+q)h\).
Both slots fit the degree bound \(2e\le b(p)\) eventually.
In fact (1) yields
\[
              \log W<4e(p+q)\log Q\ \le\ 2b(p)(p+q)\log Q
\]
uniformly over every such \(q\), since \((1+1/b(p))h<\log Q\) eventually.
Consequently comparing this guaranteed divisor alone with the Hadamard upper budget \(Q^{2b(p)(p+q)}\) cannot reject even a single sufficiently large partner for this hypothetical choice.
This comparison uses the permitted width; individual rows can have lower degrees or a smaller ordinary resultant.
Thus uniformity in the seed at a suitably selected growing width is available, while an exclusion by coefficients still needs information beyond this scalar comparison.
