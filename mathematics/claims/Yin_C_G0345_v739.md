# Trinomials on reciprocal conjugates at the critical exponent span

<a id="statement"></a>
## Finite bounds for the original conjugates

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic integer of degree \(d\), absolute logarithmic height \(h\) and total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=dh\).
Put
\[
                   e_*=\min_{j\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\alpha^j):\mathbb Q].
\]
Let \(Q\) be any nonzero complex Laurent polynomial with at most three terms.
Remove its lowest monomial power and divide its coefficients by their Euclidean norm.
Write \(W\) for its exponent span and use \(Q\) for this normalized polynomial.
With \(S_t=\{\sigma:|Q(\sigma\alpha)|\le t\}\), counting the \(d\) original embeddings, for \(0<t<1/3\) one has
\[
       \frac{|S_t|}{d}
        \le 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{e_*}}+
          \left(\frac{12(\log\sqrt3+Wh)}{\log(1/(3t))}\right)^{1/3}.    \tag{1}
\]
At \(t=0\) the first term alone suffices.
For a genuine trinomial with exponents \(0<a<b\) and zero, the exact-zero fraction is at most
\(\sqrt{2/[\mathbb Q(\alpha^{\gcd(a,b)}):\mathbb Q]}\).
The coefficients need not be algebraic.

For every sequence of nonzero algebraic integers with \(m\to0^+\), the numbers are eventually reciprocal units by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), and \(e_*\to\infty\).
Consequently at \(Wh=O(1)\), including \(W\asymp d/m\), normalized trinomials take values tending uniformly to zero on only \(o(d)\) conjugates.
The same conclusion holds beyond this scale if \(1+Wh=o(\log(1/t))\) for the chosen thresholds \(t\to0\).
For exactly vanishing values there is no span restriction.
The [earlier factor gap](Yin_C_G0345_v181.md#statement) covers rational trinomials vanishing at the entire orbit.
Estimate (1) concerns approximate vanishing on arbitrary subsets with varying complex coefficients and strengthens the [short-span test](Yin_C_G0345_v738.md#statement) in its dependence on the permitted span.

<a id="incidence"></a>
## Inversion changes a line into a conic

For supports \(0,a,b\), \(0<a<b\), take the distinct points
\[
                       (x,y)=(z^a,z^b),\qquad z=\sigma\alpha .
\]
They form one transitive Galois orbit of size \(D=[\mathbb Q(\alpha^{\gcd(a,b)}):\mathbb Q]\), since the two powers generate exactly that field by Bézout's identity for the exponents.
Each point is represented \(d/D\) times and coordinatewise inversion permutes the points by reciprocity.
View them as the projective rows \([1:x:y]\).

A line with all three coefficients nonzero which contains \(M\ge2\) of the distinct points is defined over the splitting field, since two of its points determine it.
Write it as \(A+Bx+Cy=0\).
Its inverted points lie on \(Axy+By+Cx=0\).
This is an irreducible conic: multiplying by \(A\) rewrites it as \((Ax+B)(Ay+C)=BC\) with \(BC\ne0\).
Every conjugate of the original line therefore meets it in at most two points, by substitution or by Bézout's theorem.
Among the orbit points the conic contains exactly \(M\), since inversion is an involution on the torus.
For a uniform automorphism of the splitting field, each of those \(M\) points lies on the image line with probability \(M/D\).
Hence \(M^2/D\le2\).
This also bounds lines containing at most one point.
For a line with one coefficient zero, its points instead have one fixed value of \(z^j\) for \(j=a,b\), or \(b-a\), with at most a fraction \(1/e_*\) of all embeddings.
A line with two coefficients zero contains none.
Thus every line has fraction at most \(L=\sqrt{2/e_*}\) and every individual projective row has fraction \(1/D\le L\).

<a id="volume"></a>
## From small errors to collinear triples

Write \(v_i=(1,z_i^a,z_i^b)\) and \(u_i=v_i/\|v_i\|_2\) for the \(d\) conjugates.
Over all ordered triples with nonzero determinant, the product of the unnormalized determinants is a nonzero rational algebraic integer, since automorphisms permute the entire list.
The empty product is one.
Hadamard's inequality gives \(-\log|\det(u_i,u_j,u_k)|\ge0\).
Summing these costs over the nonzero triples gives at most
\[
       3d^2\sum_i\log\|v_i\|_2
           \le 3d^3(\log\sqrt3+Wh).
\]
Indeed the integer product of the raw determinants costs no positive logarithmic loss and every row contains the constant entry one.

For \(i\in S_t\) the normalized coefficient functional has absolute value at most \(t\) on \(u_i\).
Make it the last coordinate by a unitary change of basis.
Expanding in that column costs at most \(3t\) for each triple from \(S_t\), since every complementary minor has modulus at most one.
Put \(\delta=|S_t|/d\).
Independently sampling three original rows gives probability at least \(\delta(\delta-1/D)(\delta-L)\) of obtaining three independent rows in \(S_t\) when \(\delta>L\).
For the second draw exclude equal projective rows, and for the third draw exclude the line through the first two.
If \(\delta>2L\) this lower bound is at least \(\delta^3/4\); each such triple costs at least \(\log(1/(3t))\).
The total bound proves (1).
Exact zeros lie on the coefficient line itself.
For a binomial of span \(W>0\) the same proof can append a zero coefficient at any missing exponent in \([0,W]\) when \(W\ge2\).
When \(W=1\), use the two rows \((1,z_i)\) in pairs instead: the nonzero determinants cost at most \(2d^2(\log\sqrt2+h)\), the fraction of nonzero pairs in \(S_t\) is at least \(\delta(\delta-1/d)\), and each costs \(\log(1/(2t))\).
If \(\delta>2L\), then \(\delta^2/2\ge\delta^3/4\), which implies the weaker stated bound with its constants.
A normalized monomial has modulus one.

To verify the uniform escape of all powered degrees, fix any \(j\) and let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha^j)\).
The [norm descent](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#primitive-phase) gives a nontorsion unit \(\gamma=\operatorname{N}_{K/E}\alpha\) whose measure is at most \(m\) and whose degree is at most \([E:\mathbb Q]\).
Indeed \(\gamma^j=(\alpha^j)^{[K:E]}\), giving \(h(\gamma)=[K:E]h\).
Bounded degree and bounded measure give only finitely many monic minimal polynomials, by bounding their coefficients with their elementary symmetric sums.
Their positive measures cannot tend to zero, proving \(e_*\to\infty\) uniformly over the choices of \(j\).

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Four terms in two equal-modulus pairs on the neutral roots

Consider instead four distinct exponents in \([0,W]\) with nonzero coefficients normalized as above.
Suppose they can be paired with equal coefficient moduli within each pair, and with the sum of the two exponents in one pair different from that in the other pair.
For thresholds \(t\downarrow0\) the fraction of all \(d\) embeddings which are neutral and satisfy \(|Q(\sigma\alpha)|\le t\) is
\[
          O\!\left(e_*^{-1}+
              \sqrt{\frac{1+Wh}{\log(1/t)}}\right),                    \tag{2}
\]
with an absolute constant, uniformly for sufficiently small \(t\).
For a Salem input the denominator can equivalently be its number \(d-2\) of neutral conjugates, changing the constant only.
Four coefficients of equal modulus always admit a pairing with different exponent sums by pairing the two smallest and the two largest exponents.
Paired magnitudes with equal sums for their only available pairing leave a symmetric support outside this particular test.

For any power \(\beta=\alpha^j\), of degree \(D_j\), its distinct conjugates \(w_i\) have chordal distances
\[
 \chi(w_i,w_k)=|w_i-w_k|/
       \big(\sqrt{1+|w_i|^2}\sqrt{1+|w_k|^2}\big)\le1.
\]
The integral nonzero discriminant of its monic minimal polynomial gives
\(\sum_{i\ne k}-\log\chi(w_i,w_k)\le D_j(D_j-1)(\log2+2jh)\).
A set of chordal diameter \(0<\epsilon<1\) containing \(N\) of these roots therefore satisfies
\[
                N/D_j\le D_j^{-1}+
                    \sqrt{(\log2+2jh)/\log(1/\epsilon)} .              \tag{3}
\]
The fraction among the original embeddings is the same by the constant powered multiplicity.

At a neutral root write the two pair sums as \(U\) and \(V\), with common coefficient lengths \(A,B>0\) in the two pairs, choosing \(A\ge B\), so \(A\ge1/2\).
If \(\xi,\eta\) are the products of the two unit phases in the respective pairs, then \(U=\xi\overline U\) and \(V=\eta\overline V\).
From \(|U+V|\le t\) obtain \(|(\xi-\eta)\overline U|\le2t\).
Either \(|U|\le\sqrt t\), or \(|\xi-\eta|\le2\sqrt t\).
In the first case a power with nonzero exponent equal to the difference of two exponents in the first pair lies within Euclidean distance \(2\sqrt t\) of a fixed unit point.
In the second case the difference of pair sums of exponents serves as the nonzero exponent, again with distance at most \(2\sqrt t\).
Taking the positive exponent merely conjugates the target if needed, since the root is neutral.
Both exponents are at most \(2W\) in absolute value.
Each disk has chordal diameter at most \(4\sqrt t\).
Applying (3) to the two disks yields (2), also permitting \(Wh\) to grow subject to \(1+Wh=o(\log(1/t))\) for vanishing density.
At exact zero the two disks collapse to points, so at most \(2d/e_*\) neutral embeddings can occur for any span.
