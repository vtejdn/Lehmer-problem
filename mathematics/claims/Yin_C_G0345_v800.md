# Simultaneous independence of the complete exterior set at polynomially large slope

<a id="statement"></a>
## A threshold valid for all exterior branches

Let \(k,l\) be coprime positive integers, \(s=l/k\), and \(P(X)=F(X^k,X^l)\) for the [quadratic parent](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement)
\[
 F(U,V)=A(U)V^2+U^2V+A^*(U),\qquad
 A(U)=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1),\quad A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U).
\]
There are effectively determinable constants \(C,A_0>0\), depending only on \(F\), such that for \(s\ge C(2k)^{A_0}\) **all \(2k\) exterior roots of \(P\) are multiplicatively independent together**.

Let \(S\) be any monic rational irreducible factor of \(P\), \(d=\deg S\), \(H\) its exterior count and
\[
 D_{\rm ab}=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(M(S)):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}].
\]
In this same range there is a fixed \(C_F>0\) such that
\[
 D_{\rm ab}>
 \frac{(l/C_F)^H}{H!\,c_2(H)\{H\log(3d)\}^{\kappa_2(H)}},                \tag{1}
\]
where \(a_H=2(H+1)^2(H+1)!\), \(c_2(H)=(2H^2)^H\exp(64H^2H!\,a_H^{2H})\), and \(\kappa_2(H)=3H a_H^H\).
For \(k\to\infty\), if \(H^2\log(2H)=o(\log\log l)\), then
\[
 \frac{\log D_{\rm ab}}{H\log d}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{\log[\mathbb Q(M(S)):\mathbb Q]}{H\log d}\longrightarrow1 .        \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(k^2\log(2k)=o(\log\log l)\) suffices for (2) simultaneously for every factor, with no bound on its number of exterior roots.
This leaves the actual occurrence of proper factors open.

<a id="heights"></a>
## Height and relation bounds with the tuple length retained

All roots \(z\) are units by monicity and the constant coefficient one.
For \(U=z^k,V=z^l\) one has \(A(U)\ne0\).
Otherwise \(U-U^{-1}=j\) for \(j=3\) or \(4\), and the equation would give the nonunit \(V=-A^*(U)/U^2=-(j+3)(j+4)\).
Write \(M_v=\max(|A(U)|_v,|U^2|_v,|A^*(U)|_v)\) at a place of a field of definition.
For \(|V|_v>1\), the equation gives \(|V|_v\le c_v M_v/|A(U)|_v\), with \(c_v=2\) at infinity and \(c_v=1\) elsewhere.
This right side is at least one at every place.
Bounding each of the three polynomial values by \(\max(1,|U|_v)^4\) times the archimedean coefficient norm where needed, and summing with the usual normalized weights, gives
\[
                (l-4k)h(z)\le\log(2L_F),\qquad
 L_F=\max(1,\|A\|_1,\|A^*\|_1).
\]
The denominator \(A(U)\) contributes zero globally by the product formula.
For \(l\ge8k\) one may take \(C_F=2\log(2L_F)\) in the uniform bound \(h(z)\le C_F/l\).

Apply the [short-relation packing argument](Yin_C_G0345_v798.md#relations) to the entire exterior set with \(t=2k\).
These are \(2k\) distinct roots for sufficiently large \(s\), uniformly in \(k\), by the [polar expansions](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#lifts) and the whole-section count there.
Their joint field degree \(D\) satisfies \(D\le(4k+2l)^t\).
Increase the constant in the slope hypothesis so \(h(z_i)\le1\).
If the roots were dependent, that packing argument gives an exact nonzero integer relation \(\prod_i z_i^{b_i}=1\) with
\[
  B=\max(2,|b_1|,\ldots,|b_t|)\le
                    2D^2\lceil(2+40tD^4)^t\rceil,\qquad
  \log(tB)\ll k^2\log(2l).                                                 \tag{3}
\]
In particular the height and degree costs here are uniform for the full tuple.

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Leading coefficients through order proportional to the short exponent

Use the [pole expansions](Yin_C_G0345_v798.md#series) at \(\beta=\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\), \(j=3,4\).
Their equation \(x=w e^{s g(w)}\) has \(x=\beta^{-s}T\), \(g(0)=0\), \(a=g'(0)\ne0\) and phase \(T=\nu^{-l}\), where \(\nu^k=1\).
For \(f_s(T)=\log(z^k/\beta)\) on this analytic branch write \([T^n]f_s(T)=\beta^{-ns}P_{\beta,n}(s)\).
The residue formula from that proof, expanding the exponential and using \((g^r)'=r g' g^{r-1}\), yields the exact polynomial
\[
          P_{\beta,n}(s)=\sum_{r=1}^{n}
                      \frac{(-ns)^{r-1}}{r!}[w^n]g(w)^r .                \tag{4}
\]
There is a fixed \(C_1\ge1\) with \(|[w^h]g(w)/(aw)|\le C_1^h\) for \(h\ge1\), where the coefficient is taken in the analytic function \(g(w)/(aw)\) with constant coefficient one.
This follows from its convergence on a fixed disk at zero.
Thus \(|[w^n]g(w)^r|\le |a|^r\binom{n-1}{n-r} C_1^{n-r}\), by taking the \(r\)-th power of the geometric series majorant.
For \(r=n\) the summand in (4) is \(a^n(-ns)^{n-1}/n!\).
For \(r=n-j<n\) its absolute value divided by the absolute value of that leading term is at most
\[
 \frac{n!}{(n-j)!}\frac{(C_1/|a|)^j}{(ns)^j}\binom{n-1}{j}
                   \ \le\ \frac{(C_1n/(|a|s))^j}{j!}.
\]
Consequently all the lower powers of \(s\) together have relative size at most \(\exp(C_1 n/(|a|s))-1\).
For every fixed \(C_2>0\), whenever \(1\le n\le C_2 k\) and \(s\) exceeds a sufficiently large constant times \(k\), this is at most \(1/2\).
Uniformly in that range and at both poles,
\[
           \exp(-C_3 k)\le |P_{\beta,n}(s)|\le \exp(C_3k\log(2l)).
                                                                         \tag{5}
\]
For the lower bound use \(n^{n-1}/n!\ge 1/n\) and \(s\ge1\), and for the upper bound use the displayed leading term.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Separating all phases in one relation

At either pole, the [branch radius](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#lifts) gives \(f_s(T)=O(e^{-s\log\beta})\) for \(|T|=1\).
If \(b^{(j)}\) denotes the sum of the \(b_i\) at pole \(\beta_j\), the logarithm of the modulus of the exact relation raised to the \(k\)-th power gives
\[
          |b^{(3)}\log\beta_3+b^{(4)}\log\beta_4|
                                  \ll tB e^{-s\log\beta_3}.
\]
The two units are multiplicatively independent as shown in the [fixed-rank argument](Yin_C_G0345_v798.md#moments).
The [Baker–Wüstholz bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#wus02-u001) for these fixed logarithms gives a lower bound \((2tB)^{-C_4}\) if the form is nonzero, with effective \(C_4\).
Thus \(b^{(3)}=b^{(4)}=0\) if \(s\) exceeds a large constant times \(k^2\log(2l)\).
The entire complex sum of the \(b_i f_{j,s}(T_i)\) is then zero, since it is in \(2\pi i\mathbb Z\) and its modulus is bounded by \(O(tB e^{-s\log\beta_3})<1\).

Put \(Y=2k\log\beta_4\) and \(N_j=\lfloor Y/\log\beta_j\rfloor\).
All \(N_j+1\) are at most \(C_2 k\) for an absolute choice of \(C_2\).
The distinct weights \(n\log\beta_j\), \(1\le n\le N_j+1\), have minimum separation at least \(c(2k)^{-b}\) for effective \(b,c>0\).
For weights at one pole the separation is at least \(\log\beta_3\).
For the two poles use the same Baker–Wüstholz bound with coefficients at most \(C_2k\) and their multiplicative independence.
Let \(Z_{j,n}=\sum_{i\in I_j} b_i T_i^n\), where \(I_j\) lists the roots at that pole.
Each nonzero moment is an algebraic integer of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_k)\) with absolute value at least \((tB)^{1-\varphi(k)}\), since all its conjugates have absolute value at most \(tB\).

If some moment with weight at most \(Y\) is nonzero, let \(\lambda\) be the smallest such weight.
In the zero complex sum its term is \(e^{-s\lambda}P_{\beta_j,n}(s)Z_{j,n}\).
For each pole, the uniform tail of \(f_{j,s}\) beyond \(N_j\) is bounded by
\[
  \frac{2C_5}{1+s}
    \left(\frac{1+s}{c_5}\right)^{N_j+1} e^{-(N_j+1)s\log\beta_j},
\]
using Cauchy's bound at \(R=c_5\beta_j^s/(1+s)\ge2\).
All later listed weights and both tail weights exceed \(\lambda\) by at least \(c(2k)^{-b}\).
Using (5) at the first term and all other listed terms, then (3), gives
\[
 \log |Z_{j,n}|\le C_6 k^2\log(2l)-cs(2k)^{-b},\qquad
 \log |Z_{j,n}|\ge -C_7 k^3\log(2l).
\]
These are incompatible as soon as \(s>C_8(2k)^{b+3}\log(2l)\).
For instance \(A_0=b+5\) and a sufficiently large fixed \(C\) in the statement suffice for all \(k\), since \(l=ks\) and \(s/\log(2ks)\) increases for \(s\ge2\).
This choice also enforces the preceding bounds on \(s\).
Hence every listed moment vanishes.
At each pole the zeroth moment is \(b^{(j)}=0\), and the first \(k-1\) positive moments are among those listed.
There are exactly \(k\) distinct phases at each pole since \(\gcd(k,l)=1\), so the two Vandermonde matrices force every \(b_i=0\).

For effectivity, the two-logarithm input supplies its constant and the degrees and heights of the two fixed units are known.
The analytic disk and coefficient constants above can be bounded from the fixed polynomial and its two simple algebraic roots.
For the function \(g\), choose a rational radius for a disk about \(\beta\) isolating its root of \(A\), with radius less than \(\beta/2\).
On its boundary a positive lower bound for \(|A|\) follows by factoring \(A\) and using the distances to its four known roots.
A small rational bound on \(|w|\) then makes \(|U^2w+A^*(U)w^2|\) smaller than this minimum throughout the boundary.
This bounds \(|g|\) there by \(-\log(1-r/\beta)\) on the resulting branch, with \(r\) the chosen disk radius, and Cauchy on a smaller parameter disk gives an explicit geometric majorant for \(g/(aw)\).
For the \(s\)-dependent disk use the same product lower bound on \(|U-\beta|=r/(1+s)\) and \(|U^{-s}|\le\beta^{-s}\exp(2r/\beta)\) there.
These estimates determine admissible positive branch-radius constants uniformly by the contour argument of the [analytic branch proof](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#lifts).
The other bounds use only arithmetic estimates, the displayed packing inequality and Voutier's explicit corollary.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## The full tuple over the maximal abelian field

The unit roots are nontorsion by the [torsion exclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#torsion), so every factor has \(H\ge1\).
For its ordered tuple of all \(H\) exterior roots let \(\delta\) be the joint degree over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) after adjoining them.
By the complete independence just proved, [Delsinne's relative product inequality](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) gives
\[
           (l/C_F)^H < c_2(H)\delta\{\log(3\delta)\}^{\kappa_2(H)} .
\]
In a normal field containing the roots, the stabilizer of \(M(S)\) is the setwise stabilizer of its \(H\) exterior roots, by uniqueness of their product's maximal modulus among all \(H\)-element subsets.
This equality of stabilizers persists in the subgroup acting over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
Each image of the exterior set admits just \(H!\) different orders, so \(\delta\le H!D_{\rm ab}\).
Also \(\delta\le d^H\), giving (1).

The printed constants give \(\log c_2(H),\kappa_2(H)\le\exp(C_9 H^2\log(2H))\), as follows directly from \(H!\le H^H\).
If \(H^2\log(2H)=o(\log\log l)\), these bounds are \((\log l)^{o(1)}\).
Since \(d\le 4k+2l\le 6l\), we have \(\log(H\log(3d))=O(\log\log l)\) in this range.
The logarithms of the denominator of (1) and of \(C_F^H\) are therefore \(o(H\log l)\).
It follows that \(\log D_{\rm ab}\ge(1-o(1))H\log l\).
The upper bounds \(D_{\rm ab}\le[\mathbb Q(M(S)):\mathbb Q]\le\binom dH\le d^H\) and \(\log d\le\log(6l)\) finish (2).
Finally \(H\le2k\) for every factor, and \(k^2\log(2k)=o(\log\log l)\) implies the required polynomial slope and bounds uniformly in \(H\).
