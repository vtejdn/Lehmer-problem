# A uniform bound for genuine sparse rows at a unit and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## Term count, exponent and the degree of the smallest power field

For any integer \(s\ge2\) and rational \(\delta>0\) there are effective constants \(n_0(s,\delta)\) and \(C(s,\delta)>0\) with the following property.
Let \(\gamma\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(n>n_0(s,\delta)\) be an integer, and let \(b\ge0\) be an integer width.
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be nonzero of degree at most \(b\) with at most \(s\) nonzero terms and one-norm \(H\).
Suppose that for some sign \(\varepsilon\) the graph
\[
                A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n})
\]
vanishes at both \(\gamma,\gamma^2\) and \(A(\gamma^n)\ne0\).
Let \(e\) be the minimum of \([\mathbb Q(\gamma^t):\mathbb Q]\) over positive integers \(t\).
Then
\[
                    \max(n,e)\le C(s,\delta)(1+\log H)^{s-1+\delta}.      \tag{1}
\]
The width, the size of the original degree and the positions of the terms are unrestricted.
The assertion uses simple graph zeros and applies to composite \(n\).
For the original two-sign test it applies separately to each genuine row.

For a Salem number, or any unit whose every positive power preserves its degree \(d\), one has \(e=d\).
At \(H\le\exp(Kn)\) with fixed \(K>0\), no row of at most \(s\) terms can work along \(d\to\infty,\ n\to\infty,\ n=O(d^\theta)\) for \(0<\theta<1/(s-1)\).
In particular when \(\log n=o(\log d)\), the number of occupied positions required tends to infinity, uniformly in such units of degree \(d\).
This includes the primes of order \((\log d)^2\) used in the narrow-width test.
The [five-term real-root estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v479.md#statement) has a larger exponent range for five positions and permits the numerator to vanish.
For a general unit at any width, the [all-power norm representative](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) and [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) give
\[
 m(\gamma)>
 \frac{2}{\bigl(\log(3C(s,\delta))+(s-1+\delta)\log(1+\log H)\bigr)^3}, \tag{2}
\]
on increasing \(C(s,\delta)\) to at least one.
This lower bound depends on the coefficient norm.

<a id="block"></a>
## Choosing terms without a simultaneous vanishing subcollection

Write \(j_i\) for the distinct occupied positions and \(a_i\) for their coefficients.
Label the two monomials arising from \(j_i\) by the vectors \(+e_i,-e_i\) of the standard lattice, with coefficients \(a_i,\varepsilon a_i\) and exponents \(q(+e_i)=nj_i,\ q(-e_i)=nb+1-nj_i\).
All these exponents are distinct because the two signs of the label give different residues modulo \(n\).
Partition the labels into inclusion-minimal nonempty sets \(I\) whose terms sum to zero at both \(\gamma,\gamma^2\), successively removing such a set from the remaining two zero sums.
At least one set has nonzero sum of its positive-label terms at \(\gamma\), since their total is \(A(\gamma^n)\ne0\).
Fix this set.
It contains both kinds of label, with both partial sums nonzero at \(\gamma\).
It has at least three terms: two proportional zero equations at \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma^2\) for two distinct monomials would force their powers of \(\gamma\) to agree.

Take a pivot \(\nu_0\in I\) of negative label and let \(L\) be the lattice generated by the differences \(\nu-\nu_0\).
Its rank is at most \(s\).
There is a surjective weight \(W:L\to\mathbb Z\) and an exponent map \(E:L\to\mathbb Z\) given by
\[
              W(v)=\tfrac12\sum_i v_i,\qquad
              E(v)=n\sum_i j_i v_i-(nb+1)W(v).
\]
The weights on the support differences are zero or one, and \(E(\nu-\nu_0)=q(\nu)-q(\nu_0)\).
In particular
\[
                              E(v)\equiv-W(v)\pmod n .                     \tag{3}
\]
Write \(g>0\) for the positive generator of \(E(L)\).
A mixed difference has weight one, hence \(\gcd(g,n)=1\).
Two of the at least three labels have the same kind of sign; their exponent difference is a nonzero multiple of \(n\).
Consequently the range of \(E(\nu-\nu_0)/g\) on this block has length at least \(n\).

<a id="components"></a>
## The two equations on a quotient torus

We also work with free lattice quotients of \(L\) by primitive relations on which both \(E,W\) vanish.
Use the same names for the induced maps.
The projected differences still generate the whole lattice, retain their two weights, and are all distinct because their actual exponents \(E(\nu-\nu_0)\) are distinct.
In a basis of the current rank-\(r\) lattice the torus point of interest has coordinates \(\gamma^{E(v_i)}\).
Let \(F\) denote the Laurent sum of the selected terms after division by the pivot monomial.
The point satisfies \(F=F^{[2]}=0\), where \(F^{[2]}(Y)=F(Y_1^2,\ldots,Y_r^2)\).
The minimality of the simultaneous zero sums and the two nonzero partial sums at \(\gamma\) survive each quotient.

Choose one basis element of weight one and the others in the kernel of \(W\).
In these coordinates \(F=P(Z)+TQ(Z)\), where both \(P,Q\) have nonzero values at the point.
Every irreducible component of the common zero set through this point has codimension at least two.
Indeed the Laurent ring over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) is factorial.
A common irreducible divisor independent of \(T\) would divide both \(P,Q\), so could not vanish at the point.
A common divisor depending on \(T\) would give \(f(Z^2)=f(Z)^2\) for \(f=-P/Q\) by division over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(Z)\).
This identity forces \(f\) to be a Laurent monomial of coefficient one.
To see this choose an integer vector \(v\) separating every distinct member of the finite set of all support differences of \(P,Q\), including those between the two supports.
Neither polynomial loses any terms under \(Z_i=Y^{v_i}\).
The resulting one-variable function has the same squaring identity and hence is \(Y^h\) by [comparison of its leading term and first correction](Yin_C_G0345_v471.md#proof).
All paired terms in the specialized numerator and denominator differ by \(h\), so separation forces a common vector difference and matching coefficients in the original polynomials (with the numerator sign included).
The case of constant \(f\) gives \(f=1\) as well.
At the point we would then have equality between \(T\) and that monomial.
Since \(\gamma\) is nontorsion, this gives an exact exponent relation of weight one, impossible by (3).

Nor can any positive-dimensional coset in this zero set contain the point.
On translating its underlying subtorus to that point and grouping monomials by their restricted characters, each group's coefficient sum for \(F\) must vanish.
For \(F^{[2]}\) the groups are the same because the character lattice is torsion-free, so each group also has zero coefficient sum at the squared point.
There are at least two groups: if all terms had the same restriction, their differences would annihilate the subtorus even though those differences span the character lattice.
This contradicts the chosen minimality.
In rank one the point is already impossible: the surjective \(W\) is a lattice isomorphism, whereas at least three distinct projected terms have just two weights.

<a id="descent"></a>
## Iterating the bounded-relation alternative without losing the weight

Apply [Amoroso, Sombra and Zannier, Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U001.md#intersection) in rank \(r\ge2\).
Write \(u_i=E(v_i)/g\) in the current basis, so the coordinate vector \(u\) is primitive.
The parameter on its one-dimensional subtorus at our point is the nontorsion number \(\gamma^g\).
The preceding component and coset arguments put the point in the required open part of a component of codimension at least two.
The defining equations have rational coefficients; after monomial clearing their degrees are bounded by the current support and their logarithmic heights by \(\log H+O_s(1)\), with extra constants allowed for each fixed quotient.
Take \(\xi=\delta/(s-1+\delta)\) as the source's parameter in \((0,1)\).
Either
\[
       \|u\|_\infty\le C_1(1+\log H)^{(r-1)/(1-\xi)}
                         \le C_1(1+\log H)^{s-1+\delta},                  \tag{4}
\]
or a nonzero bounded character relation \(v\) satisfies \(E(v)=0\).
The bound on the character in the second alternative depends only on the current lattice presentation, \(s,\delta\), independently of the coefficients' heights, \(n\) and the unit.
In the torsion equation the constant is one since a nontorsion number has no nontrivial torsion power.

One can choose \(n_0\) before any coefficient or exponent data are supplied.
There are finitely many initial signed blocks at term count at most \(s\) and effective bases for their difference lattices.
At each such presentation the source gives an effective bound and hence a finite list of primitive relations to consider, dividing by their contents if necessary.
Choose an integral basis of each quotient by a primitive vector of weight zero, retain the projected support and choose the basis adapted to its primitive weight.
Repeat for at most \(s-1\) steps, since each quotient loses a dimension.
This finite construction depends only on \(s,\delta\).
Take \(n_0\ge2\) larger than every absolute weight of every bounded primitive relation at every presentation in it.
For any actual instance, (3) then forces each bounded relation in the second alternative to have weight zero.
Both \(W\) and \(E\) descend, with the image of \(E\) still exactly \(g\mathbb Z\).
Thus the actual descent follows this finite tree until (4) holds, since the rank-one endpoint cannot occur.

Every projected support difference has bounded coordinates throughout that tree.
At a node satisfying (4) the true normalized exponents \(E(\nu-\nu_0)/g\) consequently have range at most \(C_2(s,\delta)(1+\log H)^{s-1+\delta}\).
This range was at least \(n\).
It also bounds the degree of \(\gamma^g\): inserting \(Y_i=T^{u_i}\) into \(F\) and clearing its lowest monomial gives a nonzero integral polynomial of exactly this degree range vanishing at \(\gamma^g\).
There is no cancellation since the specialized exponents are distinct.
As \(e\le[\mathbb Q(\gamma^g):\mathbb Q]\), this proves (1).
The stated consequences for growing degrees follow by taking \(\delta\) small enough that \(\theta(s-1+\delta)<1\).
For (2) the all-power representative has the same positive measure as \(\gamma\) and degree \(e\ge2\), so its degree estimate supplies the displayed lower bound.

<a id="span"></a>
## Normalized support when the two zero sums are minimal together

If the full list of signed terms has no proper subcollection vanishing at both nodes, the selected block above contains every term.
Let \(k\) be the gcd of the differences of positions of the terms of \(A\).
There are at least two such positions, and \(g\) divides \(nk\) by the differences between positive labels.
Since \(\gcd(g,n)=1\), one has \(g\mid k\).
The range of normalized exponents for the positive labels is \(n(\max j_i-\min j_i)/g\).
Consequently the normalized span of the row satisfies
\[
             \frac{\max j_i-\min j_i}{k}
                    \le \frac{C(s,\delta)(1+\log H)^{s-1+\delta}}{n}.
\]
With proper simultaneous zero sums the degree and exponent bounds in (1) still apply, since one mixed block with a nonzero positive part suffices.
In particular degree inflation over a small power field is a remaining possibility for bounded term count.
The [positive density constructions](Yin_C_G0345_v477.md#intervals) actually use such inflations.

A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin proposed applying the quantitative torus intersection theorem to minimal simultaneous zero sums.
The coordinating agent selected a mixed block from the nonzero numerator and carried its weight through the bounded restrictions to exclude all codimension-one components at the point.
