# Energy outside long intervals for quotients at degree-preserving powers

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive cost outside a moving observation set

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\), and put \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
For an integral Laurent polynomial \(A\) indivisible by \(P\), write \(A/P=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}h_j X^j\) on the circle.
Consider a sequence with \(m\to0\) and \(m\sum_j |h_j|^2\) bounded.
Suppose every positive power of each \(\alpha\) has the same degree as \(\alpha\), as happens at [strict degree records](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
Put \(b= r_0^2+2\log(2r_0)-4r_0+2\) and \(c=r_0^2\), with \(r_0\) as in the [angular spacing bound](Yin_C_G0345_v663.md#statement).

For any interval \(I\) of at most \(o(\log(1/m)/m)\) consecutive integers, with arbitrary location,
\[
                   \liminf m\sum_{j\notin I}|h_j|^2\ \ge\ b/2 .       \tag{1}
\]
The lower bound is \(c/2\) if each numerator has width below the degree of \(P\).
Both bounds also hold for a union of \(o(\log(1/m))\) intervals having total cardinality \(o(\log(1/m)/m)\), independently of their separation.
For the unrestricted bound, the coefficients may be replaced throughout by their distances to the nearest integers, using the integral representative obtained by [coefficient rounding](Yin_C_G0345_v653.md#statement).
The proof gives a criterion for more general observation sets and only uses degree preservation at one chosen power per input.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Distinct progressions stay separated

Choose an integer \(k\ge2\) preserving the degree and put \(t=km\).
By the [section formula](Yin_C_G0345_v664.md#sections), the coefficient sequence at positions \(kj+r\), for \(j\in\mathbb Z\), is that of \(B_r/P_k\), where \(P_k\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^k\).
At \(\beta=\alpha^k\), inversion of the finite Fourier transform in that formula gives
\[
                B_r(\beta)=\alpha^{-r}(AD)(\alpha)/k,\qquad
                D=P_k(X^k)/P .
\]
Indeed among the \(k\) preimages of \(\beta\) only \(\alpha\) is a root of \(P\).
The factor \((AD)(\alpha)\) is nonzero.
Thus \(B_r-B_s\) is indivisible by \(P_k\) whenever \(0\le r<s<k\), since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
If \(A\) is short, translate its least exponent to zero and translate the observation set accordingly.
Then \(\deg(AD)< k\deg P_k\) and every \(B_r-B_s\) is short.

In the real Hilbert space of square-summable two-sided sequences put \(v_r=(\sqrt t\,h_{kj+r})_j\).
If \(t\to0\), [the two energy bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v663.md#statement) give
\[
            \min_{r<s}\|v_r-v_s\|^2\ge \lambda-o(1),\qquad
            \frac1k\sum_r\|v_r\|^2=m\sum_j |h_j|^2\le C,              \tag{2}
\]
where \(\lambda=b\) in general and \(\lambda=c\) for short numerators.
The error is uniform over pairs, by choosing a pair of least distance at each input.
For short numerators the translated observation set is used from this point onward.

<a id="packing"></a>
## Variance on progressions with nearby observed coefficients

Suppose \(k\to\infty\), \(t\to0\), and a finite set \(J\subset\mathbb Z\) of cardinality \(n=o(\log k)\) contains every \(\lfloor j/k\rfloor\) for \(j\) in the observation set.
Write \(p_r\) for the restriction of \(v_r\) to \(J\) and \(w_r\) for the restriction to its complement.
Fix \(R>0\) and \(\delta>0\) with \(\delta^2<\lambda\).
By (2) at most \(kC/R^2\) of the \(p_r\) have norm greater than \(R\).
Cover the radius-\(R\) ball in \(\mathbb R^n\) by at most \(M=(1+4R/\delta)^n\) balls of radius \(\delta/2\).
This follows by taking a maximal \(\delta/2\)-separated set and comparing the volumes of the disjoint radius-\(\delta/4\) balls with the volume of the radius-\((R+\delta/4)\) ball.
Assign each retained point to a covering ball that contains it.
Within a group of \(a\) assigned rows, all squared distances between distinct \(w_r\) are at least \(\lambda-\delta^2-o(1)\), by orthogonality in (2).
The identity for the sum of squared pairwise distances gives
\[
                \sum_{\rm group}\|w_r\|^2
                    \ \ge\ \tfrac12(a-1)(\lambda-\delta^2-o(1)).
\]
As \(M=o(k)\), summing over occupied groups and dividing by \(k\) gives a lower limit of at least \((1-C/R^2)(\lambda-\delta^2)/2\) for the mean squared norm on the complement of \(J\), taking \(R^2>C\).
Every such coefficient is outside the observation set.
Let \(R\to\infty\) and \(\delta\to0\) to obtain (1) with the stated value of \(\lambda/2\).

<a id="scale"></a>
## Selecting the power for a union of intervals

Write \(L=\log(1/m)\), \(W\) for the sum of the interval cardinalities and \(s\) for their number.
Suppose \(W=o(L/m)\) and \(s=o(L)\).
Take \(u=\max\{L^{-1/2},\sqrt{mW/L}\}\) and \(k=\lfloor u/m\rfloor\).
Then \(km\to0\), \(k\to\infty\), \(\log k\sim L\) and \(W/k=o(L)\).
Each interval of cardinality \(w\) meets at most \(w/k+2\) different quotient indices \(\lfloor j/k\rfloor\).
Their union thus uses at most \(W/k+2s=o(\log k)\) such indices, independently of where the intervals lie.
This proves the claims for moving intervals and their unions.
In the rounded case the unrounded coefficients are real and tend to zero at both ends for each fixed input.
Only finitely many coefficients need an integral correction, which is addition of an integral Laurent multiple of \(P\) to the numerator and preserves its nonzero residue class.
In particular the boundedness hypothesis there concerns the rounded energy itself.
These tail estimates still require another argument to exclude a shrinking sequence with broadly distributed energy.
