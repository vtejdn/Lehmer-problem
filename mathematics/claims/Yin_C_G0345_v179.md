# Common roots of the two end blocks of sparse multiples

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite-scale theorem for both orientations

Fix an integer \(L\ge2\).
For a nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) of coefficient length \(\|P\|_1\le L\), remove its common monomial, let \(D=\deg P\), and write \(P^*(X)=X^DP(1/X)\).
There is a constant \(K_L>1\) with the following property.
If \(R\ge2\) and \(D>K_L R^{K_L}\), the support of \(P\) splits into at least two consecutive blocks.
Let \(B\) be the highest block of \(P\) translated to have lowest exponent zero, and \(E\) the highest block of \(P^*\) obtained by reversing the lowest block of \(P\) and making the same translation.
The choice of blocks depends on \(P,L,R\) alone.
Their maximum width \(W=\max(\deg B,\deg E)\) is at most \(K_L R^{K_L}\).
There is a common radius \(r\le e^{-R}\) with the following properties.

* Every common zero \(\alpha\) of \(P,P^*\) with \(\log|\alpha|\ge1/R\) lies within \(r/2\) of a unique common root \(\beta\) of \(B,E\) satisfying \(\log|\beta|\ge1/(2R)\).
  A positive real \(\alpha\) has a positive real \(\beta>1\).
  The presence of even one such \(\alpha\) forces both end blocks to have coefficient length at least three.
* For each common root \(\beta\) satisfying \(\log|\beta|\ge1/(2R)\), the open disk of radius \(r\) centered at \(\beta\) contains exactly \(\operatorname{ord}_\beta B\) zeros of \(P\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_\beta E\) zeros of \(P^*\), counted separately with multiplicities.
  The disks are pairwise disjoint.
  Thus the number of zeros of reciprocal divisors in any one disk, counted in the greatest common divisor of \(P,P^*\), is at most the smaller of the two multiplicities.

<a id="scales"></a>
## Choosing a gap from the actual support

Take \(c_L=10\log(4L+4)\), \(A_L=1000(1+c_L)\), and put
\[
       U_0=1,\qquad U_{j+1}=A_L R^2(1+LU_j)^4,\qquad 0\le j<L .
\]
The assumption on \(D\), on increasing \(K_L\) in terms of \(L\) only, gives \(D>(L-1)U_L\).
Indeed each \(U_j\) is a polynomial in \(R\) with coefficients depending only on \(L,j\).
The at most \(L-1\) successive gaps between nonzero coefficients sum to \(D\), so at least one exceeds \(U_L\).
Among the \(L\) disjoint intervals \((U_j,U_{j+1}]\) choose one containing none of these gaps.
Cut at every gap larger than \(U_{j+1}\).
The end blocks are disjoint, each has width at most \(LU_j\), and their respective gaps to the nearest remaining term both exceed \(G=U_{j+1}\).
In particular \(W\le LU_j\), and \(G\ge A_L R^2(1+W)^4\).
Write \(P=X^sB+H\) with \(s-\deg H>G\).
For \(|z|\ge1\) one has \(|z^{-s}H(z)|\le L|z|^{-G}\).
The same bound holds in the orientation with \(E,P^*\).

<a id="disks"></a>
## Root separation and the two disk counts

The roots of \(B,E\) have modulus at most \(L+1\) by the coefficient root bound, since each nonzero leading coefficient is an integer and each coefficient length is at most \(L\).
Any two distinct roots in their union are separated by at least
\[
                  \Delta=\exp[-c_L(1+W)^2].
\]
In fact the primitive squarefree part of \(BE\) has degree \(n\le2W\) and leading coefficient of absolute value at most \(L^2\), by factorization over the integers.
For \(n\ge2\) its nonzero integer discriminant bounds one root difference below by \( (L^2)^{-(n-1)}(2L+2)^{-[n(n-1)/2-1]}\), since all other differences have modulus at most \(2L+2\).
This implies the displayed estimate, and for \(n\le1\) the separation is vacuous.

Set \(r=\Delta e^{-3R-5}\).
The choice of \(G\) gives
\[
             L e^{-G/(3R)} < (r/2)^{\max(1,W)} .
\]
If \(\alpha\) is as in the first assertion, the end-block estimates give \(|B(\alpha)|,|E(\alpha)|\le L e^{-G/R}\).
Neither polynomial can be constant, since a nonzero integer has absolute value at least one.
The leading coefficient of each has absolute value at least one, so the product of its root distances places at least one of its roots within \(r/2\) of \(\alpha\).
The separation \(\Delta>2r\) identifies these two roots and gives uniqueness.
Since \(r\le e^{-3R-5}\), the shared root has modulus at least \(e^{1/R}-r/2> e^{1/(2R)}\).
Complex conjugation fixes this root when \(\alpha>1\), and its proximity to \(\alpha\) makes it positive.
A nonconstant block with nonzero constant coefficient and length at most two is a signed binomial with both endpoint coefficients of absolute value one, whose roots all have modulus one.
This proves the length assertion.

For a common root \(\beta\) with \(|\beta|\ge e^{1/(2R)}\), every \(z\) on its disk boundary has \(|z|\ge e^{1/(2R)}-r>e^{1/(3R)}\).
All its distances to roots of \(B\) are at least \(r\), by \(\Delta>2r\), hence \(|B(z)|\ge r^{\deg B}\).
The inequality above gives \(|z^{-s}H(z)|<|B(z)|\).
Rouché's theorem and the disjointness from zero show that \(P\) has exactly the multiplicity of \(\beta\) in \(B\) inside the disk.
The same argument for \(P^*,E\) gives the second count, with no assumption that the two polynomials share the zeros they contribute there.

<a id="factors"></a>
## Consequences for small Salem numbers and other reciprocal units

Suppose \(P\) has a Salem factor with positive root \(\tau=e^t\) of degree \(d\), where \(\|P\|_1\le L\).
For every fixed \(A\ge1\), all sufficiently small \(t\) admit end blocks with widths \(O_{L,A}(t^{-A K_L})\) and a common positive real root \(\beta>1\) satisfying
\[
                     |\tau-\beta|\le \exp(-t^{-A}).
\]
Indeed \(D\ge d\), and the [Dobrowolski lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(t\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\) at large \(d\).
Bounded degree gives only finitely many units of bounded measure by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
Thus \(d\) grows faster than any fixed power of \(1/t\) along such a sequence, and one may take \(R=t^{-A}\).
Reciprocity gives \(P^*(\tau)=0\), and \(t\ge 1/R\).
The common root lies in a shared noncyclotomic irreducible factor over the rationals.

Alternatively take \(\eta=1/(2K_L)\) and \(R=d^\eta\).
For all sufficiently large \(d\), \(D\ge d>K_L R^{K_L}\).
The same theorem locates the positive root of every such small Salem factor within \(\exp(-d^\eta)\) of a common positive real root of the two end blocks for this cut, since \(t\gg(\log(2d))^{-3}\).
The two choices of \(R\) can select different blocks and roots.

More generally, for every monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic unit factor of degree \(d\) and positive logarithmic measure \(m\), all exterior conjugates with logarithmic modulus at least \(d^{-\eta}\) lie in the stated disks for \(R=d^\eta\), simultaneously, at large \(d\).
If none reaches that threshold, the number \(h\) of exterior conjugates satisfies
\[
                   h>m d^\eta\gg d^\eta/(\log(2d))^3 .
\]
This follows by summing their logarithmic moduli and using the same bound at degree \(d\).
In particular \(h\le m d^\eta\) forces a common exterior end-block root.
These are restrictions on factors admitting a multiple of bounded coefficient length.
The disk counts do not identify which irreducible factor of the multiple contains an individual zero.
