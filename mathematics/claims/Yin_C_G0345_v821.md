# Ramified critical orbits in each rational factor

<a id="statement"></a>
## The odd contacts required by the degree of a factor

Let \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) be a squarefree [two-pole section](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) in a positive primitive direction, with rational irreducible factors \(S_i\), root fields \(K_i\), and degrees \(d_i\).
For every rational prime \(p\),
\[
  \sum_i v_p(|\operatorname{disc}K_i|)\le 16+32\lfloor\log_p17\rfloor.          \tag{1}
\]
Use the [degree-56 exceptional form](Yin_C_G0345_v812.md#statement) \(B(k,l)\), assumed nonzero.
At \(p\nmid B(k,l)\), each residue Frobenius orbit of critical points \(c=(u_c,v_c)\) with positive odd contact \(b_c=v_p(1-v_c^k/u_c^l)\) belongs to a unique factor.
The two local branches at each geometric residue point of the orbit are roots of that factor.
Write \(a_{i,p}\) for the sum of the orbit sizes belonging to \(S_i\) among these odd contacts.
Then \(v_p(|\operatorname{disc}K_i|)=a_{i,p}\) at every such prime, and \(\sum_i a_{i,p}\le16\).

Along \(k\to\infty,\ l/k\to\infty\), put \(\mathcal P_i=\{p\nmid B(k,l):a_{i,p}>0\}\).
Uniformly over all irreducible factors,
\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_i}a_{i,p}\log p\ \ge\ (2+\log(\pi/4)-o(1))d_i,
 \qquad
 \log\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_i}p\ \ge\ \frac{2+\log(\pi/4)-o(1)}{16}d_i.           \tag{2}
\]
In particular each factor contains both branches over some good ramified prime \(p\gg d_i\), with an absolute implied constant.
Different orbits over the same rational prime may belong to different factors.

<a id="different"></a>
## Bounding the different at every prime

For a place \(w\mid p\) of any \(K_i\), denote its ramification index and residue degree by \(e_w,f_w\).
The reduction of the factor over \(\mathbb Q_p\) belonging to \(w\) is a power of the minimal polynomial of the residue of the chosen root.
Indeed every conjugate reduces to a Frobenius conjugate of that residue, and all such residue conjugates occur with equal multiplicity.
If the residue polynomial has degree \(g\), it contributes exponent \(e_w f_w/g\), with \(g\mid f_w\).
For a fixed irreducible residue polynomial of degree \(g\) and total multiplicity \(m\) in \(\bar P\), the corresponding part of \(\sum_w f_w(e_w-1)\) is therefore \(gm-\sum_w f_w\le g(m-1)\).
The latter is at most the degree contributed by this polynomial to \(\gcd(\bar P,\bar P')\), also in characteristic dividing \(m\).
Summing and using the [uniform derivative bound](Yin_C_G0345_v805.md#statement) gives \(\sum_w f_w(e_w-1)\le16\), so every \(e_w\le17\) and \(\sum_{e_w>1}e_wf_w\le32\).

Over the maximal unramified local subfield a uniformizer generates the integer ring and its Eisenstein polynomial has degree \(e_w\).
Its derivative generates the different and the leading derivative term has uniformizer valuation \(e_w-1+e_w v_p(e_w)\).
The valuations of all nonzero derivative terms have different residues modulo \(e_w\), so the valuation of their sum is at most that of the leading term.
The unramified subextension has trivial different.
Taking norms of the local differents thus gives (1), including wild primes.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Field discriminants and the local quadratic equation

At a good prime each simple residue root gives an unramified place.
Every other residue disk is described by the [critical quadratic equation](Yin_C_G0345_v808.md#local) \(z^2=(1-w_c)/C\), where \(w_c=v_c^k/u_c^l\) and \(C\) is a unit of the unramified base of degree equal to the orbit size \(f\).
This base is contained in the completion at either branch because its residue coordinate generates the degree-\(f\) residue field of the original disk.
The quadratic equation accounts for the full degree \(2f\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\) in that orbit.
Since \(p\) is odd, even contact gives two split branches or an unramified quadratic extension, with zero different in either case.
Odd contact gives a single ramified quadratic field over the unramified base, so the two branches lie in one irreducible local factor of degree \(2f\) and hence in one rational factor.
Its tame different has exponent one, contributing exactly \(f\) to the rational discriminant exponent.
There are at most sixteen geometric critical points, giving the stated good-prime bound.

For steep primitive directions \(B(k,l)\ne0\) eventually, and (1) bounds the log contribution of its prime divisors to the product of all field discriminants by \(O(\log(k+l))\), using the degree-56 bound on the radical of its value.
Minkowski's discriminant bound gives \(|\operatorname{disc}K_i|^{1/2}\ge (\pi/4)^{r_2} d_i^{d_i}/d_i!\), with \(r_2\le d_i/2\) complex pairs.
Stirling's formula bounds its log discriminant below by \((2+\log(\pi/4))d_i-O(\log(2+d_i))\).
The [uniform single-root degree bound](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives \(d_i=l^{1+o(1)}\), so the whole bad-prime error is \(o(d_i)\).
The exact allocation at good primes now gives (2).
Chebyshev's bound \(\sum_{p\le y}\log p=O(y)\) gives the asserted lower bound for the largest good ramified prime of each factor.
