# Frobenius on the embeddings of a nonnormal base field

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative height and a probability on cosets

Let \(F\) be any number field of degree \(s\), \(L\) its normal closure over \(\mathbb Q\), \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), and \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\).
Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero algebraic number of infinite order, \(D=[F(\alpha):F]\), and \(h\) the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
At each odd prime \(p\) choose one prime of \(L\) above it and let \(\Phi_p\) be the coset of its inertia group consisting of the lifts of the arithmetic residue Frobenius.
Writing \(C(x)\) for the conjugacy class of \(x\) in \(G\), put
\[
 a_p(Hg)=\frac1{|\Phi_p|}\sum_{x\in\Phi_p}
           \frac{|C(x)\cap Hg|}{|C(x)|},
 \qquad w_p=\max_{Hg\in H\backslash G}a_p(Hg).                       \tag{1}
\]
These quantities are independent of the prime chosen above \(p\).
In particular \(w_p\ge1/s\), and at an unramified prime (1) uses just one conjugacy class.
For every finite set \(\mathcal P\) of odd primes, \(t=|\mathcal P|\), and every integer \(T\ge1\),
\[
 T\sum_{p\in\mathcal P} w_p\log p
 \le \tfrac12(T^2+t)\log(D(T+t))
        +(T+t)\left(T+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p\right)D h(\alpha).       \tag{2}
\]
For a real \(B\ge1\) and a nonnegative integer \(u\), if \(w_p\ge1/B\) away from at most \(u\) odd primes, then for an absolute \(c>0\),
\[
 D h(\alpha)\ge\frac{c}{B(\sqrt{u+1}+B\log(2D)/\log\log(5D))^3}.    \tag{3}
\]
More locally, with \(u=0\), (3) follows if \(w_p\ge1/B\) throughout the single test interval specified [below](#range).

Let \(n_1(F,p)\) count the prime ideals of \(F\) over \(p\) with residue degree one, including ramified primes, counting each once.
Then \(a_p(H)=n_1(F,p)/s\).
If the action of \(G\) on its \(s>1\) cosets is doubly transitive, all the other values are \((1-n_1(F,p)/s)/(s-1)\).
For any prescribed integer \(D_0\ge1\) there exist nonnormal fields of arbitrarily large degree with symmetric normal closure group, no nontrivial subfield normal over \(\mathbb Q\), and
\[
 D h(\alpha)\ge c_0\left(\frac{\log\log(5D)}{\log(2D)}\right)^3
                      \qquad(1\le D\le D_0)                            \tag{4}
\]
for every such \(\alpha\), with an absolute \(c_0>0\) independent of \(D_0\) and of the field.
The normal closures can simultaneously have arbitrarily large wild ramification indices at every test prime in this construction.
For integral inputs with \(F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), multiplying a relative bound by \(s\) gives its implication for the original total logarithmic Mahler measure.

<a id="weights"></a>
## Frobenius lifts and residue-degree-one primes

Choose a uniformly random prime \(P\) of \(L\) over \(p\), then a uniformly random Frobenius lift \(x\in\Phi_P\) there.
Equivalently, choose a uniform lift at the initially chosen prime and conjugate it by a uniform member of \(G\).
Conjugating transports the whole coset, so uniform lifts remain uniform even if several conjugators give the same prime.
Thus (1) is exactly the probability of \(Hx=Hg\).
Write \(v=P\cap F\), \(e_v=e(F_v/\mathbb Q_p)\), and \(I_P\) for inertia in \(L\).
Conditionally on \(P\), the coset \(\Phi_P=I_P x_0\) meets exactly \([I_P:I_P\cap H]=e_v\) distinct right cosets of \(H\), in equal numbers.
Indeed \(I_P\cap H\) is inertia in \(L_P/F_v\), so the index follows by the local tower formula.
Hence
\[
 a_p(Hg)=\mathop{\rm average}_{P\mid p}
          \frac{\mathbf1_{\Phi_P\cap Hg\ne\varnothing}}{e_v}.          \tag{5}
\]
The lifts meet \(H\) exactly when the residue degree \(f_v\) is one: the decomposition group for \(L_P/F_v\) maps onto the residue Galois group over the residue field of \(F_v\).
The fraction of the primes \(P\) restricting to \(v\) is \(e_v f_v/s\), by the local degrees in the two towers.
Substitution gives the asserted count for \(a_p(H)\).
Conjugation invariance makes \(a_p\) constant on the orbits of the right action of \(H\) on \(H\backslash G\).
Double transitivity gives the two stated values.
For example, at an unramified transposition in the natural action of \(S_s\), \(s\ge4\), the largest value is \((s-2)/s\) although the conjugacy class has \(s(s-1)/2\) members.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Comparing coefficients from different conjugates of the base

Apply the [relative class-product reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#relative-compression) to the \(F\)-conjugates, whose transitivity needs no normality of \(F/\mathbb Q\).
The resulting \(\beta\) has \(D'\le D\) relative conjugates \(a_i\) with distinct powers \(a_i^j\) at each fixed \(j\ge1\), and \(D'h(\beta)=Dh(\alpha)\).
Let \(f\) be its monic relative minimum.
At each \(p\) choose \(g\) maximizing (1), extend it to the algebraic closure, and use the \(D'\) additional nodes \((g^{-1}a_i)^p\).
The nodes within a packet are distinct by the class reduction, and the positive heights distinguish packets at different primes and the original packet.

Fix \(P\) with \(x\in\Phi_P\cap Hg\), so \(g^{-1}a=x^{-1}a\) for every \(a\in F\).
Scale \(f\) by an inverse coefficient of largest absolute value at \(v\), obtaining \(f_0\) of local Gauss norm one.
For any coefficient \(a\) of \(f_0\), choose its residue representative \(z\in F_v\) satisfying \(z^{p^{f_v}}=z\).
The representative exists uniquely by simple-root lifting, also in \(L_P\) for the same residue.
Consequently \(x(z)=z^p\), and \(v_p(a-z)\ge1/e_v\) with \(v_p(p)=1\).
Since \(x\) preserves the valuation, reduction about \(x^{-1}z\) gives
\[
                   v_p\big(a-(g^{-1}a)^p\big)\ge1/e_v .
\]
All mixed terms in a \(p\)-th power have coefficients divisible by \(p\).
Therefore every coefficient of \(f_0(X^p)-(g^{-1}f_0(X))^p\) has valuation at least \(1/e_v\).
This comparison uses the ramification of \(F_v\) even if the two coefficient fields together generate a more ramified completion.

At the nodes \(z_i=g^{-1}a_i\), the second polynomial term vanishes.
Evaluation and Gauss norm multiplicativity, as in the [nonintegral determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#determinant), save \(p^{-D'/e_v}\) in the product of cross differences relative to the usual local maxima.
In detail, if \(f_0=c f\), then \(|c|_P^{-1}=\prod_j\max(1,|a_j|_P)\), and each \(|f_0(z_i^p)|_P\) is bounded by \(p^{-1/e_v}\max(1,|z_i^p|_P)^{D'}\), at every extension of the place.
Use multiplicity \(T\) on the original nodes and one on all other nodes.
The cross savings are raised to the power \(T\), while all places over each \(P\) carry its original total normalized weight.
The \(P\)'s themselves have equal weights since \(L/\mathbb Q\) is Galois.
Formula (5) gives the contribution \(TD'w_p\log p\), including ramified places.
The nonzero confluent determinant has archimedean row factor \((D'(T+t))^{D'(T^2+t)/2}\).
The other local maxima charge at most \((D'(T+t)-1)m_z\) times the absolute height at each node of multiplicity \(m_z\).
The [normalized product formula](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#local), division by \(D'\) and \(D'\le D\) now give (2).

<a id="range"></a>
## A finite test and symmetric fields realizing it

Put \(T=\lceil A(\sqrt{u+1}+B\log(2D)/\log\log(5D))\rceil\), \(X=T^2\log(2T)\), for a sufficiently large absolute \(A\).
Use the primes with \(w_p\ge1/B\) in \([X,2X]\).
The [prime interval argument](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#consequences) uses only this interval: deleting at most \(u\) leaves \(\asymp T^2\) primes, and (2) then gives \(Dh(\alpha)\gg1/(BT^3)\).
This proves (3) and its single-interval form.

For the realization take the finite union \(\mathcal S\) of the odd primes in all those intervals with \(u=0,B=2\) and \(1\le D\le D_0\).
Take arbitrarily large integers \(k\ge2\) divisible by every prime in \(\mathcal S\), and let \(s=2k\).
At each \(p\in\mathcal S\) prescribe a monic integer polynomial of degree \(s\) sufficiently close coefficientwise in \(\mathbb Z_p\) to
\[
             (Z^k-p)\prod_{j=1}^{s-k}(Z-a_j)
\]
with distinct positive integers \(a_j\equiv1\pmod p\).
The [strong Hensel inequality](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) at its simple roots \(a_j\) gives \(s-k\) roots of the perturbed polynomial in disjoint neighborhoods of those integers, all local units.
Indeed the derivatives at these finitely many points have fixed finite valuations and the errors can be made arbitrarily small by a coefficient congruence.
After dividing out their monic linear factors in \(\mathbb Z_p[Z]\), the remaining monic factor reduces to \(Z^k\) and its constant term has valuation one, since the original constant term has valuation one and the divided roots are units.
It is Eisenstein and gives a totally ramified degree-\(k\) completion, as also follows by the root valuation \(1/k\).

At three further distinct rational primes larger than \(s\), prescribe squarefree reductions of types \((s)\), \((s-1,1)\) and \((2,1,\ldots,1)\), using [irreducible polynomials of prescribed degrees](../audits/literature/Hun74/Yin_U006.md#hun74-u006).
The coefficient Chinese remainder theorem gives a single monic integral polynomial satisfying all prescriptions.
The first auxiliary reduction makes it irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
As in the [squarefree Frobenius argument](Yin_C_G0345_v255.md#symmetric), lifting the distinct roots to the unramified local extension gives the prescribed cycle types in its Galois group.
Transitivity and the \((s-1)\)-cycle give a transitive point stabilizer on the remaining points.
Double transitivity then conjugates the transposition to every transposition, proving the group is \(S_s\).
The root field \(F\) corresponds to \(S_{s-1}\), whose normal closure as a subgroup contains every transposition.
Hence \(F\) has no nontrivial subfield normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
At every test prime there are \(s-k+1\) residue-degree-one primes, so \(w_p\ge(s-k+1)/s>1/2\).
The index of ramification in \(L\) is a multiple of \(k\) there, and the exponents \(v_p(k)\) may be taken simultaneously arbitrarily large.
The single-interval result proves (4) with the same absolute constant for every indicated relative degree.
The finite prescriptions depend on \(D_0\).
