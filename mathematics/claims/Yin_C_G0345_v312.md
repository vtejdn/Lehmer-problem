# The cyclotomic factors and limiting root distribution of a prime-indexed Boyd–Mossinghoff family

<a id="statement"></a>
## What can occur at the smaller limiting measure

Let \(p\ge5\) be an odd prime, let \(F(x,y)=(1+x)+(1+x+x^2)y+x(1+x)y^2\), and set
\[
 H=F(z,-z^{p-1})=z^{2p}+z^{2p-1}-z^{p+1}-z^p-z^{p-1}+z+1 .
\]
Write \(\lambda=M(F)\), where \(M\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure, and set
\[
 \delta=\frac2\pi\arcsin\!\left(\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}\right).
\]
The cyclotomic part of \(H\), with multiplicities, is \(\Phi_{12}\) if \(p\equiv11\bmod12\) and is \(1\) otherwise.
There is exactly one real exterior root, which is negative and simple.
For every monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(S\) of degree \(d\), uniformly as \(p\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{\#\{\text{neutral roots of }S\}}d\to 1-\delta,\qquad
 \frac{\#\{\text{nonreal exterior roots of }S\}}d\to\frac\delta2,\qquad
 \log M(S)=\frac{d}{2p}\log\lambda+o(1).                         \tag{1}
\]
Here neutral means modulus one, and conjugates in the second count are counted individually.
The relative degree \(d/(2p)\) stays bounded below for these factors by the [separated-block estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#factors).
In particular the formula determines the measure from the degree up to a vanishing error, even if the factorization changes with \(p\).

Restrict to \(p\not\equiv11\bmod12\).
At each sufficiently large reducible prime there is a factor with \(M(S)\le\sqrt{M(H)}\).
Along any infinite sequence of such primes its upper limiting measure is at most \(\sqrt{\lambda}<1.121\), and both counts in (1) grow linearly with \(p\).
Along any infinite sequence of irreducible primes in this set, the inputs have prime half-degree and \(M(H)\to\lambda<1.256\).
For any nontorsion radical of a splitting-field unit of measure at most \(M(H)\), the number of its exterior conjugates then satisfies
\[
                      b\ge(\delta/2+o(1))\,p/\log p .             \tag{2}
\]
For radicals of the group generated by the input roots, the bound improves to \(b\ge(\delta/2+o(1))p\).
Infinitely many primes are available in the restricted set, for instance [primes \(1\bmod12\)](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#sut21-u001).
At least one alternative is infinite.
The argument leaves their individual infinitude open.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Cyclotomic factors at every odd prime

Suppose a primitive \(m\)-th root \(x\) is a zero and put \(y=-x^{p-1}\).
If \(m\) is odd, \(x^2\) is conjugate to \(x\), so \(F(x^2,-y^2)=0\).
If \(m=2s\) with \(s\) odd, \(-x^2=x^{s+2}\) is conjugate to \(x\) because \(\gcd(s+2,2s)=1\).
Since \(p-1\) is even, \(F(-x^2,-y^2)=0\).
If \(4\mid m\), \(-x=x^{1+m/2}\) is conjugate to \(x\) and \(F(-x,y)=0\).
Eliminating \(y\) by the resultant with \(F(x,y)\), one obtains in the three respective cases
\[
 4x^4\Phi_5(x)^2,\qquad 4x^3(x+1)^2\Phi_{30}(x),\qquad
 4x^2\Phi_{12}(x).
\]
These identities and the remaining finite polynomial divisions can be verified over \(\mathbb Z\) by the following exact calculation.
For a given \(m\) it is enough to test odd \(p\) modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(2,m)\).
The table records residues giving divisibility, including the composite residue \(27\bmod30\).

~~~python
from math import lcm
from sympy import cyclotomic_poly, diff, expand, rem, resultant, symbols

x, y = symbols("x y")
F = (1+x)+(1+x+x**2)*y+x*(1+x)*y**2
phi = lambda m: cyclotomic_poly(m, x)
for X, Y, expected in [
    (x**2, -y**2, 4*x**4*phi(5)**2),
    (-x**2, -y**2, 4*x**3*(x+1)**2*phi(30)),
    (-x, y, 4*x**2*phi(12)),
]:
    assert expand(resultant(F, F.xreplace({x:X, y:Y}), y)-expected) == 0
for m, expected in [(2, []), (5, []), (12, [11]), (30, [27])]:
    found = [j for j in range(1,lcm(2,m),2)
             if rem(F.subs(y,-x**(j-1)),phi(m),x) == 0]
    assert found == expected
assert rem(diff(F,x).subs(y,-x**10),phi(12),x) == -2*x
assert rem(-x**9*diff(F,y).subs(y,-x**10),phi(12),x) == -x**2-x-1
~~~

For \(p\equiv11\bmod12\), the derivative of \(H\) has residue \(-2x-(p-1)(x^2+x+1)\) modulo \(\Phi_{12}\) by the chain rule.
This nonzero polynomial has degree less than four, proving simplicity.
The residue \(27\bmod30\) contains no primes.

<a id="real"></a>
## The two real rays

Use \(z^{-p}H=C_p+C_{p-1}-X-1\), with \(X=z+z^{-1}\) and \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
At \(z=e^t\), \(t>0\), the terms \(2\cosh((p-1)t)\) and \(2\cosh(pt)\) exceed or equal \(2\cosh t\) and \(2\), respectively.
At \(z=-e^t\), the trace expression is \(1\) at \(t=0\) and tends to \(-\infty\).
It equals \(-2\cosh(pt)+2\cosh((p-1)t)+2\cosh t-1\).
Its derivative is strictly negative for \(t>0\), since \(p\sinh(pt)-(p-1)\sinh((p-1)t)\ge\sinh(pt)>\sinh t\).
Reciprocity gives its inverse inside.

<a id="distribution"></a>
## Localizing neutral roots and measuring radial displacement

By [the adjacent-family bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#exterior), every root can be written \(z=e^{i(u+iv)}\) with \(|v|=O(\log p/p)\).
All noncyclotomic factors are eventually reciprocal, their degrees satisfy \(d\gg p\), and their logarithmic measures are bounded.
The [angular estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) therefore gives convergence of their empirical arguments to uniform measure on the circle, uniformly over the choice of factor.
Indeed its estimate makes every fixed nonzero Fourier moment tend to zero under those degree and measure bounds.
Trigonometric approximation then gives the assertion for continuous functions, and interval counts follow by upper and lower continuous approximation.

For \(\theta=u+iv\) away from \(\pi\bmod2\pi\), the root equation is
\[
 \cos((p-1/2)\theta)=g(\theta),\qquad
 g(\theta)=\frac{1+2\cos\theta}{4\cos(\theta/2)} .
\]
Use local angle representatives for this equation.
On a compact subarc of \(|g(u)|<1\), one has \(|g(u+iv)|\le1-\eta\) for some \(\eta>0\) and all large \(p\).
Thus \(|\cos((p-1/2)u)|\le1-\eta\), and the absolute sine there is bounded below by a positive constant \(c\).
Analyticity and reality of \(g\) on the real arc give \(|\Im g(u+iv)|\le K|v|\).
The imaginary part of the root equation implies \(c\sinh((p-1/2)|v|)\le K|v|\), forcing \(v=0\).

On a compact subarc of \(|g(u)|>1\) avoiding the pole, no neutral root is possible.
Moreover \((p-1/2)|v|\) is bounded there because \(|\cos(a+ib)|^2=\cos^2a+\sinh^2b\).
Pass to convergent subsequences of \(u,(p-1/2)u\bmod2\pi,(p-1/2)|v|\) and of the sign of \(v\).
In the limit the cosine is real with modulus greater than one, so its real angle is a multiple of \(\pi\), and its imaginary argument has absolute value \(\operatorname{arcosh}|g(u)|\).
This proves the uniform local limit \(p|v|-\operatorname{arcosh}|g(u)|\to0\).
The map \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) pairs interior and exterior roots of each reciprocal real factor at the same argument.

With \(s=2|\cos(u/2)|\), the region \(|g(u)|>1\) is \(0<s<\sqrt2-1\) and has normalized length \(\delta\).
Discarding arbitrarily small fixed neighborhoods of its endpoints and of the pole costs arbitrarily little angular mass, uniformly in the factors.
This proves the first two limits in (1), since the polynomial has only one real exterior root.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## The share of the measure carried by each factor

Let \(A(u)=\operatorname{arcosh}|g(u)|\) on \(|g|>1\), and zero on \(|g|\le1\), ignoring the single pole.
It is nonnegative and integrable, with at worst a logarithmic singularity at the pole.
Its mean equals \(\log\lambda\) by the [Jensen integral](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#integral), since \(2|\cos(u/2)|\) has the same distribution as \(2|\cos u|\).
For any sequence of the indicated factors, the local radial limit and equidistribution, tested against continuous cutoffs on compact subarcs and then exhausted towards the full positive region, give
\[
 \liminf \frac{2p}{d}\log M(S)
   =\liminf \frac1d\sum_{S(z)=0}p\big|\log|z|\big|
   \ \ge\ \int A(u)\frac{du}{2\pi}=\log\lambda.
\]
The equality uses the unit norm.
The lower bound is uniform over factors in the sequential sense.
Count factors with multiplicity and give each weight \(d/(2p)\).
The noncyclotomic weights sum to \(1-O(1/p)\) by the exact cyclotomic classification, and every one is bounded below independently of \(p\).
Their weighted average of \((2p/d)\log M(S)\), with the full degree as denominator, is exactly \(\log M(H)\to\log\lambda\).
A subsequence on which one normalized value exceeds \(\log\lambda\) by a fixed positive amount would violate that average and the uniform lower bound on the others.
This proves the measure limit in (1) without assuming uniform integrability of the radial displacement for individual factors.

<a id="bound"></a>
## A rational upper bound for the limiting measure

The layer-cake integral of \(A\) gives
\[
 \log\lambda=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty
   \arcsin\left(\frac1{2(\cosh u+\sqrt{1+\cosh^2u})}\right)\,du .
\]
Indeed \(A>u\) means \(s<(\cosh u+\sqrt{1+\cosh^2u})^{-1}\) for \(u\ge0\), up to sets of measure zero.
The integrand decreases, so a left-endpoint sum bounds the integral on \([0,12]\) from above.
For \(u\ge12\) its arcsine argument is at most \(e^{-u}/2\), and \(\arcsin x\le2x\) there.
For the finite sum, the Taylor polynomial of degree 14 bounds \(\cosh u\) below.
Rounding that bound and the indicated square root down gives a rational upper bound \(x\) for the arcsine argument.
Since the positive arcsine coefficients of order three and higher are at most \(1/6\), \(\arcsin x\le x+x^3/(6(1-x^2))\) for \(0\le x<1\).
The following integer and rational calculation rounds each upper summand upwards.
It uses \(\pi>333/106\) and the alternating lower sum for \(\log(1+32/125)\).

~~~python
from fractions import Fraction as Q
from math import factorial, isqrt
N, S = 512, 10**8
D = factorial(14)*N**14
weights = [factorial(14)//factorial(2*k)*N**(14-2*k) for k in range(8)]
total = 0
for j in range(12*N):
    c = S*sum(w*j**(2*k) for k,w in enumerate(weights))//D
    x = Q(S, 2*(c+isqrt(S*S+c*c)))
    upper = x+x**3/(6*(1-x*x))
    total += -((-upper.numerator*S)//upper.denominator)
assert sum(Q(12**j,factorial(j)) for j in range(25))>10**5
I = Q(2*106,333)*(Q(total,S*N)+Q(1,10**5))
v = Q(32,125)
lower_log = sum((-1)**(j+1)*v**j/j for j in range(1,9))
assert total == 18307179515
assert I<lower_log
assert Q(157,125)<Q(1121,1000)**2
~~~

Thus \(\lambda<157/125=1.256\), and its square root is strictly below \(1.121\).
The [source family](../audits/literature/BM05/Yin_U001.md#source-quadratic-families) has reported value about \(1.255433\).
Only the rational bounds above enter the numerical thresholds here.

<a id="alternative"></a>
## The two consequences for primes with no cyclotomic factor

If \(H\) splits into at least two irreducible factors counted with multiplicities, they all have positive measure by the torsion computation and Kronecker's theorem.
Their measures multiply to \(M(H)\), giving the square-root conclusion.
If \(H\) is irreducible, its \(p\) inverse pairs include one real exterior and a positive proportion of neutral pairs.
The number \(c\) of pairs of nonreal exterior conjugates is \((\delta/2+o(1))p\) by (1).
The [prime-degree radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) gives \(b\ge1+c\) for reciprocal nontorsion outputs in the radical of the root-generated group.
For radicals in the entire splitting field the [ambient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v310.md#statement) gives \(b\ge(p-1)/(1+(p-1)\log(p-1)/c)\), hence (2).
An output of measure at most \(M(H)\) is eventually below the plastic number, hence reciprocal by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001).
The analysis does not establish unrestricted Lehmer or a sequence with measure tending to one.
