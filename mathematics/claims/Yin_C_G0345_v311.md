# Irreducible factors below the earlier prime-family limit

The coordinating researcher proposed using the smallest reported BM05 bivariate measure to improve the earlier prime-family cutoff.
The agent-S researcher derived the specialization, the factorwise exterior growth and the rational comparison of the two limiting integrals below.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Uniform bounds for actual irreducible factors

Write \(M\) for total multiplicative Mahler measure; an exterior root has modulus greater than one.
For integers \(n\ge3\), put
\[
 H_n(z)=z^{2n+2}+z^{2n+1}-z^{n+2}-z^{n+1}-z^n+z+1.             \tag{1}
\]
There are constants \(c_0,c_1,c_2>0\) and \(n_0\) such that for every \(n\ge n_0\), every monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor \(S\in\mathbb Z[z]\) of \(H_n\) is reciprocal and satisfies
\[
 \deg S\ge c_0 n,\qquad
 c_1\le\log M(S)\le\log M(H_n)<\frac6{25},\qquad
 \#\{\text{nonreal exterior roots of }S\}\ge c_2 n.             \tag{2}
\]
There is at least one such factor for every sufficiently large \(n\), and the number of these factors, counted with multiplicity, is bounded independently of \(n\).
Moreover
\[
             \log M(H_n)\longrightarrow I_{23},\qquad
             0<I_{23}<\frac6{25}<\frac{197}{800}<I_{306},       \tag{3}
\]
where \(I_{306}\) is the logarithmic limit in [C306](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit).
The quantity \(\exp I_{23}\) is the BM05 value reported as approximately \(1.255433\); the strict comparison in (3) does not depend on that decimal.

Thus choosing any noncyclotomic irreducible factor at each sufficiently large \(n\) gives an actual growing-degree sequence with linearly many nonreal exterior conjugates and \(\limsup M(S_n)\le\exp I_{23}\).
Neither irreducibility of the entire \(H_n\) nor prime half-degree of the chosen factors is proved.
In particular, (2) does not transfer C306's lower bound for all radical outputs to this lower cutoff.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## The bivariate polynomial and the prime-degree attempt

The [BM05 family (2–2)](../audits/literature/BM05/Yin_U001.md#source-quadratic-families) at \((a,b)=(2,3)\) is
\[
 P_{23}(x,y)=(1+x)+(1+x+x^2)y+x(1+x)y^2.                       \tag{4}
\]
The reported value is identified with this parameter pair in the original journal edition, §2, printed p. 406. [Author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38338596_Small_Limit_Points_of_Mahler%27s_Measure)
Direct expansion gives \(H_n(z)=P_{23}(z,-z^n)\).
The [fixed-polynomial specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002), applied to \(P_{23}(x,-y)\) and directions \((1,n)\), gives
\[
                         I_{23}=m(P_{23}).                     \tag{5}
\]
The shortest nonzero integral relation perpendicular to \((1,n)\) has maximum norm \(n\), and the sign change preserves normalized torus measure.

Let \(C_j\) be the monic Chebyshev polynomial characterized by \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).
Then
\[
 H_n(z)=z^{n+1}\{C_{n+1}(z+z^{-1})+C_n(z+z^{-1})
                              -(z+z^{-1})-1\}.                \tag{6}
\]
In particular \(n=p-1\), with \(p\) an odd prime, gives total degree \(2p\) and trace polynomial
\[
                         C_p(X)+C_{p-1}(X)-X-1.                \tag{7}
\]
Modulo \(p\), this is \(X^p+C_{p-1}(X)-X-1\), rather than the Artin–Schreier polynomial used in C306.
No irreducibility conclusion follows from that earlier modular argument.
All proved assertions in (2)–(3) nevertheless hold along these prime-indexed parameters.

<a id="factors"></a>
## A separated block controls every noncyclotomic factor

Write
\[
 H_n=z^{2n+1}B+D_n,\qquad B=z+1,\qquad
                   D_n=-z^n(z^2+z+1)+z+1.
\]
The coefficient lengths are \(\|B\|_1=2\), \(\|D_n\|_1=5\), and all zeros of \(B\) lie on the unit circle.
For the notation of the [separated-block factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement), its gap is \(g=n-1\), its small-block degree is \(b=1\), and its total degree is \(2n+2\).
For \(n\ge7\),
\[
                    g-2b=n-3\ge\tfrac14(2n+2).
\]
Taking \(L=5\) and \(\delta=1/4\) in that theorem proves uniform positive factor measure and degree \(\gg n\).
Every monic factor has constant coefficient of absolute value one because \(H_n(0)=1\).
Thus noncyclotomic factors are nontorsion unit polynomials, and both parts of that theorem apply.
The degree lower bound also bounds their number, including multiplicities.

Equivalently, the [all-slope Newton-polygon result](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) applies: the vertices of the Newton polygon of \(P_{23}\) are
\[
                  (0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,1),
\]
and each edge polynomial is a binomial with a root of unity as its sole root.
The direct separated-block application above suffices for the particular direction (1).

The full polynomial has coefficient length seven, so \(\log M(H_n)\le\log7\).
Once (3) is proved, it has \(\log M(H_n)<6/25\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Also \(e^{6/25}<13/10<\theta\), where \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\): the first inequality follows from
\(\log(1+3/10)\ge3/10-(3/10)^2/2>6/25\), and the second by substitution in the cubic.
By [Smyth's nonreciprocal gap](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), every noncyclotomic factor is then reciprocal.
Positivity of \(I_{23}\) and convergence ensure that at least one such factor exists.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## A fixed arc gives linear exterior growth in every factor

On the unit circle, divide (1) by \(z^{n+1}\) and put \(z=e^{i\vartheta}\).
A root must satisfy
\[
       4\cos(\vartheta/2)\cos((n+1/2)\vartheta)
                                      =1+2\cos\vartheta.       \tag{8}
\]
If the circular distance from \(\vartheta\) to \(\pi\) is at most \(h=1/4\), the left side has absolute value at most \(4\sin(1/8)<1/2\), whereas
\[
              |1+2\cos\vartheta|
                  \ge2\cos(1/4)-1\ge15/16.
\]
Thus this entire fixed arc contains no unit-circle root of \(H_n\), for any \(n\).

Take any noncyclotomic factor \(S\), with degree \(d\) and logarithmic measure \(m\).
The [angular estimate (4) in C108](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) holds for every monic irreducible unit polynomial, independently of the shift hypotheses in that claim's main statement.
For the triangular function \(\psi_h\) supported in the arc of half-width \(h\), it gives
\[
       \frac1d\sum_{S(\alpha)=0}\psi_h(\arg\alpha-\pi)
                         =\frac{h}{2\pi}+o(1),                \tag{9}
\]
uniformly over the present factors, since \(d\gg n\) and \(m\le\log7\).
As \(0\le\psi_h\le1\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) the arc contains at least \(d/(16\pi)\) distinct conjugates.
The now reciprocal, real polynomial \(S\) pairs each such root \(\alpha\) with \(1/\overline\alpha\).
This involution preserves its argument, has no fixed point in the arc by (8), and interchanges exterior and interior roots.
Consequently \(S\) has at least \(d/(32\pi)\) exterior conjugates in this arc.

Descartes' rule bounds the positive real roots of \(H_n\), with multiplicity, by two and its negative real roots by six, from the at most seven displayed terms.
Therefore
\[
       \#\{\text{nonreal exterior roots of }S\}
                                \ge\frac{d}{32\pi}-8\gg n.    \tag{10}
\]
This proves the factorwise conclusion, without any equidistribution assertion for a union of unrelated factors.

There is also a direct uniform radial bound.
At an exterior root, write \(r=|z|>1\).
The equation gives
\[
       r^{2n+1}(r-1)\le3r^{n+2}+2r\le5r^{n+2},
                  \qquad r^{n-1}(r-1)\le5.
\]
Putting \(t=(n-1)\log r\), one has \(t e^t\le5(n-1)\), and hence
\[
                   \log r\le\frac{\log(5(n-1))}{n-1}.         \tag{11}
\]
Reciprocity of \(H_n\) gives the same bound for the absolute logarithmic radius of every root.
The factor measure lower bound combined with (11) already gives an exterior count \(\gg n/\log n\); the fixed-arc argument improves it to (10).

<a id="integral"></a>
## Exact integral for the smaller limit

Use the measure-preserving finite torus cover from the [BM05 comparison](../audits/literature/BM05/Yin_U001.md#finite-cover-and-exact-control):
\[
 P_{23}(x^2,-x^{-1}Y)
   =xY\{(x+x^{-1})(Y+Y^{-1})-(x^2+1+x^{-2})\}.
\]
Its exponent matrix has determinant two, so normalized Haar measure is preserved, and the monomial factor contributes zero logarithmic measure.
For \(t=2\cos\vartheta\ne0\), the root contribution in Jensen's formula is zero when \(|t-t^{-1}|\le2\), and is \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|t-t^{-1}|/2)\) otherwise.
The leading coefficient \(x+x^{-1}\) has zero logarithmic measure.
For \(0<t\le2\), the contribution is positive exactly when \(t<a=\sqrt2-1\).
Symmetry and the distribution of \(2\cos\vartheta\) therefore give
\[
 I_{23}=\frac2\pi\int_0^a
       \frac{\operatorname{arcosh}((1-t^2)/(2t))}
                                      {\sqrt{4-t^2}}\,dt.      \tag{12}
\]
The integrand is positive inside the interval and has only an integrable logarithmic singularity at zero.
In particular \(I_{23}>0\).

On \(0<t<a\),
\[
 \operatorname{arcosh}\frac{1-t^2}{2t}
 =\log\frac{1-t^2+\sqrt{1-6t^2+t^4}}{2t}
 \le-\log t+\log(1-2t^2)
 \le-\log t-2t^2,                                             \tag{13}
\]
because \(\sqrt{1-6t^2+t^4}\le1-3t^2\).
Take \(b=5/12>a\).
The last expression in (13) is positive on \((0,b]\), so
\[
 I_{23}
 \le\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{4-b^2}}
           \left(b(1-\log b)-\frac{2b^3}{3}\right)
 <\frac{120000}{157\cdot2347}
       \left(\frac5{12}\left(1+\frac{219}{250}\right)
                                  -\frac23\left(\frac5{12}\right)^3\right)
 <\frac6{25}.                                                \tag{14}
\]
Here \(\pi>157/50\), \(\sqrt{551}>2347/100\), and \(\log(12/5)<219/250\).
The last logarithm inequality follows by comparing \(12/5\) with the degree-five Taylor lower bound for \(\exp(219/250)\).
The last comparison in (14) is rational.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## A rational lower bound for the earlier limit

The exact [C306 integral](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit) is
\[
       I_{306}=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}f(u)\,du,
                         \qquad f(u)=\operatorname{arcosh}(1/2+\cos u).
\]
The function is continuous and concave on this interval, since cosine is concave there and \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) is increasing and concave above one.
Thus the eight-interval trapezoid rule is a lower bound.
At the first eight nodes \(u=k\pi/24\), valid strict lower bounds for \(100f(u)\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
 k&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\ \hline
 100a_k&96&95&93&89&83&74&63&46.
\end{array}
\]
At the last node \(f(\pi/3)=0\).
Consequently
\[
 I_{306}>\frac{a_0+2\sum_{k=1}^7a_k}{48}
                                  =\frac{197}{800}>\frac6{25}. \tag{15}
\]

For completeness the node inequalities have a finite rational verification.
Put \(v_k=11k/84>k\pi/24\) for \(k>0\), using \(\pi<22/7\).
For every listed \(a=a_k\le1\),
\[
 \cosh a\le\sum_{j=0}^3\frac{a^{2j}}{(2j)!}
                  +\frac{90}{89}\frac{a^8}{8!}
 <\frac12+1-\frac{v_k^2}{2}+\frac{v_k^4}{24}-\frac{v_k^6}{720}
 \le\frac12+\cos(k\pi/24).
\]
The hyperbolic tail has successive ratio at most \(1/90\), and the cosine lower bound is its alternating Taylor truncation.
The following Python 3 code, using only exact rational arithmetic, was executed and all assertions passed; it reproduces the eight node checks and (14)–(15).

```python
from fractions import Fraction as Q
from math import factorial

x = Q(219, 250)
assert (1 + Q(5, 12))**2 > 2
assert Q(2347, 100)**2 < 551
assert sum(x**j / factorial(j) for j in range(6)) > Q(12, 5)
upper = Q(120000, 157 * 2347) * (
    Q(5, 12) * (1 + x) - Q(2, 3) * Q(5, 12)**3)
assert upper < Q(6, 25)
nodes = [Q(a, 100) for a in [96, 95, 93, 89, 83, 74, 63, 46]]
for k, a in enumerate(nodes):
    v = Q(11 * k, 84)
    cosine_lower = 1 - v**2 / 2 + v**4 / 24 - v**6 / 720
    cosh_upper = sum(a**(2*j) / factorial(2*j) for j in range(4))
    cosh_upper += Q(90, 89) * a**8 / factorial(8)
    assert cosh_upper < Q(1, 2) + cosine_lower
lower = (nodes[0] + 2 * sum(nodes[1:])) / 48
assert lower == Q(197, 800) > Q(6, 25)
```

## What remains open for this route

The factors in (2) are actual irreducible reciprocal unit polynomials with growing degree and exterior count, at measures uniformly below the C306 limiting value.
The positive lower bound \(c_1\) also shows that this fixed family cannot supply a sequence with measure tending to one.
Their measures need not individually converge to \(\exp I_{23}\), since measure may be shared among several noncyclotomic factors.

The argument does not identify their Galois groups, prime half-degrees, multiplicative relation spaces or neutral-root allocation.
In particular it does not yet prove that their root-generated radical minima grow, nor that enlarging to units of their conjugate fields preserves those minima.
Such claims would need additional hypotheses beyond the exterior growth established here.
The unresolved modular step (7) is one possible route to such additional structure, rather than an assumption of this construction.
