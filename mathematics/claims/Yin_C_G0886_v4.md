# Canonical covers for all two-cusp words over D4 blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Statement and exact scope

Let
\[
D_4=\{x\in\mathbb Z^4:x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4\equiv0\pmod2\}
\]
have the standard Euclidean pairing, and let
\[
H_r=D_4^{\oplus r},\qquad L_r=H_r\oplus U,\qquad r\ge1,
\]
where \(e^2=f^2=0\) and \((e,f)=1\) on the standard integral hyperbolic plane \(U\).
Every vector of \(H_r\) has even squared norm.
For \(v\in H_r\), use the actual integral transvections
\[
T_{e,v}(x)=x-(v,x)e+(e,x)v-\tfrac12(v,v)(e,x)e,
\]
and the analogous \(T_{f,v}\).
Positive integral isometries of \(H_r\) extend to fix \(e,f\).

Consider any specified finite word \(A\) in these generators, with arbitrary parameter norms, positive isometries and word length.
Each positive factor permutes the \(r\) D4 blocks by a uniquely determined permutation.
Let \(\sigma\) be the ordered product of these permutations in this specified word, and put \(d=\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)\).
For a word with no positive factors, take \(\sigma=1\) and \(d=1\).
Write \(P_A(z)=\det(zI-A)\), using the entire original rank-\(4r+2\) integral action, and let \(\lambda_{\rm L}\) be the Lehmer root fixed in [v2](Yin_C_G0886_v2.md#statement).
Then
\[
M(P_A)=\rho(A),\qquad
M(P_A)=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad M(P_A)\ge\lambda_{\rm L}^{1/d}.       \tag{1}
\]
If \(d=1\), then also
\[
1<M(P_A)<6/5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(P_A)=\lambda_{\rm L}.    \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the unpowered bound and (2) hold for every block-preserving word, uniformly in \(r\), and also for words whose positive block permutations cancel only in their final ordered product.
For every fixed integer \(D_0\ge1\), all specified words with \(d\le D_0\) have logarithmic gap \(\log\lambda_{\rm L}/D_0\).
A sequence of such presentations with \(1<M(P_A)\to1\) must have \(d\to\infty\).

For \(r=1\), one has the further consequence
\[
M(P_A)=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad M(P_A)\ge13/10.                    \tag{3}
\]
This fixed-rank consequence is not the reason for claiming uniformity in (1).
No presentation-independent block-permutation homomorphism on the entire generated integral group is asserted.
The [standard-embedding and E8 conclusions of v3](Yin_C_G0886_v3.md#statement) remain unchanged; this version adds another actual canonical cover, rather than asserting its standard integer embedding hypothesis for D4 triality.

<a id="d4-positive-group"></a>
## Direct coverage of the entire positive D4 isometry group

Let \(u_1,\ldots,u_4\) be the standard orthonormal real coordinates of one D4 block and put
\[
a_1=u_2-u_3,\quad a_2=u_3-u_4,\quad
a_3=\sqrt2\,u_4,\quad
a_4=(u_1-u_2-u_3-u_4)/\sqrt2.
\]
Each has squared norm two, and \(s_a(x)=x-(a,x)a\) denotes its reflection.
Let \(G\) be the group generated by these four reflections.
The first three reflections give every signed permutation of coordinates \(2,3,4\).
The fourth is the reflection \(s_h\) in the norm-one vector
\[
h=(u_1-u_2-u_3-u_4)/2.
\]
For \(x\in D_4\), the integer
\(k=2(x,h)=x_1-x_2-x_3-x_4\) is even.
Thus \(s_hx=x-kh\) has integer coordinates and even coordinate sum, since \(\sum_i h_i=-1\).
Hence \(s_h\), and consequently every generator of \(G\), preserves D4.

The signed permutations on the last three coordinates supply the reflection in \(u_3+u_4\).
The exact identity
\[
s_h(u_3+u_4)=u_1-u_2
\]
therefore places the reflection swapping coordinates one and two in \(G\).
Together with the existing swaps and sign changes, this gives the full signed permutation group \(B_4\subseteq G\).

To prove that \(G\) is the full D4 isometry group, compute the dual lattice directly.
Pairing with every \(u_i-u_j\) and \(u_i+u_j\) shows that
\[
D_4^*=\mathbb Z^4\ \cup\ (\mathbb Z+1/2)^4.
\]
Conversely, both displayed classes pair integrally with all even-sum integer vectors, so this equality is exact.
Its vectors of squared norm one are precisely
\[
\mathcal U=\{\pm u_i:1\le i\le4\}
\ \cup\ \{(\pm u_1\pm u_2\pm u_3\pm u_4)/2\},
\]
a set of 24 vectors.
Every D4 isometry preserves its dual and hence \(\mathcal U\).
The stabilizer of \(u_1\) permutes the six elements of \(\mathcal U\) perpendicular to \(u_1\), namely \(\pm u_2,\pm u_3,\pm u_4\), so it has at most \(2^3\cdot3!=48\) elements.
The action on \(\mathcal U\) is faithful because this set spans the real space.
It follows that \(|O(D_4)|\le24\cdot48\).

The group \(G\) already contains the 48 signed permutations of the last three coordinates fixing \(u_1\).
Its orbit of \(u_1\) contains all eight signed coordinates and, since
\[
s_hu_1=(u_1+u_2+u_3+u_4)/2,
\]
all sixteen half-coordinate vectors by the action of \(B_4\).
Orbit-stabilizer therefore gives \(|G|\ge24\cdot48\), proving
\[
G=O(D_4),\qquad |O(D_4)|=1152.                           \tag{4}
\]
This is a direct proof of the familiar identification with \(W(F_4)\), not a new group-classification claim.
Its primary-source attribution and the actual inspection limits are recorded [below](#source-attribution).

The norm-two vectors in \(H_r\) are exactly the D4 roots \(\pm u_{j,i}\pm u_{j,k}\), with \(i\ne k\), in one block at a time.
Indeed every nonzero block vector has squared norm at least two.
Join two such roots when their inner product is nonzero.
The roots of each D4 block form a connected component: a connected D4 simple basis lies inside it, and every other root pairs nontrivially with at least one member of that basis because the basis spans the block.
Different blocks are orthogonal.
Every isometry permutes these components, which span the actual D4 summands.
After removing that uniquely determined block permutation, it acts separately on each D4 block.
Thus
\[
O(H_r)=O(D_4)^r\rtimes S_r,                              \tag{5}
\]
with the actual block permutation used in the statement.

<a id="d4-canonical-star"></a>
## The canonical star and its real radical

Let \(F_r\) be a real vector space with basis
\[
\mathsf a_{j,i}\ (1\le j\le r,\ 1\le i\le4),\qquad
\mathsf b_j\ (1\le j\le r),\qquad \mathsf c.
\]
Define \(q:F_r\to L_r\otimes\mathbb R\) by
\[
q(\mathsf a_{j,i})=a_{j,i},\qquad
q(\mathsf b_j)=e-\theta_j,\quad \theta_j=u_{j,1}+u_{j,2},\qquad
q(\mathsf c)=-e-f.
\]
The four \(a_{j,i}\) span their entire real positive block.
Adding any \(q(\mathsf b_j)\) supplies \(e\), and then \(q(\mathsf c)\) supplies \(f\), so \(q\) is surjective.
Give \(F_r\) the pullback of the actual form on \(L_r\).
Every displayed basis vector has squared norm two.
Within a block, its only nonzero distinct positive pairings are
\[
(\mathsf a_{j,1},\mathsf a_{j,2})=-1,\quad
(\mathsf a_{j,2},\mathsf a_{j,3})=-\sqrt2,\quad
(\mathsf a_{j,3},\mathsf a_{j,4})=-1.
\]
The only additional nonzero distinct pairings are
\[
(\mathsf b_j,\mathsf a_{j,1})=-1,\qquad
(\mathsf c,\mathsf b_j)=-1.
\]
All other distinct pairings, including those between different \(\mathsf b_j\)'s, are zero.
This is exactly a canonical Coxeter Gram matrix, with labels \(2,3,4\) corresponding to pairings \(0,-1,-\sqrt2\).
For \(r=1\), the graph is the six-node chain with successive labels \(3,3,3,4,3\).
Let \(\mathcal W_r\) denote its actual canonical matrix group.

Define
\[
\mathsf t_j=2\mathsf a_{j,1}+3\mathsf a_{j,2}
             +2\sqrt2\,\mathsf a_{j,3}+\sqrt2\,\mathsf a_{j,4},
\qquad \delta_j=\mathsf b_j+\mathsf t_j.
\]
Direct substitution gives \(q(\mathsf t_j)=\theta_j\) and \(q(\delta_j)=e\).
The \(r-1\) independent vectors \(\delta_j-\delta_1\), \(2\le j\le r\), therefore lie in \(\ker q\).
Surjectivity and \(\dim F_r=5r+1\), \(\dim L_r=4r+2\) give
\[
N=\ker q=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
           \{\delta_j-\delta_1:2\le j\le r\}.             \tag{6}
\]
Because the target form is nondegenerate, \(N\) is precisely the radical of the pullback form.
The signature of the latter is \((4r+1,1,r-1)\).
Every canonical generating reflection fixes \(N\) pointwise and descends through \(q\) to the reflection in its displayed image.
For \(w\in\mathcal W_r\) with quotient action \(\bar w\), the full characteristic identity is
\[
\chi_w(z)=(z-1)^{r-1}P_{\bar w}(z).                     \tag{7}
\]
No integral basis or integral quotient assertion is needed for (6)--(7).
The original quotient action will be integral because it is the actual specified word on \(L_r\).

<a id="d4-generator-coverage"></a>
## Both complete transvection groups

Equation (4) puts every block-preserving positive isometry in the quotient image of \(\mathcal W_r\).
In particular, the reflection \(s_{\theta_j}\) lies in that image.
The actual reflection calculation already fixed in [v2](Yin_C_G0886_v2.md#standard-rank-two-up) gives
\[
s_{e-\theta_j}s_{\theta_j}=T_{e,\theta_j}.              \tag{8}
\]
Positive signed permutations within block \(j\) send \(\theta_j\) to every root \(\pm u_{j,i}\pm u_{j,k}\).
Conjugating (8) supplies transvections in all such roots.
These roots generate the entire D4 lattice.
Explicitly, for \(v=\sum_i v_i u_i\in D_4\) and \(s=\sum_i v_i\in2\mathbb Z\),
\[
v=\sum_{i<4}v_i(u_i-u_4)+s u_4,
\qquad 2u_4=(u_1+u_4)-(u_1-u_4).
\]
The [addition and inverse identities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#short-word-reduction) therefore supply every \(T_{e,v}\), \(v\in H_r\), without any norm or support restriction.
The reflection in \(-e-f\) sends \(e\mapsto-f\), \(f\mapsto-e\) and fixes \(H_r\).
Its conjugation gives \(T_{e,v}\mapsto T_{f,-v}\), so the entire other transvection group is covered too.

## Whole-word lifts, permutation cost and complete measure

A permutation of the \(r\) blocks acts on \(F_r\) by permuting the corresponding \(\mathsf a\)- and \(\mathsf b\)-symbols and fixing \(\mathsf c\).
It preserves the canonical Gram matrix and normalizes \(\mathcal W_r\).
It descends to the same block permutation on \(L_r\), and sends \(\delta_j\mapsto\delta_{\sigma(j)}\).
By (5), choose a lift of each positive factor as a product of a block-preserving element of \(\mathcal W_r\) and its actual block permutation.
Use the lifts from (8) and the subsequent coverage argument for every transvection.
Their ordered product \(\widetilde A\in\mathcal W_r\rtimes S_r\) descends to the entire original word \(A\), and its permutation component is exactly the stated \(\sigma\).
The semidirect-product multiplication, before taking its matrix image, gives \(\widetilde A^d\in\mathcal W_r\).
This uses a specified presentation and does not infer a permutation homomorphism for all quotient matrices.

On \(N\), the action of \(\widetilde A\) is the standard permutation action of \(\sigma\) on the differences of the \(\delta_j\)'s, since every \(\mathcal W_r\) factor acts trivially there.
If the cycle lengths of \(\sigma\) are \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s\), its characteristic polynomial on \(N\) is
\[
Q_\sigma(z)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^s(z^{\ell_i}-1)}{z-1}.    \tag{9}
\]
For \(r=1\), this means \(Q_\sigma=1\).
Thus all roots of \(Q_\sigma\) are roots of unity, with multiplicity, and the exact identities are
\[
\chi_{\widetilde A}(z)=Q_\sigma(z)P_A(z),\qquad
\chi_{\widetilde A^d}(z)=(z-1)^{r-1}P_{A^d}(z).    \tag{10}
\]
No original root, leading coefficient or nonneutral complementary factor has been removed.

The original integral isometry acts on a nondegenerate form of negative index one.
The [complete expanding-count argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v13.md#negative-index-bound) gives \(M(P_A)=\rho(A)\), including all generalized eigenspaces and root multiplicities.
The same applies to \(A^d\), and powering gives
\[
M(P_{A^d})=M(P_A)^d,\qquad \rho(A^d)=\rho(A)^d.
\]
Equation (10) identifies these quantities with the corresponding spectral radii of the canonical lifts, since the added radical roots have modulus one and an isometry has radius at least one.
The accepted McM02 canonical theorem applied to \(\widetilde A^d\) yields either \(M(P_A)=1\) or \(M(P_A)^d\ge\lambda_{\rm L}\), proving (1).

If \(d=1\), the lift itself lies in \(\mathcal W_r\), so the same source's exact small-spectrum table and the [six rational evaluations in v2](Yin_C_G0886_v2.md#six-fifths-classification) prove (2).
For \(r=1\), the canonical representation has dimension six.
Every value in the accepted list for \((1,13/10)\) has algebraic degree eight or ten.
The negative-index-one argument makes an expanding spectral radius the absolute value of a real eigenvalue, and changing its sign leaves its algebraic degree unchanged.
It therefore cannot occur for this degree-six characteristic polynomial, proving (3).
This dimensional corollary makes no claim about higher-rank spectra.

## Remaining global gap

The result enlarges actual canonical coverage beyond standard integer embeddings admitting signed-permutation extensions.
It does not bound the block-permutation order uniformly, represent every positive integral lattice, or cover every primitive integer polynomial.
The minimum transvection-count obstruction of [C885v8](Yin_C_G0885_v8.md#statement) remains separate and unchanged.
Neither arbitrarily many D4 blocks nor the existence of their canonical cover removes the original unrestricted [G0001 gap question](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
