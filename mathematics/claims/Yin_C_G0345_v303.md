# The exact reciprocal exterior minimum for roots with real exterior conjugates

## Exact minimum and a bounded-coefficient construction

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with \(1\le e<m\) exterior roots, all real.
Use its splitting field \(K\), Galois group \(G\), and set \(I\) of \(m\) inverse-pair labels.
Choose one root \(x_i\) from each pair, taking the exterior root on the exterior label set \(E\subset I\).
Two labels belong to the same atom if their memberships in \(gE\) agree for every \(g\in G\).
The [real-root decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) gives atoms of a common size
\[
 t\mid\gcd(m,e),\qquad n=m/t,\qquad r=e/t.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
There are \(n\) atoms, exactly \(r\) of them exterior.

Let \(\mathcal L\subset\mathbb Z^I\) be the lattice of exponent vectors whose monomial in the \(x_i\) is torsion.
Put
\[
 V=\mathbb Q^I/(\mathcal L\otimes\mathbb Q)
       =\bigoplus_B V_B,
 \qquad
 V_B=\mathbb Q^B/\bigl((\mathcal L\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap\mathbb Q^B\bigr).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Every \(V_B\) is nonzero.
For one atom let \(H=G_B\) be its setwise stabilizer, with its induced signed action on \(V_B\).
Define
\[
 b_0=\min_{0\ne v\in V_B}|H\cdot\{v,-v\}|.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\{v,-v\}\) is an antipodal pair, not a quotient by arbitrary rational scaling.
The minimum is independent of the chosen atom and satisfies \(1\le b_0\le t\).
No independence of exterior logarithms, circuit relation, or prescribed permutation group is assumed.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the multiplicative group generated by the roots of \(P\), and let
\[
 \widetilde\Gamma=
 \{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:y^N\in\mu_\infty\Gamma
                                      \text{ for some }N\ge1\}.
\]
Write \(b(y)\) for the number of exterior conjugates of \(y\), counted as distinct roots of its minimal polynomial.
Then
\[
 \min_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma\text{ nontorsion}\\
                 y\text{ has reciprocal minimal polynomial}}}
                          b(y)=r b_0.                            \tag{4}
\]
This minimum is attained by an ordinary monomial
\[
                  \beta=\prod_{i\in B}x_i^{a_i},
                    \qquad a_i\in\{0,1,-1\},                    \tag{5}
\]
supported on one atom.
Its minimal polynomial is monic integral, irreducible and reciprocal, of degree exactly \(2n b_0\), with exactly \(r b_0\) real exterior roots.
If \(s=|\{i:a_i\ne0\}|\), then
\[
 c=\frac{b_0s}{t}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},\qquad
 0<\log M(\beta)\le c\log M(P)\le b_0\log M(P).
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
The degree and exterior count are both multiplied by the same factor \(b_0/t\le1\).
Squaring \(\beta\) preserves this degree and exterior count, makes all exterior roots positive, and multiplies its logarithmic measure by two.

Given the signed quotient representation (2), the minimum in (3) can be found by the finite search over \(a\in\{0,1,-1\}^B\) whose image in \(V_B\) is nonzero.
This is a finite characterization, not a claim that the quotient representation has been computed for every input.

## Atom projections and the real logarithm map

The roots are nontorsion algebraic units, so the signed action on inverse pairs is defined and transitive.
Let \(\tau\in G\) be complex conjugation at the embedding defining \(E\).
It fixes each root on \(E\) and inverts the chosen root on every neutral pair.
Thus \((1+\tau)/2\) is the coordinate projection onto \(E\).
Its conjugates are the projections onto \(gE\).
They preserve the rational relation space, commute, and their products and complements give every atom projection.
This is precisely the [projection argument in C345v58](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection), including saturation, the direct sum (2), transitivity on atoms, and transitivity of \(G_B\) on the labels within \(B\).
A coordinate class cannot vanish because its root is nontorsion; in particular \(V_B\ne0\).

Let \(\Delta\le G\) be generated by all conjugates of \(\tau\).
On \(\mathbb Q^I\) these are commuting diagonal sign matrices.
Their action on each atom is scalar, with character
\[
                         \chi_B:\Delta\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
Different atoms have different characters: one generating conjugate of \(\tau\) distinguishes their membership patterns.
The same statement holds on \(V_B\) because this quotient is nonzero.
The characters may satisfy many multiplicative relations; independent sign changes are not asserted.

Let
\[
              V_E=\bigoplus_{B\subset E}V_B,
 \qquad \ell:V_E\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad \ell([a])=\sum_{i\in E}a_i\log|x_i|.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
This is a well-defined injective \(\mathbb Q\)-linear map.
To prove injectivity, clear the denominators of a representative supported on \(E\).
If its logarithm is zero, its monomial is real of absolute value one, hence is \(1\) or \(-1\).
It is therefore an actual relation modulo torsion and its class vanishes.
This argument concerns the entire exterior direct sum, not merely each atom separately.
If \(p_E:V\to V_E\) is the atom projection, the logarithm of the modulus of any root monomial of class \(v\) is \(\ell(p_Ev)\), since the other labels are neutral.

Finally \(e<m\) supplies pure inversion on each individual atom.
Send a chosen atom to a neutral atom and conjugate \(\tau\) back.
The resulting element lies in \(G_B\), fixes its labels individually, and acts as \(-I\) on all of \(\mathbb Q^B\).
In particular the \(G_B\)-orbit of every nonzero vector in \(V_B\) contains its negative.
This fact will ensure actual reciprocity in the construction.

## Counting exterior profiles in a reciprocal radical orbit

Take a reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\).
After raising it to a positive power, remove both the radical denominator and the torsion multiplier and write
\[
                             w=y^N\in\Gamma.
\]
The number \(y\) is an algebraic unit: both it and its inverse are integral over the algebraic integers because their \(N\)-th powers are units.
Let \(v\in V\) be the nonzero class of \(w\), and put \(\mathcal O=Gv\).
Reciprocity of \(y\) implies that \(w^{-1}\) is a conjugate of \(w\), hence \(\mathcal O=-\mathcal O\).
Take all conjugates before projecting, and let
\[
 \mathcal X=\{\{u,-u\}:0\ne u=p_E(gv),\ g\in G\}.
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
By (8), different elements of \(\mathcal X\) give different nonzero absolute logarithms.
The symmetry \(\mathcal O=-\mathcal O\) supplies a positive logarithm from each such pair, hence at least one distinct exterior conjugate of \(w\).
Every conjugate of \(w\) lifts to a conjugate of \(y\), and its logarithm is multiplied by \(N\).
Consequently
\[
                              |\mathcal X|\le b(y).              \tag{10}
\]

For each exterior atom \(B\), let \(\mathcal A_B\) be the set of nonzero antipodal component classes
\[
                  \{p_B(gv),-p_B(gv)\},\qquad g\in G.
\]
This set is nonempty: move any nonzero component of \(v\) to \(B\) by transitivity on atoms.
It is invariant under the full \(G_B\), because the component of \(hgv\) on \(B\), for \(h\in G_B\), is \(h p_B(gv)\).
It does not matter that \(h\) might move other exterior atoms outside \(E\).
Thus the definition of \(b_0\) gives
\[
                         |\mathcal A_B|\ge b_0.                  \tag{11}
\]

A profile of an element of \(\mathcal X\) records its nonzero atom support \(S\subseteq\{B:B\subset E\}\) and its component antipodal class on each supported atom.
It forgets the relative signs of these components.
Fix one profile with \(|S|=k\ge1\) and one tuple realizing it.
The group \(\Delta\) preserves this profile, and its orbit on the tuple modulo a common sign has size equal to the order of the relative-sign image
\[
 D_S=\operatorname{im}\left(
 \Delta\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}^{S}/\{\text{common sign}\}
                         \right).
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
The action of this image on that tuple is free because every supported component is nonzero.
Choose \(B_*\in S\).
The \(k\) characters \(\chi_B\chi_{B_*}\), for \(B\in S\), factor through \(D_S\) and remain distinct, including the trivial character at \(B_*\).
A finite elementary abelian two-group has as many distinct sign characters as elements, as follows by choosing a basis over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Therefore \(|D_S|\ge k\).
Every profile has at least as many global antipodal tuples as its support size, without any independent-sign assumption.

Sum this bound over all profiles.
Every pair consisting of an exterior atom and a member of \(\mathcal A_B\) occurs in at least one profile.
Hence
\[
 |\mathcal X|\ge\sum_{\text{profiles}}|S|
       \ge\sum_{B\subset E}|\mathcal A_B|
       \ge r b_0.
                                                               \tag{13}
\]
Equations (10) and (13) prove the lower bound in (4).

<a id="attainment"></a>
## An exact signed lift of a minimum orbit

Fix an atom \(B\), put \(U=\mathbb Q^B\), and let \(\pi:U\to V_B\) be the quotient map.
Choose a vector \(0\ne v\in V_B\) attaining (3).
Its antipodal-pair stabilizer is
\[
 J=\{h\in H:hv\in\{v,-v\}\},\qquad [H:J]=b_0.
\]
There is a character \(\lambda:J\to\{1,-1\}\) determined by \(hv=\lambda(h)v\).
For a coordinate vector \(e_i\in U\), form the integral vector
\[
                   u_i=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda(j)j e_i.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
At least one \(\pi(u_i)\) is nonzero.
Indeed the same averaging operator on \(V_B\) sends \(v\) to \(|J|v\), and the images of the coordinate vectors span \(V_B\).
Fix such an \(i\) and write \(u=u_i\).
Then \(j u=\lambda(j)u\) for every \(j\in J\).
The vector \(u\) is supported on the \(J\)-orbit of the label \(i\), and all coefficients on that orbit have the same nonzero absolute value, by transitivity and the signed action.
Dividing by this common positive integer gives
\[
 a\in\{0,1,-1\}^B,\qquad \pi(a)=v'\ne0,
                   \qquad ja=\lambda(j)a\quad(j\in J).
                                                               \tag{15}
\]
The antipodal stabilizer of \(v'\) contains \(J\), so its orbit has at most \(b_0\) elements.
Minimality in (3) forces equality, and its antipodal stabilizer is exactly \(J\).
The pure inversion from the end of the atom argument belongs to \(J\) and has \(\lambda=-1\).
Thus \(\lambda\) is nontrivial.

Evaluate the actual monomial \(\beta=\prod_{i\in B}x_i^{a_i}\).
For \(j\in J\), (15) is an equality of exponent vectors before taking the quotient, so
\[
                              j\beta=\beta^{\lambda(j)}.         \tag{16}
\]
There is no unremoved root-of-unity factor in this identity.
Conversely, if \(g\beta=\beta\), its nonzero class supported on \(B\) forces \(g\in H\), then \(g\in J\), and then \(\lambda(g)=1\).
It follows that the actual stabilizer of \(\beta\) in \(G\) is exactly \(\ker\lambda\).
Therefore
\[
 [\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]=[G:\ker\lambda]
                  =2[G:H][H:J]=2n b_0.                           \tag{17}
\]
Equation (16) with \(\lambda=-1\) gives an actual inverse conjugate.
Since \(\beta\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit, its minimal polynomial is monic integral, irreducible and reciprocal.

There are exactly \(b_0\) inverse pairs of these conjugates supported on each atom.
Different antipodal exponent classes in that atom give different inverse pairs, and different atoms give different nonzero classes in (2).
On a neutral atom every value has modulus one.
On an exterior atom every value is real, and (8) makes its logarithm nonzero.
Exactly one member of each inverse pair is exterior.
Thus \(b(\beta)=r b_0\), establishing equality in (4).

The same stabilizer argument shows that two conjugates with torsion quotient must already be equal: equality of their classes forces their quotient automorphism into \(\ker\lambda\), where (16) is exact.
In particular squaring does not merge any conjugates.
The assertions concerning \(\beta^2\) now follow from its positive real exterior values and the root-product formula for measure.

## Uniform support incidence and the measure bound

Let \(s\) be the support size of \(a\) in (15).
The \(b_0\) antipodal exponent vectors in its \(H\)-orbit all have this support size.
Their antipodal stabilizer in the original signed permutation space is also exactly \(J\): it contains \(J\) by (15), and any extra stabilizer would give an extra stabilizer of \(v'\).
Thus these are precisely \(b_0\) distinct shapes with coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\).
Because \(H\) is transitive on the \(t\) labels, each label occurs in the same number \(c\) of their supports.
Counting incidences gives \(tc=b_0s\).
Their supports cover every label, so \(c\ge1\), proving the integrality assertion in (6).

On an exterior atom \(B'\), transport these shapes and choose one exponent vector from each antipodal pair.
The representatives on \(E\) were chosen exterior, so \(\log|x_i|>0\) there.
The contribution of this atom to \(\log M(\beta)\) is consequently bounded by
\[
 \sum_{\text{shapes }a'}\left|\sum_{i\in B'}a'_i\log|x_i|\right|
       \le c\sum_{i\in B'}\log|x_i|.
                                                               \tag{18}
\]
Summing over exterior atoms proves (6).
For the bound \(b_0\le t\), take the nonzero class of one original coordinate vector.
Its \(H\)-orbit modulo sign has at most \(t\) members because \(H\) permutes the original labels with signs.
The averaging argument also proves the finite search assertion after (6).

## Exact logarithmic rank for each output orbit

There is a complementary invariant that does not require \(e<m\).
Allow also \(e=m\), retain (1)–(2), and take any reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\).
As above clear its radical and torsion to obtain \(w=y^N\in\Gamma\), with class \(v\), and set
\[
                            W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}(Gv).
\]
Every atom projector is a product of operators \((1+g\tau g^{-1})/2\) or their complements.
It preserves the \(G\)-submodule \(W\), hence
\[
 W=\bigoplus_B W_B,\qquad W_B=p_BW\subset V_B.
\]
Transitivity on atoms makes all these spaces have the same positive dimension \(d_0\).
Applying the injective map (8) gives the exact identity
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}
       \{\log|\sigma(y)|:\sigma\}
       =r d_0.                                                   \tag{19}
\]
Indeed the span of the projected orbit is \(p_EW=\bigoplus_{B\subset E}W_B\), and the logarithms of conjugates of \(y\) differ from those of \(w\) by the common factor \(1/N\), with equal restriction multiplicities.
Reciprocity makes every nonzero conjugate logarithm the positive or negative of an exterior logarithm.
There are only \(b(y)\) exterior conjugates, so
\[
                b(y)\ge r d_0\ge r\ge\frac e{\gcd(m,e)}.
                                                               \tag{20}
\]
For the input root itself this is the same block-rank identity used for the compressed input in [C345v67](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#real).
Here it applies to the submodule generated by every possible reciprocal radical output.
Each \(W_B\) is a nonzero \(G_B\)-submodule of \(V_B\).
In particular, if this rational atom representation is irreducible of dimension \(\rho\), every such output has logarithmic rank exactly \(r\rho\) and at least \(r\rho\) exterior conjugates.
In general the orbit submodule need not be irreducible, and its actual dimension \(d_0\), rather than the full dimension of \(V_B\), is required in (19).

If \(\gcd(m,e)=1\), then \(t=1\), and (20) says that no reciprocal output in the radical saturation has fewer than \(e\) exterior conjugates.
The original root attains \(e\), so this bound is sharp.
Under \(e<m\), it also follows from (4), since \(b_0=1\) on a singleton atom.
The rank inequality remains valid in the totally real case \(e=m\); the general attainment proof above is only claimed when a neutral pair supplies pure atom inversion.

## One-dimensional atom representations and low-exterior compression

Under \(e<m\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(b_0=1\).
2. \(V_B\) contains a one-dimensional rational \(G_B\)-subrepresentation.
3. Some reciprocal nontorsion output in \(\widetilde\Gamma\) has exactly \(r\) exterior conjugates.

An invariant rational line has a sign action because the acting group is finite, and therefore gives an antipodal orbit of size one.
The converse is immediate from such an orbit.
The equivalence with the output assertion follows from (4).
In this case the signed average (15) has support all of \(B\), since \(J=H\) is transitive, so \(s=t\) and \(c=1\).
It gives an actual unit of degree \(2m/t\), exactly \(e/t\) real exterior roots, and logarithmic measure at most \(\log M(P)\).
The corresponding orbit submodule has \(d_0=1\) in (19).
Conversely an output with \(d_0=1\) supplies the required invariant atom lines.
Thus the possible dimension-one case in the rank obstruction is genuinely attainable with controlled measure.

More generally, for any positive integer \(b\), a reciprocal nontorsion radical output with at most \(b\) exterior conjugates exists if and only if
\[
                                r b_0\le b.                       \tag{21}
\]
Whenever it exists, (5) supplies such an output with
\[
                             \log M(\beta)\le b\log M(P).
                                                               \tag{22}
\]
For a sequence of supplied inputs with measures tending to one and a fixed target count \(b\), this is a bounded-cost compression exactly when (21) holds at each selected input.
The theorem supplies neither those shrinking inputs nor automatic satisfaction of (21).

## Comparison and the reciprocal boundary

The [C345v58 decomposition](Yin_C_G0345_v58.md#projection) is the essential structural premise.
The [two-exterior analysis](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations) treats the initial two-label relation and matching cases.
The [four-label quotient construction](Yin_C_G0345_v59.md#conjugates) already obtains actual reciprocal outputs by enumerating the signed orbit in its specified rank-two dihedral quotient, with a stronger measure estimate in that class.
The [input rank formula](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#real) and its fixed-rank Mahler bound do not determine the smallest exterior count of every submodule generated by an output.
The new count uses the profiles of all exterior atom projections, where the distinct diagonal characters replace a false assumption of independent atom signs.

The [rank-one radial reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v65.md#statement) and its [two-nonreal-root construction](Yin_C_G0345_v66.md#statement) concern a different signature and reflection-system mechanism.
The [positive-radical minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) computes measure and degree among radicals of one specified positive unit, rather than exterior counts of the root-generated radical group.
Those results are comparisons, not premises of (4).

For a real exterior circuit, there is one exterior atom and its quotient is the coordinate-sum-zero representation after the orientations in [C345v302](Yin_C_G0345_v302.md#setup).
The signed stabilizer acts there by ordinary permutations and common inversion.
Its invariant \(b_0\) is exactly the cut-orbit minimum of [C345v302](Yin_C_G0345_v302.md#cuts).
That circuit theorem is stronger in two ways at its own scope: it permits nonreciprocal radical outputs as well, and its partial-product identities give the sharper circuit-specific costs.
The present theorem removes the circuit and logarithmic-independence assumptions, permits several exterior atoms, and treats their actual signed quotient representations.

Reciprocity of the output is used in (10): absolute nonzero logarithms must each supply a positive conjugate logarithm.
The character profile count alone does not justify that step for an arbitrary nonreciprocal output.
For any sequence of nontorsion outputs in \(\widetilde\Gamma\) with \(M(y)\to1\), the outputs are units as proved above, and [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) makes them reciprocal eventually.
Its exact threshold is the real root \(\theta>1\) of \(X^3-X-1\): \(M(y)<\theta\) suffices.
Only this retained theorem and its actual source-inspection scope are used for that last consequence.
Thus the reciprocal restriction does not exclude any hypothetical vanishing-measure radical output sequence.
No positive Mahler gap for unrestricted inputs or universal compression to one or two exterior roots follows.
