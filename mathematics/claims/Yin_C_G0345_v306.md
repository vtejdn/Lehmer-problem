# Prime trace degree and unbounded exterior counts below the plastic number

The coordinating researcher developed the prime-cycle argument and its application to the five-term trace family.
The dimension bound for a reciprocal conjugate orbit also arose in the parallel study of units in the whole splitting field.

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound and an explicit limiting family

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2p\), where \(p\) is an odd prime, with nontorsion roots.
Write \(R\) for the number of real exterior roots, \(C\) for the number of complex-conjugate pairs of nonreal exterior roots, and \(N=p-R-2C\) for the number of neutral inverse pairs.
A root is exterior when its modulus exceeds one.
Let \(\Gamma\) be the multiplicative group generated by the roots, and let \(\widetilde\Gamma\) consist of numbers for which some positive power is a root of unity times an element of \(\Gamma\).
For a reciprocal nontorsion \(y\in\widetilde\Gamma\), let \(b(y)\) denote the number of exterior roots of its minimal polynomial.

If \(R,N>0\), then every such output satisfies
\[
                       b(y)\ \ge\ R+C.                              \tag{1}
\]
In this case one representative from each inverse pair of \(P\) is multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
More generally, if \(M(P)<(3+\sqrt5)/2\), then (1) holds whenever \(N>0\), even when \(R=0\).
If \(N=0\) at this measure, one still has \(b(y)\ge R+C-1\).
No hypothesis on independent pair inversions is made.

For each odd prime \(p\ge5\), take the five-term polynomial
\[
       P_p(z)=z^{2p}-z^{p+1}-z^p-z^{p-1}+1.                        \tag{2}
\]
It is irreducible of degree \(2p\), has precisely one real exterior root, and its total exterior count \(e_p\) satisfies
\[
                   e_p=p/3+O(1).
\]
In particular
\[
              \inf_{\substack{y\in\widetilde\Gamma_p\ \mathrm{nontorsion}\\
                               y\ \mathrm{reciprocal}}} b(y)
                        \ \ge\ (e_p+1)/2=p/6+O(1).                 \tag{3}
\]
The exterior count of the input itself gives an upper bound \(e_p\) for this infimum.
The measures converge to \(L=\exp I\), where
\[
 I=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}\operatorname{arcosh}
                             \bigl(\tfrac12+\cos t\bigr)\,dt,
              \qquad 1<L<\theta,\qquad \theta^3-\theta-1=0,\quad\theta>1.
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Thus even on a sequence eventually below the plastic number, the finite reduction in the [signed-product theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#statement) can require unboundedly many exterior roots.
For any fixed cutoff \(A>L\), all sufficiently large inputs have \(M(P_p)<A\).
The limiting value is strictly greater than one.

## The dimension visible at the chosen complex embedding

Let \(G\) be the splitting-field Galois group, \(\tau\) complex conjugation, and \(U=\mathbb Q^p\) the signed permutation representation on inverse-pair representatives \(x_i\).
Let \(V\) be the quotient by the rational space of multiplicative relations modulo torsion as in the [complex-conjugation criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v304.md#circle).
For \(w=y^k\in\Gamma\) with \(k>0\) clearing both the radical and its torsion factor, write \(v\in V\) for its nonzero class.
The subspace \(W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q} Gv\) is \(G\)-invariant.
The projection \((1+\tau)/2\) of its orbit spans \(W^\tau\).
Its distinct nonzero projections up to sign, each represented by either of its two vectors, must therefore number at least \(\dim W^\tau\).

The real logarithm of absolute value is a rational-linear functional on exponent classes.
It vanishes on the anti-invariant space and is injective on the \(\tau\)-fixed rational space: if a fixed class has zero logarithm, clear denominators and apply the circle criterion to its monomial to see that its class is also anti-invariant, hence zero.
Distinct nonzero projected classes up to sign thus yield distinct *absolute logarithms*.
The orbit of classes is closed under negation because \(y^{-1}\) is a conjugate of \(y\).
Each such projected pair gives an actual positive logarithm of a conjugate of \(w\), and hence an exterior conjugate of \(y\), by extending an embedding through \(y^k=w\).
Consequently
\[
                             b(y)\ge\dim W^\tau.                    \tag{5}
\]
This reasoning applies to every degree and signature; prime degree will force \(W\) to be large.
On \(U\), complex conjugation fixes each real exterior pair pointwise, inverts every neutral pair, and exchanges two distinct labels for each nonreal exterior conjugate pair.
A signed exchange on two labels has one eigenvalue of each sign since \(\tau^2=1\).
Thus
\[
                              \dim U^\tau=R+C.                      \tag{6}
\]

<a id="prime"></a>
## What prime half-degree forces

The transitive permutation image on \(p\) labels has order divisible by \(p\) and contains a \(p\)-cycle by Cauchy's theorem.
Any signed lift of this cycle has \(p\)-th power \(I\) or \(-I\), as is seen by multiplying the signs around the cycle.
It or its square is therefore an element \(g\) of order \(p\) acting by a \(p\)-cycle on the labels.
The signed permutation representation is faithful on \(G\), since the chosen roots and their inverses generate the splitting field.
On restriction to \(\langle g\rangle\), the vectors \(g^j e_1\) for \(0\le j<p\) form a basis, so \(U\) is its rational regular representation.
It has one trivial summand and one irreducible summand of dimension \(p-1\), by the factorization \((X-1)\Phi_p(X)\) and the irreducibility of \(\Phi_p\).
In particular a decomposition of \(U\) into nonzero proper \(G\)-summands must have a one-dimensional summand.
All invariant subspaces have invariant complements by the signed permutation inner product.

On a \(G\)-invariant rational line, a generator has coefficients of equal nonzero absolute value by transitivity on the labels.
We may scale it to entries in \(\{1,-1\}\).
If \(R,N>0\), \(\tau\) fixes the coordinate at a real exterior label and negates the coordinate at a neutral label.
Such a vector cannot be a \(\tau\)-eigenvector, so \(U\) has no \(G\)-invariant line.
Consequently \(U\) is rationally irreducible.
Since each \(x_i\) is nontorsion, the quotient \(V\) is nonzero and equals \(U\) via the quotient map.
Every nonzero orbit spans it.
This proves independence and (1) by (5)–(6).

For the measure-qualified case, a one-dimensional \(G\)-summand whose image in \(V\) is nonzero would give a nontorsion monomial \(\beta\) from its signed generator.
It has all conjugates in \(\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\}\), since the action on the signed generator is an actual equality of exponent vectors.
A rational unit is torsion, so \(\beta\) would have degree two and reciprocal minimal polynomial \(X^2-aX+1\) with \(|a|\ge3\).
Its measure is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\).
On the other hand \(M(\beta)\le M(P)\) since its unique exterior logarithm is at most \(\sum_i|\log|x_i||=\log M(P)\).
This contradicts the assumed measure bound.
By taking an invariant complement to the relation space, it follows that \(V\) has no one-dimensional summand.
Its nonzero dimension and the restriction to the \(p\)-cycle show that \(V\) is irreducible and has codimension at most one as a quotient of \(U\).
Indeed two summands of dimension at least \(p-1\) would exceed \(p\).
If the relation kernel is a line and there is a neutral label, the generator of this line is nonzero at that label, on which \(\tau\) acts by \(-1\).
Thus passing to \(V\) preserves the entire \(\tau\)-fixed dimension in this case.
In the absence of a neutral label it can lower that dimension by at most one.
Equations (5)–(6) give both claimed bounds.

## Irreducibility and the actual conjugate signature

Let \(C_n\) be the monic Chebyshev polynomial for \(n\ge1\) given by \(C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\).
The recurrence with \(C_0=2,C_1=X\) gives integer coefficients.
Set \(Q_p(X)=C_p(X)-X-1\), so \(P_p(z)=z^pQ_p(z+z^{-1})\).
In characteristic \(p\), \(C_p(X)=X^p\) by the Frobenius identity and the injectivity of substitution \(X=z+z^{-1}\).
Every root \(a\) of \(X^p-X-1\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) has Frobenius iterates \(a^{p^j}=a+j\), and therefore degree exactly \(p\).
The monic degree-\(p\) polynomial \(Q_p\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
This trace reduction is the prime-degree case of the [earlier finite-field calculation](Yin_C_G0539_v1.md#sparse-trace-artin-schreier); its earlier characteristic-zero estimates were made for a different central coefficient.

Put \(S_p=C_p-X\).
For \(t>2\) its derivative is positive, since \(C_p'(2\cosh u)=p\sinh(pu)/\sinh u>1\).
It increases from zero to infinity there, and it is odd.
Thus \(Q_p=S_p-1\) has exactly one real root above two and none below minus two.
It has no root at either endpoint.
On writing \(t=2\cos v\), \(0\le v\le\pi\), a neutral trace solves
\[
                             2\cos(pv)=1+2\cos v .
\]
It requires \(v\ge\pi/3\).
At consecutive grid points \(v=k\pi/p>\pi/3\) the two sides' difference alternates strictly in sign, since \(1+2\cos v\in[-1,2)\).
This gives at least \(2p/3-O(1)\) distinct neutral traces, and at least one for every \(p\ge5\).
In the real embedding furnished by any such trace \(t\in(-2,2)\), \(t^2-4<0\).
It is therefore not a square in \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Adjoining a root of \(z^2-tz+1\) doubles the degree from \(p\) to \(2p\), proving irreducibility of (2).

For the reverse count, the \(p\) roots of \(S_p\) are the two grids \(v=2\pi j/(p-1)\) and \(v=2\pi j/(p+1)\) in \([0,\pi]\), with duplicates removed and interpreted as \(t=2\cos v\).
The grids agree only at \(0,\pi\), since \(\gcd(p-1,p+1)=2\).
They give \(p\) distinct roots as required.
Of these, \(2p/3+O(1)\) lie in \([-2,1]\), by counting equally spaced grid points in \([\pi/3,\pi]\).
The derivative \(S_p'\) has exactly one root between each consecutive pair of roots of \(S_p\), by Rolle's theorem and its degree.
If \(Q_p\) has \(k\) roots in \([-2,1]\), then \(S_p'=Q_p'\) has at least \(k-1\) roots there, and interlacing supplies at least \(k-2\) roots of \(S_p\) in the same interval.
This bounds the neutral count above by \(2p/3+O(1)\).
Every nonneutral trace contributes one exterior inverse-pair label.
Exactly one of these exterior roots is real, so (1) gives (3).

## The fixed two-variable limit and its strict plastic-number comparison

Take the Laurent polynomial \(F(x,y)=y+y^{-1}-1-x-x^{-1}\).
Multiplication by a Laurent monomial changes neither the torus logarithmic measure nor its one-variable specializations' measures.
The [fixed-polynomial specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) applies along \((1,p)\), whose shortest nonzero integral perpendicular vector has maximum norm \(p\).
It gives \(\log M(P_p)\to m(F)\).
The cited theorem is used at the recorded statement level.

For \(x=e^{it}\), integrate first in \(y\).
The monic quadratic \(y^2-(1+2\cos t)y+1\) contributes zero logarithmic measure when \(|1+2\cos t|\le2\).
Otherwise its contribution is \(\operatorname{arcosh}((1+2\cos t)/2)\), since \(1+2\cos t\ge-1\).
Jensen's formula and symmetry give (4), whose integrand is positive on the interior of its interval.
By concavity of \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) above one,
\[
      3I\le\operatorname{arcosh}\left(\tfrac12+
                                  \frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi}\right).
\]
The argument is less than \(27/20\), using \(\pi>31/10,\ \sqrt3<7/4\).
Since \(\theta>13/10\), monotonicity of \(s+s^{-1}\) for \(s>1\) and \(\theta^3=\theta+1\) give
\[
           \cosh(3\log\theta)>
                   \tfrac12(23/10+10/23)>27/20 .
\]
This proves the strict upper bound in (4) without a numerical approximation.

<a id="scope"></a>
## What the family decides

For every fixed \(B\) and every \(A>L\) the prime family eventually has \(M(P_p)<A\) and admits no reciprocal nontorsion radical output with at most \(B\) exterior conjugates.
This includes cutoffs \(A\) strictly below the plastic number.
By [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), any nontorsion radical output whose own measure is below \(\theta\) is already reciprocal.
Thus (3) also applies when that measure condition replaces an explicit reciprocity hypothesis on the output.
No bound for arbitrary nonreciprocal large-measure outputs is inferred.

The family approaches the fixed positive logarithmic measure \(I\).
A uniform compression principle restricted to input measures in a smaller neighborhood of one remains possible, as does a direct gap for inputs with large exterior count.
The proof here uses the prime-cycle representation, the given signatures and the subplastic measure calculation together.
It assumes neither a full signed permutation group nor independent inverse-pair flips in this family.
