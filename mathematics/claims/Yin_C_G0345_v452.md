# Coprime rows at the sharp modular precision with prescribed endpoint blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## The unknown middle coefficients

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit whose positive powers all generate its field of degree \(d\).
Write \(P_1,P_2\) for the minimal polynomials of \(\delta,\delta^2\) and put \(F=P_1^2P_2^2\).
Their respective logarithmic Mahler measures are \(h>0\) and \(2h\), since squaring preserves degree.
Fix \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) and let \(n\to\infty\) through any positive integers.
For a row of formal width \(b\) write \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y)\), \(G_b(A)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n)\), and \(K_b=\{A\in\mathbb Z[Y]_{\le b}:F\mid G_b(A)\}\).
Call a row of \(K_b\) genuine if \(A(c^n)\ne0\) for every root \(c\) of \(F\).

Prescribe integers \(b=o(n)\), \(k\ge0\) with \(w=b-2k\to\infty\), and integer moduli \(L=L_n\ge2\) satisfying
\[
                      r=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log L}{nw}<\frac hd .
\]
Let \(Q_i\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\delta^{in}\) for \(i=1,2\), put \(D_n=Q_1^2Q_2^2\), and for a nonzero integer \(a\) write \([a]_L=\prod_{p\mid L}p^{v_p(a)}\).
The integer \(a_n=[D_n(1)D_n(-1)]_L\) measures the contribution from primes of the modulus at the two fixed points of inversion.
Set \(g=\limsup(\log a_n)/n\) and \(C=\max(3h,\min(6h,4d(r+g)))\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\) there are a genuine row \(A\in K_b\) and a row \(B\) congruent modulo \(L\) to some row of \(K_b\) such that

* \(\gcd_{\mathbb Q[Y]}(B,B_b^*)=1\), and \(B(c^n)G_b(B)(c)\ne0\) at every root \(c\) of \(F\).
* At each end \(B\) agrees with \(A\) in the first \(k+\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\) coefficients, and \((A(0),A_b^*(0))\ne(0,0)\).
* The normalized log one-norm of \(A\) tends to \(3h\), and
\[
  3h\ \le\ \liminf\frac{\log\|B\|_1}{n}
       \ \le\ \limsup\frac{\log\|B\|_1}{n}
       \ \le\ C.
\]

If \(g=0\), the slope of \(B\) is \(3h\) throughout \(r\le3h/(4d)\).
The condition \(g=0\) holds for moduli supported on any fixed finite set of primes, and also allows certain moving primes [as shown below](#primes).
At each fixed exterior radius the limiting coefficient faces of \(B\) are those of \(A\) by [finite truncation](Yin_C_G0345_v430.md#radii), since their common endpoint pair is nonzero and both normalized log norms are bounded.
Whenever \(8C<\log2\), these rows satisfy the strict budget \(((2nb+1)\|B\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) eventually.
In particular they pass at every strictly subcritical precision \(r<h/d\) if \(g=0\) and \(h\le(\log2)/32\), or for unrestricted moduli if \(h<(\log2)/48\).
Simultaneous reduction at several moduli follows by prescribing their least common multiple.

<a id="upper"></a>
## The upper bound with known ends

Suppose \(A\in K_b\), \(B\bmod L\in K_b\bmod L\), \(P_1\nmid G_b(B)\), and the outer \(k\) coefficients at each end agree.
Then \(B-A=Y^kD\) with \(\deg D\le w\), and \(G_b(B)-G_b(A)=X^{nk}G_w(D)\).
As \(c=\delta\) is a unit, \(G_w(D)(c)\) belongs to \(L\mathbb Z[c]\) and is nonzero.
Taking its field norm as in the [resultant bound](Yin_C_G0345_v445.md#upper) gives
\[
   \log L\le\log\big(2(\|A\|_1+\|B\|_1)\big)+(nw+1)h/d.
\]
Hence \(h/d\) bounds the limiting precision for all pairs with bounded normalized log one-norms.
When \(h\le(\log2)/32\), this bound is sharp even for passing coprime rows with the prescribed ends and moduli that are powers of any chosen prime \(p\).
In fact some such moduli attain the limiting ratio \(h/d\) for each prescribed width sequence.
For each integer \(s\ge2\) take \(L=p^q\) with \(q=\max(1,\lfloor(1-1/s)nwh/(d\log p)\rfloor)\).
On the eventual range for this \(s\) the asserted rows pass and their normalized log norms lie within \(1/s\) of the asserted lower and upper bounds on the appropriate sides.
Choosing increasing cutoffs for these ranges and taking \(s\) successively on the intervening intervals gives the limiting ratio and preserves the strict budget.

<a id="perturbations"></a>
## An interior perturbation missing both factors

At width \(w\) we first obtain \(J_0\bmod L\in K_w\bmod L\) whose signed graph misses both minima, whose two blocks of \(\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\) endpoint coefficients vanish, and whose normalized log norm has upper limit at most \(3h\).
Use the proof of the [sharp precision construction](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#residues) with \(L\) instead of \(2^q\).
The conjugate profiles of the units now subtract \(\log L\) at each of the \(d\) embeddings.
Their sum still has strictly positive margin of order \(nw\), since \(\sum_\sigma|\log|\sigma\delta^i||=2ih\) and the truncation radius is \(w/2-o(w)\).
In the [rounding argument](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#rounding) the real Laurent coefficients are multiplied by \(L\), and the rounded difference is \(MN-LW\) for the same integral target column \(W\).
The boundary errors are still \(o(1)\), the upper slope is \(3h\), and translation gives a coefficientwise lift modulo \(L\) to an exact column.
For each target the two translations used in [signed projection](Yin_C_G0345_v450.md#projection) give the same noncancellation test, since \(L\) is a common nonzero scalar at both conjugate nodes.
Adding the two projected rows gives \(J_0\).
Set \(J=Y^kJ_0\).
Translation multiplies the graph by \(X^{nk}\), so \(J\bmod L\in K_b\bmod L\) and \(G_b(J)(c)\ne0\) at every target.
The one-parameter selection used with the genuine comparison row in the same projection proof gives slope \(3h\) for unrestricted moduli if coprimality is omitted.

<a id="scalar"></a>
## A scalar perturbation with an integral lift

The monic polynomial \(D_n\) is reciprocal of degree \(4d\) with unit constant term, and \(D_n(X^n)\) is divisible by \(F\).
Use the free abelian group \(R_n=\mathbb Z[Y]/(D_n)\), in which \(Y\) is invertible and inversion of \(Y\) is an automorphism.
Let \(l=w-2\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\), \(a=[D_n(1)D_n(-1)]_L\), and \(L'=La^l\).
Among the polynomials of degree at most \(l\) with coefficients between \(0\) and \(T=\lceil(L')^{4d/(l+1)}\rceil\) there are more than \((L')^{4d}\) possibilities.
A nonzero difference \(W\) therefore belongs to \(L'R_n\) and has one-norm at most \((l+1)T\).
One of \(W+W_l^*, W-W_l^*\) is nonzero and lies in the same ideal.
Strip its common formal endpoint zeros by a power of \(Y\) and shorten its width accordingly.

Now divide out all factors \(Y-1\) and \(Y+1\) as integral polynomials.
For each \(p\mid L\), multiplication by \(Y-\sigma\) on \(R_n\otimes\mathbb Z_p\), \(\sigma=\pm1\), has determinant \(\pm D_n(\sigma)\ne0\).
By the integer adjugate, each such division can lose at most \(v_p(D_n(\sigma))\) of the retained \(p\)-adic digits.
There are at most \(l\) divisions, and \(L'\) covers this loss at each \(p\mid L\).
The remaining integral \(H\) thus lies in \(LR_n\), has nonzero constant and top coefficients, and has no zeros at \(1,-1\).
Its reversal has sign plus since a minus sign would force \(H(1)=0\).
By the coefficient bound for divisors and the construction,
\[
 \log\|H\|_1\le \frac{4d}{l+1}\big(\log L+l\log a\big)
                         +O(w+\log(w+1)).
\]
This gives upper slope \(4d(r+g)\).
Alternatively \(H=D_n\) works for every \(L\) once \(l\ge4d\), with \(\log\|D_n\|_1\le4d\log2+6nh\) by multiplicativity of Mahler measure and the power-degree hypothesis.

Take the choice for \(H\) with smaller one-norm and denote its degree by \(m\).
To see the integral lift at the required width, divide \(H=D_nC+LE\) using monic division, with \(C,E\) integral and \(\deg(D_n C)\le\deg H\) whenever \(C\ne0\).
The divisibility of its remainder coefficients by \(L\) follows from \(H\in LR_n\).
With \(t=k+\lfloor(w-m)/2\rfloor\) the polynomial \(Y^tD_n C\) and its width-\(b\) reversal are both divisible by the reciprocal polynomial \(D_n\).
It therefore belongs to \(K_b\) and lifts \(Y^tH\bmod L\).
Put \(K=Y^tH\) and \(e=b-2t-m\in\{0,1\}\).
Then \(K_b^*=Y^e K\), and both ends of \(K\) have zero blocks of length at least \(k+\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\).

<a id="primes"></a>
## The primes dividing the fixed-point values

The condition \(g=0\) holds if each prime dividing both \(L\) and \(D_n(1)D_n(-1)\) belongs to a fixed finite set or divides \(n\).
Indeed \(\lvert D_n(1)D_n(-1)\rvert=\lvert N(\delta^{2n}-1)N(\delta^{4n}-1)\rvert^2\), where \(N\) is the norm of the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\delta)\).
The first norm divides the second.
The [valuation estimate for fixed primes and prime divisors of the exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#bad) bounds the logarithm of the indicated prime part of \(N(\delta^{4n}-1)\) by \(o(n)\).

<a id="comparison"></a>
## Preparing the genuine row at the roots of the reciprocal perturbation

Use the [sharp even-order theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v439.md#statement) at width \(v=\lfloor\sqrt w\rfloor\) to start with a genuine exact signed row of slope \(3h\).
The power-degree hypothesis separates conjugates within each orbit, and the different heights separate the two orbits.
Remove the common initial monomial of this row and its formal reversal, shortening its width by twice the removed order.
As in the [preparation of a genuine row](Yin_C_G0345_v445.md#pencil), remove their entire common multiplicity at roots of \(H\) by dividing by the primitive gcd of the two rows and \(H^{v+1}\).
Its nonzero roots have inversion-invariant multiplicities and it has no root at \(1\), so this divisor is reciprocal with sign plus.
Genuineness ensures that the divisor avoids every \(c^n\), even when \(H(c^n)=0\).
After shortening the width by its degree, division therefore preserves exact signed divisibility by \(F\) and all genuine values.
By multiplicativity of Mahler measure and the coefficient bound for integral divisors the quotient norm is at most \(2^v\) times the original norm.
Pad to width \(b\) by a power of \(1+Y\), costing at most \(2^b=\exp(o(n))\).
The resulting \(A\) is genuine, has a nonzero endpoint pair, and \(A,A_b^*\) have no common zero on \(H\), since \(H(-1)\ne0\).
The even-order lower bound gives \(\log\|A\|_1/n\to3h\).

<a id="pencils"></a>
## Choosing two integer coefficients

For rows of width \(b\) write \(\Delta(U,V)=UV_b^*-U_b^*V\).
We have \(\Delta(A,K)\ne0\), since otherwise \(A_b^*=Y^e A\) and \(G_b(A)(c)=A(c^n)(1+\varepsilon c^{1+ne})\ne0\) at a nontorsion target.
Take \(B_0=A+uJ\) with \(u\) a positive integer.
At each root of \(H\) at most one \(u\) makes both \(B_0,B_{0,b}^*\) vanish, since the corresponding pair of \(A\)-values is nonzero.
At each of the \(2d\) targets at most one \(u\) makes \(B_0(c^n)=0\).
At most one \(u\) makes \(\Delta(B_0,K)\) identically zero, by its nonzero value as a polynomial at \(u=0\).
For every positive \(u\), \(G_b(B_0)(c)=uG_b(J)(c)\ne0\).
There are two admissible choices \(u_1,u_2\le m+2d+3\).
The identity \(u_2(A+u_1J)-u_1(A+u_2J)=(u_2-u_1)A\) lets us choose one with \(\|B_0\|_1\ge\|A\|_1/(2(m+2d+3))\).

Finally put \(B=B_0+zK\).
A common zero of \(B,B_b^*\) must be a root of the nonzero polynomial \(\Delta(B_0,K)\) of degree at most \(2b\).
Where \(K,K_b^*\) do not both vanish, at most one value of \(z\) creates a common zero at that root.
Their simultaneous zeros are confined to \(0\) and the roots of \(H\).
At the former point \(B_0\) has the nonzero endpoint pair of \(A\), and at the latter points the choice of \(u\) prevents a common zero for every \(z\).
The two nonzero target values \(B_0(c^n),G_b(B_0)(c)\) each exclude at most one \(z\) per target.
Choose two surviving positive integers \(z_1,z_2\le2b+4d+2\) and use the same two-choice identity to retain \(\|B\|_1\ge\|B_0\|_1/(2(2b+4d+2))\).
The scalar coefficients cost \(O(\log(b+2))=o(n)\) in the upper norm bound.
Both perturbations lie in \(K_b\bmod L\) after reduction and preserve the stated coefficient blocks, proving all assertions.
