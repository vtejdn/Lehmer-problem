# Short multiplicative relations among roots of a critical six-term section

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded products and relative norms

Fix positive constants \(C,K\), and let \(s\to\infty\) through the [critical six-term sections](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#statement) with parameters \((s,a,b)\).
In each section retain the roots of all nontorsion irreducible rational factors having at most \(Ka\) exterior roots.
For each fixed integer \(H\ge1\), once \(s\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(C,K,H\), every relation
\[
                \prod_{i=1}^n x_i^{m_i}\in\mu_\infty,
                \qquad \sum_i |m_i|\le H
\]
among retained roots cancels completely after identifying inverse pairs and collecting repeated roots.
Here \(\mu_\infty\) denotes the group of roots of unity.
In particular, products of at most \(h\) chosen representatives of distinct inverse pairs, allowing repetitions, determine their multisets uniquely even modulo torsion, for fixed \(h\) and sufficiently large \(s\).
If \(x\) is retained and \(L\subseteq\mathbb Q(x)\) with \([\mathbb Q(x):L]\le H\), then \(N_{\mathbb Q(x)/L}(x)\) is torsion exactly when the relative conjugates of \(x\) form a union of inverse pairs, in which case the norm is one.
For each fixed \(n\), the phases of any \(n\) retained roots from distinct inverse pairs, averaged jointly over embeddings of their compositum, converge to Haar measure on \((S^1)^n\).

<a id="promotion"></a>
## Promotion of a bounded relation to a torus identity

Suppose a sequence of exceptions exists, and collect its multiplicities with respect to inversion so that \(1\le n\le H\), every \(m_i\ne0\), and the \(x_i\) come from distinct inverse pairs.
The retained factors are reciprocal for large \(s\), so both choices of representative satisfy the same section equation.
Let \(\Gamma\) be the subgroup generated by the \(x_i\) modulo torsion and choose lifts of a basis of this free abelian group.
Write \(x_i=\eta_i t^{v_i}\) using these lifts \(t\), exponent vectors \(v_i\in\mathbb Z^r\), and roots of unity \(\eta_i\).
The [separation of rational powers](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#statement) makes the nonzero vectors \(v_i\) pairwise nonproportional over \(\mathbb Q\).

Put \(R_s=-g_s/B_s\) with \(g_s(T)=T^s-T-1\) and \(B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\).
Writing \(\xi=\prod x_i^{m_i}\), the section equations give \(\prod R_s(x_i^a)^{m_i}=\xi^b\).
After clearing denominators this equality is a signed sum of at most \(2\cdot3^H\) monomials in the \(x_i\) with roots of unity as coefficients, and each monomial has total degree at most \(Has\).
The [height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v510.md#statement) gives \(h(x_i)=O(1/b)\), while the [critical scales](Yin_C_G0345_v507.md#scales) give \(as/b\to0\) and an off-circle fraction \(O(s^{-2})\) for each \(x_i\).

Replace each \(x_i\) formally by \(\eta_i T^{v_i}\) and group the resulting terms by their Laurent exponent vectors.
At every embedding where all \(x_i\) are on the unit circle the sum of phases of the original signed terms is zero.
The exceptional fraction is \(O_H(s^{-2})\), and each phase has absolute value one, so the average square modulus of the phase sum tends to zero.
Here embeddings are counted equally in a number field containing all quantities used in the substitutions.
For distinct exponent groups each quotient of original terms is a nontorsion algebraic unit of height \(O_H(as/b)=o(1)\).
Its mean phase tends to zero by the [small-height orbit argument](Yin_C_G0345_v527.md#orthogonality), so all cross terms between different groups contribute \(o(1)\) in total.
Within a group the terms share a common phase factor at every embedding and their remaining coefficients are roots of unity.
The mean square modulus of their coefficient sum is at least one whenever the sum is nonzero, by its nonzero integral norm and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
Consequently all Laurent coefficient sums vanish for sufficiently large \(s\), giving the rational function identity
\[
                \prod_i R_s(\eta_i^a T^{a v_i})^{m_i}=\xi^b.
\]

<a id="divisors"></a>
## Divisors in distinct character directions

A common root \(z\) of \(g_s,B_s\) would satisfy \(z^s=1+z\) and \(z^2+z+1=0\) by the [section graph calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v517.md#argument).
Then \(z\) has order three but \(1+z=-z^2\) is not a cube root of unity, which is impossible.
Every root of either polynomial is nonzero.
Over \(\mathbb C\), a zero or pole of \(R_s(\eta_i^a T^{a v_i})\) therefore gives an irreducible Laurent factor \(T^{w_i}-c\), with \(w_i\) a primitive vector on the line of \(v_i\) and \(c\ne0\).
Indeed a unimodular coordinate change sends the primitive character to one coordinate and the substituted numerator and denominator then split into linear factors in that coordinate without cancellation.
Binomials from different lines cannot be associates, since multiplying by a Laurent monomial and a constant preserves the difference of their two exponent vectors up to sign.
Each nonzero \(m_i\) therefore leaves an uncancelled divisor, contradicting the identity.
The bounded-product and relative-norm assertions follow by applying the relation statement to the quotient of the two products or to the list of relative conjugates.
For the joint limit, each fixed nonzero Fourier character evaluates on the phase tuple as the phase of a nontorsion unit for all sufficiently large \(s\), by the same relation statement.
Its height tends to zero by the triangle inequality, and its phase average tends to zero by the same orbit argument.
Trigonometric approximation now gives the joint Haar limit.
