# Concentration near exterior edge roots and a gap for torsion-translated sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## A necessary pair of oriented edges at every signature

Let \(F\) be a fixed primitive integral Laurent polynomial, irreducible over the rationals, with two-dimensional Newton polygon.
Suppose \(z\) runs through roots of \(F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0\) with positive logarithmic measures \(m=m(z)\to0\), allowing arbitrary roots of unity \(\xi,\eta\) and positive or negative integer exponents \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\).
Put \(N=\max(|a|,|b|)\) and \(d=\deg z\).
Every limiting normalized direction must expose opposite parallel edges, and on a subsequence one can orient a fixed primitive tangent character so that its exponent \(k>0\).
Write \(U=\omega z^k\) for this character, where \(\omega\) is a root of unity.
In coordinates \((U,V)\) completing this character to a basis and pointing the exponent of \(V\) toward the upper edge, write
\[
                   F=\sum_{j=0}^J B_j(U)V^j
\]
up to a monomial, with \(B_J B_0\ne0\), \(V=\omega' z^{b'}\), and \(b'\asymp_F N>0\).
After passing to the subsequence,
\[
              1/m\ll_F k\ll_F N m^2,\qquad s=b'/k\to\infty.             \tag{1}
\]
The top polynomial \(B_J\) has a zero of modulus greater than one, and the bottom polynomial \(B_0\) has a nonzero zero of modulus less than one.
The two face polynomials are primitive.
Hereafter a monomial power of \(U\) may be removed from each face polynomial when discussing its nonzero zeros.

More precisely let \(Z_+\) be the set of exterior zeros of \(B_J\).
For embeddings of a common number field containing \(z,\xi,\eta\), put \(Y=b'\log^+|\sigma z|\).
There are constants \(c,C>0\) and a set of embeddings \(E_+\) with \(|\sigma z|>1\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
     &\operatorname{dist}(\sigma U,Z_+)\le C e^{-cs}
                                  &&(\sigma\in E_+),\\
     &c s\le Y\le C s             &&(\sigma\in E_+),\\
     &\mathbb E\big[Y^2\mathbf1_{\sigma\notin E_+}\big]\le C,\qquad
       \frac{c}{s^2m^2}\le \mathbb P(E_+)\le\frac{C}{s}.                \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]
The average counts all embeddings with their original multiplicities.
Reciprocating \(U,V,z\) gives the corresponding estimates for interior conjugates near nonzero interior zeros of \(B_0\).
In particular the exceptional disks carry a fraction tending to one of the exterior squared radial moment and of the analogous interior squared moment.

If no pair of opposite exposed edges admits one common orientation with both prescribed zeros and both primitive edge polynomials, there is \(c_F>0\) such that \(m(z)\ge c_F\) for every nontorsion root of every torsion-translated section.
This includes every such \(F\) whose edge polynomials have all their nonzero zeros on the unit circle.
In particular it applies if every edge consists of its two endpoints with coefficients of equal modulus.
The [untwisted degree and edge theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) treats ordinary sections; the argument below controls independent external phases and identifies the source of the diverging squared moments along a putative shrinking sequence.
The [archimedean limiting-law criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v774.md#statement) gives a separate edge-measure equality when almost all conjugates are neutral.

<a id="coordinates"></a>
## End gaps and the short character

The [torsion-conductor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) forces the primitive exponent norm to tend to infinity.
The [individual height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement) then gives \(Nh(z)\asymp_F1\), so \(d\asymp_F Nm\).
The roots are eventually reciprocal algebraic units by [Smyth's gap](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) and the leading and constant coefficient test.
Write \(t_\sigma=\log|\sigma z|\).
For the full orbit the [squared logarithm bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) says \(d^2\mathbb E t_\sigma^2\ge1/10\); reciprocity gives equal positive and negative contributions.

Fix a limit of \((a,b)/N\).
If its maximizing face is a single vertex, the largest projected exponent is separated from all others by \(\gg N\) on this subsequence.
At every exterior embedding the triangle inequality with bounded coefficient length and unit-modulus phases gives \(N t_\sigma\ll1\).
This contradicts \(N^2\mathbb E(t_\sigma)_+^2\gg_F1/m^2\).
The minimizing face must also be an edge by the same calculation for the interior conjugates.
Choose a primitive tangent and a complementary lattice character; choose the first sign so that its exponent \(k\) is positive on a subsequence, and the second sign so that the normal exponent \(b'\) is positive.
Then \(k=o(N)\) and \(b'\asymp_F N\).
If \(k=0\) infinitely often, the primitive direction on those indices is constant, contrary to the primitive-norm conclusion.
After a monomial shift all \(B_j\) are ordinary polynomials of bounded degree and \(B_0 B_J\ne0\).
The [finite-place width condition](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) makes each exposed face primitive on this subsequence.
For that application the powered unit pairs leave finite sets since their primitive directions grow.

<a id="tails"></a>
## A uniform square-moment bound away from exterior edge zeros

Write \(D=\max_j\deg B_j\).
Choose disjoint fixed small disks about the exterior zeros of \(B_J\), contained in \(1+\delta<|U|<C_0\) for some positive \(\delta,C_0\).
Define \(E_+\) by membership in these disks at exterior embeddings.
For all exterior conjugates the equation gives
\[
 |B_J(\sigma U)|\le C_F \exp(-(b'-Dk)t_\sigma)\le C_F e^{-Y/2}
\]
for sufficiently large \(s\), by bounding all lower terms, since \(|\sigma V|=e^Y\) and \(|\sigma U|=e^{Y/s}\).
Inside the disks \(Y=s\log|\sigma U|\asymp_F s\).
The displayed inequality and factorization of the fixed polynomial imply distance \(O_F(e^{-c_Fs})\) to its set of exterior zeros, including multiplicities.

On exterior embeddings outside those disks, all factors from zeros off the unit circle have modulus bounded below by a fixed positive constant (and actually grow at infinity).
Consequently for large \(Y\) the point \(\sigma U\) is within \(C e^{-cY}\) of a zero of \(B_J\) on the unit circle; if there is no such zero, \(Y\) is bounded there.
Since \(e^{Y/s}-1\ge Y/s\), this also gives \(Y\ll_F\log(2s)\) outside the disks.
It remains to control the proportion approaching those circle zeros.
The estimate must use the full orbit of \(U=\omega z^k\), since the translating phase may vary over a field of arbitrarily large degree.

Here \(h(U)=k h(z)\ll_F1/s\).
Write \(D_U=[\mathbb Q(U):\mathbb Q]\); the [degree-height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) implies \(h(U)\gg D_U^{-2}\) uniformly for nontorsion \(U\), so \(D_U\gg_F\sqrt{s}\).
At each of the finitely many circle zeros use a cutoff equal to one in the disk of radius \(\rho=\max(Ce^{-cT},s^{-1/8})\) and zero outside the concentric disk of radius \(2\rho\), linear between them.
For \(T\) and \(s\) sufficiently large the angular mean of this cutoff is \(O(\rho)\), its global Lipschitz constant is \(O(1/\rho)\), and its planar Dirichlet integral is \(O(1)\) by scaling in two real dimensions.
The [compact-test discrepancy estimate](../audits/literature/Pri11/Yin_U002.md#pri11-u002) for the primitive minimal polynomial of \(U\), at smoothing radius \(\varepsilon=s^{-1/4}\), bounds its orbit mean by
\[
   O_F\!\left(\rho+\varepsilon/\rho+
               \sqrt{h(U)+\log(1/\varepsilon)/D_U+\varepsilon}\right)
       \ll_F e^{-cT}+s^{-1/9}.
\]
Here the discriminant term only improves the upper bound, and absolute embeddings repeat each conjugate of \(U\) equally.
This bounds the probability of \(Y>T\) outside \(E_+\).
Integration against \(2T\,dT\) up to \(C_F\log(2s)\) proves the uniform square-moment bound in (2).
The calculation adapts the [circle-zero estimates for one free long exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v168.md#angular) to a short character whose power and torsion multiplier both vary.

<a id="cost"></a>
## The squared moment forces exterior and interior roots

Reciprocity and the squared logarithm bound give \(\mathbb E Y^2\gg_F1/m^2\).
After the bounded complementary contribution is removed, the remaining second moment is still \(\gg_F1/m^2\) and is supported where \(Y\asymp_F s\).
This proves nonemptiness, the lower probability bound in (2), and the limiting share assertion.
On \(E_+\), \(\log^+|\sigma U|\ge\log(1+\delta)\), so its probability is \(O_F(h(U))=O_F(1/s)\).
Together the two bounds give \(k\ll_F Nm^2\).
Also \(k t_\sigma=\log|\sigma U|\gg_F1\) there, whereas \(t_\sigma\le m\) because \(z\) is a unit.
This gives \(k\gg_F1/m\).
Apply the same reasoning at exterior embeddings of \(1/z\), taking \(1/U,1/V\) as coordinates and reversing the polynomial.
The new top polynomial is the reversal of \(B_0\), and the two powers remain \(k,b'\), giving the stated common orientation.
The uniform gap follows by contradiction from the absence of any limiting direction satisfying these necessary conditions.
