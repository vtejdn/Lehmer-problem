## 1. The candidate and its exact scope

Let P be a monic irreducible polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit alpha, K=Q(alpha), O=O_K, degree d, and m=log M(P)>0.
Put

    C_n=|Norm(alpha^n-1)|,       D_n=|Norm(Phi_n(alpha))|.

These are nonzero positive integers.
Define the end-seeded ideal set

    S_end={prime ideals q of O : q divides (alpha-1)(alpha+1)}.

For each n>=1, its complete actual contribution is

    A_end(n)=sum_(q in S_end)
                   f_q ord_q(alpha^n-1) log p_q,
    B_end(n)=[A_end(n)-d log2]/n.                         (1)

The valuation is normalized by ord_q(q)=1 and Norm(q)=p_q^f_q.
L G0074 gives m>=B_end(n).
Every certified lower bound using only these ideals, at this same exponent, is at most A_end(n), even if it uses both signs, several initial exponents, all prime-power lifts, and exact higher valuation layers.

This is a restriction on IDEALS, not merely on rational primes.
Another ideal above a rational prime already represented in S_end is outside the restriction unless it itself divides an end value.
Such new ideals can have different residue orders and new mass.
Nor does (1) restrict primitive contributions at other rational primes in a cyclotomic resultant.
Those are precisely possible new inputs beyond the mechanism being tested.

For context, reduction of the elementary cyclotomic identities gives

    Phi_(p^j)(t)=(t-1)^(phi(p^j)) modulo p,
    p divides D_(p^j)  iff  p divides P(1),       j>=1.
                                                               (2)

For odd p the analogous identity at the negative end is

    Phi_(2p^j)(t)=(t+1)^(phi(p^j)) modulo p,
    p divides D_(2p^j) iff p divides P(-1).              (3)

Indeed Phi_(p^j)=(t^(p^j)-1)/(t^(p^(j-1))-1), and Frobenius gives its displayed polynomial reduction.
For odd p replace t by -t.
A resultant over F_p vanishes exactly when its two monic polynomials have a common root in the algebraic closure, which proves the divisibility equivalences.
These are tests for the INDEX prime p only; they say nothing against other prime divisors of D_(p^j) or D_(2p^j).
They supply no automatic d-fold scalar congruence when just one ideal returns.

The retained Lehmer control in L G0079 has P(1)=-1 and P(-1)=1, so its S_end is empty and (1) supplies zero mass at every n.
That same fixed record has D_4=9: the prime 3 in that layer is a new return ideal, not a consequence of a prime-2 end seed.
This is a previously proved exact control, not a new numerical run.
It does not rule out an arithmetic theorem on a smaller measure window excluding that input.

## 2. An actual infinite irreducible family with a single ramified seed

Use exactly the family already proved in L G0131:

    f=t^3-t-1,        f*=1-t^2-t^3,
    P_N=t^N f+f*,
    N=9 modulo 120,   N>=9,       d=N+3.                 (4)

Each P_N itself is the monic irreducible Salem minimal polynomial; there is no cyclotomic padding or selected-factor ambiguity.
Its degree is even and unbounded.
Its measures tend to the plastic number above one, so this is not asserted to be a near-one family.
All norm and ideal statements below concern this actual P_N and its maximal order, not a reducible delay presentation.

For any root alpha of P_N, direct evaluation gives

    P_N(1)=-2,          P_N(-1)=2,
    P_N(i)=4,           P_N(-i)=4,
    C_1=2,              C_2=4,              C_4=64.       (5)

For the middle two values use N=1 modulo 4, f(i)=-1-2i and f*(i)=2+i; conjugation gives the other.
The norms follow by multiplying P_N over the first, second or fourth roots of unity and taking absolute values.
In particular C_4=|-2 times 2 times 4 times 4|=64.

The ideal (alpha-1) has norm two, so it is a single prime ideal q with Norm(q)=2 and residue degree f_q=1.
Since 2 belongs to q, alpha+1=(alpha-1)+2 belongs to q as well.
Its principal ideal also has norm two, hence

    (alpha-1)=(alpha+1)=q,       S_end={q}.               (6)

This identifies the whole seed set, including any possible ramification.
In particular one must not count the two signs as distinct ideals.

Here is a direct check of the ramification index e_q, without a maximal-order computation or an unramified lifting assumption.
Let u be an indeterminate.
Since N=1 modulo 8, in F_2[u] one has (1+u)^N=1+u modulo u^8.
Also

    f(1+u)=1+u^2+u^3 modulo 2,
    f*(1+u)=1+u+u^3 modulo 2.

Thus

    P_N(1+u)=u^2+u^3+u^4 modulo (2,u^8).                (7)

In particular its quadratic coefficient is odd.
Reciprocity and P_N(1)=-2 give P_N'(1)=(d/2)P_N(1)=-d.
Since d=4 modulo 8, v_2(d)=2.
Write pi=alpha-1, so ord_q(pi)=1 by (6), and e=ord_q(2).
In the equation P_N(1+pi)=0, the respective valuations are

    constant term: e,
    linear term:   2e+1,
    quadratic term: 2,
    every higher term: at least 3.

If e=1 the constant has uniquely least valuation.
If e>=3 the quadratic term has uniquely least valuation.
Both contradict vanishing of the sum.
Therefore

    e_q=2,        f_q=1.                                (8)

This also explains the double root at 1 in the mod-2 reduction, but no inference of good reduction has been made.

## 3. Every common exponent, including the exceptional initial lift

Equations (5)--(6) give

    ord_q(alpha-1)=1,        ord_q(alpha^2-1)=2.

Since C_4=64, every prime ideal dividing alpha^4-1 lies above 2.
At such a prime ideal its residue satisfies x^4=1 in characteristic two, hence (x-1)^4=0 and x=1.
That ideal must divide alpha-1, so by (6) it is q.
Its residue norm is two.
Consequently

    (alpha^4-1)=q^6,          ord_q(alpha^4-1)=6.         (9)

This is a real exceptional initial lift; it must not be replaced by a good-prime automatic floor.

If x=1+y and ord_q(y)=s>2, then e_q=2 gives

    ord_q(2y)=s+2<2s=ord_q(y^2).

The uniquely least term in x^2-1=2y+y^2 therefore proves that each subsequent squaring adds exactly two to the integer valuation.
Starting with (9), for k>=2 one obtains 6+2(k-2)=2k+2.
For any odd positive integer v and x=alpha^(2^k),

    x^v-1=(x-1)(1+x+...+x^(v-1)).

The second factor is v=1 in O/q, so it is a q-unit.
Thus it does not change the valuation.
For EVERY n>=1, put k=v_2(n).
The exact result is

    ord_q(alpha^n-1)=
         1               if k=0,
         2               if k=1,
         2k+2            if k>=2,
    A_end(n)=ord_q(alpha^n-1) log2.                      (10)

Odd prime-power multipliers of n do not increase this mass.
The formula already incorporates every possible higher depth at the single allowed ideal, at arbitrary common multiples.

It also spells out exactly how cyclotomic layers may be added.
From their polynomial factorizations, their q-valuations are

    ord_q Phi_1(alpha)=1,
    ord_q Phi_2(alpha)=1,
    ord_q Phi_4(alpha)=4,
    ord_q Phi_(2^j)(alpha)=2,             j>=3.          (11)

All other cyclotomic indices have zero q-valuation, by the exact residue-order-one local layer formula of L G0078.
Summing (11) over the divisors of n gives precisely (10).
The layers are legitimately additive as disjoint valuation increments in the factorization alpha^n-1=product_(r|n)Phi_r(alpha), not as several independent copies of the complete end valuation.

For pure powers n=2^k, this is also the complete rational-2 part of C_n: an ideal over 2 dividing alpha^(2^k)-1 must have residue alpha=1 and hence be q.
For arbitrary n with an odd factor, other ideals above 2 may have other residue orders dividing n.
They are outside S_end and are NOT bounded by (10).
Odd rational prime divisors of C_n are likewise unrestricted.
No all-prime or all-ideal norm bound is asserted.

## 4. Exact all-n optimum and the unavoidable exponent cost

Write d=2D, so D>=6.
If k=0 or 1 the numerator of B_end(n) in (1) is negative.
For k>=2 it is

    (2k+2-d)log2.

It is zero at k=D-1, and positive precisely when k>=D.
For a positive numerator and n=2^k v with v odd, the largest certificate at that k is attained uniquely at v=1.
Write k=D+a, a>=0.
At that exponent the certificate is

    B_end(2^(D+a))=2(a+1)log2/2^(D+a).                   (12)

The sequence (a+1)/2^a takes its maximum one at a=0 and a=1; thereafter it decreases strictly, since (a+2)/[2(a+1)]<1 for a>=1.
Negative or zero values cannot improve this positive maximum.
Hence the complete all-exponent optimization within the stated seed set is

    max_(n>=1) B_end(n)=2^(1-d/2)log2,
    attaining exponents exactly 2^(d/2), 2^(d/2+1).       (13)

In particular the first positive certificate already requires n>=2^(d/2).
The positive rate furnished by this mechanism tends to zero exponentially along actual irreducible inputs of unbounded degree; this is an upper bound on the restricted certificate, not an upper bound on their Mahler measures.

The failure also holds in the exact mass/time form of L G0074.
Whenever B_end(n)>0, k>=D and

    n/A_end(n)>=2^k/[(2k+2)log2]
               >=2^D/[(2D+2)log2].                     (14)

The latter sequence is increasing for k>=1.
Its right side diverges with degree.
If the stronger target A_end(n)>=(1+epsilon)d log2 is imposed for any fixed epsilon>0, one additionally needs k>=ceil((1+epsilon)D-1).
In particular there is no common constant C for n<=C A_end(n) along this family, even just after demanding a positive generic surplus.

## 5. What this screens, and what must be new next

This is an exact structural screen of the P(1)/P(-1)-seeded partial-return mechanism.
It uses one actual ramified prime ideal and its full depths, not a scalar-return requirement, good-prime approximation or finite scan.
Repeating prime-power lifts or adding the same cyclotomic layers cannot exceed (10), and the exact all-n optimum (13) already pays their exponent cost.

The family is the same adopted plastic-limit control used in L G0131; no optimization of its successful triple-return mechanism is proposed.
At that triple return n=3N, the present seed mass is only log2 because N is odd.
On L G0131's selected successful subsequence, the established linear full norm mass therefore comes from ideals outside S_end.
Those additional ideals may lie above an already used rational prime; no rational-prime novelty is inferred.
This distinguishes the new input needed from merely retaining more depth at the existing end seed.

Neither this family nor the earlier Lehmer control refutes coverage on some smaller sufficiently near-one window.
Formula (13) closes only the explicitly stated automatic end-seed scheme on this bounded-measure, varying-degree family.
A new mechanism must locate and quantify additional actual return ideals or a different joint archimedean estimate, while retaining the same common exponent.
Primitive existence alone, the generic local table, a new seed encoding, and a norm limit with an unknown inverse-m threshold do not supply that information.

No near-one input family is presumed to exist.
