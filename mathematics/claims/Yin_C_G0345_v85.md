# A unique full torsion interlacer over a prescribed cyclotomic subfield

<a id="statement"></a>
## A one-embedding interlacer that loses half the gaps on descent

Fix an odd prime \(p\) and an integer \(e\ge2\) dividing \((p-1)/2\).
Let \(K\) be the unique degree-\(e\) subfield of the real \(p\)-th cyclotomic field.
At conductor \(p^a\), \(a\ge1\), put \(k=\varphi(p^a)/(2e)\), and assume \(k\ge2\).
Choose one orbit over \(K\) of primitive \(p^a\)-th root-of-unity traces and let \(A\) be its monic polynomial of degree \(k\).
For every real Salem number \(\sigma\) of degree \(2(k+1)\) there is a set of positive integers \(n\) of positive natural density with both properties below.

* The trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^n\) is strictly interlaced by \(A\) at the chosen embedding of \(K\).
* Among all monic polynomials of degree \(g\le k\) with distinct torsion trace roots and with coefficient field of degree at most \(e\), the only one partially interlacing this trace polynomial with \(g>\lceil k/2\rceil\) is \(A\) itself.

Partial interlacing means that each open interval cut out by the nodes, including the two outer intervals, contains a trace root.
In particular every rational candidate has degree at most \(\lceil k/2\rceil\) if it interlaces, as does any interlacer working at all embeddings of its coefficient field within the stated degree bound.
There are examples in unbounded degree already with \(p=5,\ e=2,\ K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
Thus even a full one-embedding torsion interlacer over this fixed quadratic field need not give a simultaneous or rational interlacer of bounded defect.
The construction gives no upper bound on \(M(\sigma^n)\).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The field is forced by a single node

A primitive trace \(y=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) at conductor \(p^a\) generates the full real cyclotomic field \(L\) there, of degree \(ek\).
Its Galois group is cyclic by [cyclicity at odd prime powers](../audits/literature/Con26c/Yin_U001.md#con26c-u001) and the identification of inverse pairs in the [primitive trace orbit](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#count).
It has a unique subfield of degree \(e\), namely the indicated \(K\), embedded from conductor \(p\).
The orbit polynomial \(A\) belongs to \(K[x]\), and its coefficients generate \(K\) since the setwise stabilizer of one coset of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) under the regular action is that subgroup.
Its other coefficient embeddings give distinct polynomials.

Suppose a candidate \(C\) of degree \(g\le k\) with coefficient field \(F\) of degree at most \(e\) contains any chosen root \(y\) of \(A\).
Then
\[
        ek=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\le [F(y):\mathbb Q]
                =[F(y):F][F:\mathbb Q]\le g e\le ke .
\]
All inequalities are equalities, hence \(F(y)=L,\ F\subseteq L\), and \([F:\mathbb Q]=e\).
Consequently \(F=K,\ g=k\), and the monic \(C\) is \(A\).
Every node of every candidate, chosen or not, has rational degree at most \(ek\) by the same tower bound.
There are only finitely many torsion traces of degree at most \(ek\), since primitive order \(l\ge3\) has trace degree \(\varphi(l)/2\) and \(\varphi(l)^2\ge l/2\).

<a id="intervals"></a>
## Neutral conjugates on the two sides of isolated nodes

Order the roots of \(A\) as \(-2<y_1<\cdots<y_k<2\).
For \(j=1,\ldots,\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) choose disjoint small open neighborhoods of \(y_{2j-1}\) in \((-2,2)\) containing no other torsion trace of degree at most \(ek\).
Prescribe one neutral trace in the left half and one in the right half of each neighborhood.
When \(k\) is odd, also prescribe one in \((y_{k-1},y_k)\), disjoint from the neighborhoods.
The neighborhoods can be chosen within the two adjacent bins, so these prescriptions place one neutral trace in each of the \(k\) lower bins of \(A\).
The exterior trace supplies its top bin.

Assign the \(k\) distinct prescribed open intervals bijectively to the neutral inverse pairs of \(\sigma\).
By [joint equidistribution of the powered Salem angles](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers), this event has natural density equal to the product of the positive arcsine measures of the intervals.
All powers retain the given degree.
For any such exponent, the \(k+1\) ordered trace roots have \(k\) internal gaps.
The paired prescriptions give \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) different gaps, each wholly inside its corresponding neighborhood.
Its only possible torsion node of degree at most \(ek\) is the distinguished root \(y_{2j-1}\).

Every partially interlacing candidate has at most one node in each internal gap and none outside them, because it needs a trace root between every two nodes and on each outer side.
No node is itself a trace root because neutral powered Salem roots are nontorsion and the exterior trace exceeds two.
Thus a candidate of degree greater than \(k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) must contain one of the distinguished nodes, and the field argument identifies it with \(A\).
Any other conjugate of \(A\) has the same degree but is a different polynomial, so it fails.
A candidate of that degree working at every embedding is therefore impossible.
The [Salem families in each degree \(2(k+1)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v56.md#examples) supply actual inputs for every \(k\) in the construction.
This excludes an automatic bounded-defect passage from a single embedding to the [simultaneous interlacing criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v79.md#statement) across all Salem inputs.
Whether additional small-measure hypotheses can supply such a passage remains open.
