# Bounded Mahler measures over composita of independent splitting fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## Independent alternating factors and the exact spectrum

For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(z\), let \(L_z\) be its rational splitting field, \(\gamma_z\) its [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels), and \(d_z=[\mathbb Q(\gamma_z):\mathbb Q]\).
Write \(P_z\subseteq L_z\) for the normal field of the action on the multiplicative labels of \(\gamma_z\), as in the [two-field comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#statement).
For a finite normal field \(H/\mathbb Q\), set \(\mathcal S(H)=\{z\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,*}:\operatorname{Gal}(H L_z/H)\text{ is solvable}\}\).
This includes the elements of arbitrary finite towers of radical adjunctions over \(H\).
Denote by \(\Sigma_N(H)\) the set of positive logarithmic Mahler measures at most \(N\) attained by elements of \(\mathcal S(H)\).

Fix \(M,N>0\) and take any finite nonempty family \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) with \(0<m(x_i)\le M\).
Assume no conjugate of one input is multiplicatively dependent modulo torsion with a conjugate of another input.
For all \(i\) assume \(d_{x_i}\) sufficiently large in terms of \(S=\max(1,M,N)\) alone.
Let \(u_i,b_i\) be the number and size of the [minimal blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) for \(x_i\), and assume
\[
                        \min_i b_i> U,\qquad U=\max_i u_i .
\]
Put \(P_i=P_{x_i}\), \(G_i=\operatorname{Gal}(P_i/\mathbb Q)\), \(D_i=(A_{b_i})^{u_i}\) in its natural block action, and \(B_i\subset P_i\) the field of the permutation action on the \(u_i\) blocks.
Write \(P=\prod_i P_i,\ L=\prod_i L_{x_i},\ B=\prod_i B_i,\ Z=\prod_i P_i^{D_i}\), where products of fields inside \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) denote their composita.

1. Under restriction one has \(\operatorname{Gal}(P/Z)=\prod_i D_i\).
   Its factors \(D_i\) are exactly the minimal nontrivial normal subgroups of \(\operatorname{Gal}(P/\mathbb Q)\).
   In particular the fields \(P_i Z\) are linearly disjoint as a family over \(Z\), and \([P:Z]=\prod_i(b_i!/2)^{u_i}\).
2. For \(y\in\mathcal S(L)\) with \(0<m(y)\le N\), either \(y\in\mathcal S(B)\) or \(y\) matches exactly one input \(x_i\), meaning that a conjugate of \(y\) is multiplicatively dependent modulo torsion with a conjugate of \(x_i\).
   In a match the label fields and label actions agree, and for matched compressed roots \(a\) of \(\gamma_{x_i}\) and \(t\) of \(\gamma_y\), the fields \(\mathbb Q(a)=\mathbb Q(t)\) agree inside \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
   Their rational splitting fields and compressed degrees also agree, and nonzero powers of \(a,t\) agree up to torsion.
   The two alternatives are disjoint for elements.
   In the first alternative \(d_y\ll_N U(\log(3U))^{212}\), and \(b_y\le U\) whenever \(d_y\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(N\).
3. For each input put \(a_i=\gamma_{x_i}\).
   Inside \(\mathbb Q(a_i)^*\), let \(k_i\) be the index of the subgroup generated by \(a_i\) in its saturation modulo torsion.
   This is a finite positive integer also for inputs that are not units.
   One has the exact equality of sets of real numbers
   \[
     \Sigma_N(L)=\Sigma_N(B)\ \cup\
        \bigcup_{i=1}^r
        \left\{\frac{j\,m(x_i)}{k_i}:j\ge1,\ j\in\mathbb Z,\
                                         \frac{j\,m(x_i)}{k_i}\le N\right\}.       \tag{1}
   \]
   When all label actions are primitive, \(B=\mathbb Q\).
   Thus (1) then adds precisely the indicated progressions to the spectrum of rationally solvable algebraic numbers, allowing overlaps of numerical values.

Neither the structural constants nor the thresholds on the inputs depend on \(r\).
One may use different bounds on the input measures by taking \(M\) to be their maximum.

<a id="socles"></a>
## The kernel over the auxiliary fields

Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(P/\mathbb Q)\), viewed as a subgroup of \(\prod_iG_i\) projecting onto each coordinate.
In \(G_i\) let \(K_i\) denote the block-action kernel, so \(B_i=P_i^{K_i}\).
The [alternating action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) makes \(D_i\) the unique minimal normal subgroup of \(G_i\), and \(K_i/D_i\) embeds by block parities in \((C_2)^{u_i}\).
Every nonabelian simple section of any subgroup of \(\prod_i G_i/D_i\) has order at most \(U!\).
Indeed intersect a subgroup with the successive coordinate kernels to get a normal series whose quotients are subgroups of the individual coordinates.
Each \(G_i/D_i\) has an abelian normal subgroup with quotient in \(S_{u_i}\).
Refining the induced series shows the bound for all nonabelian simple composition factors, including simple quotients of arbitrary subgroups.
The same bound holds for \(\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbb Q)\), which acts in \(\prod_i S_{u_i}\).
For every \(i\), \(b_i\ge9\) and \(b_i>U\) imply \(b_i!/2>U!\).

For distinct \(i,j\), let \(G_{ij}\) be the image of \(G\) on the pair of coordinates.
If its projection onto \(G_j\) had trivial kernel, then \(P_i\subseteq P_j\).
Since \(L_{x_i}/P_i\) is [solvable](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal), the compositum \(L_{x_i}L_{x_j}\) would be solvable over \(L_{x_j}\).
The [two-field comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#statement) would force either conjugate dependence or \(b_i\le u_j\), both excluded.
The nontrivial kernel of this projection is therefore a subgroup supported on coordinate \(i\), and it is normal in \(G_i\) since \(G_{ij}\) projects onto \(G_i\).
It contains \(D_i\).
Interchanging \(i,j\) gives \(D_i\times D_j\subseteq G_{ij}\).

Put \(H=G\cap\prod_iD_i=\operatorname{Gal}(P/Z)\).
Its image in \(G_i\) is normal, and the quotient by that image is a quotient of \(G/H\), a subgroup of \(\prod_j G_j/D_j\).
If the image were trivial this quotient would have the simple section \(A_{b_i}\), exceeding the order bound.
Thus the image is all of \(D_i\).
On two coordinates the image \(H_{ij}\) is normal in \(G_{ij}\), contained in \(D_i\times D_j\), and \(G_{ij}/H_{ij}\) is likewise a quotient of \(G/H\).
If some simple direct factor of \(D_i\times D_j\) survived in this quotient, it would embed there, again violating the simple section bound.
Every factor therefore lies in \(H_{ij}\), giving \(H_{ij}=D_i\times D_j\).

The subgroup \(H\) now projects onto every simple factor in the \(D_i\), and onto the product of any two such factors, also within a single coordinate.
Such a subgroup of a finite product of nonabelian simple groups is the whole product.
For a direct proof, project away the last factor and use induction.
The kernel in that factor is normal and hence trivial or the entire factor.
In the trivial case the subgroup is the graph of a surjective homomorphism from the preceding product onto the last factor.
The factor images are commuting normal subgroups in a nonabelian simple group, so exactly one maps onto it, isomorphically.
This contradicts the full projection on the corresponding pair.
This proves the claimed independent product and the degree assertion.
Each \(D_i\) is minimal normal in \(G\) since its constituent simple factors are transitively permuted.
It has trivial centralizer in its own \(G_i\), because a nontrivial normal centralizer would contain \(D_i\), which is centerless.
An additional minimal normal subgroup of \(G\) would commute with every \(D_i\), hence project trivially everywhere, proving the assertion about the full socle.

## Which inputs survive in the splitting field of a new element

For arbitrary \(y\), put \(T_y=L_y^{R_y}\), where \(R_y\) is the largest solvable normal subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_y/\mathbb Q)\).
The group of \(T_y\) has trivial solvable radical, and \(T_y\subseteq P_y\), because the [two kernels from roots to labels](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal) are solvable in every degree.
For \(d_y\) large in terms of \(N\), the label group itself has trivial radical, giving \(T_y=P_y\).
For \(y\in\mathcal S(L)\), the extension \(L L_y/P\) is solvable, since \(L/P\) has group embedding in the product of the solvable input kernels.
Its normal image in the group of \(T_y\) is trivial, so \(T_y\subseteq P\).
Write \(\pi:G\to\operatorname{Gal}(T_y/\mathbb Q)\) for restriction.

If every \(D_i\) is killed by \(\pi\), then \(T_y\subseteq B\).
Indeed the kernel of the block action on all the inputs, modulo \(\prod_i D_i\), embeds in the product of the abelian groups \(K_i/D_i\).
Its image is therefore solvable and normal, hence trivial.
Since \(L_y/T_y\) is solvable, \(y\in\mathcal S(B)\).
Conversely, for \(y\in\mathcal S(B)\) the same restriction to the radical-free field gives \(T_y\subseteq B\), so every \(D_i\) is killed.
When \(d_y\) is large in terms of \(N\), the group of \(T_y=P_y\) has section \(A_{b_y}\).
The section bound for the block compositum now gives \(b_y\le U\).
The [block-size inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) yields \(d_y\ll_N U(\log(3d_y))^{212}\), hence \(d_y\ll_N U(\log(3U))^{212}\), first using \(U\gg_N\sqrt{d_y}\) for large \(d_y\).
Enlarging the constant covers bounded \(d_y\) as well.

Choose a common comparison threshold \(d_0(S)\) above which the block and two-field results apply at the bound \(S\), and take the input degrees large enough that every \(b_i>d_0(S)\).
The last adjustment depends only on \(S\) by the block-size inequality.
If \(d_y\le d_0(S)\), then \([T_y:\mathbb Q]\le d_y!\) by the faithful label action of \(P_y\).
Every \(D_i\) is killed since its simple factors have order \(b_i!/2>d_y!\).
This handles all small degrees without any structural assumption on their label groups.

Suppose now that \(\pi(D_i)\ne1\).
We are in the large-degree range \(T_y=P_y\) and \(G_y=\operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q)\) has unique minimal normal subgroup \(D_y=(A_{b_y})^{u_y}\).
The restriction of \(\pi\) to \(D_i\) is injective by its minimal normality in \(G\), and its image is minimal normal in \(G_y\) since \(\pi\) is surjective.
Thus \(\pi(D_i)=D_y\).
The kernel of the projection \(G\to G_i\) commutes with the supported \(D_i\), hence maps into the trivial centralizer of \(D_y\) in \(G_y\).
It follows that \(G_y\) is a quotient of \(G_i\) retaining \(D_i\), so the new quotient kernel in \(G_i\) is trivial.
Thus \(P_y=P_i\), and all other \(D_j\) are killed.
The [matching of natural labels and height-product argument](Yin_C_G0345_v278.md#letters) supplies equivariantly matched labels and the asserted multiplicative dependence of their compressed representatives.
The degree thresholds here are uniform because the same \(S\) bounds both input and target measures.

For any matched compressed roots \(a,t\), write \(t^n=\xi a^m\) for a root of unity \(\xi\) and nonzero integers \(m,n\).
An automorphism of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) fixing \(a\) sends \(t\) to a conjugate whose quotient by \(t\) is torsion.
Distinct conjugates of \(t\) have nontorsion quotient, so this automorphism fixes \(t\).
Interchanging \(a,t\) proves equality of the two root fields, hence equality of their normal closures and of their degrees.
The original numbers need not have the same degree, since their torsion-class fibers can have different sizes.
Conversely any dependence between original conjugates gives such a power relation between the corresponding compressed roots, since each compressed root is a power of its original representative times torsion.
Their equal root fields give a Galois-equivariant bijection of the compressed conjugates by transporting \(a,t\) together.
This identifies the labels since conjugates transported together satisfy the same power relation modulo torsion, and two conjugates in one orbit are on the same label exactly when they are multiplicatively dependent.
Hence \(P_y=P_i\) in any such match, which excludes membership in \(\mathcal S(B)\) by the section bound and precludes a second matching input.

<a id="progressions"></a>
## All measures contributed by one matching input

Fix \(a=\gamma_{x_i}\), \(F=\mathbb Q(a)\), and write \(\mu(F)\) for its roots of unity.
For \(t\in F^*\) with \(t^n=\xi a^m\), \(n>0\) and \(\xi\in\mu(F)\), the rational number \(m/n\) is unique since \(a\) is nontorsion.
It defines a homomorphism on the saturated group, with kernel \(\mu(F)\) and image an additive subgroup of \(\mathbb Q\) containing 1.
The height is \(h(t)=|m/n|h(a)\).
Bounded intervals in this image are finite by Northcott's theorem in \(F\), so the least positive value exists and generates the image by division with remainder.
Since 1 is in the image, this value is \(1/k_i\) for an integer \(k_i\ge1\).
Choose \(\rho\in F\) representing it, so \(a=\eta\rho^{k_i}\) for some \(\eta\in\mu(F)\).
Every nontorsion element in the saturation is \(\zeta\rho^j\), \(\zeta\in\mu(F)\), \(j\ne0\).

For any such \(t=\zeta\rho^j\), if an absolute Galois automorphism sends \(t\) to a torsion multiple of \(t\), it sends \(a\) to a torsion multiple of \(a\) by the displayed power relations.
Compression forces it to fix \(a\), hence every element of \(F\), including \(t\).
Thus \(t\) generates \(F\) and has no torsion quotient between distinct conjugates.
In particular every positive power of \(\rho\) realizes degree \(d_{x_i}\) and has measure \(j\,m(x_i)/k_i\), using \(m(a)=m(x_i)\).
These powers belong to \(L\).
Conversely a matching \(y\) has a conjugate of \(\gamma_y\) in this saturation by the exact root-field equality, after moving the matching input root to \(a\).
Thus its measure occurs in this progression, since \(m(y)=m(\gamma_y)\).
Together with \(\mathcal S(B)\subseteq\mathcal S(L)\) this proves (1).
For fixed \(U,N\) the contribution of \(B\) always lies in a finite set independent of the family, by the compressed degree bound and Northcott's theorem.
