# Multiplicative orders in the odd part of a reciprocal section index

<a id="statement"></a>
## Necessary size of the odd cofactor

Consider a sequence of integers \(q\to\infty\), \(0<u<v\) with \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), nonzero integers \(a,b\) with \(|a|=q,\ |b|=q+1\), signs \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and dilations \(g\ge1\).
Suppose there is a nontorsion algebraic unit \(z\) satisfying
\[
 P(z^g)=0,\qquad P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v).
\]
Take \(N=v-u\) for even \(q\) and \(N=v+u\) for odd \(q\), and write \(N=2^i m\) with \(m\) odd.
For odd \(t>1\) put \(b_2(t)=|\langle 2,-1\rangle|\) in \((\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z)^\times\).
Let \(J\) count the exterior conjugates of \(\beta=z^g\).
At every such endpoint, with no condition on the prime factors of \(m\),
\[
  m\ \ge\ \bigl(\tfrac12-o(1)\bigr)\frac{u}{J}\,q\log m,
  \qquad m\gg q\log q.
  \tag{1}
\]
The arithmetic restriction
\[
  m\ \ge\ \left(\frac{\log 2}{4}-o(1)\right)\frac{u}{J}\,q d
  \tag{2}
\]
holds with either of the following choices of \(d\).

* Take \(d=\min_{p\mid m}b_2(p)\), over prime divisors \(p\).
* Choose a nonempty set of at most \(r\) distinct prime divisors of \(m\), where \(r\) is fixed, with their least member divided by \(\log q\) tending to infinity.
  For \(s\) the product of their full prime powers in \(m\), take \(d=b_2(s)\).

In particular, the odd part of \(N\) cannot be \(o(q\log q)\), even when all its prime divisors vary.
For \(m=p\) prime, (2) uses the full \(b_2(p)\), uniformly in \(i\).
For every \(\eta>0\), a set of primes \(p\) of relative natural density one excludes *every* \(q\ge p^{1/2+\eta}\) at \(N=2^i p\), for all \(i\), directions, signs and dilations.
In each excluded case every positive-measure integral factor has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log 2\), by the [integral factor criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Removing residue factors of small period

Write \(S\) for the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), \(D=\deg S\), and \(h=h(\beta)\) for the absolute logarithmic height.
By the [near-pole estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement), \(S\) is eventually reciprocal, \(1\le J\le u\), and \(Dh=(1+o(1))J/(uq)\).
The [binary endpoint reduction and cofactor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#statement) give \(m\gg q\), \(Nh=\log(q+1)+o(1)\), and \(\overline S\mid X^N-1=(X^m-1)^{2^i}\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Here the cofactor bound uses the single selected prime 2, and the height is its [endpoint estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#reduction).
Every irreducible factor of \(\overline S\) therefore has root order \(t\mid m\) and multiplicity \(e\le 2^i\).
For \(t>1\), Frobenius and inversion together generate an orbit of size \(b_2(t)\) on such a root.
Each member has multiplicity \(e\) in \(\overline S\) by reciprocity, so \(e b_2(t)\le D\).
If \(t_0>1\) divides \(t\), reduction modulo \(t_0\) maps the generated subgroup onto \(\langle2,-1\rangle\bmod t_0\), giving \(b_2(t_0)\le b_2(t)\).

To prove (1), set \(k=\lfloor\log_2(m/(\log m)^5)\rfloor\), eventually positive, and take the exponents
\[
  n_{j,\pm}=2^i(2^j\pm1),\qquad 1\le j\le k.
\]
Every order \(t>1\) with \(b_2(t)\le2k\) divides some \(2^j-1\) or \(2^j+1\) in this range.
Indeed if \(-1\notin\langle2\rangle\bmod t\), the order of 2 is \(b_2(t)/2\); otherwise \(-1\) is the unique involution of the cyclic subgroup, so \(2^{b_2(t)/2}\equiv-1\bmod t\).
Order one is covered by \(2^1-1=1\).
Since \(2^j\pm1\) is odd, the corresponding binomial \(X^{n_{j,\pm}}-1\) contains the full multiplicity for each such root.
Every escaped factor has \(b_2(t)\ge d=2k+1\).

For the first choice in (2), use the one exponent \(n_1=2^i\).
Then \(n_1h=Nh/m\to0\), and \(X^{n_1}-1\) contains every order-one factor with its full multiplicity.
Every other factor has \(b_2(t)\ge d\), since \(t\) has a prime divisor of \(m\).
For the second choice take the exponents \(n_p=N/p\) for the selected primes.
Each \(n_p h\) tends to zero.
Whenever \(s\nmid t\), some selected prime \(p\) has \(t\mid m/p\), so its full factor power divides \(X^{N/p}-1\).
Every remaining factor has \(b_2(t)\ge b_2(s)=d\).
In each case let \(\mathcal N\) denote the list of these exponents.

<a id="norm"></a>
## An integer norm for the remaining Frobenius orbits

For \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), write \(\nu(j)=D^{-1}\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta^j-1)|\).
This is nonnegative, and the [cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) gives \(\nu(j)\ll\sqrt{jh\log(1/(jh))}\) as \(jh\to0\).
For the \(2k\) exponents used for (1), both \(nh\) and \(2nh\) are \(O((\log m)^{-4})\), since \(2^k\le m/(\log m)^5\), \(Nh=O(\log q)\), and \(m\gg q\).
Their normalized log norms at either scale sum to \(O(\sqrt{\log\log m}/\log m)=o(1)\).
For (2), both sums tend to zero because there are only boundedly many terms and each \(nh\to0\).
Some irreducible factor power of \(\overline S\) must escape the product of \(X^n-1\) over \(n\in\mathcal N\) eventually.
Otherwise evaluation of the product at \(\beta\) lies in \(2\mathbb Z[\beta]\) by monic division, giving \(\log 2\le\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\nu(n)=o(1)\) along a subsequence.
Consequently \(D\ge d\) and every escaped factor power has multiplicity \(e\le D/d\).
Let \(B\) be the least power of two at least \(\lfloor D/d\rfloor\), so \(1\le B<2D/d\).
Put \(L=m B\).
Since \(X^m-1\) contains each order \(t\mid m\) with multiplicity one, its \(B\)-th power contains all the escaped factor powers.
It follows that
\[
 G_-(X)=(X^L-1)\prod_{n\in\mathcal N}(X^n-1)
\]
is divisible by \(\overline S\) after reduction modulo 2.
The same is true for the polynomial \(G_+\) obtained by changing all minus signs to plus signs.
Both evaluations at \(\beta\) belong to \(2\mathbb Z[\beta]\) and are nonzero.
Multiplying them, taking the normalized log norm and using the usual archimedean bound gives
\[
 2\log 2\le \nu(2L)+\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\nu(2n)
             \le \log 2 + 2Lh +o(1).
\]
Thus \(Lh\ge(\log 2)/2-o(1)\).
Together with \(L<2mD/d\) and \(Dh=(1+o(1))J/(uq)\) this proves (2) for the indicated orbit sizes and gives the same inequality with \(d=2k+1\).
Since \(2k+1\sim2\log m/\log2\), this last choice proves (1), using \(J\le u\) and \(m\gg q\) for its second assertion.

<a id="density"></a>
## How many primes can occur at large coefficient height?

For \(X\ge3\) and all sufficiently large \(Q\), the number of primes \(X<p\le2X\) admitting any of the above units with \(N=2^i p\) and any \(q\ge Q\) is
\[
 \ll \frac{X^2}{Q^2\log X}.
\]
Indeed (2) gives \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\le C X/Q\) with an absolute \(C\).
This necessary condition is uniform, since a failure of any fixed constant larger than \(8/\log 2\) on arbitrarily large \(q\) would supply a sequence contradicting (2).
Each such prime divides \(\prod_{1\le j\le C X/Q}(2^j-1)\), whose logarithm is \(O((X/Q)^2)\) when the product is nonempty.
The product of the distinct primes under consideration therefore has logarithm at least their number times \(\log X\) and at most this bound.
When \(Q/\sqrt X\to\infty\), the proportion among primes in \((X,2X]\) tends to zero by the [prime number theorem at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz).
Taking \(Q=X^{1/2+\eta}\) gives the asserted relative natural density after summing dyadic intervals, since the finitely many initial intervals have vanishing weight.
In particular the exclusion applies to almost all prime cofactors even for \(q=\lceil p^{2/3}\rceil\), where \(p/(q\log q)\to\infty\).
