# Joint splitting fields of bounded Salem generators with alternating trace actions

<a id="statement"></a>
## The full group after adjoining abelian constants

Fix \(M>0\).
Take any finite collection of distinct primitive Salem numbers \(\alpha_i>1\) of degrees \(2n_i\) with \(\log\alpha_i\le M\), where all \(n_i\) are sufficiently large in terms of \(M\) alone.
Primitivity is in the unit group modulo torsion of the individual Salem field.
Assume that the Galois group on the \(n_i\) conjugates of \(t_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}\) contains the natural alternating group \(A_{n_i}\).
Write \(S_i\) for the splitting field of \(t_i\), \(L_i\) for the full splitting field of \(\alpha_i\), and \(E=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) for the maximal abelian extension of \(\mathbb Q\) in the chosen algebraic closure.
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Gal}\big((\prod_i L_i)E/E\big)
        =\prod_i\big(V_i^0\rtimes A_{n_i}\big),\qquad
 V_i^0=\{v\in\mathbb F_2^{n_i}:\textstyle\sum_j v_j=0\}.               \tag{1}
\]
Products of fields denote composita.
Here \(V_i^0\) acts by inverting any even number of reciprocal pairs, and the permutation factor has the natural action on their labels.
Thus the full splitting fields \(L_iE\) are linearly disjoint as a family over \(E\), including among different inputs of equal degree.
No assumption is made that their discriminants have independent square classes.

For the group over \(\mathbb Q\), let \(H_i\) be the monic minimum of \(t_i\), with discriminant \(\Delta_i\), and put \(b_i=H_i(2)H_i(-2)\).
The only restrictions in the product of the signed permutation groups are the joint values of the signs of the trace permutations and the parities of the inversions.
Their possible values are exactly the simultaneous actions on the square roots \(\sqrt{\Delta_i},\sqrt{b_i}\) in the multiquadratic field they generate over \(\mathbb Q\).
In particular this field is the maximal abelian subfield of the compositum of the \(L_i\).

<a id="trace"></a>
## Independent trace actions over the abelian closure

Each \(S_iE/E\) has group \(A_{n_i}\): the commutator of either \(A_{n_i}\) or \(S_{n_i}\) is \(A_{n_i}\) at the degrees used here, and the remaining quotient is abelian.
The joint action is a subdirect product of these nonabelian simple groups.
If it is proper, a pair of its coordinates has diagonal image up to isomorphism.
Indeed by induction, if every two-coordinate projection is full then the projection to the first \(k-1\) coordinates is the full product.
In the subdirect product with the last simple factor, the kernel of that projection is either the entire last factor or trivial.
In the latter case a surjection from the first product onto the last simple factor must factor through one coordinate, since the coordinate images are commuting normal subgroups.
This contradicts the pairwise condition.

For a diagonal pair the degrees coincide, say \(n\), by the orders of the groups.
We may assume \(n\ge7\).
Use the [alternating diagonal and relabeling argument](Yin_C_G0345_v841.md#diagonal): every automorphism of \(A_n\) at these degrees is induced by a permutation of the \(n\) labels.
After relabeling, the rational joint action normalizes the ordinary diagonal, since the group over \(E\) is normal.
The centralizer of \(A_n\) in \(S_n\) is trivial, so on these two sets every rational automorphism acts by the same permutation.
Matched trace labels consequently have the same stabilizer and generate the same embedded field \(B\).
Each matched reciprocal root generates a quadratic extension of this \(B\) and has total measure at most \(M\).
For large \(n\) the [common-base height product](Yin_C_G0901_v48.md#extensions) forces the roots to be multiplicatively dependent.
Some nonzero signed powers therefore coincide.
The largest absolute values among the conjugates of this equality give \(\alpha_i^{|a|}=\alpha_j^{|b|}\) for nonzero integers \(a,b\).
The two positive powers generate their original Salem fields by the unique dominant conjugate, so the positive numbers \(\alpha_i,\alpha_j\) are in the same real field.
By the [rank-one norm kernel](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units), for that field they are the same positive primitive generator.
Here their inverters coincide because both invert the same generator \(\alpha_i^{|a|}=\alpha_j^{|b|}\).
This contradicts distinctness and proves independence of all the trace actions.

<a id="inversions"></a>
## Independent inversions from commutators with complex conjugation

Use the action of \(\operatorname{Gal}(\prod_i L_i/\mathbb Q)\) on the labeled reciprocal pairs; after choosing one orientation per pair this embeds it in the product of the signed permutation groups.
Ordinary complex conjugation acts trivially on every trace label since all are real.
In each set of pairs it flips every pair except the unique pair of real roots, as in the [single-input inversion calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0162_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population).
To obtain a flip supported on just two indices in one input, use trace independence to take an element over \(E\) fixing the trace labels in every other input and sending the exceptional real label in the chosen input to another prescribed label.
Lift to the joint splitting fields; restriction in the finite Galois extension after base change is surjective.
Conjugating complex conjugation by this lift permutes its flip vector according to the trace permutations.
The commutator therefore flips precisely the selected two labels in the one input and no others.
It belongs to the group fixing the abelian intersection, as every commutator acts trivially on abelian quotients.
These vectors, using the original real label in each pair of flips, generate the full product of the \(V_i^0\).

The parity of all flips at input \(i\) is exactly the change of sign on \(\sqrt{b_i}\).
Indeed the product over pair labels of the oriented root differences \(z_j-z_j^{-1}\) has square \(\prod_j(t_j^2-4)=H_i(2)H_i(-2)\), with \(t_j=z_j+z_j^{-1}\).
Permuting the labels leaves this product unchanged, and reversing any one orientation negates it.
The permutation sign likewise describes the action on the Vandermonde square root of \(\Delta_i\).
Both signs are thus trivial over \(E\).
The proven independent even flip kernel and full alternating projection give exactly (1): every lift over \(E\) has even flip vector in each component, and one may remove that vector with the proven kernel.
Over \(\mathbb Q\) the kernel of the simultaneous sign map is already the group in (1).
Restriction to the finite multiquadratic field is surjective, giving the exact rational description and the assertion about the maximal abelian subfield.

<a id="primes"></a>
## Consequence for rational prime factorizations

For any fixed finite collection as above, adjoin to the multiquadratic field any prescribed finite abelian field.
Conditional on a fixed Frobenius element of this enlarged abelian field, the compatible signed permutations of the different inputs are independently uniform on the indicated cosets of \(V_i^0\rtimes A_{n_i}\), in the sense of conjugacy-stable tests.
Indeed the joint fiber is a coset of the entire product in (1): the intersection of the full splitting compositum with the enlarged abelian field is the stated maximal abelian subfield.
Applying the [Chebotarev density theorem](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001) to unions of conjugacy classes gives the product of the individual coset proportions.
This is a density for each fixed finite collection; it supplies no uniform upper bound for the first prime with a prescribed factorization.
