# An arithmetic restriction on adjacent-record measures approaching an integer

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative norms near a prescribed integer

Use the [adjacent degree-record rows](Yin_C_G0901_v163.md#statement) with \(C_{i,a}=A_i^aA_{i+1}\), \(m_i=\log A_i\), and let \(P_{i,a}\) be the monic rational minimum of the **first measure** \(C_{i,a}\), of degree \(D_i\).
The degree within a row is independent of \(a\).
Suppose first that the unrestricted gap fails, so \(m_i\downarrow0\).
For every fixed integer \(t\ge2\) and every sufficiently late row choose the second closest exponent from below, that is,
\[
                 a=\left\lfloor\frac{\log t-m_{i+1}}{m_i}\right\rfloor-1.
\]
Then
\[
 C_{i,a}<t,\qquad C_{i,a}\longrightarrow t,\qquad
 \log M(C_{i,a})\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
                 |P_{i,a}(t)|^{1/D_i}\longrightarrow t.                \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(t-C_{i,a}\) is an algebraic integer whose absolute norm grows as \(\exp(D_i(\log t+o(1)))\) and is coprime to \(t\).
For **any one prescribed real target** \(t>1\), failure of the unrestricted gap is equivalent to the existence of a sequence of admissible rows with \(C_{i,a}\to t\) strictly from below.
The sequence can be required to satisfy all of (1), including the evaluation of the minimum at \(t\).
For a real target take the second strictly lower exponent \(a=\lceil(\log t-m_{i+1})/m_i\rceil-2\), allowing exact equality with some other point in the row.

Assume again that the unrestricted gap fails.
For the **closest** exponent from below, a fixed deficit in the normalized log norm is possible only if the first log measure misses \(\log t\) by at most \(\exp(-c/m_{i+1})\), with \(c>0\) depending only on that deficit.
More precisely this necessity holds for a deviation in either direction, and for every admissible exponent approaching the target from below.
For the two fixed targets \(2,3\), in every two consecutive sufficiently late rows at least one of the four closest choices has log distance from its target at least a fixed positive power of \(m_{i+1}\) times an absolute constant, where \(i\) is the first row.
For one of those four choices the normalized log norm therefore differs from the log target by \(o(1)\), uniformly as \(i\to\infty\).
In particular all four translates cannot be units, nor can all four have a fixed normalized log norm deficit.
These are restrictions on integer values of the minima at the targets; a uniform upper bound for the second measure at a target remains open.

<a id="energy"></a>
## An exterior logarithmic potential averaged over a varying first field

Let \(A,B>1\) be strictly dominant positive algebraic integer units: each is strictly larger than the moduli of its other conjugates.
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(A)\), \(q=[K(B):K]\), \(b=\log B\), \(C=A^a B\) for \(a\ge1\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(C):\mathbb Q]\) and let \(P_C\) be the rational minimum of \(C\).
Fixing the product forces the largest modulus in both factors, hence fixes each of \(A,B\), and \(\mathbb Q(C)=K(B)\). [Product field](Yin_C_G0901_v155.md#fields)
For \(z\in\mathbb C\) with \(|z|>C\), put \(s=\log(|z|/C)>0\), \(L=\log^+(1/s)\).
We claim, uniformly in the degrees, the exponent and the argument of \(z\), that
\[
 \left|D^{-1}\log|P_C(z)|-\log|z|\right|
       \ll\sqrt{\left(b+\frac{\log(2q)+L}{q}\right)(1+L)} .          \tag{2}
\]

For an embedding \(\sigma:K\to\mathbb C\), let \(y_1,\ldots,y_q\) be the roots of the transported relative minimum of \(B\), and \(v_j=e^{-b}y_j\).
They lie in the closed unit disk.
With \(\rho=\exp(-\min\{s/2,1/q\})\) define
\[
  E_\sigma=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2k}}k
                       \left|q^{-1}\sum_jv_j^k\right|^2.
\]
Expanding the logarithm gives \(q^2 E_\sigma=-\sum_{j,l}\log|1-\rho^2 v_j\overline v_l|\).
For \(j\ne l\) one has \(|1-\rho^2 v_j\overline v_l|\ge\rho |v_j-v_l|\), by subtracting squared moduli and using \((1-\rho^2|v_j|^2)(1-\rho^2|v_l|^2)\ge0\).
The diagonal factors are at least \(1-\rho^2\).
Writing \(\Delta_\sigma\) for the discriminant of the monic polynomial of the \(y_j\), it follows that
\[
 E_\sigma\le (1-1/q)(b+\log(1/\rho))
       +q^{-1}\log(1/(1-\rho^2))-q^{-2}\log|\Delta_\sigma|.
\]
The relative minimum has integral coefficients and simple roots, so the unweighted product over all complex embeddings of \(K\), with conjugate embeddings counted separately, of \(|\Delta_\sigma|\) is at least one.
Consequently \(\mathbb E_\sigma E_\sigma\ll b+(\log(2q)+L)/q\).
This averages the discriminants before discarding them, as in the [relative energy estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v628.md#energy).
The bound on their mean does not require a degree bound on \(K\).

In the fiber at \(\sigma\), set \(w=(\sigma A)^a e^b/z\), so that \(|w|\le e^{-s}\) and \(|w|/\rho\le e^{-s/2}\).
Expanding \(q^{-1}\sum_j\log|1-wv_j|\) and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to its Taylor coefficients bounds its square by
\[
        E_\sigma\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(|w|/\rho)^{2k}}k
                  \le E_\sigma\log(1/(1-e^{-s})) .
\]
The last logarithm is \(O(1+L)\).
This bounds the mean square over the first-field embeddings of the difference between the fiberwise normalized log product and \(\log|z|\).
Its mean is \(D^{-1}\log|P_C(z)|-\log|z|\), which proves (2).
No small absolute height for the powered first factor is required.

<a id="rows"></a>
## A norm deficit forces an exponentially close first measure

Apply (2) to \(A=A_i\), \(B=A_{i+1}\), \(b=m_{i+1}\).
These are strict positive Perron units in sufficiently late record rows.
Their [relative-degree estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v159.md#orbits) gives \(q\gg d_{i+1}/(\log(3d_{i+1}))^{212}\) under the fixed original measure ceiling.
By the [degree lower bound on positive total measure](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), \(b\gg(\log(3d_{i+1}))^{-3}\).
In particular for every fixed \(k>0\), \(\log(2q)/q=o(b^k)\) along a shrinking record sequence, using eventual monotonicity in \(q\).
If the left side of (2) is at least \(\varepsilon>0\), then \(L\ge c_\varepsilon/b\) for all sufficiently late rows.
Indeed substituting \(L<c_\varepsilon/b\) bounds the squared right side, including its absolute implied constant, by \(O(c_\varepsilon)+o(1)\).
This proves the exponential proximity assertion even for complex points of modulus \(t>C\).

The row ceiling makes the two strictly lower exponents nearest each fixed \(t>1\) admissible for all sufficiently large \(i\). [Row endpoints](Yin_C_G0901_v163.md#rows)
The closest such exponent is \(\lceil(\log t-m_{i+1})/m_i\rceil-1\); its log distance is positive and at most \(m_i\).
For integer \(t\ge2\) it equals the floor, since the unit \(C_{i,a}\) cannot equal \(t\).
Decreasing the closest strictly lower exponent by one gives \(m_i<s\le2m_i\), and then (2) tends to zero since \(m_{i+1}\le m_i\).
By the same row bound, \(\log M(C_{i,a})\ge b_{i+1}a m_i\to\infty\), where \(b_{i+1}\to\infty\) is the size of a canonical block and \(a m_i\to\log t\).
For an integer target the unit constant of the monic integer minimum gives \(P_{i,a}(t)\equiv\pm1\pmod t\), proving the coprimality assertion.
In the reverse direction, if the record list is infinite with positive log infimum \(\ell\), any sequence with bounded first logs has bounded exponents.
A fixed-exponent subsequence with indices tending to infinity has first logs strictly above their limit \((a+1)\ell\).
Thus it cannot approach \(\log t\) strictly from below.
For finitely many rows there are only finitely many inputs.
This proves the claimed one-sided equivalence. [Record limits](Yin_C_G0901_v163.md#limits)

<a id="two"></a>
## The nearest choices at two rational targets in consecutive rows

Write \(h=m_i\), \(k=m_{i+1}\), and for \(t=2,3\) let \(r_t\) be the nonnegative difference between \(\log t\) and the closest first log from below in row \(i\).
Define \(s_t\) by the same rule in row \(i+1\).
All four differences tend to zero.
Let \(p\) be the exponent at 2 in the first row, and let \(u,v\) be the exponent at 3 minus that at 2 in the first and second rows, respectively.
Put \(\lambda=\log(3/2)\).
For large \(i\) we have \(p,u\asymp1/h\) and \(v\asymp1/k\).
The two row spacings give \(\lambda=uh+r_3-r_2=vk+s_3-s_2\) and \(\log2=ph+k+r_2\), hence
\[
  uv\log2-(pv+u)\lambda
                    =uvr_2-pv(r_3-r_2)-u(s_3-s_2).                    \tag{3}
\]
The left side is nonzero because the rationals \(2,3/2\) are multiplicatively independent.
The [Baker–Wüstholz bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) for their two fixed real logarithms in degree one gives an absolute lower bound \(c(hk)^{C_0}\) for its absolute value, since both integer coefficients are \(O(1/(hk))\).
The right side is \(O((hk)^{-1}\max_t\{r_t,s_t\})\).
Thus that maximum is at least \(c'(hk)^{C_0+1}\ge c'k^{2(C_0+1)}\).
For a maximizing choice (2) has \(L=O(1+\log(1/k))\).
The later source log entering (2) is either \(k\) or \(m_{i+2}\le k\), with negligible relative-degree terms in both cases by the preceding estimates.
This proves the two-row conclusion for the nearest choices, including when the original record degrees have arbitrarily large jumps.
