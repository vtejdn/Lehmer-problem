# A moment test for vanishing loss with signed cyclotomic factors

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exact attainment by a cyclotomic product

Let \(P,u,m\) be as in the [signed cyclotomic transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#statement) and write \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_h\) for the exterior roots.
Take \(R=A/B\) to be a ratio of monic cyclotomic products, allowing the empty product and disregarding the overall sign.
Normalize \(R(0)=1\), and write \(\ell_P(R)=\int D(|R|^2/u)\,d\mu\).
At fixed \(P\) the infimum of \(\ell_P(R)\) is zero exactly when a single cyclotomic product \(A\) satisfies \(|A|^2\le4u\) throughout the circle.
This property is decidable, with a finite positive lower bound for every \(R\) in the negative case.

Here is a test in terms of \(c=\log 2+m/2\) and \(U_j=\sum_i\beta_i^{-j}\), which are real by conjugation.
For each \(n\ge1\) choose integers \(e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1}\), and form the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix \(T_n=(c_{i-j})_{0\le i,j<n}\), where
\[
   c_0=c,\qquad c_j=c_{-j}=\frac{\sum_{d\mid j}d e_d-U_j}{2j}\quad (1\le j<n).
\]
The affirmative case is equivalent to the existence of a positive semidefinite choice at every depth.
At a feasible node the next exponent has at most \(\lfloor4c\rfloor+1\) feasible values, hence at most three whenever \(m<3/2-2\log2\).
In addition the [angular discrepancy criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#fourier) has a uniqueness radius independent of the input, as quantified [below](#separation).

<a id="finite"></a>
## The logarithmic moments and their negative part

By factoring \(1-X^j\) into cyclotomic polynomials and applying divisor inversion, every normalized ratio \(R\) is a product \(\prod_{j\ge1}(1-X^j)^{e_j}\) with finitely many nonzero integers \(e_j\).
Conversely every such product is a signed cyclotomic ratio up to sign.
The exponents are unique because the first nonzero exponent in a nontrivial product gives a nonzero Taylor coefficient at its index.
The exterior polynomial \(G\) from the [factorization of \(4u\)](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#statement) has \(G(0)=2e^{m/2}\) by Jensen's formula, and \(G/G(0)=\prod_i(1-X/\beta_i)\).
The integrable real boundary function \(v=\log|G|-\log|R|\) has Fourier coefficients \(c_j\) above.
Indeed \(\log|1-e^{ijt}|\) has coefficients \(-1/(2k)\) at frequencies \(\pm kj\), as follows from its power series at radius below one and convergence in \(L^2\) of those Fourier coefficients.
Its remaining nonzero frequencies are absent and its mean is zero.
The analogous expansion of the finite exterior product converges absolutely.
Therefore \(T_n\) is the compression of the quadratic form given by integration against \(v\), and its least eigenvalue is at least \(-n\int v^-\,d\mu\).
Here \(v^-=\max(0,-v)\), and the negative part contributes a positive semidefinite matrix of trace \(n\int v^-\,d\mu\).
The integrand comparison in the [numerator bound](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#compression) gives \(2\int v^-\,d\mu\le\ell_P(R)\).
Consequently a loss at most \(2t/n\) requires \(T_n+tI\succeq0\) for the exponents of that ratio.
Its two-coordinate principal minors require \(|c_j|\le c+t\).
At each \(j\) this puts \(e_j\), given the earlier exponents, in an interval of length \(4(c+t)\), giving the stated branching estimate at \(t=0\).

<a id="limit"></a>
## A nonvanishing analytic limit with integral coefficients

Suppose positive semidefinite choices exist at every depth.
The leading principal matrices give prefix closure, so finite branching produces an infinite sequence of exponents passing every test.
The resulting moments have \(|c_j|\le c\).
The analytic function \(V(z)=c+2\sum_{j\ge1}c_jz^j\) has nonnegative real part on the disk.
Indeed test \(T_n\) on \((1,\bar z,\ldots,\bar z^{n-1})\), multiply by \(1-|z|^2\), and let \(n\to\infty\).
The geometric sum of the entries of each diagonal gives \(\operatorname{Re}V(z)\), with absolute convergence for \(|z|<1\).
Now \(R_*(z)=G(z)\exp(-V(z))\) is nonvanishing on the disk, has constant one, and has modulus at most \(|G(z)|\).
Its Taylor series agrees formally with \(\prod_{j\ge1}(1-z^j)^{e_j}\), since the constant and every coefficient of the formal logarithms agree.
Only finitely many factors enter each Taylor coefficient, so those coefficients are integers.
Boundedness in the disk and Parseval imply finite squared coefficient sum, hence \(R_*\) is a polynomial.
Its reciprocal polynomial is monic integral with all roots in the closed disk and nonzero constant.
Kronecker's theorem gives a product of cyclotomic polynomials, so \(R_*\) itself is such a product up to sign.
Passing to the circle gives the pointwise bound and hence zero loss.
Conversely a cyclotomic product with the pointwise bound has \(v\ge0\) and passes at every depth.

If there is no such product, at some depth \(n\) every \(T_n\) has a strictly negative eigenvalue.
To extract a uniform positive penalty there, first keep the finite lists permitted by \(|c_j|\le c+1\) for \(1\le j<n\), enlarging intervals outward by rational endpoints if needed.
All omitted lists already fail \(T_n+I\succeq0\).
Among the finitely many retained matrices, choose rational \(0<t<1\) such that each \(T_n+tI\) still has a strictly negative eigenvalue.
This can be certified by rational interval approximations because the inequalities are strict and all entries are computable.
The resulting lower bound \(2t/n\) applies to every signed ratio.
A decision procedure searches over depths and rational strict certificates in parallel with enumerating all finite cyclotomic products and testing the pointwise inequality by exact real polynomial positivity on \([-2,2]\), as in the [integer test](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#termination).
One search terminates in each case.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Separation between distinct signed angular approximants

Let \(C_R\) denote the centered cumulative discrepancy in the [angular transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#statement) for a fixed input and reduced ratio \(R\).
For distinct normalized ratios \(R_1,R_2\), let \(j\) be the first nonzero exponent in the Euler product for \(R_1/R_2\).
The difference of their signed root counts at Fourier frequency \(j\) is \(j e_j\), since the \(d\)-th roots of unity sum to \(d\) at this frequency when \(d\mid j\) and to zero otherwise.
The Fourier coefficients of \(C_{R_1}-C_{R_2}\) at frequencies \(\pm j\) each have absolute value at least \(1/(2\pi)\).
Parseval gives \(\|C_{R_1}-C_{R_2}\|_2\ge1/(\sqrt2\,\pi)\).
Thus at most one reduced signed ratio has discrepancy norm strictly below \(1/(2\sqrt2\,\pi)\) for a fixed input.
If the infimum of the discrepancy norm at that input is zero, there is an exact signed match by this separation.
Zero discrepancy forces equality of the atomic measures, since their difference would otherwise have to equal a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure by the defining derivative.
After cancellation this match has no denominator and consists of the actual exterior phase multiset, with all its multiplicities.
The fixed-input separation does not rule out approximation by different ratios as the inputs vary.
