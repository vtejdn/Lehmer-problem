# A resultant bound for all pairs of conjugates of a single seed at two exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## The diagonal contributes twice and the other pairs contribute four times

Let \(a\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(e\) and conjugates \(a_1,\ldots,a_e\).
Let \(m,n\) be distinct positive integers for which the powers \(a^m,a^n\) both have degree \(e\).
Write \(z_i=a_i^m,\ w_i=a_i^n\).
The two powered sets are disjoint because all seed conjugates have the same positive absolute height and powering multiplies that height by the exponent.
Define the nonzero rational integers
\[
 B=\prod_{i\ne j}(z_i-w_j),\qquad D=\prod_i(z_i-w_i)
\]
by Galois invariance and integrality.
For \(s=m,n\) write \(A_s\) for the minimum of \(a^s\), \(I_s=[\mathbb Z[a]:\mathbb Z[a^s]]\), and \(\tau_s=|\operatorname{disc}A_s|^2=|\operatorname{disc}P|^2 I_s^4\).
Suppose \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) are nonzero and coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(P(X)^2\) divides \(U(X^s)+XV(X^s)\) at both exponents.
For the ordinary resultant \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) at the actual degrees one has
\[
 B^4D^2 \ \mid\
 R\,(\tau_m\tau_n)^{2e^2}(mn)^{4e(e-1)}|\operatorname{disc}P|^4 .       \tag{1}
\]
This includes primes at which several powered conjugates coincide after reduction.

Put \(h=\log M(P)\), \(x_i=\log|a_i|\) in a fixed complex embedding and \(F(m,n)=\sum_{i,j}\max(mx_i,nx_j)\).
For fixed \(P\), as \(\max(m,n)/\gcd(m,n)\to\infty\) through the stated powers, every such pair satisfies
\[
             \log|R|\ \ge\ 4F(m,n)-2|m-n|h-o_P(m+n).                    \tag{2}
\]
The bound holds in particular for every sufficiently large pair of distinct primes, with the error interpreted uniformly in the pair for this fixed seed.
Reciprocity is unnecessary for (1) and (2).

<a id="local"></a>
## The two local jet equations at equal and unequal labels

Work over the discrete valuation ring \(O\) of a finite prime in a common splitting field, with integral valuation \(v\).
For each pair \(i,j\) put \(z=z_i,\ w=w_j,\ d=z-w,\ t=Y-z\) and
\[
                        T_{ij}=O[t]/(t^2,(t+d)^2).
\]
Its length is \(4v(d)\), from the determinant \(d^4\) of multiplication by \(d^2+2dt\) on \(O[t]/(t^2)\).
Write \(N_{ij}=T_{ij}/(U,V)\).
When \(i\ne j\), the [two different label equations](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#labels) apply to these conjugates at \(z,w\): their vanishing with first derivatives uses only the two contacts and \(a_i\ne a_j\).
In particular the argument before the Sylvester comparison there gives
\[
      \operatorname{length}N_{ij}\ge
          4v(d)-4v(mn(a_i-a_j)).
\]
This allows the right hand side to be negative.

For \(i=j\) denote the common label by \(c=a_i\).
The polynomial \(G=U+cV\) vanishes at \(z,w\), so \(G=(Y-z)(Y-w)H\) with \(H\in O[Y]\), by monic division at the two distinct nodes.
The derivative of the graph at \(c\) gives \(mzG'(z)+cV(z)=0\), hence \(V(z)=-(mz/c)dH(z)\in dO\).
Further quotient \(T_{ii}\) by \(t(t+d)\).
The resulting ring has additive presentation
\[
                O/(d^2)\ \oplus\ (O/(d))t,\qquad t^2=dt=0.
\]
Indeed the three defining quadratics generate \((t^2,dt,d^2)\) in every residue characteristic.
Its length is \(3v(d)\), and the ideal \((d,t)\) has square zero.
Here \(G=0\) and the image of \(V\) belongs to \((d,t)\), so the row ideal is generated by \(V\), is spanned by it over \(O\), and is killed by \(d\).
Taking the further row quotient loses at most \(v(d)\), proving \(\operatorname{length}N_{ii}\ge2v(d)\).

For the constraints at just these two labeled nodes this coefficient two is attained away from \(mn\).
Take \(G=(Y-z)(Y-w)\) and choose the affine \(V\) with \(V(z)=-mz d/c,\ V(w)=nw d/c\).
Its slope is \(-(mz+nw)/c\), so its coefficients belong to \(O\).
Then \(U=G-cV\) satisfies both contact equations at \(c\), and \(\operatorname{Res}(U,V)=V(z)V(w)=-mnzw d^2/c^2\), since \(G\) is monic and adding a constant multiple of \(V\) preserves the resultant here.
This is a local labeled example; the coefficients need not be rational integers.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## Combining the contributions in a single ordinary Sylvester cokernel

Map \(C_m=O[Y]/(A_m^2)\) by reduction to \(E_m=\bigoplus_i O[Y]/((Y-z_i)^2)\).
Its image contains \(\tau_m E_m\).
Indeed for a prescribed component put \(H_i(Y)=\prod_{k\ne i}(Y-z_k)^2\).
In that component \(H_i^{-1}=H_i(z_i)^{-1}-(Y-z_i)H_i'(z_i)/H_i(z_i)^2\) over the fraction field.
The integer \(\tau_m=\prod_{i<k}(z_i-z_k)^4\) clears this denominator for each component.
Multiplying the cleared inverse by \(H_i\) isolates that component with value \(\tau_m\) and vanishes on the others.
Apply the same argument to \(C_n,E_n\) at the nodes \(w_j\).
The stated formula for \(\tau_s\) follows by changing the basis of the trace pairing between the two orders of index \(I_s\).

Tensor the two reduction maps over \(O[Y]\).
The source is \(M=O[Y]/(A_m^2,A_n^2)\) and the target is \(\bigoplus_{i,j}T_{ij}\).
The image contains \(\tau_m\tau_n\) times the target because simple tensors generate and the two scalars clear the two factors separately.
On taking row quotients let \(Z\) be the image of \(M/(U,V)\) in \(N=\bigoplus_{i,j}N_{ij}\).
Then \(N/Z\) is killed by \(\tau_m\tau_n\) and has at most \(2e^2\) generators over \(O\), by the generators \(1,t\) in each summand.

First normalize the rows by their common integer content.
As in the [ordinary Sylvester comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#length), \(M/(U,V)\) is a finite-length quotient and its reduction is the residue polynomial ring modulo the gcd of the two squared powered minima and the two rows.
At least one normalized row survives reduction, so the degree of this gcd is at most \(\deg U+\deg V\).
The ordinary Sylvester target therefore surjects onto the row quotient by Nakayama's lemma, and its image from the Sylvester source vanishes there.
It follows that \(\operatorname{length} Z\le v(R)\).
Restoring the content preserves the asserted divisibility since it multiplies \(R\) by an integral power of that content.
Adding the local lower bounds and using \(\operatorname{length}N\le v(R)+2e^2v(\tau_m\tau_n)\) proves (1): the product of all ordered differences \(a_i-a_j\) with \(i\ne j\) is the discriminant up to sign.
The finite-prime inequalities in the splitting field descend to divisibility between the displayed rational integers.

## The cost along exponent pairs with unbounded reduced direction

The complete powered resultant is \(BD=\operatorname{Res}(A_m,A_n)\).
The [fixed-unit estimate for overlap with the two indices](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) applies also to these identical seeds at disjoint powered nodes.
It gives \(\log\gcd(|BD|,I_m I_n)=o_P(m+n)\).
To use (1), retain only the gcd of \(|B|^4|D|^2\) with its correction factor.
At each prime the loss from the index powers is bounded by a constant depending on \(e\) times the valuation of \(\gcd(|BD|,I_m I_n)\), by taking the minimum of the two valuations.
The remaining factors cost \(O_P(\log(2mn))\).
Thus \(\log|R|\ge4\log|BD|-2\log|D|-o_P(m+n)\).
The [archimedean estimate for the full resultant](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#statement) gives \(\log|BD|=F(m,n)+O_P(\log(2mn))\).
Since \(D\) differs by a unit factor of absolute norm one and a sign from \(N_{\mathbb Q(a)/\mathbb Q}(a^{|m-n|}-1)\), its logarithm is \(|m-n|h+O_P(\log(2+|m-n|))\) by the [norm estimate at all conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate).
Together these estimates give (2).

For a fixed Salem seed with exterior root of log modulus \(h\), there is one positive \(x_i=h\), one negative \(x_i=-h\), and the remaining \(x_i\) are zero.
For \(p<q\) distinct primes, direct summation gives \(F(p,q)=e(p+q)h-2ph\).
In particular (2) gives
\[
          \log|R|\ \ge\ \big[(4e-6)p+(4e-2)q\big]h-o_P(p+q).           \tag{3}
\]
This applies, for example, to the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial.
With \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), Hadamard's bound on the ordinary Sylvester matrix gives \(\log|R|\le(\deg U+\deg V)\log H\).
For any sequence with \(\log H/(p+q)\to h\), the degree sum is therefore eventually at least \(4e-4\), and at least \(4e-3\) if \(\liminf q/p>1\).
The [sharp prime-pair construction](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) supplies such sequences for a Salem seed at increasing widths, for instance on arbitrarily large prime pairs in separated comparable subintervals by its bounded exceptional-partner count and a diagonal choice of excess.
The onset and the loss term depend on the seed; neither yields a positive lower bound uniform over all seed degrees.
