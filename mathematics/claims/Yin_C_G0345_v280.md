# Radial pairs, reflected resultants and discriminants of reciprocal units

<a id="statement"></a>
## Discriminant restrictions at small logarithmic measure

Let \(P\) be monic, separable and reciprocal, with real coefficients, degree \(d\ge2\), and \(r>0\) exterior roots of positive logarithmic moduli \(s_1,\ldots,s_r\).
Write \(L=m(P)=\log M(P)=\sum_j s_j\).
Then
\[
 \frac{|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}}{d}
 \le e^{2(d-1)L/d}\prod_{j=1}^r\min(1,(d-1)s_j)^{2/d}
 \le e^{2(d-1)L/d}\min(1,(d-1)L/r)^{2r/d}.                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, a positive lower bound on each of \(r/d\) and \(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}/d\) gives a uniform positive lower bound on \(L\).
There is no degree, coefficient, irreducibility or first-coefficient condition in (1).

Suppose now that \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of a reciprocal algebraic unit in the [oriented block case](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields).
Write \(\mathcal H\) for its \(n=2u\) halves, \(b=d/n\) for the size of each half, and \(f_H\) for the monic polynomial on the roots in \(H\).
Put \(L_H=m(f_H)\), so \(\sum_H L_H=L\).
For the block field \(E\) of degree \(u\), with \(c_E\) complex places, (1) applies with \(r/d\ge c_E/u\).
Define the nonnegative deficits and their mean by
\[
 D_H=\log b+\frac{2(b-1)}b L_H-\frac1b\log|\operatorname{disc}f_H|,
 \qquad \overline D=\frac1n\sum_H D_H .
\]
Thus the mean uses the absolute norm of the polynomial discriminant of the orientation polynomial.
On halves let \(\iota\) take inverses, let \(c\) be ordinary complex conjugation in the chosen embedding, and put \(\psi=c\iota\).
Consider a Galois orbit \(\mathcal O\) of ordered pairs of distinct halves containing \(t>0\) reflected edges \((H,\psi H)\), and put \(\alpha=t/|\mathcal O|\).
Then
\[
             \overline D\ \ge\ \alpha\log\!\frac{t}{2L}
                                   -\log 2-\frac{2L}{n}.                 \tag{2}
\]
At bounded \(n\), even one moved half forces \(\overline D\to\infty\) along any such sequence with \(L\to0\).
If \(n\to\infty\) and \(L\) remains bounded, the same divergence holds when some orbit has bounded outdegree and contains reflected edges from a fixed positive fraction of the halves.
In this case the right side grows at least as a positive constant times \(\log n\), up to a bounded error.
Both conclusions allow arbitrary unit constant coefficients of the orientation polynomial.

<a id="radial"></a>
## Subtraction at reciprocal conjugate roots

Pair \(z=e^{s+i\theta}\) outside the circle with the root \(1/\bar z=e^{-s+i\theta}\) inside it.
These pairs are disjoint, including for real roots.
In the Vandermonde matrix on the roots divide each exterior row by \(e^{(d-1)s}\), leaving every entry of every row of modulus at most one.
Subtract the inner row in a pair from its normalized outer row.
At column \(k=0,\ldots,d-1\) the difference after removal of the common phase is
\[
                          e^{-(d-1-k)s}-e^{-ks}.
\]
Its absolute value is at most \(\min(1,(d-1)s)\), by the Lipschitz bound for \(e^{-v}\) at \(v\ge0\).
Hadamard's inequality and restoration of the row factors prove the product bound in (1).
The product of the \(r\) truncated factors is at most both one and \(((d-1)L/r)^r\), by arithmetic and geometric means.
For \(r/d\ge\delta>0,\ L\le\delta\), the last bound in (1) is at most \(e^{2L}(L/\delta)^{2\delta}\).
At each nonreal embedding of \(E\), complex conjugation moves its label block to a different block.
Every one of the \(b\) labels in this block is nonneutral, since a root on the unit circle has a conjugate inverse and its label is fixed by \(c\).
The \(2c_E b\) labels so obtained each contribute an exterior root, proving the asserted density.

The [earlier paired determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v133.md#pair) concerns the largest residual radial slack after a distinguished real exterior root and uses the integer discriminant floor.
Formula (1) retains the individual logarithmic moduli and the actual discriminant.
The [two-exterior block estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) instead uses a height product, and (1) supplies no density lower bound when the exterior count stays fixed.
Even the linear order in \(L\) at \(r=d/2\) is attained over the reals.
For \(P=(X^q-e^s)(X^q-e^{-s})\), \(q\ge1,\ s>0\), the two binomial discriminants have product of absolute value \(q^{2q}\), and their absolute resultant is \((2\sinh s)^q\).
Thus \(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}/d=\sinh s\) and \(L=s\).
These examples have real coefficients and supply no sequence of integral inputs with \(s\to0\).

<a id="reflection"></a>
## A local resultant estimate independent of the degree

For any monic \(f\) with nonzero roots \(z_1,\ldots,z_b\), put
\(f^\#(X)=X^b\overline{f(1/\bar X)}\) and \(S=\sum_i|\log |z_i||\).
Then
\[
            |\operatorname{Res}(f,f^\#)|^{1/b}\ \le\ 2e^{2m(f)}S .      \tag{3}
\]
Indeed its absolute resultant is \(\prod_{i,j}|1-z_i\bar z_j|\).
Set \(a_i=z_i/\max(1,|z_i|)^2\), inside the closed disk.
After dividing each factor by \(\max(1,|z_i|)\max(1,|z_j|)\), its modulus equals \(|1-a_i\bar a_j|\) for roots on the same side, and \(|a_i-a_j|\) for roots on opposite sides.
The latter is at most \(|1-a_i\bar a_j|\) by the disk identity for the difference of their squares.
The product of the normalizing denominators is \(e^{2bm(f)}\).
In \(\prod_{i<j}|1-a_i\bar a_j|^2\), the factors involving any one variable can jointly be written as the squared modulus of a polynomial in that variable.
Maximizing successively on the boundary of each disk bounds this product by the maximum squared circle Vandermonde, at most \(b^b\) by Hadamard's inequality.
The diagonal factors \(1-|a_i|^2\le2|\log|z_i||\) have product at most \((2S/b)^b\), proving (3).

The constant in (3) at small measure cannot be improved uniformly even at unit constant modulus.
For \(f=(X^q-e^s)(X^q+e^{-s})\) one has \(b=2q,\ S=2s,\ m(f)=s\).
The two self-resultants with the reflected binomials have moduli \((e^{2s}-1)^q,(1-e^{-2s})^q\), and each cross-resultant has modulus \(2^q\).
Their product to the power \(1/b\) equals \(4\sinh s\).

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Compensation by the other conjugate resultants

For distinct halves \(H,J\) set \(U_{HJ}=b^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(f_H,f_J)|\).
The coefficients of each \(f_H\) are algebraic integers and the root sets partition the distinct roots of \(P\), so the resultants are nonzero algebraic integers.
For every ordered Galois orbit their full product is a nonzero rational integer, and hence the sum of the \(U_{HJ}\) over that orbit is nonnegative.
The usual Vandermonde row bound \(|\operatorname{disc}f_H|\le b^b e^{2(b-1)L_H}\) gives \(D_H\ge0\).
The same bound in degree \(2b\) for the product \(f_H f_J\), after division by both individual discriminants in the discriminant product identity, gives
\[
                   U_{HJ}\le L_H+L_J+\log 2+(D_H+D_J)/2 .
\]

The roots of \(f_H^\#\) form exactly \(\psi H\), and \(f_{\psi H}=f_H^\#/\overline{f_H(0)}\).
Consequently its self-reflection resultant is zero exactly when \(\psi H=H\), equivalently \(cH=\iota H\).
Such equality can pair different roots within the half and requires no individual root on the circle.
If \(\psi H\ne H\), apply (3) and use \(S=L_H+L_{\psi H}\) and \(\log|f_H(0)|=L_H-L_{\psi H}\) to obtain instead
\[
          U_{H,\psi H}\le L_H+L_{\psi H}+\log\bigl(2(L_H+L_{\psi H})\bigr).
\]
Here \(L_H+L_{\psi H}>0\) since a neutral root would be shared by the two halves.
Every vertex has equal outdegree and indegree in the chosen orbit, both \(|\mathcal O|/n\), by transitivity.
Average the first upper bound over this orbit, using the sharper second bound on the \(t\) reflected edges.
At those edges one may still add the nonnegative half-sum of the deficits.
Their additional terms beyond the \(\log2\) baseline are \(\log(L_H+L_{\psi H})\), whose average is at most \(\log(2L/t)\) by concavity and injectivity of \(\psi\).
This proves (2) from the nonnegative orbit sum.
For bounded \(n\), any moved half gives such an orbit with \(\alpha\ge1/(n(n-1))\).
For bounded outdegree and \(t\ge\eta n\) the fraction \(\alpha\) stays bounded below.

An entire off-diagonal orbit can consist of reflected edges only if \(\psi\) commutes with the Galois action on the halves.
Indeed transitivity of its first projection and uniqueness of \(\psi H\) as partner make this orbit the graph of \(\psi\), so Galois invariance gives the commutation.
Conversely, if \(\psi\) commutes with the action and moves a half, its graph is one off-diagonal orbit.
The [relative Mahler gap](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#gap) already treats a totally real or CM field with canonical conjugation different from the orientation involution.
At general complex places the other edges in (2) account for the missing arithmetic control on an individual reflected resultant.
Neither the exterior density nor a bound for the relevant discriminant deficits follows for all reciprocal units.
