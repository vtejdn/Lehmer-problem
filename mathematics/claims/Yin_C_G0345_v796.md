# Exterior conjugate pairs and subfields of the reciprocal trace field

<a id="statement"></a>
## Subfields seen by the nonreal trace embeddings

Put \(A(U)=(U^2-3U-1)(U^2-4U-1)\), \(A^*(U)=U^4A(1/U)\), and
\[
 P(X)=A(X^k)X^{2l}+X^{2k+l}+A^*(X^k)
\]
for positive coprime \(k,l\) with \(k\to\infty\) and \(s=l/k\to\infty\).
Let \(S\) be a reciprocal irreducible rational factor, let \(z\) be one of its roots, and set \(T=\mathbb Q(z+z^{-1})\).
Write \(d=\deg S\) and let \(B\) be the set of its \(H\) exterior roots.
For every proper subfield \(\mathbb Q\subseteq L\subsetneq T\), put \(b=[T:L]\) and write \(e(L)\) for the number of nonreal embeddings \(L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), counting conjugate embeddings separately.
Uniformly in the factor, its chosen root and the subfield,
\[
                                  b\,e(L)=o(H).                           \tag{1}
\]
If some exterior root of \(S\) is nonreal at every index, then
\[
               \min_{\mathbb Q\subseteq L\subsetneq T}[T:L]\longrightarrow\infty .
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
In particular this holds for every sequence with \(H\to\infty\).
A sequence of irreducible factors with \(H\to\infty\) and \(H=o(k)\) is reciprocal eventually, so its trace fields satisfy both restrictions.
For (2), any intermediate field is allowed to vary with the section.
The conclusion also excludes nonidentity automorphisms of these trace fields of bounded order, by taking the fixed field of the cyclic group generated by the automorphism.

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Simultaneous visits by every distinct exterior pair

For any two distinct exterior roots \(r,t\) of the entire section, possibly in different irreducible factors, let \(\mathcal O(r,t)\) be their joint absolute Galois orbit.
Writing \(B_P\) for the exterior roots of \(P\), we have uniformly
\[
 s\,\frac{|\mathcal O(r,t)\cap (B_P\times B_P)|}{|\mathcal O(r,t)|}\longrightarrow0. \tag{3}
\]
The [plane-curve torsion quotient theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#statement) applies to the [absolutely irreducible parent](Yin_C_G0345_v789.md#example).
Its projected support differences have gcd one, since consecutive terms of \(A^*(X^k)\) at exponents 0 and \(k\) give a difference \(k\), and the middle term has exponent \(2k+l\).
Here the exponents are distinct for sufficiently large \(s\).
The [torsion exclusion and signature calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) ensure that all roots are nontorsion units.
Consequently distinct roots have nontorsion quotient for all sufficiently large indices.

By the [projected width formula](Yin_C_G0345_v769.md#statement), the heights \(h(r)\) and \(h(t)\) are both asymptotic to the same positive constant divided by \(2l+4k\), uniformly over the section roots.
Thus \(h(r^k)+h(t^k)=O(1/s)\).
For any fixed nonzero character \((i,j)\) a torsion relation \(r^i t^j\in\mu_\infty\) requires \(|i|h(r)=|j|h(t)\).
If it occurs infinitely often, then \(|i|=|j|\ne0\).
The same signs are impossible since both roots are exterior, and opposite signs would give a torsion quotient.
Taking \(k\)-th powers leaves the test for torsion unchanged, so any such sequence of powered pairs is strict.
The [strict-to-generic argument](Yin_C_G0345_v792.md#calibration) makes it generic in the two-dimensional torus, including when the two roots visit the same pole.

Write \(\beta_j=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\) for \(j=3,4\).
Every exterior root \(u\) satisfies \(\min_{j=3,4}|u^k-\beta_j|\le C e^{-cs}\) by the [polar expansions](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
Each of the four possible centers \((\beta_i,\beta_j)\), including the two diagonal centers, consists of units and has all conjugates off the compact unit torus.
The [calibrated exceptional height](Yin_C_G0345_v792.md#blowup) at each center and its conjugates therefore tends to zero on the generic powered pairs.
Summing over the finite union of all four center orbits gives nonnegative local contributions.
At every embedding with both original roots exterior, the local sum is at least \(cs-O(1)\), since some center is exponentially close in both coordinates.
The embedding count with equal extension multiplicities gives precisely the orbit fraction in (3), as in the [ordered-pair height computation](Yin_C_G0345_v792.md#orbit).
Any failing subsequence would itself give strict powered pairs, proving uniformity.

In particular every rational irreducible factor \(S\), even without the reciprocal hypothesis, with at least two exterior roots has
\[
                        [\mathbb Q(M(S)):\mathbb Q]/s\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
Here \(M(S)\) is its multiplicative Mahler measure.
It is the signed product of its exterior roots, since the monic factor is integral and those roots are closed under complex conjugation.
Among subsets of conjugates of size \(H\), exactly the full exterior set gives this maximal product modulus.
Every automorphism fixing \(M(S)\) stabilizes that set and sends the chosen exterior pair into \(B_P\times B_P\).
Its relative frequency in the Galois group of a splitting field is the reciprocal of the displayed field degree.
The asserted growth follows from (3).

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Counting pairs above each embedding of the subfield

For reciprocal \(S\), the roots come in distinct inverse pairs, each determining one embedding of \(T\) by \(z+z^{-1}\).
Indeed inversion on \(\mathbb Q(z)\) is a nonidentity order-two automorphism since \(S\) is reciprocal and its roots are nontorsion, and its fixed field is \(T\) by the quadratic equation for \(z\).
Above any embedding \(w\) of \(L\) there are \(b\) trace embeddings, and each is associated with two inverse roots.
Let \(a_w\) be the number of exterior roots of \(S\) in these pairs, so \(0\le a_w\le b\) and \(\sum_w a_w=H\).

Consider the Galois-invariant relation on ordered roots of \(S\) requiring two distinct trace embeddings restricting to the same \(w\).
Each root has exactly \(2(b-1)\) neighbors.
This relation is a union of joint orbits with total size \(2d(b-1)\).
Orbits without two exterior entries contribute no exterior pairs, and (3) bounds their fraction in every other orbit by \(o(1/s)\) uniformly, even as the number of orbits grows.
Since an inverse pair contains at most one exterior root, summing these estimates gives
\[
             \sum_w a_w(a_w-1)=o\bigl(d(b-1)/s\bigr)=o\bigl(H(b-1)\bigr). \tag{4}
\]
The last equality uses the [uniform pole allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth).
Above a nonreal embedding \(w\), each of the \(b\) trace embeddings is nonreal and its inverse pair has exactly one exterior root.
Thus \(a_w=b\) there, giving \(e(L)b(b-1)\) as a lower bound in (4).
This proves (1).

For a real embedding \(w\), every nonreal exterior root counted by \(a_w\) has its distinct complex conjugate above the same \(w\).
These roots cannot be inverses of each other because both are exterior.
For a nonreal \(w\) one has \(a_w=b\ge2\), so every exterior root there also has another exterior root in that block.
Consequently the left side of (4) is at least the number of nonreal exterior roots of \(S\).
At most four exterior roots in the entire section are real by the [real polar labels](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement).
Whenever \(S\) has any nonreal exterior root, at least \(H/5\) of its exterior roots are nonreal, using \(H-4\) for \(H\ge5\) and integrality otherwise.
Formula (4) now prevents \(b\) from staying bounded along any subsequence, proving (2).

Finally the [uniform short-coordinate radius](Yin_C_G0345_v795.md#radius) gives \(m(S)\le H\log(6)/k\) also before reciprocity is known.
If \(H=o(k)\), this tends to zero and [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001) forces eventual reciprocity.
The proof leaves trace fields without proper subfields of bounded relative degree available.
