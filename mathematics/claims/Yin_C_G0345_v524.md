# Lower bounds for the primitive Mahler measure of every rational combination of two common rows

<a id="statement"></a>
## An odd-exponent test with a prescribed rate loss

Use the [odd blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#blocks) at base scale \(L\), with \(K=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\), block length \(w=\lfloor L/(100K^3)\rfloor\), exponent set \(S\), cardinality \(r=Kw\), and base sum \(s=\sum_{p\in S}p\).
For any \(g=3^a\), \(a\ge0\) integral, use the actual exponents \(gS\) with sum \(\Sigma=gs\).
Use the [same-seed double graph test](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#statement) on these exponents, with coefficient norm \(H\le2^\Sigma\) and width \(b=rj\) as chosen below.
Write \(e=\deg P,\ h=\log M(P)\) for the common reciprocal unit minimum and suppose every graph contains \(P^k\), \(k\ge2\).
There is a computable \(j=j(L,g)\le K\), tending to infinity uniformly over \(g\) as \(L\to\infty\), such that for every nonzero rational combination of \(U,V\) its primitive integral representative \(A\), unique up to sign, satisfies
\[
       m(A)=\log M(A)\gg k g w\,h/e \gg k g wj/K.                    \tag{1}
\]
The implied constants are absolute.
Deleting all cyclotomic and monomial factors preserves this lower bound.
The largest absolute coefficient of the resulting primitive polynomial is at least \(\exp(m(A))/\sqrt{b+1}\), by the circle mean-square bound for Mahler measure.
In particular each of the two original rows has this primitive measure bound separately.

For any prescribed computable nondecreasing unbounded \(f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N_{>0}\), one can choose the dilations effectively so that, for all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
                          m(A)\ge\Sigma/f(\Sigma)                    \tag{2}
\]
simultaneously on the entire rational pencil.
For each such choice of dilations, the minimum normalized log coefficient cost of the test tends to zero if and only if the unrestricted logarithmic Lehmer infimum vanishes.
The loss \(f\) in (2) can grow arbitrarily slowly, with the tests chosen after \(f\).

## Calibration and periods of large totient

Let \(\mathcal N_0\) be the disjoint period lists from the [block construction](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#blocks).
They satisfy \(2L\le n\le4L\), \(|\mathcal N_0|\asymp K^2w\), and \(\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_0}\varphi(n)\gg LK^2w\).
Keep the set \(\mathcal N\) of periods with totient at least half the average over \(\mathcal N_0\).
Each retained totient is \(\gg L\), and the discarded terms contribute at most half the total, so \(T=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\varphi(n)\gg LK^2w\gg Ks\).

For a reciprocal seed write \(D_d\) for the part of \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_d)|\) prime to \(d\).
Choose the largest integer \(2\le j\le K\) for which every reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum of degree \(e\le j\) and measure \(M(P)\le2\) satisfies
\[
 M(P)^K\ge2^{ej},\qquad D_{gn}^{\,j}\ge M(P)^{(j-1)\varphi(gn)}
                     \quad (n\in\mathcal N_0).
\]
Take \(j=1\) if there is no such integer.
The [finite enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#width) and algebraic comparisons are effective.
For each fixed \(j\), the [primitive norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) holds at all sufficiently large orders for all seeds on its finite list.
Since \(gn\ge2L\), this proves uniform divergence of the selected width multiplier.
The [graph tangencies](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) apply at the distinct positive exponents \(gp\), giving \(e\le j\) and \(h\le\log2\) for the actual seed as in the [odd-exponent test](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#width).
In particular \(h/e\ge j\log2/K\), \(b\le K^2w=o(L)\), and \(\varphi(n)>b\) on \(\mathcal N\) for large \(L\).
Also \(\varphi(gn)\ge(2/3)g\varphi(n)\), by the prime-power formula.

Every retained \(n\) has representations \(p+q=n\) in which the first exponents run over at least \(w/2\) consecutive odd integers in an interval of length less than \(n\).
At least \(c_0w\) of these first exponents are coprime to \(n\), for an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Indeed inclusion-exclusion on the odd prime divisors gives, in a run of length \(u\), the count \(2u\varphi(n)/n+O(2^{\omega(n)})\), where \(\omega\) counts distinct prime factors.
Here \(n\) is even, and \(2^{\omega(n)}\le\tau(n)\le2\sqrt n=o(w)\), where \(\tau\) is the divisor count.

## Primitive components of a rational pencil

Write \(W=uU+vV=cA\) with relatively prime integers \(u,v\) and positive coefficient content \(c\).
The two coefficient vectors are linearly independent since proportional coprime rows would both be constant and could not have any such graph contacts.
For each two-by-two minor \(\Delta\) of these vectors, \(c\) divides both \(u\Delta\) and \(v\Delta\) by using each coefficient of \(W\) against the corresponding coefficients of \(V\) and \(U\).
Thus \(c\) divides every minor, including some nonzero one, and \(c\le 2H^2\) independently of \(u,v\).

Fix a rational prime \(\ell\) and take one representative \(x_i\) from each Frobenius orbit of roots of \(\bar P\) of exact order \(gn\), \(n\in\mathcal N\), with \(\ell\nmid gn\).
In an unramified extension let \(\xi_i\) be the root of unity of that order lifting \(x_i\), let \(f_i\) be the residue degree, and put \(t_i=v_\ell(P(\xi_i))\), with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
The [component norm identity](Yin_C_G0345_v513.md#layers) gives \(\sum_{i:\,\mathrm{ord}(x_i)=gn} f_i t_i=v_\ell(D_{gn})\) when \(\ell\nmid gn\), and \(v_\ell(D_{gn})=0\) otherwise.
Moreover \(\sum_i f_i\le e\), since these are distinct irreducible factors of \(\bar P\).

For a selected coprime first exponent \(p\), the [sum-period inclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v504.md#statement) at the odd exponents \(gp,gq\) gives \(W(\xi_i^{gp})\in(P(\xi_i)^k)\).
The points \(\xi_i^{gp}\) at this one \(i\) are distinct primitive \(n\)-th roots, since \(\xi_i^g\) has order \(n\) and the first exponents are distinct modulo \(n\).
Each contributes at least \(a_i=(kt_i-v_\ell(c))_+\) to the valuation of the nonzero integer \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_n,A)|\).
Nonvanishing follows from \(\deg A\le b<\varphi(n)\).
Over an unramified splitting field for \(\Phi_n\), the full valuation of \(R_n\) sums the nonnegative contributions from all primitive roots.
Thus \(v_\ell(R_n)\ge c_0 w a_i\) for every such \(i\) individually.
Weighting by \(f_i\) and using the degree budget bounds the effect of repetitions of the same primitive root and gives
\[
 e\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log R_n
 \ \ge\ c_0w\left(k\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log D_{gn}-e\log c\right).    \tag{3}
\]
Indeed at each \(\ell\) sum first over the representatives of each order, then over orders, using \(\sum_i f_i a_i\ge k\sum_i f_i t_i-e v_\ell(c)\), and finally weight by \(\log\ell\).
The width test and totient bound give \(\sum_n\log D_{gn}\gg hgT\).
Since \(\log c\le2\Sigma\log2+\log2\), \(T\gg Ks\), and \(hK/e\gg j\), the content expense is \(O(1/j)\) of this main term after its multiplication by \(k\).

## Bounding the cyclotomic resultants by the true row measure

For \(n\ge2\) and every complex \(z\),
\[
 |\Phi_n(z)|\le \exp\!\big(1+\tau(n)\log(3n)\big)\max(1,|z|)^{\varphi(n)}.
\]
On \(|z|=1+1/n\), use the Möbius product with factors \(1-z^{-d}\), \(d\mid n\), after extracting \(z^{\varphi(n)}\).
Each such factor has modulus between \(1/(n+1)\) and \(2\), and \(|z|^{\varphi(n)}\le \exp(1)\).
The maximum principle gives the bound on the unit disk and reciprocity gives it outside.
Factoring \(A\) over the complex numbers, including its leading coefficient in the resultant, now yields
\[
 \sum_{n\in\mathcal N}\log R_n
     \le Tm(A)+O\!\left(b|\mathcal N|\sqrt{L}\log(2L)\right).
\]
After division by \(T\) the error is \(O(b\log(2L)/\sqrt L)=o(w/K)\).
This estimate and (3) prove (1), uniformly over all admitted dilations, rows and rational directions.

## Choice of dilation and relation to the unrestricted infimum

Choose the least \(g=3^a\) with \(f(gs)\ge L^2\).
This search terminates, and \(\Sigma\le2gLKw\) gives \(m(A)f(\Sigma)/\Sigma\gg jL/K^2\to\infty\), proving (2).
For each prescribed schedule the [tangency lower bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(h\Sigma\le\log H\).
Conversely if the unrestricted infimum is zero, choose fixed reciprocal unit seeds of arbitrarily small positive measure for which all powers preserve degree by the [torsion-class reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v523.md#equivalence).
The [integral Hermite construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) at the actual exponents gives two qualifying rows of degree at most \(2er+2\) and log norm at most \(2h\Sigma+2er\log2+\log(5er\prod_{p\in S}gp)\).
For each fixed seed this width eventually fits, and \(er/\Sigma\le e/(gL)\) while \(\log(\prod gp)/\Sigma\le\log(2gL)/(gL)\).
Thus every positive tolerance is realized eventually, using the fallback \(2^\Sigma\) for the finite minimum as in the [common test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common).
The converse follows from the pointwise bound and the positive fallback rate \(\log2\).
With \(g=1\), (1) already forces primitive logarithmic measure \(\gg Lj/(\log L)^4\) throughout the pencil.
