# Bounded common exponents in four-term Salem relations with a fixed pole

<a id="statement"></a>
## A field radical bound and its consequence at the critical scale

Let \(B>1\) be real algebraic, let \(A_B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be its primitive minimal polynomial with positive leading coefficient, and put \(e_B=\deg A_B\), \(L_B=2^{e_B}\sum_j|[X^j]A_B|\).
There exists \(s_B\) such that the following holds for positive integers \(v>u\) with \(v/u>s_B\), either sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and any real algebraic unit \(\beta>1\) satisfying
\[
                 \beta^v(\beta^u-B)+\epsilon(B\beta^u-1)=0.             \tag{1}
\]
Write \(h\) for the absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(R_K(x)=\max\{r\ge1:x^{1/r}\in K\}\) for a positive real nontorsion \(x\) in a real number field \(K\), with all real radicals taken positive.
For every prime power \(p^j\) dividing \(R_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}(\beta)\), one has
\[
                      p^j\le L_B\exp\!\left(
                           \frac{e_B(u+v)h(\beta)}p\right).             \tag{2}
\]
The threshold depends only on the pole \(B\); no bound on \(u\) or on the degree of \(\beta\) is needed.

In particular let \(\alpha_i\) be a shrinking Salem sequence with a [critical four-term test](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement), its exact exponents \(a_i<b_i\), pole \(B_i\) in the fixed finite set \(\mathcal B\), and \((a_i+b_i)h(\alpha_i)\le C\).
Put \(K_i=\mathbb Q(\alpha_i)\), \(g_i=\gcd(a_i,b_i)\) and
\[
 N=\left\lfloor \max_{B\in\mathcal B} L_B \exp(e_B C/2)\right\rfloor,
 \qquad H=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N).
\]
For a nonempty seed set, eventually
\[
                    g_i\,R_{K_i}(\alpha_i)\ \mid\ H .                  \tag{3}
\]
Hence the reduced denominator \(a_i/g_i\) of \(b_i/a_i\) tends to infinity and is at least \(a_i/H\).
This applies to all the allowed algebraic poles, including the integer two and nonintegral poles, at either sign.

<a id="separation"></a>
## The possible positive roots after removing a prime power

For \(s>1\), \(t>1\) and a conjugate \(D\) of \(B\), consider the real-variable equation
\[
                  t^s(t-D)+\epsilon(Dt-1)=0.                           \tag{4}
\]
All conjugates differ from \(1\), since \(B>1\).
Near \(t=1\), the function
\[
                    t\longmapsto \log\left|\frac{Dt-1}{t-D}\right|
\]
is continuously differentiable and vanishes at \(1\).
Thus on one fixed interval \(1<t<1+\delta\) its absolute value is at most \(M\log t\), with \(\delta,M>0\) common to the finite set of conjugates.
For \(s>M\), (4) has no solution in that interval, by comparing with \(s\log t\).
All its solutions for \(s>1\) are also bounded above by a constant depending only on \(B\).
Indeed for \(t\ge2\max(1,\max_D|D|)\), the modulus of the quotient is at most \(2(\max_D|D|+1)\), whereas \(t^s\ge t\).
At a solution \(t=D\) is impossible since \(D^2\ne1\).

For any sequence of solutions of (4) with \(D=B\) and \(s\to\infty\), these bounds imply \(t\to B\), since \(|t-B|\le t^{-s}(|B|t+1)\).
We claim that for all sufficiently large \(s\), uniformly at all those solutions, no \(w=t^{1/p}\) with \(p\) prime solves (4) at any conjugate \(D\) and the same sign.
Otherwise choose such solutions along \(s\to\infty\), passing to a fixed conjugate \(D\).
If the primes are unbounded, pass to \(p\to\infty\); then \(w\to1\) from above, contradicting the uniform exclusion interval.
If they are bounded, pass to a fixed \(p\).
Then \(w\to B^{1/p}>1\), and applying \(|w-D|\le w^{-s}(|D|w+1)\) forces \(D=B^{1/p}\).
Conjugation preserves height, while \(h(B^{1/p})=h(B)/p<h(B)\) since \(B>1\).
This contradiction proves the claim and supplies the threshold \(s_B\) for both signs.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The integer polynomial and its prime-power test

Put \(U=X^{u+v}-\epsilon\), \(V=X^v-\epsilon X^u\) and define the integer polynomial
\[
       P_{u,v}=V^{e_B} A_B(U/V)
              =[X^{e_B}]A_B\,\prod_{D}(U-DV),
\]
where the product ranges once over the conjugates of \(B\) and \([X^{e_B}]A_B\) denotes the leading coefficient of \(A_B\).
The degree of this polynomial is \(e_B(u+v)\) because \(U\) has larger degree than \(V\).
Its coefficient length is at most \(L_B\) by expanding the homogenization into \(U^j V^{e_B-j}\), each of length at most \(2^{e_B}\).
This handles the denominator of a nonintegral pole as part of the primitive polynomial.
Equation (1) gives \(P_{u,v}(\beta)=0\).
Writing \(t=\beta^u\) and \(s=v/u\) gives (4) at \(D=B\).
The [positive-root separation](#separation) therefore gives \(P_{u,v}(\beta^{1/p})\ne0\) for every prime \(p\) when \(s>s_B\).

For a fixed real number field and a fixed positive nontorsion element the admissible positive radical exponents are finite by bounded degree and height, and they are precisely the divisors of their maximum.
Indeed they are closed under divisors and least common multiples, the latter by Bézout's identity using positive radicals.
For \(p^j\) dividing the maximum in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), apply the [prime-power norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) with its integer polynomial equal to \(P_{u,v}\).
The congruence of the characteristic polynomials at consecutive levels in the tower gives \(P_{u,v}(\beta^{1/p})\in p^j\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\beta)}\).
Its nonzero norm and the inequality \(|P_{u,v}(z)|\le L_B\max(1,|z|)^{e_B(u+v)}\) now give (2), including primes dividing the leading coefficient of \(A_B\).

<a id="dilation"></a>
## Applying the estimate before a common dilation

For a Salem number \(\alpha\), each positive power generates \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), since two conjugates with equal positive powers could be taken by one automorphism to its unique exterior root and a different root of the same modulus.
For \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), put \(u=a/g,\ v=b/g,\ \beta=\alpha^g\).
Then \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and the exact four-term equation at \(\alpha\) becomes (1).
The [four-term limits](Yin_C_G0345_v743.md#statement) give \(a\log\alpha-\log B\to0\), \(b/(ad)-h(B)/\log B\to0\), and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\).
As \(B\) ranges over a finite set greater than one, \(a\to\infty\) and \(v/u=b/a\to\infty\).
Thus the thresholds in (2) are met simultaneously for all sufficiently large indices.
Also \((u+v)h(\beta)=(a+b)h(\alpha)\le C\).
Every prime power dividing \(R_K(\beta)\) is then at most \(N\), so \(R_K(\beta)\mid H\).

In fact \(R_K(\alpha^g)=gR_K(\alpha)\).
One direction follows by taking the \(gR_K(\alpha)\)-th root.
Conversely, if \(\alpha^{g/r}\in K\) and \(c=\gcd(r,g)\), taking a Bézout combination of the exponents \(g/c\) and \(r/c\) yields \(\alpha^{1/(r/c)}\in K\).
Thus \(r/c\mid R_K(\alpha)\), which implies \(r\mid gR_K(\alpha)\).
This proves (3) and the lower bound on the reduced denominator.
