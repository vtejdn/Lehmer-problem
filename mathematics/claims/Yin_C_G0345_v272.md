# Full signed permutation groups in a five-term Salem family

<a id="statement"></a>
## The trace group and the sign kernel

For a prime \(q\ge5\), let \(L_q\) be the rational splitting field of
\[
 P_q(X)=X^{2q}-2X^{2q-1}+X^q-2X+1
\]
and \(T_q\) the splitting field of its degree-\(q\) trace polynomial \(W_q\), defined by \(P_q(X)=X^qW_q(X+X^{-1})\).
Put \(H_q=\operatorname{Gal}(T_q/\mathbb Q)\) in its permutation action on the \(q\) inverse pairs.
For all sufficiently large primes \(q\) one has \(H_q=A_q\) or \(S_q\), and \(H_q=S_q\) if \(q\equiv5,7\pmod8\).
For **every** prime \(q\equiv1\pmod5\), all \(2^q\) independent inversions occur over \(T_q\), so \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_q/\mathbb Q)=(C_2)^q\rtimes H_q\) in its signed permutation action.
Consequently every sufficiently large prime in the two classes \(q\equiv21,31\pmod{40}\) gives the full signed symmetric group \((C_2)^q\rtimes S_q\).
Their union contains infinitely many primes.

<a id="trace"></a>
## Prime degree and binary Frobenius

The [offset-one Salem result](Yin_C_G0345_v261.md#statement) gives irreducibility, a unique exterior root \(\tau\) with \(3/2<\tau<5/2\), its real inverse and \(2q-2\) roots on the unit circle.
Thus \(m(\tau)=\log\tau\) is uniformly bounded.
A conjugate differing from \(\tau\) by a root of unity would have its modulus, so its torsion-quotient class is a singleton.
Transitivity gives the same conclusion for every conjugate.
As the roots occur in inverse pairs, the [multiplicative labels](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) are precisely those pairs, equivalently the distinct traces \(x+x^{-1}\).
The action on them is transitive of prime degree and hence primitive, since the size of a block divides \(q\).
The [natural alternating action on a minimal block](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#partition) therefore gives \(A_q\le H_q\le S_q\) for sufficiently large \(q\), the compressed degree being \(2q\).

Modulo two the roots of \(P_q\) are the simple roots of orders \(3\) and \(3q\).
Passing to inverse pairs gives one trace of order-three roots and \(q-1\) distinct further traces.
On the latter, Frobenius acts by multiplication by \(-2\) on \(\mathbb F_q^*\). [Binary trace orbits](Yin_C_G0345_v262.md#labels)
In particular \(W_q\bmod2\) is squarefree and this permutation is realized by a Frobenius element of \(H_q\).
Put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)\) and \(h=(q-1)/f\).
The permutation has one fixed bottom trace and \(h\) cycles of length \(f\), giving sign \((-1)^{q-1-h}=(-1)^h\).
In the cyclic group \(\mathbb F_q^*\) the subgroup generated by \(-2\) has odd index precisely when \(-2\) is a nonsquare.
The quadratic character of \(-2\) is negative at \(q\equiv5,7\pmod8\), which selects \(S_q\) from the two alternatives.

<a id="five"></a>
## A simple trace reduction at five

Let \(C_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\), so \(W_q=C_q-2C_{q-1}+1\).
Then \(W_q(2)=-1\) and \(W_q(-2)=-5\).
From \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Z,\ C_{j+1}=ZC_j-C_{j-1}\) one obtains \(C_j'(-2)=(-1)^{j-1}j^2\) for \(j\ge1\).
Consequently \(W_q'(-2)=q^2+2(q-1)^2\equiv1\pmod5\) when \(q\equiv1\pmod5\).

For such \(q\), set \(Y=X^{q-1}\) in characteristic five.
Its derivative is zero, and
\[
 P_q=Y^2X^2+(-2Y^2+Y-2)X+1.
\]
At a repeated root \(x\ne0\), the quadratic in \(X\) with coefficient \(y=x^{q-1}\) therefore has a double root at \(x\).
Its discriminant equals \(4(y-1)^2(y^2+y+1)\) in characteristic five, and its root product gives \(xy=\pm1\).
If \(y^2+y+1=0\), then \(y\) has order three and \(x=\pm y^{-1}\) has order divisible by three.
This contradicts \(x^q=xy=\pm1\) because \(q\ne3\).
Thus \(y=1\) and \(x=\pm1\).
Any repeated trace root would lift under \(X+X^{-1}\) to a repeated root of \(P_q\), so its only candidates are \(2,-2\).
The first is not a root and the second is simple by the derivative above.
It follows that \(5\nmid\operatorname{disc}(W_q)\).
The splitting field \(T_q\) is unramified at five, since all the simple residue roots lift in an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_5\) with residue field splitting \(W_q\bmod5\).

<a id="flips"></a>
## Independent inversions and the supply of primes

Choose one root \(x_i\) in each pair, and set \(t_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\).
Complex conjugation fixes the real pair and flips every other pair, so its action lies in the kernel \(K=\operatorname{Gal}(L_q/T_q)\le\mathbb F_2^q\).
By transitivity its conjugates give the vectors \(\mathbf1+e_i\) for every \(i\).
Their pairwise sums \(e_i+e_j\) generate the even subspace. [Salem sign kernel](Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) · [Christopoulos and McKee, Proposition 2.3](../audits/literature/CM10/readme.md#cm10-salem-kernel-attribution)

The element \(D=\prod_i(x_i-x_i^{-1})\) satisfies
\[
                 D^2=\prod_i(t_i^2-4)=W_q(2)W_q(-2)=5.
\]
For \(q\equiv1\pmod5\), five has valuation one at every prime over it in \(T_q\), hence \(D\notin T_q\).
Some element of \(K\) negates \(D\), so it flips an odd number of pairs.
Together with the even subspace this gives \(K=\mathbb F_2^q\).
The signed permutation group above \(H_q\) has precisely \(2^q|H_q|\) elements, all of which consequently occur in the Galois action.

Given any finite list of old odd primes, choose \(N>1\) divisible by them and by five with \(N\equiv2\pmod8\).
The integer \(A=\Phi_5(N)=N^4+N^3+N^2+N+1\) is \(7\bmod8\), so its Jacobi symbol \(\bigl(\frac{-2}{A}\bigr)=-1\).
Some prime \(q\mid A\) therefore satisfies \(\bigl(\frac{-2}{q}\bigr)=-1\).
It divides neither \(N\) nor five, and \(N\equiv1\pmod q\) would give \(q\mid5\).
As \(N^5\equiv1\pmod q\), the residue of \(N\) has order five, giving \(q\equiv1\pmod5\).
The resulting prime is new and is \(21\) or \(31\bmod40\).
