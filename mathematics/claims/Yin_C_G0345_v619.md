# Prescribing coefficient prefixes on a single orbit under multiplication by roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Equal degrees and conjugate moduli for prescribed prefixes

Let \(k,r\ge1\), and prescribe \(r\) integer polynomials \(H_0,\ldots,H_{r-1}\) of degree below \(k\), each with constant term one.
There is an integer \(m_0>1\) such that for arbitrarily large common degrees \(d\) there are algebraic units \(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_{r-1}\) generating the same degree-\(d\) number field and satisfying \(\gamma_s^{m_0}=\gamma_0^{m_0}\) for every \(s\).
Their monic minimal polynomials \(P_s\in\mathbb Z[X]\) are reciprocal, have no root on the unit circle, and satisfy
\[
              P_s(X)\equiv H_s(X)\pmod {X^k}.
\]
Through the embeddings of the common field, the lists of absolute values of their conjugates agree term by term.
Consequently their total logarithmic Mahler measures agree, and the construction gives an upper bound for this common value depending only on the prescribed data, uniformly in \(d\).

For example, take integral representatives of the [excluded binary prefix](Yin_C_G0345_v613.md#example) and of the constant prefix of the same length.
The latter passes the [binary square test](Yin_C_G0345_v609.md#parity) with multiplier one.
The two residues can occur at the same degree, total measure, and complete list of conjugate moduli, even with the same powers of the underlying units.
The construction below keeps the resulting measures bounded away from zero.
Whether the excluded prefix can occur along irreducible inputs with measures tending to zero remains open.

<a id="traces"></a>
## Simultaneous integral trace values

Choose distinct odd primes \(p,q>\max(3,k,r)\), primitive roots of unity \(\zeta_p,\zeta_q\), and set \(\omega=\zeta_p\zeta_q\), \(m_0=pq\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\omega)\), \(D=(p-1)(q-1)\).
Write \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\omega]\) for its ring of integers, and write a bar for complex conjugation.
For \(1\le j<k\) define the homomorphism
\[
 L_j(a)=\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\omega^{sj}a)\bigr)_{0\le s<r},
 \qquad a\in\mathcal O.
\]
Its image is \(\mathbb Z^r\).
Indeed, for a selected coordinate \(i\) take \(u=r\) and
\[
 a=(\zeta_p^{-ij}-\zeta_p^{-uj})\zeta_q^{-uj}.
\]
The embeddings of \(K\) range independently over those of its two prime cyclotomic subfields.
In either subfield the trace of a power of its primitive root is the prime minus one if the exponent is divisible by that prime, and is \(-1\) otherwise.
The \(p\)-part of \(L_j(a)_s\) is therefore \(p\) at \(s=i\) and zero elsewhere, while its \(q\)-part is always \(-1\).
This gives \(-p\) times the \(i\)-th basis vector, and exchanging \(p,q\) gives \(-q\) times that vector.
Bézout's identity proves surjectivity.

Choose a prime \(t\) dividing \(\Phi_{4pq}(4pq)>1\).
Since the constant coefficient of this cyclotomic polynomial is one, \(t\) is prime to \(4pq\).
The factorization of \(X^{4pq}-1\) into cyclotomic polynomials stays separable modulo \(t\), so the residue of \(4pq\) has exact order \(4pq\).
In particular \(t\equiv1\bmod 4pq\).
Evaluation at the \(D\) distinct primitive \(pq\)-th roots in \(\mathbb F_t\) identifies \(\mathcal O/t\mathcal O\) with \(\mathbb F_t^D\).
Fix a coordinate \(\lambda\) and its distinct complex-conjugate coordinate \(\bar\lambda\), corresponding to inverse roots.

Every prescribed \(v\in\mathbb Z^r\) is \(L_j(a)\) for some \(a\in\mathcal O\) vanishing at both selected coordinates modulo \(t\).
Indeed, reduction of the trace sums gives the columns \((1,x^j,\ldots,(x^j)^{r-1})^\mathsf{T}\) as \(x\) runs over the primitive roots.
The \(x^j\) are distinct since \(j\) is prime to \(pq\).
After setting the two selected coordinates to zero, any \(r\) of the remaining columns form an invertible Vandermonde matrix, since \(D-2\ge r\).
Solve for \(v\bmod t\), lift the solution to \(b\in\mathcal O\), and choose \(c'\in\mathcal O\) with \(L_j(c')=(v-L_j(b))/t\) by integral surjectivity.
Then \(a=b+tc'\) has all the required properties.

<a id="polynomials"></a>
## A family of irreducible polynomials over the cyclotomic field

Choose \(a_1,\ldots,a_{k-1}\) successively, each vanishing at \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\) modulo \(t\).
In the coefficient of \(X^j\) in
\[
     \prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)}
       \sigma\!\left(1+\sum_{\nu=1}^j a_\nu\omega^{s\nu}X^\nu\right)
\]
the contribution from \(a_j\) is exactly \(L_j(a_j)_s\).
The remaining contribution is an integer fixed by the earlier choices, so the simultaneous trace result gives the coefficient \([X^j]H_s\) for every \(s\).
At the first unconstrained index \(k\) choose \(a_k=t\omega^b\), where \(b+sk\) is prime to \(pq\) for \(0\le s<r\).
Such a \(b\) exists by avoiding at most \(r\) residues modulo each prime and applying the Chinese remainder theorem.
Thus \(a_k\omega^{sk}\) generates \(K\) for each \(s\).
Put \(A(X)=1+\sum_{j=1}^k a_jX^j\).

As \(t+1>2\) and \(t+1\equiv2\bmod4\), an odd prime \(\ell\) divides \(t+1\).
Choose an element \(\xi\) of order \(t+1\) in \(\mathbb F_{t^2}^*\).
Its conjugate is \(\xi^{-1}\), so \(W(X)=X^2-(\xi+\xi^{-1})X+1\) is its irreducible polynomial over \(\mathbb F_t\).
For every \(a\ge0\), the polynomial \(W(X^{\ell^a})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_t\).
Indeed any root has order \((t+1)\ell^a\), since its \(\ell^a\)-th power has order \(t+1\) divisible by \(\ell\).
An exponent \(e\) with \(t^e\equiv1\bmod (t+1)\ell^a\) must be even by reduction modulo \(t+1\).
For \(e=2v\) one has
\[
 v_\ell(t^{2v}-1)=v_\ell(t+1)+v_\ell(v).
\]
Here \(v_\ell(t^2-1)=v_\ell(t+1)\) since \(\ell\nmid t-1\).
For \(w\equiv1\bmod\ell\) the binomial expansion gives \(v_\ell(w^\ell-1)=v_\ell(w-1)+1\) because \(\ell\) is odd, and an exponent prime to \(\ell\) preserves this valuation by the geometric sum identity.
This proves the displayed formula, so the order of \(t\) modulo \((t+1)\ell^a\) is \(2\ell^a\).
The Frobenius orbit of a root already has the full polynomial degree.

Fix a positive integer \(c\equiv-(\xi+\xi^{-1})\bmod t\) large enough that
\[
               c>2+2\max_{\sigma}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^k|\sigma(a_j)|\right).
\]
For \(h=\ell^a>k\) form the monic polynomial of degree \(2h\)
\[
        F_h(X)=A(X)+cX^h+X^{2h}\bar A(X^{-1}).
\]
Its reduction at \(\lambda\) is \(W(X^h)\), since the nonconstant coefficients of \(A\) vanish at both \(\lambda\) and \(\bar\lambda\).
Thus \(F_h\) is irreducible over \(K\), as the monic factors of a monic integral polynomial over \(K\) would have integral coefficients and preserve their degrees on reduction.
For every embedding \(\sigma\) and \(|z|=1\), the two noncentral summands of \(\sigma F_h(z)\) have combined absolute value at most \(2(1+\sum_j|\sigma(a_j)|)\), since complex conjugation commutes with \(\sigma\).
In particular \(|\sigma F_h(z)|>2\), uniformly in \(h\).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Minimal polynomials and their common power

Define
\[
          P_s(X)=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)}
                              \sigma\bigl(F_h(\omega^s X)\bigr).
\]
Each factor has constant term one, and the product is integral and monic because \(\operatorname{N}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\omega)=1\).
The relation \(X^{2h}F_h(X^{-1})=\bar F_h(X)\) makes the reciprocal of \(F_h(\omega^s X)\) equal to \(\omega^{2hs}\bar F_h(\omega^{-s}X)\).
Taking norms proves reciprocity of \(P_s\), and \(h>k\) gives the required prefix from the construction of \(A\).
Every conjugate factor is irreducible over \(K\) by the invertible change of variable.
They are pairwise nonassociate because their constants are one and the coefficient \(a_k\omega^{sk}\) of \(X^k\) generates \(K\).
The Galois group permutes these factors transitively, so any rational monic factor must contain them all or none.
This proves that \(P_s\) is irreducible of degree \(d=2hD\).

Take a root \(\beta\) of \(F_h\) and \(\gamma_s=\omega^{-s}\beta\), a root of \(P_s\).
Each generates \(K(\beta)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) since both field degrees are \(2hD\).
The constant and leading terms of \(P_s\) make each \(\gamma_s\) an algebraic unit, and \(\gamma_s^{pq}=\beta^{pq}\).
Every embedding sends \(\omega\) to a root of unity, giving identical absolute values at that embedding for all the \(\gamma_s\).
Every \(P_s\) has no circle root by the estimate on \(\sigma F_h\).
With normalized circle measure \(d\mu\), Jensen's formula and rotation invariance give the common bound
\[
 D\log2 < m(P_s)=\int\log|P_s|\,d\mu
 \le \sum_{\sigma}\log\!\left(c+2\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^k|\sigma(a_j)|\right)\right).
\]
Both bounds are independent of \(h\).
