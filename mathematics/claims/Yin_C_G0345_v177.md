# The exact resultant of a factor with the reduced trinomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Divisibility at the seed

Let \(Q=X^a-X^b-1\), \(0\le b<a\), \(k=\gcd(a,b)\), and \(q=Q/\gcd(Q,Q^*)\) with the gcd monic and \(f^*=X^{\deg f}f(1/X)\).
Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be any divisor of \(X^Nq\pm q^*\) with \(N\ge1\).
This includes every Salem minimal polynomial from the [length-six reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v174.md#statement), because its roots have infinite order and the removed common factor is cyclotomic.
Put \(A=a/k\), \(B=b/k\).
The following table determines the finite ring \(\mathbb Z[X]/(q,q^*)\) and the exact positive integer \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,S)|\).
In a row with \(p,H\), the ring is \(\mathbb F_p[X]/H\), the class of \(X\) corresponding to \(X\), and
\[
          |\operatorname{Res}(q,S)|=
                       p^{\deg\gcd(H,S\bmod p)}.                      \tag{1}
\]
In a unit-ideal row the ring is zero and the resultant has absolute value one.

| Exponents | Ring data |
| --- | --- |
| \(b=0\) | \(p=3,\ H=X^a+1\) |
| \(b>0,\ B\) odd, \(A\equiv 2B\pmod 3\) | \(p=2,\ H=X^{2k}+X^k+1\) |
| \(b>0,\ B\) even, \(A\not\equiv 2B\pmod 3\) | \(p=3,\ H=X^k+1\) |
| All other \(b>0\) | Unit ideal |

Consequently, for each complex root \(\beta\) of \(q\), \(S(\beta)\) divides \(p\) in \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) in a prime row and is a unit there in a unit-ideal row.
The same assertions hold for \(q(\tau)\) in \(\mathbb Z[\tau]\) for any root \(\tau\) of \(S\).
In particular, with \(k=1\) and \(b>0\) at most one irreducible factor of the reduced multiple, counted with multiplicity, can have nonunit resultant with \(q\).
The complete nonunit contribution, when present, is \(4\) in the binary row or \(3\) in the ternary row.
These constraints at finite primes sharpen the [resultant bound used in the degree asymptotic](Yin_C_G0345_v176.md#elimination).
They leave open whether the Salem factors can tend to one.

<a id="primitive"></a>
## The two coprime exponents

First suppose \(b>0\), and write \(G(Z)=Z^A-Z^B-1\) with coprime \(A>B>0\).
At any root \(z\) of \(G\), put \(u=z^B\), so \(z^A=1+u\).
Its reversed polynomial satisfies
\[
                    G^*(z)=-\frac{u^2+u+1}{u}.
\]
The map \(z\mapsto u\) gives a bijection with the roots of \(J(U)=U^A-(1+U)^B\).
Indeed \(u\ne0,-1\) and if \(hB+jA=1\), the inverse is \(z=u^h(1+u)^j\), using \(u^A=(1+u)^B\).
The derivative test in the [squarefreeness argument](Yin_C_G0345_v176.md#elimination) applies to \(G\), so this bijection between the roots of two degree-\(A\) polynomials also proves \(J\) squarefree.
Every \(z\) and \(u\) in question is an algebraic unit.
For a primitive cube root \(\omega\), put \(\Phi_3(U)=U^2+U+1\) and \(\Phi_6(Z)=Z^2-Z+1\).
If \(J(\omega)\ne0\), taking the product over the roots of \(G\) gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Res}(G,G^*)|
 =|\operatorname{Res}(J,\Phi_3)|
 =|\omega^A-(-1)^B\omega^{2B}|^2 .                                 \tag{2}
\]
The last expression is \(4\) for odd \(B\) in the congruence class \(A\equiv2B\pmod3\), and \(1\) for the other odd-\(B\) classes.
It is \(3\) for even \(B\) outside that congruence class.

<a id="cancellation"></a>
## Cancellation in the common-root case

It remains to consider even \(B\) and \(A\equiv2B\pmod3\), precisely the case \(J(\omega)=0\).
The two common roots of \(G,G^*\) correspond to \(u=\omega,\bar\omega\).
Each inverse \(z=u^h(1+u)^j\) has order dividing six, and \(z^A=1+u\) has order six.
Thus they are exactly the two primitive sixth roots and their common factor is \(\Phi_6\).
Write \(g=G/\Phi_6\) and \(J_0=J/\Phi_3\).
The identity for \(G^*(z)\), applied only to roots of \(g\) and divided by \(\Phi_6(z)\), yields
\[
 |\operatorname{Res}(g,g^*)|
          =\frac{|J_0(\omega)|^2}{|g(\zeta)|^2},
\]
where \(\zeta\) is the root of \(\Phi_6\) with \(\zeta^B=\omega\).
At this pair \(J_0(\omega)=J'(\omega)/\Phi_3'(\omega)\) and \(g(\zeta)=G'(\zeta)/\Phi_6'(\zeta)\).
Both denominator moduli are \(\sqrt 3\).
For the numerators, \(J'(\omega)=\omega^A(A/\omega-B/(1+\omega))\), while \(\zeta G'(\zeta)=A(1+\omega)-B\omega\).
Since \(|\omega|=|1+\omega|=|\zeta|=1\), these moduli agree, and the reduced resultant has absolute value one.
In every nonexceptional class put \(g=G\).

<a id="rings"></a>
## From the primitive resultant to each individual divisor

For any monic \(g\), the additive group of \(\mathbb Z[Z]/g\) is free of rank \(\deg g\).
Multiplication by \(g^*\) has determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(g,g^*)\), so the quotient by both polynomials has order the absolute resultant.
In the order-four case a primitive cube root in \(\mathbb F_4\) annihilates both primitive polynomials because \(B\not\equiv0\pmod3\) and \(A\equiv2B\pmod3\).
Evaluation at it is onto \(\mathbb F_4\), and the order proves the kernel in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) is exactly \((2,\Phi_3(Z))\).
In the order-three case \(G(-1)=-3\) since \(A\) is odd and \(B\) even.
Evaluation at \(-1\) modulo three likewise identifies \((g,g^*)=(3,Z+1)\).
Order one means the generators generate the unit ideal.
Replacing \(Z\) by \(X^k\) takes \(g\) to \(q\), since the squarefree common factor, when present, becomes \(\Phi_6(X^k)\) and the remaining coprime pair stays coprime over \(\mathbb Q\).
Extending the displayed ideals to \(\mathbb Z[X]\) gives the table for \(b>0\).
At \(b=0\) one has \(q=X^a-2\), \(q^*=1-2X^a\), hence \((q,q^*)=(3,X^a+1)\).

Write \(F=X^Nq\pm q^*\).
Since \(S\mid F\), one has \((q,q^*)=(q,F)\subseteq(q,S)\), so quotienting the tabulated ring further by \(S\) yields precisely \(\mathbb Z[X]/(q,S)\).
Using the same multiplication determinant for \(S\) on \(\mathbb Z[X]/q\) gives (1), including repeated roots of \(H\).
Membership of \(p\), or of \(1\) in a unit-ideal row, in \((q,S)\) gives the two assertions about divisibility by evaluation at \(\beta,\tau\).
When \(k=1\) in the two nontrivial \(b>0\) rows, \(H\) is irreducible of degree two or one over its indicated field.
Thus (1) permits only \(1\) or the full order \(4\) or \(3\) respectively, and multiplicativity over every factor of \(F\) gives the final assertion.
