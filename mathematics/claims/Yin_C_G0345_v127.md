# An infinite Salem descent at level minus one without a rational trace root

<a id="statement"></a>
## The input, the smaller Salem factor and the only possible cofactor

Put \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(f(Z)=Z^2-Z-1\) and \(f^*(Z)=Z^2f(1/Z)=1-Z-Z^2\).
For every integer \(n\ge2\), set
\[
      P_n(Z)=Z^{2n-2}f(Z)+f^*(Z),\qquad
      Q_n(Z)=P_n(Z)+Z^n,\qquad
      P_n(Z)=Z^n S_n(Z+Z^{-1}).
\]
The source uses the classical [Salem construction from a Pisot polynomial](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#smy15-u002).
For this choice every \(P_n\) is already the irreducible minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\sigma_n\) of degree \(2n\).
Thus \(S_n\) is its actual trace minimal polynomial.
The lift at the original integer level \(S_n(X)=-1\) is \(Q_n\).
It has exactly one Salem factor, whose positive exterior root \(\tau_n\) satisfies \(1<\tau_n<\sigma_n\).
More precisely the two factors in the following description are monic:
\[
 Q_n=\begin{cases}
       \operatorname{minpol}(\tau_n)\,\Phi_{18}, & n\equiv6\pmod{18},\\
       \operatorname{minpol}(\tau_n), & n\not\equiv6\pmod{18}.
      \end{cases}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The degree of \(\tau_n\) is \(2n-6\) in the first case and \(2n\) in the second.
The full trace polynomial \(S_n+1\) has no rational root in either case.
All its roots are real, exactly one lies above \(2\), and that root is \(\tau_n+\tau_n^{-1}<\sigma_n+\sigma_n^{-1}\).
Thus the rational trace roots found in [all seven successful levels in Boyd's 45-row sample](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0164/readme.md#boyd-descent-search) are not necessary for descent.
The family includes descents of unbounded degree both with and without a degree drop.

For \(n\ge3\), one has \(\tau_n<\varphi<\sigma_n\), and
\[
    \varphi^{\,2n-2}(\sigma_n-\varphi)\longrightarrow 2\varphi/\sqrt5,
    \qquad
    \varphi^{\,n-2}(\varphi-\tau_n)\longrightarrow 1/\sqrt5 .        \tag{2}
\]
Both logarithmic Mahler measures tend to \(\log\varphi>0\).
For every \(n\ge7\) with \(n\not\equiv6\pmod{18}\), the level \(-1\) of the intermediate Salem trace \(S_n+1\) gives a second strict Salem descent.
Its exterior root also tends to \(\varphi\).

<a id="oscillation"></a>
## Alternating values at every interior sampling interval

The reciprocal monic polynomial \(P_n\) has a monic integral trace \(S_n\) of degree \(n\), by the recurrence for \(Z^j+Z^{-j}\).
Write \(z=e^{i\theta},\ t=z^{n-1},\ v=z-z^{-1}\).
The Laurent identity, valid for every \(z\ne0\), is
\[
                    z^{-n}P_n(z)=v(t-t^{-1})-(t+t^{-1}).             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(f(z)/z=v-1\) and \(f^*(z)/z=-v-1\).
At \(\theta_j=j\pi/(n-1)\), \(0\le j\le n-1\), one has \(t=(-1)^j\) and hence \(S_n(2\cos\theta_j)=-2(-1)^j\).
The points \(2\cos\theta_j\) strictly decrease from \(2\) to \(-2\).
Both \(S_n\) and \(S_n+1\) consequently have a root in each of the \(n-1\) open intervals they cut out.
Each polynomial is negative at \(2\) and monic, giving one further root strictly above \(2\).
These \(n\) distinct real roots exhaust the degree in each case, so all are simple.
Moreover \(S_n+1\) equals one at the exterior trace of \(S_n\), putting its unique exterior trace strictly between \(2\) and the exterior trace of \(S_n\).

Each reciprocal lift now has just one positive real inverse pair off the circle, with every root simple.
Each of its monic irreducible factors is an algebraic-unit polynomial since its integral constant term divides one.
The factor containing the exterior root must also contain the interior root, by the product of its root moduli.
Every other factor has all roots on the unit circle and is cyclotomic by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
We determine all such factors for both lifts from (3).

<a id="source-torsion"></a>
## No root of unity in the source

Let \(z=\zeta\) be torsion and \(T=\zeta^{2n-2}=t^2\).
If \(P_n(\zeta)=0\) then \(t\ne\pm1\) by (3), and
\[
          v=\zeta-\zeta^{-1}=\frac{T+1}{T-1}.
\]
For \(q=\operatorname{ord}(T)\), extend an embedding sending \(T\) to \(e^{2\pi i/q}\) to the field of \(\zeta\).
It gives \(|\cot(\pi/q)|\le2\), since the conjugates of \(v\) all have modulus at most two.
This excludes \(q\ge7\), as \(2\cos(2\pi/7)>6/5\) implies \(\cot(\pi/7)>2\) by the half-angle identity.
For the cosine bound, \(2\cos(2\pi/7)\) is the largest root of \(X^3+X^2-2X-1\) from \(\Phi_7\), and this cubic changes sign between \(6/5\) and \(2\).

For a primitive root \(\xi\) of prime-power order \(\ell^h\), the norm of \(1-\xi\) in \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) is \(\Phi_{\ell^h}(1)=\ell\), by the geometric sum for this cyclotomic polynomial.
Thus \(c/(1-\xi)\) integral for a nonzero rational integer \(c\) requires \(\ell\mid c\), whether integrality is first obtained in \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) or in a larger field.
Here \(v-1=2/(T-1)\) is integral, ruling out \(q=3,5\).
For \(q=2\) one gets \(v=0\), so \(\zeta=\pm1\) and \(T=1\), which was excluded.
For \(q=4\) one has \(v^2=-1\), whence \(\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}=1\), so \(\zeta^2\) has order six.
For \(q=6\) one has \(v^2=-3\), giving \(\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}=-1\), so \(\zeta^2\) has order three.
In neither case can the power \(T=(\zeta^2)^{n-1}\) have the proposed order.
This exhausts \(q\), proving torsion exclusion and irreducibility of \(P_n\).
Its degree at least four and the neutral roots already counted make its exterior root a Salem number.
Irreducibility of \(P_n\) also gives irreducibility of \(S_n\), since a trace factorization would lift to a nontrivial factorization of \(P_n\).

<a id="output-torsion"></a>
## The only possible order after the shift

If instead \(Q_n(\zeta)=0\), equation (3) gives \(t\ne\pm1\) and
\[
             v=\frac{t+t^{-1}-1}{t-t^{-1}}
               =\frac{t^2-t+1}{t^2-1}.
\]
Write \(q=\operatorname{ord}(t)\).
At each primitive conjugate \(t=e^{i\theta}\) the absolute value \(|2\cos\theta-1|/(2|\sin\theta|)\) must be at most two.
If the distance \(\delta\) from \(\theta\) to \(\pi\) lies in \((0,\pi/5]\), this quotient exceeds two.
Indeed \(1+2\cos\delta\) decreases and \(4\sin\delta\) increases on that interval, and at \(\pi/5\) the difference of their squares is \(7\varphi-10>0\).
For odd \(q\ge5\), the reduced residue \((q-1)/2\) gives \(\delta=\pi/q\).
For \(4\mid q,\ q\ge12\), use \(q/2-1\) with \(\delta=2\pi/q\).
For \(q\equiv2\pmod4,\ q\ge22\), the residue \(q/2-2\) is coprime to \(q\), since its gcd with \(q\) divides four and it is odd.
It gives \(\delta=4\pi/q\).
The surviving orders after excluding \(t=\pm1\) are \(3,4,6,8,10,14,18\).

The integrality of \(v-1=(2-t)/(t^2-1)\) implies that \(3/(t^2-1)\) is integral, by multiplying by \(2+t\) and adding one.
For \(q=4,8,10,14\) the respective orders of \(t^2\) are \(2,4,5,7\), so the prime-power norm criterion excludes them.
For \(q=3\) one obtains \(v^2=-4/3\), also impossible for an algebraic integer.
For \(q=6\) the numerator \(t+t^{-1}-1\) vanishes, giving \(\zeta=\pm1\), inconsistent with \(t=\zeta^{n-1}\) of order six.

At \(q=18\), \(t^3+t^{-3}=1\), hence \(v=t^{-2}-t^2\) by multiplying by \(t-t^{-1}\).
The two solutions of \(\zeta-\zeta^{-1}=t^{-2}-t^2\) are \(t^{-2}\) and \(-t^2=t^{11}\).
The former has order nine and cannot have \(t\) as a power.
For the latter the power condition is \(11(n-1)\equiv1\bmod18\), or \(n\equiv6\bmod18\).
Conversely at every such \(n\), as \(t\) runs over the primitive eighteenth roots, \(\zeta=t^{11}\) does also, and (3) verifies \(Q_n(\zeta)=0\).
The trace oscillation gives multiplicity one.
The remaining irreducible factor has degree \(2n\) or \(2n-6\), at least four in either case, and so has neutral conjugates as well as its positive real inverse pair.
This proves (1) and the strict Salem descent.
Writing the lift of its irreducible trace polynomial and, when present, that of \(\Phi_{18}\) gives the entire trace factorization of \(S_n+1\).
For \(r=\tau_n+\tau_n^{-1}\), a neutral embedding of the totally real field \(\mathbb Q(r)\) makes \(r^2-4\) negative.
Thus \(\tau_n\) has degree two over its trace field.
The optional eighteenth-root trace is the irreducible cubic \(X^3-3X-1\), by \(t^3+t^{-3}=1\) and the rational-root test.
In particular neither factor can supply a rational trace.

<a id="limits"></a>
## Limits of the two exterior roots

At \(\varphi\), \(P_n(\varphi)=f^*(\varphi)=-2\varphi\), and \(Q_n(\varphi)=\varphi^n-2\varphi>0\) for \(n\ge3\).
Together with the unique exterior roots and the negative values at one, these evaluations give the stated ordering.
At any fixed \(z>1\) with \(z\ne\varphi\), both \(z^{2-2n}P_n(z)\) and \(z^{2-2n}Q_n(z)\) tend to \(f(z)\).
Opposite signs at fixed points on the two sides of \(\varphi\) show \(\sigma_n,\tau_n\to\varphi\).
The root equations give
\[
 f(\sigma_n)=-\sigma_n^{2-2n}f^*(\sigma_n),\qquad
 f(\tau_n)=-\tau_n^{2-n}\bigl(1+\tau_n^{-n}f^*(\tau_n)\bigr).
\]
Both sequences are eventually bounded below by a constant greater than one, \(f^*\) is bounded there near \(\varphi\), and \(f'(\varphi)=\sqrt5\).
These formulas show exponential convergence, so raising \(\sigma_n/\varphi\) or \(\tau_n/\varphi\) to an exponent of magnitude at most \(2n\) gives \(1+o(1)\).
The mean value theorem now gives (2).
In particular the construction establishes strict descent at the specified positive limit.

<a id="second"></a>
## A second descent at the intermediate trace's own level minus one

Suppose \(n\ge7\) and \(n\not\equiv6\bmod18\), so (1) makes \(S_n+1\) the actual trace minimal polynomial of \(\tau_n\).
With \(h=n-1\), (3) gives the exact lift
\[
   P_n(z)+2z^n=(z^h-1)A_h(z),\qquad A_h(z)=z^hf(z)-f^*(z).
\]
On the circle \(f\) never vanishes and \(f^*/f=(1+2i\sin\theta)/(1-2i\sin\theta)\).
This quotient has the periodic argument \(2\arctan(2\sin\theta)\), whose derivative \(4\cos\theta/(1+4\sin^2\theta)\) has absolute value at most four.
Thus \(h\theta-2\arctan(2\sin\theta)\) strictly increases by \(2\pi h\) over a turn.
The equation \(A_h(e^{i\theta})=0\) has exactly \(h\) simple solutions there.
Also \(A_h(1)=0,\ A_h'(1)=4-h<0\) and \(A_h(\tau_n)=\tau_n^n/(\tau_n^h-1)>0\).
Hence \(A_h\) has a root \(\rho_n\in(1,\tau_n)\).
Anti-reciprocity supplies its inverse, exhausting degree \(h+2\).
As in [the original root count](#oscillation), exactly one irreducible factor is noncyclotomic and contains this inverse pair.
It cannot have degree two because \(2<\rho_n+\rho_n^{-1}<\varphi+\varphi^{-1}=\sqrt5<3\) contains no integer trace.
Thus the smaller positive root \(\rho_n\) is again Salem.
At any fixed \(z>1\), \(z^{-h}A_h(z)\to f(z)\), so bracketing \(\varphi\) proves \(\rho_n\to\varphi\).
