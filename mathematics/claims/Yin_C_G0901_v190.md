# How many dyadic conjugates can have bounded local degree?

<a id="statement"></a>
## An absolute bound independent of the height and exponents

Let \(q\ge2\) be an integer, put \(p=q+1\), and take coprime positive integers \(u<v\).
For any integer \(B\ge1\), the number of distinct dyadic unit solutions of
\[
                \left(\frac{pz-q}{p-qz}\right)^u=z^v,\qquad
                [\mathbb Q_2(z):\mathbb Q_2]\le B,
\]
including trivial solutions, is at most
\[
                     8\cdot2^B+32 B^2\,2^{\lfloor B/2\rfloor}.         \tag{1}
\]
In particular it is \(O(2^B)\) with an absolute constant.
The exponential order \(2^B\) is attained up to an absolute factor among these graph equations.
For a [genuine integral trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) every root of the marked reciprocal minimum satisfies the equation. [Graph criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion)
Thus if its degree \(d\) tends to infinity, the proportion of its conjugates of local degree at most \(B(d)\) tends to zero whenever \(\log_2 d-B(d)\to+\infty\).
In particular the largest degree of an irreducible dyadic factor is at least \(\log_2 d-O(1)\).
In a hypothetical family with \(q\to\infty\), the degrees necessarily tend to infinity, since bounded-degree monic polynomials with all complex roots of modulus at most \(3/2\) have bounded coefficients, whereas their unique exterior roots tend to one from above.

<a id="disk"></a>
## Counting roots in a disk of positive depth

Write \(\nu(2)=1\), and fix an integer \(E\ge1\).
Every closed disk \(\nu(z-c)\ge1/E\) contains fewer than \(16E^2\) dyadic unit solutions.
For \(E=1\) it contains at most four.
To see this, center a disk containing a unit solution at one of its solutions \(z_0\), and write \(z=z_0(1+w)\).
Use \(x=q/p,\ m=u,\ N=v-u\) at even \(q\), and \(x=p/q,\ m=-u,\ N=v+u\) at odd \(q\).
Then \(\nu(x)\ge1\) and the equation is \(z^N=V(x,z)^m\) for \(V(x,z)=(1-x/z)/(1-xz)\).
Set \(A=(1-x/z_0)^{-1}\), \(D=xz_0/(1-xz_0)\).
At every solution in the disk, division by the equation at \(z_0\) and taking logarithms give
\[
        G(w)=(N+m)\log(1+w)-m\log(1+Aw)+m\log(1-Dw)=0 .
\]
Only the forward implication is used, and the logarithms converge and are additive on principal units of strictly positive depth.
By the [quadratic numerator and integral recurrence](Yin_C_G0901_v183.md#disk), the derivative \(G'(w)=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jw^j\) is nonzero and \(\min_{j\ge0}\nu(h_j)=\min_{0\le j\le2}\nu(h_j)=t_0\).
Work in a finite extension containing the roots under comparison and an element of valuation \(1/E\).
After scaling \(w\) by that element, one of the first three coefficients of \(G\) has valuation at most \(t_0+3/E\).
The coefficient of degree \(j\ge1\) has valuation at least \(t_0+j/E-\nu(j)\), which tends to infinity.
Consequently the last index \(J\) attaining the smallest coefficient valuation satisfies
\[
                          (J-3)/E\le \nu(J).
\]
Division by linear factors in the scaled disk bounds the number of distinct zeros by \(J\), as in the cited argument.
If \(j\ge16E^2\), then \((j-3)/E\ge3j/(4E)\ge3\sqrt j>2\sqrt j\ge\log_2j\ge\nu(j)\), so \(J<16E^2\).
For \(E=1\), \(j-3>\nu(j)\) for every \(j\ge5\), which gives the bound four.
This counts the full unit equation and includes any roots \(1,-1\) in the disk.
For the smaller open disks \(\nu(z-c)>1\), the [two-root bound after removing the signs](Yin_C_G0901_v188.md#statement) gives a different count at the endpoint.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Residue degree and ramification of a unit of bounded degree

For a unit root \(z\) let \(E_z,f_z\) be the ramification index and residue degree of \(\mathbb Q_2(z)\).
Its degree is \(E_z f_z\).
Write \(r_z=[\mathbb F_2(\bar z):\mathbb F_2]\), which divides \(f_z\) and can be smaller.
The unique odd-order root of unity \(\xi\) reducing to \(\bar z\) lies in \(\mathbb Q_2(z)\) by simple-root lifting of \(X^{2^{r_z}-1}-1\).
Consequently \(\nu(z-\xi)\ge1/E_z\), with \(\nu(0)=+\infty\).
For each possible residue there is just one such center \(\xi\) in the fixed algebraic closure.

There are fewer than \(2^{B+1}\) possible nonzero residues of degree at most \(B\), by summing the sizes of \(\mathbb F_{2^j}^{\times}\) for \(1\le j\le B\).
If \(r_z>B/2\) and \(E_z f_z\le B\), then \(E_z=1\).
The bound four on the disk at \(\xi\) gives at most \(8\cdot2^B\) roots of this kind.
For \(r_z\le\lfloor B/2\rfloor\), there are fewer than \(2^{\lfloor B/2\rfloor+1}\) possible residues.
All the corresponding roots of degree at most \(B\) satisfy \(\nu(z-\xi)\ge1/B\), so each such disk contains fewer than \(16B^2\) roots.
This proves (1), and \(B^2 2^{-B/2}\) is bounded for positive integers \(B\).
If every local factor of a degree-\(d\) genuine minimum has degree at most \(B\), this gives \(d\le C2^B\) for an absolute \(C\).
Applying the same count only to its low-degree conjugates gives the proportion assertion.

<a id="ramification"></a>
## The power of two in the exponent difference can force ramification

Put \(s=\nu(N),\ e=\nu(pq)\).
Apart from \(1,-1\) and the [possible single reciprocal pair in the two sign disks](Yin_C_G0901_v188.md#signs), every unit solution satisfies
\[
                   s\le e+1+\lceil\log_2 E_z\rceil.                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(l=\nu(z-z^{-1})\le2\) away from those two disks.
For \(s>0\) coprimality gives \(m\) odd, so that the graph equation yields
\[
         \nu(z^N-1)=\nu(V(x,z)^m-1)=\nu(V(x,z)-1)=e+l\le e+2 .
\]
Here \(V-1=x(z-z^{-1})/(1-xz)\), and taking an odd integral power preserves the valuation of the difference from one for every principal unit, also for negative exponents.
Since \(\bar z^{N/2^s}=1\), one has \(\nu(z^{N/2^s}-1)\ge1/E_z\).
Repeated squaring increases a positive difference valuation \(t\) to at least \(\min(2t,t+1)\).
After \(k=\lceil\log_2 E_z\rceil\) squarings the valuation is at least one and every later squaring adds at least one.
If \(s\ge k\) this gives \(1+s-k\le e+2\), while \(s<k\) and \(s=0\) already satisfy (2).

<a id="sharpness"></a>
## Unramified examples and the global question

Take \(B\ge4,\ q=4,\ u=1,\ v=2^B\).
Here \(N=2^B-1\) is odd, so [simple-root lifting](Yin_C_G0901_v185.md#count) places all \(2^B-1\) unit roots in the unramified extension of degree \(B\), one above each nonzero element of \(\mathbb F_{2^B}\).
Only \(1\) among the signs satisfies the equation, leaving \(2^B-2\) nontrivial roots.
This proves sharp exponential order for the count in (1) even after deleting the signs.
These examples concern the graph equation and do not supply a genuine integral trace pole.
The estimate leaves room for a hypothetical genuine family whose dyadic conjugates mostly generate fields of increasing degree.
