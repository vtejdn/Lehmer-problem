# A single finite prime can carry the obstruction in the powered capacity tests

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational irreducible examples with a long integral prefix

Fix any rational prime \(p\).
There exist monic reciprocal irreducible polynomials \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/p][Z]\) of unbounded even degree \(d=2D\) such that exactly two roots have modulus different from one.
They form a positive real inverse pair \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\), with \(\log\lambda\asymp d^{-1/2}\).
The other roots lie on the unit circle, all roots are simple, and no quotient of two distinct roots is a root of unity.
For every integer \(k\ge1\) the compact set
\[
 K_k(F)=\bigcup_{F(\alpha)=0}\bigl([0,\alpha^{2k}]\cup[0,\alpha^{4k}]\bigr)
\]
has logarithmic capacity strictly greater than one.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that all Newton sums of orders \(1\le j\le c\sqrt d\) vanish.
The maximum coefficientwise distance of \(F\) from an integral monic reciprocal polynomial of the same degree is \(O(d^{-1/2})\).

Here \(m_\infty(F)=\sum_\alpha\log^+|\alpha|=\log\lambda\) uses only the archimedean roots of the monic rational polynomial.
In the construction the primitive integer minimum has positive leading coefficient \(p^{hD+1}\), with \(h\) a positive integer allowed to grow for each \(d\).
Its total log Mahler measure is exactly \((hD+1)\log p+\log\lambda\).
All finite places away from \(p\) are unit places for the roots.
Consequently these are examples for the relaxed conditions and give no shrinking sequence for the integer-polynomial problem.

The geometric capacity test of Dimitrov in the formulation of van Ittersum, Ringeling and Zudilin applies to a monic irreducible noncyclotomic polynomial in \(\mathbb Z[Z]\) of degree greater than one when the polynomial obtained by squaring every root is still irreducible.
It also applies after every positive powering when all such powers preserve degree.
Their unrestricted geometric examples let the product of the expanded spine lengths tend to one. [Published paper, Section 2 and Theorems 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972721000654)
The [existing square-energy bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) for integral units gives \(d\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert>1}\log^2|\alpha|\ge1/20\) under its stated reciprocal hypotheses.
The following construction retains the two linked powers of a common rational conjugate set, the tests after all positive powers, and growing exact initial Newton data.
It isolates a failure of sufficiency when integrality at one prime is relaxed.

<a id="star"></a>
## Capacity after extending one arm of the regular star

Let \(S_n=\{z:z^n\in[0,1]\}\), the regular star with \(n\) arms of length one.
On its unbounded complementary domain the Green function with pole at infinity is \(n^{-1}g_{[0,1]}(z^n)\), by harmonicity, boundary values and the normalization at infinity.
Comparison at infinity gives \({\rm cap}(S_n)=4^{-1/n}\).
On the positive real ray \(R>1\) the interval formula gives
\[
 g_n(R)=\frac1n\operatorname{arcosh}(2R^n-1)
       =\frac2n\operatorname{arcosh}(R^{n/2}).
\]
The normalized exterior conformal map sends the ray \(1<R<\infty\) to the positive real ray \(1<w<\infty\) by real symmetry, the orientation at infinity and injectivity.
It sends \(R\) to \(e^{g_n(R)}\).
After extending the positive arm of the star to \(R\), the complementary image is the exterior of the closed unit disk with the extra slit \([1,e^{g_n(R)}]\).
The [disk and slit formula](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#argument), equivalently applying \(w+w^{-1}\) to this domain and using interval length divided by four, now gives
\[
 \log {\rm cap}(S_n\cup[1,R])
       =-\frac{\log4}{n}+2\log\cosh\bigl(g_n(R)/2\bigr).                 \tag{1}
\]
For \(R=e^{4/\sqrt n}\), \(g_n(R)=4/\sqrt n+O(1/n)\).
The right side of (1) is \((4-\log4+o(1))/n>0\).
The strict inequality continues to hold for every larger \(R\) at that \(n\).

<a id="prefix"></a>
## Neutral angles, the expanding root and the exact Newton sums

Choose a sufficiently large odd prime \(n\), let \(J=\lfloor\sqrt n/4\rfloor\), and set \(t=1/\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\).
Write
\[
 B_n(Z)=1+Z+\cdots+Z^{n-1},\qquad
 Q_t(Z)=(Z-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-tZ^{J+1},\qquad F_t=B_n Q_t .
\]
The positive real pair of \(Q_t\) is \(e^{\pm u}\), where
\[
             t=4\sinh(u/2)\sinh((J+1/2)u).
\]
The right side is continuous and strictly increasing for \(u>0\).
At \(u=a/\sqrt n\), its product with \(\sqrt n\) tends to \(2a\sinh(a/4)\).
At \(a=1\) this is less than one, and at \(a=2\) it is greater than one.
It follows that \(1/\sqrt n<u<2/\sqrt n\).

On \(Z=e^{i\theta}\) the real value of \(Z^{-J-1}Q_t(Z)\) equals
\[
              -4\sin(\theta/2)\sin((J+1/2)\theta)-t .
\]
At the \(J+1\) consecutive points \(\theta_l=(2l+1)\pi/(2J+1)\), \(0\le l\le J\), these values have alternating signs for large \(n\), because \(4\sin(\pi/(4J+2))>t\).
There is a root in every intervening open interval.
With their conjugates and \(e^{\pm u}\) these account for the degree, so all are simple.
No root of unity \(\zeta\) can be a root of \(Q_t\): the resulting expression \(t=(\zeta-1)(\zeta^{2J+1}-1)\zeta^{-J-1}\) is an algebraic integer, whereas \(0<t<1\) is rational.
Hence \(F_t\) has only simple roots and none is \(1\) or \(-1\).

For \(n\nmid k\), the \(2k\)-th powers of the zeros of \(B_n\) permute the \(n\)-th roots of unity other than one.
Together with the positive real arm up to \(e^{4ku}\) they include the extended star in (1).
For \(n\mid k\), the interval \([0,e^{4ku}]\) alone has capacity \(e^{4ku}/4>1\).
This proves the strict capacity inequalities for the auxiliary \(F_t\).

Its degree is \(d=n+2J+1=2D\).
The identity
\[
        F_t=(Z^n-1)(Z^{2J+1}-1)-t Z^{J+1}B_n
\]
shows that the first \(J\) coefficients after the leading one vanish.
Newton's identities give zero for all the first \(J\) power sums.
Also the largest coefficientwise distance from the displayed integral product is \(t\).

<a id="place"></a>
## Making the whole conjugate set irreducible while preserving all powered tests

Choose \(t_h\in p^{-h}\mathbb Z\) tending to \(t\) and put
\[
       F_h=B_nQ_{t_h}+p^{-hD-1}Z^D .
\]
This monic reciprocal polynomial has constant one and the same first \(J\) zero coefficients.
It can be written uniquely as \(Z^D H_h(Z+Z^{-1})\) with \(H_h\) monic of degree \(D\).
Indeed the reciprocal Laurent pairs \(Z^j+Z^{-j}\) are monic integral polynomials of degree \(j\) in \(Z+Z^{-1}\) by their two-term recurrence.
In the trace polynomial for \(B_nQ_{t_h}\) all coefficients therefore belong to \(p^{-h}\mathbb Z\); the extra term adds \(p^{-hD-1}\) to its constant alone.
Thus the constant of \(H_h\) has \(p\)-adic valuation exactly \(-hD-1\) and all its other coefficients have valuation at least \(-h\).
Every point of its Newton polygon except the endpoints lies strictly above the segment from \((0,-hD-1)\) to \((D,0)\).
Equivalently, if \(v\) is the valuation of any root, then \(v\ge0\) would make the constant the unique summand of smallest valuation; for \(v<0\), the interior terms all lie strictly above the lesser of the valuations of the two endpoint terms by the segment inequality.
The endpoints must therefore tie and \(v=-(hD+1)/D\).
The denominator in lowest terms is \(D\), so the ramification degree and the degree of each root over \(\mathbb Q_p\) are at least \(D\).
This proves irreducibility of \(H_h\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\), hence over \(\mathbb Q\).

As \(h\to\infty\) for fixed \(n\), the coefficients approach those of \(F_t\).
The corresponding real trace polynomial for \(F_t\) has one simple root greater than two and its other \(D-1\) distinct simple roots in \((-2,2)\).
Choosing disjoint real intervals with opposite endpoint signs preserves this count for \(H_h\) at large \(h\).
The trace field of any root \(x\) is totally real, and \(x^2-4\) is negative in one of its real embeddings.
Thus \(Z^2-xZ+1\) is irreducible over that field and \(F_h\) is irreducible of degree \(2D\).
Its unique exterior root \(\lambda_h>1\) converges to \(e^u\).
Two distinct conjugates cannot have torsion quotient: an automorphism taking one to \(\lambda_h\) would take the other to a distinct root of the same modulus.
This also proves degree preservation for every positive power.

For completeness the strict capacity inequalities for \(F_t\) survive **simultaneously**.
Take \(h\) large enough that \(\log\lambda_h>u/2\).
Whenever \(k\ge\lceil\log4/(2u)\rceil\), the single interval of length \(\lambda_h^{4k}\) suffices.
Only finitely many \(k\) remain for each fixed \(n\), all less than \(n\) at large \(n\).
For each, the neutral roots arising from the zeros of \(B_n\) remain on the unit circle and converge to their original positions.
Take their \(2k\)-power arms and the positive real \(4k\)-power arm.
The perturbed star contains an image of the original extended star under an angular reparametrization and a uniform radial factor tending to one.
Indeed the piecewise linear increasing circle map between consecutive old and new arm angles, fixing zero, has all slopes tending to one.
The ratio of its new to old circular chord lengths is uniformly bounded below by \(1-o(1)\), by the lower bound for the shorter arc length and concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/2]\).
The formula \(|re^{i\theta}-se^{i\phi}|^2=(r-s)^2+4rs\sin^2((\theta-\phi)/2)\) gives the same lower bound for all planar distance ratios.
Use radial factor \(\min(1,\lambda_h^{4k}/e^{4ku})\) to fit the real endpoint.
By the finite-point definition of transfinite diameter, the image capacity is at least \(1-o(1)\) times the original capacity, which is strictly greater than one.
Taking \(h\) large for these finitely many \(k\) proves the assertion for every \(k\).

Finally all coefficients of \(F_h\) have denominators dividing \(p^{hD+1}\) and its middle coefficient has this exact denominator.
Multiplying by \(p^{hD+1}\) makes a primitive polynomial: the leading coefficient is a power of \(p\) and the middle one is a \(p\)-adic unit after this multiplication.
This gives the claimed total log measure and identifies exactly the contribution discarded in the archimedean test.
For \(h\) sufficiently large the maximum coefficient discrepancy is at most \(2t\), and \(J\asymp\sqrt d\).
