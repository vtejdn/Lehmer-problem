# The row resultant at repeated contacts with two different minimal polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## A global divisor and its full multiplicity at individual primes

Let \(P,Q\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be distinct monic minimal polynomials of algebraic units, of degrees \(e,f\), respectively.
Let \(p,q\) be positive integers, and let \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be nonzero and coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), with ordinary resultant \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\).
Suppose
\[
              P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad
              Q(X)^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q).
\]
For any roots \(a\) of \(P\) and \(b\) of \(Q\), let \(A_p,B_q\) be the monic minimal polynomials of \(a^p,b^q\).
They have degrees \(e,f\) and are distinct, so \(C=\operatorname{Res}(A_p,B_q)\ne0\).

If \(\deg U+\deg V\ge2\), then in the ring of integers of a common splitting field and in \(\mathbb Z\), respectively, one has
\[
          (a^p-b^q)^4\mid R[pq(a-b)]^4,\qquad
          C^4\mid R^{ef}(pq)^{4ef}\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)^4.             \tag{1}
\]
The degree condition holds automatically if at least one of the two units is nontorsion and its exponent is at least two.

Define the two positive power-order indices by
\[
        i_p=[\mathbb Z[a]:\mathbb Z[a^p]],\qquad
        j_q=[\mathbb Z[b]:\mathbb Z[b^q]] .
\]
For every rational prime \(\ell\nmid pq\,\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)i_pj_q\), the full local multiplicity satisfies
\[
                     4v_\ell(C)\le v_\ell(R).                            \tag{2}
\]
This estimate holds for arbitrary actual row degrees, including when \(\ell\) divides a seed discriminant.
The excluded indices concern the two specified powers of the seeds.

<a id="nodes"></a>
## Recovery of the units and the degree condition

At \(z=a^p\) one has \(V(z)\ne0\), since the graph equation would otherwise also give \(U(z)=0\).
Putting \(T=-U/V\) therefore yields \(T(z)=a\), so \(\mathbb Q(a^p)=\mathbb Q(a)\).
The same argument recovers \(b\) from \(w=b^q\).
This proves the two degree assertions and the finiteness of the order indices.
Every pair of conjugate labels \(a,b\) has \(a\ne b\) because the minima are distinct, and \(z=w\) would imply \(a=T(z)=T(w)=b\).
The powered minima thus have disjoint root sets.

Differentiating the graph \(V(X^p)(X-T(X^p))\) at its double zero \(a\) gives \(pzT'(z)=T(z)=a\), as in the [common tangency equations](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies).
If \(a\) is nontorsion and \(p\ge2\), this excludes a constant \(T\).
If the degree sum is at most one, the only remaining possibilities are \(T(Y)=uY+v\) with \(u\ne0\), or \(T(Y)=u/(Y+v)\) with \(u\ne0\), using rational \(u,v\).
The jet equation gives \((p-1)uz=v\) in the first case and \((p+1)z=-v\) in the second.
Thus \(z\) would be a rational algebraic unit and hence \(\pm1\), contradicting the nontorsion of \(a\).
The argument at \(b\) gives the other case.

<a id="labels"></a>
## Each pair of conjugate labels

Fix any such \(a,b\) and use the discrete valuation ring \(O\) at any finite prime of the common splitting field, with integral valuation \(v\).
Write \(d=z-w\) and \(t=Y-z\).
In the [quotient for two double zeros](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#local),
\[
                    D=O[t]/(t^2,(t+d)^2),
\]
the classes of \(1,t\) generate an \(O\)-module of length \(4v(d)\).
Indeed multiplication by \(d^2+2dt\) on \(O[t]/t^2\) has determinant \(d^4\) and cokernel \(D\).
The graph value and derivative for each seed imply in \(D\) that
\[
      pU+\big(pa+(a/z)t\big)V=0,\qquad
      qU+\big(qb+(b/w)(t+d)\big)V=0 .
\]
For example, the first polynomial and its derivative vanish at \(Y=z\) by the graph and \(pzT'(z)=a\), so \((Y-z)^2\) divides it.
The coefficients belong to \(O\) because \(z,w\) are units.
The determinant of the two equations is \(D_0+D_1t\) with \(D_0=pq(b-a)+pb\,d/w\) and \(D_1\in O\).
The adjugate shows that this determinant annihilates \(J=(U,V)D\), and multiplication by \(D_0-D_1t\) then shows \(D_0^2J=0\).

If \(v(d)>v(q)+v(a-b)\), the first summand of \(D_0\) has strictly smaller valuation, hence \(v(D_0)=v(pq(a-b))\).
The elementary divisors of the two-generator torsion module \(D\) show that its submodule killed by \(D_0^2\) has length at most \(4v(D_0)\).
Consequently \(D/J\) has length at least \(4v(d)-4v(pq(a-b))\).
In the complementary case the right side is nonpositive and the same lower bound holds.
The Sylvester map for the actual row degrees has determinant \(\pm R\).
Its target, the polynomials of degree less than \(\deg U+\deg V\), spans \(D/J\) over \(O\), since it contains \(1,Y\) under the degree hypothesis.
The Sylvester image maps to zero, and the length of its cokernel therefore gives \(v(R)\ge4v(d)-4v(pq(a-b))\).
As this holds at every finite prime, it proves the first divisibility in (1).

Take its product over all independent choices of \(a\) and \(b\) in the splitting field.
Powering carries the roots of each seed bijectively to the roots of its powered minimum by the degree equalities.
The products of \(a^p-b^q\) and of \(a-b\) are thus \(C\) and \(\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)\), respectively.
The resulting quotient is both rational and an algebraic integer, giving the integer divisibility in (1).

<a id="power-coordinate"></a>
## Recovering the original variable modulo the square of the powered minimum

Let \(\ell\) satisfy the hypotheses of (2), put \(O=\mathbb Z_\ell\), and consider
\[
               D_P=O[Y]/(A_p^2),\qquad I=(A_p)/(A_p^2)\subset D_P.
\]
The equality of the two power orders after tensoring with \(O\) identifies \(D_P/I=O[Y]/(A_p)\) with \(O[X]/(P)\) by \(Y\mapsto X^p\).
Thus there is an element \(G\in D_P\) whose residue modulo \(I\) corresponds to \(X\).
Its residue is a unit since \(P(0)=\pm1\), so \(G\) is a unit because \(I^2=0\).
We have \(G^p-Y\in I\) and \(pG^{p-1}\) is invertible in \(D_P\).
One correction gives
\[
                        E=G-\frac{G^p-Y}{pG^{p-1}},\qquad E^p=Y ,
\]
by the binomial expansion and \(I^2=0\).
Also \(P(E)\in I\), hence \(P(E)^2=0\).
The resulting map \(O[X]/(P^2)\to D_P\) sending \(X\) to \(E\) is surjective since its image contains \(Y=E^p\).
Both modules are free of rank \(2e\), so this is an isomorphism.
Its inverse sends \(Y\) to \(X^p\).
Transporting the graph equation yields \(U(Y)+EV(Y)=0\) in \(D_P\).
The same construction in \(D_Q=O[Y]/(B_q^2)\) gives \(F\) with \(F^q=Y,\ Q(F)^2=0\) and \(U(Y)+FV(Y)=0\).
The isomorphisms used only the two local index equalities, unit roots and invertibility of the corresponding exponents.

<a id="full-length"></a>
## The full local resultant length

In \(M=O[Y]/(A_p^2,B_q^2)\) take the images of \(E,F\) from the two preceding rings.
The \(O\)-module \(M\) has finite length \(4v_\ell(C)\), because it is the cokernel of multiplication by \(B_q^2\) on \(O[Y]/(A_p^2)\), of determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(A_p^2,B_q^2)=C^4\).
If \(M\ne0\), each residue field of \(M\) has characteristic \(\ell\).
If \(E-F\) belonged to any maximal ideal, its residue field would contain a common zero of \(\overline P,\overline Q\), since \(P(E)^2=Q(F)^2=0\).
The unit resultant \(\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)\in O^\times\) gives a Bézout identity for the two reductions, ruling out such a zero.
Therefore \(E-F\) is a unit, and subtraction of the graph equations gives \(V=0\), then \(U=0\), in \(M\).
The same vanishing is immediate for \(M=0\).

To compare lengths, first divide both rows by their common positive integer content \(c\).
The monic graph divisors persist by integral monic division, and the original resultant is \(c^{\deg U+\deg V}\) times the normalized resultant.
It suffices to prove the estimate for the normalized rows, to which the preceding graph argument applies.
At least one of their reductions modulo \(\ell\) is nonzero.
Put \(g=\gcd(\overline A_p^{\,2},\overline B_q^{\,2})\) in \(\mathbb F_\ell[Y]\).
Then \(M/\ell M=\mathbb F_\ell[Y]/(g)\).
Since the normalized rows vanish there, the degree of the nonzero reduction is at least \(\deg g\), whence \(\deg U+\deg V\ge\deg g\).
The Sylvester target therefore spans \(M/\ell M\), where the powers of \(Y\) of degrees less than \(\deg g\) suffice.
Nakayama's lemma lifts this surjection to \(M\), and the Sylvester image maps to zero there.
Taking lengths proves (2), by the same [ordinary resultant cokernel comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v492.md#resultant) at the actual row degrees.
