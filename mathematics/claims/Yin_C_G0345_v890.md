# Realizing five integral values and a long integral prefix on the same trace grid

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous conditions at prime-power coefficient sums

Let \(q\equiv4\bmod12\) be a positive integer, \(p=q+1,\ M=qp,\ K=(2q+1)^2,\ h=1/M\).
For every \(1\le k\le q/2\) there is an infinite arithmetic progression of even degrees \(D=2n\) with the following property.
For \(N=n-1\ge1000M(k+1)\) in this progression there are monic polynomials \(H\in\mathbb Q[x]\) and \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(n\) such that

* \(H\) has one root in \((0,h)\) and \(N\) distinct roots interlacing the \(N+1\) nodes \(t_i=2\cos(i\pi/N)-2\) in \([-4,0]\).
* Their first \(k\) coefficients below the leading term agree, and \(M^n H(h)=M^n P(h)=K\).
* They agree at all five integers \(-a\), \(0\le a\le4\), where \(0<|H(-a)|\le(1+aM)a!(4-a)!\).

In particular one may take \(q=(9^s-1)/2\) for every positive integer \(s\).
Then \(2q+1=3^{2s}\), and the number of jointly realized leading integral coefficients can grow as \(q/2\).
The rational polynomial has the root signature, while the integral polynomial certifies the joint compatibility of all the arithmetic data.
The remaining coefficients of \(H\) need not be integral.

<a id="progression"></a>
## Selecting the degree and an integral interpolant

Put \(\rho=q/p\), \(A(z)=(1-\rho z)(1-\rho^{-1}z)=1-(2+h)z+z^2\).
Prescribe the reciprocal prefix \(z^nP(z+z^{-1}-2)\equiv (1-z)^2\bmod z^{k+1}\).
For \(n>k\) this fixes the first \(k\) coefficients of the monic \(P\) by integral triangular equations with unit diagonal.
Indeed the term of trace degree \(n-j\) starts at reciprocal index \(j\) with coefficient one.

For each \(b=q,-p\) impose \((1+b)^{D-2}\equiv K\bmod |b|^{k+1}\).
At these \(q\), the even base is divisible by four.
By the [local orders](Yin_C_G0345_v887.md#digits), \(1+b\) generates the whole group of the \(|b|^k\) classes congruent to one modulo \(|b|\).
Here \(K=(1+2b)^2\) belongs to that group.
The two exponent conditions therefore pick a class modulo \(M^k\).
At the first level \(D-2\equiv4\bmod q\) and modulo \(p\), by expanding modulo \(b^2\), hence \(D\equiv6\bmod M\).
Since \(4\mid M\) and \(3\nmid M\), we may also impose \(D\equiv2\bmod12\), getting an infinite arithmetic progression with \(6\mid N\).
The power-of-three choices satisfy \(9^s\equiv9\bmod24\).
For example \(q=40,\ k=4,\ n=3282851794363\) has \(6\mid n-1\) and exceeds the degree threshold.
At \(b=40,-41\), the sum \(\sum_{j=0}^4\binom{2n-2}{j}b^j\) reduces to 6561 modulo \(|b|^5\), verifying the two exponent conditions.
At this same degree the [complete integral polynomial is excluded](Yin_C_G0345_v888.md#statement) on the exact grid.

Start with any monic integral polynomial with the chosen leading prefix.
Its homogeneous pole evaluation is \((1+b)^D S(b/(1+b))\) at both bases, where \(S=z^nP(z+z^{-1}-2)\).
The choice of prefix makes this congruent to \((1+b)^{D-2}\bmod |b|^{k+1}\).
Thus its pole value after multiplication by \(M^n\) differs from \(K\) by a multiple of \(M^{k+1}\).
Adjust the coefficient of \(x^{n-k-1}\) by the corresponding integer to achieve equality.

All five integers \(-a\) are nodes since \(6\mid N\).
Choose the sign of the prescribed integer \(v_a\) at each one to be the sign required by \(H(0)<0\) and alternation.
We may change \(P(-a)\) independently by any multiple of \((1+aM)a!(4-a)!\), by adding integer multiples of
\[
 (Mx-1)\prod_{0\le c\le4,\ c\ne a}(x+c).
\]
Their degree five lies below the fixed prefix, and they vanish at the pole.
Choose the smallest nonzero magnitude in the available class with the required sign, and call the resulting integral polynomial \(P\).
It satisfies the stated bounds and equalities at the five nodes.

<a id="masses"></a>
## Prescribing positive weights at all five integer nodes

Let \(Q(x)=\prod_{i=0}^N(x-t_i)\), \(Q_i=Q/(x-t_i)\), and \(\delta=K/(M^n Q(h))\).
The exact grid has node sum \(-2n\); the [chord bound](Yin_C_G0345_v888.md#moments) gives \(0<\delta\le(2q+1)^{1-N}\le1/N\).
Consider real polynomials of the form
\[
 H(x)=\delta Q(x)+(x-h)\sum_{i=0}^N b_i Q_i(x),\qquad b_i>0,\qquad \sum b_i=1-\delta .
\]
These polynomials are monic, have the exact pole value and the required strict signs.
At each node \(-a=t_i\), \(H(-a)=(-a-h)b_i Q'(t_i)\).
Here \(Q=x(x+4)U_{N-1}((x+2)/2)\), with \(U_{N-1}(\cos t)=\sin(Nt)/\sin t\).
Differentiation at the interior nodes gives \(|Q'|=2N\); at the endpoints it gives \(4N\) by the endpoint values of \(U_{N-1}\).
The prescribed masses \(v_a/[(-a-h)Q'(t_i)]\) are positive and at most \(12M/N\) each.

For the exact grid its reciprocal node polynomial is \((1-z^2)(1-z^{2N})\).
Write \(S=z^nH(z+z^{-1}-2)\).
The [weighted cosine moments](Yin_C_G0345_v888.md#moments) show that \(S\equiv(1-z)^2\bmod z^{k+1}\) is equivalent, besides the total mass, to
\[
 \sum_i 2b_i\cos(ji\pi/N)=\tau_j-\delta(\rho^j+\rho^{-j}),\quad
 \tau_j=h[z^{j-1}]A(z)^{-1},\qquad 1\le j\le k.
\]
Indeed \(S/(A(1-z^{2N}))\) through this order should equal \((1-z)^2/A=1+hz/A\).
The \(j\) positive summands in \([z^{j-1}]A^{-1}\) are at most \((1+1/q)^{j-1}<2\) since \(j\le q/2\).
Thus \(\sum_{j=1}^k|\tau_j|\le k(k+1)/M<1/3\).

<a id="completion"></a>
## Adjusting the other nodes

At every remaining node put \(b_i=N^{-1}(\lambda_0+\sum_{\ell=1}^k\lambda_\ell\cos(\ell i\pi/N))\).
The full grid with weights \(1/N\) and endpoint half weights exactly integrates the constant and cosine moments through frequency \(2k<2N\) against normalized angle measure.
This follows by summing a geometric progression and taking real parts, or pairing the full \(2N\)-th roots of unity.
For the test functions \(1,2\cos(j\theta)\) and the basis \(1,\cos(\ell\theta)\) its moment matrix is the identity.
Omitting the five specified nodes removes total quadrature weight \(4/N\).
The resulting matrix \(T\) has induced column-sum norm \(\|T-I\|_1\le8(k+1)/N\).
Write \(y\) for the right side after subtracting the five prescribed contributions and \(u=(1,\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_k)\).
Each prescribed mass is at most \(12M/N\), the test functions are bounded by two, and \(\rho^j+\rho^{-j}<3\).
It follows that \(\|y-u\|_1\le(k+1)(120M+3)/N\).
Since \(N\ge1000M(k+1)\), \(M\ge20\) and \(\|u\|_1<4/3\), the Neumann series gives
\[
 \|T^{-1}y-u\|_1\le
 \frac{\|y-u\|_1+\|T-I\|_1\|u\|_1}{1-\|T-I\|_1}<1/4.
\]
With \(\lambda=T^{-1}y\), this implies \(\lambda_0-\sum_{\ell\ge1}|\lambda_\ell|>1-1/3-1/4>0\).
All remaining masses are strictly positive.
The polynomial with these weights matches \(P\) on the prefix, the pole and all five integer values, and its strict signs give \(N\) negative roots and one root between zero and \(h\).

Those equalities are rational affine constraints on the ordinary coefficients, and the integral \(P\) is a point in the same affine space.
The homogeneous system has a rational basis by elimination, so rational points are dense in its real solution set.
Approximating the real solution there preserves its strict signs on the finite grid.
This yields the stated rational \(H\).

For these same data the full set of integral candidates is exactly
\[
 P(x)+(Mx-1)\prod_{a=0}^4(x+a)G(x),\qquad G\in\mathbb Z[x],\quad \deg G\le n-k-7,
\]
including \(G=0\).
The difference vanishes at the six distinct specified points and has degree at most \(n-k-1\).
The degree-six divisor is primitive, so Gauss's lemma gives an integral quotient, and the converse follows by substitution.
The strict signs give an open nonempty set of real quotients containing a rational point.
Whether it contains an integral quotient is the remaining condition on this grid.
