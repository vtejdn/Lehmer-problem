# Cyclotomic multiplicities on a primitive diagonal using three Galois conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound in terms of Newton area for the factor containing the input

Let \(B\in\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]\) be irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with Newton polygon of ordinary Euclidean area \(V\).
Let \(p,b\) be relatively prime integers, \(p>0\), and normalize \(B(x^p,x^b)\) by a nonzero scalar and a Laurent monomial to a monic polynomial \(J\) with nonzero constant.
Suppose \(J=P C_0\), where \(C_0\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials and \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) is monic irreducible, has an expanding root and has gcd one among its support differences.
Then
\[
                             \deg C_0\le10V.                            \tag{1}
\]
This includes every multiplicity of a cyclotomic factor.
For fast width \(s\) and diagonal degree \(d=\deg J\), if the specialization preserves the extreme projected exponents, \(V\le ds/p\).
Writing \(D=\deg P\) consequently gives
\[
                             D\ge d(1-10s/p).                          \tag{2}
\]

In particular, take a monic irreducible unit \(P\) as above with \(0<m(P)<\log\theta\), where \(m=\log M\) and \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
If some cyclotomic multiple occupies exactly two or three classes modulo an odd prime \(p\), then
\[
                               p\le D+20 .                             \tag{3}
\]
For exactly two classes the graph of the reciprocal seed gives the stronger \(p\le D+9\).
Both bounds allow arbitrary degree and multiplicity in the original cyclotomic multiplier.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Counting the multiplicities by three intersections

For each of \((\varepsilon,a)=(-1,1),(1,2),(-1,2)\) put
\[
 B_{\varepsilon,a}(X,Y)=B(\varepsilon^p X^a,\varepsilon^b Y^a).
\]
Each pair \(B,B_{\varepsilon,a}\) has no common component on the complex torus.
Indeed a common factor over \(\mathbb C\) for rational Laurent polynomials yields a nontrivial rational gcd, by extension of scalars for the univariate gcd over \(\mathbb Q(X)\) and by checking pure \(X\)-factors separately.
Irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) would then force \(B\mid B_{\varepsilon,a}\).
On the diagonal this gives \(J(x)\mid J(\varepsilon x^a)\) in the Laurent ring.
For \(a=2\), a root of \(J\) of largest modulus \(R>1\) would give a root of modulus \(R^2\), which is impossible.
For \((\varepsilon,a)=(-1,1)\), equal degrees force \(J(-x)\) to be a scalar multiple of \(J(x)\).
Its only noncyclotomic irreducible factor would yield \(P(-x)=\pm P(x)\), since changing the sign of the variable preserves roots of unity.
The exponents of \(P\) would all have the same parity, contrary to the support hypothesis.

For a torsion root \(\xi\) of \(J\), choose a transform according to its order \(n\).
If \(4\mid n\), then \(-\xi=\xi^{1+n/2}\) is conjugate to \(\xi\).
If \(n\) is odd, then \(\xi^2\) is conjugate to \(\xi\).
If \(n=2u\) with \(u\) odd, then \(-\xi^2=\xi^{u+2}\) is conjugate to \(\xi\).
In each case the indicated exponent is a unit modulo \(n\).
In particular, if \(\xi\) has multiplicity \(e\) in \(J\), then \(J(\varepsilon x^a)\) also vanishes to order \(e\) at \(\xi\), since the derivative of the power map is nonzero there.

The torus point \((\xi^p,\xi^b)\) lies on both curves for the chosen transform.
Different \(\xi\) produce different points by coprimality.
The local intersection number there is at least \(e\).
Indeed \(x\mapsto(x^p,x^b)\) identifies the one-dimensional torus with a closed subtorus, as Bézout's identity expresses \(x\) as a Laurent monomial in its two image coordinates.
Restricting the local algebra of the intersection to that subtorus gives a quotient of it whose length is \(e\), because its two equations are units times \(J(x)\) and \(J(\varepsilon x^a)\).
The original local length is therefore at least the multiplicity required here.

By [Beukers and Smyth, Lemma 2 and its proof](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#intersection-multiplicities), the sum of local intersection numbers is bounded by the mixed Newton area.
The second Newton polygon is \(a\) times the first, so the bound is \(2aV\).
Summing over the three transforms gives \((2+4+4)V=10V\), proving (1).
The transforms are selected on the parameter \(x\) of the original diagonal; their sign changes in the two torus coordinates are thus tied to the same sign.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The degree of a carrier before removing the remaining factors

The exponent map \((i,j)\mapsto(pi+bj,j)\) multiplies area by \(p\).
If the extreme projected exponents survive, the image polygon fits inside a rectangle of side lengths \(d,s\), proving (2).
For a prime \(p\) and the [centered lift](Yin_C_G0345_v343.md#lift) of a [reduced cyclotomic multiple](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v146.md#coverage) with fast width \(h<p\), every Laurent factor has fast width at most \(h\).
All its monomials survive on the diagonal since two distinct fast exponents cannot be congruent modulo \(p\).
The reduced lift has primitive rows, so its irreducible factors have positive fast width.
Their nonconstant diagonal images multiply to \(P\) times cyclotomics, up to a Laurent unit, and exactly one of them, \(B\), contains \(P\).
Reciprocity of \(P\) in the indicated measure interval and the [symmetry of the lift](Yin_C_G0345_v345.md#reciprocal) make \(B\) reciprocal up to a Laurent unit by uniqueness.
These arguments precede the classification of the other factors in the [narrow-factor theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v347.md#factor).
In particular (2) already applies to the carrier for \(h<p\), including when \(24h\ge p\).

For \(k=2,3\), the centered fast width is two for every \(p>3\).
If \(d<p\), the reciprocal diagonal of \(B\) would contain at most three terms and would be excluded by the [unit-endpoint trinomial calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v348.md#primes).
Thus \(d\ge p\).
For \(p>20\), (2) gives \(D\ge d(1-10s/p)\ge p-20\).
The smaller primes satisfy (3) directly.

<a id="two"></a>
## A linear graph in the two-class case

After removing the common row gcd, the [two-row normal form](Yin_C_G0345_v341.md#rows) gives
\[
  J(x)= f(x^p)+\sigma x^S f^*(x^p),\qquad S>0,\quad (p,S)=1,\quad \sigma=\pm1,
\]
up to sign, with \(f(0)=\pm1\) and \(\gcd(f,f^*)=1\).
Here \(f^*(X)=X^n f(1/X)\) for \(n=\deg f\).
The case \(n=0\) gives a binomial of measure one, so \(n\ge1\).
The primitive polynomial \(f(X)+\sigma Y f^*(X)\) is irreducible, since it is linear in \(Y\) over \(\mathbb Q(X)\).
Both its extreme rows extend from base exponent zero to \(n\), so its Newton area is exactly \(n\).
The diagonal has degree \(pn+S\) and contains \(P\) exactly once with only cyclotomic cofactors.
Applying (1) yields
\[
                              (p-10)n+S\le D.
\]
For \(p>10\) this implies \(p\le D+9\).
The remaining odd primes satisfy the same bound directly.
