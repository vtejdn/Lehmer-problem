# Binary periods of individual factors of reciprocal five-term polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform gap with one further prime of unbounded exponent

Let \(a,c\) be odd integers, let \(b\ne0\) be even, and put \(w=v_2(b)\).
For \(0<l<n\) consider the polynomial
\[
             P=a(X^{2n}+1)+b(X^{n+l}+X^{n-l})+cX^n.
\]
First assume \(\gcd(n,l)=1\), and write \(n=N3^v h\), where \(N=2^u\) and \(\gcd(h,6)=1\).
Fix a prime \(q\ge5\), put \(e_q=v_q(2^{\operatorname{ord}_q(2)}-1)\), and suppose \(h=Hq^j\) with \(\gcd(H,q)=1\).
Every integral divisor with positive logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
                      m\gg\frac{1}{w2^w H q^{e_q}},                    \tag{1}
\]
with an absolute implied constant, independent of \(u,v,j,l\) and the coefficient magnitudes.
For arbitrary \(n,l\) use the prime factorization of \(n/\gcd(n,l)\) in (1), with the additional denominator \(R_L\) from the [radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) for \(L=2|a|+2|b|+|c|\).
In particular \(a=1,b=-2,c=1\) has a real root greater than one since \(P(1)=-1\).
The gap applies, for example, at \(n=5^{2s}\), \(n-l=5^s+1\), \(s\ge1\), where the part of \(n\) prime to six is unbounded, and both \(n-l\) and \(l/(n-l)\) tend to infinity.

There is also a criterion using the actual reduction of a factor, with no restriction on \(h\).
Let \(S\) be a monic irreducible nontorsion unit factor, \(d=\deg S\), still assuming coprime \(n,l\).
Write \(\bar S\) for its reduction modulo two.
Its roots have orders \(3^{v+1}t\) for a nonempty set \(\mathcal T\) of divisors \(t\mid h\).
Write \(f_t=\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}t}(2)\).
For any nonempty subset \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal T\), put \(T=\operatorname{lcm}_{t\in\mathcal U}t\), and let \(\delta\) be the fraction of the \(d\) residual roots with \(t\in\mathcal U\), counted with multiplicity.
If \(d>4\), then
\[
 m(S)\gg
    \frac{\delta}{2^{\lceil1/\delta\rceil}}\,
    \frac{\max_{t\in\mathcal T} f_t}{w2^w\,3^{v+1}T}.                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(d\le4\) the measure has an absolute positive lower bound.
In particular the least common multiple in (2) can omit any orders that are absent from the factor and may omit further orders at the stated cost in \(\delta\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Degree at a selected residual order

The [binary reduction and orbit calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v235.md#orbits) gives \(\bar P=(X^{2\cdot3^v h}+X^{3^v h}+1)^N\), whose roots have the orders stated above, each with multiplicity \(N\).
An irreducible residual factor of order \(3^{v+1}t\) has degree \(f_t\), so \(d\ge f_t\) for every represented \(t\).
We will show that whenever \(d>4\),
\[
                       d\gg \frac{N}{w2^w}\max_{t\in\mathcal T}f_t.    \tag{3}
\]
If \(N<2^{w+4}\), the bound \(d\ge f_t\) suffices.
For the remaining range use \(J=N/2^{w+1}\ge8\) as in the [local Newton polygon estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v235.md#polygon).
At a Teichmüller lift \(\xi\) of a represented residue root write \(y=\bar\xi^l\).
The valuation of the constant term is that of \(c-a+b(\xi^l+\xi^{-l})\).
If \(v\ge1\), the order of \(y\) is divisible by nine, since \(3\nmid l\).
If \(t>1\), its order is divisible by \(t\), since \(\gcd(t,l)=1\).
In either case \(y+y^{-1}\notin\mathbb F_2\), because the two possible trace values require \(y=1\) or \(y^2+y+1=0\).
If \(v_2(c-a)<w\), the constant term has that valuation.
Otherwise division by \(2^w\) gives a nonzero residue, including when \(c=a\).
Thus every class with \(v\ge1\) or \(t>1\) has constant term of valuation at most \(w\).
The cited polygon estimate with its initial index zero gives ramification index at least \((J-2)/w\) for every root in such a class after unramified extension.
The local orbit argument there gives \(d\ge f_t(J-2)/w\) for a factor meeting that class.

The only remaining order has \(v=0,t=1\), with \(f_1=2\).
If any other order is present, its degree is at least two, so the bound from that order suffices also for \(f_1\).
If only order three occurs, there are two residue classes.
The cited polygon estimate allows at most two exceptional roots per class, counted with multiplicity.
When \(d>4\) some root has ramification index at least \((J-2)/w\), giving \(d\ge2(J-2)/w\) by Frobenius conjugacy.
This proves (3).
For \(d\le4\), the [quadratic radial bound](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(4d\,m(S)^2\ge1/10\), since the sum of the absolute logarithmic moduli of a unit is \(2m(S)\).

<a id="period"></a>
## The period needed by the represented orders

Take \(D=N3^{v+1}T\).
Every residual root selected by \(\mathcal U\) has order dividing \(3^{v+1}T\) and multiplicity in \(\bar S\) at most \(N\).
In characteristic two, \(X^D-1=(X^{3^{v+1}T}-1)^N\) and the polynomial inside the power is separable.
Thus \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,X^D-1)\ge\delta d\).
For \(\mathcal U=\mathcal T\) this gives \(\bar S\mid X^D-1\), even if some of the represented classes have multiplicity less than \(N\).
Since \(S\) is monic, division in \(\mathbb Z[X]\) then gives \(\alpha^D-1\in2\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) for every root \(\alpha\) of \(S\).
For general \(\mathcal U\) apply the [binary period inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v166.md#statement) at \(D\) with \(i=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\).
The numerator is at least \(((i+1)\delta-1)d\log2\ge\delta d\log2\), and the denominator is \(2^iD\).
Combining this with (3) proves (2).

<a id="tower"></a>
## An additional prime power

Now take \(\mathcal U=\mathcal T\) and \(i=\max_{t\in\mathcal T}v_q(t)\).
Then \(T\mid Hq^i\), even if \(i<j\).
Choose a represented \(t\) realizing \(i\).
The order \(f_t\) is divisible by \(3^v\) by the [order computation at three](Yin_C_G0345_v235.md#orbits).
It is also divisible by \(q^{\max(0,i-e_q)}\).
Indeed, with \(k=\operatorname{ord}_q(2)\), binomial expansion of \(2^k=1+q^{e_q}z\), \(q\nmid z\), gives \(v_q(2^{km}-1)=e_q+v_q(m)\).
For \(q\nmid m\) the linear term gives valuation \(e_q\), and each subsequent raising to the power \(q\) increases this valuation by one since \(q\) is odd.
For \(i>0\) apply the formula with \(km=f_t\), which is a multiple of \(k\) and satisfies \(2^{f_t}\equiv1\bmod q^i\).
The asserted divisibility is immediate for \(i=0\).
The powers of three and \(q\) are coprime, so \(f_t\ge3^v q^{\max(0,i-e_q)}\).
Substitution in (2) gives (1) for a nontorsion unit factor of degree greater than four.
The bounded-degree estimate handles the other unit factors of positive measure.
A primitive irreducible integer polynomial whose leading or constant coefficient has absolute value at least two costs at least \(\log2\).
Additivity of the logarithmic measure and the same bound for nonunit content give (1) for all positive-measure integral divisors.
For common dilation, the [individual-factor radical bound](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) compares a nontorsion root with its \(\gcd(n,l)\)-th power at the stated cost.
