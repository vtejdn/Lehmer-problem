# Discriminant repulsion confines the short exponent to a power of the logarithm

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction independent of the primes in the exponents

Consider \(P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v)\), where \(a,b\) are nonzero integers, \(0<u<v\) are coprime integers, \(\epsilon=\pm1\), \(|a|=q\) and \(|b|=q+1\).
There are absolute constants \(q_0,c,C>0\) with the following property.
Suppose \(q\ge q_0\), \(g\) is a positive integer and \(P(z^g)=0\) for some nontorsion algebraic unit \(z\).
Put \(\beta=z^g\), \(k=v/u\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), and let \(J\) count the exterior conjugates of \(\beta\).
Then
\[
 \log k\ge c q^{1/3}\log q,\qquad
 J\le C\frac{\log k}{\log q},\qquad
 u\le C\frac{(\log k)^4}{q\log q\,(\log\log k)^3}.
\]
In particular all directions with \(v\le u^A\) are excluded for sufficiently large \(q\) depending only on the fixed \(A>0\).
For each fixed \(q\ge q_0\), among \(1\le u<v\le X\) at most \(O(X(\log X)^4/(q\log q))\) primitive pairs can admit a nontorsion unit for any choice of signs or dilation, for \(X\) sufficiently large.
Their proportion among the primitive pairs tends to zero.
The estimates allow unrestricted prime factors of all three indices \(v-u,v,v+u\).
They leave open the existence of a unit on exceptional directions of sufficiently large slope, including the case of just one exterior conjugate.

<a id="field"></a>
## Distinct conjugates of the short power

Set \(B_0=b/a\) and \(\gamma=\beta^u\).
The section gives \(\beta^v=-\epsilon(1+B_0\gamma)/(\gamma+B_0)\), whose denominator is nonzero since \(B_0^2\ne1\).
As \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), an integral combination of \(u,v\) equals one and \(\beta\in\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Thus \(\gamma\) is an algebraic unit of degree \(D\), with exactly \(J\) exterior conjugates and no collisions under this powering.
Kronecker's theorem gives \(J\ge1\).
By the [primitive slope estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#statement), \(k\gg q^2\log q\).
The [individual height and radial localization](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement) imply, uniformly for large \(q\),
\[
 \frac DJ\asymp\frac{k}{q\log q},\qquad
 m(\beta)\ll\frac{J}{qu},\qquad
 |\sigma\gamma+B_0|\le C_1q^{-1}\exp(-c_1 k/q)\quad (|\sigma\gamma|>1).
\]
Uniformity follows from the sequential statements by selecting a violating factor at each of an unbounded sequence of \(q\) if any one of the bounds failed.
In particular every exterior conjugate has modulus at most \(R=\exp(2/q)\) after increasing \(q_0\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Divided differences at the clustered conjugates

Label the \(J\) exterior conjugates of \(\gamma\) first in the list \(w_1,\ldots,w_D\).
Their diameter is at most \(E=2C_1 q^{-1}\exp(-c_1 k/q)\).
The determinant of the Vandermonde matrix \((w_i^j)_{1\le i\le D,\,0\le j<D}\) has absolute value at least one, because its square is the nonzero integer discriminant of the monic minimal polynomial.
For \(i=1,\ldots,J\), replace row \(i\) by the divided differences of the original monomials at \(w_1,\ldots,w_i\), leaving the other rows in place.
The change in the leading \(J\) rows is lower triangular with diagonal \(\prod_{h<i}(w_i-w_h)^{-1}\), by the explicit interpolation formula for distinct nodes.
Thus the absolute determinant of the original matrix is at most \(E^{J(J-1)/2}\) times the absolute determinant of the new matrix.

At order \(t=i-1\), the divided difference of \(x^j\) vanishes for \(j<t\).
For \(j\ge t\) it is the complete homogeneous polynomial of degree \(j-t\) in the \(t+1\) nodes.
Indeed the divided difference of the generating function \((1-zx)^{-1}\) is \(z^t/\prod_{h=1}^{t+1}(1-zw_h)\) by its elementary recursion.
Taking the coefficient of \(z^j\) gives a sum of \(\binom jt\) monomials, each bounded in modulus by \(R^{j-t}\).
The Euclidean norm of the replaced row is at most \(\sqrt D\binom{D-1}{t}R^{D-1}\).
Each unchanged row has norm at most \(\sqrt D\), since every exterior node was included in the replacements.
With \(K=J(J-1)/2\), Hadamard's inequality yields
\[
 0\le \tfrac D2\log D+J(D-1)\log R
       +K\log E+\sum_{t=1}^{J-1}\log\binom{D-1}{t}.
\]
When \(J\ge2\) the binomial sum is at most \(K(\log(D/J)+C_2)\) with an absolute constant \(C_2\).
In fact \(\binom{D-1}{t}\le(eD/t)^t\), and \(\sum_{t=1}^{J-1}t\log(J/t)=O(J^2)=O(K)\) by \(s\log(1/s)\le1/e\) for \(0<s\le1\).
Using \(D/J\asymp k/(q\log q)\), the binomial cost per pair is \(O(1+\log(k/q))=o(k/q)\), uniformly since \(k/q\gg q\log q\).
Also \(J(D-1)\log R\ll JD/q\ll (k/q)J^2/(q\log q)\).
For \(J\ge2\) this term can be absorbed in the negative term \(K\log E\) for all sufficiently large \(q\).
It follows that
\[
 (k/q)J(J-1)\ll D\log D,\qquad (J-1)\log q\ll\log D .
\]
The last inequality also holds for \(J=1\).

<a id="slope"></a>
## From the cluster size to the exponent count

We have \(D\ll k(1+\log D/\log q)/(q\log q)\), which implies \(\log D\ll\log k\).
Indeed taking logarithms gives \(\log D\le\log k+O(1)+\log(1+\log D)\), and the last two terms are at most half of \(\log D\) for large \(D\).
The [primitive degree lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree) gives \(\log D\gg q^{1/3}\log q\), and hence the first displayed restriction on \(k\).
The bound on \(J\) follows because \(\log k/\log q\to\infty\).
[Voutier's ordinary height bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m(\beta)\gg(\log\log D/\log D)^3\) at these growing degrees.
Combining with \(m(\beta)\ll J/(qu)\), the estimate for \(J\), and monotonicity of \(x/\log x\) for \(x>e\), gives the stated bound on \(u\).
This excludes \(v\le u^A\) along any sequence with \(q\to\infty\): if \(u\) stays bounded then \(k\) stays bounded, and if \(u\to\infty\) the upper bound for \(u\) would be \(O_A((\log u)^4)=o(u)\).
The same argument after passing to subsequences covers arbitrary \(u\).
For \(v\le X\), drop the denominator \((\log\log k)^3\ge1\) after increasing \(q_0\); the remaining bound allows only \(O((\log X)^4/(q\log q))\) possible short exponents at each \(v\) that admits a unit.
There are \(\gg X^2\) primitive pairs, since among all pairs the union of those divisible in both entries by some prime has density at most \(\sum_{n\ge2}1/n^2<1\) in the square, and symmetry transfers this lower bound to the triangle.
This proves the density assertion.
In all excluded cases the [integral divisor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree) gives logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log2\) for every positive-measure integral divisor at every common dilation.
