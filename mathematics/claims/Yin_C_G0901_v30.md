# Bounded cyclotomic multiplicity at unrestricted degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A coefficient and support bound for arbitrary integer multiples

Let \(P\) be the monic minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(d=\deg P\), \(h=m(P)>0\), and \(b\ge1\).
Set \(A_b=\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,P)|/\varphi(b)\).
Let \(F_a\ne0\) be integer polynomials divisible by \(P^a\), with \(a\to\infty\), and let \(H_a=\|F_a\|_1\), \(s_a=|\operatorname{supp}F_a|\).
If \(\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_b} F_a\le c\) for a fixed nonnegative integer \(c\), then
\[
       \liminf_{a\to\infty}\frac{\log H_a}{a}
                   \ \ge\ \max\{h,A_b/(c+1)\}.                            \tag{1}
\]
More precisely, on every subsequence with \(\log H_a=O(a)\),
\[
           a A_b\le \log H_a+c\log s_a+O_{P,c}(\log(a+2)),                \tag{2}
\]
where the constant may also depend on the upper bound for \((\log H_a)/a\).
The degree of the original multiple is unrestricted and its cofactor may be any integer polynomial.
In particular for the degree-ten Lehmer polynomial and \(b=4\), every bound \(c\le5\) gives a rate strictly above \(m(P)\), even with unrestricted degree.

<a id="gaps"></a>
## Splitting an integer multiple across a gap

Fix \(a\), write \(H=H_a\) and \(s=s_a\), and divide out an initial power of \(X\).
This preserves the multiplicities at \(P,\Phi_b\).
For an integer polynomial \(G\) with \(P^a\mid G\), \(\|G\|_1\le H\) and constant term nonzero, put \(D=\deg G\).
Suppose \(u<v\) are consecutive support exponents and write \(G=A+X^v B\) with \(\deg A=u\).
We claim that if
\[
                   (v-u)h>d\,[\log H+a\log(D+2)],                         \tag{3}
\]
then \(P^a\) divides both \(A\) and \(B\).

Otherwise \(t=\operatorname{ord}_P A<a\), since divisibility of \(G\) and \(A\) by \(P^a\) would give the assertion for \(B\).
Put \(T=X\,d/dX\).
At every root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), \((T^t A)(\alpha)\ne0\) by exact multiplicity, separability and \(\alpha\ne0\), and \((T^t G)(\alpha)=0\).
The operator \(T^t\) multiplies the coefficient of \(X^k\) by \(k^t\), using the identity operator for \(t=0\).
When \(|\alpha|\ge1\), use \(A\) to bound \(|(T^t A)(\alpha)|\) by \(H(D+2)^t|\alpha|^u\).
When \(|\alpha|<1\), use \(T^t A=-T^t(X^v B)\) at \(\alpha\) to bound it by \(H(D+2)^t|\alpha|^v\).
The product over all roots is the absolute value of a nonzero integer resultant.
The exterior log moduli sum to \(h\), and the interior log moduli sum to \(-h\) since \(P\) is a monic unit polynomial.
It follows that \(0\le d(\log H+t\log(D+2))-(v-u)h\), contradicting (3).

Split at any gap satisfying (3), removing the shift from the right summand, and repeat on each piece with its own degree.
Each split reduces the number of terms in each descendant, preserves integral coefficients and \(P^a\) divisibility, and never increases the one-norm.
The original polynomial is a sum of monomial shifts of the terminal pieces.
Every terminal piece has degree \(D\) satisfying
\[
             D\le \frac{sd}{h}\bigl(\log H+a\log(D+2)\bigr),               \tag{4}
\]
by summing its at most \(s-1\) consecutive gaps.
For \(K=2+(sd/h)(a+\log H)\) and \(y=D+2\), this gives \(y\le K(1+\log y)\), hence \(y\le4K\log(4K)\).
For the last implication use the monotonicity of \(y/(1+\log y)\) for \(y\ge1\) and evaluate it at \(4K\log(4K)\).
If \(\log H=O(a)\), then \(s\le H\) by integrality, and taking logarithms gives
\[
                         \log(D+2)\le\log s+O_P(\log(a+2)),               \tag{5}
\]
with dependence on the same upper bound as in (2).

<a id="multiplicity"></a>
## A terminal piece with bounded order at the tested roots

Some terminal piece \(G\) satisfies \(t'=\operatorname{ord}_{\Phi_b}G\le c\), since otherwise every shifted summand and their sum would be divisible by \(\Phi_b^{c+1}\).
At a primitive \(b\)-th root, apply the [divided derivative estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#derivative) to \(G\), whose cofactor after division by \(P^a\) is integral.
It gives
\[
               aA_b\le\log\|G\|_1+\log\binom{D}{t'}
                       \le\log H+c\log(D+2).
\]
Together with (5) this proves (2).
Since \(s\le H\), every subsequence of bounded normalized log norms has lower limit at least \(A_b/(c+1)\).
The separate bound \(\log H\ge ah\) follows by Mahler multiplicativity and the nonnegative measure of the integer cofactor.
This proves (1).

For [Lehmer's polynomial](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#statement), \(A_4=\log3\).
Its [Salem root](Yin_C_G0901_v22.md#representatives) is its only root of modulus greater than one, and is real and positive.
Since \(P(1)=-1\) and \(5^{10}P(6/5)=6762031>0\), it is smaller than \(6/5\).
The integer inequality \(6^6<3\cdot5^6\) gives \(h<(\log3)/6\), as asserted.
For general inputs having \(A_b>0\), approach to the unrestricted rate \(h\) with bounded tested multiplicity requires \(c+1\ge A_b/h\).
In particular at \(b=1\), any input with \(|P(1)|>1\) requires \(c+1\ge(\log2)/h\) under these hypotheses.
