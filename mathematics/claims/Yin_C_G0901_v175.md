# An effective finiteness theorem for sparse integral trace-pole realizations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded support over all degrees, heights and separating grids

Let \(q,D\ge2\) be integers and let \(H\) be a [genuine integral realization at the first nonunit pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), so its reciprocal lift \(F(Z)=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\) is monic integral of degree \(2D\).
Write \(s\) for the number of nonzero coefficients of \(F\), counting the end coefficients.
There is an absolute effective \(C\) such that
\[
 \frac{\log(3D)}{\log\log(9D)}\le C s,\qquad
 q\le C s^3,\qquad D\le \exp\big(C s\log(2s)\big).                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, for each fixed \(s_0\) there are only finitely many such pairs \((q,H)\) over all heights, grids and degrees with \(s\le s_0\), and they can in principle be enumerated with an effective cutoff.
There is no input coefficient bound or hypothesis of an initial block of zeros.
The result concerns the support of the actual reciprocal lift at its special rational pole.
It does not bound the support of every conceivable small Mahler measure witness.

<a id="gaps"></a>
## The cost of reaching the next nonzero coefficient

Write \(F(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{2D}c_i Z^{2D-i}\), with \(c_0=c_{2D}=1\) and \(c_i=c_{2D-i}\).
Put \(p=q+1\), \(K=(2q+1)^2\), and let \(u,t\) be the even and odd members of \(\{p,q\}\), respectively.
Exact reciprocal evaluation gives
\[
                 \sum_{i=0}^{2D}c_i t^{2D-i}u^i=K .
\]
Indeed the displayed value with \(t=p\) is \(q^{2D}F(p/q)=K\), and swapping \(p,q\) gives the same sum by reciprocity.
Let \(w<j\) be any two consecutive indices with nonzero coefficient, including \(w=0\) or \(j=2D\).
Set \(\Gamma_w=\sum_{i=0}^w c_i t^{w-i}u^i\), an odd integer since \(t\) is odd.
Then
\[
 v_2\!\left(t^{2D-w}\Gamma_w/K-1\right)\ge j v_2(u).
\]
All omitted terms carry at least \(u^j\) and \(K\) is odd.
The rational number inside the valuation is nonzero: equality \(t^{2D-w}\Gamma_w=K\) is impossible since \(t\ge3\), \(2D-w\ge1\) and \(\gcd(t,K)=1\).

Use [Yu's dyadic logarithm bound](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#yu99-u001) in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), unramified of residue degree two at 2.
Keep just two rational local units, \(t\) and \(\Gamma_w/K\), with exponents \(2D-w\) and \(1\).
No multiplicative independence is needed.
The argument includes a negative prefix sum because Yu bounds the nonzero multiplicative difference itself.
The fixed field constant and the two modified heights, followed by \(B=3D\), give
\[
 jv_2(u)\ll \log(2q+1)\log(3D)
                 \max\{1,\log K,\log|\Gamma_w|\}.                      \tag{2}
\]
This extends the [initial zero-prefix estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v174.md#dyadic) to successive occupied positions, keeping the actual size of the preceding evaluated coefficients.

Here \(\log M(F)<\log(1+1/q)\), so every coefficient has
\(\lvert c_i\rvert\le (1+1/q)\binom{2D}{i}\) by the elementary symmetric sums of the roots.
For \(0\le i\le w\) the binomial is at most \((2D)^w\).
Consequently
\[
      \max\{1,\log K,\log|\Gamma_w|\}
               \ll (w+1)\big(\log(2q+1)+\log(3D)\big).
\]
By [Voutier's degree estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) applied to the Salem root of degree \(2D\), \(q<(\log(6D))^3/2\).
It follows from (2), using \(v_2(u)\ge1\), that for some absolute effective \(C_0\ge1\) every next occupied position satisfies
\[
             j+1\le (w+1)C_0[\log(3D)]^2\log\log(9D).
\]
Starting at \(w=0\), exactly \(s-1\) such steps reach \(j=2D\).
Taking logarithms therefore gives \(\log(3D)\ll s\log\log(9D)\).
For example, to invert this estimate write \(X=\log(9D)\): \(X/\log X\ll s\), and this increasing function above \(e\) exceeds the required multiple of \(s\) at \(X=C_1s\log(2s)\) with a sufficiently large effective \(C_1\).
This proves the degree cutoff in (1).
Finally the stronger inequality in Voutier's theorem gives \(q<4(\log(2D)/\log\log(2D))^3\); its ratio is comparable to the ratio in (1) for large \(D\), and bounded small degrees only adjust the constant.
This proves the height cutoff.
The coefficient bound above and the [exact pole and root checks](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#statement) make the resulting finite candidate sets decidable.
