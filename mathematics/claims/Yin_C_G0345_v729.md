# Perfect-power Salem seeds and a positive proportion of prime-square seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coprimality of the radical index and irreducibility at every delay

Let \(n\ge7,\ a\ge3\) be integers and \(\epsilon=\pm1\).
Write \(r>1\) for the exterior Salem root of \(G_a(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), \(P\) for its minimal polynomial, and \(R\) for the largest positive integer such that \(r^{1/R}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
If \(a=b^k\) for integers \(b,k\ge2\), then
\[
                              \gcd(R,k)=1 .                             \tag{1}
\]
At every such seed and both signs, \(P(X^h)\) is irreducible of logarithmic Mahler measure \(\log r\) for every \(h\ge1\) whose prime factors all divide \(k\).

For an odd prime \(p\), call \(p\) admissible here when neither \(p^2-1\) nor \(p^2+1\) is divisible by the square of any odd prime.
Among the primes \(p\le X\), the proportion of admissible primes tends to
\[
        \mathfrak C=\prod_{\ell\ {\rm odd\ prime}}
                \left(1-\frac{2+2\mathbf1_{\ell\equiv1\ (4)}}{\ell(\ell-1)}\right)>0 . \tag{2}
\]
The same limiting proportion \(\mathfrak C\) holds for the admissible primes that also have \(R_{p^2,n,\epsilon}=1\) for every \(n\ge7\) and both signs.
Indeed at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt{\log(X+1)}))\) admissible primes \(p\le X\) can fail this all-delay property.
For every admissible prime having the all-delay property, at seed \(a=p^2\), every \(n\ge7\), either sign and every dilation \(h\ge1\), the unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(G_a(X^h)\) is \(P(X^h)\), with
\[
                            m(P(X^h))=\log r>\log(p^2-1).
\]
Here \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
In contrast the earlier divisor estimate \(\log r/H(p^2)\) tends uniformly to zero, since \(H(p^2)\ge p\).
The denominator in (2) is the number of primes up to \(X\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## An exterior conjugate too close to an integer

The [seed root classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(a-1<r<a+1\), and
\[
                  0<|r-a|=(ar-1)r^{-n}<a r^{1-n}.
\]
The difference is nonzero since \(G_a(a)=\epsilon(a^2-1)\).
For \(a=b^k\ge4\) we have \(a/(a-1)^6\le4/3^6<1/4\); hence \(r>a-1/4\).
Suppose \(\beta=r^{1/k}\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(r)\).
It is integral and generates the same field.
Its \(k\)-th powers of conjugates are all the conjugates of \(r\), without repetitions.
The neutral conjugates give reciprocity and hence the positive interior conjugate \(\beta^{-1}\), as in the [field radical signature argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
In particular \(\beta\) is the only exterior conjugate.
Since \(\beta/b=(r/a)^{1/k}\ge\min(1,r/a)>15/16\), the first two terms of \(b^{k-1}+b^{k-2}\beta+\cdots+\beta^{k-1}\) already exceed \(31b^{k-1}/16\).
Factoring \(r-a\) therefore gives
\[
                         |\beta-b|<\tfrac{16}{31}b r^{1-n}.
\]
Write \(d=[\mathbb Q(r):\mathbb Q]\le n+1\).
At the interior conjugate \(|\beta^{-1}-b|<b\), and at each of the \(d-2\) neutral conjugates the analogous distance is at most \(b+1\).
Thus the nonzero integral norm satisfies
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(r)/\mathbb Q}(\beta-b)\right|
       <\frac{16}{31}b^2
                    \left(\frac{b+1}{b^2-1/4}\right)^{n-1}<1.
\]
For the last inequality, \(b(b+1)/(b^2-1/4)\le8/5\) for \(b\ge2\), equivalently \((3b+1)(b-2)\ge0\).
The norm bound is at most \((16/31)b^2(8/(5b))^6\le(8/5)^6/31<1\).
This rules out the \(k\)-th field root itself.
For each prime \(\ell\mid k\), repeat the same argument with \(a=(b^{k/\ell})^\ell\) to rule out the \(\ell\)-th root, proving (1) by the [positive field exponent theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).
The same theorem gives the asserted substitution degrees and measures.

<a id="sieve"></a>
## Square factors of the two neighbors of a prime square

For an odd prime \(\ell\), the congruences \(z^2=1\bmod\ell^2\) and \(z^2=-1\bmod\ell^2\) occupy disjoint sets of reduced residue classes.
The first has two roots.
The second has two for \(\ell\equiv1\bmod4\) and none otherwise, since the roots modulo \(\ell\) are simple and lift uniquely.
Write \(\nu_\ell=2+2\mathbf1_{\ell\equiv1\ (4)}\).
For any fixed \(Y\), the Chinese remainder theorem and the prime number theorem on the reduced residue classes of \(\prod_{3\le\ell\le Y}\ell^2\), over primes, give surviving proportion
\(\prod_{3\le\ell\le Y}(1-\nu_\ell/\varphi(\ell^2))\) among the primes up to \(X\) as \(X\to\infty\).
The finitely many primes dividing this modulus have no effect.
We use the [inspected Siegel–Walfisz estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz), also for the following short range.

Put \(Z=(\log X)^2\).
For \(Y<\ell\le Z\), there are at most four forbidden reduced classes modulo \(\ell^2\le(\log X)^4\).
The error form in the cited theorem, with \(A=6\), bounds the total number lost there by
\[
       \operatorname{li}(X)\sum_{\ell>Y}\frac{4}{\ell(\ell-1)}
                              +O\big(X/(\log X)^4\big).
\]
For \(Z<\ell\le X^{2/3}\) it suffices to count all integers in the forbidden classes.
They contribute \(O(X/Z+X^{2/3})=o(X/\log X)\), by summing \(4(X/\ell^2+1)\).
If \(\ell>X^{2/3}\) and \(\ell^2\mid p^2-1\), oddness forces \(\ell^2\) to divide \(p-1\) or \(p+1\), which is impossible for large \(X\).

In the remaining tail \(\ell^2\mid p^2+1\), put \(t=(p^2+1)/\ell^2\le2X^{2/3}\).
Counting all positive solutions \(x^2-ty^2=-1\) with \(x\le X\) suffices for each such \(t\).
A square \(t\) gives no positive solution, by factoring \(ty^2-x^2=1\).
For nonsquare \(t\), a solution determines the unit \(x+y\sqrt t\) of norm \(-1\) in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt t]\), strictly between one and \(2X+1\).
The ratio \(w>1\) of two such units in increasing order is again in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt t]\), of norm one.
Then \(w+w^{-1}\) is an even integer strictly greater than two, so \(w\ge2+\sqrt3\).
It follows that there are \(O(1+\log X)\) such solutions for each \(t\), with an absolute constant and no requirement that \(t\) be squarefree.
The whole tail is \(O(X^{2/3}\log X)=o(X/\log X)\).

After division by \(\pi(X)\sim\operatorname{li}(X)\) the limiting loss from \(\ell>Y\) is \(O(\sum_{\ell>Y}1/(\ell(\ell-1)))\), which tends to zero.
Each local factor in (2) is positive and the deficits are summable.
This proves the asserted positive relative natural density.
Siegel–Walfisz is used only in its stated fixed and polylogarithmic modulus ranges; the density argument requires no effective numerical threshold.

<a id="all"></a>
## Removing the exceptional seeds simultaneously over all delays

For admissible \(p\ge5\) set \(a=p^2\), and put \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{i=-1,0,1}((a+i)/\operatorname{rad}(a+i))\).
The central entry equals \(p\), and neither neighbor has a repeated odd prime factor.
So the entire prime support of \(H(a)\) is contained in \(\{2,p\}\).
By (1) a square seed excludes \(2\mid R\) for both signs at every delay.
The [matching-prime seed obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v716.md#statement) at \(a=p^2,\ p\ge5\) excludes \(p\mid R\) at every delay and sign as well.

By the [uniform exceptional-seed count](Yin_C_G0345_v703.md#seeds) at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt{\log(X+1)}))\) distinct seeds up to \(X^2\) have \(R\nmid H(a)\) at any delay \(n\ge7\) or either sign.
The map \(p\mapsto p^2\) is injective, so the same bound applies to the number of admissible prime parameters discarded; the finite prime \(p=3\) can also be discarded.
All remaining admissible primes satisfy \(R=1\) uniformly over delays and signs.
Since the discarded count is \(o(\pi(X))\), (2) is unchanged.
The [exact factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives irreducibility and preserves \(\log r\) at every dilation.
The other factors of \(G_a\) before substitution are linear with roots \(1,-1\), so they contribute only roots of unity afterwards.
Finally \(\log r/H(a)\le\log(p^2+1)/p\), whereas the actual factor has logarithmic Mahler measure exceeding \(\log(p^2-1)\).
