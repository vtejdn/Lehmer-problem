# Sharp measures and all least admissible exponents in the lower Littlewood Salem family

<a id="statement"></a>
## Minima and limits of the complete measures

For \(m\ge3,\ n\ge1\), put \(N=2mn+1\), \(f_m(X)=X^{m-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}X^j\), and \(f_m^*(X)=X^{m-1}f_m(1/X)\).
The [lower Littlewood polynomial of Borwein, Dobrowolski and Mossinghoff](../audits/literature/BDM07/Yin_U001.md#lower-littlewood-sections) is
\[
 A_{m,n}=(X^N f_m-f_m^*)/(X^m-1).
\]
Let \(\sigma_{m,n}\) be its unique exterior root and \(\beta_m\) the exterior root of \(f_m\).
Write \(e_{m,n}\) for the least positive exponent whose power of \(\sigma_{m,n}\) admits the [Pisot return with limiting branch mass \(1/2\)](Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#statement).
The multiplicative Mahler measures of the minimal polynomials of \(\sigma_{m,n}\) and \(\sigma_{m,n}^{e_{m,n}}\) are respectively these numbers themselves, by the [Salem factor calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v478.md#roots) and the [conjugates of powers](Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#local).

The least input measure is \(\sigma_{3,1}\), attained only at \((m,n)=(3,1)\).
The least measure among **all admissible powers** of all inputs is \(\sigma_{3,4}^{\,5}\), attained only at \((m,n,k)=(3,4,5)\).
As the minimal polynomial degrees tend to infinity, the finite limits on the input and least-power sides are, respectively,
\[
 \{\beta_m:m\ge3\}\cup\{2\},
 \qquad
 \{\beta_m^{\,e}:m\ge3,\ e\in E_m\}
 \ \cup\ \{2^{\,e}:e\in E\}.
\]
Here \(E_m\) is the explicit set below and \(E\) consists of the odd prime powers at least five and the products of two distinct odd primes.
The smallest of the respective limits are the golden ratio \(\varphi\) and \(\varphi^5\).

<a id="exponents"></a>
## Possible least exponents at fixed seed degree

Set \(a=m-1\) and let \(b=(m-2)/2\) for even \(m\), \(b=1\) for odd \(m\).
Then \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
For each odd prime \(p\nmid m\) put
\[
 t_p=\max\{v_p(a),v_p(b),v_p(3)\}.
\]
The set \(E_m\) consists of the following prime powers and products.

- \(p^{\,t_p+1}\) for every odd prime \(p\nmid m\).
- \(pq\) for distinct odd primes \(p,q\nmid m\) such that \(t_p,t_q\ge1\), \(pq\nmid a\), and \(pq\nmid b\).

For a given \(N=2mn+1\), the admissible exponents are precisely the multiples of the members of \(E_m\) dividing \(N\).
In particular \(e_{m,n}=\min\{e\in E_m:e\mid N\}\).
Each member is attained as that minimum for infinitely many \(n\).

Indeed the [full cofactor and residue classification](Yin_C_G0345_v478.md#factors) tests the divisors of \(N\).
Within the odd positive integers coprime to \(m\), the excluded individual orders are exactly the divisors of \(a,b,\) or \(3\), including \(1\).
Order three alone has insufficient residue weight even when it survives cofactor removal.
The permitted orders are closed under taking multiples within this domain.
For a prime power the least permitted one on its prime is \(p^{t_p+1}\).
For an order containing several primes, any pair \(p,q\) with \(pq\nmid a\) and \(pq\nmid b\) gives a permitted divisor \(pq\).
If one of the individual primes is already permitted, the order is not minimal.
Otherwise \(pq\) is itself minimal, and the original order can only be minimal when it equals this pair.
If every pair divides \(a\) or \(b\), coprimality forces the entire prime support into the same one of these two integers.
A permitted order on that support must exceed an exponent of that integer.
The first offending prime power is itself permitted, because the exponent then exceeds one and the other integer is coprime.
This exhausts the minimal elements under divisibility and proves the test for \(N\).

Given \(e\in E_m\), choose \(u\equiv e^{-1}\pmod{2m}\) and \(u\equiv1\pmod p\) for every prime \(p\le e\) not dividing \(2m\).
The Chinese remainder theorem supplies an unbounded progression of positive \(u\).
For \(N=eu>2m\), every divisor of \(N\) at most \(e\) divides \(e\), since \(u\) has no prime factor at most \(e\).
Thus \(N=2mn+1\) and its least permitted divisor is \(e\).

Every \(e\in E\) occurs in \(E_m\) for infinitely many \(m\).
For a prime \(p\ge5\) take \(m\equiv-1\pmod p\).
For \(p^j\) with odd \(p\) and \(j\ge2\), take \(m\equiv1+p^{j-1}\pmod{p^j}\).
For \(pq\) take even \(m\equiv1\pmod p\), \(m\equiv2\pmod q\).
In the last case \(p\mid a,\ q\mid b\), and their product divides neither.
Together with the list for fixed \(m\), this gives \(\bigcup_m E_m=E\) and realizes every member even when \(m\to\infty\).

<a id="limits"></a>
## Letting both parameters vary

For \(x>1\) the polynomial \((x-1)(x^Nf_m(x)-f_m^*(x))\) is
\[
 G_{m,N}(x)=(x-2)x^{N+m-1}+x^N+x^m-2x+1 .
\]
It has just the simple zero \(\sigma_{m,n}\) on this interval and is positive above it.
For fixed \(1<x<2\), division by \(x^{N+m-1}\) gives a limit of \(x-2<0\) as \(m\to\infty\), uniformly in \(n\) since \(N\ge2m+1\).
Hence \(\sigma_{m,n}\to2\) uniformly in \(n\), using \(\sigma_{m,n}<\beta_m<2\).
At fixed \(m\) the [strict increase in \(n\)](Yin_C_G0345_v478.md#roots) gives the other limit \(\beta_m\).

For \(m\ge4\) we have \(G_{m,N}(\varphi)<0\).
Indeed \(\varphi-2=-\varphi^{-2}\) gives
\[
 G_{m,N}(\varphi)/\varphi^N
 =1-\varphi^{m-3}+\varphi^{m-N}-(2\varphi-1)\varphi^{-N}<0,
\]
since \(\varphi^{m-3}-1\ge\varphi-1=\varphi^{-1}>\varphi^{m-N}\).
Thus \(\sigma_{m,n}>\varphi\) for \(m\ge4\), while \(\sigma_{3,n}\) increases strictly from \(\sigma_{3,1}\) to \(\beta_3=\varphi\).
This proves the asserted unique minimum on the input side.
Also \(A_{3,1}(3/2)=-41/64\), so \(\sigma_{3,1}>3/2\).

With \(m=3\) the least admissible exponent is the smallest prime factor of \(6n+1\), and the first such number divisible by \(5\) is \(25\), at \(n=4\).
The least output from this seed with exponent five is therefore \(\sigma_{3,4}^5<\varphi^5\).
Every \(m\ge4\) gives an admissible power greater than \(\varphi^5\).
At \(m=3\) any admissible exponent other than five is at least seven, since it is a multiple of a prime factor of \(6n+1\).
Its output exceeds \((3/2)^7>(5/3)^5>\varphi^5\), proving the second unique minimum.

Finally, any parameter sequence escaping every finite set has a subsequence with \(m\to\infty\) or with fixed \(m\) and \(n\to\infty\).
In both regimes the minimal degrees tend to infinity.
Along any bounded-degree subsequence the complete measure bound of two bounds all integral coefficients by the elementary symmetric functions.
Only finitely many possible roots would remain, whereas the first regime approaches two strictly from below and the second approaches its fixed limit strictly from below.
Conversely degrees tending to infinity force the parameters to escape every finite set.
Powers preserve the Salem degree, and a finite limit of \(\sigma_{m,n}^{e_{m,n}}\) forces bounded exponents by the lower bound \(3/2\).
Passing to a constant exponent and applying the two regimes gives exactly the indicated candidates.
The fixed-\(m\) progressions and the unbounded-\(m\) realizations above attain all of them.
The least limits follow from \(\beta_3=\varphi\), \(\beta_m>\varphi\) for \(m\ge4\), and \(5\in E_3\).
