# Excluding the linear limiting prefix at positive Chebyshev separation two

<a id="statement"></a>
## The remaining constant prefix

Write \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\) and \(C_k=2D_k+D_{k-2}\), \(k\ge2\).
Suppose that Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\) have trace minimal polynomials \(S_i\) of degree \(m_i=k_i+1\) strictly interlaced by \(C_{k_i}\).
Then \(P_i=z^{m_i}S_i(z+z^{-1})\) converges coefficientwise to \(1\).
The symmetric circle lift of the weights \(C_{k_i}(t)/S_i'(t)\) at the interior roots \(t\in(-2,2)\) converges weakly to the measure with density \((4-x^2)(x^2+1)/2\) against normalized Haar measure, where \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
The [separation-two classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v143.md#families) leaves the two possible subsequential limits \(P_0=1-z,1\) with their respective densities.
We exclude the former under the actual interlacing hypothesis.

<a id="row"></a>
## The integer polynomial at the first varying coefficient

Assume a subsequence has \(P_i\to P_0=1-z\).
Suppress the subscript on \(k,m,\sigma\) when working with one member.
The [finite phase table](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v143.md#table) supplies the limiting moment series \(G=(1+z)(2+z^2)\) and doubled circle density
\[
                  D=(x+2)(x^2-x+1).
\]
In particular the exterior weight \(t_i=C_k(T)/S_i'(T)\), \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), tends to zero, since a positive limiting weight would give an atom at \(x=2\).
Let \(\lambda_i\) be the symmetric interior circle measure in the statement.
It tends to \(D\,dm/2\), with \(dm\) normalized Haar measure.
For \(h\ge1\) the actual moments \(g_{i,h}=[z^h]G_i\) satisfy
\[
 \begin{split}
   G_iP_i&=(1-z^2)\big[2+z^2+z^{2k-2}(1+2z^2)\big],\\
   g_{i,h}&=2\int u^h\,d\lambda_i(u)+t_i(\sigma^h+\sigma^{-h}).
 \end{split}
\]
These are the [positive trace moment identities](Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) for this interlacer.
Let \(n\) be the first index at which \(P_i\) differs from \(1-z\), and set \(p_i=[z^n](P_i-P_0)\).
Then \(n\to\infty\) and \(n\le m\) eventually, since agreement through \(m\) would give by reciprocity \(P_i=(1-z)(1-z^{2m-1})\) with every root on the circle.
As \(m=k+1\to\infty\), the first departure precedes the reflected band \(2k-2\) by a distance tending to infinity.
Consequently \(g_{i,h}=[z^h]G\) for \(h<n\), and \(g_{i,n}-[z^n]G=-2p_i\).
The bounded moment at \(n-1\) and the bounded mass of \(\lambda_i\) give \(t_i\sigma^n=O(1)\).
The formula at \(n\) then bounds the nonzero integer \(p_i\).

Use the [moving moment construction](Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#moving), with its reflected first coordinate.
More explicitly extract \(p_i=p\), \(t_i\sigma^n\to s\) and a weak limit \(\Phi\) of twice \(\lambda_i\) under \(u\mapsto(z,w)=(u^{-1},u^n)\).
The projection of \(w\,d\Phi\) has density \(U\) dominated in modulus by \(D\), and for every fixed \(j\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
                  \lim_i g_{i,n+j}=\widehat U(j)+s,\qquad
                  \widehat U(j)=\int z^{-j}U(z)\,dm(z).
\]
For negative \(j\) the left side vanishes by agreement with \(G\), and \(\widehat U(j)\to0\) as \(j\to-\infty\) by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.
Thus \(s=0\), and all \(\widehat U(j)\) are integer limits since \(G_i\) has integer coefficients.
They vanish for negative \(j\) and for sufficiently large positive \(j\) by decay.
It follows that \(U\) is an integer polynomial, \(U(0)=-2p\), and \(|U|\le D\) on the whole circle.

The double zero of \(D\) at \(-1\) forces \((1+z)^2\mid U\).
Write \(P_i=P_0+z^n F_i\).
Subtract \(G P_0=(1-z^2)(2+z^2)\) from the displayed numerator identity and divide by \(z^n\).
For each fixed degree the reflected band is eventually absent, while \((G_i-G)/z^n\to U\) coefficientwise.
Since \(G_i(0)=2\), recursive comparison yields a coefficientwise limit \(F\in\mathbb Z[[z]]\) satisfying \(GF=-P_0 U\).
At a root \(\alpha\) of \(2+z^2\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_2\) we have \(|\alpha|_2=2^{-1/2}<1\) and \(1-\alpha\ne0\).
The integral series converges there, so \(U(\alpha)=0\).
As \(2+z^2\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), monic and coprime to \(1+z\), we obtain
\[
                       U=(1+z)^2(2+z^2)V,\qquad
                       V\in\mathbb Z[z],\quad V(0)=-p\ne0.             \tag{1}
\]

<a id="norm"></a>
## Three circle tests rule out the remaining integer polynomial

Cancel \(|1+z|^2=x+2\) in the bound \(|U|\le D\) and use continuity at \(-1\).
Because \(|2+z^2|^2=1+2x^2\), equation (1) gives
\[
 |V|^2\le\frac{(x^2-x+1)^2}{1+2x^2}
       =\frac{x^2}{2}-x+\frac54-\frac{x+1/4}{1+2x^2}.
\]
The Haar average of \(x^2\) is two and odd functions of \(x\) integrate to zero.
The geometric series for \(1/(2+z^2)\) gives \(\int |2+z^2|^{-2}dm=1/3\).
Hence Parseval bounds the squared sum of the integer coefficients of \(V\) by \(13/6<3\).
Since its constant is nonzero, \(V\) is a signed constant of modulus one or, up to overall sign, \(1\pm z^d\) for some \(d\ge1\).

At a primitive sixth root \(z=e^{\pi i/3}\) we have \(x=1\), so \(|V(z)|^2\le1/3\).
Every nonzero value among \(1\) and \(1\pm z^d\) has modulus at least one there.
Thus \(V\) is a binomial and vanishes there.
At \(z=i\), \(x=0\) gives \(|V(i)|\le1\), while the nonzero binomial values have modulus \(\sqrt2\) or two.
A plus binomial would require \(d\equiv3\bmod6\) and \(d\equiv2\bmod4\), which is impossible.
A minus binomial must have \(12\mid d\).
Now put \(z=e^{i(\pi/3+\pi/d)}\), for which \(z^d=-1\) and \(0\le x\le1\).
On this interval \(x^2-x+1\le1\) and \(1+2x^2\ge1\), so the circle bound yields \(|V(z)|\le1\), contradicting \(|1-z^d|=2\).
This excludes the linear prefix.
Every subsequence therefore admits a further subsequence with the constant prefix and its stated continuous marginal.
This gives the two convergences in the statement, since the exterior mass must again tend to zero on such a subsequence.
