# The auxiliary trace polynomial for a Salem number has an explicit limiting zero distribution

<a id="statement"></a>
## Real zeros, two nonreal curves and the cost of an integral factor

Let \(\alpha\) range over a sequence of nontorsion reciprocal algebraic units of degree \(2n\to\infty\).
Write \(m=\log M(\alpha)\) for total logarithmic Mahler measure and \(l\) for the number of individual exterior conjugates.
Assume either that \(m\) is bounded and the trace field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) is totally real, or that \(m\to0\) and \(l=o(n)\).
In particular the first case includes any bounded-measure sequence of Salem numbers of growing degrees.
Let \(H_n(X)\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}-2\), and set \(A_n(X)=H_n(X)-X^n\).
Write \(\mu\) for the law of \(2\cos\theta-2\) with \(\theta\) uniform on \([0,\pi]\), and define
\[
            z(s)=\frac{\exp(2\pi i s)}{1-\exp(\pi i s)},\qquad 0<s<1/3 .
\]
Eventually \(A_n\) has degree \(n-1\).
Its roots counted with multiplicity and weight \(1/n\) converge weakly to the probability measure
\[
        \lambda=\mathbf1_{(-1,0)}\mu+
                   \int_0^{1/3}(\delta_{z(s)}+\delta_{\overline{z(s)}})\,ds .
                                                                            \tag{1}
\]
The proportion of actual real roots tends to \(1/3\), and the proportion of actual nonreal roots tends to \(2/3\).
On every compact subinterval of \((0,1/3)\), each grid index \(k/n\) in that subinterval gives a distinct upper half-plane zero at \(z(k/n)+o(1)\) uniformly, and likewise a conjugate lower zero.
Moreover
\[
 \frac{\log M(A_n)}n\longrightarrow
 C_*=-\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\sin(\theta/2))\,d\theta>0 .          \tag{2}
\]
There is an absolute \(c_0>0\) such that every nonconstant integral factor \(B_n\) of \(A_n\) satisfies, uniformly over the factors of the given input,
\[
                \frac{\log M(B_n)}n
                   \ge c_0\left(\frac{\deg B_n}n\right)^2-o(1).            \tag{3}
\]
For every fixed \(D\), the irreducible rational factors of degree at most \(D\) together supply \(o(n)\) roots, counted with multiplicity.
For every fixed \(T\), all primitive irreducible factors of total logarithmic measure at most \(T\) together also supply \(o(n)\) roots, without a degree bound.

In the signed pole-norm-one inverse construction, take \(q\ge2\), \(M_0=q(q+1)\) and a monic integral \(H\) of degree \(n\ge2\) with a single root in \((0,2/q)\), all other roots in \((-4,0)\), and \(M_0^nH(1/M_0)=1\).
These hypotheses already imply irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\).
The [Salem trace construction](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) gives a Salem number \(1<T_0<(q+1)/q\).
As \(n\to\infty\), the same conclusions (1)–(3) and both factor bounds apply to the integer auxiliary polynomial \(G=(H-X^n)/(M_0X-1)\).
They apply for fixed or varying \(q\), and \(q\to\infty\) itself forces \(n\to\infty\).
Thus the search for this branch can use the root signature and the single integral evaluation alone, but any successful sequence must pay (2) in its auxiliary polynomials and place asymptotically two thirds of their roots off the real axis.
The [local nonreal-zero estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v884.md#zeros) controls individual roots near the far ends of these curves, including fixed indices.
The new distribution and factor restrictions here do not construct such inverse inputs at unbounded \(q\).

<a id="trace"></a>
## The shifted trace roots and their logarithmic potential

The inverse pairs give \(n\) distinct traces because \(u+u^{-1}=v+v^{-1}\) implies \(u=v^{\pm1}\).
They form the orbit of the trace, so \(H_n\) has degree \(n\), is separable and has integral coefficients.
Write \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) for its roots.
All lie in a fixed compact set since \(\max(|\sigma\alpha|,|\sigma\alpha|^{-1})\le e^m\).
For every fixed nonzero frequency the normalized phase sum of the \(2n\) roots tends to zero by the [reflected discriminant estimate and the phase correction](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases).
In fact the squared reflected sum at frequency \(h>0\) is \(O_h(n(m+\log(2n)))\), and the unnormalized correction costs at most \(2hm\).
Trigonometric approximation therefore gives uniform angular measure in the limit.
When the trace field is totally real every root off the unit circle is real, since a root with real trace is real or has modulus one.
Such roots give only the phases zero and \(\pi\), so their proportion tends to zero by the atomless angular limit.
In the other case at most \(2l=o(n)\) actual roots are nonneutral, by reciprocity.
Disregarding those exceptional pairs in either case shows that the empirical trace law tends to \(\mu\), and all but \(o(n)\) of the \(w_i\) are real in \([-4,0]\).
In the second case all the imaginary parts tend uniformly to zero, since each is bounded by \(2\sinh m\).
In the first case they vanish identically.
The bounded first moment gives \(-n^{-1}\sum_i w_i\to2\), proving the assertion about the degree of \(A_n\).

For \(\operatorname{Im}z>0\) put \(z=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}-2\), choosing the inverse branch with \(|\zeta|>1\).
The function \(U(z)=\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu(w)\) equals \(\log|\zeta|\).
Indeed \(z+2-2\cos\theta=\zeta(1-e^{i\theta}/\zeta)(1-e^{-i\theta}/\zeta)\), and the two last log terms together have mean zero by the circle mean of \(\log|1-a e^{i\theta}|\) for \(|a|<1\).
Conjugation gives the lower half-plane formula.
On \([-4,0]\) the same factorization has \(|\zeta|=1\) and the circle logarithm still has integral zero, including the endpoints by the integrability of \(\log|2\sin(\theta/2)|\).
In particular \(U=0\) throughout that interval.

<a id="curve"></a>
## One nonreal zero at each interior grid index

On upper half-plane compact sets use principal logarithms to form
\[
       f_n(z)=\frac1n\sum_i\log(z-w_i)-\log z .
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z-w_i)>0\) on each such compact set.
Weak convergence and uniform continuity on compact sets separated from the roots imply locally uniform convergence to \(f(z)=\int\log(z-w)\,d\mu(w)-\log z\).
The preceding potential formula and the normalization \(f(z)\to0\) as \(z\to i\infty\) identify the analytic function
\[
                        f(z)=-2\log(1-1/\zeta),
\]
using the logarithm that vanishes at \(\zeta=\infty\).
At \(z=z(s)\) one has \(\zeta=(1-e^{\pi i s})^{-1}\), with real part \(1/2\), positive imaginary part and modulus greater than one for \(0<s<1/3\).
Thus \(z(s)\) is in the upper half-plane, \(f(z(s))=-2\pi i s\), and \(f'(z(s))\ne0\), since neither differentiation in \(\zeta\) nor the change \(z=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}-2\) has zero derivative there.

For any sequence of grid indices \(k/n\to s\) in this interval, Rouché's theorem on arbitrarily small disks around \(z(s)\) gives a solution \(z_{n,k}\to z(s)\) of \(f_n(z_{n,k})=-2\pi i k/n\). [Rouché's theorem](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk)
The solutions may be chosen at distance \(o(1)\) from \(z(k/n)\) uniformly on each compact parameter interval, since any sequence violating this would have a convergent parameter subsequence and the same disk argument there.
Exponentiating \(nf_n\) shows that these are zeros of \(A_n\).
Distinct indices give distinct solutions because their values under the same logarithm branches differ.
Riemann sums on compact parameter intervals now give the nonreal parts of (1) as lower counts, with complex conjugation supplying the second curve.

<a id="real"></a>
## Actual real zeros near most trace roots in \((-1,0)\)

For \(w_i\in[-4,0]\), the product of distances from \(w_i\) to the other trace roots, with up to one more root omitted, is at most \(\exp(o(n))\) uniformly.
To prove this, replace \(\log|x-w|\) by \(\log\max(\epsilon,|x-w|)\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
The truncated empirical integrals converge uniformly for \(x\in[-4,0]\), by compactness and equicontinuity there, and omitting up to two summands costs \(O_\epsilon(1/n)\) after normalization.
The truncated integrals of \(\mu\) decrease uniformly to the continuous function zero on that compact interval by monotone convergence and Dini's theorem.
This gives the claimed upper bound on taking \(\epsilon\) down to zero.
The \(o(n)\) exceptional roots outside the interval have derivatives of modulus at most \(\exp(O(n))\) because all roots stay bounded.
Consequently \(\sum_i\log^+|H_n'(w_i)|=o(n^2)\).
The absolute product of these derivatives is the nonzero absolute integer discriminant, so \(\sum_i\log^-|H_n'(w_i)|=o(n^2)\) as well, where \(\log^- t=\max(0,-\log t)\).

Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), and choose \(\epsilon>0\) so small that \(4\epsilon<-\log(1-\delta/2)\).
Except for \(o(n)\) roots in \(J=[-1+\delta,-\delta]\) one has \(|H_n'(w_i)|\ge e^{-\epsilon n}\).
For any such root, omission of one more distance in the preceding upper bound gives \(|w_i-w_j|\ge e^{-2\epsilon n}\) for every \(j\ne i\) eventually.
Put \(h=e^{-3\epsilon n}\).
Then
\[
             H_n(w_i\pm h)=(\pm h)H_n'(w_i)
                         \prod_{j\ne i}\left(1\pm\frac{h}{w_i-w_j}\right)
                    =(\pm h)H_n'(w_i)(1+o(1))
\]
uniformly, since the sum of the perturbation magnitudes is at most \(ne^{-\epsilon n}\).
These two real values have opposite signs and magnitudes at least \(e^{-4\epsilon n}/2\).
They exceed \(|w_i\pm h|^n\le(1-\delta/2)^n\) in magnitude, so \(A_n\) has a real zero in each such interval.
The intervals are disjoint.
The real points used in \(J\) represent \(\mu|_J\) asymptotically: all but \(o(n)\) trace roots are real and the endpoints have zero limit mass.
On letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) one obtains the real part of (1) as a lower count.
Its mass is \(1/3\), since \(-1<2\cos\theta-2<0\) is equivalent to \(0<\theta<\pi/3\).
The two curves each supply mass \(1/3\) as their compact parameter intervals increase to \((0,1/3)\).
These disjoint constructions exhaust \(n-o(n)\) roots in the sense that their omitted fraction has arbitrarily small limit superior.
Since \(\deg A_n=n-1\), this proves (1) against every bounded continuous test function and gives the claimed counts of actual real and nonreal roots.

<a id="cost"></a>
## Total Mahler measure and all bounded-measure factors

On the unit circle one has \(\log|A_n(z)|\le\log2+\log^+|H_n(z)|\).
For every nonreal point of the circle the normalized logarithm of \(|H_n|\) tends to \(U(z)\), since the roots are eventually separated from the point and their empirical measures converge.
The boundedness of the roots provides a uniform upper bound on \(n^{-1}\log^+|H_n|\).
Jensen's formula and dominated convergence give
\[
       \limsup\frac{\log M(A_n)}n
       \le\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}U(e^{it})\,dt
       =\int\log^+|w|\,d\mu(w)
       =-\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}\log(2\sin(\theta/2))\,d\theta .
\]
Here \(U(z)=\log|\zeta|\ge0\) off the trace interval.
The middle equality follows by interchanging integrals and applying the circle mean formula to \(z-w\).
The integrands \(\log|e^{it}-w|\) have uniformly bounded positive parts for \(-4\le w\le0\), while their means are nonnegative, giving integrability of the negative parts for this interchange.
The last equality uses \(|w|=4\sin^2(\theta/2)\) and the zero mean of its logarithm.
For the opposite inequality the integral leading coefficient of \(A_n\) has modulus at least one.
Sum just the nonreal curve roots on compact parameter subintervals, where \(\log |z(s)|=-\log(2\sin(\pi s/2))>0\).
Exhausting \((0,1/3)\) gives the same lower bound and proves (2).

The limiting law gives no mass to the origin or to any circle centered there.
On the real component \(|w|=4\sin^2(\theta/2)\) increases from zero to one and reaches one with nonzero derivative at \(\pi/3\).
On each nonreal curve \(|z(s)|=1/(2\sin(\pi s/2))\) decreases from infinity to one, also with nonzero derivative at \(1/3\).
Hence for absolute \(t_0,C>0\) the limit mass in \(e^{-t}\le |z|\le e^t\) is at most \(Ct\) for \(0\le t\le t_0\).
By (1) the empirical annulus masses are at most \(Ct+o(1)\) uniformly over this interval: monotonicity in \(t\) and continuity of the limiting distribution give uniform convergence by a finite mesh.
The absence of a zero at zero follows from \(A_n(0)=H_n(0)\ne0\), since the trace \(0\) here would give \(\alpha=1\).
For each integral factor \(B_n\), its nonzero integral leading and constant terms give \(\sum_{\xi:B_n(\xi)=0}|\log|\xi||\le2\log M(B_n)\), counting multiplicity.
Write \(a_n=\deg B_n/n\).
For every \(0<t\le t_0\) the roots outside the annulus therefore give
\[
                  2\,\frac{\log M(B_n)}n
                       \ge t\bigl(a_n-Ct-o(1)\bigr).
\]
Taking \(t=\kappa a_n\) with \(0<\kappa\le\min(t_0,1/(2C))\) proves (3).

For fixed degree bound \(D\) and measure bound \(T\), only finitely many primitive integer polynomials are possible by the usual bounds on coefficients in terms of degree and Mahler measure.
The multiplicity of each of their individual roots in \(A_n\) is \(o(n)\), by atomlessness of (1).
The degree-\(\le D\) factors of measure greater than \(T\) supply at most \(D\log M(A_n)/T\) roots since Mahler measure is multiplicative and all integral factors have nonnegative logarithmic measure.
Let \(T\to\infty\) after \(n\to\infty\) and use (2) to obtain the bounded-degree assertion.
For a fixed positive ceiling \(T\), take instead the product \(B_n\) of all primitive factors of logarithmic measure at most \(T\), with all their multiplicities.
Those of degree at most \(D\) contribute \(o_D(n)\) to its logarithmic measure by the finite list, and the others contribute at most \(T n/D\).
Increasing \(D\) gives \(\log M(B_n)=o(n)\), so (3) proves the bounded-measure assertion, also allowing an empty product.
The zero ceiling follows by inclusion in any positive one.

## Irreducibility in the signed norm-one inverse problem

For a polynomial \(H\) in the stated signature and evaluation conditions,
\[
                   \prod_{H(w)=0}(1-M_0w)=1 .
\]
Every factor corresponding to a negative \(w\) is greater than one, so the single positive root is strictly below \(1/M_0\).
For every monic irreducible factor \(Q\) of \(H\) the corresponding product \(M_0^{\deg Q}Q(1/M_0)\) is now a positive integer.
Their product, with multiplicities, is one.
A factor using only negative roots would give a value strictly greater than one, which is impossible, and there is just one positive root counted with multiplicity.
This proves irreducibility and allows direct application of the [norm-one Salem criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion).
Conversely, to search over \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree at most \(n-2\), setting \(H=X^n+(M_0X-1)G\) imposes monicity and the evaluation automatically.
The specified root signature for this \(H\) alone suffices.

The primitive linear divisor \(M_0X-1\) of \(H-X^n\) has an integral cofactor by Gauss's lemma.
The positive leading coefficient \(a=-\sum w_i<4(n-1)\) of \(H-X^n\) is a multiple of \(M_0\), as in the [moment bound](Yin_C_G0345_v884.md#moments).
Thus \(M_0<4n\), so \(q\to\infty\) forces unbounded degree and \(\log M_0/n\to0\) along a growing-degree sequence.
Removal of the one root \(1/M_0\) preserves (1) and the limiting proportions of real and nonreal zeros.
Since \(\log M(M_0X-1)=\log M_0\), it preserves (2) as well.
Every integral factor of \(G\) is already a factor of \(A_n\), proving all the asserted factor restrictions for this inverse construction.
