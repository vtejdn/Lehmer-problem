# Cyclotomic polynomials with an initial block of zero coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## The sharp width of the central block

Let \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le s<n/3\) be integers.
The monic products of cyclotomic polynomials of degree \(2n\) whose coefficients of \(z^{2n-j}\) vanish for \(1\le j<n-s\) are precisely
\[
\begin{split}
 &z^{2n}+1+c z^n,\quad c\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\},\qquad z^{2n}-1,\\
 &z^{2n}+\epsilon+\delta(z^{n+j}+\epsilon z^{n-j}),
       \quad 1\le j\le s,\quad \epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{split}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Repeated cyclotomic factors are allowed.
At the boundary \(n=3s\), \(s\ge1\), the product \((z^{2s}+1)^3\) satisfies the same vanishing hypothesis and lies outside (1).

For any monic integral input \(P\) of degree \(2n\) with this initial zero block, the longest prefix it can share with a monic cyclotomic product of the same degree is the maximum of its agreement lengths with the finite list (1).
Indeed \(z^{2n}+1\) already realizes the given zero block.
In particular, if \(s\ge1\) and the next coefficient of \(P\), at \(z^{n+s}\), has absolute value at least two, the longest prefix has exactly \(n-s-1\) coefficients below the leading one.
These assertions concern prescribed zero coefficients.
The [general cyclotomic completion problem](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement) also admits nonzero prefix data.

<a id="quadrature"></a>
## Vanishing moments and root multiplicities

Let \(Q\) be such a product, set \(N=n-s-1\), and put mass \(1/(2n)\) at each root, counted with multiplicity, to obtain a probability measure \(\mu\) on the circle.
Newton's identities and the first \(N\) zero coefficients give \(\int z^h\,d\mu=0\) for \(1\le h\le N\).
Complex conjugation gives the same assertion at the negative frequencies.
Integration against \(\mu\) therefore agrees with normalized circle integration on every trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(N\).
At a node \(\xi\), apply this identity to \(\left|\sum_{k=0}^N(\bar\xi z)^k\right|^2\).
Its circle integral is \(N+1\), and its value at the node is \((N+1)^2\).
The multiplicity at \(\xi\) is consequently at most \(2n/(N+1)=2n/(n-s)<3\).

Every such \(Q\) satisfies \(z^{2n}Q(1/z)=\epsilon Q(z)\), with \(\epsilon=Q(0)=\pm1\), by invariance of its multiset of roots under inversion.
On writing \(z=e^{i\theta}\), divide \(z^{-n}Q(z)\) by \(1\) for sign \(+1\) and by \(i\) for sign \(-1\).
The result is \(H(\theta)+J(\theta)\), where the two possibilities are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
H=2\cos(n\theta), &J=c+2\sum_{j=1}^s a_j\cos(j\theta),\\
H=2\sin(n\theta), &J=2\sum_{j=1}^s a_j\sin(j\theta),
\end{array}                                                        \tag{2}
\]
with integral coefficients.
At all nodes \(|J|=|H|\le2\).
Since \(2s\le N\), the circle mean of \(J^2\) equals its \(\mu\)-mean and is at most four.
It equals the integer \(c^2+2\sum a_j^2\), with \(c=0\) in the second line.

<a id="classification"></a>
## Exclusion of the two largest nonconstant squared norms

If the mean is four, every node has \(|J|=|H|=2\), hence \(H'=0\) there.
At a double root the derivative of \(H+J\) also vanishes, giving \(J'=0\) at that node.
Thus \(J^2-4\) vanishes at every root with at least its multiplicity, since no multiplicity exceeds two.
After multiplication by \(z^{2s}\) this is a polynomial of degree at most \(4s<2n\).
It must vanish identically, so \(J\) is the constant \(2\) or \(-2\).

If the mean is three, (2) forces the cosine case with \(J=\pm(1+2x)\), where \(x=\pm\cos(j\theta)\) for some \(1\le j\le s\).
At every node \(-1\le x\le1/2\).
The first two moments of \(z^j\) vanish since \(2j\le N\), so the \(\mu\)-mean of \((x-1/2)(x+1)\) is \(1/2-1/2=0\).
The integrand is nonpositive at every node, forcing \(x=-1\) or \(x=1/2\).
There are at most three possible phases \(z^j\), hence at most \(3j\) possible nodes.
Their multiplicities total at most \(6j<2n\), which is impossible.

The remaining squared norms zero, one and two give the zero function, a constant \(\pm1\), or one cosine or sine with coefficient \(\pm2\).
Together with the two constants of squared norm four, (2) gives exactly (1).
For the converse, \(w^2+cw+1\) with the five listed values of \(c\) has only roots of unity, as does its substitution \(w=z^n\).
The last line of (1) factors as \((z^{n-j}+\delta)(z^{n+j}+\epsilon\delta)\).
All the displayed products have the required initial coefficients.
