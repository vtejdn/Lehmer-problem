# Localization of a possible nonatomic obstruction in finite conductor avoidance

<a id="statement"></a>
## Only the forbidden orbits can carry a divergent logarithmic integral

Let \(B\) be a finite set of forbidden orders, let \(\mu\) be an even probability on the unit circle satisfying \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for \(n\notin B\), and let \(\nu\) be the measure left after removing all atoms.
For every \(s\) that divides no member of \(B\),
\[
                              \int\log|\Phi_s|\,d\nu=0.                  \tag{1}
\]
No regularity or logarithmic integrability at any forbidden point is assumed here.
If \(\int\log|\Phi_b|\,d\nu>-\infty\) for each \(b\in B\), then \(\nu=\nu(\mathbb T)\eta\), with \(\eta\) normalized arc length.
The removed atoms may have positive mass and are already [uniform on their respective primitive orbits](Yin_C_G0901_v23.md#statement).
Thus if a general real polynomial avoiding the forbidden roots has a restricted coefficient rate strictly above its maximum Haar and forbidden-orbit averages, every maximizing dual measure has a nonatomic residual with infinite negative logarithmic integral at some forbidden orbit.
The condition here concerns the residual, so an atom at the same orbit does not suffice to produce this excess.

<a id="envelope"></a>
## Near returns to primitive fractions

Use coordinates \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), with \(\|x\|\) denoting distance to the nearest integer.
Fix \(s\) as in (1).
Let \(E_s(x)=\log(1/\operatorname{dist}(x,\{j/s:(j,s)=1\}))\) in circle distance, including \(0\) as the primitive fraction when \(s=1\).
For every \(k\ge1\), the polynomial \(\Phi_s(X^k)\) has only allowed cyclotomic factors, since a root of order \(b\) can map to order \(s\) only if \(s\mid b\).
Its log integral against \(\mu\) is nonnegative and its pointwise logarithm is at most \(\varphi(s)\log2\).
Near a nearest primitive fraction the chord factor is at most \(2\pi\) times the distance, and all its other factors have modulus at most two.
Consequently
\[
                     \sup_{k\ge1}\int E_s(kx)\,d\nu(x)<\infty.           \tag{2}
\]
This follows by first bounding the integral of the negative part of \(\log|\Phi_s(z^k)|\) against \(\mu\), then restricting to the submeasure \(\nu\).
It also holds with any fixed power image of \(\nu\) in place of \(\nu\).

We show that (2) gives
\[
                 \int \sup_{q\ge T} E_s(qx)/q\,d\nu(x)\longrightarrow0.  \tag{3}
\]
In the [short-interval least common multiple estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v27.md#lcm) partition the integers in \([N,2N)\) into \(O(1/\delta)\) intervals of length at most \(\delta N\), for fixed \(0<\delta<1/4\) and large \(N\).
Subdivide each interval according to the vector of valuations \((v_p(q))_{p\mid s}\).
There are at most \(O_s((1+\log N)^{\omega(s)})\) classes per interval.
For each nonempty class its least common multiple \(D\) satisfies \(\log D\le C\delta\log(2/\delta)N\) by the containing interval's bound.
For \(q\) in that class the multiplier \(D/q\) is coprime to \(s\) and therefore maps primitive fractions of denominator \(s\) to primitive fractions of that same denominator.
The Lipschitz bound for that power map gives \(E_s(qx)\le\log D+E_s(Dx)\).
The integral of the positive part of \(\sup_{N\le q<2N}E_s(qx)/q-C\delta\log(2/\delta)\) is consequently \(O_{s,\nu}((1+\log N)^{\omega(s)}/(\delta N))\).
The sum on dyadic blocks starting at \(T\) tends to zero for each \(\delta\).
Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives (3), and finitely many initial terms have finite integrals by (2).

<a id="rotation"></a>
## A truncation estimate at the protected roots of unity

For irrational \(x\), choose \(1\le q=q_N(x)\le2N\) minimizing \(\|q s x\|\) through this range, taking the smallest minimizer, and write \(p\) for the nearest integer to \(q s x\).
Then \((p,q)=1\), \(q_N(x)\to\infty\), and \(|q s x-p|\le1/(2N+1)\) by the circle pigeonhole principle.
For \(1\le n\le N\) with \(q\nmid n\), comparison of \(nsx\) with \(np/q\) gives \(\|nsx\|\ge1/(2q)\).
For indices in any one block of length at most \(q\), the points \(nsx\) are mutually separated by \(1/(2q)\), by the [integer determinant spacing argument](Yin_C_G0901_v27.md#rotation) applied to \(sx\).
Among nonmultiples of \(q\) in that block at most \(O(qy)\) points have \(\|nsx\|<y\), for \(y<1/2\): separation gives \(1+4qy\), and the count is zero if \(y\le1/(2q)\).
Distance to a primitive fraction is at least \(\|nsx\|/s\).
Thus integration of this count at \(y=se^{-u}\) for \(u\ge J\ge\log(2s)\), using at most \(1+N/q\) blocks and \(q\le2N\), gives a normalized nonmultiple contribution \(O_s(e^{-J})\) to \(\sum_{n\le N}(E_s(nx)-J)_+/N\).

For \(n=lq\le N\) the nearest integer to \(nsx\) is \(lp\), since \(|l(qsx-p)|<1/2\).
If \((lp,s)>1\), the primitive fractions are at distance at least \(1/(2s)\) from \(nx\).
Otherwise \((p,s)=1\), so the primitive fraction nearest \(qx\) is at distance \(|qsx-p|/s\).
The distance at \(nx\) to its nearest primitive fraction is \(l\) times this, and \(E_s(nx)\le E_s(qx)\).
The total normalized contribution of the multiples to the truncated tail is therefore at most \(E_s(q_N(x)x)/q_N(x)\).
The residual is atomless, so the estimates for irrational \(x\) suffice.
The last expression tends to zero in \(L^1(\nu)\) by (3) and dominated convergence, exactly as for ordinary near returns.

Write \(g_s(x)=\log|\Phi_s(e^{2\pi i x})|\).
Near each of its finitely many simple zeros, \(g_s+E_s\) stays bounded, and it is bounded away from the zeros as well.
The preceding tail bound permits replacement of \(g_s\) by continuous lower truncations in Cesàro mean of the integrated absolute error.
For a continuous test, atomlessness gives Cesàro mean absolute convergence to the Haar integral times the mass, by the [geometric sum and second-moment argument](Yin_C_G0901_v27.md#rotation).
The Haar integral of \(g_s\) is zero.
Applying the same proof to the image under any fixed \(k\)-th power gives
\[
           \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\left|\int g_s(knx)\,d\nu(x)\right|
                            \longrightarrow0\qquad(k\ge1).               \tag{4}
\]

<a id="localization"></a>
## Recovering the residual integrals

For fixed \(s\) dividing no forbidden order, (4) allows the selection of three pairwise coprime integers \(j_i>1\), all congruent to one modulo \(s\) and all forbidden orders, with all seven nonempty subproduct integrals of \(g_s\) arbitrarily small.
Choose them successively on the progression congruent to one modulo the preceding indices and the fixed modulus.
The bad indices for each of finitely many prescribed multiples have density zero by (4), and the progression has positive density.
The [two and three multiplicative difference polynomials](Yin_C_G0901_v21.md#differences) have only allowed factors and logarithm zero at every removed atom.
Their nonnegative integrals bound \(\int g_s\,d\nu\) on both sides by errors tending to zero.
This proves (1).

Assume now the forbidden log integrals against \(\nu\) are finite.
Every allowed logarithm is integrable against \(\mu\), hence against \(\nu\).
Thus all cyclotomic moments against \(\nu\) are finite and only the finite list of divisors of forbidden orders can have nonzero values.
Writing \(I_s=\int g_s\,d\nu\), we have
\[
            \int\log|1-z^k|\,d\nu=\sum_{s\mid k}I_s .
\]
The right side is bounded uniformly in \(k\) and periodic in \(k\).
Since the power logarithms are bounded above, their singular parts give the uniform return bound used in the [ordinary rotation lemma](Yin_C_G0901_v27.md#lcm).
Normalize \(\nu\) when it is nonzero.
That lemma and its rotation argument imply that the Cesàro mean of the absolute values of the displayed integrals tends to zero, so the periodic sequence vanishes identically.
Finite Möbius inversion and the [full cyclotomic moment characterization](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement) give the desired Haar conclusion.
The claim about a strict excess now follows from the [dual formula](Yin_C_G0901_v19.md#dual) and the unconditional uniformity of the atomic masses.
