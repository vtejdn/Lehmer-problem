# Collisions under powering divide the resultant of two polynomial rows

<a id="statement"></a>
## The prime-to-\(p\) part of the power index

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\).
Suppose that \(p\) is a prime and \(\beta=\alpha^p\) also has degree \(d\).
Write \(I=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\), and \(I_{p'}=I/p^{v_p(I)}\).
For \(0\le r<s<p\) suppose that
\[
             P^e\mid F=x^rU(x^p)+x^sV(x^p),\qquad e\ge1,
\]
where the two nonzero integer polynomials \(U,V\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Remove the powers of \(X\) from their respective lowest terms to form \(U_0,V_0\), and put \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|>0\).
Scalar content in either row is allowed.
Then
\[
                    I_{p'}^{\,e^2}\ \bigm|\ H^{\,\binom{\min(p,d)}2}.    \tag{1}
\]
Every prime different from \(p\) is included, even if it divides \(\operatorname{disc}P\).
The exponent \(\binom p2\) is necessary in general when \(d=p,\ e=1\).
The following local form can retain substantially more of the index.

<a id="branches"></a>
## The two inverse branches at a collision

For a prime \(\ell\ne p\), work over the ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) of a finite splitting extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) containing the conjugates \(\alpha_i\) and the \(p\)-th roots of unity.
Use \(v(\ell)=1\) and put \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\).
Partition the \(\beta_i\) by their residue \(b\) in the residue field of \(\mathcal O\).
Within each such class, group the indices \(i\) by the residue of \(\alpha_i\).
For two different groups \(A,B\) above \(b\), put
\[
         w(A,B)=\sum_{i\in A,\,j\in B} v(\beta_i-\beta_j),
           \qquad w_b=\max_{A\ne B} w(A,B),
\]
setting \(w_b=0\) for a class with only one group.
The stronger local estimate is
\[
                         v_\ell(H)\ \ge\ e^2\sum_b w_b.                 \tag{2}
\]

Fix \(b\) and choose one of its \(\beta_i\) as a center \(\gamma\).
Set \(T=X-\gamma\).
For each group \(A\) above \(b\), Hensel's lemma supplies a root \(u_A^p=\gamma\) having the common residue of its \(\alpha_i\), since the derivative \(pu_A^{p-1}\) is a unit there.
There is an inverse branch \(a_A(T)\in\mathcal O[[T]]\) with \(a_A(0)=u_A\) and \(a_A(T)^p=\gamma+T\).
Equating successive coefficients determines each new coefficient by division by the unit \(pu_A^{p-1}\).
For \(i\in A\), the quotient \((a_A^p-\alpha_i^p)/(a_A-\alpha_i)\), written as a geometric sum, has a unit constant term.
For \(i\notin A\), \(a_A-\alpha_i\) itself is a unit in \(\mathcal O[[T]]\).
It follows from \(P^e\mid F\) that
\[
       U(X)+a_A(T)^{s-r} V(X)\ \in\ (g_A),\qquad
        g_A(X)=\prod_{i\in A}(X-\beta_i)^e ,
\]
in this power series ring, after division by the unit \(a_A(T)^r\).
For distinct \(A,B\), the residues of \(u_A,u_B\) have quotient of order \(p\).
Their \((s-r)\)-th powers are distinct since \(0<s-r<p\).
The two linear combinations can thus be inverted over \(\mathcal O[[T]]\), giving \(U,V\in(g_A,g_B)\) there.

<a id="length"></a>
## Adding the local lengths in the Sylvester map

Take a pair \(A,B\) attaining \(w_b>0\).
The polynomial quotient \(C_b=\mathcal O[X]/(g_A,g_B)\) is the cokernel of multiplication by \(g_B\) on the free \(\mathcal O\)-module \(\mathcal O[X]/g_A\).
Its determinant is nonzero and has valuation \(e^2 w_b\) by the monic resultant formula, since all \(\beta_i\) are distinct.
Writing \(\pi\) for a uniformizer and \(q=v_\pi(\ell)\), one obtains \(\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O} C_b=q e^2 w_b\).
The ring \(C_b\) has finite length, and its reduction modulo \(\pi\) is the residue polynomial ring modulo \((X-b)^{m_b}\), where \(m_b=\min(\deg g_A,\deg g_B)\).
Hence \(T=X-\gamma\) is nilpotent in \(C_b\), and the preceding power series identities map into \(C_b\).
Both \(U\) and \(V\) vanish there.
Because \(b\ne0\), \(X\) is a unit in \(C_b\), so \(U_0,V_0\) also vanish there.

First divide the common integer content from \(F\).
Monicity of \(P^e\) preserves its divisibility under this normalization, so the preceding local argument applies and at least one of \(U_0,V_0\) is nonzero modulo \(\pi\).
That reduction must contain \((X-b)^{m_b}\) for every selected \(b\).
Consequently \(\deg U_0+\deg V_0\ge\sum_b m_b\).
For each \(b\), choose the lower-degree one of the two monic polynomials \(g_A,g_B\).
Polynomials chosen for distinct \(b\) have relatively prime reductions, hence unit resultant and comaximal ideals in \(\mathcal O[X]\).
By the Chinese remainder theorem and monic division, polynomials of degree less than \(\sum_b m_b\) already map onto \(\bigoplus_b C_b\).
The target of the Sylvester map of \(U_0,V_0\) contains these representatives, and its image vanishes in every \(C_b\).
Its cokernel therefore surjects onto their direct sum.
Its length is \(qv_\ell(H)\), proving (2) for the primitive normalization.
Restoring a common positive integer content \(c\) multiplies \(H\) by \(c^{\deg U_0+\deg V_0}\), which proves the same bound for the original rows.

<a id="index"></a>
## Counting pairs and the sharp exponent

The [power-index identity](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) gives
\[
       v_\ell(I)=\sum_{i<j}\bigl(v(\beta_i-\beta_j)-v(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\bigr).
\]
Within one original residue group the geometric sum for the \(p\)-th power difference is a unit, so the two valuations cancel.
Different powered residues give zero in both terms.
All other pairs contribute precisely their terms in \(w(A,B)\).
At most \(\min(p,d)\) original residue groups can lie above one powered residue, since their distinct residues solve \(z^p=b\).
Thus \(v_\ell(I)\le\binom{\min(p,d)}2\sum_b w_b\), proving (1) from (2).

Fix primes \(p\ne\ell\) and take \(a=\ell^t\) with \(t>0,\ a\equiv1\bmod p\), for instance \(t=p-1\).
The polynomial \(P(x)=x^p-ax-1\) reduces modulo \(p\) to \(x^p-x-1\).
A root \(z\) of the reduction has Frobenius iterates \(z^{p^j}=z+j\), so its orbit has length \(p\) and \(P\) is irreducible.
Its constant term is a unit and it has a real root greater than one.
For any root \(\alpha\), the equation \(\beta=\alpha^p=1+a\alpha\) gives degree preservation and \(I=a^{\binom p2}\) by the difference-product identity.
Take \(e=1,\ F=P,\ U=X-1,\ V=-a\).
Then \(H=a\), so (1) is exact at \(\ell\) even with a scalar in one row.
This verifies sharpness of the universal exponent in the stated case.

<a id="cost"></a>
## What this divisibility controls about Mahler measure

Normalize \(F\) to be primitive and write \(h=\log M(P)\).
Let \(R\) be the product, with multiplicities, of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P^e\) other than \(x\), so \(m(F)=e h+m(R)\).
Let \(N=\deg U_0+\deg V_0\) and \(k\) be the number of nonzero terms of \(F\).
Here \(N>0\), since a two-term polynomial vanishing at \(\alpha\) would put a positive power of \(\alpha\) in \(\mathbb Q\), where the only algebraic units are \(\pm1\).
The Sylvester determinant bound and the [sparse coefficient estimate](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) give, as in the [row resultant bound at the support prime](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v152.md#cost),
\[
 e h+m(R)\ \ge\
 \frac{e^2}{N}\sum_{\ell\ne p}\log\ell\sum_b w_b
       -(k-2)\log2-\tfrac12\log k .                                    \tag{3}
\]
Indeed each row has Euclidean coefficient norm at most \(\sqrt{k}\,2^{k-2}M(F)\).
The local weights concern distinct reductions of the original roots and can occur already at \(e=1\).
The bound does not supply a lower growth rate for the prime-to-\(p\) part of the index or for its local weights sufficient to force a Lehmer gap.
