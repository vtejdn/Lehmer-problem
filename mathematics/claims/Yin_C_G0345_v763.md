# Conjugates differing by a root of unity on a fixed rational graph

<a id="statement"></a>
## A single power stabilizes all primitive sections at large slope

Fix a nonzero \(R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(Z)\) other than a monomial \(cZ^j\), \(c\ne0,\ j\in\mathbb Z\), and choose a number field of definition.
There exist \(s_R>0\) and an integer \(N_R\ge1\) with the following properties.
Let \(\beta\) be any nonzero algebraic number of infinite multiplicative order satisfying
\[
                  \beta^v=R(\beta^u),\qquad
                  \gcd(u,v)=1,\qquad v/u>s_R
\]
for positive integers \(u,v\).
No integrality or signature condition is required.
If a conjugate \(\sigma\beta=\eta\beta\) differs by a root of unity, then \(\eta^{N_R}=1\), and the conjugate map obeys the identity
\[
                   R^\sigma(\eta^u Z)=\eta^v R(Z).                      \tag{1}
\]
The same conclusion holds for any torsion multiplier \(\eta\) such that \(\eta\beta\) lies on the section with the same exponents of a coefficient conjugate \(R^\tau\), with \(R^\tau\) in (1).

For every \(k\ge1\), putting \(N=N_R\) and \(d=\gcd(k,N)\) gives
\[
    \mathbb Q(\beta^k)=\mathbb Q(\beta^d),\qquad
    [\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\beta^k)]\le d,\qquad
    \mathbb Q(\beta^{Nk})=\mathbb Q(\beta^N).                            \tag{2}
\]
In particular all powers coprime to \(N\) preserve the original field, and all powers of \(\beta^N\) preserve its field.
For every root of unity \(\xi\), even outside \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\),
\[
                  [\mathbb Q(\beta,\xi):\mathbb Q(\xi\beta)]\le N.        \tag{3}
\]
These bounds concern fields obtained by powering or by multiplying by roots of unity.
The [radical comparison for dilations](Yin_C_G0345_v761.md#statement) instead treats the degree and measure of roots obtained by extracting powers.

The [torsion quotient theorem for plane curves](Yin_C_G0345_v251.md#curve) proves the exact multiplier group on a single absolutely irreducible rational curve at all sufficiently large primitive directions.
The [comparison between different absolute components](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#twists) already proves the bound on torsion orders and stabilization after one common power for the whole rationally irreducible curve.
It applies to the rational product of the distinct conjugates of the graph \(B(Z)Y-A(Z)\), which are absolutely irreducible, form a transitive orbit up to scalar and have support differences of rank two for a nonmonomial map.
After a Laurent monomial is removed, rank one would make the graph a translate of a one-dimensional subtorus and force \(R\) to be a monomial, since each nonzero finite coordinate \(Z\) away from poles has just one image \(Y\).
The present graph calculation gives the explicit signed pole restriction and a rational map realizing degree loss, and records the field of every power and of a translate by an arbitrary external root of unity.

<a id="identity"></a>
## A bounded relative degree forces the identity

Put \(D=\deg R\) and take a fixed normal number field \(E\) containing the coefficients of a coprime numerator and denominator \(A,B\).
Thus \(D=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\ge1\).
There is a constant \(C_R\) such that \(h(R(x))\le D h(x)+C_R\) wherever the map is finite, by the triangle inequality at the archimedean places and the coefficient maxima at finite places in homogeneous coordinates.
Here \(h\) denotes the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
For \(X=\beta^u\) this gives
\[
                      (v/u-D)h(X)\le C_R .
\]
Hence \(h(X)\) tends uniformly to zero as \(v/u\) tends to infinity, while \(X\) is nontorsion.

If \((\eta\beta)^v=R^\tau((\eta\beta)^u)\), then \(R^\tau(\eta^u X)=\eta^v R(X)\).
If this equality of values did not come from an identity, \(X\) would be a zero of the nonzero polynomial
\[
              A^\tau(\eta^u Z)B(Z)-\eta^v A(Z)B^\tau(\eta^u Z)
                 \ \in E(\eta)[Z].
\]
It has degree at most \(2D\), so \([\mathbb Q(\eta,X):\mathbb Q(\eta)]\le 2D[E:\mathbb Q]\) by the tower law.
The field \(\mathbb Q(\eta)\) is abelian, and the [rank-one relative height bound](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U002.md#am16-u002) gives a fixed positive lower bound for \(h(X)\) at this bounded relative degree, independently of the order of \(\eta\).
Taking \(s_R>D\) large enough excludes the equality of values without the identity.
Applying a Galois automorphism to the original graph equation gives the case of conjugates, using its restriction to \(E\) for the coefficients.

<a id="multipliers"></a>
## The finite multipliers of the divisor

Since \(R\) is not a monomial, it has a zero or pole \(z_0\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}\).
Indeed removing the power coming from its order at zero would otherwise leave a rational function without any finite zeros or poles and with nonzero value at zero, hence a nonzero constant.
For each of the finitely many coefficient conjugates \(R^\tau\), consider pairs \((a,b)\in\mu_\infty^2\) satisfying \(R^\tau(a Z)=bR(Z)\).
Then \(a z_0\) is a zero or pole of the same kind of \(R^\tau\).
This gives a finite list of possible \(a\), and for each \(a\) the nonzero rational function determines at most one \(b\).
Let \(N\) be the least common multiple of the orders of both coordinates of every such pair over all the conjugates.
The identity conjugate supplies the pair \((1,1)\).
In (1) both \(\eta^{uN}\) and \(\eta^{vN}\) are one, so \(\eta^N=1\) by coprimality.
The integer can be read from the divisors and coefficient identities of the maps independently of any root or specialization.

<a id="fields"></a>
## The field of each power and of an arbitrary torsion translate

An automorphism of a normal closure fixing \(\beta^k\) must send \(\beta\) to \(\eta\beta\) for some \(\eta^k=1\).
By (1) and the multiplier bound it has \(\eta^N=1\) as well.
It therefore fixes \(\beta^d\) for \(d=\gcd(k,N)\), and the converse holds since \(d\mid k\).
Equality of stabilizers proves the field equality, which gives the index bound from the polynomial \(Z^d-\beta^d\).
Replacing \(k\) by \(Nk\) proves stability under every further power.
This uses a common \(N\) across the graph, whereas the [general descent by torsion classes](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) takes a common multiple for one orbit at a time.
For (3), each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta,\xi)\) fixing \(\xi\beta\) sends \(\beta\) to \((\xi/\sigma\xi)\beta\).
There are at most \(N\) possible images of \(\beta\) since the multiplier is an \(N\)-th root of unity.
The fixed product then determines the image of \(\xi\) uniquely, bounding the number of embeddings by \(N\).
In particular the same inequality covers twists whose orders are unbounded.

<a id="poles"></a>
## Rational poles and signed algebraic pole conjugates

For a Möbius map over \(\mathbb Q\) with a pole or zero in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^\times\), any identity \(R(aZ)=bR(Z)\) fixes that unique pole or zero, giving \(a=b=1\).
Thus every positive power of every primitive solution preserves the original field at large slope and \(\mathbb Q(\beta,\xi)=\mathbb Q(\xi\beta)\) for every root of unity.
For the [four-term pole map](Yin_C_G0345_v750.md#winding) \(R_B(Z)=\epsilon(BZ-1)/(B-Z)\) with \(\epsilon=\pm1\) and any algebraic \(B\ne0,\pm1\), the zero and pole are \(1/B,B\).
If \(B'\) is the coefficient conjugate, the identity \(R_{B'}(aZ)=bR_B(Z)\) requires \(a/B=1/B'\) and \(a B=B'\).
It follows that \(a^2=1,\ B'=a B\) and substitution gives \(b=a\).
One can therefore take \(N=2\), and \(N=1\) if \(-B\) is not a conjugate of \(B\).
A nontrivial torsion collision in these primitive sections requires both exponents odd, sends \(\beta\) to \(-\beta\), and exchanges \(B\) with \(-B\).
All odd powers preserve the field in every case, and the only possible proper power field is \(\mathbb Q(\beta^2)\) of relative index two.

<a id="examples"></a>
## Degree loss at a rational map and the monomial boundary

The uniform exponent cannot always be one, even over the rationals.
Take \(R(Z)=2Z+Z^{-1}\), \(u=1,\ v=2^a-1\) for \(a\ge3\).
The section has polynomial \(P(Z)=Z^{2^a}-2Z^2-1\).
Modulo two \(P(Z+1)=Z^{2^a}\), and its constant term over the integers is \(-2\).
It is Eisenstein at two after translation and is therefore irreducible.
It is even and has a positive exterior root by its signs at one and at large positive inputs.
For that root \(-\beta\) is a distinct conjugate and \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\beta^2)]=2\) at arbitrarily large primitive slopes.

Monomials can give unbounded indices at primitive slopes.
Take the constant \(R(Z)=c=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(u=1\), and the positive root \(\beta=c^{1/v}\).
The [positive radical degree formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) gives \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(c)]=v/\gcd(v,r)\), where \(r\) is the fixed largest exponent of a positive root of \(c\) in \(\mathbb Q(c)\).
This index for the field of \(\beta^v\) is unbounded as \(v\to\infty\).
