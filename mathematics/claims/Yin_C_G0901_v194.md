# Large factors of the binary period occur in almost every residual root order

<a id="statement"></a>
## From the global pole to the orders of individual residues

Let \(F\) be a [genuine integral Salem minimum at the first nonunit trace pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), of degree \(d\), with positive exterior root \(T\), integral \(q\ge2,\ p=q+1,\ L=2q+1\).
Write \(T=\beta^u\) and \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)=\beta^v\) with coprime positive integers \(u<v\) and \(\beta>1\).
Let \(c\) be the even member of \(\{q,p\}\), put \(\epsilon=1\) when \(c=q\) and \(\epsilon=-1\) when \(c=p\), and set \(N=v-\epsilon u,\ e=v_2(c)\).
Count conjugate embeddings in a fixed dyadic algebraic closure, with every conjugate counted once.
For every odd divisor \(t\) of \(N\), let \(P_t\) be the fraction of residues of conjugates of \(T\), counted with their embedding multiplicities, whose orders divide \(N/t\).
Set \(h_t=(N/t)\log\beta/d\).
Then
\[
  e(\log 2)P_t\ \le\
  \min\!\left\{\log2+h_t,\
         \inf_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\ k\ge2}}\left(\frac{\log k}{k-1}+\frac{k+1}{3}h_t\right)\right\}.        \tag{1}
\]
Here and below \(\log\) without subscript denotes the natural logarithm.
At growing realized degrees,
\[
 h_t=\frac{\log p+O(\sqrt{\log(2d)\log L/d})}{t}                                       \tag{2}
\]
uniformly in \(t,q\).
In particular, \(P_t=o(1)\) if \(t/\log p\to\infty\), including when \(e=1\).
When \(c=2^e\) and \(q\to\infty\) one also has \(P_t\le t^{-1}+O(1/\log q)\) uniformly in odd \(t\mid N\).

Fix \(\delta>0\).
Let \(N_*\) be the product of the exact prime powers \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\) for primes \(\ell>(\log d)^{2+\delta}\) dividing \(N\).
Apart from \(O_\delta(d/(\log d)^{\delta/2})\) conjugates, each dyadic residue order is a multiple of the entire \(N_*\).
The local degree of each of these conjugates is a multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{N_*}(2)\), taken to be one for \(N_*=1\).
In particular such a conjugate cannot have local degree at most \(\tfrac12\log_2 d\) if \(N_*>\sqrt d\).
The statement controls which large prime powers occur at most embeddings, in addition to the [upper bound on the low-degree population](Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement).

At the other coefficient sum there is a separate exclusion.
If \(v\) is prime and \(d\) is sufficiently large, let \(\Gamma\subseteq(\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z)^\times\) be generated by the residues of **all** primes dividing \(L\).
Then for an effective absolute \(a>0\),
\[
 |\Gamma|\mid\gcd(v-1,d-2),\qquad
 \frac{d-2}{\gcd(v-1,d-2)}>\exp\{a\log d/\log^2 L\}.                                \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(v\mid L^{d-2}-1\), and even the group generated by the coefficient-sum primes has index in \((\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z)^\times\) larger than \((v-1)\exp\{a\log d/\log^2 L\}/(d-2)\).
For every prime \(r\mid L\), precisely \(d-2\) roots have residue order \(v\) and generate unramified local extensions of the common degree \(f=\operatorname{ord}_v(r)\).
The other two have local degree at most two.
In particular for every integral cutoff \(2\le B\le\log_r d\) exactly two roots at this odd prime have local degree at most \(B\).
No primality of \(N\) or squarefreeness of \(F\bmod2\) is required.

<a id="norm"></a>
## A cyclic norm charges every conjugate in a proper residue subgroup

The [common-power criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) and Bézout give \(\mathbb Q(T)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), since \(Y\) is rational in \(T\).
They give equality of the completions generated by corresponding conjugates as well.
The unit \(\beta\) has only one exterior conjugate, since its \(u\)-th powers give the conjugates of \(T\) without repetition.
The [integral coefficient-modulus relation](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#periods) gives \(\beta^N=1\bmod c\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Every dyadic residual order of \(\beta\) divides \(N\) and is unchanged by raising to \(u\), since \(\gcd(u,N)=1\).
The same congruence holds at every embedding.

Put \(n=N/t,\ \gamma=\beta^n\).
At each counted embedding \(\sigma\), \(\sigma\gamma\) is one in the residue field.
When \(t\) is odd, the quotient \(((\sigma\gamma)^t-1)/(\sigma\gamma-1)\) reduces to one, so \(v_2(\sigma\gamma-1)=v_2((\sigma\beta)^N-1)\ge e\).
The denominator is nonzero because \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
At every remaining finite place \(\gamma-1\) is integral, and its absolute norm is therefore at least \(2^{edP_t}\).
At infinity, the product of \(|\sigma\gamma-1|\) is at most \(2^d\beta^n\), yielding the first bound in (1).
For the other bound apply the [unit cyclic norm inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) to \(\gamma\) with absolute height \(h_t\).
It controls the same norm in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), including any powered-field multiplicity, by the displayed infimum.
Its auxiliary is the product of pair differences of \(1,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma^{k-1}\) up to a unit, with Hadamard's bound at the complex places.
In particular the infimum is \(O(\sqrt{h_t\log(1/h_t)})\) as \(h_t\downarrow0\).

The [archimedean pole estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#real) gives \(\log Y=d\log p+O(\sqrt{d\log(2d)\log L})\).
Since \(N\log\beta=\log Y-\epsilon\log T\) and \(0<\log T<1/q\), this proves (2).
If \(c=2^e\), then \(e\log2=\log c=\log p+O(1/q)\), proving the stated power-of-two specialization.
The norm proof also works simultaneously at arbitrary primes \(r\mid c\) with \(r\nmid t\): if \(P_{t,r}\) is the analogous fraction of roots at \(r\), the left side of (1) can be replaced by \(\sum_{r\mid c,\ r\nmid t}v_r(c)(\log r)P_{t,r}\).
Here the residual order of \(\beta\) equals that of \(T\) also at these places, and the geometric sum reduces to the nonzero class of \(t\).
The same argument at the odd member of \(\{p,q\}\) uses its corresponding exponent \(v+u\) for \(p\) or \(v-u\) for \(q\).

<a id="large"></a>
## Almost all residues contain the same part of the period

Write \(X=(\log d)^{2+\delta}\).
The [degree and exponent estimates](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#words) give \(q,u\ll(\log(3d))^3\) and \(N\ll d(\log(3d))^3\log\log(9d)\).
For each prime \(\ell>X\) dividing \(N\), the fraction of embeddings whose residual order misses the full \(\ell\)-part of \(N\) is exactly \(P_\ell\).
By (2), \(h_\ell\ll\log\log(9d)/X\) uniformly.
For large degrees \(h\log(1/h)\) increases on the interval up to this upper bound, so (1) gives
\[
 P_\ell\ll_\delta\frac{\log\log(9d)}{(\log d)^{1+\delta/2}} .
\]
The number of these primes is at most \(\log N/\log X\ll_\delta\log d/\log\log(9d)\).
Summing their exceptional sets proves the claimed count with \(N_*\mid\operatorname{ord}(\overline{\sigma T})\) on its complement.
At any such place, the multiplicative order of a nonzero residue in degree \(f\) divides \(2^f-1\).
It follows that \(\operatorname{ord}_{N_*}(2)\mid f\), hence it divides the full local degree.
When \(N_*>\sqrt d\), \(f\ge\log_2(N_*+1)>\tfrac12\log_2d\).

<a id="sum"></a>
## The degree offset at all primes of the coefficient sum

Suppose \(v\) is prime.
By (2) at \(t=1\), the bounds \(q\ll(\log(3d))^3,\ \log\beta\le\log T<1/q\), and \(v\log\beta=\log Y\), one has \(v>L\) at all sufficiently large degrees.
In particular \(v\) is odd and prime to \(L\).
For each prime \(r\mid L\), the [rank-two splitting at the exact pole](Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#trace) gives \(F(Z)=(Z+1)^2 D_r(Z)\) over \(\mathbb F_r\), where \(D_r\) is monic of degree \(d-2\) and divides \((Z^v-1)/(Z-1)\).
In fact the cited complementary factor comes from \(J\mid\Psi_v\) under the trace substitution, and dividing \(Z^v-1\) by \(Z-1\) gives the reciprocal lift of \(\Psi_v\).
Since \(r\ne v\) and \(v\) is prime, the residual roots of \(D_r\) are simple and all have order \(v\).
Every nontrivial \(v\)-th root has Frobenius orbit length \(\operatorname{ord}_v(r)\), whence that length divides \(d-2\).
The factor \(D_r\) is separable and prime to \(Z+1\), since \(v\) is odd and \(r\ne2\).
Each degree-\(f\) irreducible factor of \(D_r\) lifts by Hensel's lemma to a monic degree-\(f\) factor with irreducible separable reduction, whose roots each generate the unramified extension of degree \(f\).
The complementary quadratic factor has at most quadratic local fields.
As \(r^f\ge v+1\) and \(v>d\) for large degrees by \(v\log\beta/d=\log p+o(1)\) and \(\log\beta\le\log T<1/q\), one has \(f>\log_r d\).
The cyclic group \((\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z)^\times\) now gives the divisibility in (3) simultaneously for all \(r\mid L\).
In particular \(L^{d-2}\equiv1\bmod v\).

Put \(g=\gcd(v-1,d-2),\ b=(d-2)/g,\ j=(v-1)/g\).
Then \(bv-jd=b-2j\).
Use the [binary period identity and repulsion](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) for \(A=\operatorname{ord}_{(2,F)}(Z)\) and \(N=kA=v-\epsilon u\), \(1\le k<2\log_2 L\). [Period identity](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities)
Consequently
\[
                      |kbA-jd|\le b+2j+bu\ll b(\log(3d))^{C}
\]
for an absolute \(C\), using \(j/b=(v-1)/(d-2)\) and the degree and exponent estimates.
Fix the repulsion exponent \(\eta=1/4\), with cutoff \(\exp(c_\eta\log d/\log^2 L)\).
If \(b\le\exp(a\log d/\log^2L)\), choose the absolute \(a>0\) small enough that \(b\le d^{1/8}\) and \(a<c_\eta/2\).
For large degrees \(k\le\exp((c_\eta/2)\log d/\log^2L)\), since \(q\ll(\log(3d))^3\).
Then \(kb\) is within the repulsion range but the last display is \(o(d^{3/4})\), contrary to that bound.
This proves (3).
A root in a proper subgroup of the \(N\)-th roots of unity in the residue field can still have large residue degree.
These estimates leave open the requested lower bound on low-degree mass at two.
