# Radicals of arbitrary algebraic units on a fixed rational graph

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound without a real-embedding restriction

Fix a nonzero rational function \(R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(Z)\).
There exist \(s_R\) and a positive integer \(H_R\) such that whenever a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\beta\) satisfies
\[
              \beta^v=R(\beta^u),\qquad u,v\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad v/u>s_R,
\]
every positive integer \(r\) for which \(\beta\) has an \(r\)-th root in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) divides \(H_R\).
All complex embeddings and all choices of an \(r\)-th root are allowed.
In particular, if a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) satisfies \(\alpha^b=R(\alpha^a)\), \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), and \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^g)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), then \(g r\mid H_R\) for every \(\theta\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) with \(\theta^r=\alpha\), whenever \(b/a>s_R\).
The same common integer works for any prescribed finite list of maps after taking a least common multiple.
This extends the [positive real graph bound](Yin_C_G0345_v749.md#statement) to arbitrary signatures and includes poles or zeros at one.

A more general separation holds.
At sufficiently large slope, no solution \(\beta\) as above and no root \(\gamma^k=\beta\) of any integer order \(k\ge2\), even in the algebraic closure, can satisfy \(\gamma^v=R^\tau(\gamma^u)\) for a coefficient conjugate \(R^\tau\).
The arithmetic bound uses this separation for \(k\) prime.

<a id="height"></a>
## A common limiting height over all coefficient embeddings

Choose a number field \(E\) and coprime \(A,B\in\mathcal O_E[Z]\) with \(R=A/B\), using coprimality over \(E\) and \(B\ne0\).
Set \(D=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\), and for finite \(w\) put \(G_w=\max_i\{|[Z^i]A|_w,|[Z^i]B|_w\}\).
At a complex embedding \(\tau\) put \(I_\tau=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A^\tau(z)|,|B^\tau(z)|)\,d\mu(z)\) with normalized angular measure \(\mu\).
Define
\[
  H(R)= [E:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\sum_\tau I_\tau
            +\sum_{w\nmid\infty}\frac{[E_w:\mathbb Q_p]}{[E:\mathbb Q]}\log G_w .
\]
It is unchanged by rescaling the presentation by a common nonzero element, extension of \(E\), or coefficient conjugation.
For every sequence of nontorsion algebraic units \(X\) with \(h(X)\to0\),
\[
                               h(R(X))\longrightarrow H(R).             \tag{1}
\]
The degrees of the \(X\)'s tend to infinity by Northcott, so they eventually avoid all fixed zeros and poles.
Use the product formula to compute the height from the local maximum of the two evaluations.
Above each archimedean embedding use the continuous projective test
\[
  z\longmapsto\log\max(|A^\tau(z)|,|B^\tau(z)|)-D\log^+|z|.
\]
This is continuous also at infinity, by coprimality and the choice of \(D\).
Adding the subtracted terms back costs at most \(D h(X)\) in the full normalized sum.
The full relative orbit over each fixed coefficient embedding converges to angular measure by [small-point equidistribution](../audits/literature/BR06/Yin_U001.md#br06-galois-small-point-equidistribution), which applies to pairwise distinct points on further subsequences.
The circle integral is exactly \(I_\tau\).

At a finite place with \(G_w=1\) and a unit Bézout identity for \(A,B\) the local maximum is one on units, including at all extensions of that place.
These conditions hold except at finitely many places after fixing the polynomials.
At each exceptional place divide both polynomials by a coefficient of modulus \(G_w\).
Their maximum on units now lies between a fixed positive constant and one, by a local Bézout identity over the field.
One of the two polynomials, say \(P\), has a nonzero reduction.
After removing a possible initial monomial its reduction divides \(Z^N-1\) for some fixed \(N\), using a power of the residue characteristic for multiplicities.
Thus at every extension place, on units, \(|X^N-1|\le\max(|P(X)|,|\pi|)\) with \(\pi\) a uniformizer of the coefficient field.
By the preceding lower bound the negative logarithm of the normalized local maximum is at most a fixed multiple of \(-\log|X^N-1|\).
Its weighted sum tends to zero by the [finite-place cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) at \(h(X^N)\to0\).
Restoring \(\log G_w\) proves (1).
This argument uses the finite-place calculation for [reciprocal quotients](Yin_C_G0345_v748.md#height) for an arbitrary pair of coprime polynomials.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Why a root and its power cannot both satisfy a conjugate graph equation

There is a fixed \(c_R\ge0\) with \(h(R(x))\le D h(x)+c_R\), by bounding the local coefficient lengths at infinity and coefficient maxima at the finite places in the homogeneous height formula.
For solutions along \(v/u\to\infty\) it follows that \(v h(\beta)\) is bounded and \(u h(\beta)\to0\).
Thus (1) gives \(v h(\beta)\to H(R)\).
Whenever such solutions exist at unbounded slopes, \(H(R)>0\).
Indeed the fixed conjugate product
\[
     P_{u,v}(Z)=\prod_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
                    \big(Z^v B^\tau(Z^u)-A^\tau(Z^u)\big)
\]
is integral, is nonzero for \(v>Du\) and has degree at most \(e(v+Du)\), with \(e=[E:\mathbb Q]\).
Its number of terms is bounded independently of the exponents because each factor has uniformly bounded term count.
If \(v h(\beta)\to0\), the true exponent span of this polynomial times \(h(\beta)\) tends to zero.
All original conjugates are exact zeros, contradicting the [subcritical sparse bound](Yin_C_G0345_v737.md#statement) at threshold zero.
This positivity argument concerns maps which actually have the specified solutions and also covers constant or monomial maps whenever they have them.

If the separation failed at arbitrarily large slopes, take a failing sequence and write \(\gamma^k=\beta\), \(k\ge2\).
The units \(\gamma^u\) have height at most \(u h(\beta)/2\to0\).
Apply (1) to \(\gamma^u\) along a subsequence with the same conjugate map.
The assumed equation gives \(v h(\gamma)\to H(R)\) whereas \(v h(\gamma)=v h(\beta)/k\le v h(\beta)/2\).
This contradicts positivity.
It proves the uniform separation even for unbounded \(k\).

<a id="radicals"></a>
## An integer independent of all exponents

At large slopes, the preceding separation makes \(P_{u,v}(\gamma)\ne0\) for every \(\gamma^p=\beta\), \(p\) prime.
Indeed if one of its factors vanished then its denominator could not vanish by coprimality, and it would give exactly the forbidden conjugate graph.
The length \(\sum_i |[Z^i]P_{u,v}|\) is at most the fixed product \(L=\prod_\tau(\|A^\tau\|_1+\|B^\tau\|_1)\).
If \(\beta=\theta^{p^j}\) with \(\theta\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\), take \(\gamma=\theta^{p^{j-1}}\).
The [prime-power norm test](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#norm-test) gives
\[
             p^j\le L\exp\!\big(e(v+Du)h(\beta)/p\big).
\]
For \(v>2Du\), with \(v>0\) also when \(D=0\), the height bound above gives \((v+Du)h(\beta)\le3c_R\).
Thus one may take \(H_R=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\max(1,\lfloor L e^{3e c_R/2}\rfloor))\) after choosing the slope threshold from the separation.
For any admissible \(r\) each full prime power dividing it satisfies this inequality, giving \(r\mid H_R\) without any choice of simultaneous branches.
For the last assertion of the statement use \(\beta=\alpha^g,\ u=a/g,\ v=b/g\); the proposed \(\theta\) is already a \((gr)\)-th root of \(\beta\) in the required field.
