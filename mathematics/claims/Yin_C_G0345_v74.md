# Sharp Mahler bounds for conjugate monomials with two nonreal exterior roots in one block

<a id="statement"></a>
## The least cost beyond powers of individual conjugates

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), \(m\ge8\), whose only exterior roots are a nonreal conjugate pair of common radius \(R\).
Suppose distinct roots never have root-of-unity quotient and the [signed graph of the exterior inverse pairs](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#statement) is connected on all \(m\) labels.
Set \(L=\log M(P)=2\log R\).
Choose one representative \(v_i\) per inverse pair and, for \(b\in\mathbb Z^m\), form the algebraic unit \(\gamma=\prod_i v_i^{b_i}\).
Use the measure of the minimal polynomial of \(\gamma\), counting each distinct conjugate once.

* If the graph is balanced, orient the representatives so that \(\zeta=\prod_i v_i\) is a root of unity.
  Constant exponents give torsion.
  If the exponents are equal to \(h\) with one exception \(h+u\), \(u\ne0\), then \(\gamma=\zeta^h v_i^u\) has degree \(2m\) and \(\log M(\gamma)=|u|L\).
  For every other choice, \(\log M(\gamma)\ge(m-2)L\), with equality attained by \(v_i v_j\) for any \(i\ne j\).
* If the graph is unbalanced, only \(b=0\) gives torsion.
  A single nonzero exponent \(u\) gives degree \(2m\) and \(\log M(\gamma)=|u|L\).
  With at least two nonzero exponents, \(\log M(\gamma)\ge(2m-3)L\), with equality attained by \(v_i^{\pm1}v_j^{\pm1}\) for any \(i\ne j\).

In either case the logarithmic measures in the last class tend to infinity uniformly as \(m\to\infty\), allowing the polynomial and the exponents to vary.

<a id="averages"></a>
## Two formulas at the actual field degree

The coordinating agent proposed these sharp thresholds and the orbit counts for the two signed actions.
Write \(G\) for the splitting-field Galois group and \(D=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
Every conjugate occurs equally often among \(g\gamma\), \(g\in G\).
Since \(\gamma\) is a unit, its conjugate logarithms sum to zero.
It follows that \(\log M(\gamma)=\tfrac12 D\,\mathbb E_{g\in G}|\log|g\gamma||\).
The projected permutation action on the inverse pairs is the [full symmetric group](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action).

In the balanced orientation the two nonzero logarithms \(\log|v_i|\) are \(\log R\) and \(-\log R\), by the torsion product.
The [orientation character](Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#field) gives a simultaneous sign for every permutation.
Taking absolute values removes this sign, and the two exponent positions arriving at the nonneutral labels form a uniform ordered pair.
Consequently
\[
          \frac{\log M(\gamma)}{L}
            =\frac{D}{2m(m-1)}\sum_{i<j}|b_i-b_j| .                    \tag{1}
\]
In the unbalanced case the [signed Galois group](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#statement) contains all even sign changes and the representatives are independent modulo torsion.
Conditional on any projected permutation, the two signs at the nonneutral labels are jointly uniform because their prescribed values can be realized with an even sign change using a third label.
The mean of \(|\pm x\pm y|\) for two independent signs is \(\max(|x|,|y|)\).
This gives
\[
          \frac{\log M(\gamma)}{L}
            =\frac{D}{4\binom m2}\sum_{i<j}\max(|b_i|,|b_j|).          \tag{2}
\]

<a id="balanced"></a>
## Counting balanced orbits in the presence of torsion

The [relation lattice modulo torsion](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations) is generated by the all-ones vector in the chosen orientation.
Two exponent vectors differing by a constant integer vector thus give values differing by a known power of \(\zeta\), which need not be one.
To lower-bound \(D\), it suffices to separate vectors even modulo constants.
Two distinct permutations of \(b\) cannot differ by a constant, since their coordinate sums agree.
The two possible groups in the [quadratic-field description](Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#field) both contain the pure even permutations and both realize a negative orientation.
When some coordinate is repeated, an odd permutation fixing \(b\) lets the even permutations realize every distinct permutation of \(b\).
The orbit then also realizes the negatives of all these permutations.

For constant \(b\) the value is \(\zeta^h\).
For a single exceptional coordinate \(h+u\), the positive and negative permutation lists are disjoint modulo constants.
Indeed the repeated value of multiplicity \(m-1\) must match the repeated value, leaving respective exceptions differing from it by \(u\) and \(-u\).
Each list has \(m\) members, and these \(2m\) exponent vectors exhaust the possible actual Galois images.
Thus \(D=2m\) regardless of \(\zeta\), and (1) gives the stated cost.

In all remaining cases the sum in (1) is at least \(2(m-2)\).
If a largest multiplicity \(k\) satisfies \(2\le k\le m-2\), the pairs joining this class to its complement alone contribute at least \(k(m-k)\ge2(m-2)\).
When every entry is distinct, all \(\binom m2\) pairs contribute at least one.
We claim that \(D\ge m(m-1)\) in these remaining cases.
For multiplicities \(m-2,2\) the positive list has size \(\binom m2\) and the negative list is disjoint modulo constants by the same repeated-value argument, now with multiplicities \(m-2\) and two.
Indeed each entry in the smaller class differs from the repeated major value by the same nonzero integer, whose sign reverses under negation.
This gives exactly \(D=2\binom m2\), as the two lists exhaust the orbit.
With multiplicities \(m-2,1,1\) the positive list alone has \(m(m-1)\) entries.

If the largest multiplicity is at most \(m-3\) and an entry is repeated, some union of value classes has size \(j\) between \(3\) and \(m-3\).
Take a single class of size at least three if one exists, or combine classes of size at most two until their total first reaches three or four.
Every possible \(j\)-element set of positions for this union occurs, giving at least \(\binom mj\ge\binom m3\ge m(m-1)\) distinct permutations since \(m\ge8\).
When all entries differ, pure even permutations alone yield \(m!/2\ge m(m-1)\) distinct vectors modulo constants.
This proves the degree bound.
Substitution in (1) gives the threshold \(m-2\), and two unit exponents of the same sign with all other entries zero attain both bounds.

<a id="unbalanced"></a>
## Counting supports in the unbalanced group

Independence modulo torsion makes distinct exponent vectors give distinct actual conjugates whenever they occur in the same orbit.
In particular a nonzero exponent on one label realizes all \(2m\) signed powers at that exponent magnitude, and its measure has the stated power scaling.
Suppose there are \(s\ge2\) supported labels.
If \(s<m\), the \(S_m\) projection places them on every \(s\)-set and an unused label permits every sign choice there by even flips.
Thus \(D\ge2^s\binom ms\).
Every pair of labels meeting the support contributes at least one to the sum in (2).
Consequently
\[
 \frac{\log M(\gamma)}{L}
 \ \ge\ 2^{s-2}\left(\binom ms-\binom{m-2}s\right).
\]
The binomial difference counts the \(s\)-sets meeting a fixed pair, by double counting incidences with unordered pairs in (2).
For \(2\le s\le m-2\) it equals \(\binom{m-1}{s-1}+\binom{m-2}{s-1}\ge2m-3\).
For \(s=m-1\) it equals \(m\) and the factor \(2^{s-2}\) gives more than \(2m-3\).
For \(s=m\), at least \(2^{m-1}\) sign patterns occur and all pairs contribute at least one, giving \(D/4\ge2^{m-3}\ge2m-3\) in (2).
Taking \(s=2\) and both absolute exponents equal to one realizes precisely \(4\binom m2\) distinct vectors.
Then (2) gives the exact cost \(2m-3\), agreeing with the [squared-radius calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#statement) when the two exterior roots are selected.

Finally the [two-height estimate at one block](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) gives \(L\ge c/(\log(6m))^{106}\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
Multiplication by either of the two sharp thresholds proves divergence as claimed.
