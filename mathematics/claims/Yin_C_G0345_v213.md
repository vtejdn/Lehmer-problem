# Reciprocal reduction of the whole block and delays that are powers of five

<a id="statement"></a>
## An order bound for factors at shorter delays

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\), and a residue characteristic \(\ell\).
For positive integers \(u_i,n\) and a sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) write
\[
 A=\prod_i(X^{u_i}-p_i),\quad D=\sum_i u_i,\quad A^*=X^D A(1/X),
 \qquad P=X^nA+\varepsilon A^*.
\]
Set \(\Delta=u_i\) if \(p_i=\ell\), and \(\Delta=0\) otherwise.
Let \(V=X^{-\Delta}\bar A\in\mathbb F_\ell[X]\), where the bar denotes reduction.
Suppose that \(V^*=cV\) for some \(c\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times\), with reversal taken in the degree of \(V\).
Put \(N=n+\Delta=mM\), where \(M=\ell^{v_\ell(N)}\), and \(o=\operatorname{ord}_m(\ell)\), taking \(o=1\) when \(m=1\).
Put \(s=\max(1,\omega(m))\), with \(\omega\) the number of distinct prime divisors.
There are positive constants \(C,C_1,c_1\), depending only on the fixed primes, such that if
\[
                  n\ge C(D+1)\min(m,M)\log^3(2n),                     \tag{1}
\]
then every irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(S\) of \(P\) satisfies
\[
               \min\!\left(\frac{\deg S}{n},m(S)\right)
                   \ge c_1\,\frac{o}{m}\exp(-C_1s^2).                 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(m(S)\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
The sufficient range \(n\ge C'(D+1)^2\log^6(2D)\) works for every \(m,M\).
The [bounded-order-ratio and fixed-prime-set consequences](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#smooth) of (2) hold throughout (1).
For the two blocks \(X^g-2,X^g-3\), the characteristic \(5\) gives this result with effective delay \(n\), alongside the tests at \(2,3\) with effective delay \(n+g\).
If \(n\) is a power of \(5\) and \(n\ge C''(g+1)\log^3(2g)\), the parent has exactly one noncyclotomic irreducible factor, it occurs once, and its measure is \(\log6+O(\exp(-c_2 n/g))\) for an absolute \(c_2>0\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## The reversal condition and its local consequences

The polynomial \(V\) is monic with nonzero constant coefficient and \(\overline{A^*}=V^*\).
The reversal condition is equivalent to invariance of the multiset of roots of \(V\) in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell\) under inversion, including multiplicities.
Reversing the identity again yields \(c^2=1\).
One sufficient condition is that for each fixed exponent \(w\), the multiset of the residues \(p_i\bmod\ell\) with \(p_i\ne\ell,\ u_i=w\) is invariant under inversion.
In fact, the reciprocal of \(X^w-a\) is \(-a(X^w-a^{-1})\), so these factors may be permuted in pairs.
Testing \(V^*=cV\) directly also allows coincidences among roots of factors of different degrees.

Choose \(b\in\{1,-1\}\) congruent to \(-\varepsilon c\pmod\ell\), taking \(b=1\) for \(\ell=2\), and set
\[
 H=bA+\varepsilon X^\Delta A^*,\quad h=v_\ell(\operatorname{content}H),
 \quad B=D+\Delta,\qquad F=X^\Delta P .
\]
Then \(H\) is zero modulo \(\ell\), since \(X^\Delta\overline{A^*}=c\bar A\).
It is nonzero in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), since a zero of \(A\) has modulus greater than one and is neither zero nor a zero of \(A^*\).
Its coefficient length is at most \(2\prod_i(1+p_i)\), so \(1\le h\le\log_\ell(2\prod_i(1+p_i))\).
When \(\Delta>0\), the constant coefficient is divisible by \(\ell\) exactly once and \(h=1\).
Because \(b^M=b\), we have
\[
             \bar F=(X^m-b)^M\bar A,\qquad
 F(\zeta(1+Y))=H(\zeta(1+Y))+
            bA(\zeta(1+Y))((1+Y)^N-1)                                \tag{3}
\]
at each \(\zeta^m=b\).
These are the two identities used in the [valuation count at the lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v201.md#local).
In particular, when \(B<M/\ell^h\), all but \(mB+D\) roots of \(F\), counted with multiplicity, have
\[
             0<v(x^m-b)\le (M/\ell^{h-1}-B)^{-1},\qquad v(\ell)=1.    \tag{4}
\]
The substitution in (3) preserves the minimum coefficient valuation \(h\) in the degree-at-most-\(B\) polynomial \(H\), and the second summand has exactly the binomial coefficient bounds of that count.
The at most \(D\) roots outside the \(m\) residue classes follow from the reduction in (3).
Thus no condition on a single seed residue is needed beyond the displayed whole-block identity.

<a id="range"></a>
## Ramification counts in the two size ranges

Let \(x\) be a root of \(S\), \(d=\deg S\), and let \(\mathrm h(x)\) denote absolute logarithmic height.
The parent is monic with constant term \(\pm1\), hence \(x\) is a nontorsion unit.
For \(n\ge2D\) the [two height bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v203.md#heights) used for prime blocks give \(\mathrm h(x)\asymp1/n\) without a restriction on \(\gcd(n,u_1,\ldots,u_k)\).
Indeed, applying the height bound for the ratio of \(A^*\) and \(A\) at \(x\) gives \((n-D)\mathrm h(x)\le\log\prod_i(1+p_i)\), while the [width inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) for \(P\) with at most \(2^{k+1}\) terms gives \((n+D)\mathrm h(x)\gg1\).
Thus \(m(S)\asymp d/n\).
The [degree-dependent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v199.md#height) \(m(S)\gg1/\log^3(2d)\), together with \(d\le n+D\), now gives \(d\gg n/\log^3(2n)\).
Enlarge \(C\) in (1) so that \(n\ge2D\) and
\[
                  d\ge 32(D+1)\min(m,M).
\]

If \(m\le M\), (1) also gives \(M\ge n/m\ge 2\ell^h(B+1)\), since \(h\) is bounded.
The exceptions in (4) number at most \(mB+D\le d/4\).
At every remaining conjugate, \(x^m-b\) has positive valuation in \(K_x=\mathbb Q_\ell(x)\), whose value group is \(e_x^{-1}\mathbb Z\) for the ramification index \(e_x\).
Thus \(e_x\ge M/\ell^{h-1}-B\gg M\), and \(\bar x^m=\bar b\).
Reduction in (3) gives residue multiplicity at most \(M+D\le2M\), so \(e_x\le2M\) by the [local conjugate multiplicity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#embeddings).

If \(M<m\), discard the classes outside \(X^m=\bar b\) and the roots of \(\bar A\) or \(\overline{H/\ell^h}\) on this locus.
By (3) at most \((D+B)M+D\le d/4\) roots of \(F\) are discarded, even counting multiplicities.
Every remaining conjugate has residue multiplicity \(M\), so \(e_x\le M\).
In its class \(x=\zeta(1+y)\), where \(\zeta^m=b\), the identity (3) gives \(v((1+y)^N-1)=h\), because \(v(A(x))=0,\ v(H(x))=h\).
The [binomial valuation estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#embeddings) gives \(0<v(x^m-b)=v(y)\le\ell^{h-1}/M\), hence \(e_x\gg M\).
This use of the binomial estimate depends only on (3) and the bounded \(h\).

In both cases at least three quarters of the \(d\) conjugates have \(\bar x^m=\bar b,\ c_0M\le e_x\le2M\) for one constant \(c_0>0\).
The [residue-order and common-exponent argument](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#order) now gives \(d/n\gg (o/m)\exp(-C_1 s^2)\).
Its other inputs are \(N=mM\in[n,2n]\), the upper bound \(\mathrm h(x)\ll1/n\), and the fact that \(x\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit, all valid here.
Using \(m(S)\asymp d/n\) proves (2).
For the sufficient range independent of \(m,M\), use \(\min(m,M)\le\sqrt{N}\le\sqrt{2n}\).
The function \(\sqrt{x}/\log^3(2x)\) is increasing for large \(x\), and at \(x=C'(D+1)^2\log^6(2D)\) it exceeds any specified constant times \(D+1\) on taking \(C'\) sufficiently large.
This proves (1) throughout the claimed range.

<a id="five"></a>
## Exact factor count for the blocks \(2,3\)

For \(A=(X^u-2)(X^v-3)\), the characteristics \(2,3\) meet the original [individual-seed criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v201.md#statement).
For any other prime \(\ell\), comparison of the constant and leading terms in \(\bar A^*=c\bar A\) forces \(6^2\equiv1\bmod\ell\), so \(\ell=5\) or \(7\).
If \(u\ne v\), comparison at exponents \(u,v\) forces \(2\equiv3c,\ 3\equiv2c\bmod\ell\).
Neither \(c=1\) at \(5\) nor \(c=-1\) at \(7\) satisfies these congruences.
For \(u=v=g\), put \(Y=X^g\); then
\[
          A=Y^2-5Y+6,\qquad A^*=6Y^2-5Y+1 .
\]
At \(7\) the coefficient of \(Y\) excludes \(c=-1\).
At \(5\) both polynomials reduce to \(Y^2+1\), giving \(c=1,\ \Delta=0,\ b=-\varepsilon,\ H=5\varepsilon(Y^2-1),\ h=1\).
Consequently \(2,3,5\) are exactly the characteristics satisfying the whole-block criterion in the equal-exponent family.
The effective delays are \(n+g,n+g,n\), respectively, and at \(5\) the reduction is \((X^m+\varepsilon)^M(X^{2g}+1)\).

If \(n=5^a\) then \(m=1,\ M=n,\ B=D=2g\) at \(5\).
For \(n\ge C''(g+1)\log^3(2g)\), (1) holds, since \(n/\log^3(2n)\) is eventually increasing and \(C''\) can be enlarged uniformly.
The bound (4) leaves at most \(B+D=4g\) roots outside \(0<v(x-b)\le1/(n-2g)\), and the degree bound above makes \(d>4g\) for every noncyclotomic factor.
Each such factor therefore has a conjugate with ramification index at least \(n-2g\) and has degree at least \(n-2g\).
With \(n>6g\) we have \(2(n-2g)>n+2g=\deg P\), so at most one such factor can occur, even with multiplicity.

To see existence and evaluate its measure, take \(\delta=\exp(-c_2 n/g)\) with \(0<c_2<\log(3/2)\), and suppose \(n/g\) is sufficiently large.
On each of the four circles \(|X|^g=2\pm\delta,\,3\pm\delta\), the factor nearest its target radius in \(A\) has modulus at least \(\delta\), and the other has modulus at least \(1-\delta\).
There \(|X|^n\ge(3/2)^{n/g}\) and \(|A^*(X)|\) is bounded by an absolute constant.
Consequently \(|X^n A(X)|>|A^*(X)|\) on these circles, and Rouché's theorem puts exactly \(g\) roots of \(P\) in each respective annulus around radii \(2^{1/g},3^{1/g}\).
The [exterior root bound](Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#allocation) gives at most \(2g\) exterior roots altogether, so these exhaust them.
Their total log modulus equals \(\log6+O(\delta)\), since the \(g\) logarithms from each annulus lie between \(g^{-1}\log(j-\delta)\) and \(g^{-1}\log(j+\delta)\) for \(j=2,3\).
These exterior roots give the unique noncyclotomic factor, and all its complementary factors have logarithmic measure zero.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Primitive inputs outside both individual-seed order tests

Let \(T_t\) be a product of \(t\ge1\) distinct primes greater than \(5\), and take \(g=5T_t-1\).
Take any sufficiently large multiple \(a\) of \(\operatorname{ord}_{T_t}(5)\) and put \(n=5^a\), including choices with \(n\ge C(2g+1)^9\).
Then \(\gcd(n,g)=1\) and \(T_t\mid n+g\).
For each \(\ell=2,3\), all \(t\) odd primes of \(T_t\) divide the prime-to-\(\ell\) part \(m_\ell\) of \(n+g\).
The elementary [least common multiple bound for the order](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#smooth) gives \(m_\ell/\operatorname{ord}_{m_\ell}(\ell)\ge2^{t-1}\).
Both original order ratios can thus grow without bound on these same primitive inputs.
At \(5\) the effective delay is the pure power \(n\), and the exact single-factor conclusion above holds for both signs.
Here \(n+g\) is allowed to be composite; this construction does not resolve the [prime-delay family with simultaneous small orders](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#limits).
