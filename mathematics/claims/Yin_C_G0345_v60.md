# Pairing nonreal exterior roots without taking powers

<a id="matching"></a>
## A reduction to real exterior roots at the original measure

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) whose \(2s>0\) exterior roots consist of \(s\) nonreal conjugate pairs.
Label the \(m\) unordered inverse pairs of roots, and let \(G\) be the Galois group of the splitting field.
On the labels, join the two members of each exterior conjugate pair and take all the translates of these edges under \(G\).
Ignore repeated edges for the moment.
If this graph is a matching, there exists a monic reciprocal integer polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(2m\), with \(M(Q)=M(P)\) and exactly \(s\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity, all positive real.
If \(M(P)<2\), some monic irreducible reciprocal factor \(F\) of \(Q\) has \(1<M(F)\le M(P)\) and at most \(s\) exterior roots, all positive real.
This conclusion applies regardless of rational relations among the exterior log moduli.

Indeed, the inverse-pair action is transitive and the graph covers every vertex.
Each matched block either contains the two labels of one original exterior pair or contains only neutral labels, since the matched partner of every exterior label is prescribed.
For a block choose representatives \(x,y\) and list \(xy,x/y,y/x,(xy)^{-1}\).
This multiset is independent of orientations and ordering, so the polynomial on all listed values is integral, monic and closed under inversion, with constant one.
Every neutral block contributes only unit-circle roots.
Writing the exterior roots of a nonneutral block as \(\lambda,\overline\lambda\), that block contributes \(|\lambda|^2,|\lambda|^{-2},\lambda/\overline\lambda,\overline\lambda/\lambda\).
This proves the claims about \(Q\).
Take the minimal polynomial of any exterior value.
Each of its off-circle conjugates is positive, and the product of its exterior moduli is at most \(M(Q)\).
If it had no unit-circle conjugate, all its conjugates \(\gamma\) would be real in \((0,2)\), with \(0<|1-\gamma|<1\), contradicting the nonzero integral norm of \(1-\gamma\).
A unit-circle conjugate and its complex conjugate are mutually inverse, so its irreducible polynomial is reciprocal.

## Relations for any number of independent exterior log moduli

Write \(\lambda_j,\overline{\lambda_j}\) for the exterior pairs and choose representatives \(v_i\) of all \(m\) inverse-pair labels.
Suppose now that the \(s\) real numbers \(\log|\lambda_j|\) are rationally independent.
Retain parallel edges and attach sign \(-ef\) to the translated edge whose endpoints carry \(g\lambda_j=v_i^e,\ g\overline{\lambda_j}=v_k^f\), where \(e,f\in\{1,-1\}\).
The full lattice of integer vectors \(a\) with \(\prod_i v_i^{a_i}\) a root of unity is exactly the solution lattice of \(a_i=(-ef)a_k\) on all signed edges.
Indeed its defining absolute-log equations test every conjugate of that algebraic unit, by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001).
For the conjugate with embedding \(g^{-1}\), the equation groups as \(\sum_j(ea_i+fa_k)\log|\lambda_j|=0\) with the endpoints and signs of the corresponding edges.
Independence is precisely what allows the coefficients to be set to zero separately.

All connected components have the same size \(b\), by transitivity.
A component where every cycle has positive sign has a generator taking values \(\pm1\) on its vertices, obtained by propagating a value \(1\) at one vertex.
A component with a negative cycle forces every coefficient there to vanish.
This balance condition is unchanged by switching an individual representative with its inverse, which changes signs on its incident edges, so transitivity makes the components either all balanced or all unbalanced.
For relations supported on the \(q=m-2s\) neutral labels, only balanced components entirely disjoint from the exterior labels contribute generators.
In particular the size of the set of exterior pairs plays no role in solving the neutral lattice.

## Interlacing powers in the absence of a matching

Continue to assume independence of the exterior log moduli and suppose the underlying graph is not a matching.
Thus \(b\ge3\).
Every power polynomial \(P_n\) on the \(n\)-th powers of the original roots is irreducible of degree \(2m\), with \(M(P_n)=M(P)^n\).
Indeed all power fibers have the same size by transitivity.
A nonsingleton fiber above an exterior value can contain only the two members of its own conjugate pair, since distinct pairs have different moduli.
Then each exterior pair fills one fiber.
The fibers on the roots project to pairs of distinct inverse labels, since no original root is torsion and inversion carries a fiber to a disjoint inverse fiber.
They induce a matching on labels containing all the original edges and their translates, contrary to the hypothesis.

For \(q\ge2\), a set of exponents of positive natural density gives one neutral trace in each complementary interval of the \(q-1\) zeros of \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\), namely \(2\cos(k\pi/q)\) for \(1\le k<q\).
This is partial torsion interlacing with exactly \(2s\) unused traces.
If there are no neutral relation generators, the density equals \(q!/q^q\).
If there are \(t>0\) such generators, let \(L\) clear the orders of their torsion products.
Then the density of the occupancy event exists and is at least \((8bq)^{-(q-t)}/L\).
For \(q=1\) the single neutral trace supplies the empty interlacer at every power.

To see the density assertion, the [signed-block torus and equal-bin construction](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#bins) applies with \(t\) constrained blocks of \(b\) coordinates and \(q-bt\) free coordinates.
For even \(b\) assign \(b\) consecutive bins per constrained block, and for odd \(b\) assign the first \(3t\) bins to the signed triples and the next \((b-3)t\) to adjacent pairs, exactly as in that construction.
The unassigned bins are in bijection with the \(q-bt\) free coordinates and receive their centers.
The same signed zero sums in the constrained blocks therefore have the same endpoint margins, including when every bin has been assigned to a constrained block.
On multiples of \(L\) the neutral tuple is equidistributed on the connected torus of those block equations, by the character criterion and the full neutral relation lattice.
Each other residue class equidistributes on a translate, and every one-coordinate projection is uniform there.
This makes the bin boundaries null on every residue class and proves existence of the overall density.
Varying the \(q-t\) independent angular coordinates by less than \(\pi/(8bq)\) on the identity torus gives the displayed lower bound.
With no generators all coordinates are independent uniform phases in the limit and the \(q!\) permissible assignments each have probability \(q^{-q}\).
These densities give no uniform upper bound on the first successful exponent.
