# Cyclotomic multiples attain the exact coefficient rate of a fixed polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## The minimum with all additional roots of unity

Let \(P\) be a fixed nonzero real polynomial and \(B(a)\) a nonnegative integer budget for the degree of a product \(C\) of monic cyclotomic polynomials, with multiplicities allowed and \(C=1\) permitted.
If \(B(a)/a\to\infty\), then
\[
     \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac1a\log \min_{\deg C\le B(a)}\|P^a C\|_1
                          =m(P).                                         \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure, and \(\|\cdot\|_1\) sums absolute coefficients.
Conversely, if \(P\) is not a monomial, every sequence of cyclotomic products reaching this rate has \(\deg C/a\to\infty\).
This necessity already holds with the supremum norm on the unit circle.
Thus for each fixed nonmonomial \(P\) the degree condition for (1) is exact.

More generally, for finitely many fixed nonzero complex polynomials \(P_1,\ldots,P_j\), a common product \(C\) gives the rate
\[
 \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac1a\log\min_{\deg C\le B(a)}\max_i\|P_i^a C\|_1
      =\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max_{i,\pm}|P_i(z^{\pm1})|\,d\eta(z),       \tag{2}
\]
where \(\eta\) is normalized angular measure.
The converse degree assertion above concerns a single real polynomial.

<a id="dual"></a>
## Finite separation for cyclotomic logarithms on the circle

Write \(g(z)=\log\max_{i,\pm}|P_i(z^{\pm1})|\) and \(f_n(z)=\log|\Phi_n(z)|\).
These functions are invariant under conjugation on the circle, bounded above and continuous with possible logarithmic singularities regarded as values \(-\infty\).
Their integrals against \(\eta\) are finite, and \(\int f_n\,d\eta=0\).
Let \(I=\int g\,d\eta\).
For every \(\epsilon>0\) there are finitely many \(w_n\ge0\) with
\[
                    \sup_{\mathbb T}\left(g+\sum_{w_n>0}w_n f_n\right)
                                     \le I+\epsilon.                    \tag{3}
\]
Use the following [cyclotomic moment theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v13.md#statement): an even probability measure on the circle with \(\int f_n\,d\mu\ge0\) at every \(n\) is \(\eta\).
To see its application, suppose (3) fails for some \(\epsilon\).
For positive integers \(N\), truncate \(g,f_1,\ldots,f_N\) from below at \(-N\), obtaining continuous functions \(g_N,f_{1,N},\ldots,f_{N,N}\).
Their joint moment vectors over probability measures form a compact convex set in \(\mathbb R^{N+1}\).
It meets \([I+\epsilon,\infty)\times[0,\infty)^N\).
Indeed strict separation from that orthant would give a separating normal all of whose entries are nonnegative, with its supremum on the moment set strictly below its infimum on the orthant.
Its first entry must be positive, since the truncated \(f_{n,N}\) have nonnegative Haar integrals.
Normalize this entry to one.
Point masses then give a supremum strictly below \(I+\epsilon\) for a nonnegative combination added to \(g_N\).
Removing the truncations only decreases it, contradicting the assumed failure.

Choose an even probability measure from each intersection by symmetrizing a representing measure.
Take a weakly convergent subsequence.
For each fixed truncation from below at \(-L\) and each fixed \(n\), the truncated moments at this level dominate their counterparts at level \(N\) once \(N\ge\max(n,L)\).
Passing to the limit with \(L\) fixed and then letting \(L\to\infty\) gives \(\int f_n\,d\mu\ge0\) for all \(n\) and \(\int g\,d\mu\ge I+\epsilon\).
The moment theorem now contradicts the definition of \(I\), proving (3).

<a id="integer"></a>
## An integral product under any divergent normalized degree budget

For fixed weights in (3), take \(C_a=\prod_{w_n>0}\Phi_n^{\lceil a w_n\rceil}\).
Rounding upwards increases the log supremum by at most \(\sum_{w_n>0}\log\|\Phi_n\|_{\mathbb T}\) beyond \(a(I+\epsilon)\).
The bound also holds at the zeros of the product.
The degree of \(C_a\) is \(O_\epsilon(a)\), with the input polynomials fixed, so it fits in the prescribed budget eventually.
For each \(P_i^a C_a\) its coefficient sum is at most the square root of one plus its degree times its circle supremum, by Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval.
Thus the limiting upper rate for the coefficient norm is at most \(I+\epsilon\).
For the lower bound, the largest of the circle suprema of \(P_i^a C\) is the exponential of \(\sup_z(a g(z)+\log|C(z)|)\), since \(|C(z)|=|C(\bar z)|\).
This supremum is at least its integral \(a I\).
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves (2), and real coefficients give (1).

<a id="necessity"></a>
## Bounded normalized degree cannot attain the rate for a nonmonomial

Suppose for a subsequence \(a\to\infty\) that \(\deg C_a/a\) is bounded and \(\log\|P^a C_a\|_{\mathbb T}/a\to m(P)\) for a single real \(P\).
As \(\int\log|P^a C_a|\,d\eta=a m(P)\), the functions \(a^{-1}\log|P^a C_a|-m(P)\) have mean zero and positive parts with integrals tending to zero.
They tend to zero in \(L^1(\eta)\).
Let \(\nu_a\) put mass \(1/a\) at each root of \(C_a\), counting multiplicity.
After taking a subsequence its mass and the measures themselves converge.
The limit is a nonnegative combination of the uniform measures on the finite primitive-root orbits together with a nonnegative multiple of \(\eta\).
Indeed the probability measure on primitive \(n\)-th roots converges to \(\eta\) as \(n\to\infty\).
For each fixed \(k\ge1\) its \(k\)-th moment is bounded in modulus by \(\sum_{d\mid k}d/\varphi(n)\), by Möbius inversion on all \(n\)-th roots, and \(\varphi(n)\to\infty\).
Trigonometric polynomial approximation proves the convergence on continuous functions.
Extract each fixed conductor's mass by a diagonal subsequence and use this convergence uniformly on the remaining tail for each test function.

For each root of \(P\) replace it by \(w=1/\bar z\) if it is outside the circle, and by itself otherwise.
Let \(\nu_P\) be the sum of the Poisson probability measures for these \(w\) in the open disk, using the point mass when \(|w|=1\).
Use multiplicities in this sum, so its mass is \(\deg P\).
The \(k\)-th moment of the Poisson measure is \(w^k\).
For \(k\ge1\) the integral of \(z^k\log|z-w|\) against \(\eta\) is \(-w^k/(2k)\), by the logarithm's power series and, for boundary \(w\), radial convergence in \(L^1\).
For the latter convergence one may use \(\log|1-rz|\ge\log|1-z|+\tfrac12\log r\) and the uniform upper bound \(\log2\).
Reflection only adds a constant to the root logarithm.
It follows from the preceding \(L^1\) convergence that \(\nu_P+\lim\nu_a\) has every nonconstant Fourier coefficient zero.
It equals its total mass times \(\eta\).
Positivity rules out every atomic part of \(\lim\nu_a\), so this limit itself is a multiple of \(\eta\).
Thus \(\nu_P=(\deg P)\eta\) and all positive power sums of the finitely many reflected roots vanish.
Newton's identities make all these roots zero, which is possible only for a monomial \(P\).

<a id="lehmer"></a>
## The unrestricted fixed-square test with a single nontorsion factor

In the [fixed-exponent Lehmer criterion](Yin_C_G0901_v9.md#lehmer) take \(q=2\), either prescribed sign \(\sigma\), and any computable width schedule with \(B(a)/a\to\infty\), \(\log(B(a)+2)=o(a)\).
Impose the additional conditions that the signed graph
\[
                     A(X^2)+\sigma X A^{*B}(X^2)
\]
is monic, has one nontorsion monic irreducible factor \(P\) of exact multiplicity \(a\), and all its other irreducible factors are cyclotomic.
Retain \(A(\alpha^2)\ne0\) at a root of \(P\).
With the same default candidate as in that criterion, the resulting minimum still satisfies
\[
                         \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{\log H_a}{a}=\lambda .
\]
Here \(\lambda\) is the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures.
The lower bound follows already from \(a m(P)\le\log(2\|A\|_1)\), since the two coefficient classes have total norm \(2\|A\|_1\); the default is \(2^a\).

For the converse choose any reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum used to [approach the unrestricted infimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower), and use an [all-power representative of the same measure](Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm), again reciprocal.
Write \(P\) for its monic minimum and \(\alpha\) for a root.
Then \(P(-\alpha)\ne0\) because squaring preserves degree.
For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\) the construction above gives a monic \(G=P^a C_a\) of degree \(O_{P,\epsilon}(a)\), with coefficient rate at most \(m(P)+\epsilon+o(1)\).
It is reciprocal up to a sign \(\delta\), since its factors are.
For all large \(a\), let \(j=2B+1-\deg G>0\) and multiply by \(X^j+\sigma\delta\).
This is a product of cyclotomic polynomials, fixes the formal reversal sign at \(\sigma\), and costs at most a factor two in norm.
The resulting monic polynomial of odd degree \(2B+1\) has the displayed signed form by splitting into even and odd exponents.
Its value at \(-\alpha\) is nonzero because \(P(-\alpha)\ne0\) and none of the added roots is nontorsion.
At \(\alpha\) its order is exactly \(a\).
The sum of its values at \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\) is \(2A(\alpha^2)\), which proves the required nonvanishing.
Take the infimum over representatives after \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
This also realizes the [degree-free coefficient infimum](Yin_C_G0901_v12.md#width) using just one noncyclotomic irreducible factor and cyclotomic cofactors.
An absolute lower coefficient rate for this restricted set would settle the unrestricted Lehmer problem.
