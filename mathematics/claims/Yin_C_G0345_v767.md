# Divisibility of a bounded-length unit modulo roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary phases in the original field and in external radical equations

Let \(L\ge2\) be an integer and let \(\beta\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit annihilated by a nonzero integer polynomial of coefficient length at most \(L\).
Put \(F=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(d=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and \(m=m(\beta)=d h(\beta)\), where \(h\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
There is a positive integer \(H_L\) with \(\log(2H_L)\ll L^3\log(2L)\) such that for every equation
\[
                   \theta^r=\xi\beta,\qquad \theta\in F,\quad
                   \xi\in\mu(F),\quad r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},
\]
one has
\[
               \frac r{\gcd(r,\operatorname{ord}\xi)}\mid H_L .           \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\mu(F)\) is the group of roots of unity in \(F\).
The phase \(\xi\) is arbitrary, and the integer \(H_L\) is independent of the polynomial, degree and field.

Let \(k\) be the largest such \(r\) allowing any \(\xi\), equivalently the index of the subgroup generated by \(\beta\) in its saturation in \(\mathcal O_F^\times/\mu(F)\).
Put \(G(d)=(\log\log(5d)/\log(2d))^3\) and \(J(d)=\log\log\log(100d)>0\).
Then
\[
                 k\ \ll_L\sqrt{m J(d)/G(d)}.                           \tag{2}
\]
For any external root of unity \(\eta\), any \(g\ge1\), and any root \(\alpha^g=\eta\beta\), put \(t=[F(\alpha):F]\).
If \(\kappa\) is the number of conjugates of \(\beta\) whose quotient by \(\beta\) is a root of unity, then
\[
             g/\gcd(g,t)\mid k,\qquad
             m(\alpha)\ge \frac{m}{\kappa k}
                     \gg_L \frac1\kappa\sqrt{\frac{mG(d)}{J(d)}}.        \tag{3}
\]
In particular, for nontorsion algebraic-unit solutions on the [primitive sections of a fixed nonmonomial rational graph](Yin_C_G0345_v763.md#statement) at large slope, these bounds hold with constants depending on the graph alone and without the factor \(1/\kappa\) after adjustment of the constant.
The [bounded-length radical theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) already gives the stronger bound \(m(\alpha)\ge m/R_L\) without \(\kappa\) for every ordinary radical \(\alpha^g=\beta\).
The new divisibility problem concerns the class of \(\beta\) modulo arbitrary roots of unity, also permitting externally twisted radical equations.

<a id="local"></a>
## The first nonzero evaluation in a chain of twisted prime powers

Use the [shortest-relation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v208.md#internal) for \(\beta\) to choose an integer relation \(P\) of minimum length, with minimum exponent zero, degree \(T\) and \(s\le L\) terms.
Then \(T h(\beta)\le H=(L-1)\log L\), since every proper cut is nonzero and every consecutive gap times \(h(\beta)\) is at most \(\log L\).
Fix an absolute \(0<c_0\le1\) such that the [sparse width inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(T h(x)\ge c_0/s^2\) whenever \(P(x)=0\) for a nontorsion unit \(x\).
Put \(A=\max(1,H L^2/c_0)\).

For \(p^a\Vert r\), set \(c=v_p(\operatorname{ord}\xi)\) and let \(\zeta\) be the \(p\)-primary component of \(\xi\).
Raising to \(p^a\) is invertible on the group generated by the prime-to-\(p\) component of \(\xi\).
Dividing \(\theta^{r/p^a}\) by the appropriate element of this group gives \(y\in F\) with \(y^{p^a}=\zeta\beta\).
For \(0\le i\le a\) put \(z_i=y^{p^{a-i}}\), \(x_i=z_i/\zeta\) and \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
Then \(x_0=\beta\), \(h(x_i)=h(\beta)/p^i\), and \(E(z_i)=E(y)=F\) since the relevant power of each \(z_i\) gives \(\zeta\beta\).
All these elements are algebraic units.

Let \(j\) be the largest index with \(P(x_i)=0\) for every \(0\le i\le j\).
Applying the width inequality at \(x_j\) gives \(p^j\le A\).
If \(a\le j+c\) then \(p^{\max(0,a-c)}\le p^j\le A\).
Otherwise \(j<a\), \(P(x_{j+1})\ne0\), and the monic relative minimal polynomials of \(z_j,z_{j+1}\) over \(E\) are the characteristic polynomials of successive powers \(M^{p^{a-j}},M^{p^{a-j-1}}\) of the companion matrix of the relative minimal polynomial of \(y\).
Indeed all three elements generate \(F\) over \(E\), so no conjugate is repeated in either characteristic polynomial.
The [matrix congruence over the prime-power cyclotomic field](Yin_C_G0345_v766.md#congruence) applies since \(a-j>c\).
It includes \(c=0\) and the dyadic cases and gives a coefficientwise congruence modulo \(p^{a-j-c}\mathcal O_E\).
As \(P(Y/\zeta)\in\mathcal O_E[Y]\) vanishes at \(z_j\), it is divisible there by the monic relative minimal polynomial.
Evaluation at \(z_{j+1}\) thus gives a nonzero \(P(x_{j+1})\in p^{a-j-c}\mathcal O_F\).
Taking the absolute norm, bounding the values by the length and degree, and taking \(d\)-th roots gives
\[
      p^{a-j-c}\le L\exp(T h(x_{j+1}))
                    \le L\exp(H/p^{j+1}).
\]
Consequently whenever \(a>c\),
\[
                          p^{a-c}\le A L\exp(H/p).                     \tag{4}
\]
If \(j\ge1\), such a prime satisfies \(p\le A\).
If \(j=0\) and \(a>c\), one has \(p\le L\exp(H/p)\), which is impossible for \(p>2L\) and \(p>2H\).
Take \(Q=\max(A,2L,2H,2)\) and
\[
               H_L=\prod_{p\le Q,\; p\ {\rm prime}}
                         p^{\lfloor\log(AL\exp(H/p))/\log p\rfloor}.
\]
The [elementary prime summation](Yin_C_G0345_v208.md#exponents) gives \(\log(2H_L)\ll L^3\log(2L)\).
By (4) every prime power in \(r/\gcd(r,\operatorname{ord}\xi)\) divides this same integer, proving (1).

<a id="saturation"></a>
## The index of the class of the original unit

All \(\theta\) satisfying \(\theta^r=\xi\beta\) in \(F\) are units and have \(h(\theta)=h(\beta)/r\).
There are only finitely many possible \(r\) by Northcott in the fixed field.
In the torsion-free group \(\mathcal O_F^\times/\mu(F)\) these exponents are exactly the divisors of the coordinate gcd of the class of \(\beta\) in a basis of the free unit group.
Equivalently the set is closed under divisors and least common multiples by Bézout.
Indeed if \(r,s\) are admissible and \(l=\operatorname{lcm}(r,s)\), choose integers \(A',B'\) with \(A'l/r+B'l/s=1\); the product of the two roots to powers \(A',B'\) then has \(l\)-th power \(\beta\) modulo torsion.
This proves the characterization of \(k\) and that every admissible \(r\) divides it.

Use a witness \(\theta^k=\xi\beta\) and put \(w=\operatorname{ord}\xi\).
Then \(k\le H_L w\) by (1).
Here \(\mathbb Q(\xi,\theta)=F\), so \([\mathbb Q(\xi,\theta):\mathbb Q(\xi)]=d/\varphi(w)\) and \(h(\theta)=m/(kd)\).
For clarity, the [relative cubic height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v727.md#statement) applies over \(\mathbb Q(\xi)\) with Frobenius class size one.
Only the primes dividing \(w\) can ramify, since at other primes \(Z^w-1\) has separable reduction and its roots lift in an unramified extension.
There are at most \(\log_2 w\) exceptional odd primes.
The elementary inequality \(w\le2\varphi(w)^2\le2d^2\) follows by multiplying the factors \(p^{a-2}(p-1)^2\) of \(\varphi(w)^2/w\), of which only the factor at \(2^1\Vert w\) can be below one.
Thus the square root of one plus the exception count is \(O(\sqrt{\log(2d)})\).
The other term in the cubic height denominator is \(\log(2D)/\log\log(5D)\) with \(D=d/\varphi(w)\le d\), and is \(O(\log(2d)/\log\log(5d))\) by eventual monotonicity.
These estimates give \(D h(\theta)\gg G(d)\), or
\[
                          k\varphi(w)\ll m/G(d).
\]
Since the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) divides \(P\) and the nonzero integer quotient has nonnegative measure, \(m\le\log L\).
It follows from \(w\le2\varphi(w)^2\) that \(w\ll_L\log^6(2d)\).
The [totient lower bound](../audits/literature/BV19b/Yin_U002.md#bv19b-collision-partitions) gives \(w/\varphi(w)\ll\log\log(5w)\ll_L J(d)\), including bounded orders.
Consequently \(k^2\le H_L k w\ll_L m J(d)/G(d)\), proving (2).

<a id="external"></a>
## Radical equations with an external torsion multiplier

In \(\alpha^g=\eta\beta\) one has \(\eta\in F(\alpha)\), and both \(\alpha\) and its inverse are algebraic integers.
Take the norm to \(F\) and write \(b=\operatorname N_{F(\alpha)/F}\alpha\).
The norm of \(\eta\) is a root of unity in \(F\), so \(g[b]=t[\beta]\) in the free unit group modulo torsion.
In basis coordinates, the gcd of the coordinates of \([\beta]\) is \(k\).
Therefore \(g\mid tk\), equivalently \(g/\gcd(g,t)\mid k\).
This argument extends the [norm test for a primitive class](Yin_C_G0345_v336.md#norm) to its actual index, as in the [saturation description](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#statement).

An embedding of \(F(\alpha)\) fixing \(\alpha\) sends \(\beta\) to \((\eta/\sigma\eta)\beta\).
It is determined by this image and therefore \([F(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\le\kappa\).
Since \(h(\alpha)=h(\beta)/g\), the degree formula gives \(m(\alpha)/m=t/(g[F(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha)])\ge1/(\kappa k)\).
Each fixed nonmonomial graph at the stated primitive slopes supplies a bounded-length integer relation, and the [torsion quotient bound for its conjugate components](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#twists) gives bounded \(\kappa\), as also computed for the [four-term pole](Yin_C_G0345_v763.md#poles).
This proves (3) and its application.

<a id="comparison"></a>
## The cost of removing a phase before applying the ordinary radical theorem

Equation (1) measures divisibility inside \(F\) after allowing an arbitrary phase.
Converting it to an untwisted radical requires adjoining an \(r\)-th root of that phase, and the new relative degree can be the entire \(r\).
For example let \(\zeta_5=\exp(2\pi i/5)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\), \(\theta=(1+\sqrt5)/2\in F\), \(r=5^a\) with \(a\ge1\), and \(\beta=\theta^r/\zeta_5\).
Here \(\theta=1+\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}\).
The field \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) is an intermediate field of the abelian extension \(F/\mathbb Q\) and is stable under conjugation.
It contains \(\overline\beta/\beta=\zeta_5^2\), so it equals \(F\).
Every \(r\)-th root of \(\beta\) equals \(\theta\omega\) with \(\omega^r=\zeta_5^{-1}\).
All such \(\omega\) have order \(5^{a+1}\), giving relative degree \(\varphi(5^{a+1})/\varphi(5)=r\).
The [ordinary radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) is thus one in this example, while \(r\) is arbitrarily large and \(\xi=\zeta_5\) has fixed order.
The coefficient length of a relation for these \(\beta\)'s cannot be bounded as \(a\) varies, by (1).
The example distinguishes the two arithmetic quantities even though it supplies no unbounded saturation at a fixed length.
