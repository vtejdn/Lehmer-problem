# A uniform bound on the radical exponent of normalized prime blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## The gap under every common dilation

Fix distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\), \(k\ge2\), and put
\[
 L=2\prod_{i=1}^k(1+p_i),\qquad H=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,L^2).
\]
For positive integers \(b_i\) with \(\gcd_i b_i=1\), let \(D=\sum_i b_i\), \(A(X)=\prod_i(X^{b_i}-p_i)\), and
\[
 P(X)=X^N A(X)+\varepsilon X^D A(1/X),\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
There is a constant \(C\) depending only on the primes such that the following holds for \(N\ge C(D+1)^9\).
Let \(S\) be the [unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(P\)](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement), let \(\beta\) be any positive real root of \(S\), and write \(B=m(S)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure.
The largest integer \(r\) for which the positive root \(\beta^{1/r}\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) divides \(H\).
More precisely, for each prime power \(q^a\mid r\), \(a\ge1\), one has \(q^a\le L^{1+2/q}\).
For every integer \(h\ge1\),
\[
    \min_{F\mid P(X^h),\,F\ {\rm noncyclotomic}} m(F)
       =\frac{B}{\gcd(h,r)}
       \ \ge\ \frac{\sum_i\log p_i}{2H},                              \tag{1}
\]
where \(F\) ranges over monic irreducible factors.
Each such factor occurs once.
The same lower bound holds for both signs, independently of the normalized exponents, delay and common dilation.

<a id="norm-test"></a>
## A norm bound for one prime power

Let \(\beta\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), and let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be a nonzero polynomial vanishing at \(\beta\).
Write \(n=\deg P\), \(\ell=\sum_j|[X^j]P|\), and \(B=m(\beta)\).
Suppose \(\beta=\theta^{q^a}\) with \(q\) prime, \(a\ge1\) and \(\theta\in K\).
Put \(\gamma=\theta^{q^{a-1}}\).
If \(P(\gamma)\ne0\), then
\[
                         q^a\le \ell\exp\!\left(\frac{nB}{qd}\right). \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the test applies in a polynomial with several noncyclotomic factors whenever its value at this \(\gamma\) is nonzero.

Both \(\theta\) and \(\gamma\) are units since they and their inverses are integral over the ring of integers of \(K\).
The field inclusions \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\gamma)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\theta)\subseteq K\) are equalities, so each of the three units has degree \(d\).
For each embedding \(\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), one has \(|\sigma\gamma|^q=|\sigma\beta|\), whence \(m(\gamma)=B/q\).
Let \(M\) be the integral companion matrix of the monic minimal polynomial of \(\theta\).
The characteristic polynomials of \(M^{q^a}\) and \(M^{q^{a-1}}\) are the respective minimal polynomials \(S_\beta,S_\gamma\), since their eigenvalues run through all \(d\) conjugates of \(\beta,\gamma\), respectively.
The [power congruence](#congruence) gives \(S_\beta-S_\gamma\in q^a\mathbb Z[X]\), and hence \(S_\beta(\gamma)\in q^a\mathcal O_K\).
Monic division gives \(P\in S_\beta\mathbb Z[X]\), so \(P(\gamma)\in q^a\mathcal O_K\).
When this value is nonzero, its absolute norm is at least \(q^{ad}\).
The triangle inequality gives the upper bound
\[
  |\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}P(\gamma)|
  \le \prod_\sigma \ell\max(1,|\sigma\gamma|)^n
  =\ell^d\exp(nB/q).
\]
Taking \(d\)-th roots proves (2).

<a id="congruence"></a>
## Congruence of characteristic polynomials

For an integral square matrix \(M\), a prime \(q\) and \(a\ge1\), the characteristic polynomials of \(M^{q^a}\) and \(M^{q^{a-1}}\) are coefficientwise congruent modulo \(q^a\).
This is Theorem 1.1(ii) of Mazur and Petrenko, *Generalizations of Arnold's version of Euler's theorem for matrices*, printed p. 184 of the [authors' journal-layout copy](../literature/pdf/MP10.pdf#page=2).
Their proof on pp. 185–187 lifts the coefficient congruence under the operation that raises every root of a monic polynomial to its \(q\)-th power.
Here it also follows by counting closed walks.

For any integral square matrix \(T\), replace its entries by their least nonnegative residues modulo \(q^a\) to obtain \(T_0\).
Traces of every positive power of the two matrices are congruent modulo \(q^a\).
Use \(T_0\) as the adjacency matrix of a directed multigraph with distinguishable edges.
The trace of \(T_0^m\) counts closed walks of length \(m\) with a specified initial position.
Among closed walks of length \(q^a\), the walks fixed by rotating through \(q^{a-1}\) positions are exactly the \(q\)-fold repetitions of closed walks of length \(q^{a-1}\).
Every other walk has least period \(q^a\), hence belongs to an orbit of \(q^a\) walks under cyclic rotation.
It follows that \(\operatorname{tr}(T^{q^a})\equiv\operatorname{tr}(T^{q^{a-1}})\pmod{q^a}\).
For \(T=\bigwedge^j M\) the entries are integer minors, and \((\bigwedge^j M)^m=\bigwedge^j(M^m)\).
Its trace is the sum of the principal \(j\)-minors of \(M^m\), which after multiplication by \((-1)^j\) gives the coefficient of degree \(\dim M-j\) in its characteristic polynomial.
Applying the trace congruence for \(1\le j\le\dim M\) proves the assertion, including \(q=2\).

<a id="prime-blocks"></a>
## Bounding all prime powers in the radical exponent

The [exterior disks](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#disks) give a positive real root of \(S\) near any positive binomial root.
By the [normalized factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement), \(P=SU\) with \(S\) simple and \(U\) cyclotomic of total degree at most \(10D\).
Writing \(d=\deg S\) and \(n=N+D\), we have \(d\ge N-9D\), hence \(n/d\le2\) once \(N\ge19D\).
Increase \(C\) to ensure this inequality and \(B\ge\frac12\sum_i\log p_i\) throughout the stated range, using the measure error in the same factorization.
The coefficient length of \(P\) is at most \(L\) by the product and triangle inequalities.
Also \(B=m(P)\le\log L\) by Jensen's formula on the unit circle, since \(U\) has measure zero.

The [positive radical theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#positive-norm) gives a finite largest exponent \(r\) whose divisors are exactly the admissible exponents in the specified real embedding.
For each \(q^a\mid r\), take \(\theta=\beta^{1/q^a}\in K\) and \(\gamma=\beta^{1/q}\).
Since \(m(\gamma)=B/q\) is positive and differs from \(B\), \(\gamma\) is neither torsion nor a conjugate of \(\beta\).
All roots of \(P\) are of these two kinds, proving \(P(\gamma)\ne0\).
Substitution into (2) gives \(q^a\le L^{1+2/q}\le L^2\).
Every prime power dividing \(r\) consequently divides \(H\), proving \(r\mid H\).
The roots of \(U(X^h)\) are torsion, and those of \(S(X^h)\) are nontorsion and simple since \(S\) is separable with nonzero roots.
The [exact minimum for \(S(X^h)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now gives (1).
