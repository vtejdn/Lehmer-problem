# A vanishing degree fraction at all three local characteristics

<a id="statement"></a>
## A global divisor of the specified degree would give small measure

Let \(h\ge3\) be odd and prime to five.
There are arbitrarily large pairs of primes \(r<q\) with \(g=q-r\) a positive multiple of \(2h\), \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) for any prescribed absolute \(C\), and the following properties.
Set \(b=g/h\) and \(d=2(q-1)/h\).
For either \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) put
\[
 P=X^r(X^g-2)(X^g-3)+\varepsilon(1-2X^g)(1-3X^g),\qquad
 Q=P/(X+\varepsilon).
\]
The removed linear polynomial is the [entire cyclotomic part](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion).
At each of \(\ell=2,3,5\), \(Q\) has a monic reciprocal degree-\(d\) divisor over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) with constant term one and coprime reduction to its complement.
Consequently for every \(a_2,a_3,a_5\ge1\) there is a factorization into monic reciprocal polynomials modulo \(2^{a_2}3^{a_3}5^{a_5}\) with this first degree.

For any monic rational divisor \(S\mid Q\) of degree \(d\), whether or not it has the specified local reductions, the total exterior share is \(2g/h\) and
\[
                         m(S)=\frac{2}{h}\log 6
                               +O\!\left(g^2 e^{-c r/(2g+1)^2}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure and \(c>0\) is absolute.
Thus a rational divisor of this degree for a sequence of unbounded \(h\) would produce positive Mahler measures tending to zero, even if the divisors were reducible.
Existence over the three local fields leaves this rational divisibility question open.

<a id="prime-pairs"></a>
## Two nearby split primes

For each fixed \(h\) use the finite Galois field
\[
 L_h=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2h},2^{1/h},3^{1/h},5^{1/h}).
\]
It contains all the roots of the three binomials, so is normal and separable.
The [fixed-field Chebotarev density input](../audits/literature/BV19b/Yin_U001.md#theorem-4b-implies-a) gives positive natural density among rational primes for its completely split primes.
Together with the ordinary prime number theorem this gives \(\gg_h x/\log x\) such primes in \([x,2x]\) for sufficiently large \(x\), by subtracting the two asymptotic counts.
Two consecutive members of this list differ by \(O_h(\log x)\), since all intervening gaps sum to at most \(x\).
Call these primes \(r<q\), omitting primes dividing \(30h\).
At each, reduction into its prime field supplies a primitive \(2h\)-th root of unity and \(h\)-th roots of \(2,3,5\).
Primitivity survives since \(X^{2h}-1\) has separable reduction.
Both primes are \(1\bmod 2h\) and each of the three bases has order dividing \((r-1)/h\) modulo \(r\) and \((q-1)/h\) modulo \(q\).
For each fixed \(h\), taking \(x\) sufficiently large gives \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) and \(r>4g\), since \(g=O_h(\log x)\).
Also \(\gcd(r,g)=\gcd(q,g)=1\).
No bound for gaps uniform in the field \(L_h\) is required.

<a id="three"></a>
## Matching degrees across the two different grids

Over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) for \(\ell=2,3\), the [degree-\(q-1\) grid](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#degrees) in \(\bar Q\) consists of the simple reductions of \(-\varepsilon\zeta_q^j\), \(1\le j<q\).
An orbit under Frobenius and inversion has size \(t_\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(\ell))\).
Because \((q-1)/h\) is even and divisible by this order, the number \(J_\ell=(q-1)/t_\ell\) of such orbits is divisible by \(h\).
Select \(2J_\ell/h\) of them.
Their product of linear factors is a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(d\), constant term one and coprime to the complementary part of \(\bar Q\).

In characteristic five the [whole-block identity](Yin_C_G0345_v215.md#five) gives
\[
                  \bar Q=(X^{2g}+1)\frac{X^r+\varepsilon}{X+\varepsilon}.
\]
The \(r-1\) simple roots in the second factor have orders \(r\) or \(2r\) and are disjoint from the roots of the first, whose orders divide \(4g\).
Their Frobenius and inversion orbits have size \(t_5=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_r(5))\), since the unit groups modulo \(r\) and \(2r\) are identified and the unit group modulo the odd prime \(r\) is cyclic.
Again \(J_5=(r-1)/t_5\) is divisible by \(h\).
Select \(2J_5/h\) orbits, with total degree \(2(r-1)/h\).
Adjoin the full factor \(X^{2b}+1\) from the first part.
It divides \(X^{2g}+1\) because \(h\) is odd, and is coprime to its complement there: the value of \((Z^h+1)/(Z+1)\) at \(Z=-1\) is \(h\ne0\bmod5\).
This takes every multiplicity in that selected factor even if \(5\mid b\).
It is reciprocal with constant term one and no root \(1,-1\).
The total degree is now \(2(r-1)/h+2g/h=d\).

The numerator two is the smallest positive choice when the same fraction \(k/h\) is selected on both primitive grids, with no exceptional roots at \(2,3\) and a reciprocal exceptional divisor at five.
Indeed put \(v=v_2(g)\ge1\).
Every root of \(X^{2g}+1\) in characteristic five has order whose dyadic part is \(2^{v+2}\), since its \(2g\)-th power has order two.
The subgroup generated by \(5,-1\) in the units modulo \(2^{v+2}\) has size \(2^{v+1}\).
Successive squaring gives \(v_2(5^{2^i}-1)=i+2\), and every power of five is \(1\bmod4\), excluding \(-1\).
Thus each orbit of exceptional roots under Frobenius and inversion has size divisible by \(2^{v+1}\), by restricting the action on powers of a root to the dyadic part of its order.
In a reciprocal divisor these roots have equal multiplicity on each orbit, so its degree is divisible by \(2^{v+1}\).
For \(k=1\) the required degree \(g/h\) has dyadic valuation \(v\), which excludes it.

At each prime the coprime monic factors lift uniquely to \(\mathbb Z_\ell[X]\) with their given reductions.
The [Hensel lifting and normalized reversal argument](Yin_C_G0345_v216.md#lifts) applies also at five.
Indeed each selected reduction has constant term one and avoids \(1,-1\), and \(Q\) is monic reciprocal with constant term one.
The normalized reversal must give the same lift by uniqueness, so the constant term of the lift is \(1\) or \(-1\).
At odd residue characteristic it reduces to one, and at two the alternative \(-1\) would give a root at one by reversal in characteristic zero.
Thus the lifted divisor and its complement are reciprocal with constant one.
Applying the Chinese remainder theorem to their coefficients gives the asserted joint factorizations at every precision.

<a id="global"></a>
## The measure condition and two further norms

If \(S\mid Q\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is monic of degree \(d\), it is integral.
By [prime-block allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) its irreducible factors have positive integer shares with sum \(s\le g\), and each of their degree-to-\(r\) ratios differs from its share divided by \(g\) by less than \(1/(3(2g+1)^2)\).
There are at most \(g\) such factors, including multiplicity since \(Q\) is squarefree in this range.
Hence
\[
  \left|\frac{s}{g}-\frac{2}{h}\right|
             \le \frac{g}{3(2g+1)^2}+\frac{2(g-1)}{hr}<\frac1g
\]
on enlarging \(C\), using \(d/r-2/h=2(g-1)/(hr)\).
Since \(2g/h\) is an integer this forces \(s=2g/h\).
Summing the [individual measure estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) gives (1).
Along \(h\to\infty\), \(2h\le g\) and the stated lower bound on \(r\) makes the error \(o(1/h)\).
All factors of \(S\) have positive measure, so any one of them also has measure tending to zero along such a sequence.

If a rational divisor matches the selected reductions, (2) of the [two-grid group theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v215.md#statement) would give absolute resultants \(6^d\) against \((X^q+\varepsilon)/(X+\varepsilon)\) and \(5^{d-2b}\) against \((X^r+\varepsilon)/(X+\varepsilon)\).
It would also satisfy
\[
         |\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-2)|=|\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-3)|=5^b. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, evaluating the parent at every root of \(X^g-j\) gives \(\varepsilon(1-2j)(1-3j)\) for \(j=2,3\).
Its two absolute resultants are \(15^g,40^g\), so only \(2,3,5\) can contribute to (2).
The selected roots at \(2,3\) have order divisible by \(q\), and raising them to \(g\) preserves that divisor of their orders, excluding \(j\bmod\ell\).
At five the selected roots from the \(r\)-grid likewise cannot reduce with \(X^g=2,3\).
The remaining reduction \(X^{2b}+1\) is the product of \(X^b-2\) and \(X^b+2\) in \(\mathbb F_5[X]\).
The two values \(2^h,(-2)^h\) are distinct and comprise \(2,3\bmod5\), so exactly \(b\) roots counted with multiplicity satisfy each condition \(X^g=j\bmod5\).
For a lift \(z\) of any of these roots, \(z^r+\varepsilon\) is a unit at five: the exceptional roots avoid the primitive \(r\)-grid and \(-\varepsilon\).
Writing \(Y=z^g\) in the identity
\[
             (z^r+\varepsilon)(Y-2)(Y-3)=-5\varepsilon(Y^2-1)
\]
gives \(v_5(Y-j)=1\), with \(v_5(5)=1\), since the other linear factor and \(Y^2-1\) are units.
Multiplication over all lifts gives (2).
These equalities restrict a global divisor with these reductions, and do not certify that its residue factors arise from a common integral factor.
