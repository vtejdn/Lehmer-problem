# A signed pole norm excludes one consecutive-coefficient family at every long exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two signs at short exponent one

Let \(q\ge3\) be an integer, \(p=q+1\), \(L=p+q\), \(M=pq\), and \(v>1\) an integer.
For \(\sigma=\pm1\) put
\[
 F_\sigma(X)=X^v(qX-p)+\sigma(pX-q).
\]
Then \(F_-(X^g)\) has no nontorsion algebraic-unit root for any \(g\ge1\).
Every positive-measure integral divisor therefore has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log 2\).

For \(F_+\), the existence of a nontorsion algebraic-unit root at any dilation requires \(v>L\).
The unique exterior root \(\alpha\) of the primitive polynomial must then itself be an algebraic unit with \(1<\alpha<p/q\).
Its minimal polynomial \(S\) is monic reciprocal of even degree \(D\), and \(p-q\alpha\) is a unit of norm one.
In the real trace field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), the number
\[
 \bigl(1+M(2-\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\bigr)^{-1}
\]
is a totally positive Pisot unit.
In \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) the element \((\alpha^v-1)/(\alpha-1)\) generates the ideal \((L)\).
If \(v\) is even, \(S(-1)=1\) and \(L\mid 2^D-1\).
These conditions leave the existence of unit roots for unbounded \(q\) in this sign open.

For the original four-term polynomial \(a(1+\epsilon X^{u+v})+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v)\) with \(|a|=q\), \(|b|=p\), put \(s=-\operatorname{sgn}(b/a)\).
At \(u=1\), the change \(X=sY\) and multiplication by a sign give \(F_\sigma(Y)\) with \(\sigma=-\epsilon s^{v+1}\).
A unit at dilation gives a unit root of the primitive polynomial by taking its \(g\)-th power.

<a id="norm"></a>
## The pole norm at any primitive direction

Take any coprime positive integers \(u<v\) and a nontorsion unit root \(\beta\) of the original primitive polynomial.
Write \(T=s\beta^u\) and \(Y=-\epsilon s\beta^v\).
The section equation gives
\[
 Y=\frac{pT-q}{p-qT},\qquad (p-qT)(Y+1)=T+1 .
\]
The denominator cannot vanish because \(p^2-q^2=L\ne0\).
The field \(\mathbb Q(T)\) contains both \(\beta^u\) and \(\beta^v\), hence equals \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) by Bézout's identity.
Suppose the minimal polynomial \(S_T\) of \(T\) is reciprocal and put \(D=\deg S_T\).
The nontorsion unit \(T\) then has even degree and \(S_T\) is monic with constant coefficient one.
Its signed norm at the pole satisfies
\[
 K=N_{\mathbb Q(T)/\mathbb Q}(p-qT)=q^D S_T(p/q),\qquad
 K\mid L^u,\qquad K\equiv1\pmod M .
\]
Indeed \(Y^u=\pm T^v\), so \(S_T\) divides an integer polynomial \(A(Z)=Z^v(p-qZ)^u\pm(pZ-q)^u\) of degree \(v+u\).
Evaluating the factorization at \(p/q\) and clearing denominators gives \(K\mid q^{v+u}A(p/q)=\pm q^v L^u\).
Monicity gives \(K\equiv p^D\equiv1\bmod q\), and the constant coefficient gives \(K\equiv q^D\equiv1\bmod p\).
Coprimality gives both assertions.

For each fixed \(u\le4\) and all sufficiently large \(q\), these conditions force
\[
 K\in\{L^{2j}:0\le j\le\lfloor u/2\rfloor\}
\]
for **every** primitive nontorsion unit root.
Indeed along any such sequence the [approaching-pole theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#pole) gives reciprocity of the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\) eventually.
An automorphism sending \(\beta\) to \(\beta^{-1}\) sends \(T\) to \(T^{-1}\), proving the assumed reciprocity.
For \(u=1\), \(M-1>L\) whenever \(q\ge3\), leaving only \(K=1\).
For \(u=2,3\), let \(K'=L^u/K\) and suppose \(K\ne1\).
Then \(|K|\ge M-1\) and \(|K'|\le L^u/(M-1)=O(L^{u-2})\).
Since \(L^2=4M+1\), \(K'\) is congruent modulo \(M\) to \(1\) or \(L\), according to the parity of \(u\).
The size bound forces \(K'=1\) or \(L\), respectively, for large \(q\).
For \(u=4\), the cases \(K=1\) and \(K'=1\) give the two endpoints.
Otherwise both absolute values are at least \(M-1\) and at most \(L^4/(M-1)=O(M)\).
Writing \(K=1+hM\) and \(K'=1+kM\) gives bounded integers \(h,k\), with
\[
 h+k+hkM=8+16M .
\]
Consequently \(hk=16\) and \(h+k=8\) for large \(M\), giving \(h=k=4\) and \(K=L^2\).

The identity \((p-qT)(Y+1)=T+1\) also has a consequence without any reciprocity assumption.
Every prime ideal dividing \(Y+1\) divides \(\beta^{2u}-1\).
If \(Y=\beta^v\), the element \(\beta^{2v}-1\) has no primitive prime ideal divisor, meaning one absent from all \(\beta^j-1\) with \(1\le j<2v\).
Indeed a primitive divisor would have residue order \(2v\), would divide \(\beta^v+1\), and would have order dividing \(2u\).
If \(Y=-\beta^v\) and \(v>2\), the same argument rules out a primitive prime ideal divisor of \(\beta^v-1\), since \(v\nmid 2u\).

<a id="sign"></a>
## Using the sign of the norm at short exponent one

Both \(F_\sigma\) have at most one exterior root counted with multiplicity, by the [circle winding count](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#count).
Reciprocity up to sign gives the same bound inside the circle.
Any irreducible nontorsion unit factor has an exterior conjugate by Kronecker's theorem, and its unit norm forces an interior conjugate.
The unique exterior root \(\alpha\) is real by complex conjugation, and the unique interior root is its reciprocal.
Thus the factor contains both and its monic minimal polynomial \(S\) is reciprocal of even degree.
Every remaining conjugate lies on the circle in a nonreal conjugate pair.
For \(r>1\), \(r^v(qr+p)>pr+q\) since \(r(qr+p)-(pr+q)=q(r^2-1)>0\).
Hence neither sign has a negative exterior root and \(\alpha>1\).

In the normalized variable the pole norm is \(q^D S(p/q)=1\) by the \(u=1\) argument above.
For \(F_-\), \(F_-(p/q)=-L/q<0\) and \(F_-(x)\to+\infty\) as \(x\to+\infty\).
The unique exterior root therefore exceeds \(p/q\).
The factor \(p/q-\alpha\) in \(S(p/q)\) is negative, while \(p/q-\alpha^{-1}>0\) and the nonreal pairs contribute positive squared moduli.
This contradicts the positive pole norm.
The claim about integral divisors now follows from the [nonunit factor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree).

For \(F_+\) one has
\[
 F_+(X)/(X-1)=q(X^v+1)-\sum_{j=1}^{v-1}X^j .
\]
For \(x>1\), strict convexity in the exponent bounds the sum by \((v-1)(1+x^v)/2\) strictly.
Thus there is no positive exterior root when \(v\le L\).
When \(v>L\) the quotient is negative at \(1\) and positive at \(p/q\), giving its unique exterior root in the asserted interval.
For a unit root, \(\eta=p-q\alpha\) has norm one and is invertible already in \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), by the monic equation of \(\eta\) with unit constant coefficient.
The equation for \(F_+\) yields
\[
 \eta(\alpha^v-1)=L(\alpha-1),\qquad \eta(\alpha^v+1)=\alpha+1 .
\]
The first identity gives the ideal equality.
The trace-field element
\[
 \eta(p-q\alpha^{-1})=1+M(2-\alpha-\alpha^{-1})
\]
is a unit and belongs to \((0,1)\) at the exterior real embedding because \(1<\alpha<p/q\).
At every other embedding of the trace field, the image of the trace is \(2\cos\theta<2\), so the image of this unit is greater than one.
Taking the inverse proves the Pisot assertion.

When \(v\) is even, evaluation of \(F_+/(X-1)\) at \(-1\) gives \(L\), so \(S(-1)\mid L\).
Reducing the signed norm \(1=q^D S(p/q)\) modulo \(L\) gives \(q^DS(-1)\equiv1\bmod L\), since \(p\equiv-q\bmod L\).
The even-degree reciprocal polynomial with one positive reciprocal pair and the other roots in nonreal pairs has \(S(-1)>0\).
Thus \(S(-1)=1\), and \(2q\equiv-1\bmod L\) gives the claimed congruence.
