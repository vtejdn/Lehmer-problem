# Denominators and independent ideals of dyadic block evaluations

<a id="denominator"></a>
## The reduced denominator of one evaluation

Use the oriented polynomial \(f\) and field \(K\) of the [reversed exterior block](Yin_C_G0345_v809.md#statement), and write \(g=\tau f\), \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]=2u\), \(R=e^{m/2}\).
For an integer \(q>R\), let \(D(q)\) be the integral denominator ideal of \((f(q)/g(q))\) in lowest terms.
Then
\[
 N D(q)\ \ge\ \left(\frac{q-R}{2b}\right)^{nb}e^{-(4b-2)m}.
\]
In particular the reduced denominator grows exponentially in \(nb\) once \(q-R\ge4b\) and \(m\) tends to zero.
All its primes lie over split primes of the block field by inversion under \(\tau\).

Indeed \(ND(q)=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}g(q)|/N((f(q))+(g(q)))\), and every conjugate root has modulus at most \(R\), giving numerator at least \((q-R)^{nb}\).
The adjugate of the Sylvester matrix puts the nonzero resultant \(\operatorname{Res}(f,g)\) in the ideal generated by the evaluations, since the two orientations have disjoint roots.
Put \(H=fg\), whose discriminant equals \(\operatorname{disc}(f)\operatorname{disc}(g)\operatorname{Res}(f,g)^2\).
The first two factors have nonzero integral absolute norms.
At each embedding \(\sigma\), Hadamard's inequality on the Vandermonde matrix bounds \(|\operatorname{disc}(\sigma H)|\) by \((2b)^{2b}M(\sigma H)^{4b-2}\), where \(M\) is multiplicative Mahler measure.
The [norm polynomial of each orientation](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement) is the signed-root minimal polynomial, so \(\prod_\sigma M(\sigma H)=e^{2m}\).
Taking norms and square roots gives \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\operatorname{Res}(f,g)|\le (2b)^{bn}e^{(4b-2)m}\), as required.

## Full rank on sufficiently late dyadic intervals

Suppose \(m<\log 2\), and take positive integers \(T,s\) with
\[
 T-R>\bigl(R(2^{s-1}+1)\bigr)^{b(2s-1)}.
\]
The principal fractional ideals \((f(2^jT)/g(2^jT))\), \(0\le j<s\), are multiplicatively independent.
Consequently the union of their supports meets at least \(s\) distinct split primes of the block field, since the valuations in each conjugate pair are opposites.
In particular each fixed dyadic ray contains arbitrarily long intervals of full rank, using only root separation and integrality after the oriented factorization has been given.
For a ray starting at an integer \(Q\ge2\), one may take the interval starting at \(T=2^{4bs^2}Q\), since \(R<\sqrt2\) and \(R(2^{s-1}+1)<2^{s+1}\).
A search for an ideal collision can therefore restrict to starting arguments below the displayed threshold for its chosen interval length.

Write \(A_{j,h}=(h(2^jT))\) for \(h=f,g\), and set \(C=R(2^{s-1}+1)\).
For any two distinct entries \((i,h),(j,k)\) ordered by \(i\le j\), the norm of their ideal sum is at most \(C^{nb^2}\).
Indeed the ideal sum contains \(\operatorname{Res}_X(h(X),k(2^{j-i}X))\) by evaluation at \(X=2^iT\).
For \(i=j\) this is nonzero by disjointness, and for \(i<j\) it is nonzero because the root moduli are in \([R^{-1},R]\) with \(2/R>R\).
At each embedding it is a product of \(b^2\) differences \(2^{j-i}\alpha-\beta\), each of modulus at most \(C\).

In a nontrivial integral relation among the fractional ideals, choose an index \(j\) of largest absolute exponent.
At every prime of \(K\), take the valuation of the relation and isolate the term from \(A=A_{j,f}\).
Dividing by that largest absolute exponent gives \(v(A)\le\sum_{B\ne A}v(B)\), where the sum runs over the other \(2s-1\) integral entries.
This implies \(v(A)\le\sum_{B\ne A}\min(v(A),v(B))\), since either one term already reaches \(v(A)\) or truncation changes none of them.
Taking ideal norms therefore gives \((T-R)^{nb}\le NA\le\prod_{B\ne A}N(A+B)\le C^{nb^2(2s-1)}\), contradicting the hypothesis.
