# A uniform obstruction to angular approximation by signed cyclotomic counts

<a id="statement"></a>
## The discrepancy stays positive as the measure shrinks

Let \(P\) be a nonconstant monic reciprocal integer polynomial with no unit-circle roots, write \(m=m(P)\), and let \(C\) be the centered cumulative discrepancy between its exterior phases and any signed cyclotomic root counts as in the [angular transfer](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#statement).
Let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(x^3-x-1\).
Then
\[
                      m+\pi\|C\|_{L^2(\mathbb T)}\ \ge\ \log\theta .
\]
Thus the vanishing discrepancy hypothesis in that transfer cannot hold on a sequence with \(m\to0\), even when all fixed-frequency parity tests pass.
The same estimate with \(dm\) on the left applies when counting all exterior phases with an integer multiplicity \(d\ge1\).
In particular any bounded positive multiplicity has the same obstruction.

<a id="linear"></a>
## Separation of two logarithmic moduli

For nonzero integer polynomials \(U,V\) with nonzero constant terms write \(V^*(X)=X^{\deg V}V(1/X)\).
If \(U\ne\pm V^*\), then
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|U|,|V|)\,d\mu
   =\tfrac12\big(m(U)+m(V)+\|\log|U|-\log|V|\|_1\big)\ \ge\ \log\theta .
\]
Here \(\mu\) is normalized Haar measure.
Indeed the integral on the left is \(m(U(X)+Y V(X))\) by Jensen in \(Y\).
For \(N\) exceeding twice the sum of the two degrees plus two, the low block of \(U+X^N V\) is \(U\) and the low block of its reciprocal is \(V^*\).
Comparing these disjoint blocks excludes reciprocity up to sign by the hypothesis.
If the leading coefficient has absolute value at least two, the measure is already at least \(\log 2\).
Otherwise sign normalization makes the polynomial monic, and some irreducible factor must be nonreciprocal up to sign because a product of reciprocal factors up to sign remains so.
That factor has log measure at least \(\log\theta\) by [Smyth's nonreciprocal theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-u001), and every other integral factor has nonnegative measure.
The [scalar specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Luc22/Yin_U002.md#luc22-abelian-matrix-approximation) gives the bound on passing to \(N\to\infty\).
The equality with the distance between the logarithms follows pointwise away from the finitely many zeros by taking their maximum.

<a id="phases"></a>
## Comparing the full polynomial to its exterior phases

Take \(A,B\) to be the numerator and denominator cyclotomic products, and apply the separation estimate with \(U=P B^2,V=A^2\).
Both are reciprocal up to sign, and a root of \(P\) excludes \(U=\pm V^*\).
Writing \(u=|P|\), \(s=|A|^2/(u|B|^2)\), and \(x=\log|A/(BH)|^2\) for the polynomial \(H\) with the exterior phase multiset as roots, we obtain
\[
             \log\theta\ \le\ m+\int\log^+s\,d\mu.
\]
The [radial pairing and Fourier identities](Yin_C_G0345_v611.md#fourier) give \(u\ge|H|^2\), \(\int x\,d\mu=0\), and \(\|x\|_2=2\pi\|C\|_2\).
Consequently \(\log^+s\le x^+\) and \(\int x^+\,d\mu=\|x\|_1/2\le\pi\|C\|_2\).
This proves the bound, including for \(P^d\) since irreducibility was never required.

<a id="loss"></a>
## Moderate ratios in the quadratic loss

When \(P\) is irreducible and \(A,B\) are arbitrary nonzero integer polynomials, the integral inequality becomes \(\log\theta\le m+2m(B)+\int\log^+s\,d\mu\).
Remove initial monomials and now use \(U=PBB^*,V=AA^*\) in the linear estimate.
They are reciprocal and the multiplicity of \(P\) in the first is odd and in the second even.
Their boundary moduli are \(u|B|^2\) and \(|A|^2\), respectively.
If \(\ell=\int D(s)\,d\mu\) is the quadratic transfer loss, then \((\log s-\log4)^+\le D(s)\).
It follows that
\[
 \int \min(\log^+s,\log4)\,d\mu\ \ge\ \log\theta-m-2m(B)-\ell.
\]
For \(0\le t<\log4\) this bounds \(\mu\{s>e^t\}\) below by \((\log\theta-m-2m(B)-\ell-t)/(\log4-t)\) whenever the numerator is positive.
The angular lower bound alone does not give a lower bound on the excess above the level four.
