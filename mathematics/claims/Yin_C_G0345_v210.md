# A bounded denominator for the measures of complex radicals

<a id="statement"></a>
## Every factor under a common dilation

For each integer \(L\ge2\) there is an integer \(R_L\ge1\), with \(\log(2R_L)\ll L^3\log(2L)\) and an absolute implied constant, having the following property.
Let \(\beta\ne0\) be a nontorsion root of a nonzero polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) whose coefficient length \(\sum_j|[X^j]P|\) is at most \(L\).
For any \(N\ge1\) and any complex root \(\alpha\) of \(X^N-\beta\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):K]\).
Then
\[
           \frac{N}{\gcd(N,e)}\mid R_L,\qquad
           \frac{m(\alpha)}{m(\beta)}=\frac eN
                 \in\frac{1}{\gcd(N,R_L)}\mathbb Z_{>0}.               \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m(z)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of the primitive integer minimal polynomial of \(z\).
In particular \(m(\beta)/R_L\le m(\alpha)\le m(\beta)\).
If \(S\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\), \(S(X^N)\) has at most \(\gcd(N,R_L)\) irreducible factors over \(\mathbb Q\), each simple, and is irreducible whenever \(\gcd(N,R_L)=1\).
Thus (1) compares individual factor measures even when \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) has arbitrary degree over \(K\) or \(K\) has no real embedding.

For a specific choice of \(R_L\), use the constants in the [bound for prime powers in the seed field](Yin_C_G0345_v208.md#internal), with the same absolute \(0<c\le1\) from the [width estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement).
Put
\[
 H=(L-1)\log L,\quad A=\max(1,HL^2/c),\quad Q=\max(A,2L,2H,2),
 \qquad
 R_L=2\prod_{\substack{q\le Q\\q\ {\rm prime}}}
    q^{\lfloor\log(AL\exp(H/q))/\log q\rfloor}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For a unit \(\beta\), every odd prime power \(q^s\), \(s\ge1\), dividing \(N/\gcd(N,e)\) satisfies \(q\le Q\) and \(q^s\le AL\exp(H/q)\).
For \(q=2\) the corresponding bound is \(2^s\le2AL\exp(H/2)\).
For a nonunit \(\beta\) the entire denominator is at most \(\log L/\log2\).

<a id="affine"></a>
## A lower bound for the relative degree of any chosen radical

Fix \(q^a\Vert N\) and let \(\delta=\alpha^{N/q^a}\), so \(\delta^{q^a}=\beta\).
Set \(F=K(\zeta_q)\) for odd \(q\) and \(F=K(i)\) for \(q=2\).
Let \(j\) be the largest \(0\le j\le a\) for which \(\beta\) has some \(q^j\)-th root in \(F\).
We claim that \(q^{a-j}\mid e\).
If \(j=a\) this is immediate.

Otherwise fix a primitive \(q^a\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\).
The splitting field \(E=F(\delta,\zeta)\) of \(X^{q^a}-\beta\) over \(F\) is Galois.
On its roots \(\zeta^x\delta\), every automorphism \(\sigma\) acts on \(x\bmod q^a\) as \(x\mapsto t x+b\), with \(\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^t\) and \(\sigma(\delta)=\zeta^b\delta\).
This action is faithful, since fixing all roots fixes their ratios and \(\delta\).
The multipliers have \(t\equiv1\bmod q\) for odd \(q\) and admit a representative \(t\equiv1\bmod4\) for \(q=2\), including \(a=1\).
The resulting group of possible multipliers has \(q\)-power order, as does the translation group.
Hence \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\) is a \(q\)-group, and \([F(\delta):F]\), the size of the orbit of \(\delta\), is a power of \(q\).

The element \(\delta^{q^{a-j-1}}\) is a \(q^{j+1}\)-th root of \(\beta\), so it lies outside \(F\) by maximality of \(j\).
An automorphism moving it must have \(b\not\equiv0\bmod q^{j+1}\).
Choose such an automorphism and an integer representative \(b\) with \(v_q(b)\le j\).
After \(q^m\) iterations, its action on \(x=0\) gives \(b(1+t+\cdots+t^{q^m-1})\bmod q^a\).
The geometric sum has \(q\)-adic valuation \(m\).
Indeed, it is the product, for \(0\le k<m\), of \(1+u+\cdots+u^{q-1}\) with \(u=t^{q^k}\).
For odd \(q\) and \(u=1+qv\), binomial expansion modulo \(q^2\) makes this factor congruent to \(q+qv\cdot q(q-1)/2\equiv q\bmod q^2\).
For \(q=2\) it is \(1+u\equiv2\bmod4\).
Consequently the cycle of \(\delta\) under this automorphism has length \(q^{a-v_q(b)}\), since its length is a power of \(q\).
The full orbit has \(q\)-power size at least this large, proving \(q^{a-j}\mid[F(\delta):F]\).

The field \(F(\delta)\) is contained in \(F(\alpha)\).
Moreover \(F/K\) is Galois, so restriction on \(F\) embeds \(\operatorname{Gal}(F(\alpha)/K(\alpha))\) into \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)\).
Thus \([F(\alpha):K(\alpha)]\) divides \([F:K]\), and the tower formula shows that \([F(\alpha):F]\) divides \(e\).
This proves the claim and gives
\[
                 s=v_q\!\left(N/\gcd(N,e)\right)
                         =\max(0,a-v_q(e))\le j .                       \tag{3}
\]

<a id="descent"></a>
## Descending powers from the cyclotomic extension

Suppose first that \(\beta\) is a unit and \(s>0\).
By (3) choose \(y\in F\) with \(y^{q^j}=\beta\), \(j\ge s\).
For \(f=[F:K]\) the norm \(w=\operatorname{Norm}_{F/K}y\) satisfies \(w^{q^j}=\beta^f\).
For odd \(q\) we have \(f\mid q-1\).
Choosing integers \(u,v\) with \(uf+vq^j=1\) gives \((w^u\beta^v)^{q^j}=\beta\) in \(K\).
The same conclusion is immediate for \(q=2\) when \(F=K\).
In these cases the [in-field prime-power bound](Yin_C_G0345_v208.md#internal) gives \(q\le Q\) and \(q^s\le q^j\le AL\exp(H/q)\).
The hypothesis on the minimum coefficient length holds because \(P(\beta)=0\).

If \(q=2\) and \(f=2\), then \(w^{2^j}=\beta^2\), so \(w^{2^{j-1}}\) equals \(\beta\) or \(-\beta\).
For \(j\ge2\) the same bound applies to this sign and exponent \(j-1\), since substitution \(X\mapsto-X\) preserves the length of a relation.
Thus \(2^s\le2^j\le2AL\exp(H/2)\), which also holds for \(j=1\).
Equivalently, the exponent of two needed in \(R_L\) exceeds the in-field bound in (2) by at most one.

Now suppose that \(\beta\) is a nonunit.
There is a finite prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) with \(v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)\ne0\), where \(v_{\mathfrak p}\) takes integer values and assigns value one to a uniformizer.
With \(z=\operatorname{Norm}_{K(\alpha)/K}\alpha\), the identity \(z^N=\beta^e\) gives \(N v_{\mathfrak p}(z)=e v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)\).
Therefore \(N/\gcd(N,e)\) divides \(|v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)|\).
In the [height formula over \(K\)](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization), this place contributes \(|v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta)|\log\operatorname{Norm}(\mathfrak p)\) to the total measure of \(\beta\) or \(\beta^{-1}\), according to the sign.
Both have measure \(m(\beta)\), and \(m(\beta)\le m(P)\le\log L\) by integral factorization and Jensen's formula.
This proves the nonunit bound.
Every positive integer at most \(\log L/\log2\le L\) divides the product in (2), because its prime powers are at most \(L\).

Together these bounds prove the divisibility in (1).
The [sum over primes estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v208.md#exponents) bounds \(\log R_L\) by \(\log2+\pi(Q)\log(AL)+H\sum_{2\le t\le Q}1/t\ll L^3\log(2L)\).
By the [radical measure identity](Yin_C_G0206_v1.md#statement), \(m(\alpha)/m(\beta)=e/N\), whose reduced denominator divides both \(N\) and \(R_L\).
Also \(e\le N\), proving the stated two-sided comparison.
For each irreducible factor of \(S(X^N)\) some conjugate root lies above \(\beta\), since the embeddings of its \(N\)-th power extend to the root field.
All roots are simple because \(S\) is separable and has nonzero constant term.
The degrees of the factors are \((\deg S)e\), so the relative degrees sum to \(N\).
Their positive fractions \(e/N\) sum to one and each is at least \(1/\gcd(N,R_L)\), giving the asserted count.

<a id="primitive"></a>
## Reduction to polynomials with primitive support

Let a family of nonzero integral polynomials of coefficient length at most \(L\) be given.
For each polynomial having at least two terms, divide out the largest common power of \(X\), let \(N\) be the greatest common divisor of its positive remaining support exponents, and write the result as \(P_0(X^N)\).
The positive support exponents of \(P_0\) have greatest common divisor one, and its coefficient length is unchanged.
For every nontorsion nonzero root \(\alpha\) of the original polynomial, its power \(\beta=\alpha^N\) is a nontorsion root of \(P_0\).
Every such \(\beta\) occurs, and each lift satisfies (1).
Consequently a positive uniform lower bound for the individual nontorsion factor measures of the reduced family is equivalent to such a bound for the original family.
A sequence of measures tending to zero among the original factors yields such a sequence among the reduced factors because \(m(\beta)\le R_Lm(\alpha)\).
Conversely one can take any lift of each reduced seed since \(m(\alpha)\le m(\beta)\).
Monomials contribute no nonzero roots.

In particular, fix the block primes in the [prime-binomial family](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement), now allowing arbitrary \(g=\gcd_i u_i\) and either sign.
For a delay \(n\) put \(h=\gcd(n,g)\).
The parent is exactly \(P_0(X^h)\), where \(P_0\) has delay \(n/h\), exponents \(u_i/h\), and block gcd \(g/h\) coprime to \(n/h\).
The parent and \(P_0\) have coefficient length at most \(2\prod_i(1+p_i)\).
If \(D=\sum_i u_i\), the range \(n\ge C(D+1)^9\) implies \(n/h\ge C(D/h+1)^9\), because \(D/h\ge1\) gives \(D+1\ge\sqrt h(D/h+1)\).
Thus the positive-gap question for all parents in this range is equivalent to the question for parents with delay coprime to the block gcd.
The latter gcd can still grow, and (1) allows the seed measure itself to tend to zero.
