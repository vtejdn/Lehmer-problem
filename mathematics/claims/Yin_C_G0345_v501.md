# Resultant growth for every two-prime row of vanishing normalized cost

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound in terms of the actual coefficient cost

Take the [computable width](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width) \(b=b(p)\) for \(Q=(1091/1000)^{1/3}\).
Let \(p<q\le 2p\) be primes, \(\Sigma=p+q\), and let \(U,V\) be any pair passing the [two-prime test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width with \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)<Q^\Sigma\).
Write \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) for the ordinary integer resultant and \(\eta=(\log H)/\Sigma\).
For every sufficiently large \(p\), every such row with \(0<\eta<\min(\log Q,2/81)\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{\log|R|}{\log H}\ \ge
 \left(1-\frac1b\right)\frac{4\sqrt2}{3}
                 \exp\!\left(\left(\frac{2}{3\eta}\right)^{1/3}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
Thus on every sequence of passing rows at this width with \(p\to\infty\) and \(\eta\to0\), both \(\log|R|/\log H\) and \(\log|R|/\Sigma\) tend to infinity.
Only two exponent primes occur in each row.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree times measure at each contact

Choose one qualifying reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P_i\) at each prime, allowing \(P_1=P_2\), and put \(e_i=\deg P_i\), \(h_i=\log M(P_i)>0\).
The [contact inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) gives \(p h_1+q h_2\le\eta\Sigma\).
Since \(q\le2p\), both \(h_i\) are at most \(3\eta\).
Both degrees are at least two because a rational algebraic unit is \(\pm1\).
[Voutier’s Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h_i>2/(\log(3 e_i))^3\), hence \(3e_i>\exp((2/h_i)^{1/3})\).
For \(t>0\) the derivative of \(t\exp((2/t)^{1/3})\) has the sign of \(1-(2/t)^{1/3}/3\).
The function is decreasing on \((0,2/27)\), which contains \((0,3\eta]\).
Consequently each seed satisfies
\[
          e_i h_i> \eta\exp\!\left(\left(\frac{2}{3\eta}\right)^{1/3}\right).       \tag{2}
\]

<a id="resultant"></a>
## The mixed and repeated seed inequalities

The [calibrated divisor](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#statement) gives \(\log|R|\ge(1-1/b)\Gamma\) for every qualifying choice.
For distinct seeds its [mixed expression](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#divisors) satisfies
\[
 \Gamma=4F\ge4\max(e_2 p h_1,e_1 q h_2)
                \ge4\sqrt{pq}\sqrt{e_1h_1 e_2h_2}.
\]
Writing \(t=q/p\), the function \(\sqrt t/(1+t)\) decreases on \([1,2]\), so \(\sqrt{pq}/\Sigma\ge\sqrt2/3\).
Together with (2) this proves (1).
For a repeated seed of degree \(e\) and measure \(h\) in logarithmic form, the [same-seed expression](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#divisors) is \(\Gamma=4F-2(q-p)h\) with \(F\ge e q h\).
As \(4e q-2(q-p)-2e\Sigma=2(e-1)(q-p)\ge0\), one has \(\Gamma/\Sigma\ge2eh\).
Applying (2) again proves (1) since \(2>4\sqrt2/3\).
For the second divergence in the statement multiply (1) by \(\eta\) and use \(\eta\exp((2/(3\eta))^{1/3})\to\infty\) along with \(b(p)\to\infty\).

<a id="gap"></a>
## Quantifiers when the unrestricted infimum vanishes

Let \(\lambda\) be the unrestricted infimum of the positive logarithmic Mahler measures.
Then \(\lambda=0\) if and only if there exists a sequence of passing rows at this width with \(p\to\infty\) and \(\eta\to0\).
Indeed any such sequence gives \(\lambda\le\eta\) pointwise by the contact inequality.
Conversely, suppose \(\lambda=0\) and prescribe any sequence of positive numbers \(\tau_j\downarrow0\), for example \(\tau_j=1/j\).
For each \(j\) choose a fixed positive \(t_j<\min(\tau_j,\log Q,2/81)\).
The [bounded number of rejected partners](Yin_C_G0345_v491.md#threshold) applied at threshold \(\exp(t_j)\) gives constants \(p_j,K_j\) such that, at every prime \(p\ge p_j\), all but at most \(K_j\) primes \(q\in(p,2p]\) admit a row of this width with \(\eta<t_j\).
Here \(1<\exp(t_j)< Q<6/5\) and \(b(p)\to\infty\), as required by that result.
The [prime count at modulus one](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) lets us enlarge \(p_j\) so that each such interval has more than \(K_j\) partners.
Selecting one partner and row at each of an increasing sequence of primes \(p\ge p_j\) realizes the prescribed upper rates.
Independently of that selection, (1) applies to every passing row at all sufficiently large primes whenever its own \(\eta\) lies in the stated range.
The constants and starting primes for existence may depend on \(j\).
The divergences constrain possible small-cost rows while leaving the existence of such sequences unresolved.
