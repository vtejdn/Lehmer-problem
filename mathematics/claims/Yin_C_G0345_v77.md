# Degrees of varying rational functions between numbers of bounded Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction without a signature or integrality hypothesis

Let \(x\ne0\) be an algebraic number of degree \(d\) which is not a root of unity.
Let \(R\in\mathbb Q(X)\) be nonconstant of degree \(k\), defined at \(x\), with \(y=R(x)\ne0\).
Suppose \(R(X)\) is not identically \(X^j\) or \(-X^j\) for any integer \(j\).
For a nonzero algebraic number \(z\), write \(L_z=\log M(z)=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]h(z)\), using the primitive minimal polynomial and its leading coefficient.
Put \(e=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q(y)]\).
There are absolute constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) with the following alternatives:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
   e L_x L_y \ \ge\ c_1 d/(\log(3d))^{212},
       & x,y\text{ multiplicatively independent},\\[2mm]
   kL_x+e L_y \ \ge\ c_2 d/(\log(3d))^{48},
       & x,y\text{ multiplicatively dependent}.
 \end{array}
\]
Here \(e\le k\) in both cases, and the second case includes torsion \(y\).
For each fixed \(H>0\), \(L_x,L_y\le H\) therefore implies \(k\ge c(H)d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
In particular if \(d\to\infty\), \(L_x\) stays bounded and \(k=o(d/(\log(3d))^{212})\), then \(L_y\to\infty\).
The rational function and all of its coefficients may vary with the input.
No bounded exterior count, special Galois action or higher-rank independence among the conjugates of \(x\) is assumed.

<a id="independent"></a>
## Independent points in one field

Write \(R=A/B\) in lowest terms with \(\max(\deg A,\deg B)=k\).
Since \(R\) is nonconstant, \(A(X)-yB(X)\) is a nonzero polynomial over \(\mathbb Q(y)\) of degree at most \(k\), giving \(e\le k\).
In the independent case the pair \((x,y)\) has joint field \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The [rank-two product inequality of Amoroso and David](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) applied with this joint degree gives
\[
     \frac{L_x}{d}\frac{eL_y}{d}
        \ge \frac{c_1}{d(\log(3d))^{212}} .
\]
This proves the first alternative.
It also bounds the individual degree of any such independent output by \(c_1^{-1}L_xL_y(\log(3d))^{212}\), regardless of the chosen rational expression.

<a id="dependent"></a>
## Clearing a relation over the field of its torsion factors

Suppose the coordinates are multiplicatively dependent.
The subgroup of \(\mathbb Q(x)^\times\) that they generate is finitely generated of rank one, since \(x\) is nontorsion.
Choose a lift \(\rho\) of a generator of its free quotient, with the sign chosen so that
\[
              x=\zeta\rho^u,\qquad y=\eta\rho^v,
                    \qquad u>0,\quad v\in\mathbb Z ,
\]
where \(\zeta,\eta\) are roots of unity in the subgroup.
Put \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta,\eta)\), an abelian extension of the rationals.
Both the torsion factors and \(\rho\) lie in \(\mathbb Q(x)\), and \(x\in F(\rho)\), so \(F(\rho)=\mathbb Q(x)\).
Write \(D=[F(\rho):F]\).

At \(X=\rho\) one has \(R(\zeta X^u)=\eta X^v\).
This is not an identity of rational functions: an identity would force every zero and pole of \(R\) to belong to \(\{0,\infty\}\), since \(X\mapsto\zeta X^u\) maps the nonzero algebraic numbers onto themselves.
Thus \(R=cX^j\) for rational \(c\ne0\), and matching Laurent monomials would force \(c=\eta\zeta^{-j}\) to be a rational root of unity, i.e. \(c=\pm1\).
After clearing the denominator and, when needed, a negative power of \(X\), we therefore get a nonzero polynomial over \(F\) of degree at most \(ku+|v|\) vanishing at \(\rho\).
In particular \(D\le ku+|v|\), also when \(v=0\).

Use the [rank-one case of Delsinne's relative height theorem](../audits/literature/Del09/Yin_U001.md#del09-u001) for the nontorsion \(\rho\).
Its relative degree over \(\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\) is at most \(D\), since \(F\) is abelian.
The logarithmic exponent at rank one is \(3(2(1+1)^2(1+1)!)=48\).
Hence \(D h(\rho)\ge c_2/(\log(3d))^{48}\).
Since \(u h(\rho)=h(x)\) and \(|v|h(\rho)=h(y)\), the polynomial degree bound yields the second alternative.
This argument retains the torsion field inside the relative degree, even if many conjugates of the input differ by roots of unity.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequences for measure descent

In the first case \(kL_xL_y\) bounds the left side from above, and in the second case \(k(L_x+L_y)\) does.
This gives the uniform degree bound at fixed \(H\).
If an infinite subsequence had \(L_y\) bounded while \(L_x\) stayed bounded and \(k=o(d/(\log(3d))^{212})\), the same degree bound would be contradicted on that subsequence.
This proves the divergence statement.
The [fixed rational-function height floor](Yin_C_G0852_v1.md#statement) concerns one fixed function; here the functions, their coefficients and their degrees may all change, and it is the total output measure that must diverge in the indicated range.

Degree \(d\) itself always allows a rational function different from all signed powers to return the original value when \(d\ge2\), for example \(X+P_x(X)\) with \(P_x\) the primitive minimal polynomial, unless that particular sum is a signed power.
Adding \(2P_x(X)\) in place of \(P_x(X)\) works in that exception.
Thus an unrestricted search using low-degree rational substitutions must account for the degree of the expression even when the intended output already lies in the input field.
