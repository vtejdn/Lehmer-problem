# Cyclotomic perturbations by a translated fixed polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two fixed factors and every shift position

Fix nonzero \(T,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
For \(n\ge2\) and integers \(k\ge0\), put \(P=T\Phi_n+X^kB\) and omit identically zero inputs.
Then \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) tends to infinity as the number \(\omega(n)\) of distinct prime divisors tends to infinity, uniformly in \(k\).
Over all \(n,k\), every positive \(m(P)\) is bounded below by a constant depending only on \(T,B\).
The factors may have common roots and may have arbitrary zeros on the unit circle.
Changing the sign of \(B\) covers subtraction.

<a id="local"></a>
## Growth against a weight with finitely many Fourier coefficients

Let \(W\) be a nonzero nonnegative trigonometric polynomial on the circle, and integrate against Haar probability \(d\nu\).
Write \(L_n=\log|\Phi_n|\), \(L_n^+=\max(L_n,0)\).
We first show
\[
                          \int W L_n^+\,d\nu\longrightarrow\infty
                             \qquad(\omega(n)\longrightarrow\infty).       \tag{1}
\]
Put \(q=\operatorname{rad}(n),\ N=n/q,\ r=\omega(n),\ A=\varphi(q)/q\).
Since \(L_n(\theta)=L_q(N\theta)\), its nonzero Fourier coefficients are supported at multiples of \(N\) and satisfy \(\widehat L_n(Nh)=-c_q(h)/(2|h|)\) for \(h\ne0\).
Here \(c_q(h)\) is the [primitive Ramanujan sum](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#fourier), with \(|c_q(h)|\le(q,h)\).
In particular every Fourier coefficient is bounded by \(1/2\), including the zero mean.

For large \(r\) take \(s=\lfloor(r/200)^{1/3}\rfloor\) and retain the primes dividing \(q\) whose ranks in increasing order are strictly above \(\lfloor r/4\rfloor\) and at most \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\).
These include at least \(r/5\) primes, each at least \(r/4\), and leave at least \(r/2\) larger primes unused.
From this range select \(s\) primes by the [separation or short interval argument](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#selection).
Indeed grouping successively into intervals \([p,4p)\) starting at the least remaining prime either gives \(s\) separated first primes or places more than \(r/(5s)\) in one interval.
Its subdivision into \(12s\) equal parts then places at least \(s\) primes within relative width \(1/(4s)\).
For an odd nonempty subset \(J\) of selected primes and any signs, write \(h_J=\sum_{p\in J}\pm q/p\).
The separation or the odd cardinality in the short interval gives
\[
                           |h_J|\ge q/(2p_*),\qquad p_*=\min J .
\]

Use the [bounded test](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#test)
\[
                G=\operatorname{Im}\prod_p(1+i s^{-1/2}\cos(Nq\theta/p)).
\]
Its supremum is at most \(e^{1/2}\), and \(\int L_n G\,d\nu\gg A\sqrt{s}\) for large \(r\).
Indeed the singleton contributions sum to at least \(A\sqrt{s}/2\) and the mixed terms cost \(O(A s^{3/2}/r^2)\) by the same coefficient formula, since the selected primes are at least \(r/4\).

For a fixed nonzero Fourier frequency \(l\) of \(W\), the integral of \(e^{il\theta}L_n G\) involves coefficients at \(Nh_J+l\) up to sign.
They vanish unless \(N\mid l\).
If \(N\mid l\), put \(e=l/N\).
Since every prime outside \(J\) divides \(h_J\), we have
\[
       (q,h_J+e)\le |e|\prod_{p\in J}p,\qquad
       |\widehat L_q(h_J+e)|\le
            \frac{2|e|p_*}{\prod_{p\notin J,\ p\mid q}p}
            \ll_W 2^{-r/2}.                                           \tag{2}
\]
Here \(|h_J+e|\ge |h_J|/2\) for large \(r\), since the unused larger primes already give \(q/p_*\ge2^{r/2}\).
One unused prime cancels the bound \(p_*\) in the numerator of (2), while all the other unused larger primes are at least two.
The absolute sum of the coefficients in the expansion of \(G\) is at most \((1+s^{-1/2})^s\le e^{\sqrt{s}}\).
Thus all nonconstant frequencies of \(W\) together contribute \(o(1)\) to \(\int W L_n G\,d\nu\).
Its constant coefficient is positive and \(A\sqrt{s}\to\infty\) by the [totient bound](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#test).
Consequently \(\int W|L_n|\,d\nu\to\infty\).
Since \(\int W L_n\,d\nu\) is bounded by the finite Fourier expansion, (1) follows.

<a id="phase"></a>
## A relative phase that varies around the circle

Write \(d=\varphi(n)\), \(z=e^{i\theta}\) and form the integer Laurent polynomial
\[
             R=z^{d-2k}T(z)B(1/z)-T(1/z)B(z).
\]
Off the zeros of the factors, let \(\psi\) be the argument of \(T\Phi_n/(z^kB)\).
Reciprocity of \(\Phi_n\) and reality of the coefficients give \(|\sin\psi|=|R|/(2|TB|)\).
If \(R\ne0\), its logarithmic measure is nonnegative by multiplying it by a monomial and applying Jensen's formula to the resulting nonzero integer polynomial.
Taking the perpendicular component of either summand of \(P\) relative to the other gives
\[
       \log|P|\ge \max\{L_n+\log|T|,\log|B|\}+\log|\sin\psi|.
\]
The mean of the last term is at least \(-\log2-m(T)-m(B)\).
With \(f^-=\max(0,-f)\), the maximum is at least \(L_n^+-(\log|T|)^--(\log|B|)^-\).
The subtracted functions have finite integrals since \(T,B\) are fixed.
Thus the [growth of the unweighted logarithm](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#statement) gives \(m(P)\to\infty\) on this branch, including arbitrarily large shifts \(k\).

For \(R=0\), comparison of the finite supports of \(T(z)B(1/z)\) and \(T(1/z)B(z)\) gives \(|d-2k|\le2\max(\deg T,\deg B)\).
Hence \(k=d/2+O_{T,B}(1)\), and \(D=\deg P\le d+O_{T,B}(1)\).

<a id="moments"></a>
## Bounded Mahler measure controls each fixed Fourier coefficient

Suppose \(R=0,\ r\to\infty\) and \(m(P)\) stays bounded along a sequence.
Then \(d\to\infty,\ k\to\infty\).
Remove the zero at the origin by setting \(F=z^{-v}P\), where \(v=\operatorname{ord}_0(T)\) for all sufficiently large indices.
Write \(c=F(0)\ne0\), a fixed nonzero integer.
For every fixed positive integer \(h\), the coefficients of \(F\) through degree \(h\) stay bounded.
Indeed these are coefficients of \(z^{-v}T\Phi_n\), and the factors \((1-z^j)^{\mu(n/j)}\) for \(j\mid n,\ j\le h\) have only finitely many possible choices.
Therefore \([z^h]\log(F/c)=-\sum_\alpha\alpha^{-h}/h\) stays bounded, with the roots of \(F\) counted with multiplicity.

For the boundary logarithm, twice its \(h\)-th Fourier coefficient equals \(-1/h\) times the sum with terms \(\alpha^{-h}\) for \(|\alpha|\ge1\) and \(\bar\alpha^h\) for \(|\alpha|<1\), by the Fourier expansion of each linear root factor.
For \(|\alpha|<1\) write \(b_\alpha=\log|\alpha|^{-1}\).
Jensen gives \(\sum b_\alpha=m(P)-\log|c|\le m(P)\).
The change in that root term has modulus at most \(2h b_\alpha e^{h b_\alpha}\).
Summing shows that the coefficients of \(\log|P|=\log|F|\) at \(h\) are bounded for each fixed \(h\), and likewise at \(-h\) by reality.
The constant term is the bounded mean \(m(P)\).
It follows for every fixed trigonometric polynomial \(W\) that
\[
                               \int W\log|P|\,d\nu=O_W(1).              \tag{3}
\]

<a id="poles"></a>
## Vanishing weights at the circle roots of the added factor

Let \(\Gamma\) be the finite set of arguments of the distinct unit-circle roots of \(B\), and choose
\[
                        W(\theta)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
                                         |e^{i\theta}-e^{i\gamma}|^4 ,
\]
with empty product one.
Set \(\delta=C/\sqrt d\), where \(C>0\) is a sufficiently large constant depending on \(T,B\).
Let \(E\) be the union of the angular \(\delta\)-neighborhoods of \(\Gamma\).
On its complement consider \(u=L_n+\log|T|-\log|B|\), outside its finitely many singular points.
The roots off the circle in \(T,B\) contribute a smooth function with second derivative bounded by a fixed constant.
For the circle roots of \(T\Phi_n\) let
\[
                       S=\tfrac14\sum_\beta \sin^{-2}((\theta-\beta)/2).
\]
On each arc \(I\) of length \(\ell\) between their consecutive distinct angles, we have \(S\ge\max(d/4,4/\ell^2)\) by the [circle-root curvature estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#curvature).
The adverse contribution to \(u''\) from the circle roots of \(B\) is at most \(C_B/\delta^2\) on the complement of \(E\), by \(\sin(t/2)\ge t/\pi\) for angular distance \(0\le t\le\pi\).
The choice of \(C\) and then large \(d\) give \(-u''\ge S/2\ge2/\ell^2\) there.

Deleting \(E\) splits each original arc into at most \(2|\Gamma|+1\) intervals.
On each remaining interval \(u\) has at most two monotone branches by the strict curvature.
On a branch the portion with \(|u|\le t\) has length at most \(\ell\sqrt{2t}\), since variation over distance \(s\) is at least \(s^2/\ell^2\) by integration of the derivative bound and at most \(2t\) within that range.
This argument also covers a branch ending at the edge of \(E\).
Summing first over the bounded number of pieces and then over the original arcs gives \(\nu\{\theta\notin E:|u|\le t\}\ll_B\sqrt{t}\).
The [logarithmic cancellation integral](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#integral) on this restricted set thus yields
\[
                   \int_{\theta\notin E}-\log(1-e^{-|u|})\,d\nu=O_B(1).
\]
By the reverse triangle inequality, on this set the logarithm of \(|P|\) is at least \(\max\{L_n+\log|T|,\log|B|\}+\log(1-e^{-|u|})\).
The fixed weight is bounded and nonnegative, so
\[
              \int_{\theta\notin E} W\log|P|\,d\nu
                   \ \ge\ \int_{\theta\notin E}W L_n^+\,d\nu-O_{T,B}(1).
\]

If \(E\) is nonempty, then \(\sup_E W=O_B(\delta^4)\).
Also \(L_n^+\le d\log2\) by the product over unit roots, so \(\int_E W L_n^+\,d\nu=O_B(d\delta^4)=o(1)\).
For every nonzero integer polynomial \(P\), \(\int(\log|P|)^-d\nu\le D\log2\).
Indeed each root factor normalized by \(\max(1,|\alpha|)\) has zero mean logarithm and its positive part is at most \(\log2\), while the remaining constant is \(M(P)\ge1\).
Thus \(\int_E W(\log|P|)^-d\nu=O_B(D\delta^4)=o(1)\) since \(D\le d+O_{T,B}(1)\).
Together with (1) this gives \(\int W\log|P|\,d\nu\to\infty\), contradicting (3).
The divergence now holds for every shift.

<a id="gap"></a>
## Positive measures at bounded prime count

For \(\omega(n)\le r_0\), multiply \(P\) by the denominator binomials of the Möbius product for \(\Phi_n\).
At most \(2^{r_0}\) binomials occur altogether in the numerator and denominator, including for nonsquarefree \(n\) by substitution from the radical.
The resulting nonzero integer polynomial has boundedly many terms in terms of \(T,B,r_0\) and has the same Mahler measure, as in the [cyclotomic clearing argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0457_v2.md#bounded-prime-count-completion).
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) supplies a positive gap there.
Taking \(r_0\) large enough for the divergence bound proves the asserted gap over all conductors.
