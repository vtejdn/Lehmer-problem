# A Mahler gap for a cyclotomic polynomial times a bounded-degree factor plus one monomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrary integer coefficients and monomial exponents

Fix an integer \(H\ge0\) and a real number \(B\ge1\).
Let \(n\ge2\), let \(T\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree at most \(H\), and take \(a\in\mathbb Z,\ 1\le |a|\le B,\ k\ge0\) with \(k\) an integer.
Every nonzero polynomial
\[
                      P(X)=T(X)\Phi_n(X)+aX^k
\]
satisfies \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\ge\eta(H,B)>0\).
Moreover, along every sequence with \(T\ne0\) and with the number \(\omega(n)\) of distinct prime divisors tending to infinity, \(\log M(P)\to\infty\).
The coefficients and roots of \(T\), the prime powers in \(n\), and the position of the added monomial can all vary.
For \(T=0\), \(M(P)=|a|\).

In particular, let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and reciprocal of even degree \(d\ge2\), and suppose some \(n\ge3\) satisfies
\[
               d\le\varphi(n)+H,\qquad
               U_n(P)<\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}-1 ,
\]
where \(U_n(P)\) is its [primitive absolute mean](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#statement).
Then \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\) has a positive lower bound depending only on \(H\).

<a id="factor"></a>
## Bounded measure forces the factor into a finite set

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) and give the circle its normalized angular measure \(\nu\).
Assume \(T\ne0\), with leading coefficient \(t\) and roots \(\alpha_j\) listed with multiplicity.
Choose a radius \(\rho\in[1/4,3/4]\) at distance at least \(\delta=1/(8(H+1))\) from all the numbers \(|\alpha_j|\).
The excluded intervals have total length at most \(2H\delta<1/2\).
For \(|z|=1\) and \(|\alpha_j|\le2\) we have \(|\rho z-\alpha_j|\ge\delta\ge(\delta/2)\max(1,|\alpha_j|)\).
For \(|\alpha_j|>2\) the same bound follows from \(|\rho z-\alpha_j|\ge|\alpha_j|/2\).
Multiplying these estimates gives
\[
         |T(\rho z)|\ge(\delta/2)^H |t|\prod_j\max(1,|\alpha_j|)
                      =(\delta/2)^H M(T).
\]
The Möbius product for \(\Phi_n\) gives the absolute lower bound
\[
          |\Phi_n(\rho z)|\ \ge\
            \prod_{j\ge1}\frac{1-(3/4)^j}{1+(3/4)^j}>0 ,
\]
since each exponent in \(\prod_{e\mid n}(X^{n/e}-1)^{\mu(e)}\) is \(0,1\) or \(-1\).
Thus \(|P(\rho z)|\ge c_H M(T)-B\) with \(c_H>0\), including when \(k=0\).
If the right side is positive, monotonicity of the circle mean of \(\log|P|\) with radius gives \(M(P)\ge c_H M(T)-B\).
The same inequality is immediate when the right side is nonpositive.
Consequently
\[
                            M(T)\le c_H^{-1}(M(P)+B).
\]
An integer polynomial of degree at most \(H\) with bounded measure has bounded coefficients, by expansion in its roots.
Therefore on any sequence with bounded \(M(P)\) only finitely many \(T\) occur.

<a id="curvature"></a>
## Absorbing the roots of a fixed factor off the circle

Fix \(T\ne0\) and put \(L_n(\theta)=\log|\Phi_n(e^{i\theta})|\), \(h(\theta)=\log|T(e^{i\theta})|\), and \(u=L_n+h\).
Separate from \(u\) the contributions of all roots on the circle, including those of \(T\).
The remaining part is a constant plus a finite sum of smooth periodic functions \(\log|e^{i\theta}-\alpha_j|\) for \(|\alpha_j|\ne1\).
Its second derivative has absolute value bounded by a finite \(C_T\).
On any open arc \(I\) of length \(\ell\) between consecutive distinct circle roots of \(T\Phi_n\), the negative second derivative of the circle-root contribution is
\[
                S(\theta)=\frac14\sum_\beta
                         \frac{1}{\sin^2((\theta-\beta)/2)}
                   \ \ge\ \max\{\varphi(n)/4,4/\ell^2\} .
\]
Here \(\beta\) ranges over the circle-root angles with multiplicities.
The \(\varphi(n)\) roots of \(\Phi_n\) give the first bound and the nearer endpoint of \(I\) gives the second, exactly as in the [circle-root curvature estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#curvature).
Use consecutive lifts if there is only one distinct root angle.
Once \(\varphi(n)\ge8C_T\), we have \(-u''\ge S-C_T\ge S/2\ge 2/\ell^2\) throughout every arc.
The function \(u\) tends to \(-\infty\) at both endpoints.
On each side of its unique maximum the part on which \(|u-b|\le t\) has length at most \(\ell\sqrt{2t}\) for any real \(b\) and \(t>0\).
Indeed the change over a distance \(s\) on one monotone branch is at least \(s^2/\ell^2\) by the second derivative bound and at most \(2t\) inside the band.
Summing both branches over the arcs gives
\[
                    \nu\{|u-b|\le t\}\le 2\sqrt{2t}.
\]

Take \(b=\log|a|\ge0\).
The [logarithmic integral estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#integral) now has its constant multiplied by \(\sqrt2\).
In detail, the level bound gives \(\int e^{-j|u-b|}\,d\nu\le\sqrt{2\pi/j}\) for every \(j\ge1\), by integrating \(j e^{-jt}\nu\{|u-b|\le t\}\) over \(t>0\).
The reverse triangle inequality and the series for \(-\log(1-e^{-|u-b|})\) give
\[
                 m(P)\ \ge\ \int\max(u,b)\,d\nu
                                      -\sqrt{2\pi}\,\zeta(3/2).
\]
This estimate uses only the constant modulus of \(a e^{ik\theta}\).
Write \(h^-=\max(0,-h)\), which is integrable because the finitely many circle zeros have logarithmic singularities.
Since \(\max(u,b)\ge\max(L_n,0)-h^-\) and the mean of \(L_n\) is zero, we obtain
\[
                  m(P)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\|L_n\|_{L^1(\nu)}
                               -\int h^-\,d\nu-\sqrt{2\pi}\,\zeta(3/2).
\]

<a id="gap"></a>
## Many prime divisors and bounded excess degree

The [growth of the cyclotomic boundary logarithm](Yin_C_G0345_v91.md#statement) gives \(\|L_n\|_1\to\infty\) whenever \(\omega(n)\to\infty\), including conductors with repeated prime factors.
Also \(\varphi(n)\ge2^{\omega(n)-1}\).
If \(m(P)\) failed to tend to infinity with \(\omega(n)\) on a sequence with \(T\ne0\), a subsequence of bounded measure would have a fixed \(T\) by the interior-circle estimate.
The preceding curvature bound now applies for all sufficiently large indices on this subsequence and gives \(m(P)\to\infty\), a contradiction.

For \(\omega(n)\le r\), multiply \(P\) by the product of denominator binomials in the Möbius product for \(\Phi_n\).
There are at most \(2^r\) numerator and denominator binomials in total.
The resulting integer polynomial is nonzero, has the same measure, and has boundedly many terms in terms of \(H,r\), since \(T\) has at most \(H+1\) terms.
The [bounded-term theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) therefore separates its positive logarithmic measures from zero.
A sequence with \(1<M(P)\to1\) would have a subsequence with bounded \(\omega(n)\) or one with \(\omega(n)\to\infty\).
The two bounds exclude these possibilities for \(T\ne0\), and the integer values \(M(P)=|a|\) handle \(T=0\).

Finally the [small-mean congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v89.md#statement) writes the monic reciprocal input of the statement as \(T\Phi_n+vX^{d/2}\) with \(v\in\{0,1,-1\}\) and monic \(T\in\mathbb Z[X]\) of degree \(d-\varphi(n)\le H\).
The two nonzero shifts satisfy the gap just proved with \(B=1\).
For \(v=0\), \(M(P)=M(T)\), and the finite set of integer polynomials of degree at most \(H\) and measure at most two gives the required gap there as well.
