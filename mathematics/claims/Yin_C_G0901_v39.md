# Congruent traces of units of small height and an explicit gap for integral trace equations

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rigidity at a fixed prime and an archimedean consequence

Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(T_j\) for the monic polynomial satisfying \(T_j(v+v^{-1})=v^j+v^{-j}\) for \(j\ge1\).
Suppose \(x,y\) are algebraic units in a common number field \(K\), at least one is nontorsion, and
\[
                x+x^{-1}\equiv y+y^{-1}\pmod{2\mathcal O_K}.
\]
If \(h(x)+h(y)<7\log(2)/170\), then \(y\) is one of \(x,x^{-1},-x,-x^{-1}\).
In particular, for every nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), the traces \(\alpha^j+\alpha^{-j}\) at positive indices \(j\) with \(2j h(\alpha)<7\log(2)/170\) are pairwise distinct modulo \(2\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})}\).

For any nonzero number of torus variables \(\mathbf X\), let \(Q\in\mathbb Z[\mathbf X^{\pm1}]\) be any integral Laurent polynomial, with no restriction on its support.
Then the total logarithmic Mahler measure of the equation
\[
                            Y+Y^{-1}-Q(\mathbf X)
\]
is zero or at least \(\log(13/4)/14\).
The zero cases are the constant integers in \([-2,2]\) and \(Q=\pm(\mathbf X^v+\mathbf X^{-v})\) for a nonzero integer vector \(v\).
For such trace equations the constant here is explicit and independent of the number of variables; the [relative-height bound in terms of vertical width](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#fiber) applies to more general two-variable polynomials.

<a id="dyadic"></a>
## The two factors of the trace difference

For a nonzero algebraic integer \(u\) in a number field \(E\), write \(w_2(u)\) for the logarithm of the dyadic part of its absolute ideal norm, divided by \([E:\mathbb Q]\).
The definition is unchanged on extending \(E\) by multiplicativity of the ideal norm on extended ideals.
It is additive on products, \(w_2(2)=\log2\), and \(w_2(u)\le [E:\mathbb Q]^{-1}\log|N_{E/\mathbb Q}u|\).
If \(z\) is a nontorsion unit of height at most \(H\), the [triangular cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0901_v26.md#integral) with \(k=1,N=15\) gives
\[
                     w_2(1-z)\le\frac{\log16}{15}+\frac{17H}{3}.
\]
For \(H<7\log2/170\) this is strictly less than \(\tfrac12\log2\).
For a root of unity \(\omega\ne1,-1\), \(w_2(1-\omega)\le\tfrac12\log2\).
Indeed its primitive norm is \(|\Phi_b(1)|\) for order \(b>2\).
The identity \(\prod_{d\mid b,\,d>1}\Phi_d(1)=b\), from the geometric sum at one, gives by induction \(\Phi_b(1)=q\) at powers of a prime \(q\), and one otherwise.
At \(b=2^e>2\) one divides the logarithm by \(\varphi(2^e)\ge2\).

The trace difference multiplied by \(x\) is \((1-xy)(1-x/y)\).
If either ratio \(xy,x/y\) equals \(1\) or \(-1\), the conclusion holds.
Otherwise both have height at most \(h(x)+h(y)\), and at least one is nontorsion since their product is \(x^2\) and their quotient is \(y^2\).
Their two dyadic norm contributions for the factors \(1-xy,1-x/y\) consequently sum to strictly less than \(\log2\), contrary to the congruence.
For the trace sequence a congruence in the trace field extends to the field of \(\alpha\), and equality up to sign and inversion between two distinct positive powers of a nontorsion unit is impossible.

<a id="frobenius"></a>
## Comparing the trace at two powers of the torus variables

For \(\mathbf z\) on the unit torus, choose one root \(v\) of \(v+v^{-1}=Q(\mathbf z)\) with \(|v|\ge1\).
Put \(g(\mathbf z)=\log|v|\), which is independent of the choice and continuous by continuity of the unordered roots of a monic polynomial.
Jensen's formula in \(Y\) gives the stated multivariate measure \(m=\int g\) under normalized product arc length.
For every odd prime \(p\) the integral Laurent polynomial
\[
                        B_p=T_p(Q(\mathbf X))-Q(\mathbf X^p)
\]
has every coefficient divisible by \(p\).
Indeed \(T_p(U)\equiv U^p\bmod p\) follows by substitution \(U=V+V^{-1}\) in characteristic \(p\) and injectivity of this substitution.
The remaining congruence is the Frobenius identity on integer Laurent coefficients.
If \(B_p\ne0\), its log Mahler measure is at least \(\log p\), since every nonzero integer Laurent polynomial has nonnegative log Mahler measure by iterated Jensen using a leading coefficient.
On the torus \(|T_p(Q(\mathbf z))|\le2e^{p g(\mathbf z)}\) and \(|Q(\mathbf z^p)|\le2e^{g(\mathbf z^p)}\).
Consequently
\[
    \log p\le m(B_p)\le\log4+
             \int\!\big(p\,g(\mathbf z)+g(\mathbf z^p)\big)=\log4+(p+1)m .
\]
The power map preserves product arc length.
Taking \(p=13\) gives the claimed bound whenever \(B_{13}\ne0\).

The same argument over the conjugates of a single nontorsion algebraic unit \(a\) supplies a test without any limiting distribution.
Let \(Q\) now be in one variable and choose units \(v,v'\) satisfying \(v+v^{-1}=Q(a)\), \(v'+(v')^{-1}=Q(a^p)\); they and their inverses are integral by their quadratic equations.
If \(B_p(a)\ne0\), its normalized absolute log norm in their compositum is at least \(\log p\).
Using the same pointwise estimates with \(g=|\log|v||\) and \(g'=|\log|v'||\), the averages of which are \(2h(v)\) and \(2h(v')\) for units, gives
\[
                      2p\,h(v)+2h(v')\ge\log(p/4).
\]
Thus if the left side is strictly smaller at any \(p>4\), \(Q(a^p)=T_p(Q(a))\) and \(v'=v^{\pm p}\).
In particular this applies directly to \(v=a^j\) whenever \(Q(a)=a^j+a^{-j}\).

<a id="commuting"></a>
## Laurent polynomials for which the comparison is an identity

Suppose \(B_{13}=0\).
If \(Q\) is a constant integer, the defining power equation implies that a root \(v\) of \(v+v^{-1}=Q\) satisfies \(v^{13}=v^{\pm1}\).
Thus \(|Q|\le2\), and conversely all constants in this interval have zero measure.
For one variable and nonconstant \(Q\), invert that variable if necessary so the largest exponent \(k\) is positive; denote its leading coefficient by \(c\).
Near infinity, the root \(v(X)\) of \(v+v^{-1}=Q(X)\) asymptotic to \(Q(X)\) exists as a convergent Laurent series, by the expansion of \(\sqrt{1-4/Q(X)^2}\) near one.
The equation gives \(v(X^{13})=v(X)^{13}\), since both are the root of large modulus at infinity.
Their leading terms give \(c^{12}=1\), hence \(c=\pm1\).
If \(v(X)/(cX^k)=1+b X^{-j}+\cdots\) with a first nonzero \(b\), then its thirteenth power has nonzero term \(13bX^{-j}\), while substitution of \(X^{13}\) has none.
Thus \(v(X)=cX^k\) and \(Q(X)=c(X^k+X^{-k})\).

In several variables, specialize \(\mathbf X=(X^{a_1},\ldots,X^{a_l})\) with an integer vector \(a\) for which distinct support exponents have distinct scalar products with \(a\), and nonzero individual exponents and nonzero sums of two support exponents have nonzero scalar product.
Such a choice avoids finitely many proper hyperplanes.
The identity survives specialization and the one-variable classification forces exactly two support exponents with equal coefficients \(\pm1\) and opposite scalar products.
The hyperplane choices force the original exponents themselves to be opposite.
Conversely the listed Laurent binomials have both vertical roots of modulus one on the torus, so their measure is zero.
