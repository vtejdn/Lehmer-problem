# Simultaneous residue degrees and cyclotomic norms for the blocks 2 and 3

<a id="statement"></a>
## Prime effective delay

Let \(q>3\) be a prime, let \(g\ge1\) be prime to \(q\), and put \(n=q-g\).
Write \(A(Y)=(Y-2)(Y-3)\), \(A^*(Y)=(1-2Y)(1-3Y)\), and
\[
          P(X)=X^n A(X^g)+\varepsilon A^*(X^g),\qquad \varepsilon=\pm1 .
\]
Assume \(n\ge C(2g+1)^9\) with a sufficiently large absolute \(C\).
The entire monic cyclotomic part \(C_0(X)\) of \(P\) is given by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &g\ \mathrm{odd}&g\ \mathrm{even}\\ \hline
 \varepsilon=-1& X^2-1&X-1\\
 \varepsilon=+1& 1&X+1 .
\end{array}
\]
Set \(W=g+1-\deg C_0\).
For \(\ell=2,3\) let \(f_\ell=\operatorname{ord}_q(\ell)\), \(t_\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(2,f_\ell)\) and \(J_\ell=(q-1)/t_\ell\).

Every irreducible factor \(S\) of \(P/C_0\) is reciprocal, has even degree \(d\), and satisfies
\[
         d=k_2t_2+\delta_2=k_3t_3+\delta_3,\qquad
         1\le k_\ell\le J_\ell,\quad 0\le\delta_\ell\le W,\quad
         \delta_\ell\ \mathrm{even}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
If \(\gcd(t_2,t_3)>W\), the two remainders agree and
\[
                   d\ge\operatorname{lcm}(t_2,t_3)
                     =\frac{q-1}{\gcd(J_2,J_3)} .                      \tag{2}
\]
In particular, if this greatest common divisor of the indices is one, \(P/C_0\) is irreducible under the same remainder condition.

Independently of that condition, let \(s\in[1,g]\) be the [integer exterior share](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) of \(S\), so \(s\) of its exterior roots lie near the roots of each block.
For every \(\ell\) with \(J_\ell\le8g\), one has \(s/g=k_\ell/J_\ell\).
In particular, when both indices satisfy this bound, the reduced denominator of \(s/g\) divides \(\gcd(g,J_2,J_3)\).
If that gcd is one, \(P/C_0\) is irreducible.
More generally, if the gcd is bounded on such a family then every noncyclotomic factor has logarithmic Mahler measure bounded below by a positive constant.

There is also an exact norm at both signs.
For a primitive \(q\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\) put \(\xi=-\varepsilon\zeta\).
The number \((P/C_0)(\xi)\) is six times a unit of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\).
For each \(S\) in (1), its value generates a squarefree ideal dividing \((6)\) and
\[
             \left|\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q} S(\xi)\right|
                    =2^{d-\delta_2}3^{d-\delta_3}.                      \tag{3}
\]
At each \(\ell\) its supporting primes are exactly the irreducible factors common to \(\bar S\) and the reduction of the minimal polynomial of \(\xi\), with conjugation acting by inversion on their roots.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Roots of unity on the rational graph

Set \(Z=Y A^*(Y)/A(Y)\).
The denominator is nonzero for a root of unity \(Y\), and
\[
                   Z-1=\frac{6(Y-1)(Y^2+1)}{(Y-2)(Y-3)}.              \tag{4}
\]
We show that the torsion pairs \((Y,Z)\) are exactly \((1,1),(-1,-1),(i,1),(-i,1)\).
Work first at a prime \(\ell\), with \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
A root of unity \(u\) reducing to one has \(\ell\)-power order, since a root of order prime to \(\ell\) reducing to one must be one by the geometric sum.
If its order is \(\ell^a>1\), all primitive powers \(u^j\) have \(v_\ell(1-u^j)=v_\ell(1-u)\), again by the geometric sum.
Their product of differences is \(\Phi_{\ell^a}(1)=\ell\), so \(v_\ell(1-u)=1/\varphi(\ell^a)\).

Suppose \(Y\ne1,-1\).
At 2, \(Y-2\) is a unit.
If \(Y-3\) is a nonunit, \(Y\) reduces to one, and its order is \(2^a\) with \(a\ge2\).
Then \(v_2(Y-1)<1\) and \(v_2(Y-3)=v_2(Y-1)\).
Thus (4) has positive valuation, including when it vanishes.
At 3, \(Y-3\) is a unit.
If \(Y-2\) is a nonunit, \(-Y\) reduces to one and is distinct from one, so \(v_3(Y+1)\le1/2\) and \(v_3(Y-2)=v_3(Y+1)\).
The numerator in (4) is divisible by 3, giving \(v_3(Z-1)>0\) as well.
A torsion \(Z\) must now have both 2-power and 3-power order and hence be one.
Equation (4) gives \(Y=\pm i\) in the case under consideration, and direct substitution gives the two remaining pairs.

For any torsion root \(x\) of \(P\), take \(Y=x^g\) and \(Z=-\varepsilon x^q\).
The equation \(P(x)=0\) gives precisely this rational graph.
The classification shows \(x^{4g}=x^{2q}=1\).
Since \(q\) is odd and prime to \(g\), the order of \(x\) divides two.
Substituting \(x=1,-1\) gives the displayed \(C_0\), with simple roots by the [unit-circle derivative bound](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#torsion) since \(n>6g\).
This torsion classification for \(P\) also applies with any odd effective delay \(n+g\) coprime to \(g\) when \(n>6g\).

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Reciprocal Frobenius orbits and the integer share

At both residue characteristics one has
\[
              \bar P=(X^g+1)(X^q+\varepsilon),
\]
by reducing \(A(Y)=Y^2-5Y+6\) and \(A^*(Y)=6Y^2-5Y+1\).
In the second factor remove the linear factor at \(-\varepsilon\).
The remaining \(q-1\) roots are precisely the reductions of the conjugates of \(\xi\).
They are simple and disjoint from \(X^g+1\), since their orders are \(q\) or \(2q\) and \(q\nmid g\).
The reduction of \(P/C_0\) thus consists of this set of \(q-1\) simple roots and an exceptional part of degree \(W\), with multiplicities allowed in the exceptional part.

The [exterior count and allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) give \(d\ge n/(2g)>4g\) and exactly \(2g\) exterior roots of \(P\).
Reciprocity up to sign gives \(2g\) interior roots as well.
The real polynomial \(S\) therefore has a root \(z\) on the unit circle and also has the root \(\bar z=1/z\).
Irreducibility makes it reciprocal up to a scalar, so its root set is closed under inversion.
It has no roots \(1,-1\), and inverse pairs give even degree and constant coefficient one.

At each \(\ell\), the roots selected by \(\bar S\) from the simple set of size \(q-1\) are consequently stable under the \(\ell\)-power map and inversion.
Indexing the roots by \(\xi^a\) for the units modulo its order (or by their reductions) gives orbits of size \(t_\ell\).
Indeed, for odd \(q\) the units modulo \(2q\) are identified with those modulo \(q\), and the subgroup generated by \(\ell,-1\) in the units modulo \(q\) has order \(f_\ell\) if \(f_\ell\) is even and \(2f_\ell\) otherwise.
In characteristic 2 the grid has order \(q\), also when \(\xi\) has order \(2q\) before reduction.
Counting selected orbits and exceptional roots gives (1), with \(k_\ell\ge1\) because \(d>W\).
The remainders differ by a multiple of \(\gcd(t_2,t_3)\), which gives equality if that gcd exceeds \(W\).
This proves (2), and two factors of degree at least \(q-1\) would exceed the degree \(q+g-\deg C_0<2(q-1)\) of the quotient.

For the approximation by the share use \(|d/n-s/g|<1/(3(2g+1)^2)\) from the same allocation.
Since \(d\le q+g\), \(n=q-g\) and \(0\le\delta_\ell\le g+1\), one has
\[
  \left|\frac{d}{n}-\frac{k_\ell}{J_\ell}\right|
   =\frac{d(g-1)}{n(q-1)}+\frac{\delta_\ell}{q-1}
   \le\frac{4g}{n}.
\]
Taking \(C\) large enough for \(n\ge96g^3\) gives \(|s/g-k_\ell/J_\ell|<1/(8g^2)\).
Distinct rationals with these denominators differ by at least \(1/(gJ_\ell)\).
This proves equality whenever \(J_\ell\le8g\).
The positive integers \(s\) sum to \(g\) over the factors, and the [measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement) gives \(m(S)\ge (s/(2g))\log6\) on increasing \(C\).
These facts give the asserted irreducibility and uniform gap under the denominator test.

The [simultaneously splitting delays](Yin_C_G0345_v211.md#limits) still illustrate the limitation of both tests.
There \(g\mid(q-1)/f_\ell\) for both primes, so \(g\mid2J_\ell\) and \(g/\gcd(g,2)\) divides both \(J_2,J_3\).
In particular the combined period \(\operatorname{lcm}(t_2,t_3)\) can be at most \(2(q-1)/g\) there.
No supply of prime delays satisfying a small joint index is used.

## Evaluation at the primitive roots

At \(\xi\) one has \(\xi^q=-\varepsilon\), so (4) gives
\[
                  \xi^g P(\xi)=6\varepsilon (Y-1)(Y^2+1),
                         \qquad Y=\xi^g=(-\varepsilon)^g\zeta^g .
\]
The factor \(Y^2+1\) is a unit, since \(\zeta^{2g}\) is primitive of order \(q\) and \(\Phi_q(-1)=1\).
If \((-\varepsilon)^g=-1\), \(Y-1\) is also a unit, and the table gives \(C_0=1\).
Otherwise \(Y-1=\zeta^g-1\) differs from \(\zeta-1\) by a unit, since their quotient is a geometric sum of absolute norm one.
The factor \(X+\varepsilon\) belongs to \(C_0\) and its value at \(\xi\) is \(-\varepsilon(\zeta-1)\).
Any remaining linear factor of \(C_0\) then evaluates to a unit because \(1+\zeta\) is a unit.
This proves \((P/C_0)(\xi)=6u\) with \(u\) a unit of the ring of integers.

Let \(F\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\xi\), which is \(\Phi_q\) or \(\Phi_{2q}\).
At \(\ell=2,3\), its reduction has the \(q-1\) distinct primitive roots used in (1).
Hence \(\ell\) does not divide its discriminant.
The power basis of \(\xi\) spans an order with index prime to \(\ell\) in the ring of integers, since its trace discriminant is the index squared times the field discriminant by change of basis.
Over \(\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}\) this order is therefore already the integral closure.
The [monogenic prime factorization](../audits/literature/Sut21c/Yin_U001.md#sut21c-u001) gives one unramified prime per irreducible factor of \(\bar F\), of residue degree \(f_\ell\), with reduction of \(\xi\) satisfying that factor.
At such a prime, \(S(\xi)\) has positive valuation exactly when this factor divides \(\bar S\).
Since \(S(\xi)\) divides \(6u\), each positive valuation is one and all prime divisors lie over 2 or 3.
Multiplying the residue sizes of its prime divisors yields the two exponents in (3), their total degree at \(\ell\) being \(\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar F)=d-\delta_\ell\).
Complex conjugation takes the residue of \(\xi\) to its inverse and fixes this set of prime divisors by reciprocity.
