# Counting full coefficient assignments from the excess of a largest edge

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cost of strict coefficient dominance over an entire tree

Let \(\alpha\) range over nontorsion algebraic units of shrinking positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), write \(L=\log(1/m)\), and use the finite window and weight \(j\) of the [ternary support count](Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#scale).
For a complete rooted tree of ordinary rational splits and each internal vertex \(v\), put
\[
 h_v=\max\{0,\,2\max_i |b_{vi}|-\textstyle\sum_i|b_{vi}|\},
 \qquad B(\mathcal T)=\sum_v h_v
\]
for the complete signed tuple incident to \(v\).
The \(h_v\) are nonnegative integers.
Let \(A_j(K)\) count the translation classes of minimal original supports admitting some sign choice and some complete tree of ordinary rational splits with \(B(\mathcal T)\le Kj\), for real \(K\ge0\).
Let \(A_j\) denote all the original classes at that weight.
If \(\log(2+K)=o(L)\) and
\[
            \log(A_j/A_j(K))=o((j-2)L),
\]
then for every fixed \(\lambda\ge0\) a single full integer coefficient tuple, including its output, has a number \(W\) of ordinary rational-terminal marked lists satisfying
\[
         \log W-\lambda\log H_2(\mathbf b)\ge(1-o(1))(r-2)L
\]
for some \(r\to\infty\).
The quantity \(H_2\) is the product of \(1+|b_i|\) outside the largest two magnitudes.
The exponents and rational coefficients satisfy the [rational edge bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#near) from the original interval, with \(|b_i|\le (1+K)j\) as well.
If all powers preserve degree or a real conjugate exists, the same logarithmic estimate holds for the number of primitive marked lists.
There is no conjugate-radius hypothesis in this sufficient criterion.

A separate unconditional conclusion holds for the following restricted splitting rule on every shrinking sequence.
At each vertex allow an ordinary rational split only when both resulting lists of coefficient magnitudes satisfy all polygon inequalities, that is, their largest length is at most the sum of the other lengths.
Then there is a full tuple with at least \(H_2(\mathbf b)^\lambda(c_\lambda/m)^{r-2}\) marked lists terminal for this restricted rule, with \(r\to\infty\), and the same primitive logarithmic conclusion at power-stable or real inputs.
Every ordinary split passes this extra test when there is a unit-circle conjugate.
Away from the circle the two notions can differ, as the [four-term example](#example) shows.

<a id="labels"></a>
## One additional leaf at a vertex with excess

Fix a plane tree representing a counted original support.
At every original internal vertex attach an auxiliary leaf of length \(h_v\) in a fixed position, allowing zero.
The enlarged list of incident lengths satisfies all polygon inequalities.
Indeed the added length exactly compensates any excess at the largest original edge and never exceeds that edge.
Original edge signs are accounted for separately, and auxiliary leaves do not enter the field equations.

For any ordered list of nonnegative integer child lengths \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\), all integer output lengths in
\[
 [\max(0,2\max_i x_i-\textstyle\sum_i x_i),\ \textstyle\sum_i x_i]
\]
can be obtained by binary triangle combinations \(|a-b|\le c\le a+b\) in a fixed left bracketing.
For one child the assertion is immediate.
On adjoining \(x\) to a previous interval \([l,u]\), the new upper endpoint is \(u+x\), and the lower endpoint is the distance from \(x\) to \([l,u]\), or zero when \(x\) belongs to the interval.
The integer intervals from consecutive previous values have no missing integer between them, also for \(x=0\).
These endpoints equal the displayed formula for the augmented list.
Thus every actual set of enlarged edge lengths extends to the predetermined binary tree even when no embedding supplies a Euclidean polygon.
The triangle sum \(\sum_{c=|a-b|}^{a+b}(2c+1)=(2a+1)(2b+1)\) bounds its number of assignments by the product of the incoming leaf weights. [Tree count](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#lengths)

There are at most \(j-2\) additional leaves.
The number of nonnegative assignments of their lengths summing to at most \(\lfloor Kj\rfloor\) is at most \(\binom{\lfloor Kj\rfloor+j}{j}\le(e(1+K))^j\).
For any fixed assignment their weight product is at most \((1+2K)^j\), padding the lengths with zeros to \(j\) entries and applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
Original nonroot leaves contribute \(3^{j-1}\).
Together with the original plane trees and signs on the actual edges this bounds the total number of complete signed coefficient descriptions by \(\exp(Cj\log(2+K))\), for an absolute constant \(C\).

<a id="weights"></a>
## Charging the product of the smaller coefficient magnitudes

Give mass one to each original nonroot leaf and mass \(h_v\) to each additional leaf.
Their combined mass is at most \(j(1+K)\).
By the polygon inequalities, an original edge length is at most the total descendant mass, by induction from the leaves.
At each vertex omit its actual parent and its actual child with largest descendant mass when bounding \(H_2\), ignoring the auxiliary leaf as a candidate child.
Along any path, the masses of successive non-omitted actual edges grow at least twofold toward the root because each has a sibling with at least its own mass.
The same holds for a path starting at an auxiliary mass.

Put \(b=1+K\).
There are fewer than \(2j\) original edges, so masses \(s\le b\) cost at most \(2j\log(1+b)\) in the sum of \(\log(1+s)\).
For \(s>b\) write \(\log(1+s)\le\log(1+b)+\log(1+s/b)\) and charge the latter log equally to the \(s\) units of descendant mass.
On each path and in each range \(2^a b<s\le2^{a+1}b\), at most one non-omitted edge is encountered.
The charge per mass unit along the entire path is at most \(b^{-1}\sum_{a\ge0}2^{-a}\log(1+2^{a+1})\).
As total mass is at most \(jb\), the product of all actual local \(H_2\) is at most \(\exp(C'j\log(2+K))\).

<a id="reconstruction"></a>
## Comparing the two counts

Let \(M\) be the maximum of \((W/H_2^\lambda)^{1/(r-2)}\) over the prescribed coefficient tuples with positive count, weights \(3\le r\le j\), and the stated finite windows.
For each signed coefficient assignment, reconstruction from the child subtrees gives at most the product of its local list counts, since each local exponent list positions all its children.
The signs at both ends of a field edge are opposite and the distinguished root leaf fixes the translation. [Original reconstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#trees)
Using \(\sum_v(\deg v-2)=j-2\) for the original tree, the resulting bound is
\[
                  A_j(K)\le M^{j-2}\exp(C_\lambda j\log(2+K)).
\]
The assumed proportion and \(A_j\ge(\log(2)/(32m))^{j-1}\) yield the claimed weighted logarithmic bound.
For fixed weights the number of lists is uniformly bounded by the [mixed-embedding argument](Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#embeddings), which needs only nondegeneracy at \(\alpha\) and a rational output.
The minimum power degree tends to infinity by [power-field descent](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v270.md#descent).
Hence a maximizing weight tends to infinity.

For the restricted rule, start with each original minimal ternary relation, whose coefficient lengths all equal one, and repeatedly take a permitted split until there are none.
All resulting nodes satisfy the polygon inequalities and every relation remains nondegenerate in the field by complementation in its parent.
Thus every original support contributes with zero total excess for this rule.
The same count gives \(M\ge c_\lambda/m\), yielding its assertion with an absolute positive \(c_\lambda\) for fixed \(\lambda\).
For either rule the all-zero exponent list contributes at most one.
On any other primitive ray, collecting equal exponents gives a nonzero Laurent polynomial with at most \(r\) terms, since vanishing of an entire exponent group would be a proper zero subsum.
The [determinant or real-conjugate estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#primitive) therefore removes dilations at logarithmic cost \(O(L+\log(2r))=o((r-2)L)\).

<a id="example"></a>
## A rational split excluded by the coefficient test

Take the positive root \(\alpha>1\) of \(X^2-3X+1\).
The relation
\[
          7\alpha+7\alpha^{-1}-3\alpha^2-3\alpha^{-2}=0
\]
follows from \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}=3\) and \(\alpha^2+\alpha^{-2}=7\).
It has distinct exponents and no proper zero subsum.
Indeed its terms are nonzero, a triple is minus the remaining term, and none of its two-term sums vanishes.
The pair of positive terms sums to 21, and the negative pair sums to \(-21\), giving an ordinary rational split.
The child with coefficients \(7,7,-21\) fails the polygon test.
Any mixed pair has negative quadratic field norm: up to multiplication by a unit of norm one and field conjugation its norm is \(58-21(\alpha^q+\alpha^{-q})\) with \(q=1\) or \(3\).
Every nonzero rational multiple of an integer power has positive norm in this quadratic field.
Thus the displayed ordinary split is the only partition into two carriers and the original polygonal tuple survives the restricted test.
This is a distinction between the rules, not a sequence of measures approaching one.
