# Balanced ternary multiples on prescribed sets of exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite coefficient and degree bound

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nonzero algebraic integer \(\alpha\), of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Choose any set \(S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N\}\) of size \(s\), an integer \(1\le t\le s\), and an integer \(k\ge1\), with \(N\ge1\).
There is a nonzero polynomial supported in \(S\), with coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), divisible by \((X-1)P^k\) when \(P\ne X-1\), whenever
\[
 \log {s\choose t}>kNm+dk\log(8t)+\tfrac12 dk(k-1)\log(N+1).                 \tag{1}
\]
It has equally many positive and negative coefficients and coefficient length at most \(2\min(t,s-t)\).
For \(P=X-1\) the guaranteed divisor is \(P^k\).
All bounds concern the original exponents and the original minimum.

<a id="density"></a>
## The density and coefficient budget

Write \(H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\) for \(0<x<1\).
Fix the seed, \(k\), a density \(0<\delta\le1\) and a weight fraction \(0<x\le1/2\).
If \(\delta H(x)>km\), every sufficiently large \(N\) and every mask of size at least \(\delta N\) contain such a relation of length at most \(2x\delta N+2\).
Indeed, restrict to \(s=\lceil\delta N\rceil\) allowed slots and use \(t=\lfloor xs\rfloor\).
Stirling's formula gives \(\log {s\choose t}=\delta NH(x)+O_{x,\delta}(\log N)\), while every finite-degree cost in (1) is logarithmic in \(N\).
For fixed \(\delta\) and sufficiently small \(u=km/\delta>0\), the choice \(x=2u/\log(1/u)\) works because \(H(x)\ge x\log(1/x)\sim2u\).
Thus one can require growing multiplicity \(k\) along an arbitrary shrinking-measure sequence with \(km\to0\), preassign the allowable slots of any fixed positive density, and obtain length per ambient degree budget \(O(km/\log(\delta/(km)))+O(1/N)\).
Here \(N\) is the prescribed ambient degree bound and its required size depends on the seed and multiplicity through (1).
Absolute coefficient length can still diverge, as the [escape under powering](Yin_C_G0345_v183.md#statement) requires on some hypothetical sequences.

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the integral jets of fixed-weight subsets

For each \(t\)-element subset \(A\) of \(S\), form \(F_A=\sum_{a\in A}X^a\).
Use the \(k\) algebraic integers \(z_j=\sum_{a\in A}\binom aj\alpha^a\), indexed by \(0\le j<k\).
They are the divided derivatives at \(\alpha\) multiplied by \(\alpha^j\), so equality of all coordinates for two subsets gives divisibility of their difference by \(P^k\) in characteristic zero.
For an embedding \(\sigma\), the modulus of its \(j\)-th coordinate is at most \(R_{\sigma,j}=t(N+1)^j\max(1,|\sigma\alpha|)^N\).
Partition each real embedding interval and each real or imaginary coordinate interval for one embedding from each complex pair into half-open intervals of length \(1/2\).
Each coordinate uses at most \(8R_{\sigma,j}\) intervals because \(R_{\sigma,j}\ge1\).
Two algebraic integers assigned to the same box have difference of modulus less than one under every embedding, so its integral norm forces equality.
Multiplying over the \(d\) real dimensions, with each complex pair counted twice, and then over \(j\), gives at most the exponential of the right side of (1) possible jet tuples.
Two distinct subsets therefore give equal jets.
Their common cardinality gives vanishing at one, and their symmetric difference bounds the coefficient length by both \(2t\) and \(2(s-t)\).
This uses the [integral jet count](Yin_C_G0345_v529.md#counts) with a constant-weight input family on an arbitrary mask.

<a id="span"></a>
## Coefficient length relative to the actual span

One can also bound length divided by the actual exponent span, uniformly in the mask.
Let \(q=\lfloor\delta N/3\rfloor\), and retain the first and last \(q\) allowed slots as two disjoint sets.
Their distance is at least \(\delta N-2q\ge\delta N/3\).
Choose \(t=\lfloor xq\rfloor\) slots in each set, giving a square array with \(B={q\choose t}\) rows and columns.
Count the jets as above, substituting \(2t\) for the total input weight in the right side of (1).
If \((\delta/3)H(x)>km\), then for sufficiently large \(N\) this count is less than \(B\), since \(\log B=(\delta/3)NH(x)+O_{x,\delta}(\log N)\).
Some fiber has more than \(B\) members and contains two members in different rows and different columns.
Indeed, if two members are in different rows and share a column, every member in any other column must differ in row from at least one of them.
The difference polynomial thus uses both groups, has span at least \(\delta N/3\), and has coefficient length at most \(4t\).
Its length divided by its actual span is at most \(4x\), and the sum of its coefficients in each group separately is zero.
For \(km/\delta\to0\), taking \(x=6(km/\delta)/\log(\delta/(km))\) makes this ratio tend to zero.
Shifting the smallest exponent to zero preserves divisibility by \(P^k\) and turns the span into the ordinary degree; the original unshifted relation has the promised mask.
