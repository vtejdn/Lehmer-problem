# Energy on arithmetic progressions under powers of algebraic units

<a id="statement"></a>
## Integer quotients for the separate progressions

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a hyperbolic algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\), where hyperbolic means that every conjugate lies off the unit circle.
For \(k\ge1\) write \(P_k\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^k\), \(d_k=\deg P_k\), \(e=d/d_k\), \(m=\log M(P)\) and \(m_k=\log M(P_k)=km/e\).
For every integral Laurent polynomial \(A\) indivisible by \(P\), write \(A/P=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}h_jX^j\) on the circle.
There are integral Laurent polynomials \(B_0,\ldots,B_{k-1}\) such that
\[
       B_r/P_k=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z} h_{kj+r}X^j,\qquad
       \|A/P\|_2^2=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\|B_r/P_k\|_2^2 .                 \tag{1}
\]
At least \(k/e\) of the \(B_r\) are indivisible by \(P_k\).
Consequently the [unrestricted infimum](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#statement) satisfies
\[
             m\,\mathcal E(P)\ \ge\ m_k\,\mathcal E(P_k).
\]
If \(e>1\), equality in the count of indivisible sections requires \(e\mid k\) and \(P(X)=Q(X^e)\) for an integral polynomial \(Q\).
When \(e=1\), every progression gives an indivisible numerator for the powered input.

<a id="sections"></a>
## The numerator and the number of surviving poles

Every conjugate of \(\alpha^k\) has precisely \(e\) preimages among the conjugates of \(\alpha\), by extension of embeddings.
Taking their exterior moduli gives \(e m_k=km\).
The polynomial \(P_k(X^k)\) has simple nonzero roots and is divisible by \(P(X)\), so \(D=P_k(X^k)/P\) is a monic integral polynomial.
Write \(AD=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1} X^r B_r(X^k)\) by collecting exponents modulo \(k\).
Division by \(P_k(X^k)\), orthogonality of the \(k\) residue classes and convergence of the Laurent expansions on an annulus about the circle prove (1).

Fix a root \(\beta\) of \(P_k\), choose \(\gamma^k=\beta\), and let \(\omega\) be a primitive \(k\)-th root of unity.
The vector \(u_i=(AD)(\gamma\omega^i)\) has precisely \(e\) nonzero entries, since \(A\) is nonzero on every conjugate of \(\alpha\) and \(D\) vanishes at exactly the other preimages.
It is the unnormalized finite Fourier transform of \(v_r=\gamma^r B_r(\beta)\).
The support \(S\) of \(v\) consists of exactly the \(r\) for which \(P_k\nmid B_r\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\|u\|_\infty^2\le |S|\|v\|_2^2\) and finite Parseval gives \(\|u\|_2^2=k\|v\|_2^2\), using counting measure here.
Since \(\|u\|_2^2\le e\|u\|_\infty^2\), this proves \(e|S|\ge k\).

Suppose \(e|S|=k\) and let \(I\) be the support of \(u\).
Each \(i\in I\) attains equality in Cauchy–Schwarz, so the nonzero numbers \(v_r\omega^{ir}\) are equal for \(r\in S\).
Comparing two rows gives \(\omega^{(i-i')(r-r')}=1\) for every \(i,i'\in I\) and \(r,r'\in S\).
If \(H\) is the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\) generated by \(I-I\), then \(I\) lies in a coset of \(H\) and \(S\) in a coset of its annihilator.
Their cardinalities have product \(k\), so both are whole cosets and \(|H|=e\).
The argument works at each \(\beta\), because the indivisibility set \(S\) is unchanged by conjugation.
Thus every fiber of roots of \(P\) is invariant under multiplication by all \(e\)-th roots of unity, and its monic root polynomial has the form \(X^e-c\).
Multiplication over fibers gives the asserted integral substitution.

<a id="width"></a>
## Where a construction below the short bound must spend its energy

Suppose now that \(e=1\) and translate \(A\) by a monomial so its smallest exponent is zero.
Divide \(A=PQ+R\) integrally with \(\deg R<d\).
Let \(u_k(Q)\) count the residue classes modulo \(k\) occupied by a nonzero coefficient of \(Q\), taking \(u_k(0)=0\).
The \(k\) sections of \(RD\) have degree less than \(d_k\), since \(\deg(RD)<k d_k\).
For a residue missed by \(Q\), the corresponding numerator \(B_r\) equals its section of \(RD\).
It is therefore nonzero and short.
This also holds with any Laurent \(Q\) and any remainder supported on \([0,d-1]\), regardless of where the integral quotient is supported.

Consider a sequence with \(m\to0\), \(e=1\), and \(km\to0\), allowing \(k\) to vary.
Write \(b=\tfrac52+\log 2-2\sqrt2+10^{-8}\).
The [unrestricted estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v660.md#statement) and the [short estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#statement) at \(P_k\) yield
\[
       m\|A/P\|_2^2
        \ \ge\ b+\bigl(\tfrac12-b\bigr)
                  \bigl(1-u_k(Q)/k\bigr)-o(1).                       \tag{2}
\]
Indeed each section costs at least \(b/m_k\), and in a missed residue its cost is at least \((1/2-o(1))/m_k\).
The latter error tends to zero uniformly over the short sections, by applying the short bound to a section minimizing their energy on each input where one exists.
For ordinary division \(u_k(Q)\le\max(0,\operatorname{width}(A)-d+1)\).
In particular, when powers preserve degree and \(m\max(0,\operatorname{width}(A)-d)\to0\), choose \(k\) much larger than the excess width plus one and with \(km\to0\).
Then \(\liminf m\|A/P\|_2^2\ge1/2\).
Formula (2) also applies to corrections with a large span concentrated in few residue classes.
It constrains an upper construction on the original input without giving a positive lower bound for \(m\).
