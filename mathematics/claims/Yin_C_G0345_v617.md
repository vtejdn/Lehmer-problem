# Compressing on nonzero coefficients gives uniform gaps for quadratic transfers

<a id="statement"></a>
## A bound independent of the positions of the coefficients

Let \(H\) be a nonzero rational function regular at zero whose Taylor coefficients are integers.
For \(C\ge1\) set
\[
 T_C(H)=\sum_{0<|\beta|<1,\ \beta\ {\rm pole}}\log(1/|\beta|)
             +\int_{\mathbb T}\log^+(|H|/C)\,d\mu ,
\]
counting pole multiplicity and using normalized angular measure.
If \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) are the first \(k\) nonzero Taylor coefficients in order of exponent, then
\[
                 \sum_{i=1}^k |a_i|^2\le C^2 e^{2k T_C(H)}.                \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(H\) is not a polynomial, \(T_C(H)\ge\log(k/C^2)/(2k)\) for every positive integer \(k>C^2\).
If its total squared coefficient sum exceeds an integer \(J=C^2\), taking the first prefix to exceed \(J\) gives \(T_C(H)\ge\log(1+1/J)/(2(J+1))\).
These estimates also apply after multiplying by a monomial to clear a pole at zero.
The [quadratic application](#quadratic) gives an elementary bound \(\log(11/4)/22\) for the total cost of every admissible integral numerator transfer in the hyperbolic problem.
The [complementary upper-tail cost](#upper) is also uniformly positive.

<a id="compression"></a>
## A triangular matrix on the occupied positions

Let \(I\) be the product of Blaschke factors for the interior poles, normalized by \(I(0)>0\), and put \(f=IH/C\).
Thus \(-\log I(0)\) is the pole sum, \(f\) has at most boundary poles in the closed disk, and \(|I|=1\) on the circle.
There exist analytic \(b,g\) of modulus at most one in the open disk such that \(gH=Cb\) as germs and \(g(0)=e^{-T_C(H)}\).
For clarity, take \(v=\log^+|f|\) on the circle away from its finite singularities and set
\[
 h(z)=\int\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}v(\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta),
 \qquad g=I e^{-h},\qquad b=f e^{-h}.
\]
Since \(v\ge0\), the first function \(g\) has modulus at most one.
For \(b\), first perform this construction for \(f(rz)\) with \(r<1\), using the maximum principle and continuity as in the [multiplication estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#compression).
The boundary values of \(\log^+|f(r\zeta)|\) converge in \(L^1\) to \(v\) as \(r\uparrow1\), by the [integrable domination at boundary poles](Yin_C_G0345_v613.md#boundary).
The corresponding analytic integrals converge on compact subsets, giving \(|b|\le1\) on taking the limit.
The value of \(g(0)\) follows from \(h(0)=\int v\,d\mu\).

Write \(s_1<\cdots<s_k\) for the positions of the selected coefficients.
Compress multiplication by \(g\) to the same \(k\) monomials on input and output, obtaining \(D_{ij}=[X^{s_i-s_j}]g\), with negative indices giving zero.
This matrix is a contraction by Parseval, since multiplication by \(g\) has norm at most one and discarding coefficients cannot increase the norm.
It is triangular with determinant \(g(0)^k\), so its least singular value is at least \(g(0)^k\).
At each \(s_i\) the coefficient of \(gH\) involves only \(a_1,\ldots,a_i\), since all earlier nonzero positions are among the chosen ones.
Thus \(D(a_1,\ldots,a_k)^t\) is the vector of coefficients of \(Cb\) at the same positions and has norm at most \(C\).
This proves (1) with the number of coefficients in place of the *length* of the ordinary initial segment.
The remaining two assertions follow from integrality and, for the finite crossing, \(k\le J+1\).

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## A nonintegral quotient in a symmetric quadratic pencil

Let \(U,W\) be nonzero integral Laurent polynomials in \(X\) and suppose \(R=W/U\) is real on the unit circle away from its poles.
Suppose \(U\) does not divide \(W\) in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Then
\[
 m\bigl(U(Y+Y^{-1})+W\bigr)
   =m(U)+\int_{\mathbb T}\operatorname{arcosh}^+(|R|/2)\,d\mu
   \ \ge\ c_{\rm q}:=\frac{\log(11/4)}{22}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{arcosh}^+(x)=0\) for \(0\le x\le1\) and equals \(\operatorname{arcosh}(x)\) above one.
The equality is the quadratic Jensen formula at each regular fiber, with integrable logarithmic singularities at exceptional circle points.
If \(m(U)\ge\log2\) the inequality already holds.
Otherwise the first and last nonzero integer coefficients of \(U\) have magnitude one by Jensen's lower bound applied to \(U\) and its reciprocal after a monomial shift.
Normalize \(H=X^jR\) at zero to have a nonzero constant, so its Taylor coefficients are integral by formal division and its nonzero interior pole sum is at most \(m(U)\), again by Jensen at zero.
The failure of Laurent divisibility makes this series infinite.
Since \(\log^+(|H|/2)\le\operatorname{arcosh}^+(|R|/2)\), (1) with \(C=2,k=11\) proves the bound.

For a hyperbolic monic irreducible reciprocal \(P\), put \(u=\pm X^{-\deg(P)/2}P>0\) on the circle.
In the [rational numerator construction](Yin_C_G0345_v613.md#statement) take a nonzero \(A\) coprime to \(P\) and nonzero \(B\) with \(|B(0)|=1\).
Use \(U=uB(X)B(X^{-1})\) and \(W=A(X)A(X^{-1})-2U\).
Every root of \(P\) is a zero of \(U\) and cannot be a zero of \(W\) by reciprocity and coprimality.
Here \(m(U)=m(P)+2m(B)\) and \(\operatorname{arcosh}^+(|W/U|/2)=D(|A/B|^2/u)\) on the circle.
Thus the entire transfer cost is at least \(c_{\rm q}\), independent of degree, denominator and coefficient prefix.
In particular an exact [pointwise numerator](Yin_C_G0345_v607.md#statement) with \(B=1\) forces **the original measure** \(m(P)\ge c_{\rm q}\).
The estimate excludes approaching zero by the bound on the whole specialization; it allows an individual irreducible factor of a positive-measure specialization to have much smaller measure.

<a id="upper"></a>
## The complementary upper tail

With the same \(u\), let \(V=2-u\), \(V_1=V\), \(V_2=V^2-2\) and \(\ell'=\int D(u)\,d\mu\).
One has \(|V_i|\le2e^{iD(u)}\) for \(i=1,2\) on the circle: inside \([-2,2]\) use \(V=2\cos t\), and outside use \(|V|=2\cosh D(u)\).
Thus after monomial shifts the two integral polynomials have \(T_2(V_i)\le i\ell'\).
If \(20\ell'<\log(5/4)\), (1) with \(J=4\) shows that each has squared coefficient sum at most four.
The nonconstant symmetric \(V\) has squared sum \(E\ge2\).
If \(E=2\), then \(V=\pm(X^s+X^{-s})\) and \(2-V=u\) has a circle zero, impossible.
If \(E=3\), \(V\) has the same two outer coefficients and constant \(\pm1\), hence \(V^2-2\) has coefficients of magnitude two at both \(\pm s\).
If \(E=4\), \(V^2-2\) has constant \(E-2=2\) and nonzero outer coefficients.
Both cases contradict the second energy bound.
Consequently every such input satisfies \(\ell'\ge\log(5/4)/20\).
The same proof works for any nonconstant strictly positive symmetric integral Laurent polynomial \(u\).

<a id="layers"></a>
## The coefficient rows at any fixed width

For a bivariate integer Laurent polynomial \(F=\sum_{j=0}^d U_j(X)Y^j\), with \(U_0 U_d\ne0\), suppose \(m(F)<\log2\).
Jensen gives \(m(U_d)\le m(F)\) and therefore unit extreme coefficients for \(U_d\).
For each nonzero row normalize \(H_j=U_j/U_d\) by a monomial at zero and put \(C_j=\binom dj\).
At each regular \(X\) the elementary symmetric functions of the \(d\) fiber roots give
\[
 \log^+(|H_j|/C_j)\le m_Y(F(X,Y))-\log|U_d(X)|.
\]
Its pole sum is at most \(m(U_d)\), so \(T_{C_j}(H_j)\le m(F)\).
In particular at sufficiently small \(m(F)\) depending only on \(d\), every quotient is a Laurent polynomial of squared coefficient sum at most \(C_j^2\), by (1) and its finite crossing criterion.
The factor \(F/U_d\) then has at most \(\sum_j C_j^2=\binom{2d}{d}\) monomials.
The [bounded-monomial gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives either zero or a positive lower bound depending only on \(d\) for the measure of this factor.
The [earlier bounded-width theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) already gives the qualitative gap for a nontorsion horizontal component with any number of base variables.
Here the coefficient compression supplies explicit quadratic constants and a restriction on each individual row quotient, including when some measure is carried by the base factor.
