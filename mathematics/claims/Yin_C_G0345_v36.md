# A sparse delay-one test for the unrestricted Lehmer gap

<a id="statement"></a>
## Irreducible units with one exceptional real conjugate

Write \(F^*(z)=z^{\deg F}F(1/z)\) for polynomials with nonzero constant and \(M\) for multiplicative Mahler measure.
Fix any function \(B:\mathbb N\to[1,\infty)\) tending to infinity.
For \(m\ge2\) put \(R_m=2^{3m+1}\), \(A_m=2^{3(m+1)^2}\), and \(\delta_m=2^{-m^2}\).
Consider the following class of monic irreducible integer polynomials \(f\) of degree \(2m\) and constant one.

* For one of \(C=\pm A_m\), the coefficients of \(f+Cz^{m-1}(z-1)\) have absolute sum at most \(B(m)\) and are supported on multiples of a power of two \(n\ge\sqrt m\) dividing \(m\).
* The skew part is \(f-f^*=Cz^{m-1}(1-z^2)\).
* Exactly \(m\) roots have modulus greater than \(R_m\), exactly \(m-1\) have modulus less than \(1/R_m\), and the last is real in \((1-\delta_m,1)\).

The unrestricted existence of a positive Lehmer gap is equivalent to the existence of a uniform positive lower bound for \(\log M(zf+f^*)\) whenever this logarithm is positive and \(f\) is in this class.
The equivalence holds for each choice of \(B\), however slowly it increases.
For any root \(\alpha\) of any member of the class, \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for every integer \(k\ge1\).

<a id="central"></a>
## A prescribed pair of middle coefficients for any reciprocal target

Let \(P=P^*\) be any monic integer polynomial of degree \(2m\ge4\) with \(M(P)\le2\) and \(P(1)\ne0\).
No simplicity condition is needed on its roots.
Put \(R=R_m,\ A=A_m=(4R)^{m+1}\), take \(C=-A\operatorname{sgn}P(1)\), and set
\[
          f=P-Cz^{m-1}(z-1).
\]
The two corrected coefficients give the stated skew part and the exact identity \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).
In particular the measure of the return is exactly \(M(P)\).
This specializes the [delay-one polynomial correction](Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) to a constant trace polynomial.

For \(|z|=r\), the factorization of \(P\) gives \(|P(z)|\le2(1+r)^{2m}\).
On \(|z|=R\) the central term has modulus at least \(A R^m/2>2(2R)^{2m}\).
On \(|z|=1/R\) its modulus is at least \(A R^{1-m}/2>2\cdot4^m\).
Rouché's theorem gives \(m\) roots of \(f\) inside the first circle and \(m-1\) inside the second, counted with multiplicity.
At \(b=1-\delta_m\), Bernoulli's inequality gives \(b^{m-1}\ge1-(m-1)\delta_m>1/2\).
Since \(A\delta_m/2>2\cdot4^m\), \(f(b)=P(b)+C b^{m-1}\delta_m\) has the sign of \(C\).
The value \(f(1)=P(1)\) has the opposite sign.
There is exactly one root \(\beta\) in the annulus between the two circles, and it is simple and lies in \((b,1)\).

Every nonconstant monic integer factor of \(f\) contains an exterior root, since its constant is a nonzero integer and an interior-only factor has constant modulus less than one.
If such a factor \(g\) omits \(\beta\), then
\[
       |g(1)|>(R-1)(1-1/R)^{m-1}
             \ge (R-1)2^{1-m}>2\cdot4^m\ge |P(1)|.
\]
The first inequality uses one exterior root and at most \(m-1\) small roots, with all remaining exterior distances from one greater than one.
This contradicts \(g(1)\mid P(1)\) in any monic integral factorization.
Both factors of a proper factorization would therefore require the simple root \(\beta\), proving irreducibility by Gauss's lemma.
This gives a degree-only version of the [factor test at one](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v136.md#factor).
For a seed in the stated class, every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) fixing \(\beta^k\) must send \(\beta\) to a conjugate of the same modulus.
The exceptional annulus contains only \(\beta\), so separability gives \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\beta^k)]=1\).
Conjugating proves the same assertion for every \(\alpha\).

<a id="reduction"></a>
## Arbitrary integer inputs and arbitrarily slow coefficient growth

If the unrestricted gap fails, take nonzero primitive integer polynomials of positive log measure tending to zero.
By multiplicativity and the bound \(M\ge1\) on nonconstant integer polynomials, one may select an irreducible factor \(D\) of positive log measure tending to zero and normalize its leading coefficient to be positive.
Eventually \(M(D)<\sqrt2\), so its leading and absolute constant coefficients are one, using \(M(D)\ge |D(0)|\) and excluding the factor \(z\).
The polynomial \(Q=D(0)D D^*\) is then monic reciprocal of degree \(2h\) with \(h=\deg D\), has \(Q(1)\ne0\), and has \(1<M(Q)=M(D)^2<2\).
For a target already monic reciprocal of degree \(2h\) with nonzero value at one and \(1<M(Q)\le2\), use that target directly and preserve its measure exactly.

For each such fixed \(Q\) choose \(n\) a power of two with \(n\ge h,\ m=nh\ge2\), large enough that \(B(m)\ge\sum_j|[z^j]Q|\).
Then \(n\ge\sqrt m\) and \(P(z)=Q(z^n)\) has all the same coefficient norms and the same Mahler measure as \(Q\), since the product of the exterior moduli of the \(n\)-th roots of each root equals its original exterior contribution.
It also has \(P(1)=Q(1)\ne0\).
The central construction now gives a seed in the class, with \(M(zf+f^*)=M(Q)>1\).
Conversely \(zf+f^*\) itself is a nonzero monic integer polynomial, so any sequence of its positive log measures tending to zero disproves the unrestricted gap.
The degree dilation and large signed coefficient guarantee membership in the restricted class; neither construction supplies a lower bound or an escaping sequence.
