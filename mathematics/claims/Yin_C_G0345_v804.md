# Small degree of the Mahler measure at a least-degree reciprocal witness

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior signatures below the quadratic degree scale

Fix \(M,A>0\), and let \(x\) be a reciprocal unit of degree \(d\), of least degree among all algebraic numbers with positive logarithmic Mahler measure at most \(m(x)\), where \(m(x)\le M\).
Put \(\delta=\exp(m(x))\) and \(D_{\rm ab}=[\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}(\delta):\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}]\).
Write \(u,b\) for the number and size of the [canonical blocks](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) of inverse pairs.
For all sufficiently large \(d\) depending only on \(M,A\), if \(D_{\rm ab}\le d^A\), then \(\delta\), up to a root of unity, is a product supported on at most \(\lfloor A\rfloor\) inverse pairs with exponents of absolute value at most two.
The exterior set itself has the block description proved below.

In particular, for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), if \(D_{\rm ab}\le d^{2-\varepsilon}\), exactly one of the following occurs for sufficiently large \(d\).

* A single exterior root \(r\) is real, \(\delta=|r|\), and the other inverse pairs are neutral.
* Precisely one block is exterior at every label and every other block is neutral.
  Relative to one canonical orientation in that block, precisely one exterior choice is reversed.
  Its root \(r\) is real and \(\delta=r^2\).
  The degree-\(u\) block field \(E\) is totally real, and the degree-\(2u\) oriented block field \(K\) has two real embeddings and \(u-1\) pairs of complex embeddings.

In both cases \([\mathbb Q(\delta):\mathbb Q]=d\).
Thus along any sequence of these least-degree witnesses of unbounded degree outside the two signatures, \(D_{\rm ab}\ge d^{2-o(1)}\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Independent block orbits of the exterior set

The [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) preserves measure and lowers degree for a nonsingleton class, so distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient.
The block estimate gives \(b=d^{1-o(1)}\), uniformly at bounded measure.
The relative degree counts the orbit of the exterior set under the commutator of the rational splitting group.
Indeed its product differs from \(\delta\) only by a rational sign, and among subsets of the same size the exterior set uniquely maximizes absolute product, as in the [stabilizer argument](Yin_C_G0345_v801.md#degree).
The commutator induces all independent alternating actions on the blocks, since the product of the alternating groups is perfect.

In the [even-inversion alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement), if a block contains \(h>0\) exterior labels, inverting even subsets gives at least \(2^{h-1}\) exterior sets on that block.
Thus \(h\le1+A\log_2 d<b/2\) for large \(d\).
Put \(t_j=h_j\) on each block.
The independent alternating groups give at least \(\prod_j\binom b{t_j}\) distinct supports, by transitivity on subsets of prescribed size.

In the oriented alternative, color every label by \(0,+,-\) according as it is neutral or exterior in one of the two canonical orientations.
On block \(j\) let \(t_j\) be \(b\) minus the largest of its three color counts.
Forget the order of the two nonzero colors separately in every block.
This quotient coloring transforms by ordinary label permutations, and its orbit is bounded above by \(D_{\rm ab}\).
On each block the alternating group realizes every coloring with the prescribed ordered counts: some color repeats, allowing correction of permutation parity.
The number modulo swapping the two nonzero colors is at least \(\binom b{t_j}/2\) if \(t_j>0\), and at least one otherwise.
Independence multiplies these bounds.

In either case \(0\le t_j\le2b/3\).
Fix an integer \(k>A\).
For large \(d\), every \(k\le t_j\le2b/3\) would give \(D_{\rm ab}\ge\binom bk/2>d^A\).
Consequently all \(t_j<k\), and with \(T=\sum_j t_j\) the orbit bound gives \(D_{\rm ab}\ge(b/(2k))^T\), using \(\binom bt\ge(b/t)^t\).
Since \(b=d^{1-o(1)}\), eventually \(T\le\lfloor A\rfloor\).
In the even-inversion case this counts all exterior roots.
In the oriented case each majority color is unique, and replacing every label by the majority color has changed exactly \(T\) labels across all blocks.
Every nonzero constant block contributes a torsion product by [least-degree descent](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement).
The corrections have exponents in \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\), giving the claimed monomial.

<a id="one"></a>
## One exceptional inverse pair

When \(A<2\), an everywhere constant coloring is impossible because \(\delta>1\).
A nonzero majority block must contain a reversed exterior root: without one, every root in its majority orientation has modulus at least one, and at least one has modulus greater than one, contradicting its torsion product.
If all majority colors are zero or the even-inversion alternative holds, precisely one root is exterior, so complex conjugation fixes it.
Otherwise the only exceptional label is the unique reversal in a single wholly exterior block.
Complex conjugation preserves this block, its unique majority orientation and its exceptional exterior root \(r\).
The absolute product on the majority orientation is one, so reversing its exceptional choice gives \(\delta=|r|^2=r^2\).
On each other block complex conjugation fixes every inverse pair and interchanges the two orientations because all its roots have modulus one and are nonreal.
Hence complex conjugation fixes all \(u\) blocks, fixes both orientations of the exceptional block, and swaps the orientations in each of the others.
The embeddings of \(E\) and \(K\) are precisely the blocks and oriented blocks by [their construction](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement), yielding the signatures.
Finally the squares of conjugates are all distinct by the torsion-quotient condition, so \(r^2\) and \(r\) both have degree \(d\).
