# Balanced prime-degree specializations of the Boyd–Mossinghoff polynomial

The author-side construction researcher for [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) developed the balanced specializations, torsion calculation, real and neutral root tests and factorization dichotomy below.
The [adjacent-exponent study](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#statement) treats the different slopes \((1,n)\) and supplies the comparison between the two limiting integrals.

<a id="statement"></a>
## The irreducibility alternative

Let \(p>1680\) be prime and set
\[
 b=420\lfloor p/840\rfloor,\qquad a=p-b,\qquad
 H_p(z)=(1-z^a)(1-z^b)(1+z^p)-z^p.                              \tag{1}
\]
Then \(H_p\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(2p\), has no cyclotomic factor, and has exactly one real exterior root, which is positive.
There are absolute constants \(c_0,c_1,c_2>0\) such that, for every sufficiently large prime \(p\), **each** monic irreducible factor \(S\) of \(H_p\) is reciprocal and satisfies
\[
 c_0\le\log M(S)\le\log M(H_p),\qquad
 \#\{\text{nonreal conjugate pairs exterior to the circle in }S\}\ge c_1p,
 \qquad
 \#\{\text{neutral inverse pairs in }S\}\ge c_2p.                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular the factor containing the positive real exterior root has that root and both growing counts simultaneously.
The full measures converge to \(\lambda=\exp I_{23}\), where \(0<I_{23}<6/25<I_{306}\) and \(I_{306}\) is the [five-term logarithmic limit](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#limit).
The value of \(\lambda\) is reported as approximately \(1.255433\) in the [Boyd–Mossinghoff comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#specialization).

The following two implications hold independently.

* Along any infinite set of primes for which \(H_p\) is irreducible, (1) gives actual prime half-degrees, one real exterior root, and linearly many nonreal exterior pairs and neutral pairs, at limiting measure \(\lambda\).
  The [prime-degree lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v306.md#statement) gives a linearly growing minimum exterior count for reciprocal nontorsion radicals of the roots on this subsequence.
* Along any infinite set of primes for which \(H_p\) is reducible, a monic irreducible factor \(S_p\) satisfies (2) and
  \[
                        \limsup M(S_p)\le\sqrt\lambda<113/100.           \tag{3}
  \]
  After passage to a subsequence the measures converge to some value in \([\exp c_0,\sqrt\lambda]\).
  The half-degrees of these factors need not be prime.

At least one of the two sets of primes is infinite; the argument leaves open whether both are infinite.
In particular, failure of eventual irreducibility implies actual examples satisfying (2) and \(M(S)<113/100\) in arbitrarily large degrees.
This construction provides a uniform positive floor on the measures of all its factors.
It does not produce measures tending to one.

<a id="balanced"></a>
## Bounds on factors in all balanced directions

The [Boyd–Mossinghoff polynomial with parameters \(2,3\)](../audits/literature/BM05/Yin_U001.md#source-quadratic-families) has the exact symmetric expression
\[
 F(X,Y)=(1+X)(1+Y)(1+XY)-XY
       =(1+X)+(1+X+X^2)Y+X(1+X)Y^2.                              \tag{4}
\]
For positive relatively prime \(a,b\), put \(p=a+b\), and take \(\epsilon,\delta\in\{1,-1\}\).
Primality of \(p\) is unnecessary in this section.
The specialization \(H=F(\epsilon z^a,\delta z^b)\) is monic reciprocal with constant one and degree \(2p\).
Its other exponents belong to \(0,a,b,p,p+a,p+b\) with the sum of the absolute values of their coefficients at most six, even in case of a collision.
Consequently each exterior root has
\[
                         \log|z|\le\frac{\log6}{\min(a,b)}.       \tag{5}
\]
Reciprocity gives the same bound for \(|\log|z||\) at all roots.

As \(p\to\infty\) through these primitive directions, the shortest nonzero perpendicular integral vector has maximum norm \(\max(a,b)\ge p/2\).
The [fixed-polynomial specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(\log M(H)\to m(F)=I_{23}\), since the four sign translations preserve Haar measure.
There are only four choices, so signs may vary with \(p\).
The [positive integral and upper bound](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#integral) and the [strict lower bound on the five-term limit](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#comparison) give
\[
                           0<I_{23}<6/25<197/800<I_{306}.         \tag{6}
\]

For all signs the Newton polygon of (4) has successive vertices
\((0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,1)\).
Its six edges are primitive binomials whose sole roots are \(1\) or \(-1\).
The [uniform factor gap for these Newton polygons](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) thus gives a fixed \(c_0>0\) with \(\log M(S)\ge c_0\) for every noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor of every specialization.
Take the minimum of the four valid constants.
All these factors are units, and (6) and [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) make them reciprocal for large \(p\), since \(e^{6/25}<13/10\) is below the plastic number.
For a fixed \(\gamma>0\), assume \(\min(a,b)\ge\gamma p\).
By (5), each such factor has at least \(c_0\gamma p/\log 6\) exterior roots, since their positive logarithms sum to \(\log M(S)\).
The full specialization has at most twelve real roots by Descartes' rule on the positive and negative axes, using its seven terms.
Hence the number of nonreal conjugate exterior pairs in **each** such \(S\) grows at least linearly in \(p\), uniformly for this whole range of directions.
In (1), \(p>1680\) gives \(b>p/4\), \(0<a-b<840\), and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) follows from \(p=a+b\) prime.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## A cyclotomic exclusion with modulus 420

We use the sign and square substitutions in the [cyclotomic-point method of Beukers and Smyth](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area).
Suppose \(F(x,y)=0\) for roots of unity \(x,y\), and let \(n\) be the exponent of the group they generate.
They generate all \(n\)-th roots of unity.
If \(n\) is odd, the Galois action of squaring gives \(F(x^2,y^2)=0\).
If \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), then \(2+n/2\) is coprime to \(n\) and gives \(F(\epsilon x^2,\delta y^2)=0\) for some signs.
If \(4\mid n\), then \(1+n/2\) is coprime to \(n\) and gives \(F(\epsilon x,\delta y)=0\) with at least one negative sign, since one coordinate has the full two-primary part of \(n\).

The following exact resultants eliminate \(Y\) between \(F(X,Y)\) and \(F(\epsilon X^e,\delta Y^e)\).
Here \(\Phi_j=\Phi_j(X)\) is the \(j\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.

| \(e\) | \((\epsilon,\delta)\) | Resultant in \(Y\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \((-,-),(-,+)\) | \(4X^2\Phi_{12}\) |
| \(1\) | \((+,-)\) | \(4X\Phi_2^2\Phi_3^2\) |
| \(2\) | \((-,-),(-,+)\) | \(4X^3\Phi_2^2\Phi_{30}\) |
| \(2\) | \((+,-)\) | \(4X^4\Phi_5^2\) |
| \(2\) | \((+,+)\) | \(4X^2\Phi_7^2\) |

For direct verification, use the three coefficients \(u=1+X,\ v=1+X+X^2,\ w=X(1+X)\) in ascending powers of \(Y\).
Set \(t=\epsilon X^e\) and \(U=1+t,\ V=\delta(1+t+t^2),\ W=t(1+t)\), so the second polynomial has coefficients \(U,V,W\) in powers of \(Y^e\).
At \(e=1\) the resultant is \((wU-uW)^2-(wV-vW)(vU-uV)\).
At \(e=2\) replace the first quadratic by the quadratic \(w^2 Z^2+(2uw-v^2)Z+u^2\), whose roots are the squares of its roots in \(Y\).
Its resultant with \(WZ^2+VZ+U\) gives the same result, by the product formula including the leading coefficient \(w^4\).
These identities give the displayed table by polynomial multiplication.

The first coordinate therefore has order dividing \(420=\operatorname{lcm}(2,3,5,7,12,30)\), and symmetry gives the same for the second.
If \(\zeta\) were a root of unity of \(F(\epsilon z^a,\delta z^b)\), then both \(\zeta^{420a}\) and \(\zeta^{420b}\) would equal one.
Coprimality implies \(\zeta^{420}=1\).
Choosing \(420\mid b\) forces the second coordinate to equal \(\delta\).
Here \(F(X,-1)=X\), and \(F(X,1)=2X^2+3X+2\) has no root of unity because such a root \(X\) would make the noninteger rational \(X+X^{-1}=-3/2\) an algebraic integer.
This excludes every cyclotomic factor for all four signs in this residue class.

<a id="real"></a>
## The real root selected by the signs

For \(z=r>1\), put \(d=r^a,\ e=r^b\).
For signs \((+,+),(+,-),(-,+)\), the quantity \(F(\epsilon d,\delta e)\) is positive.
For the positive signs use \((1+d)(1+e)(1+de)>de\); for mixed signs the two negative factors give a positive product and \(-\epsilon\delta de>0\).
For \((-,-)\), dividing the equation \(F(-d,-e)=0\) by \(de\) and writing \(s=\log r\) gives
\[
           8\sinh(as/2)\sinh(bs/2)\cosh(ps/2)=1.                 \tag{7}
\]
The left side increases strictly from zero to infinity for \(s>0\).
Therefore (1) has exactly one real positive exterior root and it is simple.
At \(z=-r\) the effective signs become \((\epsilon(-1)^a,\delta(-1)^b)\); since \(p\) is odd, exactly one flips.
Thus (1) has no negative exterior root.
The untwisted polynomial in the same family has no real exterior root of either sign.
Reciprocity supplies the corresponding interior counts.

<a id="neutral"></a>
## A fixed arc in which every root is neutral

For the signs in (1), set \(k=a-b\), an odd integer between one and 839.
The circle identities used here hold at complex angles as well.
Writing \(z=e^{i\theta}\), \(y=\cos(p\theta/2)\) and \(t=\cos(k\theta/2)\), direct expansion of (4) gives
\[
 z^{-p}H_p(z)
  =1+2\cos(p\theta)-2\cos(a\theta)-2\cos(b\theta)
  =4y^2-4ty-1.                                                    \tag{8}
\]
Let \(h=1/4000\) and choose \(\theta=u+iv\) with \(|u-\pi|\le h\).
By (5), at any root one has \(|v|\le4\log6/p\).
Since \(k\) is odd,
\[
 |t|^2=\cos^2(ku/2)+\sinh^2(kv/2)
 \le (839h/2)^2+\sinh^2(1678\log6/p)<1/4
\]
uniformly for all sufficiently large \(p\).
At a root, (8) gives \(y\ne0\) and \(|y|\le(|t|+\sqrt{|t|^2+1})/2<(\sqrt5+1)/4<\sqrt3/2\).
In particular \(|\sin(pu/2)|>1/2\), since \(|\cos(pu/2)|\le|y|\).
Using \(t=y-1/(4y)\) gives \(|\Im y|\le|\Im t|\).
Thus
\[
 \tfrac12\sinh(p|v|/2)
       \le|\Im y|\le|\Im t|\le\sinh(k|v|/2).
\]
Since \(\sinh x/x\) is increasing for \(x>0\), \(p>2k\) forces \(v=0\).
This proves that every root in the fixed arc has modulus one.

By the exterior bound in the [balanced specialization](#balanced), every irreducible factor \(S\) has degree \(d\gg p\), whereas its logarithmic measure is bounded above by \(\log 7\).
The [angular estimate for unit polynomials](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) applied to the triangular function supported on \(|u-\pi|\le h\) gives a normalized average \(h/(2\pi)+o(1)\), uniformly in these factors.
For all sufficiently large \(p\), at least \(dh/(4\pi)\) roots of each factor lie in the arc, as the function takes values in \([0,1]\).
They are neutral by (8); at most two belong to any one neutral inverse pair.
This proves the last bound in (2).

For an elementary count on the entire polynomial, the expression in (8) is \(-1\) at every odd grid angle \((2j+1)\pi/p\), and it is \(3-4\cos(2\pi aj/p)\) at \(2j\pi/p\).
As \(j\) runs modulo \(p\), the residues \(aj\) run over all residues.
A proportion \(1-\arccos(3/4)/\pi+O(1/p)\) of these even grid points give positive values.
Each has two disjoint adjacent open half-grid intervals with opposite endpoint signs.
This gives at least \((1-\arccos(3/4)/\pi)p-O(1)\) neutral pairs for \(H_p\) itself.
The fixed-arc proof gives the separate lower bound for each factor.

<a id="dichotomy"></a>
## What reducibility would imply

At every reducible prime in the large-\(p\) range, \(H_p\) has at least two noncyclotomic irreducible factors counted with multiplicities, by the exclusion above.
Choosing one of minimum measure gives \(M(S_p)\le M(H_p)^{1/2}\).
The uniform floor and the two linear conjugate counts apply to this chosen factor as well.
Along increasing reducible primes the limit for the full polynomial proves (3), using \(\log(113/100)\ge 13/100-(13/100)^2/2>3/25\).
Along increasing irreducible primes, (1) itself supplies the required mixed prime-degree signature with the smaller limit.
Without controlling the split between these two sets of primes, one cannot claim that infinitely many prime half-degrees realize that limit, or that any measure below \(113/100\) actually occurs.
