# Turning a sparse cyclotomic multiple into one with actual boundary periods

<a id="transfer"></a>
## A support bound independent of the exponents

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic with no root of unity, and suppose \(F=PC\) has \(k\ge2\) nonzero coefficients for a monic cyclotomic product \(C\), allowing repetitions and \(C=1\).
There is a monic cyclotomic multiple \(P C'\) with at most \(k2^{H_k}\) terms in which
\[
                (X-1)C'=\prod_{h\ge1}(X^h-1)^{m_h},\qquad m_h\ge0.
\]
Here \(H_k\) depends only on \(k\).
A possible choice is
\[
 H_k=(k-1)(1+2^{\pi(k)})^{\,2^{\pi(k)}\mathcal B_k^{\,k-1}},
\]
where \(\mathcal B_k\) is the number of partitions of a set of \(k\) elements and \(\pi(k)\) counts primes at most \(k\).
The construction adds at most \(H_k\) binomial factors to the given multiple, with no bound on their exponents.
It uses the actual polynomial and permits changing its cofactor.

For irreducible uncompressed reciprocal \(P\) of degree \(2g\ge4\), meaning \(P\notin\mathbb Z[X^j]\) for \(j>1\), the resulting cofactor therefore has an [open hyperbolic realization capping to \(P\)](Yin_C_G0883_v9.md#volume).
The minimum support with admissible boundary cycles is bounded on a family if and only if the unrestricted minimum cyclotomic-completion support is bounded there.
The bound does not give a linear comparison or produce an initial economical multiple for a shrinking record.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## Describing all the torsion roots and their multiplicities

On roots of unity write \(I_h(\zeta)=\mathbf1_{\zeta^h=1}\).
The finitely supported integer combinations of the \(I_h\) form a ring of functions under pointwise operations since \(I_h I_l=I_{\gcd(h,l)}\).
The functions \(I_h\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Z\), by evaluating at a primitive root of a maximal order in a finite index set and proceeding downwards.
For a combination let its norm be the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients in this basis.
The norm is submultiplicative by the gcd identity.

We show that the torsion-root multiplicity function of \(F\) is such a combination with norm at most \(H_k\).
Multiplication by \(X\) preserves these multiplicities and allows all exponents \(e_i\) of the resulting polynomial \(G=\sum_{i=1}^k b_iX^{e_i}\) to be positive.
For \(1\le l\le k-1\), multiplicity at least \(l\) is equivalent to
\[
             \sum_i b_i e_i^j\zeta^{e_i}=0,\qquad 0\le j<l .
\]
There is no multiplicity at least \(k\) by the nonsingular Vandermonde matrix on the distinct \(e_i\).

Partition the summands of each vanishing equation into minimal vanishing blocks.
Every block has at least two indices.
Let \(g\ge1\) be the gcd of the nonzero exponent differences within all the blocks of all \(l\) partitions.
By [Mann's theorem for rational coefficients](Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#orders), the order of \(\zeta^{e_i-e_s}\) divides \(K=\prod_{p\le k}p\) for each such difference.
Bézout's identity then gives \((\zeta^g)^K=1\).
For a given tuple of partitions, and a chosen index \(s\) in each block, its equations after removing \(\zeta^{e_s}\) involve only \(\omega=\zeta^g\).
The \(j\)-th block equation is \(\sum_{i\ \mathrm{in\ block}} b_i e_i^j\omega^{(e_i-e_s)/g}=0\).
All coefficients are rational, so these equations hold for one primitive root of order \(q\mid K\) precisely when they hold for every such root.
Conversely every solution of these block equations gives all \(l\) original vanishings, even if some chosen block has a smaller vanishing subsum.
Consequently the locus of multiplicity at least \(l\) is a union of at most \(N_l=2^{\pi(k)}\mathcal B_k^{\,l}\) sets of the form \(\operatorname{ord}(\zeta^g)=q\) with \(q\mid K\).

Each such set has indicator \(\sum_{e\mid q}\mu(q/e)I_{eg}\), of norm at most \(2^{\pi(k)}\).
Inclusion-exclusion and submultiplicativity give the norm bound \((1+2^{\pi(k)})^{N_l}-1\) for the union indicator.
Summing these indicators over \(l\) proves the claimed bound for the multiplicity function.

<a id="correction"></a>
## Removing the negative cycle counts by sparse multiplication

The torsion multiplicities of \(C\) equal those of \(F\), as \(P\) has none.
Add \(I_1\) to their function to represent the multiplicities of \((X-1)C\) and denote the resulting signed coefficients by \(b_h'\).
Adding \(I_1\) cannot increase the sum of the negative parts \(v_h=\max(0,-b_h')\), so \(\sum_h v_h\le H_k\).
Take \(C'=C\prod_h(X^h-1)^{v_h}\).
The multiplicities of \((X-1)C'\) now agree with those of \(\prod_h(X^h-1)^{b_h'+v_h}\) at every root of unity.
Both polynomials are monic and cyclotomic, which proves their equality.
There is at least one cycle since \((X-1)C'\) vanishes at one.
The support of \(PC'\) is at most \(k\prod_h(1+v_h)\le k2^{H_k}\).

For a given input one can compute the actual \(v_h\) by [inverting the multiplicities](Yin_C_G0883_v8.md#cycles) before constructing \(C'\).
This gives a potentially smaller support bound \(k\prod_h(1+v_h)\) and the exact number \(1+\operatorname{ord}_{X=1}C+\sum_h v_h\) of boundary cycles.
It treats arbitrary period lengths, while the general available volume estimate still involves the support of \(P\).
