# Frobenius orbits forcing irreducibility at every dilation of selected Salem seeds

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound on the degrees of all factors after substitution

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), with nonzero constant term and degree \(d\).
Let \(\lambda\) be a rational prime such that the reduction of \(P\) has a root of exact order \(m\ge2\) and multiplicity \(u\).
For \(h\ge1\) with every prime divisor dividing \(m\), put
\[
   f=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{mh}(\lambda)}{\operatorname{ord}_m(\lambda)} .
\]
Then every irreducible factor \(T\) of \(P(X^h)\) has degree \(dt\), where
\[
                   \frac{f}{\gcd(f,u)}\mid t.                         \tag{1}
\]
The order hypothesis gives \(\lambda\nmid mh\).
If \(f=h\) and \(u\) is prime to \(h\), the entire substitution is irreducible.
For arbitrary \(f,u\) each such factor has logarithmic Mahler measure \(t\,m(P)/h\), where \(m(P)\) denotes the logarithmic measure of \(P\).

To see this, a root \(\alpha\) of \(T\) generates an extension of \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^h)\), whose degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(d\).
Writing the relative degree as \(t\), the multiset of \(h\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) consists of \(t\) copies of the roots of \(P\).
The monic polynomial with these powered roots is computed by the resultant of \(T(Y)\) and \(X-Y^h\) in \(Y\), with \(T\) monic.
Reduction thus preserves the powered multiset identity with all multiplicities.
Every preimage of the specified order-\(m\) root has exact order \(mh\), since for every \(p\mid h\) the order before powering must have valuation \(v_p(m)+v_p(h)\), and the other prime valuations are unchanged.
Frobenius on such a source has orbit size \(\operatorname{ord}_{mh}(\lambda)\), and its image has size \(\operatorname{ord}_m(\lambda)\).
Each occurring source orbit contributes \(f\) times its multiplicity to the chosen target.
Consequently \(f\mid tu\), proving (1).
When (1) forces \(h\mid t\), the degree bound \(t\le h\) makes \(T\) the entire substitution.
Taking the sum of the positive parts of the logarithms of the conjugate moduli in the same characteristic-zero multiset identity gives the factor measure.

<a id="progression"></a>
## Two auxiliary characteristics for both signs

Take the integer Salem family \(G_a=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\), \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7,\ \epsilon=\pm1\).
Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of its exterior root \(r\), obtained by removing the [linear cyclotomic factors](Yin_C_G0345_v711.md#specialization).
For every \(24\mid n\) and every \(a\equiv59\bmod145\), the polynomial \(P(X^{2^i3^j})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for all \(i,j\ge0\), for both signs.
In particular the largest positive field radical index \(R\) is prime to six by the [positive-unit factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement).

For the plus sign use \(\lambda=5\), and for the minus sign use \(\lambda=29\).
In either case \(a\equiv-\epsilon\bmod\lambda\), so that the reduction of \(G_a\) is \((X+\epsilon)(X^n-1)\).
Each primitive root of order 24 has multiplicity \(u=\lambda^{v_\lambda(n)}\), as the power of \(X^{n/u}-1\) accounts for all inseparability.
Removing linear factors at \(1,-1\) leaves that multiplicity unchanged.
For both primes the order modulo 24 is two, and \(\lambda^2-1\) is respectively 24 or 840, each with valuations three at two and one at three.
For \(p=2,3\) the binomial expansion gives
\[
 v_p(\lambda^{2k}-1)=v_p(24)+v_p(k),\qquad k\ge1 .
\]
Indeed taking a \(p\)-th power adds one to the valuation at each step, including squaring since the base is one modulo eight, while a power prime to \(p\) preserves it.
Thus for \(h=2^i3^j\) the least exponent giving one modulo \(24h\) is \(2h\).
Applying (1) with \(f=h\) proves the assertion even when \(n\) is divisible by arbitrarily large powers of 5 or 29.

<a id="sieve"></a>
## Unbounded seeds with no loss of measure at any dilation

For every sufficiently large integer \(j\) one can effectively find an integer \(a_j\) divisible by \(6^j\) such that at \(n_j=24j\) both signs give irreducible \(P(X^k)\) for every \(k\ge1\).
Consequently the unique noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(G_{a_j}(X^k)\) has logarithmic Mahler measure exactly \(\log r\), which tends to infinity with \(j\).
Along this sequence the [older divisor bound](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) \(\log r/H(a_j)\) tends to zero.

Set \(Q=25\cdot29^2\cdot6^j\) and choose \(A\in[1,Q]\) by the Chinese remainder theorem with \(A\equiv0\bmod6^j,\ A\equiv4\bmod25,\ A\equiv30\bmod29^2\).
Then \(A\equiv59\bmod145\).
Among \(a=A+Qb,\ 1\le b\le K=256Q\), the primes 5 and 29 never divide any of \(a-1,a,a+1\) to order two.
For any other prime \(v\ge5\), the three forbidden classes modulo \(v^2\) exclude at most \(3(K/v^2+1)\) choices.
Only \(v\le\sqrt{Q(K+1)+1}\) can occur, so the total number lost is at most
\[
 3K\sum_{m\ge5}m^{-2}+3\sqrt{Q(K+1)+1}
 \le \tfrac34 K+3\sqrt{Q(K+1)+1}<K.
\]
Here the sum is bounded by the integral of \(x^{-2}\) from four.
Take any surviving \(a_j\).
For \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{i=-1,0,1}((a+i)/\operatorname{rad}(a+i))\), the prime support of \(H(a_j)\) is contained in \(\{2,3\}\), and \(6^{j-1}\mid H(a_j)\).
We have \(6^j\le a_j\le Q(K+1)\), so \(\log(a_j+1)=O(j)\) and \(\log\log(a_j+3)\to\infty\).
The [effective delay bound](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#statement) therefore gives \(R\mid H(a_j)\) at \(n_j=24j\) for all sufficiently large \(j\).
The two auxiliary characteristics give \(\gcd(R,6)=1\), hence \(R=1\).
The [factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v205.md#statement) now gives the assertion for every substitution.
The linear factors removed from \(G_a\) have only roots of unity after substitution, and \(a_j-1<r<a_j+1\) by the [root estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v701.md#size).
This proves both measure limits.
