# A factor gap for reciprocal sections with four terms in each block

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two independent short exponents

Let \(0<u<v,\ n>u+v\), and let \(a,b,c,\epsilon\) be signs.
Put
\[
 A(X)=1+aX^u+bX^v+cX^{u+v},\qquad
 F(X)=A(X)+\epsilon X^n X^{u+v}A(1/X).
\]
Write \(n=L H\) with \(L=2^{v_2(n)}\) and \(H\) odd, and put \(\operatorname{ord}_1(2)=1\).
For each \(\rho>0\), there is a positive lower bound depending only on \(\rho\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure of every positive-measure integral divisor of \(F\) whenever \(\operatorname{ord}_H(2)\ge\rho H\).
In particular, a fixed finite set of primes supporting \(n\) gives a uniform factor gap, whatever the ratio \(v/u\) or the common divisors of the three exponents.
The conclusion also holds when the factor of \(n\) supported outside that finite set is bounded, since its prime divisors then belong to another fixed finite set.

More precisely, on any hypothetical sequence of irreducible factors with positive log measures tending to zero, their degrees \(d\) eventually satisfy
\[
                  d\ \ge\ 2^{-5} L\operatorname{ord}_V(2)
\]
for any sequence of divisors \(V\mid H\) with bounded numbers of distinct prime divisors.

<a id="scales"></a>
## The size and dimension of the short powers

If \(c=ab\), then \(A=(1+aX^u)(1+bX^v)\) and its reversal is a signed copy, so all roots of \(F\) are roots of unity.
Take \(c=-ab\), let \(x\) be a root of one of the proposed irreducible factors and put \(m=\log M(x)>0,\ D=n+u+v\).
All roots are algebraic units, and the [sparse factor bound](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#dob06-u001) gives \(d/D\ll m\to0\).
The [separated monomial or binomial bound](Yin_C_G0345_v169.md#statement) first gives \(u/D\to0\) by the gap after the constant term.
Applied to the initial \(1+aX^u\), it gives \((v-u)/D\to0\) as well.
Thus \(u+v=o(n)\).

Set \(Y=ax^u,\ Z=bx^v,\ A_0=1+Y+Z-YZ,\ B_0=-1+Y+Z+YZ\).
The equation becomes \(x^n=G=-\epsilon ab A_0/B_0\).
A common zero of numerator and denominator would give \(YZ=1\) and \(Y+Z=0\), forcing \(x\) to be torsion.
At an infinite place both blocks have absolute value at most \(4\max(1,|x|)^{u+v}\), and their coefficients are integral at every finite place.
The projective height of their ratio gives \((n-u-v)h(x)\le\log 4\), so both short powers have heights tending to zero.

Pass to a subsequence with signs fixed and with either fixed or pairwise distinct ratios \(v/u\).
In the fixed case write \(u=ir,\ v=jr\) with fixed coprime \(0<i<j\).
Use the base projective line with coordinate \(W=x^r\) and substitute \(Y=aW^i,\ Z=bW^j\).
The base points are generic since \(h(W)>0\) tends to zero.
In the distinct-ratio case use the base \((\mathbf P^1)^2\) with coordinates \(Y,Z\).
For any nonzero integer pair \((i,j)\), the character \(Y^iZ^j\) can be torsion only when \(iu+jv=0\), which occurs at most once.
The small base points are thus strict on the torus.
[Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) gives Haar measure on the two unit circles for their complex conjugate orbits, also along every subsequence.
A fixed proper closed subset over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and its finitely many Galois conjugates have intersection of Haar measure zero with this torus, by the one-variable zero bound and Fubini.
Consequently the base points are generic in the surface.

<a id="cover"></a>
## A simple divisor on either base

On the surface \(A_0=(1+Y)+(1-Y)Z\) has a simple prime divisor because \(1+Y\) and \(1-Y\) are coprime over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
It is not a divisor of \(B_0\), since the simultaneous zero equations have just two torus solutions.
On the line it also suffices to find one simple zero of the numerator at which the denominator is nonzero.
If \(i,j\) have opposite parity, changing \(W\) to \(-W\) if necessary makes the two signs of \(W^i,W^j\) agree.
For the two positive signs the ordered coefficients of \(1+W^i+W^j-W^{i+j}\) have one change of sign and the polynomial crosses zero on the positive axis.
For the two negative signs \(1-W^i-W^j-W^{i+j}\) is strictly decreasing there and crosses zero.
In the first case Descartes' rule counts the unique positive zero with multiplicity one, and the derivative is strictly negative in the second case.
At either zero \(Y+Z\ne0\), precluding a common zero.
If both \(i,j\) are odd, then \(j\ge3\).
The reduction of the numerator modulo two is \((1+W^i)(1+W^j)\), with a simple zero at any primitive \(j\)-th root in \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\).
It lifts to a simple zero in an unramified dyadic extension.
A common zero in characteristic zero would have \(W^{4i}=W^{4j}=1\), hence \(W^4=1\), whose reduction is one.
This proves the required simple divisor in every case.

On either base let \(\xi\) be its Gauss point at two, normalized by \(v_\xi(2)=1\).
There \(G\) reduces to one and \(v_\xi(G^2-1)=2\).
Indeed \(A_0^2-B_0^2=4(1-YZ)(Y+Z)\), both displayed factors having nonzero reduction, and \(B_0\) is a Gauss unit.
This remains true on the line since \(i,j\) are positive and distinct.

<a id="residue"></a>
## Fixed and increasing odd primes at a single place

If the degree estimate failed along a subsequence, pass again so that the prime divisors of \(V\), in increasing order, are each constant or tend to infinity, with fixed total count.
Let \(t\) be the product of the constant primes, with empty product one.
Normalize the projective base in the extension \(U^t=G\).
The simple divisor just proved gives geometric irreducibility and degree \(t\) by Eisenstein.
The normalization maps finitely to the base, and above its smooth torus locus where \(G\) is a unit the cover is étale.
The points with \(U=x^{n/t}\) therefore give lifts, and genericity on the base implies genericity above by finiteness.
Use the pullback of the standard metric on \(O(1)\) on the line or the product metric on \(O(1,1)\) on the surface.
The bundle is ample and semipositive, the variety height is zero, and the point heights tend to zero by the estimates on the base coordinates.
[Yuan's theorem and the height and measure normalizations](../audits/literature/Yua08/Yin_U001.md#surface-finite-pullback) give weak convergence of the conjugate measures to the normalized curvature measure, both on the base and above.

The source limit is supported on the fibre of the single base Gauss mass because pushing the orbit measures forward repeats the base conjugates equally.
Over the completed geometric Gauss field, \(U^t-G\) splits into \(t\) simple linear factors by reduction to \(U^t-1\) and Hensel lifting.
The resulting \(t\) analytic points have equal mass.
For example, choose as comparison sets the full fibres over the Galois orbits of the generic base sequence used above.
Every choice of one orbit of lifts from each set has the same limiting measure by genericity and equality of heights.
Hence the normalized counting measures on the full sets also converge to it, by bounding the discrepancy for each continuous test function by the greatest discrepancy among their orbits.
The full sets are invariant under rotation of \(U\) by \(t\)-th roots of unity, which transitively permutes the fibre over \(\xi\).

Choose the branch where \(U\) reduces to a primitive \(t\)-th root.
An open neighborhood imposes that reduction and \(1<v(G^2-1)<3\).
It contains at least \(1/(2t)\) of the conjugate embeddings eventually.
On these embeddings the residue order \(\ell\) of \(x\) divides \(n\) because \(x^{2n}\equiv1\) and \(\ell\) is odd.
Since \(\bar x^{n/t}\) has order \(t\), the full \(q\)-part of \(n\) divides \(\ell\) for every \(q\mid t\).

For an increasing prime \(q\mid V\) the nontorsion unit \(x^{2n/q}\) has height \(O(1/q)\).
The [cyclotomic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v554.md#norm) gives \(d^{-1}\log|N_{\mathbb Q(x)/\mathbb Q}(x^{2n/q}-1)|\to0\).
On embeddings in the neighborhood missing the full \(q\)-part, \(x^{2n/q}\equiv1\), and raising it to the odd power \(q\) gives \(v(x^{2n/q}-1)=v(x^{2n}-1)>1\).
Thus the missing fraction tends to zero by integrality at the finite places.
After subtracting the fractions for the bounded number of increasing primes, one embedding has \(V\mid\ell\), so its residue degree is at least \(\operatorname{ord}_V(2)\).
At this same place, of ramification index \(e\), one has \(v(x^\ell-1)\ge1/e\) and \(v(x^{2L\ell}-1)=v(x^{2n}-1)<3\) by the odd complementary exponent.
Successive squarings give depth at least \(\min(2s,1+s)\) from depth \(s>0\).
After at most \(\lceil\log_2 e\rceil\) squarings the depth is at least one, and two more bring it to at least three.
In particular \(e\ge L/32\), proving the claimed degree estimate by the local degree formula.

If \(\operatorname{ord}_H(2)\ge\rho H\), the number of distinct prime divisors of \(H\) is bounded in terms of \(\rho\), since all odd prime-power totients are even and their least common multiple bounds this order.
Applying the estimate to \(V=H\) gives \(d\gg\rho n\), contradicting \(d/D\to0\) and \(D/n\to1\).
Every positive-measure integral divisor contains a positive-measure irreducible factor or has content of absolute value at least two.
For fixed prime support the [prime-power order divisibilities](Yin_C_G0345_v242.md#orders) give \(\operatorname{ord}_H(2)\gg H\) with a constant depending only on that support.
