# A uniform modulus for irreducible radicals of a unit

<a id="statement"></a>
## Minimal polynomials of all roots from one divisor of the order

Let \(x\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(F=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(e=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and \(h=h_{\rm W}(x)>0\) its absolute logarithmic Weil height.
Choose an effective \(\mu>0\) bounding below the heights of all nontorsion units in \(F\).
For any integer \(B\ge\max(4,h/\mu)\) set
\[
       J=B\lceil\log_2 B\rceil,\qquad M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)^J .
\]
For every \(g\ge1\) put \(m=\gcd(g,M)\) and \(k=g/m\).
For each root \(\beta^m=x\), the polynomial \(T^k-\beta\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
In particular, all \(k\) roots \(\gamma^k=\beta\) have the same monic minimal polynomial \(P_\beta(T^k)\) over \(\mathbb Q\), where \(P_\beta\) is the monic minimum of \(\beta\).
Every \(g\)th root of \(x\) occurs in this description.
Its degree is \(k\deg P_\beta\) and its logarithmic Mahler measure is that of \(P_\beta\).

A \(g\)th root so obtained is reciprocal and has the same degree as its square if and only if \(\beta\) is reciprocal, \(k\) is odd and \(\beta\) has the same degree as its square.
Thus one can decide both restrictions for every \(g\) by testing the roots of \(T^m-x\) only at divisors \(m\) of the single integer \(M\).

<a id="binomials"></a>
## Detecting a reducible binomial by a prime divisor or a fourth power of the variable

Let \(K\) be a number field, \(y\in K^\times\), and \(n\ge1\).
If \(T^n-y\) is reducible over \(K\), then \(y\in K^r\) for a prime divisor \(r\) of \(n\), or \(4\mid n\) and \(T^4-y\) is reducible over \(K\).
For prime \(r\), a proper irreducible factor of degree \(t<r\) gives a root \(\alpha\) of that degree and \(N_{K(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)^r=y^t\).
Bézout for \(r,t\) puts \(y\) in \(K^r\).
Conversely, an \(r\)th root in \(K\) gives a linear factor.

To prove the assertion for composite \(n\), suppose every prime divisor passes this irreducibility test and \(T^4-y\) is irreducible whenever \(4\mid n\).
First treat the power of two dividing \(n\).
The cases of exponents \(1,2,4\) follow from the assumptions.
Suppose \(s\ge2\) and \(T^{2^s}-y\) is irreducible.
Take a root \(z\), put \(t=z^{2^{s-1}}\), and suppose \(z\) is a square in \(K(z)\).
Taking the norm to \(K\) shows that \(-y\) is a square in \(K\).
Thus \(K(t)\), with \(t^2=y\), contains a square root of \(-1\).
Since \([K(t):K]=2\), the minimum of \(z\) over \(K(t)\) is \(T^{2^{s-1}}-t\).
The norm to \(K(t)\) shows that \(-t\) is a square there and hence that \(t\) is a square there.
This contradicts irreducibility of \(T^4-y\), since a square root of \(t\) would belong to the quadratic field \(K(t)\).
Thus \(T^2-z\) is irreducible and the field degrees multiply to give the next full power of two.

Now adjoin the odd prime factors of \(n\) one at a time, including repetitions.
If \(T^a-y\) is irreducible over \(K\) with root \(z\), its norm is \((-1)^{a+1}y\).
If \(z\) were an \(r\)th power in \(K(z)\) for a remaining odd prime \(r\), the norm would put \(y\) in \(K^r\), as either sign is an \(r\)th power.
The prime test over \(K(z)\) and the tower formula give the full degree \(ar\) for a root of \(T^{ar}-y\).
This proves the assertion.

<a id="loss"></a>
## The number and size of divisions at which the degree decreases

Fix any \(\gamma^g=x\).
At a divisor \(a\mid g\) let \(y=\gamma^{g/a}\), so \(y^a=x\) and \(F\subseteq K=\mathbb Q(y)\).
Write \(l=[K:F]\le a\).
The unit \(v=N_{K/F}(y)\) satisfies \(v^a=x^l\), so it is nontorsion and
\[
                    \frac1B\le\frac{\mu}{h}\le\frac la\le1 .
\]
Begin with \(a=1\).
If \(T^{g/a}-y\) is reducible, the preceding argument supplies a prime \(r\mid g/a\) with \(y=\theta^r\) in \(K\), or a divisor \(4\mid g/a\) with \(T^4-y\) reducible.
In the prime case \(\theta\) is a unit and \(N_{K/F}(\theta)^{ar}=x^l\).
Its norm has positive height \(lh/(ar)\le h/r\), whence \(r\le h/\mu\le B\).
Take \(s=r\) in this case and \(s=4\) in the second case.
The polynomial \(T^s-y\) is reducible and every root has degree at most \(s-1\) over \(K\), independently of the original choice of \(\gamma\).
Replace \(a\) by \(as\), using the corresponding root \(\gamma^{g/(as)}\).
The ratio \(l/a\) decreases by a factor at most \((s-1)/s\le1-1/B\).

After \(J\) such divisions the ratio would be at most \((1-1/B)^J<\exp(-J/B)\le1/B\), a contradiction.
At termination \(T^{g/a}-y\) is irreducible over \(K\) and \(a\) is a product of fewer than \(J\) positive integers at most \(B\).
Therefore \(a\mid M\) and \(a\mid m\).
For \(\beta=\gamma^{g/m}\), the field \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\) lies between \(K\) and \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), since \(\beta^{m/a}=y\).
The two degrees up this tower are at most \(m/a\) and \(g/m\), respectively, and their product is the full \(g/a\), so both are maximal.
In particular \([\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(\beta)]=k\).
The equality of minimal polynomials and the surjectivity onto the possible \(\beta\) follow by taking any \(k\)th root of each \(\beta\).

<a id="square"></a>
## Reciprocity and degree under squaring

Substitution \(T\mapsto T^k\) preserves Mahler measure, since each conjugate of \(\beta\) gives all its \(k\)th roots.
The list of conjugates of \(\gamma\) is closed under inversion exactly when the list for \(\beta\) is.
For any nonzero algebraic number the degree decreases under squaring precisely when its negative is a conjugate.
In the degree-two case the two roots over the square are the number and its negative, and any conjugation carrying one to the other fixes the square.
At even \(k\), \(-\gamma\) belongs to the same fiber above \(\beta\).
At odd \(k\), \(-\gamma\) is a conjugate precisely when \(-\beta\) is one, by the full substituted minimum.
This gives the announced test.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## One modulus for all bounded degrees and measures

If \(C\ge1\), \(e\le2C\), and the logarithmic Mahler measure \(eh<2\), one may take the same
\[
                  B(C)=\max\{4,\,1+\lceil(\log(6C))^3\rceil\}
\]
for every \(x\) in this range.
Indeed every nontorsion unit in \(F\) has some degree \(2\le f\le e\).
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives height greater than \(2/(f\log^3(3f))\), so \(\mu=2/(e\log^3(3e))\) is available for the whole field.
Then \(h/\mu<(\log(3e))^3\le(\log(6C))^3\).
This supplies an effective common modulus for the [radical condition of the compressed graphs](Yin_C_G0345_v474.md#progressions) whenever their units meet the displayed degree and measure bounds.
