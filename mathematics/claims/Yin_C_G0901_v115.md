# A quantitative gap when any conjugate monomial or radical has few conjugates on one closed side of the circle

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound in terms of the witness alone

For a nontorsion algebraic unit \(x\), count its distinct conjugates in the closed regions
\[
   P(x)=\#\{\sigma:|\sigma x|\ge1\},\qquad Q(x)=\#\{\sigma:|\sigma x|\le1\},
        \qquad k(x)=\min(P(x),Q(x)).
\]
Each neutral conjugate contributes to both counts.
There is a positive uniform Lehmer gap for all units \(w\) whose divisible conjugate group \(D(w)\) contains a nontorsion \(x\) with \(k(x)\) at most any prescribed bound.
The degree and measure of \(x\) are unrestricted.
This includes arbitrary units all but a bounded number of whose conjugates are strictly inside the circle, or strictly outside it.

In fact for \(m(w)=\log M(w)>0\) sufficiently small and any such \(x\), there is a compressed reciprocal unit \(z\) among the [successive block products of \(w\)](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent), with \(m(z)\le m(w)\) and
\[
              d=\deg z\ll k(x)(\log(3k(x)))^{212}.                       \tag{1}
\]
All implied constants are absolute.
In particular \(k(x)\to\infty\) uniformly over the entire group as \(m(w)\to0\).
As \(k=k(x)\to\infty\), uniformly over the original inputs and witnesses, one has
\[
                 m(w)\ \ge\ \bigl(\tfrac14-o(1)\bigr)
                     \left(\frac{\log\log k}{\log k}\right)^3 .           \tag{2}
\]
No lower bound for \(w\) with all possible witness counts unbounded is asserted by the fixed-count gap.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Removing a leading summand while preserving both closed counts

Write \(V_{z_i}=W_i\oplus V_{z_{i+1}}\) at a nonterminal step of the small-measure chain, with \(W_i\) the leading absolutely irreducible module and all the summands pairwise inequivalent; at the final step \(V_{z_s}=W_s\).
Any \(x\in D(w)\) gives a class with a nonempty set of active summands, those with nonzero component.
First replace \(x\) by its [torsion-class product](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree).
This preserves its active summands and measure and does not increase either closed count, since all conjugates inside a torsion class have the same modulus.
The resulting unit has splitting field the kernel field of its class span. [Kernel field of a compressed unit](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#fields)
If \(i\) is its first active index, this field is contained in the splitting field of \(z_i\), since its class span lies in \(V_{z_i}\).

Suppose at least one later summand is active.
Then \(i\) is a nonterminal oriented step.
In the splitting field of \(z_i\) take \(N\) to be the normal subgroup fixing the splitting field of \(z_{i+1}\).
It acts trivially on \(V_{z_{i+1}}\) and has zero fixed space on \(W_i\).
Indeed it contains the supported alternating permutations of each individual oriented block: these fix every half product and therefore the compressed next unit and its conjugates.
The ordinary alternating actions have no fixed vectors on the respective coordinate-sum-zero spaces. [Supported permutations](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles) · [Oriented block module](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#oriented)
The average over \(N\) consequently projects onto the later part.

Take the product of the distinct members in the \(N\)-orbit of the current unit.
Its class equals the orbit size times the indicated average of the original vector, and therefore retains exactly the later active summands.
In particular it is nontorsion.
Normality of \(N\) partitions the full conjugate set into such orbits of equal size, whose products give all the conjugates of the new unit, possibly with repetitions.
If a product has modulus at least one, that orbit must contain at least one original conjugate of modulus at least one.
Disjointness bounds the number of such products, even before removing repetitions, by the original \(P\).
The same argument gives the bound for \(Q\).
The sum of positive log moduli also cannot increase when one multiplies the members of each orbit.
Compress again if needed, preserving the resulting class span and both inequalities for the counts.
Repeating this finite operation produces a nontorsion unit \(y\) with class in \(W_j\) alone, where \(j\) is the last active index of the original class.
Both \(P(y)\le P(x)\) and \(Q(y)\le Q(x)\) hold, and this construction does not require \(m(x)\) to be small.

<a id="pure"></a>
## Counting in the last active block representation

Put \(z=z_j\), \(d=2ub\) with \(u\) blocks of \(b\) inverse pairs.
The vector space generated by the orbit of \([y]\) is \(W_j\), of dimension \(t\le ub\).
In the independent signed case the coordinates are the ordinary signed coordinate lines; in the oriented case use the images of the coordinate vectors in the blockwise sum-zero spaces.
If \([y]\) lies on such a coordinate line in \(W_j\), its orbit consists of \(2ub\) vectors in opposite pairs, as proved by the [orbit argument for every chain summand](Yin_C_G0901_v113.md#rank).
The multiset of log moduli at actual embeddings is symmetric about zero, since each orbit vector occurs with the same multiplicity.
Thus both closed counts are at least \(ub\).

Otherwise the orbit size is at least \(u b(b-1)/2\). [Noncoordinate orbit count](Yin_C_G0901_v84.md#orbit)
The [height and largest-log inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v114.md#projection) gives
\[
             m(y)\ge\frac{\deg y}{2t}\max_\sigma|\log|\sigma y||
                       \ge\frac{b-1}{4}\max_\sigma|\log|\sigma y||.
\]
Since \(m(y)\) is both the sum of the positive log moduli and the absolute sum of the negative ones, there must be at least \((b-1)/4\) conjugates of each strict sign.
The maximum on the right is positive by Kronecker.
It follows in both cases that \(k(x)\ge k(y)\ge(b-1)/4\).

All chain degrees tend uniformly to infinity as \(m(w)\to0\) by coefficient finiteness and \(0<m(z)\le m(w)\).
At the fixed upper bound one for \(m(z)\), the [block estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) gives \(d\ll b(\log(3d))^{212}\).
Together these facts first imply \(k\to\infty\), then \(\log(3d)\ll\log(3k)\) and (1).
For the middle implication, at sufficiently large \(d\) the logarithm of the polylogarithmic factor is less than half \(\log d\); bounded remaining degrees are absorbed by the constants.
The [Voutier estimate](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) applied at \(d\) and the monotonicity of \(\log\log d/\log d\) for large \(d\) yield (2), since the logarithm of the upper bound in (1) is \(\log k+O(\log\log k)\).
Inputs with \(m(w)\) bounded below by the fixed smallness cutoff automatically satisfy (2) for large \(k\).
