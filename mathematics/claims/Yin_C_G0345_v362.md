# The uniform cofactor cost of repeated vanishing on prescribed residue classes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A prime range and a degree window for varying inputs

Fix an integer \(k\ge2\) and \(0<\delta<1\).
Let \(p\) be prime and \(P\) the monic minimal polynomial of any nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(d\le p^{1-\delta}\).
Write \(h=m(P)=\log M(P)>0\), and put
\[
          a=\lfloor p^{\delta/4}\rfloor,\qquad
          b=\lfloor p^{1-\delta/4}\rfloor,\qquad D=p(b+1).
\]
Prescribe any \(k\) distinct exponent classes modulo \(p\).
Consider nonzero integral multiples \(F\) of \(P^a\) occupying exactly those classes with degree at most \(D\).
In \(F/P^a\) take \(R\) to be the product of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors other than \(x\), with their multiplicities and positive leading coefficients.
For all sufficiently large \(p\) these sets of witnesses are nonempty and
\[
                   \frac{\min_F m(R)}{a p h}
                         =\frac1k+O_{k,\delta}(p^{-\delta/4}).            \tag{1}
\]
This is uniform in the unit, its degree and measure, and the choice of the \(k\) classes.
It remains true when the \(k\) rows must have no common nonconstant factor in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
No condition on the real or complex exterior roots is needed.
The repeated vanishing is part of the assertion.

<a id="power"></a>
## Powering above the original degree

For every nonzero algebraic number \(\alpha\) of degree \(d<p-1\), putting \(\beta=\alpha^p\) gives \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
Indeed let \(e=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\beta)]\le d<p\), and \(c=N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(\beta)}(\alpha)\).
Then \(c^p=\beta^e\).
Choose integers \(u,v\) with \(ue+vp=1\).
The element \(\gamma=c^u\beta^v\) in the smaller field has \(\gamma^p=\beta\).
If \(\alpha\ne\gamma\), the larger field would contain the primitive \(p\)-th root \(\alpha/\gamma\), contradicting \(d<p-1\).
In our range \(d<p-1\) for all sufficiently large \(p\).
Thus the monic minimal polynomial \(Q\) of \(\beta\) has the distinct \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\) as its roots and \(m(Q)=p h\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## An integral determinant that detects repeated roots

Suppose \(F=\sum_{j=1}^{l}G_j\) has \(l\le k\) nonzero rows \(G_j=x^{r_j}U_j(x^p)\) with \(0\le r_j<p\).
Write \(E=x\,d/dx\), and take the determinant with row index \(i=0,\ldots,l-1\) and columns indexed by \(j\):
\[
      \det(E^iG_j)=x^{\sum_j r_j}W(x^p),\qquad W\in\mathbb Z[X].
\]
It is nonzero.
The highest monomial in each column has original exponent \(n_j=\deg G_j\), with the \(n_j\) distinct modulo \(p\).
At the highest total exponent the coefficient is the product of the leading coefficients times the Vandermonde determinant on the \(n_j\).
This also covers \(l=1\).

Assume \(P^t\mid F\), where \(t\ge\max(1,l-1)\), and let \(c\) be the order at \(X=\beta\) of the common gcd of the nonzero rows \(U_j\).
Every column factor \(G_j\) is divisible by \(A=(x^p-\beta)^c\).
Leibniz's rule expresses the rows of the derivative matrix of \(A(G_j/A)\) as a lower triangular transformation of the derivative rows of the quotients, with diagonal entries \(A\).
Hence the determinant acquires \(A^l\).
In the remaining determinant replace one column by the sum of all columns.
The derivatives of \(F/A\) in that column vanish at \(\alpha\) to orders at least \(\max(0,t-c-i)\).
Since \(x\mapsto x^p\) is unramified at \(\alpha\), this gives
\[
             \operatorname{ord}_{\beta}W
                    \ \ge\ lc+\max(0,t-c-l+1)
                    \ \ge\ t+(l-1)c-l+1 .
\]
By rationality and monicity \(Q^{\,t+(l-1)c-l+1}\mid W\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\).

Write \(T\) for the number of terms of \(F\).
If \(F\) has degree at most \(D\), the coefficient sum of \(W\) is at most
\[
              l!(1+D)^{l(l-1)/2}\|F\|_1^l .
\]
Indeed \(E^i\) multiplies each coefficient of exponent \(n\) by \(n^i\), and removing a monomial or undoing power substitution leaves the coefficient sum unchanged.
A multiple vanishing at \(\alpha\ne0\) has \(T\ge2\).
The [sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(\log\|F\|_1\le m(F)+(T-2)\log2+\log T\).
Comparing upper and lower measures of \(W\) proves the finite estimate
\[
 \bigl(t+(l-1)c-l+1\bigr)p h
   \le l\,m(F)
     +l((T-2)\log2+\log T)
     +\log(l!)+\tfrac12l(l-1)\log(1+D).                                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(l=2,t=1,c=0\) the left side vanishes.
This identifies the missing derivative in trying to apply (2) directly to the multiplicity-one problem.

<a id="jets"></a>
## An integral construction with prescribed derivatives

For each prescribed residue use a row of degree at most \(b\) in \(X\).
There are \(n=k(b+1)\) available coefficients, with original exponents at most \(D\).
For vectors in \([0,H]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n\), consider the first \(t\) divided derivatives at \(\alpha\), of orders \(j=0,\ldots,t-1\).
For a monomial \(x^e\), the \(j\)-th such value is \(\binom ej\alpha^{e-j}\) when \(e\ge j\) and zero otherwise.
All the values are algebraic integers.
At a conjugate \(\alpha_i\) their absolute values are bounded by
\[
             nH(1+D)^j\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^D .
\]
In the real embeddings and both coordinates of one representative of each complex pair, partition each coordinate interval into pieces of length at most \(1/2\).
Distinct values of an algebraic integer cannot share a box, by the integral norm of their difference, as in the [single-value argument](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#upper).
The full vector of \(t\) values therefore has at most
\[
         (6nH)^{dt}(1+D)^{dt(t-1)/2}\exp(tD h)
\]
distinct outputs.
If \(n>dt\), choose \(H\) as the integer part of the exponential of
\[
          \frac{tD h+dt\log(6n)+\tfrac12dt(t-1)\log(1+D)}{n-dt}
\]
plus one.
The \((H+1)^n\) choices of coefficients now exceed the upper bound on the number of outputs.
Subtract two vectors with identical outputs to obtain a nonzero integer polynomial \(F_0\) supported on the allowed classes, divisible by \(P^t\) since the characteristic is zero.
Its coefficient sum gives
\[
   m(F_0)
    \le \frac{tD h+dt\log(6n)+\tfrac12dt(t-1)\log(1+D)}{n-dt}
                        +\log(2n).                                       \tag{3}
\]
Initially some rows might vanish or share a common factor.

<a id="uniform"></a>
## Losing at most one copy when the rows are made coprime

For \(t=a+1\), \(dt/n=O_k(p^{-\delta/2})\), and \(t\,dt/n=O_k(p^{-\delta/4})\).
For \(d\ge2\), [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h>2/(\log(3d))^3\).
Degree one supplies no nontorsion algebraic units.
In particular \(1/h=O((\log p)^3)\) uniformly throughout the range.
As \(D/n=p/k\), the first term involving \(h\) on the right of (3) is
\((t/k+O_k(p^{-\delta/4}))p h\).
The two terms from the boxes are \(O_k((dt/n)(1+t)\log p)\), and the final term is \(O_k(\log p)\).
Consequently
\[
                        m(F_0)\le (t/k+o_{k,\delta}(1))p h.              \tag{4}
\]
For all witnesses of degree at most \(D\) on these classes, \(T\le k(b+2)\).
The additive terms outside \(l\,m(F)\) in (2) are \(O_k(b+\log D)=o_{k,\delta}(p h)\).

Apply (2) to \(F_0\).
If \(l<k\), its leading lower coefficient in units of \(p h\) is at least \((t-l+1)/l\), which exceeds \(t/k+o(1)\) for large \(p\).
Thus all \(k\) rows occur.
At \(l=k\), comparison with (4) yields \((k-1)c\le k-1+o_{k,\delta}(1)\), hence \(c\le1\) by integrality.
Divide all rows by their primitive integral polynomial gcd and divide out any scalar content.
Gauss's lemma gives an integral polynomial of no larger measure or degree, on the same prescribed rows.
It still contains \(P^{t-c}\), hence \(P^a\).
This proves existence for both witness classes.
For each fixed degree bound, the minimum extra measure is attained, since only finitely many primitive \(R\) of bounded degree and bounded measure are possible by the bounds on their elementary symmetric coefficients.

For a lower bound we may normalize any witness to be primitive, so \(m(F)=a h+m(R)\).
By (2) with \(t=a,\ l=k\) and \(c\ge0\), it satisfies
\[
               (a-k+1-o_{k,\delta}(1))\,p h/k\ \le\ m(F).
\]
The constructed primitive witness satisfies \(m(F)\le (a+1+o_{k,\delta}(1))\,p h/k\).
Subtracting \(a h\) and dividing by \(a p h\) proves (1).

<a id="finite"></a>
## A finite comparison for each original polynomial

For instance fix \(k=2,\ \delta=1/2\), prescribe \(0,1\) and for all sufficiently large \(d\) choose a prime \(d^2\le p\le2d^2\).
Such a prime exists by [Bertrand's postulate](../audits/literature/Erd32/Yin_U001.md#erd32-u001).
Here \(a=\lfloor p^{1/8}\rfloor\) and the witness degree is \(O(d^{15/4})\).
If \(C(P)\) denotes the minimum in (1) divided by \(ap\) for this choice, then
\[
                         C(P)=(1/2+O(d^{-1/4}))m(P).
\]
The comparison uses a single prime in a prescribed interval for each input and its error is independent of the input polynomial.
Uniform positive growth of \(C(P)\) on the nontorsion units of large degree would give a Lehmer gap, since bounded degree has a positive gap by finiteness at bounded measure.
Conversely a positive Lehmer gap gives this growth.
The [multiplicity-one two-class rate](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#statement) concerns a different witness requirement and fixes \(P\) before taking a prime limit.
The new finite comparison supplies no positive absolute lower bound for \(C(P)\).
