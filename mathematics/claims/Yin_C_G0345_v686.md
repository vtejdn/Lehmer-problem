# A square-root bound for exceptional integer seeds across all delays

<a id="statement"></a>
## An estimate uniform over delays and substitutions

For \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7,\ \epsilon=\pm1\), let \(r>1\) be the exterior root of \(G(X)=X^{n+1}-aX^n+\epsilon(aX-1)\).
Write \(P\) for its Salem minimal polynomial and \(R_{a,n,\epsilon}\) for the largest \(l\ge1\) such that \(r^{1/l}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
Set \(H(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{j=-1,0,1}((a+j)/\operatorname{rad}(a+j))\) as in the [integer-seed bound](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#statement).
Let \(\mathcal B\) be the set of \(a\) for which \(R_{a,n,\epsilon}\nmid H(a)\) for some \(n\ge7\) and some sign.
With an absolute constant \(C\), for all sufficiently large \(X\),
\[
       |\mathcal B\cap[3,X]|
          \le \sqrt X\exp\!\left(C\sqrt{\log X}\,(\log\log X)^2\right).
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular this set has natural density zero, with no delay restriction.

Let \(D_*(a)\) denote the infimum of the logarithmic Mahler measures of all monic noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(G(X^h)\), over \(n\ge7\), both signs and every integer \(h\ge1\).
Outside \(\mathcal B\),
\[
                        D_*(a)\ge \frac{\log(a-1)}{H(a)} .              \tag{2}
\]
For every \(0<s<1\) and \(0<t<1\) the upper density of seeds with \(D_*(a)<t\log a\) is \(O_s(t^s)\).
For any positive \(f(a)=o(\log a)\), \(D_*(a)/f(a)\) tends to infinity in natural density.
There is also a set of seeds of natural density \(\prod_p(1-3/p^2)>0\) on which \(P(X^h)\) is irreducible for all the delays, signs and substitutions.
A fixed seed always has the [positive all-delay gap](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#statement).
The density statements leave possible small-measure subsequences on thin sets of varying seeds.

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## An exponent prime equal to the delay

The [delayed trace test](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#traces) implies \(p^e\mid H(a)\) for every odd \(p<n\) with \(p^e\mid R_{a,n,\epsilon}\).
The [two-adic calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#radicals) gives the same divisibility at \(p=2\) for every \(n\ge7\), because it uses only traces through index six.
Only primes \(p\ge n\) could violate the divisibility.

If \(p=n\) is prime, it too satisfies the divisibility.
Suppose \(p^e\mid R_{a,p,\epsilon}\) with \(e\ge1\), and let \(G^{[p]}\) denote the monic polynomial obtained by raising the roots of \(G\) to their \(p\)-th powers.
The [characteristic-polynomial congruence](Yin_C_G0345_v206.md#congruence) applied to the minimum of the positive \(p^e\)-th root of \(r\) gives \(P^{[p]}\equiv P\bmod p^{e+1}\).
The cyclotomic cofactor consists of roots from \(\{1,-1\}\) by the [torsion classification](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots).
Odd powering fixes them, so \(G^{[p]}\equiv G\bmod p^{e+1}\).

The trace formula at \(j=p=n\) gives \(s_p(G)-s_1(G)=a(a^{p-1}-1-\epsilon p)\).
If \(p\mid a\), its valuation equals \(v_p(a)\), whence \(p^{e+1}\mid a\) and \(p^e\mid H(a)\).
If \(p\nmid a\), we will exclude the radical altogether.
For a root \(z\) put \(x=z^p\).
Then \(z=(ax+\epsilon)/(x+\epsilon a)\).
The denominator cannot vanish since \(a^2\ne1\).
This inverse function distinguishes the \(p+1\) distinct roots, giving the monic formula
\[
            G^{[p]}(X)=X(X+\epsilon a)^p-(aX+\epsilon)^p .
\]
The coefficients at \(X^j\) for \(j=2,3,4\) vanish in \(G\) and must therefore vanish modulo \(p^2\) in this formula.
Using \(\binom pi/p\equiv(-1)^{i-1}/i\bmod p\) for \(0<i<p\), the coefficient at \(j\) gives
\[
                     (j-1)(a^2)^{j-1}+j\epsilon\equiv0\pmod p .
\]
Indeed its two binomial coefficients are \(\binom p{j-1}\) and \(\binom pj\), and \(a^{p-1}\equiv1\bmod p\).
The first congruence gives \(a^2\equiv-2\epsilon\).
The other two would require \(p\mid8+3\epsilon\) and \(p\mid20\), impossible for \(p=n\ge7\).
Thus every exceptional seed has a prime field radical with prime \(p>n\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting the remaining prime radicals

Take an exceptional \(a\le X\), one such delay and sign, and put \(\beta=r^{1/p}\in\mathbb Q(r)\).
The element \(\beta\) is a Salem number of the same degree \(d\le n+1\) as \(r\).
Indeed it generates the same field and is an algebraic unit with a unique exterior conjugate and neutral conjugates, as in the [field-radical argument](Yin_C_G0345_v680.md#substitution).
Since \(r<a+1\), its logarithmic measure is less than \(\log(X+1)/p\).
[Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) now yields
\[
              n<p<\tfrac12 z\log^3(3p),\qquad z=\log(X+1),
\]
using \(d\le n+1\le p\).
Consequently \(p=O(z\log^3(2z))\) with an absolute implied constant.
To see this, \(v/\log^3(3v)\) increases for \(v\ge8\), and at \(v=C_0 z\log^3(2z)\) its ratio to \(z\) tends to \(C_0\) as \(z\to\infty\).

For fixed \(n,p,\epsilon\), count all Salem numbers \(\beta\le B=e^{z/p}\) through degree \(D=n+1\) using the [uniform count in the degree and exterior bound](Yin_C_G0345_v685.md#statement).
Their number is at most
\[
         D e^{zD/(2p)}\exp\!\left(C_1\sqrt{D\log(2DB)}\right).
\]
Here \(D\le p\) makes the first exponential at most \(\sqrt{X+1}\), and \(D\log(2DB)=O(z\log^4(2z))\) by the bound for \(p\).
Each \(\beta\) determines at most one seed for these parameters, since \(r=\beta^p>1\) and
\[
                    a=\frac{r^{n+1}-\epsilon}{r^n-\epsilon r}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero.
Summing over the two signs and at most \(O(z^2\log^6(2z))\) choices of \(n,p\) absorbs all polynomial factors into the displayed error in (1).

<a id="measures"></a>
## Least factor measure across all delays

The [field radical and factor formula](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#radicals) bounds each noncyclotomic factor measure from below by \(\log r/R_{a,n,\epsilon}\).
Outside \(\mathcal B\), all these indices divide \(H(a)\) and \(r>a-1\), giving (2).
The [distribution of \(H(a)\)](Yin_C_G0345_v684.md#distribution) assigns finite moment of order \(s\) to its limiting law for each \(0<s<1\).
Since \(\log(a-1)\ge\tfrac12\log a\) at \(a\ge3\), (2) and Markov's inequality bound the upper density in the statement by \(O_s(t^s)\), after adding the zero-density set \(\mathcal B\).
For any fixed \(K>0\) and \(t>0\), \(Kf(a)<t\log a\) eventually, so letting \(t\downarrow0\) proves the stated divergence in density.
The seeds with \(H(a)=1\) outside \(\mathcal B\) have the product density in the statement.
There the radical index is one at every delay and the same factor formula gives irreducibility.
