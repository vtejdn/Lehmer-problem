# Periodic sets of powers realize the supremum of relation-free densities

<a id="statement"></a>
## Concatenating finite blocks without losing their residues

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nonzero algebraic integer \(\alpha\), with logarithmic Mahler measure \(m>0\), and let \(k\ge1\).
For a binary polynomial of degree less than \(N\), call its image modulo \(P^k\) its residue.
Let \(s_0\) be the number of conjugates of \(\alpha\) on the unit circle, counted among all complex embeddings.
There is an explicit \(C=C(P,k)\ge0\) such that for every \(N\ge1\) one may take
\[
          g=\left\lceil\frac{C+ks_0\log N}{m}\right\rceil,\qquad T=N+g.
\]
For every finite list of block residues \(r_i\), the single residue \(\sum_i X^{iT}r_i\bmod P^k\) determines the ordered list when its length is specified.
The separator \(g\) is independent of \(N\) when \(s_0=0\).

Write \(A_k(N)\) for the maximum size of a subset of \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) whose binary subsets have distinct residues, and \(D_k(P)=\lim A_k(N)/N\).
Such an exponent set will be called admissible.
Repeating any admissible set in successive intervals of length \(T\) gives a periodic set all of whose finite subsets remain admissible.
Consequently
\[
   \frac{A_k(N)}{N+g}\ \le\ D_k(P)\ \le\ \frac{A_k(N)}{N},
   \qquad
   D_k(P)=\sup\{\text{densities of periodic admissible sets}\}.          \tag{1}
\]
Together with the [degree-independent density bound](Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement), this gives an equivalent form of the unrestricted Lehmer gap.
It asks whether there is an absolute \(\delta>0\) such that each nontorsion algebraic unit admits a periodic set of exponents of density at least \(\delta\) on which all finite binary subset sums in that unit are distinct.
The period may depend on the unit.

<a id="separation"></a>
## An integral derivative of the last contributing block

Put \(B_k(t)=\sum_{h\ge0}(1+h)^{k-1}t^h\) for \(0\le t<1\), and take
\[
 C=\sum_{|\beta|<1}\log B_k(|\beta|)
    +\sum_{|\beta|>1}\log\!\left(\frac{B_k(|\beta|^{-1})}{1-|\beta|^{-1}}\right),
\]
where \(\beta\) runs over the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Suppose two different lists give the same residue.
Represent every equal pair of block residues by the same binary polynomial, and take the differences \(R_i\) of representatives.
Each nonzero \(R_i\) has coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), degree less than \(N\), and order of vanishing at \(\alpha\) strictly below \(k\).
Let \(j\) be the least such order and \(t\) the last block index attaining it.
Write \(\mathcal D=X\,d/dX\).
Every derivative \(\mathcal D^h R_i\) with \(0\le h<j\) vanishes at every conjugate of \(\alpha\), since \(P^j\mid R_i\).
Applying \(\mathcal D^j\) to the assumed relation gives
\[
   \sum_{i\le t}\beta^{iT}(\mathcal D^jR_i)(\beta)=0
\]
at each conjugate, since higher-order blocks vanish also after this derivative.
In particular \(z=(\mathcal D^jR_t)(\alpha)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer.

If \(|\beta|<1\), the modulus of the corresponding conjugate of \(z\) is at most \(B_k(|\beta|)\) by the coefficient bound.
For \(|\beta|=1\) it is at most \(N^{j+1}\le N^k\).
For \(r=|\beta|>1\), one may use \((\mathcal D-(N-1))^j\) on each block in place of \(\mathcal D^j\), since all lower derivatives vanish at \(\beta\).
Expanding from the high end of the block gives \(|(\mathcal D^jR_i)(\beta)|\le r^{N-1} B_k(r^{-1})\), including \(j=0\).
The displayed relation and a geometric series over the earlier blocks now bound the conjugate of \(z\) by
\[
 r^{N-1} B_k(r^{-1})\frac{r^{-T}}{1-r^{-T}}
 \ \le\ r^{-g-1}\frac{B_k(r^{-1})}{1-r^{-1}} .
\]
Multiplication over the embeddings gives an upper bound \(\exp(C+ks_0\log N-(g+1)m)<1\) for the absolute norm of \(z\), contradicting its integrality.
This proves injectivity, including when arbitrary representatives of the original residues are used since the sum depends only on their classes.

<a id="rates"></a>
## Effective finite approximations

The upper bound in (1) follows from subadditivity of \(A_k(N)\).
The injection gives the lower bound by repeating a maximizing set.
The relative error of these two bounds tends to zero, proving the supremum identity.
The quantities \(A_k(N)\) are obtainable by finite enumeration and integer polynomial division.
An effective upper bound on \(g\) follows by isolating the conjugates, testing exact equality of \(\beta\bar\beta\) with one, and rounding the elementary upper estimates upwards.
Thus (1) also computes \(D_k(P)\) to arbitrary prescribed accuracy for each input \(P,k\).

Let \(h_N\) be the Shannon entropy in natural logs of the residue of \(N\) independent binary digits with a fixed common law, or the logarithm of the number of their possible residues.
For either choice the limit \(h=\lim h_N/N\) exists by subadditivity.
One has \(h_N/(N+g)\le h\le h_N/N\).
Indeed the residue carried by \(l\) separated blocks has entropy \(l h_N\) by injectivity and independence, and adding the independent digits in the intervening gaps cannot decrease discrete entropy.
For cardinality, fixing all gap digits embeds a product of support sets; translating a finite set preserves its cardinality.
Use \(l\) periods and let \(l\to\infty\).
For ordinary scalar Bernoulli sums with real algebraic parameter in \((1,2)\), an explicit entropy approximation was already obtained by [Akiyama, Feng, Kempton and Persson, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07118).
The block argument above supplies an injective map for arbitrary multiplicity and bounds the density of sets of independently selectable exponents as well.
