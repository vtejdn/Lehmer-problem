# Two odd contact orders among arbitrary even orders at distinct positive radii

<a id="statement"></a>
## Cost of the odd contacts

Fix distinct real numbers \(c_1>\cdots>c_N>1\), put \(r_i=\log c_i\), and prescribe integer orders \(t_i\ge1\).
Suppose exactly two orders are odd, at \(a<b\), and write \(v_i=\lfloor t_i/2\rfloor\), \(S_0=\sum_i v_i r_i\), \(W=\sum_i t_i r_i\).
Let a nonzero integer graph \(U(X^n)-XV(X^n)\) vanish to order at least \(t_i\) at each \(c_i\), and let \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
Along any such sequence with \(n\to\infty\), with no restriction on width or on common row zeros,
\[
            \liminf n^{-1}\log H\ \ge\ S_0+r_b
                    = \tfrac12(W-r_a+r_b).
\]
If \(a=1\) and \(c_1\) is algebraic irrational, the lower bound improves to \(S_0+r_b+(r_1-r_2)/2\).
In particular if \(a=1,b=2\) this gives the full lower slope \(W/2\).

In the latter case suppose every \(c_i\) is a Salem number or positive real quadratic reciprocal unit, and let \(P_i\) be its minimal polynomial.
For either sign and every integer width sequence \(w(n)\to\infty\), the least row one-norm for genuine signed contacts with \(\prod_i P_i^{t_i}\) has normalized logarithm tending to \(W/2\) along all positive integers \(n\to\infty\).
Here a genuine row \(A\) has degree at most \(w\), satisfies \(\prod_iP_i^{t_i}\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_w^*(X^n)\) with \(A_w^*(Y)=Y^w A(1/Y)\), and has \(A(c_i^n)\ne0\) for every \(i\).
For each positive excess in the slope, one fixed width suffices eventually.
The list may contain arbitrarily many distinct positive powers of the same Salem or quadratic unit, including powers whose exponents are all odd.

## The columns split between consecutive nodes

Use the [reversed row equations](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#approximation) after removing common initial zero pairs, so the integer pair \(w_0=(p,q)\) is nonzero.
Write \(w_j=(u_j,v'_j)\) for the coefficient pairs.
The equations at \(c_i\) use Euler derivatives of orders \(0\le\ell<t_i\).
Their entries at index \(j\) have the common factor \(c_i^{-nj}\), multiplying the columns of powers \((-nj)^\ell\) and \(-c_i(1-nj)^\ell\).
Let \(2m=\sum_i t_i\) and list \(s_1,\ldots,s_{2m}\) as \(t_i\) copies of \(r_i\) in decreasing order of radius.
Consider the square matrix on the first \(m\) coefficient pairs and put \(E=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2m}s_\ell\lfloor(\ell-1)/2\rfloor\).
Its determinant has absolute value at least \(n^{-O(1)}e^{-nE}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Indeed the optimal assignment in the rearrangement inequality allots to each node its consecutive segment of the list \(0,0,1,1,\ldots,m-1,m-1\).
A pair of columns at one index splits between two consecutive nodes exactly at the boundaries after nodes \(a,\ldots,b-1\), where the number of preceding rows is odd.
Each maximizing permutation obeys these restrictions, and every permutation obeying them maximizes.
After factoring out \(e^{-nE}\) from all maximizing terms, their combined coefficient is the determinant of the matrix with the exponential factors removed and with zero entries wherever the indicated index cannot be allotted to the node.
It is a polynomial in \(n\).

At a given node \(i\), let \(I_i\) be the set of indices assigned to it as full pairs.
Their columns are confined to that node and independent there, by the Vandermonde matrix at the distinct numbers \(-nj,-nj+1\) for \(j\in I_i\).
The annihilating row polynomials of degree less than \(t_i\) are precisely the multiples of
\[
                   Q_i(s)=\prod_{j\in I_i}(s+nj)(s+nj-1)
\]
in that degree range.
After eliminating the full columns one therefore needs only the residual rows on the split pairs.
Nodes outside \(a,\ldots,b\) have none.
At an odd endpoint \(i=a,b\), the single row \(Q_i\) acts on its only shared index \(j\) by
\[
                   (Q_i(-nj),-c_i Q_i(1-nj)).
\]
Divided by \(n^{2|I_i|}\), this tends to \(d_{ij}(1,-c_i)\), with \(d_{ij}=\prod_{k\in I_i}(k-j)^2>0\).

An intermediate even node \(a<i<b\) has two distinct shared indices \(j_L<j_R\), separated by \(v_i\ge1\), and \(v_i-1\) full pairs.
Use the two annihilating rows \(Q_i(s)\) and \(sQ_i(s)/n\).
After the same scaling their limiting restrictions at \(j=j_L,j_R\) are \(d_{ij}(1,-c_i)\) and \(-j d_{ij}(1,-c_i)\).
The two-by-two scalar matrix on these two endpoint functionals is invertible since \(j_L\ne j_R\) and both \(d_{ij}\ne0\).
An invertible change of the two limiting rows thus isolates one direction \((1,-c_i)\) at each shared index.
At a shared index between nodes \(i,i+1\), the two resulting directions are independent since \(c_i\ne c_{i+1}\).
The limiting residual matrix is consequently nonsingular, including when there are only the two odd endpoints.
This proves nonvanishing of the maximizing polynomial for all large \(n\).
All other assignments have a fixed strictly smaller exponential rate and at most polynomial prefactors, proving the determinant bound.

## Bounds on the initial coefficient pair

Put \(D_j=\sum_{k=j+1}^m s_{2k}\) for \(0\le j<m\), and \(D_j=0\) for \(j\ge m\).
Once the preceding determinant lower bound holds, the [column replacement estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#two) gives
\[
                         |w_j|\le n^{O(1)}H e^{-nD_j}.
\]
Indeed replacement of one column at \(j<m\) by a tail column at \(h\ge m\) increases the least rearrangement cost by \(D_j+(h-m)s_{2m}\).
The last weight absorbs polynomial growth in \(h\) uniformly in the width.
The integer pair \(w_0\ne0\) then yields lower slope \(D_0\).
Counting the even positions of the ordered row list gives \(v_i\) contributions at each node, with one additional contribution at the second odd node \(b\).
Thus \(D_0=S_0+r_b\).

If \(a=1\), take \(Q(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{v_1}(s+nj)(s+nj-1)\) in the equation at \(c_1\).
The remaining terms after the initial form \(Q(0)p-c_1Q(1)q\) start at index \(v_1+1\).
For these terms \(jr_1+D_j\) is minimized at \(v_1+1\), because subsequent steps of \(D_j\) decrease by at most \(r_2\).
From the first \(2v_1+1\) entries of the row list, \(D_0-D_{v_1+1}=v_1r_1+r_2\).
Hence the initial form has absolute value at most \(n^{O(1)}H e^{-n(D_0+r_1-r_2)}\), by the coefficient estimates and the geometric tail.
Its integer inputs \(Q(0)p,Q(1)q\) are not both zero and have size at most \(n^{O(1)}H e^{-nD_0}\).
The [rational approximation bound](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#approximation) at \(c_1\) gives the asserted gain \((r_1-r_2)/2\) upon sending its positive exponent excess to zero.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Attaining the leading-pair cost

For \(a=1,b=2\) the preceding bound is \(W/2\) for every nonzero two-row graph with the required contacts.
Each selected Salem or quadratic minimum has exactly one exterior root, of log modulus \(r_i\), and each positive power preserves its degree.
Distinct exterior roots separate the powered minima, so every exponent separates the complete root list, as in the [two-node power calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#attainment).
Apply the [whole-list jet-fiber argument](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#attainment) to these minima and orders.
Its only lower-bound hypothesis concerns the whole list at each fixed width and is supplied by the unrestricted estimate just proved.
It gives genuine rows at either sign with arbitrarily small fixed slope excess, as well as padding to the prescribed diverging widths.
Together with the unrestricted lower bound this proves the limit.

The proof-development agent for Sheng Yin extended the [two-odd-node calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v447.md#two) by resolving all boundaries at which adjacent nodes take one column from the same pair.
