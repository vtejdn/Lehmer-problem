# Identifying a strict degree record with a nonzero Salem projection

<a id="statement"></a>
## The detected record is itself a signed Salem conjugate

Use total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit that first attains its positive measure at its degree \(d\) and minimizes all positive integer-polynomial measures up to \(d\).
For all sufficiently large \(d\), if \(m(w)<c_{\mathbb R}=\tfrac12\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) and \(w\) has a [nonzero Salem projection](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#statement), then \(w\) is a signed conjugate of a primitive Salem number.
In particular, under failure of the unrestricted gap, arbitrarily small strict degree records can be selected on at least one of the following two branches:

* the records are signed Salem conjugates;
* the multiplicative group generated by their conjugates, even after adjoining all roots of unity and taking all roots of products, contains no Salem number.

The second criterion has an algebraic meaning for every algebraic unit, independently of any degree-record assumption.
Some projection onto the conjugates of a primitive Salem number \(\alpha\) is nonzero exactly when that saturated group contains \(\alpha\).
In that case the splitting field of \(\alpha\) is contained in the splitting field of \(w\).

<a id="algebra"></a>
## Interpreting the inner products as algebraic membership

Take a common finite Galois field \(N\), and use \(f_v(g)=\log|g(v)|\) as in the [conditional norm formula](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#coefficient).
For a selected conjugate \(\gamma\) of \(\alpha\), let \(\rho=N_{\mathbb Q(w,\gamma)/\mathbb Q(\gamma)}w\) and let \(\tau\) invert \(\gamma\).
The projection coefficient is nonzero precisely when \(\rho/\tau\rho=\pm\gamma^a\) with \(a\ne0\).
Both norms in the ratio are products of conjugates of \(w\); an extension of \(\tau\) to \(N\) gives this also for \(\tau\rho\).
Thus a positive power of \(\gamma\) belongs to the group generated by the conjugates of \(w\), after squaring to remove a possible sign and inverting if necessary.
The group is Galois-stable, proving membership for \(\alpha\).
Every positive power of a Salem number generates the original field by its unique dominant conjugate, so the splitting field inclusion follows as well.

Conversely if that saturated group contains \(\alpha\), some positive power of \(\alpha\) is a product of integer powers of conjugates of \(w\), after removing a torsion multiplier by taking a further power.
Taking archimedean log functions expresses a positive integer multiple of \(f_\alpha\) as a linear combination of those functions.
If all the indicated projections of \(w\) were zero, simultaneous Galois invariance would make \(f_\alpha\) orthogonal to every term; this contradicts \(\langle f_\alpha,f_\alpha\rangle>0\).
For a nonprimitive Salem number use the [positive primitive generator](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units) of the same relative unit group.

<a id="record"></a>
## The possible sizes of the support at equality of degrees

At a detected record with \(d\ge6\), the [comparison for arbitrary units](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#infimum) gives \(\deg\alpha=d=2n\) and \(m(w)=\log\alpha\) for a detected primitive Salem number.
Choose an orientation in each of its \(n\) reciprocal pairs, let \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\) be the projection vector of \(w\), and let \(k>0\) be its support size.
The [orbit bounds](Yin_C_G0901_v52.md#degrees) give
\[
              2^k n/k\le 2n\quad (k<n),\qquad
              2^{n-1}\le 2n\quad (k=n).
\]
In the latter case \(n\le4\), which can be excluded at large degree.
In the former case \(k=1\) or \(2\).
For \(k=2\) the full orbit of the signed vector has at least four times as many elements as the orbit of its support, and at most \(2n\) elements.
The orbit of the support must therefore consist of exactly \(n/2\) unordered pairs covering all \(n\) labels, with each label appearing exactly once.
This is an invariant partition into pairs of the \(n\) embeddings of the Salem trace field \(B=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).

Indeed the stabilizer of the pair containing the positive trace label contains the point stabilizer with index two: transitivity gives an element sending that point to its partner, which fixes the unique pair containing both.
In the trace splitting field the two stabilizers have fixed fields \(C\subset B\) with \([B:C]=2\).
This would give \(B\) a nontrivial rational automorphism.
The [uniqueness theorem over the whole totally real base](Yin_C_G0901_v47.md#base) precludes this for sufficiently large \(n\) at the fixed bound \(c_{\mathbb R}\).
This rules out \(k=2\).

For \(k=1\), the stabilizer of the signed vector is exactly the stabilizer of the single oriented root \(\gamma\) at its nonzero coordinate: taking it to its inverse negates that coefficient.
The stabilizer of \(w\) is contained in this subgroup and its index in the full group is \(\deg w=\deg\gamma\).
Thus \(\mathbb Q(w)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\).
Transport to its real Salem embedding.
By the [classification below two](Yin_C_G0901_v44.md#units), the generator \(w\) is a signed power of the primitive Salem unit; a quadratic golden-ratio unit cannot generate this large field.
The equality \(m(w)=\log\alpha\) forces the absolute exponent to be one, proving the statement for the original embedding as well.

If the unrestricted infimum vanishes, bounded-degree finiteness gives a sequence of successively smaller positive degree records with degrees increasing to infinity.
An irreducible positive-measure factor of a first-degree minimizer has the same measure and degree, by its two minimality properties.
Eventually these factors are monic unit minima since a nonunit extreme coefficient costs at least \(\log2\).
At least one of the two alternatives in the statement occurs on an infinite subsequence.
