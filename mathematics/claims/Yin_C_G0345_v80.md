# The possible torsion angles above a real Salem measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## An exact test in the real trace field

Let \(\beta>1\) be a Salem number, \(T=\beta+\beta^{-1}\), \(B=\mathbb Q(T)\), and let \(\zeta\) be a primitive root of unity of order \(N>2\).
Put \(c=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
A complex Salem number \(\lambda\) with \(M(\lambda)=\beta\) and \(\lambda/\bar\lambda=\zeta\) exists if and only if
\[
                c\in B,\qquad (T+2)/(2+c)\in B^{\times 2}.
\]
Here a complex Salem number is an algebraic integer with exactly itself and its nonreal conjugate outside the unit circle and at least one conjugate on the circle.
We use multiplicative Mahler measure.
Whenever the test holds, the two possible values with the prescribed quotient are
\[
         \lambda=(1+\zeta)\frac{\beta+1}{(2+c)t},
                      \qquad t\in B,\quad t^2=\frac{T+2}{2+c}.
\]
They generate \(\mathbb Q(\beta,\zeta)\), of degree \(d=2[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\), divisible by \(2\varphi(N)\).
For odd \(N\) the positive square root of \(\beta\) must itself be a Salem number of degree \([\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\).
In particular no such odd order occurs over a \(\beta\) minimizing the Salem number among all degrees up to its own.
Order two, with quotient \(-1\), has the [unconditional lift \(\sqrt{-\beta}\)](Yin_C_G0345_v72.md#lift).

For every \(N>2\), the least possible degree of such a complex Salem number as \(\beta\) varies is exactly \(\max(8,2\varphi(N))\).
The least power with real value is always \(N\), and at the minimal degree one can arrange that the least power with positive real value is \(2N\).
Over all degrees \(N\ll d\log\log(3d)\), and this order of growth is attained along primorial orders.
Taking the measure into account gives \(N\ll X\log\log(3+X)\) for \(X=\min\{d,(\log\beta)(\log(3d))^{48}\}\), with an absolute implied constant.
The examples attaining the unrestricted order bound do not have bounded measure.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## The quadratic extension and the square class

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and let \(\iota\) be its automorphism sending \(\beta\) to \(\beta^{-1}\), with fixed field \(B\).
For a lift \(\lambda\) as in the statement, \(\beta=\lambda\bar\lambda\), \(\lambda^2=\beta\zeta\), and \(\lambda^{2N}=\beta^N\).
By [degree preservation of Salem powers](Yin_C_G0345_v52.md#powers), \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta^N)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\lambda)\), which also contains \(\zeta\).
An automorphism of the normal closure fixing \(\beta\) must send both factors of \(\lambda\bar\lambda\) to conjugates of modulus \(\sqrt\beta\), since that is the largest modulus and the product remains \(\beta\).
Only the two exterior roots have that modulus, and ordinary complex conjugation interchanges them.
Thus \([\mathbb Q(\lambda):K]=2\), with complex conjugation fixing precisely \(K\) in this quadratic extension.
Since \(\zeta\) is nonreal, \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)=K(\zeta)\) and \(c\in K\).
Every totally real subfield of \(K\) is contained in \(B\).
Indeed at an embedding \(\tau\) sending \(\beta\) to a unit-circle conjugate, one has \(\tau(\iota y)=\overline{\tau(y)}\) for every \(y\in K\), as can be checked on the generator \(\beta\).
If \(y\in K\) is totally real then \(\tau(\iota y)=\overline{\tau(y)}=\tau(y)\), giving \(\iota y=y\).
In particular \(c\in B\).

Now \(s=\lambda+\bar\lambda\) belongs to \(K\) and satisfies \(s^2=\beta(2+c)\ne0\).
Thus \(\iota(s)=\pm s/\beta\).
At a neutral embedding \(\tau\), the product \(\tau(s\iota(s))=|\tau(s)|^2\) is strictly positive, and \(\tau(2+c)>0\) since a primitive conjugate of \(\zeta\) cannot be \(-1\).
This forces the positive sign in \(\iota(s)=s/\beta\).
It follows that \(t=(\beta+1)/s\) is fixed by \(\iota\) and \(t^2=(T+2)/(2+c)\).
Also \((1+\zeta)s/(2+c)=\lambda\), yielding the formula.

Conversely suppose \(c,t\in B\) meet the test.
Then the displayed \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\lambda/\bar\lambda=\zeta\) and \(\lambda^2=\beta\zeta\), so it is an algebraic unit.
It lies in \(K(\zeta)\) and generates it by \(\lambda^{2N}=\beta^N\) and \(\zeta=\lambda^2/\beta\).
This extension has degree two over \(K\) since \(c\in B\), \(K\) is embedded in the reals, and \(\zeta\) is nonreal.
Each conjugate of \(\beta\) has two extensions, and the moduli of the corresponding conjugates of \(\lambda\) are the square roots of the moduli at \(\beta\).
Hence the two exterior roots are \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\), their product is \(\beta\), and there are neutral roots.
The minimal polynomial is reciprocal since any neutral conjugate and its inverse are complex conjugates.
The two signs of \(t\) exhaust the possibilities by necessity.
Since \(\mathbb Q(c)\subseteq B\), the cyclotomic degree formula \([\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(N)/2\) gives \(2\varphi(N)\mid d\).

If \(N\) is odd, take \(2j\equiv1\bmod N\).
Then \((\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j})^2=2+c\) is a square in \(\mathbb Q(c)\).
Since \(s^2=\beta(2+c)\), the positive \(\sqrt\beta\) lies in \(K\), generates \(K\) by squaring, and is an algebraic unit.
It has just one exterior conjugate, above \(\beta\), and at least one neutral conjugate, hence is a Salem number strictly smaller than \(\beta\).

<a id="sharp"></a>
## Minimal-degree examples at each order

Given \(N>2\), take \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}\) and \(F=\mathbb Q(c)\) unless \(F\) would have degree one, in which case use \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).
Thus \(f=[F:\mathbb Q]=\max(2,\varphi(N)/2)\).
There is an algebraic integer \(t\in F\) positive and arbitrarily large in the chosen real embedding and arbitrarily small in absolute value at every other embedding.
To see this, choose an integral generator \(c_0=c\) or \(\sqrt2\) of the field.
The \((H+1)^f\) sums \(\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}n_j c_0^j,\ 0\le n_j\le H\), are distinct and their other \(f-1\) embeddings lie in a box of side \(O_F(H)\).
For any fixed \(\epsilon>0\) this box is covered by \(O_{F,\epsilon}(H^{f-1})\) boxes of side \(\epsilon\).
Two sums in one small box give a nonzero integral difference with other embeddings of absolute value at most \(\epsilon\).
Its nonzero integer norm bounds its distinguished absolute value below by \(\epsilon^{1-f}\), and the sign can be chosen positive.

With \(\epsilon\) small enough, \(T=(2+c)t^2-2\) is above two at the chosen embedding and strictly between \(-2\) and two at every other one, since \(0<2+c'<4\) at every conjugate of \(c\).
The unique value above two forces \(\mathbb Q(T)=F\) by the equal multiplicities of the embeddings over each image of \(T\).
The equation \(\beta+\beta^{-1}=T\) defines a Salem number \(\beta>1\) of degree \(2f\), since \(T^2-4\) is negative at another real embedding and cannot be a square in \(F\).
The lift in the criterion now has degree \(4f\), which realizes the lower bound because a real Salem number has degree at least four.
One has \(\lambda^n\in\mathbb R\) precisely when \(\zeta^n=1\), so the least such \(n\) is \(N\).
The selected \(t>0\) makes the argument of \(\lambda\) equal to \(\pi/N\), giving least positive real power \(2N\).
In fact the value at that power is the Salem number \(\beta^N\).

<a id="size"></a>
## The order bound with and without a bound on the measure

The [classical totient estimate recorded in BV19b](../audits/literature/BV19b/Yin_U002.md#bv19b-collision-partitions) gives \(N\ll\varphi(N)\log\log(3+\varphi(N))\).
The degree divisibility already gives the first upper bound.
For the second, apply the [abelian subfield bound](Yin_C_G0345_v78.md#subfields) to \(\mathbb Q(c)\subset K\) and the generator \(\beta\).
Its distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotients by degree preservation under every positive power.
Its logarithmic measure is \(\log\beta\) and its degree is \(d/2\), so \(\varphi(N)\ll (\log\beta)(\log(3d))^{48}\).
Together with the totient estimate this proves the bound in \(X\).

Take \(N=\prod_{p\le y}p\) and use the minimal-degree examples, so \(d=2\varphi(N)\) for large \(y\).
Then \(2N/d=\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-1})^{-1}\ge\sum_{n\le y}1/n\ge\log y\).
Since \(N\le y^y\), one has \(\log\log(3d)\ll\log y\).
This proves sharpness in order of growth, even when the successful exponent is only required to be real.
The bound with \(X\) shows that at bounded measure the possible \(N\) are instead bounded by a power of \(\log(3d)\).
