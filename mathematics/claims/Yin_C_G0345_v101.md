# Removing a cofactor from a shifted product of cyclotomic polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## Transfer at the square root of the degree

Let \(A=\prod_{n\ge1}\Phi_n^{e_n}\) be any nonconstant monic product with nonnegative integer multiplicities and degree \(d\).
Write \(s=A(0)\in\{-1,1\}\), \(L(\theta)=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\), \(u=\|L\|_1/2\) in normalized angular measure, and \(G=A+aX^k\) with nonzero integer \(a\) and integer \(k\ge0\).
For \(2k\ne d\), every integer divisor \(F\) of \(G\) with \(m(F)>0\) has \(m(F)\ge\log\theta_0\), where \(m=\log M\) and \(\theta_0>1\) is the real root of \(x^3-x-1\).
This also holds at \(2k=d\) if \(s=-1\).

Suppose \(2k=d\) and write \(G=FQ\) with \(F,Q\) monic integer polynomials and \(e=\deg Q\).
Then
\[
             0\le m(G)-m(F)=m(Q)
             \le \frac ed(\log|a|+u)+\frac e{\sqrt d}\sqrt u .       \tag{1}
\]
The following consequences hold for sequences with \(d\to\infty\).

* If \(e\le K\sqrt d\) for a fixed \(K>0\), bounded \(m(F)\) forces bounded \(m(G)\).
  If \(m(G)\to\infty\) in this range, then \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\).
* For each \(B\ge0\) there is an absolute \(c_B>0\), depending only on \(B\), such that \(m(F)\le B\) and \(e\le c_B\sqrt d\) force \(Q\) to be a product of cyclotomic polynomials for all sufficiently large \(d\).
  Thus \(m(F)=m(G)\) exactly, including whenever \(m(F)\) stays bounded and \(e=o(\sqrt d)\).
* In any class of these central inputs having a uniform gap for \(m(G)>0\), the divisors with \(e\le c_1\sqrt d\) have a uniform gap for \(m(F)>0\).

For central shifts in the [classes with binomial exponents on boundedly many multiplicative intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement), the divisor gap holds for \(e\le K\sqrt d\) with every fixed \(K\).
For products with at most \(H\) distinct cyclotomic factors, it holds for \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\) for all fixed \(K,\delta>0\), and \(m(F)\) tends to infinity when the largest prime count of an occupied conductor or the largest multiplicity tends to infinity.
These applications use the respective whole-polynomial theorems.
The general comparison (1) requires no assumption that the full family of shifts has a Mahler gap.

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Sign and reciprocal roots

The coordinator supplied the inverse-pair test, the estimate from the Poisson integral and the degree splitting for the signed binomial exponents below.
The identity \(X^dA(1/X)=sA(X)\) follows factor by factor, including \(\Phi_1=X-1\).
If a nonzero root \(\alpha\) of \(G\) and its inverse both occur, substitution in their two equations gives \(\alpha^{d-2k}=s\).
Off center this forces \(\alpha\) to be a root of unity, and at the center with \(s=-1\) the equation is impossible.
Every monic noncyclotomic irreducible factor with a nonzero root in these cases is therefore nonreciprocal and satisfies [Smyth's lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number).
Nontrivial integer content and a primitive factor with leading coefficient of absolute value at least two each give \(M\ge2\), while a possible factor \(X\) has measure one.
Multiplicativity gives the asserted divisor gap, as in the [single-conductor classification](Yin_C_G0345_v99.md#reciprocity).
Reversing both signs of \(A,a\) multiplies the whole polynomial by \(-1\) and allows an overall negative leading sign for the cyclotomic product as well.
With \(a=0\) every nonconstant irreducible divisor is cyclotomic up to sign and all integer divisors have measure one.
For nonzero \(a\) the shifted polynomial cannot vanish identically because \(A\) has a nonzero circle root.

<a id="poisson"></a>
## A bound for every root from the boundary logarithm

Use the central shift \(k=d/2\).
The polynomial \(G\) is monic of degree \(d\) with constant coefficient \(s=\pm1\).
The angular mean of \(L\) is zero because \(A\) is monic with all its roots on the circle, so \(\int L^-=\int L^+=u\).
For \(z=e^{-t+i\theta}\), \(t>0\), one has
\[
       \log|A(z)|=(P_{e^{-t}}*L)(\theta),\qquad
       0\le P_{e^{-t}}\le \frac{1+e^{-t}}{1-e^{-t}}\le 1+\frac2t .
\]
The identity holds for each linear root factor by the Fourier expansion of \(\log|1-e^{i\theta}|\) and the Poisson coefficients \(e^{-|j|t}\), and summation covers all multiplicities.
In particular \(\log|A(z)|\ge -(1+2/t)u\).
At an interior root of \(G\) the same logarithm equals \(\log|a|-dt/2\).
The positive-root bound for the resulting quadratic in \(t\) gives
\[
               t\le\frac2d(\log|a|+u)+2\sqrt{\frac ud}=:\tau .        \tag{2}
\]
At an exterior root, its inverse is an interior root of \(A+s aX^{d/2}\), by reversal of \(G\).
Both signs give the same bound (2), so \(|\log|\alpha||\le\tau\) for every root of \(G\).
This argument includes an anti-reciprocal \(A\) without requiring \(G\) itself to be reciprocal.
Every monic integer divisor of \(G\) has constant coefficient of absolute value one.
Consequently \(2m(Q)\) is the sum of the absolute logarithmic moduli of its roots with multiplicities, and \(m(Q)\le e\tau/2\) gives (1).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Bounded remaining measure and exact equality

Set \(C_0=\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)\) and \(Y=m(G)+C_0\).
The [circle-root integral bound](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) gives \(Y\ge\int\max(L,\log|a|)\ge\max(u,\log|a|)\).
Thus
\[
                    m(Q)\le(2e/d)Y+(e/\sqrt d)\sqrt Y .              \tag{3}
\]
If \(e\le K\sqrt d\) and \(m(F)\le B\), then \(Y\le B+C_0+2KY/\sqrt d+K\sqrt Y\).
For \(d\ge64K^2\) use \(K\sqrt Y\le Y/4+K^2\) to get \(Y\le2(B+C_0+K^2)\).
If \(m(G)\to\infty\) instead, division of (3) by \(m(G)\) gives \(m(Q)/m(G)\to0\).

For the second assertion take \(K=1\), let \(Y_B=2(B+C_0+1)\), and set
\[
                   c_B=\min\{1,(\log2)/(16\sqrt{Y_B})\}.
\]
When \(m(F)\le B,\ e\le c_B\sqrt d\) and \(d\) is sufficiently large, (2) gives \(e\tau\le4c_BY_B/\sqrt d+2c_B\sqrt{Y_B}<(\log2)/4\).
A noncyclotomic irreducible factor of \(Q\) would have degree \(f>1\), because its monic linear factors with unit constant can only be \(X-1\) and \(X+1\).
The [house bound of Dimitrov recorded in Smy08-U001](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#house-and-its-relation-to-the-gap) would give \(f\tau\ge(\log2)/4\), contrary to \(f\le e\).
Hence \(Q\) is cyclotomic up to the empty product.

To transfer a whole-polynomial gap, suppose \(0<m(F)\to0\) along inputs with \(e\le c_1\sqrt d\).
For bounded \(d\), integer factors of degree at most \(d\) and measure at most two belong to a finite set by expansion in their roots.
On a subsequence with \(d\to\infty\), use \(m(F)\le1\) eventually to obtain \(m(G)=m(F)>0\), contradicting the specified parent gap.

<a id="mass"></a>
## The total absolute binomial exponent

For an independent bound valid with no restriction on the occupied conductors, write
\[
        A(X)=\pm\prod_{v\ge1}(1-X^v)^{b_v},\qquad
        b_v=\sum_{n:\,v\mid n}e_n\mu(n/v),\qquad S=\sum_v |b_v|.
\]
Then for every \(\epsilon>0\)
\[
                         S\ll_\epsilon (1+\|L\|_1)d^{1/2+\epsilon}.   \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the [Fourier inversion formula](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity) gives
\[
                    |b_v|\le2\|L\|_1\prod_{p\mid v}(1+1/p)
                           \ll_\eta \|L\|_1 v^\eta
\]
for each \(\eta>0\), because every sufficiently large prime has \(1+1/p\le p^\eta\).
At \(T=\sqrt d\) the indices \(v\le T\) therefore cost \(O_\eta(\|L\|_1 T^{1+\eta})\).
For \(0<\eta<1\), one has \(2^{\omega(n)}/\varphi(n)\ll_\eta n^{-1+\eta}\), since each factor \(2p/(p-1)\) of \(n2^{\omega(n)}/\varphi(n)\) is at most \(p^\eta\) for sufficiently large \(p\).
Each conductor \(n>T\) contributes to at most \(2^{\omega(n)}\) binomials and the smaller conductors contribute nothing to \(v>T\).
Hence the high indices cost at most
\[
                 \sum_{n>T} e_n 2^{\omega(n)}
                         \ll_\eta T^{-1+\eta}\sum_n e_n\varphi(n)
                         =T^{-1+\eta}d .
\]
Combining the bounds with \(0<\eta<\min(1,2\epsilon)\) proves (4).

<a id="classes"></a>
## Consequences for two classes with whole-polynomial gaps

The signed binomial expression gives another bound at an interior root of a central shift.
For \(|z|=e^{-t}<1\), a numerator factor \(1-z^v\) has modulus at least \(1-e^{-t}\), and an inverse factor has modulus at least \(1/2\).
It follows that \(|A(z)|\ge((1-e^{-t})/2)^S\).
For \(t\ge1/d\), \(1-e^{-t}\ge1/(2d)\), and for \(t<1/d\) the following upper bound is immediate since \(S\ge1\).
As in (2), apply the same estimate with scalar \(s a\) for exterior roots to obtain
\[
              |\log|\alpha||\le\frac2d(\log|a|+S\log(4d))=:\tau_S
                      \qquad (G(\alpha)=0).                          \tag{5}
\]

Fix \(h,C\) and suppose \(\{v:b_v\ne0\}\) is contained in at most \(h\) intervals of the form \([x,Cx]\).
If \(e\le K\sqrt d\) and \(m(F)\) remains bounded on a sequence with \(d\to\infty\), (3) bounds \(m(G)\) and hence \(\log|a|\) and \(\|L\|_1\).
The [binomial-exponent result for these intervals](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement) then bounds \(S\).
It follows from (5) that \(e\tau_S=O_{h,C,K,B}(\log(4d)/\sqrt d)\to0\) when \(m(F)\le B\).
The same house argument forces \(Q\) to be cyclotomic eventually.
The [whole-polynomial gap in that class](Yin_C_G0345_v98.md#statement) now gives the asserted divisor gap with parameters \(h,C,K\) by the bounded-degree and unbounded-degree alternatives above.
Also \(S\to\infty\) implies \(m(G)\to\infty\) there and \(d\to\infty\), since bounded degree admits only finitely many monic cyclotomic products by coefficient bounds.
Therefore \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\) in that regime and \(m(F)\to\infty\).

Now suppose that at most \(H\) of the \(e_n\) are positive.
Put \(E=\max_n e_n\) and \(r=\max_{e_n>0}\omega(n)\).
The [multiplicity bound](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity) gives \(E\ll_H(1+r)\|L\|_1\), and the Möbius product gives \(S\le H E2^r\).
Since \(\varphi(n)\le d\) for every occupied conductor and \(\varphi(n)\ge\omega(n)!\), we have \((1+r)2^r\log(4d)=d^{o(1)}\) as \(d\to\infty\).
Here the factorial estimate is the [prime-order bound for the conductor](Yin_C_G0345_v99.md#transfer), including \(n=1\) with \(0!=1\).
For \(e\le Kd^{1-\delta}\), (5) and \(Y\ge\max(\log|a|,\|L\|_1/2)\) give \(e\tau_S=o(1)Y\), uniformly as \(d\to\infty\) with \(H,K,\delta\) fixed.
Consequently \(m(Q)\le e\tau_S/2=o(1)Y\), and bounded \(m(F)\) forces bounded \(Y=m(F)+m(Q)+C_0\).
It then follows that \(e\tau_S\to0\), so \(Q\) is eventually cyclotomic on every such bounded sequence.
The [gap for shifts with boundedly many distinct factors](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#statement) now gives a positive divisor gap depending only on \(H,K,\delta\), including bounded \(d\) by finiteness.
If \(\max(r,E)\to\infty\), then \(d\to\infty\) and the same whole-polynomial result gives \(m(G)\to\infty\).
Dividing \(m(Q)\le o(1)Y\) by \(m(G)\) gives \(m(F)/m(G)\to1\), hence divergence of the remaining measure.
