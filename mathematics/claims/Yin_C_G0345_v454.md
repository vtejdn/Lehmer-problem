# Degree at least 74 at measure 1.068, total degrees under a fixed budget, and the prime 503

<a id="statement"></a>
## Degree and prime bounds

Every nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(1<M(P)\le267/250\) has degree at least 74.
For an algebraic unit \(c\) in the same measure interval, \([\mathbb Q(c^k):\mathbb Q]\ge74\) for every positive integer \(k\).
For \(r\ge3\) primitive irreducible integer factors with positive logarithmic measures of sum strictly below \((\log 2)/4\), their total degree is at least \(226,332,440\) for \(r=3,4,5\), respectively, and at least \(112r-124\) for \(r\ge6\).
At \(r=3\), total degree 226 requires the ordered degrees \((92,74,60)\) in increasing order of measure.

For each prime through 499, at most two distinct positive measure values qualify on any row at either sign of the [signed prime count](Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) or of the [fixed-double test](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) at \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\).
At the next prime 503, any three distinct qualifying values have ordered degrees \((86,74,72),(86,86,60),(88,72,72)\), or \((92,72,68)\) in increasing order of measure.
Each triple has total degree 232, the entire width of the row.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Exclusion in degree 72

Take a positive-measure irreducible factor of \(P\).
Its degree is at least 68, and normalization makes it monic, reciprocal and of even degree by the [small-measure reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree).
Degrees 68 and 70 have strict lower cutoffs \(1.082,1.0775\), respectively, by the [degree-specific census](Yin_C_G0345_v440.md#census).
The [degree-72 enumeration](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v14/readme.md#census) excludes the remaining degree at \(T=267/250\).
All 243,366,685 sign-normalized representatives undergo the [certified trace tests](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v14/readme.md#bounds).
Of the 3,521 final polynomials, 3,446 have a cyclotomic divisor, and one of the coefficients of each of the other 75 violates its Graeffe upper bound.
The [separate calculations](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v14/readme.md#verification) check these exclusions.
This proves the polynomial assertion by multiplicativity.
The [relative norm down to the power field](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree) is a unit of measure in \((1,M(c)]\), so its degree gives the stated lower bound for the power field.

## Total degree under the full measure budget

Write \(D\) for the total degree of the \(r\) factors and assume \(D\le112r\), since a larger value already suffices.
By the [largest-measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#sums) each measure is then below Lehmer's measure, and every factor is a reciprocal unit of even degree at least 56 after normalization.
For even \(56\le d\le110\), let \(f(d)\) be the following strict lower bound on multiplicative measure.
Use the [four cutoffs](Yin_C_G0345_v440.md#statement) \(1.101,1.0965,1.082,1.0775\) at degrees \(56,58,68,70\), and \(1.068\) at degree 72 by the [new exclusion](#degree).
Use [1.091](Yin_C_G0345_v429.md#degree) at the remaining degrees below 68, [1.06](Yin_C_G0345_v438.md#degree) at those from 74 through 84, and the [thirteen individual cutoffs](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v13/readme.md#census) from 86 through 110.

Let \(s\) be the number of factors with degree below 112.
Their lower cutoffs have product strictly below \(2^{1/4}\).
Each is at least \(1.02\), and \(1.02^{36}>2\), so \(s\le8\).
For these factors put \(h=\sum(112-d)\), giving \(D\ge112r-h\).
For \(s\) factors and a given \(h\), minimize the product of their cutoffs, starting with \(G_0(0)=1\) and taking
\[
 G_{s+1}(h)=\min_{56\le d\le110,\ d\ {\rm even}} f(d)G_s(h-112+d)
\]
over defined entries.
An entry whose fourth power is at least two can be dropped because every \(f(d)>1\).
For each \(s\) the [exact rational calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v14/check.py) gives the largest surviving \(h\).

| \(s\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Largest \(h\) | 0 | 56 | 98 | 110 | 116 | 120 | 124 | 120 | 100 |

Taking the maximum over \(s\le r\) gives the four stated lower bounds.
For three factors with \(D\le226\), order their degrees \(i,j,k\) by increasing measure.
The [ordered degree bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#sums) give \(i\ge86,\ j\ge68,\ k\ge56\).
Among even triples with these bounds and \(i+j+k\le226\), only \((92,74,60)\) satisfies
\[
 \big(f(i)\max(f(i),f(j))\max(f(i),f(j),f(k))\big)^4<2.
\]
The same rational verifier tests all these triples.

## The strict derivative budget at 499 and 503

The [preceding prime theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v449.md#primes) covers every prime through 491 and leaves only \((86,72,72)\) at 499.
For \(p=499,b=230\), put \(z=(521/500)(267/250)^2=37141569/31250000\).
The integer comparison \(37141569^{4p}(2pb+1)^4>31250000^{4p}2^p(pb+1)^4\) rules out this product by the [strict derivative budget](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion).

At \(p=503\) the width is 232 by \(231^8<503^7<232^8\).
Both prescribed orders are two and \(2b\le p+1\), so the [prime-width criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v437.md#statement) gives \(i+j+k\le232\).
The same ordered lower bounds \(i\ge86,\ j\ge68,\ k\ge56\) apply.
For each of these even triples let \(z=f(i)\max(f(i),f(j))\max(f(i),f(j),f(k))\).
The [integer comparison for every triple](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v14/check.py) retains precisely the four listed in the statement under \(z^{4p}(2pb+1)^4<2^p(pb+1)^4\).
Each uses the full width, so the same criterion gives the additional necessary condition that, after dividing out its content, the row modulo 503 is a nonzero scalar multiple of the product of the three reduced minima.
Whether any such triple occurs remains open.

The computation contributor for Sheng Yin obtained these results in the [unrestricted Lehmer investigation](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
