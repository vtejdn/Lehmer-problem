## 1. Complete all-scale obstruction from the original geometric mean

Let P be any nonzero real polynomial, m=integral_T log|P| dmu and M=exp(m)>0, with normalized circle Haar measure.
For u>0 define

    E_P(sqrt(u))=(1/2)integral log(1+u/|P|^2)dmu.

The finitely many circle zeros have measure0, and log|P| is integrable.
The function g_u(y)=(1/2)log(1+u exp(-2y)) is convex on R: g_u''(y)=2u exp(-2y)/(1+u exp(-2y))^2>=0.
Its derivative has absolute value at most1, so g_u(log|P|) is integrable.
Integrating the supporting line at y=m gives

    E_P(sqrt(u)) >= (1/2)log(1+u/M^2).           (1)

This is the geometric-mean supporting-line mechanism already used in L382, now applied to the exact regularization error.
It is a LOWER bound on the actual error, not a coarse upper bound proposed for a certificate.

For an original monic reciprocal P of degree2h>=2 with constant1, use C123's trace residual delta_b=b^-h P(b)=T(b+b^-1) at any real b>1 and its scale u>0.
The exact remaining-income expression is

    F_P(b,u)=(1/2)log[(b^2 u+delta_b^2)/(u+delta_b^2)]
                                     -E_P(sqrt(u)).

By(1),

    F_P(b,u) <=(1/2)log[
       M^2(b^2u+delta_b^2)/((u+delta_b^2)(M^2+u))]. (2)

The denominator minus the numerator inside the ratio is exactly

    u[u+delta_b^2-M^2(b^2-1)].                   (3)

Consequently

    delta_b^2>=M^2(b^2-1)
         => F_P(b,u)<0 for EVERY u>0.           (4)

No choice of precision, large/small scale, or sharper upper estimate on E can defeat(4).
