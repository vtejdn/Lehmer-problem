# Independent alternating actions and the size of the splitting field

<a id="statement"></a>
## A product of large alternating groups in the action on conjugate labels

For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(\alpha\) of logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\), form the [class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) \(\gamma\) and put \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\).
The \(d\) conjugates of \(\gamma\) give \(v=d/w\) distinct multiplicative labels, with the same number \(w\in\{1,2\}\) of conjugates at each label.
Let \(G_0\) be the permutation image on these labels and \(G_\gamma\) the full Galois group of the rational splitting field \(L_\gamma\).
Fix \(M>0\).
If \(0<m\le M\) and \(d\) is sufficiently large depending only on \(M\), the following statements hold.

* The minimal blocks of size at least two form a unique partition of the labels into \(u\) blocks of size \(b=v/u\), with \(u\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\).
  The single-block partition is allowed.
  In this permutation action one has
  \[
                  (A_b)^u \ \le\ G_0\ \le\ S_b\wr S_u .                    \tag{1}
  \]
  Each factor \(A_b\) acts on its own block and fixes every other label.
  The product \((A_b)^u\) is the unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of \(G_0\).
* With \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), the relative splitting degree satisfies
  \[
        v\log d-O_M\!\left(d\log\log(3d)\right)
           \ \le\ \log [F L_\gamma:F]
           \ \le\ \log[L_\gamma:\mathbb Q]\ \le\ v\log d+O(d).             \tag{2}
  \]
  In particular \(\log[FL_\gamma:F]/\log(v!)\to1\) as \(d\to\infty\) at bounded measure.
  The splitting field of \(\alpha\) contains \(L_\gamma\), so it inherits the lower bound.
* Omit at least one label from each block.
  Choosing one conjugate above each of any of the remaining labels always gives a multiplicatively independent set.
  In particular the multiplicative rank of the conjugates is at least \(v-u\).
  In each single block the rank is \(b\) or \(b-1\).
  In any multiplicative relation modulo torsion written with one representative per label, the coefficient vector on a block is either zero or has all coordinates of the same nonzero absolute value.

All three conclusions apply eventually to any sequence with \(0<m(\alpha)\to0\), since \(m(\alpha)=m(\gamma)\) and the compressed degree then tends to infinity.
They are necessary conditions on a hypothetical sequence.

<a id="partition"></a>
## A unique partition and the action of its kernel

The [block-size estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#block) gives \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\) for every block of size at least two.
Two minimal such blocks containing the same label either agree or intersect only there, since an intersection containing that label is again a block.
In the latter case all translates in their respective block systems meet in at most one point.
Indeed two distinct minimal blocks with a common point have at most one in the intersection, and if the two systems shared a block they would be the same system of translates.
For the competing sizes \(b,b'\) this would give \(bb'\le v\), by intersecting the first block with all \(v/b'\) blocks of the second system.
The size bound rules this out for large \(d\).
Thus every minimal nontrivial block is one of the translates of a single block, and \(u=v/b\ll_M(\log(3d))^{212}\).
In particular \(b>u+2\) and \(b\ge9\) for large \(d\).

By the [primitive reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#statement) and the [exclusion of classical subspace actions](Yin_C_G0345_v269.md#statement), the group induced by each block stabilizer is \(A_b\) or \(S_b\) naturally on its \(b\) labels.
Let \(K\) be the kernel of the action of \(G_0\) on the \(u\) blocks.
Its projection to each block is normal in this induced group, of index at most \(u!\).
Since \(b>u+2\), this normal subgroup contains \(A_b\), by simplicity of \(A_b\) and triviality of its centralizer in \(S_b\).
The commutator subgroup \(K'\) is therefore contained in \((A_b)^u\) and projects onto every factor.
The [subdirect product argument](Yin_C_G0345_v268.md#normal) realizes \(K'\) as a product of diagonal copies of \(A_b\), each linking one or more whole blocks by automorphisms.

For \(b\ge9\), every automorphism of \(A_b\) comes from a permutation of the \(b\) letters.
Here is an elementary verification for the form used below.
Among order-three elements the single three-cycles have strictly largest centralizer.
For \(j\ge1\) disjoint three-cycles the centralizer has size \(3^j j!(b-3j)!/2\), since for \(j=1\) two fixed letters can be transposed and for \(j\ge2\) two cycles can be interchanged by an odd permutation.
For \(j\ge2\) the ratio to the single-cycle centralizer is \(3^{j-1}j!(b-3j)!/(b-3)!\), strictly less than one.
For \(j=2\) this follows from \(b\ge9\), and for \(j\ge3\) it is at most \(3^{j-1}j!/(3j-3)!<1\), by induction from \(j=3\).
Thus an automorphism permutes the cyclic groups on triple supports.
Distinct ones commute precisely when their supports are disjoint.
Counting their common disjoint triple supports gives \(\binom{b-6+i}{3}\) for intersection size \(i\), which distinguishes \(i=0,1,2\).
In particular the action preserves adjacency by intersection size two.
The largest families of mutually adjacent triples are the \(b-2\) triples through a fixed pair.
Indeed after choosing two triples, any further one either contains their common pair or lies inside their four-element union.
If the latter occurs without the common pair, any adjacent triple in the family must lie in that union, giving at most four members.
The induced permutation of pairs preserves intersection, since two pair families meet in a triple exactly when their pairs meet.
The largest pairwise intersecting families of pairs are the \(b-1\) pairs through one letter, since any family without a common letter is contained in a triangle.
This recovers a permutation of the letters inducing the original action on triples.
After removing it the automorphism fixes all triple subgroups, so conjugation by \(g\) and by its image has the same action on triples for every \(g\in A_b\).
Faithfulness gives equality for each \(g\), proving the assertion.

Now identify labels whose stabilizers as subgroups of \(K'\) coincide.
Normality of \(K'\) makes these classes blocks for \(G_0\).
Within a diagonal set of linked blocks there is exactly one such label per block for each label in a fixed block.
Indeed the coordinate isomorphisms preserve the natural point stabilizers up to relabeling, and these stabilizers are distinct within a block.
Different diagonal factors have different point stabilizers in \(K'\), since one factor acts trivially on the other one's labels.
A diagonal set linking two or more blocks would therefore give a block of size between two and \(u\), contradicting the uniform block-size lower bound.
This proves \(K'=(A_b)^u=:D\) and (1).

Any subgroup of \(D\) normal in \(D\) is a product of simple factors.
Indeed for each nontrivial projection of such a subgroup its commutators with that factor form a nontrivial subgroup contained in the intersection, which is normal in the simple factor.
Since \(G_0\) permutes the blocks transitively, \(D\) is minimal normal in \(G_0\).
Its centralizer in the label permutation group is trivial, since commuting with each factor preserves its moved set and centralizes the natural alternating group there.
A normal subgroup disjoint from \(D\) would commute with it, proving uniqueness.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Relative degree over the maximal abelian field

The intersection \(L_\gamma\cap F\) is the maximal abelian subextension of the finite Galois extension \(L_\gamma/\mathbb Q\).
Thus \([FL_\gamma:F]=|G_\gamma'|\).
The surjection \(G_\gamma\to G_0\) maps its commutator subgroup onto \(G_0'\), which contains the perfect subgroup \(D\).
Consequently the relative degree is at least \((b!/2)^u\).
The full group has order at most \(2^v v!\), since the kernel on labels permutes each fiber of at most two conjugates.
Using \(\log(b!)\ge b\log b-b\) and \(b=d/(wu)\) gives (2) with the stated uniformity.
The same estimates give the ratio to \(\log(v!)\).

<a id="rank"></a>
## All multiplicative relations require whole blocks

Fix a representative \(x_i\) above each label and map a formal vector \(e_i\) to the image of \(x_i\) in the torsion-free multiplicative group tensored with \(\mathbb Q\).
Galois acts on the formal coordinates by signed permutations, since a root over an image label is the representative or its inverse modulo torsion.
Inside a single block let \(V_B\) be the span of its \(b\) images and let \(r=\dim V_B\).
The action of \(A_b\) on subsets is transitive at every cardinality, so every \(r\)-subset is independent.
Here \(r\ge2\) since different labels are not multiplicatively dependent.

Suppose \(2\le r\le b-2\).
The dual configuration on the space of relations has rank \(b-r\) and every \(b-r\) of its coordinate functionals is independent.
Indeed projection of the relation space onto any \(b-r\) coordinates is injective because the \(r\) complementary columns are independent, hence is an isomorphism.
The signed permutations act on the dual projective labels with the same underlying \(A_b\) action.
Using the smaller of \(r,b-r\), we would get \(b\) projective points of rank \(s\) with \(2\le s\le b/2\), every \(s\) independent.
Choose \(s+1\) of them.
A projectivity fixing these individually is the identity, since the first \(s\) form a basis and all coordinates of the last in this basis are nonzero.
As \(b-(s+1)\ge3\), a three-cycle on the remaining letters lifts to a linear map fixing this projective frame but moving other points, a contradiction.
Thus \(r\) is \(b\) or \(b-1\), and the internal relation space \(I_B\) has dimension at most one.

Fix a block \(B\) and lift its one alternating factor (acting trivially on all other labels) to its full preimage in \(G_\gamma\).
The eventual derived subgroup \(P\) of that preimage is perfect and still projects onto \(A_b\).
It fixes every root in the other blocks, since its action there is through a product of groups of order at most two.
The fixed vectors of \(P\) on the signed formal coordinate space \(\mathbb Q^B\) have dimension at most one, since one coordinate determines them all by transitivity.
If \(I_B\ne0\) it is a line fixed pointwise by \(P\), since a perfect group has no nontrivial character.
In the quotient \(V_B=\mathbb Q^B/I_B\), the fixed vectors are images of fixed vectors in \(\mathbb Q^B\), by averaging over the finite group.

Take any global relation and restrict its formal coefficient vector to \(B\).
Its image in \(V_B\) is the negative of the contribution from all other blocks, hence is fixed by \(P\).
If \(I_B\ne0\), the fixed space of the quotient is zero and the restricted vector is in \(I_B\).
If \(I_B=0\), the restricted vector is itself fixed by \(P\).
In either case it is a signed invariant vector on a transitive set, with constant absolute value of the coordinates.
Omitting a coordinate in every block therefore leaves an independent set, proving the final assertions.
