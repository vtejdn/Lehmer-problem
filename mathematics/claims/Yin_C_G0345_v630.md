# Reciprocal tail determinants force a quadratic loss in the inverse Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A uniform bound in every coefficient dimension

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and irreducible of degree \(d\), with \(|P(0)|=1\) and no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=\sum_{P(\rho)=0}\log^+|\rho|>0\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure.
All circle integrals use Haar probability measure.
For \(0\le q<d\), put \(k=q+1\) and let \(\Pi_q\) be orthogonal projection in \(L^2\) onto the span of \(1,z,\ldots,z^{q-1}\), with \(\Pi_0=0\).
Let \(H_q\) be the Gram matrix of \((1-\Pi_q)(z^j/P)\) for \(0\le j\le q\).
Parseval identifies this with the [two-tail matrix](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#statement), and we write \(D_q=(\det H_q)^{1/k}\).
Then
\[
                        D_0\ge D_1\ge\cdots\ge D_{d-1}>0.             \tag{1}
\]

Suppose in addition that \(P\) is reciprocal, so \(P(z)=z^dP(1/z)\).
Its degree is \(d=2h\), with interior roots \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_h\) and exterior roots \(1/\overline{\alpha_i}\).
Put \(p(z)=\prod_i(z-\alpha_i)\) and \(\mathcal D=\prod_{i,j}|1-\alpha_i\overline{\alpha_j}|\).
Then
\[
 \det H_{d-1}=e^{-(4d-2)m}\mathcal D^{-4},\qquad
 \mathcal D\le(1-e^{-2m})^h.                                          \tag{2}
\]
Equality in the product bound holds exactly for \(p(z)=z^h+a\).
In the present integer setting this gives precisely the trinomials \(P(z)=z^{2h}+tz^h+1\) among the polynomials under consideration, with \(|t|\ge3\) and \(m\ge\operatorname{arcosh}(3/2)\).
Consequently, uniformly for all allowed \(q,d\),
\[
              D_q\ \ge\ \frac{e^{-(4-2/d)m}}{(1-e^{-2m})^2}
                          \ \sim\ \frac1{4m^2}\qquad(m\downarrow0).    \tag{3}
\]
The asymptotic is for the displayed lower bound.
Both determinant expressions bounding the energy of an admissible integral numerator from above in [the finite determinant test](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#statement) are at least \(D_q\).
Thus along any reciprocal sequence with \(m\to0\), even choosing \(q\) as a function of the input cannot give an \(O(1/m)\) energy bound from either expression.

For every nonzero complex Laurent polynomial \(B\) of width less than \(h\), let \(b\) be its coefficient at its least exponent.
There are also the estimates
\[
       \int_{\mathbb T}\frac{|B|^2}{|P|}\ \ge\ \frac{|b|^2}{2\sinh m},
       \qquad
       \left\|\frac{B^2}{P}\right\|_2^2\ \ge\ \frac{|b|^4}{4\sinh^2m},
       \qquad
       \min_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\ \le\ 2\sinh m.                            \tag{4}
\]

<a id="shifts"></a>
## Successive distances for the two interleaved lists

Put \(f=1/P\).
For each \(k\le d\) take the first \(2k-1\) functions from the list
\[
                     f,\ 1,\ zf,\ z,\ z^2f,\ z^2,\ldots .
\]
Ordering the \(k-1\) pure monomials first gives an identity Gram block, and subtracting their orthogonal projections from the remaining functions gives the block \(H_{k-1}\).
Thus the determinant of the full Gram matrix is \(F_k=\det H_{k-1}\).
It is positive since a linear relation would give \(A/P=B\) with \(\deg A<d\) and hence \(A=B=0\).

In the interleaved order let \(u_j\) and \(v_j\) denote the squared distances of \(z^jf\) and \(z^j\) from the preceding span.
Multiplying all functions preceding either term by \(z\) takes them into the span preceding the next term of the same type.
The shift is unitary, so \(u_{j+1}\le u_j\) and \(v_{j+1}\le v_j\).
Also \(v_0\le\|1\|_2^2=1\).
With \(F_0=1\), the Gram determinant formula gives
\[
               F_1/F_0=u_0,\qquad F_k/F_{k-1}=v_{k-2}u_{k-1}
                          \quad(2\le k\le d).
\]
The first ratio dominates the second, and each later ratio is a product of two nonincreasing positive factors.
Their first \(k\) terms therefore have nonincreasing geometric mean \(F_k^{1/k}\), proving (1).

<a id="roots"></a>
## Cancellation of the Vandermonde factors

Let \(V=\prod_{i<j}|\alpha_i-\alpha_j|^2\).
All roots are distinct by irreducibility in characteristic zero.
For the interior nodes, the determinant of the matrix \(K_{ij}=(1-\overline{\alpha_i}\alpha_j)^{-1}\) is \(V/\mathcal D\).
Indeed, pivoting at a node \(\beta\) leaves on the remaining nodes the Schur complement with entries
\[
             \overline{\frac{\alpha_i-\beta}{1-\bar\beta\alpha_i}}\,
             K_{ij}\,\frac{\alpha_j-\beta}{1-\bar\beta\alpha_j}.
\]
Iteration gives one factor \((1-|\alpha_i|^2)^{-1}\) per diagonal pivot and one factor \(|(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)/(1-\bar\alpha_i\alpha_j)|^2\) per pair.
The inverse exterior nodes are the conjugates of the interior nodes, so their kernel determinant has the same value.

Since \(\prod_i|\alpha_i|=e^{-m}\), the squared Vandermonde product on the exterior nodes is \(Ve^{2(h-1)m}\).
The product of squared distances over all interior-exterior pairs is \(\mathcal D^2e^{2hm}\), by writing \(|\alpha_i-1/\bar\alpha_j|=|1-\alpha_i\bar\alpha_j|/|\alpha_j|\).
Hence \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=V^2\mathcal D^2e^{(4h-2)m}\).
The [full-dimensional root formula](Yin_C_G0345_v629.md#roots) multiplies the two kernel determinants by \(e^{-2dm}/|\operatorname{disc}P|\).
Substitution gives the identity in (2).

<a id="schur"></a>
## The stable resultant and its equality case

For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(s\) write \(s^*(z)=z^n\overline{s(1/\bar z)}\).
Start with \(p_h=p\).
For any monic \(p_n\) of degree \(n\ge1\) with all roots in the open disk let \(a_n=p_n(0)\), so \(|a_n|<1\), and define
\[
          p_{n-1}(z)=\frac{p_n(z)-a_np_n^*(z)}{z(1-|a_n|^2)} .
\]
The numerator has zero constant coefficient and leading coefficient \(1-|a_n|^2\).
On the unit circle \(|a_np_n^*|<|p_n|\), so Rouché's theorem gives \(n\) interior zeros of the numerator counting multiplicities.
Thus \(p_{n-1}\) is monic and strictly stable.
The reciprocal identity in length \(n\) gives
\[
   p_n=zp_{n-1}+a_np_{n-1}^*,\qquad
   p_n^*=(1-|a_n|^2)p_{n-1}^*+\overline{a_n}p_n .
\]
Put \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(p_n,p_n^*)|\).
Evaluating the second identity at the \(n\) roots of the monic \(p_n\) gives
\[
 R_n=(1-|a_n|^2)^n|\operatorname{Res}(p_n,p_{n-1}^*)|
     =(1-|a_n|^2)^n R_{n-1}.                                         \tag{5}
\]
For the last equality, \(p_n\) and \(zp_{n-1}\) have the same degree and the same values at the roots of \(p_{n-1}^*\).
Their resultants against \(p_{n-1}^*\) therefore agree, including the factor from its actual leading coefficient.
Multiplicativity and \(p_{n-1}^*(0)=1\) remove the factor \(z\).
This also applies when reversal has reduced the actual degree of \(p_{n-1}^*\), including the constant case.

Now \(R_0=1\), \(R_h=\mathcal D\), and \(|a_h|=e^{-m}\).
Formula (5) gives \(\mathcal D=\prod_{n=1}^h(1-|a_n|^2)^n\le(1-e^{-2m})^h\).
Equality holds exactly when \(a_1=\cdots=a_{h-1}=0\), or equivalently \(p(z)=z^h+a_h\), as follows in both directions from the recursion.
The interior root set is invariant under complex conjugation, so \(a_h\) is real.
In the equality case the exterior monic factor is \(z^h+a_h^{-1}\), giving \(t=a_h+a_h^{-1}\).
Conversely a trinomial of the indicated form with \(|t|>2\) has such an interior factor by solving the quadratic in \(z^h\).
Integrality and \(0<|a_h|<1\) give \(2\cosh m=|t|\ge3\).

<a id="energy"></a>
## Consequences for energies and the circle minimum

Taking the \(d\)-th root in (2) and using (1) proves (3).
The Hermite expression \(q/4+\gamma_kD_q\) is at least \(D_q\) because the cubic lattice gives \(\gamma_k\ge1\).
The second expression \((k+q)\max(D_q,D_q^{k/(k+q)})\) is also at least \(D_q\).
These comparisons give a limitation on both upper estimates, leaving the actual shortest energy free to be smaller.
For instance, the [irreducible family \(P(z)=z^{2h}-8z^h+1\), \(h=2^j\)](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#substitution) has fixed \(m=\log(4+\sqrt{15})\) and admits the numerator \(1\) of energy \(8/60^{3/2}\).
At \(q=d-1\) the first expression grows at least as \(q/4\), and the second grows at least as \((2d-1)D_{d-1}\), where (3) gives a degree-independent positive lower bound.

For (4), factor \(P=p\,p^*/\overline{p(0)}\) and observe that \(|P|=e^m|p^*|^2\) on the circle.
Translate \(B\) by a monomial so \(B(0)=b\) and \(\deg B<h\).
Following the [evaluation argument for short numerators](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation), put \(I=p/p^*\), \(g=B/p^*\), and \(c=I(0)=p(0)\).
Both functions are analytic on the closed disk, and \(I\) has boundary modulus one.
On the circle \(g\bar I=B/p\), whose exterior Laurent expansion has vanishing constant coefficient since all poles lie in the open disk and \(\deg B<h\).
Consequently \(\int g\bar I=0\), \(\int g=b\), and \(\int I=c\).
Cauchy–Schwarz with \(1-\bar c I\) gives
\[
           |b|^2\le\|g\|_2^2\|1-\bar c I\|_2^2
                   =(1-e^{-2m})\|g\|_2^2.
\]
Multiplying the estimate for \(\|g\|_2^2\) by \(e^{-m}\) proves the first inequality of (4).
The second follows by squaring that inequality and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to \(|B|^2/|P|\) against the constant one.
For \(B=1\), the integral of \(1/|P|\) is at most \(1/\min_{\mathbb T}|P|\), which gives the last inequality.
