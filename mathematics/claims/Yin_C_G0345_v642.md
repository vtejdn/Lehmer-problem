# The span left after deleting the two largest coefficients of a Salem relation

<a id="statement"></a>
## A common rational power for relations with short surviving span

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(d\), let \(0<m=\log\alpha\le1\), and fix an integer \(T\ge5\).
Consider all relations \(\sum_{i=1}^r b_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) with distinct integer exponents, \(5\le r\le T\), and nonzero rational integers \(|b_i|\le T\).
Delete two terms of largest absolute coefficient, in any admissible way when there are ties.
Let \(A\ge B\) be their magnitudes, \(u\) their positive exponent difference and \(w\) the span of the remaining exponents.
Put \(G=\log(2T)/m\).

If \(d\ge4G\), every deletion leaves \(w\ge d/2\).
If \(w\le d-8G^2\), the deleted pair lies strictly to one side of all remaining terms and has opposite signs.
The term farther from the surviving interval has magnitude \(B<A\), and
\[
   1\le u\le G,\qquad
   |e^{mu}-A/B|\le 2T^2 e^{-m(d-w)}.                              \tag{1}
\]
Whenever there is at least one deletion with \(w\le d-8G^2\), there are a single positive integer \(g\) and a single rational number \(R>1\) such that every such deletion, across all relations at this \(\alpha,T\), satisfies
\[
                 (u,A/B)=(kg,R^k),\qquad
                 1\le k\le\lfloor\log_2 T\rfloor .                  \tag{2}
\]
In particular, at a fixed full coefficient tuple there is at most one possible deleted gap in this range, even with ties.

For the [terminal witnesses](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement) on a hypothetical sequence of Salem numbers with \(m\to0\), one can take \(T=O(d\log(2d))\), increasing the constant to include every weight and coefficient in those windows.
Then \(G^2=O((\log(2d)/m)^2)=o(d)\).
Every witness has surviving span at least \(d/2\) eventually.
A surviving span at most \(d-8G^2\) requires one of only \(O(\log d)\) possible pairs of gap and reduced coefficient ratio at the given input.
All spans here are measured before dividing the surviving exponents by their gcd.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree of the surviving autocorrelation

In fact let \(\alpha\) be any reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), keeping the same distinctness, integrality and deletion assumptions.
Translate the surviving terms into an integer polynomial \(C(X)\) with nonzero constant and degree \(w\).
Write \(a,b\) for the signed deleted coefficients in order of increasing exponent.
The [reciprocal trace identity](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v313.md#recovery) makes \(\alpha\) a zero of the Laurent polynomial
\[
 D(X)=C(X)C(X^{-1})-a^2-b^2-ab(X^u+X^{-u}).
\]
This polynomial is nonzero.
Indeed an identity would first give \(w=u\) by comparing the largest exponents.
The coefficient at \(w\) would give \(|C(0)[X^w]C|=AB\), while each endpoint has magnitude at most \(B\).
This forces \(A=B\) and both endpoints to have magnitude \(B\).
The constant coefficient of the first product is the sum of squares of the surviving coefficients.
Its two endpoints alone contribute \(2B^2=a^2+b^2\), and there is at least one further term.
Clearing negative powers now proves
\[
                            d\le 2\max(w,u).                         \tag{3}
\]
More precisely, if \(h\) divides \(u\) and all differences of surviving exponents, \(D\) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in \(X^h\).
The same argument gives \(h[\mathbb Q(\alpha^h):\mathbb Q]\le2\max(w,u)\).

<a id="placement"></a>
## Locating the deleted pair

Return to a Salem number.
Evaluation at a conjugate on the unit circle gives \(A\le(r-1)B\).
If a deleted term is at an extreme exponent, let \(v\) be its distance to the next exponent.
Evaluate at \(\alpha\) for the right extreme or at \(\alpha^{-1}\) for the left extreme.
After division by the power at the next exponent, the extreme term has magnitude at least \(B e^{mv}\), and all other terms together have magnitude at most \(A+(r-2)B\).
Hence \(v\le\log(2r-3)/m<G\).

The full span is at least \(d\), since the translated relation polynomial is a nonzero multiple of the minimal polynomial.
Unless both deleted terms lie strictly on the same side of the surviving interval, removing them decreases the full span by at most \(2G\).
If both lie to one side, their gap \(u\) is itself an extreme gap and satisfies \(u<G\).
Thus \(d\ge4G\) gives \(w\ge d/2\) in the first case by the full span and in the second by (3).

If \(w\le d-8G^2\), then \(d-w>2G\), since \(T\ge5\) and \(m\le1\).
Both terms consequently lie to the same side.
Let \(l\) be the gap from the nearer term to the closest surviving exponent, so that \(l\ge d-w-u\).
By reversing every exponent if necessary, evaluate with the pair to the right.
Let \(b_{\rm out},b_{\rm in}\) be the coefficients of the farther and nearer terms.
Divide their sum and the surviving sum by \(b_{\rm out}\) times the power at the nearer term to obtain
\[
    |e^{mu}+b_{\rm in}/b_{\rm out}|
       \le (r-2)e^{-ml}\le 2T^2e^{-m(d-w)} ,
\]
using \(e^{mu}\le e^{mG}=2T\).
Set \(E=2T^2e^{-8mG^2}\).
One has \(E<m\) and \(2GE<T^{-2G}\).
Indeed with \(L=\log(2T)>2\) we have \(G=L/m\ge L\), \(\log(2T^2)\le2L\), \(\log(2G)\le G\), \(\log(1/m)\le G\) and \(8mG^2=8GL\).
Since \(e^{mu}\ge 1+m\), the rational number \(-b_{\rm in}/b_{\rm out}\) exceeds one, which proves the sign, orientation and estimate in (1).

<a id="ratios"></a>
## Comparing rational ratios across all coefficient tuples

For each deletion in the short range write \(q=A/B>1\).
By (1) and the derivative bound for the logarithm on \([1,\infty)\), one has \(|\log q-mu|\le E\).
For any two such pairs this gives
\[
              |u_2\log q_1-u_1\log q_2|\le 2GE<T^{-2G}.
\]
The positive rational number \(q_1^{u_2}/q_2^{u_1}\) can be written with numerator and denominator at most \(T^{u_1+u_2}\le T^{2G}\).
For unequal positive integers \(a',b'\), integration of \(1/x\) between them gives \(|\log(a'/b')|\ge1/\max(a',b')\).
Thus \(q_1^{u_2}=q_2^{u_1}\), so all the numbers \(\log q/u\) have the same positive value \(c\).

The distinct gaps form a finite nonempty set of positive integers with gcd \(g\).
An integer linear combination for \(g\) shows that \(R=e^{cg}\) is a product of integer powers of the corresponding rational ratios, hence is rational.
Writing each gap as \(kg\) gives \(q=R^k\).
The numerator of \(R\) in lowest terms is at least two, and its \(k\)-th power is the reduced numerator of \(q\), which is at most \(T\).
This proves (2).

On a shrinking Salem sequence the degrees tend to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness.
The [Dobrowolski lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#section-4-checked-mechanisms-and-external-theorems) gives \(m\gg(\log\log d/\log d)^3\) at large degree.
It implies \(G^2=o(d)\) for the indicated witness windows.
The estimates constrain spans and erased terms, while the number of surviving primitive shapes in the remaining windows is still open.
