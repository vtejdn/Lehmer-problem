# Critical slopes and the distribution of exterior roots among seed zeros

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cubic slope range with at most a linear number of exterior roots

Fix constants \(B,K>0\).
Let \(s\ge3\), let \(a,b\) be relatively prime positive integers with \(a\ge2\), and put \(r=b/a\).
For \(g_s(T)=T^s-T-1\) and \(B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), let \(P\) be any monic nontorsion irreducible rational factor of \(g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a)\).
Write \(d=\deg P\), \(m=\log M(P)\), and let \(k\) count all its roots of modulus greater than one.
If \(s\le r\le Bs^3\) and \(k\le Ka\), then for all sufficiently large \(s\) one has
\[
 r\asymp s^3,\qquad k\asymp a,\qquad m\asymp s^{-1},\qquad
 d\asymp as^2,\qquad \log a\gg s^{1/3}\log s .                       \tag{1}
\]
The implied constants and all thresholds in this result may depend on \(B,K\) only, and \(P\) is reciprocal.

For each interior zero \(\zeta_j\) of \(g_s\), put \(L_j=-\log|\zeta_j|\).
Assign an exterior root \(x\) of \(P\) to \(\zeta_j\) when \(x^{-a}\) lies in its [localization disk](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles), and let \(k_j\) denote the number assigned there.
Every exterior root has exactly one assignment in this range.
There exist positive constants \(u<U\) and \(v<V\), depending only on \(B,K\), such that the set
\[
       J=\{j:u\le sL_j\le U,\quad va/s\le k_j\le Va/s\}
\]
satisfies \(|J|\gg s\) and \(\sum_{j\in J}k_j\gg a\).
Thus any such factor must distribute a positive proportion of its exterior roots over a positive proportion of the seed zeros, with both their radii and their occupancies in the displayed intervals.
Existence of factors realizing these necessary conditions remains open.

<a id="scales"></a>
## Measure, degree and the growth of the first exponent

Put \(h=am\).
The [estimate at the shorter slopes](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#small) gives \(k/a\gg s/(\log(2s))^{11/2}\) if \(s\le r<Cs(\log(2s))^8\), with the absolute constant \(C\) in the [exterior-root bound](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#statement).
This exceeds \(K\) for all sufficiently large \(s\), so the bound applies and \(k/a\gg s^{3/2}/\sqrt r\).
Since \(r\le Bs^3\) and \(k\le Ka\), this proves \(r\asymp s^3\) and \(k\asymp a\).
It also yields \(m\gg\sqrt{s/r}\gg1/s\).
If \(h<\log(2d)\), the [small projected measure estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#small) gives \(k/a\gg s/(\log(2s)(\log(2r))^3)\gg s/(\log(2s))^4\), contradicting \(k\le Ka\) for large \(s\).
Hence \(h\ge\log(2d)\), and the [first moment estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#moments) gives \(h\ll k/s\ll a/s\).
Together these bounds give \(m\asymp1/s\) and \(h\asymp a/s\).
[Smyth's lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) forces \(P\) to be reciprocal for large \(s\).

The [degree bound from the sparse multiple](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#discriminant) gives \(d\ll rh\ll as^2\).
For the opposite inequality, the [second moment estimate and all-root square bound](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#moments) give \(1/10\ll dh/(a^2s)\), so \(d\gg as^2\).
In particular \(d\to\infty\).
Set \(y=\log d\).
By [Voutier's Theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), \(m>\tfrac14(\log y/y)^3\), hence \(y\gg s^{1/3}\log y\).
First \(y\gg s^{1/3}\), then \(\log y\gg\log s\), giving \(y\gg s^{1/3}\log s\).
Subtracting \(2\log s+O(1)\) gives the same lower bound for \(\log a\).

<a id="collisions"></a>
## Discriminant losses at every occupied seed zero

By (1), \(r\gg s^3\), which exceeds the threshold \(C_0s^2\log(2s)\) for [localization of the entire exterior set](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v250.md#poles).
The disjoint disks have radius \(c_1s^{-4}\), lie inside the unit disk, and contain all the \(z=x^{-a}\) for exterior roots of the parent, with one original root per branch of the \(a\)-th root map at each seed zero.
In particular \(\sum_j k_j=k\).
For such a point \(z\) assigned to \(\zeta_j\), put \(L=-\log|z|\).
The same localization proof gives \(L_j\gg s^{-2}\), \(|\zeta_j|>1/2\), and \(L=L_j+O(s^{-4})\), uniformly in the disk.
Thus \(L_j/2\le L\le2L_j\) for sufficiently large \(s\), including at the zeros closest to the unit circle.
That proof also gives \(|g_s'(\zeta_j)|\gg1\) and \(|g_s''|\le s^2\) throughout the disk.
Taylor's estimate along the segment from \(\zeta_j\) to \(z\) therefore gives \(|g_s(z)|\gg|z-\zeta_j|\).
The section equation at \(x^{-1}\) gives \(|g_s(z)|\le3e^{-rL}\), hence
\[
                    |z-\zeta_j|\ll e^{-rL_j/2}.
\]
For two distinct assigned points \(z,z'\) in this disk, \(1-|z||z'|\ge1-e^{-L_j}\gg L_j\), using \(0<L_j<\log2\).
Their disk distance ratio is bounded by
\[
 \frac{|z-z'|}{|1-z\overline{z'}|}\ll s^2e^{-rL_j/2}\le e^{-rL_j/4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(s\), since \(rL_j\gg s\).

The [degree-preserving projection and discriminant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#discriminant) apply to the same irreducible polynomial of \(x^{-a}\), with all its \(d\) conjugates distinct and its log measure equal to \(h\).
Using the preceding ratio on every pair in every occupied disk gives
\[
 \frac r4\sum_j L_jk_j(k_j-1)\le d\log d+2(d-1)h .
\]
Adding \(\sum_j L_jk_j\le2h\) after division and using \(d\ll rh\), \(h\ge\log(2d)\), we have
\[
                    \sum_j L_jk_j^2\ll h^2.                            \tag{2}
\]
This estimate now includes the full occupancy at each pole for an individual factor with the hypotheses of (1).

<a id="spread"></a>
## Selecting a positive proportion of the poles and roots

Write \(H=\sum_j L_jk_j\), so \(h/2\le H\le2h\), and fix \(c_0>0\) with \(h\ge c_0a/s\).
For \(sL_j<u\), the contribution to \(H\) is at most \(uk/s\le uKa/s\le h/8\) on choosing \(u\) sufficiently small.
At the other end the [seed tail estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v506.md#moments) and (2) give
\[
 \sum_{sL_j>U}L_jk_j
 \le\left(\sum_j L_j k_j^2\right)^{1/2}
    \left(\sum_{sL_j>U}L_j\right)^{1/2}
 \ll h\sqrt{(1+U)e^{-U}}\le h/8
\]
for a sufficiently large fixed \(U\).
Thus \(I=\{j:u\le sL_j\le U\}\) contributes at least \(h/4\) to \(H\).
It follows that \(\sum_{j\in I}k_j\ge sh/(4U)\ge c_2a\) for a constant \(c_2>0\).
Also (2) gives \(\sum_{j\in I}k_j^2\le(s/u)\sum_j L_jk_j^2\le C_2a^2/s\) for a constant \(C_2\).
There are at most \(s\) seed zeros, so indices in \(I\) with \(k_j<va/s\) contribute at most \(va\) roots.
Those with \(k_j>Va/s\) contribute at most \((s/(Va))\sum_{j\in I}k_j^2\le C_2 a/V\) roots.
Choose \(v<c_2/4\) and \(V>\max(v,4C_2/c_2)\).
The remaining indices are exactly \(J\), and their occupancies sum to at least \(c_2a/2\).
Since each contributes at most \(Va/s\), the number of such indices is at least \(c_2s/(2V)\).

A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin derived these necessary scales and the joint bounds for radii and occupancies.
