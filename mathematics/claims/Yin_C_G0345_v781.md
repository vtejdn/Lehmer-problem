# An irrational ratio of pole occupancies forces arbitrarily many exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finiteness theorem for every bounded exterior count

Let \(F=0\) be the primitive integral plane curve parametrized by
\[
          X(t)=\frac{9t^2-1}{t^2-9},\qquad
          Y(t)=\frac{2t-1}{t-2}
                        \left(\frac{2t+1}{t+2}\right)^2 .
\]
For every integer \(H\ge1\) there are only finitely many nontorsion algebraic units \(z\) with at most \(H\) conjugates of modulus greater than one that solve
\[
       F(\xi z^a,\eta z^b)=0,\qquad
       \xi,\eta\in\mu_\infty,\qquad (a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}.
\]
In particular all Salem numbers in these sections form a finite set.
At the two poles on the only eligible axis the short-coordinate moduli both exceed one.
The obstruction comes from their required occupancy ratio
\[
                        \frac{\log49}{\log(49/5)} .
\]

<a id="faces"></a>
## The short coordinate on the surviving axis

As the degrees of \(X,Y\) are two and three, they generate the rational function field over \(\mathbb Q\) and over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), by the degree tower.
Thus the parametric image is absolutely irreducible and \(t\) is a rational parameter over \(\mathbb Q\) off finitely many singular points.
Let \(G\) be the resultant in \(t\) of \((X-9)t^2+1-9X\) and \(Y(t-2)(t+2)^2-(2t-1)(2t+1)^2\).
To compute it, multiply the last polynomial at \(t\) and \(-t\), substitute \(t^2=(9X-1)/(X-9)\), and multiply by \((X-9)^3\).
The primitive part \(F\), up to sign, has the following faces.

| Face | Polynomial |
| --- | --- |
| Coefficient of \(Y^2\) | \(-5(X+7)^3\) |
| Constant in \(Y\) | \(-5(7X+1)^3\) |
| Coefficient of \(X^3\) | \(-(Y-35)(5Y-49)\) |
| Constant in \(X\) | \(-(35Y-1)(49Y-5)\) |

All four corners have absolute coefficient \(5\) or \(1715\), and the polygon is the full rectangle.
The [finite-place criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v772.md#statement) forces every accumulation direction of distinct rank-one unit pairs to be parallel to the \(X\)-axis since the corner and the two \(Y\)-extreme faces have nontrivial content.
Suppose such a sequence were realized by the units in the statement.
Both functions satisfy \(X(1/t)=1/X(t)\) and \(Y(1/t)=1/Y(t)\).
After taking a subsequence and, if necessary, making this inversion simultaneously at every index, we may thus assume \(a>0\), with \(z\) still the original unit.
The condition \(|b|/a\to0\) now gives \(h(Y)\to0\) by the [coordinate height limit](Yin_C_G0345_v770.md#statement).

In fact \(b=0\) is impossible on a rank-one unit pair.
For real \(c>1\), the difference \(|ct-1|^2-|t-c|^2=(c^2-1)(|t|^2-1)\) shows that the ratio of the two moduli is strictly below or strictly above one according as \(|t|<1\) or \(|t|>1\).
Replacing \(t\) by \(-t\) gives the same assertion for the other linear factor.
Hence \(|Y|=1\) forces \(|t|=1\), where also \(|X|=1\).
If \(Y\) is torsion, every conjugate of the unit \(X\) has modulus one, forcing \(X\) to be torsion as well.

<a id="occupancy"></a>
## Height at each pole and the two embedding counts

For each pair put \(L=\mathbb Q(z,\xi,\eta)\) and \(D_L=[L:\mathbb Q]\).
After omitting finitely many pairs the parameter \(t\) lies in \(L\).
The long coordinate \(X\) has two simple rational poles \(Q_+=3,Q_-=-3\), with
\[
           q_+=Y(3)=49/5,\qquad q_-=Y(-3)=35.
\]
Take disjoint fixed sufficiently small disks about these poles in which \(|X|>1\) and \(|Y|>1+\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\).
Let \(n_+\) and \(n_-\) be the numbers of embeddings \(L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) for which \(t\) lands in the corresponding disks.
The pairs are distinct, so their lifts are generic on the projective line.
Both coordinates are units, the degree of \(Y\) is three and \(h(Y)\to0\).
For each disk, the [boundary height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v779.md#statement) therefore gives
\[
      \frac{1}{D_L}\sum_{\sigma t\ \mathrm{in\ disk}\ \pm}
                     \log|\sigma X|
                 \longrightarrow \frac{h(q_\pm)}{3}>0.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular both counts are eventually positive, and \(\log|\sigma X|=(a/b)\log|\sigma Y|\) on the disks forces \(b>0\).
There \(|\sigma X|\ge(1+\delta)^{a/b}\to\infty\), forcing the parameters uniformly toward the respective pole.
Consequently all corresponding \(\log|\sigma Y|\) are \(\log q_\pm+o(1)\) uniformly on each disk.
Taking the ratio of (1) at the two poles cancels the common factor \(a/(bD_L)\), giving
\[
         \frac{n_+}{n_-}\longrightarrow
         \frac{h(q_+)/\log q_+}{h(q_-)/\log q_-}
         =\frac{\log49}{\log(49/5)} .
\]
This number is irrational, since a positive rational value \(r/s\) would give \(49^s=(49/5)^r\), contradicting the valuation at five.

Every embedding counted has \(|\sigma z|>1\) because \(a>0\) and \(|\sigma X|=|\sigma z|^a>1\).
The [relative degree bound for the two translations](Yin_C_G0345_v775.md#statement) gives \([L:\mathbb Q(z)]\le22\operatorname{area}(\operatorname{Newt}F)\), independently of their conductors.
Thus a bound \(H\) on the exterior conjugates of \(z\) would bound \(n_++n_-\) by this constant times \(H\).
The quotients of bounded positive integers form a finite set, contradicting their irrational limit.
Only finitely many distinct powered pairs can occur with this exterior bound.

<a id="roots"></a>
## Finiteness of the roots above each remaining pair

Fix one such nontorsion pair and a nontorsion coordinate \(W\) of that pair.
Every realizing root satisfies \(W=\omega z^j\) with \(j\ne0\) and a root of unity \(\omega\).
Let \(D_z=[\mathbb Q(z):\mathbb Q]\) and \(C_W=\max_{\tau}|\log|\tau W||\), taking all complex embeddings of the coordinate field.
For every conjugate \(z'\) we have \(|j|\log^+|z'|\le C_W\).
Because \(z\) is a unit with at most \(H\) exterior conjugates,
\[
             |j|D_z h(z)\le HC_W,\qquad |j|h(z)=h(W)>0 .
\]
So \(D_z\le HC_W/h(W)\), and \(h(z)\le h(W)\).
Northcott finiteness over the finite set of pairs gives the asserted finiteness of the roots.
