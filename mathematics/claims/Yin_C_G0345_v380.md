# Irreducible dyadic towers for every primitive cubic direction

<a id="statement"></a>
## The two square classes

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers and put
\[
 g(T)=T^3-T-1,\qquad B(T)=1-T^2-T^3,\qquad A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a).
\]
For every nontorsion root \(\alpha\) of \(A_{a,b}\), both \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\) are nonsquares in \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
If \(P\) is the monic minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\), then \(P(X^{2^k})\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for every integer \(k\ge0\).
It has degree \(2^k\deg P\) and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)\).

<a id="negative"></a>
## Exclusion of a square root of \(-\alpha\)

Write \(A=A_{a,b}\), \(f=-g/B\), and use a bar for reduction modulo two.
The polynomial \(-A\) is monic reciprocal with constant one, so \(\alpha\) is a unit.
If \(\epsilon\alpha\) is a square for either sign, the [multiple-root argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#dyadic) makes every zero \(x\) of \(\overline P\) a simple zero of \(\overline A\).
For such a zero put \(t=x^a\), so \(x^b=f(t)\), with denominator invertible by the cubic Bézout identity.
The [lift of Frobenius](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#frobenius) forces \((H_{\epsilon^a,\epsilon^b}/2)(t)=0\bmod2\), where \(H\) is the cleared polynomial relating \(f(t)^2\) and \(f(\epsilon^a t^2)\) in the [cyclotomic calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic).

Take \(\epsilon=-1\).
For even \(a\) the possible orders of \(t\) are \(1,3,5\), for even \(b\) they are \(1,9\), and for both exponents odd they are \(3,15\).
At each of these orders the cubic calculation gives \(f(t)\) as a power of \(t\).
Since \(a,b\) are coprime, \(t\) and \(f(t)\) generate \(\langle x\rangle\), hence the order \(j\) of \(t\) also equals that of \(x\).
Except for \(j=3\) with both exponents odd, the congruence \(x^b=f(t)\) and the parity imply that \(\Phi_{2j}\) divides \(A\), as detailed in the [residue matching](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0169/readme.md#negative).
Reduction of \(\Phi_{2j}\) contains every root of order \(j\) in characteristic two.
Since \(P\) is nontorsion, it is distinct from this cyclotomic factor, and a shared residue would violate simplicity in \(\overline A\).

The remaining order gives \(b\equiv2a\bmod3\) and \(\overline P=X^2+X+1\), so \(\deg P=2\).
A square \(-\alpha\) would force \({\rm N}(\alpha)={\rm N}(-\alpha)=1\).
Such a nontorsion quadratic unit has integral trace of absolute value at least three, hence \(M(P)\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\), where \(M=\exp m\).
The only possible collisions among the six signed monomials of \(A\) require \(b=a\) or \(b=3a\), both excluded here by the congruence and coprimality.
Jensen's inequality for the logarithm and Parseval's identity give \(M(P)\le M(A)\le\|A\|_2=\sqrt6\), a contradiction.

<a id="positive"></a>
## A positive square would give a degree-ten divisor

For \(\epsilon=1\) the same Frobenius test puts \(t\) among the zeros of the Lehmer polynomial \(L(T)=T^{10}+T^9-T^7-T^6-T^5-T^4-T^3+T+1\) modulo two.
Its reduction is the product of the irreducible quintic \(T^5+T^3+T^2+T+1\) and its distinct reciprocal.
On the first orbit \(t\) has order \(31\) and \(f(t)=t^{23}\), by [finite-field arithmetic](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0169/readme.md#positive).
The second orbit consists of their inverses, and \(f(t^{-1})=f(t)^{-1}\).
It follows again that \(\langle x\rangle=\langle t\rangle\), so \(b\equiv23a\bmod31\) and \(31\nmid a\).
The possible \(x\)'s form two reciprocal orbits of size five, the images of those of \(t\) under powering by \(a^{-1}\bmod31\).

If \(\overline P\) contains just one orbit, the distinct monic reciprocal of \(P\) divides \(A\) and accounts for the other orbit.
If \(\overline P\) contains both, its monic reciprocal must equal \(P\), since otherwise every residue in these orbits would be multiple in \(\overline A\).
In the latter case the constant term of \(P\) is \(+1\), as an anti-reciprocal polynomial vanishes at one.
Thus in either case \(A\) has a monic reciprocal degree-ten divisor \(D\) with reduction
\[
             \overline D=\gcd(X^{31}-1,\overline L(X^a)).
\]
The endpoint \(D(1)\) is odd and divides \(A(1)=-2\), so \(D(1)=\pm1\).

The [height bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#frobenius) give \(1\le a\le437\) and \(-218\le b-3a\le218\).
For every primitive positive pair in that range satisfying the order-\(31\) congruence, simplicity makes the lift of \(\overline D\) as a monic factor of \(A\) unique at each power of two.
An integral \(D\) must therefore survive the endpoint test \(D(1)\equiv\pm1\) at every stage.
Moreover \(|[X^i]D|\le\binom{10}{i}M(D)\le252\sqrt8<1024\), since \(\|A\|_2\le\sqrt8\) even when two exponents coincide.
The centered lift at modulus \(2048\) must then equal \(D\) over the integers, so exact division by that lift decides every remaining candidate.
Of the 3,468 eligible pairs, fifteen survive the endpoint tests and none yields zero remainder in integer division. [Exact finite certificate](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0169/readme.md#enumeration)
This excludes the positive square in the entire remaining range.

<a id="towers"></a>
## Degrees and measures in the tower

The following [norm induction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#all-towers) now applies without restrictions on the primitive direction.
The first square root has degree two over \(K\), since \(\alpha\) is a nonsquare.
If \(X^{2^k}-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\) for \(k\ge1\), the norm to \(K\) of any root \(\gamma\) equals \(-\alpha\).
A square root of \(\gamma\) in \(K(\gamma)\) would make this norm a square in \(K\).
Thus adjoining a square root doubles the degree at each step.
Since \(\alpha=\gamma^{2^k}\) gives \(K\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), the degree over \(\mathbb Q\) equals \(2^k\deg P\), the full degree of \(P(X^{2^k})\).
Each root \(\beta\) of \(P\) has \(2^k\) preimages of modulus \(|\beta|^{1/2^k}\), so the root product defining \(M\) gives \(m(P(X^{2^k}))=m(P)\).
