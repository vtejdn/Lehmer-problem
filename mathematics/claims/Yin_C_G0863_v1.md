## Statement

Let H be a connected subtorus of T^4, and let chi_j=z_j|H be its four coordinate characters.
Assume that these characters are nontrivial and distinct up to inversion.
Set

    F_epsilon=sum_(j=1..4)epsilon_j(chi_j-chi_j^(-1)),
    epsilon in {+1,-1}^4,
    m_H(F_epsilon)=integral_H log|F_epsilon|,
    gamma=9/(4sqrt11),
    eta_large=49/72-gamma>0.

All integrals use normalized Haar measure and natural logarithms, including their integrable torus zeros.
The following are the precise partial bounds and reductions proved below.

1. The sign-minimum function is upper semicontinuous on the compact space K of all such tori.
   If it is strictly less than log2 at every H in K, it has a uniform positive margin there and consequently on every tuple of distinct positive integer frequencies.
   No universal strict-inequality hypothesis is asserted.
2. On the full four-dimensional torus, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<23/50.
   This assertion includes the exact finite arithmetic certificate and its analytic bridge below.
3. If some character is outside the rational span of the other three, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=gamma<log2-5/1296.
4. Let L be the annihilator of H in Z^4.
   If L annihilates some delta in {+1,-1}^4, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=log2-1/512.
5. Let X=Z^4/L and let X_j be generated by the three coordinate characters other than j.
   If q_j=[X:X_j] is finite, every sign satisfies m_H(F_epsilon)<=gamma+2(log2)/q_j.
   In particular q_j>=128 gives m_H(F_epsilon)<=log2-eta_large, at any rank.
6. At rank three, write L=Zc with c primitive.
   For every nonzero c_j, the stronger bound is m_H(F_epsilon)<23/50+2(log2)/|c_j|.
   Thus max_j|c_j|>=6 gives m_H(F_epsilon)<log2-11/12150.
7. The remaining rank-three configurations are contained in the finite set of primitive full-support vectors c with max_j|c_j|<=5 whose absolute coefficients admit no equal-weight partition.
   Every rank-three configuration has at most two sign-measure classes, and has only one if sum_j c_j is odd.
   The remaining finite set, general small-index dependent rank-two configurations and the corresponding actual rank-one tuples are not resolved by these results.
8. On the rank-two torus with character vectors e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2,e_1-e_2, all sixteen signs have the same measure and satisfy m_H(F_epsilon)<log2-log(93/92).
   This is a Haar-torus bound, not the same asserted bound for every one-variable specialization of this template.

The full proof also identifies the precise positive-integer-frequency limit space, verifies the noncollision and common-phase conditions, and gives the conditional finite reduction for rank-one tori after all higher-rank tori are covered.
The author proof and the selected independent-review finding have the distinct roles recorded in the decision below.

<a id="admissible-limit-tori"></a>
## The precise compact space and its excluded degeneracies

Put

    F_epsilon(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)
       =sum_(j=1..4) epsilon_j (z_j-z_j^(-1)),
    epsilon in {+1,-1}^4.

For an ordered tuple a of distinct positive integers, let H_a be the image of the circle under t -> (t^(a_1),...,t^(a_4)), and let mu_a be its normalized Haar measure.
The logarithmic Mahler measure of the actual Laurent polynomial F_epsilon(t^a) is the integral of log|F_epsilon| against mu_a.
Common divisors of the a_j change the parametrization but not H_a or this integral.

For a subgroup L of Z^4 let H_L={z in T^4: z^c=1 for every c in L}.
The annihilator of H_a is

    L_a={c in Z^4: c dot a=0}.

Any sequence of these lattices has a subsequence for which membership of every fixed c is eventually constant.
This follows by diagonal extraction over the countable set Z^4.
The set L of eventually present elements is a subgroup and is saturated: if m*c belongs to L for a nonzero integer m, then eventually m*(c dot a)=0 and hence c dot a=0.
Thus Z^4/L is torsion-free, and H_L is a connected torus.
The character integrals of mu_a are the indicators of membership in L_a, so their limits identify the weak limit uniquely as Haar measure on H_L.
No disconnected subgroup or translated torsion coset is introduced by this limit.

The actual positive-frequency limit space consists exactly of saturated L for which L-perp contains a vector of distinct positive integers.
For necessity, choose a finite basis of L; its elements all belong to L_a eventually, so one of those actual a is the required vector.
For sufficiency, the positive distinct vectors form a nonempty relatively open cone in the rational vector space L-perp.
For each N choose an integer vector a_N in that cone avoiding the finitely many hyperplanes c dot a=0 with c not in L and ||c||_infinity<=N.
Each such hyperplane is proper because L is saturated.
A finite union of proper rational hyperplanes cannot exhaust that relatively open cone, and a rational point can be scaled to an integer point.
Then L_(a_N) converges to L in the stated membership sense.

In particular, every such L excludes

    e_j,              1<=j<=4,
    e_i-e_j, e_i+e_j, 1<=i<j<=4.                         (LT1)

The restricted characters chi_j=z_j|H_L are therefore nontrivial and pairwise distinct even after inversion.
Every F_epsilon|H_L has exactly eight distinct nonzero character terms with coefficients +/-1, so none is identically zero.
An integer frequency tending to zero relative to the largest frequency does not make its torus character trivial.
For example the first coordinate in (1,N,N^2,N^3) remains a nontrivial coordinate in the full-torus limit.
Similarly, (N,N+1,...) does not acquire an equality of its first two characters merely because their ratio tends to one.
The topology records exact fixed integer relations, not convergence of normalized real frequency vectors.

For sign-minimization it is harmless and convenient to use the slightly larger compact space K of all saturated subgroups L satisfying (LT1).
Subgroups and saturation are closed conditions in {0,1}^(Z^4), and (LT1) excludes only finitely many membership coordinates, so K is compact.
Every member can be changed into an actual positive-frequency limit by inverting some coordinate characters.
Indeed choose an integer vector p in L-perp outside the finitely many hyperplanes p_j=0 and p_i=+/-p_j; these hyperplanes are proper by (LT1).
After replacing coordinate j by its inverse when p_j<0, the new annihilator admits the positive distinct vector (|p_1|,...,|p_4|).
Coordinate inversion merely permutes the available epsilon signs.
Consequently the supremum of the minimum sign measure is the same on K and on the actual positive-frequency limit space.

## Strict inequalities on every limit torus would be uniform

For H in K write

    m_H(F_epsilon)=integral_H log|F_epsilon|,
    g(H)=min_epsilon m_H(F_epsilon).

The integrals are finite because each restriction is a nonzero Laurent polynomial on a connected torus.
This uses the ordinary Mahler integrability convention retained in [Den09-U001](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U001.md#mahler-measure), including its recorded source-proof limits.

For delta>0 the function

    f_delta(z)=(1/2)log(|F_epsilon(z)|^2+delta)

is continuous on the ambient torus.
Its integrals are continuous under weak convergence of the Haar measures, and decrease to m_H(F_epsilon) as delta decreases to zero.
The decrease may be justified by monotone convergence after subtracting a common upper bound.
It follows that H -> m_H(F_epsilon) is upper semicontinuous.
Equivalently, if H_n tends to H, then

    limsup_n m_(H_n)(F_epsilon)<=m_H(F_epsilon).          (LT2)

This is a direct abelian instance of the already retained [Den09 Theorems17/22 mechanism](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#marked-groups), not a new determinant-approximation theorem.
The elementary regularization above states exactly the part needed here.
Neither invertibility nor a lower bound away from the torus zero set is required for this upper inequality.

The finite minimum g is also upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum on compact K.
Therefore the following implication is rigorous:

    [for every H in K, some epsilon has m_H(F_epsilon)<log2]
      => [there exists eta>0 such that for every H in K,
          some epsilon has m_H(F_epsilon)<=log2-eta].   (LT3)

The conclusion includes every actual positive integer-frequency tuple.
This proof is non-effective unless the strict torus bounds and their neighborhoods are made effective.
It does not establish the hypothesis of (LT3).
In particular, checking each individual circle with an input-dependent strict margin does not check the higher-dimensional limit tori that compactness requires.

[Den09-U002's Theorem14/Example15 interface](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md) supplies full Lawton convergence for a fixed Laurent polynomial when its shortest nonzero relation tends to infinity.
That stronger two-sided limit is useful but unnecessary for (LT3).
The accepted [BGMP24 Proposition4.10 interface](../audits/literature/BGMP24/Yin_U001.md#accepted-regularization-interface) gives a uniform logarithmic regularization estimate under its explicit rho(A)>D hypothesis.
It cannot be applied indiscriminately to every dependent torus in K with the fixed ambient template: for F_epsilon in four variables D=2, and a dependent torus may have a relation of infinity norm1 or2.
One can reparametrize a fixed torus and use its own polynomial, but this is not a common quantitative bound over all changing parametrizations.
No disputed BGMP24 Theorem4.1 constant is used here.

## An exact finite certificate for the independent four-torus

On the full torus every sign choice has the same measure by independent coordinate translations z_j -> epsilon_j z_j.
The common translation z_j -> i*z_j changes sum_j(z_j-z_j^(-1)) into i*R, where

    R=sum_(j=1..4)(z_j+z_j^(-1)).

Thus R is real on the torus, has |R|<=8, and its logarithmic measure equals the common independent-torus measure m_free.
For k>=0 let A_k be the constant coefficient of R^(2k).
Counting balanced positive and negative steps separately in each coordinate gives

    A_k=(2k)! sum_(k_1+...+k_4=k) product_(i=1..4)1/(k_i!)^2
       =binom(2k,k) sum_(j=0..k) binom(k,j)^2
                    binom(2j,j) binom(2k-2j,k-j).      (IC1)

For the second equality, group k_1+k_2=j and k_3+k_4=k-j, and use sum_l binom(j,l)^2=binom(2j,j) in each group.
That binomial identity is the coefficient of x^j in (1+x)^j(1+x)^j.
These are exact nonnegative integer moments; in particular A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3 are 1,8,168,5120.

Put X=R^2/64, so 0<=X<=1.
For an integer J>=1, the positive logarithm series gives pointwise, with value minus infinity at X=0,

    log|R|<=(1/2)log64-(1/2)sum_(j=1..J)(1-X)^j/j.

Its right side is an integrable polynomial, and log|R| is integrable because R is a nonzero Laurent polynomial.
Integrating this finite inequality requires no infinite-series interchange or numerical zero-set cutoff:

    m_free<=(1/2)log64-(1/2)S_J,
    S_J=sum_(j=1..J)B_j/(j*64^j),
    B_j=sum_(k=0..j)(-1)^k binom(j,k) A_k 64^(j-k).
                                                               (IC2)

The moment interpretation gives B_j=64^j integral(1-X)^j and hence 0<=B_j<=64^j.
For J=4096 and scale D=2^40, define the finite integer

    N=sum_(j=1..4096)floor(D*B_j/(j*64^j)).             (IC3)

Every floor rounds downward, so S_4096>=N/D.
The [J134 exact program](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0134/independent-torus.py) evaluates (IC1)--(IC3) using arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic only.
Its binomial recurrence starts at binom(k,0)=1 and updates by the exact division binom(k,j+1)=binom(k,j)*(k-j)/(j+1).
The second loop evaluates each signed sum B_j exactly before division, and asserts the interval 0<=B_j<=64^j.
The sole rounding is the displayed nonnegative integer floor in (IC3).
Thus cancellation in B_j produces no floating-point loss.

The author calculation by root, using

    python mathematics/computations/programs/Yin_J_G0134/independent-torus.py --order 4096 --bits 40

returned the exact payload retained in the [tracked certificate](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0134/inputs/independent-torus-certificate.json):

    N=3565677176283,
    D=1099511627776,
    25N-81D=81487557219>0.                              (IC4)

The command runs from the repository root with Python's standard-library arbitrary-precision integers.
The program source, parameters and certificate are necessary finite computation inputs to this proof; the generated output is reproducible and is not substituted for them.
The torus_limits contributor read the entire program, checked its correspondence with (IC1)--(IC3), inspected the generated order4096 result and checked the exact comparison in (IC4).
The generator and selected certificate report the 81/25 comparison in (IC4) for the same complete computed integer N.
This is author verification of the actual program/result interface, not an independently implemented calculation, an order4096 rerun by that contributor, independent review or formal verification.
The finite calculation establishes only (IC4) for this J and scale, with its application supplied by (IC2); no quadrature or frequency grid is a proof premise.

For the logarithm constants, the atanh series gives

    log2=2 sum_(k>=0)1/((2k+1)3^(2k+1))>56/81.

In the tail k>=2, replace 1/(2k+1) by 1/5 and sum the geometric series to obtain

    log2<56/81+1/540=1123/1620<52/75,
    52/75-1123/1620=1/8100.                            (IC5)

Consequently log64=6log2<104/25, while (IC3)--(IC4) give S_4096>81/25.
Substitution in (IC2) proves

    m_free<23/50.                                     (IC6)

The strict bound is the finite-certificate conclusion used in the rank-three refinement below.
It is distinct from an asymptotic approximation or an uncertified decimal estimate of m_free.

<a id="coloop-bound"></a>
## An independent coordinate gives an explicit uniform margin

Let v_j be the character chi_j represented in the free lattice X(H)=Z^4/L.
Assume that for some index j, v_j is outside the rational span of the other three characters.
This is the coloop condition for this four-element character configuration.
It is stronger than the pairwise noncollision condition (LT1).

Haar averaging makes chi_j an independent uniform circle variable relative to the other three characters.
For example the joint image of H in the circle for chi_j and the torus generated by the other characters has no relation with a nonzero chi_j coefficient, so its annihilator is the product annihilator and its Haar measure is the product measure.
Primitivity of v_j as an individual lattice element is not needed; a nonzero power map still pushes circle Haar measure to circle Haar measure.

Absorb the fixed signs into the sine terms, and condition on

    A=sum_(i!=j) epsilon_i sin(theta_i).

One-variable Jensen, with its unit-circle zero convention, gives

    integral_t log|2(A+sin t)|
       =h(|A|),
    h(x)=0 for 0<=x<=1,
    h(x)=arcosh x for x>1.                              (LT4)

For u>=0,

    cosh(u)^2 >= 1+u^2+u^4/3
      =(u^2-1/2)^2/3+(4/3)u^2+11/12
      >=(2sqrt11/3)u.

The first inequality is the nonnegative-term series of (1+cosh(2u))/2, and the last is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on the last two terms.
Thus h(x)<=3x^2/(2sqrt11) for every x>=0.
Condition (LT1) gives orthogonality of the three remaining sine terms, each with second moment 1/2.
Consequently, for every sign choice,

    m_H(F_epsilon)<=9/(4sqrt11)<11/16<log2.             (LT5)

For the first strict rational comparison, square 36<11sqrt11 to obtain 1296<1331.
For the second, the positive series

    log2=2 sum_(k>=0) 1/((2k+1)3^(2k+1))>56/81

gives log2-11/16>5/1296.
Hence eta=5/1296 is a permissible common margin in this class.
The full rank-four torus is covered, as are rank-three tori whose unique relation omits at least one character and rank-two configurations having such an independent character.
An actual four-frequency circle has rank one and does not have a coloop; one must not integrate one of its frequencies as an independent variable.

## A common phase reduces directly to the existing energy mechanism

Assume that there is a vector delta in {+1,-1}^4 annihilated by every c in L:

    c dot delta=0 for every c in L.                    (LT6)

This condition produces a cocharacter direction along which each oriented character chi_j^(delta_j) has the same positive nonzero exponent.
More explicitly, the homomorphism from Z^4/L to Z sending v_j to delta_j is well-defined; dually it defines a cocharacter.
Choose its primitive direction as one torus coordinate t.
For a suitable integer h>=1 and remaining coordinates y one has

    chi_j^(delta_j)=t^h y^(b_j),
    F_epsilon=t^h H(y)-t^(-h) H(y^(-1)),
    H(y)=sum_j epsilon_j delta_j y^(b_j).               (LT7)

The b_j are pairwise distinct.
If b_i=b_j, the common exponent h in (LT7) would imply chi_i^(delta_i)=chi_j^(delta_j), contrary to (LT1).
Thus H has exactly four distinct terms of coefficient modulus 1; no +/-2 monomial or cancellation exception can appear.

For each y where H(y) is nonzero, H(y^(-1)) is its complex conjugate, and Jensen in t gives logarithmic mean log|H(y)|.
The zero set of the nonzero polynomial H has Haar measure zero, and ordinary logarithmic integrability gives legitimate Fubini through it.
It follows that

    m_H(F_epsilon)=m(H).                               (LT8)

The strict bound m(H)<log2 already follows from strict coefficient-energy Jensen.
An explicit common bound follows by the same extreme-lag mechanism as [C844 ER3](../claims/Yin_C_G0844_v1.md#uniform-energy-logarithmic-margin), with its multivariate applicability checked here.
Put W=|H|^2/4.
Then 0<=W<=4 and integral W=1.
Choose a real linear functional separating the four exponents b_j, with unique maximum b_+ and minimum b_-.
The Fourier coefficient of W at b_+-b_- is the product of their two coefficients divided by4.
No other ordered support pair contributes to this difference: its linear-functional value is the largest possible support difference and requires both unique extremes.
The opposite lag has the conjugate coefficient, so Parseval gives Var(W)>=1/8.

For 0<x<=4, strong concavity gives

    log x <= (x-1)-(x-1)^2/32.

The same inequality is valid with value minus infinity at x=0; logarithmic integrability permits integration with W.
Therefore

    m(H)-log2=(1/2)integral log W
       <=-Var(W)/64<=-1/512.                            (LT9)

This is a direct multivariate adaptation of C844's accepted energy argument, not a separate new mechanism for finding auxiliaries.
It applies to every sign choice satisfying the same character configuration.

<a id="finite-fiber-bound"></a>
## A rank-three relation with a large coefficient has a uniform margin

Let H_c have rank three, with primitive relation c in Z^4, and choose a nonzero coefficient q=|c_j|.
Permute coordinates to make j=4.
Projection from H_c onto its first three circle coordinates is surjective, since every prescribed (z_1,z_2,z_3) admits exactly q solutions for z_4 in

    z_4^(c_4)=product_(i=1..3)z_i^(-c_i).

The kernel has q elements.
The pushforward of Haar measure is Haar measure on T^3, and conditional Haar measure is uniform on the q-element fiber.
This remains true even when q>1 and the coordinate character is not primitive relative to the projection.
There is no assumption that the sign choices themselves can be implemented by translations in H_c.

Multiply F_epsilon by epsilon_4, which leaves its absolute value unchanged, and write

    A=sum_(i=1..3) epsilon_i epsilon_4 sin(theta_i).

On a fiber z=z_4 with z^q=w and |w|=1, the resulting function equals 2i(A+sin t), where z=exp(it).
If c_4 is negative, replace the determined right side by its inverse to obtain the same z^q=w form.
Multiplication by z gives the monic quadratic

    R_A(z)=z^2+2iAz-1=(z-r_1)(z-r_2).

For either root r and every |w|=1,

    (1/q)sum_(z^q=w)log|z-r|
       =(1/q)log|r^q-w|
       <=log^+|r|+(log2)/q.                            (LT10)

The equality and inequality are understood with value minus infinity when a factor vanishes.
The inequality is the elementary bound |r^q-w|<=2max(1,|r|)^q.
Applying it to both roots and using circle Jensen for R_A gives the fiber bound

    average_(z^q=w) log|2i(A+sin t)|
       <=h(|A|)+2(log2)/q,

where h is exactly the function in (LT4).
The full restriction F_epsilon|H_c is nonzero by (LT1), so its logarithm is integrable.
Fubini and the finite Haar fibers justify integrating this inequality; exceptional zero fibers have base measure zero.
Since the projected three angles are independent uniform circle variables, their signed sine sum has second moment 3/2.
The same bound on h as before therefore yields, for every sign,

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<=gamma+2(log2)/q,
    gamma=9/(4sqrt11).                                (LT11)

For q>=128 this supplies a fixed positive margin

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<=log2-eta_large,
    eta_large=49/72-9/(4sqrt11)>0.                     (LT12)

Indeed (63/64)log2>(63/64)(56/81)=49/72, so the difference between log2 and the right side of (LT11) is strictly larger than eta_large.
Positivity follows from 49sqrt11>162, whose squared comparison is 26411>26244.
Thus every rank-three torus whose primitive relation has maximum coefficient modulus at least 128 is covered, regardless of its balanced-partition or sign-symmetry properties.
This is an explicit cutoff for this torus inequality; it does not enumerate or certify the remaining finitely many small relation vectors.

For rank three the projected three variables are genuinely independent, so one can retain their exact integral instead of using the second-moment bound.
Adding an independent fourth circle to that base identifies integral_(T^3)h(|A|) with m_free by (LT4) and Fubini.
The signs do not affect m_free, as shown in the independent-torus argument.
Hence the sharper finite-fiber conclusion is

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<=m_free+2(log2)/q
                      <23/50+2(log2)/q.               (LT12a)

For q>=6, the right side is at most 23/50+(log2)/3, and

    (2/3)log2-23/50>(2/3)(56/81)-23/50=11/12150.

It follows that every sign on such a rank-three torus satisfies

    m_(H_c)(F_epsilon)<log2-11/12150.                  (LT12b)

The cutoff 6 applies to rank three because the base is the full independent three-torus.
It must not replace the cutoff 128 in the lower-rank extension that follows.

The same finite-fiber argument also applies at lower rank.
For any H in K and index j, let X_j be the subgroup of X(H) generated by the other three characters.
If X_j has the same rank as X(H), its index q_j=[X(H):X_j] is finite, and the quotient is cyclic because v_j together with X_j generates X(H).
Projection onto the other three characters has a q_j-element kernel, and chi_j identifies that kernel with the q_j-th roots of unity.
Every conditional fiber therefore has the same z^(q_j)=w form used in (LT10).
The projected three characters remain nonzero and distinct up to inversion: they belong to the subgroup X_j of X(H), so no new character equality appears.
Their sine sum still has second moment 3/2 by orthogonality, without assuming that these three characters are mutually independent.
Consequently (LT11) and (LT12) hold with q=q_j at every rank whenever this finite index is defined.
If the rank drops on deletion, the character is a coloop and the earlier independent-circle argument applies instead.

For an actual circle tuple a, this deletion index is

    q_j=gcd(a_i: i!=j)/gcd(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4).           (LT13)

Thus an actual tuple with some q_j>=128 is already covered by (LT12).
For a rank-two torus one may compute q_j as the gcd of the j-th coordinates of a basis of L; a zero gcd means the coloop case.
Indeed X(H)/X_j is generated by v_j with precisely the relations c_j*v_j=0 for c in L.
Small deletion indices alone do not bound the full rank-two relation lattice.

<a id="rank-two-cross-margin"></a>
## An explicit rank-two cross margin for all signs

Consider the four character vectors

    v_1=(1,0), v_2=(0,1), v_3=(1,1), v_4=(1,-1),      (XC1)

with phase image (exp(ix),exp(iy),exp(i(x+y)),exp(i(x-y))).
Its sign translations are exactly (s,t,st,st), with s,t in {+1,-1}.
The first two coordinates determine the last two, and choosing x,y in {0,pi} realizes every such translation.
Combining these translations with global sign reversal gives eight sign flips, exactly those having the same third and fourth signs.
There are consequently two translation/global-reversal orbits, with representatives (++++) and (+++-).
Interchanging x and y preserves the third character and inverts the fourth while exchanging the first two, and carries one representative to the other.
This integer torus automorphism proves equality of all sixteen sign means.

For the all-positive sign write

    F=2i[sin x+sin y+sin(x+y)+sin(x-y)]
     =2i[A(y)sin x+B(y)],
    A(y)=1+2cos y,  B(y)=sin y.

For each y, multiplication by exp(ix) gives the quadratic A z^2+2iBz-A.
When |B|<=|A|, both roots have modulus1 and the x-mean is log|A|.
When |B|>|A| and A!=0, the two roots have reciprocal moduli and the outside root has modulus (|B|+sqrt(B^2-A^2))/|A|.
The x-mean is then log(|B|+sqrt(B^2-A^2)).
At A=0 the original expression is constant in x and has logarithmic mean log(2|B|), in agreement with the same expression.
Thus the exact Jensen reduction is

    m(F)=integral_T log R(y),
    R(y)=|A(y)|                       if B(y)^2<=A(y)^2,
         |B(y)|+sqrt(B(y)^2-A(y)^2)   if B(y)^2>A(y)^2.  (XC2)

The strict second case is equivalent to -4/5<cos y<0, since B^2-A^2=-cos y*(4+5cos y).
Outside that interval |A|>=3/5, while inside it |B|>=3/5.
Thus R>=3/5 everywhere, including at A=0.
Also R<=3, since R=|A|<=3 outside the interval and R<=2|B|<=2 inside it.
In particular this reduced logarithm has no singularities.

Let E denote normalized y-integration and I={y:-4/5<cos y<0}.
By (XC2),

    E R<=E(|A|1_(T\I))+2E(|B|1_I)
        <=E|A|+2E(|B|1_I).                            (XC3)

The set where A<0 is (2pi/3,4pi/3), and EA=1, so direct integration gives

    E|A|=1-(1/pi)integral_(2pi/3)^(4pi/3)(1+2cos y)dy
         =1/3+2sqrt3/pi.

If beta=arccos(-4/5), the components of I are (pi/2,beta) and (2pi-beta,3pi/2), with equal integrals of |sin y|.
It follows that

    E(|B|1_I)=(1/pi)integral_(pi/2)^beta sin y dy=4/(5pi),
    E R<=1/3+(2sqrt3+8/5)/pi.                          (XC4)

The elementary bounds sqrt3<7/4 and pi>31/10 therefore imply

    E R<1/3+(51/10)/(31/10)=184/93<2.                 (XC5)

For an exact check of the lower bound on pi, the inscribed regular twelve-gon in the unit circle gives pi>12sin(pi/12)=3(sqrt6-sqrt2).
The square of the last quantity is 72-36sqrt3>135/14>961/100, using sqrt3<97/56 and 97^2=3*56^2+1.
Both quantities being positive, this proves 3(sqrt6-sqrt2)>31/10.
No decimal approximation to pi or to a radical enters (XC5).

Since 3/5<=R<=3, logarithmic Jensen applies directly to (XC2)--(XC5) and yields

    m_H(F_epsilon)<log(184/93)
                 =log2-log(93/92)                    (XC6)

for every sign on (XC1).
The proof uses no numerical quadrature and covers this fixed rank-two Haar template and its equivalent full-rank parametrizations.
It does not give the same margin for each one-variable specialization, or cover arbitrary rank-two configurations.

## The dependent configurations that still discriminate

For rank three, L is generated by one primitive vector c in Z^4.
The coloop estimate handles every c having a zero coordinate.
The common-phase estimate handles every c for which c dot delta=0 for some delta in {+1,-1}^4.
Equivalently, the absolute values |c_j| admit a partition into two groups of equal total weight.
The refined finite-fiber estimate handles every c with max_j|c_j|>=6.
The remaining rank-three tests therefore lie in the finite set of primitive full-support relation vectors with max_j|c_j|<=5 and no equal-weight partition.
No complete check of that finite set is claimed.

The first simple full-support relation escaping these tests is

    c=(1,1,1,-2),       z_1 z_2 z_3=z_4^2.             (LT14)

Its absolute weights have odd total 5 and cannot be partitioned equally.
The relation (1,1,2,-1) is equivalent by permuting and inverting characters, so it is not a different sign-minimization test.
The torus in (LT14) is an actual positive-frequency limit: for example (1,2,5,4) is a positive distinct vector in its orthogonal complement, and the generic-vector construction above avoids all additional fixed relations.
No strict Mahler bound or counterexample is established here for (LT14).

There is a further exact sign simplification for every rank-three torus.
Translation by a vector sigma in {+1,-1}^4 preserves H_c exactly when product_j sigma_j^(c_j)=1, and then it replaces F_epsilon by F_(epsilon*sigma).
Since c is primitive, its coefficients are not all even; the indicated sign subgroup has index two.
Global sign reversal also preserves Mahler measure.
If sum_j c_j is odd, global reversal lies outside this subgroup, so all sixteen choices have the same Mahler measure.
If that sum is even, there are at most two potentially different sign measures.
In particular all signs on (LT14) have the same mean.
A rigorous value at or above log2 there would obstruct the proposed uniform circle margin through full Lawton approximation within that fixed torus; a strict value below log2 would resolve that limit torus, not all remaining relation vectors.
The two-sided limit needed for this possible obstruction is stronger than (LT2) and must not be inferred from upper semicontinuity alone.

For rank two there are two independent integer relations.
The same coloop and common-phase tests remain sufficient, with (LT6) required for both relations simultaneously.
The finite-fiber estimate further covers every configuration having some deletion index at least 128.
Apart from the cross template (XC1) and its equivalent parametrizations just treated, configurations lacking the first two properties and having all four deletion indices at most 127 remain unresolved here.
For rank one, four nonzero distinct characters up to inversion have neither a coloop nor a common phase: the latter would force all four character magnitudes equal.
The finite-fiber estimate covers the large indices in (LT13), while the remaining small-index tuples are still actual original circle cases, rather than artificial lower-dimensional degeneracies.

The full rank-four strict bound and upper semicontinuity already show qualitatively that rank-three relation vectors escaping every fixed bounded set cannot by themselves be an obstruction.
The refined finite-fiber estimate makes that particular reduction effective through (LT12b).
Dependent small relations, followed by further relations inside their tori, are the genuine discriminating layers.
The rank-two and rank-one layers are not covered merely by the finite list of rank-three host tori.

If all admissible tori of dimension at least two were shown to have some strict good sign, compactness would leave only finitely many actual rank-one tori to check outside their good-sign neighborhoods.
To see this, dimension at least two is a closed condition: a limit lattice cannot contain three independent relations unless sufficiently late lattices contain them too.
Its strict good-sign neighborhoods have a finite subcover.
The complement is compact and consists of rank-one tori, each isolated among admissible tori by requiring its three basis relations; saturation and (LT1) exclude a proper further quotient.
A compact discrete space is finite.
This is conditional reduction only; the unresolved rank-two and rank-three inequalities are not supplied by the compactness argument.
