# Short binary periods and reciprocal polynomials of bounded Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## A modular gap criterion and its limits

For a monic integral polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\ge1\) and a positive integer \(n\), put
\[
 r_P(n)=\deg\gcd(\bar P,X^n-1)
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[X],
\]
where the bar denotes reduction modulo two.
Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) for the logarithmic Mahler measure.
If \(P\) has no cyclotomic factor, then for every integer \(j\ge0\),
\[
 m(P)\ \ge\ \frac{((j+1)r_P(n)-d)\log 2}{2^j n}.                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, a class admitting \(n\le Cd\) with \(r_P(n)\ge\delta d\), for fixed \(C>0\) and \(0<\delta\le1\), has a uniform positive gap.
This criterion allows arbitrarily many nonzero coefficients.

There are monic reciprocal integral polynomials of arbitrarily large degree, with constant coefficient one, every coefficient positive, no cyclotomic factor, and \(2\le M(P)\le52\), for which
\[
                     r_P(n)\le 2(1+\sqrt n) \qquad(n\ge1).          \tag{2}
\]
There are also monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic unit polynomials of unbounded degree with \(M(P)\le52\) satisfying (2).
Full support is asserted for the first family.
For either family the maximum of \(r_P(n)/d\) over \(1\le n\le Cd\) tends to zero as \(d\) tends to infinity for each fixed \(C\).
The construction addresses the prime two.
The [separate specialization estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v167.md#binary) settles the question left by the binary argument of whether the measures of the extracted factors can approach one.

<a id="resultants"></a>
## Doubling the exponent

For any integral polynomial \(B\) coprime to \(P\) over \(\mathbb Q\), its multiplication matrix on \(\mathbb Z[X]/(P)\) has nonzero determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(P,B)\).
Its reduction modulo two has nullity \(\deg\gcd(\bar P,\bar B)\), since its cokernel is \(\mathbb F_2[X]/(\bar P,\bar B)\).
The Smith normal form thus bounds the 2-adic valuation of the determinant below by that nullity.
This is the [companion-matrix calculation for cyclic resultants](Yin_C_G0513_v1.md#statement), applicable to repeated factors as well.
In the factorization
\[
 X^{2^j n}-1=(X^n-1)\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(X^{2^i n}+1)
\]
every factor reduces to a multiple of \(X^n-1\) in characteristic two.
The associated resultants are all nonzero by the cyclotomic exclusion.
Multiplicativity gives a factor \(2^{(j+1)r_P(n)}\) in the absolute resultant of \(P\) and \(X^{2^j n}-1\).
For every complex root \(\alpha\) of \(P\), the triangle inequality bounds \(\lvert\alpha^{2^j n}-1\rvert\) by \(2\max(1,\lvert\alpha\rvert)^{2^j n}\).
Multiplication over the \(d\) roots proves (1).
Taking \(j=\lceil 1/\delta\rceil\) gives the positive lower bound \(\delta\log 2/(2^j C)\).
For irreducible nontorsion units the qualitative gap also follows from the companion calculation and the [small-measure cyclic norm estimate](Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#statement).
The [bounded-residue-degree result](../ledger/Yin_L_G0205_v1.md#bounded-residue-mass-near-one-escape) detects a different concentration by bounding the degrees of the reduction factors.

<a id="binary"></a>
## A reciprocal binary reduction with few short periods

Put \(q=2^k\) for \(k\ge1\), \(f=X^q+X+1\) and \(D=1+X+\cdots+X^{q-1}\).
Write \(A^*=X^{\deg A} A(X^{-1})\) for a polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient.
The two polynomials
\[
             P_q^\pm=f f^*\ \pm 2X^q+2X D D^*                       \tag{3}
\]
are monic reciprocal of degree \(2q\) with constant coefficient one.
Their common binary reduction is \(\bar f\bar f^*\).
The binary translation equation for \(\bar f\) previously gave [short-period bounds for nonreciprocal units](Yin_C_G0130_v2.md#translation-root-subgroup-bound).
Here the reciprocal pair of root sets permits both integral lifts in (3).

The roots of \(\bar f\) form the set \(L=\{a:a^q=a+1\}\) of \(q\) distinct nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), since \(\bar f'=1\) and \(a^{q^2}=a\).
If distinct \(a,b\in L\) have ratio \(c=a/b\), then \(c^q(b+1)=cb+1\).
If \(c^q=c\), this identity forces \(c=1\), contrary to distinctness.
Otherwise it determines \(b=(1-c^q)/(c^q-c)\), and then \(a=cb\).
This is the quadratic Bose–Chowla distinct-ratio property of an affine line. [Nathanson, §2, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2021.08.005)
Thus for \(s\) elements of \(L\) in the subgroup of \(n\)-th roots of unity, the \(s(s-1)\) ordered ratios of distinct elements are distinct nonidentity members of that subgroup.
It has at most \(n\) elements, so \(s\le 1+\sqrt n\).

The roots of \(\bar f^*\) are the inverses of those of \(\bar f\), with the same subgroup count.
Each of the two factors is squarefree.
Even where they intersect, a root of their product has multiplicity at most two and is counted once for each factor containing it.
Consequently the gcd of the product with \(X^n-1\) has degree at most \(2s\), also when \(n\) is even.
This proves (2) for both lifts and for every monic integral factor of either lift.

<a id="dense"></a>
## A dense family without cyclotomic factors

The polynomial \(2X D D^*\) has positive coefficients in all degrees from \(1\) through \(2q-1\).
Thus \(P_q^+\) has every coefficient positive.
For \(\lvert z\rvert=1\),
\[
       z^{-q}P_q^\pm(z)=|f(z)|^2\pm2+2|D(z)|^2.                   \tag{4}
\]
The plus sign makes the right side at least two, so \(P_q^+\) has no cyclotomic factor and Jensen's formula gives \(M(P_q^+)\ge2\).
For both signs, \(D^*=D\) and \((X-1)^2 D^2=(X^q-1)^2\).
Multiplying (3) by \((X-1)^2\) leaves the Mahler measure unchanged and bounds the coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm of the product by \(4(9+2)+8=52\).
The circle integral therefore gives \(M(P_q^\pm)\le52\).

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## Irreducible reciprocal factors of unbounded degree

At each \(q\)-th root \(z=e^{i\theta}\) with \(\cos\theta<-3/4\), one has \(D(z)=0\) and \(f(z)=2+z\).
For the minus sign in (4) the value is then \(3+4\cos\theta<0\).
At each intervening point with \(z^q=-1\), one has \(f(z)=z\) and \(|D(z)|=2/|z-1|\ge1\), so the value in (4) is at least one.
The grid points with \(\cos\theta<-3/4\) occupy an arc of fixed positive length.
Pairing each such grid point with the next midpoint gives disjoint open arcs whose number grows linearly with \(q\), each containing a root of \(P_q^-\).

Only finitely many roots of unity can occur anywhere in this sequence.
Indeed, if \(\Phi_b\) divides \(P_q^-\), its monic reduction divides both \(\bar P_q^-\) and \(X^b-1\).
Hence \(\varphi(b)\le2(1+\sqrt b)\) by (2).
On the other hand \(\varphi(b)\ge c b^{3/4}\) for an absolute \(c>0\), by multiplying the prime-power formula and using \(p-1\ge p^{3/4}\) at \(p\ge5\), with fixed constant losses at \(2\) and \(3\).
This bounds \(b\) independently of \(q\).
The numbers of distinct nontorsion roots on the circle therefore grow without bound.

For each such root its monic irreducible polynomial \(A\) divides \(P_q^-\), has unit constant coefficient and satisfies \(M(A)\le52\) by multiplicativity.
The conjugate of the circle root equals its inverse, so \(A\) and its reciprocal share a root.
Irreducibility identifies them up to a sign, and the negative sign would force \(A(1)=0\).
Hence \(A=A^*\).
At bounded degree there are only finitely many such \(A\), since each coefficient of a monic degree-\(h\) polynomial of measure at most 52 is bounded by \(52\binom{h}{i}\) at index \(i\).
The unbounded number of distinct nontorsion circle roots forces unbounded degrees among these actual reciprocal factors.
All of them inherit (2).
