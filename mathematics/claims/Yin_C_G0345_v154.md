# A two-variable measure for reciprocal polynomials with a central block

<a id="statement"></a>
## Comparing the full measure with its quadratic in a second variable

Let \(P\) be monic, separable and integral of degree \(d=2n\), with
\[
 P(z)=z^{2n}+1+z^nJ(z),\qquad
 J(z)=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^s a_j(z^j+z^{-j}),\qquad 1\le s<n/3,\quad a_s\ne0.
\]
Write \(L=\log M(P)\le A\), \(b=\log\max_{P(\alpha)=0}|\alpha|\), \(H=\max_{|z|=1}|J(z)|\), \(Q=\log^+(H/2)\) and \(D_d=\log(2d)/\sqrt d\).
Use \(\log^+(v)=\max(0,\log v)\), with value zero at \(v=0\).
There is \(c_A>0\) such that if \(sD_d\le c_A\), then
\[
                         Q\ll_A 1+\sqrt{sL}+s^2D_d.                            \tag{1}
\]
For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/3\), uniformly when \(s\le d^{1/3-\epsilon}\),
\[
                 m_2\big(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X)\big)
                          =L+O_{A,\epsilon}(d^{-\gamma}),\qquad
                   \gamma=\min(3\epsilon/4,1/24).                              \tag{2}
\]
Here \(m_2\) is logarithmic Mahler measure with both variables integrated over their circles.
The error is independent of the individual coefficients.
For noncyclotomic \(P\) this two-variable measure is strictly positive.

Suppose in addition that \(P\) is irreducible and nontorsion.
Along every such sequence with \(L\to0\), one has
\[
                s\gg L^{-3},\qquad
                sL\gg(db)^2\quad\hbox{if }s^2 D_d\le1.                        \tag{3}
\]
In the range of (2), a shrinking sequence would give strictly positive two-variable measures asymptotic to \(L\).
For general signatures and a macroscopic first nonzero coefficient there is also a degree-independent lower bound by a constant times the reciprocal of the number of nonzero coefficients, proved below.
The coordinator supplied the peak argument, the proposed transfer via conjugates, the two-variable interpretation and the sparse coefficient consequence.

<a id="discrepancy"></a>
## Angular discrepancy and movement along a ray

Write \(\alpha_i=e^{u_i+i\theta_i}\) and use normalized circle measure throughout.
The discrepancy between the fraction of the phases in any arc and its normalized length is \(O_A(D_d)\).
For details, let \(h=d^{-1/2}\) and majorize the indicator of the arc by convolving its enlargement by \(h\) at each end with normalized interval measure on \([-h,h]\).
Use the corresponding contraction for the minorant, allowing the empty or full circle at an endpoint of the length range.
Their means differ from the given length by \(O(h)\), and their nonconstant Fourier coefficients satisfy \(|c_k|\ll |k|^{-1}\min(1,(h|k|)^{-1})\).
For \(1\le k\le d\), the [reflected discriminant estimate (3)](Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases) gives a normalized squared sum with weight \(1/k\) of order \(O_A(\log(2d)/d)\).
Its proof uses only monicity, separability, an integral nonzero discriminant and total logarithmic measure, so applies to the stated products as well.
As \(\sum k|c_k|^2\ll\log(2/h)\), Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the contribution of the reflected Fourier coefficients by \(O_A(D_d)\).
The replacement by actual phases costs at most \(2kL/d\) per normalized moment, since \(\sum_i|u_i|=2L\) for a unit polynomial.
After multiplication by \(|c_k|\) the summed cost is \(O_A(\log(2d)/(dh))\).
The absolute Fourier tail past \(d\) contributes \(O(1/(dh))\), proving the discrepancy assertion for all arcs.
By integration by parts, any periodic function of bounded variation has phase-mean error at most this discrepancy times its total variation.

Put \(K(z)=z^sJ(z)\), a polynomial of degree \(2s\) with nonzero constant term.
Call a phase bad if its circular distance from the argument of any zero of \(K\) is at most \(\kappa=d^{-1/2}\).
The preceding arc estimate bounds the fraction of bad conjugate phases by \(O_A(sD_d)\), using a union bound.
On any other ray, for every nonzero radius the logarithmic derivative satisfies
\[
                 \left|\frac{zJ'(z)}{J(z)}\right|
                     =\left|-s+\sum_{K(\xi)=0}\frac z{z-\xi}\right|
                      \ll s/\kappa .
\]
Indeed the distance from \(1\) to a ray making angle at least \(\kappa\) with the positive axis is at least \(\sin\kappa\) when its angle is acute, and at least one otherwise.
Thus along a good ray from \(e^{i\theta_i}\) to \(\alpha_i\) the integral of the absolute logarithmic derivative against logarithmic radius is at most \(C s|u_i|/\kappa\).
The mean of these lengths over the full list, assigning zero to omitted rays, is \(O(sL/(d\kappa))\).

<a id="peak"></a>
## From the mean of the positive logarithm to the maximum

Put \(h_0(w)=\log^+(|w|/2)\) and \(I=\int h_0(J(e^{i\theta}))\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
The real trigonometric polynomial \(J(e^{i\theta})\) has at most \(2s\) critical points and at most \(2s\) zeros on the circle.
Between successive such points its absolute value is monotone, so the variation of \(h_0(J)\) is \(O(sQ)\).
At every root of \(P\), \(J(\alpha_i)=-(\alpha_i^n+\alpha_i^{-n})\), hence \(h_0(J(\alpha_i))\le n|u_i|\), with normalized sum at most \(L\).
On a good ray \(h_0\) changes by at most the logarithmic path length, and at a bad phase \(h_0(J(e^{i\theta_i}))\le Q\).
Angular discrepancy followed by radial comparison therefore gives
\[
                    I\le L+O_A(s QD_d+sL/\sqrt d).                             \tag{4}
\]

When \(Q>0\), put \(y=\min(1,Q/(4s))\).
The maximum on \(|z|=e^{-y}\) of \(|K(z)|\) is at least \(e^{-2sy}H\): the maximum modulus principle in the exterior, applied to \(K(z)/z^{2s}\), compares this circle with the unit circle.
At a maximizing point on the smaller circle \(\log|K/2|\ge Q-2sy\ge Q/2\).
The subharmonic function \(\log|K/2|\) is majorized inside the disk by the Poisson extension of the continuous boundary majorant \(h_0(K)=h_0(J)\).
The kernel at radius \(e^{-y}\) has maximum \(\coth(y/2)\ll1/y\), whence
\[
                         I\gg \min(Q,Q^2/s).                                   \tag{5}
\]
This bound also holds at \(Q=0\).
If \(sD_d\le c_A\) with a sufficiently small constant, (4) and (5) imply \(Q\ll_A L\) in the branch \(Q\ge s\).
For \(Q\le s\) they give \(Q^2\ll_A sL+s^2D_d Q\), yielding (1).

At a root of maximal modulus, the same exterior polynomial bound now gives \(|J(\alpha)|\le e^{sb}H\).
The root equation yields \(e^{nb}(1-e^{-2nb})\le e^{sb}H\).
Consequently \(nb\ll 1+Q\) since \(n-s>2n/3\).
For an irreducible shrinking input the [radial estimate at the house](Yin_C_G0345_v152.md#statement) gives \(db\gg1/L\) at first nonzero depth \(n-s>d/3\).
It follows that \(Q\gg db\) eventually.
When \(s^2D_d\le1\), (1) now yields \(sL\gg(db)^2\gg L^{-2}\).
If \(s^2D_d>1\), then \(s>d^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log(2d)}\), and the [first coefficient measure bound](Yin_C_G0345_v151.md#statement) gives \(L^{-3}\ll(\log(2d))^3\).
This proves (3) for every central span in the original range.

<a id="quadratic"></a>
## Transferring the quadratic measure

Let \(g(w)\) be the logarithm of the largest root modulus of \(Y^2-wY+1\).
It is nonnegative and even, and on the real line equals \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|w|/2)\) for \(|w|>2\) and zero otherwise.
Jensen's circle formula in \(Y\) identifies the left side of (2) with \(\int g(J(e^{i\theta}))\,d\theta/(2\pi)\).
At the actual conjugates \(g(J(\alpha_i))=n|u_i|\), because the two quadratic roots are \(-\alpha_i^{\pm n}\).
The empirical mean at these points equals \(L\) exactly.
On the circle \(g(J)\) has supremum at most \(Q+\log2\), and the same monotonicity intervals as above give total variation \(O(s(1+Q))\).
The bound \(nb\ll1+Q\) controls the root values even on bad rays.

For a nonzero complex path \(w\) of logarithmic length \(\lambda=\int|dw/w|\), the difference between the two endpoint values of \(g\) is \(O(\sqrt{\lambda}+\lambda)\), with an absolute constant.
To see this first take \(\lambda\le1/10\), with starting point \(v\).
The other endpoint has distance at most \(2\lambda|v|\) from \(v\).
For \(|v|\le4\) match the signs of the two square roots in the quadratic formula so that their difference is at most \(\sqrt{|(w^2-4)-(v^2-4)|}\).
The two matched pairs of quadratic roots then have distances \(O(\sqrt\lambda)\).
Taking the largest root modulus followed by its logarithm preserves this order since both maxima are at least one.
For \(|v|>4\) the small disk containing the path avoids \([-2,2]\), where the larger root would have unit modulus.
The analytic logarithm of the larger root there has derivative of modulus \(1/|\sqrt{w^2-4}|\ll1/|v|\), giving \(O(\lambda)\).
For \(\lambda>1/10\), subdivision into \(O(1+\lambda)\) subpaths of length at most \(1/10\) gives the asserted bound.

Apply this to the values of \(J\) along each good radial path.
Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the mean square-root length by the square root of the mean length.
Combining the bad phase count and the variation estimate with the radial bound yields
\[
 \left|m_2(Y+Y^{-1}+J(X))-L\right|
       \ll_A s(1+Q)D_d+\sqrt{sL/\sqrt d}+sL/\sqrt d.                           \tag{6}
\]
Inserting (1) for \(s\le d^{1/3-\epsilon}\) bounds its first term by a constant times \(sD_d+s^{3/2}D_d+s^3D_d^2\).
These and both remaining terms are \(O_{A,\epsilon}(d^{-\gamma})\) at the displayed \(\gamma\).
This proves (2).
For a shrinking irreducible input the measure lower bound used for (3) makes the error \(o(L)\) throughout this span range.

The two-variable measure vanishes exactly when \(|J|\le2\) on the entire circle, by continuity and the formula for \(g\) on real values.
In this case \(a_0^2+2\sum a_j^2=\int|J|^2\le4\).
Equality forces \(|J|\equiv2\), hence \(J\) constant.
Below four, a nonconstant integral profile is either \(\pm(z^s+z^{-s})\) or that polynomial plus a constant \(\pm1\).
The latter has supremum three, and the former makes \(P=(z^{n+s}\pm1)(z^{n-s}\pm1)\) with equal signs.
The constant profiles of absolute value at most two likewise give cyclotomic products.
This proves the claimed strict positivity for every noncyclotomic input.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## Coefficient length at a macroscopic gap in any signature

Let now \(P=z^d+\sum_{j=q}^d c_j z^{d-j}\) be any nontorsion algebraic integer unit minimal polynomial with \(c_q\ne0\) and \(q\ge\delta d\), for a fixed \(\delta>0\).
Write \(K_1=\sum_{j\ge q}|c_j|\), \(k\) for the total number of nonzero coefficients including the leading coefficient, and \(H_P\) for its coefficient height.
At a root of modulus \(e^b\), the equation directly gives \(e^{qb}\le K_1\).
Thus \(K_1\ge2\) by integrality, and the [house bound](Yin_C_G0345_v152.md#statement) proves \(L\log K_1\gg_\delta1\) as \(L\to0\).
The same inequality holds uniformly since \(K_1\ge2\) covers \(L\) bounded away from zero.
In particular
\[
          L\gg_\delta \frac1{\log(2+(k-1)H_P)},\qquad L\gg_\delta\frac1k .
\]
Indeed \(K_1\le(k-1)H_P\), and the [sparse coefficient height theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(H_P\le2^{k-2}e^L\).
For \(L\le1\) this bounds \(\log K_1\) by \(O(k)\), and \(L>1\) already gives the second assertion.
