# Near-one Mahler values with few exterior conjugates are Salem numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## Exterior conjugates of the actual value of an integer polynomial

Let \(P\ne0\) be an integer polynomial, \(A=M(P)>1\) its multiplicative Mahler measure, and \(m=\log A\).
Write \(l(A)\) for the number of distinct conjugates of the algebraic number \(A\) strictly outside the unit circle.
For every \(0<\eta<1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\) and all sufficiently small \(m>0\) depending only on \(\eta\), either \(A\) is a real Salem number or
\[
                 l(A)\ \ge\ \exp\!\big(\eta m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\big).
\]
The input may be reducible with arbitrary multiplicities.
For every fixed bound on \(l(A)\), sufficiently small positive measures in this class therefore come only from actual Salem values, including powers that are themselves Salem.

Consider also the nondegenerate relative delayed polynomials \(X^kP_0\pm(P_0^\tau)^*\) over the coefficient fields and under the real and complex place hypotheses in the [seed construction](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#statement).
Write \(s=\deg P_0\ge1\) and \(R>1\) for the unique exterior root at the marked split embedding.
For each fixed upper bound on \(s\), if \(R\) is sufficiently close to one, \(R^2\) is a real Salem number; if also \(k+s\ge3\), then \(R\) itself is Salem.
Conversely **every** real Salem number is realized as \(R\) by a nondegenerate seed of degree one and delay one, with norm polynomial equal to the square of its minimum.
Thus a uniform gap above one for these exterior roots with bounded seed degree is equivalent to the real Salem gap, even with unbounded coefficient fields, coefficients and delays.
The equivalence already holds for the class with seed degree and delay both one.

<a id="values"></a>
## Counting on both alternatives for the second iterate

Every conjugate of \(A\) has modulus at most \(A\), by the exterior-product construction or the [individual factor argument](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#factors).
At sufficiently small \(m\), \(A\) is an algebraic unit.
Hence \(\log M(A)\le l(A)m\).
On the [large second-iterate branch](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#statement) this gives the desired estimate immediately, taking \(m<1\).

On the remaining branch the [factor decomposition and projection](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval) write \(A=\rho^h\) for a positive reciprocal Perron unit \(\rho\) with \(m(\rho)=\log M(\rho)\le m\).
The power has full degree \(D=\deg\rho\).
Unless \(A\) is Salem, the projected difference at the selected coordinate of \(\rho\) is two: in its oriented half the coefficient of the exterior root \(\rho\) is \(1\), and the coefficients at each of the other \(b-1\) labels are \(-1\).
Its reciprocal half therefore supplies \(b-1\) more strictly exterior roots.
The labels correspond to distinct actual roots, so \(l(A)=l(\rho)\ge b\), whether or not the other blocks are neutral.

Here \(D=2ub\) and \(u\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\) at the absolute small-measure cutoff by the [block theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) applied to the matched chain term of degree \(D\).
The [degree inversion for block sizes](Yin_C_G0901_v89.md#minimal) applies because \(0<m(\rho)\le m\).
Explicitly, \(t=\log D\) tends to infinity by bounded-degree finiteness, and [Voutier's inequality](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(t>a\log t\) with \(a=(4m)^{-1/3}\).
For small \(m\) this implies \(t>a\log a\).
Subtracting \(212\log(t+\log3)+O(1)\) from \(t\) gives a lower bound for \(\log b\); this expression increases on the relevant interval.
At \(a\log a\) its ratio to \(m^{-1/3}\log(1/m)\) tends to \(1/(3\sqrt[3]4)\).
This proves the asserted estimate.

<a id="delays"></a>
## Bounded seed degree at unrestricted delay

Put \(H=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(X^kP_0\pm(P_0^\tau)^*)\), where \(K\) denotes the coefficient field of the relative construction.
The delayed polynomial is monic with constant \(\pm1\), so \(H\) is a monic integer polynomial.
At the marked embedding the other \(s+k-1\) roots are strictly interior, with product of moduli \(1/R\).
The opposite real embedding gives the reciprocal root multiset and all complex embeddings give only neutral roots by the [root location argument](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#delay).
Consequently \(M(H)=R^2\) and the entire norm polynomial has precisely \(s+k\) exterior roots counted with multiplicity.
Since \(R\) is a root of \(H\), \(l(R^2)\le l(R)\le s+k\).
The first inequality allows distinct roots to coalesce when squared.
The [uniform exclusion at delay at least ten](Yin_C_G0901_v128.md#statement) applies for every permitted coefficient degree \(u\ge2\).
Thus for \(R\) sufficiently close to one \(k\le9\), and \(l(R^2)\) stays bounded when \(s\) is bounded.
Applying the first assertion to \(H\) makes \(R^2\) a real Salem number.

If \(s+k\ge3\), each individual interior modulus at the marked place is strictly greater than \(1/R\), since their product is \(1/R\) and there are at least two of them.
Thus \(R\) is the unique largest-modulus root of \(H\), counting multiplicity.
Its conjugates remain distinct upon squaring, since a quotient \(-1\) between distinct conjugates could be transported to a pair of maximal modulus.
The conjugate moduli of \(R^2\) now imply that \(R\) has just one exterior root, one interior root and at least one neutral root.
It is reciprocal because a neutral conjugate and its complex conjugate are mutual inverses.
Hence \(R\) is itself Salem.

<a id="realization"></a>
## A linear seed for every Salem number

Fix a real Salem number \(\alpha>1\), put \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and let \(E=\mathbb Q(t)\) with its embedding \(t>2\).
The field is totally real of degree \(u\ge2\), with \(-2<\sigma t<2\) for all its other embeddings.
Choose a totally positive unit \(v\) of \(E\) with \(v>1\) at the marked place and \(0<\sigma v<1\) at all the others.
Indeed the logarithmic unit lattice has full rank in the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane by Dirichlet's unit theorem.
Large multiples of a vector with this strict sign pattern lie within bounded distance of lattice points in the same open cone; squaring the corresponding unit ensures positivity.
For a sufficiently large positive integer \(N\) write \(d_0=1-v^N\).
At the marked place \(d_0<0\), and at every other place \((\sigma t)^2/4<\sigma d_0<1\).
Let \(a>a'\) at the marked place be the roots of \(Y^2-tY+d_0\).
Its discriminant is positive there and negative everywhere else, so \(K=E(a)\) is the required quadratic extension and \(\tau a=a'\).
The roots are algebraic integers.
At the chosen place \(a>1\) and \(a'<0\); at all other places they are complex conjugates of common modulus \(\sqrt{\sigma d_0}<1\).
In particular \(P_0=X-a\) has nonzero constant and is nondegenerate.
For \(|z|=1\),
\[
          |z-a|^2-|1-a'z|^2=(a-a')(t-2\operatorname{Re}z)>0.
\]
Thus the seed meets the strict boundary inequality there as well.
With delay one and the plus sign its polynomial is exactly
\[
          X(X-a)+(1-a'X)=X^2-tX+1 .
\]
Its exterior root is \(\alpha\).
Its norm from \(E\) to \(\mathbb Q\) is the minimum of \(\alpha\), because \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):E]=2\); taking the norm from \(K\) instead repeats it twice.
