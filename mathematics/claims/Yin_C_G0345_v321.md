# A six-step Frobenius obstruction and two irreducible prime-degree specializations

The author-side strategy researcher for [G0001](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) derived the finite-field obstruction while testing whether the prime-degree method for the five-term family extends to the Boyd–Mossinghoff family.
The obstruction depends on the sign and on reducing at the characteristic of the exponent parameter.
The explicit characteristic-two certificate below shows that it is not an obstruction to irreducibility over the rationals.

<a id="statement"></a>
## Factor degrees at the parameter characteristic

Let \(q\ge2\) be a prime power, and define \(C_n\in\mathbb Z[X]\) by
\[
 C_0=2,\quad C_1=X,\quad C_{n+1}=XC_n-C_{n-1},
 \qquad C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}.
\]
For any \(c\in\mathbb F_q\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_{q,c}(X)&=C_q(X)+C_{q-1}(X)+X+c,\\
 H_{q,c}(z)&=z^qQ_{q,c}(z+z^{-1})\\
 &=z^{2q}+z^{2q-1}+z^{q+1}+cz^q+z^{q-1}+z+1.
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The integer coefficients of the \(C_n\) are reduced in \(\mathbb F_q\).
Every irreducible factor of \(Q_{q,c}\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) has degree in \(\{1,2,3\}\).
Every irreducible factor of \(H_{q,c}\) has degree in \(\{1,2,3,6\}\).
These assertions concern the degrees of the distinct factors and do not assert squarefreeness.
More precisely, each nonzero root \(z\) of \(H_{q,c}\) satisfies at least one of
\[
                   z^{q^2}=z,\qquad z^{q^3}=z^{-1}.              \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the squarefree part of \(H_{q,c}\) divides
\[
                  (z^{q^2-1}-1)(z^{q^3+1}-1),                  \tag{3}
\]
and the squarefree part of \(Q_{q,c}\) divides
\[
                     (X^{q^2}-X)(X^{q^3}-X).                   \tag{4}
\]

In particular, for any integer \(c\) and prime \(p>3\), the reduction of the monic degree-\(p\) polynomial \(C_p+C_{p-1}+X+c\) modulo \(p\) is reducible.
Changing only \(c\), even as a function of \(p\), cannot produce an irreducibility certificate from this reduction.
The negative-sign trace \(C_p+C_{p-1}-X-1\) from [the adjacent specialization](Yin_C_G0345_v311.md#specialization) is a different polynomial and is not covered by this assertion in odd characteristic.

At the fixed prime \(p=127\), both integer polynomials
\[
                   Q^{\pm}(X)=C_{127}(X)+C_{126}(X)\pm(X+1)    \tag{5}
\]
are nevertheless irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), as are their monic reciprocal lifts
\[
 \begin{split}
 H^{\pm}(z)&=z^{127}Q^{\pm}(z+z^{-1})\\
 &=z^{254}+z^{253}\pm z^{128}\pm z^{127}
                         \pm z^{126}+z+1.                     \tag{6}
 \end{split}
\]
The latter polynomials have degree \(254\).
This is one certified finite example for each sign, not an assertion of infinitely many irreducible prime indices or of a new limiting Mahler measure.

<a id="hexagon"></a>
## The two Frobenius alternatives

Over \(\mathbb F_q\), consider
\[
 K_c(x,y)=x^2y+xy^2+x^2+y^2+cxy+x+y.
\]
This polynomial is symmetric, and
\[
 K_c(x,y)=(x+1)y^2+(x^2+cx+1)y+x(x+1),\qquad
                         K_c(z,z^q)=zH_{q,c}(z).               \tag{7}
\]
All roots of \(H_{q,c}\) are nonzero because its constant coefficient is one.
Let \(x=z\), \(y=z^q\), and \(u=z^{q^2}\) for such a root in an algebraic closure.
Since the coefficients of \(K_c\) lie in \(\mathbb F_q\), Frobenius gives \(K_c(y,u)=0\), while symmetry gives \(K_c(y,x)=0\).

If \(y=-1\), then \(x=-1\), since Frobenius is an automorphism and fixes \(-1\).
This root lies in \(\mathbb F_q\) and satisfies the first alternative of (2), including in characteristic two.
Otherwise \(K_c(y,T)\) is a genuine quadratic whose constant term divided by its leading coefficient is \(y\).
If \(u=x\), the first alternative of (2) again holds.
If \(u\ne x\), the two distinct roots \(x,u\) of that quadratic satisfy \(xu=y\), so
\[
           z^{q^2}=\frac{z^q}{z},\qquad
           z^{q^3}=\frac{z^{q^2}}{z^q}=z^{-1}.                 \tag{8}
\]
This proves (2) without any separability assumption on the original specialized polynomial.

The second case has a literal six-step description on the two-dimensional torus:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \tau(x,y)&=(y,y/x),\\
 (x,y)&\longmapsto(y,y/x)\longmapsto(y/x,1/x)
 \longmapsto(1/x,1/y)\\
 &\longmapsto(1/y,x/y)\longmapsto(x/y,x)
 \longmapsto(x,y).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{9}
\]
The Laurent equation
\[
          K_c(x,y)/(xy)=x+y+x/y+y/x+x^{-1}+y^{-1}+c=0
\]
is invariant under \(\tau\).
When (8) holds, Frobenius on the pair \((z,z^q)\) equals \(\tau\); these maps commute, so its iterates have period dividing six.
In the first case Frobenius swaps the pair and has period dividing two.
In particular there is no freedom to choose a new branch at each iteration after the first alternative has been determined.

Both cases put \(z\) in \(\mathbb F_{q^6}\), proving the stated factor-degree possibilities for \(H\).
If \(T=z+z^{-1}\), the first case puts \(T\) in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), while the second gives
\[
                   T^{q^3}=z^{-1}+z=T.
\]
Conversely every root \(T\) of \(Q\) has a nonzero lift solving \(z^2-Tz+1=0\), and (1) puts that lift among the roots of \(H\).
Thus each trace root has degree dividing either two or three over \(\mathbb F_q\).
The polynomials on the right sides of (3)–(4) vanish at every respective root, which gives the squarefree-part divisibilities.

<a id="signs"></a>
## Which Boyd–Mossinghoff specialization is affected

For
\[
 F(x,y)=(1+x)+(1+x+x^2)y+x(1+x)y^2,
\]
the case \(c=1\) in (1) is exactly
\[
                         H_{q,1}(z)=F(z,z^{q-1}).              \tag{10}
\]
By contrast, \(F(z,-z^{q-1})\) has the three negative central coefficients in (6), and its trace has \(-X-1\).
For odd characteristic its Frobenius equation is \(K_1(z,-z^q)=0\), which does not supply the two common roots of the same quadratic used in (7)–(8).
No bounded factor-degree assertion for that negative-sign family follows from the argument.
In characteristic two the signs agree, but the exponent parameter must still be a power of that characteristic in order to use (1).

The two sign translations have the same fixed two-variable measure and the same limiting full measure under growing primitive directions, as recorded in [the balanced specialization argument](Yin_C_G0345_v313.md#balanced).
This equality of limiting measures therefore does not make their reductions at the parameter prime interchangeable.

## An exact irreducibility certificate at another prime

The two polynomials in (5) have the same reduction \(f\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
The following complete Python 3 calculation uses only integer bit operations and a trial-division check that \(127\) is prime.
Bit \(i\) represents the coefficient of \(X^i\).
It was executed as written, and every assertion passed.

```python
from math import isqrt

p = 127
assert all(p % d for d in range(2, isqrt(p) + 1))
c0, c1 = 0, 2                    # C_0=0 and C_1=X over F_2
for n in range(2, p + 1):
    c0, c1 = c1, (c1 << 1) ^ c0
f = c1 ^ c0 ^ 2 ^ 1
assert f == 0xe6060006000000060000000000000005
assert f.bit_length() - 1 == p
assert f & 1                     # f(0)=1
assert f.bit_count() % 2 == 1    # f(1)=1

def remainder(a):
    while a.bit_length() - 1 >= p:
        a ^= f << (a.bit_length() - 1 - p)
    return a

def square(a):
    b, i = 0, 0
    while a:
        if a & 1:
            b |= 1 << (2 * i)
        a >>= 1
        i += 1
    return remainder(b)

a = 2
for k in range(p):
    a = square(a)
assert a == 2                    # X^(2^127)=X modulo f
```

The final equality says that \(f\) divides \(X^{2^{127}}-X\), whose roots are exactly \(\mathbb F_{2^{127}}\) and are simple.
Each irreducible factor of \(f\) consequently has degree dividing \(127\).
The two preceding value checks exclude both linear factors over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Since \(127\) is prime and \(\deg f=127\), \(f\) itself is irreducible.
Both monic integer polynomials in (5) are therefore irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).

Finally \(C_{127}(0)=0\), \(C_{126}(0)=-2\), and \(C_n(2)=2\), so
\[
 Q^+(0)=-1,\quad Q^+(2)=7,\qquad
 Q^-(0)=-3,\quad Q^-(2)=1.
\]
Each \(Q^{\pm}\) has a real root \(T\in(0,2)\), giving a real embedding of its degree-\(127\) root field \(K\) at which \(T^2-4<0\).
Thus \(T^2-4\) cannot be a square in \(K\).
The equation \(z^2-Tz+1=0\) is irreducible over \(K\), and its root has degree \(254\) over \(\mathbb Q\), since \(T=z+z^{-1}\).
The monic degree-\(254\) polynomial \(H^{\pm}\) vanishes at that root, proving its irreducibility.

<a id="scope"></a>
## Consequence for construction choices

For the positive-sign prime family, reduction at the parameter prime cannot establish irreducibility, even if the central scalar coefficient is changed arbitrarily.
Reduction at another prime remains a valid route, as the example proves for both signs.
This work does not provide an infinite irreducible prime subsequence, and applying Chebotarev to a fixed polynomial would not establish that varying-index assertion.
The [fixed-polygon factor gap](Yin_C_G0345_v171.md#statement) still applies to the fixed \(c=1\) Boyd–Mossinghoff family.
The finite-field theorem allows varying \(c\), but supplies neither a new small-measure construction nor a measure estimate uniform in that variation.
The fixed-support obstruction in [Dobrowolski's theorem](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#sparse-measure) also excludes convergence to one through measures strictly greater than one for the full integral polynomials (1), which have at most seven collected terms, irrespective of the integer \(c\).
None of these observations is a uniform gap for arbitrary factors of arbitrary moving polynomials.
