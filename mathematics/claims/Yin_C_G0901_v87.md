# A large new alternating extension over all unrelated fields of smaller compressed degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## The base formed from every bounded-measure input through the target degree

Fix \(M>0\).
For a nonzero nontorsion algebraic number \(z\) let \(L_z\) be its rational splitting field, \(\gamma_z\) the product in its conjugate class modulo roots of unity, and \(d_z=\deg\gamma_z\).
Use total logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Say two inputs match when some conjugates have nonzero powers equal up to torsion.
Take \(y\) with \(0<m(y)\le M\), let \(d=d_y\), and let
\[
  K=\prod_{\substack{0<m(x)\le M,\ d_x\le d\\ x\ {\rm does\ not\ match}\ y}} L_x
\]
be the compositum in a fixed algebraic closure; an empty compositum is \(\mathbb Q\).
In particular the base includes every bounded-measure input in this range with smaller compressed degree, and arbitrary many unrelated inputs at the same degree.
The bound \(M\) applies to each contributing input, not to unrestricted generators of the fields once formed.

Let \(P_y\) be the normal field of the permutation action on the multiplicative labels of \(\gamma_y\), identifying conjugates with quotient or product torsion.
Take \(d\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M\) alone.
The [canonical alternating block action](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) has \(u\) blocks of size \(b\) and \(D_y=(A_b)^u\) acting independently on them.
Even after adjoining to \(K\) all towers of single algebraic adjunctions of degree at most \(b-1\) and all solvable extensions (at any steps), with resulting field denoted \(K_*\), restriction has
\[
       D_y\ \subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(K_*P_y/K_*)\ \le\
                      \operatorname{Gal}(P_y/\mathbb Q).                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular \([K_*P_y:K_*]\ge(b!/2)^u\).
The least allowable degree bound for such a tower over \(K\) to reach \(y\) is exactly \(b\).
This remains true if one starts only with \(\mathbb Q\) or any intermediate normal base \(\mathbb Q\subseteq K'\subseteq K\).
Solvable steps are allowed even in interpreting the lower bound.

Consequently, for a sequence of strictly decreasing degree records in any bounded measure range with degrees tending to infinity, every sufficiently late record requires such a new extension even over the combined splitting fields of *all* earlier records.
The required degree bound \(b\) is at least a constant depending on \(M\) times \(d/(\log(3d))^{212}\).
These restrictions do not prohibit a sequence using genuinely new nonsolvable fields.

As a consequence, let \(K_{\le D}\) be the compositum of splitting fields of all inputs with \(0<m(x)\le M\) and \(d_x\le D\).
For sufficiently large \(D\), its bounded-measure spectrum stays exactly the degree-\(D\) spectrum after arbitrary solvable steps and arbitrary steps of degrees up to \(c_M D/(\log(3D))^{212}\) for some \(c_M>0\).
Every bounded-measure element in that range already belongs to \(K_{\le D}\).
If any bounded-measure inputs of larger compressed degree exist, the least step bound which can introduce one is the minimum of their canonical block sizes.

<a id="small"></a>
## Reducing the entire base to a finite list of label fields

The [class product](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels) preserves total measure.
Its conjugates are distinct modulo torsion.
Two matching inputs have equivariantly matched compressed conjugates and equal compressed degrees and splitting fields.
Indeed any automorphism fixing one matched compressed number must fix the class of the other, hence its actual value by separation modulo torsion.
The same argument in reverse gives equality of their root fields after matching embeddings.
This uses the [matching-field argument](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#progressions); one can also raise the defining power relation before and after compression since each class product is a power of its representative up to torsion.

There are only finitely many compressed conjugate sets in the range \(d_x\le d,\ 0<m(x)\le M\), by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
The [solvable kernel over the label field](Yin_C_G0345_v267.md#normal) includes the contribution from the original radical and torsion fibers: \(L_x/P_x\) is solvable for each input, where \(P_x\) uses the compressed labels.
For small compressed degree the weaker fact that \(L_x/L_{\gamma_x}\) is solvable suffices; a power of every original root belongs up to torsion to the compressed field.
In the finite compressed list choose a single representative per matching class, excluding that of \(y\).

Choose a common degree threshold \(d_0(M)\) for the alternating block theorem and the [uniform independence theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v279.md#statement) at measure bound \(M\).
There is an integer \(U\ge d_0(M)\) with \(U\ll_M 1+(\log(3d))^{212}\) bounding the block counts of all inputs in the list with degree at least \(d_0(M)\), as well as the count for \(y\).
Since \(b\gg_M d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), eventually \(b>U\).
Partition the list according to whether it has an alternating block of size greater than \(U\) beyond the degree threshold.
Call these representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_t\); the list may be empty.

The other fields contribute no finite nonabelian simple section of order greater than \(U!\) beyond their solvable kernels.
Here a section is a quotient of a subgroup.
For compressed degree below \(d_0(M)\) the splitting group on the compressed conjugates is a subgroup of \(S_U\).
Otherwise the label group embeds in \(S_{b_x}\wr S_{u_x}\), with both indices at most \(U\).
The order bound on simple sections follows from the wreath product series and the coordinate projections in a direct product.
Explicitly, for a simple section of a subgroup of an extension, the image of the intersection with the normal kernel either surjects onto that simple group or is trivial, in which case the section comes from the quotient.
This argument also shows that the condition of having no \(A_b\) section is preserved by subgroups, quotients, extensions and finite direct products.
It is preserved on finite Galois subextensions of directed composita of such normal fields, since every finite set of elements already occurs in a finite compositum.
The inequality \(|A_b|=b!/2>U!\) excludes \(A_b\) from all these contributions.

<a id="independence"></a>
## Isolating the target before adjoining the other fields

Apply the independence theorem to \(y,x_1,\ldots,x_t\).
They are pairwise unmatched, all their block sizes exceed \(U\), and all their block counts are at most \(U\).
Define \(Z\) as the compositum of their label fields fixed by their individual alternating products \(D_z\).
Over \(Z\), the Galois group of the combined label fields is the direct product \(D_y\times\prod_i D_{x_i}\) under restriction.
Thus with \(B_0=Z\prod_iP_{x_i}\), the extension \(B_0P_y/B_0\) has group exactly \(D_y\), acting on its original labels.
When \(t=0\) this follows immediately from \(Z=P_y^{D_y}\).

Over \(B_0\), every finite Galois subextension of \(B_0K/B_0\) has no section \(A_b\).
For the large-block representatives their label fields are already present; all matching original fields add only solvable normal extensions.
For all other representatives use the preceding simple-section bounds and base change.
Both assertions persist on taking the possibly infinite compositum.
As \(B_0K/B_0\) is normal, its intersection with \(B_0P_y\) would give a common quotient of \(D_y\).
Every nontrivial quotient of a direct product of copies of the nonabelian simple group \(A_b\) retains a copy of \(A_b\), so the intersection is just \(B_0\).
It follows that \(B_0KP_y/B_0K\) still has group \(D_y\).

Over \(K\) the union \(K_*\) of the allowed towers is a normal field, stable also under rational conjugation because \(K/\mathbb Q\) is normal.
Composita of towers are again contained in towers by base change.
A finite tower of the prescribed steps embeds in a finite Galois extension over its starting field with no \(A_b\) section: take Galois closures one stage at a time, placing conjugate extensions in a direct product over the preceding normal field.
Each stage of degree below \(b\) has normal closure a subgroup of \(S_{b-1}\), and a solvable stage contributes only solvable groups.
The same reasoning after base change to \(B_0K\) and the same intersection argument preserve the whole \(D_y\) over \(B_0K_*\).
Restriction to \(P_y\) over the smaller base \(K_*\) proves (1).

<a id="degree"></a>
## The first sufficient adjunction size

If \(y\) were already in \(K_*\), rational normality would give \(P_y\subseteq L_y\subseteq K_*\), contrary to (1).
Conversely the label permutation group embeds in \(S_b\wr S_u\), with \(u<b\).
Stabilize the blocks one at a time, then the individual labels inside each block one at a time.
The successive subgroup indices are at most \(u\) in the first part and at most \(b\) in the second, and the last stabilizer is trivial by the faithful label action.
The associated intermediate fields give single adjunctions of these degrees from \(\mathbb Q\) to \(P_y\) by the primitive element theorem.
A solvable step reaches \(L_y\); base change supplies the desired tower over \(K\) or \(K'\).
This proves the exact threshold without a hypothesis on how fast the successive record degrees grow.
At a strict record the compression has the original degree, so strictly earlier degree records in the stipulated measure range are among the inputs of \(K\).
For a decreasing sequence one may enlarge its fixed bound \(M\) to cover the finitely many initial terms if necessary.

<a id="filtration"></a>
## Stability of the whole spectrum through a given degree

Fix \(D\) sufficiently large in terms of \(M\), and consider any \(y\) with \(0<m(y)\le M\) and \(d_y>D\).
Every field generating \(K_{\le D}\) is among the unmatched fields in the base for this \(y\), by inequality of the compressed degrees.
The exact threshold just proved gives \({b_y}\) as the first degree bound that reaches \(y\) also from \(K_{\le D}\).
All such \(b_y\) are bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of \(D/(\log(3D))^{212}\), by monotonicity of this function for sufficiently large arguments and the uniform alternating block estimate.
Choose \(c_M\) strictly smaller than that multiple.
Towers with smaller step bounds therefore admit no new element of measure in \((0,M]\); inputs already in that interval with compressed degree at most \(D\) belong to the base by definition.
The values they attain are exactly the positive measures up to \(M\) attained by primitive irreducible integer polynomials of degree at most \(D\) with nonzero roots, since compression preserves the measure and a degree-at-most-\(D\) input has compressed degree at most \(D\).
In particular, whenever this set is nonempty, its least measure is the ordinary positive minimum through degree \(D\), provided this minimum is attained by a nonzero algebraic number or lies below \(\log2\), as it does for large \(D\).
If some larger compressed degree occurs in the interval, the least of the positive integers \(b_y\) exists, and the individual realization proves sharpness at that bound.
