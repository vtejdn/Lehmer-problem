# Minimum-cardinality rows at a reciprocal unit with independent inverse pairs

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound at arbitrary positions and an exact rate at minimum width

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d=2s\ge4\).
Choose representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) for its inverse pairs and suppose they are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Put \(h=\log M(P)\), \(t_i=|\log|x_i||\), \(m=2d\), and let \(G=P P_2\), where \(P_2\) is the minimum of the square of a root of \(P\).
Fix either sign \(\varepsilon\).
Consider a nonzero integer row \(A\) of formal width \(b\) with
\[
              G^2\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A(X^{-n}).
\]
For every sequence \(n\to\infty\) of such rows on at most \(m+1\) positions, even when the width and positions vary arbitrarily, one has
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log\|A\|_1}{n}\ \ge\
 \sum_{i<j}\bigl(2\max(2t_i,t_j)+2\max(t_i,2t_j)+4\max(t_i,t_j)\bigr)
 \ \ge\ (3d-6)h.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Every passing row in the strict test \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) therefore needs at least \(2d+2\) positions at sufficiently large \(n\), with the onset allowed to depend on \(P\).
For a Salem seed (1) gives the stronger lower bound \((5d-10)h\).

At minimum width \(m\), let \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_d\) be the sorted list of the \(d\) numbers \(t_i,2t_i\).
Let \(T=x_1+x_1^{-1}\).
Provided its minimal polynomial is different from each of
\[
 T^2-46T-128,\qquad T^2+50T-32,\qquad T^2+14T+16,                   \tag{2}
\]
the primitive generator at width \(m\) satisfies the exact full-sequence limit
\[
       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_n\|_1}{n}
            =\sum_{k=1}^{d}(m-2k+2)v_k-2h .                         \tag{3}
\]
In particular (3) applies whenever \(d\ge6\) or \(M(P)\le2\).
Bound (1) applies also to the three trace polynomials in (2).

<a id="collisions"></a>
## Isolated and simultaneous collisions on a fixed sparse support

Independence implies that the \(2d\) elements \(x_i^{\pm1},x_i^{\pm2}\) are distinct and every quotient of two distinct elements is nontorsion.
They give all the roots of \(G\), and their group is freely generated by the representatives.
At a fixed width \(b\) and fixed set \(J\) of \(m+1\) positions use the [signed maximal minors and their height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) over the splitting field \(K\).
The position-\(l\) entries at each site \(y=z_i^k\), with \(c=\varepsilon x_i^k,\ k=1,2\), are \(y^l+cy^{b-l}\) and \(Nly^l+c(1+N(b-l))y^{b-l}\), for independent \(z_i\) and formal exponent \(N\).
Write \(C\) for the tuple of minors and \(D\) for its gcd in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\), and set \(Q=C/D\).
At \(N=0\) the two rows of each site separate into evaluations at \(y,y^{-1}\), up to invertible operations when \(y\ne0\).
The tuple \(C(0,\mathbf z)\) is nonzero by interpolation on \(m\) generic distinct positive nodes.
Indeed \(k\) distinct monomials form a nonsingular interpolation system for \(k\) positive nodes counted with multiplicities by the [positive zero bound](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#jets).
In particular \(D(0,\mathbf z)\) and \(Q(0,\mathbf z)\) are nonzero.

Work in the Laurent polynomial ring in the \(z_i\).
For a pair \(i<j\) consider the six irreducible divisors given by the vanishing of
\[
 z_i^2-z_j,\quad z_i^2 z_j-1,\quad z_j^2-z_i,\quad z_j^2z_i-1,
 \quad z_i-z_j,\quad z_i z_j-1 .                                    \tag{4}
\]
Each equation eliminates a variable using a Laurent monomial over \(\mathbb Q\), so its rational function field over \(\mathbb Q\) has constant field \(\mathbb Q\).
The first four give an isolated site collision and its inverse at a generic point.
The [sparse jet and projected rank calculations](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#rank) exclude common zeros of \(C\) with \(N\ne0\) approaching such a point when the other variables are fixed and algebraically independent over the algebraic numbers.
The use of the seed in that calculation consists of the nontorsion unit condition on the product of the two oriented labels and the inequality of the labels, both of which follow here from independence.

For each of the last two divisors, orient the second representative as \(x_j'\in\{x_j,x_j^{-1}\}\) so the two ordinary sites meet at \(w\), and their squares at \(w^2\).
At a generic positive point, \(w,w^{-1},w^2,w^{-2}\) and all uninvolved nodes are distinct.
Write \(\theta\) for the log separation of the ordinary sites, so the square separation is \(2\theta\).
In the [first-order collision determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#ordinary), the exceptional directions are \(\theta/N=\pm(q-q^{-1})\) where \(q^2=x_j'/x_i\) for the ordinary block.
For the square block they are \(2\theta/N=\pm(q^2-q^{-2})\).
There is no common exceptional direction because it would imply \(q+q^{-1}=\pm2\) and hence \(q^2=1\).
Along any putative sequence of rank loss pass to a convergent projective direction and normalize the four rows of a nonexceptional block as in the [two-collision proof](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#simultaneous).
Their limiting row space imposes values and first derivatives at the resolved inverse pair.
The remaining external conditions, including the fixed site of the other collision, give \(m-2\) conditions in total, with simple evaluations at the unresolved \(u,u^{-1}\), where \(u=w\) or \(w^2\).

The limiting conditions are rational over the function field of this divisor, including the derivatives at the resolved pair.
They are independent on the sparse columns: after adjoining first derivatives at \(u,u^{-1}\) and a second derivative at either of these two points, there are \(m+1\) conditions at positive nodes counted with multiplicities.
The positive zero bound gives a nonzero determinant for each enlarged system.
It follows that both determinants are nonzero rational functions and that the same ranks hold at an algebraically independent specialization of the free coordinates.
This argument also applies after reversing a site.
Let \(A_0\) span the one-dimensional kernel after adjoining just the two first derivatives.
The ratio
\[
                u^{4-b}\frac{A_0''(u)}{A_0''(u^{-1})}
\]
is a nonzero rational function over \(\mathbb Q\) on the divisor, since \(A_0\) can be chosen by rational minors.
At the chosen specialization it cannot equal either square root of the product \(pc\) of the two unresolved oriented labels.
Indeed \(pc\) is a nontorsion algebraic integer unit, hence is not rational, whereas an algebraic value of the displayed function at algebraically independent free coordinates would have to be rational.
The [third-coordinate obstruction](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#rank) therefore excludes rank loss for the unresolved block.
Its Taylor estimates use a convergent basis of the kernel of the remaining rows, available by solving at an invertible limiting minor after normalizing the resolved block.
They do not differentiate this basis with respect to the block parameters.
This proves the required exclusion also for the two simultaneous collisions.

<a id="binomials"></a>
## The binomial factors remaining at formal exponent zero

For each divisor in (4) take a transverse one-variable slice by fixing the variables other than \(z_i\) at algebraically independent positive values over the algebraic numbers.
At the selected point \(D(0,\mathbf z)\) cannot vanish: its restriction is not identically zero, and any such zero would persist nearby for small \(N\ne0\) by continuity of the roots, giving the common zeros just excluded.
For a square-root fiber either branch suffices to test nondivisibility in the Laurent polynomial ring, since the divisor is irreducible.
Thus none of (4) divides \(D(0,\mathbf z)\).

At \(N=0\), subtracting one evaluation row from the other in every colliding pair shows that each of the first four binomials divides each maximal minor at least twice, once at a site and once at its inverse.
Each of the last two divides it at least four times, from the four row differences at the ordinary sites and their squares and inverses.
Indeed the differences of the evaluation monomials vanish modulo the indicated binomial, and all row normalizations here are units in the Laurent ring.
All the listed binomials over all \(i<j\) are pairwise nonassociate primes, as seen from their distinct primitive exponent differences up to sign.
Consequently their product with the stated powers, denoted by \(B\), divides every entry of \(Q(0,\mathbf z)\).

At an embedding \(\sigma\) give \(z_i\) the weight \(u_i=\log|\sigma(x_i)|\).
Specializing \(N=0\) can only decrease the maximum exponent weight in the joint support of \(Q\).
Maximum weights add under multiplication of nonzero Laurent polynomials.
Choose one monomial from any one nonzero entry of \(Q(0,\mathbf z)/B\); its weights average to zero over all embeddings by the unit product formula.
The [projective height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) therefore gives a rate at least the average of the maximum weight of \(B\).
For the \(i,j\) factors this maximum, up to a linear form whose average is zero, equals
\[
 |2u_i-u_j|+|2u_i+u_j|+|u_i-2u_j|+|u_i+2u_j|
              +2|u_i-u_j|+2|u_i+u_j|.
\]
Using \(|a-b|+|a+b|=2\max(|a|,|b|)\) gives precisely the summand in (1) with \(t_i=|u_i|,t_j=|u_j|\).
The multiset of absolute weights is the same at every embedding because it permutes the inverse pairs up to inversion.
That summand is at least \(6(t_i+t_j)\), and \(\sum_i t_i=h\), giving (1) for each fixed support.
For a Salem seed only one \(t_i=h\) is nonzero and every incident pair contributes \(10h\).

For varying positions it suffices to consider a subsequence on which \(\log\|A\|_1/n\) is bounded and tends to the lower limit.
[Width compression](Yin_C_G0345_v459.md#statement) preserves the coefficients and divisibility by \(G^2\) at a width bounded independently of \(n\) when the number of terms is at most \(m+1\).
Pass to one fixed compressed support.
By [column independence](Yin_C_G0345_v446.md#statement) the row eventually has exactly \(m+1\) terms and is an integer multiple of the primitive generator.
This proves (1) with the stated quantifiers.
The [degree estimate from Voutier's Corollary 2](Yin_C_G0345_v457.md#height) gives \((3d-6)h>(\log2)/8\) for every \(d\ge4\), proving the budget exclusion.

<a id="exact"></a>
## The exact rate at the consecutive positions

At width \(m\) the [minimum-width gcd proof](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#ends) uses the Salem assumption to obtain independence, to verify three nonzero center expressions, and finally to average the weights.
The ordinary collision, inversion collision, simultaneous collision and endpoint computations themselves use only the nonsingular interpolation of consecutive monomials, independence and nontorsion of the labels.
To check the center explicitly put \(T_i=x_i+x_i^{-1}\).
The leading jet determinant at the two signs is nonzero if \(T_i^2-46T_i-128\ne0\) and \(T_i^2+50T_i-32\ne0\), respectively, by the [center calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#center).
The remaining positive-sign obstruction uses \(a=(1-x_i)/(1+x_i)\) and \(6-3a^2-a^4\).
Here \(a^2=(T_i-2)/(T_i+2)\), so multiplying the last expression by \((T_i+2)^2\) gives \(2(T_i^2+14T_i+16)\).
All other factors and denominators in that calculation are nonzero by nontorsion.
All \(T_i\) are conjugate and \(\deg T_i=d/2\), since the inverse conjugate furnishes the second distinct root of \(X^2-T_i X+1\) over the trace field.
The three quadratics have nonsquare discriminants \(2628,2628,132\).
Each has a conjugate of absolute value greater than \(5/2\) by the sum of its roots.
For \(M(P)\le2\) each root has \(1/2\le|x_i|\le2\) by reciprocity, hence \(|T_i|\le5/2\) for every conjugate.
These facts verify the conditions asserted after (3).

When (2) is avoided, the [local exclusions and the projective-line argument](Yin_C_G0345_v456.md#height) now give exactly the same raw gcd \(\prod_i z_i^2(z_i-1)^4\) up to scalars.
For generic directions on the \(z_i\), the [extremal center minor](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) retains its unique highest monomial after substituting each pair of sites by \(z_i,z_i^2\).
Its weights use the distinct absolute values of the \(d\) site weights, and continuity gives the exact maxima also when weights tie or vanish.
For arbitrary \(u\) let \(v_k\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \(|u_i|,2|u_i|\).
The joint maximum of the raw tuple is \(3m\sum_i u_i+\sum_{k=1}^d(m-2k+2)v_k\).
Subtract \(2\sum_i u_i+4\sum_i\max(u_i,0)\) for the gcd.
The sums linear in the signed weights average to zero, while \(\sum |u_i|=h\) in every embedding.
Thus the average of the subtracted expression is \(2h\), and the fixed-support height formula proves (3).
