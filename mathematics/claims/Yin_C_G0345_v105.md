# The boundary logarithm of a cyclotomic product on a divisibility chain

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two-sided bounds and a uniform gap for one-monomial shifts

Let \(d_1<\cdots<d_N\) be positive integers with \(d_i\mid d_{i+1}\), and let \(b_i\) be real.
Write \(\nu=d\theta/(2\pi)\), \(\|b\|_2^2=\sum_i b_i^2\), and
\[
            L(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^N b_i\log|1-e^{id_i\theta}|.
\]
Then
\[
     \frac{\|b\|_2}{3\sqrt2(e^{1/2}+1/2)}
                  \ \le\ \|L\|_{L^1(\nu)}
                  \ \le\ \frac{\pi}{2}\|b\|_2 .
     \tag{1}
\]
The constants are independent of the chain length and all the ratios.
In particular, for nonzero integer \(b_i\) and \(S=\sum_i|b_i|\), the norm bounds \(\sqrt S\) from below up to an absolute constant, and \(N\le S\).

Suppose \(A(X)=\pm\prod_i(1-X^{d_i})^{b_i}\) is a nonconstant polynomial with integer exponents of either sign.
For every complex \(a\) with \(|a|\ge1\) and integer \(k\ge0\), one has
\[
        1+m(A+aX^k)\ \asymp\ 1+\|b\|_2+\log|a|,
        \qquad m=\log M ,
     \tag{2}
\]
with absolute constants.
In particular the full logarithmic measures tend to infinity when \(S\to\infty\), uniformly over \(k\), \(a\) and the chain.
For every nonzero integer \(a\), the positive values of \(m(A+aX^k)\) have an absolute uniform gap above zero.
The chain condition concerns the occupied indices of the signed binomial exponents of \(A\), which may extend over arbitrarily many multiplicative scales.

<a id="frequencies"></a>
## Fourier coefficients of signed sums of chain frequencies

Put \(\widehat L(t)=\int L(\theta)e^{-it\theta}\,d\nu\).
The [binomial Fourier formula](Yin_C_G0345_v94.md#multiplicity) gives, for \(t\ne0\),
\[
          -2|t|\widehat L(t)=\sum_{d_j\mid t}b_jd_j .
\]
Let \(c_i=-2\widehat L(d_i)=d_i^{-1}\sum_{j\le i}b_jd_j\).
Then \(b_1=c_1\) and \(b_i=c_i-(d_{i-1}/d_i)c_{i-1}\) for \(i>1\).
The ratios \(d_{i-1}/d_i\le1/2\) give \(\|b\|_2\le(3/2)\|c\|_2\) by the triangle inequality.
Also \(|b_i|\le3\|L\|_1\) since each \(|c_i|\le2\|L\|_1\).

Select either the even or the odd indices of the original chain so that \(\sum_{i\ {\rm selected}}c_i^2\ge\|c\|_2^2/2\).
List the selected indices as \(i_1<\cdots<i_s\) and put \(D_j=d_{i_j}\), so \(D_{j+1}\ge4D_j\).
For any nonempty subset of selected frequencies with signs \(\pm1\), write \(t\) for their signed sum and let \(u,v\) be its first and last selected positions.
The smaller frequencies have absolute sum at most \(D_v/3\), giving \(|t|\ge D_v/2>0\).
Every original \(d_j\) with \(j\le i_u\) divides \(t\).
If \(i_u<N\), then \(t/D_u\equiv\pm1\bmod (d_{i_u+1}/D_u)\), so no later original \(d_j\) divides \(t\).
Consequently
\[
          -2\widehat L(t)=\frac{c_{i_u}D_u}{|t|},
          \qquad
          |\widehat L(t)|\le2\|L\|_1\,4^{-(v-u)} .
     \tag{3}
\]
This uses the entire original chain, including its unselected indices.

<a id="riesz"></a>
## A weighted trigonometric product

The coordinator supplied the divisibility test for mixed frequencies and the imaginary-part Riesz product.
Weighting each selected coefficient by its contribution to the square sum gives (1).
If \(b=0\), (1) holds immediately.
Otherwise \(T=(\sum_j c_{i_j}^2)^{1/2}>0\).
Set \(u_j=|c_{i_j}|/T\), \(\sigma_j=\operatorname{sgn}(-c_{i_j})\), and use the real test
\[
          R(\theta)=\operatorname{Im}\prod_{j=1}^s
             \bigl(1+i\sigma_j u_j\cos(D_j\theta)\bigr).
\]
Since \(\sum_j u_j^2=1\), its absolute value is at most \(\prod_j(1+u_j^2)^{1/2}\le e^{1/2}\).
The linear terms of \(\int LR\,d\nu\) contribute
\[
        \sum_j\sigma_j u_j\widehat L(D_j)=T/2 .
\]
Only odd-sized subsets contribute to the imaginary part.
For each subset of size at least three, the product of cosines is the average of the exponentials of all its signed frequency sums.
If its endpoints are at positions \(j,j+h\), then \(h\ge2\), and (3) bounds its integral against \(L\) in absolute value by \(2\|L\|_1\,4^{-h}\).
The sum of the coefficient weights of all such subsets with those endpoints is at most
\[
             u_j u_{j+h}\prod_{j<l<j+h}(1+u_l)
                \le u_j u_{j+h}\,2^{h-1}.
\]
For every \(h\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_j u_j u_{j+h}\le1\), with the sum over existing pairs.
Therefore the sum of all mixed contributions in absolute value is at most
\[
        2\|L\|_1\sum_{h\ge2}4^{-h}2^{h-1}=\tfrac12\|L\|_1 .
\]
As \(|\int LR\,d\nu|\le e^{1/2}\|L\|_1\), it follows that \(T/2\le(e^{1/2}+1/2)\|L\|_1\).
Together with \(\|c\|_2\le\sqrt2T\) and \(\|b\|_2\le(3/2)\|c\|_2\), this proves the lower bound in (1).

<a id="upper"></a>
## The quadratic upper bound

For \(d>0\) put \(\lambda_d(\theta)=\log|1-e^{id\theta}|\).
Its constant Fourier coefficient is zero.
Its coefficients at \(t=\pm md,\ m\ge1\), are \(-1/(2m)\), with all other coefficients zero.
For \(d_i\mid d_j\), Parseval on their common frequencies gives
\[
       \int\lambda_{d_i}\lambda_{d_j}\,d\nu
          =\frac{d_i}{2d_j}\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{m^2}
          =\frac{\pi^2}{12}\frac{d_i}{d_j}.
\]
Thus the absolute covariance is at most \((\pi^2/12)2^{-|i-j|}\) in either order.
For each difference \(h\), \(\sum_i |b_i b_{i+h}|\le\|b\|_2^2\), and \(\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}2^{-|h|}=3\).
This bounds \(\|L\|_2^2\) by \((\pi^2/4)\|b\|_2^2\) and proves the upper bound for \(\|L\|_1\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Signed binomial products and their shifts

For integer \(b_i\), polynomiality of the displayed product is equivalent to \(\sum_{j\ge i}b_j\ge0\) for each \(i\).
Necessity follows from the multiplicity at a primitive \(d_i\)-th root.
For sufficiency, any root of any binomial factor has order dividing a suffix of the chain, so its net multiplicity is one of those sums.
All finite poles therefore cancel, and the remaining product is an integer polynomial up to sign by cyclotomic factorization.
For a nonconstant such product all roots lie on the unit circle, the constant and leading coefficients have modulus one, and \(L=\log|A(e^{i\theta})|\) has mean zero.

Let \(G=A+aX^k\) with \(|a|\ge1\).
The [circle-root integral bound](Yin_C_G0345_v90.md#statement) and the pointwise triangle inequality give
\[
 \max\{\tfrac12\|L\|_1,\log|a|\}-\sqrt\pi\,\zeta(3/2)
       \ \le\ m(G)\ \le\ \tfrac12\|L\|_1+\log(1+|a|).
\]
Here \(\int L^+\,d\nu=\|L\|_1/2\).
For \(k>0\) the constant coefficient of \(G\) has modulus one, and for \(k=0\) its leading coefficient has modulus one, so \(m(G)\ge0\) in all cases.
Using (1) and adding one gives (2).
For integer \(b_i\), one has \(S\le\sum_i b_i^2\), which proves divergence as \(S\to\infty\).
The square-root order in \(S\) is attained up to constants for \(d_i=2^{i-1}\), all \(b_i=1\), and fixed \(|a|=1\), because then \(S=N=\|b\|_2^2\).

Now let \(a\) be a nonzero integer.
If \(m(G)\le1\), (1) and the circle-root estimate bound \(S\) by an absolute constant.
Multiplying \(G\) by \(\prod_{b_i<0}(1-X^{d_i})^{-b_i}\) gives the sum of two monomial multiples of products of at most \(S\) binomials each, with bounded term count independent of the exponents and the scalar.
This integer polynomial has measure \(M(G)\) because the multiplier has measure one.
The [bounded-term gap theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) gives an absolute lower bound for \(m(G)>0\) in this range, and the complementary range already has \(m(G)>1\).
