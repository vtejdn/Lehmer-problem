# The joint degree and width spectrum of sparse transverse rows

<a id="statement"></a>
## A computable relation for each positive measure

Fix \(s\ge2\), an integer \(H\ge1\), and a prime \(p>\max(3,p_0(s))\), using the effective bound in the [mixed-block base enumeration](Yin_C_G0345_v484.md#bases).
Let \(A_+,A_-\) be nonzero integer rows of at most \(s\) terms each and one-norm at most \(H\), with positions independently chosen in a common integer width \(b\ge0\).
Require that the two signed graphs
\[
       F_\sigma(X)=A_\sigma(X^p)+\sigma X^{pb+1}A_\sigma(X^{-p}),
                        \qquad \sigma\in\{1,-1\},
\]
have genuine double contacts at \(\gamma,\gamma^2\), with nonzero numerators \(A_\sigma(\gamma^p),A_\sigma(\gamma^{2p})\).
Here \(\gamma\) is a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) whose square also has degree \(d\).
The [transverse determinant](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement) may additionally be required to be nonzero.

There is an effective finite list of the distinct logarithmic Mahler measures \(h=m(\gamma)>0\) that occur.
For each one it is possible to compute the entire relation
\[
    \mathcal R_h=\{(d,b)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
           \text{such a pair of measure }h\text{ exists at degree }d
           \text{ and formal width }b\}.
\]
The output for \(\mathcal R_h\) is a finite Boolean combination of integer linear inequalities and congruences with fixed moduli, a Presburger formula in \(d,b\).
The width, its ratio to the degree, and the normalized span of the row positions may all be unbounded.
Higher powers of \(\gamma\) may have smaller degrees.
Both one-coordinate projections and every prescribed degree or width fiber are computable finite unions of one-sided arithmetic progressions and finite sets.
At every realized degree the least admissible width is computable, and the graph of this minimum has an effective Presburger formula.
Further linear conditions on degree and width can be imposed directly.

<a id="radicals"></a>
## A finite family of bases for all radical degrees

The [base construction](Yin_C_G0345_v484.md#bases) used no width bound.
Indeed a nonzero numerator at \(\gamma^p\) supplies a mixed minimal simultaneous zero subset \(I\) in one graph.
With \(g\) the gcd of the exponent differences in this subset, the [normalized range theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v483.md#statement) bounds its normalized exponent range effectively in terms of \(s,H\) at the chosen \(p\).
The normalized integer block has one-norm at most \(2H\) and vanishes at \(x=\gamma^g\).
Enumerating the finitely many such polynomials gives a list containing the required \(x\).

Use the [radical modulus and square-degree test](Yin_C_G0345_v475.md#statement) for the nontorsion unit roots on this list.
This yields finitely many \(M,m,\xi\), where \(m\mid M,\ \xi^m=x\), and \(\xi\) is reciprocal of degree \(f=[\mathbb Q(\xi^2):\mathbb Q]\).
For each tuple take \(k\ge1\) odd with \(\gcd(k,M/m)=1\), and any \(\gamma^k=\xi\).
Every such \(\gamma\) is reciprocal with \(d=fk=[\mathbb Q(\gamma^2):\mathbb Q]\) and \(m(\gamma)=m(\xi)\).
Conversely every passing unit occurs this way, using \(m=\gcd(g,M)\) for its block.
For \(u=1,2\), the first \(k\) powers starting with one of \(\gamma^u\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q(\xi^u)\), since \((\gamma^u)^k=\xi^u\) and the two field degrees have ratio \(k\).

<a id="patterns"></a>
## Lifting fixed-base exponent differences to variable radical order

Fix such radical data and a finite labeled list of nonzero coefficients \(c_i\).
Consider a Laurent sum \(B(X)=\sum_i c_iX^{a_i}\) with integer exponent variables \(a_i\).
For \(u=1,2\) choose required orders \(l_u\in\{0,1,2\}\) with at least one positive order.
We claim that the full set of \((k,(a_i)_i)\) satisfying those orders at \(\gamma^u\), among the permitted \(k\), is described by a finite union of systems of the form
\[
          a_i-a_{i_B}=k\,t_{B,i}\qquad(i\in B,\ B\in\mathcal P).          \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\mathcal P\) runs over partitions of the labeled terms, \(i_B\) is a pivot in \(B\), and the finitely many integers \(t_{B,i}\) are obtained from the fixed base \(\xi\) with \(t_{B,i_B}=0\).
The translations of different parts of the partition are unrestricted.

To prove necessity for any actual exponents, form the residue classes \(v\bmod k\) using \(0\le v<k\), and in each write \(a_i=kw_i+v\).
The value at \(\gamma^u\) is
\[
              \sum_v(\gamma^u)^v\sum_{i:\,a_i\equiv v}c_i(\xi^u)^{w_i}.
\]
Independence forces the inner sums to vanish when \(l_u\ge1\).
For \(l_u=2\) the Euler derivative contributes the same sums multiplied termwise by \(kw_i+v\).
The value equation cancels the \(v\) contribution, so the additional requirement is the vanishing of \(\sum_i w_i c_i(\xi^u)^{w_i}\) in each such class.
Thus for the polynomial in the \(w_i\) of a single class the conditions are exactly the fixed orders \(l_u\) at the corresponding \(\xi^u\).

Apply the [finite enumeration of differences in minimal vanishing subsets](Yin_C_G0345_v485.md#positions) to these coefficients and this base.
Its proof needs only that \(\xi\) is a nontorsion unit, so it has an embedding of modulus greater than one.
After translating the top exponent to zero, a prefix of a minimal subset has a nonzero value or Euler derivative at one prescribed conjugate power.
The remaining terms and their needed derivatives tend uniformly to zero there when all their distances below the top grow.
This bounds the next distance effectively for each prefix and leaves finitely many relative exponent lists for the minimal subsets.
Enumerate over every possible labeled subset and partition.
Within each resulting block the fixed differences of the \(w_i\) become (1).
There is no need to bound the quotients \(w_i\) themselves.

For sufficiency, each normalized template polynomial \(\sum_{i\in B}c_iY^{t_{B,i}}\) has the indicated orders at \(\xi^u\).
By (1) this block contributes \(X^{a_{i_B}}\sum_{i\in B}c_i(X^k)^{t_{B,i}}\) to \(B(X)\).
It vanishes to the required orders at \(\gamma^u\) by substitution at a nonzero point, independently of the offsets of the other blocks.
Summing the blocks proves the claimed equivalence, including possible coincidences between different block remainders modulo \(k\).
Every constraint (1) is linear in \((k,(a_i)_i)\) since its right-hand coefficient is fixed.

<a id="relation"></a>
## The joint relation and its fibers

Enumerate the ordered coefficient lists of both rows at the prescribed norm and term bounds.
Impose \(0\le j_{\sigma,1}<\cdots<j_{\sigma,t_\sigma}\le b\) and substitute \(a_i=pj_{\sigma,i}\) or \(a_i=pb+1-pj_{\sigma,i}\), with the corresponding coefficients, into (1).
For each graph use \(l_1=l_2=2\).
For each numerator and each \(u\) use only its first-layer terms with \(l_u=1\) and \(l_{3-u}=0\).
Negate that single-node vanishing formula to impose the desired nonzero value.
For the transverse determinant, use the [partition by equal exponents](Yin_C_G0345_v484.md#placement) of its two products with the formally reversed rows.
At least one combined coefficient must be nonzero, and all equality and disequality tests on these exponents are again linear.
The residue restrictions on \(k\) and \(d=fk\) are linear conditions with fixed moduli.

Use the [integer elimination procedure](Yin_C_G0345_v484.md#projection) to project out all positions and \(k\), retaining both \(d\) and \(b\).
Union the formulas for tuples with equal measure \(m(\xi)\), deciding equality by comparing the algebraic multiplicative measures.
Discard empty formulas by a further projection.
The forward and converse in (1), together with realization by any admitted \(k\)th root of \(\xi\), give exact equality with \(\mathcal R_h\).
Projecting out either coordinate or specializing a fixed coordinate yields a formula in a single integer.
Outside an effective finite interval its inequalities stabilize, leaving periodic congruences.
For the graph of the minimum width retain \(\mathcal R_h(d,b)\) together with the negation of \(\exists t\,(0\le t<b\ \land\ \mathcal R_h(d,t))\), using the same elimination.

<a id="threshold"></a>
## The strict threshold spectrum at each sufficiently large prime

Fix an exactly specified real algebraic \(Q\) with \(1<Q\le6/5\) and choose \(\epsilon=0\) or \(1\).
Take a computable term cap \(s_p\to\infty\) on a cofinite set of primes \(p>3\), slow enough that \(p>p_0(s_p)\) for each tested prime.
For example one may take the largest \(2\le s\le p\) with \(p>\max_{2\le t\le s}p_0(t)\), omitting primes with no such \(s\).
Retain genuine transverse pairs over all \(d,b\) satisfying
\[
                    2(2pb+1)^\epsilon H_+H_-<Q^{6p}.                      \tag{2}
\]
Let \(V_p(Q)\) be the set of their positive logarithmic measures.
An effective individual norm bound follows from (2) and \(H_\pm\ge1\).
At fixed coefficient lists the optional width charge gives a computable upper bound on \(b\), or an empty branch, by exact algebraic comparison.
Thus the full bivariate relation for each value in \(V_p(Q)\) is computable under either budget.

With \(\lambda\) the infimum of the positive logarithmic Mahler measures of nonzero integer polynomials, one has
\[
\begin{split}
 \lambda\ge\log Q
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_p(Q)=\varnothing\ \text{at every tested prime}\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_p(Q)=\varnothing\ \text{at infinitely many tested primes}.
\end{split}
\]
Every represented value lies strictly below \(\log Q\) by the [width-independent determinant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v468.md#statement).
Conversely every positive irreducible measure strictly below \(\log Q\) occurs at every sufficiently large prime by the [fixed-seed construction](Yin_C_G0345_v480.md#spectrum).
Its all-power representative is included in the present target class, its fixed width gives a fixed term bound, and the additional charge costs \(o(p)\) in the logarithm.
If \(\lambda<\log Q\), some integer polynomial below the threshold has a positive irreducible factor of measure below the threshold.
This proves the equivalence while leaving the behavior across infinitely many primes undetermined.

The coordinating agent and a collaborating agent for Sheng Yin independently combined fixed-base difference patterns with the radical basis decomposition to remove the bound on the width-to-degree ratio.
