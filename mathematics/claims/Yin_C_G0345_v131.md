# Arbitrarily long chains of quadratic trace descents ending at Lehmer's number

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two fixed rules below the plastic number

Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\), \(f=z^3-z-1\), \(f^*=1-z^2-z^3\), and \(\theta>1\) for the real root of \(f\).
An operation on a Salem minimal polynomial \(z^m S(x)\) adds an indicated integer polynomial \(R(x)\) to its monic trace polynomial and takes the Salem factor of the full lift \(z^m(S+R)(x)\).
The descents below all strictly decrease the **full** Mahler measure of the lift and give exactly one noncyclotomic factor.

For each \(c\in\{-2,2\}\) take the sequence \(n_0=8,\ n_{j+1}=2n_j+c\).
Each polynomial
\[
             B_n(z)=\frac{z^n f(z)-f^*(z)}{z-1},\qquad n=n_j,
\]
is the entire irreducible minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(\beta_n\).
Its degree is \(n+2\), and \(\beta_8\) is Lehmer's number.
The \(\beta_{n_j}\) strictly increase to \(\theta\).
From any \(j>0\), repeated use of the single quadratic
\[
             R_{-2}(x)=x^2+x-1,\qquad R_{2}(x)=(x+1)^2               \tag{1}
\]
chosen according to \(c\) gives exactly the \(j\) strict steps from \(\beta_{n_j}\) to \(\beta_8\) along the displayed indices.
One further use of that rule at \(\beta_8\) gives a product of cyclotomic polynomials.

One can also start above \(\theta\) from arbitrarily large inputs of the family on which [all nonzero constant levels fail](Yin_C_G0345_v129.md#statement) and [exactly two quadratic shifts succeed](Yin_C_G0345_v130.md#statement).
The first step \(R(x)=x^2-2\) takes that input below \(\theta\) to \(\beta_n\) and the remaining steps end at Lehmer's number.
There are at least \(2^j\) distinct such starting inputs with chains of \(4j+3\) strict steps for every sufficiently large \(j\).
The lengths, root inequalities and factor identities are exact.
The two continuations in (1) give measure one at the terminal input.

<a id="minus"></a>
## The increasing Salem roots of the negative completions

For \(n\ge8\) put \(F_n=z^nf-f^*\).
The cubic is Pisot, with one root \(\theta>1\) and its other two roots in the open unit disk, as checked for the [positive completions](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family).
For \(0<t<1\), Rouché's theorem on the unit circle gives \(z^n f-tf^*\) exactly \(n+2\) roots inside, since \(|f|=|f^*|>0\) there.
By continuity of the roots, \(F_n\) has at most one root outside with multiplicity.
Now \(F_n(1)=0,\ F_n'(1)=7-n<0\), and \(F_n(\theta)=-f^*(\theta)>0\).
Thus it has a simple root \(\beta_n\in(1,\theta)\), and anti-reciprocity supplies its inverse as the only interior root.
The monic reciprocal \(B_n\) has constant coefficient one.
Its unique outer and inner roots belong to the same irreducible factor, since all factors are monic units and their products of root moduli are one.
The other factors are cyclotomic by [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001), and the reciprocal quadratic exception is impossible since \(\theta+\theta^{-1}<3\).
Hence the exterior root is Salem.
Since
\[
 F_{n+1}(\beta_n)=(\beta_n-1)\beta_n^n f(\beta_n)<0,
\]
we have \(\beta_{n+1}>\beta_n\).
Their limit is \(\theta\) by taking \(n\to\infty\) in \(f(\beta_n)=\beta_n^{-n}f^*(\beta_n)\), since \(\beta_n\ge\beta_8>1\).
Direct division at \(n=8\) gives
\[
       B_8=z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1 .
\]
This is [Lehmer's polynomial](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0164/readme.md#boyd-descent-search).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## When the negative completion is the actual minimal polynomial

For even \(n\ge8\) let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(d\)-th root dividing \(F_n\), and \(g=f^*\).
There is no pole of \(g/f\) at \(\zeta\).
The equation \(g(\zeta)/f(\zeta)=\zeta^n\) also holds at every primitive conjugate.
For odd \(d\) compare \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta^2\), for \(4\mid d\) compare \(\zeta\) and \(-\zeta\), and for \(d\equiv2\pmod4\) compare \(\zeta\) and \(-\zeta^2\).
In each case the second point is primitive of the same order.
Since \(n\) is even, the three resulting eliminants respectively are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g(z^2)f(z)^2-f(z^2)g(z)^2&=-2(z-1)^3(z+1)\Phi_3^2\Phi_5,\\
 g(-z)f(z)-f(-z)g(z)&=-2z\Phi_{12},\\
 g(-z^2)f(z)^2-f(-z^2)g(z)^2&=2\Phi_{12}\Phi_{30}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are polynomial identities by multiplication, with the cyclotomics evaluated at \(z\).
The candidates are therefore \(d=1,3,5,12,30\).
For \(d=3,5,12,30\), respectively, division of \(z^j f-g\) by \(\Phi_d\) has remainder zero at \(j=1,2,4,6\), respectively.
Each such \(j\) then determines the only class modulo \(d\), since \(\zeta\) is primitive and \(f(\zeta)\ne0\).
The root at one is removed exactly once by \(F_n'(1)=7-n\).
In particular \(B_n\) is precisely the Salem minimal polynomial whenever
\[
         n\not\equiv1\pmod3,\quad n\not\equiv2\pmod5,\quad
         n\not\equiv4\pmod{12},\quad n\not\equiv6\pmod{30}.           \tag{2}
\]
For constant \(c=-2\), the residues of the sequence starting at 8 modulo 60 are first 8 and then the cycle \(14,26,50,38\).
For \(c=2\) they are first 8 and then the cycle \(18,38\).
All satisfy (2).

<a id="transfer"></a>
## Exact products at every step

For even \(n\) and \(c=\pm2\) expand
\[
 (z^{n+c}+1) B_n
 =B_{2n+c}+z^{n+(c+2)/2}\,
       \frac{z^{-(c+2)/2}(f-z^c f^*)}{z-1}.                         \tag{3}
\]
The last quotient is the symmetric Laurent polynomial \(R_c(x)\) of (1), by substitution.
The exponent \(n+(c+2)/2\) is half the degree of \(B_{2n+c}\).
Thus (3) applies directly to its minimal trace when (2) holds and adds only the cyclotomic cofactor \(z^{n+c}+1\) to the smaller polynomial.
At \(2n+c=8\), use \(n=5\) for \(c=-2\) and \(n=3\) for \(c=2\).
Identity (3) still holds by its algebraic derivation, and
\[
             B_5=(z+1)(z^6-z^3+1),\qquad B_3=(z+1)(z^4+1)
\]
are cyclotomic products.

For the entry from above \(\theta\), take \(N=2n+1\) and use the positive completion \(q_N=z^N f+f^*\).
For all indices in either constant-\(c\) sequence, \(N\) is odd, \(N\not\equiv7\bmod8\), and \(N\not\equiv21\bmod30\), by their listed residues and evenness of \(n\).
Thus \(q_N\) itself is the Salem minimal polynomial by the [positive-completion factor table](Yin_C_G0248_v1.md#family-cyclotomic-factors).
The [quadratic product identity](Yin_C_G0345_v130.md#factorization) here reads
\[
      q_{2n+1}+z^{n+2}(x^2-2)=(z^{n+1}-1)(z-1)B_n.                 \tag{4}
\]
The input lies above \(\theta\) and the full output has measure \(\beta_n<\theta\).

<a id="many"></a>
## Many starting polynomials at the same chain length

First take the two steps \(8\to18\to38\) in the increasing index direction, both with \(c=2\).
Starting from any index congruent to 38 modulo 60, one block of four choices \(c=2\) returns to that residue via \(18,38,18,38\).
Four choices \(c=-2\) also return there via \(14,26,50,38\).
Every intermediate index therefore satisfies (2).
The two block maps are \(n\mapsto16n+30\) and \(n\mapsto16n-30\).
After \(j\) independently chosen blocks the \(2^j\) indices are distinct, since in a difference of two signed base-sixteen sums the largest differing power dominates the sum of all smaller powers.
They all satisfy \(n\equiv38\bmod60\), so (4) joins their positive completions to the chains.
The initial positive Salem numbers are distinct by the [positive family calculation](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family).
Reversing the two initial steps and the \(4j\) block steps after (4) gives \(4j+3\) strict descents.
These starting Salem numbers approach \(\theta\) from above as the chain lengths grow, while the terminal number stays fixed.
