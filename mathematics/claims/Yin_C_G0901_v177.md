# Two full residue periods recover the exponents of a trace-pole realization

<a id="statement"></a>
## A dense-coefficient test and a polynomial-time bounded-degree census

Let \(q,D\ge2\), with \(q,D\) integers, \(p=q+1\), \(M=pq\), \(L=p+q\), and \(d=2D\).
Let \(F(Z)=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\) be the monic integral reciprocal minimum of a [genuine first nonunit trace-pole realization](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return).
Write \(A_c(F)\) for the multiplicative order of \(Z\) modulo \((c,F)\), including all multiplicities and using the entire integer modulus \(c\).
Put \(A=A_q(F),\ B=A_p(F)\).
For \(q\ge9\) one has
\[
             A=v-u,\quad B=v+u,\qquad
             u=(B-A)/2,\quad v=(B+A)/2,                                \tag{1}
\]
where \(T=\beta^u\) is the exterior root of \(F\) and \((pT-q)/(p-qT)=\beta^v\) for the common positive unit with coprime exponents.
Thus the two actual residue words of \(F\) determine the exponents.
In particular \(\gcd(A,B)\mid 2\), \(B>A\), and
\[
                  0<B-A<(\log(3d))^3/q .
\]
Neither the sizes nor the supports of the coefficients of \(F\) are restricted.
The [earlier composite period result](Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#composite) concerns the minimum of the primitive source \(\beta\) and sufficiently large heights on more general reciprocal sections.
Here the index between the two orders and the prescribed pole give (1) on the final polynomial with the stated numerical threshold.

For all \(q\ge2\), every binary reduction \(F\bmod 2\) divides some \(Z^N-1\) with \(N\ll d(\log(3d))^3\log\log(9d)\).
The number of monic degree-\(d\) binary polynomials passing this last necessary test is at most
\[
                      \exp\!\left(O\!\left(\frac{d\log\log(9d)}
                                                   {\log(3d)}\right)\right).   \tag{2}
\]
This bounds the output of an explicit residue sieve on coefficient words before any root or height tests.
The [stronger count of actual realizations](Yin_C_G0345_v899.md#statement) includes arithmetic and archimedean conditions.

There is also a deterministic algorithm which lists every pair \((q,H)\) of trace degree at most \(X\), over all heights and grids, in bit time polynomial in the numerical degree bound \(X\).
It requires at most
\[
             O\!\left(X(\log(3X))^6(\log\log(9X))^2\right)
\]
integer polynomials to factor, each of degree \(X(\log(3X))^{O(1)}\) and coefficient bit length \((\log(3X))^{O(1)}\).
This complexity assertion is independent of coefficient density and does not assert an executed census or an all-degree exclusion.

<a id="periods"></a>
## The composite orders of the final polynomial

Let \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)>1\).
The [two powers](Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion) give the \(u,v,\beta\) of the statement.
The fields generated by \(\beta\) and \(T\) agree, since \(Y\in\mathbb Q(T)\) and Bézout's identity expresses \(\beta\) as a Laurent monomial in \(T,Y\).
In every embedding \(|\sigma\beta|^u=|\sigma T|\), hence \(\beta\) has exactly one conjugate of modulus greater than one and \(m(\beta)=\log\beta\).
All these powers are units.
In \(R=\mathbb Z[\beta]\) the equation
\[
              q(\beta^{u+v}-1)=p\beta^u(\beta^{v-u}-1)
\]
gives \(\beta^{v-u}=1\bmod qR\) and \(\beta^{v+u}=1\bmod pR\).
These congruences follow by cancellation of units in each quotient, without assuming a field or reduced ring.
The suborder \(\mathbb Z[T]\) has [additive index \(L\)](Yin_C_G0345_v897.md#index) in \(R\).
Since \(L\) is prime to \(pq\), its inclusion induces isomorphisms modulo \(q\) and \(p\), as follows from a matrix for the inclusion of the equal-rank free abelian groups with determinant of absolute value \(L\).
The order of \(\beta\) in either quotient divides \(v-u\) or \(v+u\) respectively, both prime to \(u\).
Its \(u\)-th power \(T\) has the same order there.
Consequently \(A\mid v-u,\ B\mid v+u\).

The pole norm \(N(p-qT)=L^2\) and \((p-qT)(Y+1)=T+1\) give
\[
           Y+1\le (T+1)L^{d-3},\qquad YT<L^d .
\]
Indeed \(|p-q\sigma T|\le L\) at every other embedding and \(1<T<p/q\le3/2\).
If the order \(a\) at a modulus \(c=q,p\) were a proper divisor of the corresponding \(v\mp u\), then \(\beta^a-1\in cR\) and
\[
                   c^d\le |N(\beta^a-1)|<2^d\beta^a
                              \le 2^d\sqrt{YT}.
\]
The integer norm is nonzero and every conjugate other than \(\beta\) contributes at most two.
This implies \(c^2<4L\), which is impossible for either full modulus as soon as \(q\ge9\).
It proves (1).
Finally [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#vou96-u001) gives \(\log\beta>2/(\log(3d))^3\); multiplying by \(u\) gives \(\log T<1/q\), proving the difference bound.

<a id="words"></a>
## An explicit binary sieve and an incompatible pair of dense words

Even for \(q<9\), the order in the binary quotient divides the order at the even full modulus, which divides \(v-u\) or \(v+u\).
The preceding bounds give
\[
 q<(\log(3d))^3/2,\qquad
 v+u\le \frac{d\log L}{\log\beta}
                 \ll d(\log(3d))^3\log\log(9d).
\]
Thus the required \(N\) is polynomially bounded, and modular powering suffices to test the binary divisibility.
For a fixed such \(N\), split the binary irreducible factors at degree \(\lfloor\frac12\log_2 d\rfloor\).
There are \(O(\sqrt d)\) distinct polynomials below or at that cutoff, and their multiplicities in a degree-\(d\) divisor give at most \((d+1)^{O(\sqrt d)}\) possible choices.
Above the cutoff there are at most \(N\) distinct irreducible factors of \(Z^N-1\); at most \(k=O(d/\log(3d))\) occurrences can be selected, counting repeats.
There are at most \(\binom{N+k}{k}\) such multisets, allowing unused slots.
Taking \(k\) of order \(d/\log d\) and using the displayed upper bound on \(N\) gives \(\log\binom{N+k}{k}=O(d\log\log(9d)/\log(3d))\).
Summation over polynomially many \(N\) preserves the bound (2).
The argument also covers repeated inseparable factors in characteristic two.

To see the effect of testing the two orders together, fix \(q\ge9\).
Choose coprime odd \(a,b\ge3\) with \(a\equiv3,\ b\equiv1\bmod M\).
For example choose \(b=M+1\) and choose arbitrarily large \(a\equiv3\bmod M,\ a\equiv1\bmod b\).
The dense reciprocal polynomial
\[
        P=(1+Z+\cdots+Z^{a-1})(1+Z+\cdots+Z^{b-1})
\]
has degree \(d=a+b-2\) and coefficient 2 immediately below its leading one.
The order of \(Z\) in the quotient by each geometric sum, modulo any \(c\ge2\), is exactly its length \(a\) or \(b\).
The power of that length is one, and a proper positive divisor is smaller than the monic degree when the length is at least three.
Over the integers the product divides \(Z^{ab}-1\), since the nonidentity roots of the two geometric sums are disjoint and simple.
It follows that the order for \(P\) modulo every \(c\) is \(ab\).
In particular at \(q,p\) the two positive orders agree.

This is a consistent residue class of the exact integral pole lattice.
Under the triangular reciprocal-to-trace map the coefficient immediately below the leading term of the monic trace polynomial of \(P\) is \(d+2\equiv4\bmod M\).
The integral [pole lattice](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#simplex) is \(X^{D-1}(X+4)+(MX-1)\mathbb Z[X]_{\le D-2}\); modulo \(M\) it realizes every choice of the remaining lower trace coefficients.
Choose a representative in this lattice reducing to the trace of \(P\).
For any prime \(\ell\nmid M\), [one-prime approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v171.md#approximation) then gives an irreducible monic reciprocal polynomial over \(\mathbb Z[1/\ell]\) with the same reductions, exact pole and strict root signature.
No integral representative with the strict signature can exist in this entire class by (1).
This test concerns coefficients modulo the joint modulus; the dense integral polynomial \(P\) above supplies the residue word and need not itself have the pole.

## Enumerating exponents before factoring over the rationals

Let \(X\ge2\) and choose an effective integer \(U\) comparable with \((\log(6X))^3\) and above \((\log(6X))^3/2\).
Such an integer can be taken as \(8(1+\lceil\log_2(6X)\rceil)^3\) using binary length.
By the preceding bounds every true pair of degree at most \(X\) has
\[
      2\le q\le U,\qquad 1\le u\le U/q,\qquad v\le 2XU\log(2q+1).
\]
Round the last cutoff upwards using binary length if desired.
Summing the number of triples gives \(O(XU^2(\log(2U))^2)\), since \(\sum_{q\le U}q^{-1}\log(2q+1)=O((\log(2U))^2)\).
For each such triple with \(u<v,\ \gcd(u,v)=1\), factor the integer polynomial
\[
                     Z^v(p-qZ)^u-(pZ-q)^u .
\]
It is nonzero, of degree \(v+u\), and its coefficient length is at most \(2L^u\) by the binomial theorem.
The common-power equation makes the minimum \(F\) of every realization a factor for its triple.
Use deterministic [rational polynomial factorization](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454) and keep monic integral reciprocal factors of even degree at most \(2X\).
Recover each monic trace polynomial by the integral triangular map and check the exact pole and prescribed simple root counts as in the [grid-independent decision](Yin_C_G0345_v892.md#digits).
Output the passing \((q,H)\), removing duplicates.
The stated factor degree and coefficient bit bounds make each factorization polynomial in \(X\), as are construction of the factor list and rational arithmetic for the pole.

For clarity the exact signature test also has polynomial bit cost.
The factorization theorem bounds the bit size of its returned factors by its polynomial running time.
The reciprocal triangular conversion can use \(Z^j+Z^{-j}\) expressed by the integral two-term recurrence in \(Z+Z^{-1}\), and the translation by two; their coefficient bit sizes are \(O(X)\).
For a squarefree trace factor \(H\), the rational trace matrix on the power basis weighted by a linear polynomial \(g\) has entries \(\operatorname{Tr}(g(x)x^{i+j})\) in the rational algebra \(\mathbb Q[x]/H\).
Its signature counts the signs of \(g\) at all real roots, while every nonreal conjugate pair has zero signature for nonvanishing \(g\), by the decomposition over \(\mathbb R\) into real lines and complex planes.
Apply this first at \(g=1\) to require all roots real, then at \(x+4,\ 1/M-x,\ x\), excluding roots at the endpoints and checking the numbers of positive and negative roots.
Square tests by gcd and these trace entries (computed by Newton's identities), as well as the determinant minors used for rational matrix inertia, have polynomial bit complexity: the matrix orders are \(O(X)\), entries have polynomial bit length by the recurrence, and fraction-free elimination uses minors of polynomial bit length by Hadamard's bound.
Thus all postprocessing and deduplication preserve polynomial bit time.
The parameter \(X\) is the degree ceiling itself; this estimate does not give a height-independent cutoff on the degree.
