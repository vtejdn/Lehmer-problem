# Large multiplicative orders at common residue roots for separated exponent primes

<a id="statement"></a>
## A residue field of large characteristic and a common root of large order

Let \(P_1,P_2\) be monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minima of degrees \(e,f\) and logarithmic Mahler measures \(h_1,h_2\), respectively.
The minima may coincide.
Let \(K\) be the compositum of their splitting fields and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\), write \(D=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and set \(\delta=1/(2(D+1))\).
For a prime \(p\), let \(P_{1,p}\) be the polynomial with roots the \(p\)-th powers of all roots of \(P_1\), counted with their original multiplicities.
Use the corresponding notation \(P_{2,q}\).
Suppose \(P_{1,p}\) has degree \(e\) as an irreducible polynomial and \(\operatorname{Res}(P_{1,p},P_2)\ne0\).
These conditions hold for every sufficiently large \(p\) at fixed seeds.

Fix such a \(p\) and let \(q\) tend to infinity through primes.
For all sufficiently large \(q\), the positive divisor \(B_0\) defined by the [local integer conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local) exists for these two powered lists.
Put
\[
 N=\prod_{\substack{\ell\mid B_0\ \mathrm{prime}\\ \ell>q^\delta,\ \ell\nmid pq}}\ell^{v_\ell(B_0)}.
\]
Then
\[
      \liminf_{q\to\infty}\frac{\log N}{q}\ge\frac{h_2}{f}>0.       \tag{1}
\]
Suppose rationally coprime integral rows \(U,V\) have the two double contacts \(P_{1}^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) and \(P_{2}^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q)\).
Then \(N\) divides the positive generator of \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\).
For **each** prime divisor \(\ell\) of \(N\), both rows and both powered minima have a common nonzero residue root of degree at most \(\min(e,f)\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
Its multiplicative order \(t\) in the residue field satisfies
\[
        t>\frac{\delta\log q-e\log 2}{p h_1}.                       \tag{2}
\]
The mass in (1) counts prime powers.
The order in (2) is already measured in a field.

<a id="mass"></a>
## A linear lower bound after removing all small characteristics

Write \(z_i=a_i^p,\ w_j=c_j^q\) for roots \(a_i,c_j\) of the two seeds, and \(d_{ij}=z_i-w_j\).
Full degree at a sufficiently large prime for a fixed seed follows from the [power criterion](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#gcd).
A coincidence between \(a_i^p\) and \(c_j\) requires \(p h_1/e=h_2/f\) by equality of absolute heights, which excludes at most one first prime.
A coincidence between \(a_i^p\) and \(c_j^q\) likewise requires \(p h_1/e=q h_2/f\), which excludes at most one second prime for the chosen \(p\).
Thus the powered lists have full size and are disjoint for all sufficiently large \(q\).
Their integer resultant \(C=\prod_{ij}d_{ij}\) satisfies
\[
        \log|C|\ge eqh_2-O_{P_1,P_2}(\log(2q))
\]
by the [mixed-power norm estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#archimedean).

For any finite prime of \(K\) write \(v\) for its integral valuation.
The [local conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local) give \(v(B_0)\ge v(d_{ij})\) on equal labels.
On unequal labels they give \(v(B_0)\ge v(d_{ij})-v(2)-3v(pq(a_i-c_j))\), because \(v(d_{ij})\ge0\).
Summing over the \(ef\) labels and then over the finite places with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/D\) gives
\[
            ef\log B_0\ge\log|C|-O_{P_1,P_2,p}(\log(2q)).
\]
The error includes the fixed nonzero label differences, all ramification and both exponent primes.
Passing to \(K\) preserves the integrality tests and their least positive rational integer.

The [two-logarithm valuation argument](Yin_C_G0345_v508.md#small), using [Yu's bound](../audits/literature/Yu99/Yin_U001.md#fixed-input-logarithmic-bound), applies with exponent maximum \(q\) in this same field.
In particular \(v(d_{ij})\ll_{P_1,P_2}\ell^D\log(2q)\) at each prime over \(\ell\).
Because \(B_0\mid |C|^3\), the logarithmic cost of removing all \(\ell\le q^\delta\) is \(O_{P_1,P_2}(q^{1/2}(\log(2q))^2)=o(q)\).
This already removes the fixed \(p\) for large \(q\).
In characteristic \(q\), all elementary symmetric functions of the \(c_j^q\) are the \(q\)-th powers of the corresponding elementary symmetric functions of the \(c_j\).
Their rational integer coefficients give \(P_{2,q}\equiv P_2\bmod q\).
Hence \(C\equiv\operatorname{Res}(P_{1,p},P_2)\bmod q\), a fixed nonzero integer modulo every sufficiently large \(q\).
Such \(q\) are absent from \(B_0\), proving (1).

<a id="order"></a>
## The order of a first powered node

For each \(\ell\mid N\), a label and a place above \(\ell\) realizing a positive condition on \(B_0\) give the [common row and powered-node residue](Yin_C_G0345_v508.md#rows).
This argument uses only the original double contacts, the local divisibilities and the nonvanishing of the node units.
The integer Bézout divisibility follows from the [same local conditions](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local).
In fact every root of \(P_{1,p}\) modulo \(\ell\) is the reduction of some \(a_i^p\) at a place of \(K\) above \(\ell\).
If such a residue has order \(t\), its place divides \(a_i^{pt}-1\).
The rational integer \(\left|N_{\mathbb Q(a_i)/\mathbb Q}(a_i^{pt}-1)\right|\) is nonzero and divisible by \(\ell\).
Bounding each conjugate difference in absolute value by \(2\max(1,|a_j|)^{pt}\) yields
\[
           q^\delta<\ell\le 2^e\exp(pt h_1),
\]
which proves (2).

<a id="schedule"></a>
## An unrestricted zero-infimum test with prescribed orders

Let \(b_n\to\infty\) and \(T_n\to\infty\) be any computable sequences of positive integers.
There exist computable strictly increasing sequences of first and second primes \(p_n,q_n\), with \(p_n<q_n\), such that the following assertions hold at each \(n\).
Use the finite list of reciprocal nontorsion unit minima of degree at most \(b_n\) and multiplicative Mahler measure at most two.
For every ordered pair from this list with double contacts at the two primes, the rows admit the divisor \(N\) from (1), with \(N^{2f}\ge M(P_2)^{q_n}\).
At every prime factor of \(N\) the four polynomials have a common nonzero root of multiplicative order greater than \(T_n\).

Enumerate the finite list by the [coefficient and real algebraic tests](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width), with cutoff two in the elementary symmetric coefficient bound.
Search for the first prime above \(n\) and the preceding first prime, if any, with full powered degree and \(\operatorname{Res}(P_{1,p},P_2)\ne0\) for every ordered pair of listed seeds.
Each fixed finite list meets these tests for all sufficiently large first primes.
For this first prime, choose the first prime \(q>p\) above the preceding second prime, if any, such that every listed ordered pair has full disjoint powered degrees and satisfies
\[
           N^{2f}\ge M(P_2)^q,\qquad q^\delta\ge 2^e M(P_1)^{pT_n}.
\]
The splitting field degree, \(B_0\), its rational prime factors and the displayed real algebraic comparisons are all computable, using the [local divisor test](Yin_C_G0345_v505.md#local).
Equations (1) and (2) justify termination and the assertion about every prime of \(N\).
If the list is empty these tests are vacuous.

The unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures is zero if and only if actual [two-prime rows](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) of widths \(b_n\) exist for every sufficiently large \(n\), with \(\log H_n/(p_n+q_n)\to0\) and with at least one listed qualifying seed at each prime.
Here \(H_n=\max(\|U_n\|_1,\|V_n\|_1)\).
Indeed the [weighted contact inequality](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) bounds the unrestricted infimum from above by the normalized row cost.
Conversely, if the infimum is zero, take reciprocal unit minima with positive measures tending to zero as in the [proof of the common-row limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap).
For each fixed such seed with \(M<2\), the [common interpolation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#construction) eventually works within the given widths at both primes and gives limsup normalized cost at most twice its log measure.
Thus the minimum among rows with listed qualifying seeds tends to zero, by taking these fixed measures arbitrarily close to zero.
The graph test and its integer coefficients make each finite minimum attainable once its normalized cost is below the initial value \(\log 2\).
This leaves open whether such rows of vanishing cost exist.

A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin derived the separated-prime argument by combining the fixed first-node norm with exclusion of the second exponent prime by Frobenius.
