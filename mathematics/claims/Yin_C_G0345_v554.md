# Degree and measure of every factor of a reciprocal six-term polynomial with unit coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A criterion at all exponents

Let \(0<a<t<N/2\), \(c_0,c_1,c_2,\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), and put
\[
 A(X)=c_0+c_1X^a+c_2X^t,\qquad
 F(X)=A(X)+\epsilon X^N A(1/X).
\]
These are all reciprocal or antireciprocal polynomials with six distinct terms of coefficient modulus one and nonzero constant term, up to sign.
For every monic irreducible noncyclotomic rational factor \(P\) of \(F\), put \(d=\deg P\) and \(m=\log M(P)\).
With absolute implied constants, one has
\[
 \log(1+0.31/6!)\le \frac{N m}{d}\le 5\log 6,\qquad
 d\gg \max(t,\sqrt{aN}).                                             \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(m\to0\) if and only if \(d/N\to0\), with no restriction on the exponents.
Whenever \(t/N\) stays bounded below, each such factor occupies a uniformly positive fraction of the degree.

On **every** sequence with \(t/N\to0\), for any choice of the factor one has
\[
                   \frac{Nm}{d}-\log M(A)\longrightarrow0 .           \tag{2}
\]
This also holds on every sequence with \(d/N\to0\), by (1).
If \(t/\gcd(a,t)\to\infty\), the limit in (2) of \(Nm/d\) is the same positive number
\[
       \mu=\int_{\mathbb T}\log^+|1+u|\,du
\]
for all choices of signs, where \(du\) denotes normalized arclength.
For fixed reduced exponents \(a/\gcd(a,t)=p,\ t/\gcd(a,t)=q\), the comparison value is the log measure of \(c_0+c_1X^p+c_2X^q\).
The degree criterion leaves existence of a sequence of small-degree factors open.

<a id="cuts"></a>
## The middle cut and the degree bound

For variables \(y,z\) define
\[
 U=c_0+c_1y+c_2z,\quad V=c_0z+c_1z/y+c_2,\quad
 \Phi(y)=1+c_0c_1 y+y^2 .
\]
The identity
\[
          c_2 y V-(c_0y+c_1)U=-c_0c_1\Phi(y)                         \tag{3}
\]
eliminates \(z\), and \(\Phi\) is either the third or the sixth cyclotomic polynomial.
At a root \(x\) of \(P\) take \(y=x^a,z=x^t\), so \(U=-\epsilon x^{N-t}V\).
These are algebraic integers, \(x\) is a unit and \(y\) is nontorsion.
Thus \(U,V\ne0\), and both generate the same principal ideal in the ring of integers of \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
This proves nonvanishing at the middle cut of the six terms.
At each other cut, one side is a monomial or signed binomial and cannot vanish at \(x\).
The [sparse cut estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v250.md#cuts) summed over the five cuts gives \(Nm/d\le 5\log 6\).
The [sparse divisor bound](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#dob06-u001) and concavity of the logarithm give the other side of (1).
The [six-term cluster bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v202.md#clustering) give \(t/N\ll m,\ a/N\ll m^2\) for small positive \(m\), with absolute constants.
They also hold for \(m\) bounded below since \(a,t<N\); equivalently a violation of a uniform constant would give a sequence with \(m\to0\).
Using the upper bound on \(m\) gives the two absolute degree estimates.

<a id="norm"></a>
## Controlling the logarithm at a common zero

Assume \(t/N\to0\), and write \(\mathbb E\) for the uniform average over all embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(x)\) into \(\mathbb C\).
Use the same letters for the embedded quantities inside the average.
For an algebraic unit \(v\), its absolute logarithmic height is \(h(v)=\mathbb E\log^+|v|\), and \(\mathbb E|\log|v||=2h(v)\).
By (1), \(h(x)=m/d\ll1/N\), so \(h(y)+h(z)\to0\).
Since \(U=-\epsilon x^{N-t}V\),
\[
       (N-t)h(x)=\mathbb E\log\max(|U|,|V|)-\mathbb E\log|V|.        \tag{4}
\]
Equation (3) shows \(V\mid\Phi(y)\) as algebraic integers.
Their nonzero integral norms therefore give
\[
       0\le\mathbb E\log|V|\le\mathbb E\log|\Phi(y)|.
\]

The conjugates of \(y\) tend weakly to Haar measure on the unit circle by [Bilu's theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001).
Indeed \(y\) is nontorsion and \(h(y)\to0\), so it eventually leaves each finite subgroup.
For fixed \(L>0\), the average of \(\max(-L,\log|\Phi(y)|)\) tends to its circle integral.
To see this, the function is continuous after filling its values at zeros; outside a fixed annulus about the circle its positive part is at most \(\log 3+2\log^+|y|\), the exceptional probability tends to zero and \(h(y)\to0\).
As \(L\to\infty\) these integrals decrease to zero by Jensen's formula for \(\Phi\).
The nonnegative norm average implies both
\[
 \mathbb E\log|\Phi(y)|\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup \mathbb E(-L-\log|\Phi(y)|)_+=0.           \tag{5}
\]
In particular the finite norm contribution in (4) tends to zero.

Put \(f=\log\max(|U|,|V|)\).
Its positive part is at most \(\log 3+|\log|y||+|\log|z||\).
By (3) one has \(f\ge\log|\Phi(y)|-\log(1+2|y|)\), where \(\log(1+2|y|)\le\log3+\log^+|y|\).
Consequently (5) and \(h(y)\to0\) give \(\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup\mathbb E(-L-f)_+=0\).
This estimate holds for all relative sizes of \(a,t\) and all conjugates, including roots off the unit circle.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Limiting torus and the moving trinomial

It suffices to work along subsequences with fixed signs.
Put \(g=\gcd(a,t),\ p=a/g,\ q=t/g\).
Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \((p,q)\) is fixed or \(q\to\infty\).
In the fixed case \(w=x^g\) is nontorsion and \(h(w)\to0\), so the joint limiting measure of \((y,z)\) is Haar measure on the image of \(w\mapsto(w^p,w^q)\) on the circle.
In the second case \((y,z)\) is a strict sequence in the two-dimensional torus.
Indeed for each fixed \((i,j)\ne(0,0)\), \(ip+jq\ne0\) eventually by coprimality, and \(y^iz^j\) is then nontorsion.
Every proper algebraic subgroup is contained in the kernel of some nontrivial character, and the tuple height is bounded by \(h(y)+h(z)\).
Bilu's theorem gives joint Haar measure on \(\mathbb T^2\).
Averaging over \(\mathbb Q(x)\) agrees with averaging over the orbit of the tuple because each embedding of its field has the same number of extensions.

For either limiting measure, \(\max(-L,f)\) is continuous on the complex torus with its natural values at simultaneous zeros.
Weak convergence gives convergence of these truncated averages by cutting off outside a fixed compact annulus in each coordinate, using the positive bound on \(f\) and the two heights tending to zero.
The \(y\)-marginal of either limit is uniform on the circle, and (3) bounds the negative logarithm on that torus by an integrable function of \(y\).
Hence (5) and its consequence for \(f\) allow removal of the truncation in both the orbit averages and the limit integral.
On the unit torus \(V=z\overline U\), so this integral is the average of \(\log|c_0+c_1y+c_2z|\).
In the fixed case it is exactly \(\log M(A)\), by invariance of circle measure under \(w\mapsto w^g\).
In the second case integration first in \(z\) gives \(\mu\); the signs are absorbed by rotations.
The [Lawton specialization theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(\log M(A)\to\mu\) on the same subsequence since the least relation of \((p,q)\) has maximum norm \(q\).
Finally \(t h(x)\to0\), so (4) proves (2) along every subsequence.

For the [original trinomial sections](Yin_C_G0345_v548.md#statement), if \(s\to\infty,\ r/s\to\infty\), then the lower block has \(t=as,\ q=s\), and \(N=as+b\sim b\).
Thus \(b\,m/d\to\mu\) for every nontorsion factor as soon as \(r/s\to\infty\).
In the opposite regime \(r\to\infty,\ s/r\to\infty\), the lower block has \(t=b,\ q=b\) by coprimality and \(N\sim as\), giving \(as\,m/d\to\mu\).
At comparable \(r,s\to\infty\) with six distinct terms, (1) already forces \(d\gg N\).
