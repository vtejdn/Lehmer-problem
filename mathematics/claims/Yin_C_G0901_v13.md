# Circle measures with nonnegative integrals of all cyclotomic logarithms

<a id="statement"></a>
## Characterization by conjugation symmetry

Let \(\nu\) be a Borel probability measure on the unit circle \(\mathbb T\), let \(\lambda\) denote normalized arc length on \(\mathbb T\), and put \(c(z)=\bar z\).
For each positive integer \(n\), let \(\Phi_n\) denote the monic cyclotomic polynomial of order \(n\).
The following two conditions are equivalent.

1. For every \(n\ge1\), \(\int_{\mathbb T}\log|\Phi_n(z)|\,d\nu(z)\ge0\), with \(\log0=-\infty\).
2. The symmetrized measure \((\nu+c_*\nu)/2\) equals \(\lambda\).

Consequently all the integrals in the first condition are zero.
For every nonzero real polynomial \(P\), the same measure integrates \(\log|P|\) to the full logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)\).
Among conjugation-invariant probabilities the conditions characterize normalized arc length itself.
For example, \((1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta/(2\pi)\) also satisfies both conditions.

<a id="power"></a>
## All power logarithms have mean zero

Assume the first condition.
Each logarithm is bounded above, so its negative part is integrable.
Write \(f_k(z)=\log|1-z^k|\) and \(A(k)=\int f_k\,d\nu\).
The factorization of \(X^k-1\) into distinct cyclotomic polynomials gives \(A(k)\ge0\).
The factorization of \((X^{kj}-1)/(X^k-1)\) gives \(A(kj)\ge A(k)\) for every \(j\ge1\).

For \(N\ge2\) put \(w_j=1-j/(N+1)\) for \(1\le j\le N\), \(W=\sum_{j=1}^N w_j=N/2\), and set
\[
 F_N(z)=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{j=1}^N w_j\log|1-z^j|.
\]
There is an absolute \(C\) such that
\[
                    F_N(z)\le C\frac{\log(2N)}{N}
                    \qquad (z\in\mathbb T).                         \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(1+2\sum_{j=1}^N w_j\cos(jt)=|\sum_{j=0}^Ne^{ijt}|^2/(N+1)\ge0\).
For \(0<r<1\) and \(|u|=1\), the maximum of \(|1-u|/|1-ru|\) is \(2/(1+r)\), as follows by squaring and writing the quotient in terms of \(\operatorname{Re}u\).
Use this inequality for \(u=z^j\) and the absolutely convergent series for \(\log|1-rz^j|\).
For \(z=e^{it}\) one obtains
\[
 F_N(z)\le\log\frac{2}{1+r}
 -\frac1W\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{r^\ell}{\ell}
                       \sum_{j=1}^N w_j\cos(j\ell t)
 \le\log\frac{2}{1+r}+\frac{\log(1/(1-r))}{2W}.
\]
Taking \(r=1-1/N\) proves (1).
Integrating (1) at \(z^k\) gives \(0\le A(k)\le W^{-1}\sum_jw_j A(kj)\le C\log(2N)/N\), hence \(A(k)=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).

<a id="arcs"></a>
## Uniform mass estimates for powers of the measure

For \(0<\delta<1/8\), take \(N=\lfloor1/(2\delta)\rfloor\).
If \(|1-z^k|<\delta\), then \(|1-z^{kj}|\le j|1-z^k|\le1/2\) for \(1\le j\le N\), so \(F_N(z^k)\le-\log2\) there.
Since \(\int F_N(z^k)\,d\nu=0\), bounding the complementary integral by (1) proves
\[
     \nu\{z:|1-z^k|<\delta\}\ll\delta\log(2/\delta)
                     \qquad(k\ge1).                                  \tag{2}
\]
All implied constants below are absolute.
Let \(\nu_j\) be the image of \(\nu\) under \(z\mapsto z^j\).
In the coordinate \(z=e^{2\pi i t}\), measure distances in \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) by \(\|t\|=\min_{n\in\mathbb Z}|t-n|\) and denote by \(B(t,x)\) the arc of radius \(x\) about \(t\).
For any center \(t\) and sufficiently small \(x>0\), take \(Q=\lceil x^{-1/2}\rceil\).
Placing the \(Q+1\) points \(0,t,\ldots,Qt\) in \(Q\) equal intervals gives \(1\le k\le Q\) with \(\|kt\|\le1/Q\).
If \(u\in B(t,x)\), then \(\|ku\|\le1/Q+Qx\ll\sqrt x\).
The preimage under \(z\mapsto z^j\) of the whole arc therefore satisfies \(|1-z^{jk}|\ll\sqrt x\).
Applying (2) with this exponent, and increasing the constant for larger \(x\), yields
\[
          \nu_j(B(t,x))\ll \sqrt{x}\log(2/x)\ll x^{1/3}
          \qquad (0<x\le1/2,\ j\ge1).                                \tag{3}
\]

<a id="fourier"></a>
## Absolute convergence for arithmetic inversion

Define \(\widehat\nu(k)=\int z^k\,d\nu(z)\), so \(\widehat{\nu_j}(n)=\widehat\nu(jn)\).
For \(M\ge1\) the Fejér kernel
\[
 K_M(t)=\frac1{M+1}\left|\sum_{n=0}^M e^{2\pi i n t}\right|^2
      =\sum_{|n|\le M}\left(1-\frac{|n|}{M+1}\right)e^{2\pi i n t}
\]
is bounded by \(C\min(M+1,1/((M+1)\|t\|^2))\), by the geometric sum and \(|\sin(\pi t)|\ge2\|t\|\).
Taking \(M=2N\), (3) bounds \(\int K_{2N}(u-t)\,d\nu_j(u)\) uniformly in \(j,t\) by \(C N^{2/3}\).
Indeed the arc \(\|u-t\|\le1/N\) contributes \(O(N^{2/3})\), and the annulus \(2^l/N<\|u-t\|\le2^{l+1}/N\) contributes \(O(N^{2/3}2^{-5l/3})\).
The bound (3) extends with a constant to radii up to one by the total mass bound.
Integrating a second time in \(t\) against \(\nu_j\), the coefficient of \(|\widehat\nu(jn)|^2\) in the kernel expansion is at least \(1/2\) for \(|n|\le N\).
It follows that
\[
                         \sum_{n\le N,\ n\ge1}|\widehat\nu(jn)|^2
                              \ll N^{2/3}.                            \tag{4}
\]

Write \(\tau(n)\) for the number of positive divisors and \(\tau_4(n)\) for the number of ordered factorizations into four positive integers.
Their prime-power formulas give \(\tau(n)^2\le\tau_4(n)\), since \((b+1)^2\le\binom{b+3}{3}\) for every integer \(b\ge0\).
Summing over the first three factors gives \(\sum_{n\le X}\tau_4(n)\le X(1+\log X)^3\).
Thus Cauchy–Schwarz and (4) give
\[
       \sum_{N\le n<2N}\frac{\tau(n)|\widehat\nu(jn)|}{n}
            \ll N^{-1/6}(1+\log(2N))^{3/2}.
\]
The sum over dyadic \(N\) converges uniformly in \(j\).

For \(r\uparrow1\) the identity \(|1-rz^j|^2=r|1-z^j|^2+(1-r)^2\) gives
\[
 \tfrac12\log r+f_j(z)\le\log|1-rz^j|\le\log2 .
\]
The two bounds give dominated convergence for \(r\ge1/2\) because \(f_j\) is integrable.
The Fourier series with coefficients \(r^n/n\) may be integrated termwise before taking the limit.
The preceding dyadic bound permits passage to \(r=1\) in the integrated series, giving
\[
                  0=A(j)=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(jn)}{n}.
\]
If \(\mu_{\rm ar}\) is the arithmetic Möbius function, the double sum obtained by weighting this identity at \(j\ell\) with \(\mu_{\rm ar}(\ell)/\ell\) has absolute sum bounded by \(\sum_{m\ge1}\tau(m)|\widehat\nu(jm)|/m\).
Grouping terms with \(m=n\ell\) therefore gives
\[
       0=-\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(jm)}{m}
                       \sum_{\ell\mid m}\mu_{\rm ar}(\ell)
        =-\operatorname{Re}\widehat\nu(j)
        \qquad (j\ge1).
\]
The Fourier coefficients of \((\nu+c_*\nu)/2\) thus agree with those of \(\lambda\) at every integer.
Density of trigonometric polynomials in continuous functions on the circle proves equality of the measures.

Conversely, the logarithm of the modulus of each real polynomial on the circle is invariant under conjugation.
Its absolute value is integrable against \(\lambda\) for every nonzero polynomial, including one with circle roots, where the singularities are logarithmic.
Equality of the symmetrization with \(\lambda\) therefore gives equality of the logarithmic integrals against \(\nu\) and \(\lambda\).
The arc length integral of \(\log|\Phi_n|\) is zero because all its roots have modulus one and it is monic, by Jensen's formula.
This proves the converse and the asserted identity for \(P\).
