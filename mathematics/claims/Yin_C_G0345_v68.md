# Permutation blocks in a possible small-measure sequence with two nonreal exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## The action on inverse pairs

Suppose \(P\) is monic, irreducible and reciprocal, of degree \(d=2m\) over \(\mathbb Z\), with exactly two nonreal conjugate exterior roots.
Assume distinct roots never have root-of-unity quotient, as holds after [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) if two exterior roots remain.
Take the orbit under the Galois group of the edge joining the two exterior inverse-pair labels, and let \(t\) be the number of connected components of the resulting graph.
The components have the same size \(b=m/t\ge2\), each is a complete graph, and the induced Galois group on the \(m\) labels contains the direct product of the \(t\) full symmetric groups on these blocks.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
                 \log M(P)\ \ge\
                    c\,\frac{\sqrt t}{(\log(3d))^{106}} .
\]
Consequently on any sequence in this family with \(M(P)\to1\) one has \(d\to\infty\), \(t=o((\log(3d))^{212})\) and \(b\to\infty\).
When \(t=1\) the group on all inverse-pair labels is the full symmetric group.

<a id="action"></a>
## One ordinary transposition and its conjugates

Ordinary complex conjugation exchanges the two labels of the exterior roots and fixes every other label, since each neutral root has its inverse for complex conjugate.
It therefore induces a single transposition on the labels.
Every edge in the specified orbit furnishes its own transposition, by conjugating this element.
The transpositions on the edges of a connected graph generate the entire symmetric group of the component, acting trivially on all other vertices.
Indeed composing successive transpositions along a path transports the first edge to a transposition joining its initial vertex to any other vertex of the path.
These symmetric groups act independently on disjoint components and transport each original edge to every edge of its component.
Transitivity on labels identifies the component sizes.
These are statements about the inverse-pair permutation action, which may have a nontrivial kernel on the actual roots.

<a id="blocks"></a>
## The two-height estimate for any invariant partition

More generally, consider any compressed monic irreducible reciprocal unit polynomial of degree \(d=2m\) and measure greater than one.
Every two roots \(x,y\) from distinct inverse labels are multiplicatively independent.
Indeed a dependence would give a nonzero power of one equal to a power of the other, allowing a torsion factor without changing heights.
The absolute heights of the roots are equal and positive, so the two powers have equal absolute exponents.
Choosing an inverse if necessary gives a torsion quotient between distinct roots, contrary to compression.
This extends the [off-pair height argument for Salem units](../audits/literature/AM16/Yin_U004.md#am16-u004) to these inputs.

Suppose the Galois group preserves any partition into \(t\) sets of \(b\ge2\) inverse labels, with \(bt=m\).
Choose \(x,y\) on two distinct labels in one block.
For each image of \(x\) there are at most \(2(b-1)\) possible images of \(y\), so the joint degree \(D=[\mathbb Q(x,y):\mathbb Q]\) is at most \(2d(b-1)\le d^2/t\).
The [rank-two height product](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) gives
\[
          (\log M(P)/d)^2
               \ \ge\ \frac{c_2}{D(\log(3D))^{212}} .
\]
Since \(\log(3D)\le2\log(3d)\), this proves the stated bound for every such invariant partition.
A sequence of positive measures tending to one has unbounded degree tending to infinity, by bounded-degree coefficient finiteness.
The displayed estimate gives the little-oh bound and hence \(b\to\infty\) for the two-exterior graph.
It still permits a single component of unbounded size.
