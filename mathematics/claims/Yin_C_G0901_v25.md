# A conductor-divisibility exclusion survives dilation and can charge the entire circle maximum

<a id="statement"></a>
## A positive cost that can persist along a putative shrinking sequence

Let \(M\ge2\), and allow only cyclotomic cofactors having no order divisible by \(M\).
For a nonzero real polynomial \(P\) define
\[
       \gamma_M^\times(P)=\inf_{a\ge1,C}
                           a^{-1}\log\|P^a C\|_1
\]
under this restriction on monic cyclotomic products \(C\), including \(1\).
The infimum is also the limit at increasing multiplicity by repeating any fixed witness.
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
             \gamma_M^\times(P(X^n))\ \ge\ \gamma_M^\times(P),          \tag{1}
\]
with equality if \(\gcd(n,M)=1\).
For arbitrary dilations with \(v_p(n)\to\infty\) at every \(p\mid M\),
\[
          \gamma_M^\times(P(X^n))\ \longrightarrow\
                              \log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}.                     \tag{2}
\]
The polynomial \(P\) is fixed in this limit.
A finite estimate below controls varying degrees and dilations as well.
The [individual loss for excluding all multiples](Yin_C_G0901_v24.md#selection) uses the nonzero reflected roots of \(P\); (2) identifies the full limiting loss.

For Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial \(L\), one has \(\gamma_4^\times(L)=\log3\) and \(\gamma_4^\times(L(X^n))\ge\log3\) for every \(n\).
All these polynomials are irreducible and have the same Mahler measure.
More generally, if the unrestricted Lehmer infimum is zero, then for every fixed \(M\) there are irreducible nontorsion unit minima of measures tending to zero on which this restricted coefficient rate is at least \(\tfrac12\log3-o(1)\).
Thus a positive estimate on these representatives under this infinite prohibition would be compatible with a vanishing unrestricted gap.

<a id="rotation"></a>
## Taking powers of all cofactor roots

For a set \(B\) of orders, even an infinite one, taking the product of all rotated witnesses proves the [pullback identity](Yin_C_G0901_v18.md#pullback) with forbidden set \(B^{[n]}=\{c:c/\gcd(c,n)\in B\}\).
The proof is unchanged because each individual cofactor is a finite polynomial.
In particular, with \(B\) the multiples of \(M\), the forbidden preimage consists of multiples of \(M\prod_{p\mid M}p^{v_p(n)}\).
It is contained in \(B\), so imposing \(B\) on \(P(X^n)\) costs at least as much as imposing the preimage.
This gives (1), and the two sets coincide in the coprime case.

<a id="averaging"></a>
## Averaging over all lifts of an angle

For integrable functions on the circle define \(A_k f(z)=k^{-1}\sum_{w^k=z}f(w)\), and let \(V_k=k A_k\).
The averages commute, compose according to multiplication of indices and contract \(L^1\) norms.
For \(f_c=\log|\Phi_c|\), \(V_k f_c\) is the boundary log of the monic polynomial whose root list is the \(k\)-th powers of all the primitive \(c\)-th roots, by factorization, with irrelevant constant phase removed.
Write \(S\) for the primes dividing \(M\), \(e_p=v_p(M)\) and \(r_p=v_p(n)\), supposing first \(r_p\ge e_p-1\) for all \(p\in S\).
For every allowed conductor \(c\) at least one \(p\in S\) has \(v_p(c)\le e_p-1\).
For this prime the multiset of powered roots is identical at exponents \(p^{r_p}\) and \(p^{e_p-1}\), since after \(p^{e_p-1}\) the resulting orbit has order coprime to \(p\).
Consequently
\[
               \prod_{p\in S}(V_{p^{r_p}}-V_{p^{e_p-1}})\log|C|=0 .
\]
This includes the case of coinciding exponents.
Expand the product, compose with the residual \(V_{n/\prod_{p\in S}p^{r_p}}\), and divide by \(n\).
It expresses \(A_n\log|C|\) as a sum of normalized averages with signs and positive weights whose total is
\[
                         \delta(n,M)=
                \prod_{p\mid M}(1+p^{e_p-1-r_p})-1.                    \tag{3}
\]
For each nonempty subset of \(S\) the weight is the product of \(p^{e_p-1-r_p}\) on that subset.
Since \(\log|C|\) has mean zero, its positive and negative parts have the same integral.
Thus the integral of the negative part of \(A_n\log|C|\) is at most \(\delta(n,M)\int(\log|C|)_-\).

Put \(g(z)=\log|P(z)|,\ h=m(P),\ H=\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\).
For an individual \(F=P(X^n)^a C(X)\) write \(u=a^{-1}\log\|F\|_{\mathbb T}\).
If \(u\le H\), the triangle inequality on the positive part of \(g(z^n)-a^{-1}\log|F(z)|\) gives
\[
     a^{-1}\int(\log|C|)_-\le (H-h)+(u-h)\le2(H-h).
\]
Indeed \(a^{-1}\log|F|\) has mean \(h\), so the integral of its negative deviation from \(h\) equals that of the positive deviation.
Applying \(A_n\) at the individual angles gives \(u\ge g+a^{-1} A_n\log|C|\) almost everywhere.
We have therefore proved
\[
                  \int (g-u)_+\le 2\delta(n,M)(H-h).                   \tag{4}
\]
All integrals here use normalized arc length.

<a id="quantitative"></a>
## Recovering the circle supremum

Suppose \(d=\deg P\ge1\) and \(x=H-u\ge0\).
At a maximizing angle the modulus of \(P\) is \(e^H\).
On an arc of angular radius \(\min(1,x)/(4d)\) around it, the [circle derivative inequality](Yin_C_G0901_v12.md#disk) gives \(|P|\ge e^H(1-\min(1,x)/4)\).
Thus \(g-u\ge x/2\) on the arc.
Its normalized length and (4) yield
\[
                      x\min(1,x)\le
                         16\pi d(H-h)\delta(n,M).                       \tag{5}
\]
Every individual normalized coefficient log norm is at least its circle counterpart.
The empty cofactor gives the limiting upper rate \(H\): \(\|P(X^n)^a\|_1=\|P^a\|_1\le\sqrt{ad+1}\exp(aH)\).
Using (5) whenever \(u\le H\) gives (2), also trivially for a constant input.
No estimate of the total cofactor degree was needed.

For the example, forbidding all multiples of four includes the [order-four lower bound](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#statement), and every additional cofactor order in the [matching witness](Yin_C_G0901_v16.md#lehmer) is permitted.
Then (1) and [irreducibility of every substitution](Yin_C_G0901_v22.md#representatives) give the stated conclusions for \(L\).

Finally choose any sequence of nontorsion reciprocal unit minima of measures tending to zero under the zero-gap hypothesis.
For each input separately take a coherent tower of roots of order \(M\).
The [fixed-tower stabilization theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v17.md#tower) supplies a nontorsion unit minimum \(Q\) of no larger positive measure such that \(Q(X^{M^j})\) is irreducible and has that same measure for every \(j\ge1\).
This uses a full extension of degree \(M\) at every later step and equality of degrees in the iterated composition.
Every nontorsion unit minimum has at least three nonzero integer coefficients, since a monic unit binomial has only torsion roots.
Thus \(\|Q\|_{\mathbb T}\ge\|Q\|_2\ge\sqrt3\) by Parseval.
For each fixed \(Q\) choose \(j\) large enough in (2) that the error tends to zero along the original sequence.
The resulting irreducible minima establish the last statement without any uniform claim on the onset of tower stabilization.
