# Exceptional units and differences from finitely many roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Comparison of the infima of total measures

For a nonempty finite set \(\Sigma\) of complex roots of unity, let \(\lambda_\Sigma\) be the infimum of the positive total logarithmic Mahler measures \(m(\beta)\) of algebraic units \(\beta\) such that \(\beta-\zeta\) is an algebraic unit for every \(\zeta\in\Sigma\).
Both the number and its inverse must be algebraic integers, so the unit condition on a difference may be tested in any number field containing it.
Put \(\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}=\lambda_{\{1\}}\), the infimum for nontorsion exceptional units.
If \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function and \(c_\Sigma=\min_{\zeta\in\Sigma}\varphi(\operatorname{ord}\zeta)\), then
\[
                 \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}}{c_\Sigma}
                   \le\lambda_\Sigma\le\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}.              \tag{1}
\]
In particular the two infima are equal if \(1\in\Sigma\) or \(-1\in\Sigma\), and vanishing of either infimum is equivalent to vanishing of the other for every fixed \(\Sigma\).
The sets defining the infima are nonempty, since the golden ratio is exceptional and the construction below applies to it.

If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}=0\), for every prescribed sequence of finite sets \(\Sigma_j\) there are nontorsion units \(\beta_j\) satisfying all tests in \(\Sigma_j\) and \(m(\beta_j)\to0\).
Conversely such sequences for every choice of tests imply \(\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}=0\), by taking the single point \(1\).
For a fixed root-extraction exponent, a coherent tower over one exceptional unit eventually preserves its total measure at every remaining step, while satisfying increasing sets of torsion tests.

Consider the group of algebraic numbers \(\beta\) satisfying \(\beta^t=\omega(4+\sqrt{15})^u\) for integers \(t\ge1,u\in\mathbb Z\) and a root of unity \(\omega\).
For every nontorsion \(\beta\) in this group and every root of unity \(\zeta\), \(\beta-\zeta\) fails the unit test.
Thus a sequence of unrestricted units with measures tending to zero would still require an argument supplying exceptional units in order to use (1).

<a id="lifting"></a>
## Twisting a difference and taking roots of an exceptional unit

Write \(h\) for absolute logarithmic Weil height, with the [total measure normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) \(m(\alpha)=[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\,h(\alpha)\).
Multiplication by a root of unity preserves \(h\), and \(h(\alpha^N)=N h(\alpha)\) for \(N\ge1\).
If \(\beta\) satisfies the tests for \(\Sigma\), fix a point \(\zeta\) of order \(b\) in \(\Sigma\).
Then \(\beta/\zeta\) is a nontorsion unit and \(\beta/\zeta-1=(\beta-\zeta)/\zeta\) is a unit.
The field of \(\beta/\zeta\) has degree at most \(\varphi(b)[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\) because it lies in \(\mathbb Q(\beta,\zeta)\).
Therefore \(\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}\le \varphi(b)m(\beta)\), and minimizing first over the points of \(\Sigma\) and then over \(\beta\) gives the left inequality in (1).

Conversely let \(\alpha\) be any nontorsion exceptional unit, let \(N\) be a positive multiple of all the orders in \(\Sigma\), and choose any complex root \(\beta\) of \(\beta^N=\alpha\).
Both \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{-1}\) are integral by their monic equations, and \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
In a number field containing \(\beta\) and the \(N\)-th roots of unity,
\[
                        \prod_{\xi^N=1}(\beta-\xi)=\alpha-1 .
\]
Since the product is a unit and each factor is integral, every factor is a unit.
Moreover \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\beta)\) and
\[
            m(\beta)=\frac{[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]}{N}\,
                            m(\alpha)\le m(\alpha).                       \tag{2}
\]
This proves the right inequality in (1), whether or not the binomial is irreducible.
For growing test sets one may choose a separate common multiple for each \(j\) and apply (2) to any exceptional units with positive measures tending to zero.
There is no uniform bound on these common multiples in this assertion.

If \(\Sigma\) contains every primitive root of each order in a finite set \(S\), the tests at one such order \(s\) are equivalent to \(\Phi_s(\beta)\) being a unit, by the same product argument.
For the monic minimum \(P\) of \(\beta\) this last condition is equivalent to \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_s)|=1\), because the resultant is the norm of \(\Phi_s(\beta)\).
In particular the unit condition at \(1\) is \(|P(1)|=1\).

<a id="tower"></a>
## Stabilization along a coherent tower

Fix \(N\ge2\), set \(\beta_0=\alpha\), and at every step choose any \(\beta_{j+1}\) with \(\beta_{j+1}^N=\beta_j\).
If \(\alpha\) is exceptional, \(\beta_j-\zeta\) is a unit for all \(\zeta^{N^j}=1\).
For any nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) the total measures of the tower stabilize at a positive value after finitely many steps.
Indeed put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(K_j=\mathbb Q(\beta_j)\), \(d_j=[K_j:K]\), and \(v_j=N_{K_j/K}(\beta_j)\).
These are nested fields and \(v_j\) is a unit in \(K\) satisfying \(v_j^{N^j}=\alpha^{d_j}\).
Consequently \(h(v_j)=d_j h(\alpha)/N^j>0\).
The positive heights of units of \(K\) are bounded away from zero.
For example, a bound on the height bounds all conjugate moduli at the fixed degree and hence the integral coefficients of each monic minimum, leaving only finitely many candidates.
It follows that \(d_j/N^j\) is bounded below by a positive constant depending on \(\alpha\).
At a step with \([K_{j+1}:K_j]<N\), this ratio decreases by a factor at most \((N-1)/N\), and it never increases.
There can therefore be only finitely many such steps along the tower.
For every later step \([K_{j+1}:K_j]=N\), and (2) applied to successive terms gives exact equality of their measures.
The stabilized value can be smaller than the initial measure when an earlier step has deficient degree.

<a id="obstruction"></a>
## A divisible group with no exceptional translate

Put \(\alpha=4+\sqrt{15}\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{15})\), and \(\delta=\sqrt{\alpha}=(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt6)/2\), with positive real square roots.
The unit \(\alpha\) has conjugate \(\alpha^{-1}\) and is the least positive real unit of \(K\) greater than one.
Indeed the ring of integers is \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{15}]\), and \(x^2-15y^2=-1\) is impossible modulo three.
A unit \(v=x+y\sqrt{15}>1\) therefore has conjugate \(1/v\), so \(y\ge1\) and \(x\ge4\), with equality attained at \(\alpha\).

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the compositum of the finite abelian extensions of \(\mathbb Q\).
It contains \(\delta\), all roots of unity, and no positive real \(p\)-th root of \(\delta\) for any prime \(p\).
To see the last assertion, a real \(u=\delta^{1/p}\) in \(\mathcal A\) would have only real conjugates, since complex conjugation commutes with all automorphisms of a finite abelian extension containing \(u\).
The conjugates over \(K\) would then be among the two real roots of \(X^{2p}-\alpha\).
Hence \(u^2=\alpha^{1/p}\) would be fixed under all embeddings over \(K\) and belong to \(K\).
It would be a unit strictly between \(1\) and \(\alpha\), a contradiction.
Adjoining an arbitrary root of unity to a choice of \(p\)-th root does not change this conclusion because all such roots of unity already belong to \(\mathcal A\).

Every nontorsion \(\beta\) in the stated group can be written \(\beta=\xi\delta^{r/s}\), with a root of unity \(\xi\), relatively prime integers \(r,s\), \(s>0\), \(r\ne0\), and the positive real value of the rational power.
The field \(\mathcal A(\delta^{r/s})\) is the splitting field over \(\mathcal A\) of \(X^s-\delta^r\).
Its Galois group embeds in the \(s\)-th roots of unity by the quotient of the image of \(\delta^{r/s}\) by its original value.
If the image has order \(d\mid s\), then \(\delta^{rd/s}\in\mathcal A\).
Writing \(b=s/d\), Bézout's identity for \(r,b\) implies \(\delta^{1/b}\in\mathcal A\).
If \(b>1\) this contradicts the obstruction for any prime divisor of \(b\).
Thus the image has full order \(s\).

If \(1-\beta\) were a unit, the product of the images of \(1-\beta\) over this Galois group would also be a unit.
This product is \(1-\xi^s\delta^r\), since the images of \(\beta\) consist of all \(s\) multiples by \(s\)-th roots of unity.
Choose a finite abelian field \(F\) containing \(\delta\) and \(\xi\).
Exactly half its embeddings have \(|\sigma(\delta^r)|=R=\alpha^{|r|/2}\), and the other half have modulus \(1/R\), by restriction to \(K\).
The triangle inequality implies
\[
    \left|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(1-\xi^s\delta^r)\right|^{1/[F:\mathbb Q]}
         \ge \sqrt{(R-1)(1-1/R)}
         =\sqrt{R+R^{-1}-2}>1 .
\]
In fact \(R+R^{-1}\ge\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\alpha^{-1}}=\sqrt{10}>3\).
This contradicts the absolute norm one of a unit.
For any root of unity \(\zeta\), \(\beta/\zeta\) belongs to the same group, and \(1-\beta/\zeta\) differs from \(\beta-\zeta\) by a unit factor.
This proves the obstruction at every torsion point.
Products, integer powers, extraction of arbitrary positive integer roots, and twists by roots of unity starting from this one input all stay in the same group.
The example addresses an individual starting unit of fixed positive measure, leaving the relation between \(\lambda_{\mathrm{exc}}\) and the unrestricted Lehmer infimum open.
