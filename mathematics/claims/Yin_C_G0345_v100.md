# Truncated inverse cyclotomic series in reciprocal interpolation

<a id="statement"></a>
## A Mahler gap when at most one denominator step is short

Let \(n\ge3\) have at most \(r_0\) distinct prime factors, and let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be nonzero and reciprocal of even degree \(d=2m\le\varphi(n)+H\) with \(H\ge0\).
Suppose that for a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta_n\) the centered value is \(\zeta_n^{-m}P(\zeta_n)=\epsilon(\zeta_n^a+\zeta_n^{-a})\), where \(\epsilon=\pm1\) and \(0\le a\le n/2\).
For an exterior exponent \(a>m\), use the [reciprocal interpolation formula](Yin_C_G0345_v96.md#wrapping) with
\[
 k=a-m,\qquad N=2a-\varphi(n)\ge0,\qquad
 \ell=\min\{k,\lfloor(N+1)/2\rfloor\}.
\]
Fix \(C\ge0\).
If all but at most one of the denominator exponents \(e\) in the Möbius product for \(1/\Phi_n\) satisfy \(\ell\le Ce\), a product of boundedly many binomials \(1-X^h\) makes \(P\) have bounded term count.
The same holds for \(a\le m\).
Both bounds depend only on \(r_0,C,H\), so \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\ge\eta(r_0,C,H)>0\).
Two exceptional denominator steps are also allowed if their Euclidean algorithm takes at most \(E_0\) divisions, with the bounds then depending on \(E_0\) as well.

For \(n\) with \(\operatorname{rad}(n)=pqr\), \(p<q<r\) odd primes, put \(s=n/(pqr)\) and \(L=\lceil\ell/s\rceil\).
The gap applies whenever \(L\le Cq\), in particular when \(k\le Csq\) after increasing \(C\) by one.
Put \(D=(p-1)(q-1)\).
If \(r>D\), write \(L=Jr+u\) with \(0\le u<r\).
The same gap holds in each of the following ranges.

* \(J\ge p\).
* \(u>D\).
* \(0\le u\le D\) and \(\min(u,D+1-u)\le Cq\).

The original interpolation imposes \(J<q\) in this range of \(r\).
Thus on any sequence with \(r>D\) and \(1<M(P)\to1\), one must eventually have \(J<p\), \(0\le u\le D\), and \(\min(u,D+1-u)/q\to\infty\).
There is also a gap whenever \(\min(L,D)\le Cr\) and the ordinary Euclidean algorithm on \((q,p)\) takes at most \(E_0\) divisions, with bounds depending only on \(C,E_0,H\).
In particular this covers every cutoff when \(r>D\) and the Euclidean lengths are bounded, irrespective of the sizes of the partial quotients.

<a id="one-step"></a>
## Truncating one geometric progression at a time

Write \([F]_{<h}\) for the terms of degrees \(0,\ldots,h-1\) of a power series \(F\), with zero prefix at \(h=0\).
For \(n>1\) its inverse cyclotomic series is
\[
 \frac1{\Phi_n(X)}
   =\frac{\prod_{e\in A}(1-X^e)}{\prod_{e\in B}(1-X^e)},\qquad
 A=\{e\mid n:\mu(n/e)=-1\},\quad B=\{e\mid n:\mu(n/e)=1\}.
\]
Both sets have \(2^{\omega(n)-1}\) entries.
Choose \(b\in B\) so that \(\ell\le Ce\) for all other \(e\in B\).
Expand the numerator and the geometric series for these other exponents only up to degree \(\ell-1\).
There are at most \(2^{|A|}(1+\lfloor C\rfloor)^{|B|-1}\) choices of a numerator term and a tuple of geometric terms that can contribute, counted before collection.
For each fixed choice of total degree \(j<\ell\), the remaining degrees are \(j,j+b,\ldots,j+vb<\ell\), for the largest such \(v\).
Multiplying this truncated progression by \(1-X^b\) leaves its two endpoint terms.
This bounds the term count of \((1-X^b)T\) for \(T=[1/\Phi_n]_{<\ell}\).
The identical argument applies to any ratio of finitely many such binomials when the number of factors and the stated ratios of cutoff to exponent are bounded.

For the transfer to \(P\), one may more generally take any product \(E\) of boundedly many binomials \(1-X^h\) for which \(ET\) has bounded term count.
Write \(Q=T(X)+X^N T(1/X)+X^\ell W(X)\) as in the [exterior interpolation](Yin_C_G0345_v96.md#wrapping), where \(W=0\) or its degree is at most \(H\).
Since \(E(X)=\pm X^{\deg E}E(1/X)\), the reflected term also has bounded term count after multiplication by \(E\).
Consequently \(EQ\) has bounded term count.
Clear the denominator of \(\Phi_n\) with \(\prod_{e\in A}(1-X^e)\) in the identity
\[
                 \epsilon X^kP=1+X^{2a}-\Phi_n Q.
\]
This gives a bounded-term integer polynomial after multiplying \(P\) by the indicated binomials and removing the monomial shift.
For \(a\le m\) the same clearance works directly on \(P=\epsilon(X^{m-a}+X^{m+a})+\Phi_n Q\) with \(Q=0\) or \(\deg Q\le H\).
Each multiplier has Mahler measure one, so the [bounded-term gap theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) transfers the gap to \(P\).

<a id="three-primes"></a>
## The binary block left by three prime divisors

For \(v=\operatorname{rad}(n)\), \(s=n/v\), one has \(\Phi_n(X)=\Phi_v(X^s)\) by the [radical substitution](Yin_C_G0345_v89.md#conductors).
Hence \(T=([1/\Phi_v]_{<L})(X^s)\) with \(L=\lceil\ell/s\rceil\), and binomial clearing for this prefix transfers by substitution.
When \(v=pqr\), the denominator steps for \(1/\Phi_v\) are \(p,q,r,pqr\).
Only \(p\) can violate the bound \(L\le Ce\) if \(L\le Cq\).

Now assume \(r>D=\varphi(pq)\).
The identity \(\Phi_{pqr}(X)=\Phi_{pq}(X^r)/\Phi_{pq}(X)\) gives
\[
 \frac1{\Phi_{pqr}(X)}=\Phi_{pq}(X)\sum_{j\ge0}b_jX^{jr},\qquad
 \sum_{j\ge0}b_jY^j
     =\frac{(1+Y+\cdots+Y^{p-1})(1-Y^q)}{1-Y^{pq}}.
\]
The blocks of degree \(D\) with starting points \(jr\) have disjoint supports.
The coefficient \(b_j\) is one on the residues \(0,\ldots,p-1\bmod pq\), minus one on \(q,\ldots,q+p-1\bmod pq\), and zero elsewhere.
For \(L=Jr+u\), the full blocks and the one cut by the truncation give
\[
       [1/\Phi_{pqr}]_{<L}
          =\Phi_{pq}(X)\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}b_jX^{jr}
                       + b_J X^{Jr}[\Phi_{pq}]_{<u}.
\]
To see the restriction on \(J\), let \(v-\varphi(v)=2h+1\), an odd integer.
Then \(2\ell\le N+1\le n-\varphi(n)+1=s(2h+1)+1\), and so \(L\le h+1\).
Therefore
\[
       2L\le v-\varphi(v)+1
           =r(p+q-1)+D+1\le r(p+q).
\]
This gives \(J\le(p+q)/2<q\), whence \(\sum_{j<J}b_j X^{jr}\) is just the progression with \(0\le j<\min(J,p)\).
Multiplication by \(1-X^r\) clears it, and multiplication by \((1-X^p)(1-X^q)\) makes \(\Phi_{pq}(X)\) a product of two binomials.

The cut block disappears when \(J\ge p\) and is a full block when \(u>D\).
For \(u\le Cq\), apply the progression argument to the binary cyclotomic polynomial
\[
         \Phi_{pq}(X)=\frac{(1-X)(1-X^{pq})}{(1-X^p)(1-X^q)} .
\]
Only the denominator step \(p\) can be short relative to \(u\).
For \(0\le u\le D\), reciprocity gives
\[
       [\Phi_{pq}]_{<u}
            =\Phi_{pq}(X)-X^D([\Phi_{pq}]_{<D+1-u})(1/X).
\]
Thus a cutoff within \(Cq\) of the other end also clears with boundedly many binomials.
Taking the product of the clearing factors for the full blocks and cut block preserves bounded term count, including after the substitution \(X\mapsto X^s\).
The general transfer to \(P\) proves the asserted gap.

<a id="euclidean"></a>
## Truncating two geometric series by the Euclidean algorithm

For positive integer steps \(p\le q\) and an integer cutoff \(t\), define
\[
      V_t(X)=\sum_{\substack{i,j\ge0\\pi+qj<t}}X^{pi+qj}.
\]
Take \(V_t=0\) for \(t\le0\).
For \(t>0\), set \(h=1+\lfloor(t-1)/q\rfloor\) and write \([x]_P\) for the least nonnegative residue modulo \(P\).
Telescoping each \(p\)-progression gives
\[
 (1-X^p)V_t=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}X^{qj}
                -X^t\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}X^{[qj-t]_p}.
\]
The step beyond the cutoff in row \(j\) is the least integer at least \(t\) congruent to \(qj\bmod p\), giving the second sum.
The first sum clears with \(1-X^q\).

For the second sum, more generally write
\[
     H(P,b,c,h)=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}X^{r_j},\qquad
     r_j=[c+jb]_P,\quad 0\le c,b<P .
\]
Here \(h\) is any nonnegative integer, so residues may recur.
The cases \(h=0\) and \(b=0\) already have at most one term.
Otherwise, let \(w=\lfloor(c+hb)/P\rfloor\).
Whenever the passage from \(r_j\) to \(r_{j+1}\) crosses a multiple of \(P\), \(r_j+b=P+r_{j+1}\).
The successive crossing levels are exactly \(vP,\ 1\le v\le w\), and at the \(v\)-th crossing the landing residue is \([c-vP]_b\), since it lies between zero and \(b-1\) and is congruent to \(c-vP\bmod b\).
Telescoping all steps therefore yields
\[
 (1-X^b) H(P,b,c,h)
   = X^{r_0}-X^{r_h}+
      (1-X^P)\sum_{v=1}^{w} X^{[c-vP]_b}.
\]
Reading the final sum in reverse order gives \(H(b,[P]_b,[c-wP]_b,w)\).
Thus the successive modulus and step pairs are precisely \((P,b),(b,[P]_b),\ldots\) in Euclidean division.
At a nonterminal step multiply by \(1-X^b\) and then by the clearing factors for the smaller pair.
The endpoint terms multiplied by these factors have bounded term count at bounded depth, and multiplication by \(1-X^P\) at most doubles the smaller cleared sum's term count.
Induction gives bounds for both the number of factors and the number of surviving terms that depend only on the number of divisions.
This works for all \(c,h\) using the same clearing factors for a fixed initial pair, allowing unused factors when \(h=0\).
Starting with \(P=p\) and \(b=[q]_p\) proves the uniform assertion for \(V_t\) at bounded Euclidean length of \((q,p)\).
For the general Möbius product with two exceptional steps \(p,q\), first expand the numerator and the boundedly many contributions of the other denominator steps, as in the one-step argument.
Each resulting shift \(j\) leaves precisely \(V_{\ell-j}\), and all these truncations use the same clearing factors.
This proves the two-exception extension at arbitrary bounded \(\omega(n)\).
Applying it to the four shifted truncations in \((1-X)(1-X^{pq})/((1-X^p)(1-X^q))\) clears \([\Phi_{pq}]_{<t}\) for every integer \(t\).

To allow overlapping binary blocks for \(1/\Phi_{pqr}\), return to \(\sum_j b_jX^{jr}\Phi_{pq}(X)\).
A prefix at \(L\) includes every block with \(jr+D<L\) in full and cuts only blocks with \(0<L-jr\le D\).
There are at most \(1+\min(L,D)/r\) cut blocks.
The full blocks contribute \(\Phi_{pq}(X)\sum_{j<J_0}b_j X^{jr}\), summing from \(j=0\) through the last full block.
For any \(J_0\ge0\), the prefix \(\sum_{j<J_0} b_jY^j\) is a whole number of periods followed by at most two truncated intervals from the last period.
Its product with \((1-Y)(1-Y^{pq})\) has bounded term count because the whole-period part is a finite geometric progression with step \(pq\) times \((1+\cdots+Y^{p-1})(1-Y^q)\).
Substitute \(Y=X^r\) and clear the remaining full factor \(\Phi_{pq}(X)\) as before.
If \(\min(L,D)\le Cr\), boundedly many shifted prefixes of \(\Phi_{pq}\) remain, all cleared by the Euclidean argument.
This proves the claimed additional range with unrestricted prime powers.

<a id="monicity"></a>
## At full degree monicity imposes no condition on the cutoff

Let \(n\ge3\), \(d=\varphi(n)=2m\).
For every integer \(k\) with \(1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor-m\) and each sign \(\epsilon\), a unique monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(d\) has centered value \(\epsilon(\zeta_n^{m+k}+\zeta_n^{-m-k})\).
Indeed \(a=m+k\), \(N=2k\), \(\ell=k\) in the interpolation formula.
Write \(c_k=[X^k]1/\Phi_n\) and \(T=[1/\Phi_n]_{<k}\).
The only possible quotient is
\[
             Q=T(X)+X^{2k}T(1/X)+(c_k-\epsilon)X^k.
\]
The last coefficient follows from the constant term \(P(0)=1\) because \(\Phi_n(0)=1\) and \(Q\) agrees with \(1/\Phi_n\) below \(k\).
Conversely this quotient is monic reciprocal of degree \(2k\), and the reconstruction \(P=\epsilon X^{-k}(1+X^{2a}-\Phi_n Q)\) is integral reciprocal with constant term one and exact degree \(d\).
Thus the first coefficient discrepancy is prescribed for every cutoff, whatever the value of \(c_k\).
The [order-parity condition](Yin_C_G0345_v96.md#parity) separately determines whether the specified primitive mean is below \(4/\pi\).
Monicity gives no additional exclusion even at cutoffs meeting that condition.
