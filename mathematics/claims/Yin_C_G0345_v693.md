# A discriminant bound for signed words in scalar quotients at a Salem number

<a id="statement"></a>
## Even alternating words

Let \(\alpha>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2D\ge4\), let \(n=D-1\), \(m=\log\alpha\), and \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
Write \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and let \(\Delta\) be the positive discriminant of the power basis of \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
Fix an integer \(q\ge2\) and an even number \(r\ge2\) of exponents \(0=e_1<\cdots<e_r=W\).
Set \(G(X)=\sum_{i=1}^r(-1)^{i-1}X^{e_i}\).
If the word vanishes in the full scalar quotient \(R/qR\), then
\[
 \frac{4\sinh^2(Wm/2)+r^2}{q^2}
 \ \ge
 \frac{\Delta^{1/(2n)}}{2^{1-1/n}\sqrt n}
                   \left(\frac{2q}{r}\right)^{n-1}.                    \tag{1}
\]
This necessity includes composite moduli and the case where the multiplicative order of \(\alpha\bmod qR\) exceeds the entire span \(W\).
For general integral words the [relative norm test](#general) gives an exact detection criterion under the stated circle and span bounds.
The two signs may be reversed, and a common integer translation of the exponents preserves modular and exact vanishing because \(\alpha\) is a unit.

Along any sequence of such modular vanishings with \(D\to\infty\) and \(q/r\ge c>1/2\), for all sufficiently large \(D\) one has
\[
 Wm\ \ge\ 2\log q+(D-2)\log(2q/r)-\tfrac12\log(D-1)-\log4.          \tag{2}
\]
For \(1/2<c<1\), the gain below the coefficient length already appears when \(q/r\to c\).
The ordinary modulus norm estimate with the same alternating real-place bounds gives only
\[
                       Wm\ge2\log q+(d-2)\log(q/r).                   \tag{3}
\]
Exact vanishing of an even alternating word is already excluded by its sign at the real root.
Thus (1) rules out modular candidates of this sign order regardless of any support minimality requirement.

<a id="general"></a>
## An exact alternative for other coefficient patterns

Let \(G\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be any nonzero polynomial with least exponent zero, degree \(W\), coefficient length \(S=\sum_i|[X^i]G|\) and circle maximum \(H=\max_{|z|=1}|G(z)|\).
Suppose \(G(\alpha)\in qR\), and write
\[
 y=\frac{G(\alpha)G(\alpha^{-1})}{q^2},\qquad
 A=\frac{H^2}{q^2},\qquad
 B=\frac{\Delta^{1/(2n)}}{2^{1-1/n}\sqrt n}
                       \left(\frac{2q}{H}\right)^{n-1}.
\]
If \(y\notin[0,A]\), then \(y\) generates \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and
\[
                       |y|+A\ \ge B.                                  \tag{4}
\]
If \(y\in[0,A]\) and \(H<2q\), then
\[
 y=2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1},\qquad
 2\le\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\le
                    \frac{\pi}{\arccos(H/(2q))}                       \tag{5}
\]
for a root of unity \(\zeta\).
In particular, when \(q>H\), (5) forces \(G(\alpha)=0\).
It follows that whenever
\[
       q>H,\qquad \frac{S^2e^{Wm}+H^2}{q^2}<B,                        \tag{6}
\]
the test \(G(\alpha)=0\bmod qR\) is equivalent to \(G(\alpha)=0\) in the number field.
Everywhere in (4)–(6) one may substitute a known upper bound for \(H\).
For \(H/2<q\le H\), (5) can include nonzero values, so the exact implication in (6) is asserted only under its stated hypotheses.

<a id="trace"></a>
## The relative norm and its real embeddings

The \(D\) distinct conjugates of \(t\) come from the inverse pairs of conjugates of \(\alpha\).
One lies above two and the other \(n\) lie in \((-2,2)\).
The map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\) is the nontrivial automorphism of the quadratic extension \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)/\mathbb Q(t)\).
Since \(R=\mathbb Z[t]+\alpha\mathbb Z[t]\), any \(\eta=G(\alpha)/q\in R\) has the form \(u+\alpha v\) with \(u,v\in\mathbb Z[t]\).
Indeed \(t\in R\) since \(\alpha\) is a unit with monic integral minimal polynomial, and the quadratic relation \(\alpha^2=t\alpha-1\) reduces all powers.
The relative norm \(y=u^2+tuv+v^2\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
At each of the \(n\) neutral inverse pairs its value is \(|G(z)/q|^2\in[0,A]\).
If its distinguished value is outside that interval, it occurs just once among the embeddings of \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Every embedding of \(\mathbb Q(y)\) has \([\mathbb Q(t):\mathbb Q(y)]\) extensions, so that degree is one.
Consequently the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of \(y\) is \([\mathbb Z[t]:\mathbb Z[y]]^2\Delta\), by the basis change in the trace form.

If the distinguished value belongs to \([0,A]\) with \(A<4\), all conjugates of \(y-2\) lie in \([-2,2]\).
The [quadratic lift and Kronecker theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-house) give \(y-2=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
Indeed both solutions of \(X^2-(y-2)X+1=0\) are algebraic integers and every conjugate of either solution has modulus one.
The root \(\zeta=1\) would give \(y=4>A\).
For its order \(k\ge2\), one conjugate of \(y\) equals \(2+2\cos(2\pi/k)=4\cos^2(\pi/k)\).
Bounding that conjugate by \(A\) gives (5).
If \(q>H\), then \(k<3\), giving \(k=2\) and \(y=0\).
The relative norm vanishes exactly when \(G(\alpha)=0\).

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Separating one conjugate from an interval

Denote the \(n\) neutral values of the primitive \(y\) by \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\).
Use the [monic Chebyshev basis on an interval](Yin_C_G0897_v1.md#high-tail) in their Vandermonde determinant.
Its constant polynomial is one and for \(j\ge1\) its degree-\(j\) polynomial is \(2(A/4)^j T_j(2x/A-1)\).
After the factors \(2(A/4)^j\) are extracted from columns \(1,\ldots,n-1\), each column has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt n\) because \(|T_j|\le1\) on \([-1,1]\).
Hadamard's inequality gives
\[
 \prod_{1\le i<j\le n}|y_i-y_j|
       \le n^{n/2}2^{n-1}(A/4)^{n(n-1)/2}.
\]
For \(n=1\) the empty product is one.
The remaining \(n\) differences in the full determinant have magnitude at most \(|y|+A\).
Its absolute value is at least \(\sqrt{\Delta}\) by the discriminant calculation.
Taking \(n\)-th roots gives (4).
The elementary bounds \(|G(\alpha)|\le S e^{Wm}\) and \(|G(\alpha^{-1})|\le S\) now give (6).
Increasing the supplied value of \(H\) enlarges the interval without increasing the right side of (4), and the same arguments give the stated upper-bound version.

<a id="gain"></a>
## What improves on the ordinary norm estimate

For an even alternating word \(G(\alpha)<0<G(\alpha^{-1})\).
Each difference in an ordered pair has constant sign, and the corresponding intervals between its consecutive endpoint powers are disjoint.
Their combined lengths therefore give \(|G(\alpha)|\le\alpha^W-1\) and \(|G(\alpha^{-1})|\le1-\alpha^{-W}\).
The product is at most \(4\sinh^2(Wm/2)\).
Using \(H\le r\) and \(y<0\) in (4) yields (1).
When \(q/r\ge c>1/2\), the right side of (1) is at least \((2q/r)^{n-1}/(2\sqrt n)\), which eventually exceeds \(2(r/q)^2\) uniformly at that \(c\).
After subtracting \((r/q)^2\), use \(4\sinh^2(Wm/2)\le e^{Wm}\) to get (2).
For comparison the nonzero absolute norm of \(G(\alpha)/q\) bounds \(q^d\) by \(e^{Wm}r^{d-2}\), proving (3).
For any fixed limit \(q/r\to c\in(1/2,1)\), the right side of (2) after subtracting \(2\log q\) grows at least linearly in the degree, while the corresponding term in (3) is negative.

There is a gain for exact detection with arbitrary signs as well.
Consider sequences with \(D\to\infty\), \(q/S\to c\in(1,2)\), and a modularly vanishing word that is nonzero in the number field.
Use \(H\le S\), so (5) would force an exact zero, and (4) must hold with \(H\) replaced by \(S\).
The bounds used in (6), together with \(\Delta\ge1\), yield
\[
           \liminf \frac{Wm}{d}\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log(2c).
\]
Indeed the right side of (4) grows as \((2q/S)^{n-1}/O(\sqrt n)\) or faster, while its left side is at most \((S/q)^2(e^{Wm}+1)\).
The ordinary coefficient-length norm bound \(q^d\le S^d e^{Wm}\) gives only \(\log c\) in that limit, strictly smaller for \(c<2\).
These estimates concern the full scalar congruence in the Salem order, including all of its coefficient coordinates.
They impose no hypothesis on the modulus order and give no general count of surviving words or uniform positive Lehmer gap.
