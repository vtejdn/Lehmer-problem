# The contribution of direct and reciprocal powers to the derivative denominator

<a id="statement"></a>
## An exponent sum for the structured part

Fix a monic reciprocal irreducible polynomial \(P\) of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(e=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and write \(h=\log M(P)\).
Let \(S\) be any nonempty set of integers in \([L,2L]\), with \(L\ge2\) integral.
Define for \(p\in S\)
\[
 T_p=\prod_{q\in S,\ q\ne p}(\alpha^{|p-q|}-1)\prod_{q\in S}(\alpha^{p+q}-1),\qquad
 \mathfrak a=\bigcap_{p\in S}(T_p)\subseteq\mathcal O_K .
\]
Write \(D_{\pm}\) for the positive generator of \(\mathfrak a\cap\mathbb Z\).
For \(d\le4L\), let \(m_p(d)\) count the factors in this product whose exponent is a multiple of \(d\), with repetitions, and put
\[
 a_d=\max_{p\in S}m_p(d),\qquad A(S)=\sum_{d\le4L}a_d\varphi(d).
\]
Uniformly over the choice of \(S\) at fixed \(P\),
\[
 \log N\mathfrak a=h A(S)+O_P\bigl(L\log^3(2L)\bigr),\qquad
 \frac1e\log N\mathfrak a\le\log D_{\pm}\le\frac12\log N\mathfrak a+O_P(1). \tag{1}
\]
When all powered conjugates are distinct as in the [powered-node denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v521.md#statement), \(D_{\pm}\) divides that denominator \(D\).
For \(e=2\), all node differences come from the two indicated conjugates, so \(D=D_{\pm}\) and \(\log D= h A(S)/2+O_P(L\log^3(2L))\).
For the full set of primes of \([L,2L]\), one has \(A(S)\asymp L^2\) with absolute implied constants.
Thus for every fixed reciprocal seed the direct and reciprocal powers alone already contribute logarithmic size \(\asymp_P L^2\), before including collisions with other conjugates or deleting characteristics.

<a id="layers"></a>
## Order layers in the ideal intersection

Factor each \(T_p\) into the values \(\Phi_d(\alpha)^{m_p(d)}\).
The norm of the product of the ideals \((\Phi_d(\alpha))^{a_d}\) bounds \(N\mathfrak a\) above by taking valuations one prime ideal at a time.
For the reverse estimate, use just the part of each \((\Phi_d(\alpha))\) supported away from the rational primes dividing \(d\).
At such a prime ideal its residue root has exact order \(d\), so the retained ideals for different orders have disjoint supports.
At any prime ideal in one retained layer the maximum of \(v(T_p)\) is at least \(a_d v(\Phi_d(\alpha))\), by choosing a maximizing \(p\) for this \(d\).
Consequently the product of the retained ideal norms with weights \(a_d\) bounds \(N\mathfrak a\) below.
Both the [full cyclotomic norm](Yin_C_G0345_v493.md#aggregate) and the [prime-to-order part](Yin_C_G0345_v509.md#layers) have logarithm \(h\varphi(d)+O_P((1+\tau(d))\log(2d))\), increasing the constant for the finitely many small orders.
The congruences \(q\equiv p\) and \(q\equiv-p\pmod d\) each have at most \(1+L/d\) solutions in the interval.
Hence \(a_d\le2(1+L/d)\ll L/d\) for \(1\le d\le4L\).
Summing the norm error gives \(O_P(L\log^3(2L))\), since \(\sum_{d\le x}\tau(d)/d=\sum_{uv\le x}1/(uv)\le(1+\log x)^2\).
This proves the first estimate of (1) even when maximizing exponents change between orders.

<a id="rational"></a>
## Contraction and the reciprocal involution

For each rational prime \(\ell\) let \(s_{\mathfrak p}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)/e_{\mathfrak p}\) at \(\mathfrak p\mid\ell\), with absolute ramification index \(e_{\mathfrak p}\) and residue degree \(f_{\mathfrak p}\).
The exponent in \(D_{\pm}\) is \(\lceil\max_{\mathfrak p\mid\ell}s_{\mathfrak p}\rceil\), while that in \(N\mathfrak a\) is \(\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid\ell}e_{\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak p}s_{\mathfrak p}\).
The weights sum to \(e\), giving the lower bound on \(\log D_{\pm}\).
Reciprocity supplies the nonidentity field involution \(\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}\).
It preserves all the ideals \((T_p)\) because \(\alpha\) is a unit, so it also preserves \(\mathfrak a\).
If a prime attaining the maximum is moved, its orbit contributes weight at least two at the maximum.
If it is fixed, it is the only prime over its restriction to \(K^{\sigma}\) in the quadratic extension \(K/K^{\sigma}\), so its relative ramification index times relative residue degree is two and its absolute weight is at least two.
In either case the norm exponent is at least twice the maximum.
Rounding this maximum costs less than one at each ramified rational prime and costs zero at unramified primes, giving the upper bound in (1) with error bounded by \(\sum_{\ell\mid\operatorname{disc}K}\log\ell\).
Under the distinct-node hypothesis, at the node \(\alpha^p\) the product of its differences against \(\alpha^q\) for \(q\ne p\) and \(\alpha^{-q}\) for all \(q\) equals \(T_p\) times a unit.
It divides the full derivative at that node in the splitting integers.
Therefore \(D/T_p\) is integral for each \(p\), giving \(D_{\pm}\mid D\).
For a quadratic seed these lists are exhaustive, and conjugation gives the same integrality condition at the inverse nodes, proving equality.

<a id="prime-interval"></a>
## Size of the exponent sum on the full prime interval

The bound \(a_d\ll L/d\) and \(\varphi(d)\le d\) give \(A(S)\ll L^2\) for every set in the interval.
For the full prime set, each distinct sum of two distinct exponent primes gives an index \(d\) with \(a_d\ge1\).
The [dyadic totient estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v494.md#statement) makes their total totient \(\gg L^2\), proving the matching lower bound.
The fixed-seed error in (1) is then lower order.
These estimates concern the collision denominator before deletions and carry seed-dependent errors when the degree is allowed to grow.
