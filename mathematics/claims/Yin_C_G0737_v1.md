## Complete argument

### UT2. Actual canonical low2^(t+2) values

We first record the elementary integer binomial fact used for each full composition.
For N>0 and actual polynomials f,g,

    (f+2g)^N=f^N modulo2^(v_2(N)+1).              (UT4)

Indeed i*binom(N,i)=N*binom(N-1,i-1) gives v_2(binom(N,i))+i>=v_2(N)+i-v_2(i)>=v_2(N)+1 for every i>=1.
This proves the statement coefficientwise without replacing an integer power by a binary class.

The integer differences Phi(C_2)-Phi^2 and Phi(C_3)-Phi L_3 are even, where C_2=X^2-2, C_3=X^3-3X and L_3=X^4+X^3+1.

Using the complete actual decomposition W=Phi^A W_Q+2^t V before substitution therefore gives

    T(C_2)=2^(t+2)V(C_2),
    T(C_3)=2^(t+2)V(C_3)
                                 modulo(2^(t+3),Phi^A).       (UT5)

For the dyadic canonical value use the actual identity Delta(T)=TQ_D+a, where Delta(T)=(T(C_2)-T^2)/2.
Take its monic Phi^A remainder over the integers before dividing the numerator by2.
UT2 and UT5 imply

The T^2 term has coefficient2^(2t+4) before this half, and the TQ_D carry has coefficient2^(t+2), so both disappear at the displayed precision.
Now V(C_2)bar=nu(X^2)=nu^2 is zero modulo phi^A.
Its actual monic Phi^A remainder is therefore even, proving a in(2^(t+2),Phi^A).
Every half in this step is a genuine half of a known integer remainder; no division of an abstract binary ideal occurs.

Define the actual integer odd quotients by T(C_3)=TQ_3+3b and T(C_5)=TQ_5+5c.
UT2--UT5 give b in(2^(t+2),Phi^A), and, with lambda=X^3+X and q_3=Q_3bar,

    b_0bar=nu(lambda)+nu q_3 modulo phi^A.          (UT6)

The evaluation nu(lambda) is legitimate here: phi(lambda)=phi L_3, so lambda preserves the full phi^A ideal, and UT5 itself was obtained by composing complete integer polynomials.
