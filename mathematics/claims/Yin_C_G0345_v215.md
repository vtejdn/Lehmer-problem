# Exact resultant groups on two grids for equal prime blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two simultaneous restrictions on each factor

Let \(g,n\ge1\) be coprime, \(N=n+g\) odd, and \(n>6g\).
For \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) put
\[
 A(Y)=(Y-2)(Y-3),\quad A^*(Y)=(1-2Y)(1-3Y),\qquad
 P=X^n A(X^g)+\varepsilon A^*(X^g).
\]
Write \(C\) for its [full cyclotomic part](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion).
For \(\varepsilon=-1\) this is \(X^2-1\) if \(g\) is odd and \(X-1\) otherwise.
For \(\varepsilon=1\) it is \(1\) if \(g\) is odd and \(X+1\) otherwise.
Define the monic integer polynomials
\[
 Q=P/C,\qquad B=(X^N+\varepsilon)/(X+\varepsilon),\qquad
 K=(X^n+\varepsilon)/C.
\]
Let \(e=1\) when \(g\) is odd and \(\varepsilon=1\), and let \(e=0\) otherwise.
The following are isomorphisms of finite additive groups:
\[
 \mathbb Z[X]/(B,Q)\simeq(\mathbb Z/6)^{N-1},\qquad
 \mathbb Z[X]/(K,Q)\simeq(\mathbb Z/5)^{n-\deg C}
                              \oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^{2e}.                \tag{1}
\]
They hold for composite \(N,n\), including at primes dividing the orders of roots in either grid.

For any monic factor \(S\mid Q\) over \(\mathbb Z\), of degree \(d\), put
\[
 r_\ell=\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar B)\quad(\ell=2,3),\qquad
 r_5=\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar K)\quad(\ell=5).
\]
Each bar refers to reduction in the indicated characteristic, and the gcd counts multiplicity.
If \(e=1\) put \(u=\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar K)\) in characteristic two, and otherwise put \(u=0\).
Then \(0\le u\le2e\) and
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathbb Z[X]/(B,S)&\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)^{r_2}\oplus(\mathbb Z/3)^{r_3},\\
 \mathbb Z[X]/(K,S)&\simeq(\mathbb Z/5)^{r_5}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^u,\\
 |\operatorname{Res}(S,B)|&=2^{r_2}3^{r_3},\qquad
 |\operatorname{Res}(S,K)|=5^{r_5}2^u,                               \tag{2}\\
 0\le d-r_2,\ d-r_3&\le g+1-\deg C,\qquad 0\le d-r_5\le2g .
 \end{split}
\]
These bounds compare both resultants for the same irreducible factor even if its share of the exterior roots tends to zero.
In particular, as \(n\to\infty\) with \(n\ge C_1(2g+1)^9\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C_1\), every choice of a noncyclotomic irreducible factor satisfies
\[
       \frac1d\log\frac{|\operatorname{Res}(S,B)|}
                           {|\operatorname{Res}(S,K)|}
                         \longrightarrow \log(6/5).                   \tag{3}
\]
The [prime-delay calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#ideals) identifies the actual supporting prime ideals in the field of a primitive \(N\)-th root when \(N\) is prime.
Equations (1) and (2) concern the full integral polynomial quotients on both grids.

<a id="order"></a>
## Units on the complete grid of nontrivial roots

For \(a>1\) set \(G_a(T)=(T^a-1)/(T-1)\).
In \(R_a=\mathbb Z[T]/G_a\) one has \(T^a=1\).
If \((a,b)=1\) and \(b\ge1\), multiplication by \(G_b(T)\) on the integral power basis has determinant
\[
       \prod_{\substack{\tau^a=1\\ \tau\ne1}}
          (1-\tau^b)/(1-\tau)=1 .
\]
Indeed after complexification multiplication is diagonal at the distinct roots, and exponentiation by \(b\) permutes them.
The inverse matrix is integral, so in particular there is an inverse element in \(R_a\), given by the inverse image of one.
For odd \(a\), \(1+T^b\) is also a unit because its determinant is \(G_a(-1)=1\) under the same coprimality hypothesis.
These statements include products over composite orders in the single integral quotient.

<a id="six"></a>
## The grid of order \(n+g\)

In \(\mathbb Z[X]\), with \(Y=X^g\), direct expansion gives
\[
 X^g P=(X^N+\varepsilon)A(Y)+6\varepsilon(Y-1)(Y^2+1).
\]
In the ring modulo \(B\), the variable \(T=-\varepsilon X\) identifies this ring with \(R_N\), since \(N\) is odd.
Here \(X\) and \(1+Y^2\) are units by \((2g,N)=1\).
If \((-\varepsilon)^g=-1\) then \(C=1\), and \(Y-1=-(T^g+1)\) is a unit as well.
The identity therefore writes \(Q\) as six times a unit.

If \((-\varepsilon)^g=1\), the factor \(X+\varepsilon\) divides both \(C\) and \(Y-1\).
Divide the polynomial identity by \(X+\varepsilon\) before reducing modulo \(B\).
The first term on the right becomes zero and \((Y-1)/(X+\varepsilon)\) is \(G_g(T)\) up to sign, a unit since \((g,N)=1\).
The quotient \(C/(X+\varepsilon)\) is one or the other linear factor, whose image is \(1+T\) up to sign and hence a unit.
This again gives \(Q=6\) times a unit in \(\mathbb Z[X]/B\), proving the first assertion of (1).

<a id="five"></a>
## The grid of order \(n\)

The identity \(A^*(Y)-A(Y)=5(Y^2-1)\) gives directly
\[
 Q=K A(Y)+5\varepsilon H,\qquad H=(X^{2g}-1)/C.
\]
If \(g\) is even then \(n\) is odd, and setting \(T=-\varepsilon X\) makes \(K\) and \(H\) equal up to signs to \(G_n(T)\) and \(G_{2g}(T)\).
Coprimality makes \(H\) a unit modulo \(K\).
If \(g\) is odd and \(\varepsilon=-1\), these polynomials are \(G_{n/2}(X^2)\) and \(G_g(X^2)\).
The inverse identity in \(R_{n/2}\) remains valid under \(T\mapsto X^2\) because \((g,n/2)=1\).
In both cases the asserted group follows from \(Q=5\varepsilon H\bmod K\).

In the remaining case \(g\) is odd, \(n\) even, \(C=1\), \(K=X^n+1\) and \(H=X^{2g}-1\).
In the quotient by \(K,H\), raising \(X^n=-1\) to the odd power \(g\) gives \(-1\), whereas raising \(X^{2g}=1\) to \(n/2\) gives \(1\).
Thus \(2=0\) there and the quotient is exactly \(\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^2-1)\), since \(\gcd(n,2g)=2\) and the Euclidean algorithm for \(X^a-1,X^b-1\) works in every characteristic.
In \(R=\mathbb Z[X]/K\) the ideals \(5R\) and \(HR\) are consequently comaximal.
The Chinese remainder theorem gives \(R/(5H)\simeq R/5R\times R/HR\), proving the exceptional case of (1).

<a id="factors"></a>
## Ranks in the residue characteristics

Since \(S\mid Q\), the groups for \(S\) are quotients of the respective groups in (1).
Every primary component has prime exponent, including when the grid polynomial has repeated residue roots.
Reducing the quotient by \(\ell\) gives the vector space \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]/(\bar S,\bar B)\) or \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]/(\bar S,\bar K)\), of dimension the degree of the corresponding gcd.
This proves both group descriptions and \(u\le2e\).
On the free power basis modulo \(S\), the cokernel of multiplication by either grid polynomial is its displayed finite quotient, and the absolute determinant is its order.
The determinant is the resultant since over \(\mathbb C\) the eigenvalues are the values at each root of \(S\) with multiplicity, including when \(S\) has repeated roots.
This proves the norm formulas.

At 2 and 3 one has \(\bar P=(X^g+1)(X^N+\varepsilon)\), and the table for \(C\) gives \(\bar Q=\bar B V\) with \(V=(X^g+1)(X+\varepsilon)/\bar C\) of degree \(g+1-\deg C\).
Here \(\bar C\) divides the numerator in both characteristics, including the repeated linear root at 2.
In characteristic five the second polynomial identity gives \(\bar Q=\bar A(X^g)\bar K\), with complementary degree \(2g\).
For \(\bar S\mid U V\) over any field, \(\bar S/\gcd(\bar S,U)\) divides \(V\), as is seen at each irreducible polynomial by comparing its multiplicities.
This gives the three claimed degree deficits without a separability assumption.
Taking logs in (2) now gives \(d\log(6/5)+O(g)\) for the logarithm of the quotient in (3).
The [exterior allocation](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#integers) gives \(d\ge n/(2g)\) throughout the stated large-delay range, so \(g/d\to0\) uniformly there.
This proves (3).
