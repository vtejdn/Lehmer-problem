## Complete argument

### 1. Actual maximal multiplicity and exclusion of the constant quotient

By the original residual identities and C611v1, F_s is a nonzero monic integer polynomial of degree 15d, constant coefficient 1, reciprocal, and divisible by P.
Repeated division by the monic P stays in Z[z].
Thus the largest exponent nu exists, is at least 1, and is at most 15 by degree.

To exclude nu=15, suppose it held.
The remaining quotient has degree zero and leading coefficient 1, so F_s=P^15 exactly.
Let k be the smallest positive exponent with nonzero coefficient p_k in P.
It exists with 1<=k<=d, since P is nonconstant and monic, and

    P(z)=1+p_k z^k+terms of higher degree,
    [z^k]P(z)^15=15p_k!=0.                       (13)

Every nonconstant term in P(z^j), for j=2,3,5, starts in degree at least jk>k.
The same is true of their positive powers after subtracting their constant terms.
Consequently each H23, H25 and H35 has zero coefficient at z^k.
The shifted term z^h H23 has no degree-k contribution because h=9d/2>d>=k.
Likewise the shifted part z^(5d)H25 has none because 5d>k.
The definition (1) therefore gives [z^k]F_s=0, contradicting (13).
This argument includes k=d; it makes no assumption about an earlier nonzero coefficient of P.
It proves nu<=14.

The quotient Q_s is monic and integral, has degree (15-nu)d and constant coefficient 1.
For a polynomial A with nonzero constant coefficient write A^*(z)=z^(deg A)A(1/z).
The exact equality F_s=P^nu Q_s and reciprocal identities for F_s and P give

    P^nu Q_s^*=F_s^*=F_s=P^nu Q_s.

Cancellation in Z[z] proves Q_s^*=Q_s.
If Q_s(alpha)=0, the minimal polynomial P divides Q_s over Q[z], hence over Z[z] by monic division.
That contradicts maximality of nu, proving Q_s(alpha)!=0 and gcd(P,Q_s)=1.
Every conjugate value is nonzero by the same minimal-polynomial argument.

The primitive H35 and monic Q_s are primitive integer polynomials, so their product is primitive by Gauss's lemma.
Their degrees add without cancellation and their leading and constant coefficients multiply, proving the exact Ntilde_s data in A.
Mahler multiplicativity applied to F_s=P^nu Q_s and D_s=P^(nu-1)Q_s proves (3).
The inequality uses m(P)>=0, with no upper Mahler bound inferred.
For nu=1, Q_s=D_s; for nu>=2 the displayed factor P^(nu-1) gives D_s(alpha)=0.
Thus the new construction includes precisely the old zero-value branch as well as its nonzero branch.

### 2. The full expanded numerator and all six congruences

    Delta_2=(P_2-P^2)/2 in Z[z],
    Delta_5=(P_5-P^5)/5 in Z[z].

Their coefficientwise integrality follows by reducing in F_p[z]: mixed binomial terms vanish and every integer coefficient satisfies a^p=a modulo p.
Thus P_2=P^2+2Delta_2 and P_5=P^5+5Delta_5.
No order-index or factorization hypothesis modulo p is used.

The four coefficients 27000, 2000, 1944 and 320 in (14) are divisible by 8, hence also by 4 and 2.
Only the P_2^3 and P_2^5 terms need to be considered for the three dyadic rows.
Their COMPLETE expansions are

    P_2^3=P^6+6P^4Delta_2+12P^2Delta_2^2+8Delta_2^3,
    P_2^5=P^10+10P^8Delta_2+40P^6Delta_2^2
             +80P^4Delta_2^3+80P^2Delta_2^4+32Delta_2^5.

Modulo 8, every term not already divisible by P^2 has coefficient divisible by 8.
Modulo 4, every term not divisible by P^4 has coefficient divisible by 4.
Modulo 2, the surviving first terms are P^6 and P^10, both divisible by P^6.
Multiplication by the actual integral scalars, z^h and Z preserves those ideal memberships.
This proves the first three assertions in (4).

The four coefficients 30375, 27000, 2000 and 375 in (14) are divisible by 125, hence also by 25 and 5.
For the first two 5-adic rows the surviving terms are -1944P_5^3 and 320ZP_5^2.

    320ZP_5^2=320ZP^10+3200ZP^5Delta_5+8000ZDelta_5^2.

Modulo 125, every term not divisible by P^5 has coefficient divisible by 125; in particular 8000=64*125.
Modulo 25, every term not divisible by P^10 has coefficient divisible by 25; this includes 75, 125, 3200 and 8000.
For modulus 5 the entire coefficient 320 vanishes, and -1944P_5^3 reduces to -1944P^15.
Thus the last three assertions in (4) hold.
Every phase and cross term is included, and no coefficientwise divisibility of F_s by the listed moduli has been asserted.

### 3. Repeated monic cancellation and exact original-order income

Use the precise monic cancellation fact, with its proof retained here for the larger exponents.
If A=P B in Z[z], P is monic and

    A=P^j U+m V, j>=1, m>=1,

then P divides m V.
The unique remainder R=rem_P V belongs to Z[z] and has degree less than d.
Taking the remainder of m V gives m R=0 coefficientwise in Z, so R=0.
Therefore V=P W for an integer polynomial W, and cancellation gives

    B=P^(j-1)U+m W.

If A=P^nu Q and j>=nu, repeating this argument nu times proves

    Q in (P^(j-nu),m).                            (15)

To force divisibility of Q(alpha) by m one needs j>nu, which is explicitly imposed below.
There is no requirement that P be coprime to m, and repeated factors modulo a prime cause no problem.

For nu=1 the row (P^2,8) has j>nu, giving Q_s in (P,8).
For nu=2 or 3 use (P^4,4); for nu=4 or 5 use (P^6,2).
These give exactly the dyadic exponent e_2 in (5).
For nu>=6 this argument asserts no nontrivial dyadic factor; divisibility by 2^0=1 is automatic.

For nu=1 through 4 use (P^5,125).
For nu=5 through 9 use (P^10,25).
For nu=10 through 14 use (P^15,5).
Each selected row has j>nu and gives exactly the exponent e_5 in (5).
The proof of nu<=14 is what ensures at least the last factor 5 survives.

Taking the monic remainder modulo P after (15), each coefficient of rem_P Q_s is divisible by the applicable power of 2 and by the applicable power of 5.
These integers are coprime, so each coefficient is divisible by their product c_nu.
This proves the first two assertions of (6) in Z[z] itself, rather than after saturation in an overorder.
Evaluation gives Q_s(alpha)=c_nu b_s(alpha) in the actual ring Z[alpha]=Z[z]/(P), which is free over Z with basis 1,alpha,...,alpha^(d-1).
Since Q_s(alpha)!=0, also b_s(alpha)!=0.
Every element b_s(alpha) is an algebraic integer, and its full K norm is a nonzero rational integer.
Taking norms over all d original embeddings proves (6), including the uniform lower bound 5^d.

No derivative is divided out of this norm estimate.
The integrality of b_s(alpha) does not make the monic polynomial Q_s coefficientwise divisible by c_nu.
Its rational normalization Q_s/c_nu remains of degree (15-nu)d, while b_s only represents its original value.
Their circle functions, supports and Mahler measures are not identified.

For (11), use (3) and c_nu>=5 to obtain
