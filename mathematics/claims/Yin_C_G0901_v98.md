# The loss of Mahler measure at an oriented block norm

<a id="statement"></a>
## Two bounds on the loss and the field of the measure

Let \(z\) be a compressed reciprocal nontorsion unit in the [oriented block alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#statement), of degree \(d=2ub\), with \(u\) blocks of \(b\ge9\) inverse pairs.
Here compression means that distinct conjugates have nontorsion quotient.
Let \(L\) be the splitting field of \(z\), let \(F\subset L\) be the fixed field of the kernel of its Galois action on the \(2u\) oriented halves, and let \(\beta\) be the product in one half.
Write \(M\) for multiplicative Mahler measure and \(m=\log M\) for total logarithmic measure.
Put \(e=2u/\deg\beta\), the multiplicity of each conjugate in the list of half products.
The weighted loss
\[
                   \Delta=m(z)-e\,m(\beta)
\]
is nonnegative, including when \(\beta\) is torsion.

Choose either half in each block \(B\) and let \(p_B,n_B,q_B\) count its exterior, interior and unit-circle roots, respectively.
Let \(P_B,N_B\) be the sum of its positive log moduli and the absolute sum of its negative log moduli.
In each block choose the positive or negative class having the smaller log mass, with either choice at a tie, and let \(k\) be the total number of labels in the chosen classes.
Then
\[
 \Delta=2\sum_B\min(P_B,N_B),\qquad
 [F(M(z)):F]=
        \prod_B\frac{b!}{p_B!\,n_B!\,q_B!}=:D .                             \tag{1}
\]
If \(\Delta=0\), the exterior roots form a union of complete oriented halves, \(M(z)=M(\beta)^e\), and \(\mathbb Q(M(z))=\mathbb Q(M(\beta))\).
Conversely \(\Delta=0\) whenever \(M(z)\in F\), or even when \(M(z)\) belongs to a solvable extension of \(F\).
More precisely the relative degree in (1) remains \(D\) after adjoining all finite solvable Galois extensions of \(F\).
If \(\Delta>0\), then \(D\ge b\), and
\[
    \Delta\ \ge\ \frac{k}{d-1}\,d^{-d/(2k)}
                 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(d-1)m(z)}{k}\right).                   \tag{2}
\]
For nontorsion \(\beta\) there is also an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
            m(z)\bigl(m(z)-\Delta\bigr)
                 \ge \frac{2c u}{(\log(3d))^{212}} .                       \tag{3}
\]
These estimates apply at every nonterminal step of the [constituent decomposition](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent), without a degree-record hypothesis.

<a id="mass"></a>
## Half products and the exterior proportion

At the chosen complex embedding a half contributes \(P_B+N_B\) to \(m(z)\) when counted together with its reciprocal half.
Its product has log modulus \(P_B-N_B\), and the opposite product has the negative log modulus.
By transitivity on the \(2u\) halves, their contributions sum to \(\sum_B|P_B-N_B|=e\,m(\beta)\), proving the first formula in (1).
If a half contains a neutral root \(y\), ordinary complex conjugation sends \(y\) to \(y^{-1}\) in its opposite half.
It therefore sends the entire half to that opposite half, so \(P_B=N_B\).
In particular a half product of modulus different from one requires all \(b\) labels in its block to be nonneutral.
When \(\min(P_B,N_B)=0\), a half containing a neutral root is consequently entirely neutral.
A half without a neutral root and with this zero minimum lies entirely on one side of the circle.
Thus \(\Delta=0\) is equivalent to each block having a single color in the counts of (1).

Write \(l(v)\) for the number of exterior conjugates of \(v\).
There are \(e\,l(\beta)\) halves with product of modulus greater than one, at most one per block.
Each such block has \(b\) exterior roots across its two halves.
It follows that
\[
                 l(z)\ge be\,l(\beta),\qquad
                 \frac{l(z)}{d}\ge\frac{l(\beta)}{\deg\beta}.               \tag{4}
\]
At zero loss the counts in (4) are equalities.
Compression by products of torsion classes preserves the proportion on the right, since every class has the same size and its members have equal absolute values. [Torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0901_v68.md#degree)

<a id="orbit"></a>
## The actual field degree, also over the solvable closure

The product over the set \(S\) of exterior roots differs from \(M(z)\) by a rational sign.
It uniquely maximizes the absolute product among subsets of the same size, so the stabilizers of \(M(z)\) and \(S\) agree. [Exterior-set stabilizer](Yin_C_G0345_v801.md#degree)
Over \(F\) each half is fixed as a set, and the action permutes the \(b\) indices in its block with the same permutation on the inverse half.
The three colors specify exactly which member of each inverse pair belongs to \(S\), including the case where neither does.
The kernel over \(F\) contains the product of the pure \(A_b\) actions on individual blocks by the [supported three-cycle lifts](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#cycles).
On any one block \(A_b\) attains every ordered coloring with the given counts, since some color repeats and a transposition within that color corrects parity.
These actions are independent on distinct blocks, and fixing each half setwise allows no other color counts.
This gives precisely the multinomial degree in (1), using the block coloring also considered in the [least-degree orbit bound](Yin_C_G0345_v804.md#count).

For every finite solvable Galois extension \(K/F\), the quotient of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\) acting on \(K\cap L\) is solvable.
The perfect product of the alternating factors therefore fixes \(K\cap L\), and its elements still occur on restriction from \(KL/K\).
The same holds over the full solvable closure since its intersection with the finite Galois extension \(L/F\) is contained in a finite solvable Galois extension.
Thus the same product attains all \(D\) colorings after either base change.
Any nonconstant coloring has at least \(b\) images, since the positions of a color of multiplicity between one and \(b-1\) alone have at least \(b\) possibilities.
The characterization of zero loss from the colors now proves both field criteria.
At zero loss (1) gives \(M(z)=M(\beta)^e\) with \(M(\beta)>1\).
The same exterior-set argument shows \(\mathbb Q(M(\beta)^e)=\mathbb Q(M(\beta))\), since fixing a positive power fixes its modulus and hence the exterior set giving \(M(\beta)\).

<a id="separation"></a>
## A discriminant cost for mixing interior and exterior roots

When \(\Delta>0\) the \(k\) selected labels have positive absolute log moduli \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\) with \(\sum_jt_j=\Delta/2\).
For each label let \(a_j=e^{-t_j}v_j\) be its unique interior root, where \(|v_j|=1\).
The number \(1/\overline{a_j}=e^{t_j}v_j\) is an exterior conjugate by reciprocity and complex conjugation.
These \(k\) pairs of actual roots are disjoint because the interior roots are distinct and the reflected exterior roots are their injective images.

Use the Vandermonde matrix of all \(d\) roots with columns of powers \(0,\ldots,d-1\).
Its absolute determinant is at least one since its square up to sign is the nonzero integral discriminant.
Subtract from each of the \(k\) exterior rows its paired interior row, preserving the determinant.
At exponent \(a\) the new entry has absolute value \(e^{a t_j}(1-e^{-2a t_j})\), bounded by \(e^{(d-1)t_j}\min(1,2(d-1)t_j)\).
Every unaltered row of root \(y\) has norm at most \(\sqrt d\,\max(1,|y|)^{d-1}\), and the modified rows satisfy this same norm bound at their exterior roots with the additional minimum factor.
Hadamard's inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean give
\[
  1\le d^{d/2}e^{(d-1)m(z)}
             \prod_{j=1}^k\min(1,2(d-1)t_j)
     \le d^{d/2}e^{(d-1)m(z)}
             \left(\frac{(d-1)\Delta}{k}\right)^k .
\]
This proves (2).
For example, at \(m(z)\le H\) and \(k\ge\theta d>0\), it implies \(\Delta\ge\theta e^{-H/\theta}d^{-1/(2\theta)}\).
Even with no lower bound on \(k/d\), at most \(\Delta/(2\tau)\) labels on the selected sides have absolute log modulus at least any prescribed \(\tau>0\).

For (3) choose the half containing \(z\).
Its product \(\beta\) belongs to \(\mathbb Q(z)\), since every automorphism fixing \(z\) preserves that half. [Block norm fields](Yin_C_G0345_v274.md#fields)
The units \(z,\beta\) are multiplicatively independent when \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
Indeed a relation involving \(z\) with nonzero exponent, conjugated by a supported three-cycle moving \(z\) within its half and fixing \(\beta\), would give a torsion quotient between distinct roots of \(z\).
A relation with zero exponent of \(z\) would make \(\beta\) torsion.
The [rank-two height product](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) at joint degree \(d\) gives \(h(z)h(\beta)\ge c/(d(\log(3d))^{212})\).
Multiplying by \(d\deg\beta\) and using \(e\deg\beta=2u\) proves (3).

<a id="chain"></a>
## Simultaneous bounds along the constituent decomposition

Take \(w\) and the compressed units \(z_1,\ldots,z_s\) under the small-measure hypotheses of [C0901 v93](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#statement).
For \(i<s\), the oriented half product \(\beta_i\) is nontorsion and \(z_{i+1}\) is its compression.
Write \(e_i,\Delta_i,D_i\) for the quantities above, and put \(E_i=\prod_{h<i}e_h\), including \(E_1=1\).
As compression preserves total measure, successive substitutions and (4) give
\[
 m(w)=E_s m(z_s)+\sum_{i<s}E_i\Delta_i,\qquad
 \frac{l(z_1)}{\deg z_1}\ge\cdots\ge\frac{l(z_s)}{\deg z_s}.
\]
Thus the [Voutier bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) at \(d_s=\deg z_s\) yields
\[
    m(w)>\frac{E_s}{4}\left(\frac{\log\log d_s}{\log d_s}\right)^3
                  +\sum_{i<s}E_i\Delta_i ,
\]
with the explicit lower bound (2) at each stage of positive loss.
In particular \(m(z_{i+1})>m(z_i)/2\) forces \(e_i=1\), so near equality of the two actual measures controls the unweighted mixed log mass directly.

Let \(T_i=\mathbb Q(M(z_i),\ldots,M(z_s))\).
All later units and their conjugates belong to the splitting field of \(\beta_i\), since each descending step takes products of actual conjugates.
This splitting field lies in \(F_i\), the field of the oriented halves at stage \(i\).
Hence \(T_{i+1}\subseteq F_i\), and \([T_i:T_{i+1}]\ge [F_i(M(z_i)):F_i]=D_i\).
At zero loss \(M(z_i)=M(z_{i+1})^{e_i}\), so the relative degree is exactly one there.
In particular
\[
                    [T_1:T_s]\ge\prod_{i<s}D_i .
\]
This distinguishes a lossless stage from an arbitrarily small positive loss by a relative degree of at least \(b_i\) in the latter case.
The estimates allow a lossless stage with \(e_i=1\) and a smaller-degree representative with the same measure.
At such a stage the mass identity alone supplies no positive additive cost and does not rule out multiple constituents.
