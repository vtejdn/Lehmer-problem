# Prime orders with every returning prime above the order times its logarithm

<a id="statement"></a>
## A choice of prime orders for every unit

Let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(D\), with logarithmic Mahler measure \(0<m(Q)\le1\).
There are absolute positive constants \(c,C,y_0\) with the following property.
For each integer \(y\ge\max(D,y_0)\) one can choose at least \(\exp(cy)\) distinct primes \(p\) in \([\exp(cy),\exp(Cy)]\) such that every rational prime \(\ell\) dividing \(U_p=|\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_p)|\) satisfies
\[
   \ell\equiv\pm1\bmod p,\qquad \ell>c p\log p,\qquad
   \ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}Q .
\]
Every corresponding prime ideal at which \(\alpha\) has order \(p\) has absolute residue degree one in the positive class and two in the negative class.
All valuations of \(U_p\) come from these exact orders.
Uniformly for the indicated bases and orders as \(y\to\infty\),
\[
                       \log U_p=(1+o(1))(p-1)m(Q).
\]
For each such order one can form an irreducible \(P(z)=Q(z^a)\) of degree \(d\) with \(d<p<2d\), preserving the Mahler measure, the primitive resultant, and every local residue field and valuation contributing to it.
In particular a positive absolute Lehmer gap is equivalent to \(\log U_p\ge c_1 d\), with an absolute \(c_1>0\), on all sufficiently large representatives so constructed.
It is enough for this bound to hold along an unbounded choice of those orders for each fixed base, with the same \(c_1\).

<a id="orders"></a>
## Constructing the orders and excluding small multiples

Put \(M=4\prod_{3\le q\le y+1}q\) over odd primes \(q\).
Take primes \(s\in(y+1,2(y+1))\), \(u\in(e^y,2e^y)\), using the [dyadic Chebyshev bounds](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units) for large \(y\).
For each of the \(u-1\) nonzero residues modulo \(u\), choose the least prime \(p\) in the simultaneous classes
\[
                   p\equiv-1\bmod M,\qquad 2p\equiv-1\bmod s
\]
and in that residue modulo \(u\).
The moduli are coprime and all classes are reduced.
The [least-prime bound of Xylouris](../audits/literature/Xyl11/Yin_U001.md#xyl11-u001) gives \(p\ll(Msu)^{5.18}\).
Different residues modulo \(u\) give different primes.
Since \(p\ge M-1\) and \(\log M\asymp y\) by the same Chebyshev estimates, the stated exponential bounds follow.

For every \(1\le f\le D\), \(\gcd(f,p-1)\) divides two.
Indeed \(p\equiv3\bmod4\), and no odd prime at most \(D\) divides \(p-1\).
Consequently \(p\mid\ell^f-1\) implies \(\ell\equiv\pm1\bmod p\) by cyclicity of the prime field multiplicative group of order \(p-1\).
Every prime in those two residue classes is larger than \(yp-1\).
To see this, a candidate \(ip\pm1\) up to \(yp-1\) must have even \(2\le i\le y\), since \(p\) is odd and 2 is in neither class.
For \(ip-1\), an odd prime divisor of \(i+1\) divides \(M\) and hence \(ip-1\).
For \(ip+1\) with \(i>2\), use a prime divisor of \(i-1\) in the same way.
The remaining candidate \(2p+1\) is divisible by \(s\).
All these divisors are strictly smaller than the candidates for large \(y\).

Discard the sampled primes dividing the nonzero integer \(Q(1)\) or dividing \(\ell^2-1\) for any rational prime \(\ell\mid\operatorname{disc}Q\).
Their number is \(O(D\log(2D)/y)\), since \(\log|Q(1)|\le D\log2+m(Q)\), the [discriminant bound](Yin_C_G0899_v2.md#discriminant) is \(D\log D+2(D-1)m(Q)\), and each discarded prime has logarithm \(\gg y\).
This leaves the required exponential number of choices even for \(y=D\).

<a id="norm"></a>
## Local degrees and the rate of the full norm

In characteristic \(p\), \(\Phi_p(x)=(x-1)^{p-1}\), so \(p\nmid U_p\) since \(p\nmid Q(1)\).
For \(\ell\mid U_p\) there is a root of \(Q\bmod\ell\) of exact multiplicative order \(p\), giving \(p\mid\ell^f-1\) for some \(f\le D\).
The preceding congruences and exclusions imply the asserted size and good reduction.
At every prime ideal contributing to the norm of \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\), the residue of \(\alpha\) has order \(p\).
Good reduction of the polynomial implies that \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) has index prime to \(\ell\) in the integer ring, so the residue field is generated by that root.
Its degree is \(\operatorname{ord}_p(\ell)\), exactly one or two as asserted.
The exact-order prime valuations of \(\alpha^p-1\) there coincide with those of \(\Phi_p(\alpha)\), since \(\alpha-1\) is a unit at each such prime.

The [signed resultant bound](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) at exponent \(p\) gives
\[
   \log U_p=p\,m(Q)-\log|Q(1)|+
             O(D^3\log(2D)\log(2p)(1+m(Q))).
\]
The [positive-measure degree bound](Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#transfer) gives \(m(Q)\gg(\log(2D))^{-3}\).
Since \(D\le y\ll\log p\), both errors are uniformly \(o(p\,m(Q))\).

<a id="representatives"></a>
## Realizing the prime in the linear degree window

Choose a prime \(p/(2D)<a<p/D\) by the dyadic Chebyshev bounds, since \(p/D\to\infty\) uniformly.
All nontorsion algebraic units have \(D\ge2\), so \(a<p\).
The [prime substitution argument](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) makes \(Q(z^a)\) irreducible unless \(\alpha\) is an \(a\)-th power of some \(\gamma\) in \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
In that case \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=K\), \(\gamma\) is an algebraic unit, and its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(m(Q)/a\) since the conjugate powers are distinct.
The same degree bound then gives \(a\ll\log^3(2D)\), impossible uniformly for these choices.
The substitution preserves the Mahler measure, and raising to \(a\) permutes the primitive \(p\)-th roots, so the primitive resultant is \(U_p\).
The degree \(d=Da\) satisfies \(d<p<2d\).
Every contributing characteristic exceeds \(p\) and hence \(a\).
In the residue group of order \(p\) generated by \(\bar\alpha\), there is a unique \(a\)-th root of \(\bar\alpha\); it still has order \(p\).
The other roots have order \(ap\).
As in the [local transport argument](Yin_C_G0899_v3.md#transport), the unique returning root lifts by Hensel to the base completion, giving exactly one returning prime above the original prime and the same residue field.
The quotient of the \(ap\)-th power minus one by the \(p\)-th power minus one at that lift reduces to \(a\), preserving the full valuation.

If the absolute gap holds, the uniform norm estimate and \(p>d\) give the required bound.
Conversely for each fixed base take \(y\to\infty\) along the asserted choices and use \(d>p/2\) to deduce \(m(Q)\ge c_1/2\).
The cases of larger measure, nonunit constant or nonunit leading coefficient already have absolute positive lower bounds, and the measure is additive in logarithms across irreducible factors.
Thus the new restriction on all contributing prime ideals preserves the full gap question.
