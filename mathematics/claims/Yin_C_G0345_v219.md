# A quartic norm obstruction to rational factors of two nearby prime grids

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational divisors with no exceptional roots at two and three

Let \(r<q\) be odd primes greater than five, \(g=q-r\), and \(r>4g\).
Assume \(r\ge C(2g+1)^9\) with the absolute constant of the [prime-block allocation theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v198.md#statement).
For \(\varepsilon=\pm1\) put
\[
 A(Y)=(Y-2)(Y-3),\qquad A^*(Y)=(1-2Y)(1-3Y),\qquad
 Q=\frac{X^rA(X^g)+\varepsilon A^*(X^g)}{X+\varepsilon}.
\]
Every monic rational divisor \(S\) of \(Q\) is integral and reciprocal with constant term one and even degree \(d\), by the [torsion and reciprocity results](Yin_C_G0345_v212.md#torsion).
For \(p=q,r\) write \(B_p=(X^p+\varepsilon)/(X+\varepsilon)\).
For \(\ell=2,3\) define \(\delta_\ell=d-\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar B_q)\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\); define \(\delta_5=d-\deg\gcd(\bar S,\bar B_r)\) over \(\mathbb F_5\).
These degrees count the roots outside the respective primitive grids, with multiplicity.
Indeed the [disjoint reductions](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#three) of \(Q\) are \((X^g+1)B_q\) at two and three and \((X^{2g}+1)B_r\) at five.

If \(\delta_2=\delta_3=0\), put \(v=v_2(g)\) and \(b=\delta_5/2\).
Then \(b\) is an integer and
\[
                    2^v\mid b,\qquad b/2^v\equiv d\pmod4.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(2^{v+2}\mid\delta_5\), and \(2^{v+3}\mid\delta_5\) whenever \(4\mid d\).
Both signed binomial resultants \(\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-2)\) and \(\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-3)\) equal \(5^b\).

For each fixed odd \(h>8\) prime to five there are arbitrarily large prime pairs as above with \(2h\mid g\) such that a rational divisor of degree \(k(q-1)/h\), \(1\le k<h\), requires \(8\mid k\).
If \(39\mid h\), all four choices \(k=2,4,6,8\) give degrees of reciprocal local divisors at two, three and five simultaneously to all precisions for both signs.
Thus \(k=2,4,6\) on these pairs give genuine local factorizations whose prescribed degree is impossible over \(\mathbb Q\).
For \(k=8\) the congruence allows the local degree and rational divisibility remains open.

<a id="sign"></a>
## The signs of the binomial resultants

Write \(N_j=\operatorname{Res}(S,X^g-j)\), the product of \(x^g-j\) over the roots \(x\) of \(S\).
Evaluation of the numerator of \(Q\) at \(X^g=j\) gives the constant \(\varepsilon A^*(j)\), of absolute value \(15\) or \(40\) for \(j=2\) or \(3\), respectively.
By multiplicativity of the resultant, the only possible prime divisors of \(N_2N_3\) are \(2,3,5\).
At \(\ell=2,3\) every root of \(\bar S\) is \(-\varepsilon z\) with \(z\) of order \(q\), since both exceptional degrees vanish.
Its \(g\)-th power has order \(q\), so it cannot equal the residue of \(2\) or \(3\).
At five the primitive \(r\)-grid likewise contributes neither residue.
Every exceptional root has \(g\)-th power \(2\) or \(3\) modulo five; inversion interchanges those classes and preserves multiplicities.
Each class therefore has \(b=\delta_5/2\) roots.
For a five-adic root \(x\) of \(S\) reducing to either class, write \(y=x^g\).
The root equation gives
\[
                 (x^r+\varepsilon)(y-2)(y-3)=-5\varepsilon(y^2-1).
\]
Here \(x^r+\varepsilon\) is a unit because the exceptional residue of \(x\) has order dividing \(4g\), is different from \(1,-1\), and \(r>4g\) is prime.
The factor \(y^2-1\) and exactly one of \(y-2,y-3\) are also units.
The other factor has valuation one with \(v_5(5)=1\), including when \(5\mid g\).
Consequently \(|N_2|=|N_3|=5^b\).

To determine the sign of \(R=N_2/N_3\), first note that \(Q(\varepsilon)=2\) since \(g\) is even and \(r\) is odd.
Thus \(S(\varepsilon)\mid2\), and the absence of a root at one modulo two makes it odd.
Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the primitive \(q\)-th roots \(z=-\varepsilon\bar x\) for \(\bar S(\bar x)=0\) form a multiset stable under inversion and Frobenius.
For each \(z\) let \(w\) be its unique square root in the group of \(q\)-th roots.
In the product of \(1+z=w(w+w^{-1})\) the factors \(w\) cancel by inversion.
The remaining product is the square of the product of \(w+w^{-1}\) over the unordered inverse pairs.
That latter product is Frobenius fixed and nonzero because \(q\) is odd, hence its square is one in \(\mathbb F_3\).
Since \(d\) is even this gives \(S(\varepsilon)=\pm1\) with residue \(\prod(1+z)=1\), so \(S(\varepsilon)=1\).

Conjugate pairs of nonreal roots contribute positively to each of \(N_2,N_3,S(\varepsilon)\).
For each inverse pair of real roots choose the root \(x\) with \(|x|>1\); \(Q\) has no root \(1,-1\).
Then \(y=x^g>1\), and the sign of the pair's contribution to \(R\) equals the sign of \((y-2)(y-3)\) since \(1/y<1\).
The root equation and \(A^*(y)>0\) identify this sign with \(-\varepsilon\operatorname{sgn}(x)\), the sign of \((\varepsilon-x)(\varepsilon-1/x)\).
Hence \(R\) has the sign of \(S(\varepsilon)\), and the absolute norm calculation gives \(R=1\).

<a id="quartic"></a>
## The product of the exceptional residues at five

The following residue computation applies to every monic rational divisor of \(Q\), with arbitrary \(\delta_2,\delta_3\), using \(R=N_2/N_3\) and \(b=\delta_5/2\).
In a five-adic splitting field pair every root of \(S\) with its inverse.
When \(\bar y\ne2,3\), the contribution to \(R\) reduces to
\[
           \frac{(y-2)(1-2y)}{(y-3)(1-3y)}
                       \equiv\frac{2}{3}=-1\pmod5 .
\]
For each exceptional pair choose \(x\) for which \(\bar y=2\).
Substitution of \(x^r A(y)=-\varepsilon A^*(y)\) gives its exact contribution as
\[
            -\varepsilon x^{-r}\left(\frac{1-2y}{y-3}\right)^2.
\]
The quotient in parentheses reduces to \(3\), so the contribution reduces to \(\varepsilon\bar x^{-r}\).
Define \(T\in\mathbb F_5^\times\) to be the product of the residues \(\bar x\) over these \(b\) choices, retaining multiplicities.
It lies in the base field by Frobenius stability and satisfies
\[
               \bar R=(-1)^{d/2-b}\varepsilon^b T^{-r}.                \tag{2}
\]

Write \(g=2^v u\) with \(u\) odd and \(v\ge1\).
Decompose \(\bar x\) into commuting parts of power-of-two and odd order and denote its power-of-two part by \(w\).
Since \(\bar x^g=2\) has order four, \(w\) has order \(2^{v+2}\) and \(w^{2^v}=2^{u^{-1}}\), where \(u^{-1}\) is the inverse of \(u\bmod4\).
In a Frobenius orbit of length \(f\), the divisibility \(2^{v+2}\mid 5^f-1\) implies \(2^v\mid f\), because successive squaring gives \(v_2(5^{2^i}-1)=i+2\) and an odd exponent preserves that valuation.
Therefore \(2^v\mid b\).
The product of \(\bar x\) on the orbit is \(\bar x^M=w^M\), where \(M=(5^f-1)/4\), since the norm of its odd-order part lies in \(\mathbb F_5^\times\) and has odd order.
Expanding \((1+4)^f\) gives
\[
                            M\equiv-f\pmod{2^{v+2}} .
\]
Indeed the first two terms \(f+4\binom f2=-f+2f^2\) give \(-f\) modulo \(2^{v+2}\), using \(v\ge1\).
For \(j\ge3\), the valuation of \(4^{j-1}\binom fj\) is at least \(2j-2+v-v_2(j)\ge v+2\), by \(j\binom fj=f\binom{f-1}{j-1}\) for \(j\le f\).
Multiplying the orbit products, with their multiplicities, now gives
\[
                          T=2^{-u^{-1} b/2^v}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5.
\]
In particular its quadratic character is \((-1)^{b/2^v}\), for all exceptional counts.
Return to the case \(\delta_2=\delta_3=0\), in which \(R=1\).
Since \(b\) is even and \(r\) odd, (2) gives \(T=(-1)^{d/2}=2^d\) by invertibility of the odd power map on the group of order four.
Consequently \(b/2^v\equiv-ud\equiv d\bmod4\), proving (1).
Finally \(N_2\equiv\prod(1+\bar x^g)=1\bmod3\) by the same inverse-pair argument applied to the primitive \(q\)-th roots \(\bar x^g\).
Since \(b\) is even and \(R=1\), both resultants equal \(+5^b\).

## Close split primes with large multiplicative orders

Fix odd \(h>8\) prime to five.
The [fixed-field density count](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#prime-pairs) gives \(\gg_h X/\log X\) split primes in \([X,2X]\) in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2h},2^{1/h},3^{1/h},5^{1/h})\).
Set \(L=\lfloor(\log X)^2\rfloor\).
For each \(a=2,3,5\), every prime with \(\operatorname{ord}_p(a)\le L\) divides the positive integer \(\prod_{j=1}^L(a^j-1)\), whose logarithm is \(O(L^2)\).
Discarding these \(O((\log X)^4)\) primes leaves \(\gg_h X/\log X\) choices, so two consecutive retained primes \(r<q\) have \(g=q-r=O_h(\log X)\).
Both primes are \(1\bmod 2h\), whence \(2h\mid g\); for sufficiently large \(X\) the size conditions on \(r\) hold and all three orders at both primes exceed \(2g+2\).
Splitting also makes \(2,3,5\) all \(h\)-th powers modulo both primes.

For a monic rational divisor of degree \(d_k=k(q-1)/h\), put
\[
    t_2=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(2)),\quad
    t_3=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(3)),\quad
    t_5=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_r(5)).
\]
These are the sizes of the [primitive grid orbits under Frobenius and inversion](Yin_C_G0345_v218.md#three).
The first two divide \((q-1)/h\), and \(t_5\mid(r-1)/h\), by the power residues and even quotients.
At two and three the grid degree is a multiple of \(t_\ell\), so \(\delta_\ell\equiv d_k\equiv0\bmod t_\ell\).
Since \(0\le\delta_\ell\le g<t_\ell\), both are zero.
At five the same reasoning gives \(\delta_5\equiv k g/h\bmod t_5\).
Both values lie in \([0,2g]\) and \(t_5>2g\), so \(\delta_5=k g/h\).
Thus \(b=k g/(2h)\); since \(u/h\) is odd, \(2^v\mid b\) requires \(k\) even.
Now \(4\mid d_k\) and (1) gives \(4\mid b/2^v=(k/2)(u/h)\), hence \(8\mid k\).

## Local divisors with numerators two, four, six and eight

Assume in addition \(39\mid h\).
Let \(p=q\) for \(\ell=2,3\) and \(p=r\) for \(\ell=5\), and put \(J_\ell=(p-1)/t_\ell\).
For \(k=2,4,6,8\) take \(kJ_\ell/h\) whole orbits in the corresponding primitive grid, possible since \(h\mid J_\ell\) and \(k<h\).
They give reciprocal factors with constant one, of degrees \(k(q-1)/h\) at two and three and \(k(r-1)/h\) at five.
Each primitive grid has only simple roots and is disjoint from its exceptional part.

At five set \(Z=X^{2g/h}\) and select the following monic reciprocal factor \(E_k(Z)\) of \(Z^h+1\):
\[
 E_2=Z+1,\qquad E_4=\Phi_6(Z),\qquad
 E_6=(Z+1)\Phi_6(Z),\qquad E_8=H(Z),
\]
where \(H\) is any monic irreducible factor of \(\Phi_{26}\) over \(\mathbb F_5\).
The last factor has degree four and is reciprocal since \(5^2\equiv-1\bmod26\) and \(5\) has order four modulo \(26\).
Each selected factor has constant term one, since its roots excluding \(-1\) occur in distinct inverse pairs and \(Z+1\) has constant term one.
All these factors divide \(Z^h+1\) because \(h\) is an odd multiple of \(3\) and \(13\), and each is coprime to the remaining factor since \(5\nmid h\).
After substitution, a Bezout identity still gives coprimality with the complement in \(X^{2g}+1\), including when \(5\mid g\).
The additional degree is \((k/2)(2g/h)=kg/h\), giving \(d_k\) at all three primes.
The [coprime lifting and reciprocity argument](Yin_C_G0345_v216.md#lifts) lifts these divisors with constant term one to \(\mathbb Z_\ell[X]\); reduction and the Chinese remainder theorem give a pair of monic reciprocal factors of \(Q\) modulo \(2^{a_2}3^{a_3}5^{a_5}\) for arbitrary positive exponents.
The first three local degrees are excluded over \(\mathbb Q\) by (1), whereas \(k=8\) satisfies (1).
