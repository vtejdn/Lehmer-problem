# A square-root width for uniform comparison of trace delays

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coefficient-independent comparison at the square-root scale

Let \(1\le d<n\), let \(b(t)\) be a real-valued trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\) and let \(\phi\in\mathbb R\).
Use normalized circle measure and the natural logarithm, and set
\[
 f(t)=2\cos(nt+\phi)-b(t),\qquad
 \mu=\int\log|f(t)|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi},\qquad
 I=\iint\log|2\cos v-b(t)|\,\frac{dv\,dt}{(2\pi)^2}.
\]
Let \(K\) be the number of distinct nonzero Fourier terms in \(f\), so \(2\le K\le2d+3\).
For an absolute constant \(C\),
\[
              |\mu-I|\le C\frac d n
                      \left(K+\log(2n/d)\right).                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\mu-I\to0\) uniformly over coefficients and phases if \(d=o(\sqrt n)\).
The [endpoint Taylor example](Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#statement) has a strictly positive lower limit of \(I-\mu\) already at \(d=\lfloor5\sqrt n\rfloor\), and gives a discrepancy of order at least \(d^2/n\) when \(d/\sqrt n\to\infty\).
Consequently the square-root order of the general coefficient-independent convergence window is sharp.
For these Taylor examples with \(1\le d=o(n)\), their logarithmic Mahler measures grow as \(2d\log(n/d)\).

When \(\|b'\|_\infty\le n/4\) and \(\|b''\|_\infty\le n^2/4\), the \(K\) term can be omitted in (1).
Thus in this class the comparison converges for every \(d=o(n)\).
It suffices that \(d\|b\|_\infty\le n/4\), by two applications of [Bernstein's trigonometric derivative inequality](../audits/literature/QZ19/Yin_U001.md#qz19-u001) (a self-contained bound with a different absolute constant is given below).

For \(b(t)=B(2\cos t)\), \(B\in\mathbb Z[X]\), and \(\phi=0\), let \(P(z)=z^n(C_n-B)(z+z^{-1})\), with \(C_n(z+z^{-1})=z^n+z^{-n}\).
There are absolute constants \(c,\eta>0\) such that every sufficiently large \(n\) and every noncyclotomic \(P\) with \(d\le c\sqrt n\) satisfy \(\log M(P)\ge\eta\).
There is also a uniform positive lower bound along noncyclotomic sequences with \(d=o(n)\) satisfying the displayed derivative bounds.
The statements apply to the whole polynomial and allow reducibility and multiple roots.
For these inputs \(I\) is the average of \(\operatorname{arcosh}(|b|/2)\) on \(|b|>2\), with zero contribution elsewhere.

<a id="quadrature"></a>
## Clipped logarithms and variation of the slow variable

Fix \(0<u<1\), and replace each \(\log|s|\) by \(\log\max(u,|s|)\) to obtain \(\mu_u,I_u\).
For \(a\in[-2,2]\) consider
\[
                 L_a(x)=\log\frac{\max(u,|a-x|)}{2+|x|}.
\]
Its range is contained in \([\log(u/6),0]\): use the denominator bound for \(|x|\le4\), and \((|x|-2)/(2+|x|)\ge1/3\) otherwise.
It has a bounded absolute number of monotone pieces, by splitting at zero and \(a\pm u\); on the outer pieces the ratio is a quotient of affine functions.
A real trigonometric polynomial of degree \(d\) has at most \(2d\) critical points unless it is constant.
Thus the variation of \(L_a(b(t))\) is \(O(d\log(6/u))\), uniformly in \(a,b\).
A shifted mesh of \(n\) equally spaced points estimates the normalized mean with error at most the variation divided by \(n\), by integrating over each mesh interval.
Conditioning on \(v=nt+\phi\bmod2\pi\) gives exactly this shifted mesh in \(t\).
The subtracted term \(\log(2+|b(t)|)\) has the same mean in both integrals.
Consequently
\[
                          |\mu_u-I_u|\ll d\log(6/u)/n .          \tag{2}
\]
For every real \(x\) and \(0<v\le1\), the normalized length where \(|2\cos t-x|\le v\) is \(O(\sqrt v)\).
Indeed the cosine is monotone on each half-period, and integrating the arcsine density over any interval of length \(2v\) gives that bound at its square-root endpoints.
Integration against \(dv/v\) gives \(0\le I_u-I\ll\sqrt u\).

<a id="periods"></a>
## How many periods can contain fast changes of the slow term?

Divide the circle into periods of length \(2\pi/n\), starting at a maximum of the fast cosine.
For nonconstant \(b\), flag a period if it meets \(b=3\), \(b=-3\), or one of the following subsets:
\[
 \{|b|\le3,\ |b'|>n/4\},\qquad
 \{|b|\le3,\ |b'|\le n/4,\ |b''|>n^2/4\}.
\]
There are \(O(d)\) flagged periods.
To see this, the threshold equations for \(b,b',b''\) partition the circle into \(O(d)\) intervals.
Each derivative level used here is nonzero, and an identically constant \(b\) may be treated directly.
On each component of the first set the derivative has constant sign, and \(b\) varies through at most six units.
Its length is at most \(24/n\).
On a component of the second set, \(b''\) has constant sign, and \(b'\) varies by at most \(n/2\), giving length at most \(2/n\).
Isolated threshold points add only \(O(d)\) period intersections.
An interval of length \(L\) meets at most \(2+nL/(2\pi)\) periods, up to a harmless endpoint convention.
This proves the count without a bound on the coefficients.

On each unflagged period either \(|b|>3\) throughout, or the first two derivative bounds hold throughout.
In the former case \(|f|>1\).
In the latter case rescale the period to length \(2\pi\).
The rescaled slow term has first and second derivatives bounded by \(1/4\).
Where \(|\sin|\ge1/2\), the first derivative of the difference has absolute value at least \(3/4\) and constant sign on each connected piece.
On the remaining intervals its second derivative has constant sign and absolute value at least \(\sqrt3-1/4\).
On an interval where \(|h''|\ge1\) with constant sign, the length of \(|h|\le v\) is \(O(\sqrt v)\): the portion where \(|h'|\le\sqrt v\) has length at most \(2\sqrt v\), and each of the other two monotone portions has length at most \(2\sqrt v\).
The first-derivative pieces cost \(O(v)\).
There are a bounded number of pieces per period, so the relative measure of \(|f|\le v\) is \(O(\sqrt v)\) for \(0<v\le1\) on every unflagged period.
Also \(\sup|f|\ge1\) there, since values of the fast cosine term differ by four between its maximum and minimum whereas the slow term changes by at most \(\pi/4\) in that half-period.

<a id="runs"></a>
## The cost of an exceptional interval

When there are any unflagged periods, adjoin one neighboring unflagged period to each maximal consecutive run of flagged periods, working with a periodic lift at the endpoints if necessary.
The resulting intervals have total length \(O(d/n)\), measured in radians, and on each interval \(J\) one has \(\sup_J|f|\ge1\).
If all periods are flagged, then \(n=O(d)\) and the full circle supplies one such interval: its supremum is at least one by the Fourier coefficient at \(n\).
Empty exceptional sets contribute zero.

For the \(K\) distinct imaginary frequencies of \(f\), the [measurable-set Turán–Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) on \(J\) gives
\[
                |\{t\in J:|f(t)|\le v\}|/|J|
                           \le C_0 v^{1/(K-1)},\qquad 0<v\le1 .
\]
The constant is absolute because the exponential factor in that inequality is one and the supremum on \(J\) is at least one.
Integrating with \(dv/v\) up to \(u\) bounds the negative logarithmic truncation loss by \(O(K)|J|\), even at a multiple zero.
The unflagged periods instead cost a total of \(O(\sqrt u)\) by the derivative estimates.
All integrands for these losses are nonnegative, so any overlap of enlarged runs is harmless for this bound.
Together with (2) this yields
\[
             |\mu-I|\ll \sqrt u+
                       \frac{d\log(6/u)}{n}+\frac{dK}{n}.
\]
Set \(u=(d/n)^2\).
If the derivative bounds hold everywhere, use the rescaled sublevel proof on every period, omitting the flag construction and the last term completely.
For constant \(b\) the two means agree by periodic substitution.

<a id="height"></a>
## The amplitude condition and integral examples

For a self-contained amplitude estimate, \(p(z)=z^d b(t)\) on \(z=e^{it}\) extends to a polynomial of degree at most \(2d\).
The maximum modulus principle inside the circle and on the reversed polynomial gives \(|b(t+is)|\le e^{d|s|}\|b\|_\infty\).
Cauchy's formula on a complex \(t\)-disk of radius \(1/d\) implies
\[
              \|b'\|_\infty\le ed\|b\|_\infty,\qquad
              \|b''\|_\infty\le 2ed^2\|b\|_\infty .
\]
Hence \(d\|b\|_\infty\le n/(8e)\) is sufficient for both derivative bounds as \(d<n\).

The [trace-quadratic height bound](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(I=0\) or \(I\ge c_2>0\) for the integral symmetric Laurent polynomial here.
The zero cases are integer constants of absolute value at most two and \(B=\pm C_j\).
In the constant cases \(z^{2n}-Bz^n+1\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials, by solving the quadratic for \(z^n\).
For \(+C_j\) the polynomial factors as \((z^{n-j}-1)(z^{n+j}-1)\); for \(-C_j\) both signs in these binomials are plus.
Thus for every noncyclotomic input \(I\ge c_2\).
In (1), \(dK/n=O(d^2/n+d/n)\), and \(d\log(2n/d)/n\to0\) uniformly for \(d\le c\sqrt n\).
Take \(c\) sufficiently small and then \(n\) sufficiently large to obtain \(\mu\ge c_2/2\).

The larger derivative-controlled window permits dense integral central polynomials.
For example start with \(A_0=B_0=1\) and put
\[
 A_{j+1}=A_j+z^{2^j}B_j,\qquad B_{j+1}=A_j-z^{2^j}B_j.
\]
The two polynomials have coefficients of absolute value one throughout the exponents \(0,\ldots,2^j-1\) at stage \(j\); splitting into disjoint halves proves this by induction.
Their squared moduli on the unit circle sum to \(2^{j+1}\), since each recursion doubles that sum.
For \(d=2^j\), take \(b(t)=zA_j(z)+z^{-1}A_j(z^{-1})\).
It is a polynomial in \(2\cos t\) with integral trace coefficients, has all \(2d\) nonzero Laurent terms, and has \(\|b\|_\infty\le2\sqrt{2d}\).
For all sufficiently large \(j\), taking \(n\ge16e\sqrt2\,d^{3/2}\) ensures the derivative condition, uniform convergence of the comparison, and a uniform positive whole-polynomial gap because \(I\ge c_2\) and \(d/n\to0\).
This furnishes examples at width of order \(n^{2/3}\).
