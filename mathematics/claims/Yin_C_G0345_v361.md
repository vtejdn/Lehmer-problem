# An effective two-class cofactor rate from nonreal exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree-dependent threshold without a fixed-field error term

Write \(m=\log M\).
Fix \(L>0\), and let \(P\) range over the monic minimal polynomials of nontorsion reciprocal algebraic units with \(m(P)\le L\).
Suppose that at each modulus \(\rho>1\) of an exterior conjugate, the conjugates of that modulus consist of exactly one nonreal complex-conjugate pair.
There is no condition on neutral conjugates.
Put \(d=\deg P,\ B=2e^{2L}\), and for an odd prime \(p\) take \(b=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor\).
If
\[
                               B^{4d^4}\ \le\ p,                          \tag{1}
\]
then, as \(p\to\infty\), uniformly for these **varying** inputs,
\[
 \left|\frac{\min_F m(R)}{p\,m(P)}-\frac12\right|
                               \ \ll_L\ p^{-1/4}.                         \tag{2}
\]
Here \(F\) runs over nonzero integer multiples of \(P\) occupying exactly the adjacent exponent classes \(0,1\bmod p\) with \(\deg F\le p(b+2)\).
The product \(R\) consists of the primitive noncyclotomic irreducible factors of \(F/P\), with multiplicities, omitting \(x\) and integer content.
The same estimate holds with the additional requirement that the two rows be coprime in the rational Laurent ring.
The bounds below give an alternative finite test using measured radii and angles in place of the coarse degree threshold (1).

<a id="envelope"></a>
## Breakpoints forced by a nonreal conjugate

Take a primitive two-row multiple written as
\[
      F(x)=\sum_j x^{pj}(a_j+x^s b_j),
           \qquad 1\le s<p,\quad a_j,b_j\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Discard indices at which both coefficients vanish, let \(h_j=\max(|a_j|,|b_j|)\), and set
\[
                A(t)=\max_j(\log h_j+jt),\qquad H=\max_j h_j.
\]
For any nonreal exterior conjugate \(\gamma\) with \(w=\gamma^s\notin\mathbb R\), put
\[
       t_\gamma=p\log|\gamma|,\qquad
       K_\gamma=\log\left(1+\frac{2(1+|w|)^2}{|\Im w|}\right).
\]
Then \(A\) has a breakpoint in \([t_\gamma-K_\gamma,t_\gamma+K_\gamma]\).
Each real pair \(a,b\) satisfies
\[
    \frac{|\Im w|}{1+|w|}\max(|a|,|b|)
      \ \le\ |a+bw|\ \le\ (1+|w|)\max(|a|,|b|).
\]
For the lower bound, the imaginary part recovers \(b\) and the imaginary part after multiplication by \(\bar w\) recovers \(a\).
The upper envelope \(A\) is thus the same for all sampled conjugates even though their scalar coefficients \(a_j+b_jw\) vary.

Indeed if the distance from \(t_\gamma\) to every breakpoint exceeds \(K_\gamma\), one index \(j_0\) maximizes \(A\) throughout a closed interval of radius \(K>K_\gamma\) centered at \(t_\gamma\).
At its endpoints the affine forms give
\[
     h_j e^{jt_\gamma}\le h_{j_0}e^{j_0t_\gamma} e^{-|j-j_0|K}.
\]
The sum over both tails is at most \(2/(e^K-1)\) times the dominant term before applying the coefficient comparisons.
After those comparisons the \(j_0\) term of \(F(\gamma)\) strictly dominates the sum of all others in absolute value.
This contradicts \(F(\gamma)=0\), including if there is only one index.
The scalar one-variable localization and its two geometric tails occur in [Avendaño, Kogan, Nisse and Rojas](../audits/literature/AKNR18/Yin_U001.md#tail).
Here the comparisons with the common numbers \(h_j\) allow different radii to be counted against one envelope.

<a id="sum"></a>
## Adding the costs at separated radii

Take one such \(\gamma\) from each of any collection of radii for which the displayed intervals are disjoint and contained in \((0,\infty)\).
Each resulting breakpoint \(u>0\) increases the slope of \(A\) by a positive integer \(e_u\).
If \(j_{\max}\) is the largest occupied base index, then
\[
               \log H-\log h_{j_{\max}}=\sum_{u>0} e_u u .
\]
This follows by integrating \(j_{\max}-A'(t)\) from zero to beyond the last positive breakpoint and using the terminal affine form.
As \(h_{j_{\max}}\ge1\), every \(T\)-term witness satisfies the finite estimate
\[
             m(R)\ \ge\ \sum_\gamma
                   (p\log|\gamma|-K_\gamma)-(T-2)\log2-m(P).             \tag{3}
\]
Here \(T\ge2\) and the [sparse coefficient estimate](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(\log H\le m(F)+(T-2)\log2\).
Dividing out the scalar content initially preserves the roots and \(R\), so \(m(F)=m(P)+m(R)\) at this step.
Neither row primitivity nor monicity of \(F\) enters (3).
If the geometric intervals overlap, (3) can still use any disjoint subcollection.

<a id="effective"></a>
## Bounds on separation in terms of the original degree

Take \(s=1\).
All conjugates have modulus at most \(e^L\).
For any two distinct values \(z,z'\) in the set of squared exterior radii together with one, \(z-z'\) is a nonzero algebraic integer of degree at most \(d^4\).
Indeed each squared radius is a product of two conjugates, and the composite generated by at most four conjugates has degree at most \(d^4\).
Every conjugate of the difference has absolute value at most \(B\).
The integral norm gives \(|z-z'|\ge B^{1-d^4}\).
The mean-value theorem then gives a separation of at least \(B^{-d^4}\) between the distinct logarithmic radii, including zero.
Moreover \(\gamma-\bar\gamma\) is a nonzero algebraic integer of degree at most \(d^2\) with all conjugates of absolute value at most \(2e^L\).
It follows that every \(K_\gamma\) is at most
\[
             K_*=\log\bigl(1+4(1+e^L)^2(2e^L)^{d^2}\bigr).
\]
Thus \(p B^{-d^4}>2K_*\) suffices for (3) with all exterior radii.
There are at most \(d/2\) of them, their log radii sum to \(m(P)/2\), and \(m(P)\ge 2 B^{-d^4}\).

Under (1), \(d=O_L((\log p)^{1/4})\), \(K_*=O_L(1+d^2)\) and \(pB^{-d^4}\ge p^{3/4}\).
Hence the separation test holds for all sufficiently large \(p\), uniformly.
There are at most \(2(b+3)\) terms under the degree cap and \(p\,m(P)\ge2p^{3/4}\).
Dividing (3) by \(p\,m(P)\) gives the lower half of (2) with the stated error.

<a id="upper"></a>
## Uniform construction in the same range

The [integer box argument](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#upper) is finite and uses only \(d,b,p,s,m(P)\).
With \(n=2(b+1)>d,\ s=1\), it yields a nonzero multiple with rows supported in \(0,1\) and
\[
              m(F_0)\le\frac{(pb+1)m(P)+d\log(6n)}{n-d}+\log(2n).
\]
The growth bounds following (1) give \(m(F_0)/(p\,m(P))\le 1/2+O_L(p^{-1/4})\) by division.
For large \(p\) this is strictly less than one.
Distinct conjugates of \(P\) have distinct \(p\)-th powers in this range: a quotient of two of them lies in a field of degree at most \(d^2\), whereas a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity has degree \(p-1>d^2\).
Thus the powered minimal polynomial has measure \(p\,m(P)\) in logarithmic units.
The [common-row argument](Yin_C_G0345_v358.md#primitive) excludes a missing row or a common base factor carrying \(P\), and removing the integral common gcd gives the required coprime witness without increasing degree or measure.
Its degree is at most \(pb+1\).
Finite-degree attainment follows from the same coefficient bound for bounded-degree products \(R\) of bounded measure as in that argument.
This proves (2) for both witness classes.

For example, if \(S\) is a Salem minimal polynomial of degree at least four and \(\lambda>1\) its real exterior root, then \(S(-x^2)\) is irreducible: \(-\lambda\) is negative in the given real embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) and cannot be a square there.
A root \(\sqrt{-\lambda}\) therefore has degree \(2\deg S\).
The only exterior roots are \(i\sqrt\lambda,-i\sqrt\lambda\), and \(m(S(-x^2))=m(S)\).
This gives genuine reciprocal units in the class above whenever \(m(S)\le L\).
The rate in (2) remains proportional to the original measure, whose uniform positive lower bound is open.
