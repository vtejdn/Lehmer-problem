# Simultaneous residue collisions in the index of a Salem trace generator

<a id="statement"></a>
## A joint budget for primes, residue degrees and ramification indices

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(t)\) for an algebraic integer \(t\), let \(Q\) be its monic minimal polynomial, \(s=\deg Q\) and \(I=[\mathcal O_F:\mathbb Z[t]]\).
For an integer \(q\ge2\) and \(c\ge0\) integral, write \(c=aq+r\), \(0\le r<q\), and set
\[
                B_q(c)=q a(a-1)+2ar\ \ge\ (c^2/q-c)_+, \qquad
                                      x_+=\max(x,0).
\]
At each of any finite set of rational primes \(p\), choose disjoint sets \(S_{p,i}\) of places of \(F\) over \(p\).
For each set choose \(k_{p,i}\ge1\) divisible by the residue degree \(f_v\) of every member.
For each \(j\ge1\) define
\[
                c_{p,i,j}=\sum_{\substack{v\in S_{p,i}\\e_v\ge j}} f_v
\]
using the actual local ramification index \(e_v\).
Then the single global inequality
\[
           \sum_{p,i}\sum_{j\ge1} B_{p^{k_{p,i}}}(c_{p,i,j})\log p
                         \ \le\ 2\log I                                \tag{1}
\]
holds without assumptions on the local orders or the ramification.
For example one may use all places with \(f_v\mid k_p\) as a single set, or use all the disjoint sets of common exact residue degree \(f\) with \(k_{p,i}=f\).
Only primes dividing the index can make positive contributions.

For a Salem number \(\alpha>1\) with minimal polynomial \(P\), \(d=\deg P=2s\), \(m=\log\alpha\), apply (1) with \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
The budget satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 2\log I
 &=\tfrac12\log|\operatorname{disc}P|
       -\tfrac12\log|Q(2)Q(-2)|-\log|\operatorname{disc} F|\\
 &\le s\log d+(d-1)m
       -\tfrac12\log|Q(2)Q(-2)|-\log|\operatorname{disc} F| .          \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every layer of excess occupation at every test prime competes for the same polynomial index, even when the trace order is not maximal.

<a id="local"></a>
## Factoring the trace order after unramified base extension

Fix \(p\) and use \(v_p(p)=1\) on \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\).
Choose a finite unramified extension \(U/\mathbb Q_p\) whose residue degree is a common multiple of all the \(f_v\) and all the \(k_{p,i}\) in use.
Over \(U\), the conjugates of \(t\) split into sets indexed by pairs \(\lambda=(v,\iota)\), where \(\iota\) embeds the residue field at \(v\) into that of \(U\).
There are \(f_v\) such sets of size \(e_v\) at \(v\).
Indeed, the maximal unramified subfield \(W_v\) of \(F_v\) has degree \(f_v\) and splits under tensoring with \(U\) into one copy of \(U\) for each residue embedding.
Its integer algebra likewise splits over \(\mathcal O_U\), by lifting a residue-field generator with separable irreducible reduction and applying the Chinese remainder theorem to its distinct lifted factors.
The remaining totally ramified extension has degree \(e_v\); a local uniformizer has an Eisenstein polynomial over \(W_v\) which stays Eisenstein over \(U\) at each embedding.
Thus each resulting field component has degree \(e_v\) over \(U\), and its projection idempotent belongs to \(\mathcal O_F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_U\).

Write \(Q_\lambda\in\mathcal O_U[X]\) for the monic polynomial of the \(e_v\) images of \(t\) in component \(\lambda\).
The original primitive element still generates the entire product as a \(U\)-algebra since \(F=\mathbb Q[t]\), so the component polynomials are pairwise coprime and their product is \(Q\).
The intermediate order \(\prod_\lambda\mathcal O_U[X]/(Q_\lambda)\) lies between the diagonal power-basis order \(\mathcal O_U[X]/(Q)\) and \(\mathcal O_F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_U\), by the integral projection idempotents.
The valuation of the index of the first order in this product is
\[
               \sum_{\lambda<\mu}v_p(\operatorname{Res}(Q_\lambda,Q_\mu)).
\]
Indeed the polynomial discriminant of a product is the product of the component discriminants times the squares of all cross resultants.
The product order has the product discriminant, and a finite-index change of bases multiplies the discriminant by the square of its determinant.
Since \(U\) is unramified, extending any integral pair of bases for the original inclusion to \(\mathcal O_U\) preserves its index valuation \(v_p(I)\).
Thus the sum of all cross-resultant valuations is at most \(v_p(I)\).
This includes components from different residue embeddings of the same place.

Each component at \(v\) has a single residue value for \(t\).
All its \(e_v\) images are within valuation distance \(v_p(t'-z)\ge1/e_v\) of the same representative \(z\) of that residue satisfying \(z^{p^{f_v}}=z\).
This representative belongs to the embedded maximal unramified subfield, by the unique simple-root lift in that residue class.
If two components \(\lambda,\mu\), at places \(v,w\), have the same residue value, their representatives coincide by uniqueness of the lift in the common unramified field (using a common multiple of the two residue degrees).
Consequently each of their \(e_v e_w\) cross differences has valuation at least \(\min(1/e_v,1/e_w)\), and the resultant valuation is at least \(\min(e_v,e_w)\).

Inside \(S_{p,i}\), all residue values belong to the \(q=p^{k_{p,i}}\) element field.
At level \(j\) count only the components with \(e_v\ge j\).
If \(n_z\) counts those with residue value \(z\), then \(\sum_z n_z=c_{p,i,j}\).
Each pair sharing a residue value occurs on exactly \(\min(e_v,e_w)\) levels.
Therefore the sum of \(\sum_z n_z(n_z-1)\) over all levels and disjoint sets is at most twice the sum of the cross-resultant valuations, hence at most \(2v_p(I)\).
Among occupations of \(q\) bins by \(c=aq+r\) objects the minimum is attained with \(r\) bins of size \(a+1\) and the remaining bins of size \(a\): moving an object from a bin of size at least two larger than another decreases the sum.
The minimum equals \(B_q(c)\); Cauchy–Schwarz and positivity also give the displayed real-valued lower bound.
Multiplication by \(\log p\) and summation prove (1).

## The trace discriminant and the scope of the coset interpretation

Write \(t_1,\ldots,t_s\) for the distinct conjugates of \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
The conjugate inverse pairs give \(P(X)=\prod_i(X^2-t_i X+1)\).
For \(i\ne j\) the resultant of two of these quadratics is \((t_i-t_j)^2\), by evaluating their difference \((t_i-t_j)X\) at the two roots of the first quadratic, whose product is one.
The within-pair discriminants are \(t_i^2-4\), whose product is \(Q(2)Q(-2)\).
Hence \(|\operatorname{disc}P|=|Q(2)Q(-2)|\,|\operatorname{disc}Q|^2\).
The endpoint factor is a nonzero integer because no Salem conjugate is \(1\) or \(-1\).
Taking \(|\operatorname{disc}Q|=I^2|\operatorname{disc}F|\) gives (2) by applying the Vandermonde row bound \(\sqrt d\max(1,|\alpha_i|)^{d-1}\) at each root of \(P\) and squaring the product.
In particular \(\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|\le s\log d+(d-1)m-\tfrac12\log|Q(2)Q(-2)|\).

For one test power \(q=p^k\) take the single set \(f_v\mid k\) at \(p\), and write \(c=c_{p,1,1}\) for the first level.
The [identity-coset formula](Yin_C_G0345_v731.md#powers) identifies \(A=c/s\) with the probability that a uniform lift of the residue \(q\)-power map at a uniformly selected prime of the normal closure lies in the subgroup fixing \(F\).
At \(p=2\) the same count holds: conditionally on a prime restricting to \(v\), the subgroup has lift probability \(1/e_v\) when \(f_v\mid k\) and zero otherwise, and that restriction occurs with probability \(e_vf_v/s\).
Formula (1) gives \(B_q(sA)\log p\le2\log I\) as well as the simultaneous constraints for all chosen primes and layers.
If the Galois action on the \(s>1\) embeddings of \(F\) is doubly transitive, conjugation invariance makes every other coset probability equal to \((1-A)/(s-1)\), as in the [coset orbit calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v728.md#weights).
Hence in this case the maximum is exactly \(\max(A,(1-A)/(s-1))\).
For general actions (1) directly restricts the identity coset.

The [height bound on every coset](Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#cosets) already forces the largest probability to tend to zero for all Salem trace fields at bounded \(m\) and \(q=o(s)\) as \(s\to\infty\).
Indeed \((q+1)h(\alpha)\to0\) since \(h(\alpha)=m/(2s)\); choosing \(n\to\infty\) slowly in that bound makes both terms of \(U_n((q+1)h(\alpha))\) vanish and \(\log p\ge\log2\).
The index bound records the combined cost of the actual local factorizations, including residue embeddings of ramified places and multiple disjoint residue-degree ranges at the same prime.
It supplies no positive lower bound for those coset probabilities on an arbitrary shrinking sequence.
