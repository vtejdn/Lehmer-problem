# Counts of primitive labels in a cyclic subfield and irreducibility at every inner exponent

<a id="statement"></a>
## Rational factors constrained by the class of a split prime

Let \(q>3\) be prime, \(f=\operatorname{ord}_q(-2)\), \(h=(q-1)/f\), and \(0<l<q\).
Write
\[
 P_l=X^{2q}-2X^{q+l}+X^q-2X^{q-l}+1
     =X^q W(X+X^{-1}),\qquad W=C_q+1-2C_l ,
\]
where \(C_j(x+x^{-1})=x^j+x^{-j}\).
Let \(r\mid h\) be prime and \(K\) the subfield of degree \(r\) in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3q})^+\).
Two splits completely in \(K\).
Fix a prime \(\mathfrak p\mid2\) of \(K\), let \(z=f[\mathfrak p]\) in its ordinary ideal class group written additively, and set
\[
 B_r=\prod_{\substack{\ell^v\parallel\operatorname{ord}(z)\\
                  \Phi_r\bmod\ell\ {\rm irreducible}}}\ell^v .
\]
Index the \(r\) primes by powers of a Galois generator starting at \(\mathfrak p\).
For any monic rational divisor of \(W\), let \(a_i\) count its primitive binary trace orbits above the \(i\)-th prime.
Each count lies between zero and \(h/r\).
Then
\[
                 a_0\equiv a_1\equiv\cdots\equiv a_{r-1}\pmod {B_r}.       \tag{1}
\]
A factor containing any primitive label contains at least \(\min(r,B_r)\) of them, and if \(B_r>h/r\) the counts are equal as integers.
In particular, in the latter case a reciprocal irreducible nontorsion factor \(S\) carrying a primitive label has
\[
                   m(S)\ \ge\ \frac{(q-1)r}{qh}\frac{\log 2}{3}.
\]
Indeed its degree is at least \(2fr\) and the [full binary period](Yin_C_G0345_v258.md#five) gives \(m(S)\ge(\deg S)\log2/(6q)\).

If \(h=r\) is prime, \(B_r>1\) and \(q\ge29\), then for **every** \(0<l<q\),
\[
             P_l=\Phi_6^{\,e} S_l,\qquad
             e=\begin{cases}1&\gcd(l,6)=1,\\0&\gcd(l,6)>1,\end{cases}     \tag{2}
\]
where \(S_l\) is irreducible of degree \(2q-2e\).
The four primes \(107,587,1297,2153\) satisfy these hypotheses.
The factorization ranges over all offsets even for the last two primes, where a single primitive binary trace orbit consists of two ordinary Frobenius orbits on roots.

<a id="module"></a>
## Action of the Galois generator on the dyadic class

The [valuation of the sampled trace](Yin_C_G0345_v259.md#ideals) identifies the \(h\) labels with the primes over two in the real cyclotomic field and computes the residue degree \(f\).
Its cyclic Galois group of order \(q-1\) has decomposition subgroup of order \(f\).
Since \(r\mid h\), the subgroup fixing \(K\) contains that decomposition subgroup.
There are \(h/r\) labels over each of the \(r\) primes of \(K\).
For a monic rational trace divisor \(V\mid W\) apply that valuation calculation to its monic rational reciprocal lift \(X^{\deg V}V(X+X^{-1})\mid P_l\).
With \(\sigma\) a Galois generator of \(K\), principality gives \(A(\sigma)z=0\) for \(A=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_i X^i\).
Also \(\Phi_r(\sigma)z=0\) because the product of the conjugate primes of \(K\) is \((2)\).

For each \(\ell^v\) in the product defining \(B_r\), multiply \(z\) by its order divided by \(\ell^v\) to obtain \(x\) of order exactly \(\ell^v\), with the same two annihilation relations.
If \(v=1\), the annihilator of \(x\) in \(\mathbb F_\ell[X]\) contains the irreducible polynomial \(\Phi_r\) and is a proper ideal, so it equals \((\Phi_r)\).
As \(A\) has degree at most \(r-1\), all its coefficients agree modulo \(\ell\).
For \(v>1\) apply this argument to \(\ell^{v-1}x\), subtract an integer multiple of \(\Phi_r\) from \(A\) and divide all coefficients by \(\ell\).
The resulting polynomial annihilates \(\ell x\) of order \(\ell^{v-1}\), so induction gives equality of the original coefficients modulo \(\ell^v\).
This proves (1).
When the common residue modulo \(B_r\) is positive, all \(r\) counts are positive.
When it is zero, any positive count is at least \(B_r\).
The capacity bound gives the assertion about equality as integers.

## From a single trace factor to a single reciprocal factor

When \(h=r\) and \(B_r>1\), the counts are either all zero or all one.
The reduction of \(W\) is squarefree, since \(P_l\bmod2=(X^{3q}-1)/(X^q-1)\) is squarefree.
Exactly one monic rational irreducible trace factor \(V\) takes primitive labels, and it takes all of them.
There is just one more binary trace root, \(Y=1\) coming from order three.
Consequently any other rational irreducible factor is linear with odd integral root \(y\).

We exclude such \(y\) with \(|y|\ge3\).
One solution of \(x+x^{-1}=y\) would have \(|x|\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Set \(k=q-l\).
If \(k\ge2\), at an exterior root the leading term of \(P_l\) bounds \(|x|^k\le6\) by the triangle inequality applied to the remaining terms of degrees at most \(2q-k\).
If \(k=1\), then \(l\) is even and \(x^q+x^{-q}+1-2(x^l+x^{-l})<0\) for \(x<-1\).
For \(x\ge2\), the leading pair \(x^{2q}-2x^{2q-1}\) is nonnegative and \(x^q-2x+1>0\).
Thus only \(y=\pm1\) are possible.
Evaluation gives \(W(-1)=-2C_l(-1)\in\{2,-4\}\) and \(W(1)=2-2C_l(1)\), which vanishes exactly for \(\gcd(l,6)=1\).
The root is simple by squarefreeness, so \(W=(Y-1)^e V\).

For a root \(\beta\) of \(V\), its reciprocal lift is irreducible exactly when \(\beta^2-4\) is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(\beta)\), by the quadratic equation over that field.
For \(e=0\) the rational norm is \(W(2)W(-2)\), equal to \(5\) when \(l\) is even and \(-3\) when \(l\) is odd.
Neither is a rational square.
For \(e=1\) it suffices to find a real conjugate of \(\beta\) in \((-2,2)\).
At \(\theta=j\pi/l\), \(0\le j\le l\), the sign of \(W(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(q\theta)+1-4\cos(l\theta)\) is negative for even \(j\) and positive for odd \(j\).
For \(l>1\) this supplies at least two distinct roots in \((-2,2)\), of which at most one is \(1\).
For \(l=1\), the interval \([q/3,q/2]\) contains two consecutive integers when \(q\ge29\).
At their corresponding angles \(j\pi/q\), the first term alternates between \(2\) and \(-2\), and \(1-4\cos\theta\in[-1,1]\).
The sign change gives a root strictly between the two angles, hence distinct from the one at \(\pi/3\).
The real embedding at the resulting root of \(V\) excludes a square, giving (2).

## Four quadratic fields with two labels

In the table \(D\) is the squarefree radicand of \(K\), \(c\) is the exact order of a dyadic prime class and \(U>1\) is a real unit.
The [quadratic subfield calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v259.md#ideals) gives \(D=q\) or \(3q\) according to whether \(q\equiv1\) or \(3\bmod4\).

| \(q\) | \(f\) | \(D\) | \(c\) | Element of norm \(-2^c\) | \(U\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 107 | 53 | 321 | 3 | \((17+\sqrt{321})/2\) | \(215+12\sqrt{321}\) |
| 587 | 293 | 1761 | 7 | \((377+9\sqrt{1761})/2\) | \(1175+28\sqrt{1761}\) |
| 1297 | 648 | 1297 | 11 | \((59+3\sqrt{1297})/2\) | \(36+\sqrt{1297}\) |
| 2153 | 1076 | 2153 | 5 | \((45+\sqrt{2153})/2\) | \(232+5\sqrt{2153}\) |

Primality of the four \(q\) and of \(53,293,269\) follows by division by the primes up to their square roots.
In the first two rows \(-2\) is a nonidentity square as \(q\equiv3\bmod8\) and \((q-1)/2\) is prime.
In the last two rows repeated squaring gives \((-2)^f=1\bmod q\), and at exponents \(f/p\) for the distinct primes \(p\mid f\) in increasing order the residues are respectively \(1296,931\) and \(2152,16\).
Thus \(h=2\) in every row.
The norm and unit identities follow by multiplication.
The units have norms \(1,1,-1,-1\) in order.
In each displayed element the coefficient of \((1+\sqrt D)/2\) is odd, so its ideal is the \(c\)-th power of just one prime over two.

It remains to rule out norm \(\pm2\), since all four proposed orders are prime.
Multiplying a hypothetical element by a power of \(U\), arrange \(\sqrt{2/U}\le|\alpha|\le\sqrt{2U}\).
Writing \(\alpha=(x+y\sqrt D)/2\) as an algebraic integer, the difference of its two real embeddings gives
\[
            y^2\le 2(U+1/U+2)/D .
\]
The right side is less than four in the first two rows, and less than one in the last two.
The case \(y=0\) gives an integral square norm.
For \(|y|=1\) in the first two rows, none of \(313,329,1753,1769\) is a square.
Hence the dyadic class orders are as claimed.
Each \(c\) is coprime to \(f\) and the linear \(\Phi_2\) is irreducible modulo every prime, so \(B_2=c>1\).
