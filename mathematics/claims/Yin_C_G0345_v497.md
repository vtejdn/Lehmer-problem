# The mixed-power resultant and the common divisor with the two power-order indices

<a id="statement"></a>
## A fourth-power divisor with sublinear logarithmic loss

Fix nontorsion algebraic units with monic minimal polynomials \(P,Q\) of degrees \(e,f\), respectively.
Write \(a_1,\ldots,a_e\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_f\) for the conjugates, allowing \(P=Q\).
For positive integers \(p,q\), assume the two sets of powers \(\{a_i^p\}\) and \(\{b_j^q\}\) have their full sizes \(e,f\) and are disjoint.
Put
\[
  C=\prod_{i,j}(a_i^p-b_j^q),\qquad
  I_p=[\mathbb Z[a_1]:\mathbb Z[a_1^p]],\qquad
  J_q=[\mathbb Z[b_1]:\mathbb Z[b_1^q]] .
\]
Then \(C\) is the nonzero integral resultant of the two powered minima.
Write \(N=\max(p,q)\) and \(g=\gcd(p,q)\).
As \(N/g\to\infty\), one has
\[
       \log\gcd(|C|,I_pJ_q)=o_{P,Q}(N).                              \tag{1}
\]
With \(s_i=\log|a_i|\) and \(t_j=\log|b_j|\), define
\[
      T(p,q)=\sum_{i,j}\max(p s_i,q t_j).
\]
At every pair of exponents satisfying the degree and disjointness conditions one has
\[
  \log|C|=T(p,q)+O_{P,Q}(\log(2N)),\qquad
  T(p,q)\ge\max\{fp\log M(P),eq\log M(Q)\}.                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular (1) and (2) hold for all pairs of distinct sufficiently large primes, including when the two seeds have the same minimum.

Suppose now that \(P\ne Q\) and nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\), coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), satisfy
\[
       P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad
       Q(X)^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q).
\]
These contacts give the required degrees and disjointness by [recovery of the seeds from the rows](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#nodes).
Put \(R=\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\), \(\rho=|\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)|\), \(d=2\min(e,f)\) and \(K=p^e q^f I_p^4J_q^4\rho^3\).
Then
\[
    D_{p,q}=\frac{|C|^4}{\gcd(|C|^4,K^d)}\ \mid\ |R|,\qquad
    \log D_{p,q}=4T(p,q)+o_{P,Q}(N) \quad (N/g\to\infty).             \tag{3}
\]
Thus every such row pair satisfies \(\log|R|\ge 4\max\{fp\log M(P),eq\log M(Q)\}-o_{P,Q}(N)\), with an error independent of the rows.
The [rank-one specialization](#rank-one) gives exact eventual coprimality for distinct primes and a counterexample when a seed is torsion.
The common divisor in (1) measures the smaller of the two valuations at each prime.
Controlling the entire part of \(C\) supported on the index primes would require an estimate for the excess valuation of \(C\) there.

<a id="gcd"></a>
## Reduction to common divisors of two unit differences

Work in a common splitting field \(L\) of degree \(n\).
At a finite prime \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\), use its integral ideal valuation \(v\) and weight it by \(\log \mathrm N\mathfrak l/n\).
The weighted sum of \(v(m)\) for a nonzero rational integer \(m\) equals \(\log|m|\).
The [power-basis determinant](Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) expresses \(I_p\) as the absolute value of the product, over \(i<k\), of
\[
     \frac{a_i^p-a_k^p}{a_i-a_k}
        =a_k^{p-1}\frac{r^p-1}{r-1},\qquad r=a_i/a_k\ne1 .
\]
The divided difference is an algebraic integer with valuation between \(0\) and \(v(r^p-1)\).
Each factor \(x^p-y^q\) of \(C\), where \(x=a_i\) and \(y=b_j\), has valuation \(v(x^p y^{-q}-1)\).
At each prime the minimum of two sums of nonnegative numbers is bounded by the sum of the minima over all pairs of summands.
Indeed, summing \(\min(s,t_j)\) over \(j\) gives at least \(\min(s,\sum_j t_j)\), and the same inequality applies to the other sum.
Applying this inequality to the two products bounds \(\log\gcd(|C|,I_p)\) by a finite sum of normalized logarithmic ideal gcds for one divided difference and one mixed difference at a time.

If \(r\) has finite order, the divided difference up to its unit factor takes only finitely many values as \(p\) varies, and the full degree assumption excludes zero.
Its contribution is bounded independently of \(p,q\).
For \(r\) nontorsion put \(u=r^p\) and \(w=x^p y^{-q}\).
Then \(u,w\ne1\), and their absolute logarithmic Weil heights are \(O_{P,Q}(N)\).
Fix \(\epsilon>0\).
By [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), with only the archimedean places in the set of allowed denominators, the normalized logarithmic norm of \((u-1,w-1)\) is at most \(\epsilon\max\{h(u),h(w)\}\) outside finitely many pairs and finitely many subgroups \(u^A=w^B\), for coprime integers \(A,B\).
The finitely many pairs have bounded contribution since neither difference vanishes.
To deal with a subgroup, write \([z]\) for the class of a unit in \((\mathcal O_L^\times/\mu(L))\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\), where \(\mu(L)\) is the group of roots of unity in \(L\).
If \(B=0\), membership would force \(u\) to be torsion.
For \(B\ne0\), membership gives
\[
      p\big(B[x]-A[r]\big)=qB[y].
\]
Here \(B[y]\ne0\), since \(y\) is nontorsion.
Consequently this subgroup allows at most one ratio \(q/p\).
At a fixed rational ratio the height \(N/g\) of the reduced exponent pair is constant.
All exceptional subgroups are therefore absent once \(N/g\) is sufficiently large for this \(\epsilon\).
The weighted contribution from this pair of factors is \(o_{P,Q}(N)\), by first fixing \(\epsilon\) and then letting it decrease to zero.
Summing over the finitely many factors gives the bound for \(\log\gcd(|C|,I_p)\).
Interchanging the seeds and using \(w^{-1}\) in the reverse comparison gives the bound for \(\log\gcd(|C|,J_q)\).
Their sum bounds the left side of (1).

For completeness, the full degrees and disjointness hold at all pairs of distinct sufficiently large primes for fixed nontorsion seeds.
An internal collision forces a quotient of distinct conjugates to have order equal to the prime exponent.
Only finitely many quotients occur.
A mixed coincidence \(x^p=y^q\) forces \(p h(x)=q h(y)\) with both heights positive, so it prescribes one rational ratio if it occurs at all.
At most one ordered pair of distinct primes has that ratio.

<a id="archimedean"></a>
## The size of the mixed resultant

Fix roots \(x=a_i,y=b_j\) in the chosen complex embedding and divide \(x^p-y^q\) by the power of larger absolute value.
The logarithm of the resulting absolute value is \(\log|1-z|\), with \(z=x^p y^{-q}\) or its inverse, and \(|z|\le1\).
If \(|1-z|\ge1/2\), this logarithm is bounded in absolute value by \(\log 2\).
Otherwise choose principal logarithms and an integer \(k=O(N)\) so that
\[
      \Lambda=\pm(p\log x-q\log y)-2k\log(-1)
\]
is the principal logarithm of \(z\), using \(\log(-1)=i\pi\) and choosing the sign to match the quotient.
Since \(z\ne1\), the [Baker–Wüstholz bound for a nonzero complex logarithmic form](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) gives \(\log|\Lambda|\ge-O_{x,y}(\log(2N))\).
Integration of \(1/z\) on the segment from \(1\) to \(z\) gives \(|\Lambda|\le2|1-z|\).
Adding back the logarithm of the larger absolute value and summing over \(i,j\) yields the equality in (2).
For each fixed \(s_i\), convexity of \(t\mapsto\max(p s_i,t)\) and \(\sum_j t_j=0\) give \(\sum_j\max(p s_i,q t_j)\ge f\max(p s_i,0)\).
The sum in \(i\) is at least \(fp\log M(P)\).
Interchanging \(i,j\) gives the other lower bound.

The [integral two-seed divisibility](Yin_C_G0345_v496.md#statement) gives \(|C|^4\mid R K^d\), so the integer \(D_{p,q}\) in (3) divides \(|R|\).
The discarded factor satisfies
\[
   \gcd(|C|^4,K^d)
   \le (p^e q^f\rho^3)^d\gcd(|C|,I_pJ_q)^{4d}.
\]
Indeed at each prime the minimum of \(4v(C)\) and \(4d\,v(I_pJ_q)\) is at most \(4d\min(v(C),v(I_pJ_q))\).
The logarithm of the first factor on the right is \(O_{P,Q}(\log(2N))\), and (1) controls the second.
Combining this bound with (2) proves (3).

<a id="rank-one"></a>
## Exact coprimality when the conjugates generate a group of rank one

Suppose the group generated by all \(a_i,b_j\) modulo torsion has rank one.
For all sufficiently large distinct primes \(p,q\) one then has \(\gcd(|C|,I_pJ_q)=1\).
To prove this, write every conjugate and its ratios as a root of unity times an integral power of a fixed unit \(t\) lifting a generator of this group.
Let \(m=|\mu(L)|\).
For a torsion quotient \(r\ne1\) of order \(k\), take \(p\) prime to \(k\) and choose a positive \(h\) with \(ph\equiv1\bmod k\).
Then \((1-r^p)/(1-r)\) has integral inverse \(\sum_{j=0}^{h-1} r^{pj}\), so this quotient contributes no prime to \(I_p\).

For a nontorsion quotient fix \(r=\zeta_r t^c\) and a mixed pair \(x=\zeta_x t^a,\ y=\zeta_y t^b\), with roots of unity \(\zeta_r,\zeta_x,\zeta_y\) and \(bc\ne0\).
If a finite prime divides both the divided difference at \(r\) and the mixed difference at \(x,y\), reduction gives \(r^p=x^p y^{-q}=1\).
Thus the reduction of \(t\) has order dividing \(m\,\gcd(cp,ap-bq)\).
For distinct primes \(p,q\), this gcd is at most \(|c|\gcd(p,bq)\le |bc|\).
The prime therefore divides one of the fixed nonzero integers of \(L\) given by \(t^{mk}-1\), \(1\le k\le|bc|\).
Only finitely many finite primes arise as the internal and mixed pairs vary.
At any one of them, a nontrivial reduction of \(r\) has fixed finite order \(h>1\), so \(r^p=1\) forces the prime exponent \(p=h\).
If instead \(r\) reduces to \(1\), the divided difference reduces to the unit \(a_k^{p-1}\) times \(p\), so the residual characteristic must equal \(p\).
Both alternatives exclude this prime for sufficiently large \(p\).
The same argument with the two seeds interchanged excludes every common prime of \(C\) and \(J_q\).

Both seeds being nontorsion is essential to (1).
For \(P=X^2-3X+1,\ Q=X-1\), let \(a>1\) be the larger root of \(P\) and let \(p\) be odd.
The integer \(S_p=a^{-(p-1)/2}(a^p-1)/(a-1)\) is fixed by inversion of \(a\), and
\[
 |C|=S_p^2,\qquad
 I_p=S_p\,a^{-(p-1)/2}\frac{a^p+1}{a+1}.
\]
The second factor is also an algebraic integer fixed by inversion, since \(p\) is odd.
Consequently \(\log\gcd(|C|,I_p)\ge\log S_p=\tfrac12 p\log a+O(1)\), including for distinct primes \(p,q\) of comparable size.
