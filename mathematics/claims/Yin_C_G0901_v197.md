# The real pole bounds a common Frobenius degree at all three coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A sublinear bound and a class of excluded exponents

Consider a [genuine integral Salem realization of the exact trace pole](Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return), with height parameter \(q\ge2\), \(p=q+1,\ L=2q+1\), minimum degree \(d=2D\) and coprime exponents \(0<u<v\).
Consider each of the following pairs \((m,c)\) and **every** prime \(r\mid c\):
\[
                   (v-u,q),\qquad(v,L),\qquad(v+u,p).
\]
Fix \(0<\kappa<1\), and let \(R\) be the product of the full prime powers in \(m\) belonging to primes \(>d^\kappa\), with empty product one.
For sufficiently large \(d\), \(r\nmid R\); write \(f=\operatorname{ord}_R(r)\) and use \(f=1\) if \(R=1\).
All except \(O_\kappa(d^{1-\kappa}(\log(3d))^C)\) of the conjugates have residue order divisible by \(R\) at \(r\), for an absolute \(C\).
Thus their residue degrees, and hence their local degrees, are multiples of \(f\).
The real pin forces the following upper bound on this particular divisor:
\[
               f < d\exp\!\left(-c_\kappa\frac{\log d}{\log^2 L}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
The constant \(c_\kappa>0\) is effective and independent of the choices of \(q,m,c,r\).
If all prime factors of \(m\) exceed \(d^\kappa\), more than \(\exp(c_\kappa\log d/\log^2 L)\) distinct irreducible factors of the reduction at \(r\) have roots of exact order \(m\).
These factors are separable with multiplicity one, apart from possible coincidence with the exceptional two roots at \(r\mid L\); in this all-large-primes case \(m\) is odd and \(>2\), so no such coincidence occurs.

In particular, for any fixed positive integers \(h,j\), none of the three exponents \(m\) can equal \(hP\) in sufficiently large degree if \(P\) and \((P-1)/j\) are both prime.
This includes a safe prime or any fixed multiple of a safe prime, for each of the three exponents.
The local-degree implication of (1) concerns the divisor \(f\); other factors of a local degree can still be large.
Even when \(f\) is the entire degree of all the primitive factors, its upper bound in (1) remains above a logarithmic cutoff.

<a id="count"></a>
## How many conjugates can miss one of the large prime powers?

At \(r\mid q\) or \(r\mid p\), the reduction of \(F\) divides \(Z^m-1\).
Indeed the [power relation modulo the whole coefficient](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#periods) holds for \(T=\beta^u\) in its power order, using the index prime to \(pq\).
At \(r\mid L\) the two [odd-exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v895.md#trace) and [even-exponent](Yin_C_G0345_v897.md#even) splittings give \(F\bmod r=(Z+1)^2D_r\), with \(D_r\mid Z^v-1\).
In this case reserve the two indicated root occurrences throughout the argument even if the two factors share a residue.
The bounds on the [height and exponents](Yin_C_G0901_v177.md#census) and the [real logarithm at the pole](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#real) give, uniformly at sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
  q,u\ll(\log(3d))^3,\quad d<m\ll d(\log(3d))^C,\quad r\le L\ll(\log(3d))^3 .
\]
For the lower bound use \(v\log\beta/d=\log p+o(1)\), \(\log\beta\le\log T<1/q\), and \(u=o(d)\).

Outside the reserved occurrences, count roots of the binomial with their residual multiplicities.
For a prime \(\lambda\mid R\), a root missing the full \(\lambda\)-part of \(m\) belongs to \(Z^{m/\lambda}-1\).
Since \(r\ne\lambda\), any such root has the same multiplicity in this smaller binomial as it has in \(Z^m-1\): both multiplicities are the exact \(r\)-power part of \(m\).
The \(\lambda\)-deficient occurrences in \(F\) outside the reserved pair therefore number at most \(m/\lambda\).
As \(m\ll d(\log(3d))^C\), the number of distinct primes \(>d^\kappa\) in \(m\) is \(O_\kappa(1)\).
The union of the deficient and reserved roots has cardinality
\[
            z\le 2+\Delta,\qquad
            \Delta=m\sum_{\lambda\mid R,\ \lambda\ {\rm prime}}\frac1\lambda
                    \ll_\kappa d^{1-\kappa}(\log(3d))^C .
\]
All counts here equal counts of conjugates with the designated reductions by monicity.
Among the \(d-z\) other occurrences every Frobenius orbit has size a multiple of \(f\), since each root has order a multiple of \(R\).
The sets and multiplicities are Frobenius invariant.
Thus \(t=(d-z)/f\) is a positive integer.

<a id="upper"></a>
## The upper bound from the real period restriction

Write \(\varphi\) for Euler's totient, put \(b_0=m/R\), and \(j_0=\varphi(R)/f\), an integer, including when \(R=1\).
Then
\[
 b_0j_0\le \frac{m}{f}=\frac{tm}{d-z}\ll t(\log(3d))^C .
\]
The totient product gives \(m-b_0\varphi(R)\le\Delta\).
Using \(d=tf+z\) now gives an estimate with integer multiplier and integer target,
\[
       |tm-b_0j_0d|
           \le t\Delta+b_0j_0 z
           \ll_\kappa t d^{1-\kappa}(\log(3d))^{C'}
\]
for an absolute \(C'\).
Let \(A\) be the order of the binary word \(Z\bmod(2,F)\).
The [dyadic order relation](Yin_C_G0901_v181.md#identities) gives \(v-\epsilon u=kA\), \(1\le k<2\log_2 L\), for the parity sign \(\epsilon\) of \(q\) used there.
For all three choices \(|m-kA|\le2u\).
Consequently \(|ktA-b_0j_0 d|\) satisfies the same upper estimate, after increasing its constant.

Apply [binary period repulsion](Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement) with \(\eta=\min(1/4,\kappa/4)\).
If \(t\le\exp(c_\kappa\log d/\log^2 L)\), choose \(c_\kappa\) small enough to have \(t\le d^{\kappa/4}\) and \(kt\) within the allowed denominator range.
This second choice is uniform since \(q\ll(\log(3d))^3\) and \(\log k=O(\log\log L+1)=o(\log d/\log^2 L)\).
The preceding upper estimate is then \(o(d^{1-\eta})\), contradicting the lower bound for \(|ktA-b_0j_0d|\).
This proves the lower bound on \(t\), hence (1).
If \(R=m\), then \(r\nmid m\) and all counted binomial factors are simple.
The \(d-z\) nondeficient roots are primitive \(m\)-th roots with orbit size exactly \(f\); the integer \(t\) now counts their actual irreducible factors.

<a id="exclude"></a>
## Fixed multiples of primes with a large prime divisor of their predecessor

Suppose \(m=hP\) with fixed \(h\) and primes \(P\) and \(s=(P-1)/j\) with fixed \(j\).
For large \(d\) take \(\kappa=1/2\); then \(P>d/h\) and \(R=P\).
At any one prime \(r\mid c\) we have \(1< r^j\le L^j<P\) eventually.
Hence \(\operatorname{ord}_P(r)\) cannot divide \(j\).
Since this order divides \(js\) and \(s>j\) for large \(d\), it must be a multiple of \(s\).
This gives \(f\ge(P-1)/j\gg_{h,j}d\), contradicting (1), whose exponential factor tends to zero uniformly at the allowed heights.
