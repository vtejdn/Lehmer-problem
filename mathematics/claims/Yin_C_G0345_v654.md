# Root angles and Taylor digits at the short integral energy threshold

<a id="statement"></a>
## A common pattern through moving Taylor orders

Let \(P\) be monic in \(\mathbb Z[X]\), of degree \(d\), with \(|P(0)|=1\), no unit-circle roots and \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
Let \(T\) be a nonzero integral numerator of width less than \(d\), translated to have lowest exponent zero.
Consider any sequence with \(m\to0\) and \(m\|T/P\|_2^2\to1/2\), using Haar probability measure \(\tau\) on the circle.
Let \(\mu\) give each root \(\rho\) mass \(|\log|\rho||/(2m)\) at \(\rho/|\rho|\), with multiplicity, and write \(\widehat\mu(k)=\int z^{-k}\,d\mu(z)\).
List the occupied exponents of the Taylor series of \(H=T/P\) as \(0=u_0<u_1<\cdots\), with nonzero integer coefficients \(q_i\).

Take any positive integer windows \(G,N\) with \(N\to\infty\) and \(m(G+N)\to0\).
There is a pattern \(c_0,\ldots,c_G\) with \(c_0=1\) and
\[
         \max_{0\le k\le G}|\widehat\mu(k)-c_k|=o(1).                 \tag{1}
\]
Either all its positive-index digits are zero, or there are an integer \(1\le h\le G\) and a sign \(\sigma\) such that \(c_{jh}=\sigma^j\) for \(jh\le G\) and all other positive-index digits are zero.
For all sufficiently late inputs \(|q_i|=1\) for \(i<N+G\).
At each \(k\le G\), at most two of the first \(N\) occupied positions fail
\[
                   [X^{u_i+k}]H=q_i c_k.                             \tag{2}
\]
The exceptional positions may depend on \(k\).
In particular at least \(N-2G\) of the positions carry the complete word through order \(G\).
The weak limits of \(\mu\) are Haar measure or uniform measure on one set \(z^h=\pm1\) for fixed \(h\).
The conclusions apply to the short remainder of any [balanced division attaining the unrestricted lower threshold](Yin_C_G0345_v643.md#statement), after translating its lowest exponent to zero.

<a id="center"></a>
## A bound for every subset of the normalized remainders

Put \(C=1/(2\sinh(2m))\), \(E=\|T/P\|_2^2\), and let \(L\) be the minimum energy over nonzero integral short numerators.
The [integral packing inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#statement) gives \(L/C\to2\), since \(L\le E\) and \(E/C\to2\).
Take the distinct short polynomials \(S_i=\operatorname{sign}(P(0)q_i)T_i\) from [successive divisions](Yin_C_G0345_v644.md#remainders), with \(T_i/P=X^{-u_i}(H-\sum_{j<i}q_jX^{u_j})\).
The same triangle inequality with starting norm \(\sqrt E\) gives a bound \(B=\sqrt E/(1-\sqrt{(N+G-1)/C})\) for all these norms through \(i<N+G\).
The lowest-coefficient estimate gives \(q_i^2\le B^2/C=2+o(1)\), hence \(S_i(0)=1\).

Let \(S_*\) be the short complex constant-one minimizer.
For any nonempty subset \(I\subset[0,N)\cap\mathbb Z\) of size \(s\), let \(\bar S_I=s^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}S_i\).
The squared norm of \((S_i-S_*)/P\) is at most \(B^2-C\).
Each distinct pair is at squared distance at least \(L\).
The sum of squared distances to the sample mean gives
\[
  \frac{\|(\bar S_I-S_*)/P\|_2^2}{C}
             \le B^2/C-1-\frac{s-1}{2s}\frac LC
             \le \frac1s+o(1).                                      \tag{3}
\]
The error is uniform even for fixed \(s\).

<a id="coefficients"></a>
## Uniform estimates for a growing Taylor order

Use the real outer polynomial \(O\), the inner functions \(U=P/O,\ V=O^*/P,\ J=UV\), and \(b=J(0)\) of [short-width evaluation](Yin_C_G0345_v621.md#evaluation).
Write \(t=e^{-m}\), \(F=U/U(0)\) and \(D=V/V(0)\).
The [extremizer identity](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#extremizer) and its [factor expansions](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#angles) give
\[
       P(0)S_*/P=(F^{-1}-e^{-4m}D)/(1-e^{-4m}).
\]
For any order \(1\le k\le G\), the logarithmic coefficients of \(F^{-1}\) and of \(D^{-1}\) are sums over their respective reflected root sets of \(2\sinh(k\ell)e^{-ik\theta}/k\), with \(\ell=|\log|\rho||\) and angle \(\theta=\arg\rho\).
Each set has total radial displacement \(m\), and \(\ell\le m\).
Through order \(G\) each logarithmic coefficient is \(O(m)\).
At order \(k\) it differs from \(2\sum\ell e^{-ik\theta}\) by \(O(m(km)^2)\).
For either sign of the logarithm the nonlinear exponential terms total \(O(km^2)\) at order \(k\), by summing \((C_1 m)^j\binom{k-1}{j-1}/j!\) for \(2\le j\le k\) with an absolute \(C_1\).
These bounds are uniform since \(Gm\to0\).
They give
\[
 [X^k]P(0)S_*/P=\widehat\mu(k)+O(km),\qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^G |[X^j]F^{-1}|=1+O(Gm).                                \tag{4}
\]
The coefficient at zero of \(P(0)S_*/P\) is one.

For any short polynomial \(W\), the function \(W/O\) belongs to \(H^2\ominus JH^2\).
The projection of each \(X^j,\ j\ge0\), onto this space has norm at most \(\sqrt{1-|b|^2}\), by its inner product with \(JX^j\).
Consequently every Taylor coefficient of \(W/O\) is bounded by \(\sqrt{1-|b|^2}\|W/P\|_2\).
Since \(|U(0)|=t\) and \(C=t^2/(1-|b|^2)\), convolution with \(F^{-1}\) in (4) gives
\[
 \max_{k\le G,\ k\ge0}|[X^k]W/P|\le(1+O(Gm))\,\|W/P\|_2/\sqrt C.    \tag{5}
\]

For each \(k\) the integers \(a_{ik}=[X^k]P(0)S_i/P\) equal \(q_i[X^{u_i+k}]H\).
These belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\) through the indicated windows, since at most \(k\) subsequent occupied positions can occur up to \(u_i+k\).
Choose the most frequent digit \(c_k\) and its index set \(I\), so \(s\ge N/3\).
Equations (3)–(5) on \(W=\bar S_I-S_*\) prove (1) uniformly.
If a different digit appeared at least twice in the same column, that subclass would give a distance from \(\widehat\mu(k)\) of at most \(1/\sqrt2+o(1)\), although the distance is at least \(1-o(1)\).
This proves (2).

Conjugation symmetry makes the moments real.
Whenever \(|c_j|=1\), \(\|z^j-c_j\|_{L^2(\mu)}=o(1)\) uniformly in such \(j\).
Multiplying the phases shows that if \(|c_i|=|c_j|=1\) and \(0\le j\le i\le G\), then \(\widehat\mu(i-j)=c_i c_j+o(1)\), hence \(c_{i-j}=c_i c_j\) for all sufficiently late inputs.
The same holds at \(i+j\le G\).
Taking the least positive occupied index, closure under differences and addition proves the claimed progression and signs.
For each fixed index all limiting moments are in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), so the [subgroup argument for probability measures](Yin_C_G0345_v639.md#limits) gives the weak-limit classification.

<a id="energy-law"></a>
## Which angular energy the common prefix detects

For any \(I\) as in (3) with \(s\to\infty\), form \(D_I=s^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}q_i X^{-u_i}\).
The comparison identifies the entire weighted energy measure, even when the set \(I\) varies:
\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}\left|
       \frac{|H D_I|^2}{C}-\frac{|1-\bar bJ|^2}{1-|b|^2}
                  \right|\,d\tau\ \longrightarrow0 .                 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the difference between \(P(0)H D_I\) and \(\bar S_I/P\) has norm at most \(\sqrt N\) by the stripping identity and orthogonality, since the occupied coefficients have modulus one.
Equation (3) now gives \(\|P(0)H D_I-S_*/P\|_2=o(\sqrt C)\).
Factoring a difference of squared moduli and applying Cauchy–Schwarz proves (6) using the explicit formula for \(S_*\).
The second density defines the [denominator-dependent probability](Yin_C_G0345_v650.md#angular) whose image under \(J\) is Haar.
Thus the angular probabilities of a single denominator and the actual occupied Taylor exponents test the same distribution at this threshold.

<a id="powers"></a>
## An exclusion when the radial displacements are comparable to their mean

Suppose in addition that \(P\) is irreducible and \(\max_\rho |\log|\rho||\le A(2m/d)\) with \(A\) fixed.
Then the zero pattern is the only eventual possibility for every \(G=o(1/m)\).
Indeed if a nonzero digit occurs at a varying \(1\le h\le G\), choose a subsequence with constant sign \(\sigma=c_h\).
Equation (1) puts \(1-o(1)\) of the radial mass of \(z^h\) in each fixed arc around \(\sigma\).
For any root \(\alpha\), the absolute logarithmic height of \(\alpha^h\) is \(hm/d\to0\).
The powers are nontorsion, and their vanishing heights prevent any fixed power value from recurring infinitely often.
[Bilu's one-dimensional theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#bil97-u001) therefore gives Haar distribution for the conjugate angles of \(\alpha^h\).
Each of those conjugates is repeated the same number of times among the \(h\)-th powers of the \(d\) conjugates of \(\alpha\).
The radial probability is bounded by \(A\) times that counting probability, so a fixed arc of normalized length less than \(1/(2A)\) contradicts the asserted concentration.
Thus all but at most \(2G\) of the first \(N\) gaps exceed \(G\) in this case.
This argument gives a necessary condition for equality and leaves the existence of shrinking inputs open.
