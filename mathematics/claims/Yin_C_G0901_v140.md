# A near-one integer-polynomial Mahler value with totally real trace is Salem

<a id="statement"></a>
## A restriction with no bound on the second iterate or the number of real exterior roots

There is an absolute \(\varepsilon>0\) such that for every nonzero integer polynomial \(P\) with \(1<A=M(P)<1+\varepsilon\),
\[
       A+A^{-1}\text{ is totally real}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
                                      A\text{ is a real Salem number}.
\]
In particular the gap for the actual values in this trace class is equivalent to the real Salem gap.
The trace condition means exactly that every conjugate of \(A\) is real or of modulus one.
It also holds if \(A\) has at least one neutral conjugate and all its exterior conjugates are real, since a neutral conjugate forces reciprocity.
No bound on the degree, the number of real exterior conjugates, or \(M(A)\) is assumed.

More generally take any family of nontorsion algebraic units \(w_i\) of uniformly sufficiently small positive total logarithmic measure, and let \(\Gamma\) consist of torsion twists and radicals of finite products of their conjugates and inverses.
Any nontorsion \(x\in\Gamma\) with \(x+x^{-1}\) totally real lies on the rational multiplicative line modulo torsion of one conjugate of the terminal compressed term \(z\) of an individual input.
Every conjugate of that \(z\) is real or neutral, and it has the independent signed block representation.
There is no hypothesis on the height of \(x\).

There is an exact description on each of these lines.
At a real embedding giving \(|z|>1\), choose the primitive positive unit \(\rho>1\) on the line in the real field \(\mathbb Q(z)\).
Up to conjugation and rational sign, all nontorsion members of the asserted trace class are the nonzero integral powers of these \(\rho\), together with their half-integral powers if \(\rho\) is positive at every real embedding of its field.
The latter condition concerns only the real embeddings; all complex embeddings of \(\rho\) have modulus one.

<a id="action"></a>
## Complex conjugations on the active constituents

It suffices to use finitely many inputs supporting \(x\).
Use the [joint decomposition of the rational class spaces](Yin_C_G0901_v118.md#modules), with classes taken modulo roots of unity.
The span of the orbit of \([x]\) is the sum of its active absolutely irreducible constituents \(W\), identifying repeated copies as the same actual subspace.
Each \(W\) comes from the leading space of a compressed term \(z\) in a chain for one of the inputs, of degree \(D=2ub\) and small positive total logarithmic measure.
The kernel field of \(W\) is the full splitting field of \(z\). [Faithfulness](Yin_C_G0901_v101.md#chain)
Use a common finite normal field containing all actual values, and write \(G\) for its group, \(c\) for ordinary conjugation and \(N\) for the normal subgroup generated by the conjugates of \(c\).
The elementary identity \(\operatorname{Im}(y+y^{-1})=(|y|-|y|^{-1})\sin(\arg y)\) gives the asserted trace criterion.
Thus \(c\) takes every conjugate of \(x\) either to itself or to its inverse.
Every conjugate of \(c\) preserves each of the conjugate class lines of \(x\), by signs.
These classes span their module, so \(N\) acts there through commuting involutions and \([x]\) has one character \(\chi:N\to\{\pm1\}\).
Its nonzero projection to every active \(W\) has the same character.

By faithfulness the image of \(N\) on the roots of each attached \(z\) is abelian, hence so is its image on inverse-pair labels.
In that label group the [unique minimal nontrivial normal subgroup](Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) is the nonabelian \((A_b)^u\).
It follows that the abelian normal image is trivial.
In particular \(c\) fixes all pair labels, which means every root of \(z\) is real or neutral.
In the oriented alternative its sign is uniform on each block, so every block would consist entirely of real or entirely of neutral roots.
At least one real pair exists by Kronecker and reciprocity.
But a full real block would give a fraction of real embeddings at least \(1/u\), whereas the [real-place bound](Yin_C_G0901_v65.md#statement) gives \(O(\sqrt{\log(3D)/D})\) at a fixed total measure ceiling.
Here \(u\ll(\log(3D))^{212}\) and \(D\) tends to infinity uniformly at all terms as the input ceiling shrinks. [Small-unit chains](Yin_C_G0901_v93.md#descent)
Thus the attached term is independent signed and terminal, and \(W\) is its entire class module.

<a id="characters"></a>
## One character determines one pair label on one terminal term

On the signed coordinate space of such a \(z\), all of \(N\) acts diagonally by signs since it fixes all pair labels.
The signs of \(c\) on a real and a neutral label are respectively positive and negative.
Choose one block containing a real pair.
It also contains a neutral pair, by the exclusion of a full real block just proved.
Lift a three-cycle of its labels on which both signs occur to the root group.
Conjugating \(c\) by that lift permutes its diagonal signs on exactly those three labels, irrespective of the signs of the lift.
The product with \(c\) negates exactly two coordinates and lies in the image of \(N\).
Conjugation by the label action gives every double flip on every block, by two-transitivity on each block and transitivity between blocks.
Consequently distinct coordinates have distinct \(N\)-characters: flip one and a spare label of its block while avoiding the other.
The \(\chi\)-eigenspace on \(W\) therefore consists of just one coordinate line.

If two distinct active constituents occurred, let \(a,a'\) be roots on their selected coordinate lines.
In either representation the stabilizer in \(G\) of \(\chi\) is exactly the stabilizer of the selected inverse pair: conjugation transports its character with the label, and no other label has that character.
The two trace fields \(\mathbb Q(a+a^{-1})\) and \(\mathbb Q(a'+a'^{-1})\) are thus the same embedded field, and \(\deg a=\deg a'=D\).
Each root has degree at most two over this common field of degree \(D/2\), so their compositum has degree at most \(2D\).
They would be multiplicatively independent since their nonzero classes belong to two distinct summands.
The [rank-two height-product inequality](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001), multiplied by \(D^2\), gives \(m(a)m(a')\gg D/(\log(3D))^{212}\), impossible at bounded total log measures and uniformly growing \(D\).
There is therefore one active constituent, and the given class is proportional to the selected coordinate on it.

<a id="radicals"></a>
## Which actual radicals retain the trace property

Every nontorsion \(x\) in this trace class has a real exterior conjugate up to rational sign by the unit norm and Kronecker.
Use that conjugate and sign to take \(x>1\).
Write \(a\) for its matched root, real by compression since \(c\) fixes its class, choosing a sign and inverse to have \(a>1\).
Put \(K=\mathbb Q(a)\) and take its [primitive unit on this line](Yin_C_G0901_v43.md#primitive) oriented by \(\rho>1\).
Thus \(a\) is a positive power of \(\rho\) and \(\mathbb Q(\rho)=K\).
A real image of \(a\) gives a real embedding of \(K\), and every nonreal image of \(a\) is neutral, so all nonreal embeddings of \(\rho\) are neutral as well.
For every automorphism fixing \(K\), its image of \(x\) is a torsion multiple of \(x\).
It has modulus \(x>1\) and is therefore real by the trace condition, hence is \(x\) or \(-x\).
This gives \(x^2\in K\); as a positive unit on the same line it equals \(\rho^k\) for an integer \(k>0\).
All integer powers of \(\rho\) and their signs are in the group and satisfy the trace condition.
The primitive \(\rho\) has no square root in \(K\), by saturation.
For odd \(k\), every complex embedding sends a square root of \(\rho^k\) to a neutral value, while a real embedding with negative \(\rho\) gives a nonreal square root of modulus different from one.
At a real embedding \(\rho\ne\pm1\) by injectivity and nontorsion.
This proves both directions of the half-integral criterion.

<a id="values"></a>
## Returning to the entire integer polynomial

At small positive \(\log A\), powers of \(X\), signs and cyclotomic factors can be removed and all contributing irreducible factors are monic units.
The value \(A\) lies in their combined divisible group.
If \(A+A^{-1}\) is totally real, the preceding argument makes \(A\) match the single active terminal term, in its independent signed coordinate model.
Use the [factor decomposition at a match](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#factors); after the match is known its proof uses no bound on \(M(A)\).
It gives \(A=\rho^k=M(\rho)^e B\) with \(\rho>1\) a primitive matched Perron unit, \(k,e\ge1\), \(B\ge1\) the product of the remaining factor measures, and \([B]\) of zero projection to the active constituent.
The [independent signed coordinate comparison](Yin_C_G0901_v120.md#interval) forces \(k/e=1\), since the exterior product has coefficient one at the selected exterior coordinate.
Thus \(B=1\) and \(M(\rho)=\rho\).
This reciprocal large-degree unit has a single exterior root and a neutral conjugate, hence is Salem, as is \(A=\rho^k\).
Conversely every real Salem number is the Mahler measure of its own minimum and all its conjugates are real or neutral.
