# Terminal exponent lists in a union of intervals of total width below the degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## A degree bound independent of the separation between intervals

Let \(\alpha\ne0\) be algebraic of degree \(d\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Fix the full order of \(r\ge3\) nonzero rational integer coefficients \(\mathbf b=(b_1,\ldots,b_r)\).
Consider the strictly increasing integer lists \(t_1<\cdots<t_r\), up to translation, for which \(\sum_i b_i\alpha^{t_i}=0\) and no nonempty proper subsum vanishes.
Write \(H_2(\mathbf b)\) for the product of \(1+|b_i|\) over all but the two largest coefficient magnitudes.
Prescribe \(v\ge1\) finite integer intervals, with the width of \([a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\) equal to \(b-a\).
If the sum of their widths is less than \(d\), the number of lists having some translate supported in their union is at most
\[
                         (C v)^r H_2(\mathbf b)^2                         \tag{1}
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\).
The distances between intervals are arbitrary.

Take a sequence of nontorsion units with a unit-circle conjugate and \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\), or the [near-unit-circle alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v595.md#statement) with fixed \(K\).
Use the full tuple and the collection of marked terminal lists in the [weighted witness count](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement) with \(\lambda=2\).
Put \(L=\log(1/m)\), so \(L\to\infty\) and the weight \(r\to\infty\).
For \(0\le\theta<1\) and \(v\le m^{-\theta}\), a prescribed union as above captures at most the proportion
\[
                         \exp\big(-(1-\theta-o(1))rL\big).                \tag{2}
\]
The intervals may depend on the input, tuple and position of the mark in its full order.
The estimate allows a different common translation for every list.
It applies to the original inputs with arbitrary growth of \(r\).

There is also a restriction when the occupied intervals can be chosen after seeing the list.
Choose any integer \(1\le v\le d\), put \(q=\lceil d/v\rceil\), translate the least exponent to zero and partition the nonnegative integers into bins \([jq,(j+1)q-1]\).
Suppose that \(v\le m^{-\theta}\) and for some fixed \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
             (v-1)\big(L+\log\log(2d)\big)
                         \le (1-\theta-\epsilon)rL.                      \tag{3}
\]
Then the proportion of the lists occupying at most \(v\) bins is at most \(\exp(-(\epsilon-o(1))rL)\).
Each bin has width \(q-1\), so these are distances set by the algebraic degree.
The conditions in (3) quantify the cost of allowing the bins to vary with the list.

<a id="lift"></a>
## A common coefficient function for every occupied interval

Merge overlapping intervals, which cannot increase their total width, and order the resulting disjoint intervals.
For a fixed choice of \(j\) occupied intervals and their positive occupancies, select one placement for each qualifying translation class with these data.
Denote the chosen left endpoints and widths by \(a_s,h_s\), for \(1\le s\le j\).
The labels in each interval form a fixed consecutive group \(I_s\).
For a placement \(\ell\) write \(t_{\ell i}=a_s+u_{\ell i}\) for \(i\in I_s\) and form the row with polynomial entries
\[
               P_{\ell s}(X)=\sum_{i\in I_s} b_iX^{u_{\ell i}} .
\]
The column degrees are at most \(h_s\).
Thus every nonzero square minor has degree at most \(\sum_s h_s<d\) and stays nonzero at \(\alpha\).
The rank over \(\mathbb C(X)\) equals the rank at \(\alpha\), since the minors have rational integer coefficients.
At \(\alpha\) the vector with entries \(\alpha^{a_s}\) belongs to the common kernel.

Choose a maximal nonzero minor and keep every nonpivot coordinate equal to its value in this vector.
Solving the pivot equations gives rational functions \(f_s(X)\) regular at \(\alpha\), with \(f_s(\alpha)=\alpha^{a_s}\).
All rows annihilate the resulting vector since the pivot rows span the row space.
This uses the [common lift by a pivot minor](Yin_C_G0345_v592.md#lifting) with a degree bound for the minors in place of a Mahler measure bound.
In every row the labeled identity
\[
                         \sum_s\sum_{i\in I_s} b_i f_s(X)X^{u_{\ell i}}=0
\]
is nondegenerate because a proper zero subsum would remain zero at \(\alpha\).
In particular, the function multiplying \(b_i\) is the same for all labels in the same interval.

<a id="multiplicities"></a>
## Permuting repeated coefficients within each interval

Write \(n_{s,b}\) for the number of labels having coefficient \(b\) in \(I_s\).
Each placement gives \(\prod_{s,b}n_{s,b}!\) labeled identities by permuting the exponents of labels having the same interval and signed coefficient.
Keep the arrays of internal exponents up to simultaneous translation.
The permutations of any one placement are distinct even after this normalization, since a finite nonempty set in a given interval cannot be a nonzero translate of itself.
Arrays from distinct translation classes cannot coincide after a permutation and translation either.
Indeed adding the fixed endpoint \(a_s\) back to each coordinate and sorting recovers the original list up to that same translation.
The [binary-tree count](Yin_C_G0345_v655.md#trees) therefore bounds the number of placements selected for these data by
\[
                         \frac{(2r-3)!!}{\prod_{s,b}n_{s,b}!}.
\]

Omit two labels of largest magnitude and let \(n'_{s,b}\) denote the remaining multiplicities.
Put \(Z=\sum_{b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}(1+|b|)^{-2}<\infty\).
The multinomial probability of these multiplicities in \(r-2\) independent draws with probabilities \(1/(jZ(1+|b|)^2)\) is at most one.
Consequently
\[
       \frac{r!}{\prod_{s,b}n_{s,b}!}
       \le r(r-1)\frac{(r-2)!}{\prod_{s,b}n'_{s,b}!}
       \le r(r-1)(jZ)^{r-2}H_2(\mathbf b)^2.
\]
As \((2r-3)!!\le 2^{r-1}(r-1)!\), the cost at fixed data is at most \((C_0j)^r H_2(\mathbf b)^2\) for an absolute \(C_0\).
There are at most \(\binom vj\binom{r-1}{j-1}\) choices of intervals and occupancies.
For \(j\le\min(v,r)\) one has \(\binom vj j^r\le e^j v^r\), by \(\binom vj\le(ev/j)^j\).
Summation over \(j\) proves (1).

<a id="witness"></a>
## Comparing the counts with the coefficient-weighted witnesses

The marked coefficient can occupy at most \(r\) places in the full order, and translation equivalence with that place fixed preserves the whole marked list after the mark is reset to zero.
The [weighted count](Yin_C_G0345_v600.md#statement) supplies at least \(H_2(\mathbf b)^2(c_2/m)^{r-2}\) lists.
For large \(r\) they have distinct exponents, and terminality excludes every proper zero subsum.
The logarithm of the fraction in the prescribed unions is therefore at most
\[
                   -(r-2)L+r\log v+O_K(r+\log r),
\]
where an absolute implied constant suffices for an exact unit-circle conjugate.
This gives (2).

For the variable bins use the witness span bound \(W=O_K(d\log(2d)/m)\) with \(W\ge d\).
Bin zero is occupied, and there are at most \(\lfloor W/q\rfloor\) other available bins.
If at least \(v\) bins are available, pad an occupied collection to \(v\) and choose its other \(v-1\) members in at most \((2eW/d)^{v-1}\) ways for \(v\ge2\), using \(q\ge d/v\).
If fewer bins are available, their entire union suffices, and for \(v=1\) the first bin alone is the only possibility.
Any selected union has total width at most \(v(q-1)<d\).
Taking the union bound adds at most \((v-1)(L+\log\log(2d)+O_K(1))\) to the preceding logarithmic estimate.
Under (3), the term \(O_K(v-1)\) is \(o(rL)\), since \(L\to\infty\).
This proves the variable-bin estimate.
Counting the lists spread across more bins than this estimate covers remains necessary for an unrestricted Mahler gap.
