# Near-equal coefficients exclude unit factors of reciprocal four-term sections

<a id="statement"></a>
## Coefficient separation, slope and primitive degree

Let \(0<u<v\) be coprime integers, \(g\ge1\), \(\epsilon=\pm1\), and \(a,b\) nonzero coprime integers.
Put \(W=u+v\), \(B=|b/a|\), \(H=\max(|a|,|b|)\), and
\[
 P(X)=a(1+\epsilon X^W)+b(X^u+\epsilon X^v).
\]
When \(B\le1\), every algebraic-unit root of \(P(X^g)\) is a root of unity.
When \(B>1\), let \(M_{\rm ext}(P)\) denote the sum of \(\log|x|\) over all exterior complex roots, counted with multiplicities.
Then \(M_{\rm ext}(P)\le\operatorname{arcosh}B\).
If \(H\ge8\) and the parent at any dilation has a nontorsion unit root, the stronger estimate
\[
 M_{\rm ext}(P)\le 3(B-1)
\]
holds.
Each estimate also bounds the sum of logarithmic Mahler measures of **all nontorsion unit factors** of \(P(X^g)\), counted with multiplicities as monic irreducible rational factors.

More precisely, for any such root \(z\), put \(\beta=z^g\), \(D=[\mathbb Q(\beta):\mathbb Q]\) and \(\delta=B-1>0\).
For \(H\ge8\) one has
\[
 \frac{u\log2}{12\delta}\le D\le W,
 \qquad \log(H/2)\le Wh(\beta)\le \frac{3W\delta}{D},
 \qquad \frac vu\gg\frac{\log H}{\delta^2} .
\]
Writing \(G_3(t)=(\log\log(5t)/\log(2t))^3\), one also has \(G_3(D)\ll\delta\).
In a sequence with \(B\downarrow1\) and a nontorsion unit root the primitive degree and span therefore satisfy
\[
 W\ge D\ge \exp\!\left(c\delta^{-1/3}\log(1/\delta)\right)
\]
eventually, with an absolute \(c>0\).
All constants are independent of coefficients, directions and dilations.
For example, \(|a|=q, |b|=q+1\) requires \(v/u\gg q^2\log q\) and \(W\ge\exp(c q^{1/3}\log q)\) for large \(q\).
Violation of either necessary bound excludes nontorsion unit roots at every common dilation, and then every positive-measure integral factor has logarithmic Mahler measure at least \(\log2\).
These estimates concern arbitrary nontorsion units and varying rational poles, complementing the [fixed-pole allocation at large slope](Yin_C_G0345_v764.md#statement) and the [residue-period restrictions for fixed Salem pole data](Yin_C_G0345_v755.md#statement).

<a id="radius"></a>
## A radial inequality at every exterior root

Write \(T=x^u\), \(Y=x^v\) and \(B_0=b/a\) at a root \(x\).
The graph equation is \(Y=-\epsilon(1+B_0 T)/(T+B_0)\).
For \(B\ne1\) the two linear forms in this quotient have no common zero.
Subtracting squared moduli gives
\[
 |1+B_0 T|^2-|T+B_0|^2=(B^2-1)(|T|^2-1).
\]
If \(B<1\) this excludes exterior roots, hence also interior roots by reciprocity.
At \(B=1\) the polynomial factors as \(a(1+B_0X^u)(1+\epsilon B_0X^v)\).
Kronecker's theorem now gives the unit assertion, including dilations.

Let \(B>1\), let \(t=\log|x|>0\) and put \(r=e^{ut},s=e^{vt}\).
The modulus identity and \(|T+B_0|\ge|r-B|\) give
\[
 (r-B)^2(s^2-1)\le (B^2-1)(r^2-1),\qquad s|r-B|\le Br-1,
\]
where the second inequality follows by adding \((r-B)^2\) before taking square roots.
In particular \(s(r-B)\le Br-1\), hence
\[
 \delta\ge\frac{(r-1)(s-1)}{r+s}
 =\frac{2\sinh(ut/2)\sinh(vt/2)}{\cosh((v-u)t/2)}
 \ge ut\tanh(vt/2).
\]
If \(r>B\), using \(s>r\) gives \(r(r-B)<Br-1\), whence \(ut<\operatorname{arcosh}B\).
If \(r\le B\), this strict bound follows from \(\log B<\operatorname{arcosh}B\).

<a id="count"></a>
## Counting exterior roots and controlling their total measure

On the unit circle the graph function \(R(T)=-\epsilon(1+B_0T)/(T+B_0)\) has modulus one.
For \(B>1\) its zero is inside and its pole outside, so its circle mapping degree is one by the argument principle.
The map \(x\mapsto x^v/R(x^u)\) has degree \(v-u>0\) on the circle, hence at least \(v-u\) distinct preimages of one.
A continuous lift of its argument changes by \(2\pi(v-u)\) over a period and attains representatives of all the intervening levels.
Reciprocity pairs each exterior root with an interior one of equal multiplicity, so at most \(u\) roots counted with multiplicity are exterior.
The radial bound proves \(M_{\rm ext}(P)\le\operatorname{arcosh}B\).
Every root of a factor at dilation \(g\) maps to a primitive root on taking its \(g\)-th power, and summing positive logarithms over all lifts with multiplicities leaves the exterior sum unchanged.
Monic unit factors have their entire logarithmic Mahler measure in this exterior sum.

Suppose a nontorsion unit occurs and \(H\ge8\).
The unit \(\beta=z^g\) gives the projective expression
\[
 [a:b]=[-(\beta^u+\epsilon\beta^v):1+\epsilon\beta^W].
\]
Neither coordinate vanishes because \(\beta\) is nontorsion.
At every place the maximum of the displayed absolute values is bounded by \(\max(1,|\beta|)^W\), with an additional factor two at archimedean places.
Thus \(\log(H/2)\le Wh(\beta)\).
All finite places of the unit contribute zero, so \(h(\beta)\le t_{\max}\), where \(t_{\max}\) is the largest positive logarithmic modulus among **all** roots of \(P\).
It follows that \(v t_{\max}/2\ge(v/2W)\log(H/2)\ge\tfrac14\log4\).
As \(\tanh(\tfrac14\log4)=1/3\), the radial inequality applied to a root attaining this maximum gives \(ut_{\max}\le3\delta\).
There are at most \(u\) exterior roots, proving the sharper total estimate.

<a id="degree"></a>
## An absolute degree obstruction for algebraic units

The [house theorem of Dimitrov](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#house-and-its-relation-to-the-gap) gives a conjugate of the nontorsion unit \(\beta\) with logarithmic modulus at least \((\log2)/(4D)\).
Here \(D>1\), since rational units are torsion.
All such conjugates are roots of the degree-\(W\) polynomial \(P\), giving \(D\le W\) and \(u\log2/(4D)\le3\delta\).
Their positive logarithmic moduli sum to \(D h(\beta)\le M_{\rm ext}(P)\le3\delta\).
Combining this with the projective height inequality yields the displayed slope bound since \(W<2v\) and \(\log(H/2)\gg\log H\) for \(H\ge8\).

[Voutier's ordinary height theorem](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(G_3(D)\ll D h(\beta)\), with its positive low-degree corollary covering bounded degrees.
Thus \(G_3(D)\ll\delta\).
Coprimality ensures \(H\to\infty\) when \(B\downarrow1\), and the last inequality forces \(D\to\infty\).
For large \(D\) it gives \(\log D\gg\delta^{-1/3}\log\log D\), first implying \(\log\log D\ge\tfrac13\log(1/\delta)-O(1)\), then the claimed bound by substitution.
When no nontorsion unit factor exists, positive logarithmic Mahler measure of an integral divisor requires nontrivial content or a nonunit irreducible factor, whose leading or constant coefficient in primitive normalization has absolute value at least two.
