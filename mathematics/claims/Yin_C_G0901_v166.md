# Both plastic Salem–Boyd families have primitive Salem factors at every sufficiently large delay

<a id="statement"></a>
## An explicit cutoff for all dilations

Let \(B(X)=X^3-X-1\), \(B^*(X)=1-X^2-X^3\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the plastic number, the real zero of \(B\).
For \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) put
\[
                  P_{n,\epsilon}(X)=X^n B(X)+\epsilon B^*(X).
\]
For every \(n\ge16\) there is a single strictly exterior root \(r=r_{n,\epsilon}\), which is positive real and Salem.
Let \(S=S_{n,\epsilon}\) be its monic minimum and \(d=\deg S\).
For every \(n\ge2560\) one has simultaneously for all integers \(g\ge1\)
\[
      S(X^g)\ \text{irreducible over }\mathbb Q,\qquad
             \deg S(X^g)=gd,\qquad M(S(X^g))=r.                      \tag{1}
\]
The conclusion also holds at any earlier \(n\ge16\) with \(d\ge512\).
Every nontorsion irreducible factor of \(P_{n,\epsilon}(X^g)\) is consequently this single factor for \(n\ge2560\).
The numbers \(r_{n,\epsilon}\) themselves, after discarding finitely many delays, are pairwise distinct multiplicatively independent atoms of the full monoid of integer-polynomial Mahler measures.
They accumulate at \(\theta\) from below for \(\epsilon=-1\) and from above for \(\epsilon=1\).
The atom assertion uses the degree cutoff at a fixed ceiling in the [generator theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v158.md#statement); the explicit cutoff in (1) concerns irreducibility.

<a id="roots"></a>
## The unique exterior root and its degree

The root-count argument is the classical [Salem–Boyd construction](../audits/literature/Hir06/Yin_U001.md#hir06-u001).
The cubic \(B\) has one real zero \(\theta>1\) and a nonreal conjugate pair of modulus \(\theta^{-1/2}<1\), by its discriminant \(-23\) and its root product one.
On \(|X|=1\) one has \(|B^*|=|B|>0\).
For \(0\le t<1\), Rouché's theorem gives \(X^nB+t\epsilon B^*\) exactly one exterior root counting multiplicity.
By continuity at \(t=1\) the number of roots strictly outside can only decrease.

At \(X=5/4\) the values of \(64B,64B^*\) are \(-19,-161\); at \(X=4/3\) those of \(27B,27B^*\) are \(1,-85\).
The inequalities \((5/4)^{16}>161/19\) and \((4/3)^{16}>85\) give opposite signs at these endpoints for either family and every \(n\ge16\).
Hence \(5/4<r<4/3\) and this is the unique exterior root with multiplicity one.
Since the full polynomial is reciprocal up to sign and has unit constant, the only interior root is \(r^{-1}\).
The monic factor \(S\) has constant term of modulus one, so it contains both roots and all its remaining roots have modulus one.
A reciprocal quadratic unit in this interval would have integer trace \(r+r^{-1}\) strictly between two and three.
Thus \(r\) is genuinely Salem, \(M(S)=r\), and the other irreducible factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

The polynomials \(B,B^*\) are coprime over every field since \(B+B^*=-X(X+1)\) and \(B(0)=B(-1)=-1\).
In particular \(S\) and \(B\) have nonzero integral resultant.
At \(r\) we have \(|B(r)|=r^{-n}|B^*(r)|<4r^{-n}\); at the other \(d-1\) roots of \(S\), the bound is \(|B|\le3\).
Consequently
\[
               3^{n/5}<r^n<4\cdot3^{d-1}<3^{d+1},
             \qquad d>n/5-1,                                         \tag{2}
\]
using \((5/4)^5>3\).
The identity \(B(r)=-\epsilon r^{-n} B^*(r)\) also gives \(r\to\theta\) since every limit point in the compact interval is a zero of \(B\).
Here \(B^*(r)<0\), so \(r\) lies on the stated side of \(\theta\).
Distinct delays for the same sign cannot give the same \(r>1\), because \(r^n\) is determined by \(r\) and the sign.

<a id="certificate"></a>
## A nonzero univariate certificate at every prime

Write \(A=\epsilon B^*\) and let \(p\) be any prime.
The polynomial
\[
           H_p(X)=-\frac{(-A(X))^p B(X^p)+ A(X^p)B(X)^p}{p}
                     \ \in\mathbb Z[X],\qquad \deg H_p\le6p          \tag{3}
\]
is integral by Frobenius, including at \(p=2\).
If \(r=\rho^p\) in \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), then
\[
                           \bar S\mid\bar H_p
                             \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[X].            \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the [necessary defect divisibility](Yin_C_G0901_v161.md#defect), applied to the multiple \(P=P_{n,\epsilon}\) of \(S\), gives \(\delta_p P(r)=-P(r^p)/p\in p\mathbb Z[r]\).
As \(B(r)r^n=-A(r)\), multiplication of this exact defect value by \(B(r)^p\) gives \(H_p(r)\).
This proves (4) in the order \(\mathbb Z[r]\cong\mathbb Z[X]/(S)\), retaining multiplicities even if \(\bar S\) is inseparable.

The reduction \(\bar H_p\) is nonzero for every prime.
Choose a simple root \(\xi\) of \(\bar A\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\).
It is nonzero, and \(B(\xi)\ne0\) by the coprimality above.
Differentiating (3) over the integers before reduction gives
\[
                   \bar H_p'(\xi)=-\xi^{p-1}\bar A'(\xi^p)
                                          \bar B(\xi)^p\ne0.
\]
All terms differentiated from \((-A)^p B(X^p)/p\) vanish at \(\xi\), and the other product-rule term contains \(\bar A(\xi^p)=0\).
The remaining derivative is nonzero because \(\bar A'(\xi^p)=\bar A'(\xi)^p\).
The reciprocal cubic is separable for \(p\ne23\).
At \(p=23\) the factorization of \(B\) is \((X-10)^2(X-3)\), so \(\xi=3^{-1}=8\) is still a simple root of \(B^*\).
This includes every prime without an exceptional reduction.
It follows that an in-field \(p\)-th root would imply \(d\le6p\).

<a id="primitivity"></a>
## Excluding all primes together and identifying the atoms

If \(r=\rho^p\) in \(K\), then \(\rho\) is a unit of degree \(d\), since \(\mathbb Q(r)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\rho)\subseteq K\).
The degree and absolute-height formula gives \(\log M(\rho)=\log r/p\).
By [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) this exceeds \(2/(\log(3d))^3\).
As \(\log r<\log(4/3)<1/3\), (4) now gives
\[
                      d\le6p<(\log(3d))^3 .
\]
This is impossible at \(d\ge512\), since \(\log(3\cdot512)<8\) and \(d/(\log(3d))^3\) is increasing throughout that range.
Inequality (2) gives \(d\ge512\) for \(n\ge2560\).
The real embedding of \(K\) has \(r>0\), excluding \(r\in-4K^4\).
Thus the [binomial and composition criteria](../audits/literature/JP18/Yin_U001.md#jp18-u001) give irreducibility for every \(g\), together with the displayed degree.
Taking all \(g\) roots above each original conjugate preserves the total Mahler measure.
Dilation of a cyclotomic factor has only roots of unity, giving the assertion about every factor of the full parent.

In the marked real field \(K\) the only roots of unity are the rational signs.
If the conjugate class of \(r\) on its rational line in \(K^\times/\mu(K)\) were divisible by a prime, the positive representative of a dividing class would give an actual positive \(p\)-th root in \(K\).
Thus \(r\) is primitive on this line.
All torsion-quotient classes of its conjugates are singletons, since they have common size and \(r\) is the only strictly exterior conjugate.
The exterior product is \(r\) itself, whose class is one coordinate: it projects nontrivially to the full signed space, and in the oriented model its projection to the sum-zero space on its block is the centered coordinate vector.
Both statements apply above the fixed-ceiling threshold by the [two block models](Yin_C_G0901_v158.md#blocks).
Two distinct such primitive positive Salem numbers cannot match.
Indeed a matching relation between conjugates can be transported to the marked \(r\), after which the other conjugate must be the positive exterior Salem number or its positive inverse by equality of absolute values of nonzero powers.
Taking absolute values gives equality of positive integral powers of the two marked Salem numbers.
Their powers generate their full fields by uniqueness of the strictly largest conjugate.
In that same real field, two primitive classes with equal positive powers give the same positive generator greater than one.
The values are distinct by the two sides and the distinct-delay calculation above.
For the single fixed ceiling \(4/3\), (2) takes every sufficiently late source beyond the degree cutoff, and the [independence and atom theorem](Yin_C_G0901_v158.md#independence) therefore applies to these unmatched primitive lines in both families together.
Their atom accumulation is at the plastic number itself, at a positive distance from one.
