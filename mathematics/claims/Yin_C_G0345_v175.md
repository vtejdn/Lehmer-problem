# A second exterior root falls outside the Salem factor of a length-six multiple

<a id="statement"></a>
## The primitive example

Let \(Q=X^5-X-1\), \(Q^*=1-X^4-X^5\), and \(P=X^{23}Q+Q^*\).
The [length-six family](Yin_C_G0345_v174.md#statement) here has \((N,a,b)=(23,5,1)\) and \(\gcd(N,a,b)=1\).
Write \(\rho\) for the positive root of \(Q\), and \(\lambda\) for Lehmer's Salem number.
The positive root \(\lambda\) of \(P\) satisfies \(|\lambda-\rho|<0.02\).
There is a second root \(\eta\) of \(P\) within \(0.043\) of a conjugate \(\beta\) of \(\rho\), where
\[
                     1<|\beta|<1.12<\rho,\qquad
                     1<|\eta|<1.12<\lambda .
\]
This second exterior root belongs to a cofactor of the Salem minimal polynomial.
Thus localizing exterior roots of a reciprocal multiple near distinct exterior conjugates of its seed does not by itself identify their irreducible factors.
The example has fixed exponents and leaves open whether an additional asymptotic hypothesis on the gap forces a different distribution among the factors.

<a id="factorization"></a>
## The real roots and the exact factors

Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L(X)&=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1,\\
 C(X)&=X^{12}-X^{11}+X^{10}-X^8+X^7-X^6+X^5-X^4+X^2-X+1 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct polynomial multiplication gives
\[
       (X^6+1)L(X)C(X)
          =X^{28}-X^{24}-X^{23}-X^5-X^4+1=P(X).                    \tag{1}
\]
The [trace-polynomial proof for \(L\)](../ledger/Yin_L_G0170_v1.md#lehmer-infinite-core-proof) gives irreducibility and the complete Salem signature, with \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\) real and the other eight roots on the unit circle.
For the seed, a root \(z\) of \(Q\bmod5\) satisfies \(z^5=z+1\).
Inductively \(z^{5^j}=z+j\), so its Frobenius orbit has length exactly five.
Thus the monic quintic is irreducible modulo five and \(Q\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).
The polynomial \(Q\) increases on \([1,\infty)\).
Any root \(w\) with \(|w|\ge1\) satisfies \(|w|^5\le |w|+1\), with equality only when \(w\) is positive real.
Consequently \(\rho\) strictly exceeds the modulus of each of its other conjugates.
Substitution of the indicated rational arguments gives
\[
            Q(1.16)<0<Q(1.17),\qquad L(1.17)<0<L(1.18).
\]
It follows that \(1.16<\rho<1.17<\lambda<1.18\).

<a id="disks"></a>
## Two disks with rational centers

Take \(r=1/100\), \(z_Q=(18+108i)/100\), and \(z_C=(20+109i)/100\).
For \(F=Q,C\), expand \(F(z_F+w)=\sum c_jw^j\).
Write \(\|z\|_{\square}=|\Re z|+|\Im z|\), and put \(T_3=\sum_{j\ge3}|c_j|r^j\).
The following bounds use rational arithmetic throughout.

| \(F\) | Upper bound on \(\|c_0\|_{\square}\) | Lower bound on \(|\Re c_1|\) | Upper bound on \(\|c_2\|_{\square}\) | Upper bound on \(T_3\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(Q\) | \(0.037\) | \(4.67\) | \(18\) | \(0.00002\) |
| \(C\) | \(0.009\) | \(6.46\) | \(109\) | \(0.0015\) |

For the first three entries of each row, substitute the center in \(F,F',F''/2\).
For the last entry, write \(F=\sum f_mX^m\) and use
\[
 T_3\le r^3\sum_{m\ge3}|f_m|\binom m3(1.12)^{m-3}.
\]
Indeed \(|z_F|+r<1.12\) for either center, and the binomial expansion on the right dominates the terms of orders \(j\ge3\) in the Taylor tail because \(\binom m3\binom{m-3}{j-3}=\binom mj\binom j3\).
On \(|w|=r\), each row gives \(|c_0|+|c_2|r^2+T_3<|\Re c_1|r\).
Rouché's theorem with the comparison \(c_1w\) therefore gives one zero \(\beta\) of \(Q\) in its disk and one zero \(\eta\) of \(C\) in its disk.
Both disks are strictly outside the unit circle by their imaginary parts, and both lie in \(|z|<1.12\).
Their roots have distance less than \(2r+\sqrt5/100<0.043\).
Irreducibility of \(Q\) makes \(\beta\) a conjugate of \(\rho\).
Equation (1) places \(\eta\) in \(P\), and the established signature of \(L\) precludes its membership in \(L\).
