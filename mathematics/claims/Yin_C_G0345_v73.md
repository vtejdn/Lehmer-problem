# A height bound from the endpoints and the discriminants of reciprocal unit fields

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite estimate on the whole unit circle

Let \(P\) be monic and separable over \(\mathbb Z\), of degree \(d\ge2\).
Write \(L=\log M(P)\) and \(\|P\|_{\mathbb T}=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\).
For every integer \(1\le k\le d\), with \(N=d+k\),
\[
 k\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}
 \le \tfrac12N\log N+(N-1)L+
             \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\log {N-1\choose j}.
\]
Consequently for any sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and \(L/d\to0\),
\[
         d^{-1}\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
If \(\log|P(1)P(-1)|\ge\epsilon d\) for a fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the estimate instead gives an explicit positive lower bound on \(L/d\) for all sufficiently large \(d\).
In the irreducible noncyclotomic unit case this is a lower bound on the absolute height.

<a id="determinant"></a>
## Confluence at a freely chosen point

Use all \(d\) roots of \(P\) as evaluation nodes, and a circle point \(z\) with \(P(z)\ne0\) as a node of multiplicity \(k\).
In columns indexed by \(1,X,\ldots,X^{N-1}\), its rows evaluate derivatives of orders \(0,\ldots,k-1\) divided by their factorials.
As in the [endpoint separation determinant](Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#endpoint-degree), taking divided differences in the ordinary Vandermonde formula gives determinant of modulus \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}P|}|P(z)|^k\).
The first factor is at least one by separability and integrality.
Each root row at \(u\) has norm at most \(\sqrt N\max(1,|u|)^{N-1}\), while the normalized derivative row of order \(j\) has norm at most \(\sqrt N\binom{N-1}{j}\) because \(|z|=1\).
Hadamard's bound at a maximizing \(z\) proves the finite inequality.

Using \(N\le2d\) and \(\binom{N-1}{j}\le(eN/j)^j\) for \(j\ge1\), monotonicity of \(u\log(eN/u)\) on \((0,N]\) gives
\[
 \frac{\log\|P\|_{\mathbb T}}{d}
 \le \frac{\log(2d)}k+\frac{2L}k
                   +\frac{k}{d}\log(2ed/k).
\]
The left side is nonnegative by the formula for the leading coefficient as a circle integral.
Taking \(k=\lfloor \tau d\rfloor\) for each fixed \(0<\tau<1\), then sending \(\tau\) to zero, proves the limit when \(L=o(d)\).
For the lower bound take any \(0<\tau<1/2\) with \(\tau\log(2e/\tau)\le\epsilon/8\).
When \(k=\lfloor\tau d\rfloor\ge\tau d/2\) and \(\log(2d)/k\le\epsilon/8\), the endpoint hypothesis makes the left side at least \(\epsilon/2\), so \(L/d\ge\tau\epsilon/16\).
All constants and the degree test in this implication depend only on \(\epsilon\).

<a id="relative"></a>
## The quadratic extension of the trace field

Suppose now that \(P\) is irreducible and reciprocal, \(M(P)>1\), \(d=2m\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) for a root \(\lambda\), \(t=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\), and \(B=\mathbb Q(t)\).
The relative degree is two by the distinct inverse pairs, and \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) for the trace minimal polynomial \(S\).
The relative discriminant ideal \(\mathfrak d_{K/B}\) divides the order discriminant \((t^2-4)\) of \(1,\lambda\).
Indeed this is the determinant of the matrix of the two relative embeddings squared, and at each prime a change from a local integral basis multiplies its discriminant by the square of the integral index.
Thus, with \(E=S(2)S(-2)\),
\[
             {\rm N}_{B/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{K/B})
                  \ \le\ |E|=|P(1)P(-1)|.
\]
The absolute discriminant tower formula gives
\[
       \frac{\operatorname{rd}(K)}{\operatorname{rd}(B)}
            ={\rm N}_{B/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{K/B})^{1/(2m)},
       \qquad \operatorname{rd}(T)=|\operatorname{disc}(T)|^{1/[T:\mathbb Q]} .
\]
Consequently this ratio tends to one on every growing-degree sequence of these inputs with absolute height tending to zero, with any number of exterior roots.
If the ratio is at least \(e^\epsilon\), the finite lower bound above applies directly.

<a id="signed"></a>
## Balanced blocks and the sign of the trace discriminant

Return to the compressed one-block case with only one nonreal conjugate pair exterior and \(m\ge3\).
If the [imaginary quadratic field \(F\)](Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#field) exists, use the ramification quantity \(W(B,F)\) of the [local endpoint estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v69.md#growth).
It is at most \(\log|E|\).
In particular any fixed lower bound \(W(B,F)/m\ge\epsilon>0\) forces a positive absolute height bound, using \(d=2m\).
More explicitly, for each rational prime \(p\) ramified in \(F\), set \(a_p=\sum_{e_v\ {\rm odd}} f_v\) over the places \(v\) of \(B\) above \(p\).
One has
\[
          m-a_p\ \le\ 2\sum_{v\mid p}(e_v-1)f_v
                         \ \le\ 2v_p(\operatorname{disc} B).
\]
For odd \(e_v\), the contribution on the left is \((e_v-1)f_v\), and for even \(e_v\) one has \(e_v\le2(e_v-1)\).
For the second inequality the local different exponent is at least \(e_v-1\).
This follows by passing to the maximal unramified intermediate local field and using the derivative of an Eisenstein uniformizer polynomial of degree \(e_v\): every derivative term has valuation at least \(e_v-1\), and the unramified step has trivial different.
Since \(a_p\log p\le W(B,F)\) also at 2, on every growing-degree balanced sequence of absolute heights tending to zero one must have \(v_p(\operatorname{disc} B)\ge (m-o(m))/2\) simultaneously for all \(p\) ramified in \(F\).
Every imaginary quadratic field has a ramified finite prime.

In the unbalanced case of odd \(m\), if the signed group is proper in the full signed permutation group then [the sign character test](Yin_C_G0345_v70.md#discriminant) gives \(E/\operatorname{disc}(S)\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}\).
The polynomial discriminant and \(\operatorname{disc}(B)\) differ by the square of the order index.
Hence the squarefree part of \(|\operatorname{disc}(B)|\), meaning the product of primes of odd valuation, divides \(|E|\).
In this branch the log of that squarefree part is \(o(m)\) whenever the absolute heights tend to zero and \(m\to\infty\).

For example, on a growing odd-\(m\) sequence with squarefree \(|\operatorname{disc}(B)|\) and absolute heights tending to zero the signed group must eventually be the full group.
The balanced case is excluded by the ramified prime bound.
To see the unbalanced obstruction, the trace field has \(m-2\) real embeddings and one complex place.
In its real and complex embedding space the lattice of integers has covolume \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(B)|}/2\), by changing from the two conjugate rows to their real and imaginary parts.
The open body \(\sum_i |x_i|+2|z|<m\) contains no nonzero integer of this lattice by the norm and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
The half-scale body has disjoint lattice translates, since a difference of its points lies in the open body.
Its volume is therefore at most the covolume.
Integration over the real absolute values and the complex radius gives volume \(2^{m-3}\pi m^m/m!\) for the original body.
This yields \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(B)|}\ge (\pi/4)m^m/m!\), exponential in \(m\).
With squarefree field discriminant this contradicts the \(o(m)\) bound for its logarithm in the proper unbalanced case.
The full signed group itself is compatible with the endpoint and discriminant tests proved here.
