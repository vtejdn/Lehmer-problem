# A primitive absolute mean below \(4/\pi\) for reciprocal polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## A gap at the full cyclotomic degree

Fix integers \(r\ge1,\ H\ge0\) and a real number \(0\le B<4/\pi\).
Suppose \(n\ge2\) has at most \(r\) distinct prime divisors and \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) is nonzero with even degree \(d\le\varphi(n)+H\).
Assume \(P\) is reciprocal, so \(X^dP(1/X)=P(X)\).
If its [primitive absolute mean](Yin_C_G0345_v83.md#statement) satisfies \(U_n(P)\le B\), then
\[
                 M(P)=1\quad\text{or}\quad
                 \log M(P)\ge\eta(B,r,H)>0 .
\]
This includes irreducible reciprocal polynomials at \(d=\varphi(n)\).

The threshold comes from a statement about cyclotomic integers.
Write \(\zeta_n=e^{2\pi i/n}\) and average the absolute values of an element of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)\) over the primitive substitutions \(\zeta_n\mapsto\zeta_n^u\), \((u,n)=1\).
For every \(K\ge1\) and \(B<4/\pi\), there is a bound \(C(K,B)\) with the following property.
If \(\alpha\) is a sum of at most \(K\) signed powers of \(\zeta_n\) with this mean at most \(B\), then
\[
             \alpha=\zeta_n^a\beta,\qquad
             \beta\in\mathbb Z[\zeta_c],\quad c\mid n,\quad c\le C(K,B).
\]
If \(\alpha\) is real, one can take \(a=0\).
This statement on signed sums allows arbitrary conductors.

<a id="limit"></a>
## Limits of primitive characters on a fixed number of frequencies

To prove the bounded-conductor assertion, suppose there is a sequence on which the least admissible \(c\) tends to infinity, using the real conclusion in the real case.
Discard zero sums, for which \(c=1\) works.
Pass to a subsequence on which the number \(k\le K\) of summands and their signs \(\epsilon_j\) are fixed.
Let \(f_i:\mathbb Z^k\to\mathbb Z/n_i\mathbb Z\) send the \(j\)-th basis vector to the exponent of the \(j\)-th summand.
By a diagonal subsequence, for every \(w\in\mathbb Z^k\) the indicator of \(f_i(w)=0\) is eventually constant.
The eventual relations form a subgroup \(\Lambda\), and we put \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^k/\Lambda\).
Since \(\Lambda\) is finitely generated, for all sufficiently large \(i\) the maps \(f_i\) factor through maps \(\rho_i:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z/n_i\mathbb Z\).

Write \(\Gamma=T\oplus\mathbb Z^s\) with \(T\) finite.
Its nonzero torsion elements survive under \(\rho_i\) simultaneously for large \(i\), so \(T\) embeds in a finite cyclic group.
Thus \(T\) is cyclic of order \(c\) and \(c\mid n_i\).
An element of order \(m\) in \(T\) has exact image order \(m\) eventually.
An element with nonzero free component has image order tending to infinity, since bounded image orders along a subsequence would make one fixed positive multiple vanish there.

A uniform primitive substitution gives a random character \(\chi_i(w)=\zeta_{n_i}^{u\rho_i(w)}\) of \(\Gamma\).
For \(m=\operatorname{ord}(\rho_i(w))\), its expectation is \(\mu(m)/\varphi(m)\).
Indeed reduction of units modulo \(m\mid n_i\) is onto, and summing all primitive \(m\)-th roots gives \(\mu(m)\) by Möbius inversion.
Consequently these character laws converge to the product of the uniform measure on faithful characters of \(T\) and Haar measure on the \(s\)-torus, with the trivial character used when \(c=1\).
The character expectations agree in the limit on \(T\) and tend to zero at every nonzero free frequency because \(\varphi(m)\to\infty\) with \(m\).
Finite linear combinations of characters are uniformly dense in the continuous functions on the compact dual of \(\Gamma\), which gives the claimed convergence of integrals.

Let \(F\) be the Laurent polynomial on this dual given by \(\sum_j\epsilon_j\chi(e_j)\), where \(e_j\) denotes the image of the original basis vector.
In the chosen splitting write it as
\[
                        F(\tau,z)=\sum_v h_v(\tau) z^v .
\]
Each \(h_v\) is an integer combination of characters evaluated at elements of \(T\), hence its values at faithful \(\tau\) are the full Galois conjugates, counted once per embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_c)\), of a cyclotomic integer.
Remove terms whose coefficient vanishes at a faithful character, since it then vanishes at them all.
The remaining support is common to all faithful \(\tau\).
The limiting mean of \(|F|\) is at most \(B\) by convergence and the original primitive-mean bound.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Two extreme frequencies cost at least \(4/\pi\)

Suppose there are at least two remaining free frequencies.
Choose a real linear functional taking distinct values on this finite support and let \(v_-,v_+\) be its minimum and maximum.
Put \(w=v_+-v_-\ne0\).
For each faithful \(\tau\), multiply \(F(\tau,z)\) by \(z^{-v_-}\) and average its translates by the subgroup \(\{z:z^w=1\}\) of the torus.
The surviving characters have frequency in \(\mathbb Zw\), as follows by changing the integer basis so that \(w\) becomes a multiple of the first basis vector.
Among the shifted frequencies only \(0,w\) are such multiples by the choice of extremes.
The averaging contracts the integral of the absolute value, and \(z^w\) has uniform circle law under Haar measure.
Thus the conditional integral of \(|F|\) is at least
\[
      \int_0^{2\pi}|h_{v_-}(\tau)+h_{v_+}(\tau)e^{it}|
                         \,\frac{dt}{2\pi}
            \ \ge\ \frac{2}{\pi}
                     \bigl(|h_{v_-}(\tau)|+|h_{v_+}(\tau)|\bigr).
\]
For the last inequality rotate the two coefficients to nonnegative moduli \(a,b\) and use \(|a+be^{it}|\ge(a+b)|\cos(t/2)|\).
Each endpoint coefficient is a nonzero algebraic integer, so the product of its absolute conjugates is at least one.
Their arithmetic mean is therefore at least one at each endpoint.
The limiting integral would be at least \(4/\pi\), contradicting \(B<4/\pi\).

If the support is empty, the sums are zero for all sufficiently large \(i\), since the actual restriction of \(\chi_i\) to \(T\) is faithful even at the substitution \(u=1\).
For a single free frequency \(v\), evaluation there gives a power of \(\zeta_{n_i}\) times a coefficient in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_c]\).
When the original samples are real, their Galois conjugates are real as complex conjugation commutes with the primitive substitutions.
The integral of \(|\operatorname{Im} F|^2\) is consequently zero in the limit.
Continuity makes \(F(\tau,z)\) real throughout every faithful torus fiber.
A nonzero single term \(h_v(\tau)z^v\) can have this property only for \(v=0\).
Thus the fixed \(c\) works in either version of the assertion, contrary to its supposed unbounded minimum.

<a id="lift"></a>
## Recovering a reciprocal polynomial at the interpolation degree

If the claimed gap failed, a sequence would have \(1<M(P_i)\to1\).
Its degrees \(d_i\) tend to infinity, since bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound every integer coefficient.
The degree assumption then gives \(\varphi(n_i)\to\infty\), and the [fractional-margin gap](Yin_C_G0345_v86.md#statement) gives \(d_i/\varphi(n_i)\to1\).
The [bounded signed representation](Yin_C_G0345_v87.md#statement) applied to \(P_i(\zeta_{n_i})\) also gives one of bounded length for the centered value \(\alpha_i=\zeta_{n_i}^{-d_i/2}P_i(\zeta_{n_i})\).
Reciprocity makes \(\alpha_i\) real, and its mean is \(U_{n_i}(P_i)\).
By the bounded-conductor assertion we may pass to a subsequence and fix a divisor \(c\mid n_i\) with \(\alpha_i\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_c)^+\) for every \(i\).

Pass again to a subsequence on which the number of distinct primes of \(n_i\) is fixed and each ordered prime divisor is either fixed or tends to infinity.
Enlarge \(c\) by taking its least common multiple with the product of all the fixed primes.
It remains fixed, divides \(n_i\), and contains every fixed prime divisor.
Euler's product then gives \(\varphi(n_i)\sim n_i\varphi(c)/c\), since there are at most \(r\) other factors and each tends to one.

For \(c=1,2\) the centered values are rational constants.
For \(c\ge3\) the real cyclotomic field is generated by \(y=\zeta_c+\zeta_c^{-1}\) and has degree \(\varphi(c)/2\), since \(\zeta_c\) is a nonreal root of \(X^2-yX+1\).
In its rational power basis every \(\alpha_i\) has a representative of degree at most \(\varphi(c)/2-1\) in \(y\).
Expand that representative at \(y=X^{n_i/c}+X^{-n_i/c}\) to obtain a Laurent polynomial \(A_i\) over \(\mathbb Q\) with boundedly many terms and \(A_i(\zeta_{n_i})=\alpha_i\).
For \(c=1,2\) use the constant representative.
For \(c\ge3\) its largest absolute exponent is at most \((\varphi(c)/2-1)n_i/c<d_i/2\) eventually, since \(d_i\sim\varphi(c)n_i/c\).
It follows that \(S_i=X^{d_i/2}A_i\) is an ordinary polynomial of degree at most \(d_i\) and
\[
                      P_i=S_i+\Phi_{n_i} Q_i
\]
for a rational polynomial \(Q_i\) which is zero or has degree at most \(H\).
The factorization \(\Phi_n(X)=\prod_{e\mid n}(X^{n/e}-1)^{\mu(e)}\) has at most \(2^r\) nontrivial factors.
Multiply by the product \(D_i\) of its denominator binomials.
Both \(D_i\) and \(D_i\Phi_{n_i}\) have bounded term count and \(M(D_i)=1\).
The displayed decomposition bounds the term count of the nonzero integer polynomial \(D_iP_i\), irrespective of the rational coefficients of its two summands.
The [bounded-term Mahler theorem](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) now contradicts \(1<M(D_iP_i)=M(P_i)\to1\).

<a id="sharp"></a>
## Primitive samples at the endpoint

At odd primes \(p\to\infty\), the real cyclotomic integers \(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\) have unbounded degree \((p-1)/2\).
Their primitive mean is
\[
        \frac{2\csc(\pi/(2p))-2}{p-1}
           =\frac4\pi-\frac{2-4/\pi}{p-1}+O(p^{-2}) .
\]
Indeed the sum of \(|\cos(2\pi j/p)|\) over the full grid equals the sum of \(\cos(\pi j/p)\) over the centered representatives \(|j|\le(p-1)/2\), by permuting residues modulo \(p\) and taking absolute values.
The latter finite geometric sum is \(\csc(\pi/(2p))\), and removing \(j=0\) gives the formula.
These samples are centered values at full degree for the monic reciprocal polynomials \(\Phi_p(X)+X^{(p-1)/2-1}+X^{(p-1)/2+1}\) when \(p\ge5\).
Thus the bounded-conductor assertion for real residues fails at the cutoff \(B=4/\pi\), even though the means approach it from below.
The Mahler-gap question at that endpoint for arbitrary bounded numbers of prime divisors remains open in this argument.
