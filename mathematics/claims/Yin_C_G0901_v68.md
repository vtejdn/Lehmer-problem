# A Lehmer gap at bounded multiplicative rank of the conjugate arguments

<a id="statement"></a>
## The number of independent arguments must grow on a shrinking sequence

Let \(w\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), with conjugates \(\Omega\) at a fixed complex embedding, and write \(m(w)=d h(w)\) for its total logarithmic Mahler measure.
Let \(k\) be the rank, modulo roots of unity, of the multiplicative group generated by \(a/|a|\) for \(a\in\Omega\).
Equivalently, these arguments span a space of dimension \(k\) over \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Q)\).
Let \(V\) be the rational multiplicative span of \(\Omega\) modulo torsion and write \(b=\dim_{\mathbb Q}V\).
Then for every fixed \(K\ge0\) there is an effective positive lower bound for \(m(w)\) over all inputs with \(k\le K\).
There is no restriction on \(d\), the number of real embeddings, or the rational rank of the log moduli.

More precisely, put \(c_0=\tfrac12\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)>0\).
If \(m(w)<c_0\), then \(k\ge1\), \(b<8k^2\), and after replacing each class of conjugates with root-of-unity quotients by the product of its members, the resulting unit \(w_0\) satisfies
\[
        m(w_0)=m(w),\quad k(w_0)=k,\quad
        [\mathbb Q(w_0):\mathbb Q]\le3^{b^2}<3^{64k^4}.                  \tag{1}
\]
In particular every sequence of positive measures tending to zero has \(k\to\infty\); its compressed degrees \(d_0\) tend to infinity and require \(k>(\log d_0/(64\log3))^{1/4}\) eventually.
The gap still leaves units of unbounded argument rank.

<a id="selection"></a>
## Producing a unit whose real conjugates reflect fixed vectors

Let \(L\) be the splitting field, \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\), and \(c\) ordinary complex conjugation.
The vectors \((1-c)[a]\) are the classes of \(a/c(a)=(a/|a|)^2\).
Their span is the negative eigenspace of \(c\) on \(V\), of dimension \(k\).
This is complementary to the [fixed subspace describing the log moduli](Yin_C_G0901_v62.md#modules).
Use a \(G\)-invariant positive definite rational inner product on \(V\), obtained by averaging.

For any nonzero rational \(v\in V\), select from \(\Omega\) the \(a\) with \((v,[a])>0\).
Since \(\sum_{a\in\Omega}[a]=0\) by the unit norm and the classes span \(V\), both strict signs occur.
Use \(v\) or \(-v\) so that the positive subset has at most \(d/2\) members.
Its product \(z\in L\) is nontorsion: the pairing of its class with this signed choice of \(v\) is strictly positive.
In particular \(h(z)\le m(w)/2\).
Every \(s\in G\) fixing \(v\) permutes the actual selected conjugates and fixes their product as an algebraic number, even in the presence of torsion collisions.
Thus whenever \(g^{-1}cg\) fixes \(v\), \(g(z)\) is real.
Proportions computed with uniform \(g\in G\) give proportions of embeddings of the field generated by \(z\).
If this proportion is at least \(1/2\), the [real-conjugate height bound](../audits/literature/Gar07/Yin_U001.md#real-conjugates) gives
\[
         h(z)\ \ge\ \tfrac14\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4),
             \qquad m(w)\ge c_0.
\]

<a id="overlap"></a>
## The trace of two projections is quantized

Consider a nonzero rational irreducible submodule \(W\) of \(V\).
Write \(r=\dim W\), \(j=\dim W^{-c}\), and use the invariant inner product on \(W\).
If \(j=0\), every conjugate of \(c\) acts trivially on \(W\), so the preceding argument applies to any nonzero rational vector in \(W\).
Otherwise let \(P=(1-c)/2\) on \(W\), and \(P_g=(1-g^{-1}cg)/2\).
These are orthogonal projections of rank \(j\).
For each \(g\),
\[
                     4\operatorname{tr}(PP_g)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.         \tag{2}
\]
Indeed expansion gives traces of the identity and three group elements; every such trace is rational and an algebraic integer because the eigenvalues are roots of unity.
Also \(\operatorname{tr}(PP_g)=\operatorname{tr}(PP_gP)\) is the sum of the squared lengths of the projections by \(P_g\) of an orthonormal basis of \(PW\).
In particular, a zero trace says that \(g^{-1}cg\) fixes \(PW\) pointwise.

The average \(\mathbb E_g P_g\) is \((j/r)I\) on \(W\).
To see this also for a rational irreducible module that splits over \(\mathbb C\), the average is central and acts on each complex irreducible summand by a scalar.
Its trace there equals the dimension of the negative eigenspace of \(c\) on that summand, so the scalar is rational.
The average is a rational matrix, and the eigenspaces for these rational scalars are rational invariant subspaces.
Rational irreducibility gives a single eigenvalue; taking the trace proves the assertion.
It follows from (2) that
\[
         \Pr_g(P_gP\ne0)\le4\,\mathbb E_g\operatorname{tr}(PP_g)
                                      =4j^2/r .
\]
If \(r\ge8j^2\), at least half of the conjugates of \(c\) fix all of \(PW\).
A nonzero rational \(v\in PW\) and the selected product above therefore give \(m(w)\ge c_0\).
More generally, if \(p=1-4j^2/r>0\), the same proof and the cited height bound give \(m(w)\ge p\operatorname{arsinh}(2^{-1/p})\).

Decompose \(V\) as a direct sum of rational irreducibles by complete reducibility, counting multiplicities.
Below \(c_0\), every constituent has \(j\ge1\) and \(r<8j^2\).
Summation and \(\sum j=k\) give \(b<8\sum j^2\le8k^2\).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Removing torsion collisions and making the bound effective

Two conjugates belong to the same class if their ratio is a root of unity.
These classes are permuted transitively, hence all have the same size \(e\).
Their products are distinct even modulo torsion, since their classes in \(V\) equal \(e[a]\) for any member of the corresponding class.
They form the full orbit of \(w_0\).
All members of each class have the same absolute value, so taking the product preserves the total logarithmic measure counted over the orbit.
The rational conjugate span and its \(c\)-action do not change: these are the same vectors up to the common nonzero scalar \(e\).
These properties are also the [torsion-class reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).

The finite group induced on \(V\) permutes the integral span of its classes, a lattice of rank \(b\).
It acts faithfully on this lattice by definition of the induced group.
The orbit of the classes of the compressed roots is an orbit for this finite subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}_b(\mathbb Z)\), and its size is \(d_0\).
Reduction modulo three is injective on every finite subgroup, as in the [finite integral matrix bound](Yin_C_G0345_v67.md#field), whence its order is at most \(3^{b^2}\).
This proves (1).
For \(K\ge1\), below \(c_0\) one need only use a degree bound \(B=3^{64K^4}\).
List the monic integer polynomials with degree at most \(B\), constant term \(\pm1\), and coefficients of order \(i\) bounded by \(2\binom{B}{i}\).
This contains the relevant compressed minima, since \(\exp(c_0)<2\).
The minimum of \(c_0\) and the positive total measures in that finite list is effective by root isolation and algebraic comparison of multiplicative measures, ignoring zero measures.
For \(K=0\), the selected-product proof already gives \(c_0\).
Bounded-degree finiteness and (1) now give the conclusions for any shrinking sequence.
