# Power-size dyadic factor degrees at the exact real pole after releasing one denominator prime

<a id="statement"></a>
## Every conjugate can have large local degree

Let \(q\) be any sufficiently large multiple of four, \(\ell\) any odd prime and \(B\ge1\) any integer fixed before the degree is chosen.
Put \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ u=2^B,\ \theta=u\log p/\log s\).
There are arbitrarily large \(d=2D\) and monic irreducible reciprocal \(F\in\mathbb Z[1/\ell][Z]\) with all the conclusions of the [one-prime period and real-pole construction](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#statement) and the following stronger degree distribution.
For an absolute \(c>0\),
\[
        [\mathbb Q_2(z):\mathbb Q_2]\ge c d^{1/4}
        \quad\text{for every root }z,\qquad
        \#\{z:[\mathbb Q_2(z):\mathbb Q_2]<c\sqrt d\}
                     =O_{q,B}(\sqrt d).                                  \tag{1}
\]
All the local extensions are unramified.
In particular the number of roots of local degree at most \(\tfrac12\log_2 d\) is zero at sufficiently large degrees.
One can choose \(q\to\infty\) and \(B\to\infty\) successively by taking \(d\) sufficiently large for each choice.
The exact archimedean signature consists of \(T,T^{-1}\) with \(1<T<s\) and all remaining roots of modulus one.
The pole is \(q^dF(s)=(2q+1)^2\), the pin is \(s-T\asymp q p^{-d}\) with absolute constants, and the full graph power congruence at precision \(2^B\) holds with the same exponents that give bounded real logarithmic error.
The primitive integer minimum has measure \(\ell^hT\) for the admissible sufficiently large denominator exponents of the [one-prime approximation](Yin_C_G0901_v172.md#statement).
Thus (1) tests what survives all these conditions when integrality at \(\ell\) is released.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Three Frobenius orbit lengths with sufficiently many choices

Take the progression \(D_0\bmod P\) of admissible half-degrees for the [prescribed pole and binary precision](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#period), where all prime divisors of \(P\) are at most \(p\).
Choose a prime \(t\) strictly between \(H=\max(31,q+B+2)\) and \(2H\) by Bertrand's inequality.
In particular \(t\) is prime to \(P\) and \(t<\theta\) for all sufficiently large \(q\), uniformly for \(B\ge1\).
Indeed \(\theta\ge2^B q\log(q+1)\) and \(2(q+B+2)\le C2^B q\) with an absolute \(C\).

Choose arbitrarily large \(a\equiv-1\bmod 2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot31\cdot(t-1)\) and put \(b=a+2\).
Then \(a,b\) are coprime odd integers, both prime to \(2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot31\).
Define \(m_1=2\cdot3^{j-1}\) and \(m_2=5\cdot31^{k-1}\) by choosing, in each progression, the largest term at most \(W=2^a/(100a^2)\).
For large \(a\), both exponents \(j,k\) are positive, \(W/3<m_1\le W,\ W/31<m_2\le W\), and
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{3^j}(2)=m_1,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{31^k}(2)=m_2 .
\]
Indeed \(2^2=1+3\) and \(2^5=1+31\), with respective orders two and five at the first prime powers.
For an odd prime \(r\), raising \(1+r^v w\) to the \(r\)-th power when \(v\ge1,\ r\nmid w\) raises the valuation of the difference from one to exactly \(v+1\), by the binomial expansion.
Raising it to any positive exponent prime to \(r\) preserves that valuation, giving both displayed orders.

Use the product
\[
           n=(2^a-1)(2^b-1)3^j31^k .
\]
Its four odd factors are pairwise coprime: the first two have gcd \(2^{\gcd(a,b)}-1=1\); neither of their exponents is divisible by two or five.
Also \(t\nmid n\), since \(\gcd(a,t-1)=\gcd(b,t-1)=1\) and the order of \(2\bmod t\) is greater than one.
The group of \(n\)-th roots in the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_2\) thus factors uniquely as the product of the four respective groups.
In its \((2^a-1)\)-component choose any element of full residue degree \(a\) and multiply by any primitive \(3^j\)-th root in the third component.
Call the resulting set \(P_1\).
Define \(P_2\) using elements of full degree \(b\) and primitive \(31^k\)-th roots in the two other components.
The sets \(P_1,P_2,P_1P_2\) are disjoint, and each is stable under squaring and inversion.
All elements in the respective sets have exact Frobenius orbit lengths
\[
           X=a m_1,\qquad Y=b m_2,\qquad L=XY .
\]
This follows by taking the least common multiple of the lengths in the distinct components, since \(a,b,m_1,m_2\) are pairwise coprime.
No orbit in any of these sets contains the inverse of its representative: projection to the full-degree component of odd degree \(a>1\) or \(b>1\) would make inversion a commuting involution on an odd Frobenius cycle.
Such an involution on the cycle is the identity, and in characteristic two an element equal to its inverse must be one.
Thus the Frobenius orbits paired by inversion have sizes \(2X,2Y,2L\).

The count of full-degree elements in \(\mathbb F_{2^w}\) is \((1-o(1))2^w\) as \(w\to\infty\): the proper subfields supply at most \(\sum_{h\le w/2}2^h\) elements.
There are exactly \(m_1\) primitive \(3^j\)-th roots and \(6m_2\) primitive \(31^k\)-th roots.
It follows that \(P_1\) supplies at least \(2^a/(4a)\) paired blocks, and \(P_2\) supplies at least \(2^b/(4b)\), for all large \(a\).
Also \(|P_1P_2|/n\to(2/3)(30/31)=20/31\), by the primitive fractions in the two prime-power components.
The mixed blocks include the block of an element of order exactly \(n\), using generators in all four components.

<a id="fill"></a>
## Preserving the bounded real-period error while avoiding small blocks

Choose \(S\) nearest to \(n/(2\theta)\) subject to \(tS\equiv D_0\bmod P\).
Then \(|S-n/(2\theta)|\le P/2\).
We shall select paired blocks of total size \(2S\).
Take \(J_0=\lfloor(S-L)/L\rfloor\) mixed blocks including one of order \(n\).
For large \(a\) this is possible since \(L\asymp 2^{2a}/a^2,\ n\asymp 2^{4a}/a^4\) with absolute comparison constants, and \(1/\theta<20/31\).
There remain \(2E\) positions with \(L\le E=S-J_0 L<2L\).
As \(\gcd(X,Y)=1\), choose \(0\le i<Y\) with \(Xi\equiv E\bmod Y\).
Then \(i'=(E-Xi)/Y\) satisfies \(0\le i'<2X\).
Take \(i\) blocks from \(P_1\) and \(i'\) from \(P_2\).
The preceding counts suffice since \(Y\le bW<2^a/(4a)\) and \(2X\le2aW<2^b/(4b)\) at large \(a\).
Their sizes realize \(E=Xi+Yi'\) without restricting \(S\) to a coarser progression.

The selected roots give a reciprocal Frobenius-stable set in \(\mu_n\).
Take their Teichmüller lifts and the monic product \(V\in\mathbb Z_2[Z]\), of degree \(2S\).
Use \(U(Z)=V(Z^t)\) with \(d=2tS\).
Then \(U\mid Z^{tn}-1\) over \(\mathbb Z_2\), its binary reduction is squarefree, and its full binary period is \(A=tn\) because the primitive \(n\)-th root in the selection has a primitive \(tn\)-th root as a preimage under the \(t\)-th power.
In particular \(|A-\theta d|\le\theta tP\).
All conditions for the [integer pole correction and lifting at the prescribed precision](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#lift) hold: \(t>q+B+2\), the required end coefficients vanish, the degree is admissible, and \(\gcd(t,n)=1\).
The resulting orbit has exactly the residue polynomial \(\bar U\) and the [archimedean and period estimates](Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#arch).
In particular \(u=2^B,\ v=A+u\) work modulo \((2^B,F)\), the logarithmic error at the real place is \(O_{q,B}(1)\), and the initial \(q/2+1\) Newton sums and all powered capacity conditions hold as in the cited construction.
The odd denominator keeps the characteristic-zero graph power identity from holding.

For a residue root \(\bar z\) of \(U\), its degree over \(\mathbb F_2\) is divisible by the degree of \(\bar z^t\).
The latter is \(X,Y\), or \(L\), hence at least \(\min(X,Y)\asymp 2^a/a\).
Every root of \(F\) generates an unramified extension of exactly its residue degree because its binary reduction is squarefree and the irreducible residue factors lift with the same degrees.
For fixed \(q,B\), \(d/n\to t/\theta<1\), so for all sufficiently large \(a\) we have \(d\le n\).
Therefore \(\min(X,Y)\ge c n^{1/4}\ge c d^{1/4}\) and \(L\ge c\sqrt n\ge c\sqrt d\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
Only the \(2tE<4t L=O_{q,B}(\sqrt d)\) preimages of the filling blocks could have degree below \(c\sqrt d\).
This proves (1).
