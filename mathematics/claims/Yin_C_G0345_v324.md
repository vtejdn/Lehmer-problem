# A two-thirds width for low-measure trace delays with unrestricted coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## A stronger comparison when the actual measure is bounded

Let \(b\) be a real-valued trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(1\le d<n\), and fix a real phase \(\phi\).
Use the [trace comparison notation](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#statement): \(\mu\) is the normalized mean of \(\log|2\cos(nt+\phi)-b(t)|\), and \(I\) the independent two-circle mean of \(\log|2\cos v-b(t)|\).
Fix \(A\ge0\).
Uniformly over all coefficients and phases with \(\mu\le A\), for \(1\le d\le n^{2/3}\) one has
\[
       |\mu-I|\le C_A\,\frac d n\big(\log(2n/d)+\sqrt d\big).       \tag{1}
\]
Thus the means converge together uniformly if \(d=o(n^{2/3})\).
The coefficients, the number of terms and the multiplicities of the roots are unrestricted.
The boundedness hypothesis here concerns the diagonal mean \(\mu\), the one that is small on a putative Lehmer sequence.

For an integral central trace \(b(t)=B(2\cos t)\), \(\phi=0\), and \(P=z^n(C_n-B)(z+z^{-1})\), this gives absolute \(c,\eta>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
          d\le c n^{2/3},\quad M(P)>1
                          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log M(P)\ge\eta.       \tag{2}
\]
A second comparison depending on amplitude gives the following height-bounded result, with no hypothesis on \(\mu\).
For any fixed \(H_0\ge1\) and \(\alpha\ge0\), if \(\|b\|_\infty\le H_0 n^\alpha\), then \(\mu-I\to0\) uniformly for \(d=o(n/\log(2n))\).
The same integral gap conclusion holds for \(d\le c_{H_0,\alpha}n/\log(2n)\), where \(c_{H_0,\alpha}>0\).
This applies, for instance, when each Laurent coefficient is bounded by a fixed power of \(n\), by increasing \(\alpha\) to allow the number of terms.

<a id="orders"></a>
## An effective derivative order on a fast period

Put \(D=n/d>1\), \(H=\|b\|_\infty\) and
\[
              h=\max(1,\lceil\log(4\max(1,H))/\log D\rceil).
\]
By repeated [Bernstein inequalities](../audits/literature/QZ19/Yin_U001.md#qz19-u001), in the normalized fast variable \(s=nt+\phi\), the derivatives of \(b((s-\phi)/n)\) of orders \(h,h+1\) have absolute value at most \(HD^{-h}\le1/4\).
Partition any full fast period into a bounded absolute number of intervals where either the \(h\)-th or the \((h+1)\)-st derivative of \(2\cos s\) has absolute value at least one.
This is possible with an absolute number of pieces, since the two derivatives are sine and cosine up to sign.
On each such interval the corresponding derivative of the difference \(f=2\cos(nt+\phi)-b\), taken in the fast variable, has absolute value at least \(3/4\).

For clarity, if a real \(k\)-th derivative is of magnitude at least \(3/4\) on an interval, the length \(m\) on which its underlying function has magnitude at most \(v\) is at most \(C k v^{1/k}\).
For \(m>0\) one can pick \(k+1\) points of the closed sublevel set separated by at least \(m/(2k)\): starting at the leftmost point and then picking the next point after that separation, exhaustion using at most \(k\) such points would cover the set by intervals of total length at most \(m/2\).
Writing the divided difference at those points as a sum, its absolute value is at most \(v(2k/m)^k 2^k/k!\), by the factorial spacing bounds to both sides of each point.
The generalized mean-value theorem for divided differences, obtained by subtracting the interpolant and applying Rolle's theorem \(k\) times, gives the asserted estimate.
In particular on every fast period the relative measure with \(|f|\le v\le1\) is at most \(\min(1,C(h+1)v^{1/(h+1)})\).
Integration against \(dv/v\) from zero to one gives the uniform cost
\[
                        O(h\log(2h)) .                            \tag{3}
\]
Here it is important to integrate the minimum with one: the two regimes meet at \(v=(C(h+1))^{-(h+1)}\).

Use now the [period count](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#periods).
Only \(O(d)\) periods need the cost (3); on every other period the actual logarithmic loss after clipping at \(0<u<1\) is \(O(\sqrt u)\) in its own normalized mean.
The [clipped quadrature estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#quadrature) and the independent two-circle tail then give, with \(u=D^{-2}\),
\[
                |\mu-I|\ \ll\
          D^{-1}\big(\log(2D)+h\log(2h)\big).                    \tag{4}
\]
The [term-count bound](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#runs) may replace \(h\log(2h)\) by the actual number of Fourier terms when that is smaller.

<a id="amplitude"></a>
## Controlling the amplitude by the independent mean

The quadratic Jensen identity gives \(I=\int g(b(t))\,dt/(2\pi)\), with \(g(x)=\operatorname{arcosh}(|x|/2)\) for \(|x|>2\) and zero otherwise.
Hence \(I\ge0\) and \(\int\log^+|b(t)|\,dt/(2\pi)\le I+\log2\).
For \(p(z)=z^d b(t)\) on \(z=e^{it}\), a polynomial of degree at most \(2d\), write \(M_r=\max_{|z|=r}|p(z)|\).
Exterior maximum modulus applied to the reversed polynomial gives \(H=M_1\le r^{-2d}M_r\) for \(0<r<1\).
The logarithm of the modulus is subharmonic, and the Poisson integral of the continuous nonnegative function \(\log^+|p|\) majorizes it on the disk.
Thus \(\log M_r\le(1+r)(I+\log2)/(1-r)\).
Choose \(r=e^{-s}\) with \(s=\min(1,\sqrt{(1+I)/d})\); since \((1+r)/(1-r)\ll1/s\),
\[
      \log(4\max(1,H))\ \ll\ 1+I+\sqrt{d(1+I)}.                  \tag{5}
\]
If \(p\) is identically zero, the same conclusion is immediate.
This estimate uses the independent logarithmic mean despite the possible very large pointwise coefficients.

<a id="feedback"></a>
## Closing the estimate from the diagonal measure alone

The polynomial \(z^n f(t)\) on \(|z|=1\) has degree \(2n\) and leading coefficient of modulus one.
Its coefficients at the interior frequencies are those of \(-b\), and its logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\mu\).
Each coefficient has modulus at most \(2^{2n}e^\mu\), by expansion in elementary symmetric functions of its roots.
Adding the \(2d+1\) possible coefficients of \(b\) gives \(\log(4\max(1,H))\ll_A n\) when \(\mu\le A\).
This rough bound is used only to infer \(\log(2h)\ll_A\log(2n)\) when \(d\le n^{2/3}\).
In that range \(\log D\ge(\log n)/3\).
From the definition of \(h\) it follows that
\[
 h\log(2h)\ll_A\log(2D)+\log(4\max(1,H)).
\]
Combining (4) and (5) yields
\[
 I\le A+
 C_A D^{-1}\{\log(2D)+1+I+\sqrt{d(1+I)}\}.                     \tag{6}
\]
For \(n\) sufficiently large, \(C_A/D\le1/4\), and \(\sqrt d/D=d^{3/2}/n\le1\).
The term \(C_A\sqrt{1+I}\) on the right can be bounded by \(I/4+O_A(1)\).
It follows that \(I=O_A(1)\), uniformly despite the absence of an initial bound on \(I\).
The finitely many smaller \(n\) are harmless by the preceding elementary coefficient bound.
Returning to (4) and (5) with this uniform estimate proves (1).

In (2) it suffices to consider \(\mu\le1\), since all larger values already give a positive logarithmic gap.
A noncyclotomic integral trace input has \(I\ge c_2>0\) by [the trace-quadratic height bound and its zero cases](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#height).
With \(d\le c n^{2/3}\), the \(\sqrt d\) term in (1) contributes at most \(C_1c^{3/2}\), while \(D^{-1}\log(2D)\to0\) uniformly.
Take \(c\) sufficiently small to leave, for example, \(c_2/2\) as a positive floor.

<a id="bounded-height"></a>
## Polynomial amplitude and a nearly linear width

For \(H\le H_0 n^\alpha\), if \(D\ge\log(2n)\) and \(n\) is large, the definition gives \(h\le1+C_{H_0,\alpha}\log(2n)/\log D\).
In this range \(\log(2h)=O_{H_0,\alpha}(\log\log(3n))\) and \(\log D\ge\log\log(2n)\).
Consequently \(h\log(2h)=O_{H_0,\alpha}(\log(2n))\).
Formula (4) proves uniform convergence when \(D/\log(2n)\to\infty\).
For \(D\ge \log(2n)/c_{H_0,\alpha}\) it bounds the discrepancy by \(O_{H_0,\alpha}(c_{H_0,\alpha})\) with an implied constant independent of the chosen width multiplier.
Using the same \(I\ge c_2\) and taking the multiplier sufficiently small proves the height-bounded gap.

These bounds do not treat all growing central widths or all bounded-measure inputs.
For example the [Taylor family](Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#asymptotic-growth) that obstructs a coefficient-independent comparison at square-root width violates the present bounded diagonal-measure hypothesis.
Every monic reciprocal unit polynomial of even degree \(2n\) has such a trace expansion with width at most \(n-1\); proving the result when the width constraint approaches that full range would require additional control.
