# Squarefreeness above the delay in the radical indices of the integer Salem families

<a id="statement"></a>
## The large-prime part of a radical index

Fix integers \(a\ge3,\ n\ge7\) and \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\).
Let \(r>1\) be the exterior Salem root of \(G(X)=X^n(X-a)+\epsilon(aX-1)\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(r)\), and take all real radicals to be positive.
For every prime \(p>n\),
\[
       r^{1/p}\in K\ \Longrightarrow\ r^{1/p}<p,\quad a<1+p^p,
       \qquad r^{1/p^2}\notin K.                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(r^{1/q}\in K\) for an integer \(q\ge1\), then \(v_p(q)\le1\) at every \(p>n\).
This applies to the largest field radical index \(R\), so its part supported on primes larger than \(n\) is squarefree.

<a id="prime"></a>
## A sharper upper bound for the first radical

Suppose \(\beta=r^{1/p}\in K\), put \(L=n-1\), and let \(U\) be the product of the linear factors of \(X^L-\epsilon\) at \(1,-1\).
Write \(A=(X^L-\epsilon)/U\) and \(D=\deg A=[K:\mathbb Q]-2\ge4\).
By the [small resultant calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#resultant), \(\beta\) is Salem, and \(|N_{K/\mathbb Q}A(\beta)|\) divides \(1\) when \(U\ne1\) and divides \(4\) when \(U=1\).
These norms are nonzero.
If a neutral conjugate has a lift matching a root of \(A\) in the [angular pairing](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#angles), the [matched norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v687.md#norm) gives \(\beta<L<p\).
Also \(\beta<2\) immediately gives \(\beta<p\).

Assume now that \(\beta\ge2\) and none of the lifts matches.
For every neutral conjugate \(w\) of \(\beta\), the unmatched lift \(\rho\) satisfies \(|\rho^L-\epsilon|\ge4/p\) and \(|w^L-\rho^L|\le\pi/(ap)\).
Consequently
\[
                         |w^L-\epsilon|>\frac{8}{3p},
\]
since \(a\ge3\) and \(\pi<4\).
Put \(x=\beta^{-1}\le1/2\).
Reciprocity of \(A\) up to sign gives \(|A(\beta)A(x)|=\beta^D|A(x)|^2\).

When \(U\ne1\) it is \(X-1,\ X+1\) or \(X^2-1\), each of modulus at most \(2\) on the unit circle.
In the positive-sign cases \(A(x)\) is a geometric sum with constant term one and nonnegative terms.
In the negative-sign case with \(U\ne1\) one has \(U=X+1\) and \(A(x)=(1+x^L)/(1+x)\ge2/3\).
Thus \(A(x)\ge2/3\) throughout these cases, and \(|A(w)|>4/(3p)\) at all \(D\) neutral conjugates.
The norm-one identity forces
\[
                 1>\beta^D(2/3)^2(4/(3p))^D,\qquad
                 \beta<\frac{3p}{4}(3/2)^{2/D}
                        \le\frac{3p}{4}\sqrt{3/2}<p .
\]

When \(U=1\), \(A=X^L+1\), so \(A(x)>1\) and \(|A(w)|>8/(3p)\).
The norm upper bound gives \(\beta<(3p/8)4^{1/D}\le(3p/8)\sqrt2<p\).
This proves the prime bound in every case.
The [exterior root interval](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) gives \(a-1<r=\beta^p<p^p\).

<a id="square"></a>
## Excluding the second level

If \(\gamma=r^{1/p^2}\in K\), then \(\beta=\gamma^p\in K\).
Put \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
The [prime-power height estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v698.md#statement) gives \(\log\gamma\ge0.427800\,d/(n+1)\) since \(p\ge11\).
The [Salem cofactor degree](Yin_C_G0345_v681.md#roots) satisfies \(d\ge n-1\), so \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\).
It follows that
\[
                       \log\beta=p\log\gamma>p/4>\log p .
\]
For the last inequality \(t/4-\log t\) is increasing for \(t\ge11\), and \(\sum_{j=0}^4(11/4)^j/j!>11\) gives \(\log11<11/4\).
This contradicts the first part of (1).
The same inequalities cover \(n=7,\epsilon=1\) and \(n=9,\epsilon=1,p=11\), since they use only \(D\ge4\) and \(d/(n+1)\ge3/4\).
Finally \(p^2\mid q\) would give \(r^{1/p^2}=(r^{1/q})^{q/p^2}\in K\), proving the squarefreeness statement.
