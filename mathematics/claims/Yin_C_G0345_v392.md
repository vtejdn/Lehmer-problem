# Which powers of a Salem input realize half branch mass

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite residue-order criterion for every power

Let \(\sigma>1\) be a Salem number of degree \(2s\ge4\), with minimal polynomial \(P(z)=z^sQ(z+z^{-1})\).
For \(k\ge1\), let \(Q_k\) be the monic trace minimal polynomial of \(\sigma^k\).
Call \(k\) admissible here if there are actual even-degree Pisot inputs of degrees tending to infinity, with constant term the square of their degree, whose full return has exterior root \(\sigma^k\) and whose total neutral branch mass tends to \(1/2\).
The [fixed-input classification](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v200.md#gap) says that admissibility is equivalent to \(4\mid Q_k(0)\) or to both \(Q_k(0)\) and \(Q_k'(0)\) being even.

For each odd \(n\ge1\), put \(w_n\) equal to half the number of roots of \(\bar P\) of multiplicative order exactly \(n\) over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), counted with multiplicity.
These weights are nonnegative integers with finite support and sum \(s\).
Put \(u_k=\sum_{n\mid k}w_n\).
Then \(k\) is admissible exactly in the following cases:
\[
   u_k\ge2,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
   u_k=w_1=1,\quad k\ {\rm odd},\quad 4\mid Q(0).                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular every Salem input has an admissible odd power.
If \(1\) is already admissible, its least admissible exponent is \(1\).
Otherwise that least exponent \(e(\sigma)\) is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{initial condition}& e(\sigma)&\text{sharp upper bound}\\ \hline
 Q(0)\ {\rm odd} & \min\{n>1:w_n\ge2\}&2^s+1\\
 Q(0)\equiv2\bmod4,\ Q'(0)\ {\rm odd}
                 & \min\{n>1:w_n>0\}&2^{s-1}+1 .
\end{array}                                                             \tag{2}
\]
Both bounds are attained by actual Salem numbers for every \(s\ge2\).

<a id="local"></a>
## The two possible simple zeros of the powered trace

All powers of a Salem number still have degree \(2s\).
Indeed, if two conjugates of \(\sigma\) had the same \(k\)-th power, a Galois automorphism taking one to the unique exterior conjugate \(\sigma\) would take the other to a conjugate with that same modulus and hence to \(\sigma\) itself.
The powers thus give precisely the conjugates, with one exterior root and the reciprocal interior root.
In particular \(Q_k\) has the \(s\) distinct values \(C_k(t)\) at the roots of \(Q\), where \(C_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\).

Over the residue field a trace \(t=z+z^{-1}\) is zero precisely at \(z=1\), and \(z+z^{-1}=(z-1)^2/z\) there.
At every other trace the two reciprocal preimages are distinct and have the same order.
Thus \(w_n\) counts precisely the trace roots of \(Q\) whose lift has order \(n\), with their original multiplicities.
The powered traces reducing to zero are exactly those whose order divides \(k\).
Consequently \(u_k\) is the multiplicity of zero in \(\overline{Q_k}\).
When \(u_k=0\) the constant is odd.
When \(u_k\ge2\) both inputs to the parity test are even.

If \(u_k=1\), exactly one simple residue trace has entered zero after powering, and Frobenius fixes it since it is unique.
The residue trace is therefore either zero or one.
Its simple root lifts by Hensel to \(t\in\mathbb Z_2\).
All other powered traces are units at two, so \(v_2(Q_k(0))=v_2(C_k(t))\), and \(Q_k'(0)\) is odd.
For residue zero we have \(w_1=1,\ v_2(t)=v_2(Q(0))\).
For odd \(k\), \(C_k(x)\) is odd with linear coefficient \((-1)^{(k-1)/2}k\), by the Chebyshev recurrence.
Hence \(v_2(C_k(t))=v_2(t)\).
For even \(k\), \(C_k(t)=C_{k/2}(t)^2-2\equiv2\bmod4\), since every \(C_j(t)\) with even \(t\) is even.
For residue one the inverse pair has order three, and \(3\mid k\).
The lift \(t\) is odd and \(C_3(t)=t(t^2-3)\) has valuation exactly one by reduction modulo eight.
Applying the preceding even-input computation to \(C_{k/3}(C_3(t))\) keeps that valuation equal to one for every \(k\).
These alternatives prove (1).

<a id="least"></a>
## The first successful exponent

When \(w_1=1\) and the original input is inadmissible, any additional positive order weight makes \(u_k\ge2\), giving the second row of (2).
Suppose instead \(Q(0)\) is odd, so \(w_1=0\).
An order \(n>1\) with \(w_n=1\) would give a single simple trace fixed by Frobenius.
The trace must be one, hence \(n=3\).
There cannot be two different orders of weight one.
It follows that \(u_k\ge2\) precisely when a divisor \(n\mid k\) has \(w_n\ge2\), and such a divisor exists for some \(k\) because \(s\ge2\).
This gives the first row.
In both rows any trace of residue degree \(f\) has inverse pair fixed as a set by the \(2^f\)-power Frobenius.
Thus the order \(n\) of either member divides \(2^f-1\) or \(2^f+1\).
Here \(f\le s\), and \(f\le s-1\) in the second row since the simple zero already consumes one degree.
This gives the upper bounds.

<a id="sharp"></a>
## Salem examples attaining both exponential bounds

For each \(f\ge1\), take a generator \(z\) of the order-\((2^f+1)\) subgroup of \(\mathbb F_{2^{2f}}^\times\) and put \(t=z+z^{-1}\).
Its trace lies in \(\mathbb F_{2^f}\) and has degree exactly \(f\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
Indeed for \(0<j<f\), neither \(2^j-1\) nor \(2^j+1\) is divisible by \(2^f+1\), so Frobenius to that power does not fix the inverse pair.
Write \(D_f\) for the irreducible polynomial of \(t\); its constant is nonzero.
The circle-coordinate roots above all conjugates of \(t\) have the same exact order \(2^f+1\).

For the odd-constant row prescribe the monic degree-\(s\) reduction \(D_s\bmod2\).
For the second row prescribe \(X D_{s-1}\bmod2\) and constant coefficient \(2\bmod4\).
In each case choose a monic integral \(H_0\) realizing the required coefficients modulo four and a monic irreducible degree-\(s\) polynomial modulo three by the Chinese remainder theorem.
For positive integers \(M\), put
\[
                        Q_M(X)=H_0(X)-12M C_{s-1}(X).
\]
It is irreducible by reduction modulo three and keeps the given dyadic data.
For all sufficiently large \(M\), it has a real root close to each of the \(s-1\) distinct real zeros of \(C_{s-1}\) inside \((-2,2)\).
This follows by opposite signs at the ends of disjoint small intervals around the simple zeros after division by \(-12M\).
The remaining root is real and tends to \(+\infty\), by the sum of the roots.
For this root \(T>2\), the discriminant \(T^2-4\) becomes negative at an embedding into \(\mathbb R\) sending \(T\) to an interior conjugate.
Thus \(z^2-Tz+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\), and its larger root is a Salem number of degree \(2s\).
In the first case all \(s\) trace weights are at order \(2^s+1\).
In the second case there is one zero trace weight and all \(s-1\) other weights are at \(2^{s-1}+1\).
Formula (2) proves sharpness in each degree.
The exterior traces in these examples can grow arbitrarily large.

<a id="changing"></a>
## Entropy cost when the Salem input changes

Suppose \(\sigma_j>1\) is any supplied sequence of Salem numbers tending to one.
There exist powers \(\sigma_j^{k_j}\) still tending to one for which each fixed powered input admits a half-mass limit if and only if
\[
                              e(\sigma_j)\log\sigma_j\longrightarrow0.   \tag{3}
\]
Necessity follows from \(1\le e(\sigma_j)\le k_j\); sufficiency takes \(k_j=e(\sigma_j)\).
In the latter case the [even-grid](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#half) and [odd-grid](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v181.md#statement) constructions permit a diagonal choice of increasing even degrees with half-mass limit and with the Pisot excess over the square constant tending to zero, since the chosen exterior traces tend to two.
The criterion concerns this construction through individually admissible powers.
The size bounds in (2) and the large-trace extremal examples leave the behavior of \(e(\sigma)\log\sigma\) near one unresolved.
