# The three cyclotomic full interlacing inverse images of irrational integral traces

<a id="statement"></a>
## Classification of all presentations at measure one

Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be monic irreducible of degree \(m\ge2\), and let \(g=A/B\) be an [integral interlacing map](Yin_C_G0345_v117.md#statement).
Thus \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), \(A\) is monic of degree \(s\ge1\), \(B\) has degree \(s-1\) with leading coefficient \(k\ge1\), and
\[
       g(X)=X/k+c-\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\frac{w_i}{X-b_i},
       \qquad b_1<\cdots<b_{s-1}\text{ real},\quad w_i>0 .
\]
Exclude integer translations \(g=X+c\).
For \(x=z+z^{-1}\) put \(P_g(z)=z^{sm}B(x)^m S(g(x))\), retaining all fibers.
There exists an absolute \(\epsilon>0\) such that \(\log M(P_g)\le\epsilon\) holds exactly for the three rows below and their simultaneous integer translations.

| Normalized source \(S_0(Y)\) | Normalized map \(g_0(X)\) | Full polynomial \(P_g(z)\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(Y^2-Y-1\) | \(X-1/(X+1)\) | \(\Phi_{30}(z)\) |
| \(Y^2+Y-1\) | \(X-1/(X-1)\) | \(\Phi_{15}(z)\) |
| \(Y^2-2\) | \(X-1/X\) | \(\Phi_{24}(z)\) |

The original presentations are \(g=g_0+n,\ S(Y)=S_0(Y-n)\), with arbitrary \(n\in\mathbb Z\).
In particular every such full polynomial has degree eight.
The [absolute positive gap](Yin_C_G0345_v122.md#statement) reduces the small-measure assertion to the exact classification at measure one.
The coordinator supplied the source classification by the conductor of a cyclotomic trace.
Agent-S obtained the three presentations by applying the endpoint inequalities a second time to the reciprocal of the pole transform.

<a id="sources"></a>
## The source span and the possible conductors

Suppose \(M(P_g)=1\).
The monic reciprocal \(P_g\) has all roots on the unit circle, so every solution of \(A(x)-yB(x)=0\) over a root \(y\) of \(S\) lies in \([-2,2]\).
Coprimality gives \(B(x)\ne0\) at these solutions, hence \(y=g(x)\) is real.
Write \(d_-= \min y,\ d_+=\max y,\ D=d_+-d_-\).
The [integer norm bound](Yin_C_G0345_v120.md#span) gives \(D>2\).
On each real fiber successive solutions straddle each pole, so all poles lie strictly between \(-2\) and \(2\), and the extreme real branches give \(g(-2)\le d_-\le d_+\le g(2)\).
Consequently
\[
       D\le g(2)-g(-2)
       =4/k-\sum_i \frac{4w_i}{4-b_i^2}\le4/k-\sum_i w_i .
\]
It follows that \(k=1\), and the exclusion of translations then gives \(s\ge2\).
Division at infinity with monic \(B\) makes \(c\) and \(W=\sum_iw_i\) integers.
Since \(W>0\) and \(D>2\), we get \(W=1\) and \(D\le3\).
Normalize \(g_0=g-c,\ S_0(Y)=S(Y+c)\), and use \(g,S,d_-,d_+\) for the normalized quantities henceforth.
Now \(g(-2)>-2\) and \(g(2)<2\), so all normalized source conjugates lie in \((-2,2)\).

By [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) applied to the monic unit-circle lift \(z^m S(z+z^{-1})\), the normalized source is the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_n+\zeta_n^{-1}\) for a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity.
Its conjugates are the traces of all primitive \(n\)-th roots.
For odd \(n\ge7\), the indices \(1,(n-1)/2\) are units modulo \(n\) and give span at least \(2\cos(2\pi/n)+2\cos(\pi/n)\).
This expression increases with \(n\) and already exceeds three at \(n=7\).
Indeed the three primitive traces at \(7\) have polynomial \(X^3+X^2-2X-1\), by dividing \(\Phi_7(z)\) by \(z^3\) and writing \(X=z+z^{-1}\).
This cubic has opposite signs at \(-2,-9/5\) and at \(6/5,2\), exhibiting two roots separated by more than three.
For \(n\equiv2\bmod4\), negating a primitive root gives a primitive root of odd order \(n/2\) and negates its trace.
For \(4\mid n,\ n\ge12\), the indices \(1,n/2-1\) give span at least \(4\cos(2\pi/n)\ge2\sqrt3>3\).
After excluding rational traces, only \(n=5,10,8\) remain, yielding the three quadratic sources in the table.

<a id="poles"></a>
## The reciprocal pole transform forces a single pole

Let \(p=s-1\ge1\) and write
\[
      R(X)=X-g(X)=C(X)/B(X)=\sum_{i=1}^p\frac{w_i}{X-b_i}.
\]
Here \(C=XB-A\) is monic integral of degree \(p-1\), since \(W=1\).
The endpoint bounds on the source give
\[
          0<R(2)\le 2-d_+,\qquad 0<-R(-2)\le2+d_- .       \tag{1}
\]
Suppose \(p\ge2\).
The derivative \(R'(X)<0\) away from the poles and the one-sided limits show that \(C\) has exactly one simple zero in each gap \((b_i,b_{i+1})\).
Call these zeros \(a_j\).
The residues of \(1/R\) at them are \(1/R'(a_j)<0\).
For \(m_\nu=\sum_iw_i b_i^\nu\), division at infinity gives
\[
        1/R(X)=X-m_1-\sum_j\frac{v_j}{X-a_j},\qquad
        v_j>0,\quad V=\sum_j v_j=m_2-m_1^2 .
\]
The monic integer numerator and denominator of \(C/B\) make \(m_1,m_2\) integers by its expansion at infinity.
In particular \(V\) is a positive integer.
Since \(|a_j|<2\), apply (1) at both endpoints to obtain
\[
     1\le V\le\sum_j\frac{4v_j}{4-a_j^2}
        =4-\frac1{R(2)}+\frac1{R(-2)}
        \le 4-\frac1{2-d_+}-\frac1{2+d_-}.                  \tag{2}
\]
The two reciprocal terms on the right have sum \(2+3\sqrt5/5>3\) for either source of discriminant five and \(2+\sqrt2>3\) for the source of discriminant eight.
This contradicts (2), so \(p=1\).

Write \(g(X)=X-1/(X-b)\).
The monic integral denominator and the strict pole bounds give \(b\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
For \(Y^2-Y-1\), inequality (1) at \(2\) gives \(1/(2-b)\le(3-\sqrt5)/2<1/2\), forcing \(b=-1\).
Reflection gives \(b=1\) for \(Y^2+Y-1\).
For \(Y^2-2\) the bounds \(1/(2\mp b)\le2-\sqrt2<1\) give \(b=0\).
Substitution in the three rows gives respectively the full trace polynomials
\[
    X^4+X^3-4X^2-4X+1,\qquad X^4-X^3-4X^2+4X+1,\qquad X^4-4X^2+1 .
\]
Their lifts \(z^4H(z+z^{-1})\) equal \(\Phi_{30},\Phi_{15},\Phi_{24}\) respectively, verifying existence in all three cases.
