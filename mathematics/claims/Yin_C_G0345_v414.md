# Coefficient growth at the seed degree for independent inverse pairs

<a id="statement"></a>
## The rate for a fixed seed and an exclusion across growing degrees

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal irreducible minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(e=2s\).
Choose one representative \(x_i\) of each inverse pair of roots.
Suppose their only integral product equal to a root of unity is the product with all exponents zero.
In descending order write \(t_1\ge\cdots\ge t_s\) for the numbers \(\big|\log|x_i|\big|\), and put \(h=m(P)=\sum_jt_j\).
At each integer \(p\ge2\) take the two-class graph, coprimality, reflected determinant and coefficient cost \(H\) of the [finite minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement), requiring the repeated factor to be this \(P\) and the row-degree bound to be \(e\).
Before imposing a cutoff the minimum \(H_p(P)\) exists at every sufficiently large integer \(p\), and
\[
       \lim_{p\to\infty}p^{-1}\log H_p(P)
          =F(P):=\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)t_j .
\]
For every fixed Salem seed of degree \(e\) this is \(e\log\rho\), where \(\rho\) is its exterior root.
For the full independent class \(F(P)>(e+2)/\log^3(3e)\), uniformly positive and tending to infinity with degree.

For every sufficiently large odd prime \(p\), one can exclude all qualifying pairs of cost \(H\le2^{\lfloor p/3\rfloor}\) in this class with seed degree at most \(B(p)\), for some nondecreasing \(B(p)\to\infty\), when each seed is tested at its own degree.
At the full cutoff \(2^p\) a fixed seed in the class can qualify arbitrarily late precisely when \(\exp F(P)<2\).
All such seeds have degree at most \(254\) and \(M(P)<2^{1/(s+1)}\).
The theorem places no common numerical lower bound on the qualifying prime indices.

<a id="proof"></a>
## Maximal minors with no common polynomial factor

Use the two signed matrices of [the equal-width construction](Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions) with the integer exponent replaced by \(N\) and its powered conjugates by independent variables \(Y_i\).
At each sign the \(e+1\) coordinates of the kernel vector are signed maximal minors \(C_d\), indexed by the deleted column \(0\le d\le e\).
At \(N=0\) both kernels are generated by the reciprocal monic polynomial \(\prod_i(Z-Y_i)(Z-Y_i^{-1})\).
This gives full generic rank at both signs and a nonzero mixed edge expression at \(N=0\).

The [rank calculation at fixed points of inversion](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#simple) and [the calculation at collisions](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#collision) put the polynomial gcd of all minors in \(L[N]\), where \(L\) is the splitting field.
For clarity, their hypotheses on the labels \(c_i=\pm x_i\) are \(c_i\ne0,\pm1\), \(c_i\ne c_j\) and \(c_ic_j\ne1\) for distinct indices.
They hold regardless of how many roots of \(P\) lie outside the circle.
For each variable \(Y_i\) the argument fixes the remaining variables away from their collision loci and studies \(N\to0\) in a complex disk.
The constant and highest coefficients preclude a simultaneous zero escaping to zero or infinity.
The two Taylor calculations exclude zeros approaching either fixed point or any other node and its inverse.
Compactness then excludes a common factor of positive degree in \(Y_i\).
At \(s=1\) the fixed-point calculation uses the three-dimensional space of quadratics and there are no other nodes.

For a real weight vector \(u=(u_i)\), let \(v_j\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \((|u_i|)\).
The [center minor calculation](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) gives the following exact maximum weight over all monomials occurring in the minors, with coefficients polynomial in \(N\):
\[
                    e\sum_i u_i+\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)v_j .
\]
Each selected column \(d\) contributes at most \(s u_i+|d-s||u_i|\) at node \(i\).
Deleting column \(s\) leaves the \(s\) distances \(s,s-1,\ldots,1\), each twice.
For distinct positive absolute weights, assigning each pair of equally distant columns to one node in decreasing order of \(|u_i|\) attains the rearrangement upper bound.
The two coefficient vectors in its leading block have determinant \(c_i\) or \(-c_i\).
The extreme monomial consequently has nonzero constant coefficient \(\pm\prod_i c_i\), so there is no nonconstant common polynomial factor in \(N\) either.
Continuity of the maximum of finitely many linear forms gives the same support formula at ties and zero weights.

Independence modulo torsion makes every nonzero polynomial in \(N,\mathbf Y\) eventually nonzero after evaluating at \(p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_s^p\).
Indeed its distinct exponential bases have nontorsion ratios after terms of the same exponent vector are combined.
The [local growth and finite-place gcd estimates](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#xia24-u001) now apply as in the [projective-height calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#height).
At each embedding the archimedean log maximum of the minors is \(p\) times their maximum weight at \(u_i=\log|\sigma x_i|\), up to \(o_P(p)\).
For the finite common valuation one can take one minor and an integer linear combination of the others avoiding all its irreducible factors.
The contribution to the almost-unit condition outside the archimedean places is only \(\log p\), and eventual nonvanishing avoids the theorem's exceptional algebraic set for any fixed tolerance.
The normalized finite common valuation is thus \(o_P(p)\).
Each \(u_i\) has mean zero over the embeddings, and their sorted absolute values are always the same list \(t_j\).
This gives the projective height \(pF(P)+o_P(p)\) for each sign.
The generic rank and nonzero edge expression persist eventually, and the [exact two-line minimum](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) gives existence, coprimality and the same rate for the uncapped minimum.
Every Salem seed satisfies the independence premise, since each representative can be sent to \(\rho\) by an embedding sending all other representatives to the unit circle.

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Uniform exclusion at the seed degree

The decreasing weights \(e-2j+2\) have mean \(s+1\).
Their nonnegative covariance with the \(t_j\) gives \(F(P)\ge(s+1)h\).
By [Voutier's degree bound](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), \(h>2/\log^3(3e)\).
Since \(x/\log^3(3x)\) on \(x\ge2\) attains its minimum at \(x=\exp(3)/3\), one has
\[
                   F(P)>\exp(3)/81>\log(2)/3.
\]
For the last comparison \(\exp(3)>20\) and \(\log2<7/10\) follow from the exponential series.
Any qualifying seed at \(H\le2^p\) has \(M(P)\le2\) by [reflected elimination](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
At each bounded degree there are only finitely many such polynomials by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
For every fixed bound one may therefore take a common onset after which all independent seeds up to that degree exceed \(2^{\lfloor p/3\rfloor}\) at their own widths.
Making these onsets strictly increasing and taking the largest eligible degree index at each prime gives the stated budget \(B\), without an effective growth assertion.
The unrestricted Lehmer coefficient test also permits larger widths and dependent inverse pairs.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## A finite range for the full cutoff

For each \(j\), \(e-2j+2\) is even.
In the chosen embedding, \(\exp F(P)\) is therefore a product of integer powers of the algebraic units \(x_i\overline{x_i}\), with negative powers when the chosen representative lies inside the circle.
It is an algebraic unit, so it cannot equal the rational number two.
The strict alternatives \(F(P)<\log2\) and \(F(P)>\log2\) give eventual qualification and eventual exclusion respectively, by the limit and eventual existence above.
When the first holds, \((s+1)h\le F(P)<\log2\) proves the sharper measure cap.
For \(e\ge256\), monotonicity gives \(F(P)>256/\log^3(768)>256/343>\log2\).
Here \(\log768<7\), and \(256/343>7/10>\log2\).
The even degree must therefore be at most 254.
These finite bounds make no assertion about how many further seeds pass below the cutoff in that degree range.
