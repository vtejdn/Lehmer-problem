# Multiplicative relations and interlacing powers for reciprocal units with real exterior conjugates

<a id="statement"></a>
## Decomposition by the Galois translates of the exterior set

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\), with \(r\ge1\) exterior roots \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\), all real.
Let \(G\) be its splitting-field Galois group, acting on the \(m\) inverse pairs, and let \(E\) be the set of \(r\) pair labels containing an exterior root.
Choose a representative from each inverse pair, using the exterior root on \(E\).
A relation is an integral exponent vector whose monomial in the representatives is a root of unity.
Put \(q=m-r\), the number of neutral inverse pairs.

Two labels belong to the same block if they have the same membership in \(gE\) for every \(g\in G\).
The relation lattice is the direct sum of its restrictions to these blocks.
All blocks have the same size \(b\mid\gcd(m,r)\) and the same relation rank.
If this rank is one, the primitive relation in each block has every coefficient equal to \(1\) or \(-1\), and its value is \(1\) or \(-1\).
In particular \(\gcd(m,r)=1\) forces independence of all \(m\) representatives modulo roots of unity.

If the \(r\) positive numbers \(\log|\alpha_i|\) are rationally independent, all representatives are independent modulo roots of unity.
If they form a **circuit**, meaning that they are rationally dependent and every proper subset is independent, the translates of \(E\) themselves partition the pair labels.
There is exactly one primitive signed relation up to negation on each such block of size \(r\).
The full and neutral relation ranks are \(m/r\) and \(m/r-1\), respectively.

In particular when \(r=3\), every pair of exterior logarithms is rationally independent.
Either the three logarithms are independent or they form a circuit, in which case the largest exterior modulus equals the product of the other two.
In both cases all powered conjugates remain distinct at every positive exponent, and for \(q\ge2\) a positive natural density of powers have one neutral trace in each of the \(q\) bins of the monic torsion trace polynomial \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\).
This gives partial interlacing with deficit three.
The [same power construction](#powers) applies to independent representatives for any \(r\), and to rank-one blocks of any common size \(b\ge3\), including every exterior circuit with \(r\ge3\).
For distinct exterior moduli it covers every \(\gcd(m,r)\le3\).
An opposite pair of real exterior roots instead permits the [exact degree reduction](#three) without changing \(M(P)\).

<a id="projection"></a>
## Complex conjugation projects a relation onto the real exterior pairs

The coordinating agent proposed the conjugation projections, the real circuit argument and their application to three real exterior roots, extending the [signed Galois action for two exterior conjugates](Yin_C_G0345_v54.md#relations).
All roots of \(P\) are distinct algebraic units, and none is a root of unity because one is exterior.
The action on inverse pairs is transitive and acts on exponent vectors by signed permutations since every automorphism commutes with inversion.
Let \(\mathcal L\subset\mathbb Z^m\) denote the relation lattice.
It is \(G\)-stable and saturated, since an element whose positive power is torsion is itself torsion.
In particular \(\mathcal L=(\mathcal L\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap\mathbb Z^m\).

Fix the complex embedding used to specify \(E\).
Complex conjugation \(\tau\) fixes both roots of each pair in \(E\) and interchanges the two roots of each neutral pair.
Thus \((I+\tau)/2\) acts on rational exponents as projection onto the coordinates in \(E\) and preserves \(\mathcal L\otimes\mathbb Q\).
Its conjugates by \(G\) are the diagonal projections onto \(gE\).
These projections and their complements commute.
The product that selects a given pattern of membership in all \(gE\) is the coordinate projection onto its block.
Hence each block projection preserves the rational relation space, and also preserves \(\mathcal L\) because the projected integral vector is integral and the lattice is saturated.
This proves the integral direct sum.

The partition is permuted by \(G\), and transitivity on labels gives transitivity on blocks and transitivity of the setwise block stabilizer on the labels within that block.
Every block has size \(b\), and \(E\) is a union of whole blocks, proving \(b\mid m,r\).
The restrictions of the relation lattice on the various blocks are signed permutations of one another.
In rank one the block stabilizer takes a primitive generator to itself or its negative.
Thus all its entries have the same absolute value by transitivity on the block.
This value is one by primitivity and saturation.
In any rank, a block can be sent into \(E\), where the monomial of a block relation is real, so its torsion value is \(1\) or \(-1\).
The direct sum gives the same assertion for every relation.
On a singleton block even rank one is impossible because no root of \(P\) is torsion.

<a id="circuit"></a>
## Rational relations between the exterior logarithms

Testing a monomial relation in the fixed complex embedding gives a rational zero relation between the exterior logarithms, using only the exponents on \(E\).
If these logarithms are independent, every such exponent is zero.
Transitivity, applied to the Galois translates of the original relation, then forces every exponent to vanish.

For a circuit let \((a_1,\ldots,a_r)\) span the rational nullspace of the exterior logarithms, with coprime integral entries.
The nullspace has dimension one and every entry is nonzero by minimal dependence.
Since the exterior roots are real, \(\prod_j\alpha_j^{a_j}\) has absolute value one and equals \(1\) or \(-1\).
Thus the vector supported on \(E\) is an actual relation.
Projecting it to any block contained in \(E\) gives another relation, nonzero on that entire block.
Minimal dependence rules out a proper subblock, so \(E\) is one whole block and its translates partition all labels.
Equivalently, two translates sharing a label must coincide, since sending their common label to \(E\) makes both translated relations meet \(E\), and a nonzero restriction must use every exterior site.
On \(E\) every relation belongs to the one-dimensional rational nullspace and the primitive integer generator is realized.
The block decomposition now gives exactly these signed translates as a basis of the full lattice.
All blocks except \(E\) are neutral, giving the stated neutral rank.
In particular the coefficients of the primitive exterior logarithmic dependence all have absolute value one.

<a id="three"></a>
## Three exterior roots and the small common divisors

For any root \(\gamma\) of \(P\), form the real vector \((\log|g(\gamma)|)_{g\in G}\).
Its Euclidean norm is positive and is the same for all roots by transitivity.
Suppose two distinct real exterior roots have rationally dependent logarithms.
There are positive integers \(d,e\) with \(|\alpha_i|^d=|\alpha_j|^e\).
Reality gives \(\alpha_i^d=\pm\alpha_j^e\), so the corresponding log vectors multiplied by \(d,e\) are equal.
Equality of their norms forces \(d=e\), hence the two roots are opposite.

An opposite pair makes \(P(z)=P(-z)\), since the two monic irreducible polynomials share a root.
Negation then pairs all exterior roots without fixed points, forcing \(r\) even.
For \(r=3\) this is impossible, so each pair of logarithms is independent.
This gives the independent and circuit alternatives above.
In the circuit case a signed zero sum of three positive logarithms makes one the sum of the other two.
In particular \(M(P)\) is the square of the largest exterior modulus in this case.

More generally, if the exterior moduli are distinct, a nonzero relation supported on at most two labels is impossible.
For two labels in one block, send that block into \(E\), where a two-term relation would give a proportional pair of exterior logarithms, contradicting the preceding norm argument.
A relation on different blocks would project onto a forbidden singleton relation.
In a block of size three, a relation space of dimension at least two would have a nonzero rational vector vanishing at one coordinate, hence an integral relation on at most two labels after clearing denominators.
Thus blocks of size at most two have rank zero, and blocks of size three have rank at most one.
This proves the asserted alternatives for \(\gcd(m,r)\le3\).

If opposite exterior roots occur, write \(P(z)=Q(z^2)\).
The polynomial \(Q\) is monic, integral, irreducible and reciprocal, by substitution and comparison of coefficients.
Its roots are the squares of one root from each pair under negation.
Its \(r/2\) exterior roots are positive real numbers with distinct moduli, and \(M(Q)=M(P)\) by the root product.
Its degree \(m\) is even, since a monic reciprocal polynomial of odd degree vanishes at \(-1\), and \(Q\) is irreducible with an exterior root.
Thus the new half-degree and exterior count are \(m/2,r/2\), and their greatest common divisor is \(\gcd(m,r)/2\).
When the original common divisor is at most three the distinct-modulus result applies after this single degree reduction, with the power assertions referring to \(Q\).

<a id="powers"></a>
## Interlacing with one signed constraint per neutral block

Suppose the representatives are independent, or all blocks have rank one and common size \(b\ge3\).
In the latter case there cannot be a two-label relation because the supports of the block generators are disjoint and each generator has support size \(b\).
In either case opposite exterior roots are impossible, so all real exterior moduli are distinct.
The root of largest modulus is then unique.
Under the map raising roots to the \(n\)-th power, for each \(n\ge1\), its image has only one preimage by modulus.
Every fiber has the same size by Galois transitivity.
Consequently all powered roots remain distinct and conjugate, giving a monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(P_n\) of degree \(2m\) and a trace minimal polynomial of degree \(m\).
The root product gives \(M(P_n)=M(P)^n\).

If \(q\ge2\), the nodes of \(U_{q-1}(x/2)\) are the torsion traces \(2\cos(j\pi/q)\), \(1\le j<q\), cutting the folded angles into \(q\) equal bins.
In the independent case product Haar equidistribution of neutral powers gives density \(q!/q^q\) for one neutral trace per bin, by the [power and bin argument](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#bins).
In the rank-one case there are \(t=q/b\) wholly neutral blocks of size \(b\) and no other neutral labels.
Each block imposes a single signed product with value \(1\) or \(-1\), and these primitive vectors generate the entire neutral relation lattice modulo torsion.
On even powers every such product is one.
These equations define a connected torus of dimension \(q-t\), by taking all but one coordinate per block freely.
Every other character has nontorsion value on the chosen neutral representatives, so geometric sums give Haar equidistribution on this torus along the even powers and on its translate along the odd powers, as in the [progression proof](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#degree).
Each coordinate has uniform marginal, so the node boundaries have measure zero on both cosets.

To see positivity on even powers, use the [signed block assignment](Yin_C_G0345_v55.md#bins) on exactly \(q=bt\) bins.
For even \(b\) each block uses \(b\) consecutive bins, with oppositely signed adjacent pairs giving total residual at most one in bin-width units.
For odd \(b\) the first \(3t\) bins supply the triples of that construction with residual \(\pm1/2\), and its additional adjacent pairs use precisely \((b-3)t\) further bins.
Thus both constructions exhaust the \(bt\) bins without needing any additional free labels.
The signs of the circle arguments absorb the signs prescribed on each block.
Adjust each folded center by the signed correction of magnitude at most \(1/b\) in the even case and \(1/(2b)\) in the odd case.
The signed sums vanish and every selected angle remains at least \(1/4\) bin-width from its endpoints.
Independent perturbations of the \(q-t\) free angles by less than \(\pi/(8bq)\) remain in the respective bins, including the dependent last angle of each block.
Consequently the occupancy event has a natural density, at least \(\tfrac12(8bq)^{-(q-t)}\) on all powers.
The selected neutral traces witness partial torsion interlacing with defect \(m-(q-1)-1=r\), leaving the \(r\) exterior traces unused.

For \(q=1\) the one neutral trace supplies partial interlacing with \(A=1\) and defect \(r\) at every positive power.
For \(q=0\) all traces are real and \(A=1\) can use one of them with defect \(r-1\).
At fixed \(r\), this last case has bounded degree \(2r\) and hence a positive Mahler gap by finiteness of monic integer polynomials of bounded degree and measure.

<a id="all-three"></a>
## The two possible real signatures with three exterior roots

An irreducible reciprocal unit with exactly three exterior conjugates has either the three real roots treated here or one real exterior root and a nonreal conjugate pair.
In the second signature the [mixed three-exterior classification](Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#statement) gives exact descent to a Salem or reciprocal quadratic unit of measure \(M(P)\) when all three moduli are equal.
Together the cases show that outside this descent every positive power preserves degree, and for \(q\ge2\) the neutral-bin occupancy event has positive natural density and gives partial torsion interlacing of defect three.
For \(q=1\) use the unique neutral trace with \(A=1\), and for \(q=0\) use the trace of one real exterior root with \(A=1\) and defect two.
