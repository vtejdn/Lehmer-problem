# The least integer in the ideal of two rows of small normalized cost

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound for every integer Bézout identity

For nonzero \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) coprime over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\), let \(B(U,V)\) be the positive generator of \((U,V)\cap\mathbb Z\).
This ideal contains a nonzero integer by clearing the denominators in a rational Bézout identity.
The additive group of \(\mathbb Z[Y]/(U,V)\) has exponent \(B(U,V)\), since an integer annihilates the ring exactly when it kills the class of one.
This characterization applies also when the additive group is infinite.

Fix \(Q=(1091/1000)^{1/3}\).
There is a computable width \(b=b(p)\) tending to infinity through the primes such that, for every sufficiently large prime \(p\), every prime \(p<q\le2p\), and every pair passing the [common two-prime test](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#common) at this width, the following holds.
Write \(\Sigma=p+q\), \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), and \(\eta=\log H/\Sigma\).
For \(0<\eta\le10^{-3}\), let \(T_\eta>\mathrm e\) be the larger solution of \(T/\log T=(4\eta)^{-1/3}\).
Then
\[
       \frac{\log B(U,V)}{\log H}>
                         \frac{1-1/b}{2}\exp(T_\eta).                 \tag{1}
\]
Along every sequence of passing rows at this width with \(p\to\infty\) and \(\eta\to0\), both \(\log B(U,V)/\log H\) and \(\log B(U,V)/\Sigma\) tend to infinity.
In fact
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log(\log B(U,V)/\log H)}
                   {\eta^{-1/3}\log(1/\eta)}
                     \ \ge\ \frac{4^{-1/3}}3 .                        \tag{2}
\]
Such a sequence exists if and only if the unrestricted infimum of positive logarithmic Mahler measures is zero.
The width below is chosen to control valuations of individual differences as well as the estimates available at the [earlier resultant width](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width).

<a id="local"></a>
## A divisor obtained from each pair of powered conjugates

Fix two monic irreducible nontorsion reciprocal unit polynomials \(P_1,P_2\), possibly equal, with degrees \(e_1,e_2\), logarithmic Mahler measures \(h_1,h_2\), and respective conjugates \(a_i,c_j\).
Use one finite Galois splitting field \(L\) of degree \(n\).
For exponents \(p,q\) as above suppose their powered lists have full degrees and are disjoint.
Write \(z=a_i^p,\ w=c_j^q,\ d_{ij}=z-w\).
Define \(B_0\) as the least positive integer \(m\) satisfying, in the ring of integers of \(L\), all the divisibilities
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 d_{ij}^{\,3}\mid 2[pq(a_i-c_j)]^3 m, & a_i\ne c_j,\\
 d_{ij}\mid m, & a_i=c_j .
 \end{array}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
The integers satisfying these conditions form a nonzero ideal of \(\mathbb Z\).
Indeed \(C=\prod_{ij}d_{ij}\) is a nonzero rational integer and \(C^3\) satisfies all the conditions.
Thus \(B_0\) is computable even by testing the positive divisors of \(|C|^3\) for algebraic integrality of the displayed quotients.
At a finite prime \(\mathfrak l\) above a rational prime \(\ell\), let \(v\) be the integral ideal valuation.
If a condition requires \(v(m)\ge t\), it imposes \(v_\ell(m)\ge\max(0,\lceil t/v(\ell)\rceil)\).
Taking the maximum of these bounds over all conditions and all primes above \(\ell\) gives the exact valuation of \(B_0\).

Suppose \(P_1^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) and \(P_2^2\mid U(X^q)+XV(X^q)\).
We show \(B_0\mid B(U,V)\).
Fix a pair, a finite prime, and its valuation ring \(O\), and abbreviate \(a=a_i,\ c=c_j,\ d=z-w\).
For \(a\ne c\), work in \(D=O[t]/(t^2,(t+d)^2)\) with \(Y=z+t\).
The [two jet equations](Yin_C_G0345_v495.md#labels) show that \(D_0^2\) kills the ideal generated by \(U,V\) in \(D\), with \(D_0=pq(c-a)+pcd/w\).
If \(v(d)>v(q(c-a))\), then \(v(D_0)=v(pq(c-a))\).
Multiplication by \((t+d)^2\) on \(O[t]/(t^2)\) has matrix with columns \((d^2,2d)\) and \((0,d^2)\).
Its determinant and the minimum valuation of its entries give the larger Smith valuation \(3v(d)-\min(v(2),v(d))\).
A scalar killing the class of one annihilates \(D\), so it has at least this valuation.
For any nonzero integer \(m\in(U,V)\), the scalar \(D_0^2m\) kills the class of one.
It follows that \(v(m)+v(2)+3v(pq(c-a))\ge3v(d)\).
This last inequality also holds when \(v(d)\le v(q(c-a))\), by nonnegativity alone.

When \(a=c\), \(G=U+aV\) vanishes at both nodes, so \(G=(Y-z)(Y-w)J\) with \(J\in O[Y]\).
The [equal-label derivative equation](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#local) gives \(aV(z)=-pz\,dJ(z)\).
As \(a\) is a unit, \(d\) divides \(V(z)\) and \(U(z)=-aV(z)\).
Evaluation of an integral identity for \(m\) at \(z\) gives \(d\mid m\).
These two arguments prove (3) for every integer in the row ideal.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## Coincidences among differences with a large common divisor

In each list of original conjugates identify two roots when their quotient is a root of unity, and write \(k_1,k_2\) for the common class sizes within the respective lists.
The Galois action is transitive on each list and preserves this relation.
Set \(k=\min(k_1,k_2)\) and \(u_{ij}=a_i^p c_j^{-q}\), so \(v(d_{ij})=v(u_{ij}-1)\) at each finite prime.
For fixed seeds all sufficiently large prime pairs have full and disjoint lists by the [powering and height argument](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#gcd).
At each such pair group the indices by the actual equalities \(u_{ij}=u_{rs}^{\pm1}\).
Within each group all valuations \(v(d_{ij})\) agree because inversion changes the difference from one by a unit factor.

For sufficiently large \(p\), every group contains at most \(2k\) index pairs.
Moreover, the normalized logarithmic ideal gcd of two differences whose indices belong to different groups is \(o_{P_1,P_2}(\Sigma)\), uniformly over \(p<q\le2p\).
To see both facts, write \([x]\) for the image of a unit in \((\mathcal O_L^\times/\mu(L))\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\).
For any fixed coprime exponents \(A,B\), membership in the subgroup \(u_{ij}^{A}=u_{rs}^{B}\) implies
\[
        p\big(A[a_i]-B[a_r]\big)
             =q\big(A[c_j]-B[c_s]\big).                             \tag{4}
\]
Unless both vectors vanish, (4) prescribes at most one ratio \(q/p\), hence at most one ordered pair of distinct primes.
If both vanish, equality up to torsion of \(a_i^A,a_r^B\) and equality of the positive absolute heights of these conjugates give \(|A|=|B|\).
Coprimality now gives \(|A|=|B|=1\).
For the two signs in an actual equality \(u_{rs}=u_{ij}^{\pm1}\), we may therefore require, after excluding the finitely many ratios from (4) for those signs, that \(a_r/a_i^{\pm1}\) and \(c_s/c_j^{\pm1}\) are roots of unity with the same choice of sign.
For a prescribed sign there are at most \(k_1\) choices of \(a_r\), and each power \(a_r^p\) fixes \(c_s^q\) uniquely by the given equality.
The full powered degree makes \(c_s\) unique.
Reversing the two choices gives the bound \(k_2\), proving the multiplicity bound.

For the gcd assertion apply [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) to the ordinary units \(u_{ij},u_{rs}\) with the archimedean places as the allowed set.
For any \(\epsilon>0\) the normalized logarithmic ideal gcd is at most \(\epsilon\max(h(u_{ij}),h(u_{rs}))\) outside finitely many pairs and finitely many subgroups of the form just considered, where \(h(\cdot)\) here is absolute logarithmic Weil height.
These heights are \(O_{P_1,P_2}(\Sigma)\).
Exceptional pairs have bounded gcd since all powers are disjoint.
For each exceptional subgroup, (4) excludes all sufficiently large prime pairs unless its points satisfy \(u_{ij}=u_{rs}^{\pm1}\).
Taking \(\epsilon\) arbitrarily small and using the finite lists of indices proves the asserted estimate for indices in different groups.
This use retains the source unit's inspection of the statement and normalization of Proposition 2.

<a id="size"></a>
## Summing the retained valuations

Set \(F=\sum_{ij}\max(p\log|a_i|,q\log|c_j|)\) in any one complex embedding and put
\[
 G=\begin{cases}
      3F, & P_1\ne P_2,\\
      3F-2(q-p)h_1, & P_1=P_2 .
    \end{cases}
\]
For fixed seeds the integer in (3) satisfies
\[
                  \log B_0\ \ge\ G/(2k)-o_{P_1,P_2}(\Sigma)         \tag{5}
\]
uniformly over \(p<q\le2p\) as \(p\to\infty\).
Assign weight \(s_{ij}=3\) to unequal labels and \(s_{ij}=1\) to equal labels.
At a fixed prime let \(w_{ij}=s_{ij}v(d_{ij})\).
Conditions (3) give \(v(B_0)\ge\max w_{ij}-E_v\), where \(E_v\) is the sum of \(v(2[pq(a_i-c_j)]^3)\) over unequal labels.
Its sum with weights \((\log N\mathfrak l)/n\) over all finite primes is \(O_{P_1,P_2}(\log(2\Sigma))\), by multiplicativity of the ideal norm and the fixed nonzero label differences.

In each group take a largest weight \(w_{ij}\).
The sum of these representatives is at least \(\sum_{ij}w_{ij}/(2k)\).
The sum less its largest term is bounded above by the sum of pairwise minima of representatives, by pairing each other term with a largest one.
Each minimum is at most three times the smaller valuation of the two underlying differences.
Bounding by the sum over all pairs of indices in different groups permits the representatives to vary with the finite prime.
The normalized sum of these minima over all primes is \(o_{P_1,P_2}(\Sigma)\) by the preceding gcd estimate.
The normalized sum of \(v(B_0)\) is \(\log B_0\), including above ramified primes.
Thus \(\log B_0\) is at least \(1/(2k)\) times the normalized sum of all \(w_{ij}\), up to \(o_{P_1,P_2}(\Sigma)\).
The product of the differences is the powered resultant \(C\), and \(\log|C|=F+O_{P_1,P_2}(\log(2\Sigma))\) by the [complex logarithmic form estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#archimedean).
For equal seeds the diagonal product has logarithmic absolute value \((q-p)h_1+O_{P_1}(\log(2\Sigma))\), by the [same-seed diagonal estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v499.md#rate).
The weighted sum therefore equals \(G+O_{P_1,P_2}(\log(2\Sigma))\), proving (5).

## Choosing the width and applying the height bound after compression

For each fixed pair \(G/\Sigma\) stays bounded away from zero by \(F\ge\max(e_2ph_1,e_1qh_2)\), as proved in the [mixed resultant estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v497.md#archimedean).
For equal seeds, writing \(e=e_1=e_2\), one has
\[
          G\ge(3eq-2(q-p))h_1\ge (3/2)e\Sigma h_1
\]
because \(e\ge2\) and \(q>p\).
To choose the width, at each \(p\) run the [earlier finite degree and measure tests](Yin_C_G0345_v500.md#width) for each candidate integer \(2\le j\le p\).
In each such test also require, for every ordered seed pair on its list and every prime \(p<q\le2p\),
\[
                         B_0^{\,2kj}\ge \exp(G)^{\,j-1}.
\]
The power on the right is real algebraic by the formula for \(F\) and by algebraicity of the Mahler measures before taking logarithms.
The \(k_i\) are computable by testing the conjugate quotients for roots of unity, equivalently testing their minimal polynomials for cyclotomicity.
Take the greatest passing \(j\), defaulting to one.
For each fixed \(j\) the list is finite, the original tests eventually pass, and (5) with the positivity just proved gives the new comparison eventually for the entire list.
Thus \(b(p)\to\infty\).

For any passing row of the stated cost, choose one qualifying seed at each prime.
The [weighted measure and degree estimates](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#tangencies) give \(ph_1+qh_2\le\eta\Sigma\) and \(e_1+e_2\le b\).
Each \(h_i<3\log Q\) by \(\eta\le10^{-3}<\log Q\) and \(q\le2p\), so both seeds are on the finite test list.
Select one with the smaller measure \(h=\min(h_1,h_2)\), and let \(e_*,k_*\) be its degree and torsion class size.
Put \(d=e_*/k_*\).
The [products of the roots in each class](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers) form one Galois orbit of size \(d\).
Indeed each product is a fixed representative raised to \(k_*\) times a root of unity, so equality of two products implies their representatives lie in the same class.
The roots in each class have equal modulus, whence the sum of positive logarithmic moduli of the products is \(h\).
This supplies a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\) and logarithmic Mahler measure exactly \(h\).

For distinct seeds the term of \(\max(e_2ph_1,e_1qh_2)\) using \(e_*\) is at least \(e_*\Sigma h/3\).
Since \(k\le k_*\), this gives \(G/(2k)\ge \Sigma dh/2\).
For equal seeds the preceding estimate gives \(G/(2k)\ge3\Sigma dh/4\).
The width test, (3), and \(h\le\eta\) now give
\[
     \frac{\log B(U,V)}{\log H}\ge (1-1/b)\frac{dh}{2\eta}.
\]
At \(0<h\le\eta\le10^{-3}\), [Voutier's height bounds](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) and the [strict inversion for \(T_\eta\)](Yin_C_G0345_v502.md#inversion) give \(d>\exp(T_h)\) and \(h\exp(T_h)\ge\eta\exp(T_\eta)\).
This proves (1).
The same inversion gives \(T_\eta\sim (4\eta)^{-1/3}\log((4\eta)^{-1/3})\), implying (2) and both divergences.
For the existence assertion, the contact measure bound gives \(\lambda\le\eta\), where \(\lambda\) is the unrestricted infimum.
Conversely, if \(\lambda=0\), the [common two-prime construction and its limit](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v243.md#gap) provide vanishing normalized minima for this growing width, for instance along consecutive prime pairs in the dyadic interval.
Eventually those minima lie strictly below the initial candidate \(2^\Sigma\) and hence come from actual rows.
Whether \(\lambda=0\) remains open.

The collaborating research agent for Sheng Yin derived the bound from the local two-jet quotient, the common divisor estimate and the class-product compression.
