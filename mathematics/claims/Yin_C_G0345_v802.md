# Prime ideal allocation at the two poles of a quadratic section

<a id="statement"></a>
## Almost coprime divisors attached to rational factors

Use the [quadratic section](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) \(P=F(X^k,X^l)\) with positive coprime exponents.
For \(j=3,4\) put \(\beta=(j+\sqrt{j^2+4})/2\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\), \(c=(j+3)(j+4)\), \(w=\sqrt{j^2+4}\), and
\[
 \gamma=c^k-(-1)^k\beta^l,\qquad D=kw\big((2j-7)c^2+2j+7\big)-lc.
\]
Write \(P=\prod_i S_i\) as a product of monic rational irreducibles with multiplicities and put \(Q_i(U)=\prod_{S_i(z)=0}(U-z^k)\).
There is a partition of the full prime ideal powers of \((\gamma)\) into pairwise coprime integral ideals \(B_i\) in \(K\) such that
\[
 \sum_i\left|\log N B_i-\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}Q_i(\beta)|\right|
 \le 2\log|N_{K/\mathbb Q}D|=O(\log(2+k+l)).                 \tag{1}
\]
The partition can be chosen separately at each pole.
Along \(k\to\infty\), \(s=l/k\to\infty\), for every factor of degree \(d_i\), uniformly,
\[
                \log N B_i=(\tfrac12\log\beta+o(1))d_i.       \tag{2}
\]
Thus each pole needs at least as many distinct prime ideals in \((\gamma)\) as the section has rational factors, eventually.
A sequence of factors with \(H=o(k)\) exterior roots requires, at both poles simultaneously, divisors composed of full prime ideal powers whose log norms divided by \(\log\beta\) are asymptotic to the same \(d_i/2=o(l)\).
In particular a prime-power ideal at either pole forces eventual irreducibility on any such steep sequence where the condition holds.
No distribution of prime ideals of these exponential values is assumed.

<a id="derivative"></a>
## An integral derivative congruence

For \(t=U-U^{-1}\) write \(a=(t-3)(t-4)\), \(b=(t+3)(t+4)\), and \(F=U^2(aV^2+V+b)\).
Write \(R(a,b,Y)=\prod_{v^k=Y}(av^2+v+b)\), a polynomial with integral coefficients.
Over \(U=X^k\) the values of \(V\) range through \((\zeta X)^l\) for all \(\zeta^k=1\).
Consequently \(G(U)=\prod_iQ_i(U)=U^{2k}R(a,b,U^l)\), the sign being positive since \(\deg P=4k+2l\) is even.
At \(U=\beta\) this yields \(G(\beta)=\beta^{2k}\gamma\).
The identity \(R(0,b,Y)=b^k-(-1)^kY\) gives its two partial derivatives in \(b,Y\).
At \((a,b,Y)=(0,c,(-c)^k)\), the only vanishing factor in the product over the complex roots is at \(v=-c\), so \(R_a=c^2R_b=kc^{k+1}\).
This identity at integer inputs remains valid modulo \((\gamma)\) after substituting \(Y=\beta^l\).
Since \(Udt/dU=w\) at \(U=\beta\), the chain rule gives
\[
            \beta G'(\beta)\equiv \beta^{2k}c^{k-1}D\pmod{(\gamma)}.
\]
Both \(\beta\) and \(c\) are units modulo \((\gamma)\), and \(D\ne0\) by irrationality of \(w\).
For each prime ideal put \(e_i=v_{\mathfrak p}(Q_i(\beta))\), \(E=\sum_i e_i\).
The derivative product rule gives \(E-\max_i e_i\le v_{\mathfrak p}(G'(\beta))\).
This difference is also at most \(E=v_{\mathfrak p}(\gamma)\), so the congruence bounds it by \(v_{\mathfrak p}(D)\).
Assign the full power \(\mathfrak p^E\) to one index attaining the maximum.
The sum of absolute changes in the valuations is \(2(E-\max_i e_i)\), giving (1) by summation.

<a id="size"></a>
## Archimedean size of each divisor

For \(S_i\), the [two pole mass limits](Yin_C_G0345_v790.md#allocation) give \(s b_j/d_i\to1/2\), where \(b_j\) counts its exterior roots with powered coordinate near \(\beta\).
The [polar expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v793.md#statement) gives \(\log|\beta-z^k|=-s\log\beta+O(1)\) for those roots.
All remaining off-circle powered coordinates lie near the other positive exterior pole or near the two positive inverse poles, by reciprocity of the full section.
Their total number is \(O(d_i/s)\) by the [exterior count](Yin_C_G0345_v794.md#growth) applied also to the reciprocal factor.
At the negative conjugate \(\beta'=-1/\beta\) all off-circle distances are bounded above and away from zero.
The powered conjugates equidistribute on the unit circle by small-height equidistribution, since \(h(z^k)=k\log M(S_i)/d_i=O(1/s)\) by the exterior count and bounded powered radii, and the degrees of the powered points tend to infinity by Northcott and their positive heights.
Their circle portion has the same limit, since the complementary proportion tends to zero.
The circle means of \(\log|\beta-u|\) and \(\log|\beta'-u|\) are \(\log\beta\) and zero respectively.
It follows that \(d_i^{-1}\log|Q_i(\beta)|\to(\log\beta)/2\) and \(d_i^{-1}\log|Q_i(\beta')|\to0\).
The [degree estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v799.md#statement) gives \(d_i=l^{1+o(1)}\), hence absorbs the logarithmic loss in (1).
All limits hold along arbitrary choices of factors, giving uniformity, and \(H\sim d_i/s\) gives the final implication.
