# Separation of two residue classes at a number and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## The modulus and the power field of each mixed subsum

Fix an integer \(m\ge3\) and a rational \(\delta>0\).
Let \(G(X)=\sum_i c_iX^{a_i}\) be an integer Laurent polynomial with at most \(m\) distinct occupied exponents and \(H=\sum_i|c_i|\).
Suppose its exponents occupy two distinct residues \(r_0,r_1\bmod p\), where \(p\ge2\) is any integer.
Let \(q=p/\gcd(p,r_1-r_0)\) be the additive order of the difference of these residues.
Let \(\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}\) have infinite multiplicative order, with \(G(\gamma)=G(\gamma^2)=0\).
Call a nonempty subset \(I\) minimal if its terms sum to zero at both points and no proper nonempty subset does so.
Call it mixed if it meets both residues.
For each mixed minimal subset put
\[
                 g_I=\gcd_{i,k\in I}|a_i-a_k|,\qquad
                 R_I=(\max_{i\in I}a_i-\min_{i\in I}a_i)/g_I .
\]
There are effective constants \(q_0,C\), depending only on \(m,\delta\), such that for \(q>q_0\) each such subset satisfies
\[
           \max\!\left(q,[\mathbb Q(\gamma^{g_I}):\mathbb Q]\right)
                   \le R_I\le C(1+\log H)^{m-2+\delta}.                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular if \(d=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q]\), then \(g_I\ge d/R_I\).
Writing the exponents in class \(h\) as \(a_i=r_h+pj_i\), all positions \(j_i\) from this class in \(I\) agree modulo \(g_I/\gcd(g_I,p)\).
These statements concern each individual mixed minimal subset, even when the full sum from either class vanishes at both points.

Write \(e=\min_{k\ge1}[\mathbb Q(\gamma^k):\mathbb Q]\).
If \(q>q_0\) and \(\max(q,e)>C(1+\log H)^{m-2+\delta}\), no mixed minimal subset occurs, and each of the two class sums vanishes separately at both points.
Indeed the whole support can be partitioned by successively removing minimal subsets from the two zero sums.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## The two weights at both evaluation points

Fix a mixed minimal \(I\).
It contains at least three terms, since two terms vanishing simultaneously would give \(\gamma^{a_i-a_k}=1\) for distinct exponents.
Choose a pivot \(i_0\) from the first residue.
Start with the free lattice on the symbols indexed by \(i\in I\setminus\{i_0\}\), representing differences from the pivot.
Give each symbol weight \(W=0\) or \(1\) according to its class, and send it by \(E\) to \(a_i-a_{i_0}\).
The pivot has vector zero.
The map \(W\) is surjective and these maps satisfy
\[
                    E(v)\equiv (r_1-r_0)W(v)\pmod p .                     \tag{2}
\]
The lattice rank is at most \(m-1\) and \(E\) has image \(g_I\mathbb Z\).
On quotienting by a primitive vector killed by \(E,W\), the projected supports still generate the quotient, retain their two weights and remain distinct because their \(E\)-values are distinct.
At the torus point \(x\) with coordinates \(\gamma^{E(v_j)}\) in a basis \((v_j)\), every subsum of the projected Laurent polynomial equals the original subsum at \(\gamma\) divided by the pivot monomial.
The analogous assertion holds at \(x^2\) and \(\gamma^2\).
Minimality is therefore preserved.

In a basis adapted to \(W\) the equations are
\[
                P(Z)+TQ(Z)=0,\qquad P(Z^2)+T^2Q(Z^2)=0 ,
\]
with \(T\) of weight one.
At least one of \(x\) and \(x^2\) has both class sums nonzero.
Indeed if the first class sum vanished at both points, it would itself be a nonempty proper simultaneous zero subsum.
At the selected point the second class then cancels the first.

Every component of the common zero set through \(x\) has codimension at least two.
In the geometric Laurent ring a common irreducible divisor independent of \(T\) divides all four of \(P,Q,P(Z^2),Q(Z^2)\).
If such a divisor vanished at \(x\), both class sums would vanish at both points.
For a divisor depending on \(T\), work over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(Z)\), where \(T=-P/Q\) is the only root of the first equation.
Common divisibility forces the rational function \(f=-P/Q\) to satisfy \(f(Z^2)=f(Z)^2\).
The [monomial characterization](Yin_C_G0345_v482.md#components) yields \(f(Z)=Z^v\) with coefficient one.
At the selected point with nonzero class sums we may divide by \(Q\), giving \(T=Z^v\) at \(x\) or \(T^2=(Z^2)^v\) at \(x^2\).
As \(\gamma\) has infinite order, either equality puts a vector of weight one in \(\ker E\), contrary to (2).

No positive-dimensional coset containing \(x\) is contained in this zero set.
On a translate of a subtorus through \(x\), distinct restricted characters are linearly independent.
Group terms by equal restrictions to the underlying subtorus.
The partition for the second equation is identical, since the character lattice is torsion-free.
Consequently each group is a simultaneous zero subsum.
Minimality allows only one group, forcing every support difference to vanish as a character on the subtorus.
These differences generate the full lattice, so the subtorus is trivial.

<a id="bounds"></a>
## Bounded restrictions and the actual gcd of the exponents

Use [Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4 of Amoroso, Sombra and Zannier](../audits/literature/ASZ17/Yin_U001.md#intersection) as in the [finite descent for a signed row](Yin_C_G0345_v482.md#descent).
In current rank \(r\), the exponent vector \(u=(E(v_j)/g_I)_j\) is primitive and the parameter of the point is \(\gamma^{g_I}\).
The preceding arguments verify codimension and exclusion of cosets at every permissible quotient.
After clearing Laurent monomials the degrees of the two rational equations are bounded by the current support coordinates and their logarithmic heights by \(\log H+O_m(1)\).
Take \(\eta=\delta/(m-2+\delta)\).
The theorem gives either \(\|u\|_\infty\ll(1+\log H)^{(r-1)/(1-\eta)}\), or a nonzero bounded relation in \(\ker E\).
In the latter case take the primitive relation on the same line.
By (2) its weight is divisible by \(q\).

All choices before bounding \(q\) are finite and effective.
There are finitely many starting configurations of the two weights on at most \(m\) labels.
At each node, bound the primitive relations by the source constant for its rank, fixed degree bound, rational field of definition and \(\eta\).
Form the quotients by every such relation of weight zero and choose integral quotient bases, to depth at most \(m-2\).
Choose \(q_0\) larger than the absolute weight of every candidate bounded primitive relation in this finite tree.
Then every relation supplied on an actual branch with \(q>q_0\) has weight zero.
The quotient preserves the image \(g_I\mathbb Z\) of \(E\).
Rank one is impossible because the surjective weight would be an isomorphism although at least three different terms have just two weights.
Thus a bounded \(\|u\|_\infty\) is reached.
The normalized support differences are its pairings with the bounded support coordinates in the tree, so their range \(R_I\) is bounded by the right side of (1), using \(r\le m-1\).

The univariate sum with the least exponent subtracted and all differences divided by \(g_I\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(R_I\) having \(\gamma^{g_I}\) as a root.
Also \(\gcd(g_I,p)=\gcd(r_1-r_0,p)\), because every difference is either zero or \(\pm(r_1-r_0)\) modulo \(p\) and a mixed difference occurs.
At least two of the three or more exponents in \(I\) belong to the same residue.
Their nonzero normalized difference is divisible by \(p/\gcd(g_I,p)=q\), giving \(R_I\ge q\).
Within one class \(g_I\mid p(j_i-j_k)\), which proves the claimed congruence of positions.
Finally \([\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(\gamma^{g_I})]\le g_I\) gives \(d\le g_I R_I\).

## Double zeros without a numerator condition

Fix \(s\ge2\) and rational \(\delta>0\).
There are effective constants \(p_0,C_s\), depending on \(s,\delta\), with the following property.
Let \(p>p_0,\ b\ge0\) be integers and \(N=pb+1\).
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) be nonzero of degree at most \(b\), have at most \(s\) occupied positions and have one-norm \(H\).
Suppose \(F(X)=A(X^p)+\varepsilon X^N A(X^{-p})\), \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), has double zeros at a nontorsion algebraic \(\gamma\ne0\) and at \(\gamma^2\).
If
\[
                      \max(p,e)>C_s(1+\log(2NH))^{s-1+\delta},             \tag{3}
\]
then the minimum polynomials of \(\gamma^p,\gamma^{2p}\) both divide \(A\) with multiplicity at least two, and
\[
          b\ \ge\ 2\bigl([\mathbb Q(\gamma^p):\mathbb Q]
                       +[\mathbb Q(\gamma^{2p}):\mathbb Q]\bigr)\ \ge\ 4e. \tag{4}
\]
Reciprocity and integrality of \(\gamma\) are unrestricted.

Apply the separation argument to \(F\) and to its Euler derivative \(XF'(X)\), both of which vanish at both points.
Their exponents occupy the same two lists \(pj,N-pj\), possibly with zero-coefficient terms dropped from the derivative.
For either polynomial, a mixed block fits the signed difference lattice of [the original positions](Yin_C_G0345_v482.md#block), of rank at most \(s\).
In detail label the two positions by \(\pm e_j\) in the lattice whose basis is indexed by the occupied \(j\)'s.
Choose a negative pivot, assign \(W(v)=\tfrac12\sum_j v_j\) to a difference and let \(E(v)=p\sum_j jv_j-NW(v)\).
Then \(E\equiv-W\bmod p\) on this lattice and agrees with the true exponent differences.
The rank bound replaces \(m-2+\delta\) in (1) by \(s-1+\delta\), taking \(\eta=\delta/(s-1+\delta)\) over these finitely many signed supports.
The argument at \(x,x^2\) required only nonzero coefficients within the selected block, so the unequal derivative coefficients \(pjc_j,\ \varepsilon(N-pj)c_j\) are allowed.
The norms of the two full lists are \(2H\) and \(NH\), respectively.
Condition (3) rules out every mixed minimal block for either polynomial.
The sums in residue zero therefore vanish separately for both polynomials at \(\gamma,\gamma^2\).
These are the value of \(A(X^p)\) and its Euler derivative \(pX^p A'(X^p)\).
Thus \(\gamma^p,\gamma^{2p}\) are double zeros of \(A\).
They have different minimum polynomials, since their positive absolute logarithmic Weil heights are \(p h(\gamma)\) and \(2p h(\gamma)\).
Characteristic zero gives separability, proving (4).

In particular suppose that every positive power of \(\gamma\) has degree \(d\), as for a Salem number, and let \(d\to\infty\).
If \(b<4d,\ p\to\infty,\ p=O(d^\theta)\) for some \(0<\theta<1/(s-1)\), and \(H\le\exp(Kp)\) for fixed \(K\), no such signed row exists for large \(d\).
Indeed \(e=d\) and \(\log(2NH)=O_K(p+\log d)\).
Choose rational \(\delta>0\) with \(\theta(s-1+\delta)<1\), giving (3) and contradicting (4).
This includes \(p\asymp(\log d)^2,\ b=5d/2-1\), for every fixed \(s\).
For units whose powers lose degree, (4) uses their actual power degrees and need not contradict \(b<4d\).
