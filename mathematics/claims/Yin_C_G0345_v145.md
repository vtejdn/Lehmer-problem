# A longest cyclotomic completion can have unit resultant with an actual Salem polynomial

<a id="statement"></a>
## Simultaneous values at every conductor of a longest completion

For each integer \(A\ge4\), put \(\beta=(A+\sqrt{A^2-4})/2\).
For every sufficiently large integer \(k\) there is a Salem minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\) and a monic reciprocal cyclotomic product \(Q\) of degree \(d\) with the following properties.

1. The first \(k\) coefficients below the leading term agree, as do the last \(k\) above the constant.
   No degree-\(d\) monic cyclotomic product matches the first \(k+1\) coefficients of \(P\).
   Along this construction \(d\sim\beta^k/(1-\beta^{-1})\) and \(M(P)\to\beta\).
2. At every root \(\zeta\) of \(Q\), and every root of unity of order at most \(k\), one has \(\zeta^{-d/2}P(\zeta)=-1\).
   Thus every primitive and cyclic resultant through order \(k\) has absolute value one, as does the full \(\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)\), with all multiplicities.
3. Given fixed integers \(h\ge1,\ q>h\), there is also a monic reciprocal integer polynomial \(B\) of the same degree \(d=2(qt+1)\) with no roots on the unit circle and \(d/2\) roots outside it, counted with multiplicity.
   Its Mahler measure is bounded in terms of \(A,q,h\).
   The first and last \((q-h)t\) coefficients below the leading and above the constant terms agree with \(P\).
   The values and derivatives of \(P,B\) through order \(2h-1\) agree at every root in part 2.
   Their longest cyclotomic prefix at degree \(d\) is the same \(k\).
   The ratio \((q-h)t/d\) can thus tend to any fixed value of the form \((1-h/q)/2\), arbitrarily close to \(1/2\).

For \(A=3\) the same construction yields parts 2 and 3 concerning values, derivatives and signatures, and a degree-\(d\) comparison sharing \(k\) coefficients at each end.
Writing \(K(P)\) for the longest cyclotomic prefix at that degree, it gives \(\liminf k/K(P)\ge\log\beta/\lambda>0.87\), where \(\lambda<1.106\) is defined below.
Both \(P\) and \(B\) have the same \(K(P)\) here as well.

There are also actual Salem polynomials with measures tending to the plastic number \(\theta<4/3\) from above, with reciprocal cyclotomic comparisons of the same degree \(d\) satisfying \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)|=1\) and matching \(k\) coefficients at each end, where \(\liminf k/\log d\ge1/\lambda\).
Their centered value is \(-1\) on every root of \(Q\) and at every primitive root of order at most \(k\) other than order eight.
These fixed positive limiting measures distinguish the constructions from a sequence approaching measure one.

The contributor supplied the Salem families, unit evaluations, maximal completion, and change of signature with identical values.
The coordinator supplied the arbitrary order of agreement at the sampled roots and the resulting agreement arbitrarily close to half the degree.

## A Salem polynomial with a full set of unit values

Write \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\), the integer polynomial determined by \(D_0=2,\ D_1=X,\ D_{j+1}=XD_j-D_{j-1}\).
For even \(n\ge2\) and integer \(A\ge3\), define
\[
 S_n(x)=(x-A)(D_n(x)-2)-1,\qquad
 P_n(z)=z^{n+1}S_n(z+z^{-1})=(z^2-Az+1)(z^n-1)^2-z^{n+1}.             \tag{1}
\]
At \(x=2\cos(j\pi/n)\), \(0\le j\le n\), the values of \(S_n\) alternate between \(-1\) and \(4(A-x)-1>0\).
This gives \(n\) distinct roots in \((-2,2)\).
Since \(S_n(A)=-1\) and \(S_n\) is monic of degree \(n+1\), its remaining root \(T_n\) is simple and exceeds \(A\).
If \(S_n\) had a proper factorization, some irreducible monic factor would have only neutral roots \(x\).
The identity \((A-x)(2-D_n(x))=1\) would make \(A-x\) an algebraic integer unit, whereas all of its conjugates exceed one, including when \(A=3\).
Thus \(S_n\) is irreducible.
In the totally real field of \(T_n\), \(T_n^2-4\) has a negative conjugate, so it is not a square.
Consequently \(P_n\) is irreducible of degree \(2(n+1)\), with one real conjugate \(\sigma_n>1\), its inverse and \(2n\) neutral conjugates.
Since \(D_n\) is increasing on \([A,\infty)\),
\[
 0<T_n-A=\frac1{D_n(T_n)-2}\le\frac1{\beta^n+\beta^{-n}-2}=O_A(\beta^{-n}).
\]
It follows that \(\sigma_n=\beta+O_A(\beta^{-n})\).
Equation (1) gives \(z^{-(n+1)}P_n(z)=-1\) at every \(z^n=1\).

## Choosing the degree and filling it with conductors of unit resultant

Let \(\Lambda(k)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)\).
We use the elementary bound
\[
 \log\Lambda(k)\le\lambda k+O(\log^2(2k)),\quad
 \lambda=\frac65\left(\frac{\log2}{2}+\frac{\log3}{3}+\frac{\log5}{5}
                              -\frac{\log30}{30}\right)=1.10555\ldots .       \tag{2}
\]
Indeed set \(\psi(x)=\sum_{p^a\le x}\log p\) for primes \(p\) and integers \(a\ge1\).
In the factorial ratio \(\lfloor x\rfloor!\lfloor x/30\rfloor!/(\lfloor x/2\rfloor!\lfloor x/3\rfloor!\lfloor x/5\rfloor!)\), the weight on \(\log p\) from \(p^a\) is \(\Delta(x/p^a)\), where
\[
 \Delta(y)=\lfloor y\rfloor+\lfloor y/30\rfloor-\lfloor y/2\rfloor-\lfloor y/3\rfloor-\lfloor y/5\rfloor .
\]
It is periodic of period 30, equal to one when the residue of \(\lfloor y\rfloor\) is \(1,2,3,4,5,7,8,9,11,13,14,17,19,23,29\), and zero otherwise.
The logarithm of the ratio therefore dominates \(\psi(x)-\psi(x/6)\).
Integral comparison gives \(\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)=x\log x-x+O(\log(2x))\) for \(x\ge1\).
The ratio thus has logarithm \(5\lambda x/6+O(\log(2x))\), and summation down the scales \(x/6^j\) proves (2).

Through order \(k<n\) the reversal of \(P_n\) agrees with \(1-Az+z^2\).
For its integral Euler exponents \(c_j\), the [formal prefix construction](Yin_C_G0345_v138.md#packets) gives
\[
 jc_j=\sum_{e\mid j}\mu(j/e)(\beta^e+\beta^{-e}).
\]
Here \(c_1=A\), and for \(j\ge2\) the absolute contribution of the proper divisors is bounded by \(\sum_{e=1}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}(\beta^e+\beta^{-e})<(\beta^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor+1}+1)/(\beta-1)<\beta^j\), since \(\beta\ge(3+\sqrt5)/2\).
Thus the monic product \(\prod_{j\le k}(z^j-1)^{c_j}\) realizes this prefix.
Its degree satisfies
\[
        D_k=\sum_{j\le k}j c_j=\frac{\beta^k}{1-\beta^{-1}}+O_A(k\beta^{k/2}+1).     \tag{3}
\]
Let \(u\) be the least odd integer above \(k\) and put \(v=u+2\).
Append \(z^u-1\) if the multiplicity of \(\Phi_1=z-1\) is odd.
The resulting product is reciprocal with constant one and has degree \(D'_k\) between \(D_k\) and \(D_k+u\).
Its normalized reversal still matches the prefix.
The coprime degrees \(u,v\) fill every surplus \(b\ge uv\) by nonnegative combinations of \(u,v\), since one of \(b,b-v,\ldots,b-(u-1)v\) is a nonnegative multiple of \(u\).
Use \(z^u+1,\ z^v+1\) for these additions.

Fix \(q>h\ge1\), set \(L_k=2uv\Lambda(k)\), and choose \(t\) as the least positive multiple of \(L_k\) for which \(d=2(qt+1)\ge D'_k+uv\).
Use \(n=qt\) in (1) and the indicated filling to obtain \(Q\).
Every root used has order dividing \(t\), as does every root of unity of order at most \(k\).
Thus all signed centered values equal \(-1\), and the norm of each tested monic cyclotomic factor and the absolute resultant of their product equal one.
The step in the admissible degree is \(2qL_k\).
When \(A\ge4\), \(\log\beta\ge\log(2+\sqrt3)>\lambda\), so \(L_k=o(D_k)\) and \(d\sim D_k\).
In particular \(k< n\) for all sufficiently large \(k\), as used in constructing the prefix.
By the [sharp minimum degree for Salem comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v140.md#statement), at prefix length \(k+1\) the least degree is asymptotic to \(\sigma_n^{k+1}/(1-\sigma_n^{-1})\sim\beta D_k\).
Its range applies because \((k+1)\log\sigma_n/\log d\to1\) and \(k|\log\sigma_n-\log\beta|\to0\).
Therefore \(k+1\) cannot be attained at degree \(d\), proving exact maximality.
For \(A=3\) the same choice has \(d\ge D_k\) and \(\log d\le(\lambda+o(1))k\).
The same sharp cutoff gives \(K(P_n)/\log d\to1/\log\beta\), proving the stated fraction.

<a id="signature"></a>
## Changing every neutral root while preserving the sampled derivatives

Use \(n=qt\) and let \(s=2-D_t(x)\).
For \(x=2\cos\alpha\in[-2,2]\) one has \(0\le2-D_{qt}(x)\le q^2s\) by \(|\sin(qy)|\le q|\sin y|\).
Put \(L=(A+2)q^2,\ C=(2L)^h\), and form the monic trace polynomial \(S_n(x)-Cs^h\).
On the neutral interval it is bounded above by \(Ls-1-Cs^h\).
For \(s\le1/(2L)\) this is at most \(-1/2\), and for \(s\ge1/(2L)\) one has \(Cs^h\ge Ls\).
The reciprocal lift \(B\) has no circle root, so inverse pairing gives precisely \(d/2\) exterior roots with multiplicity.
Its circle supremum is at most \(4(A+2)+1+C4^h\) by (1), giving the measure bound.
The exact lift difference is
\[
        B(z)-P_n(z)=-C(-1)^h z^{n+1-ht}(z^t-1)^{2h}.                         \tag{4}
\]
This proves the derivative and coefficient statements, including the two boundary coefficients of the matched range.
As \(K(P_n)=O_A(\log d)\), the polynomials agree through \(K(P_n)+1\) at both ends for large \(k\), giving the identical longest prefix also for \(A=3\).

## A prescribed unit comparison below \(4/3\)

Use \(f=z^3-z-1,\ f^*=1-z^2-z^3\) and \(N\equiv-1\bmod120\).
For all sufficiently large such \(N\) the [plastic Salem construction](Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family) and [cyclotomic table](Yin_C_G0248_v1.md#family-cyclotomic-factors) give the actual Salem polynomial
\[
    P_N=(z^Nf+f^*)/\Phi_8,\qquad d=N-1,\qquad\Phi_8=1+z^4 .
\]
Its measure tends to \(\theta\) from above and is eventually below \(4/3\).
Since \(z^{-1}f+f^*=-z^{-1}\Phi_8\), at every \(\zeta^{(N+1)/2}=1\) of order other than eight one has \(\zeta^{-d/2}P_N(\zeta)=-1\).

The reversal through \(k<N\) equals \(f^*/\Phi_8\).
The [primitive cyclic word count](Yin_C_G0248_v1.md#family-repair) gives nonnegative Euler exponents for \(1-z^2-z^3\), counting words in letters of weights two and three up to cyclic permutation.
Dividing by \(\Phi_8=(1-z^8)/(1-z^4)\) increases the fourth exponent by one and decreases the eighth by one.
The primitive word of weights \(2,3,3\) contributes to the eighth exponent, so every resulting exponent is nonnegative.
The power sums in its logarithmic derivative have absolute value \(O(\theta^j+1)\) from the three reciprocal roots of \(f^*\) and the four roots of \(\Phi_8\).
Thus the initial positive binomial product \(\prod_{j\le k}(z^j-1)^{c_j}\) has degree \(O(k^2\theta^k)\) by the same divisor formula.

Take \(a,b\) the least powers of three and five respectively that exceed \(k\).
Replace every \(\Phi_8\) in this product by
\[
 U(z)=\frac{\Phi_8(z)\Phi_8(z^{ab})}{\Phi_8(z^a)\Phi_8(z^b)}
      =\prod_{\substack{i\mid a,\ i>1\\j\mid b,\ j>1}}\Phi_{8ij}(z).
\]
The factorization follows by comparing root orders, since \(a,b\) are odd and coprime.
The replacement has the same normalized reversal as \(\Phi_8\) through degree \(k\), uses only orders dividing \(8ab\), and avoids order eight.
Its degree is \(O(k^2)\), leaving the full product of degree \(O(k^4\theta^k)\).
Make the same parity correction at \(u\) and the same filling with \(u,v\) as above.
These added factors avoid order eight since \(u,v\) are odd.
Require \(N+1\) to be a multiple of \(240(8ab)(2uv)\Lambda(k)\) and take the least such multiple leaving at least \(uv\) degrees after the corrected product.
The resulting \(d=N-1\) is at most \(O(k^4\theta^k)+\exp((\lambda+o(1))k)\) and tends to infinity, with \(N>k\).
This proves the stated lower bound on \(k/\log d\) and all the asserted unit evaluations, including those at every repeated factor of the comparison.
Here the excluded primitive order eight occurs in the numerator and is divided out before the evaluation, which explains the separate order condition in this family.
