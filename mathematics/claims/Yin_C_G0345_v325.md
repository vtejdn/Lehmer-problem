# Uniform Mahler comparison on a central band of fixed relative width

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative error for arbitrary real coefficients

Let \(1\le d<n\), let \(b\) be a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(d\), and let \(\phi\in\mathbb R\).
Write \(D=n/d\), \(F(t)=2\cos(nt+\phi)-b(t)\), and use normalized circle measure \(d\nu(t)=dt/(2\pi)\).
Define the logarithmic means
\[
       \mu=\int\log|F(t)|\,d\nu(t),\qquad
       I=\iint\log|2\cos v-b(t)|\,d\nu(v)d\nu(t).
\]
There are absolute constants \(D_0,C\) such that for \(D\ge D_0\)
\[
            |\mu-I|\le\frac C D\{1+I+\log(2D)\}.                  \tag{1}
\]
After increasing \(D_0,C\), the same bound holds with \(I\) replaced by \(\mu\) on the right.
In particular \(\mu-I\to0\) uniformly for bounded diagonal measure and \(d=o(n)\), with arbitrary coefficient growth, multiplicities and phases.
Even at unbounded measures the discrepancy divided by \(1+I\) tends to zero when \(D\to\infty\).
Absolute errors can still grow in that setting, as in the [Taylor remainder](Yin_C_G0345_v314.md#statement).

For integers \(B\in\mathbb Z[T]\) of degree at most \(d\), put \(b(t)=B(2\cos t)\), \(\phi=0\) and \(P(z)=z^{2n}+1-z^n B(z+z^{-1})\).
There are absolute \(\epsilon,\eta>0\) such that
\[
                 d\le\epsilon n,\quad M(P)>1
                        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log M(P)\ge\eta .         \tag{2}
\]
This is a whole-polynomial statement with no separability or coefficient-height hypothesis.
In particular an irreducible shrinking sequence cannot keep the whole central block within this fixed fraction of the half-degree.
The unrestricted problem still includes every larger central width.
A small factor of a product can have a much smaller measure than that product.

<a id="setup"></a>
## Separating the slow energy from the logarithmic loss

Set \(L(t)=\log^+|b(t)|\), with \(\log^+0=0\), set \(f=1+L\) and let \(E=\int f\,d\nu\).
Quadratic Jensen in the fast angle gives \(I=\int_{|b|>2}\operatorname{arcosh}(|b|/2)\,d\nu\), and hence \(1\le E\le1+\log2+I\).
The [clipped mesh comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#quadrature) has error \(O(D^{-1}\log(6/u))\) at clipping threshold \(0<u<1\), and clipping the double integral costs \(O(\sqrt u)\).
We prove that clipping the diagonal costs at most
\[
                           C\sqrt u+C d E/n .                     \tag{3}
\]
It suffices to do so when \(E<\gamma d\) for a fixed sufficiently small absolute \(\gamma>0\).
Indeed, for \(E\ge\gamma d\), the full [term-count comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#statement) with \(K\le2d+3\) gives (1) directly.
The constant case has \(\mu=I\) by substitution.
Assume from now on that the slow term is nonconstant.

<a id="packing"></a>
## An adaptive cover paid for by the integral of the slow energy

Let \(P_r(t)=\sinh r/(\cosh r-\cos t)\) be the circular Poisson kernel, integrated with \(d\nu\).
Fix a small absolute \(r_*\), for instance \(1/128\).
For every \(x\) choose \(r=r(x)\) between \(1/(2d)\) and \(r_*\) such that
\[
                            (P_r*f)(x)=dr.                        \tag{4}
\]
At the lower endpoint \(P_r*f\ge1\); at the upper endpoint it is at most \(\|P_{r_*}\|_\infty E<dr_*\) by the choice of \(\gamma\).
This choice also ensures that the two endpoints are in the stated order since \(E\ge1\).
Continuity supplies a radius, and in fact \(dr\ge1\) for every chosen one.
No regularity or uniqueness of its dependence on \(x\) is needed.

Select a finite family of centers \(x_i\), with radii \(r_i\), whose arcs of radius \(r_i/5\) are disjoint and whose arcs of radius \(r_i\) cover the circle.
To obtain it, choose a still available arc of radius at least half the supremum of available radii, and discard those intersecting it.
Every discarded center is within \(3r_i/5\) of a chosen center, and the disjoint small arcs have a positive uniform lower length, so selection terminates.
In particular \(\sum_i r_i=O(1)\).
A stronger estimate is
\[
                            \sum_i (dr_i)^2\ll dE .                \tag{5}
\]

Write \(\sigma=f\,d\nu\), and use \(B(x,R)\) for a circular arc of radius \(R\), taking the full circle once \(R\ge\pi\).
The kernel is bounded by \(C r/(r^2+\operatorname{dist}(t,0)^2)\) for \(r\le r_*\).
Dividing (4) by \(r_i\) and using dyadic annuli gives
\[
          d\le C\sum_{k=0}^{k_i}
                 \frac{\sigma(B(x_i,2^k r_i))}{(2^k r_i)^2},
\]
where \(k_i\) is the first index with \(2^{k_i}r_i\ge\pi\).
Since \(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k/2}<\infty\), each \(i\) can be assigned to one \(k\) for which its \(R_i=2^k r_i\) satisfies
\[
                 \sigma(B(x_i,R_i))\ge c\, d\,2^{-k/2}R_i^2.
\]
Fix \(k\), and greedily choose disjoint arcs at these enlarged radii in order of decreasing radius.
Assign each discarded arc to a selected arc \(j\) that it meets and for which \(R_j\ge R_i\); assign the selected arc to itself.
The original disjoint small arcs of this group lie within \(3R_j\) of \(x_j\), so \(\sum_{i\mapsto j} r_i\ll R_j\).
Moreover \(r_i\le2^{-k}R_j\), and therefore \(\sum_{i\mapsto j}r_i^2\ll2^{-k}R_j^2\).
Summing over the disjoint enlarged arcs at this \(k\) bounds the contribution by \(O(2^{-k/2}E/d)\).
Summation over \(k\) proves (5), including enlarged arcs comparable to the whole circumference.

<a id="analytic"></a>
## Analytic bounds on a disk at the chosen scale

The entire periodic extension of \(b\) satisfies
\[
 \log^+|b(x+iy)|\le d|y|+(P_{|y|}*L)(x),\qquad y\ne0.
\]
Indeed in each half plane the corresponding Laurent monomial shift is a polynomial in \(e^{it}\) or \(e^{-it}\) on the unit disk, with boundary log positive part \(L\).
The Poisson integral majorizes its logarithmic modulus, and the monomial shift costs \(d|y|\).
To keep the bound effective even near the real axis, use the periodic strip \(|y|\le R\), \(R=32r_i\).
The harmonic function
\[
          dR+(P_{2R+y}*L)(x)+(P_{2R-y}*L)(x)
\]
majorizes \(\log^+|b|\) on both boundary lines and throughout the strip by the maximum principle.
For \(|x-x_i|\le 16r_i,\ |y|\le16r_i\), each kernel in this expression is at most a fixed multiple of \(P_{r_i}(x_i-\cdot)\), by the Poisson kernel formula and the triangle inequality for circular distance.
Consequently
\[
                \sup_{|z-x_i|\le16r_i}\log^+|b(z)|\ll dr_i.       \tag{6}
\]
Here and below disks are in the unwrapped complex coordinate \(t\).

<a id="levels"></a>
## Localizing the periods that need a logarithmic estimate

Fix one center \(x_i\) and write \(r=r_i,\ M=dr\ge1\).
For \(g_0=b,\ g_1=b'/n,\ g_2=b''/n^2\), (6) and Cauchy's estimate on circles of radius \(r\) give \(\log^+|g_j|\ll M\) on the disk of radius \(8r\), since \(nr\ge D\).
For \(g_0\) choose positive and negative thresholds with respective magnitudes in \([3,4]\); for \(g_1,g_2\) use magnitudes in \([1/8,1/4]\).
Choose each threshold from the two ends of its indicated interval so that its distance from \(g_j(x_i)\) is at least \(1/16\).
Jensen's formula on the larger disk then bounds the number of crossings of each threshold on the real interval \([x_i-4r,x_i+4r]\) by \(O(M)\), counting even complex zeros in the disk of radius \(4r\).
One can use a circle of radius between \(7r\) and \(8r\) avoiding boundary zeros; the lower bound at its center and (6) give the same absolute estimate.
This choice of thresholds is local to the center and no comparison of thresholds between centers is needed.

Call a full fast period flagged here if it crosses or meets an endpoint threshold for \(b\), meets a point where \(b\) is within its two thresholds but \(g_1\) is outside its thresholds, or meets a point where both lower quantities are within their thresholds but \(g_2\) is outside its thresholds.
For the periods entirely inside \([x_i-4r,x_i+4r]\), including boundary truncations in the counting argument, at most \(C_1M\) are flagged for one absolute \(C_1\).
Indeed the six threshold equations partition the interval into \(O(M)\) pieces.
On a piece of the first derivative danger set, \(b'\) has constant sign and magnitude at least \(n/8\) while \(b\) ranges over length at most eight; its length is \(O(1/n)\).
On a piece of the second derivative danger set, \(b''\) has constant sign and magnitude at least \(n^2/8\) while \(b'\) ranges over length at most \(n/2\); the same length bound holds.
Threshold points and either endpoint of the counting interval affect only \(O(M)\) more periods.

On each remaining full period the relative sublevel measure of \(|F|\le v\le1\) is \(O(\sqrt v)\), and its supremum is at least one.
If \(b\) is outside its thresholds then \(|F|>1\).
Otherwise the normalized derivatives of \(b\) are at most \(1/4\) in absolute value, and the [first and second derivative test on a fast period](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#periods) applies.
All these conclusions are uniform in \(\phi\).

<a id="clusters"></a>
## The logarithmic cost of a consecutive run

Use the cover to bound the nonnegative loss \((\log u-\log|F|)_+\) on each arc \([x_i-r,x_i+r]\).
Enlarge \(D_0\) once so that \((C_1M+6)2\pi/n\le r/100\) for every \(M=dr\ge1\) and \(D\ge D_0\).
Each run of flagged periods intersecting the arc can then be continued at both ends to its first neighboring unflagged periods inside \([x_i-4r,x_i+4r]\).
Otherwise there would be more than \(C_1M\) consecutive flagged periods wholly inside that larger interval.
Adjoin one such neighbor to each run.
The resulting intervals have total length \(O(M/n)\) for this center, each has length \(l\le r/100\), and each contains a real anchor \(\xi\) with \(|F(\xi)|\ge1\).

On \(|z-\xi|\le8l\), the slow term has logarithmic upper bound \(O(M)\) by (6).
The fast cosine term there is at most \(2e^{8nl}\) in modulus, also giving logarithmic bound \(O(M)\).
Thus \(\sup\log^+|F|\ll M\) on this disk.
We claim that the integral of \(\log^-|F|\) over the real run is \(O(Ml)\), where \(\log^-v=\max(0,-\log v)\).
Jensen's formula at \(\xi\) bounds the number of zeros within distance \(5l\) by \(O(M)\), using a circle between \(7l\) and \(8l\).
Choose \(4l<R'<5l\) with no boundary zeros and remove the disk Blaschke factors for all zeros inside \(R'\).
These factors have boundary modulus one.
For the zero-free quotient \(G\), \(C M-\log|G|\) is nonnegative harmonic by maximum modulus, and at \(\xi\) it is at most \(CM\), since division by the factors only increases the modulus at that point.
Harnack's inequality gives \(\log|G|\ge-O(M)\) throughout the run, which is within distance \(l\) of \(\xi\).
For a removed zero \(a\), measured from \(\xi\), the negative logarithm of its Blaschke factor on the run is at most \(\log^+(6l/|t-\xi-a|)\).
Its integral is \(O(l)\) uniformly in \(a\), by projecting \(a\) to the real line and integrating the logarithmic singularity over length \(l\).
This proves the claim, including zero multiplicities.
In particular there is no extra logarithm of \(M\) in this cost.

The total exceptional cost at the center is \(O(M^2/n)\).
For the unflagged periods intersecting its arc, the good sublevel bound gives a cost \(O((r+1/n)\sqrt u)\) after integration with \(dv/v\).
All estimates concern positive losses, so adding them over covering arcs is legitimate.
Since \(\sum r_i=O(1)\) and \(1/n\le r_i\), the good costs sum to \(O(\sqrt u)\).
By (5) the exceptional costs sum to \(O(dE/n)\).
This proves (3).

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## A uniform positive gap at a fixed fraction of the half-degree

Taking \(u=D^{-2}\), summing the two clipping costs and using the mesh comparison proves (1).
As \(\mu\ge I-C(1+I+\log(2D))/D\), choosing \(D_0>2C\) permits absorption of the coefficient of \(I\) and proves the version with \(\mu\) on the right.
For the integral trace specialization \(I\ge c_2>0\) whenever \(M(P)>1\), by the [uniform arithmetic trace gap and its cyclotomic zero cases](Yin_C_G0345_v320.md#height).
Take \(D\) sufficiently large that \(C/D\le1/4\) and \(C(1+\log(2D))/D\le c_2/4\).
Then \(\mu\ge c_2/2\).
Choosing the reciprocal of this fixed lower threshold for \(D\) as \(\epsilon\) proves (2).
Integers of trace degree zero give identical independent and diagonal means and satisfy the same conclusion when noncyclotomic.

The coordinating researcher developed the relative comparison, the local threshold count and the analytic estimate on each run.
The ambient-unit researcher proved the quadratic packing estimate, and the strategy researcher checked the strip majorant, derivative normalization and the limits of an argument using disjoint arcs alone.
