# The abelian subfield degree of an algebraic integer of small total Mahler measure

<a id="statement"></a>
## Relative cubic bounds for the original root field

Let \(\alpha\) be a nonzero nontorsion algebraic integer, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(m=\log M(\alpha)>0\).
Let \(F\subseteq K\) be any Galois subfield over \(\mathbb Q\), \(s=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(D=d/s\), and \(B\) the largest conjugacy class size in \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\).
Put \(g(x)=(\log\log(5x)/\log(2x))^3\) for \(x\ge1\).
There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
               m\ge c\min\{\log(2d),\;s\,g(d)/B^4\}.                  \tag{1}
\]
No degree-preservation hypothesis on powers of \(\alpha\) is needed.

In particular let \(F=K\cap\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\), or any abelian subfield of \(K\).
For \(m\le1\) one has, with another absolute constant,
\[
                            m\ge c\,s\,g(D).                           \tag{2}
\]
For every sequence with \(m\to0\), in its original fields one necessarily has
\(s=O(m/g(d))=o((\log d/\log\log d)^3)\).
If \(s\ge\eta/g(d)\) for fixed \(\eta>0\), (1) gives a positive lower bound depending only on \(\eta\).
These tests allow all conductors, including composita with small individual prime-power intersections.
The established [general relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) gives \(m\gg s(\log\log(5D))^3/(\log(2D))^4\) without the small-total-measure hypothesis of (2).
The [prime-conductor estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#statement) gives the cubic exponent without a total measure cutoff over the single prime-conductor base considered there.

<a id="primes"></a>
## The cost of ramified primes and degree-lowering powers

Write \(\operatorname{rd}(L)=|\operatorname{disc}L|^{1/[L:\mathbb Q]}\).
The discriminant tower formula, the power-basis index and the [Vandermonde bound](Yin_C_G0345_v707.md#resultants) give
\[
       \operatorname{rd}(F)\le\operatorname{rd}(K)
                    \le|\operatorname{disc}P_\alpha|^{1/d}\le d e^{2m}.
\]
At each prime \(p\) ramified in \(F\), all places over \(p\) have the same ramification index \(e\ge2\).
The [different exponent bound](Yin_C_G0345_v73.md#signed) gives \(v_p(\operatorname{disc}F)\ge s(1-1/e)\ge s/2\), including wild ramification.
Consequently
\[
                \sum_{p\ {\rm ramified\ in}\ F}\log p
                                      \le 2\log d+4m.                   \tag{3}
\]
In particular a small total measure pays for ramification even at a conductor with many prime divisors.

An odd prime \(p\) at which two distinct \(F\)-conjugates of \(\alpha\) have the same \(p\)-th power satisfies either \(p\mid D\) or \(\zeta_p\in K\).
Indeed, with \(l=[K:F(\alpha^p)]>1\), one has \(l\le p\).
If \(l<p\), the relative norm of \(\alpha\) to \(F(\alpha^p)\) is \(\alpha^l\zeta_p^v\).
Combining it with \(\alpha^p\) by Bézout gives \(\alpha\zeta_p^b\in F(\alpha^p)\).
The exponent \(b\) cannot vanish modulo \(p\), since \(l>1\), so \(K\) contains a primitive \(p\)-th root.
If \(l=p\) then \(p\mid D\).
This is the [power-degree argument](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#primes) applied over the actual subfield.
The product of \(p-1\) over distinct odd primes with \(\zeta_p\in K\) is at most \(d\), by the degree of their compositum at coprime conductors.
The number of excluded power primes is thus \(O(\log(2d))\), uniformly also when the original powers drop degree.

<a id="weighted"></a>
## A relative determinant weighted by the Frobenius conjugacy classes

Choose any finite set \(\mathcal P\) of odd unramified primes of \(F\) at which all the \(D\) relative conjugates have distinct \(p\)-th powers.
Write \(t=|\mathcal P|\) and \(b_p\) for the size of the Frobenius conjugacy class in \(G\).
For every positive integer \(T\) and \(S=D(T+t)\),
\[
 T\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac{\log p}{b_p}
 \le \tfrac12(T^2+t)\log S+
             (T+t)\left(T+\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p\right)\frac{m}{s}.   \tag{4}
\]
This finite inequality can use the actual local classes even when \(B\) is large.

Let \(f\) be the monic minimal polynomial over \(F\).
For each \(p\) choose one Frobenius automorphism \(\sigma_p\) and the monic polynomial \(f_p=\sigma_p^{-1}(f^{[p]})\), where the superscript raises all roots to their \(p\)-th powers.
Its roots are distinct and all have absolute logarithmic Weil height \(p m/d\).
Conjugation over \(\mathbb Q\) preserves height, so these root sets at different primes and the original root set are mutually disjoint.
At every place \(\mathfrak p\) of \(F\) over \(p\) having Frobenius \(\sigma_p\), reducing \(f^{[p]}\) raises its coefficients to the \(p\)-th power.
Thus \(f_p\equiv f\bmod\mathfrak p\), since \(\sigma_p\) preserves that place and induces the same action on its residue field.
By monicity, the cross resultant \(\operatorname{Res}(f,f_p)\) belongs to \(\mathfrak p^D\): this follows by taking the determinant of multiplication by \(f_p-f\) on the free algebra \(\mathcal O_{F,\mathfrak p}[X]/f\).
The places with this specific Frobenius have total normalized local degree \(1/b_p\).
Indeed \(G\) acts transitively on the places over \(p\), all have the same local degree, and the map from places to Frobenius elements is equivariant and onto this conjugacy class.

Apply the [confluent Vandermonde argument](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#determinant) with multiplicity \(T\) at the original \(D\) nodes and multiplicity one at the nodes of each \(f_p\).
At the places just counted, the cross differences contribute an extra local factor \(p^{-DT}\), since \(p\) is unramified and the cross resultant appears to power \(T\).
All other finite factors have absolute value at most one by integrality.
At every archimedean place the derivative rows divided by their factorials have norm at most \(S^{j+1/2}\max(1,|z|)^{S-1}\) for derivative order \(j\) at node \(z\).
Their \(S\)-factors multiply to \(S^{D(T^2+t)/2}\).
Taking the normalized product over places of a common splitting field preserves the fraction \(1/b_p\) and charges the absolute heights of all the nodes with their multiplicities.
The product formula for the nonzero determinant, followed by division by \(D\), gives (4), using \(D h(\alpha)=m/s\).

<a id="range"></a>
## A common interval of test primes

It suffices to prove \(m\gg s g(d)/B^4\) when \(m\le\log(2d)\).
Choose
\[
 T=\left\lceil A B\,\frac{\log(2d)}{\log\log(5d)}\right\rceil,
                  \qquad X=T^2\log(2T),
\]
with a sufficiently large absolute \(A\).
The [prime interval estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v164.md#choice) supplies \(\asymp T^2\) rational primes in \([X,2X]\).
By (3), the number lost to ramification is \(O(\log(2d)/\log X)\), and the power condition costs \(O(\log(2d))\).
Both are \(o(T^2)\) as \(d\to\infty\), uniformly in \(B\), and a sufficiently large \(A\) ensures that a fixed positive proportion survives for every \(d\).
Use all survivors as \(\mathcal P\), so that \(t\asymp T^2\).
The left side of (4) is at least a constant times \(T^3\log(2T)/B\).
Since \(B\le s\le d\), the first term on its right is \(O(T^2\log(2d)+T^2\log(2T))\).
It is at most half the left side for sufficiently large absolute \(A\): one has \(T\gg A B\) and \(T\log(2T)\gg A B\log(2d)\) with absolute implied constants.
The remaining coefficient of \(m/s\) is \(O(T^6\log(2T))\), because every \(p\le2X\).
Thus \(m/s\gg1/(BT^3)\gg g(d)/B^4\).
When \(m>\log(2d)\) the first term in the minimum of (1) suffices, proving the unconditional inequality.

In the abelian case \(B=1\).
If \(m\le1\) and \(d\ge2\), the stronger bound just proved in the range \(m\le\log(2d)\) gives \(s\ll 1/g(d)\).
As \(d\to\infty\) this is polylogarithmic, so \(D=d/s\) satisfies \(\log D/\log d\to1\) uniformly on that range.
Hence \(g(D)\asymp g(d)\), which proves (2), with bounded degrees handled by lowering the absolute constant.
In degree one the nontorsion integer has \(m\ge\log2\).
For an abelian extension \(L/\mathbb Q\) extending outside \(K\), the degree of \(\alpha\) over \(L\) equals \([K:K\cap L]\), by finite Galois base change, or its finite-level reduction for an infinite abelian extension.
Thus (2) also bounds the corresponding relative degree times height throughout the same small-total-measure range.
