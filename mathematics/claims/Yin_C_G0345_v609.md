# Sparse initial coefficients forced by a small neutral transfer loss

<a id="statement"></a>
## An integer constraint on the measure and the loss

Let \(P\) be monic, irreducible and reciprocal of positive degree with no unit-circle root, so \(P(0)=1\).
Write \(m=m(P)\), \(u=|P|\) on the circle and \(\ell=\int D(|A|^2/u)\,d\mu\) for the [numerator loss](Yin_C_G0345_v605.md#bound) of any nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Remove the initial monomial from \(A\), preserving \(\ell\), and expand \(H=A^2/P=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jX^j\) at zero.
Its coefficients are integers and \(h_0=A(0)^2\).
For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
                  \sum_{j<n}h_j^2\le16\exp\!\big(2n(m+\ell)\big).
\]
In particular, if \(2n(m+\ell)<\log(17/16)\), the initial squared coefficient sum is at most sixteen.
This gives [a test using only residues modulo two](#parity) and [an exact integer test](#integer) on a prescribed initial segment of \(P\).
For the complementary upper-tail loss \(\ell'=\int D(u)\,d\mu\) one has the more direct estimate \(\sum_{j<n}|[X^j]P|^2\le16 e^{2n\ell'}\).
All estimates concern the specified transfers and allow the choice of numerator to depend on the input.

<a id="poles"></a>
## Removing the interior poles

Form the finite Blaschke product \(B(z)=\eta\prod_{P(\alpha)=0,\,|\alpha|<1}(z-\alpha)/(1-\bar\alpha z)\), taking \(|\eta|=1\) and \(B(0)>0\).
Its circle modulus is one and \(B(0)=e^{-m}\) because the product of the absolute values of all roots of \(P\) is one.
The function \(f=BH/4\) is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk since \(B\) cancels all its possible interior poles.
On the circle one has \(\log^+|f|=\log^+(|A|^2/(4u))\le D(|A|^2/u)\) by \(2\operatorname{arcosh}(t)\ge2\log t\) for \(t\ge1\).
The [multiplication estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v608.md#compression) gives \(\|L_n(f)\|\le e^{n\ell}\), where \(L_n\) is the lower triangular matrix of truncated multiplication on power series.
The matrix \(L_n(B)\) is a contraction by the same estimate, and has determinant \(B(0)^n=e^{-nm}\).
Its least singular value is at least \(e^{-nm}\) since the product of all the other singular values is at most one.
The identity \(L_n(H)=4 L_n(B)^{-1}L_n(f)\) follows by multiplying the Taylor series at zero.
Applying its norm bound to the first coordinate vector proves the integer estimate, including when \(P\) divides the numerator.
For the upper-tail estimate, apply the multiplication bound directly to \(P/4\), whose positive logarithm on the circle is at most \(D(u)\).

<a id="integer"></a>
## Finite exclusion with integral coefficients

Let \(E_n(P)\) be the minimum of \(\sum_{j<n}h_j^2\) over integer lists with \(h_0\) a positive square for which \(P\sum_{j<n}h_jX^j\) is a square of an integer polynomial modulo \(X^n\).
The choice \(A=1\) bounds the finite enumeration by the initial squared coefficient sum of \(1/P\).
For any proposed list choose the positive square root \(a_0=\sqrt{h_0}\).
Successive comparisons of coefficients in \(A^2=P H\bmod X^n\) determine each \(a_i\) uniquely by division by \(2a_0\), rejecting a noninteger at once.
Every passing list is realized by the polynomial with those first \(n\) coefficients, since \(P(0)=1\).
Hence \(m+\ell\ge(2n)^{-1}\log^+(E_n(P)/16)\) for every nonzero integer numerator.

<a id="parity"></a>
## A binary test on a quarter of the reciprocal loss scale

Fix \(k\ge1\) with \(4k(m+\ell)<\log(17/16)\) and take \(n=2k\).
There exists \(J\in\mathbb F_2[X]\) of degree below \(k\), with constant one and at most sixteen nonzero coefficients, such that \(P J\bmod (2,X^k)\) has no term of odd exponent.
Indeed, if \(|A(0)|=1\), truncate \(H\) to the first \(k\) positions and reduce modulo two.
At most sixteen positions can be odd, and \(P H=A^2\) reduces to a polynomial in \(X^2\).
The only other possible constant magnitude is two because \(h_0^2\le16\).
In this case \(h_0=4\) exhausts the bound, so \(A^2=4P\bmod X^{2k}\).
Reducing modulo two shows that every coefficient of \(A\) through degree \(k-1\) is even.
Halving that prefix gives \(P\) itself as a square modulo \(X^k\) over the integers, and \(J=1\) suffices.

Among all \(2^{k-1}\) binary prefixes of length \(k\) with constant one, the fraction satisfying this necessary condition is at most
\[
          2^{-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
             \sum_{j=0}^{\min(15,k-1)} {k-1\choose j}.
\]
For fixed \(J\), multiplication by \(J\) is a bijection on this set of units modulo \(X^k\), and the target fixes all \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) odd positions to zero.
Summing over the allowed supports proves the bound.
Failure of the test gives \(m+\ell\ge\log(17/16)/(4k)\) uniformly in \(A\).
This count is over all binary strings with the specified constant coefficient.
The distribution of prefixes of actual small-measure irreducible polynomials remains open.
