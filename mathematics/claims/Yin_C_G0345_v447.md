# Sharp cost for two odd contact orders at positive Salem or quadratic units

<a id="statement"></a>
## The cost at one or two exterior roots

Fix two distinct positive real algebraic irrationals \(c_1>c_2>1\), and odd orders \(t_i=2v_i+1\ge1\).
Put \(r_i=\log c_i\) and \(W=t_1r_1+t_2r_2\).
For nonzero integer graphs \(U(X^n)-XV(X^n)\) vanishing to order at least \(t_i\) at each \(c_i\), the larger row one-norm \(H\) satisfies
\[
                       \liminf_{n\to\infty} n^{-1}\log H\ \ge W/2.
\]
The widths are unrestricted, and both row values may vanish at a powered node.
At one real algebraic irrational \(c>1\) the same lower bound holds with \(W=t\log c\) for every positive odd order \(t\).

Suppose each selected \(c_i\) is a Salem number or a real quadratic reciprocal unit, and let \(P_i\) be its monic minimal polynomial.
For either sign \(\varepsilon\) and any integer widths \(b(n)\to\infty\), the least one-norm of a row \(A\) with
\[
       \prod_iP_i^{t_i}\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
       A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y),\qquad A(c_i^n)\ne0\quad\text{for every }i
\]
has normalized logarithm tending to \(W/2\) along all positive integers \(n\to\infty\).
For any positive excess one fixed width suffices eventually for the corresponding upper slope.
This includes the minima of \(\delta^a,\delta^b\) for distinct positive integers \(a,b\) and a single Salem or positive real quadratic reciprocal unit \(\delta>1\), even when \(a,b\) are both odd.

<a id="approximation"></a>
## The rational approximation at the first coefficient

For a fixed real algebraic irrational \(c\) and every \(\epsilon>0\) there is a constant \(C>0\) such that all nonzero integer pairs satisfy
\[
            |p-cq|\ge C\max(|p|,|q|)^{-1-\epsilon}.                  \tag{1}
\]
This is the rational approximation consequence of the [Subspace Theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#subspace-and-unit-equations).
Indeed take a normal number field containing \(c\) and all its archimedean places.
For each chosen embedding \(\sigma\) use the two forms \(X-\sigma^{-1}(c)Y,Y\), identifying the normal field with its image.
On primitive rational pairs the archimedean weights sum to one and the projective height is \(\log\max(|p|,|q|)\).
A failure of (1) with constant one and arbitrarily large height would give an excess at least \(\epsilon\) in the two-form bound because \(|q|\le\max(|p|,|q|)\).
The finitely many exceptional projective points admit at most finitely many primitive pairs, and \(p-cq\ne0\) there.
The case \(q=0\) and multiplication by a common integer preserve (1).

Reverse both rows at a common formal width and divide out their common initial monomial.
After multiplication by a monomial in \(x\) the graph has terms \(u_jx^{-nj}-v_jx^{1-nj}\), with \(w_j=(u_j,v_j)\) and \(w_0=(p,q)\ne0\).
For any polynomial \(Q\) of degree less than the order at \(c\), applying \(Q(x\,d/dx)\) gives
\[
          \sum_{j\ge0}c^{-nj}\bigl[Q(-nj)u_j-c Q(1-nj)v_j\bigr]=0.  \tag{2}
\]
For a finite set \(I\) of positive integers write \(Q_I(s)=\prod_{j\in I}(s+nj)(s+nj-1)\).
At order \(2v+1\) take \(I=\{1,\ldots,v\}\) in (2).
The first coefficient contributes \(Q_I(0)p-cQ_I(1)q\), and indices \(1,\ldots,v\) have disappeared.
All polynomial factors in \(n,j\) in the remaining tail can be absorbed into \(n^{O(1)}\) times the exponential weight at its first index, uniformly over the width, by geometric decay.

For a single node, this bounds the absolute value of the first contribution by \(n^{O(1)}Hc^{-(v+1)n}\).
Also \(|w_0|\le n^{O(1)}Hc^{-vn}\), where \(|\cdot|\) on pairs is the maximum norm.
For \(v=0\) this follows from the row norm.
For \(v\ge1\) use \(Q_{\{1,\ldots,v-1\}}(s)\) multiplied by \(s\) and by \(1-s\) in (2) to bound the two first coordinates separately.
The nonzero integers \(Q_I(0),Q_I(1)\) have polynomial size at \(n\ge2\).
Applying (1) to \((Q_I(0)p,Q_I(1)q)\), taking logarithms, dividing by \(n\) and sending \(\epsilon\) to zero gives the one-node bound.

<a id="two"></a>
## The shared pair of columns in the two-node determinant

Set \(m=v_1+v_2+1\) and form a list \(s_1,\ldots,s_{2m}\) consisting of \(t_1\) copies of \(r_1\) followed by \(t_2\) copies of \(r_2\).
Use equations (2) with \(Q(s)=s^\ell,\ 0\le\ell<t_i\), at both nodes and solve for the first \(m\) coefficient pairs.
Their square coefficient matrix has columns indexed by \((j,e)\), \(0\le j<m,\ e=0,1\), with entries \(c_i^{-nj}(-nj+e)^\ell\) times \(1\) or \(-c_i\), respectively.
Write \(E=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2m}\lfloor(\ell-1)/2\rfloor s_\ell\).
The determinant has absolute value at least \(n^{-O(1)}e^{-nE}\) eventually.

Indeed the rearrangement inequality gives precisely the following assignments at the largest exponential rate.
The first node receives both columns at \(0,\ldots,v_1-1\), the second receives both at \(v_1+1,\ldots,m-1\), and each receives one column at \(v_1\).
Within each node, after removing exponential factors, the columns at the fully assigned indices are nonzero scalar multiples of Vandermonde columns on the distinct integers \(-nj,-nj+1\).
Their annihilator on the row space is spanned by \(Q_{I_i}\), using the same product formula also for index zero, where \(I_i\) is that node's set of fully assigned indices.
After eliminating the full columns, the leading determinant is nonzero precisely when the two rows
\[
              \bigl(Q_{I_i}(-nv_1),-c_iQ_{I_i}(1-nv_1)\bigr)
\]
are independent.
The quotient of the two polynomial values in each row tends to one since \(v_1\notin I_i\).
Thus the coefficient of \(e^{-nE}\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(n\), while every subleading assignment has an exponentially smaller rate and polynomial prefactor.
This also covers either or both \(I_i\) empty.

For \(0\le j<m\) put \(D_j=\sum_{k=j+1}^m s_{2k}\), and put \(D_j=0\) for \(j\ge m\).
Cramer's rule gives
\[
                         |w_j|\le n^{O(1)}H e^{-nD_j}.                 \tag{3}
\]
To see this, replace one column at \(j\) by a tail column at \(h\ge m\).
In the ordered column-index list its deletion and the insertion at the end increase the total rearrangement cost by \(D_j+(h-m)s_{2m}\).
Indeed the increase is one at the even positions \(2j+2,2j+4,\ldots,2m\) when \(h=m\).
The determinant of the replaced matrix is therefore at most \(n^{O(1)}(1+h)^{O(1)}e^{-n(E+D_j+(h-m)r_2)}\) in absolute value.
Division by the established lower bound and summation against the absolute tail coefficients prove (3) uniformly in their number.
For \(j\ge m\) the norm bound itself suffices.

In particular \(D_0=v_1r_1+(v_2+1)r_2\).
At \(c_1\) use \(I=\{1,\ldots,v_1\}\) in (2).
For \(j\ge v_1+1\), the minimum of \(jr_1+D_j\) is \((v_1+1)r_1+v_2r_2\), since each later step of \(D_j\) drops by at most \(r_2\).
Using (3) up to \(m-1\) and the geometric tail thereafter proves
\[
\begin{split}
   \max(|Q_I(0)p|,|Q_I(1)q|)&\le n^{O(1)}H e^{-n[v_1r_1+(v_2+1)r_2]},\\
   |Q_I(0)p-c_1Q_I(1)q|&\le n^{O(1)}H e^{-n[(v_1+1)r_1+v_2r_2]} .
\end{split}
\]
The two displayed exponents sum to \(W\).
Applying (1) as at one node proves the lower slope.

<a id="attainment"></a>
## Two short graphs with a nonzero determinant

More generally, let distinct monic reciprocal irreducible unit minima \(P_i\) with \(h_i=\log M(P_i)>0\) and positive orders \(t_i\) have \(W=\sum_it_ih_i\).
Consider a sequence of exponents separating their combined roots.
Suppose all nonzero two-row graphs with these contacts have lower slope \(W/2\), at every fixed width.
This alone suffices for genuine signed upper slope \(W/2+\eta\) at a fixed width for each \(\eta>0\).

The [one-factor fiber count](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#upper) compares the size of a jet fiber to the number of points having a single ratio.
Here we use the lower slope for the full list to bound the packing of every proportional subspace.
Choose \(0<\epsilon<\min(\eta,\min_i h_i/2)\), a fixed width \(w\) to be made large, and the integral coefficient box \([0,K]^{2(w+1)}\), where \(K=\lfloor e^{(W/2+\epsilon)n}\rfloor\).
Put \(J=\sum_it_i\deg P_i\).
The [divided-derivative count](Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#seeds) with the [individual orders](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) bounds the number of jet images by
\[
             K^J \exp\bigl(n(w+1)W+O_w(\log(2n))\bigr).
\]
Thus some fiber has log cardinality at least \(n[2(w+1)\epsilon-J(W/2+\epsilon)]-O_w(\log(2n))\).
Every nonzero difference satisfies all contacts and has maximum coefficient norm at least \(\exp((W/2-o(1))n)\), by the assumed lower bound and the fixed width.
If all differences from one fixed point in the fiber were proportional over \(\mathbb Q(Y)\), their real span would have dimension at most \(w+1\).
Indeed one polynomial coordinate projects their rational span injectively into the polynomials of degree at most \(w\), by proportionality to a single nonzero pair and choice of its nonzero coordinate.
In any \(d\)-dimensional normed space, disjoint balls of radius half the minimum separation about points of a ball of radius \(K\) give cardinality at most \((1+2K/{\rm separation})^d\) by volume comparison.
Apply this with the induced maximum coefficient norm after translation by the fixed point.
The resulting upper log cardinality would be \((w+1)(\epsilon+o(1))n\).
Fixing \(w\) so large that \((w+1)\epsilon>J(W/2+\epsilon)\) contradicts the lower count eventually.

We obtain two difference pairs \((U_1,V_1),(U_2,V_2)\) of this width with nonzero determinant \(D=U_1V_2-U_2V_1\) and entries bounded in one-norm by \((w+1)K\).
Let \(Q_i\) be the minimal polynomial of \(c_i^n\) for a root \(c_i\) of \(P_i\).
Separation makes the \(Q_i\) distinct of log measure \(nh_i\).
Near \(Y=c_i^n\) choose the analytic branch \(x(Y)\) of \(x^n=Y\) through \(c_i\).
Then \(U_k-x(Y)V_k\) vanishes to order at least \(t_i\).
Subtracting the products of these errors with \(V_2,V_1\) shows \(Q_i^{t_i}\mid D\).
If both pairs vanished at that powered node, both denominators \(V_k\) would vanish there and give \(Q_i^{t_i+1}\mid D\).
The resulting measure lower bound \(n(W+h_i)\) would exceed \(\log\|D\|_1\le n(W+2\epsilon)+O_w(1)\).
Thus for each \(i\) at least one pair is nonzero there.
Some integer combination of the first pair plus \(k\) times the second, with \(0\le k\le\#\{i\}\), keeps all the values nonzero since each orbit forbids at most one \(k\).
The [signed projections](Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) and [width padding](Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) retain the contacts and nonzero values at either prescribed sign for bounded additional norm cost.

A Salem or positive quadratic reciprocal unit has just one exterior conjugate, so \(h_i=r_i\).
Its powers retain their degree because a collision of two conjugates would, by conjugating the pair, give a second conjugate of the same modulus as the unique exterior root.
Two such units with distinct exterior values have no cross-orbit power collision either, since their powered minima have different unique exterior roots.
Their powers are again units of the indicated types.
The one-node and two-node lower bounds therefore give the asserted genuine attainment.
