# A Mahler gap from the circle norm of a partial interlacing quotient

<a id="statement"></a>
## A quotient of unrestricted degree

Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic of degree \(m\) and put \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\).
Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic of degree \(g\), with distinct roots \(y_1<\cdots<y_g\) that are traces of roots of unity.
Suppose there are roots \(t_0<y_1<t_1<\cdots<y_g<t_g\) of \(S\), with at most one of the selected \(t_j\) outside \([-2,2]\).
The remaining roots of \(S\) are unrestricted.
Allow \(A=1\) and let \(J\) be the polynomial quotient in the Euclidean division of \(S\) by \(A\).

Fix \(B>0\).
If
\[
                    \int_0^{2\pi}|J(2\cos\theta)|\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
                         \le B,                                        \tag{1}
\]
then \(M(P)=1\) or \(\log M(P)\ge\delta(B)>0\).
When \(M(P)\le2\), an integer polynomial with a bounded number of terms in terms of \(B\) has exactly the same Mahler measure as \(P\).
There is no degree restriction on \(J\).

On any sequence of Salem numbers tending to one, the infimum of the norm in (1) over all such partial torsion interlacers of each trace polynomial tends to infinity.
Every such interlacer for a Salem trace automatically meets the one-exterior-root condition, and \(A=1\) makes the collection nonempty.
In particular this applies to the monic parts of all strict integer interlacers on a sequence of lower-degree Salem minima, by the [descent at these minima](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#records).

<a id="orthogonality"></a>
## Interpolation between the poles and the selected roots

Suppose \(M(P)\le2\) and \(g\ge1\).
As in the [partial interlacing identities](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#poles), every trace root has modulus at most \(5/2\).
Put \(S_0(x)=\prod_{j=0}^g(x-t_j)\).
The two reciprocal partial fractions are
\[
 H(x)=\frac{A(x)}{S_0(x)}=\sum_{j=0}^g\frac{p_j}{x-t_j},\qquad
 \frac1{H(x)}=x-a-\sum_{l=1}^g\frac{\rho_l}{x-y_l},
\]
where \(p_j,\rho_l>0\), \(\sum_jp_j=1\), \(a=\sum_jp_jt_j\) and
\(\sum_l\rho_l=\sum_jp_jt_j^2-a^2\le25/4\).

Write \(R=J-S/A=\sum_l\lambda_l/(x-y_l)\), allowing zero residues.
Then \(RH=JH-S/S_0\) is proper, is \(O(x^{-2})\) at infinity and has residues \(p_jJ(t_j)\) at its only possible poles \(t_j\).
Thus
\[
 RH=\sum_j\frac{p_jJ(t_j)}{x-t_j},\qquad
 \sum_j p_jJ(t_j)=0,\qquad
 -\frac{\lambda_l}{\rho_l}=\sum_j\frac{p_jJ(t_j)}{y_l-t_j}.             \tag{2}
\]
The last identity follows by taking the limit at \(y_l\), since \(H'(y_l)=-1/\rho_l\).

The real \(g\)-by-\((g+1)\) matrix \(U_{lj}=\sqrt{\rho_l p_j}/(y_l-t_j)\) has orthonormal rows.
Indeed \(\sum_jp_j/(y_l-t_j)^2=-H'(y_l)=1/\rho_l\).
For two different rows the off-diagonal sum vanishes by subtracting \(H(y_l)=H(y_{l'})=0\) and dividing by \(y_l-y_{l'}\).
The unit vector with entries \(\sqrt{p_j}\) spans the kernel because \(H(y_l)=0\) for every row.
Hence (2) even gives the identity
\[
                    \sum_l\frac{\lambda_l^2}{\rho_l}
                              =\sum_j p_j J(t_j)^2.                      \tag{3}
\]
For the single exterior column we use an absolute first moment instead of (3).

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Uniform total variation with one exceptional column

Let \(C=\sup_{-2\le x\le2}|J(x)|\).
Restrict the vector \((\sqrt{p_j}J(t_j))_j\) to \(|t_j|\le2\), setting any exceptional column to zero.
It has Euclidean norm at most \(C\).
By the row orthogonality, (2) and Cauchy–Schwarz, the contribution of these columns to \(\sum_l|\lambda_l|\) is at most \(C\sqrt{\sum_l\rho_l}\le5C/2\).

If there is an exceptional root \(t_e\), the middle identity in (2) gives \(|p_eJ(t_e)|\le C\).
As \(t_e\) is an extreme selected root, all \(t_e-y_l\) have the same sign, and evaluating \(1/H\) at \(t_e\) gives
\[
              \sum_l\frac{\rho_l}{|t_e-y_l|}
                 =\left|\sum_l\frac{\rho_l}{t_e-y_l}\right|
                 =|t_e-a|\le5.
\]
Thus the exceptional contribution is at most \(5C\), including for an exceptional root below \(-2\).
Together the two estimates yield \(\sum_l|\lambda_l|\le15C/2\).

Write \(Q(z)=J(z+z^{-1})\) and let \(s_Q\) count its nonzero Laurent coefficients.
They are integers of absolute value at most the norm in (1).
The [integer Fourier support inequality](../audits/literature/JKS24/Yin_U001.md#jks24-u001) gives \(\log(1+s_Q)\le52B\).
Consequently \(C\le Bs_Q\), so both the number of Laurent terms and the total variation are bounded in terms of \(B\).

<a id="measure"></a>
## Removal of the torsion denominators

The [cyclotomic clearing argument](Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#sparse) uses the degree of the quotient only to bound its number of terms.
For clarity, the moments \(\sum_l\lambda_l y_l^k\), \(k\ge0\), are integers by expansion of \(R=J-S/A\) at infinity, since \(A\) is monic integral.
Choose \(\xi_l+\xi_l^{-1}=y_l\) with \(\xi_l\) a root of unity.
The integer trace recurrence gives a periodic integer sequence \(F_k=\sum_l\lambda_l(\xi_l^k+\xi_l^{-k})\).
Its Fourier algebra norm on the common finite period group is at most \(2\sum_l|\lambda_l|\).
By [Sanders's theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001), it is a sum of \(s\) signed congruence indicators, with \(s\) bounded in terms of \(B\).
Write the signs as \(\epsilon_i\), moduli as \(d_i\) and least positive representatives as \(r_i\).
With \(V=\prod_{i=1}^s(1-z^{d_i})\), \(V_i=V/(1-z^{d_i})\), \(b=\sum_l\lambda_l\in\mathbb Z\), and \(r=m-g=\deg J\), the cleared polynomial is
\[
 W=(1-z^2)V z^r Q(z)
    -z^{r+1}\left(bV+\sum_{i=1}^s\epsilon_i z^{r_i}V_i\right).
\]
Indeed summing the geometric series in the pole expansion gives
\(W\,z^g A(z+z^{-1})=(1-z^2)VP\).
The monic quotient gives \(W(0)=1\).
The term count is at most \((2s_Q+1+s)2^s\), also when \(s=0\).
Every factor other than \(W,P\) in the cleared identity has Mahler measure one.
Therefore \(M(W)=M(P)\).
For \(g=0\) simply take \(W=P=z^m Q\), whose term count has the same bound from (1).
The [bounded-term gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) applied to \(W\), together with the threshold \(\log 2\) for larger measures, proves the assertion.
If the infima along a Salem sequence stayed bounded on a subsequence, choosing a quotient within one of each infimum would contradict this gap.
