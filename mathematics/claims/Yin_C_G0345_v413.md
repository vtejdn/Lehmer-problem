# The exact coefficient growth rate at the degree of a Salem seed

<a id="statement"></a>
## Quantized growth and the full cutoff through width eight

Let \(P\) be the minimum of a Salem number \(\rho>1\) of degree \(e=2s\ge4\), and put \(h=\log\rho\).
For each integer \(p\ge2\), take the [least coefficient cost for this seed](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) at row degree at most \(e\), before imposing any height cutoff.
The rows are integral, are coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), satisfy \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\), and have \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0\), where \(\iota(Y)=1/Y\).
Write \(H_p(P)\) for their least \(\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
Such rows exist at every sufficiently large integer \(p\).
There is an integer \(d(P)\le2e\), determined by polynomial minors over the fixed splitting field, for which
\[
          \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_p(P)}p=\frac{d(P)}2 h .
\]
One has \(d(P)\ge5\) for \(e=4\) and \(d(P)\ge6\) for \(e\ge6\).
For \(P=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\) the exact value is \(d(P)=16\), so the rate is \(8\log\rho\).
Consequently the [unrestricted coefficient minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) at every row degree through eight is \(2^p\) for every sufficiently large odd prime, with no genuine pair even at equality.
These assertions give no numerical onset.

<a id="directions"></a>
## Two signed polynomial directions and existence

Choose one root \(x_i\) from each inverse pair, \(1\le i\le s\), in the splitting field \(L\).
These representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, since for each \(i\) some embedding sends \(x_i\) to \(\rho\) and every other representative to the unit circle.
All positive powers preserve the degree.
For sign \(\epsilon=\pm1\) and indeterminates \(N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\), form the \(e\)-by-\((e+1)\) matrix with the following two entries for root \(i\) in column \(j=0,\ldots,e\):
\[
 Y_i^j+\epsilon x_iY_i^{e-j},\qquad
 NjY_i^j+\epsilon x_i(1+N(e-j))Y_i^{e-j}.
\]
The value and Euler derivative at \(x_i\) give these entries when \(N=p\) and \(Y_i=x_i^p\).
Formal reversal supplies the equations at \(x_i^{-1}\).
More explicitly put \(A^*(Y)=Y^e A(1/Y)\), \(f=A+\epsilon x_i A^*\), \(g=NY f'+\epsilon x_i A^*\).
Replacing \(x_i,Y\) by \(x_i^{-1},Y^{-1}\) sends the pair of expressions to \(\epsilon x_i^{-1}Y^{-e}(f,(Ne+1)f-g)\).
Thus either choice of each representative gives the same linear conditions after inverting its variable.

Let \(a_{\epsilon,j}\) be \((-1)^j\) times the maximal minor deleting column \(j\).
At \(N=0\) both signed systems simply require \(A(Y_i)=A^*(Y_i)=0\) for all \(i\).
Over \(L(\mathbf Y)\) their unique monic solution is \(\prod_i(Z-Y_i)(Z-Y_i^{-1})\), with constant term one.
Both systems therefore have generic rank \(e\), and the edge expression \(a_{+,0}a_{-,e}+a_{+,e}a_{-,0}\) is nonzero at \(N=0\).
Divide each signed tuple of minors by the gcd of its nonzero entries in \(L[N,\mathbf Y]\), then clear constant denominators to get an integral polynomial tuple \(F_{\epsilon,j}\) with no common nonconstant factor.

Every nonzero polynomial in these variables specializes along \((p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_s^p)\) to an eventually nonzero sequence.
Grouping its terms by exponent vector gives a power sum with polynomial coefficients and distinct bases whose ratios are nontorsion.
The [fixed-place estimate for such recurrences](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch), after division by the power of a base of maximal modulus at one embedding, gives eventual nonvanishing.
This applies to a nonzero minor at each sign, its cleared common factor and the edge expression.
The two specialized signed kernels are therefore lines, and their edge expression is nonzero for all sufficiently large integers \(p\).
The lines have primitive rational integer generators because the signed conditions are equivalently remainders modulo \(P^2\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
The [two-line formula](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) now gives coprime qualifying pairs at every such \(p\).
It also bounds their least cost above and below by constant multiples, depending only on \(e\), of the maximum coefficient of the two primitive generators in absolute value.
Indeed the parity multiplier is one or two, and the larger one-norm of the sum and difference lies between the larger of the individual one-norms and twice that number.

<a id="height"></a>
## Projective height and symmetry in the inverse pairs

For one sign let \(S\) be the union of the supports in the \(Y\)-variables of its nonzero reduced entries \(F_j\), allowing coefficients in \(L[N]\).
Write \(W_i=\max_{a\in S}a_i-\min_{a\in S}a_i\).
At any embedding \(\sigma:L\to\mathbb C\), the log maximum of the specialized entries is
\[
       p\max_{a\in S}\sum_i a_i\log|\sigma x_i|+o_P(p).
\]
For each nonzero entry, divide by the \(p\)-th power of a base of maximum modulus there.
The same fixed-place estimate gives a lower bound \(-o_P(p)\) for the normalized logarithm, and the triangle inequality gives the upper bound \(O_P(\log(2p))\).
In particular ties among the leading moduli are allowed.

The sum of the common finite valuations of the entries, weighted by \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\), is \(o_P(p)\).
To see this choose one nonzero entry and an integer linear combination of the entries avoiding all its nonconstant irreducible factors.
Each factor forbids a proper linear subspace of coefficients, so such a choice exists.
For a constant entry the finite bound follows directly.
Otherwise the two resulting integral polynomials are coprime in \(L[N,\mathbf Y]\), and their common valuations bound those of the whole tuple above.
Apply [Xiao's almost-unit gcd estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) with the archimedean places as the allowed set.
The excess from the integer coordinate \(p\) is \(\log p\), and the point has height at least \(ph/e\).
For each fixed tolerance its proper exceptional closed set is avoided eventually by the nonvanishing argument above, applied to a nonzero polynomial vanishing on that set.
Letting the tolerance tend to zero proves the bound.

For each \(i\), exactly \(1/e\) of the embeddings send \(x_i\) to \(\rho\) and \(1/e\) to \(\rho^{-1}\).
All other representatives at those embeddings have modulus one.
Hence the projective log height of the specialized tuple is \(ph\sum_i W_i/e+o_P(p)\).
This height equals the logarithm of the maximum absolute coefficient of the primitive rational generator of its line.

The widths \(W_i\) are all equal for this sign.
Each Galois element permutes the inverse pairs and may invert their representatives.
Apply it to the coefficients and simultaneously send \(Y_i\) to the correspondingly permuted variable or its inverse, according to its action on \(x_i\).
By the reversal identity above the transformed equations have the original kernel over \(L(N,\mathbf Y)\).
In the Laurent polynomial ring \(L[N,Y_1^{\pm1},\ldots,Y_s^{\pm1}]\) both the transformed and the original kernel tuple have no common nonunit factor.
Proportionality and unique factorization make their ratio a unit, hence a constant times a Laurent monomial.
Its translation of every support does not affect widths, and inversion preserves width in the inverted coordinate.
Transitivity of the action on inverse pairs now gives equality of all \(W_i\).
Each is at most \(2e\), since only two matrix rows involve \(Y_i\), each of degree at most \(e\), and division by a common polynomial cannot increase width.
Taking \(d(P)\) to be the larger of the two signed widths proves the rate formula.
The [row norm bound at \(b=e\)](Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#salem) gives \(d(P)/2\ge3-2/e\), proving the integer lower bounds.

<a id="octic"></a>
## An exact rate for the octic and exhaustion at width eight

Take the four representatives of the octic at a prime over \(1987\) with residues \(308,670,1452,1555\), using its [eight distinct reduced roots](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#specialization).
Set \(N=5\) and \((Y_2,Y_3,Y_4)=(3,5,7)\).
Regard \(a_{\epsilon,1},a_{\epsilon,2}\) as polynomials in \(T=Y_1\) over this residue field.
Both have degree 16 for both signs.

| Sign | Leading coefficient of minor 1 | Leading coefficient of minor 2 | Resultant of minors 1 and 2 modulo 1987 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(+\) | 737 | 1635 | 1924 |
| \(-\) | 1721 | 515 | 633 |

To reproduce the table, evaluate each signed minor at the integers \(T=0,\ldots,16\).
For its value list \(v_t\) recover the polynomial as \(\sum_{k=0}^{16}(\Delta^kv)_0\binom{T}{k}\) modulo \(1987\).
Take the determinant of the Sylvester matrix of the resulting two degree-sixteen polynomials, with minor 1 first.
All divisions by \(k!\) are valid in this field.
The leading coefficients and the resultant of the original minors in \(Y_1\) are consequently nonzero over the generic coefficient field.
The two minors are coprime in that variable, so the common polynomial removed from the entire tuple has degree zero in \(Y_1\).
At least one of the two minors has nonzero constant term in \(Y_1\) as well, since otherwise their resultant would vanish.
The common width is therefore 16 at both signs.

Even the general lower rate \(3h\) suffices for the cutoff, since the [root comparison](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#specialization) gives \(\rho>32/25\) and \(32^3>2\cdot25^3\).
The [signed value ratio](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#ratio) independently gives the lower rate \(45h/16\) for this seed.
At odd powers simultaneous variable sign change preserves costs and double divisibility.
The [octic census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0178/versions/v2/readme.md#census) exhausts 54 sign classes within \(M(P)\le2\).
The one-row norm bound already excludes the remaining 53 at width eight.
The [degree reduction and lower-degree exclusions](Yin_C_G0345_v412.md#seeds) leave no other possibilities there, proving the full claim.
The argument at growing seed degree still requires control of both the integer \(d(P)\) and the measure, and width above the seed degree has higher-dimensional kernels.
