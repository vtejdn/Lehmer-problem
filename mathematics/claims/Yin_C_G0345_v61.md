# Descent by common powers and by commensurable radii of two exterior conjugate pairs

<a id="statement"></a>
## Four exterior roots in two conjugate pairs

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2m\) with exactly four exterior roots, arranged as two nonreal conjugate pairs.
Write their radii as \(R\le S\).
When \(R<S\) and \(\log S/\log R=a/b\) is rational in lowest terms, there exists a Salem number or a real reciprocal quadratic unit \(\gamma>1\) of degree at most \(m\) such that
\[
                     R^2=\gamma^b,\qquad S^2=\gamma^a,\qquad
                     M(P)=\gamma^{a+b}.
\]
In particular this case cannot give a minimum of Mahler measure greater than one among monic integer polynomials of degree at most \(2m\).

For any number \(e>0\) of exterior roots, a quotient of distinct roots that is torsion gives an [exact reduction](#fibers) to fewer exterior roots and smaller degree with the same Mahler measure.
For the four-exterior input this covers collisions anywhere among the roots, including a collision on the unit circle.

If \(R=S\) and there are no such collisions, \(\theta=M(P)=R^4\) is an algebraic unit whose modulus strictly exceeds that of every other distinct conjugate.
It has at most \(49\) exterior conjugates, and \(M(\theta)\le M(P)^{25}\), where \(M(\theta)\) denotes the Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial.
Either that polynomial is reciprocal or its degree is at most \(99\).
Hence along any such sequence with \(M(P)\to1\), the resulting units \(\theta\) are eventually reciprocal, of unbounded degree.
If no conjugate of \(\theta\) has either intermediate modulus \(R^2\) or \(R^{-2}\), then \(\theta\) is itself Salem or a real reciprocal quadratic unit.

<a id="fibers"></a>
## Maximal classes of roots with torsion quotient

Let \(P\) now have \(e>0\) exterior roots and otherwise the general monic, irreducible and reciprocal hypotheses above.
Partition its roots by \(\xi\sim\xi'\) when \(\xi/\xi'\) is a root of unity.
The partition is invariant under Galois automorphisms and inversion.
All classes have a common size \(f\) by transitivity, and \(f\mid e\) because each class has constant modulus.
For each class \(C\) put \(v_C=\prod_{\xi\in C}\xi\).
These are algebraic units.
Choose a common multiple \(N\) of the finitely many orders of the torsion quotients within classes.
Then \(v_C^N=\xi^{fN}\) for \(\xi\in C\).
If \(v_C=v_D\) for two classes, the last identity forces their representatives to have torsion quotient, so \(C=D\).
In fact the same argument applies if \(v_C/v_D\) is torsion.
The distinct class products form one Galois orbit closed under inversion.
Their monic polynomial is consequently integral, irreducible and reciprocal of degree \(2m/f\).
There are exactly \(e/f\) exterior roots and their product of moduli equals \(M(P)\).
Thus \(f>1\) gives the stated exact descent, and the new polynomial has no torsion collision.
This extends the [single exterior power fiber](Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#general) to all classes at once.

<a id="matching"></a>
## Unequal commensurable radii force a partition into pairs

Return to the four exterior roots and assume \(a>b\) as in the statement.
Let \(A\) be the two inverse-pair labels of the roots \(\lambda,\bar\lambda\) of radius \(R\), and \(B\) the two labels of \(\mu,\bar\mu\) of radius \(S\).
A label denotes the unordered pair consisting of a root and its inverse.
These four labels are distinct.
Write \(\alpha=\lambda\bar\lambda=R^2,\ \beta=\mu\bar\mu=S^2\).
Their equality \(\alpha^a=\beta^b\) is an identity of algebraic units since both products are positive real.
Use the common log unit \(\log R/b=\log S/a\).
For an automorphism \(g\) of the splitting field the signed sums \(F,G\) of log weights on \(g(A),g(B)\), respecting the images of the chosen exterior representatives, satisfy \(aF=bG\).
The available individual weights are \(0,\pm b,\pm a\), so \(a\mid G\).
If \(g(B)\) meets a small-radius label, its other label must be the other small-radius label, since a single contribution \(\pm b\) cannot cancel modulo \(a\) against \(0,\pm a\).

The Galois translates of \(B\) cover all \(m\) labels by transitivity.
If two translates share a label, apply an automorphism carrying it to a small-radius label.
Both resulting translates equal \(A\) by the divisibility just proved.
Thus the translates partition the labels into pairs, and \(A\) itself is a block of that partition.
Each \(g(A)\) is consequently either \(A\), \(B\) or a block with both labels neutral.

When \(g(A)=B\), the two image representatives must have opposite log signs.
Equal signs would give \(|F|=2a\), hence \(|G|=2a^2/b>2a\), impossible for a sum of two weights.
Thus the only possible exterior value of \(g(\alpha)\) is \(\alpha\) itself, on \(A\) with both representatives exterior.
Its only possible interior value is \(\alpha^{-1}\), and that value must occur by the unit norm.
Similarly \(|g(\beta)|>1\) implies \(|g(\alpha)|>1\), giving \(|g(\beta)|=S^2\).
This requires both large exterior roots themselves, so \(g(\beta)=\beta\).
The corresponding interior value is necessarily \(\beta^{-1}\).
In particular the stabilizers of \(\alpha,\beta\) coincide, and their generated fields are equal.

There are at most two distinct conjugate values of \(\alpha\) per block.
Indeed choose \(h\) sending \(A\) to \(B\).
It sends the two outward choices on \(A\) to opposite log signs on \(B\).
If an element stabilizing \(A\) took the outward choices to one outward and one inward on \(A\), composing with \(h\) would produce equal signs on \(B\), which has been excluded.
Thus the set stabilizer of \(A\) sends \(\alpha\) only to \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\).
There are \(m/2\) blocks, proving \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]\le m\).

Choose \(u,v\in\mathbb Z\) with \(bu+av=1\) and put \(\gamma=\alpha^u\beta^v>1\).
Then \(\gamma^b=\alpha,\ \gamma^a=\beta\), and \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(\beta)\).
For any conjugate of \(\gamma\) of modulus greater than one its powers are \(\alpha,\beta\) themselves, so it equals \(\gamma\).
The same argument gives \(\gamma^{-1}\) for every interior conjugate.
That conjugate exists by the unit norm, and inversion therefore preserves the orbit.
All others lie on the unit circle, giving the claimed Salem or quadratic classification and the exact measure identity.

<a id="equal"></a>
## Counting the intermediate conjugates when both radii are equal

Assume \(R=S\) and distinct roots of \(P\) never have torsion quotient.
Here \(\alpha=R^2\) has two factorizations into disjoint pairs of actual exterior roots, one for each conjugate pair.
Every conjugate \(c\) of \(\alpha\) inherits two disjoint factorizations into roots of \(P\).
The possible root moduli are \(R,1,R^{-1}\).
If \(|c|=R^2\), both factorizations use two exterior roots, so together they use the four exterior roots exactly once.
Thus \(c^2=\lambda\bar\lambda\mu\bar\mu=R^4=\theta\).
All conjugates of \(\theta=\alpha^2\) are squares of conjugates of \(\alpha\), so \(\theta\) is the sole distinct value of largest modulus.

If \(|c|=R\), the two factorizations give \(c=xu=yv\) with distinct exterior roots \(x,y\) and neutral roots \(u,v\).
Fix an order of the two chosen exterior roots.
Then \(u/v=y/x\) is nontorsion.
By [Kronecker's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-u001) and the unit norm there is an embedding of the splitting field under which \(|g(y/x)|>1\).
Each representation of this quotient as \(u/v\) must then have \(gu\) exterior or \(gv\) interior.
There are at most four choices of the former and four of the latter, and either choice determines its partner from the fixed quotient.
Hence at most eight distinct \(c\)'s arise for this unordered pair \(x,y\).
There are only \(\binom42=6\) choices of the pair, so at most \(48\) intermediate exterior values \(c\) and therefore at most \(48\) conjugates of \(\theta\) of modulus \(R^2\).
Together with its unique exterior value of modulus \(R^4\) this gives \(M(\theta)\le R^{4+96}=M(P)^{25}\).

A unit-circle conjugate of \(\theta\) would have its inverse as its complex conjugate, so the minimal polynomial of \(\theta\) would be reciprocal.
In the nonreciprocal case all other conjugates are strictly interior or exterior.
Every interior conjugate has modulus \(R^{-2}\) or \(R^{-4}\).
The unit norm and the upper bound \(R^{100}\) on the total exterior product allow at most \(50\) interior roots.
This gives degree at most \(49+50=99\).
For bounded degree and bounded largest conjugate modulus, only finitely many monic integer polynomials occur, so this case cannot persist with \(\theta\to1\).
The same finiteness forces the reciprocal degrees to tend to infinity.
If both intermediate moduli are absent, the unit norm forces one interior conjugate of modulus \(R^{-4}\).
Its two factorizations before squaring use all four interior roots, so it equals \(\theta^{-1}\).
The minimal polynomial is then reciprocal with a single exterior root, which proves the last assertion.
