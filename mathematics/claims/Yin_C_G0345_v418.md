# The total Mahler measure of repeated factors of a rational fixed-point equation

<a id="statement"></a>
## A simultaneous bound from the rational function alone

Let \(f=u/v\) be a nonzero rational function over \(\mathbb Q\) satisfying \(f(Y)f(1/Y)=1\), represented by coprime integral polynomials of primitive joint content.
Write \(d=\max(\deg u,\deg v)\).
On the unit circle \(|u|=|v|\), so the two logarithmic Mahler measures are equal.
For an integer \(p\ge2\) put \(G(X)=u(X^p)-Xv(X^p)\).
For each distinct primitive irreducible factor \(P\) of \(G\) with a nonzero nontorsion root and with multiplicity \(t_P\ge2\), count its measure once with the indicated weight.
Then
\[
             p\sum_P(t_P-1)m(P)\ \le\ 2m(u)+\log(1+pd).                 \tag{1}
\]
The bound holds with nonmonic and nonreciprocal factors.
Scaling away common divisors in a supplied signed presentation before applying (1) also removes repeated factors introduced solely by that presentation.

There is a finite approximation to the unrestricted Lehmer infimum \(\lambda\) using only the degree and Mahler measure of these rational functions.
At prime \(p\) take \(a,b,C_p,D_p\) from the [explicit high-multiplicity test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#statement).
Let \(B_p\) be the minimum of \(C_p\) and the multiplicative measures \(M(u)=\exp m(u)\le C_p\) of these reduced functions with \(d\le b\) and at least one such factor with \(t_P\ge a\).
Then the real algebraic quantities \(B_p\) are computable and
\[
     Z_p=\frac{2\log B_p+\log(1+pb)}{(a-1)p}\ge\lambda,\qquad
                            Z_p\longrightarrow\lambda .                 \tag{2}
\]
There is also a constraint on the distribution of the moduli of the numerator and denominator roots.
In order of increasing log modulus, the nonzero roots of \(uv\) can be matched, with multiplicities, to the nonzero roots of an integral polynomial containing every powered repeated factor.
Each matched pair of log moduli differs by at most \(4d\log(2+2pd)\).
In the explicit width of (2) this difference is \(o(p)\), uniformly over all the functions tested there.

<a id="circle"></a>
## All the repeated factors share one derivative budget

The two summands in \(G\) occupy distinct exponent classes, hence \(G\ne0\).
At any nonzero root \(\alpha\), coprimality ensures \(v(z)\ne0\) for \(z=\alpha^p\).
Since \(\alpha=f(z)\), the fields generated by \(\alpha,z\) agree.
For their positive-leading primitive integer minima \(P,Q\), the [height normalization](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) gives \(m(Q)=p\,m(P)\).
Distinct factors \(P\) give distinct \(Q\), since all conjugates of \(f(z)\) over the conjugates of a given \(z\) form one orbit.
The [derivative identity and exact order at a nonzero root](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination), applied with rows \(u,-v\), put
\[
                    \prod_P Q^{t_P-1}\ \mid\
               W=pY(u'v-uv')-uv\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[Y].
\]
The lowest coefficient of \(uv\) acquires the nonzero multiplier \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0u-\operatorname{ord}_0v)-1\), so \(W\ne0\).
It follows that the left side of (1) is at most \(m(W)\).

There are no zeros or poles of the reduced \(f\) on the unit circle.
Indeed the reflection \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) exchanges the zero and pole divisors of \(f\), and a reduced rational function cannot have both at a fixed point of that reflection.
Dividing out the finite Blaschke factors \(B_c(Y)=(Y-c)/(1-\bar cY)\) at each disk zero and multiplying them back at each disk pole leaves a constant of modulus one.
This follows by reflection at the exterior points including infinity, with \(B_0(Y)=Y\).
The combined number of disk zeros and disk poles with multiplicity is \(d\), since the disk poles correspond to exterior zeros and the total zero divisor on the sphere has degree \(d\).
Constant functions have \(d=0\).
At \(|Y|=1\), the [Poisson formula](../audits/literature/GMR18/Yin_U001.md#gmr18-u001) gives \(YB'_c/B_c=(1-|c|^2)/|Y-c|^2\), a nonnegative function of normalized mean one.
It follows by factoring \(f\) that \(\int_{\mathbb T}|Yf'/f|\le d\).
Thus Jensen's inequality and the rational identity \(W/(uv)=pYf'/f-1\) give
\[
        m(W)-m(u)-m(v)
                 \le\log\int_{\mathbb T}|pYf'/f-1|
                 \le\log(1+pd).
\]
Isolated zeros of the integrand have integrable logarithms.
This proves (1).
The additive constant is attained by \(f=Y^{-d}\), for which \(W=-(pd+1)Y^d\).
That example concerns the differential inequality and has no nontorsion repeated factors.

<a id="minimum"></a>
## Intrinsic measure in the finite test

There are only finitely many eligible reduced pairs up to simultaneous sign with \(d\le b,\ M(u)\le C_p\).
For each coefficient of either polynomial of degree \(j\le b\), the elementary symmetric estimate bounds its absolute value by \(C_p\binom ji\) in position \(i\).
The equality \(m(v)=m(u)\) supplies the same bounds for the denominator.
The circle identity is an exact polynomial test, multiplicative measures are real algebraic, and the [powered-factor test](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) decides the root condition by factoring \(W\) and testing \(f(z)^p=z\) in a number field.
This proves finite computability without any given seed.
For a genuine function the lower estimate in (2) follows from (1).
For the fallback \(2\lfloor ap/2\rfloor\ge(a-1)p\) and \(\lambda\le\log2\).

For all sufficiently large primes the [single signed coefficient construction](Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#statement) gives a winning row \(A\) below its fallback with graph sign \(\varepsilon\).
Reduce the pair \((-A,\varepsilon A_b^*)\) by its primitive polynomial gcd and its joint scalar content to obtain \(u,v\).
Its rational function satisfies the circle identity and \(d\le b\).
The common divisor is nonzero at the designated powered root, so its removal preserves the multiplicity in \(G\).
Integral factorization gives \(m(u)\le m(A)\le\log\|A\|_1\).
Consequently \(B_p\) is at most the coefficient minimum, and its normalized upper limit is at most \(\lambda\) by the explicit high-multiplicity theorem.
Together with the lower bound this proves (2), and the degree-ten benchmark in the same theorem makes the fallback strictly larger than the minimum eventually.

If \(\lambda>0\), each minimizing function for all sufficiently large \(p\) has exactly one nontorsion irreducible factor \(P_*\) of multiplicity at least \(a\).
In fact (1) bounds \(\sum_P(t_P-1)\le(a-1)Z_p/\lambda=(a-1)(1+o(1))\).
The designated factor uses at least \(a-1\) of this sum, hence \(t_{P_*}=a+o(a)\) and \(\sum_{P\ne P_*}(t_P-1)=o(a)\), with the latter sum over the other repeated nontorsion factors.
This consequence would distinguish a positive gap from a sequence of minimizing functions with two different factors of the required high multiplicity.
It does not supply such a sequence.

<a id="radii"></a>
## Matching the logarithmic root radii

Write \(L=\log(2+2pd)\) and count the nonzero roots of \(uv\) with multiplicities, in number \(n\le2d\).
At zero and at the leading exponent, the coefficients of \(W\) are the corresponding extreme coefficients of \(uv\) multiplied respectively by \(p(\operatorname{ord}_0 u-\operatorname{ord}_0v)-1\) and \(p(\deg u-\deg v)-1\).
Both integers are nonzero.
Thus \(W\) and \(uv\) have equal degrees and equal orders at zero.

Take a circle of radius \(r>0\) whose log radius is at distance at least \(L\) from the log modulus of every nonzero root of \(uv\).
Write \(k\) for the number of zeros of \(u\) inside the circle minus the number of zeros of \(v\) there, counting zero and multiplicities.
On the circle \(Y f'/f\) is the signed sum of \(Y/(Y-\gamma)\) over their roots.
For a root inside, the term differs from one by at most \(1/(\exp L-1)\), and for a root outside its modulus is bounded by the same number.
Each zero at the origin contributes exactly one before its sign.
It follows that
\[
              \left|\frac{W}{uv}-(pk-1)\right|
                  \le\frac{2pd}{\exp L-1}<1\le |pk-1|.
\]
Rouché's theorem applied to the polynomials \(W\) and \((pk-1)uv\) gives the same number of zeros inside.
It also excludes zeros of \(W\) on any such circle.

Take the union of the closed intervals of radius \(L\) about the \(n\) log moduli of \(uv\).
Every nonzero root of \(W\) has its log modulus in this union.
Each connected component contains equal root counts on the two sides, by the circle comparisons at its endpoints, where no roots occur.
Its length is at most \(2nL\).
Ordering the two lists increasingly therefore matches every pair within \(2nL\le4dL\), including the empty case.
For the high-multiplicity parameters \(d\le b=O(p^{7/8})\), this bound is \(o(p)\).
In particular the \(t_P-1\) copies of the log radius \(p\log|\alpha|\) for each conjugate of each contacted \(P\) correspond to distinct counted roots of \(uv\) within that error.
For \(p\)-dependent inputs this is an absolute error on the normalized log moduli and need not separate radii approaching each other.
