# Energy of integral quotients and Fourier decay in hyperbolic periodic quotients

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds in terms of the logarithmic Mahler measure

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\), and suppose \(P\) has no root on the unit circle.
Write \(m=m(P)>0\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure, use normalized Haar measure for circle \(L^2\) norms, and put
\[
 K(m)=\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}} k\exp(-2km).
\]
Every \(A\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) which is not divisible by \(P\) satisfies
\[
                         \|A/P\|_2^2\ge K(m),
 \qquad K(m)\sim\frac1{2\mathrm e m}\quad(m\downarrow0).           \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\min_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\le K(m)^{-1/2}\).
Every nonzero square-summable centered solution \(t\) of \(P(S)t=0\bmod1\), where \((St)_j=t_{j+1}\), has \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}t_j^2\ge K(m)\).

Put \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) and \(G_n=R/(\alpha^n-1)R\).
Let \(F_n\) be the largest absolute Fourier coefficient at a nontrivial character of the law of \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\alpha^j\) in \(G_n\), for independent fair digits \(\xi_j\in\{0,1\}\).
Take \(F_n=0\) if \(G_n\) is trivial.
Its limit \(F(P)\) satisfies
\[
                      F(P)\le \exp(-\pi^2 K(m)/2).                 \tag{2}
\]
For \(n\ge2d\) put
\[
 a_n=\frac{\|P\|_1}{\min_{|z|=1}|P(z)|}\sqrt{\frac{2d}{n}},
 \qquad \|P\|_1=\sum_{i=0}^d|P_i|.
\]
If \(a_n<1\), every nonzero centered \(n\)-periodic solution has period energy \(E=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}t_j^2\) satisfying
\[
             E\ge\frac{K(m)}{(1+a_n)^2},
 \qquad F_n\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi^2K(m)}{2(1+a_n)^2}\right).   \tag{3}
\]
The bound applies to every nontrivial character at the indicated period.
The threshold for \(n\) depends on the given polynomial.

<a id="quotient"></a>
## Multiplication by the inner factor on occupied Taylor coefficients

Multiply \(A/P\) by a power of \(X\) to get \(H\) regular at zero with nonzero constant term.
Since \(P(0)=\pm1\), its Taylor germ has integer coefficients.
It has infinitely many nonzero coefficients, since a terminating germ would give an integral Laurent quotient.
Irreducibility and the Laurent nondivisibility of \(A\) imply that every interior root of \(P\) is a pole of \(H\).
Indeed divisibility over \(\mathbb Q\) after clearing negative exponents would also give integer divisibility by monic polynomial division.
Let \(I\) be the finite Blaschke product for these interior roots, normalized by \(I(0)>0\).
As \(|P(0)|=1\), the product of the moduli of its interior roots is \(\exp(-m)\), giving \(I(0)=e^{-m}\).
The product \(IH\) is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk and \(\|IH\|_2=\|A/P\|_2\), since \(|I|=1\) on the circle.

Take the first \(k\) nonzero coefficients \(h_1,\ldots,h_k\) of the Taylor germ of \(H\) at exponents \(s_1<\cdots<s_k\).
The [occupied-coefficient compression](Yin_C_G0345_v617.md#compression) for multiplication by \(I\) is the triangular matrix \(D_{ij}=[X^{s_i-s_j}]I\), with negative coefficients zero.
It is a contraction as an orthogonal compression of multiplication by an inner function, and its determinant is \(I(0)^k\).
Its least singular value is at least \(e^{-km}\) because the other singular values are at most one.
Every nonzero Taylor coefficient of \(H\) through exponent \(s_k\) occurs in the selected list.
Consequently \(D(h_i)_i\) is exactly the vector of coefficients of \(IH\) at these positions, even though \(H\) has poles inside the disk.
Parseval applied to \(IH\) gives
\[
           \|A/P\|_2^2\ge \|D(h_i)_i\|_2^2
                  \ge e^{-2km}\sum_i|h_i|^2\ge k e^{-2km}.
\]
The integer maximum exists since \(k e^{-2km}\to0\) as \(k\to\infty\).
The continuous maximum over positive real \(k\) is \(1/(2\mathrm e m)\) at \(k=1/(2m)\), and the value at \(k=\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor\) has ratio tending to one to that maximum as \(m\downarrow0\).
Taking \(A=1\) gives the circle-minimum assertion because \(\|1/P\|_2\le1/\min|P|\).

<a id="recurrence"></a>
## Energy of a centered recurrence and its Fourier product

For a centered square-summable solution, \(b=P(S)t\) is a square-summable integer sequence and hence is finitely supported.
With Fourier convention \(T(X)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z} t_j X^{-j}\), the shift \(S\) acts as multiplication by \(X\).
Thus \(T=B/P\) in \(L^2\), where \(B=\sum_j b_jX^{-j}\) is an integral Laurent polynomial.
If \(P\) divided \(B\), the coefficients of \(T\) would be integers, hence zero since they lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\).
For \(t\ne0\) this proves \(\sum_j t_j^2=\|B/P\|_2^2\ge K(m)\).
On the centered interval one has \(|\cos(\pi x)|\le e^{-\pi^2x^2/2}\), by integrating \(\tan y\ge y\) for \(0\le y<\pi/2\) and using symmetry and continuity.
For hyperbolic \(P\), the [periodic Fourier limit](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#hyperbolic) is the maximum of \(\prod_{j\in\mathbb Z}|\cos(\pi t_j)|\) over these nonzero centered square-summable solutions, giving (2).
In particular every sequence of hyperbolic unit minima with \(m\downarrow0\) satisfies \(\limsup m\log F(P)\le-\pi^2/(4\mathrm e)\).

<a id="period"></a>
## Cutting a periodic solution to an integral numerator

Choose centered representatives of the periodic solution periodically, including a consistent choice at half-integers.
For a cyclic starting index \(c\), put \(u_j=t_{c-j}\) for \(0\le j<n\), put \(u_j=0\) otherwise, and let \(U=\sum_j u_j X^j\).
The first and last \(d\) positions are disjoint for \(n\ge2d\).
Averaging over \(c\bmod n\) shows that some cut has total squared norm at these \(2d\) positions at most \(2dE/n\).
Fix such a cut, and write \(U_{\partial}\) for the polynomial consisting of those positions only.

For \(d\le j<n\) the coefficient of \(X^j\) in \(PU\) is \(\sum_{i=0}^d P_i t_{c-j+i}\), an integer by the recurrence modulo one.
Let \(A\) contain just these coefficients of \(PU\), with all other coefficients zero.
The error \(PU-A\) is supported in \([0,d-1]\cup[n,n+d-1]\).
On this support it agrees with \(PU_{\partial}\), since each term in the convolution there uses a position in the first or last \(d\) entries of \(U\).
Parseval and the triangle inequality for the shifts of \(U_{\partial}\) give
\[
        \|PU-A\|_2\le\|P U_{\partial}\|_2
          \le\|P\|_1\sqrt{2dE/n},\qquad
        \|A/P-U\|_2\le a_n\sqrt E .
\]
For every integral Laurent polynomial \(Q\), \(\|Q-U\|_2\ge\sqrt E\).
Indeed each centered real coefficient of \(U\) is at least as close to zero as to any other integer, with ties permitted.
Since \(E>0\) and \(a_n<1\), \(A/P\) cannot be an integral Laurent polynomial.
By (1) and the triangle inequality, \(\sqrt{K(m)}\le\|A/P\|_2\le(1+a_n)\sqrt E\).
Finally the [periodic character identification](Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#compactness) gives \(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}|\cos(\pi t_j)|\) for the amplitude at the character with phases \(t_j\).
The cosine estimate now gives (3).
