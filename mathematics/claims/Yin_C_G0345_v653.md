# The typical energy of integer multiples in a Laurent residue class

<a id="statement"></a>
## The logarithmic rate and the degree bound

Let \(P\) be the monic irreducible polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), of degree \(d\), with no root on the unit circle.
Put \(m=\log M(P)\) and fix a nonzero \(x\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
The order admits integral Laurent representatives since \(\alpha\) is a unit.
For an integral representative \(T\) of \(x\), let \(h_j\) be the real Laurent coefficients of \(T/P\) on the circle and write \(D(t)=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mathbb Z)\).
Define
\[
 E(kx)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}D(kh_j)^2,\qquad
 V(kx)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}D(kh_j)(1-D(kh_j)).
\]
These quantities depend only on the class \(kx\).
The first equals the minimum squared circle \(L^2\) norm of \(A/P\) over all integral Laurent representatives \(A\) of \(kx\), for Haar probability measure. [Nearest-integer minimization](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v320.md#rounding)

There is an explicit \(\Gamma_x>0\) such that, with \(k\) uniform on \(1,\ldots,K\), one has convergence in mean square of both coordinates as \(K\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{(E(kx),V(kx))}{\log K}
       \ \longrightarrow\ \Gamma_x(1/12,1/6).                       \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the ratios with denominator \(\log k\) have the same limits in natural density on \(k\ge2\).
Indeed \(\log k/\log K\to1\) in probability under this sampling.
For each fixed \(2\le p<\infty\), the infimum of \(\|A/P\|_p\) over integral Laurent representatives of \(kx\) is bounded above and below by positive multiples of \(\sqrt{\Gamma_x\log k}\) outside a set of density zero.
The lower bound follows from \(E(kx)\) and monotonicity of probability norms, and the upper bound uses a representative realizing the [variance bound for finite moments](Yin_C_G0345_v650.md#rounding).

Let \(f\) be the common size of a class of conjugates of \(\alpha\) under root-of-unity quotient.
Let \(\delta_-\) and \(\delta_+\) be the smallest of \(|\log|\rho||\) over interior and exterior roots, respectively.
Then
\[
 \Gamma_x\ \ge\ \frac1f\left(\frac1{\delta_-}+\frac1{\delta_+}\right)
                \ \ge\ \frac{d}{fm}.                               \tag{2}
\]
When \(f=1\) the first inequality is equality for every nonzero \(x\).
In particular the density limit of \(m E(kx)/\log k\) is at least \(d/12\) at a strict degree record, meaning a unit whose positive measure is minimal through its degree and smaller than every positive measure at lower degrees.
Indeed taking the product in each torsion-quotient class yields a unit of the same measure and degree \(d/f\). [Class-product compression](Yin_C_G0345_v264.md#labels)

The sampling limit keeps \(P,x\) fixed and supplies no uniform onset as \(m\) decreases.
Exceptional scalar sequences can have bounded energies at fixed reciprocal quadratics. [Quadratic classification](Yin_C_G0345_v633.md#quadratics)
At two different expanding radii an [all-scalar lower bound](Yin_C_G0345_v636.md#statement) also applies to exceptional sequences.

<a id="poles"></a>
## Computing the rate from the two Laurent tails

Choose the ordinary representative \(T\) of \(x\) of degree \(q<d\).
All its values \(T(\rho)\) at roots of \(P\) are nonzero by irreducibility.
The [partial fraction expansion](Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails) gives two real sequences \(h_{-j-1}\) and \(h_{q+j}\) for \(j\ge0\), each of the form \(u_j=\sum_\rho c_\rho\lambda_\rho^j\).
Their terms are
\[
 (\lambda_\rho,c_\rho)=
 \begin{cases}
   (\rho,T(\rho)/P'(\rho)),&|\rho|<1,\\
   (\rho^{-1},-\rho^{-q-1}T(\rho)/P'(\rho)),&|\rho|>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The remaining \(q\) coefficients contribute a bounded amount to \(E\) and \(V\) for all \(k\).

Take \(Q\ge1\) divisible by the orders of all root-of-unity quotients of distinct conjugates.
For each side \(J\) and \(0\le a<Q\), group its sum \(u_{a+Qn}\) by common value of \(b=\lambda_\rho^Q\).
The coefficient of \(b^n\) is \(C_b(a)=\sum_{\lambda_\rho^Q=b}c_\rho\lambda_\rho^a\).
Omit a residue class on one side when all its coefficients vanish.
For every remaining pair \((J,a)\), define
\[
 R_{J,a}=\max\{|b|:C_b(a)\ne0\},\qquad
 \beta_{J,a}=-\log R_{J,a},\qquad
 \Gamma_x=\sum_{(J,a)}\frac1{\beta_{J,a}}.                         \tag{3}
\]
The sampling limit will in particular show that (3) is independent of the chosen admissible \(Q\).

In each remaining progression write \(u_{a+Qn}=R_{J,a}^n(F(n)+O(c^n))\) for some \(0<c<1\).
Here \(F(n)\) is the sum of \(C_b(a)(b/|b|)^n\) over its nonzero largest-modulus terms.
The quotient of any two distinct phases in this sum has infinite order since equality after any common power would already have grouped their original bases.
For every \(\epsilon>0\), outside a set of \(n\) of density zero,
\[
 e^{-(1+\epsilon)\beta_{J,a}n}\ \le\ |u_{a+Qn}|
                       \ \le\ C e^{-\beta_{J,a}n},                  \tag{4}
\]
and the upper bound holds for every \(n\).

For the lower bound let \(G\) be the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by the tuple of phases.
The sequence of nonnegative powers is Haar equidistributed on \(G\) by the geometric sum for each nontrivial character. [Character criterion](../audits/literature/Tao10/Yin_U001.md#tao10-u001)
On each component the coordinate characters have distinct restrictions to the connected torus \(G^0\), since equality of two restrictions would give their ratio finite image on \(G\).
Their linear combination with the indicated nonzero coefficients is therefore nonzero as a trigonometric polynomial on every component.
Its zero set has Haar measure zero, including when \(G^0\) is a point. [Torus zero sets and closed sublevel estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v537.md#phases)
For each fixed \(\eta>0\), equidistribution bounds the upper density of \(\{n:|F(n)|\le\eta\}\) by the Haar measure of the closed sublevel set.
These measures tend to zero as \(\eta\downarrow0\).
A failure of the lower bound in (4) implies \(|F(n)|\le e^{-\epsilon\beta_{J,a}n}+O(c^n)\), proving the asserted density bound.

<a id="sampling"></a>
## Averaging one exponentially decaying sequence over an integer multiplier

Let \(w_n\) be a real sequence indexed by \(n\ge0\) satisfying (4) with exponent \(\beta>0\), including its all-index upper bound.
For any real Lipschitz function \(g\) of period one with \(g(0)=0\) and mean \(\bar g=\int_0^1g(t)\,dt\), we show that
\[
 \frac1{\log K}\sum_{n\ge0}g(kw_n)\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{\bar g}{\beta}
 \quad\text{in mean square on }1\le k\le K.                         \tag{5}
\]
Put \(N=(\log K)/\beta\).
The terms \(n>N\) together have absolute sum \(O(1)\), uniformly in \(k\le K\), by the Lipschitz bound and the geometric tail.
It remains to average \(g(kw_n)-\bar g\) for \(0\le n\le N\).
Uniform approximation by trigonometric polynomials reduces this to a single nonzero integer frequency \(h\) and the second moment of \(N^{-1}\sum_{0\le n\le N}\exp(2\pi i hkw_n)\).
Indeed one may subtract the mean of each approximating polynomial, with at most twice its uniform approximation error.

Fix \(0<\theta<1/4\), keep \(\theta N\le n\le(1-\theta)N\), and discard the exceptional indices of (4) for \(\epsilon=\theta/4\).
The discarded terms change the normalized character sum by at most \(2\theta+o(1)\) pointwise.
For remaining indices \(n<n'\) with \(n'-n\ge\theta N\) one has
\[
 |w_{n'}|/|w_n|\le C K^{-\theta+\epsilon},\qquad
 K|w_n|\ge K^{1-(1+\epsilon)(1-\theta)},\qquad
 |w_n|\le C K^{-\theta}.
\]
Thus \(|h(w_n-w_{n'})|\) tends uniformly to zero while \(K|h(w_n-w_{n'})|\) tends uniformly to infinity.
The average over \(k\) of \(\exp(2\pi i hk(w_n-w_{n'}))\) tends uniformly to zero by the finite geometric sum.
The diagonal and pairs at distance less than \(\theta N\) number at most \(2\theta N^2+O(N)\), and every such correlation has modulus at most one.
Expanding the second moment gives a limiting upper bound \(2\theta\) for the mean squared modulus of the retained normalized character sum.
Adding back the discarded terms in mean-square norm and then letting \(\theta\downarrow0\) proves (5).

Apply (5) to every nonzero progression for each of \(g(t)=D(t)^2\) and \(g(t)=D(t)(1-D(t))\).
Their means on \([-1/2,1/2]\) are \(1/12\) and \(1/6\).
The finite sum of the mean-square errors tends to zero by the triangle inequality even when some progressions are identical.
Together with the bounded middle terms this proves (1) and (3).

<a id="degree"></a>
## Surviving residue classes at the nearest poles

Conjugation in the splitting field permutes the torsion-quotient classes transitively, so each has size \(f\).
Each class has a common modulus.
On one side choose a class at the closest radial distance \(\delta_J\) to the circle.
Write its bases as \(\lambda_\rho=\lambda_0\zeta_\rho\) with distinct \(Q\)-th roots of unity \(\zeta_\rho\).
The coefficient at their common \(Q\)-th power is \(\lambda_0^a H(a)\), with \(H(a)=\sum_\rho c_\rho\zeta_\rho^a\).
Character orthogonality and Cauchy-Schwarz give
\[
 \sum_{a=0}^{Q-1}|H(a)|^2=Q\sum_\rho|c_\rho|^2,\qquad
 |H(a)|^2\le f\sum_\rho|c_\rho|^2.
\]
Hence at least \(Q/f\) values of \(a\) retain a pole at this modulus and contribute \(1/(Q\delta_J)\) each to (3).
When \(f=1\) every residue retains such a pole.
The two sides give the first inequality in (2) and its stated equality.
On each side \(J\) the absolute logarithmic distances sum to \(m\) by the unit norm identity, so \(1/\delta_J\ge\#J/m\).
This proves the degree bound.

For a sharp example take \(P(X)=X^4-4X^2+1=U(X^2)\) with \(U(Y)=Y^2-4Y+1\).
Its positive largest root is \((\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2\), whose square is \(2+\sqrt3\).
The root generates \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\), since its square yields \(\sqrt3\) and its own value is \(\sqrt2(1+\sqrt3)/2\).
Thus \(P\) is irreducible with \(d=4,\ f=2,\ m=\log(2+\sqrt3)\).
The two Laurent tails of \(1/U\) are nonzero single geometric progressions with logarithmic decay exponent \(m\).
Substitution of \(X^2\) places them on the even frequencies, so \(\Gamma_1=2/m=d/(fm)\).
Multiplication by \(1+X\) duplicates those coefficients on the odd frequencies, yielding \(\Gamma_{1+\alpha}=4/m\).
Thus the dependence on the class and the factor \(f\) in the universal bound both occur.
