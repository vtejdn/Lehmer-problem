# A uniform Mahler gap from partial interlacing by traces of roots of unity

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounded difference between the trace degree and the number of interlacing nodes

Fix an integer \(H\ge0\).
Let \(S\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic of degree \(m\), and put \(P(z)=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\).
Suppose there exists a monic \(A\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(g\) with distinct real roots \(y_1<\cdots<y_g\), each of the form \(\xi+\xi^{-1}\) for a root of unity \(\xi\).
Suppose \(S\) has real roots \(t_0<y_1<t_1<\cdots<y_g<t_g\), and \(0\le h=m-g-1\le H\).
The other \(h\) roots of \(S\) may be real, repeated or nonreal.
There is an \(\eta(H)>0\) such that
\[
                        M(P)=1\quad\text{or}\quad\log M(P)\ge\eta(H).
\]
More precisely, when \(M(P)\le2\) there is an integer polynomial of bounded term count in terms of \(H\) alone, with exactly the same Mahler measure as \(P\).

Consequently, if Salem numbers tend to one, there is a sequence tending to one of Salem numbers strictly smaller than every Salem number of lower degree.
On any such sequence, for every strict integer interlacer of the trace polynomial, the total degree of its primitive nonmonic irreducible factors must tend to infinity, with factors taken up to sign and content discarded.
In particular the least positive leading coefficient \(t(S)\) of a strict integer interlacer is eventually at least two.
If positive leading coefficients of the chosen interlacers are bounded, their largest nonmonic irreducible factor degree tends to infinity.

<a id="poles"></a>
## Bounded signed weights at the torsion nodes

It suffices to treat \(M(P)\le2\).
Every root of \(P\) has modulus in \([1/2,2]\) by monicity and reciprocity.
Thus every root of \(S\) has modulus at most \(5/2\), by writing it as \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) and using \(|\alpha|+|\alpha|^{-1}\le5/2\).
For \(g=0\), \(P\) itself has at most \(2H+3\) terms.
Otherwise put \(S_0=\prod_{j=0}^g(x-t_j)\) and let \(D=S/S_0\) be the monic real polynomial of degree \(h\) from the unused roots.
Interlacing gives
\[
 \frac{A(x)}{S_0(x)}=\sum_{j=0}^g\frac{p_j}{x-t_j},
 \qquad
 \frac{S_0(x)}{A(x)}=x-a-\sum_{l=1}^g\frac{\rho_l}{x-y_l},
\]
where \(p_j=A(t_j)/S_0'(t_j)>0\), \(\sum p_j=1\), and \(\rho_l=-S_0(y_l)/A'(y_l)>0\) by the alternating signs.
Expanding both sides of the reciprocal relation at infinity gives \(a=\sum p_jt_j\) and \(\sum_l\rho_l=\sum p_jt_j^2-a^2\le25/4\).
On multiplying by \(D\), polynomial division at infinity yields
\[
            \frac{S(x)}{A(x)}=J(x)-\sum_{l=1}^g\frac{\lambda_l}{x-y_l},
       \qquad \lambda_l=D(y_l)\rho_l,\qquad J\in\mathbb Z[x].
\]
Here \(J\) is the monic quotient of degree \(h+1\) of the two integer polynomials, and the residues match also when an unused root coincides with a node.
As \(|y_l|\le2\), one has \(\sum_l|\lambda_l|\le(25/4)(9/2)^H\).
The Laurent expansion of \(J-S/A\) at infinity has integer coefficients because \(A\) is monic, so all the power sums \(\sum_l\lambda_l y_l^k\), \(k\ge0\), are integers.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## A bounded-term polynomial with the complete original measure

Choose \(\xi_l+\xi_l^{-1}=y_l\) and put \(F_k=\sum_l\lambda_l(\xi_l^k+\xi_l^{-k})\).
These values are integers by the integral trace recurrence \(D_0=2,\ D_1=x,\ D_{k+1}=xD_k-D_{k-1}\).
They are periodic in \(k\), of a common period \(N\) divisible by all the orders.
The normalized discrete Fourier coefficients of \(F\) on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) are the masses of the signed measure assigning \(\lambda_l\) to each of \(\xi_l,\xi_l^{-1}\), combined at coincident points.
Thus its Fourier algebra norm is at most \((25/2)(9/2)^H\), including nodes at either endpoint.
By [Sanders's finite abelian theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#san20-u001) the sequence is a signed sum of \(s=O_H(1)\) congruence indicators, with signs \(\epsilon_i=\pm1\), moduli \(d_i\mid N\) and least positive representatives \(1\le r_i\le d_i\).
A class of residue zero uses \(r_i=d_i\).
Consequently \(\sum_{k\ge1}F_k z^k=\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{r_i}/(1-z^{d_i})\) for \(|z|<1\).

Write \(V=\prod_i(1-z^{d_i})\), \(V_i=V/(1-z^{d_i})\), \(B=\sum_l\lambda_l\in\mathbb Z\), and \(A^\sharp=z^g A(z+z^{-1})\).
Geometric series give, for \(x=z+z^{-1}\),
\[
 \sum_l\frac{\lambda_l}{x-y_l}
     =\frac{z}{1-z^2}\left(B+\sum_{k\ge1}F_k z^k\right).
\]
After clearing the geometric denominators the polynomial
\[
  W=(1-z^2)V z^{h+1}J(z+z^{-1})
       -z^{h+2}\left(BV+\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{r_i}V_i\right)
\]
therefore satisfies \(W A^\sharp=(1-z^2)V P\).
It has integer coefficients and constant one.
The shifted quotient has at most \(2h+3\) coefficients and each product \(V,V_i\) has at most \(2^s\) terms.
This bounds the term count independently of all exponents and conductor orders, including \(s=0\).
Since \(A^\sharp\) is monic with all roots on the unit circle and the other cleared factors are cyclotomic up to sign, multiplicativity gives \(M(W)=M(P)\).
The [bounded-term theorem of Dobrowolski and Smyth](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) now gives the uniform positive gap in logarithmic measure.
Together with \(\log 2\) this also covers inputs above measure two.

<a id="records"></a>
## Descent at a Salem number smaller than all lower-degree examples

There are only finitely many Salem numbers of bounded degree and bounded size, since those bounds bound all coefficients.
Given a sequence tending to one, take minima among Salem numbers of degree at most the successive input degrees, using an input itself as a bound on size.
Choose a witness of least degree for each minimum and discard repetitions.
The resulting sequence approaches one and is strictly smaller than every Salem number of lower degree.

Take any strict interlacer \(C\) of a trace polynomial at such a witness, of the usual degree \(m-1\).
All its roots are above \(-2\) and below \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), with at most one above two.
If a monic irreducible factor of \(C\) has a root \(v>2\), its monic reciprocal lift has exactly one exterior root \(\tau\), with \(1<\tau<\sigma\), and degree at most \(2m-2\).
The irreducible factor containing \(\tau\) is a unit and must contain \(\tau^{-1}\), since no other root has modulus below one.
It defines a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit, with the quadratic case giving \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\ge3\).
For \(\sigma<2\) that case is impossible, and the Salem case contradicts the lower-degree minimum.
Thus every monic irreducible factor has all roots in \([-2,2]\); equivalently its lifted roots are roots of unity by Kronecker's theorem for monic integer polynomials.
Let \(A\) be the product of those monic factors, or one if the product is empty.
Its nodes occupy different gaps of \(S\) by strict interlacing, giving at least one real root of \(S\) in every interval between consecutive nodes and on either side.
Here \(h=m-1-\deg A\) is exactly the total degree of the nonmonic factors.
Any subsequence with bounded \(h\) contradicts the uniform gap just proved.
If \(C\) is monic then \(h=0\).
Under a leading-coefficient bound \(J\), at most \(\log_2 J\) primitive nonmonic factors can occur since each contributes a factor of at least two.
This proves the remaining assertions, with the [interlacer minimum](Yin_C_G0345_v34.md#statement) taken in its actual trace order.
