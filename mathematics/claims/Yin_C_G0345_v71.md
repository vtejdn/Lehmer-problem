# Mahler measures of monomials for one nonreal exterior pair and real exterior roots

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound growing with the size of a permutation block

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(d=2m\) and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(L=\log M(P)\).
Assume distinct roots have nontorsion quotient, as after [torsion-class compression](Yin_C_G0345_v61.md#fibers).
All parameters and monomials below refer to this polynomial.
Suppose its exterior roots comprise one nonreal complex-conjugate pair and \(a\ge1\) real roots.
The Galois group acts on the \(m\) inverse-pair labels.
Form the graph of Galois translates of the edge joining the two labels of the nonreal exterior roots.
Write \(t\) for the number of its connected components and \(b=m/t\) for their common size.
Call a label neutral when its two roots are on the unit circle.

Suppose \(b\ge5\) and some component contains both a real exterior label and a neutral label.
This second condition holds whenever \(b\ge a+3\).
Choose one representative \(v_i\) of each inverse pair.
These representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
For any integers \(n_i\), with \(s=\#\{i:n_i\ne0\}\ge2\), the algebraic unit \(\gamma=\prod_i v_i^{n_i}\) has
\[
      \log M(\gamma)\ \ge\ \frac b3 L
             \ \ge\ c\,\frac{\sqrt{bd}}{(\log(3d))^{106}}
\]
with an absolute \(c>0\), where \(M(\gamma)\) is the measure of its minimal polynomial.
In particular the measure of every such monomial tends to infinity as \(d\to\infty\), uniformly in the choices of exponents on the indicated inputs.
For \(s=1\) the measure is \(M(P)^{|n_i|}\) for its one nonzero exponent and the degree is \(d\).

If \(a\) is fixed, every sequence of these polynomials with \(M(P)\to1\) eventually satisfies both component conditions.
In particular the conclusion applies to the mixed three-exterior case with irrational ratio of the two exterior log moduli.
It constrains the proposed reductions by products of the conjugates while leaving the existence of such an input sequence open.

<a id="flips"></a>
## A real label and a neutral label force even sign changes

Agent-S proposed extracting sign changes by comparing two conjugates of complex conjugation on disjoint edges.
Write \(G\) for the splitting-field Galois group and \(c\in G\) for ordinary complex conjugation.
The permutation of labels induced by \(c\) is the single transposition \(\pi=(j\,k)\) on the nonreal exterior labels.
Every other label is fixed, with \(c\) acting identically on both roots of each real label and by inversion on each neutral one.
The argument for [the transposition graph](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#action) thus applies in this signature too.
The graph components have common size by transitivity, and the induced permutation group contains the direct product of their full symmetric groups, since the edge transpositions generate each component group and fix all the other components.
In particular every pair of distinct vertices in a component is the transposed pair of some Galois conjugate of \(c\).

Take a real exterior label \(i\) and a neutral label \(\ell\) in the same component.
When \(b\ge a+3\), the component of any real exterior label has at least one neutral label, since at most \(a+2\) labels in the whole graph are nonneutral.
The transposition \(\tau=(i\,\ell)\) is disjoint from \(\pi\).
Choose \(c'\) conjugate to \(c\) whose permutation is \(\tau\).
Write \(c=(u,\pi)\) and \(c'=(v,\tau)\) in signed-permutation notation, with the sign vector in \(\mathbb F_2^m\) acting after the coordinate permutation.
One has \(u_i=0,\ u_\ell=1\) for any choice of representatives.
The signed actions of \(cc'\) and \(c'c\) have the same permutation and sign vectors \(u+\pi v\) and \(v+\tau u\).
Their quotient is the diagonal sign change
\[
                  (1+\tau)u+(1+\pi)v
                  =e_i+e_\ell+\delta(e_j+e_k),\qquad \delta\in\{0,1\}.
\]
It belongs to \(G\) and has a nonconstant restriction to the component of \(i\), since it includes \(i\) and has support at most four.
Choose a coordinate transposition within that component exchanging a supported and an unsupported vertex, acting trivially in all other components in the label permutation.
Conjugating this diagonal element by any Galois lift of that transposition permutes its sign vector, regardless of the lift's signs.
Multiplication by the original diagonal element therefore gives a change of just those two signs.
Permuting within the component and transporting to the other components proves that \(G\) contains every even sign change in each separate component.

Two representatives on distinct inverse labels are independent modulo torsion by the [equal-height argument after compression](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks).
If \(\prod v_i^{h_i}\) is torsion, dividing it by its image under the flip on labels \(i,\ell\) of one component makes \(v_i^{2h_i}v_\ell^{2h_\ell}\) torsion.
Independence of that pair gives \(h_i=h_\ell=0\), and every label belongs to such a pair.
Consequently different integral exponent vectors represent distinct actual monomials.

<a id="small"></a>
## Supports omitting a coordinate of each component

Put \(A_i=|\log|v_i||\), so \(\sum_i A_i=L\).
Let \(\mathcal W\) be the set of coordinatewise absolute exponent vectors in the Galois orbit of \((n_i)\).
The integer \(D=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}w_i\) is independent of \(i\) by label transitivity and is positive.
The orbit of actual values is counted exactly by the signed exponent vectors, by independence.
As the norm of a unit has modulus one, its logarithmic measure is half the sum of the absolute logarithms over its conjugates, even for a nonreciprocal output.

When \(2\le s<b\), a support omits at least one vertex in every component.
Thus every choice of its \(s\) signs occurs, correcting parity at an omitted vertex whenever necessary.
For each \(w\in\mathcal W\), the average of \(\left|\sum_i\epsilon_i w_i A_i\right|\) over independent uniform signs is at least \(s^{-1}\sum_i w_i A_i\).
Indeed, averaging first over any chosen supported sign gives at least the corresponding \(w_i A_i\) by the triangle inequality, as in the [original sign-orbit bound](Yin_C_G0345_v62.md#orbit).
Summing over all \(w\) gives \(\log M(\gamma)\ge 2^{s-1}DL/s\).

Fix two labels \(i,j\) in one support.
The label permutations fixing \(i\) move \(j\) through at least \(b-1\) labels by the independent component symmetric groups, whether \(i,j\) lie in the same component or in distinct ones.
Each support containing \(i\) accounts for at most \(s-1\) of these labels, and its absolute exponent at \(i\) is at least one.
Therefore \(D\ge(b-1)/(s-1)\), giving
\[
          \frac{\log M(\gamma)}L\ \ge\
          \frac{2^{s-1}}{s(s-1)}(b-1)\ \ge\ \tfrac23(b-1)\ \ge\ b/3 .
\]
The middle factor \(2^{s-1}/(s(s-1))\) attains its minimum over integers \(s\ge2\) at \(s=3,4\).

<a id="full"></a>
## Parity on a fully occupied component

Now suppose \(s\ge b\).
On the support of \(w\), the even flips realize every assignment of signs in a partially supported component and preserve only the product of the signs in a fully supported one.
Thus the sign vectors over \(w\) are a union of cosets, each of size \(2^{s-f}\) for the number \(f\le\lfloor s/b\rfloor\) of fully supported components.
On each such coset every choice of values of any four or fewer specified signs is uniform.
Indeed each parity condition has at least five coordinates, leaving one unspecified sign available for correction in each affected full component.

For fixed \(w\), take \(\epsilon\) uniform on a coset, put \(x_i=w_i\log|v_i|\), and write \(X=\sum_i\epsilon_i x_i\).
These sign marginals give \(\mathbb E X^2=\sum_i x_i^2\) and \(\mathbb E X^4\le3(\sum_i x_i^2)^2\) by expansion.
Hölder's inequality \(\mathbb E X^2\le(\mathbb E|X|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E X^4)^{1/3}\) and Cauchy–Schwarz then give
\[
                  \mathbb E|X|\ge \frac{(\sum_i x_i^2)^{1/2}}{\sqrt3}
                    \ge \frac{\sum_i w_i A_i}{\sqrt{3s}},
\]
including when all \(x_i=0\).
Using at least one full coset above every \(w\) and \(D\ge1\), we obtain
\[
       \frac{\log M(\gamma)}L
          \ge \frac{2^{s-\lfloor s/b\rfloor-1}}{\sqrt{3s}}D
          \ge \frac{2^{4s/5-1}}{\sqrt{3s}}
          \ge s/3\ge b/3 .
\]
The penultimate inequality holds at \(s=5\), and \(2^{4s/5}/s^{3/2}\) is increasing for real \(s\ge5\) by logarithmic differentiation.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Degree growth and the three-exterior specialization

For two different labels in one of the blocks, a prescribed image of the first root permits at most \(2(b-1)\) images of the second.
Their joint field degree is at most \(2d(b-1)\le d^2/t\), and the roots are independent modulo torsion.
The [two-height estimate for this invariant partition](Yin_C_G0345_v68.md#blocks) gives \(L\ge c_0\sqrt t/(\log(3d))^{106}\), with an absolute \(c_0>0\).
Since \(b\sqrt{t}=\sqrt{bd/2}\), this proves the degree-dependent bound.
For a single nonzero exponent, compression makes the power map injective on the roots, giving degree \(d\) and the stated power of the measure by reciprocity.

On any sequence of such compressed inputs with \(M(P)\to1\), the degrees tend to infinity by finiteness of the bounded-degree monic integer polynomials of measure at most two.
The partition estimate already applies before any component condition other than \(b\ge2\), and gives \(t=o((\log(3d))^{212})\), hence \(b=d/(2t)\to\infty\).
For a fixed number \(a\ge1\) of real exterior roots this eventually gives \(b\ge\max(5,a+3)\).
The mixed three-exterior case has \(a=1\).
Its [irrational log-ratio alternative](Yin_C_G0345_v57.md#general) already implies multiplicative independence and thus distinct roots modulo torsion, so the conclusion there applies directly to monomials in the original roots.
