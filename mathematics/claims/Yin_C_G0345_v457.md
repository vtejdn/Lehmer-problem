# A uniform height obstruction on fixed supports of minimum cardinality for a Salem unit and its square

<a id="statement"></a>
## A lower bound independent of the width and the positions

Let \(\delta\) be a Salem number of degree \(d=2s\ge4\), put \(m=2d\) and \(h=\log\delta\), and let \(P_k\) be the minimum of \(\delta^k\) for \(k=1,2\).
Fix a width \(b\ge m\), any \(m+1\) positions \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,b\}\), and a sign \(\varepsilon\).
At all sufficiently large positive integers \(n\), let \(A_n\) be the unique primitive integral row up to sign on \(J\) satisfying
\[
       (P_1P_2)^2\mid A_n(X^n)+\varepsilon X^{nb+1}A_n(X^{-n}).
\]
These are the supports of minimum cardinality, and the signed row gives genuine contacts at every prescribed powered root by the [minimum-support theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#statement).
Its height rate exists and satisfies
\[
       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|A_n\|_1}{n}
               \ \ge\ (3d-6)h\ >\frac{3d}{(\log(3d))^3}
               \ \ge\frac{\exp(3)}{27}>\frac{\log2}{8}.
\]
In particular the strict test \(((2nb+1)\|A_n\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) eventually fails.
For a fixed seed this exclusion is simultaneous over either sign and all supports of minimum cardinality in any prescribed finite set of widths.
It also holds at all nonnegative central translations of these supports, with the corresponding translated widths.
The bound on the fixed-support limits grows without bound with the seed degree.
The onset may depend on the seed and the original finite set of widths.

<a id="divisors"></a>
## Six isolated collisions per other representative

Choose one representative \(x_i\) of each inverse pair of conjugates of \(\delta\) and let \(K\) be the splitting field.
The representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, since an embedding sending \(x_i\) to \(\delta\) puts every other representative on the unit circle.
The \(2d\) elements \(x_i^{\pm1},x_i^{\pm2}\) give precisely the distinct roots of \(P_1P_2\) and all distinct pairs have nontorsion quotient.
Thus the root group is freely generated by the \(x_i\), and the [fixed-support projective height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) gives a limit on the full sequence of exponents.

Use \(z_i\) for \(x_i^n\) and \(N\) for \(n\).
The matrix on columns \(l\in J\) has at \(y=z_i^k\) with label \(c=\varepsilon x_i^k\) the two entries \(y^l+cy^{b-l}\) and \(Nly^l+c(1+N(b-l))y^{b-l}\).
Write \(C\) for the tuple of signed maximal minors, \(D\) for their gcd in \(K[N,\mathbf z]\) and \(Q=C/D\).
Fix \(i\), put \(z=z_i\), and fix all the other \(z_j\) at positive real values algebraically independent over the algebraic numbers.
For each \(j\ne i\) consider
\[
                    z^2=z_j^{\pm1},\qquad z=z_j^{\pm2}.
\]
These give six distinct nonzero points per \(j\) on the \(z\)-line, with disjoint sets for different \(j\).
At each point \(z_0\), exactly one moving site \(y=z^k\) meets a fixed site or its inverse \(w=z_j^{\pm l}\); the inverse sites meet at \(w^{-1}\ne w\).
Here \((k,l)=(2,1)\) or \((1,2)\).
The respective labels are \(c=\varepsilon x_i^k\) and \(p=\varepsilon x_j^{\pm l}\) after orienting the fixed site to \(w\).

On each of the four displayed algebraic relations with a specified sign, the field of rational functions over \(\mathbb Q\) is purely transcendental.
For \(z^2=z_j^{\pm1}\) eliminate \(z_j\), and for \(z=z_j^{\pm2}\) eliminate \(z\).
The surviving coordinates remain algebraically independent over the algebraic numbers on our slice at each corresponding \(z_0\).
In the first case this follows because squaring \(z_0\) recovers \(z_j^{\pm1}\), so replacing \(z_j\) by either square root preserves transcendence degree.
Consequently a nonzero rational function on such a relation, defined over \(\mathbb Q\), has nonzero value at every one of our corresponding points wherever its denominator is nonzero.
Its value there can be algebraic only if the rational function is a rational constant.

<a id="jets"></a>
## The second derivatives of a sparse kernel polynomial

On a chosen relation, work first over its rational function field \(E\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Omit the two rows of the moving site \(y\).
At \(N=0\) the remaining rows separate into evaluation at \(m-2\) distinct nodes, including \(w,w^{-1}\).
On \(\langle Z^l:l\in J\rangle\), append first derivatives at these two points, giving \(m\) conditions.
These \(m\) conditions are independent over \(E\), and appending a second derivative at either \(w\) or \(w^{-1}\) gives \(m+1\) independent conditions in each case.

To see the requisite nonzero determinants, choose positive values of the free coordinates on the relation so that the above \(m-2\) nodes are distinct.
Such choices exist because the only forced identifications and their inverses were removed with the moving site.
A nonzero real polynomial on \(t\) distinct monomials has at most \(t-1\) positive zeros counted with multiplicity.
Indeed divide by its lowest power and differentiate, leaving at most \(t-1\) terms, and apply Rolle's theorem counting multiplicities and induction from one term.
Thus the \(m+1\) value and derivative conditions in either enlarged system have zero kernel on the real space generated by \(J\).
Their real determinants are nonzero and lift to nonzero rational functions, establishing all the stated ranks over \(E\) and at our specialized points.
This argument uses positive nodes to verify a rational function on the relation and so covers either choice of square root on the slice.

Let \(A_0\) generate the one-dimensional kernel of the \(m\) conditions over \(E\), choosing its coefficients in \(E\) by the signed minors.
It has double zeros at \(w,w^{-1}\) and nonzero second derivatives at both points.
In particular the ratio
\[
              L=w^{4-b}\frac{A_0''(w)}{A_0''(w^{-1})}
\]
belongs to \(E^\times\) and is defined at the selected point after a choice of nonzero kernel minor there.
Neither square root of \(pc=x_i^k x_j^{\pm l}\) can equal \(L\) or \(-L\) there.
Indeed \(pc\) is a nontorsion algebraic integer unit by independence.
A rational algebraic integer unit must be \(1\) or \(-1\), so the two square roots are nonrational algebraic numbers, excluded by the [constant-field calculation](#divisors).

<a id="rank"></a>
## The projected derivative obstruction

Suppose \(N\ne0,\ (N,z)\to(0,z_0)\) along a sequence where all minors vanish.
Omit the moving rows as above and choose a basis of their three-dimensional remaining kernel converging to a basis of the limiting evaluation kernel, by solving at an invertible minor.
With \(Y=w e^\xi\) define \(U(\xi)=Y^{-b/2}A(Y)\) and \(V(\xi)=Y^{b/2}A(Y^{-1})\) on the basis, choosing a consistent local logarithm also for odd \(b\).
Primes denote derivatives in the independent variable \(\xi\) with the polynomial held fixed, and subscripts denote derivatives at \(\xi=0\).
All Taylor remainders used below are uniform by convergence of these bounded-degree bases.

Set \(\theta=\log(y/w)\) and \(a=1-c/p\).
Centering the moving rows gives \(f=U(\theta)+cV(\theta)\) and \(g=N(U'(\theta)+cV'(\theta))+cV(\theta)\).
The fixed equations on the kernel give \(U_0=N(U_1+pV_1)\), \(V_0=-N(U_1+pV_1)/p\).
For an inverted fixed site these follow after exchanging the centered evaluations, taking the new value row and subtracting the old derivative row divided by its label from that value row.
The vectors \(U_1,V_1\) are independent in the limit by the two independent derivative conditions in the [sparse interpolation calculation](#jets).
Use them as two moving coordinate vectors and complete them with a fixed third vector.
In the first two coordinates \(f,g\) have leading parts
\[
                  (Na+\theta,Npa+c\theta),\qquad (Na,c\theta)
\]
with errors \(O(|N\theta|+|\theta|^2)\), by Taylor expansion with the two fixed identities.
Their dependence forces \(c\theta^2-pa^2N^2=O((|N|+|\theta|)^3)\).
Since \(a,c,p\ne0\), the ratio \(|\theta/N|\) is bounded above and away from zero.
Pass to a subsequence with \(\theta/N\to t=aq=q-q^{-1}\) for a choice \(q^2=p/c\).
Here \(q\ne\pm1\) and the first coordinate of \(f/N\) tends to \(a(1+q)\ne0\).
Consequently \(g=\lambda f\) with \(\lambda\to1/(1+q)\).

Project onto the third coordinate, which kills the current \(U_1,V_1\) exactly and therefore also \(U_0,V_0\).
In the limit this projection is evaluation of a row functional on a common nonzero scalar multiple of \(A_0\), because \(A_0\) spans the annihilator of the two first-derivative functionals inside the three-dimensional limiting kernel.
Write \(\alpha,\beta\) for the respective projected limits of \(U_2,V_2\).
At the double zeros of \(A_0\) these are the same nonzero scalar times \(w^{2-b/2}A_0''(w)\) and \(w^{b/2-2}A_0''(w^{-1})\), so \(\alpha,\beta\ne0\) and \(\alpha/\beta=L\).

In this coordinate
\[
 f=(\alpha+c\beta)\theta^2/2+o(|N|^2),\qquad
 g=(\alpha+c\beta)N\theta+c\beta\theta^2/2+o(|N|^2).
\]
Thus the projected limit of \((g-\lambda f)/(N^2t)\) is
\[
  \alpha+c\beta+\frac{t}{2}\left(c\beta-\frac{\alpha+c\beta}{1+q}\right)
        =\frac{1+q}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{q}+c\beta\right).
\]
Its vanishing would force \(L=-cq\), whose square is \(pc\).
This contradicts the constant-field exclusion, and proves full rank throughout a punctured neighborhood with \(N\ne0\) of each selected collision.

## Retained zeros, exponent width and the coefficient budget

The specialized raw tuple \(C(0,z)\) on our slice is not identically zero.
Indeed, at any generic positive \(z\) away from collisions one obtains \(m\) distinct positive evaluation points, which impose independent conditions already on any \(m\) of the chosen monomials by the positive-zero bound.
Thus \(D(0,z)\) is also not identically zero.
At each of the \(6(s-1)\) selected points \(z_0\), one has \(D(0,z_0)\ne0\).
Otherwise, on arbitrarily small disks around this zero with zero-free boundary, continuity of polynomial roots gives zeros of \(D(N,z)\) for sufficiently small nonzero \(N\), contradicting the preceding rank conclusion.

At \(N=0\) every raw minor at \(z_0\) vanishes to order at least two.
After splitting evaluation rows by invertible local operations, subtract the fixed row at \(w\) from the moving row at \(y\), and do the same at \(w^{-1}\) for the moving inverse row.
All entries in each of these two resulting rows vanish at \(z_0\), so every determinant contains \((z-z_0)^2\).
The same divisibility holds for the entries of \(Q(0,z)\), since \(D(0,z_0)\ne0\).
Any nonzero entry of this tuple has exponent width at least \(12(s-1)\), by removing its lowest monomial and counting the indicated nonzero roots with multiplicity.
Specializing \(N\) and the other variables can only decrease the joint width in \(z_i\).
This proves the lower bound \(12(s-1)\) for every coordinate of the fully reduced tuple.

For each \(i\), a fraction \(1/d\) of the embeddings of \(K\) send \(x_i\) to \(\delta\) and another fraction \(1/d\) send it to \(\delta^{-1}\), with all other representatives then on the unit circle.
The [height formula](Yin_C_G0345_v451.md#height) is consequently \(h/d\) times the sum of the joint coordinate widths.
It gives the lower bound \(12s(s-1)h/d=(3d-6)h\).
By [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height), \(h=\log M(\delta)>2/(\log(3d))^3\), and \(2(3d-6)\ge3d\) for \(d\ge4\).
For \(x>1\), \(x/(\log x)^3\) has minimum \(\exp(3)/27\) at \(x=\exp(3)\), which proves the displayed numerical comparisons since \(\exp(1)>2\) and \(\log2<1\).

The strict budget would require \(8\log\|A_n\|_1<n\log2\), which eventually contradicts the rate for a fixed \(b,J,\varepsilon\).
Only finitely many signs and supports occur in a fixed finite set of widths, so the maximum of the corresponding onsets suffices.
For any \(u\ge0\) integral, the substitution \(A(Y)\mapsto Y^u A(Y)\), \(J\mapsto J+u\), \(b\mapsto b+2u\) multiplies the signed graph by \(X^{nu}\) and bijects the coefficient vectors of the two systems.
It preserves primitive norms, and the enlarged budget factor \(2n(b+2u)+1\) can only increase.
Thus the same onset excludes all these translations, even if the translation depends on \(n\).

A collaborating agent verified the sparse interpolation, rational functions on both square-root branches, local rank argument and simultaneous finite-width exclusion.
