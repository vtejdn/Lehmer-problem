# A Mahler gap for integral polynomials supported on separated blocks of exponents

<a id="statement"></a>
## Varying block coefficients and arbitrary exterior signature

After multiplying by a monomial, let \(F(X,Y)\) be a nonzero complex polynomial with exponent widths \(w\ge1\) in \(X\) and \(R\ge1\) in \(Y\), and \(k\) nonzero coefficients.
For an integer \(n>w\), put \(u=w/n\) and \(P_\phi(z)=F(z,e^{i\phi}z^n)\).
Use logarithmic Mahler measures, writing \(m_2(F)\) for the two-variable measure.
Uniformly in all coefficients and phases there is an absolute \(C\) such that
\[
 \begin{split}
  m(P_\phi)-m_2(F)&\le C R^2u\log(2/u),\\
  m_2(F)-m(P_\phi)&\le C R^2u\{R+k\log(2Rk/u)\}.                       \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
In particular none of the coefficients in the top or bottom \(Y\)-layer has to be a unit, and repeated roots are allowed.

Suppose \(F\) is integral and \(P_0\) is irreducible and noncyclotomic in the integer Laurent polynomial ring, with at least two occupied \(Y\)-layers.
For \(c_R=[2\exp(16384)(\log(3R))^{48}]^{-1}\), one has
\[
       m(P_0)\ \ge\
              c_R-C R^2(w/n)\{R+k\log(2Rkn/w)\}.                       \tag{2}
\]
Thus a fixed \(R\) gives a uniform positive gap as soon as the error is at most \(c_R/2\).
For instance it suffices that \(w\le c(R)\sqrt{n/\log(2n)}\), for some sufficiently small positive \(c(R)\).
This allows every position in each of the \(R+1\) intervals \([jn,jn+w]\) to be occupied, including the next-to-leading coefficient.
Every noncyclotomic product whose lift has a horizontal nontorsion component also satisfies (2), without irreducibility.
A base-only factor can carry its own one-variable measure when that component hypothesis is dropped.

<a id="length"></a>
## Counting intersections of the moving roots with coordinate lines

First multiply by a Laurent monomial so that the minimum exponent in each coordinate is zero.
For root tracking, remove any common \(X\)-polynomial factor of the coefficients of \(F\) in \(Y\), leaving \(F_0\) of \(X\)-degree \(w_0\le w\).
For \(X=e^{it}\), follow the \(R\) roots \(y_i(t)\) in the Riemann sphere with multiplicities and include a root at infinity when the leading coefficient vanishes.
They can be labeled continuously on the closed parameter interval \([0,2\pi]\), with a possible permutation at the endpoints.
Indeed the distinct roots of the squarefree part over \(\mathbb C(X)\) continue analytically outside finitely many parameter values.
At the exceptional values the homogeneous fiber is nonzero by the removed common factor.
Disjoint neighborhoods of its finitely many roots retain their respective counts under a sufficiently small change of parameter, by the argument principle in local coordinates.
The branches on the two sides therefore have limiting values which can be matched with multiplicities.
Constant factors in \(Y\) are covered by constant paths.

Set \(v_i=y_i\) on the closed disk and \(v_i=1/\bar y_i\) outside it, with the value zero at infinity.
This operation is continuous on the sphere.
We claim the sum of the lengths of the resulting disk paths is at most \(C R^2 w\).
For a generic real \(a\) count parameter values at which an original finite root has real part \(a\).
At such a point \(y=a+ib\), and \(b\) is a common root in the variable \(V\) of
\[
          F_0(X,a+iV),\qquad X^{w_0}\overline{F_0(1/\bar X,a+i\bar V)} .
\]
Their resultant in \(V\) has degree at most \(2Rw_0\) in \(X\) by the determinant formula.
For all but finitely many \(a\) it is nonzero: fix a single circle point where the leading and constant coefficients in \(Y\) are nonzero.
A common complex \(V\) at that point would require \(2a=y_i+\bar y_j\) for two roots of the fixed fiber, a finite list.
Each value of \(X\) on the parameter circle occurs at most twice on the chosen interval and contributes at most \(R\) branches.
The same reasoning applies to roots with imaginary part \(a\), substituting \(V+ia\) for \(a+iV\).

For exterior roots the points \(1/\bar y_i\) are roots of the polynomial
\[
              X^{w_0}Y^R\overline{F_0(1/\bar X,1/\bar Y)}
\]
on the parameter circle; it has the same degree bounds.
This counts crossings of a generic horizontal or vertical line by the corresponding folded paths, with at most \(C R^2 w\) branch-parameter pairs per line.
For each continuous coordinate path its variation on any partition is the integral over the level of the number of partition subintervals whose endpoint values straddle that level.
Apart from finitely many endpoint levels, each such subinterval contains a distinct crossing.
Summing over all paths and integrating over \([-1,1]\) gives the same bound for the sum of coordinate variations.
Euclidean path length is bounded by the sum of the two coordinate variations, proving the claim.

<a id="series"></a>
## Fourier coefficients and the logarithmic integral

Write \(A_R(X)\) for the original leading coefficient in \(Y\).
At almost every \(t\), its univariate logarithmic fiber measure is
\[
        b(t)=\log|A_R(e^{it})|+\sum_i\log^+|y_i(t)|.
\]
Its circle average is \(m_2(F)\) by Jensen's formula and iterated integration; the fiber measure is bounded above by the logarithm of the sum of coefficient moduli and below by the integrable \(\log|A_R|\).
On the unit circle in \(Y\), factorization gives
\[
             |F(e^{it},Y)|=e^{b(t)}\prod_i |1-\bar v_i(t)Y|.
\]
For \(0<r<1\) substitute \(Y=r e^{i(nt+\phi)}\) in the factors on the right, leaving \(b\) as above, and call the logarithmic mean \(m_{\phi,r}\).
The variation of \(\sum_i\bar v_i^j\) is at most \(C j R^2 w\) by the disk path length estimate.
This sum is periodic even if individual roots are permuted.
Integration by parts at frequency \(jn\), followed by the absolutely convergent logarithmic series, gives
\[
                   |m_{\phi,r}-m_2(F)|
                             \le C R^2 u\log(1/(1-r)).                       \tag{3}
\]
Write \(\epsilon=1-r\le1/2\).
The [disk-factor comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v158.md#small) bounds \(m(P_\phi)-m_{\phi,r}\) by \(-(R/2)\log r\).
Taking \(\epsilon=u/2\) proves the first inequality of (1).

<a id="layers"></a>
## Regularization when several roots are outside the unit circle

Let \(B\) be the sum of the absolute coefficient values of \(F\).
Then \(e^{b(t)}\le B\).
Since \(n>w\), specialization identifies no two occupied monomials, so Parseval gives \(\|P_\phi\|_\infty\ge B/\sqrt{k}\).
For a measurable choice of one folded root put \(D=|1-\bar v_i e^{i(nt+\phi)}|\).
All other factors have modulus at most two, hence on \(\{D<v\}\)
\[
                 |P_\phi|/\|P_\phi\|_\infty\le 2^{R-1}\sqrt k\,v .
\]
If \(k=2\), both the two-variable and the one-variable binomial measures equal the logarithm of the greater coefficient modulus, so (1) holds directly.
For \(k\ge3\) use \(h=1/(k-1)\le1/2\).
The [measurable-set Turán--Nazarov inequality](../audits/literature/BS13/Yin_U001.md#bs13-u001) gives
\[
                 \Pr(D<v)\le C(2^{R}\sqrt{k}\,v)^h .
\]
Integrating this bound against \(\epsilon/[v(v+\epsilon)]\) over the positive axis costs at most \(Ck(2^{R}\sqrt{k}\epsilon)^h\), by splitting at \(v=\epsilon\).
This bounds the excess of each regularized logarithm over the original, since \(|1-rz|\le |1-z|+\epsilon\) in the unit disk.
Choose
\[
                 \epsilon=\frac{1}{2^{R}\sqrt{k}}
                                     \left(\frac{u}{2Rk}\right)^{k-1}.
\]
The total cost for the \(R\) factors is \(O(u)\) and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\ll R+k\log(2Rk/u)\).
Together with (3) this proves (1).

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Excluding a measure contributed by a base-only polynomial

The [bounded partial-degree gap](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(m_2(F)\ge c_R\) if some geometric irreducible component depending on \(Y\) is not a torsion coset.
Otherwise its [factorization conclusion](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) writes \(F\), up to monomials and sign, as an integral \(X\)-only factor times cyclotomic polynomials in monomials.
Under \((X,Y)=(z,z^n)\) the factors depending on \(Y\) specialize to nonconstant cyclotomic products up to monomials.
Indeed exponent width in \(X\) is additive on products over the Laurent polynomial domain, so each factor has \(X\)-width at most \(w\).
Any factor depending on \(Y\) has at least two \(Y\)-layers and its specialized exponents in those layers cannot coincide when \(n>w\).
At least one such factor occurs since \(R>0\).
This factorization is incompatible with the stipulated irreducible noncyclotomic \(P_0\).
This proves (2) and the analogous claim under the component hypothesis.
Finally \(k\le(R+1)(w+1)\), and for fixed \(R\), \(\log(2Rkn/w)\ll_R\log(2n)\), giving the sufficient width in the statement.
