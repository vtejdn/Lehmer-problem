# A Mahler gap for integer polynomials supported in a fixed number of short intervals

<a id="statement"></a>
## Arbitrarily placed intervals on the degree scale

For every positive integer \(k\) there are constants \(\epsilon_k,\eta_k>0\) with the following property.
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be irreducible of degree \(N\ge1\), with nonzero constant term and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m(P)\).
If the exponents of its nonzero coefficients are contained in the union of \(k\) real intervals, each of length at most \(\epsilon_k N\), then
\[
                                  m(P)\ge\eta_k .                              \tag{1}
\]
The intervals can be placed anywhere and their centers can approach one another.
Their relative positions and all polynomial coefficients may change with \(N\).
There is no bound on the number of terms inside an interval.
The constants obtained below by compactness are not evaluated numerically.

Equivalently, along any sequence of irreducible inputs with \(0<m(P)\to0\), no subsequential Hausdorff limit of the closed sets
\[
                    \{j/N:[X^j]P\ne0\}\subset[0,1]
\]
is finite, provided \(P(0)\ne0\).
Common factors matter for reducible inputs, since multiplying a polynomial by \(X^L-1\) preserves its measure and can place its support in two short intervals relative to the new degree.

<a id="model"></a>
## A finite set of fast directions

Fix \(k\ge2\), distinct vectors \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\in\mathbb Z^r\), and a real vector \(\beta\) with rationally independent coordinates.
Let \(q_N\in\mathbb Z^r\) satisfy \(q_N/N\to\beta\) as the positive integer scale \(N\to\infty\).
For each \(N\) take nonzero integral Laurent polynomials \(b_{i,N}\) supported in \([-d,d]\), with \(1\le d=d_N=o(N)\), whose gcd in \(\mathbb Z[X^{\pm1}]\) is a unit.
Set
\[
   F_N(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^k b_{i,N}(X)Y^{a_i},\qquad
   H_N(X)=\sum_{i=1}^k b_{i,N}(X)X^{a_i\cdot q_N},
   \qquad I_N=m_{r+1}(F_N),\quad\mu_N=m(H_N).
\]
Here \(Y^{a_i}\) denotes the corresponding Laurent monomial on the \(r\)-torus.
For bounded \(\mu_N\) we prove that \(I_N-\mu_N\to0\).
All normalized circle and torus integrals below use Haar probability measure.
The frequencies \(\omega_{i,N}=a_i\cdot q_N/N\) lie in a fixed bounded interval and are uniformly pairwise separated for large \(N\), because their limits \(a_i\cdot\beta\) are distinct.
The specialized bands are then disjoint and \(H_N\) is nonzero.
Both logarithmic means are nonnegative by integrality.

Let \(A(t)\) be the maximum of the absolute values among twice the real and imaginary parts of \(b_{i,N}(e^{it})\), and use the relative modulus
\[
              V(t,y)=\frac{|F_N(e^{it},e^{iy})|}{A(t)},\qquad y\in\mathbb T^r .
\]
The rows have no common nonzero complex zero by primitivity, so \(A(t)>0\) everywhere.
Subtracting \(\int\log A\) changes the two means by the same amount.
We write \(C\) for constants allowed to depend on the fixed exponent vectors and their limiting frequencies.

<a id="clipped"></a>
## Comparison of the clipped integrals without a common fast period

Fix \(0<u<1\) and put \(f(t,y)=\log\max(u,V(t,y))\).
For every fixed \(y\), its variation in \(t\) is at most \(C d\log(C/u)\).
The [quotient variation calculation](Yin_C_G0345_v327.md#quadrature) applies because the numerator is the modulus of a degree-\(d\) slow Laurent polynomial at fixed phases.
There are \(O_k(d)\) monotonicity pieces after splitting by the maximizing coordinate, clipping levels and zeros of the numerator of the derivative of the squared quotient.
The possible \(y\)-values just change the coefficients.
As functions of \(y\), the profiles \(f(t,\cdot)\) are uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz for this \(u\), because the exponents are fixed and \(|b_{i,N}|/A\le1\).
For any \(\xi>0\) there is therefore one product Fejér kernel of finite order which approximates every such profile uniformly within \(\xi\).
Indeed the kernels are positive approximate identities of mass one and the same modulus of continuity controls their tails.

The resulting polynomial is \(\sum_{h\in S} g_h(t)e^{ih\cdot y}\) for a fixed finite \(S\subset\mathbb Z^r\).
Its constant coefficient is the full \(y\)-mean of \(f(t,y)\).
Each \(g_h\) has variation at most \(C d\log(C/u)\) by integration of the \(t\)-variation, since the Fejér multipliers have modulus at most one.
For \(h\ne0\) in this fixed set, the integer \(|h\cdot q_N|\) is at least \(c_S N\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), by \(h\cdot\beta\ne0\).
Periodic integration by parts for a function of bounded variation bounds \(\left|\int g_h(t)e^{i(h\cdot q_N)t}\,d\nu(t)\right|\) by its variation divided by \(2\pi |h\cdot q_N|\).
The difference of the two clipped means consequently tends to zero for each fixed \(u\), by taking the upper limit and then \(\xi\downarrow0\).

The independent clipping loss is \(O(u^{1/(k-1)})\).
To see this choose an integer \(w\in\mathbb Z^r\) such that the \(k\) integers \(a_i\cdot w\) are distinct, avoiding finitely many proper hyperplanes.
Average the fast shape along \(y+vw\) over \(y,v\); invariance of Haar measure gives the same full mean.
On each such line the normalized coefficients are bounded above and some coefficient has modulus at least \(1/2\).
The Vandermonde matrix for the \(k\) distinct frequencies gives a lower bound on some derivative of order zero through \(k-1\) at every \(v\).
After subdividing a full interval of length \(2\pi\) into a fixed number of short intervals, a real or imaginary component of one such derivative stays separated from zero on each, since the next derivatives are uniformly bounded.
The [real divided-difference estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v324.md#orders) bounds the relative length of the sublevel set at \(w_0\le1\) by \(C w_0^{1/(k-1)}\).
Order zero excludes sufficiently small values directly.
Integration up to \(u\) in \(dw_0/w_0\) proves the asserted clipping bound.

<a id="diagonal"></a>
## Logarithmic loss along the actual line of frequencies

We show that clipping the diagonal costs at most
\[
                       C\{u^{1/(k-1)}+(d/N)(1+I_N)\}.                       \tag{2}
\]
We give the changes required in the [integral band comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#statement) to account for the moving real frequencies and the new independent mean.
The slow real coordinates, their holomorphic extensions and the random phase sum used in its [averaged zero estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#curvature) are defined purely from the \(k\) integral coefficients \(b_{i,N}\).
They have no common zero; their realification uses Gaussian integer Laurent coefficients and the same cross-resultant calculation applies.
The bound required there is
\[
             \int\log A(t)\,d\nu(t)\le I_N+O(1).
\]
In fact, let \(w_j\) be the width of \(\{a_i\}\) in fast coordinate \(j\).
The coefficient of any monomial in a Laurent polynomial of these widths is at most its multiplicative Mahler measure times \(2^{\sum_j w_j}\) in modulus.
Extract one coordinate at a time using the univariate coefficient bound from the elementary symmetric formulas, integrating its logarithm over the remaining coordinates.
Exceptional vanishing fibers have Haar measure zero for every nonzero intermediate coefficient polynomial.
Applying this to the fast polynomial for each \(t\) proves the stated bound.
It follows that the energy of the swept zero measure is \(O(d(1+I_N))\) with the original fast variables replaced in the mean.
The [packing and local mass estimates](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#packing) give arcs of radii \(r_i\) with \(\sum r_i=O(1)\), \(M_i=dr_i\ge\delta>0\), and \(\sum M_i^2=O(d(1+I_N))\).
Their enlarged disks contain at most \(O(M_i)\) of the averaged zero mass.

Use grid intervals of length \(2\pi/N\).
They need not be periods of the fast phases taken together.
The [real harmonic normalization](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#green), the fixed orthogonal rotation of the real coordinate vector and the [local threshold count](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#charts) through derivative order \(k-1\) involve only the slow coordinates, with derivatives now normalized by \(N\).
On a cover arc with \(M=dr\), the local control disk, separated threshold numerators at the common anchor and Jensen crossing counts are unchanged.
In particular \(Nr=(N/d)M\) tends uniformly to infinity.
Each component where a ratio first exceeds a normalized derivative threshold has length \(O(1/N)\).
Inside the larger counting arc there are \(O(M)\) flagged grid intervals, and on each remaining interval one chart persists with bounded ratios and with normalized derivatives through order \(k-1\) smaller than any prescribed fixed tolerance after choosing its thresholds.

In the fast variable \(s=Nt\) on such an interval the normalized diagonal divided by the nonvanishing chart coordinate is \(\sum_i c_i(s)e^{i\omega_{i,N}s}\).
The inverse real rotation and the chart normalization give upper and lower positive bounds for the Euclidean coefficient norm uniformly, and upper bounds of order the prescribed tolerance for its positive derivatives through order \(k-1\).
Freezing these coefficients on the interval of length \(2\pi\) changes the function and these derivatives by \(O(1)\) times the tolerance.
At any point the phase-weighted frozen coefficient vector has the same Euclidean norm.
The Vandermonde matrix \(((i\omega_{j,N})^h)_{0\le h\le k-1,\,1\le j\le k}\), where \(i\) in the entries is the imaginary unit, has a uniformly bounded inverse by the fixed separation and boundedness of the frequencies.
All derivatives of the frozen shapes through order \(k\) are uniformly bounded.
The same real sublevel argument as above therefore works on each interval when the tolerance is sufficiently small, independently of its initial phases.
The chart denominator is comparable in modulus to the normalized maximum coordinate.
Consequently on each unflagged interval the relative sublevel measure for \(|H_N(e^{it})|/A(t)\le w_0\) is \(O(w_0^{1/(k-1)})\), and at least half of the interval has \(|H_N|\ge cA\).

The [dyadic grouping and disk estimate](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#blocks) use the grid lengths, positive proportion of these good points and the complex growth of the fast phases.
For clarity the selected parent blocks have marked fraction between \(1/8\) and \(1/4\), with total length \(O(M/N)\); those meeting the cover arc lie inside the counting arc when \(N/d\) is large.
On a block of length \(l\) the harmonic normalization on its disk of radius \(16l\) finds an anchor with normalized log modulus at least \(-CM\) among a set of length at least \(3l/8\).
The fast exponentials on this disk cost \(O(Nl)=O(M)\) in logarithm since \(\max_i|\omega_{i,N}|\) is bounded, with either sign of the frequency.
Jensen counting, Blaschke factors and Harnack thus give \(O(Ml)\) for the relative clipping loss on that block.
Summation costs \(O(\sum M_i^2/N)\) for the blocks, and \(O(u^{1/(k-1)})\) for the remaining intervals.
This proves (2).
The constant-coordinate case has zero curvature and can instead use the same fixed-coefficient Vandermonde sublevel estimate on every grid interval.

Fixing \(u=1/2\) first, the clipped comparison and both loss bounds show \(I_N\le \mu_N+O(1)+C(d/N)I_N\).
Hence bounded \(\mu_N\) implies bounded \(I_N\) by absorption.
For each fixed smaller \(u>0\), the upper limit of \(|I_N-\mu_N|\) is then at most \(C u^{1/(k-1)}\).
Letting \(u\downarrow0\) proves the comparison in the model.

<a id="arithmetic"></a>
## Positive measure in a bounded number of fast directions

For the fixed \(\{a_i\}\), choose a primitive integer vector \(w\) separating their dot products, and complete it to an integral unimodular change of the fast lattice with these dot products as the exponents of one variable \(T\).
The \(T\)-width \(R\) is fixed and positive.
The coefficients at its distinct powers are the individual \(b_{i,N}(X)\) times monomials in the other fast variables.
Every Laurent factor independent of \(T\) divides all these coefficients, and therefore is a unit over the integral Laurent ring by their gcd hypothesis.
This uses the fact that adjoining other Laurent indeterminates preserves the gcd of univariate polynomials, by Gauss's lemma.

If any \(T\)-dependent geometric component is not a torsion translate of a subtorus, the [uniform partial-degree theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#statement) gives \(I_N\ge c_R>0\).
Otherwise the [torsion-component factorization](Yin_C_G0345_v156.md#horizontal), with the preceding base-factor observation and the inverse lattice change, expresses \(F_N\) as a Laurent unit times cyclotomic factors
\(\Phi_e(X^bY^v)\) with nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Z^r\).
Coordinate widths add under products over an integral domain.
Thus each \(|v_j|\varphi(e)\le w_j\) for the original fixed coordinate widths \(w_j\), and \(|b|\varphi(e)\le2d\).
There are only finitely many candidate vectors \(v\ne0\).
Rational independence of \(\beta\) gives \(|v\cdot q_N|\ge c N\) uniformly on this finite list for large \(N\), so \(b+v\cdot q_N\ne0\).
Each specialized factor is then a cyclotomic product up to a Laurent unit.
It follows that, eventually, \(\mu_N>0\) implies \(I_N\ge c_R\).
Together with the comparison, this excludes \(0<\mu_N\to0\) for the model.

<a id="compactness"></a>
## Passing from the limiting frequencies to arbitrary intervals

Suppose first that \(N\to\infty\), \(0<m(P_N)\to0\), and the normalized supports converge in Hausdorff distance to a finite set \(\{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k\}\).
It contains both zero and one, so \(k\ge2\).
The additive subgroup of \(\mathbb R\) generated by these numbers is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group and has an integral basis \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_r\).
These basis elements are rationally independent, and \(\lambda_i=a_i\cdot\beta\) for distinct integer vectors \(a_i\).
Take \(q_N\) by rounding each \(N\beta_j\) to a nearest integer.
Assign each occupied exponent to its nearest \(\lambda_iN\), resolving ties in a fixed order.
Every cluster eventually occurs by Hausdorff convergence.
Subtracting \(a_i\cdot q_N\) from the exponents of cluster \(i\) produces its slow Laurent row.
Their joint support lies in \([-d_N,d_N]\) with \(1\le d_N=o(N)\), and the original polynomial is exactly their diagonal.

Let \(h\) be an integral Laurent gcd of these rows.
If it is removed from all rows, the joint \(X\)-width of the quotient is still at most \(2d_N\), by width additivity with coefficients in the fast Laurent ring.
The surviving rows in its diagonal are still disjoint for large \(N\) because the separations of their centers are bounded below by a positive multiple of \(N\).
At least two are present, so that diagonal is a Laurent nonunit.
Irreducibility of \(P_N\) with nonzero constant coefficient implies its irreducibility in the integer Laurent ring, forcing \(h\) to be a Laurent unit.
The model now gives the contradiction.

Finally, failure of (1) for a fixed \(k\) would give a sequence with \(0<m(P_j)<1/j\), each supported in \(k\) intervals of length at most \(N_j/j\).
The degrees tend to infinity after passage to a subsequence, since bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure bound all integer coefficients by the elementary symmetric formulas, leaving finitely many polynomials.
Choose an occupied exponent in each nonempty interval and divide by \(N_j\).
Pass to a subsequence with fixed number of nonempty intervals and convergent choices in \([0,1]\).
The shrinking normalized lengths imply Hausdorff convergence of the supports to the set of these limits, allowing coincidences.
This contradicts the preceding paragraph and proves (1).
Conversely a finite Hausdorff limit puts every sufficiently late support in arbitrarily short relative intervals centered at its finitely many points, giving the stated equivalence.
