# Dyadic degrees of factors of reciprocal five-term polynomials

<a id="statement"></a>
## A gap when the part of the middle exponent prime to six is bounded

Let \(a,c\) be odd integers and let \(b\ne0\) be even, with \(w=v_2(b)\).
For \(0<l<n\) set
\[
 P(X)=a(X^{2n}+1)+b(X^{n+l}+X^{n-l})+cX^n .
\]
First suppose \(\gcd(n,l)=1\) and write \(n=2^u3^v h\), with \(\gcd(h,6)=1\).
There is an absolute \(C>0\) such that every positive-measure integral divisor of \(P\) has logarithmic Mahler measure at least
\[
                         \frac{C}{w2^w h}.                              \tag{1}
\]
This bound is uniform in the coefficient magnitudes at fixed \(w\).
More precisely every irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q\) has degree \(d\gg n/(w2^w h)\), apart from factors with \(v=0\) and \(d\le4h\).
For arbitrary \(n,l\), put \(g=\gcd(n,l)\) and use the part \(h\) of \(n/g\) prime to six.
If \(2|a|+2|b|+|c|\le L\), (1) holds with an additional factor \(1/R_L\), where \(R_L\) is the [common-dilation denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement).

There is also an exact local factorization when \(w=1,\ v\ge1,\ 3^v\nmid l\), without a coprimality assumption on \(n,l\).
For each \(t\mid h\) and each Frobenius orbit of primitive roots of order \(s=3^{v+1}t\) in characteristic two, there is precisely one irreducible factor over \(\mathbb Q_2\), of degree \(2^u\operatorname{ord}_s(2)\).
These are all the local factors, up to scalars.
In particular \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) whenever \(h=1\) under these hypotheses.
For instance \(a=1,\ b=-2,\ c=1\) gives a real root greater than one at every \(n,l\), since \(P(1)=-1\).
Its positive-measure irreducible factors have a uniform gap whenever the part of \(n/\gcd(n,l)\) prime to six is bounded.
Agent-S found the valuation-one test for pure \(2,3\)-power middle exponents.
The coefficient and Newton polygon estimates below treat higher valuations and the exceptional residue classes.

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Orders and valuation one

Put \(N=2^u\) and \(H=3^v h\).
The reduction of \(P/a\) modulo two is \((X^{2H}+X^H+1)^N\).
The base polynomial is squarefree since it is the quotient \((X^{3H}-1)/(X^H-1)\) of products of distinct linear factors in characteristic two.
Its roots have orders precisely \(3^{v+1}t\) with \(t\mid h\).
The orbit of any such root has length \(f_s=\operatorname{ord}_s(2)\), divisible by \(2\cdot3^v\).
Indeed \(v_3(4^{3^j}-1)=1+j\) by induction using the factorization of a difference of cubes, and an odd exponent of two cannot be one modulo three.
This gives \(\operatorname{ord}_{3^{v+1}}(2)=2\cdot3^v\), including \(v=0\).

Work in a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\) containing the Teichmüller lifts of the residual roots, with valuation \(v_2(2)=1\).
Write \(\xi\) for such a lift and \(y=\bar\xi^l\).
Since \(\xi^n\) is a primitive cube root, the value of \(P\) satisfies
\[
                    \xi^{-n}P(\xi)=c-a+b(\xi^l+\xi^{-l}).               \tag{2}
\]
When \(3^v\nmid l\) and \(v\ge1\), the order of \(y\) is divisible by nine.
In characteristic two the equality \(y+y^{-1}\in\mathbb F_2\) forces \(y=1\) or \(y^2+y+1=0\).
Thus for every \(w\) the right side of (2) has valuation at most \(w\).
Indeed \(v_2(c-a)<w\) gives the valuation directly, and otherwise division by \(2^w\) gives a nonzero residue.
For \(w=1\) its valuation is exactly one because \(a,c\) are odd.
The [Hensel and translated Eisenstein argument](Yin_C_G0345_v180.md#local) gives a single irreducible degree-\(N\) block at each root in the unramified field.
Over \(\mathbb Q_2\), Hensel separates the coprime powers of the irreducible residual factors, each of degree \(Nf_s\) including its multiplicity.
A local divisor meeting one root class contains its entire degree-\(N\) block after unramified extension and, by Frobenius conjugacy, the blocks at all \(f_s\) roots of that orbit.
This proves the exact assertion.

<a id="polygon"></a>
## Bounds even when the first residue vanishes

Assume henceforth that \(\gcd(n,l)=1\).
For \(N<2^{w+4}\), reduction modulo two alone gives \(d\ge f_s\ge2\cdot3^v\) at every root class of a factor.
Leading and constant coefficients are units at two after monic normalization over \(\mathbb Q_2\), so reduction preserves each local factor degree.
This proves the claimed large-degree bound in this range.
For \(N\ge2^{w+4}\) put \(J=N/2^{w+1}\ge8\).
Now \(l\) is odd.
Use the local coordinate \(X=\xi(1+T)\) and write
\[
 Q(T)=a\{\xi^n(1+T)^n+\xi^{-n}(1+T)^{-n}\}
       +b\{\xi^l(1+T)^l+\xi^{-l}(1+T)^{-l}\}+c .
\]
For \(1\le i\le J\) both \(\binom{\pm n}{i}\) are divisible by \(2^{w+1}\), by the identity
\(\binom{\pm n}{i}=(\pm n/i)\binom{\pm n-1}{i-1}\) and \(v_2(i)\le u-w-1\).
Consequently every coefficient of \(Q\) in degrees \(1,\ldots,J\) has valuation at least \(w\).
There is an index \(j\le2\) with coefficient of valuation at most \(w\).
Use \(j=0\) if (2) has valuation at most \(w\).
Otherwise the degree-one coefficient divided by \(2^w\) reduces to \((b/2^w)l(y+y^{-1})\), which is nonzero for \(y\ne1\).
For \(y=1\) use degree two, where the sum of the two binomial coefficients is \(\binom l2+\binom{-l}2=l^2\), odd.
If \(v\ge1\), the coprimality assumption gives \(3\nmid l\) and the preceding order argument always gives \(j=0\).

The polynomial \(P(\xi(1+T))=\xi^n(1+T)^n Q(T)\) satisfies the same coefficient valuation bounds through \(J\).
Indeed multiplication by \((1+T)^n\) in these degrees agrees with multiplication by one modulo \(2^{w+1}\).
Its reduction has a zero of multiplicity \(N\) at zero.
The lower Newton polygon therefore has negative slopes through abscissa \(N\), positive ordinates to its left, and ordinate zero at \(N\).
If \(P(\xi)=0\), discard the initial power of \(T\) when drawing the polygon and count the discarded roots among the first \(j\).

Every negative-slope segment covering an interval to the right of \(j\) ends at an abscissa \(q>J\).
In fact the polygon is at height at most \(w\) at \(j\), is decreasing up to \(N\), and every coefficient at indices \(j<i\le J\) has valuation at least \(w\).
The absolute slope of this segment is at most \(w/(J-j)\), since slopes are nondecreasing and the total drop from \(j\) to \(q\) is at most \(w\).
The Newton polygon theorem counts the roots of positive valuation with multiplicities by the horizontal lengths of these segments.
Thus, except for at most \(j\) roots in this residue class, their local coordinates satisfy
\[
                    0<v_2(T)\le w/(J-2).
\]
For any such root the ramification index over the unramified field is at least \((J-2)/w\), because its positive valuation belongs to the reciprocal of that index times the integers.
The irreducible local factor containing this root has at least that degree over the unramified field.
Frobenius permutes the residue classes in an orbit transitively and preserves these valuations under the corresponding choice of lift.
An irreducible rational divisor containing any such root has degree at least \(f_s(J-2)/w\gg n/(w2^w h)\).

If the divisor contains only exceptional roots, then \(v=0\), since \(j=0\) at every residue class for \(v\ge1\).
There are \(2h\) residue classes when \(v=0\) and at most two exceptional roots per class, so its degree is at most \(4h\).
This proves the degree alternative.

<a id="measure"></a>
## Passage from degree to Mahler measure

Take a primitive positive-measure irreducible factor \(S\).
A factor which is not a monic algebraic unit polynomial up to sign costs at least \(\log2\), by its endpoints.
For a nontorsion unit factor of large degree, the [five-term width inequality](Yin_C_G0345_v186.md#statement) gives \(m(S)\gg d/n\).
For \(d\le4h\), the [quadratic radial inequality](Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives \(d(2m(S))^2\ge1/10\) by bounding the sum of squares by the square of the sum of the absolute logarithmic moduli.
This gives \(m(S)\gg h^{-1/2}\), which also implies (1).
Additivity and the cost of integer content give the same bound for a positive-measure divisor.
For a common dilation of the exponents, a nontorsion root \(\alpha\) has \(\alpha^g\) as a nontorsion root at the reduced exponents with the same coefficients.
The [uniform radical denominator](Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) gives the additional factor \(1/R_L\).
