# Cyclotomic coefficient costs with finitely many conductors forbidden

<a id="statement"></a>
## The simultaneous resultant price for cyclotomic inputs

Let \(B\) be a finite set of positive integers, and let \(Q\) be a monic product of cyclotomic polynomials whose orders lie outside \(B\).
The product \(1\) is allowed.
Write \(A_b(Q)=\varphi(b)^{-1}\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_b,Q)|\).
Minimize the coefficient one-norm of \(Q^a C\) over monic products \(C\) of cyclotomic polynomials with orders outside \(B\).
Then
\[
       \lim_{a\to\infty}\frac1a\log\min_C\|Q^aC\|_1
                       =\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{A_b(Q):b\in B\}\bigr).      \tag{1}
\]
The same limit holds with any additional degree budget \(\deg C\le W(a)\) satisfying \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
It also holds when arbitrary nonzero integer cofactors coprime to every forbidden \(\Phi_b\) are allowed.
In particular, any fixed cyclotomic product with unit resultants at all forbidden orders can be required to multiplicity \(a\) without changing the limiting coefficient rate of an otherwise arbitrary input polynomial at those forbidden orders.

<a id="measures"></a>
## Averaging powers of measures under the allowed tests

Let \(\mu\) be any probability on the circle with \(\int\log|\Phi_n|\,d\mu\ge0\) for every \(n\notin B\).
It has no atom at an allowed root of unity since the corresponding logarithm is bounded above and its integral is nonnegative.
Let \(w_b\) be its total mass on the primitive \(b\)-th roots for \(b\in B\).
We claim that for any \(Q\) in the statement,
\[
               0\le \int\log|Q|\,d\mu
                    \le \sum_{b\in B} w_b A_b(Q).                       \tag{2}
\]
Take \(L\) to be a common multiple of all forbidden and all occupied orders, with \(L=1\) if there are none.
For every \(j\ge1\) coprime to \(L\), \(Q\mid Q(X^j)\) with an integral monic cyclotomic quotient.
Indeed raising to the \(j\)-th power permutes each primitive orbit in \(Q\), preserving multiplicities since the power map is unramified there.
It also permutes each forbidden orbit, so no forbidden factor enters the quotient.
Consequently \(\int\log|Q(z^j)|\,d\mu\ge\int\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu\), with finite integrals.

Average the power images of \(\mu\) over \(1\le j\le T\) coprime to \(L\).
They converge weakly as \(T\to\infty\) to
\[
          \left(1-\sum_{b\in B}w_b\right)\eta+
                         \sum_{b\in B}w_b\eta_b ,
\]
where \(\eta\) is normalized arc length and \(\eta_b\) is the uniform measure on primitive \(b\)-th roots.
For each point \(z\) of infinite order, the powers on every coprime residue class modulo \(L\) are equidistributed, by the geometric sum for each nonconstant Fourier mode and trigonometric approximation.
For \(z\) of order \(b\in B\), reduction of the unit classes modulo \(L\) is onto the unit classes modulo \(b\), with equal fiber sizes, so the average at this point gives \(\eta_b\).
Integrating continuous tests by dominated convergence establishes the weak limit, without requiring the initial masses to be uniform on the exceptional orbits.
Use the continuous truncations \(\max(\log|Q|,-J)\) as upper bounds and then let \(J\to\infty\).
The Haar integral is zero and the \(\eta_b\) integrals are \(A_b(Q)\).
This proves (2).
In particular every allowed cyclotomic logarithm with unit resultants at all exceptional orbits has integral zero for every such \(\mu\).

<a id="rate"></a>
## Integer cofactors and the cost for an additional mandatory factor

Put \(A=\max(\{0\}\cup\{A_b(Q):b\in B\})\).
For any \(\epsilon>0\) there are nonnegative weights on finitely many allowed cyclotomic logarithms such that adding them to \(\log|Q|\) gives circle supremum at most \(A+\epsilon\).
Indeed the [finite separation and compactness argument](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#dual) applies to the allowed orders alone.
If the asserted weights did not exist, first truncate \(\log|Q|\) and a finite list of allowed logarithms from below.
The compact convex set of their moment vectors would meet the orthant requiring first moment at least \(A+\epsilon\) and all other moments nonnegative.
Strict separation otherwise gives the desired weights since the last coordinates are nonnegative at Haar measure.
Exhaust the allowed orders and let the truncations decrease to the functions as in that argument.
The limit would be a probability violating (2).
Rounding \(a\) times the weights upwards yields a product \(C\) of degree \(O_{Q,\epsilon,B}(a)\); the [coefficient conversion](Yin_C_G0901_v14.md#integer) loses \(o(a)\) in log norm.
This proves the limiting upper bound, including the given degree budgets.
For the lower bound take the resultant at each forbidden order.
Every nonzero integral cofactor coprime to \(\Phi_b\) contributes an integer of modulus at least one, so \(\log\|Q^aC\|_1/a\ge A_b(Q)\).
The integer norm is also at least one.

More generally let \(P\ne0\) be an integer polynomial avoiding those primitive roots and define
\[
   \gamma_B(P)=\inf_{a\ge1,C}\frac1a\log\|P^a C\|_1
\]
with only allowed cyclotomic cofactors.
The same quantity is the limit of the minima at increasing \(a\), also for any cofactor degree allowance \(W(a)/a\to\infty\).
To see this, fix any one witness at order \(a_0\), repeat it \(\lfloor a/a_0\rfloor\) times and multiply by the bounded remaining power of \(P\).
Its cofactor degrees grow at most linearly and submultiplicativity gives the upper limit.
The defining infimum gives the lower limit.
Hence
\[
       \gamma_B(P)\le\gamma_B(PQ)\le\gamma_B(P)+
                   \max(\{0\}\cup\{A_b(Q):b\in B\}).
\]
The lower bound absorbs \(Q^a\) into the allowed cofactor.
The upper bound uses separate near-optimal multiples and their product at the same multiplicity.
This proves the unchanged rate when all tested resultants of \(Q\) are units.
Formula (1) concerns cyclotomic inputs; for a general noncyclotomic \(P\) these resultants remain lower bounds.

<a id="pullback"></a>
## Exact transfer of a forbidden set under power substitution

For \(N\ge1\) set \(B^{[N]}=\{m:m/\gcd(m,N)\in B\}\), a finite set since \(m\mid Nb\) for some \(b\in B\).
For the same integer \(P\) one has
\[
                      \gamma_{B^{[N]}}(P(X^N))=\gamma_B(P).
\]
Substituting \(X^N\) in any witness preserves its coefficient norm, and every root of the substituted cofactor maps under powering to an allowed root of the original.
For the reverse inequality take \(F=P(X^N)^a C(X)\) with \(C\) avoiding \(B^{[N]}\).
Up to a sign the product of \(C(\omega X)\) over \(\omega^N=1\) is a monic polynomial in \(Y=X^N\) with integer coefficients.
Indeed the product is invariant under these rotations and under rational Galois conjugation, and its coefficients are algebraic integers.
Its leading coefficient before normalization is \(1\) or \(-1\).
Its roots are precisely \(N\)-th powers of roots of \(C\), with multiplicity, so the resulting polynomial \(H(Y)\) is an allowed cyclotomic product.
The corresponding product of the rotated \(F\)'s is \(\pm P(X^N)^{aN}H(X^N)\) and has one-norm at most \(\|F\|_1^N\).
Dividing the logarithm by \(aN\) gives the reverse inequality.
In particular, forbidding all orders dividing \(N\) on \(P(X^N)\) is exactly the single-order-one avoidance problem on \(P\).
This concerns the full substituted polynomial, whose irreducible factors need not remain together in a separate single-seed test.
