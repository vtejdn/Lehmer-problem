# Power substitutions and individual exterior roots for reciprocal sections of every seed degree

<a id="statement"></a>
## Common dilations of the reciprocal sections

Fix \(s\ge3\), put \(g=U^s-U-1,\ B=1-U^{s-1}-U^s,\ F=g+VB\), and take positive coprime integers \(a,b\).
Write \(A_{a,b}(X)=F(X^a,X^b)\), \(D=s+1\), and let \(P\) be a monic nontorsion irreducible factor of this polynomial.
Its roots are units by the parent endpoints.
There are only finitely many primitive directions in which any \(P(X^q)\) can be reducible for any \(q\ge1\).
In fact all \(q\) preserve irreducibility whenever
\[
       \deg P>\max\{8(D+1)^2,\ D^2(40D^2(\log 6)^2+2)\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The [degree growth of primitive factors](Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives the finiteness assertion, since \(F\) is absolutely irreducible of rank two in characteristic zero.
The [good-prime theorem](Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#reciprocal) already proves (1) except for the possible prime two when \(s\equiv2\bmod3\).
We show in this case that \(\delta\alpha=\gamma^2\) in \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), for a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\) and \(\delta\in\{1,-1\}\), implies \(\deg P\le8(D+1)^2\).
The [prime and norm induction](Yin_C_G0345_v387.md#substitutions) then gives (1) for arbitrary products of primes.
For a fixed large primitive direction the nontorsion factors after common dilation \(q\) are exactly the \(P(X^q)\), with the original multiplicities and log measures, since roots above torsion are torsion.

<a id="nonvertical"></a>
## The component with both variables in characteristic two

Suppose \(s\equiv2\bmod3\) and put \(C=U^2+U+1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).
One has \(\gcd(\bar g,\bar B)=C\), including multiplicities.
Indeed their sum is \(U(U^{s-2}+1)\), \(\bar g(0)=1\), and reduction of \(\bar g\) modulo \(U^{s-2}+1\) gives \(C\), which divides \(U^{s-2}+1\).
Write \(\bar F=CH\), with \(H=G+VB_0\) and relatively prime \(G,B_0\).
The polynomial \(H\) is absolutely irreducible, and its support differences span \(\mathbb F_2^2\).
Indeed \(G(0)=B_0(0)=1\), and the linear coefficient of \(B_0=\bar B/C\) is one.
Let \(h_x\) be the multiplicity at a nonzero residue \(x\) of \(H(X^a,X^b)\), taken as zero off its roots.
The [toric derivative intersection](Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#tangencies) gives \(\sum_{h_x>0}(h_x-1)\le D^2\).

Consider the signed section of \(F_\delta=F(\delta^a U,\delta^b V)\) with root \(\beta=\delta\alpha=\gamma^2\).
Fix a place over two in a splitting field also containing the conjugates of \(\gamma\).
Outside \(C(x^a)=0\), residues with \(h_x\ge2\) account for at most \(2D^2\) conjugates.
Those with \(h_x=1\) are distinct and fixed by inertia as in the [Frobenius correction argument](Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#correction).
At each such embedding \(u=\gamma^a,v=\gamma^b\) are unramified units.
Their residues lie on \(H=0\), off \(C=0\), and annihilate the reduction \(J\) of \((F_\delta(U^2,V^2)-F_\delta(U,V)^2)/2\).
Here the value \(F_\delta(u,v)\) vanishes modulo the maximal ideal and therefore is divisible by two in the unramified extension, while \(F_\delta(u^2,v^2)=0\).
The function field of \(H\) has \(E_iJ=C^2(E_iH)^2\) for \(E_1=U\partial_U,E_2=V\partial_V\), by differentiation before reduction.
If \(J=HW\), then \(W E_iH=C^2(E_iH)^2\) for both \(i\).
The support condition makes \(E_1H,E_2H,E_1H-E_2H\) all nonzero on \(H\), giving a contradiction.
Thus there are at most \(2D^2\) such residue pairs by plane Bézout.
Distinct simple residues give distinct pairs since squaring is injective and \(a,b\) are coprime.
The total contribution off the vertical component is at most \(4D^2\).

<a id="vertical"></a>
## Ramification at the vertical component

Work over a finite unramified extension \(E/\mathbb Q_2\) containing all needed residue elements and their odd-order roots of unity lifting them.
For a vertical residue \(x\) let \(\xi\) be this lift of \(x\) and put \(\omega=\xi^a\).
Then \(\omega\) is a primitive cube root of unity.
Let \(T(X)=F_\delta(X^a,X^b)\), and let \(m_x\) be its multiplicity at \(x\) after reduction.
Up to its unit leading sign, Hensel factorization separates the degree-\(m_x\) monic factor whose roots reduce to \(x\) from factors which are units at \(\xi\).
If \(v_2(T(\xi))=1\), translating this factor by \(\xi\) makes it Eisenstein, since its reduction is a pure power.
Every root \(\beta'\) in this residue has \(v_2(\beta'-\xi)=1/m_x\) and degree \(m_x\) over \(E\), with \(v_2(2)=1\).

For any such root, if \(\beta'=\gamma'^2\) in \(E(\beta')\), take the odd-order lift \(\eta\) of the square root of \(x\), so \(\eta^2=\xi\).
If \(v_2(\gamma'-\eta)\ge1\), both factors of \(\gamma'^2-\xi\) have valuation at least one.
If \(v_2(\gamma'-\eta)<1\), the two factors have this same valuation, which must then equal \(1/(2m_x)\).
Both possibilities contradict the valuation \(1/m_x\) and the field degree \(m_x\).
This excludes the whole residue, including conjugates ramified over \(\mathbb Q_2\), because \(\gamma\in\mathbb Q(\beta)\).

For each \(\omega\) there is at most one vertical residue \(x\) with \(v_2(T(\xi))\ne1\).
Indeed \(g(\omega)=2\omega^2,\ B(\omega)=2\).
For \(s\) even, \(g(-\omega)=-2,\ B(-\omega)=-2\omega^2\); for \(s\) odd, \(g(-\omega)=2\omega,\ B(-\omega)=-2\omega\).
Thus both coefficients have valuation exactly one for either sign, and after division by two the equation imposes one value of \(x^b\) for the specified \(x^a\).
Bézout in \(a,b\) recovers \(x\).
If \(4\nmid a\), the vertical multiplicity from \(C(X^a)\) is at most two at each such residue.
Since \(h_x\le1+D^2\), at most \(2(3+D^2)\) conjugates remain above the two exceptions.
Together with the nonvertical bound this suffices.

If \(4\mid a\), then \(b\) is odd, and an exceptional residue has \(\xi^b=\omega^2\) by uniqueness of the odd-order lift.
The point \(z=-\delta\xi\) is a genuine root of \(T\) with this residue, since \((\delta z)^a=\omega,(\delta z)^b=-\omega^2\).
It lies off \(H\) after passage to the pair of residues: differentiating \(\bar F=CH\) in \(U\) and using \(C'=1\) gives \(H(\bar\omega,\bar\omega^2)=1+\bar\omega^2\) for even \(s\) and \(\bar\omega^2+1\) for odd \(s\).
Both are nonzero.
Hence \(m_x=2^{v_2(a)}\ge4\).
In \(zT'(z)\) the \(U\) derivative term is divisible by four and the \(V\) derivative term is \(-2b\omega^2\), of valuation one.
The remaining degree-\((m_x-1)\) local factor after removing \(X-z\) is Eisenstein on translation by \(z\), since its value at \(z\) has valuation one by this derivative.
For each of its roots \(\beta'\), one has \(v_2(\beta'-z)=1/(m_x-1)<1\), hence also \(v_2(\beta'-\xi)=1/(m_x-1)\) since \(z=\pm\xi\).
Its degree over \(E\) is \(m_x-1\), and the square-root valuation argument again excludes it.
The omitted root \(z\) is torsion.
This completes the bound at two for both signs.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## How exterior roots contribute to a rational factor

Let \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_t\) be the roots of \(B\) of modulus greater than one.
For \(r=b/a\) sufficiently large, every root of \(A_{a,b}\) is simple.
Given a factor \(P\), let \(k_j(P)\) count its roots \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha^a\) in a fixed small disk around \(\tau_j\).
There exist constants \(c_s,C_s>0\) such that
\[
 \sum_P k_j(P)=a,\qquad
 \left|m(P)-\frac1a\sum_{j=1}^t k_j(P)\log|\tau_j|\right|
 \le \frac{C_s e^{-c_s r}}a\sum_{j=1}^t k_j(P),                         \tag{2}
\]
where the sum on the left of the first equality is over the nontorsion monic irreducible factors.
The list is nonempty: \(g\) has a positive real root greater than one and its roots have absolute product one, so at least one lies inside the circle.
The polynomials \(g,B\) are reversals with simple roots and neither has a unit-circle zero.
Indeed \(g=g'=0\) would give \(U=-s/(s-1)\) and \(|U|^{s-1}=1/s\); on the circle \(g=0\) would give \(|U+1|=1\), making \(U\) a primitive cube root with \(U^s=U+1=-U^2\), impossible.
Their [characteristic-zero coprimality](Yin_C_G0345_v389.md#reciprocal) permits the rational function \(f=-g/B\).

As in the [cubic projection](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#projection), \(X\mapsto T=X^a\) identifies the roots, with multiplicity, with those of \(T^bB(T)^a-(-g(T))^a\).
Indeed \(T^b=f(T)^a\) gives the inverse \(T^u f(T)^v\) for \(au+bv=1\), and in the product over the \(a\)-th root rotations of \(X\) just one factor can vanish at a given root.
The function \(f\) has modulus one on the circle and winding number \(2t-s\) because \(g\) has \(t\) interior zeros and \(B\) has \(s-t\).
For a smooth argument lift \(\phi\) of \(f\) the function \(b\theta-a\phi(\theta)\) has positive derivative once \(r>\max|\phi'|\) and gains \(2\pi(b-a(2t-s))\) in one turn.
Thus exactly \(b-a(2t-s)\) of the \(sa+b\) roots are on the circle and are simple.
Reciprocity of the original section leaves exactly \(at\) roots outside.
On each of \(t\) fixed disjoint disks around the simple exterior poles, lying entirely outside the unit circle, the boundary satisfies \(|T|^r |B(T)|>|g(T)|\) for all sufficiently large \(r\).
Rouché gives \(a\) roots per disk; at each root \(|B(T)|=|T|^{-r}|g(T)|\) shows \(|T-\tau_j|\ll_s e^{-c_s r}\).
A repeated root would give \(Tf'/f=r\) while the left side equals \(-T/(T-\tau_j)+O_s(1)\) there and has exponentially large modulus.
The reciprocal interior roots are also simple.
Each exterior root contributes \(a^{-1}\log|T|\) to its own factor, which proves (2).

<a id="criterion"></a>
## The remaining existence question for each fixed seed

For this whole family at a fixed \(s\), allowing arbitrary positive exponent pairs, a sequence of nontorsion factors of log measure tending to zero exists if and only if there is a sequence of primitive directions with \(b/a\to\infty\) and actual nontorsion rational irreducible factors for which \(\sum_j k_j(P)=o(a)\).
Such a sequence necessarily has \(a\to\infty\), and its late primitive parents have at least two nontorsion factors.

Indeed for \(a\ge4b\) the leading block \(X^{sa}(1-X^b)\) has gap \(a-b\) from the other block of length at most four, and subtracting twice its width gives \(a-3b\ge(sa+b)/(4s+1)\).
The [separated-block bound](Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) gives a positive lower factor bound depending only on \(s\).
For \(a<4b\), the [endpoint estimate](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#finite) with at most six terms and coefficient length at most six bounds \(m(P)^2\) below by a fixed positive multiple of \(\min(a,b)/(sa+b)\).
The endpoint gaps have this size even after coincident inner terms cancel.
These arguments allow a common divisor, so a shrinking sequence forces \(b/a\to\infty\).
Its reduced primitive directions then grow, and (1) makes all nontorsion factors common substitutions of actual primitive factors of the same measure.
The constant positive minimum and finite maximum of the \(\log|\tau_j|\) in (2) give both implications.
Each nontorsion factor receives at least one exterior root by Kronecker, and the full allocation sums to \(at\), proving the last assertions.
