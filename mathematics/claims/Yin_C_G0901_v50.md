# Classifying bounded-degree correspondences between traces of small numbers

<a id="statement"></a>
## A finite list for bounded total Mahler measure

Write \(t(x)=x+x^{-1}\), and \(T_j(t(x))=x^j+x^{-j}\).
Fix positive bidegree bounds \(A,B\) and a total logarithmic measure bound \(M\).
Suppose \(x,y\) are nonzero nontorsion algebraic numbers with \(m(x),m(y)\le M\), and an irreducible \(P\in\mathbb Q[U,V]\) of bidegree \((a,b)\), \(1\le a\le A,\ 1\le b\le B\), satisfies
\[
                               P(t(x),t(y))=0.
\]
Once \(\max(\deg x,\deg y)\) is sufficiently large in terms only of \(A,B,M\), the two numbers are multiplicatively dependent, and up to nonzero rational scaling \(P\) is one of the following polynomials, with coprime positive integers \(p,q\).

* \(T_p(V)-T_q(U)\) or \(T_p(V)+T_q(U)\).
* For a primitive \(s\)-th root of unity \(\xi\) with \(s\ge3\), the product over the distinct rational conjugates \(c\) of \(\xi+\xi^{-1}\) of
\[
       T_q(U)^2+T_p(V)^2-c\,T_q(U)T_p(V)+c^2-4.
\]

The bidegrees are \((q,p)\) in the first case and \((q\varphi(s),p\varphi(s))\) in the second.
This list is finite at bounded bidegree and is uniform in all coefficients of \(P\).
Each listed polynomial admits pairs of nontorsion reciprocal units of unbounded degrees and bounded total measures.
If the unrestricted Lehmer infimum vanishes, then every listed polynomial admits such pairs with both total measures tending to zero.

<a id="height"></a>
## A uniform height test for the dependence and the functional identity

Put \(d_x=[\mathbb Q(x):\mathbb Q]\), \(d_y=[\mathbb Q(y):\mathbb Q]\), and \(D=[\mathbb Q(x,y):\mathbb Q]\).
The coefficients of \(P\) as a polynomial in \(V\) have no common nonconstant factor in \(\mathbb Q[U]\), by irreducibility and \(b>0\); they cannot all vanish at \(t(x)\).
Thus \(t(y)\) has degree at most \(b\) over \(\mathbb Q(x)\), and \(y\) has degree at most two over the field obtained by adjoining \(t(y)\).
By symmetry,
\[
                   D\le2\min(bd_x,ad_y).                                  \tag{1}
\]
At fixed bidegree both input degrees are therefore comparable.
If the coordinates are independent, the [rank-two height bound](../audits/literature/AD99/Yin_U001.md#ad99-u001) requires
\[
             m(x)m(y)\gg\frac{\max(d_y/b,d_x/a)}{(\log(3D))^{212}} .
\]
This excludes independence at sufficiently large degrees.

In the dependent case replace \(y\) by its inverse if needed, preserving its trace.
Modulo torsion write \(x=z^p,\ y=z^q\) with \(p,q\) positive and relatively prime.
This takes place in the group generated by \(x,y\) modulo torsion, a free group of rank one, so a generator has a representative \(z\in\mathbb Q(x,y)\) and
\[
                    x=\omega z^p,\qquad y=\eta z^q
\]
for roots of unity \(\omega,\eta\) in that same field.
The heights satisfy \(p h(z)=h(x),\ q h(z)=h(y)\).
If
\[
        P(\omega Z^p+\omega^{-1}Z^{-p},\eta Z^q+\eta^{-1}Z^{-q})           \tag{2}
\]
is not the zero Laurent polynomial, it vanishes at \(z\) and has exponent span at most \(2(ap+bq)\).
Consequently the degree \(D'\) of \(z\) over the maximal abelian extension is at most both \(D\) and \(2(ap+bq)\).
The [abelian relative height bound](../audits/literature/AD07/Yin_U001.md#rational-base) then gives
\[
              a h(x)+b h(y)\gg
                       \frac{(\log\log(5D))^3}{(\log(2D))^4},
\]
using the [uniform comparison for smaller relative degrees](Yin_C_G0345_v163.md#fiber).
At fixed bidegree the left side is \(O_{A,B,M}(1/\max(d_x,d_y))\).
Together with (1) this excludes nonidentity in (2) at large degrees.

<a id="curves"></a>
## The irreducible equation of the parametrized image

Set \(X=\omega Z^p,\ Y=\eta Z^q\), so \(Y^p=\xi X^q\) with \(\xi=\eta^p/\omega^q\).
If \(\xi=\pm1\), then \(T_p(t(Y))=\xi T_q(t(X))\).
Otherwise put \(c=\xi+\xi^{-1}\).
Expansion at \(W=X^q\) of the two traces \(W+W^{-1}\) and \(\xi W+\xi^{-1}W^{-1}\) verifies the quadratic equation displayed in the statement.

To check that these are the full geometric equations, work over \(\mathbb C\).
The function \(U(Z)=t(X)\) has degree \(2p\).
For generic \(Z\), equality of the pair of traces at \(Z'\) and \(Z\) means that \(X',Y'\) equal \(X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}\), respectively.
The two positive signs give only \(Z'=Z\) by coprimality of \(p,q\).
Either choice of mixed signs forces a nonzero power of \(Z\) to equal a constant by \(Y'^p=\xi X'^q\), and hence gives no generic point.
For two negative signs the condition is \(\xi^2=1\).
In that case there is exactly one corresponding \(Z'\): the equations \((Z'Z)^p=\omega^{-2}\), \((Z'Z)^q=\eta^{-2}\) are compatible and determine one value of \(Z'Z\) by Bézout.
This second point is distinct generically.
Thus the generic degree of \(Z\mapsto(U,V)\) is one for \(\xi\ne\pm1\), two otherwise.
The degree in \(V\) of the irreducible equation of the image is accordingly \(2p\) or \(p\) by the function-field tower over \(\mathbb C(U)\).
Each proposed equation is monic in \(V\) and already has that degree, so it is the irreducible equation over \(\mathbb C(U)\) and in \(\mathbb C[U,V]\).
Different conjugates of \(c\) give different monic equations since \(T_q(U)T_p(V)\) is nonconstant.
Their product is the rational irreducible equation by transitivity on the conjugates; every rational polynomial vanishing identically in (2) is divisible by it.
This yields the list, with the claimed bidegrees since \([\mathbb Q(c):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(s)/2\) for \(s\ge3\).
Bounded \(\varphi(s)\) allows only finitely many \(s\), for instance by \(\varphi(s)^2\ge s/2\).

<a id="examples"></a>
## Realization with bounded measures and the unrestricted zero alternative

For each listed equation one can take \(\omega=1\) and any fixed root of unity \(\eta\) with \(\eta^p=\xi\), using \(\xi=\pm1\) for the first two cases.
Take \(z\) to be the positive \(j\)-th root of the Salem root of Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial.
These units have degree \(10j\) and fixed positive total measure by the [irreducible power substitutions](Yin_C_G0901_v22.md#representatives).
The units \(x=z^p,\ y=\eta z^q\) have bounded positive total measures, since \(h(x)=p h(z),\ h(y)=q h(z)\), and both degrees are at most \([\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]\,10j\).
Their degrees tend to infinity: adjoining an indicated root and the fixed torsion factor recovers a field containing \(z\) at bounded relative cost.
Each has a conjugate on the circle by extending an embedding of \(z\) with that property, and is therefore reciprocal.

If the unrestricted infimum vanishes use any sequence of nontorsion units \(z\) of positive measures tending to zero from the [unrestricted reduction](Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower).
The same fixed powers and torsion factors give \(m(x),m(y)\to0\), and bounded-degree finiteness forces both degrees to diverge.
These units are eventually reciprocal by [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
The realization in this case remains conditional on the unrestricted zero alternative.
