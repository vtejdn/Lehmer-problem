# Local degree bounds for the binomial and equally spaced Salem candidates

<a id="statement"></a>
## Bounds at two primes

Let \(P=X^NQ+\sigma Q^*\), where \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), \(Q^*(X)=X^{\deg Q}Q(X^{-1})\), and \(N>\deg Q\).
Let \(d\) be the degree of a nonconstant irreducible rational factor of \(P\) whose roots are not roots of unity.
For \(p\) prime write \(v_p(n)\) for the exponent of \(p\) in the positive integer \(n\) and \(n_{p'}=n/p^{v_p(n)}\).

* Suppose \(Q=X^a-2\), \(a\ge1\), and put \(D=N+a\).
  With \(k=v_2(D)\) and \(w=\min(v_2(a),v_2(N))\), if \(k-w\ge3\) then
  \[
                     d\ \ge\ 2^k-2^{w+1}.
  \]
  With \(k=v_3(N)\) and \(w=\min(v_3(a),k)\), if \(k-w\ge2\) and \(d>a\), then
  \[
                     d\ \ge\ 3^k-3^w.
  \]
* Suppose \(Q=X^{2b}-X^b-1\), \(b\ge1\), and \(d>2b\).
  Write \(k=v_2(N)\) and \(w=\min(v_2(b),k)\).
  For \(\sigma=1\) one has \(d\ge2^k\).
  For \(\sigma=-1\) and \(k-w\ge3\) one has \(d\ge2^k-2^{w+1}\).

Consequently, along any sequence of such Salem factors with positive root tending to one, the binomial case requires
\[
             \liminf \frac{(N+a)_{2'}}{a}\ \ge1,\qquad
             \liminf \frac{N_{3'}}{a}\ \ge1.                          \tag{1}
\]
The equally spaced case requires
\[
             \liminf \frac{N_{2'}}b\ \ge1 .                           \tag{2}
\]
Each condition excludes a shrinking sequence throughout the range where its quotient is bounded above by a fixed constant less than one.
There is no restriction that the exponents be coprime.

<a id="local"></a>
## A residue class with a factor of large degree

Work over the integers of a finite unramified extension \(K\) of \(\mathbb Q_p\) whose residue field splits the reductions under consideration.
Normalize the valuation by \(v(p)=1\).
Each nonzero residue class has a unique root of unity of order prime to \(p\) as its Teichmüller representative, and these representatives are multiplicative.
Suppose a monic integral polynomial has a residual root \(\bar\zeta\) of multiplicity \(m>0\) and its value at the representative \(\zeta\) has valuation one.
Hensel factorization for the coprime residual factors \((X-\bar\zeta)^m\) and the remaining polynomial gives a monic factor of degree \(m\) containing precisely the roots in that class.
The complementary factor is a unit at \(\zeta\).
After translation by \(\zeta\) the degree-\(m\) factor reduces to \(X^m\) and its constant term has valuation one, hence it is Eisenstein over \(K\).
Every rational irreducible divisor meeting this class has degree at least \(m\), since it is divisible over \(K\) by this irreducible factor.

We also use this argument after removing explicit torsion roots from the class.
When \(P(X)=B(X^{p^w})\), set \(\xi=\zeta^{p^w}\).
Removing a monic factor \(H(Y)\) of \(B(Y)\) means removing \(H(X^{p^w})\) from \(P\).
Each factor \(Y-\xi\), or \(Y+\xi\) when \(p=2\), removed this way contains only roots of unity and reduces after substitution to \((X-\bar\zeta)^{p^w}\).
Thus the multiplicity decreases by precisely \(p^w\) for each one, and the new polynomial takes the value \((B/H)(\xi)\) at \(\zeta\).
A nontorsion factor which meets the class still meets it after these removals.
Indeed its roots in the class cannot be any of the deleted torsion roots.

<a id="binomial-two"></a>
## The binomial at two

Here \(P=X^D-2X^N-2\sigma X^a+\sigma\).
Modulo two this is \((X^{D_{2'}}-1)^{2^k}\), and each residual root has multiplicity \(2^k\).
Write \(Y=X^{2^w}\), \(A=a/2^w\) and \(L=N/2^w\), so \(B=Y^{L+A}-2Y^L-2\sigma Y^A+\sigma\).
Under \(k-w\ge3\), both \(A,L\) are odd and \(8\mid L+A\).
For any residual representative \(\zeta\), put \(\xi=\zeta^{2^w}\) and \(u=\xi^A\).
Their orders are odd, \(\xi^{L+A}=1\), and \(\xi^L=u^{-1}\).

For \(\sigma=1\) the value \(B(\xi)=2(1-u-u^{-1})\) has valuation one unless the reduction of \(u\) satisfies \(\bar u^2+\bar u+1=0\).
In this exceptional case \(u\) has exact order three by the Teichmüller lift.
Now \(u+u^{-1}=-1\), so \(B(\xi)=4\), while the oddness of \(A,L\) gives \(B(-\xi)=0\).
The quotient by \(Y+\xi\) takes the value \(4/(2\xi)\) at \(\xi\), of valuation one.
The residual multiplicity after removing this torsion factor is \(2^k-2^w\).

For \(\sigma=-1\) the value \(B(\xi)=2(u-u^{-1})\) has valuation one unless \(u=1\), because odd order and \(\bar u^2=1\) imply \(u=1\).
In this exceptional case \(B(\xi)=B(-\xi)=0\), and \(\xi B'(\xi)=3A-L=4A-(L+A)\) has valuation exactly two.
The quotient by \(Y^2-\xi^2\) takes the value \(B'(\xi)/(2\xi)\) at \(\xi\), of valuation one.
The remaining multiplicity is \(2^k-2^{w+1}\).
The local argument applies in every class, giving the first degree bound for all \(d\).

<a id="binomial-three"></a>
## The binomial at three

Modulo three, \(P=(X^a+1)(X^N+\sigma)\).
Since \(d>a\), some root of the given divisor reduces to a root \(\bar\zeta\) of \(X^N+\sigma\).
Otherwise its reduction would divide \(X^a+1\) by coprimality with \(X^N+\sigma\), contradicting the degree.
Here we use the monic integral representative of the divisor, which exists as \(P\) is monic.
The multiplicity of this class in the reduction of \(P\) is at least \(3^k\), including a possible overlap of the two residual polynomials.
With \(Y=X^{3^w}\), \(A=a/3^w\), \(L=N/3^w\), use the same displayed formula for \(B(Y)\) as in the binomial at two.
Now \(9\mid L\) and \(3\nmid A\).
The Teichmüller lifts give \(\xi^L=-\sigma\), as both signs are roots of unity of order prime to three.
At \(u=\xi^A\) one gets \(B(\xi)=3\sigma(1-u)\).
Its valuation is one unless \(u=1\).
When \(u=1\), \(\xi\) is a torsion root and \(\xi B'(\xi)=\sigma(L-3A)\) has valuation one.
Dividing by \(Y-\xi\) therefore leaves valuation one at the center, losing only \(3^w\) copies from the class.
The local argument proves \(d\ge3^k-3^w\).

<a id="equal"></a>
## Equally spaced exponents

Put \(R(U)=U^2-U-1\), so \(Q=R(X^b)\) and \(R^*(U)=1-U-U^2\).
Modulo two, \(P=(X^{2b}+X^b+1)(X^N+1)\).
By \(d>2b\), the same coprimality argument supplies a residual class of \(X^N+1\) meeting the divisor, of multiplicity at least \(2^k\) in \(P\).
The representative satisfies \(\zeta^N=1\).
For \(\sigma=1\), evaluation gives \(P(\zeta)=R(\zeta^b)+R^*(\zeta^b)=-2\zeta^b\), so the full multiplicity contributes to the local degree.

For \(\sigma=-1\), write \(Y=X^{2^w}\), \(A=b/2^w\), \(L=N/2^w\), and \(B=Y^L R(Y^A)-R^*(Y^A)\).
Under the stated hypothesis \(8\mid L\) and \(A\) is odd.
At \(\xi=\zeta^{2^w}\) one has \(\xi^L=1\), so \(B(\xi)=2(\xi^{2A}-1)\).
Its valuation is one unless \(\xi^A=1\), since \(\xi\) has odd order.
In that case \(B\) vanishes at both \(\xi\) and \(-\xi\), and \(\xi B'(\xi)=-L+4A\) has valuation two.
Dividing out \(Y^2-\xi^2\) leaves valuation one at \(\xi\) and residual multiplicity at least \(2^k-2^{w+1}\).
This proves the second set of bounds.

<a id="limits"></a>
## The size of the prime-to-\(p\) part

Let \(e^t\) be the positive root of a sequence of Salem factors with \(t\to0\).
Write \(r=a\) in the binomial case and \(r=b\) in the equally spaced case.
In either displayed multiple the upper and lower blocks each have coefficient length three and are disjoint.
Reading the coefficients down from the highest degree determines the split uniquely when their cumulative absolute value reaches three, so the two-block reduction uses the exponents of this multiple.
The [two-block bounds](Yin_C_G0345_v174.md#statement) imply \(d\ge1/(20t^2)\), \(r=O(\log(2/t)/t)\), and \(|Q(e^t)|\to0\).
In particular \(d/r\to\infty\).
For the binomial, \(e^{rt}\to2\) and \(m(Q)=\log2\), where \(m\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
For the equally spaced trinomial, \(e^{rt}\to\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), since \(R(e^{rt})\to0\) and \(e^{rt}>1\).
Here \(m(Q)=\log\phi\), by the two zeros of \(R\) and invariance of Mahler measure under power substitution.
In each case \(rt=m(Q)+o(1)\), so \(r\to\infty\).
The [degree balance](Yin_C_G0345_v176.md#statement) gives \((N-\deg Q)t/d=m(Q)+o(1)\).
Consequently \(N/(rd)\to1\), since \(\deg Q\le2r\) and \(d\to\infty\).
Also \((N+a)/(ad)\to1\) in the binomial case.

In each of the three desired bounds take \(M=N+a,\ p=2\), or \(M=N,\ p=3\), or \(M=N,\ p=2\), respectively, with the corresponding width \(r\).
It suffices to test subsequences along which \(M_{p'}/r\) is bounded.
For \(k=v_p(M)\) and the appropriate normalization \(w\) above one has \(p^w\le r\).
Thus \(p^{k-w}=M/(M_{p'}p^w)\gg M/r^2\to\infty\).
The cutoff on \(k-w\) and the relevant factor-degree threshold therefore both hold eventually.
The local estimates give \(d\ge p^k(1-o(1))\), and \(M/(rd)\to1\) yields
\[
                \frac{M_{p'}}r=\frac{M}{rd}\,\frac{d}{p^k}
                     \ \ge\ 1-o(1).
\]
This proves (1) and (2).
