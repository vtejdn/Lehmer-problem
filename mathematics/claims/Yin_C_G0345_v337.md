# The sharp plastic threshold for two short end blocks

<a id="statement"></a>
## When the upper block is a signed copy of the lower block

Write \(m=\log M\) for logarithmic Mahler measure, and let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(X^3-X-1\).
Put \(L=\log\theta\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(N\), nonzero constant coefficient and support in \([0,W]\cup[N-W,N]\), where \(W\) is an integer and \(0\le W<N/2\).
This also covers two arbitrarily placed intervals of length at most \(W\), since one must contain \(0\) and the other \(N\).
Write \(U=\sum_{j=0}^{W}[X^j]P\,X^j\), \(a=\deg U\), and \(n=N-a\).
There are absolute constants \(C,D_0>0\) with the following property.
If \(a\ge1\), \(D=n/a\ge D_0\), and \(P\) is not of the form \(U(1\pm X^n)\), then
\[
                  m(P)\ \ge\ L-\frac{C}{D}\bigl(1+L+\log(2D)\bigr).       \tag{1}
\]
When \(a=0\), the same exclusion of a signed copy implies \(m(P)\ge L\).
For every \(\delta>0\) there is consequently an \(\epsilon_\delta>0\) such that
\[
            W\le\epsilon_\delta N,\qquad m(P)\le L-\delta
          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P=U(1\pm X^n),\qquad m(P)=m(U).       \tag{2}
\]
These conclusions hold for the whole polynomial, including products and repeated factors.
In particular for any sequence of irreducible inputs of positive measure with \(W/N\to0\), one has \(\liminf m(P)\ge L\).

Both bounds on the limiting threshold are sharp.
There are whole polynomials with \(W=3\), distinct end blocks even up to sign and \(0<m(P)<L\) tending to \(L\) from below.
Thus even the strict inequality \(m(P)<L\) cannot replace the fixed margin in (2).
There are also irreducible Salem polynomials with \(W=3\) whose measures tend to \(L\) from above.
For the examples from below we determine the first-order deficit and the support of the actual irreducible Salem factor.

<a id="rigidity"></a>
## Reciprocal polynomials and the independent measure of the low block

For \(V(0)\ne0\), write \(V^*(X)=X^{\deg V}V(1/X)\), and allow reciprocity up to sign.
Every integral polynomial \(V\) with \(V(0)\ne0\) and \(m(V)<L\) satisfies \(V^*=\pm V\).
Indeed the leading and constant coefficients have absolute value at most \(M(V)<\theta<2\), so both are units.
After a sign normalization all irreducible factors are monic and each has measure at most \(M(V)\), by multiplicativity and nonnegativity of logarithmic integral Mahler measures.
[Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives reciprocity of each factor other than \(X-1\), and that factor changes sign under reversal.
The product, with its multiplicities, is therefore reciprocal up to sign.

If \(m(P)<L\), split \(P^*=\pm P\) across its disjoint end intervals.
The high block is exactly the signed reversal of the low one, giving \(P=U+sX^n U^*\) for \(s\in\{-1,1\}\).
This proves the constant-block assertion when \(a=0\).
For \(a\ge1\), apply the [two-variable band comparison](Yin_C_G0345_v332.md#statement) to \(U(X)+sY U^*(X)\), with slow degree bound \(a\) and fast exponent \(n\).
On \(|X|=1\) the two row moduli coincide.
The linear Jensen identity in \(Y\) gives the independent logarithmic mean \(m(U)\), since their common zeros on the circle form a finite set and the baseline \(\log|U|\) is integrable.
The whole-polynomial estimate permits a common row factor and gives
\[
               |m(P)-m(U)|\le\frac{C}{D}\{1+m(U)+\log(2D)\}.              \tag{3}
\]
In fact (3) applies to every mirrored pair \(U+sX^nU^*\) in this range.
When \(U^*=\pm U\) the two blocks are signed copies, so \(P=U(1\pm X^n)\), and the binomial has zero logarithmic measure.
In the other case the preceding consequence of Smyth gives \(m(U)\ge L\).
Increase \(D_0\) until \(1-C/D\ge0\).
Inserting \(m(U)\ge L\) in the lower inequality in (3) proves (1), which already holds when \(m(P)\ge L\).

For \(a\ge1\), \(D\ge(N-W)/W\), so the error in (1) is less than \(\delta\) once \(W/N\) is sufficiently small.
For an irreducible positive-measure \(P\), a signed copy would have a nonconstant binomial factor of measure zero and an integral cofactor \(U\), hence is impossible.
This proves the irreducible lower limit including the case of bounded degree and \(W=0\).

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Two signs at the plastic number

Let \(f=X^3-X-1\), \(g=f^*=1-X^2-X^3\), and \(P_n^\pm=X^n f\pm g\).
For \(n\ge8\) each has width-three end blocks which are distinct up to sign.
The [circle count for this cubic](Yin_C_G0162_v3.md#salem) gives at most one root strictly outside the circle, including multiplicity, for either sign.
Indeed \(|f|=|g|>0\) on the unit circle, and \(X^n f\pm t g\) for \(0\le t<1\) has the same \(n+2\) interior roots in number as \(X^n f\) by Rouché's theorem.
At \(t=1\) any strictly exterior zeros persist with their total multiplicity under a small perturbation.
Since \(P_n^-(1)=0\), \((P_n^-)'(1)=7-n<0\), and \(P_n^-(\theta)=-g(\theta)=\theta^2+\theta>0\), this sign has a simple root \(\beta_n^-\in(1,\theta)\).
The plus sign has a simple real root \(\beta_n^+>\theta\) since \(P_n^+(\theta)=g(\theta)<0\) and the leading coefficient is positive.
Each is the sole exterior root of its polynomial, so \(m(P_n^\pm)=\log\beta_n^\pm\).
At every fixed \(x\in(1,\theta)\) the term \(x^n f(x)\) tends to \(-\infty\), and at each fixed \(x>\theta\) it tends to \(+\infty\).
The resulting brackets show \(\beta_n^\pm\to\theta\) from the indicated sides.

The rate for either sign is
\[
 \beta_n^\pm=\theta\ \pm K\theta^{-n}+O(n\theta^{-2n}),\qquad
 m(P_n^\pm)=L\ \pm (K/\theta)\theta^{-n}+O(n\theta^{-2n}),\qquad
 K=\frac{\theta^2+\theta}{3\theta^2-1}.                              \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(f(\beta_n^\pm)=\mp g(\beta_n^\pm)(\beta_n^\pm)^{-n}\).
For any fixed \(1<c<\theta\), the simple root of \(f\) and the brackets first give \(|\beta_n^\pm-\theta|=O(c^{-n})\).
Thus \(n|\log(\beta_n^\pm/\theta)|\to0\), so \((\beta_n^\pm)^{-n}=O(\theta^{-n})\) and the same equation improves the root displacement to \(O(\theta^{-n})\).
Expanding the exponential now gives \((\beta_n^\pm)^{-n}=\theta^{-n}(1+O(n\theta^{-n}))\).
The Taylor expansions of \(f,g\) at \(\theta\), followed by that of the logarithm, give (4).

For \(n\equiv9\bmod120\), \(n\ge9\), the [irreducible progression](Yin_C_G0336_v1.md#argument) consists of the complete \(P_n^+\).
It gives the matching upper limit \(L\) among irreducible polynomials with end width \(3\).
Every \(P_n^-\) has the factor \(X-1\), so its strict inequality below \(L\) is a whole-polynomial endpoint example.

<a id="salem-support"></a>
## The actual Salem factor for the negative sign

For all sufficiently large \(n\) the [factorization for all delays](Yin_C_G0336_v2.md#transfer) gives \(P_n^-=C_n S_n\), where \(S_n\) is the monic irreducible Salem polynomial of \(\beta_n^-\) and \(C_n\) is cyclotomic with \(1\le h_n=\deg C_n\le30\).
The lower bound follows from \(P_n^-(1)=0\).
Although \(m(S_n)=m(P_n^-)\), every interval of \(30\) consecutive integer exponents contained in \([4,n-1]\) meets the support of \(S_n\).
In particular its support divided by \(\deg S_n\) converges in Hausdorff distance to \([0,1]\).

To see this, fix \(n\) in the stated range, abbreviate \(h=h_n\), and write the power series \(-g/C_n=\sum_{j\ge0}s_j X^j\), which exists because \(C_n(0)=\pm1\).
Modulo \(X^n\) it equals \(S_n\).
For \(j>3\) the coefficient identity \(C_n\sum s_j X^j=-g\) determines \(s_j\) from the previous \(h\) coefficients.
Any run of \(h\) zeros starting at \(j\ge4\) would force all subsequent terms to vanish.
The series would then be a polynomial, implying \(C_n\mid g\), whereas \(X-1\mid C_n\) and \(g(1)=-1\).
This proves the asserted gap bound on the support.
Finally \(\deg S_n=n+3-h=n+O(1)\), with both ends occupied, so the bound on the interior gaps proves the Hausdorff limit.

The contributing researcher derived the finite-width inequality, the signed asymptotic and the support bound for the negative-sign Salem factors, and used the existing positive-sign progression for sharpness among irreducibles.
