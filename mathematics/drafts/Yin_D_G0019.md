## Exploratory lead: the original Hankel sequence as divisor arithmetic

Set U=P_2, V=P_4 and G=sqrt(V/U), with the constant coefficient one and all original roots retained as in C860 v8.
For the shifted leading Hankel determinants use exactly the coefficient convention of C858/C860, rather than changing a shift to fit a source theorem.
The proposed change is to study the determinant sequence itself through continued fractions and divisor translation on the curve C_P: y^2=U(x)V(x).
This keeps relations among successive determinants and among their prime divisors that the minimum rational-approximation rank does not record.
It is therefore a genuinely different potential source of information from another Pade competitor, while remaining a branch of the S9 determinant mechanism rather than requiring a new S merely for a name.

The concrete source correspondence is partial.
[Hone, arXiv:1907.05204v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05204v3), Theorem 4.1 and equations (4.14), expresses the iteration variables for the stated J-fraction as ratios of Hankel determinants.
The full sigma-function formula in genus two is Conjecture 5.5 in that inspected edition, not an available arbitrary-genus theorem.
[Zannier, arXiv:1602.00934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00934), Theorem 1.1, Remark 1.2 and the divisor constructions in Section 2, connects continued-fraction degree patterns and Hankel vanishing with translation in generalized Jacobians.
Its Theorem 1.5 gives fixed-curve quadratic height growth with curve-dependent constants and does not supply a uniform-in-degree lower bound.
[De Jong and Mueller, arXiv:1306.4030v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.4030v1), Theorems 2.1--2.3, provides division-polynomial local-height limits under its stated odd-degree pointed-hyperelliptic and theta-avoidance hypotheses.
That theorem is not already a formula for the present even-degree original Hankel sequence.
[Chang and Liu, arXiv:2603.11670v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11670v4), Section 3.4 and Appendix A, supplies a corrected bilateral matching framework for its quadratic orthogonal pairs; it does not establish the missing uniform arithmetic lower bound here.
These are inspected primary statements and selected explanatory passages, without full proof review or adoption as project dependencies.

## Original geometry that the first test must preserve

The following elementary setup calculation is author work, included to make the proposed correspondence concrete.
Power stability gives distinct roots within U and within V.
A common root of U and V would equate the positive heights of an original conjugate squared and another conjugate to the fourth power, which is impossible for a nontorsion input.
Thus the characteristic-zero product UV is squarefree of degree 2d, and C_P has genus d-1 and two rational points at infinity.
Good or bad reduction at a finite prime is a separate matter.
The obvious prospective translation class is delta=[infinity_+-infinity_-], but identifying the actual shifted Hankel sequence may require a base divisor and cannot be replaced by asserting this class is the answer.
The power stability, radical and source-height scopes here are those already retained in L445.

There are two exact original identities worth testing before invoking a general Jacobian theorem:

    V(x^2)=U(x)U(-x),
    h=(y-U)/(2U),   h(h+1)=(V-U)/(4U),   G=1+2h.

The first follows by multiplying (1-alpha_j^2 x)(1+alpha_j^2 x) over the original roots.
The second follows directly from y^2=UV and retains the actual integral binary germ of L445.
These identities give specific arithmetic and dynamical relations, rather than an arbitrary hyperelliptic curve selected by analogy.
They do not give an unpriced self-map of C_P: a power or pullback can change the curve, degree, divisor and Mahler cost.

Write d=2s and U=x^s A(x+x^(-1)), V=x^s B(x+x^(-1)).
Reciprocity gives the involution (x,y) -> (x^(-1),y/x^d).
With z=x+x^(-1), w=y/x^s and v=(x-x^(-1))w, its two natural quotient equations are

    w^2=A(z)B(z),
    v^2=(z^2-4)A(z)B(z).

The quotient genera are s-1 and s when the stated original distinctness is retained.
The displayed maps are directly checkable, but any claimed isogeny, polarization or height comparison remains a separate obligation.
Even these quotient genera still grow with d; this is not a bounded-dimension descent.
Their possible utility is to identify the actual divisor components and charges, not to assume that halving a genus solves the uniformity problem.

## What would have to survive normalization

For every nonzero integral determinant Delta_n one has exactly

    log|Delta_n|=sum_p v_p(Delta_n) log p.

Summing all finite places therefore supplies no inequality by itself.
An actual gain must come from the original divisor orbit, a forced local intersection, or a constraint on changing prime divisors that is independent of that identity.
If a proposed theta formula replaces Delta_n by a(P)b(P)^n c(P)^(n^2) times a theta value, the c(P) term is a principal quadratic cost at every place.
It cannot be suppressed as a harmless normalization.
Rational multipliers, changes of Hankel shift, finite exceptional zero minors, model discriminants, theta avoidance, polarization factors and passage to either quotient must all be charged.

One possible sufficient output, stated only to show the required scale, would be an original-sequence estimate

    liminf_(n -> infinity, Delta_n != 0) n^(-2) log|Delta_n| >= c m(P)

for an absolute c>0 on every sufficiently small positive power-stable reciprocal input, with a compatible nonzero-index subsequence if that is what the exact analytic estimate requires.
Combined with the existing same-germ upper bound at most 5 E_2 <=5m(P)^2, this would force m(P)>=c/5.
This is not a conjecture currently supported by the inspected sources.
Its purpose is to expose the first missing quantitative step: non-torsion or positive canonical height for each fixed curve is insufficient, and replacing the displayed lower bound by an unspecified positive constant depending on P does not solve G0001.
No known theorem inspected here supplies the needed uniform genus dependence.
A result confined to Salem inputs would additionally leave the unrestricted reciprocal core uncovered.

## Characteristic-zero power identity: exact maps and the lost index

The following continuation was developed for Yin on 2026-11-05 after the finite-field component was adopted in C858 v3.
It is complete author work for the displayed algebraic calculations, without independent review or an adopted new Claim.
The coordinating agent proposed checking the actual power identity and its divisor transfer; the research contributor developed the fiber-product, pointwise push-pull and approximation-cost calculations below.
Only this added continuation is new here; the unresolved scalar-normalization and unrestricted-gap boundaries above remain in force.
No new external height theorem is used.

### Original input and the exact identity

Let P be a monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic integer polynomial of degree d whose positive powers retain degree d, as in C858 v3.
Thus d is even, and all the powered polynomials below are monic, reciprocal, of degree d and constant coefficient one.
Put

    U=P_2, V=P_4, W=P_8,
    G_P(z)=sqrt(V(z)/U(z)),
    G_(P_2)(z)=sqrt(W(z)/V(z)),

with constant coefficient one in both square roots.
Direct multiplication over all original roots gives

    U(z)U(-z)=V(z^2), V(z)V(-z)=W(z^2),
    G_P(z)G_P(-z)=G_(P_2)(z^2).                         (CZ1)

The last identity follows by squaring and then comparing constant coefficients; its scalar is exactly one.
It is an identity of these normalized formal germs, not a self-map of the curve carrying G_P.

Set A(X)=U(X)V(X) and B(X)=U(-X)V(-X).
Both A and B are squarefree and they are coprime.
Within a powered level, a coincidence after changing sign would identify two original conjugates after a further positive power, contrary to the power-stable degree hypothesis.
Between the squared and fourth-powered levels, even a sign-twisted coincidence would equate positive absolute Weil heights 2h_abs(alpha) and 4h_abs(alpha), which is impossible.
The same argument gives the squarefreeness and coprimality of V and W.
Thus the subsequent function fields have their stated degrees, rather than degrees obtained by ignoring a common branch factor.

### The two degree-two steps and the actual degree-four map

Use smooth projective models of

    C:  y^2=U(X)V(X),
    Y:  y_1^2=A(X), y_2^2=B(X),
    D:  Z^2=A(X)B(X)=V(X^2)W(X^2),
    C': Z^2=V(T)W(T).

The independent square classes of A and B make Q(Y) a degree-four extension of Q(X).
Its three nontrivial square-class characters have representatives y_1, y_2 and y_1y_2.
The maps

    pi_1:Y -> C,   (X,y_1,y_2) -> (X,y_1),
    pi_2:Y -> C,   (X,y_1,y_2) -> (-X,y_2),
    lambda:Y -> D, (X,y_1,y_2) -> (X,Z=y_1y_2),
    nu:D -> C',    (X,Z) -> (T=X^2,Z)

all have degree two.
Consequently rho=nu composed with lambda has degree four.
Equivalently Q(Y) has degree eight over Q(T), while Q(C') has degree two over Q(T).
This explicitly pays the extra degree which would be missed by calling rho a quadratic map.
The involutions (y_1,y_2)->(-y_1,-y_2) and (X,y_1,y_2)->(-X,y_2,y_1) generate its order-four deck group.

The source and powered target C,C' each have genus d-1.
The intermediate genera are g(D)=2d-1 and g(Y)=4d-3, so the construction is not a bounded-genus reduction.
These values can be checked using the same regular-function and Riemann--Roch argument already inspected in C858 v3, without a new ramification theorem.
For Y the affine model is smooth because the branch sets of A and B are disjoint.
Every function regular at finite points is uniquely a polynomial combination of 1,y_1,y_2,y_1y_2.
To see that its rational coefficients are polynomials, project onto these four characters by sign averaging; a finite pole cannot be cancelled at a simple branch point because the square-root factor there has odd order one and base-function orders are even.
At its four infinity points the four leading character vectors are linearly independent, so for N>=2d the functions with poles of order at most N at each infinity have coefficient-degree bounds N,N-d,N-d,N-2d.
Their dimension is 4N-4d+4, and Riemann--Roch gives genus 4d-3.
For D the identical two-character calculation with a square root of degree 4d gives genus 2d-1.
The Riemann--Roch/model inputs are exactly those of [The26b-U006](../audits/literature/The26b/Yin_U006.md#accepted-curve-lattice-inputs); they apply over Q as well as over finite fields.

### Characteristic-zero Hankel positions, with no scalar formula

Let P_+,P_- be the two rational infinity points of C, characterized by y/U tending to +1,-1, and let D_U be the reduced branch divisor over U=0.
Because U and V are reciprocal of the same degree, the expansion of y/U at P_+ in z=1/X is exactly the original G_P(z).
Put

    E_h=(h+d-1)P_--(h-d+1)P_+-D_U,
    delta=P_+-P_-.

The complete coefficient reversal and odd-characteristic divisor argument of [C858 v3](../claims/Yin_C_G0858_v3.md#odd-proof) applies over Q before its separate finite-class-group step.
Explicitly, a kernel vector of (g_(i+j+1))_(0<=i,j<h) gives polynomials A_h,B_h of degree at most h-1 with B_h G_P(1/X)-A_h=O(X^(-h-1)).
The function R=U A_h-B_h y lies in L(E_h).
Conversely every R in L(E_h) is of that form: finite regularity and sign averaging give polynomial coefficients, the zeros along D_U give divisibility of the rational coefficient by U, and the two infinity bounds give both degree bounds h-1.
Thus over Q one has

    ker H_h(G_P) is isomorphic to L(E_h),
    Delta_h(G_P)=0 iff l(E_h)>0,
    L(E_0)=0.                                             (CZ2)

No use of finite-field cardinality occurs in these steps.
Over Q the class of delta need not have finite order, so no finite-field period conclusion is transferred.
More importantly, (CZ2) identifies vanishing positions and kernel dimensions only.
It supplies neither the value of a nonzero Delta_h nor its normalization as a theta section, local height or division polynomial.

### Exact infinity push-pull and the three different sign characters

The four rational infinity points of Y will be denoted Q_(s,t), where s,t belong to {+1,-1} and y_1/X^d tends to s while y_2/X^d tends to t.
The parity assumption d even makes the pi_2 image have sign t rather than a sign changed by (-X)^d.
Let P'_+,P'_- be the infinity points of C', and put delta'=P'_+-P'_-.
Each pi_i is unramified at these points.
The map rho sends Q_(s,t) to P'_(st), and has ramification index two there: the parameter 1/T pulls back to (1/X)^2.
There are two points above each P'_sign, each of residue degree one, so the total local degree is four as required.

In the free abelian group of divisors define

    D_1 =Q_(+,+)+Q_(+,-)-Q_(-,+)-Q_(-,-),
    D_2 =Q_(+,+)+Q_(-,+)-Q_(+,-)-Q_(-,-),
    D_12=Q_(+,+)+Q_(-,-)-Q_(+,-)-Q_(-,+).

Pointwise pullback and pushforward give the exact identities

    pi_1^*delta=D_1, pi_2^*delta=D_2,
    rho^*delta'=2D_12,
    rho_*pi_1^*delta=rho_*pi_2^*delta=0,
    pi_1_*rho^*delta'=pi_2_*rho^*delta'=0.                 (CZ3)

For example the two terms with s=+1 under rho contribute one copy of each target infinity point, and the terms with s=-1 subtract the same pair.
The other cancellations follow from the displayed sums.
These are formal-divisor equalities before passing to divisor classes, so they do not require a conjectural polarization or height comparison.
They distinguish the separate sign characters s,t from the product character st; no orthogonality statement about a height pairing is being assumed.

For completeness rho has ramification index two at each of the four points above X=0 as well, since both y_i are units there and T=X^2.
At a finite branch point of A or B the target point is a simple branch point of C'; the base coordinate map X->X^2 is unramified there and the two quadratic branch indices cancel in the tower, so rho is unramified there.
Away from X=0, infinity and those branch points all relevant covers are unramified.
The common product identity has therefore not concealed an additional infinity multiplicity or a lost component.

### The whole Hankel divisor loses its varying index under this map

Let D'_V be the reduced branch divisor over V(T)=0 on C', and put

    E'_k=(k+d-1)P'_--(k-d+1)P'_+-D'_V.

The finite branch divisor also has an exact paid image:

    rho_*pi_i^*D_U=2D'_V, i=1,2.                          (CZ4)

Indeed each root of V(T) has two square roots X; exactly one is a root of U(X), and the other is a root of U(-X), since those root sets are disjoint.
Above the first one, pi_1^*D_U consists of two points, distinguished by the nonzero second square root, and both map to the unique branch point Z=0 over that root of V.
The pi_2 calculation is the same with the signs exchanged.
Counting geometric points gives the stated multiplicity two, and these Galois-invariant divisor identities descend to Q.

Together with the infinity calculations, (CZ4) yields for every h>=0

    rho_*pi_i^*E_h
       =(2d-2)(P'_++P'_-)-2D'_V
       =2E'_0, i=1,2.                                   (CZ5)

The h coefficient is exactly zero, not a small error.
The degrees agree: pullback by pi_i doubles deg E_h=d-2, and the resulting degree 2d-4 is the degree of 2E'_0.
As a useful exact normalization, div(V)=2D'_V-d(P'_++P'_-), while the differential dT/Z has divisor (d-2)(P'_++P'_-).
At each finite simple branch point, both dT and Z have order one; at infinity their orders give d-2, which proves the latter assertion directly.
Hence 2E'_0 differs from the displayed canonical divisor by -div(V).
This calculation explains the fixed target of (CZ5); it does not turn its index-independent image into a growing determinant estimate.

### The precision and original-measure costs do not disappear

There is also an elementary precision loss in using the normalized germ identity as a product approximation.
Suppose A(z),B(z) are polynomials of degrees at most n, A(0)=B(0)=1, and for some h>=1 and e!=0 one has

    A(z)-B(z)G_P(z)=e z^(2h)+O(z^(2h+1)).

This is an explicitly supplied approximation, not an assertion that every Hankel kernel has a representative with these nonzero constant terms.
The polynomials A(z)A(-z) and B(z)B(-z) are even, so write them as A_2(z^2), B_2(z^2), with degrees at most n and constant terms one.
By (CZ1), multiplying out the errors gives

    A_2(w)-B_2(w)G_(P_2)(w)=2e w^h+O(w^(h+1)), w=z^2.    (CZ6)

The leading coefficient is 2e because both even-order errors contribute e at the constant-one branch; the product of the two errors has higher order.
The result is even, so the next possible remainder has the indicated w order.
Thus this operation retains the degree bound n but cuts the supplied even precision 2h to h.
Odd leading errors may cancel; their later order needs its own calculation and is not an automatic precision doubling.
No determinant scalar identity follows from this polynomial-product calculation.

The powered input P_2 still has original degree d, while

    m(P_2)=2m(P),
    sum_(|alpha_i^2|>1) log^2|alpha_i^2|
          =4 sum_(|alpha_i|>1) log^2|alpha_i|.             (CZ7)

The positive total measure and radial-square cost therefore increase by factors two and four respectively.
The identity has no hidden function-level scalar, but it does not remove these input costs or the missing determinant-level quadratic gauge.

### Bounded conclusion of this test

The characteristic-zero normal-index interface (CZ2) and the original-power geometry (CZ1)--(CZ7) are explicit author derivations at the stated hypotheses.

The particular natural pull-push route through Y annihilates the original infinity translation and sends the entire varying Hankel divisor to a fixed divisor on the powered curve.
It therefore supplies no direct positive multiple of the original varying divisor class, and the elementary product approximation pays a precision loss in addition to the exact powered-input measure cost.
This is a concrete failure of that proposed direct transfer, not a claim that every correspondence, scalar identity, joint determinant or height relation between these curves is impossible.
Further work through this interface needs a different nonvanishing arithmetic quantity or an actual normalized determinant identity with a surviving uniform original-m return; neither is supplied here.
Repeating the same push-pull calculation or replacing fixed-curve positivity by an unspecified curve-dependent height would not meet that condition.
