### SR2. What the existing primary interfaces actually supply

**BDM07, selected monic specialization of Theorem 2.4.**
The [retained published original](../literature/pdf/BDM07.pdf), printed351--352/PDF5--6, says that for a prime p and an integral polynomial f, a polynomial F with every coefficient1 modulo p, f dividing F, and M(F)=M(f), exists exactly when f modulo p is a product of reductions of integer cyclotomic polynomials.
For the present comparison restrict to monic f, so no leading-sign normalization issue enters this statement.
The complete cyclotomic reductions are required; being a divisor of x^N-1 in F_p[x] is not the same hypothesis.
The displayed construction supplies no bound linear in deg f for the degree of F.
Consequently it does not turn a general periodic reduction into the degree-controlled completion needed for an absolute gap.
The resulting F can also have cyclotomic factors, so the all-coefficients-one lower bound for a polynomial with NO cyclotomic factors cannot be applied to F merely because M(F)=M(f).

The statement and its selected proof were text-read, and printed351--352 were rendered.
One literal indexing slip matters if the construction is reused: printed352 sets n=ell*p^t+1, but the displayed product over d dividing ell has degree ell*p^t and equals x^(ell*p^t)-1, not x^n-1 with that printed n.
The resulting geometric-sum polynomial has degree ell*p^t-1.
This observation does not refute the characterization statement.
No fixed project proof here relies on the printed constructor or a newly asserted whole-paper proof audit.
The earlier BDM07-U001 and JT26-U001/U002 scopes remain unchanged.

**Sam07, Theorem 4.1.**
The [retained original](../literature/pdf/Sam07.pdf), printed216/PDF8, treats f in Z[x] of degree n*r satisfying

    f(x) congruent to (x^n-1)^r modulo m.

For a factor g without cyclotomic factors it gives

    mu(g)>=c deg(g)/(n 2^r),

where c>0 is the stated universal constant.
For the monic original minimal polynomial g=P, mu(g)=log M(P).
This is potentially useful only after constructing an ACTUAL polynomial multiple f with those hypotheses and controlled n*2^r/deg(P).
The source does not construct that completion for arbitrary periodic coefficient residues.
A finite-field existence argument with uncontrolled multiplicative order or multiplicity does not supply the required uniform bound.

**Sam07, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.2.**
Printed220/PDF12 treats equal-degree integral polynomials f,T with f congruent to T modulo m, and a factor g of f coprime to T.
In the rational/integer case its nu_infinity(T) is log ||T||_circle, and the conclusion is

    deg(f) mu(g)>=deg(g)(log m-log ||T||_circle).

Applied to a sparse-reversal comparison with deg(f)=D+k and g=P, this would give only

    log M(P)>=D/(D+k)(log m-log ||F_(D,G)||_circle).

Positivity requires the ACTUAL supremum norm to be below m.
The hypothesis M(G)<m does not imply that supremum-norm condition.
Thus this established interface does not replace the finite-family mean-logarithm criterion.
The exact statements, local norm convention, degrees and coprimality conditions were read; printed216 and220 were rendered.
Their complete proofs and the source's general local-height identity were not newly audited or imported as a dependency of C842.

### SR3. Exact compatibility and two limitations of reciprocal multipliers

The following is a direct algebraic check for continued investigation.
Let G be monic of actual degree k, and let Q be monic of actual degree q with

    Q*(x)=x^q Q(1/x)=Q(x).

In particular Q(0)=1.
Let Gnew be monic of actual degree k+q with Gnew congruent to QG modulo m.
Every star below uses that polynomial's stated full degree.
Coefficientwise reversal and multiplication then give

    Gnew* congruent to Q*G*=QG* modulo m,
    x^D Gnew-Gnew* congruent to Q(x^D G-G*) modulo m.   (SR1)

Consequently x^D G-G* in(P,m) implies x^D Gnew-Gnew* in(P,m).
This preserves the SAME original exponent D.
A degree change that is not accounted for in the reversal convention is not covered by this identity.

Ordinary integral multiplication itself cannot improve Mahler measure:

    M(QG)=M(Q)M(G)>=M(G).                              (SR2)

This uses the standard multiplicativity in [Smy08-U001](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) and M(Q)>=1 for nonzero integral Q.
Cyclotomic multiplication preserves the measure instead of reducing it.
A useful improvement would therefore require genuine coefficient changes by multiples of m in the new lift, not just the actual product QG.

There is also a precise obstruction to attempting the strongest target M(Gnew)=1.
Choose a prime p dividing m and assume G(0) is nonzero modulo p.
All three monic polynomials retain their actual degrees after reduction; the reductions of Q and G are nonzero.
In F_p(x), (SR1) gives the invariant

    Gnew*/Gnew=G*/G.                                   (SR3)

The equivalent cross-product equality is Gnew* G=Gnew G* in F_p[x].
Since Gnew(0) is congruent to G(0) modulo p, it is nonzero as an integer.
If M(Gnew)=1, Kronecker's theorem makes the monic Gnew a product of cyclotomic polynomials, with no zero factor.
Hence Gnew*=epsilon Gnew for epsilon in{1,-1}.
Reducing and cancelling the nonzero Q gives the necessary condition

    G* congruent to epsilon G modulo p.                (SR4)

Failure of both signs at one such p therefore rules out every reciprocal-multiplier lift of measure1, regardless of q.
For p=2 the two signs coincide.
This is ONLY an obstruction to measure1; it does not obstruct 1<M(Gnew)<m.
For application to the fixed-family criterion, the chosen Q and Gnew must additionally range over a fixed finite family before D varies.
Neither (SR1) nor (SR3) provides the strict bound M(Gnew)<m or that finiteness by itself.

### SR4. An existing cubic class already excludes a cyclotomic lift shortcut

Take m=2 and the period-three class

    D=1 modulo3,  (r_0,r_1,r_2)=(0,0,1).

The actual cubic comparison already retained in [C842 FG5](../claims/Yin_C_G0842_v1.md#fg5-a-fixed-finite-cubic-lift-set-with-mgm) can be chosen as

    G(x)=x^3+x-1,
    G*(x)=1+x^2-x^3.

The residue compatibility is r_0=r_1, and G(0) is odd.
Modulo2,

    G=x^3+x+1,    G*=x^3+x^2+1,

which are unequal.
Thus (SR4) prohibits ANY monic Gnew of degree3+q with Gnew congruent to QG modulo2, Q monic self-reciprocal of degree q, and M(Gnew)=1.
This applies to a real compatible comparison shape, not merely an unrelated polynomial chosen for the obstruction.

It does not challenge the existing small-Mahler construction:
Parseval/Jensen already gives M(x^3+x-1)<=sqrt(3)<2.
The point is that a universal reduction to cyclotomic measure1 lifts is strictly stronger than the needed M(Gnew)<m assertion and can fail even in an already covered short-period class.
The remaining arbitrary-period question is a strict small-measure modular-lift or different coverage theorem, with the fixed-family and actual-degree conditions still explicit.
No unrestricted Lehmer conclusion, new accepted Claim or novelty assertion is made here.
