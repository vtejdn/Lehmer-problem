# Complete trace-group images and the first square-saturation discriminator

## Question and complete finite scope

Put phi=X^2+X+1 and S_N=F_2[X]/(phi^N) for N in{8,16}.
Let G_N be the multiplicative subgroup generated by ALL complete primitive odd-order trace polynomials W_n with n>1 and n!=5.
Let E_N=<G_N,X>, retaining the endpoint factor separately.
The injective square map Fr:S_8^* -> S_16^* sends a polynomial class to its square.
The exact questions are whether X^2 belongs to G_16, and whether G_16 intersect Fr(S_8^*) equals Fr(G_8), with the same equality also tested for E.
These are statements about finite local images, not about global polynomial square roots, maximal common Frobenius powers, actual normalized gate realization or original integer fields.

## All-order reduction before arithmetic

Let B_n be the complete nonidentity n-th-root trace polynomial for odd n, B_1=1, B_3=X+1 and B_(n+2)=X B_n+B_(n-2).
The recurrence matrix A=[[X,1],[1,0]] satisfies A^5-I=phi[phi A+(X+1)I].
In characteristic two this gives A^80=I modulo phi^16, so B_(n+160)=B_n modulo phi^16.
For 5 not dividing n, these are units and the64 odd residue classes 1<=n<160 with 5 not dividing n exhaust their local image.
For 5 dividing n, the squarefree B_n has exactly one phi factor; define D_n=B_n/phi.
The identity A^160=I modulo phi^17 follows because its difference from I is divisible by phi^32.
Thus B_(n+320)=B_n modulo phi^17, and exact polynomial division gives D_(n+320)=D_n modulo phi^16.
The32 classes n=5,15,...,315 exhaust these units.
For every odd n, B_n=product_(d|n,d>1)W_d and D_n=product_(d|n,d>1,d!=5)W_d when 5 divides n.
Induction over divisors therefore proves that the subgroup generated by the specified96 classes is EXACTLY G_16, including every higher power of5 order.
Reduction of the same96 classes generates exactly G_8.
No arbitrary order or degree cutoff is used.

## Filtration algorithm and declared bounds

For each N, remove the residue-field part with t=(X+1)^N, which has exact order3 and is itself in G_N.
Principal units have residue1 modulo phi and the filtration 1+phi^j S_N has successive additive quotient F_4 for 1<=j<N.
Use the binary ordered basis 1,X of each quotient.
A pivot unit has a first nonzero bit in these2(N-1) ordered positions.
Insertion cancels an existing first pivot by its inverse, or installs a new pivot and queues its square exactly once.
Every cancellation moves strictly to a later position, and at most2(N-1) pivots can be installed.
After all original generators and all installed pivot squares reduce, the pivot normal forms are closed under multiplication and inversion: commuting pivot factors are reduced to binary exponents, and each carried square is already generated by later pivots.
The subgroup therefore has exactly3*2^p elements when p pivots remain, with membership decided by reduction to1.
Every pivot retains its exact exponent vector in the original96 generators, reduced modulo48, the exponent of S_16^*.
The endpoint-extended groups use the extra97th generator X.

There are exactly four subgroup runs, for G_8,G_16,E_8,E_16.
Each run has at most97+30 insertion events and at most30 cancellations per event.
The logarithmic derivative u'/u is additive, so binary elimination on the at most30 pivot images gives the exact rank and a kernel basis.
A unit of S_16 is a square exactly when its canonical polynomial has only even powers ofX; taking alternate coefficients gives its unique root in S_8.
The size of the square intersection is3*2^(p-rank), and Fr(G_8) has size |G_8|; the analogous formula holds for E.
If equality fails, the first kernel-basis word whose root fails membership is retained as an exact product witness; no extra search is permitted.
The first-priority target X^2 is checked separately.
All computations stop after these four groups and their exact square-kernel checks.
