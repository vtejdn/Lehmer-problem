### 2. A complete scalar-sign control

First choose any integer q>=2 and put

    F_q=P-a_0 q.                                        (3)

It is monic, primitive and of degree d.
At every root F_q(alpha_i)=-a_0 q, so its signed resultant is(-a_0 q)^d, and every p-adic valuation is exactly v_p(q).
Thus all prime incomes refer to this one actual polynomial.
Its complete norms are

    S_2(F_q)^2=q^2-2q+B,
    S_1(F_q)=q+L-2.                                    (4)

These follow by replacing the constant coefficient a_0 by a_0(1-q); every other coefficient is retained.
Therefore log q-log S_2(F_q)>0 if and only if q>B/2.
The plus-sign translate in L229 and this minus-sign translate have different constant-term energy; no old formula is corrected or discarded.

For a specific dyadic choice take q to be the least power of two at least B.
Then B<=q<2B, and

    rho(F_q)<=4,
    log q-log S_2(F_q)>1/(4B).                         (5)

For the first inequality, q+L-2<=2(q-1) and S_2(F_q)^2=(q-1)^2+B-1>=(q-1)^2.
For the second put x=(2q-B)/q^2.
It lies in(0,1), x>=1/q, and -log(1-x)>=x.
Hence the margin is at least1/(2q)>1/(4B).
This proves a primitive degree-d all-root deeper-floor construction with uniformly bounded rho on every input, but its guaranteed margin can tend to zero through B.
Bounded B would already imply a bounded-length gap by L12/Mig78, so this observation is not a new bounded-coefficient theorem.

<a id="monomial-projection-uniform-margin"></a>
### 3. Coefficient projection gives a uniform margin

Identify the real polynomials of degree at most2d-1 with R^(2d) by their full coefficient vectors, using the Euclidean inner product.
Let A be the2d-by-d integer matrix whose column k is the coefficient vector of t^k P, for0<=k<d.
These columns are independent: their highest nonzero degrees are d,d+1,...,2d-1, each with leading coefficient one.
Let V be their span, exactly the space of real P multiples in this degree window.
Its dimension is d.
The matrix

    G=A^T A,
    Pi=A G^(-1) A^T                                  (6)

is rational, symmetric and idempotent, since G is invertible.
Its image is V, and x-Pi x is orthogonal to every column of A.
These facts prove directly that Pi is the orthogonal projection onto V.
Also trace(Pi)=trace(G^(-1)A^T A)=d.

For each coefficient unit vector e_j,0<=j<2d, set

    v_j=(I-Pi)e_j,
    sigma_j^2=||v_j||_2^2=1-Pi_(j,j).

Because I-Pi is also symmetric idempotent,

    sum_(j=0)^(2d-1) sigma_j^2=trace(I-Pi)=d.          (7)

Choose j with sigma_j^2<=1/2.
It has sigma_j>0: otherwise t^j would be a real multiple of P, impossible because P has a nonzero root and t^j does not vanish there.
Write Pi e_j=A b, where b=G^(-1)A^T e_j is a rational vector of length d.
Thus the residual polynomial is t^j-P(t) sum_k b_k t^k.
This identity explains why its eventual value at alpha will be the unit monomial alpha^j rather than zero.

There is an exact simultaneous-prime form before restricting to one prime.
Choose a positive integer q clearing the denominators of b and put

    F=q t^j-P(t) sum_k(q b_k)t^k=q v_j.              (8)

This is an integral, nonzero polynomial of degree at most2d-1, and F(alpha_i)=q alpha_i^j at every root.
Consequently |Res(P,F)|=q^d and S_2(F)=q sigma_j<=q/sqrt2.
The scalar q can have several prime factors; all its valuations occur at every original root of this same F.
The content normalization below preserves the full energy margin.
Denominator size is not asserted bounded uniformly in P, and no cheap independent-prime representatives are combined.
Formula(8) already supplies a uniform margin (1/2)log2, but its modulus is not necessarily dyadic.

For a dyadic construction, choose q=2^s to be the least power of two satisfying

    q^2>=d^2 B,
    d sqrt B<=q<2d sqrt B.                            (9)

Round each q b_k to an integer u_k with |u_k-q b_k|<=1/2; ties may be chosen either way.
Define U(t)=sum_k u_k t^k and

    F=q t^j-P U=q v_j-E,
    E=A(u-qb) in V.                                  (10)

The residual q v_j lies in V's orthogonal complement, so the two terms in(10) are exactly orthogonal.
Every column of A has Euclidean norm sqrt B.
The triangle inequality therefore gives ||E||_2<=sum_k |u_k-q b_k|sqrt B<=d sqrt B/2<=q/2.
It follows that

    ||F||_2^2=q^2 sigma_j^2+||E||_2^2
             <=q^2/2+q^2/4=3q^2/4.                 (11)

This is the complete rounding cost; no cancellation between orthogonal components is assumed.
The construction is finite rational linear algebra and integer rounding.
No search cap, floating approximation, asymptotic denominator argument or unproved availability of a short lattice vector is used.
Unlike choosing an arbitrary short vector in V, it cannot return a zero resultant, since its value at every root is exactly q alpha_i^j.

### 4. Actual order, primitive normalization and every root

Both constructions(8) and(10) have an identity F=q t^j-P U with U integral.
Let c>=1 be the coefficient content of F and put F_0=F/c.
Then c divides q.
Indeed evaluation gives q alpha^j=c F_0(alpha), and alpha^(-j) belongs to the actual order O because alpha is a unit.
Thus q/c=F_0(alpha)alpha^(-j) belongs to O.
The rational elements of this integral order are integers: in the integral power basis1,alpha,...,alpha^(d-1), a rational element has only its constant coordinate, which must be integral.
Hence q/c is an integer, as claimed.
No passage to a maximal order or assumption on its index is needed.

Put Q=q/c.
At every original root,

    F_0(alpha_i)=Q alpha_i^j,
    Res(P,F_0)=Q^d [(-1)^d a_0]^j,
    v_p(F_0(alpha_i))=v_p(Q) for every prime p.       (12)

Each alpha_i and its inverse is p-adically integral, so it has valuation zero; this proves the individual equalities before using the norm.
Since F_0-Q t^j is integral and vanishes at alpha, monic division by P supplies an integral quotient.
Consequently F_0 belongs to the actual ideal(P,Q), not merely an integral-closure class.
Content removal preserves rho and the exact net margin:

    log Q-log S_2(F_0)=log q-log S_2(F).              (13)

No discarded scalar contributes income after division.

For(8), S_2(F_0)<=Q/sqrt2; for(10), S_2(F_0)<=sqrt3 Q/2.
In either case Q>=2, because Q=1 would make the norm of a nonzero integer coefficient vector strictly less than one.
In the dyadic case c divides2^s, so Q=2^r with an integer1<=r<=s.
This proves the precise depth and complete arithmetic assertions in(2).
The nonzero full resultant, including its possible sign in(12), follows without choosing an expanding root or deleting a factor of F_0.

F_0 is nonconstant.
If j=0, a constant F_0 must equal Q by(12), contradicting S_2(F_0)<Q.
If j>0 and F_0 were constant, then alpha^j would be rational.
As a rational algebraic unit it would be+/-1, contradicting nontorsion.
The degree T of F_0 is therefore between1 and2d-1, including any actual leading-coefficient cancellations.
Finally Cauchy--Schwarz over its at most2d coefficient positions gives rho(F_0)<=2d.
This proves(2) with the entire original degree, all prime/root multiplicities, primitive content and effective support accounted for.
The scalar q in(9), which is before normalization, has the explicit bound q<2d sqrt B; no uniform bound on B or the retained depth r is inferred.

### 5. What the construction forces near one

The resulting universal auxiliary does not meet every hypothesis of L229's sufficient gap criterion.
It meets nonvanishing, uniform positive net margin and T/d<2 on every input, but only has rho<=2d, rather than a uniform rho bound or rho epsilon(Tm/d)->0.
This is a concrete distinction, not an unproved objection to auxiliary existence.

For the dyadic construction let T=deg F_0, h=Tm/d and rho=rho(F_0).
At0<2m<=h_0, apply L229 to this same complete primitive polynomial.
Equations(2),(12),(13) and h<=2m give

    eta_0<=h+(1/2)log[1+(rho-1)epsilon(h)+4rho h]
          <=2m+(1/2)log[1+(rho-1)epsilon(2m)+8rho m]. (14)

All dyadic income here is exactly log|Res(P,F_0)|; no complementary prime or root is missing.
Rearranging gives the explicit necessary inequality

    rho >= [(4/3)exp(-4m)-1+epsilon(2m)]
                          /[epsilon(2m)+8m].         (15)

The denominator is positive.
In particular when additionally m<=log(16/15)/4, the numerator is at least1/4, so

    rho >=1/[4(epsilon(2m)+8m)].                      (16)

L229's retained epsilon(u)=O(u^(1/4)sqrt(1+log(1/u))) implies that along any hypothetical sequence of positive total measures tending to zero, every dyadic output constructed above has

    rho >= constant * m^(-1/4)/sqrt(1+log(1/m))

for all sufficiently small m, with a fixed positive constant.
The same inequality applies to any other nonvanishing integral auxiliary having the margin eta_0 and T<=2d, not just the rounding choices used here.
The stronger exact-rational margin from(8) can be inserted in the same formula with4/3 replaced by2.
No limiting degree is silently held fixed, and no near-one sequence is assumed to exist.

This states a necessary loss in effective coefficient support for actual universal higher-income representatives.
The weak resulting lower bound on d from rho<=2d is not advertised as an improvement on known degree-dependent measure bounds.
Likewise, the scalar construction(5) alone gives a bound involving B through L229, weaker than the already retained Mig78 bounded-length statement since L<=B.
The new useful income/expense separation is that one cannot dismiss the whole high-depth method for lack of a nonzero linear-degree auxiliary or a fixed positive coefficient-energy margin: both exist here.
The unresolved part is the actual joint-conjugate error, or an alternative analytic estimate that can handle these growing-support, input-adapted coefficients.
