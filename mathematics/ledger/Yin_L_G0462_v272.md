# Signed boundary evaluations at arbitrary integer exponents

<a id="rate"></a>
## The cost of prescribed contacts

Let \(P_i\) be a fixed finite nonempty list of distinct monic reciprocal irreducible polynomials of nontorsion algebraic units, put \(h_i=\log M(P_i)\), and take integers \(t_i\ge1\).
Write \(F=\prod_i P_i^{t_i}\) and \(W=\sum_i t_i h_i\).
Choose one root from each inverse pair of the combined root set.
On an infinite set of positive integers \(n\), assume that the \(n\)-th powers of all distinct roots remain distinct.
For each such \(n\) suppose there is a sign \(\sigma_n\) such that every multiplicatively dependent pair \(x,y\) among the chosen roots admits a relation \(x^u y^v=\eta\) with coprime nonzero integers \(u,v\), a root of unity \(\eta\), and
\[
                              \eta^n\ne\sigma_n^{u+v}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The choice of orientation for an inverse pair does not affect (1), since inversion of an exponent preserves its parity.

For either prescribed sign \(\varepsilon\), take integral polynomials \(A\) of degree at most \(b=b(n)\) with
\[
 F\mid A(X^n)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^n),\qquad
 A_b^*(Y)=Y^b A(1/Y),\qquad A(\alpha^n)\ne0\quad(F(\alpha)=0).
\]
If \(b\to\infty\) and \(b=o(n)\), their least one-norm \(H_n\) exists for all sufficiently large \(n\) in the indicated set and satisfies
\[
                              \frac{\log H_n}{n}\longrightarrow W/2.           \tag{2}
\]
For each positive excess over \(W/2\), a single fixed width attains the corresponding upper bound throughout the same tail.
The coordinating research agent for Sheng Yin derived this arbitrary-exponent extension of the [prime-exponent result](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#statement) for the [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-19.

## Primes dividing the exponent

Fix a number field \(L\) of degree \(d\) and a nontorsion unit \(x\) in it.
For \(m=2n\), the sum of \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^m-1)\log N\mathfrak l\) over primes of \(L\) above rational primes dividing \(m\), or above any additional fixed finite set, is \(o_{L,x}(n)\).
At a fixed prime above \(\ell\), write \(a\) for the residue order of \(x\).
There is no contribution unless \(a\mid m\).
For the normalized valuation \(v(\ell)=1\), raising \(1+z\) to an \(\ell\)-th power increases \(v(z)>0\) by at least \(\min(1,(\ell-1)v(z))\).
After finitely many such steps \(v(z)>1/(\ell-1)\), when every subsequent step increases it by exactly one, by the binomial formula.
Powers prime to \(\ell\) preserve this valuation, and \(x\) is nontorsion.
Thus each fixed prime, including all ramified primes and those above two, contributes \(O_{L,x,\ell}(\log(2m))\).

For any remaining odd \(\ell\mid m\), at a contributing unramified prime \(\mathfrak l\) of residue degree \(f\), one has
\[
 a\mid m/\ell^{v_\ell(m)},\qquad a\mid \ell^f-1,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^m-1)=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^a-1)+v_\ell(m).
\]
The first term times \(\log N\mathfrak l\) is at most \(\log|N_{L/\mathbb Q}(x^a-1)|\le C_{L,x}(1+a)\), by the product over embeddings.
All lifting terms together cost at most \(d\log m\).
For \(\ell\le m^{1/(2d)}\), one has \(a\le\sqrt m\) and there are at most \(\log_2 m\) such rational prime divisors.
For larger \(\ell\) there are at most \(2d\) divisors, each with \(a\le m/\ell\le m^{1-1/(2d)}\).
There are at most \(d\) primes of \(L\) over each \(\ell\), proving the asserted \(o(n)\).
The same bound applies to \(x^n-\sigma_n\), a divisor of \(x^{2n}-1\).

## Simultaneous evaluation on all signed roots

Put \(R_n=|\operatorname{Res}(F,X^n-\sigma_n)|\).
The [signed resultant estimate (2)](Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) gives \(\log R_n=nW+O_F(\log(2n))\).
Work in a fixed Galois splitting field \(L\) of \(F\).
Discard the rational primes dividing \(2n F(1)F(-1)\) and those ramified in \(L\).
At every remaining \(\ell\) define \(v_\ell(B_n)=\max_{z^n=\sigma_n} v_\ell(F(z))\), taking all nodes in an algebraic closure of the local field and normalizing \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Use exponent zero at the discarded primes.
All these nodes lie in an unramified extension and have distinct reductions, since \(\ell\nmid 2n\).
The maxima are nonnegative integers with finite support by the resultant product, so \(B_n\) is a positive integer.
If a maximum is positive, a maximizing node and its inverse have distinct reductions since \(F(1)F(-1)\) is a unit at \(\ell\).
Reciprocity gives them equal valuations.
Consequently \(2\log B_n\le\log R_n\).

At a prime \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\) set \(w_x=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^n-\sigma_n)\).
For the chosen roots use multiplicities \(t_x=t_i\) from \(P_i\).
The sum of \(\sum_x t_x w_x\) over all primes with weights \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\) equals \(\tfrac12\log R_n\), since \(x^{-n}-\sigma_n\) differs from \(x^n-\sigma_n\) by a unit.
At retained primes each positive \(w_x\) comes from exactly one factor \(x-z\) with \(z^n=\sigma_n\), by distinct reduction.
At that node \(F(z)\) contains \((z-x)^{t_x}\), giving \(e_{\mathfrak l}v_\ell(B_n)\ge\max_x t_xw_x\), where \(e_{\mathfrak l}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\ell\).
Summing the left side with the indicated weights gives \(\log B_n\) by the local degree formula.

The discarded weights cost \(o_F(n)\) by the preceding estimate.
The excess of a sum of nonnegative terms over their maximum is at most the sum of their pairwise minima.
For an independent pair, [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd) applied to the ordinary units \(\sigma_n x^n,\sigma_n y^n\) bounds the weighted sum of \(\min(w_x,w_y)\) by \(o_F(n)\).
Indeed these are distinct pairs as \(n\) varies, their heights are \(O_F(n)\), and independence modulo torsion excludes every exceptional dependent pair.
For a dependent pair the ideal generated by \(x^n-\sigma_n,y^n-\sigma_n\) contains \(\eta^n-\sigma_n^{u+v}\), because the underlying numbers are units.
By (1) these are nonzero elements of a fixed finite set, so the weighted sum of the minima is bounded.
Multiplicities cost at most the fixed factor \(\max_i t_i\).
It follows that \(\log B_n=nW/2+o_F(n)\), also for every fixed subproduct using the same signs.

For any nonzero integral graph \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\) containing \(F\), divide the two rows by their maximal common power \((Y-\sigma_n)^j\).
The quotient graph still contains \(F\), since all roots of \(F\) are nontorsion.
At a maximizing pair \(z,z^{-1}\) for each positive \(v_\ell(B_n)\), the two values of the quotient graph vanish modulo \(\ell^{v_\ell(B_n)}\).
Their determinant \(z^{-1}-z\) is a unit.
Thus \(B_n\) divides both quotient rows evaluated at \(\sigma_n\), at least one of which is nonzero.
These integers are the order-\(j\) divided derivatives of the original rows there.
With \(H_*=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) and degrees at most \(b\), their magnitudes are at most \(\binom bj H_*\), giving \(\log H_*\ge nW/2-o(n)\) when \(b=o(n)\).

For the upper bound the [integral jet count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) applies at every integer exponent \(n\ge2\) with the prescribed multiplicities, counting the value alone when \(t_i=1\).
At width \(b_0=b-1\) it gives a two-row graph of log one-norm at most \(nW/2+o(n)\), or at a sufficiently large fixed width any prescribed positive excess after dividing by \(n\).
Injectivity of powering on the combined root set gives pairwise distinct powered minima \(Q_i\) of measures \(\log M(Q_i)=nh_i\).
If both rows contain \(Q_i\), division by it leaves a graph containing \(F/P_i\), since \(Q_i(X^n)\) has simple roots and vanishes on no other original orbit.
The previous lower bound gives a quotient row of log one-norm at least \(n(W-h_i)/2-o(n)\), also for an empty remaining list by integrality.
Its log measure is at least this large minus \(b_0\log 2\), by the elementary symmetric coefficient bound.
Restoring \(Q_i\) then costs at least \(n(W+h_i)/2-o(n)\) in log one-norm, a contradiction, taking the fixed excess below \(\min_i h_i/2\) when necessary.
Vanishing of either row at one powered root would force both to contain its minimum by the graph equation.
The [signed projections](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#attainment) now retain all factors and nonzero numerator values at either prescribed \(\varepsilon\), at one additional unit of width and a fixed norm multiplier.
This proves (2).

## A two-value exclusion on any infinite set of exponents

Put \(B=(\log 2)/4\) and use the [fixed-double-factor count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement).
For a positive irreducible log measure \(h\) and integer \(r\ge1\) satisfying \(2(2^r-1)h<B\), take an [all-power representative](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) \(\delta\) of measure \(h\).
The minima of \(\delta^{2^j}\) for \(0\le j<r\) are reciprocal by [Smyth's bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), and have distinct measures \(2^jh\).
All powers of each retain their degree, and the distinct heights of the different orbits exclude collisions between orbits.
Within the orbit of \(\delta\), dependent conjugates must have quotient or product torsion by equality of absolute heights.
The all-power property excludes a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates, so dependence only connects a root with itself or its inverse.
It follows that between dyadic orbits every dependence, after orientation, matches a higher power to an even power of the same conjugate.
Its primitive relation has \(\eta=1\) and odd exponent sum.
Thus \(\sigma_n=-1\) works at every integer \(n\).
With \(t_i=2\) one has \(W=2(2^r-1)h<B\), so choosing a fixed-width excess smaller than \((B-W)/2\) gives the strict passing budget \(((2nb+1)\|A\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) for every large \(n\).
Multiplying the fixed row by \(Y^k(Y+1)^e\) for \(e=0,1\) pads it to any width tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\), at a cost of at most a factor two.
Consequently, if fewer than two values occur at one prescribed sign on any infinite subsequence of such widths, the unrestricted infimum \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\lambda\ge B/6=(\log 2)/24\).
Indeed otherwise a small positive irreducible log measure \(h<B/6\) exists by factorization, and \(r=2\) contradicts the exclusion.
In particular a subsequence of powers of two suffices if the required exclusion can be proved there.

## Arbitrary pairs at odd exponents

Any two distinct positive irreducible measures \(h_1,h_2\) with \(h_1+h_2<B/2\) occur on one passing double-factor row, with either prescribed graph sign, for every sufficiently large odd \(n\).
Use their reciprocal all-power representatives.
Within each orbit the preceding independence argument applies.
A torsion quotient across orbits would identify two powered conjugates of full degree and equal absolute height, contradicting the unequal measures.
When the orbits are dependent, the [matching of conjugates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v425.md#two) gives, after orientation, a common primitive slope \(u/v>0\) and conjugate phases \(\eta=y^v/x^u\).
If their order is odd, negate an entire orbit whose exponent is odd to make every phase \(-\eta\) have even order.
The all-power property is preserved since equality of two powered conjugates is unchanged, and reciprocity and measures are preserved.
Now \(\eta^n\ne1\) for all odd \(n\), so \(\sigma_n=1\) works.
If \(u,v\) have opposite parity one may even use every \(n\), choosing \(\sigma_n=-1\) when \(\eta^n=1\) and \(+1\) otherwise.
The choice works simultaneously because conjugates of a phase have \(n\)-th power one together.
For independent orbits either sign works at every exponent.
Taking \(t_1=t_2=2\) in (2) and using the fixed slack and padding above proves the assertion.
All convergence statements keep the input algebraic numbers fixed as \(n\) increases.
