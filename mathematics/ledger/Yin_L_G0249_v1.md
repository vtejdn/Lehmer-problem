### 2. Reflection, actual interpolation and the missing coefficient band

Let P be a monic irreducible reciprocal nontorsion polynomial in Z[t], with constant term1 and degree d=2s.
Let alpha_1,...,alpha_d be all its original roots.
It has no roots1 or-1.
Write

    P(t)=t^s Q(t+t^(-1)),
    Q in Z[x] monic, deg Q=s.                         (1)

This follows by expressing reciprocal pairs of monomials through C_0(x)=2, C_1(x)=x, C_(k+1)=x C_k-C_(k-1), so C_k(t+t^(-1))=t^k+t^(-k).
The original roots of P are simple, so Q has simple roots and none at+/-2.
Let r be the number of roots of Q in(-2,2), equivalently the number of neutral conjugate pairs of P.
The accepted [Smy08 measure-one/Kronecker interface](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization), including its p.322 precision guard, gives r<=s-1 for a nontorsion P; otherwise every root of the monic P would be on the unit circle.
Its original-source proof is not newly audited here.
Q may also have nonreal roots; it is not assumed totally real or Salem.
The argument below retains all such roots.

Choose an integer j>=s, let N=2j+1, and use the real Euclidean coefficient space of all polynomials of degree at most2j.
Its inner product is sum of products of corresponding coefficients.
Define

    V_j={P U: U in R[t], deg U<=2j-d},
    R_j(t)=t^j-Proj_(V_j)(t^j),
    l_j=||R_j||_2^2.                                 (2)

This is the same actual relation-space projection used by the retained work, at the specified centered window.
Its Gram matrix is rational and nonsingular, so R_j has rational coefficients.
R_j is nonzero because it takes the exact nonzero values

    R_j(alpha_i)=alpha_i^j   for every original i.

The orthogonal decomposition also gives l_j=(R_j)_j, its central coefficient, and0<l_j<=1.

Reversal of2j+1 coefficients is an orthogonal involution.
Since P is reciprocal, it preserves V_j and fixes t^j; it therefore fixes R_j.
Consequently there is a real rational polynomial L_j of degree at most j such that

    R_j(t)=t^j L_j(t+t^(-1)),
    L_j(x_i)=1 for every root x_i of Q.                (3)

In particular L_j and Q have no common root.
Reflection here is with respect to the entire declared window, even if the outer coefficients of R_j vanish.
It does not assert that the actual degree is2j.

There is also an exact coefficient-boundary identity.
For k=d,...,2j, the coefficient of t^k in P(t)R_j(t) equals the inner product of R_j with t^(k-d)P, because P is reciprocal.
Every one of these coefficients vanishes by(2).
Thus

    P(t)R_j(t)=A_j(t)+t^N B_j(t),
    deg A_j,deg B_j<=d-1,
    B_j(t)=t^(d-1) A_j(t^(-1)).                      (4)

The last equality uses formal reversal of total degree2j+d=N+d-1 and remains valid when outer coefficients vanish.
In particular the FULL coefficient product has an empty band from degree d through2j.
This identity concerns one specific optimal column, not an arbitrary small-energy representative or a deletion of middle coefficients after evaluation.

For j>=d-1, l_j<1.
Otherwise the projection vanishes and t^j is perpendicular to all the displayed shifts of P.
At j=d-1 those inner products see every interior coefficient p_1,...,p_(d-1); at any j>=d one of them sees p_0=1.
The interior coefficients cannot all vanish, since then P=t^d+1 would be torsion.
This establishes strictness on the range used for the root-count corollary, without asserting a uniform distance of l_j from one.

### 3. Signed orthogonality forces many circle zeros

Let nu be the probability measure on(-2,2) with density

    d nu(x)=dx/[pi sqrt(4-x^2)].

The substitution x=2cos(theta) identifies its polynomial integrals with circle averages of the corresponding symmetric Laurent polynomials.
For every real polynomial V of degree at most j-s, the polynomial

    P(t) t^(j-s) V(t+t^(-1))

belongs to V_j: its multiplier is a genuine polynomial of degree at most2(j-s).
Taking its inner product with R_j and removing the common central phase on the unit circle gives the EXACT signed relation

    integral L_j(x) Q(x) V(x) d nu(x)=0,
                            deg V<=j-s.             (5)

No positive-root reweighting is present; Q changes sign whenever it has a neutral trace root.
The equalities in(3) mean those sign changes cannot be canceled by a zero of L_j at the same point.

Here is the full finite sign argument.
If a nonzero real polynomial W is orthogonal under nu to every polynomial of degree at most K, then W has at least K+1 sign-changing zeros in(-2,2).
If it had only h<=K, let V be the product of the h corresponding linear factors.
W V has one constant sign throughout the interval except at its finitely many zeros and is not identically zero.
Positivity of the density gives a nonzero integral, contradicting orthogonality.
Even-multiplicity zeros and zeros outside the interval change no sign; neither endpoint is needed.
All integrals are finite because the interval is bounded and the density integrable.

Apply this to W=L_j Q and K=j-s.
The polynomial Q has precisely r simple sign-changing zeros in the interval; by(3) none are roots of L_j.
All other sign changes must therefore be distinct odd-multiplicity zeros of L_j.
Hence L_j has at least

    k_j=max{0,j-s+1-r}                               (6)

distinct sign-changing zeros in(-2,2).
Each gives a pair of distinct unit-circle roots t=e^(+/-i theta) of R_j, with the same multiplicity as that trace zero.
These roots are not roots of P.
In particular for j>=d-1 one has k_j>=s-r>=1: the normalized circle profile t^(-j)R_j(t) actually takes both positive and negative values, despite its value1 at EVERY original neutral trace root.
A proposed nonnegative-profile shortcut for these actual columns is false on the whole stated range.

Let b be the order of vanishing of R_j at zero.
Reflection then makes its actual degree2j-b, and

    R_j(t)=t^b G_j(t),
    G_j(0)!=0, deg G_j=2(j-b), G_j reciprocal.

The degree of L_j is j-b.
Equation(6) gives j-b>=k_j, and hence b<=s+r-1 when k_j>0.
When k_j>0, after removing only this monomial, the number of remaining roots OFF the unit circle, counted with multiplicity, is at most

    2(j-b)-2k_j=2(s+r-1-b)<=2(s+r-1).               (7)

Additional circle zeros only improve this bound; they are not presumed simple in general.
Thus the possible off-circle count is independent of the enlarged centered window at each fixed original P.
For Salem degree2s, r=s-1 and the upper bound is4s-4=2d-4.
For general reciprocal units it retains the actual r.
Equation(7) is not a bound on the modulus of those exceptional roots or on the leading coefficient, and its dependence on d remains substantial at a linear window.

### 4. Primitive normalization and the whole cost

Take j>=d-1.
Choose a positive integer D clearing the coefficients of R_j, let c be the positive gcd of all coefficients of D R_j, and put

    F_j=(D/c)R_j,       q=D/c.

The polynomial F_j is primitive and integral.
Since F_j(alpha)=q alpha^j and alpha is an algebraic unit, q=F_j(alpha)alpha^(-j) is a rational algebraic integer.
Thus q is a positive INTEGER; content division does not leave an uncharged rational scalar.
The exact identities are

    F_j(alpha_i)=q alpha_i^j  for every i,
    |Res(P,F_j)|=q^d,
    S_2(F_j)^2=q^2 l_j,
    log q-log S_2(F_j)=-(1/2)log l_j>0.              (8)

Here q>=2, since q=1 would give a nonzero integral polynomial with S_2<1.
F_j is not constant or a monomial: the projection and(3),(6) already give nonzero circle roots.
Its full actual degree is2j-b.
Monic division also gives F_j-q t^j in P Z[t], because this integral difference vanishes at alpha; the congruence therefore holds in the actual order.
For every rational prime p, every original p-adic root has exactly v_p(q) valuation in F_j(alpha_i), since alpha_i is a unit.
All roots, powers and primes in this one norm are retained.
There is no dyadic-denominator claim, and no guarantee that the selected center enjoys the uniform margin of L237's separately selected column.

Write F_j=t^b H_j with H_j primitive reciprocal of degree2(j-b) and nonzero leading/constant coefficient a_j.
Its Mahler measure is

    m(F_j)=m(H_j)
      =log|a_j|+sum_(H_j(zeta)=0) log^+|zeta|,       (9)

with multiplicity.
Equation(7) bounds only the number of positive summands in the last sum.
In particular it does not permit dropping log|a_j|, or treating all circle-root factors as integral monic factors.
The complete norm can equivalently be written

    |Res(P,F_j)|=|a_j|^d
                       product_(H_j(zeta)=0)|P(zeta)|.

The omitted monomial contributes |P(0)|^b=1.
No subset of these circle evaluations is separately presumed rational or an integer norm.

Parseval and the integral arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give the familiar bound m(F_j)<=log S_2(F_j).
The edge identity(4), scaled to F_j, also gives integer polynomials A,B with deg A,deg B<=d-1 and

    P F_j=A+t^N B,  B=t^(d-1) A(t^(-1)),
    ||P F_j||_2^2=2||A||_2^2,
    m(P)+m(F_j)<=log2+m(A).                          (10)

For the last line, |A+t^N B|<=2|A| on the circle and logarithmic integration suffices; A is nonzero.
All factors and any zeros at zero remain in the actual products.
A need not be monic, a unit seed, coprime to its reciprocal or exterior-separated.
Consequently L139's uniform seed theorem cannot be applied to(10) without genuinely proving its missing hypotheses.
This is a precise signed-column/edge relation, not a new universal delayed-seed coverage argument.

### 5. An exact actual-unit control: every auxiliary root is on the circle

Fix an integer a>=3 and take the actual reciprocal unit input

    P_a(t)=t^2-a t+1,
    alpha=(a+sqrt(a^2-4))/2>1.

The discriminant a^2-4 lies strictly between(a-1)^2 and a^2, so P_a is irreducible over Q.
Its roots are alpha and alpha^(-1), and its total measure is the FIXED positive log alpha.
Here s=1,r=0.
For every j>=1 define integer C_k=C_k(a)=alpha^k+alpha^(-k), using C_0=2,C_1=a and the recurrence from(1).
Put

    H=2+sum_(k=1)^j C_k^2,
    g=gcd(2,a) in{1,2},
    F_j(t)=[2t^j+sum_(k=1)^j C_k(t^(j+k)+t^(j-k))]/g,
    q=H/g.                                         (11)

These are exactly the primitive centered residual auxiliaries, not merely other polynomials with similar roots.
To verify this, a symmetric real coefficient polynomial has the form t^j(c_0+sum_(k=1)^j c_k(t^k+t^(-k))).
Its norm squared is c_0^2+2sum c_k^2.
Prescribing its value alpha^j at alpha gives c_0+sum c_k C_k=1; reciprocity gives the correct value at alpha^(-1) as well.
The unique minimum of that quadratic norm under the linear condition, by Cauchy--Schwarz with its equality condition, is attained at

    c_0=2/H,       c_k=C_k/H,
    l_j=2/H.                                       (12)

Among all nonsymmetric feasible polynomials, averaging with reversal preserves feasibility and cannot increase the norm.
The actual residual is the unique minimum-norm polynomial with those two values, so it is precisely(12).
This is the single-column instance of the retained projection principle, applied here to compute an exact control.

The coefficient gcd of the numerator in(11) is exactly g: it divides both2 and C_1=a, and the recurrence makes every C_k even when a is even.
Thus(11) is primitive, q is integral, its full degree is2j, and its leading/constant coefficient is C_j/g.
By(6), its trace polynomial has at least j sign-changing zeros in(-2,2).
Its degree is exactly j, so these are all its zeros and are simple.
EVERY one of the2j roots of F_j is therefore simple and on the unit circle.
The full exact quantities are

    M(F_j)=C_j/g,
    Res(P_a,F_j)=q^2,
    S_2(F_j)^2=2H/g^2,
    log q-log S_2(F_j)=(1/2)log(H/2).                (13)

The ordinary orthogonal-polynomial explanation is consistent with the checked Nat26 scope: L_j is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree at most j-1 for the POSITIVE weight(a-x)d nu(x).
The local sign proof already establishes the conclusion, so no unexamined source theorem is needed.

For example a=3,j=1 gives the exact primitive F_1=3t^2+2t+3, q=11, l_1=2/11, M(F_1)=3, S_2(F_1)^2=22 and Res(P_3,F_1)=121.
These roots are not roots of unity: the primitive quadratic is irreducible by its negative discriminant, and a root of unity would have monic integral minimal polynomial, contrary to this irreducible primitive nonmonic polynomial.
Thus even an auxiliary whose ENTIRE root set is on the circle can carry positive Mahler cost in its leading coefficient.

As j tends to infinity at this fixed input, C_j=alpha^j+alpha^(-j) gives

    m(F_j)/j ->log alpha,
    (log q)/j ->2log alpha,
    [log q-log S_2(F_j)]/j ->log alpha.              (14)

For the middle limit use C_j^2<=H and H<=2+4sum_(k=1)^j alpha^(2k), both with fixed alpha>1; the other limits follow from(13).
The original degree remains2 and the auxiliary degree is2j, so this control is not a degree-linear near-one family.
It decisively prevents treating the circle-root count in(7) as a bound for the full Mahler cost or as a collection of free cyclotomic factors.
