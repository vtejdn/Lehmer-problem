# Reciprocal inputs and simultaneous disk interpolation for two rows

<a id="reflection"></a>
## The reciprocal branch of the cofactor test

Let \(P\) be a reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial, \(h=m(P)\), and \(p\) an odd prime.
Suppose a primitive integral Laurent polynomial \(F=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p)\), \(0<r<p\), has two nonzero coprime Laurent rows and contains \(P^e\), \(e\ge1\).
If \(F\) is not reciprocal up to sign and monomial, the [bound for the whole reciprocal gcd](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v359.md#statement) applies to \(G=\gcd(F(x),F(1/x))\).
Since \(P^e\mid G\), it gives
\[
                          m(F)\ge ep h/2 .                                \tag{1}
\]
In particular every such double-root witness of any span has noncyclotomic cofactor \(R\) with \(m(R)/p\ge(1-2/p)h\).
This involves the sum \(h\) across the exterior conjugates, even when the [largest conjugate displacement](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v382.md#house) accounts for a small part of it.

Thus on a reciprocal input, a value strictly below \(ph\) for \(m(F)\) at multiplicity two forces the witness itself to be reciprocal.

## A finite matrix test for the simultaneous interior jets

Use the [lowest-exponent orientation and disk normalization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v382.md#jet), now with \(P^2\mid F\) for any nontorsion unit minimal polynomial \(P\).
Let \(a_i\) run over its open-disk roots, and let \(\delta\) be the largest \(|H(0)|\) among functions in the unit ball of the Hardy space of series supported on \(0,r\bmod p\) satisfying \(H(a_i)=H'(a_i)=0\) for all \(i\).
Here the squared norm is the sum of squared absolute Taylor coefficients.
With
\[
 K(z,w)=\frac{1+(z\bar w)^r}{1-(z\bar w)^p},\qquad
 G_{(i,j),(l,t)}
 =\left.\partial_z^j\partial_{\bar w}^t K(z,w)\right|_{z=a_i,w=a_l},
 \qquad c_{(i,j)}=\mathbf1_{j=0}\quad(j,t=0,1),
\]
one has
\[
       \delta^2=1-c^*G^{-1}c,\qquad
       I\ge-\log\delta-\tfrac12\log2,\qquad
       -\log\delta\le2ph .                                              \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(K\) is the sum of the kernels of the individual monomials in the allowed residues.
Its differentiated columns represent the jet evaluations by termwise differentiation of the convergent series.
The \(a_i^p\) are distinct by the [coprime-row fiber argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#determinant).
Since \(pa_i^{p-1}\ne0\), Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial in \(z^p\) with arbitrary first jets at the \(a_i\), proving independence of the functionals.
The orthogonal projection of the constant \(1\) onto the span of these columns has squared norm \(c^*G^{-1}c\), by the normal equations.
The length of its residual is exactly the norm of evaluation at zero on the common jet kernel, proving the first assertion.
The actual disk-normalized \(H=u(x^p)+x^rv(x^p)\) has squared norm at most two by orthogonality of its two supports and \(|u|,|v|\le1\) on the disk.
Its constant has modulus \(|U(0)|e^{-I}\ge e^{-I}\), proving the lower bound on \(I\).
Finally the function
\[
                   \prod_i\left(\frac{z^p-a_i^p}{1-\overline{a_i^p}z^p}\right)^2
\]
belongs to the jet kernel, has norm one by its boundary modulus, and has constant modulus \(\prod_i|a_i|^{2p}=e^{-2ph}\) by the unit condition.
This proves the last bound and in particular \(\delta>0\).
The matrix depends on angles as well as moduli and needs no unknown witness coefficients.
For prescribed residues before lowest-term translation, the actual orientation can give either \(r\) or \(p-r\), so a bound uniform in the witness may use the weaker of the two tests.
Prescribing double zeros at any additional finite set \(T\) on the unit circle leaves this Hardy-space supremum equal to \(\delta\), even among functions holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk.
Indeed the normalized residual realizing \(\delta\) is rational with poles strictly outside the closed disk, by the formula for \(K\).
Multiply it by
\[
             L_N(z)=\prod_{a\in T}
               \left[1-\left(\frac{1+\overline{a^p}z^p}{2}\right)^N\right]^2 .
\]
The product supplies those boundary jets, preserves the two supports and the interior jets, and has \(L_N(0)\to1\).
On the unit circle it is bounded by \(4^{|T|}\) and converges to one except at finitely many points, so the norms of the modified extremizers tend to one by dominated convergence.
Renormalizing proves the assertion.
This relaxation allows arbitrary degree and scalar coefficients.
Integrality, row coprimality and exterior jets can impose further constraints on actual witnesses.

## A single marked double root has optimal displacement coefficient one

For any fixed integer \(n\ge2\), any \(p\ge2\) and \(1\le r<p\), the trinomial
\[
             (p-r)-p n^r x^r+r n^p x^p
\]
has a zero of multiplicity exactly two at \(1/n\).
Its value and first Euler derivative vanish there, while its second Euler derivative equals \(pr(p-r)\).
The second row is a nonzero constant, so the rows are coprime in the rational Laurent ring.
Divide by scalar content for a primitive witness.
The two original row suprema on the circle are at most \(2pn^p\), and removing content can only decrease the row integral.
The [single-root lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v382.md#house) therefore sandwiches its primitive row integral between \(p\log n-\log(8p)\) and \(p\log n+\log(2p)\), uniformly in \(r\).
This establishes the optimal leading coefficient using only a single double root, an integral endpoint coefficient and two coprime rows.
The marked root here is a rational nonunit.
A sharp upper witness for a specified unit minimal polynomial must meet the jets of all its conjugates.
