# Integer endpoint and two-moment exclusions on the full three-power grid

For Yin's [G0001 investigation](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#decision-resume-small-shell-2026-11-08), a delegated agent derived and self-checked the following cuts on 2026-11-08.
They use the fixed power-of-two resultant and the two [necessary moment targets](Yin_L_G0464_v7.md#prefix-cut) before whole-measure computation.

<a id="endpoint-moment-test"></a>
## Two positive sums and the integral endpoint

Take the [full odd grid](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#three-power-cost) at \(p=3^m\), with \(n=(p-1)/2\), decreasing poles \(b_j\), and \(r_j=(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)/p\) for integral \(h\).
Assume the last \(k\) residues are negative, the others positive, and take any \(y\) in the open transition gap.
Put \(K=\sum_jr_j=x^2+2\), \(L=\sum_jb_jr_j=-4x\), and \(a=2x\) for \(x\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\).
For \(R=Q/B\) one has \(R(-2)=-2-2x-U\), where \(U=\sum_j r_j/(2+b_j)\) is integral since \(|B(-2)|=1\).
The strict endpoint condition \(R(-2)<0\) therefore gives \(U\ge-1-2x\).
Two positive sums are
\[
 T=L-yK=\frac1p\sum_j(4-b_j^2)(b_j-y)h(b_j),\qquad
 J=(4+y)K-L-4(2+y)U=\frac1p\sum_j(2-b_j)^2(b_j-y)h(b_j).
\]
The second identity follows by writing \((2-b)/(2+b)=4/(2+b)-1\) in the sum \(\sum_j (b_j-y)r_j(2-b_j)/(2+b_j)\).
Thus for \(x=-1\) the endpoint gives \(J\le-y\), and for \(x=-2\) it gives \(J\le-8-6y\).
For \(x=1,2\) the moment targets give \(T=-4-3y,-8-6y\), respectively.

On layer \(\ell\) put \(d_\ell=3^{\ell-1}\), \(F_\ell(t)=1+T_{d_\ell}(t)\) with \(T_d(z+z^{-1})=z^d+z^{-d}\), and write \(A_\ell\) for the absolute norm of \(h\) on this layer.
The monic polynomial \(F_\ell\) has precisely the primitive-layer nodes as its roots because \(z^{d_\ell}\) is a primitive cube root at each such node.
As \(F_\ell(2)=3\) and \(|F_\ell(-2)|=1\), the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality separately on each layer gives
\[
 pT\ \ge\sum_\ell d_\ell(3 A_\ell |F_\ell(y)|)^{1/d_\ell},\qquad
 pJ\ \ge\sum_\ell d_\ell(9 A_\ell |F_\ell(y)|)^{1/d_\ell}.
\]
Its version on the whole grid gives \(pT\ge n(p\Delta |B(y)|)^{1/n}\) and \(pJ\ge n(p^2\Delta |B(y)|)^{1/n}\) for \(\Delta=\prod A_\ell\).
In particular \(T,J>1/2\) whenever \(p|B(y)|>2\), since \(\Delta\ge1\) and \((p/(p-1))^n<e^{1/2}<2\).

## Which fixed-resultant inputs belong to the same unit orbit

Let \(R=\mathbb Z[t]/B(t)\) and \(R_\ell=\mathbb Z[t]/F_\ell(t)\).
Two inputs \(h,h_0\in R\) with the same strict signs and pure two-power resultant lie in the same totally positive unit orbit if and only if their layer valuation vectors \((t_\ell)\) agree.
A unit has absolute layer norms one, giving necessity.
For sufficiency, write the common full absolute norm as \(2^E\).
Multiplication by \(h_0\) has determinant of absolute value \(2^E\), so its adjugate gives \(g=2^E h/h_0\in R\).
In each \(R_\ell\), the two inputs are \(2^{t_\ell}\) times elements of absolute norm one, by the irreducibility argument above and the absence of odd prime factors.
Consequently each component of \(g\) belongs to \(2^E R_\ell\).
The \(F_\ell\) are pairwise coprime modulo two since their primitive root orders differ even after identifying inverse roots.
The Bezout relation lifts modulo \(2^E\) because an element congruent to one modulo two is invertible in \((\mathbb Z/2^E)[t]\) by the finite geometric series.
For \(E>0\), the Chinese remainder theorem thus gives \(R/2^E R\simeq\prod_\ell R_\ell/2^E R_\ell\), so \(g\in2^E R\).
It follows that \(h/h_0\in R\), also directly when \(E=0\).
Its multiplication determinant has absolute value one, hence it is a unit, and the common signs make it totally positive.

For \(p=27,k=8,E=5\) the sign parities are \((1,0,1)\).
Here \(t_3=0\), \(t_1\) is even, \(t_2\) is odd, and \(t_1+3t_2=5\), forcing \((2,1,0)\).
Thus the [constructed representative](Yin_L_G0464_v8.md#integral-sign-representative) and its totally positive unit multiples exhaust the prescribed sign pattern at \(\Delta=32\).
The unique valuation vector at \(p=81,k=18,E=4\) proved above gives the corresponding exhaustion there.

For the surviving profile \(x=0\) at \(p=243\), only the layer vectors \((2,2,2,1,0)\) and \((8,0,2,1,0)\) remain possible at the prescribed \(\Delta\).
Indeed at \(y=-33/40\) the profile requires \(T=33/20\), and the lower bounds for \((3|F_\ell(y)|)^{1/d_\ell}\) are \((52,205,123,103,101)/100\), checked by the same powers.
In the even solutions of \(t_1+3t_2+9t_3=26\), a first exponent at least 14 already violates the budget \(pT=8019/20\).
For \(t_1\in\{2,8\}\) the two other possibilities for the first three exponents are \((2,8,0)\) and \((8,6,0)\), giving respectively \(T>172498/24300\) and \(T>67522/24300\).
In either surviving stratum the endpoint value is further restricted to \(P(-1)\in\{1,2\}\).
Indeed \(K-(2+y)U=p^{-1}\sum_j(2-b_j)(b_j-y)h(b_j)\) has the same layer-product lower bound as \(T\) since \(\prod_{j\text{ in layer }\ell}(2+b_j)=1\).
In the two surviving cases the same rational certificates give lower bounds \(20839/24300\) and \(32098/24300\), respectively, both greater than \(33/40\).
If \(U\ge1\) the left side is at most \(-y=33/40\).
It follows that \(-1\le U\le0\), and \(P(-1)=2+U\) for \(x=0\).
Both strata have integral representatives of the requested signs.
Starting from the representative \(h_0\) in the first stratum, take \(h_1=h_0T_3^8/T_9^2\) on the grid.
The correction is positive at every node and changes the layer valuations by \((6,-2,0,0,0)\), since \(T_{3^e}\) equals two on layers \(\ell\le e\) and is a unit on every other layer.
Thus \(h_1\) is integral on every layer and lies in \(R[1/2]\) because the denominator has two-power norm.
The same Chinese remainder argument gives \(h_1\in R\).
The construction supplies a second unit orbit to test if the prescribed total resultant alone is held fixed.
Whether either orbit at \(p=243\) contains an exact moment hit satisfying the endpoint is open in this calculation.

## An integral filter inside the remaining strata

For a fixed valuation vector write \(h=2^{t_\ell}f_\ell\) on each layer and set \(D_\ell=2^{t_\ell}d_\ell\).
With \(b\) a generic layer node and \(\tau_\ell=\operatorname{Tr}_\ell/d_\ell\), the three numbers
\[
 s_\ell=\tau_\ell((4-b^2)f_\ell),\quad w_\ell=\tau_\ell(b(4-b^2)f_\ell),\quad
 u_\ell=\tau_\ell((2-b)f_\ell)
\]
are integers satisfying \(\sum D_\ell(s_\ell,w_\ell,u_\ell)=p(K,L,U)\).
Indeed integral polynomials have integral expansions in \(1,T_1,T_2,\ldots\) by monicity, and the trace of \(T_a\) sums the \(a\)-th powers over all primitive \(3^\ell\)-th roots.
Subtracting the sum over the roots of order dividing \(3^{\ell-1}\) from the sum over all \(3^\ell\)-th roots gives only \(0,-d_\ell,2d_\ell\).

Each layer containing both signs permits its own separator \(z\) between the two signs in that layer.
For any such \(z\) and any real \(v\), the factor \(1-vb+v^2=|1-v\zeta|^2\) is strictly positive at every layer node \(b=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).
Applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to \((2-b)(b-z)(1-vb+v^2)f_\ell(b)\) yields
\[
 (1+v)^2\bigl(s_\ell-(2+z)u_\ell\bigr)-v(w_\ell-zs_\ell)
 \ \ge\ \bigl[3|F_\ell(z)|(1+v^{d_\ell}+v^{2d_\ell})\bigr]^{1/d_\ell}.
\]
The norm of \(f_\ell\) has absolute value one, and the last factor is \(\Phi_{3^\ell}(v)\) by the primitive roots.
Together with the global integer equality this tests proposed layer triples using rational \(z,v\) and integer powers.
Even compatible triples still require simultaneous realization by a single integral \(h\) of the specified signs and norms.
