# A certificate for the degree-34 trace bound in the auxiliary-function example

<a id="repair"></a>
## Recovering the integer bound with new rational weights

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic, irreducible, reciprocal and noncyclotomic of degree 34.
Write \(s_1\) for the sum of its roots and \(\theta\) for the real root greater than one of \(X^3-X-1\).
Then \(|s_1|\le4\) in either of the following ranges.

- \(M(P)\le\theta\) and every root has modulus at most \(\sqrt\theta\).
- \(M(P)\le1177/1000\), with no further bound on individual moduli.

The first range includes the degree-34 second-case example in [Flammang, Rhin and Sac-Épée, §5a](../audits/literature/FRS06/Yin_U001.md#printed-example).
The second includes every such polynomial strictly below Lehmer's measure, since the exterior root of the [degree-ten polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#minus-delay-progression) is less than \(1177/1000\) by direct substitution and uniqueness.
This recovers the integer conclusion used in that example after the discrepancy in its stated real minimum.
It supplies a first-trace bound only.
The other auxiliary functions and historical enumerations have their original evidence scope.

Use the [elliptic maxima and resultant argument](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/readme.md#ellipse) with the eleven polynomials in the printed order and exact weights \(10^{-8}\) times
\[
 (21213400,53642100,55319300,4541000,19745100,10342300,
     8422800,485600,6321600,5909800,8019100).
\]
For a representative on or outside the unit circle from each inverse pair put \(v_i=\log|\alpha_i|\).
As in J179 let \(U(v)\) denote the upper maximum for the negative of the weighted auxiliary function on the trace ellipse of radius \(e^v\).
For a root \(\alpha\) of the irreducible polynomial, its trace \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\) satisfies the monic trace polynomial of degree 17, while \(\alpha\) satisfies \(X^2-tX+1\) over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Thus \([\mathbb Q(\pm t):\mathbb Q]=17\), whereas every auxiliary has degree at most five, so all the resultants are nonzero at either sign.
More generally, the estimates below apply to a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree 34 whenever its trace polynomial at each sign is coprime to the eleven active auxiliaries, as required by the [source's signed coprimality condition](../audits/literature/FRS06/Yin_U001.md#auxiliary).
The same upper bound \(\sum_{i=1}^{17}U(v_i)\) holds for each of \(s_1,-s_1\).
The function \(U\) is even and convex, hence nondecreasing on the nonnegative half-line.

## Three bounds on the entire ellipses

The [rational Bernstein program](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/trace_bounds.py) gives the upper bounds \(0.2107,\ 0.7256,\ 0.8225\) at radii \(1,\ 144/125,\ 1177/1000\), respectively.
Here is a reproduction command in Python from the repository root.
The rational evaluation points select trial upper bounds, each verified separately on the whole interval by the program.

~~~python
from fractions import Fraction as F
import sys

sys.path.insert(0, "mathematics/computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179")
import trace_bounds as cert

weights = (21213400, 53642100, 55319300, 4541000, 19745100, 10342300,
           8422800, 485600, 6321600, 5909800, 8019100)
data = (
    (F(1), F(-100374581, 10**9), 210700000000),
    (F(144, 125), F(-888449401, 10**9), 725600000000),
    (F(1177, 1000), F(-177430807, 200000000), 822500000000),
)
for radius, witness, upper in data:
    cert.T = radius
    result = cert.certify(1, weights, witness, 10**8)
    assert result["upper"] == upper
assert cert.LOG_SCALE == 10**12
~~~

For the first range put \(R=144/125\).
One has \(R^2>\theta\), by \(R^2>1\) and \((R^2)^3-R^2-1>0\).
Each \(v_i\) lies in \([0,\log R]\) and their sum is at most \(2\log R\).
The chord bound for the convex function yields
\[
 |s_1|\le15U(0)+2U(\log R)
       \le15(0.2107)+2(0.7256)=4.6117.
\]
For the second range choose \(R=1177/1000\).
Now \(\sum_i v_i\le\log R\), so
\[
 |s_1|\le16U(0)+U(\log R)
       \le16(0.2107)+0.8225=4.1937.
\]
Integrality gives the claimed bounds in both cases.
