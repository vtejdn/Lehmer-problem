# Large mean angular fluctuations on a single cyclotomic orbit

<a id="window"></a>
## A lower bound at every prime in the fourth-logarithmic window

For a monic polynomial \(H\) put \(J(H)=\int_{\mathbb T}(\log|H|-m(H))_+\,d\mu\), where \(m\) is logarithmic Mahler measure and \(\mu\) is Haar probability.
There is an absolute \(c>0\) and a sequence of cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_N\) of degrees \(d\to\infty\) such that
\[
                    J(\Phi_N)\ge \exp(c\sqrt{\log d}).
\]
For every fixed \(A>0\), all primes \(p\le(\log(3d))^A\) eventually preserve the primitive orbit and hence give this same bound for the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_N^p\).
In particular \(J/p\) tends uniformly to infinity throughout the [fourth-logarithmic window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement).
The positive part measures an angular fluctuation even when the total logarithmic measure is zero.
The [coprime-row estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#rows) concerns irreducible nontorsion units of positive measure.

<a id="conductors"></a>
## Choosing the prime divisors of the conductor

Fix a large integer \(s\), put \(D=2^s,\ T=D^2\), and require \(N\) to be a product of \(s\) distinct primes in \([X,2X]\), where \(X=\exp(Cs)\) with an absolute \(C\) sufficiently large.
We can ensure that no sum of at most four *distinct* divisors of \(N\) with nonzero integral coefficients of total absolute value at most \(4T\) vanishes.
Start with the divisor set \(\{1\}\), where the property holds.
If the current set is \(B\), the new divisors on adjoining a prime \(q\) are \(B\) and \(qB\).
A relation reduces to \(u+qv=0\) by grouping its unscaled and scaled terms.
Within each group the old divisors are distinct and the total coefficient bound is preserved, so any nonempty group has nonzero sum by induction.
A relation using both groups forbids at most one new prime.
There are \(O((D T)^4)\) candidate relations by choosing at most four divisors, their two groups and their signed coefficients.
The [dyadic prime bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#primes) supplies \(\gg X/\log X\) primes, exceeding this number and the \(s\) primes already chosen when \(C\) is large.
This proves the assertion.
In particular \(d=\varphi(N)=\prod_{q\mid N}(q-1)\) has \(\log d\asymp s^2\), while all conductor primes exceed every fixed power of \(\log(3d)\) eventually.

<a id="moments"></a>
## The first absolute moment of the cyclotomic logarithm

On the circle with angular coordinate \(t\), put \(f(t)=\log|1-e^{it}|\).
It has mean zero, belongs to \(L^4\) by its logarithmic singularity, and has Fourier coefficients \(-1/(2|k|)\) for \(k\ne0\), by the [centered root expansion](Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#energy).
Let \(f_T\) be its Fejér mean with multiplier \((1-|k|/(T+1))_+\).
Convolution with this probability kernel gives \(\|f_T\|_4\le\|f\|_4\).
Parseval, summing the squared errors separately at \(|k|\le T\) and \(|k|>T\), gives \(\|f-f_T\|_2\ll T^{-1/2}\).
In particular \(\|f_T\|_2\) stays bounded below.

Möbius inversion gives \(\log|\Phi_N(e^{it})|=\sum_{b\mid N}\mu_{\rm M}(N/b) f(bt)\) almost everywhere, where \(\mu_{\rm M}\) is the Möbius function.
In the corresponding truncated sum \(S(t)\) replace \(f\) by \(f_T\).
All \(D\) signs are \(\pm1\) because \(N\) is squarefree.
For any product of at most four of these trigonometric polynomials, each Fourier term integrates to zero unless the total frequency at *each distinct divisor* vanishes.
Indeed, after terms at the same divisor are collected, a nonzero residual would violate the conductor property with total coefficient size at most \(4T\).
Thus the moments through order four agree with those of the sum of independent signed copies of \(f_T\) evaluated at a uniform angle.
The centered second and fourth moments satisfy
\[
 \|S\|_2^2=D\|f_T\|_2^2,\qquad
 \|S\|_4^4=D\|f_T\|_4^4+3D(D-1)\|f_T\|_2^4 .
\]
Interpolation gives \(\|S\|_1\ge\|S\|_2^3/\|S\|_4^2\gg\sqrt D\).
Undoing the truncation costs at most \(D\|f-f_T\|_2=O(1)\) in \(L^1\).
The full sum has mean zero, so its positive part has integral exactly half its \(L^1\) norm.
This proves \(J(\Phi_N)\gg\sqrt D\), and the upper bound \(J(\Phi_N)\ll\sqrt D\) follows from \(\|S\|_2\) and the same truncation error.
The [second-moment formula of Lalín and Sinha](../audits/literature/LS11/Yin_U001.md#ls11-u001) gives \(V(\Phi_N)=(\pi^2/12)D\varphi(N)/N\) for this squarefree conductor.
The fourth-moment estimate above gives the lower bound on the first absolute moment.

<a id="positive"></a>
## Positive-measure controls with a cyclotomic factor

Fix a monic irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial \(P\) of degree \(e\) and form the squarefree product \(P\Phi_N\).
It has exactly the same positive total measure as \(P\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), the \(p\)-th powers of the roots of \(P\) are distinct, since only finitely many quotients of distinct roots can be roots of unity of prime order.
They remain nontorsion.
For \(p\nmid N\) preserving that degree, the polynomial obtained by powering all roots of the product is \(P_p\Phi_N\), where \(P_p\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha^p\) for a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\).
For any monic degree-\(e\) polynomial \(G\) the centered circle logarithm has \(L^1\) norm \(O(e)\) uniformly in its roots.
Indeed each centered root factor, after reflection into the closed disk, has bounded \(L^2\) norm by the same Fourier coefficients multiplied by powers of its radius.
Thus \(J(P_p\Phi_N)\ge J(\Phi_N)-O(e)\) by the triangle inequality for centered \(L^1\) norms.
For fixed \(P\), the powered product has the same superlogarithmic lower bound throughout the fourth-logarithmic window of its own total degree.
Even along a hypothetical sequence of positive measures tending to zero one could choose the conductor separately for each input so that \(\sqrt D\) dominates \(e\) and the window exceeds all exceptional powering primes.
Here the unpowered product has measure \(m(P\Phi_N)=m(P)\), while the powered product has measure \(m(P_p\Phi_N)=p\,m(P)\) whenever powering preserves the degrees.
The existence of inputs with positive \(m(P)\to0\) remains open.
An argument to make the angular deviation small for the [two coprime rows](Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#rows) must use information that distinguishes the nontorsion irreducible orbit from these controls.
