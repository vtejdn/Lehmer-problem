# The prime-power divisor of a two-row resultant and sharpness inside the coefficient box

<a id="divisor"></a>
## The repeated seed forces common factors after reduction

Take a qualifying pair \(U,V\) in the [finite coefficient minimum](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) at an odd prime \(p\).
Let \(P\) be any qualifying repeated seed factor of \(F(x)=U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\), choose a root \(\alpha\), put \(z=\alpha^p\), and let \(Q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(z\).
The [field recovery](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) gives \(\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)=K\) and \(\deg Q=\deg P=e\).
Write \(\overline P=\prod_i\phi_i^{m_i}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), with distinct monic irreducibles \(\phi_i\), and put
\[
       D_p=\prod_i\phi_i^{\lceil(2m_i-1)/p\rceil},\qquad t=\deg D_p .
\]
Then
\[
      D_p\mid\gcd(\overline U,\overline V),\qquad
      p^t\mid\operatorname{Res}(U,V).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(p^e\mid\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) whenever \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\).

Indeed, \(P^2\mid F\) gives \(\overline P^2\mid\overline U(x)^p+x\overline V(x)^p\).
If at least one reduced row is nonzero, factor \(\phi_i^s\) from both reduced rows, with \(s\) the minimum of their two orders and the zero polynomial assigned infinite order.
The residual graph after removing \(\phi_i^{ps}\) has the form \(A^p+xB^p\) with at least one of \(A,B\) nonzero modulo \(\phi_i\).
If \(B=0\bmod\phi_i\), this graph is nonzero there.
Otherwise its derivative \(B^p\) is nonzero there.
Thus its order at \(\phi_i\) is at most one and \(ps+1\ge2m_i\).
When both reduced rows vanish, divisibility of each by \(D_p\) holds directly.

Write \(d_U=\deg U,d_V=\deg V\).
The square \(Q^2\) divides the nonzero reflected determinant after a monomial shift, so \(e\le\max(d_U,d_V)\) by its Laurent span.
In particular \(t\le e\le d_U+d_V\).
Reduction of the Sylvester map \((A,B)\mapsto AU+BV\) into polynomials of degree \(<d_U+d_V\) has image divisible by \(D_p\), a subspace of codimension \(t\).
Consequently at least \(t\) invariant factors of its integral matrix are divisible by \(p\), proving (1).
The same dimension argument applies if reduction lowers the degree of either row.

There is a second norm divisibility which holds even at the primes of non-squarefree reduction.
The exact double root gives
\[
 V(z)=-p\alpha^{p-1}\{U'(z)+\alpha V'(z)\},\qquad U(z)=-\alpha V(z).
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(V(z)\in p\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and \(p^e\mid N_{K/\mathbb Q}(V(z))\ne0\).
For an indeterminate \(T\),
\[
       \operatorname{Res}_Y(Q(Y),U(Y)+TV(Y))
                =N_{K/\mathbb Q}(V(z))P(T).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
This follows by multiplying \(V(z_j)(T-\alpha_j)\) over the \(e\) conjugates.
The absolute norm is therefore the content of this resultant in \(T\).

## Simultaneous squarefree reduction for slowly growing row caps

Put \(b=b(p)\) and \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
For every candidate with \(H\le2^p\), the individual coefficient lower bound gives \(m(P)\le\log2\), and the determinant calculation above gives \(e\le b\).
The [Vandermonde discriminant bound](../audits/literature/DK25/Yin_U001.md#dk25-u001) gives
\[
  1\le |\operatorname{disc}P|
     \le e^e\exp((2e-2)m(P))\le b^b\,2^{2b-2}.
\]
Indeed the row \((1,\alpha_j,\ldots,\alpha_j^{e-1})\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt e\max(1,|\alpha_j|)^{e-1}\), and the squared Vandermonde determinant gives the absolute discriminant.
Thus \(b^b2^{2b-2}<p\) ensures squarefree reduction simultaneously for all candidate seeds at this prime.
This holds eventually for every \(b(p)\to\infty\) with \(b(p)=o(\log p/\log\log p)\), retaining the limit of [C408](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement).
Under this choice every genuine candidate passes the filter \(p^e\mid\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\) at all sufficiently large primes, including candidates whose seeds vary with \(p\).

Hadamard's inequality on the integral Sylvester matrix bounds its nonzero determinant by \(\|U\|_1^{d_V}\|V\|_1^{d_U}\).
The resulting lower bound on \(\log H\) from this divisor alone is \(e\log p/(d_U+d_V)\le\log p\), which is \(o(p)\).
The next construction realizes the exponent \(e\) exactly among candidates satisfying the full height cap.

## A capped witness with exactly \(e\) factors of \(p\)

Fix a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit seed of even degree \(e\ge4\) and log measure \(h\) such that \(3h<\log2\).
The [degree-ten example](Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#fixed-degree-benchmarks) works since \(3\log(6/5)<\log2\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), there are qualifying degree-\(\le4e\) rows with \(H\le2^p\) and
\[
                   v_p(\operatorname{Res}(U,V))=e .
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Hence (4) holds in the actual search box of every diverging \(b(p)\) eventually for this seed.

Take \(p>4e\) sufficiently large to preserve the seed degree, avoid \(\operatorname{disc}P\) and avoid \(P(1)P(-1)\).
Put \(B=4e,\ m=e/2,\ q=Y^{-m}Q,\ D=Yq'\), and \(\delta=Y-Y^{-1}\).
The identity \(\overline Q=\overline P\) follows coefficientwise by Frobenius on the elementary symmetric functions of the roots of \(P\).
Thus the conjugates \(z_j=\alpha_j^p\) have distinct reductions, all different from \(0,1,-1\).
In the positive-sign [explicit construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#rows) use its formula (3) at \(w=1,L=1\).
The free choice of \(L\) suffices here since the finite minimum requires only one graph with a repeated seed factor.
Multiply the two Laurent rows by \(Y^{2e}\) to obtain \(a,b_0\), and add \(Q^2(1+Y^{2e})\) to \(a\) as in the [endpoint and norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#bounds).
Now \(a\) is monic of degree \(B\) with constant one and \(b_0(0)=0,\ \deg b_0<B\).
Set \(V=a,\ U_0(Y)=Y^B b_0(1/Y)\).
The graph \(U_0(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) is \(x\) times the seed graph of that construction, so it contains \(P^2\).
At each \(z_j\) its second row satisfies
\[
       \frac{V(z_j)}p=z_j^{2e}D(z_j)^3\delta(z_j),\qquad
       z_j V'(z_j)\equiv z_j^{2e}D(z_j)^2\pmod p.
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
The first identity is the value \(u=pD(z_j)^3\delta(z_j)\) before the monomial shift.
The derivative congruence follows from the centered first derivative \(D(z_j)^2\) and \(p\mid u\) in the ring of integers.
Since \(D(z_j)=z_j^{1-m}Q'(z_j)\), both right sides of (5) are units at every place above \(p\).

Replace \(U_0\) by \(U=U_0+cQ^2\).
The double root persists and \(\deg U<B\).
At every root of \(\overline V\) outside the roots of \(\overline Q\), at most one residue of \(c\) is excluded by requiring \(\overline U\ne0\) there.
There are at most \(B\) such roots, so one of the distinct residues \(0,\ldots,B\) works.
The only common reduced roots are then the \(e\) roots of \(\overline Q\), and (5) makes each a simple root of \(\overline V\).

Take a finite completion containing the \(z_j,\alpha_j\), with valuation normalized by \(v(p)=1\).
By [simple-root lifting](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001), \(V\) has a root \(y_j\) reducing to each \(z_j\), and Taylor expansion with (5) gives \(y_j=z_j-V(z_j)/V'(z_j)\bmod p^2\).
Consequently
\[
       U(y_j)\equiv
       -V(z_j)\frac{U'(z_j)+\alpha_jV'(z_j)}{V'(z_j)}
                                               \pmod {p^2}.
\]
The derivative combination is a unit by (2) and (5), so \(v(U(y_j))=1\).
All other roots of the monic \(V\) are integral and reduce to points where \(\overline U\ne0\), counted with multiplicity in a splitting field.
Their evaluations contribute valuation zero to \(\operatorname{Res}(V,U)\), proving (4) and coprimality over \(\mathbb Q\).
The determinant \(UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) is nonzero because its outer coefficients come solely from the two unit endpoints of \(V\).
The row \(U\) is nonzero since \(U(z_j)=-\alpha_jV(z_j)\ne0\).
Finally \(\log H\le3ph+O_P(\log(2p))\) by the cited coefficient bound and the additional norm cost at most \(B\|Q\|_1^2\le B\,4^e\exp(2ph)\).
The strict inequality \(3h<\log2\) puts these rows inside the height cap for all sufficiently large primes.
