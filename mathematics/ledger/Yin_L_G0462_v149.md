# Turning points and radial bounds for a two-row phase

2026-11-18T09:46.

<a id="turns"></a>
## Replacing the degree by angular variation and reversals of direction

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(n\ge1\), constant term \(\pm1\), and \(\gcd(f,g)=1\) for its reversal \(g=X^n f(1/X)\).
Let \(p\ge2\) and \(S\ge1\) be coprime, \(\sigma=\pm1\), and \(J(x)=f(x^p)+\sigma x^Sg(x^p)\).
There are no unit-circle roots of \(f\) or \(g\), by reality and coprimality.
Use normalized arc measure \(d\mu=dt/(2\pi)\).
Write \(u=-\sigma f/g\), \(\Psi(X)=u(X)^p/X^S\), and choose a continuous lift \(\Phi(t)=\arg\Psi(e^{it})\) on the real line.
The [grid formula and angular derivative](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v352.md#two) give
\[
 p\{m(J)-m(f)\}=\int\log|1-\Psi|\,d\mu=\log2-L,\qquad
 L=\int\log\frac2{|1-\Psi|}\,d\mu,\qquad
 v=|\Phi'|=\left|\frac{K}{fg}\right|,\quad K=pX(f'g-fg')-Sfg .
\]
The signed derivative is the real value \(K/(fg)\) on the circle.
Put \(A=\int v\,d\mu\), the total variation in full turns per period, and let \(r\) count only the zeros of \(K\) at which \(\Phi'\) changes sign on the circle.
Here \(A>0\) since \(K(0)=-Sf(0)g(0)\ne0\).
For every \(0<\alpha<1\) put \(C_\alpha=\int (2/|1-z|)^\alpha\,d\mu(z)<\infty\).
Then
\[
 \alpha L\le \log C_\alpha+
       \left(\log A-\int\log v\,d\mu\right)+\log(1+r/A).              \tag{1}
\]
The middle term is the logarithm of the ratio of the arithmetic mean speed to the geometric mean speed and is nonnegative.
The logarithm of the geometric mean is \(\int\log v\,d\mu=m(K)-2m(f)\).
Thus a sequence with \(p\to\infty\) for which both nonnegative terms in (1) are \(o(p)\) satisfies \(m(J)-m(f)\to0\), with no restriction on \(n\).

To prove (1), if \(r>0\), cut one period at its \(r\) reversals of direction.
On a monotone arc of sweep \(a\) in full turns, each regular target has at most \(a+1\) preimages, by lifting the target to points at angular spacing \(2\pi\).
The sweeps sum to \(A\).
If \(r=0\), the map is monotone and wraps exactly \(A\) times, so each regular target has \(A\) preimages.
In both cases the valence is at most \(A+r\) outside the finitely many critical values.
Changing variables on the monotone arcs, splitting at stationary points as needed, gives \(\int (2/|1-\Psi|)^\alpha v\,d\mu\le C_\alpha(A+r)\).
Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives (1).
All logarithms are integrable by rationality and the nonzero constant term of \(K\).

If \(r=0\), the argument principle gives \(A=|p(n_- -n_+)-S|\), where \(n_-,n_+\) count the roots of \(f\) inside and outside the circle with multiplicities.
Indeed \(g\) has exactly \(n_+\) disk roots.
Since \(m(K)\ge\log(S|\operatorname{lc}f|)\), in this case
\[
 \alpha L\le 2m(f)+\log\frac{C_\alpha |p(n_- -n_+)-S|}{S|\operatorname{lc} f|}.
\]
In particular a monotone phase with \(|n_- -n_+|/S\) bounded along a sequence with \(p\to\infty\) gives \(\liminf m(J)\ge\log\theta\), where \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0,\ \theta>1\).
This follows on substituting the bound in the grid identity, since the coefficient of \(m(f)\) in the lower bound for \(m(J)\) is \(1-2/(\alpha p)\) and the [nonreciprocal seed gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v337.md#rigidity) gives \(m(f)\ge\log\theta\).
The sign condition can be tested on the circle from the real rational function \(K/(fg)\).

## Exponential convergence from a pole-free annulus

Suppose \(0<\rho<1\), \(u\) has no poles in \(\rho\le|z|\le1\), and
\[
             q=\rho^{-S}\left(\max_{|z|=\rho}|u(z)|\right)^p<1.
\]
Then
\[
                        |m(J)-m(f)|\le-\frac1p\log(1-q).              \tag{2}
\]
The same conclusion holds if \(\Psi^{-1}\) is analytic on that annulus and its maximum modulus on \(|z|=\rho\) is \(q<1\).
For a specified rational radius, a zero count for \(g\) in the closed annulus and a strict polynomial inequality on the inner circle suffice to check the first condition.

For each \(j\ge1\), the boundary mean of \(\Psi^j\) is its contour integral against \(dz/(2\pi i z)\).
Move the contour to radius \(\rho\) to bound the mean by \(q^j\) in absolute value.
Integrate the absolutely convergent power series for \(\log|1-t\Psi|\), \(0<t<1\).
The resulting mean has absolute value at most \(\sum_{j\ge1}(tq)^j/j\).
As \(t\uparrow1\) the mean tends to that for \(\log|1-\Psi|\): on the circle the integrand is bounded above by \(\log2\) and below by \(\log|1-\Psi|+\tfrac12\log t\), using \(|1-t\Psi|^2=(1-t)^2+t|1-\Psi|^2\).
The boundary logarithm is integrable since \(\Psi\) is nonconstant.
The grid identity proves (2), and \(|1-\Psi^{-1}|=|1-\Psi|\) on the circle proves the reciprocal case.

For example, if all roots of \(f\) lie inside the circle and \(S/p\le1/4\), then
\[
             q\le\big((4/5)\,2^{1/4}\big)^p<1
\]
at \(\rho=1/2\), uniformly in degree and coefficients.
Indeed \(u\) is analytic on the closed disk with boundary modulus one and \(|u(0)|=1/|\operatorname{lc} f|\le1/2\), since the root product has modulus strictly below one and \(f(0)=\pm1\).
The Schwarz lemma applied to the disk automorphism sending \(u(0)\) to zero gives \(|u(z)|\le(|z|+|u(0)|)/(1+|z||u(0)|)\le4/5\) on the half-radius circle.
This provides an absolute exponential rate even if the seed measures are unbounded.
For a general coprime seed the required annulus or the sign condition in (1) need not follow from its two-row representation.

## Fixed measure and small signed winding with many inverse images

Take \(f_N(X)=X^{2N}-X^N-1,\ g_N=f_N^*,\ S=1\).
The base \(z^2-z-1\) is nonreciprocal and coprime to \(1-z-z^2\), as are their two substitutions.
Its two roots lie respectively inside and outside the unit circle.
Thus \(m(f_N)=\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\) and the signed winding of \(\Psi\) is \(-1\) for every \(N,p\).
Yet direct differentiation gives
\[
 \Phi'(t)=-\frac{4pN\cos(Nt)}{5-4\cos^2(Nt)}-1.
\]
Indeed the base Wronskian \(f'g-fg'\) is \(-2(1+z^2)\), and the product \(fg/z^2\) equals \(3-2\cos(2t)\) at \(z=e^{it}\).
The function \(y/(5-4y^2)\) is strictly increasing on \([-1,1]\), so \(\Phi'\) changes sign exactly \(2N\) times in one period.
Moreover \(A\ge c pN-1\), where \(c\) is the positive circle average of \(4|\cos t|/(5-4\cos^2t)\), and \(A\le4pN+1\).
Changing variables as above with constant integrand one gives mean valence exactly \(A\).
Thus some regular target has at least \(A\) preimages although the signed winding has absolute value one.
This rules out replacing the degree in the coarea argument by signed winding alone for arbitrary seeds.
The [sparse two-row comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v342.md#statement) already gives convergence on these seeds, which have three terms.
