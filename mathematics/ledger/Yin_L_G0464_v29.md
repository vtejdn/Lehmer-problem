# A conductor bound for rational cut factors in the full quotient order

<a id="modular-cut"></a>
## Rational linear factors modulo the trace polynomial

Let \(p=2n+1\ge5\) be odd, \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\) for \(1\le j\le n\), and \(B_n(t)=\prod_{j=1}^n(t-b_j)\).
Suppose the integral canonical coordinate \(h\) of a strict-cut input matches the fixed zero profile through coefficient index four in the [coordinates of L464 v28](Yin_L_G0464_v28.md#rational-factor).
If
\[
    h\equiv(At+\beta)v\pmod{B_n},\quad
    A,\beta\in\mathbb Z,\quad A\ge1,\quad \gcd(A,\beta)=1,\quad
    v\in\mathbb Z[t],\quad v(b_j)>0\ (1\le j\le n),
\]
then \(p<10^{14}\).
This covers primes and composites with arbitrary coefficient heights, under the strict signs alone.
For a factor in the ordinary polynomial ring, [L464 v28](Yin_L_G0464_v28.md#finite-test) already excludes every odd conductor.
The present bound leaves a finite range of conductors for the congruence, with unrestricted denominators \(A\) there.

<a id="height-estimate"></a>
## A uniform estimate as the rational cut varies

It suffices to consider \(p\ge10^{14}\).
Put \(\alpha=-\beta/A\).
The first three moments and the integral norm of \(v\) give \(-2<\alpha<-3/2\) and [inequality (7) of L464 v28](Yin_L_G0464_v28.md#quotient-order),
\[
   \min\{1/4,\,2/A\}\ \ge\frac{4\beta-6A}{A^2}
          \ \ge\frac np\bigl(p^2|B_n(\alpha)|^2\bigr)^{1/n}.       \tag{1}
\]
Choose \(w\) on the upper unit semicircle with \(w+w^{-1}=\alpha\).
Its primitive minimal polynomial is \(Az^2+\beta z+A\), since its discriminant is negative.
The two unit roots give \(h_{\rm abs}(w)=\tfrac12\log A\).
A root of unity has algebraic integer trace, whereas the rational \(\alpha\in(-2,-3/2)\) is not an integer.
Thus \(w^p\ne1\).

Apply the [ordinary two-logarithm bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) to \(w,-1\) in the quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(w)\).
Use the principal logarithms and take \(k\) nearest to \(p\arg(w)/(2\pi)\).
Then \(p\log w-2k\log(-1)\) is nonzero, and the modified height of its integer coefficient vector is at most \(\log(2p)\).
The modified argument heights are bounded by \(\tfrac12\max\{\log A,\pi\}\le(\pi/2)(1+\log A)\) and \(\pi/2\), respectively.
By the [conversion to a distance](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle),
\[
 -\log|1-w^p|\le\log 2+
   C(2,2)(\pi^2/4)(1+\log A)\log(2p)
 \le K(1+\log A)\log(2p),\qquad K=8\cdot10^{10}.
\]
Indeed \(C(2,2)=18\cdot6\cdot8\cdot64^4\log8\), with \(\log8<2.1\), \(\pi^2/4<2.5\), and \(C(2,2)\pi^2/4+1<K\).
The identity \(z^nB_n(z+z^{-1})=(z^p-1)/(z-1)\) gives \(|B_n(\alpha)|\ge |1-w^p|/2\).
Since \(p/2\ge1\), the right side of (1) is at least
\[
   \frac np e^{-\gamma} A^{-\gamma},
        \qquad \gamma=\frac{2K\log(2p)}n .
\]

For \(p\ge10^{14}\), one has \(n/p\ge12/25\) and \(\gamma<1/8\).
The latter follows from the decrease of \(\log(2p)/p\) on this range and \(\log(2\cdot10^{14})<33\), giving \(\gamma<2K\cdot33/((12/25)10^{14})=11/100\).
At \(A=8\) the lower bound therefore exceeds
\[
      \frac{12}{25}\exp\bigl(-(1+\log8)/8\bigr)
             >\frac{12}{25}e^{-1/2}>\frac14 .
\]
For \(1\le A\le8\) the lower bound decreases with \(A\) and already exceeds \(1/4\) at the right endpoint.
For \(A\ge8\) its ratio to \(2/A\) increases with \(A\), since \(1-\gamma>0\), and the ratio exceeds one at the left endpoint.
Both intervals contradict (1).
