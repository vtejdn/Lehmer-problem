## Reuse and exact question

The local screen for a cyclic relative-norm subgroup, a single unit's power profiles and strict convexity found the complete positive-full-norm obstruction in [L372](Yin_L_G0372_v1.md#mixed-signed-unit-constant-obstruction), the field-profile construction in [L388](Yin_L_G0388_v1.md), the fixed quartic in [L384](Yin_L_G0384_v1.md), and the actual relative-unit kernel in [L385](Yin_L_G0385_v1.md#actual-order-relative-unit-generator), but no statement excluding the whole cyclic relative-norm recipe at growing degree.
The new argument is a finite embedding count and an explicit second derivative of a finite exponential sum; no external convexity theorem, classification algorithm or novelty claim is needed.

Fix a genuine Salem number alpha>1, original degree d=2s>=4, K=Q(alpha), F=Q(alpha+alpha^-1), sigma(alpha)=alpha^-1, and a sigma-stable full order O.
All specified elements and inverses belong to O.
Let w be one actual O-unit and put c=w sigma(w) in (O intersection F)^times.
Consider any finite nonempty set of actual units u_j with

    u_j sigma(u_j)=c^(n_j), n_j in Z,
    lambda_j>0, sum_j lambda_j=1,
    sum_j lambda_j rho_i(u_j sigma(u_j))=E
       for EVERY trace embedding rho_i:F->R.          (1)

Real weights are allowed for this obstruction, but (1) explicitly requires the SAME scalar E with the SAME weights at EVERY original trace embedding.
Alternatively, when all lambda_j and E are rational, the single identity sum_j lambda_j u_j sigma(u_j)=E in F implies (1) by conjugacy.
A single equality at the distinguished real embedding with arbitrary real weights is not a premise of the theorem and does not imply (1).

## 1. A negative-full-norm generator has full trace degree

At each of the s-1 circle trace places, rho_i(c)=|u(z_i)|^2>0, with u=w here.
If Norm_(K/Q)(w)=-1, the norm tower gives Norm_(F/Q)(c)=-1, so the remaining distinguished value rho_0(c) is negative.
There is exactly one negative value among the s actual F embeddings.

Consequently Q(c)=F.
Indeed, if e=[F:Q(c)]>1, each embedding of Q(c) extends to exactly e embeddings of F in characteristic zero.
The negative distinguished value would occur at least e times, contradicting uniqueness.
Thus c has degree s, and its s conjugates are distinct.
In particular the s-1 positive circle values are pairwise distinct.
This conclusion concerns the relative norm c, not an unsupported claim that every arbitrary order unit generates K.

If Norm_(K/Q)(w)=+1, c is positive at every trace place: its circle values are positive and their full product with the distinguished value is+1.
Every c^(n_j) is then totally positive with full F norm1.
The accepted L372 positive-trace comparison gives E>=1 in(1).
This case needs no new convexity argument.

<a id="three-circle-values-and-cyclic-profile-obstruction"></a>
## 2. Exact cyclic-profile exclusion

Assume d>=8, so s-1>=3.
Under(1), it is impossible to have0<E<1.

For the negative-full-norm w case, let y_i=log rho_i(c) at the circle trace places and define

    f(y)=sum_j lambda_j exp(n_j y),
    f(0)=1,
    f''(y)=sum_j lambda_j n_j^2 exp(n_j y).            (2)

If all n_j=0, (1) gives E=1.
Otherwise f''(y)>0 for every real y.
The three distinct circle values from Section1 give three distinct real y_1<y_2<y_3 with f(y_i)=E.
Strict convexity contradicts that equality: express y_2 as a strict convex combination of y_1,y_3 to get f(y_2)<E.
Thus no constant profile at all can arise from a nontrivial finite mixture in this negative-generator case.
The positive-generator case already gives E>=1.
Together they prove the stated low-profile exclusion, for arbitrary integer exponents of either sign, arbitrary finite support and arbitrary positive normalized weights.

A useful smaller-degree guard follows from the same calculation.
If two circle values of c lie on opposite sides of1, then their logarithms straddle0, so f(0)<=E by convexity, contradicting E<1.
If a circle value equals1, (1) gives E=f(0)=1 directly.
This is only a necessary obstruction; two values on the same side of1 do not by themselves prove realizability.
The theorem does not claim that the d=4 or d=6 cases are all excluded.

## 3. Multiplicative rank and twists

The primary theorem allows every direction of the form u_j=w^(n_j)v_j with v_j sigma(v_j)=1; equivalently it depends only on the relative norms in(1).
In particular it covers all independent signed monomial twists +/-alpha^(k_j) w^(n_j) when those twists are actual O-units.
If O contains alpha and alpha^-1, every such twist is admissible.
For a general stable order this membership is a separate requirement.
The relative-norm-one kernel from L385 may be used at its exact scope, without assuming alpha is its primitive generator.

More invariantly, any exact low constant profile on a genuine Salem input d>=8 must have its relative norms generate a multiplicative group of rank at least2.
Here is the precise rank-one argument.
The group generated by finitely many actual relative norms lies in B^times for B=O intersection F and is finitely generated abelian.
Its torsion is trivial: a torsion element of a totally real field is+/-1, and every element of this relative-norm image is positive at every circle trace place, excluding-1.
A finitely generated torsion-free rank-one abelian group is cyclic.
A generator is an integer product of the original relative norms, hence equals w sigma(w) for the corresponding integer product w of actual units.
The primary theorem applies.
Rank zero gives only relative norm1 and E=1.

This is a lower bound on multiplicative rank in the relative-norm image, not on the number of labels alone, coefficient rank, a Gram rank, or free-probability independence.
Adding many powers, copies or monomial twists of one unit does not change it.
Two independent relative norms are only necessary, not a sufficient field profile or cheap Laurent construction.
