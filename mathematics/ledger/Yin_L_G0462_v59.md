# A gap in the denominator coefficients and exclusion of comparable Chebyshev offsets

<a id="interior"></a>
## Offsets separated from both ends of the degree range

Fix \(0<\eta\le1/2\).
On the positive-constant Pisot branch \(j=2\), the [inverse construction](Yin_L_G0462_v43.md#inverse) with
\[
 C_h(x)=2U_h(x/2)+\tau U_{h-d}(x/2),\qquad \tau\in\{-1,1\},
 \qquad \eta h\le d\le(1-\eta)h
\]
has \(q\) bounded in terms of \(\eta\).
Consequently \(\theta-q\) has a positive lower bound on this class by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
For general \(d\), the argument constrains the coefficients between the multiples of \(d\) studied in the [projected progression](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#progression).
The limit argument and coefficient bounds below were obtained on 2026-11-16.

Write \(B^\sharp=\sum_n b_nz^n=z^{h+1}B(z+z^{-1})\), \(J_q=2/K_q=\sum_n j_nz^n\), and \(E=\min(d,h-d)\).
There are absolute \(c>0,q_0,E_0\) such that \(q\ge q_0\), \(E\ge E_0\) imply
\[
                    b_n=0\qquad (1\le n\le cE).                         \tag{1}
\]
We take \(c<1\).
Under these conditions every interval of integer indices of diameter at most \(cE\) in \([1,d-1]\) has at most four nonzero \(b_n\).
In \([d+1,h+1]\) every such interval has at most eight.
The reflected coefficients obey the same bounds by reciprocity.

<a id="seam"></a>
## Integrality on both sides of the offset

Suppose \(q_i\to\infty\), \(E_i\to\infty\), and choose any positive integers \(t_i=o(E_i)\).
Pass to a subsequence of constant sign \(\tau\).
Use the [signed measure](Yin_L_G0462_v49.md#signed-measure) \(\omega_i\) of mass one representing \(J_{q_i}\), with negative mass \(\gamma_i=1/[2(q_i-1)]\) at \(1\).
Push it forward by \(v\mapsto(x,y)=(v^{t_i},v^{d_i})\) and take a weak limit \(\Omega\).
Since \(\gamma_i\to0\), this is a probability invariant under simultaneous complex conjugation.
Set \(M(r,k)=2\int x^r y^k\,d\Omega(x,y)\) for \(r,k\in\mathbb Z\).
These moments are real, \(M(0,0)=2\), and each is the limit of the corresponding doubled moments of the original measures, including at moving indices.

The exact trace identity reads
\[
 B^\sharp=\left(1+\frac{\tau z^d}{2}
       -\frac{\tau z^{2h+2-d}}2-z^{2h+2}\right)J_q .
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
For \(r>0\), \(rt_i<d_i\) eventually, so \(j_{rt_i}=b_{rt_i}\in\mathbb Z\) and \(M(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\).
At the index \(d_i+rt_i\) for fixed \(r\), (2) has no reflected term because \(rt_i<2(h_i-d_i)+2\) eventually.
For \(r<0\) this index is below \(d_i\), while at \(r=0\) the term from \(j_0=1\) enters.
Thus
\[
 M(r,1)\in\mathbb Z\ (r<0),\qquad
 M(0,1)+\tau/2\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 M(r,1)+\tfrac{\tau}{2}M(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\ (r>0).                    \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(2M(r,1)\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z\) for every integer \(r\).
As a function of \(r\), this is a Fourier–Stieltjes transform on \(\mathbb Z\), by weighting \(\Omega\) with \(4y\) and projecting to \(x\).
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) makes it periodic outside a finite set, with the same periodic part on both tails.
The negative tail is even by (3), so the positive tail is eventually even too.
Consequently \(M(r,0)\) is even for all sufficiently large positive \(r\).

<a id="haar"></a>
## The first marginal must be normalized arc length

Let \(J\) be the Herglotz transform of the first marginal, with constant one and coefficients \(M(r,0)\) at positive \(r\).
Both \(J\) and \(1/J\) have integer Taylor coefficients, constant one and positive real part in the disk.
Applying the same integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem to the doubled representing measure of each makes both rational with at most simple cyclotomic poles, by the [periodic decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic).
Hence \(J=U/V\) for relatively prime squarefree signed cyclotomic products with constant terms one.
Its marginal cannot be purely atomic.
Indeed it would then be supported on finitely many roots of unity, so \(M(r,1)\) would be periodic in \(r\).
Equation (3) makes that sequence integral at every negative index and half-odd-integral at zero.

Now \(J(x)+J(1/x)\) gives the nonzero nonnegative continuous doubled density away from the poles.
By reciprocity it equals \((U/V)(1+\epsilon x^{\deg V-\deg U})\) with \(\epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
As in the [moving-offset classification](Yin_L_G0462_v55.md#moving), equal degrees force both products constant by even multiplicity of density zeros and absence of poles in the density.
For unequal degrees the binomial has \(|\deg V-\deg U|\) simple circle roots, which by the same parity condition are exactly the roots of \(UV\).
Thus \(J=1\), \(1\pm x^\ell\), or \(1/(1\pm x^\ell)\) for some \(\ell\ge1\).
The reciprocal binomials violate eventual evenness of \(M(r,0)\), since they have unit coefficients at every positive multiple of \(\ell\).

Suppose \(J=1+\delta x^\ell\) with \(\delta=\pm1\).
The marginal has density \(1+\delta\cos(\ell\arg x)\) with respect to normalized arclength.
The measure obtained by weighting \(\Omega\) by \(4y\) and projecting onto \(\bar x\) therefore has density \(Q(x)\) with
\[
      [x^r]Q=2M(r,1),\qquad
      |Q(x)|\le 2|1+\delta x^\ell|^2 .
\]
Here the bracket denotes the \(r\)-th Fourier coefficient.
Its integer coefficients tend to zero at both ends by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, so \(Q\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
The bound extends everywhere and forces double zeros at all roots of \(1+\delta x^\ell\).
Monic division after multiplying by a monomial gives \((1+\delta x^\ell)^2\mid Q\) over the integer Laurent polynomials.
On the other hand (3) gives \(Q\equiv 1+x^\ell\bmod 2\), since the sole nonzero positive coefficient of \(J\) is at \(\ell\).
This is impossible in \(\mathbb F_2[x,x^{-1}]\) because \((1+x^\ell)^2\) does not divide \(1+x^\ell\).
Only \(J=1\) remains, giving normalized arclength as the first marginal.

In particular \(b_{t_i}=j_{t_i}\to M(1,0)=0\), so these integers eventually vanish.
If (1) failed for every choice of absolute constants, taking \(c=1/i,\ q_0=E_0=i\) would give counterexamples with \(t_i\le E_i/i\) and \(b_{t_i}\ne0\).
This proves (1).
Taking \(t_i=1\) also shows that the unscaled normalized series \(K_{q_i}\) tends to the constant two whenever \(q_i,E_i\to\infty\).

## Bessel's inequality in a coefficient interval

For a single input satisfying (1), the characters \(v^n\) with distinct indices in an interval of diameter at most \(cE\) have Gram matrix \(I\) against \(\omega_q\).
Indeed the measure is symmetric and at each positive index in their difference range its doubled moment is \(j_n=b_n=0\).
The positive part is \(\lambda=\omega_q+\gamma\delta_1\), \(\gamma=1/[2(q-1)]\), so for \(L\) such characters the Gram matrix against \(\lambda\) is \(I+\gamma{\bf1}{\bf1}^{\mathsf T}\).
Its operator norm is \(1+L\gamma\), bounding the sum of squared inner products against any function by that factor times its squared \(L^2(\lambda)\) norm.

For \(1\le n<d\), equation (2) gives \(b_n=2\int v^n\,d\omega_q\).
Five nonzero coefficients in the interval would imply \(5(1-2\gamma)^2\le(1+5\gamma)4(1+\gamma)\) by using the function \(2\) and allowing for the mass removed at one.
This fails for sufficiently small \(\gamma\).
For \(d<n\le h+1\) the second reflected term has yet to enter, so \(b_n=\int v^n(2+\tau v^{-d})\,d\omega_q\).
At \(n=d\), \(j_d=b_d-\tau/2\) is half-odd-integral and \(|j_d|\le2(1+2\gamma)\), giving \(|j_d|\le3/2\) for \(\gamma<1/8\).
The energy of the multiplier against \(\omega_q\) is \(5+2\tau j_d\le8\), so its energy against \(\lambda\) is at most \(8+9\gamma\).
Nine nonzero coefficients in the interval would similarly give \(9(1-3\gamma)^2\le(1+9\gamma)(8+9\gamma)\).
This too fails for all sufficiently small \(\gamma\).
Increasing the absolute \(q_0\) in (1) proves both asserted interval bounds.

The two half-ranges can be partitioned into \(O(1+h/E)\) intervals of diameter at most \(cE\).
Counting \(b_0,b_d\) separately and reflecting about \(h+1\) bounds the number of nonzero terms of \(B^\sharp\) by \(O(1+h/E)\) with an absolute implied constant.
If \(q\to\infty\), bounded \(h\) is impossible since \(q-1\mid C_h(2)\) and \(C_h(2)\le3h+3\).
For \(\eta h\le d\le(1-\eta)h\) we would then have \(E\ge\eta h\), hence a uniformly bounded number of terms.
But \(B^\sharp\) has Mahler measure equal to the unique exterior root of the positive delay-one associate, strictly above one and tending to one by [the branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) and [the delay-one bound](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This contradicts the [fixed-support Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001), proving the exclusion.

## Simultaneous restrictions on the other residue classes

Return to \(q_i,d_i,h_i/d_i\to\infty\) and take \(t_i=1\) in the two-coordinate limit.
For \(r\in\mathbb Z,\ k\ge2\), equation (2) gives eventual stabilization on the extracted subsequence at the integers
\[
 A(r,k)=\lim_i b_{kd_i+r}=M(r,k)+\tfrac{\tau}{2}M(r,k-1).
\]
For each \(k\) at most eight offsets \(r\) have \(A(r,k)\ne0\).
Indeed by the Haar first marginal these numbers are the Fourier coefficients of the reflected density of the projection weighted by \(y^{k-1}(2y+\tau)\).
Conditional Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval give \(\sum_r|A(r,k)|^2\le5+2\tau M(0,1)\le8\).
The index \(k\) is fixed as \(i\to\infty\), whereas the bound eight is common to all rows.

Moreover the entire support in \((r,k)\) with \(k\ge2\) lies in finitely many cosets of subgroups of \(\mathbb Z^2\) of rank at most one, each meeting each row in at most one point.
To see this, weight \(\Omega\) by \(2|2+\tau y|^2\) to obtain a measure of mass at most 18.
Its Fourier transform is
\[
 D(r,k)=5M(r,k)+2\tau\bigl(M(r,k+1)+M(r,k-1)\bigr).
\]
For \(k\ge2\), \(D(r,k)=4A(r,k)+2\tau A(r,k+1)\) is integral.
At \(k=1\), \(D(r,1)=4M(r,1)+2\tau(M(r,0)+A(r,2))\) is integral by (3).
At \(k=0\) integrality follows directly from the doubled first mixed moments at \(r,-r\), and conjugation supplies the negative indices.
The [locally compact integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) applies to this integer-valued member of \(B(\mathbb Z^2)\).
It gives a sum of a bounded number of signed coset indicators for \(D\), with the bound depending only on 18.

For \(k\ge2\), \(|A(r,k)|\le3\), so three backward substitutions give
\[
 \left|A(r,k)-\left(\frac{D(r,k)}4-\frac{\tau D(r,k+1)}8+
                         \frac{D(r,k+2)}{16}\right)\right|\le3/8.
\]
Thus rounding this expression recovers \(A\) exactly in that half-plane.
On the full lattice the same rounding defines an integer function of finitely many coset indicators.
Expanding the products that specify each Boolean pattern expresses this function as an integer sum of coset indicators, since nonempty intersections of cosets are cosets of the intersection subgroups.
The number of terms can still be bounded absolutely because the starting number of signed indicators and the rounding coefficients were bounded.
Fix a row \(k\ge2\).
The cosets whose subgroups contain a nonzero horizontal vector contribute a periodic function of \(r\), with empty intersections included.
Each remaining coset has rank at most one and meets that row at most once.
The first contribution must vanish on the whole row, since \(A(\cdot,k)\) and the second contribution both have finite support.
Removing the first group of terms therefore leaves a common list of points and nonhorizontal arithmetic progressions supporting all the rows.
This proves a restriction on each subsequential array and does not give a fixed period for finite inputs before extraction.

Even a Haar first marginal and the exact moment conditions alone permit a two-dimensional limit with positive density on the whole torus.
For example let \(\omega_i\) have density \(\Re(1/(1-\tau' v^{d_i}/2))\) against arclength, with \(\tau'=\pm1\) and \(d_i\to\infty\).
It is a symmetric probability with \(J_i(z)=1/(1-\tau' z^{d_i}/2)\), so the corresponding product \((1-\tau' z^{d_i}/2)J_i\) is identically one.
The graph measures converge to arclength in \(x\) times this fixed positive density in \(y\), by integrating each character and using the invariance under \(d_i\)-th roots of unity.
This example concerns the moment constraints alone and supplies no reciprocal denominator of the required degree or Pisot input.
