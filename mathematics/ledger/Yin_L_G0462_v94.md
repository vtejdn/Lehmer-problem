# Eight-term traces whose third lower exponent is the sum of the first two

<a id="shapes"></a>
## The three possible coefficient patterns

Consider actual positive-constant Pisot inputs on branch \(j=2\) with \(q\to\infty\) and eight-term trace lifts whose lower exponents are \(d,e,D=d+e\), where \(0<d<e\).
Write \(N=2h+2\) for the reflection degree and \(\Pi(z)=A(z)-z^N A(z^{-1})\).
By the [eight-term reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight), \(D=o(h)\) and \(D/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\), equivalently \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
After extraction the three coefficients of \(A\) are fixed.
On the independent base torus write the lower polynomial as \(P(x,y)=2+a x+b y+cxy\), after sign rotations in each variable to make \(a,b>0\) and interchange to make \(a\le b\).
These rotations preserve Haar measure and leave two independent signs to restore in the integer specialization.
The same reduction gives \(m(P)=\log 2\), since the primitive relation on the lower exponents is \((1,1,-1)\).

Jensen's formula in \(y\) gives the circle mean of \(\log\max(|2+a x|,|b+c x|)\), which is at least \(\log\max(2,a)\).
Interchanging the variables gives \(b\le2\) as well as \(a\le2\).
In this range equality with \(\log 2\) holds exactly when \(|b+c x|\le |2+a x|\) on the whole unit circle, since a strict excess occurs on an open arc.
The difference of the squared moduli is affine in \(\Re x\), so the tests are \(|b+c|\le2+a\) and \(|b-c|\le2-a\).
For \(a=b=1\) these give \(c=1,2\) among nonzero integers.
For \(a=1,b=2\) they give \(c=1\), and for \(a=b=2\) they give \(c=2\).
The all-even choice violates the [first nondivisible coefficient condition](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
Thus the only triples \((a,b,c)\) are \((1,1,1),(1,2,1),(1,1,2)\).
For the first, \(|2+x|^2-|1+x|^2=3+2\Re x\ge1\) on the circle, and \((1+x)/(2+x)\) has supremum less than one on the closed disk by the maximum principle.
In particular \(2+x+y+xy\) has no zero on the closed bidisk.

## Product and reciprocal patterns

The pattern \((1,2,1)\) is \((2+x)(1+y)\).
With the actual signs and either ordering of the offsets, \(A/2=(1+s z^k/2)(1+t z^\ell)\) for \(s,t\in\{1,-1\}\) and \(\{k,\ell\}=\{d,e\}\).
This agrees with \(\Pi/2\) through \(h\), and the circle supremum of \(\Pi/2\) is bounded by the sum of the absolute coefficients of \(A\).
The [rational prefix theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) with \(S(w)=1+s w/2\), \(g=k\) and \(G=1+t z^\ell\) gives a positive excess, contradicting [escape on the fixed branch](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).

For the pattern \((1,1,2)\), write \(A=2+s z^d+t z^e+2st z^D\) in the actual variable.
Since \(z^D A(z^{-1})=st A(z)\), one has identically
\[
                    \Pi(z)=A(z)(1-st z^{N-D}).
\]
Suppose \(s=t\).
Then \(A(1)=4+2s\ne0\).
The symmetric Laurent polynomial \(A(z)A(z^{-1})\) is \(G(z+z^{-1})\) for an integer polynomial \(G\), by the recurrence for \(z^k+z^{-k}\).
Its interval supremum is at most \(36\) and \(G(2)=A(1)^2\).
On an escaping sequence the trace numerator \(C\) eventually has a single primitive nonmonic irreducible factor \(K\) of leading coefficient two and multiplicity one by the [trace multiplier theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement).
For any root \(\beta\) of \(K\), take \(z+z^{-1}=\beta\).
This \(z\) is nontorsion since the trace of a root of unity is an algebraic integer, and \(\beta\) has nonmonic primitive minimal polynomial.
It is a zero of \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\) and therefore of \(A\), since the other factor of \(\Pi\) has only torsion zeros.
It follows that \(K\mid G\) over the integers by Gauss's lemma.
The bounded admissible multiplier \(G\) contradicts the same theorem.
The argument uses the common trace lift for both original degree parities, and the chosen nontorsion root avoids both endpoints.

## Opposite signs and the endpoint condition

If \(t=-s\), then \(A(1)=0\) and \(\Pi=A(1+z^{N-D})\).
Differentiation at \(1\) gives \(-2C(2)=2A'(1)\), so
\[
         C(2)=2(d+e)-s(d-e),\qquad
         q-1\mid 2(d+e)-s(d-e)
\]
by the [trace endpoint identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#strip).
The absolute values of the coefficients alone do not supply a bounded admissible multiplier in this case.

Indeed take the formal specialization \(s=1,d=1\), with \(e=2m\to\infty\), and even \(N=2h+2\) with \(e=o(h)\).
Then \(A(z)/(1-z)=S_{e+1}(z)\) in the [explicit leading-two family](Yin_L_G0462_v45.md#family).
That family proves this reciprocal polynomial primitive irreducible with only nontorsion circle roots, and \(S_{e+1}(1)=3e+1\).
Thus \(S_{e+1}(z)=z^m K_e(z+z^{-1})\), with \(K_e\) integral and irreducible of leading coefficient two, since any nontrivial trace factorization would lift to a factorization of \(S_{e+1}\).
The other factor of \(\Pi/(1-z^2)\) is \((1+z^{N-e-1})/(1+z)\), monic reciprocal of even degree with torsion roots, because \(N-e-1\) is odd.
After dividing by its middle monomial it gives a monic integer trace polynomial.
Hence \(K_e\) is the entire primitive nonmonic part of the formal trace numerator and \(K_e(2)=3e+1\).
For every \(G\in K_e\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(G(2)\ne0\) one has \(\|G\|_{[-2,2]}\ge |G(2)|\ge 3e+1\).
These formal numerators realize the divergence demanded by the multiplier theorem.
Their realization by Pisot inputs remains open here.
