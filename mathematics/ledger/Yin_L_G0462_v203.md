# Low-degree integral transformations between Salem traces are powers

<a id="powers"></a>
## A degree floor for changing the Salem input

Let \(\sigma,\tau>1\) be Salem numbers, \(d=[\mathbb Q(\sigma):\mathbb Q]\), \(T_\sigma=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\), and \(T_\tau=\tau+\tau^{-1}\).
Suppose \(H\in\mathbb Z[X]\) has degree \(k\ge1\) and \(H(T_\sigma)=T_\tau\).
Put
\[
                  L=\log(2d)+2\log\sigma+1,\qquad
                  h=[\mathbb Q(T_\sigma):\mathbb Q(T_\tau)] .
\]
Either \(H=C_k\) and \(\tau=\sigma^k\), or \(k\gg\sqrt{d/L}\) with an absolute implied constant, where \(C_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
In particular for \(1<\sigma\le2,\ d\to\infty\), every transition with \(k=o(\sqrt{d/\log d})\) is eventually that power, without a size restriction on \(\tau\).
If \(\|H\|_{[-2,2]}\le M_0\) for a fixed \(M_0\), the alternative degree floor strengthens to \(k\gg_{M_0}d/L\).

There is a finite test using the actual conjugates.
From the neutral angles of \(\sigma\) on the full circle, discard those for which \(H(2\cos\theta)=T_\tau\).
Let \(G\) be the largest consecutive gap among the remaining angles, in radians and including the gap through angle zero.
Writing \(\kappa=\arccos(2/3)\), one has
\[
             kG\le 2\kappa\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=C_k,\quad
                       \tau=\sigma^k .                                  \tag{1}
\]
The angles in this test are nonempty.
Indeed each trace conjugate of \(T_\tau\) occurs exactly \(h\) times among the values of \(H\) at the conjugates of \(T_\sigma\), by extension of embeddings.
There is just one exterior input trace, and all conjugates of \(T_\tau\) other than itself belong to \((-2,2)\).
Thus exactly \(2(h-1)\) neutral angles were discarded, all remaining sampled magnitudes are strictly below two, and \(h\le k\) since \(T_\sigma\) satisfies \(H(X)-T_\tau\) over \(\mathbb Q(T_\tau)\).

## The sharp uniform norm threshold for integer polynomials

For every nonconstant integral \(H\) of degree \(k\),
\[
   \|H\|_{[-2,2]}<3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=C_k\ \text{or}\ H=-C_k.
                                                                       \tag{2}
\]
The threshold three is attained by \(C_k+1\) for every \(k\).
After changing sign write \(H=a_0+\sum_{j=1}^k a_j C_j\) with \(a_k>0\).
The coefficients are integers, since \(C_j\) is monic integral of degree \(j\) for \(j\ge1\).
Put \(U(\theta)=H(2\cos\theta)\), \(M=\|H\|_{[-2,2]}\), \(\theta_l=l\pi/k\) for \(0\le l<2k\), and \(V_l=(-1)^l U(\theta_l)\).
The grid mean of \(V_l\) is \(2a_k\), because multiplication by \((-1)^l\) moves both top frequencies to zero modulo \(2k\).
Its discrete Fourier coefficients at indices \(k-j\) for \(1\le j<k\) are \(a_j\), and the coefficient at \(k\) is \(a_0\), using the grid mean as normalization.
The nonnegative weights \(M-V_l\) have mean \(M-2a_k\), so their nonconstant Fourier coefficients have magnitudes at most that mean.
If \(M<3\), then \(a_k=1\) and every lower coefficient has magnitude below one, hence vanishes.

For the sampling step, a real trigonometric polynomial \(U\) of degree \(k\) with \(U(\theta_0)=M=\|U\|_\infty\) satisfies
\[
                   U(\theta_0+s)\ge M\cos(ks)
                         \qquad (|s|\le\pi/k).                           \tag{3}
\]
For a strict violation with \(0<s<\pi/k\), compare \(U(\theta_0+t)\) with \((1+\delta^3)M\cos(k(t+\delta))\) for small \(\delta>0\).
The difference has strictly alternating signs at the \(2k\) successive extrema of the taller cosine.
In its first descending interval \((-\delta,\pi/k-\delta)\) the difference is negative at the left endpoint, positive at zero, negative at \(s\), and positive at the right endpoint.
This gives at least \(2k+2\) distinct zeros in a period, impossible for a nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degree \(k\).
The negative side follows by reflection, and the two endpoints in (3) follow from the norm bound.
The cosine itself attains this lower envelope.

Apply (3) at an absolute maximum, with the sign of \(U\) chosen there and the nearest retained angle at distance at most \(G/2\).
Under (1) its sampled magnitude is at least \(2M/3\) and strictly below two, so (2) applies.
Since \(C_k(T_\sigma)=\sigma^k+\sigma^{-k}>2\), the original \(H\) has the positive sign, proving (1).
When \(T_\tau<3\), the maximum gap \(G_0\) among all neutral angles gives the alternative sufficient condition \(kG_0/2<\arccos(T_\tau/3)\), since every sampled magnitude is at most \(T_\tau\).

## An explicit bound after deleting the exceptional images

For \(1<\sigma\le2\), the [neutral arc count](Yin_L_G0462_v133.md#arcs) already bounds the unpruned mesh by \(O(\sqrt{\log(2d)/d})\).
For any \(\sigma>1\) the gap in (1) satisfies
\[
            2\sin(G/4)\le
                 \frac1{2d}+\frac{4\pi h}{d}
                              +2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{2d}} .                \tag{4}
\]
Indeed let \(\mu_\epsilon\) be the uniform orbit measure on all \(d\) conjugates after replacing each point by uniform measure on a Euclidean circle of radius \(\epsilon=1/(2d)\) about it.
For Haar probability \(m\) on the unit circle, the [discriminant regularization and Dirichlet inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test) give logarithmic energy \(I(\mu_\epsilon-m)\le L/d\), since the input's logarithmic Mahler measure is \(\log\sigma\).
For a compactly supported Lipschitz test \(\psi\), its integral against that difference is bounded in magnitude by \(\big((2\pi)^{-1}\int|\nabla\psi|^2\cdot L/d\big)^{1/2}\).

At the midpoint \(w\) of the empty arc, every retained angle has chord distance at least \(s=2\sin(G/4)\).
If \(s>\epsilon\), take the disk tent \(\psi(z)=(1-|z-w|/R)_+\) with \(R=s-\epsilon\).
Its normalized Dirichlet energy is \(1/2\), and its Haar integral is at least \(R/(2\pi)\) by integrating \(1-|t|/R\) on the arc \(|t|\le R\) about its center.
The smoothed good angles contribute zero, and the two real Salem roots and \(2(h-1)\) discarded angles contribute together at most \(2h/d\).
The resulting inequality proves (4), which holds directly for \(s\le\epsilon\).
Thus a nonpower polynomial \(H\ne C_k\) requires \(2\sin(\kappa/(2k))\) to be strictly less than the right side of (4).
If \(k<\sqrt d\), use \(h\le k\) there and \(\sin u\ge2u/\pi\) for \(0\le u\le\pi/2\) to obtain \(k\gg\sqrt{d/L}\).
Larger \(k\) already satisfy the same bound.

## A stronger floor for bounded circle norm

If \(H\ne C_k\) in the Salem transition and \(M=\|H\|_{[-2,2]}\), then \(M\ge3\) and
\[
                       k\ge\frac{d}{(400\pi)^2 M^3 L}.                 \tag{5}
\]
This also applies with \(M\) replaced by any upper bound on \(|a_0|+2\sum_{j\ge1}|a_j|\) in the integral basis used above.
The many grid points needed here follow from integrality, including for an arbitrarily large output trace.

Normalize the leading sign as in the [grid argument](#grid), and let \(\mathbb E\) denote its uniform grid mean.
If \(a_k\ge2\) then \(\mathbb E(V_l-2)_+\ge2\).
If \(a_k=1\), some lower coefficient is nonzero, since the two single Chebyshev choices either give \(H=C_k\) or do not map \(T_\sigma\) to \(T_\tau>2\).
The absolute grid mean \(\mathbb E|V_l-2|\) dominates that nonzero integral Fourier coefficient.
As \(\mathbb E(V_l-2)=0\), one gets \(\mathbb E(V_l-2)_+\ge1/2\).
In both cases the number \(J\) of grid points with \(V_l>2+1/4\) is at least \(k/(2M)\), by splitting the positive part at \(1/4\) and bounding it by \(M\) on the selected points.

By [Bernstein's trigonometric derivative inequality](../audits/literature/QZ19/Yin_U001.md#qz19-u001), \(\|U'\|_\infty\le kM\).
Within angular distance \(1/(8kM)\) of each selected point, \(|U|>2\), so none of the retained good angles occurs there.
Set \(R=1/(32kM)\) and first suppose \(d\ge16kM\), so \(\epsilon\le R\).
At each selected point put a disk tent of radius \(R\) and height one.
The disks are disjoint by the grid spacing, and \(R+\epsilon\le1/(16kM)\) is smaller than the chord distance of the nearest possible good angle, by \(2\sin(u/2)\ge2u/\pi\) for \(0\le u\le\pi\).
Their sum has normalized Dirichlet energy \(J/2\), Haar integral at least \(JR/(2\pi)\), and smoothed orbit integral at most \(2h/d\).
Divide the resulting energy inequality by \(J\), then multiply by \(kM\), using \(h\le k\) and \(J\ge k/(2M)\), to obtain
\[
             \frac1{64\pi}\le \frac{4kM^2}{d}
                                    +\sqrt{\frac{kM^3 L}{d}} .
\]
If \(y=kM^3L/d\le1\), the right side is at most \(5\sqrt y\), giving (5).
It is immediate if \(y>1\), and also if \(d<16kM\).

## Consequences for exact half-mass returns

Let \(e(\sigma)\) be the least power exponent giving [actual even-degree Pisot returns with square constant and limiting neutral branch mass one half](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v392.md#statement).
Every integral trace transformation in the range of (1) yielding such an admissible \(\tau\) has \(k\ge e(\sigma)\) and is exactly \(C_k\).
More generally, the least degree of any admissible integral trace transformation lies between \(\min\{e(\sigma),c\sqrt{d/L}\}\) and \(\min\{e(\sigma),d/2-1\}\) for an absolute \(c>0\).
For the upper bound take \(C_{e(\sigma)}\), or its remainder modulo the monic degree-\(d/2\) minimal polynomial of \(T_\sigma\).
The remainder is still integral and maps \(T_\sigma\) to the same irrational Salem trace.
Thus the least degree equals \(e(\sigma)\) when \(e(\sigma)\le c\sqrt{d/L}\).
Outside the degree range, different integral polynomials can represent the same value at a fixed trace, so admissibility alone need not determine the representing polynomial.
