# A positive branch-mass gap for the remaining fixed Salem inputs

<a id="gap"></a>
## A bound for every full return of a fixed Salem input

Fix a Salem number \(\sigma\) with monic minimal polynomial \(P_0(z)=z^sQ_0(z+z^{-1})\), where \(s=\deg Q_0\ge2\).
Write
\[
   q_*(z)=Q_0(z+z^{-1})=\sum_{j=-s}^{s}a_jz^j,\qquad
   K=\sum_j|a_j|,\qquad A=Q_0(0).
\]
Thus \(K\ge2\) because \(a_s=a_{-s}=1\).
Consider actual even-degree Pisot inputs with constant term \(d^2\), degree \(d\to\infty\), and fixed \(\sigma\) as exterior root of their full return.
Let \(\nu_d\) be the positive neutral branch measure on traces, including the neutral conjugates of the fixed Salem number.
If \(A\equiv2\bmod4\) and \(Q'_0(0)\) is odd, then every such sequence satisfies
\[
                  \liminf_d\,\nu_d([-2,2])\ \ge\ \frac12+\frac1{2K}.       \tag{1}
\]
The estimate allows arbitrary cyclotomic factors and all positive branch residues of each actual return.

More generally, for any fixed Salem input and any sequence on which the total mass tends to \(L<1/2+1/(2K)\), all sufficiently late returns in the sequence have a single complete midpoint grid of new neutral traces with exactly uniform branch weights.
If there are \(k\) new traces, their polynomial is \(C_k(2\cos t)=2\cos(kt)\) and each weight is \(1/(2k)\).
Together with the [odd central obstruction](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#fixed), the [even-grid construction](Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#half) and the [odd-grid construction](Yin_L_G0462_v181.md#statement), (1) decides the fixed-input half-mass realization question.
It is attainable precisely if \(4\mid A\), or if \(A\) and \(Q'_0(0)\) are both even.

<a id="twists"></a>
## Integer moments at moving frequencies

The [full return](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches) has simple roots and no roots at \(1,-1\).
After the Salem factor is removed, every remaining factor is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem, since its roots are algebraic integer units and all its conjugates lie on the circle.
Distinct circle roots give distinct traces except for the two members of each inverse pair.
Thus the new trace measure consists of strictly positive weights on a set of distinct torsion traces.
Lift this measure to the unit circle, dividing each weight equally between the two preimages, and call the lift \(\mu_d\).
Lift the entire \(\nu_d\) by the same convention.
The difference is a positive measure supported on the fixed finite set of neutral Salem conjugates.
Put \(m_{d,r}=2\int z^r\,d\mu_d\), which is real by inversion symmetry, for each \(r\in\mathbb Z\).
The [integral branch moments](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#moments) give
\[
                  2\int z^r q_*(z)\,d\mu_d
                     =\sum_j a_j m_{d,r+j}\in\mathbb Z.                \tag{2}
\]
For nonzero \(r\) the left side is the trace moment against \(Q_0 C_{|r|}\), and at zero it is twice the moment against \(Q_0\).
Each removed Salem trace is a zero of \(Q_0\), so removing its branch residue preserves (2) exactly.

Suppose the full mass tends to \(L<1/2+1/(2K)<1\), and put \(\delta=L-1/2\).
By the [fixed-input limit structure](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#structure), \(\delta\ge0\) and any weak limit of the full lift is \(h/2\) plus a positive atomic measure of mass \(\delta\), where \(h\) is Haar probability.
For any sequence of integers \(r_d\), pass to a subsequence on which the full lifts, the old masses and \(2z^{r_d}\mu_d\) converge weakly, writing \(\theta\) for the last limit.
Testing against continuous functions gives \(|\theta|\le 2\lim\mu_d\), since each twisting factor has modulus one.
Consequently the absolutely continuous part of \(\theta\) has density of modulus at most one and its singular part has total variation at most \(2\delta\).

For \(\lambda=q_*\theta\), all Fourier coefficients are integers by (2) at \(r_d+n\) for each fixed \(n\in\mathbb Z\).
Its singular part has total variation at most \(2K\delta<1\).
The Fourier coefficients of its absolutely continuous part tend to zero in both tails by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma.
Thus all sufficiently high absolute frequencies of \(\lambda\) have integer coefficients of modulus strictly less than one and are zero.
Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients gives \(\lambda=D(z)h\) for an integral Laurent polynomial \(D\), and the density bound gives \(|D(z)|\le|q_*(z)|\) on the whole circle by continuity.

The Salem polynomial has at least one neutral conjugate, also when \(s=2\).
At such a conjugate \(D\) vanishes, so irreducibility of \(P_0\) and monic division after clearing powers of \(z\) give \(D=q_*H\) for \(H\in\mathbb Z[z^{\pm1}]\).
On the circle \(|H|\le1\), and Parseval's identity makes the sum of squares of its integer coefficients at most one.
Thus \(H=0\) or \(H=\pm z^j\).
The absolutely continuous part of \(\theta\) is \(Hh\), as \(q_*\) is nonzero almost everywhere.
Its integral belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and the remaining part has variation at most \(2\delta\).
Applying this to a sequence of frequencies witnessing any failure of uniformity proves
\[
       \limsup_d\ \sup_{r\in\mathbb Z}
         \operatorname{dist}(m_{d,r},\{-1,0,1\})\le 2\delta .           \tag{3}
\]

<a id="coset"></a>
## Exact inversion on the finite group of new circle roots

For each sufficiently large \(d\), the nonempty support of \(\mu_d\) lies in the group of \(N_d\)-th roots of unity for some \(N_d\), so its coefficients \(m_{d,r}\) are \(N_d\)-periodic.
By (3) and \(2\delta<1/K\le1/2\), round them to \(N_d\)-periodic \(h_{d,r}\in\{-1,0,1\}\) with uniform error strictly less than \(1/K\).
Equation (2) is integral, as is \(\sum_j a_j h_{d,r+j}\).
Their difference has modulus less than one, giving \(\sum_j a_j(m_{d,r+j}-h_{d,r+j})=0\) for every \(r\).
The eigenvalues of this convolution on the finite cyclic group are \(q_*(z)\) at its roots of unity, all nonzero since \(P_0\) is irreducible and nontorsion.
Finite Fourier inversion therefore gives \(m_{d,r}=h_{d,r}\) for all \(r\).
Positivity now gives \(m_{d,0}=1\).

Choose \(z_0\) in the support and let \(G\) be the subgroup generated by its ratios \(z/z_0\) as \(z\) ranges through the support.
Since \(2\mu_d\) is a probability, equality in the triangle inequality gives \(|m_{d,r}|=1\) exactly when \(z^r\) is constant on its support, equivalently when \(r\) annihilates \(G\).
Then \(m_{d,r}=z_0^r\), and every other coefficient is zero by the three-value conclusion.
These are precisely the Fourier coefficients of uniform probability on \(z_0G\), proving equality by finite Fourier inversion.
Writing \(n=|G|\), that coset is the set \(z^n=z_0^n\).
Inversion symmetry forces \(z_0^n=\pm1\).
The choice \(+1\) contains the forbidden circle root \(1\), and the choice \(-1\) with odd \(n\) contains the forbidden root \(-1\).
Hence \(n=2k\) and the coset is \(z^{2k}=-1\).
Projecting its uniform probability and restoring the factor \(1/2\) gives the stated \(k\) midpoint weights \(1/(2k)\).

Along these degrees \(k=d/2-s\to\infty\).
For the remaining parity the [endpoint congruence](Yin_L_G0462_v194.md#endpoint) precludes such uniform weights for every \(k\ge2\).
A liminf strictly below the right side of (1) would give a sequence converging to such an \(L\), which proves the bound.
For example \(Q_0(x)=x^2-3x-2\) has roots \((3\pm\sqrt{17})/2\), with the larger above two and the smaller in \((-2,2)\).
The discriminant of \(z^2-Tz+1\) is negative at the smaller embedding, giving a degree-four Salem number at the larger trace \(T\).
Here \(q_*=z^2+z^{-2}-3(z+z^{-1})\), so (1) gives a total branch mass liminf of at least \(9/16\).
The constant in (1) depends on the fixed input, and its optimal value for this example and for the remaining parity in general is open.
