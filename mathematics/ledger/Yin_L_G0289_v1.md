### 2. Complete periodic diagonal as a changing arithmetic quotient

For a chronological word w=(eta_1,...,eta_n) put

    S(w)=sum_(j=1)^n eta_j alpha^(n-j),
    p(w)=product_(j=1)^n mu(eta_j),
    x(w)=-S(w)/(alpha^n-1).                              (1)

Since alpha is nontorsion, alpha^n-1 is nonzero.
The affine map defined by w has the UNIQUE fixed point x(w) in Q(alpha).
It is an actual integral fixed point exactly when S(w) belongs to I_n=(alpha^n-1)O.
If x(w) belongs to D, every state on its length-n closed path belongs to D: append fixed paths from zero to x(w) and back.
Thus

    t_n:=sum_(x in D) K^n(x,x)
        =sum_(w in{-1,0,1}^n) p(w) 1_(x(w) in D),
    0<=t_n<=1.                                         (2)

There are at most3^n states with nonzero diagonal at this n.
This proves finiteness without truncating the state space or counting each word at several possible fixed points.
For a general nontorsion unit, (2) is a restricted congruence-return identity; integrality alone need not imply actual core membership.

There is an exact simplification under a PROVED full-strip Salem hypothesis.
Let alpha=beta in(1,2) be Salem and suppose the actual core is

    D={x in O: |x(beta)|<B, |x(beta^(-1))|<S},
    B=1/(beta-1), S=beta/(beta-1).                       (3)

This is precisely the conditional theorem retained in [L170](Yin_L_G0170_v1.md#exact-salem-return-strip), whose narrow co-return premise has been proved for the fixed quartic, original Lehmer and, by L199, the stated sextic input.
We assert the following identity only for inputs with this actual equality, not for every Salem number.

At the expanding real embedding, (1) and the finite geometric sum give |x(w)(beta)|<=B.
Equality can hold only if every eta_j is the same extremal sign +1 or -1: every coefficient in that real sum is strictly positive.
At the contracting embedding the same calculation gives |x(w)(beta^(-1))|<=S, with the same equality condition.
Every other word therefore gives STRICT inequalities at both places.
If its fixed point is integral, (3) puts it and its entire periodic orbit in the actual core, regardless of neutral radius.
The two constant extremal words have fixed points +/-B, which are excluded from D.
They are integral exactly when B belongs to O.
Set

    epsilon_P=1 if (beta-1)^(-1) belongs to O, else0,
    Q_n=O/I_n, D_n=|Q_n|=|Norm(beta^n-1)|.

Multiplication by beta^n-1 is an injective integral endomorphism of the rank-d order, so Q_n is finite with the stated nonzero determinant.
The constant extremal words satisfy the congruence exactly when epsilon_P=1.
Hence

    t_n=Pr_original{S_n=0 in Q_n}-2 epsilon_P 4^(-n).
                                                               (4)

All probabilities and the boundary subtraction are literal.
No finite quotient is held fixed as n grows.
In the fixed quartic and original Lehmer cases P(1)=-1, so beta-1 is a unit and epsilon_P=1.
For another full-strip input its integrality must be checked separately.

Finite-character orthogonality also gives

    t_n+2 epsilon_P 4^(-n)
      =D_n^(-1) sum_(chi in Q_n_hat)
          product_(j=0)^(n-1) cos^2(pi chi(beta^j)),
    D_n=product_(all embeddings v)|v(beta)^n-1|.        (5)

Here chi takes values in R/Z and the cosine square is well-defined.
Indeed the characteristic function of one original difference digit is 1/2+(1/4)e^(2pi it)+(1/4)e^(-2pi it)=cos^2(pi t).
Averaging a character over the finite dual gives one at the zero residue and zero elsewhere: for a nonzero residue choose a character nontrivial there, and translate the sum by it.
Taking expectations proves(5).
This is a finite exact identity, with no mixing assertion, independent-conjugate assumption, or replacement of the original law.
In particular its trivial character supplies the valid but possibly weak lower bound t_n>=D_n^(-1)-2 epsilon_P4^(-n).

### 3. Removing cycles avoiding zero: the exact coefficient identity

Let B_0 be the kernel K killed on hitting zero, acting on D minus {0}.
Define its finite diagonal sum and the nonnegative difference

    t_n^avoid=sum_(x!=0)(B_0^n)(x,x),
    c_n=t_n-t_n^avoid>=0.                              (6)

Each summand counts original words; t_n^avoid counts precisely the rooted closed paths never visiting zero, including their initial state.
Both sums are finite by the unique-fixed-point argument.
Thus c_n is the full original weight of rooted periodic paths that DO visit zero, with each chronological starting state/word counted once.
It is not p_n in general.

The exact formal coefficient identity is

    c_n=n sum_(ell=1)^n (f^(*ell))_n/ell,
    sum_(n>=1)c_n z^n/n=-log(1-F(z))=log U(z)
                                      for0<=z<R.       (7)

Here convolution counts ordered complete first-return words with their original probabilities.
We give the full marking argument to handle periodic repetitions.
A rooted closed path of length n which visits zero has a positive integer L equal to its number of zero departures among times0,...,n-1.
Mark one of these L departures, rotate the chronological word to put that mark first, and retain the old starting-time offset in {0,...,n-1}.
The rotated path is rooted at zero, has exactly L consecutive first excursions, and the original word weight is unchanged.
Conversely, a zero-rooted path with L excursions and any old offset determines the marked path.
If cyclic rotations repeat, both constructions still retain the integer offset and chosen zero departure, so multiplicities are equal.
Weight each marked path by 1/L.
Summing first over all marks on a fixed original rooted path gives its weight once.
Summing instead over the n offsets of zero-rooted paths with ell excursions gives n(f^(*ell))_n/ell.
This proves the first identity without a free action assumption.

For each coefficient the sums are finite.
For real0<=z<R, F(z)<1; nonnegative summation and the elementary series sum_(ell>=1)F(z)^ell/ell=-log(1-F(z)) prove the second identity.
No global convergence of sum t_n z^n/n or sum t_n^avoid z^n/n is required separately: their NONNEGATIVE coefficient difference is what converges.
In particular, when R>1 neither series is silently evaluated by subtracting two infinities.
Nor is a determinant of an infinite operator being invoked.

The same marking proof gives a useful bridge form.
For a zero-rooted length-n returned word let L_0(w) be its number of zero departures.
Then

    c_n=n sum_(w:0 to0, |w|=n) p(w)/L_0(w),
    p_n<=c_n<=n p_n.                                  (8)

The denominator records actual zero visits; it cannot be replaced by n or by one without its corresponding inequality.
Differentiating(7) strictly below R, or multiplying its formal coefficients, also gives

    c_n=sum_(j=1)^n j f_j p_(n-j).                      (9)

These are classical rooted-loop/renewal identities in the exact present arithmetic graph, not a new general loop theorem.
Equations(2)--(5) and the explicit avoidance requirement describe how that identity is connected here to actual algebraic congruences.

### 4. Critical loop defect, full mean costs and an effective finite lower test

Assume from now on F(R)=1, as L285 proves unconditionally for the actual quartic.
Put b_j=R^j f_j, a_j=R^j p_j and C_n=R^n c_n.
The b_j form a probability law, a_0=1 and0<a_j<=1.
Define the extended critical mean mu_R=sum j b_j.
For0<u<1, (7) gives the exact signed-series expression

    mu(u):=(1-F(Ru))/(1-u)=sum_(j>=1)b_j sum_(k=0)^(j-1)u^k,
    log mu(u)=sum_(n>=1)(1-C_n)u^n/n,
    mu(u) increases to mu_R.                            (10)

The signed series is absolutely convergent at each u<1: its absolute sum is bounded by sum u^n/n plus sum C_n u^n/n, the latter equal to log U(Ru).
Thus it does not subtract undefined endpoint sums.
The first expression proves monotonicity and positivity mu(u)>=1 even though individual coefficients 1-C_n need not be positive.
In particular finite critical mean requires a finite Abel limit of the full harmonic deficit in(10), and that limit is exactly log mu_R.
A growing unbounded defect proves infinite mean.
This is a specific arithmetic-loop route to the existing mean question, not a claim that recurrence already settles the defect.

Equation(9) provides a useful necessary coefficient test:

    C_n=sum_(j=1)^n j b_j a_(n-j)
        <=sum_(j=1)^n j b_j<=mu_R.                      (11)

Consequently unbounded critical ROOT-VISITING diagonal differences C_n force infinite mean.
The adjective matters: R^n t_n alone is not C_n, and an upper bound on t_n^avoid sufficient to use this test is a genuine remaining obligation.
Formula(5) supplies the complete changing-modulus quantity t_n, not that missing avoidance estimate.
If q_neu<=2, L285 also gives

    sum_(n>=1) C_n u^n/n=log U(Ru)
        >=log[1+c_P log(1/(1-u))].                     (12)

This confirms divergence of the loop sum but does not make the deficit(10) bounded or divergent; the two logarithmic scales must not be conflated.

There is a separate universal degree expense at critical recurrence.
For1<=n<=d the only zero-return word is all zero, since a nonzero polynomial of degree below d cannot vanish at alpha.
Therefore f_1=1/2 and f_n=0 for2<=n<=d.
If any nontrivial first excursion exists, its least length ell_* is at least d+1.
Hence

    mu_R>=1+d(1-R/2),
    mu_R>=1+(ell_*-1)(1-R/2) when ell_* exists.          (13)

To verify this, b_1=R/2 and every other length is at least d+1, or ell_*.
If no other first return exists, F(R)=1 forces R=2 and mu_R=1, consistent with the first bound.
Under finite-mean attainment L201 gives pi_*(0)=1/mu_R; the degree cost cannot be dropped from that stationary mass.
For a class with small m and R<=exp(m), it forces mu_R>=1+d(1-exp(m)/2) whenever that latter expression is useful.
It is a cost, not a contradiction or a uniform entropy floor.

The unknown radius can be paid explicitly in finite lower tests.
For an integer N>=1 define

    L_N(r)=(N+1)-sum_(j=1)^N(N+1-j)f_j r^j.

At the actual R,

    L_N(R)=E_b min(tau,N+1) increases to mu_R.           (14)

This follows by splitting the expectation at N and using sum b_j=1; it also shows L_N(R)>=1.
The function L_N(r) is decreasing for r>0.
Choose any integer M>=1 and use L255's full operator expense

    E_M=C_non[2+sqrt(2pi M)]^(q_neu),
    r_M=p_M^(-1/M), epsilon_M=log(E_M)/M.

Its original all-time comparison supplies 0<=log(r_M/R)<=epsilon_M.
There is consequently a fully finite certified lower bound and error estimate

    L_N(r_M)<=mu_R,
    0<=L_N(R)-L_N(r_M)
        <=N(N+1) epsilon_M exp(N epsilon_M).            (15)

Indeed put delta=log(r_M/R)>=0.
The difference equals sum_(j<=N)(N+1-j)b_j(exp(j delta)-1), at most N(N+1)delta exp(Ndelta)sum_(j<=N)b_j.
That last sum is at most one.
This proves(15) without an estimate on unobserved first returns or a presumed value h=m.
Coefficients f_1,...,f_N are exactly recovered from original p_0,...,p_N by the finite recursion f_n=p_n-sum_(j=1)^(n-1)f_j p_(n-j).

For a FIXED unit input, one may take M=N^3.
Then epsilon_M=O_P(log N/N^3), so the error in(15) tends to zero.
Thus max{1,L_N(r_(N^3))} converges to mu_R, with infinity allowed; taking successive maxima yields increasing certified lower bounds with the same limit.
This is an exact finite-data lower test with the complete additional observation-time and degree/nonneutral costs.
It does not give an upper bound for the mean, a finite stopping rule for proving it finite, or a feasible enumeration claim: computing p_(N^3) exactly may itself be prohibitively expensive, and no such computation is performed here.
Across varying inputs, C_non and q_neu in epsilon_M must remain; the fixed-input limit is not asserted uniform.

### 5. Complete actual quartic control and a finite critical-mean certificate

Take beta>1 with P=t^4-t^3-t^2-t+1.
Its full-strip core is actually established by L169 and L170.
The polynomial identity

    P(t)=(t-1)(t^3-1)-t^2

implies (beta-1)(beta^3-1)=beta^2.
Since beta is a unit, beta-1 and beta^3-1 are both units: their inverses are respectively (beta^3-1)beta^(-2) and (beta-1)beta^(-2), actual order elements.
Thus Q_3 is the trivial group and epsilon_P=1.
Every one of the27 original three-digit words has an integral periodic fixed point, and only the two constant extremal words lie on the excluded real boundaries.
Equation(4) gives

    t_3=1-2*4^(-3)=31/32.                             (16)

The map from a length-three word to its fixed point is injective here: equality would give a polynomial of degree at most two vanishing at beta, impossible at actual degree four.
Thus there are25 actual periodic fixed states counted at this time after the two boundary states are removed.
If any nonzero periodic path at time three visited zero, cyclically rotating its word to that visit would give a nonzero zero-return polynomial of degree at most two.
That is also impossible.
Only the all-zero word visits zero.
Its probability is 2^(-3)=1/8.
Therefore all remaining24 periodic paths avoid zero and

    t_3^avoid=31/32-1/8=27/32,
    c_3=p_3=1/8,
    C_3=(R/2)^3.                                    (17)

This is a complete analytic enumeration of all27 words, not a sample or a numerical search.
It demonstrates a large actual zero-avoiding contribution even at the first nontrivial periodic modulus.
In particular (16) by itself is neither the rooted return probability nor the rooted critical loop coefficient in(10)--(11).
A proposal that identifies those objects is false on this fixed nontorsion input.
It does not demonstrate infinite critical mean: (17) is below one because R<2.

For an exact small-time mean certificate, every length-five return polynomial is hP with integer constant h and height at most one.
The leading coefficient forces h=0,+1,-1.
The two nonzero words +/-P have all five digits nonzero and have no earlier zero return, by minimal degree.
Thus

    f_1=1/2, f_2=f_3=f_4=0, f_5=2*4^(-5)=1/512.

Equation(14) at N=5, together with the already adopted R<=beta, gives

    mu_R>=6-(5/2)R-R^5/512
           >=6-(5/2)beta-beta^5/512>3/2.              (18)

For the final strict rational comparison, beta<7/4: divide P(t) by t^2, write u=t+t^(-1), and use the increasing u for t>1.
