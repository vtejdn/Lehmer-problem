# Collision entropy and Fourier moments above a conductor cutoff

<a id="moments"></a>
## The exponential rate of a complete Fourier moment

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit with minimum \(P\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), \(O=\mathcal O_K\), and put \(m=\log M(P)>0\).
Write \(J_n=(\alpha^n-1)O\), \(G_n=O/J_n\), and let \(A_n(\chi)=|\mathbb E\chi(S_n)|\) for the fair binary sum \(S_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\alpha^j\).
For an integer \(k\ge1\) let \(T_{n,k}\) be the sum of \(k\) independent copies of \(S_n\), let \(p_{n,k}=\Pr(T_{n,k}=T'_{n,k})\) for an independent copy, and put \(h_{k,2}=\lim_n -n^{-1}\log p_{n,k}\).
Equality of both independent blocks suffices for equality after concatenation, so \(p_{n+r,k}\ge p_{n,k}p_{r,k}\) and the limit exists by subadditivity, as in the [block collision argument](Yin_L_G0089_v1.md#binary-flow-renyi-rate-comparison).
Then for each fixed \(k\),
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G_n} A_n(\chi)^{2k}=m-h_{k,2}.
\]
Indeed finite-group Plancherel identifies the sum exactly with \(|G_n|\Pr(T_{n,k}\equiv T'_{n,k}\bmod J_n)\).
Each fiber on the support of \(T_{n,k}\) contains at most \((2n)^{O_{P,k}(1)}\) distinct values by the [periodic fiber argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v578.md#quotient), since its difference digits now range from \(-k\) to \(k\).
Squaring the sum of atom masses in each fiber bounds the reduced collision probability below by \(p_{n,k}\) and above by this fiber-size bound times \(p_{n,k}\), by Cauchy–Schwarz.
The group size has logarithm \(nm+O_P(\log(2n))\), giving the limit.

<a id="cutoff"></a>
## Removing the characters of small conductor

Write \(\mathfrak a_\chi=\{z\in O:\chi(zy)=1\ \forall y\in O\}\), and put \(\delta_k=m-h_{k,2}\).
If \(0\le\kappa<\delta_k/m\), then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log
 \sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G_n\\\log N\mathfrak a_\chi>\kappa mn}} A_n(\chi)^{2k}=\delta_k.
\]
The conductor divides \(J_n\) and a given divisor \(\mathfrak a\) accounts for at most \(N\mathfrak a\) characters, since these factor through the additive group of \(O/\mathfrak a\).
Writing \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), the number of ideal divisors is at most \(\tau(NJ_n)^d\), since at most \(d\) prime ideals lie above each rational prime and each valuation in \(J_n\) is at most the corresponding valuation of its norm.
For any \(\eta>0\), \(\tau(q)\ll_\eta q^\eta\) follows from \(a+1\le2^a\le p^{\eta a}\) at sufficiently large primes and a finite product of constants at the remaining primes.
Thus the ideal divisor count is \(\exp(o_P(n))\).
The discarded contribution is at most \(\exp(\kappa mn+o_P(n))\) because every amplitude is at most one, proving the formula by the strict gap in the exponents.

<a id="neutral"></a>
## Neutral inputs and dissociated families

Suppose \(P\) has a unit-circle root.
Every character of \(G_n\) restricting nontrivially to \(R\) pulls back to a nontrivial character of \(R/(\alpha^n-1)R\) with the same amplitude, hence all these amplitudes tend uniformly to zero by the [digit-order maximum theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v579.md#statement).
The characters trivial on \(R\) have amplitude one, and their number is \([O:R+J_n]\le [O:R]=I\).
Their conductors contain \(IO\), since \(IO\subset R\) and \(IO\) is an ideal of \(O\), giving \(N\mathfrak a_\chi\le I^d\).
In particular every fixed positive conductor fraction excludes these exceptional characters for large periods.

For any dissociated set \(\Gamma_n\subset\widehat G_n\), its subset products are all distinct, so \(2^{|\Gamma_n|}\le|G_n|\).
For the continuous function \(g\) of the [entropy inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v578.md#statement), \(g(0)=0\) and \(g(1)=\log2\).
Consequently \(\sum_{\chi\in\Gamma_n}g(A_n(\chi))=o_P(n)\) uniformly in the dissociated set, since at most \(I\) summands are exceptional and all remaining summands tend uniformly to zero.
When \(\delta_k>0\), the full moment above any strict cutoff as above still grows exponentially.

<a id="exterior"></a>
## One exterior conjugate

If \(P\) has just one exterior conjugate, set \(q=\lceil\log2/m\rceil\).
The [spaced exponents](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v577.md#statement) \(0,q,2q,\ldots<n\) give distinct binary sums, since at the exterior conjugate the last differing digit dominates the finite geometric sum of its predecessors even when its base has modulus exactly two.
Conditioning on the remaining digits of all walks, leaving these digits of the first walk free, bounds every atom of \(T_{n,k}\) by \(2^{-\lceil n/q\rceil}\).
Since the collision probability is at most the largest atom, for every \(k\ge1\)
\[
 0\le\delta_k\le m-\frac{\log2}{\lceil\log2/m\rceil}\le\frac{m^2}{\log2+m}.
\]
Nonnegativity follows from the trivial character in the complete moment.
The upper bound is uniform in the degree and the number of walks.
For a real Salem input below two, the [greedy atom bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0235_v1.md#greedy-atom-bound) also gives \(\delta_k\le m-m\log(2)/\log(1+e^m)\), by conditioning on the other walks.
Taking the smaller of this bound and the ceiling bound preserves the exact value of the latter at each input and improves the upper coefficient near zero to \(1/(2\log 2)\).
