# The uniform first-coefficient profile under a single Salem delay

<a id="uniform-delay"></a>
## Exact sign and threshold for every delay

Write \(F\) for the signed Pisot quotient with \(F(0)=q\ge2\), and \(K\) for its [normalized function](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit).
Solving the normalization at \(K=1\) gives \(F=N/G\), where
\[
 N=z^3-qz^2+q,\qquad G=1-qz-2z^2+qz^3,\qquad A=G^*=N-2z .
\]
Indeed \(F/(1+zF)=N/(1-z^2)^2\) and \(G+zN=(1-z^2)^2\).
The quotient is reduced since \(N(1)=1,\ N(-1)=-1\).
For all integers \(q\ge2,\ n\ge1\) and \(\eta=\pm1\), form the monic delayed polynomial
\[
                            P=z^n A+\eta N .
\]
After removing all cyclotomic factors, \(P\) has exactly one irreducible factor, of multiplicity one and constant coefficient \(-q\).
Its exterior roots consist of a single positive \(\theta>q\) precisely when
\[
                         \eta=(-1)^n,\qquad n\le4q+4 .                \tag{1}
\]
In the other cases there is also a negative root below \(-1\).
In particular no irreducible positive-constant Pisot factor can be extracted anywhere in this family.
The main researcher proposed testing the uniform profile by delayed constructions, and the proof contributor obtained the exact factorization and sign criterion.

<a id="root-count"></a>
## Counting exterior and boundary roots

There is one root of \(A\) in each of \((-\infty,-1),(0,1),(q,\infty)\), by its cubic leading term and its values \(1,q,-1,-q\) at \(-1,0,1,q\).
On \(z=e^{it}\) one has
\[
                       |A|^2-|N|^2=4-4\Re(z^{-1}N)=8\sin^2t .
\]
Rouché's theorem gives \(z^nA+s\eta N\) exactly two exterior roots for \(0<s<1\).
By continuity \(P\) has at most two, counted with multiplicities, and its only possible circle roots are \(1,-1\).
At \(1\) the plus sign gives a simple root with derivative \(4-4q-n<0\), and the minus sign gives the value \(-2\).
Thus \(P\) is negative just to the right of one for either sign, and \(P(q)=-q^{n+1}+\eta q<0\).
Its positive leading coefficient and the count of at most two exterior roots give exactly one simple root \(\theta>q\) on this ray.
Any remaining exterior root is negative and simple, by complex conjugation and the same count.

Put \(p=(-1)^n\).
The sign near \(-\infty\) is \(-p\) and \(P(-1)=p-\eta\).
When \(\eta\ne p\) the opposite signs force the negative exterior root.
When \(\eta=p\) we have \(P'(-1)=p(4q+4-n)\).
For \(n<4q+4\) the signs on the left of \(-1\) and at \(-\infty\) agree, ruling out a simple negative exterior root, whereas for \(n>4q+4\) they differ.
At equality \(n\) is even, \(\eta=1\), and direct differentiation gives \(P''(-1)=-8\).
The root at \(-1\) is double and both signs on the negative exterior ray near its ends are negative.
This proves (1), including its endpoint.

The cyclotomic divisor therefore consists only of the indicated factors \(z-1,z+1\), with the multiplicities just computed.
The monic quotient \(h\) has constant \(-q\), since a single factor \(z-1\) occurs precisely for \(\eta=1\), and all factors \(z+1\) have constant one.
Every nonconstant monic integer factor of \(h\) needs an exterior root, as its constant is a nonzero integer and it has no circle roots.
In the one-exterior case this proves irreducibility and the strict Pisot property.

<a id="irreducible"></a>
## Excluding a separate factor with a negative exterior root

If the two exterior roots belonged to different irreducible factors, the absolute value \(t\) of the negative root would be a Pisot number.
At \(z=-t\), the defining equation for \(P\) implies
\[
 \left|1-\frac{2}{t^2+q(t-t^{-1})}\right|=t^{-n}.
\]
Put \(c=13/10\).
The denominator increases for \(t\ge c\) and exceeds two already at \(q=2,\ t=c\).
Consequently \(t<c\) whenever \(1-2/[c^2+q(c-c^{-1})]>c^{-n}\).
Such a \(t\) cannot be Pisot.
In fact \(c\) is less than the plastic number, so the [nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number) excludes a nonreciprocal Pisot root up to \(c\).
A reciprocal Pisot root has just two conjugates, since another inverse pair would require another exterior root or a unit-circle root.
Its integral trace is at least three, which also excludes this interval.

The strict numerical sufficient condition increases with \(q\) on the left and decreases with \(n\) on the right.
It holds for all \(n\ge5\) already at \(q=2\).
For \(n=1,2,3,4\) the exceptional integers range from 2 through \(13,6,3,2\), respectively.
They all satisfy \(n<4q+4\), so only \(\eta=-(-1)^n\) has two exterior roots there.
In that case there is no factor \(z+1\) and the residual is \(P/(z-1)\) for odd \(n\), or \(P\) for even \(n\).
For \(q\) in increasing order within each row, the primes in the following exact check certify that every such monic residual is irreducible over the indicated finite field, hence over \(\mathbb Q\).

~~~python
from fractions import Fraction
from sympy import Poly, symbols

z = symbols("z")
c = Fraction(13, 10)
primes = {
    1: [3, 2, 3, 2, 5, 2, 3, 2, 3, 2, 11, 2],
    2: [3, 37, 3, 3, 5],
    3: [3, 5],
    4: [3],
}

def lower(q):
    return 1 - 2 / (c*c + q*(c - 1/c))

assert lower(2) > c**(-5)
for n, row in primes.items():
    last = len(row) + 1
    assert lower(last) < c**(-n) < lower(last + 1)
    for q, prime in enumerate(row, 2):
        N = z**3 - q*z**2 + q
        sign = (-1)**(n + 1)
        P = Poly(z**n*(N - 2*z) + sign*N, z)
        h = P.exquo(Poly(z - 1, z)) if sign == 1 else P
        assert Poly(h.as_expr(), z, modulus=prime).is_irreducible
~~~

The coefficient and delay restrictions above were checked by exact fractions, and all twenty reductions passed.
Each exterior root is simple, so the resulting unique irreducible factor has multiplicity one.

<a id="haar-limit"></a>
## Exact profiles on the negative-constant branch

For the full quotient \(h\) the signed rational function is \(F=-h/h^*=\eta P/P^*\), because reversal negates a factor \(z-1\) and preserves a factor \(z+1\).
Hence
\[
 F=\frac{N+\eta z^nA}{G+\eta z^nN^*},\qquad
             K(z)=\frac{1-\eta z^n}{1+\eta z^n}.
\]
For the second identity use \(AG-NN^*=-2z(1-z^2)^2\) and \(N^*+zA=G+zN=(1-z^2)^2\) in the normalization.
The function \(K\) is the Herglotz transform of the uniform probability on \(u^n=-\eta\).
At the positive exterior root the defining equation also gives
\[
          (\theta-q)(\theta-\theta^{-1})
            =\frac{1-\eta\theta^{-n}}{1+\eta\theta^{-n}},
\]
so \(\theta-q=q^{-1}+O(q^{-2})\) uniformly in \(n\ge1\) as \(q\to\infty\).
Indeed \(\theta>q\) first bounds the right side by \(1+O(1/q)\) and the excess by \(O(1/q)\), after which substitution gives the expansion.
Taking, for instance, \(n=2q,\ \eta=1\) satisfies (1), and the actual strict Pisot polynomials of negative constant then have \(K\to1\) locally uniformly on the open disk.
This gives an exact simultaneous threshold for these moving torsion measures, complementing the [fixed-profile construction](Yin_L_G0462_v38.md#opposite-sign).
It leaves the occurrence of the uniform profile for unrestricted positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomials open.
