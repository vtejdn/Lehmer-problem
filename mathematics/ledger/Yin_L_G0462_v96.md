# Uniform positive excess for the reciprocal eight-term trace at the sum of two offsets

<a id="reciprocal"></a>
## The opposite-sign case

On the positive-constant Pisot branch \(j=2\), all actual eight-term trace lifts with lower exponents \(0<d<e<D=d+e\) have bounded \(q\) and \(\theta-q\) bounded below by an absolute positive constant.
The [coefficient classification and factor exclusions](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#shapes) and the [strictly stable case](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#sum-offset) reduce this assertion to the opposite-sign lower polynomial
\[
  A(x,y)=2+s x+t y+2stxy,\qquad s=\pm1,\quad t=-s,\qquad
  S_i(z)=A(z^d,z^e)/2 .
\]
Assume along a sequence that \(q\to\infty\), equivalently \(\theta-q\to0\) by the [branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second), and pass to fixed signs.
Write \(h\) for the trace degree and \(N=2h+2\).
By the [eight-term exponent theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight), \(e=o(h)\) and \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
The [exact reciprocal factorization](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#opposite) gives \(\Pi/2=S_i(1+z^{N-D})\), hence agreement with \(S_i\) through \(h\).
The actual monic integer denominator satisfies \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\), where \(J_i\) is the constant-one Herglotz series of a symmetric real signed circle measure \(\omega_i\) of mass one with negative mass tending to zero, as in the [inverse-measure representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality).

## A uniformly bounded measure for the reciprocal series

Write \(R_i=1/S_i\) and
\[
   F_i(z)=-t z^e\,\frac{1+2s z^d}{2+s z^d},\qquad
   R_i(z)=\frac{2}{2+s z^d}\frac1{1-F_i(z)} .
\]
Since \((1+2s w)/(2+s w)=s(w+s/2)/(1+s w/2)\), \(F_i\) is a finite Blaschke product of degree \(D\) vanishing at zero.
It has \(D\) simple preimages \(\zeta\) of \(1\) on the unit circle, because its phase there increases by \(2\pi D\), with derivative \(e+d(1-1/4)/|\zeta^d+s/2|^2>0\).
The strictly proper rational function \((1-F_i)^{-1}\) has the partial fraction expansion \(\sum_{\zeta}\lambda_\zeta/(1-z/\zeta)\), with \(\lambda_\zeta=1/(\zeta F_i'(\zeta))>0\) by this derivative.
Their sum is one by evaluation at zero.
The only poles of the strictly proper \(R_i=2/A(z^d,z^e)\) are these same simple poles.
Thus, with \(c_\zeta=2\lambda_\zeta/(2+s\zeta^d)\), one has
\[
   R_i(z)=\sum_\zeta\frac{c_\zeta}{1-z/\zeta},\qquad
   \sum_\zeta|c_\zeta|\le2,\qquad
   r_n=\sum_\zeta c_\zeta\zeta^{-n}\quad(n\in\mathbb Z).
\]
For \(n\ge0\), \(r_n=[z^n]R_i\).
For any fixed \(C>0\),
\[
          \sum_{0\le n\le Ce}|r_n|
             \le 2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor C\rfloor}3^k .
\]
Indeed \(2/(2+s z^d)\) has absolute coefficient sum at most two, \(F_i\) at most three, and \(F_i^k\) begins at degree \(ke\) in the geometric expansion.
For the negative indices expand the same partial fractions at infinity.
The coefficient of \(z^{-n}\) is \(-r_{-n}\) for \(n\ge1\), whereas reciprocity gives \(R_i(z)=st z^{-D}R_i(1/z)\).
Consequently \(r_{-n}=0\) for \(1\le n<D\) and \(r_{-n}=-st\,r_{n-D}\) for \(n\ge D\).
This proves a uniform absolute-sum bound for the two-sided moments on \([-Ce,Ce]\).
In particular the positive coefficients of \(z^aR_i\) are moments of a measure of uniformly bounded variation for every \(0\le a\le Ce\).
Translate the moment sequence by \(a\) using a character multiplier, then remove \(r_{n-a}\) for \(1\le n<a\) by singleton Fourier corrections whose total variation is bounded by the negative-index sum.

## Vanishing mixed densities at a curve of torus zeros

Take the limiting marginal \(\mu\) of \((\bar v^d,\bar v^e)\) and extract the signs of all nonzero lattice frequencies \(r d+l e\) as in the [ordered-lattice calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer).
Its order is defined since \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
The prefix gives integrality of \((A/2)J\) on this lattice for the ordered Herglotz transform \(J\) of \(\mu\).
In particular \(\mu\) cannot be Haar, since a Haar marginal would give \(J=1\) and the two separate coefficients \(s/2,t/2\) are nonintegral.
For \(e\ll T_i\ll h\), meaning \(e/T_i,T_i/h\to0\), let \(\Omega\) be any joint limit above this marginal with \(u=v^{T_i}\).
Tests at \(kT_i+rd+le\), \(k>0\) fixed, show that the projection \(\nu_k\) of \(A u^k\,d\Omega\) to the base has integer Fourier coefficients.
On the zero layer \(A^*((A/2)J)+A((A/2)J)^*\) gives those of \(|A|^2\mu\), where star conjugates on the torus.
Multiplication by \(A^*\) on the positive layers and reflection on the negative layers give an integer transform for \(|A|^2\Omega\).

Apply the [coset decomposition and separated-slope calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) to these positive weighted measures and the integral rows \(\nu_k\).
The set \(A=0\) on the torus is the graph \(y=y_0(x)=-(2+s x)/(t(1+2s x))\) over the whole first circle, of base Haar measure zero.
Mass above it contributes zero to every weighted row \(\nu_k\).
Away from it division of \(|A|^2\Omega\) by \(|A|^2\) recovers the original positive measure, and each component has finite mass.
For the graph components projecting over the full base, grouping them by slopes and taking high common multiples of their denominators and translation orders therefore gives \(H=A f\) integral Laurent for each grouped nonnegative integrable base density \(f\).
Indeed the full-torus term vanishes at high harmonics and the other slopes separate by diverging Fourier translates of \(L^1\) functions, just as in that calculation.

If \(H(x,y_0(x))\) were nonzero at one \(x\) on the circle, it would be bounded below in modulus near a whole arc of the graph.
There \(|A(x,y)|=|1+2s x|\,|y-y_0(x)|\), making \(H/A\) nonintegrable in the transverse circle variable.
Hence \(H(x,y_0(x))=0\) on the entire circle.
This substitution is a rational function of \(x\), so it vanishes identically.
The two coefficients \(2+s x,t(1+2s x)\) are relatively prime in the integral Laurent polynomial ring in \(x\), as they are primitive and have distinct roots.
After clearing monomials, degree-one division in \(y\) over \(\mathbb Q(x)\) and Gauss's lemma give \(A\mid H\) in \(\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]\).
Thus \(f\) is integral Laurent.
If it is nonzero, its positive integer mean must be one since \(\mu\) has mass one, leaving the entire marginal with this density.
Every nonconstant Fourier coefficient then has modulus strictly less than one by absolute continuity and so vanishes by integrality, contradicting the exclusion of the Haar marginal.
All graph densities vanish.

Only the full-torus term can now produce absolutely continuous positive rows, and it has finite harmonic support in \(u\), since its trigonometric polynomial density was divided by a function of the base alone.
At the last nonzero such harmonic, the [conditional positivity argument](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) bounds the integral Laurent density \(H_k\) by \(|A| f_0/2\), where \(f_0\) is the absolutely continuous density of \(\mu\).
Here \(M(f_0)<1\) by Jensen and the non-Haar marginal, and \(M(A)=2\) by the [two-variable Jensen calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md#shapes).
This contradicts \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(A)M(f_0)/2\).
Each \(\nu_k\) is consequently supported on the fixed finite union of singular cosets from the decomposition of \(|A|^2\mu\).
Indeed, away from \(A=0\) it is absolutely continuous with respect to \(|A|^2\mu\), and on \(A=0\) it vanishes.
The [annihilating characters](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) give a finite product \(U_i\) of cyclotomic binomials, of degree \(O(e)\), nonzero after specialization.
For either \(V_i=1-z^{p_i}\) with \(p_i=2(e-d)\) or \(6d\), the same fixed lattice shifts and diagonal extraction give a fixed \(L\) such that \(U_iV_i B^\sharp\) vanishes in degrees \(Le\le n\le h/L\) eventually.

## Local coefficients and the exact denominator comparison

If \(e/d\) stays bounded, use \(p_i=2(e-d)\).
The [comparable-offset block estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparable) bound the absolute coefficient sum of \(V_iB^\sharp\) through \(Ce\) for every fixed \(C\).
Their first-index tests at \(d+l u\) for \(u=o(d)\) and fixed \(l\) see the same two signed halves and never reach \(d+e\).
The remaining tests split intervals at the lift offsets and their translates and only require bounded partial coefficient sums for their multipliers.
For \((e-d)/d\) bounded below they likewise bound the unfiltered \(B^\sharp\) through \(Ce\), and [the local inverse bound](#inverse) transfers this to \(J_i=B^\sharp R_i\) on that prefix.
When \(e-d=o(d)\), \(p_i<d\) eventually, and the same first-index alternatives in the comparable-offset bound give a bounded absolute sum of \(J_i\) through \(p_i\).

If \(d/e\to0\), use \(p_i=6d\).
The [sixth-root filter and block estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#separated) apply unchanged below \(e\), where the only lower term after the constant is \(s z^d/2\).
They bound \(J_i\) through \(p_i\), and the two-sided filtered moment Gram matrices on blocks of diameter a fixed fraction of \(e\).
In extending the Bessel test through \(Ce\), the extra coefficient at \(d+e\) and its reflection are bounded, and are included among the finitely many offsets and translated offsets at which the intervals are split.
Thus the absolute coefficient sum of \(V_iB^\sharp\) is bounded through \(Ce\) in this regime as well.
These alternatives cover every sequence after extraction.

On each such initial interval \(V_iJ_i=(V_i B^\sharp)R_i\), so its absolute coefficient sum is bounded there by convolution and the local inverse bound.
The [two-sided correction for the actual measure](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparison) now represents the positive coefficients of \(U_iV_i J_i\) by a measure of bounded variation.
Explicitly, if \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\), then \(m_l=m_{-l}=[z^l]J_i\) for \(l>0\), and \(m_0=2\).
Correcting \(m_l-m_{l-p_i}\) to the nonnegative coefficients of \(V_iJ_i\) costs \(O(1+\sum_{1\le l\le p_i}|[z^l]J_i|)\).
Its negative coefficients at \(-l\) are \(m_l-m_{l+p_i}=-[z^{l+p_i}]V_iJ_i\) for \(l>0\), giving a bounded initial correction under all the \(O(e)\) shifts in \(U_i\).

Truncate \(U_iV_iB^\sharp\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(e)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
The positive Taylor coefficients of \(Q_0 R_i\) are moments of a uniformly bounded-variation measure by the [shifted partial fractions](#inverse).
This series agrees with \(U_iV_iJ_i\) through the cutoff by prefix agreement.
The reciprocal factorization gives the polynomial identity
\[
 U_iV_iB^\sharp-(1+z^{N-D})Q_0
       =(\Pi/2)(U_iV_iJ_i-Q_0R_i).
\]
Its left side has degree \(O(h)\), \(\Pi/2\) has bounded circle supremum, and the difference on the right has a zero prefix of positive fractional length and a bounded Fourier measure on its positive indices.
The [two interval cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) bound the circle \(L^1\) norm of the left side, hence of \(U_iV_iB^\sharp\).
This integer polynomial has [bounded support](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) and, since its added factors are cyclotomic up to sign, its Mahler measure equals \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one.
The [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) excludes the sequence and proves the claimed bounds.
