# First cyclic divisibility at an index proportional to the degree

<a id="cyclic-depth"></a>
## Simultaneous local formulas with bounded total measure

Fix a finite nonempty set \(S\) of rational primes and an odd prime \(\ell\notin S\).
For \(k\to\infty\) put
\[
  N=\ell^{k+1},\qquad b=N/\ell,\qquad d=(\ell-1)b,\qquad
  A=\ell^2\prod_{p\in S}p,\qquad
  W_d(z)=z^{d-1}+4z^{d-3}+z+4z^3 .
\]
The monic reciprocal unit polynomials \(P(z)=\Phi_N(z)-A W_d(z)\) are irreducible for all sufficiently large \(k\).
They have one real positive dominant root and two nonreal conjugate exterior roots, with all remaining roots on the unit circle or reciprocal to these three.
All positive powers of any root retain degree \(d\).
Their total logarithmic Mahler measures satisfy \(m(P)\le\log(\ell+10A)\), independently of \(k\), and tend to \(\log(4A)\).

For positive integers \(n,u,v\) let \(c_n=\prod_{P(x)=0}(x^n-1)\) and \(R_{u,v}=\prod_{P(x)=P(y)=0}(x^u-y^v)\), taking all original ordered pairs.
For each sufficiently large \(k\) the following identities hold simultaneously for every \(p\in S\), every \(n\ge1\), and every \(a\ge0\):
\[
 v_p(c_n)=
 \begin{cases}
 0 & N\nmid n,\\
 d(1+v_p(n))& N\mid n,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 v_p(R_{p^{a+1},p^a})=d(a+1).                                        \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(p\) first divides a cyclic resultant at \(N=\ell d/(\ell-1)\).
At exponent one the mixed resultant realizes the [universal Frobenius floor](../claims/Yin_C_G0237_v1.md#base-prime-power-extra-valuation) exactly.
On every ordered-pair orbit the [compatible fraction](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v2.md#statement) satisfies \(\beta\le d^{-1+o(1)}\).
On 2026-11-16 the proof contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this family by prescribing both the order of every reduced root and its first displacement from a root of unity.

<a id="mixed-signature"></a>
## Irreducibility and the three exterior roots

One has \(\Phi_N(z)=1+z^b+\cdots+z^{(\ell-1)b}\).
Modulo \(\ell\) this equals \((z-1)^d\) by the binomial theorem in characteristic \(\ell\).
Since \(\Phi_N(1)=\ell\) and \(\ell^2\mid A\), the polynomial \(P(z+1)\) is Eisenstein at \(\ell\).
For \(d>6\) the perturbation has smaller degree than \(\Phi_N\), zero constant coefficient and the requisite reciprocal symmetry.
The circle supremum bound \(\|P\|_\infty\le\ell+10A\) proves the stated measure bound.

Uniformly on compact subsets of \(|z|>1\), the functions \(z^{3-d}P(z)\) tend to
\[
                      f(z)=z^3-Az^2-4A .
\]
This follows by dividing the displayed finite geometric sum by \(z^{d-3}\) and letting \(b\) grow.
The cubic has exactly one real root \(r\in(A,2A)\), since it is negative for \(z\le A\), increases for \(z>A\), and is positive at \(2A\).
The other roots are simple nonreal conjugates with squared modulus \(4A/r\in(2,4)\).
By [Rouché's theorem](../audits/literature/Sha23/Yin_U001.md#rouche-disk) on small disjoint disks around these roots, \(P\) has a nearby root for each of them eventually.
The root near \(r\) is real by conjugation.

Write \(h=d/2-3\).
At angles \(\theta=j\pi/h\), \(0\le j\le h\), the centered perturbation \(e^{-id\theta/2}W_d(e^{i\theta})\) equals \(2(-1)^j(4+\cos(2\theta))\).
Its magnitude times \(A\) exceeds the bound \(\ell\) for the centered cyclotomic term, which is real by reciprocity.
The alternating signs produce at least \(h\) roots on the open upper semicircle and \(h\) conjugate roots on the lower one.
These \(d-6\) roots, the three exterior roots already located, and their three inverse roots exhaust the degree.
The product of the three exterior moduli therefore tends to the absolute product \(4A\) of the roots of \(f\).
In particular there is a unique conjugate of largest modulus since \(r>A>2\) and the limiting nonreal moduli are below two.
If two distinct conjugates had equal positive powers, conjugating one of them to that unique dominant root would contradict equality of their moduli.

## First-order displacement at the prescribed primes

Fix \(p\in S\).
Modulo \(p\) the polynomial is \(\Phi_N\), whose \(d\) distinct roots have exact order \(N\), as \(p\nmid N\).
In the splitting field at a chosen place over \(p\), inertia fixes every root because its reduction identifies it uniquely.
Consequently the completion is unramified and \(p\) is a uniformizer.
For each root \(x\) let \(\zeta\) be the unique lift of its reduction satisfying \(\zeta^N=1\), by [simple-root lifting](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001).
It has order \(N\), and \(\omega=\zeta^b\) has order \(\ell\).
Write \(x=\zeta(1+p u)\) with \(u\) integral locally.
Taylor expansion of \(P(x)=0\) modulo \(p^2\) yields
\[
   \bar u=\overline{\frac{(A/p)(\omega-1)}{N}}\,
               \overline{W_d(\zeta)},\qquad
   W_d(\zeta)=\omega^{-1}(\zeta^{-1}+4\zeta^{-3})+\zeta+4\zeta^3.      \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\zeta\Phi_N'(\zeta)=N/(\omega-1)\) follows by differentiating \((z^N-1)/(z^b-1)\), and \(\zeta^d=\omega^{-1}\).
The scalar in (2) is nonzero since \(v_p(A)=1\).

For \(c=0,1\) the equality \(\bar u=c\), after multiplication by \(\bar\zeta^3\), would make \(\bar\zeta\) a root of a polynomial of degree at most six over \(\mathbb F_p(\bar\omega)\).
This polynomial is nonzero even at \(p=2\), since its coefficient of degree four is the nonzero scalar in (2).
The coefficient field has degree at most \(\ell-1\).
But the [finite-field degree](../audits/literature/Hun74/Yin_U006.md#hun74-u006) of a root of order \(N\) is the order of \(p\bmod N\).
If this degree were at most \(6(\ell-1)\), then \(N\le p^{6(\ell-1)}-1\).
Thus \(N>\max_{p\in S}p^{6(\ell-1)}\) ensures \(\bar u\notin\{0,1\}\) for every root and every designated prime.

For any local unit \(1+p v\) with \(\bar v\ne0\), and additionally \(\bar v\ne1\) for \(p=2\), one has
\[
                v_p((1+p v)^n-1)=1+v_p(n)\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Raising first to the prime-to-\(p\) part of \(n\) preserves order one and multiplies the first residue coefficient by that part.
For odd \(p\) the binomial theorem raises each positive order by exactly one on taking a \(p\)-th power.
At \(p=2\) the first squaring has order exactly two because \(\bar v(1+\bar v)\ne0\), and every subsequent squaring raises the order by one.
For \(x^n-1\), the residue already excludes \(p\)-divisibility unless \(N\mid n\).
When \(N\mid n\), the lifted root of unity disappears and the local formula applies to (2).
Summing over \(d\) roots gives the cyclic assertion in (1).

For each \(x\), precisely one root \(y\) has \(\bar y=\bar x^p\).
Its lifted root of unity is \(\zeta^p\), so \(y=\zeta^p(1+p u_y)\).
Then \(x^p/y\equiv1-pu_y\bmod p^2\).
The first residue is nonzero and differs from one when \(p=2\), by (2).
This compatible edge therefore gives exact valuation \(a+1\) in \(x^{p^{a+1}}-y^{p^a}\).
All other edges have valuation zero, since raising to a \(p\)-power is injective on the residue field.
There are \(d\) compatible edges, proving the mixed formula in (1).
More precisely in the [ratio field](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v2.md#local-proof) of any orbit the rational valuation of the complete norm of \((x^p/y)^{p^a}-1\), divided by the ratio-field degree, equals \((a+1)\beta\) by the equal embedding multiplicities.

## Matching fractions and the total-measure boundary

No matching edge has \(y=x\) or \(y=x^{-1}\) once \(N>p+1\), since all reduced roots have order \(N\).
Any remaining pair is multiplicatively independent by the [dependent-pair comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0483_v1.md#independent-and-dependent-pairs) and the established degree preservation for all powers.
Indeed dependence would give \(y=\xi x^{\pm1}\) with \(\xi\) torsion, and powering away \(\xi\) forces equality or inversion since the original polynomial is reciprocal.
Write \(ds\) for the size of an orbit of such a pair.
The [rank-two estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0483_v1.md#independent-and-dependent-pairs) gives \(m(P)\ge C_\epsilon d^{1/2-\epsilon}s^{-1/2-\epsilon}\) for each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
The uniform total-measure bound and \(s\le d\) imply \(s=d^{1-o(1)}\) uniformly on these orbits.
Each has at most \(d\) compatible edges, so \(\beta\le1/s\le d^{-1+o(1)}\).
The dominant root tends to \(r>A\), so this family does not approach measure one.
It shows that bounded total measure and preservation of all power degrees allow both a linear first cyclic index and the exact local mixed valuations in (1) at every prime in a prescribed finite set.
