# Explicit departures from the small ternary thresholds at the Lehmer number

<a id="thresholds"></a>
## A finite exception and an eventual slope

For the [unrestricted ternary threshold](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#statement), the Lehmer number witnesses
\[
 R_1(119)\ge355>3\cdot119-3,\qquad R_1(t)\ge4t\quad(t\ge732).
\]
In fact \(R_1(t)\ge3t-2\) for every \(t\ge119\).
These bounds give explicit points beyond the [initial range of exact thresholds](Yin_L_G0462_v285.md#thresholds), which has \(R_1(t)=3t-3\) for \(2\le t\le11\).
They do not determine the first exception.

## Separation at the real embedding

Let \(r>1\) be the root of the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](Yin_L_G0170_v1.md#lehmer-infinite-core-proof)
\[
 H(X)=X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1.
\]
The other conjugates are \(1/r\) and four complex conjugate pairs on the unit circle, represented by \(u_1,\ldots,u_4\).
Substitution gives \(H(7/6)<0<H(q)\) for \(q=11763/10000\), so \(7/6<r<q\) by uniqueness of the exterior root.

Fix an exponent set \(S\subseteq[0,N)\) of size \(m=t+1\), and let \(F(X)=\sum_{s\in S}\xi_s X^s\) for independent uniform binary digits \(\xi_s\).
The expected sum over \(j\) of \(|F(u_j)-\mathbb E F(u_j)|^2\) equals \(m\), since the cross terms vanish and each digit has variance \(1/4\).
At least \(2^m/5\) binary words have this sum at most \(5m/4\), by Markov's inequality.
For two such words with difference polynomial \(G\), the triangle inequality in \(\mathbb C^4\) gives \(\sum_j |G(u_j)|^2\le5m\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality then yields \(\prod_j |G(u_j)|^2\le(5m/4)^4\).
Also \(|G(1/r)|\le1/(1-1/r)<7\).

Suppose the binary sums on \(S\) were distinct at \(r\).
For two distinct selected words, \(G(r)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer whose absolute norm equals \(|G(r)G(1/r)|\prod_j |G(u_j)|^2\).
Since the norm is at least one, the real values of the selected words are spaced by at least \(1/[7(5m/4)^4]\).
All lie in an interval of length at most \((r^N-1)/(r-1)<6q^N\).
Consequently
\[
 \frac{2^{t+1}}{5}\le1+42(5m/4)^4q^N,
 \qquad 2^t<260(t+1)^4q^N,
\]
where the second inequality uses \(5/2+105(5/4)^4<260\) and \(m^4q^N\ge1\).
Whenever the final inequality fails, every such \(S\) supports a nonzero ternary multiple of \(H\), giving \(R_1(t)\ge N\).

## Integer comparisons for both ranges

Write \(q=A/B\) with \(A=11763\) and \(B=10000\).
For \(N=at+b\), the ratio \(2^t/[260(t+1)^4q^N]\) increases from \(t\) to \(t+1\) when \(2B^a(t+1)^4>A^a(t+2)^4\).
Once true this condition persists because \((t+2)/(t+1)\) decreases.
A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin checked the initial inequality and its propagation for each range by the following integer comparisons on November 20, 2026.

```python
A, B = 11763, 10000
for a, b, t in [(3, -2, 119), (4, 0, 732)]:
    n = a*t+b
    assert 2**t * B**n > 260*(t+1)**4 * A**n
    assert 2 * B**a * (t+1)**4 > A**a * (t+2)**4
```
