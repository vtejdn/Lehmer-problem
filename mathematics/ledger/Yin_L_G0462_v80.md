# A uniform gap for the branch-two cubic \(2-2w+w^3\)

<a id="statement"></a>
## The offsets \(d,3d\) with a coefficient of modulus two

For every positive-constant Pisot input on branch \(j=2\) whose trace numerator is
\[
        C(x)=2U_h(x/2)-2sU_{h-d}(x/2)+sU_{h-3d}(x/2),
        \qquad s=\pm1,\qquad 1\le d\le h/3,
\]
there is an absolute positive lower bound on \(\theta-q\) and an absolute upper bound on \(q\).
This excludes the middle polynomial in the [primitive cubic list](Yin_L_G0462_v77.md#cubic) and its rotation by \(w\mapsto-w\).
The argument also applies to perturbations on any branch.
Fix \(M<\infty,\eta>0\) and suppose an actual normalized trace lift satisfies \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M\) on the circle and agrees through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) with
\[
       G(z) S_i(z^d),\qquad
       S_i(w)=1+\lambda_i w+\mu_i w^2+\nu_i w^3,\qquad
       G\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]],\qquad 1\le d\le h .
\]
There are positive constants \(\varepsilon,c\), depending only on \(M,\eta\), such that \(\theta-q\ge c\) whenever the real coefficient triple has maximum-norm distance at most \(\varepsilon\) from \((-s,0,s/2)\) for either sign.

<a id="torsion"></a>
## The torsion points of a cubic quotient

Put \(A=2-2w+w^3,\ A^*=1-2w^2+2w^3,\ R=A/A^*\).
The cubic is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and all its roots have modulus greater than one by the [real root and conjugate-product test](Yin_L_G0462_v77.md#cubic).
The sign and squaring argument for the [quadratic quotient](Yin_L_G0462_v74.md#torsion) applies to this rational function.
In detail, if both \(\zeta\) and \(R(\zeta)\) are roots of unity of joint order \(n\) with \(4\nmid n\), express them as signs times odd-order roots.
The squaring automorphism on the latter roots gives \(R(a\zeta^2)=bR(\zeta)^2\) for \(a,b=\pm1\).
For \(4\mid n\), the automorphism of exponent \(1+n/2\) gives \(R(a\zeta)=bR(\zeta)\), with at least one negative sign because one of the two original coordinates has the full 2-part of \(n\).

The following table lists the distinct cyclotomic divisors by order of
\[
     E_{k,a,b}(w)=A(aw^k)A^*(w)^k-bA^*(aw^k)A(w)^k .
\]
It can be checked by taking the remainder of the displayed polynomial modulo \(\Phi_m\) for each \(1\le m\le288\) with \(\varphi(m)\le12\).
This finite range follows from \(\deg E\le12\) and \(\varphi(m)\ge\sqrt{m/2}\).

| \(k\) | \((a,b)=(+,+)\) | \((+,-)\) | \((-,+)\) | \((-,-)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(1\) | identity omitted | none | none | \(1,2,12\) |
| \(2\) | \(1,2\) | \(12\) | \(6\) | \(1,2,10\) |

For example \(E_{1,-,-}=-4(w^2-1)(w^4-w^2+1)\) and \(E_{2,-,-}=-2(w-1)(w+1)^3\Phi_{10}(w)(3w^4-7w^3+9w^2-7w+3)\).
Consequently every torsion \(\zeta\) with torsion \(R(\zeta)\) has order in \(\{1,2,6,10,12\}\), which suffices for the classification.

## Five possible projected series

Let \(J\) be the Herglotz transform of a conjugation-invariant circle probability such that \(B=(A/2)J\) has integer Taylor coefficients.
As in the [rationality and squarefree-factor argument](Yin_L_G0462_v74.md#profiles), \(K=2/J=A/B\) has positive real part and integer coefficients.
The integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem applied to its doubled measure yields eventual periodicity and rationality.
The bounded integer coefficients of the rational \(B\) are likewise eventually periodic by its linear recurrence, giving \(B=V/U\) with \(U\) a squarefree signed cyclotomic product and \(V\) integral, both of constant one.
These are taken in lowest terms.
Since \(A\) has no closed-disk zero and \(\Re J>0\) inside, Kronecker's theorem on the monic reciprocal of \(V\) makes \(V\) a signed cyclotomic product as well.
The Herglotz bound \(\Re J(t\zeta)\ge(1-t)/(1+t)\) at a boundary zero excludes multiplicity greater than one.

Put \(n=\deg V-\deg U\), \(\epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\) and \(x=w+w^{-1}\).
The regular doubled density of \(K=AU/V\) is the nonnegative Laurent polynomial
\[
                  \rho=K(w)+K(1/w)=(U/V)[A+\epsilon w^{n-3}A^*].
\]
The pole contributions of Herglotz atoms cancel in this sum on the regular circle.
The density is not identically zero, since \(K\) vanishes at a root of \(A\) while its value at the reciprocal is nonzero.
The odd-order circle zeros of the bracket are precisely the roots of \(UV\), by even multiplicity of each density zero and squarefreeness.
In particular \(UV\) divides the product of the five cyclotomic factors allowed by the torsion test.
Label them by their orders, normalizing order one as \(1-w\) and the others as \(\Phi_m(w)\).
A dash denotes the constant one.
In the mass column \(x=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) at the particular root \(\zeta\) of the listed factor of \(V\).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(2\) | — | \((x+2)(x^3-x^2-4x+5)\) | — |
| — | \(1,2\) | \(2-x\) | \(1/4,3/4\) |

For explicit exhaustion take each \(n=-12,\ldots,12\), the degree bound given by the full product, and both signs \(\epsilon\).
Divide \(w^{\max(0,3-n)}[A+\epsilon w^{n-3}A^*]\) repeatedly by each of the five factors and retain the factors whose multiplicities are odd.
Assign each retained factor exclusively to \(U\) or \(V\), keeping assignments with the prescribed \(n,\epsilon\).

The residues \(-A(\zeta)U(\zeta)/(2\zeta V'(\zeta))\) give the tabulated masses and they are all positive.
The cubic \(F(x)=x^3-x^2-4x+5\) in the first row has discriminant \(-23\), and its sole real zero lies between \(-21/10\) and \(-41/20\) by the signs at these endpoints.
Thus \(F>0\) on \([-2,2]\).
All five densities are nonnegative.
Removing the atom kernels and taking harmonic extension from the continuous boundary values proves \(\Re K>0\) in the disk for every listed case.
Conversely all five therefore give probability transforms \(J=2/K\) with integral \(B\).
At the level of actual denominators the list is necessary, and every projected atom of \(J\) lies at \(-1\) in the first row, with none in the other rows.
After rotation the possible atom lies at \(+1\).

## Small envelopes for all five rows

Let \(\sigma=2\Re J\) on the regular circle and \(D=|A|\sigma/2\).
The separated atom kernels give a bounded continuous density for the regular part.
As \(\sigma=2\rho/|K|^2\) away from atoms and \(M(K)=M(A)=2\), one has \(M(D)=M(\rho)/2\).
In the last four rows this is at most one since \(M(2-x)=1\).
For the first row \(M(x+2)=1\).
Write \(-t\) for the real root of \(F\) and \(a\pm ib\) for its nonreal pair.
Then
\[
              41/20<t<21/10,\qquad a=(1+t)/2<31/20,
              \qquad b^2=5/t-a^2<1/8 .
\]
For the last inequality use \(a>61/40\).
The factor \(w^2+t w+1\) over the real trace contributes less than \(7/5\) to the Mahler measure, since \(21/10<7/5+5/7\).
For either complex trace the exterior root has modulus less than \(4/3\).
Indeed the image of \(|w|=4/3\) by \(w+w^{-1}\) is the ellipse with semiaxes \(25/12,7/12\), and
\[
               \frac{a^2}{(25/12)^2}+\frac{b^2}{(7/12)^2}
                     <\frac9{16}+\frac{18}{49}<1 .
\]
The ellipses for larger radii are strictly wider in both directions, so these traces cannot come from such a radius.
Pairing the reciprocal roots over the three traces yields \(M(F(w+w^{-1}))<(7/5)(4/3)^2\).
Thus in all rows \(M(D)<\sqrt2\).

Suppose a common family of conditional circle probabilities has moments \(c_k\) such that \(H_k=(A/2)\sigma c_k\) is an integer Laurent polynomial for each \(k\ge1\).
Their bounds \(|H_k|\le D\) give only finitely many nonzero shapes up to monomial shifts by the [separated-block inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes).
For each such shape \(S\), \(M(S)\le M(D)<2=M(A)\), so the irreducible \(A\) divides no shift of \(S\).
The rational function \(S(w)A(1/w)/(S(1/w)A(w))\) has a pole at a root of \(A\) of modulus greater than one because its numerator is nonzero there.
The [prime specialization and last-harmonic argument](Yin_L_G0462_v74.md#harmonics) consequently applies without change of field or positivity assumptions.
In detail, the squaring identity for this function fails by iterating the exterior pole.
At sufficiently large prime order the squaring automorphism excludes a real value for every nonzero conditional moment by the phase quotient, simultaneously for all the shapes.
At that node all moments come from one probability by continuity of their finite moment matrices at regular points of positive density.
The sequence is finite-valued by finiteness of shapes and the root-of-unity shifts, hence periodic on the tails by interpolation of level indicators and the integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem.
Its positive-multiple progression average gives the real mass on the period's root group and must vanish, forcing all progression averages to vanish.
Taking the node away from shape zeros makes all late rows vanish.
A last nonzero row would have \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by positivity of the conditional probability after the \(k\)-th power pushforward, contrary to \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(D)/2<1\).
Thus every row vanishes also after rotating by \(-1\).

## The first fractional coefficient and the period \(2d\)

If the stable prefix assertion failed, take a sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\) and triples tending to \((-s,0,s/2)\) at fixed sign.
Write \(X=B^\sharp/G\), an integral series matching \(S_i(z^d)J_i\) through the given prefix, where \(J_i=j/K_q\) is the inverse transform of the actual signed measure.
The [divisibility and inverse-measure bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) give \(h\to\infty\), negative mass tending to zero and \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one.
The normalized lift has an integral prefix strictly below \(d\) up to the common prefix, giving \(d=o(h)\) by the [divisibility theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
Write \(j_l=[z^l]J_i\), with \(j_0=1\).
The \(j_l\) are uniformly bounded and integral below \(d\).
In fact their distance to integers is uniformly \(o(1)\) for every \(0\le l<3d\).
This follows successively on \([d,2d)\) and \([2d,3d)\) from \(j_l=[z^l]X-\lambda_i j_{l-d}-\mu_i j_{l-2d}\), omitting negative indices, since \(\lambda_i\to-s,\ \mu_i\to0\).

For \(d\to\infty\) and any positive \(t=o(d)\), test the two-circle limit under \((v^t,v^{3d})\).
At \(3d+rt\) for fixed \(r\) the terms from \(\lambda_i,\mu_i\) use indices below \(3d\) and differ from integers by \(o(1)\).
At the cubic term the multiplier tends to \(s/2\).
Thus the limiting doubled mixed moments are integral for \(r<0\), half-odd-integral for \(r=0\), and for \(r>0\) become integral on addition of \(s/2\) times the doubled first marginal moment.
This is exactly the [fractional seam forcing Haar](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar).
By the [uniform first block and Bessel argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v18.md#seams), \(X\) has boundedly many nonzero coefficients on \([0,Ld]\) for each fixed \(L\).
Its open blocks now split at \(0,d,2d,3d\), and the fixed multipliers are twice partial sums of \(S_i(v^{-d})\), uniformly bounded in circle supremum.
For bounded \(d\) the same support bound is immediate.

For \(d/t\to0,\ t/h\to0\) push the inverse measures to \((v^d,v^t)\) and take a weak probability limit.
At every fixed multiple of \(d\), the integral prefix gives \(S J\) integral for the first marginal transform, where \(S=A(sw)/2\).
At each fixed \(kt+ld\) with \(k\ge1,\ l\in\mathbb Z\), all four indices in the polynomial product are positive and inside the same prefix.
The coefficient perturbation tends to zero since the original moments are bounded.
Thus the [reflected mixed rows](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) have integer Fourier coefficients, with continuous densities \(S\sigma c_k\) as above and atomic parts supported on atoms of the marginal.
Extinction leaves only atoms at square roots of unity, so integrality gives \([z^{t+2d}]X=[z^t]X\) eventually.
Diagonal extraction gives this equality throughout \(Ld\le t\le h/L\) eventually at one fixed \(L\) with \(1/L<\eta\).

Set \(U_0=1-z^{2d}\), and truncate \(U_0X\) at \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q\).
The period and the initial support bound give \(\deg Q\le(L+2)d\) with bounded coefficient sum in absolute value.
The comparison \(J_*=Q/[U_0S_i(z^d)]\) agrees with \(J_i\) through that truncation index.
Since the limiting \(S\) is zero-free on a disk of radius greater than one, the coefficients \(a_m=[w^m]1/S_i\) satisfy a uniform bound for \(\sum_{m\ge0}(1+m)|a_m|\) by Cauchy's estimate on a fixed smaller disk of radius still greater than one.
The [congruence-class expansion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#comparison) for step \(2d\) thus represents the positive coefficients of \(J_*\) by a measure of bounded variation, since each shifted monomial of \(Q\) at \(e+md\) needs only \(O(1+(e+md)/(2d))\) corrections.
The [two-cutoff estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v18.md#far) applies to the identity
\[
      S_i(z^d)U_0 B^\sharp-Q\Pi/j
         =S_i(z^d)U_0(\Pi/j)(J_i-J_*).
\]
The left degree is \(O(h)\), the difference on the right has a zero prefix of length comparable to \(h\) and bounded representing measure at positive indices, and all its polynomial multipliers have bounded supremum.
Also \(Q\Pi/j\) is bounded, and \(\min_{|w|=1}|S_i(w)|\) is bounded away from zero.
Consequently \(\|U_0 B^\sharp\|_1\) is bounded and this integer polynomial has bounded support by the [prime-grid argument](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
Its measure equals \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, contrary to the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
For the exact branch-two inputs the specified prefix is present through \(h\) with \(G=1\), the normalized six-term lift has bounded supremum, and the [fixed-branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) bounds \(q\) from the positive excess.
