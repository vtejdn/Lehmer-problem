# A fixed value at one forces irreducibility of the large-root inverse

<a id="factor"></a>
## A bounded nonzero integer value assigns the root near one to every factor

Let \(f_i\) be monic integer polynomials of a common degree \(d\), with \(f_i(0)\ne0\), \(0<|f_i(1)|\le B\), and no roots on the unit circle.
Suppose there is at least one exterior root, the least exterior modulus tends to infinity, and precisely one interior root \(\beta_i\), counted with multiplicity, tends to one while every other interior root stays at distance at least \(c>0\) from one.
Then \(f_i\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) for all sufficiently large \(i\).

In fact every nonconstant monic integer factor \(g\) needs an exterior root, since \(g(0)\) is a nonzero integer and an interior-only factor would give \(|g(0)|<1\).
If \(g\) omits \(\beta_i\), then for all large \(i\),
\[
            |g(1)|\ \ge\ \min(1,c)^{d-1}
                   \left(\min_{f_i(\eta)=0,\ |\eta|>1}|\eta|-1\right).
\]
This follows by isolating one exterior root in the product at one and using \(|1-\eta|\ge1\) at the remaining exterior roots.
But the nonzero integer \(g(1)\) divides \(f_i(1)\), contradicting the bound \(B\).
Every nonconstant monic factor must therefore contain the same simple root \(\beta_i\).
Gauss's lemma then excludes a factorization into two nonconstant factors over \(\mathbb Q\).

<a id="inverse"></a>
## Irreducibility for every sufficiently large signed parameter

For a fixed monic irreducible noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomial \(P(z)=z^m Q(z+z^{-1})\), use the [integral delay-one inverse](Yin_L_G0462_v132.md#inverse).
In the notation there put \(r=\deg G,\ h=m-r,\ x=z+z^{-1}\), where \(Q=GH\) and \(H\) has precisely the traces in \((-2,2)\).
Take all sufficiently large integers \(|C|\) with \(C G(2)<0\).
Then the full seed \(f_C=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}(C H+E_C)(x)\) is the minimal polynomial of each of its exterior roots.
For \(r\ge2\) it is an irreducible unit polynomial of the same degree as \(P\).

Indeed \(f_C(1)=P(1)\ne0\), and \(f_C(0)\) is one for \(r\ge2\) and \(1+C\ne0\) for \(r=1\).
The [circle-root exclusion and exterior scaling](Yin_L_G0462_v132.md#exterior) show that every exterior modulus is asymptotic to \(|C|^{1/r}\).
The [bounded-root factor](Yin_L_G0462_v132.md#recover) converges coefficientwise at fixed degree to
\[
          z^h H(z+z^{-1})\,z^{r-1}(z-1).
\]
Its root at one is simple since \(H(2)\ne0\), and all its other roots lie in a finite set separated from one.
Root continuity therefore provides precisely the simple root and the positive distance in the factor argument above.
This applies on the entire parameter tail with the rounding choices of the construction.

The root \(\beta_C\) tending to one is real by uniqueness and conjugation.
For large \(|C|\), \(H(\beta_C+\beta_C^{-1})\ne0\), and the equation at that root gives
\[
 C(\beta_C-1)=
    \frac{\beta_C G(\beta_C+\beta_C^{-1})}
         {1+E_C(\beta_C+\beta_C^{-1})/(C H(\beta_C+\beta_C^{-1}))}
       \longrightarrow G(2).
\]
The boundedness of the coefficients of \(E_C\) and fixed degree justify the limit.
In particular \(0<\beta_C<1\) eventually and
\[
             (1-\beta_C)M(f_C)\longrightarrow |G(2)|,
\]
since the product of the \(r\) exterior moduli is asymptotic to \(|C|\).
Every assertion fixes the target polynomial before the parameter tends to infinity.
