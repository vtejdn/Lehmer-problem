# The least possible average for sparse nonnegative polynomial trace residues

<a id="mean"></a>
## A sharp integer lower bound on the circle

Let \(W\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be nonnegative on \([-2,2]\), satisfy \(W(2)=0\) and take a positive value at some \(T>2\).
Its mean \(B=\int W\,d\rho\) against the arcsine probability on \([-2,2]\) is an even integer at least four.
To see this put \(U(z)=W(z+z^{-1})=B+\sum_{j>0}u_j(z^j+z^{-j})\).
All coefficients are integers, \(B>0\) by nonnegativity, and \(B=-2\sum_{j>0}u_j\) by \(U(1)=0\).
If \(B=2\), the integer polynomial \(K(z)=1+\sum_{j>0}u_j z^j\) has nonnegative boundary real part \(U/2\), positive real part in the disk and constant one.
Its representing measure has a continuous density of mass one.
The [integral positive-real classification](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) therefore gives \(K=1\) or \(1\pm z^s\) for an integer \(s\ge1\), since the reciprocal binomials are not polynomials.
The zero at one leaves only \(U=2-z^s-z^{-s}\), which is negative at every positive real \(z>1\), contradicting \(W(T)>0\).
The [inverse trace weight](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#residues) \(W=(x^2-4)(D_k(x)-2)\) attains \(B=4\) for every \(k\ge3\) and is positive for \(x>2\).
Here \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\), so the constant is read off directly from the Laurent product.

<a id="sparse"></a>
## A lower bound for the branch per degree on Salem inputs

Suppose Salem numbers \(\sigma_i\to1\) have degrees \(d_i\), exterior traces \(T_i=\sigma_i+\sigma_i^{-1}\) and circle traces \(x_{i,\ell}\), one per conjugate pair.
For each \(i\) assume a polynomial \(W_i\) satisfies the hypotheses above with \(T=T_i\), the support sizes of \(W_i(z+z^{-1})\) are bounded, and
\[
                     (\deg W_i)\log\sigma_i=o(d_i).
\]
Write \(B_i\) for its arcsine mean and \(j_i=W_i(T_i)+\sum_\ell W_i(x_{i,\ell})\).
Then
\[
           \frac1{d_i B_i}\sum_\ell W_i(x_{i,\ell})\longrightarrow\frac12,
                \qquad \liminf_i j_i/d_i\ge2 .
\]
The coefficients of \(W_i(z+z^{-1})/B_i\) have modulus at most one, since they are Fourier coefficients of a nonnegative density of mass one.
Every sequence of nonzero positive exponents \(b_i\) in these supports gives nontorsion algebraic numbers \(\sigma_i^{b_i}\) of height at most \((\deg W_i)\log(\sigma_i)/d_i\to0\).
Their circle phases therefore equidistribute by the [one-dimensional small-points theorem](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures).
The pushforward of the full orbit of \(\sigma_i\) under this power is the orbit measure for \(\sigma_i^{b_i}\), since all fibers have the same cardinality.
After discarding the two real conjugates at cost \(2/d_i\) in the averaged phase, the empirical mean of each such nonconstant Laurent monomial tends to zero.
This convergence is uniform over the exponents in each support by choosing a worst exponent on any failing subsequence.
The bounded term count and coefficient bound then give the limit from the constant term alone, with one trace counted for every two circle phases.
Positivity at \(T_i\) and \(B_i\ge4\) prove the lower bound.
The [semicircle construction at zero scaled exterior mass](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit) satisfies the sparse support and height hypotheses with this \(W_i\) and gives \(j_i/d_i\to2\).
Thus the bound is sharp in this class of inverse weights along any given Salem sequence tending to one.
The circle positivity, bounded support and height condition are hypotheses on the weight and do not describe every Pisot inverse.

<a id="square"></a>
## Square residues can attain the same limit

In the construction for any fixed \(\lambda\ge0\), one may use the least even integer \(k=2l\) with \(\sigma^k\) above the target in the [scale choice](Yin_L_G0462_v129.md#limit).
Overshoot is by at most a factor \(\sigma^2\to1\), so the proof still gives the same root excess, branch ratio and weak limit.
The [integer inverse and its error](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#cost) use only the size of that power and positivity of the residues, as the [explicit construction](Yin_L_G0462_v126.md#seed) shows.
Now at every trace one has exactly
\[
      W(x)=(D_{l+1}(x)-D_{l-1}(x))^2 .
\]
Indeed the difference in parentheses is \((z-z^{-1})(z^l-z^{-l})\) for \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
In particular the whole branch is the field trace of the square of an algebraic integer in \(\mathbb Q(T)\), simultaneously with the limiting law.
