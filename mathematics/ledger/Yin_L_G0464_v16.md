## Which condition was missing from the earlier root models?

The [genuine terminal-interval construction](Yin_L_G0464_v15.md#endpoint-value) satisfies all seven polynomial trace equations at its fixed double root, but its endpoint value lies in \(0.022<U<0.023\).
The original branch requires \(U=1\), so restoring genuine root-to-pole intervals did not restore the full original input.
The [earlier five step witnesses](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0151/endpoint_model.md#endpoint-value) also fail this extension because they occupy a positive fraction of their first coarse cell, making the endpoint logarithmic integral diverge.
Neither failure excludes other occupations or other exceptional-root positions.

Allowing the exceptional roots to be \(s\pm\sqrt d\) changes every polynomial moment as well as the endpoint target.
For the actual terminal-interval indicator, the additional equality is
\[
 \int_{-2}^2\frac{w(t)}{2+t}\,dt
 =\log\frac{(2+s)^2-d}{2+c},\qquad c=b_{7851}.
\]
The polynomial moments do not imply this logarithmic equality.

## What does a polynomial dual test establish?

The [C882v9 dual inequalities](../claims/Yin_C_G0882_v9.md#endpoint-duals) hold for every real degree-at-most-six polynomial, with a continuous positive-part integral compensating for excursions above the endpoint kernel.
The purely polynomial version instead bounds a density's integral against \(q\) by the integral of \(q_+\).
A fixed rational polynomial with a rigorous positive separation can therefore exclude its stated parameter region even when the numerical LP that proposed it has no evidentiary role.
A successful floating optimum or an infeasible floating LP alone is not such an exclusion.

The finite failures do not show that such a certificate is impossible, nor that the unresolved boxes contain actual root configurations.
