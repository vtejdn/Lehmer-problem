# Can the exact trace pole force many conjugates of small dyadic degree?

<a id="answer"></a>
## The proposed proportion already has a Mahler cost

Let \(d\) be the degree of a genuine integral Salem minimum \(F\) with exterior root \(1<T<(q+1)/q\) and \(q^dF((q+1)/q)=(2q+1)^2\).
Write \(N_B=\#\{z:F(z)=0,\ [\mathbb Q_2(z):\mathbb Q_2]\le B\}\) in a fixed dyadic algebraic closure, and \(B=\lfloor(\log_2 d)/2\rfloor\).
Each conjugate is counted once; one irreducible factor of degree \(j\) contributes \(j\) to the count if \(j\le B\).
Bounded degree forces boundedly many monic integer polynomials with all complex roots of modulus at most \(3/2\).
Thus \(q\to\infty\) along such realizations would imply \(d\to\infty\).
The [graph equation](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement) already gives \(N_B=O(\sqrt d)\).

Even without the precise pole value, every monic squarefree integer polynomial of bounded Mahler measure has \(N_B=o(d)\) for its dyadic unit roots.
Roots with a common residue of degree \(r\le B\) cluster around that residue's Teichmüller lift to depth at least \(1/\lfloor B/r\rfloor\).
Their contributions to the discriminant, compared with the complex Vandermonde upper bound, give
\[
 \frac{N_B^2}{5\,2^B}-N_B\le d\log_2d+(2d-2)\log_2 M(F).
\]
For \(M(F)=T<1+1/q\) this gives \(N_B=O(d^{3/4}\sqrt{\log d})\).
Conversely, \(N_B\ge\delta d\) for fixed \(\delta>0\) costs \(\log M(F)\gg_\delta\sqrt d\).
This cost is sharp even among genuine Salem polynomials when the exterior root is allowed to grow. [Discriminant bound and sharpness](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v192.md#statement)

Hence the positive-proportion assertion would by itself imply nonexistence of the unbounded-height branch.
It holds vacuously if the heights of all genuine realizations are bounded.
No unconditional counterexample to that implication follows from exhibiting a model which releases one of its hypotheses.
Ruling out this branch alone would leave the unrestricted Lehmer gap open. [Scope of the trace construction](Yin_L_G0462_v328.md#gap)

## What the exact pole requires at several primes

Write \(T=\beta^u\), \(((q+1)T-q)/(q+1-qT)=\beta^v\), with \(0<u<v\) coprime.
Put \(N=v-u\) at even \(q\) and \(N=v+u\) at odd \(q\).
For \(\delta>0\), let \(N_*\) be the product of the full prime powers in \(N\) with primes exceeding \((\log d)^{2+\delta}\).
Apart from \(O_\delta(d/(\log d)^{\delta/2})\) roots, each dyadic residue order is divisible by \(N_*\).
The proof combines the local multiplicative congruence with integer norms of differences of powers of \(\beta\), using the unit-circle embeddings to bound these norms. [Norm and density estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v194.md#statement)
If a fixed positive proportion were to have local degree at most \(B\), this would require \(N_*\mid 2^j-1\) for some \(j\le B\), hence \(N_*<\sqrt d\), for all sufficiently large members of that family.

The real pole also supplies an upper constraint.
For \((m,c)=(v-u,q),(v,2q+1),(v+u,q+1)\), fix \(r\mid c\) prime and \(0<\kappa<1\).
Write \(R\) for the part of \(m\) supported on primes exceeding \(d^\kappa\).
Almost all residue degrees at \(r\) are multiples of \(f=\operatorname{ord}_R(r)\).
Applying separation of the real period from low-denominator multiples of \(d\) shows \(f<d\exp(-c_\kappa\log d/\log^2(2q+1))\).
This bound is sublinear, and the range down to \(B\) remains wide. [Frobenius bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v197.md#upper)
One actual exclusion is that none of \(v-u,v,v+u\) can be a fixed multiple of a safe prime in a family of unbounded degree.
For \(m=hP\) with \(h\) fixed and \(P=2s+1\) with \(P,s\) prime, the order of \(r\bmod P\) exceeds two when \(d\) is sufficiently large, so it is at least \(s\gg_h d\). [Fixed-multiple exclusion](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v197.md#exclude)
Moreover, when \(v\) itself is prime, at every \(r\mid2q+1\) the pole forces precisely two roots of local degree at most two and \(d-2\) unramified roots, each of degree \(\operatorname{ord}_v(r)>\log_r d\). [Odd-prime decomposition](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v194.md#sum)

## Testing the roles of the pole and all finite places

The entire prescribed complex root distribution, exact real pole and dyadic data are compatible with uniformly large dyadic local degrees after one specified odd prime is allowed in the denominator.
At every sufficiently large \(q\) divisible by four, with any chosen precision \(b\) fixed before the degree, all dyadic roots can have degrees at least \(c d^{1/4}\) and all but \(O_{q,b}(\sqrt d)\) can have degrees at least \(c\sqrt d\), with absolute \(c>0\).
The examples retain the growing zero prefix, powered capacity tests, sharp real pin and bounded additive error in the real period. [Unramified degree construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v193.md#statement)
For a specified odd denominator prime coprime to \(q\), they can simultaneously have the correct order at the **full modulus \(q\)**, and the graph congruence modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(q,2^b)\).
Their primitive integer measure includes the prime denominator, and the full order at \(q+1\) and the characteristic-zero graph identity have not been obtained together on these models. [Simultaneous modulus](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v196.md#statement)

If integrality and the full Salem signature are retained but the real pole is changed, there are actual Salem minima with \(F(2)=3\), \(2^d(2-T)\to3\), and **no** dyadic conjugate below the proposed cutoff.
At the cubic Pisot unit \(\rho^3=\rho+1\), there are also actual Salem minima with \(S(\rho)=1\), exterior roots pinned from below at rate \(\rho^{-d}\), and just \(d^{o(1)}\) conjugates at or below the cutoff as an upper bound. [Fixed-pole examples](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v195.md#statement)
The varying rational pole near one remains indispensable to any argument seeking a contradiction by low local degrees.
A concrete remaining question is whether the three residue orders forced by the **same** pair \(u,v\) and the same integral orbit can satisfy the real period estimates when the large divisors above are replaced by unrestricted factorizations.
