# Composite primitive orders and the Mahler measure of power substitutions

On 2026-11-16 the proof-side contributor investigated whether admitting all composite orders in the [unrestricted prime-window argument](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units) supplies an absolute lower bound for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The normalization by the order and the normalization by the polynomial degree have different consequences in a superlinear window.

<a id="composite"></a>
## A bound outside at most one order per root

Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)\), \(D_j(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|\), \(X_j(P)=\log D_j(P)/\varphi(j)\), \(C_j(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^j-1)|\), and \(Z_j(P)=\log C_j(P)/j\).
Allow \(\log0=-\infty\).
For every monic complex polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\) and real \(Y\ge3\), one can delete at most \(d\) integer orders in \([Y,2Y]\) so that every remaining order \(j\) satisfies
\[
 \left|\log D_j(P)-\varphi(j)m(P)\right|
       \le C d\,2^{\omega(j)}\log(2Y).                                  \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\omega(j)\) counts distinct prime factors and \(C\) is absolute.
For real \(P\) at most \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) deletions suffice.

For each root \(\alpha\) put \(w=\alpha\) if \(|\alpha|\le1\) and \(w=1/\alpha\) otherwise.
Cyclotomic reciprocity gives \(\log D_j(P)-\varphi(j)m(P)=\sum_w\log|\Phi_j(w)|\) with multiplicity.
The pool of primitive roots of all window orders has mutual normalized angular spacing at least \(1/(4Y^2)\) by subtraction of distinct reduced fractions.
For \(w\ne0\), delete the order of one node closest in angle to \(w\).
Any remaining node \(\zeta\) has angular distance at least \(1/(8Y^2)\) from \(w\), since the deleted node was at least as close.
Consequently \(|w-\zeta|\gg Y^{-2}\) throughout the closed disk, by minimizing the distance to \(\zeta\) on the radial segment at that angle.
For real \(w\ne0\) no deletion is needed, since each primitive node has angular distance at least \(1/(4Y)\) from both \(1\) and \(-1\).
Conjugate nonreal roots permit conjugate choices of closest nodes of the same order, and \(w=0\) contributes zero logarithm.

Put \(r=1-(2Y)^{-4}\).
At the remaining nodes the logarithms of \(|w-\zeta|\) and \(|rw-\zeta|\) differ by \(O(Y^{-2})\), by the triangle inequality and the preceding separation (with the absolute constant adjusted for bounded \(Y\)).
Summing over the \(\varphi(j)\le2Y\) nodes costs \(O(1)\) per root.
Inside the disk the product identity
\[
            \log|\Phi_j(rw)|=\sum_{k\mid j}\mu(j/k)\log|1-(rw)^k|
\]
has \(2^{\omega(j)}\) nonzero summands.
Each modulus lies between \(1-r\) and \(2\).
Taking the union of the deletions over the roots proves (1).

When the window contains at least \(d\) orders the number of allowable deletions is sharp for complex coefficients.
Choose a primitive node from each of \(d\) different orders as the roots of \(P\).
All \(d\) corresponding resultants vanish.
For real coefficients use the conjugate pairs from \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) different orders, inserting a zero root if \(d\) is odd.
Moving the chosen nodes radially inward by a common sufficiently small distance preserves \(m(P)=0\) and makes all the chosen resultants positive and arbitrarily small.

## Density-one approximation and an absolute degree-normalized consequence

For each \(\epsilon>0\), if \(Y\to\infty\) and \(Y/[d(\log(2Y))^{1+\log 2+\epsilon}]\to\infty\), then
\[
            X_j(P)=m(P)+o(1)
       \quad\text{outside }o(Y)\text{ orders in }[Y,2Y].               \tag{2}
\]
The error and exceptional count are uniform over the coefficients.
In particular \(Y=d(\log(2d))^2\) works as \(d\to\infty\), and removing the primes still leaves a density-one subset of the integer window.

For details, for each fixed \(t>0\) the divisor expansion of \(2^{t\omega(j)}\) over squarefree divisors gives
\[
 \frac1Y\sum_{Y\le j\le2Y}2^{t\omega(j)}
   \ll\prod_{p\le2Y}\left(1+\frac{2^t-1}{p}\right)
   \ll_t(\log(2Y))^{2^t-1}.
\]
One may use \(\sum_{p\le x}1/p\le\log\log x+O(1)\) here directly by comparing with \(\log\zeta(1+1/\log x)\le\log(1+\log x)\).
The comparison costs at most \((\log x)^{-1}\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)/p=O(1)\) by partial summation and the [elementary Chebyshev upper bound](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units).
Given \(\delta>0\), choose \(t>0\) small enough that \((2^t-1)/t<\log2+\delta\).
Markov's inequality then gives \(2^{\omega(j)}\le(\log(2Y))^{\log2+\delta}\) outside \(o(Y)\) orders.
Also \(j/\varphi(j)\le(\log(2Y))^\delta\) outside \(o(Y)\) orders by the [bounded first moment](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#exceptional-orders).
Using these two bounds with \(\delta=\epsilon/4\) in (1), and \(d=o(Y)\), proves (2).

For the monic minimal polynomial of any nontorsion algebraic unit, fix \(\epsilon>0\) and take
\[
          Y=\frac{d(\log(2d))^{4+\log2+\epsilon}}{(\log\log(3d))^3}.
\]
Then outside \(o(Y)\) integer orders in this window, as \(d\to\infty\),
\[
 \log C_j(P)\ \ge\ \log D_j(P)
      =(1+o(1))\varphi(j)m(P)
      \gg_\epsilon d(\log(2d))^{1+\log2+\epsilon/2}.                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(m(P)\gg(\log\log(3d)/\log(2d))^3\) by the [degree-dependent Mahler bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument).
With the same \(\delta=\epsilon/4\), the main term is \(\gg_\epsilon d(\log(2d))^{1+\log2+3\epsilon/4}\) and the absolute error in (1) is \(O_\epsilon(d(\log(2d))^{1+\log2+\epsilon/4})\).
All other cyclotomic factors of \(C_j(P)\) are positive integers in absolute value.
The [prime estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#prime-scale) reaches a smaller order scale for its prime sample.
In (3) the order grows fast enough that \(j\,m(P)/d\) can be large even for a sequence with \(m(P)\to0\).

## Exact sampling on irreducible power substitutions

Fix any nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) with monic minimal polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(D\).
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\), \(F_p(z)=Q(z^p)\) is the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion unit, with degree \(Dp\) and \(m(F_p)=m(Q)\).
Indeed, over \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), any proper monic factor of \(z^p-\alpha\) of positive degree \(k<p\) gives an element \(b\in K\), the product of its roots, with \(b^p=\alpha^k\).
Integers \(u,v\) with \(ku+pv=1\) give \((b^u\alpha^v)^p=\alpha\).
There are only finitely many elements \(\gamma\in K\) with \(\gamma^p=\alpha\) for some prime \(p\), since they are integral of degree at most \(D\) and all their conjugates have modulus bounded by \(\max(1,\max_\sigma|\sigma(\alpha)|)\).
The elementary symmetric coefficient bounds give this finiteness, and any such \(\gamma\) can serve only one \(p\) since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
Thus \(z^p-\alpha\) is irreducible over \(K\) eventually.
The degree tower proves the assertion over \(\mathbb Q\), and taking all \(p\)-th roots of each conjugate gives the Mahler identity.

For every \(j\ge1\), with \(k=j/\gcd(j,p)\), one has exactly
\[
                      X_j(F_p)=X_k(Q),\qquad Z_j(F_p)=Z_k(Q).          \tag{4}
\]
The power map \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^p\) on all \(j\)-th roots hits every \(k\)-th root with equal multiplicity.
On the primitive roots it hits each primitive \(k\)-th root \(\varphi(j)/\varphi(k)\) times, by reduction of unit residue classes and the Chinese remainder theorem.
Taking the two averages of \(\log|Q(\zeta^p)|\) gives (4).

For the fixed \(Q\), both \(X_k(Q)\) and \(Z_k(Q)\) tend to \(m(Q)\) as \(k\to\infty\).
For a conjugate off the circle, extracting \(k\log^+|\alpha_i|\) from \(\log|1-\alpha_i^k|\) leaves a bounded term, and on the circle the [two-logarithm distance estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#circle) gives \(O_Q(\log(2k))\).
Consequently \(\log C_k(Q)=k m(Q)+O_Q(\log(2k))\).
Divisor inversion gives \(\log D_k(Q)=\varphi(k)m(Q)+O_Q(\tau(k)\log(2k))\), which suffices by \(\tau(k)\ll k^{1/8}\) and \(\varphi(k)\ge\sqrt{k/2}\) as in the [primitive-orbit estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#regularized-orbits).

On any interval of consecutive orders with lower end and length tending to infinity with \(p\), the two statistics of \(F_p\) therefore tend uniformly to \(m(Q)\) after discarding the multiples of \(p\), a proportion \(O(1/p+1/(\text{interval length}))\).
In a fixed linear window \(ADp\le j\le BDp\), \(0<A<B\), the only possible exceptions are \(j=kp\) for integers \(AD\le k\le BD\), where (4) gives the exact two base values.
On the entire tail \(j\ge H_pDp\) with \(H_p\to\infty\), the convergence is uniform without any deletions, since \(j/\gcd(j,p)\ge H_pD\).
It therefore holds for any probability-weighted average of either statistic on the tail, including weights chosen after seeing the polynomial.

## What an absolute lower bound for a sampled average would imply

For \(Y_d=d(\log(2d))^2\) the following two assertions are equivalent.

1. There is a constant \(\eta>0\) with \(m(Q)\ge\eta\) for all nontorsion algebraic units.
2. There is a constant \(c>0\) such that every nontorsion unit of sufficiently large degree \(d\), with minimal polynomial \(P\), has some order \(Y_d\le j\le2Y_d\) with \(X_j(P)\ge c\).

The same equivalence holds upon replacing \(X_j\) by \(Z_j\), or upon requiring a fixed positive proportion of composite orders in place of a single order.
Under the first assertion, (2) gives \(X_j(P)\ge\eta/2\) in density one, and a fixed positive proportion also have \(\varphi(j)/j\ge a\) for an absolute \(a>0\) by the bounded first moment of \(j/\varphi(j)\).
Integrality gives \(Z_j(P)\ge(\varphi(j)/j)X_j(P)\) on this set.
Conversely the entire superlinear tail conclusion applied to \(F_p\) gives \(m(Q)\ge c\) for each \(Q\), even if only a single order is required and its choice depends on \(F_p\).
This converse works on every range with lower end divided by \(d\) tending to infinity.
For the linear window the same obstruction holds for any fixed positive proportion of integer orders, by the finite list of exceptional multiples in (4).
These lifts attain the coefficient one of the base measure for each fixed \(Q\).
The near-linear absolute estimate (3) uses normalization by \(d\), while the two equivalent criteria use \(\varphi(j)\) or \(j\).
