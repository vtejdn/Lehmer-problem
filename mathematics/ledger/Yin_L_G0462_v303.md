# Single-exponent splittings at hyperbolic units and the two terminal supports of the plastic number

<a id="regular"></a>
## Recognizing a split inside the span

Fix a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\) with no conjugate of modulus one, and let \(P\) be its monic minimum.
The binary endpoint-to-endpoint words of the [minimal ternary supports](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v576.md#series) that admit no [split at one intermediate exponent](Yin_L_G0462_v295.md#splitting) form an effectively regular language.
This holds both for translation classes and for patterns obtained by removing translations and all common dilations.
The two ordinary generating series by support size are rational and effectively computable.
Frougny and Steiner recognize zero with a Pisot carry automaton and minimize digit weight among equivalent representations with a separate test. [Pisot automata](../audits/literature/FS08/Yin_U001.md#zero)
Here the test for a minimal support checks all proper subsets for a nontrivial zero representation.

Take the minimal-support automaton and allow leading and trailing zeros when reading each of two auxiliary words of the same length as the input.
At an input one, assign a one to exactly one auxiliary word; at an input zero, give both a zero except at exactly one position, where both receive a one.
Accept this auxiliary test if both padded words are minimal supports.
Their shared position is the intermediate exponent, and every position in their symmetric difference is an original exponent.
Conversely any split in the original span gives one of these assignments.
Determinizing the auxiliary test, complementing it and intersecting with the original minimal-support language therefore recognizes the nonsplitting translation classes.
No condition on the signs is needed, since each child relation can be multiplied by either sign to cancel the term at the shared exponent.

Let \(Q\) be an effective integer such that \(\rho^{Q+1}>2\), where \(\rho>1\) is the house of \(\alpha\).
A dilation \(q\) in any realizing support satisfies \(q\le Q\) by the [house bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v576.md#series).
For each \(1\le q\le Q\) feed the first bit of the candidate into the nonsplitting automaton directly, and insert \(q-1\) zeros before each subsequent bit.
The automaton then tests the actual dilated word at the original input, including possible intermediate exponents outside the residue class of its support modulo \(q\).
This last condition prevents a direct substitution of the nonsplitting test at \(\alpha^q\) for the one at \(\alpha\).
Take the union over \(q\), and require gcd one by excluding words with all exponents divisible by some prime \(p\le Q\), using the least exponent as origin.
Indeed a common exponent gcd \(g\) in a word accepted for \(q\) would imply a ternary relation at \(\alpha^{qg}\), whence \(qg\le Q\).
Each resulting deterministic automaton counts distinct words once, regardless of the number of witnesses.
In either language the number of words of fixed weight is finite by the fixed-input span bound for minimal supports, since dividing exponents only reduces span.
The trimmed graph therefore has no zero-only cycle, and the [weighted transfer matrix](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v576.md#series) yields the asserted rational series.

## Plastic-number classification

For \(P=X^3-X-1\), the only nonsplitting minimal supports up to translation are \(\{0,1,3\}\) and \(\{0,4,5\}\).
Both have exponent gcd one, so both weight series are exactly \(2z^3\).
For comparison, the complete series already has 206 translation classes at weight seven. [All minimal supports at the plastic number](Yin_L_G0462_v293.md#results)
Thus every minimal relation support in this example can be obtained by repeated cancellation of one intermediate exponent from translates of these two triples, with all intermediate supports inside the original span.
This is a statement about supports and does not count sign assignments or assert uniqueness of the decomposition.

The following reproducible calculation uses the fixed predecessor construction from L293 and runs from the repository root with Python and SymPy.
It reads high exponents first, so the two words correspond in order of increasing span to \(X^3=X+1\) and \(X^5=X^4+1\).
After the mandatory first bit the product graph has 149 nonterminal reachable states, of which seven can reach acceptance.
The accepting suffixes are enumerated on the live graph, where the path assertion checks that no directed cycle can generate further words.
As a separate finite check, exact grouping of the \(2^{14}\) binary subset sums modulo \(P\) gives 174 minimal supports inside \([0,14)\); testing all pairs for a single internal intersection splits 154, leaving the eleven and nine translates of the two triples.
The graph calculation supplies the unrestricted classification.
