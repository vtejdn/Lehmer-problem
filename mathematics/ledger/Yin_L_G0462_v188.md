# A square-root lower bound for the measure of every cubic factor and its power lifts

<a id="finite"></a>
## A finite estimate from the gaps at the two ends of a sparse polynomial

Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have nonzero constant coefficient, degree \(D>0\), at most \(s\) nonzero coefficients and coefficient one-norm at most \(L>1\).
Let \(G>0\) be a lower bound on the distance from each endpoint exponent to its nearest other occupied exponent.
For every monic irreducible nontorsion unit factor \(P\mid F\), its logarithmic Mahler measure satisfies
\[
                    m(P)^2\ge
       \frac{\log(1+0.31/s!)}{20\log L}\,\frac G D .                    \tag{1}
\]
Indeed at any exterior root \(z\) comparison with the leading term gives \(|z|^G\le L\), since the absolute leading coefficient is at least one.
Reversing \(F\) gives the same bound on \(1/|z|\) for an interior root.
If \(P\) has degree \(d\) and \(u_j\) are the logarithmic moduli of its roots, then \(\sum |u_j|=2m(P)\) by the unit condition.
The [all-root square bound, Part A](../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) gives
\[
                  \tfrac1{10}\le d\sum_j u_j^2
                            \le (2d\log L/G)m(P).
\]
On the other hand [Dobrowolski's sparse divisor inequality](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible) and concavity of the logarithm give \(m(P)\ge(d/D)\log(1+0.31/s!)\).
Multiplying the two lower bounds for \(m(P)\) proves (1).
For the cubic parent \(A(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\), \(g(T)=T^3-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), and \(b>a\), the two endpoint gaps are at least \(a\).
For \(A(X^q)\) they are at least \(qa\), its degree is \(q(3a+b)\), and one can use \(L=s=6\) for every \(q\ge1\).

<a id="limit"></a>
## The asymptotic bound for arbitrary power lifts

Let \(a,b\) be coprime, \(r=b/a\to\infty\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the real zero of \(g\).
For every nontorsion irreducible factor \(S\) of \(-A(X^q)\), uniformly over \(q\ge1\), one has
\[
 \sqrt r\,m(S)\ \ge\ \frac{1-o(1)}{\sqrt{10}},\qquad
 \deg S\ \ge\ \frac{(1-o(1))q\sqrt{ab}}{\sqrt{10}\log\theta}.           \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the total number of nontorsion factors of \(-A(X^q)\), counted with multiplicity, is at most \((\sqrt{10}\log\theta+o(1))\sqrt r\), independently of the substitution exponent.

First take \(q=1\), write \(P=S\), and let \(k\) be the number of complex-conjugate pairs of exterior roots received by \(P\).
The [power projection near the two cubic poles](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#projection) and the [exterior partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) give \(2k\) exterior roots, each with logarithmic modulus
\[
                   (\log\theta+O(e^{-c r}))/(2a).
\]
The remaining nonzero logarithmic moduli belong to the \(2k\) reciprocal interior roots and have opposite signs.
In particular \(m(P)=(k/a)(\log\theta+O(e^{-cr}))\), the sum of the squared logarithms is \((1+o(1))m(P)^2/k\), and the [individual degree comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#degree) gives \(d=(1+o(1))kr\), uniformly over all such factors.
It follows that \(d\sum u_j^2=(1+o(1))r m(P)^2\).

For arbitrary \(q\), take a root \(\gamma\) of \(S\), put \(\alpha=\gamma^q\), let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\), and put \(t=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):\mathbb Q(\alpha)]\).
Each conjugate of \(\alpha\) has exactly \(t\) lifts among the conjugates of \(\gamma\), all with modulus equal to the \(q\)-th root of the original modulus.
Consequently \(\deg S=td,\ m(S)=(t/q)m(P)\), and its degree times its sum of squared logarithmic moduli is exactly \((t/q)^2 d\sum u_j^2\).
Dividing by \(m(S)^2\) cancels the lift factor, leaving \((1+o(1))r\) with the same uniform error as on the base.
The all-root square bound on \(\gamma\) proves the first part of (2).
The ratio \(m(S)/\deg S=m(P)/(qd)=(1+o(1))\log\theta/(qb)\) gives the degree assertion.
Finally the total log measure of \(-A(X^q)\) is \(m(A)=\log\theta+O(e^{-cr})\) by the partition, so summing the individual lower bounds proves the count.

<a id="allocation"></a>
## Required growth of the larger exponent

For a base factor the first bound in (2) requires
\[
                 \frac{b k^2}{a^3}\ge
                          \frac{1-o(1)}{10(\log\theta)^2}.               \tag{3}
\]
For a lift of relative degree \(t\), the same lower bound holds with \(k\) replaced by \(kt/q\).
In particular a bounded exterior allocation along \(a\to\infty\) requires \(b\) at least of cubic order in \(a\).
The [previous uniform degree floor](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) gives the additional restriction \(k/a\gg(\log\log b/\log b)^3\) on the base.
For fixed bounded \(k\), that restriction already forces much faster growth of \(b\) than (3) does.
The square-root estimate adds information in slowly escaping directions, where \((b/a)^{-1/2}\) exceeds this logarithmic scale, and controls the loss in all power lifts with the explicit asymptotic constant in (2).
An absolute lower bound for the individual measure as \(b/a\to\infty\) remains open.
