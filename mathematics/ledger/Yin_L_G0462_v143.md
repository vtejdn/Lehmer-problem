# A uniform Salem gap and two remaining limiting profiles at Chebyshev separation two

<a id="families"></a>
## Two signs at fixed separation

Let \(D_j(z+z^{-1})=z^j+z^{-j}\) and \(C=2D_k+\delta D_{k-2}\), \(k\ge2,\ \delta=\pm1\).
For \(\delta=-1\), the Salem numbers whose monic trace minimal polynomials \(S\) have degree \(m=k+1\) and are strictly interlaced by \(C\) are uniformly bounded away from one.
For \(\delta=1\), on any hypothetical sequence of such Salem numbers tending to one, every subsequence admits a further subsequence on which \(P=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) converges coefficientwise to \(1-z\) or \(1\).
Writing \(x=z+z^{-1}\), the corresponding limiting symmetric circle measures for the trace weights have densities respectively
\[
                    (x+2)(x^2-x+1)/2,\qquad (4-x^2)(x^2+1)/2
\]
against Haar probability.
Realization or exclusion of these last two profiles remains open here.

## The torsion factors of the limiting rational function

Along a sequence \(\sigma\to1\), \(k\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness.
Use the [positive trace weights, moment series and limiting rationality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) with this \(C\).
Explicitly \(C^\sharp=z^k C(z+z^{-1})=B(z)+z^{2k-2}B^*(z)\), where \(B=2+\delta z^2,\ B^*=2z^2+\delta\).
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) applies to the doubled weak limiting circle measure as there.
Thus the limit moment series \(G\) has positive real part, is rational with simple cyclotomic poles, and satisfies
\[
      G P_0=(1-z^2)B,\qquad P_0=\lim P\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]] .
\]
The endpoint argument gives \(G(-1)=0\), since \(|C(-2)|=|4+2\delta|\le6\) bounds the inverse endpoint integral.
The numerator of reduced \(G\), with integral squarefree cyclotomic denominator normalized at zero, is primitive.
Indeed its constant is two and if all its coefficients were even, the displayed identity would be even coefficientwise, although \([z^2](1-z^2)B\) is odd.
Its unique irreducible even-constant factor has a dyadic root of modulus less than one by the [Gauss norm argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#phase).
Evaluating the displayed series identity at that root forces the factor to be \(B\) up to sign, since \(B\) is irreducible by Eisenstein at two.
As in the same argument, every remaining factor is cyclotomic by positivity and Kronecker's theorem.

Write \(G=B E/F\) with the coprime cyclotomic products \(E,F\) normalized at zero, \(r=-2-\deg E+\deg F\), and \(E(1/z)/F(1/z)=\eta z^{r+2}E(z)/F(z)\).
The boundary-pole and boundary-zero argument in the same [phase proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#phase) applies to \(2\Re G=(E/F)H_r\), where \(H_r=B+\eta z^r B^*\).
It gives squarefreeness of \(E,F\) and requires each of their roots to be a zero of \(H_r\).
Here \(1+z\mid E\) and \(H_r(-1)=0\) give \(\eta=-(-1)^r\).

At a torsion zero \(\xi\) put \(a=\delta\xi^2,\ b=-\eta\delta\xi^r\).
The equation becomes \(2+a=b(1+2a)\), or \(2(1-ab)=b-a\).
All algebraic conjugates of the integer \(1-ab\) have modulus at most one.
If it is zero then \(b=a=\pm1\).
Otherwise its nonzero norm forces all those moduli to equal one.
Equality \(|b-a|=2\) gives \(b=-a\) and substitution gives \(a^2+a+1=0\).
Consequently all possible orders of \(\xi\) are \(1,2,3,4,6\) for \(\delta=1\), and \(1,2,4,12\) for \(\delta=-1\).

## Exact division and the circle densities

Order two is mandatory in \(E\).
The reversal sign \(\eta\) requires the factor \(1-z\) to appear in \(E\) or in \(F\) precisely for even \(r\), as it alone reverses with a minus sign.
Order four is allowed by \(H_r\) precisely for \(r\equiv2\bmod4\), by substituting \(\xi^2=-1\).
For each remaining order the second torsion case above requires \(\xi^{r-2}=\eta\).
For the plus sign this allows order three only for \(r\equiv5\bmod6\) and rules out order six by parity.
For the minus sign it allows order twelve only for \(r\equiv8\bmod12\).

Assign an eligible factor a sign \(1,0,-1\) according as it lies in \(E\), is absent, or lies in \(F\).
For odd \(r\) the only possible degree difference \(\deg E-\deg F\) is one, with an optional addition of \(\pm2\) from order three in the plus case.
The identity \(\deg E-\deg F=-2-r\) gives \(r=-3\) with no order three, or \(r=-1\) with order three in the denominator, after its congruence is imposed.
For even \(r\), the order-twelve option would require \(r\equiv8\bmod12\), which excludes order four.
Including order twelve would then give a degree difference \(1\pm1\pm4\), none yielding that congruence.
It remains to take the difference \(1\pm1\) from orders two and one, with an optional contribution \(\pm2\) from order four when \(r\equiv2\bmod4\).
These choices give exactly the following rows before testing the sign of the density.
Here \(D=2\Re G\) away from the poles is twice the absolutely continuous density.
The last column gives each individual atom of the mass-two limiting measure.

| \(r\) | \(E/F\) | \(P_0=(1-z^2)F/E\) | \(D\) for \(\delta=1\) | \(D\) for \(\delta=-1\) | Atom mass at each listed point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(-3\) | \(1+z\) | \(1-z\) | \((x+2)(x^2-x+1)\) | \((x+2)(3+x-x^2)\) | None |
| \(-1\) | \((1+z)/(1+z+z^2)\) | \(1-z^3\) | \(x+2\) | Ineligible | \(1/2\) at each primitive third root |
| \(-4\) | \(1-z^2\) | \(1\) | \((4-x^2)(x^2+1)\) | \((4-x^2)(3-x^2)\) | None |
| \(-6\) | \(1-z^4\) | \(1/(1+z^2)\) | \((4-x^2)x^4\) | \((4-x^2)^2x^2\) | None |
| \(-2\) | \((1-z^2)/(1+z^2)\) | \(1+z^2\) | \(4-x^2\) | \(-(4-x^2)\) | \((2-\delta)/2\) at each of \(i,-i\) |
| \(-2\) | \((1+z)/(1-z)\) | \((1-z)^2\) | \(-x(x+2)\) | \(x(x+2)\) | \(2+\delta\) at \(1\) |

The density entries follow by adding \(G(z)\) and \(G(z^{-1})\).
At a pole \(\xi\) the atom is \(\lim_{u\uparrow1}(1-u)G(u\xi)/2\).
The two primitive third roots have equal mass by conjugation and the continuous density has mass one, giving their individual masses.
The other pole entries follow directly from \(1+z^2\) or \(1-z\) in the denominator.
For \(\delta=-1\) only \(r=-6\) has nonnegative density throughout \([-2,2]\).
For \(\delta=1\) only the last row fails this condition.

## Removing the profiles with small prediction norm

In the \(r=-6\) row the doubled density equals \(|h|^2\), where \(h=(1-z^2)(1+z^2)^2\) for the plus sign and \(h=(1-z^2)^2(1+z^2)\) for the minus sign.
In the additional plus rows \(r=-1\) and \(E/F=(1-z^2)/(1+z^2)\), take \(h=1+z\) and \(h=1-z^2\), respectively.
All these \(h\) have constant one and only circle zeros.
Choose an integral polynomial \(A\) of constant one with only circle zeros that vanishes at one and at every atom in the row, for instance the product of the corresponding distinct normalized cyclotomic factors.
There is a fixed real polynomial \(q\) divisible by \(A\), \(q(0)=1\), such that \(\int |qh|^2\,dm<2\).
Indeed \((Ah)(z)/(Ah)(uz)\to1\) in \(L^2\) as \(u\uparrow1\) by bounded convergence for the finitely many individual unit-circle factors, as in the [prediction argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v46.md#prediction).
For fixed \(u<1\), approximate \(1/(Ah)(uz)\) by Taylor polynomials and multiply by \(A(z)\).
The resulting \(q\) has squared norm below one against the limiting circle measure, including its atoms.

Write \(P_0=N/J\) in lowest terms with \(N(0)=J(0)=1\).
In the four profiles just considered, \(J=1\) or \(1+z^2\), and \(N=1,\ 1-z^3\) or \(1+z^2\).
The integral reciprocal polynomial \(J P\) has even degree \(2m+\deg J\), retains the exterior root and tends coefficientwise to \(N\).
Its first coefficient differing from \(N\) occurs at an index \(n\to\infty\) with \(n\le m+\deg J/2\).
Otherwise, by reciprocity \(JP=N+z^{2m+\deg J}N(1/z)\), a product of \(N\) and a signed binomial because \(N\) is signed reciprocal.
All its roots would be on the circle.
Thus for large \(k\) this \(n\) occurs strictly before the upper band at \(2k-2\) in the moment numerator \(J(1-z^2)C^\sharp\).

The identity \(G_i(JP)=J(1-z^2)C^\sharp\) and its limit \(GN=J(1-z^2)B\) show that actual moments agree with \(G\) below \(n\) and differ at \(n\) by \(-2p\), where \(p=[z^n](JP-N)\) is a nonzero integer.
At \(n-1\) the baseline coefficients are bounded by the doubled mass, so positivity of the neutral measure gives \(t_i\sigma_i^n=O(1)\) for the exterior weight \(t_i\).
Apply the [first varying coefficient pairing](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#departure) to \(q(z)\) and \(z^nq(z^{-1})\) on that neutral measure.
Its baseline cross moment vanishes for large \(n\) since \(q\) vanishes at all atoms and \(|h|^2\) has finite Fourier support.
The top lag contributes \(-p\), and removing the exterior contribution costs \(o(1)\) since \(q(1)=0\) and \(t_i\sigma_i^n\) is bounded.
The equal squared norms of the two test vectors tend to the limiting squared norm of \(q\), strictly below one, contradicting Cauchy–Schwarz.
This excludes the only nonnegative minus profile and leaves exactly the two necessary plus profiles in the statement.
