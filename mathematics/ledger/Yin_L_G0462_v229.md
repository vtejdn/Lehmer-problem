# Four residue classes suffice for two coprime rows on irreducible power substitutions

<a id="rows"></a>
## Simultaneous support at the substitution prime and the powering prime

Use the [irreducible representatives](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#statement) \(P(x)=P_0(x^n)\), where \(P_0\) is the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion unit \(\alpha\), \(e=\deg P_0\ge4\), \(n\) is an odd prime, \(d=ne,\ e\le\log(3d)\), and \(h=m(P)=m(P_0)\) is logarithmic Mahler measure.
Let \(\gamma^n=\alpha\), \(q=\gamma+\gamma^{-1},\ q_0=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), and let \(p\) be any prime in the fourth-logarithmic window of \(d\).
For sufficiently large \(d\) one has \(p<n\), \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^p)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), and \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
Take \(1\le r<n\) such that \(pr=1+vn\) with \(1\le v<p\), and write \(\mathcal E=X\,d/dX\).

For either prescribed sign \(\sigma=\pm1\) there are two primitive integral diagonals
\[
 F_i(x)=A_i(x^p)+\sigma x^{4dp+1}A_i(x^{-p}),\qquad
 A_i(X)=a_i(X^n)+X^r b_i(X^n),\qquad
 \deg a_i=4e,\quad a_i(0)\ne0,\quad 0\le\deg b_i\le4e-1 .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Each \(A_i\) is coprime to \(A_i^*(X)=X^{4d}A_i(1/X)\) over \(\mathbb Q[X]\), and \(P^2\mid F_i\).
The four occupied residue classes modulo \(np\) are exactly the four combinations \(0,1\bmod p\) and \(0,1\bmod n\).
Both rows have nonzero values at \(\gamma^p\) and linearly independent value and Euler derivative vectors there.
The two reduced odd trace polynomials on each row are \(U_i(C_n(Y)),V_i(C_n(Y))\) with \(U_i,V_i\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degrees \(<e/2\), where \(C_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
Their ratio \(V_i(q_0)/U_i(q_0)\) is irrational, and each individual derivative remainder is nonzero.
Both cofactor measures, all the normalized norms and all the reduced trace costs in [C403](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) obey the same \(O(ph)\) bounds.
In addition \(\log\|A_i\|_1\ll ph\), with an absolute constant.

Two residue classes modulo \(n\) in \(A_i\) are necessary for a nonzero value at \(\gamma^p\) in this endpoint normalization.
Indeed one class would have to be zero, so \(A_i=a_i(X^n)\).
In the decomposition of \(F_i\) by residues modulo \(n\), divisibility by \(P_0(x^n)^2\) would force its component \(a_i(t^p)\) to be divisible by \(P_0(t)^2\), and hence \(A_i(\gamma^p)=0\).

<a id="space"></a>
## Centered jets with both reduced traces in the seed field

Put \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha),\ K_0=\mathbb Q(q_0)\), \(m_0=e/2,\ z=\alpha^p,\ L=ph+\log(2dp)\), and \(J=4dp+1\).
Choose the coefficient variables of \(a,b\) in the power basis, allowing degrees \(4e,4e-1\), and those of \(U,V\) in the basis \(1,C_1,\ldots,C_{m_0-1}\).
For \(u=U(q_0),w=V(q_0)\) impose the four linear equations in \(K\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 z^{-2e}a(z)&=u,& z^{-2e}\alpha^v b(z)&=-\sigma u,\\
 z^{-2e}\{2pn(\mathcal Ea)(z)-J a(z)\}&=w,&
 z^{-2e}\alpha^v\{2pn(\mathcal Eb)(z)+(2pr-J)b(z)\}&=\sigma w .
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{2}
\]
Since \((\gamma^p)^r=\gamma\alpha^v\), these equations give
\[
 (\gamma^p)^{-2d}A(\gamma^p)=(1-\sigma\gamma)u,\qquad
 (\gamma^p)^{-2d}\{2p(\mathcal EA)(\gamma^p)-JA(\gamma^p)\}
                          =(1+\sigma\gamma)w .
\]
Applying the inverse involution proves the value and derivative cancellations \(P^2\mid F\), as in the [odd trace chart](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd).
We have \(q_0=C_n(q)\), and composition with \(C_n\) sends \(C_j\) to \(C_{nj}\) for \(j\ge1\).
Thus the proposed reduced traces at \(q\) have degrees \(<d/2\), unchanged Chebyshev coefficients and the indicated values.

There are \(9e+1\) integral coefficient variables.
We give the size argument for the four seed-field equations, since imposing them on the full field would lose the support bound.
Take coefficients in \(\{0,\ldots,T\}\) and form the four differences in (2), which are algebraic integers since \(\alpha,z\) are units.
At an embedding with image \(\alpha'\), set \(s=|\log|\alpha'||\).
Every difference there has absolute value at most
\[
        C(e+1)(J+1)(T+1)\exp((2ep+p)s)
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\).
Indeed the centered exponents of \(z\) have absolute value at most \(2e\), the factor \(\alpha^v\) adds less than \(p\) to the absolute exponent, and the trace monomials have absolute exponents below \(e/2\).
The coefficients from the Euler operator and the two shifts cost \(O(J)\).
Divide the real embedding coordinates and both real coordinates of each complex pair into intervals of length at most \(1/3\).
Two algebraic integers in one such box must coincide by the integral norm of their difference.
Using \(\sum_{\alpha'} |\log|\alpha'||=2h\), the number of possible quadruples is at most \((T+1)^{4e}\exp(C_1 e L)\) for an absolute \(C_1\).
With \(l=\lfloor e/4\rfloor\ge1\) take \(\log(T+1)>2C_1eL/l\) and \(\log(T+1)=O(L)\).
One fiber has more than \((T+1)^{5e+1-l}\) points.
Some set of \(s'\) coordinate projections is injective on any rational subspace of dimension \(s'\), so the differences in this fiber span a solution space of dimension \(>5e+1-l\) with a basis of integral coefficient vectors of size at most \(T\).

For \(u=w=0\), equations (2) require \(a,b\) both divisible by \(Q_0^2\), where \(Q_0=\min_{\mathbb Q}(z)\) has degree \(e\).
The trace representatives then vanish and the kernel has dimension at most \((2e+1)+2e=4e+1\).
The image in \(K_0^2\) therefore has rational dimension \(>e-l>m_0\).
By the [linear trace argument](Yin_L_G0462_v206.md#remainders), this image admits pairs with \(u\ne0\) and \(w/u\notin\mathbb Q\), and two vectors with \(u_1w_2-u_2w_1\ne0\).
These conditions can hold simultaneously on the two choices.
Indeed their failures are avoided by a product of nonzero rational polynomial components of the first values, coordinate minors for irrationality and a component of the two-vector determinant.
This product has bounded total degree, so evaluation on a bounded integer grid supplies two combinations of the short basis with logarithmic coefficient size \(O(L)\).
The identity \(2p(\gamma^p)^{-4d}W=(1-\gamma^2)(u_1w_2-u_2w_1)\) gives the required nonzero determinant \(W\) of the full value and derivative vectors.

## Removing common roots without leaving the two residue classes

For either choice of \(A\), the formal reversal about \(4d\) gives \(E=A-A^*\) with \(E(\gamma^p)\ne0\) by the [centered value identity](Yin_L_G0462_v222.md#perturbation).
Add to \(a(Y)\) the polynomial
\[
        Q_0(Y)^2\bigl\{\lambda(1+Y^{2e})+\nu Y^e\bigr\}.               \tag{3}
\]
After substituting \(Y=X^n\) this change is reciprocal about \(4d\), keeps \(E\) fixed, and preserves (2).
The powered minimum is \(Q_0(X^n)\) by [C403](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers).
Thus at every nonzero zero \(\xi\) of \(E\) the coefficient of \(\nu\) in the new \(A(\xi)\) is nonzero.
We may simultaneously make \(a(Y)\) coprime to \(b^\flat(Y)=Y^{4e-1}b(1/Y)\).
Indeed \(b(z)\ne0\) by (2), so by the reciprocity of \(Q_0\) its roots are disjoint from the nonzero roots of \(b^\flat\).
At every nonzero root of \(b^\flat\) the coefficient of \(\nu\) in the new \(a\) is therefore nonzero as well.
Both endpoint coefficients of \(a\) have slope one in \(\lambda\).
The product of these nonvanishing conditions has degree at most \(4d+4e+2\), and a grid from zero through that degree in each parameter gives both gcds and both nonzero endpoints.
Since \(\log\|Q_0\|_1\le ph+e\log2\), the final \(\log\|A\|_1\) is \(O(L+e)\).
Divide each row by its positive scalar content.
The odd chart keeps its reduced traces integral after division and all nonvanishing conditions and coprimality persist.
Since the full Chebyshev representatives have only indices divisible by \(n\), this division also leaves integral seed representatives \(U,V\).
The two nonempty classes of \(A\) yield exactly the four classes of \(F\) in (1), since the two halves of the diagonal lie in disjoint residue classes modulo \(p\).

Throughout the given window \(e+\log(2dp)=o(ph)\) by [C403](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence).
The coefficient norm gives \(m(F)\ll ph\), and hence the same upper bound for the cofactor measure.
The squarefree lift of the first reduced trace has measure \(O(L)\) by its Chebyshev coefficients and the [individual norm estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v199.md#norm) bounds the log norm of a nonzero remainder divided by \(d/2\) by \(O(L)\).
For \(A(\gamma^p)\) and \(W\), the coefficient norm and \(\|\mathcal EA\|_1\le4d\|A\|_1\) give log norms divided by \(d\) of size \(O(ph)\), since \(m(\gamma^p)=ph\) and the respective degree bounds are \(4d,8d\).

## A two-class double-root multiple on the original seed

For either final row put
\[
 G(t)=a(t^p)+\sigma t^{4ep-v}b(t^{-p})
     =a(t^p)+\sigma t^{p-v}b^\flat(t^p).
\]
Then \(\deg G=4ep\), the two classes are \(0,p-v\bmod p\), and their rows are coprime by the second gcd imposed above.
With \(t=x^n\), direct grouping gives
\[
               F(x)=G(t)+\sigma x\,t^{4ep}G(1/t).
\]
The first class modulo \(n\) now gives \(P_0(t)^2\mid G(t)\) because \(P_0(x^n)^2\mid F(x)\).
In fact \(m(F)\le m(G)+(\log2)/n\).
For \(|t|=1\), multiply the preceding identity over the \(n\) roots \(x^n=t\).
The product equals \(G(t)^n-(-\sigma)^n t(t^{4ep}G(1/t))^n\) and has absolute value at most \(2|G(t)|^n\).
Integration of the logarithm proves the bound by the equal-weight averaging over these roots.
Since \(\|G\|_1\le\|A\|_1\), both \(m(F)\) and \(m(G)\) are \(O(ph)\).
More precisely the [two-class determinant estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#finite) at the full input gives \(ph\le2m(F)+O(\log(2+4dp+1))\).
Subtracting \(2h\) at each input shows that both noncyclotomic cofactor measures are between \((1/2-o(1))ph\) and \(O(ph)\) uniformly in the window.
Thus the same four residue classes exhibit two genuine coprime-row comparisons, one for the dilated input and one for the original seed at the same prime.
The [unrestricted shrinking equivalence](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) consequently still holds when the two witnessing diagonals are required to have the four classes in (1) and the stated compressed traces.
Indeed the degrees of reciprocal irreducible seeds in a shrinking sequence tend to infinity, since bounded degree and bounded measure permit only finitely many monic integer polynomials by the elementary symmetric coefficient bounds.
Thus \(e\ge4\) eventually, and the present construction gives the two witnesses at every window prime of arbitrarily large representatives of each such seed.
Conversely the cofactor lower bound forces shrinking of the seed measures on any such sequence with both cofactor measures \(o(p)\).

## The individual remainder norm in the seed trace field

For the final row write \(u=U(q_0),\ w=V(q_0)\) and set \(C=w+u,\ B=2n(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})U'(q_0)\).
The [odd derivative remainder](Yin_L_G0462_v189.md#odd) of the full row is
\[
 D=w+u-2(2\sigma-q)\,(U\circ C_n)'(q)
   =C+B\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\gamma+\sigma}.                           \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(C_n'(q)=n(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})/(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})\) and
\((2\sigma-q)/(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})=-(\gamma-\sigma)/(\gamma+\sigma)\).
If \(D=0\) then \((C+B)\gamma+\sigma(C-B)=0\), which forces \(C=B=0\) since \(\gamma\notin K\), contrary to \(w/u\notin\mathbb Q\).
The exact relative norm is
\[
 N_{\mathbb Q(q)/K_0}D
       =\frac{\alpha(C+B)^n+\sigma(C-B)^n}{\alpha+\sigma}.              \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(K\mathbb Q(q)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\), since adjoining \(\alpha\) to \(\mathbb Q(q)\) breaks its quadratic inverse involution, and \([\mathbb Q(q):K_0]=[\mathbb Q(\gamma):K]=n\).
Thus the desired norm equals the norm from \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) to \(K\).
Taking the product in (4) over all \(n\)-th roots of \(\alpha\) gives (5) because \(n\) is odd.
