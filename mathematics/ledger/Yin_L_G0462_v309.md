# Arbitrary marked primitive supports with varying rational coefficients

<a id="statement"></a>
## Realizing a prescribed exponent pattern

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\ge2\) whose positive powers all have degree \(d\).
For any \(r\ge d+2\) and any distinct integers \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\), there is a common positive dilation \(Q\) for which the powers \(v_i=\alpha^{Qu_i}\) support a terminal relation with nonzero integer coefficients under the [rational splitting rule](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement).
One may mark any term, translate its exponent to zero, and divide out the common integer content of the coefficients.
Thus every marked primitive exponent pattern occurs when the rational coefficient list may vary.
At fixed \(r\), the patterns containing \(0,1\) and \(r-2\) other positions from \(2,\ldots,N\) already give \(\binom{N-1}{r-2}\) different shapes marked at zero.
The power-degree hypothesis holds at [strict degree records](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v588.md#norm).
The conclusion controls neither the coefficient height nor the size of the dilation and gives no lower bound for the number of shapes sharing one prescribed outgoing list.

<a id="dilation"></a>
## Independence after a common dilation

Embed all conjugates \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) in a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), with \(v_2(2)=1\).
Each is a local unit, so a common positive power \(J\) puts all \(x_i^J\) in \(v_2(x_i^J-1)>1\), first by killing the finite residue group and then by repeated squaring.
The logarithms \(\lambda_i=\log(x_i^J)\) are distinct because the exponential inverts the logarithm on this ball and the \(d\) conjugates of \(\alpha^J\) are distinct.
For any choice of \(d\) of the exponents write them as \(w_1,\ldots,w_d\).
The analytic determinant \(\det(\exp(z\lambda_i w_j))\) has first nonzero coefficient in degree \(D=d(d-1)/2\), equal to
\[
 \frac{\det(\lambda_i^a)_{i,0\le a<d}\det(w_j^a)_{0\le a<d,j}}{\prod_{a=0}^{d-1}a!}.
\]
Indeed repeated degrees in the exponential expansion yield repeated columns in the determinant, and the permutations of \(0,\ldots,d-1\) give the displayed two Vandermonde determinants.
Consequently every such determinant is nonzero at \(z=2^n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), simultaneously for the finitely many subsets.
With \(Q=J2^n\) every \(d\) columns \(v_i\) are independent over \(\mathbb Q\), and every larger subset spans \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).

<a id="avoidance"></a>
## Rational partial sums in the coefficient lattice

Write \(L=\{b\in\mathbb Q^r:\sum b_iv_i=0\}\) and \(\ell=r-d\).
For any nonempty proper subset \(I\), the image of the partial sum map \(p_I(b)=\sum_{i\in I}b_iv_i\) on \(L\) is the intersection of the spans of the columns indexed by \(I\) and its complement, since the two representations of an element of the intersection can be subtracted.
Its dimension is \(\min(|I|,d)+\min(r-|I|,d)-d\).
This is positive for singletons and at least two when both sides have at least two terms.
Take an integral basis of the lattice \(L\cap\mathbb Z^r\) and let its coordinates range independently from \(-H\) to \(H\).
All partial sums \(z\) are algebraic integers, with each archimedean conjugate bounded by \(C H\) for a constant depending on the fixed columns and basis.
For \(H\ge2\) we have \(h(z)\le\log H+O(1)\).
If \(z=c\alpha^a\ne0\) for rational \(c\) and integral \(a\), then \(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(z)=\pm c^d\), so \(h(c)\le h(z)\) and \(|a|h(\alpha)\le2h(z)\).
There are consequently only \(O(1+\log H)\) candidate exponents in this box.
For each candidate and each subset with at least two terms on both sides, \(p_I^{-1}(\mathbb Q\alpha^a)\) lies in a proper rational linear subspace of \(L\) by the dimension formula.
Each proper subspace contains at most \((2H+1)^{\ell-1}\) box points since a nonzero linear equation determines one coordinate from the others.
The same estimate excludes a zero singleton coefficient, and its complement then cannot sum to zero either.
The proportion of exceptional points is \(O((1+\log H)/H)\) for the fixed input, giving the claimed integral terminal relations.

## A quartic unit with no dilation needed

For the totally positive unit \(\alpha=2+\zeta_{15}+\zeta_{15}^{-1}\), all four conjugates are distinct and positive.
Indeed it generates the maximal real cyclotomic subfield and is the product \((1+\zeta_{15})(1+\zeta_{15}^{-1})\) of units since \(\Phi_{15}(-1)=1\).
For distinct positive conjugates and increasing integer powers, singularity of a square evaluation matrix would give a real exponential sum with at most \(d\) terms and \(d\) distinct real zeros at the logarithms of the conjugates.
Dividing by the first exponential and applying Rolle's theorem inductively bounds the number of distinct real zeros of a nonzero sum by its number of terms minus one.
Thus the same construction works at \(Q=1\) for every support of weight at least six at this fixed quartic unit.
Even at these original exponents, bounded weight and the partial-sum tests alone leave all primitive supports available as the coefficients vary.
