# Prescribing the offset and both endpoint coefficients of a compressed row

<a id="window"></a>
## A representative for every sufficiently large prescribed prime

Fix the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial \(P_0\) of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), write \(e=\deg P_0\), and let \(h=m(P_0)>0\) be its logarithmic Mahler measure.
For every sufficiently large prime \(p\) and every \(1\le v<p\), there is an odd prime \(n\) for which \(P(x)=P_0(x^n)\) is irreducible, \(d=ne,\ e\le\log(3d)\), and
\[
    y<p\le2y,\qquad y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil,\qquad
    pr=1+vn,\qquad 1\le r<n.                                          \tag{1}
\]
The onset may depend on \(P_0\) and is uniform in \(v\).
Different prescribed offsets at the same \(p\) require different classes of \(n\bmod p\).

Indeed put \(X=\exp(0.9p^{1/4})/(3e)\), and choose a prime \(n\) in \((X,2X]\) in the reduced class \(-v^{-1}\bmod p\).
The [Siegel–Walfisz prime-counting estimate](../audits/literature/Ked07/Yin_U001.md#siegel-walfisz) at \(X,2X\) gives for the count, uniformly in the class,
\[
     \frac{\operatorname{Li}(2X)-\operatorname{Li}(X)}{p-1}
          +O\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^8}\right).
\]
The main term is at least \(X/((p-1)\log(2X))\), of order \(X/(\log X)^5\) for the fixed seed.
It follows that the count tends to infinity.
For all the candidate primes, \((\log(3en))^4/p\to0.9^4\in(1/2,1)\), so the ceiling in (1) causes no endpoint ambiguity.
One has \(n>p\) eventually and \(r=(1+vn)/p\) is an integer strictly between zero and \(n\).
The [prime power-substitution argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers) gives irreducibility at all sufficiently large primes \(n\), the original measure \(h\), and preservation of the degrees under \(p\)-th powering for both the seed and its representative.

## The seed jet conditions for the prescribed offset

Write \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\), \(K_1=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(z=\alpha^p\), and \(Q_0=\min_{\mathbb Q}(z)\).
Fix either sign \(\sigma\), put \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\), and set \(f=Y^{-2e}a,\ g=Y^{-2e}b\) for the two integral seed polynomials.
For \(u,\ell\in K_0\), the four [compressed jet equations](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#space) take the equivalent form
\[
 \begin{aligned}
   (f(z),(\mathcal E f)(z))&=(u,\ell),\\
   (g(z),(\mathcal E g)(z))&=\sigma\alpha^{-v}(-u,\ell+vu/p),\qquad
   w=2pn\ell-u.                                                        \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed \(J=4pne+1\) makes the centered \(a\)-derivative \(2pn\mathcal E f-f\).
The centered \(b\)-derivative including its shift is \(\alpha^v(2pn\mathcal E g+(2pr-1)g)\).
Substitution of its value and \(pr-1=vn\) gives the second line of (2), and reversing the substitutions proves sufficiency.
Only the conversion from \(\ell\) to \(w\) uses \(n\).
For \(u\ne0\) the condition \(w/u\notin\mathbb Q\) is equivalent to \(\ell/u\notin\mathbb Q\), and \(u_1w_2-u_2w_1=2pn(u_1\ell_2-u_2\ell_1)\).

Put \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[z]=\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}]\), since \(z\) is a unit with monic minimum of unit constant term.
For \(c\in\mathcal O\) write \(C_c\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) for its unique power-basis representative of degree \(<e\).
The exact image \(\mathcal J\subset K_1^2\) of the value and Euler derivative of integral Laurent polynomials consists of
\[
      c\in\mathcal O,\qquad d'-(\mathcal E C_c)(z)\in Q_0'(z)\mathcal O
                          \quad\text{for the pair }(c,d').             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed all lifts of \(c\) have the form \(C_c+Q_0 H\) with \(H\) an integral Laurent polynomial.
Their derivative increments are \(zQ_0'(z)H(z)\), filling precisely the stated ideal since \(z\) is a unit.
The kernel of the jet map is \((Q_0^2)\), by applying the same argument to the zero jet and using \(Q_0'(z)\ne0\).
Both pairs in (2) must therefore belong to (3), and that condition is sufficient.
It also automatically gives integral reduced trace representatives for \(u,w\) at \(q_0=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\).
Indeed the fixed subring of \(\mathbb Z[z]\) under inversion is \(\mathbb Z[z+z^{-1}]\), by the unique expansion in \(1,z\) over that trace order, and \(z+z^{-1}=C_p(q_0)\) for the integral Chebyshev polynomial \(C_p\).
Thus \(u,\ell,w\in\mathbb Z[q_0]\), where each may be reduced modulo the monic minimum of \(q_0\).

Every admissible pair has integral representatives within the [seed support limits](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows).
For each class of \(f,g\) modulo \(Q_0^2\), multiply by \(Y^{2e}\) and take its unique integral polynomial remainder of degree \(<2e\).
This works for Laurent classes because \(Y\) is invertible modulo the monic \(Q_0^2\) with constant term one.
The resulting \(a,b\) have the required jets, even before allowing the additional positions through degrees \(4e,4e-1\).
This is a statement about integrality and support, with no height restriction on the specified jets.

## A necessary divisor of the first value at every offset

Put \(s=z+z^{-1}\), let \(R=\mathbb Z[q_0]\), and write \(W_j=(\alpha^j-\alpha^{-j})/(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\in R\).
The recurrence starts at \(W_0=0,W_1=1\) and satisfies \(W_{j+1}=q_0 W_j-W_{j-1}\).
Since \(\alpha^{-v}=(W_{p+v}-zW_v)/W_p\) and \(\mathbb Z[z]=\mathbb Z[s]\oplus z\mathbb Z[s]\), the condition \(\alpha^{-v}u\in\mathbb Z[z]\) on the second value in (2) is exactly
\[
                u W_v/W_p,\quad u W_{p+v}/W_p \ \in\ \mathbb Z[s].     \tag{4}
\]
Here \(W_p\ne0\) since \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
For \(v=1\) the first test is simply \(u/W_p\in\mathbb Z[s]\).
For every \(v\) under (1), (4) forces \(W_p\mid u\) in \(R\).
Indeed the \(k\)-th power of the recurrence matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}q_0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has rows \((W_{k+1},-W_k)\) and \((W_k,-W_{k-1})\).
Modulo \(W_k\) it is the scalar \(-W_{k-1}\), a unit by its determinant one.
Consequently reduction of the larger index modulo the smaller preserves the ideal generated by two \(W\)'s, so \((W_v,W_p)=R\) by the Euclidean algorithm and \(\gcd(v,p)=1\).
Now \(uW_v/W_p\in\mathbb Z[s]\subset R\) proves the asserted divisibility.
One also has \(p\mid u\) in \(R\).
Indeed \(\sigma\alpha^v(\mathcal E g)(z)=\ell+vu/p\) lies in \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), whose fixed elements lie in \(R\), and \(p\) is coprime to \(v\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\) at fixed seed, the two ideals \((p),(W_p)\) are comaximal in \(R\).
Indeed \(W_p\equiv(q_0^2-4)^{(p-1)/2}\pmod p\) for odd primes, by the binomial identity in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(\mathbb F_p\), and all sufficiently large primes avoid \(N_{K_0/\mathbb Q}(q_0^2-4)\ne0\).
Hence \(pW_p\mid u\) in \(R\) for all these primes.
In particular every nonzero admissible \(u\) satisfies \(\log|N_{K_0/\mathbb Q}(u)|\ge (e/2)\log p+ph-O_{P_0}(\log(2p))\), since \(W_p^2=(s^2-4)/(q_0^2-4)\) and the [powered endpoint product](Yin_L_G0462_v233.md#growth) has logarithm \(2ph+O_{P_0}(\log(2p))\).

## Keeping the exact jets while making both endpoints one

Take any pair admitted by (2) and (3) with \(u\ne0\), take \(n,r\) as in (1), and start with its integral representatives \(a,b\) of degrees at most \(B=4e\) and \(B-1\), respectively.
In \(a\), add
\[
        Q_0(Y)^2\big((1-a(0))+(1-[Y^B]a)Y^{2e}\big).                 \tag{5}
\]
Since \(Q_0\) is monic reciprocal of degree \(e\) with constant term one, both endpoint coefficients of the resulting \(a\) are exactly one.
All value and derivative data persist.
For this modified \(a\) put \(A(X)=a(X^n)+X^r b(X^n)\), \(A^*(X)=X^{Bn}A(1/X)\) and \(E=A-A^*\).
At \(\beta=\gamma^p\) with \(\gamma^n=\alpha\), (2) gives \(\beta^{-2d}A(\beta)=(1-\sigma\gamma)u\).
Reciprocity therefore gives \(\beta^{-2d}E(\beta)=-\sigma(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})u\ne0\).
So \(E\) and the powered minimum \(Q_0(X^n)\) have no common root.
Also \(b(z)=-\sigma z^{2e}\alpha^{-v}u\ne0\), and reciprocity of \(Q_0\) shows that \(Q_0\) is coprime to \(b^\flat(Y)=Y^{B-1}b(1/Y)\).

Now add \(\nu Y^eQ_0(Y)^2\) to \(a\).
Its support lies in \([e,3e]\), and it is reciprocal about degree \(B\), so neither endpoint nor \(E\) changes.
At each nonzero zero of \(E\) the new \(A\) has nonzero slope in \(\nu\), and at each nonzero zero of \(b^\flat\) the new \(a\) has nonzero slope as well.
At most \(Bn+B-1\) values of \(\nu\) are excluded, so one can choose \(0\le\nu\le Bn+B\) giving
\[
       a(0)=[Y^B]a=A(0)=[X^{Bn}]A=1,\qquad
       \gcd(A,A^*)=\gcd(a,b^\flat)=1.                                 \tag{6}
\]
Zero cannot give a common root in either gcd because of the endpoints.
The final diagonal \(F=A(x^p)+\sigma x A^*(x^p)\) has constant term one and leading coefficient \(\sigma\).
It is primitive without rescaling any of the original jets.
The original equations imply \(P^2\mid F\) by [the value and derivative cancellations](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#space).
The seed graph \(G(t)=a(t^p)+\sigma t^{pB-v}b(t^{-p})\) contains \(P_0(t)^2\), by [separating the two classes modulo \(n\)](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed).
Its two classes are \(0,-v\bmod p\), which are adjacent when \(v=1\).

## Signed short witnesses and the reciprocal integral residual

For \(e\ge4\) start (5) with the two [short rows of L229](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows), at the particular representative just produced for \(p,v\) and either chosen sign.
Their initial \(\log\max(\|a\|_1,\|b\|_1)\) is \(O(ph)\), since the two residue classes inside \(A\) are disjoint.
The additions cost at most \((2+2\|a\|_1+Bn+B)\|Q_0\|_1^2\) in coefficient one-norm.
Since \(\|Q_0\|_1\le 2^e\exp(ph)\) and \(e+\log(2dp)=o(ph)\) in the [representative window](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence), the final coefficient logarithms and both diagonal measures are still \(O(ph)\) with absolute implied constants.
Thus for every sufficiently large prescribed \(p\) and every \(v\), including \(v=1\), both signed choices admit exact offset-one lifted witnesses of span \(4pd+1\) with unit endpoints as in (6).
For each sign their two rows retain the original independent value and Euler derivative vectors, irrational trace ratios and nonzero derivative remainders.
Shortness here gives an \(O(ph)\) estimate and leaves the leading constant in the original span open.
The arithmetic description (2) and (3) also holds in trace degree one, where a trace ratio cannot be irrational.

Finally the [reflected seed determinant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#four) gives
\[
 Y^B\big(a(Y)a(1/Y)-b(Y)b(1/Y)\big)=Q_0(Y)^2 K(Y).
\]
Both extreme coefficients of the left side are one by (6) and \(\deg b<B\).
Thus \(K\) is a monic reciprocal integral polynomial of degree \(6e\) with constant term one.
All its roots, including those of the noncyclotomic part, are algebraic units, while cyclotomic factors and reducibility are allowed.
The [interior perturbation in L234](Yin_L_G0462_v234.md#perturbation) can also be applied starting with (6) if its additional circle estimate is desired, since it changes neither endpoint.
