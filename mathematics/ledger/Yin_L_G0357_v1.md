## 2. Inverse pairing forces a pointwise lower value on every original circle conjugate

Say that mass theta, 0<=theta<=1, is paired under inversion if the weighted unit labels can be decomposed into finitely many pairs (u,u^(-1)), with each member of pair a having weight p_a/2 and sum_a p_a=theta, and arbitrary remaining unit labels of total weight 1-theta.
Repeated units or labels are allowed; this is a decomposition of weights, not a unit-group classification.
The two auxiliaries in each pair are the actual residuals in (1).
Full inversion symmetry means theta=1.

**Proposition 1.** Suppose P has k>0 original conjugates on the unit circle, and let

    zeta_l=(1/k) sum_(|alpha_i|=1) Re(alpha_i^l),
    delta_circ=max_(1<=l<=T)|zeta_l|.              (4)

Then any bundle with paired mass theta satisfies

    G(alpha_i)>=theta for every |alpha_i|=1,
    E_0+2 sum_l H_l zeta_l>=theta,
    kappa delta_circ>=theta S-1.                 (5)

These statements hold with either signed norm +1 or -1, at every window and every positive original Mahler measure.
If theta S>1, exact complementary cancellation H_l=0 for all l is impossible.
In particular a fully inversion-symmetric unit bundle cannot have exact complementarity and ANY positive true surplus eta>0.

*Proof.* At an original circle conjugate z the evaluation map O->C is a field embedding.
By (1), F_u(z)/q=u(z) and F_(u^-1)(z)/q=u(z)^(-1), both nonzero.
For a=|u(z)|>0,

    (a^2+a^(-2))/2-1=(a-a^(-1))^2/2>=0.          (6)

Each paired mass p_a therefore contributes at least p_a to G(z).
The remaining terms are nonnegative squares.
This proves the first inequality.
Average (2) over the k actual circle conjugates and divide by E_0.
The result is 1+2 sum C_l zeta_l>=theta S.
The absolute-value bound 2 sum C_l zeta_l<=kappa delta_circ proves (5), without dividing by a possibly zero delta_circ.
If H=0, its middle inequality gives E_0>=theta, whereas theta S>1 means E_0<theta.
For theta=1, positive eta in (3) is exactly E_0<1.
This is the stated contradiction.
No estimate of the expanding or contracting conjugates was discarded from the income: their complete norm was already used in (1)--(3).
The pointwise assertion simply supplies additional restrictions at existing circle embeddings.
