# Detecting a single cubic factor by reduction of its trace polynomial

<a id="criterion"></a>
## One long irreducible factor modulo a prime

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers with \(b/a\) sufficiently large, and put
\[
 A(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a),\qquad g(T)=T^3-T-1,\qquad B(T)=1-T^2-T^3.
\]
Remove from \(-A\) the factors in the [cyclotomic table](Yin_L_G0462_v172.md#cyclotomic), and write the remaining monic polynomial as \(P(X)=X^n H(X+X^{-1})\), with \(H\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) monic of degree \(n\).
Set \(h=n\) for odd \(n\), and \(h=n-1\) for even \(n\).
Then \(P\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) if and only if at some prime \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H\) the reduction of \(H\) has an irreducible factor of degree \(h\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
For any fixed integer \(m\ge1\), one may require \(p\equiv1\bmod m\) in this equivalence.

Indeed the [exterior partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) gives simplicity and reciprocity of the nontorsion factors, and their degrees grow uniformly by the [factor-degree bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).
For a root of each such factor, adjoining it to the field of its trace has degree two, since the distinct reciprocal root is conjugate and has the same trace.
Thus \(H\) is the product of their distinct monic irreducible trace polynomials, each of degree at least two for sufficiently large \(b/a\).
If there are at least two, every modular factor comes from a rational trace factor of degree at most \(n-2\).
If there is just one, the [individual trace theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#statement) places \(A_n\) inside its rational permutation group \(G\).
An \(h\)-cycle, fixing the remaining letter if \(n\) is even, has even sign.
At a prime of good reduction the roots reduce distinctly in the splitting field, and the Frobenius orbit lengths are precisely the irreducible factor degrees over \(\mathbb F_p\).
The positive densities proved below realize the required degree.

<a id="density"></a>
## Density within a prescribed residue class

Suppose \(P\) is irreducible and write \(K\) for the splitting field of \(H\), \(D=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\operatorname{disc}H})\), and \(B_m=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\).
For a unit residue \(c\bmod m\), the relative natural density of the specified factorizations among primes \(p\equiv c\bmod m\) is
\[
\begin{cases}
2/h,&D=\mathbb Q,\\
1/h,&D\ne\mathbb Q,\quad D\not\subseteq B_m,\\
2/h,&D\ne\mathbb Q,\quad D\subseteq B_m,\quad \sigma_c|_D=1,\\
0,&D\ne\mathbb Q,\quad D\subseteq B_m,\quad \sigma_c|_D\ne1,
\end{cases}
\qquad \sigma_c(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^c .
\]
For reducible \(P\) the density is zero in every residue class by the degree obstruction.

The trace group is \(A_n\) or \(S_n\), with \(n\ge5\) in the stated range.
The sign on the trace labels acts on a Vandermonde square root of \(\operatorname{disc}H\).
Since \(A_n\) is perfect and the commutator subgroup of \(S_n\) is \(A_n\), \(D\) is exactly the maximal abelian subfield of \(K\).
Thus \(K\cap B_m=D\cap B_m\).
The group of \(KB_m/\mathbb Q\) consists of all pairs of automorphisms agreeing on that intersection, by the degree formula for finite Galois composita.
Among pairs restricting to \(\sigma_c\), the first coordinates therefore fill \(A_n\), \(S_n\), or a prescribed sign coset in \(S_n\), as the displayed cases specify.
By [Chebotarev's density theorem in counting form](../audits/literature/KW22/Yin_U001.md#kw22-u001), a conjugation-invariant subset of each such fiber has relative natural density equal to its proportion in the fiber.
Here the full set of \(h\)-cycles with any remaining letter fixed has \(n!/h\) elements, all even, since a cycle has \(h\) equivalent starting points.
The event uses this full set, including both conjugacy classes if the symmetric-group class splits in \(A_n\).
Division by the fiber size gives the formula.
The identity class \(c=1\) always permits the even permutations.

<a id="examples"></a>
## Three untwisted examples with \(b-ka=1\)

Let \(C_0=2,\ C_1=Y,\ C_{j+1}=YC_j-C_{j-1}\), so \(C_j(X+X^{-1})=X^j+X^{-j}\).
For each of the three coprime pairs below, \(a,b\) are odd, \(b>3a\), and the torsion table gives neither \(\Phi_8\) nor \(\Phi_{30}\).
Thus \(-A=X^n H(X+X^{-1})\) with \(n=(b+3a)/2\) and \(H=C_n+C_{n-a}-C_{n-3a}\).
The following checks prove irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) directly for these pairs, independently of the threshold in the trace theorem.

| \((a,b)\) | \(k=(b-1)/a\) | \(H\) | \(p\) | Polynomial \(U\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) | \((j,\deg\gcd(U,Y^{p^j}-Y))\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \((7,85)\) | \(12\) | \(C_{53}+C_{46}-C_{32}\) | \(3\) | \(H\) | \((1,0),(53,53)\) |
| \((9,127)\) | \(14\) | \(C_{77}+C_{68}-C_{50}\) | \(3\) | \(H\) | \((7,0),(11,0),(77,77)\) |
| \((9,109)\) | \(12\) | \(C_{68}+C_{59}-C_{41}\) | \(11\) | \(H/(Y-6)\) | \((1,0),(67,67)\) |

To reproduce the table, generate the three Chebyshev terms by the displayed recurrence modulo \(p\), and reduce \(H\) with those coefficients.
In the last row evaluation gives \(H(6)=0\bmod11\), so the indicated quotient is exact.
Start with \(w=Y\), replace \(w\) by the remainder of \(w^p\) on division by \(U\) at each step \(j=1,\ldots,\deg U\), and take the monic gcd of \(w-Y\) and \(U\) at the listed steps.
With \(d=\deg U\), the final gcd of degree \(d\) makes \(U\) squarefree with all factor degrees dividing \(d\).
The earlier gcds exclude every degree dividing \(d/\ell\) for any prime \(\ell\mid d\), proving irreducibility of \(U\) in each row.
This proves rational irreducibility of the first two trace polynomials.
In the last row a rational factorization of \(H\) would have to include a linear factor by monicity and the irreducible degree \(67\) in its reduction.
Its constant term is \(2\), and \(H(1)=H(-1)=-1,\ H(2)=H(-2)=2\) rule out all possible integer roots.

In the first two rows \(H(-2)=-2,\ H(2)=2\), and in the third row \(H(0)=2,\ H(1)=-1\).
Each irreducible \(H\) therefore has an embedding of its root field with trace \(t\in(-2,2)\).
At that embedding \(t^2-4<0\), so \(X^2-tX+1\) is irreducible over the trace field.
The corresponding root of \(-A\) has degree \(2n\), proving irreducibility of the entire untwisted polynomial in all three cases.

The density concerns primes for one fixed input \((a,b)\).
The examples impose \(b-ka=1\) with \(k=12,14\), so keeping either value fixed makes \(b/a\) tend to that value when \(a\) grows.
Establishing an infinite sequence of such one-factor inputs with both \(a\to\infty\) and \(b/a\to\infty\) remains open here.
