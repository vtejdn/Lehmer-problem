# Exact cost at the minimum row degree for independent inverse pairs

<a id="rate"></a>
## The rate in terms of the moduli of the conjugates

Let \(P\) be the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, with degree \(e=2s\), and choose representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) of its inverse pairs in a splitting field \(L\subset\mathbb C\).
Assume they are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity, meaning that a product \(\prod_i x_i^{a_i}\) with integral exponents can be torsion only if all \(a_i=0\).
Changing any representatives to their inverses preserves this condition.
Arrange the numbers \(\big|\log|x_i|\big|\) in decreasing order \(t_1\ge\cdots\ge t_s\ge0\), and let \(h=m(P)=\sum_j t_j\).

At each integer \(p\ge2\), consider coprime \(U,V\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degrees at most \(e\) with \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\) for \(\iota(Y)=1/Y\) and \(P^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\).
Such rows exist for every sufficiently large \(p\).
Write \(H_p(P)\) for their least cost \(\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) before imposing a cutoff.
Then
\[
             \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_p(P)}p
                      =\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)t_j.                         \tag{1}
\]
Each of the two primitive signed generators has this same logarithmic growth rate.

Use the [signed matrices and cofactor convention](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions).
For each sign \(\varepsilon\), put \(c_i=\varepsilon x_i\).
At node \(i\), column \(d\) has entries
\[
        Y_i^d+c_iY_i^{e-d},\qquad
        NdY_i^d+c_i\{1+N(e-d)\}Y_i^{e-d},\qquad 0\le d\le e .
\]
The signed minors \(C_d\) are \((-1)^d\) times the determinants with column \(d\) removed.
The [fixed-point calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#simple) and [collision calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v253.md#collision) apply to these formal matrices because \(c_i\ne0,\pm1\), and for \(i\ne j\) one has \(c_i\ne c_j\) and \(c_ic_j\ne1\).
These conditions follow from the distinct inverse pairs and the nontorsion hypothesis.
The local calculation chooses the independent *variables* \(Y_j\) at generic complex values, and imposes no restrictions on the complex moduli of the fixed labels \(c_i\).
For \(s=1\) its remaining-row kernel at \(N=0\) is simply \(\langle1,Z,Z^2\rangle\) and only the two fixed points need to be checked.
Consequently the common polynomial divisor of the maximal minors in \(L[N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s]\) belongs to \(L[N]\) up to a nonzero constant.
The calculation of the center minor below shows that even this divisor is constant.

<a id="support"></a>
## Support of the minor with the center column deleted

For \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_s)\in\mathbb R^s\), give a monomial \(\mathbf Y^{\mathbf a}\) weight \(\sum_i a_i u_i\), treating its nonzero coefficient in \(L[N]\) as weight zero.
Let \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_s\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \((|u_i|)_i\).
The maximum weight of the union of the supports of all minors is
\[
               e\sum_i u_i+\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)v_j.                   \tag{2}
\]
The support of \(C_s\) alone attains this value in every direction.

Indeed in each determinant term two distinct selected columns are assigned to each node.
For a column \(d\) assigned to node \(i\), both available exponents \(d,e-d\) have weight at most \(s u_i+|d-s|\,|u_i|\).
The \(e+1\) distances \(|d-s|\) are \(s,s,s-1,s-1,\ldots,1,1,0\).
Omitting the smallest distance and pairing the rest with the two copies of each \(|u_i|\) in decreasing order gives an upper bound for every minor by rearrangement.
Adding the \(s u_i\) terms for both rows gives (2).

First take all \(|u_i|\) positive and distinct.
Upon deletion of column \(s\), the node with the \(j\)-th largest absolute weight must receive precisely columns \(l,e-l\), where \(l=j-1\), to attain this bound.
For \(u_i>0\) both columns must use exponent \(e-l\), with leading coefficient vectors \((c_i,c_i(1+N(e-l)))^\mathsf T\) and \((1,N(e-l))^\mathsf T\) in increasing column order.
Their determinant is \(-c_i\).
For \(u_i<0\) the node uses exponent \(l\) in both columns, with coefficient vectors \((1,Nl)^\mathsf T\) and \((c_i,c_i(1+Nl))^\mathsf T\) and determinant \(c_i\).
Only the two orderings of the assigned pair within each block can contribute at the maximal weight.
After the columns are permuted into these blocks the coefficient of the resulting monomial is therefore \(\pm\prod_i c_i\), a nonzero constant.
This proves sharpness and excludes any nonconstant common divisor in \(L[N]\).
Both the maximum of finitely many linear weights and the expression on the right of (2) are continuous in \(u\).
Taking limits gives (2) at tied absolute values and zero coordinates as well.

Substitute \(N=p,\ Y_i=x_i^p\).
For any nonzero polynomial in these variables the distinct \(\mathbf Y\)-exponents give bases with nontorsion pairwise ratios, so the [fixed-place power-sum estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) makes the specialization eventually nonzero.
At every embedding \(\sigma\), the logarithm of the maximum of the absolute values of the minors is \(p\) times (2) at \(u_i=\log|\sigma x_i|\), plus \(o_P(p)\).
In particular, the specialization statement does not require the extremal weight to come from a unique monomial at the embedding under consideration.
The minors have algebraic-integer coefficients.
Since they have no common nonconstant polynomial factor, the [almost-unit gcd estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) bounds their normalized finite common valuation sum by \(o_P(p)\), as follows.
One nonzero minor and an integer linear combination avoiding every nonconstant irreducible factor of that minor form a coprime pair.
A constant in this pair gives a bounded finite contribution directly.
Otherwise the outside-archimedean height cost of \((p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_s^p)\) in the almost-unit condition is \(\log p\), its height is at least \(ph/e\), and the sum of its coordinate heights is \(O_P(p)\).
For each fixed tolerance its proper exceptional closed set is eventually avoided by applying the same nonvanishing argument to a nonzero polynomial vanishing on that set.
Letting the tolerance tend to zero gives the asserted sublinear contribution. [Height computation for a tuple](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#height)

At every embedding the sorted absolute values of \(u_i\) are exactly the original \(t_j\), since Galois permutes inverse pairs and can invert representatives.
The average of each individual \(u_i\) over the embeddings is zero because \(x_i\) is a unit.
Averaging (2) and subtracting the finite common valuation sum therefore gives \(p\sum_j(e-2j+2)t_j+o_P(p)\) as the absolute projective logarithmic height of each signed vector.

At \(N=0\) and generic nodes both kernels are the lines spanned by the reciprocal monic polynomial \(\prod_i(Z-Y_i)(Z-Y_i^{-1})\).
Thus a maximal minor at each sign and the polynomial \(C_{+,0}C_{-,e}+C_{+,e}C_{-,0}\) are generically nonzero and remain nonzero at every sufficiently large \(p\).
The powered nodes \(x_i^{\pm p}\) are distinct by independence and nontorsion.
The value and derivative equations propagate to the inverse nodes by signed reversal, so the specialized line is the kernel of rational remainder equations modulo \(P^2\).
Its primitive integral generator has logarithmic maximum coefficient equal to the projective height just computed.
Its one-norm differs from that maximum by a factor between one and \(e+1\).
The nonzero edge expression gives coprime qualifying pairs by the [exact two-line minimum formula](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines).
That formula bounds the least cost above and below by positive absolute constant multiples of the larger of the two generator one-norms, proving (1).

## The fixed-seed rates grow with degree

The weights \(e-2j+2\) decrease from \(e\) to \(2\) and have mean \(s+1\).
Their covariance with the decreasing sequence \(t_j\) is nonnegative, since every product of corresponding pairwise differences is nonnegative.
Consequently the rate in (1) is at least \((s+1)h\).
For a Salem seed only \(t_1\) is positive, recovering \(eh\).
For the full independent class [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h>2/(\log(3e))^3\), hence
\[
               \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_p(P)}p
                       >\frac{e+2}{(\log(3e))^3}.
\]
The right side diverges with \(e\), giving a degree-dependent lower bound for the fixed-seed limits throughout the class.
The specialization and gcd errors depend on \(P\), so the argument yields no common onset for simultaneous limits of \(p\) and the seed degree.

There is nevertheless an absolute margin above \((\log 2)/3\).
The minimum of \(x/(\log(3x))^3\) for \(x>1/3\) is \(\exp(3)/81\), attained at \(\log(3x)=3\).
This exceeds \((\log2)/3\), since \(\exp(1)>8/3\), \(\log2<7/10\) by the exponential series, and \(512/2187>7/30\).
Thus every fixed-seed rate in (1) is greater than \((\log2)/3\) by a common positive margin.

Restrict the [prime coefficient test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement) to seeds satisfying the independence hypothesis and to rows of degree at most their seed degree \(e\), with \(e\le B(p)\).
The [individual measure bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#lower) gives \(p\,m(P)\le\log H\), so at \(H\le 2^{\lfloor p/3\rfloor}\) one only needs seeds with \(\exp(m(P))\le2\).
For each fixed degree budget \(k\) these seeds form a finite set, by the elementary symmetric coefficient bound \(2\binom ej\).
There is consequently an onset \(p_k\) past which no such seed of degree at most \(k\) can furnish rows inside that cutoff.
Choose these integer onsets strictly increasing and set \(B(p)=k\) for \(p_k\le p<p_{k+1}\), with any positive value before \(p_1\).
Then \(B(p)\to\infty\) and the restricted test excludes every genuine pair of cost \(H\le2^{\lfloor p/3\rfloor}\) at every sufficiently large odd prime.
The [full zero-gap criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#upper) also admits rows wider than their seed degree and seeds with multiplicative relations among representatives, for which this exclusion gives no conclusion.
