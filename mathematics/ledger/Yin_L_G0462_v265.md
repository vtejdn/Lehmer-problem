# The exact rank of the integer rows for a prescribed reciprocal product

<a id="rank"></a>
## Passing from local multiplicity to all integral solutions

Fix a prime \(p\), a sign \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\) and a formal width \(b\).
Let \(P_i\) be distinct monic irreducible reciprocal minima of nontorsion algebraic units and put
\[
    F=\prod_i P_i^{t_i},\qquad t_i\ge1,\qquad d=\deg F .
\]
Write \(K_b\) for the integral coefficient vectors of the rows \(A\) of degree at most \(b\) satisfying
\[
              F(X)^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_b^*(X^p)
                   \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[X].
\]
At each root \(\xi\) of \(\bar F\) in a splitting field over \(\mathbb F_p\), let \(E_i(\xi)=\operatorname{ord}_\xi\bar P_i,\ e_\xi=\max_i E_i(\xi)\) and \(k_\xi=\sum_i t_i E_i(\xi)\).
Assume \(e_\xi<p-1\) and \(2k_\xi-1<p-e_\xi\) on all occupied disks.
Then \(K_b=0\) for \(b<d\).
For \(b\ge d\), \(K_b\) is a saturated integral lattice of rank \(b+1-d\), and its reduction is exactly
\[
                     \bar F\cdot\mathbb F_p[Y]_{\le b-d}.
\]
With \(A=FC+R,\ \deg R<d\), the map \(A\mapsto C\) on \(K_b\otimes\mathbb Z_p\) is an isomorphism onto \(\mathbb Z_p[Y]_{\le b-d}\).
Thus for each \(j\ge1\) **all** reductions of the exact integer kernel modulo \(p^j\) are parametrized bijectively by \(C\bmod p^j\).
This assertion concerns divisibility by \(F^2\).
The coefficient budget and the nonzero row-value condition can be tested separately.

For the upper rank bound apply the [residue-disk contact estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#disk) to any row in the kernel whose scalar content is prime to \(p\).
There are at least \(2k_\xi\) contacts in each disk, because each original root above it has multiplicity at least \(2t_i\) in the graph.
The local radii follow from the [factor over the residue disk](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#degree).
If the joint weight \(s<k_\xi\), then \(2s+1\le2k_\xi-1<p-e_\xi\), a contradiction.
Consequently \(\bar A\) vanishes at \(\xi^p\) to order at least \(k_\xi\), giving \(\bar F\mid\bar A\) by Frobenius invariance.
This uses the original graph multiplicities even for rows vanishing at a powered root.
The kernel is saturated because it is the kernel of the integral linear map taking a row to its graph remainder modulo the monic \(F^2\).
Its rank therefore equals the dimension of its image modulo \(p\).
This immediately gives the assertions for \(b<d\) and the upper rank bound otherwise.

For the opposite bound consider that remainder map over \(\mathbb Q\).
The involution \(H(X)\mapsto X^{pb+1}H(1/X)\) on \(\mathbb Q[X]/(F^2)\) is well defined since \(X\) is a unit and \(F\) is reciprocal.
Every graph remainder is in its \(\varepsilon\)-eigenspace by signed reversal.
Over a splitting field the distinct reciprocal roots \(\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\) give pairs of local factors of equal dimension \(2t_i\) which the involution interchanges.
No root is \(1\) or \(-1\).
On the sum of two exchanged factors each eigenspace has dimension exactly that of one factor, hence the specified eigenspace in the whole quotient has dimension \(d\).
Thus there are at most \(d\) independent equations.
The two rank bounds give equality.

On the reduction of the kernel, monic division by \(\bar F\) is now a bijection onto all polynomial quotients of degree at most \(b-d\).
An integral basis of \(K_b\) gives a square quotient-coordinate matrix with determinant prime to \(p\).
This proves the asserted isomorphism over \(\mathbb Z_p\) and after reduction at every precision.
Equivalently one may prescribe the top \(b+1-d\) coefficients of the row, as monic division gives a triangular change of these coordinates with unit diagonal.
For a specified \(F\), the matrix for the original equations is computable by modular powering in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(F^2)\).
A saturated integer kernel basis gives the quotient-coordinate inverse to any requested precision.
Surjectivity concerns \(p\)-adic parameters and their finite residues.
A specified ordinary integer parameter vector need not be the quotient of an ordinary integer solution with precisely those integer values.

## Nonvanishing at every powered node in the squarefree case

Suppose \(t_i=1\) and \(p\) is odd, avoids \(\operatorname{disc}(F)F(1)F(-1)\), and meets the preceding local inequalities.
At any width \(b=d+r\) with \(r\ge0\) choose
\(C_0(Y)=Y^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}(Y+1)^{r\bmod2}\).
It is reciprocal at width \(r\) and nonzero on every occupied residue.
The rank result supplies an integral row with \(C\equiv C_0\bmod p\).
Then the reduction \(\bar A=\bar F\bar C_0\) is reciprocal at width \(b\).
Every powered root \(z=\alpha^p\) is in an unramified extension locally since \(\bar F\) is squarefree.
For the prime-to-\(p\) root of unity \(\omega\) lifting \(\xi=\bar\alpha\), one has \(z\equiv y=\omega^p\bmod p^2\) by expansion at \(\omega\).
At weight one the [second-digit equation](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#digits) therefore gives
\[
       A(z)/p\bmod p
        = \bar y\,\bar F'(\bar y)\bar C_0(\bar y)(1+\varepsilon\xi)\ne0 .
\]
Indeed \(V=\varepsilon A_b^*\) has reduction \(\varepsilon\bar A\).
Neither endpoint is occupied and every residue of \(F\) is simple.
Consequently \(v_p(A(\alpha^p))=1\) at every node, and \(\operatorname{Res}(Q_i,A)\) has valuation exactly \(\deg P_i\), where \(Q_i\) is the minimum of \(\alpha_i^p\).
The conjugate powers retain all their degrees here since their residues are distinct.
The exact graph equations also give nonzero second row values.
At \(b=d\) the same conclusions apply to **every primitive generator** of the rank-one lattice, since its reduction is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\bar F\).
For any fixed squarefree reciprocal product these results hold for every sufficiently large prime and either sign, before imposing an archimedean size cutoff.

## Exact searches for a specified pair at 223

The [necessary bounds at 223](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#frontier) give \(b=114,\ H_*=1091659\), \(d=112\) or \(114\), and \(2k_\xi-1<p-e_\xi\) for every pair that could pass.
If a proposed pair fails the local hypotheses through violating the established necessary bounds, it is already excluded.
For any remaining pair the two signed kernels can be found separately.
In the degree-\((56,58)\) case each is an actual integral line.
Compute its primitive generator, compare its one-norm to \(H_*\), then check the two values, their nonvanishing and the required factors.
In the degree-\((56,56)\) case the integral rank is three, so enumerate the vectors below the one-norm cutoff in that lattice.
Alternatively prescribe the top three coefficients within the budget.
Since \(223^3>2H_*\), their residues modulo \(223^3\) determine at most one possible integer row in the budget by taking the centered lift of every coefficient.
The same statement uses just the leading coefficient in the rank-one case.
The final exact tests discard a centered lift that meets only finite-precision equations.
This gives a decision for a **specified** pair and leaves the search over all candidate minima at 223 open.
