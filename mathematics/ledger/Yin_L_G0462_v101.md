# An extremal torus minimum forces a binomial and excludes an independent lower offset

<a id="minimum"></a>
## The two possible polynomials

Let \(T\) be a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial in any finite number of variables, with a Newton vertex of coefficient modulus \(m\ge2\).
Write \(M(T)\) for its multiplicative Mahler measure and \(\mu(T)=\min_{X\in\mathbb T^s}|T(X)|\).
If \(M(T)=m\) and \(\mu(T)\ge m-1\), then after multiplication by a signed Laurent monomial the polynomial is \(m\) or \(m+\sigma X^u\), with \(u\ne0\) and \(\sigma=\pm1\).
Both forms attain the stated conditions, and the binomial has minimum exactly \(m-1\).
The classification has no restriction on support or dimension.

First take one variable with constant coefficient \(T(0)=m\), so Jensen's formula puts every root outside the open unit disk if \(M(T)=m\).
Assume \(T\) is nonconstant.
The lower bound on the circle also excludes boundary zeros, so \(1/T\) is analytic on the closed disk.
The maximum principle gives \(|T(z)|>m-1\) in its interior.
Set \(E=T-(m-1)\), an integer polynomial with constant coefficient one.
It has no interior roots.
The product of their moduli is the reciprocal of the modulus of its nonzero integral leading coefficient.
Consequently that coefficient is \(\sigma=\pm1\), and all roots of \(E\) lie on the unit circle.
With \(n=\deg E\), real conjugation and the endpoints give
\[
                        z^n E(1/z)=\sigma E(z).
\]

The rational function \((T+(m-1))/(T-(m-1))=1+2(m-1)/E\) has positive real part in the open disk.
At any boundary pole \(\zeta\) its leading term has the form \(b(1-z/\zeta)^{-k}\).
For each fixed \(-\pi/2<\phi<\pi/2\), the points \(z=\zeta(1-t e^{i\phi})\) lie in the disk for all sufficiently small positive \(t\).
Positivity requires \(\Re(b e^{-ik\phi})\ge0\) throughout this interval, so \(k=1\) and \(b\) is positive real.
It follows that every zero of \(E\) is simple and \(\zeta E'(\zeta)\) is real and nonzero.
Differentiating the reciprocal identity at this zero gives
\[
               \sigma\zeta E'(\zeta)
                    =-\zeta^n\,\overline{\zeta E'(\zeta)} .
\]
Hence each zero satisfies \(\zeta^n=-\sigma\).
All \(n\) roots are simple, so \(E=1+\sigma z^n\) by the constant term.
This proves the one-variable assertion.

For general \(T\) translate its marked vertex to zero and normalize its sign.
Choose an integral functional \(\lambda\) strictly positive at the other exponents and taking distinct values on the support.
This is possible in the open cone exposing the vertex by avoiding finitely many hyperplanes.
For any base phase \(X\), shifting the monomials \(X^v\) by \(z^{\lambda(v)}\) gives a one-variable polynomial with constant \(m\), of fixed degree and with leading coefficient of fixed nonzero modulus.
Its log measure is at least \(\log m\), depends continuously on \(X\), and has Haar mean \(\log M(T)=\log m\) by the [torus translation identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0900_v1.md#endpoint).
It therefore equals \(\log m\) at the trivial phase too.
The resulting integer polynomial has circle minimum at least \(\mu(T)\), so the preceding calculation applies.
As its exponents remain distinct, it recovers the asserted monomial count and coefficients of \(T\).

<a id="separation"></a>
## Uniform separation at bounded support

For every \(K,m\) with \(m\ge2\) there is \(\epsilon(K,m)>0\) such that a polynomial with the same vertex hypothesis, \(3\le\#\operatorname{supp}T\le K\) and \(M(T)=m\) satisfies
\[
                           \mu(T)\le m-1-\epsilon(K,m).
\]
In the preceding exposing specialization the torus minimum cannot decrease, and the number of terms, the endpoint modulus and the equality of measures are preserved.
It suffices to prove the gap in one variable.
If the gap failed, normalize the constant to \(m\), and extract a sequence with one fixed tuple of at least three coefficients by the [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound).
Use the [stabilized lattice construction](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations) on the positive exponents to obtain a fixed Laurent polynomial \(F\) with the same distinct terms on a free lattice, and evaluation maps whose shortest nonzero relation tends to infinity.
The constant remains a vertex since any sufficiently late evaluation is strictly positive on the other exponents.
Lawton's theorem in that construction gives \(M(F)=m\).
The Haar probabilities on the specializing circles converge weakly to Haar probability on the whole limiting torus, since their Fourier coefficient at any fixed nontrivial character eventually vanishes.
If \(|F|<m-1\) anywhere, then on a nonempty open set it is at most \(m-1-\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\).
Such a set contains a neighborhood of positive Haar measure and is met by every sufficiently late specializing circle, contradicting convergence of their minima to \(m-1\).
By the classification \(F\) has at most two terms, contradicting preservation of the support.

<a id="independent"></a>
## Every pair of lower offsets in an escaping eight-term lift

Consider a positive-constant Pisot sequence on branch two with eight terms in its trace lifts and \(\theta-q\to0\).
Let \(0<d_1<d_2<d_3\) be its three lower exponents.
For every pair \(j<k\) one necessarily has
\[
                        \frac{d_k}{\gcd(d_j,d_k)}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
In the [two-variable limit](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight), the unique primitive persistent relation therefore has all three coefficients nonzero.
Together with the [nonzero coefficient sum](Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#relation), this means that every three of the four exponent points, including the origin, are affinely independent.

Indeed, a subsequence with bounded reduced ratio fixes a nonzero relation on the chosen pair after extraction.
In the rank-two limiting lattice it spans the rational relation space of the three nonconstant exponent vectors.
The two dependent vectors are positive multiples of a common primitive lattice vector since their evaluations are positive, and the third vector is linearly independent of it.
The resulting polynomial on the limiting torus can be written \(T(x)+c y\), \(T(x)=2+a x^p+b x^q\), with \(0<p<q\) and nonzero integers \(a,b,c\).
The two selected characters \(x,y\) push Haar probability onto the full two-torus by their linear independence.
The prescribed total log measure is \(\log 2\).
Integrating first in \(y\) gives the mean of \(\log\max(|T(x)|,|c|)\), which is at least \(\log M(T)\ge\log2\).
Thus \(M(T)=2\) and \(\mu(T)\ge|c|\ge1\), since a strict smaller value on one open arc would increase the integral strictly above \(\log M(T)\).
The classification at \(m=2\) excludes this trinomial.
Conversely, a persistent relation with one zero coefficient makes the corresponding pair's reduced ratio constant, so it is excluded as well.

More generally the same argument works for a rank-independent character \(y\) added to any \(T\) with a vertex coefficient of modulus \(m\ge2\) when \(|c|\ge m-1\).
If \(M(T+cy)=m\), then the part \(T\) has at most two terms and is one of the stated forms.
