# The least Pisot root at constant 1000 above the Lehmer Salem number

<a id="minimum"></a>
## The first two roots in the prescribed fiber

Let \(P=z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1\) be the [Lehmer polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#lehmer-control).
Among all degree-ten Pisot minimal polynomials \(f\) with \(f(0)=1000\) and \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\), the least exterior root \(\theta\) is given by the coefficient list of \(f\) in increasing powers,
\[
                 (1000,2027,2106,1241,409,-411,-1243,-2107,-2026,-998,1).
\]
Its excess satisfies the strict bounds
\[
                0.028051294773716529184
            <   \theta-1000
            <   0.028051294773716529185.                               \tag{1}
\]
Exactly one other member of this fiber has exterior root at most \(1000.0281\).
Its increasing coefficient list is
\[
                  (1000,2027,2107,1243,410,-412,-1245,-2108,-2026,-998,1).
\]
The proof-side contributor applied the [prescribed-constant simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-16.
The fiber supplies an upper bound on the unrestricted infimum at \(q=1000\), for which the Salem output and degree may also vary.

<a id="pruning"></a>
## A bound on the sum of the four positive residues

Writing \(P=z^5 Q(z+z^{-1})\) gives \(Q=x^5+x^4-5x^3-5x^2+4x+3\).
Let \(x_1<\cdots<x_4<T\) be its roots, with \(T>2.0264\) and \(x_1>-1.9\), and put \(H=\prod_{i=1}^4(x-x_i)\).
The exact simplex represents every member uniquely as
\[
     f=P+(z-1)z^4 R(z+z^{-1}),\qquad
     R=-999x^4+a x^3+b x^2+c x+e,\qquad (a,b,c,e)\in\mathbb Z^4.
\]
With \(H_i=H/(x-x_i)\), its conditions are \(b_i=-R(x_i)/H'(x_i)>0\) and \(\sum_i b_i/(2+x_i)<1000+T/2\).
One has \(R=-999H-\sum_i b_i H_i\).
For an exterior root \(\theta\le1000.0281\), the [exact excess formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) gives, with \(x_\theta=\theta+\theta^{-1}\),
\[
     \sum_i b_i
       \le(x_\theta+2)
                \left(\theta-1000-\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}(T-2)\right)
       <1003(0.0281-0.0264)<2 .                                      \tag{2}
\]
The coefficients of \(-999H\) at degrees \(3,2,1,0\) respectively lie in the open intervals
\[
      (-3024,-3023),\quad(-1132,-1131),\quad(2701,2702),\quad(1478,1479).
\]
Each \(H_i\) is monic cubic and its remaining coefficients at degrees \(2,1,0\) lie in \((1,4),(-2,5),(-3,2)\), respectively.
Together with (2), these bounds put \((a,b,c,e)\) in the inclusive box
\[
 [-3025,-3024]\times[-1139,-1132]\times[2692,2705]\times[1475,1484].
\]
Only two of these \(2240\) integer points have the required alternating signs \(+,-,+,-\) of \(R(x_i)\).
In lexicographic order the two are
\[
          (-3025,-1136,2699,1479),\qquad (-3025,-1135,2702,1481).
\]
Conversely at either point the identity \(\sum_i b_i=[x^3](-999H)-a\) gives \(\sum_i b_i<2\).
Therefore \(\sum_i b_i/(2+x_i)<20\), and the simplex proves that both polynomials are Pisot minimal polynomials.
Evaluation at the rational cutoff puts both exterior roots below it, since the polynomials have \(f(1)=P(1)<0\) and a unique simple exterior root.
The second point's \(R\) minus the first point's \(R\) is \((x+1)(x+2)\), which is positive at every \(x=z+z^{-1}\) with \(z>1\).
Its \(f\) is therefore positive at the first point's exterior root and has a strictly smaller exterior root itself.
This proves global minimality within the stated fiber and identifies the runner-up.

<a id="exact-check"></a>
## Reproducing the sign and root certificates

For \(D=10^{12}\), the five isolating intervals \((u/D,(u+1)/D)\) below have opposite endpoint signs for \(Q\).
Their disjointness and \(\deg Q=5\) certify the full root list and its order.
The coefficients of \(H\) and \(H_i\) are multilinear functions of the chosen roots, so their extrema over the interval boxes occur at corners.
Over the candidate box \(\sup_{[-2,2]}|R'|<80000\), by the triangle inequality term by term.
The integer Horner evaluations below put \(|R(u/D)|>80000/D\) for each of the four roots and each candidate, certifying the sign at the actual root.
The following code uses rational and integer arithmetic throughout.

~~~python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from itertools import product

def horner(coeff, x):
    value = 0
    for c in coeff:
        value = x*value + c
    return value

def monic_coeff(roots):
    coeff = [1]
    for root in roots:
        coeff = [a-root*b for a, b in zip(coeff+[0], [0]+coeff)]
    return coeff

D = 10**12
u = (-1886609645151, -1468876009904, -584663995452,
      913731701317, 2026417949186)
ends = [(F(v,D), F(v+1,D)) for v in u]
Q = (1, 1, -5, -5, 4, 3)
assert all(horner(Q,l)*horner(Q,r)<0 for l,r in ends)
assert ends[0][0]>F(-19,10) and ends[-1][0]>F(20264,10000)
base = [(-3024,-3023), (-1132,-1131), (2701,2702), (1478,1479)]
for corner in product(*ends[:4]):
    assert all(l < -999*v < r for (l,r), v in zip(base, monic_coeff(corner)[1:]))
    for i in range(4):
        v = monic_coeff(corner[:i]+corner[i+1:])
        assert 1<v[1]<4 and -2<v[2]<5 and -3<v[3]<2

box = ((-3025,-3024), (-1139,-1132), (2692,2705), (1475,1484))
rows = []
for a,b,c,e in product(*(range(l,r+1) for l,r in box)):
    signs = []
    for v in u[:4]:
        N = horner((-999, a*D, b*D**2, c*D**3, e*D**4), v)
        assert abs(N) > 80000*D**3
        signs.append(N>0)
    if signs == [True,False,True,False]:
        rows.append((a,b,c,e))
assert rows == [(-3025,-1136,2699,1479), (-3025,-1135,2702,1481)]

lehmer = (1,1,0,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,0,1,1)
high = (1000,2027,2107,1243,410,-412,-1245,-2108,-2026,-998,1)
low = (1000,2027,2106,1241,409,-411,-1243,-2107,-2026,-998,1)
for (a,b,c,e), coeff in zip(rows, (high,low)):
    # Eleven distinct evaluations identify each polynomial of degree ten.
    for z in range(1,12):
        x = z+F(1,z)
        assert horner(coeff[::-1],z) == (horner(lehmer,z)
            +(z-1)*z**4*horner((-999,a,b,c,e),x))
    assert horner(coeff[::-1], F(10000281,10000))>0
lo = 1000 + F(28051294773716529184,10**21)
assert horner(low[::-1],lo)<0<horner(low[::-1],lo+F(1,10**21))
~~~
