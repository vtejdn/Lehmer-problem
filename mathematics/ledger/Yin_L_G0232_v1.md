### 1. Actual return series at every state

Let alpha be any algebraic unit, including torsion.
Retain the actual core D of all states on a finite zero-to-zero original path, the original update x -> alpha x+eta with weights p_0=1/2,p_+=p_-=1/4, and the kernel K killed outside D.
D is countable and irreducible through zero.
Put

    p_n=K^n(0,0), U(z)=sum_(n>=0) p_n z^n,
    F(z)=sum_(n>=1) f_n z^n, R=exp(h), h=h_2(alpha).

These are exactly L201's original quantities; U=1/(1-F), 1<=R<=2 and 0<F(R)<=1.
For x in D let

    u_(n,x)=K^n(x,x), U_x(z)=sum_(n>=0)u_(n,x) z^n,
    F_x(z)=sum_(n>=1) f_(n,x) z^n,                       (1)

where f_(n,x) includes every complete original first-positive-return word to x inside D.
The radius of U_x is R.
Indeed fixed positive paths x to0 and0 to x, of total length b and product weight c>0, imply u_(n+b,x)>=c p_n.
The reverse upper bound below gives the opposite radius inequality.
No aperiodic limit at x is needed for this conclusion.

Let nu_n be the fair binary sum law on Z[alpha] and mu_n its difference law.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives mu_n(v)<=mu_n(0)=p_n for every v.
An x-to-x path has displacement (1-alpha^n)x, so the reused L186/L173 inequality is

    0<=u_(n,x)<=mu_n((1-alpha^n)x)<=p_n.                (2)

Consequently, for 0<z<R, nonnegative renewal at x gives

    1<U_x(z)<=U(z)<infinity,
    0<F_x(z)=1-1/U_x(z)<=F(z)<1.                       (3)

Positivity follows because x is on an actual closed path.
At R the inequalities hold by monotone limits, including infinite U.
There is no coefficientwise assertion f_(n,x)<=f_n: (3) is a generating-function comparison, and that distinction is essential.

If alpha!=1 and x!=0, the inequality F_x(z)<F(z) is strict for every 0<z<R.
At n=1 a nonzero fixed state requires a nonzero original digit, whose weight is at most1/4, while p_1=1/2.
Thus U_x(z)<=U(z)-z/4, with strictness inherited by the increasing function 1-1/u.
This uses actual digit weights, not merely graph irreducibility.

### 2. Stationary classes need not contain zero

Allow any stationary probability pi and Markov digit rows Q on D using the original allowed edges.
The support need not be irreducible or contain zero.
Define, with zero-probability terms omitted,

    I(pi,Q)=sum_x pi(x) D(q_x||p),  0<=I<=log4.         (4)

For an irreducible positive recurrent class C containing x, let pi_C be its normalized invariant probability and q_x(w) its complete first-return word law.
Its mean is 1/pi_C(x).
Exactly the finite-mean stopped likelihood and occupation argument of L201 applies with x as root.
Each step has expected absolute log likelihood bounded by log3+log4, so finite mean justifies summing without assuming finite entropy of pi_C.
For 0<z<R, normalize the complete original return words at x by p(w)z^|w|/F_x(z).
Their relative entropy gives

    I_C>=log z-pi_C(x)log F_x(z)>=log z.               (5)

Letting z increase to R proves I_C>=h.
The exact identity at R is

    I_C=h+pi_C(x)[D(q_x||nu_(R,x))-log F_x(R)],        (6)
    nu_(R,x)(w)=p(w)R^|w|/F_x(R).

Here 0<F_x(R)<=1, and the same finite-mean integrability proves the identity; the critical original excursion mean may be infinite.

For clarity, no additional recurrence assumption is hidden in the general stationary class in(4).
Every positive-mass state belongs to a positive recurrent closed class, and pi decomposes into the corresponding invariant probabilities.
One elementary verification is as follows.
Fix pi(x)>0, delete row/column x from Q to get B, and write b for the row from x to its complement.
Iterating stationarity gives

    pi_off>=pi(x) sum_(j>=0) b B^j.

Summing proves that the original Q excursion from x returns almost surely with finite mean mu<=1/pi(x).
Its expected occupation vector v, including v(x)=1, satisfies vQ=v by the finite-mean excursion shift, and pi-pi(x)v is nonnegative invariant and zero at x.
Every state reached with positive excursion occupation can reach x; invariance and a positive path to x force that residual to vanish there.
The excursion support is consequently a closed irreducible class C, and pi|C=pi(x)v.
Its total mass is w_C=pi(x)mu>0, and pi_C=v/mu.
Repeating over the countable positive support partitions it into such disjoint classes with total weight one.
This is the standard countable stationary decomposition, exposed here using the same occupation argument already retained in L206.

Thus I=sum_C w_C I_C>=h.
The rooted finite chains of L201 still approximate h, so

    inf_(all stationary pi,Q on D) I=h.               (7)

There is no new smaller variational value in an auxiliary class that avoids zero.

The attainment condition is unchanged: (7) is attained if and only if F(R)=1 and R F'(R)<infinity, and then the only stationary pair is L201's full-core critical law.
To prove necessity beyond the rooted case, equality of the nonnegative component defects forces I_C=h in each positive component.
At any x in that component, (6) forces F_x(R)=1, q_x=nu_(R,x) and finite critical mean.
Every state of D lies on a first-return word to x: concatenate paths through that state and split at consecutive visits to x.
Every such original word therefore has positive Q probability.
Hence C=D, which contains zero; L201's exact rooted necessity and uniqueness now apply.
Conversely its critical law already belongs to the larger class.
This also excludes a nontrivial mixture of minimizers with different recurrent supports.

### 3. A common quantitative bound for every stationary atom

Write Delta=I-h>=0.
For pi(x)>0, let w be the mass of its positive recurrent component.
Applying(5) there and the lower bound h in all other components gives

    Delta>=w(log z-h)-pi(x)log F_x(z)
          >=log z-h-pi(x)log F(z),                    (8)

because w<=1, log z-h<0, and F_x(z)<=F(z)<1.
This step explicitly retains the component's weight; it does not replace conditional stationary mass by global mass without compensation.
The same conclusion is trivial if pi(x)=0.
Therefore for every actual state, simultaneously,

    I>=log z-pi(x)log F(z),       0<z<R.              (9)

Put t=log(R/z)>0 and g(t)=-log F(R exp(-t))>0.
Since a probability on a countable set has an attained largest atom, a=||pi||_infinity is a maximum, and

    a <= B(Delta):=min{1, inf_(t>0)(Delta+t)/g(t)}.    (10)

In particular pi(A)<=|A|B(Delta) for every finite set A, even if A is chosen after Q and its location varies.
This bound has the actual complete F of the original input, not a stationary reweighting or a truncated return series.

If the original critical law is transient, F(R)<1, then with A_R=-log F(R)>0, letting t decrease to zero gives

    ||pi||_infinity<=Delta/A_R.                       (11)

If F(R)=1 and R F'(R)=infinity, L212's established relation g(t)/t -> infinity implies B(Delta)->0 as Delta decreases to zero: for any epsilon>0 choose a fixed t with t/g(t)<epsilon/2, then Delta<epsilon g(t)/2 makes(10) less than epsilon.
Thus, in either nonattaining critical case,

    I(pi_j,Q_j)->h  implies  ||pi_j||_infinity->0.     (12)

This excludes concentration at a moving single state, not merely at zero or any fixed state.
It forces the mass of every uniformly bounded-cardinality moving set to vanish.
It still permits concentration on sets whose cardinality grows, and is not a rate uniform over algebraic inputs.

When F(R)=1 and mu_*=R F'(R)<infinity, g(t)/t ->mu_*.
For a minimizer(10) gives ||pi_*||_infinity<=1/mu_*=pi_*(0).
Thus zero is a largest atom of the unique critical minimizing law.

### 4. The maximum-atom price and the inherited fixed-mass curve

    J_max(a)>=sup_(0<z<R)[log z-a log F(z)].            (13)

    a(z)=F(z)/(zF'(z)),
    I(Q_z)=log z-a(z)log F(z),
    pi_z(0)=a(z)=||pi_z||_infinity.                   (14)

Indeed applying(9) to this same law and this same z gives pi_z(x)<=pi_z(0), since -log F(z)>0.
If alpha!=1 and x!=0, the strict F_x comparison in(3), applied to its full irreducible class before replacing F_x by F, gives strict inequality.

Let a_*=lim_(z up R) a(z), with a_*=0 if the limiting mean is infinite.

    J_max(a)=J(a)=log z-a log F(z).                   (15)

on Q_R.

### 5. A strict arithmetic profile at every nonzero state

Now assume m=log M(alpha)>0.
There is at least one expanding embedding sigma, with rho=|sigma(alpha)|>1.
For x!=0 define

    L_(sigma,x)=floor(1/[(rho-1)|sigma(x)|])+1,
    kappa_x=min_(rho>1) cos(pi/(L_(sigma,x)+1)).       (16)

Every number L_(sigma,x) is finite because an embedding of a nonzero field element is nonzero.
For an actual nonzero core state, the usual suffix geometric bound gives |sigma(x)|<1/(rho-1), so L_(sigma,x)>=2 and 0<kappa_x<1.
No uniform lower bound on |sigma(x)| is assumed.

Then for every n>=1 the strengthened original diagonal comparison is

    u_(n,x)<=kappa_x p_n.                             (17)

Here is a complete proof.
Set v=(1-alpha^n)x, which is nonzero.
Partition the finite support of the binary law nu_n into cosets modulo the infinite cyclic additive subgroup Z v.
On each such coset the nonnegative values of nu_n form a finite sequence.
Break its support into consecutive nonzero runs.
If one run has l positions, its endpoint embedding distance is (l-1)|sigma(v)|.
The diameter of the entire binary support at this embedding is at most sum_(j=0)^(n-1)rho^j=(rho^n-1)/(rho-1), whereas

    |sigma(v)|=|1-sigma(alpha)^n| |sigma(x)|
               >=(rho^n-1)|sigma(x)|.

Therefore l<=L_(sigma,x).
For a length-l real sequence b, the symmetric matrix with off-diagonal entries1/2 and zero diagonal has Rayleigh maximum cos(pi/(l+1)); its sine eigenvectors are the same standard finite-path calculation retained in L172/NPR13, here with delta=0 and sigma=tau=1/2.
Direct substitution of sin(j h pi/(l+1)) gives every eigenvalue cos(h pi/(l+1)).
Consequently

    sum_j b_j b_(j+1)<=cos(pi/(L_(sigma,x)+1))sum_j b_j^2.

Sum over the disjoint runs and cosets.
The left side is exactly the autocorrelation mu_n(v), not an absolute-value relaxation of an unrelated measure; the right squared norm is p_n.
Apply(2), then minimize over expanding embeddings, to obtain(17).
Complex embeddings are permitted: only modulus and the reverse triangle inequality were used.
Holes in a coset simply split its finite runs, and there is no torsion in its additive subgroup.
The finite path spectrum is classical; its new use is this actual all-n diagonal profile.

Summing(17) while retaining the n=0 term gives

    U_x(z)<=1+kappa_x[U(z)-1],
    F_x(z)<=C_x(z):=kappa_x F(z)/[1-(1-kappa_x)F(z)], (18)

for0<z<R and by endpoint limits.
The denominator is positive since F<=1.
These bounds keep every original return word.
In particular the common atom envelope can be sharpened pointwise to

    pi(x)<=(Delta+t)/[-log C_x(R exp(-t))], t>0.      (19)

For the full subcritical canonical law, equality of its zero-root cost and the same-state entropy inequality give

    pi_z(x)<=pi_z(0)
       *[-log F(z)]/[-log C_x(z)]<pi_z(0).           (20)

For a positive recurrent CRITICAL original tilt, let pi_* be the actual unique minimizing probability from Section2, with mu_*=1/pi_*(0)=R F'(R).
Every base state's first-return word weights are p(w)R^|w| by telescoping that same critical kernel.
Hence F_x(R)=1 and R F_x'(R)=1/pi_*(x)<infinity.
Taking z up R in(18), or comparing the first-order expansions of 1-F_x and 1-F, yields

    pi_*(x)<=kappa_x pi_*(0)<pi_*(0),   x!=0.         (21)

Explicitly, U_x/U tends to (R F'(R))/(R F_x'(R))=pi_*(x)/pi_*(0), while(18) bounds its limsup by kappa_x.
No pointwise renewal limit or an unstated aperiodicity theorem is needed.
Thus, whenever a positive-measure input actually has an attaining critical law, zero is its unique largest atom with this arithmetic profile.
This does not prove that such a law exists for a Salem input.

The factor can be exact at an actual nontorsion input and a finite time.
Take alpha=phi, the positive root of t^2-t-1, and x=1.
The state is in the actual core: 0 ->1 ->phi-1 ->0 uses digits1,-1,-1.
Its expanding reciprocal width gives 1/[(phi-1)|x|]=phi, hence L=2 and kappa_x=1/2.
At n=2, irreducibility makes the four binary endpoints0,1,phi,1+phi distinct, so p_2=1/4.
The length-two return to1 uses digits-1,0 and has probability1/8; these are the only digits solving the relation by the basis(1,phi).
Hence u_(2,1)/p_2=1/2, exactly(17).
The fixed positive measure log phi is explicit; this is no near-one family and no assertion of an optimal profile for other states or times.
