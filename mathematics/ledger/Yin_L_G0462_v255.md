# The two signed lattices for the octic Salem seed at row degree nine

<a id="plane"></a>
## The height of each kernel plane

Fix the Salem minimum \(P=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\), with exterior root \(\rho>1\), and put \(h=\log\rho\).
Let \(p\ge2\) be an integer and write \(A_w^*(Y)=Y^wA(1/Y)\).
For each sign \(\epsilon\), define \(K_{\epsilon,p}\) as the space of rational \(A\) of degree at most nine with \(P^2\mid A(X^p)+\epsilon X A_9^*(X^p)\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\) this is a rational plane, and its lattice \(K_{\epsilon,p}\cap\mathbb Z^{10}\) has successive Euclidean minima \(\lambda_{1,\epsilon}\le\lambda_{2,\epsilon}\) with
\[
             \log(\lambda_{1,\epsilon}\lambda_{2,\epsilon})=9ph+o(p).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]

Choose representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_4\) of the inverse pairs as in the [octic specialization](Yin_L_G0462_v252.md#specialization).
In the splitting field \(L\) use independent variables \(N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_4\).
For columns \(j=0,\ldots,9\), the two entries for each \(i\) in the signed matrix are
\[
       Y_i^j+\epsilon x_iY_i^{9-j},\qquad
       NjY_i^j+\epsilon x_i(1+N(9-j))Y_i^{9-j}.
\]
At \(N=p,\ Y_i=x_i^p\) these give the value and Euler derivative conditions, with the inverse representatives giving equivalent conditions by formal reversal.
At \(N=0\) the kernel consists of the multiples of \(\prod_i(Z-Y_i)(Z-Y_i^{-1})\) of degree at most nine, so the generic rank is eight.
The [fixed-place estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) makes every nonzero polynomial in these variables eventually nonzero after specialization, since the four representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
Thus the specialized matrix has rank eight eventually, and its kernel is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) by the polynomial remainder condition.

The 45 maximal minors, with the usual alternating signs on complementary pairs of columns, are the Plücker coordinates of the plane.
Divide out their polynomial gcd in \(L[N,\mathbf Y]\) and clear constant denominators.
For each \(i\), let \(W_i\) be the largest minus the smallest exponent of \(Y_i\) across the supports of the resulting nonzero entries.
The [projective height calculation for polynomial tuples](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#height) applies verbatim to these minors.
At an embedding sending \(x_i\) to \(\rho\) or \(\rho^{-1}\), only its own coordinate contributes a nonzero log modulus.
The two cases each have weight \(1/8\).
The common finite valuation of the specialized tuple has normalized log norm \(o(p)\), since its entries have no common polynomial factor and one can apply the [almost-unit gcd estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) to one entry and a suitable fixed linear combination.
Hence the plane's logarithmic projective height is \(ph\sum_i W_i/8+o(p)\).
Galois permutes the inverse pairs transitively and can invert each chosen representative.
The transformation on the variables inverts the same coordinates, and the value and derivative conditions on the coefficient space are preserved by the [signed reversal identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions), with formal width nine.
Thus the transformed Plücker tuple is proportional to the original one.
After removal of the common factor, proportionality in the Laurent ring changes the tuple only by a unit, which translates the exponent supports.
The four widths are consequently equal.

Each \(W_i\le18\) since only two matrix rows involve \(Y_i\), and each has degree at most nine.
For the reverse inequality reduce at the prime over \(1987\) with representatives \(308,670,1452,1555\), set \(N=5,\ (Y_2,Y_3,Y_4)=(3,5,7)\), and write \(T=Y_1\).
Let \(D_{12},D_{34}\) be the determinants obtained by deleting columns \(1,2\) or \(3,4\), respectively, from columns numbered \(0,\ldots,9\), leaving their original order and taking no extra sign.
The exact computations in \(\mathbb F_{1987}[T]\) give

| Sign | Degrees of \(D_{12},D_{34}\) | Constant coefficients | Leading coefficients | \(\operatorname{Res}(D_{12},D_{34})\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| \(+\) | \(18,18\) | \(1812,903\) | \(175,1084\) | 573 |
| \(-\) | \(18,18\) | \(1218,494\) | \(769,1493\) | 521 |

For reproduction evaluate the two determinants at \(T=0,\ldots,18\) modulo \(1987\).
Interpolate each value list \(v\) as \(\sum_{j=0}^{18}(\Delta^jv)_0\binom{T}{j}\) and take the determinant of the degree-18 Sylvester matrix in the displayed argument order.
The nonzero leading coefficients and resultants show that the generic minors have full degree and no common positive-degree divisor in \(Y_1\).
Their nonzero constant coefficients give minimum exponent zero.
This proves \(W_i=18\) at both signs.

An integral basis of the saturated kernel lattice has a primitive exterior product, since a saturated sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^{10}\) is a direct summand.
Its largest absolute coordinate gives the primitive Plücker height after taking a logarithm, and its Euclidean norm is the covolume \(\Delta\) in the plane.
These norms differ by at most a constant factor.
A shortest vector of length \(\lambda_1\) is primitive and extends to a lattice basis, whose second vector can be reduced to have parallel component of length at most \(\lambda_1/2\).
Its perpendicular component has length \(\Delta/\lambda_1\), which must be at least \(\sqrt3\,\lambda_1/2\) by minimality.
Any two independent lattice vectors span area at least \(\Delta\), and the reduced basis therefore gives \(\Delta\le\lambda_1\lambda_2\le2\Delta/\sqrt3\).
This proves (1).

## Genuine row pairs below the degree-eight rate

Let \(H_{p,9}(P)\) be the least uncapped cost \(\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\) for the fixed seed at width at most nine with the double-root, coprimality and unequal-modulus conditions of the [finite test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement).
Then
\[
 \frac{21}{8}h\ \le\ \liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_{p,9}(P)}p
 \ \le\ \limsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_{p,9}(P)}p
 \ \le\ \frac92h .                                                    \tag{2}
\]
The upper rate is strictly smaller than the [exact degree-eight rate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#octic) \(8h\).
At each sufficiently large \(p\), all genuine rows of cost at most \(2^p\), if any exist, have proportional plus components and proportional minus components.

Write \(B_\epsilon\) for the primitive degree-eight signed line generators, and write \(R_\epsilon=(B_\epsilon,\epsilon (B_\epsilon)_8^*)\).
Their norms separately have log rate \(8h\), verified for each sign by [the two degree-sixteen resultant certificates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#octic).
The polynomial vectors \(R_+,R_-\) have nonzero determinant at every sufficiently large \(p\) by the [edge test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions).
Set \(J=1+Y,\ K=Y-1\).
Since \(J_1^*=J\) and \(K_1^*=-K\), the signed pairs of width nine have bases
\[
                J R_+,\ K R_- \quad\text{and}\quad J R_-,\ K R_+
\]
for the plus and minus signs, respectively.
Independence follows from the degree-eight determinant and dimension two from the plane calculation.
The first coordinates of these four vectors are primitive by Gauss's lemma.
Each has log norm \(8ph+o(p)\), since multiplying by \(Y\pm1\) and dividing back at bounded degree change the coefficient one-norm by bounded factors.

Choose a shortest integral vector in each signed plane and denote the resulting signed pairs by \(S_+,S_-\).
Their first-coordinate one-norms have logarithms at most \((9/2)ph+o(p)\) by (1) and equivalence of norms in dimension ten.
Neither vector can lie in one of its two basis directions above, so write \(S_+=aJ R_+ + bK R_-\) and \(S_-=cJ R_-+dK R_+\) with all four rational scalars nonzero.
Their polynomial determinant equals \((acJ^2-bdK^2)\det(R_+,R_-)\ne0\), since \(J^2,K^2\) are linearly independent.
The sum \(S_++S_-=(U,V)\) has the required double-root graph and its swapped formal reversal is \(S_+-S_-\).
The nonzero determinant of these two vectors is \(Y^9(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\), where \(\iota(Y)=Y^{-1}\).

For every sufficiently large \(p\), no nonzero signed vector \(A\) in either plane vanishes at a powered conjugate.
Indeed \(Q=\min(x_1^p)\) is reciprocal of degree eight, so vanishing implies \(A=Q C\) with \(\deg C\le1\).
Removing \(Q(X^p)\), which has a simple zero at each original conjugate, leaves a signed simple-root condition for \(C=c_0+c_1Y\).
Its value equation at each \(x_i\) has coefficient row \((1+\epsilon x_iY_i,\,Y_i+\epsilon x_i)\) specialized at \(Y_i=x_i^p\).
The determinant for \(i=1,2\) is a nonzero polynomial since the direction of each row varies with its own independent variable, using \(x_i^2\ne1\).
Eventual nonvanishing forces \(c_0=c_1=0\).
If \(Q\) divided both \(U,V\), then its reciprocity would give \(Q\mid U\pm V_9^*\), hence it would divide the two nonzero signed first coordinates, an impossibility.
Dividing by the primitive common polynomial divisor of \(U,V\) over \(\mathbb Q[Y]\) therefore retains \(P^2\) after substitution.
It also retains the nonzero reflected determinant because division removes only the product of this common polynomial and its reciprocal Laurent polynomial.
For an integral quotient \(A/D\) of degree at most nine, \(\log\|A/D\|_1\le9\log2+m(A/D)\le9\log2+\log\|A\|_1\).
Consequently cleaning preserves the logarithmic upper rate and proves existence of genuine rows as claimed.

For genuine pairs the [single-row norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#salem) at \(e=8,\ b=9\) proves the lower bound in (2).
It also gives that lower rate for every nonzero integral signed first coordinate, since the preceding argument guarantees its nonvanishing at the powered conjugates and reversal has the same one-norm.
In particular (1) gives \(\liminf p^{-1}\log\lambda_{2,\epsilon}\ge(9/2)h\) and \(\limsup p^{-1}\log\lambda_{2,\epsilon}\le(9-21/8)h\).
The signed projections of a genuine pair of cost at most \(2^p\) are nonzero and have one-norm at most \(2^{p+1}\).
Since \(\rho>32/25\) and \(32^9>4\cdot25^9\), two such independent directions would violate the second-minimum lower bound.
On the other side \(10^8P(13/10)=39127721>0\), giving \(\rho<13/10\) by the unique exterior real root and \(P(1)<0\).
The inequality \(13^{21}<2^8 10^{21}\) thus gives \((21/8)h<\log2<(9/2)h\).
The interval in (2) leaves the width-nine exclusion at the cutoff open for this seed.
