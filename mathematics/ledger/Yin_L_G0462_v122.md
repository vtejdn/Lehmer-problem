# A linear gap after the first nonzero integer comparison coefficient

<a id="separated"></a>
## Separation for positive delay-two excess

In the notation of the [window estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window), let \(p=n+1\) and \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2 F)\).
There exist absolute \(c,\eta>0\) such that every matched delay-two input in the unit case with \(1<M(z^2f+e f^*)<1+\eta\) has
\[
                [z^{p+j}]K^0=0 \qquad(1\le j\le cn).
\]
Here \(n\) is the first Taylor discrepancy from \(1/(1-2z)\) in the signed seed quotient and \([z^p]K^0=\delta=\pm1\).
The assertion strengthens fixed-offset concentration to a proportional gap; it gives no upper bound on \(n\).

Suppose the assertion fails.
Take a sequence with positive excess tending to zero and with smallest positive offset \(k\) of a nonzero coefficient after \(p\) satisfying \(k=o(n)\).
The [fixed-binomial exclusion](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#unique) and the centered identity \(K^0=(1-z^pV)/(1+z^pV)\) give \(k\to\infty\).
Extract fixed signs and bounded integer coefficient values at \(p\) and \(p+k\), writing them as \(a,b\) with \(a=\pm1,\ b\ne0\).
Boundedness follows from uniform moment approximation and bounded mass.
Use the torus variables \(x=\bar v^k,\ y=v^p\).
Their joint weak limit has first marginal normalized arclength, since for every fixed nonzero \(r\) one has \(0<|r|k<p\) eventually and the doubled moment at this index tends to zero.
For each \(l\ge1,\ r\in\mathbb Z\) the moment at \(lp+rk\) is approximated by an integer by the same window estimate, since \(k=o(n)\).
Consequently the [integer conditional slice argument](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#slice) again gives polynomials \(P_l=2\mathbb E[y^l\mid x]\) of circle norm at most two.
Here \(P_1\) has constant coefficient \(a\) and coefficient \(b\) at exponent one.
Its two known terms exhaust its support by the integer norm bound, and \(b=\pm1\).
The [two conditional laws](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#two) now apply with exponent one in the \(x\) variable.

Returning to \(v\), the same polynomials \((1-a z^p)(1-bz^{p+k})\) or \(1-abz^{2p+k}\) have vanishing squared integral in the limit at \(\bar v\).
Their degrees are still \(O(n)\) and their coefficient absolute sums are at most four.
Hence the [integer bilateral recurrence](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#recurrence) holds through \(4n\).
Choose the [corresponding neutral seed](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#neutral) with these actual varying \(n,k\).
Its bilateral moments agree on \([-p-k,p+k]\) because the original input has coefficients \(a,b\) at \(p,p+k\), and \(k\) was the first nonzero offset after \(p\).
Here \(k<p\) eventually, so no second multiple of \(p\) enters that initial interval.
The recurrence thus fixes the same Taylor jets through degree \(4n-1\) in the signed quotients.
Since \(2n+k+4<4n-1\) eventually even with this varying \(k\), [Schur uniqueness](Yin_L_G0462_v121.md#unique) identifies the neutral seed with the positive-excess input, a contradiction.
