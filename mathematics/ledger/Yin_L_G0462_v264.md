# Higher coefficient congruences on a signed row with repeated factors

<a id="energy"></a>
## A bound from the powered resultants for the whole residue multiset

Let \(p\) be prime and \(G(X)=U(X^p)+XV(X^p)\) with nonzero integral rows of width \(b\).
Select distinct monic irreducible minima \(P_i\) of nontorsion algebraic units, occurring in \(G\) to orders \(m_i\ge2\), with \(U(\alpha_i^p)\ne0\) at their roots.
Let \(Q_i\) be the powered minima, \(d_i=\deg P_i,\ D=\sum_i d_i,\ F=\prod_iP_i\), and \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\).
The [recovery and derivative identities](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v417.md#elimination) give distinct \(Q_i\) of degrees \(d_i\) and measures \(pm(P_i)\).
Write \(E_i(\xi)=\operatorname{ord}_\xi\bar P_i,\ E(\xi)=\sum_iE_i(\xi)\), summing over the distinct roots of \(\bar F\) in a finite extension of \(\mathbb F_p\).
Work in the unramified local field with that residue field, with \(v(p)=1\) and \(\omega\) the prime-to-\(p\) root of unity lifting \(\xi\).
Assume \(e_\xi=\max_i E_i(\xi)<p-1\) at each occupied disk.
This ensures \(v(x/\omega-1)\ge1/e_\xi\) for its original roots and \(v((x^p/\omega^p-1)/p)>0\) by the [residue factor and power map](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#degree).

Let \(s_U,s_V\) be the two separate minimum coefficient valuations of the polynomials \(U(\omega^p(1+pZ)),V(\omega^p(1+pZ))\) on this disk.
Then
\[
 \sum_i(m_i-1)E_i(\xi)\le s_U+s_V,\qquad
 (\log p)\sum_\xi E(\xi)\sum_i(m_i-1)E_i(\xi)
                 \le2D\log H+2pb\,m(F).                                 \tag{1}
\]
For the first inequality write the two polynomials as \(p^{s_U}L,p^{s_V}M\).
Both reductions \(\bar L,\bar M\) are nonzero, of degrees at most \(s_U,s_V\) because the original coefficient at position \(j\) is divisible by \(p^j\).
In these coordinates the derivative polynomial \(pY(U'V-UV')-UV\) divided by \(p^{s_U+s_V}\) equals
\[
                  (1+pZ)(L'M-LM')-LM .
\]
Its reduction has degree exactly \(\deg\bar L+\deg\bar M\), since the derivative products have smaller degree than \(\bar L\bar M\).
Every powered contact gives a zero of this polynomial of order \(m_i-1\) or greater at an integral \(Z\) of positive valuation.
These powered nodes are distinct by recovery.
After splitting the nodes, divide by the monic product of their linear factors with the indicated multiplicities.
Monic division preserves integrality, so on reduction its nonzero polynomial is divisible by \(Z^{\sum_i(m_i-1)E_i(\xi)}\).
This proves the disk inequality without estimating an analytic tail.

At each of the \(E(\xi)\) powered nodes the valuations of \(U,V\) are at least \(s_U,s_V\) respectively.
Their values are nonzero, so this bounds the valuation of the nonzero integer
\(\prod_i\operatorname{Res}(Q_i,U)\operatorname{Res}(Q_i,V)\) below by the sum on the left of (1) before multiplication by \(\log p\).
The archimedean bounds for each row give \(H^{2D}\exp(2pb\,m(F))\) for its absolute value, proving (1).
In particular at double contacts the left side is \((\log p)\sum_\xi E(\xi)^2\).

## The next digit of every lower Hasse derivative

Now take \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon A_b^*\) with one fixed sign and with \(A\) primitive in scalar content.
Suppose the selected \(P_i\) are reciprocal.
At each occupied disk put
\[
                k_\xi=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_i m_iE_i(\xi)}2\right\rfloor
                \quad\hbox{and assume}\quad 2k_\xi-1<p-e_\xi.           \tag{2}
\]
As before \(e_\xi<p-1\).
The integers \(k_\xi\) are constant under both Frobenius and inversion.
The [local contact bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#disk) gives \(\min(s_U,s_V)\ge k_\xi\), since a smaller joint weight \(s\) would force \(\sum_i m_i E_i(\xi)\le2s+1<2k_\xi\).
Write \(k=k_\xi,\ y=\omega^p\), and let \(a_j\) and \(b_j\) be the Taylor coefficients of \(A(y(1+Z))\) and \(V(y(1+Z))\) respectively in powers of \(Z\).
They are \(y^j\) times the corresponding Hasse derivatives.
The weight bound says that for \(j<k\) both coefficients belong to \(p^{k-j}\mathcal O\).

There is one more specified residue for every such derivative.
Form the polynomials of degree at most \(k\) over the residue field
\[
       L_0(T)=\sum_{j=0}^k\overline{p^{j-k}a_j}\,T^j,\qquad
       M_0(T)=\sum_{j=0}^k\overline{p^{j-k}b_j}\,T^j.
\]
They satisfy
\[
         L_0(T)+\xi\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k-1}\frac{T^\ell}{\ell!}\right)M_0(T)
                      \equiv0\pmod {T^{2k}} .                          \tag{3}
\]
In fact divide the inverse-coordinate graph of C422 by \(p^k\).
The coefficients through degree \(2k-1\) reduce to the left side because the inverse series \((1+pT)^{1/p}\) reduces there to the truncated exponential.
If any coefficient at \(j_0<2k\) were a unit, the closed-disk zero count in C422 would give at most \(j_0\) contacts.
Indeed coefficients through degree \(p-1\) are integral, and the remaining coefficients have valuation at least \(-v_p(j!)\) with \(j-e_\xi v_p(j!)\ge p-e_\xi>j_0\).
There are at least \(2k\) contacts.
This proves (3), also when the two polynomials both vanish modulo \(p\).

Given the two highest coefficients \(\bar a_k,\bar b_k\), (3) uniquely specifies both lists of \(k\) lower coefficients.
For its homogeneous system on degrees at most \(k-1\), the coefficients in degrees \(l=k,\ldots,2k-1\), multiplied by \(l!\), evaluate the falling-factorial polynomial of the second unknown's coefficients times \(\xi\) at \(k\) distinct residues.
Its degree is less than \(k\), hence it is zero, which forces both unknowns to vanish.
There are \(2k\) equations in \(2k\) unknowns, and existence and linearity follow over the residue field.
Both top coefficients are determined by the single polynomial \(\bar A\), including the formal width in the reversal.
Thus the lower numerator coefficients \(a_j\) are prescribed modulo \(p^{k+1-j}\), and the rule is equivariant under Frobenius.
For \(k=1\) it says explicitly
\[
           A(y)\equiv p\, y\{A'(y)+\omega V'(y)\}\pmod {p^2}.
\]
This congruence uses the root-of-unity lift of the residue, which is computable to the indicated precision.

There is also an arithmetic consequence for the individual resultants.
At an original lift \(x\) above \(\xi\), apply the weight bound at the disk mapping to \(\xi\) under Frobenius.
The root-of-unity center there is \(y=\omega\), and \(k_{\xi^{1/p}}=k_\xi\).
For \(E_i(\xi)>0\) the valuations of all terms in the Taylor evaluation of \(A(x)\) are at least \(k_\xi/E_i(\xi)\), since \(v(x/\omega-1)\ge1/E_i(\xi)\) and \(v(a_j)\ge\max(k_\xi-j,0)\).
For \(A(x^p)\) instead each valuation is at least \(k_\xi\), since its local coordinate has valuation greater than one before division by \(p\).
Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 v_p\operatorname{Res}(P_i,A)&\ge\sum_{\xi:E_i(\xi)>0}k_\xi
                    &&\text{if the resultant is nonzero},\\
 v_p\operatorname{Res}(Q_i,A)&\ge\sum_\xi E_i(\xi)k_\xi
                    &&\text{with nonzero resultant by the contact condition}.
 \end{split}                                                            \tag{4}
\]

## The finite quotient for the necessary numerator congruences

Index the irreducible factors \(\phi\) of \(\bar F\) by their Frobenius orbits, with degree \(f_\phi\) and common weight \(k_\phi\).
Set \(K=\sum_\phi f_\phi k_\phi\).
Equation (2) implies \(\prod_\phi\phi^{k_\phi}\mid\bar A\), so a primitive row requires \(K\le b\).
For these data and a fixed sign, let \(\mathcal L\subset\mathbb Z^{b+1}\) consist of coefficient vectors satisfying the lower numerator congruences prescribed by (3), with both top residues computed from this same vector.
Include the divisibilities required to define those lower residues.
For \(b\ge K\) the exact quotient is
\[
 \mathbb Z^{b+1}/\mathcal L
 \simeq\bigoplus_\phi\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k_\phi-1}
                (\mathbb Z/p^{\,k_\phi+1-j}\mathbb Z)^{f_\phi}.        \tag{5}
\]
In particular its index is \(p^{\sum_\phi f_\phi k_\phi(k_\phi+3)/2}\), a factor \(p^K\) greater than the index for the weight divisibilities alone.
Every actual row belongs to \(\mathcal L\).
Solving this numerator condition alone gives no sufficiency assertion for an integral graph with the prescribed contacts.

For each orbit take the monic polynomial \(\Phi_\phi=\prod(Y-y)\) of its root-of-unity lifts.
It belongs to \(\mathbb Z_p[Y]\) by Frobenius invariance and reduces to \(\phi\).
Let \(\mathcal T=\prod_\phi\Phi_\phi^{k_\phi}\), and divide \(A=\mathcal T C+R\) over \(\mathbb Z_p\), with \(\deg R<K\).
At one lift \(y\) per orbit the first \(k_\phi\) scaled Taylor coefficients of \(R\) give an integral linear isomorphism onto the product of the \(k_\phi\) copies of the unramified integer ring of degree \(f_\phi\).
Indeed its reduction has zero kernel, because a remainder with all these coefficients zero has a zero of multiplicity at least \(k_\phi\) at every conjugate residue.
The source and target have the same rank \(K\), so the determinant is a unit.
These low coordinates of \(R\) equal those of \(A\).
They all vanish modulo \(p\) for a row in \(\mathcal L\), hence \(\bar R=0\).
Consequently \(\bar A=\bar{\mathcal T}\bar C\) computes both top coefficients in (3), including the one contributed by the reversal.
Conversely use this last formula to compute the unique lower numerator digits from \(\bar C\), as residue-linear functions \(l_{\phi,j}(\bar C)\) taking values in \(\mathbb F_{p^{f_\phi}}\).
Then the exact conditions defining \(\mathcal L\) are
\[
        a_j(R)\equiv p^{k_\phi-j}l_{\phi,j}(\bar C)
                    \pmod {p^{k_\phi+1-j}}\qquad(0\le j<k_\phi).
\]
They imply \(\bar R=0\), so using \(\bar{\mathcal T}\bar C\) in place of \(\bar A\) is consistent in both directions.
A lift of each residue-linear map to an integral \(\mathbb Z_p\)-linear map supplies a change of coordinates subtracting the displayed right sides, with the free quotient coefficients of \(C\) unchanged.
This diagonalizes the congruences and proves (5) over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
All conditions use only coefficients modulo \(p^{1+\max k_\phi}\), giving the same finite quotient over the ordinary integers.

In particular for each given \(C\bmod p^2\) there is precisely one \(A\bmod p^2\) in this necessary lattice, with both reductions interpreted in the division by \(\mathcal T\).
If \(K=b\), every primitive row has unit leading coefficient at \(p\), so there is only one possible projective class modulo \(p^2\) for the given data and sign.
For a fixed squarefree product of reciprocal unit minima of degree \(D\), prescribed common multiplicity \(2t\) and \(b\ge Dt\), (5) has index \(p^{Dt(t+3)/2}\) at every \(p>2t+1\) outside the prime divisors of the product discriminant.
Here every occupied disk has \(e_\xi=E(\xi)=1\).
This index constrains integer rows at arbitrarily large primes without estimating the least archimedean norm of a vector in the lattice.

## The two signed congruence classes for a quadratic unit at 17

Take \(P=Y^2-3Y+1,\ p=17,\ b=2,\ m_1=2\).
Its reduction is irreducible since \(5\) is a nonsquare modulo 17.
The two residues \(y,y^{-1}\) are exchanged by Frobenius, so the residue \(\xi\) producing \(y\) is \(y^{-1}=3-y\).
Modulo \(17^2=289\) the lift polynomial is \(\Phi=Y^2-207Y+1\).
Indeed it reduces to \(P\bmod17\) and divides \(Y^{18}-1\bmod289\), as is seen by iterating \((u,v)\mapsto(-v,u+207v)\) eighteen times from \((1,0)\) modulo 289 in the basis \(1,Y\).
The \(18\)-th roots of unity lift uniquely since \(17\nmid18\).
Normalize the leading coefficient to one and write \(A=\Phi+17(r_0+r_1Y)\pmod{289}\).
Then \(yP'(y)=3y-2\) in the residue field, and the two derivative residues are related by the sign since \(\bar P\) is reciprocal.
The \(k=1\) congruence becomes
\[
           r_0+r_1y=(1+\varepsilon y^{-1})(3y-2)
                    =-2-3\varepsilon+(3+2\varepsilon)y .
\]
Thus the normalized low-to-high coefficient vectors are \((205,167,1)\) for the plus sign and \((18,99,1)\) for the minus sign, both in lattices of index \(17^4\).
Primitive integral rows realizing these two classes, in the same ordering, are respectively
\[
             (-25655489,-14544692049635,71293590),\qquad
             (54214939,72723460248107,151164018).
\]
For direct verification set \(Z=X^{17}\) in the quotient by \(P(X)^2\).
Each displayed triple \((u,v,w)\) gives zero there on substituting in \(u+vZ+wZ^2+\varepsilon X(w+vZ+uZ^2)\).
Their leading coefficients are units modulo 17 and normalization gives the two vectors above.
Their endpoint coefficients differ, so the reciprocal quadratic minimum of the powered root cannot divide either row.

## The possible degrees and the numerator classes at prime 223

Use the strict coefficient cutoff and distinct-measure convention of the [two-value problem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement).
The [exclusion through 211](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#conclusion) leaves \(223\) as its first open prime.
At \(p=223\) the width \(b=114\) follows from \(113^8<223^7<114^8\), and the prescribed multiplicity is two.
The largest allowed coefficient sum is \(H_*=1091659\), obtained by \( ((2pb+1)H_*^2)^4<2^p<((2pb+1)(H_*+1)^2)^4\).
Suppose a primitive row counts two different values and take \(F\) to be the product of **every** distinct qualifying minimum for that row, each with prescribed order two.
By the [reduction to reciprocal units](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper) these are monic reciprocal of even degree.
The [derivative product](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#product) and its [sharpened row bound](Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) give
\[
               D\le2b,\qquad
               m(F)<\tfrac14\log2-p^{-1}\log((2pb+1)/(pb+1)).
\]
The same product bound counts each distinct factor regardless of whether its measure repeats.
Each degree is at least eight.
The [quadratic and quartic list](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short), the [quartic lower bounds](Yin_L_G0462_v247.md#five) and the [sextic census](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0178/readme.md#census) give measures above this budget in all smaller even degrees.
Since there are at least two factors, each degree is at most \(228-8=220<p-1\).
This verifies the initial radius hypothesis of (1).
Using \(H_*<2^{28}\), \(p>2^7\) and \(m(F)<(\log2)/4\) in (1) gives
\[
        \sum_\xi E(\xi)^2<
        \frac{2\cdot228\cdot28+223\cdot114/2}{7}<61^2 .
\]
Thus each \(e_\xi\le E(\xi)\le60\) and \(2E(\xi)-1<223-e_\xi\).
We may use (2) at every disk with \(k_\xi=E(\xi)\), including the endpoints.
In particular \(\bar F\mid\bar A\), giving \(D\le114\).

Let \(h_L\) be the logarithmic Lehmer measure.
If any measure were at least \(h_L\), each other one would be less than
\[
                  0.69315/4-0.162356-0.6926/223<1/108 ,
\]
by the [verified logarithm bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite) and \((2pb+1)/(pb+1)>1.999\).
At degree at most 114 [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives a strict lower bound \(2/\log^3(342)>1/108\).
Therefore every qualifying measure lies strictly below \(h_L\).
The [Mossinghoff–Rhin–Wu degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree) gives \(d_i\ge56\).
Exactly two qualifying minima occur and the only possible degree pairs are \((56,56)\) and \((56,58)\).
A nontrivial power substitution in either minimum would give a smaller-degree polynomial of the same strict sub-Lehmer measure, which the same theorem excludes.

Here \(K=D\), so (5) fixes the whole row modulo \(223^2\) for each residue class of the quotient \(C\) modulo \(223^2\).
This leaves three quotient coefficients in the \((56,56)\) case and only the leading scalar in the \((56,58)\) case, for each reduced product and sign.
Formula (4) also gives \(223^{\sum E(\xi)^2}\le H_*^{114}\exp(223\cdot114\,m(F))\) on multiplying the powered resultants.
The integer inequality \(223^{4\cdot34^2}>1091659^{4\cdot114}2^{223\cdot114}\) then gives \(E(\xi)\le33\) throughout.
These restrictions leave existence of a qualifying pair at 223 open.
