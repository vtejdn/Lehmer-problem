# Small primes can miss a true quartic Salem exclusion

<a id="least"></a>
## A least certificate prime at the cubic logarithmic scale

Let \(g(T)=T^4-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^3-T^4\), and write \(P_{a,b}(X)=-g(X^a)-X^bB(X^a)\).
We use odd \(a\), positive \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
Here the [quartic cyclotomic table](Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#orders) makes the entire parent its cyclotomic-free part, \(P_{a,b}=X^nH_{a,b}(X+X^{-1})\).
At sufficiently large \(r=b/a\), a good prime \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}H_{a,b}\) certifies that the negative exterior root \(-\sigma\) does not give a Salem number if the least odd irreducible degree of \(H_{a,b}\bmod p\) is at least \(r\).
The [one-prime theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v220.md#maximum) ensures such a prime exists whenever \(\sigma\) is not Salem.

There are primitive directions with \(a,r\to\infty\),
\[
            a\asymp(\log b/\log\log b)^3,
            \qquad p_{\rm least}\gg\log b\asymp a^{1/3}\log a ,
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
for which \(\sigma\) is not Salem and \(p_{\rm least}\) is the least good certifying prime.
For these directions a common false divisor of degree three persists modulo every integer up to a positive constant times \(\log b\).
The leading constant in the choice of \(a\) below is taken large compared with the constant in the necessary height bound.

To construct the directions, take an integer \(Y\to\infty\), \(L_Y=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Y)\), and the fixed seed
\[
 P_*=P_{1,2}=X^6+X^5-X^4-X^2+X+1
     =X^3\big[(X+X^{-1})^3+(X+X^{-1})^2-4(X+X^{-1})-2\big].
\]
Let \(t\) be the order of \(X\) in the unit group of \((\mathbb Z/L_Y\mathbb Z)[X]/(P_*)\) and set \(M=4\cdot 2^Y t\).
The unit constant term and monicity give \(t\le L_Y^6\).
The elementary [least common multiple bound](Yin_L_G0462_v207.md#moduli) gives \(\log L_Y=O(Y)\), so \(\log M\asymp Y\).
Choose an odd prime \(a\asymp D(Y/\log Y)^3\) coprime to \(M\), for a sufficiently large fixed constant \(D\), and set \(b=2a+M\).
Then \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), and \(r\to\infty\).
Also \(\log b\asymp Y\), with an absolute eventual upper bound \(C Y\) for any fixed \(D\).

For the prime choice, put \(\vartheta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\).
The bounds \(c x\le\vartheta(x)\le C'x\) for large \(x\) suffice.
Indeed for integral \(x\) the largest binomial coefficient in row \(x\) is at least \(2^x/(x+1)\) and divides \(L_x\), since each power of a prime contributes at most one to its valuation by the factorial formula.
The difference \(\log L_x-\vartheta(x)=O(\sqrt{x}\log x)\) follows by counting only primes at most \(\sqrt{x}\), each contributing at most \(\log x\).
These give the lower estimate, and the upper one follows from \(\log L_x=O(x)\).
For some fixed \(K>1\), the sum of \(\log p\) over \(A<p\le KA\) therefore exceeds \(\log M\) whenever \(A/Y\to\infty\).
Taking \(A=D(Y/\log Y)^3\) gives the required prime in that interval.

By the [primitive height and exterior estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#counts), a Salem output at these slopes would require \(a\le C_0(\log b/\log\log b)^3\) with a fixed \(C_0\).
The preceding upper bound \(\log b\le C Y\) and lower bound \(\log b\ge Y\log2\) contradict this for sufficiently large \(D\).
Thus an actual certificate exists.
The congruence argument below excludes all \(p\le Y\), giving (1).
For a choice independent of the unspecified \(C_0\), one may instead use \(A=(Y/\log Y)^3\log\log Y\), obtaining unconditionally \(p_{\rm least}\gg a^{1/3}\log a/(\log\log a)^{1/3}\).

## The common cubic divisor, including at prime powers

In \(R=\mathbb Z/L_Y\mathbb Z\) put \(\mathcal A=R[X]/(P_*)\) and denote the class of \(X\) by \(x\).
For every odd \(a\) coprime to \(M\), choose a positive integer \(e\) with \(ae\equiv1\bmod t\) and put \(z=x^e\).
Then \(z^a=x,\ z^M=1\), so \(z\) generates \(\mathcal A\) and satisfies \(P_{a,2a+M}(z)=0\).
The characteristic polynomial \(Q\) of multiplication by \(z\) is monic of degree six and annihilates \(z\).
Thus \(R[Z]/(Q)\to\mathcal A,\ Z\mapsto z\), is surjective between finite free modules of equal rank, hence bijective by equal cardinality.
It follows that \(Q\mid P_{a,2a+M}\) in \(R[Z]\).
This uses the full ring, including any repeated residual roots.

The involution \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\) takes \(z\) to \(z^{-1}\), and the determinant of multiplication by \(z\) is one since the same holds for \(x\).
Consequently \(Q\) is reciprocal with unit constant term one.
Its monic quotient in the reciprocal parent is also reciprocal, by cancellation of the monic polynomial \(Q\).
Both have even degree.
Over any coefficient ring a monic reciprocal polynomial of degree \(2h\) equals \(Z^h q(Z+Z^{-1})\) for a monic \(q\) of degree \(h\), by pairing symmetrical coefficients and using the integral recurrence for \(Z^k+Z^{-k}\).
This represents the product of the two trace polynomials as the trace polynomial of the parent, since substitution \(Y'\mapsto Z+Z^{-1}\) is injective by its outermost coefficient.
Thus one monic cubic \(q\in R[Y']\) divides the trace modulo \(L_Y\) and hence modulo each \(2\le m\le Y\).
At every such prime the cubic has an irreducible constituent of degree one or three, with multiplicities allowed.
The primitive [individual degree lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) eventually excludes even a rational divisor of degree three in the trace since its reciprocal lift would have degree six.

The same construction works with \(A=Y^\alpha\) for any fixed \(1<\alpha<3\), keeping \(a\asymp(\log b)^\alpha\) inside the necessary height range.
The least good prime attaining the full odd degree in L220 still exceeds \(Y\), since that degree tends to infinity.
In this range the construction alone does not determine whether the marked factor is Salem.

## A false common divisor at the nearest predicted Salem trace degree

There is also a family in which every modulus \(2\le m\le Y\) admits *the same integral trace polynomial* of growing odd degree \(j\) as a divisor modulo \(m\), with
\[
                        \left|j-\frac r2\right|=\frac2a .
\]
Here the first exponent is much larger than the cubic logarithmic scale of (1).

Take \(j=2Y+1,\ b_*=2j-4\), so \(P_{1,b_*}\) has no cyclotomic factor, and its trace \(H_*\) is monic of degree \(j\).
With \(L_Y\) as above, form \(t\) using \(P_{1,b_*}\) in place of \(P_*\) and again set \(M=4\cdot2^Yt\).
Choose
\[
                 a=1+M,\qquad b=b_*a+4M=2ja-4 .
\]
Then \(a\) is odd, \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), and \(\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,4)=1\).
In the original finite ring \(x^a=x\) and \(x^b=x^{b_*}\), so the seed parent divides the new parent modulo \(L_Y\).
The same reciprocal quotient argument gives \(H_*\mid H_{a,b}\bmod L_Y\).
The degree relation follows from \(r=2j-4/a\), making \(j\) the unique nearest integer to \(r/2\) eventually.

Since \(t\le L_Y^{2j}\), we have \(\log b=O(Y^2)\), while \(a\ge2^Y\) and \(r\to\infty\).
The necessary Salem height bound excludes a Salem factor, and the primitive degree lower bound exceeds \(2j\) for all large \(Y\), excluding any rational trace divisor of degree \(j\).
The least good certifying prime thus still exceeds \(Y\gg\sqrt{\log b}\).
Intersecting possible modular divisor degrees across all of these primes retains the very integer nearest the one-exterior-root prediction, even if each modulus up to \(Y\) is used.
Whether such simultaneous retention at the nearest predicted degree occurs when \(a\) grows within the necessary Salem height range remains open.
