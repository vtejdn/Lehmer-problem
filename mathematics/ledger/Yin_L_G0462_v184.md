# Integer moments of the neutral branch measure at a fixed Salem input

<a id="fixed"></a>
## An obstruction to total branch mass one half independent of the grid

Fix a Salem number \(\sigma>1\) with trace \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\) and monic trace minimal polynomial \(Q_0\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
Put \(A=Q_0(0)\ne0\) and \(M=\max_{[-2,2]}|Q_0|\).
Consider actual Pisot inputs at the square constants \(q=d^2\), of even degrees \(d\to\infty\), whose full return has \(\sigma\) as exterior root.
Write \(\nu_d\) for the positive measure assigning the neutral **branch** residues to their traces, and \(\rho(dx)=dx/(\pi\sqrt{4-x^2})\) for the arcsine probability.
For every such sequence,
\[
 \liminf_d \nu_d([-2,2])\ \ge\
 \begin{cases}
   1/2                         & A\ \text{even},\\
   1/2+1/(2M)                  & A\ \text{odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Here \(M\ge |A|\ge1\).
For each fixed input, total branch mass tending to \(1/2\) forces the entire measure to tend weakly to \(\rho/2\).
Thus even the total \(1/2\) is impossible for fixed inputs with odd \(A\), allowing arbitrary cyclotomic factors and arbitrary positive nonuniform residues in each actual return.
For example \(Q_0=x^2-3x+1\) has \(A=1\), \(M=11\), and gives the lower bound \(6/11\).
Its roots \((3\pm\sqrt5)/2\) are respectively above two and in \((-2,2)\), and the quadratic \(z^2-Tz+1\) is irreducible over the real trace field because its discriminant becomes negative at the other real embedding.

<a id="moments"></a>
## Exact integral tests with the exterior pole removed

For a single actual full return, let \(Q=(x-T)H\) be its monic trace polynomial, of degree \(d/2\), and \(R\) its integral inverse polynomial with leading coefficient \(1-q\) and degree \(d/2-1\).
Its trace roots are distinct.
The [branch rational function](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches) is
\[
 \mathcal B(x)=1-q-(x-2)R(x)/Q(x)
              =\frac{c_T}{x-T}+\sum_i\frac{c_i}{x-x_i},
                 \qquad \nu_d=\sum_i c_i\delta_{x_i}.
\]
Its Laurent series at infinity begins at \(x^{-1}\), since the leading constant cancels.
All its coefficients are integers by formal division using monicity of \(Q\).
Consequently every moment of \(c_T\delta_T+\nu_d\) against an integer polynomial is an integer.
Taking the polynomial divisible by \(Q_0\) eliminates the exterior residue exactly, giving
\[
                            \int Q_0 W\,d\nu_d\in\mathbb Z
                               \qquad(W\in\mathbb Z[x]).              \tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(Q_0\), every finite weak limit \(\nu\) satisfies the same tests, by compactness of the trace interval and closedness of the integers.

Write \(C_j(2\cos t)=2\cos(jt)\) for \(j\ge1\).
The basis \(1,C_1,C_2,\ldots\) is monic and integral.
For \(Q_0=a_0+\sum_{j>0}a_j C_j\) and \(W=b_0+\sum_{j>0}b_j C_j\), orthogonality gives
\[
       \int Q_0W\,d\rho=a_0 b_0+2\sum_{j>0}a_j b_j
                      \equiv Q_0(0) W(0)\pmod2,
\]
since every \(C_j(0)\) is even.
Thus the tests (2) on the proposed limit \(\rho/2\) hold exactly for the fixed inputs with even \(A\).

<a id="structure"></a>
## The absolutely continuous branch density is a half-integral polynomial

Take any bounded-mass subsequence and any of its weak limits \(\nu\).
The [angular product estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#angular) applies since \(\sigma\) is fixed, \(d\to\infty\) and \(\log q=o(d)\).
It gives \(\rho\ll\nu\), in particular \(\nu\) cannot be purely atomic.
Lift \(2Q_0(x)\nu(dx)\) to a finite signed measure on the circle by placing equal masses at the two preimages of \(x=2\cos t\), interpreted as a single preimage at either endpoint.
At nonzero Fourier index \(j\) its coefficient equals \(\int Q_0 C_{|j|}\,d\nu\), and at zero it equals \(2\int Q_0\,d\nu\).
All these coefficients are integers by (2).

The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem on \(\mathbb Z\)](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) expresses the coefficients as a finite integer sum of singleton and congruence-class indicators.
A singleton contributes an integer Laurent monomial density against Haar probability.
A class modulo a positive integer contributes a measure supported on roots of unity, by finite Fourier inversion.
Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients identifies the lifted measure with this sum.
Its absolutely continuous part is real and invariant under inversion by uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition, so its Laurent density is \(D(x)\) for some \(D\in\mathbb Z[x]\) in the basis above.
Pushing down gives \(2Q_0\nu=D\rho\) plus a measure supported on finitely many torsion traces.

Away from the finite zero set of \(Q_0\), the density of \(\nu\) is \(D/(2Q_0)\) and its remaining part consists of finitely many atoms.
Any mass on the zero set is also atomic.
Every Salem trace polynomial has a simple real conjugate in \((-2,2)\).
At such a conjugate the sign change of \(Q_0\) and positivity of \(\nu\) force \(D\) to vanish.
Rational irreducibility and monicity then give \(D=Q_0 H_1\) with \(H_1\in\mathbb Z[x]\).
Thus every bounded-mass weak limit has the form
\[
       \nu=\tfrac12 H_1\,\rho+\eta,\qquad H_1\ge0\text{ on }[-2,2],  \tag{3}
\]
where \(\eta\) is a positive finite sum of atoms at torsion traces or at neutral Salem conjugates.
The domination \(\rho\ll\nu\) implies \(H_1\ne0\).
The integer \(\int H_1\,d\rho\) is therefore positive.
If it equals one, every nonzero Fourier coefficient of \(H_1(2\cos t)\) has modulus strictly less than one by the strict triangle inequality on an open interval where the density is positive.
Those coefficients are integers, so \(H_1=1\).
In particular every limit of mass below one has the same absolutely continuous part \(\rho/2\).

For odd \(A\), (2) with \(W=1\) now gives \(\left|\int Q_0\,d\eta\right|\ge1/2\) whenever the limit has mass below one.
Hence \(\eta([-2,2])\ge1/(2M)\).
This proves (1) for such limits, while limits of mass at least one satisfy it automatically.
Any finite liminf over the original sequence is realized on a bounded-mass subsequence with a convergent further subsequence.
Equation (3) also gives uniqueness of the possible mass-\(1/2\) limit.

<a id="atoms"></a>
## Where one correcting atom can occur

For fixed odd \(A\), suppose \(\rho/2+\lambda\delta_s\) passes (2), with \(\lambda>0\) and \(-2\le s\le2\).
Then
\[
                       s\in\{-2,0,2\},\qquad
                           2\lambda Q_0(s)\in2\mathbb Z+1.            \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(N=2\lambda Q_0(s)\) is an odd integer by the constant test, and \(Ns^j\) is an even integer for every \(j\ge1\) by testing \(x^j\).
The first of these power tests makes \(s\) rational and all of them together force its reduced denominator to equal one.
Then \(Ns\) even forces \(s\) even.
Conversely (4) suffices for every test (2), because \(W(s)\equiv W(0)\bmod2\).
For an atom at zero its smallest possible mass is \(1/(2|A|)\), attained by the [odd-grid construction](Yin_L_G0462_v181.md#statement).
This establishes central-atom optimality across all actual returns of this fixed input when the rest of the limiting measure equals \(\rho/2\).
For an endpoint atom (4) is a moment condition, and no realization is asserted.

<a id="varying"></a>
## Quantitative tests for finite returns and the varying-input boundary

If \(A\) and \(W(0)\) are odd, then each finite return already satisfies
\[
             \left|\int Q_0W\,d(\nu_d-\rho/2)\right|\ge\tfrac12.
\]
In the total variation norm, defined as the integral of the absolute measure, this gives
\(\|\nu_d-\rho/2\|_{\rm TV}\ge1/(2\|Q_0W\|_\infty)\).
The same test gives a bounded-Lipschitz distance at least \(1/(2(\|Q_0W\|_\infty+\|(Q_0W)'\|_\infty))\) for the convention that the sum of the uniform norm and the Lipschitz constant is at most one.
Both inequalities hold also for a fixed-input weak limit by (2).
They apply separately at each member of a varying sequence, with the actual test norm in that member.
In particular uniformly bounded test norms in the bounded-Lipschitz inequality preclude weak convergence to \(\rho/2\) for a bounded-mass sequence.
Bounds on the central integers \(Q_0(0)\) alone do not control these norms as the polynomials change.
The decomposition argument used one fixed polynomial throughout the weak limit.
It does not supply a positive uniform gap above \(1/2\) for varying Salem inputs with bounded odd central integers.

The [even grid](Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#half) realizes the half measure if \(x^2\mid\overline Q_0\), and the [odd grid](Yin_L_G0462_v181.md#statement) realizes it if \(4\mid Q_0(0)\).
For fixed \(Q_0(0)\equiv2\bmod4\) with odd \(Q_0'(0)\), the complete moment tests for the half measure pass and those two constructions leave its realization open.
