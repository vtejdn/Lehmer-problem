# Exterior pairs determine the factor measures in the escaping cubic family

<a id="partition"></a>
## A partition of the smaller exponent

Set \(g(T)=T^3-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), \(f=-g/B\), and let \(\theta>1\) be the real root of \(g\).
For coprime positive integers \(a,b\) put \(A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) and \(r=b/a\).
There are fixed constants \(r_0,c,C>0\) such that for \(r\ge r_0\) all roots of \(A_{a,b}\) are simple.
There are exactly \(b-a\) roots on the unit circle and exactly \(a\) complex conjugate pairs of exterior roots.
Each nontorsion monic irreducible factor \(P\) of \(-A_{a,b}\) is reciprocal and receives an integer \(k_P\ge1\) of those pairs.
These integers satisfy
\[
 \sum_P k_P=a,\qquad
 \left|m(P)-\frac{k_P}{a}\log\theta\right|
                  \le C\frac{k_P}{a}e^{-cr},                            \tag{1}
\]
where \(m=\log M\).
For \(d=\deg P\) there is also a uniform estimate
\[
 \left|\frac{b\,m(P)}d-\log\theta\right|
             \ll\sqrt{\frac ab}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}d}.                \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(d/b=(k_P/a)(1+o(1))\), with a relative error tending to zero uniformly over all factors as \(r\to\infty\).

<a id="projection"></a>
## A power equation with fixed exterior centers

The identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) makes \(g,B\) coprime.
For a root \(\alpha\) put \(t=\alpha^a\) and \(y=\alpha^b=f(t)\).
The roots are nonzero algebraic integer units, and the Bézout identity gives that \(B(t)\) is a unit as well.
Choose integers \(u,v\) with \(au+bv=1\).
Then \(\alpha=t^u f(t)^v\), so \(\mathbb Q(t)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
In fact taking \(a\)-th powers identifies the full set of roots with the roots of
\[
                          D(T)=T^b B(T)^a-(-g(T))^a .
\]
At any root \(t\) of \(D\) both \(t\) and \(f(t)\) are nonzero, \(t^b=f(t)^a\), and the inverse formula \(t^u f(t)^v\) gives the desired root.
Multiplicities agree because \(\prod_{\zeta^a=1} A_{a,b}(\zeta X)\) is \(D(X^a)\) up to sign, by coprimality of \(a,b\).
At most one factor of this product vanishes at any given root, as two such rotations would have equal \(a\)-th and \(b\)-th powers.
The power map has nonzero derivative at every such root.

The [cubic root calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#seed) gives one exterior real root of \(g\) and an interior nonreal pair.
Accordingly \(B\) has just two exterior roots \(\lambda,\overline\lambda\), each simple, of modulus \(\theta^{1/2}\).
Take fixed disjoint small closed disks about them in \(|T|>1\), each containing no other zero of \(gB\).
On either boundary \(|T|^r|B(T)|>|g(T)|\) for all sufficiently large \(r\).
Rouché's theorem gives exactly \(a\) roots of \(D\) in each disk, counted with multiplicity.
At a root in the disk about \(\lambda\), we have \(|B(t)|=|t|^{-r}|g(t)|\) and \(|B(t)|\gg|t-\lambda|\), so \(|t-\lambda|\ll e^{-cr}\) for a fixed \(c>0\).
The conjugate disk has the same estimate.
These roots are simple for large \(r\).
Indeed a repeated root would give \(t f'(t)/f(t)=r\), while near a simple pole at \(\lambda\) the left side equals \(-t/(t-\lambda)+O(1)\) and has modulus \(\gg e^{cr}\) at these roots.

<a id="circle"></a>
## Counting the circle roots

On \(|t|=1\) one has \(|f(t)|=1\), and neither \(g\) nor \(B\) vanishes there.
A smooth lift \(\phi(s)=\arg f(e^{is})\) satisfies \(\phi(s+2\pi)-\phi(s)=2\pi\), since \(g\) has two zeros and \(B\) has one zero in the disk.
The periodic function \(\phi'\) is bounded.
For \(r>\max|\phi'|\), the lift \(s\mapsto bs-a\phi(s)\) is strictly increasing, has positive derivative, and gains \(2\pi(b-a)\) over a period.
Thus \(D\) has precisely \(b-a\) simple roots on the circle.
The reversal identity \(T^{b+3a} D(1/T)=(-1)^{a+1}D(T)\) supplies the reciprocal interior roots of all the exterior roots found above.
Together they exhaust its degree \(b+3a\).

The root correspondence preserves complex conjugation and maps the exterior roots of \(-A_{a,b}\) bijectively to the two nonreal disks.
For a monic irreducible factor \(P\), its exterior roots therefore occur in pairs, one per disk in each pair.
Each exterior pair contributes \(a^{-1}(\log\theta+O(e^{-cr}))\) to \(m(P)\), by \(\log|\alpha|=a^{-1}\log|t|\).
A nontorsion unit factor must have at least one exterior root by Kronecker's theorem.
This proves (1), and summing also gives \(m(A_{a,b})=\log\theta+O(e^{-cr})\).
The polynomial \(A_{a,b}\) is reciprocal, since reversal exchanges its two summands.
A nonreciprocal irreducible unit factor would occur together with its distinct reversal, each of measure at least \(\log\theta\) by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions).
The total measure is strictly less than \(2\log\theta\) for all sufficiently large \(r\), proving the reciprocity assertion.

<a id="degree"></a>
## Reading a factor degree from its exterior pairs

Write \(h\) for the absolute logarithmic height.
The equality of fields above and the unit \(B(t)\) give the exact identity
\[
  b h(\alpha)=d^{-1}\sum_{\sigma}\log\max(|g(\sigma t)|,|B(\sigma t)|).
\]
The pointwise upper bound is \(\log3+3\log^+|\sigma t|\), so \(h(\alpha)\le\log3/(b-3a)\) for \(r>3\).
In particular \(h(t)=a h(\alpha)\ll 1/r\) uniformly for \(r\ge6\).
The own-degree logarithmic measure of \(t\) is \(m_t=d h(t)\).

Apply [the quantitative orbit average, equation (3)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test), to the fixed pair \(g,B\) and the monic minimal polynomial of \(t\) of degree \(d\) and measure \(m_t\).
It bounds the error from the circular mean of the displayed test by
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{m_t+1}{d}+
               \sqrt{\frac{m_t+1+\log(2d)}{d}}\right).
\]
On the unit circle \(|g|=|B|\) and the circular mean is \(m(g)=\log\theta\).
Substitution of \(m_t/d\ll1/r\) proves (2).
The degrees tend to infinity uniformly as \(r\to\infty\), since \(b\to\infty\) and the upper height bound tends to zero, whereas algebraic numbers of bounded degree and bounded height form a finite set.
Combining (1) and (2) gives the stated relative estimate for \(d/b\).

<a id="small-parts"></a>
## The remaining arithmetic question

There are at most \(a\) positive-measure irreducible factors.
The measures of selected factors tend to zero as \(r\to\infty\) precisely when their assigned parts satisfy \(k_P=o(a)\).
This specifies the exterior roots that must occur in the [general sublinear-degree criterion for primitive directions](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement).
Its degree floor also gives \(k_P\gg a(\log\log b/\log b)^3\) for large \(r\).
The partition identity records the parts allocated to actual rational irreducible factors.
It leaves open whether arbitrarily small positive proportions of the \(a\) exterior pairs occur in such factors.
