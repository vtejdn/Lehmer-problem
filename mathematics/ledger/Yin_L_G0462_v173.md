# Simultaneous concentration of powered-order exponents in a nonreciprocal family

<a id="family"></a>
## One irreducible factor for every prescribed prime

Let \(S\) be a nonempty finite set of odd primes, \(n=\prod_{p\in S}p\), and let \(m\ge2\) be even.
Consider the monic unit polynomial
\[
  F(x)=(x+2)(x^{nm}+1)+x^n-1=x^{nm}(x+2)+x^n+x+1 .
\]
There is a nonreciprocal irreducible factor \(P\) of degree \(d>nm/8\), with \(\log\theta\le\log M(P)\le\tfrac12\log8\), where \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
For a root \(\alpha\) of this same \(P\), every \(p\in S\) preserves degree and, with \(I_p=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]]\) and \(s_p=\exp(\mathbb Z[\alpha]/\mathbb Z[\alpha^p])\),
\[
 \frac{\log s_p}{p}\ge\left(\frac{n-1}{3n}-\frac1p\right)\log2,\qquad
 \frac{\log I_p}{pd}\le\frac{n(p-1)\log2}{2pd}<\frac{4\log2}{m}.       \tag{1}
\]
Every pair of prescribed classes at each such \(p\) also has an explicit primitive two-row witness of degree at most \(p(16d+1)\) with \(P\) of multiplicity exactly two and
\[
                 \frac{\log H}{Np}\le\frac{\log p}{p}+\frac{\log2}{2m}, \tag{2}
\]
where \(H\) is its absolute stripped row resultant and \(N\) is its stripped degree sum.
This gives explicit instances of the [small-index upper bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v378.md#window) with large exponent rate.

For \(Z=x^n\), a simultaneous root \(x\) of \(F(x)\) and \(F(1/x)\) satisfies
\[
  Z(x+2)(1+2x)-(Z+x+1)(x+Z+xZ)=(x+1)(x-Z)(Z-1)=0
\]
by eliminating \(x^{nm}\) from the two equations.
Thus every reciprocal factor is cyclotomic after irreducible factorization.
Parseval and the logarithmic mean inequality give \(M(F)\le\sqrt8\), since the five coefficients have squared sum eight.
By [Smyth's nonreciprocal bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions), each noncyclotomic irreducible factor has measure at least \(\theta\).
Since \(\theta>33/25\) by substitution in its cubic and \((33/25)^4>3>\sqrt8\), there are at most three such factors counted with multiplicities.

For \(p\in S\) put \(\nu=n/p,\ k=nm/p,\ X=x^p\), and write
\[
             B=X^k+1,\qquad A=2B+X^\nu-1,\qquad F=A+xB .
\]
The polynomials \(A,B\) cannot vanish together, since \(X^\nu=1\) implies \(X^k=1\).
At any zero of \(F\), the relation \(x=-A(x^p)/B(x^p)\) therefore proves degree preservation for this \(p\).
In characteristic two every \(\omega\in\mu_n\) is a simple root of \(F\bmod2\), since \(x^{nm}+1\) and its derivative vanish there and \(n\omega^{n-1}\ne0\).
Thus the \(n\) residues lift uniquely to simple roots of \(F\) in a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\).
A root of unity reducing to a nontrivial \(\omega\in\mu_n\) would have order divisible by some \(p\in S\).
Taking its \(p\)-th power lowers its cyclotomic degree by the formula for Euler's totient, contradicting degree preservation because \(p\) is odd.
At least \(n-1\) marked lifts are therefore nontorsion.
Select one irreducible noncyclotomic \(P\) containing at least \((n-1)/3\) of them.
It occurs simply in \(F\), since it contains a simple marked root.

For any root \(\alpha\) of \(P\) the relation \(\alpha^{nm}=-(\alpha^n+\alpha+1)/(\alpha+2)\) bounds its degree from below.
Indeed at each place the maximum modulus of the numerator and denominator is at most \(\max(1,|\alpha|)^n\), times three at an archimedean place.
The denominator is nonzero because \(F(-2)=(-2)^n-1\ne0\).
Taking the absolute projective logarithmic height and using the product formula gives \(nm\,h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)\le n\,h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)+\log3\).
As \(d\,h_{\rm abs}(\alpha)=\log M(P)\ge\log\theta\), we get
\[
                d\ge n(m-1)\frac{\log\theta}{\log3}
                     > n(m-1)/4\ge nm/8 .                               \tag{3}
\]

## The whole quotient from the marked residues

Fix \(p\in S\).
Let \(t_b\) count the marked roots of \(P\) above residues \(\omega\in\mu_n\) with \(\omega^p=b\), for \(b\in\mu_\nu\) in the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_2\).
Include the mark of residue one if it is a root of \(P\).
Then the whole additive quotient is
\[
 \mathbb Z[\alpha]/\mathbb Z[\alpha^p]\simeq
       \bigoplus_{b:\ t_b>0}\ \bigoplus_{j=1}^{t_b-1}\mathbb Z/2^j\mathbb Z .
                                                                            \tag{4}
\]
In particular \(I_p=2^{\sum_b\binom{t_b}{2}}\) and \(s_p=2^{\max_b(t_b-1)}\).

To verify the full prime support, write \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{nm+1}\) for all the complex roots of \(F\) with multiplicity, and \(D=nm+1\).
The symmetric polynomial \(K_p(u,v)=(u^p-v^p)/(u-v)\) is evaluated as a geometric sum also at \(u=v\).
For every nontrivial \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), one has \(F(\zeta\xi_i)=(\zeta-1)\xi_i B(\xi_i^p)\).
Since \(B\) is monic, evaluation at its roots gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,A)|=|\operatorname{Res}(B,X^\nu-1)|=2^\nu\).
Indeed \(B\) takes the value two at every \(\nu\)-th root of unity.
Evaluating at the roots of \(B(x^p)\) consequently gives \(\prod_i |B(\xi_i^p)|=2^{\nu p}=2^n\).
Multiplying the rotation identity over \(i,\zeta\ne1\) gives \(p^D2^{n(p-1)}\).
On expanding \(F\) into linear factors, the diagonal terms give \(p^D\) since \(\prod_i|\xi_i|=1\), and every unordered pair gives \(|K_p(\xi_i,\xi_j)|^2\).
This [rotation computation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) gives the absolute value of the integer pair product as \(2^{n(p-1)/2}\).
The [power-index formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index) gives \(I_p\) as the absolute product restricted to pairs of conjugates of \(\alpha\).
Its complementary product is also a rational algebraic integer by Galois invariance, so \(I_p\) has only possible prime divisor two.

Over two normalize \(v(2)=1\).
Above each \(b\in\mu_\nu\) the full polynomial has \(p\) marked lifts with distinct original residues and powered residues equal to \(b\).
Every marked pair in this fiber contributes at least one to \(v(K_p)\), since their power difference has positive integral valuation in the unramified field and their original difference is a unit.
All other pair valuations are nonnegative by integrality of the geometric sum.
The \(\nu\binom p2=n(p-1)/2\) marked pairs already exhaust the full valuation, so every one contributes exactly one and all other pairs contribute zero.
This proves the asserted order of the quotient for \(P\).

For the invariant factors extend scalars from \(\mathbb Z_2\) to the integers \(\mathcal O\) of a finite unramified extension containing the marked lifts and splitting the residue polynomials.
Hensel factorization and the Chinese remainder theorem split the two orders into blocks according to the residue of \(\beta=\alpha^p\), compatibly with the inclusion.
Indeed the coprime residue factors of the powered minimal polynomial lift to idempotents whose evaluations on the original roots select the same powered residues.
For \(b\in\mu_\nu\) every original conjugate above \(b\) must reduce to \(\mu_n\), so the larger order in that block is \(\mathcal O^{t_b}\) by the simple original roots.
The smaller order is the evaluation lattice generated by powers of \(\beta\) at these \(t_b\) coordinates, with the powers of degrees \(0,\ldots,t_b-1\) sufficient by monic division.
Choose a lift \(c\in\mathcal O\) of \(b\) and write the coordinates as \(c+2y_i\).
The reductions of the \(y_i\) are pairwise distinct because each power difference has valuation exactly one.
In the basis of powers of \(\beta-c\) the evaluation matrix is the Vandermonde matrix of the \(y_i\) times \(\operatorname{diag}(1,2,\ldots,2^{t_b-1})\).
The first matrix has unit determinant, giving precisely the indicated elementary divisors.
Their total length already equals \(v_2(I_p)\), so all remaining blocks have zero cokernel.
The integer Smith factors have the same two-adic valuations after this unramified extension, which still has uniformizer two, proving (4).
Finally \(\sum_b t_b\ge(n-1)/3\) and each of the \(\nu=n/p\) fibers has at most \(p\) marks.
Taking the largest fiber for \(s_p\) and the full pair product for \(I_p\) gives (1) using (3).

## Exact resultants and degrees for all shifts

For any prescribed \(0\le a<b<p\) put \(r=b-a\).
Use \(L=xB(X)\) and \(W=-A(X)\), so \(L-W=F(x)\), and define
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_r&=rL^p+(p-r)W^p-pL^rW^{p-r}=U_r(X)+x^r V_r(X),\\
 U_r&=rXB^p+(p-r)W^p,\qquad V_r=-pB^r W^{p-r}.
 \end{split}
\]
In powers of \(L-W\) the expression starts with \(rp(p-r)W^{p-2}(L-W)^2/2\), by binomial expansion as in the [two-row doubling identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v373.md#witness).
At every root of \(P\), \(W=xB(x^p)\ne0\), and \(P\) occurs simply in \(F\), so \(P\) occurs exactly twice in \(J_r\).
Both rows have nonzero constant terms and degrees \(nm+1,nm\).
The leading coefficient and absolute constant term of \(U_r\) are \(r,p-r\), so \(J_r\) is primitive.
At roots of \(B\) the first row equals \((p-r)W^p\), giving \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,U_r)|=(p-r)^k2^{\nu p}\).
At roots of \(A\) it equals \(rXB^p\).
The factor from the leading coefficient of \(A\) is \(2^{nm+1}\), the product of its root moduli is \(1/2\), and \(\prod_{A(z)=0}|B(z)|=2^\nu/2^k\).
Hence \(|\operatorname{Res}(A,U_r)|=r^k2^{\nu p}\).
This proves coprimality, and multiplicativity gives
\[
      H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_r,V_r)|
        =p^{nm+1}(p-r)^{kr}r^{k(p-r)}2^{np},\qquad N=2nm+1 .            \tag{5}
\]
The polynomial \(x^aJ_r\) occupies exactly the prescribed classes \(a,b\), and the stripped rows, multiplicity and primitivity persist.
Its ordinary degree is \(p(nm+1)+a<p(nm+2)\).
By (3) the integer \(nm\) is at most \(8d-1\), so this degree is below \(p(8d+1)\) and lies within the [comparison cap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) \(p(16d+1)\) for the selected factor itself.
Since \(kr+k(p-r)=nm\), (5) gives (2) uniformly in the chosen classes.

## Prescribing several primes in the actual window

Fix \(0<\delta<1\) and let \(Y\to\infty\).
For any nonempty choice \(S\) of primes in \([5Y/4,7Y/4]\) with \(n=\prod_{p\in S}p\le\exp((1-\delta)Y^{1/4})\), take \(m=2\lceil\exp(Y^{1/4})/(6n)\rceil\).
Then \(m\to\infty\) and \(nm\sim\exp(Y^{1/4})/3\) uniformly in the permitted choices.
The factor degree satisfies \(nm/8<d\le nm+1\), so \(y(d)=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil=Y+O(Y^{3/4})\).
Consequently every prescribed prime belongs to \((y(d),2y(d)]\) for this one \(P\).
Simultaneously on \(S\), the exponent rate has lower limit at least \((\log2)/3\), the normalized index tends to zero, and (5) gives witnesses with \(\log H/(Np)\to0\) in every pair of classes.
These conclusions concern the prescribed primes and the full row resultant.
The nonreciprocal factors used here have the positive measure lower bound \(\log\theta\).
