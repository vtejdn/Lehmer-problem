# The collision dimensions of all finite convolutions at the two quartic Salem parameters

<a id="dimensions"></a>
## Strictly increasing dimensions tending to one

Let \(\beta\in(1,2)\) be one of the two quartic Salem numbers, \(m=\log\beta\), and let \(\nu\) be the law of \(\sum_{j\ge0}\xi_j\beta^{-j}\) for independent fair bits.
Write \(\nu_k=\nu^{*k}\), and let \(h_{k,2}\) be the collision entropy rate of \(k\) finite walks from the [packet argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v581.md#statement).
For every \(k\ge1\) the ordinary correlation dimension exists and equals \(h_{k,2}/m\).
These dimensions are strictly increasing in \(k\), remain below one at every finite \(k\), and tend to one.
Their deficits are \(O(k^{-1/4}/\log k)\) with an absolute constant for \(k\ge256\).

To see the dimension identity, couple two independent samples from \(\nu_k\), and put \(C_k(t)=\Pr(|X-X'|\le t)\).
Each sample's tail from time \(n\) lies in \([0,L_k\beta^{-n}]\), where \(L_k=k/(1-\beta^{-1})\).
For the finite prefix sums the collision probability equals \(p_{n,k}\) of the increasing-power walk by scaling and reversal.
Equal prefixes give \(C_k(L_k\beta^{-n})\ge p_{n,k}\).
Conversely a difference of two distinct prefixes is \(\beta^{1-n}F(\beta)\), with the integral coefficients of \(F\) bounded by \(k\) and supported below \(n\).
The values of \(F\) at the contracting conjugate are bounded by \(L_k\), and at the two neutral conjugates by \(kn\).
The nonzero integral norm therefore gives prefix separation at least \(c_{k,\beta}\beta^{-n}/n^2\).
If the full sums differ by at most \(L_k\beta^{-n}\), their prefixes differ by at most \(2L_k\beta^{-n}\).
Each prefix has at most \(O_{k,\beta}(n^2)\) neighbors at this distance.
Summing the products of their atom masses using \(2ab\le a^2+b^2\) proves \(C_k(L_k\beta^{-n})\ll_{k,\beta} n^2p_{n,k}\).
Taking logarithms and interpolating by monotonicity between consecutive scales gives \(\lim_{t\downarrow0}\log C_k(t)/\log t=h_{k,2}/m\).

The [complete periodic moments](Yin_L_G0462_v300.md#moments) give \(\delta_k=m-h_{k,2}\) as an exponential rate.
They decrease with \(k\), and Cauchy–Schwarz applied to successive even powers gives \(2\delta_{k+1}\le\delta_k+\delta_{k+2}\).
All \(\delta_k>0\) by the [finite-convolution result](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v581.md#packets), and the [one-expanding-conjugate bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v582.md#scalar) gives \(\delta_k/m\ll k^{-1/4}/\log k\).
If two consecutive deficits agreed, convexity and monotonicity would force every subsequent deficit to equal their common positive value, contradicting this limit.

<a id="lower"></a>
## A quantitative lower bound for the deficits

At either quartic there is also a constant \(C_P>0\) such that
\[
       \delta_k\ge\exp(-C_P k^\gamma)\quad(k\ge1),
       \qquad \gamma=1+\frac{\log 2}{\log(5/4)} .
\]
For the [quartic packet sequence](../audits/literature/CFMZ26/Yin_U002.md#controlled-returns-and-final-amplification), let \(t_j=\log(1/\Delta_j)\), \(N_j=\lceil e^{t_j}\rceil\), and write \(C_{N_j}\) for the per-placement cost.
The checked estimates give \(t_j\ge10(5/2)^j\), \(E_j\le C_E2^j\), and the [fixed-power argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v581.md#packets) gives \(C_{N_j}=E_j+o(1)\) and \(\log b_{N_j}\ge t_j-O_P(1)\).
For the neutral root \(\zeta\) and the mirror separation \(L_j\) one has \(\Delta_j=\Delta_{j-1}|1-\zeta^{L_j}|\), with \(L_j\le\exp(3t_{j-1}/2)\).
The [linear-form bound](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#wus02-u001) gives \(|1-\zeta^L|\gg_P (2L)^{-B_P}\) by comparing \(L\log\zeta\) to the nearest multiple of \(2\pi i\), since \(\zeta\) is nontorsion.
Consequently \(t_j\le A_P(1+t_{j-1})\).

Choose a large constant \(B\) and, beyond a fixed starting index \(j_0\) for these cost bounds, take the first \(j\ge j_0\) such that \(t_j\ge Bk2^j\).
Then \(\log b_{N_j}-kC_{N_j}\ge t_j/2\).
The lower growth of \(t_j\) guarantees \(j\le \log k/\log(5/4)+O_P(1)\).
If \(j>j_0\), the preceding index missed the threshold, so \(t_j\ll_P k2^j\ll_P k^\gamma\).
At \(j=j_0\) the same upper bound holds by enlarging its constant.
Applying the positive-gain bound \(\delta_k\ge(\log b_{N_j}-kC_{N_j})/(2N_j)\) proves the lower estimate.

## Integrability of a possible density

None of the \(\nu_k\) admits a density in \(L^p(\mathbb R)\) for any \(p>1\).
Indeed an \(L^2\) probability density \(f\) has \(C(t)\le2t\|f\|_2^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz applied to its translates, whereas every \(\nu_k\) has correlation dimension strictly less than one.
If some \(\nu_k\) had an \(L^p\) density with \(p>1\), it would belong to \(L^a\) for \(1\le a\le p\) by interpolation with \(L^1\).
Choose an integer \(\ell\ge1\) so that \(a=(1-1/(2\ell))^{-1}\le p\).
Repeated Young convolution inequalities would put the density of \(\nu_{k\ell}\) in \(L^2\), since \(\ell/a-(\ell-1)=1/2\).
This contradiction proves the asserted integrability exclusion.
The argument about densities alone leaves open the possibility of an \(L^1\) density outside all these higher integrability classes.
