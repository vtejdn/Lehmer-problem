# Primitive and full resultants at orders comparable to the original degree

<a id="linear"></a>
## Relative approximation for unrestricted integer inputs

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[z]\) have degree \(d\ge1\), no root of unity, and positive logarithmic Mahler measure \(m=m(P)\).
The polynomial can be nonmonic, reducible and have repeated roots.
Write \(D_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|\) and \(C_j=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^j-1)|\).
As \(Y\to\infty\) with \(Y\ge d\), uniformly in the input, the following bounds hold for integer orders \(Y\le j\le 2Y\).

1. Outside \(o(Y)\) orders one has simultaneously \(\log D_j\le(1+o(1))\varphi(j)m\) and \(\log C_j\le(1+o(1))jm\).
2. Outside at most \(d/2+o(Y)\) orders one has \(\log D_j=(1+o(1))\varphi(j)m\). In the \(d/2\) term one can instead count just the distinct nonreal conjugate pairs of roots of \(P\).
3. If \(Y/d\to\infty\), then additionally \(\log C_j/(jm)\to1\) in density on this window.

In the original degree window \(d\le j\le2d\), a fixed positive proportion of the composite orders satisfy \(\log C_j\ge\log D_j\gg d\,m(P)\).
The [earlier composite-order estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#density) used a longer window for a two-sided approximation without a support bound.
Here the Gram matrix of all the primitive tests has small off-diagonal entries, and the integer discriminant controls their common input.

<a id="energy"></a>
## Discriminant energy for the reflected root sums

Set \(t=1-(2Y)^{-6}\) and \(L=\log(2Y)\), taking \(Y\ge3\).
For a root \(\alpha_i\) put \(A_i=\max(1,|\alpha_i|)\) and \(w_i=\alpha_i/A_i^2\), preserving its angle on reflection into the disk.
Use the vector \(u_F(k)=t^{k/2}\sum_i w_i^k/\sqrt{k}\), \(k\ge1\), first for a primitive irreducible factor \(F\) of degree \(D\) and leading coefficient \(a\).
Its squared norm is
\[
             \|u_F\|_2^2=-\sum_{i,l}\log|1-tw_i\overline{w_l}|.
\]
For distinct roots, \(|1-w_i\overline{w_l}|\ge|\alpha_i-\alpha_l|/(A_i A_l)\).
When one root is outside the disk and the other inside this is equality.
When both are inside, it follows from \(|1-x\bar y|^2-|x-y|^2=(1-|x|^2)(1-|y|^2)\); when both are outside, apply the same identity to their reflected points.
For \(|v|\le1\), \(|1-tv|^2=t|1-v|^2+(1-t)(1-t|v|^2)\ge t|1-v|^2\).
The diagonal terms cost at most \(D\log(1/(1-t))\), so
\[
 \|u_F\|_2^2\le D\log(1/(1-t))
    -\tfrac12D(D-1)\log t
    +2(D-1)m(F)-\log|\operatorname{disc}F|.                            \tag{1}
\]
In fact the squared difference product contributes \(|a|^{-2D+2}|\operatorname{disc}F|\), accounting for the leading coefficient inside \(m(F)\).
The nonzero discriminant is an integer.
This gives the requisite energy directly; the [earlier Salem energy estimate](Yin_L_G0167_v1.md#actual-powered-salem-discriminant-energy) treats a powered Salem orbit.

Each factor other than \(z\) has \(m(F)\gg L^{-3}\).
A nonunit leading or nonzero constant coefficient of absolute value at least two gives \(m(F)\ge\log2\); in the remaining nontorsion unit case use the [degree-dependent bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) at degree \(D\le d\le Y\), with bounded degrees covered by the same bound after adjustment.
The factor \(z\) has zero vector.
It follows from (1) that \(\|u_F\|_2/m(F)\ll\sqrt d\, L^{7/2}\) for the positive-measure factors, since \(D^2(1-t)\ll1\).
Adding vectors with factor multiplicity and using the triangle inequality yields
\[
                    \|u_P\|_2^2\ll d L^7 m^2.                          \tag{2}
\]
The content contributes nonnegatively to \(m\) and contributes nothing to the root sums.
This use of irreducible factors avoids applying a discriminant estimate to a polynomial with repeated roots.

<a id="tests"></a>
## Two Gram matrices for the return tests

For a conjugation-invariant finite set of nodes \(E\) on the circle put \(v_E(k)=t^{k/2}\sum_{\zeta\in E}\zeta^k/\sqrt{k}\).
Its scalar product with \(u_P\) is real and equals
\[
                  -\sum_{i,\zeta\in E}\log|1-tw_i\bar\zeta|.
\]
Indeed conjugation pairs the terms in the absolutely convergent logarithm series.
For the primitive and full orbits used below, the actual resultant logarithm is
\[
                |E|m+\sum_{i,\zeta\in E}\log|1-w_i\bar\zeta| .        \tag{3}
\]
This follows from \(|\alpha_i-\zeta|=A_i|1-w_i\bar\zeta|\), including exterior roots, together with the leading coefficient of \(P\).

First take \(E_j\) to be the primitive \(j\)-th roots.
On the diagonal the same argument as (1) for the monic \(\Phi_j\) gives \(\|v_{E_j}\|^2\ll YL\).
For distinct orders \(j,l\) all pairwise distances between \(E_j,E_l\) are \(\gg Y^{-2}\), by the reduced-fraction spacing.
Replacing \(t\) by one in their scalar product costs \(O(Y^2(1-t)Y^2)=O(Y^{-2})\).
Its value at one equals \(-\log|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_j,\Phi_l)|\).
The [cyclotomic resultant formula](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#cyclotomic-clearing) gives zero unless one order is a prime-power multiple of the other.
In this dyadic interval that can only occur at the two endpoints when they are integers and their quotient is two, at a cost \(O(Y)\) to each affected row.
Hence the absolute row sums, and therefore the operator norm of this Gram matrix, are \(O(YL)\).

For the full root set \(E_j=\mu_j\) the character sum is \(j\,1_{j\mid k}\), giving the Gram entry
\[
              -\gcd(j,l)\log(1-t^{\operatorname{lcm}(j,l)}).
\]
Its absolute value is at most \(6L\,\gcd(j,l)\).
The row sum of these gcds is \(O(Y\,\tau(j))\), by expanding \(\gcd(j,l)=\sum_{b\mid j,\,b\mid l}\varphi(b)\) and counting multiples by \(2Y/b\).
The [elementary divisor bound](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#regularized-orbits) gives Gram operator norm \(O(Y^{9/8}L)\).

For either family the sum of squared scalar products with \(u_P\) is bounded by the Gram norm times (2).
Outside \(O(Y/L)\) orders, \(\varphi(j)\ge Y/L\) by the [bounded mean of \(j/\varphi(j)\)](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#exceptional-orders).
The number of orders where the scalar product exceeds \(Y^{-1/8}mY/L\) in absolute value is
\[
             O\big(dY^{-5/8}L^{10}\big)=o(Y)
\]
for both families, by the larger of the two Gram estimates.
Thus the smoothed error is \(o(|E_j|m)\) for both outside a uniform \(o(Y)\) orders.

<a id="actual"></a>
## Passing to the actual circle

The inequality \(|1-w_i\bar\zeta|\le t^{-1/2}|1-tw_i\bar\zeta|\) bounds the true sum in (3) from above by the smoothed sum plus \(O(d|E_j|(1-t))\).
The error is \(o(|E_j|m)\), since any positive-measure input has \(m\gg L^{-3}\) including the content-only case.
This proves both upper bounds without any geometric deletions.

For the two-sided primitive estimate use the [one-order deletion](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#composite) for each distinct nonreal conjugate pair of reflected roots.
It leaves \(|w_i-\zeta|\gg Y^{-2}\) at every remaining primitive node, including for the real roots, which require no deletion.
Multiplicities do not require new choices.
The radial perturbation then changes each logarithm by \(O((1-t)Y^2)\) in both directions.
Summing over \(d|E_j|\) terms gives \(o(|E_j|m)\) on the undeleted orders.

For the full lower estimate when \(d=o(Y)\), fix \(0<e<1/2\).
Cover \([eY,2Y]\) by a bounded number, depending on \(e\), of dyadic windows with lower ends at least \(eY/2\).
The primitive estimate just proved fails on only \(o_e(Y)\) orders in this union, because the number of geometric deletions in each is \(o(Y)\).
Each bad order at least \(eY\) divides at most \(2/e\) window indices.
For all other \(j\in[Y,2Y]\), every divisor \(b\ge eY\) contributes \(\log D_b=(1+o_e(1))\varphi(b)m\).
Small divisors have aggregate totient weight bounded by
\[
       \sum_{Y\le j\le2Y}\ \sum_{\substack{b\mid j\\b<eY}}\varphi(b)
                \le 2Y\sum_{b<eY}\varphi(b)/b\le 2eY^2.
\]
Outside \(O(\sqrt e\,Y)\) indices they thus contribute at most \(\sqrt e\,Y\) weight to \(\sum_{b\mid j}\varphi(b)=j\).
Every \(D_b\) is a nonzero integer in absolute value, and \(C_j=\prod_{b\mid j}D_b\).
Summing only the large divisors proves \(\log C_j/(jm)\ge1-\sqrt e-o_e(1)\) with the stated exceptions.
Letting \(e\downarrow0\) and using the density-one upper estimate gives the full convergence in density.

## The order size required for a degree-normalized lower bound

Uniformly for \(Y\ge d\) sufficiently large, a fixed positive proportion of the composite orders \(Y\le j\le2Y\) give
\[
                        \log C_j\ge\log D_j\ge cY m(P)
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\).
Indeed at most \(Y/2+o(Y)\) orders are lost in the two-sided primitive estimate, while \(\varphi(j)<c_0Y\) discards at most \(O(c_0Y)\) by the bounded first moment; take \(c_0\) sufficiently small.
There are \(o(Y)\) primes by the [elementary Chebyshev bound](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units).

Consequently, for any sequence \(Y\ge d,\ Y\to\infty\), the condition \(\liminf Y m(P)/d>0\) is equivalent to the existence of constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) such that at least \(c_1Y\) orders in each sufficiently late window satisfy \(\log D_j\ge c_2d\).
The same equivalence holds with \(C_j\), and with only composite orders counted.
The converse in either case follows from the corresponding density-one upper bound and \(|E_j|\le2Y\).
At \(Y=d\) this identifies the fixed-positive-proportion, degree-normalized return condition with a positive lower bound for \(m(P)\), without a sparsity assumption.
For nontorsion units of growing degree the [degree-dependent bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0147_v5.md#argument) already guarantees such degree-normalized growth on a positive proportion at \(Y=d(\log(2d)/\log\log(3d))^3\).
It does not give a degree-independent positive lower bound for \(m(P)\) in the original degree window.
