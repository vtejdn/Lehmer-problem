# An odd Chebyshev grid and its exact correction at the zero trace

<a id="statement"></a>
## An actual branch measure for every fixed Salem number

Fix a Salem number \(\sigma\) with trace \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}=2+u\) and monic trace minimal polynomial \(Q_0=(x-T)H_0\) of degree \(h+1\).
Write \(A=Q_0(0)\ne0\) and
\[
 \Lambda(A)=
 \begin{cases}
       0                 &4\mid A,\\
       1/|A|             &A\equiv2\pmod4,\\
       1/(2|A|)          &A\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
There are actual Pisot minimal polynomials of degrees \(d=2h+2+2k\), with odd \(k\to\infty\), constant \(q=d^2\) and exterior root \(\theta\), whose full returns equal the prescribed Salem minimal polynomial times \(z^{2k}+1\).
The genuine Salem inverse and branch residues have total masses tending to zero.
The full neutral branch measures tend weakly to
\[
                   \tfrac12\rho+\Lambda(A)\delta_0,\qquad
                   d\rho(x)=\frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{4-x^2}} .
\]
Here \(\delta_0\) is unit point mass at the central trace zero.
The excess satisfies \(\theta-q=u+O_T(1/k)\).
The construction and the stated branch limit hold for every fixed Salem input.

For a supplied sequence of Salem numbers tending to one, the choices can produce actual \(\theta-q\to0\) and total branch mass tending to \(1/2\) whenever \(\Lambda(Q_0(0))\to0\).
This includes sequences with \(4\mid Q_0(0)\) throughout and sequences with \(|Q_0(0)|\to\infty\).
The divisibility here is independent of the derivative condition in the [even Chebyshev grid](Yin_L_G0462_v179.md#half).
For example \(Q_0=x^2-x-4\) has \(4\mid Q_0(0)\) and \(Q'_0(0)\) odd.
Its roots \((1\pm\sqrt{17})/2\) lie respectively above \(2\) and inside \((-2,2)\).
The discriminant of \(z^2-Tz+1\) becomes negative at the second real embedding, so this quadratic is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(T)\) and supplies a degree-four Salem input.

## Integral interpolation and the smallest correction at zero

Use \(B=C_k\), \(C_k(2\cos t)=2\cos(kt)\), and \(V_j(2\cos t)=\sin((j+1)t)/\sin t\).
Take odd \(k\ge3\), \(s=(-1)^{(k-1)/2}\), \(B_1=B/x\), \(V=V_{k-1}\) and \(S=V_{k-2}/x\).
The two quotients by \(x\) are integral polynomials by parity.
The Chebyshev identities give
\[
 B_1(2)=1,\quad B_1(0)=ks,\quad B'=kV,\quad
 S(2)=(k-1)/2,\quad S(0)=-s(k-1)/2 .
\]
For an integral full inverse \(R\) and the full trace polynomial \(Q_0 B\), its branch residue at a grid root \(y\) is \((2-y)R(y)/(Q_0(y)B'(y))\), by [the branch formula](Yin_L_G0462_v165.md#branches).
All denominators are nonzero, since the old Salem roots and new torsion roots are disjoint.
Impose \(c_y=1/(2k)\) at every nonzero grid point.
This is equivalent to \(R(y)=Q_0(y)V(y)/(2(2-y))\) there.
The identity \(B=xV-2V_{k-2}\) gives \(V(y)=2S(y)\) at those points.
Hence one integral interpolant is
\[
                 U_1=\frac{Q_0S-Q_0(2)S(2)B_1}{2-x}.
\]
Its numerator vanishes at two.
Every other integral interpolant differs from it by an integral multiple of \(B_1\), by monic division and the simple nonzero nodes.
In particular the central value can be increased by any multiple of \(ks\) and only by such multiples.
Its initial value satisfies
\[
        \frac{U_1(0)}{ks}=\frac{A}{4k}-\frac A4-Q_0(2)\frac{k-1}{4}.
\]
Writing \(c_0=1/(2k)+\lambda\), since \(c_0=2R(0)/(Aks)\), the necessary and sufficient central congruence is
\[
             \frac{A\lambda}{2}
                \equiv-\frac A4-Q_0(2)\frac{k-1}{4}\pmod1 .         \tag{2}
\]
Here \(Q_0(2)\equiv A\bmod2\).
For even \(A\) the second term on the right is integral at every odd \(k\), and the least nonnegative correction is given by the first two cases of (1).
For odd \(A\), the two classes \(k\equiv1,3\bmod4\) give values differing by \(1/2\) modulo one.
After multiplication by the sign of \(A\) the two residues are \(1/4\) and \(3/4\), proving the last case of (1) on the minimizing progression.
For a fixed input all possible negative corrections in (2) are bounded away from zero, so every one eventually contradicts \(c_0>0\).
This proves minimality of (1) among odd grids subject to the prescribed equal residues at all nonzero nodes.

<a id="lattice"></a>
## Completing the central interpolation in the Salem coefficient lattice

Fix a minimizing progression modulo four and put \(\lambda=\Lambda(A)\).
Define the rational polynomial
\[
          D=\frac{2V-kB}{4(2-x)}+\frac{\lambda}{2}B_1 .
\]
The numerator of the first quotient vanishes at two.
The leading coefficient of \(D\) is \(\ell=k/4+\lambda/2\), and the grid residues \((2-y)D(y)/B'(y)\) are exactly the prescribed ones including the correction at zero.
By (2) we can change \(U_1\) by an integer multiple of \(B_1\) to interpolate \(Q_0D\) at zero as well as at every other grid root.
Let \(U\) be its remainder modulo \(B\), still integral, of degree less than \(k\).
Thus \(L=(U-Q_0D)/B\) is a rational polynomial of degree \(h\) with leading coefficient \(-\ell\).
The integral full expression is
\[
               R=B R_0+Q_0D,\qquad R_0\in L+\mathbb Z[x] .
\]

The fractional coefficient lattice in this last condition stabilizes along the chosen progression.
Indeed \(L\) is minus the polynomial part of \(Q_0D/B\) at infinity because \(U/B\) is proper.
We have
\[
       \frac DB=\frac{k}{4(x-2)}
                -\frac{V/B}{2(x-2)}+\frac{\lambda}{2x}.
\]
With \(x=z+z^{-1}\) at infinity and the branch \(\sqrt{x^2-4}\sim x\),
\[
 \frac{V}{B}
  =\frac1{\sqrt{x^2-4}}\frac{1-z^{-2k}}{1+z^{-2k}}
  =\frac1{\sqrt{x^2-4}}+O(x^{-2k-1}).
\]
Consequently for all sufficiently large \(k\) the polynomial part from the second term after multiplication by \(Q_0\) has stabilized exactly.
The third term is fixed, and increasing \(k\) by four in the first term changes the polynomial part by that of \(Q_0/(x-2)\), an integral polynomial.
This gives a fixed rational shift modulo the integer coefficient lattice, including the leading coefficient.

Require \(R_0\) to have leading coefficient \(1-q-\ell\), which belongs to the required coset \(-\ell+\mathbb Z\).
Then \(R\) has leading coefficient \(1-q\).
For any \(\varepsilon>0\) there are arbitrarily large \(k\) on the progression and such \(R_0\) for which all old residues \(b_i=-R_0(x_i)/H'_0(x_i)\) are positive and
\[
                       \sum_i b_i<\varepsilon,\qquad
                       \sum_i b_i/(2+x_i)<\varepsilon .
\]
Indeed the lower coefficients of \(R_0-(1-q-\ell)H_0\) form the translate by \((q+\ell-1)e\) of the fixed rational coset just proved, where \(e\) lists the lower coefficients of \(H_0\).
Together with \(1\) those coefficients are rationally independent since they are monic polynomials in \(T\) of successive degrees.
Writing \(k=k_0+4v\) gives \(q+\ell-1\) quadratic in \(v\) with leading coefficient \(64\).
The [quadratic equidistribution and invertible interpolation argument](Yin_L_G0462_v170.md#squares) therefore reaches the open residue box giving the two strict inequalities.

## Exact new masses and the square-constant excess

At every grid root \(b_y=c_y(1+u/(2-y))\).
Symmetry and logarithmic differentiation give \(\sum_y(2\pm y)^{-1}=k^2/2\) for both signs, since \(C_k'(2)=k^2\) and \(C_k(2)=2\).
The resulting grid masses and the grid part of the stability condition are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
    \sum_y c_y&=\tfrac12+\lambda,\\
    \sum_y b_y&=\tfrac12+\tfrac{uk}{4}+\lambda(1+u/2),\\
    \sum_y \frac{b_y}{2+y}
               &=\tfrac{k}{4}+\tfrac{uk}{8}+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1+u/2).
 \end{aligned}
\]
At an old trace the factors \(B(x_i)\) cancel in \(-R(x_i)/(H_0B)'(x_i)\), leaving the old residues just chosen.
Every branch residue is positive and smaller than the corresponding inverse residue.
For sufficiently large \(k\) the full stability sum is less than \(q+T/2\), since its grid part grows linearly in \(k\).
The [strict inverse and exact excess formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v369.md#simple-return) give the stated actual irreducible Pisot polynomial and the estimate
\[
    0<\theta-q-u
       \le \frac{u}{q-1}
          +\frac{1/2+uk/4+\lambda(1+u/2)+\varepsilon}{q-2}.
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon\to0\) and midpoint sums over the roots of \(C_k\) gives the claimed weak limit.
For varying Salem inputs tending to one, each index \(k\) can be selected after the input and the tolerance, with no uniform upper cutoff.
When the central corrections do not tend to zero, this construction leaves the corresponding extra branch mass.
Possibility of removing it by a different grid or different noncentral residues remains open.
