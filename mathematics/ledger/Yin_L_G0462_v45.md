# An integral interpolation obstruction on the leading-two Pisot branch

<a id="order"></a>
## An additive order measured only at the torsion factors

Use the [positive-constant trace representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) on the fixed branch \(j=2\).
Along a hypothetical sequence with \(\delta=\theta-q\to0\), one eventually has \(C=UA\), where \(U\) is the monic torsion part and \(A\) is a single primitive irreducible factor of leading coefficient two.
All these factors have simple zeros in \((-2,2)\).
For each monic divisor \(V\mid U\) put \(P=AV,\ W=U/V\) and define
\[
 Q_P(x)=\frac{P(x)-P(2)}{x-2},\qquad
 b(P,W)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rm add}\big([Q_P]\text{ in }\mathbb Z[x]/(P,W)\big).
\]
The zero class has order one, including when \(W=1\).
Then necessarily
\[
            \min_{V\mid U}|A(2)V(2)|\,b(AV,U/V)\ \longrightarrow\ \infty .
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(A(2)\) remains bounded, both \(b(A,U)\) and \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,A)|\) must tend to infinity.
This uses the size of a multiplier on a finite set of trace roots even when its supremum on \([-2,2]\) is large.
The main researcher proposed testing the single nonmonic factor by its arithmetic, and the proof contributor obtained (1) and the examples below.

For monic \(W\ne1\) the integral module \(\mathbb Z[x]/(W)\) is free of rank \(\deg W\).
Multiplication by \(P\) on it has nonzero determinant \(\operatorname{Res}(W,P)\), since \(P\) and \(W\) have disjoint zeros.
Its cokernel is the finite additive group \(\mathbb Z[x]/(P,W)\).
In particular \(b(P,W)\) divides \(|\operatorname{Res}(W,P)|\).
Equivalently it is the least common denominator of the coefficients of the unique polynomial of degree less than \(\deg W\) congruent to \(Q_P/P\) modulo \(W\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed divisibility of \(b Q_P\) by \(P\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]/(W)\) is precisely integrality of this representative multiplied by \(b\).

<a id="interpolation"></a>
## A multiplier constant on the retained trace roots

Putting \(b=b(P,W)\), choose \(J\in\mathbb Z[x]\) with \(PJ\equiv-bQ_P\bmod W\).
For \(W=1\) take \(J=0\).
Then \(G=P(b+(x-2)J)\) is an integral multiple of \(A\) and
\[
            G(x)-bP(2)=(x-2)(bQ_P+PJ)\in(x-2)W\mathbb Z[x].
\]
Thus \(G\) takes the same nonzero integer value \(e=bP(2)\) at 2 and at every zero of \(W\), and vanishes on the zeros of \(AV\).
The order \(b\) is the least positive possible coefficient of \(P(2)\) in this interpolation when \(G/P\) has the form \(b+(x-2)J\).
In fact any such interpolation forces \(W\mid bQ_P+PJ\), since \(W\) and \(x-2\) are relatively prime over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(W\) is monic.

Let \(\lambda\) be the positive measure on the zeros of \(UA\) in the [stripped trace identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#strip).
There \(s=\int (2-y)^{-1}\,d\lambda(y)\le4+2/(q-1)\).
For this \(G\) the new measure is supported on the retained torsion traces and its endpoint integral is
\[
 s'=\int\frac{G(y)^2}{2(2-y)}\,d\lambda(y)
    =\frac{e^2}{2}\sum_{W(y)=0}\frac{\lambda(\{y\})}{2-y}
    \le\frac{e^2}{2}\,s .
\]
The exact division in that identity gives \(s'\in\mathbb Z+e^2/(q-1)\) because the content is one.
If (1) failed, one could choose divisors along a subsequence with nonzero bounded \(|e|\).
We have \(q\to\infty\), and the [torsion lifting formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#atom) then gives positive measures with integer Fourier coefficients, bounded total mass \(\lceil2s'\rceil\), and mass \(1-2e^2/(q-1)\) at 1.
This approaches one strictly from below in contradiction with [one-sided isolation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v6.md#isolation).

The ordinary endpoint relation is \(A(2)U(2)=-(q-1)B(2)\), with \(B(2)\) a nonzero integer.
For bounded \(A(2)\) it forces \(U(2)\) to grow, but by itself gives no growth for \(b(A,U)\), as the following example shows.

<a id="family"></a>
## Irreducible leading-two factors with endpoint one

For any \(m\ge1\), put \(N=2m+1\) and
\[
    S_N(w)=\frac{2w^N+w^{N-1}-w-2}{w-1},\qquad
                 z^m A_m(z+z^{-1})=S_N(-z).
\]
The coefficients of \(S_N\) are two at both endpoints and three in every interior position.
It is primitive and reciprocal, and the symmetric Laurent polynomial on the right after division by \(z^m\) gives \(A_m\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(m\) and leading coefficient two.
The equation for the numerator is \(w^{N-1}(2w+1)/(w+2)=1\).
On the circle its left side winds \(N\) times with strictly positive angular derivative, since \((2w+1)/(w+2)\) is the disk factor at \(-1/2\).
Consequently it has \(N\) distinct unit roots including \(1\).

Only \(1\) can be a root of unity among these \(N\) points.
Indeed for a torsion root \(w\) write \(w^{N-1}=T(w)=(w+2)/(2w+1)\).
At odd order the squaring automorphism gives \(T(w^2)=T(w)^2\), whose numerator after clearing denominators is \(2(w-1)^3(w+1)\).
At order twice an odd integer \(h\), the automorphism of exponent \(h+2\) sends \(w\) to \(-w^2\).
Since \(N-1\) is even this forces \(T(-w^2)=T(w)^2\).
After clearing denominators the numerator up to sign is \(2(w^4-2w^3-7w^2-2w+1)\).
It cannot vanish on the circle, since division by \(w^2\) would give \(x^2-2x-9=0\) with \(x=w+w^{-1}\in[-2,2]\).
At order \(h\) divisible by four, the automorphism of exponent \(1+h/2\) gives \(T(-w)=T(w)\), contrary to injectivity of \(T\).
Thus \(S_N\) has only nontorsion unit-circle roots.
Its primitivity and leading coefficient two allow only one nonmonic irreducible factor, of multiplicity one.
A nonconstant monic factor would be cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Hence \(S_N\) and its sign substitution are irreducible, and a factorization of \(A_m\) would factor its reciprocal lift.
All roots of \(A_m\) are therefore strictly inside \((-2,2)\).

Since \(S_N(-1)=1\) and \(S_N(1)=3N-2\), we have
\[
                A_m(2)=1,\qquad A_m(-2)=(-1)^m(3N-2).
\]
For an odd prime \(p\) take \(m=p^k\), \(k\ge1\), and let \(U\) contain exactly one trace \(x=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\) from each inverse pair of nontrivial \(m\)-th roots as its simple roots.
This gives a monic integral \(U\), with \(z^{(m-1)/2}U(z+z^{-1})=(z^m-1)/(z-1)\) and \(U(2)=m\).
At each such \(\zeta\), \(w=-\zeta\ne1\) satisfies \(w^{N-1}=1\), whence \(S_N(w)=1\).
Also \(\zeta^m=1\), so \(A_m(x)=1\) there.
Thus \(A_m\equiv1\bmod U\), giving \(\operatorname{Res}(U,A_m)=1\) and \(b(A_m,U)=1\).
With \(q=m+1\), the endpoint divisibility is exact with \(B(2)=-1\), the nonmonic degree is unbounded, and \(\|A_m\|_{[-2,2]}\) grows.
Nevertheless these data cannot occur along a near-integer Pisot sequence, by (1).
They also admit the bounded multiplier \((x+2)A_m\), since \(|z+1|^2=2+x\) on the circle and the numerator defining \(S_N(-z)\) has modulus at most six.
Thus this family separates the arithmetic condition from endpoint divisibility and growth of the original sup norm, while satisfying the earlier criterion for exclusion by a bounded multiple.
No trace denominator \(B\) with all the Pisot properties is assumed in the example.

<a id="odd-primes"></a>
## Integrality at all odd primes permits increasing identity atoms

Take instead \(m=2^k\), \(k\ge1\), and let \(\rho_k\) be the uniform probability on the \(2m\) roots of \(S_N(-z)\).
All these roots are units over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\), because both the leading and constant coefficients of their polynomial are two.
The sums of their positive and negative integral powers are rational and integral over \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\), hence belong to that ring.
The normalized moments also belong to \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) since \(2m\) is a power of two.
The positive conjugation-symmetric measures
\[
                   (1-2^{-k})\delta_1+2^{-k}\rho_k
\]
have mass one and are integral in every Fourier coefficient at every odd prime.
Their mass at 1 increases strictly to one while their nontorsion support lies on one irreducible leading-two orbit of unbounded degree with \(A_m(2)=1\).
Thus these local moment conditions alone cannot give the required isolation at the identity.
