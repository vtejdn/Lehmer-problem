# A long prefix with integer value and derivative at zero

For the exceptional parent \(F_2=(1+z^2)^3\), consider real central perturbations \(H\) in the [odd-grid construction](Yin_L_G0464_v20.md#real-degree).
For every sufficiently large odd \(p=2n+1\) there is an \(H\) with
\[
 n-\deg H\ge c p^{2/5},\qquad H(0)=0,\qquad H'(0)=-B_n(0)\in\{-1,1\}
 \tag{1}
\]
for an absolute \(c>0\), such that precisely the last residue is negative and the strict endpoint holds.
The remaining coefficients can be chosen rational.
On 2026-11-16 a construction contributor for Yin's [unrestricted Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this example to test what the two integer Taylor coefficients alone can imply.
For the prescribed values \(H(0)=0,\ |H'(0)|=1\), the [two inverse moment estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0891_v2.md#integrality) already give \(n-\deg H=O(p^{2/5})\) before the integer root-count argument, so (1) attains the exponent for this specific condition.
If both coefficients vanish, the [rational quartic](Yin_L_G0457_v7.md#rational-witness) instead attains \(n-\deg H=n-4\) with the same signs and endpoint.
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v53.md#rational-prefix-review) checks the construction and the distinction between these two coefficient conditions.

## A kernel for the neutral residues

Use \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\), \(b_j=2\cos\theta_j\), \(r=p-4\), \(m=(r-1)/2\) and \(1\le j\le n\).
Here \(B_n(2\cos\theta)=\sin(p\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2)\) and the neutral trace is \(Q_0=t^3B_m\).
The [positive grid prefactor](Yin_L_G0457_v4.md#residue-formula) times the phase \(\sqrt{2+b_j}\,b_j^4\) gives the neutral residues
\[
            \mu_j=\frac{Q_0(b_j)}{B_n'(b_j)}
                 =\frac{(4-b_j^2)b_j^4}{p}>0 .
 \tag{2}
\]
Put \(d=\lfloor cp^{2/5}\rfloor\), where \(c\in(0,1)\) will be chosen small.
For \(2d+6<p\), the discrete squared norm \(\|f\|_\mu^2=\sum_j\mu_j f(b_j)^2\) on real polynomials of degree at most \(d\) equals
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\pi
          (4-t^2)t^4 f(t)^2\,d\theta,\qquad t=2\cos\theta.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the integrand is an even trigonometric polynomial of degree at most \(2d+6\).
Every nonconstant Fourier mode has mean zero on the full \(p\)-point grid and the zero-angle term vanishes.
Reflection gives (3) on the half grid.

There is an absolute \(A\) such that for all \(d\ge1\)
\[
                \|f\|_{[-2,2]}^2\le A d^5\|f\|_\mu^2
                \qquad(2d+6<p).
 \tag{4}
\]
To prove this, put \(S=\sup_\theta|f(2\cos\theta)|\).
The [trigonometric derivative estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0891_v1.md#localization) bounds the angular derivative by \(edS\).
A maximizing point in \([0,\pi]\) thus lies at the end of an interval inside \([0,\pi]\) of length \(\delta=1/(4ed)\) on which \(|f|\ge3S/4\).
Discard the subintervals of distance less than \(\delta/16\) from \(0,\pi/2,\pi\), losing at most \(3\delta/8\) of this length.
On the remainder \(|\sin\theta|\) and \(|\cos\theta|\) are each at least \(2(\delta/16)/\pi\), using the lower chord bound for sine.
At least one of them is at least \(1/\sqrt2\).
The weight \(64\sin^2\theta\cos^4\theta\) is therefore at least a constant times \(\delta^4\) throughout the remainder, including when the maximizing point is an endpoint or \(\pi/2\).
Equation (3) now gives (4).

Choose an orthonormal basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_d\) for this inner product and define
\[
 K(x,t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^d e_\ell(x)e_\ell(t).
\]
Then \(\sum_j\mu_j K(x,b_j)f(b_j)=f(x)\) for every \(f\) in this polynomial space.
Applying (4) to \(K(x,\cdot)\), whose squared norm is \(K(x,x)\), gives \(K(x,x)\le Ad^5\).
Cauchy–Schwarz in the basis sum yields \(|K(x,t)|\le Ad^5\) for all \(x,t\in[-2,2]\).

## Choosing the two central masses

Let \(x_+,x_-\) be the two adjacent grid traces straddling zero, with \(x_+>0>x_-\), and let \(x_*=b_n\).
Write \(j_i\) for the corresponding index when \(i=+,-,*\).
With real weights \(w_i\) form
\[
  h_j=\sum_{i=+,-,*} w_i\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{j=j_i\}}-\mu_j K(x_i,b_j)\bigr).
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\sum_j h_j f(b_j)=0\) for every \(f\) of degree at most \(d\).
Fix \(w_*=-2\mu_n=O(p^{-3})\), since \(b_n=-2\cos(\pi/p)\).
The other weights will be positive and \(O(p^{-2})\).

For \(k=1,2\) put
\[
 A_{ki}=x_i^{-k}-\sum_j\mu_j K(x_i,b_j)b_j^{-k}.
\]
The subtracted term has absolute value at most \(C d^{5/2}\), uniformly in \(i\) and \(p\).
Indeed \(\sum_j\mu_jK(x_i,b_j)^2=K(x_i,x_i)\le Ad^5\) and
\(\sum_j\mu_j b_j^{-2k}=p^{-1}\sum_j(4-b_j^2)b_j^{4-2k}\) is bounded.
Impose the exact equations
\[
 \begin{split}
  A_{1+}w_++A_{1-}w_-&=-A_{1*}w_*,\\
  A_{2+}w_++A_{2-}w_-&=1-A_{2*}w_*.
 \end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
By (5) they give \(\sum_j h_j/b_j=0\) and \(\sum_j h_j/b_j^2=1\).

The quantities \(p|x_+|,p|x_-|\) tend to \(\pi,3\pi\) in some order on each of the two odd residue classes modulo four, by taking the nearest indices on the two sides of \(p/4\).
Since \(d^{5/2}\le c^{5/2}p\), there are absolute constants bounding \(A_{1+}/p\) above and away from zero, and the same holds for \(-A_{1-}/p\), provided \(c\) is sufficiently small.
Likewise both \(A_{2\pm}/p^2\) are positive, bounded above and bounded away from zero for large \(p\).
Also \(|A_{k*}|=O(p)\) since \(x_*\to-2\).
Put \(w_\pm=u_\pm/p^2\) and multiply the first equation in (6) by \(p\).
The resulting matrix has first row \((a,-b)\) and second row \((g,\ell)\) with \(a,b,g,\ell\) between fixed positive constants, while its right side is \((O(p^{-1}),1+O(p^{-2}))\).
Its determinant \(a\ell+bg>0\) and the two solution numerators are \(b+O(p^{-1})\), \(a+O(p^{-1})\).
Thus both \(u_\pm\) are positive and bounded above independently of \(p\) and of all sufficiently small \(c\).

## The trace, signs and endpoint

Set
\[
 H(t)=B_n(t)\sum_j\frac{h_j}{t-b_j},\qquad Q=Q_0+H .
\]
The simple roots of \(B_n\) cancel the denominators.
The vanishing moments through degree \(d\) in (5) make \(H/B_n=O(t^{-d-2})\) at infinity, so \(\deg H\le n-d-2\).
At zero, (6) gives \((H/B_n)(0)=0\) and \((H/B_n)'(0)=-1\).
Since \(B_n(0)=\sin(p\pi/4)/\sin(\pi/4)=\pm1\), these prove (1).

Put \(D_j=\sum_iw_iK(x_i,b_j)\).
The uniform kernel and weight bounds give \(\sup_j|D_j|\le C c^5\).
Choose \(c\) so small that this bound is below \(1/2\).
The perturbed residues are \(\mu_j+h_j=\mu_j(1-D_j)+\sum_{i:j_i=j}w_i\).
At \(j<n\) they are positive, while at \(n\) the extra weight \(-2\mu_n\) makes the residue negative.

The neutral endpoint ratio is \(Q_0(-2)/B_n(-2)=-8\).
The change there is \(H(-2)/B_n(-2)=-\sum_j h_j/(2+b_j)\).
By (2),
\[
             \sum_j\frac{\mu_j}{2+b_j}
              =\frac1p\sum_j(2-b_j)b_j^4=O(1).
\]
The density terms \(\mu_j D_j\) thus change the endpoint by \(O(c^5)\).
The two central atoms contribute \(O(p^{-2})\), and the last atom contributes \(O(p^{-1})\) by the same cancellation of \(2+b_n\) in (2).
Decreasing \(c\) once more preserves the strict negative endpoint for all sufficiently large \(p\).
Finally, fix the constant and linear coefficients of \(H\) and approximate its remaining coefficients through degree \(n-d-2\) by rationals.
The finitely many strict inequalities persist under sufficiently small changes at each \(p\).
This yields the rational version of (1), leaving integrality of the remaining coefficients as a separate arithmetic condition.
