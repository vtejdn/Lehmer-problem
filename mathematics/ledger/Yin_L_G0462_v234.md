# Measuring each four-class cofactor from its seed coefficients

<a id="height"></a>
## A coefficient estimate before changing either row

Use the [four-class representatives](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows) \(P_0(x^n)\), with \(e=\deg P_0,\ h=m(P_0),\ B=4e\) and \(p\) in the fourth-logarithmic window.
For either original short witness let \(a,b\) be its two integral seed rows, \(G\) its seed diagonal and \(F\) its lift.
Write \(J=\log\max(\|a\|_1,\|b\|_1)\) and \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Then
\[
       |m(G)-J|\le B\log2+2J/p,\qquad
       m(R_F)=J-2h+O(e+J/p+ph/n),                                      \tag{1}
\]
where \(R_F\) is the actual noncyclotomic cofactor after removing the two prescribed copies.
In particular each individual \(m(R_F)=J+o(ph)\) uniformly over the window on these representatives.
The same estimate holds with \(J\) replaced by the logarithm of the largest absolute coefficient of the two seed rows, since the difference is at most \(\log(B+1)\).

Indeed \(I=\int\log\max(|a|,|b|)\) lies between \(J-B\log2\) and \(J\).
The lower bound follows from \(\|f\|_1\le2^{\deg f}\exp(m(f))\) applied to the row of larger one-norm, by multiplying the bounds for its linear factors.
The [reflection identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) gives \(0<L=2I-m(H)\le2J\), since the nonzero integral reflected polynomial \(H=Y^B(aa^\iota-bb^\iota)\) has \(m(H)\ge0\).
Its circle estimate gives \(|m(G)-I|\le L/p\).
Primitivity and the [two-sided lift estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v232.md#measure) prove (1), using \(J=O(ph)\) and \(e+\log(2npB)=o(ph)\) on the short rows.
Thus in the [shrinking equivalence](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) one can test the two largest coefficient magnitudes of the two witnesses, one maximum per witness, on the scale of their logarithms divided by \(p\).
The four-class sufficiency and converse are the [seed construction and lower bound](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed).

## A uniform circle estimate for two nearby polynomials

For complex polynomials \(f,g\) of degrees at most \(D\ge1\), set \(S=\sup_{\mathbb T}|f|>0\) and \(0<\delta=\sup_{\mathbb T}|f-g|/S\le1/4\).
There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that
\[
                  |m(f)-m(g)|\le C D^2\delta^{1/(4D)}.                 \tag{2}
\]
Here the measure of a nonzero complex polynomial is the circle mean of its logarithmic modulus.
To verify (2), normalize by \(S\), and put \(E=\{|f|\le S\sqrt\delta\}\) before normalization.
For a complex root \(z\), write \(d_z(T)=|T-z|/\max(1,|z|)\).
Uniformly in \(z\), the normalized arc measure of \(\{d_z<t\}\) is \(O(t)\) for \(0<t\le1\).
Indeed for \(t<1/4\), a nonempty set requires \(|z|\) bounded above and below by absolute positive constants, and its points have chord distance \(O(t)\) from the radial projection of \(z\).
For larger \(t\) the probability bound suffices.
For \(k=\deg f>0\) one has \(|f|=\exp(m(f))\prod d_z\), and \(S\le2^D\exp(m(f))\).
At some root \(d_z\le2\delta^{1/(2D)}\) on \(E\), as also follows using \(S\le2^k\exp(m(f))\) and \(k\le D\).
Constant \(f\) gives empty \(E\).
Thus \(q=\mu(E)\le\min(1,CD\delta^{1/(2D)})\).

For either polynomial \(v=f,g\) with its own supremum \(S_v\), the integral of \(-\log(|v|/S_v)\) on \(E\) is at most \(C Dq\log(2/q)\), with value zero at \(q=0\).
Indeed \(\log S_v-m(v)\le D\log2\), and integrating the distribution of \((-\log d_z)_+\) on \(E\) gives at most \(\int_0^\infty\min(q,Ce^{-s})\,ds\) per root.
The ratio \(S_g/S\) is between \(1-\delta\) and \(1+\delta\).
On the complement of \(E\) the logarithms of the two absolute values differ by at most \(2\sqrt\delta\).
Combining these estimates and \(q\log(2/q)\ll\sqrt q\) proves (2).
Identical polynomials give zero difference as well.

## Preserving the jets while making the circle term exponentially small

Start with either of the two primitive short witnesses, let \(Q_0\) be the monic minimum of \(\alpha^p\), and put \(C(Y)=Y^e Q_0(Y)^2\).
Replace \(a\) by \(a_t=a+tC\) and leave \(b\) fixed.
One can choose a positive integer \(t\) with \(\log t=O(ph)\) so that all four support classes, both endpoints of \(a\), primitivity, both coprimality conditions and the original values and first derivatives persist.
In particular both adjusted rows retain their original independent jets, even before taking norms.

Let \(g\) be the positive content of \(b\).
Take \(t\) among multiples of \(g\) starting at \(4(\|a\|_1+\|b\|_1)\) or larger, using the least such multiple as the first choice.
No prime dividing \(g\) can divide every coefficient of \(a_t\), since \(a_t\equiv a\bmod g\) and the initial combined coefficient list is primitive.
The double zero of \(C\) at every root of \(Q_0\) keeps the seed jets fixed.
Its degrees range from \(e\) to \(3e\), and its reciprocal about \(4e\) equals itself.
Consequently the lifted difference \(A-A^*\) remains fixed, nonzero and of degree at most \(4d\), where \(d=ne\).
At each of its nonzero roots, the new evaluation of \(A\) is affine in \(t\) and nonzero at zero by the initial coprimality.
The same holds for \(a_t\) at all nonzero roots of \(Y^{B-1}b(1/Y)\).
The endpoints of \(a_t\) stay nonzero.
At most \(4d+B\) scalar values are forbidden, so one of the first \(4d+B+1\) choices works.
Since \(g\le\|b\|_1\) and \(\log\|Q_0\|_1\le ph+e\log2\), both \(\log t\) and the logarithm of the new coefficient norm are \(O(ph)\).
The bounds for the actual rows and remainders in the [four-class construction](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#coprime) therefore still apply.

For the adjusted witnesses put \(H_t=Y^B(a_ta_t^\iota-bb^\iota)=Q_0^2 K_t\), let \(L_t\) be the positive circle term, and write \(R_{F_t}\) for the lifted cofactor.
For absolute constants \(c,C'>0\) let \(\eta=C'e^2\exp(-cph/e)\).
Uniformly on sufficiently large representatives,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  0<L_t&\le\eta,\\
  m(K_t)&=2\log t+2ph+O(\eta),\\
  m(R_{F_t})&=\log t+(2p-2)h+O(\eta+ph/n).
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(M=\sup_{\mathbb T}|C|\ge\exp(2ph)\).
For every unit complex scalar \(w\), the polynomial \(a_t+wb\) differs from \(tC\) by at most \(t/4\) on the circle.
Equation (2) for \(D=B\) gives \(m(a_t+wb)=\log t+2ph+O(\eta)\) uniformly in \(w\).
Integration over \(w\) gives \(I_t=\log t+2ph+O(\eta)\) by the linear-factor Jensen formula.
The iterated logarithms are integrable since their positive parts are bounded and their inner integrals have the displayed uniform lower bound.
On the circle the difference between \(H_t\) and \(H_0'=t^2Y^B CC^\iota\) has modulus bounded by \(2tM\|a\|_1+\|a\|_1^2+\|b\|_1^2\).
Its ratio to \(\sup|H_0'|=t^2M^2\) is at most \(1/M\).
Equation (2) for \(D=2B\) gives \(m(H_t)=2\log t+4ph+O(\eta)\).
Now use \(L_t=2I_t-m(H_t)\), \(m(K_t)=m(H_t)-2ph\) and the [seed cofactor and lift formulas](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#four).
The representative degree bound \(e\le\log(3d)\) and [Voutier's Corollary 2 at seed degree \(e\)](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) give \(ph/e\gg(\log(3d))^3/(\log(3e))^3\) for bounded positive seed measure, so \(\eta\to0\) even when the seed varies.

The angular contribution can therefore be made negligible in absolute size on both genuine witnesses while keeping the original derivative data.
The term \(2\log t\) in \(m(K_t)\) records the chosen perturbation and supplies no lower bound for the least attainable coefficient size.
For an absolute gap, (1) leaves the arithmetic question of how small the two primitive coefficient lists solving the simultaneous seed equations can be when the seed varies.
