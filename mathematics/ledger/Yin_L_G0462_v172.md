# Cyclotomic factors and square-root towers for the primitive cubic directions

<a id="cyclotomic"></a>
## The complete cyclotomic part

Take \(g(T)=T^3-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^2-T^3\), \(f=-g/B\), and \(A_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^b B(X^a)\) for coprime positive integers \(a,b\).
The cyclotomic part is the product of exactly the \(\Phi_M\) specified in the table, each once.

| \(M\) | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| \(2\) | \(a,b\) have opposite parity |
| \(6\) | \(\gcd(a,6)=2\) and \(b\equiv3+2a\pmod6\) |
| \(8\) | \(a\) odd and \(b\equiv a\pmod8\) |
| \(10\) | \(\gcd(a,10)=2\) and \(b\equiv5+3a\pmod{10}\) |
| \(12\) | \(\gcd(a,12)=1\) and \(b\equiv2a\pmod{12}\) |
| \(18\) | \(\gcd(a,18)=1\) and \(b\equiv4a\pmod{18}\) |
| \(30\) | \(\gcd(a,30)=1\) and \(b\equiv9a\pmod{30}\) |

For \(a,b\) both odd only orders \(8,30\) can occur, giving total degree at most \(12\).
For \(a\) even only \(2,6,10\) occur, giving at most \(7\), and for \(b\) even only \(2,12,18\) occur, giving at most \(11\).
The total degree bound \(12\) is attained at \((a,b)=(1,9)\).

For the proof let \(\xi\) be a primitive \(M\)-th root with \(t=\xi^a\) and \(y=\xi^b=f(t)\).
Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), the two coordinates generate the same cyclic group as \(\xi\).
The cubic \(B\) has no root on the circle.
For \(\eta,\delta\in\{1,-1\}\) put
\[
 H_{\eta,\delta}(T)=-g(\eta T^2)B(T)^2-\delta\,g(T)^2B(\eta T^2).
\]
The following factorizations follow by expansion, with \(L=T^{10}+T^9-T^7-T^6-T^5-T^4-T^3+T+1\).
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_{+,+}&=-2TL,& H_{-,-}&=2\Phi_{12}\Phi_{30},\\
 H_{+,-}&=-2(T-1)^3(T+1)\Phi_3^2\Phi_5,&
 H_{-,+}&=2T(T-1)(T+1)^3\Phi_{18}.
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
For \(M\) odd the squaring automorphism gives \(H_{+,+}(t)=0\), and the [odd-order calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v158.md#cyclotomic) excludes every odd-order root of \(L\), including \(1\).
For \(M=2u\) with \(u\) odd, the automorphism \(\xi\mapsto-\xi^2\) gives \(H_{(-1)^a,(-1)^b}(t)=0\).
When \(a,b\) are odd, \(t\) has order twice an odd number, leaving order \(30\).
When \(a\) is even its order is odd, leaving \(1,3,5\), and when \(b\) is even its order is twice an odd number, leaving \(2,18\).
For \(4\mid M\), use the automorphism \(\xi\mapsto-\xi\), which gives \(f((-1)^a t)=(-1)^b f(t)\).
Even \(a\) is impossible since \(f(t)\ne0\).
For odd \(a\), \(4\mid\operatorname{ord}t\), and the cleared numerators of \(f(-t)+f(t)\) and \(f(-t)-f(t)\) are respectively \(-2(t-1)(t+1)\Phi_8(t)\) and \(-2t\Phi_{12}(t)\).
This leaves order \(8\) for odd \(b\) and order \(12\) for even \(b\).

For the surviving orders \(j\), reduction modulo \(\Phi_j(t)\) gives these values of \(y\).
Each entry \(y\) can be verified by reducing \(g(t)+y B(t)\) to zero.

| \(j\) | \(1\) | \(2\) | \(3\) | \(5\) | \(8\) | \(12\) | \(18\) | \(30\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(f(t)\) | \(-1\) | \(1\) | \(-t^2\) | \(-t^3\) | \(t\) | \(t^2\) | \(t^4\) | \(t^9\) |

Recovering \(M=\operatorname{lcm}(j,\operatorname{ord}y)\) and testing \(\xi^a=t,\ \xi^b=y\) gives exactly the congruences in the first table, including their sufficiency.
At a repeated root we must have \(t f'(t)/f(t)=b/a\) by differentiating the original equation.
At orders \(1,2,3\) the left side is respectively \(-7,1,2\).
These values are impossible respectively by positivity, by the opposite parity required at order \(2\) for \(t=-1\), and by the required even \(a\) and odd \(b\) at order \(3\).
For \(j=5,8,12,18,30\), the remainder of \(t(g'B-gB')/(gB)\) modulo \(\Phi_j\), using inversion of the denominator, is respectively
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 5t^3+5t^2+8,\qquad 4t^3-4t+5,\qquad 2t^3-4t+2,\\
 6t^5-3t^4-3t^2-3t+4,\qquad
 3t^6+t^5-2t^4-t^3-2t^2-4t+2 .
 \end{gathered}
\]
Each is nonconstant of degree less than \(\varphi(j)\), hence cannot equal the rational number \(b/a\).
This proves simplicity throughout the table.

## Both square classes in the factor field

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion root, \(P\) its monic minimal polynomial, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\).
If either \(\alpha\) or \(-\alpha\) is a square in \(K\), then
\[
                 d\le10,\qquad |b-3a|<219,\qquad a<438,\qquad b<1533.   \tag{2}
\]
Outside these necessary conditions every \(P(X^{2^k})\) is irreducible, of exactly the degree \(2^k d\) and the same logarithmic Mahler measure as \(P\).
Indeed the [norm induction](Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#all-towers) uses solely the nonsquareness of these two signs, and hence applies at every root and every step.

Write \(t=\alpha^a\), \(y=\alpha^b=f(t)\).
The identity \(-Tg+(1-T)B=1\) gives that \(B(t)\) is a unit, and \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) recovers \(\alpha\) from \(t,f(t)\) by an integral combination of powers.
Use a bar for reduction modulo two.
At a multiple root \(x\) of \(\overline A_{a,b}\), its coordinate \(t=x^a\) must satisfy \(a t f'(t)=b f(t)\).
The coordinates and both cubic values are nonzero there.

If \(a\) is even the reduced derivative condition is impossible.
When \(a,b\) are odd, its cleared polynomial is \(T(g'B-gB')-gB\equiv(T+1)^6\bmod2\), giving \(t=f(t)=1\), hence \(x=1\).
For any \(P\) with \(\overline P(1)=0\), the endpoint values \(A_{a,b}(1)=A_{a,b}(-1)=-2\) imply \(|P(1)|=|P(-1)|=2\).
For each sign \(\epsilon\), the ideal generated by \(1-\epsilon\alpha\) is prime of norm two.
If \(\epsilon\alpha=u^2\), then the unit \(u\) reduces to \(1\) there, so \(1-u\) and \(1+u\) both belong to the prime, contradicting valuation one of their product.

When \(a\) is odd and \(b\) even, \(g'B-gB'\equiv(T^2+T+1)^2\bmod2\).
At a multiple root \(t\) thus has order three, and \(f(t)=t^2\) by the torsion calculation reduced modulo two.
Recovering \(x\) from \(t,f(t)\) shows that \(x\) has order three too and \(b\equiv2a\bmod3\), with \(3\nmid a\).
Let \(\omega\) be a primitive complex cube root of unity.
At the unique dyadic prime \((2)\) of \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\), both
\[
             A_{a,b}(\omega)=-2\omega^a,\qquad
             A_{a,b}(-\omega)=2(\omega^{2a}-1)
\]
have valuation one under these congruences.
If \(\overline P\) has a root of order three, choose a conjugate of \(\alpha\) and a prime of \(K(\omega)\) at which \(\alpha\) reduces to \(\omega\), interchanging \(\omega,\omega^2\) if needed.
The minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\) is a monic divisor of \(-A_{a,b}\) over \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\).
Consequently the norm to \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\) of each \(\omega-\epsilon\alpha\) has valuation at most one at \((2)\), for both signs.
If \(\epsilon\alpha=u^2\), the reduction of \(u\) at the chosen prime must be \(\omega^2\), the unique square root of \(\omega\) in the residue field.
Thus \(\omega-\epsilon\alpha=(\omega^2-u)(\omega^2+u)\) has valuation at least two there, contradicting the norm bound.

## The smooth reduction and an explicit finite range

The preceding cases exclude both square classes for any factor meeting a possible multiple residue, so it remains to treat \(\overline P\) squarefree.
The discriminant is odd, hence \(\mathcal O_K/4\mathcal O_K=(\mathbb Z/4)[X]/(P)\).
It admits an endomorphism \(\sigma\) lifting the squaring map modulo two by [the polynomial lifting construction](Yin_L_G0462_v164.md#frobenius).
If \(\alpha=\epsilon u^2\) with \(\epsilon=\pm1\), this endomorphism sends \(\alpha\) to \(\epsilon\alpha^2\) modulo four.
It follows that \(\sigma(t)=\epsilon^a t^2\), \(\sigma(y)=\epsilon^b y^2\), and \(H_{\epsilon^a,\epsilon^b}(t)=0\bmod4\), since the denominators are units.
Consequently the reduction of \(H_{\epsilon^a,\epsilon^b}/2\) vanishes at the \(t\)-coordinates of all \(d\) geometric points of \(\overline P\).
These coordinates are distinct because \(x\) is recovered from \(t,f(t)\) in the residue field.

For signs \(+,+\), (1) bounds their number by \(10\) after removing the factor \(T\).
For \(-,-\), the reduction of \(\Phi_{12}\Phi_{30}\) is \(\Phi_3^2\Phi_{15}\bmod2\), giving at most \(2+8=10\) distinct roots.
For \(+,-\) the distinct roots in (1) have orders among \(1,3,5\) in characteristic two, giving at most seven.
For \(-,+\) they have orders among \(1,9\) after removing zero, also giving at most seven.
This proves \(d\le10\).

Write \(h\) for the absolute logarithmic height and \(s=b-3a\).
The Bézout identity and reversal give
\[
  \tfrac13\max(1,|z|)^3\le\max(|g(z)|,|B(z)|)\le3\max(1,|z|)^3 .
\]
Taking means over the embeddings as in [the power-equation height identity](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#degree) gives \(|s|h(\alpha)\le\log3\).
The equation \(g(t)+\alpha^s t^3B(t)=0\) gives
\[
         t^6+t^5-(1+\alpha^{-s})t^3+\alpha^{-s}t+\alpha^{-s}=0 .
\]
The monic polynomial root bound at every embedding gives \(\max(1,|t|)\le3\max(1,|\alpha^{-s}|)\).
As both numbers are units this gives \(a h(\alpha)\le\log3+|s|h(\alpha)\).
For the nontorsion unit \(\alpha\) of degree \(2\le d\le10\), [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(h(\alpha)>2/(d(\log(3d))^3)\).
Thus \(\log3/h(\alpha)<(10\cdot1.1/2)(3.41)^3<219\).
Combining the three inequalities gives (2).
The bound supplies a finite exceptional range and does not classify the square classes inside that range.

A repeated nontorsion root in characteristic zero must satisfy \(a t(g'B-gB')-b gB=0\), a polynomial of degree at most six and constant term \(b\ne0\).
Since \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)=\mathbb Q(t)\), it has \(d\le6\).
The same height and exponent estimates therefore show squarefreeness outside the numerical exponent range in (2), independently of the escaping-direction root count.
