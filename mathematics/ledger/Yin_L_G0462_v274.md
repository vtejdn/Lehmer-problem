# Dyadic congruences for a prescribed pair of double factors

<a id="precision"></a>
## Rows satisfying the finite-precision equations

Fix a monic polynomial \(F\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with unit constant coefficient, and write \(R=\mathbb Z[X]/(F)\).
For the [two-value criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests), take \(F=P^2Q^2\), where \(P,Q\) are the distinct minima of the reciprocal nontorsion units \(\delta,\delta^2\) and every positive power of \(\delta\) has full degree.
The following examples test the proposal to exclude this pair by reducing the graph remainder modulo \(2^q\).
They apply to either graph sign and allow repeated factors in the reduction of \(F\) modulo two.
This exploration for the [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) was obtained by a research subagent for Sheng Yin on 2026-11-19.

Let \(M\) be the order of the class of \(X\) in the finite group \((R/2R)^\times\).
For \(n=2^k\) one has \(X^{Mn}-1\in 2^{k+1}R\), since \(X^M\in1+2R\) and squaring increases the known divisibility by at least one power of two.
Given any integer \(q\ge1\), put \(s=\lceil q/(k+1)\rceil\) and allow any formal width \(b\ge Ms\).
For the row \(A_0=(Y^M-1)^s\) both \(A_0(X^n)\) and \(A_{0,b}^*(X^n)\) belong to \(2^qR\).
Indeed the reversed row is \((-1)^sY^{b-Ms}A_0\).
The signed graph factors as
\[
 (X^{Mn}-1)^s\bigl(1+\varepsilon(-1)^s X^{1+n(b-Ms)}\bigr).
\]
Its roots are all roots of unity and \(A_0(\alpha^n)\ne0\) for every nontorsion unit \(\alpha\).
For any positive integer sequence \(q=o(n\log n)\), the choice \(b=Ms\) gives \(b=o(n)\) and \(\log\|A_0\|_1=s\log2=o(n)\).
Thus the strict budget \(((2nb+1)\|A_0\|_1^2)^4<2^n\) holds eventually.

There are also examples with coprime numerator and reversed numerator.
Use
\[
                  A=(Y^M-1)^s+2^{q+1}.
\]
Substitution of \(X^n\) in the two rows gives the same images as before modulo \(2^qR\), and \(A\) is monic.
Since \(s\le q\), the polynomial \(A\) has no zero on the closed complex unit disk.
Every zero of \(A_b^*\) lies in its interior, so the rows are coprime.
The function \(A_b^*/A\) has modulus one on the circle and at most one in the disk by the maximum modulus principle.
Consequently \(1+\varepsilon X(A_b^*/A)(X^n)\) has no zero in the open disk.
Signed reversal of the graph about degree \(nb+1\) then excludes zeros outside the circle as well.
Each of \(P,Q\) has a conjugate inside the circle and a conjugate outside, since it is reciprocal and has positive measure.
The first ensures nonvanishing of \(A\) at all its \(n\)-th powers by rational conjugacy, and the second excludes the factor even with multiplicity one.
If \(0<\eta<1/8\) and \(q\le(1/8-\eta)n\), then \(\log_2\|A\|_1\le q+2\) and \(Ms=O_F(n/\log n)\).
The strict budget holds eventually at \(b=Ms\), or at any prescribed width \(b\ge Ms\) with \(\log b=o(n)\).
In particular these false positives persist at any fixed precision slope below \(1/8\) on the single sublinear width sequence \(b=\lceil n/\sqrt{\log n}\rceil\), eventually for each fixed \(F\).
These examples test the remainder of the graph in \(R/2^qR\).
When \(\bar P\) is squarefree the same obstruction applies even to reduction of the exact integral solution lattice, as shown below.

## Four contact equations on one residue disk

For the same prescribed pair assume now that \(\bar P=P\bmod 2\) is squarefree, and write \(d=\deg P\).
The [squaring identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) gives \(\bar Q=\bar P\).
In a single unramified splitting field over \(\mathbb Q_2\), each residue root \(\xi\) lifts uniquely to a root \(x\) of \(P\) and a root \(y\) of \(Q\).
The lifts are distinct because their minima are distinct.
Normalize the valuation by \(v(2)=1\), put \(r=y/x\), and let \(a=v(r-1)\ge1\).
Write \(D=\max_\xi(4a-2)\).
For every \(n=2^k\), \(k>D\), suppose \(F\mid U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\) for integral rows \(U,V\).
At the odd-order root of unity \(\omega\) lifting any residue root of \(\bar P\), both rows \(H=U,V\) satisfy
\[
                  v(H(\omega))\ge 2k-D,\qquad v(H'(\omega))\ge k-D.       \tag{1}
\]
Their values at \(x^n,y^n\) also satisfy the first bound.

Put \(z=x^n,\ S=r^n,\ h=(S-1)/n\).
As \(r\in 1+2\mathcal O\), successive squaring gives \(v(h)\ge1\).
The value and derivative equations at \(x\) give \(U(z)=-xV(z)\) and \(nz(U'(z)+xV'(z))=-xV(z)\).
In particular \(u_0=U(z)/n,\ v_0=V(z)/n,\ u_1=zU'(z),\ v_1=zV'(z)\) are integral and satisfy
\[
                       u_0=-xv_0,\qquad u_1=-xv_1-xv_0.
\]
The polynomial \(U(z(1+nT))/n\) has constant and linear coefficients \(u_0,u_1\) and all higher coefficients in \(n\mathcal O\), and the same holds for \(V\).
Evaluation at \(T=h\) gives the values at \(y^n=zS\) scaled by \(n\).
After using the double-root equations at \(y\), the two resulting congruences modulo \(n\) are
\[
 u_0+yv_0+h(u_1+yv_1)\equiv0,\qquad
 S(u_1+yv_1)+y(v_0+hv_1)\equiv0 .
\]
Substituting the equations at \(x\) gives a homogeneous congruence on \((v_0,v_1)\) with matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 y-x-xh & h(y-x)\\
 y-xS & S(y-x)+yh
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is \(S(y-x)^2-xyh^2=Sxy(T_0-T_k/4^k)\), where \(T_j=r^{2^j}+r^{-2^j}-2\).
Here \(v(T_0)=2a\) and \(T_{j+1}=4T_j+T_j^2\), so \(v(T_j)\ge2a+2j\).
The difference \(T_k/4^k-T_0=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T_j^2/4^{j+1}\) has valuation exactly \(4a-2\), since its initial term has that valuation and each later term has valuation at least \(4a+2j-2\).
Multiplication by the integral adjugate therefore puts \(u_0,v_0,u_1,v_1\) in \(2^{k-D}\mathcal O\).
This gives the desired bounds for both rows at \(z\).
The same computation with \(x,y\) exchanged treats \(y^n\).
If \(\omega_\xi\) is the odd-order lift of \(\xi\), then \(v(x^n-\omega_\xi^n)\ge k+1\) by successive squaring.
Taylor expansion now gives (1) at \(\omega_\xi^n\), including the derivative bound by integrality of its Taylor coefficients.
The \(2^k\)-power map permutes the lifted roots because the residue root set is stable under Frobenius.

<a id="rank"></a>
## The exact integral rank and the size of the local condition

Take \(U=A,\ V=\varepsilon A_b^*\).
For \(k>D\), (1) forces \(\bar P^2\mid\bar A\).
The integral solution lattice is saturated because it is the kernel of the graph remainder map into the free abelian group \(R\).
Consequently its rank equals the dimension of its reduction inside the ambient coefficient space.
For \(b<2d\) its rank is zero.
For \(b\ge2d\) the preceding divisibility bounds its rank above by \(b+1-2d\).
The [reciprocal eigenspace calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#rank) supplies the reverse bound at every exponent.
Indeed the signed graph lies in one eigenspace of reversal about degree \(nb+1\) on the rational remainder space, which interchanges the length-two factors of each of the \(d\) inverse pairs of roots of \(PQ\).
Thus that eigenspace has dimension \(2d\).
The solution lattice has rank exactly \(b+1-2d\), and its reduction consists of all multiples of \(\bar P^2\) of degree at most \(b\).
At each of the \(d\) roots of the minimum \(P_n\) of \(\delta^n\), the value bound gives
\[
                  2^{d(2k-D)}\mid\operatorname{Res}(P_n,A)
\]
when contact values are nonzero.
The logarithm of this forced divisor grows as \(O_P(\log n)\).

## False positives in the reduction of the exact lattice

Write \(L_b(A)\) for the remainder of the signed graph in \(R\), \(K_b=\ker L_b\), and assume \(k>D\).
The proof of (1) still gives \(\bar P^2\mid\bar A\) if \(L_b(A)\in 2^{2k}(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2)\), allowing coefficients of \(A\) in \(\mathbb Z_2\).
Indeed the graph and its derivative then have values in \(2^{2k}\mathcal O\) at \(x,y\), since \(F\) has a double zero at both roots.
The first derivative equation at \(x\) still gives \(V(z)\in n\mathcal O\), and the value equation then gives \(U(z)\in n\mathcal O\).
Dividing the value and logarithmic derivative equations by \(n\) at each node retains all four congruences modulo \(n\) used in the determinant argument.
This proves the asserted divisibility.

For any \(b\ge2d-1\), restrict \(L_b\) to the coefficients of degrees \(0,\ldots,2d-1\), keeping the reversal at the actual width \(b\).
A primitive \(2\)-adic vector in this restriction cannot have remainder divisible by \(2^{2k}\), since \(\bar P^2\mid\bar A\) would force \(\bar A=0\).
Thus the restricted columns are independent and every diagonal valuation in their Smith normal form over \(\mathbb Z_2\) is at most \(2k-1\).
Indeed a zero diagonal or an entry divisible by \(2^{2k}\) would furnish a primitive vector contradicting the bound.
They span the whole signed eigenspace over \(\mathbb Q_2\), by its dimension \(2d\).
It follows in Smith coordinates that if \(L_b(A)\in2^{q+2k}(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2)\), then it is \(2^q L_b(C)\) for a \(2\)-adic polynomial \(C\) of degree at most \(2d-1\).
Hence \(A-2^qC\) lies in the \(2\)-adic kernel and is congruent to \(A\bmod 2^q\).
The integral Smith normal form of the full remainder map identifies that kernel with \(K_b\otimes\mathbb Z_2\), whose elements modulo \(2^q\) are represented by ordinary integral rows of \(K_b\).

Apply the [coprime construction](#precision) with internal precision \(q+2k\) at the width \(b=\lceil n/\sqrt{\log n}\rceil\).
For every fixed \(\eta>0\) and \(1\le q\le(1/8-\eta)n\) the extra \(2k\) digits cost \(o(n)\) in the logarithmic budget, so the rows still pass eventually.
They have nonvanishing numerators and even their coefficient vectors modulo \(2^q\) occur in \(K_b\bmod 2^q\), while neither target is an actual graph factor.
For the examples allowing a common row factor the same lifting works at any \(q=o(n\log n)\) by taking \(b=\max(2d-1,M\lceil(q+2k)/(k+1)\rceil)=o(n)\).
These constructions leave open exclusions using a smaller width, greater precision, or the archimedean sizes of global representatives of a local kernel class.
It remains open whether each admissible input admits arbitrarily large exponents excluding the exact paired contacts on passing rows of a prescribed sign and widths tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\).
