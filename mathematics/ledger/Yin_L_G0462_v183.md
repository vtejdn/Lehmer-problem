# The algebraic degree of the Mahler measure reads the exterior allocation

<a id="degrees"></a>
## Degrees over the rationals and the maximal abelian field

Let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers, \(R=b/a\), and put
\[
 A(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a),\qquad g(T)=T^3-T-1,\quad B(T)=1-T^2-T^3 .
\]
For sufficiently large \(R\), write \(P_i\) for all the distinct monic nontorsion irreducible factors of \(-A\), and put \(n_i=\deg P_i/2\).
Let \(k_i\) count the complex-conjugate pairs of exterior roots in \(P_i\), and set \(\ell_i=2k_i\).
Write \(M\) for the multiplicative Mahler measure and \(F=\mathbb Q^{\rm ab}\).
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_i&=[\mathbb Q(M(P_i)):\mathbb Q]=[F(M(P_i)):F]
                       =2^{\ell_i}\binom{n_i}{\ell_i},\\
 [\mathbb Q(M(A)):\mathbb Q]&=[F(M(A)):F]=\prod_i D_i .               \tag{1}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The numbers \(\ell_i\) count occupied inverse-pair labels, each contributing one exterior root.
Indeed the [exterior partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#partition) gives only nonreal exterior roots, occurring in conjugate pairs, with reciprocal factors, simple roots and \(\sum_i k_i=a\).
The product of all exterior roots of each \(P_i\) is positive and equals \(M(P_i)\).
The same partition gives \(n_i/\ell_i=(R/4)(1+o(1))\) uniformly, so every factor has an unused inverse-pair label for large \(R\).

<a id="orbits"></a>
## Distinct products in the signed-subset orbit

For one reciprocal algebraic integer, Boyd identifies the rational degree of its Mahler measure with the orbit size of the subset of exterior roots.
The exterior subset uniquely maximizes the absolute product among subsets of that size, so distinct members of its Galois orbit give distinct products. [Boyd, §1, pp. 3–4](../audits/literature/Boy87/Yin_U001.md#boy87-u001)
Here the joint group determines the orbit already over \(F\), and its sign changes give independence for arbitrary integer exponents across all factors.

Choose one representative \(z_{ij}\) in every inverse pair of every \(P_i\).
In the compositum of their splitting fields with \(F\), the [joint root group](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v384.md#statement) permits independent even sign changes and alternating permutations in each factor.
The \(n_i\) are uniformly at least three in this range.
If \(\prod_{i,j} z_{ij}^{e_{ij}}\in F^\times\) for integers \(e_{ij}\), fix \(i\) and write \(u_j=z_{ij}^{2e_{ij}}\).
Flipping just the representatives of labels \(j,l\) in \(P_i\) fixes the monomial, giving \(u_j u_l=1\).
For distinct \(j,l,s\), multiplying the equations for \(j,l\) and \(j,s\) and dividing by that for \(l,s\) gives \(z_{ij}^{4e_{ij}}=1\).
Every representative is nontorsion, so every exponent vanishes.
This proves independence of all the representatives modulo \(F^\times\), strengthening the [pairwise independence](Yin_L_G0462_v177.md#independence) for this application.

The exponent vector of \(M(P_i)\) has exactly \(\ell_i\) entries equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) and all others zero.
The alternating group is transitive on the \(\ell_i\)-subsets because a permutation carrying one subset to another may have its parity adjusted by a transposition in the target subset or its complement, one of which has at least two labels.
After moving the subset, all signs on it can be chosen by even flips, using an unoccupied label to absorb an odd parity.
The group over \(F\) therefore realizes all \(D_i\) signed subsets.
They give distinct values by the monomial independence just proved.
Every rational conjugation also preserves the rational factor and its inverse pairs, so its orbit has no further values.
This proves the first line of (1).
For \(M(A)=\prod_i M(P_i)\), cyclotomic factors have measure one and every noncyclotomic factor occurs once.
The independent group actions realize the Cartesian product of all the signed-subset orbits, and independence across factors keeps their products distinct.
Rational conjugation again preserves each rational factor individually, proving the second line.
In fact \(M(A)\) generates the compositum of the individual measure fields over either base, since it belongs to that compositum and its degree already equals the product of their degrees.

<a id="criterion"></a>
## Recovering a factor's share from its measure degree

For fixed \(n\), the ratio of \(2^{j+1}\binom n{j+1}\) to \(2^j\binom nj\) is \(2(n-j)/(j+1)\).
Thus \(n_i,D_i\) recover \(\ell_i\), and hence \(k_i\), uniquely for all sufficiently large \(R\).
Indeed the values are strictly increasing up to \(\lfloor n_i/2\rfloor\), and for \(j>n_i/2\) they are at least \(2^{n_i/2}\).
The actual \(\ell_i=o(n_i)\) gives \(\log D_i=o(n_i)\) uniformly by the upper binomial bound below.

The elementary bounds \((n/j)^j\le\binom nj\le(en/j)^j\) for \(1\le j\le n\) and \(n_i/\ell_i=(R/4)(1+o(1))\) give
\[
 \log D_i=2k_i(\log R+O(1)),\qquad
 \frac{\log D_i}{a\log R}
      =\frac{2k_i}{a}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac1{\log R}\right)\right).     \tag{2}
\]
Both errors are uniform over the factors as \(R\to\infty\), even for \(k_i=1\).
The lower binomial bound follows by multiplying \((n-s)/(j-s)\ge n/j\) for \(0\le s<j\), and the upper follows from \(j!\ge(j/e)^j\).
In particular the total log degree in (1) is \(2a\log R+O(a)\), independent of the allocation to leading order.

Along any sequence of selected factors in this range, the [small-part criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#small-parts) and (2) give
\[
     \log M(P_i)\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
     k_i/a\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
     \log D_i=o(a\log(b/a)).
\]
For example, an exact annihilating polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\) for the actual \(M(P_i)\) gives an upper bound on \(D_i\) by its degree.
A proved upper bound with logarithm \(o(a\log(b/a))\) along an infinite sequence with \(b/a\to\infty\) would therefore exhibit nontorsion algebraic units of measure tending to one.
Finite degree determinations recover the corresponding finite allocations and leave the existence of such a sequence open.

## A finite check using two residue characteristics

At \((a,b)=(1,3)\) the monic polynomial is \(P=x^6+x^5-2x^3+x+1=x^3q(x+x^{-1})\), where \(q(t)=t^3+t^2-3t-4\).
The cubic has no rational root and has discriminant \(-83\), with its unique real root between one and two.
At that real embedding \(t^2-4<0\), so \(x^2-tx+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
Hence \(P\) is irreducible and has one unit-circle pair and two nonreal conjugate exterior roots, one over each nonreal trace.
Their product is \(M(P)\).

For a trace \(t\), the other two traces have sum \(-1-t\) and product \(B=t^2+t-3\).
Choosing one root above each of these two traces gives four products with polynomial \(Y^4-BY^3+(5-t^2)Y^2-BY+1\), by expansion using the inverse pairs.
Taking its resultant in \(t\) with \(q\) gives the monic polynomial
\[
 H(Y)=Y^{12}+3Y^{11}+4Y^{10}-11Y^8-24Y^7-29Y^6-24Y^5-11Y^4+4Y^2+3Y+1.
\]
It contains \(M(P)\) among its roots.
For a finite-field verification let \(u_0=Y,\ u_{j+1}=u_j^p\bmod H\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
The following degrees of \(\gcd(H,u_j-Y)\) follow by polynomial powering and the Euclidean algorithm.

| \(p\) | \(j\) | Degrees in the same order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(3\) | \(2,3,6\) | \(0,0,12\) |
| \(5\) | \(2,4\) | \(0,12\) |

Thus every factor modulo three has degree six, and every factor modulo five has degree four, with both reductions squarefree.
Every monic rational divisor has integral coefficients and degree divisible by both six and four.
It follows that \(H\) is irreducible and \([\mathbb Q(M(P)):\mathbb Q]=12=2^2\binom32\).
This checks the absolute degree directly at a finite direction with three inverse pairs.
