# Sharp collision valuations for repeated quadratic factors

<a id="simultaneous"></a>
## One two-row multiple attains all the prescribed valuations

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of a nontorsion quadratic unit \(\alpha\), let \(p\) be prime, and assume that \(\beta=\alpha^p\) has degree two.
Write \(I=[\mathbb Z[\alpha]:\mathbb Z[\beta]]\), and let \(S\) consist of the prime divisors \(\ell\ne p\) of \(I\).
For every integer \(e\ge1\) there is a primitive integer polynomial
\[
          F(x)=U(x^p)+xV(x^p),\qquad P^e\mid F,
        \qquad \deg U\le e,\quad\deg V<2e,
\]
with both rows nonzero and coprime in \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
For this single witness the row resultant satisfies
\[
           v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{Res}(U,V)\bigr)=e^2 v_\ell(I)
                      \quad\text{for every }\ell\in S.                  \tag{1}
\]
The equalities still hold after removing individual row monomials when forming the resultant.
In degree two, the [collision lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#branches) gives exactly the right side of (1) at each such prime.
Equality imposes no restriction on \(e\) relative to \(\ell\).
The construction allows the resultant to have other prime divisors.

<a id="local"></a>
## Producing the exact intersection at one prime

Fix \(\ell\in S\), and use the normalized valuation \(v(\ell)=1\) in a finite Galois extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) splitting \(P\) and containing the \(p\)-th roots of unity.
Let \(\mathcal O\) be its ring of integers and \(\pi\) a uniformizer.
Write \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) for the roots and \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^p\).
If \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) had the same residue, their \(p\)-th power difference quotient would reduce to the nonzero element \(p\bar\alpha_1^{p-1}\).
By the [quadratic power-index formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#powered-order-index), the two original residues therefore differ, the two powered residues agree, and \(v(\beta_1-\beta_2)=v_\ell(I)\).
The common residue \(b\ne0\) lies in \(\mathbb F_\ell\), because its orbit under the residual Galois group is a singleton.
Choose \(\gamma\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\) reducing to \(b\), and put \(T=X-\gamma\).

For \(i=1,2\) there is a unique \(u_i\in\mathcal O\) with \(u_i^p=\gamma\) and \(u_i\equiv\alpha_i\bmod\pi\) by Hensel's lemma.
Both \(u_i\) and \(u_1-u_2\) are units.
In \(\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]\) there is a unique series \(t(0)=1,\ t(T)^p=1+T/\gamma\), obtained by successively dividing by \(p\) in its coefficient recursion.
The branches \(a_i(T)=u_i t(T)\) satisfy \(a_i^p=X\) and \(a_i(\beta_i-\gamma)=\alpha_i\).
Let
\[
              A=(X-\beta_1)^e,\qquad B=(X-\beta_2)^e .
\]
The simultaneous equations \(U+a_1V=A,\ U+a_2V=B\) have the solution
\[
           U=\frac{u_1 B-u_2 A}{u_1-u_2},\qquad
           V=\frac{A-B}{(u_1-u_2)t(T)} .
\]
Local Galois automorphisms preserve the paired labels or exchange them, so \(U\in\mathbb Z_\ell[X]\) and \(V\in\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]\).
The polynomial \(U\) is monic of degree \(e\) and reduces to \((X-b)^e\).

Write \(Q\) for the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
Replace \(V\) by its polynomial remainder \(V_\ell\) of degree less than \(2e\) modulo \(Q^e=AB\) in \(\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]\).
This division holds because \(Q(\gamma+T)^e\) is monic and reduces to \(T^{2e}\).
Explicitly, divide the series modulo \(\ell\) by \(T^{2e}\), lift its quotient and remainder, and repeat on the error divided by \(\ell\).
The resulting sums converge coefficientwise and give the asserted division.
Uniqueness follows by reducing a proposed zero decomposition modulo \(\ell\) and iterating, since division by \(T^{2e}\) has unique quotient and remainder over the residue field.

The equations now give \(U+a_i V_\ell\in ((X-\beta_i)^e)\) in \(\mathcal O[[T]]\).
At \(x=\alpha_i+z\), the substitution \(X=x^p\) sends \(T\) into \((\pi,z)\), so these series can be evaluated in the complete ring \(\mathcal O[[z]]\).
Here \(a_i(x^p-\gamma)=x\) by uniqueness of the \(p\)-th root with residue \(\bar\alpha_i\).
Consequently the polynomial \(U(x^p)+xV_\ell(x^p)\) vanishes to order at least \(e\) at each \(\alpha_i\).

In the local ring \(\mathcal O[[T]]\), the identities before division give \((U,V)=(A,B)=J\) because \(a_1-a_2\) is a unit.
The remainder changes \(V\) by an element of \((AB)\subseteq(\pi,T)J\), as both \(A,B\) belong to the maximal ideal.
Nakayama's lemma gives \((U,V_\ell)=J\) there.
Since \(U\) is monic and reduces to \(T^e\), the same division argument identifies \(\mathcal O[X]/(U)\) with \(\mathcal O[[T]]/(U)\).
The corresponding statement holds for \(A\).
Thus the two polynomial quotients \(\mathcal O[X]/(U,V_\ell)\) and \(\mathcal O[X]/(A,B)\) have the same length.
After division by \(v_\pi(\ell)\) the former length is \(v_\ell(\operatorname{Res}(U,V_\ell))\), by the determinant of multiplication by \(V_\ell\) modulo the monic \(U\).
The latter is \(e^2 v(\beta_1-\beta_2)\) by the same determinant formula for \(A,B\).
This proves exact equality over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) and also shows that the two local polynomial rows have nonzero resultant.

<a id="integer"></a>
## A single integral choice

For degree bounds \(\deg U\le e,\ \deg V\le 2e-1\), the coefficients of the remainder of \(U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\) on division by the monic \(P^e\) are integral linear forms in the row coefficients.
Let \(L\) be their common kernel on the integral coefficient vectors.
For every \(\ell\), \(L\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\) is their kernel over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), as seen by integral Smith normal form.
Choose an integral basis of \(L\).
For each \(\ell\in S\), express the local solution just obtained in that basis over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
The Chinese remainder theorem gives one integral combination matching these basis coordinates modulo \(\ell^{1+e^2v_\ell(I)}\) at every selected prime.

The Sylvester determinant padded to row degrees \(e,2e-1\) is an integer polynomial in these coefficients.
When \(U\) has degree \(e\) and \(V\) has smaller than the padded degree, its absolute value differs from that of the ordinary resultant by the corresponding extra power of \(|\operatorname{lc}U|\), by the root-product formula.
Locally \(U\) was monic, and its approximating leading coefficient is a unit at every selected prime.
The congruences therefore preserve each exact resultant valuation in (1).
If \(S\ne\varnothing\), they also give a nonzero resultant over \(\mathbb Q\), excluding every common polynomial factor.
Both rows are nonzero, since the local \(U\) is monic and a zero \(V\) would force the padded determinant to vanish.
Removing common integer content leaves (1) unchanged since the leading coefficient of \(U\) is a unit at every \(\ell\in S\).
Moreover \(U(0)\) is a unit at those primes, by the reduction \((X-b)^e\) with \(b\ne0\).
Thus removing any power of \(X\) dividing the second row changes the absolute resultant only by an integer unit at those primes, and \(U\) already has nonzero constant term.

If \(S\) is empty, take \(U=1\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
At each \(\beta_i\), prescribe the first \(e\) Taylor coefficients of \(V\) to equal those of the negative reciprocal of the local \(p\)-th root branch through \(\alpha_i\).
The two nodes are distinct in characteristic zero, so Hermite interpolation gives a unique \(V\) of degree less than \(2e\), and the Galois symmetry of the data gives rational coefficients.
The constant Taylor values are nonzero.
Clearing denominators and removing content gives coprime nonzero rows, with vanishing order at least \(e\) at each root of \(P\).
This completes the existence assertion also when (1) is empty.

## Explicit primes and multiplicities

For \(P=x^2-3x+1\), the relation \(\alpha^2=3\alpha-1\) gives \(\alpha^7=377\alpha-144\).
Hence \(I=377=13\cdot29\) for \(p=7\), and a single integral witness realizes valuation \(e^2\) at both \(13\) and \(29\) for every \(e\).
For a collision in residue characteristic two take \(P=x^2-x-1,\ p=3\), so \(\alpha^3=2\alpha+1\) gives \(I=2\) and (1) is exact at \(2\) for arbitrarily large even \(e\).

When \(p=2\) there is a direct formula at all primes for \(P=x^2-ax-1\) with an integer \(a\ge1\).
This polynomial has no rational root, its real root above one certifies nontorsion, and \(\alpha^2=1+a\alpha\) gives \(I=a\).
Take \(F=P^e=U(x^2)+xV(x^2)\), so \(U\) is monic of degree \(e\).
At a zero \(z\) of \(F\) one has \(F(-z)=-2zV(z^2)\), and \(|F(0)|=1\).
The absolute resultant of \(F(x),V(x^2)\) equals that of \(U(x^2),V(x^2)\), since both \(F(x)\) and \(U(x^2)\) are monic of degree \(2e\) and agree at all zeros of \(V(x^2)\).
This remains true for constant \(V\) by the same leading-degree formula.
Substitution \(x^2\) makes the latter absolute resultant \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|^2\).
Therefore
\[
  2^{2e}|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|^2
       =|\operatorname{Res}(F(x),F(-x))|
       =|\operatorname{Res}(P(x),P(-x))|^{e^2}
       =(2a)^{2e^2},
\]
where \(P(-z)=2az\) at a zero of \(P\).
The absolute row resultant is \(2^{e^2-e}a^{e^2}\).
This example separates the support-prime contribution from the exact odd-prime contributions in (1).
