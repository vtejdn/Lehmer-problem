# Angular energy in double-root witnesses for genuine Salem inputs

<a id="cost"></a>
## Exact energy and cofactor cost

Let \(6\mid d\), let \(p>3\) be a prime divisor of \(d\), put \(n=d/p\), and take any \(1\le r<p\).
Use the [Salem minimal polynomial](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) \(S_d=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1\).
For \(X=x^p\), put \(W=2X^n+1,\ V=X^n+2\), and form the [exact double-root witness](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v373.md#witness)
\[
 F_r(x)=U(X)+x^r T(X),\qquad U=(p-r)W^p+rXV^p,\qquad T=-pW^{p-r}V^r .
\]
It is primitive, has coprime rows with nonzero constant terms, and contains \(S_d\) with multiplicity exactly two.
Translating by a monomial gives any prescribed residue pair with this difference within the degree cap.
The higher row degree is \(k=d+1\).
On \(\mathbb T\), \(|W|=|V|\), so \(|T|=p|W|^p\ge|U|\).
The circle integral of \(f=\log\max(|U|,|T|)\) is exactly \(I=p\log2+\log p\).
The [homogeneous resultant formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#identity) applied to the [ordinary resultant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v373.md#witness) gives
\[
 B=p^{d+1} r^{n(p-r)+1}(p-r)^{nr}3^{dp}.
\]
The extra factor \(r\) is the leading coefficient of \(U\), since the two row degrees differ by one.
For \(g=f-k\log^+|z|\), the exact Dirichlet identity gives, uniformly in \(r\) as \(p,d\to\infty\),
\[
  \frac{\mathcal D(g)}{kp}= \frac{2I}{p}-\frac{\log B}{kp}
         =\log(4/3)+O\!\left(\frac{\log p}{p}+\frac1d\right).
\]
If \(\log d=o(p^2)\), the [diagonal comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#cofactor) gives \(m(F_r)/p\to\log2\).
The noncyclotomic cofactor after removal of \(S_d^2\) has the same limiting rate since \(m(S_d)\to\log2\).

<a id="annulus"></a>
## All but two roots of every pencil member lie near the unit circle

Assume now \(p\to\infty\) and \(d/p\to\infty\).
Put \(s=60\log(p)/d\).
For all sufficiently large parameters and every \(|t|=1\), \(U-tT\) has exactly one zero in \(|X|<e^{-s}\), exactly one in \(|X|>e^s\), and all its other \(d-1\) zeros in the open intervening annulus, with multiplicity.

To see this, on \(|X|=e^s\) we have
\[
 \left|\frac{W}{V}\right|\ge\frac{2e^{ns}+1}{e^{ns}+2}\ge e^{ns/4}.
\]
For the first bound, \(|2Y+1|^2-|Y+2|^2=3(|Y|^2-1)\) with \(Y=X^n\), and the smallest ratio occurs when \(Y\) is positive.
For the second, \(ns=60\log(p)/p\to0\) and the logarithm of the middle expression has derivative \(1/3\) at zero as a function of \(ns\).
On \(|X|=e^{-s}\) the same lower bound holds for \(|V/W|\) by inversion.
Write \(v=\exp(-15\log(p)/p)\).
Relative to the outer dominant term \((p-r)W^p\), the sum of the absolute values of the other terms is bounded by the fraction
\[
       e^s\frac{p v^\ell+\ell v^p}{p-\ell},\qquad \ell=r .
\]
Relative to the inner dominant term \(rX V^p\), the same bound holds with \(\ell=p-r\).
Both fractions are strictly less than one, uniformly.
Indeed \(\log(pv^\ell/(p-\ell))=\ell\log v-\log(1-\ell/p)\) is convex in \(\ell\), so its maximum on \([1,p-1]\) occurs at an endpoint.
At one it is at most \((-15\log p+2)/p\), and at \(p-1\) it is at most \(-13\log p\) for large \(p\).
Also \(\ell v^p/(p-\ell)\le p^{-14}\) and \(s=o(\log(p)/p)\).
Rouché's theorem on the outer circle counts the \(d\) zeros of \(W^p\), while on the inner circle it counts the single zero of \(XV^p\) in that disk.
The pencil has degree \(d+1\) for every phase, proving the assertion.

<a id="radial"></a>
## The contribution from the radial mean tends to zero

Each pencil member has coefficient norm at most \(2p3^p\), leading coefficient \(r\ge1\), and constant term of modulus at least \(p2^r-(p-r)\ge1\).
The Cauchy root bound for it and its reversal places the logarithmic radius of every zero in \([-L,L]\), \(L=\log(1+2p3^p)\).
Let \(G(u)=\int_{\mathbb T}g(e^u z)\,d\sigma(z)\).
The [radial derivative identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#radial) expresses \(G'\) at negative \(u\) as the mean number of interior pencil roots and at positive \(u\) as minus the mean number of exterior ones, almost everywhere.
The annular localization therefore gives
\[
     \int_{\mathbb R} |G'(u)|^2\,du\le 2L+2s k^2,\qquad
     \frac{1}{kp}\int_{\mathbb R}|G'(u)|^2\,du
           =O\!\left(\frac1k+\frac{\log p}{p}\right).
\]
The same upper bound after normalization holds for every annular statistic \(\tau(n_-^2+n_+^2)/(kp)\), including a width chosen after seeing both rows.
In the [actual fourth-logarithmic window](Yin_L_G0462_v156.md#first-primes), the hypotheses hold simultaneously for the prescribed list of primes on this same Salem input.
Thus the full energy has positive limit on the \(kp\) scale while the energy of the radial mean vanishes on that scale, even for actual double roots and every prescribed residue pair.

For adjacent classes the limiting cofactor rate is optimal.
Compare any admissible adjacent double-root witness to the single-root graph \(xV(x^p)-W(x^p)=(x-1)S_d(x)\).
The coprime ratios must differ, since an equal ratio would give a scalar Laurent monomial multiple and preserve its simple zero.
The [two-graph determinant bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#statement) with one shared copy gives \(I_{\rm double}\ge p\,m(S_d)-2\log2\), as the row integral of the single-root graph is \(\log2\).
The diagonal comparison on the cap now bounds every adjacent cofactor rate below by \(m(S_d)-o(1)\).
Our witnesses attain this rate in the limit.
For every difference \(r\), the [double-root house estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v382.md#cofactor) likewise bounds all admissible cofactor rates below by \(m(S_d)-o(1)\) in the fourth-logarithmic window, since the single exterior conjugate realizes the whole measure.
Hence the described witnesses simultaneously attain the optimal limiting rate for all residue pairs in that window.
A proof using a radial count for arbitrary reciprocal inputs would have to exploit a property specific to an input with small positive measure.

## Sharpness of the local interpolation radius for an actual Salem factor

For \(r=1\) and every phase, the innermost pencil root lies between \((p/2)2^{-p}\) and \(4p2^{-p}\) in modulus once \(p\) is large.
In fact on \(|X|\le4p2^{-p}\),
\[
 U-tT=(p-1)+2pt+2^p X+o(1)
\]
uniformly in \(n\ge1\) and \(|t|=1\).
To check the error, write \(Y=X^n\), so \(|Y|\le4p2^{-p}\) there.
The binomial bound \(|(1+w)^j-1|\le e^{j|w|}-1\) shows \(W^p=1+O(p|Y|)\), \(V^p=2^p(1+O(p|Y|))\), and \(W^{p-1}V=2+O(p|Y|)\).
The total error on this disk is \(O(p^3 2^{-p})\).
The constant term of the linear approximation has modulus between \(p+1\) and \(3p-1\).
Rouché's theorem against the constant on the inner boundary and against \(2^pX\) on the outer one proves the assertion.

Let \(\lambda_d>1\) be the Salem root, \(\alpha=\lambda_d^{-1}\) and \(b=\alpha^p\).
The equation \((2-\lambda_d)\lambda_d^d=2\lambda_d-1\) and \(\lambda_d\to2\) give \(b=(1+o(1))2^{-p}\), since \(p\le d\).
The [inverse hyperbolic derivative](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v381.md#statement) is
\[
      D_b^{-1}=p\,\sinh(\log\lambda_d)/\sinh(p\log\lambda_d)
              =(3/2+o(1))p\,2^{-p}.
\]
Every pencil root has disk distance at least a fixed positive multiple of \(D_b^{-1}\) from \(b\) when it is in the open disk, since \(|z-b|\ge |z|-|b|\) and \(|1-\bar b z|\le1+|b|\).
Every phase also has an interior root within a fixed multiple of \(D_b^{-1}\), by the outer radius above.
Thus the local radius has the correct order on all phases of this double-root witness, even though the normalized energy of the radial mean tends to zero.
