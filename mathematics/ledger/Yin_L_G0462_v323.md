# Exact binary block matching in Salem return paths near two

<a id="blocks"></a>
## Matching a block to a represented state

Let \(r=r_n\in(1,2)\) be the [Salem root approaching two](Yin_L_G0462_v296.md#barrier) of \(F_n(X)=X^n+1-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X^j\), with \(n\ge4\).
Write \(\delta=2-r\), so \(\delta r^n=2r-1\).
Use the recurrence \(y\mapsto ry+d\), with digit \(d\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and critical step weight \(x=r/4\) for a nonzero digit and \(2x\) for a zero.
The [two defining excursions](Yin_L_G0462_v301.md#deficit) cover an asymptotic fraction \(2/3\) of the critical mass missing after the one-step zero return.

Fix a representation \(y=-\delta A(r)\) with \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\).
Read an \(n\)-digit block as the coefficients of \(Q\), starting with the coefficient of \(X^{n-1}\) and allowing leading zeros.
Its final state is \(Q(r)-(2r-1)A(r)\).
Whenever \(Q(2)=3A(2)\), the quotient
\[
               B(X)=\frac{Q(X)-(2X-1)A(X)}{X-2}
\]
is integral, so the final state is \(-\delta B(r)\).
The total critical weight of these blocks is exactly
\[
          w_n(A)=\left(\frac r2\right)^n
                       \left(1-\frac{3|A(2)|}{2^n}\right)_+,          \tag{1}
\]
where \(v_+=\max(v,0)\).
Indeed each digit word occurs as the difference of two binary words in \(2^{\#\{d=0\}}\) ways.
Among two integers represented by \(n\) bits, the number of ordered pairs of difference \(t\) is \((2^n-|t|)_+\).
Multiplication by \(x^n\) gives (1).
Thus \(k\) consecutive exact matches have total weight at most \((r/2)^{nk}\), including when a representative is chosen from the past after each block.

The exact matching test depends on the representative because adding a multiple of \(F_n\) can change \(A(2)\) while preserving \(A(r)\).
There are further integral transfers whenever \(Q(2)-3A(2)=3b\) with \(b\in\mathbb Z\).
Since \(F_n(2)=3\) and \(F_n(r)=0\), subtracting \(bF_n(X)\) from the numerator in \(B\) again gives an integral quotient representing the same final state after multiplication by \(-\delta\).
Divisibility alone permits large real values of \(B(r)\).
For instance, from \(A=0\) take all digits \(+1\) when \(n\) is even, and change the last digit to zero when \(n\) is odd.
Then \(Q(2)\) is a multiple of three and \(Q(r)\) grows exponentially with \(n\).
To use such transitions for a return estimate one must control the actual states, reachable exits and total first-return weight.

<a id="crossing"></a>
## An extra loop during a sign change

Set \(u=(r-1)/r\) and \(s_j=-\delta r^j\).
From \(u\) the digit \(-1\) reaches \(s_0\), and \(n-2\) zeros followed by \(+1\) end at \(-u\).
At the intermediate state \(s_{n-3}\), the word \((+1,0,-1)\) instead ends at \(s_1\).
Indeed the first digit gives \(1-\delta r^{n-2}=u^2\), and the other two give \(r^2u^2-1=-\delta r\).
Another \(n-4\) zeros restore \(s_{n-3}\).
Thus the original crossing can include any number of copies of a loop of weight \((r/2)^{n-1}/4\).
The choice to loop starts with \(+1\), while the final departure starts with zero.
The distinct resulting words from \(u\) to \(-u\) have total weight
\[
          \frac{(r/2)^n/4}{1-(r/2)^{n-1}/4}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac13 ,
\]
using \(n\log(r/2)\to0\).
A first return to zero also pays for reaching this crossing and exiting it.

<a id="failures"></a>
## Frequency of failed matches under conditioning on a collision

Start at zero and choose an integer target for the next value \(Q(2)\) as an arbitrary function of the digit history before that block.
For \(k\) successive blocks of length \(n\), let \(D_k\) count failed matches.
Condition the original independent fair-bit differences on their \(nk\)-step return to zero.
For \(0<p<1/2\), with \(H(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)\), the conditioned probability satisfies
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac1k\log\Pr(D_k\le pk\mid y_{nk}=0)
       \le H(p)+pn\log2-n\log(2/r).                                   \tag{2}
\]
In particular any \(p\) below the unique root \(p_n^*\in(0,1/2)\) of \(H(p)+pn\log2=n\log(2/r)\) gives exponential rarity.
As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
                           p_n^*\sim\frac{3}{4\log2}\,2^{-n}.           \tag{3}
\]

For every past, the total weight of blocks hitting its one selected target is at most \(w=(r/2)^n\) by the binary count, while all blocks have total weight \(r^n=2^nw\).
Successively summing over each block with a specified set of \(j\) exceptions bounds its weight by \(w^k 2^{nj}\).
Hence the weight of all words with \(D_k\le pk\) is at most
\[
       w^k \sum_{0\le j\le pk}\binom kj 2^{nj}
             \le\exp\{k[\log w+H(p)+pn\log2]+o(k)\}.
\]
At the fixed length \(m=nk\), multiplication of the original path probabilities by \(r^m\) gives exactly the critical weights.
The denominator for conditioning is therefore \(r^m p_{m,1}\), where \(p_{m,1}\) is the collision probability of two independent fair-bit sums in base \(r\).
For fixed \(r\) of degree \(d\), the support of one such sum has size \(O_r(r^m m^{d-2})\).
Indeed in the Minkowski lattice of \(\mathbb Z[r]\) the expanding coordinate is \(O_r(r^m)\), the contracting coordinate is \(O_r(1)\), and each of the \(d-2\) real coordinates from the complex pairs is \(O(m)\).
Thickening their enclosing box by a bounded fundamental parallelepiped proves the count.
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(r^m p_{m,1}\gg_r m^{-(d-2)}\), which proves (2).

The left side of the root equation is continuous and strictly increasing on \([0,1/2]\).
The [family bound](Yin_L_G0462_v305.md#digits) \(r>\sqrt2\) gives \(0<n\log(2/r)<(n/2)\log2\), so the required root exists.
From \(2^n(2-r)\to3\), its right side is asymptotic to \((3/2)n2^{-n}\).
For each fixed \(c>0\), substituting \(p=c2^{-n}\) into the left side gives \((2c\log2+o(1))n2^{-n}\).
Bracketing the root proves (3).
Thus true zero returns typically leave every one-target matching rule a positive fraction of the time for each fixed \(n\).
The estimate gives a necessary frequency of other transitions and leaves their contribution to the strict collision deficit unresolved.
