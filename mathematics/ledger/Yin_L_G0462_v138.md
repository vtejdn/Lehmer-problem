# An endpoint obstruction for bounded-leading interlacers at Salem degree records

<a id="endpoint"></a>
## Moving the entire trace polynomial by one interlacer

Suppose Salem numbers \(\sigma\to1\) are minima among all Salem numbers of degree at most their own degree, as in the [record extraction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#records).
Write \(P=z^m S(z+z^{-1})\) for their minimal polynomials.
Fix \(L\ge1\).
Eventually every strict integer interlacer \(A\) of \(S\) with positive leading coefficient \(\ell\le L\) satisfies
\[
                          1\le |S(-2)|<|A(-2)|.
\]

In fact \(S_1=S+A\) is monic of degree \(m\).
At consecutive roots \(t_1<\cdots<t_m\) of \(S\) the signs of \(S_1(t_j)=A(t_j)\) are those of \(S'(t_j)\).
Thus \(S_1\) has one simple root below \(t_1\), one in every gap of \(S\), and its top root is strictly below \(T=t_m=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\).
Since \(-2<t_1\) and the first root of \(A\) is above \(t_1\), the signs of \(S(-2)\) and \(A(-2)\) are respectively \((-1)^m\) and \((-1)^{m-1}\).
If \(|S(-2)|\ge|A(-2)|\), the lowest root of \(S_1\) is at least \(-2\), as follows by the sign of \(S_1(-2)\) and the locations of its other roots.

If the top root of \(S_1\) is above two, its monic reciprocal lift has just one exterior root \(\tau\), with \(1<\tau<\sigma\), and one root below modulus one.
The irreducible factor of \(\tau\) has unit constant and must also contain \(\tau^{-1}\) by the product of its root moduli.
It gives a Salem number or a reciprocal quadratic unit, the latter having \(\tau+\tau^{-1}\ge3\) and hence being impossible for \(\sigma\to1\).
The Salem factor would violate minimality among all degrees at most \(2m\), including degree \(2m\) itself.
It remains to handle the case where every root of \(S_1\) belongs to \([-2,2]\).

<a id="torsion"></a>
## A torsion denominator of the same degree

By Kronecker's theorem \(S_1^\sharp=z^m S_1(z+z^{-1})\) is cyclotomic up to products and multiplicities.
At each root \(y\) of \(S_1\), strict alternation gives \(\rho_y=-S(y)/S_1'(y)>0\).
Expansion at infinity gives
\[
            \frac{S}{S_1}=1-\sum_y\frac{\rho_y}{x-y},
                  \qquad \sum_y\rho_y=\ell ,
\]
since \(S_1-S=A\) has degree \(m-1\) and leading coefficient \(\ell\).
All power moments \(\sum_y\rho_y y^j\) are integral by monicity of \(S_1\).
For each \(y=\xi_y+\xi_y^{-1}\) choose a root of unity \(\xi_y\).
Then \(F_j=\sum_y\rho_y(\xi_y^j+\xi_y^{-j})\) is integral for every integer \(j\) by the Chebyshev recurrence, is periodic, and has Fourier algebra norm at most \(2\ell\) by the positive masses.
The [bounded Fourier decomposition](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v38.md#sparse) therefore expresses \(F_j\) as a sum of a bounded number of signed congruence indicators in terms of \(L\).
Write \(\sum_{j\ge1}F_jz^j=\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{r_i}/(1-z^{d_i})\), with \(1\le r_i\le d_i\), and put \(V=\prod_i(1-z^{d_i})\), \(V_i=V/(1-z^{d_i})\).
The geometric series for each trace pole gives
\[
  W=(1-z^2)V-z\left(\ell V+\sum_i\epsilon_i z^{r_i}V_i\right),
       \qquad W S_1^\sharp=(1-z^2)V P .
\]
The integer polynomial \(W\) has constant one, bounded term count in terms of \(L\), and \(M(W)=M(P)=\sigma\).
The [bounded-term Mahler gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes a sequence of such \(\sigma\to1\).
Since \(S(-2)\) is a nonzero integer, this proves the endpoint obstruction.
In particular a bounded-leading interlacer with \(|A(-2)|=1\) is impossible at sufficiently small such records, regardless of the degrees of its nonmonic factors.
