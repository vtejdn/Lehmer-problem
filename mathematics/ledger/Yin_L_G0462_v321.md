# How a single real pole attains the relaxed quotient-energy bound

<a id="example"></a>
## A test of what the successive-row argument can detect

On 2026-11-20 the G0001 inquiry tested whether the [successive-row lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#statement) could be improved by using only the sizes of the nonzero coefficients of the rational Taylor germ.
For \(0<t<1\) let \(I(z)=(t-z)/(1-tz)\), and let \(N\) be the last index with \(\gamma_i=(1+t)t^{i-1}-1>0\).
Put \(F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^N\gamma_i z^{i-1}\) and \(H=F/I\).
The product of the moduli of the interior poles of \(H\) is \(t\), since \(F(t)>0\).
On the circle \(|I|=1\), so \(\|H\|_2^2=\sum_{i=1}^N\gamma_i^2\).
Its Taylor coefficients are all real and at least one.

Indeed the coefficients of \(I\) are \(t\) at zero and \(-(1-t^2)t^{j-1}\) at \(j\ge1\).
Multiplication by a prefix of \(N\) ones gives the first \(N\) coefficients \(\gamma_i\), so the coefficients of \(H\) at indices \(0,\ldots,N-1\) are one by triangularity.
The first zero coefficient of \(F\), at index \(N\), gives
\[
                      [z^N]H=(1+t)(1-t^N)/t\ge1 .
\]
For \(j>N\), the consecutive zero coefficients of \(F\) in \((t-z)H=(1-tz)F\) give \([z^j]H=t^{-1}[z^{j-1}]H\).
If all these coefficients were integers, their common ratio \(1/t\) would be rational and could have no denominator greater than one, since all its powers times the same nonzero integer are integers.
For \(1/t=k\in\mathbb Z\), \(k\ge2\), one has \(N=1\) and \([z]H=k-1/k\).
Thus this rational model attains the energy bound for every \(t\) and always fails the full integrality test.
Its pole and its Taylor tail isolate the arithmetic information lost when coefficient size replaces integrality.

## A necessary condition on an integral quotient near the floor

For an actual quotient use the notation \(F,h_i,f_i,s,e_i\) of the [weighted prefix calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v637.md#weights), with \(c_i=\gamma_i\).
Write \(n_i\) for its occupied exponents and \(S=\sum_{i=1}^N\gamma_i^2\).
Before replacing \(|h_i|\) by one, the same calculation gives the following accounting of the excess:
\[
 \|F\|_2^2-S\ \ge\
 \sum_{n\notin\{n_1,\ldots,n_N\}}|\widehat F(n)|^2
 +\sum_{i=1}^N(|f_i|-\gamma_i)^2
 +2\sum_{i=1}^N(t\gamma_i-se_i)(|h_i|-1).
\]
Indeed expand the two squares and telescope (4) there with the chosen weights.
Every term on the right is nonnegative and \(t\gamma_i-se_i>0\).
At any fixed admissible polynomial the first sum is positive, since \(F\) still has an exterior pole.
An exterior root of the irreducible denominator is a pole of the normalized quotient and cannot be a zero of the finite inner factor, whose zeros are all interior.
Thus an exact equality is unavailable there.
As \(m\to0\), this strict inequality alone places no lower bound on the excess as a fraction of \(1/m\).
For a stronger asymptotic constant, test whether the integral recurrence permits both a near-geometric tail and small excess outside the selected frequencies.
