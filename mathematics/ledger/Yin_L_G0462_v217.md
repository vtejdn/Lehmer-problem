# Logarithmic precision can retain false quartic trace divisors in every odd progression

<a id="precision"></a>
## Passing subsets of growing degree and growing precision

Use the [quartic pole test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v398.md#statement) at first exponent three.
Write
\[
 A_b=1+X^3-X^{12}+X^b(X^{12}+X^9-1),\qquad
 A_b=C_bP_b,\qquad P_b=X^{n_b}H_b(X+X^{-1}),
\]
where \(C_b\) is the cyclotomic part and \(H_b\) is the monic nontorsion trace polynomial.
For a monic trace divisor \(f\) let \(U(f)=(-1)^{\deg f}\operatorname{Res}(W,f)\) as in that test.
Throughout take odd \(b>18\) prime to three.
Each monic rational divisor has \(U=+1\), and each monic divisor modulo three gives a unique subset of the local Hensel factors since \(\bar H_b\) is squarefree.
Call a subset false if its monic product over \(\mathbb Z_3\) is not a rational divisor of \(H_b\).

Fix any arithmetic progression containing infinitely many such \(b\).
For every \(0<\lambda<1\) it contains exponents \(b\to\infty\) with false subsets of degrees \(j\) and \(n_b-j\) both passing the test \(U\equiv1\bmod 3^k\), where
\[
        \frac{k}{\log_3 b}\longrightarrow\lambda,\qquad
        \frac{j}{\log_3 b}\longrightarrow1-\lambda .                  \tag{1}
\]
There is also such a sequence with \(k/\log_3 b\to1,\ j\to\infty\).
In particular the least precision recovering all rational divisors, which [exists for all sufficiently large \(b\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v398.md#precision), has limsup ratio to \(\log_3 b\) at least one on every one of these progressions.
At each fixed precision the exponents with false subsets have positive lower density within the progression.

<a id="construction"></a>
## Retaining an earlier trace modulo a prime power

Choose a subprogression of fixed residue modulo 30 consisting of admissible odd exponents and let \(m\) be a period of this subprogression divisible by 30.
Its [cyclotomic part \(C_b\)](Yin_L_G0462_v212.md#simple) is constant, either \(X+1\) or \((X+1)\Phi_{10}\), and \(n_b=b/2+O(1)\).
Fix an exponent \(c\) there with \(j=n_c\ge1\).
The order \(t_c\) of \(X\) in \(\mathbb F_3[X]/(\bar A_c)\) exists by the unit constant term.
It satisfies
\[
                           t_c\le10\,\mathrm e\,j\,3^j .                \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(\bar A_c\) is squarefree by its derivative and the unit pole resultant, and the roots of the removed cyclotomic part \(\bar C_c\) have orders dividing 10.
For an irreducible trace constituent of degree \(u\) the \(3^u\)-power map fixes the trace and hence fixes or inverts either quadratic lift.
The common order of all those lifts divides \(3^u-1\) or \(3^u+1\).
Thus \(t_c\le10\prod_i(3^{u_i}+1)\), with \(\sum_i u_i=j\).
At most \(3^u/u\) different irreducibles of degree \(u\) occur, since they supply disjoint sets of \(u\) roots in \(\mathbb F_{3^u}\).
It follows that \(\sum_i\log(1+3^{-u_i})\le\sum_{u=1}^j 1/u\le1+\log j\), giving (2).

For \(k\ge1\) let \(D_{c,k}=m t_c3^{k-1}\).
Every \(b>c\) with \(b\equiv c\bmod D_{c,k}\) satisfies
\[
                      H_c\mid H_b\quad\text{in }(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z)[Y].
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
In the monic quotient by \(A_c\) one has \(X^{t_c}\equiv1\bmod3\).
Each cubing gains at least one further power of three by the binomial formula, so \(X^{b-c}=1\bmod(3^k,A_c)\).
Evaluating the section there gives \(A_c\mid A_b\bmod3^k\).
The same monic \(C_b=C_c\) can be cancelled over the composite coefficient ring to give the divisibility for \(P_c,P_b\).
After multiplying by monomial units, comparison in the free basis \(1,X\) over the trace variable gives (3), as in the [Laurent-ring descent](Yin_L_G0462_v207.md#moduli).

The old trace \(H_c\) has integer signed norm \(+1\) by the [odd pole sign](Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#sign).
In (3) its reduction and its complement are coprime modulo three, so they are the unique monic lifts of their two subsets at precision \(k\).
The old trace passes at this precision, and the complement passes by multiplicativity since the whole new trace also has norm \(+1\).
Every rational nontorsion irreducible factor of \(P_b\) has degree \(\gg b\) by the [quartic degree and exterior count comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#count), using \(a=3\) and at least one exterior root.
A rational trace divisor of degree \(j>0\) would give a nontorsion polynomial divisor of degree \(2j\).
Consequently both subsets are false for all sufficiently large \(b\) along any such construction with \(j=o(b)\).
A rational complementary divisor would give a rational divisor of degree \(j\) by monic division.
For fixed \(c,k\), all sufficiently late \(b\) in the subprogression modulo \(D_{c,k}\) have this obstruction, which gives the positive lower density assertion.

For (1), take \(c\to\infty\) in the chosen subprogression and \(k=\lceil\lambda j/(1-\lambda)\rceil\).
Choose the first \(b\equiv c\bmod D_{c,k}\) at or above \(B=j^2 3^{k+j}\).
By (2) the spacing is \(O_m(j3^{k+j})\), giving \(b/B=1+O_m(1/j)\).
Now \(j=o(b)\) and \(\log_3 b=k+j+O(\log j)\), proving both limits.
Choosing \(k=j^2\) gives the endpoint with \(k/\log_3 b\to1\) and still unbounded \(j\).
These are false subsets at the specified precisions whether or not the new rational core splits in a different degree.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## A square-root upper bound for copying factors between exponents

For any distinct positive exponents \(b,c\) prime to three, let \(N\) be the prime-to-three part of \(|b-c|\).
Their nontorsion traces satisfy the finite bound
\[
                   \deg\gcd(\bar H_b,\bar H_c)\le2\sqrt N+2.           \tag{4}
\]
Here the bars denote reduction modulo three, and either parity is allowed with its own cyclotomic part removed.
For a common parent root \(x\), the unit constant and pole resultant give \(x^{b-c}=1\), hence \(x^N=1\).
The section equation in characteristic three is
\[
                   x^c=f(x)^3,\qquad
                   f(X)=\frac{X^4-X-1}{X^4+X^3-1}.
\]
Choose \(e\) with \(3e\equiv c\bmod N\).
Injectivity of cubing gives \(f(x)=x^e\).
For any integer \(J\ge1\), two of \(0,e,\ldots,Je\bmod N\) differ on the residue circle by at most \(N/(J+1)\), by considering the \(J+1\) cyclic gaps, allowing a repeated residue.
Thus there are integers \(u,v\) with \(u+ev\equiv0\bmod N\), \(|u|\le N/(J+1)\), \(1\le|v|\le J\).
All common roots solve \(x^u f(x)^v=1\).
This is a nonconstant rational function since \(f\) has a finite nonzero zero disjoint from its poles.
Indeed a common zero of its numerator and denominator would satisfy \(X^3+X=0\), and neither zero nor either square root of \(-1\) solves the numerator.
After clearing denominators the equation has degree at most \(|u|+4|v|\).
With \(J=\lceil\sqrt N/2\rceil\) this bounds the number of common parent roots by \(4\sqrt N+4\).
Both parent reductions are squarefree and a root of a reciprocal trace core lifts to two distinct roots, since a double quadratic lift would contradict that squarefreeness.
Dividing by two proves (4).
In particular copying a trace factor of degree comparable to \(b\) from a distinct \(c=O(b)\) is impossible for large \(b\).

## When local factor degrees permit false subsets near one third

For an admissible odd \(b>18\) let \(d_{\max}\) be the largest irreducible degree of \(\bar H_b\) and set \(g_k=2\cdot3^{k-1}\).
For any \(0<\alpha<1\) with \(\alpha n_b+3g_k d_{\max}<n_b\), at least one false subset passes \(U\equiv1\bmod3^k\) and has degree in
\[
                    [\alpha n_b,\ \alpha n_b+3g_k d_{\max}].           \tag{5}
\]
In fact the signed values of the individual Hensel factors lie in the group \((\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z)^\times\) of order \(g_k\), with total product one.
Partition them into minimal nonempty sequences of product one, successively removing such a sequence from the remaining product.
Each has length at most \(g_k\), since its prefix products starting with the empty product and ending just before its last term must all be distinct.
Each block thus has degree at most \(g_k d_{\max}\).
The first union of consecutive blocks reaching \(\alpha n_b\) and the next two larger such unions have different degrees in (5) and are all proper by the strict upper bound.
Each passes the test.
There are at most two proper nonconstant rational trace divisors because the [quartic splitting criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#quartic) allows at most two simple nontorsion rational factors.
This proves (5).

For a sequence with \(3^k d_{\max}=o(n_b)\), (5) at \(\alpha=1/3\) gives a false subset of degree \(n_b/3+o(n_b)\).
In a possible split the sign-changed Salem factor has one exterior root of log modulus tending to \((\log\beta_4)/3\).
The [height limit](Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(b h\to\log\beta_4\), since both reciprocal blocks have the same modulus on the circle and the [quartic denominator](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic) has the single exterior zero \(-\beta_4\).
Hence its rational trace degree divided by \(n_b\) tends to \(1/3\) along any such splits with \(b\to\infty\).
The factor-size hypothesis for (5) at this scale is additional.
Whether false subsets at this fraction and unbounded precision can be forced in a prescribed progression remains open.
