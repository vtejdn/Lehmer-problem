# Volumes of the signed row lattices at every fixed width

<a id="volume"></a>
## An exact product of successive minima

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum of degree \(e=2s\), with representatives \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\) of the inverse pairs multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.
Write \(t_1\ge\cdots\ge t_s\) for the decreasing rearrangement of \(|\log|x_i||\), \(h=\sum_jt_j=m(P)\), and \(F(P)=\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)t_j\), the [least-cost rate at width \(e\)](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#rate).
Fix an integer \(w\ge e\), put \(d=w+1-e\) and write \(A_w^*(Y)=Y^wA(1/Y)\).
For each sign \(\varepsilon\), let \(\Lambda_{\varepsilon,p,w}\) consist of the integer coefficient vectors of rows \(A\) of degree at most \(w\) with \(P^2\mid A(X^p)+\varepsilon X A_w^*(X^p)\).
For every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\) it is a saturated rank-\(d\) lattice, whose successive Euclidean minima satisfy
\[
       \lim_{p\to\infty}\frac1p\log\prod_{j=1}^d\lambda_{j,\varepsilon}
                                =F(P)+(w-e)h.                           \tag{1}
\]
The limit fixes \(P,w\) and controls the product of the minima.

In the splitting field \(L\), form the matrix \(M_{w,\varepsilon}\) with columns \(a=0,\ldots,w\) and two rows at each \(i\), of entries
\[
 Y_i^a+c_iY_i^{w-a},\qquad NaY_i^a+c_i\{1+N(w-a)\}Y_i^{w-a},
                  \qquad c_i=\varepsilon x_i.
\]
Its maximal minors have no common nonconstant factor in \(L[N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s]\).
For this assertion set \(k=w-e\), and let \(g\) be either \((1+Y)^k\) or \((1-Y)^k\).
Write \(g_k^*=\delta g\), with \(\delta=1\) in the first case and \(\delta=(-1)^k\) in the second.
Multiplying a degree-\(e\) row by \(g\) acts on its coefficients by a constant \((w+1)\)-by-\((e+1)\) matrix \(T_g\).
At each node the two corresponding blocks of \(M_{w,\varepsilon}T_g\) and \(M_{e,\varepsilon\delta}\) differ by left multiplication by
\[
             \begin{pmatrix}g(Y_i)&0\\ NY_i g'(Y_i)&g(Y_i)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed the signed value of \(gA\) is \(g\) times the value for \(A\) with sign \(\varepsilon\delta\), and the second row differentiates this product by the same Euler rule.
If an irreducible polynomial \(B\) divided all maximal minors of \(M_{w,\varepsilon}\), Cauchy–Binet would put it in every maximal minor of \(M_{w,\varepsilon}T_g\).
These are the corresponding minors of \(M_{e,\varepsilon\delta}\) multiplied by \(\prod_i g(Y_i)^2\).
The [absence of common factors at width \(e\)](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) implies \(B\mid\prod_i g(Y_i)^2\).
The two choices for \(g\) give relatively prime products, which proves the assertion, also when \(k=0\).

## The outermost column pairs determine every support weight

Give a monomial in the \(Y_i\) the weight \(\sum_i a_i u_i\) for its exponent vector \((a_i)\), leaving its polynomial coefficient in \(N\) unweighted.
For \(u\in\mathbb R^s\) write \(v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_s\) for the ordered \(|u_i|\).
The maximum weight of the union of the supports of all maximal minors is
\[
                      w\sum_i u_i+\sum_{j=1}^s(w-2j+2)v_j.              \tag{2}
\]
For a column \(a\) assigned to node \(i\), the weight is at most \(wu_i/2+|a-w/2|\,|u_i|\).
Each node receives two different columns in a term of a maximal minor.
The \(e\) largest distances from \(w/2\) among all the columns consist of two copies of \(w/2-l\) for \(0\le l<s\).
Rearranging these distances against the doubled list of absolute weights gives the upper bound in (2), for odd and even \(w\).

Take the single minor formed by columns \(l,w-l\), \(0\le l<s\).
For positive distinct \(|u_i|\) the node with \(j\)-th largest absolute weight must receive the two columns with \(l=j-1\).
When \(u_i>0\), choosing the higher power \(w-l\) in both columns gives leading column vectors \((c_i,c_i(1+N(w-l)))^\mathsf T\) and \((1,N(w-l))^\mathsf T\).
Their determinant is \(-c_i\).
When \(u_i<0\), the lower power \(l\) in both columns gives \((1,Nl)^\mathsf T\) and \((c_i,c_i(1+Nl))^\mathsf T\), with determinant \(c_i\).
Thus the complete leading monomial has coefficient \(\pm\prod_i c_i\ne0\), since only permutations within these assigned column pairs can contribute.
The same minor attains (2) for tied and zero weights by continuity of the support functions.

At \(N=p,\ Y_i=x_i^p\), the signed matrix tests the graph and its derivative at the representatives, and reversal about \(wp+1\) supplies both equations at their inverses.
The resulting kernel is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) by the remainder modulo \(P^2\).
Multiplicative independence modulo torsion makes each nonzero polynomial specialize to a nondegenerate exponential polynomial with polynomial coefficients, by collecting the exponents of \(\mathbf Y\).
The [local recurrence estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives eventual nonvanishing and the log maximum of the minors at each embedding \(\sigma\) as \(p\) times (2) at \(u_i=\log|\sigma x_i|\), plus \(o_{P,w}(p)\).
In particular the eventual rank is \(e\).
The [finite-place estimate for a coprime tuple](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#support) applies because the minors are integral and have polynomial gcd one up to scalar.
Indeed one nonzero minor and an integer linear combination avoiding its irreducible factors form a coprime pair, the outside-archimedean height cost of the specialization is \(\log p\), and its height is at least \(ph/e\).
The [almost-unit gcd theorem](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) then gives normalized finite common valuation sum \(o_{P,w}(p)\), with a bounded contribution if one of the pair is constant.
For each tolerance the proper exceptional set is avoided eventually by the same nonvanishing argument.
Since Galois permutes and inverts representatives, the ordered \(|u_i|\) at every embedding are \(t_1,\ldots,t_s\).
Each \(u_i\) averages to zero by the unit norm, so the projective logarithmic height of the maximal-minor tuple is \(p\sum_j(w-2j+2)t_j+o_{P,w}(p)\).

These minors, indexed by their complementary columns and given the corresponding signs, are Plücker coordinates of the rational kernel.
Its lattice of all integral points is saturated because a positive integral multiple lies in the kernel exactly when the vector itself does.
A basis of this lattice extends to a basis of the ambient integral lattice, so its wedge product is primitive.
The largest coordinate in absolute value has logarithm equal to the projective height just computed, and the Euclidean norm of the wedge is the lattice covolume \(\Delta\).
The two norms differ by at most \(\sqrt{\binom{w+1}{d}}\).

For completeness, \(\Delta\le\prod_j\lambda_j\) by taking independent vectors at the successive minima and bounding their spanned volume by the product of their lengths.
For the reverse inequality project the lattice orthogonally off a shortest vector of length \(\lambda_1\), which is primitive.
The projected lattice has covolume \(\Delta/\lambda_1\) by extending that vector to an integral basis.
Any nonzero projected vector has a lift with parallel component of length at most \(\lambda_1/2\), hence its projected length is at least \(\sqrt3\,\lambda_1/2\) and that lift has length at most \(2/\sqrt3\) times the projected length.
Lifting independent projected vectors at their successive minima and adjoining the shortest vector gives, by induction on \(d\), \(\prod_j\lambda_j\le(2/\sqrt3)^{d(d-1)/2}\Delta\).
Taking logarithms proves (1), since \(\sum_j(w-2j+2)t_j=F(P)+(w-e)h\).

## Coprime rows one degree above the seed

Write \(H_{p,e+1}(P)\) for the least uncapped cost of coprime integral pairs of width at most \(e+1\) with the same double factor and nonzero reflected determinant as in the [coefficient test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement).
Then
\[
          \limsup_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log H_{p,e+1}(P)}p
                          \le \frac{F(P)+h}{2}<F(P).                    \tag{3}
\]
For a Salem seed the upper rate is \((e+1)h/2\).

Let \(B_+,B_-\) be the primitive width-\(e\) generators and put \(R_\varepsilon=(B_\varepsilon,\varepsilon(B_\varepsilon)_e^*)\).
At every sufficiently large \(p\) their polynomial determinant is nonzero, and each generator has logarithmic rate \(F(P)\) by the [minimum-width theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v254.md#rate).
With \(J=1+Y,\ K=Y-1\), a basis of signed polynomial pairs at width \(e+1\) for sign \(\varepsilon\) is \(J R_\varepsilon,K R_{-\varepsilon}\).
Their membership follows from the two reversal signs, their independence from the width-\(e\) determinant, and their spanning from the plane dimension in (1).
Both first coordinates are primitive by Gauss's lemma and retain rate \(F(P)\), since multiplying or dividing by either fixed linear polynomial changes their one-norms by bounded factors at this fixed degree.
As \(F(P)\ge(s+1)h>h\), a nonzero integral row of rate at most \((F(P)+h)/2\) must eventually avoid each of these two lines.

First suppose \(s\ge2\).
In each signed plane choose a shortest integral first coordinate with its signed reversed partner.
The first-coordinate norms satisfy the upper rate in (3) by (1).
Write \(Q=\min(x_1^p)\), which is reciprocal of degree \(e\) at every \(p\) by independence.
If \(Q\mid A\) for a nonzero signed first coordinate in either plane, write \(A=Q(c_0+c_1Y)\).
The factor \(Q(X^p)\) has only simple zeros at the original conjugates, so after removing it the remaining signed graph must vanish at every \(x_i\).
For \(c_0,c_1\) its equation at \(x_i\) has coefficients \(1+\varepsilon x_iY_i,\ Y_i+\varepsilon x_i\), evaluated at \(Y_i=x_i^p\).
The rows at \(i=1,2\) have generically nonzero determinant because each ratio of their two entries varies with its own independent variable, using \(x_i^2\ne1\).
Their determinant remains nonzero at all sufficiently large \(p\) by the same recurrence estimate, a contradiction.
Thus the chosen rows at both signs have \(Q\nmid A\).

For \(s=1\), choose \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|>1\) and \(z=\alpha^p\), so \(h=\log|\alpha|\) and \(F(P)=2h\).
A nonzero integral signed row \(A\) with \(Q\nmid A\) has nonzero values there, and the [single-row norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#statement) at \(e=2,\ w=3,\ m=2,\ D=2h\) gives lower rate \(3h/2\).
For \(Q\mid A\), monic division gives an integral nonzero linear or constant \(C=c_0+c_1Y\).
The simple-root equation gives
\[
        |\varepsilon\alpha c_0+c_1|=|c_0+\varepsilon\alpha c_1|/|z|
                           \ll_P\|C\|_1/|z|.
\]
The form on the left is a nonzero quadratic-field integer, since \(\alpha\) is irrational, and its conjugate is \(O_P(\|C\|_1)\).
Its nonzero integral norm implies \(\|C\|_1\gg_P|z|^{1/2}\).
Hence \(\|A\|_1\ge M(A)=M(Q)M(C)\ge|z|\|C\|_1/2\gg_P|z|^{3/2}\), with \(M\) the multiplicative Mahler measure.
Every nonzero integral row in the signed plane thus has lower rate \(3h/2\) uniformly in that row.
By (1) both successive minima of each plane have rate \(3h/2\).
The simple-root equation is a nonzero linear condition on \(c_0,c_1\), so \(Q\)-divisible first coordinates form at most a line in either plane.
Among two independent rows of lengths at most the second minimum choose one outside that line at each sign.
Their rate \(3h/2\) is strictly below the two basis-direction rates \(2h\).

For either case call the chosen signed pairs \(S_+,S_-\).
As in the [octic plane construction](Yin_L_G0462_v255.md#bounds), write \(S_+=aJ R_++bK R_-\) and \(S_-=cJ R_-+d'K R_+\), with all four scalars nonzero by the strict rate separation.
Their determinant is \((acJ^2-bd'K^2)\det(R_+,R_-)\ne0\) because \(J^2,K^2\) are linearly independent.
Their integral sum \((U,V)\) has the common double-root graph, and its swapped reversal is \(S_+-S_-\).
The determinant of this sum with its swapped reversal is therefore nonzero, giving \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\).
If \(Q\) divided both entries, its reciprocity would also make it divide the signed first coordinates recovered by adding and subtracting the swapped reversal.
Both entries are nonzero, since a zero entry would force the other to vanish at \(x_1^p\) by the graph equation, putting both in \((Q)\).
Thus their primitive common polynomial divisor \(G\) is coprime to \(Q\), so \(G(X^p)\) is coprime to \(P\).
Dividing \(U,V\) by \(G\) preserves the double factor and removes \(G(Y)G(1/Y)\) from the nonzero Laurent determinant.
At fixed degree the quotient costs are bounded, for example by \(\|U/G\|_1\le2^{e+1}M(U/G)\le2^{e+1}\|U\|_1\) and the analogous inequality for \(V\).
This proves (3) with genuine coprime pairs.
In degree two the same single-row lower bound applies to every such genuine pair, so its least-cost rate at width three is exactly \(3h/2\).
