# Exterior counts throughout the reciprocal sections and the quartic Salem factor

<a id="count"></a>
## Exterior counts and degrees in all directions

Fix \(s\ge3\), \(g(T)=T^s-T-1\), \(B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and put
\[
        F_{a,b}(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a),\qquad r=b/a,\qquad a,b\ge1 .
\]
For any nontorsion monic rational irreducible factor \(P\) of \(F_{a,b}\), let \(N(P)\) count its roots of modulus strictly greater than one.
Write \(m(P)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure and \(d=\deg P\).
Uniformly over all positive exponents and all these factors,
\[
                    m(P)\asymp_s \frac{N(P)}a
                           \asymp_s\frac{d}{a+b}.                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular for every \(R>0\) there is \(c_{s,R}>0\) such that \(N(P)\ge c_{s,R}a\) whenever \(r\le R\), including fixed \(b\) and unbounded \(a\).
Hence any sequence of distinct pairs \((a,b)\) admitting factors with bounded exterior count must have \(r\to\infty\).

To see the uniformity as \(r\) decreases to zero, put \(w=e^{T/a}z\) with \(T\ge2\), \(|z|=1\).
Dividing \(-F_{a,b}(w)\) by \(w^{sa+b}\) leaves \(1-w^{-b}\) plus four monomials with reciprocal exponents at least \(a\).
Their sum has absolute value at most \(4e^{-T}\).
Jensen's formula on this circle gives, counting all roots with multiplicity,
\[
 \sum_{\alpha:F_{a,b}(\alpha)=0}\bigl(\log|\alpha|-T/a\bigr)_+
       =\int_{\mathbb T}\log|w^{-sa-b}F_{a,b}(w)|\,dm(z)
       \ll (1+T)e^{-T},                                             \tag{2}
\]
where \(m\) in the integral is Haar probability and the implied constant is absolute.
Indeed \(w^{-b}\) has the distribution of \(u e^{i\theta}\) under uniform \(\theta\), with \(u=e^{-rT}<1\), and the mean of \(\log|1-u e^{i\theta}|\) is zero.
The excess is bounded above by the mean of \(\log(1+4e^{-T}/|1-u e^{i\theta}|)\).
For \(u<1/2\) the denominator is at least \(1/2\), and otherwise its square is \((1-u)^2+4u\sin^2(\theta/2)\gg\theta^2\) for \(|\theta|\le\pi\).
With \(\epsilon=4e^{-T}\), integration of \(\log(1+C\epsilon/|\theta|)\) on \(|\theta|\le\epsilon\) gives \(O(\epsilon)\) by rescaling, and on the remainder gives \(O(\epsilon\log(\pi/\epsilon))\).
This proves (2) uniformly even when the leading binomial approaches a zero on the contour.

Every factor is a unit polynomial by the parent endpoints.
For \(r\le R\) its measure satisfies \(c_{s,R}'\le m(P)\le\log6\), by the [separated leading block and the endpoint estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#criterion) and the parent coefficient length.
For any chosen factor the nonnegative tail in (2) gives \(m(P)\le T N(P)/a+C(1+T)e^{-T}\).
Fix \(T\) large enough that the last term is below \(c_{s,R}'/2\).
This gives \(N(P)\gg_{s,R}a\), and \(N(P)\le d\le sa+b\) gives both comparisons in (1) for bounded slopes.
On an infinite subsequence of distinct pairs with \(r\le R\), \(a\) tends to infinity.

At sufficiently large primitive slopes the [allocation among exterior poles](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation) gives \(m(P)\asymp_s N(P)/a\), since the finitely many exterior poles all have positive logarithmic modulus.
The [individual height limit](Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(bm(P)/d\to I_s>0\) uniformly and \(d\gg_s a\sqrt r\).
The [power-substitution theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#statement) applies for all primitive directions of sufficiently large slope.
Consequently for every common dilation \(q\), the nontorsion factors become exactly \(P(X^q)\), with the same measure, \(qN(P)\) exterior roots and degree \(qd\).
This proves (1) in the remaining directions.

<a id="real"></a>
## The unique real exterior branch

For all sufficiently large primitive slopes \(r\), there is a real exterior root exactly when \(s\) is even and \(a\) is odd, and in that case there is exactly one.
It is negative.
Let \(\beta_s>1\) be the unique solution of \(\beta_s^{s-1}(\beta_s-1)=1\) for even \(s\).
For the negative root \(-\sigma\), write \(u=\sigma^a\), \(V_s(u)=u^{s-1}(u-1)-1\) and \(G_s(u)=u^s+u-1\).
Then, uniformly as \(r\to\infty\),
\[
 u=\beta_s+(-1)^b
       \frac{G_s(\beta_s)}{V_s'(\beta_s)}\,\beta_s^{-r}
           +O_s(r\beta_s^{-2r}).                                     \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(\sigma\) lies above \(\beta_s^{1/a}\) for even \(b\) and below it for odd \(b\).

By the [exterior allocation theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#allocation), every exterior root has \(\alpha^a\) arbitrarily close to an exterior zero of \(B\), uniformly for large \(r\).
There is no positive real such zero since \(B(u)<0\) for \(u>1\).
For \(u>1\), \(B(-u)=1+u^{s-1}(u-1)>0\) when \(s\) is odd, and \(B(-u)=-V_s(u)\) when \(s\) is even.
Thus the only possible real exterior root occurs when \(s\) is even and \(a\) odd and has \(u\) close to \(\beta_s\).
Its exact equation is
\[
                 V_s(u)=(-1)^b u^{-r}G_s(u).
\]
On a fixed small real interval around \(\beta_s\) contained in \((1,\infty)\), \(V_s'\) is positive and bounded away from zero.
The right-hand side and its derivative tend uniformly to zero there, so the difference is strictly increasing and has opposite signs at the endpoints.
This proves existence and uniqueness, and positivity of \(G_s\) gives the indicated sign.
The allocation estimate initially gives \(u-\beta_s=O_s(e^{-c_s r})\).
Consequently \(u^{-r}/\beta_s^{-r}=1+o_s(1)\), and the exact equation first improves the displacement to \(O_s(\beta_s^{-r})\).
Taylor expansion then gives \(u^{-r}/\beta_s^{-r}=1+O_s(r\beta_s^{-r})\), proving (3).

For these primitive slopes every nontorsion irreducible factor is reciprocal and has unit-circle conjugates.
Indeed there are just \(2at_s\) off-circle roots in the parent, with \(t_s\) the fixed number of exterior zeros of \(B\), whereas [every factor degree is \(\gg_s a\sqrt r\)](Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height).
A factor containing a unit-circle root shares that root with its reversal, since its coefficients are real, so irreducibility gives reciprocity up to sign.
A single exterior root must be real by complex conjugation and must therefore be the negative root above.
In that case the sign change makes \(\sigma\) a Salem number with conjugates consisting of itself, its positive inverse and roots on the unit circle.
Conversely if \(\sigma\) is Salem, the factor containing \(-\sigma\) has a single exterior root.

## The accumulation points required by single-exterior factors

Write \(S\) for the set of Pisot numbers, \(T\) for the set of Salem numbers and \(T'\) for its derived set in the real line.
Boyd suggests \(T'=S\), and recalls Siegel's theorem that the least member of \(S\) is the real root \(\theta>1\) of \(X^3-X-1\), on printed page 455 (PDF page 3) of his [published paper](../literature/pdf/Boy81--published.pdf#page=3).
We use the proposed equality only for its conditional implication \(T'\cap[1,\theta)=\varnothing\).
The [survey's account of the derived-set conjecture](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#closedness-and-delay-one) also distinguishes it from closedness of \(S\cup T\).

For fixed \(s\), any infinite sequence of distinct pairs admitting a factor with one exterior root has \(r\to\infty\) by (1).
In sufficiently large primitive directions all common dilations preserve the irreducibility of every nontorsion factor by [C391](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#statement).
For a dilation by \(q\) the factor \(P(X^q)\) has exactly \(qN(P)\) exterior roots, and substitution into a cyclotomic factor produces only torsion roots.
Reducing a direction by its common divisor preserves \(r\), so all late pairs with a single exterior root must themselves be primitive.
The real branch then forces \(s\) even and \(a\) odd.

If \(a\to\infty\) along a subsequence, (3) gives actual Salem numbers \(\sigma>1\) tending to one, and hence a subsequence of distinct Salem numbers decreasing to one.
For fixed \(a\), infinitely many such factors instead give \(\beta_s^{1/a}\in T'\).
Indeed their degrees tend to infinity by the factor-degree bound, so a finite set of Salem numbers cannot supply this sequence.
Formula (3) gives the limit, with its side determined by \(b\).
For every even \(s\ge6\) one has \(\beta_s\le\beta_6<\theta\), since \(\theta^5(\theta-1)=\theta>1\).
For \(s=4\) and odd \(a\ge3\), one has \(\beta_4<2<\theta^3\), hence \(\beta_4^{1/a}<\theta\).
Consequently, under \(T'\cap[1,\theta)=\varnothing\), only finitely many pairs at each fixed \(s\) admit a single exterior root in a rational irreducible factor, apart from the family \(s=4,\ a=1\).
This covers all positive \(a,b\) by taking a constant or unbounded subsequence of \(a\).
The production of a convergent Salem sequence from an infinite supply of actual factors is unconditional.

## When the quartic section at \(a=3\) splits

For \(s=4\) the denominator \(B\) has just one exterior zero, \(-\beta_4\).
In fact \(g(X)=X^4-X-1\) has only the two real roots \(-1/\beta_4\) and \(\gamma>1\), as its derivative has just one real zero.
Since \(g(\beta_4)=\beta_4^3-\beta_4>0\), one has \(\gamma<\beta_4\).
The nonreal conjugate pair of roots of \(g\) has squared modulus \(\beta_4/\gamma>1\) by the product of the four roots.
Reversing \(g\) proves the assertion about \(B\), and also shows that \(\beta_4\) is Pisot.
There are therefore precisely \(a\) exterior roots in each primitive quartic section with large \(r\), and every nontorsion factor receives at least one.

For \(a=1\) there is a single simple nontorsion factor for all sufficiently large \(b\), and after changing sign its exterior root is Salem and tends to \(\beta_4\).
For \(a=2\) and sufficiently large odd \(b\), both exterior roots are nonreal and form a conjugate pair.
They must belong to the same simple rational irreducible factor, which is again the entire cyclotomic-free part.
These cases also follow from the [fixed-block single pole or conjugate pair criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v193.md#statement).

For \(a=3,\ 3\nmid b\) and \(b\) sufficiently large, the cyclotomic-free part splits over \(\mathbb Q\) **if and only if** the modulus \(\sigma\) of the unique negative exterior root is a Salem number.
There are exactly three exterior roots, one real and one nonreal conjugate pair.
Any splitting therefore assigns the real root to one factor and the pair to a second factor.
Conversely a Salem factor containing the real root leaves the nonreal pair to a different rational factor.
In the split case there are exactly two simple nontorsion factors.
The Salem numbers obtained from any infinite sequence of such splits tend to \(\beta_4^{1/3}<9/8\), which lies below Lehmer's number.
Indeed \(\beta_4<7/5<(9/8)^3\), and Lehmer's polynomial \(X^{10}+X^9-X^7-X^6-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1\) takes the value \(-941480999/8^{10}\) at \(9/8\).
Thus even a single sufficiently late split gives a Salem number below Lehmer's number, and infinitely many splits would contradict Boyd's proposed derived set.
For odd \(a\ge5\) at the quartic seed, the exterior count permits the real root to share its factor with a conjugate exterior pair while another pair goes to a second factor.
The single-root limit obstruction leaves such partitions undecided.
