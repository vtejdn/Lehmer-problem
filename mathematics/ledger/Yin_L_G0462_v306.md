# Real embeddings bound the integer dilations of a marked exponent pattern

<a id="dilations"></a>
## Fixed outgoing coefficients and an output of bounded degree

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) have \(R\) real embeddings, and let \(p\) be the largest number of real embeddings whose images of \(\alpha\) are pairwise multiplicatively dependent, when \(R>0\).
Fix nonzero \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\in K\), with arbitrary heights, and integers \(v_1\le\cdots\le v_s\), not all zero.
Let \(J\) be the set of positive integers \(q\) for which \(c_q=\sum_i a_i\alpha^{qv_i}\) is nonzero of absolute degree at most \(B\) and the outgoing terms have no nonempty vanishing subsum.
If \(R>pB\), then
\[
            |J|\le \frac{(4s-2)B}{1-pB/R}.
\]
In particular \(R\ge2pB\) gives \(|J|\le(8s-4)B\).
For \(B=1\) and \(R>p\), one also has \(|J|\le4s-2\) using just one pair of real embeddings.
The coefficients need no degree bound for these dilation estimates.

To prove them assume \(J\ne\varnothing\) and combine coefficients at each equal exponent \(v\) into \(a(v)\).
All these sums are nonzero, since even one admissible \(q\) precludes a zero group.
For two independent real images \(x=\sigma(\alpha)\), \(y=\tau(\alpha)\), equality \(\sigma(c_q)=\tau(c_q)\) on a fixed parity of \(q\) gives a real exponential sum at \(q\),
\[
   \sum_v \sigma(a(v))\operatorname{sgn}(x)^{qv} (|x|^v)^q
    -\sum_v \tau(a(v))\operatorname{sgn}(y)^{qv} (|y|^v)^q=0.
\]
The signs are constant on this parity, including for negative exponents.
The bases within either side are distinct since real nontorsion conjugates have absolute value different from one.
No nonzero-exponent base can coincide across the sides: equality of the absolute powers would give a multiplicative relation after squaring.
At least one such exponent occurs, with nonzero coefficient, so after collecting a possible common constant this is a nonzero sum with at most \(2s\) distinct positive bases.
A sum of \(k\) real exponentials with distinct real exponents and nonzero coefficients has at most \(k-1\) distinct real zeros, by dividing out one exponential and applying Rolle's theorem inductively to the derivative.
Thus in both parities combined each independent pair accounts for at most \(4s-2\) integers.

For every \(q\in J\), the \(R\) images of \(c_q\) take at most \(B\) values.
Cauchy–Schwarz on their class sizes gives at least \(R^2/B\) ordered pairs of equal images including the diagonal.
Of these at most \(pR\) are dependent pairs, since multiplicative dependence of two nontorsion elements is equivalence up to a rational power and torsion.
Summing over any finite subset of \(J\) and using the pair bound on at most \(R^2\) pairs gives the displayed estimate.
For rational outputs every pair has equal images, so fixing a single independent pair suffices whenever there is one.

<a id="records"></a>
## Primitive shapes on inputs with several real embeddings

Always \(p\le4\), since all conjugates have the same positive height and multiplicatively dependent ones must therefore be equal up to inversion and a torsion factor; the only real torsion factors are signs.
At a strict degree record for positive unit measure one has \(p\le2\).
Indeed distinct conjugates \(x,-x\) would give \([K:\mathbb Q(\alpha^2)]\ge2\), whence \(m(\alpha^2)=2m(\alpha)/[K:\mathbb Q(\alpha^2)]\le m(\alpha)\) at strictly smaller degree, contradicting the record.
Among \(x,-x,x^{-1},-x^{-1}\) any three include such an opposite pair.
For two reciprocal real conjugates alone, this comparison can have no independent pair, so it does not cover all Salem inputs.

Use the [terminal relations with a distinguished term at zero](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v584.md#statement), including its hypotheses \(1\le B\le d_*/4\), \(Bm\to0\), \(m=\log M(\alpha)\to0\).
For each fixed coefficient list achieving its maximum \(W_r\), divide each nonzero sorted outgoing exponent list by the gcd of the absolute values of its entries, retaining negative entries, order, repetitions and the marked origin.
Here a nonzero list means one with at least one nonzero entry; zero individual entries are permitted.
Write \(V_r\) for the number of resulting primitive lists for those same coefficients, including the all-zero list just once if it occurs.
On inputs with \(R\ge2pB\), or with \(B=1\) and \(R>p\), the dilation bounds show \(W_r\le 8rB V_r\).
The all-zero case contributes at most one actual list independently of the formal dilation.
The cited terminal-count result supplies weights \(r\to\infty\) with \(W_r\ge(c_0/m)^{r-2}\), and therefore
\[
             \log V_r\ge (1-o(1))(r-2)\log(1/m).
\]
Indeed \(Bm\to0\) gives \(\log B\le\log(1/m)\) eventually, and \(\log r=o(r)\).
This allows growing degree bounds and arbitrary heights of the fixed outgoing coefficients, including cases in which the unit has no conjugate on the unit circle, provided the stated number of real embeddings is available.
For example \(R\ge4B\) suffices on strict records, or \(R\ge3\) there when \(B=1\).
The estimate reduces the search for an obstruction in these signatures to counting genuinely different marked primitive shapes at the same coefficients.
