# Small relations and small values at the exact degree of a six-term reciprocal polynomial

<a id="six-term"></a>
## What a failure of exponential growth would require

Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial with positive logarithmic Mahler measure and exactly six terms.
After removing the common divisor of its three frequencies, write \(P(z)=Q(z^h)\), where
\[
 Q(z)=z^{2n}+1+b(z^{n+r}+z^{n-r})+e(z^{n+s}+z^{n-s}),\qquad
    0<r<s<n,\quad be\ne0,\quad \gcd(n,r,s)=1 .
\]
Put \(H=\max(|b|,|e|)\), \(d=\deg P=2nh\), \(C_d(P)=|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^d-1)|\), and \(m=\log M\).
The normalized logarithm \(\log C_d(P)/d=\log C_{2n}(Q)/(2n)\) only depends on this primitive pattern.
Let \(\ell\) be the smallest sup norm of a nonzero vector in \(\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:ur+vs\equiv0\bmod n\}\).
Write one such vector as \(q(a_0,b_0)\) with \(\gcd(a_0,b_0)=1,\ q>0,\ S=\max(|a_0|,|b_0|)\), so \(\ell=qS\).
There are absolute constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[
    \frac{\log C_d(P)}{d}
       \ge (1+\log H)\left(c-C\,\frac{q^4 S^3\log(2\ell)\log(2n)}{n}\right).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular fixed signed relations between the offsets, their small orders, and varying relations in the range where the error tends to zero all give a positive exponential rate at the exact degree.

If a sequence of these inputs has \(\log C_d(P)/d\to0\), then \(n\to\infty\) and
\[
   \ell\gg (n/\log^2(2n))^{1/4}.
\]
At some \(n\)-th root of unity \(z\) its nonzero value satisfies
\[
 \left|2+b(z^r+z^{-r})+e(z^s+z^{-s})\right|
 \le H\exp\!\left[-c(1+\log H)
                  \left(\frac{\ell}{\log(2\ell)}\right)^{1/3}\right].
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
In particular the value is at most \(\exp(-c' n^{1/12}/\sqrt{\log(2n)})\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
For prime \(n\) the lower bound on \(\ell\) has exponent \(1/3\) in place of \(1/4\).
These are necessary conditions on a failure of the six-term estimate left open by the [five-term theorem and seven-term example](Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#seven-term-unit).
The argument does not establish that an exponentially small normalized return or a sequence satisfying (2) exists.
The proof-side contributor obtained this reduction for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-16.

## The cyclic polynomials with constant Laurent coefficient two

Write \(T_j(t)=t^j+t^{-j}\) in this section.
For coprime integers \(0<u<v\) and nonzero integers \(B,E\), all possibilities for
\[
                  M(2+B T_u+E T_v)=1
\]
are \((u,v,B,E)=(1,2,\pm1,1),(1,2,\pm2,1),(2,3,-2,\pm1)\).

To prove completeness, let \(t\) be a torsion zero and set \(X=t^u,\ Y=t^v\).
If the five displayed rationally weighted terms form a minimal vanishing relation, the [relative-order theorem with its direct proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order) gives \(X^{30}=Y^{30}=1\).
Otherwise two of the five terms cancel, because a proper vanishing subsum splits the five nonzero terms into parts of sizes two and three.
If the constant cancels one term, its phase is \(\pm1\), its conjugate term is also \(-2\), and the trace of the other phase equals \(2/B\) or \(2/E\).
This is a nonzero rational algebraic integer in \([-2,2]\), so the other phase has order dividing six.
If a conjugate pair cancels, that phase has order four and the remaining trace is again a nonzero integer in \([-2,2]\), giving order dividing six.
A cross-pair cancellation would also cancel its conjugate pair, leaving the constant two.
By coprimality \(t\) belongs to \(\mu_{30}\cup\mu_{12}\), which has 36 elements.

At a nonzero root the multiplicity is at most four by the Vandermonde matrix obtained from the first five powers of \(t\,d/dt\) at the five distinct exponents.
Measure one implies that the ordinary degree-\(2v\) polynomial \(t^v(2+B T_u+E T_v)\) has leading coefficient of absolute value one and all its roots torsion.
Thus \(v\le72,\ |E|=1\), and the [five-term coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives \(|B|\le8\).
The following finite sieve uses only integer arithmetic.
Reduction modulo 601 preserves the distinct sixtieth roots, so a candidate whose complex roots all lie in the stated union retains at least \(2v\) units of multiplicity when the multiplicity at each residue root is capped at four.

~~~python
from math import gcd

p, w = 601, 441
assert all(p % d for d in range(2,25))
assert pow(w,60,p)==1 and all(pow(w,d,p)!=1 for d in (30,20,12))
roots = [pow(w,j,p) for j in range(60) if j%2==0 or j%5==0]
def jet(h,t):
    return tuple((h**i*pow(t,h,p)+(-h)**i*pow(t,-h,p))%p for i in range(4))
values = [[jet(h,t) for t in roots] for h in range(73)]
found = []
for v in range(2,73):
    for u in range(1,v):
        if gcd(u,v)!=1:
            continue
        for B in (*range(-8,0), *range(1,9)):
            for E in (-1,1):
                count = 0
                for U,V in zip(values[u],values[v]):
                    if (2+B*U[0]+E*V[0])%p:
                        continue
                    count += 1
                    for j in (1,2,3):
                        if (B*U[j]+E*V[j])%p:
                            break
                        count += 1
                if count>=2*v:
                    found.append((u,v,B,E))
assert found == [(1,2,-2,1),(1,2,-1,1),(1,2,1,1),(1,2,2,1),
                 (2,3,-2,-1),(2,3,-2,1)]
~~~

Writing \(x=t+t^{-1}\), the first four surviving polynomials are \(x(x+B)\), and the last two are \((Ex-2)(x^2-3)\).
Their lifted roots are roots of unity in every case.

## The mean over the connected components of the two sampling sets

For \(z^n=\epsilon\) write \(X=z^r,\ Y=z^s\).
The absolute original value at these nodes is that of
\[
        F_\epsilon(X,Y)=2\epsilon+b(X+X^{-1})+e(Y+Y^{-1}).
\]
Write \(a=a_0,\ b'=b_0,\ j=ar+b's,\ g=\gcd(j,n)\).
The order of \(j\bmod n\) is \(q=n/g\), since a smaller positive order would shorten the chosen relation.
Choose \(c_0,d_0\in\mathbb Z\) with \(ad_0-b'c_0=1\) and use the torus coordinates \(W=X^aY^{b'},\ V=X^{c_0}Y^{d_0}\).
Thus \(X=W^{d_0}V^{-b'},\ Y=W^{-c_0}V^a\).
For each sign, the parameter \(W\) takes the \(q\) values \(W^q=\epsilon^{j/g}\).
At a fixed such value \(w\), the \(g\) nodes run over exactly \(V^g=\eta_w\) for some \(\eta_w\in\mu_{2q}\).
Indeed \(z^g\) on this slice is determined by \((z^g)^q=\epsilon,\ (z^g)^{j/g}=w\), since \(q\) and \(j/g\) are coprime.
The power \(c_0r+d_0s\) is coprime to \(g\) by the unimodular change and \(\gcd(n,r,s)=1\).

Let \(F_{\epsilon,w}(V)\) be the resulting Laurent polynomial on this parameter circle.
We show
\[
       \frac{1}{2q}\sum_{\epsilon,w}m(F_{\epsilon,w})\ge c(1+\log H).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
First suppose \(|a|\) and \(|b'|\) are distinct and nonzero.
In every component the extreme coefficients have the same integer modulus at least one; hence each logarithmic measure is nonnegative.
The largest of the four nonconstant coefficient moduli is \(H\).
On the full two-dimensional torus one has
\[
        m(F_+)\ge c_0(1+\log H).
\]
Integration in a coordinate with coefficient of modulus \(H\) gives at least \(\log H\), by the leading coefficient of the quadratic after clearing a monomial.
For \(H=1\), on at least a third of the other coordinate circle the middle coefficient \(2\pm2\cos t\) is at least three.
The larger root modulus of the quadratic is then at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\), giving the required positive constant.

The Haar measure of the \(q\) plus components annihilates all Fourier monomials outside \(\mathbb Z q(a,b')\).
For \(\rho>0\), regularize the logarithm of \(|F_+|/H\) by replacing it with \(\tfrac12\log((|F_+|/H)^2+\rho^2)\).
On each parameter circle its integral exceeds \(m(F_{+,w})-\log H\) by at most \(C\rho^{1/8}\) using the [sparse regularization estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#regularity), since its five Laurent coefficients are distinct and their largest modulus is at least \(H\) before normalization.
Choose \(\rho\) so this loss is less than \(c_0/4\).
The regularized function is uniformly approximable within \(c_0/8\) by a trigonometric polynomial of bounded degree in both \(X,Y\), independently of the coefficients.
Indeed one can approximate \(u\mapsto \tfrac12\log(u^2+\rho^2)\) on \([-6,6]\) by an ordinary polynomial and substitute the real function \(F_+/H\).
For \(\ell=qS\) above this fixed degree the approximating polynomial has exactly the same mean on the plus components as on the full torus.
The full regularized integral is at least \(m(F_+)-\log H\).
This proves (3) for large \(\ell\).
For bounded \(\ell\) and \(H\to\infty\), the five-term coefficient bound gives \(m(F_{\epsilon,w})\ge\log(H/8)\) on every component.

It remains in this distinct-frequency case to rule out zero total mass for bounded \(\ell,H\), where all the component polynomials, up to monomials and circle reparametrization, range over a finite set.
One can invert or interchange the coordinates so that the positive identity component is \(X=t^u,\ Y=t^v\), \(0<u<v,\ \gcd(u,v)=1\).
Its other components then have \(Y^u/X^v=\tau\) for \(\tau^q=1\).
Use \(B,E\) for the corresponding coefficients.
Unless the identity polynomial occurs in the preceding six-entry list, this component already has positive logarithmic measure.

If \(q\ge2\), choose \(\tau\in\mu_q\) with \(\Re\tau<0\).
For \(u=1,\ v=2,\ E=1\), write \(X=t,\ Y=\tau t^2\); on the circle \(F_+=|1+\tau t^2|^2+2B\Re t\).
Hence a unit root must have \(B\Re t\le0\).
The four roots after clearing \(t^{-2}\) have sum \(-B/\tau\), contradicting this inequality if all were on the circle.
For \(u=2,\ v=3,\ B=-2,\ E=\pm1\), put \(X=t^2,\ Y=w t^3,\ w^2=\tau\).
The root equation on the circle forces \(\Re(t^2)\ge0\), since \(2-4\Re(t^2)\in[-2,2]\).
The coefficients at degrees \(6,5,4\) of the cleared polynomial are \(Ew,-2,0\), respectively; hence the sum of the squares of the six roots is \(4/\tau\), again impossible for six unit roots.
In both cases the endpoint coefficients have equal modulus one, so some root is outside the circle and the logarithmic measure is positive.

If \(q=1\), the minus component has \(Y^u/X^v=\tau=\pm1\).
At \(\tau=1\) the two component polynomials are the two levels \(B T_u+E T_v\pm2\).
Their joint measure cannot be one by [two-endpoint rigidity](Yin_L_G0462_v35.md#endpoint-rigidity), since this middle polynomial has two distinct nonzero frequencies.
At \(\tau=-1\), the first exceptional shape implies that the original frequency \(s_2\) of the coefficient \(E=1\) satisfies \(s_2\equiv n\pm2s_1\bmod 2n\), where \(s_1\) is the other positive frequency.
This follows by evaluating \(Y/X^2\) at \(z=e^{\pi i/n}\) and allowing the coordinate inversions.
Then \(s_2=|n-2s_1|\) and the original polynomial factors as
\[
              (1+z^{2s_1})(z^{2(n-s_1)}+Bz^{n-s_1}+1).
\]
For the second shape let \(s_1\) have coefficient \(-2\) and \(s_2\) coefficient \(E\).
Now \(2s_2\equiv n\pm3s_1\bmod 2n\), so \(s_1\equiv n\bmod2\).
At \(E=1\) there is a root at one.
At \(E=-1\) there is a root at minus one if \(n+s_2\) is odd.
Otherwise all three frequencies have the same parity and must be odd by primitivity.
The polynomial is then a polynomial in \(z^2\) with reciprocal quotient of odd degree \(n\), so it has a root at \(i\).
This completes the bounded case for distinct nonzero frequencies.

If one frequency on the parameter circle is zero, then one original coordinate is fixed in each component, with its plus values running through \(\mu_q\); here \(q\ge2\) since each original frequency lies strictly between zero and \(n\).
The other coordinate gives a quadratic with leading coefficient the corresponding nonzero integer.
On an absolute positive fraction of the plus components the real middle coefficient is at least \(2+|b_{\rm fixed}|\): choose the sign of \(\Re w\) to agree with \(b_{\rm fixed}\) and \(|\Re w|\ge1/2\).
For \(q\ge6\) an arc of length one third of the circle contains at least \(q/3-1\ge q/6\) equally spaced points, and \(q=2,3,4,5\) give at least one each for either sign.
The measure of these quadratics is at least both the absolute leading coefficient and half the absolute middle coefficient, while every other component has nonnegative logarithmic measure.
This gives (3).

If the two frequencies have equal modulus then \(|a|=|b'|=1\); after coordinate inversion each component has \(X=t,\ Y=\tau t\).
Its quadratic has opposite endpoint coefficients conjugate to each other, of common modulus \(k=|b+e\tau|\), and middle coefficient \(2\epsilon\).
Its Mahler measure is at least \(\max(1,k)\): for \(0<k\le1\) it is \(1+\sqrt{1-k^2}\), and for \(k=0\) the measure is two.
For \(q\ge2\) the same arc count on the plus components gives \(k^2\ge b^2+e^2+|be|\) on an absolute positive fraction.
For \(q=1\), the original frequencies are distinct and congruent up to sign modulo \(n\), so \(r+s=n\); the two components have coefficient moduli \(|b+e|\) and \(|b-e|\), one equal to \(|b|+|e|\).
This finishes (3) for all relations.

## Passing to the integer and the remaining concentration of small values

After multiplying each Laurent polynomial by \(V^S\), its degree is at most \(2S\).
The product over the \(2q\) components is a nonzero integer polynomial by Galois invariance of the phases for each sign.
Its degree is at most \(4qS\) and its logarithmic measure is at most \(2q\log(2+4H)\) by the circle bound.
Thus each nonzero root in any component has degree \(O(qS)\) and its primitive minimal polynomial has logarithmic measure \(O(q(1+\log H))\).
Every sampled value is nonzero since \(Q\) is an irreducible noncyclotomic input.
For a nontorsion root \(v\), invert it when outside the circle, extract the usual exterior factor, and bound the remaining distance by \(|v^{2n}-1|/(2q)\) from below since \(\eta_w^{2q}=1\).
The [two-logarithm distance estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v2.md#circle) makes the logarithmic error at each such root \(O(q^3S^2\log(2\ell)\log(2n)(1+\log H))\).
For a torsion root its order is \(O(\ell^2)\) and \(v^g\ne\eta_w\), giving the same bound by elementary angular spacing.
A zero root has unit normalized distance, and all upper normalized distances are at most two.
The leading coefficient contributes \(g\) times its logarithm exactly, including when the degree drops.
Multiplying over the roots with multiplicity now gives
\[
       \log C_{2n}(Q)=g\sum_{\epsilon,w}m(F_{\epsilon,w})
            +O\big(q^4 S^3\log(2\ell)\log(2n)(1+\log H)\big).
\]
This and (3) prove (1).
Since \(Q(1)\) is a nonzero even integer, a failure sequence must have \(n\to\infty\).
Using \(q^4S^3\le\ell^4\) in (1) proves the asserted minimum relation length.
For prime \(n\), the pigeonhole principle applied to pairs in \(\{0,\ldots,\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\}^2\) gives \(\ell\le\sqrt n<n\), so \(q\mid n\) forces \(q=1\).

For (2), average on the plus grid \(G=\{(z^r,z^s):z^n=1\}\).
For a Lipschitz function \(f\) on the two-dimensional torus, the difference between its means on \(G\) and on the full torus is at most \(C\,\operatorname{Lip}(f)D_\ell\), where \(D_\ell=\log(2\ell)/\ell\).
Indeed convolve in each variable with the Fejér kernel of degree less than \(\ell\).
The convolved function has identical means by Fourier orthogonality, and its uniform approximation error follows by integrating the distance to zero against the kernel.
The elementary kernel bound \(C\min(\ell,1/(\ell t^2))\) gives first moment \(O(D_\ell)\).
Set \(\rho=D_\ell^{2/3}\) and \(F=F_+\).
The truncated logarithm \(\log H+\log\max(\rho,|F|/H)\) has Lipschitz constant \(O(1/\rho)\) and full mean at least \(c_0(1+\log H)\) as above.
Its grid mean is consequently at least \(c_0(1+\log H)/2\) for large \(\ell\).

On the full torus, \(\Pr(|F|<2H\rho)\le C\sqrt{\rho}\).
Indeed, after fixing the coordinate whose coefficient is smaller, the cosine of the remaining uniform angle must lie in an interval of length \(O(\rho)\); integrating its arcsine density gives the bound uniformly in the interval.
The function \(\max(0,\min(1,2-|F|/(H\rho)))\) majorizes the indicator of \(|F|<H\rho\) and has Lipschitz constant \(O(1/\rho)\).
Thus at most \(C n (\sqrt{\rho}+D_\ell/\rho)\le C'nD_\ell^{1/3}\) grid values can fall below \(H\rho\).
The untruncated plus-grid mean is at most \(\log C_{2n}(Q)/n=o(1)\), since the minus-grid resultant is a nonzero integer.
The full deficit between the two grid means is supported on those few values.
At least one must have logarithmic deficit at least \(c(1+\log H)D_\ell^{-1/3}\), which gives (2).
