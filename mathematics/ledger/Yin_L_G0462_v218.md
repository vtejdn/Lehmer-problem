# Unramified Salem polynomials force class group quotients after an imaginary quadratic extension

<a id="salem"></a>
## A class group test using any chosen set of traces

Let \(P=X^n f(X+X^{-1})\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2n\) with \(|P(1)|=|P(-1)|=1\).
Suppose its trace roots \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\) are all real and precisely one is outside \([-2,2]\), with \(n>1\).
This includes unramified Salem polynomials and their sign changes.
Fix \(j\) of these roots, \(1\le j\le n-1\).
For **any** totally real number field \(E\) containing the chosen roots and **every** totally imaginary quadratic extension \(M/E\), there is a surjection of ordinary ideal class groups
\[
                   {\rm Cl}(M)\ \longrightarrow\ (\mathbb Z/2)^j.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular \({\rm Cl}(\mathbb Q(y_1)(i))\) has even order.
If \(E\) contains all the trace roots, the signs of its units satisfy at least \(n-1\) independent linear relations over \(\mathbb F_2\) on its real embeddings, and the ordinary class group of \(E(i)\) has 2-rank at least \(n-1\).
Here 2-rank means the dimension of \({\rm Cl}(M)/2{\rm Cl}(M)\).
Both conclusions also hold when the traces have \(0<s<n\) exterior real values and the trace group contains the alternating group \(A_n\), for \(n\ge3\).

<a id="signs"></a>
## The signs of the discriminants of the quadratic lifts

Put \(\delta_i=y_i^2-4\) and let \(T\) be the totally real splitting field of \(f\).
The endpoint assumptions give \(|f(2)|=|f(-2)|=1\).
Since \(f(2)\equiv f(-2)\bmod4\), their product is one and
\[
                          \prod_{i=1}^n\delta_i=1 .
\]
In particular \(n-s\), the number of negative discriminants in any real embedding of \(T\), is even.
For \(s=1\) each embedding gives the sign pattern with exactly one positive entry and all other entries negative, and transitivity realizes each possible position of the positive entry.
For \(0<s<n\) in the alternating case the embeddings realize every choice of \(n-s\) negative entries.
Indeed any permutation between two subsets of this size may be made even by a transposition inside the subset or its complement, one of which has size at least two.

Let \(w\in\mathbb F_2^n\) be a nonconstant choice of exponents.
Choose positions \(u,v\) with \(w_u\ne w_v\), and two allowed negative sets differing by replacing \(u\) with \(v\).
Their dot products with \(w\) differ, so \(\prod_i\delta_i^{w_i}\) has both signs among its real conjugates.
The two constant choices give the value one.
It follows that every nonempty proper subset product has mixed signs.
This remains true over any totally real \(E\) containing its selected roots: the two embeddings of the field generated by those roots, obtained by restricting embeddings of \(T\), each extend to a real embedding of \(E\).

Consequently the \(j\) selected \(\delta_i\) are independent in \(E^\times/(E^\times)^2\).
For a quadratic extension \(M=E(\sqrt h)\), the only square classes of \(E\) that become squares in \(M\) are those of \(1,h\), by squaring \(u+v\sqrt h\) and comparing its two coordinates.
Since \(M\) is totally imaginary and \(E\) totally real, \(h\) is totally negative.
No nonempty product of the selected \(\delta_i\) can represent either class, hence they remain independent over \(M\).
Adjoining the \(j\) square roots to \(M\) gives an elementary abelian extension of degree \(2^j\), by successive quadratic adjunction, or the elementary square-class correspondence.

<a id="classes"></a>
## Finite ramification, ordinary ideal classes and unit signs

The field \(\mathbb Q(y_i,\sqrt{\delta_i})\) is the quadratic lift at \(y_i\).
It is unramified at every finite prime by [Gross and McMullen, Proposition 3.1](../audits/literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#unramified-reciprocal).
Directly, the monic defining polynomial \(X^2-y_i X+1\) has unit discriminant because \(\delta_i\) is an algebraic integer unit by the product formula above.
This also proves the finite-place assertion after any extension of the base containing \(y_i\), including at primes over two: the reduced polynomial has nonzero discriminant, so it splits in the maximal unramified local extension by lifting its distinct roots from a finite residue field extension.
Thus the compositum of all \(j\) quadratic extensions over \(M\) is unramified at every finite place.
There are no real places of \(M\).
The [Artin surjection on ordinary ideal classes](../audits/literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#artin-sign) for this abelian extension gives (1).

For the unit statement, take \(E\) containing all \(y_i\).
For each \(i\) the quadratic extension \(E(\sqrt{\delta_i})/E\) ramifies at just the real places with negative \(\delta_i\).
For any unit \(u\) the [principal-ideal sign formula](../audits/literature/GM02/Yin_U001.md#artin-sign) therefore gives
\[
                  \prod_{\tau:\,\tau(\delta_i)<0}\operatorname{sgn}\tau(u)=1 .
\]
The \(n\) indicator vectors of these sets of real embeddings have rank \(n-1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\): their sum is zero, and the mixed-sign argument rules out every other nonzero relation.
This proves the signature assertion, with the same rank for the displayed relations independently of any larger deficiency of the units.

<a id="applications"></a>
## Consequences for small measures

In primitive quartic directions with \(4\mid b\) and sufficiently large \(b/a\), every rational irreducible factor of the cyclotomic-free core has [absolute endpoint values one](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v400.md#parity).
If the negative factor yields a Salem number and has trace degree \(n\), (1) applies directly to its traces.
Along a putative shrinking sequence the [degree formula for a single exterior root](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v399.md#counts) gives \(n\sim b/(2a)\).
The normal trace field \(T\) then forces ordinary class group 2-rank at least \(n-1\) over every totally imaginary quadratic extension of \(T\).
For a smaller field generated by only \(j\) chosen trace roots the same argument forces rank at least \(j\), and its degree over \(\mathbb Q\) is at most \(n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)\) by the ordered tuple action.

For example the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](../claims/Yin_C_G0359_v1.md#lehmer-bundle-reuse-domain) is Salem with trace polynomial \(Y^5+Y^4-5Y^3-5Y^2+4Y+3\).
Both trace endpoint values are \(-1\).
Every totally imaginary quadratic extension of its quintic trace field therefore has even class number, and every such extension of the trace splitting field has an elementary abelian class group quotient of order sixteen.

More generally, among the unramified Salem polynomials for which some totally imaginary quadratic extension of the normal trace field has ordinary class group 2-rank at most a fixed \(B\), the degrees are at most \(2(B+1)\).
Thus they have a positive logarithmic measure gap: at bounded measure, the coefficients in this bounded degree range are bounded by the elementary symmetric polynomial estimates, leaving finitely many candidates.
The bound also applies to the alternating case with several exterior real traces under the hypotheses above.
Neither the class rank nor the size of the normal trace field is bounded a priori in the quartic family.
