# The leading measure of two-class double-root multiples on power substitutions

<a id="width"></a>
## Fixed width and growing width over each seed

Fix the monic reciprocal minimal polynomial \(P_0\) of a nontorsion algebraic unit, set \(e=\deg P_0\) and \(h=m(P_0)>0\), and write \(m\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Take the [irreducible representatives](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers) \(P(x)=P_0(x^n)\) of degree \(d=ne\), with \(n\) running through sufficiently large odd primes.
In the entire prime window \(y<p\le 2y\), \(y=\lceil(\log(3d))^4\rceil\), there is a primitive integral multiple \(F\) of \(P^2\) in the two classes \(0,1\bmod p\), with coprime rows in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\), such that
\[
          \deg F\le 4pd,\qquad
          1-o(1)\ \le\ \frac{m(F)}{ph}\ \le\ \frac{8e}{6e+1}+o(1).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The upper constant is strictly less than \(4/3\).
If \(b=b_p\to\infty\) and \(b=o(p)\), one can instead attain
\[
          \deg F\le npb,\qquad \frac{m(F)}{ph}\longrightarrow1.    \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(b=\max(4e,\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor)\) works for every fixed seed.
These constructions first fix \(P_0\), and their inequalities and limits are uniform in the residue offset and over the ensuing whole prime window.
Along a sequence of different seeds one may choose a sufficiently large representative separately for each seed, with errors tending to zero at all its window primes.
The argument gives no uniform threshold in terms of \(d\) alone for arbitrary representatives of varying seeds.
For these primitive witnesses, removing the two required copies and the cyclotomic factors subtracts exactly \(2h\) from \(m(F)\).

<a id="jets"></a>
## The two jets and the cost of a common powered factor

Let \(1\le r<p\) and first work in the seed variable \(t\), with possible terms \(U(t^p)+t^r V(t^p)\), where \(\deg U\le b\) and \(\deg V\le b-1\).
There are \(N=2b+1\) positions, all of exponent at most \(D=pb\).
For \(N>2e\) the [two-jet integer box](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#jets) gives a nonzero \(G\) on these positions, divisible by \(P_0^2\), with
\[
       m(G)\le \log\|G\|_1\le
       \frac{2D h+2e\log(6N)+e\log(1+D)}{N-2e}+\log(2N).           \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the values and first derivatives at a root of \(P_0\) are algebraic integers, and coefficients from \(0\) through \(T\) give at most \((6NT)^{2e}(1+D)^e\exp(2Dh)\) possible pairs by the norm boxes at all embeddings.
Two equal pairs yield the asserted double root.
The choice \(b=4e\) gives the upper constant in (1), and \(b\to\infty, b=o(p)\) gives the upper constant one in (2).

For the lower estimate, let \(H\ne0\) occupy at most two classes, have degree \(o(p^2)\) and contain \(P_0^j\), with \(j=1\) or \(2\).
Apply the [contraction divisor](Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#contraction) at both signs to \(P_0^j\).
Outside \(p\), the valuations at all primitive nodes for \(P_0\) are multiplied by \(j\), including their maximum, so the two divisors are \(D_+^j,D_-^j\) in the notation of that result.
For the noncyclotomic part \(R\) after deleting \(P_0^j\), the two divisibilities and the root bound yield
\[
      j(\log D_++\log D_-)
           \le (\deg R)\log 2+2p\,m(R),\qquad
      \frac{m(R)}{ph}\ge \frac j2-o(1).                              \tag{4}
\]
Here the [fixed-input exponent limits](Yin_L_G0462_v150.md#fixed-input) give \(\log D_\pm/(p-1)^2\to h/2\), uniformly in \(H\) subject to the stated degree error.
For large \(p\) the powered minimum \(Q_0\) of a root of \(P_0\) has degree \(e\) and measure \(ph\).
If both rows of \(G\) were divisible by \(Q_0\), with the zero row allowed, the integral quotient \(G/Q_0(t^p)\) would contain \(P_0\), since \(Q_0(t^p)\) is separable.
It would have at most two classes and degree at most \(pb=o(p^2)\).
By (4) this would give \(m(G)\ge(3/2-o(1))ph\), contrary to (3) in either width regime.
A one-class multiple of \(P_0^2\) necessarily has its nonzero row divisible by \(Q_0^2\) because substitution is unramified at the nonzero roots.
Thus there are two rows, and division by their primitive polynomial gcd and scalar content retains \(P_0^2\) and both classes, with degree and measure no greater.
The lower bound (4) applies again after division.
Take \(r n\equiv1\bmod p\) and substitute \(t=x^n\) in this cleaned \(G\).
The resulting base rows are \(U(X^n)\) and \(X^{(nr-1)/p}V(X^n)\); a Bézout identity preserves their Laurent coprimality.
The substitution preserves measure, primitivity and the double divisibility, proving (1) and (2).
This lift occupies a single class modulo \(n\), and its second base row generally has zero constant term.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Two different short ratios in growing width

In (2) we can also choose a seed double-root graph with unequal row moduli on the unit circle, retaining the leading constant and increasing the seed degree bound to \(p(b+1)\).
Here is a counting refinement which permits reciprocal lifting without a constant-term hypothesis.
Fix \(0<\eta<1/4\), take \(T=\lfloor\exp((1+\eta)ph)\rfloor\), and use the box of the preceding section.
With \(P_0,\eta\) fixed and \(b\to\infty,\ b=o(p)\), the largest fiber of the value and derivative map contains at least
\[
            \frac{(T+1)^N}{(6NT)^{2e}(1+D)^e\exp(2D h)}
            =\exp\big((2\eta+o(1))bph\big)
\]
coefficient vectors.
Subtract a fixed member to obtain that many different solutions, each of coefficient one-norm at most \(NT\).
Every nonzero solution retains both copies after removing its row gcd, by (4) and \(1+\eta<3/2\).
If all nonzero solutions had the same rational row ratio, take an integral coprime generator \(G_*\) of that ratio, with primitive joint scalar content and with the minimal row exponent normalized to zero.
Every solution is \(W(t^p)G_*(t)\) with \(W\in\mathbb Z[X]\), \(\deg W\le b\), by coprimality over \(\mathbb Q[X]\) and Gauss's lemma on joint content.
Since the cleaned generator itself contains \(P_0^2\), (4) gives \(m(G_*)\ge(1-o(1))ph\).
Consequently each nonzero \(W\) satisfies \(m(W)\le(\eta+o(1))ph\), so every coefficient of \(W\) is at most \(2^b\exp((\eta+o(1))ph)\) in absolute value by the elementary symmetric bounds.
Since \(b=o(ph)\) for the fixed seed, this allows only \(\exp((\eta+o(1))bph)\) polynomials \(W\), a contradiction.

Choose two short nonproportional pairs \((U_i,V_i)\) from the fiber differences, and write \(f^\iota(X)=f(1/X)\).
If both pairs satisfy \(U_iU_i^\iota=V_iV_i^\iota\), consider their sums with the second pair multiplied by \(1\) and by \(X\) respectively.
If both sums still satisfy the equality, \(K=U_1U_2^\iota-V_1V_2^\iota\) obeys \(K+K^\iota=X^{-1}K+XK^\iota=0\), so \(K=0\).
Together with the equality for the second pair this gives \(U_1/V_1=U_2/V_2\), a contradiction.
One of the four choices therefore gives a double-root graph \(G\) with \(UU^\iota\ne VV^\iota\), \(\deg G\le p(b+1)\) and \(m(G)\le\log(2NT)\).
The same gcd division preserves this inequality of rational functions and both imposed copies, since \(1+\eta<3/2\).
Taking \(\eta\downarrow0\) by diagonal choice as \(p\to\infty\) gives \(m(G)/(ph)\to1\), uniformly in \(r\).

## Reciprocal graphs with unrestricted endpoints

Choose the last cleaned \(G\) at \(nr\equiv1\bmod p\), put \(L=n(b+1)\), and take either sign \(\sigma\).
Form the integral polynomial
\[
       F_0(x)=G(x^n)+\sigma x^{pL+1}G(x^{-n}).                       \tag{5}
\]
Its two summands lie in different classes modulo \(n\), it contains \(P^2\) by reciprocity of \(P\), and \(m(F_0)\le m(G)+(\log2)/n\) by the [circle product on the \(n\)-th roots](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#seed).
In classes \(0,1\bmod p\) write its rows as \(A(X)\) and \(B(X)=\sigma X^L A(1/X)\).
At \(X=\beta=\gamma^p\), \(\gamma^n=\alpha\), the two contributions to \(A(\beta)\) are \(U(\alpha^p)\) and \(\sigma\beta^{L-w} V(\alpha^{-p})\), where \(w=(nr-1)/p\).
Both \(U,V\) are nonzero at \(\alpha^{\pm p}\) by reciprocity and the two coprime rows of \(G\).
As \(n\mid L\) and \(\beta^{-w}=\gamma\alpha^{-r}\), the contributions belong respectively to \(K^\times\) and \(\gamma K^\times\) for \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), with \([K(\gamma):K]=n\).
Thus \(A(\beta)\ne0\), and cleaning the two rows preserves \(P^2\).
Their common divisor is reciprocal up to sign and a Laurent monomial since reversal interchanges them.
After removal of common factors, monomials and scalar content we obtain a primitive reciprocal polynomial up to sign, with two coprime Laurent rows, of degree at most \(np(b+1)+1\).

Its measure still satisfies \(m(F)/(ph)\to1\).
To see the lower bound without assuming anything about the two constant terms, take any two distinct rotations \(x\mapsto\zeta^j x\) at the substitution prime \(n\).
In (5) they leave the first summand fixed and multiply the second by distinct scalars because \(n\mid L\).
The row vectors of the two summands over \(Y=x^p\) are
\[
       \big(U(Y^n),Y^w V(Y^n)\big),\qquad
       \sigma\big(Y^{L-w} V(Y^{-n}),Y^L U(Y^{-n})\big).
\]
Their determinant is \(\sigma Y^L(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)(Y^n)\ne0\), so every pair of rotated row vectors has nonzero determinant, also after common-factor division and monomial normalization.
Use the [Fourier determinant](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v404.md#determinant) on the two integral rows of the cleaned polynomial in their original residue classes, allowing zero constant terms and removing a common row monomial only.
Reduction modulo \(Z^n-1\) proves integrality independently of endpoint coefficients, and the pairwise determinants just computed prove nonvanishing.
Each pair determinant is divisible by \(Q^2\), with \(Q\) the powered minimum of \(P\), by elimination at the rotated double roots and unramified substitution.
Thus the integral Fourier quotient is divisible by \(Q^{n(n-1)}\), and monic division gives an integral quotient of nonnegative measure.
Hadamard's bound after circle integration as in [C404](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v404.md#circle) gives \(2I(F)\ge2ph\), where \(I(F)=\int\log\max(|A|,|B|)\) for the cleaned rows.
The [degree-dependent diagonal inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row) gives \(m(F)\ge(1-4/p)I(F)-2\log(C\deg F)/p\) after harmless monomial shifts, for an absolute \(C\).
Here \(\log(2np(b+1))=O(\log(3d))\), so this error is \(o(ph)\).
Combining with (5) gives the limit, also for the noncyclotomic cofactor after subtraction of \(2h\).

The prescribed sign in (5) precedes gcd division, which can remove an antireciprocal factor.
The construction does not require the two extreme coefficients of the same row to be nonzero in the specified formal degree, or furnish a pair of independent value and Euler derivative vectors after this reflection.
The constant one here concerns growing width with unrestricted endpoints, while (1) concerns the original degree cap for ordinary coprime two-class multiples.
The [signed endpoint normalization and joint trace conditions](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#rows) impose additional requirements.
