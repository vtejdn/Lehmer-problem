# Computing the cost of a two-class multiple from its reciprocal factor

2026-11-18T10:06.

<a id="contraction"></a>
## An integer divisor computed from the input polynomial

Fix an odd prime \(p\), a primitive \(p\)-th root \(\zeta\), and a sign \(\epsilon\).
Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be reciprocal up to sign with \(\alpha_\epsilon=P(\epsilon\zeta)\ne0\).
In \(O=\mathbb Z[\zeta]\), put
\[
 N_\epsilon=|\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q}\alpha_\epsilon|,
 \qquad (\alpha_\epsilon)O\cap\mathbb Z=B_\epsilon\mathbb Z,\quad B_\epsilon>0.
\]
The integer \(B_\epsilon\) is the additive exponent of \(O/(\alpha_\epsilon)\), with exponent one for the zero group.
Indeed an integer annihilates this quotient exactly when it kills the class of one.
Write \(a'=a/p^{v_p(a)}\) for each positive integer \(a\), and put
\[
                  D_\epsilon=\frac{(B_\epsilon')^{p-1}}{N_\epsilon'}.
\]
Compute the inverse \(V_\epsilon(X)\) of \(P(\epsilon X)\) modulo \(\Phi_p(X)\) over \(\mathbb Q\), reduced to degree \(<p-1\).
The least common denominator of its coefficients is \(B_\epsilon\), since \(1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{p-2}\) is an integral basis of \(O\).
One resultant gives \(N_\epsilon=|\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_p(X),P(\epsilon X))|\).
Removing powers of \(p\) from these two integers suffices to compute \(D_\epsilon\).

Suppose a nonzero multiple \(F=PCR\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) occupies at most two exponent classes modulo \(p\), where \(C\) is a nonzero integer times a monomial and a product of cyclotomic polynomials and \(R\in\mathbb Z[x]\) has no roots of unity.
Then \(D_\epsilon\) is an integer and
\[
                  D_\epsilon \mid |\operatorname{Res}(x^p-\epsilon,R)|.       \tag{1}
\]
This tests the extra nontorsion factors without factoring the cyclotomic multiplier.
For each prime \(\ell\ne p\), take an extension \(v\) of the valuation normalized by \(v(\ell)=1\) and set \(w_j=v(P(\epsilon\zeta^j))\) for \(1\le j<p\).
The reduction of \(\Phi_p\) divides the separable polynomial \(X^p-1\) modulo \(\ell\), so \(O\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\) is a product of unramified integer rings with all ramification indices equal to one.
In particular the \(w_j\) are integers.
The Vandermonde matrix \((\zeta^{jr})_{1\le j<p,\ 0\le r<p-1}\) and its inverse are integral at \(\ell\) because the nodes remain distinct.
Thus \(\ell^a V_\epsilon\) has integral coefficients at \(\ell\) exactly when all the values \(\ell^a/P(\epsilon\zeta^j)\) are integral there.
It follows that \(v_\ell(B_\epsilon)=\max_j w_j\), while \(v_\ell(N_\epsilon)=\sum_j w_j\).

Reciprocity gives \(w_j=w_{p-j}\), since the reciprocal evaluations differ by a root of unity and possibly a sign.
The maximum is attained at two distinct positions, forming a progression of length two with nonzero step.
The [local valuation estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v356.md#local) therefore gives \(v(R(\epsilon\zeta^j))\ge\max_i w_i-w_j\) at every primitive position.
Summing over \(j\) shows that the nonnegative exponent \((p-1)\max_i w_i-\sum_i w_i\) in \(D_\epsilon\) is paid by the primitive norm of \(R(\epsilon\zeta)\).
The remaining factor \(R(\epsilon)\) in the resultant is a nonzero integer, proving (1).
At \(p\) the primitive nodes coalesce and the denominator can record ramification without any valuation difference.
For example, take \(P=(x-1)^2\), \(\epsilon=1\) and \(p\ge5\).
The element \(1-\zeta\) has norm \(p\), and \(p\) is a unit times \((1-\zeta)^{p-1}\) by the product of the \(1-\zeta^j\), since their ratios to \(1-\zeta\) are integral of norm one.
Thus \(B_+=p,\ N_+=p^2\), whereas \(F=(x^p-1)^2\) occupies one class with \(R=1\).
Keeping the \(p\)-part would incorrectly charge \(p^{p-3}\).

## Quadratic degree or linear measure for a fixed reciprocal unit

Fix the monic minimal polynomial \(P\) of a nontorsion reciprocal algebraic unit and let \(p\) run through the odd primes.
Write \(m(P)=\log M(P)>0\).
The monic polynomial \(Q_\epsilon(X)=P(\epsilon X)\) is again an irreducible reciprocal unit polynomial of the same even degree \(d\) and the same measure.
Write \(Q_\epsilon(X)=X^{d/2}T_\epsilon(X+X^{-1})\), with \(T_\epsilon\) the monic minimal polynomial of the trace of a root and its inverse.
Put \(t=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), \(A=\mathbb Z[t]\), and let \(S_p\) be the minimal polynomial of \(t\).
The identity \(\zeta^2=t\zeta-1\) gives \(O=A\oplus\zeta A\), since \(t\) is real and \(\zeta\) is not.
Multiplication by \(T_\epsilon(t)\) acts on both summands.
The unit \(\zeta^{d/2}\) does not change its ideal, so \(B_\epsilon\) is also the additive exponent of \(A/(T_\epsilon(t))\).
The order of this last group is \(H_\epsilon=|\operatorname{Res}(T_\epsilon,S_p)|\), by the determinant of multiplication in the power basis of \(A\).
Consequently \(B_\epsilon\mid H_\epsilon\) and \(N_\epsilon=H_\epsilon^2\).

Let \(G_\epsilon=\max_{a\in\mathbb Z}\gcd(T_\epsilon(a),S_p(a))\).
For each such positive gcd \(g\), evaluation at \(a\) gives a surjection \(A/(T_\epsilon(t))\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\), so \(G_\epsilon\mid B_\epsilon\).
The [fixed-input common-modulus estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v5.md#statement) gives \(\log G_\epsilon/(p-1)\to m(P)/2\) and \(\log(H_\epsilon/G_\epsilon)=o_P(p)\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\) one has \(p\nmid P(\epsilon)\), a fixed nonzero integer.
Since \(\Phi_p(X)\equiv(X-1)^{p-1}\bmod p\), this implies \(p\nmid N_\epsilon\), hence \(p\nmid B_\epsilon\).
Squeezing \(G_\epsilon\le B_\epsilon\le H_\epsilon\) and using \(N_\epsilon=H_\epsilon^2\) gives, for each sign,
\[
                    \frac{\log D_\epsilon}{(p-1)^2}\longrightarrow\frac{m(P)}2 .   \tag{2}
\]

For any choice of two-class multiples at these primes with extra factors \(R=R_p\), both signs apply.
Their integer divisibilities multiply because \(x^{2p}-1=(x^p-1)(x^p+1)\).
The root product for the resultant, including the \(2p\)-th power of the leading coefficient of \(R\), gives
\[
 D_+D_- \mid |\operatorname{Res}(x^{2p}-1,R)|,\qquad
 \log D_++\log D_-\le (\deg R)\log2+2p\,m(R).                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus on any unbounded sequence of these primes,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m(R)=o(p)&\ \Longrightarrow\
       \liminf \frac{\deg R}{p^2}\ge \frac{m(P)}{\log2},\\
 \deg R=o(p^2)&\ \Longrightarrow\
       \liminf \frac{m(R)}p\ge \frac{m(P)}2 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
For a root \(\gamma\) of the fixed \(P\), the [two-class interpolants](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v354.md#statement) at primes satisfying \(\mathbb Q(\gamma^p)=\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) have degree at most \(p(d+2)+1\).
Their extra nontorsion part therefore incurs the second lower bound.
The degree condition holds for all sufficiently large primes for this fixed \(P\), since a collision of distinct conjugates after taking \(p\)-th powers requires their quotient to have exact order \(p\), and there are only finitely many pairs.
The constant in (2) and the onset of its estimate both depend on \(P\).
A uniform positive lower bound for \(m(P)\) or control of the onset along varying inputs remains open here.
The [multinacci trace-ideal example](../claims/Yin_C_G0899_v8.md#statement) exhibits the distinction for a varying input.
Its entire trace ideal is \((2)\) in \(A\) at the positive sign, so \(B_+=2\), \(N_+=2^{p-1}\), and \(D_+=1\) despite the growing norm.

## A trivial and a nontrivial test on Lehmer's polynomial

For the [degree-ten Lehmer polynomial](Yin_L_G0462_v3.md#minus-delay-progression)
\[
 P=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1=x^5T(x+x^{-1}),
 \qquad T(t)=t^5+t^4-5t^3-5t^2+4t+3,
\]
the two signs require the real evaluations \(T(t)\) and \(T(-t)\), up to units.
At \(p=5\), the remainders modulo \(S_5=t^2+t-1\) are \(2+t\) and \(3t-2\), both of real norm one by \(t+t'=-1,\ tt'=-1\).
Thus \(D_+=D_-=1\).

At \(p=7\), \(S_7=t^3+t^2-2t-1\) and \(T(-t)\equiv h=4-3t^2\bmod S_7\).
Using \(t^3=-t^2+2t+1\), multiplication by \(h\) in the basis \(1,t,t^2\) has matrix
\[
             \begin{pmatrix}4&-3&3\\0&-2&3\\-3&3&-5\end{pmatrix}
\]
of determinant \(13\).
The real quotient consequently has prime order and exponent \(13\), giving \(B_-=13,\ N_-=13^2\).
Formula (1) forces \(13^4\mid|\operatorname{Res}(x^7+1,R)|\) for every two-class multiple at seven.
In particular no purely cyclotomic multiplier attains two classes there.
