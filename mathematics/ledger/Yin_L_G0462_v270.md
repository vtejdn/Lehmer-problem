# Low-degree power fields and the next primes in the two-value test

<a id="descent"></a>
## A small measure descends to every power field

Suppose every integer polynomial of degree less than \(d_0\) has multiplicative Mahler measure one or greater than \(T\), apart from the zero polynomial, where \(1<T<2\).
If \(\alpha\) has \(1<M(\alpha)\le T\) and is an algebraic unit, then for every positive integer \(k\),
\[
                 [\mathbb Q(\alpha^k):\mathbb Q]\ge d_0 .
\]
Indeed write \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\), \(e=[K:F]\), and \(u=N_{K/F}(\alpha)\).
Every factor of this norm has the same \(k\)-th power, giving \(u^k=(\alpha^k)^e\).
Hence \(u\) is a nontorsion algebraic unit and its absolute logarithmic height is \(e h(\alpha)\).
The [degree-height identity](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) yields
\[
 0<\log M(u)=[\mathbb Q(u):\mathbb Q]\,e h(\alpha)
       \le [F:\mathbb Q]\,e h(\alpha)=\log M(\alpha).
\]
The minimum polynomial of \(u\in F\) therefore has degree at least \(d_0\).
This uses the [relative norm for powers](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) directly at the given exponent.
In particular every power preserves degree when \([K:\mathbb Q]<2d_0\), since \([F:\mathbb Q]\) divides \([K:\mathbb Q]\).
Also \(\pm(\alpha^k+\alpha^{-k})\) cannot be a root of any nonzero rational auxiliary polynomial of degree less than \(d_0/2\), since \(\alpha^k\) is quadratic or linear over its trace field.
The last assertion allows auxiliary polynomials of bounded degree without testing their roots individually.

First, take \(T=1089/1000\) and \(d_0=56\) from the [published strict sub-Lehmer degree theorem](../audits/literature/MRW08/Yin_U001.md#degree).
Here \(T<1.17628<\rho\), where \(\rho\) is Lehmer's measure.
This already certifies nonvanishing at every power and either sign for *all* eleven [auxiliaries of degree at most five](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/readme.md#ellipse), including in degree 60 where five divides the degree.

<a id="degree"></a>
## The degree threshold at measure 1.089

The [complete degree-56 and degree-58 calculation](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0179/versions/v3/readme.md#counts) raises \(d_0\) to 60 at this \(T\).
To see that it covers arbitrary nonzero polynomials, any \(1<M(P)\le T\) supplies an irreducible factor of measure in the same interval by Gauss's lemma and multiplicativity.
The leading coefficient and the nonzero constant term of this factor have absolute value one by the root product, so it is a unit minimum up to sign.
The [nonreciprocal lower bound](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) and reciprocal symmetry force even degree, since an odd-degree reciprocal polynomial has a factor \(X+1\) and an antireciprocal one has a factor \(X-1\).
The published theorem leaves only degrees 56 and 58 below 60.
A power substitution there has a smaller-degree integer polynomial of exactly the same measure, of degree at most 29, also excluded by the published theorem.
The exhaustive calculation handles the remaining reciprocal representatives up to \(X\mapsto-X\).
Thus every polynomial with \(1<M(P)\le1.089\) has degree at least 60.
For any unit realizing a measure in this range every power field also has degree at least 60, and auxiliaries through degree 29 are available by the preceding argument.

## Degrees at primes from 223 through 241

Use the [original signed-row test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#statement) with \(p\in\{223,227,229,233,239,241\}\), either sign, and width \(b=1+\lfloor p^{7/8}\rfloor\).
If a row has two different qualifying values, select one factor per value, and put \(D=d_1+d_2\) for their degree sum.
The [improved derivative estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v262.md#exclusion) bounds \(D\le2b\) and gives
\[
       m(P_1)+m(P_2)<\frac{\log2}{4}
                    -\frac1p\log\frac{2pb+1}{pb+1}.
\]
Both factors are reciprocal unit minima of even degree at least eight by the [small-degree exclusions](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#frontier).
For each indicated prime \(2b-8<p-1\), hence the maximum residue multiplicity within either polynomial is less than \(p-1\).
Apply the [double-contact resultant bound](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#energy) to the selected pair, after dividing the row by its content.
Since \(p>2^7,\ H<2^{31},\ D\le244,\ b\le122\), it gives
\[
    \sum_\xi E(\xi)^2 < (2\cdot244\cdot31+241\cdot122/2)/7<66^2 .
\]
Thus \(E(\xi)\le65\), and \(2E(\xi)-1<p-e_\xi\) at every occupied residue.
The [local derivative divisibilities](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#digits) force the reduction of the entire product \(P_1P_2\) to divide the reduction of the nonzero primitive row, so \(D\le b\).

Both measures are strictly smaller than \(\rho\).
Otherwise the other logarithmic measure would be less than \(0.69315/4-0.162356-0.6926/241<1/108\) using \((2pb+1)/(pb+1)>1.999\) and the [verified logarithm bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#finite).
Its degree is at most 122, so [Voutier's Corollary 2](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) gives \(m(P)>2/\log^3(366)>1/108\), since \(e^6>366\).
Thus both degrees are at least 56.
The smaller measure is below \(1.089\) at all six primes by the following integer checks, so its degree is at least 60.

~~~python
for p, b in [(223,114), (227,116), (229,117), (233,118), (239,121), (241,122)]:
    assert (b-1)**8 < p**7 < b**8
    assert 2*b-8 < p-1
    assert 1089**(8*p)*(2*p*b+1)**4 > 2**p*1000**(8*p)*(p*b+1)**4
~~~

At 223 the degree sum would be at least \(60+56>114\), excluding both signs.
Together with the [earlier finite theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v422.md#statement) this excludes two values at every prime through 223.
At 227 and 229 the only ordered degree pair, smaller measure first, is \((60,56)\).
At 233 the possibilities are \((60,56),(60,58),(62,56)\).
In particular a degree-60 exclusion at \(T\) would settle both 227 and 229, and additionally excluding degree 62 would settle 233.
