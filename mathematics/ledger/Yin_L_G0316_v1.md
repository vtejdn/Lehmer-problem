### 2. Infinitely many actual states make both nonzero digits move outward

**Lemma 1.** For every fixed epsilon in(0,1/2), there are infinitely many actual states x, of unbounded rho, such that all three original successors y_eta=beta*x+eta belong to D and are nonzero, and

    |Re(zeta sigma_zeta(x))|<epsilon,
    rho(y_0)=rho(x),
    rho(y_+)^2,rho(y_-)^2 >rho(x)^2+1-2epsilon.       (2)

*Proof.* Choose d_+,d_->0 with

    beta d_++1<B, beta^(-1)d_-+1<S.                  (3)

They exist because1<beta<2 and B,S>1.
Consider the three real linear coordinates on the power-basis lattice O:

    x_+, x_-, Re(zeta sigma_zeta(x)).                 (4)

For N>=1, the(N+1)^4 distinct order elements with power-basis coefficients in{0,...,N} have each coordinate(4) in an interval of length at most C_P N.
Partition those intervals into half-open cells of respective fixed widths d_+/2,d_-/2,epsilon/2.
The total number of three-dimensional cells is at most C_(P,d_+,d_-,epsilon)(N+1)^3.
One cell therefore contains at least c_(P,d_+,d_-,epsilon)(N+1) distinct elements.
Subtract one element in that cell from the others.
The resulting distinct order elements satisfy

    |x_+|<d_+, |x_-|<d_-,
    |Re(zeta sigma_zeta(x))|<epsilon.                 (5)

The union of these difference sets is infinite because their cardinalities grow without bound.
If rho were bounded on that union, both real coordinates and both neutral coordinates would be bounded, contradicting discreteness of the full Minkowski image of the actual order.
Thus rho is unbounded.
This argument permits the occupied cell and its base point to depend on N; it does not require one fixed cell, density of an angular orbit, a maximal order or a fractional module.

Conditions(3),(5) put all three successors inside the strict real core, hence inside the actual D.
Write z=zeta sigma_zeta(x), r=|z| and u=Re z.
Since |zeta|=1,

    |z+eta|^2=r^2+2 eta u+eta^2.                    (6)

For eta=+/-1 this exceeds r^2+1-2epsilon.
The zero digit preserves r.
Taking only the unbounded states with r>1 makes every successor nonzero.
This proves all claims.
The gain in(2) is a gain in SQUARED radius; no fixed positive gain in radius itself is claimed.

### 3. The true critical transform does not rescue a monotone radial barrier

**Theorem 2.** Let f:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity) be finite and nondecreasing, with no continuity, differentiability or growth hypothesis.
Put W(x)=f(rho(x)).
On infinitely many genuine core states outside every finite set,

    (QW)(x)>=W(x).                                   (7)

Consequently W cannot satisfy W>=1+Q^0 W outside a finite set, nor QW<=W-delta outside a finite set for any delta>0.
Equivalently the H-weighted ORIGINAL candidate

    v(x)=H(x)f(rho(x))                               (8)

cannot satisfy v>=H+RK^0v outside a finite set.
Here Q^0 and K^0 restrict successors to D minus{0}.

*Proof.* At every state of Lemma1 all three successors are nonzero and legal, and their radii are at least rho(x).
Their Q probabilities are positive and sum to1 by(1).
Monotonicity gives each successor value at least W(x), proving(7).
Since Q^0 equals Q on that row, the positive additive term1 cannot be paid.
Multiplication by H(x)>0 gives exactly the equivalence with(8), using the original weights in(1).
The unbounded sequence escapes every finite set because bounded-rho portions of D are finite.
If f is strictly increasing, at least the two nonzero-digit values are strictly larger and the inequality in(7) is strict; strictness is not needed for the obstruction.

This conclusion differs from L310's earlier unweighted radial v obstruction.
It rules out an entire H-weighted monotone class even when H has arbitrary actual arithmetic or boundary dependence, and applies to polynomial, exponential, nonsmooth and arbitrarily rapidly increasing f.
It does not exclude nonmonotone radial functions, nonradial functions, radial estimates of coefficients or occupations, or finite mu.
The zero function also satisfies(7) and cannot pay the positive forcing term; it is not an exception.

For a quantitative display of the actual row, take W=rho^2 at a state where all three digits are legal.
Denote H_eta=H(beta*x+eta), u=Re(zeta sigma_zeta(x)).
Equation(6) and the full stochastic identity(1) give

    Q(rho^2)(x)-rho(x)^2
      = R/[4H(x)] [H_+ + H_- +2u(H_+-H_-)].          (9)

At the states of Lemma1 the bracket is at least(1-2epsilon)(H_++H_-)>0.
Thus even the unknown true harmonic tilt cannot turn the elementary quadratic radius into a negative-drift function on every distant state.
No assumption that Q has symmetric +/- digit probabilities is made; the argument uses that BOTH actual possible moves go outward.
This eliminates a tempting misuse of an averaged neutral inward direction at those rows.

There is also a precise necessary size for a possible correction.
If W=f(rho)+g and QW<=W-delta at one of these rows, then(7) forces

    g(x)-(Qg)(x)>=delta,
    max_(eta in{-1,0,1})[g(x)-g(beta*x+eta)]>=delta.   (10)

Thus a correction whose variation across all three outgoing edges tends to0 along the unbounded tangent sequence cannot provide a fixed negative drift.
This is only a necessary local variation test; no converse or obstruction to all nonradial corrections is asserted.

### 4. A complete paid repair if a genuinely nonradial tail estimate is found

The preceding failure concerns a concrete ansatz.
The following sufficient theorem makes the remaining nonradial task precise without demanding the global Poisson inequality in advance.

**Theorem 3.** Fix an integer r>=2 and C=D_r={x in D:rho(x)<r}, including0.
Suppose a function W:D->[0,infinity), finite at EVERY state, and a constant delta>0 satisfy the actual full-row condition

    (QW)(x)<=W(x)-delta for every x in D outside C.   (11)

Define the finite constants

    L=max{1,ceil(A*r+B0)}, epsilon=(R/4)^L,
    M=max{W(y):y in D, rho(y)<=r+L},
    T=L+M/delta, U=T/epsilon.                        (12)

Then every state has finite mean hitting time to0 and the actual critical first-return mean obeys

    mu<=1+(1-R/2)U.                                  (13)

All constants refer to the entire actual core; M is over the ENTIRE displayed finite set.
Neither a partial state list nor a bound only on one component suffices.
R remains the unknown true original return radius.

*Proof: reaching C.*
Let s be the first hit of C from x outside C.
Kill the Q rows on entering C.
Discarding the nonnegative W values on C in(11) gives W>=delta+Q_(D\C)W on its complement.
Iterating this inequality n times yields

    W(x)>=delta sum_(j=0)^(n-1)Pr_x(s>j).

All sums are nonnegative, so increasing n proves E_x s<=W(x)/delta and s<infinity almost surely.
This is a direct killed-row calculation.
It assumes neither an existing stationary probability nor integrability of W under one, and uses no optional-stopping theorem.
Starting in C gives s=0.

*Proof: repeated finite attempts from C.*
At any nonzero z in C, L309 gives an actual selected first-hit word of length l<=L.
Its Q probability is R^l p(word)/H(z)>=(R/4)^l>=epsilon.
The selected word may leave C and reenter; it remains in the whole D, which is all this attempt requires.
Start the actual chain from z and run it until0 or time L.
If0 is not hit by L, the resulting state y satisfies rho(y)<=rho(z)+L<r+L because each original neutral increment has modulus at most1.
Thus W(y)<=M.
From that point wait until hitting C, unless already there; the preceding killed-row estimate bounds the expected additional waiting by M/delta.
A hit of0 during this wait terminates the return.
Otherwise a new attempt starts at another nonzero state of C.

The expected time consumed per initiated attempt, including this possible wait, is at most T.
Conditional on every attempt's starting state, its chance of terminating within its first L steps is at least epsilon.
Hence the probability of initiating the kth attempt is at most(1-epsilon)^(k-1).
Summing the expected lengths over initiated attempts gives at most T/epsilon=U.
This argument can first be stopped after a finite number of attempts and then increased; all terms are nonnegative.
The waits return to C almost surely, so no hidden infinite waiting event remains between attempts.
Consequently E_z tau_0<=U for every nonzero z in C, and

    E_x tau_0<=W(x)/delta+U for x outside C.           (14)

No independence of attempts is needed; the uniform conditional bounds suffice.

*Proof: the first positive return.*
Both nonzero first successors+/-1 have radius1<r and belong to C.
At0 the zero digit has critical probability R/2 and ends the return at time1.
The other two probabilities sum to1-R/2.
Conditioning on this actual first row, then using U, proves(13).
This retains the original time-one contribution and does not replace the nonzero probabilities by1/2 or by an auxiliary law.

Finiteness of M in(12) follows from full Minkowski lattice discreteness together with the two bounded real coordinates, and finiteness of W at each state.
