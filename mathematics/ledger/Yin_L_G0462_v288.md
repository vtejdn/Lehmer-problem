# Arithmetic progression tests for power-primitive unit polynomials

<a id="single"></a>
## What all single progressions can detect

Let \(P\) be the monic minimum of a nontorsion algebraic unit \(\alpha\), and let \(P_d\) be the minimum of \(\alpha^d\).
For fixed positive integers \(D,k\), all sufficiently long arithmetic progressions of each step \(1\le d\le D\) support a nonzero ternary multiple of \(P^k\) if and only if each \(P_d^k\) has at least one nonzero ternary multiple.
Indeed a polynomial on a progression of start \(s\) and step \(d\) is \(X^s H(X^d)\).
At \(\alpha\), the map \(X\mapsto X^d\) has nonzero derivative and the monomial has nonzero value, so the order of vanishing is exactly the order of \(H\) at \(\alpha^d\).
If a ternary multiple exists for every \(d\), the maximum of their chosen degrees plus one suffices for every step and start.

If \(k d\log M(P)<\log 2\), then \(P_d^k\) has a ternary multiple.
Indeed \(\log M(P_d)=d\log M(P)/[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^d)]\), and the [upper density bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement) gives \(D_k(P_d)<1\).
Consequently some full finite interval supports a relation.
Thus \(k D\log M(P)<\log2\) suffices simultaneously at all steps up to \(D\).
Conversely a conjugate of \(\alpha^d\) of modulus at least two makes all these powers dissociated even at multiplicity one, since its largest selected power strictly exceeds the sum of the absolute values of all lower powers.
This obstructs step \(d\) for all positive multiplicities.

For all unions of at most \(B\) progressions in \([0,N)\) with at least \(\delta N\) selected positions, some constituent has length at least \(\delta N/B\).
Once this lower bound is at least two, its step is at most \(2B/\delta\).
It follows that a fixed input with \(k\lfloor2B/\delta\rfloor\log M(P)<\log 2\) survives every such union for all sufficiently large \(N\), whatever the number of tests.
This conditional obstruction applies to power-primitive minima as well as to radical lifts.
For the [degree-ten Lehmer unit](Yin_L_G0462_v287.md#separation), one has \(r^4<2<r^5\), by the rational brackets \(7/6<r<11763/10000\).
For this fixed power-primitive minimum and \(k=1\), steps up to four all eventually admit relations, and every step at least five always gives an admissible progression.
The minimum has no substitution \(P(X)=Q(X^j)\) with \(j\ge2\), because its support contains both exponents zero and one.

<a id="many"></a>
## A common radical witness for all unions of short progression lists

With the same density and list size, put \(h=\lfloor2B/\delta\rfloor\) and \(Q=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,h)\).
Fix a ternary polynomial \(H\) of degree \(e\ge1\) with a nontorsion unit root.
Whenever \(eQ+1\le\delta N/B\), every union in the preceding paragraph contains a translated copy of the support of \(H(X^Q)\).
Indeed the longest constituent contains a progression of step \(Q\) and length \(e+1\) starting at its first element, since its step \(d\) divides \(Q\) and \(eQ/d\) is less than its length.
Thus the same noncyclotomic monic unit factor of \(H(X^Q)\) survives all these tests in the [common-factor criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#polynomial).
To see that such a factor exists, take any \(Q\)-th root of the chosen unit root of \(H\).
For the Lehmer polynomial all these factors have measure at most the Lehmer number, by multiplicativity and \(M(H(X^Q))=M(H)\).

The conclusion still allows \(B\) proportional to \(\log N\) at fixed density.
For \(L(m)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)\), the quotient \(L(2m)/L(m)\) divides \(\binom{2m}{m}\).
Every prime in this quotient has a power between \(m\) and \(2m\), and at most one of its powers occurs in that interval.
The factorial valuation formula therefore gives a factor of that prime in the binomial coefficient.
By iterating over powers of two and rounding \(h\) up to the next power of two one obtains \(Q\le 2^{4h}\).
For instance \(B\le\delta\log_2 N/16\) gives \(Q\le\sqrt N\), which eventually meets the required length for every fixed \(e,\delta>0\).
The witness from this construction can have imprimitive support.

## Two progression blocks for a power-primitive trinomial

Take \(F_n=X^n-X-1\) and its positive root \(\alpha>1\), where \(n\ge4\).
By [Selmer's irreducibility theorem](../audits/literature/Sel56/Yin_U001.md#sel56-u001), \(\alpha\) has degree \(n\), and \(\beta=\alpha^n=\alpha+1\) also has degree \(n\).
For every integer \(3\le j<n\), let
\[
 T_{n,j}=\{in:0\le i<n-j\}\ \cup\ \{in+j:0\le i<n-j\}.
\]
All binary subset sums supported on \(T_{n,j}\) are distinct at \(\alpha\).
Indeed a signed relation gives \(A(\beta)+(\beta-1)^j B(\beta)=0\) with ternary polynomials \(A,B\) of degrees at most \(n-j-1\).
The polynomial \(A(X)+(X-1)^j B(X)\) has degree below \(n\), so it vanishes identically.
If \(B\) is nonzero, normalize the constant sign of \((X-1)^j\) and write the identity as \(\widetilde A=(1-X)^jB\), where \(\widetilde A\) is ternary.
At the position following the lowest nonzero coefficient of \(B\), the difference between the coefficients of \(\widetilde A\) and \(B\) has absolute value \(j\).
This exceeds two even if the compared coefficient lies beyond the written degree of one polynomial, where its coefficient is zero.
Thus \(A=B=0\).
For offset zero the two blocks coincide, and their union is dissociated at every length because \(\beta=1+\alpha>2\).
For offset one the defining relation \(\alpha^n=1+\alpha\) gives a collision when the unshifted block has length at least two and the shifted block has length at least one.
For offset two a collision occurs when the unshifted block has length at least four and the shifted block has length at least two, since \((1-X)^2(1+X)=1-X-X^2+X^3\) is ternary and \(\alpha^2=(\beta-1)^2\).
