# Projected prefixes near a fixed rational ratio and the degeneration at zero

<a id="ratio"></a>
## Stability at an interior rational ratio

Fix \(r=m/n\in(0,1)\) in lowest terms with \(n\ge3\).
There exist positive \(\delta,\gamma\) and \(N_0\), depending on \(r\), with the following property.
Let \(s\in(0,1)\) satisfy \(|s-r|\le\delta\), let \(J\) be the Herglotz transform of a conjugation-invariant signed circle measure of mass one and negative mass at most \(\delta\), and suppose \(B=(1-sw)J\) has integer coefficients through degree \(N\ge N_0\).
Through degree \(\lfloor\gamma N\rfloor\) these coefficients agree with a single series \(B_0\) from the [eight-entry table](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#prefix).
Its first row \(B_0=1/(1-w^2)\) can occur only for \(r\le1/2\); the row \(B_0=1/(1-w)\) can occur only for \(r\le1/3\).
The sign choice \(1+sw\) replaces every listed series by \(B_0(-w)\).
The ratio \(r=1/2\) also has this neighborhood stability with the [nine-entry table](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix), by the comparison below and the row exclusion already proved at that ratio.

## Rational coefficients and the circle bracket

In a hypothetical sequence of failures take \(s_i\to r\), negative mass tending to zero, \(N_i\ge i\), and failure through \(\lfloor N_i/i\rfloor\).
After weak extraction the transforms have limit \(J_0\) of a symmetric probability, and \(B_0=(1-rw)J_0\) has integer coefficients stabilizing at each fixed index.
The function \(K=n/J_0=(n-mw)/B_0\) has positive real part and integer Taylor coefficients, with its linear coefficient congruent to \(-m\bmod n\).
As in the [cyclotomic classification](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#classification), the integer Fourier–Stieltjes decomposition makes \(K\) rational with squarefree cyclotomic denominator, and the analyticity and integrality of \(B_0\) make all poles of \(B_0\) torsion.
The congruence and \(\gcd(n,m)=1\) therefore force
\[
                 K=(n-mw)U/V,\qquad B_0=V/U ,
\]
with \(U,V\) coprime squarefree signed cyclotomic products of constant one.
Indeed the zero at \(n/m\) must be present or all coefficients would be divisible by \(n\), and it can occur only once.
The normalized numerator \(n-mw\) is primitive, and comparing the constant terms gives the displayed factorization.
The continuous doubled density \(\rho=K(w)+K(1/w)\) away from atoms is a nonnegative Laurent polynomial and is not identically zero by evaluation at \(w=n/m\) and at its reciprocal.

Put \(b=\deg V-\deg U-1,\ \epsilon=\operatorname{lc}(U)\operatorname{lc}(V)\).
The bracket \(P\) defined by \(\rho=(U/V)P\) is
\[
                P(w)=(n-mw)+\epsilon w^b(nw-m).
\]
At a torsion zero set \(t=(n-mw)/(nw-m)\), also torsion.
The relation \(n(tw-1)=m(t-w)\) and a Bézout identity for \(m,n\) give \((t-w)/n\) integral over \(\mathbb Z\): if \(um+vn=1\), this number is \(u(tw-1)+v(t-w)\).
All its conjugates have modulus at most \(2/n<1\), so \(t=w=\pm1\), allowing either endpoint.
Every odd-order circle zero must be torsion by even multiplicity in \(\rho\).
The map \(w^b(w-r)/(1-rw)\) on the circle has winding number \(b+1\).
At the fixed target \(-\epsilon\) each odd-order phase crossing contributes \(1\) or \(-1\) to this number and each tangency contributes zero.
This follows by taking a continuous lift of the argument over a full turn starting away from the target and counting upward minus downward passages of its translates by \(2\pi\).
The map is nonconstant since \(0<r<1\) and it has a zero at \(r\).
Thus \(|b+1|\le2\) without requiring the phase to be monotone.
For \(b=-3,1\) neither endpoint is a target when \(\epsilon=1\), excluding those choices.

The remaining factorizations follow from the [integer-branch bracket table](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#classification) by replacing \(j\) with \(1/r\) and multiplying the bracket by \(m\).
Let \(\lambda(t)=(t+\sqrt{t^2-4})/2\) for \(t\ge2\).
Since \(M(\rho)=M(P)\), one obtains

| \(b,\epsilon\) | Necessary range beyond \(0<r<1\) | \(M(\rho)/n\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(-3,-\) | \(r\le1/2\) | \(r\lambda(1/r)\) |
| \(-2,+\) | all | \(r\lambda(1/r+1)\) |
| \(-2,-\) | \(r\le1/3\) | \(r\lambda(1/r-1)\) |
| \(-1,+\) | all | \(1+\sqrt{1-r^2}\) |
| \(-1,-\) | all | \(r\) |
| \(0,+\) | all | \(1-r\) |
| \(0,-\) | all | \(1+r\) |
| \(1,-\) | all | \(1\) |

The range restrictions discard the cases where \(w^2-(1/r)w+1\) or \(w^2-(1/r-1)w+1\), respectively, has a simple circle zero different from \(\pm1\).
Such a root is disjoint from the other bracket factors in its row and would have to be a torsion zero of the bracket.
In the remaining ranges parity of circle multiplicities and \(\deg V-\deg U=b+1\) give the same \(B_0\) as before, including the triple endpoint at \(r=1/3\); in \(b=-1,\epsilon=-1\) the alternative allocation has negative density \(m(w+w^{-1}-2)\).

## Two finite lists of larger rows

Put \(\sigma=2\Re J_0\) on the regular circle and \(D=|1-rw|\sigma\).
Then \(M(D)=M(\rho)/n\) because \(\sigma=n\rho/|K|^2\) and \(M(K)=n\).
All rows in the table except \((-1,+),(0,-)\) have \(M(D)\le4/3\).
For \((-2,+)\) this follows from
\[
 r\lambda(1/r+1)
     =\tfrac12(1+r+\sqrt{(1+r)^2-4r^2})\le4/3 ,
\]
since \((5/3-r)^2-((1+r)^2-4r^2)=4(r-2/3)^2\).
The other stated bounds follow from \(\lambda(t)\le t\).
The [finite-shape lemma](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v9.md#finite-shapes) applies to these small envelopes.

For \((-1,+)\), \(K=n-mw\) and \(D^2=(2-rx)^2/(1+r^2-rx)\) with \(x=w+w^{-1}\).
It is at most four, with equality only at \(w=\pm1\), since the cleared difference is \(r^2(4-x^2)\).
For an integer Laurent row \(H\) bounded by \(D\), the squared mean bound strictly below four and the [trinomial fourth moment](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#extinction) leave at most two unit terms.
Their exponent difference is at most two by counting points of alignment, and \(M(D)=1+\sqrt{1-r^2}<2\).

For \((0,-)\), \(K=(n-mw)/(1-w)\), giving \(D=(1+r)|1-w|^2/|1-rw|\).
A nonzero row \(H\) must vanish at least quadratically at one.
Hence \(L=H/(1-w)\) is an integer Laurent polynomial, vanishes at one and satisfies
\[
               |L(w)|\le (1+r)|1-w|/|1-rw|\le2.
\]
The squared numerator subtracted from \(4|1-rw|^2\) gives \((1-r)^2(2+x)\), so equality is possible only at \(w=-1\).
Its squared mean is strictly below four, and the same fourth-moment argument leaves at most two unit terms in \(L\).
Vanishing at one excludes a monomial, and all the alignment points of a binomial must be \(-1\).
Thus the only possible nonzero \(H\) are \(\pm w^a(1-w)^2\), \(a\in\mathbb Z\), and \(M(D)=1+r<2\).

These finite lists up to shifts yield the same [harmonic extinction](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#extinction).
The phase ratio of a nonzero representative \(S\) now has factors \((n-mw^{-1})/(n-mw)\).
It has a pole at \(n/m>1\), since \(M(S)<2<n\) and vanishing at \(n/m\) would imply divisibility of the shifted integral polynomial by the primitive factor \(n-mw\) of measure \(n\).
The prime specialization and last-harmonic argument therefore exclude every nonzero integral family \(H_k=(1-rw)\sigma c_k\) of conditional probability moments.

## Coefficients before and after the first discrepancy

Let \(n_i\) be the first discrepancy of \(B_i=(1-s_iw)J_i\) from the identified \(B_0\).
Then \(n_i\to\infty\) and \(n_i/N_i\to0\) in the hypothesized counterexamples.
The exact difference identity is
\[
 (1-s_iw)(J_i-J_0)=B_i-B_0+(s_i-r)wJ_0 .
\]
The coefficients of \(J_0\) have modulus at most two.
Thus \([w^a](J_i-J_0)=O(|s_i-r|/(1-s_i))=o(1)\) uniformly for \(0\le a<n_i\).
In the [two-circle comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v52.md#harmonics) this gives zero limiting differences at all negative offsets from \(n_i\), exactly as for a fixed multiplier.
The identification of the deterministic phases at limiting torsion atoms and subtraction of all their harmonics follows unchanged.
At \(kn_i+l\) and its predecessor, \(k\ge1,l\in\mathbb Z\) fixed, the coefficients of \(J_i\) are uniformly bounded by the bounded total variation.
Replacing \(1-s_iw\) by \(1-rw\) consequently changes the coefficient of \(B_i\) by \(o(1)\), preserving the integral limits of all centered differences and the nonzero first discrepancy.
These differences give an integral Laurent row family \(H_k=(1-rw)\sigma c_k\), \(H_1\ne0\), by the [moving harmonic argument](Yin_L_G0462_v65.md#projection).
This contradicts extinction.
For \(r=1/2\) the same moving comparison uses the nine profiles and their [fixed-ratio extinction](Yin_L_G0462_v57.md#prefix).

## A positive-measure counterexample to uniformity as the ratio tends to zero

The constants in the prefix conclusion cannot be uniform over all \(r=1/j\), even for probability measures.
Fix \(l\ge3\) and take any \(N\ge l\).
Choose an even integer \(L\) with \(Ll>N\).
For some \(t>0\) the continuous function
\[
                1+\cos(l\theta)+t\cos(Ll\theta)
\]
is strictly positive on the circle: the last cosine equals one at all the zeros of the nonnegative first two terms, and on the compact complement of small neighborhoods of the zeros the first two terms have a positive minimum.
Choose any sufficiently large integer \(j\) and put
\[
             J(w)=\frac{1+w^l}{1-w/j}+tw^{Ll}.
\]
Its real part on the circle is positive by uniform convergence to the preceding function as \(j\to\infty\).
Its analytic disk extension is therefore the Herglotz transform of its symmetric probability density \(\Re J\) against normalized arc length.
The coefficients of \((1-w/j)J\) through degree \(N\) are exactly those of \(1+w^l\).
No series from the eight-entry table matches these through degree \(l\).
Taking arbitrarily large \(N\) disproves any common positive prefix fraction and starting length.

One can even prescribe a sufficiently small negative atom at one while preserving those coefficients.
Add \(a(D_N\,dm-\delta_1)\) to the measure, where \(D_N(w)=\sum_{k=-N}^N w^k\), \(m\) is normalized arc length and \(a>0\).
This addition has zero moments through order \(N\) and zero total mass.
For sufficiently small \(a\) the corrected continuous density stays positive, so the sole negative atom has mass \(-a\).
In particular \(a=j/[4(q-1)]\) is possible by choosing a sufficiently large integer \(q\).
This example tests the moment hypotheses; occurrence as a trace numerator and denominator in the Pisot inverse construction is not established.
