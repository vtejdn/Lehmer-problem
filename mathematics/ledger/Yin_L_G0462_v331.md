# Does a real trace pole force a fourth root in a dyadic disk?

<a id="answer"></a>
## The two-root bound and the missing global input

Let \(q\ge2\) be integral, \(p=q+1,\ s=p/q,\ d=2D\), and write \(F(Z)=Z^D H(Z+Z^{-1}-2)\).
The question concerns \(q^dF(s)=(2q+1)^2\) and a real root \(1<T<s\).
For an actual integral Salem realization, \(T\) is the only exterior root, its inverse is inside, and every other root has modulus one.
Both \(T\) and \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\) are positive powers of the same algebraic unit, so one pair of coprime exponents works at every conjugate. [Trace realization](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v891.md#return) and [unit relation](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v179.md#statement)

For every such realization, **each open disk \(v_2(z-c)>1\) contains at most two conjugates**, including for centers in the algebraic closure.
Thus forcing even a third root into a closed disk of radius \(1/8\) for all sufficiently large \(q\) would exclude this construction at unbounded height.
The proof takes logarithms of the graph equation on disks away from the signs and bounds the number of zeros by the quadratic coefficient.
Near \(1\) or \(-1\), the parameter \((\sigma z-1)/(\sigma z+1)\) gives a series in its square with at most one root of positive valuation; the two signs cannot both contribute. [Disk proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v188.md#ordinary) and [signs](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v188.md#signs)
When \(v_2(v-\epsilon u)<v_2(qp)\), with \(\epsilon=1\) at even \(q\) and \(-1\) at odd \(q\), different unit roots of the graph are as far apart as the corresponding roots of unity, and each radius-\(1/8\) disk contains at most one. [Exponent criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v185.md#statement)

## Exact arithmetic examples on the two sides of the signature condition

At every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of four there are arbitrarily large degrees for which the exact pole holds on a single irreducible integral orbit, all roots have distinct binary residues, and \(T\) is the only real exterior root.
With absolute constants its location satisfies
\[
        \frac{c}{D p^d}\le s-T\le \frac{Cq^2}{D p^d}.
\]
All generators \(T,T^{-1},Y,Y^{-1}\) are integral.
The conjugates on the unit circle account for \(D-3\) reciprocal pairs.
Exactly one additional pair of nonreal conjugates has modulus \(R>1\); it gives \(M(F)=T R^2\asymp pqD\) and makes the two units multiplicatively independent.
This proves that integrality, the pole and the uniqueness of the **real** exterior root are compatible with complete dyadic separation. [Construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v187.md#round) and [estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v187.md#pin)
The same distinction is visible in degree eight for every \(q\ge2\), with \(p^8(s-T)/q\to 2/5\). [Explicit quartic trace](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v187.md#quartic)

If all other conjugates except \(T^{-1}\) are required to stay on the circle, releasing integrality at just one specified odd prime also permits complete dyadic separation.
Here \(s-T\asymp q p^{-d}\), the first \(q/2+1\) power sums vanish, and the primitive integer polynomial has Mahler measure \(\ell^a T\). [One-prime models](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v184.md#statement)
For any precision \(B\) chosen before the degree, one can enforce the full power congruence modulo \(2^B\) and put the binary period within \(O_{q,B}(1)\) of the irrational slope predicted by the real equation.
The characteristic-zero power equality fails. [Period construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v189.md#statement)
These two families are separate mathematical examples, each preserving one of the conditions released in the other.

## What the real pole can already exclude

The real exponent estimate and a lower bound for a linear form in logarithms separate the binary period \(A\) of a genuine realization from low-denominator rational multiples of the degree.
For example, \(|A-nd|>d^{3/4}\) for all integers \(n\) at sufficiently large \(d\). [Period estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v186.md#statement)
If the entire binary reduction is irreducible, then \(d=\operatorname{ord}_A(2)\mid\varphi(A)\).
Using \(n=\varphi(A)/d\) shows that \(A\) must have a prime divisor of size \(d^{o(1)}\); this excludes a single orbit of prime order or prime-power order of bounded exponent.
If the order is prime but the reduction splits, the number of irreducible factors must grow. [Restrictions on the residual orbits](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v191.md#statement)

Independently, at most \(C2^B\) roots of the graph equation have \([\mathbb Q_2(z):\mathbb Q_2]\le B\), for an absolute \(C\).
The proof counts units near their Teichmüller representatives and treats ramification and residue degree separately.
For every putative genuine sequence with \(q\to\infty\), \(d\to\infty\), and the fraction of conjugates below any cutoff \(B\) with \(\log_2 d-B\to\infty\) tends to zero. [Local degree count](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#statement)
The order \(2^B\) is attained in the graph equation alone. [Sharpness example](../claims/Yin_C_G0901_v190.md#sharpness)
A sufficient new global result would put a fixed positive fraction of the orbit below such a cutoff using the full Salem signature and simultaneous integrality, or would directly force three roots into one disk.
The present results supply neither lower bound on concentration, and existence at unbounded height remains open.
Even excluding this first-nonunit-pole construction would settle only this sufficient route to a vanishing Lehmer gap. [Scope of the route](Yin_L_G0462_v328.md#gap)
