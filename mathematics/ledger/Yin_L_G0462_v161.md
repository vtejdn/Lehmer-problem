# All invariant factors of the Salem power order at three

<a id="group"></a>
## The exponent and the multiplicities of the cyclic factors

Let \(6\mid d\), let \(p>3\) be a prime dividing \(d\), and put \(m=d/p\).
Let \(\alpha\) be a root of the Salem polynomial \(P=x^d-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x^j+1\) and set \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Then there is an isomorphism of finite additive groups
\[
 \frac{\mathbb Z[\alpha]}{\mathbb Z[\beta]}
 \ \simeq\
   \left(\bigoplus_{t=1}^{p-2}
        (\mathbb Z/3^t\mathbb Z)^{m}\right)
     \ \oplus\ (\mathbb Z/3^{p-1}\mathbb Z)^{m-1}.                       \tag{1}
\]
The group exponent equals \(3^{p-1}\), and this is already the least positive integer \(s\) with \(s\alpha\in\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Thus in the [full-resultant upper test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#statement) the exponent has \((\log s)/p\to\log3\), even when \(d/p\) varies.

## Triangular generators before deleting the root one

Write \(E=(x-1)P\) and work first in \(D=\mathbb Z[x]/(E)\), writing \(x\) also for its residue class.
Let \(X=x^p,\ R=\mathbb Z[X]\subset D,\ Z=x-2,\ B=X^m+2\), where \(R\) denotes the image of evaluation, and note the exact identity \(BZ=-3\) in \(D\).
All \(d+1\) distinct roots of \(E\) have distinct \(p\)-th powers, including the root one, by the [rotated-root calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem).
Consequently \(R\) has the power basis through degree \(d\), since the distinct powers have a monic integral polynomial of degree \(d+1\).
The Vandermonde index of these two full power bases is
\[
                            [D:R]=3^{d(p-1)/2}.
\]
Indeed the calculation before removal of the pairs involving one in the same source gives \(3^{d(p-1)}\) for the square of this determinant quotient.
This argument for the full squarefree polynomial uses all its complex roots and hence also applies in the product algebra.

Let \(c_i=\binom pi2^{p-i}\) for \(0\le i\le p\).
For \(1\le r<p\) set \(t=p-r\) and take the monic degree-\(r\) polynomial
\[
                        Y_r(Z)=\sum_{i=t}^p c_i Z^{i-t}.
\]
Multiplying \(X=(Z+2)^p\) by \(B^t\), the terms with \(i<t\) become \(c_i(-3)^i B^{t-i}\in R\), while the other terms sum to \((-3)^tY_r(Z)\).
Therefore \(3^{p-r}Y_r(Z)\in R\).
An integral basis of \(D\) consists of \(X^j x^r\) with \(0\le j<m,\ 0\le r<p\), together with \(X^m\), by the consecutive degrees from zero to \(d\).
Replacing \(x^r\) by \(Y_r(x-2)\) for \(r\ge1\) is an integral unitriangular change within each block \(j\).
The classes with \(r=0\) and the additional \(X^m\) already lie in \(R\).
We obtain a surjection
\[
              \bigoplus_{r=1}^{p-1}(\mathbb Z/3^{p-r}\mathbb Z)^m
                 \longrightarrow D/R,\qquad e_{r,j}\longmapsto X^jY_r(Z).
\]
The source has order \(3^{mp(p-1)/2}=[D:R]\), so this is an isomorphism.

## The single cyclic summand lost at the root one

Evaluation at \(\alpha\) maps \(D/R\) onto \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]/\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
In \(D\) the kernel of evaluation at \(\alpha\) is generated additively by \(P\), since \(xP=P\).
An element whose evaluation belongs to \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) can first be reduced by an element of \(R\), so the induced kernel on quotients is cyclic.
The [Salem index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) is \(3^{(d-2)(p-1)/2}\).
The cyclic kernel thus has order \(3^{p-1}\), the exponent of \(D/R\).
In the decomposition just obtained, a generator of this kernel must have a unit coordinate in a summand of this highest order.
Projecting onto that coordinate identifies the kernel with the coordinate group, so the complementary coordinate groups give the quotient (1).

To verify the denominator of the element itself, \(Y_1(Z)=Z+2p=x+2(p-1)\).
Thus the \(m\) classes \(3^{p-2}X^j x\), \(0\le j<m\), are independent elements of order three in \(D/R\).
If \(3^{p-2}\alpha\) belonged to \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\), all of them would map to zero, since their evaluations are obtained by multiplication by \(\beta^j\).
A cyclic group has at most one independent element of order three, whereas \(m\ge6\).
The image of \(x\) therefore has order \(3^{p-1}\), as claimed.
