# Dense positive-constant Pisot traces with a sparse cyclotomic multiple

<a id="clearing"></a>
## The minimum term count over all cyclotomic multiples

Let \(\theta\) be a Pisot number with positive integer constant \(q\ge2\), branch index \(j\), trace numerator degree \(h\) and [trace lift](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) \(\Pi=(1-z^2)z^h C(z+z^{-1})\).
A cyclotomic multiplier \(H\) is a product of cyclotomic polynomials, with its sign chosen so that \(H(0)=1\).
The empty product is allowed.
For every fixed \(j,K\) there is a positive lower bound for \(\theta-q\) if some \(H\Pi\) has at most \(K\) terms.
The degree and the multiplicities of \(H\) are unrestricted.
Equivalently, on any escaping sequence on a bounded set of branches, the minimum number of terms of \(H\Pi\) tends to infinity.
The [branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) also bounds \(q\) on each of these fixed \(j,K\) classes.

More generally, let \(j\) vary on a sequence with \(\theta-q\to0\) and suppose multipliers \(H\) give a bounded term count.
Write \(F=H\Pi/j\) and \(n=\deg F\).
Eventually \(\Pi/j\) has a first nonintegral coefficient, at an index \(d\le h\) common to \(\Pi/j\) and \(F\).
Then \(d=o(n)\) and for every fixed \(A\ge1\),
\[
           \max_{0\le t\le A d}\operatorname{dist}([z^t]F,\mathbb Z)
                   \ \longrightarrow\ 0.
\]
At \(d\) the original and multiplied coefficients have the same fractional part.
Thus the reduced denominator of the original coefficient there tends to infinity.
In particular a bound on just this first denominator, or an entirely integral normalized lift, gives a uniform positive gap for fixed \(K\).
The [first-scale theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v26.md#first-scale) previously imposed the term bound on the original lift.
On 2026-11-17 agent-S adapted its argument for Yin's [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) and constructed the [actual dense branch-two inputs](#example) below.

## Original atoms and the first nonintegral coefficient

On a sequence as above write \(B_0=B^\sharp\) for the original monic denominator lift, and \(B=HB_0\) for its integral multiple of the same degree \(n=2h+2+\deg H\) as \(F\).
The [inverse representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) gives
\[
 B=FJ,\qquad B(0)=F(0)=J(0)=1,\qquad
 M(F)=1,\qquad 1<M(B)=M(B_0)\longrightarrow1 .
\]
Here \(J\) is the original series of a signed symmetric measure of mass one, with negative mass tending to zero at \(1\) and with \(2h\) distinct positively charged interior lifts.
Multiplication creates no new positive atoms in this measure.
We have \(h\to\infty\) by bounded-degree finiteness for the integral \(B_0\) of bounded measure.
The [sparse height inequality](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds the coefficients of \(F\) and their absolute sum in terms of its term count.
Convolving with the bounded Taylor coefficients of \(J\) bounds all integer coefficients of \(B\), even if \(H\) has large coefficients.

If \(\Pi/j\) is integral, then \(C/j\) is monic integral.
Indeed removing \(1-z^2\) by formal division leaves an integral symmetric lift, and the polynomials \(z^k+z^{-k}\) are monic integral in \(z+z^{-1}\) for \(k\ge1\).
The nonmonic part in the [trace multiplier obstruction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#statement) is then \(1\), and the admissible constant polynomial \(1\) contradicts escape on such a subsequence.
Hence \(d\) exists eventually.
Antireciprocity of \(\Pi\) of degree \(2h+2\) and its zero middle coefficient give \(d\le h\).
At any degree through \(d\), multiplication by \(H\) contributes an integer combination of the earlier integral coefficients to the coefficient of \(\Pi/j\).
Thus \(F\) has the same first nonintegral index and the same fractional part there.

Suppose \(d\ge c n\) on a subsequence, for some \(c>0\).
Then \(n=O(h)\), and formal division of \(B\) by \(F\) gives integer Taylor coefficients of \(J\) up to \(d-1\).
The [positive-atom count](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#atoms) and [integer-prefix models](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#model) give \(J=V/U\) on a prefix of length at least a fixed positive fraction of \(h\), with \(U,V\) equal to one or signed binomials of degree \(O(h)\) and constant one.
The nonvanishing argument uses the original \(2h\) atoms since the degree of each moving-index test there is \(o(h)\).
The [two interval cutoffs](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#cutoff) now apply to \(UB-VF=UF(J-V/U)\).
Indeed the polynomial on the left has degree \(O(h)\), the constant-free difference on the right has a zero prefix of length comparable to \(h\) and uniformly bounded representing measures, and \(\|UF\|_\infty,\|VF\|_\infty\) are bounded.
They give a bounded circle \(L^1\) norm for the integral \(UB\).
Its [bounded term count](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) contradicts the [fixed-support Mahler gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), since \(M(UB)=M(B)>1\) tends to one.
This proves \(d=o(n)\) without a bound on \(n/h\).

## Propagation up to the degree of the multiplier product

We check the change of scale in the [first-scale proof for the original pair](Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#scales).
If the asserted convergence fails, extract a coefficient at an exponent \(e\le A d\) tending to a noninteger.
Freeze the bounded coefficient tuple of \(F\) and the persistent relations among its support exponents.
The [uniform logarithmic truncation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#moving) gives measure one to the limiting polynomial from \(M(F)=1\).
Partition the scales from \(d\) to \(n\) using the finitely many support exponents and subsequential limits of their pairwise ratios.
At each separated pair \(s,S\), freeze the terms at \(O(s)\) to get a face polynomial of constant one and measure one by [face exposure](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v24.md#faces).
It contains the selected noninteger coefficient since \(e=O(d)\), and every omitted exponent is at least a fixed positive fraction of \(S\).
Its specialization \(T\) approximates the actual prefix in coefficient absolute sum by \(o(1)\), and \(M(T)\to1\) by Lawton specialization.

The [initial all-interval estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v106.md#initial) applies to \(B=FJ\) at \(d\).
For bounded \(d\) the bounded integer coefficients suffice.
For unbounded \(d\) the integral prefix of \(J\) gives its [short integer model](Yin_L_G0462_v68.md#low), because each moving-index test of degree \(o(d)\) has degree \(o(h)\) by \(d\le h\).
The ensuing block Bessel bound uses the signed measure, integer coefficients of \(B\), and bounded absolute sums of partial sums of \(F\), including the finitely many transitions at its actual offsets.
It provides \(P=1\) or a signed binomial of degree \(O(d)\) with a uniform bound on the coefficient absolute sum of \(PB\) on every interval of length \(d\).

At later scales the positive atom count is no longer used.
For \(s/t,t/S\to0\), every mixed row at \(kt+O(s)\) with fixed \(k>0\) lies in the prefix of the current face.
Its integrality in the limit follows from \(B=FJ\) and the \(o(1)\) replacement error, even when \(t\) exceeds the original \(h\).
The [nonintegral-face density argument](Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#lattice) and [intermediate row proof](Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#scales) therefore give a bounded product \(U\) of cyclotomic binomials of degree \(O(s)\) for which \(UB\) vanishes on \(Ls\le l\le S/L\) with fixed \(L\).
These arguments require the bounded integer rows, the nonintegral coefficient in the frozen face and the weak positive limit of the signed measures.
They impose no lower bound on the number of positive atoms relative to \(S\).

The [real-coefficient induction](Yin_L_G0462_v110.md#propagation) upgrades the all-interval estimate for \(PB\) from \(s\) to \(S\) with new multiplier \(PUT\).
In detail, \(PUB\) has bounded coefficient absolute sum up to \(S/L\) using the preceding estimate and the zero interval.
There its coefficients differ by \(o(1)\) from those of \(PUTJ\), which gives the bounded weighted Gram matrices on each fixed number of indices by the two-sided moment identity.
Off the transitions of \(F\), Bessel controls the count of nonzero coefficients of \(PUTB\) because \(B\) has bounded integer coefficients and the finitely supported multiplier coefficients range in a fixed finite real set.
In a transition of length \(O(s)\) the previous all-interval bound applies to \((UT)(PB)\).
Thus the induction reaches \(n\), and the final \(PB\) has degree \(O(n)\), bounded term count, coefficients in a fixed finite real set and constant one.
Its measure tends to one strictly from above, since \(M(B)>1\) and each face factor has constant one and measure tending to one.
The [fixed-coefficient endpoint argument](Yin_L_G0462_v97.md#translations) rules this out.
This proves the first-scale assertion, and the original fractional part then forces its reduced denominator to diverge.
A fixed branch bounds this denominator by \(j\), proving the stated fixed-branch conclusion.

## Genuine branch-two inputs with an unbounded number of terms

Write \(P=z^3-5z^2+5z-3\), \(P^*=z^3P(1/z)\) and \(Q=(P-P^*)/2=2z^3-5z^2+5z-2\).
For all sufficiently large multiples \(N\) of six, put
\[
               L_N=z^N P-Q,\qquad f_N=L_N/(z^3+1).
\]
The cubic \(P\) has discriminant \(-268\), and \(P(3)=-6,\ P(5)=22\).
Thus it has one real root \(\theta_*\in(3,5)\), and its nonreal roots have modulus \(\sqrt{3/\theta_*}<1\).
On the circle \(|Q|\le|P|\) by the triangle inequality, with equality precisely at \(P^*=-P\), or \(z^3=-1\) since \(P+P^*=-2(z^3+1)\).
At these three points \(Q=P\) and \(z^N=1\), so all are roots of \(L_N\), and no other circle roots are possible.
They are eventually simple because the derivative there contains \(N z^{N-1}P\) with \(P\ne0\), while the other terms are bounded.
Rouché for \(z^NP-tQ\), \(0<t<1\), followed by \(t\uparrow1\), permits at most one strictly exterior root counted with multiplicity.
The signs at fixed positive points bracketing \(\theta_*\) above one give one such root \(\theta_N\to\theta_*\).
After the displayed exact division all other roots of the monic integral \(f_N\) lie strictly inside the disk.
Its nonzero constant \(2\) prevents a further monic factor formed solely from interior roots, proving irreducibility and the Pisot property.

Since \(z^3+1\) equals its reversal, \(f_N/f_N^*=L_N/L_N^*\), with stars taken at the actual degrees.
The low coefficients of \(L_N\) start \(2,-5\), those of \(L_N^*\) start \(1,-5\), and \([z]f_N/f_N^*=5\).
The branch index is \(5-2^2+1=2\).
As \(f_N\) has even degree \(N\), its delay-one denominator \(D=zf_N+f_N^*\) has odd degree and \(D=(z+1)B^\sharp\).
The [trace identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) and [definition of \(K_q\)](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) give
\[
  (z^3+1)\Pi=\frac{1-z}{z}\big[(1-2z)L_N-(2-z)L_N^*\big].
\]
Its part obtained from the low blocks \(-Q,P^*\) is \((1-z^2)(z^2-2z+2)\), and terms from the high blocks begin no earlier than \(N-1\).
Antireciprocity gives the opposite reflected copy at the upper end, so the product has exactly ten terms for large \(N\).
Below \(N-1\) the lift \(\Pi\) therefore agrees with the Taylor series of
\[
 \frac{(1-z^2)(z^2-2z+2)}{1+z^3}
                  =2-z-\frac{z}{1-z+z^2}.
\]
Here \((1-z+z^2)^{-1}=(1+z)/(1+z^3)\) has a periodic sequence of coefficients with a positive proportion nonzero.
Thus the original term count grows at least linearly in \(N\) on actual branch-two inputs, while \(z^3+1\) clears it to bounded support.
Their positive excess tends to \(\theta_*-2\).
The [earlier sparse-seed criterion](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) also covers these examples because \((z^3+1)f_N=L_N\) has bounded support.
They distinguish bounded support of a trace multiple from bounded support of the original trace on actual inputs.
Constructing an escaping sequence on this branch would require the term count to diverge even after arbitrary cyclotomic multiplication.
