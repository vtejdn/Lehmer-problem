## Exact input and nearest retained comparisons

The tested object is the [normalized rank-two germ in L133 v15](Yin_L_G0133_v15.md#universal-normalized-rank-two-germ), including its actual original pole expense and analytic-part functional.
The original input is a monic irreducible reciprocal P in Z[z] of degree 2d, with P(0)=1 and m(P)>0.
Write
\[
 p=\varepsilon z^{-d}P
   =c+\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_k(z^k+z^{-k}),\qquad
 \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},\qquad p_d=\varepsilon,
\]
\[
 g=2\sum_{k=1}^{d}p_kz^k,\qquad
 q=\frac{\varepsilon z^d}{P},\qquad
 F_2=\begin{pmatrix}1-gq&2g-g^2q\\-q&1-gq\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1}
\]
All circle integrals use normalized Haar measure and all logarithms are natural.
The exact interface is [C868 v5](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#statement), at its [selected rational-price assessment](../claims/Yin_C_G0868_v5.md#decision-whole-rational-price-review-2026-11-05).
For this normalized integer germ, L133 v15 gives
\[
 N(F_2)=m(P),\qquad
 {\cal A}(P)=\frac12\int\log(1+|g|^2)
             +\frac12\int\log(1+|g+2c|^2),
\]
\[
 {\cal A}(P)-\log2\le\tau_{\min}(F_2)\le{\cal A}(P).
 \tag{2}
\]
The full determinant expense tau_min is not divided by rank or degree.
No proof of that price interface is repeated here.

The [six-term Salem completion in C222](../claims/Yin_C_G0222_v1.md#sparse-salem-delay-family) gives a sparse raw q_N=p_N c_N with an irreducible Salem factor p_N and a cyclotomic factor c_N.

## The actual original Salem family

Use exactly the [C494 moving-exponent schedule](../claims/Yin_C_G0494_v1.md#fixed-trace-moving-exponent-schedule), with its [selected complete SELF adoption](../claims/Yin_C_G0494_v1.md#decision-complete-current-self-adoption).
That decision has status proved, date 2026-10-22T01:20, and selects R706's whole current SELF finding.
Its MS05/RY26 source-statement and selected construction-proof scope remains unchanged; this use adds no whole original-source audit.
In particular the all-large-degree counting theorem is not an input.

Let K be the exact even four-exponent killer used there:
\[
 K=\left(\prod_{\substack{\ell\le48\\\ell\ \text{prime}}}\ell\right)
    \operatorname{lcm}\left(1,\ldots,
                  \left\lceil8^5 5^{5/2}\right\rceil\right).
 \tag{3}
\]
For each j>=1 select
\[
 a_j=\min\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ell>(j+1)K\},\qquad
 b_j=\min\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ell>(K+a_j+3)^2\},
\]
\[
 t_j=\min\{\ell\text{ prime}:\ell>(K+a_j+b_j+3)^2\},\qquad
 L_j=K+a_j+b_j+2.
 \tag{4}
\]
Here t_j is the exponent called c_j in C494; it is distinct from the balanced central coefficient c in (1).
Thus t_j>(L_j+1)^2.
For ell=(K,a_j,b_j,t_j), the actual numerator and original polynomial are
\[
 N_j=(z^2-1)\prod_{i=0}^{3}(z^{\ell_i}-1)
   -\frac z2\sum_{s=0}^{3}(z^{\ell_s}+1)
                       \prod_{i\ne s}(z^{\ell_i}-1),\qquad
 P_j=\frac{N_j}{(z-1)^3}.
 \tag{5}
\]
C494 supplies the integrality of N_j, exact removal of (z-1)^3, and the fact that P_j is the actual monic irreducible reciprocal Salem minimal polynomial with constant term one and no remaining cyclotomic factor.
It also supplies
\[
 n_j=\deg P_j=t_j+L_j-3\quad\text{even},\qquad
 \log2<m(P_j)<\log4.
 \tag{6}
\]
The even degree follows from even K and the three odd primes.
We keep every coefficient of the original P_j; no reducible cyclotomic padding is inserted.

The [exact decomposition and G2 calculation in C494](../claims/Yin_C_G0494_v1.md#moving-exponent-gram-feasibility) give
\[
 N_j=A_j+z^{t_j}B_j,\qquad A_j,B_j\in\mathbb Z[z],
 \qquad\deg A_j,\deg B_j\le L_j,
\]
\[
 A_j=(z-1)^2A_{2,j},\qquad
 B_j=(z-1)^2B_{2,j},\qquad
 A_{2,j},B_{2,j}\in\mathbb Z[z],
\]
\[
 \deg A_{2,j},\deg B_{2,j}\le L_j-2,\qquad
 A_{2,j}(1)=W_j:=Ka_j+Kb_j+a_jb_j.
 \tag{7}
\]
These are reused exact identities, rather than conclusions inferred from coefficient growth or numerical samples.
The prime schedule gives W_j>=a_jb_j tending to infinity.

<a id="canonical-central-coefficient-plateau"></a>
## The middle coefficient is exactly minus W_j

After the two known divisions in (7), equation (5) reads
\[
 P_j=\frac{A_{2,j}+z^{t_j}B_{2,j}}{z-1}.
 \tag{8}
\]
At zero, the formal series 1/(z-1)=-sum_(n>=0) z^n has integer coefficients.
For 0<=n<t_j the shifted B term contributes nothing, so
\[
 [z^n]P_j=-\sum_{s=0}^{n}[z^s]A_{2,j}.
\]
Since deg A_(2,j)<=L_j-2, every integer n with L_j-2<=n<t_j therefore satisfies
\[
 \boxed{[z^n]P_j=-A_{2,j}(1)=-W_j.}
 \tag{9}
\]
This is a plateau in the coefficients of the original irreducible polynomial.
Let h_j=n_j/2=(t_j+L_j-3)/2.
It is an integer, and
\[
 h_j-(L_j-2)=\frac{t_j-L_j+1}{2}>0,\qquad
 t_j-h_j=\frac{t_j-L_j+3}{2}>0.
\]
Thus the middle exponent lies in (9), and
\[
 [z^{h_j}]P_j=-W_j.
 \tag{10}
\]
For either phase epsilon in (1), the corresponding balanced central coefficient satisfies c_j^{bal}=-epsilon W_j and hence |c_j^{bal}|=W_j.

<a id="canonical-analytic-expense-divergence"></a>
## Central growth forces the complete canonical expense to diverge

For any input in (1) with c nonzero, the leading coefficient of g is 2epsilon and the constant coefficient of g+2c is 2c.
The root formula for Mahler measure and Jensen at zero respectively give
\[
 m(g)\ge\log2,\qquad m(g+2c)\ge\log(2|c|).
 \tag{11}
\]
The second inequality follows by applying Jensen's formula to the nonzero constant coefficient; any zeros inside the disk contribute nonnegative terms.
Boundary zeros cause no difficulty because the logarithm of the modulus of a nonzero polynomial is integrable.
Applying the convexity inequality already used in L133 v15,
\[
 \int\log(1+|f|^2)\ge\log(1+e^{2m(f)}),
\]
to the two polynomials in (11) gives
\[
 {\cal A}(P)\ge\frac12\log5+\frac12\log(1+4c^2).
 \tag{12}
\]
On the actual family (5), equations (10)--(12) imply
\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}(P_j)\ge\frac12\log5
                    +\frac12\log(1+4W_j^2)\longrightarrow\infty.}
 \tag{13}
\]
Using the complete price comparison (2), rather than only its boundary term, yields
\[
 \boxed{\tau_{\min}(F_{2,j})
    \ge\frac12\log\bigl(5(1+4W_j^2)\bigr)-\log2
    \ge\log W_j+\frac12\log5\longrightarrow\infty.}
 \tag{14}
\]
Together with 0<m(P_j)<log4, this proves
\[
 \frac{\tau_{\min}(F_{2,j})}{m(P_j)}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{15}
\]
Consequently this particular canonical realization admits no finite uniform constant C with tau_min(F_2)<=C m(P) on all positive-measure monic irreducible reciprocal original inputs.
The failure occurs already on actual Salem minimal polynomials and includes the original pole expense exactly once.

<a id="canonical-positive-symbol-upper-bound"></a>
## Strict positivity instead gives an explicit central-coefficient upper bound

Assume now that the phase in (1) has been chosen so that p is strictly positive everywhere on the circle.
Then c=integral p is positive.
Put H=g+c; on the circle Re H=p.
The harmonic minimum principle gives Re H>0 on the closed disk.
Consequently
\[
 f=\frac{H-c}{H+c}=\frac{g}{g+2c}
\]
is analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk, satisfies f(0)=0, and has |f|<1 throughout that disk.
Here f is a new scalar function local to this calculation, not the original matrix germ.
Solving for g gives
\[
 g=\frac{2cf}{1-f},\qquad g+2c=\frac{2c}{1-f}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Let
\[
 B=(c+\sqrt{c^2+1})^2>1,\qquad
 B+B^{-1}=4c^2+2.
\]
For every complex f with |f|<=1, direct subtraction gives
\[
 B|1-f/B|^2-\bigl(|1-f|^2+4c^2|f|^2\bigr)
       =(B-1)(1-|f|^2)\ge0,
\]
\[
 B|1-f/B|^2-\bigl(|1-f|^2+4c^2\bigr)
       =(1-B^{-1})(1-|f|^2)\ge0.
 \tag{17}
\]
Both 1-f and 1-f/B are zero-free on a neighborhood of the closed disk and equal one at zero.
Their logarithmic modulus means on the circle are therefore zero.
Substituting (16) into each summand of the functional in (2), then taking logarithms in (17), shows
\[
 \int\log(1+|g|^2)\le\log B,\qquad
 \int\log(1+|g+2c|^2)\le\log B.
\]
Thus this positive-symbol subdomain satisfies the exact bound
\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}(P)\le\log B
             =2\operatorname{arsinh}(c),\qquad
         \tau_{\min}(F_2)\le2\operatorname{arsinh}(c).}
 \tag{18}
\]
Strict positivity is essential to this argument and is not supplied for the Salem family (5), whose original polynomials have circle roots.
Equation (18) controls expense by the actual central coefficient, not by m(P).
It supplies no upper comparison of 2 arsinh(c) with C m(P), and hence no original-measure gap.
This bounded calculation does not reopen the fixed-central-coefficient subproblem or claim a new fixed-central gap.

<a id="canonical-failure-near-one-boundary"></a>
## Precise boundary and the direct cancellation comparison

The family (5) has m(P_j)>log2 throughout.
Thus (15) does not refute a comparison restricted to a sufficiently smaller positive-measure interval, for example 0<m(P)<log2.
It also does not exclude optimized signed certificates, other normalized germs or general matrix realizations.
No family with m(P) tending to zero, new original Mahler lower bound or unrestricted G0001 strategy follows.
The useful conclusion is the failure of the stated all-input canonical price comparison; a smaller-range comparison would need its own original-input argument.
The positive-symbol estimate (18) concerns a different domain and retains its explicit central-coefficient expense.

The most direct removal of the polynomial part returns to an already retained mechanism.
Write E(t)=[[1,t],[0,1]] and L(t)=[[1,0],[t,1]].
The factorization in (1) gives the exact identity
\[
 E(-g)F_2E(-g)=L(-q).
 \tag{19}
\]
Because g(0)=q(0)=0, the right side is a normalized integer germ.
The two outer factors are everywhere-invertible polynomial unimodular matrices, so they introduce no new poles and preserve each original simple pole's nonzero rank-one residue and local colength.
This gives N(L(-q))=m(P).
The [exact shear formula](Yin_L_G0133_v10.md#rank-one-shear-exact-cost) and [integer-numerator floor](Yin_L_G0133_v10.md#integer-numerator-boundary-floor), specialized to the monomial numerator -epsilon z^d, give
\[
 \tau_{\min}(L(-q))
   =m(P)+\int\operatorname{arsinh}\frac1{2|P|},\qquad
 J(L(-q))\ge\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{e^{-m(P)}}2,
\]
\[
 \tau_{\min}(L(-q))\ge\operatorname{arsinh}(1/2).
 \tag{20}
\]
These are the existing monomial-numerator case, also identified at the [companion SISO interface](Yin_L_G0133_v10.md#companion-siso-shear-transfer); no new proof or route is claimed.
Choosing L(-q) as a new candidate removes g, so (15) is not a divergence assertion about that different candidate.
Transporting an old Schur pair through (19) would instead have to pay for the polynomial shears, which are not free Euclidean contractions.
The stripped candidate retains the established positive boundary floor on a hypothetical sequence with m(P) tending to zero; (20) supplies no upper comparison by C m(P).
