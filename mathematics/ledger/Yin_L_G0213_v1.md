### 1. Capture all depths before the exact linear tail

Let P be the monic irreducible polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit alpha, with actual degree d and total logarithmic measure m>0.
Put C_n=|Norm_(Q(alpha)/Q)(alpha^n-1)|.
Every C_n is a nonzero positive integer.
Reciprocity, good reduction, local maximality and minimal Frobenius valuation are not assumed.

For a rational prime p, label all original roots alpha_i in a p-adic splitting field, and normalize v(p)=1.
Each is a unit.
Let f_i be the degree over F_p of its reduction, and o_i its multiplicative order.
Then p does not divide o_i, o_i divides p^(f_i)-1, and f_i<=o_i.
For the last inequality, the f_i distinct Frobenius conjugates of that residue are among the o_i roots of t^(o_i)-1; it also covers o_i=f_i=1.

Define complete masses and full selected valuation income by

    w_(p,f)=#{i:f_i=f},       W_(p,F)=sum_(f<=F) w_(p,f),
    A_(p,F)(n)=(log p) sum_(i:f_i<=F) v(alpha_i^n-1).       (1)

Thus w_(p,f)=sum_(deg phi=f)e_phi f for Pbar=product phi^(e_phi), with all repeated copies retained.
The selected contribution is nonnegative and at most v_p(C_n)log p.
No integrality of the selected contribution is needed below.
For different primes their selected contributions can be added at the same n and the sum is at most log C_n.

For each 1<=f<=d put

    k_f=min{k>=0:p^k>=d/f},
    r_f=k_f+1,          N_f=(p^f-1)p^(r_f),
    B_(p,f)=sum_(i:f_i=f) v(alpha_i^(N_f)-1).               (2)

Empty degree classes have B_(p,f)=0.
The following all-time inequality holds without a small-measure hypothesis:

    A_(p,F)(n)
       <=(log p) sum_(f<=min(F,d)) B_(p,f)
                     +W_(p,F) log^+(n/d),    n>=1.         (3)

Here log^+(x)=max(0,log x).
The constants B_(p,f) retain every possible initial or exceptional cancellation depth; no upper depth is assumed.

To prove (3), fix a selected original root with degree f and order o.
Its own field Q_p(alpha_i) has residue degree at least f and total degree at most d, so its ramification index e_i is at most d/f.
This statement concerns the individual root field, not the common splitting field.
Therefore

    v(alpha_i^o-1)>=1/e_i>=f/d.

L72's binomial inequality v(x^p-1)>=min(p v(x-1),1+v(x-1)), capped at one, raises this valuation to at least one after k_f powers.
One more power raises it to at least two.
For any t=v(x-1)>=2, the term p(x-1) is the unique lowest-valuation term in x^p-1: its valuation is 1+t, the middle terms have at least 1+2t, and pt>1+t.
Hence all later p-powers have exact valuation increments one, including p=2 and all ramified cases.
The chosen r_f is past every exceptional tie.

Raising an element congruent to one to a power u prime to p preserves its valuation, since the linear binomial term u(x-1) is uniquely lowest.
Since (p^f-1)/o is prime to p, the captured valuation in (2) is exactly v(alpha_i^(o p^(r_f))-1).
If o does not divide n, the target valuation is zero.
Otherwise write n=o p^r u with p not dividing u.
Monotonicity before r_f and exact slope after it give

    v(alpha_i^n-1)
       <=v(alpha_i^(N_f)-1)+(r-r_f)_+.                    (4)

When r>r_f, n>=o p^r and p^(r_f)>=p d/f, so o p^(r_f)>=d because o>=f.
Consequently

    (r-r_f)log p <=log(n/(o p^(r_f)))<=log(n/d).

This proves (3) after summing the original indices.
In particular the later expense is log^+(n/d), not log n; dropping that centering would lose the useful growing-input conclusion.

### 2. An input-uniform bound including exceptional depths

Reuse L209's explicit increasing expense modulus

    c=log2/4,           h_0=c exp(-20),
    b(h)=max(h,sqrt(h/c) log sqrt(c/h)),
    Omega(h)=19 b(h)^(1/6),              0<h<=h_0.          (5)

Its complete actual-powered-degree theorem is

    log C_n/d <=Omega(nm/d) whenever nm/d<=h_0,
    Omega(h)=O(h^(1/12)(1+log(1/h))^(1/6)).                (6)

The norm tower and any powered-degree drop are already included in this accepted local interface.
They are not replaced by an assumption that alpha^n keeps degree d.

Suppose p and F>=1 satisfy H_2=m p^(F+2)<=h_0, and put H_1=m p^(F+1).
Minimality of k_f in (2), including the case k_f=0, gives

    p^(r_f)<=p^2 d/f,
    N_f m/d <=m p^2(p^f-1)/f<=H_2.                       (7)

Since the omitted roots have nonnegative p-adic valuations, (6) and monotonicity give, separately for each nonempty f<=F,

    (log p) B_(p,f)<=log C_(N_f)<=d Omega(H_2).

Combining these bounds with (3) proves, for every integer n>=1,

    A_(p,F)(n)/d
       <= F Omega(H_2)+(W_(p,F)/d) log^+(n/d).            (8)

L209 also gives W_(p,F)/d<=F Omega(H_1)/log p.
Thus an entirely input-uniform form is

    A_(p,F)(n)/d
       <=F Omega(H_2)+[F Omega(H_1)/log p]log^+(n/d)
       <=F Omega(H_2)[1+log^+(n/d)/log p].                (9)

These inequalities price the full initial capture, including any arbitrarily deep exceptional cancellation, before following the exact subsequent slope.
They are not obtained by multiplying a mass bound by an unproved uniform depth cap.
They neither identify the different capture exponents N_f nor add their norm incomes into one cyclic norm; those norms supply upper bounds on the separate pieces of the actual selected income at the one target n.

A useful inverse formulation follows directly from (8).
Set gamma=W_(p,F)/d and b_*=F Omega(H_2).
If gamma=0 the selected income is zero at every n.
If gamma>0 and A_(p,F)(n)>=eta d for some eta>b_*, then

    n >= d exp((eta-b_*)/gamma).                          (10)

Thus deep income on a small selected mass cannot appear at an arbitrary early time once its entire transient depth has been captured.
This is a necessary time bound, not a theorem that the threshold is eventually attained.

### 3. Full selected income vanishes on an exponential time window

Fix 0<epsilon<1.
As m tends to zero through actual nontorsion algebraic units, uniformly over all p,F with

    p^(F+2)<=m^(-1+epsilon),

and all integers

    1<=n<=d exp(m^(-epsilon/24)),                          (11)

one has

    A_(p,F)(n)/d ->0.                                     (12)

For sufficiently small m, H_2<=m^epsilon<=h_0 and F<=log(1/m)/log2.
The right side of (9) is therefore at most

    [log(1/m)/log2] Omega(m^epsilon)
                      [1+m^(-epsilon/24)/log2]
      =O_epsilon(m^(epsilon/24)(1+log(1/m))^(7/6)),        (13)

which tends to zero.
Empty parameter ranges are vacuous.
Every local valuation depth is included at the target n, even at bad or index primes.
Here f_i is the degree of the reduced root itself; the residue field of its individual local field can be larger.
No equality with a maximal-order residue degree is inferred from a potentially nonmaximal power basis.

This time window is much larger than a fixed linear window or d/m.
At its upper end nm/d=m exp(m^(-epsilon/24)) diverges, so L168/L209's small-powered-height estimate does not directly control the complete C_n there.
The new conclusion concerns its selected low-residue-degree prime contribution.
Higher residue degrees and other primes may carry the rest of the full norm, and no bound on that rest is claimed.

The exponents 1/24 and the sufficient prime-degree range are explicit convenient choices, not optimality claims.
Improving these constants alone is not proposed as a new gap mechanism.

### 4. A growing collection of primes at the same target time

Fix an integer F>=1 and a real 0<kappa<1/(F+14).
Put

    Y=m^(-kappa),
    delta=[1-kappa(F+14)]/24>0,
    A_(Y,F)(n)=sum_(p<=Y) A_(p,F)(n).                     (14)

This is actual selected arithmetic income at the same n.
Its prime parts do not overlap; the degree classes at each prime retain all their own multiplicities.
No independence or disjointness of the root index sets at different primes is assumed.

For all integers 1<=n<=d exp(m^(-delta)), one has uniformly

    A_(Y,F)(n)/d ->0 as m->0.                             (15)

Indeed each H_2<=m^(1-kappa(F+2)), with positive exponent.
For small m this is within h_0.
There are at most Y primes, so (9) gives the explicit bound

    A_(Y,F)(n)/d
      <= F Y Omega(m^(1-kappa(F+2)))
                             [1+log^+(n/d)/log2].          (16)

The asymptotic in (6) makes this, on the stated window,

    O_(F,kappa)(m^delta (1+log(1/m))^(1/6)) ->0.

The exponent calculation uses

    [1-kappa(F+2)]/12-kappa=2delta.

This differs from L209's growing-prime mass total: it controls the full valuation income, including all initial and later depths, at every one of these very large target times.
It uses separate capture norms only as upper bounds on actual pieces, not as combined income.
For the inverse-time interpretation one may instead sum (8), obtaining the same-time bound A_(Y,F)(n)/d<=b_S+gamma_S log^+(n/d), where b_S=F sum_(p<=Y)Omega(m p^(F+2)) and gamma_S=sum_(p<=Y)W_(p,F)/d.
If eta>b_S and gamma_S>0, income eta d consequently requires n>=d exp((eta-b_S)/gamma_S).
The prime sum may count a root in several different local fields; that is precisely its intended definition and does not invalidate this upper bound.

The jump from one to three is an actual exceptional cancellation, and later squarings have exact slope one.
