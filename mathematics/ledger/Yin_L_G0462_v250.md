# An exterior-root count for six-term sections with varying seed degree

<a id="count"></a>
## An exclusion uniform in both exponents and the seed

Let \(s\ge3\) be an integer, let \(a,b\) be positive coprime integers with \(a\ge2\), and put \(r=b/a\ge2\), \(u=\min(s,r)\), \(v=\max(s,r)\), \(N=sa+b\).
For \(g_s(T)=T^s-T-1,\ B_s(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), take any monic nontorsion rational irreducible factor \(P\) of \(g_s(X^a)+X^bB_s(X^a)\).
Write \(k\) for the number of its exterior roots, counting both members of a nonreal conjugate pair, \(d=\deg P\), and \(m=\log M(P)\).
There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
       \frac{k}{a}\ge c\frac{(u-1)^{3/2}}
                       {\sqrt{s+r}\,[\log(1+4(u-1))]^{3/2}}.             \tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(u\to\infty,\ k=o(a)\) requires \(v/(u/\log u)^3\to\infty\), regardless of the distribution of these roots.
For comparable \(s,r\to\infty\) one even has \(k/a\gg u/\log(2u)\).

The monic associate of the parent is \(X^N+X^{N-a}-X^{sa}-X^b+X^a+1\).
At an exterior root of log modulus \(t>0\), its two leading terms have absolute value at least \(e^{(N-a)t}(e^{at}-1)\), and the remaining four terms have absolute sum at most \(4e^{(N-au)t}\).
Hence \(at\,e^{a(u-1)t}\le4\).
Using \(e^z\le1+ze^z\) for \(z\ge0\) gives
\[
             t\le T=\frac{\log(1+4(u-1))}{a(u-1)}.                     \tag{2}
\]
The parent is reciprocal, so the same bound holds for the absolute log modulus of each interior root, including those of a nonreciprocal factor.
Every factor here is a monic unit polynomial, whence \(\sum_{P(x)=0}|\log|x||=2m\) and \(m\le kT\).
By the [all-root square inequality, part A](../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) and [Dobrowolski's sparse divisor lemma](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#irreducible),
\[
       1/10\le d\sum_{P(x)=0}\log^2|x|\le2dTm,\qquad
       m\ge c_0 d/N,\quad c_0=\log(1+0.31/6!).
\]
The latter uses concavity of the logarithm on \(0\le d/N\le1\).
Thus \(k^2\ge c_0/(20NT^3)\), proving (1).
For the comparable case the [two-cluster inequality](Yin_L_G0462_v202.md#clustering) prevents \(m\to0\), since the third exponent from the constant term is \(au\) and \(au/N\) is bounded away from zero.
The six exponents are distinct here by \(a\ge2,\ (a,b)=1,\ r\ge2\).
Equation (2) now gives the stronger count in that case.

There is also a degree-sensitive restriction in very skew directions.
For \(N\ge16\), substituting the [degree lower bound for log measure](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height) into \(k\ge m/T\) gives
\[
       \frac{k}{a}\gg\frac{u}{\log(2u)}
                 \left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)^3.             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(d\le N\), the ratio is decreasing beyond \(e^e\), and the finitely many smaller degrees have an absolute positive lower bound by the cited corollary.

<a id="poles"></a>
## Uniform localization and second moments of the pole radii

Let \(\zeta_j\) run over the roots of \(g_s\) inside the open unit disk.
The exterior poles of the section are \(\tau_j=1/\zeta_j\), with \(L_j=\log|\tau_j|>0\).
Their number \(t_s\asymp s\) is given by the [exact pole count](Yin_L_G0462_v230.md#poles).
With absolute implied constants one has
\[
              \min_j L_j\gg s^{-2},\qquad
              \sum_j L_j\ll1,\qquad \sum_j L_j^2\ll s^{-1}.             \tag{4}
\]
For \(r\ge C s^2\log(2s)\) with an absolute \(C\), each pole receives exactly \(a\) exterior roots of the whole parent.
Each such root \(x\) satisfies \(|x^{-a}-\zeta_j|\ll s^{-4}\) and \(0<a\log|x|\le2 L_j\).

To prove the radial separation, write \(\zeta=e^{-h+i\theta}\).
The identity \(|1+\zeta|=e^{-sh}\) gives \(\big||1+e^{i\theta}|-1\big|\le(s+1)h\).
If \(sh\) is small, the unit point \(e^{i\theta}\) is within \(O(sh)\) of one of the two solutions of \(|1+e^{i\theta}|=1\), the primitive cube roots \(\omega\), since the angular derivative of this modulus is nonzero there.
But \(|g_s(\omega)|=|\omega^s+\omega^2|\ge1\), and \(|g_s'|\le s+1\) on the closed unit disk.
The segment from \(\omega\) to \(\zeta\) thus forces \(1/(s+1)\le|\zeta-\omega|\ll sh\).
The case of \(sh\) bounded below also gives \(h\gg s^{-2}\).

For \(0<\delta\le1\), Jensen's formula at \(-1\), where \(|g_s(-1)|=1\), gives
\[
 \#\{g_s(z)=0:\ |1+z|\le\delta\}\log2
       \le\log\!\big((1+2\delta)^s+2\delta\big)\le2(s+1)\delta .
\]
The count includes multiplicities, using the maximum on the circle of radius \(2\delta\).
Since \(sL_j=-\log|1+\zeta_j|\), integrating this tail bound with \(\delta=e^{-w}\) against \(1\) and \(2w\) on \(w\ge0\) proves both moment estimates in (4).

For the localization, every inner seed root satisfies \(|\zeta|>1/2\) since \(|\zeta|^s=|1+\zeta|\ge1-|\zeta|\).
For large \(s\) one has \(|g_s'(\zeta)|\ge1/2\).
Otherwise \(|\zeta|^{s-1}\le3/(2s)\), which implies \(1-|\zeta|\gg\log s/s\), whereas \(1-|\zeta|\le|1+\zeta|=|\zeta|^s\le3/(2s)\).
For the finitely many smaller \(s\) the roots are simple, since a multiple root would satisfy \(\zeta=-s/(s-1)\) and \(s\zeta^{s-1}=1\).
Thus \(|g_s'|\) at the inner roots has an absolute positive lower bound.
Taylor's formula with \(|g_s''|\le s^2\) on the closed unit disk then separates distinct inner roots by \(\gg s^{-2}\).
Take disjoint disks \(|z-\zeta_j|\le c_1s^{-4}\) with sufficiently small absolute \(c_1>0\).
The radial separation in (4) keeps them in \(|z|\le e^{-c_2/s^2}\), Taylor's estimate gives \(|g_s(z)|\gg s^{-4}\) on their boundaries, and \(|B_s(z)|\le3\) there.
For each holomorphic branch \(w=z^{1/a}\) on a disk, \(|w^b|\le e^{-c_2r/s^2}\).
Rouché's theorem gives one simple zero of \(g_s(z)+w^bB_s(z)\) per branch when \(r\ge C s^2\log(2s)\).
Branches and disks give distinct roots \(w\) of the parent.
Their reciprocals are exterior roots, and the [upper bound of \(at_s\) at every slope](Yin_L_G0462_v230.md#poles) exhausts the exterior set.
Finally \(-\log|z|=L_j+O(c_1s^{-4})\) because \(|\zeta_j|>1/2\); (4) and the choice of \(c_1\) give the stated relative bound.

<a id="balanced"></a>
## Factors with bounded occupancy at every pole

In this localization range let \(k_j\) count the exterior roots of \(P\) at \(\tau_j\), and suppose that for some \(K\ge1\),
\[
                      k_j\le K k/t_s\qquad\text{for every }j .
\]
Then, with an absolute implied constant,
\[
                  \frac{k}{a}\gg\frac{s^{3/2}}{K\sqrt{s+r}}.            \tag{5}
\]
Indeed (4) and the bound \(a\log|x|\le2L_j\) give
\[
       m\ll\frac{Kk}{as},\qquad
       \sum_{|x|>1,\ P(x)=0}\log^2|x|\ll\frac{Kk}{a^2s^2}.
\]
For a reciprocal factor its total square sum is twice the exterior sum.
Substituting these inequalities and \(d\le Nm/c_0\) into the all-root square inequality proves (5).
For a nonreciprocal factor [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy71/Yin_U001.md#smy71-statement-and-conventions) gives an absolute positive lower bound for \(m\), so \(k/a\gg s/K\), which implies (5) as well.
Using the degree lower bound for \(m\) instead also gives \(k/a\gg (s/K)(\log\log N/\log N)^3\) in the localization range.

In particular, take \(s\to\infty,\ s\le r=O(s^3)\).
Where \(r<C s^2\log(2s)\), inequality (1) alone bounds \(k/a\) away from zero.
At the remaining indices (5) does so if \(K\) stays bounded.
Thus these six-term sections cannot supply \(k=o(a)\) with uniformly bounded relative occupancy at the poles in this region.
For example, \(a=2^{s^2},\ b=as^3+1\) are primitive and meet the localization condition for every sufficiently large \(s\).
At those directions (5) gives \(k\gg a/K\), even though the right sides of (1) and (3) tend to zero.
The fixed-seed distribution result [L238](Yin_L_G0462_v238.md#pointwise) concerns actual equidistribution at suitable divisors of \(a\).
Here bounded occupancy is an explicit hypothesis for (5), and the constants allow \(s\) to vary.
For bounded \(s\) with \(r\to\infty\), or much more unequal growing \(s,r\) outside the displayed exclusions, existence of a balanced rational factor with \(k=o(a)\) remains open.
