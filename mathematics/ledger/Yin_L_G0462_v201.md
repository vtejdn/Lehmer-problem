# The location of the correcting mass for a fixed Salem return

<a id="atoms"></a>
## A test on the atoms of an individual weak limit

Use the [actual square-constant returns and branch normalization](Yin_L_G0462_v200.md#gap) at any fixed Salem input.
Let \(P_0(z)=z^s Q_0(z+z^{-1})\) be its monic irreducible polynomial.
Write \(h\) for Haar probability on the circle.
Take any subsequence of increasing degrees whose full neutral circle measures converge to \(h/2+\eta\) with mass below \(3/4\).
The [fixed-input limit structure](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#structure) gives \(\eta\ge0\) atomic and \(\delta=\eta(\mathbb T)<1/4\).
Its support consists of torsion points and neutral Salem conjugates.

Let \(S_1,\ldots,S_l\) be nonzero primitive integral Laurent multiples of \(P_0\) whose gcd over the rational Laurent ring is \(P_0\) up to a unit.
If
\[
                 \int_{\mathbb T}|S_i|\,d\eta<\tfrac12
                         \qquad(1\le i\le l),                            \tag{1}
\]
then every sufficiently late return on this subsequence has precisely a complete midpoint grid on the new nodes, with mass \(1/(2k)\) at each of its \(k\) new traces.
In particular \(\eta\) can then be supported only at old conjugates, since the uniform grids tend to Haar measure after doubling and lifting.
In (1) every old conjugate already contributes zero.
For the fixed parity \(Q_0(0)\equiv2\bmod4,\ Q'_0(0)\) odd, the [endpoint congruence](Yin_L_G0462_v194.md#endpoint) consequently requires at least one of these integrals to be at least \(1/2\) in every such limit.

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Rounding the convolution at a moving frequency

Lift the positive weights on the new traces equally to inverse points and double them, obtaining \(\lambda_d\) with real Fourier values \(m_{d,r}=\int z^r\,d\lambda_d\).
For every integral Laurent multiple \(S\) of \(P_0\), all \(\int z^r S\,d\lambda_d\) are integers by linear combination and shifts of the [exact trace moments](Yin_L_G0462_v200.md#twists).
For an arbitrary sequence of frequencies \(r_d\), pass further so \(z^{r_d}\lambda_d\) converges to a complex measure \(\theta\).
The positive full lifts doubled converge to \(h+2\eta\), and dominate the variations of these twists.
Thus \(\theta=Hh+\theta_{\rm sing}\) with \(|H|\le1\) almost everywhere and \(|\theta_{\rm sing}|\le2\eta\).

For each \(i\), \(S_i\theta\) has integral Fourier coefficients, and the norm of its singular part is strictly smaller than one by (1).
Its absolutely continuous part has Fourier coefficients tending to zero at both ends.
The integral coefficients therefore vanish eventually in both directions, so \(S_i\theta=D_i h\) for an integral Laurent polynomial \(D_i\).
In particular \(S_i\theta_{\rm sing}=0\) for all \(i\), giving \(P_0\theta_{\rm sing}=0\) by the gcd.
The same rational Bézout combination of \(D_i=S_iH\) shows \(P_0H\) equals a rational Laurent polynomial \(D\) almost everywhere.
On the circle \(|D|\le |P_0|\) by continuity.
At any unit-circle conjugate of the Salem number this forces \(D=0\) at that point.
Irreducibility of \(P_0\) implies \(H=D/P_0\) is rational Laurent polynomial, including the case \(D=0\).
The identity \(S_1H=D_1\) and primitivity of \(S_1\) give integral coefficients for \(H\) by Gauss's lemma.
Parseval then gives \(H=0\) or \(H=\pm z^j\) for some integer \(j\).

For every fixed shift \(u\), the limiting value of \(m_{d,r_d+u}\) is within \(2\delta<1/2\) of the integer \(\int z^u H\,dh\).
Rounding to the nearest integer therefore recovers that exact integer eventually along the extracted subsequence.
The argument with arbitrary moving frequencies gives unique nearest values \(h_{d,r}\in\{-1,0,1\}\) simultaneously for all \(r\) at all sufficiently large degrees.
Write \(P_0=\sum_u c_u z^u\).
Then for all sufficiently large \(d\) and all \(r\),
\[
                   \sum_u c_u(m_{d,r+u}-h_{d,r+u})=0.                   \tag{2}
\]
Otherwise choose violating frequencies and make the preceding extraction.
Along it the left side converges to \(\int P_0\,d\theta_{\rm sing}=0\).
It is an integer for every \(d\), so it must eventually vanish, a contradiction.
This argument uses the joint limit of the finitely many shifted errors and retains their signs.

The new roots at each degree lie in a finite cyclic group.
Convolution by \(P_0\) is invertible there since it has no torsion zero.
Equation (2) gives \(m_{d,r}=h_{d,r}\) for all \(r\).
The [finite-group argument](Yin_L_G0462_v200.md#coset) identifies the nonempty positive measure with uniform probability on \(z^{2k}=-1\), using inversion symmetry and the absence of \(1,-1\) from the roots.
Restoring the original weights proves the assertion.

## A circle-norm lower bound and a numerical example

Put \(B=\max(2,\max_i\max_{|z|=1}|S_i(z)|)\).
For any fixed input in the remaining parity, every sequence of its full returns satisfies
\[
                  \liminf_d \nu_d([-2,2])\ge \tfrac12+\tfrac1{2B}.       \tag{3}
\]
A smaller liminf admits a subsequence with \(\delta<1/(2B)\) and a convergent full lift, satisfying (1).
The excluded midpoint congruence gives the contradiction.
In particular take the single multiplier \(S_1=P_0\).
Here \(\max_{|z|=1}|P_0(z)|=\max_{[-2,2]}|Q_0|\ge2\), since the monic Chebyshev polynomial \(C_s\) has alternating extrema of absolute value two at \(s+1\) points, and a strictly smaller norm for a monic \(Q_0\) would force \(s\) zeros of \(Q_0-C_s\) of degree less than \(s\).
Thus (3) has the same circle-norm denominator as the [odd-constant gap](Yin_L_G0462_v184.md#fixed).
A family of such finite sets of test polynomials with uniformly bounded circle norms gives the same lower bound for the infimum of the fixed-input liminfs.
The onset of each bound still depends on its fixed Salem input.

For \(Q_0(x)=x^2-3x-6\), the roots \((3\pm\sqrt{33})/2\) consist of one trace greater than two and one in \((-2,2)\).
The polynomial is irreducible, and \(z^2-Tz+1\) has negative discriminant at the smaller real trace embedding, giving a Salem number of degree four at the larger.
The constant is two modulo four and the derivative at zero is odd.
Here the coefficient length of \(z^2Q_0(z+z^{-1})\) is twelve, while \(\max_{[-2,2]}|Q_0|=33/4\), attained at \(x=3/2\).
The lower bound consequently improves from \(13/24\) to \(1/2+2/33=37/66\).
