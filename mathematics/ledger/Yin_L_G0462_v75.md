# Smooth composite orders and pairs of nearby orders in the linear resultant window

<a id="criteria"></a>
## Two degree-independent tests on the orders

Let \(P\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a nontorsion algebraic unit, let \(d=\deg P\) and let \(m(P)=\log M(P)\).
For integer \(j\ge2\) put
\[
 X_j(P)=\frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,\Phi_j)|}{\varphi(j)},\qquad
 Z_j(P)=\frac{\log|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^j-1)|}{j}.
\]
Write \(P^+(j)\) for the largest prime divisor of \(j\).
Fix \(e^{-1/2}<\alpha<1\), for example \(\alpha=2/3\).
For either \(W=X\) or \(W=Z\), each of the following conditions is equivalent to an absolute positive lower bound for \(m(P)\) over all nontorsion algebraic units.

1. For some \(c>0\) and every such \(P\) of sufficiently large degree, there exists \(j\in[d,2d]\) with \(P^+(j)\le j^\alpha\) and \(W_j(P)\ge c\).
2. For some \(c>0\) and every such \(P\) of sufficiently large degree, there exist two distinct composite orders \(j,k\in[d,2d]\) with \(|j-k|\le2\) and \(\min(W_j(P),W_k(P))\ge c\).

Both equivalences also hold with \(\min(X,Z)\) as the statistic.
In the first test the eligible orders are necessarily composite, and membership is determined by the order alone.
In the second, both orders may be chosen after \(P\) is given.
These replace the [sparse degree-dependent deletions](Yin_L_G0462_v69.md#single) with restrictions that never use the prime divisors of \(d\).

<a id="abundance"></a>
## A simultaneous lower bound on nearly half the orders

For each fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) there is \(\kappa_\epsilon>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(d\), at least \((1/2-\epsilon)d\) composite orders \(j\in[d,2d]\) satisfy
\[
                \min(X_j(P),Z_j(P))\ge \kappa_\epsilon\,m(P).
\]
Indeed the [two-sided primitive estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v70.md#linear) gives \(X_j(P)\ge m(P)/2\) outside at most \(d/2+o(d)\) orders, uniformly in \(P\).
The [bounded mean of \(j/\varphi(j)\)](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#exceptional-orders) allows a fixed threshold \(\delta_\epsilon>0\) such that at most \(\epsilon d/2\) indices have \(\varphi(j)/j<\delta_\epsilon\).
The primes cost \(o(d)\) further indices by the [Chebyshev estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v54.md#all-units).
Since every cyclotomic resultant is a nonzero integer, \(Z_j\ge(\varphi(j)/j)X_j\).
These estimates give the assertion after taking \(\kappa_\epsilon=\min(1,\delta_\epsilon)/2\).

<a id="smooth"></a>
## A positive proportion with largest prime factor at most \(j^\alpha\)

The number of \(j\in[d,2d]\) with a prime divisor exceeding \(j^\alpha\) is at most
\[
          \sum_{d^\alpha<p\le2d}\left(\frac d p+1\right)
               \le \bigl(\log(1/\alpha)+o(1)\bigr)d.
\]
For the required reciprocal-prime tail put \(S(x)=\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)/p\).
In the prime-power expansion of \(\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)\), replacing the first-power floor counts by \(x/p\) costs at most \(\sum_{p\le x}\log p=O(x)\) by Chebyshev.
All higher-power terms together cost at most \(x\sum_p(\log p)/(p(p-1))=O(x)\).
The integral estimate for the logarithm of the factorial therefore gives \(S(x)=\log x+O(1)\).
Partial summation on \((d^\alpha,2d]\) now gives
\[
    \sum_{d^\alpha<p\le2d}\frac1p
       =\log\!\left(\frac{\log(2d)}{\log(d^\alpha)}\right)
          +O_\alpha(1/\log d)
       =\log(1/\alpha)+o(1).
\]
The extra count of primes is \(O(d/\log d)\).
Since \(\log(1/\alpha)<1/2\), one can choose \(0<\epsilon<1/2-\log(1/\alpha)\).
After removing every order failing the smoothness condition, the preceding lower estimate still gives a positive proportion of the whole interval with both returns at least \(\kappa_\epsilon m(P)\).
For \(\alpha=2/3\) the available margin before choosing \(\epsilon\) is \(1/2-\log(3/2)\).

<a id="pairs"></a>
## Pairs at distance one or two

Use the simultaneous estimate with \(\epsilon=1/12\), giving at least \(5d/12\) good composite orders.
Partition the interval into consecutive disjoint triples and a possible terminal block of size at most two.
If \(B\) of the triples each contain at least two good orders, the total count is at most \(\lfloor(d+1)/3\rfloor+2B+2\).
Consequently \(B\ge d/24-O(1)\).
There are therefore linearly many disjoint pairs of good composite orders of distance one or two, with an absolute lower bound relative to \(m(P)\) at both orders of each pair.
In particular the positive Mahler gap implies both stated test conditions.

<a id="converse"></a>
## Coprimality on substitutions of prime degree

Fix any nontorsion algebraic unit with minimal polynomial \(Q\) and degree \(D\).
For sufficiently large primes \(p\), the [substitution and sampling theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v60.md#lifts) supplies the minimal polynomial \(Q(z^p)\) of degree \(d=Dp\) and measure \(m(Q)\).
For \(j\in[Dp,2Dp]\) coprime to \(p\) the two normalized resultants equal \(X_j(Q),Z_j(Q)\) exactly.
These tend uniformly to \(m(Q)\) over the coprime orders in the window, by the two ordinary fixed-\(Q\) limits.
When \(P^+(j)\le j^\alpha\), the inequality \(p>(2Dp)^\alpha\) holds eventually for fixed \(D,\alpha<1\), so every eligible order is coprime to \(p\).
For two distinct orders of distance at most two, \(p>2\) makes at least one coprime to \(p\).
Thus either test on these polynomials forces \(m(Q)\ge c\), also for the simultaneous statistic.
An absolute positive lower bound for either test remains open.
