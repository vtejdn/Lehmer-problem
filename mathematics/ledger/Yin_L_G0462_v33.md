# The limiting least Pisot excess and a degree bound for the inverse construction

<a id="least-excess"></a>
## Taking the infimum at each integer constant

For each integer \(q\ge2\) let \(b_q\) be the infimum of \(\theta-q\) over Pisot numbers whose monic minimal polynomial has positive constant \(q\).
The [quadratic examples](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#dominant-coefficient) make these sets nonempty.
Let \(b=\inf_\sigma(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2)\), where \(\sigma\) ranges over Salem numbers.
Then
\[
                      \lim_{q\to\infty} b_q=b.                         \tag{1}
\]
Indeed the [explicit Salem family below the golden ratio](Yin_L_G0462_v28.md#degree-scale) gives \(b<1\).
The [positive delay-one construction](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return) gives an exterior unit \(\sigma>1\) whose irreducible reciprocal polynomial has only one root strictly inside and one strictly outside the unit circle.
If this factor is quadratic, its integral trace is at least three, so \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\ge1>b\).
Otherwise it gives a Salem number, so the same trace excess is at least \(b\) by definition.
In both cases [Boyd's inequality](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#near-integer-lemma) implies \(b_q\ge bq(q-1)/(q+1)^2\).
Conversely, for every fixed Salem number the [prescribed-constant inverse](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#statement) gives \(\limsup_{q\to\infty} b_q\le\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\), proving (1).
In particular the same limit holds along powers of any one fixed prime.

<a id="degree-uniform"></a>
## An explicit constant coefficient chosen from the Salem degree alone

Suppose \(P\) is the minimal polynomial of a Salem number \(1<\sigma\le2\), with \(d=2m=\deg P\ge4\) and \(T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}\).
For every integer
\[
                    q\ \ge\ Q_d=(2d)^4(32\sqrt d)^d                    \tag{2}
\]
there is a Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) of degree \(d\), constant \(q\) and exterior root \(\theta\), such that \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\) and
\[
    \left|\frac{\theta-q}{T-2}-1\right|\le \frac1d .                  \tag{3}
\]
Consequently the Salem near-one question can be tested at the single constant \(2^{\lceil\log_2 Q_d\rceil}\) prescribed at each even degree in the inverse direction.
The existence of positive-constant Pisot roots with excess tending to zero along those prescribed pairs would conversely produce Salem numbers tending to one by the positive delay-one construction.
This condition does not presume that either sequence exists.

Use the notation \(H,V,E,p_q,C\) and the paired coefficient rounding of the [inverse proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#rounding).
At a neutral root \(\zeta=e^{it}\) of \(P\), \(0<t<\pi\), delete the row of \(\zeta\) from the list of roots and take the square Vandermonde matrix of the other \(d-1\) roots with powers from \(0\) to \(d-2\).
Hadamard's inequality bounds the absolute determinant by \(\Lambda=(d-1)^{(d-1)/2}2^{d-2}\), since the product of the exterior moduli of the remaining roots is at most two.
Multiplying this determinant in absolute value by \(|P'(\zeta)|\) gives \(\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(P)|}\ge1\), hence \(|P'(\zeta)|\ge1/\Lambda\).
With \(x=2\cos t\), differentiation of \(P=(z^2-Tz+1)V\) gives
\[
  2\sin(t/2)|H'(x)|=\frac{|P'(\zeta)|}{2(T-x)\cos(t/2)}
                   \ge \frac{1}{9\Lambda},
\]
using \(T\le5/2\).
Thus \(L=10d\Lambda\) works simultaneously at all the roots in the [strict inward perturbation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v2.md#inward).
The Laurent coefficient sum of \(H'(z+z^{-1})\) is at most \((m-1)4^{m-2}\), by differentiating the product of factors with real roots in \((-2,2)\).
It follows from \(C=LE+U\), \(\|U\|_1\le d\), that
\[
              \|C\|_1\le A_d=11d^2 4^d d^{d/2} .                       \tag{4}
\]
Also \(|V(-1)|=|P(-1)|/(2+T)\ge2/9\) by integrality.
The lower bound in (2) exceeds \(10A_d\) and \(2d\), so \(|(2q+T)V(-1)|>\|C\|_1\).
All circle-crossing checks in the inverse proof now hold, and the constructed polynomial has its unique exterior root \(\theta>q\).

Since \(\deg C\le d-2\) and every root of the monic \(V\) has modulus one, its equation \(p_q(\theta)V(\theta)=-C(\theta)\) gives
\[
                |p_q(\theta)|\le A_d(1-1/\theta)^{-(d-2)}\le3A_d .
\]
For the last inequality use \(-\log(1-1/\theta)\le1/(\theta-1)\) and \(\theta>2d\).
The identity \(p_q(\theta)=(\theta-1)(\theta-q)-\theta(T-2)\) bounds the absolute error in the excess by \((3A_d+1)/(q-1)\).
Finally \(1\le |P(1)|=(T-2)|V(1)|\le (T-2)2^{d-2}\).
Since \(Q_d-1\ge d\,2^{d-2}(3A_d+1)\) for \(d\ge4\), division by \(T-2\) proves (3).
The threshold in (2) is sufficient; this argument does not determine the smallest possible constant coefficient.
