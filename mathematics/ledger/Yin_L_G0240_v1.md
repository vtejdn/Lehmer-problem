### 1. Two genuine dual probability densities

Let alpha be an algebraic unit, O=Z[alpha] with its actual power basis, and X=Hom(O,R/Z) its compact d-dimensional dual torus.
Let m_X be Haar probability and chi_x(theta)=exp(2pi i theta(x)).
The toral automorphism S is defined by

    (S theta)(x)=theta(alpha x).

It preserves m_X because multiplication by alpha is an integer unimodular map.
Throughout, the digit probabilities are the ORIGINAL difference law1/2,1/4,1/4.
Write p_n for the full zero-return probability, h=h_2, R=exp h in[1,2], U(z)=sum p_n z^n=1/(1-F(z)) for0<z<R, exactly as in L236.

Put

    w(theta)=(2+chi_1(theta)+chi_(-1)(theta))/4
            =cos^2(pi theta(1)),
    W_n(theta)=product_(j=0)^(n-1) w(S^j theta),
    V_n(theta)=product_(j=1)^n w(S^(-j) theta),         (1)

with both empty products one.
W_n is the squared modulus of the fair-binary endpoint characteristic function.
Thus its Fourier coefficients are the actual displacement probabilities mu_n(x), and its integral is p_n.
Since V_n=W_n composed with S^(-n), it too has integral p_n and coefficient mu_n(alpha^n x).
Signs cause no ambiguity because every function and coefficient here is real and even.

For0<z<R define

    f_z=U(z)^(-1) sum_(n>=0) z^n W_n,
    g_z=U(z)^(-1) sum_(n>=0) z^n V_n,
    a_z=f_z m_X,       b_z=g_z m_X.                    (2)

Both sums converge in L1 and almost everywhere to finite nonnegative densities: the sum of their integrals is U(z)<infinity.
They have integral one.
The point theta=0 may have an infinite displayed series when z>=1; this Haar-null point does not invalidate the L1 assertion.
No L2 or boundedness premise is inserted.

Fourier integration of the nonnegative L1 sums, or absolute convergence of the integrals, gives exactly

    a_z_hat(x)=A_z(x),    b_z_hat(x)=B_z(x),            (3)

in L236's whole-order notation.
These are measures on the compact dual of O, not the stationary probabilities on the countable actual core and not the expanding Bernoulli distribution of L224.

Splitting off the first factor of the products in(1) yields the precise L1 equations

    f_z=U(z)^(-1)+z w (f_z composed with S),
    g_z=U(z)^(-1)+z (w g_z) composed with S^(-1).       (4)

Equivalently, with pushforwards of measures and pointwise weighting,

    a_z=U(z)^(-1)m_X+z w (S^(-1)_* a_z),
    b_z=U(z)^(-1)m_X+z S_*(w b_z).                     (5)

For example the first weighted term acts on a continuous test phi as integral phi(S^(-1)theta)w(S^(-1)theta) da_z(theta).
This specifies both orientations; the measures are not being asserted S-invariant.

### 2. Critical limits on the entire order

L236's first-hit and last-zero decompositions extend to every x in O, including those outside the core.
Let L_z(x), x!=0, sum paths from0 to x without a further visit to0, and let H_z(x) sum first-hit paths from x to0.
Set both at zero equal to one.
Decomposing any ambient path at its last zero or first zero gives

    A_z(x)=L_z(x),    B_z(x)=H_z(x)                    (6)

on ALL O, with a zero value when the required path does not exist.
The same convolution identities with U remain valid: a path need not return after its endpoint for the last-zero decomposition, and need not be reachable from zero for the first-hit decomposition.
The complete autocorrelation bound from L236 keeps both quantities at most one.
Thus they increase pointwise as z increases to R, with limits A_R,B_R in[0,1] and values one at zero.

Compactness of Borel probabilities on X, using the inspected Wal18 foundation, gives subsequential weak limits of a_z and b_z.
Their Fourier coefficients must be A_R,B_R.
Trigonometric polynomials are uniformly dense among continuous torus functions, so those coefficients determine at most one probability measure.
Consequently the entire families have unique weak limits

    a_z -> a_R,   b_z -> b_R   as z increases to R.    (7)

This does not assert convergence of probabilities of arbitrary Borel sets.
At every x the product coefficient is sigma_R(x)=A_R(x)B_R(x), supported precisely on the actual core: a finite witnessing path retains positive weight at R.
In particular the whole-order statement has not enlarged that support.

If F(R)<1, then U(R)<infinity.
Monotone convergence in(2) and convergence of its normalization give L1 convergence to

    f_R=U(R)^(-1) sum R^n W_n,
    g_R=U(R)^(-1) sum R^n V_n.                         (8)

Hence a_R,b_R are absolutely continuous in the transient case, whether or not the first derivative F'(R) is finite.
At critical recurrence F(R)=1 the source term in(5) instead vanishes, and weak continuity against continuous tests gives

    a_R=R w (S^(-1)_*a_R),    b_R=R S_*(w b_R).        (9)

These are eigenmeasure equations with the exact original digit multiplier and critical radius.

### 3. Nontorsion recurrence forces purely singular factors

Assume now alpha is nontorsion and its polynomial is irreducible, as is automatic for its minimal polynomial.
No conjugate can be a root of unity, since then that irreducible polynomial would be cyclotomic.
Wal18's checked toral criterion makes S ergodic with respect to m_X, including when some conjugates lie on the unit circle.

We need the exact scalar integral

    integral_X log w dm_X=-2log2.                     (10)

The coordinate theta(1) is a standard circle coordinate because1 belongs to the power basis.
Its Haar distribution is uniform.
The logarithm has only integrable logarithmic singularities.
To check its value directly, let I=integral_0^(pi/2) log(sin t)dt, equal by reflection to the cosine integral.
The identity sin(2t)=2sin(t)cos(t), followed by u=2t and symmetry about pi/2, gives I=(pi/2)log2+2I, so I=-(pi/2)log2.
This proves(10), including its factor two.

Suppose F(R)=1 and the absolutely continuous part of a_R is nonzero, with nonnegative L1 density f.
A toral automorphism preserves Haar-null sets, and multiplication by the continuous w preserves singularity.
Uniqueness of the absolutely-continuous/singular decomposition in(9) therefore gives

    f(theta)=R w(theta) f(S theta)   almost everywhere. (11)

The set where f is positive is S-invariant up to null sets because w>0 almost everywhere.
Ergodicity and nonzero mass imply f>0 almost everywhere.
Its finite L1 value implies f<infinity almost everywhere.
Thus Y=log f is a finite real measurable function, without an assumption that Y is integrable, and

    Y-Y composed with S=log R+log w.                  (12)

The right side is integrable.
Define c_M(t)=max(-M,min(t,M)).
Haar invariance gives integral[c_M(Y)-c_M(Y composed with S)]dm_X=0.
The truncation is one-Lipschitz, so the absolute integrand is at most |Y-Y composed with S|, the integrable right side of(12).
Dominated convergence as M tends to infinity gives

    0=log R-2log2,    hence R=4.                      (13)

This contradicts R<=2 from the actual zero digit path.
Therefore a_R has NO absolutely continuous component.

For b_R a putative density g satisfies g(S theta)=R w(theta)g(theta).
The same invariant positivity set and truncated-log argument again force R=4.
Thus both a_R and b_R are purely singular probability measures at every critically recurrent nontorsion unit.
Together with(8),

    F(R)<1  iff both limiting measures have L1 densities;
    F(R)=1  iff both limiting measures are purely singular. (14)

There is no mixed case for these actual limiting measures.
The converse directions use the exhaustive alternative F(R)<=1 and the fact that a probability cannot be both purely singular and absolutely continuous.
Torsion is excluded from the ergodicity proof, rather than assigned a false toral ergodicity hypothesis.

An additional concrete consequence is concentration of the subcritical densities.
For any1<p<=infinity their extended Lp norms obey

    ||f_z||_p -> infinity,    ||g_z||_p -> infinity
    whenever alpha is nontorsion and F(R)=1.           (15)

Indeed choose a compact subset K of a Haar-null set carrying a_R with mass greater than1-epsilon, an open neighborhood V of K with arbitrarily small Haar mass, and a continuous function between0 and1 equal to one on K and supported in V.
Weak convergence in(7) gives integral_V f_z>=1-2epsilon for all sufficiently large z<R.
Holder then gives ||f_z||_p>=(1-2epsilon)m_X(V)^(-(1-1/p)); the same argument works for infinity and for b_R.
The neighborhoods may depend on the actual input and tolerance.
This proves divergence, not a rate uniform in degree or m, and allows a norm already infinite at a subcritical z.

### 4. Finite critical mean is a separate convolution regularity condition

The convolution c_z=a_z*b_z is a genuine probability measure on X, with Fourier coefficients sigma_z=A_z B_z.
By the FULL L236 occupation identity,

    sum_(x in O) sigma_z(x)=T(z)=zF'(z)+1-F(z).        (16)

All coefficients are nonnegative.
They are summable at z<R, so c_z has the continuous density

    C_z(theta)=sum_(x in O) sigma_z(x) chi_(-x)(theta),
    0<=C_z(theta)<=C_z(0)=T(z),
    integral C_z dm_X=1.                              (17)

The series converges absolutely and uniformly.
Its positivity and its identification as the convolution density follow from equality of all Fourier coefficients; it is not an unproved pointwise convergence of f_z or g_z.
As z increases to R, convolution of weakly converging probabilities on the compact group converges weakly to c_R=a_R*b_R.
This also follows directly by character coefficients and uniform trigonometric approximation.

Monotone convergence of sigma_z on the countable order gives the possibly infinite total

    T_R=sum sigma_R=RF'(R)+1-F(R).                    (18)

If T_R is finite, the coefficient vectors converge in l1 by their nonnegative monotonicity.
Hence C_z converges uniformly to a continuous density C_R of c_R, and ||C_R||_infinity=C_R(0)=T_R.

Conversely suppose c_R has an essentially bounded density with norm M.
In power-basis coordinates on X, convolve it with the product Fejer kernel at zero.
This nonnegative integral is at most M because that kernel has integral one.
Its exact finite Fourier expansion is

    sum_(x in Z^d) sigma_R(x)
                  product_(j=1)^d (1-|x_j|/N)_+ <= M.

Each nonnegative summand increases to sigma_R(x).
Monotone convergence yields T_R<=M.
Thus

    c_R has a bounded density iff RF'(R)<infinity,
    and in that case it has a continuous density
    with exact norm RF'(R)+1-F(R).                    (19)

The Fejer kernel identity follows directly by expanding the squared modulus of sum_(j=0)^(N-1) exp(2pi i jt), divided by N; no pointwise Fourier inversion theorem is needed for the bounded density.
If the mean diverges the convolution may be singular or may have an unbounded density; neither alternative is classified.

For nontorsion units, (14), (19) and L232 now give an exact two-part reformulation:

    The original information infimum is attained
    iff a_R and b_R are purely singular
        AND a_R*b_R has a bounded density.            (20)

Both factors singular encodes recurrence; the bounded convolution encodes the finite critical mean.
When this holds, RF'(R) is its exact density norm and pi_*(0)=1/(RF'(R)).
Neither condition alone establishes the other.
In particular boundedness or L2 regularity of either individual Abel density is not an admissible extra premise in a recurrent proof.

For reciprocal alpha the already retained additive involution Jx=alpha^(-1)sigma(x), sigma(alpha)=alpha^(-1), gives mu_n(alpha^n x)=mu_n(Jx) by reversing the n digits.
It applies also to n=0 because J is invertible.
Thus B_z(x)=A_z(Jx), and b_z is the pushforward of a_z by the dual involution J^vee, defined by (J^vee theta)(x)=theta(Jx).
The same relation holds at R.
Consequently (19)--(20) test the convolution of one singular measure with its actual reciprocal image.
It is not ordinary symmetry a_z=b_z, nor an l2 square norm unless an additional symmetry has been proved.

### 5. Actual singular factors can convolve to Haar

Take the actual reciprocal quadratic unit alpha=2+sqrt3, with minimal polynomial t^2-4t+1.
Its actual singleton core and dyadic radical controls are already retained in [L206](Yin_L_G0206_v1.md#radical-critical-fixed-scope).

It cannot vanish.
Therefore every zero-return digit word is the all-zero word, giving

    D={0},    p_n=2^(-n),    F(z)=z/2,    R=2,
    RF'(R)=1,    pi_*=delta_0.                         (21)

The input is irreducible, reciprocal, nontorsion and has positive fixed total measure log(2+sqrt3).
Its critical a_R,b_R are purely singular by Section3.
But sigma_z is delta_0 on the whole order at EVERY0<z<R, because its exact support is D and its value at zero is one.
Thus

    a_z*b_z=m_X,     a_R*b_R=m_X.                     (22)

Both limiting singular factors therefore have exactly Haar convolution, with density one and finite critical attainment.
The reciprocal-image relationship is genuine here.
This actual control excludes an inference from singularity of the individual factors to failure of their bounded convolution.
