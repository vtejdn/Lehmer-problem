# Coefficient obstructions for Pisot roots near the positive constant of their minimal polynomial

<a id="boyd-return"></a>
## The delay-one associate and the number of terms

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta\), with \(r=\deg f\) and \(q=f(0)\ge2\).
Write \(\delta=\theta-q\), \(g=z^r f(1/z)\), \(D=zf+g\), and \(m(P)=\log M(P)\) for logarithmic Mahler measure.
Here \(r\ge2\), and \(q=\theta\prod_{\beta\ne\theta}|\beta|<\theta\), where the other roots of \(f\) have modulus less than one.
Also \(f(1)<0\), since \(1-\theta<0\), each other real factor \(1-\beta\) is positive, and the nonreal factors occur in conjugate pairs.
Thus \(D(1)=2f(1)<0\), and the monic reciprocal \(D\) has a real root \(\sigma>1\).
For \(0<t<1\), Rouché's theorem on the unit circle gives \(zf+tg\) the same interior root count as \(zf\), since \(|g|=|f|>0\) there.
Letting \(t\uparrow1\) shows that \(D\) has at most one exterior root, counting multiplicities.
Consequently \(M(D)=\sigma\), and [Boyd's Lemma 5](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#near-integer-lemma) gives
\[
       0<\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2
            \le\delta\,\frac{(q+1)^2}{q(q-1)}\le\tfrac92\delta .
\]
If \(\delta\to0\), then \(\sigma\to1\).
Reciprocity gives \(\sigma^{-1}\) as the unique interior root, and every monic integer factor of \(D\) has unit constant.
Thus the irreducible factor containing \(\sigma^{-1}\) must also contain the exterior root \(\sigma\).
Hence all remaining conjugates of \(\sigma\) have modulus one.
Eventually \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}<3\), excluding a reciprocal quadratic with integer trace, so these are Salem numbers tending to one.

For each \(K\) use the positive fixed-support gap \(\eta_K\) of [Dobrowolski and Smyth](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001).
If any product of cyclotomic polynomials \(C\), including \(1\), gives \(CD\) at most \(K\) nonzero coefficients, then \(m(D)=m(CD)\ge\eta_K\).
The increasing function \(x+x^{-1}-2\) for \(x>1\) gives the uniform bound
\[
                     \delta\ge\tfrac29(e^{\eta_K}+e^{-\eta_K}-2).
\]
If \(Cf\) has at most \(s\) terms, then \(CD=z(Cf)\pm(Cf)^*\) has at most \(2s\) terms, because \(C^*=\pm C\), with star denoting reversal in the actual degree.
Thus along any such near-integer sequence the minimum number of terms in a cyclotomic multiple of \(D\), and also that minimum for \(f\), must both tend to infinity.
This applies the existing [cyclotomic support obstruction](Yin_L_G0019_v1.md#cyclotomic-support-escape) to the positive delay-one return.
The main researcher selected the near-integer route, the two coefficient families and the sparse perturbation argument.
The proof contributor obtained the explicit four-term cutoff and the cyclotomic restriction on the leading pattern.

<a id="dominant-coefficient"></a>
## A sharp lower bound when one coefficient dominates

Suppose the absolute value of one coefficient of \(f\) exceeds the sum of the absolute values of all the others.
If its position is \(j\), comparison with that monomial on \(|z|=1\) gives exactly \(j\) interior zeros by Rouché's theorem, hence \(j=r-1\).
The sign of this coefficient is the sign of \(f(1)\), so write it as \(-A\).
Let \(B\) be the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients in positions \(1,\ldots,r-2\), taking \(B=0\) for \(r=2\).
Integrality and strict dominance give \(A\ge q+2+B\).
In particular, every \(f\) as above with just one negative coefficient meets the strict dominance condition, since \(f(1)<0\).
Evaluating \(f(\theta)=0\) and dividing by \(\theta^{r-1}\) gives
\[
        \theta-q\ge A-q-B/\theta-q/\theta^{r-1}
              >1+B(1-1/\theta),
\]
where \(q/\theta^{r-1}<1\).
The infimum of \(\theta-q\) over this class is one.
Indeed \(z^2-(q+2)z+q\) has one root in \((0,1)\) and one above \(q+1\), and is irreducible because its interior root cannot be an integer.
Its Pisot root satisfies \(\theta-q=1+2/(\sqrt{q^2+4}+q)\to1\).

<a id="four-term"></a>
## A four-term family with two negative coefficients

Take \(1\le k<r-1\) and
\[
               f=z^{r-1}(z-q)+q(1-z^k).
\]
Since \(f(q)<0\), there is a real root \(\theta>q\), necessarily the Pisot root if \(f\) is a Pisot minimal polynomial.
Its equation gives
\[
              0<\theta-q=q(\theta^k-1)/\theta^{r-1}<q/\theta<1.
\]
In this family \(D=z^{r+1}-qz^{k+1}-qz^{r-k}+1\), where the middle terms combine to \(-2qz^{k+1}\) when \(2k=r-1\).
Its coefficient maximum is at least \(q\).
The [sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) for at most four terms gives \(q\le4M(D)\).
If \(f\) were a Pisot minimal polynomial with \(q\ge12\), then \(M(D)=\sigma\ge3\), so \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2\ge4/3\).
Boyd's bound and \(\theta-q<1\) give \(\sigma+\sigma^{-1}-2<169/132<4/3\), because \((q+1)^2/[q(q-1)]\) decreases for \(q\ge2\).
This excludes every \(q\ge12\), independently of both exponents.

<a id="sparse-pattern"></a>
## The leading integral pattern for a sublinear difference

More generally consider a sequence with \(q\to\infty\), \(f=qA+B\), nonzero \(A\in\mathbb Z[z]\), and \(h=\|B\|_1/q\to0\), where the norm is the sum of the absolute coefficients.
Suppose \(A\) and \(f\) each have at most \(s\) nonzero terms for some fixed \(s\ge2\).
Then
\[
              |m(f/q)-m(A)|\le C_s h^{1/(2s-1)}
                   \qquad(0<h\le1/2).
\]
Indeed the circle supremum of \(|f/q-A|\) is at most \(h\), the largest coefficient of \(A\) in absolute value is at least one, and that of \(f/q\) is at least \(1-h\).
For each polynomial the [sparse logarithm regularization estimate (2)](Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#regularity), including its monomial case, bounds the error on replacing \(\log|P|\) in the mean by \(\tfrac12\log(|P|^2+t^2)\) by \(O_s(t^{1/[2(s-1)]})\) for \(0<t\le1\).
The regularized means differ by at most \(h/(2t)\) by the derivative bound.
Taking \(t=h^{2(s-1)/(2s-1)}\) gives the estimate, with equality of the means when \(h=0\).

If \(f\) is a Pisot minimal polynomial and \(\theta-q=o(q)\), then \(m(f/q)=\log(\theta/q)\to0\), so \(m(A)\to0\).
The fixed-support gap gives \(m(A)=0\) eventually, hence \(A\) is a signed monomial times a product of cyclotomic polynomials by Kronecker's theorem.
In fact \(A(0)=1\) eventually because \(f(0)/q=1\) and \(h\to0\).
The monicity and degree of \(f\) likewise give \(\deg A\le r-1\) eventually, by coefficientwise approximation and integrality.
Boyd's inequality gives \(M(D)=\sigma\le2+\tfrac92\delta=o(q)\), since here \(\delta=o(q)\).
The polynomial \(D\) has at most \(2s\) terms, so its coefficient maximum is \(o(q)\) by the sparse coefficient bound.
In the identity
\[
       D/q=zA+z^r A(1/z)+\big(zB+z^r B(1/z)\big)/q
\]
the last summand has coefficient maximum at most \(2h\).
Thus every coefficient of \(zA+z^r A(1/z)\) has absolute value \(o(1)\) uniformly, and this integer polynomial vanishes identically eventually.
After division by \(z\) we obtain \(A=-z^{r-1}A(1/z)\).
Since \(A(0)=1\), its degree is exactly \(r-1\) and its leading coefficient is \(-1\).
In particular the surviving leading pattern is an antireciprocal cyclotomic product up to sign, divisible by \(z-1\).

For the four-term family \(A=1-z^k-z^{r-1}\), and \(f/q-A=z^r/q\) has circle supremum \(1/q\).
The reversal of \(A\) cannot equal \(A\), by the endpoint coefficients, and cannot equal \(-A\), since the middle coefficient has the same sign after reversal even if its position is fixed.
Consequently the monic unit polynomial \(-A\) has a nonreciprocal irreducible factor, giving \(M(A)\) at least the plastic number by [Smyth's theorem](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U002.md#smy08-reciprocal-number).
The perturbation estimate thus excludes a large-\(q\) Pisot sequence even at the relative scale \((\theta-q)/q\to0\).
The leading-pattern restriction leaves open the existence of positive-constant Pisot seeds with \(\theta-q\to0\) and unbounded support after cyclotomic multiplication.
