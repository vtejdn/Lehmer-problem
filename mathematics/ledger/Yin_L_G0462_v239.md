# Extending one prescribed value to two independent integral rows

<a id="extension"></a>
## Keeping the same first value and the same degree cap

Use the notation of the [finite integer test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement).
Thus \(P_0\) is a monic reciprocal irreducible nontorsion unit polynomial of even degree \(e\ge4\), \(\alpha\) is a root, \(h=m(P_0)\) is its logarithmic Mahler measure, \(z=\alpha^p\), and \(K^+=\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\).
It suffices here that \(p\ge2\) preserves the degree \(e\).
Set \(B=4e,\ f=Y^{-2e}a,\ g=Y^{-2e}b\) and \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\).
Suppose one pair \(a,b\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) with \(\deg a=B,\ a(0)=[Y^B]a=1,\ \deg b<B\) satisfies
\[
 f(z)=u\ne0,\qquad (\mathcal E f)(z)=\ell,\qquad u,\ell\in K^+,\qquad
 (g(z),(\mathcal E g)(z))=\alpha^{-1}(-u,\ell+u/p).                 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(H\) be its maximum absolute coefficient.
There are two rows satisfying all the conditions of the test, with this same \(u\), both unit endpoints and maximum absolute coefficient at most
\[
                    H+8e\,4^e\exp(2ph).                              \tag{2}
\]
The construction permits a rational initial ratio \(\ell/u\) and an arbitrary initial gcd.
If the given row already passes the individual test, it can be retained as the first row with its original coefficients.

At the prescribed \(p=p_e\) of C406 let \(H_1\) denote the least possible \(H\) under (1), with the same unit endpoints and degree caps.
Whenever a row exists,
\[
 H_1\ \le\ H(P_0)\ \le\ H_1+8e\,4^e e^{2ph}
       \ \le\ H_1+8e\,4^e(4e+1)^2H_1^2,                              \tag{3}
\]
where \(H(P_0)\) is the two-row minimum of C406.
Indeed (1) already implies \(P_0^2\mid G=a(x^p)+x^{pB-1}b(x^{-p})\).
The centered expression \(x^{-2ep}G=f(x^p)+x^{-1}g(x^{-p})\) vanishes at \(\alpha\) by (1) and inversion.
Its Euler derivative there is \(p\ell+u-p(\ell+u/p)=0\).
The [individual coefficient lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#integer) therefore applies with these endpoints, giving \(ph\le\log((4e+1)H_1)\) without the coprimality or irrationality requirements.
The reflected elimination used there has nonzero extreme coefficients because \(\deg b<B\) and both endpoints of \(a\) are one.
In particular \(\log H_1=o(p_e)\) is equivalent to \(\log H(P_0)=o(p_e)\) along growing degrees.
Along any shrinking sequence the possible additive difference of the two logarithms is \(O(ph+e+\log e)=o(p_e)\).
These comparisons apply directly to a specified small first row, independently of the existence proof of C406.

## An integral perturbation with zero value and nonzero derivative

Put \(m=e/2,\ Q(Y)=\min_{\mathbb Q}(z)\).
Index the conjugates \(\alpha_i\) and set \(z_i=\alpha_i^p\), which are pairwise distinct.
Use the integral polynomial
\[
 T(Y)=\sum_i \alpha_i^{p-1}\prod_{j\ne i}(Y-z_j).
\]
Its coefficients are algebraic integers invariant under the absolute Galois group and hence are rational integers.
At \(z=\alpha^p\) one has \(T(z)=\alpha^{p-1}Q'(z)=z\alpha^{-1}Q'(z)\).
Define
\[
 \Delta a=Y^{e+1}QQ',\qquad \Delta b=Y^e QT,\qquad
 \lambda=(z^{1-m}Q'(z))^2 .
\]
The centered reciprocal Laurent polynomial \(Y^{-m}Q(Y)\) is fixed by \(Y\mapsto Y^{-1}\).
Its Euler derivative changes sign under inversion, with nonzero value \(z^{1-m}Q'(z)\) at \(z\), so \(\lambda\in K^+\setminus\{0\}\).
Both centered perturbations vanish at \(z\), and direct differentiation gives
\[
 \bigl(\mathcal E(Y^{-2e}\Delta a)(z),\mathcal E(Y^{-2e}\Delta b)(z)\bigr)
                              =(\lambda,\alpha^{-1}\lambda).
\]
Thus they preserve (1) on replacing \(\ell\) by \(\ell+\lambda\).
They may both be multiplied by \(S(Y)=Y+Y^{-1}\), since their values vanish.
This replaces \(\lambda\) by \(s\lambda\) with \(s=z+z^{-1}\).
The trace \(s\) has degree \(e/2\) because \(z\) generates the original field and its inverse involution fixes \(\mathbb Q(s)\) with index two.
Consequently \(s\notin\mathbb Q\), and at least one of \(\lambda/u,\ s\lambda/u\) is irrational.
Choose the corresponding perturbation and call its derivative \(\lambda_*\).
In either choice all occupied exponents in \(\Delta a,\Delta b\) lie strictly between \(0\) and \(4e\), since the unmultiplied degrees are at most \(3e,3e-1\) and the initial exponents are at least \(e+1,e\).

For \(k=0,1,2\) the three perturbed rows have first value \(u\) and slopes \((\ell+k\lambda_*)/u\).
At most one slope is rational, and the determinant for any two distinct parameters is \((k_2-k_1)u\lambda_*\ne0\).
If the original slope was already irrational, keep \(k=0\) as one choice.
Every selected row has \(b(z)=-z^{2e}\alpha^{-1}u\ne0\) with its current coefficients.
To obtain the required seed gcd, add \(tY^e Q(Y)^2\) to its \(a\) alone.
At each nonzero root \(\xi\) of \(Y^{B-1}b(1/Y)\), this direction is nonzero because \(Q\) is reciprocal and \(b(z)\ne0\).
Each such root forbids at most one \(t\), and zero is already excluded by \(a(0)=1\).
Hence \(0\le t\le B-1\) suffices, allowing \(t=0\) for an already coprime row.
Both centered first jets and the two unit endpoints survive this second perturbation.

For the coefficient estimates we have \(\|Q\|_1\le2^e\exp(ph)\) and \(\|Q'\|_1\le e\|Q\|_1\).
In each summand of \(T\), \(|\alpha_i|^{p-1}\le\max(1,|z_i|)\) replaces the missing \(i\)-th factor, giving \(\|T\|_1\le e2^{e-1}\exp(ph)\).
Each unmultiplied perturbation has norm at most \(e4^e\exp(2ph)\), and the optional \(S\) at most doubles it.
The two coefficients \(k\le2,\ t\le4e-1\) now prove (2).
By taking more integer parameters one likewise obtains arbitrarily many pairwise nonproportional columns at the same nonzero first value.

## Lifted coprimality, representatives without prescribed endpoints and cost

If the lifted gcd in the [four-class construction](Yin_L_G0462_v229.md#coprime) is also required, take an irreducible admissible representative \(P_0(x^n)\) with offset \(v=1\), \(pr=1+n\) and \(d=ne\).
Assume that \(p\) preserves the full degree, as at the substitution prime of [C406](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#uniform).
For either row after its \(k\)-perturbation form \(A(X)=a(X^n)+X^r b(X^n)\) and \(A^*(X)=X^{4d}A(1/X)\).
Equation (1) gives its two centered traces \(u,\,2pn\ell_{\rm new}-u\) by the [offset jet identities](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#jets).
In particular \(E=A-A^*\) is nonzero at the powered root of the full input since \(u\ne0\).
Adding \(tY^eQ^2\) to \(a\) leaves \(E\) fixed by reciprocal symmetry.
The powered minimum \(Q(X^n)\) misses every nonzero root of \(E\), so at each such root at most one value of \(t\) is forbidden.
Imposing the seed gcd simultaneously therefore permits \(0\le t\le4d+B-1\), giving (2) with \(8e\) replaced by \(8e+4d\).
The change in the logarithmic bound from the additional choices is at most \(O(\log(2d))\), which is \(O(e)\) at the degree-only prime \(n_e\) since \(p_e\asymp e^4\) and \(\log(3d)\asymp p_e^{1/4}\).
An existing row already meeting both gcd requirements and the slope condition may again be retained.

If (1) is supplied by integral representatives of size \(H\) and degrees at most \(B,B-1\) but the endpoints of \(a\) have not been prescribed, first add
\[
            (1-a(0))Q^2+(1-[Y^B]a)Y^{2e}Q^2
\]
to \(a\), as in the [endpoint corrections](Yin_L_G0462_v235.md#endpoints).
This fixes both endpoints, retains both jets and costs at most \(2(H+1)4^e\exp(2ph)\) in coefficient size before applying (2).
The resulting bound depends on the sizes of the supplied representatives.

The nonzero derivative is essential to changing the column at fixed \(u\).
For example, multiplying both centered entries of (1) by the same Laurent polynomial \(D\) gives a new admissible jet of nonzero first value only if \(2u(\mathcal E D)(z)=0\), by comparing the two product rules in (1).
Its column is proportional to the original.
Any nonzero integral perturbation of the first polynomial leaving \(u\) fixed and changing \(\ell\) is divisible by \(Q\), so its logarithmic coefficient maximum is at least \(ph-\log(B+1)\) in this degree range.
For the particular direction constructed above, this logarithmic maximum for \(\Delta a\) is \(2ph+O(e+\log e)\).
Indeed \(\|Q'\|_\infty\ge\|Q\|_\infty\ge\exp(ph)/(e+1)\) since \(Q\) is monic with constant one, and \(\|Q'\|_\infty\le2^{e-1}\exp(m(Q'))\) by the elementary symmetric bounds.
Thus \(m(\Delta a)=ph+m(Q')\ge2ph-(e-1)\log2-\log(e+1)\), unchanged by the possible \(S\) of logarithmic measure zero.
The coefficient upper bound above and \(m(\Delta a)\le\log((4e+1)\|\Delta a\|_\infty)\) give the stated scale.
The optimum additive cost of a second row at a fixed positive seed measure remains undetermined by these estimates.
