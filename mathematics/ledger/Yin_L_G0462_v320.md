# Matching radial pairs to cyclotomic roots in the quotient energy

<a id="matching"></a>
## The cost of moving the two roots in one pair

On 2026-11-20 the G0001 inquiry tested integral numerators which move each reciprocal-conjugate pair of a hyperbolic input to two roots on the circle.
Let \(P\) be a monic irreducible reciprocal integer polynomial of degree \(2h>0\) with no unit-circle root and logarithmic Mahler measure \(m\).
Index its pairs by \(e^{\ell_j}u_j,e^{-\ell_j}u_j\), where \(|u_j|=1\), \(\ell_j>0\), and \(\sum_j\ell_j=m\).
Take any monic product \(B\) of cyclotomic polynomials of total degree \(h\), and match its roots \(\omega_j\), counted with multiplicity, to the pair labels in any order.
Write \(b_j=|\omega_j-u_j|\), \(s_j=2\sinh(\ell_j/2)\) and \(K(m)=\max_{k\ge1,\ k\in\mathbb Z}k e^{-2km}\).
The integral numerator \(B^2\) is coprime to \(P\).
The [energy inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#statement) and the matching give
\[
 \sqrt{K(m)}\le\|B^2/P\|_2\le
 \prod_{j=1}^h\left(\operatorname{sech}^2(\ell_j/2)+
                                  \frac{b_j^2}{\sinh^2\ell_j}\right).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
In particular all matchings along a hypothetical shrinking sequence satisfy
\[
 \sum_j\log\!\left(1+\frac{b_j^2}{\sinh^2\ell_j}\right)
   \ge \tfrac12\log K(m)
   =\tfrac12\log(1/m)-\tfrac12\log(2e)+o(1).
\]

On \(|z|=1\) the pair has product of distances \(|z-u_j|^2+s_j^2\).
For one pair put \(a=e^{-\ell_j}\) and suppress the index \(j\).
Its ratio after replacing the pair by the double zero at \(\omega\) is
\[
 \frac{|z-\omega|^2}{|z-u|^2+s^2}
       =a\left|\frac{1-\bar\omega z}{1-a\bar u z}\right|^2 .
\]
The Möbius function sends the circle to a circle with center \((1-a\bar\omega u)/(1-a^2)\) and radius equal to the modulus of that center.
Indeed solving for \(z\) gives the circle equation \(|1-w|=|\bar\omega-a\bar u w|\), and \(z=\omega\) gives \(w=0\).
Its maximum modulus is therefore \(2|1-a\bar\omega u|/(1-a^2)\).
Using \(|1-a\bar\omega u|^2=(1-a)^2+ab^2\) yields the individual factor in (1).
The product of individual maxima bounds the supremum of the full quotient.

<a id="overlap"></a>
## When the individual maxima occur at different angles

A second bound retains the actual positions of the pair phases.
Define
\[
 W(z)=\sum_j\frac{b_j}{\sqrt{|z-u_j|^2+s_j^2}},\qquad |z|=1.
\]
The triangle inequality gives \(|z-\omega_j|/\sqrt{|z-u_j|^2+s_j^2}\le 1+b_j/\sqrt{|z-u_j|^2+s_j^2}\).
Multiplication and integration show
\[
                K(m)\le \int_{\mathbb T} e^{4W(z)}\,d\mu(z),
                \qquad \sup_{\mathbb T} W\ge\tfrac14\log K(m).
\]
Here \(\mu\) is normalized circle measure.
This test keeps the local overlap of the phase errors, which the product in (1) discards.
Both estimates use the original radial distances and apply to every matching at the given input.
The [older angular discrepancy](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v615.md#statement) permits signed cyclotomic counts and compares their cumulative distribution with the exterior phases.
For the present direct numerator construction, it remains to exhibit matchings with sufficiently small energy on hypothetical strict degree records.

<a id="rounding"></a>
## Optimizing within a chosen numerator class

One can improve a matched numerator without choosing new root angles.
For any fixed \(Q\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]\) indivisible by \(P\), write \(t_n\) for the real Laurent coefficients of \(Q/P\) on the circle.
They decay exponentially because this rational function is analytic in an annulus around the circle.
Parseval gives the exact least energy within its residue class,
\[
 \min_{B\in\mathbb Z[X,X^{-1}]}\|(Q-PB)/P\|_2^2
                =\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{dist}(t_n,\mathbb Z)^2 .
\]
A nearest integer at every position attains the right side, since only finitely many coefficients have magnitude at least \(1/2\).
The fixed-class correction may use either tail as well as the middle positions in the [rounded numerator estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v623.md#tails).
For example, take \(Q=X^h\) or the square of the matched cyclotomic product.
At \(P=X^2-aX+1\), \(a\ge3\), the coefficients of \(X/P\) have magnitudes at most \(1/\sqrt{a^2-4}<1/2\), by the [geometric expansion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v622.md#quadratic).
The central monomial therefore already minimizes its class, although \(1-X\) has smaller energy when \(a=3\).
On a hypothetical shrinking sequence the [energy lower bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v620.md#statement) forces the lower limit of \(m\) times the displayed sum to be at least \(1/(2e)\) for each such choice, even if \(Q\) varies with the input.
The [reciprocal determinant bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v630.md#statement) controls the volume of the tail lattice and leaves these distances to individual integers available for a direct test.
