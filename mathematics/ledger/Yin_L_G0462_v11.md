# Neutral prefix supply, multiplicity rigidity and integer pole mass

<a id="scope"></a>
## What was tested after the whole-measure upper bound

The [adopted prefix upper bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#statement) makes a growing exact prefix sufficient to control the whole measure under the original strict signs and endpoint.
It does not supply a noncyclotomic integer completion.
This exploration compared an explicit cyclotomic completion mechanism, rounding of real completions, and arithmetic information at the rational zero of a prescribed profile.

No mechanism giving positive noncyclotomic counts at unbounded matching lengths was obtained.
The useful return is more specific: a natural exact integer supply is trapped at measure one, while an integer value at an actual pole yields a new quantitative length obstruction.
The latter complete statement and proof are retained in [adopted C881v4](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v4.md#decision-integer-pole-prefix).
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v26.md#neutral-supply-review) covers this ledger's complete arguments and comparisons at their stated scopes.
This material owns the strategic development; the Claim and review own their separate assessment and findings.

<a id="neutral-integer-supply"></a>
## An explicit supply of arbitrarily long integer prefixes

Fix \(x\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\).
Take \(r\ge4\) even and \(s\ge3\) odd, and set \(p=r+s=2n+1\).
Consider
\[
 P^{(0)}_{r,s,x}(z)
  =\frac{(1-xz+z^2)^2(1-z^r)(1-z^s)}
          {(1-z)^2(1+z)}.                                 \tag{1}
\]
The two binomials supply the two factors \(1-z\), and the even binomial supplies \(1+z\).
Thus (1) is an integer polynomial.
It is monic, reciprocal, has constant coefficient one, and has degree \(p+1=2n+2\).
The factor \(1-xz+z^2\) is cyclotomic, with multiplicities permitted for \(x=\pm2\).
After the displayed divisions, the remaining binomial factors are also products of cyclotomic polynomials.
Hence the complete measure in (1) is exactly one.

For every \(L<\min(r,s)\), its coefficients through index \(L\) agree exactly with
\[
                        P_{*,x}(z)
                          =\frac{(1-xz+z^2)^2}{(1-z)^2(1+z)}.
\]
Letting both \(r,s\) grow therefore gives actual integer completions at unbounded matching lengths.
Their counts contribute only to the neutral count, not to the strict positive shell.

The reciprocal integer polynomial (1) has a monic integer trace representation
\[
                 P^{(0)}=z^{n+1}Q^{(0)}(z+z^{-1}).
\]
For example, this follows by expressing \(z^{-(n+1)}P^{(0)}\) in the integer basis \(1,T_1,\ldots,T_{n+1}\).
Its first coefficient fixes the quotient by \(B_n\) to be \(t-2x\), so
\[
 Q^{(0)}=(t-2x)B_n+C,\qquad \deg C<n.
\]
The [recurrence identity in C881v3](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates) includes
\(E_{n-1}B_{n-1}\equiv1\pmod{B_n}\).
Thus \(h=E_{n-1}C\bmod B_n\) is an actual integer input in the original affine domain.

The pole residues have the explicit form
\[
 r_j^{(0)}
   =\frac{(b_j-x)^2(2-T_r(b_j))}{p}
   =\frac{4(b_j-x)^2\sin^2(\pi rj/p)}p
   \ge0.                                                  \tag{2}
\]
To obtain (2), evaluate (1) at \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i j/p}\), use \(s=p-r\), and divide its trace value by the derivative of \(B_n\) given in C881v3.
The phases cancel because
\(\zeta^{-n-1}\zeta^{1/2}=\exp(-\pi i j)=(-1)^j\).
In particular the family has no strictly negative terminal block.
If \(\gcd(r,p)=1\) and \(x\) is not a grid point, all its residues are strictly positive.
When \(x=-1\) and \(3\mid p\), the residue at the actual pole \(-1\) is zero, which already violates the strict input domain.

This supplies genuine integer prefixes but does not supply admissible positive-measure completions.
Increasing the number of such neutral completions is not a lower bound for the desired noncyclotomic count.

<a id="double-factor-obstruction"></a>
## Keeping the double profile factor forces the whole measure to one

A broader failure affects perturbations that preserve the factor \((t-x)^2\).
Take any real monic \(Q\) of degree \(n+1\) on the original full odd grid, with the strict single-cut residues and strict endpoint \(Q(-2)/B_n(-2)<0\).
Suppose
\[
                              Q(t)=(t-x)^2R(t),
                              \qquad x\in[-2,2].
\]
If \(x\) were a grid pole, a residue would vanish, contradicting strictness.
If \(x=-2\), the endpoint would vanish, also contradicting strictness.
Thus the square is strictly positive at every pole and at \(-2\).

The residues of \(R/B_n\) have exactly the same strict signs as those of \(Q/B_n\).
The endpoint sign of \(R/B_n\) is also the same.
There is therefore a root of \(R\) in each of the \(n-2\) same-sign gaps, and a further root between \(-2\) and the smallest pole.
These are \(n-1\) distinct roots in \((-2,2)\).
Since \(R\) is monic of degree \(n-1\), they account for all its roots.
Together with the two retained roots at \(x\), every trace root of \(Q\) lies in \([-2,2]\), with its original multiplicity.
Every root of the complete reciprocal lift is consequently on the unit circle, and its whole measure is one.

This argument concerns the entire polynomial, not a selected factor.
For integral inputs, Kronecker's characterization at the retained [Smy08-U001 scope](../audits/literature/Smy08/Yin_U001.md#smy08-height-normalization) also identifies it as a cyclotomic product.
Only that theorem statement is reused, not a new examination of its original proof.

Thus gluing neutral blocks or using Fejer-square expressions while retaining \((t-x)^2\mid Q\) cannot contribute to the desired strict positive count in this chamber.
For example, when \(r=2m\le n-1\), the residues in (2) correspond directly to
\[
                              h(t)=(t-x)^2E_{m-1}(t)^2.
\]
This convenient integral square construction has the exact neutral factor built into it.
Any useful deformation must break that factor and retain the original strict signs and endpoint simultaneously.

<a id="rounding-comparison"></a>
## Why continuous completion and coefficient rounding add no supply here

The free tail after fixing a prefix is an integer coefficient lattice, so a real completion with a sufficiently large certified rounding margin could in principle supply an integer completion.
But a real point with strict signs supplies no dimension-independent margin.
Rounding the free coefficients changes every original pole value, and the same prefix fixes the positive weighted mass to an amount independent of the number of free coordinates.
No covering-radius or sign-margin estimate adequate for this lattice was obtained.

This is the same missing actual-lattice transfer exposed by [L449](Yin_L_G0449_v1.md#uniform-volume-and-lattice-obstruction), rather than a new positive-count theorem.
An average over translated lattices would concern a different distribution.
The neutral family above does not fix that problem: it lies outside the required negative-block chamber, and at a rational profile pole it has a zero rather than a robust positive margin.
Accordingly neither a continuous small-ball event nor many cyclotomic completions is used to claim a positive original probability.

<a id="integer-pole-return"></a>
## The arithmetic return: a mandatory mass at the rational profile pole

For the \(x=-1\) profile and \(3\mid p\), the point \(-1\) belongs to the original grid.
If the cut lies below \(-1\), the strict residue sign forces the actual integer value \(h(-1)\) to be at least one.
This is the discrete jump missing from the neutral completions, where that value vanishes.

[C881v4](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v4.md#statement) measures that jump using a polynomial normalized to one at \(-1\).
Its exact moment is determined by the prefix, while the mass at the pole has an explicit positive arithmetic floor.
The resulting necessary inequality is
\[
 27a^3\le4p
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad
 6a-1\le L\le n+1.
\]
No endpoint condition or Mahler estimate is used in this obstruction.
The complete polynomial-divisibility, degree, quadrature and Laurent-count arguments belong to C881v4.

At the fixed conductor \(19683\), the single choice \(a=15\) already excludes every integer matching length \(89\le L\le9842\) in the stated negative-one chamber.
This is an exact arithmetic test, not a numerical failure or a norm-budget exclusion.
It does not affect the main agent's shorter seven-moment input by silently treating it as a length-89 prefix.

The asymptotic necessary scale is \(L^3\lesssim32p\).
Thus this ternary-conductor supply must let the conductor grow at least cubically with a requested long matching length.
Logarithmic matching lengths remain outside the obstruction.
For conductors not divisible by three, the specific rational pole is absent and this argument supplies no corresponding exclusion.
It therefore changes how one should compare conductors for long-prefix construction, while providing no surviving integer completion.

<a id="jet-screen"></a>
## What remains of the integer-jet proposal

For the five integer profile values, the double-factor obstruction suggests inspecting the actual integers \(Q(x)\) and \(Q'(x)\), rather than testing only real feasibility of a prefix.
If the complete real relaxation forced both into \((-1,1)\), successively with \(Q(x)=0\), integrality would force the neutral double factor.
The [finite covering coefficient bound](Yin_L_G0462_v10.md#actual-count-obligation) makes each fixed real sign relaxation bounded, so its jet ranges are concrete linear optimization quantities.

For example, the existing \(p=81,\ \kappa=3,\ x=0,\ L=4\) domain fixes \(a_0=2,a_1=0,a_2=4\).
One could optimize \(Q(0)\), then \(Q'(0)\) on its zero slice, using the entire real coefficient domain and the necessary integer endpoint \(P(-1)\ge1\).
Floating optimization would only propose bounds; an exact or certified algebraic dual would be needed to force either integer value.
This would examine the domain of the [bounded third-moment test](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0155/third_moment.md#result), not infer infeasibility from its time limit.

No estimate placing these entire jet ranges inside \((-1,1)\) was obtained here.
Near-pole denominators prevent a small range from following merely from low moments and bounded total weighted mass.
Accordingly this jet idea remains an unselected exploratory lead, not a substitute assumption for positive counts and not a new numerical assignment.
The rational-pole test above was preferred because it has an explicit kernel and a completed discriminating return without assuming unknown small jets.

<a id="strategy-return"></a>
## Remaining construction question

The inspected mechanisms do not yet provide noncyclotomic integer long-prefix extensions or a positive actual count.
They distinguish two issues that a new supply must solve: break the neutral double factor, and pay the integer mass forced at any rational profile pole while preserving the full moment prefix and strict chamber.
The exact pole test supplies a conductor-dependent arithmetic restriction, not a uniform gap and not a refutation of the prefix strategy.
No new scan, larger mixed-integer budget or changed conductor is selected by this exploration.
