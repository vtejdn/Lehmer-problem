# Schinzel's rational specialization conjecture and the measures of sparse factors

<a id="shortness"></a>
## A conditional bound for cyclotomic multiples

Fix a nonzero rational Laurent polynomial \(F\) in \(n\) variables.
Write \(F(t^{\mathbf a})=F(t^{a_1},\ldots,t^{a_n})\) for \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z^n\), and consider only nonzero specializations.
Represent each rational irreducible Laurent factor by a primitive integer polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term and positive leading coefficient.
Call such a factor nontorsion if its roots are not roots of unity, and write \(m(P)\) for its logarithmic Mahler measure.
Let \(\ell(f)\) denote the sum of the absolute coefficients of an integral Laurent polynomial after collecting terms.
The [cyclotomic shortness](../audits/literature/ESM25/readme.md#shortness-scope) of \(P\) is the least \(\ell(PC)\) over all products \(C\) of cyclotomic polynomials, including the empty product and allowing repetitions.

Assume [Schinzel's rational specialization conjecture](../audits/literature/AS18/Yin_U001.md#as18-u001) for the irreducible noncyclotomic polynomials encountered in the finite descent below.
There is an integer \(B(F)\ge2\) such that every nontorsion irreducible factor of every nonzero \(F(t^{\mathbf a})\) has cyclotomic shortness at most \(B(F)\).
Consequently all these factors have \(m(P)\ge\delta_{B(F)}>0\), where \(\delta_s\) is a [lower bound for the positive log measure of an entire integer polynomial with at most \(s\) terms](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#sparse-measure), for \(s\ge2\).
Indeed \(m(PC)=m(P)\) and the term count of the integer multiple \(PC\) is at most its length.

Factor \(F\) over the rational Laurent ring and consider each irreducible factor \(H\) separately.
A multivariate cyclotomic factor is associated to \(\Phi_d(\mathbf X^{\mathbf u})\) for a primitive integer vector \(\mathbf u\).
Each nonzero univariate specialization of this factor is constant or has only torsion roots.
At a remaining factor in \(r\) variables, Conjecture 2.1 gives finitely many exceptional rational hyperplanes and finitely many nonsingular integer matrices \(M\). [Amoroso and Sombra, pp. 364–365](../audits/literature/AS18/readme.md#journal)
Off these hyperplanes, write \(\mathbf a=M\mathbf b\) for a choice guaranteed by the conjecture, using the convention \((t^{\mathbf b})^M=t^{M\mathbf b}\).
Among the rational irreducible factors \(G\) of \(H(\mathbf X^M)\), one specializes to a polynomial whose cyclotomic-free part is the specified \(P\), up to a rational Laurent unit.
Choose \(G\) primitive integral, remove a monomial and the integer content of \(G(t^{\mathbf b})\), and fix its sign.
The result is \(PC\) for a cyclotomic product \(C\), by Gauss's lemma, and \(\ell(PC)\le\ell(G)\) because collecting exponents and removing content can only decrease length.
The maximum of the lengths of the finitely many \(G\) bounds the cyclotomic shortness on these directions.

For an exceptional equation \(\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf a=0\), let the columns of \(D\) be an integral basis of \(\mathbf c^\perp\cap\mathbb Z^r\).
Write \(\mathbf a=D\mathbf b\) and apply the same argument to the rational factors of \(H(\mathbf X^D)\), now in \(r-1\) variables.
An identically zero restriction contributes no nonzero specialization of the original parent along this branch.
Laurent units and rank-zero restrictions contribute no factors.
Each node has finitely many children, and each descent lowers \(r\), so the required conjectural inputs and the maximum \(B(F)\) are finite.
The conjecture for a single irreducible \(F\) only controls directions away from its own exceptional hyperplanes.
In rank two its exceptional lines already have an unconditional factor gap by the [common-dilation bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) applied to the two primitive directions of each line.
Thus in rank two the conjecture for \(F\) itself suffices for the gap, as in the [cubic single-core case](Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#schinzel).

<a id="obstruction"></a>
## A shrinking sequence identifies a particular counterexample to the conjecture

Suppose that actual nontorsion factors of specializations of a fixed \(F\) have positive log measures tending to zero.
Unconditionally, there exist a connected rational subtorus of rank \(r\ge2\), a single rational irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(H\) of the restriction of \(F\) to that subtorus, and primitive directions \(\mathbf v_j\in\mathbb Z^r\) with the following properties.
The factor \(H\) can be chosen reciprocal up to a Laurent unit, with support differences spanning \(\mathbb Q^r\) and \(2r\le\#\operatorname{supp}F\).
Every proper rational hyperplane contains only finitely many \(\mathbf v_j\), and there are nontorsion factors \(P_j\mid H(t^{\mathbf v_j})\) with \(m(P_j)\to0\).
Fix any finite set of nonsingular integer \(r\times r\) matrices.
For all sufficiently large \(j\), take any matrix \(M\) in the set with \(\mathbf v_j=M\mathbf b\) for integral \(\mathbf b\).
Every irreducible pullback factor \(G\mid H(\mathbf X^M)\) whose specialization contains \(P_j\) then specializes with a second, distinct nontorsion rational irreducible factor.
In particular the rational specialization conjecture fails for this one \(H\).

To remove common dilations, first normalize \(F\) to be primitive integral and write each original nonzero direction as \(k\mathbf w\) with \(k>0\) and \(\mathbf w\) primitive.
For a root \(\alpha\) of the chosen shrinking factor, \(\beta=\alpha^k\) is a nontorsion root of \(F(t^{\mathbf w})\).
After removing a monomial the latter parent has integer length at most \(L=\max(2,\ell(F))\).
The [radical measure inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v210.md#statement) gives \(0<m(\beta)\le R_Lm(\alpha)\), uniformly in \(k\) and \(\mathbf w\).
Thus the minimal polynomials of the \(\beta\) give shrinking factors already along primitive directions.

Choose a rational linear subspace \(W\) of least dimension containing infinitely many of these primitive directions, retaining just those indices.
Write its integral lattice as \(D\mathbb Z^r=W\cap\mathbb Z^n\) in an integral basis, so the columns of \(D\) describe a connected subtorus and \(\mathbf w_j=D\mathbf v_j\) with \(\mathbf v_j\) primitive.
Every proper rational hyperplane in the new coordinates meets the sequence only finitely often by minimality.
Rank zero has no primitive directions and rank one has only two, each giving just finitely many root measures, so \(r\ge2\).
The restriction \(F(\mathbf X^D)\) is nonzero and one rational irreducible factor \(H\) accounts for a shrinking subsequence by finite factorization.
The torsion-root observation excludes a cyclotomic \(H\).
Call the retained minimal polynomials \(P_j\).

The \(P_j\) are eventually reciprocal by the endpoint bounds and [Smyth's nonreciprocal gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#rank-one).
Thus \(H(\mathbf X)\) and \(H(\mathbf X^{-1})\) both vanish at \((\beta_j^{v_{j,1}},\ldots,\beta_j^{v_{j,r}})\), where \(\beta_j\) is a root of \(P_j\).
If the two Laurent polynomials were nonassociate, their common zero set would have codimension at least two, since a shared geometric divisor and its Galois conjugates would give a common rational divisor.
The [bounded-relation theorem on this intersection](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#source) would put each such \(\mathbf v_j\) on one of finitely many proper rational hyperplanes.
This contradicts escape, so \(H\) is reciprocal.

To give \(H\) full support rank while retaining a factor of a restriction of the original \(F\), extend a basis of its saturated support-difference lattice to an integral basis of all characters.
In the resulting coordinates \((\mathbf U,\mathbf Z)\), \(H\) is a monomial times a reciprocal irreducible \(H_0(\mathbf U)\) of full support rank \(q\ge1\). [Support lattice reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#rank)
The projected directions \(\mathbf u_j\) in the \(q\) coordinates still escape every fixed proper rational hyperplane, since its inverse image is a proper rational hyperplane of the original coordinates.
If \(q<r\), restrict further to an integral monomial graph \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf U^A\) on which all monomials of the current restriction of \(F\) remain distinct.
Indeed a support difference \((d,e)\) with \(e=0\) stays nonzero, and for \(e\ne0\) the equation \(d+A^{\mathsf T}e=0\) excludes only a proper affine subspace of the matrix parameters.
Finitely many such subspaces cannot cover all integer matrices.
On this connected subtorus the restricted parent is nonzero and still has factor \(H_0\).
Its specialization along \(\mathbf u_j\) is eventually nonzero because the finitely many distinct exponents of that parent eventually pair distinctly with \(\mathbf u_j\).
The polynomial \(H_0(t^{\mathbf u_j})\) contains \(P_j\) by the original factorization, up to the removed monomial.
Divide each projected direction by its positive gcd and apply the same radical measure inequality to \(H_0\) to obtain shrinking factors in primitive directions, which still escape all fixed proper rational hyperplanes.
Rank \(q=1\) is excluded by the two primitive directions argument.
Replace \(H,r,\mathbf v_j,P_j\) by these full-rank data when a reduction was needed.
The centrally symmetric Newton polytope of \(H\) has at least \(2r\) vertices, since its opposite vertex pairs span \(r\) dimensions.
The polytope of the restricted parent has at least as many vertices by the [Newton polytope sum for a product](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#rank), and the restriction has at most \(\#\operatorname{supp}F\) terms.
This proves the rank bound.

For a nonempty finite set of matrices, take \(s\) to be the maximum number of monomials among all their irreducible pullback factors of \(H\).
Here \(s\ge2\), since a nonsingular monomial map preserves distinct exponents and all Laurent monomials are units.
Whenever \(\mathbf v_j=M\mathbf b\), at least one such factor \(G\) specializes to a multiple of \(P_j\), and every specialized \(G\) is nonzero since \(H(t^{\mathbf v_j})\ne0\).
For any such multiple, let \(g\) be its primitive integral specialization after removing a monomial.
If \(g\) has \(h\le s\) occupied exponents, then every nonzero root has multiplicity at most \(h-1\).
Indeed, at a root of multiplicity at least \(h\), the first \(h\) powers of \(t\,d/dt\), starting with power zero, would give a homogeneous Vandermonde system in the nonzero monomial values, with the distinct exponents as nodes.
Writing \(e=\operatorname{ord}_{P_j}(g)\), the [full-polynomial sparse gap](../audits/literature/Dob06/Yin_U001.md#sparse-measure) and this multiplicity bound give
\[
                   m(g/P_j^e)\ \ge\ \delta_s-(s-1)m(P_j)>0
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\), uniformly over the whole finite set.
The primitive integer quotient therefore has a nontorsion factor distinct from \(P_j\).
Given any proposed finite exceptional set of hyperplanes and matrices for \(H\), eventually the directions avoid every listed hyperplane, and every compatible matrix fails to give the predicted irreducible specialization containing \(P_j\).
This proves the asserted failure even if the proposed matrices cover every direction under consideration.
For a plane component of full support rank the [degree criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v253.md#statement) additionally forces factors of degree \(o(\|\mathbf v_j\|_\infty)\).

## Uniformity over parents of bounded coefficient length

For a primitive nontorsion irreducible \(P\), let \(\lambda(P)\) be the minimum coefficient length of any nonzero integral polynomial multiple of \(P\), as in the [short-relation definition](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v204.md#statement).
Under the rational specialization conjecture in all ranks, for each integer \(L\ge2\) there is \(B_L\ge2\) such that \(\lambda(P)\le L\) implies cyclotomic shortness at most \(B_L\) and \(m(P)\ge\delta_{B_L}\).
In particular boundedness of minimum length and boundedness of cyclotomic shortness are then equivalent on every collection of such \(P\), since a cyclotomic multiple is also an integral multiple.

To see the uniformity, remove a monomial from an integral multiple of length at most \(L\), and list its nonzero coefficients \(c_0,\ldots,c_{s-1}\) in increasing order of exponent.
There are finitely many ordered nonzero integer tuples with \(\sum_i|c_i|\le L\).
Each such multiple is a specialization of \(F_{\mathbf c}=c_0+c_1X_1+\cdots+c_{s-1}X_{s-1}\), with \(2\le s\le L\).
Take the maximum of the finitely many constants in the [descent](#shortness).
Conversely, without any conjecture, a shrinking-factor sequence with \(\lambda(P_j)\le L\) must occur for one fixed tuple after passage to a subsequence.
The [obstruction](#obstruction) then gives a reciprocal full-rank factor \(H\) of a single connected subtorus restriction of this \(F_{\mathbf c}\) for which the rational conjecture fails, in a rank \(2\le r\le\lfloor s/2\rfloor\).
In particular a uniform gap for bounded-length parents with at most \(s\) terms follows already from the conjecture on full-rank reciprocal polynomials in ranks \(2,\ldots,\lfloor s/2\rfloor\).
For four or five terms, the [finite plane families for fixed support count and coefficient height](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v236.md#statement) sharpen this to finitely many conjectural plane instances.
A shrinking sequence gives a single plane parent from that collection, and its own exceptional lines have the factor gap proved above.

There is also a precise alternative when the coefficient tuples vary at bounded support count \(s\ge2\).
Normalize the parents \(f_j\) to be primitive integral after removing a monomial, and let \(H(f_j)\) be their largest absolute coefficient.
If \(P_j\mid f_j\) and \(m(P_j)\to0\) through positive values, then either one of the reciprocal conjectural instances above fails or \(H(f_j)\to\infty\) and \(m(f_j/P_j)\to\infty\).
Indeed any bounded-height subsequence has bounded length and supplies the first alternative.
In the other case [Dobrowolski and Smyth's sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) gives
\[
             m(f_j/P_j)\ge\log H(f_j)-(s-2)\log2-m(P_j).
\]
In particular a bound on the full parent measure at bounded support count already bounds its coefficient length by \(s\,2^{s-2}\exp(m(f_j))\).
The existence proof leaves the numerical dependence on \(L\) open, and bounding only the number of terms allows full parent measures to diverge.
