# Small measure forces many separated primitive relation supports

<a id="separation"></a>
## Randomly choosing one exponent per block

Let a nontorsion algebraic unit of minimum \(P\) witness the [universal threshold](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v572.md#statement) at \(t,N\) with multiplicity one and \(N\ge 8t\).
Put \(q=\lfloor N/(4t)\rfloor\).
Let \(A_j(q)\) count the translation classes of inclusion-minimal ternary relation supports of size \(j\) occurring in \([0,N)\) in which every successive gap exceeds \(q\).
Then
\[
             A_j(q)\ge (q/8)^{j-1}\qquad\text{for some }3\le j\le t+1.
\]
Indeed take \(2t\) consecutive disjoint blocks of length \(2q\), and independently choose one position uniformly among the first \(q\) positions of each block.
The chosen set has size \(2t\) and every gap is at least \(q+1\).
Delete one position per contained minimal support of weight at most \(t+1\), taking the union of all deletions.
The remaining set has at most \(t\) elements, since every \(t+1\) positions support a relation.
Consequently at least \(t\) such minimal supports occur in every outcome.
Each fixed support of size \(j\) has selection probability at most \(q^{-j}\), with probability zero if two of its positions fall in the same block.
Every class has at most \(N\) placements, giving
\[
                 tq/N\le\sum_{j=3}^{t+1}A_j(q)q^{1-j}.
\]
Here \(tq/N\ge1/8\) because \(N/(4t)\ge2\).
The series \(\sum_{j\ge3}8^{1-j}=1/56<1/8\) proves the assertion, and weights below three are impossible for a nontorsion unit.

## Nearly critical gaps and the two ends of a Salem relation

Along any sequence of nontorsion units of log measure \(m\to0\), write \(H=(\log 2)/m\) and put \(\eta=\exp(-\sqrt{\log(1/m)})\).
For each sufficiently small \(m\) there is a weight \(j\to\infty\) and a collection of distinct primitive \(j\)-element patterns numbering at least \(\exp((1-o(1))(j-1)\log(1/m))\).
Each arises from a minimal relation support on which **every** two consecutive exponents are at distance at least \((1-O(\eta))H\), before dividing the exponents by their gcd.
The interval length here may depend on the unit.

Indeed the [upper density bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v570.md#statement) gives \(\lim_N a_N(P,1)/N\le1/H\).
Choose \(N\) sufficiently large that \(a_N(P,1)\le t=\lceil(1+\eta)N/H\rceil\), and that \(t\ge10/\eta\).
Take \(b=\lceil(1+\eta)t\rceil\) blocks of length \(L=\lfloor N/b\rfloor\), and use the first \(w=\lfloor\eta H\rfloor\) positions of each block for independent uniform choices.
Increasing \(N\) if necessary, \(L-w\ge(1-5\eta)H\) for sufficiently small \(m\), since \(1/H=o(\eta)\) and \((1+\eta)^{-2}\ge1-2\eta\).
Let \(A_j\) count the minimal translation classes so separated and of span less than \(N\).
The deletion argument now gives \(\sum_{j=3}^{t+1} A_jw^{1-j}\ge(b-t)w/N\ge\eta^2/2\).
Since \(\sum_{j\ge3}(\eta/4)^{j-1}<\eta^2/2\), one weight has \(A_j\ge(\eta w/4)^{j-1}\).
Here \(\log(\eta w/4)=(1-o(1))\log(1/m)\), which makes \(j\to\infty\) by the [fixed-weight unit-equation estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v290.md#shapes).
The [dilation budget](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v575.md#dilations) loses at most a factor \(2^{j-2}\log(j)/m\) when counting distinct primitive patterns, with logarithm \(o((j-1)\log(1/m))\).

For Salem numbers all the supports obtained this way satisfy a more rigid conclusion.
Write \(r>1\) for the Salem root, so \(\log r=m\) and \(r^{-1}\) is a conjugate.
At each end of every separated support, the first \(K=\lfloor\log(1/\eta)/(3\log2)\rfloor\) gaps all have size \((1+o(1))H\), uniformly over that end segment, and all the next \(K\) coefficients have sign opposite to the endpoint coefficient for every vanishing choice of signs.
In particular the weight is at least \(K+1\), of order \(\sqrt{\log(1/m)}\).

For the upper end divide the signed relation evaluated at \(r\) by its leading term and move that term to the other side.
The resulting sum is \(\sum_{i\ge1} s_i x_i=1\), where \(s_i\) is minus the relative sign, \(x_i\) is the ratio of the \(i\)-th earlier power to the last, and both are extended by zero beyond the support.
Writing \(a=2^{-1+5\eta}\) gives \(0\le x_i\le a^i\).
Consequently every nonnegative deficit \(a^i-s_ix_i\) is at most \(a/(1-a)-1=O(\eta)\).
For \(1\le i\le K\) one has \(a^i\ge2^{-K}\ge\eta^{1/3}\), so \(s_i=1\) and \(x_i/a^i=1+O(\eta^{2/3})\) there.
Taking ratios of consecutive \(x_i\), with \(x_0=1\), proves the gap estimate since \(-\log a=(1-5\eta)\log2\).
Applying the same proof to the reversed relation at \(r\), using vanishing at \(r^{-1}\), gives the opposite endpoint.
The interior exponents and coefficients beyond these two segments remain free in this estimate.
