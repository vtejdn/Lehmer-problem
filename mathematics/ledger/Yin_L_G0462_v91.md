# Uniform Pisot excess near every nonconstant real polynomial with strictly exterior roots

<a id="exterior"></a>
## Real coefficients, moving offsets and an arbitrary integral factor

Fix a nonconstant \(S(w)\in\mathbb R[w]\) with \(S(0)=1\) and all its roots of modulus greater than one.
Fix an integer \(N\ge\deg S\), a circle supremum bound \(M<\infty\), and \(\eta>0\).
There are \(\varepsilon,c>0\), depending only on these data, with the following property.
Let \(S'\) be a real polynomial with constant one and degree at most \(N\), within \(\varepsilon\) of \(S\) in maximum coefficient distance after padding by zeros.
Consider any positive-constant Pisot input with constant \(q\ge2\), exterior root \(\theta\), branch \(j\ge1\), trace degree \(h\) and normalized lift \(\Pi/j\) as in the [varying-branch trace theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
If \(\|\Pi/j\|_\infty\le M\) on the unit circle and its coefficients through \(\lfloor\eta h\rfloor\) agree with
\[
                  G(z) S'(z^g),\qquad
                  1\le g\le h,\qquad G\in1+z\mathbb Z[[z]],
\]
then \(\theta-q\ge c\).
The coefficients of the center may be transcendental.
By a finite cover the same lower bound works for \(S'\) in a neighborhood of any fixed compact set of such centers in degree at most \(N\), with \(N,M,\eta\) fixed.
The [fixed rational theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) also admits roots on the unit circle for its exact rational prefix.

Suppose there is no such neighborhood and bound at one center.
There are then inputs with \(\theta-q\to0\) and polynomials \(S_i\to S\) of degree at most \(N\).
The [inverse estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) give \(h\to\infty\), an integral polynomial \(B^\sharp\) of degree \(2h+2\) with \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tending to one, and \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/j)J_i\).
Here and below \(M(B^\sharp)\) denotes multiplicative Mahler measure.
The Herglotz series \(J_i\) has constant one and coefficients \(j_m=2\int v^m\,d\omega_i(v)\) for \(m>0\), with \(\omega_i\) a real symmetric signed measure of total mass one whose negative mass tends to zero.
The normalized lift is integral below \(g\) within the common prefix, so the [first nondivisible index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) gives \(g=o(h)\).
The formal quotient \(X_i=B^\sharp/G\) has integer coefficients and agrees with \(S_i(z^g)J_i\) throughout the common prefix.
All measure limits below can be taken after extraction and are positive probabilities.

## The first nonintegral real coefficient

Write \(S=\sum s_j w^j\), and let \(l\ge1\) be its first nonintegral coefficient index, with \(\lambda=s_l\).
Such an index exists because the leading coefficient has modulus strictly between zero and one by the product of the root moduli and \(S(0)=1\).
Put \(C(w)=\sum_{j<l}s_j w^j\in1+w\mathbb Z[w]\).
The integral series \(X_i/C(z^g)\) agrees in the prefix with \((S_i/C)(z^g)J_i\).
In \(S_i/C\) the coefficients of orders \(1,\dots,l-1\) tend to zero and the coefficient of order \(l\) tends to \(\lambda\).
The raw moments \(j_m\) are uniformly bounded, so these finitely many earlier shifts make errors \(o(1)\) even at moving Taylor indices.

For \(g\to\infty\) take any positive \(t=o(g)\), and let \(\Omega'\) be a weak limit of the laws of \((x,y)=(v^t,v^{lg})\).
Set \(D(r,k)=2\int x^r y^k\,d\Omega'\), real by simultaneous conjugation symmetry.
Testing at \(rt\) for positive \(r\), and at \(lg+rt\) for every fixed integer \(r\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  D(r,0)&\in\mathbb Z &&(r>0),\\
  D(r,1)&\in\mathbb Z &&(r<0),\\
  D(0,1)+\lambda&\in\mathbb Z,\\
  D(r,1)+\lambda D(r,0)&\in\mathbb Z &&(r>0).
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
The offset \(lg\) contributes the Taylor constant one at \(r=0\), while higher offsets never enter these tests.
At \(0<rt<g\) the integer coefficient is \(j_{rt}\) exactly.

A finite-valued Fourier–Stieltjes transform on a discrete lattice is a finite linear combination of coset indicators.
Indeed each level indicator is a polynomial in the transform by finite interpolation, hence belongs to the Fourier–Stieltjes algebra by convolution and the constant transform.
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) applies to each indicator.
In dimension one such a function has the same periodic part on both tails, with only finitely many exceptions.
The sequence \(D(r,1)\) is Fourier–Stieltjes by weighting the joint measure with \(2y\) and projecting onto \(\bar x\).
It has finite range by (1) because the moments are bounded and \(D(r,0)\) is integral for positive \(r\).
The negative tail of \(D(r,1)\) is integral, so the positive tail is eventually integral too.
Thus \(\lambda D(r,0)\in\mathbb Z\) for all sufficiently large \(r>0\).

The Herglotz transform \(J\) of the first marginal has integer Taylor coefficients.
If that marginal were purely atomic it would have finitely many torsion atoms by the same integer-transform decomposition of twice the marginal, making \(D(r,1)\) periodic even at the origin.
This contradicts (1) at zero and at negative multiples of its period.
The [integral Herglotz classification](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#haar) with a nonzero continuous part gives \(J=1,\ 1+\delta w^u\), or \(1/(1+\delta w^u)\), with \(u\ge1,\ \delta=\pm1\).
The reciprocal binomial has coefficients of modulus one at all positive multiples of \(u\), contrary to eventual integrality of \(\lambda D(r,0)\).

For the binomial \(B(w)=1+\delta w^u\), the doubled first marginal has density \(|B|^2\).
The mixed projection with coefficients \(D(r,1)\) therefore has density \(F\) with \(|F|\le|B|^2\).
By (1), \(H=F+\lambda B\) has integer Fourier coefficients, and they tend to zero at both ends, so \(H\) is an integer Laurent polynomial.
The circle bound forces \(H-\lambda B\) to vanish doubly at all the simple zeros of \(B\).
First \(T=H/B\) is an integer Laurent polynomial by integral division, and then \(T(\zeta)=\lambda\) at every zero of \(B\).
Modulo \(B\), every integer Laurent polynomial has an integer polynomial representative of degree less than \(u\) since \(w^u=-\delta\) there.
Its values at the \(u\) distinct roots force that representative to be the constant \(\lambda\), contradicting \(\lambda\notin\mathbb Z\).
This leaves the Haar marginal \(J=1\).

Consequently \(j_t\) tends to zero and is eventually zero by integrality for every positive \(t=o(g)\).
A violating subsequence would otherwise give such a \(t\), so some \(\kappa\in(0,1)\) gives \(j_m=0\) for all \(1\le m\le\kappa g\) eventually.
For each fixed \(C_0\) this gives a bounded sum of the absolute coefficients of \(X_i\) through \(C_0g\).
Indeed, on a block of diameter \(\kappa g\) the signed character Gram is the identity, and passing to the positive part costs \(o(1)K\) in operator norm on any fixed \(K\) indices.
Split at the at most \(N+1\) offsets and use the bounded partial sum of \(2S_i(v^{-g})\) active on each open piece as multiplier.
The [fixed-size Bessel test](Yin_L_G0462_v59.md#sparse) bounds the number of nonzero integer coefficients, since for fixed sufficiently large \(K\) the \(K\) squared moments would exceed the bounded energy after the \(o(1)\) correction.
The sizes of all coefficients, including those at the offsets, are bounded by the moment estimate.
For bounded \(g\) that size bound alone suffices.

## A nonzero slope would force rational coefficients

Fix a weak limit \(\mu\) on \(w=\bar v^g\).
With the convention \(\widehat\mu(r)=\int w^{-r}\,d\mu\), set \(J_0(0)=1,\ J_0(r)=2\widehat\mu(r)\) for \(r>0\) and \(J_0(r)=0\) for \(r<0\).
Testing \(X_i\) at each fixed \(rg\) gives integer coefficients for the convolution \(B_0=SJ_0\).
In particular \(\mu\) cannot be Haar, which would give \(B_0=S\).
For every sequence \(g/t,t/h\to0\) and every further joint limit \(\Omega\) of \((w,u)=(\bar v^g,v^t)\), let \(\nu_k\) be the projection of \(2S(w)u^k\,d\Omega\) for \(k\ge1\).
Its Fourier coefficients are integers, as limits of the coefficients of \(X_i\) at \(kt+rg\) for each fixed \(r\in\mathbb Z\).
All shifted raw moments in these tests are positive-index moments, and replacing \(S_i\) by \(S\) costs \(o(1)\).
The integers are bounded uniformly in \(r,k\) by \(2\|S\|_\infty\).

The Fourier transform of the positive measure \(2|S|^2\Omega\) has finite range on the whole lattice.
On the zero layer its coefficients are those of \(S^*B_0+SB_0^*\), since \(J_0+J_0^*=2\widehat\mu\), where star is conjugate reversal.
The uniformly bounded integer coefficients of \(B_0\) take finitely many values.
The other layers are obtained by multiplying each \(\nu_k\) by \(S^*\) and by conjugation, so the same finite-range conclusion holds with a single finite set for all layers.
The interpolation argument above decomposes the weighted measure into Haar measures on finitely many torsion translates of connected subtori with trigonometric polynomial densities, just as in the [coset calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer).
The scalar weights of this decomposition may be real or complex.
Positivity gives nonnegative densities once coincident components are combined.
Each \(\nu_k\) itself has integer transform, so its full-dimensional density \(H_k\) on the base is an integer Laurent polynomial, contributed by singleton cosets.
Applying the decomposition also to the zero layer alone shows that the singular part of \(\mu\) consists of finitely many torsion atoms, since \(|S|>0\) on the circle.

Apart from the full two-torus, a component projecting onto the full base lies on \(u^b=\zeta w^a\), with \(b>0,\ a\in\mathbb Z\), and \(\zeta\) torsion.
Divide the component measures by \(2|S|^2\) and group them by slope \(a/b\).
Write \(f\) for the nonnegative projected density of one slope.
On large common multiples \(k\) of all \(b\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)\) the full-torus term contributes zero, while a slope contributes \(w^{ka/b}\,2Sf\).
This uses the finite \(u\)-degree before division by a function of the base alone.
After shifting \(H_k\) by the negative frequency of the chosen slope, the other slopes contribute Fourier coefficients tending to zero by Riemann–Lebesgue, since their shifts diverge and their densities are integrable.
Thus all coefficients of \(H=2Sf\) are integral, and their decay makes \(H\) an integer Laurent polynomial.
This is the [separation of slopes](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) applied to the finite-valued joint transform and the integer-valued row transforms.

Suppose \(f\ne0\).
Its mass is at most one, and \(M(f)=\exp\int\log f<1\) by Jensen, allowing zero for divergent negative integral.
Indeed equality would require \(f=1\) almost everywhere, exhausting the marginal as Haar measure.
Since \(M(S)=1\) by its constant and exterior roots, \(M(H)=2M(f)<2\).
But on the circle \(f\) is real, so as rational functions
\[
              \frac{H(w)}{H^*(w)}
                 =\frac{S(w)}{S^*(w)}
                 =\frac{w^nS(w)}{w^n S(1/w)},\qquad n=\deg S .
\]
The expression on the left lies in \(\mathbb Q(w)\).
The denominator on the right has only interior roots and is nonzero at zero, so it is coprime to \(w^nS\) over \(\mathbb C\).
Uniqueness of a reduced rational expression makes \(w^n S(w)\) a scalar multiple of a rational polynomial.
The coefficient at \(w^n\) equals one, so \(S\in\mathbb Q[w]\).
Moreover \(H/S\) equals its conjugate reciprocal as a rational function.
A pole at a nonzero zero of \(S\) would force a pole at its reciprocal conjugate in the disk, so the quotient is Laurent polynomial.
Division thus gives \(H/S\in\mathbb Q[w,w^{-1}]\).
Let \(m\ge2\) be the least common denominator of \(S\), which is nonintegral by its leading coefficient.
Then \(mS\) is primitive, since a common divisor of its coefficients would also divide its constant \(m\).
Gauss's lemma, after clearing monomials, gives \(H=(mS)T\) with \(T\) a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial.
It follows that \(M(H)\ge M(mS)=m\ge2\), a contradiction.
For \(0<|s_n|\le1/2\), even real divisibility by \(S\) already excludes \(M(H)<2\), since any integer Laurent multiple has measure at least the product \(1/|s_n|\) of the exterior root moduli, counted with multiplicity.
The rational-function argument removes this restriction on \(s_n\).

## The remaining rows and the actual denominator

All nonvertical slopes have vanished.
Only the full-torus term contributes to the absolutely continuous base rows \(H_k\), so these vanish above a finite harmonic.
Write \(f_0\) for the absolutely continuous density of the whole marginal and \(c_k\) for conditional fiber moments on \(f_0>0\), so \(H_k=2S f_0 c_k\).
If \(k\) were the last nonzero positive row, all conditional moments at \(2k,3k,\dots\) would vanish there almost everywhere.
The pushforward by the \(k\)-th power would have only its constant and first harmonics, hence \(|c_k|\le1/2\) by positivity.
As \(M(f_0)<1\) by the same strict Jensen argument, this gives \(1\le M(H_k)\le M(S)M(f_0)<1\).
Every positive row is therefore supported on the fixed torsion atoms of \(\mu\).
Choose \(T\ge1\) divisible by all their orders, taking \(T=1\) for an empty list.
For \(W_i=1-z^{Tg}\) and any intermediate scale as above, the coefficient of \(W_i X_i\) at \(t\) tends to zero, since \(1-w^{\pm T}\) vanishes on the support of \(\nu_1\).
Integrality makes it eventually zero.
If no fixed \(L\) gave vanishing throughout \(Lg\le t\le h/L\) eventually, diagonal selection of violating indices would contradict this for \(g/t,t/h\to0\).
Enlarge \(L\) to have \(1/L<\eta\).

Truncate \(W_iX_i\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to get \(Q_i\) of degree \(O_L(g)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum by the short-interval estimate.
The series \(J_*=Q_i/(W_iS_i(z^g))\) agrees with \(J_i\) through that truncation.
For some \(R>1\) the limit \(S\) has no zero in \(|w|\le R\), and coefficient convergence in degree at most \(N\) gives a uniform lower bound there for \(|S_i|\) eventually.
Consequently \(|[w^r](1/S_i)|\ll R^{-r}\).
For each monomial of \(Q_i\) shifted by \(rg\), the positive coefficients after division by \(W_i\) are one congruence class modulo \(Tg\) with at most \(O_{L,T}(1+r)\) initial exceptions.
A character times uniform measure on the \((Tg)\)-th roots represents the full class, and each exception costs a single monomial density.
Summing their variations against the bound \(R^{-r}\) and the absolute coefficients of \(Q_i\) gives a uniform representing measure for the positive coefficients of \(J_*\), as in the [progression comparison](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#comparison).
The exact identity
\[
 S_i(z^g)W_i B^\sharp-Q_i\Pi/j
       =S_i(z^g)W_i(\Pi/j)(J_i-J_*)
\]
has a polynomial of degree \(O(h)\) on the left, bounded circle multipliers on the right, and a zero prefix of length comparable to \(h\) for the difference of the series with uniformly bounded representing variation.
The [two interval cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) bound the circle \(L^1\) norm of the left side.
Since \(Q_i\Pi/j\) is uniformly bounded and \(|S_i|\) has a uniform positive lower bound on the circle, \(\|W_i B^\sharp\|_1=O(1)\).
The [integer support estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window) bounds its term count, while \(M(W_iB^\sharp)=M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one, contradicting the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return).
This proves the neighborhood assertion for every nonconstant strictly exterior center.

## Actual lifts with bounded reduced lower offsets

Fix an integer \(K\ge1\) and consider actual normalized trace lifts of the form
\[
  \Pi/j=S_i(z^g)-z^{2h+2}S_i(z^{-g}),\qquad
  S_i(w)=1+\sum_{r=1}^K s_{i,r}w^r ,
\]
where \(1\le g\le h\), each \(j s_{i,r}\) is integral, and \(rg\le h\) whenever \(s_{i,r}\ne0\).
One may take \(g\) to be the gcd of the nonzero lower offsets when present.
Along every sequence of such lifts with \(\theta-q\to0\), necessarily \(j\to\infty\).
Their coefficient vectors are bounded, and each subsequential limit \(S\) is nonconstant, has no interior root, and has at least one unit-circle root.
In particular fixed \(K\) and an upper bound on \(j\) give a uniform positive lower bound for the excess.

The lift has all roots on the unit circle by the [interior trace zeros](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) and the endpoint factors.
Hence \(M(\Pi/j)=1\), and there are at most \(2(K+1)\) nonzero coefficients.
The [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) bounds all coefficients and the circle supremum by constants depending only on \(K\).
The unreflected portion is the exact prefix through \(h\).
Consequently \(g=o(h)\) on an escaping sequence by the first nondivisible index theorem, and a subsequential coefficient limit exists.
The [interior coefficient lower bound](Yin_L_G0462_v64.md#dichotomy) applies to the exact normalized interior sum \(2\sum_{r=1}^K|s_{i,r}|\), so every such limit is nonconstant.

If \(S\) had an interior root, choose \(0<r_0<1\) enclosing that root such that \(S\) is nonzero on \(|w|=r_0\).
There \(S_i\to S\) uniformly, so \(S_i\) has an enclosed root and its modulus on the contour is bounded below eventually.
On \(|z|=r_0^{1/g}\), the modulus of the reflected term is at most
\[
      r_0^{(2h+2)/g}\sup_{|w|=1/r_0}|S_i(w)|\longrightarrow0
\]
by \(h/g\to\infty\), bounded coefficients and fixed \(K\).
Rouché's theorem would give \(\Pi/j\) an interior zero, since \(S_i(z^g)\) has one, a contradiction.
If every root of \(S\) were strictly exterior, the neighborhood theorem with \(N=K,\ G=1,\ \eta=1\) would contradict the escaping sequence.
This proves the asserted boundary-root restriction.

Finally, a bounded subsequence of branch indices would allow extraction at a fixed \(j\) and then a single fixed rational \(S_i=S\), since the coefficient bounds leave finitely many possibilities in \(j^{-1}\mathbb Z\).
At least one coefficient of \(S\) must be nonintegral, since otherwise every coefficient of the lift would be divisible by \(j\) before normalization, contrary to the [first nondivisible index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
For this exact rational prefix the [fixed rational theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) applies even with circle roots and contradicts vanishing excess.
This proves \(j\to\infty\) and the bounded-branch gap.
