# Positive Pisot excess for the trapezoid with a double offset difference

<a id="trapezoid"></a>
## Both orders and signs of the primitive trapezoid

There are absolute constants \(c>0\) and \(Q<\infty\) such that an actual positive-constant Pisot trace lift on branch two with lower polynomial
\[
        A_i(z)=2+s z^d+t z^e(1-z^{2d}),\qquad s,t\in\{1,-1\},
        \qquad d,e\ge1,\quad d\ne e,\quad e+2d\le h
\]
satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c\) and \(q\le Q\).
The offsets \(d,e\) can occur in either order.
Write \(S_i=A_i/2\), \(p=\min(d,e)\), \(b=\max(d,e)\), \(D=e+2d\), and \(N=2h+2\).
Suppose along a sequence that \(q\to\infty\), equivalently \(\theta-q\to0\) by the [branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second), and fix both signs by extraction.
The [three-offset and rank conclusions](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) give \(D=o(h)\) and \(b/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\), since \(b\le D\le3b\) and the three offsets have gcd \(\gcd(d,e)\).
The normalized lift agrees with \(S_i\) through \(h\) and has bounded circle supremum.
The [actual denominator representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) gives \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\), where \(J_i\) is the normalized Herglotz series of the symmetric signed inverse measure of mass one and negative mass \(o(1)\).

## A cyclotomic factor regularizes the reciprocal in two variables

Use the signed variables \(X=t z^e,\ Y=s z^d\) and put
\[
       A=2+Y+X(1-Y^2),\qquad S=A/2,\qquad W=1-Y^3 .
\]
The [trapezoid identity](Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#trapezoid) gives \(M(A)=2\) and the zero set \(X=-1,\ Y^2+Y+1=0\) on the two-torus.
For \(0<r<1\) replace \(X\) by \(rX\) to form \(S_r\).
The ratio \((1-Y^2)/(2+Y)\) has modulus at most one on the closed disk by the same identity on the circle and the maximum principle.
Thus \(S_r\) has no zero on the closed bidisk, and \(S\) has no zero in the open bidisk.
For \(|X|=|Y|=1\),
\[
 |W|^2
   = |1-Y|^2\big(|2+Y|^2-|1-Y^2|^2\big)
   \le 20\big(|2+Y|-|1-Y^2|\big)\le40|S_r| .
\]
The same bound holds for \(S\).

The Taylor coefficients of the fixed function \(W^{10}/S\) on the bidisk have finite absolute sum.
Indeed \(G_r=W^{10}/S_r\) is holomorphic through the closed bidisk for each \(r<1\).
Its angular derivatives on the torus through total order two are uniformly bounded as \(r\uparrow1\).
For a derivative of order \(k\le2\), the quotient rule gives terms bounded by a constant times \(|W|^{10-k}|S_r|^{-k-1}\), since the polynomial numerators, denominators and their derivatives are bounded above.
The displayed inequality bounds this by a constant times \(|S_r|^{(10-k)/2-k-1}\) with nonnegative exponent.
Parseval therefore bounds \(\sum_{m,n}(1+m^2+n^2)^2|\widehat G_r(m,n)|^2\) uniformly.
Cauchy–Schwarz and summability of \((1+m^2+n^2)^{-2}\) on \(\mathbb Z^2\) give a uniform absolute Fourier sum.
Each \(G_r\) has only nonnegative powers, and each Taylor coefficient converges to that of \(W^{10}/S\) by expansion at zero.
Fatou's lemma proves the assertion, hence its specialization \(W_i^{10}/S_i\), where \(W_i=1-(s z^d)^3\), has uniformly bounded absolute coefficient sum.

For the unweighted \(1/S_i\) we only need a local bound.
Keep the first signed half in \(P_i=1+\alpha z^p/2\) with \(\alpha=\pm1\).
The other two terms start at \(b\) and have absolute coefficient sum one.
Since \(1/P_i\) has absolute coefficient sum at most two, expansion of \(1/S_i=P_i^{-1}(1+(S_i-P_i)/P_i)^{-1}\) through \(Cb\) uses at most \(\lfloor C\rfloor\) nonconstant-factor powers.
Thus the absolute coefficient sum there is at most \(2\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor C\rfloor}2^k\) for each fixed \(C>0\), in both orders of \(d,e\).

## Intermediate mixed rows and the initial coefficient sums

Use the actual unsigned characters \((\bar v^e,\bar v^d)\) and the polynomial \(A(t x,s y)\) in these coordinates.
Since their two exponents have no bounded nonzero relation along the sequence, pass to a fixed base marginal and lattice order as in the [two-circle argument](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#integer).
Its ordered Herglotz transform \(J\) satisfies the integrality test for \(S(t x,s y)J\), so the marginal cannot be Haar, by the separate coefficients \(s/2,t/2\).
For \(b/T,T/h\to0\), testing at \(kT+me+nd\), for fixed \(k>0\) and fixed integers \(m,n\), gives an integer Fourier transform for the projection of \(A(t x,s y) w^k\,d\Omega\) onto the base of each joint limiting probability, where \(w\) is the extra circle coordinate.
The integer zero layer given by the base test and its conjugate and the mixed-layer tests give an integer transform for \(|A(t x,s y)|^2\Omega\).

The [coset and graph-slope proof](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) consequently gives, for each grouped graph slope with positive absolutely continuous base density \(f\), an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=A(t x,s y)f\).
Division of the positive weighted measure is required only away from the two torus zeros, which project to a null set and give zero to every \(A\)-weighted row.
The coefficients \(2+Y,1-Y^2\) are relatively prime in \(\mathbb Z[Y^{\pm1}]\), so \(A\) is irreducible in the integer Laurent ring by degree one in \(X\) and Gauss's lemma.
Its support is not a translate of its negative, since the two extreme \(X\)-levels have vertical spans one and two.
These facts persist under the two sign changes, so reality of \(f\) forces \(A(t x,s y)\mid H\) by comparison with the conjugate reciprocal.
The resulting nonnegative integer Laurent density, if nonzero, has mean one and accounts for the entire marginal.
Every other Fourier coefficient then has modulus strictly less than one by absolute continuity, so integrality would give the excluded Haar marginal.
This eliminates all graph densities.
The full-torus component has finite harmonic support in the extra coordinate after division by the squared modulus on the base.
At its last nonzero positive row the [conditional positivity bound](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction) would give \(M(H_k)\le M(A)M(f_0)/2<1\) for the nonzero integer Laurent density \(H_k\), where \(f_0\) is the absolutely continuous marginal density and \(M(f_0)<1\) by Jensen and exclusion of Haar.
Thus all positive rows are singular and carried by the fixed proper torsion translates from the weighted base marginal, with the two torsion zeros harmless.
The [annihilating characters](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) give a product \(U_i\) of finitely many cyclotomic binomials of degree \(O(b)\), all nonzero after specialization.
For \(V_i=1-z^a\) with either \(a=2(b-p)\) or \(a=6p\), the extra shift is a fixed base lattice combination after fixing the order of \(d,e\).
Consequently the same mixed-row test and diagonal choice yield a fixed \(L\) with \([z^l]U_i V_iB^\sharp=0\) for \(Lb\le l\le h/L\) eventually.

For comparable offsets \(b/p=O(1)\), choose \(a=2(b-p)\).
The [comparable-offset estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparable) give a bounded absolute coefficient sum of \(V_iB^\sharp\) through \(Cb\) and of \(J_i\) through \(a\).
Indeed the first-index tests at \(p+lu\) for \(u=o(p)\) and fixed \(l\) see the two signed halves at \(p,b\) when active and cannot see \(D\ge 3p\).
The [interval Bessel bounds](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) use bounded partial multipliers on intervals split at every active or translated lift offset, including \(D\) and its reflection.
If \((b-p)/p\) stays bounded below, the local bound holds for the unfiltered denominator as well and transfers to \(J_i\) by the local inverse bound above.
If \(b-p=o(p)\), then \(a<p\) eventually, and the first-index alternatives in the comparable-offset proof give bounded absolute sum directly through \(a\), also for coalescing offsets.

For \(p/b\to0\), take \(a=6p\).
Below \(b\) the sole lower term after the constant is the signed half at \(p\), giving the short \(J_i\) bound through \(6p\) and the [sixth-root filtered Gram bound](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#separated) on blocks of diameter a fixed positive fraction of \(b\).
Its transition intervals have length at most \(6p\), where the unweighted short-prefix Gram bound applies after splitting, or direct counting applies for bounded \(p\).
Splitting at all lift offsets and their translates, with uniformly bounded partial sums, gives bounded absolute coefficient sum of \(V_i B^\sharp\) through \(Cb\).
These two regimes cover every sequence after extraction.

## Comparison after regularization

Within every such initial interval \(V_iJ_i=(V_iB^\sharp)/S_i\), so the local inverse bound gives a bounded absolute coefficient sum for \(V_iJ_i\) through \(Cb\).
The positive coefficients of \(W_i^{10}U_iV_iJ_i\) are moments of a measure of uniformly bounded variation.
In detail, for \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\) one has \(m_{-l}=m_l=[z^l]J_i\) for \(l>0\) and \(m_0=2\).
The nonnegative terms of \(m_l-m_{l-a}\) differ from the coefficients of \(V_iJ_i\) by an absolute sum \(O(1+\sum_{l=1}^a |[z^l]J_i|)\).
At index \(-l<0\) the difference is \(-[z^{l+a}]V_iJ_i\), whose absolute sum through \(Cb\) is bounded.
Thus each shift by \(O(b)\) in \(W_i^{10}U_i\) uses only bounded singleton corrections to the shifted two-sided moments, as in the [denominator comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v95.md#comparison).
There are boundedly many shifts with bounded coefficients.

Truncate \(U_iV_iB^\sharp\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(b)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
By the [regularized inverse](#wiener), \(Q_0W_i^{10}/S_i\) has uniformly bounded absolute coefficient sum, giving a bounded-variation measure for its positive coefficients by monomial densities.
It agrees with \(W_i^{10}U_iV_iJ_i\) through the cutoff.
The polynomial identity
\[
 S_iW_i^{10}U_iV_iB^\sharp-W_i^{10}Q_0\Pi/2
    = S_i(\Pi/2)\big(W_i^{10}U_iV_iJ_i-Q_0W_i^{10}/S_i\big)
\]
has bounded circle multipliers on the right, a difference with zero prefix of positive fractional length and left degree \(O(h)\).
The [two cutoffs](Yin_L_G0462_v51.md#window) bound the circle \(L^1\) norm on the left, hence also that of \(S_iW_i^{10}U_iV_iB^\sharp\).
Since \(|W_i|^2\le40|S_i|\), the integer polynomial \(W_i^{12}U_iV_iB^\sharp\) has bounded circle \(L^1\) norm and [bounded term count](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window).
All added factors have Mahler measure one, while \(M(B^\sharp)>1\) tends to one on the assumed sequence.
This contradicts the [fixed-support gap](Yin_L_G0462_v32.md#boyd-return), proving both bounds.

## All coefficient choices for the exponent relation

Consequently every actual branch-two eight-term lift with lower exponent set \(\{d,e,e+2d\}\) has \(q\) bounded above and \(\theta-q\) bounded below by absolute positive constants, with arbitrary nonzero integer coefficients and either order of \(d,e\).
Indeed on a hypothetical escaping sequence the [rank-two reduction](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) makes the images of \(d,e\) independent after extraction, and the four lower support points form the trapezoid with parallel sides of relative lengths one and two.
The [parallel-side classification](Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#actual) leaves precisely the signed family above and the [family with an integral factor](Yin_L_G0462_v100.md#integral-factor), both excluded.
More generally the limiting four-point support of an escaping eight-term input cannot form any convex quadrilateral with a pair of parallel sides.
The same classification reduces unequal sides to the two families just treated and equal sides to the [sum of two offsets](Yin_L_G0462_v96.md#reciprocal).
