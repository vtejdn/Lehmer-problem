# Arbitrarily slow prime and support growth in the finite integer test

<a id="criterion"></a>
## A degree-by-degree exclusion with two prescribed budgets

On the even integers \(d\ge4\), fix any prime-valued function \(p(d)\to\infty\) and any positive function \(s(d)\to\infty\).
Neither function needs to be monotone.
Put \(p=p(d)\), \(b_d=\min\{p,\lfloor s(d)\rfloor\}\), and fix the plus sign in the [integer row test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#statement).
Use its equations at degree \(d\), width \(B=4d\), and the specified \(p\), without assuming degree preservation in advance.
Require in addition that the total number of nonzero coefficients across its four lists \(a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2\) be at most \(b_d\).

The unrestricted positive Lehmer gap is equivalent to the existence of integers \(k\ge1,d_0\) such that for **every** even \(d\ge d_0\) no pair passing this test on a nontorsion reciprocal unit minimum of degree \(d\) has every coefficient bounded in absolute value by \(2^{\lfloor p(d)/k\rfloor}\).
This equivalence uses the [explicit two-jet construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#statement) on a fixed seed before increasing the degree.
The same equivalence holds with a fixed minus sign and the corresponding signed jet equation.
For each specified triple \(d,p,b_d\) the exclusion is a finite integer decision, even if no procedure to generate the two schedules is specified.

<a id="tower"></a>
## Two sparse rows on a tower with the seed measure unchanged

Fix a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimum \(P_0\), a root \(\alpha\), its degree \(e\ge4\), and its logarithmic Mahler measure \(h>0\).
By the [irreducible tower construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#powers) one can choose an odd prime \(t\) such that every \(P(x)=P_0(x^n)\), \(n=t^j\), is irreducible of degree \(d=ne\) and has measure \(h\).
Let \(\gamma^n=\alpha\).
Along this tower \(p=p(d)\) eventually exceeds both \(t\) and \(e+1\).
Then \(\alpha^p\) has degree \(e\) by the [prime degree argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#bounds).
Indeed \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)]\) divides \(e\) and is coprime to \(p\), so the norm of \(\alpha\) followed by Bézout gives a \(p\)-th root of \(\alpha^p\) in \(\mathbb Q(\alpha^p)\).
Its ratio to \(\alpha\) is a \(p\)-th root of unity in a field of degree \(e<p-1\), so the ratio is one.
Also \(\gcd(p,n)=1\), which gives the unique \(1\le r<n\) satisfying \(pr=1+vn\), with \(1\le v<p\).
This step allows \(p>n\) as well as \(p<n\).

Apply [C407](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#statement) at the seed, prime \(p\), either sign \(\sigma\) and offset \(w=-v\).
For either resulting pair let \(a_0,b_0\) be its integral seed polynomials, \(f_0=Y^{-2e}a_0,\ g_0=Y^{-2e}b_0\), and \(z=\alpha^p\).
Write \((u,\ell_0)=(f_0(z),(\mathcal E f_0)(z))\) with \(\mathcal E=Y\,d/dY\).
At that point \(g_0\) has value \(-\sigma\alpha^v u\) and Euler derivative \(\sigma\alpha^v(\ell_0-vu/p)\).
Define the new polynomial and centered rows by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
       a(Y)&=a_0(Y^n),& b(Y)&=Y^{-r} b_0(Y^n),\\
       f(Y)&=Y^{-2d}a(Y)=f_0(Y^n),&
       g(Y)&=Y^{-2d}b(Y)=Y^{-r}g_0(Y^n).
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Since \(b_0(0)=0\), its new lowest exponent is at least \(n-r>0\), and its highest is at most \(n(4e-1)-r<B\).
The new \(a\) has degree \(B\) and both endpoint coefficients one.
Writing \(b_0^\flat(Y)=Y^{4e-1}b_0(1/Y)\), the reversal required by the test is \(Y^{B-1}b(1/Y)=Y^{n+r-1}b_0^\flat(Y^n)\).
The coprimality of \(a_0,b_0^\flat\) persists after the substitution by a Bézout identity, and \(a(0)=1\) handles the remaining monomial.

At \(\beta=\gamma^p\) one has \(\beta^n=z\) and \(\beta^{-r}\alpha^v=\gamma^{-1}\).
Consequently the lifted jets satisfy
\[
   (f(\beta),(\mathcal E f)(\beta))=(u,n\ell_0),\qquad
   (g(\beta),(\mathcal E g)(\beta))
                    =\sigma\gamma^{-1}(-u,n\ell_0+u/p).               \tag{2}
\]
Indeed differentiating the prefactor \(Y^{-r}\) contributes \(ru\) inside the expression for \((\mathcal E g)(\beta)\), and \(r-nv/p=1/p\).
The seed traces belong to \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\subset\mathbb Q(\gamma+\gamma^{-1})\), since \(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}=C_n(\gamma+\gamma^{-1})\).
Multiplication of the derivative by the positive integer \(n\) preserves the irrationality of each slope and multiplies the nonzero determinant of the two columns by \(n\).
The two seed values are the same nonzero \(u\), so the lifted rows still share that value.
Finally if \(G_0(t)=a_0(t^p)+\sigma t^{4ep+v}b_0(t^{-p})\), then
\[
      a(x^p)+\sigma x^{pB-1}b(x^{-p})=G_0(x^n)
\]
because \(pr-1=vn\).
Thus \(P_0^2\mid G_0\) gives \(P^2\) dividing the lifted graph.

Each coefficient list in (1) has exactly the same nonzero coefficients as its seed list.
The four lifted lists together occupy at most \(2((4e+1)+(4e-1))=16e\) positions, so they meet the ceiling \(b_d\) at every sufficiently high tower level.
Their largest coefficient magnitude \(H\) satisfies
\[
                   \log H\le 3p h+3e\log2+O(\log(2ep)),              \tag{3}
\]
with the absolute constant in [the seed estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v407.md#bounds).
On each fixed seed tower the last two terms are \(o(p)\), uniformly in the offset selected by the level.
No rate of divergence of \(p(d)\) relative to \(\log d\) or to \(d\) is required.

<a id="exclusion"></a>
## Detecting degree preservation and comparing the two height bounds

For any row passing the test on a root \(\gamma\), its nonzero value and the offset-one value equations imply
\(\gamma=-\sigma f(\gamma^p)/g(\gamma^p)\in\mathbb Q(\gamma^p)\).
In this formula only the value equations are used, independently of the derivative's value.
Thus \(Q=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\gamma^p)\) has degree \(d\) and measure \(p\,m(P)\), where \(P=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\gamma)\).
Since \(P\) is reciprocal and occurs twice in the graph, the [reflected elimination](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity) gives
\[
      Q(Y)^2\mid Y^B\big(a(Y)a(1/Y)-b(Y)b(1/Y)\big).
\]
The right side is a nonzero integral polynomial by the two endpoint coefficients of \(a\) and the smaller degree of \(b\).
On the unit circle its modulus is at most \(\max(|a|,|b|)^2\).
If \(H_*\) is the largest coefficient magnitude of this row and \(t_*=\max(|\operatorname{supp}a|,|\operatorname{supp}b|)\), integration gives
\[
           \log H_*\ \ge\ p\,m(P)-\log t_*
                       \ \ge\ p\,m(P)-\log p.                        \tag{4}
\]
Here \(t_*\le b_d\le p\) for every admissible row, however large \(d\) may be.

If there is a uniform positive gap, choose \(k\) with \((\log2)/k\) smaller than the gap.
As \(\log p/p\to0\), (4) excludes the asserted boxes in every sufficiently large even degree.
If the gap fails, [reciprocal reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v403.md#equivalence) gives nontorsion reciprocal unit seeds of arbitrarily small positive measure and unbounded even degree.
For each fixed \(k\) choose just one such seed of degree \(e\ge4\) with \(3h<(\log2)/k\).
Then (3) puts both constructed rows inside the box on every sufficiently high level of its tower, including the floor in the exponent, and the total support is eventually at most \(b_d\).
This contradicts any exclusion at all sufficiently large degrees.

At any one degree, enumerate the finitely many bounded coefficient lists with the stated supports, form the two graphs, and factor the gcd of the first one and its derivative to obtain the possible repeated irreducible inputs of degree \(d\).
Testing reciprocity, the unit and nontorsion conditions, the two multiplicities, the gcds and the prescribed jets with their trace and rank conditions uses exact polynomial and number-field arithmetic as in [C406](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v406.md#integer).
The existence criterion still asks for a single \(k\) working in all sufficiently large degrees.
