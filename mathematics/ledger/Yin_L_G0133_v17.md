<a id="positive-dirichlet-bounded-measure-control"></a>
## Strict positivity and an unbounded central coefficient at bounded measure

For n>=2 define
\[
 D_n(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^{n-1},\qquad
 P_n(z)=D_n(z)^2+z^{n-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The restriction n>=2 is necessary for the stated monic constant-one domain.
Since D_n is reciprocal of degree n-1, P_n is monic reciprocal of degree 2(n-1), has integer coefficients and has constant term one.
Its centered symbol is exactly
\[
 p_n(z)=z^{-(n-1)}P_n(z)
       =D_nD_n^\star+1=|D_n|^2+1>0
       \quad(z\in\mathbb T).
 \tag{2}
\]
The constant Laurent coefficient is
\[
 c_n=n+1\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
This follows directly from the n unit coefficients of D_n.

All roots of D_n are roots of unity, by (z-1)D_n=z^n-1, so m(D_n)=0.
Apply the existing L11 two-row inequality to (2), with nonzero rows D_n and 1, to obtain
\[
 m(P_n)\ge\log2.
 \tag{4}
\]
For z on the circle with z!=1,
\[
 |D_n(z)|=\left|\frac{1-z^n}{1-z}\right|
       \le\frac2{|1-z|}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \log p_n(z)
 \le\log\bigl(|1-z|^2+4\bigr)-2\log|1-z|.
 \tag{5}
\]
The possible logarithmic singularity at z=1 on the right is integrable, and its second term has integral zero by m(1-z)=0.
For lambda=3+2sqrt(2) and rho=lambda^(-1)=3-2sqrt(2), one has
\[
 |1-z|^2+4=6-2\cos\theta
         =\lambda|1-\rho z|^2,\qquad z=e^{i\theta}.
\]
Since 0<rho<1, the last squared factor has logarithmic integral zero.
Integrating (5) therefore gives the complete bounds
\[
 \boxed{\log2\le m(P_n)\le\log(3+2\sqrt2),\qquad c_n=n+1.}
 \tag{6}
\]
Every member has a strictly positive centered symbol and no unit-circle root.

No irreducibility assertion is made for P_n, and none is needed for this control.
It is a positive-measure bounded family, uniformly separated from measure zero, not a sequence approaching the Lehmer boundary.
Thus bounded m(P_n) does not bound c_n, and the expression 2arsinh(c_n) from the retained positive-symbol upper bound is unbounded along this control.
This observation concerns the limitation of that central-coefficient bound, not an assertion that every possible realization of P_n has unbounded expense.
It does not contradict a comparison restricted to a different irreducible or small-measure domain.

## The actual parity split, signs and support

Now let P in Z[z] be monic, irreducible over Q, reciprocal of degree 2d with d>=1, and P(0)=1.
Assume P is not even:
\[
 P(-z)\ne P(z).
 \tag{7}
\]
This is an explicit input condition; no reduction of every original polynomial to this endpoint is proved here.
Positive original measure is required only when discussing the Lehmer return, not for the algebraic formulas or the calibration below.

Choose epsilon in {+1,-1} and put
\[
 p(z)=\varepsilon z^{-d}P(z),\qquad
 a(z)=\frac{p(z)+p(-z)}2,\qquad
 b(z)=\frac{p(z)-p(-z)}2.
 \tag{8}
\]
Reciprocity makes p real on the unit circle and p^star=p.
The two expressions in (8) simply select its even and odd Laurent exponents, respectively.
Thus a,b are integer Laurent polynomials, a^star=a, b^star=b, with supports contained in [-d,d].
Both are nonzero: P has a nonzero even part because its leading and constant coefficients are one, and has a nonzero odd part by (7).

Write the actual integer parity decomposition as
\[
 P(z)=A(z^2)+zB(z^2),\qquad A,B\in\mathbb Z[x].
 \tag{9}
\]
Here A is monic of degree d with A(0)=1, while B!=0 and deg B<=d-1.
The sign and parity dependence in (8) is explicitly
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &a(z)&b(z)\\ \hline
 d\ {\rm even}
   &\varepsilon z^{-d}A(z^2)&\varepsilon z^{1-d}B(z^2)\\
 d\ {\rm odd}
   &\varepsilon z^{1-d}B(z^2)&\varepsilon z^{-d}A(z^2).
 \end{array}
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus epsilon only changes both row signs; all squared constructions below are independent of it.

Define
\[
 Q(z)=z^{2d}(a^2+b^2)
     =\frac{P(z)^2+P(-z)^2}{2}
     =A(z^2)^2+z^2B(z^2)^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
The middle expression has integer coefficients because of the final one.
It has no leading cancellation: A(z^2)^2 is monic of degree 4d, while the other term has degree at most 4d-2.
Its constant coefficient is one, and Q is reciprocal and even.

<a id="degree-preserving-positive-parity-descent"></a>
## Exact descent produces a strictly positive polynomial of the original degree

Since Q is actually even, set
\[
 \boxed{R(x)=A(x)^2+xB(x)^2,\qquad Q(z)=R(z^2).}
 \tag{12}
\]
Then R is monic of degree 2d with integer coefficients and constant term one.
The original reciprocity gives the precise identities
\[
 A(x)=x^dA(x^{-1}),\qquad
 B(x)=x^{d-1}B(x^{-1}).
 \tag{13}
\]
The second identity uses the indicated padded degree d-1 even when some endpoint coefficients of B vanish.
It follows either coefficient by coefficient from P, or by separating the even and odd powers in its reciprocal identity.
Equations (12)--(13) show that R is reciprocal and that its centered symbol is
\[
 \boxed{r_R(x)=x^{-d}R(x)
             =AA^\star+BB^\star.}
 \tag{14}
\]
This is an actual undoubled two-row integer SOS with deg A=d and deg B<=d-1, inside the degree-at-most-d window for the new degree-2d polynomial R.
No support-compression assertion beyond the displayed identities is used.

Strict positivity follows from the original input, not merely from writing a sum of squares.
The polynomials P(z) and P(-z) are both monic of degree 2d.
If they shared a root, irreducibility over Q would force P to divide P(-z), hence force their equality, contradicting (7).
Thus they have no common root.
For z on the unit circle, a(z)^2+b(z)^2 could vanish only if both real values a(z),b(z) vanished, equivalently if p(z)=p(-z)=0.
This would give a common root of P and P(-z).
Consequently
\[
 a(z)^2+b(z)^2>0\quad(z\in\mathbb T),\qquad
 r_R(x)>0\quad(x\in\mathbb T),
 \tag{15}
\]
where the second assertion uses the surjective circle covering x=z^2.
In particular R has no circle root.

Normalized Haar measure under the same covering gives
\[
 \boxed{m(R)=m(Q)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log(a^2+b^2).}
 \tag{16}
\]
The first equality can also be checked by splitting a 4pi integral into its two 2pi periods.
Thus the final polynomial R preserves the original degree 2d; Q's doubled degree is not an unavoidable price.
Irreducibility of R is not proved or assumed.

For comparison, the usual multiplicative parity transform is
\[
 T(x)=A(x)^2-xB(x)^2,\qquad
 T(z^2)=P(z)P(-z),\qquad m(T)=2m(P).
 \tag{17}
\]
That is the actual root-squaring or Graeffe expression, with its full factor of two in total measure.
The plus sign in (12) changes this object and supplies positivity; R is not entitled to (17)'s measure identity.

<a id="parity-positivity-exact-cosh-expense"></a>
## The exact original measure and the unavoidable positivity expense

Set u(z)=|p(z)| and v(z)=|p(-z)|.
Away from the finite set of their zeros, (8) gives
\[
 a^2+b^2=\frac{u^2+v^2}{2},\qquad
 \log\frac{u^2+v^2}{2}
 =\log u+\log v+
       \log\cosh(\log u-\log v).
 \tag{18}
\]
Define the full expense
\[
 {\cal E}(P)=
 \int_{\mathbb T}
    \log\cosh\bigl(\log|p(z)|-\log|p(-z)|\bigr)\,d\mu.
 \tag{19}
\]
This integral is finite.
Indeed both logarithms are integrable polynomial logarithms, and 0<=log cosh(t)<=|t| bounds the displayed integrand by their integrable absolute values.
The finitely many points where a logarithm is undefined do not change the integral.
No radial smoothing, tail removal or omission of a circle root is involved.

Rotation by -1 preserves Haar measure, and both integrals of log u and log v are m(P).
Combining (16)--(19) therefore gives the exact original-input identity
\[
 \boxed{m(R)=2m(P)+{\cal E}(P).}
 \tag{20}
\]
The expense is nonnegative.
It is in fact positive under (7): if it vanished, equality u=v would hold almost everywhere, hence p(z)^2=p(-z)^2 as Laurent polynomials.
The integral domain property would force p(z)=p(-z) or p(z)=-p(-z), making one of a,b zero, contrary to (10).

Apply the already proved L11 two-row inequality to (14).
There are two actual nonzero integer polynomial rows, so
\[
 \boxed{m(R)\ge\log2+m(A)+m(B)\ge\log2.}
 \tag{21}
\]
Equivalently, its arbitrary-Laurent-width version applied before descent gives log2+m(a)+m(b).
Equation (10), monomial invariance and Haar covering identify that latter sum with m(A)+m(B), with the two labels interchanged when d is odd.
Thus both the support and the full row measures agree through the descent.

In particular,
\[
 \boxed{{\cal E}(P)\ge
   \log2+m(A)+m(B)-2m(P)\ge\log2-2m(P).}
 \tag{22}
\]
Together with nonnegativity, the final lower bound can be written max(0,log2-2m(P)).
On any hypothetical sequence in this actual domain with positive m(P) tending to zero, this positivity expense has liminf at least log2.
This is a conditional diagnosis of the transformation, not the construction of such a sequence or a proof that one exists.

## Calibration and the original-input boundary

For the actual irreducible non-even cyclotomic input
\[
 P(z)=\Phi_3(z)=z^2+z+1,
\]
one has d=1, A(x)=x+1, B(x)=1 and
\[
 R(x)=x^2+3x+1,\qquad
 x^{-1}R=x+x^{-1}+3>0\quad(x\in\mathbb T).
 \tag{23}
\]
The input has m(P)=0, while its output has
\[
 m(R)=\log\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}>0
\]
by its two explicit reciprocal roots.
This is a zero-to-positive control for the unconditional transformation, not a positive original sequence approaching measure zero.

The construction supplies a real map from every stated non-even irreducible original input to the strictly positive symbol domain, even when the original P has circle roots.
It does so with an explicit integer SOS, the same polynomial degree, and the exact nonzero expense (19).
Neither positivity nor degree preservation makes that expense a multiple of the original small m(P).
The uniform lower bound in (21) belongs to the new R; it cannot be returned as a lower bound for P through (20), whose extra term is positive and not bounded from above by the original measure here.

The even case is deliberately excluded: then B=0, the two-row inequality no longer applies and coprimality of P with P(-z) fails.
No irreducibility claim for R, certification of a positive near-zero original family, all-input cheap-return theorem or unrestricted G0001 conclusion is asserted.
