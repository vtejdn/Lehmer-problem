# The first coefficient departure from the Pisot function at two

<a id="departure"></a>
## The Taylor coefficients after the geometric prefix

Let \(f_i\) be monic minimal polynomials of Pisot units, put \(s_i=f_i(0)\in\{-1,1\}\), \(A_i=s_i f_i,\ Q_i=f_i^*,\ F_i=A_i/Q_i\), and suppose \(F_i\) converges meromorphically on compact subsets of the open disk to \(F_0=1/(1-2z)\).
Here \(f^*(z)=z^{\deg f}f(1/z)\).
This is the topology needed from the [compact Pisot class](../audits/literature/BM96/Yin_U001.md#bm96-u001).
Convergence near zero and integrality give an increasingly long prefix with \([z^j]F_i=2^j\).
Each \(F_i\) has circle modulus one, while \(F_0\) has this modulus only at one, so there is a first discrepant index \(n_i\to\infty\).
Define
\[
 \delta_i=[z^{n_i}](F_i-F_0),\qquad
 V_i(z)=\frac{(1-2z)F_i(z)-1}{z^{n_i}\big((z-2)-zF_i(z)\big)}.
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(V_i\) converges on compact subsets of the disk to \(R/2\), where
\[
 R\in\{\,2,-2,1,-1\,\}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 R=\epsilon+\eta z^k,\qquad k\ge1,\quad \epsilon,\eta\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
On that subsequence \(\delta_i=-R(0)\) eventually.
If the associated delay-two polynomials have Mahler measures strictly greater than one tending to one, only the values \(\delta_i=\pm1\) survive.
The proofs also give the exact mod-two condition on \(f_i\) below.
Boyd already identified \(F_0\) as a second derived point in his [discussion of the delay-two search](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#fixed-delay-enumeration).
The limits in (1) concern the coefficients at and beyond the moving first discrepancy.

<a id="schur"></a>
## The circle bound after removal of the common prefix

Suppress \(i\) and write \(n=n_i\).
On the circle \(|(1-2z)F-1|=|(z-2)-zF|\), by \(|F|=1\) and conjugation.
The polynomial \((z-2)Q-tzA\) has exactly one disk zero for \(0\le t<1\), by Rouché's theorem since \(|z-2|\ge1\) on the circle and \(Q\) has just one disk zero.
Here and below a finite initial segment of the sequence can be discarded: a reciprocal seed would give a constant \(F\) and cannot match the linear coefficient of \(F_0\).
Letting \(t\uparrow1\) leaves at most one disk zero counted with multiplicity.
After cancellation \(V\) thus has circle modulus one, no circle poles and at most one disk pole, since the factor \(z^n\) removes a zero of exact order \(n\).
Any such pole escapes compact subsets of the disk: \(1/F_i\to1-2z\) holomorphically there and \((z-2)/F_i-z\to-2(1-z)^2\) has no disk zero.
Multiplying \(V_i\) by the Blaschke factor of its possible pole gives a Schur function, by the maximum modulus principle.
Normalize the factor to be positive at zero.
It tends to one on compact subsets as the pole tends to the circle, and the factor is taken to be one when there is no pole.
Montel's theorem now gives a subsequential holomorphic limit \(G\) of \(V_i\) with \(|G|\le1\).

Put \(T_i=((1-2z)F_i-1)/z^{n_i}\in\mathbb Z[[z]]\).
The identity
\[
 (1-2z)((z-2)-zF_i)=-2(1-z)^2-z^{n_i+1}T_i
\]
shows that the coefficients through degree \(n_i\) of \(2(1-z)^2 V_i\) agree with those of \(-(1-2z)T_i\).
Consequently \(2(1-z)^2G\) has integer coefficients.
It belongs to \(H^\infty\), so its Taylor coefficients are square summable and only finitely many are nonzero.
The resulting polynomial has a double zero at one since its modulus is at most \(2|1-z|^2\) in the disk.
Division by the monic polynomial \((1-z)^2\) gives \(R=2G\in\mathbb Z[z]\) with \(\|R\|_\infty\le2\).
Its constant term is eventually \(-\delta_i\), since \(2V_i(0)=-\delta_i\) is a nonzero integer.
The [sharp norm bound for three or more terms](Yin_L_G0462_v98.md#norm) leaves at most two terms.
For two terms the sup norm is the sum of the absolute coefficients, giving the list (1).

<a id="extreme"></a>
## Unique completions at discrepancies of size two

The [Boyd interval formula](../audits/literature/Boy78/Yin_U001.md#narrow-branches) gives width \(w(F_0)=2\).
Indeed \(|1-2z|^2-1=2|1-z|^2\) on the circle, and \(M(1-2z)=2,\ M(1-z)=1\).
Write \([a_n,b_n]\) for the next coefficient interval along \(F_0\), so \(2^n-a_n\to2\) and \(b_n-2^n\to2\).
After an admissible choice \(u=2^n+\delta\) its next interval has length \(4(b_n-u)(u-a_n)/(b_n-a_n)\), with a terminal choice giving a unique possible continuation.
For \(\delta=2\) or \(-2\) this length tends to zero.
The subsequent intervals have nonincreasing length, by the same formula and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.
Thus for large \(n\) an admissible extreme prefix has at most one integral continuation in the Pisot class.

For each sign \(t\), the monic trinomial \(p_{n,t}(z)=z^n(z-2)+t\) supplies such a continuation.
For large \(n\) it has a root above \(3/2\), since \(p_{n,t}(3/2)<0\), and at most one exterior root counted with multiplicity, by Rouché applied to \(z^n(z-2)+rt\) for \(0\le r<1\) and then continuity.
Its only possible circle root is \(1\), since \(|z-2|=1\) on the circle only there.
The exterior root is therefore Pisot, and the remaining monic cofactor is cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Since a monic cyclotomic product \(C\) satisfies \(C^*=C(0)C\), the normalized quotient of the Pisot factor is
\[
 t\,p_{n,t}/p_{n,t}^*
   =\frac{1-2t z^n+t z^{n+1}}{1-2z+t z^{n+1}}.
\]
It first departs at \(n\) with \(\delta=-2t\), and uniqueness identifies the actual seed via its unique disk pole.
The trinomial is a cyclotomic multiple of that seed, so the [bounded-term gap argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v27.md#compact) separates its positive Mahler excess from zero at every delay and either sign.

<a id="parity"></a>
## A reciprocal polynomial modulo two

For a surviving \(|\delta_i|=1\), write \(d=\deg f_i,\ n=n_i,\ s=s_i\) and
\[
 U=z^{-n}\big((1-2z)A_i-Q_i\big)\in\mathbb Z[z].
\]
It has degree \(D=d+1-n\), leading coefficient \(-2s\) and \(U(0)=\delta_i\).
Reversing at degree \(d+1\) before removing the initial power of \(z\) gives \(s U^*=(z-2)Q_i-zA_i\), and hence the exact identity
\[
                 s U^*+z^{n+1}U=-2(Q_i+z^2 A_i).                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular the reduction \(\bar U\) modulo two is reciprocal of exact degree \(d-2n\), so \(d\ge2n\).
Indeed \(\bar U(0)=1\), and (2) says \(z^D\bar U(1/z)=z^{n+1}\bar U(z)\).
The least exponent on the left is \(D-\deg\bar U\), giving the degree and then reciprocity.

If \(M(Q_i+z^2 A_i)>1\), the function \(V_i\) must have a disk pole.
In fact \(Q_i+z^2 A_i\) is reciprocal up to sign and has leading and constant coefficients of modulus one, so it has a disk zero.
If \(U,U^*\) have a common disk zero \(\zeta\), their defining equations imply \((1-\zeta)^2 A_i(\zeta)=(1-\zeta)^2Q_i(\zeta)=0\), impossible for a nonreciprocal irreducible seed.
Thus (2) gives \(1+z^{n+1}V_i=0\) at a disk zero of the delay polynomial, whereas a pole-free \(V_i\) would be Schur.
Its only disk pole is real because nonreal poles come in conjugate pairs.
In fact it lies in \((0,1)\).
With \(a=1/\theta_i\) and \(b_a(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)\), the function \(b_a F_i\) is Schur.
For \(-1<x<0\) we have \(b_a(x)>|x|\), hence \(|xF_i(x)|<1<|x-2|\); zero is also excluded by the constant denominator.
Writing the pole as \(1/\beta_i\), we therefore have \(\beta_i>1\) and \(\beta_i\to1\) by the compact convergence in the [circle bound](#schur).

The reciprocal correspondence makes \(\beta_i\) the unique exterior zero of \(U\), counted with multiplicity.
Its only possible circle zero is \(1\), by the equality \(|1-2z|=1\) needed for \((1-2z)A_i=Q_i\).
The multiplicity there is at most two: the determinant of the two linear combinations of \(A_i,Q_i\) defining \(z^n U,sU^*\) is \(-2(1-z)^2\), up to sign, and \(A_i,Q_i\) are coprime.
For large \(i\) the primitive irreducible factor of \(U\) containing \(\beta_i\) must have leading coefficient of absolute value two.
If it were monic up to sign, \(\beta_i\) would be Pisot, but the set of Pisot numbers is closed in \(\mathbb R\) and omits one, as recalled in the [source on the Pisot class](../audits/literature/BM96/Yin_U001.md#bm96-u001).
Since \(U(0)=\pm1\) and its leading coefficient is \(\pm2\), every remaining irreducible factor is monic up to sign and has all roots in the closed disk.
Kronecker's theorem and the circle-zero test make the remaining factor a power of \(z-1\) of exponent at most two, up to sign.
The reduction to \(\beta_i\) concerns the signed delay polynomial in (2).
The first-departure classification supplies necessary conditions for near-one sequences and leaves the possible constant or binomial limits with \(|R(0)|=1\) open.
