# Three sublinear offsets are necessary for sparse trace lifts on branch two

<a id="three-offsets"></a>
## The first three lower exponents

Let a sequence of positive-constant Pisot inputs on branch \(j=2\) have constants \(q\to\infty\) and trace lifts \(\Pi\) with a bounded number of nonzero terms.
Write \(h\) for the trace degree, \(N=2h+2\), and \(0<d_1<\cdots<d_r<N/2\) for the positive exponents below the midpoint.
Then eventually \(r\ge3\) and \(d_3/h\to0\).
For exactly eight terms, the three lower exponents admit a bounded nonzero integer relation, whereas \(d_3/\gcd(d_1,d_2,d_3)\to\infty\).
After extraction their persistent relations therefore form a lattice of rank exactly one.
In this statement a relation is persistent if the same integer coefficients give an exact equality at all sufficiently large indices of the subsequence.

The [six-term gap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#statement) proves the first assertion about \(r\), since [the branch identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) makes \(q\to\infty\) equivalent to \(\theta-q\to0\) on branch two.
By the [first two offsets theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v61.md#four), \(d=d_1,\ e=d_2\) are \(o(h)\).
If the new conclusion fails, extract \(d_3\ge\eta h\) with \(\eta>0\).
Fix \(r\) and the coefficients \(a_s=[z^{d_s}]\Pi\) by extraction using the [sparse height estimate](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound), since every root of the lift has modulus one and its leading coefficient has modulus two.
This also gives a uniform bound for \(\|\Pi/2\|_\infty\).
Write \(a=a_1,\ b=a_2\).
At least one of \(a,b\) is odd by the [first nondivisible index](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement).
The normalized lift agrees through \(\lfloor\eta h/2\rfloor\) with \(S_i=1+(a/2)z^d+(b/2)z^e\).

## Mahler measure of the two early terms and the constant

Write \(\mathbf n_i=(d_1,\ldots,d_r,N)\), and extract so that all \(d_s/N\) converge and each fixed integral relation on \(\mathbf n_i\) either holds eventually or fails eventually.
The persistent relations form a saturated subgroup \(R\) of \(\mathbb Z^{r+1}\).
The quotient \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^{r+1}/R\) is free abelian, and evaluation on \(\mathbf n_i\) gives maps \(\lambda_i:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z\) eventually because \(R\) has a finite basis.
The shortest nonzero relation on the evaluations of a fixed basis of \(\Gamma\) tends to infinity, with infinity allowed for an empty set of relations.
Indeed a bounded sequence of relations gives a fixed one on a subsequence, which by the extraction already belongs to \(R\).
Let \(F\in\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\) be the Laurent polynomial obtained by projecting the exponents of the full lift, including the reflections.
Its one-variable specializations are exactly \(\Pi_i\), so [Lawton's theorem](../audits/literature/Den09/Yin_U002.md#den09-u002) gives \(m(F)=\log 2\), with \(m\) the logarithmic Mahler measure.

The limits of \(\mathbf n_i/N\) define a real linear functional on \(\Gamma\).
It vanishes on the two early exponents and is strictly positive on all remaining nonconstant terms, including all reflections.
Write \(v_1,v_2\) for the classes of the first two exponents in \(\Gamma\).
There is an integral linear functional vanishing on \(v_1,v_2\) and positive on the same remaining exponents, by rational approximation in the rational subspace defined by these two equalities and clearing denominators.
Consequently the constant term in the new variable \(t\) after multiplying each monomial of \(F\) by \(t\) to the power of its functional value is \(A=2+aX^{v_1}+bX^{v_2}\).
For each \(t\) on the unit circle the change in the base torus is a Haar-preserving translation.
Jensen's inequality in \(t\), followed by integration over the base, gives \(m(A)\le m(F)=\log 2\).

If \(e/\gcd(d,e)\) stays bounded, fix the primitive pair \((m,n)\) after extraction.
The polynomial \(A\) then has the same measure as \(P(w)=2+a w^m+b w^n\), because its one nonzero base character ranges over the whole circle.
Its constant term and Jensen's formula give \(m(P)\ge\log 2\), with equality only if there is no root in the open disk.
The rational polynomial \(P/2\) has a nonintegral coefficient and gives the exact prefix above at scale \(\gcd(d,e)\).
The [rational prefix theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v21.md#statement) contradicts \(\theta-q\to0\).

Otherwise extract \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
Then \(v_1,v_2\) are independent, so their pair of characters pushes Haar probability onto Haar probability on the full two-torus.
By simultaneous phase rotation and Jensen's formula,
\[
 m(A)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\log\max(2,|a x+b y|)\,dx\,dy .
\]
Here and below circle integrals use Haar probability.
Equality with \(\log 2\) forces \(|a|+|b|\le2\), since a strict excess is attained on an open set if the maximum exceeds two.
Thus \(a,b=\pm1\).

## Applying the two-offset argument to a long initial interval

In this independent case the [mixed-row proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#rows) applies to \(A(x,y)=2+a x+b y\) using the actual denominator \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\), where \(J_i\) is the Herglotz series of the signed inverse measure.
We verify where the length of the two-term prefix enters.
The tests at \(u d+v e\) and \(k t+u d+v e\) for fixed integers \(u,v,k\) and \(e\ll t\ll h\) use only coefficients at \(o(h)\).
Thus every required coefficient used in the Fourier decomposition is unchanged by the later terms, and the same singular components give the [cyclotomic product](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#period) \(U_i\) of degree \(O(e)\).
With \(W=1-z^{2(e-d)}\) the integer coefficients of \(U_i W B^\sharp\) vanish on \(Le\le t\le h/L\) for a fixed sufficiently large \(L\), which may be increased to satisfy \(1/L<\eta/2\).

Only the initial interval of length \(C e\) is needed from the [local support proof](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#support) for each fixed \(C\).
The tests for coalescing offsets use \(d+lu\) with \(u=o(d)\) and fixed \(l\), and the separated-offset tests use \(e+ld\) or \(k t+ld\) for \(d\ll t\ll e\).
All lie inside the prescribed prefix whenever positive.
For the [block Bessel estimates](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) in this initial interval, the only active coefficients of the normalized numerator are its constant and the two lower coefficients.
The same Gram matrices and the same bounded partial multipliers therefore give bounded absolute sums for \(W B^\sharp\) through \(Ce\) and \(J_i\) through \(2(e-d)\).
In the case \(e/d\to\infty\) this uses the [atom-free first marginal](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#hierarchy), whose second-offset congruences are all tested at \(e+ld\).

Truncate \(U_i W B^\sharp\) at \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) to obtain \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(e)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
Through this cutoff \(Q_0/S_i\) agrees with \(U_iWJ_i\).
The [bounded-variation comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#return) uses only the initial \(O(e)\) coefficient sums for its shifts and the explicit inverse of \(S_i\).
Its global polynomial identity remains
\[
 S_i U_i W B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2=S_i(\Pi/2)(U_iWJ_i-Q_0/S_i).
\]
The whole numerator enters only via its bounded circle supremum and degree \(N=O(h)\).
The two cutoffs bound the circle \(L^1\) norm of \(S_i U_i W B^\sharp\), and \(|W|^2\le32|S_i|\) then bounds that of \(U_i W^3 B^\sharp\).
This integer polynomial has bounded support and the same Mahler measure as \(B^\sharp\), which approaches one strictly from above.
The [bounded-support gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes the sequence.
This proves \(d_3=o(h)\) without any bound on the size of the fixed support other than finiteness.

## The remaining eight-term exponent patterns

For exactly eight terms \(r=3\).
The [bounded-relation sieve](Yin_L_G0462_v92.md#relation) supplies a nonzero bounded integer relation on \(d_1,d_2,d_3,N\).
Since all three \(d_s=o(N)\), the coefficient of \(N\) vanishes eventually.
After extraction a fixed nonzero relation holds on the three offsets.
Two independent persistent relations would put their triples on a fixed rational line, making \(d_3/\gcd(d_1,d_2,d_3)\) bounded.
The [bounded reduced degree result](Yin_L_G0462_v91.md#bounded-offsets) also excludes a bounded subsequence of that ratio directly on the fixed branch.
Thus the ratio tends to infinity, and the one-relation patterns are the remaining eight-term candidates.

There are only finitely many resulting two-variable Laurent polynomials up to a monomial change of basis.
Indeed the primitive generator \(\rho\) of the persistent relation lattice divides a bounded nonzero relation, so it lies in a fixed finite set.
On \(\Gamma_0=\mathbb Z^3/\mathbb Z\rho\), the three exponents of \(A=2+\sum_{s=1}^3 a_s X^{v_s}\) are then fixed up to basis, and the integer coefficients belong to a fixed finite set by the height bound.
The limiting lattice including \(N\) is exactly \(\Gamma_0\oplus\mathbb Z\), since every persistent relation has zero coefficient at \(N\).
Consequently Lawton's theorem gives \(m(A-tA^*)=\log 2\) on the three-torus, where \(A^*(X)=A(X^{-1})\).
Integration in the last variable gives \(m(A)=\log 2\), since \(A,A^*\) have equal modulus on the base torus.
This finite reduction concerns the possible Laurent polynomials; the integer specializations of each surviving polynomial still range over unbounded exponents.
