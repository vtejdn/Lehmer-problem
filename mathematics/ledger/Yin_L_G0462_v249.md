# Equal edge coefficients at a quartic Salem seed and the two endpoint factors at width five

<a id="signs"></a>
## Each sign has only finitely many equal-edge exponents

Fix a quartic Salem minimum \(P\) with roots \(\rho,\rho^{-1},\eta,\eta^{-1}\), where \(\rho>1\) and \(|\eta|=1\).
For a row of degree at most \(w\), write \(A_w^*(Y)=Y^wA(1/Y)\).
For each fixed \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), only finitely many positive integers \(p\) admit a nonzero rational row \(A=\sum_{i=0}^4 a_iY^i\) with
\[
                 P(x)^2\mid A(x^p)+\varepsilon x A_4^*(x^p),
                 \qquad a_4=a_0 .
\]

Put \(x_1=\rho,\ x_2=\eta,\ t_i=x_i+x_i^{-1},\ d_i=x_i-x_i^{-1}\), and set
\[
 S_i=x_i^p+x_i^{-p},\quad \delta_i=x_i^p-x_i^{-p},\quad
 H_i=(t_i-2\varepsilon)/d_i,\quad K_i=2\varepsilon/(t_i+2\varepsilon).
\]
The conjugate \(\eta\) has infinite order, since otherwise irreducibility would make \(\rho\) a root of unity.
In particular \(\delta_1\delta_2\ne0\), and \(S_1>2>|S_2|\).
For \(H_\varepsilon(t)=(t-2\varepsilon)/(x-x^{-1})\) on the local branch through \(x_i\), one has \(d_iH_\varepsilon'(t_i)=K_i\) by differentiating \((x-x^{-1})^2=t^2-4\).
The [Hermite secant identity for equal edges](Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#edges) therefore requires
\[
 (S_1-S_2)\left(H_1S_1/\delta_1+H_2S_2/\delta_2+(K_1+K_2)/p\right)
             =2(H_1\delta_1-H_2\delta_2).                              \tag{1}
\]
Multiplication by \(p\delta_1\delta_2\) gives the equivalent zero equation \(E_\varepsilon(p)=0\), where
\[
\begin{split}
 E_\varepsilon(p)={}&(S_1-S_2)\big[pH_1S_1\delta_2+pH_2S_2\delta_1
                          +(K_1+K_2)\delta_1\delta_2\big]\\
                   &-2p\delta_1\delta_2(H_1\delta_1-H_2\delta_2).
\end{split}
\]
Each factor \(S_i,\delta_i\) is a sum or difference of two powers with fixed nonzero bases in the splitting field of \(P\).
Expansion thus expresses \(E_\varepsilon(p)\) as a finite sum of terms \((u_\nu p+v_\nu)(\rho^{i_\nu}\eta^{j_\nu})^p\), with fixed algebraic coefficients in that field and integral \(i_\nu,j_\nu\).
If it has infinitely many positive zero indices, [Lech's exponential-polynomial lemma](../audits/literature/Lec53/Yin_U001.md#zero-set) gives an entire infinite progression of positive zero indices.
At every such index (1) implies \(H_2S_2/\delta_2=H_1+O_P(1/p)\), since \(S_1/\delta_1=1+O_P(\rho^{-2p})\), \(|S_2|,|\delta_2|\le2\), and \((H_1\delta_1-H_2\delta_2)/(S_1-S_2)=H_1+O_P(\rho^{-p})\).
On every tail of the progression, \(\eta^{2p}\) is dense on the unit circle because the step is a positive integer and \(\eta\) has infinite order.
Taking a subsequence with \(\eta^{2p}\to-1\) gives \(S_2/\delta_2=(\eta^{2p}+1)/(\eta^{2p}-1)\to0\), contradicting \(H_1\ne0\).
This proves finiteness separately for both signs.

<a id="primes"></a>
## All odd-prime exceptions for the two seeds in the coefficient box

Let \(T(Z)=(Z-t_1)(Z-t_2)\in\mathbb Z[Z]\) and \(j=t_1+t_2\).
For a prime exponent \(p\nmid 2\operatorname{disc}(T)\,T(2)T(-2)\), fix a prime of the splitting field above \(p\).
There \(t_1-t_2,d_i,t_i+2\varepsilon\) are units, since \((t_1-t_2)^2=\operatorname{disc}(T)\), \(d_i^2=t_i^2-4\), and the products of the two conjugate endpoint differences are \(T(\pm2)\).
Reduction gives \(S_i\equiv t_i^p,\ \delta_i\equiv d_i^p\), so \((S_1-S_2)\delta_1\delta_2\) is a unit there.
Reducing \(E_\varepsilon(p)=0\) therefore forces the numerator of
\[
               K_1+K_2=\frac{2\varepsilon(j+4\varepsilon)}{T(-2\varepsilon)}
\]
to vanish modulo \(p\).
When \(j+4\varepsilon\ne0\), every prime with an equal-edge row divides the explicit nonzero integer \(2\operatorname{disc}(T)\,T(2)T(-2)(j+4\varepsilon)\).
The progression proof also covers \(j+4\varepsilon=0\).

For the [two seeds in the coefficient box](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short), \(T_A=Z^2-Z-3\) and \(T_B=Z^2-2Z-1\).
Taking the union over the signs leaves only \(3,5,13\) for \(A\) and \(3,7\) for \(B\) among odd primes.
The following entries are \(a_4-a_0\) for the primitive generators in the orientation of the [exact row kernel](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/minimum.py).

| Seed | \(p\) | Plus | Minus |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(A\) | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| \(A\) | 5 | 0 | 80 |
| \(A\) | 13 | 3835 | 12415 |
| \(B\) | 3 | 0 | 48 |
| \(B\) | 7 | 28826 | 2842 |

To reproduce each entry, use the named seed in the program and take the two kernels returned as the second component of `row_system(seed,p,4)`.
For each sign its five columns are the coefficient vectors of \(x^{pi}+\varepsilon x^{p(4-i)+1}\bmod P^2\), in the basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^7\), for \(0\le i\le4\).
Each of the ten kernels has rank one, so testing the last minus first coordinate of the returned primitive generator decides the entire rational line. [Kernel method](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0177/readme.md#method)
Thus the only odd-prime equal-edge cases are the plus line at \(p=5\) for seed \(A\) and the plus line at \(p=3\) for seed \(B\).

<a id="cost"></a>
## A lower bound for every nonzero degree-four pair

Take integral rows \(U,V\) of degrees at most four, at least one nonzero, such that \(P(x)^2\mid U(x^p)+xV(x^p)\).
At least one of \(A_\varepsilon=U+\varepsilon V_4^*\) is nonzero, and each satisfies the signed double divisibility by adding \(\varepsilon\) times the reversal of the original graph about \(4p+1\).
For all sufficiently large positive integers \(p\), each nonzero component has unequal edges by the [single-sign finiteness](#signs).
The [collision divisor](Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#collisions) gives \(\log|a_4-a_0|\ge p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) for such a component.
The [exterior value and derivative](Yin_L_G0462_v246.md#edges) give \(|a_4-a_0|\ll_P p\|A_\varepsilon\|_1/\rho^p\).
Writing \(H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\), one has \(\|A_\varepsilon\|_1\le2H\), whence
\[
                         \log H\ge 2p\log\rho-o_P(p).
\]
This estimate is uniform over all nonzero row pairs, including proportional reflected rows and pairs with common factors.

<a id="endpoints"></a>
## Excluding a root of the width-five quotient at either endpoint

Now let \(U,V\) have degree at most five with the same double divisibility, and write \(D(Y)=U(Y)U(1/Y)-V(Y)V(1/Y)\).
Suppose \(D\ne0\) and \(D(\xi)=0\) for \(\xi=1\) or \(-1\).
Both \(A_\varepsilon=U+\varepsilon V_5^*\) are nonzero, as \(U=\pm V_5^*\) would imply \(D=0\).
Their product at \(\xi\) is \(U(\xi)^2-V(\xi)^2=D(\xi)\), so at least one sign has \(A_\varepsilon=(Y-\xi)C\) for a nonzero integral \(C\) of degree at most four.
Formal reversal gives \((A_\varepsilon)_5^*=-\xi(Y-\xi)C_4^*\).
Reversing the original graph about \(5p+1\) again gives the double divisibility for its signed component.
The resulting factor \(x^p-\xi\) has only torsion roots and is coprime to \(P\), so
\[
                    P(x)^2\mid C(x^p)-\varepsilon\xi x C_4^*(x^p).
\]
Synthetic division bounds each of the at most five coefficients of \(C\) by \(\|A_\varepsilon\|_1\), hence \(\|C\|_1\le10H\).
Applying the [degree-four bound](#cost) to \(C,-\varepsilon\xi C_4^*\) yields \(\log H\ge2p\log\rho-o_P(p)\) whenever either endpoint vanishes.

At the cutoff \(H\le2^p\) and all sufficiently large odd primes, [C410](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v410.md#statement) reduces the genuine width-five candidates to the two positive seeds above up to change of sign, both with \(\rho^2>2\).
For either positive seed the [integral reciprocal quotient](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v410.md#quadratic) has \(Y^5D=Q^2(cY^2+dY+c)\), where \(Q\) is the minimum of \(\rho^p\) and \(Q(\pm1)\ne0\).
The preceding estimate excludes \(d=-2c\) at \(1\) and \(d=2c\) at \(-1\).
Combining this with the [existing exclusion of \(c=0\)](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v410.md#quartic), the remaining quadratics have \(c(d^2-4c^2)\ne0\) and \(2|c|+|d|\le4(2/\rho)^{2p}\).
They have two distinct roots, and neither is an endpoint.
The existence of genuine rows giving these remaining quadratics at arbitrarily large primes is open.
