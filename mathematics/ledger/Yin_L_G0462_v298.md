# Binary Fourier amplitudes at prime ideals

<a id="orbit"></a>
## Doubling on the multiplicative orbit

Let a unit reduce to \(u\) in a residue field \(k=\mathbb F_Q\) of odd characteristic \(p\), let \(H=\langle u\rangle\) have order \(r\), and take \(r\mid n\).
For a nontrivial additive character \(\psi\), write \(Z=|H\cap\ker\psi|\) and \(A(\psi)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}|(1+\psi(u^i))/2|\).
Let \(t\) be the least positive integer such that \(2^t\in H\cup(-H)\), and put \(\psi_b(x)=\psi(bx)\).
Then
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{t-1} A(\psi_{2^j})=2^{-tn(1-Z/r)}.
\]
On the nontrivial phases use \(|1+z|=|1-z^2|/|1-z|\), and telescope in \(j\).
The last numerator and first denominator agree after permutation of \(H\) and possible complex conjugation.
The \(Z\) trivial phases contribute one to every normalized product.
In particular a prime-field character has \(Z=0\), and \(2\in\pm H\) forces \(A=2^{-n}\) for every nontrivial character, even when the period is short.

Independently of doubling, put \(S_b=\sum_{x\in H}\psi(bx)\).
Orthogonality gives \(\sum_{b\in k}|S_b|^2=Qr\), and \(S_b=S_1\) for all \(b\in H\), so \(|S_1|\le\sqrt{Q-r}\) after removing \(b=0\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on \(|(1+\psi(x))/2|^2=(1+\operatorname{Re}\psi(x))/2\) now yields
\[
 A(\psi)^{2/n}\le \tfrac12\bigl(1+\sqrt{Q-r}/r\bigr).
\]
Thus an amplitude at least \(e^{-\eta n}\) with \(0\le\eta<(\log2)/2\) requires \(r\le\sqrt{Q-r}/(2e^{-2\eta}-1)\).
This excludes large multiplicative subgroups as witnesses using only field orthogonality.

<a id="order"></a>
## Which residue field the generated order sees

If the finite quotient comes from the maximal order of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the additive span of the reduced powers is exactly \(k_0=\mathbb F_p[u]\), which can be a proper subfield of \(k\).
When it is proper, a nonzero linear functional on \(k\) annihilating \(k_0\) gives a nontrivial additive character with \(A=1\), while its conductor in the maximal order is the entire prime ideal in the sense that the quotient by the conductor is \(k\).
Indeed for nonzero \(z\in k\), multiplication by \(z\) is onto, so the character cannot be trivial on every \(zy\).
For an explicit example take \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), \(\epsilon=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), the inert prime \(3\), and \(\alpha=\epsilon^8\).
The polynomial \(X^2-X-1\) is irreducible modulo 3, so \(k=\mathbb F_9\), and \(u=1\) by the order of its multiplicative group.
The conjugate of \(\epsilon^8\) has modulus below one, so \(\alpha\) still generates \(K\).
A character trivial on \(\mathbb F_3\) therefore gives perfect amplitude in every period, while all binary sums are supported on \(\mathbb F_3\).
Using the generated order \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) or measuring the restriction to its additive image removes this character from the nontrivial tests.
