# Local row divisibility for a multiple with repeated zeros

2026-11-18T10:52.

<a id="separation"></a>
## Selecting a row and its divided derivatives

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic unit \(\alpha\), let \(d=\deg P\), let \(p\) be prime and set \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Suppose
\[
 F=\sum_{j=1}^k x^{r_j}U_j(x^p),\qquad U_j\in\mathbb Z[X]\setminus\{0\},
 \qquad P^a\mid F
\]
for distinct \(r_j\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\).
This is the [repeated-zero determinant setting](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#determinant).
Fix \(e\ge1\) with \(ke\le a\).
The linear polynomials \(T-r_j\) have pairwise unit differences over \(\mathbb Z_p\).
The Chinese remainder theorem for their \(e\)-th powers therefore gives a polynomial \(H_i(T)\in\mathbb Z_p[T]\) of degree at most \(ke-1\) equal to \(1\) modulo \((T-r_i)^e\) and \(0\) modulo the other powers.
For \(E=x\,d/dx\), the operator \(H_i(E)\) acts on \(x^n\) by the scalar \(H_i(n)\), which is congruent to the indicator of row \(i\) modulo \(p^e\) on the occupied exponents.
Each application of \(E\) lowers the guaranteed \(P\)-multiplicity by at most one.
Consequently
\[
                x^{r_i}U_i(x^p)\in(p^e,P^{a-ke+1})\mathbb Z_p[x].       \tag{1}
\]
In particular all \(U_i(\beta)\) are divisible by \(p^e\) in \(A=\mathbb Z_p[x]/(P)=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\otimes\mathbb Z_p\), because \(\alpha\) is a unit.

More generally, for \(0\le t<e\) take the target residue of \(H_i(T)\) modulo \((T-r_i)^e\) to be \(\prod_{h=0}^{t-1}(T-r_i-hp)\), leaving the other residues zero.
The same degree bound holds.
On a target exponent \(n=r_i+pm\) the specified product is \(p^t t!\binom mt\).
Applying this operator to \(F\) and evaluating at \(\alpha\) gives
\[
                  p^t t!\,\beta^t U_i^{[t]}(\beta)\in p^e A,            \tag{2}
\]
where \(U_i^{[t]}(X)\) is the coefficient of \(Y^t\) in \(U_i(X+Y)\).
This retains all divided derivatives with the same degree cost.
When \(e\le p\), it gives \(U_i^{[t]}(\beta)\in p^{e-t} A\) for every \(0\le t<e\).
The full valuation \(v_p(t!)\) must be allowed when using (2) beyond that range.

<a id="rows"></a>
## Powers of the ideal generated by the base polynomial and the prime

Now assume \(p\nmid\operatorname{disc}P\), and let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
The ring \(A/pA=\mathbb F_p[x]/(\overline P)\) is a product of finite fields, and Frobenius is an automorphism.
The reductions of \(1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{d-1}\) are the Frobenius images of the original power basis, hence a basis.
Their change-of-basis determinant is a \(p\)-adic unit.
It follows that \(\deg Q=d\), \(A=\mathbb Z_p[\beta]\), and \(A=\mathbb Z_p[X]/(Q)\).
Modulo \(p\) the conjugate nodes \(\overline\beta_j\) are distinct, being the \(p\)-th powers of the distinct roots of \(\overline P\); in particular \(Q'(\beta)\) is a unit in \(A\).
This use of the full rank-\(d\) algebra includes every local factor.
For arbitrary \(e\) in (1), the remainder of each \(U_i\) modulo \(Q\) now has every coefficient divisible by \(p^e\).

For \(e\le p\), (2) yields the stronger containment
\[
                       U_i\in I^e,\qquad I=(p,Q)\subset\mathbb Z_p[X].   \tag{3}
\]
To see this, expand \(U_i=\sum_{j\ge0}C_jQ^j\) by monic division, with \(\deg C_j<d\).
Inductively \(p^{e-j}\) divides every coefficient of \(C_j\) for \(j<e\).
At step \(j\), the previous terms already have coefficients divisible by \(p^{e-j}\).
Their \(j\)-th divided derivatives therefore have the same divisibility.
At \(\beta\) the later terms contribute zero, and the \(j\)-th term contributes \(C_j(\beta)Q'(\beta)^j\).
The claim follows from (2), invertibility of \(Q'(\beta)\), and the integral basis in powers of \(\beta\).
These coefficient conditions give (3) directly.

<a id="resultant"></a>
## A square exponent in the two-row resultant

Take \(k=2\), write \(U,V\) for the rows and assume \(e\le p,\ 2e\le a\).
We may divide the content of \(F\) out first, since \(P\) is monic.
At least one row then has nonzero reduction modulo \(p\), and (3) gives degree at least \(de\) for that row.
For a nonzero resultant assume \(U,V\) coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X]\).
If they are only coprime in the Laurent ring, remove the lowest powers of \(X\) from the individual rows in this section.
These powers are units modulo \(I^e\), since \(Q(0)\) is a unit and \(X\) is invertible modulo \(I\), hence also modulo \(I^e\).

The Sylvester map \((H,J)\mapsto HU+JV\), with row multipliers of degree less than \(\deg V,\deg U\) respectively, has target the polynomials of degree \(<\deg U+\deg V\).
Its cokernel maps onto \(\mathbb Z_p[X]/I^e\).
Indeed the target polynomials contain representatives of degree \(<de\), and (3) kills the image.
In the expansion in powers of \(Q\), this quotient has \(d\) coefficients modulo \(p^{e-j}\) at every \(0\le j<e\).
Consequently \(p^{d e(e+1)/2}\) divides \(|\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|\).
Already (1) and the quotient modulo \((p^e,Q)\) give the weaker divisor \(p^{de}\) without the restriction \(e\le p\), since a row nonzero modulo \(p\) then has degree at least \(d\).

In fact (3) implies
\[
                         p^{d e^2}\mid |\operatorname{Res}(U,V)|.        \tag{4}
\]
Use \(v(p)=1\) in a splitting field over \(\mathbb Q_p\), and write \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d\) for the roots of \(Q\).
For each \(p\)-primitive row homogenize and factor it into linear forms \(L(X,Y)=uX+v_0Y\), normalizing each so the minimum coefficient valuation is zero.
The remaining scalar has valuation zero, by multiplicativity of the Gauss norm, which follows by reducing primitive factors in the residue polynomial ring.
At the \(j\)-th node define \(t_j(L)=\min(1,v(L(\beta_j,1)))\), with \(v(0)=+\infty\).
This number lies in \([0,1]\) and is positive at at most one node, since positive valuation forces \(u\) to be a unit and fixes the reduced root \(-v_0/u\).
The Gauss valuation of \(L(\beta_j+pZ,1)\) is \(t_j(L)\).
Indeed the coefficient of \(Z\) is \(pu\), with valuation one if \(L(\beta_j,1)\) reduces to zero.

If \(U,V\) are both \(p\)-primitive, (3) makes both \(U(\beta_j+pZ)\) and \(V(\beta_j+pZ)\) divisible coefficientwise by \(p^e\).
Thus the sum of \(t_j(L)\) over the linear factors of each row is at least \(e\) at each node.
For a factor \(L=uX+v_0Y\) of \(U\) and \(L'=u'X+v'_0Y\) of \(V\), write their determinant as \(uL'(\beta_j,1)-u'L(\beta_j,1)\).
Its valuation is at least \(\min(t_j(L),t_j(L'))\ge t_j(L)t_j(L')\).
A pair can make a positive contribution at only one node, so these lower bounds add over the nodes.
Summing over factor pairs and using the linear-factor product formula for the resultant gives at least \(d e^2\).

If one row, say \(U\), has \(p\)-content \(p^c\) up to a unit, then \(V\) is \(p\)-primitive with \(\deg V\ge de\).
For \(U/p^c\) the Gauss valuation at every disk is at least \((e-c)_+\).
The primitive factor-pair contribution is therefore at least \(d e(e-c)_+\).
Restoring the scalar adds \(c\deg V\ge cde\), proving (4) in this case also.
Restoring any common integer content multiplies the resultant by an integer.
For (3) alone the exponent in (4) is attained by the coprime rows \(Q^e,Q^e+p^e\), whose resultant has absolute value \(p^{d e^2}\).
This pair tests the ideal implication only.

<a id="cost"></a>
## Degree and coefficient costs

With \(m_U=\deg U,\ m_V=\deg V\) after the indicated monomial removals, Hadamard's inequality for the Sylvester determinant gives
\[
        d e^2\log p\le m_V\log\|U\|_2+m_U\log\|V\|_2.
\]
If \(T\) denotes the total number of terms of the primitive \(F\), both row norms are at most \(\sqrt{T}\,2^{T-2}M(F)\) by the [sparse coefficient bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound).
For the logarithmic measure \(m(F)=a\,m(P)+m(R)\), with \(R\) the extra primitive noncyclotomic factors other than \(x\), this yields
\[
      a\,m(P)+m(R)\ \ge\
      \frac{d e^2\log p}{\deg U+\deg V}-(T-2)\log2-\tfrac12\log T .    \tag{5}
\]
The degree requirement \(\max(\deg U,\deg V)\ge de\) requires no archimedean estimate.
It also limits the positive term on the right of (5) to \(e\log p\).
In the [uniform repeated-root range](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v362.md#uniform) one has \(e\le a/2,\ d\le p^{1-\delta}\) and \(m(P)>2/\log^3(3d)\) by the [height bound used there](../audits/literature/Vou96/Yin_U001.md#height).
Hence \(e\log p/(a p\,m(P))=O(\log^4(3p)/p)\).
The resultant detects an exact arithmetic cost, but even before the sparse-height loss its contribution in (5) is too small to recover the leading \(a p\,m(P)\) scale.
The repeated-root result itself allows bad discriminant primes, which are outside (3)–(5).

## The derivative order and the obstruction at multiplicity one

For two residues the bound \(2e-1\) on an integral universal projector is sharp when \(p>2e-2\).
It suffices to test the classes \(0,1\).
Suppose \(H\in\mathbb Z_p[T]\) takes the prescribed indicator values modulo \(p^e\) on all nonnegative exponents in both classes.
For \(r=0,1\), evaluate \(H(r+pX)\) at \(X=0,\ldots,e-1\).
Terms of degree at least \(e\) have coefficients divisible by \(p^e\).
The remaining Vandermonde matrix has unit determinant, so \(H(r)\) has the prescribed value modulo \(p^e\) and \(H^{[j]}(r)\equiv0\bmod p^{e-j}\) for \(1\le j<e\).
Thus modulo \(p\) the two indicator jets are constant to order \(e\).
For target \(1\) the unique representative of degree below \(2e\) is
\[
             T^e\sum_{h=0}^{e-1}\binom{-e}{h}(T-1)^h .
\]
It is zero modulo \(T^e\) and equals one modulo \((T-1)^e\) by the truncated inverse of \(T^e\).
Its leading coefficient \((-1)^{e-1}\binom{2e-2}{e-1}\) is nonzero modulo \(p\), so every such projector has degree at least \(2e-1\).
Consequently replacing \(k\) by \(k-1\) in the universal separation budget fails already for two classes.
Particular bounded exponent sets can require smaller projectors.

For each fixed \(k\ge2,e\ge1\), degree \(ke-1\) is necessary on some \(k\) classes for all sufficiently large primes.
Use the classes \(0,\ldots,k-1\) and target \(k-1\).
Write \(B(T)=\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}(T-j)^e\).
The least-degree rational Hermite representative is \(B(T)\) times the Taylor polynomial of degree \(e-1\) of \(1/B(T)\) about \(T=k-1\).
The coefficient of order \(e-1\) in this inverse is nonzero because in \(\prod_{u=1}^{k-1}(u+Z)^{-e}\) every summand of this coefficient has sign \((-1)^{e-1}\).
After excluding the finitely many primes dividing its numerator or the denominators and taking \(p>\max(k,e)\), the representative keeps degree \(ke-1\) modulo \(p\).
The same evaluation on each residue class forces any integral universal projector to reduce to the required Hermite data.
This fixed-\(k,e\) sharpness gives no new degree lower bound at exceptional primes.

There is also an actual obstruction at \(a=1\) for every unit \(P\) and good prime \(p\) above.
The integral order \(\mathbb Z[\beta]\) has finite index \(c\) in \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), with \(p\nmid c\) by the power-basis argument.
For any \(0<r<p\) choose \(W\in\mathbb Z[X]\) with \(W(\beta)=c\alpha^r\).
Then \(c x^r-W(x^p)\) is a nonzero multiple of \(P\) with two coprime rows.
Their resultant is a \(p\)-adic unit because one row is the constant \(c\); dividing out integer content preserves this property.
This excludes any compulsory positive \(p\)-valuation in the analogous multiplicity-one resultant.
