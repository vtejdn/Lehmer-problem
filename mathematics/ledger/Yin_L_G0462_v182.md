# Localization of circle double roots and a finite-degree reciprocal interpolation limit

<a id="localization"></a>
## Each circle jet needs an interior row root nearby

In the [positive-shift reciprocal form](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#row), write
\[
 F(x)=A(x^p)+\sigma x^S A^*(x^p),\qquad
 A^*(X)=X^n A(1/X),\quad n=\deg A,\quad A(0)\ne0,
 \quad S>0,\quad (S,p)=1,\quad \sigma\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
Take \(p\ge2\), real coefficients and coprime rows.
The rows have no unit-circle zeros and have the same modulus there, so their row integral is \(I=m(A)\).
Put \(L=I-\log|A(0)|\).
For every unit-circle double root \(a\) of \(F\), with \(\beta=a^p\), there exists a root \(\gamma\) of \(A\) in the open disk satisfying
\[
                           |\beta-\gamma|\le\sqrt{2pL/S}.                \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if \(t\) such powered marks have pairwise Euclidean distances at least \(\eta>0\) and \(A\) has fewer than \(t\) open-disk roots counted with multiplicity, then
\[
                                 L\ge S\eta^2/(8p).                      \tag{2}
\]
For an integral row one has \(I\ge L\), and for \(|A(0)|=1\) one has \(I=L\).

Indeed the quotient \(A(x^p)/A^*(x^p)\) agrees with \(-\sigma x^S\) to first order at \(a\).
Thus the angular derivative of the argument of \(A/A^*\) at \(\beta\) equals \(S/p\).
The real coefficients give, up to a constant, \(A(X)/A^*(X)=\prod_\gamma(X-\gamma)/(1-\bar\gamma X)\), with multiplicities.
For \(|\beta|=1\), differentiating the argument of one factor with respect to \(\arg\beta\) gives \((1-|\gamma|^2)/|\beta-\gamma|^2\).
Discarding the negative contributions gives
\[
               0<S/p\le
                   \sum_{|\gamma|<1}\frac{1-|\gamma|^2}{|\beta-\gamma|^2}.
\]
In particular the sum contains at least one interior root.
Jensen's formula gives \(L=\sum_{|\gamma|<1}-\log|\gamma|\), so the numerators sum to at most \(2L\).
Bounding the denominators below by the smallest squared distance proves (1).
If \(L<S\eta^2/(8p)\), each mark has an interior root in its open disk of radius \(\eta/2\), and these disks are disjoint, proving (2).

<a id="construction"></a>
## Interpolation with coprime real rows of degree twice the number of marks

Let \(\mathcal A\) be a nonempty finite set of \(t\) distinct nonzero complex numbers, none a root of unity.
Suppose the set is closed under conjugation and inversion and the numbers \(\beta=a^p\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), are distinct.
For any \(1\le r<p\) with \((r,p)=1\), put \(N=2t\) and \(K=pN+r\).
There are reciprocal real polynomials \(F_j\) with exactly the two classes \(0,r\bmod p\), ordinary degree \(K\), both diagonal endpoint coefficients equal to \(1\), and two coprime rows of degree \(N\), such that
\[
          F_j(a)=F_j'(a)=0\quad(a\in\mathcal A),\qquad
          \lim_j I(F_j)=2p\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\log^+|a| .              \tag{3}
\]
If all marks lie on the unit circle this proves \(\inf_j I(F_j)=0\), with a fixed bound on the row degree.
The one common normalization fixes the two endpoints of the diagonal and permits different endpoint coefficients within one row.

Set \(g(X)=\prod_{a\in\mathcal A}(X-a^p)^2\).
Inverse pairing gives \(g(0)=1\) and \(g(X)=X^Ng(1/X)\), and conjugation gives real coefficients.
For \(a\in\mathcal A\) write \(w(a)=1-a^K\ne0\).
There is a unique polynomial \(T\) of degree less than \(N\) prescribed by
\[
          T(a^p)=w(a),\qquad a^pT'(a^p)=\frac{K}{2p}w(a).
\]
These are the two Hermite data at each of \(t\) distinct nonzero points, and conjugation invariance makes \(T\) real.
For real \(\epsilon\ne0\) set \(A_\epsilon=g+\epsilon T\), and reverse to degree \(N\), writing \(A_\epsilon^*(X)=X^N A_\epsilon(1/X)\).
Consider
\[
                F_\epsilon(x)=A_\epsilon(x^p)+x^r A_\epsilon^*(x^p)
                               =A_\epsilon(x^p)+x^K A_\epsilon(x^{-p}).
\]
It is reciprocal with respect to degree \(K\).
At \(a\), its value is \(\epsilon[w(a)+a^K w(1/a)]=0\).
The Euler derivative at \(a\) is
\[
 p\beta A_\epsilon'(\beta)
     +a^K\{K A_\epsilon(1/\beta)-p\beta^{-1}A_\epsilon'(1/\beta)\}
       =\frac{\epsilon K}{2}[w(a)+a^K w(1/a)]=0,
\]
because \(g\) vanishes to order two at both powered marks.

Let \(T^*(X)=X^NT(1/X)\).
For each mark, \(T^*(\beta)=-a^{-r}w(a)\ne T(\beta)\), since \(a\) is nontorsion.
Hence \(T-T^*\) has finitely many zeros.
Every common zero of \(A_\epsilon,A_\epsilon^*\) belongs to that fixed finite set by subtraction.
At the roots of \(g\), \(A_\epsilon(\beta)=\epsilon w(a)\ne0\).
At each other candidate \(z\), the equation \(g(z)+\epsilon T(z)=0\) excludes at most one value of \(\epsilon\).
Take a sequence of nonzero real values tending to zero outside these exceptions.
The leading coefficient of \(A_\epsilon\) is one and \(A_\epsilon(0)\to1\).
For sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), dividing the entire diagonal by \(A_\epsilon(0)\) gives exactly degree \(K\), constant and leading coefficient \(1\), and both rows of degree \(N\).
The normalized row integral is \(m(A_\epsilon)-\log|A_\epsilon(0)|\), because reversal preserves modulus on the circle.
Continuity of the multiset of roots of monic polynomials of fixed degree gives \(m(A_\epsilon)\to m(g)=2p\sum_a\log^+|a|\).
This proves (3).

## What the finite cap permits

For circle marks, (2) forces a positive cost if the row degree is less than their number, whereas (3) makes the cost arbitrarily small at row degree \(2t\).
Thus prescribing only the boundary jets, which have zero cost in the [Hardy-space relaxation](Yin_L_G0462_v178.md#gram), can also have zero infimal cost at finite degree for real reciprocal polynomials with coprime rows and exact diagonal endpoints.
The intermediate degree range can depend on the marked data and is not determined by these estimates.

For the full set of conjugates of a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit of degree \(d\), take any degree-preserving prime \(p\).
The hypotheses above hold, \(t=d\), and the sum in (3) is the log measure \(h\) of its minimal polynomial \(P\).
Here \(g,T\) have rational coefficients because the conjugate set is stable under every automorphism of the splitting field and the unique Hermite polynomial with these algebraic data is fixed by all of them.
One may take rational \(\epsilon\) excluding the same finite set.
Thus \(P^2\mid F_j\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with diagonal endpoints exactly one, \(I(F_j)/p\to2h\) for the given input, and \(2pd+r\le p(16d+1)\) inside the [integral cofactor degree cap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement).
Clearing the unrestricted rational denominators can change both the endpoints and the measure.
To extract a gap in this cap, the localization test would need, for instance, control of the number or positions of interior row roots using the integral input and witness.
