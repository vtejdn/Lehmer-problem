# Reciprocal-pair blocks can have only trivial proper-subfield norms

<a id="norm-test"></a>
## The archimedean test for relative norms

Let \(\alpha\) be a nontorsion algebraic unit, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), and \(\mathbb Q\subseteq E\subsetneq K\).
The embeddings of \(K\) form blocks indexed by their restrictions \(v:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\).
For \(\gamma=N_{K/E}\alpha\) and \(f=[E:\mathbb Q(\gamma)]\) one has
\[
 f\log M(\gamma)=\sum_v\max\left(0,\sum_{w|v}\log|w(\alpha)|\right)
                      \le\log M(\alpha).
\]
Indeed \(v(\gamma)=\prod_{w|v}w(\alpha)\), and each embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\gamma)\) occurs exactly \(f\) times.
All finite places have absolute value one, so Kronecker's theorem makes \(\gamma\) nontorsion exactly when at least one displayed block sum is nonzero.
The degree then strictly decreases.
This is the elementary relative-norm version of the [nonzero field projection](../audits/literature/FM13/Yin_U001.md#restricted-arithmetic-set) restriction.
For \(E=\mathbb Q(\alpha^k)\subsetneq K\), the [all-power norm descent](../claims/Yin_C_G0231_v1.md#all-positive-power-existence-descent) already proves nonvanishing and an exact measure formula.
On 2026-11-16 the proof contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) tested nonvanishing when the Galois action on inverse pairs admits a nontrivial partition into blocks.
The degree-eight example below has just one pair of nonreal trace conjugates and makes every proper-subfield norm equal to one.
The independent reviewer checked the [norm inequality](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v19.md#relative-norm-review), the [Salem block calculation](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v19.md#salem-block-review) and the [degree-eight example](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v19.md#nonreal-block-review).

<a id="salem-blocks"></a>
## Salem conjugates and oriented block products

Consider a genuine Salem number \(\alpha>1\) of degree \(2r\), with one real inverse pair and \(r-1\) neutral inverse pairs.
The [actual sign kernel](../claims/Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population) contains every even collection of pair flips and contains all flips when \(r\) is even.
The [retained source attribution](../audits/literature/CM10/readme.md#cm10-salem-kernel-attribution) is Christopoulos and McKee, Proposition 2.3.
Concretely, conjugates of complex conjugation flip all pairs except a designated one, and their products give every double flip.

Every nontrivial block \(B\) of the action on the individual roots that contains \(\alpha\) also contains \(\alpha^{-1}\).
Otherwise it must contain a root from a different pair.
There is a flip that inverts \(\alpha\) and fixes this other root, using a third pair to balance parity for \(r\ge3\), or the full kernel for \(r=2\).
The translate of \(B\) intersects \(B\) at the fixed root and so equals \(B\), a contradiction.
In particular for any proper subfield \(E\subset K\), some automorphism over \(E\) sends \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha^{-1}\), since both are in its embedding block at the given inclusion of \(E\).
On \(K\) this automorphism is the trace involution, whose fixed field is \(\mathbb Q(\alpha+\alpha^{-1})\) by the quadratic trace equation.
Thus \(E\) is contained in that totally real field and \(N_{K/E}\alpha=1\) by norm transitivity.

Suppose that a block on the \(r\) inverse pairs has size \(1\le m<r\).
Choose one root from each of its pairs and let \(\eta\) be their product.
By the [signed monomial orbit calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0866_v1.md#salem-monomial-orbits), one orientation per inverse pair is multiplicatively independent modulo torsion, and the degree of a monomial equals the size of its exponent orbit.
There are exactly \(r/m\) block images, and every one admits all \(2^m\) signs by using an unused coordinate to balance parity.
Exactly one block contains the real pair, on which half its orientations have modulus \(\alpha\) and half have modulus \(\alpha^{-1}\).
The remaining block images give modulus one.
It follows that
\[
       [\mathbb Q(\eta):\mathbb Q]=(r/m)2^m,\qquad
                     \log M(\eta)=2^{m-1}\log\alpha .
\]
These are the block-support specialization of the existing orbit method and the consequent ordinary Mahler measure.
For \(m\ge2\) there is no degree decrease.

## Two nonreal trace conjugates and full independent flips

Take
\[
    T(t)=((t-1)^2+2)^2-5,\qquad P(z)=z^4T(z+z^{-1}).
\]
Its trace roots are \(1\pm\sqrt{-2+\sqrt5}\) in \((-2,2)\) and \(1\pm i\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}\).
For the first real root \(t_1=1+\sqrt{-2+\sqrt5}\), the field \(\mathbb Q(t_1)\) contains \(\sqrt5\).
The norm of \(-2+\sqrt5\) to \(\mathbb Q\) is \(-1\), so it is not a square in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
This proves irreducibility of \(T\) and gives a nontrivial block partition of its four roots according to the two values of \((t-1)^2+2\).

Let \(F\) be the splitting field of \(T\) and \(u_i=t_i^2-4\) for its four roots.
Modulo 29 the polynomial has four distinct roots \(-2,4,-3,5\).
By the [simple-root Hensel theorem](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) they lift to four roots in \(\mathbb Q_{29}\), giving an embedding of \(F\) there.
Exactly one \(t_i\) reduces to \(-2\), and none reduces to \(2\).
Since \(T(-2)=116=4\cdot29\), the factor \(t_i+2\) at that root has valuation one, by taking their full product.
Thus exactly one \(u_i\) has valuation one and the rest have valuation zero.
Conjugating this valuation in the transitive trace group gives an individual odd valuation for each \(u_i\).
Consequently their four square classes are independent in \(F^\times/F^{\times2}\).

Adjoining the four square roots of \(u_i\) therefore gives every independent sign flip.
For detail, after adjoining an independent list, its square-root monomials form a basis with distinct sign characters.
Any element of that field whose nonzero square belongs to \(F\) is an eigenvector for each sign flip and so is a scalar times one of the basis monomials.
An additional independent square class cannot already have a square root there, which proves the assertion inductively.
The lifts \(z=(t_i\pm\sqrt{u_i})/2\) are interchanged individually by these flips.
Trace transitivity and the flip on each fiber give irreducibility of \(P\).
Each of its roots \(\alpha\) is a reciprocal unit of degree eight.
The nonreal traces each yield one exterior and one interior root, and the two real traces supply four roots on the unit circle.
In particular \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.

The argument for embedding blocks now uses single flips, giving \(N_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)/E}\alpha=1\) for every proper subfield \(E\).
One chosen root per pair is independent modulo torsion in this example too.
Indeed applying just the \(i\)-th flip to a torsion relation and dividing gives a torsion power \(\alpha_i^{2n_i}\), so \(n_i=0\).
It follows that every positive power of \(\alpha\) retains degree eight, since its eight images stay distinct.
More generally any nontorsion integer monomial in the roots uses some \(1\le m\le4\) inverse pairs after cancelling opposite exponents.
The conjugate supports cover the four pairs and each support admits at least \(2^m\) exponent vectors, so its degree is at least \(4\cdot2^m/m\ge8\).

For the specific block partition, an oriented product on either two-pair block has degree eight.
In the image containing the two nonreal traces the four sign choices have moduli \(\lambda^2,1,1,\lambda^{-2}\), where \(\lambda>1\) is the common exterior radius.
In the other image all four products have modulus one.
Thus the oriented product has exactly \(M(\alpha)=\lambda^2\), with no decrease in degree.
The inequality \(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\ge|\Im t_i|=\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}>2\) at a nonreal trace shows that this concrete measure exceeds \((1+\sqrt2)^2\).
The example obstructs deduction of a nonzero lower-degree projection from the imprimitive pair action and this conjugate signature alone.
It gives no family with measure approaching one.
