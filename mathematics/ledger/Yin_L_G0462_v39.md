# Exact finite moment restrictions on the positive-constant Pisot branches

<a id="coefficient-window"></a>
## A uniform comparison through square-root order

Use the [positive-constant notation](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second) for a Pisot minimal polynomial at \(q\ge2\) and a fixed first-coefficient branch \(j\ge1\).
Write \(K_q=j+\sum_{n\ge1}c_nz^n\), where the coefficients are integers, and \(H_q=1+\sum_{n\ge1}h_nz^n\), where \(H_q\) has positive real part and real coefficients.
Let \(\lambda_q\) be its Herglotz probability measure, so \(h_n=2\int v^n\,d\lambda_q(v)\) for \(n\ge1\) by the real symmetry.
Define \(A(0)=2j\), \(A(n)=c_{|n|}\) for \(n\ne0\).
There are constants \(C_j,c_j'>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(q\) on the branch and \(1\le n\le c_j'\sqrt q\),
\[
      \max_{|k|\le n}\left|A(k)-2j\int v^k\,d\lambda_q(v)\right|
               \ \le\ C_j(1+n^2)/q.                                   \tag{1}
\]
This estimate uses a single genuine input at its given constant.

In the [exact rational identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#limit) put \(\epsilon=\delta/\theta\), where \(\delta=\theta-q\), and solve for \(K_q=B/(1-Z)\) with
\[
 B=q\delta H_q+\delta z^{-1}(1-H_q)-\delta z+\epsilon(1-qz)H_q,
 \qquad Z=\frac{\epsilon z(\theta-z)H_q}{1-z^2}.
\]
The numerator is regular at zero because \(H_q(0)=1\), and \(Z(0)=0\).
The branch estimate \(\delta=O_j(q^{-1})\), \(q\delta=j+O_j(q^{-1})\) and the Herglotz bound \(|h_i|\le2\) show that every coefficient of \(B-jH_q\) is \(O_j(1/q)\), uniformly in its index.
Also \(|[z^i]Z|\le C_j i/q\) for \(i\ge1\) by summing the coefficients of \(1/(1-z^2)\).
In computations modulo \(z^{n+1}\), the coefficient sum of \(Z\) is at most \(C_j n^2/q\), hence at most \(1/2\) for an appropriate \(c_j'\).
The coefficient sum of \(Z/(1-Z)\) modulo the same power is therefore at most \(2C_j n^2/q\) by convolution.
Since the coefficients of \(B\) are uniformly bounded in terms of \(j\), the identity \(K_q=B+B Z/(1-Z)\) proves (1).

<a id="finite-positivity"></a>
## Integral positivity on a growing window

For sufficiently large \(q\) the real symmetric integral Toeplitz matrix \(G_n=(A(a-b))_{0\le a,b<n}\) is positive semidefinite whenever
\[
      1\le n\le \frac{\log q-5\log\log q}{\log(2j)}.                 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed it differs in operator norm by at most \(e=C_j n(1+n^2)/q\) from the positive moment matrix \((2j\int v^{a-b}\,d\lambda_q)\), by the row-sum bound.
Each principal submatrix has eigenvalues at least \(-e\) and trace \(2j\ell\), where \(\ell\) is its size.
If a principal determinant is negative, at least one eigenvalue has absolute value at most \(e\), and \(\ell\ge2\) because the diagonal is positive.
The sum of the absolute values of all eigenvalues is at most \(\ell(2j+2e)\).
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality bounds the absolute determinant by
\[
 e\left(\frac{\ell(2j+2e)}{\ell-1}\right)^{\ell-1}
       \le \exp(1)\,e(2j+2e)^{n-1}.
\]
For (2), \(ne\to0\) uniformly and \((2j)^n\le q/(\log q)^5\), so this bound tends to zero.
The determinant is an integer, excluding a negative sign for large \(q\).
Nonnegativity of all principal minors gives the asserted positive semidefiniteness, including possible singular matrices.

<a id="finite-period"></a>
## A maximal moment forces an exact finite recurrence

Let \(L\ge1\) be fixed and suppose \(A(L)=2j\tau\) with \(\tau\in\{1,-1\}\).
There is \(b_j>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(q\) depending on \(j,L\),
\[
            A(k+L)=\tau A(k)\qquad(0\le k\le b_j\sqrt q-L).            \tag{3}
\]
From (1) at the fixed index \(L\), \(\int|v^L-\tau|^2\,d\lambda_q=O_{j,L}(1/q)\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz the moments of \(2j\lambda_q\) at \(k+L\) and \(k\), after applying the sign, differ by \(O_{j,L}(q^{-1/2})\), uniformly in \(k\).
Use (1) with \(n=\lfloor b_j\sqrt q\rfloor\) and choose \(b_j\le c_j'\) so small that the two approximation errors together are eventually less than \(1/2\).
Then the two integers in (3) differ in absolute value by less than one, proving equality.

In particular every [purely atomic branch limit](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) is supported on roots of unity of some common order \(L\).
Its \(L\)-th Fourier moment has full mass, so along the convergent subsequence the fixed coefficient \(A(L)\) eventually equals \(2j\).
The first \(L\) coefficients also eventually agree with that limit, and (3) gives agreement throughout \(0\le k\le b_j\sqrt q\).
At every finite positive-constant input the rational \(K_q\) still has an interior pole, so a departure must occur beyond this window.
This result gives a finite recurrence that can be tested on candidate inputs, while leaving open whether the eventual departure can coexist with strict Pisot conjugate inequalities.
