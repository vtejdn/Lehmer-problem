# Using a positive element of the trace codifferent to bound the integer Pisot branch

<a id="minimum"></a>
## The least interlacer leading coefficient

For a Salem trace polynomial \(S\) of degree \(m\), write \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\in(-2,2)\) for its circle traces and \(x_m=T=\sigma+\sigma^{-1}>2\).
Its Salem polynomial is \(P(z)=z^mS(z+z^{-1})\).
Let \(t(S)\) be the least leading coefficient of an integer polynomial \(C\) of degree \(m-1\) that strictly interlaces \(S\) and has positive leading coefficient.
Equivalently the numbers
\[
             c_l=C(x_l)/S'(x_l)
\]
are all positive, and the leading coefficient \(t\) equals \(\sum_l c_l\), by partial fractions of \(C/S\).
The sign condition gives a zero of \(C\) in each gap between consecutive roots of \(S\), and strict interlacing with positive leading coefficient gives exactly these signs.
The choice \(C=S'\) gives \(1\le t(S)\le m\).

In the order \(\mathbb Z[T]\), the codifferent is the set of \(y\in\mathbb Q(T)\) for which \(\operatorname{Tr}(y\mathbb Z[T])\subseteq\mathbb Z\).
It is \(S'(T)^{-1}\mathbb Z[T]\).
Indeed for a rational polynomial \(C\) of degree \(<m\), the trace of \(T^a C(T)/S'(T)\) is the coefficient of \(x^{m-1}\) in the remainder of \(x^a C\) modulo \(S\), by the same partial fractions.
For \(0\le a<m\) these coefficients successively give the coefficients of \(C\) from highest to lowest, each plus an integer combination of the previously determined coefficients, since \(S\) is monic integral.
Thus \(t(S)\) is precisely the least trace of a totally positive element of this codifferent.

Every same-degree positive-constant Pisot minimal polynomial \(f\) with \(q=f(0)\ge2\) and \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\) has integer branch \(j\ge t(S)\).
In fact its [trace numerator](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v7.md#trace) has leading coefficient \(j\), with positive residues at all roots of \(S\).
Explicitly, in the notation \(f=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1})\), the rational trace is \(1-q+(x-2)p/S\).
Its numerator is integral and has degree \(m-1\).
Here \(j=[z](f/f^*)-q^2+1\), with the series expanded at zero.

<a id="frequencies"></a>
## Two choices of frequency for an interlacer

Suppose \(\sigma_i\to1\) is any sequence of Salem numbers.
Suppress \(i\), take an arbitrary strict interlacer \(C\) as above, put \(a=\log\sigma,\ u=T-2\sim a^2\), and let \(D_k(z+z^{-1})=z^k+z^{-k}\).
Define the integer parameters \(M=\lfloor a^{-1/8}\rfloor,\ H=\lceil a^{-1/2}\rceil\) on the eventual sequence.
There is an even \(s=2h\), \(1\le h\le M\), with
\[
                \sum_{l<m}c_l(2-D_s(x_l))\le(2+2/M)t.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed the Fejér kernel \(1+2\sum_{h=1}^M(1-h/(M+1))\cos(2h v)\) is nonnegative, being the squared modulus of \(\sum_{r=0}^M e^{2irv}\) divided by \(M+1\).
The displayed weights without the factor two sum to \(M/2\).
Thus their normalized average of \(D_{2h}(2\cos v)=2\cos(2hv)\) is at least \(-2/M\) for every \(v\).
Integrating against the positive masses \(c_l\) and selecting one \(h\) proves (1).

Set \(L_s=(D_s(T)-2)/u=(\sinh(sa/2)/\sinh(a/2))^2\).
Uniformly for \(1\le s\le2M\), \(L_s=(1+o(1))s^2\).
Let \(K\) be the least positive integer with \(\sigma^K\ge t/(a c_m L_s)\).
This target tends to infinity since \(c_m\le t\) and \(aM^2\to0\).
For every \(k\) from \(K\) through \(K+H-1\), one has
\[
              c_m L_s(D_k(T)-2)=(1+o(1))t/a                       \tag{2}
\]
uniformly, because \(Ha\to0\).
At any circle point \(z\ne1\) the mean of \(D_k(z+z^{-1})\) over this block has modulus at most \(4/(H|1-z|)\), by summing a geometric progression.
Moreover \(2-D_s(z+z^{-1})=|1-z^s|^2\le s^2|1-z|^2\).
The error after multiplication by \(2-D_s\) is therefore \(O(M^2/H)=o(1)\), uniformly on the circle, including the zero at one.
It follows from (1) that for some \(k\) in the block
\[
          \sum_{l<m}c_l(2-D_s(x_l))(2-D_k(x_l))\le(4+o(1))t.       \tag{3}
\]
The choices and error estimates are uniform in the positions and sizes of the positive \(c_l\), including \(c_m\).

<a id="seed"></a>
## An actual Pisot seed with branch at most four times the interlacer trace

Define
\[
   g=\frac{(D_s-2)(D_k-2)}{x-2},\qquad
   p\equiv gC\pmod{S},\quad\deg p<m,\qquad
   f=P-(z-1)z^{m-1}p(z+z^{-1}).
\]
Here \(D_s(2)=2\) makes \(g\) integral, and the monic remainder gives integral \(p,f\).
The constant \(q=f(0)\) satisfies \(q=1+\sum_l c_l g(x_l)\), by partial fractions and the leading coefficient in degree \(m-1\) of \(p\).
For \(x=2\cos v\) we have
\[
       0\le\frac{2-D_s(x)}{4-x^2}
           =\frac{\sin^2(hv)}{\sin^2 v}\le h^2
\]
with the endpoint limits understood.
Consequently \(-g(x_l)>0\) on each interior trace since the associated circle conjugates are nontorsion, and \(-g(x_l)\le4h^2(2+x_l)\).
Their total contribution to the constant has absolute value \(O(M^2 t)\), while the exterior contribution is (2), giving \(q\sim t/a\).
In particular \(q\ge2\) eventually.

The [Pisot inverse simplex](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#simplex) has residues \(b_l=(T-x_l)c_l(-g(x_l))>0\) for \(l<m\).
Its endpoint sum \(\sum_{l<m}b_l/(2+x_l)\) is \(O(M^2t)=o(q)\) because \(T\to2\).
It follows that \(f\) is a monic Pisot minimal polynomial of degree \(2m\), with exterior root \(\theta>q\) and \(zf+f^*=(z+1)P\).
The [exterior root formula](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v4.md#second-order) gives
\[
    0<\theta-q-u=\frac{u}{\theta-1}
                 +\sum_{l<m}\frac{b_l}{\theta+\theta^{-1}-x_l}
               =O\!\left(\frac{u+M^2t}{q-2}\right)=o(1).
\]
Finally the branch residues of \(1-q+(x-2)p/S\) are \((x_l-2)g(x_l)c_l\).
Their circle part is (3), while the exterior part is \(u c_m g(T)=(1+o(1))ut/a=o(t)\).
This proves \(j\le (4+o(1))t\).

Taking a minimizing interlacer at every degree, the existence of these same-degree Pisot inverses with \(\theta-q\to0\) and \(j=o(d)\) along the given Salem sequence is equivalent to \(t(S)=o(d)\), where \(d=2m\).
Bounded branches are likewise equivalent to bounded \(t(S)\).
In particular monic strict interlacers give integral branches at most four eventually.
These equivalences use only the selected Salem sequence and do not assume any uniform bound for \(t(S)\) beyond \(m\).
