# Vanishing intermediate denominator coefficients at arbitrary fixed support and branch

<a id="intermediate"></a>
## A cyclotomic multiplier on the interval above all early offsets

Fix a positive branch \(j\) and a term bound \(K\).
Consider a sequence of actual positive-constant Pisot inputs with \(\theta-q\to0\) and trace lifts \(\Pi_i\) having at most \(K\) terms.
Write \(h_i\) for their trace degrees and \(B_i^\sharp\) for their monic integer reciprocal trace denominators.
After extraction, each of the finitely many positive lower exponents is either \(o(h_i)\) or at least a fixed positive fraction of \(h_i\).
Call those of size \(o(h_i)\) the early offsets, and write \(D_i\) for the largest of them.
There is at least one, and \(1\le D_i=o(h_i)\).
After further extraction there are polynomials \(U_i\), each a product of a bounded number of nonzero binomials \(1-z^a\) with \(a=O(D_i)\), and a fixed \(L\ge2\) such that
\[
                [z^t]U_i B_i^\sharp=0\qquad(LD_i\le t\le h_i/L).
\]
An empty product is allowed.
The constants can depend on the extracted sequence.
The assertion covers all relative sizes and integer relations of the early offsets, including a limiting polynomial with a positive-dimensional set of zeros on the torus.
More generally choose an initial group of lower exponents ending at \(D_i\) and suppose the remaining positive exponents of the full lift are at least \(H_i\), where \(D_i/H_i\to0\).
If one chosen coefficient is not divisible by \(j\), the conclusion holds for this group on \(LD_i\le t\le H_i/L\).
The cutoff \(H_i\) may be sublinear in \(h_i\).

The [sparse height bound](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U002.md#sparse-height-bound) at \(M(\Pi_i)=j\) fixes all coefficients after extraction and bounds \(\|\Pi_i/j\|_\infty\).
The [first nondivisible coefficient](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#statement) occurs at \(o(h_i)\), so some early coefficient is not divisible by \(j\).
Let \(c\) be the gcd of \(j\) and the early coefficients, and put \(m=j/c\ge2\).
Let \(\Gamma\) be the quotient of the free group on the early offsets by their persistent relations, after diagonal stabilization of all relations as in the [face calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#face).
It is free abelian and has evaluation maps \(\lambda_i:\Gamma\to\mathbb Z\) eventually.
Each fixed \(v\in\Gamma\) has \(|\lambda_i(v)|=O_v(D_i)\), and each nonzero \(v\) has eventually nonzero evaluation.
Fix its eventual sign for every nonzero \(v\) by further diagonal extraction.
This order makes every term of the early polynomial except the constant strictly positive.

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\) be the early polynomial before normalization, divided by \(c\), with constant \(m\).
Distinct terms remain distinct and \(P\) is primitive.
Write \(P_i\) for its specialization by \(\lambda_i\).
Then \(P_i/m=\Pi_i/j\) throughout a prefix of length \(\eta h_i\) for some fixed \(\eta>0\).
Moreover \(M(P)=m\).
Indeed on the quotient lattice of **all** lower offsets and the reflection degree, the functional obtained by normalizing the exponents by \(2h_i+2\) exposes exactly the early terms and the constant.
The argument of the [two-offset face bound](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#face) applies with any fixed finite list: take an integral functional vanishing on these terms and strictly positive on all others by rational approximation, then use Jensen in its phase.
It bounds the measure of the face \(cP\) by \(j\), the measure of the full limiting polynomial by Lawton's theorem.
The early lattice embeds in this full quotient with its own Haar marginal.
The reverse bound \(M(P)\ge m\) follows by Jensen along a positive exposing integral evaluation \(\lambda_i\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(i\).

For the more general group, expose its terms in the same full stabilized lattice without normalizing all the late exponents at a single scale.
Pass to the real quotient by the span of the chosen exponents.
The images of the remaining exponents admit no nonzero positive dependence with nonnegative real coefficients.
Indeed such a dependence could be chosen rational, because the quotient space, the vectors and their linear constraints are rational: intersect the dependence cone with the simplex on which the coefficients sum to one and take a vertex.
Clearing denominators would give a nonzero nonnegative integer combination of late exponents equal to a fixed integer combination of chosen exponents in the lattice, allowing another common denominator for the chosen side.
Evaluating along the sequence gives at least \(H_i\) on the late side and \(O(D_i)\) on the chosen side.
Thus the convex hull of the finitely many projected late exponents avoids zero, so strict linear separation and rational approximation give a single integral functional vanishing on the chosen exponents and positive on every remaining one.
Jensen again gives \(M(P)=m\).
In the proof below use \(t_i/H_i\to0\) in place of \(t_i/h_i\to0\), since every test of integrality lies strictly inside the new prefix.
If some lower or reflected exponents are much larger than \(H_i\), they play no further role in these coefficient tests.

<a id="division"></a>
## Real integrable quotients at a vertex of extremal Mahler measure

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\) be primitive, with constant \(m>0\) at a vertex and \(M(P)=m\).
For any \(H\in\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\), if \(H/P\) is real almost everywhere and belongs to \(L^1\) on the torus, then \(P\) divides \(H\) in \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\).
The result is immediate for \(H=0\) or \(P=1\).
Choose a primitive integral functional \(\ell\) strictly positive at all other exponents and taking distinct values on the support of \(P\).
Use a basis splitting \(\ell\), and write \(P=P(X,z)\) with \(X\) on the kernel torus and \(z\) in the exposed direction.
For fixed \(X\) it has constant \(m\) and leading coefficient equal to a nonzero constant times a character of \(X\).
Its one-variable measure is continuous in \(X\), bounded below by \(m\), and has mean log measure \(\log m\).
It therefore has no root in the open disk for any \(X\), by equality in Jensen's formula.

For almost every \(X\), the rational function \(H(X,z)/P(X,z)\) is real almost everywhere on the circle and its absolute value is integrable there.
Its nonzero poles are invariant under \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\), since reality gives equality with its conjugate reciprocal as rational functions.
It has no pole in \(0<|z|<1\) by the slice root condition, hence none outside the circle either.
A genuine pole on the circle would give divergent one-dimensional absolute integral.
Thus this rational function is Laurent polynomial for almost every \(X\).
Clear a common Laurent power of \(z\) from \(H\) before division.
Only a monomial in \(X\) times a nonzero scalar occurs as the leading coefficient of \(P\) in \(z\), so the division remainder over \(\mathbb Q(X)\) has Laurent polynomial coefficients over \(\mathbb Q\).
Since \(P(X,0)=m\ne0\), the remainder vanishes on each of these almost-everywhere slices.
Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients makes it zero over \(\mathbb Q(X)\), giving a quotient with rational Laurent coefficients in all variables.
Primitivity and Gauss's lemma then give integral Laurent coefficients.

## Removing the full-dimensional part of every positive row

Use the [actual inverse measures](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v13.md#integrality) of \(J_i\) in \(B_i^\sharp=(\Pi_i/j)J_i\).
They are symmetric signed measures of mass one with negative mass tending to zero, and their doubled moments at positive indices equal the corresponding Taylor coefficients.
Push these measures to the base torus by evaluating its characters at \(\bar v^{\lambda_i(\cdot)}\), where \(v\) denotes the original circle variable, and fix a weak probability limit \(\mu\).
With \(\widehat\mu(g)=\int X^{-g}\,d\mu\), form the ordered coefficient sequence \(J(g)=2\widehat\mu(g)\) at positive \(g\), one at \(g=0\), and zero otherwise.
Then \(B=(P/m)J\) is integral by testing \(B_i^\sharp\) at \(\lambda_i(g)\) in the common prefix, with the constant and negative indices given by the order.
The measure \(\mu\) cannot be Haar: that choice would give \(B=P/m\), which has a fractional coefficient.

For any \(D_i/t_i,t_i/h_i\to0\), let \(\Omega\) be a further joint limit of the base variables and \(u=v^{t_i}\).
It projects to \(\mu\).
For \(k>0\) the projection \(\nu_k\) of \((2P/m)u^k\Omega\) onto the base has integer Fourier coefficients by evaluating \(B_i^\sharp\) at \(kt_i+\lambda_i(g)\) for every fixed \(g\).
The transform of \(2|P|^2\Omega\) is integral: on positive layers multiply \(\nu_k\) by \(mP^*\), on negative layers conjugate, and on layer zero use \(m(P^*B+PB^*)\) from \(J+J^*=2\widehat\mu\).
Here star denotes conjugate reversal.

Apply the [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) on these finite-rank discrete lattices.
Its coset decomposition yields trigonometric polynomial densities on finitely many torsion translates of connected subtori, with nonnegative densities for each distinct component of the positive measure.
The full-dimensional density of each \(\nu_k\) is integer Laurent, since only singleton cosets contribute there.
Away from \(P=0\) divide the positive decomposition by \(2|P|^2\).
Mass above \(P=0\) contributes zero to every weighted row \(\nu_k\), and its projection lies on a Haar-null base set.
Any proper component projecting onto the whole base has an equation \(u^b=\xi X^a\) with \(b>0\) and \(\xi\) torsion, since its annihilator then has rank one.
Grouping by \(a/b\) and taking high harmonics through multiples of all \(b\,\operatorname{ord}\xi\) gives an integer Laurent polynomial
\[
                         H=(2P/m) f
\]
for each grouped nonnegative marginal density \(f\).
Indeed the full-torus term vanishes at high harmonics, and each remaining slope is separated from the others by Fourier translates tending to infinity.
Taking one Fourier coefficient at a time gives integrality by Riemann–Lebesgue, and a second application to \(Pf\in L^1\) gives finite support, as in the [slope calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction).
Now \(H/P=2f/m\) is real and integrable, so the division result makes it integer Laurent.
If \(f\ne0\), its integral is at least \(m/2\).
For \(m>2\) this exceeds total probability, and for \(m=2\) it exhausts the marginal.
In the latter case \(f\) is an integer Laurent probability density, so strict triangle inequality makes each nonconstant Fourier coefficient an integer of modulus less than one.
This would give the excluded Haar marginal.

With the graph components gone, all full-dimensional positive rows come from the full torus, where division by a base function preserves finite harmonic support in \(u\).
A last nonzero positive row would have integer Laurent density \(H_k\) with \(|H_k|\le |P|f_0/m\), where \(f_0\) is the Haar density of the absolutely continuous part of \(\mu\).
The factor \(1/2\) on the conditional moment follows by the [last harmonic positivity argument](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction): the \(k\)-th power pushforward has only constant and first harmonics.
By Jensen the geometric mean of \(f_0\) is strictly below one, since equality would give Haar marginal.
Thus \(M(H_k)<M(P)/m=1\), impossible for a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial.

## What the vanishing interval leaves open

Every \(\nu_k\) is now supported on the fixed finite union of proper torsion translates from the decomposition of \(2|P|^2\mu\), since it vanishes on \(P=0\) and away from that set is absolutely continuous with respect to this weighted marginal.
For each such component choose a nonzero character \(v\) equal to one on it, taking a multiple of an annihilator to kill the torsion translation.
The product \(U_i=\prod_v(1-z^{|\lambda_i(v)|})\) has the required sizes and is nonzero by stabilization.
Its signs in the absolute values are eventually fixed.
At any intermediate index \(t_i\) the coefficient of \(U_i B_i^\sharp\) tends to zero, since each fixed shift tests a Fourier coefficient of \(\nu_1\) and the corresponding product of character differences vanishes on its support.
Integrality then gives exact zero.
If no fixed \(L\) worked, a diagonal sequence of violating indices would satisfy \(D_i/t_i,t_i/h_i\to0\), a contradiction.
This proves the asserted interval.
The argument gives no uniform absolute sum for the earlier coefficients up to \(LD_i\).
That sum enters the return to bounded support in the [eight-term comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v99.md#return).
