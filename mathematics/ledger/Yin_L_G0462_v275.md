# Overlapping cyclotomic factors and simultaneous double contacts at consecutive powers

<a id="rate"></a>
## The endpoint contraction for powers of one unit

Let \(\delta\) be a reciprocal nontorsion algebraic unit such that \(\mathbb Q(\delta^k)=\mathbb Q(\delta)\) for every positive integer \(k\).
Write \(P_a\) for the minimum of \(\delta^a\) and \(h=\log M(P_1)>0\), so \(\log M(P_a)=ah\).
Fix a finite nonempty set \(I\) of positive integers, multiplicities \(t_a\ge1\), and \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\).
Put \(F=\prod_{a\in I}P_a^{t_a}\) and let \(\mathcal D\) be the set of orders of the roots of the polynomials \(Y^a-\sigma\), \(a\in I\).
Define
\[
    m_d=\max\{t_a:\Phi_d\mid Y^a-\sigma,\ a\in I\},\qquad
    K_\sigma(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\varphi(d)m_d .
\]
At a rational prime \(\ell\nmid 2n\), normalize the local valuation by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).
Let \(v_\ell(B_n)=\max_{z^n=\sigma}v_\ell(F(z))\) away from the finitely many additional primes specified below, and set it to zero at every discarded prime.
Then
\[
                      \log B_n=\tfrac12 nh K_\sigma(t)+o(n).                 \tag{1}
\]
This extends the [signed endpoint calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#argument) to powers with repeated cyclotomic factors in their node polynomials.
The research contributor for Sheng Yin derived (1) and its double-contact consequence below for the [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-19.

Take one representative \(x\) from each inverse pair of conjugates of \(\delta\).
Two different representatives are multiplicatively independent modulo torsion.
Indeed equal positive absolute heights force the absolute exponents of any primitive dependence to agree, so either a product or a quotient is torsion.
The all-power field condition excludes a torsion quotient of distinct conjugates, also after inversion.
Let \(L\) be the splitting field of \(P_1\) and \(M=\operatorname{lcm}_{d\in\mathcal D}d\).
Discard the primes dividing \(2nM\), the discriminant of the squarefree product \(\prod_{a\in I}P_a\), \(F(1)F(-1)\), and the primes ramified in \(L\).
The powered minima in this product have distinct measures and every one has the original degree.
Their roots therefore have pairwise distinct reductions at every retained prime.

For a prime \(\mathfrak l\) of \(L\) above a retained \(\ell\), put \(w_{x,d}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}\Phi_d(x^n)\).
The distinct cyclotomic factors of \(Y^M-1\) have disjoint reductions there, so at most one \(d\) has \(w_{x,d}>0\) for fixed \(x\).
If \(\Phi_d\mid Y^a-\sigma\) for that \(d\), then \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak l}(x^{an}-\sigma)=w_{x,d}\).
All \(n\)-th roots of \(\sigma\) have distinct residues, so this valuation comes from the unique node \(z\) reducing to \(x^a\).
The distinct reductions of the roots of the squarefree product give \(v_\ell(F(z))=t_a w_{x,d}\), since \(L\) is unramified here.
Inverting \(x\) preserves \(w_{x,d}\) because \(\Phi_d\) is reciprocal up to sign.
Conversely every positive node valuation arises from a root of that product, proving the exact local identity
\[
                        v_\ell(B_n)=\max_{x,d}m_d w_{x,d}.                  \tag{2}
\]
The maximum is interpreted as zero when every valuation vanishes.

Weight \(\mathfrak l\) by \(\log N\mathfrak l/[L:\mathbb Q]\), so the weighted sum of the left side of (2) is \(\log B_n\).
For distinct representatives \(x,y\), the common valuation of any two factors \(\Phi_d(x^n),\Phi_e(y^n)\) is bounded by that of \(x^{Mn}-1,y^{Mn}-1\).
The normalized logarithmic norm of this latter gcd is \(o(n)\) by [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](../audits/literature/CZ05/Yin_U001.md#s-unit-gcd), since the two units are independent and have heights \(O(n)\).
Thus replacing the maximum in (2) by the sum costs \(o(n)\) after summation, using the pairwise minima to bound the excess.
The [exponent-prime estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#bad) applied to \(x^{2Mn}-1\) absorbs the discarded primes with the same error.
For each \(d\), the complete weighted sum of \(w_{x,d}\) over the representatives and finite primes equals half the logarithm of \(\lvert\operatorname{Res}(P_1,\Phi_d(X^n))\rvert\).
Möbius inversion of the [fixed signed resultant estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v42.md#substitution) at exponents \(en\), \(e\mid d\), gives \(nh\varphi(d)+O_{P_1,d}(\log(2n))\) for this resultant logarithm.
Together these estimates give (1).

## A separate polynomial for each nonzero value

Use \(I=\{1,\ldots,r\}\) and \(\sigma=-1\).
Write
\[
       T=\frac{r(r+1)}2,\qquad K=\sum_{a=1}^r\varphi(2a),\qquad D=T-K.
\]
Indeed the orders occurring in the union of the roots of \(Y^a+1\) are exactly \(2j\) with \(1\le j\le r\), since \(j\mid a\) with odd quotient characterizes an order \(2j\) root of \(Y^a+1\).
Thus \(K\) is the degree of the least common multiple of these polynomials and \(D\) is the degree lost by merging repeated factors.
With \(B=(\log2)/4\), suppose
\[
                                (2T+2D)h<B.                                  \tag{3}
\]
Then at either prescribed sign a single row of the [fixed-double-factor test](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#statement) realizes \(h,2h,\ldots,rh\) at every sufficiently large \(n\).
One fixed width suffices for all such \(n\), and the result extends to every prescribed sequence of widths tending to infinity with \(\log b=o(n)\).

Fix an index \(a\), put \(k=\lfloor 2D/a\rfloor\), and prescribe order \(2+k\) at \(P_a\) and order two at each other minimum.
The [integral two-row interpolation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) gives a nonzero graph \(U(X^n)+XV(X^n)\) at a sufficiently large fixed width \(w\) with
\[
             2\log\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)/n\le (2T+ka)h+\eta
\]
for any prescribed \(\eta>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).
The same sufficiently large \(w\) works for the finitely many choices of \(a\).
Let \(Q_a\) be the minimum of \(\delta^{an}\), of logarithmic Mahler measure \(anh\).
Suppose \(Q_a^{k+1}\) divides both rows.
Dividing by that power leaves a nonzero graph with at least one contact order at \(P_a\) and two at every other \(P_j\), since substitution by \(X^n\) preserves orders at nonzero roots and the powered minima are distinct.
For this remaining product (1) has \(K_-(t)\ge 2K-a\), since only factors of \(Y^a+1\) can lose one unit of multiplicity.
The [inverse-node argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#argument) gives a quotient row of log one-norm at least \(nh(2K-a)/2-o(n)\).
Indeed dividing out the common power of \(Y+1\) and evaluating at a maximizing inverse pair forces \(B_n\) to divide a nonzero divided derivative at \(-1\), whose magnitude is at most \(2^w\) times the row norm.
The log Mahler measure of that quotient row is within \(w\log2\) of its log one-norm.
Restoring \(Q_a^{k+1}\) shows that the doubled original log norm divided by \(n\) is at least \((2K-a+2(k+1)a)h-o(1)\).
This exceeds \((2T+ka)h\) by \(((k+1)a-2D)h>0\).
Choose \(\eta\) below all these finitely many gaps and below \(B-(2T+2D)h\).

The two rows thus have at most \(k\) common copies of \(Q_a\).
Remove just these copies, leaving all the double factors and forcing both row values to be nonzero at \(\delta^{an}\) by the graph equation.
The one-norm increases by at most \(2^w\), by multiplicativity of Mahler measure and the coefficient bound at degree \(w\).
The [two signed projections](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v419.md#retention) now give a row \(A_a\) of width \(w+1\) and the prescribed sign which retains all double factors and is nonzero at \(\delta^{an}\), at bounded further norm cost.
For each \(j\le r\) the polynomial in the integer parameter \(s\) given by \(\sum_{a=1}^r s^{a-1}A_a(\delta^{jn})\) is nonzero, since its coefficient at \(a=j\) is nonzero.
Among \(0,\ldots,r(r-1)\) choose one \(s\) avoiding the roots of all \(r\) polynomials.
The corresponding linear combination is one row at the prescribed sign with all the double factors and all the nonzero values.
Its extra norm factor is bounded in terms of \(r\), and \(2T+ka\le 2T+2D\) for every witness.
The strict slack in (3) proves the passing budget at fixed width, and [width padding](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v423.md#construction) gives the stated larger widths.

## Count exclusions and the first overlap

If the count stays below \(r\) on any infinite subsequence of the indicated growing widths, (3) gives the unrestricted bound \(\lambda\ge B/(4T-2K)\).
Indeed a smaller infimum supplies a positive irreducible value \(h<B/(4T-2K)\) by factorization, with a reciprocal unit [all-power representative](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v421.md#norm) by the [small-measure reduction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v420.md#upper).
The denominators for \(r=1,2,3,4,5\) are \(2,6,14,22,34\), respectively.
For example the [dyadic construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#tests) gives \(30\) for four values.
The separate witnesses give all four consecutive multiples with denominator \(22\).

For \(I=\{1,2,3\}\) and all multiplicities two, \(\sigma=-1\) has support \(\Phi_2,\Phi_4,\Phi_6\), with \(\Phi_2\) shared between the first and third levels.
The resulting contraction has rate \(5h\), while \(\sigma=1\) gives \(4h\) from \(\Phi_1,\Phi_2,\Phi_3\).
The interpolation upper rate is \(6h\).
For the negative endpoint, removing one copy of either \(P_1\) or \(P_2\) and restoring its powered minimum gives a lower bound with rate exactly \(6h\) in each case.
Consequently that single comparison does not force both corresponding numerators to be nonzero at the interpolation rate.
The optimal coefficient rate for these three prescribed original minima remains open here.

At odd \(n\) the three values have representatives \(\delta,\delta^2,-\delta^3\) that do attain upper rate \(6h\) with all numerators nonzero.
At the negative endpoint their node polynomials for the base value \(Y=x^n\) are \(Y+1,Y^2+1,Y^3-1\), whose root sets are pairwise disjoint.
Within matched powers \(a,b\) write \(g=\gcd(a,b)\) and \(\xi_c=1,1,-1\) at \(c=1,2,3\), respectively.
The primitive relation for \(\xi_a x^a,\xi_b x^b\) has phase \(\xi_a^{b/g}\xi_b^{-a/g}\).
At odd \(n\) it equals \(-1\) for the pair \(1,3\), whose exponent sum is even, and \(+1\) for the other two pairs, whose exponent sums are odd.
All other inverse-pair representatives come from independent base conjugates, and powering is injective by the all-power condition and distinct absolute heights.
The [sharp signed-row theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v427.md#statement) therefore gives rate \(6h\) for either graph sign, and an infinite odd-exponent exclusion of three values implies \(\lambda\ge B/12\).
