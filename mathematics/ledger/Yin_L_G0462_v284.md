# Testing two residue-order criteria on the same primitive tetranomial

<a id="exponents"></a>
## Long exponents with small orders at both primes

For the reciprocal tetranomial
\[
             F=X^{n+k}-2(X^n+X^k)+1,\qquad D=n+k ,
\]
the [Frobenius-order criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v563.md#statement) tests the odd part of \(D\) at two and the prime-to-three part of \(n\) at three.
There are primitive pairs with \(k\to\infty\) and \(n/k^2\to1\) for which the least prime divisor of each of \(D,n\) tends to infinity and
\[
               \operatorname{ord}_{D}(2)/D\to0,\qquad
               \operatorname{ord}_{n}(3)/n\to0 .
\]
They give a test family for a method that also uses the allocation of exterior roots among rational factors.

For an integer \(j\ge5\), take an odd prime \(a>j\), set \(M=(3^a-1)/2\), take a prime \(b>M\), and set \(J=2^b-1\).
Every prime divisor of \(M\) is odd and has order \(a\) for the base three, because \(M\) is odd, \(a\) is prime and \(3-1=2\).
Every prime divisor of \(J\) has order \(b\) for the base two.
Thus \(M,J\) are coprime and their prime divisors exceed \(j\).
Let \(P_j\) be the product of primes at most \(j\) and take a power of two \(k>MJP_j\).
For each odd prime \(p\le j\) prescribe a residue for \(n\) avoiding \(0,-k\bmod p\), and prescribe an odd residue at two.
The Chinese remainder theorem solves these conditions together with \(n\equiv0\bmod M\) and \(n\equiv-k\bmod J\) in a single class modulo \(MJP_j\).
Take the first solution \(n\ge k^2\), giving \(k^2\le n<k^2+k\).
Then \(\gcd(n,k)=1\) and both \(n,D\) avoid every prime at most \(j\).
Reduction from units modulo \(n\) to units modulo \(M\) has kernel of size at most \(n/M\), so \(\operatorname{ord}_n(3)/n\le a/M\).
Likewise \(\operatorname{ord}_D(2)/D\le b/J\).
Both upper bounds tend to zero.

Every such parent has a real root \(r>2^{1/k}\), since substituting \(2^{1/k}\) gives \(-3\) and the leading coefficient is positive.
For this root the identity \(r^n(r^k-2)=2r^k-1\) gives \(r^k=2+3/(r^n-2)\) at sufficiently large \(n/k\).
Hence \(r^k\to2\) while \(r\to1\).
The [individual factor height law](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v555.md#binomial) still requires the rational factor carrying a candidate small measure to have degree \(o(D)\).
Locating this real root and making the two residue-order ratios small does not determine that degree.

## An exact factorization with two small orders

For a finite comparison take \(k=64,\ n=4927=13\cdot379,\ D=4991=7\cdot23\cdot31\).
The orders are \(\operatorname{ord}_{4927}(3)=378\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_{4991}(2)=165\).
For all four choices \(a,c=\pm1\), the polynomial \(X^D+2a(X^n+cX^k)+c\) has the linear factor \(X+c\) and one noncyclotomic rational irreducible factor of degree \(4990\), both simple.
A collaborating agent for Sheng Yin computed the complete integer factorizations with python-flint 0.9.0 and verified the products and cyclotomic indices.
The following code reproduces the four factorizations and the order calculations.

```python
from itertools import product
from flint import fmpz_poly
from sympy import n_order

x = fmpz_poly([0, 1])
assert n_order(3, 4927) == 378 and n_order(2, 4991) == 165
for a, c in product((-1, 1), repeat=2):
    parent = x**4991 + 2*a*(x**4927 + c*x**64) + c
    unit, factors = parent.factor()
    reconstructed = fmpz_poly([unit])
    for factor, multiplicity in factors:
        reconstructed *= factor**multiplicity
    assert reconstructed == parent
    assert (x + c, 1) in factors
    assert sorted((f.degree(), e, f.is_cyclotomic()) for f, e in factors) == [
        (1, 1, 2 if c == 1 else 1), (4990, 1, 0)
    ]
```
