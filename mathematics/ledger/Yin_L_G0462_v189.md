# An integral trace residue for the centered derivative of a reciprocal row

<a id="odd"></a>
## Odd spans and the norm of the derivative remainder

Let \(P\) be a monic reciprocal nontorsion unit minimal polynomial of degree \(d=2m\), with root \(\alpha\), and let an odd prime \(p\) preserve the degree of \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Write \(q=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\), \(\delta=\alpha-\alpha^{-1}\), and \(P(x)=x^m H(x+x^{-1})\), where \(H\) is the monic minimal polynomial of \(q\).
Set \(R=\mathbb Z[q]\) and take norms \(N\) below from \(\mathbb Q(q)\) to \(\mathbb Q\).
The order \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) is \(R\oplus\alpha R\) since \(\alpha^2=q\alpha-1\), \(\alpha\) is a unit and \([\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q(q)]=2\).

Let \(A\in\mathbb Z[X]\) have degree \(n\) and nonzero constant term, choose \(\sigma=\pm1\), and put \(J=pn+S\) with \(S>0\) prime to \(p\).
Suppose \(P^2\) divides \(F(x)=A(x^p)+\sigma x^J A(x^{-p})\).
The algebraic integer
\[
 W=2p\beta A'(\beta)-J A(\beta)\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]
\]
is \(2p(1+\sigma\alpha^J)z\) in the [centered derivative formula](Yin_L_G0462_v186.md#freedom).
For odd \(J=2k+1\) there are unique \(u,v\in R\) with
\[
 \alpha^{-k} A(\beta)=(1-\sigma\alpha)u,\qquad
 \alpha^{-k}W=(1+\sigma\alpha)v .
\]
Let \(U_0(T)\in\mathbb Z[T]\) of degree \(<m\) represent \(u\).
Then the centered derivative satisfies the integral congruence
\[
 D=v+u-2(2\sigma-q)U_0'(q)\ \in\ 2(2\sigma-q) H'(q)R .                \tag{1}
\]
Whenever \(D\ne0\), its absolute norm is a positive multiple of
\[
                   2^m\,|P(\sigma)\operatorname{disc} H|.
\]
Indeed \(N(2\sigma-q)=H(2\sigma)\), \(|H(2\sigma)|=|P(\sigma)|\), and \(|N(H'(q))|=|\operatorname{disc}H|\).

Put \(t=x+x^{-1}\) and expand the integral Laurent polynomial \(L=x^{-k}A(x^p)\) uniquely as \(U(t)+xV(t)\) over \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
This uses \(x^{-1}=t-x\) and \(x^2=tx-1\), with uniqueness by inversion over \(\mathbb Q(t)\).
The diagonal is \(x^k(1+\sigma x)(U+\sigma V)(t)\).
At \(\alpha\) the linear factor is nonzero and \(dt/dx=1-x^{-2}\ne0\).
Therefore \(H^2\mid U+\sigma V\) by irreducibility and monicity of \(H\).
We have \(L\equiv(1-\sigma x)U(t)\bmod H(t)^2\), whence \(u=U(q)\).
Applying \(2x\,d/dx-1\) at \(\alpha\) gives \(\alpha^{-k}W\) on the left, and
\[
 v=-u+2(2\sigma-q)U'(q)
\]
on the right because \((1-\sigma x)(x-x^{-1})/(1+\sigma x)=2\sigma-t\).
The two displayed factors \(1\pm\sigma\alpha\) are nonzero.
Writing \(U=U_0+HB\) gives \(U'(q)-U_0'(q)=H'(q)B(q)\), proving (1).

In addition \(A(\beta)\in p\mathbb Z[\alpha]\).
In fact \(x F'(x)\) vanishes at \(\alpha\), and reduction modulo \(p\) leaves only \(\sigma J\alpha^J A(\alpha^{-p})=-J A(\beta)\).
As \(p\nmid J\), this proves the divisibility, and the definition gives \(W\in p\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) as well.
Multiplying by the unit \(\alpha^{-k}\) and comparing the \(\alpha\) coefficients in \(R\oplus\alpha R\) yields \(u,v\in pR\) for odd \(J\).

<a id="even"></a>
## Even spans and both reciprocal signs

Let \(J=2k\) and write \(L=U(t)+xV(t)\) as above.
For \(\sigma=-1\) we have \(x^{-k}F=(x-x^{-1})V(t)\), so \(H^2\mid V\).
Put \(u=\alpha^{-k}A(\beta)=U(q)\), and let \(U_0\) be its reduced representative.
Applying \(2x\,d/dx\) to \(L\) at \(\alpha\) gives
\[
 \frac{\alpha^{-k}W}{2\delta}=U'(q),\qquad
 \frac{\alpha^{-k}W}{2\delta}-U_0'(q)\ \in\ H'(q)R .                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\delta\ne0\), and a nonzero remainder in (2) has absolute norm divisible by \(|\operatorname{disc}H|\).

For \(\sigma=1\) the diagonal gives \(H^2\mid 2U+tV\).
Thus \(2L\equiv (x-x^{-1})V(t)\bmod H(t)^2\).
Put \(b=V(q)\), so \(2\alpha^{-k}A(\beta)=\delta b\), and let \(V_0\) be the reduced representative of \(b\).
Applying \(x\,d/dx\) to \(2L\) at \(\alpha\) gives
\[
 \alpha^{-k}W=q b+(q^2-4)V'(q),\qquad
 \alpha^{-k}W-q b-(q^2-4)V_0'(q)\ \in\ (q^2-4)H'(q)R .              \tag{3}
\]
A nonzero remainder in (3) has absolute norm divisible by \(|P(1)P(-1)\operatorname{disc}H|\).
Equations (1)–(3) hold in the trace order at every prime.

## A nonzero centered derivative with zero odd-span remainder

For every input \(P,p\) above and either sign there are primitive integral witnesses with coprime rows, \(S=1\), \(n=2d\), \(z\ne0\) and \(D=0\) in (1).
Let \(Q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\).
With the [powered orders on two multiplicity layers](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#layers), let \(b>0\) be the additive order of \(x\) in
\[
 \bigl(\mathbb Z[x]/(P^2)\bigr)\big/\bigl(\mathbb Z[X]/(Q^2)\bigr),
 \qquad X=x^p .
\]
This quotient is finite, and the [constant-row representation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#denominator) gives \(b\mid p s^3\), where \(s\) is the exponent of \(\mathbb Z[\alpha]/\mathbb Z[\beta]\).
Moreover \(p\mid b\), since differentiating a congruence \(G(x^p)\equiv b x\bmod P^2\) modulo \(p\) gives \(\overline P\mid b\), and \(\overline P\) is monic of positive degree.
Since \(X^d\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(Q^2)\), the same \(b\) is the least positive integer for which \(b x^{pd}(1-\sigma x)\) belongs to the powered suborder.
Choose its integral representative \(T(X)\) of degree \(<2d\).

For \(M\in\mathbb Z\) put \(A_M=M Q^2+T\), and set \(k=pd\), \(J=2k+1\).
In the Laurent quotient by \(P^2\), \(A_M(x^p)\equiv b x^k(1-\sigma x)\).
That right side cancels identically with its signed reversal about degree \(J\), and reciprocity preserves the ideal \((P^2)\).
Thus \(P^2\mid F_M\).
Evaluation and one differentiation give \(u=b,\ v=-b\), so \(D=0\) while \(W=-b\alpha^k(1+\sigma\alpha)\ne0\).
The row value \(b\alpha^k(1-\sigma\alpha)\) is also nonzero, and \(1+\sigma\alpha^J\ne0\) shows \(z\ne0\).

For \(M\ne0\) the degree is \(2d\), and its constant term \(M+T(0)\) vanishes for at most one \(M\), since \(Q(0)=1\).
The diagonal then has nonzero endpoints and degree \(2pd+1\).
Every common root of \(A_M\) and \(X^{2d}A_M(1/X)\) must be a zero of \(T-X^{2d}T(1/X)\), since \(Q^2\) is reciprocal.
The difference at \(\beta\) is \(-\sigma b\beta^d(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})\ne0\), so there are only finitely many candidates.
No zero of \(Q\) is a zero of \(T\), by the row value at \(\beta\) and conjugacy.
Every remaining candidate excludes at most one \(M\), which proves coprimality outside a finite set.

In fact each \(A_M\) is primitive.
If a positive integer \(g\) divides its coefficients, \(\alpha^{-k}A_M(\beta)/g=(b/g)(1-\sigma\alpha)\) lies in \(R\oplus\alpha R\).
Comparing coefficients gives \(b/g\in R\cap\mathbb Q=\mathbb Z\).
Now \(A_M/g\) represents \((b/g)x^k(1-\sigma x)\) in the powered suborder, so minimality of \(b\) forces \(g=1\).
The two classes are disjoint, hence the diagonal is primitive as well.
These witnesses lie in the [fourth-logarithmic degree cap](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v363.md#statement) whenever the prime lies in that window.
Thus a norm argument using (1) needs an independent reason to exclude \(D=0\) for witnesses of low measure, or a separate estimate for those witnesses.
The construction establishes this obstruction without a bound on its cofactor measure.
