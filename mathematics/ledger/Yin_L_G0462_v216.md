# Distribution of the exterior roots among quartic pole lifts

<a id="allocation"></a>
## An allocation bound for every divisor of the first exponent

Put \(g(T)=T^4-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^3-T^4,\ F(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\) for coprime positive \(a,b\), and let \(r=b/a\).
Write \(-\beta<-1\) for the [unique exterior zero of \(B\)](Yin_L_G0462_v205.md#quartic).
For all sufficiently large \(r\) there is one exterior root near each \(a\)-th root \(\xi_{a,j}=\beta^{1/a}\exp((2j+1)\pi i/a)\) of \(-\beta\), \(0\le j<a\).
Let \(P\) be any monic nontorsion rational irreducible factor of \(F\), \(d=\deg P\), and let \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\) be the indices of its \(k\) exterior roots.
For any \(q\mid a\) put \(k_c=\#\{j\in S:j\equiv c\bmod q\}\) and
\[
 D_P=1+\log^+\!\left(\frac{d}{|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}}\right),
 \qquad \epsilon=r^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}{d}},
 \qquad \log^+ t=\max(0,\log t).
\]
Uniformly in \(a,b,P,q\) one has
\[
 \sum_{c\bmod q}\left(k_c-\frac{k}{q}\right)^2
       \ll k D_P+\frac{k^2}{q}\epsilon.                                \tag{1}
\]
The logarithmic loss \(D_P\) is at most \(1+\log d\).
The estimate uses the *same* polynomial discriminant at every divisor \(q\).

<a id="powers"></a>
## The pole branches and the integral orders of their projections

Fix a lift \(\xi_{a,j}\) and write \(X=\xi_{a,j}e^{u/a}\), so \(X^a=-\beta e^u\).
The section equation becomes
\[
 B(-\beta e^u)+\xi_{a,j}^{-b}e^{-ru}g(-\beta e^u)=0 .
\]
On \(|u|=K\beta^{-r}\) the first term has modulus \(\gg K\beta^{-r}\) by the simplicity of the pole.
The second has modulus \(O(\beta^{-r})\) for fixed \(K\) and all sufficiently large \(r\), since \(rK\beta^{-r}\to0\).
Taking \(K\) sufficiently large, Rouché's theorem supplies one simple exterior root for each lift.
The neighborhoods are disjoint because their arguments have radius \(O(\beta^{-r}/a)\), and the [quartic circle count](Yin_L_G0462_v210.md#phase) accounts for exactly \(a\) exterior roots.
In particular, for each \(\alpha\) at index \(j\) and each \(q\mid a\),
\[
       \alpha^{a/q}=\xi_{q,j}e^{u/q},\qquad |u|\le K\beta^{-r}.         \tag{2}
\]

For every \(m\mid a\) the equality of orders \(\mathbb Z[\alpha^m]=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) holds for every root \(\alpha\) of \(F\), even at small slopes.
Indeed \(\alpha\) and \(t=\alpha^a\) are units, and \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,g)|=1\) by the [quartic unit calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v211.md#sign) at \(T^4+T^3-1=0\).
Evaluating \(F\) at the roots of \(B(X^a)\) gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(B(X^a),F)|=1\), so \(B(t)\) is an algebraic unit.
Its inverse belongs to \(\mathbb Z[B(t)]\), hence to \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
The section equation gives \(\alpha^b=-g(t)/B(t)\in\mathbb Z[t]\), and \(t,\alpha^b\) both have their inverses there because they too are algebraic units.
Bézout in \(a,b\) now gives \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z[t]\).
The inclusions \(\mathbb Z[t]\subseteq\mathbb Z[\alpha^m]\subseteq\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) prove the assertion.
The minimal polynomial \(P_m\) of \(\alpha^m\) therefore has degree \(d\), has as its distinct roots all the \(m\)-th powers of the roots of \(P\), and has discriminant \(\operatorname{disc}P\) by the power bases of the common order.

By [eventual reciprocity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#trace), \(P\) and its power polynomials are reciprocal at all sufficiently large primitive slopes.
Equation (2) gives \(m(P)=k(\log\beta+O(\beta^{-r}))/a\), where \(m(P)\) denotes logarithmic Mahler measure.
The [individual height estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(bm(P)/d=\log\beta+O(\epsilon)\), because \(|g|=|B|\) on the circle and \(m(B)=\log\beta\).
Thus
\[
             d=kr(1+O(\epsilon)),\qquad
             m(P_{a/q})=\frac{k}{q}\bigl(\log\beta+O(\beta^{-r})\bigr).  \tag{3}
\]

<a id="discriminant"></a>
## The discriminant cost of several roots at the same projected pole

For the roots \(z_i\) of \(P_{a/q}\), let \(w_i=z_i/\max(1,|z_i|)^2\).
Divide \(|z_i-z_j|\) by \(\max(1,|z_i|)\max(1,|z_j|)\).
For two interior roots or two exterior roots the quotient equals \(|w_i-w_j|\), and for a pair on opposite sides it equals \(|1-w_i\bar w_j|\).
The latter expression also applies whenever one root is on the circle.
For same-side pairs retain the full ratio
\[
               \delta_{ij}=\frac{|w_i-w_j|}{|1-w_i\bar w_j|}\le1
\]
from the [reflected discriminant calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v108.md#phases).
The common product \(\prod_{i<j}|1-w_i\bar w_j|^2\) is at most \(d^d\) by the [disk maximum and boundary Vandermonde bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v280.md#reflection).
Indeed, with the other variables fixed, the factors involving one variable have product equal to the squared modulus of a polynomial in that variable.
Successive maximization on each boundary reduces to the square of a circle Vandermonde determinant, bounded by \(d^d\) by Hadamard.
Using the product formula for the polynomial discriminant gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\\text{both exterior or both interior}}}
       -\log\delta_{ij}
 \ \le\ d\log d-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|
                         +2(d-1)m(P_{a/q}).                              \tag{4}
\]

If two exterior roots occur at the same class \(c\bmod q\), (2) gives \(|z_i-z_j|\ll\beta^{-r}/q\).
Also \(|z_i\bar z_j-1|\ge |z_i||z_j|-1\gg1/q\), since each \(\log|z_i|=(\log\beta+O(\beta^{-r}))/q\).
Their folded ratio \(\delta_{ij}=|z_i-z_j|/|z_i\bar z_j-1|\) is therefore \(O(\beta^{-r})\) with a constant independent of \(q\).
The interior roots \(1/\bar z_i,1/\bar z_j\) give exactly the same ratio.
Thus (4) implies, with an absolute constant \(C\),
\[
     2(r\log\beta-C)\sum_c k_c(k_c-1)
       \le d\log d-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|+2(d-1)m(P_{a/q}).         \tag{5}
\]
Substituting (3) on the right and dividing by \(2(r\log\beta-C)\) bounds \(\sum_c k_c(k_c-1)\) by \(O(kD_P)+(k^2/q)(1+O(\epsilon))\).
Adding \(k-k^2/q\) proves (1).

## Restrictions on a shrinking allocation

The [exterior allocation criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#criterion) requires \(k=o(a)\) and \(r\to\infty\) for a shrinking sequence.
If \(q=q(a)\mid a\) and \(qD_P/k\to0\) along such a sequence, (1) gives
\[
         \frac1q\sum_{c\bmod q}
                  \left(\frac{k_c}{k/q}-1\right)^2\longrightarrow0 .
\]
In particular the factor occupies \((1-o(1))q\) residue classes.
For fixed \(q\) every class has \(k_c=(1+o(1))k/q\) whenever \(k/D_P\to\infty\).
Conversely, if the allocation misses at least \(\eta q\) classes for a fixed \(0<\eta<1\), their contribution alone is at least \(\eta k^2/q\).
Consequently \(k\ll_\eta qD_P\) at all sufficiently large slopes, and if \(k/q\to\infty\) the polynomial root discriminant satisfies \(|\operatorname{disc}P|^{1/d}\le d\exp(-c_\eta k/q)\) eventually for some \(c_\eta>0\).
For example, an allocation restricted to one parity at even \(a\) has \(k\ll\log(2d)\).
Allocations already balanced modulo the useful divisors, bounded exterior counts, and prime \(a\) admit no general positive lower bound on \(k/a\) from this estimate.
