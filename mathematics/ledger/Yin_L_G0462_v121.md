# The binomial second profiles give only neutral inputs near the dyadic Pisot limit

<a id="recurrence"></a>
## From a torus support condition to an exact finite recurrence

Suppose a sequence of unit Pisot seeds has strictly positive delay-two excess tending to zero and a fixed binomial centered limit \(R/2\) at its first discrepancy \(n\), as in the [first-departure classification](Yin_L_G0462_v117.md#departure).
Extract the joint limit of the [conditional moment calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#slice).
Use \(p=n+1\) and write \(-R=a+bz^k\) with \(a,b=\pm1,\ k\ge1\) fixed.
For the positive measure \(\mu\) of the input, the limiting distribution of \((x,y)=(\bar v,v^p)\) has one of the [two conditional laws](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#two).
Consequently, in the respective cases choose
\[
    D(z)=(1-a z^p)(1-b z^{p+k})
       \quad\hbox{or}\quad D(z)=1-ab z^{2p+k}.
\]
Then \(\int |D(\bar v)|^2d\mu(v)\to0\).
Indeed in the first case the absolute value equals \(|(y-a)(y-bx^k)|\), and in the second it equals \(|y^2-abx^k|\).
These are fixed continuous functions of the two torus variables, and they vanish on the respective limiting supports.

Put \(h_0=2\) and \(h_j=[z^{|j|}]K^0\) at all nonzero integer indices, where \(K^0=(1-z)(1+zF)/(1+z^2F)\) is the integer comparison germ.
For every fixed \(C\), the [window estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#window) gives \(h_j-2\int v^j d\mu=o(1)\) uniformly on \(|j|\le Cn\), including zero by the mass formula.
If \(D(z)=\sum d_m z^m\), Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(2\int v^jD(\bar v)\,d\mu\) now gives
\[
                      \sum_m d_m h_{j-m}=0
\]
for all \(|j|\le Cn\) once \(n\) is large.
Indeed \(\deg D=2p+k=O(n)\), its coefficient sum in absolute value is at most four, and the left side is an integer uniformly tending to zero.
Using both positive and negative indices avoids an unknown polynomial part at the beginning of the recurrence.

<a id="neutral"></a>
## Matching to an actual seed of degree at most \(2n+2+k\)

Take the [explicit unit Pisot seed](Yin_L_G0462_v119.md#seeds) with its parameters \((-a,-b,c)\) and the same \(n,k\).
Choose \(c=ab\) in the first case and \(c=-ab\) in the second.
Its normalized quotient \(F'\) belongs to a monic Pisot minimal polynomial of degree at most \(2n+2+k\), and its [matched delay has measure one](Yin_L_G0462_v119.md#delay).
The associated integer comparison germ \(K'=(1-z)(1+zF')/(1+z^2F')\), computed from its centered quotient by \(K'=(1-z^p V')/(1+z^p V')\), is respectively
\[
   \frac{1-ab z^{2p+k}}{(1-a z^p)(1-b z^{p+k})},
   \qquad
   \frac{(1+a z^p)(1+b z^{p+k})}{1-ab z^{2p+k}} .
\]
Extend its coefficients to bilateral integers \(h'_j\) by reflection and \(h'_0=2\).
They satisfy the same recurrence at every integer \(j\).
For the first germ the representing probability is the half-sum of the uniform probabilities on \(v^p=a\) and \(v^{p+k}=b\), as is seen by expanding the two geometric series.
For the second it is the uniform probability on \(v^{2p+k}=ab\) multiplied by \(1+a\operatorname{Re}(v^p)\).
Indeed summing \(v^j\) over that root set gives zero off multiples of \(2p+k\) and the corresponding power of \(ab\) on its multiples.
These probabilities have doubled moments \(h'_j\) and each is supported on \(D(\bar v)=0\).

Both inputs have zero positive comparison coefficients before \(p\).
At \(p+r\) for \(0\le r\le k\), the integer coefficients of the actual input tend to \([z^r](a+bz^k)\) by the [first conditional row](Yin_L_G0462_v120.md#slice), hence agree eventually with \(h'_{p+r}\).
By reflection the two bilateral sequences therefore agree on \([-p-k,p+k]\), an interval of length greater than \(\deg D\).
Since \(D(0)=1\), the exact recurrence identifies their coefficients successively through \(4n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
As ordinary germs \(K^0,K'\) have the same constant one and agree through that degree.
The difference formula
\[
        K^0-K'=\frac{z(1-z)^2(F-F')}{(1+z^2F)(1+z^2F')}
\]
then gives equality of the two signed Pisot quotient jets through degree \(4n-1\).

<a id="unique"></a>
## A finite Blaschke product determines its matching Schur function

Let \(r,r'\) be the poles of \(F,F'\), and multiply both quotients by \(b_r b_{r'}\), where \(b_u=(u-z)/(1-uz)\).
Both resulting functions are Schur, and the one belonging to \(F'\) is a finite Blaschke product of degree at most \(2n+k+4\).
Indeed the multiplication cancels its only disk pole, its boundary values have modulus one, and its unreduced rational numerator has degree at most \(\deg f'+2\).
The multiplication preserves the common Taylor jet.

If a Schur function agrees through degree \(d\) at zero with a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\), the two are equal.
For a nonconstant such product with constant \(c_0\), the Schur step \(G\mapsto (G-c_0)/(z(1-\bar c_0G))\) maps Schur functions with that constant to Schur functions by Schwarz's lemma.
It reduces the Blaschke degree by one and loses at most one matching Taylor coefficient.
At degree zero the common constant has modulus one, so the maximum modulus principle forces equality.
The steps are reversible, proving the assertion.
Here \(4n-1>2n+k+4\) for large \(n\), so \(F=F'\).
The pole identifies the same seed minimal polynomial and hence the same neutral matched delay, contradicting positive excess.

Thus all escaping subsequences at delay two have, after fixing the discrepancy sign \(\delta=\pm1\), the sole centered limit \(V\to-\delta/2\).
The [signed pole-scale relation](Yin_L_G0462_v118.md#side) yields \(2^n(\theta-2)-\delta/2\to0\) even if the signs alternate, by subsequence extraction.
A fixed binomial profile is realizable at measure one, yet cannot occur along positive measures tending to one.
The constant centered limits remain possible by this argument.
