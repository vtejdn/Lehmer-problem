## 2. Exact field, order and norm domain

    B=O intersection F.

This is a full order in F.

## 4. The finite positive-weight construction

**Theorem 1.** Under (1), for EVERY prescribed E in Q with E>0 there is N_0, depending on E, w and the chosen units, such that for every integer N>=N_0 there are strictly positive rational weights lambda_0,...,lambda_(s-1),lambda_- satisfying

    sum_(i=0)^(s-1) lambda_i+lambda_-=1,
    sum_(i=0)^(s-1) lambda_i A_i(N)+lambda_- A_-(N)=E in F.
                                                               (6)

There are s+1 actual O-unit directions, and the complete profile equality holds at all s trace embeddings.
No uniform N_0 or height/denominator estimate is asserted.

*Real existence and strict positivity.* For i>=1 introduce analysis variables mu_i=a_ii lambda_i, leaving lambda_0 and lambda_- unscaled.
Divide only the place-zero equation by a_00.
The full system is

    lambda_0+c_0 lambda_-+
        sum_(i=1)^(s-1) [a_i0/(a_ii a_00)] mu_i = E/a_00,
    lambda_0+lambda_-+sum_(i=1)^(s-1) mu_i/a_ii = 1,
    a_0j lambda_0+c_j a_0j lambda_-+
        sum_(i=1)^(s-1) [a_ij/a_ii] mu_i = E,  1<=j<s.
                                                               (7)

These are precisely all embeddings of the original field equation plus the normalization, with no dropped row.
As N tends to infinity, every first-row sum coefficient and its right-hand side tend to zero, every 1/a_ii tends to zero, and the circle-row matrix a_ij/a_ii tends to the identity.
Also a_0j tends to zero for j>=1.
The limiting system is

    lambda_0+c_0 lambda_-=0,
    lambda_0+lambda_-=1,
    mu_j=E, 1<=j<s.                                 (8)

Its determinant, in the displayed row/variable order, is 1-c_0>0.
Its unique solution is

    lambda_0^*=(-c_0)/(1-c_0)>0,
    lambda_-^*=1/(1-c_0)>0,
    mu_i^*=E>0.                                     (9)

Finite-dimensional determinant continuity makes the matrices in (7) invertible for all sufficiently large N.
The inverse, expressed by cofactors divided by the nonzero determinant, and the right-hand side converge to their limits.
Hence their unique solutions converge to (9).
Choosing the error smaller than half the minimum of those finitely many positive limit coordinates makes lambda_0,lambda_- and every mu_i strictly positive.
Since a_ii>0, every original lambda_i=mu_i/a_ii is strictly positive too.
The normalization is exact, not merely asymptotic.
The dependence on fixed E matters: this argument does not select one N that works uniformly down to E=0.

*Exact rationality.* Scaling was used only for asymptotic analysis; the mu_i need not be rational.
