# Integer-embedding obstacles and a D4 canonical bypass

<a id="scope"></a>
## Question, inputs and actual result

On 2026-11-10, an ordinary proof collaborator for Yin tested whether the integer embedding and signed-permutation extension conditions in [C886v3](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v3.md#statement) could be expected for general positive integral lattices.
The two direct arguments below show that the two conditions have separate obstructions, even when the embedding dimension is unrestricted.
They concern this representation mechanism, not counterexamples to the [unrestricted Lehmer objective](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract).
The resulting alternative D4 canonical construction is developed completely and adopted in [C886v4](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#decision-d4-block-canonical-cover).
The [separate complete independent finding](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1114_v16.md#embedding-obstructions-review) checks all three obstacles retained here and their exact limitations.
No numerical experiment or mathematical program was used.

<a id="unimodular-embedding-obstruction"></a>
## A positive unimodular lattice embeds only when it is standard

Let \(H\) be a positive definite symmetric integral \(g\)-by-\(g\) matrix with \(g\ge1\) and \(\det H=1\).
Suppose some integer \(N\)-by-\(g\) matrix \(V\), with arbitrary \(N\), satisfies
\[
V^{\mathsf T}V=H.
\]
Then the integral lattice with Gram matrix \(H\) is integrally isometric to \(I_g\).
No assumption that \(V\mathbb Z^g\) is primitive is needed.

Indeed \(H^{-1}\) is integral, and
\[
P=VH^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}
\]
is an integral symmetric idempotent matrix, hence the Euclidean orthogonal projection onto \(V\mathbb R^g\).
For every standard coordinate vector \(e_i\),
\[
P_{ii}=\|Pe_i\|^2\in\mathbb Z\cap[0,1].
\]
If \(P_{ii}=0\), then \(Pe_i=0\).
If \(P_{ii}=1\), orthogonal projection gives \(\|e_i-Pe_i\|^2=1-P_{ii}=0\), so \(Pe_i=e_i\).
Thus \(P\) is a diagonal coordinate projection with exactly \(g\) diagonal entries equal to one.
Moreover
\[
P\mathbb Z^N=V\mathbb Z^g:
\]
the left side is contained in the right because \(H^{-1}V^{\mathsf T}\) is integral, and the opposite containment follows from \(PV=V\).
Therefore \(V\mathbb Z^g\) is exactly a standard rank-\(g\) coordinate lattice.
The columns of \(V\) are an integral basis of that lattice and have Gram matrix \(H\), proving the assertion.
Conversely, an integral lattice isometric to \(I_g\) plainly supplies such an embedding.

In particular, no nonzero even positive unimodular lattice admits such an embedding: its lattice has no vector of squared norm one, whereas \(I_g\) does.
This includes the [positive E8 blocks used in C886v1](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v1.md#statement).
Increasing \(N\), permitting a nonprimitive embedding or changing the integral basis of \(H\) does not remove this obstruction.
A real or rational diagonalization is therefore insufficient for the unchanged-norm integer condition used by C886v3.
The observation supplies no obstruction to a different neutral ambient lattice or a direct canonical quotient.

<a id="d4-equivariance-obstruction"></a>
## D4 embeds, but triality cannot extend in any such embedding

Take the positive D4 Gram matrix
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&-1&-1\\
-1&2&0&0\\
-1&0&2&0\\
-1&0&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The four integer columns
\[
r=e_1+e_2,\qquad
a=-e_1+e_3,\qquad
b=-e_1-e_3,\qquad
c=-e_2+e_4
\]
give an explicit \(V_0^{\mathsf T}V_0=H\).
They span D4 in the usual even-sum coordinates: their determinant has absolute value two and every column has even coordinate sum.
Let \(R\) fix the central basis vector and cycle the three leaf basis vectors.
It is an order-three integral \(H\)-isometry because it preserves the displayed Gram matrix.
For every integer matrix \(V\) of arbitrary row count with \(V^{\mathsf T}V=H\), there is no signed permutation matrix \(S\) satisfying
\[
SV=VR.                                                    \tag{1}
\]

To prove this, every column of \(V\) is a norm-two integer vector, hence has exactly two nonzero coordinates, each equal to \(1\) or \(-1\).
After a signed coordinate permutation, assume its central column is \(e_1+e_2\).
Its inner product \(-1\) with each leaf forces the latter to be of the form
\[
-e_1\pm e_j\quad\hbox{or}\quad-e_2\pm e_j,\qquad j>2.
\]
At least two of the three leaves use the same one of the first two coordinates.
Relabeling the leaves for this argument, write those two as \(-e_1+\epsilon e_j\) and \(-e_1+\epsilon' e_{j'}\).
Their orthogonality gives \(j=j'\) and \(\epsilon'=-\epsilon\).
A third leaf using \(-e_1\) cannot be orthogonal to both, since its two inner products would have the form \(1+t\) and \(1-t\).
The third leaf therefore uses \(-e_2\), and its other coordinate cannot be \(j\), again by orthogonality.

Consequently exactly one of the three unordered pairs of leaves has support intersection of size two, and the other two pairs have disjoint supports.
Signed coordinate permutations preserve all such support-intersection sizes.
Equation (1) would cycle the three leaf columns and hence cycle all three unordered pairs, which cannot preserve their unique size-two pair.
This contradiction proves the assertion for every ambient dimension.

The example separates the existence of an integer Gram embedding from its compatibility with actual positive-isometry factors.
It does not say that the neutral order-three action has positive Mahler measure or that every possible word presentation of the same quotient matrix fails another sufficient condition.
In particular, moving all positive factors to one end by the [actual covariance identities](../ledger/Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#short-word-reduction) can reduce the required extension to that of their final positive product; canceling trialities need not remain an obstruction.

<a id="overlarge-coordinate-group-obstruction"></a>
## Full coordinate mixing destroys the finite positive group

The main agent supplied the following discriminator for a possible block-permutation absorption attempt.
It concerns adjoining all signed coordinate permutations of the entire positive space, rather than only the actual D4 block permutations.
Let \(n=4r\ge8\), use standard orthonormal coordinates, and take the unit vectors
\[
h=(1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0,\ldots)/2,\qquad
k=(1,0,0,0,1,1,1,0,\ldots)/2.
\]
The reflection in \(h\) preserves the first D4 block and fixes the others.
The vector \(k\) is an image of \(h\) under a permutation of all \(n\) coordinates.
Thus adjoining the full signed coordinate group \(B_n\) to the blockwise D4 symmetries necessarily adjoins both reflections.
Their inner product is \((h,k)=1/4\), and their product has rational matrix and trace
\[
\operatorname{tr}(s_hs_k)
=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(I-2hh^{\mathsf T}-2kk^{\mathsf T}
                         +4hh^{\mathsf T}kk^{\mathsf T}\bigr)
=n-4+4(h,k)^2=n-15/4.                                  \tag{2}
\]
This rational number is not an integer.
If the product had finite order, its eigenvalues would be roots of unity, so its rational trace would be an algebraic integer and hence an integer.
Equation (2) therefore proves that the generated positive orthogonal group is infinite.
It also rules out a common full-rank invariant lattice for this enlarged action, since a lattice-preserving operator has integer trace in a lattice basis.

This does not apply to \(O(D_4)^r\rtimes S_r\), which is finite by the explicit block decomposition.
The permutation taking \(h\) to \(k\) mixes a block with only part of another block and is not a D4 block permutation.
The calculation obstructs only the proposed enlargement to the full coordinate signed-permutation group with the same half-root reflection.
It neither excludes a different neutral ambient representation nor proves that the actual block-permutation group cannot be absorbed by another canonical construction.

<a id="canonical-bypass-and-allocation"></a>
## What the alternative cover changes

The [complete D4 positive-group argument in C886v4](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#d4-positive-group) uses the actual finite symmetry group in a different canonical basis, rather than extending triality by signed coordinate permutations.
The [canonical star and real radical](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#d4-canonical-star) cover arbitrary numbers of D4 blocks, while the [full-word calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0886_v4.md#d4-permutation-cost) retains the entire original characteristic polynomial and every radical eigenvalue.
This bypasses the triality obstruction for block-preserving positive factors, and also when the final block permutation is trivial.
For a nontrivial block permutation of order \(d\), the argument still pays the complete factor \(d\) in logarithmic measure.
It therefore exposes a genuine distinction between a failure of one embedding criterion and a failure of all canonical-cover mechanisms, without removing the unbounded-permutation bottleneck already present for E8 blocks.

The proof-side implication back to the original Task remains conditional on actual coverage of the original input.
These results provide neither a representation of every primitive integer polynomial nor a uniform way to remove the permutation order.
The fixed-count theorem [C885v8](../claims/Yin_C_G0885_v8.md#statement) separately forces minimum permitted transvection count to grow along a positive-to-one sequence, but its constant still depends on that count.
Improving a fixed-count bound alone, or making another fixed-rank D4 calculation, does not discharge either missing unbounded interface.
A useful next inquiry would test a concrete way to absorb the actual final block permutation into a canonical action with only neutral extra factors, or identify a necessary obstruction to that particular absorption.
No general impossibility claim about such a mechanism is inferred here.
