# Where a shrinking factor of the cubic family would have to occur

<a id="bounded"></a>
## A uniform factor gap for every bounded set of positive slopes

Put \(A_{a,b}(X)=X^{3a}-X^a-1+X^b-X^{2a+b}-X^{3a+b}\) for positive \(a,b\), and let \(m\) denote logarithmic Mahler measure.
There exists an absolute \(c>0\) such that every nontorsion irreducible factor \(P\) of \(A_{a,b}\) satisfies
\[
                              m(P)\ge\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+b/a}} .          \tag{1}
\]
In particular slopes tending to zero have a uniform positive factor gap, even when both exponents grow and have an arbitrarily large common divisor.

For \(a\ge4b\), write the polynomial as
\[
                X^{3a}(1-X^b)+(-1-X^a+X^b-X^{2a+b}).
\]
The leading block has only unit-circle zeros and both blocks have coefficient one-norm at most four.
The gap between blocks is \(a-b\) and the width of the leading block is \(b\).
Subtracting twice the width leaves \(a-3b\ge(3a+b)/13\).
The [separated-block theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v170.md#statement) with \(L=4,\ \delta=1/13\) gives an absolute positive lower bound \(c_0\) for every such factor.

For \(a<4b\), put \(r=b/a>1/4\).
The first and last gaps between occupied exponents are both at least \(\min(a,b)\), including after cancellations.
The [sparse endpoint estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v188.md#finite) therefore gives
\[
      m(P)^2\ge C\,\frac{\min(1,r)}{3+r},
      \qquad C=\frac{\log(1+0.31/720)}{20\log6}.
\]
Every factor is a unit polynomial, since the two parent endpoints have absolute coefficient one.
For \(1/4<r\le1\), the ratio on the right is at least \(1/13\); for \(r\ge1\) it is \(1/(3+r)\ge1/(2(1+r))\).
Thus \(c=\min(c_0,\sqrt{C/13})\) suffices in (1).

<a id="criterion"></a>
## An equivalent construction problem for the entire family

There are factors of positive log measure tending to zero anywhere in this family if and only if there are coprime positive directions \(a,b\) with \(b/a\to\infty\) and a nontorsion rational irreducible factor in each direction receiving \(k=o(a)\) nonreal conjugate pairs of exterior roots.

Indeed the [classification of common dilations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v388.md#statement) identifies every nontorsion factor with a substituted primitive factor of exactly the same measure.
By (1) the slopes of any shrinking sequence tend to infinity.
In that range the [exterior partition](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#small-parts) gives \(m(P)=(\log\theta+o(1))k/a\) uniformly across actual factors, where \(\theta>1\) solves \(\theta^3-\theta-1=0\).
This proves both implications and forces \(a\to\infty\), since \(k\ge1\).
Every sufficiently late parent in such a sequence has at least two nontorsion rational factors, since together they receive \(a\) pairs.
In particular, Schinzel's [factorization conjecture for this specific graph](Yin_L_G0462_v185.md#schinzel) implies a positive uniform factor gap for the entire family, with no restriction on its exponents.
Existence of the small allocation remains open.
