# Transferring a one-row rational expression for a unit to a double-root resultant

<a id="transfer"></a>
## An inequality with the other powered factors included

Let \(P\) be the minimal polynomial of an algebraic integer unit \(\alpha\), let \(p\) be a prime preserving its degree, and let \(Q\) be the monic minimal polynomial of \(\beta=\alpha^p\).
Suppose \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[X]\) satisfy \(B(\beta)\ne0,\ \alpha B(\beta)=A(\beta)\).
The polynomial \(A^p-XB^p\) vanishes at \(\beta\), so
\[
                  A^p-XB^p=QT,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z[X].
\]
Assume \(T(\beta)\ne0\).
Take any two-row witness of the form \(F=U(x^p)+x^rV(x^p)\) divisible by \(P^2\), with \(1\le r<p\), nonzero integral polynomial rows and no common factor in \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Any two residues reduce to this form by a common monomial shift, clearing negative exponents by a power of \(x^p\).
Divide out common \(\ell\)-content before applying the bound at a prime \(\ell\ne p\).

For a disk \(D\) of positive depth \(s_D\) and unit center, write \(v_D\) for the minimum coefficient valuation after the substitution \(X=c+\tau Y,\ v(\tau)=s_D,\ v(\ell)=1\).
The Gauss valuation is multiplicative and \(v_D(X)=0\).
Put \(h_D=\min(v_D(A),v_D(B))\), \(t_D=v_D(T)\), \(q_D=v_D(Q)\).
Then the common row valuation satisfies
\[
 \min(v_D(U),v_D(V))\ \ge\
 b_D:=\max\left(0,\ \max_{j\in\{r,p-r\}}
    \{\min((p-j)h_D,q_D)-t_D-v_\ell(j)\}\right).                         \tag{1}
\]
For any collection of such disks at distinct center residues, after stripping all row monomials, the absolute resultant \(H\) satisfies
\[
                            v_\ell(H)\ \ge\sum_D b_D^2/s_D .              \tag{2}
\]
All the quantities on the right may be computed from \(P,p,r,A,B\) and the chosen disks, independently of the witness.

<a id="identities"></a>
## The two row identities

Set \(X=x^p,\ E=xB(X)-A(X)\).
Evaluation at \(\beta\) gives \(B^rU+A^rV=QL\) with \(L\in\mathbb Z[X]\), by monic division.
The polynomial identities before differentiation are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B(X)^r F(x)&=Q(X)L(X)+\big((xB(X))^r-A(X)^r\big)V(X),\\
 Q(X)T(X)&=A(X)^p-(xB(X))^p .
 \end{aligned}
\]
Differentiate at \(x=\alpha\), using \(F(\alpha)=F'(\alpha)=0\) and writing \(A_\beta=A(\beta)\ne0\).
The resulting equations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0&=p\alpha^{p-1}Q'(\beta)L(\beta)+r A_\beta^{r-1}E'(\alpha)V(\beta),\\
 p\alpha^{p-1}Q'(\beta)T(\beta)&=-p A_\beta^{p-1}E'(\alpha).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(Q'(\beta)\ne0\), and \(T(\beta)\ne0\) ensures that cancellation of \(E'(\alpha)\) is valid.
Consequently the following congruences hold in \(\mathbb Z[X]/(Q)\):
\[
           rTV\equiv p A^{p-r}L,\qquad
           rTU\equiv-p X B^{p-r}L .                                      \tag{3}
\]
The second follows from \(U(\beta)=-\alpha^r V(\beta)\) and \(\alpha^r A_\beta^{p-r}=\beta B(\beta)^{p-r}\).
Evaluation at \(\beta\) suffices for both integral congruences since \(Q\) is monic.
Their right sides have disk valuations at least \((p-r)h_D\), and changing a representative modulo \(Q\) costs at most the truncation to \(q_D\).
This proves the term \(j=r\) in (1).
Multiplying the original witness by \(x^{p-r}\) gives the witness with rows \(XV,U\) and separation \(p-r\), with the same two disk valuations up to interchange.
This proves the other term.
The [separated-disk inequality](Yin_L_G0462_v154.md#resultant) now gives (2), because multiplying either row by any monomial does not change its valuation on a unit disk.
Restoring removed common scalar content only increases the valuation of the stripped resultant.

## What a rational expression must supply

The eliminant implies \(q_D+t_D\ge p h_D\).
Thus when \(t_D\le j h_D\), term \(j\) of (1) supplies at least \((p-j)h_D-t_D-v_\ell(j)\).
In particular, a disk on which both numerator and denominator have positive valuation gives a contribution growing linearly in \(p\) to the row valuations if the cofactor \(T\) has bounded disk valuation, \(h_D\) stays bounded below, and \(j\le p/2\) with \(t_D\le j h_D\).
For the [Salem presentation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) \(A=2X^{d/p}+1,\ B=X^{d/p}+2\), the eliminant has exactly the single extra factor \(X-1\) up to sign by its roots and leading coefficient.
In contrast, if \(A,B\) admit an integral Bézout combination equal to one, every unit disk gives \(h_D=0\).
Changing the expression for \(\alpha\) may change both \(h_D\) and \(t_D\), even though the powered factor \(Q\) is fixed.
The existence of expressions with enough total contribution for a general varying small-measure input remains open.
