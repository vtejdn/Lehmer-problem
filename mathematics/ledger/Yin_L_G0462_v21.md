# Bounded Salem measures with exact Frobenius matching at prescribed primes

<a id="bounded-salem-matching"></a>
## One matched orbit at every prescribed prime

Fix a finite nonempty set \(S\) of rational primes.
There is an integer \(A=A(S)\ge3\) and a sequence of Salem units \(\alpha\) of original degrees \(d=2\ell\), with \(\ell\to\infty\) through primes, such that
\[
       1<M(\alpha)=\alpha<A,\qquad M(\alpha)\longrightarrow A.
\]
Every positive power of \(\alpha\) has degree \(d\).
At every \(p\in S\) the roots have pairwise distinct reductions and the splitting field is unramified.
For the [ordered-pair densities](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v2.md#statement), exactly one Galois orbit is compatible, and on it
\[
            s=d-2,\qquad \gamma=1,\qquad \beta=\frac1{d-2}.
\]
Here compatibility means \(\bar x^p=\bar y\), \(s\) counts targets for each source root and \(\beta\) is the fraction of compatible edges in the orbit.
The Galois action on the inverse pairs is the full symmetric group \(S_\ell\).
This gives the exact sparse orbit of the [earlier finite-prime construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v2.md#finite-pool) with uniformly bounded original measures along the degrees.
The common upper bound can depend on \(S\).
The limiting measure in this construction is at least three.
The construction contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained the family on 2026-11-16.

<a id="salem-rule"></a>
## A reciprocal Salem polynomial with no cyclotomic factors

For any even \(N=2\ell\ge10\) and any integer \(A\ge3\), put
\[
 D_N(z)=z^{N+1}-A z^N+A z-1,\qquad P_N(z)=D_N(z)/(z-1).
\]
The polynomial \(P_N\) is monic integral and reciprocal of degree \(N\) with constant coefficient one.
On the unit circle, \(D_N(z)=0\) exactly when \(z^N=(z-r)/(1-rz)\) for \(r=1/A\).
The factor on the right maps the circle once around itself, and at \(z=e^{i\theta}\) its argument has derivative \((1-r^2)/|z-r|^2\), at most \((A+1)/(A-1)\le2\).
Consequently the difference between \(N\theta\) and a continuous lift of that argument increases strictly through \(2\pi(N-1)\) in a full turn.
It gives \(N-1\) distinct simple circle roots of \(D_N\), including \(1\).
Since \(D_N'(1)=N(1-A)+1+A<0\) and \(D_N(A)=A^2-1>0\), one real root \(\alpha\) lies in \((1,A)\).
Its reciprocal, also a root, accounts for the last root.
For each fixed \(z\in(1,A)\), \(D_N(z)=z^N(z-A)+Az-1<0\) eventually.
Thus \(\alpha\to A\) as \(N\to\infty\).

If a root of unity \(\zeta\) satisfies \(D_N(\zeta)=0\), then
\[
        A\zeta(\zeta^{N-1}-1)=\zeta^{N+1}-1.
\]
When the left side is nonzero its absolute norm from \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\) is at least \(A^{[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]}\), whereas the norm on the right is at most \(2^{[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]}\).
This is impossible.
If both sides vanish, the order divides \(\gcd(N-1,N+1)=1\), leaving just the simple root \(1\) canceled from \(P_N\).
By Kronecker's theorem every irreducible factor of \(P_N\) must therefore contain a root outside the unit circle.
There is just one such root, so \(P_N\) is irreducible and \(\alpha\) is a Salem number.
If two conjugates have equal positive powers, an automorphism sending the first to \(\alpha\) sends the other to a root of the same modulus.
The unique maximal-modulus root is \(\alpha\), proving equality of the two conjugates and preservation of degree by every power.

<a id="prime-congruences"></a>
## Choosing the integer and the prime degrees

Set \(T=\prod_{p\in S}p(p^2-1)\).
Choose a prime \(q>11\) not dividing \(T\).
Impose on the prime \(\ell\) the two congruences
\[
  \ell\equiv-1\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(T,q-1),
  \qquad \ell\equiv-5/4\bmod q .
\]
These define a reduced residue class, since \(q\nmid T(q-1)\).
[Dirichlet's theorem](../audits/literature/Sut21/Yin_U001.md#sut21-u001) supplies infinitely many primes \(\ell\ge5\) in it.
Thus \(N=2\ell\) has one fixed class modulo \(q(q-1)\) and satisfies \(N\equiv-2\bmod(q-1)\), \(N\equiv-5/2\bmod q\).

Require \(A\equiv0\bmod\prod_{p\in S}p\), and first choose \(a=A\bmod q=2/7\).
At \(z=2\bmod q\), \(2^N=1/4\) and hence \(D_N(2)=(2-a)/4+2a-1=0\) in \(\mathbb F_q\).
Changing \(A\) by \(q\) changes \(D_N(2)\) by \(q(2-2^N)\), of exact \(q\)-valuation one as \(2-2^N=7/4\bmod q\).
The class of \(2^N\bmod q^2\) is fixed because \(N\bmod q(q-1)\) is fixed.
Thus one may take a single lift \(A\bmod q^2\) such that \(v_q(D_N(2))=1\) simultaneously for all selected degrees.
The Chinese remainder theorem gives a positive \(A\ge3\) satisfying these requirements and those at \(S\).

For \(p\in S\), reduction gives \(P_N=(z^{N+1}-1)/(z-1)\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
We have \(N+1\equiv-1\bmod p(p^2-1)\), so this polynomial is squarefree, has no root at \(1\), and has no root in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\).
Indeed \(\gcd(N+1,p^2-1)=1\).
Inertia fixes every root because their reductions are distinct, yielding unramifiedness of the splitting field at all designated primes.

<a id="trace-transposition"></a>
## A transposition on the inverse pairs

Write \(P_N(z)=z^\ell F_\ell(z+z^{-1})\), where \(F_\ell\) is monic integral of degree \(\ell\) by reciprocity.
Modulo \(q\), a root \(z\) of \(D_N\) is nonzero and different from \(a\), since \(D_N(a)=a^2-1\ne0\).
At such a root, elimination between \(D_N(z)\) and its derivative gives the quadratic
\[
  K(z)=-Na z^2+\big[N(1+a^2)+1-a^2\big]z-Na .
\]
More precisely the polynomial identity is
\[
 z(z-a)D_N'(z)-[N(z-a)+z]D_N(z)=K(z).
\]
Thus a multiple root must be a root of \(K\).
The two roots of \(K\) are \(2,1/2\) in \(\mathbb F_q\), since \(N=-5/2\), \(a=2/7\) there and their sum is \(5/2\).
They are distinct and simple since \(q>11\).
They are both roots of \(D_N\) by the preceding calculation and antireciprocity.
The identity therefore gives zero derivative at both.
A root of multiplicity at least three would make \(K\) vanish to order at least two, so both multiplicities are exactly two.
Every other root is simple, including \(1\), and \(D_N(-1)=-2(a+1)\ne0\).
Removing \(z-1\) leaves no root over either trace endpoint \(2,-2\).
Away from \(z=\pm1\) the map \(z\mapsto z+z^{-1}\) has nonzero derivative.
Consequently \(F_\ell\) has exactly one double root \(u_0=5/2\bmod q\), and all its other roots are simple modulo \(q\).

Use \(5/2\) also as an element of \(\mathbb Z_q\).
The identity \(D_N(2)=2^\ell F_\ell(5/2)\) gives \(v_q(F_\ell(5/2))=1\) over \(\mathbb Q_q\).
Take a finite unramified extension \(U\) of \(\mathbb Q_q\) whose residue field contains all roots of the reduction of \(F_\ell\).
Such an extension is obtained by lifting the monic irreducible polynomial of a generator of a sufficiently large [finite residue field](../audits/literature/Hun74/Yin_U006.md#hun74-u006).
Irreducibility of the reduction gives the extension degree, and the reduction of its root already generates a residue extension of the same degree, so the ramification index is one.
The [simple-root Hensel theorem](../audits/literature/Con26e/Yin_U001.md#con26e-u001) lifts the \(\ell-2\) simple roots to \(U\).
After removing their monic linear factors and translating by \(5/2\), the remaining monic quadratic is \(X^2+bX+c\) with \(v_q(b)\ge1\) and \(v_q(c)=1\), since the removed factors have unit value at \(5/2\).
Its discriminant has valuation one as \(q\) is odd.
The resulting ramified quadratic extension has an automorphism interchanging just the two roots of this factor and fixing all the simple lifts.
It restricts to a transposition in the global Galois action on the trace roots, by embedding the global splitting field of \(F_\ell\) into this local splitting field.

<a id="full-off-pair-orbit"></a>
## The global orbit and the local edges

The trace polynomial is irreducible, since the Galois group is transitive on the conjugates of \(\alpha\) and hence on their \(\ell\) inverse pairs.
The resulting transitive group on \(\ell\) blocks has an element of order \(\ell\) by orbit-stabilizer and Cauchy's theorem, hence an \(\ell\)-cycle.
Conjugating the transposition by this cycle yields transpositions supported on a connected graph, since their fixed nonzero difference generates \(\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z\).
Transpositions on the edges of a connected graph generate the full symmetric group.

The kernel on blocks contains [all even collections of pair flips](../claims/Yin_C_G0383_v1.md#salem-kernel-class-population).
Indeed, complex conjugation flips precisely the \(\ell-1\) nonreal inverse pairs, and conjugation by a block mover lets one omit any chosen pair instead.
Products flip any two designated pairs.
The action on ordered pairs of roots consequently has just three orbits, for equality, inversion and distinct inverse pairs.
For the third orbit, a block permutation first moves any given two distinct source blocks to the two desired target blocks.
Pair flips then correct the two root choices, using a third block to correct parity if needed.
There are \(d(d-2)\) ordered pairs in this orbit.

At any \(p\in S\), each source root has exactly one compatible target, its image under [residue Frobenius](../audits/literature/The26b/Yin_U001.md#the26b-u001).
A matching to itself or its inverse would imply that its reduction lies in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\), excluded above.
All \(d\) compatible pairs thus belong to the third orbit, which proves the stated fractions.
