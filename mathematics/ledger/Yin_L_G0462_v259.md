# Coefficient minima at the degree of an irreducible power substitution

<a id="blocks"></a>
## One primitive line determines the exact cost at each residue

Let \(P_0\) be the reciprocal monic minimum of a nontorsion unit of degree \(e=2s\), and suppose \(P=P_0(X^d)\) is irreducible for \(d\ge2\).
Let \(p\ge2\) be coprime to \(d\), and suppose the \(p\)-th powers of the roots of \(P_0\) are distinct.
At width \(de\) use the two nonzero coprime rows, nonzero reflected determinant and uncapped minimum \(H_p(P)\) of the [coefficient problem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v408.md#statement), assigning \(+\infty\) when no pair qualifies.
Take \(1\le q<p\) inverse to \(d\bmod p\) and set \(a=(dq-1)/p\), so \(1\le a<d\).
Compute the rational space
\[
   P_0(T)^2\mid A(T^p)+T^q B(T^p),\qquad \deg A\le e,\quad \deg B<e.       \tag{1}
\]
If its dimension is one and its primitive integral generator \((A,B)\) has \(A_0A_e\ne0\), then
\[
                 H_p(P)=\max(\|A\|_1,\|B\|_1).                          \tag{2}
\]
In every other case the minimum is infinite.
In particular it is identical for every permitted \(d\) in either of the residue classes \(d_0,-d_0\bmod p\).

Write \(U(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}Y^j U_j(Y^d)\), and similarly for \(V\).
Only \(U_0,V_0\) have degree cap \(e\) in the inner variable, and all other pieces have cap \(e-1\).
Pair \(U_j\) with \(V_l\) for \(l\equiv j+a\bmod d\).
Their graph contributions have the same residue \(pj\bmod d\), and these residues are distinct as \(j\) varies.
With \(T=X^d\), divisibility by \(P_0(T)^2\) holds separately in each residue by freeness of the powers \(1,X,\ldots,X^{d-1}\) over \(\mathbb Q[T]\).
The removed monomials are units modulo \(P_0(T)^2\).
For \(0\le j<d-a\) one obtains (1), with the additional restriction \(A_e=0\) when \(j>0\).
For \(d-a\le j<d\), the normalized equation uses \(V_l\) first, \(U_j\) second and offset \(p-q\).
The only occurrence there with the first polynomial allowed degree \(e\) is \(l=0\).
Reversal of the graph in (1) about \(pe\) sends \((A,B,q)\) to \((A^*_e,B^*_{e-1},p-q)\), where \(C^*_j=Y^j C(1/Y)\), and preserves divisibility by reciprocity of \(P_0\).
This gives the symmetry between the two offsets.

The dimension of (1) is at least one, by the count of \(2e+1\) unknowns and \(2e\) remainder coordinates.
The \(p\)-th powers of the \(de\) roots of \(P\) are pairwise distinct by the two hypotheses on \(p\), so their minimum \(Q\) has degree \(de\).
Whenever a genuine pair exists, the [two-line necessity](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines) makes the entire rational graph kernel two-dimensional, one dimension of each sign.
Thus the two unrestricted residue blocks must each be a single line, and every other block must vanish.
Write \(R=(U,V)=(A(Y^d),Y^a B(Y^d))\) and \(S=(V^*_{de},U^*_{de})\).
These span the two blocks, with disjoint supports in each row.
In the Laurent ring the determinant of \(mR+nS\) equals \((m^2-n^2)(UU^\iota-VV^\iota)\) because the cross terms cancel.
Even before imposing coprimality, divisibility of the graph and its reciprocal implies \(Q^2\mid UU^\iota-VV^\iota\) by [reflected elimination](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v405.md#identity).
The Laurent coefficient of this determinant in degree \(de\) is \(A_0A_e\).
If it vanishes, the full Laurent span is less than \(2de\), forcing the determinant to vanish identically.
Thus a qualifying pair forces the stated edge condition.

Conversely, for a line with both edges nonzero the subspaces obtained by deleting either edge are zero, so all other residue blocks vanish.
The row \(R\) has nonzero determinant by its edge.
Here \(B\ne0\), since otherwise \(A\) would have double zeros at the \(e\) distinct powered base roots.
No nonconstant common divisor \(G\) of its two polynomials can contain \(Q\), because the nonzero second row has degree at most \(d(e-1)+a<de\).
Hence dividing by \(G\) preserves double divisibility and a nonzero reflected determinant, contradicting the same elimination degree bound at the smaller row cap.
This proves coprimality.
For an integral pair \(mR+nS\), both multipliers are integers by disjoint supports and primitivity of the coefficient tuple in each block.
The two row norms are \(|m|\|A\|_1+|n|\|B\|_1\) and \(|m|\|B\|_1+|n|\|A\|_1\).
Taking \(R\) attains their least possible nonzero maximum and proves (2).

## Degree-independent bounds and eventual existence for each fixed dilation

Let \(h=m(P_0)\), let \(t_1\ge\cdots\ge t_s\) be the sorted absolute logarithmic moduli of inverse-pair representatives, and let \(D=\sum_{i<j}\big|\log|x_i|-\log|x_j|\big|\) for all base roots \(x_i\).
Set \(F_0=\sum_{j=1}^s(e-2j+2)t_j\).
Whenever the minimum is finite it obeys
\[
 \frac{p}{e}\{2D-(e-2)h\}-o_{P_0}(p)
       \ \le\ \log H_p(P)\ \le\ p(2F_0-h)+O_{P_0}(\log(2p)).            \tag{3}
\]
Both bounds are uniform in \(d\), including when \(d\) varies with \(p\).
If the inverse-pair representatives of \(P_0\) are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity, then for each **fixed** permitted \(d\) the minimum is finite at every sufficiently large \(p\) coprime to \(d\).

For the upper bound use the \(2e\)-by-\((2e+1)\) integer remainder matrix in (1).
Its maximal minors divided by their integer gcd give the primitive kernel generator up to signs.
Evaluation and the Euler derivative at all \(e\) distinct nonzero roots of \(P_0\) transform its rows by a fixed invertible Hermite matrix, since these jets identify polynomials modulo \(P_0^2\).
The transformed columns at \(x_i\) are \((x_i^k,kx_i^k)\) for
\[
                  k=0,q,p,p+q,\ldots,(e-1)p,(e-1)p+q,ep .
\]
In each determinant term the two rows of each root use distinct columns.
In the full increasing exponent list, allocate the next two highest positions to the exterior root of the \(j\)-th largest \(t_j\) and the next two lowest to its inverse.
At \(t_j=0\) either orientation works.
Rearrangement bounds the contribution for every minor by this allocation, since the one unused exponent can be assigned weight zero.
The difference of the corresponding two sums of exponents is \((2e-4j+3)p\), independent of \(q\).
The derivative factors contribute at most \((ep)^e\), and the inverse determinant of the Hermite matrix is fixed.
The integer gcd is at least one and the one-norm is bounded by \(e+1\) times the largest coefficient, proving the upper estimate.

For the lower bound fix the primitive rows in (1), a splitting field of \(P_0\), and put \(z_i=x_i^p,\ y_i=x_i^q\).
The values \(B(z_i)\) are nonzero, since the minimum of \(z_i\) has degree \(e\).
At any finite prime ideal use its integer valuation \(v\), and fix \(i\ne j\).
Put \(r=v(z_i-z_j)\) and \(c=v(x_i-x_j)\).
Since the roots are units and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), Bézout's identity for the two powers of \(x_i/x_j\) gives \(\min(r,v(y_i-y_j))=c\).
The first-order inverse expansion of \(X^q\) at \(X=x_j\), in \(Y=X^p\), is \(y_j(1+q(Y-z_j)/(pz_j))\).
After multiplication by \(p\), the polynomial \(A(Y)+y_j(1+q(Y-z_j)/(pz_j))B(Y)\) is thus divisible by \((Y-z_j)^2\) over the algebraic integers.
If \(r>v(p)+c\), the correction inside the parentheses has valuation greater than \(c\), while \(v(y_i-y_j)=c\).
Evaluation at \(z_i\), using \(A(z_i)=-y_i B(z_i)\), then gives \(v(B(z_i))\ge 2r-v(p)-c\).
In all cases integrality gives the weaker \(v(B(z_i))\ge2r-2v(p)-2c\).
The [overlap and discriminant estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#overlap) involve only the fixed \(x_i\) and their \(p\)-th powers.
Applying their summation to this inequality bounds the normalized log norm of \(B(z_i)\) below by \(2pD/e-o_{P_0}(p)\).

At an exterior \(x\), the top two exponents of the graph are \(ep,(e-1)p+q\), and the remaining ones are at most \((e-1)p\).
Applying \(T\,d/dT-ep\) at the double root isolates the second of the top two terms with a nonzero integer multiplier.
The remaining terms are bounded in sum by \(2epH|x|^{(e-1)p}\).
Adding back the lower terms of \(B\) gives \(|B(x^p)|\ll_{P_0}pH|x|^{(e-1)p-q}\).
At an interior \(x\), applying \(T\,d/dT\) isolates the term at \(q\) among the bottom two, and the others have exponents at least \(p\).
This gives \(|B(x^p)|\ll_{P_0}pH|x|^{p-q}\).
The circle bound is \(H\).
Since the positive log moduli sum to \(h\) and the negative ones to \(-h\), the normalized log norm is at most \(\log H+(e-2)ph/e+O_{P_0}(\log(2p))\).
Comparison proves the lower estimate.

For eventual existence fix a residue \(p=dn+r\) coprime to \(d\).
Then \(a\) is fixed and \(q=an+(ar+1)/d\).
In the Hermite matrix the exponents of the powers of \(x_i^n\) are the distinct slopes \(0,a,d,d+a,\ldots,(e-1)d+a,ed\).
Introduce one formal variable for each inverse pair, substituting its inverse at the inverse root.
Delete column \(A_0\) or \(A_e\) from the full matrix.
Both determinants are nonzero Laurent polynomials in these variables with coefficients polynomial in \(n\).
Indeed take nonzero pairwise distinct absolute weights for the variables.
The resulting \(e\) node weights are distinct, and strict rearrangement uniquely allocates the two appropriate consecutive remaining slopes to each node at maximal weight.
The two orders within a node give a two-by-two determinant whose coefficient is a nonzero power of the base root times the difference of the two actual column exponents, a nonzero polynomial in \(n\).
The product of these coefficients is the unique extremal monomial coefficient up to sign.
Independence modulo torsion makes the bases of the resulting power sums have pairwise nontorsion ratios.
The [fixed-place estimate for nondegenerate power sums](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) gives eventual nonvanishing of both specialized minors.
There are finitely many residues for fixed \(d\), so (2) gives the asserted eventual existence.

## The quartic Salem example and all its dyadic dilations

For a Salem base \(h=\log\rho,\ e\ge4\), one has \(D=(2e-2)h\) and \(F_0=eh\).
Thus the two bounds on the exponential cost per \(p\) are \((3-2/e)h\) from below and \((2e-1)h\) from above, up to errors tending to zero uniformly in the permitted dilation.
Each fixed dilation has eventual existence because [Salem inverse pairs are independent](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v413.md#directions).
The [two quartic Salem seeds](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#short) have \(\rho^{3/2}>2\) by the [quartic trace comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v247.md#five).
Their lower bound \(5h/2\) therefore excludes \(H\le2^p\) simultaneously for all their permitted dilations at sufficiently large \(p\), including varying \(d\).

Take specifically \(P_0=X^4-X^3-X^2-X+1\).
Modulo \(17\) its simple root \(10\) has derivative \(7\) and \(10^8\equiv-1\), and \(-1\) is a square there.
Hensel lifting embeds \(K=\mathbb Q(x_1)\) in \(\mathbb Q_{17}\) with \(x_1\) reducing to \(10\).
Neither \(x_1\) nor \(-x_1\) is a square in \(K\).
It follows that \(X^{2^k}-x_1\) is irreducible over \(K\) for every \(k\ge1\).
Indeed, after the first step, the norm of any root of the preceding irreducible even-degree binomial is \(-x_1\), so that root is no square and the next quadratic extension doubles its degree.
The tower law proves irreducibility of \(P_0(X^{2^k})\).
These seeds have constant measure and unbounded degree, yet their fixed-seed upper rates are all at most \(7h\).
Consequently the [growing-degree lower bound for independent inverse pairs](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v414.md#statement) cannot extend to arbitrary dependent inverse pairs.
The existence proof in (3) gives an onset depending on \(k\).

For an exact small check, at \(p=5\) formula (2) gives \(1040\) for \(d=2\) and \(10032\) for \(d=4\).
At \(p=3\) all the dyadic dilations give \(+\infty\).
