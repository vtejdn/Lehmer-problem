# An upper bound for the least radial energy at every degree-preserving prime

<a id="bound"></a>
## A limitation on selecting the prime from the input

Let \(P\) be a monic nontorsion unit minimal polynomial of degree \(d\), and let raising a root to its \(p\)-th power preserve its degree for a prime \(p\).
For \(s>0\) define \(N_\pm(s)=\#\{\alpha:P(\alpha)=0,\ \pm\log|\alpha|>s\}\), and write \(h=m(P)\).
For every pair of residue classes there exist primitive coprime two-row witnesses with exact multiplicity two of \(P\), both stripped rows of degree \(k=2d\), and ordinary degree less than \(p(2d+1)\), for which the infimum of the normalized radial energy over such witnesses satisfies
\[
 \inf_F\frac{\mathcal R(F)}{kp}
     \ \le\ \frac{2}{d}\int_0^\infty (N_+(s)^2+N_-(s)^2)\,ds
     \ \le\ \frac{2h}{d}\bigl(N_+(0)+N_-(0)\bigr).                     \tag{1}
\]
Here \(N_\pm(0)\) count the roots strictly on the respective sides, and \(\mathcal R\) is the squared radial derivative integral of the angular mean of the [row potential](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#radial).
For every Salem input the first upper bound is \(4h/d\).
Along a bounded-measure sequence of Salem numbers of increasing degree this tends to zero, uniformly over every admissible choice of the prime and the two classes.
For example the [Salem sequence tending to two](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v371.md#salem) has precisely this property.

<a id="construction"></a>
## A family of admissible pencils with a common limiting root distribution

Let \(Q\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\beta=\alpha^p\), and \(1\le r<p\) the positive difference of the two prescribed residues.
Choose the [constant-row interpolation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v376.md#statement) \(F_0=U_0(x^p)-b x^r\), \(b>0,\ \deg U_0<2d\), divisible by \(P^2\).
For a positive integer \(M\), put
\[
 U=U_0+2M Q^2,\qquad V=b+M Q^2,\qquad F=U(x^p)-x^r V(x^p).
\]
Both rows have degree \(k=2d\) and have nonzero constant terms for sufficiently large \(M\).
Their difference \(W=U-2V=U_0-2b\) is nonzero and coprime to \(Q\), since \(W(\beta)=b(\alpha^r-2)\ne0\) for an algebraic unit.
At each zero of \(W\), at most one value of \(M\) makes \(V\) vanish, so both rows are coprime for all sufficiently large \(M\).
The term added to \(F_0\) is \(M(2-x^r)Q(x^p)^2\), with exact vanishing order two at \(\alpha\).
Consequently at most one \(M\) gives multiplicity greater than two.
Divide by the positive common content and shift by a monomial to obtain the specified residues.
For residues \(0\le i<j<p,\ r=j-i\), the degree after shifting by \(x^i\) is \(pk+j<p(k+1)\).
Scalar normalization and the shift preserve pencil roots of the stripped rows, up to rotation of the phase by a sign.

For \(|t|=1\) the pencil \(U-tV\) divided by \(M(2-t)\) is monic and converges coefficientwise to \(Q^2\) uniformly in \(t\).
This follows from \(|2-t|\ge1\).
The roots stay in a fixed compact annulus as \(M\to\infty\) at each fixed \(P,p\), by the monic coefficient bound and its reciprocal, since the constant term of \(Q^2\) is one.
On any circle avoiding the finitely many root moduli of \(Q\), Rouché's theorem gives exactly the count for \(Q^2\) uniformly in \(t\) for sufficiently large \(M\).
The radial-mean derivative thus tends almost everywhere to the corresponding signed count for \(Q^2\), bounded by \(k\) and supported in a fixed bounded interval of logarithmic radii.
Dominated convergence yields \(\mathcal R(F)\to4p\int_0^\infty(N_+^2+N_-^2)\,ds\) since the roots of \(Q\) are precisely the distinct \(p\)-th powers of the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
For a unit, \(\int N_+(s)\,ds=\int N_-(s)\,ds=h\).
Bounding \(N_\pm^2\) by \(N_\pm(0)N_\pm\) proves (1), with equality in that second bound for a Salem input.

## The cost of concentrating the roots of the pencil

The full energy can be arbitrarily large at the same radial limit.
For the unnormalized rows and fixed \(P,p\), Jensen's formula in the phase gives \(I=\int_{\mathbb T} m(U-tV)\,d\sigma(t)\).
The monic pencils above converge uniformly in coefficients to \(Q^2\), and monic Mahler measure is continuous by the root product.
As \(m(Q)=ph\) and the mean of \(\log|2-t|\) is \(\log2\), this gives \(I=\log M+\log2+2ph+o(1)\).
Writing \(s=\deg W<k\), the absolute homogeneous resultant of the rows is
\[
 B=M^{k-s}|\operatorname{Res}(W,b+M Q^2)|.
\]
Indeed subtracting twice the second homogeneous row leaves the degree-\(k\) homogenization of \(W\), with \(k-s\) zeros at infinity.
The remaining resultant divided by \(M^s\) tends in modulus to \(|\operatorname{Res}(W,Q^2)|>0\), also when \(W\) is constant.
The [energy identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v379.md#identity) now gives \(\mathcal D(g)=k\log M+O_{P,p,r}(1)\).
The energy is unchanged by common scalar division, and the content divides each coefficient of the fixed nonzero \(W\).
In this construction the cofactor cost can grow with \(M\).
The [near-minimizing Salem witnesses](Yin_L_G0462_v176.md#radial) give small radial mean energy together with a bounded cofactor rate for the prescribed primes in that example.
