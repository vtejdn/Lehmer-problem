# Odd-order fibers of a rational map and the half-degree encoding of an arbitrary reciprocal unit

<a id="odd-fibers"></a>
## Counting the nontorsion inputs at finitely many phases

Let \(F,G\in\mathbb Q[z]\) be relatively prime with nonzero constants and equal degree \(r\ge1\).
Let \(R(z)=z^k F(z)/G(z)\) for any integer \(k\), with either sign absorbed into \(F\).
It is a rational map of degree \(D=r+|k|\) and has a zero or pole in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
At most \(10r\) distinct roots of unity can map to roots of unity of odd order, regardless of \(k\) or the orders.
For a finite set of \(s\) distinct roots of unity of odd order, the number of distinct nontorsion preimages in \(\mathbb C^\times\) is at least
\[
                              sD-12r .
\]
In particular the thirteenth roots of unity have at least \(r+13|k|\) such preimages.
The bound concerns all original points, without a separability or simplicity assumption on individual fibers.
For comparison, [Beukers and Smyth](../audits/literature/BS02/Yin_U001.md#newton-area) bound torsion on a curve without a torsion-binomial component by 22 times its Newton area.
For the graph here that area is \(r\), the average horizontal width of its two rows.
The odd-order restriction permits the following smaller count.
The main researcher and the strategy contributor considered torsion-point counts for the phase interpolation question; the strategy contributor supplied the odd-target Galois comparison.

<a id="torsion-count"></a>
## Removing the even symmetry before counting points

If \(R(-z)=R(z)\) identically, comparison at zero forces \(k\) even.
Then \(F(-z)G(z)=F(z)G(-z)\); coprimality, equal degrees on reversal of sign and the nonzero constants imply that \(F,G\) are both even.
Write \(R(z)=R_1(z^2)\) with the two polynomial degrees halved and repeat until \(R_0(-z)\ne R_0(z)\).
After \(e\) reductions the polynomials of \(R_0\) have degree \(r_0=r/2^e\).
Every nonzero torsion preimage for the reduced map has \(2^e\) distinct torsion lifts, so it suffices to prove \(10r_0\) at that last stage.

Write \(R_0(z)=z^l A(z)/B(z)\), and let \(\eta\) be torsion with \(w=R_0(\eta)\) of odd order.
Set \(L=\mathrm{lcm}(\mathrm{ord}(\eta),\mathrm{ord}(w))\).
For odd \(L\), the exponent \(2\) is invertible modulo \(L\), giving \(R_0(\eta^2)=w^2\) by Galois conjugacy.
If \(L\) is twice an odd number, \(L/2+2\) is coprime to \(L\) and sends the pair to \((-\eta^2,w^2)\).
If \(4\mid L\), the exponent \(1+L/2\) sends the pair to \((-\eta,w)\).
In the last two cases the two-primary part of \(L\) all comes from \(\eta\).
The three resulting equations lie respectively on the zero sets of
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &A(z^2)B(z)^2-B(z^2)A(z)^2,\\
 &(-1)^l A(-z^2)B(z)^2-B(-z^2)A(z)^2,\\
 &(-1)^l A(-z)B(z)-B(-z)A(z).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two cannot be identically zero.
An identity \(R_0(\pm z^2)=R_0(z)^2\) would make the nonempty finite set of zeros and poles in \(\mathbb C^\times\) equal to its own preimage under \(z\mapsto\pm z^2\), doubling its cardinality.
The third polynomial is nonzero by the choice of \(R_0\).
Their respective degree bounds \(4r_0,4r_0,2r_0\) prove the torsion count.

The total ramification of a rational map of degree \(D\) on the projective line is \(2D-2\), by Riemann–Hurwitz.
For \(k\ne0\), zero and infinity each carry ramification \(|k|-1\) and both lie outside the selected fibers.
Hence at most \(2r\) of the \(sD\) preimages counted with multiplicity are lost when passing to distinct points, all in \(\mathbb C^\times\).
For \(k=0\), discarding zero and infinity costs at most two further points after the ramification loss \(2r-2\).
Subtracting the torsion count proves the assertion.

## Application to the original unrestricted reciprocal input

Let \(P\) be any monic reciprocal irreducible integer polynomial of positive Mahler measure and degree \(d=2\ell\ge6\).
Use its [exact half-degree encoding](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v1.md#universal-half-degree-delay-encoding) to choose a monic unit polynomial \(f\) of degree \(r=\ell+1\), coprime to \(g=z^r f(1/z)\), with
\[
                     z^\ell f+g=(z+1)P .
\]
The seed may have any number of exterior roots.
For \(R=-f/(z^\ell g)\) the map has degree \(D=d+1\).
Taking the seven seventh roots of unity gives at least \(7(d+1)-12(\ell+1)=d-5\) distinct nonzero nontorsion roots of the integer polynomial
\[
                         f^7+(z^\ell g)^7 .
\]
Thus its Mahler measure is strictly greater than one by Kronecker's theorem and its unit leading coefficient up to sign.
The same count applies to the seven distinct points in \(\mu_3\cup\mu_5\), so the union of the zero sets of \(f^3+(z^\ell g)^3\) and \(f^5+(z^\ell g)^5\) already contains at least \(d-5\) distinct nontorsion points.

At a node \(y^d=w\), the corresponding phase polynomial \(wf+z^\ell g\) takes the value \(y^\ell(y+1)P(y)\).
Counting its nontorsion roots therefore tests a phase model attached to the actual original input.
The count alone gives neither a uniform positive lower bound for its logarithmic Mahler measure nor the exponential size of the integer \(|\operatorname{Res}(P,z^{7d}-1)|\).
In this encoding the seed degree is \(d/2+1\); the [moving-seed error estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v3.md#statement) does not provide that transfer.
