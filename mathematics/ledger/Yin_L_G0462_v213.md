# Dyadic inertia in the exceptional reciprocal sections

<a id="inertia"></a>
## Finite inertia and the common component in characteristic two

Fix \(s\ge3\) with \(s\equiv2\bmod3\), and let \(a,b\) be coprime positive integers.
Put \(g(T)=T^s-T-1,\ B(T)=1-T^{s-1}-T^s\), and \(F(X)=g(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\).
Define \(c=2\) if either \(s\) is even and \(3\mid b\), or \(s\) is odd and \(b\equiv a\bmod3\), and put \(c=0\) otherwise.
In the rational splitting field of \(F\), each inertia element at a finite prime \(p\) moves at most \(S_p\) distinct roots, where
\[
 S_p=
 \begin{cases}
 4s,&p\ne2,\\
 4(s-2)+2c,&p=2,\ a\ {\rm odd},\\
 2a+c,&p=2,\ a\ {\rm even}.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
At an odd prime the [coprime reduction and derivative argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#inertia) applies because a common zero \(t\) of \(g,B\) would satisfy \(t^{s-2}=-1\) and \(t^2+t+1=0\), whence \(t^3=1\) and \(1=-1\).
The derivative argument there therefore bounds the total excess multiplicity of the reduction by \(2s\), including in characteristics dividing \(a\) or \(b\).

Work now over \(\mathbb F_2\) and let \(C=T^2+T+1\).
The [common factor calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v391.md#nonvertical) gives \(\gcd(\bar g,\bar B)=C\).
At either primitive cube root \(t\) the two derivatives \(\bar g'(t),\bar B'(t)\) are \(1,1\) for even \(s\), and \(t+1,t\) for odd \(s\).
In particular each zero is simple in both polynomials.
Put \(G=\bar g/C,\ V=\bar B/C,\ H(X)=G(X^a)+X^bV(X^a)\), so \(\bar F=C(X^a)H\).
The polynomials \(G,V\) are coprime of degree \(s-2\), both have constant term one, and their linear coefficients are respectively zero and one.

Every zero \(z\) of \(H\) is nonzero, with \(G(t),V(t)\ne0\) for \(t=z^a\) and \(z^b=G(t)/V(t)\).
The two powers determine \(z\) by Bézout, hence different zeros of \(H\) give different \(t\).
For \(C(t)=0\) the ratio \(G(t)/V(t)=\bar g'(t)/\bar B'(t)\) is \(1\) for even \(s\) and \(t\) for odd \(s\).
For any prescribed nonzero \(t,y\), the equations \(z^a=t,\ z^b=y\) require \(t^b=y^a\), and this condition suffices because integers \(i,j\) with \(ia+jb=1\) give \(z=t^i y^j\).
This proves that \(H\) and \(C(X^a)\) share exactly \(c\) distinct roots.

For odd \(a\), let \(e_z\) be the multiplicity of a zero \(z\) of \(H\).
With \(t=X^a\), in characteristic two one has
\[
 XV(t)H'(X)=(bV(t)+atV'(t))H(X)+R(t),\qquad
 R=aT(G'V-GV')-bGV.
\]
Here \(\deg R\le2(s-2)\).
For odd \(b\) its constant term is nonzero, while for even \(b\) the constant term of \(G'V-GV'\) is one, so \(R\ne0\) in either case.
The identity forces \(R(X^a)\) to vanish to order at least \(e_z-1\) at \(z\).
Since \(X\mapsto X^a\) has nonzero derivative there and the \(t\)-values are distinct, \(\sum_z(e_z-1)\le2(s-2)\).
The factor \(C(X^a)\) is separable for odd \(a\), so the full excess of \(\bar F\) is at most \(2(s-2)+c\).
Each residue of multiplicity \(e\ge2\) contains at most \(e\le2(e-1)\) distinct characteristic-zero roots, and inertia preserves residues and fixes singleton fibers.
This gives the odd-\(a\) bound in (1).

For even \(a\), \(b\) is odd and \(H'=X^{b-1}V(X^a)\) is nonzero at every zero of \(H\).
Consequently every nonsingleton residue lies above \(C(X^a)=0\).
These fibers account for at most \(\deg C(X^a)=2a\) roots from the common component and \(c\) additional positions from \(H\), proving (1) at two even with wild inertia.

## The block field is unramified at every odd prime

Let \(x\) be any nontorsion root of \(F\), \(K=\mathbb Q(x)\), \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and \(S=\max(4s,S_2)\).
For every \(\mathbb Q\subsetneq E\subseteq K\) one has \(2[K:E]\le S\), and for \(d>S\) no such \(E\) is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Indeed [finite inertia generates the rational splitting group up to conjugacy](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields).
Some inertia element acts nontrivially on the \(E\)-embeddings and therefore moves at least two fibers of \(K\)-embeddings, each of size \([K:E]\).
For normal \(E\) this element moves every fiber, hence all \(d\) conjugates.

Take \(\max(a,b)\) sufficiently large in terms of fixed \(s\), uniformly over the choice of \(x\).
The [characteristic-zero degree, torsion-quotient and reciprocity arguments](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v397.md#trace) also apply to these seeds because \(g,B\) are coprime in characteristic zero by the same cube-root contradiction, the support differences span \(\mathbb Z^2\), and \(M(g)\le\sqrt3<\theta^2\) for the least Pisot number \(\theta\).
Thus \(d\) tends uniformly to infinity, the minimal polynomial is reciprocal, and its \(n=d/2\) inverse-pair traces are the uncompressed labels.
Since \(m(x)\le\log6\), the [alternating block theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v270.md#statement) gives a unique partition of the traces into minimal nontrivial blocks of size \(h\gg d/(\log(3d))^{212}\), with independent copies of \(A_h\) acting on the blocks.
Let \(L_{\mathcal B}\) be the field fixed in the rational splitting field by the kernel of the action on that partition.
An element moving blocks moves at least two whole blocks and hence at least \(4h\) conjugates of \(x\).
Since \(4h>4s\) eventually, (1) shows that \(L_{\mathcal B}/\mathbb Q\) is unramified at every odd prime.
For odd \(a\) the same holds at two, since \(S_2\le4s-4\).
Minkowski's discriminant bound now gives \(L_{\mathcal B}=\mathbb Q\), and transitivity forces a single block.
The trace group is therefore \(A_n\) or \(S_n\) for every sufficiently large primitive direction with odd \(a\).

## A necessary size for an imprimitive direction

If several minimal blocks occur in a sufficiently large primitive direction, then \(a\) is even and \(L_{\mathcal B}\) is a nontrivial Galois extension ramified at two and unramified at every other finite prime.
Its Galois group is generated by conjugates of dyadic inertia.
At any prime above two some inertia element must move two or more blocks, so \(4h\le2a+c\), and at least \(4h\) conjugates of \(x\) reduce to zeros of \(C(X^a)\) there.
In particular \(d\ll (a+1)(\log(3d))^{212}\).
On writing \(r=b/a\), the [primitive factor degree estimate](Yin_L_G0462_v198.md#height) gives \(d\gg_s a\sqrt r\) for all sufficiently large \(r\), and the parent degree gives \(d\le sa+b\).
Together these imply the necessary bound
\[
                  \frac ba\ll_s\bigl(\log(3(a+b))\bigr)^{424}.
\]
Bounded \(r\) also satisfies it.
Consequently the trace group is eventually \(A_n\) or \(S_n\) for each factor along any primitive sequence with \((b/a)/(\log(3(a+b)))^{424}\to\infty\), for either parity of \(a\).
The existence of a proper block system in the remaining even-\(a\) directions is unresolved.
