# Individual factor heights and exact exterior counts on a fixed rational graph

<a id="height"></a>
## The same limiting height for every primitive section factor

Let \(A,B\in\mathbb Z[T]\) be nonzero and coprime over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(A(0)=\pm1\), leading coefficient of \(B\) equal to \(\pm1\), and support differences of \(A(T)+YB(T)\) of rank two.
For coprime positive \(a,b\), put \(r=b/a\), \(F_{a,b}(X)=A(X^a)+X^bB(X^a)\), and take \(r\to\infty\).
Let \(P\) be any monic nontorsion irreducible factor, \(d=\deg P\), and \(\alpha\) any root of \(P\).
With \(h\) the absolute logarithmic height, \(m\) the logarithmic Mahler measure and \(\sigma\) normalized circle measure, uniformly in \(a,b,P\) one has
\[
 \begin{split}
 I&=\int_{\mathbb T}\log\max(|A|,|B|)\,d\sigma>0,\qquad d\gg_{A,B}a\sqrt r,\\
 b h(\alpha)&=I+O_{A,B}\left(r^{-1/2}+
                         \sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}d}\right).
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Jensen's formula in the second variable gives \(I=m(A(T)+YB(T))\).
The [fixed two-block limit](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v193.md#statement) treats fixed \(a\), and the [cubic projection](Yin_L_G0462_v169.md#degree) treats a particular graph with unit resultant.
Here the projected arguments stay of bounded polynomial degree while \(a,b\) vary independently subject to primitivity.

For large \(r\) the two blocks are disjoint, the section has unit endpoints, and the distance from each endpoint to the next occupied exponent is at least \(a\).
In particular \(\alpha\) and \(t=\alpha^a\) are algebraic integer units.
Since \(\alpha^b=-A(t)/B(t)\), coprimality of \(a,b\) gives \(\mathbb Q(t)=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\).
The projective height of \([A(t):B(t)]\) has the exact expression
\[
 b h(\alpha)=\frac1d\sum_{\sigma':\mathbb Q(t)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
       \log\max(|A(\sigma't)|,|B(\sigma't)|)
       -\frac1d\log|N_{\mathbb Q(t)/\mathbb Q} B(t)| .                  \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the ratio is an algebraic unit, so at each finite place the two entries have equal absolute value, giving the displayed norm correction by the product formula.
This norm is a nonzero integer.
The absolute resultant of \(B(X^a)\) with \(F_{a,b}\) is \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,A)|^a\), by evaluating the section at the roots of \(B(X^a)\), with the same identity for constant \(B=\pm1\).
Thus the product of these absolute norms over all irreducible factors, including torsion factors and their multiplicities, is exactly \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,A)|^a\).

Writing \(w=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\), the archimedean test in (2) is at most \(O_{A,B}(1)+w\log^+|t|\).
Hence \(bh(\alpha)\le O_{A,B}(1)+wa h(\alpha)\), giving \(h(t)\ll_{A,B}1/r\).
At every conjugate the endpoint gaps give \(|\log|\sigma'\alpha||\le(\log L)/a\) with \(L=\|A\|_1+\|B\|_1\), by comparing with the exterior leading term or reversing the polynomial.
The [all-root square bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0855_v2.md#statement) and the unit condition yield
\[
 1/10\le d\sum_{\sigma'}(\log|\sigma'\alpha|)^2
       \le (2\log L/a)d^2 h(\alpha)\ll_{A,B}d^2/(ab).
\]
This proves the degree bound and gives \(a/d\ll_{A,B}r^{-1/2}\).
Apply the [quantitative polynomial maximum estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test) to the degree-\(d\) unit \(t\) of log measure \(dh(t)\ll d/r\) and the fixed pair \(A,B\).
The norm budget in (2) now gives (1).
For positivity, \(I=m(B)+\int\log^+|A/B|\,d\sigma\).
If \(I=0\), monic normalization and Kronecker's theorem make \(B\) a product of a monomial and cyclotomic factors, and continuity gives \(|A|\le|B|\) on the circle.
Every circle zero of \(B\) would be a zero of \(A\), so \(B\) is a signed monomial.
Then \(|A|\le1\) there and \(A(0)=\pm1\), forcing \(A\) constant by the maximum principle, contrary to rank two.

There is also a limiting distribution for the numbers \(b\log|\sigma'\alpha|\), with equal weights on all \(d\) embeddings.
It is the distribution of \(\log|A/B|\) on the circle under \(\sigma\).
Indeed the conjugates of \(t\) tend in distribution to \(\sigma\) by [small-height equidistribution](../audits/literature/Bil97/Yin_U001.md#strict-small-points-and-orbit-measures), since \(h(t)\to0\) and \(d\to\infty\).
At every conjugate \(b\log|\sigma'\alpha|=\log|A(\sigma't)/B(\sigma't)|\), and the finitely many possible singularities on the circle have Haar measure zero, which suffices for bounded continuous tests.
If the two modulus functions are not identical on the circle, their equality set there is finite by the Laurent polynomial identity test.
The fraction of exterior roots therefore tends to \(\sigma\{|A|>|B|\}\).
The positive first moment of the limiting distribution is \(I-m(B)\), whereas the positive first moments of the individual distributions tend to \(I\) by (1).

## The contribution of each irreducible denominator factor

For each monic rational irreducible divisor \(q\mid B\), let \(e_q\) be its multiplicity and \(T_q\) its set of distinct roots \(\tau\) with \(|\tau|>1\).
Around these roots of all the divisors choose disjoint fixed small closed disks in \(|T|>1\), each free of other zeros of \(AB\).
Let \(k_\tau\) count the conjugates of \(\alpha\) whose \(a\)-th powers lie in the disk around \(\tau\).
Uniformly over the factors and primitive directions, for all large \(r\),
\[
 \frac{r}{e_q d}\sum_{\tau\in T_q}k_\tau\log|\tau|
       =m(q)+O_{A,B}\left(\frac{\log(2r)}{r^{1/4}}\right).              \tag{3}
\]
This holds for nonreciprocal sections and for denominator divisors with unit-circle roots.
In particular every individual factor must reach an exterior pole of every \(q\) with \(m(q)>0\).
Summing (3) after multiplication by \(e_q\) identifies the missing positive first moment \(m(B)\) with these pole contributions.

The product of \(|N_{\mathbb Q(t)/\mathbb Q}q(t)|\) over all rational section factors with multiplicity is \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,A)|^a\), again by evaluating at the roots of the monic \(q(X^a)\).
This also holds for torsion factors since the same Bézout recovery gives their field equality.
Every individual norm is a nonzero integer.
The conjugates \(t'\) of \(t\) lie in the fixed annulus \(1/L\le|t'|\le L\) by the endpoint estimate.
Near an exterior \(\tau\) of multiplicity \(e_q\) in \(B\), the identity \(|t'|^r|B(t')|=|A(t')|\) gives
\[
 |t'-\tau|\ll e^{-c r},\qquad
           -\log|q(t')|=(r/e_q)\log|\tau|+O_{A,B}(1),                 \tag{4}
\]
for some \(c>0\), since \(A\) and \(B/q^{e_q}\) are nonzero on the chosen disk and \(q\) has a simple zero there.
The total number \(K=\sum_{q,\tau} k_\tau\) is \(O_{A,B}(d/r)\), because each such \(t'\) contributes a fixed positive amount to \(dh(t)\ll d/r\).

Outside the exterior disks, one has \(|q(t')|\gg_{A,B}1/r\) for all large \(r\).
Indeed near any zero of \(q\) strictly inside the circle, \(|t'|^r|B(t')|\to0\) uniformly, while \(A\) is bounded away from zero, so no \(t'\) occurs.
At a circle zero \(\xi\), if \(|t'-\xi|<1/r\), then \(|t'|^r\le(1+1/r)^r\le e\) and \(|B(t')|\ll1/r\), again contradicting \(|A(t')|\gg1\).
Simplicity of the zeros of \(q\) and compactness on the remaining parts of the annulus prove the bound.

Set \(n=\lceil r^2\rceil\) and \(\phi_n=\log\max(|q|,1/n)\).
On the circle its integral is \(m(q)+O_q(1/n)\), since each circle zero is simple and the integral of \(\log(1/(n|s|))\) over \(|s|\le1/n\) is \(2/n\).
Apply the [polynomial maximum estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v194.md#test) to \((nq,1)\) and subtract \(\log n\).
The error of the orbit average from its circle mean is
\[
 O_{A,B}\left(r^{-1}+d^{-1}+
 \sqrt{\log(2r)\left(r^{-1}+
                    \frac{\log(2r)+\log(2d)}d\right)}\right)
       \ll_{A,B}\frac{\log(2r)}{r^{1/4}},
\]
using \(d\gg\sqrt r\).
For large \(r\), \(\phi_n(t')-\log|q(t')|\) vanishes off the exterior disks of \(q\), by the lower bound there and separation from the other divisors' zeros.
On the disks of \(q\), (4) makes this difference \((r/e_q)\log|\tau|+O_{A,B}(\log(2r))\).
The orbit average of \(\log|q(t')|\) lies between zero and \(a\log|\operatorname{Res}(q,A)|/d\).
Combining these estimates, with \(K/d\ll1/r\) and \(a/d\ll r^{-1/2}\), proves (3).

## Exact relations when a factor receives few exterior pole roots

Suppose one divisor \(q_0\) has exactly one exterior root \(\tau_0\), and write \(e_0=e_{q_0}\), \(k_0=k_{\tau_0}\).
For each \(q\) and each distinct modulus \(\rho>1\) of a root in \(T_q\), let \(n_{q,\rho}\) count the roots there of modulus \(\rho\) and let \(k_{q,\rho}\) sum their allocated counts.
There is \(\eta_{A,B}>0\) such that whenever \(K\le r^{\eta_{A,B}}\) at sufficiently large \(r\), each divisor satisfies the exact algebraic identity
\[
             \prod_{\rho>1}\rho^{\,e_0 k_{q,\rho}-e_q k_0 n_{q,\rho}}=1 .
                                                                       \tag{5}
\]
When the distinct exterior moduli of \(q\) are multiplicatively independent, each exponent in (5) is zero.

Indeed \(m(q_0)=\log|\tau_0|>0\), so (3) at \(q_0\) gives \(d/r\ll k_0\le K\) and \(e_0 d/r=k_0+O_{A,B}(K\log(2r)/r^{1/4})\).
By Jensen's formula \(m(q)=\sum_\rho n_{q,\rho}\log\rho\).
Multiplying (3) by \(e_0 e_q d/r\) and substituting the estimate for \(e_0 d/r\) shows that the logarithm of the left side of (5) has magnitude \(O_{A,B}(K\log(2r)/r^{1/4})\).
All \(\rho\) are fixed positive algebraic numbers since \(\rho^2=\tau\bar\tau\).
For products \(x\) of these numbers to integer exponents of magnitude \(O_{A,B}(K)\), either \(x=1\) or \(|\log x|\ge\exp(-C_{A,B}(K+1))\).
To see this, choose a common integer denominator for the numbers and their inverses in one number field.
After multiplying \(x-1\) by that denominator to an \(O_{A,B}(K+1)\)-th power, its nonzero norm has absolute value at least one, and all its other conjugates are bounded by \(\exp(O_{A,B}(K+1))\).
This bounds \(|x-1|\) below on the same exponential scale, hence also \(|\log x|\) when \(x\) is near one.
This already proves (5) when \(K\le c_{A,B}\log r\) for a sufficiently small \(c_{A,B}>0\).
For the power range, the [Baker–Wüstholz theorem for an ordinary complex logarithmic form](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#logarithmic-form) gives \(|\log x|\ge(2K)^{-C'_{A,B}}\) whenever the form in the real logarithms of the fixed \(\rho>1\) is nonzero.
Indeed the field degree and modified heights of the arguments are fixed, and the modified coefficient height is \(O_{A,B}(\log(2K))\).
One may take the same \(C'_{A,B}\) for the finitely many nonempty sets of moduli.
Any positive \(\eta_{A,B}<1/(4(C'_{A,B}+1))\) makes this lower bound exceed the orbit error uniformly in \(1\le K\le r^{\eta_{A,B}}\) for sufficiently large \(r\), proving (5).

## Three exterior poles with equal counts

Take \(B(T)=(T-2)(T^3-4T+1)\) and \(A(T)=T^4 B(T^{-1})\).
For sufficiently large \(r\), every nontorsion factor with at most \(r^{10^{-14}}\) exterior roots has the same positive number of roots over each of the three exterior poles.
Its number of exterior roots is divisible by three and is divisible by six whenever \(a\) is even.

The cubic has no rational root and has three real roots in \((-3,-2),(0,1),(1,2)\), by changes of sign.
It has no common factor with its reversal, since irreducible reciprocal polynomials of odd degree have a root \(1\) or \(-1\).
Neither \(2\) nor \(1/2\) is a root of the cubic, so \(A,B\) are coprime.
The resultant in this example is genuinely nonunit.
Writing \(q=T^3-4T+1\) and \(q^*=T^3q(1/T)\), one has \(|A(2)|=21\), \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,1-2T)|=7\), and \(q^*-q=4T(1-T)\) gives \(|\operatorname{Res}(q,q^*)|=4^3|q(0)q(1)|=128\).
Thus \(|\operatorname{Res}(B,A)|=18816\).
Both endpoint hypotheses hold, the graph has rank two, and \(B\) has no circle zeros.
On the circle \(|A/B|=1\), so \(|\log|A(t')/B(t')||\le C|\log|t'||\) in a fixed annulus about it by the bounded radial derivative there.
The root equation \(r\log|t'|=\log|A(t')/B(t')|\) forces all conjugates in this annulus onto the circle once \(r>C\).
Farther outside, \(|B(t')|\ll e^{-c' r}\) on the fixed root annulus, placing every remaining exterior conjugate in a pole disk.
Thus \(K\) counts all exterior roots of the factor, and \(K>0\) since the root is a nontorsion unit.

The two exterior moduli of the irreducible cubic are multiplicatively independent.
Its three roots have product \(-1\), so their vector of logarithmic moduli has sum zero and is nonzero.
The transitive Galois group on three letters contains a three-cycle.
Any relation between the two exterior moduli, squared to remove the signs of the real roots, implies that its exponent vector with zero in the third position is orthogonal to all cyclic translates of the log vector.
These translates span the real plane of sum zero, since a three-cycle has no real invariant line on that plane.
The exponent vector must consequently be constant, hence zero.
Use \(q_0=T-2\) in (5), with both multiplicities one, to conclude that the counts at the two cubic poles equal the count at \(2\).
For the stated exponent only the two logarithms of the cubic exterior moduli enter the nontrivial comparison.
Their field has degree \(3\le D\le6\), since it contains a cubic root and is contained in the splitting field.
Each has absolute height \(m(T^3-4T+1)/3<(\log6)/3<1\), and its real logarithm is less than \(\log3\), so its modified argument height in the cited theorem is at most one.
The integer coefficients \(k_\tau-k_0\) have magnitudes at most \(K\), giving modified coefficient height at most \(\log(2K)\) for a nonzero form.
The explicit constant of that theorem is \(C(2,D)\le18\cdot 3!\cdot2^3\cdot192^4\log24<4\cdot10^{12}\), and \(10^{-14}(4\cdot10^{12}+1)<1/4\).
For even \(a\), a real conjugate of \(\alpha\) cannot have its \(a\)-th power in the disk about the negative pole, chosen in the left half-plane and stable under conjugation.
The count there is even by complex conjugation.
This yields the divisibility by six and rules out an allocation to only one or two exterior roots throughout this small-count range.

As an actual irreducibility consequence, for \(a=2\) and all sufficiently large odd \(b\), removing all cyclotomic factors leaves one simple rational irreducible factor.
Indeed \(X^{-b}F_{2,b}(X)\) converges uniformly to \(B(X^2)\) on each small exterior disk about a square root of one of the three exterior poles.
These six roots of \(B(X^2)\) are simple, so Rouché's theorem gives one root inside each disk, counted with multiplicity, and the preceding annulus argument exhausts the exterior roots.
Every nontorsion factor has a positive exterior count divisible by six, proving the assertion including simplicity.
This irreducible family has fixed \(a=2\), while the divisibility constraint also applies along growing primitive directions.
