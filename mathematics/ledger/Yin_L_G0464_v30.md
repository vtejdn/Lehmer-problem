# Nonreal roots among the factors responsible for the grid sign change

<a id="nonreal-proportion"></a>
## The proportion below the square-root degree scale

Use the [strict full odd-grid chamber](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#statement), with \(p=2n+1\), \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\), and the integral trace representative \(h\) satisfying \(p\rho_j=(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)\).
Consider a sequence with \(p\to\infty\) and \(1<M(P)\to1\) for the original reciprocal lifts.
Let \(G\) be the product, once each, of the primitive irreducible factors of \(h\) that occur with odd multiplicity and have a real zero in \((-2,2)\), taking their leading coefficients positive.
Write \(d=\deg G\) and let \(H\) count the nonreal zeros of \(G\), counting both members of a conjugate pair.
Then \(d\to\infty\), and for every \(0\le a<1/2\),
\[
       \limsup\frac{\log d}{\log p}\le a
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \liminf\frac{H}{d}\ \ge\ \frac{1-2a}{1-a}.                       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, along any subsequence where \(G\) is totally real one has \(\liminf\log d/\log p\ge1/2\).
This conclusion concerns the product of the factors that account for sign changes inside the trace interval.
On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained (1) by counting close pairs of unit-circle roots of the lift and using its two endpoint values to control external real traces.

<a id="external-real"></a>
## The two endpoint values

The [independent findings](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v69.md#nonreal-proportion-review) check these endpoint estimates and the angular pair count below.

At the grid nodes \(h\) has the strict signs of the residues, positive through \(c\) and negative thereafter.
The quotient \(h/G\) has even multiplicity at every real zero in \((-2,2)\), so its signs at all the nodes are the same.
Thus \(G\) has an odd number of zeros in the transition gap \((b_{c+1},b_c)\).
At least one irreducible factor of \(G\) contains such a zero, whose [algebraic degree tends to infinity](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#endpoint-degree).
This proves \(d\to\infty\).

For the proof of (1), fix \(\epsilon>0\) with \(a<1/2-\epsilon\).
Eventually \(d\le p^{1/2-\epsilon}\), and \(G\) is an integral divisor of \(h\).
The [divisor measure bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0895_v1.md#statement) makes \(G^*(z)=z^dG(z+z^{-1})\) monic eventually, with \(\limsup M(G^*)\le149/100\).
In particular \(G\) is monic and \(M(G^*)\le2\) eventually.
Neither \(2\) nor \(-2\) is a root of \(G\), since its defining irreducible factors each have an interior real zero.
The squarefree polynomial \(G\) therefore gives a squarefree \(G^*\), as distinct trace roots give disjoint quadratic root sets and each quadratic \(z^2-yz+1\) has two distinct roots.

The following root count and the paired determinant below also apply to squarefree integral \(G\) of arbitrary positive leading coefficient \(A\), with \(G(2)G(-2)\ne0\) and \(M(G^*)\le C\) for a fixed \(C\ge1\).
The determinant estimate additionally uses the indicated single sign transition at nonzero grid values.
For a root \(y\) of \(G\), let \(\lambda_y\ge1\) be the larger modulus of its two reciprocal lifts.
Then \(A\prod_y\lambda_y=M(G^*)\) and \(|y^2-4|\le4\lambda_y^2\), by writing \(y^2-4=(z-z^{-1})^2\).
For each of the \(q\) real roots outside \([-2,2]\), put \(\ell_y=\log\lambda_y>0\).
For those roots \(|y^2-4|=(e^{\ell_y}-e^{-\ell_y})^2\le4\ell_y^2\lambda_y^2\).
Set \(L=\log M(G^*)\le\log C\), so \(\sum_{\rm exterior}\ell_y\le L\).
For \(q>0\), the nonzero integral endpoint values and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
       1\le |G(2)G(-2)|=A^2\prod_y|y^2-4|
          \le 4^d e^{2L}(L/q)^{2q}.
\]
Consequently \(q\log(q/L)\le d\log2+L\), so \(q=O_C(d/\log d)\) as \(d\to\infty\).
Indeed \(q\le\sqrt d\) suffices directly, and otherwise \(\log(q/L)\ge(\log d)/3\) for all sufficiently large \(d\) at fixed \(C\).

<a id="close-pairs"></a>
## Pairing the unit-circle roots inside grid intervals

Let \(I\) be the number of roots of \(G\) in \((-2,2)\), so \(d=I+q+H\).
Each gives one upper and one lower unit-circle root of \(G^*\).
Partition the upper semicircle at the angles \(2\pi j/p\) for \(1\le j\le n\).
There are no roots at these nodes or at the endpoints \(1,-1\).
In every interval between successive nodes the number of roots is even except in the unique transition interval, where it is odd.
This follows from the signs of \(G\) at its endpoints and the simplicity of its roots.
Pair the roots within each of these intervals, leaving one unpaired in the transition interval, and make the conjugate pairings on the lower semicircle.
In each upper endpoint interval pair every root with its lower conjugate.
The angular distance across the endpoint is at most \(4\pi/p\) at \(1\) and at most \(2\pi/p\) at \(-1\).
Altogether this gives \(I-1\) disjoint pairs of roots of \(G^*\), each with chord distance at most \(4\pi/p\).

Put \(D=2d\) and take the Vandermonde matrix with rows \((1,z,\ldots,z^{D-1})\) at all roots of \(G^*\).
Its determinant has absolute value at least \(A^{-(D-1)}\), since the nonzero integer discriminant has absolute value \(A^{2D-2}|\det|^2\).
For each selected pair subtract the first row from the second, preserving the determinant.
The difference row for a pair \(z,w\) has norm at most \(\sqrt D\,D|z-w|\), by \(|z^k-w^k|\le k|z-w|\) on the unit circle.
Every ordinary row has norm at most \(\sqrt D\max(1,|z|)^{D-1}\).
Hadamard's inequality and \(\prod_z\max(1,|z|)=M(G^*)/A\) now give, after multiplication by \(A^{D-1}\),
\[
      1\le D^{D/2}M(G^*)^{D-1}\left(\frac{4\pi D}{p}\right)^{I-1}.
\]
Returning to the shrinking sequence, take \(C=2\).
Taking logarithms, with \(p/(4\pi D)>1\) eventually, yields
\[
     \frac{I-1}{d}\le
     \frac{\log(2d)+2\log2}{\log(p/(8\pi d))}.
\]
The upper limit on the right is at most \(a/(1-a)\).
Together with \(q/d\to0\) and \(d\to\infty\), this gives (1).
If the lower degree exponent on a totally real subsequence were less than \(1/2\), one could extract a further subsequence with upper exponent strictly below \(1/2\), contradicting (1).
