# Prime-power coefficient sums and a trace with no real root below minus four

2026-11-24.
This is an unreviewed exploration for Yin's [consecutive-unit construction](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#decision-consecutive-unit-construction-2026-11-23).
The main agent and three research agents developed the following arguments in parallel.
The attempts concern the [Salem trace criterion](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#criterion), which requires a monic irreducible trace polynomial with one root in \((0,2/q)\), all others in \((-4,0)\), and an integral second coordinate.

## Changing the endpoint sign in the Chebyshev construction

Put \(p=q+1,\ L=2q+1,\ M=pq\).
Choose \(n\ge4\) even and \(2M\ge4^{n-1}\), and put \(d=n-3\).
Take \(B_j(x)=U_j((x+2)/2)\) as in the [original family](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v883.md#family), and form
\[
 C_d=B_d+B_{d-1},\qquad H=x^{n-1}(x+4)+2(Mx-1)C_d .
\]
The degree four choice works at every \(q\ge6\), including \(q=(3^{2k}-1)/2\) for \(k\ge2\), and the allowed degrees grow with \(q\).
For \(n=4\) the polynomial is
\[
 x^4+4x^3+2Mx^2+2(3M-1)x-6 .
\]
It has a positive root \(w<1/M\) because \(H(0)=-2(2d+1)<0\) and \(H(1/M)>0\).
All nonleading coefficients are even, and the constant coefficient is two modulo four, so Eisenstein gives irreducibility.
The signed norm \(M^nH(1/M)=L^2\) gives \((2q+1)^2/(1-Mw)\) integral since an algebraic integer divides its norm.
Thus \(T+T^{-1}=2+w,\ T>1\), and \(Y=(pT-q)/(p-qT)\) give positive units with \(Y>T\), by the trace identity in the cited criterion.

At \(x=2\cos\theta-2\) the trigonometric expression for \(C_d\) is \(\sin((2d+1)\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2)\).
Its values at \(\theta_j=(2j+1)\pi/(2d+1)\), \(0\le j\le d\), alternate in sign and have absolute value at least one.
The last point is \(x=-4\).
At these points \(|x^{n-1}(x+4)|\le4^{n-1}|x|\), while \(2|Mx-1|>2M|x|\).
The signs of \(H\) therefore alternate, giving \(n-3\) roots strictly inside \((-4,0)\).
The coefficients of \(x^{n-1}\) and \(x^{n-2}\) in \(H\) are \(4\) and \(2M\).
The sum of the squares of its roots is \(16-4M<0\), which forces a conjugate nonreal pair and exhausts the degree.
All negative real conjugates now lie in the required interval.
The remaining two traces are nonreal, and either one, denoted by \(\xi\), satisfies \(|\operatorname{Im}\xi|^2\ge2M-8\).
If the two positive units were powers of a common unit, the [short-power radius bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#radius) would instead give \(|\operatorname{Im}\xi|\le 2\sqrt{(p/q)^2-1}\).
The attempted endpoint repair therefore fails to construct the required powers throughout the indicated range.

## Choosing an odd prime power for the coefficient sum

Let \(L=\ell^a\ge7\) with \(\ell\) an odd prime.
For an actual Salem solution let \(K=M^nH(1/M)\) denote the signed norm at the pole and \(u\) the shorter primitive exponent.
The [pole norm identity](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v877.md#norm) gives \(K\mid L^u\) and \(K\equiv1\bmod M\).
The multiplicative order of \(\ell\) modulo \(M=(L^2-1)/4\) is \(2a\), because a proper divisor of \(2a\) is at most \(a\) and \(1<\ell^d\le L<M\) in that range.
The unique element of order two in its cyclic subgroup is \(L\bmod M\), and \(L+1<M\).
It follows that \(K=L^{2j}\) with \(0\le 2j\le u\).
The trace factors \(1-Mw_i\) away from the positive place are positive, so \(0<w<1/M\).
For a trace whose norm divides \(L^{2n}\), the same residue argument gives powers \(L^{2j}\) with \(0\le j\le n\), before the exponent bound is used.

Write \(H(t-4)=\sum_{k=0}^n h_k t^k\).
For \(K=L^{2j}\) the coefficient test for integrality of \(L^2/(1-Mw)\) is
\[
 L^{2(j-k)}\mid h_k \qquad(0\le k<j).
\]
Indeed the monic polynomial of \(L^2/(1-Mw)\) is \(M^nX^n H((1-L^2/X)/M)/K\).
Its coefficients in the integral basis \(X^{n-k}(X-1)^k\) of polynomials of degree at most \(n\) are \(M^{n-k}L^{2k}h_k/K\).
The change of basis is unimodular and \(M\) is coprime to \(L\).
Equivalently \(H\in(x+4,L^2)^j\).
Evaluation at \(1/M\) and Gauss's lemma give the expression
\[
 H=x^{n-j}(x+4)^j+(Mx-1)G,\qquad G\in\mathbb Z[x],\quad \deg G\le n-2.
\]
For \(j\ge1\) the coefficient test on \(G\) is \(G\in(x+4,L^2)^{j-1}\), by comparing coefficients at \(x=-4\) in \(Mx-1=M(x+4)-L^2\).
At \(j=1\) every such deformation has integral graph coordinates when the distinguished trace is positive below the pole.
For \(j\ge2\) this requires \(L^{2(j-1)}\mid G(-4)\).
At every \(j\ge1\), the desired signature requires \((-1)^{n-1}G(-4)>0\).

## Simultaneous arithmetic screens for a family of heights

The following parameter proposal still requires a separate audit of its field intersection, density input and exact sieve scope.
Take \(L=3^{2k}\), so \(q=(L-1)/2\) is even, and take \(u=4\).
For each \(q\) choose \(2^h>q^3,\ 3^b>q^2\) and distinct primes \(R_-,R_+>q^3\) other than 2 and 3.
Seek arbitrarily large primes \(v\) splitting completely in \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^h},3^{1/2^h})\), with \(v\equiv4\bmod 3^bR_-\) and \(v\equiv-4\bmod R_+\).
The proposed Chebotarev application uses that \(F\) is a Galois extension of 2-power degree ramified only at 2 and 3.
Its intersection with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^bR_-R_+})\) is contained in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{3^b})\) by inertia at \(R_\pm\), and has degree at most two.
The residue 4 acts trivially there.
The relevant source for prescribed Frobenius classes is the [registered density statement](../audits/literature/KW22/readme.md#author-edition).

Splitting gives \(\operatorname{ord}_v(3)\le(v-1)/2^h\), within the [necessary degree scale](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v875.md#statement) for a hypothetical Salem factor.
Both \(v-4\) and \(v+4\) have a prime divisor exceeding \(q^3\), which supplies the outside part for each [fixed finite set of primes](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v876.md#statement) eventually.
The [binary orbit tests](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v880.md#statement) use \(N=v-4\), an odd multiple of \(3^b\).
The minimum orbit bound can use 3; a fixed bounded selection of primes all growing faster than \(\log q\) omits 3, so the product of their full powers is at most \(N/3^b\).
Choosing \(v\) sufficiently large also permits \(N\gg q\log q\).
These tests concern parameter choices only.
No field of the indicated degree or unit with these exponents has been produced.

## Further restrictions on the correction polynomial awaiting review

For a hypothetical successful sequence with \(q\to\infty\) on the prime-power branches above, \(D=2n\), \(2j\le u\), and the [degree inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v869.md#degree) gives \(j=O(n/q)\).
Write \(a_1=-\sum w_i\) and \(A,B\) for the leading two coefficients of \(G\).
The [short-trace moments](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v885.md#coefficients) give \(a_1\sim2n\) and \(\sum w_i^2\sim6n\).
The top coefficients of \(H\) are \(4j+MA\) and \(8j(j-1)+MB-A\).
Substitution into the first two Newton identities gives
\[
 MA\sim2n,\qquad 2B/A=MA+8j-3+o(1).
\]
An integral ratio \(B/A\), as for a scalar multiple of a monic Chebyshev polynomial, is incompatible with this parity condition at unbounded \(q\).
For the first nonunit branch there is also a finite test under the desired signature.
At \(n\ge6\) the integer \(a_1-4=:h\) is positive by applying the arithmetic-geometric mean to the negative roots and using \(|H(0)|\ge1\).
It is divisible by \(M\), so \(h\ge42\) for \(q\ge6\).
Put \(s_i=\sum w_k^i\).
Comparison of the first three coefficients gives the sum of the squares of the roots of the monic normalization of \(G\) as
\[
 -\frac{a_1^3(h-12)}{12h^2}+\frac{(h^2-16)s_2}{2h^2}
 +\frac{s_2^2}{4h^2}+\frac{2s_3}{3h}-\frac1{M^2}.
\]
Using \(s_2<4a_1+1\) and \(s_3<1\) bounds this above by \(-h^2/12+3h+14<0\), excluding a correction with only real roots in this range.

A second proposed extension of the [norm-one argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v884.md#zeros) uses \(a'=a_1-4j\).
For \(q\ge512\), \(4j\le96n/q\le a_1/2\) follows from the degree bound and \(a_1\ge n-1\).
Subtract \(j\log(1+4t)\) from \(\sum_i\log(1-w_it)\) and divide by \(a'\).
The resulting function has initial term \(t\).
The estimate \(s_2+16j\le6a_1+1\) gives a quadratic error bounded by \(8|t|^2\) on \(|t|\le1/32\).
This gives the candidate nonreal zeros of \(G\) near \(a'/(2\pi i k)\) for \(1\le|k|\le a'/(128\pi)\), by the same disk comparison.

There is a separate candidate restriction at the left endpoint on every branch \(j\ge1\).
Write \(V(t)=(-1)^nH(-4-t)=\prod_i(4+w_i+t)\) and \(P(t)=t^j(4+t)^{n-j}\).
Then \(V(0)=|H(-4)|\ge1\) and \(P(0)=0\).
Along a putative sequence, equidistribution of the short trace gives \(\limsup n^{-1}\log V(t_q)\le0\) whenever \(t_q\downarrow0\), by comparison with fixed positive \(t\) and the zero logarithmic potential of the arcsine distribution at the endpoint.
At \(t_q=\exp(-c n/j)\), with \(0<c<\log4\), one has \(n^{-1}\log P(t_q)\to\log4-c\).
At the first crossing \(V(t)=P(t)\), the lower bound \(V(t)\ge1\) and this upper location bound give \(\log(1/t)=(\log4+o(1))n/j\).
Thus the correction \(G\) itself would need a real root immediately to the left of \(-4\).
The same potential comparison at fixed \(x\in(-4,-1)\) gives \(n^{-1}\log|G(x)|\to\log|x|\), which would exclude corrections whose interval supremum is \(\exp(o(n))\).
The proposed quantitative refinements use \(|H(-4)|=|N(1+T)|\) with the [small-height norm bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v730.md#statement) to bound \(j\) by \(O(\sqrt{n\log n}/\log L)\).
A further suggested disk comparison would locate \(j\) roots of \(G\) near \(-4-\rho\exp(2\pi i k/j)\), where \(\rho=(|H(-4)|/4^{n-j})^{1/j}\).
It requires a uniform lower bound for \(4+w_i\) from the angle relation \(\tan(\phi/2)=L^{-1}\tan(\theta/2)\) at \(T_i=-e^{i\theta},Y_i=-e^{i\phi}\), as well as the polynomial bound for \(v\) and the error estimates on all disks.
These refinements remain to be checked.

The open construction needs both the local conditions above and exactly one positive trace with every other conjugate real in \((-4,0)\).
The explicit modification supplies integral units but misses the latter condition by a nonreal pair.
No assessment or independent review of these new arguments has been adopted.
