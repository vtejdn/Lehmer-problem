# Opposite-root shortening and exact cusp return in four-transvection words

<a id="scope"></a>

<a id="opposite-root-reduction"></a>
## Complete reduction for two opposite roots

Let $H$ be any positive definite integral lattice, and put $L=H\oplus U$, where $e^2=f^2=0$ and $(e,f)=1$.
Extend every positive integral isometry to fix $e,f$.
For integral positive vectors of even squared norm, use the retained transvections $E_x=T_{e,x}$ and $F_x=T_{f,x}$, with the rightmost factor acting first.
Consider
\[
A=R E_vF_wE_uF_t,
\qquad (w,w)=(t,t)=2,                                  \tag{1}
\]
where $R$ is a positive integral isometry and $u,v$ are arbitrary allowed parameters, including zero.
There is no requirement that $w=t$, that the parameters be independent, or that $R$ preserve their span.

For a positive norm-two root $a$, write
\[
s_a(x)=x-(a,x)a,
\qquad J=s_{e+f}.
\]
Both are integral isometries; $s_a$ fixes $e,f$, while $J$ fixes $H$ and sends $e$ to $-f$ and $f$ to $-e$.
Direct substitution in the transvection formula gives
\[
W_a:=E_aF_aE_a=J s_a,\qquad W_a^2=I.                   \tag{2}
\]
Indeed both sides send $a$ to $-a$, $e$ to $-f$ and $f$ to $-e$, and fix every positive vector perpendicular to $a$.
Those vectors span the full real space, so this checks the full operator identity, including any positive orthogonal complement.
The addition and covariance identities then give, for every allowed $x$,
\[
F_a=E_{-a}W_aE_{-a},\qquad
W_aE_xW_a^{-1}=F_{-s_a x}.                             \tag{3}
\]
All parameter transformations in (3) preserve the required even-norm integrality.

Put
\[
Q=s_ws_t,\qquad R'=RQ,
\]
and define
\[
p=Q^{-1}\bigl(v-w-R^{-1}t\bigr),\qquad
q=-s_t\bigl(u-w-t\bigr).                               \tag{4}
\]
Then the exact integral conjugacy is
\[
E_{-t} A E_t=R'E_pF_q.                                 \tag{5}
\]
To verify it, apply (3) twice and combine the adjacent e-factors:
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_wE_uF_t
 &=E_{-w}W_wE_{u-w-t}W_tE_{-t}\\
 &=E_{-w}F_{-s_w(u-w-t)}Q E_{-t},                       \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
because $W_wW_t=s_ws_t=Q$.
Moving this positive isometry to the left gives
\[
A=RQ\,E_{Q^{-1}(v-w)}F_{-s_t(u-w-t)}E_{-t},             \tag{7}
\]
where $Q^{-1}s_w=s_t$.
Finally move the conjugating $E_{-t}$ across $R'$ and combine the first two e-factors.
Their parameter is
\[
Q^{-1}(v-w)-R'^{-1}t
 =Q^{-1}(v-w-R^{-1}t)=p,
\]
which proves (5).

The reflections $s_w,s_t$, their product $Q$, and $R'$ preserve the actual integral positive lattice.
The vectors in (4) are integral and have even squared norm: sums and differences of allowed vectors retain even squared norm because cross terms are twice integral pairings.
Equation (5) therefore falls within the actual arbitrary-positive-lattice hypotheses of C885 v2.
It preserves the entire characteristic polynomial, not merely a selected factor or its largest root.

<a id="degenerate-and-other-opposite-pair"></a>
The argument includes $w=t$, $w=-t$, zero $u$ or $v$, and all other linear dependencies.
The transformed vector $p$ is zero exactly when $v=w+R^{-1}t$, and $q$ is zero exactly when $u=w+t$.
If either vanishes, the adopted two-factor zero case gives
\[
P_A(z)=\det(zI-R')(z-1)^2,\qquad M(P_A)=1.              \tag{8}
\]
No converse is asserted: two nonzero transformed parameters may also produce a neutral polynomial.
The empty positive lattice has no norm-two roots, so the premise (1) is then absent.

The same conclusion applies when the two opposite e-parameters $v,u$ instead have squared norm two.
For an explicit reduction to (1), let $C$ exchange $e,f$ and fix $H$.
Conjugation by $C F_t$ gives
\[
(CF_t)A(CF_t)^{-1}
 =R E_{R^{-1}t}F_vE_wF_u.                              \tag{9}
\]
This is one exact twisted cyclic rotation followed by the integral cusp exchange, and its two f-parameters are the specified roots.
Thus (4)--(5) apply with $(v,w,u,t)$ replaced by $(R^{-1}t,v,w,u)$.
Every original complete polynomial is preserved through this operation as well.

<a id="low-measure-norm-consequence"></a>
## Exact norm consequence for positive measure at most two

For (1), assume $1<M(P_A)\le2$.
Equation (8) implies $p\ne0$ and $q\ne0$.
The adopted C885 v2 bound states
\[
\|p\|_H\|q\|_H\ge650\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M(P_{R'E_pF_q})>2.
\]
Hence here $\|p\|_H\|q\|_H<650$.
Each positive even integral squared norm is at least two, so
\[
\|p\|_H,\ \|q\|_H<650/\sqrt2=325\sqrt2.                \tag{10}
\]
The exact inverse relations from (4) are
\[
v=w+R^{-1}t+Qp,\qquad u=w+t-s_tq.
\]
Since $R,Q,s_t$ are positive isometries and $\|w\|_H=\|t\|_H=\sqrt2$, the triangle inequality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|v\|_H&\le2\sqrt2+\|p\|_H<327\sqrt2,\\
\|u\|_H&\le2\sqrt2+\|q\|_H<327\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
(v,v)<213858,\qquad (u,u)<213858.                       \tag{11}
\]
Here $213858=2\cdot327^2$ is direct exact arithmetic from the retained constant, not a numerical root or optimization certificate.
For the other opposite pair in (9), the same argument bounds the original $w,t$ instead, since $R$ preserves the norm of $t$.

The uniform positive two-factor Mahler gap also transfers through (5) to this entire opposite-root subclass without any original norm restriction.
Neither this transfer nor (11) gives an effective exclusion of the finite interval $(1,6/5)$.
The actual restriction to two opposite norm-two parameters remains essential to this argument; no norm bound for the remaining general four-factor words is proved.

<a id="exact-cusp-return"></a>
## Exact return to the displayed cusp

Keep the arbitrary positive integral lattice and let
\[
B=F_wE_uF_t,\qquad A=R E_v B,
\]
where all parameters have even squared norm.
For this subsection first assume $u,w,t\ne0$, and write
\[
a=(u,u)/2,\qquad b=(w,w)/2,\qquad d=(t,t)/2,
\qquad \kappa=1-(u,t)+ad.
\]
Thus $a,b,d$ are positive integers.
The direct action on the primitive isotropic basis vector is
\[
Be=\kappa e+\bigl[t-du+\kappa w\bigr]-h f,
\qquad h=d+(w,t)-d(w,u)+b\kappa.                       \tag{13}
\]
Indeed $F_t e=e+t-df$; applying $E_u$ gives $\kappa e+(t-du)-df$, and then applying $F_w$ gives (13).
Expanding the positive norm gives the exact sum of squares
\[
h=\frac{bd}{2}\left\|u-\frac wb-\frac td\right\|_H^2.   \tag{14}
\]
In particular $h\ge0$, and it vanishes precisely when
\[
u=w/b+t/d.                                             \tag{15}
\]

Write $c=(w,t)$.
Under (15), direct substitution gives
\[
a=\frac1b+\frac1d+\frac{c}{bd},\qquad
(u,t)=\frac cb+2,\qquad \kappa=\frac db>0.
\]
The positive component in (13) is then $(\kappa-d/b)w=0$, so $Be=(d/b)e$.
The integral isometry $B$ is unimodular and maps primitive lattice vectors to primitive lattice vectors.
Consequently its positive scalar multiple of the primitive vector $e$ must have scalar one.
It follows that $d=b$, $\kappa=1$, and
\[
u=(w+t)/b.                                             \tag{16}
\]
Conversely, if $d=b$ and (16) holds, equations (13)--(15) give $Be=e$.
Since the coefficient of $f$ in $e$ is zero, $Be=e$ also implies $h=0$ directly from (13).
Thus, under the nonzero hypotheses,
\[
Be=e\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d=b\ \hbox{and}\ u=(w+t)/b.                            \tag{17}
\]

Equation (16) gives $c=ab^2-2b$.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $|c|\le2b$, so $ab\le4$.
The possible numerical triples are exactly the following necessary list:

| $b$ | $a$ | $c=(w,t)$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | $-1$ |
| 1 | 2 | $0$ |
| 1 | 3 | $1$ |
| 1 | 4 | $2$ |
| 2 | 1 | $0$ |
| 2 | 2 | $4$ |
| 3 | 1 | $3$ |
| 4 | 1 | $8$ |

Every such return therefore has $(u,u),(w,w),(t,t)\le8$.
The table does not assert realization in every positive integral lattice, and it is not sufficient without the actual vector relation (16).
For example, integrality of $(w+t)/b$ is an additional actual lattice condition that cannot be inferred from the three displayed scalar values alone.

<a id="zero-cusp-return-cases"></a>
If a middle parameter is zero, use the elementary shortening separately, rather than divide by $b$ or $d$ in (14).
If $u=0$, then $B=F_{w+t}$ and $Be=e$ exactly when $w+t=0$.
If $w=0$, then $B=E_uF_t$; its action on $e$ has $f$-coefficient $-(t,t)/2$, so it returns $e$ exactly when $t=0$.
If $t=0$, then $Be=F_w e$, so return occurs exactly when $w=0$.
These overlapping statements are consistent when more than one parameter vanishes; in particular $w=t=0$ permits every allowed $u$ and gives $B=E_u$.

<a id="cusp-return-neutrality"></a>
The outer factors $R E_v$ and their inverses fix $e$.
Hence $Ae=e$ if and only if $Be=e$, with no restriction on the outer parameter $v$.
For any integral isometry fixing $e$, the invariant integral filtration
\[
0\subset\mathbb Z e\subset e^\perp\cap L\subset L
\]
has trivial action on its first rank-one term and on the last rank-one quotient.
The latter assertion follows from $(e,Ax)=(A^{-1}e,x)=(e,x)$, with $(e,f)=1$.
The middle quotient $(e^\perp\cap L)/\mathbb Z e$ is the actual positive integral lattice $H$.
Its induced action $R_e$ is an integral positive isometry and thus has finite order.
Using an integral basis adapted to this filtration gives the full characteristic identity
\[
P_A(z)=(z-1)^2\det(zI-R_e),\qquad M(P_A)=1.             \tag{18}
\]
No expanding factor is removed, and neither the outer norm nor the rank enters this conclusion.
Equations (17)--(18) classify this particular cusp-return mechanism of neutrality; they do not classify all neutral words, which may fail to fix the displayed vector $e$.

<a id="neutral-unbounded-control"></a>
## A neutral family missed by adjacent-root checks

Let $\theta$ be any positive norm-two root and let $N\ge3$ be an integer.
Equations (2)--(3) give the exact relation
\[
F_\theta E_{2\theta}F_\theta=E_{-2\theta}.
\]
Thus, for every positive integral isometry $R$,
\[
A_N=R E_{N\theta}F_\theta E_{2\theta}F_\theta
    =R E_{(N-2)\theta}.                               \tag{19}
\]
A one-factor word is neutral, so
\[
P_{A_N}(z)=\det(zI-R)(z-1)^2,\qquad M(P_{A_N})=1.
\]
For a concrete standard-lattice instance, take the $I_9$ and cyclic $R$ of [L462 v14](Yin_L_G0462_v14.md#lehmer-control), with $\theta=b_0+b_1$.
This instance is inside the adopted C886 v3 domain.

The displayed four vectors are all nonzero and have squared norms
\[
2N^2,\quad2,\quad8,\quad2.
\]
No cyclically adjacent pair both has squared norm two.
The exact twisted cyclic rotations only rotate this norm pattern and apply powers of the positive isometry to moved parameters, so they preserve these facts.
Nevertheless (19) collapses to one factor, and the original displayed norms are unbounded.
In (4), this is the explicit degeneracy $q=0$.
It also belongs to the cusp-return case $(b,a,c)=(1,4,2)$ of (17).
It shows that absence of the elementary zero and adjacent-root checks does not imply irreducibility under the exact transvection relations.
It is not a counterexample to a norm reduction that assumes strictly positive complete measure.

<a id="remaining-question"></a>
## Consequence for the selected four-factor inquiry

The opposite-root branch supplies a complete shortening even when the two norm-two parameters are separated by parameters of larger norm.
A useful reduction must therefore account for this relation, or an equivalent full-word invariant, rather than treating cyclic adjacency checks as a complete normal form.
Within this branch, the actual low-positive-measure input controls the original norms by (11), while neutral examples can have arbitrarily large displayed norms.
The exact cusp-return branch (17) has bounded middle norms but permits arbitrarily large outer $v$, and its complete measure is always one by (18).

The bounded exact analysis produced no four-factor family with unbounded displayed norms, fixed low nonneutral complete measure, and no available shortening after these checks.
That failure to find an example is not a proof that a general low-measure reduction exists.
The remaining general case needs the full determinant or another exact invariant; neither the neutral control nor the opposite-root proof permits a norm bound to be inferred there.
