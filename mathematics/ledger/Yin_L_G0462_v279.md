# Precision needed to detect short signed rows by congruences

<a id="volume"></a>
## Small vectors among the modular lifts

Use the [all-power reciprocal seed](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#statement) \(\delta\) of degree \(d=2s\), its positive logarithmic measure \(h\), and the targets \(P_a=\min(\delta^a)\) for \(a\in I\).
Put \(k=|I|\), \(T=\sum_{a\in I}a\), \(m=kd\), \(N=b+1=m+r\), and fix \(1\le r\le s\) and a sign \(\varepsilon\).
At exponent \(n\) write \(D=nb+1\) and \(S(X)=A(X^n)+\varepsilon X^D A(X^{-n})\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\) the integer coefficient lattice \(\Lambda\) of rows with \(\prod_aP_a^2\mid S\) is saturated of rank \(r\), and every nonzero exact row is genuine by the [numerator rank](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#minor).
At sufficiently large prime exponents \(p\), the [quotient parametrization](Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#rank) gives precisely the reductions of \(\Lambda\) modulo every power of \(p\).

For any integer \(Q\ge2\) the preimage of these exact reductions in \(\mathbb Z^N\) is \(\Lambda+Q\mathbb Z^N\), of index \(Q^m\) because the quotient by \(\Lambda\) is free of rank \(m\).
The same preimage results from combining the reductions at the prime powers in \(Q\) by the Chinese remainder theorem.
For \(L=\lfloor Q^{m/N}\rfloor\), the cube \(\{0,\ldots,L\}^N\) has more than \(Q^m\) integral points.
Two have the same image, so their nonzero difference has one-norm at most \(NL\).
For \(Q\) sufficiently large this bound is less than \(Q/2\), so the row is already the centered representative of a nonzero residue.

Let \(\kappa=(\log 2)/8\), with strict passing budget \(\|A\|_1<H=2^{n/8}/\sqrt{2nb+1}\).
Excluding **all nonzero** rows of norm below \(H\) by their reductions requires
\[
             \log Q\ \ge\ \frac Nm\bigl(\log H-\log N\bigr)
                 =\frac Nm\,\kappa n-O_b(\log n).
\]
For \(I=\{1,2\}\) and \(r=d/2\) the coefficient is \(5\kappa/4\).
Already \(Q>2H\) distinguishes all integer rows in the budget, but the interval \(2H<Q<(H/N)^{N/m}\) still contains a short modular candidate for every such modulus.
When \(Th>\kappa\), the [lower bound for exact rows](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v434.md#attainment) forces every sufficiently late short modular candidate to fail an exact equation.
At prime exponent \(p\) the necessary common precision for this exclusion is at least \((N/m)\kappa p/\log p-O_b(1)\) base-\(p\) digits.

The cube argument permits the difference to have content divisible by a prime in the modulus.
This qualification matters even for a saturated line whose projection to its top coordinate is a local isomorphism.
For a prime \(\ell\) and \(j\ge2\) take \(\mathbb Z(1,1+\ell^{j-1})\) modulo \(Q=\ell^j\).
Its congruence lattice contains \(\ell(1,1)\), whereas every solution \((x,y)\) nonzero modulo \(\ell\) has \(\ell\nmid x\) and \(|y-x|\ge\ell^{j-1}\).
Thus the index alone leaves open a stronger test that restricts to local primitive classes in the actual signed lattice.

<a id="exactness"></a>
## A height cutoff for exact divisibility

Write \(\alpha=\delta^a\).
Suppose that modulo a positive integer \(Q_a\) the row agrees with an exact row for the individual factor \(P_a^2\).
Then \(S(\alpha),S'(\alpha)\) belong to \(Q_a\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\alpha)}\).
The same follows from direct divisibility by \(P_a^2\) modulo \(Q_a\).
For \(\|A\|_1\le H\), each value has absolute field norm at most
\[
                         (2DH)^d \exp(D a h).
\]
Indeed the coefficient one-norms of \(S,S'\) are at most \(2H,2DH\), their degrees are at most \(D\), and the product of \(\max(1,|\alpha'|)\) over all conjugates is \(\exp(ah)\).
A nonzero multiple of \(Q_a\) in this ring of integers has absolute norm at least \(Q_a^d\).
Consequently
\[
             \log Q_a>\log(2DH)+Dah/d                                      \tag{1}
\]
forces both values to vanish, giving \(P_a^2\mid S\) over the integers.
One common modulus may use \(\max I\) in (1).
At prime exponent \(p\), with \(H\) the passing budget, at most \((\kappa+b(\max I)h/d)p/\log p+O_b(1)\) base-\(p\) digits suffice.

Each individual exact lattice has codimension \(d\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), by the [signed rank calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#rank) at large primes or the [eventual rank](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#rank) for general exponents.
Its reductions modulo \(Q_a\) therefore cost index \(Q_a^d\), and intersecting the \(k\) resulting congruence lattices costs at most \(\prod_a Q_a^d\).
Choose \(Q_a\) just above the bound in (1), rounding up to powers of \(p\) when \(n=p\).
Then
\[
              \sum_a d\log Q_a=m\kappa n+bTh\,n+O_{b,d,I}(\log n).
\]
If \(bTh<r\kappa\), the cube argument for this index gives a nonzero row strictly inside the passing budget, and (1) makes it exact for every target.
Dividing out its content in the exact lattice gives a primitive genuine row.
At \(I=\{1,2\}\), \(r=d/2\) and \(b=5d/2-1\), separate precisions achieve this when \(h<\kappa/(15-6/d)\), while one common precision gives \(h<\kappa/(20-8/d)\).
The [archimedean bound from ordered conjugate moduli](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#norm) already gives the stronger slope \((15-10/d)h\) at that width.
The precision estimates explain the resulting short modular candidates without deciding whether seeds of arbitrarily small positive measure exist.

<a id="ideals"></a>
## Distributing precision among prime ideals

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), with involution \(\tau(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}\), and let \(E=K^\tau\), of degree \(s\).
The two polynomials \(S\) and \(J=(2X\,d/dX-D)S\) satisfy signed reciprocity at width \(D\) with respective signs \(\sigma=\varepsilon,-\varepsilon\).
For either polynomial \(B\) denote its value times \(\alpha^{-\lfloor D/2\rfloor}\) by \(z\).
At even \(D\), \(\tau z=\sigma z\), so take \(u=z\) for sign \(+1\) and \(u=(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})z\) for sign \(-1\).
At odd \(D\), \(\tau z=\sigma\alpha^{-1}z\), so take \(u=(1+\sigma\alpha^{-1})z\).
In every case \(u\in\mathcal O_E\), and the multiplier is nonzero because \(\alpha\) is nontorsion.
Write \(u_S,u_J\) for the two resulting integers.
Their joint vanishing is equivalent to \(P_a^2\mid S\).

For one embedding per inverse pair, the absolute value of either normalized evaluation is at most a fixed conjugate-dependent constant times \(2DH\max(|\alpha'|,|\alpha'|^{-1})^{D/2}\).
Indeed \(\|J\|_1\le D\|S\|_1\), and the possible extra half-power and the multipliers above have fixed absolute values at the chosen embedding.
The sum of the absolute logarithms of the chosen moduli is \(ah\).
Thus there is a constant \(C_a\) depending only on the fixed target such that
\[
        \max\bigl(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}u_S|,|N_{E/\mathbb Q}u_J|\bigr)
                    \le C_a(2DH)^s \exp(Dah/2).
\]
For each of \(u_S,u_J\) one may impose membership in any nonzero integral ideal of \(\mathcal O_E\) whose norm exceeds this bound.
Membership then forces vanishing, since any nonzero element in the ideal generates a subideal of at least that norm.
The index cost of the two congruences is at most the product of the two ideal norms, by mapping the integer coefficient vectors into the two residue rings.
Products of powers of different prime ideals can supply either norm.
The total logarithmic norm needed by this certificate at target \(a\) is therefore \(d\log H+Dah+O_{a,b,d}(\log n)\), attainable for example with powers of a fixed rational prime extended to \(E\).
It has the same leading term as the factor-specific cost above.
An exclusion using prime ideals would need further information about the images in the residue rings or about primitive quotient classes.

## Two explicit levels at exponents that are powers of a fixed prime

Put \(G=\prod_a P_a\), fix an odd prime \(\ell\) avoiding \(\operatorname{disc}(G)G(1)G(-1)\), and take \(n=\ell^e\), with \(e\ge1\) sufficiently large for the rank calculation.
In an unramified splitting extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), let \(\omega_\xi\) be the unique root of unity of order prime to \(\ell\) reducing to each root \(\xi\) of \(\bar G\).
The monic polynomial \(\Pi(Y)=\prod_\xi(Y-\omega_\xi)\) has coefficients in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) by Frobenius invariance, and is reciprocal because the set of roots is closed under inversion and splits into inverse pairs.
For a root \(\alpha\) of \(G\) over \(\xi\), put \(y=\alpha^n\), \(\eta=\xi^n\), and \(D_Y=Y\,d/dY\).
Writing \(\alpha=\omega_\xi(1+\ell t)\) and raising to the \(\ell\)-th power \(e\) times gives \(y\equiv\omega_\eta\bmod\ell^{e+1}\).
The [signed contact equations](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v462.md#rank) imply
\[
          (1+bn)A(y)=n\bigl((D_Y A)(y)-\varepsilon\alpha y^b(D_Y A)(1/y)\bigr).
\]
Hence \(A(\omega_\eta)\equiv0\bmod\ell^e\) at every node of \(\Pi\).
Their differences are units, so monic division or Vandermonde interpolation gives \(\Pi\mid A\bmod\ell^e\).
The multiples of \(\Pi\) at this precision and width have cardinality \(\ell^{er}\), exactly the size of the saturated lattice reduced modulo \(\ell^e\).
They are therefore all the reductions of exact rows.

There is an explicit next digit at every quotient \(C\).
Write \(A=\Pi C+\ell^e R\bmod\ell^{e+1}\), with \(\deg C<r\) and \(\deg R<m\).
Division of the contact equation by \(n\) at each node gives
\[
 \bar R(\eta)
    =\eta\bar\Pi'(\eta)
       \bigl(\bar C(\eta)+\varepsilon\xi\,\eta^{b-m}\bar C(1/\eta)\bigr).
\]
Indeed replacing \(y\) by \(\omega_\eta\) on the left changes \(A(y)/n\) by an element of \(\ell\mathcal O\), and modulo \(\ell\) the right side uses \(A=\Pi C\) and \((D_Y\Pi)(1/\eta)=-\eta^{-m}(D_Y\Pi)(\eta)\).
The \(m\) distinct nodes determine \(\bar R\) uniquely, and Frobenius invariance gives coefficients in \(\mathbb F_\ell\).
Each \(C\bmod\ell^{e+1}\) determines at most one row at this precision, and the number of these quotients is \(\ell^{(e+1)r}\), equal to the number of reductions of exact rows.
Thus the interpolation gives every exact reduction and depends on \(e\) in the residue field only through the inverse Frobenius taking \(\eta\) to \(\xi\).
Despite this explicit lifting formula, the logarithm of its modulus is only \(\log n+\log\ell\).
Excluding all short coefficient representatives at the passing budget requires logarithmic precision proportional to \(n\) by the volume bound above.
