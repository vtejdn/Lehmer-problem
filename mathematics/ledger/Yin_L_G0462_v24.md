# Choosing the return exponent for both signs of the plastic-seed family

<a id="choice-of-exponent"></a>
## A return for every sufficiently large delay

Write \(f=z^3-z-1,\ g=z^3 f(1/z)=1-z^2-z^3\).
For \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\) and all sufficiently large \(N\), let \(S_{N,\sigma}\) be the minimal polynomial of the unique exterior root of \(D=z^Nf-\sigma g\), and write \(d=\deg S_{N,\sigma}\).
Put \(n=N-1\).
For both signs and all such delays,
\[
      \log|\operatorname{Res}(S_{N,\sigma},z^n+\sigma)|
                        =n\log\lambda+O(\log n),\qquad d=n+O(1),
\]
where \(\lambda>2\) is the exterior root of \(W=zf+g=z^4-z^3-2z^2-z+1\).
In particular this integer certifies \(\log M(S_{N,\sigma})\ge\log(\lambda/2)-O((\log d)/d)\).
This extends the [selected linear returns](Yin_L_G0131_v1.md) and the [shifted subsequence](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#shifted-return) to the complete family for both signs.
Given only the original minimal polynomial, one can test the two signs at each integer exponent from \(d-4\) through \(d+26\) and take the largest certified lower bound.
At least one of these 62 tests gives the displayed asymptotic lower bound on every sufficiently large member.
On the [height-one subsequence](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#degree-returns), testing at the exact degree instead fails for every choice of a phase on the unit circle, even a phase selected after seeing the polynomial.
The main researcher obtained these two comparisons after the strategy contributor found the exact full-turn and half-turn products at the degree.

## Removing the cyclotomic factors at every delay

On the unit circle \(|f|=|g|>0\).
The [circle count](../claims/Yin_C_G0162_v3.md#salem) with multiplier \(t\sigma\), \(0<t<1\), gives at most one strictly exterior root at \(t=1\).
For every fixed pair \(1<s<\theta<t'\) bracketing the exterior root of \(f\), \(D(s)<0<D(t')\) for large \(N\).
Thus \(D\) has a unique simple exterior root tending to \(\theta\), and reciprocity up to sign gives its unique inverse root inside.
As in the circle count, its monic irreducible factor must contain that inverse, and all other irreducible factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

Write \(G=g/f=-1+z+O(z^2)\).
Every torsion root \(\eta\) has one of \(\xi\eta^h\) as a conjugate, with \((\xi,h)=(1,2),(-1,2),(-1,1)\) according to its [order modulo four](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#irreducible).
Then \(G(\xi\eta^h)=\xi^N\sigma^{h-1}G(\eta)^h\).
The two rational functions in this identity are distinct.
For \(h=2\) only the right side has a nonzero linear term at zero.
For \((\xi,h)=(-1,1)\) their constants differ if \(N\) is odd and their linear terms differ if \(N\) is even.
Clearing denominators gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most twelve, so every torsion root of \(D\) lies in a fixed finite set.
A multiple torsion root would give \(N=\eta G'(\eta)/G(\eta)\), which is bounded on that set where \(f,g\ne0\).
Thus for all large \(N\) the factor \(H=D/S_{N,\sigma}\) has bounded degree and only simple torsion roots in the fixed set.
For each \(N,\sigma\) the three cleared polynomials above have degrees at most \(12,12,6\), respectively, so \(h=\deg H\le30\).
In particular \(n=d+h-4\) lies in the stated interval.

At each \(z^n=-\sigma\) the numerator \(D\) equals \(-\sigma W\).
Writing \(u=z+z^{-1}\) gives \(W/z^2=u^2-u-4\), with roots \(u_+=(1+\sqrt{17})/2>5/2\) and \(u_-=(1-\sqrt{17})/2\in(-2,2)\).
Thus \(W\) has roots \(\lambda,\lambda^{-1},\gamma,\bar\gamma\), with \(\lambda>2\), \(|\gamma|=1\), and \(\gamma\) nontorsion because a torsion trace could not have the conjugate \(u_+>2\).
In particular \(D\) and \(z^n+\sigma\) share no root.
Every \(|\eta^n+\sigma|\) for \(H(\eta)=0\) belongs to a fixed finite set of positive numbers, so their product is bounded above and away from zero.
Taking absolute resultants in either order gives
\[
  |\operatorname{Res}(S_{N,\sigma},z^n+\sigma)|
    =\frac{|\lambda^n+\sigma|\,|\lambda^{-n}+\sigma|\,|\gamma^n+\sigma|^2}
                 {\prod_{H(\eta)=0}|\eta^n+\sigma|}.
\]
The [fixed algebraic power estimate](../audits/literature/Wus02/Yin_U001.md#unit-circle) gives \(|\gamma^{2n}-1|\gg n^{-C}\) since \(\gamma\) is a fixed nontorsion algebraic number on the unit circle.
Factoring \(\gamma^{2n}-1\) gives \(|\gamma^n+\sigma|\ge|\gamma^{2n}-1|/2\).
Taking logarithms proves the asymptotic uniformly in \(\sigma\).
The bound \(|x^n+\sigma|\le2\max(1,|x|)^n\) at each root of \(S_{N,\sigma}\) proves the stated positive certificate.

## An arbitrary phase cannot repair the exact-degree exponent

Now take the [subsequence](Yin_L_G0462_v23.md#degree-returns) with \(\sigma=1,\ H=z-1,\ d=N+2\) and write \(S=S_{N,1}\).
For \(w\) on the unit circle and \(y^d=w\) one has
\[
     y^2(y-1)S(y)=Q_w(y),\qquad
     Q_w(z)=wf(z)-z^2g(z)=z^5+z^4+wz^3-z^2-wz-w .
\]
We give a uniform bound \(M(Q_w)\le b=1140^{1/12}<2\).
For its five roots \(r\) form the monic polynomial \(J_w(X)=\prod_r(X-r^6)\).
Expanding \(\operatorname{Res}_z(Q_w(z),X-z^6)\), the coefficients in descending order are
\[
\begin{split}
1,\quad &2w^3-3w^2-6w+2,\\
 &w^6+6w^5-3w^4-2w^3+3w^2+5,\\
 &-5w^6-3w^4+2w^3+3w^2-6w-1,\\
 &-2w^6+6w^5+3w^4-2w^3,\quad -w^6 .
\end{split}
\]
Their absolute values are at most \(1,13,20,20,13,1\).
Jensen's inequality for the logarithm on the unit circle and Parseval's identity give \(M(Q_w)^6=M(J_w)\le\|J_w\|_2\le\sqrt{1140}\).

Define \(A(w)=\prod_{S(x)=0}(w-x^d)\).
Taking products over \(y^d=w\) gives for every \(|w|=1\)
\[
             |(w-1)A(w)|=\prod_{Q_w(r)=0}|w-r^d|\le 2^5 b^d .
\]
The product identity holds also at \(w=1\) by continuity.
All coefficients of the polynomial \((w-1)A(w)\) are bounded by \(2^5b^d\) by their Fourier integrals.
Each coefficient of \(A\) is a signed partial sum of at most \(d+1\) such coefficients.
Therefore
\[
             \sup_{|w|=1}|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^d-w)|
                         \le 32(d+1)^2 b^d=o(2^d).
\]
In particular every collection of \(k\ge1\) phases, even with both the collection and \(k\) depending on \(S\), has product of absolute resultants at most \((32(d+1)^2 b^d)^k\).
This excludes beating the factorwise cost \(2^{dk}\) at that exponent.
The estimate leaves open different costs for a collective auxiliary polynomial and choices of exponent on unrestricted original inputs.
