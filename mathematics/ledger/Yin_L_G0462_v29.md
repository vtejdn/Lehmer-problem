# The limiting numerator at negative offsets and its realization by multinacci seeds

<a id="negative-limit"></a>
## An integer polynomial bounds the loss at negative offsets

Use the [Pisot unit phase polynomials](Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#uniform-phase), with \(f_i\) the minimal polynomials of nonreciprocal Pisot units \(\theta_i\to\theta>1\) and \(g_i\) their reversals.
Take \(k_i=-b_i\) with positive integers \(b_i\to\infty\), positive integers \(j_i\to\infty\), signs \(\sigma_i\), and
\[
    V_i=f_i^{j_i}-(\sigma_i z^{b_i}g_i)^{j_i},\qquad
    \ell=\liminf_i m(V_i)/j_i,\qquad m=\log M .
\]
Then \(0\le\ell\le\log\theta\), by integrality and the unit-circle estimate in [the analytic normalization](Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#analytic-normalization).
Salem's [closedness theorem for the set of Pisot numbers](../audits/literature/Smy15/Yin_U002.md#closedness-and-delay-one) makes \(\theta\) a Pisot number, possibly a nonunit such as the integer \(2\).
Let \(Q\) be the reversal of the monic minimal polynomial of \(\theta\), so \(Q(0)=1\).
Along a subsequence attaining \(\ell\) there is a polynomial \(H\in\mathbb Z[z]\) with
\[
    H(0)=\pm1,\qquad m(H)\le\ell,\qquad |H(z)|\le |Q(z)|\quad(|z|=1).
                                                                    \tag{1}
\]
More precisely \(H/Q\) is the limit of \(f_i/g_i\) locally near zero, and \(Q\) is coprime to \(H\).
If \(\ell=0\), then \(H\) is, up to sign, a product of cyclotomic polynomials, allowing the empty product.
The strategy-discovery contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this criterion and the multinacci comparison below on 2026-11-16.

<a id="integer-limit"></a>
## The numerator and its logarithmic measure

Set \(a_i=1/\theta_i,\ a=1/\theta,\ b_a(z)=(z-a)/(1-az)\), and \(B_i=b_{a_i}f_i/g_i\).
As in [the disk argument](Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#analytic-normalization), \(B_i\) is analytic of modulus at most one on the disk.
Pass to a subsequence attaining \(\ell\) on which \(B_i\to B\) locally uniformly and \(f_i(0)=c_0=\pm1\) is constant.
On a neighborhood of zero \(f_i/g_i\to G=B/b_a\), whose Taylor coefficients are integers because \(g_i(0)=1\) and each coefficient converges.
In particular \(G(0)=c_0\) and \(|G'(0)|\ge1\) by the [nonzero integral linear coefficient](Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#analytic-normalization).
The function \(Q/b_a\) is a polynomial, since \(Q\) vanishes at \(a\).
Consequently \(H=(Q/b_a)B\) is bounded analytic in the disk with integral Taylor coefficients.
Parseval on circles of radii tending to one bounds the sum of their squares by \(\|H\|_\infty^2\), so only finitely many are nonzero.
This gives \(H\in\mathbb Z[z]\) and \(H(0)=c_0\).
The rational expression \(B=b_a H/Q\) extends across the circle, since \(Q\) has no unit-circle root.
Taking radial limits in \(|B|\le1\) gives the boundary inequality in (1).
If \(Q\) had a factor in common with \(H\), its irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\) would make \(G=H/Q\) a polynomial.
The boundary inequality would give \(|G|\le1\) throughout the disk, and its value of modulus one at zero would make it constant, contrary to \(G'(0)\ne0\).

Write \(C_i=\sigma_i z^{b_i}b_{a_i}\) and \(F_i=B_i^{j_i}-C_i^{j_i}\).
The identity \(|F_i|=|V_i|/|g_i|^{j_i}\) on the circle gives finite positive measures
\[
 d\nu_i=\frac{\log2-\log|F_i|}{j_i}\frac{dt}{2\pi},\qquad
 \nu_i(\mathbb T)=\frac{\log2-m(V_i)}{j_i}+\log\theta_i\to\log\theta-\ell .
\]
Finiteness includes possible logarithmic singularities at boundary zeros.
Extract a weak limit \(\nu\) with this same mass.
Because \(C_i\to0\) locally uniformly, \(j_i^{-1}\log|F_i(z)|\to\log|B(z)|\) at each point with \(B(z)\ne0\).
The [Poisson upper bound](Yin_L_G0462_v26.md#uniform-proof) thus gives \(\log|B(z)|\le-\int P_z\,d\nu\), also at its zeros in the extended sense.
For \(a<r<1\), integrate on \(|z|=r\) and use \(H=(Q/b_a)B\) to obtain
\[
 \int\log|H(re^{it})|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}
 \ \le\ \int\log|(Q/b_a)(re^{it})|\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}
          -\nu(\mathbb T).
\]
One can take \(r\) sufficiently close to one that \(Q/b_a\) is nonvanishing there.
At the boundary the integral on the right tends to \(m(Q)=\log\theta\).
For the polynomial \(H\), the integral on the left tends to \(m(H)\) by Jensen's formula, including when \(H\) has roots on the circle.
This proves \(m(H)\le\ell\).
At \(\ell=0\), Kronecker's theorem gives the stated factorization, since \(H\) is integral with nonzero constant and measure one.

<a id="multinacci"></a>
## The constant numerator occurs for multinacci Pisot units

Take \(f_m=z^m-z^{m-1}-\cdots-z-1\) for \(m\ge2\), and let \(g_m\) be its reversal.
The [multinacci root and irreducibility proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0021_v1.md#multinacci-local-roots-irreducibility-and-measure) gives Pisot units \(\theta_m\to2\).
They are nonreciprocal for all \(m\ge2\), as is seen from the displayed coefficients.
The identities
\[
 (1-z)f_m=(2-z)z^m-1,\qquad
 (1-z)g_m=1-2z+z^{m+1}
\]
give \(G=-1/(1-2z)\) in the open disk away from \(1/2\), by local uniform convergence there.
For \(\theta=2\), \(Q=1-2z\), so \(H=-1\).

<a id="uniform-multinacci"></a>
## Phase recovery for the same seeds

The cyclotomic multiple \((1-z)f_m=-1+2z^m-z^{m+1}\) has three terms for every \(m\).
The [uniform phase estimate for seeds with a sparse cyclotomic multiple](Yin_L_G0462_v31.md#sparse-phases) therefore applies to the whole multinacci family.
In particular, for every cost \(D>0\) there is one multiplier \(j_*\) for which
\[
 m\!\left(f_m^{j_*}-(\sigma z^b g_m)^{j_*}\right)>D
\]
for every sufficiently large \(m\), every positive integer \(b\), and both signs \(\sigma\).
