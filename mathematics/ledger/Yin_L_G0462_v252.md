# A height obstruction for the octic Salem seed at row degree eight

<a id="ratio"></a>
## The ratio of the two signed rows at two powered conjugates

Let \(P=X^8-X^5-X^4-X^3+1\), let \(\rho>1\) be its Salem root and put \(h=\log\rho\).
For every sufficiently large positive integer \(p\), no integral rows \(U,V\) of degrees at most eight satisfy all three conditions
\[
 P(X)^2\mid U(X^p)+XV(X^p),\qquad UU^\iota-VV^\iota\ne0,\qquad
 H=\max(\|U\|_1,\|V\|_1)\le2^p,\qquad A^\iota(Y)=A(1/Y).
\]
In particular this excludes the [octic left by the Salem norm estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v412.md#eight) and its variable-sign transform from the width-eight minimum at every sufficiently large odd prime.
The argument concerns this seed and supplies no numerical onset.

Write \(x_1,\ldots,x_4\) for one representative of each inverse pair, with the labeling specified [below](#specialization), and put \(z_i=x_i^p\).
The four representatives are multiplicatively independent even modulo roots of unity.
Indeed for each \(i\) an embedding of the splitting field \(L\) sends \(x_i\) to \(\rho\), and the other three representatives then have modulus one.
Taking absolute values at these four embeddings forces every exponent of a torsion product to vanish.
In particular the eight powered roots \(z_i^{\pm1}\) are distinct, with a common degree-eight monic minimal polynomial \(Q\).

Put \(A_b^*(Y)=Y^bA(1/Y)\) and \(A_\varepsilon=U+\varepsilon V_8^*\) for \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
The nonzero reflected determinant makes both signed rows nonzero.
Adding \(\varepsilon\) times the reversal of the original graph about \(8p+1\) yields
\[
 P^2\mid A_\varepsilon(X^p)+\varepsilon X (A_\varepsilon)_8^*(X^p).
\]
If \(A_\varepsilon(z_i)=0\), then \(A_\varepsilon=cQ\) with \(c\ne0\), since its degree is at most eight.
As \(Q_8^*=Q\), the signed graph would be \(cQ(X^p)(1+\varepsilon X)\), with a simple zero at \(x_i\), a contradiction.
All signed row values used in the following ratios are therefore nonzero.
By the [width equal to the seed degree](Yin_L_G0462_v242.md#lines), the rational signed kernels are lines whenever such a genuine pair exists.
For two chosen representatives the scalar
\[
 R_p=\frac{A_+(z_1) A_-(z_2)}{A_-(z_1) A_+(z_2)}
\]
is invariant under separate rescaling of the two generators.

Use the absolute logarithmic Weil height \(h_{\rm W}\), and logarithms of ideal norms normalized by \(n=[L:\mathbb Q]\).
At each finite place, write \(r_{ij}=\nu(z_i-z_j)\) for the differences from \(z_i\) to all other powered roots, including inverse representatives.
For each sign the signed graph and the unit \(x_i\) give \(\nu A_\varepsilon(z_i)=\nu (A_\varepsilon)_8^*(z_i)\).
The [local contact inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#contact) with multiplicity two gives the same lower bound for either sign, using a \(j\) of maximal \(r_{ij}\).
The entire finite loss in replacing this maximum by \(\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}\) and clearing \(p\) is \(o_P(p)\) after normalized summation, by the [overlap and discriminant proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#overlap).
Thus even the minimum of the two valuations satisfies
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{\mathfrak l}\min_\varepsilon\nu_{\mathfrak l} A_\varepsilon(z_i)
                     \log N\mathfrak l
 \ \ge\ \frac{2}{8}\log|\operatorname{disc}Q|-o_P(p)
 \ =\ \frac72 ph-o_P(p).
\]
Here \(D(P)=14h\) since \(P\) is Salem.
At every embedding the same [archimedean row bounds](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v411.md#ends) apply to both signed graphs of width eight with row one-norm at most \(2H\).
Taking their pointwise maximum keeps the saved exterior and interior layers, giving normalized archimedean log sum at most \(\log(2H)+(6/8)ph+O_P(\log(2p))\).
Subtracting the common finite valuation sum in the projective height of \([A_+(z_i):A_-(z_i)]\) gives
\[
 h_{\rm W}\big(A_+(z_i)/A_-(z_i)\big)
       \le\log H-\tfrac{11}{4}ph+o_P(p),\qquad
 h_{\rm W}(R_p)\le2\log H-\tfrac{11}{2}ph+o_P(p).                  \tag{1}
\]
These losses are independent of the integral rows.

<a id="specialization"></a>
## A finite-field certificate and the height after specialization

Introduce independent variables \(N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_4\).
For each sign form an eight-by-nine matrix, with two successive rows for each \(i=1,\ldots,4\) and columns \(d=0,\ldots,8\).
Its entries in those two rows are
\[
 Y_i^d+\varepsilon x_iY_i^{8-d},\qquad
 NdY_i^d+\varepsilon x_i\bigl(1+N(8-d)\bigr)Y_i^{8-d}.
\]
At \(N=p,Y_i=x_i^p\) these are the value and Euler derivative of the signed graph basis at \(x_i\).
Let \(a_{\varepsilon,d}\) be \((-1)^d\) times the determinant with column \(d\) deleted and define \(B_\varepsilon(Z)=\sum_d a_{\varepsilon,d}Z^d\).
The cofactor vector lies in the kernel.
Form the rational function
\[
 R(N,\mathbf Y)=\frac{B_+(Y_1)B_-(Y_2)}{B_-(Y_1)B_+(Y_2)}.
\]
To see that \(R\) depends on \(Y_1\), use the prime \(1987\).
The eight distinct residues in the inverse pairs \((308,729),(670,1296),(1452,1961),(1555,1964)\) are roots of \(P\), as direct substitution and multiplication verify.
Fix a prime of \(L\) over \(1987\), and label \(x_i\) by the first residues in those pairs.
All eight reductions are distinct since the reduction of \(P\) is separable.
In the table set \(N=5\), \((Y_2,Y_3,Y_4)=(3,5,7)\), and let \(D_\varepsilon\) denote the determinant of the first eight columns.
The two evaluations shown for each sign are in the normalization \(a_{\varepsilon,8}=1\), obtained by dividing each cofactor by \(D_\varepsilon\).

| \(Y_1\) | \(D_+\) | \(D_-\) | Plus evaluations at \(Y_1,Y_2\) | Minus evaluations at \(Y_1,Y_2\) | \(R\bmod1987\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| 2 | 1768 | 1884 | \(653,1909\) | \(330,1286\) | 1957 |
| 4 | 318 | 1219 | \(590,438\) | \(132,747\) | 904 |

One can reproduce each row by solving the first eight columns against the negative of the last column modulo \(1987\), appending the coefficient one and evaluating the two resulting polynomials.
All four determinants and both cross-ratio denominators are nonzero.
Independence from \(Y_1\) would give an identically zero cross-multiplied difference for the two \(Y_1\) values with all other variables held equal.
The table contradicts this identity over the residue field, hence also rules it out over \(L\).

Cancel the common polynomial factors in \(R=F/G\) over \(L[N,Y_1,\ldots,Y_4]\), scaling \(F,G\) together to have integral coefficients in \(L\).
Let \(S\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4\) consist of the exponent vectors in \(\mathbf Y\) occurring in either reduced polynomial with nonzero coefficient in \(L[N]\).
Write \(W_k=\max_{\mathbf a\in S}a_k-\min_{\mathbf a\in S}a_k\).
The finite-field certificate implies \(W_1\ge1\), since a common fixed \(Y_1\)-exponent in both polynomials would cancel from their ratio.
We shall prove
\[
 h_{\rm W}\big(R(p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_4^p)\big)
       =\tfrac18 ph\sum_{k=1}^4 W_k+o_P(p).                          \tag{2}
\]

Every nonzero polynomial \(J(N,\mathbf Y)\) upon specialization gives an exponential polynomial \(\sum_{\mathbf a} c_{\mathbf a}(p)(\mathbf x^{\mathbf a})^p\).
Distinct bases have nontorsion ratios by multiplicative independence.
At a fixed complex embedding \(\sigma\), dividing by the \(p\)-th power of one base having largest modulus gives a nondegenerate exponential polynomial with a base of modulus one.
The [local estimate for a nondegenerate recurrence](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#arch) bounds the log modulus of the normalized expression below by \(-o(p)\).
The triangle inequality gives the opposite bound \(O_J(\log(2p))\).
This also proves eventual nonvanishing, including for the cofactor minors and scalar factors cleared in forming \(F/G\).
For all sufficiently large \(p\) the signed matrices therefore have rank eight and their cofactor vectors span their kernels over \(L\).
The row eigenvectors from any qualifying pair are scalar multiples of these vectors at specialization, so the specialization is precisely \(R_p\).

For the finite places apply [Xiao's almost-unit estimate](../audits/literature/Xia24/Yin_U001.md#almost) to the two coprime polynomials and the five-coordinate point \((p,x_1^p,\ldots,x_4^p)\), taking the set of allowed places to be all archimedean places.
Its contribution outside those places to the almost-unit condition is \(\log p\), entirely from the first coordinate.
Its height is at least \(p h_{\rm W}(x_1)=ph/8\), so it is an almost-unit point for every fixed positive tolerance once \(p\) is sufficiently large.
For that tolerance the theorem's proper exceptional closed set contains only finitely many of these points, since a nonzero polynomial vanishing on the closed set gives an eventually nonzero exponential polynomial as just proved.
The sum of the five coordinate heights is \(O_P(p)\).
Letting the tolerance tend to zero shows that the normalized finite logarithmic common divisor is \(o_P(p)\).
If one reduced polynomial is a nonzero constant, its integral norm directly bounds this contribution.

At an embedding sending \(x_k\) to \(\rho\) the other three representatives have modulus one.
The local estimate just proved for each polynomial makes the log maximum of the two values \(ph\max_{\mathbf a\in S}a_k+o_P(p)\) there.
At embeddings sending \(x_k\) to \(\rho^{-1}\) it is \(-ph\min_{\mathbf a\in S}a_k+o_P(p)\).
These eight disjoint sets partition the embeddings with equal size \([L:\mathbb Q]/8\), since the octic is irreducible.
Their average, less the finite common divisor, proves (2).

Combining (1) and (2) gives \(\log H\ge(45/16)ph-o_P(p)\).
Direct integer evaluation gives \(25^8P(32/25)=-1\,788\,481\,599\), hence the unique exterior root exceeds \(32/25\).
The integer inequality \(32^{45}>2^{16}25^{45}\) gives \((45/16)h>\log 2\), proving eventual exclusion even at equality.
For odd \(p\) the transformation \((U(Y),V(Y))\mapsto(U(-Y),-V(-Y))\) handles \(P(-X)\) with the same coefficient norms.

A collaborating agent derived the formal determinant, the finite-field certificate and the specialized height formula that gives the strict exclusion.
