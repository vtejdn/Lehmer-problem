# Moving Fourier frequencies and a stationary test of the doubling constraints

<a id="moving-frequencies"></a>
## A bounded number of integer terms at arbitrary frequencies

In a [shrinking integral odd-grid sequence](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#statement), the normalized values \(U_j\) cannot be approximated in probability by sums
\[
                   F_p(j)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_p} c_{p,\ell}
                         e^{2\pi i m_{p,\ell}j/p},
             \qquad c_{p,\ell},m_{p,\ell}\in\mathbb Z,\qquad r_p\le H       \tag{1}
\]
for any fixed \(H\), even when all frequencies and coefficient sizes vary with \(p\).
Approximation means that for each \(\epsilon>0\) the proportion with \(|U_j-F_p(j)|>\epsilon\) tends to zero on the half grid, ignoring any undefined node.
This tests models for typical residues even when arbitrary exceptional residues are retained in the original polynomial.
The independent reviewer checked both the [moving-frequency obstruction](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v74.md#moving-frequency-review) and the [stationary process below](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v74.md#stationary-review).

Suppose (1) gave such approximations on a fixed profile subsequence.
Reflection replaces \(F_p\) by its complex conjugate, so the approximation to the real \(U_j\) holds on the reflected nodes as well, and adjoining node zero has no effect on a limit law.
The full-grid distributions of \(F_p\) are consequently tight.
Merge equal frequencies modulo \(p\) and remove zero coefficients.
The full-grid mean of \(|F_p|^2\) equals \(S=\sum_\ell|c_{p,\ell}|^2\) by character orthogonality, whereas \(\max|F_p|^2\le HS\).
For \(S>0\), at least a fraction \(1/(2H)\) of the nodes have \(|F_p|\ge\sqrt{S/2}\), by splitting the mean over these nodes and their complement.
Tightness thus bounds \(S\), and we may fix the remaining number of terms and all the integer coefficients after extraction.
Set \(F(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^r c_\ell z_\ell\) and sample it uniformly on the cyclic subgroup of \(\mathbb T^r\) generated by \((e^{2\pi i m_{p,\ell}/p})_\ell\).
The trivial zero sum already contradicts the [positive tail above four](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#threshold).

For each integer character vector \(a\in\mathbb Z^r\), its average is one if \(p\mid\sum a_\ell m_{p,\ell}\) and zero otherwise.
A diagonal subsequence makes each of these binary values eventually constant.
The vectors with limiting average one form a subgroup \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\).
By the integer Smith normal form its annihilator \(K\) in \(\mathbb T^r\) is a product of a torus with a finite abelian group of roots of unity in suitable monomial coordinates.
A character has Haar average one on \(K\) exactly when it belongs to \(\Lambda\), and zero otherwise, as is also immediate in these coordinates.
Trigonometric polynomial approximation on the compact torus therefore gives convergence to Haar measure on \(K\).
Thus \(F\) on \(K\) has an admissible normalized marginal law.
Its values are real and nonnegative everywhere by continuity and full Haar support, and this law has mean at most two and no atoms.

On each connected component the restriction is a Laurent polynomial whose coefficients are algebraic integers, obtained by substituting roots of unity in the finite coordinates.
No component has constant restriction, since every component has positive mass and a constant would produce an atom.
Choose one integer one-parameter direction in the connected torus which separates zero and every exponent in these finitely many restrictions.
The connected torus must have positive dimension since the law has no atoms.
Group the finite-component labels in orbits under conjugation in a common cyclotomic field.
In any such orbit the same top exponent \(N>0\) occurs on the one-parameter restrictions, since the coefficients on the different components are conjugates.
Their top coefficients \(a_\omega\ne0\) are algebraic integers and their product over the orbit is a nonzero rational integer.
The mean on a component is its constant coefficient \(c_\omega\), which the restriction preserves by the separating choice.
Averaging over rotations by \(N\)-th roots gives \(c_\omega\ge2|a_\omega|\), using reality and nonnegativity as in the [single-torus proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#torus).
It follows that the orbit mean of \(c_\omega\) is at least two by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
All components have equal Haar mass, so the global mean bound forces equality in every orbit and in every individual inequality.
The geometric mean of \(|a_\omega|\) is at least one, so equality gives \(|a_\omega|=1,\ c_\omega=2\) at each component.
The same \(N\) double zeros argument, allowing a complex phase in \(a_\omega\), makes the one-parameter polynomial \(2+a_\omega z^N+\bar a_\omega z^{-N}\).
Separation of exponents gives the same two characters and the constant on the whole component, with values in \([0,4]\).
This contradicts the tail bound, proving the moving-frequency exclusion.

## A stationary law passing every iterated dyadic norm test

The next example, proposed by agent-S for the [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract), tests the extent of the proof-side redirection.
It satisfies every [ordered inequality](../claims/Yin_C_G0898_v1.md#statement) together with nonnegativity, stationarity, absence of atoms and mean at most two.
It fails the arithmetic marginal test \(\int\log u\,d\eta\ge0\).

Use two intervals \(I=[1/4,3/10]\) and \(J=[6,601/100]\).
With probability one half choose labels constantly \(I\) at all integer times, and otherwise alternate \(I,J\) with a uniformly random starting phase.
Conditional on the full labels, draw independent uniforms on the labeled intervals at each time.
The process is stationary and the oriented pairs of intervals \(II,IJ,JI\) have probabilities \(1/2,1/4,1/4\).
Its mean is at most \((3/4)(3/10)+(1/4)(601/100)<2\), and its mean logarithm is negative since \((3/10)^3(601/100)<1\).

Let \(u,v\) be successive draws and \(D_m=T_m(v-2)-T_{2m}(u-2)\) in the trace convention \(T_m(2\cos\theta)=2\cos(m\theta)\).
We verify \(\mathbb E\log|D_m|>\log(2m)\) for every \(m=2^k\).
For \(m=1\) the absolute difference is \(|v-(u-2)^2|\), with lower bounds \(2.59,2.93,15.7\) on \(II,IJ,JI\), respectively.
For \(m=2\), substitution of the bounds for \((u-2)^2\) in \(T_2(t)=t^2-2,\ T_4(t)=T_2(t)^2-2\) gives lower bounds \(1.76,7,79\).
In each of these two rows the product of the square of the first bound and the other two bounds exceeds \((2m)^4\).

For \(m=4\) the lower bounds on \(IJ,JI\) are 192 and 37632 by \(T_4(4)=194,\ T_8(4)=37634\) and \(|T_m|\le2\) on \([-2,2]\).
On \(II\) the absolute derivative \(|T_4'(v-2)|=4|v-2|((v-2)^2-2)>6\) gives a density bound \(10/3\) for \(T_4(v-2)\).
If a real random variable \(Y\) has density at most \(K\), then for every \(c\) the negative part of \(\log|Y-c|\) has mean at most \(1+\log(2K)\) for \(2K\ge1\), by integrating \(\Pr(|Y-c|<e^{-w})\le\min(1,2Ke^{-w})\) over \(w\ge0\).
Condition on \(u\) in \(II\).
The weighted lower bound is \(-\tfrac12(1+\log(20/3))+\tfrac14\log(192\cdot37632)>\log8\).

For \(m\ge8\) and \(v\) uniform on \(I\), put \(\theta=\arccos((v-2)/2)\).
Its density is \(40\sin\theta<22\) and its interval has length less than \(0.052\) by \(\sin\theta\ge\sqrt{1-(7/8)^2}\).
Hence \(m\theta\bmod 2\pi\) has density at most \(22(1/m+0.052/(2\pi))<3\).
For circular angle \(w\) with density at most three, \(|2\sin((w-a)/2)|\ge 2\,\operatorname{dist}(w,a)/\pi\) bounds the mean negative logarithm by \(1+\log(3\pi)<3.25\) using the same tail integration.
Condition on \(u\in I\), write \(T_{2m}(u-2)=2\cos a\), and factor the cosine difference into the two sine factors centered at \(a,-a\).
This bounds the conditional mean logarithm on \(II\) below by \(-6.5\).
On \(IJ,JI\) the bounds are \(3^m-2,3^{2m}-2\) since \(T_m(4)=(2+\sqrt3)^m+(2-\sqrt3)^m\) and \(T_m\) increases on \([2,\infty)\).
The resulting weighted mean is at least \(-3.25+(3m\log3-2\log2)/4>\log(2m)\) for \(m\ge8\), by the inequality at eight and differentiation.
This proves the comparison for all dyadic orders.
The remaining feasibility question requires conditions in addition to these ordered tests; in particular the arithmetic restrictions on the marginal apply to any actual integer construction.
