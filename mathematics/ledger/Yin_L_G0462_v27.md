# Torsion preimages at the fourth and eighth multiples for Pisot seeds

<a id="eight-phases"></a>
## An eight-phase positive rate for each fixed nonreciprocal seed

Let \(f\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be the monic minimal polynomial of a Pisot number \(\theta>1\), of degree \(m\), and set \(g(z)=z^m f(1/z)\).
Assume \(f,g\) are coprime.
For \(k\in\mathbb Z\) put \(a=\max(k,0)\), \(b=\max(-k,0)\), \(A=z^a f\) and \(B=z^b g\).
Then \(M(A^8-B^8)>1\) at every offset \(k\).
Moreover \(M(A^4-B^4)>1\) apart from at most one explicitly determined offset for each seed.
The [parity criterion](#four-phase-parity) and [real-root tests](#real-fibers) below give further restrictions on that exception.

For either sign \(\sigma=\pm1\) and sufficiently large \(N\), write \(S\) for the minimal polynomial of the unique exterior root of \(z^N f-\sigma g\), \(d=\deg S\) and \(C_n=|\operatorname{Res}(S,z^n-1)|\).
Then \(\liminf_{N\to\infty,\ \sigma=\pm1}d^{-1}\log C_{8d}>0\) for each fixed seed as above.
A [quartic seed](#quartic-seed) gives an infinite sequence with \(0<\lim d^{-1}\log C_{4d}<\log2\).
The construction contributor obtained these criteria while investigating the [plastic-seed fourth-multiple theorem](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v2.md#statement) for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) on 2026-11-16.

<a id="four-phase-parity"></a>
## The even factor forced by a torsion fiber

The integer polynomials \(A,B\) are coprime and \(A^2+B^2\) and \(A^4+B^4\) have nonzero constants.
If \(M(A^4-B^4)=1\), the factor \(A^2+B^2\) also has measure one, since any nonzero integer polynomial has measure at least one.
Its roots are roots of unity by Kronecker's theorem.
At such a root \(\eta\), \(A(\eta)/B(\eta)\) has order four.
Hence \(\mathbb Q(\eta)\) contains \(i\), and the order \(n\) of \(\eta\) is divisible by four.
Indeed if \(n=u\) or \(2u\) with \(u\) odd, its field is \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_u)\) and adjoining \(i\) doubles its degree, by \(\varphi(4u)=2\varphi(u)\).
For \(4\mid n\), replacing every primitive \(n\)-th root by its negative permutes the primitive roots, as \(1+n/2\) is coprime to \(n\).
Thus each such \(\Phi_n\) is even, and so is \(A^2+B^2\).

Write \(f(z)=E(z^2)+zO(z^2)\), and put \(H(X)=E(X)O(X)\).
Both factors are nonzero, since \(f(0)\ne0\) and if \(f\) were even then \(-\theta\) would be another exterior root.
For \(g\), the product of its two corresponding components is \(X^{m-1}H(1/X)\).
This follows by reversing the even and odd coefficient arrays, interchanging their roles when \(m\) is odd.
The vanishing odd part of \(A^2+B^2\) therefore requires
\[
            X^kH(X)=-X^{m-1}H(1/X).                              \tag{1}
\]
Writing \(u\) for the smallest nonzero coefficient index of \(H\), comparison of the lowest Laurent powers gives \(k=m-1-u-\deg H\).
Evaluating (1) at \(X=1\) gives \(H(1)=0\), equivalently \(f(1)^2=f(-1)^2\).
Failure of either condition proves positive four-phase measure.

If \(M(A^8-B^8)=1\), the factor \(A^4+B^4\) now has measure one as well.
The ratios at its roots have order eight, so their fields also contain \(i\) and \(A^4+B^4\) is even by the same argument.
As both \(A^2+B^2\) and \(A^4+B^4\) are even,
\[
              2A^2B^2=(A^2+B^2)^2-(A^4+B^4)
\]
is even.
The unordered pairs \(\{A(-z)^2,B(-z)^2\}\) and \(\{A(z)^2,B(z)^2\}\) are thus the roots of the same monic quadratic over \(\mathbb Q(z)\).
It follows that \(A(-z)=\pm A(z)\) or \(A(-z)=\pm B(z)\).
The first is impossible because \(f\) has both parities.
The second forces \(a=b\) by the orders at zero, hence \(k=0\).
At that offset the leading coefficient of \(f^2+g^2\) is \(1+f(0)^2\ge2\), also impossible.
This proves the unconditional eight-phase statement.

<a id="real-fibers"></a>
## Real roots eliminate more fourth-phase exceptions

For \(k>0\), \(A+B\) has positive leading coefficient and \((A+B)(1)=2f(1)<0\).
The latter sign follows because \(\theta\) is the only real root above one.
Thus \(A+B\) has an exterior positive real zero.
The preceding leading-coefficient argument handles \(k=0\).
For \(k<0\), if \(|f(0)|>1\) the absolute leading coefficient of \(A^4-B^4\) is \(|f(0)|^4>1\).
If \(f(0)>0\), \(A+B\) again has positive leading coefficient and negative value at one.

For \(k<0\) a real conjugate \(r\ne\theta\) of \(\theta\) would give an uncanceled real pole \(1/r\) of \(A/B\) with \(|1/r|>1\).
On its side of the real line take the outermost pole and move from there toward infinity in modulus.
The continuous ratio tends from unbounded absolute value to zero, since \(\deg B>\deg A\).
On that interval it takes the value \(1\) or \(-1\), giving an exterior real root of \(A^4-B^4\).
Consequently a possible zero four-phase rate requires negative \(k\) satisfying (1), \(f(0)=-1\), odd degree and no real conjugate other than \(\theta\).
In this case \(f(1)=f(-1)<0\) by their signs and (1).
The argument leaves open whether a genuine Pisot seed realizes a zero four-phase rate.

<a id="fixed-seed-return"></a>
## Passage to the original degree

For completeness the [circle and torsion arguments for the plastic seed](Yin_L_G0462_v24.md#all-delays) extend to this fixed \(f\).
On the circle \(|f|=|g|>0\).
For \(0<t<1\), Rouché's theorem shows \(z^N f-t\sigma g\) has just one root outside the unit disk.
Continuity gives at most one strictly exterior root at \(t=1\), counted with multiplicity.
For each fixed \(1<s<\theta<s'\) sufficiently close to \(\theta\), the signs of \(z^N f-\sigma g\) at \(s,s'\) are opposite for large \(N\).
The single exterior root is therefore real, positive and approaches \(\theta\).
Reciprocity up to sign gives its sole inverse interior root.
The exterior root is a unit, so its monic irreducible factor \(S\) must contain the interior root as well, by the absolute product of its roots.
All remaining factors are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.

Write \(G=g/f\).
A torsion root \(\eta\) of the delay polynomial satisfies \(G(\eta)=\sigma\eta^N\).
According as the order is odd, twice an odd number, or divisible by four, a conjugate of \(\eta\) is \(\xi\eta^h\) with \((\xi,h)=(1,2),(-1,2),(-1,1)\), respectively.
The root must therefore satisfy \(G(\xi\eta^h)=\xi^N\sigma^{h-1}G(\eta)^h\).
None of these finitely many rational identities holds identically in \(z\).
For \(h=2\) the right side has a pole at \(\theta\) and the left is finite there since \(\theta^2>\theta\).
For \(h=1,\xi=-1\), the left side is finite at \(\theta\) since \(f(-\theta)\ne0\).
Thus the torsion roots lie in a fixed finite set.
They are simple for large \(N\), since repetition would require \(N=\eta G'(\eta)/G(\eta)\).
It follows that the cyclotomic cofactor has bounded degree, so only finitely many \(k=N-d\) occur.

The [phase-product proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0336_v2.md#transfer) now gives, for each fixed even \(j\),
\[
             d^{-1}\log C_{jd}=\log M(A^j-B^j)+O_{f,j}((\log d)/d).
\]
Indeed it uses only the finite list of offsets and simple torsion cofactors, absence of unit-circle zeros of \(f,g\), and the fixed algebraic power estimate at the finitely many resulting unit-circle roots.
The minimum of the positive eight-phase logarithms on this finite set of offsets is positive.

<a id="quartic-seed"></a>
## A fourth-multiple rate below the elementary degree cost

Take \(f=z^4-z^3-1,\ g=1-z-z^4,\ \sigma=-1\).
Its derivative \(z^2(4z-3)\) shows it has exactly two real roots \(\theta,-r\).
One has \(\theta>4/3\) and \(r>4/5\) by substitution.
The other two roots are conjugate with squared modulus \(1/(\theta r)<1\), and \(r<1\) by \(f(-1)>0\).
Every proper monic factor formed from interior roots alone would have nonzero integral constant term of modulus below one.
Thus the polynomial is irreducible and \(\theta\) is Pisot.
The reciprocal is coprime to it.

Choose \(N\equiv-1\) modulo a common multiple of all possible torsion orders from the fixed-seed argument and of six.
At a torsion root of \(z^N f+g\), one then has \(f+zg=0\).
Conversely each root of unity of \(f+zg\) is a root of that delay polynomial on this progression if its order divides the modulus.
Direct expansion gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f+zg&=-(z+1)(z^2-z+1)^2,\\
 f-zg&=(z-1)(z^4+2z^3+z^2+2z+1),\\
 f^2+z^2g^2&=(z^2+1)(z^8-z^4+1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence the simple torsion cofactor is \((z+1)(z^2-z+1)\) for large \(N\), so \(k=N-d=3-4=-1\).
All factors of \(f^4-z^4g^4\) outside the displayed reciprocal quartic with trace polynomial \(t^2+2t-1\) are cyclotomic.
Indeed \(z^8-z^4+1=\Phi_{24}(z)\).
The trace roots are \(-1\pm\sqrt2\), of which just \(-1-\sqrt2\) lies outside \([-2,2]\).
The complete four-phase measure is therefore the number \(\lambda>1\) satisfying \(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=1+\sqrt2<5/2\), giving \(\lambda<2\).
On this progression \(d^{-1}\log C_{4d}\to\log\lambda\).
