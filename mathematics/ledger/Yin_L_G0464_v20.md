# The least real perturbation degree for unbounded conductors in the exceptional family

For the exceptional cyclotomic parent in the [angular matching argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0890_v2.md#statement), real perturbations of degree at most two cannot give strict terminal cuts at unbounded conductors.
A cubic with rational coefficients gives such cuts and the strict endpoint for every \(p\equiv1\pmod8\) with \(p\ge25\).
On 2026-11-16 a construction contributor for Yin's [unrestricted Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) derived this degree distinction by comparing the phase at its interior zeros and its last grid node.
The [adjacent-index perturbations](Yin_L_G0464_v19.md#family) use two varying trace basis elements.
Here \(H\) is an arbitrary polynomial of bounded degree added directly to the trace.
The [integral coefficient cutoff](Yin_L_G0457_v7.md#effective-cutoff) addresses the arithmetic restriction on this family.
The [independent real-degree review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v45.md#real-degree-review) covers the moving-parent and multiplicity bounds and the cubic construction.

## The phase and the final block

Fix \(s\ge2\), put \(d=s+1\), and take
\[
 F_s(z)=(1+z^2)^2(1+z^2+\cdots+z^{2s-2}).
\]
Let \(r>2d+1\) be odd, \(p=r+2s=2n+1\), \(m=(r-1)/2\), \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\), and \(b_j=2\cos\theta_j\).
Write \(E_{s-1}(2\cos\theta)=\sin(s\theta)/\sin\theta\) and \(B_m(2\cos\theta)=\sin(r\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2)\).
The trace of \(F_s\) is \(C_F(t)=t^2E_{s-1}(t)\), since \(z^2t^2=(1+z^2)^2\) and \(z^{s-1}E_{s-1}(t)=1+z^2+\cdots+z^{2s-2}\) at \(t=z+z^{-1}\).
For the trace \(Q=C_FB_m+H\) with \(H\in\mathbb R[t]\) of degree at most \(h\), the [grid identity](Yin_L_G0457_v6.md#alternation) gives the sign of \(Q(b_j)/B'_n(b_j)\) as the sign of
\[
 W(b_j)+(-1)^{j+1}H(b_j),\qquad
 W(t)=\sqrt{2+t}\,D(t),\qquad D(t)=t^2 E_{s-1}(t)^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(\sin\theta/\sin(\theta/2)=\sqrt{2+t}\) in the upper half-circle.
The strict cut requires an initial nonempty positive block and a terminal nonempty negative block.
On the terminal block \(W\ge0\) forces \(H\) to take alternating nonzero signs, so the length of this block is at most \(h+1\).

Put \(u=\pi/p\), so \(b_n=-2\cos u\).
At this last node,
\[
 |H(b_n)|>W(b_n)=\frac{4\cos^2u\,\sin^2(su)}{\sin u\cos(u/2)}
 \ \ge\ \frac{4s^2}{\pi p}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(p>4s+3\), hence \(su<\pi/4\), \(4\cos^2u\ge1\), \(\sin(su)\ge 2su/\pi\), and \(\sin u\le u\).
For fixed \(s\) the middle expression is asymptotic to \(4s^2\pi/p\).
The endpoint condition, when imposed, is
\[
 Q(-2)/B_n(-2)=-4s+(-1)^nH(-2)<0
 \tag{3}
\]
by \(E_{s-1}(-2)=(-1)^{s-1}s\), \(B_j(-2)=(-1)^j\) and \(n=m+s\).

## A growing parent degree cannot support a fixed real perturbation degree

For any fixed \(h\), every sequence satisfying the strict cuts with \(p\to\infty\) has bounded \(s\).
Otherwise pass to a subsequence with \(s\to\infty\).
For each \(i=1,\ldots,h+1\), choose an interior sine zero \(\alpha_i=k_i\pi/s\) approaching \(i\pi/(h+2)\), using the nearest integer \(k_i\) to \(si/(h+2)\).
Two adjacent grid angles enclosing \(\alpha_i\) are within \(2\pi/p\) of it, also when it is a grid angle.
The two nodes belong to the initial block for all sufficiently large \(p\), since their limiting angle is strictly below \(\pi\).
At each of them (1) becomes
\[
 W(2\cos\theta)=\frac{4\cos^2\theta\,\sin^2(s\theta)}
                            {\sin\theta\sin(\theta/2)}
                   \ \le\ C_h\,s^2/p^2 .
\]
The denominators are bounded away from zero and \(|\sin(s\theta)|\le s|\theta-\alpha_i|\).
The two initial residues give \(H>-C_hs^2/p^2\) at the odd node and \(H<C_hs^2/p^2\) at the even node.
Continuity supplies a trace value \(\xi_i\) between them with \(|H(\xi_i)|\le C_hs^2/p^2\).
The \(\xi_i\) converge to distinct trace values in \((-2,2)\).
Lagrange interpolation at these \(h+1\) values, whose separations are bounded below and whose distances to \(b_n\) are at most four, gives \(|H(b_n)|\le C'_h s^2/p^2\).
This contradicts (2) as \(p\to\infty\).

## The multiplicities at a fixed parent

For fixed \(s\), let \(x\) be any distinct root of \(D\) and let \(m_x\) be its multiplicity.
These roots are in \((-2,2)\).
For a sequence with the strict cut and \(p\to\infty\), select \(h+1\) pairs of adjacent initial nodes within \(O_h(1/p)\) of \(x\), leaving at least one grid gap between successive pairs.
This can be done by starting at the grid angle nearest to \(\arccos(x/2)\) and using a fixed number of consecutive indices, since the final block has length at most \(h+1\).
On these pairs \(W=O_{s,h}(p^{-m_x})\) by (1).
The opposite parities again supply one intermediate trace value per pair where \(|H|=O_{s,h}(p^{-m_x})\).
These trace values have mutual separations at least \(c_{s,h}/p\) because the trace derivative is nonzero at the interior angle for \(x\).
After the substitution \(t=x+v/p\), their scaled coordinates \(v\) stay bounded and separated.
Lagrange interpolation thus bounds each coefficient of \(H(x+v/p)\) by \(O_{s,h}(p^{-m_x})\).
Equivalently, for \(0\le k\le h\),
\[
 |H^{(k)}(x)|=O_{s,h}(p^{k-m_x}).
 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(\|H\|\) be the largest absolute coefficient of \(H\).
Since \(|b_n|\le2\), (2) implies \(\|H\|\ge c_{s,h}/p\).
Pass to a coefficientwise convergent subsequence of \(H/\|H\|\), whose limit has norm one.
By (4) all its derivatives of order \(k<m_x-1\) vanish at \(x\), including orders above \(h\) automatically.
Summing the required multiplicities at distinct roots gives
\[
 h\ \ge\sum_x(m_x-1)
    =2s-\#\{x:D(x)=0\}
    =
    \begin{cases}s,&s\ \text{odd},\\ s+1,&s\ \text{even}.\end{cases}
 \tag{5}
\]
The \(s-1\) simple zeros of \(E_{s-1}\) are \(2\cos(k\pi/s)\), \(1\le k<s\), and they include zero exactly when \(s\) is even.
Thus every fixed parent requires \(h\ge3\) for unbounded conductors.
The boundedness of \(s\) proved above extends this necessity to arbitrary varying exceptional parents, by taking a constant-\(s\) subsequence.

## A rational cubic attains degree three

Take \(s=2\), so \(F_2=(1+z^2)^3\), \(D=t^4\), and choose \(p=8a+1\ge25\) and \(H(t)=-7t^3/p\).
Here \(r=p-4>7\), \(n=4a\), and \(b_j\ne0\) for every node.
The last residue is negative because
\[
 f(b_n)=|b_n|\sqrt{2+b_n}
       =4\cos(\pi/p)\sin(\pi/(2p))<2\pi/p<7/p .
\]
For an initial node the perturbation term in (1) has sign \((-1)^j\operatorname{sgn}(b_j)\).
It suffices to show \(f(b_j)>7/p\) when \(j\) is odd with \(b_j>0\), or \(j<n\) is even with \(b_j<0\).

On the positive side \(f(t)=t\sqrt{2+t}\) increases, and the smallest trace in the indicated set is \(b_{2a-1}=2\sin(5\pi/(2p))\ge10/p\).
On the negative side the indicated traces range from \(b_{n-2}=-2\cos(5\pi/p)\) to \(b_{2a+2}=-2\sin(7\pi/(2p))\).
There \(f(t)=-t\sqrt{2+t}\) has its sole interior stationary point at \(-4/3\), a maximum, so its minimum on the interval is at an endpoint.
Since \(7\pi/(2p)<\pi/6\), \(b_{2a+2}>-1\) and \(f(b_{2a+2})\ge 2\sin(7\pi/(2p))\ge14/p\).
The other value is \(f(b_{n-2})=4\cos(5\pi/p)\sin(5\pi/(2p))\ge10/p\), using \(\cos(5\pi/p)\ge1/2\).
Both lower estimates use \(\sin y\ge2y/\pi\) on \([0,\pi/2]\).
Every residue before the last is therefore positive.
Equation (3) gives \(-8+56/p<0\).

For general fixed \(s\), there is also a direct rational perturbation of degree \(2s\).
For any admissible \(p\) coprime to \(s\), one has \(D(b_j)>0\) at every node because \(p\) is odd and \(p\nmid 2sj\) for \(1\le j\le n\).
Choose a rational \(k\) with
\[
 \sqrt{2+b_n}<k<\min\{\sqrt{2+b_{n-1}},1/s\}
\]
and put \(H=(-1)^n kD\).
Such \(k\) exists since \(\sqrt{2+b_n}=2\sin(\pi/(2p))<\pi/p<1/s\).
Equation (1) gives the strict cut after \(n-1\), and (3) gives the endpoint value \(-4s+4s^2k<0\).
There are infinitely many permitted conductors \(p\equiv1\pmod{2s}\).
These rational constructions establish sharpness of the universal real degree threshold, while the exact least degree for each \(s>2\) lies between (5) and \(2s\).
They supply no integral sequence for Lehmer's problem.
