# Cubic inputs on unbounded odd grids and the degree required by a fourth coefficient

<a id="cubic-chamber"></a>
## Three cubic inputs at every odd conductor from nine onward

Use the [full odd-grid zero profile](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-coordinates), with \(p=2n+1\), \(b_j=2\cos(2\pi j/p)\) for \(1\le j\le n\), and \(h=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_iE_i\) in \(\mathbb Z[t]\).
Here \(E_0=1\), \(E_1=t\), \(E_{i+1}=tE_i-E_{i-1}\), and \(\rho_j=(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)/p\).
Require the residues to be strictly positive through one index \(1\le c<n\) and strictly negative thereafter.
In the lift \(P\), agreement with \(P_{*,0}=(1+z^2)^2/((1-z)(1-z^2))\) through index three means \(a_0=2,a_1=0\).

Along any sequence of such inputs of degree at most three with \(p\to\infty\), every sufficiently late input is one of the following three cubics.
Each listed cubic works at every odd \(p\ge9\), has the indicated positive endpoint, and first differs from the profile at index four.

| \(a_2\) | \(a_3\) | \(h=2+a_2E_2+a_3E_3\) | \(P(-1)\) |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| \(0\) | \(1\) | \(t^3-2t+2\) | \(3\) |
| \(-1\) | \(1\) | \(t^3-t^2-2t+3\) | \(2\) |
| \(-1\) | \(2\) | \((t-1)^2(2t+3)\) | \(1\) |

The greatest value of \(a_2\) among these cubics is zero.
The prescribed value for matching index four is four.
The construction contributor for [Yin's Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this classification and the degree bound below on 2026-11-16.
The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v72.md#cubic-baseline-review) covers the finite-conductor construction, the classification on unbounded grids and the degree-five obstruction.

<a id="fixed-degree"></a>
## Compactness and classification

The [discrete orthogonality](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#prefix-cut-compatibility) gives \(a_i=\sum_jE_i(b_j)\rho_j\) for \(0\le i<n\).
In particular \(\sum_j\rho_j=2\) and \(\sum_j b_j\rho_j=0\).
For any \(y\) in the open transition gap, \((b_j-y)\rho_j>0\) and their sum is \(-2y\), so \(y<0\).
The identity
\[
 a_i=2E_i(y)+\sum_j
       \frac{E_i(b_j)-E_i(y)}{b_j-y}(b_j-y)\rho_j
\]
bounds \(|a_i|\) by \(2\|E_i\|_\infty+4\|E_i'\|_\infty\) on \([-2,2]\), independently of the conductor.
In any bounded degree there are therefore only finitely many possible integral inputs.
For a fixed input recurring on unbounded grids, the strict cuts imply that it takes each sign in the interior and that there is exactly one sign-changing zero \(y\) there.
Indeed, additional sign changes would occupy separate gaps on sufficiently dense grids, while two points of opposite sign already occur on each admissible grid.
This zero lies in the transition gap for all sufficiently large admissible conductors.
Thus \(y<0\), \(h(-2)\le0\), and \(h(t)\ge0\) for \(y\le t\le2\).

Write \(A=a_2,B=a_3\), using zero coefficients for missing degrees.
The values \(h(0)=2-A\), \(h(\sqrt2)=2+A\) and \(h(1)=2-B\) give \(-2\le A\le2\) and \(B\le2\).
The endpoint values \(2+3A\pm4B\) give \(4B\ge|2+3A|\), hence \(B=1\) or \(B=2\).
For \(B=2\) the nonnegative polynomial on a neighborhood of \(1\) vanishes there, so \(h'(1)=2A+2=0\).
This gives the last cubic in the table.
For \(B=1\) the only possible values are \(A=-2,-1,0\).
The value \(A=-2\) gives \((t-2)(t^2-2)\), which changes sign at both interior roots \(\pm\sqrt2\).
This leaves the first two rows.

The first two cubics have discriminants \(-76,-87\) respectively and are irreducible by the rational root test.
Each has only one real root, which lies between \(-9/5\) and zero by the signs at those endpoints.
No grid node can equal this root, since every conjugate of a root-of-unity trace is real.
The third cubic changes sign at \(-3/2\), which cannot be an algebraic integer, and its double root \(1\) is the trace of a primitive sixth root of unity, absent at odd conductors.
For odd \(p\ge9\), the least grid node is \(-2\cos(\pi/p)<-9/5\), using \(2\cos(\pi/8)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}>9/5\), and the greatest node is positive.
The three cuts therefore have both signs and all their grid values are strict.
The [endpoint formula](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0155/versions/v4/readme.md#exclusion) gives \(P(-1)=2+\sum_i(-1)^i a_i=4+A-B\).

<a id="fourth-degree"></a>
## A fourth coefficient requires degree at least six along unbounded conductors

Along any sequence of strict integral inputs matching through index four with \(p\to\infty\), the canonical degree \(\deg h\) is eventually at least six.
The endpoint condition is unnecessary.
If infinitely many degrees were at most five, the preceding coefficient bound would give a fixed \(h\) along a subsequence.
Now \(\sum b_j^2\rho_j=a_2+a_0=6\).
Every separator \(y\) in a transition gap satisfies
\[
      0<\sum_j(2-b_j)(b_j-y)\rho_j=-6-4y,
\]
so \(y<-3/2\).
Dense grids imply \(h\ge0\) on \([-3/2,2]\).
Write \(h=2+4E_2+A E_3+B E_4+C E_5\).
At \(0\) and \(\pm1\) its values are \(B-2\) and \(2-B\mp A\), forcing \(B=2,A=0\).
The zero at \(0\) is then a local minimum, giving \(h'(0)=3C=0\).
The remaining polynomial is \(2t^2(t^2-1)\), which is negative at \(t=1/2\).
This proves the degree bound.
