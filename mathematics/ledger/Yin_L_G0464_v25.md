# Prime-conductor residue values generate the real cyclotomic field

<a id="prime-field"></a>
## The stabilizer of a strict cut

The [independent review](../audits/reviews/Yin_R_G1107_v59.md#prime-grid-review) covers the stabilizer and the joint, independent-pair and correlated-pair estimates below.

Let \(p=2n+1\ge5\) be prime, \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p)\), \(b_j=\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j}\), and \(K=\mathbb Q(b_1)\).
Consider any \(u\in K\) whose conjugates \(u_j\) under \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^j\), \(1\le j\le n\), are strictly positive for \(j\le n-\kappa\) and strictly negative thereafter, with \(1\le\kappa<n\).
Then \(\mathbb Q(u)=K\).
In particular all \(n\) displayed values are distinct, already at a single conductor.
On 2026-11-16 the construction contributor for Yin's [Lehmer Task](../tasks/Yin_T_G0001_v1.md#contract) obtained this fact from the odd residues in the negative block and applied it to the integral residue values below.

Indeed, any multiplier \(a\in(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\) fixing \(u\) preserves the full set of exponents \(\pm j\) where the conjugate is negative.
Doubling these exponents gives the same multiplicative stabilizer for
\[
             S=\{\pm1,\pm3,\ldots,\pm(2\kappa-1)\}\pmod p .
\]
Its elements are distinct since doubling was applied to \(2\kappa\) distinct exponents.
Now \((a^2-1)\sum_{v\in S}v^2=0\bmod p\), whereas
\[
        \sum_{v\in S}v^2=2\kappa(4\kappa^2-1)/3\ne0\bmod p .
\]
Both \(2\kappa-1\) and \(2\kappa+1\) lie strictly between zero and \(p\), and \(p\ne3\).
Thus \(a=\pm1\), which proves the assertion using the usual [cyclotomic conjugacies](../audits/literature/Bel17/Yin_U001.md#bel17-u001).

## The integral transform at the zero profile

Use the [integral chamber](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v3.md#original-domain) with \(B=B_n\), integral canonical coordinate \(h\), residues \(\rho_j=(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)/p\), strict cut as above and strict endpoint.
Here \(B_n(2\cos\theta)=\sin(p\theta/2)/\sin(\theta/2)\).
Set
\[
           U_j=\frac{p\rho_j}{b_j^2}=\frac{(4-b_j^2)h(b_j)}{b_j^2}.
\]
Each \(b_j=\zeta^{-j}(1+\zeta^{2j})\) is a unit because \(\Phi_p(-1)=1\).
The \(U_j\) are therefore conjugate algebraic integers, and the strict cut proves they generate \(K\).
The same field generation holds for \(h(b_1)\) and \((4-b_1^2)h(b_1)\).

Consider a sequence at prime conductors with \(p\to\infty\) in this chamber and matching indices tending to infinity with the fixed profile
\[
              P_{*,0}=(1+z^2)^2/((1-z)(1-z^2)).
\]
Use the [fixed-profile measure bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0881_v10.md#statement) and [completed-grid comparison](Yin_L_G0464_v23.md#completed-grid).
They give \(a=2\cos((2(n-\kappa)+1)\pi/p)\to-2\) and positive \(X_j=(b_j-a)p\rho_j\), with
\[
 \mathbb E_p X_j V(b_j)\longrightarrow\int 2(t+2)t^2 V(t)\,d\omega(t)
 \quad\text{for every continuous }V,
\]
where \(\omega\) is the arcsine law on \([-2,2]\) and \(\mathbb E_p\) denotes the mean over the \(n\) nodes.
The pairs \((b_j,U_j)\) consequently have tight empirical laws.
Indeed, outside any fixed neighborhoods of \(0,-2\) one has \(|U_j|\le C X_j\) for a constant depending on those neighborhoods for all sufficiently large \(p\).
The grid fraction of the neighborhoods can be made arbitrarily small and \(\mathbb E_p X_j\) stays bounded.

Every weak subsequential limit \(\eta\) has first marginal \(\omega\) and is supported on \(u\ge0\), since \(\kappa/n\to0\).
For each continuous \(V\ge0\) one has
\[
                   \int uV(t)\,d\eta(t,u)\le2\int V\,d\omega .       \tag{1}
\]
First take \(V\) supported away from \(0,-2\).
On this support \(U_j\ge0\) eventually and \(\mathbb E_p U_jV(b_j)\to2\int V\,d\omega\) by uniform convergence of \(V(t)/((t-a)t^2)\) to \(V(t)/((t+2)t^2)\).
Pass bounded truncations of the nonnegative \(u\) to the weak limit, then let the truncation grow.
Continuous cutoffs increasing to one outside \(\{0,-2\}\) give the general assertion by monotone convergence, since the first marginal has no atoms at these points.

The quantities \(\log^+|U_j|\) are uniformly integrable on the empirical grids.
Indeed they are bounded above by
\[
             \log^+X_j+2\log^+(1/|b_j|)+\log^+(1/|b_j-a|).
\]
The first term is uniformly integrable by the bounded first moments of \(X_j\).
Writing \(\theta_j=2\pi j/p\) and \(\phi=(2(n-\kappa)+1)\pi/p\), one has \(|b_j|\ge c|\theta_j-\pi/2|\) and \(|b_j-a|\ge c|\theta_j-\phi|^2\) with an absolute \(c>0\), by the sine chord bounds.
The respective angular distances are at least \(\pi/(2p)\) and \(\pi/p\).
Grid spacing then bounds the fractions with \(|b_j|<\epsilon\) or \(|b_j-a|<\epsilon\) by \(C\epsilon\) or \(C\sqrt{\epsilon}\), respectively, for \(0<\epsilon<1\).
Integrating these exponential logarithmic tail bounds proves the assertion.

<a id="norm-tests"></a>
## Norm and discriminant tests on limiting laws

For any nonzero \(R\in\mathbb Z[t,y]\) such that \(R(t,y)\ne0\) whenever \(-2<t<2,\ y<0\), one has
\[
                       \int\log|R(t,u)|\,d\eta(t,u)\ge0 .           \tag{2}
\]
In fact \(R(b_1,U_1)\) cannot vanish, since then every conjugate would vanish, contradicting a negative node.
Its absolute norm is therefore a positive integer.
The positive logarithmic parts are uniformly integrable by the polynomial growth of \(R\) and the preceding tail bounds.
Truncate \(\log|R|\) first below at \(-T\) and then above to pass to the weak limit, remove the upper truncation by uniform integrability, and let \(T\to\infty\).
The same conclusion holds for every fixed nonzero \(R\in\mathbb Z[y]\), including polynomials with negative roots, since the field degree \(n\) eventually exceeds \(\deg R\).

For the marginal \(\eta_U\) in \(u\), one also has
\[
              \iint\log|u-v|\,d\eta_U(u)d\eta_U(v)\ge0 .             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(\prod_{i<j}(U_i-U_j)^2\) is the nonzero integer discriminant of the monic minimal polynomial of \(U_1\).
In the product empirical measure discard the diagonal of mass \(1/n\) before applying the lower truncation.
The empirical mean of the off-diagonal logarithms is nonnegative by this discriminant.
The discarded diagonal contributes \(-T/n\) to the fixed lower truncation.
The positive parts are uniformly integrable because \(\log^+|U_i-U_j|\le \log2+\log^+|U_i|+\log^+|U_j|\).
Pass to the weak limit of the product laws and remove the truncations as in (2).
In particular \(\eta_U\) has no atoms.

There is also an arithmetic test on correlated pairs of nodes.
For each fixed \(a_0\ge2\), let \(j'\) be the representative in \(1,\ldots,n\) of \(\pm a_0j\bmod p\).
For all sufficiently large prime \(p\) one has
\[
                \prod_{j=1}^n |U_{j'}-U_j|\ge p .                  \tag{4}
\]
One may express \(U_j\) as an integral polynomial in \(b_j\), since \(B_n(0)=\pm1\) gives an integral polynomial representative of \(1/b_j\) directly from \(B_n(b_j)=0\).
Therefore \((U_{j'}-U_j)/(b_{j'}-b_j)\) is an algebraic integer, nonzero by field generation, and these quotients comprise the full list of conjugates counted with multiplicity.
The difference \(b_{j'}-b_j\) is a root of unity times \((1-\zeta^{(a_0-1)j})(1-\zeta^{(a_0+1)j})\) up to sign.
Both multipliers are nonzero modulo \(p\).
The products of the moduli on the full nontrivial grid are \(p\) for each binomial, so the half-grid product of the difference moduli is \(p\).
Combining this with the norm proves (4).
Every limiting joint law of \((U_j,U_{j'})\) consequently integrates \(\log|u-v|\) to a nonnegative value, by the same truncation argument applied to its two marginals.

## A ceiling from the shift by two

Put \(\Delta=|\operatorname{Res}(B,Q)|\).
The [grid products](Yin_L_G0464_v5.md#odd-grid-product) and \(|\prod b_j|=1\) give \(\prod_j|U_j|=p\Delta\).
Along a subsequence realizing \(D=\limsup n^{-1}\log\Delta\), take a weak limit as above.
Upper truncation controlled by the logarithmic tails and lower truncation at the origin give \(D\le\int\log u\,d\eta_U(u)\).
The right side is finite because \(D\ge0\), (1) bounds the first moment, and the limiting law has no negative support.
By (2) for \(R=y-2\) and Jensen's inequality, \(\int |u-2|\,d\eta_U\ge1\).

Let \(Z=u/2,\ m=\int Z\,d\eta_U\le1\).
Extend the probability \(\eta_U\) by zero at an additional point and extend the measure \(Z\eta_U\) to a probability by giving that point mass \(1-m\).
The relative entropy of the first probability with respect to the second is \(-\int\log Z\,d\eta_U\).
For completeness this entropy bounds one half the squared \(L^1\) distance of the two probabilities.
Partition at the set where the first density exceeds the second, writing \(r,s\) for their respective masses on that set.
Jensen's inequality for \(x\log x\) bounds the entropy below by \(r\log(r/s)+(1-r)\log((1-r)/(1-s))\).
At \(r=s\) this expression and its derivative in \(r\) vanish, and its second derivative is \(1/[r(1-r)]\ge4\).
It is thus at least \(2(r-s)^2\), also at boundary masses by limits.
The \(L^1\) distance is \(2(r-s)=\int|Z-1|\,d\eta_U+1-m\ge1/2\).
Consequently
\[
              \limsup_{p\to\infty}\Delta^{1/n}\le2e^{-1/8}
\]
along the stated prime sequence.
This inequality and the logarithmic tests constrain the determinant of a putative sequence; they permit determinant one and do not establish existence of integer inputs with unbounded matching lengths.
