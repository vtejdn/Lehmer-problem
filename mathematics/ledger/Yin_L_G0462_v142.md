# A finite list of rational moment functions for a fixed polynomial of absolute norm two

<a id="finite"></a>
## Reducing the fixed lower polynomial to finitely many phase exponents

Let \(B\in\mathbb Z[z]\) be irreducible of degree \(b\ge1\), with \(B(0)=2\), leading coefficient of modulus one and every root strictly outside the unit circle.
Suppose a sequence of Salem numbers tends to one whose trace minimal polynomials of degree \(k+1\) admit strict interlacers with reciprocal lift \(B(z)+z^{2k}B(1/z)\).
The following finite list depends only on \(B\).
Every subsequential limit of the positive trace-residue moments in the [adjacent-term calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#limit) has rational generating function
\[
              G=B\,E/F,\qquad P_0=(1-z^2)F/E,
\]
where \(P_0\) is the coefficientwise limit of the reciprocal Salem polynomials on that subsequence.

Indeed the residues still have mass two, and the bounded integer denominator series gives integer moments in every fixed degree.
The [integer Fourier–Stieltjes theorem](../audits/literature/San20/Yin_U001.md#fourier-stieltjes) gives \(G=A/F\) with squarefree cyclotomic denominator of constant one.
The coefficientwise identity \(A P_0=(1-z^2)BF\) holds with \(P_0\in\mathbb Z[[z]],\ P_0(0)=1\).
Reduction modulo two shows \(A\) has an odd coefficient, since the right side is nonzero modulo two.
Since \(A(0)=2\), it is primitive.
As in the [dyadic factor argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#phase), its unique primitive factor of even constant has a root of dyadic modulus less than one.
This root must annihilate \(B\), so the whole irreducible factor is \(B\) up to sign.
The remaining factors have constant of absolute value one and are zero-free in the open disk by the positive real part of \(G\).
Their reversals are cyclotomic by Kronecker's theorem.
Thus \(E,F\) are coprime products of cyclotomics normalized at zero, and both are squarefree since \(G\) and \(1/G\) have positive real part.

Write \(B^*=z^bB(1/z)\), \(r=-b-\deg E+\deg F\), and let \(\eta=\pm1\) be the sign under reversal of \(E/F\).
Every root of \(E\) or \(F\) must be a zero of \(H_r=B+\eta z^r B^*\), and every simple circle zero of \(H_r\) is torsion, by the [boundary sign argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v45.md#phase).
The endpoint bound \(\int d\mu/(x+2)\le 2|B(-1)|\) follows from the positive residues and \(|S(-2)|\ge1\).
Thus \(1+z\mid E\).
All these are necessary tests on the finite list.

To bound \(r\), put \(I=B^*/B\).
It is a finite Blaschke product up to a constant of modulus one, with \(b\) zeros in the disk.
Its circle phase has everywhere positive derivative, the sum of the Poisson kernels of those zeros.
For \(r>b\), \(z^r I\) has strictly increasing phase and winding \(r+b\).
Hence all roots of \(H_r\) are simple and torsion, although the two disjoint sets of coefficients make it primitive with leading coefficient of modulus two.
This is impossible.

For \(r=-s<0\), choose an integer \(K>b\) greater than the maximum of that phase derivative.
For \(s>K\), the phase of \(z^s/I\) increases strictly with winding \(s-b\).
The polynomial \(z^s B+\eta B^*\), made monic by its leading sign, therefore has exactly \(s-b\) circle zeros, all simple and torsion.
Its other \(2b\) roots form a monic integer factor \(J\) with no circle roots and with an exterior root, by signed reciprocity.
The Mahler measure of \(J\) is at most \(2\sum |[z^j]B|\) by the circle supremum bound for the full polynomial.
Its degree and this bound put computable bounds on every integer coefficient by the elementary symmetric functions.
This gives a finite enumerable list of possible \(J\).
For every candidate retain its exterior roots \(\gamma\) with \(B(\gamma)B^*(\gamma)\ne0\).
Each such root permits at most one real \(s\), by \(|\gamma|^s=|B^*(\gamma)/B(\gamma)|\).
An actual exterior root must be retained, since \(B\) and \(B^*\) have disjoint root sets.
A finite integer upper bound can also be computed with certified inequalities by repeatedly multiplying by \(|\gamma|>1\) until the right side is exceeded.
Together with \(K\) and \(r\le b\), this bounds every candidate index.
For each index and sign the nonzero Laurent polynomial \(H_r\) has only finitely many cyclotomic factors, which can be allocated to \(E,F\) or neither.
It cannot vanish identically because \(B\) and \(B^*\) have disjoint nonzero roots.
This proves the claimed finiteness without assuming uniformity in the lower polynomial.

## Four possible values for a torsion phase at any fixed separation

Take \(B=2+\delta z^h\) with \(\delta=\pm1\), \(h\ge1\).
It has the required properties by Eisenstein at two after its leading sign is normalized.
For a torsion root \(z\) of \(H_r\), put \(x=\delta z^h\) and \(y=-\eta\delta z^r\).
Then \(2+x=y(1+2x)\), or \(2(1-xy)=y-x\).
Every conjugate of the algebraic integer \(1-xy\) has modulus at most one.
If it vanishes, \(y=x\) and \(x=\pm1\).
Otherwise its norm is a nonzero integer, so every conjugate has modulus one.
Then \(|y-x|=2\) forces \(y=-x\), and substitution gives \(1+x+x^2=0\).
Consequently every root of \(E,F\) lies among the roots of
\[
                      (z^{2h}-1)(z^{2h}+\delta z^h+1).
\]
Thus \(E,F\) are products of coprime subsets of the distinct cyclotomic factors of this explicit polynomial and \(-5h\le r\le3h\).
The polynomial's roots are distinct since the two factors have disjoint root sets.
This gives a finite exact search for each fixed separation with both signs, including the necessary endpoint and boundary positivity tests.
Passing a rational moment test still requires the first varying integer coefficients and the actual target roots to satisfy strict interlacing.
