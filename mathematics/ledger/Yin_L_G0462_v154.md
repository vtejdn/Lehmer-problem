# A row resultant at several colliding power branches

2026-11-18T11:17.
An exploratory calculation for Yin compares the [two-group collision estimate](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#branches) with the ramification forced by several branches on a single disk.

<a id="disk"></a>
## A disk with separated scaled nodes

Let \(p\ne\ell\) be primes and \(K/\mathbb Q_\ell\) a finite extension containing the roots under consideration, with valuation ring \(\mathcal O\), uniformizer \(\pi\), and residue field \(k\).
Normalize \(v(\ell)=1\), so \(v(\pi)=1/q\) for the ramification index \(q\).
Let \(b\in\mathcal O^\times\), let \(\tau\in\mathcal O\) have depth \(\sigma=v(\tau)>0\), and take \(t\ge2\) units \(\alpha_i\) with
\[
               \beta_i=\alpha_i^p=b+\tau y_i,\qquad y_i\in\mathcal O.
\]
Require both the \(\bar y_i\) and the \(\bar\alpha_i\) to be pairwise distinct in \(k\).
In particular, the distances between distinct powered nodes all have valuation \(\sigma\).
The choice \(\tau=\ell^s\) gives \(\sigma=s\), while \(\tau=\pi^h\) gives \(\sigma=h/q\).

Suppose \(F=x^{r_0}U(x^p)+x^{r_1}V(x^p)\) has multiplicity at least \(e\ge2\) at each \(\alpha_i\), where \(r_0,r_1\) are distinct modulo \(p\) and the nonzero integral Laurent rows are coprime over \(\mathbb Q[X^{\pm1}]\).
Write \(U=X^u U_0,\ V=X^w V_0\) with \(U_0,V_0\) ordinary polynomials having nonzero constant terms, and set \(H=|\operatorname{Res}(U_0,V_0)|\).
This includes the conjugate roots of a unit minimal polynomial \(P\) for which \(P^e\mid F\).
Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 M_e(t)&=\left\lceil\frac{(e-1)t+2}{2}\right\rceil,\qquad
 N_e(t,\ell)=\max\{e,\min(\ell,M_e(t))\},\\
 v_\ell(H)&\ \ge\ \sigma\,N_e(t,\ell)^2 .                         \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
For \(\ell\ge M_e(t)\) the full bound is \(\sigma M_e(t)^2\).
In particular, for double roots the bound is \(\sigma\lceil(t+2)/2\rceil^2\) whenever \(\ell\ge\lceil(t+2)/2\rceil\).
Two double roots give \(4\sigma\).

<a id="jets"></a>
## The double and higher jets on the scaled disk

First divide out the common \(\ell\)-content of the rows.
For \(W=U_0,V_0\), let \(g_W\) be the minimum coefficient valuation of \(W(b+\tau Y)\), and set \(g=\min(g_{U_0},g_{V_0})\).
Divide both scaled polynomials by a scalar of valuation \(g\) and reduce to \(A,B\in k[Y]\), at least one nonzero.
The scalar exists in \(K\) because \(g\) is a coefficient valuation.
Each coefficient of \(Y^j\) in an integral \(W(b+\tau Y)\) has valuation at least \(j\sigma\).
Consequently \(n=\max(\deg A,\deg B)\le g/\sigma\), taking the degree of the zero polynomial as \(-\infty\).

For the \(i\)-th branch there is a series \(z_i(T)\in\mathcal O[[T]]\) with \(z_i(0)=\alpha_i\) and \(z_i(T)^p=\beta_i+T\).
Its coefficients are found recursively by division by the unit \(p\alpha_i^{p-1}\).
After division of \(F(z_i(T))\) by its first row's monomial, the coefficient of \(V_0(\beta_i+T)\) is the integral unit series
\[
             L_i(T)=z_i(T)^{r_1-r_0}(\beta_i+T)^{w-u}.
\]
The resulting sum vanishes to order at least \(e\) at \(T=0\).
Substitute \(T=\tau Z\) and divide both rows by the common disk scalar.
They remain integral at \(Y=y_i+Z\), while \(L_i(\tau Z)\) reduces to the constant \(\lambda_i=\bar\alpha_i^{\,r_1-r_0}\bar b^{\,w-u}\).
The ratios of distinct \(\bar\alpha_i\) are nontrivial \(p\)-th roots of unity, so the \(\lambda_i\) are distinct.
Reducing the first \(e\) Taylor coefficients now gives
\[
                     (Y-\bar y_i)^e\mid A+\lambda_i B .               \tag{2}
\]
If \(n<e\), two different \(\lambda_i\) would make \(A=B=0\).
Thus \(n\ge e\) in every characteristic.

Write \(A=J A_1,\ B=J B_1\), with the reduced polynomials coprime and \(h=\deg J\).
If the reduced pair is constant, including a zero row, (2) forces a zero of order at least \(e\) in \(J\) at all but at most one of the \(t\) nodes.
Then \(n\ge e(t-1)\ge M_e(t)\).
Otherwise write \(d_0=n-h\) and consider \(W=A_1'B_1-A_1B_1'\).
If \(W\ne0\), its degree is at most \(2d_0-2\) by cancellation of the two top terms when the row degrees agree.
Write \(h_i=\operatorname{ord}_{\bar y_i}J\).
Whenever \(h_i<e\), equation (2) makes \(A_1+\lambda_i B_1\) vanish there to order at least \(e-h_i\), while \(B_1(\bar y_i)\ne0\) by coprimality.
Differentiation gives order at least \(e-h_i-1\) in \(W\), including in positive characteristic.
As \(\sum_i h_i\le h\), we obtain
\[
              2d_0-2\ge\sum_i(e-1-h_i)_+\ge(e-1)t-h .
\]
Hence \(n\ge M_e(t)\).
If \(W=0\), the nonconstant rational function \(A_1/B_1\) belongs to \(k(Y^\ell)\), since differentiation has precisely that kernel on the basis \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{\ell-1}\) over \(k(Y^\ell)\).
Its degree is at least \(\ell\).
These cases give \(g\ge\sigma N_e(t,\ell)\).

## Converting disk valuations to the resultant

Use the [normalized linear-factor calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v152.md#resultant) in a finite extension splitting both rows, keeping \(v(\ell)=1\).
Homogenize each row to its own degree and factor it into forms \(L=u_L X+v_L Z\) whose minimum coefficient valuation is zero, times a scalar of valuation equal to the row content valuation.
This follows from the multiplicativity of the Gauss norm, or by reducing the product of primitive factors.
For a disk with center \(b_j\) and depth \(\sigma_j=v(\tau_j)>0\), assign each factor the weight
\[
                 w_j(L)=\min\{\sigma_j,v(L(b_j,1))\}.
\]
The Gauss valuation of \(L(b_j+\tau_j Y,1)\) equals this weight, because \(u_L\) is a unit whenever \(v(L(b_j,1))>0\).
Hence \(g_{W,j}=c_W+\sum_{L\mid W}w_j(L)\), with multiplicities and row content valuation \(c_W\).

Select any finite set of disks whose centers have pairwise distinct residues modulo \(\pi\).
A normalized factor has positive weight in at most one such disk.
For a factor \(L\) of \(U_0\) and a factor \(L'\) of \(V_0\), the determinant \(u_L L'(b_j,1)-u_{L'}L(b_j,1)\) has valuation at least
\(\min(w_j(L),w_j(L'))\ge w_j(L)w_j(L')/\sigma_j\).
Thus the pair's valuation covers the sum of these contributions over the selected disks.
Assume \(V_0\) is \(\ell\)-primitive, exchanging rows if needed, and let \(c=c_{U_0}\).
In particular \(\sum_j g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j\le\deg V_0\), because each normalized factor contributes at most one to the sum.
Multiplying all pair determinants and restoring the scalar in \(U_0\) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 v_\ell(H)
 &\ge c\deg V_0+\sum_j (g_{U_0,j}-c)g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j\\
 &\ge \sum_j g_{U_0,j}g_{V_0,j}/\sigma_j
 \ \ge\ \sum_j\sigma_j N_e(t_j,\ell)^2 .                              \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
Replacing a removed common content of valuation \(c_*\) adds \(c_*(\deg U_0+\deg V_0)\) to the resultant valuation.
Taking a single disk proves (1), including nonprimitive individual rows.

## Exact cost on the adjacent rows of a trinomial

Take an odd prime \(p\), a prime \(\ell\nmid p(p-1)\), and \(a=\ell^s\) with \(s\ge1\) and \(a\equiv1\bmod p\).
The [power-index example](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v365.md#index) gives an irreducible unit polynomial \(P=x^p-ax-1\) since its reduction modulo \(p\) has roots with Frobenius orbit length \(p\).
For every integral two-row multiple of \(P^2\) on the rows \(0,1\), with the coprimality above, we have the sharp bound
\[
                          v_\ell(H)\ge p^2s.                            \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(\beta_i=1+a\alpha_i\), so \(b=1,\ \tau=a,\ y_i=\alpha_i\).
The reductions of the \(\alpha_i\) are the \(p\) distinct roots of \(Y^p-1\) in the splitting residue field because \(\ell\ne p\).
Thus \(t=p\), with \(\lambda_i=\bar y_i\) in (2), including after removing base monomials.
A finite residue field extension suffices to contain the \(p\) nodes.
If both \(A,B\) had degree less than \(p\), then \(A+YB=c(Y^p-1)\) for some \(c\in k\).
By the second Taylor condition in (2), \(B(\bar y_i)=cp\bar y_i^{p-1}\) at all \(p\) nodes.
Hence \(B=cpY^{p-1}\) and \(A=c((1-p)Y^p-1)\).
The degree bound and \(\ell\nmid p-1\) imply \(c=0\), a contradiction.
It follows that \(g\ge ps\), and (3) gives (4).

The [exact doubling construction](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v364.md#doubling) applied to \(E=P=(-a)x-(1-X)\), \(X=x^p\), has rows
\[
          U_0=X(-a)^p+(p-1)(1-X)^p,\qquad
          V_0=pa(1-X)^{p-1}.
\]
At any root of \(P\) the two quantities \((-a)x\) and \(1-X\) are equal and nonzero, so the doubling identity gives exactly two copies there.
These rows have nonzero constant terms, \(\deg U_0=p\), and \(U_0(1)=(-a)^p\ne0\).
They are therefore coprime and their absolute resultant is \(p^p a^{p^2}\), attaining (4).
For every prime \(\ell>p\) one can take \(s=p-1\).
The condition \(p\mid\ell-1\) is needed only if all \(p\) nodes are required to lie in \(\mathbb F_\ell\) itself.

## Several levels of the same collision

Different disks may occupy different directions after rescaling a common parent disk while still having the same original residue.
In this situation a single normalized linear factor can contribute its depth down to the parent in every proposed child estimate.
Formula (3) adds estimates only across distinct original residues.
The remaining question is how to assign increments in valuation below each branching depth so that the parent and its children charge each factor pair only once, while keeping the critical-point information from the different scaled reductions.
