# Classification at the first positive coefficient and exact Pisot controls of opposite sign

<a id="mass"></a>
## The atomic mass at \(j=1\)

For the [fixed first-coefficient branch](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) \(j=1\), write the limiting Herglotz measure as \(\mu\) and \(\nu=2\mu\).
Its Fourier sequence \(A(n)=\widehat\nu(n)\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), is even and integer-valued, with \(A(0)=2\).
The cited result decomposes it into an integer periodic sequence and a finitely supported integer sequence.
The corresponding measures are, respectively, positive atomic on roots of unity and a nonnegative trigonometric-polynomial density against normalized arc length.
The periodic part is unique since a periodic sequence with finite support is zero.
Both parts are real and even by the same uniqueness.
Their masses, the respective values at zero, are nonnegative integers.
Write \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\) for the atomic mass of \(\nu\).

For \(t=0\), the [integer mean-two classification](../claims/Yin_C_G0403_v1.md#integer-mean-two-classification) gives density \(2\) or \(2+\eta(z^k+z^{-k})\), with \(k\ge1\) and \(\eta=\pm1\).
For \(t=1\), the density is identically one, since all its nonconstant Fourier coefficients have absolute value strictly below its mean and are integers.
The atomic part is uniform on the \(h\) solutions of \(z^h=\eta\) for some \(h\ge1\) and sign \(\eta\).
Indeed its Fourier coefficients have values \(0,\pm1\).
Equality of their absolute value to one means that the corresponding character is constant on the entire support.
Such indices form a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\) containing a common torsion order of the support, hence equal \(h\mathbb Z\).
At \(h\) the constant is a sign by symmetry, and Fourier uniqueness gives the asserted uniform measure.

## Two signed cycles, one signed cycle, or a bounded period after removing zero classes

Suppose \(t=2\), so \(A\) is entirely periodic.
For an integer \(p\ge1\) and sign \(\eta\), write \(\phi_{p,\eta}(n)=0\) unless \(p\mid n\), and \(\phi_{p,\eta}(n)=\eta^{n/p}\) otherwise.
There is an \(h\ge1\) such that \(A(n)=0\) for \(h\nmid n\), while \(A(hn)=B(n)\) for one of the following sequences.

1. \(B(n)=\phi_{p,\eta}(n)+\phi_{s,\tau}(n)\) for two lengths and two signs.
2. \(B(n)=2\phi_{p,\eta}(n)+\tau[\phi_{p,\eta}(n+k)+\phi_{p,\eta}(n-k)]\), where \(1\le k<p\) and \(\eta,\tau=\pm1\).
3. \(B\) is even, of period \(p\le60\), with \(B(0)=2\), coefficients in \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\) and nonnegative discrete Fourier transform on \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\).

These conditions describe exactly the periodic, even, positive definite integer sequences of mass two.
For sufficiency, the first two expressions are inner products of the signed coordinate orbits described below, and the third condition gives a positive atomic measure by Fourier inversion.
Inserting zeros outside \(h\mathbb Z\) preserves positivity by lifting the measure uniformly through \(z\mapsto z^h\).
This sufficiency concerns the harmonic conditions alone.

For necessity use the unitary shift \(U\) acting by multiplication by \(z\) on the finite support of \(\nu\), with \(v=1\).
The vectors \(U^n v\) have real Gram matrix \(A(n-k)\) and squared norm two in their real span.
They form a finite orbit and generate a positive definite integral lattice.
Indeed upon choosing a real basis from the orbit, every vector of its integer span has coordinates in the inverse of the integral basis Gram matrix applied to \(\mathbb Z^b\), where \(b\) is the rank.
The [finite root lattice proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0403_v1.md#finite-root-lattice-determinant-lemma) yields orthogonal components of types \(A,D,E\).
Every norm-two vector belongs to a single component, and the components are intrinsic as the connected classes of all roots under nonorthogonality.
The isometry \(U\) permutes them transitively because the orbit of \(v\) generates the lattice.
If their number is \(h\), then \(A(n)=0\) off \(h\mathbb Z\), and \(B(n)=\langle v,S^nv\rangle\) on the component of \(v\), where \(S=U^h\).

For type \(A_b\) use the standard simple roots \(e_i-e_{i+1}\) in \(\mathbb R^{b+1}\).
For type \(D_b\), \(b\ge5\), use the same differences up to \(e_{b-1}-e_b\) in \(\mathbb R^b\), with the additional root \(e_{b-1}+e_b\).
The simple Gram matrices identify these models with the preceding components.
All norm-two lattice vectors in the models have the form \(\pm e_i\pm e_k\) for distinct indices, since they have integer coordinates.
Each lattice isometry here extends to a signed coordinate permutation.
To see this, the reflections in the root hyperplanes act transitively on the chambers in the root span.
Indeed each such reflection preserves the lattice and the finite hyperplane arrangement, and reflection in a chamber wall reaches the adjacent chamber.
A generic path between interiors crossing one wall at a time proves transitivity.
After composing with these reflections, the isometry preserves a chosen chamber and so permutes its inward simple roots, which are its wall normals of squared norm two.
For the chamber of the chosen simple system this description follows from the positive-root basis proof cited above.
The reflections themselves are signed coordinate permutations.
The only possible nonidentity diagram symmetry of the \(A_b\) chain is implemented by reversing the coordinates and negating them.
For \(D_b\), \(b\ge5\), the symmetry of the two short arms is implemented by changing the sign of \(e_b\).

Thus \(v\) is supported on two coordinates under a signed permutation extending \(S\).
For a coordinate in a permutation cycle of length \(p\), its inner products with its shifts are \(\phi_{p,\eta}\), with \(\eta\) the product of the signs around the cycle.
If the two coordinates of \(v\) lie in different cycles, their cross terms vanish, giving case 1.
If they belong to one cycle, write \(v=\pm(e_i+\tau S^k e_i)\) with \(1\le k<p\), giving case 2.

The remaining components \(D_4,E_6,E_7,E_8\) all have rank at most eight.
In an integral lattice basis \(S\) is a matrix of finite order, so it is diagonalizable and its eigenvalue orders have distinct cyclotomic polynomials of total degree at most eight.
The possible orders in degrees \(1,2,4,6,8\) are respectively
\[
 \{1,2\},\quad\{3,4,6\},\quad\{5,8,10,12\},\quad
 \{7,9,14,18\},\quad\{15,16,20,24,30\}.
\]
This list follows from \(\varphi(p^a)=p^{a-1}(p-1)\) and multiplicativity for coprime factors.
With degree budget eight, a single degree-eight order is at most 30, a degree-six order and a remaining degree-two allowance have least common multiple at most 42, and two degree-four allowances give at most 60.
A single degree-four order together with orders of degrees one and two has least common multiple at most 60, and only degrees one and two give at most 12.
Hence \(S^{p}=1\) for some \(p\le60\), establishing case 3.

## Exact realization of purely atomic profiles at negative constant coefficient

More generally let \(K(z)=\int(1+\overline uz)/(1-\overline uz)\,d\mu(u)\), with conjugation-symmetric, finite, positive atomic \(\mu\) supported on roots of unity.
Assume \(K\) has integer Taylor coefficients, with \(K(0)=j\ge1\).
Such a \(K\) is the exact [normalized rational function](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#sign) of negative-constant Pisot minimal polynomials for every sufficiently large integer \(q\) if \(-1\) is outside the support.
If \(-1\) belongs to the support, exact equality to this function is impossible for a negative-constant Pisot minimal polynomial.

Write \(x=z+z^{-1}\).
Pair conjugate atoms, including real atoms once, to get
\[
  \frac{zK(z)}{1-z^2}=R(x)=\sum_i\frac{m_i}{x-b_i},\qquad
  b_i=u_i+\overline u_i,\qquad m_i>0,\qquad \sum_i m_i=j .
\]
Taylor coefficients of positive index are periodic since the support has finite torsion order.
Their integrality makes \(K\in\mathbb Q(z)\), and the expression for \(R\) is invariant under \(z\mapsto1/z\), whose fixed field in \(\mathbb Q(z)\) is \(\mathbb Q(x)\).
The distinct poles \(b_i\) are algebraic integers and their set is stable under conjugacy, so \(B(x)=\prod_i(x-b_i)\) is monic integral.
Writing \(R=C/B\), one has \(C\in\mathbb Z[x]\) of degree \(s-1\), where \(s=\deg B\).
Indeed \(zK/(1-z^2)\) has integral series at zero, and \(x^{-1}=z/(1+z^2)=z+O(z^3)\) has an integral compositional inverse.
Expansion in \(x^{-1}\) and multiplication by \(B\) thus give integral coefficients for the polynomial \(C\).

Set \(V=z^s B(x)\) and define
\[
               h_q=(z-q)V-z^s C(x).
\]
This is monic integral of degree \(2s+1\), with constant \(-q\), and \(h_q^*-zh_q=(1-z^2)V\).
The formula
\[
 \frac{-h_q}{h_q^*-zh_q}
     =\frac{q-z}{1-z^2}+\frac{zK}{(1-z^2)^2}
\]
proves the required exact normalization.
No zero of \(V\) is a zero of \(h_q\), since \(C(b_i)\ne0\).
At other nonzero points the root equation is \(z-q=R(z+1/z)\).
When \(z\) is nonreal and \(|z|\ge1\), the imaginary part of \(x\) has the sign of \(\Im z\) in the exterior and vanishes on the circle.
The imaginary part of \(R(x)\) is \(-\Im x\sum_i m_i/|x-b_i|^2\), excluding equality in either case.

For real \(|z|>1\) the function \(z-q-R(x)\) is strictly increasing on each exterior ray, because \(dx/dz>0\) and \(R'(x)<0\).
On the positive ray it is negative at \(q\) and tends to infinity, giving precisely one exterior root \(\theta>q\).
At \(z=1\) the polynomial does not vanish, either by \(C(2)\ne0\) at a trace pole or by \(1-q-R(2)<0\) in its absence.
If \(-2\) is not a pole, then \(-1-q-R(-2)<0\) for all sufficiently large \(q\), excluding the negative exterior ray and \(-1\).
Therefore every remaining root is strictly inside the disk, and the nonzero constant implies irreducibility by the integer constant of any other factor.
At its exterior root the equation \(\theta-q=R(\theta+1/\theta)\) gives \(\theta-q=j/q+O_K(q^{-2})\).
The associate \((1-z^2)V\) is entirely cyclotomic.

If \(-2\) is a trace pole, on the negative exterior ray \(z-q-R(x)\) tends from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\) as \(z\) increases to \(-1\), giving another exterior root.
This also excludes any other negative-constant Pisot polynomial with the same exact \(K\): the displayed rational identity for its normalized quotient forces its numerator to vanish at this negative real solution.
These realizations and exclusions concern exact negative-sign functions.
For positive constants the [interior pole](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#periodic) is present at every finite \(q\); the classifications above constrain its coefficient limits.
