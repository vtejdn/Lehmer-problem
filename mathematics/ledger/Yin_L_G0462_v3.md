# Distinct strict-shell measures in a single ternary box, with and without cyclotomic padding

The [strict-shell question](../claims/Yin_C_G0870_v1.md#statement) asks for a positive probability in a discrete integer model after measure one is excluded.
The [finite reciprocal-box control](../computations/programs/Yin_J_G0144/readme.md) has two sign variants of one measure below \(6/5\) in degree ten.
This 2026-11-08 comparison separates three quantities: the number of inputs, the number of irreducible inputs, and the number of different total measures that those inputs realize.
Write \(\mathcal R_D\) for the uniform monic reciprocal integer box of even degree \(D\), with constant coefficient one and coefficient height at most one.
It has \(3^{D/2}\) atoms.
Throughout, \(M\) is the multiplicative Mahler measure of the whole polynomial and \(\log\) is natural.

<a id="different-measures-one-degree"></a>
## Many different measures at one degree, each carried by an irreducible polynomial

Let \(\theta\) be the positive real root of \(x^3-x-1\).
There is a constant \(c>0\) and a sequence of even degrees \(D\to\infty\) for which \(\mathcal R_D\) contains at least
\[
   \exp\!\left(c\frac{\log D}{\log\log D}\right)
\]
irreducible polynomials of pairwise different measures in the strict shell \(1<M<\theta\).
For every fixed \(\delta>0\), all the constructed measures eventually belong to \((\theta-\delta,\theta)\).
In the uniform box the corresponding irreducible-event probability is at least this count times \(3^{-D/2}\).
There is no cyclotomic padding in any of these polynomials.
The degrees are aligned by taking roots of different algebraic units; each such substitution preserves its own base measure exactly.

<a id="minus-delay-progression"></a>
### The irreducible base progression

Put \(f=x^3-x-1\), \(f^*=1-x^2-x^3\), and for \(N\ge8\) write
\[
    F_N=x^Nf-f^*,\qquad Q_N=F_N/(x-1).
\]
This is the classical three-arm family \(P_{N+1}\) in [C578](../claims/Yin_C_G0578_v1.md#plastic-tree-all-root-cost), with its [McM02 attribution](../audits/literature/McM02/readme.md#three-arm-tree-attribution-20261010).
That argument supplies exactly one simple expanding positive root \(\lambda_N=M(Q_N)\), its contracting reciprocal and all other roots on the circle.
It also supplies a unique positive-measure irreducible factor of multiplicity one, all other factors cyclotomic, and \(\lambda_8<\lambda_9<\cdots\uparrow\theta\), where \(Q_8\) is the irreducible degree-ten Lehmer polynomial.
The coefficients of \(Q_N\), in increasing degree, are \(1,1,0\), then \(-1\) from degrees \(3\) through \(N-1\), then \(0,1,1\) from \(N\) through \(N+2\), by multiplication by \(x-1\).
In particular there is no height loss.

For every \(N\equiv8\bmod 360\), \(N\ge8\), the whole \(Q_N\) is irreducible.
To see this, transfer the support argument of [C270](../claims/Yin_C_G0270_v1.md#rank-two-cyclotomic-bound), which is stated for the plus sign, to our minus sign as follows.
If \(\zeta\) has order \(b\) and \(F_N(\zeta)=0\), partition the six signed nonzero terms into minimal vanishing blocks.
The formal coordinates \((i,j)\) encode \(\zeta^{iN+j}\); the two columns have second coordinates \(\{0,1,3\}\) for \(i=1\) and \(\{0,2,3\}\) for \(i=0\).
If the largest block size is \(\ell\), the [rational minimal-relation bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0262_v2.md#minimal-vanishing-order) gives \(b\mid P_\ell(v_1N+v_2)\) for every within-block difference \(v\), where \(P_\ell=\prod_{p\le\ell}p\) over primes.
The bound permits arbitrary rational signs, and linearity extends the divisibility to the lattice \(\Lambda\) generated by the differences.
Its rank is two: a vertical rank-one direction would require both three-term columns to vanish separately, but \(f\) has no unit-circle root.
In a nonvertical rank-one direction, blocks must be pairs matching the two columns by one common translate, impossible since the sums of the column heights are 4 and 5 and their difference is not divisible by 3.
There are distinct formal points in each block, excluding rank zero.
Two independent generating differences have nonzero determinant of magnitude at most six, so \(h=[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda]\le6\) and \((0,h)\in\Lambda\).
If there is one block then \((1,0),(0,1)\in\Lambda\), giving \(b\mid30\).
Otherwise \(\ell\le4\), giving \(b\mid6h\) with \(1\le h\le6\).
Thus \(b\mid360\) in both cases.
On all these roots of unity \(F_N(\zeta)=F_8(\zeta)\) when \(N\equiv8\bmod360\).
Since \(F_8=(x-1)Q_8\), only \(\zeta=1\) survives and \(F_N'(1)=7-N\ne0\).
Consequently \(Q_N\) has no cyclotomic factor, which gives the asserted irreducibility by C578.
No irreducibility classification in the other residue classes is used.

<a id="same-degree-radicals"></a>
### Aligning the degrees without introducing neutral or positive factors

Choose \(k\) large and \(s=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\).
There are \(k\) distinct primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) in \((k^4,k^5]\) with one common invertible residue \(r\bmod36\).
Indeed \(\binom{2n}{n}\ge4^n/(2n+1)\), and the exponent of a prime \(p\) in the binomial coefficient is at most \(\lfloor\log(2n)/\log p\rfloor\), since each floor difference at a prime power is zero or one.
It follows that \(\pi(2n)\log(2n)\ge n\log4-\log(2n+1)\).
Taking \(n=\lfloor k^5/2\rfloor\) and removing at most \(k^4\) smaller primes leaves at least \(12k\) for large \(k\), enough for the twelve invertible classes.
Choose \(1\le a\le36\) with \(ar^{k-s}\equiv1\bmod36\), and set
\[
 D=10a\prod_{i=1}^k p_i,\qquad
 h_S=\prod_{i\in S}p_i,\qquad d_S=D/h_S
       \quad\bigl(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\},\ |S|=s\bigr).
\]
Each \(d=d_S\) is \(10\bmod360\), so \(Q_{d-2}\) is irreducible of degree \(d\).
Distinct subsets give distinct \(d\), and \(\min_S d_S\to\infty\).

Write \(\beta=\lambda_{d-2}\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\beta)\subset\mathbb R\) in its positive real embedding.
If \(\beta=u^p\) with \(u\in K\) and \(p\mid h_S\), then \(u\) is an algebraic unit and \(\mathbb Q(u)=K\).
Over these same \(d\) embeddings its total logarithmic measure is \((\log\beta)/p<(\log\theta)/k^4\).
But [Voutier's bound, with its inspected statement and normalization](../audits/literature/JT26/Yin_U001.md#external-inputs), gives
\[
  d\,h(u)>\frac14\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^3
          \ge\frac{1}{4(\log D)^3}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\), uniformly in \(S\), since \(u\) is nontorsion and then \(\log\log d\ge1\).
Here \(h(u)\) denotes absolute logarithmic height, so \(d\,h(u)\) is precisely the total logarithmic measure just computed.
Since \(\log D\le\log360+5k\log k\), this contradicts the supposed \(p\)-th power for all sufficiently large \(k\).
This uses Voutier's accepted theorem statement, without a new audit of that proof; the degree-ten prime-substitution comparison in [C376 v2](../claims/Yin_C_G0376_v2.md#statement) alone does not give this moving-degree conclusion.

Set \(h=h_S\) and \(v=\beta^{1/h}>0\).
If \(x^h-\beta\) has a monic proper nonconstant factor over \(K\) of degree \(j\), the product of its roots is \(v^j\) times a root of unity and lies in \(K\subset\mathbb R\).
The multiplier must be \(1\) or \(-1\), so \(v^j\in K\).
Together with \(v^h\in K\), Bézout gives \(v^{\gcd(j,h)}\in K\), making \(\beta\) a \(p\)-th power there for any prime divisor \(p\) of the integer \(h/\gcd(j,h)>1\).
Thus the binomial is irreducible.
Since \(\beta=v^h\), the field tower gives \([\mathbb Q(v):\mathbb Q]=hd=D\), proving irreducibility of \(Q_{d-2}(x^h)\).
The substitution preserves height, reciprocity and constant one; the \(h\) roots above each conjugate \(\gamma\) have common modulus \(|\gamma|^{1/h}\), so the whole measure stays \(\lambda_{d-2}\).
Strict monotonicity in the distinct \(d\)'s gives \(\binom{k}{s}\ge2^k/(k+1)\) distinct measures, all strictly between one and \(\theta\).
Finally \(4k\log k\le\log D\le\log360+5k\log k\), so this count is at least the claimed exponential for a fixed \(c>0\) and large \(k\).
Convergence of \(\lambda_N\) and the growth of the minimum \(d_S\) give the stated concentration just below \(\theta\).

<a id="fixed-degree-benchmarks"></a>
## What a strict probability lower bound can count

There is a simpler control valid at **every** even \(D=2t\ge22\): the same box contains at least \(t-9\) distinct measure values in \((1,\theta)\) if reducible inputs are allowed.
For \(8\le N\le t-3\), use \(Q_N(x)(1+x^{D-N-2})\).
The shift exceeds \(\deg Q_N=N+2\), so the two coefficient blocks are disjoint, their heights are one, and the binomial has measure one.
Add \(Q_{D-2}\), with its own distinct measure, to the \(t-10\) shifted inputs.
This count allows cyclotomic factors and does not assert irreducibility in every even degree.

At the lower threshold \(6/5\) the above plastic family itself supplies **only** \(Q_8\)'s measure.
Indeed, for \(1<b<\theta\) the unique expanding simple root gives \(\lambda_N<b\) exactly when \(F_N(b)>0\), equivalently \(b^N<f^*(b)/f(b)\), since \(f(b)<0\).
At \(b=6/5\) this quotient is \(271/59\) and \((6/5)^8<271/59<(6/5)^9\), by integer cross multiplication.
This threshold statement concerns that family, without classifying other irreducible inputs.
The [Lehmer prime radicals](../claims/Yin_C_G0376_v2.md#statement) at prime exponents greater than forty already give irreducible ternary examples with that same measure at unbounded degrees.

For a precise count of **single-seed padding**, fix \(P=Q_8\), \(d=10\), and consider all products
\[
   P(x)\prod_{j=1}^l(1+x^{e_j}),\qquad
   e_j>b_{j-1},\quad b_0=d,\quad b_j=b_{j-1}+e_j,\quad b_l=D .
\]
Every resulting input is reciprocal of height one and total measure \(\lambda_8\in(1,6/5)\).
Let \(A_D\) be the number of these products; for large even \(D\),
\[
       \log A_D\sim\frac{(\log D)^2}{2\log2}.
\]
For the lower bound choose the largest \(k\ge0\) with \(2^{k+1}(d+k)<D\).
At step \(j\le k\) choose \(e_j=b_{j-1}+u_j\), independently \(1\le u_j\le2^j\).
Then \(b_j=2b_{j-1}+u_j\le2^j(d+j)\); the last exponent \(D-b_k>b_k\) gives exactly the required degree.
After cancelling \(P\), the least positive exponent determines the first binomial, which can be divided out to recover the next one, so distinct choices give distinct products.
There are \(2^{k(k+1)/2}\) choices and \(k=(\log D)/\log2+O(\log\log D)\) by maximality.
For the upper bound every length \(l\) is \(O(\log D)\) and \(u_j=e_j-b_{j-1}\ge1\) satisfies \(u_j\le D/2^{l-j}\) by the same recurrence.
Thus the logarithm of the number of choices at length \(l\) is at most \(l\log D-l(l-1)\log2/2\), whose real maximum is \((\log D)^2/(2\log2)+O(\log D)\).
Summing over the possible lengths proves the asymptotic.
This gives the actual uniform-box lower bound \(A_D\,3^{-D/2}\) in \((1,6/5)\) while its entire numerator consists of a single noncyclotomic core.
The upper estimate is only for these separated positive binomials; it says nothing about all cyclotomic multipliers or the full shell count.

The fixed-degree irreducible construction above removes this single-core explanation at \(\theta\), but its distinct values approach that positive-distance threshold from below.
No threshold below \(\lambda_8\), sequence of positive measures tending to one, or uniform Mahler gap follows.
The remaining probabilistic target requires a new source of core measures in shrinking shells, rather than a greater multiplicity at the present thresholds.
