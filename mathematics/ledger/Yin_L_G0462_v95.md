# A positive Pisot excess for the stable eight-term lift at the sum of two offsets

<a id="sum-offset"></a>
## The remaining polynomial on two circles

On branch two there are absolute constants \(c>0\) and \(Q<\infty\) such that any actual eight-term trace lift with lower polynomial \(2+a z^d+b z^e+ab z^{d+e}\), \(0<d<e\) and \(a,b\in\{-1,1\}\), satisfies \(\theta-q\ge c\) and \(q\le Q\).
Suppose instead that \(q\to\infty\), equivalently \(\theta-q\to0\) by the [branch derivative identity](Yin_L_G0462_v36.md#second).
The [three-offset theorem](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#three-offsets) gives \(d+e=o(h)\), and its [rank calculation](Yin_L_G0462_v93.md#eight) gives \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\).
The normalized lower polynomial of this class is
\[
 S_i(z)=S(z^d,z^e),\qquad 2S(x,y)=A(x,y)=2+a x+b y+ab xy,\qquad a,b\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Pass to fixed signs.
In the [classification for the sum of two offsets](Yin_L_G0462_v94.md) this is the strictly stable case.
Combining the results leaves only the reciprocal coefficient shape with opposite linear signs as a candidate for vanishing excess under this exponent relation.
Through degree \(h\) the normalized lift equals this prefix.
The whole lift has bounded circle supremum.
As a polynomial in \(y\), \(A\) has coefficients \(2+a x\) and \(b(1+a x)\).
For \(|x|\le1\),
\[
             |2+a x|^2-|1+a x|^2=3+2\Re(a x)\ge1 .
\]
Thus \(A\) has no zero on the closed bidisk and \(1/S\) is analytic on a larger closed bidisk.
Its Taylor coefficients are absolutely summable there on the unit bidisk by the Cauchy estimate at a slightly larger common radius, giving a uniform absolute coefficient sum for every specialization \(1/S_i\).
Also \(\inf_{i,|z|=1}|S_i(z)|>0\) by compactness.

## Mixed rows beyond both offsets

Use the actual integer denominator \(B^\sharp=(\Pi/2)J_i\) and the symmetric signed inverse probabilities \(\omega_i\) of [the two-offset calculation](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#reduction).
Their negative masses tend to zero.
The positive coefficients \(j_l\) of \(J_i\) are the doubled moments of \(\omega_i\) and \(j_0=1\).
Extract a fixed marginal limit for \((\bar v^d,\bar v^e)\) and an order on its frequency lattice as in [the two-circle proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#rows).
Here \(e/\gcd(d,e)\to\infty\) makes every fixed nonzero lattice vector eventually nonzero after specialization.
Testing at the base frequencies gives integrality of \((A/2)J\), whose coefficient at \(x\) or \(y\) excludes a Haar base marginal.
At every intermediate scale \(e\ll t\ll h\), testing at \(kt+r d+s e\) for \(k>0\) gives integer transforms for the projections of \(A u^k\Omega\), with \(u=v^t\).
The zero layer identity \(A^*((A/2)J)+A((A/2)J)^*\), followed by multiplication by \(A^*\) on the positive layers and conjugation on negative layers, gives the integer transform for the positive measure \(|A|^2\Omega\).

In the resulting [coset and slope decomposition](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#extinction), every graph slope density \(f_s\ge0\) gives an integer Laurent polynomial \(H=A f_s\).
The two coefficients of \(A\) in \(y\) are coprime in \(\mathbb Z[x^{\pm1}]\), so \(A\) is primitive and irreducible in the integer Laurent ring by degree one in \(y\) and Gauss's lemma.
Its reversal cannot be an associate, because the unique coefficient of modulus two would have to be at the same corner after reflection of the square support and translation, whereas reflection interchanges opposite corners.
The identity \(HA^*=H^*A\) forces \(A\mid H\) and \(f_s\) is integral Laurent.
If nonzero, its positive integer mean must be one, and then every nonconstant Fourier coefficient has modulus strictly less than one and is integral.
That would give the excluded Haar marginal.
The last full-torus harmonic would have an integer density with measure at most \(M(A)M(f)/2<1\), where \(f\) is the absolutely continuous density of the base marginal and \(M(A)=2\) by bidisk stability and the constant term.
This is the same last-harmonic positivity and Jensen argument as in [the two-circle proof](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v20.md#rows).
Thus every positive projected row is singular.
There are no zeros of \(A\) on the torus, so all its support lies in the fixed finite union of proper torsion translates supplied by the marginal decomposition.
The [annihilating characters](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#singular) give a finite product \(U_i\) of cyclotomic binomials with shifts \(O(e)\), nonzero after specialization.
For any further binomial \(V_i=1-z^{p_i}\) with \(p_i=6d\) or \(2(e-d)\), extraction gives an interval \(Le\le l\le h/L\) on which \(U_iV_i B^\sharp\) vanishes.
Indeed its shifts are fixed lattice vectors in the two offsets, and hence test the same singular rows.

## Local estimates for comparable offsets

We need bounded absolute coefficient sums of \(V_iB^\sharp\) through \(C e\) for every fixed \(C\), and of \(J_i\) through \(p_i\).
When \(e/d\) is bounded, take \(p_i=2(e-d)\).
The [first-coefficient argument](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#mixed) still gives the same alternatives: its tests at \(d+l u\), where \(u=o(d)\) is the first nonzero positive coefficient index and \(l\) is fixed, never see \(d+e\).
The first two coefficients are signed halves.
The [interval Bessel proof](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) permits the extra offset \(d+e\) and its reflection by splitting each interval also at these offsets and their translates.
Each piece has the same Gram bound and a partial multiplier bounded by the sum of the eight coefficient magnitudes.
It follows that \(V_i B^\sharp\) has bounded absolute sum through \(Ce\).
When \((e-d)/d\) stays bounded below the unfiltered \(B^\sharp\) has the same local bound, giving the bound for \(J_i\) by the summability of \(1/S_i\).
When \(e-d=o(d)\), one has \(p_i<d\), and \(J_i=B^\sharp\) through \(p_i\).
In the small first-index alternatives the prefix models \(1\pm z^u\) or \(1/(1\pm z^u)\) have \(e-d=u\) or \(2u\), so at most five coefficients contribute there.
If there is no first nonzero index at \(o(d)\), the constant alone contributes eventually after extraction.

## A sixth-root filter when the second offset is much larger

Suppose \(d/e\to0\) and take \(p_i=6d\), \(V_i=1-z^{p_i}\).
Below \(e\) the only nonconstant term in the normalized numerator is \(a z^d/2\).
The [one-offset short estimates](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#scales) give bounded absolute sums for \(B^\sharp\), and hence \(J_i=B^\sharp/(1+a z^d/2)\), through any fixed multiple of \(d\).
The same proof gives \(j_l=0\) for \(1\le l\le\kappa d\) for some \(\kappa>0\) along the sequence when \(d\to\infty\), by integrality below \(d\) and the fractional test at \(d+o(d)\).
For bounded \(d\) the short absolute sums hold directly.
For \(d\ll t\ll e\), all tests of the [one-offset mixed-row argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#far-rows) are below \(e\).
The projected mixed row is supported on the sixth-root atoms of the first marginal, so \([z^t] V_i B^\sharp\) vanishes eventually on every such scale.
By diagonal extraction it vanishes throughout \(L_1 d\le t\le e/L_1\) for a fixed \(L_1\).
The short estimates and the summable inverse of \(1+a z^d/2\) now give a uniform absolute coefficient sum for \(V_iJ_i\) through \(e/L_1\).

Write \(m_l=2\int v^l\,d\omega_i\) for all integers \(l\), so \(m_{-l}=m_l=j_l\) for \(l>0\) and \(m_0=2\).
The sequence \(k_l=m_l-m_{l-p_i}\) represents the two-sided moments after one difference.
At nonnegative indices it agrees with the coefficients of \(V_iJ_i\) except for an absolute sum \(O(1+\sum_{l\le p_i}|j_l|)=O(1)\).
For negative indices \(k_{-l}=-k_{l+p_i}\) by symmetry.
It follows that \(2m_l-m_{l-p_i}-m_{l+p_i}=k_l-k_{l+p_i}\) has bounded absolute sum over \(|l|\le e/(2L_1)\), using \(p_i=o(e)\).
These are the doubled signed moments of \(|1-v^{-p_i}|^2\omega_i\).
The Gram matrices of the characters weighted by \(1-v^{-p_i}\) therefore have bounded operator norm on every block of this diameter, by the sum of the absolute diagonals.

To bound \(V_iB^\sharp\) through \(Ce\), split at all lift offsets \(s\) and their shifts \(s+p_i\), leaving a bounded number of pieces and exceptional endpoints.
On a piece outside the transition windows \([s,s+p_i]\), both terms from each active offset occur, giving a signed moment of the weighted character against a fixed bounded partial multiplier from \(\Pi/2\).
The weighted Gram bound on blocks of diameter \(e/(2L_1)\) and the [fixed-size Bessel argument](Yin_L_G0462_v72.md#all-intervals) bound the number of nonzero integer coefficients there.
Indeed on any fixed \(K\) characters the positive-part Gram correction has norm \(O(K\|\omega_i^-\|)=o(1)\), and replacing each signed integral by its positive-part integral costs \(o(1)\).
On a transition piece the diameter is at most \(p_i=6d\).
For \(d\to\infty\), subdivide it into boundedly many blocks of diameter at most \(\kappa d\).
The unweighted signed Gram is the identity there by the short zero prefix, and the same Bessel test applies with the fixed bounded multiplier collecting the two possibly different partial sums.
For bounded \(d\), each transition contains boundedly many integers.
The endpoints and all coefficient magnitudes are bounded by the moment bound.
This proves the required local absolute sums even when the first marginal has surviving atoms.

## The return to the original denominator

In both scale regimes, \(V_iJ_i=(V_iB^\sharp)/S_i\) has bounded absolute coefficient sum through \(Ce\) for fixed \(C\).
In the sequence \(m_l-m_{l-p_i}\), correcting the nonnegative indices through \(p_i\) costs a bounded total by the already proved bound for \(J_i\).
The remaining negative indices \(-l\) with \(l>0\) have coefficient \(j_l-j_{l+p_i}=-[z^{l+p_i}]V_iJ_i\).
Consequently every shift by \(O(e)\) in \(U_i\) costs a bounded correction to represent the positive coefficients of \(U_i V_iJ_i\) by a measure of bounded variation.
Each singleton correction is a monomial density as in the [moving-shift comparison](Yin_L_G0462_v87.md#return).
Truncating \(U_iV_i B^\sharp\) through \(\lfloor h/L\rfloor\) gives \(Q_0\) of degree \(O(e)\) and bounded absolute coefficient sum.
The series \(Q_0/S_i\) has uniformly bounded absolute coefficient sum and agrees with \(U_iV_iJ_i\) through that cutoff.
The [two-cutoff argument](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v12.md#cutoff) applied to
\[
 S_i U_i V_i B^\sharp-Q_0\Pi/2
       =S_i(\Pi/2)(U_iV_iJ_i-Q_0/S_i)
\]
bounds the circle \(L^1\) norm of \(S_i U_i V_i B^\sharp\).
The uniform positive minimum of \(|S_i|\) bounds the \(L^1\) norm of the integer polynomial \(U_i V_i B^\sharp\).
It has [bounded support](Yin_L_G0462_v50.md#window), and all additional factors are cyclotomic up to sign, so its measure is the actual denominator measure tending to one strictly from above.
The [fixed-support gap](../audits/literature/DS17/Yin_U001.md#ds17-u001) excludes this final case.
