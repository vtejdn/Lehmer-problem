# Cyclotomic resultants and a discriminant budget for prefixes of Newton sums

<a id="target"></a>
## The smaller factor at 223

At least one of the two factors in the [remaining prime-223 case](Yin_L_G0462_v264.md#frontier) has multiplicative Mahler measure at most
\[
       \big((223\cdot114+1)\,1091659^2\big)^{1/446}<1089/1000=:T.
\]
Indeed the improved derivative norm bounds \(223(m(P_1)+m(P_2))\) by the logarithm of the base in parentheses.
The strict rational comparison follows on raising to power 446.
The relevant degrees are \(d=56,58\).
The following conditions discard entire Newton prefixes before one reconstructs their remaining coefficients.

<a id="linear"></a>
## A finite linear inequality for every cyclotomic test

Let \(P\) be any monic reciprocal irreducible noncyclotomic unit minimum of degree \(d\) with \(M(P)\le T\), where \(T>1\).
Write \(s_k=\sum_\alpha\alpha^k\) over its conjugates.
For \(m\ge1\), let \(c_m(k)=\sum_{\zeta\text{ primitive of order }m}\zeta^k\), a real integer, and \(\phi=\varphi(m)\).
Choose rational \(0<r<1\) with \(rT^2\le1\).
For each \(N\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N\frac{r^k}{k}c_m(k)s_k
 \le \phi\left\{\frac d2\log(1/r)+
 \frac1{N+1}\left[
 \frac{(d-2)r^{N+1}}{1-r}
 +\frac{(rT)^{N+1}}{1-rT}
 +\frac{(r/T)^{N+1}}{1-r/T}\right]\right\}.                       \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(m=1,2\) test ordinary and alternating signed sums of the same prefix.

For \(|z|\le T\), the identity
\(|1-rz|^2-r|1-z|^2=(1-r)(1-r|z|^2)\ge0\) applies.
Each \(\alpha\) has modulus at most \(T\).
Multiply the inequalities over all roots and primitive \(\zeta\) at \(z=\zeta\alpha\).
The product of \(|1-\zeta\alpha|\) is the absolute value of the nonzero integer resultant of \(P\) and \(\Phi_m\).
Taking negative logarithms bounds the corresponding sum for \(1-r\zeta\alpha\) by \(\phi d\log(1/r)/2\).
Expanding the logarithms gives the series on the left of (1), continued to infinity, since \(rT<1\).
Reciprocal pairing gives \(|s_k|\le d-2+T^k+T^{-k}\).
Indeed \(x\mapsto 2\cosh(kx)-2\) is superadditive on the nonnegative half-line since its derivative increases from zero, and the exterior logarithmic moduli sum to at most \(\log T\).
Summing the three geometric tail bounds using \(|c_m(k)|\le\phi\) proves (1).

For example take \(r=21/25,\ T=1089/1000\), where \(rT^2<1\).
At either \(d=56\) or \(58\), (1) excludes the prefix \(s_1=\cdots=s_{10}=6\).
The alternating version excludes its image under \(X\mapsto-X\).
These prefixes satisfy the integral Newton congruences, since the initial coefficients of \((1-X)^6\) give the constant power sums 6.
They cannot be excluded just by requiring each of the ten power sums to have absolute value at most its own bound when all those bounds are at least 6.
For a rational check, \(\log(25/21)<7/40\) by the odd power series for \(\log((1+u)/(1-u))\) at \(u=2/23\), bounding the terms from degree three by \(2u^3/[3(1-u^2)]\).
Substituting \(d=58\) and this upper bound into (1) leaves a strict violation exceeding \(2/5\).

## A quadratic inequality from the polynomial discriminant

Independently choose a rational Abel parameter \(0<q<1\), with no condition involving \(T\).
For any \(N\ge1\) the same prefixes satisfy
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^N\frac{q^k}{k}
       \big(\max\{0,|s_k|-(T^k-T^{-k})\}\big)^2
 \le d\log\frac1{1-q}+\frac{d(d-1)}2\log(1/q)
       +2(d-1)\log T-\log|\operatorname{disc}P|.                      \tag{2}
\]
For its proof put \(b_\alpha=\alpha/\max(1,|\alpha|)^2\), and \(u_k=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha^k\).
Only the exterior roots move.
By superadditivity of \(2\sinh(kx)\) on \(x\ge0\), \(|s_k-u_k|\le T^k-T^{-k}\).
The absolutely convergent logarithmic series gives
\[
   \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{q^k}{k}|u_k|^2
       =-\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}\log|1-q b_\alpha\overline{b_\gamma}|.
\]
Every diagonal factor is at least \(1-q\).
For distinct roots use
\[
 |1-q b_\alpha\overline{b_\gamma}|\ge\sqrt q\, |1-b_\alpha\overline{b_\gamma}|
 \ge \sqrt q\,\frac{|\alpha-\gamma|}
                    {\max(1,|\alpha|)\max(1,|\gamma|)}.
\]
The first inequality follows from the same identity as in (1).
For the second, if both original roots are on the same side of the unit circle, their normalized distance on the right is \(|b_\alpha-b_\gamma|\), which is at most \(|1-b_\alpha\overline{b_\gamma}|\) inside the unit disk.
If the two roots are on opposite sides, the second inequality is equality.
Multiplication over ordered distinct pairs produces the absolute discriminant with denominator \(M(P)^{2(d-1)}\).
Dropping the positive series tail yields (2).

For the two degrees choose \(q=(d-1)/(d+1)\).
The [signature-dependent Minkowski bound](../claims/Yin_C_G0345_v383.md#subfields) gives
\(|\operatorname{disc}P|\ge(157/200)^d(d^d/d!)^2\).
Here the field discriminant divides the order discriminant, the number of complex places is at most \(d/2\), and \(\pi/4>157/200\).
The right side of (2) is then strictly below 160 at degree 56 and 167 at degree 58.
A larger available lower bound on the discriminant may replace the Minkowski estimate.
The following exact comparisons check the numerical bounds and the two signs of the excluded prefix by the common \(m=1\) calculation before sign substitution.

~~~python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from math import factorial

t, r = F(1089, 1000), F(21, 25)
assert (223*114 + 1)*1091659**2 < t**446
assert r*t*t < 1
for d, limit in ((56, 160), (58, 167)):
    n = 10
    tail = ((d - 2)*r**(n+1)/(1-r)
            + (r*t)**(n+1)/(1-r*t)
            + (r/t)**(n+1)/(1-r/t))/(n+1)
    assert sum(6*r**k/k for k in range(1,n+1)) > d*F(7,80) + tail + F(2,5)
    q = F(d-1, d+1)
    bound = ((1-q)**(-d) * q**(-(d*(d-1)//2)) * t**(2*(d-1))
             * F(200,157)**d * F(factorial(d), d**d)**2)
    assert bound < sum(F(limit**j, factorial(j)) for j in range(513))
~~~

Both inequalities are one-sided necessary tests for prefixes.
They are compatible with further trace bounds and with the [exact integer-row test](Yin_L_G0462_v265.md#frontier) for complete candidates.
They do not determine whether a polynomial of either degree has measure below \(T\).
