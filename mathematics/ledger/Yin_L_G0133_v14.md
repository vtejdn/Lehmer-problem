Let d>=1 and let P in Z[z] be monic, irreducible over Q, reciprocal of degree 2d, with P(0)=1.
Choose epsilon in {+1,-1} and put
\[
 p(z)=\varepsilon z^{-d}P(z)>0\quad(z\in\mathbb T),\qquad
 p_d=\varepsilon.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here p_d is the coefficient of z^d in p.
For real-coefficient Laurent polynomials, f^star(z)=f(z^{-1}), which agrees with complex conjugation on the circle.
All circle integrals use normalized Haar measure, so
\[
 m(P)=\int_{\mathbb T}\log p,\qquad M(P)=\exp(m(P)).
 \tag{2}
\]
Assume an actual identity
\[
 2p=vv^\star+ww^\star,\qquad
 0\ne v,w\in\mathbb Z[z],\qquad \deg v,\deg w\le d.
 \tag{3}
\]
The general binary classification below does not require irreducibility or positivity; these hypotheses return at the measure consequence.

Write bars for coefficient reduction into the domain R=F_2[z,z^{-1}].
Equation (3) gives
\[
 \bar v\bar v^\star=\bar w\bar w^\star.
 \tag{4}
\]
Both reductions are nonzero.
Indeed, if one were zero, (4) and the absence of zero divisors would force the other to be zero.
Both integer rows would then have even coefficients, making every coefficient of the right side of (3) divisible by four, contrary to its coefficient 2p_d=+/-2.

<a id="binary-hermitian-norm-classification"></a>
## Complete binary classification after the common factor

For a nonzero binary Laurent polynomial, its valuation is its smallest supported exponent.
Write
\[
 \bar v=z^rV,\qquad \bar w=z^sW,
 \qquad V,W\in\mathbb F_2[z],\quad V(0)=W(0)=1.
 \tag{5}
\]
The normalized polynomials are monic because the coefficient field is F_2.
In the actual degree window, 0<=r,s<=d and deg V<=d-r, deg W<=d-s.
Set
\[
 G=\gcd(V,W),\qquad V=GA,\quad W=GB,\qquad \gcd(A,B)=1,
 \tag{6}
\]
using monic gcd and hence monic constant-one G,A,B.
Monomials have star-norm one, and cancellation of the nonzero factor GG^star in (4) gives
\[
 AA^\star=BB^\star.
 \tag{7}
\]
For a monic constant-one polynomial A of degree n, the highest Laurent exponent in AA^star is n, with coefficient one.
Consequently deg A=deg B=n.

For any nonzero monic constant-one polynomial H, define its ordinary reversal by H^R=z^{deg H}H(z^{-1}).
Multiplying (7) by z^n gives
\[
 AA^R=BB^R.
 \tag{8}
\]
Since F_2[z] is a unique-factorization domain and gcd(A,B)=1, equation (8) implies A divides B^R.
Their degrees and leading coefficients agree, so A=B^R and B=A^R.
The complete form is therefore
\[
 \boxed{\bar v=z^rGA,\qquad
        \bar w=z^sGA^R,\qquad
        \gcd(A,A^R)=1.}
 \tag{9}
\]
Conversely, every pair of the form (9) has equal star-norms, since A^R=z^nA^star and the factor z^n has star-norm one.

The coprime residual A contains no self-reciprocal irreducible factor.
For each distinct reciprocal pair of irreducible factors, the surviving multiplicity in A occurs in just one orientation and occurs in the opposite orientation in A^R.
The common factor G is unrestricted by this coprime residual condition.
These are statements in F_2[z]; they supply no factorization of the integer rows.

<a id="binary-shift-reversal-alignment-criteria"></a>
## Exactly when a single shift or reversal aligns the rows

In the binary Laurent ring, the first simple alignment satisfies
\[
 \bar w=z^k\bar v\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb Z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A=1.
 \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, equality of the normalized constant-one polynomials requires A=A^R.
Together with gcd(A,A^R)=1, this forces A=1.
The converse follows from (9), with k=s-r.

The second simple alignment satisfies
\[
 \bar w=z^k\bar v^\star\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb Z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G^R=G.
 \tag{11}
\]
To see this, let g=deg G and n=deg A.
Then
\[
 \bar v^\star=z^{-r-g-n}G^RA^R,\qquad
 \bar w=z^sGA^R.
 \tag{12}
\]
Their valuations require k=r+s+g+n, and cancellation of A^R reduces the remaining equality exactly to G=G^R.
Conversely that value of k gives (11) whenever G is reciprocal.
In the actual degree window,
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{case (10):}& k=s-r\in[-d,d],\\
 \text{case (11):}& k=s+\deg\bar v\in[0,2d].
 \end{array}
 \tag{13}
\]
These criteria classify the specified single shift or reversal followed by a half-sum construction.
They do not classify all norm-preserving integer transformations or all possible representations of p.

<a id="integral-half-sums-width-and-measure"></a>
## Integral half-sums with their full support and measure costs

In case (10), take the actual integer Laurent polynomial tilde w=z^{-k}w.
In case (11), take tilde w=z^k w^star.
In either case,
\[
 \widetilde w\equiv v\pmod2,\qquad
 \widetilde w\widetilde w^\star=ww^\star.
\]
Thus
\[
 a=\frac{v+\widetilde w}{2},\qquad
 b=\frac{v-\widetilde w}{2}
 \quad\hbox{belong to }\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}],
 \qquad
 \boxed{p=aa^\star+bb^\star.}
 \tag{14}
\]
The identity follows by expanding the two squares and cancelling their cross terms.

The actual original supports of v,w lie in [0,d].
In case (10), the support of tilde w lies in [-k,d-k], so the union used by a,b has width at most d+|k|<=2d.
In case (11), it lies in [k-d,k], and the union has width at most
\[
 \max(d,k)-\min(0,k-d)\le2d
 \quad(0\le k\le2d).
 \tag{15}
\]
A common monomial shift therefore makes a,b ordinary integer polynomials of degree at most 2d.
There is no supplied compression back to degree at most d.
In particular (14) cannot silently be treated as a new certificate within the adopted reflection theorem's original support window.

For the actual irreducible P, both a and b are nonzero.
Here is the necessary arbitrary-width check rather than an appeal to the old degree-bounded exclusion.
If one row vanished, then p=uu^star for a nonzero integer Laurent polynomial u.
Write the smallest and largest supported exponents of u as ell and h.
The coefficient of z^{h-ell} in uu^star is the product of its two nonzero endpoint coefficients; no other pair contributes to that exponent.
Therefore the actual Laurent radius of uu^star is h-ell, and (1) forces h-ell=d.
The shifted polynomial u_0=z^{-ell}u has degree d and nonzero constant term, and
\[
 \varepsilon P=u_0u_0^R.
 \tag{16}
\]
Both factors have positive degree d<2d, contrary to irreducibility.

Apply the [existing arbitrary-width term-count inequality](Yin_L_G0133_v11.md#certificate-term-count-mahler-bound) to the two nonzero rows in (14).
It gives
\[
 m(P)\ge\log2+m(a)+m(b)\ge\log2,
 \qquad
 \boxed{M(P)\ge2.}
 \tag{17}
\]
Each nonzero integer Laurent polynomial has nonnegative Mahler measure after multiplication by a monomial.
All measures in (17) are total measures; no degree normalization is used.

<a id="small-measure-two-row-binary-obstruction"></a>
## The necessary asymmetry for a small-measure two-row certificate

For an actual input (1)--(3) with M(P)<2, equations (10)--(17) imply
\[
 \boxed{A\ne1,\qquad G^R\ne G.}
 \tag{18}
\]
The threshold M(P)<2 belongs to this already supplied two-row result.
Separately, the older L11 threshold M(P)<3/2 makes an eligible reduced certificate have two rows in the first place.
Combining those two statements is legitimate, but the two-row entrance is not asserted for every certificate below M(P)=2.

There is also an exact minimum support consequence.
Every monic constant-one binary polynomial of degree at most two is reciprocal: the only positive-degree possibilities are z+1, z^2+1 and z^2+z+1.
Since A is nonconstant and gcd(A,A^R)=1, it is nonreciprocal, so deg A>=3.
Since G^R!=G, also deg G>=3.
Using deg V=deg G+deg A<=d-r<=d yields
\[
 \boxed{d\ge6.}
 \tag{19}
\]
This restriction assumes an actual two-row certificate and M(P)<2 on the irreducible original input.
It supplies no degree bound or measure gap for arbitrary P without that certificate, and it does not exclude the remaining windows d>=6.

<a id="nonreciprocal-common-factor-integer-control"></a>
## A complete binary countercontrol and its actual integer lift

In F_2[z], put
\[
 h=1+z+z^3,\qquad h^R=1+z^2+z^3.
\]
Each cubic has no root in F_2, so each is irreducible, and they are distinct.
Set
\[
 V=hh^R=1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4+z^5+z^6,\qquad
 W=h^2=1+z^2+z^6.
 \tag{20}
\]
These displayed product equalities are in F_2[z].
They give equal star-norms, with common factor G=h and residual A=h^R.
Neither alignment (10) nor (11) holds.
Equivalently, V is reciprocal and V!=W, while their equal degree and nonzero constant terms leave no nontrivial normalization shift that could make them equal.
This disproves the assertion that binary star-norm equality always forces one of the two simple alignments.

Now use the displayed zero-one polynomials themselves as actual integer rows:
\[
 v=1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4+z^5+z^6,\qquad
 w=1+z^2+z^6.
 \tag{21}
\]
No integer factorization v=hh^R or w=h^2 is asserted.
Their exact half norm is
\[
 \begin{split}
 p=\frac{vv^\star+ww^\star}{2}
  ={}&5+3(z+z^{-1})+3(z^2+z^{-2})
       +2(z^3+z^{-3})\\
     &+2(z^4+z^{-4})+(z^5+z^{-5})
       +(z^6+z^{-6}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{22}
\]
For verification, the lag-j coefficients of vv^star are 7-j for 0<=j<=6.
For ww^star the constant coefficient is three, and its positive lags 2,4,6 have coefficient one, with all other positive lags zero.
Averaging gives exactly (22).

The integer rows have no common complex root.
Indeed v(z)(z-1)=z^7-1 and v(1)=7, so any zero zeta of v is nonzero, satisfies zeta^7=1 and is not one.
At such a root,
\[
 \zeta w(\zeta)=1+\zeta+\zeta^3.
\]
For the integer polynomial h_Z=1+z+z^3, direct multiplication gives
\[
 z^7-1=(z^4-z^2-z+1)h_{\mathbb Z}+2(z^2-1).
 \tag{23}
\]
If w(zeta) also vanished, (23) would force zeta^2=1.
Neither choice is compatible with zeta^7=1 and zeta!=1.
Thus gcd(v,w)=1 over Q[z], and also as monic integer polynomials.

Consequently p>0 everywhere on the unit circle, since its two squared absolute values never vanish together.
The actual polynomial
\[
 P=z^6p
 =z^{12}+z^{11}+2z^{10}+2z^9+3z^8+3z^7
   +5z^6+3z^5+3z^4+2z^3+2z^2+z+1
 \tag{24}
\]
is monic reciprocal of degree 12 with integer coefficients and constant term one.
It has the actual degree-at-most-six doubled certificate (21)--(22).
Its integer rows are coprime although their binary reductions have the nonreciprocal common factor h.
Thus a common factor detected in (9) cannot automatically be lifted to a common factor of the integer rows.

No irreducibility or small-Mahler-measure assertion is made for (24).
This control disproves automatic binary alignment and automatic integer common-factor lifting; it is not a counterexample within the irreducible M(P)<2 branch.
It also does not rule out a different integer SOS representation of its p.

<a id="mod-two-certificate-return-boundary"></a>
## Original-input consequence and the first unsupplied step

The complete binary norm classification supplies a necessary arithmetic obstruction for a proposed small-measure two-row certificate.
A simple monomial or reversed alignment would lower its multiplier to one, at the fully retained Laurent width, and force M(P)>=2 on the irreducible original input.
Accordingly an actual two-row certificate below that threshold must retain both a nonreciprocal common factor and a nonconstant coprime residual orientation, with d>=6.

What is not supplied is an integer norm-preserving transformation for this remaining case, an integer factorization lifting its binary common factor, or an existence theorem for the original doubled certificate.
The explicit coprime integer control shows why the factor-lifting step cannot be assumed.
Other integer transformations remain possible; their existence or impossibility is not decided by (9)--(11).
The local obstruction neither covers inputs without certificates nor completes the unrestricted G0001 objective.
